Floyd-Warshall algorithm (dij​←min(dij​,dik​+dkj​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Shortest path problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
The Floyd-Warshall algorithm computes all-pairs shortest graph path lengths in a finite weighted directed graph with no negative-length cycles in a graph. Initialize dii​=0, use the arc cost for each existing directed edge, and use +∞ when no arc exists. For k=1,…,n, update every ordered pair (i,j) by the displayed rule. The storage is n2 lengths and the running time is O(n3).
For correctness, let dij(k)​ be the shortest simple graph path length whose intermediate vertices lie among the first k. A shortest allowed graph path either avoids k or splits at its unique occurrence of k, giving the recurrence. Conversely, concatenate shortest paths through k and remove any repeated-vertex sections; each such closed walk has nonnegative length, so removal cannot increase the length. This proves equality. The update can be performed in place because dkk​=0 and the kth row and column remain unchanged during stage k.
If negative-length cycles in a graph are present, a shortest walk may not exist and the recurrence need not compute shortest simple graph paths. Two optimal subpaths can concatenate into a repeated-vertex walk with a negative cycle; deleting that cycle increases its length. A negative diagonal entry in the algorithm's final array detects this obstruction to finite shortest-walk distances.
