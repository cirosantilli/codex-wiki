Narrow resonance and instrumental resolution (τ=ℏ/Γ)

A simple isolated particle resonance model starts with a survival amplitude proportional to
a(t)=e−iE0​t/ℏe−Γt/(2ℏ),t≥0.
(48)
Its survival probability is ∣a(t)∣2=e−Γt/ℏ, giving mean rest-frame lifetime τ=ℏ/Γ. The Fourier transform of this one-sided amplitude is proportional to (Γ/2−i(E−E0​))−1; its squared modulus gives a Lorentzian line shape. Write its normalized density as
L(E)=π1​(E−E0​)2+(Γ/2)2Γ/2​,∫−∞∞​L(E)dE=1.
(49)
At E−E0​=±Γ/2 the density is half its peak, so its intrinsic full width at half maximum is Γ. This is a local isolated-line approximation, extending the offset coordinate over the real line; physical production thresholds, interference and energy-dependent decay widths can require a richer quantum scattering model.
Now stipulate a centered normal distribution for the measurement response, with standard deviation σ. A measured coordinate x has density
Gσ​(x−E)=2π​σe−(x−E)2/(2σ2)​,O(x)=∫−∞∞​L(E)Gσ​(x−E)dE.
(50)
This convolution describes the smearing of an underlying line, with constant acceptance and no background. For a pair spectrometer the response summarizes invariant-mass resolution; for an idealized collider scan it summarizes incoming center-of-mass energy spread. These are different experimental sources of uncertainty even when the same mathematical response is useful.
Choose an arbitrary scale E∗​, intrinsic decay width Γ=0.20E∗​ and response standard deviations 0.40E∗​ and 1.20E∗​. Numerical integration gives apparent full width at half maximum values of approximately 1.05E∗​ and 2.93E∗​, respectively. The state and its rest-frame lifetime are identical in both cases. The normal distribution's width alone is 22ln2​σ; adding that width to Γ in quadrature is not an exact rule for this convolution.
For a purely illustrative E∗​=1MeV, taking ℏ≃6.5821×10−22MeVs gives τ≃3.2911×10−21s. These are invented line parameters, not J/psi meson measurements. Substituting the larger apparent width into ℏ/Γ would incorrectly shorten the inferred lifetime by nearly a factor of fifteen. An unresolved width can support an upper bound on intrinsic decay width, and consequently a lower bound on lifetime, only after the instrumental response and model assumptions have been assessed.
The discovery peak and its later interpretation answer different questions. The J/psi meson is understood as charmonium, a bound charm quark–charm antiquark system. Richter's 1976 lecture distinguishes the initial discovery from subsequent tests of competing explanations, including the related level structure and later evidence for particles carrying uncancelled charm. A narrow peak alone did not establish all these properties in 1974.[ref]
