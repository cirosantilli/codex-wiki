Order of a finite group (∣G∣)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group Finite group
OurBigBook.com Words: 132
The order of a finite group is its cardinality, written ∣G∣. The identity element is included: ∣Z/6Z∣=6, and the trivial group has order one, rather than zero.
Keep this count distinct from the order of a group element. In the additive finite cyclic group Z/6Z, the element 1 has order six, 2 has order three, 3 has order two, and 0 has order one. Each element's order is the least positive number of repetitions needed to return to the identity element 0. The order of an element of a finite cyclic group computes these periods directly.
The count also constrains subgroups: Lagrange's theorem gives ∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣ for a subgroup H of a finite group G, where the index of a subgroup counts its cosets. For H={0,2,4} inside Z/6Z, the two cosets are {0,2,4} and {1,3,5}, so 6=2⋅3. Divisibility is a necessary constraint; it does not imply that every divisor occurs as the order of a group element, as the Klein four-group shows.
