Angular magnification (Mθ​=θi​/θo​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Image formation Magnification
OurBigBook.com Words: 290
Angular magnification is the ratio of the signed viewing angle with an optical instrument to the signed viewing angle in a stated reference arrangement. Measure both as apparent directions from the eye, positive toward the same side of the optical axis; then an inverted view has negative Mθ​. If only positive angular sizes are compared, the result is ∣Mθ​∣ and carries no orientation information. [ref].
For the upright virtual image of a converging thin lens, let the eye be at x=e≥0 and take the unaided reference to be the same small object at a distance D from the eye. Within the small-angle approximation,
θi​≃e−vhi​​,θo​≃Dho​​,Mθ​≃me−vD​.
(37)
Specify D: keeping the object in its original position means D=e+u, whereas the customary magnifier rating uses a near-point reference, often D=25cm. A reference distance is a convention, not a claim that every eye has that near point. [ref].
For the virtual image diagram, m=4, v=−30cm and e=5cm. With D=25cm, Mθ​≃20/7, whereas keeping the object at D=e+u=12.5cm gives Mθ​≃10/7. Thus neither viewing comparison gives the transverse magnification four. A person may also be unable to focus on the unaided object at the shorter reference distance.
At u=f, each outgoing bundle is parallel with slope −ho​/f; its apparent viewing direction has angle θi​≃ho​/f. Consequently Mθ​≃D/f remains finite even though m is undefined at a finite image plane. Equivalently, in the limit u→f−, the factors m and e−v both grow without bound but their ratio tends to 1/f. For f=10cm and D=25cm, the relaxed-view prediction is Mθ​≃2.5. [ref].
