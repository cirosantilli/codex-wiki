Continuous image of a connected space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Connected space
OurBigBook.com Words: 221
If f:X→Y is a continuous map and X is a connected space, then its image f(X), with the subspace topology from Y, is a connected subset of Y. The whole codomain Y need not be connected; the assertion concerns the values actually attained. No injectivity is required.[ref]
For example, the continuous function f(x)=x2 maps the connected closed interval [−2,2] onto the connected closed interval [0,4], even though opposite inputs have the same output. The same continuous function maps the disconnected union [−2,−1]∪[1,2] onto [1,4]. Thus a connected image does not imply a connected source: a continuous map can merge different connected components.
For a continuous function r:[0,1]→R representing a continuous reading, r(0)=0 and r(1)=1 force some input with r(t)=1/2. Its image is a connected subset of the real line, hence a real interval containing both endpoint readings. For the synthetic model r(t)=t2, the missing-value test is explicit: r(1/2​)=1/2. A step reading equal to 0 for t<1/2 and 1 for t≥1/2 skips 1/2, but fails continuity at t=1/2.
Proof outline: a separation of a topological space of f(X) into nonempty open subsets pulls back under f to nonempty open subsets of X. Their intersection is empty and their union is X, contradicting connectedness. This also shows why a continuous map sends each connected component of its source into a single connected component of its target.
