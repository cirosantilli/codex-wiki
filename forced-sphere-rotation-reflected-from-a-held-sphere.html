Forced-sphere rotation reflected from a held sphere (δΩ=32πμR33a​R×F)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Stokes flow Method of reflections for Stokes flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 83
For equal distant spheres, a force F on the second generates a Stokeslet velocity (I+nn)F/(8πμR) at the held first sphere. The first sphere needs holding force −3a(I+nn)F/(4R) by Stokes drag law. Its reflected Stokeslet, combined with Faxén rotation law, gives the displayed spin on the second. This translation constraint produces order Fa/(μR3) rotation; if both centers were freely translating the same force monopole would not be emitted by the first sphere. Longitudinal forcing makes the leading cross product vanish.
