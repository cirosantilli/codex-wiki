Adiabatic demagnetization

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
A cooling method that reduces an applied magnetic field while suppressing heat transfer to or from the material. For a suitable material exhibiting paramagnetism, an approximately reversible field reduction can lower its temperature at approximately constant entropy.
The preparation matters as much as the field reduction. First establish thermal equilibrium with a cold heat bath, then raise the magnetic field while maintaining the bath temperature: this is magnetization in an isothermal process. The bath accepts heat and the material loses magnetic entropy. Next open a heat switch, thermally isolating the working material, and lower the magnetic field. If the sweep maintains internal thermal equilibrium with negligible entropy production, this second leg is an isentropic process. An adiabatic process alone does not guarantee constant entropy.
The attraction is a controllable energy scale: changing the magnetic field changes the separation of magnetic energy levels, even across a thermally insulating gap. This lets an already cold solid become colder without needing a colder contacting heat bath. A practical refrigerator also needs a load, a period of absorbing its heat, and a reset that rejects accumulated entropy to the bath. A single field sweep has finite cooling capacity.
