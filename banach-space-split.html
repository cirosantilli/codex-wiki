Banach space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Normed vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 46
A Banach space is a normed vector space over the real numbers or complex numbers that is complete for the metric d(x,y)=∥x−y∥. Explicitly, whenever a sequence (xn​) satisfies
∀ε>0∃N∀m,n≥N:∥xm​−xn​∥<ε,
(1)
there is an element x of the same space with ∥xn​−x∥→0. The hypothesis says that the approximations eventually agree to any requested accuracy; completeness says that they describe an actual object in the space. The Cauchy sequence condition can be checked before anyone knows what that object is.
This makes a Banach space a natural setting for constructing solutions by successive improvements. If corrections vk​ have ∑k​∥vk​∥<∞, their partial sums form a Cauchy sequence by the triangle inequality, and completeness supplies their sum. In fact, convergence of every such absolutely summable vector series characterizes a Banach space. The contraction mapping theorem uses precisely this mechanism: each correction is smaller than the preceding one by a fixed factor.[ref]
Every normed vector space of finite dimension is a Banach space. Infinite-dimensional examples include the absolutely summable sequence space and the l-infinity sequence space; the choice of norm remains part of the specification. A collection of increasingly accurate finite descriptions can require an infinite description for its limit, as the finitely supported sequence example below demonstrates. Completion of a normed space adds these missing limits while preserving all original distances.
