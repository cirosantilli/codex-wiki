Thévenin source suppression

 Home Electrical engineering Thévenin's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 288
To determine the Thévenin resistance, remove the external electrical load and set each independent electrical source to zero in the circuit model. A zero ideal voltage source enforces zero electric potential difference, so replace it by a short connection. A zero ideal current source enforces zero electric current, so replace it by an open connection. Retain every resistor, including any explicitly modeled supply electrical resistance. Turning an actual power supply's front-panel control to zero is not evidence that it physically becomes an ideal wire.
Retain every dependent electrical source and its controlling variable: its response to an electrical test source is part of the network's electrical resistance. Impose terminal voltage vt​ with an electrical test source and calculate jt​ entering the suppressed network at a. For a nonzero test yielding a finite ratio,
Rth​=jt​vt​​.
(36)
Alternatively inject a known entering electric current and solve for vt​. The test electric current is opposite to the delivered-current convention of the original Thévenin terminal response. [ref]
For an original illustration, put a positive resistor R across a,b and a dependent electrical source in parallel that injects electric current gvt​ from b toward a, where g has units of electrical conductance. Its control is the same terminal voltage vt​. Kirchhoff's current law gives
jt​=Rvt​​−gvt​,Rth​=R−1−g1​.
(37)
With R=1kΩ and g=0.5mS, a 1V test draws 0.5mA, giving Rth​=2kΩ. Removing the dependent electrical source would falsely give 1kΩ. With g=2mS, the model instead has Rth​=−1kΩ; with g=1mS it draws zero test electric current for any imposed voltage and has no finite ratio. An active physical realization requires a supply and has a limited operating range.
For a finite nonzero Rth​ and a well-defined short-circuit response, Isc​=Voc​/Rth​. Thus Rth​=Voc​/Isc​ is usable when Isc​=0. A source-free positive resistor has both Voc​=0 and Isc​=0, so this quotient cannot reveal its electrical resistance; an electrical test source can.
