On ζ=aeiθ, the Joukowski map gives z=2acosθ, so the image is the segment S=[−2a,2a]. Solving the resulting quadratic gives the inverse candidates
ζ=21​(z±z2−4a2​).
(72)
On D=C∖[−2a,2a], choose the analytic square root s(z) of z2−4a2 characterized by s(z)/z→1 at infinity. One explicit definition is
s(z)=(z+2a)z+2az−2a​​,
(73)
where the square root on the right is the principal square root. Its argument never lies on the nonpositive real axis for z∈D, so this defines the required holomorphic function. Then g(z)=(z+s(z))/2∼z.
The two quadratic roots have product a2. For z∈/S, neither root lies on ∣ζ∣=a, since that circle maps into S. Consequently exactly one root lies outside the circle, and it is the branch g chosen at infinity. Also, if two exterior points have the same image, then
(ζ1​−ζ2​)(1−ζ1​ζ2​a2​)=0,
(74)
whose second factor cannot vanish. Thus the Joukowski map is a conformal map from ∣ζ∣>a onto D, and g is conformal throughout D. The two endpoints are branch points and are excluded.
The complex potential in the circle plane is the sum of the uniform flow term Ue−iαζ, its circle-image term Ua2eiα/ζ, and the point vortex term −iΓlogζ/(2π). On ζ=aeiθ, the first two sum to 2Uacos(θ−α), which is real, while the imaginary part of the vortex term is the constant −Γloga/(2π). Hence the circle is a streamline, giving no normal flow. At infinity the first term gives the required uniform flow; the logarithmic term supplies circulation Γ. The potential may be multivalued by a real constant, but its velocity is single-valued.
Conformal transport therefore gives
w(z)=U(g(z)e−iα+g(z)a2eiα​)−2πiΓ​logg(z).​
(75)
In the frame where the plate is horizontal, its length is 4a and the stream has angle α; rotating the frame gives the equivalent incidence description. The complex velocity is
dzdw​=1−a2/ζ2W′(ζ)​.
(76)
At the trailing edge z=2a, corresponding to ζ=a, the denominator has a simple zero. Finite velocity requires
0=W′(a)=−2iUsinα−2πaiΓ​,
(77)
so the Kutta condition is
Γ=−4πUasinα.​
(78)
With this choice, cancellation of the common zero gives
dzdw​​z=2a​=2/aW′′(a)​=Ueiα+4πaiΓ​=Ucosα​.
(79)
Thus the limiting velocity has zero transverse component and is tangent to the plate.
