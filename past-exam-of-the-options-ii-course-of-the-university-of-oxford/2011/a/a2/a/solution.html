 ... Options II course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the options II course of the University of Oxford 2011 A A2 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
A splitting field of f over F is a field extension K/F in which f is a product of linear factors and which is generated over F by its roots. The generation condition rules out adjoining unrelated elements.
For existence, induct on the degree of a polynomial. Choose an irreducible polynomial divisor p of f. The quotient ring F[X]/(p) is a field because (p) is a maximal ideal; the class α of X is a root of p, hence of f. Over this field extension, divide out X−α and apply induction to the remaining polynomial. This gives an extension in which f splits. Inside it, the subfield generated by all roots is a splitting field. The induction starts with a linear polynomial, whose splitting field is already F. Thus every positive-degree polynomial has a splitting field; being monic is unnecessary for existence.
