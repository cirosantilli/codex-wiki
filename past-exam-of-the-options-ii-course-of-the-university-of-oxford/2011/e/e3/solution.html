A connected space has no expression as the union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets. A continuous surjection to the two-point discrete space would pull back its singleton sets to such a separation. Conversely, a separation X=U⊔V defines a continuous surjection by taking the values 0 on U and 1 on V. Thus these two conditions are equivalent, including for the empty space.
If a subset S⊆R contains a<c but omits some b between them, its intersections with (−∞,b) and (b,∞) separate it. Hence every nonempty connected subset is an interval. Conversely, let an interval I have a separation and choose a<b in its opposite sides. Restrict the associated two-valued continuous function to [a,b]⊆I and regard it as a real-valued function. The intermediate value theorem forces the value 1/2, a contradiction. Singletons and the empty set are connected directly.
For the union assertion, let u:X→{0,1} be continuous. Each restriction u∣Ai​​ is constant because Ai​ is connected. Since every pair of the Ai​ intersects, these constants agree, so u is constant on X. There is no continuous surjection, and X is connected. The empty indexing case gives X=∅ and is harmless.
To apply this to the plane, for each t∈R take the union of the horizontal line at height t with the vertical axis. Its two lines are connected and intersect; all such unions share the vertical axis and cover R2. Thus the plane is connected. For the punctured plane, take, for every r>0, the circle of radius r together with the positive horizontal ray. The circle is the continuous image of [0,2π], and intersects that connected ray. These unions share the ray and cover R2∖{0}, so the punctured plane is connected too. Translation gives the same conclusion after removing any one point.
Finally, suppose g:R2→R were a continuous injection. Its image I is a connected interval with at least three points. Choose an interior point c of I and its unique preimage p. The restriction of g to R2∖{p} is a continuous surjection onto I∖{c}. Its domain is connected, whereas its image is separated by the two half-lines on either side of c. This contradicts preservation of connectedness under continuous images. Therefore no continuous injection of the plane into the line exists.
