OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Start with O=(0,0) and E=(1,0). A constructible point is a point obtained from these by finitely many ruler-and-compass steps. At each step one may draw the line through two previously obtained distinct points, or the circle with a previously obtained centre and radius equal to the distance between two previously obtained points, and take isolated intersection points of the drawn lines or circles. A constructible line is a line through two distinct constructible points; a constructible circle has constructible centre and a radius obtainable as such a distance. These conventions also permit copying a previously obtained segment with the compass.
Finite construction from a fixed unit segment is essential: a drawing through an arbitrary unspecified real point does not make that point constructible.
