For the shifted exponential model, F(x)=1−e−λ(x−1) for x≥1. It is increasing, so the probability integral transform preserves order: F(X(r)​)=Y(r)​. The expected uniform plotting positions are r/(n+1) by part (b). Replacing the random transformed order statistic by this representative position gives
1−e−λ(x(r)​−1)≈n+1r​,x(r)​≈1+λ1​[−log(1−n+1r​)].
(140)
Thus plotting x(r)​ against tr​=−log(1−r/(n+1)) should give an approximate line with intercept one and slope 1/λ​.
The replacement is a quantile-plot approximation, not an assertion that expectation commutes exactly with the nonlinear inverse distribution function. The random points need not lie exactly on the line.
