The Neyman-Pearson lemma concerns testing two simple hypotheses with densities f0​ and f1​ relative to a common measure. A test that rejects when f1​(x)>kf0​(x), accepts when f1​(x)<kf0​(x), and randomizes on equality if necessary to achieve size α, is most powerful among all tests with size at most α:
E0​φ=α⟹E1​ψ≤E1​φ for every test ψ with E0​ψ≤α.​
(144)
Here φ is the described likelihood-ratio test and test functions take values in [0,1]. In the continuous normal setting below the boundary has probability zero, so randomization is unnecessary.
