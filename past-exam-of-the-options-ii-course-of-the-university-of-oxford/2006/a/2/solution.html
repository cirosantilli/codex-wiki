Right multiplication gives a right action (Hx)⋅g=Hxg. To obtain a homomorphism into permutations with the usual function-composition convention, use the associated left action
ρ(g)(Hx)=Hxg−1.
(1)
This is well-defined: replacing x by hx, h∈H, does not change the image coset. Moreover ρ(g)ρ(k)(Hx)=Hxk−1g−1=ρ(gk)(Hx), and each map is bijective with inverse ρ(g−1).
An element g is in the kernel precisely when Hxg−1=Hx for every x∈G. This says xg−1x−1∈H, equivalently g∈x−1Hx. Hence
kerρ=x∈G⋂​x−1Hx.​
(2)
This is the largest normal subgroup of G contained in H.
Now the index of H is q, so G/kerρ embeds into Sq​. Its order therefore divides q!. Since p>q is prime, p does not divide q!. From ∣G∣=pq it follows that p divides ∣kerρ∣. But kerρ⊆H and ∣H∣=p, forcing kerρ=H. Therefore H is normal.
For the last case let H=⟨b⟩. Normality makes conjugation by a an automorphism, so a−1ba=br with r∈{1,2,3,4}. Applying it twice and using a2=1 gives b=br2, so r2≡1(mod5). Thus (r−1)(r+1)≡0(mod5), and
a−1ba=b or b−1.​
(3)
Both possibilities are allowed; the order assumptions alone do not force the inverse case.
