The state space is {0,1,2,…}. Write q=1−p. The nonzero transition probabilities are
P(0,1)=1,P(j,0)=p,P(j,j+1)=q(j≥1).
(56)
The stationary equations give π1​=π0​ and πj​=qj−1π0​ for j≥1. Normalization gives π0​[1+∑j≥1​qj−1]=π0​(1+1/p)=1, so
π0​=1+pp​,πj​=1+pp(1−p)j−1​(j≥1).​

The remaining equation π0​=p∑j≥1​πj​ is satisfied, confirming stationarity.
The chain is irreducible: every positive state can jump to zero, and zero can reach every state through consecutive immigration steps. A stationary probability distribution makes it positive recurrent. Returns to zero can have lengths two and three with positive probability, so it is also aperiodic. The Markov-chain ergodic theorem now gives the almost-sure long-run time proportion for Xn​>K as the stationary tail:
j=K+1∑∞​πj​=1+p(1−p)K​.​
(58)
