A possibly randomized statistical test has rejection probability φ(x)∈[0,1] after observing x. Its statistical power at parameter θ is Eθ​φ, and its test size is the supremum of that statistical power over the null parameter set.
For simple hypotheses with densities f0​,f1​, the Neyman-Pearson lemma states that a level-α test is most powerful if it rejects where f1​>kf0​, accepts where f1​<kf0​, and randomizes on equality to obtain null rejection probability α. Here k≥0 is chosen to reach that test size. To prove optimality, let φ∗​ be this test and φ any test of level at most α. Pointwise,
(φ∗​−φ)(f1​−kf0​)≥0.
(65)
Integrating and using ∫(φ∗​−φ)f0​≥0 gives E1​φ∗​−E1​φ≥0. This proves maximal statistical power. For k>0, equality also forces any equally powerful test to use all its allowed test size and agree with the indicated decisions away from the likelihood-ratio tie set, up to null sets.
For the Poisson sample, S=∑i​Xi​∼Poisson(nμ) and, for every fixed μ1​>μ0​>0, the likelihood ratio is
e−n(μ1​−μ0​)(μ1​/μ0​)S.
(66)
It increases strictly with S. Choose an integer k so that Pμ0​​(S>k)≤α≤Pμ0​​(S≥k), and reject for S>k, with boundary rejection probability
r=Pμ0​​(S=k)α−Pμ0​​(S>k)​when S=k.​
(67)
This has exact test size α. Its rejection ordering and calibration do not depend on which μ1​>μ0​ is selected, so the lemma proves it uniformly most powerful for the whole one-sided alternative. Randomization is generally needed for an exact arbitrary test size in a discrete model.
For the approximate design, the central limit theorem under μ0​=1 gives critical sum n+1.645n​. Under μ1​, the sum is approximately normal with mean nμ1​ and variance nμ1​. Target statistical power 0.9 therefore gives
nμ1​​n+1.645n​−nμ1​​≈−1.282,
(68)
or n​(μ1​−1)≈1.645+1.282μ1​​. Thus the requested design expression is
n≈(μ1​−11.645+1.282μ1​​​)2.​
(69)
Round upward to obtain an integer design. This is a normal approximation to the requested statistical power, rather than an exact discrete sample-size guarantee; the exact Poisson test above provides its exact calibration.
