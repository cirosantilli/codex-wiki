For a joint probability density function, independence is equivalent to f(x,y)=fX​(x)fY​(y) almost everywhere. The displayed density is valid for ∣ρ∣<1. Complete the square:
1−ρ2x2−2ρxy+y2​=x2+1−ρ2(y−ρx)2​.
(52)
Integrating the second normal factor over y gives
fX​(x)=2π​1​e−x2/2.​
(53)
Dividing the joint probability density function by this marginal yields the conditional law
Y∣X=x∼N(ρx,1−ρ2).​
(54)
For ρ=0 the joint probability density function factors and the variables are independent. If ρ=0, the conditional mean depends on x, so independence fails; equivalently their covariance is ρ.
Define Z=(Y−ρX)/1−ρ2​. In the change of variables y=ρx+1−ρ2​z, the Jacobian 1−ρ2​ cancels the remaining density factor, leaving the product of two standard normal densities. Thus Z is standard normal and independent of X. One choice is
a=−1−ρ2​ρ​,b=1−ρ2​1​.​
(55)
Changing both signs gives the other standardized choice. The density transformation proves independence directly rather than inferring it from zero covariance without a joint-normal justification.
