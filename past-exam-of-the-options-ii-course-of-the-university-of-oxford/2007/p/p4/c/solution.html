Assume 0<ϵ<1/4, as implied by its stipulated smallness, and use the nontrivial matrix range n≥3. The row radii are ϵ at one, r=1/4+ϵ at each interior index, and 1/4 at n. Since 2r<1, all discs are disjoint. The second theorem therefore gives one eigenvalue in each disc. Each such eigenvalue is real: a nonreal eigenvalue would have its distinct complex conjugate in the same disc, contradicting its count of one. Thus
∣λ1​−1∣≤ϵ,∣λi​−i∣≤1/4+ϵ (2≤i≤n−1),∣λn​−n∣≤1/4​.
(57)
The eigenvalues are simple as well. An alternative reality argument is that a positive diagonal similarity makes this tridiagonal matrix symmetric, since its opposite off-diagonal entries have positive product.
