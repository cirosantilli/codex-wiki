The factor ηxx​/(1+ηx2​)3/2 is the signed curvature of the graph, so the pressure jump is surface tension times curvature, the planar Young–Laplace equation.
Take total velocity potentials Φ−​=ϕ−​ and Φ+​=Ux+ϕ+​. Each disturbance potential satisfies the Laplace equation. The interface moves with the fluid: for F=y−η(x,t), the material equation Ft​+v⋅∇F=0 gives ηt​+uηx​=vy​. Keeping only first-order terms and evaluating at y=0 yields
ϕ−,y​=ηt​,ϕ+,y​=ηt​+Uηx​.​
(66)
The unsteady Bernoulli equation is Φt​+∣∇Φ∣2/2+p/ρ=C(t), with any common gravitational contribution canceling across this equal-density interface. Subtracting the basic flow and linearizing gives p−′​=−ρϕ−,t​ and p+′​=−ρ(ϕ+,t​+Uϕ+,x​). The linearized pressure jump is Tηxx​. Hence
ϕ−,t​−(ϕ+,t​+Uϕ+,x​)=(T/ρ)ηxx​.​
(67)
Use complex modes with real parts understood:
η=Aei(kx−ωt),ϕ−​=B−​ekyei(kx−ωt),ϕ+​=B+​e−kyei(kx−ωt).
(68)
The decaying vertical factors solve the Laplace equation in their respective half-planes. The kinematic conditions give B−​=−iωA/k and B+​=i(ω−kU)A/k. Substitution in the dynamic condition gives
ω2+(ω−kU)2=ρT​k3,ω=2kU​±2ρTk3​−4k2U2​​.​
(69)
When the discriminant is nonnegative, the phase speeds are U/2±Tk/(2ρ)−U2/4​. They are two dispersive capillary-wave branches advected by the mean velocity U/2.
When U2>2Tk/ρ​, the frequencies are complex conjugates and one mode grows exponentially, with growth rate kU2/4−Tk/(2ρ)​. This is Kelvin-Helmholtz instability. Surface tension stabilizes sufficiently short waves, while for any nonzero shear, sufficiently long waves are unstable in this gravity-free equal-density model. A real cosine with real frequency describes the stable branches; the unstable case is represented by the real part of the corresponding growing complex mode.
