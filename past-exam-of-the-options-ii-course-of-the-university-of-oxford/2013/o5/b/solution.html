The unrestricted likelihood is maximized at the empirical proportions, while the null likelihood is maximized at πi​(θ). Twice the log of their ratio is
Λ(θ)=2i∑​ni​lognπi​(θ)ni​​.​
(89)
Large values mean the fitted null likelihood is much smaller than the unrestricted maximum, so rejection is in the upper tail.
For a regular identifiable scalar parameter lying in the interior, positive true cell probabilities, and sufficiently large expected counts, Wilks theorem gives an approximate chi-squared distribution with (k−1)−1=k−2 degrees of freedom under the null. The unrestricted probability vector has dimension k−1 and the fitted null has dimension one. At significance level α, reject if Λ(θ)>Fk−2−1​(1−α), or report the approximate p-value 1−Fk−2​(Λ).
Cells with very small expected counts may require a prespecified pooling scheme or simulation calibration; the asymptotic reference is not an exact finite-sample law. For a regular null with d fitted parameters, the corresponding degrees of freedom are k−1−d.
