For a short channel slice, the fluid volume per unit length is b(H+η) and the axial volume flux is b(H+η)u. The quasi-one-dimensional conservation of mass equation is therefore
∂t​[b(H+η)]+∂x​[b(H+η)u]=0.
(54)
Neglecting products of perturbations gives ηt​+(H/b)(bu)x​=0. This uses the section-averaged approximation stipulated in the problem; small transverse velocities ensure impermeability of varying-width walls but are not leading axial terms.
The Euler equations are ∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−ρ−1∇p−gez​. For irrotational flow, u=∇ϕ and (u⋅∇)u=∇(∣∇ϕ∣2/2), so
ϕt​+21​∣∇ϕ∣2+ρp​+gz=C(t).
(55)
At the zero-pressure free surface, linearization about rest gives ϕt​+gη=C(t); differentiate in x to get ut​+gηx​=0. Differentiating the mass equation in time and substituting gives
ηtt​=bgH​(bηx​)x​​.
(56)
Put c0​=gH​. For the exponential width, a time-harmonic amplitude satisfies
N′′+μN′+c02​ω2​N=0.
(57)
Writing N=e−μx/2V removes the first derivative and gives V′′+k2V=0, where k2=ω2/c02​−μ2/4. A nonzero real wavenumber, and hence a spatially travelling phase, exists precisely when
ω>2μ​gH​​.
(58)
For this case an outward-going solution matching the oscillating boundary is
U(x)=u0​e−μx/2−ikx,N(x)=ωH​(k+iμ/2)U(x).
(59)
Substitution in both linear equations verifies it, and Im(U(0)eiωt)=u0​sinωt. Below the cutoff the spatial exponents are real, giving evanescent responses; at equality the repeated-root solution has no travelling spatial phase.
This is the cutoff frequency in an exponentially widening shallow-water channel.
