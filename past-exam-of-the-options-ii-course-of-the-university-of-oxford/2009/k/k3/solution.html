Orient the body boundary counterclockwise. Its outward body normal satisfies nds=(dy,−dx), so the pressure force has complex form
Fx​−iFy​=−i∮pdz.
(49)
By steady Bernoulli's equation, p=p∞​+ρ(U∞2​−∣q∣2)/2, where q=w′=u−iv. Constant terms integrate to zero around a closed curve. On an impermeable boundary, velocity is tangent, so qdz=q​dz. Therefore ∣q∣2dz=q2dz, yielding the Blasius force theorem
Fx​−iFy​=2iρ​∮q2dz​.
(50)
For a slit body, the contour encloses both faces and the tips, interpreted by a limiting exterior contour.
Choose the analytic branch s(z)=z2−a2​ outside the slit with s(z)∼z at infinity. It may be written z−a​z+a​ using the principal individual square roots. For −a<x<a, approaching from above gives s+​(x)=ia2−x2​, and from below gives s−​(x)=−ia2−x2​.
Now
q(z)=cosα−is(z)z​sinα=e−iα−2z2ia2sinα​+O(z−4).
(51)
There is no z−1 term in q2. Since q2 is analytic in the exterior, enlarging the contour to a large circle shows its integral is zero. Thus
Fx​=Fy​=0​.
(52)
This total-force argument retains the slit-tip contribution automatically.
On the upper face, q+​(x)=cosα−xsinα/a2−x2​; on the lower face, q−​(x)=cosα+xsinα/a2−x2​. For 0<α≤π/2, their zeros are
x=acosα on the upper face,x=−acosα on the lower face​.
(53)
They are distinct face points even at α=π/2, when both have coordinate zero. At α=0, w=z and q=1 everywhere in the exterior: there are no velocity-zero stagnation points. The limiting positions at the two tips must not be substituted into a singular expression and treated as zeros.
For the sketch at α=π/4, the far-field velocity is (1,1)/2​, and streamlines are levels of Imw. The plate is the zero-stream-function boundary, with the two stagnation points at ±a/2​ on the indicated faces.
