The source-free Maxwell equations in vacuum are
∇⋅E=∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×B=c−2∂t​E.
(28)
Their real and imaginary parts are exactly
∇⋅W=0,∇×W=(i/c)∂t​W,W=E+icB.
(29)
Put θ=ωt−K⋅x. Differentiating gives ∇⋅W=(Ω⋅K)sinθ and ∇×W=−(Ω×K)sinθ, while (i/c)∂t​W=−iωΩsinθ/c. Thus the necessary and sufficient conditions are
Ω⋅K=0,Ω×K=iωΩ/c.
(30)
Here the complex dot product is bilinear, without complex conjugation. Taking the dot product of the second equation with Ω gives Ω⋅Ω=0. Crossing it with K gives
−∣K∣2Ω=(iω/c)2Ω,
(31)
so, since Ω=0, ∣K∣2=ω2/c2. Writing Ω=e+icb, the real conditions are
e⋅K=b⋅K=e⋅b=0,∣e∣=c∣b∣,K×e=ωb,K×b=−ωe/c2​.
(32)
Both physical fields are linearly polarized: E=ecosθ and B=bcosθ each oscillate along a fixed real direction. Combining two fields as E+icB does not turn the electric field into a circularly polarized field.
