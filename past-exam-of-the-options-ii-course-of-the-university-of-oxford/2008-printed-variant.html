B2 OurBigBook.com
The characteristic of a field is the least positive integer p with p1F​=0, or zero if none exists. Finiteness forces a positive characteristic, since the sequence of integer multiples of one must repeat. If this least integer factored into two smaller positive factors, their nonzero field multiples would multiply to zero, contradicting the absence of zero divisors. Hence it is prime. The field is a finite-dimensional vector space over its prime subfield Fp​, so choosing a basis gives ∣F∣=pa.
A nonzero polynomial of degree n over a field has at most n roots: each root supplies a linear factor by polynomial division, and induction applies to the quotient. In particular Xn−1 has at most n roots. The elements of F× whose orders divide n are precisely those roots. The permitted finite-group criterion therefore proves F× is cyclic.
For the final assertion take an odd Fermat prime p=2m+1, m≥1. Primality forces m to be a power of two: an odd factor s>1 in m=2rs would factor (22r)s+1 nontrivially. Since 2m≡−1(modp), the order of two divides 2m but not m. As 2m is a power of two, its only divisor not dividing m is 2m itself. Thus
ordp​(2)=2m​.
(3)
It generates the full group exactly when 2m=2m, which holds for m=1,2 and fails for m≥3. Therefore the possibilities are p=3,5, equivalently p≤5 among odd Fermat primes. The oddness is implicit in referring to two as a unit; p=2 is not an admissible case for that claim.

On a product of sigma-finite measure spaces, Tonelli's theorem equates product and iterated integrals for a nonnegative measurable function, allowing infinite values. A two-dimensional embedded submanifold is locally flattened by a continuously differentiable coordinate diffeomorphism of an ambient open neighbourhood onto a coordinate plane, or equivalently locally admits a regular two-parameter chart. A tangent vector at x is the velocity γ′(0) of a differentiable curve in M through x. Normal vectors form its orthogonal complement (Tx​M)⊥ in R3, which has dimension one.

The Milne-Thomson circle theorem says that, when f is a complex potential analytic inside and on ∣z∣=R and all its flow singularities are outside, the potential outside an impermeable circle is
W(z)=f(z)+f(R2/zˉ)​.
(34)
The reflected expression is analytic in the exterior: conjugating a local power series turns its argument into R2/z. It creates only interior image singularities. On ∣z∣=R we have R2/zˉ=z, so W=f+fˉ​ is real. The stream function is therefore constant on the circle, which is the impermeability condition. At infinity the reflected term is f(0)​+O(z−1), so it changes the velocity by O(z−2) and leaves the imposed far flow unchanged. These observations prove the theorem, up to the irrelevant additive potential constant and with no extra circulation introduced.
With a=z1​, ∣a∣>1, and unit radius, it gives
W(z)=z−am​+1−aˉzmˉz​​.
(35)
The image dipole is at 1/aˉ with moment −mˉ/aˉ2; writing the image term this way changes only an additive constant.
