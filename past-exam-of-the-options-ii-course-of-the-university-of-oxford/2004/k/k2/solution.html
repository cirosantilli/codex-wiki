Orient the body boundary C counterclockwise. Its outward normal vector satisfies nds=(dy,−dx), so the pressure force on the body has complex component
Fx​−iFy​=−i∮C​pdzˉ.
(35)
For steady incompressible flow and irrotational flow, Bernoulli's principle gives p=p0​−ρ∣w′∣2/2. The constant-pressure integral vanishes. Write q=w′=u−iv. The impermeability condition makes velocity tangent to C, implying qdz=qˉ​dzˉ. Thus ∣q∣2dzˉ=q2dz along the boundary, and
Fx​−iFy​=2iρ​∮C​(w′)2dz​.
(36)
This proves the Blasius force theorem, with force measured per unit length perpendicular to the flow plane.
For a circle of radius a, Milne-Thomson's circle theorem replaces an incident analytic potential f(z) by f(z)+f(a2/zˉ)​, making the stream function constant on the circle. Its reflected singularities must lie inside the circle; here the physical source lies outside. With c=Q/(2π) and d=a2/b, this yields, up to additive constants,
w(z)=c[log(z−b)+log(z−d)−logz],w′=c(z−b1​+z−d1​−z1​).
(37)
The interior image source and sink have equal and opposite strengths, so there is no extra net source inside the body. No independent vortex term is added. The potential may have logarithmic branches, but the velocity and pressure are single-valued.
Only d and 0 lie inside C. The residues of (w′)2 are
Resd​(w′)2=2c2(d−b1​−d1​),Res0​(w′)2=2c2(b1​+d1​).
(38)
Their sum is −2c2a2/[b(b2−a2)]. The residue theorem and the preceding force formula therefore give
Fx​=2πb(b2−a2)ρQ2a2​,Fy​=0​.
(39)
The force points towards the external source. Reflection symmetry across the horizontal axis independently checks the vanishing vertical component.
