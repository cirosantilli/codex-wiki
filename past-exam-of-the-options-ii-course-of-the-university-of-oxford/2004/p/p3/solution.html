The Lagrange interpolation polynomial
p(x)=∑j=0n​fj​∏0≤k≤nk=j​​xj​−xk​x−xk​​
(75)
has degree at most n and takes the required value at each node, proving existence. If two such polynomials existed, their difference would have n+1 distinct roots despite degree at most n, so it would vanish identically. This proves uniqueness.
Define P(x)=[(x−x0​)r(x)−(x−xn​)q(x)]/(xn​−x0​). It has degree at most n. At x0​ its value is q(x0​)=f0​, and at xn​ it is r(xn​)=fn​. At every interior node both q and r equal fi​, giving P(xi​)=fi​. Uniqueness therefore gives
p(x)=xn​−x0​(x−x0​)r(x)−(x−xn​)q(x)​​.
(76)
If the data come from xm with m≤n, uniqueness gives p=xm. If m<n, that same polynomial has degree at most n−1 and satisfies each smaller interpolation problem, so q=r=xm.
For the final claim assume n≥2. Put t=q−r. Its node values are t(x0​)=f0​−r(x0​), t(xi​)=0 for 1≤i≤n−1, and t(xn​)=q(xn​)−fn​. The linear spline s joining these values has a ramp on the first interval, is zero on all middle intervals, and has a ramp on the last. Thus
∫x0​xn​​(s′)2dx=x1​−x0​(f0​−r(x0​))2​+xn​−xn−1​(fn​−q(xn​))2​.
(77)
On each node interval s′ is constant and t−s vanishes at both ends. Hence ∫s′(t′−s′)dx=0 on that interval. Integrating the supplied identity and summing gives
∫x0​xn​​(t′)2dx−∫x0​xn​​(s′)2dx=∫x0​xn​​(t′−s′)2dx≥0,
(78)
which proves the claimed inequality and the minimum Dirichlet energy of a linear interpolant.
There is a source exception: with n=1, q=f0​ and r=f1​, so t is constant and the left side of the asserted bound is zero. If f0​=f1​, its right side is 2(f0​−f1​)2/(x1​−x0​)>0. Thus the final inequality requires n≥2, although the preceding interpolation statements remain valid for n=1.
