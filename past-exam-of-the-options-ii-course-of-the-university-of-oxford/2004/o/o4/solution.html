Let f0​,f1​ be densities or probability mass functions relative to a common measure, describing two simple hypotheses. A randomized hypothesis test is a measurable function 0≤φ≤1, whose value is the conditional rejection probability. Fix 0<α<1. The Neyman-Pearson lemma states that a test φ∗​ with E0​φ∗​=α is most powerful among tests with size at most α if it rejects where f1​>cf0​, accepts where f1​<cf0​, and randomizes as necessary on equality, for a threshold c≥0. Sets where f0​=0<f1​ are included in rejection. A likelihood-ratio quantile and boundary randomization supply such a test.
To prove optimality, let φ be any competitor with E0​φ≤α. Pointwise, the threshold rule makes
(φ∗​−φ)(f1​−cf0​)≥0.
(61)
Integrating and rearranging gives
E1​φ∗​−E1​φ≥c(E0​φ∗​−E0​φ)≥0.
(62)
Thus no competitor of the permitted size has larger statistical power, proving the lemma. Randomization permits exact size when discrete observations cannot supply it with a deterministic rejection region.
