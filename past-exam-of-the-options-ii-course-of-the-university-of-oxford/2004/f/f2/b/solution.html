Positive definiteness gives a positive smallest eigenvalue c with xtAx≥c∣x∣2. Hence the set xtAx=ytAy=1 is compact, and h attains a maximum there. Its two constraint gradients are (2Ax,0) and (0,2Ay); they are linearly independent because x,y are nonzero and A is invertible.
Put s=xtAy. At any constrained extremum the multiplier equations are
sAy=λAx,sAx=μAy.
(21)
Multiplication by xt and yt gives λ=μ=s2. If s=0 the objective is zero. Otherwise Ay=sAx, so y=sx and normalization gives s2=1. Thus every maximum is at most one, and the value one is attained at x=y. This proves (xtAy)2≤1​ on the normalized constraints.
For nonzero arbitrary vectors apply that result to x/xtAx​ and y/ytAy​. If either vector is zero the conclusion is immediate. Therefore
(xtAy)2≤(xtAx)(ytAy).​
(22)
Equality for nonzero vectors holds precisely when they are linearly dependent. This is the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the inner product induced by A.
