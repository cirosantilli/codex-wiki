A sufficient regular-level condition is ∇f(x)=0 at every point with f(x)=0. The implicit function theorem then makes the zero level a two-dimensional continuously differentiable submanifold. Its tangent space is
Tx​Sf​=kerDfx​={v:∇f(x)⋅v=0}.​
(35)
For a minimizing pair (a,b) and any tangent vector v∈Ta​Sf​, take a differentiable surface curve x(s) with x(0)=a, x′(0)=v. The function ∥x(s)−b∥2 has a minimum at zero, so its derivative 2(a−b)⋅v vanishes. Varying the other point similarly proves orthogonality to Tb​Sg​.
For the particular surfaces, minimize the distance from the origin to the second surface first. Put u=x2, v=y2, so u,v≥0 and z=(u−v−6)/2. Then
∥(x,y,z)∥2=u+v+4(u−v−6)2​.
(36)
Let s=u−v. Since u+v≥∣s∣, this is at least ∣s∣+(s−6)2/4, with equality when one of u,v is zero. For s≥0, this quadratic has its minimum at s=4, with value 5. For s≤0, it decreases up to zero and has minimum9 on that half-line. The global minimum is therefore5, attained at
b=(2,0,−1)or(−2,0,−1).
(37)
All points of this surface lie outside the unit sphere. For any such b, the distance to the sphere is ∥b∥−1, attained at a=b/∥b∥ by the triangle inequality. Consequently
dist(Sf​,Sg​)=5​−1,a=b/5​.​
(38)
This is a global minimization, not merely a stationary-point calculation.
