Count the pairs (g,ω) for which g fixes ω. Counting first by group elements gives ∑g​Fix(g), while counting first by points gives ∑ω​∣Stab(ω)∣. On a group orbit of size r, orbit-stabilizer theorem makes each stabilizer have size ∣G∣/r, so that group orbit contributes ∣G∣ to the second sum. Dividing by ∣G∣ proves Burnside's lemma:

A tetrahedron has four vertices. A rotational symmetry is determined by the image of one vertex, with four possibilities, and the cyclic orientation of the three vertices around it, with three possibilities. All these choices are realizable, so its rotational symmetry group of a tetrahedron has order 12. More explicitly, its elements are the identity, the two nontrivial rotational symmetries around each of four vertex-to-opposite-face axes, and the half-turn about each of three axes through opposite edge midpoints. Thus there is one element of order 1, eight of order 3, and three of order 2. The induced permutations of vertices are the even permutations, identifying the group with A4​.
Apply the group action to the set of edge colourings. A colouring is fixed by a rotational symmetry precisely when its colour is constant on each cycle of the induced edge permutation. The identity has six edge cycles and fixes 46 colourings. A vertex-axis rotational symmetry has two cycles of length three, fixing 42. An opposite-edge half-turn fixes the two edges on its axis as sets and exchanges the other four in two pairs, giving four cycles and 44 fixed colourings. Therefore
1246+8⋅42+3⋅44​=416.​
