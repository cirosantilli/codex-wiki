The product topology on X×Y has the open rectangles U×V, with U open in X and V open in Y, as a topological basis. The projection preimages are pX−1​(U)=U×Y and pY−1​(V)=X×V, so both projections are continuous.
If f:Z→X×Y is continuous, both coordinate compositions are continuous. Conversely, if both are continuous, then
f−1(U×V)=(pX​∘f)−1(U)∩(pY​∘f)−1(V)
(30)
is open for every basic rectangle, proving continuity of f. Given paths between two chosen endpoints in each factor, their coordinatewise pair is therefore a continuous path in the product, proving that a product of path-connected spaces is path-connected.
For the compact-neighborhood assertion, fix x∈K. For each y∈L, choose an open rectangle Uy​×Vy​ containing (x,y) inside W. Compactness of L chooses finitely many Vyj​​ covering L. Put
Ux​=⋂j​Uyj​​,Vx​=⋃j​Vyj​​.
(31)
Then x∈Ux​, L⊆Vx​, and Ux​×Vx​⊆W: any point of Vx​ belongs to one of those rectangles' second factors, while Ux​ lies in every first factor.
Compactness of K now selects finitely many Uxi​​ covering K. Set
U=⋃i​Uxi​​,V=⋂i​Vxi​​.
(32)
These are open, contain K and L, and if (u,v)∈U×V, choose an i with u∈Uxi​​ and use v∈Vxi​​. Hence
K×L⊆U×V⊆W.​
(33)
If K or L is empty, take the corresponding open factor empty and the other factor the whole space; the assertions then hold directly. No Hausdorff assumption on the factors is needed.
