Assume N≥2, as required by the source's selection probabilities, and put q=(1−p)/(N−1). Conditional on the red book's current position, the next independent selection determines its move without needing its earlier history. Selecting the red book moves it left, unless already leftmost. Selecting the plain book immediately to its right moves it right. Every other selection leaves its position unchanged. Thus this position process is a Markov chain on {1,…,N}, with
Pi,i−1​=p (i>1),Pi,i+1​=q (i<N),Pi,i​=1−p−q (1<i<N),
(74)
and boundary self-loop probabilities P11​=1−q, PNN​=1−p. These account for every selection and each row sums to one.
Stationarity at the first state gives π1​=(1−q)π1​+pπ2​, hence pπ2​=qπ1​. When N≥3, for the second state substitution of this identity into its stationarity equation gives pπ3​=qπ2​. More generally, summing stationarity over the initial segment {1,…,i} equates its sole outward and inward probability flows:
qπi​=pπi+1​(1≤i<N).
(75)
Write r=q/p. Then πi​=π1​ri−1. Normalization gives
πi​=1−rN(1−r)ri−1​(r=1),πi​=N1​(r=1),r=p(N−1)1−p​.​
(76)
The expression is positive also for r>1, since both numerator prefactor and denominator are negative. The uniform case is exactly p=1/N, when all books are equally likely to be selected. Since both neighboring transition probabilities are positive, the finite chain is irreducible; the displayed stationary distribution is therefore unique.
