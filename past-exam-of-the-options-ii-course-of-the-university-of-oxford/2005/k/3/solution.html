Milne-Thomson's circle theorem inserts an impermeable circular boundary into a two-dimensional irrotational incompressible flow. If f(z) is analytic in a neighborhood of the closed disc and its flow singularities lie outside, the new complex potential outside ∣z∣=a is
w(z)=f(z)+f(a2/zˉ)​.​
(61)
Reflection and coefficient conjugation make the second term analytic outside the circle, with its image singularities inside. On ∣z∣=a, a2/zˉ=z, so the two terms sum to 2Ref(z): the streamfunction is constant there. Thus no velocity crosses the circle. At infinity the image term tends to the constant f(0)​, which has no effect on velocity, and the far-field flow is retained. This proves the construction, with an additive potential constant immaterial.
Put A=μeiα, B=μe−iα. For the dipole the reflected potential is B/(a2/z−b)=Bz/(a2−bz), giving
w(z)=z−bA​+a2−bzBz​.​
(62)
Its image pole is c=a2/b, inside the cylinder. The complex velocity is
w′(z)=−(z−b)2A​+(a2−bz)2Ba2​.
(63)
Near c, the second term is C(z−c)−2 with C=Ba2/b2, while v0​(z)=−A/(z−b)2 is analytic there. The square of the pole term has no residue, nor does v02​. The cross term has residue 2Cv0′​(c), so
Resz=c​(w′)2=(a2−b2)34ABa2b​=−(b2−a2)34μ2a2b​.
(64)
Take the contour counterclockwise around the cylinder, as in the supplied Blasius force theorem. The residue theorem gives 21​iρ∮(w′)2dz=−πρRes(w′)2. Therefore
Fx​=(b2−a2)34πρμ2a2b​,Fy​=0.​
(65)
The force points toward the dipole. Its independence of dipole orientation follows from the product AB=μ2 in the sole residue contribution.
