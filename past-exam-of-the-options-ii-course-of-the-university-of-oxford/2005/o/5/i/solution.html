For the zero-mean Gaussian sample, the standardized vector (X1​/σ,…,Xn​/σ) has independent standard normal coordinates. Apply an orthogonal transformation whose first row is (1,…,1)/n​. The resulting coordinates Z1​,…,Zn​ remain independent standard normals: their joint density depends only on squared Euclidean length and the transformation has unit absolute Jacobian. Its first coordinate is Z1​=n​X/σ, while orthogonal decomposition gives
∑j=2n​Zj2​=σ21​∑i​(Xi​−X)2=σ2(n−1)S2​.

σn​​X∼N(0,1),σ2n−1​S2∼χn−12​,X and S2 independent.​

The ratio of the first independent standard normal and the square root of the normalized chi-square variable has a Student probability distribution, giving
Sn​X​∼tn−1​.​

For the paired height observations, form one cross-minus-self difference per pot. The sampling units for this test are the fifteen differences, not thirty separate plant heights. Under the usual independent normal model for differences, a null mean of zero gives a t14​ statistic. The observed statistic is
tobs​=4.713/15​2.607​.
(86)
For the two-sided alternative, reject at significance level δ if ∣tobs​∣>t14,1−δ/2​. Its two-sided p-value is 2[1−Ft14​​(∣tobs​∣)]. This uses the recorded values without performing the numerical calculation forbidden by the question. The normal difference model is what makes the small-sample Student calibration exact.
