Minimize the residual sum of squares ∥Y−Xθ∥2. Differentiating gives the normal equations XTXθ=XTY. Full column rank makes XTX positive definite and invertible, so the unique minimizer is
θ=(XTX)−1XTY.​
(77)
Since EY=Xθ, it is unbiased. The independent common-variance errors give Cov(Y)=σ2I, hence
Cov(θ)=σ2(XTX)−1.​
(78)
Independent Gaussian errors with common variance are a sufficient condition for this estimator also to be the maximum likelihood estimator: their log-likelihood differs from −∥Y−Xθ∥2/(2σ2) by terms independent of θ. Profiling an unknown common variance gives the same minimizer. Mean and variance assumptions alone do not specify a likelihood.
For the seven design points, xˉ=0 and x2=4. In increasing time order the design matrix is
X=​1111111​−3−2−10123​50−3−4−305​​.
(79)
Its columns are mutually orthogonal: the odd sums vanish and ∑(xi2​−4)=0. Their squared lengths are 7,28,84, respectively. Therefore
XTX=diag(7,28,84),Var(α)=7σ2​,Var(β​)=28σ2​,Var(γ​)=84σ2​.​
(80)
In particular γ​=∑i​(xi2​−4)Yi​/84. Testing for a quadratic contribution means testing H0​:γ=0 against γ=0.
The cooling measurements are stated to have zero-mean errors and known common variance, but normality is not explicitly assumed for them. Under the independent-error linear regression model as stated, Chebyshev's inequality gives a valid conservative level-δ test: reject when
∣γ​∣≥84δ​σ​.​
(81)
Its null rejection probability is at most δ because the estimator has mean zero and the calculated variance. If Gaussian errors are additionally assumed, the sharper exact test uses Z=84​γ​/σ∼N(0,1) under the null and rejects for ∣Z∣>z1−δ/2​. With only seven observations, exact normal calibration cannot be deduced from the printed moment assumptions alone.
