The stationary Schrödinger equation is
−2mℏ2​ψ′′(x)+V(x)ψ(x)=Eψ(x).
(55)
A nonzero bound-state wave function has ∫R​∣ψ∣2dx<∞, so it can be rescaled to have integral one. Odd parity means ψ(−x)=−ψ(x).
For −V0​<E<0, set k=2m(V0​+E)​/ℏ>0 and κ=−2mE​/ℏ>0. Oddness removes the interior cosine solution, and normalizability removes the growing exponentials outside. A continuous odd candidate can therefore be written
ψ(x)=A⎩⎨⎧​−sin(ka)eκ(x+a),sin(kx),sin(ka)e−κ(x−a),​x≤−a,∣x∣<a,x≥a.​
(56)
Continuity of the derivative at a gives kcos(ka)=−κsin(ka); oddness gives the other boundary condition. A nonzero solution cannot have sin(ka)=0, since the derivative condition would then force its interior coefficient to vanish. Thus, with ξ=ka and η=κa,
η=−ξcotξ,ξ2+η2=ℏ22mV0​a2​.​
(57)
Physical bound states require ξ,η>0. On 0<ξ<π/2, the cotangent condition gives negative η, so no physical intersection occurs. On (π/2,π), the curve −ξcotξ rises from zero to positive infinity, while the positive quadrant of the circle decreases. Their first intersection exists exactly when the circle radius R=2mV0​a2/ℏ2​ exceeds π/2.
In particular,
2mV0​<(2aπℏ​)2 ⟹ no odd-parity bound state.​
(58)
There is also no normalizable odd state at equality: the putative intersection has η=0, hence E=0, and its exterior tail does not decay. Higher positive branches begin beyond this first interval and require still larger R.
