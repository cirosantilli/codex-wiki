A sufficient differentiation under the integral sign theorem is the following local domination criterion. Suppose u(t,⋅) is measurable for each t, u(t0​,⋅) is integrable at one point of a neighborhood J of t0​, and for almost every x the function u(⋅,x) is differentiable on J. Assume an integrable function gJ​ satisfies ∣∂t​u(t,x)∣≤gJ​(x) for t∈J and almost every x. Then I is differentiable on the interior of J and
I′(t)=∫E​∂t​u(t,x)dx.​
(25)
The mean value theorem bounds the difference quotients by gJ​, and the dominated convergence theorem passes their pointwise limit through the integral. Applying this criterion locally throughout (a,b) proves differentiability there. One may impose the stronger condition that every u(t,⋅) is integrable from the outset.
