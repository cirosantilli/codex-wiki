The conformal map ζ=z3 sends the wedge 0<argz<π/3 to the upper half-plane. The physical vortex maps to ζ=c3; an opposite image vortex at c,3 makes the real axis a streamline. Pulling this potential back gives
w(z)=−2πiΓ​log(z3−c3)+2πiΓ​log(z3−c,3).
(66)
Since z3−c3=(z−c)(z2+cz+c2), splitting the first logarithm, up to an additive constant, gives exactly the required form. The image construction enforces zero normal velocity on both rays.
To find the vortex motion, hold c fixed while differentiating with respect to z, remove the self-induced singular term −iΓ/(2π(z−c)), and then evaluate at z=c. The remaining complex velocity is
2πiΓ​[−c2+c2+c22c+c​+c3−c,33c2​].
(67)
A complex velocity u−iv equals dc/dt, rather than dc/dt. Thus
dtdc​=2πiΓ​(−c1​+c3−c,33c2​).​
(68)
The overbar on the source derivative is essential to the trajectory calculation.
Write c=reiθ. Since c3−c,3=2ir3sin(3θ), multiply the equation by eiθ to obtain
r˙−irθ˙=4πr3Γ​cot(3θ)+4πriΓ​.
(69)
Taking real and imaginary parts gives
r˙=4πr3Γ​cot(3θ),θ˙=−4πr2Γ​.
(70)
Therefore
dtd​(rsin(3θ))=r˙sin(3θ)+3rcos(3θ)θ˙=0,
(71)
proving the trajectory invariant
rsin(3θ)=constant.​
(72)
The calculation also covers Γ=0, when the vortex position is stationary.
This is the sixty-degree case of a point vortex in a wedge.
