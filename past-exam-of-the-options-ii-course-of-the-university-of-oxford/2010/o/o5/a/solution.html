The significance level of a test is a bound α on its probability of rejecting a true null; for a composite null the bound is uniform over its parameter values. Its power at an alternative parameter is its rejection probability under that parameter.
The Neyman-Pearson lemma concerns a simple null density f0​ and a simple alternative density f1​ for the whole sample. Among tests of size at most α, a most powerful test rejects where f1​>kf0​, accepts where f1​<kf0​, and if necessary randomizes on equality so that its size is α. Let its rejection probability function be ϕ∗​, with 0≤ϕ∗​≤1, and let ϕ be any competing test of size at most α.

Even when the sample has a continuous density, the likelihood ratio can have an atom, so boundary randomization may still be necessary in the general statement.
