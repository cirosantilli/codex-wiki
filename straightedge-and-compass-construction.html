Straightedge and compass construction

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 343 Articles: 5
A compass-and-straightedge construction uses an unmarked straightedge to draw a Euclidean line through two already constructed points, and a compass to draw a circle with a constructed centre and radius. New points arise from intersections of these lines and circles. The allowed operations matter: an accurate numerical drawing need not supply such a construction.

General angle quintisection is impossible with straightedge and compass (96c5−120c3+30c−5=0)

Words: 132
Straightedge and compass construction OurBigBook.com
Consider the constructible angle whose cosine is 5/6 and sine is 11​/6. If one fifth of this angle were constructible, its cosine c would satisfy the displayed polynomial, by the identity cos5θ=16cos5θ−20cos3θ+5cosθ. The identity follows by taking the real part of (cosθ+isinθ)5 and substituting sin2θ=1−cos2θ.
The polynomial is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at five, so c has degree five over the rational numbers. The initial angle field Q(11​) already has degree two, and adjoining any finite collection of constructible numbers gives total degree over the rational numbers a power of two. Such a field cannot contain the degree-five element c, by the tower law for field extensions. Hence this constructible angle cannot be divided into five equal angles by straightedge and compass construction. Some particular angles can be quintisected; the obstruction disproves a construction valid for all angles.

Impossibility of doubling the cube by straightedge and compass ([Q(32​):Q]=3)

Words: 65
Straightedge and compass construction OurBigBook.com
Doubling the volume of a unit cube requires constructing its new side length 32​. Its minimal polynomial X3−2 is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at two, so this length has degree three. Every constructible number belongs to a finite tower of quadratic extensions, whose total degree is a power of two. The tower law for field extensions would force the degree three to divide that power of two, a contradiction. Thus no such straightedge and compass construction exists.

Constructible polygon

Words: 97 Articles: 2
Straightedge and compass construction OurBigBook.com
A constructible polygon is a polygon whose vertices can be obtained by a compass-and-straightedge construction from the specified initial data. The classical question for a regular polygon asks which numbers of sides permit an exact construction from a given side or circumscribed circle.

Constructible regular polygon

Words: 61 Articles: 1
Constructible polygon OurBigBook.com
A constructible regular polygon is a regular polygon admitting a compass-and-straightedge construction. The Gauss-Wantzel theorem classifies its possible numbers of sides.

Gauss-Wantzel theorem (N=2ap1​⋯pr​)

Words: 48
Constructible regular polygon OurBigBook.com
For N≥3, a regular polygon with N sides is a constructible regular polygon exactly when N=2ap1​⋯pr​, where a is a nonnegative integer and the pj​ are distinct Fermat primes; the product may be empty. Thus seventeen and fifteen qualify, while nine does not. Carl Friedrich Gauss established sufficiency, and Pierre Wantzel proved necessity in 1837.[ref]
