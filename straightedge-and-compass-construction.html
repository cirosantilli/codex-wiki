Straightedge and compass construction

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 146 Articles: 3
A compass-and-straightedge construction uses an unmarked straightedge to draw a Euclidean line through two already constructed points, and a compass to draw a circle with a constructed centre and radius. New points arise from intersections of these lines and circles. The allowed operations matter: an accurate numerical drawing need not supply such a construction.

Constructible polygon

Words: 97 Articles: 2
Straightedge and compass construction OurBigBook.com
A constructible polygon is a polygon whose vertices can be obtained by a compass-and-straightedge construction from the specified initial data. The classical question for a regular polygon asks which numbers of sides permit an exact construction from a given side or circumscribed circle.

Constructible regular polygon

Words: 61 Articles: 1
Constructible polygon OurBigBook.com
A constructible regular polygon is a regular polygon admitting a compass-and-straightedge construction. The Gauss-Wantzel theorem classifies its possible numbers of sides.

Gauss-Wantzel theorem (N=2ap1​⋯pr​)

Words: 48
Constructible regular polygon OurBigBook.com
For N≥3, a regular polygon with N sides is a constructible regular polygon exactly when N=2ap1​⋯pr​, where a is a nonnegative integer and the pj​ are distinct Fermat primes; the product may be empty. Thus seventeen and fifteen qualify, while nine does not. Carl Friedrich Gauss established sufficiency, and Pierre Wantzel proved necessity in 1837.[ref]
