Orbital speed (v)

Words: 78
Celestial mechanics OurBigBook.com
Orbital speed is the magnitude of the velocity of an orbiting body in a specified reference frame. For a Newtonian circular orbit around a point source of mass M, equating v2/r to GM/r2 gives v=GM/r​. In a curved spacetime, a speed inferred from coordinate radius and coordinate time generally differs from a speed measured by a local observer. For a circular orbit in the Schwarzschild metric, a static local observer measures vlocal​=r∣dϕ/dt∣/1−2GM/(c2r)​; r∣dϕ/dt∣ uses the clock normalization at infinity instead.

Two-body problem

The two-body problem determines the motion of two particles interacting only with one another. For a central force it separates into uniform centre-of-mass motion and one relative-coordinate problem with the reduced mass.

Equal-mass Coulomb circular orbit (T=2πms3/(2ke​q2)​)

Words: 57
Two-body problem OurBigBook.com
For two particles of equal mass m and opposite charges ±q separated by s, the centre of mass lies halfway between them. In a nonradiating classical model, each radius is s/2 and ke​q2/s2=mω2s/2. Hence ω2=2ke​q2/(ms3) and T=2πms3/(2ke​q2)​. Equivalently, relative motion has reduced mass m/2. Radiation and quantum binding invalidate an indefinitely stable classical orbit for actual microscopic charges.

Laboratory paths of an equal-mass binary

For equal masses and relative position r, the individual positions are rA​=R−r/2 and rB​=R+r/2, where the center of mass has uniform velocity in an isolated system.
