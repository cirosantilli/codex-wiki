Electroweak symmetry breaking

 ... Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Electroweak interaction Higgs field Higgs mechanism
OurBigBook.com Words: 449 Articles: 6
Electroweak symmetry breaking is the Higgs-field breaking SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​→U(1)Q​. It gives masses to the W and Z bosons while leaving the photon massless.
With the hypercharge convention Q=T3​+Y, the lower component of the Higgs doublet has T3​=−1/2 and Y=1/2. Thus Q⟨ϕ⟩=0: the vacuum expectation value is electrically neutral. This is why one combination of the neutral gauge fields remains the massless photon, as the electroweak doublet gauge-boson mass matrix shows.
Continue the chosen Higgs field potential example with g=3/5 and g′=4/5. The tree-level electroweak gauge-boson masses are mW​=72GeV, mZ​=120GeV and mA​=0. In the (W3,B) basis,
Mneutral2​=(5184−6912​−69129216​)GeV2.
(30)
Its orthonormal eigenvectors (4/5,3/5)T and (3/5,−4/5)T have eigenvalues zero and 14400GeV2. Thus cosθW​=3/5=mW​/mZ​. This is an exact model check, not a fit to measured electroweak interaction parameters.
A diagonal charged-fermion Yukawa coupling expands as
−2​yf​​(v+H)fˉ​f=−mf​fˉ​f−vmf​​Hfˉ​f,mf​=2​yf​v​.
(31)
The Yukawa coupling remains a free parameter: the mechanism relates a fermion mass to its scalar interaction but does not predict the numerical mass. This example concerns charged fermions; the minimal Standard Model has no right-handed neutrino and does not account for neutrino oscillations without an extension.
