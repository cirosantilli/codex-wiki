Lipschitz error bound for Bernstein approximation (∥Bn​f−f∥∞​≤L/(2n​))

If f is Lipschitz continuous on [0,1] with Lipschitz constant L, then Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the binomial distribution variance give
∣Bn​f(x)−f(x)∣≤LE∣K/n−x∣≤LE[(K/n−x)2]​=Lnx(1−x)​​≤2n​L​.
(55)
This is an explicit error guarantee, rather than only an existence theorem. For the Bernstein approximation of a corner, choosing n≥2500 guarantees supremum norm error at most 0.01; n>2500 guarantees error strictly less than 0.01. The guarantee is sufficient, not optimal.
For this specific target, the error reaches its maximum at x=1/2. Indeed the derivative of Bn​f is a weighted average of the slopes n[f((k+1)/n)−f(k/n)], all in [−1,1]: see derivatives of Bernstein polynomials. Subtracting the target makes the error nondecreasing to the left of the corner and nonincreasing to its right. For even n≥2, the symmetric binomial distribution at x=1/2 gives the exact value
∥Bn​f−f∥∞​=Bn​f(1/2)=n2n1​∑k=0n​(kn​)​k−2n​​=2n+1(n/2n​)​.
(56)
The last equality follows by pairing terms about n/2 and telescoping the binomial coefficients. It gives 1/4 for n=2 and 3/16 for n=4.
