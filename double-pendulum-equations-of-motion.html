Double pendulum equations of motion

Write Δ=θ1​−θ2​. Applying the Euler-Lagrange equations to the double pendulum Lagrangian, then dividing by mℓ2, gives
2θ¨1​+cosΔθ¨2​+sinΔθ˙22​+ℓ2g​sinθ1​cosΔθ¨1​+θ¨2​−sinΔθ˙12​+ℓg​sinθ2​​=0,=0.​

For example, the upper generalized momentum is ∂L/∂θ˙1​=mℓ2(2θ˙1​+cosΔθ˙2​). Its time derivative contains −mℓ2sinΔ(θ˙1​−θ˙2​)θ˙2​. Subtracting ∂L/∂θ1​=−mℓ2sinΔθ˙1​θ˙2​−2mgℓsinθ1​ cancels the mixed product and leaves the positive sinΔθ˙22​ term in the first equation. This cancellation is a useful check on the sign convention.
The matrix multiplying the two angular accelerations is
(2cosΔ​cosΔ1​),det=2−cos2Δ≥1.
(20)
It is invertible for every configuration, so specified angles and angular velocities determine both angular accelerations. For the same reason T>0 whenever the angular velocities are not both zero. The mass cancels from the motion equations, while the timescale is set by ℓ/g​.
As an exact constructed release, put θ1​(0)=α, θ2​(0)=0, and both angular velocities zero. Solving the two equations at release yields
θ¨1​(0)=−ℓ2g​2−cos2αsinα​,θ¨2​(0)=ℓ2g​2−cos2αsinαcosα​.
(21)
For 0<α<π/2, the upper rod initially moves back toward vertical, while the lower rod initially moves away from it. The lower pivot's acceleration makes this possible even though the lower rod starts vertical. The small-angle approximation gives θ¨1​(0)≃−2gα/ℓ and θ¨2​(0)≃2gα/ℓ.
The conserved mechanical energy is E=T+V, because the Lagrangian has no explicit time dependence. Directly, dE/dt is mℓ2 times the first equation multiplied by θ˙1​ plus the second multiplied by θ˙2​, so the equations imply dE/dt=0. This also supplies a check when computing a trajectory.
