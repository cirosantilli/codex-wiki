Permeability of a porous medium (k)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Porous-media flow Darcy law
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
Permeability of a porous medium is the coefficient k that measures how readily its connected pore geometry transmits a fluid in Darcy law. Its units are m2, since k/μ converts a pressure gradient in Pam−1 into a Darcy velocity in ms−1. Large open, well-connected passages generally give larger permeability of a porous medium than narrow or winding passages. Porosity alone cannot determine it: two samples can contain the same void volume but have different passage sizes and connectivity.
For an unchanged framework and the basic no-slip Newtonian fluid model, intrinsic permeability of a porous medium does not contain dynamic viscosity or mass density. These properties instead enter hydraulic conductivity K=kρg/μ. At the same horizontal pressure gradient, replacing a liquid with another of twice the dynamic viscosity halves Darcy flux even though k stays fixed. Structural changes, such as swelling or clogging, can change k; the independence claim assumes the pore geometry stays fixed.[ref]
An isotropic sample has scalar permeability of a porous medium. An anisotropic sample requires a tensor k in q=−k(∇p−ρg)/μ, so discharge need not be parallel to the net driving force. Layered porous permeability gives a concrete example: arranging individually isotropic materials in horizontal layers produces different vertical and horizontal effective permeabilities.
