Quotient group (G/N)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 570 Articles: 7
For a group G and a normal subgroup N, the quotient group G/N is the set of cosets gN, with operation
(gN)(hN)=(gh)N.
(44)
Its identity element is the coset N, and the inverse element of gN is g−1N. A quotient group treats all elements within one coset as the same input while retaining a consistent operation on the classes. The normal subgroup condition is what makes this operation independent of which representatives are chosen. [ref].
Take the additive group G=Z/6Z and N={0,3}. Since G is an abelian group, N is a normal subgroup. There are three elements in the quotient group:
N={0,3},1+N={1,4},2+N={2,5}.
(45)
Adding two copies of 2+N gives 4+N=1+N; adding 1+N and 2+N gives 3+N=N. Hence the quotient group has the addition table of Z/3Z. It does not select one representative and discard the others: its elements are the entire cosets.
This is useful whenever distinctions within a normal subgroup should be ignored. For example, the sign of a permutation retains only even or odd parity of a permutation in S3​. Its group kernel is the alternating group A3​, and the quotient group S3​/A3​ has two elements. The first isomorphism theorem explains why a group homomorphism's image is a quotient group of its domain.
