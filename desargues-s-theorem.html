Desargues's theorem (PP′,QQ′,RR′ concurrent⟹PQ∩P′Q′,QR∩Q′R′,RP∩R′P′ collinear)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic geometry Algebraic variety Projective space Projective plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 181 Articles: 2
For two projective triangles in a projective plane over a field, perspectivity from a point implies perspectivity from a line. The usual nondegenerate form assumes that the corresponding-side intersections are well defined and distinct. Its dual is the converse, by point-line duality. The result holds in the real projective plane; it is not an axiom valid in every abstract incidence plane.
One linear algebra proof explains the collinearity. Choose a representative o of the center. Rescale the paired representatives to p′=p+αo, q′=q+βo, and r′=r+γo, with nonzero coefficients in a nondegenerate configuration whose vertices differ from the center. The first corresponding-side intersection is represented by x=βp−αq=βp′−αq′. Similarly choose y=γq−βr and z=αr−γp. Then γx+αy+βz=0, so the three representatives are linearly dependent. Their projective points are consequently collinear.
