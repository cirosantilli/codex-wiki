Use the contact-interaction Bose-gas field integral for a homogeneous three-dimensional weakly interacting Bose gas with repulsive short-range interaction as the standard model. Let particle mass be mb​, scattering length as​>0, physical coupling g=4πℏ2as​/mb​ and gas parameter nas3​≪1. These are symbolic model definitions, not supplied numerical parameters. The grand-canonical coherent-state integral has periodic fields and action
SE​=∫0βℏ​dτ∫d3x[ψˉ​(ℏ∂τ​−2mb​ℏ2∇2​−μ)ψ+2g​(ψˉ​ψ)2].
(27)
The contact term is the normal symbol of gψ^​†2ψ^​2/2. In three dimensions a contact interaction needs a regulator and coupling matched to the measured scattering length; the displayed g is the low-energy physical coupling. This field integral is useful because an ordered saddle and its quantum/thermal fluctuations follow from the same action.
Stationarity with respect to ψˉ​ gives the imaginary-time Gross–Pitaevskii equation. A uniform saddle exists at μ>0 with ψ0​=n0​​eiθ0​ and condensate density n0​=μ/g. Its arbitrary phase reflects global U(1) symmetry; a selected condensate fixes a phase in the thermodynamic symmetry-breaking description. Write ψ=n0​​+η after choosing that phase. Linear terms vanish, and the quadratic part is
S2​=∫dτd3x[ηˉ​(ℏ∂τ​−2mb​ℏ2∇2​+μ)η+2μ​(η2+ηˉ​2)].
(28)
With εk​=ℏ2k2/(2mb​) and the Fourier convention eik⋅x−iωn​τ, the paired quadratic kernel is
M(k,ωn​)=(−iℏωn​+εk​+μμ​μiℏωn​+εk​+μ​),detM=(ℏωn​)2+εk​(εk​+2μ).
(29)
The one-half in the Nambu spinor action removes pair double counting. Analytic continuation of the inverse kernel gives the Bogoliubov dispersion
Ek​=εk​(εk​+2gn0​)​,Ek​≃ℏcs​k,cs​=gn0​/mb​​.​
(30)
At high momentum it approaches the free-particle kinetic energy plus its mean-field shift. The crossover scale is the healing length ξ=ℏ/2mb​gn0​​. Gaussian integration adds a fluctuation determinant to the saddle free energy. Expressed using the physical three-dimensional coupling, its zero-temperature integrand can be regularized as 21​[Ek​−εk​−μ+μ2/(2εk​)]; the subtraction follows from renormalization of a three-dimensional dilute-gas contact coupling, not discarding an infinite answer arbitrarily. The thermal contribution is kB​T∫d3k/(2π)3log(1−e−βEk​). A calculation beyond mean field must keep that matching and its thermodynamic derivatives consistent.
The density-phase action of a Bose gas makes the physical content clear, ψ=n0​+δn​eiθ. Up to a periodic density total derivative, the long-wavelength quadratic action contains
S2​=∫dτd3x[iℏδn∂τ​θ+2g​(δn)2+2mb​ℏ2n0​​(∇θ)2+8mb​n0​ℏ2​(∇δn)2].
(31)
number-phase conjugacy pairs the density fluctuation and phase in the quadratic density-phase action. Dropping the density-gradient term only in the long-wave limit and completing the density Gaussian square gives the Bose-gas phase-only action
Sθ​=21​∫dτd3x[gℏ2​(∂τ​θ)2+mb​ℏ2n0​​(∇θ)2].
(32)
This has the same sound pole. A term iℏn0​∂τ​θ is also present before restricting to the smooth zero-winding sector; although locally a total derivative, it cannot in general be ignored in topologically nontrivial sectors. The density and phase variables describe the same coupled Bogoliubov branch, not a second independent massive particle simply because the density has a static restoring cost.[ref]
A phase gradient carries superflow vs​=(ℏ/mb​)∇θ. Its energy cost defines the superfluid number density. At leading mean-field order the stiffness uses n0​, but condensate density and superfluid number density are different observables: for a Galilean-invariant continuum gas at zero temperature the latter is the full density even when interactions deplete the condensate. Bogoliubov coefficients give vk2​=[(εk​+gn0​)/Ek​−1]/2 and a small zero-temperature depletion of relative order nas3​​ in three dimensions. Large depletion or strong long-wavelength phase fluctuations would invalidate the small-fluctuation saddle approximation.
The Landau criterion for creating excitations is vL​=infk>0​Ek​/(ℏk)=cs​ for this homogeneous dispersion. Thus repulsion produces a linear sound mode and a positive critical velocity from the Landau criterion, whereas an ideal quadratic gas has zero value of this criterion. Vortices, obstacles and nucleation can reduce an actual flow threshold below cs​; the criterion alone is not a complete theory of metastable flow. At finite temperature thermal quasiparticles reduce the superfluid number density. Dimensionality and finite size also matter: the uniform three-dimensional weak-depletion condensate expansion must not be transferred unchanged to a one- or two-dimensional thermodynamic system. The field integral provides systematic corrections only within its dilute, stable and sufficiently weak-fluctuation regime.
