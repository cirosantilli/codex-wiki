The trace integrates the diagonal thermal kernel over a single fundamental interval, Z=∫02π​dϕ0​⟨ϕ0​∣e−βH^∣ϕ0​⟩. On the circle a closed path can wind an integer number m of times. Lift it to the real covering coordinate so its endpoints are ϕ(0)=ϕ0​ and ϕ(T)=ϕ0​+2πm, with T=ℏβ.
Under Wick rotation t=−iτ, ϕ˙​=i∂τ​ϕ, so the real-time phase iS/ℏ becomes minus the Euclidean action divided by ℏ, with
SE​[ϕ]=∫0T​dτ[2I​(∂τ​ϕ)2−i2πceΦB​​∂τ​ϕ].​

Thus sum the path integral e−SE​/ℏ over these lifted endpoint conditions and integrate ϕ0​ from zero to 2π. This gives the requested winding-sector representation of the trace. The flux term is a total derivative, so it leaves the local classical equation unchanged, but its weight is ei2πfm and distinguishes different winding sectors. This is the Aharonov-Bohm phase in a thermal path integral, rather than a force from a magnetic field on the ring itself.
