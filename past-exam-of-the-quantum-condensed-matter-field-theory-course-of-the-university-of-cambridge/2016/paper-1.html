For the XXZ spin chain, write Δ=1+α and replace each spin by a classical vector of length S. A bond is JS2nmT​diag(1,1,Δ)nm+1​, whose minimum is −JS2max(1,∣Δ∣). For an even periodic chain all bonds can attain that bound simultaneously. The classical ground states of an XXZ spin chain therefore give α>0 gives the two easy-axis Néel states Sm​=(−1)mSz and its reversal, with energy −JNS2(1+α).
For −2<α<0 the ground states are planar Néel configurations, Sm​=(−1)mS(cosφ,sinφ,0), with arbitrary φ: their degeneracy is a circle and their energy is −JNS2. This includes the easy-plane interval used later. Part (a), however, does not restrict negative α to that interval. For α<−2, Δ<−1 and the lowest states are instead the two uniform z-polarized ferromagnetic configurations, with energy JNΔS2. At α=−2, a rotation by π about z on alternating sites maps the classical energy to an isotropic ferromagnet: Sm​=S((−1)mnx​,(−1)mny​,nz​) for any unit vector n is a ground state. This special degeneracy is a sphere. At the other boundary, α=0, the ordinary Néel direction is arbitrary on a sphere. These statements concern classical minimizing configurations, not the degeneracy of the exact finite-chain quantum ground state.

(15)
The lattice makes k periodic modulo 2π/a, so choose one Brillouin zone. At long wavelength ωk​≃v∣k∣, where v=aκ/m0​​ is the sound speed. The continuum limit of a harmonic chain expands uj+1​−uj​≃a∂x​u and replaces ∑j​ by a−1∫dx. Defining mass density ρ=m0​/a and one-dimensional elastic coefficient K=κa gives

The continuum approximation describes acoustic wavelengths large compared with a; it is not a justification for extending the lattice dispersion and zero-point sums to arbitrarily large wavevector.[ref]
canonical quantization promotes the coordinates and momenta to operators with [uj​,pl​]=iℏδjl​ and the other equal-time commutators zero. In the continuum, [u(x),Π(x′)]=iℏδper​(x−x′). The nonzero normal modes can be represented by independent phonon operators obeying [bk​,bq†​]=δkq​. A useful lattice expansion is

A phonon is one quantum of a collective normal mode, carrying energy ℏωk​ and lattice momentum ℏk modulo a reciprocal-lattice vector. Reality of uj​ relates its Fourier coordinates by uk†​=u−k​; it does not remove the independent oppositely travelling phonon modes. Using one complete Brillouin zone avoids double-counting real standing-wave coordinates.[ref]
The free centre of mass has no restoring force and must not be treated as an oscillator of zero frequency. In three dimensions a contact interaction needs a regulator and coupling matched to the measured scattering length; the displayed g is the low-energy physical coupling. This field integral is useful because an ordered saddle and its quantum/thermal fluctuations follow from the same action.
Stationarity with respect to ψˉ​ gives the imaginary-time Gross–Pitaevskii equation. A uniform saddle exists at μ>0 with ψ0​=n0​​eiθ0​ and condensate density n0​=μ/g. Its arbitrary phase reflects global U(1) symmetry; a selected condensate fixes a phase in the thermodynamic symmetry-breaking description. Write ψ=n0​​+η after choosing that phase. The density and phase variables describe the same coupled Bogoliubov branch, not a second independent massive particle simply because the density has a static restoring cost.[ref]
A phase gradient carries superflow vs​=(ℏ/mb​)∇θ. Its energy cost defines the superfluid number density. At leading mean-field order the stiffness uses n0​, but condensate density and superfluid number density are different observables: for a Galilean-invariant continuum gas at zero temperature the latter is the full density even when interactions deplete the condensate. At finite temperature thermal quasiparticles reduce the superfluid number density. Dimensionality and finite size also matter: the uniform three-dimensional weak-depletion condensate expansion must not be transferred unchanged to a one- or two-dimensional thermodynamic system. The field integral provides systematic corrections only within its dilute, stable and sufficiently weak-fluctuation regime.

This is the Aharonov-Bohm phase in a thermal path integral, rather than a force from a magnetic field on the ring itself.
