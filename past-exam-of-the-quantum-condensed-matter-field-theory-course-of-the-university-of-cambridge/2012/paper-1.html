For the Transverse-field Ising model, use dimensionless spin one-half operators, so Sx has eigenvalues ±1/2. At zero transverse field every aligned bond contributes −J. The two ground states are
∣X±​⟩=n=1⨂N​∣±x⟩n​,E0​=−JN.​
(1)
They span an exactly two-dimensional ground space on the finite periodic ferromagnetic chain. Any linear combination is also a ground state; parity eigenstates are the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations. Choosing one ordered product state in the thermodynamic limit is spontaneous symmetry breaking.
For large positive field the leading term is −2Jg∑n​Snz​. Its unique minimizing state is
∣Z⟩=n⨂​∣+z⟩n​,E0​∼−JgN(g→∞).​
(2)
The exchange has zero first-order expectation in this state and produces virtual spin-pair fluctuations at finite g. Thus the polarized product is the limiting wavefunction, not an exact finite-field ground state. Bulk perturbation theory gives E0​/N=−Jg−J/(4g)+O(J/g3). The field-polarized phase has no twofold ordered-state degeneracy.

Let nn​=cn†​cn​ and Pn​=∏m<n​(1−2nm​). The Jordan–Wigner transformation gives 2Snx​=Pn​(cn​+cn†​) and Pn+1​=Pn​(1−2nn​). The canonical anticommutation relations imply
(cn​+cn†​)(1−2nn​)=cn†​−cn​.
(3)
Consequently the bondwise cancellation of a Jordan-Wigner string yields
4Snx​Sn+1x​=(cn†​−cn​)(cn+1†​+cn+1​),2Snz​=1−2nn​.
(4)
Substitution therefore gives
H=−Jn∑​[(cn†​−cn​)(cn+1†​+cn+1​)+g(1−2nn​)].​
(5)
The finite-ring parity qualification of an Ising quasiparticle gap matters here. For an exact periodic spin chain, the closing bond retains total fermion parity. The parity-dependent boundary condition of the Jordan-Wigner chain is cN+1​=−Pc1​, with P=(−1)Nf​. Fixing that parity determines the allowed Fourier grid, and the resulting many-body states must still have the chosen parity. Dropping this boundary issue preserves the thermodynamic energy density, but can lose a degeneracy or the correct finite-ring excitation gap. [ref]

With the specified Fourier transform convention, normal ordering gives
∑n​(cn†​cn+1​−cn​cn+1†​)=2∑k​coskck†​ck​,
(6)
where the possible constant ∑k​eik vanishes on the complete lattice momentum grid. Antisymmetry of opposite-momentum pairs similarly gives
∑n​cn†​cn+1†​=−i∑k​sinkck†​c−k†​,∑n​cn​cn+1​=i∑k​sinkck​c−k​.
(7)
Hence
H=−JgN+2J∑k​(g−cosk)ck†​ck​+iJ∑k​sink(ck†​c−k†​+ck​c−k​).
(8)
Use the Ising-chain Nambu spinor Ψk​=(ck​,−ic−k†​)T. Expanding its two diagonal terms uses c−k​c−k†​=1−c−k†​c−k​, retaining the constant. The result is
H=Jk∑​Ψk†​[(g−cosk)σz​−sinkσx​]Ψk​.​
(9)
This is a full-zone sum. Its opposite-momentum components are redundant; treating each matrix eigenvalue as an independent physical excitation would miss the factor of two in the quasiparticle energy. Self-paired momenta have zero anomalous term and are ordinary number levels.

Put dk​=(g−cosk)2+sin2k​. The matrix in the Ising-chain Nambu spinor has eigenvalues ±dk​. A canonical Bogoliubov transformation, with paired angles satisfying cosϑk​=(g−cosk)/dk​ and sinϑk​=−sink/dk​, diagonalizes it. Accounting for full-zone fermionic Nambu energy counting gives
H=k∑​ϵk​(γk†​γk​−21​),ϵk​=2Jdk​.​

E0​=−21​k∑​ϵk​,NE0​​⟶−2πJ​∫−ππ​1+g2−2gcosk​dk.​

Exact finite-chain states additionally obey their fermion parity sector.
The Ising-chain quasiparticle dispersion has bulk gap 2J∣1−g∣, closing at the Ising-chain quantum critical point. Finite-system parity splittings and the bulk gap are distinct scales. The three requested limits are illustrated below.

The Hellmann–Feynman theorem applied to the source field normalization gives
∂g∂E0​​=⟨∂g∂H​⟩=−2J∑n​⟨Snz​⟩.
(12)

Use dimensionless spin operators and nearest-neighbour exchange JH​>0 on a regular bipartite graph with N sites and coordination number of a lattice z. Count each bond once and define γk​=z−1∑δ​eik⋅δ, real for the inversion-symmetric examples considered here.
For the ferromagnetic Heisenberg model, HF​=−JH​∑⟨ij⟩​Si​⋅Sj​. Expanding the Holstein–Primakoff transformation about all spins along +z gives
Siz​=S−ai†​ai​,Si+​≃2S​ai​,HF​=Ecl​+JH​S∑⟨ij⟩​(ai†​−aj†​)(ai​−aj​),
(14)
up to interactions of higher order in boson density, with Ecl​=−NzJH​S2/2. The isotropic spin-wave vacuum energy comparison starts with this normal ordering. The linear spin-wave approximation then gives
EF​(k)=zJH​S(1−γk​),HF​=Ecl​+k∑​EF​(k)ak†​ak​.​
(15)
Each ferromagnetic magnon lowers total Sz by one. The fully aligned state is an exact eigenstate and ground state; there is no harmonic vacuum depletion or added zero-point energy in this normal-ordered Hamiltonian. The global spin-rotation multiplet remains degenerate. On a hypercubic lattice of spacing a, z=2d and γk​=d−1∑j​coskj​a, so EF​≃JH​Sa2k2. The exact one-magnon sector agrees with this result even at finite S; interactions enter for multiple magnons.
For the Heisenberg antiferromagnet, take HA​=+JH​∑⟨ij⟩​Si​⋅Sj​ and expand around a Néel state. On A, Sz=S−a†a and S+≃2S​a; on B, Sz=−S+b†b and S+≃2S​b†. A bond becomes
Si​⋅Sj​≃−S2+S(ai†​ai​+bj†​bj​+ai​bj​+ai†​bj†​).
(16)
With N/2 momenta in the magnetic Brillouin zone,
HA​=Ecl​+zJH​S∑k∈MBZ​[ak†​ak​+bk†​bk​+γk​(ak​b−k​+ak†​b−k†​)].
(17)
The bosonic Bogoliubov transformation has uk2​−vk2​=1 and tanh2θk​=γk​. For example ak​=uk​αk​−vk​β−k†​ and b−k​=uk​β−k​−vk​αk†​. It gives two branches in this reduced zone,
EA​(k)=zJH​S1−γk2​​,EA,0​=Ecl​+MBZ∑​[EA​(k)−zJH​S],vk2​=21​[1−γk2​​1​−1].​
(18)
The reduced-zone counting of bipartite spin waves gives N oscillators in total, not 2N. The negative vacuum correction and quantum depletion of Néel order, δS=(2/N)∑MBZ​vk2​, distinguish the antiferromagnet from the ferromagnet. At nonzero temperature replace vk2​ by vk2​+(uk2​+vk2​)nB​(Ek​). Hypercubic small-momentum modes have EA​≃2JH​Sad​∣k∣, hence linear physical frequency EA​/ℏ.
Self-consistency requires depletion much smaller than S and respects the Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint. In one dimension the antiferromagnetic vacuum depletion diverges logarithmically, invalidating ordered spin-wave theory; it does not alone prove an exact gap or its absence. At positive temperature, isotropic short-range models in d≤2 cannot sustain such magnetic order by the Mermin-Wagner theorem. In three dimensions the quadratic ferromagnetic modes give the low-temperature magnetization reduction proportional to T3/2, whereas the antiferromagnetic thermal reduction scales as T2. Anisotropy, fields, frustration and magnon interactions alter these conclusions. [ref]

Insert position resolutions between short-time evolution factors for H=p2/(2M)+V(q). Integrating the momenta gives the path integral, with real-time slice factor [M/(2πiℏΔt)]1/2 and weight eiS[q]/ℏ. A real classical trajectory cannot pass through a barrier with insufficient energy, but the path integral includes nonclassical paths.
Continue t=−is, with s physical imaginary time. The damped kernel is
KE​(qf​,qi​;T)=⟨qf​∣e−HT/ℏ∣qi​⟩=∫Dqe−SE​[q]/ℏ,SE​=∫0T​ds[2M​q˙​2+V(q)].
(19)
The Euclidean action saddle obeys Mq¨​=V′(q): motion in the inverted potential. For degenerate minima at ±a, subtract their common potential energy. A trajectory joining them with zero Euclidean energy satisfies
2M​q˙​2=V(q),SI​=∫−aa​dq2MV(q)​.​
(20)
It is an instanton; reversing its direction gives an anti-instanton. Thus a crossing amplitude is nonperturbative, proportional to e−SI​/ℏ. For the representative quartic well V=Mω02​(q2−a2)2/(8a2), direct integration gives
qI​(s)=atanh2ω0​(s−s0​)​,SI​=32​Mω0​a2.
(21)
The arbitrary center s0​ is an instanton translation zero mode. Expanding q=qI​+η gives a Gaussian path integral involving LI​=−∂s2​+V′′(qI​)/M. Treating the zero mode as a collective coordinate rather than a zero Gaussian denominator produces the instanton fluctuation prefactor
K=2πℏSI​​​[det′LI​detL0​​]1/2,L0​=−∂s2​+ω02​,κ=Ke−SI​/ℏ.
(22)
Common endpoint regularization defines the functional determinant ratio; K and kappa have inverse-time units. The prime removes only the zero eigenvalue, while integrating the center supplies the total available time. Large SI​/ℏ and κ≪ω0​ justify a dilute instanton gas.
Multiple crossings alternate direction. Integrating n ordered centers gives Tn/n!. Return paths contain even n, and transfer paths contain odd n, so their common local-well factor multiplies cosh(κT) and sinh(κT) respectively. The two lowest levels are consequently
E±​=Ewell​∓ℏκ,ΔE=2ℏKe−SI​/ℏ.​
(23)
The lower state is the even combination of the two localized states. Local anharmonic corrections modify Ewell​≃ℏω0​/2 and higher semiclassical corrections modify K. The thermal trace as a closed Euclidean path integral gives Z≃2e−βEwell​cosh(βℏκ) when higher local levels are negligible. This describes coherent level splitting in a stable well, not irreversible decay.
For a scattering barrier at energy E, the leading WKB tunnelling exponent gives an amplitude exp[−∫q1​q2​​2M(V−E)​dq/ℏ] and a transmission probability with twice that exponent. Endpoint matching fixes prefactors. Metastable escape instead uses a Euclidean bounce, with a negative fluctuation mode and a decay rate. Neither its negative mode nor the probability/amplitude factor should be transplanted into the stable double-well splitting. [ref]

Use the reduced attractive reduced BCS pairing Hamiltonian with volume V, ξk​=εk​−μ and attraction λ>0:
H=∑kσ​ξk​ckσ†​ckσ​−Vλ​B†B,B=∑k​c−k↓​ck↑​.
(24)
A specified pairing shell or an ultraviolet renormalization is part of the model. The trace is grand canonical. Insert fermionic coherent states into its time-sliced trace. The resulting Grassmann variables are antiperiodic, and with inverse-energy imaginary time 0≤τ≤β the dimensionless action is
S=∫0β​dτ[∑kσ​ψˉ​kσ​(∂τ​+ξk​)ψkσ​−Vλ​PˉP],P=∑k​ψ−k↓​ψk↑​.
(25)
This boundary condition is essential for Fermi-Dirac statistics; replacing it by a bosonic periodic trace changes the answer. A complex Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation uses the local identity
e(λ/V)PˉP∝∫dΔdΔ∗e−V∣Δ∣2/λ+ΔPˉ+Δ∗P,
(26)
with time-slice factors understood. The pairing field renders the fermion action quadratic. For a uniform static field the independent Nambu spinor block (ψk↑​,ψˉ​−k↓​)T has
G−1(k,iωn​)=(−iωn​+ξk​−Δ∗​−Δ−iωn​−ξk​​),ωn​=(2n+1)π/β,Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​.
(27)
Its determinant is −(ωn2​+Ek2​). The phase-independent determinant sign cancels under consistent reference normalization. Integrating the fermions gives the BCS pairing-field effective action, Seff​=∫0β​V∣Δ∣2/λdτ−TrlnG−1, with the normal-ordering/reference term retained. The fermionic pairing-block determinant normalization uses one independent pairing block for every k; a fully doubled spin-particle-hole trace instead needs one half. The subtracted grand potential of the reduced BCS model is particularly unambiguous:
Ω(Δ)−Ω(0)=λV∣Δ∣2​−β1​∑kn​lnωn2​+ξk2​ωn2​+Ek2​​.
(28)
Performing the Matsubara frequency sum yields
Ω(Δ)=λV∣Δ∣2​+∑k​(ξk​−Ek​)−β2​∑k​ln(1+e−βEk​),
(29)
up to a common constant. At zero gap this reduces exactly to the two-spin free-gas result. The Matsubara derivation of the BCS saddle follows by differentiating with respect to Δ∗ and using β−1∑n​(ωn2​+E2)−1=tanh(βE/2)/(2E) gives
1=Vλ​k∑​2Ek​tanh(βEk​/2)​,N=k∑​[1−Ek​ξk​​tanh2βEk​​].​
(30)
The second equation fixes the chemical potential at specified number. This stationary action is BCS mean-field theory, not an exact integration of pairing-field fluctuations. With constant single-spin density of states νF​ per volume and a symmetric energy cutoff Λ, weak coupling gives Δ(0)≃2Λe−1/(λνF​) and kB​Tc​≃(2eγE​/π)Λe−1/(λνF​). The zero-temperature condensation grand-potential density is −νF​Δ2/2 to leading weak-coupling order; using a spin-summed density would change these displayed factors. The BCS coherence factors are uk2​=(1+ξk​/Ek​)/2 and vk2​=(1−ξk​/Ek​)/2, and the associated BCS ground state is the normalized product of empty and occupied pair states. [ref]
Allowing a slowly varying complex pairing field generates phase stiffness and, near the transition, the Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity. A neutral paired system has a gapless phase sound mode; in a charged bulk superconductor electromagnetic coupling produces the Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor and the Meissner effect. These collective fluctuations are distinct from the gapped fermionic excitations. A microscopic dynamical calculation is needed to fix collective-mode dispersion and damping; the static saddle alone does not determine them. [ref]

For the untwisted quantum rotor, periodicity on the angle circle requires ψ(ϕ+2π)=ψ(ϕ). The normalized energy eigenstates and their angular momentum are
un​(ϕ)=2π​einϕ​,ℓn​=ℏn,En​=2Iℏ2n2​,n∈Z.
(31)
Taking the trace in this orthonormal basis therefore gives
Z=n∈Z∑​exp[−2Iβℏ2n2​].​
(32)
The n=0 state is nondegenerate and each nonzero ∣n∣ has two opposite-angular-momentum states. No flux or twisted angular boundary condition is present.

First lift the angle to the real line. The time-sliced configuration-space path integral for a free coordinate with inertia I has kinetic action ∫dtIϕ˙​2/2. The rotor kernel sums lifted endpoints separated by ϕF​−ϕI​+2πm, because all such endpoints represent the same final angle on the circle.
Continue real time as t=−iℏτ. Then e−iHt/ℏ=e−Hτ and the end of the thermal interval is τ=β, in inverse-energy units. Thus
ℏSE​​=2ℏ2I​∫0β​dτϕ˙​2.
(33)
The trace integrates the initial angle over one copy of the circle, not the whole lifted line. It yields the rotor winding-sector path integral
Z=∫02π​dϕm∈Z∑​∫ϕ(0)=ϕϕ(β)=ϕ+2πm​Dϕexp[−2ℏ2I​∫0β​dτϕ˙​2].​
(34)
The integer m is temporal winding number, not the angular-momentum label n. Locally the measure has the free-kernel normalization [I/(2πℏ2Δτ)]1/2 per time slice. Summing topological sectors and retaining this normalization are necessary for the correct thermal trace.

Analytically continue the supplied free propagator using M=I and t=−iℏβ. This gives Z0​=I/(2πℏ2β)​ without an arbitrary measure constant.
Apply the Poisson summation formula to h(x)=e−Ax2, with A=2π2I/(ℏ2β). Its Gaussian integral is
∫−∞∞​dxe−Ax2+2πinx=Aπ​​e−π2n2/A.
(38)
The prefactors cancel exactly because 2πZ0​π/A​=1. Therefore the momentum-winding duality of the quantum rotor gives
2πZ0​m∑​e−Am2=n∑​e−βℏ2n2/(2I),​

The coupled quantum rotor chain has a compact phase and conjugate integer number nj​=ℓj​/ℏ. Expanding the cosine gives
H≃−NJ+∑j​[2Iℓj2​​+2J​(ϕj+1​−ϕj​)2].
(40)
In the phase-space Euclidean path integral, using inverse-energy time, each rotor has the action term ℓj2​/(2I)−iℓj​ϕ˙​j​/ℏ. Completing its Gaussian square produces Iϕ˙​j2​/(2ℏ2). Equivalently, apply the short-time free-rotor kernel locally and retain winding sectors. The smooth-sector continuum limit at unit spacing is consequently
Z≃eβNJ∫Dϕe−S,S=∫0β​dτ∫dx[2ℏ2I​(∂τ​ϕ)2+2J​(∂x​ϕ)2].​
(41)
The overall constant is omitted when the reference bond energy is subtracted. With the same physical J as the Hamiltonian, the printed spatial coefficient has an extra factor 1/ℏ2. The imaginary-time normalization for a coupled rotor chain requires ℏ2J/2 inside a bracket with common prefactor 1/ℏ2. Setting ℏ=1 hides the discrepancy; it cannot establish the printed formula with explicit physical units.
Returning to physical time gives I∂t2​ϕ=J∂x2​ϕ. The harmonic phase mode of a quantum rotor chain therefore has
ω(k)=2J/I​∣sin(k/2)∣≃c∣k∣,c=J/I​,Ek​=ℏω(k).​
(42)
This is a gapless sound-like collective phase mode, analogous to neutral superfluid sound. In rotor-chain phase-number hydrodynamics, number fluctuations supply the compressibility and the intersite coupling supplies phase stiffness. The mode is a phase-density oscillation, not a free single-rotor transition. The global phase zero mode of a finite rotor chain is a compact rotor with inertia NI, which should not be counted as an independent zero-frequency oscillator.
The compactness qualification of a harmonic rotor chain matters here. The smooth harmonic theory requires small neighbouring phase differences and cannot settle the entire strong-fluctuation phase diagram. In one dimension its zero-temperature phase variance grows logarithmically, so the gapless regime has algebraic phase correlations rather than a true condensate. Thermal fluctuations and compact phase slips require additional care; phase-slip proliferation can produce a gapped phase outside the Gaussian regime. [ref]
