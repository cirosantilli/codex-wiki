Use the reduced attractive reduced BCS pairing Hamiltonian with volume V, ξk​=εk​−μ and attraction λ>0:
H=∑kσ​ξk​ckσ†​ckσ​−Vλ​B†B,B=∑k​c−k↓​ck↑​.
(24)
A specified pairing shell or an ultraviolet renormalization is part of the model. The trace is grand canonical. Insert fermionic coherent states into its time-sliced trace. The resulting Grassmann variables are antiperiodic, and with inverse-energy imaginary time 0≤τ≤β the dimensionless action is
S=∫0β​dτ[∑kσ​ψˉ​kσ​(∂τ​+ξk​)ψkσ​−Vλ​PˉP],P=∑k​ψ−k↓​ψk↑​.
(25)
This boundary condition is essential for Fermi-Dirac statistics; replacing it by a bosonic periodic trace changes the answer. A complex Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation uses the local identity
e(λ/V)PˉP∝∫dΔdΔ∗e−V∣Δ∣2/λ+ΔPˉ+Δ∗P,
(26)
with time-slice factors understood. The pairing field renders the fermion action quadratic. For a uniform static field the independent Nambu spinor block (ψk↑​,ψˉ​−k↓​)T has
G−1(k,iωn​)=(−iωn​+ξk​−Δ∗​−Δ−iωn​−ξk​​),ωn​=(2n+1)π/β,Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​.
(27)
Its determinant is −(ωn2​+Ek2​). The phase-independent determinant sign cancels under consistent reference normalization. Integrating the fermions gives the BCS pairing-field effective action, Seff​=∫0β​V∣Δ∣2/λdτ−TrlnG−1, with the normal-ordering/reference term retained. The fermionic pairing-block determinant normalization uses one independent pairing block for every k; a fully doubled spin-particle-hole trace instead needs one half. The subtracted grand potential of the reduced BCS model is particularly unambiguous:
Ω(Δ)−Ω(0)=λV∣Δ∣2​−β1​∑kn​lnωn2​+ξk2​ωn2​+Ek2​​.
(28)
Performing the Matsubara frequency sum yields
Ω(Δ)=λV∣Δ∣2​+∑k​(ξk​−Ek​)−β2​∑k​ln(1+e−βEk​),
(29)
up to a common constant. At zero gap this reduces exactly to the two-spin free-gas result. The Matsubara derivation of the BCS saddle follows by differentiating with respect to Δ∗ and using β−1∑n​(ωn2​+E2)−1=tanh(βE/2)/(2E) gives
1=Vλ​k∑​2Ek​tanh(βEk​/2)​,N=k∑​[1−Ek​ξk​​tanh2βEk​​].​
(30)
The second equation fixes the chemical potential at specified number. This stationary action is BCS mean-field theory, not an exact integration of pairing-field fluctuations. With constant single-spin density of states νF​ per volume and a symmetric energy cutoff Λ, weak coupling gives Δ(0)≃2Λe−1/(λνF​) and kB​Tc​≃(2eγE​/π)Λe−1/(λνF​). The zero-temperature condensation grand-potential density is −νF​Δ2/2 to leading weak-coupling order; using a spin-summed density would change these displayed factors. The BCS coherence factors are uk2​=(1+ξk​/Ek​)/2 and vk2​=(1−ξk​/Ek​)/2, and the associated BCS ground state is the normalized product of empty and occupied pair states. [ref]
Allowing a slowly varying complex pairing field generates phase stiffness and, near the transition, the Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity. A neutral paired system has a gapless phase sound mode; in a charged bulk superconductor electromagnetic coupling produces the Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor and the Meissner effect. These collective fluctuations are distinct from the gapped fermionic excitations. A microscopic dynamical calculation is needed to fix collective-mode dispersion and damping; the static saddle alone does not determine them. [ref]
