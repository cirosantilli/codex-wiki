Consider a conventional spin-singlet, weak-coupling, three-dimensional superconductor. Set ℏ=kB​=1 in the microscopic action and restore electromagnetic units below. An attraction λ>0 in an energy shell about the Fermi surface gives a Grassmann action
S=∫dτd3x[∑σ=↑,↓​ψˉ​σ​(∂τ​+ξ(−i∇))ψσ​−λψˉ​↑​ψˉ​↓​ψ↓​ψ↑​],ξk​=ϵk​−μ.
(23)
The attractive sign permits pairing even when the single-particle Fermi gas is otherwise stable. A complex Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation introduces a pair field Δ proportional at the saddle to λ⟨ψ↓​ψ↑​⟩. The action is then quadratic in fermions. Integrating them out gives the BCS pairing-field effective action
Seff​[Δ]=∫dτd3xλ∣Δ∣2​−TrlnG−1[Δ]+constant.
(24)
Here the trace uses one independent Nambu spinor pairing block; a fully doubled particle-hole representation instead carries its corresponding one-half factor. At a uniform saddle the Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian can be written as
HBdG​(k)=(ξk​Δ∗​Δ−ξk​​),Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​.
(25)
The negative branch describes the paired vacuum; excitations are Bogoliubov quasiparticles mixing particles and holes. Extremizing the effective action gives
λ1​=∫shell​(2π)3d3k​2Ek​tanh(Ek​/2T)​.​
(26)
The physical cutoff or a renormalized interaction is part of the microscopic model; an unregulated contact integral must not be used indiscriminately. With constant density of states N(0) per spin and shell half-width Ωc​, the zero-temperature weak-coupling solution is Δ0​≃2Ωc​e−1/[N(0)λ]. The exponentially small scale follows from the logarithmic pairing susceptibility of states near the Fermi surface. For this ideal weak-coupling model, 2Δ0​/(kB​Tc​)≃3.53. The microscopic theory relates the gap, pairing and thermodynamic transition.[ref]
Close to a continuous transition, for fields varying slowly compared with microscopic scales, expand the effective action in powers and gradients of Δ. Its static part is the Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity:
F=∫d3x[a(T)∣Δ∣2+2b​∣Δ∣4+K​(−i∇−ℏq​A)Δ​2+2μ0​∣B∣2​],q=−2e,b,K>0.
(27)
The normalization of Δ fixes the coefficients; here a(T) changes sign at Tc​. The uniform minimum has ∣Δ0​∣2=−a/b for a<0, condensation free-energy density −a2/(2b), and characteristic coherence-length scale ξ=K/∣a∣​. Above the transition the minimum is at zero. The quartic stabilization and gradient cost are precisely the ingredients of Landau theory for a complex order parameter. This controlled near-transition expansion supplies a bridge between microscopic BCS theory and macroscopic magnetic response; its coefficients and validity depend on the material and fluctuation regime.[ref]
Write Δ=(∣Δ0​∣+η)eiϕ. The radial fluctuation has positive local restoring curvature, whereas the phase has no local potential in a neutral theory with global U(1) symmetry. Freezing the amplitude gives
Fϕ​=2ρs​​∫d3x(∇ϕ−ℏq​A)2,ρs​=2K∣Δ0​∣2.
(28)
The neutral case supports a gapless collective phase mode. In the charged case the electromagnetic field couples to this phase rigidity. Under A↦A+∇χ and ϕ↦ϕ+qχ/ℏ, the displayed combination is invariant. Locally choosing a gauge with constant phase leaves a quadratic term in A. Varying the free energy gives the transverse London current and magnetic screening scale,
j=−ℏ2q2ρs​​A,λL−2​=ℏ2μ0​q2ρs​​.​
(29)
Combining this relation with static Maxwell equations gives exponential field penetration, the Meissner effect. Single-valuedness of the pair field around a loop with negligible current gives flux in units Φ0​=2πℏ/∣q∣=πℏ/e. These are gauge-invariant physical consequences of the condensate stiffness.
For the dynamics, the neutral phase and density mode is reorganized by the Coulomb interaction and the dynamical electromagnetic field. In a bulk three-dimensional system the long-wave longitudinal mode has a plasma gap, rather than remaining the neutral sound mode. This is the Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor: a phase coordinate becomes part of the electromagnetic collective response instead of surviving as an independent massless physical particle. Local gauge transformations are redundancies of the description, so a literal breaking of a local redundancy is not an observable. Phase coherence, current response and magnetic screening provide the physical statements.[ref]
A static Landau functional alone cannot determine that full dynamical spectrum. A two-dimensional film, for example, need not have the same long-wave plasma dispersion as a bulk charged system. Nor does a positive radial curvature automatically guarantee a sharp Higgs amplitude resonance: coupling to quasiparticles and other modes can damp it. The chosen mean-field phase is also a thermodynamic symmetry-breaking description; an isolated finite system can be formulated with definite particle number. The microscopic pairing saddle, the near-transition Landau expansion and the electromagnetic Anderson-Higgs response describe successive scales of the same superconducting state, with distinct assumptions at each step.
