This thermal trace as a closed Euclidean path integral explains the periodic imaginary-time direction. A density-matrix element instead retains open endpoints. For many identical particles the trace also sums over endpoint permutations, with a plus sign for bosons and permutation parity for fermions. In coherent-state language this becomes periodic bosonic fields and antiperiodic Grassmann fields, with corresponding Matsubara frequencies.
The Euclidean weight resembles a classical Boltzmann weight. A time-sliced local quantum field in d spatial dimensions becomes a classical-looking field model in d+1 dimensions, with anisotropic couplings and extra extent βℏ. Thus zero-temperature quantum criticality can be treated using an infinitely extended imaginary-time direction, while nonzero temperature imposes a finite thickness. The dynamical scaling of time need not equal the spatial scaling: the dynamical critical exponent specifies their relationship. This quantum-to-classical imaginary-time mapping requires qualification when weights have signs or imaginary terms; such a functional integral is not automatically an ordinary positive classical probability distribution. Even with a positive weight, the mapping concerns equilibrium statistics, not literal dissipative real-time trajectories. For a free particle the Euclidean kernel is a diffusion kernel with diffusion coefficient ℏ/(2m), illustrating the mathematical correspondence.
