Use the canonical anticommutation relations {cn​,cm†​}=δnm​ and {cn​,cm​}=0. In particular, nm​=cm†​cm​ is a projector, so (1−2nm​)2=1. Define Kn​=∏m<n​(1−2nm​). Then Kn†​=Kn​, Kn2​=1, and Kn​ commutes with cn​ and cn†​. Taking the adjoint of the given raising operator gives Sn−​=cn†​Kn​. Hence

The Holstein–Primakoff transformation converts a spin algebra into bosonic operators while retaining the finite physical spin Hilbert space. With [an​,am†​]=δnm​, write
Snz​=S−an†​an​,Sn+​=2S−an†​an​​an​,Sn−​=an†​2S−an†​an​​.
(11)
The Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint is 0≤nn​≤2S. Acting on ∣n⟩, the raising matrix element is n(2S−n+1)​ and the lowering element is (n+1)(2S−n)​. Their squared difference is 2(S−n), reproducing [S+,S−]=2Sz. Operator ordering inside the square roots is essential. For low boson occupation relative to S, expand them to obtain S+≃2S​a and S−≃2S​a†. Keeping quadratic terms gives the linear spin-wave approximation; higher terms describe magnon interactions. Large S helps control the expansion, but a low-dimensional infrared divergence can invalidate it even at large finite S.
For the ferromagnet choose HF​=−J∑n​Sn​⋅Sn+1​, J>0, with unit lattice spacing and ℏ=1. Expand around the all-up state. A longitudinal bond contributes −JS2+JS(nn​+nn+1​), and the transverse exchange contributes −JS(an†​an+1​+an+1†​an​). After a Fourier transform,
HF(2)​=−JNS2+∑k​ωF​(k)ak†​ak​,ωF​(k)=2JS(1−cosk)≃JSk2.​
(12)
These are ferromagnetic magnons. The polarized vacuum is an exact ground state of the full ferromagnetic Hamiltonian: every bond is in its maximum total-spin sector, minimizing that bond energy. It therefore has no quantum zero-point reduction of its magnetization. The one-magnon dispersion is also exact, although interacting many-magnon states need not be free bosons. Rotating the polarization generates the maximum-total-spin ground-state multiplet. Selecting a definite direction in a thermodynamic symmetry-breaking description breaks SU(2) to U(1); the two transverse spin components form a canonical pair, producing a single quadratic type-B Goldstone boson. A small aligning field gaps it by the Zeeman energy.
At nonzero temperature the one-dimensional ferromagnet is different: its thermal magnon number contains ∫dk[eβJSk2−1]−1, with infrared behaviour ∫dkT/k2. The divergent magnetization reduction signals the absence of finite-temperature long-range order in an infinite short-range isotropic chain, consistent with the Mermin-Wagner theorem. It does not disallow the polarized zero-temperature ground states just constructed.[ref]
For the antiferromagnet use HAF​=+J∑n​Sn​⋅Sn+1​, still J>0, on an even bipartite chain. The classical reference state alternates up and down. Rotate one sublattice through π so that both local quantization axes point along their reference spins. In unrotated physical components, to leading order SAz​=S−a†a, SBz​=−S+b†b, SA+​≃2S​a and SB+​≃2S​b†. Choosing Fourier phases using the actual site positions gives
HAF(2)​=−JNS2+2JS∑k∈RBZ​[ak†​ak​+bk†​bk​+cosk(ak​b−k​+ak†​b−k†​)].
(13)
The reduced Brillouin zone has M=N/2 momenta and length π. The pair creation and annihilation terms express the fact that an antiparallel reference product state is not an eigenstate. A bosonic Bogoliubov transformation with uk​=coshθk​, vk​=sinhθk​, tanh2θk​=cosk diagonalizes each two-mode block. With the sign convention ak​=uk​αk​−vk​β−k†​ and b−k​=uk​β−k​−vk​αk†​, the result is
HAF(2)​=EAF(2)​+∑k∈RBZ​ωAF​(k)(αk†​αk​+βk†​βk​),
(14)
ωAF​(k)=2JS1−cos2k​=2JS∣sink∣≃2JS∣k∣,​EAF(2)​=−JNS2+∑k∈RBZ​[ωAF​(k)−2JS].
(15)
Thus the harmonic antiferromagnetic modes have linear, rather than quadratic, long-wave dispersion. In an unfolded Brillouin-zone description the same result is represented by zeros at both zero and the antiferromagnetic wavevector; these are not extra independent degrees of freedom beyond the two-sublattice accounting.
The Bogoliubov vacuum has no α or β excitations but contains original spin bosons, with vk2​=21​(∣sink∣−1−1). Its staggered magnetization is ms​=S−M−1∑k∈RBZ​vk2​. This quantum depletion of Néel order diverges logarithmically at small k in one dimension. Linear spin-wave theory therefore does not establish a stable ordered antiferromagnetic ground state for this chain. Its nominal gapless dispersion is a result of expanding around an invalid ordered reference, not a general proof of the exact excitation spectrum. For the nearest-neighbour isotropic chain, integer-spin systems have a Haldane gap, whereas half-integer-spin systems are gapless; the spin-one-half chain in particular has fractional spin excitations rather than a simple dilute gas of ordered-state magnons. This distinction requires physics beyond the harmonic expansion, including the topological term in the continuum theory.[ref]

Tunnelling involves classically forbidden regions and can be described by imaginary-time saddles rather than real classical trajectories.
Quantum statistical mechanics follows by Wick rotation t=−iτ. The imaginary-time evolution operator is e−τH/ℏ, with Euclidean action

For many identical particles the trace also sums over endpoint permutations, with a plus sign for bosons and permutation parity for fermions. In coherent-state language this becomes periodic bosonic fields and antiperiodic Grassmann fields, with corresponding Matsubara frequencies.
The Euclidean weight resembles a classical Boltzmann weight. A time-sliced local quantum field in d spatial dimensions becomes a classical-looking field model in d+1 dimensions, with anisotropic couplings and extra extent βℏ. Thus zero-temperature quantum criticality can be treated using an infinitely extended imaginary-time direction, while nonzero temperature imposes a finite thickness. The dynamical scaling of time need not equal the spatial scaling: the dynamical critical exponent specifies their relationship. This quantum-to-classical imaginary-time mapping requires qualification when weights have signs or imaginary terms; such a functional integral is not automatically an ordinary positive classical probability distribution. Even with a positive weight, the mapping concerns equilibrium statistics, not literal dissipative real-time trajectories. For a free particle the Euclidean kernel is a diffusion kernel with diffusion coefficient ℏ/(2m), illustrating the mathematical correspondence.

These are related but distinct limits of the same quantum formulation.

Consider a conventional spin-singlet, weak-coupling, three-dimensional superconductor. Set ℏ=kB​=1 in the microscopic action and restore electromagnetic units below. An attraction λ>0 in an energy shell about the Fermi surface gives a Grassmann action
S=∫dτd3x[∑σ=↑,↓​ψˉ​σ​(∂τ​+ξ(−i∇))ψσ​−λψˉ​↑​ψˉ​↓​ψ↓​ψ↑​],ξk​=ϵk​−μ.
(23)
The attractive sign permits pairing even when the single-particle Fermi gas is otherwise stable. A complex Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation introduces a pair field Δ proportional at the saddle to λ⟨ψ↓​ψ↑​⟩. The action is then quadratic in fermions. Integrating them out gives the BCS pairing-field effective action
Seff​[Δ]=∫dτd3xλ∣Δ∣2​−TrlnG−1[Δ]+constant.
(24)
Here the trace uses one independent Nambu spinor pairing block; a fully doubled particle-hole representation instead carries its corresponding one-half factor. At a uniform saddle the Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian can be written as
HBdG​(k)=(ξk​Δ∗​Δ−ξk​​),Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​.
(25)
The negative branch describes the paired vacuum; excitations are Bogoliubov quasiparticles mixing particles and holes. Extremizing the effective action gives
λ1​=∫shell​(2π)3d3k​2Ek​tanh(Ek​/2T)​.​
(26)
The physical cutoff or a renormalized interaction is part of the microscopic model; an unregulated contact integral must not be used indiscriminately. With constant density of states N(0) per spin and shell half-width Ωc​, the zero-temperature weak-coupling solution is Δ0​≃2Ωc​e−1/[N(0)λ]. The exponentially small scale follows from the logarithmic pairing susceptibility of states near the Fermi surface. For this ideal weak-coupling model, 2Δ0​/(kB​Tc​)≃3.53. The microscopic theory relates the gap, pairing and thermodynamic transition.[ref]
Close to a continuous transition, for fields varying slowly compared with microscopic scales, expand the effective action in powers and gradients of Δ. Its static part is the Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity:
F=∫d3x[a(T)∣Δ∣2+2b​∣Δ∣4+K​(−i∇−ℏq​A)Δ​2+2μ0​∣B∣2​],q=−2e,b,K>0.
(27)
The normalization of Δ fixes the coefficients; here a(T) changes sign at Tc​. The uniform minimum has ∣Δ0​∣2=−a/b for a<0, condensation free-energy density −a2/(2b), and characteristic coherence-length scale ξ=K/∣a∣​. Above the transition the minimum is at zero. The quartic stabilization and gradient cost are precisely the ingredients of Landau theory for a complex order parameter. This controlled near-transition expansion supplies a bridge between microscopic BCS theory and macroscopic magnetic response; its coefficients and validity depend on the material and fluctuation regime.[ref]
Write Δ=(∣Δ0​∣+η)eiϕ. The radial fluctuation has positive local restoring curvature, whereas the phase has no local potential in a neutral theory with global U(1) symmetry. Freezing the amplitude gives
Fϕ​=2ρs​​∫d3x(∇ϕ−ℏq​A)2,ρs​=2K∣Δ0​∣2.
(28)
The neutral case supports a gapless collective phase mode. In the charged case the electromagnetic field couples to this phase rigidity. Under A↦A+∇χ and ϕ↦ϕ+qχ/ℏ, the displayed combination is invariant. Locally choosing a gauge with constant phase leaves a quadratic term in A. Varying the free energy gives the transverse London current and magnetic screening scale,
j=−ℏ2q2ρs​​A,λL−2​=ℏ2μ0​q2ρs​​.​
(29)
Combining this relation with static Maxwell equations gives exponential field penetration, the Meissner effect. Single-valuedness of the pair field around a loop with negligible current gives flux in units Φ0​=2πℏ/∣q∣=πℏ/e. These are gauge-invariant physical consequences of the condensate stiffness.
For the dynamics, the neutral phase and density mode is reorganized by the Coulomb interaction and the dynamical electromagnetic field. In a bulk three-dimensional system the long-wave longitudinal mode has a plasma gap, rather than remaining the neutral sound mode. This is the Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor: a phase coordinate becomes part of the electromagnetic collective response instead of surviving as an independent massless physical particle. Local gauge transformations are redundancies of the description, so a literal breaking of a local redundancy is not an observable. Phase coherence, current response and magnetic screening provide the physical statements.[ref]
A static Landau functional alone cannot determine that full dynamical spectrum. A two-dimensional film, for example, need not have the same long-wave plasma dispersion as a bulk charged system. Nor does a positive radial curvature automatically guarantee a sharp Higgs amplitude resonance: coupling to quasiparticles and other modes can damp it. The chosen mean-field phase is also a thermodynamic symmetry-breaking description; an isolated finite system can be formulated with definite particle number. The microscopic pairing saddle, the near-transition Landau expansion and the electromagnetic Anderson-Higgs response describe successive scales of the same superconducting state, with distinct assumptions at each step.

This is the sense in which the amplitude or density fluctuation is massive. The phase has no term proportional to φ2 without derivatives; a uniform phase rotation costs no action. It is the massless direction required by global U(1) symmetry.
The term iδρ∂τ​φ shows density-phase conjugacy. It is important to distinguish static density stiffness versus an excitation gap: the density and phase do not form two independent normal-mode branches with one gapped and one gapless particle. In this nonrelativistic first-order theory they supply the conjugate variables of one positive Bogoliubov branch, derived below. The omitted cubic terms include δρ∣∇φ∣2/(2m) and corrections from the density-gradient denominator.
There is also a background term iρ0​∫dτddr∂τ​φ. It vanishes for smooth fluctuations with zero temporal winding. Periodicity of ψ, however, permits φ(β)−φ(0)=2πw. The compact phase winding term must be retained when such sectors are included; it is not generally the same constant in all sectors. The local Gaussian phonon calculation concerns the smooth zero-winding sector and does not describe vortices or points where the density vanishes.

The convergent Gaussian integral can be performed by a contour shift or by its Fourier-transform identity. Its determinant is independent of φ at this order. Combining that normalization with the saddle action into a constant S0​ gives
Z=e−S0​∫Dφe−Seff​[φ],Seff​=21​∫0β​dτ∫V​ddr[g(∂τ​φ)2​+mρ0​​∣∇φ∣2].​

The saddle contribution to S0​ is −βVμ2/(2g); the additional measure-dependent constant need not be assigned an invented numerical value. The density Gaussian extends the fluctuation integration beyond its exact positivity domain, which is justified only while fluctuations around ρ0​>0 are small. The smooth zero-winding assumption is also required as discussed above. A constant global phase zero mode gives a finite compact-group volume, handled separately from nonzero phonon modes.
Fourier transforming the Bose-gas phase-only action gives a kernel ωn2​/g+(ρ0​/m)q2. Analytically continuing ωn​↦−i(ω+i0+) gives its real-frequency poles,
ω(q)=c∣q∣,c=gρ0​/m​.​
(38)
In the present units the excitation energy equals ω. Each real Fourier coordinate is a harmonic oscillator with inertia coefficient 1/g and spring coefficient ρ0​q2/m, hence frequency equal to the square root of their ratio. This is the direct relation to a quantum harmonic chain. The mode is a gapless sound phonon; its finite speed reflects both the compressibility ∂ρ/∂μ=1/g and the phase stiffness ρ0​/m.
Retaining the density-gradient term provides a useful check. Its integration instead gives a temporal coefficient 1/Aq​, so
ω2(q)=mρ0​q2​(g+4mρ0​q2​)=c2q2+4m2q4​,ω(q)=ϵq​(ϵq​+2gρ0​)​,ϵq​=2mq2​.
(39)
This is the Bogoliubov spectrum. Its small-q limit is the derived sound branch; at large q within the contact model it crosses towards particle-like behaviour. The crossover in the squared dispersion is q∼2mgρ0​​, precisely the scale beyond which the discarded density gradient is not small.
The same action checks the dimensional restriction in the saddle analysis. At nonzero temperature its zero Matsubara mode gives phase fluctuations proportional to ∫ddq/q2; at zero temperature integrating the frequency gives ∫ddq/∣q∣. Their infrared divergences invalidate uniform true long-range order for d≤2 at nonzero temperature and for d=1 at zero temperature. The long-wave collective description can remain useful for correlations even where a global condensate saddle is not a literal symmetry-broken state. The contact coupling here is an effective low-energy parameter; this calculation does not determine ultraviolet renormalization or trap-specific surface modes.
