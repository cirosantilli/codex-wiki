Use a real, even interaction V−k​=Vk​. Let V=L3 and Nex​=∑k=0​bk†​bk​. In the Bogoliubov theory expansion, keep terms with zero or two noncondensed operators. The zero-momentum term gives V0​N0​(N0​−1)/(2V). The terms with two noncondensed operators give
∑k=0​[VN0​​(V0​+Vk​)bk†​bk​+2VN0​Vk​​(bk†​b−k†​+b−k​bk​)].
(1)
Here V0​ is the direct condensate interaction and Vk​ is its exchange contribution. At fixed total particle number, N0​=N−Nex​, so
2VV0​N02​​=2VV0​N2​−nV0​Nex​+O(Nex2​/V).
(2)
The second term cancels the direct normal term. Adding the kinetic energy yields the number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian
H2​=Emf​+k=0∑​[Ak​bk†​bk​+2Bk​​(bk†​b−k†​+b−k​bk​)],Emf​=2VV0​n2​,Ak​=ϵk​+nVk​,Bk​=nVk​.​
(3)
The constant is the condensate mean-field theory interaction energy; the normal term describes moving a particle out of the condensate, while the anomalous term creates or annihilates an opposite-momentum pair by exchanging two particles with the condensate. Total particle number remains fixed in the underlying theory; the condensate amplitudes in this notation are treated as classical amplitudes.
For condensate fluctuation power counting, put δ=Nex​/N≪1. Formal interaction-amplitude counting gives H2​−Emf​∼Emf​δ, cubic terms ∼Emf​δ3/2 and quartic terms ∼Emf​δ2; these are not uniform bounds on individual modes. Thus the latter are down by δ​ and δ relative to the quadratic interaction terms. The N0​−1 correction is relative 1/N. Homogeneity eliminates terms linear in nonzero-momentum operators. Small depletion and weak interactions, not low temperature alone, justify the truncation. [ref]
