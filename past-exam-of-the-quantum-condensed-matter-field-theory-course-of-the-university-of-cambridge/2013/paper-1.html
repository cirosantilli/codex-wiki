Use a real, even interaction V−k​=Vk​. Let V=L3 and Nex​=∑k=0​bk†​bk​. In the Bogoliubov theory expansion, keep terms with zero or two noncondensed operators. The zero-momentum term gives V0​N0​(N0​−1)/(2V). The terms with two noncondensed operators give
∑k=0​[VN0​​(V0​+Vk​)bk†​bk​+2VN0​Vk​​(bk†​b−k†​+b−k​bk​)].
(1)
Here V0​ is the direct condensate interaction and Vk​ is its exchange contribution. At fixed total particle number, N0​=N−Nex​, so
2VV0​N02​​=2VV0​N2​−nV0​Nex​+O(Nex2​/V).
(2)
The second term cancels the direct normal term. Adding the kinetic energy yields the number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian
H2​=Emf​+k=0∑​[Ak​bk†​bk​+2Bk​​(bk†​b−k†​+b−k​bk​)],Emf​=2VV0​n2​,Ak​=ϵk​+nVk​,Bk​=nVk​.​
(3)
The constant is the condensate mean-field theory interaction energy; the normal term describes moving a particle out of the condensate, while the anomalous term creates or annihilates an opposite-momentum pair by exchanging two particles with the condensate. Total particle number remains fixed in the underlying theory; the condensate amplitudes in this notation are treated as classical amplitudes.
For condensate fluctuation power counting, put δ=Nex​/N≪1. Formal interaction-amplitude counting gives H2​−Emf​∼Emf​δ, cubic terms ∼Emf​δ3/2 and quartic terms ∼Emf​δ2; these are not uniform bounds on individual modes. Thus the latter are down by δ​ and δ relative to the quadratic interaction terms. The N0​−1 correction is relative 1/N. Homogeneity eliminates terms linear in nonzero-momentum operators. Small depletion and weak interactions, not low temperature alone, justify the truncation. [ref]

For each opposite-momentum pair use the Bogoliubov transformation
bk​=uk​αk​−vk​α−k†​,uk2​−vk2​=1,uk2​+vk2​=Ek​Ak​​,2uk​vk​=Ek​Bk​​.
(4)
Equivalently uk​=coshθk​, vk​=sinhθk​, with tanh(2θk​)=Bk​/Ak​. The identity uk2​−vk2​=1 preserves the canonical commutation relation. Vanishing anomalous terms require Bk​(uk2​+vk2​)−2Ak​uk​vk​=0, and hence the Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion is
Ek​=Ak2​−Bk2​​=ϵk​(ϵk​+2nVk​)​.​
(5)
Substitution and normal ordering give
H2​=Emf​+21​k=0∑​(Ek​−Ak​)+k=0∑​Ek​αk†​αk​.​
(6)
This is the requested form after putting each Ek​/2 inside the oscillator term. By momentum-pair counting in bosonic diagonalization, all sums cover the full nonzero-momentum set; no further factor of two is needed. The bosonic Bogoliubov zero-point shift combines the two vacuum sums before taking a continuum limit.
If Vk​→V0​>0 smoothly, the low-energy excitations are gapless phonons:
Ek​≃ℏc∣k∣,c=nV0​/m​.​
(7)
For contact repulsion, the healing length ξ=ℏ/2mnV0​​ separates this regime from the particle-like regime Ek​≃ϵk​+nV0​. Stability requires ϵk​+2nVk​>0 for every nonzero mode. A general momentum-dependent interaction need not share the contact model's entire curve. An unregularized contact model also makes the vacuum-energy shift ultraviolet divergent; a physical finite-range interaction or scattering-length renormalization is needed for that absolute ground-state energy.

In the Bogoliubov quasiparticle vacuum, αk​∣0α​⟩=0, but the inverse Bogoliubov transformation gives ⟨bk†​bk​⟩=vk2​. Consequently the quantum depletion of a Bose condensate is
n−n0​=V1​k=0∑​vk2​=2V1​k=0∑​(ϵk​(ϵk​+2nVk​)​ϵk​+nVk​​−1).​
(8)
This expression assumes zero temperature. At nonzero temperature, thermal Bogoliubov quasiparticles add to the depletion:
n−n0​=V1​∑k=0​[vk2​+Ek​Ak​​eβEk​−11​].
(9)
Thus the printed formula is the quantum contribution, or a low-temperature approximation when the thermal contribution is negligible. It is nonnegative for a stable quadratic Hamiltonian and vanishes in the noninteracting zero-temperature limit.

A periodic harmonic chain with N masses m0​, spacing a and nearest-neighbour spring constant K has
H=∑j=0N−1​[2m0​pj2​​+2K​(uj+1​−uj​)2],uN​=u0​.
(10)
Hamilton's equations give m0​u¨j​=K(uj+1​+uj−1​−2uj​). The discrete Fourier transform uj​=N−1/2∑k​Qk​eikja, pj​=N−1/2∑k​Pk​eikja, with k=2πl/(Na) in one Brillouin zone, diagonalizes the quadratic form:
ωk​=2K/m0​​∣sin(ka/2)∣,H=k∑​[2m0​Pk​P−k​​+2m0​ωk2​​Qk​Q−k​].​
(11)
The harmonic-chain normal-mode reality condition means Qk†​=Q−k​, not that Qk​ and Q−k​ are independent real coordinates. For canonical quantization, impose [uj​,pl​]=iℏδjl​, equivalently [Qk​,Pk′​]=iℏδk,−k′​. For k=0 set
Qk​=2m0​ωk​ℏ​​(ak​+a−k†​),Pk​=−i2m0​ℏωk​​​(ak​−a−k†​),[ak​,ak′†​]=δkk′​.
(12)
Then the quantum harmonic chain is a set of independent phonon oscillators:
H=2Nm0​Ptot2​​+k=0∑​ℏωk​(ak†​ak​+1/2).​
(13)
The centre-of-mass zero mode of a harmonic chain is the free coordinate X=N−1∑j​uj​, with [X,Ptot​]=iℏ; it cannot be normalized by dividing by ω0​​. Retaining it gives
uj​=X+N​1​∑k=0​2m0​ωk​ℏ​​(ak​eikja+ak†​e−ikja),
(14)
with the corresponding momentum expansion and uniform term Ptot​/N. The nonzero modes alone give the site commutator iℏ(δjl​−1/N); the uniform term supplies the missing 1/N. Each oscillator occupation is a nonnegative integer, and adding one phonon costs ℏωk​ and crystal momentum ℏk modulo a reciprocal-lattice vector. [ref]
In the continuum limit of a harmonic chain, ρ=m0​/a, Y=Ka and
L=21​∫dx[ρu˙2−Y(∂x​u)2],Π=ρu˙,[u(x),Π(x′)]=iℏδ(x−x′),c=Y/ρ​=aK/m0​​.​
(15)
The wave equation and linear low-k dispersion relation follow. The lattice gives an ultraviolet cutoff; the acoustic approximation does not reproduce the zone-edge flattening.
With the centre of mass fixed, the oscillator partition function and Bose-Einstein distribution are
Zosc​=∏k=0​2sinh(βℏωk​/2)1​,⟨ak†​ak​⟩=eβℏωk​−11​.
(16)
For a long chain, above its finite-size level spacing and below the zone-edge scale, integrating the two acoustic propagation directions gives C/(Na)=πkB2​T/(3ℏc). At high temperature each retained oscillator instead contributes kB​ to the heat capacity.

For H=p^​2/(2m)+V(x^), divide elapsed time t into J slices Δt=t/J and apply the Trotter product formula. Inserting complete position and momentum bases gives the regulated phase-space path integral
K(xf​,xi​;t)=limJ→∞​∫∏j=1J−1​dxj​∏j=0J−1​2πℏdpj​​exp{ℏi​∑j=0J−1​[pj​(xj+1​−xj​)−Δt(2mpj2​​+V(xj​))]}.
(17)
The endpoint values are x0​=xi​, xJ​=xf​. Each Gaussian momentum integration in a phase-space path integral can be performed explicitly, leaving the configuration-space path integral
K=∫xi​xf​​DxeiS[x]/ℏ,S[x]=∫0t​ds[mx˙2/2−V(x)].​
(18)
Its measure is the time-sliced limit of (m/(2πiℏΔt))J/2∏j=1J−1​dxj​, with the usual infinitesimal convergence prescription. The integral sums amplitudes, not positive classical probabilities. Operator ordering remains part of the time-slice definition. Expanding a short-time kernel recovers the Schrödinger equation; composing kernels reproduces the time-evolution operator.
In the classical limit, rapid phase cancellation suppresses paths away from stationary action. Varying S with fixed endpoints gives mx¨=−V′(x). The semiclassical result sums the classical-path phases multiplied by fluctuation functional determinants; for a quadratic Hamiltonian the fluctuation integral is Gaussian and this expansion is exact.
For quantum statistical mechanics, continue t=−iτ by a Wick rotation. The Euclidean action and thermal trace give
SE​=∫0βℏ​dτ[m(∂τ​x)2/2+V(x)],Z=Tre−βH=∫x(βℏ)=x(0)​Dxe−SE​/ℏ.​
(19)
The trace closes position-space paths. In a many-particle trace one also sums over endpoint permutations with the appropriate exchange signs. In a coherent state field formulation, bosonic fields are periodic and fermionic coherent states instead give antiperiodic Grassmann fields, hence bosonic and fermionic Matsubara frequencies 2πn/(βℏ) and (2n+1)π/(βℏ). [ref]
The quantum-to-classical imaginary-time mapping views a local d-dimensional quantum problem as a classical-looking field theory in d+1 dimensions, with imaginary-time extent βℏ. A genuine classical probability interpretation additionally requires a nonnegative weight; fermion signs and Berry terms can obstruct it.
For a smooth potential in the high-temperature or small-ℏ limit, imaginary-time paths fluctuate only over the thermal de Broglie wavelength λT​=2πβℏ2/m​. If this is short compared with the potential's variation scale, the diagonal free kernel gives
Z≃2πℏ1​∫dxdpe−β[p2/(2m)+V(x)]=λT​1​∫dxe−βV(x).​
(20)
This classical phase-space limit of the thermal kernel is the classical phase space partition function for one degree of freedom. A dilute identical-particle gas also needs its 1/N! factor; when exchange is appreciable, classical statistics fails. As a check, a single quantum harmonic oscillator gives Z=[2sinh(βℏω/2)]−1→1/(βℏω), precisely the classical phase-space integral.

For conventional superconductivity, an attractive interaction pairs opposite-spin fermions. A complex pairing Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation introduces a Cooper pair field Δ. The fermionic fields then appear quadratically, giving the pair-field effective action of a superconductor
Seff​[Δ,A]=∫g∣Δ∣2​−TrlnG−1[Δ,A]+SEM​,g>0.
(21)
The trace convention counts each independent spin-pair block once. The uniform saddle is the BCS gap equation; the fermionic excitations have Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​ in the fully gapped case. A slow-field expansion near the transition yields Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity. Normalize its pair field Ψ so ∣Ψ∣2 is an effective pair density, with pair mass m∗​ and signed charge q=−2e:
F=∫d3x[a∣Ψ∣2+2b​∣Ψ∣4+2m∗​1​∣(−iℏ∇−qA)Ψ∣2+2μ0​B2​],b>0.
(22)
For a<0, the uniform minimum has ρ0​=∣Ψ∣2=−a/b, with arbitrary constant phase. Writing Ψ=(ρ0​​+h)eiθ separates the amplitude mode h from the phase. The local potential has curvature term 2∣a∣h2 and coherence scale ξ=ℏ/2m∗​∣a∣​. A neutral condensate has a gapless phase Goldstone mode when suitable dynamics is included.
The charged theory is invariant under
θ↦θ+qχ/ℏ,A↦A+∇χ,ϕ↦ϕ−∂t​χ.
(23)
The gauge-invariant condensate momentum is ℏ∇θ−qA. Only ℏ∇θ−qA and ℏθ˙+qϕ enter physical phase dynamics. At nearly constant amplitude, varying F gives
j=m∗​qρ0​​(ℏ∇θ−qA),λ−2=m∗​μ0​q2ρ0​​.​
(24)
For a smooth phase, taking the curl and using Ampère's law gives ∇2B=B/λ2: the Meissner effect screens a static bulk magnetic field over the London penetration depth λ. In a gauge with θ=0, the phase stiffness is a quadratic term in A, the superconducting version of the Higgs mechanism.
To identify the dynamical mode, a simple lossless long-wave superconducting phase-density action is
L=2χp​​(ℏθ˙+qϕ)2−2m∗​ρ0​​(ℏ∇θ−qA)2+2ϵ0​E2​−2μ0​B2​,χp​>0.
(25)
Here χp​ is the pair compressibility coefficient. Eliminating the longitudinal electric potential gives
ω2=ωp2​+cs2​k2,ωp2​=ϵ0​m∗​q2ρ0​​,cs2​=m∗​χp​ρ0​​.​
(26)
Thus the neutral sound mode becomes a gapped longitudinal plasma oscillation; transverse electromagnetic waves acquire ω2=cvac2​k2+cvac2​/λ2 in this simple bulk model. This redistribution of the phase degree of freedom into electromagnetic collective modes is the Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor. Local gauge symmetry is a redundancy, not an independently observable symmetry whose breaking creates another massless particle. [ref]
The static functional alone does not determine a frequency or damping rate. The displayed mode dispersions require the added dynamical assumptions; real screening, normal carriers, boundaries and dissipation modify them. The amplitude fluctuation is distinct from the phase mode and is not removed by the gauge choice.

Use ℏ=kB​=1 and dimensionless spin operators. At one site, the canonical anticommutation relations imply the on-site spin-square normal-ordering identity
Sn2​=43​(nn​−2nn↑​nn↓​),:Sn2​:=Sn2​−43​nn​=−23​nn↑​nn↓​.
(27)
Indeed the empty and doubly occupied states have spin zero, while either singly occupied state has S2=3/4. Thus the interaction in normal-ordered form is 3U∑n​cn↑†​cn↓†​cn↓​cn↑​. There is no residual 3Un/2 chemical-potential shift: that contraction has cancelled.
Insert fermionic coherent state resolutions between imaginary-time evolution factors. With ξk​=ϵk​−μ and Dn​=ψˉ​n↑​ψˉ​n↓​ψn↓​ψn↑​, the grand-canonical coherent-state action is
Z=∫AP​D(ψˉ​,ψ)e−S0​−3U∫0β​dτ∑n​Dn​,S0​=∫0β​dτkσ∑​ψˉ​kσ​(∂τ​+ξk​)ψkσ​.​
(28)
The independent barred and unbarred Grassmann variables satisfy antiperiodic boundary conditions at 0,β. Adjacent time-slice labels in the normal-ordered symbol fix the continuum prescription and thermal normalization. The interaction coefficient is 3U, not the U of a differently normalized Hubbard model.

For the uniform mean-field approximation field M=Me^z​, the inverse fermion propagator is diagonal in momentum, frequency and spin: −iωl​+ξk​−σM. The Grassmann Gaussian integral therefore gives
Zuniform​=∫dμ(M)e−βNM2/(2U)∏k,σ=±1​[1+e−β(ξk​−σM)].
(34)
Here dμ(M) denotes the residual uniform-field measure; integration over its orientation only affects a nonextensive prefactor. Hence
Seff​(M)=2UβNM2​−k,σ=±1∑​ln[1+e−β(ϵk​−μ−σM)].​
(35)
The exchange field separates the two spin bands by 2M. Integrating out the fermions is exact for a specified auxiliary field; restricting that field to be constant is the mean-field approximation.

0=β1​∂M∂Seff​​=UNM​−∑k,σ​σnF​(ϵk​−σM).
(36)

ν(ϵ)=N2​∑k​δ(ϵ−ϵk​),N1​∑k​f(ϵk​)=21​∫dϵν(ϵ)f(ϵ).

The constant term in the spin sum cancels. Linearizing the saddle point equation gives
M=UMχ0​+O(M3),χ0​=∫dϵν(ϵ)[−∂ϵ​nF​(ϵ)].
(39)
The paramagnetic auxiliary-field curvature is
βNSeff​(M)−Seff​(0)​=21​(U−1−χ0​)M2+O(M4).
(40)
Consequently the Stoner criterion for the loss of local paramagnetic stability is
U>Uc​,Uc​χ0​=1,Uc​ν(μ)≃1(T→0).​
(41)
The printed ν(0) uses energy measured from the Fermi level. For a smooth density of states, −nF′​ becomes a narrow unit-area peak at μ; a singular density of states or appreciable temperature requires the integral form. The curvature establishes local instability, not the order or location of every possible thermodynamic transition.
With exact spin-rotation symmetry, itinerant ferromagnetism selects a magnetization direction. The two broken transverse generators form one quadratic type-B Goldstone boson, a long-wave ferromagnetic magnon with Eq​∼Dq2. Longitudinal amplitude motion is not symmetry-protected to be gapless. In a metallic ordered state, particle-hole excitations remain near each surviving exchange-split Fermi surface; these are distinct from the collective magnon. The itinerant spin-flip particle-hole continuum starts at exchange scale 2∣M∣ at q=0 in this simple mean-field spectrum, while its finite-q boundaries differ. Spin anisotropy, spin-orbit coupling or an external field can gap the transverse mode; none is present in the stated model.
