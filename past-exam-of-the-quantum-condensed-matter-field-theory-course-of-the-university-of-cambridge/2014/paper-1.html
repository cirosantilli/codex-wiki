Use the Holstein–Primakoff transformation only on the physical occupation sector 0≤n≤2S, with n=a†a and S−=a†2S−n​. Acting on number states gives
S+∣n⟩=n(2S−n+1)​∣n−1⟩,S−∣n⟩=(n+1)(2S−n)​∣n+1⟩.
(1)
Consequently S+S−=(n+1)(2S−n) and S−S+=n(2S−n+1), so
[S+,S−]=2(S−n)=2Sz,[Sz,S±]=±S±.​
(2)
The endpoint factors keep the spin operators inside the physical sector. The square-root ordering matters; replacing it by an unrestricted linear oscillator would not give the exact finite-spin algebra.

a OurBigBook.com
For the even periodic chain, every classical Néel state Sm​=(−1)mSn has energy −JNS2, with arbitrary unit vector n. Thus there is a continuous sphere of classical orientations, not merely two Ising-like states. Opposite staggered directions are connected by a global spin rotation.
A quantum Néel product is not an eigenstate: transverse exchange flips opposite neighbouring spins. The ground state therefore contains quantum superpositions and zero-point spin waves, reducing the classical staggered moment. A finite isotropic balanced chain has a symmetry-invariant ground state; a symmetry-broken reference is an approximation for analysing fluctuations, not its exact finite-system eigenstate.

The inverse transformation has negative off-diagonal entries. Choosing tanh2θk​=γk​ removes anomalous terms and gives
ωk​=2JS1−γk2​​=2JS∣sink∣,H=−JNS(S+1)+k∑​ωk​(αk†​αk​+1/2)+O(S0).​
(6)
There are gapless points at k=0 and the zone boundary; near zero, ωk​≃2JS∣k∣, a linear antiferromagnetic spin wave with velocity 2JS in the stated units. The zero modes are collective rotations: their divergent squeezing cannot be treated as ordinary normalized independent oscillator vacua. A finite-size cutoff or infinitesimal symmetry-breaking field regulates them.
As a constant-term check, the continuum spin-wave vacuum energy per site is −JS(S+1)+(2/π)JS. This is a spin-wave prediction, not an exact spectrum for every finite-S one-dimensional chain.

Choose the reference staggered direction so its classical moment is positive. The Bogoliubov vacuum has ⟨ak†​ak​⟩=sinh2θk​, hence
NMs​​=S−δS,δS=2N1​k∑​[1−γk2​​1​−1].​
(7)
Near each soft point the integrand is proportional to 1/∣k∣, so the one-dimensional thermodynamic integral diverges logarithmically. The ordered-reference expansion fails; it does not predict a physical negative magnetization. Long-range Néel order is destroyed in this isotropic one-dimensional setting. The exact finite symmetric ground state also has zero staggered expectation without selecting a direction.
For linearly dispersing modes in d dimensions, zero-temperature depletion behaves as ∫0​dkkd−2 and is infrared finite for d>1, allowing order in suitable two- and three-dimensional antiferromagnets. Finite depletion is not a guarantee for every frustrated quantum magnet. At nonzero temperature, the extra Bose occupation changes the singularity to ∫dkkd−3: short-range isotropic systems cannot sustain continuous-symmetry long-range order for d≤2. This thermal conclusion is distinct from the one-dimensional zero-point argument.

The real-time path integral sums eiS/ℏ over paths. In a classically forbidden region, the dominant semiclassical contribution is clearer after Wick rotation: the weight becomes e−SE​/ℏ and stationary paths obey mq¨​=V′(q), equivalent to motion in the inverted potential. A finite-action path joining degenerate minima is an instanton.
For V(q)=λ(q2−a2)2, λ>0, zero Euclidean mechanical energy gives mq˙​2/2=V. Thus
qI​(τE​)=atanh[2ω0​(τE​−τ0​)​],ω02​=8λa2/m,S0​=∫−aa​2mV(q)​dq=34​a32mλ​.​
(16)
The reversed trajectory is an anti-instanton.[ref] Expanding q=qI​+η produces the fluctuation operator −∂τE​2​+V′′(qI​)/m. Its translation zero mode is handled by integrating the collective centre τ0​, not by dividing by a zero determinant. The remaining determinant and Jacobian form an attempt-frequency prefactor A.
When S0​/ℏ≫1, well-separated events form a dilute gas with rate scale κ=Ae−S0​/ℏ. Paths returning to the same well have an even number of events, while paths ending in the other well have an odd number. The sums yield cosh(κT) and sinh(κT), giving
Eodd​−Eeven​≃2ℏκ.​
(17)
The ground state is the lower symmetric combination of localized well states: tunnelling lifts the apparent classical degeneracy. This splitting is nonperturbative in ℏ, beyond a power series about either minimum. In a metastable well a bounce, with a negative fluctuation mode, describes decay rather than the stable doublet splitting. Finite temperature, overlapping events or a low barrier require improvements beyond the dilute approximation.

b OurBigBook.com
A coherent state is an eigenstate of an annihilation operator. Bosonic unnormalized states have exponential overlap and the Gaussian resolution of identity used above; their overcompleteness supplies a phase-space integration measure, not an orthonormal state count. For a normal-ordered many-body Hamiltonian, coherent-state insertion gives the bosonic coherent-state path integral
Z=∫periodic​D(ϕˉ​,ϕ)exp[−∫0β​dτ(∑i​ϕˉ​i​∂τ​ϕi​+HN​(ϕˉ​,ϕ)−μ∑i​ϕˉ​i​ϕi​)].
(18)
Constants in HN​ must be retained. The exact time-sliced prescription places creation and annihilation symbols on adjacent slices; discarding that prescription can change ordering corrections, as the oscillator example demonstrates. The derivative term is first order because the fields describe phase space.
Fermionic coherent states instead use Grassmann variables. Their Gaussian identity is ∫dψˉ​dψe−ψˉ​Aψ=detA, whereas complex bosons give (detA)−1. Anticommutation in the trace produces antiperiodic fields. Accordingly bosons have νn​=2πn/β, fermions νn​=(2n+1)π/β, in these inverse-energy time units.
For one mode of grand-canonical energy ξ, the products give ZB​=(1−e−βξ)−1 and ZF​=1+e−βξ; convergence requires ξ>0 for the bosonic Gaussian. Correlation functions follow by source differentiation. Quartic interactions can be treated perturbatively or decoupled with a Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation; a saddle then describes an order parameter and quadratic fluctuations its collective modes. Such saddles require stability and an appropriate control parameter; the functional notation alone is not an exact solution of an interacting problem.

For a dilute three-dimensional weakly interacting Bose gas with repulsive short-range scattering, use a contact coupling g=4πℏ2as​/m>0 and nas3​≪1. In imaginary time τE​, the grand-canonical action is
SE​=∫0βℏ​dτE​∫d3x[ψˉ​ℏ∂τE​​ψ+2mℏ2​∣∇ψ∣2−μ∣ψ∣2+2g​∣ψ∣4],Z=∫Dψe−SE​/ℏ.
(19)
The uniform saddle has n0​=μ/g. Its real-time equation is the Gross–Pitaevskii equation; quantum depletion means condensate and total density need not coincide exactly.
Writing ψ=n0​​+δψ and retaining quadratic fluctuations gives a Nambu kernel with diagonal ϵk​+gn0​, anomalous coupling gn0​ and ϵk​=ℏ2k2/(2m). The Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion is
Ek​=ϵk​(ϵk​+2gn0​)​,Ek​≃ℏcs​∣k∣,cs​=gn0​/m​.​
(20)
Phase and density fluctuations form the gapless sound mode associated with broken global U(1) symmetry. At large momentum the spectrum crosses towards particle behaviour; the healing length is ξ=ℏ/2mgn0​​. The depletion integral ∫d3kvk2​/(2π)3 is finite in three dimensions and gives (n−n0​)/n≃8nas3​​/(3π​) to leading order. The contact coupling denotes a scattering-length-renormalized interaction; naive ultraviolet zero-point integrals require renormalization.
The condensate phase gives superfluid velocity vs​=(ℏ/m)∇θ and quantized circulation ∮vs​⋅dl=2πℏℓ/m. The Landau criterion, vc​=mink​Ek​/(ℏk), gives cs​ for this homogeneous Bogoliubov spectrum. Boundaries and vortex nucleation can cause dissipation below that value, so it is not a universal measured critical speed. Condensation and superfluid stiffness are related but distinct; dimensional fluctuations can remove true condensate order while retaining superfluid responses.
