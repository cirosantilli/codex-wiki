If g is nilpotent, every Lie subalgebra a is nilpotent, since aj⊆gj in the respective lower central series of a Lie algebra. A two-dimensional nilpotent algebra has nonzero centre by part (i). If 0=z is central, complete it to a basis (z,u); every bracket of these basis elements is zero. Thus a is abelian.
Conversely, suppose g is not nilpotent. By Engel's theorem, some ad(x) is not nilpotent. Since the field is algebraically closed, it has an eigenvalue λ=0 with eigenvector y=0. Thus
[x,y]=λy.
(4)
The vectors x,y are independent: otherwise their bracket would be zero. Their span is therefore a two-dimensional Lie subalgebra and it is nonabelian. This contradicts the hypothesis. Hence
g is nilpotent⟺every two-dimensional subalgebra is abelian.​
(5)
