Fix x with xN=0 as a matrix. On the full matrix algebra define Lx​(y)=xy and Rx​(y)=yx. These endomorphisms commute and each has Nth power zero. The Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra is ad(x)=Lx​−Rx​. The binomial theorem gives
(adx)m(y)=∑j=0m​(−1)j(jm​)xm−jyxj.
(2)
For m=2N−1, each summand has either j≥N or m−j≥N, and hence vanishes. Therefore ad(x) is nilpotent on the full matrix algebra and on its invariant subspace g.
Engel's theorem, in its adjoint form, states that a finite-dimensional Lie algebra is nilpotent if every ad(x) is a nilpotent endomorphism. Applying it to all x∈g proves
g is nilpotent.​
(3)
This is distinct from assuming that an arbitrary representation of a nilpotent algebra must act by nilpotent matrices, which is false.
