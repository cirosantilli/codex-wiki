Law of the unconscious statistician (E[g(X)]=∫g(x)dμX​(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Expected value
OurBigBook.com Words: 105
The law of the unconscious statistician computes the expected value of a measurable function of a random variable directly from the original probability distribution. If g(X) is an integrable random variable, then
E[g(X)]=∫R​g(x)dμX​(x).
(3)
Use ∑x​g(x)pX​(x) for a probability mass function, or ∫R​g(x)fX​(x)dx for a probability density function. There is no need to derive the probability distribution of g(X) first. The identity is the integration rule for a pushforward measure, and also holds with an infinite value when g is nonnegative. [ref].
For X taking −1,2,8 with probabilities 1/2,3/10,1/5, choosing g(x)=x2 gives E[X2]=21​+4103​+6451​=14.5. Its variance is therefore 14.5−1.72=11.61. Squaring the expected value would instead give 2.89, because squaring does not commute with averaging.
