Reaction intermediate

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Reaction mechanism
OurBigBook.com Words: 163 Articles: 1
A reaction intermediate is formed during a reaction mechanism and consumed in a subsequent step, cancelling from the net chemical equation. It can accumulate, as I does in the consecutive first-order reaction mechanism. A reaction intermediate can also connect transformations in energy coupling. Unlike a catalyst, it need not be present before conversion begins.[ref]
A reaction intermediate is distinct from a transition state: the former can persist between steps, whereas the latter describes passage through a barrier. The existing potential energy surface and energy profile provide the configuration-space picture; the time-dependent concentration of an intermediate instead measures an ensemble of reacting entities. Low concentration or short lifetime can make detection difficult, so failure to detect a proposed reaction intermediate is not by itself proof of absence. Conversely, detecting a chemical species does not establish that it carries the proposed route to the final reaction products; it could belong to a competing route.
