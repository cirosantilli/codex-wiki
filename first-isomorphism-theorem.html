First isomorphism theorem (G/kerφ≅imφ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
For a group homomorphism φ:G→H, the group isomorphism gkerφ↦φ(g) gives
G/kerφ≅imφ.
(1)
The group kernel records which inputs become indistinguishable, and the image records which outputs actually occur. The first isomorphism theorem says that identifying precisely those indistinguishable inputs gives a group isomorphic to the image. It is one of the general isomorphism theorems. [ref].
For example, reduction modulo three gives a surjective group homomorphism from the additive cyclic group Z/6Z to Z/3Z. Its group kernel is {0,3}. The three cosets {0,3}, {1,4}, and {2,5} become the three outputs zero, one, and two, respectively. Thus
(Z/6Z)/{0,3}≅Z/3Z.
(2)
The quotient group diagram shows both the identification and the resulting operation. More generally, fibres of a group homomorphism are cosets of its kernel explains why this map is well-defined and injective; it is surjective onto the image by definition.
