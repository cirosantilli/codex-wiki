Proof of Korselt criterion

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Carmichael number Korselt criterion
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
For sufficiency, let N be composite and squarefree, with p−1∣N−1 for every prime factor p. For any integer a coprime to N, Fermat's little theorem gives aN−1≡1(modp) for every such p. Their product is N, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives the required congruence modulo N.
For necessity, first rule out a repeated factor. If N=pkm with k≥2 and gcd(p,m)=1, the Chinese remainder theorem supplies a≡1+p(modpk) and a≡1(modm). This a is coprime to N. But the binomial theorem gives
aN−1≡(1+p)N−1≡1+(N−1)p≡1−p≡1(modp2),
(80)
contradicting the Carmichael number condition. Hence N is squarefree.
Now choose a primitive root g modulo a prime factor p. Lift it to a unit modulo N by prescribing a≡g(modp) and a≡1(modN/p). The Chinese remainder theorem applies because N is squarefree. The Carmichael congruence implies gN−1≡1(modp). Since g has multiplicative order p−1, that order divides N−1. This proves the other condition.[ref]
