Leibniz integral rule (dxd​∫a(x)b(x)​f(x,t)dt=f(x,b)b′−f(x,a)a′+∫ab​fx​(x,t)dt)

For differentiable endpoints a,b and a function f with suitable continuous partial derivative in the parameter, the moving-endpoint rule is
dxd​∫a(x)b(x)​f(x,t)dt=f(x,b(x))b′(x)−f(x,a(x))a′(x)+∫a(x)b(x)​fx​(x,t)dt.​
(91)
To prove it locally, put H(x,u)=∫cu​f(x,t)dt for a fixed reference endpoint c. The original integral is H(x,b(x))−H(x,a(x)). Apply the chain rule, use the fundamental theorem of calculus for Hu​=f, and use differentiation under the integral sign for Hx​. The two boundary terms account for the moving endpoints, while the remaining integral accounts for variation of the integrand with the parameter. For improper integrals, convergence and a suitable domination condition must be verified before interchanging the derivative and integral.
