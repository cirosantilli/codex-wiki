For K=Q(ζ), every Galois automorphism sends the primitive root ζ to another primitive mth root of unity, uniquely ζa for a∈(Z/mZ)×. The Cyclotomic character of a finite cyclotomic extension is
χm​:Gal(K/Q)⟶(Z/mZ)×,σ(ζ)=ζχm​(σ)​.
(1)
If χm​(σ)=a and χm​(τ)=b, then (στ)(ζ)=σ(ζb)=ζab, proving the group homomorphism property. Its kernel is trivial: an automorphism fixing ζ fixes the field it generates. Thus the character is injective. Here its domain is the finite cyclotomic Galois group; the corresponding map from an absolute Galois group need not be injective.

The element ζ satisfies T2−ηT+1=0, so [Q(ζ):Q(η)]≤2. Since η=ζ+ζ​ is real, Q(η)⊆R. A primitive root of order m≥3 is nonreal: the only real roots of unity are ±1, of orders at most two. Hence ζ∈/Q(η) and

Equivalently, nontrivial complex conjugation fixes precisely the maximal real subfield of a cyclotomic field in this degree-two extension.

Irreducibility of the cyclotomic polynomial gives [K:Q]=φ(15)=8. The extension is Galois, since K is the splitting field of T15−1 in characteristic zero. The injection in part (a) is consequently an isomorphism, and the Chinese remainder theorem gives
Gal(K/Q)≅(Z/15Z)×≅C2​×C4​.
(3)
The Fundamental theorem of Galois theory identifies proper intermediate fields strictly above Q with proper nontrivial subgroups. Such subgroups have order two or four. The two-torsion subgroup is C2​×C2​, with three nonidentity elements and hence three order-two subgroups. There are also three order-four subgroups: they are the kernels of the three nonzero group homomorphisms C2​×C4​→C2​. Distinct such maps have distinct kernels because the target has only one nonidentity element. Therefore
three quadratic subfields and three quartic subfields, six in total.​
(4)

ii OurBigBook.com
Put ω=ζ5, τ=ζ3, a=1+2ω, and b=1+2(τ+τ−1). The primitive cubic and quintic root identities give a2=−3, b2=5. These elements therefore provide the following six generators:
  • Q(a)=Q(−3​), degree two.
  • Q(b)=Q(5​), degree two.
  • Q(ab)=Q(−15​), degree two.
  • Q(τ), degree four.
  • Q(a+b)=Q(−3​,5​), degree four.
  • Q(ζ+ζ−1), degree four.
The three quadratic fields are distinct because −3,5,−15 are distinct rational square classes. To justify the fifth entry, the two quadratic fields Q(a) and Q(b) are distinct, so their compositum has degree four. Moreover a+b=0 and
(a+b)(b−a)=8,a=2a+b−8/(a+b)​,b=2a+b+8/(a+b)​.
(5)
Thus their sum alone generates the compositum.
The field Q(τ) has degree four by the quintic cyclotomic polynomial. Its Galois group is C4​ and its unique quadratic subfield is Q(b), so it cannot contain a. It is therefore different from Q(a,b). The final field has degree four by part (b) and is real; the preceding two quartic fields contain nonreal τ and a, respectively. Thus all three quartics are distinct, and the count in part (i) proves the list exhaustive. This gives the subfields of the fifteenth cyclotomic field without relying on a choice of square-root signs.

For the root-discriminant convention in the requested identity, define
Δf​=∏i<j​(αi​−αj​)2.
(7)
The Vandermonde determinant is detV(α)=∏i<j​(αj​−αi​). Squaring removes the signs, proving
Δf​=(detV(α))2​.
(8)
It is nonzero because the roots are distinct. It lies in F, since its root expression is fixed by every Galois automorphism and KG=F.
If f is not monic and has leading coefficient c, the usual polynomial discriminant is c2n−2∏i<j​(αi​−αj​)2. Thus the printed Vandermonde equality uses the discriminant of the monic normalization f/c. The extra factor is itself a nonzero square in F, so it does not alter the square-class criterion in the next part.

First assume charF=2. Set d=detV(α)=0. If Δf​=b2 with b∈F, then (d−b)(d+b)=0 in the field K, so d=±b∈F. For an automorphism inducing the root permutation π,
σ(d)=sgn(π)d.
(9)
Since σ fixes d and 1=−1 in F, every induced permutation has positive sign. The root action is faithful because the roots generate K, giving
G↪An​(charF=2)​.
(10)
This is the discriminant criterion for an alternating Galois group.
As printed for arbitrary fields, the assertion is false in characteristic two. Over F2​, the polynomial T2+T+1 is irreducible and separable, with roots α,α+1 in F4​. Their difference is one, so the discriminant is the square 1. Nevertheless its Galois group is C2​, whereas A2​ is trivial. In characteristic two the permutation signs become indistinguishable as field elements; square discriminants in characteristic two therefore cannot detect the alternating subgroup in this way.

a OurBigBook.com
A nonconstant polynomial is separable when it has distinct roots in a splitting field, equivalently when it is coprime to its formal derivative. For such f, Bézout's identity for polynomials gives a,b∈F[T] with af+bf′=1. This identity remains valid over any extension E/F. A repeated root γ∈E would satisfy f(γ)=f′(γ)=0, giving the contradiction 1=0. Thus there are no repeated roots in any field extension. The argument in fact applies to every separable polynomial, whether irreducible or not.

Let E=F(αp)⊆F(α). If α is a separable algebraic element over F, its minimal polynomial over E divides the original separable minimal polynomial. It also divides Tp−αp=(T−α)p, which has only one distinct root in any extension. Being separable with only one distinct root, that minimal polynomial has degree one. Hence α∈E and F(α)=F(αp).
Conversely, if the degree-n minimal polynomial m over F is inseparable, part (b) writes it as m(T)=g(Tp) with degg=n/p. Then g(αp)=0, so
[F(αp):F]≤n/p<n=[F(α):F].
(16)
The fields cannot be equal. This proves the equivalence
α separable over F⟺F(α)=F(αp)​,
(17)
the separability criterion using a pth power.

Put γ=α+β, E=F(γ) and E0​=F(γp). By the preceding separability criterion using a pth power, it suffices to prove E=E0​.
Since α is separable, α∈F(αp). But αp=γp−βp∈E0​(β), so α∈E0​(β). Consequently
M:=F(α,β)=E(β)=E0​(β).
(18)
Let h and m be the monic minimal polynomials of β over E0​ and E, respectively. Both divide its separable minimal polynomial over F, so both are separable. Also m∣h in E[T].
Every c∈E=F(γ) has cp∈F(γp)=E0​. Hence m(T)p has coefficients in E0​ and vanishes at β, forcing h∣mp in E0​[T]. Since h remains a square-free polynomial over E, this divisibility implies h∣m in E[T]. Together with m∣h, monicity gives h=m. Therefore
[M:E0​]=degh=degm=[M:E].
(19)
The tower law makes [E:E0​]=1. Part (c) now proves that γ=α+β is separable over F. This establishes closure of separable algebraic elements under addition without presupposing that closure as a field theorem.
