A nonconstant polynomial is separable when it has distinct roots in a splitting field, equivalently when it is coprime to its formal derivative. For such f, Bézout's identity for polynomials gives a,b∈F[T] with af+bf′=1. This identity remains valid over any extension E/F. A repeated root γ∈E would satisfy f(γ)=f′(γ)=0, giving the contradiction 1=0. Thus there are no repeated roots in any field extension. The argument in fact applies to every separable polynomial, whether irreducible or not.
