The natural logarithm has the parameter-integral representation
∫01​lnttx−1​dt=ln(1+x),x>−1.​
(27)
For 0<t<1, the identity (tx−1)/lnt=∫0x​tsds shows convergence: the absolute value is at most ∣x∣tmin(x,0), which is integrable when x>−1. At t=1, the apparent singularity is removable, with limiting value x.
Write the left side as I(x). On a compact parameter interval c≤x≤d with c>−1, its differentiated integrand tx is bounded by the integrable function tc. The dominated convergence theorem justifies differentiation under the integral sign, giving I′(x)=∫01​txdt=1/(1+x). Since I(0)=0, the fundamental theorem of calculus gives I(x)=ln(1+x). At x=−1, the original improper integral diverges as −∫0​dt/[t∣lnt∣], and it also diverges for x<−1.
