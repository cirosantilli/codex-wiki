Exceedance equivalence for increasing thresholds (limsupn​{maxk≤n​Xk​>an​}=limsupn​{Xn​>an​})

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability space Event Limsup of events
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
Let (an​) be a nondecreasing real sequence tending to +∞, and let (xn​) be any real sequence. The running maximum exceeds an​ infinitely often exactly when xn​>an​ infinitely often. Applying this pathwise to random variables gives the displayed event equality, without any independence assumption.
One implication follows from xn​≤maxk≤n​xk​. For the other, if xn​≤an​ from some N onwards, every later value with N≤k≤n satisfies xk​≤ak​≤an​. The finite prefix also lies below an​ for all sufficiently large n, because an​→∞. Therefore the entire running maximum eventually stays below the threshold.
Divergence of the thresholds prevents one large early observation from creating infinitely many crossings. Monotonicity ensures that a later running-maximum crossing is controlled by the corresponding earlier individual threshold. These two conditions explain why the resulting event belongs to the tail sigma-algebra when the thresholds are deterministic.
