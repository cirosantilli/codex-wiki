In weak-coupling conventional superconductivity, a useful guide is kB​Tc​∼ℏωph​exp[−1/λeff​]: an appreciable phonon scale and an effective attraction after Coulomb effects are favorable. A large density of states at the Fermi level can strengthen pairing, while light constituent masses can raise relevant phonon frequencies. Increasing electron–phonon coupling without limit is not a general strategy: lattice instabilities, competing order and changes to the phonon spectrum constrain it, and stronger coupling requires treatment beyond the simple exponential expression.
For unconventional high-temperature superconductivity, strong electronic interactions and magnetic fluctuations can support sign-changing pairing, as in d-wave superconductivity. Layered structures and proximity to magnetic order may enhance useful correlations, but can also promote competing insulating or density-wave states. Sufficient mobile-carrier phase stiffness is needed to turn pairing into coherent superconductivity; strong pairing alone does not guarantee a large transition temperature. Disorder that is relatively harmless to an isotropic conventional pair state can be pair-breaking for sign-changing gaps. There is no universal recipe established by these qualitative conditions: the pairing interaction, symmetry, carrier density, competing phases and phase coherence must all be considered.
