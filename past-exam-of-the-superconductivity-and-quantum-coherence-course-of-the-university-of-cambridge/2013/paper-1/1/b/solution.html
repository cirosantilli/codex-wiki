In BCS theory, pairing mixes an electron state with a hole in its opposite-momentum partner. For a uniform spin-singlet gap, the uniform Bogoliubov--de Gennes Hamiltonian gives the momentum-pair eigenproblem
(ξk​Δ∗​Δ−ξk​​)(vk​uk​​)=Ek​(vk​uk​​),Ek​=ξk2​+∣Δ∣2​.​
(1)
Here ξk​=εk​−μ. A fermionic Bogoliubov quasiparticle is a coherent particle–hole combination, with
∣uk​∣2=21​(1+Ek​ξk​​),∣vk​∣2=21​(1−Ek​ξk​​).
(2)
Far above the Fermi surface the excitation is mostly particle-like; far below it is mostly hole-like; at ξk​=0 the two weights agree. The transformation preserves the fermionic canonical anticommutation relations. Its mean-field particle-number mixing does not mean that the full physical theory violates charge conservation.
For a fully gapped isotropic state, the minimum single-quasiparticle energy is ∣Δ∣, while creating a pair of quasiparticles costs at least 2∣Δ∣. This produces suppressed low-temperature thermal populations, an activated electronic heat capacity and a gap feature in superconducting tunneling. Gap nodes in an unconventional state instead allow low-energy excitations, changing the temperature dependence. These fermionic excitations are distinct from collective phase and amplitude oscillations of the paired condensate.
