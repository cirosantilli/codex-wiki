They are coupled components of one positive-frequency Bogoliubov mode, not two independent excitations that happen to have equal energies. At k=0, a constant phase change is a zero mode; the nonzero-k elimination should not be used to assign an independent oscillation to a uniform density change.

In the complex plane, an amplitude oscillation moves radially relative to the chosen equilibrium ψ=1, and a phase oscillation moves tangentially, or along a short unit-circle arc beyond linear order. A first-order coupled mode combines them in quadrature. For a fixed spatial factor and k=0, one possible parametrization is

A weak neutral Bose condensate realizes the first-order structure and its sound-like Bogoliubov spectrum. A particle–hole-symmetric effective complex field can realize the second-order structure, with a gapless Goldstone mode and a distinct amplitude mode. A neutral BCS superfluid likewise has collective phase and amplitude responses, although the amplitude response near pair-breaking energy need not be a sharp undamped particle; its fermionic quasiparticles are separate excitations.
In a charged bulk superconductor, long-range Coulomb interactions and electromagnetic dynamics remove the neutral sound-like phase branch from the low-frequency spectrum. The Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor produces a longitudinal plasma mode, schematically ω2≃ωp2​+cs2​k2 in an appropriate long-wavelength model. The amplitude scale is typically associated with 2Δ/ℏ in a simple BCS regime, with damping and mixing dependent on material and symmetry. One should not simply retain the neutral Goldstone dispersion after charging the field, nor identify the static Ginzburg-Landau functional alone with a complete time-dependent microscopic theory.
