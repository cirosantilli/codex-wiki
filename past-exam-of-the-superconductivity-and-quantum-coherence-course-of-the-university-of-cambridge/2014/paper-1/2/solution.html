The electron-phonon interaction lets one electron polarize the ionic lattice and another interact with that delayed deformation. Phonon-mediated electron attraction acts over energy transfers below a characteristic phonon scale. Pairing additionally requires a net attractive interaction in some allowed pairing channel after screened Coulomb repulsion is included. In the conventional weak-coupling situation, retardation separates the low phonon scale from the much larger electronic scale, reducing the effective repulsion. The Cooper instability then makes a Fermi surface unstable to an arbitrarily weak net attraction in the idealized channel; finite temperature cuts off its logarithmic enhancement.
In BCS theory, a momentum-independent pairing amplitude within the cutoff obeys a self-consistency equation containing tanh(E/2kB​T)/(2E), with quasiparticle energy E=ξ2+∣Δ∣2​. The apparent low-density excess should not be read as a reliable transition above the ideal condensation scale of preformed pairs. On this extrapolation kB​Tc​/μ=au−1/3 grows without bound, violating the degenerate-gas and large-lower-cutoff approximations. It reaches one already near 7.5×1021m−3; well before using an arbitrarily small density, the chemical potential, pairing, density of states and pair fluctuations need a self-consistent treatment. The asymptotic formula and ideal-boson curve compare two stipulated models, rather than solving the full BCS-BEC crossover.
