A complex order parameter captures both the amplitude and phase rigidity of a superfluid. A local isotropic Ginzburg-Landau theory writes its free-energy functional, to fourth order in amplitude and second order in gradients, as
F[Ψ]=∫d3r[α∣Ψ∣2+2β​∣Ψ∣4+K∣∇Ψ∣2],β,K>0.
(19)
The uniform minimum has Ψ=0 for α>0 and ∣Ψ0​∣2=−α/β for α<0. Its phase remains arbitrary under a global U(1) transformation. Minimizing with respect to Ψ∗ gives −K∇2Ψ+αΨ+β∣Ψ∣2Ψ=0. The phenomenological description specifies the allowed structures and coefficients; microscopic input is needed to identify the normalization of Ψ and calculate those coefficients. A static functional by itself does not fix the dynamical equation or mode frequencies.
For a dilute three-dimensional Bose gas with short-range repulsion and particle mass mB​, the microscopic low-energy Hamiltonian is
H=∫d3r[2mB​ℏ2​∇ψ​†⋅∇ψ​+Vext​ψ​†ψ​+2gB​​ψ​†ψ​†ψ​ψ​],gB​=mB​4πℏ2as​​>0.
(20)
The interaction parameter is expressed through the low-energy s-wave scattering length as​, rather than an unrenormalized bare delta potential. In a symmetry-selected description, Ψ=⟨ψ​⟩ and ∣Ψ∣2=n0​ is condensate density. Equivalently, fixed-number off-diagonal long-range order identifies the macroscopically occupied natural orbital and its amplitude without a nonzero one-point expectation.
Evaluating H−μN in a coherent-field condensate variational state gives the Ginzburg-Landau functional of a dilute Bose gas, with the mean-field microscopic identifications
K=2mB​ℏ2​,α=Vext​−μ,β=gB​.​
(21)
Its stationary variational equation is the Gross–Pitaevskii equation. The time-dependent variational action additionally contains iℏΨ∗∂t​Ψ, giving
iℏ∂t​Ψ=[−2mB​ℏ2∇2​+Vext​+gB​∣Ψ∣2]Ψ.
(22)
This term expresses density-phase conjugacy. It is the missing microscopic dynamical input; a purely dissipative relaxation law would describe a different approximation.
Bogoliubov theory writes ψ​=Ψ+δψ​ and retains quadratic fluctuations. In a uniform condensate, μ=gB​n0​ and the fluctuation matrix is
(ϵk​+gB​n0​−gB​n0​​gB​n0​−ϵk​−gB​n0​​).
(23)
Its eigenvalues are ±ϵk​(ϵk​+2gB​n0​)​. The positive branch is the gapless sound mode, continuously crossing over to particle-like excitations at larger k. The same result follows by linearizing the time-dependent Gross–Pitaevskii equation about its uniform solution. Thus the microscopic condensate amplitude, scattering interaction and mass determine the mean-field static coefficients and the collective mode. Quadratic quantum fluctuations also produce quantum depletion of a Bose condensate, which a pure coherent-state product omits.
These identifications are controlled for a dilute weakly interacting gas away from a fluctuation-dominated critical region. They are not a universal statement that the exact finite-temperature GL coefficient is always −μ or that its quartic coefficient never receives corrections. Integrating out noncondensed and short-wavelength modes produces effective temperature-dependent coefficients and can change their near-critical behavior. The phase rigidity described by K and the thermodynamic order-parameter expansion remain the shared organizing structure.
For BCS theory, the microscopic order parameter is instead an anomalous pair amplitude, commonly normalized as an energy gap Δ∝⟨ψ​↓​ψ​↑​⟩. It is not an ordinary single-electron field expectation or automatically the square root of a pair number density. Integrating out fermions and expanding in small Δ and slow gradients near Tc​ gives the microscopic derivation of Ginzburg-Landau theory. The BCS pairing-field effective action supplies its coefficients through normal-state pair-response functions and thermal fermion sums.
For example, a clean isotropic three-dimensional weak-coupling s-wave model, with N(0) the density of states per spin at the Fermi energy and Δ measured in energy units, gives
Fs​−Fn​=∫d3r[αΔ​∣Δ∣2+2βΔ​​∣Δ∣4+KΔ​​(∇−ℏiqA​)Δ​2+2μ0​∣B∣2​],q=−2e,
(24)
with microscopic Ginzburg-Landau coefficients from BCS theory
αΔ​≃N(0)Tc​T−Tc​​,βΔ​=8π2(kB​Tc​)27ζ(3)N(0)​,KΔ​=48π2(kB​Tc​)27ζ(3)N(0)ℏ2vF2​​.​
(25)
The numerical factors arise from fermionic Matsubara sums and the isotropic velocity average; they depend on the stated gap and density-of-states conventions. Rescaling the order parameter rescales all three coefficients. The expansion applies near the transition with sufficiently slow spatial variation; disorder, anisotropic pairing and strong coupling alter the coefficients or require additional components. Microscopic derivation also fixes the pair charge in the covariant gradient, producing the superconducting screening current and the Meissner effect.[ref]
The neutral Bose and charged Fermi theories therefore share a complex order parameter and a phase-stiffness functional, while deriving that field from different microscopic objects. The Bogoliubov transformation mixes particle and hole fluctuations of the bosonic condensate in one case and paired fermionic excitations in the other. The static GL limit organizes long-distance behavior; the full microscopic theories retain excitation spectra and temperature dependence beyond that local near-transition expansion.
