Momentum-resolved photoemission and directional tunneling can map the magnitude and nodes of the cuprate gap. Low-temperature heat capacity, thermal transport and penetration-depth measurements constrain the low-energy quasiparticle spectrum. These probes do not all determine the relative sign of the gap. A phase-sensitive test of d-wave pairing uses Josephson interference between differently oriented crystal regions. In the tricrystal half-flux experiment, a superconducting film crosses three designed grain boundaries; a frustrated ring can produce a half-flux signal detected with a scanning SQUID. Unfrustrated geometries supply controls. This probes phase structure beyond merely finding a V-shaped tunneling spectrum.[ref]
For the ideal nearest-neighbor square lattice at one electron per site including both spins, particle-hole symmetry puts the chemical potential at zero. The two requested cuts are
ε(kx​,0)=−2t(1+coskx​a),ε(kx​,kx​)=−4tcoskx​a.
(10)
The first runs from −4t at the zone center to zero at kx​a=±π; the diagonal cut crosses zero at kx​a=±π/2. The Fermi surface obeys coskx​a+cosky​a=0, giving the diamond ∣kx​∣+∣ky​∣=π/a in the first Brillouin zone.
Near the specified node define δk=k−k0​ and
q∥​=(δkx​+δky​)/2​,q⊥​=(δky​−δkx​)/2​.
(11)
Expanding cos(π/2+δka)=−aδk+O((aδk)3) gives
εk​≃2ta(δkx​+δky​)=αq∥​,Δk​≃Δ0​a(δky​−δkx​)=βq⊥​,
(12)
where α=22​ta and β=2​Δ0​a. Therefore
E2≃α2q∥2​+β2q⊥2​.​
(13)
There are four such nodes. An energy contour about one node encloses momentum area πE2/(αβ). Counting all four nodes and two independent spin quasiparticles, with one state per (2π)2 of momentum area per unit real-space area, gives the positive-excitation count and nodal quasiparticle density of states in a two-dimensional d-wave superconductor
AN(E)​=παβ2E2​,Agqp​(E)​=παβ4E​(E>0).​
(14)
This counts thermally excitable Bogoliubov modes, including particle-like and hole-like excitations, not the electron-addition spectral density alone. That distinction prevents a factor-of-two ambiguity. It is a per-layer result; dividing by the layer spacing gives the corresponding volume density in the independent-layer approximation.
The thermal energy density above its zero-temperature value follows from Fermi occupation:
AU(T)−U(0)​=παβ4​∫0∞​eE/(kB​T)+1E2dE​=παβ4I2​​(kB​T)3,I2​=23​ζ(3)≃1.80.
(15)
Thus
AC​=παβ12I2​​kB3​T2≃παβ21.6​kB3​T2.​
(16)
This clean nodal contribution assumes kB​T small compared with the scale over which linearization fails; impurity states and other excitations can add further terms.
In the vortex state, each flux quantum occupies area of order πR2=Φ0​/B, so R∝B−1/2. Outside a core of radius ξ, the phase flow has magnitude vs​(r)≃κ/(2πr) in the simple circular-cell estimate, with κ=h/Mpair​ and condensed-pair mass Mpair​. Its cell average is
⟨∣vs​∣⟩≃πR21​∫ξR​2πrκ​2πrdr=πR2κ(R−ξ)​∝B​.
(17)
The signed vector average can vanish around a vortex; the relevant quantity is a typical magnitude or Doppler shift, not a net transport velocity. Flow shifts nodal energies by order ℏvs​⋅kF​. Because the zero-field nodal density of states is linear in energy, averaging the magnitude of these shifts produces a residual density of states proportional to B​. The resulting Volovik effect gives
C(T,B)=γ(B)T+⋯,γ(B)∝B​.​
(18)
This is the clean semiclassical nodal regime, at temperatures below the field-induced Doppler scale and in an appropriate mixed-state field range. Core states, disorder, Zeeman splitting and screening can modify it. Its replacement by a square root of applied field additionally assumes the mean induction tracks that field away from the lower-critical-field onset; it is not an exact all-field law.
