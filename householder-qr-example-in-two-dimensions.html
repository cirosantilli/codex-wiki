The first column becomes (−5,0)T and the second becomes (−11/5,2/5)T:
R=HA=(−50​−11/52/5​),A=HR.
(41)
Thus an orthogonal matrix multiplying an upper triangular matrix reconstructs A. The zero below the diagonal came from a geometric operation that preserves Euclidean norms and inner products.
Our positive-diagonal convention for QR decomposition is obtained by a sign adjustment. With D=diag(−1,1), put
Q=HD=(3/54/5​−4/53/5​),R+​=DR=(50​11/52/5​).
(42)
Then QTQ=I and A=QR+​​. The intermediate sign chooses an orientation for a basis vector; the adjustment makes the diagonal positive.
For a larger matrix, the next Householder reflection acts only on the lower rows of the remaining columns, retaining the zeros already created. Store its defining vector and apply z↦z−2u(uTz) instead of forming a dense reflection matrix. This links a simple mirror operation to efficient QR decomposition, including least-squares solutions.
