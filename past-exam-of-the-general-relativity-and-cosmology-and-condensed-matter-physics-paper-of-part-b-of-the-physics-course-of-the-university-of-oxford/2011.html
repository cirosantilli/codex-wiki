Unlettered 4 OurBigBook.com
The official notes, PDF p. 27, handwritten p. 20 derive the early-time photon count, its vanishing limit, and the failure of thermal equilibrium when the count is of order one. Two details need supplementing.
First, their constant Nγ0​ is the normalization obtained by extrapolating the early-time particle horizon formula to z=0. It is not the count within today's actual particle horizon. Let N0​=Nγ​(t0​) and let rh0​ be that horizon's physical radius. Using the paper's early-time approximation gives
1+z∗​≃N01/3​H0​rh0​c​1+zeq​​,Nγ​(z∗​)=1.​
(1)
For a present matter-dominated approximation, rh0​≃2c/H0​. Alternatively, retaining both components in the flat model exactly, ΩR​=1/(2+zeq​), and integrating the particle horizon gives
rh0​=H0​2c​2+zeq​​+12+zeq​​​,1+z∗​≃2N01/3​​(2+zeq​​+1).​
(2)
The second boxed formula uses the exact two-component normalization and the radiation-dominated early-time limit. Its large-zeq​ leading term agrees with the first expression and rh0​≃2c/H0​.
Second, a particle horizon containing only order-one photons cannot support a smooth, locally representative radiation density: discrete-particle fluctuations are of the same order as the mean, and a single photon's direction is strongly anisotropic. Thus the fluid description assuming spatial homogeneity and cosmological isotropy is not self-consistent on that causal scale. This does not by itself disprove an ensemble-averaged cosmological symmetry; it limits the extrapolation of the smooth thermal equilibrium description.

In the conventional cubic unit cell, the four face-centered cubic lattice positions contribute
SFCC​=1+eiπ(k+l)+eiπ(h+l)+eiπ(h+k).
(9)
The two-atom atomic basis of a crystal gives the zinc blende structure factor
Fhkl​=SFCC​[fGa​+fAs​eiπ(h+k+l)/2].​
(10)
Mixed-parity Miller indices have SFCC​=0. With all indices even or all odd, SFCC​=4. For real atomic scattering factors,
∣Fhkl​∣2=16⎩⎨⎧​fGa2​+fAs2​,(fGa​+fAs​)2,(fGa​−fAs​)2,​h,k,l all odd,h,k,l all even, h+k+l=0(mod4),h,k,l all even, h+k+l=2(mod4).​
(11)
Hence all same-parity reflections are generally permitted in GaAs, although the last class is weak when the two atomic scattering factors nearly coincide. These are the structural selection rules; a particular experiment must also satisfy the elastic Bragg scattering condition.

In the electron-count estimate fGa​≃31, fAs​≃33, the structure factors are F111​=4(31−33i) and F200​=4(31−33). Thus a single-directed-reflection estimate is
∣F200​∣2∣F111​∣2​=(31−33)2312+332​=512.5.
(12)
A powder X-ray diffraction peak collects all equivalent reflections. The powder diffraction multiplicities are eight for {111} and six for {200}, so the multiplicity-and-electron-count estimate is
I{200}​I{111}​​≃68​(31−33)2312+332​=6.83×102.​
(13)
At (200) the two sublattice amplitudes are opposite in phase and nearly cancel; at (111) their relative phase is a quarter cycle, so this cancellation does not occur. In silicon, the two atomic scattering factors are identical and the diamond cubic structure has an exactly forbidden (200) reflection, while (111) remains allowed in the kinematic approximation.
The numerical ratio neglects the angular dependence of the atomic scattering factors, the Lorentz-polarization factor and thermal-motion corrections. No wavelength or measurement geometry is supplied to refine those factors. This is therefore a stated structural estimate, rather than a unique precision ratio of measured peak areas.

Let a be a stable equilibrium separation: V′(a)=0, K=V′′(a)>0. With bond extension δ=x−a, the Taylor expansion is
V(a+δ)=V(a)+21​Kδ2+61​V′′′(a)δ3+⋯.
(14)
The harmonic approximation applies when the typical thermal and zero-point bond fluctuations satisfy ∣δ∣≪a and the higher terms are negligible, for example ∣V′′′(a)δ∣≪3K and ∣V′′′′(a)δ2∣≪12K. The relevant small quantity is the relative displacement of neighbouring atoms. Small bond excursions about a stable minimum give an amplitude-independent spring constant K=V′′(a). Normalize the plane waves in a periodic crystal of area or volume V. The Fourier coefficients of the periodic potential obey
⟨k∣V∣k′⟩=V1​∫V​V(r)e−i(k−k′)⋅rdr=Vk−k′​.
(23)
Translation of the integral by any lattice vector R leaves it invariant but multiplies it by e−i(k−k′)⋅R. A nonzero result therefore requires k−k′ to be a reciprocal lattice vector.
Near an isolated Bragg scattering degeneracy, ordinary nondegenerate perturbation theory fails because the free energies coincide. Retain the resonant plane waves ∣k⟩, ∣k+G⟩ and apply degenerate perturbation theory. Absorb the constant V0​ into the energy origin. Since a real potential has V−G​=VG∗​, the projected Schrödinger equation has coefficient matrix
(ϵ0​(k)−EVG​​V−G​ϵ0​(k+G)−E​).
(24)
A nonzero eigenstate requires
[ϵ0​(k)−E][ϵ0​(k+G)−E]−∣VG​∣2=0.​
(25)
Off-resonant plane waves give higher-order energy shifts omitted in this leading nearly-free electron model.

For positive λ, a fluctuation of magnetization produces a Weiss molecular field in the same direction, reinforcing alignment. The mean-field self-consistency equation is
M=Ms​BJ​[kB​TgμB​Jμ0​​(Happlied​+λM)].
(36)
Linearizing in the paramagnetic phase gives
M=TC​(Happlied​+λM),χ=T−λCC​=T−TC​C​,TC​=λC=3kB​μ0​λn(gμB​)2J(J+1)​.​
(37)
At zero applied field, the right-hand side's slope at M=0 is TC​/T. Below TC​ this exceeds one, and the saturation of the Brillouin function allows stable nonzero solutions of opposite sign: spontaneous ferromagnetism. Above TC​, only the zero-field solution M=0 remains stable. The stated linear susceptibility is the paramagnetic branch for T>TC​; it must not be continued as a negative susceptibility below the transition.
