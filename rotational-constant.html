Rotational constant (B=h/(8π2I))

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation Rigid rotor Linear rigid rotor
OurBigBook.com Words: 102
In this article the rotational constant is a frequency,
B=8π2Ih​.
(5)
Its dimensions are s−1, since the Planck constant has dimensions kgm2s−1 and a moment of inertia has dimensions kgm2. Some authors instead call hB=ℏ2/(2I) the energy-valued rotational constant; this is the convention in the earlier synthetic molecular beam electric resonance diagram. A third convention uses the spectroscopic wavenumber B=B/c, with c the speed of light. Always check units before using ν=2B(J+1).
If B is known and the two masses are specified, the rigid two-atom model gives the bond length r=h/(8π2μB)​. This is why rotational spectroscopy can constrain molecular geometry. A real fitted constant can depend on molecular vibration, so this inference alone does not establish an equilibrium bond length.
