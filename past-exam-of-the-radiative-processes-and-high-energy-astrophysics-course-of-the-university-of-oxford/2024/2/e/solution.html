Under an additional thermalization assumption, an opaque star emits approximately black-body radiation characterized by the temperature near the layer from which thermal photons escape. Planck's law gives a Rayleigh-Jeans law low-frequency tail Bν​∝Tν2 and a Wien approximation high-frequency tail Bν​∝ν3e−hν/(kB​T).
Using the gas-dominated temperature profile in d and the local escape criterion in c, the model's putative emission temperature for the Sun's parameters would be
Tesc​≃Tc​RΔResc​​≃16.1K.
(28)
Using radial τ=1 instead gives 22.8K. The frequency form of Wien's displacement law for the maximum of Bν​ is hνmax​/(kB​T)=2.82144, so these two prescriptions predict
νmax​≃9.5×1011Hzor1.34×1012Hz,​
(29)
respectively, in the far infrared/submillimetre region. These are conditional estimates for this model, not observed solar peaks. A realistic solar effective temperature near 5.8×103K gives νmax​≃3.4×1014Hz in Bν​. The maximum of Bλ​ occurs near 0.50μm; it is not obtained by simply taking c/νmax​, because spectral densities transform with a Jacobian determinant.
The model is unsuitable for the solar photosphere: fully ionized hydrogen at 16–23K is inconsistent with thermal ionization, and the assumed opacity and near-surface temperature gradient do not resemble the Sun's. Even its central temperature is substantially below the actual solar value, about 1.6×107K.
There is a further logical limitation: the question retains only Thomson scattering. In its coherent, cold-electron limit, this redistributes photon directions without generating a Planck photon distribution. For a concrete counterexample, monochromatic injected radiation remains monochromatic after any number of such elastic scatterings; large optical depth cannot turn it into black-body radiation. True absorption and emission, or a specified pre-existing thermal radiation field with appropriate energy exchange, are missing. Thus an approximately blackbody emergent spectrum is the usual additional physical assumption, while the stated scattering-only model does not uniquely predict an emergent spectrum or its peak.
