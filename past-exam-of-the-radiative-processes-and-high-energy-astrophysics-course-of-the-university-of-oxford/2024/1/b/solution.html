In the ultrarelativistic regime of a, retaining a constant mean fractional energy gain and ignoring losses gives, after N individual crossings,
EN​≃E0​(1+2cu​)N≃E0​exp(2cNu​).​
(4)
After n complete return cycles, N=2n and En​≃E0​(1+u/c)n, to the same first-order accuracy. These products approximate the mean amplification; individual electron crossing angles give a distribution around it.
The downstream electron distribution is nearly isotropic. Its crossing particle flux per unit area is na​c/4, where na​ is the accelerated-particle number density. The downstream flow permanently advects particles away at particle flux na​u2​. Their ratio estimates the escape probability after a downstream visit:
Pesc​≃na​c/4na​u2​​=c4u2​​=cu​,Pret​≃1−cu​.​
(5)
Upstream particles are swept back towards the shock wave in this idealized infinite-upstream model. Consequently this return probability applies once per complete cycle, not once per individual crossing or local scattering event.
Let A≃1+u/c be the amplification per complete cycle and P=Pret​. A steady injection of N0​ particles leaves N0​Pn surviving to reach E=E0​An. Eliminating n gives the cumulative power law and differential energy distribution
N(>E)∝ElnP/lnA,dEdN​∝E−s,s=1−lnAlnP​≃2.
(6)
The escape distribution has the same differential exponent when P is independent of energy. Thus the standard Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions give
N(>E)∝E−1,dN/dE∝E−2.​
(7)
Equivalently the phase-space one-particle distribution function is f(p)∝p−4 for ultrarelativistic particles. Equal logarithmic intervals then contain equal particle energy. A finite source age, upstream escape, and radiative losses eventually introduce cutoffs or breaks.
A particle exponent is not automatically the radiation spectral index. For optically thin uncooled synchrotron radiation from dN/dE∝E−s, the characteristic frequency scales as ν∝E2 and the emitted power as E2. Hence Lν​∝E−sE2/(dν/dE)∝ν−(s−1)/2. With the convention Lν​∝ν−α, this gives α≃1/2​ and νLν​∝ν1/2. Strong synchrotron cooling steepens a continuously injected particle distribution by one exponent, giving α≃1 above its cooling break. A photon number distribution dN˙γ​/dε∝ε−Γ has Γ=α+1, or 3/2 in the uncooled case.
Convention qualification. If the printed P=1−u/c is instead imposed independently at every individual crossing, its combination with Acrossing​=1+u/(2c) gives s≃3 and uncooled α≃1. For example u/c=0.01 gives s=1−ln(0.99)/ln(1.005)≃3.015, whereas the consistent cycle model gives s≃2.010. The standard s=2 estimate requires matching the probability and amplification to the same complete cycle.
