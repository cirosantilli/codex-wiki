Single-slit diffraction (I(θ)/I(0)=(sinu/u)2,u=πasinθ/λ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fraunhofer diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 650
Single-slit diffraction is the spreading of light transmitted by one slit. Consider an ideal rectangular slit of width a, illuminated normally by a uniform plane wave with a single wavelength λ in the surrounding medium. This monochromatic wave has uniform wave amplitude and equal incident wave phases across the slit, providing the required optical coherence. In the scalar Fraunhofer diffraction model, the normalized angular wave intensity is
I0​I(θ)​=(usinu​)2,u=λπasinθ​,I0​=I(0),limu→0​usinu​=1.
(74)
Here θ is measured from the forward normal in the plane across the slit width. The sinc function uses the unnormalized convention sincu=sinu/u. The limit makes the central value one. This ideal envelope neglects additional direction-dependent propagation and detector factors; near the forward direction it describes the normalized screen irradiance. [ref].
The noncentral zeros satisfy
asinθm​=mλ,m∈Z∖{0},​amλ​​≤1.
(75)
The restriction follows from the range of the sine of a real angle. The first minima have sinθ=±λ/a when λ/a≤1. If a<λ, this model has no noncentral zero in the forward hemisphere; equality puts the first zeros at grazing directions, outside the small-angle approximation and off any finite perpendicular screen. [ref].
For 0<λ/a<1, define the central width as the separation between the two first minima. Its angular width and its width on a perpendicular screen are
Δθ=2arcsin(λ/a)≈a2λ​,y=Ltanθ,W=2Ltan(arcsin(λ/a))≈a2Lλ​.
(76)
The angular approximation uses radians and requires λ/a≪1; the screen approximation uses y≈Lθ. The tangent relation is exact straight-line geometry, even though the underlying Fraunhofer diffraction field is approximate. This width is distinct from the full width at half maximum.
For λ=500nm, a=0.1mm and L=1m, the positive first minimum is
θ1​=arcsin(0.005)≈0.286480∘,y1​=1−0.0052​L(0.005)​≈5.00006mm,W≈10.0001mm.
(77)

The lower diagram shows the exact screen geometry with the vertical scale enlarged. Normalization removes the change in peak brightness when the slit width changes.
The wave interference explanation is especially simple: divide the slit into equal narrow strips and add their complex amplitudes, rather than their wave intensities. With wavenumber k=2π/λ, the continuous slit gives
A(0)A(θ)​=a1​∫−a/2a/2​eikxsinθdx=2iueiu−e−iu​=usinu​.
(78)
Integrating the complex exponential gives the endpoint difference; its continuous value at zero is one. Taking the squared complex modulus gives the stated wave intensity. This is the Fourier transform of an interval indicator expressed in angular coordinates. Feynman's continuous-source argument obtains the same pattern by letting the number of sources tend to infinity. [ref].
At the positive first minimum, the wave phases span exactly 2π across the slit. Every strip in one half has a partner a/2 away, with equal wave amplitude and phase difference π, so the pairs cancel. At θ=0, all the wave phases align instead. [ref].
The drawing uses N=8 equally weighted midpoint samples xj​/a=−1/2+(j+1/2)/8, with j=0,…,7. At the first minimum their complex amplitudes are a common rotation of the eight roots of unity, so their head-to-tail polygon closes exactly. This finite sum illustrates cancellation; the continuous integral, rather than eight point sources, defines the slit pattern at general angles. The circle shows the continuous range of wave phases; coloured arrows show the eight samples.
A narrower aperture requires a larger angle to accumulate the cancelling phase difference, so narrowing the slit widens the beam. This is a useful limit on geometrical optics: confining light more tightly at an opening increases its subsequent angular spread. At fixed incident irradiance and slit height, reducing a also reduces transmitted power, exposing a practical balance between diffraction-limited resolution and light throughput. In a diffraction grating, this single-slit diffraction envelope modulates the wave interference between different slits.
