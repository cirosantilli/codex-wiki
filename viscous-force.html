Viscous force (Fvisc​=∫∂V​τndS)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Dynamic viscosity Viscous stress tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
The viscous force on a fluid region is the resultant of the traction supplied by the viscous stress tensor τ. With n the outward unit normal of the region, its surface contribution is the displayed surface integral. For an incompressible Newtonian fluid, τ=η[∇u+(∇u)T], where η is dynamic viscosity and u is velocity. A force scale is ηUL for a body of size L moving at speed U, while the inertial force scale is ρU2L2; their ratio is the Reynolds number. Total hydrodynamic drag on a body includes both viscous traction and the pressure traction induced by the flow. For example, the full Stokes drag law gives 6πηRU for a no-slip sphere, rather than just the viscous-traction part alone.
