Nutrient-consuming chemotactic travelling band (B(z),C(z))

OurBigBook.com Words: 175 Articles: 1
For a Keller--Segel model with ct​=−kb, a positive-speed travelling wave B(z),C(z), z=x−vt, satisfies vC′=kB and DB′+vB−αBC′/C=0 when the integrated bacterial flux constant is zero. Writing μ=α/D>1 and ξ=v(z−z0​)/D, its positive band solution is
C=C∞​(1+e−ξ)−1/(μ−1),B=kD(μ−1)v2C∞​​e−ξ(1+e−ξ)−μ/(μ−1).
(42)
To derive it, integrate B′/B=μC′/C−v/D to obtain B=KCμe−vz/D, then separate vC′=kB. The positive integration constant is a translation of the wave. Concentration rises monotonically from zero behind to C∞​ ahead; B vanishes at both ends and has a unique maximum at ξ=−log(μ−1). The condition μ>1 describes this exponential-tail branch; it is not asserted to exclude every limiting or weak travelling wave at other parameter values.
