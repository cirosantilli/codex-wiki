Human disorders show that motivation depends on several distinguishable operations: initiating activity, translating incentive value into effort, evaluating outcomes and sustaining goal-directed behavior. A person may be able to execute an instructed movement yet fail to initiate useful action, or may respond differently to reward and effort without a global loss of intelligence. These dissociations argue against equating motivation with motor strength or with a single quantity of pleasure.
A striking starting point is auto-activation deficit. Dominique Laplane and colleagues described three people with marked reduction in spontaneous mental and behavioral activity after toxic encephalopathy and bilateral basal ganglia brain lesions, especially involving the globus pallidus on the available scans. Intellectual capacities were relatively preserved and motor impairment could be mild. External stimulation could reverse the apparent inactivity. This contrast suggested a problem in self-activation rather than inability to perform every relevant cognitive or motor operation.[ref]
The cases were mechanistically informative but not anatomically pure experiments. Toxic injury can affect more than the structures visible on a scan, and reduced initiation can interact with executive, emotional and motor processes. The observation therefore implicates basal ganglia–frontal systems in internally generated activity without showing that one nucleus contains all motivation. It also motivates assessing spontaneous behavior separately from performance after repeated prompting.
Liane Schmidt and colleagues tested a more specific incentive-to-action hypothesis. They contrasted an instructed handgrip task, requiring specified force levels, with an incentive task where effort could earn money. Patients with basal ganglia damage and auto-activation difficulty could use external instructions to produce force but showed abnormal translation of incentives into effort. The apparatus makes the dissociation concrete: an available motor response does not ensure that anticipated reward will appropriately energize it.[ref]
This helps separate motor capacity from motivational use of that capacity. Nevertheless, monetary incentives also require understanding task contingencies, representing value and maintaining the goal. A reduced force response does not uniquely identify absence of pleasure. The strength of the design is the contrasting instruction condition; the remaining question is which valuation, activation or control operation prevents self-driven use of the preserved response.
Parkinson's disease offers converging evidence while introducing its own confounds. Campbell Le Heron and colleagues tested 39 patients, with and without clinical apathy, ON and OFF their usual dopaminergic medication, alongside 32 controls. Choices varied both monetary reward and required physical effort. Apathy and medication state were associated with different patterns across the reward–effort space, rather than one uniform decrease in willingness to act. This supports distinguishing clinical motivational difficulty from the complete set of effects produced by changing dopamine availability.[ref]
The design is stronger than comparing raw movement speed alone, but an ON/OFF contrast is not a selective perturbation of one psychological computation. Medication changes motor function and potentially learning, alertness and response vigor. Apathy groups are not randomly assigned, and comprehension, depression, fatigue and motor capacity can differ. Interpreting choice patterns therefore requires suitable calibration and comparison measures. Dopamine sensitivity does not justify the statement that dopamine is simply a pleasure signal.
A small statistical model illustrates why effort-based decision-making experiments must vary both benefits and costs. Suppose acceptance depends on U=αR−κE, where R is reward, E effort, α reward gain and κ effort cost. With a fixed logistic function as the choice rule, a lower reward gain and a higher effort cost can produce exactly the same acceptance probability at one offer. This single-offer reward-effort ambiguity motivates varying both dimensions to distinguish their predictions within the model. These are alternative explanations to test, not parameter estimates obtained merely by diagnosing apathy.
Patients with prefrontal cortex damage reveal another component: evaluation of actions across outcomes and time. Antoine Bechara and colleagues introduced the procedure now called the Iowa gambling task, in which choices offered different short-term gains and longer-term consequences. Patients with ventromedial prefrontal cortex damage continued making disadvantageous choices despite relatively preserved conventional intellectual abilities. This linked real-world decision difficulty to experimentally observable valuation and learning demands.[ref]
The result supports a role in using consequences to guide behavior, but the task combines reward learning, loss sensitivity, reversal, uncertainty and human memory. It does not uniquely establish one bodily-feedback mechanism or prove that the patients have no emotional responses. Similarly, anhedonia, reduced initiation and willingness to expend effort should be measured separately rather than treated as synonyms. Several mechanisms can produce superficially similar inactivity, and a change in one response measure does not imply a change in every form of motivation.
Across these disorders, the most useful inference is computational and relational. Basal ganglia injury distinguishes instructed action from self-activation and incentive translation; Parkinsonian comparisons distinguish aspects of reward–effort choice from medication-related motor and motivational effects; prefrontal damage reveals difficulty applying future consequences. These operations interact through connected systems and need not map to one region each. Patient studies have transformed motivation from a vague explanation of behavior into separable hypotheses about initiation, valuation, effort and control, while demanding that the preserved capacities and competing explanations be tested explicitly.
