Bond order (b=(Nb​−Na​)/2)

 Home Chemistry Quantum chemistry Molecular orbital theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 74
In the elementary molecular orbital theory count, bond order is half the excess of electrons in bonding molecular orbitals over those in antibonding molecular orbitals. Thus adding one electron to a bonding molecular orbital raises b by 1/2, while adding one to an antibonding molecular orbital lowers it by 1/2. The configurations drawn above give 1, 1/2, and 0. This count summarizes net covalent bond occupancy; it is neither a measured energy nor a universal test for every kind of binding. See Chemistry 2e, 8.4: molecular orbital bond order.
