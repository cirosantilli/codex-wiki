Orthogonal complement for a sesquilinear form (W⊥={v:B(v,w)=0 for all w∈W})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Sesquilinear form
OurBigBook.com Words: 71
For a Hermitian form or a Hermitian form over a quadratic finite field, the displayed subspace is the orthogonal complement of W. If the form restricts nondegenerately to a finite-dimensional W, its invertible Gram matrix supplies, for each v, a unique w0​∈W with B(v−w0​,W)=0. Consequently V=W⊕W⊥. When the form on V is nondegenerate, its restriction to W⊥ is too: a radical vector there is orthogonal to both summands and hence is zero.
