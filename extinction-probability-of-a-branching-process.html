Extinction probability of a branching process (q=P(∃j:Zj​=0))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Galton-Watson process Galton-Watson extinction fixed point
OurBigBook.com Words: 318 Articles: 1
For a Galton-Watson process with one ancestor, the branching extinction probability is the probability that some generation is empty:
q=P(⋃n≥0​{Zn​=0})=limn→∞​P(Zn​=0).
(9)
The equality uses the absorbing state at zero. The complementary branching survival probability 1−q is the probability that every generation is nonempty. The Galton-Watson extinction fixed point computes q from the offspring distribution.
For p0​=p1​=1/4, p2​=1/2, one ancestor has q=1/2. Starting instead from m ancestors with independent descendant families gives
P(eventual extinction∣Z0​=m)=qm,P(Zn​=0∣Z0​=m)=qnm​.
(10)
All m families must die out. With three ancestors, eventual extinction has probability 1/8 and survival has probability 7/8; by generation two, extinction has probability (11/32)3=1331/32768≈0.040619. Extra ancestors change the extinction probability without changing the offspring distribution. [ref]
For finite offspring mean μ, the extinction classification is:
  • If μ<1, extinction has probability 1. For example p0​=3/4, p2​=1/4 gives μ=1/2 and F(s)=3/4+s2/4; its only fixed point in [0,1] is 1.
  • If μ=1 and p1​=1, extinction also has probability 1. For example p0​=p2​=1/2 gives F(s)−s=(s−1)2/2 and q=1, despite expected generation size in a Galton-Watson process remaining one.
  • If 1<μ<∞, extinction has probability q<1. The value can be zero: if p0​=0, every individual has at least one child, so an initially nonempty family never becomes empty. In particular p2​=1 gives Zn​=2n and q=0.
The critical exception is p1​=1: every individual has exactly one child, F(s)=s, and Zn​=1 forever from one ancestor. Thus μ=1 but q=0; a blanket claim that every critical Galton-Watson process becomes extinct would be false. These cases concern the Galton-Watson process with its common offspring distribution and independence assumptions. [ref]
This probability answers whether any lineage persists, which the offspring mean alone cannot quantify in the supercritical case. A branching process conditioned on extinction uses the same fixed point to reweight reproduction and describe families known eventually to die out.
