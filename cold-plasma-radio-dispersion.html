Cold-plasma radio dispersion (n2=1−ωp2​/ω2)

Take a cold, unmagnetized, collisionless plasma with uniform equilibrium electron density ne​, fixed neutralizing ions, and a small electromagnetic wave that is a transverse wave. Cold means that thermal-motion corrections are neglected. For electron mass me​, elementary charge magnitude e and electric field E=Re(Ee−iωt), the linear Newton's second law equation is me​v˙=−eE. Thus v=−ieE/(me​ω) and the current density is J=ine​e2E/(me​ω). Writing the induced electric polarization as J=−iωP gives a negative electric susceptibility. With vacuum permittivity ϵ0​,
ϵr​=1−ω2ωp2​​,ωp2​=ϵ0​me​ne​e2​,c2k2=ω2−ωp2​,n2=ω2c2k2​=1−ω2ωp2​​.
(68)
The plasma frequency ωp​ controls the response. For ω>ωp​, the propagating branch has real refractive index 0<n<1. For ω<ωp​, this homogeneous model has an evanescent wave, not an ordinary propagating wave with a negative real refractive index. In a slowly varying vertical profile, a normally incident ray approaches a turning region where ωp​(z)=ω; the geometrical-ray approximation fails right at that region. At oblique incidence the conserved horizontal wave vector changes the turning condition. A smooth density gradient can therefore return a skywave without a mirror or a layer of metal.
For a homogeneous propagating section, direct differentiation of the dispersion relation gives
vph​=kω​=nc​>c,vg​=dkdω​=cn<c,ng​=n+ωdωdn​=n1​.
(69)
A phase velocity greater than c does not send information faster than c. Here a narrow wave packet has group velocity vg​, while the full causal response must still respect special relativity. Even this stripped-down plasma is strongly dispersive near its plasma frequency. In the actual ionosphere, collisions produce absorption, and the magnetic field of Earth changes the response with direction and wave polarization; a single real scalar n cannot capture all of that behavior.
