Finite-conjugate microscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Optical microscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
An optical microscope whose microscope objective is designed to form a real image at a specified finite distance. The intended image distance and optical aberration corrections belong together; mechanical tube length is a mounting specification, not automatically the image distance measured from a principal plane.[ref]
Why can a small displacement produce a large image? For a converging thin lens, write uo​=fo​+ϵ with epsilon>0. Substitution into the thin lens equation gives
vo​=ϵfo​(fo​+ϵ)​,mo​=−ϵfo​​.
(19)
The similar triangles construction supplies the minus sign. The example has epsilon=2mm and hence mo​=−5. Letting epsilon decrease moves the real image farther away; it does not keep the microscope eyepiece in focus at a fixed position. Large transverse magnification therefore requires the correct conjugate geometry, not merely a short focal length.
