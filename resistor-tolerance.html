Resistor tolerance

OurBigBook.com Words: 100
Resistor tolerance specifies the permissible manufacturing deviation of a resistor's electrical resistance from its nominal value under stated conditions. For nominal electrical resistance Rnom​>0 and fractional resistor tolerance 0≤τ<1, the specified range is Rnom​(1−τ)≤R≤Rnom​(1+τ). This differs from measurement uncertainty: resistor tolerance describes a component specification, while measurement uncertainty describes knowledge of a measured value; the tolerance alone is not a probability distribution for that value.
Two nominally equal resistors can have different actual electrical resistances, changing their voltage divider ratio even without an external electrical load. In a loaded voltage divider, use the actual electrical resistances to calculate both the unloaded output and the Thévenin resistance governing voltage divider loading error. A mismatch from resistor tolerance and a change from voltmeter loading are distinct effects that can occur together.
