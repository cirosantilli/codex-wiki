Bounded Girsanov drift does not preserve true martingales under compensation (M∈M(P)⇒M−⟨M,W⟩∈M(Q),dQ=eW1​−1/2dP)

Let W be a P-Brownian motion on [0,1] and tilt by the bounded constant drift integrand one, so dQ=eW1​−1/2dP. Then WsQ​=Ws​−s is Q-Brownian motion, either by Girsanov theorem or direct Gaussian exponential tilting. Set a=1/2,
H=1+Wa2​eWa2​​,Ms​=H(Ws​−Wa​)1{s>a}​.
(49)
The density of Wa​ under P is π−1/2e−x2, so EP​H=π−1/2∫(1+x2)−1dx<∞. Independence of future Brownian increments makes M a continuous true P-martingale. Under Q, that density is multiplied by ex−1/4, so EQ​H=∞.
For s>a, its compensated local martingale is
Ms​−⟨M,W⟩s​=H(WsQ​−WaQ​).
(50)
The future Q-Brownian increment is independent of the positive variable H and has positive finite mean absolute value. Hence the displayed variable has infinite expected absolute value under Q and is not a true martingale. This shows why the general Girsanov theorem promises a local martingale, while a true-martingale conclusion needs an additional integrability condition such as Girsanov compensation with square-integrable input.
