Normal subgroup (N⊴G)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 24
A subgroup N≤G is a normal subgroup when gNg−1=N for every g∈G. In other words, conjugation by any group element preserves the subgroup as a set, although it may exchange its individual elements. Equivalently, gN=Ng for every g: the left and right cosets of N agree. Write N⊴G.
Every subgroup of an abelian group is a normal subgroup, because conjugation fixes each element. Normality can also hold in a nonabelian group: in S3​, the three even permutations form the alternating group A3​={e,(123),(132)}, a normal subgroup as the group kernel of the sign of a permutation. Its two cosets separate the even and odd permutations.
In contrast, H={e,(12)} is a subgroup of S3​ but is not a normal subgroup. With function composition of permutations acting rightmost first, choose g=(123). Then
gH={(123),(13)},Hg={(123),(23)},
(57)
which are different cosets. Also g(12)g−1=(23)∈/H, directly contradicting invariance under conjugation. Thus a subgroup alone does not suffice for quotient group multiplication.
The practical value of normality is coset multiplication is well-defined exactly for normal subgroups. Each coset can act as one element of a quotient group, with results independent of representatives. Conversely every normal subgroup is the group kernel of its quotient map of groups, so normality also identifies which distinctions a group homomorphism can consistently forget.
