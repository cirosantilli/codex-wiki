Riemannian divergence theorem (∫M​divXωg​=∫∂M​g(X,N)ωg​​)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Divergence theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
On an oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary, the divergence of a Riemannian vector field obeys the displayed integral identity for smooth compactly supported vector fields X, or for arbitrary smooth X if the manifold is compact. Here N is the outward unit normal vector and the boundary has the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. Its induced Riemannian volume form is j∗ιN​ωg​. Splitting X into its normal and tangential components gives j∗ιX​ωg​=g(X,N)ωg​​, since a top form vanishes on n boundary-tangent arguments. Apply the Stokes theorem to dιX​ωg​=(divX)ωg​.
Without a support or decay condition, flux at infinity can invalidate the result even if the integrals converge. On [0,∞) with Riemannian metric dx2, X=(arctanx)∂x​ has boundary flux zero but integral of divergence π/2.
