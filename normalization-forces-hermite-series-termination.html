For the Hermite differential equation f′′−2ξf′+(ϵ−1)f=0 with real ϵ, the wavefunction e−ξ2/2f is square integrable on the real line only if f is a polynomial. To prove this without assuming that even and odd tails cannot cancel, project a square-integrable solution onto its even and odd parts first. Each projection remains square integrable and satisfies the same equation.

The tail has constant complex phase. For any fixed 0<δ<1/2, comparison with (1−δ)/(j+1) bounds its coefficient magnitudes below by K(1−δ)j/j! for sufficiently large j. Thus on the positive real axis the tail dominates a positive multiple of ξpe(1−δ)ξ2 after subtraction of finitely many terms. The Gaussian function factor leaves exponential growth e(1/2−δ)ξ2, contradicting the square-integrable condition. Hence each nonzero parity sequence terminates. Its top coefficient forces ϵ=2n+1, giving Hermite oscillator quantization and a Hermite polynomial of degree n.
