Hydrophobic effect

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Hydrophobicity
OurBigBook.com Words: 151 Articles: 1
The hydrophobic effect is the solvent-mediated tendency of nonpolar regions to associate in water. Exposing a hydrocarbon region changes how surrounding water molecules can arrange and interact; reducing that exposure can lower the total free energy. Both water and lipids enter the accounting. The effect is not an additional covalent bond between chains. See IUPAC Gold Book: hydrophobic interaction.
At fixed temperature and pressure, compare two candidate organizations by the change in Gibbs free energy, ΔG=ΔH−TΔS, where H is enthalpy, T is absolute temperature, and S is entropy. An organization with lower G is favored in this comparison. Reducing exposed hydrocarbon area is one contribution; changes in chain packing and the entropy of the lipids also matter. This explains why hydrophobicity alone cannot predict every aggregate shape. See 16.4 Free Energy.
