Conic dual problem (sup{bTy:ATy=c, y∈K∗})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex optimization Conic optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 973 Articles: 4
A conic dual problem searches for a lower bound on a conic optimization problem. A feasible point exhibits an attainable cost; a feasible dual variable explains why every allowed decision costs at least a specified amount. If the two costs agree, the pair certifies a global minimum without relying on how the points were found.
Fix a real matrix A∈Rm×n, vectors b∈Rm, c∈Rn, and a closed convex cone K⊆Rm. With unrestricted x∈Rn, use the convention
p∗​=inf{cTx:Ax−b∈K},d∗​=sup{bTy:ATy=c, y∈K∗},
(30)
where the dual cone is K∗={y:yTs≥0 for every s∈K}. The conic dual problem uses K∗ because it must turn every allowed slack variable s=Ax−b into a nonnegative dot product. Taking K=R+m​ gives componentwise linear inequalities; taking a second-order cone gives a Euclidean norm constraint. A self-dual cone has K∗=K, but this equality does not guarantee a feasible point or strong duality.
The Lagrangian supplies a short derivation. For y∈K∗,
L(x,y)=cTx−yT(Ax−b)=(c−ATy)Tx+bTy.
(31)
Its infimum over unrestricted x is bTy when ATy=c, and −∞ otherwise. Maximizing the resulting Lagrange dual function gives the stated conic dual problem. The minus sign matches the convention Ax−b∈K.
