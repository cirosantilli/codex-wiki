Christenson-Cronin-Fitch-Turlay kaon CP violation experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Discrete symmetry in quantum field theory CP violation
OurBigBook.com Words: 213
James H. Christenson, James Watson Cronin, Val Logsdon Fitch and René Turlay reported the result in 1964. At Brookhaven's AGS, 30 GeV protons struck beryllium; collimators selected a neutral particle beam at 30 degrees. A downstream helium decay volume reduced interactions.[ref]
The flight distance suppressed the short-lived kaon component. Each arm of a two-arm magnetic spectrometer had spark chambers before and after its magnet. Cherenkov detectors and scintillation counters triggered photographic recording in coincidence.[ref]
The helium sample yielded 45±9 signal events; (2.0±0.4)×10−3 was the ratio to all charged decay channels.[ref] That denominator must be retained when comparing this result with a total branching fraction.
The evidence was kinematic. Later regenerated-beam quantum interference established that the short- and long-lived states reached the same two-pion final state, excluding alternatives such as distinct pion-like particles.[ref] The 1964 observation established evidence for CP violation; it did not determine every parameter of the CKM matrix. Inferring time-reversal symmetry violation from CP violation invokes CPT symmetry, rather than a direct comparison of time-reversed transitions in that experiment.
