Radial and angular modes of an orthogonally invariant scalar potential (mη2​=2λv2)

OurBigBook.com Words: 141
For V=m2ϕ2/2+λ(ϕ2)2/4, m2<0, the vacuum manifold is a sphere of radius v=−m2/λ​. At a chosen vacuum the Hessian matrix is 2λv2nnT, where n is its unit direction. The radial scalar has mass squared 2λv2; the n−1 angular modes are physical Goldstone bosons when the symmetry is global.
For the two-component case, write ϕ=(v+ρ)(cosθ,sinθ). The kinetic term becomes 21​(∂μ​ρ)2+21​(v+ρ)2(∂μ​θ)2. At quadratic order the canonically normalized angular field is π=vθ, with no mass term. Its Euler-Lagrange equation is the wave equation, so ω2=∣k∣2 in units where the propagation speed is one. The radial field instead obeys ω2=∣k∣2+2λv2. Thus a common rotation costs no energy, whereas a slowly varying rotation propagates as a collective wave. With the selecting source of continuous vacuum selection by an external source, use π=wθ and obtain ω2=∣k∣2+h/w. This elementary dispersion relation separates a Goldstone boson, a pseudo-Goldstone boson and a massive radial excitation within one example.
