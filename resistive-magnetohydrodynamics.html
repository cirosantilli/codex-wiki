Resistive magnetohydrodynamics

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Astrophysical fluid dynamics Magnetohydrodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 73
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics retains finite magnetic diffusivity in the coupled evolution of a conducting fluid and its magnetic field. The resistive induction equation allows field diffusion relative to the fluid, while the magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation includes the Lorentz force density. It contrasts with the zero-diffusivity limit of ideal magnetohydrodynamics.

Magnetic energy balance in resistive magnetohydrodynamics (E˙B​=−∫V​u⋅(J×B)dV−η∫V​J2dV)

Words: 68
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics OurBigBook.com
For constant electrical resistivity η and magnetic permeability μ0​, let EB​=∫V​B2/(2μ0​)dV and J=∇×B/μ0​. In a fixed volume with zero boundary energy flux, multiply the resistive induction equation by B/μ0​ and integrate by parts. This gives the displayed balance. The first term transfers energy between the magnetic field and the fluid; the second is nonpositive and represents Joule heating. The latter does not imply that the total magnetic energy must decrease if fluid motion continually supplies it.

Magnetic reconnection

Words: 41
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics OurBigBook.com
Magnetic reconnection changes connectivity between magnetic field lines. It requires departure from smooth magnetic flux freezing, for example through finite resistivity or other nonideal terms concentrated in a current sheet. Ideal viscous magnetic relaxation dissipates mechanical energy while retaining frozen connectivity, so viscosity alone does not provide reconnection.

Magnetic skin layer (δ=2η/ω​)

Words: 200 Articles: 2
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics OurBigBook.com
A magnetic skin layer is the thin region penetrated by an oscillating magnetic field in a conductor. For locally tangential boundary amplitude C(x), negligible material motion and variations along the boundary much slower than those across it, the resistive induction equation gives Bx​=C(x)e−(1+i)y/δ with time convention eiωt. The solenoidal magnetic-field constraint requires the smaller component By​=C′(x)e−(1+i)y/δδ/(1+i) to leading order. The tangential-field approximation therefore does not mean that the normal component is exactly zero at finite magnetic diffusivity.

Flux expulsion

Words: 260 Articles: 2
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics OurBigBook.com
Flux expulsion is redistribution of magnetic flux out of the interiors of persistent circulating flows into their edges and surrounding regions. In two-dimensional incompressible flow, a magnetic flux function obeys ∂t​A+u⋅∇A=η∇2A. At large but finite magnetic Reynolds number, circulation stretches gradients of A, enabling resistive smoothing and weak interior magnetic field with strong field near the circulation boundary. Finite magnetic diffusivity is essential to the eventual rearrangement; exact ideal magnetic flux freezing alone does not justify a flux-free steady cell. Magnetic back reaction can limit the process.

Phase mixing in magnetic flux expulsion (texp​∼(L/U)Rm1/3)

Words: 179 Articles: 1
Flux expulsion OurBigBook.com
Differential circulation of a weak magnetic field winds its flux function into progressively smaller radial scales. In a smooth eddy with angular-velocity gradient of order U/L2, its radial wavenumber grows as kr​∼Ut/L2. Resistive damping becomes substantial when η∫0t​kr2​dt∼1, giving t∼(L4/(ηU2))1/3=(L/U)Rm1/3. This estimate presumes differential rotation and negligible magnetic feedback; a rigidly rotating eddy does not create this phase mixing. Finite magnetic diffusivity then permits weak interior field and boundary concentration, unlike exact ideal flux freezing.

Cubic-time resistive damping in a differentially rotating cell (a∼a0​exp[−ηm2(Ω′)2t3/3])

Words: 101

In a smooth interior region with locally constant shear Ω′, a wound azimuthal Fourier mode has radial wavenumber ks​≃−mΩ′t. Integrating its resistive damping rate ηks2​ gives the exponential decay in the title. The characteristic time is τc​=[3/(ηm2Ω′2)]1/3. For m=1 and Ω′=−Ω0​/d, Ω0​τc​=(3Rm)1/3, where Rm=Ω0​d2/η. Dynamo action

Words: 4k Articles: 54
Resistive magnetohydrodynamics OurBigBook.com
Dynamo action is growth or sustained maintenance of a magnetic field by conducting-fluid motion despite magnetic diffusion. In the kinematic problem the velocity is prescribed and the resistive induction equation is linear in the magnetic field. Magnetic feedback through the Lorentz force density matters once the field becomes dynamically important.

Homopolar disc dynamo (LI˙=(Cω−R)I)

Connect a homopolar generator to a solenoid producing its own axial field. Reinforcing winding and rotation give C=μ0​Na2/(2l) and LI˙=(Cω−R)I. A seed field grows for ω>R/C, is marginal at equality, and decays below threshold. An identically zero initial field remains zero in this homogeneous ideal model; startup requires a seed or perturbation.

Dynamo quenching (α(B)=α0​/(1+B2/B∗2​))

Words: 281 Articles: 3
Dynamo action OurBigBook.com
Dynamo quenching is the reduction or modification of magnetic-field generation by magnetic feedback on the generating flow and its correlations. The Lorentz force can change shear or turbulence, while magnetic helicity affects mean-field regeneration. A phenomenological scalar example is α(B)=α0​/(1+B2/B∗2​). In the illustrative amplitude equation B˙=[cα(B)−d]B with c,d>0, a growing field saturates at B2=B∗2​(cα0​/d−1) when cα0​>d. This demonstrates the negative feedback mechanism without claiming that algebraic quenching is a complete solar closure.

Algebraic alpha quenching (α(B)=α0​−p∣B∣2)

Words: 212 Articles: 2
Dynamo quenching OurBigBook.com
Algebraic alpha quenching prescribes a field-dependent alpha effect as a closure for nonlinear mean-field dynamo feedback. The polynomial law α=α0​−p∣B∣2 with p>0 decreases alpha, so it can saturate positive alpha but makes negative alpha more negative. It therefore differs importantly from the sign-preserving rational law α0​/(1+∣B∣2/B∗2​). Its solutions must satisfy nonnegative squared-amplitude conditions; an assertion based only on large ∣α0​∣ need not hold for both signs.

Magnetic reversal sends (B,A,V) to (−B,−A,V). Anti-dynamo theorem

Words: 445 Articles: 5
Dynamo action OurBigBook.com
An anti-dynamo theorem gives a geometric or analytic restriction excluding self-sustained dynamo action under stated magnetic diffusivity, domain and boundary conditions. It excludes regeneration against resistive decay, not all transient amplification or field induced by an external source. Important restrictions include Cowling anti-dynamo theorem, the toroidal-velocity anti-dynamo theorem, and the planar anti-dynamo theorem. Backus' necessary condition for dynamo action instead bounds the stretching required for possible regeneration.

For a solenoidal three-component velocity depending on two periodic coordinates and a zero-mean magnetic field with the same dependence, the in-plane flux potential satisfies a homogeneous advection-diffusion equation. The Poincaré inequality gives ⟨A2⟩≤⟨A02​⟩e−2ηk2t. Integrating the vertical stretching term by parts bounds it by Q⟨A2⟩⟨∣∇Bz​∣2⟩​, where Q=∥∇uz​∥∞​. This permits transient generation by a nonzero vertical velocity while excluding sustained fields in this zero-mean, z-independent periodic class.

Planar anti-dynamo theorem

Words: 148 Articles: 1
Anti-dynamo theorem OurBigBook.com
In the standard two-dimensional setting, with planar velocity and magnetic field independent of the third coordinate and homogeneous energy-closed boundary conditions, the in-plane magnetic potential is an advected diffusing scalar. Its quadratic integral decreases, preventing regeneration of a dynamo field. A third field component independent of that coordinate also obeys a passive advection-diffusion equation. A flow depending on two coordinates but possessing a third velocity component is not covered by this strictly planar formulation.

Toroidal-velocity anti-dynamo theorem

Words: 74
Anti-dynamo theorem OurBigBook.com
For a bounded conducting sphere with uniform magnetic diffusivity and an insulating exterior, an incompressible flow tangent to concentric spheres cannot sustain dynamo action. Neither the velocity nor the magnetic field needs to be axisymmetric. The radial magnetic scalar has a homogeneous advection-diffusion equation, so the poloidal field decays. Once it is absent, the toroidal magnetic potential also has a homogeneous scalar advection-diffusion equation. This is a theorem about spherical flow geometry, not about a general cylindrical flow with an axial component.

Cowling anti-dynamo theorem

Words: 71
Anti-dynamo theorem OurBigBook.com
An isolated axisymmetric magnetic field cannot be sustained by ordinary resistive dynamo action with scalar positive magnetic diffusivity. Its poloidal magnetic flux function satisfies a homogeneous scalar advection-diffusion equation with no toroidal-to-poloidal source. The maximum principle excludes regeneration of that flux; after it decays the toroidal field has no lasting source either. This constrains the symmetry of the field. An axisymmetric flow may still generate a nonaxisymmetric field, and a turbulent mean-field electromotive force is not covered by the unmodified scalar-induction proof.

Magnetic concentration by an incompressible stagnation flow

Words: 122 Articles: 1
Dynamo action OurBigBook.com
The linear incompressible flow u=(−ωx,−ωy,2ωz) compresses the transverse plane and stretches the axial direction. An axial magnetic field obeys Bt​−ωrBr​=2ωB+ηΔ⊥​B. In ideal magnetohydrodynamics, its amplitude grows as e2ωt while its transverse width decreases as e−ωt. Thus its magnetic energy per unit axial length grows as e2ωt, even when opposite-polarity angular sectors give zero signed magnetic flux. Finite magnetic diffusivity limits the amplification of localized finite-flux Gaussian fields.

Backus' necessary condition for dynamo action (SR2/η≥π2)

Words: 140 Articles: 1
Dynamo action OurBigBook.com
For an isolated bounded conductor of uniform magnetic diffusivity η>0, contained in a sphere of radius R and matched to a decaying potential field in an insulating exterior, dynamo action requires maximum stretching rate S≥ηπ2/R2. Here S bounds the largest eigenvalue of the rate-of-strain tensor throughout the flow and time. Use fluid boundary conditions eliminating the stretching boundary term, for example a no-slip boundary condition, and no imposed energy input. The proof combines the magnetic free-decay spectral bound with the magnetic energy equation. This is a necessary condition, not a sufficiency criterion; changing magnetic boundary conditions changes the spectral constant.

Mean-field dynamo

Words: 2k Articles: 33
Dynamo action OurBigBook.com
A mean-field dynamo evolves a slowly varying spatially averaged magnetic field. Correlations of fluctuating velocity and magnetic field supply a mean-field electromotive force. Its local linear response includes the alpha effect; gradients of the mean field can contribute additional transport terms. The distinction between a uniform test field used to measure a response coefficient and a spatially varying mean field used to obtain growth is essential.

Dipole and quadrupole parity in a mean-field dynamo

Words: 182 Articles: 2
Mean-field dynamo OurBigBook.com
In a reflection-symmetric mean-field dynamo model, dipole parity can be represented by an even poloidal-potential amplitude and an odd toroidal-field amplitude; quadrupole parity reverses these assignments. The names describe the reflection classes appropriate to the model, not arbitrary electric multipoles. A complex amplitude P=A+iB allows a phase rotation by i to interchange the two classes when the unperturbed equations have that phase symmetry.
