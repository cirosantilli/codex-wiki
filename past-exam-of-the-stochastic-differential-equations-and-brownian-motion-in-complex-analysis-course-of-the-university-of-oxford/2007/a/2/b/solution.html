If X is Brownian motion with respect to the given filtration, independent Gaussian increments give the identity immediately.
Conversely, fix s<t and let Δ=Xt​−Xs​. Taking expectations gives its characteristic function e−ξ2(t−s)/2, so Δ∼N(0,t−s). To prove independence, for any A∈Fs​ define the finite measure νA​(E)=P(A∩{Δ∈E}). The conditional identity gives
∫eiξuνA​(du)=P(A)e−ξ2(t−s)/2.
(9)
Uniqueness of Fourier transforms of finite measures implies νA​=P(A)N(0,t−s). This is independence of Δ from Fs​. Iterating over times proves independent increments. Together with the stated continuity and X0​=0, this gives standard Brownian motion.
Here a process with the stated filtration is understood to be adapted, as required when saying it is Brownian with respect to that filtration. If adaptedness is not implicit, it must be added: an ordinary Brownian motion with a trivial filtration satisfies the displayed conditional characteristic function but is not adapted to that filtration.
