First, planar Brownian motion started away from zero almost surely never hits zero. To see this without confusing it with neighborhood recurrence, stop in an annulus with inner radius ϵ and fixed outer radius R>∣Z0​∣. The preceding logarithmic calculation gives probability log(R/∣Z0​∣)/log(R/ϵ) of reaching the inner circle first. A hit of zero before the outer exit would require all these inner hits, so its probability is zero as ϵ↓0. On every finite path interval the continuous path is bounded; a countable union over integer outer radii and integer times proves the all-time assertion.
Consequently the integrand 1/Z is locally square integrable: on every compact time interval the nonvanishing continuous path has a positive minimum modulus. Write Z=X+iY with independent standard real coordinates. Complex quadratic covariation satisfies d[Z,Z]=dt−dt+2id[X,Y]=0, so analytic Itô's lemma has no second-derivative drift. For L=∫Z−1dZ one likewise has d[L,L]=0, and d[e−L,Z]=0. The product rule therefore gives
d(Zt​e−Lt​)=e−Lt​dZt​−Zt​e−Lt​dLt​=0.
(32)
Its initial value is Z0​, proving, simultaneously for all times by continuity,
Zt​=Z0​eLt​.​
(33)
Thus ℜLt​=log(∣Zt​∣/∣Z0​∣) and ℑLt​ is the continuous accumulated change of complex argument of the path about zero, measured in radians. It is a winding angle, without reduction modulo 2π. A single-valued logarithm on the whole punctured plane is unnecessary; the path has a continuous stochastic logarithm of planar Brownian motion.
