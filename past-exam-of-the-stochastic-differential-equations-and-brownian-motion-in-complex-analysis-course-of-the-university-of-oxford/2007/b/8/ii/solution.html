Using the Cauchy distribution from the preceding part gives one common probability space for all starting points:
ϕ(x+iy)=E[f(x+yC)](y>0).
(41)
As (x,y)→(0,0) with y>0, x+yC→0 almost surely, since C is a finite real random variable almost surely. Continuity of f at zero gives f(x+yC)→f(0) almost surely, and boundedness supplies an integrable constant bound. Dominated convergence theorem therefore proves
z→0ℑz>0​lim​ϕ(z)=f(0)=ϕ(0).​
(42)
The argument applies to every sequence approaching zero, including tangential approaches. Along the real boundary the extension is already f, which is continuous. Hence the extension is continuous at zero in the closed half-plane. No finite moment of the Cauchy distribution is required.
