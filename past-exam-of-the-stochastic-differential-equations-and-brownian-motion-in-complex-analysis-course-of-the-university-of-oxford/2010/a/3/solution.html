Put Qt​=⟨M,M⟩t​ and F(x,q)=eσx−σ2q/2. In the two-variable Itô formula, Q has finite variation, while the only nonzero quadratic term is d⟨M⟩=dQ. Therefore
dF(Mt​,Qt​)=σFdMt​−21​σ2FdQt​+21​σ2FdQt​=σFdMt​.
(14)
This proves the stochastic exponential solves the equation. Its initial value is eσM0​; no normalization M0​=0 was stipulated.
The resulting process is initially a continuous local martingale. Since Qt​≥0 and ∣Mt​∣<C,
0<Zt​=eσMt​−σ2Qt​/2≤e∣σ∣C.
(15)
A bounded local martingale is a true martingale: apply the martingale identity to a localizing sequence and use dominated convergence in conditional expectations. The deterministic bound works on every finite horizon, proving Z is a martingale on every [0,T]. The bound on Q also gives a positive deterministic lower bound e−∣σ∣C−σ2C/2, although the upper bound already proves the martingale assertion.
