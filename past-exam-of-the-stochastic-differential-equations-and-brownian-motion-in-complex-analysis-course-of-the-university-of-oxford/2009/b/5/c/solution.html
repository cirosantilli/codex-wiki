Put Y=τ​ and V=Xτ∗​. Splitting according to whether V>δλ, the good-lambda bound for Brownian stopping time and maximum gives
P(Y>βλ)≤P(V>δλ)+ηP(Y>λ),η=β2−1δ2​.
(33)
Integrate against the positive measure dΦ(λ). The tail integral formula for expectation in Stieltjes form yields
EΦ(Y/β)≤EΦ(V/δ)+ηEΦ(Y).
(34)
By moderate growth, choose Kβ​ with Φ(βx)≤Kβ​Φ(x). Then
EΦ(Y)≤Kβ​EΦ(V/δ)+Kβ​ηEΦ(Y).
(35)
Fix β>1 and choose δ small enough that Kβ​η<1/2. Absorb the last term and apply moderate growth once more to the fixed dilation 1/δ. This proves
EΦ(τ​)≤CΦ​EΦ(Xτ∗​).​
(36)
To avoid subtracting infinities, perform the argument first for τ∧n, where Brownian maxima have all finite polynomial moments and moderate growth is at most polynomial, and then use monotone convergence. Applying the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem transfers this Brownian estimate to continuous local martingales, giving the lower-maximum side of the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities.
