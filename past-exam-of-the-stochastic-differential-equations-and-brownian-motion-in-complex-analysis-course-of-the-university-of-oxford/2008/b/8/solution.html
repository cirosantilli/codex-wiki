We know T<∞ almost surely. The Cauchy exit law from a Brownian half-plane assigns probability zero to BT​=0. On such a path, Bs​=0 throughout the compact interval [0,T], because it is in the open upper half-plane before exit and has nonzero endpoint. Its modulus therefore has a positive minimum and finite maximum. The displayed integrand is bounded on that interval, proving AT​<∞. The image Brownian motion exits D at this finite changed time.
The endpoint α=0 printed in the source is degenerate: z0=1, so its image is the singleton {1}, not an open domain, and the map is not conformal. If exit from that set is defined for Brownian motion started at one, its exit infimum is zero. It cannot be included in a nondegenerate conformal-domain argument.

For a fixed nondegenerate wedge, an uncentered inequality does hold with a constant allowed to depend on α as well as p. One rigorous outline for the polynomial class in this question is as follows. Separation of variables in a sector of radius R gives, for exit through its circular arc, the expansion
Preiθ​(hit radius R before the sides)=π4​∑n≥1n odd​​n1​(r/R)n/αsin(nθ/α).

If a nonzero polynomial has degree d, its real part has magnitude at least cRd on some fixed interior subarc for sufficiently large R. Therefore its maximal real-part pth moment is infinite whenever dp≥1/α. In that case the desired extended-real inequality is automatic.
