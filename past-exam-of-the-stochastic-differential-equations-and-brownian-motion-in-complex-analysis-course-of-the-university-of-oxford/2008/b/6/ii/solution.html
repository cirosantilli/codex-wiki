planar Brownian motion is recurrence of planar Brownian motion when, almost surely, it returns to every nonempty open neighborhood infinitely often, at arbitrarily large times. This does not mean that it hits each specified point; a prescribed point is polar for planar Brownian motion.
recurrence of planar Brownian motion and continuity imply that all the circle-hitting times Sk​,Tk​ in the question are finite. Starting on radius ϵ, it eventually visits the exterior of radius 2ϵ and must cross that circle; it later visits the interior of radius ϵ and must cross back. Each duration Δk​=Tk​−Sk​ is strictly positive by path continuity, because the two radii are separated.
At Sk​, the Strong Markov property restarts Brownian motion at its current point on the inner circle. rotational invariance of Brownian motion makes the law of the subsequent first exit time from the radius-2ϵ disk independent of that starting angle. Its conditional distribution given the past is therefore the same fixed law at every cycle, proving that the Δk​ are independent and identically distributed.
To prove divergence of the conformal clock for any nonconstant entire f, choose a point z0​ with f′(z0​)=0. By continuity, there exist ρ,c>0 such that ∣f′(z)∣2≥c throughout the closed disk ∣z−z0​∣≤2ρ. Use the same recurrence of planar Brownian motion circle cycles centered at z0​, with radii ρ,2ρ. On each segment from the inner circle to the outer circle, the path remains in that disk, giving
∫0∞​∣f′(Zs​)∣2ds≥c∑k≥0​(Tk​−Sk​).

The durations are positive iid variables. If their mean has not been computed, apply the strong law of large numbers to the bounded iid variables min(Δk​,1): their mean is strictly positive, so their sum, and hence ∑Δk​, diverges almost surely. Therefore
∫0∞​∣f′(Zs​)∣2ds=∞almost surely.​
(36)
Centering the disks at a noncritical point avoids an unjustified positive lower bound on ∣f′∣ around the original origin.
Write f=u+iv. Harmonicity and Itô's formula make u(Zt​),v(Zt​) continuous local martingales. The Cauchy-Riemann equations give equal brackets ∫0t​∣f′(Zs​)∣2ds and zero mutual bracket. Consequently Lévy's characterization of Brownian motion after time change of a continuous process makes W a planar Brownian motion started at f(Z0​), with no finite clock lifetime because the clock just proved is unbounded. The clock is strictly increasing: otherwise f′ would vanish along a nonconstant path interval, contradicting its isolated zeros.
For the notation in the source, ∣∇f∣2 is interpreted as the conformal scale ∣f′∣2=∣∇u∣2=∣∇v∣2. If it instead means the sum of the squared magnitudes of both complex coordinate derivatives, it is 2∣f′∣2, and the resulting W runs at half the usual Brownian speed. This convention does not change divergence of the integral. The mechanism is recurrent disk occupation makes an entire conformal clock infinite.
