Planar rotational diffusion (dϕ=2Dr​​dW)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Diffusion Rotational diffusion
OurBigBook.com Words: 301
For free planar turning with constant Dr​, no imposed torque and negligible inertia, model an unwrapped angle by the stochastic differential equation
dϕ=2Dr​​dW,Δϕ=ϕ(t)−ϕ(0),⟨Δϕ⟩=0,⟨(Δϕ)2⟩=2Dr​t.
(54)
Here W is standard one-dimensional Brownian motion, with increment variance dt, and brackets denote expected values over repeated realizations. The angular variance is conventionally reported in rad2. “Unwrapped” means recording accumulated turns continuously, rather than resetting the angle at each revolution. Directly subtracting wrapped camera angles creates artificial jumps; for an unmarked ellipsoid the wrapping period is π, and resolving successive turns additionally requires sufficiently frequent images.
For a planar arrow u=(cosϕ,sinϕ), the orientational autocorrelation is
Carrow​(t)=⟨u(t)⋅u(0)⟩=⟨cosΔϕ⟩=e−Dr​t.
(55)
For an unoriented planar axis, use an autocorrelation unchanged by replacing either endpoint angle by that angle plus π:
Caxis​(t)=⟨cos(2Δϕ)⟩=e−4Dr​t.
(56)
The same Dr​ thus gives arrow and axis correlation times 1/Dr​ and 1/(4Dr​), respectively, where a correlation time here is the lag at which the relevant curve reaches 1/e. A short explanation is enough: Δϕ has a Gaussian distribution of variance 2Dr​t, so its characteristic function gives ⟨cos(nΔϕ)⟩=e−n2Dr​t. The integer n selects what the observable can distinguish; it does not change the underlying angular spreading.
For an original numerical example, choose Dr​=0.25s−1. After 2s, the angular variance is 1rad2 and its root mean square displacement is 1rad, although the mean displacement is zero. The planar arrow autocorrelation is e−0.5≃0.607, while the planar axis autocorrelation is e−2≃0.135. Calling both “the orientation correlation” without specifying the observable would hide a factor of four in decay rate.
