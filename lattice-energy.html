Lattice energy (ΔHlatt,diss​)

 Home Chemistry Chemical bond Ionic bonding
OurBigBook.com Words: 873 Articles: 3
A lattice energy needs a reaction, a sign convention, and a stated amount. Using the lattice-dissociation enthalpy convention for sodium chloride,
NaCl(s)⟶Na+(g)+Cl−(g),ΔHlatt,diss​>0.
(5)
The reverse lattice-formation enthalpy is negative with the same magnitude when initial and final states match. Chemistry 2e gives 769kJmol−1 for this separation, per mole of sodium chloride formula units. This is about 7.97 electronvolts per formula unit, not per individual ion. It concerns separated gaseous ions, rather than neutral chemical atoms, molten salt, or hydrated ions in water.[ref]
Also distinguish an internal energy change from an enthalpy change: H=U+pV. A static point-charge calculation omits motion and thermal contributions. Reversing a reaction reverses its energy change; replacing internal energy with enthalpy additionally changes the defined quantity. The Born-Haber cycle infers a lattice-formation enthalpy by combining other enthalpies, ionization energies, and electron affinities. These imagined stages are a thermodynamic accounting path, not a claim about the actual sequence of a chemical reaction.[ref]
