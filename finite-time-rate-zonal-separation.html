Finite-time rate-zonal separation

 Home Biology Centrifugation Density-gradient centrifugation Rate-zonal centrifugation
OurBigBook.com Words: 267
Consider two dilute populations of spheres with the same buoyant density and radii b and 2b. Adopt the fixed linear density gradient and constant local centrifugal acceleration of linear-gradient isopycnic focusing, with constant dynamic viscosity and negligible diffusion. Stokes's law makes V/f proportional to b2, so doubling radius multiplies the migration rate parameter by four, although the mass increases eightfold.
Use u=z/L, τ=t/T and q=(ρb​−ρ0​)/(GL)=2. The liquid occupies 0≤u≤1, so the matching density position lies beyond its outer boundary. Both populations begin at u(0)=0.1; choose λA​=0.15 and λB​=0.6. Their band-center trajectories are
uA​(τ)=2−1.9e−0.15τ,uB​(τ)=2−1.9e−0.6τ.
(6)
At τ=0.5, they lie at approximately 0.2373 and 0.5924; at τ=1, at 0.3647 and 0.9573. Equal buoyant density has not prevented a useful finite-time separation. Solving uB​(τ)=1 using the natural logarithm gives first boundary arrival at τ=ln(1.9)/0.6≃1.0698. The corresponding arrival for A is ln(1.9)/0.15≃4.2790. Trajectories describe migration only until contact with the boundary; subsequent collection into a centrifugation pellet is not represented by extending the curve beyond u=1.
This is the useful contrast with isopycnic centrifugation: stopping time helps determine the recovered positions in rate-zonal centrifugation, whereas a stationary matching density position sets the limiting band center in isopycnic centrifugation. Real resolution also depends on initial zone width, diffusion and variations in particle properties; distinct calculated centers alone do not prove that fractions will be pure.
