Write N=⟨a⟩. The relation bab−1=a2 lets every element be expressed as aibj with 0≤i<7 and 0≤j<3. There are 21 such expressions, so the assumption ∣G∣=21 makes them distinct. In particular a has order seven, b has order three, and N is a normal subgroup. This is the nonabelian group of order 21, the semidirect product C7​⋊C3​ with the action given by multiplication by two modulo seven.
Conjugation by a fixes N, while conjugation by b sends ai to a2i. The nonidentity conjugacy classes inside N are therefore
K1​={a,a2,a4},K2​={a3,a5,a6}.
(34)
For the other elements, direct calculation gives
at(aibj)a−t=ai+(1−2j)tbj.
(35)
When j=1 or 2, the coefficient 1−2j is nonzero modulo seven, so letting t vary gives every member of the coset Nbj. These cosets cannot merge under conjugation, since G/N is an abelian group and conjugation acts trivially on that quotient group. Thus the five conjugacy classes have representatives and sizes
representativeclass size​11​a3​a33​b7​b27​​.
(36)
To find the linear characters, note that the commutator subgroup G′ contains bab−1a−1=a. The quotient group G/N≅C3​ is abelian, so G′⊆N; hence G′=N. The abelianization is C3​. With ω=e2πi/3, its three linear characters are
λj​(a)=1,λj​(b)=ωj,j=0,1,2.
(37)
This exhausts the linear characters because one-dimensional characters factor through the abelianization.
For the remaining irreducible characters, put ζ=e2πi/7 and take the linear characters θt​ of N defined by θt​(a)=ζt. The six nontrivial linear characters form two orbits under conjugation, with exponent sets {1,2,4} and {3,5,6}. Define the induced characters
ψ=IndNG​θ1​,ψ​=IndNG​θ3​.
(38)
For ai∈N, the character formula for an induced representation gives
ψ(ai)=ζi+ζ2i+ζ4i,ψ​(ai)=ζ3i+ζ5i+ζ6i.
(39)
Both vanish outside N, since no element outside a normal subgroup can be conjugate into it. This is induction from an abelian normal subgroup with a free character orbit. For completeness, its irreducibility here follows directly from Frobenius reciprocity and character orthogonality on the cyclic group N:
⟨ψ,ψ⟩G​=⟨θ1​,ResNG​ψ⟩N​=⟨θ1​,θ1​+θ2​+θ4​⟩N​=1.
(40)
The same calculation with θ3​,θ5​,θ6​ proves that ψ​ is an irreducible character.
Let
α=ζ+ζ2+ζ4,β=ζ3+ζ5+ζ6=α.
(41)
The sum of the nontrivial seventh roots of unity gives α+β=−1. Squaring and reducing exponents modulo seven gives
α2=α+2β=−α−2.
(42)
Thus α,β are the two polynomial roots of T2+T+2, and
α=2−1+i7​​,β=2−1−i7​​.
(43)
To fix the signs with our specified ζ, observe that Imα=sin(2π/7)+sin(3π/7)−sin(π/7)>0, using the increase of sine on (0,π/2). These Gaussian periods for seventh roots of unity make the answer explicit.
The resulting character table is

(44)
The two degree-three irreducible characters are distinct because α=β. Together with the three linear characters, they exhaust the irreducible characters: the sum of squares of irreducible degrees is 3⋅12+2⋅32=21=∣G∣. Equivalently, we have five distinct irreducible characters for the five conjugacy classes.
As a check on part (b), inversion interchanges K1​ and K2​, and also interchanges the two conjugacy classes outside N. Only the identity conjugacy class is real. Correspondingly λ0​ is the only real-valued character among the irreducible characters; λ1​,λ2​ and ψ,ψ​ are two conjugate pairs.
