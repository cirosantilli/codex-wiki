Hermitian Rayleigh quotient range ([λmin​,λmax​])

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Rayleigh quotient
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
For a finite-dimensional Hermitian matrix A, the Rayleigh quotient has precisely the range [λmin​,λmax​], where the endpoints are its extreme eigenvalues. The finite-dimensional spectral theorem writes a nonzero vector as x=∑j​cj​ej​ in an orthonormal eigenbasis, giving
RA​(x)=∑j​λj​wj​,wj​=∑k​∣ck​∣2∣cj​∣2​,wj​≥0,∑j​wj​=1.
(92)
Thus the quotient is a convex combination of eigenvalues. The bounds follow immediately, and equality at either endpoint means that x lies in the corresponding extreme eigenspace, including any repeated eigenvalue. Every intermediate value occurs: for orthonormal extreme eigenvectors, x=1−t​emin​+t​emax​ gives RA​(x)=(1−t)λmin​+tλmax​ for 0≤t≤1. If all eigenvalues are equal, the quotient is constant.
The Hermitian hypothesis matters. The real matrix B=(10​41​) has only the eigenvalue 1, but (1,1)B(1,1)T/2=3. Its quotient is governed by the symmetric part of a matrix (B+BT)/2, not by a real orthonormal eigenbasis of B. A nonsymmetric matrix therefore need not obey the displayed eigenvalue bounds.
