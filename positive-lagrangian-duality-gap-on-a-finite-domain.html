Positive Lagrangian duality gap on a finite domain (p∗​=3,d∗​=0)

OurBigBook.com Words: 249
A nonconvex primal problem can have a positive duality gap even when both optima are attained. Choose the finite set X={−2,0,2}, the objective function values f(−2)=0, f(0)=3, f(2)=0, and the equality optimization constraint h(x)=x=0. The only feasible point is 0, so p∗​=3.
With unrestricted equality Lagrange multiplier ν, the three possible optimization Lagrangian values and their minimum are
L(−2,ν)=−2ν,L(0,ν)=3,L(2,ν)=2ν,q(ν)=min{−2ν,3,2ν}=−2∣ν∣.
(44)
The concave Lagrange dual function has unique maximizer ν∗​=0, with d∗​=0. Thus the duality gap is 3. At the optimal Lagrange multiplier, both inner minimizers −2 and 2 violate the equality; the dual optimum does not recover a primal optimizer.
Why can no slope close the gap? Any affine function a+bx lying below the two endpoint costs obeys a−2b≤0 and a+2b≤0. Adding gives a≤0, so its value at residual zero cannot reach the feasible cost 3. A convex combination of the endpoint cost-residual pairs with equal weights has residual zero and average cost zero. This averaged pair is unattainable: the actual decision x=0 costs 3. This geometry explains precisely what the dual bounds fail to distinguish. Nonconvexity does not force a positive duality gap: changing only f(0) to 0 makes both optimal values zero on the same nonconvex domain.
