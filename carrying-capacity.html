Carrying capacity (K)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Population dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
A carrying capacity is an abundance scale at which a specified environment's net population growth vanishes. In the logistic growth equation, positive unharvested populations approach K. It depends on the species, resources and environment rather than being an immutable universal population limit.
The useful distinction is between a growth rate and a sustainable abundance. In a logistic differential equation, r controls how quickly a population responds, while the carrying capacity K sets the positive equilibrium point. Increasing r with K fixed accelerates approach without raising that equilibrium. Increasing K changes the destination. The logistic differential equation diagrams show populations approaching the same carrying capacity from below and above.
In ecological use, the carrying capacity describes what a particular environment can support under specified conditions. Nutrient supply, available space, and accumulated waste can change those conditions; it is not a permanent property of a species alone. A population temporarily above a carrying capacity is possible. In the simplest constant-K logistic growth equation, its net growth becomes negative; real population dynamics can include delays, seasonal variation, and changing resources that the basic mathematical model omits.
