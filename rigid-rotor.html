Rigid rotor

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A rigid rotor models rotational motion with fixed internal separations. Its use in the synthetic Stark effect figure omits vibrational motion and additional interactions in real molecules.
Rigidity fixes the molecular geometry, not the orientation, and does not require a spherical mass distribution. Remove motion of the centre of mass, choose principal axes of the inertia tensor, and write the classical rotational kinetic energy as
Trot​=21​(Ia​ωa2​+Ib​ωb2​+Ic​ωc2​)=2Ia​La2​​+2Ib​Lb2​​+2Ic​Lc2​​.
(4)
Here Ia​,Ib​,Ic​ are the principal moments of inertia, ωa​,ωb​,ωc​ components of angular velocity, and La​,Lb​,Lc​ components of angular momentum. Quantization replaces these last components by angular momentum operators. Different principal moments of inertia produce different spectra: the single-constant linear rigid rotor is a special case, not a formula for every molecule.[ref]
