Functor (F:C→D)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Category theory Category
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 96
A functor assigns an object of a category F(A) to each object A and a categorical morphism F(f):F(A)→F(B) to each arrow f:A→B. It preserves the rules for composing arrows:
F(1A​)=1F(A)​,F(g∘f)=F(g)∘F(f).
(1)
For example, the forgetful functor from the category of groups to the Category of sets keeps the underlying sets and maps of elements while forgetting the group operations. A group homomorphism remains a function, and composing the underlying functions gives the underlying map of the composite group homomorphism.
Another example sends a set X to its power set P(X). It sends a function f:X→Y to the function P(f):P(X)→P(Y) defined by S↦f(S), the image of the subset S. For f(x)=x+1 and g(x)=2x, the subset {0,2} goes to {1,3} and then {2,6}; applying the composite g∘f directly also gives {2,6}. The equality of these routes expresses preservation of composition in a category.

Bifunctor (F:C×D→E)

Words: 70
Functor OurBigBook.com
A bifunctor is a functor on the product of two categories. It assigns F(c,d) to each pair of objects and F(u,v) to a pair of morphisms, preserving identity morphisms and composition in a category. Equivalently, it is functorial separately in each argument, with the two actions commuting: F(1,v)F(u,1)=F(u,1)F(1,v) whenever the expressions have the corresponding sources and targets. The product category has pairs as objects, pairs of morphisms as arrows, and componentwise composition in a category. A monoidal tensor product is a bifunctor from a category times itself.

Discrete opfibration (F:C→D)

Words: 169 Articles: 1
Functor OurBigBook.com
A functor with a unique lift, from each specified object c, of every arrow Fc→d. The lifted arrow's target is part of the uniqueness requirement. This is dual to a discrete fibration. Lifting a cocone of a finite connected category gives a cocone upstairs: lifts of its legs have a common target because uniqueness identifies targets along every diagram arrow. Thus a discrete opfibration over a weakly filtered category has weakly filtered domain, including the possibility of an empty domain.

Connected components of discrete-opfibration pullbacks (π0​(A×D​B)≅π0​(A)×π0​(D)​π0​(B))

Words: 94
Discrete opfibration OurBigBook.com
For discrete opfibrations A→D←B with weakly filtered categories, the map taking a pair to its connected components of a category is a bijection. For surjectivity, send two base objects in the same component to a common target and lift both arrows. For injectivity, choose common targets upstairs for two representatives; a cocone over the resulting finite connected diagram downstairs makes their lifted composites agree. Hence the representatives have a common target in the pullback in a category. Over a filtered category, the component functor on the slice of discrete opfibrations preserves all finite categorical limits: it preserves pullbacks and takes the terminal object to a singleton.

Full functor

Words: 52
Functor OurBigBook.com
A functor F:C→D is full when each function C(A,B)→D(FA,FB) is surjective. It is a faithful functor when each is injective. A full and faithful functor identifies its source, up to equivalence, with a full subcategory of the target. Fullness only concerns morphisms between image objects; it does not require every target object to lie in the image.

Flat functor (F:C→E)

Words: 97 Articles: 1
Functor OurBigBook.com
A covariant functor into a Grothendieck topos is flat when its tensor extension from presheaves preserves finite limits. For set values, its category of elements with arrows carrying source elements to target elements is cofiltered; equivalently the functor is a filtered colimit of covariant representables. If the indexing category has finite limits, flatness is equivalent to preservation of finite limits. For a site, cover-to-joint-epimorphism continuity adds the condition needed by the Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms.

Flat functor and finite-limit preservation (C finitely complete⟹(F flat⟺F left exact))

Words: 36
Flat functor OurBigBook.com
For a small category with finite limits, a set-valued functor preserving them has a category of elements with finite limits, so its opposite is a filtered category. The covariant density presentation is therefore a filtered colimit of representable functors. Conversely, representable functors preserve categorical limits, and filtered colimits commute with finite limits in sets, proving the criterion.

Kan extension (Lanf​,Ranf​)

Words: 36
Functor OurBigBook.com
A left Kan extension is left adjoint to precomposition, when it exists; a Right Kan extension is right adjoint. For small indexing categories and set-valued functors, both exist by comma-category colimit and limit formulas. These two adjoints give the essential geometric morphism induced by a functor between presheaf indexing categories.

Finite-limit-preserving functor

Words: 80 Articles: 1
Functor OurBigBook.com
A finite-limit-preserving functor preserves a terminal object and pullbacks, equivalently all finite limits. It is also called left exact. Such functors from a Cartesian syntactic category to a finite-limit category correspond to models of the associated Cartesian theory.

Regular functor

Words: 46
Finite-limit-preserving functor OurBigBook.com
A functor between regular categories is regular if it preserves finite limits and regular epimorphisms. It then preserves image factorizations, since finite-limit preservation preserves monomorphisms. A representable functor C(P,−) is regular exactly when P is projective with respect to regular epimorphisms. Regularity of a functor does not alone imply faithfulness or reflection of isomorphisms.

Conservative functor

Words: 38
Functor OurBigBook.com
A functor is conservative when it reflects isomorphisms: if F(f) is invertible then f is invertible. It need not be full or faithful functor. The crude monadicity theorem uses this reflection property to lift invertibility of the comparison-adjunction counit from the base category.

Limit-reflecting functor

Words: 213 Articles: 3
Functor OurBigBook.com
For a specified diagram shape, a functor reflects categorical limits when a cone over a diagram is limiting whenever its image is limiting. This condition tests an existing categorical cone. It does not assert that every limiting categorical cone in the codomain has a lift.

Limits reflected by full and faithful functors

Words: 47
Limit-reflecting functor OurBigBook.com
A full and faithful functor reflects every existing shape of categorical limit. If the image of a cone is limiting, apply its universal property to the image of any competing cone. Fullness lifts the unique mediating morphism; faithfulness proves the cone equations and uniqueness of the lift. No essential-surjectivity hypothesis is needed.

Limit-creating functor

Words: 131 Articles: 1
Limit-reflecting functor OurBigBook.com
A functor creates categorical limits of a specified shape when every given limiting categorical cone over an image diagram uniquely lifts to a categorical cone over the original diagram, and the lift is limiting. This is stronger than just being a limit-reflecting functor. Forgetting arrows from a functor category creates its pointwise limits in a functor category.

Creation of limits up to isomorphism

Words: 89
Limit-creating functor OurBigBook.com
An equivalence-invariant lifting property permits the underlying cone of the lift to be identified with the chosen cone by an isomorphism, rather than requiring literal equality. Lifts are unique up to a unique isomorphism compatible with that identification. A monadic adjunction defined by equivalence of its comparison functor has this property by monad algebra forgetful functor creates limits. Literal creation follows if the comparison is an isomorphism over the base, or suitable strict transport of objects is part of the convention. An equivalence alone does not guarantee a literally prescribed underlying object lies in the image.

Colimit-preserving functor

Words: 49
Functor OurBigBook.com
Relative to a specified class of diagram shapes, a functor preserves colimits when it takes each existing colimit cocone of those shapes to a colimit cocone. A left adjoint preserves all existing colimits, including large ones when their universal properties are meaningful. Small-colimit preservation alone does not imply preservation of a class-indexed colimit.

Limit-preserving functor

Words: 109 Articles: 1
Functor OurBigBook.com
Relative to a specified class of diagram shapes, a functor preserves limits when it takes each existing categorical limit cone of those shapes to a categorical limit cone. The usual completeness and general adjoint functor theorem hypotheses refer to small shapes; a possibly large diagram requires an explicit preservation hypothesis covering its shape.

Equalizer preservation by pullback-preserving functors

Words: 64
Limit-preserving functor OurBigBook.com
If C has finite categorical limits, every functor F:C→D preserving pullbacks in a category also preserves equalizers, even when it does not preserve a terminal object. Factor it through the slice category D/F1 by sending A to F(A→1). This lifted functor preserves pullbacks because the slice forgetful functor reflects them; it also preserves the terminal object, so it preserves finite limits. The slice forgetful functor preserves equalizers, completing the proof.

An endofunctor is a functor from a category to itself. A monad adds a unit and multiplication to an endofunctor, satisfying the unit and associativity laws.

Comonad ((G,ϵ,δ))

Words: 399 Articles: 8
Endofunctor OurBigBook.com
A comonad is an endofunctor G with natural transformations ϵ:G→1 and δ:G→G2 satisfying Gϵδ=ϵG​δ=1G​ and Gδδ=δG​δ. It is the categorical dual of a monad. Its coalgebras for a comonad carry compatible maps into their image under G.

Cartesian comonad ((G,ε,δ))

Words: 37
Comonad OurBigBook.com
In the finite-limit setting, a Cartesian comonad is a comonad whose underlying endofunctor preserves finite limits. Its category of coalgebras for a comonad has finite limits created by the forgetful functor. On an elementary topos, equalizer constructions of exponentials in a coalgebra topos and the subobject classifier of a coalgebra topos make that coalgebra category an elementary topos.

Idempotent comonad (δ:G≅G2)

Comonad OurBigBook.com
A comonad is idempotent when its comultiplication G→G2 is invertible. Its coalgebra category identifies with the coreflective subcategory of objects on which the counit is invertible. A coreflective inclusion produces such a comonad.

For a comonad, a coalgebra is a map a:A→GA satisfying ϵA​a=1A​ and δA​a=Gaa. A morphism f:(A,a)→(B,b) obeys bf=Gfa. This is distinct from the tensor-based notion of a coalgebra in algebra.

The category EG consists of coalgebras for a comonad and their structure-preserving morphisms. Its forgetful functor has the cofree coalgebra as right adjoint. For a comonad, the cofree coalgebra on X is (GX,δX​). A map UA→X transposes to the coalgebra morphism Gfa:A→GX. This gives the adjunction U⊣R with the forgetful functor.

Finite-limit-preserving set-valued functor

Words: 55
Functor OurBigBook.com
A functor F:C→Set is finite-limit-preserving if it carries each finite limit cone to a limiting cone. When C is a small category with finite limits, this is equivalent to F being a filtered colimit in a category of covariant representable functors, and to the opposite of its category of elements being a filtered category. It is also equivalent to every comma category (S↓F) having finite limits, for all sets S.

Equivalence of categories

Words: 168 Articles: 2
Functor OurBigBook.com
An equivalence consists of functors F:C→D and G:D→C with natural transformation isomorphisms GF≅1C​ and FG≅1D​. A full and faithful functor which is essentially surjective, meaning every target object is isomorphic to an image object, is part of an equivalence under the usual choice convention. An isomorphism of categories is stronger: it has a strictly inverse functor.

Essential surjectivity

Words: 48
Equivalence of categories OurBigBook.com
A functor F:C→D is essentially surjective when every object of D is isomorphic to FA for some A∈C. Together with full and faithful, this characterizes an equivalence of categories under the appropriate axiom of choice convention. For large categories, choosing a quasi-inverse on all objects requires a universe or a class-choice convention.

Isomorphism of categories

Words: 64
Equivalence of categories OurBigBook.com
An isomorphism of categories is a functor with a strictly inverse functor. It is equivalently bijective on objects and on each hom-set. An equivalence of categories only requires inverse composites up to invertible natural transformations. For example, the category of partial functions and the category of pointed sets are equivalent but their actual object collections prevent an isomorphism: the former has one zero object, the empty set, while the latter has distinct singleton pointed set objects that are all zero objects.

Pointwise monomorphism in a set-valued functor category (α monic⟺∀A, αA​ injective)

Words: 61
Functor category OurBigBook.com
A natural transformation between functors to the Category of sets is a monomorphism in the functor category exactly when every component is an injective function. Componentwise cancellation proves sufficiency. For necessity, the Yoneda lemma converts two elements with equal component images into two transformations from a covariant representable functor; monicity cancels these transformations, forcing the elements to coincide. The argument remains valid for a locally small category in an ambient universe where the functor category is formed.

Pointwise limits in a functor category ((limj​Hj​)(d)=limj​Hj​(d))

Words: 52
Functor category OurBigBook.com
For small diagrams with complete codomain, choose each objectwise categorical limit. For u:d→d′, its projections uniquely define the arrow L(u) through pj​(d′)L(u)=Hj​(u)pj​(d). The universal property proves identity and composition laws. The same projection calculation makes every categorical cone factorization a natural transformation. Hence these are genuine categorical limits in the functor category, and the objectwise forgetful functor is a limit-creating functor.

Constant diagram functor (ΔJ​:C→[J,C])

Functor category OurBigBook.com
The constant diagram functor sends C to the functor on J with constant object value C and identity arrow values; a morphism f becomes the natural transformation with every component f. When all J-shaped categorical limits exist, it is a left adjoint to the limit functor.

Adjoints to constant presheaves on open sets (ev∅​⊣Δ⊣evS​)

Words: 183 Articles: 1
Constant diagram functor OurBigBook.com
For a topological space S, let ΔA be the constant presheaf of sets on its open-set category, with identity restriction maps, including the value at the empty open set. A natural transformation P→ΔA is determined by its map P(∅)→A; its component at U composes with P(U)→P(∅). A transformation ΔA→P is determined by A→P(S); its component at U restricts sections from S. These two bijections prove the displayed adjunctions. This concerns presheaves, so no sheafification is involved.
Five adjoints for constant presheaves on open sets (Λ⊣ev∅​⊣Δ⊣evS​⊣∇)
Words: 103
Adjoints to constant presheaves on open sets OurBigBook.com
For a nonempty topological space S, the constant diagram functor on its open-set presheaf category lies in this five-term adjunction chain. The leftmost functor sends A to the categorical presheaf equal to A at the empty open set and empty elsewhere. The rightmost sends A to the presheaf equal to A at S and a singleton elsewhere. A natural transformation from the former is determined by its empty-open component; a transformation into the latter is determined by its whole-space component. The middle adjunctions follow from restriction maps to the empty open set and from the whole space. Neither endpoint extends: the leftmost functor fails to preserve the terminal object, and the rightmost fails to preserve the initial object.

Canonical colimit presentation of a presheaf (P≅colim(c,x)∈∫P​Hc​)

Words: 70
Presheaf category OurBigBook.com
For a categorical presheaf P on a small category, its category of elements has objects (c,x) with x∈P(c) and arrows u:(c,x)→(d,y) satisfying P(u)y=x. The canonical natural transformation Hc​→P sends v:a→c to P(v)x. These maps exhibit P as the displayed colimit. At a, a representative (c,x,v) maps to P(v)x, with inverse z↦(a,z,1a​). The arrow v:(a,P(v)x)→(c,x) identifies every representative with its canonical inverse image, proving the bijection and the colimit universal property.

Free cocompletion (C=[Cop,Set])

Words: 106
Presheaf category OurBigBook.com
For a small category C, its presheaf category is the free enlargement under small colimits. Each categorical presheaf P is the colimit of the diagram sending (C,x)∈∫P to the representable presheaf yC. The map at (C,x) sends f:A→C to P(f)x. It is surjective at each A because z∈P(A) is the image of 1A​ at (A,z). If two representatives have the same image z, they are both identified with the representative 1A​ at (A,z) by arrows in the category of elements, proving injectivity. Thus P≅colim(C,x)∈∫P​yC. For a cocomplete target, a functor on C extends by these colimits, uniquely up to natural isomorphism among colimit-preserving extensions.

Grothendieck topology (J)

Words: 911 Articles: 16
Presheaf category OurBigBook.com
A Grothendieck topology assigns covering sieves to each object. The maximal sieve covers; pulling back a covering sieve gives a covering sieve; and a sieve locally covering along all arrows of a covering sieve itself covers. It defines the sheaves on a site. Families of arrows generate sieves and can be used to generate the topology.
Double-negation topology (J(A)={R:∀f:B→A, f∗R=∅})
Words: 149 Articles: 1
Grothendieck topology OurBigBook.com
The dense sieves form the double-negation Grothendieck topology. Its sheaf topos is a Boolean topos: the union of a subobject with its pseudocomplement is dense and thus becomes the whole object after sheafification. This need not be the atomic topology, which requires every nonempty sieve to be dense.
Separating tower site with vanishing product (An​↠1,∏n​An​=0)
Words: 104
Double-negation topology OurBigBook.com
Consider a site indexed by natural-number objects with arrows only downwards, the cancellation condition fh=gk⇒f=g for equally typed f,g, and the separation condition that arrows f:m→n, g:m→n+1 admit h,k:m+1→m with fh=fk but gh=gk. Its dense-sieve sheaf topos is a Boolean topos and a two-valued topos. The representables are separated. No nonempty partial map yn​→yn+1​ exists, by the separation condition and naturality. The associated sheaves An​ consequently admit no maps yn​→An+1​. Each has nonzero support and maps epimorphically to one, but their product has an empty value at every stage m, because its Am+1​ factor does.
Zariski coverage on finitely presented rings
Words: 60
Grothendieck topology OurBigBook.com
On the opposite of the category of finitely presented unital commutative rings, a basic covering family is A→A[fi−1​], where finitely many fi​ generate the unit ideal. Include the empty cover of the zero ring. Localization commutes with base change, so these families define a coverage on a category. Their sheaf topos classifies the coherent theory of local rings. This coverage is subcanonical: representable functors obey the usual localization gluing equations.
Coverage on a category
Words: 199 Articles: 2
Grothendieck topology OurBigBook.com
A coverage specifies covering families on a small category, stable under refinement along arrows: for a covering family into C and f:B→C, there is a covering family into B whose composites with f factor through the original family. Passing to generated sieves and imposing maximality and local transitivity gives its associated Grothendieck topology, with the same sheaves on a site. One can equivalently specify the saturated covering sieves directly. Rigidity refers to these covering sieves, so it is unchanged by replacing a family presentation by its generated topology.
Rigid coverage
Words: 58

A coverage on a category is rigid when each object is covered by the sieve generated by arrows from J-irreducible objects of a site. Restriction to their full subcategory identifies the sheaves on a site with its presheaf category. A presheaf on that subcategory extends by the right Kan extension C↦Nat(yC∣D​,F): every covering sieve restricts to the full representable on D, which proves the sheaf condition directly.
J-irreducible object of a site (J(C)={yC})
Words: 56
Coverage on a category OurBigBook.com
An object of a site is J-irreducible when its only covering sieve on a category is the maximal sieve. In particular the empty sieve cannot cover it. Every arrow from a J-irreducible object belongs to any covering sieve on its codomain: the pulled-back sieve must contain the identity. This makes the induced topology on the full subcategory of J-irreducible objects trivial.
