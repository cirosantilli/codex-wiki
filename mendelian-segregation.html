Mendelian segregation (Aa×Aa:41​AA, 21​Aa, 41​aa)

 Home Biology Genetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 782 Articles: 2
In ordinary diploid meiosis, the two copies at a genetic locus separate: each gamete receives one copy. With fair transmission, a heterozygote Aa produces gametes carrying A or a with probability 1/2 each. This is a transmission mechanism conditional on a parental genotype, rather than a statement about how common either allele is in a population.
For the one-locus cross Aa×Aa, assume independent parental transmissions, no new mutation, and no differences in fertilization or survival between the resulting genotypes. Multiplying the two gamete probabilities gives four equally likely ordered outcomes: AA,Aa,aA,aa, each with probability 1/4. Combining the two orders of the heterozygote gives
P(AA)=0.25,P(Aa)=0.50,P(aa)=0.25.
(2)
Only under the additional ideal model of complete dominance of A, complete penetrance, and no environmental changes to the scored characteristic do AA and Aa share the dominant phenotype. Its probability is then 0.25+0.50=0.75. The inheritance calculation alone does not determine a phenotype or predict a generic disease risk.
These are probabilities, not a requirement that every four offspring contain one AA, two Aa and one aa. For 100 independent offspring under the ideal model, the expected values of the three counts are 25,50,25; the aa count has a binomial distribution with standard deviation 100(0.25)(0.75)​≈4.33. Each new transmission keeps the same conditional probabilities given the parental genotypes.
In his paper published in 1866, Mendel reported 5,474 round and 1,850 wrinkled pea seeds: 5474/7324≈0.7474 round, close to the ideal 0.75. Following descendants also distinguished two inherited classes within the round phenotype, illustrating why genotype cannot always be read from appearance.[ref] At several genetic loci, transmission patterns additionally depend on genetic linkage and genetic recombination.
