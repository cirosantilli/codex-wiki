Assume f∈Cn+1[a,b] and reorder the interpolation nodes increasingly as x0​<⋯<xn​. The polynomial interpolation error e=f−pn​ vanishes at all of them. Rolle's theorem supplies
ηi​∈(xi−1​,xi​),e′(ηi​)=0,i=1,…,n.
(25)
These n points are distinct because their containing intervals are disjoint. Thus q=pn′​ is a polynomial of polynomial degree at most n−1 that interpolates the function f′ at the interpolation nodes η1​,…,ηn​.
Apply the polynomial interpolation error theorem to f′ with these n interpolation nodes. Since the nth derivative of f′ is f(n+1), for every x∈[a,b] there is a ξx​∈(a,b) with
f′(x)−q(x)=n!f(n+1)(ξx​)​πn​(x),πn​(x)=i=1∏n​(x−ηi​).​
(26)
For x=ηi​ the equality is zero on both sides; otherwise the same auxiliary-function and repeated-Rolle's theorem proof applies. This also covers n=1, where q is a constant polynomial and the proof is the ordinary mean value theorem for f′.
Writing M=max[a,b]​∣f(n+1)∣ gives the genuine error bound
∣f′(x)−q(x)∣≤n!M​i=1∏n​∣x−ηi​∣≤n!M​(b−a)n.​
(27)
The first formula is sharper, but its intermediate interpolation nodes depend on f; the last bound eliminates those unknown locations. This is derivative interpolation error from Rolle points.
