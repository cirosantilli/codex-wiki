Write Pn​ for the vector space of polynomials of polynomial degree at most n, and put pn​(x)=∑j=0n​aj​xj. As the question calls the weighted integral an inner product, its finiteness is implicit; for example, a positive integrable weight w suffices for all the continuous functions involved here.
For every q∈Pn​, differentiating the squared weighted norm ∥f−pn​−tq∥w2​ at its minimum t=0 gives the orthogonality equations
⟨f−pn​,q⟩w​=0.
(5)
In particular, the requested linear system is
j=0∑n​aj​∫−11​w(x)xj+kdx=∫−11​w(x)f(x)xkdx,k=0,…,n.​
(6)
Its Gram matrix Gkj​=∫−11​w(x)xk+jdx is a symmetric positive-definite matrix: for any nonzero vector a,
aTGa=∫−11​w(x)(∑j=0n​aj​xj)2dx>0.
(7)
Indeed a nonzero polynomial is nonzero on some open interval, and the weight is positive. Therefore G is an invertible matrix, proving uniqueness. Conversely, if these equations hold, the Pythagorean theorem in an inner-product space gives, for every q∈Pn​,
∥f−(pn​+q)∥w2​=∥f−pn​∥w2​+∥q∥w2​,
(8)
so the solution really is the unique best approximation.
