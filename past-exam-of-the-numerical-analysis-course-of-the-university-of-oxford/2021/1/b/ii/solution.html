Let r=f−pn​ be the continuous function describing the error. The preceding orthogonality equations say
∫−11​w(x)r(x)q(x)dx=0(q∈Pn​).
(9)
If r vanishes identically, any n+1 distinct points work. If r already has at least n+1 distinct zeros, the assertion also follows. Suppose instead that it has at most n distinct zeros. Among its interior zeros, list those at which its sign changes as t1​<⋯<tm​, where m≤n, and define the polynomial
q(x)=∏j=1m​(x−tj​),
(10)
with q=1 if m=0. Between consecutive zeros of r, its sign is constant by the intermediate value theorem. At each tj​, both r and q change sign; at every other zero of r, neither sign changes. Hence rq has one fixed sign throughout [−1,1] wherever it is nonzero. Replacing q by −q if necessary makes rq≥0 everywhere.
Since r is not identically zero and q has only finitely many zeros, rq is strictly positive on some open interval. Positivity of w gives
∫−11​w(x)r(x)q(x)dx>0,
(11)
contradicting orthogonality because q∈Pn​. Thus
f(xi​)=pn​(xi​) at at least n+1 distinct points in [−1,1].​
(12)
This is the interpolation property of weighted least-squares polynomial approximation. It supplies interpolation nodes depending on f and w, rather than nodes prescribed in advance.
