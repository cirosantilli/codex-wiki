The Euclidean norm is invariant under an orthogonal matrix, so the full singular value decomposition gives
∥Ax−b∥22​​=​(ΣVTx−UTb−U⊥T​b​)​22​=∥ΣVTx−UTb∥22​+∥U⊥T​b∥22​.​
(31)
The second term is independent of x; the first attains its unique minimum zero because Σ is an invertible matrix. The linear least squares solution is therefore
x∗​=VΣ−1UTb=A+b,xmin​∥Ax−b∥2​=∥U⊥T​b∥2​.​
(32)
Here A+=VΣ−1UT is the Moore-Penrose inverse, and the fitted vector is the orthogonal projection Ax∗​=UUTb onto the image of a linear map defined by A.
With the singular value decomposition supplied, forming UTb costs O(mn) arithmetic operations, diagonal scaling costs O(n), and multiplication by V costs O(n2). Since m≥n,
Cost per right-hand side: O(mn+n2)=O(mn).​
(33)
One need not form U⊥​, A+ or ATA to calculate x∗​. Computing the needed thin singular value decomposition first has standard dense cost O(mn2); explicitly forming an entire m×m orthogonal completion can require additional work when m is much larger than n.
