We use the spectral theorem to construct a sufficiently accurate approximate eigenpair. Complete x to an orthonormal eigenbasis x,x2​,…,xn​ of the symmetric matrix B, with corresponding eigenvalues λ,λ2​,…,λn​. Simplicity of λ means that every λj​−λ below is nonzero. Put
a=xTEx,v=−∑j=2n​λj​−λxjT​Ex​xj​.
(42)
The orthogonal decomposition of Ex shows that xTv=0 and
(B−λI)v=−(E−aI)x.
(43)
With uε​=x+εv and με​=λ+εa, direct expansion cancels every first-order term:
(B+εE)uε​−με​uε​​=ε((B−λI)v+(E−aI)x)+ε2(E−aI)v=ε2(E−aI)v.​
(44)
Also ∥uε​∥22​=1+ε2∥v∥22​≥1. Thus its eigenpair residual has Euclidean norm O(ε2) in Big O notation.
For completeness, a real symmetric matrix M always has an eigenvalue within ∥(M−μI)u∥2​/∥u∥2​ of any real μ and nonzero u. Indeed, writing u=∑j​cj​qj​ in an orthonormal eigenbasis with eigenvalues θj​ gives
∥(M−μI)u∥22​=∑j​(θj​−μ)2cj2​≥(minj​∣θj​−μ∣)2∥u∥22​.
(45)
Apply this symmetric eigenvalue residual bound to M=B+εE. There is an eigenvalue λε​ such that
∣λε​−(λ+εa)∣≤ε2∥(E−aI)v∥2​,
(46)
which proves
λε​=λ+ε(xTEx)+O(ε2).​
(47)
This establishes the first-order perturbation of a simple symmetric eigenvalue without presupposing differentiability of the perturbed eigenvector. If n=1, the sum defining v is empty and the displayed eigenvalue formula is exact.
