Conservative Hénon stability boundary (detDF=1(b=1))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Discrete dynamical system Hénon map
OurBigBook.com Words: 101
At b=1 the Hénon map has unit Jacobian determinant. The upper fixed point is linearly elliptic for −1<μ<3, and its newborn two-cycle is elliptic for 3<μ<4. These are neutral multiplier statements, not attraction. The endpoints have double multipliers +1 and −1. Constant-area scaling also excludes a simple invariant closed curve enclosing positive area when b>0 and b=1: invariance of its interior would require both area(F(D))=barea(D) and F(D)=D. Thus the b=1 elliptic stability boundary is a conservative degeneracy, not a generic dissipative Neimark-Sacker bifurcation. Arbitrary non-area-preserving perturbations can change this behavior even though generic fold and flip curves persist.
