Markov chain

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process
OurBigBook.com Words: 23k Articles: 398
The term Markov chain commonly denotes a Markov process on a finite or countable state space. Time may be discrete, as for a daily record, or continuous, as for a continuous-time Markov chain. Some authors also use “Markov chain” for discrete-time processes on more general state spaces; specifying both time and state space avoids this ambiguity.
For a Discrete-time Markov chain, its initial probability distribution μ0​ and transition matrix P specify the probability of each finite path:
P(X0​=i0​,…,Xn​=in​)=μ0​(i0​)∏k=0n−1​Pik​ik+1​​.
(1)
This is the Markov factorization, obtained by applying conditional probability repeatedly. See Hao Wu's MIT lecture 1, definition and distribution evolution.
For the constructed sunny/rainy Markov chain below, starting sunny makes the path sunny, rainy, sunny have probability 1×0.2×0.4=0.08. Starting instead with stationary distribution (2/3,1/3) makes that path's probability (2/3)×0.2×0.4=4/75. The transition probabilities are identical in both experiments; the initial probability distribution distinguishes them.
