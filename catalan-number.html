Catalan number (Cn​=n+11​(n2n​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Lattice path Dyck path
OurBigBook.com Words: 634 Articles: 7
The Catalan number Cn​ counts Dyck paths with semilength of a Dyck path n. For a nonnegative integer n,
Cn​=n+11​(n2n​),(C0​,C1​,C2​,C3​,C4​,C5​)=(1,1,2,5,14,42).​
(42)
The count C0​=1 includes the empty path. Of the (36​)=20 paths with three up steps and three down steps, exactly five stay at nonnegative height. Their shapes and balanced parentheses encodings appear below.[ref]
The useful connection is a shared decomposition. A Dyck path splits uniquely at its first-return decomposition of a Dyck path; a binary tree splits at its root into left and right subtrees; a binary parenthesization splits at its last operation. Each removes one outer unit and leaves an ordered pair of smaller objects. The resulting Catalan recurrence lets one count several different structures with the same calculation. A count of five does not mean the structures are physically identical: a bijection supplies the precise translation between them.
