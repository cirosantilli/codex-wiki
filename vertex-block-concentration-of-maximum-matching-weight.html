Vertex-block concentration of maximum matching weight

 ... Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Concentration inequality Bounded differences property McDiarmid's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
Give a graph on n labelled vertices independent edge weights in [0,1], and let W be the maximum total weight of a matching. Put each edge into the block of its lower-numbered endpoint. There are at most n−1 independent blocks. Every matching uses at most one edge from any block, since the block's edges share that endpoint. Changing a whole block therefore changes the weight of each fixed matching by at most one. Comparing the old and new maximizing matchings shows the same bound for W.
The Doob exposure martingale for these blocks has increments bounded by one, so Azuma's inequality gives
P(∣W−EW∣≥t)≤2exp(−2(n−1)t2​),n≥2.
(43)
The important feature is grouping independent edge coordinates into vertex-centred blocks. Exposing all edges separately would give a weaker denominator of order n2 on a dense graph. Identical distributions are unnecessary; independence and the common range bound suffice.
