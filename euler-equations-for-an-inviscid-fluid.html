Euler equation in a uniformly rotating frame (∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u+2Ω×u=−∇Π)

Words: 53
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid OurBigBook.com
For constant mass density ρ0​, a frame with constant angular velocity Ω absorbs centrifugal acceleration into reduced pressure Π=p/ρ0​−∣Ω×r∣2/2. Indeed Ω×(Ω×r)=−∇∣Ω×r∣2/2. Transforming the material acceleration in the Euler equations gives the displayed equation. The Coriolis acceleration remains velocity dependent and cannot be absorbed into a scalar potential. A conservative physical body force can be included by adding its potential to Π.

Density scaling invariance of incompressible Euler flow (ρ↦Cρ, p↦Cp)

Words: 58
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid OurBigBook.com
A homogeneous fluid in a prescribed gravitational field obeys Du/Dt=−∇(p/ρ)+g and ∇⋅u=0. Multiplying both constant mass density and pressure by the same positive factor preserves the velocity solution, provided initial and boundary data remain compatible. Thus velocity-only observations do not determine density in this model. Independently prescribed pressure gradients or the fluid's own gravity can break this invariance.

Linearized Euler equations

Words: 63
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid OurBigBook.com
The linearized Euler equations retain only first-order perturbations about a specified inviscid fluid state. Together with the linearized continuity equation and a pressure-density constitutive relation, they describe small-amplitude acoustic waves. About a uniform resting state, ρ0​∂t​u′=−∇p′, ∂t​ρ′+ρ0​∇⋅u′=0, and an isentropic pressure perturbation has p′=c02​ρ′. Gradients and advection of a moving background must be retained when its variation is not negligible.

Conservative energy flux of a polytropic ideal gas (E=21​ρu2+γ−1p​,FE​=ux​(E+p))

Words: 61
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid OurBigBook.com
For an unmagnetized ideal gas without gravity, the pressure equation gives internal-energy balance ∂t​e+∂x​(ux​e)=−p∂x​ux​, where e=p/(γ−1). The momentum equation gives kinetic-energy balance with source −ux​∂x​p. Their sum is conservative with the displayed total-energy flux. Tangential velocities contribute to kinetic energy even when all derivatives are in the normal direction. Smooth polytropic flow advects its entropy parameter; a physical shock need not preserve that parameter across the discontinuity.

For a fixed-composition inviscid fluid, specific enthalpy satisfies dw=Tds+dp/ρ. Combining this identity with the vorticity cross-product identity gives the displayed equation, with ω=∇×u and ε=u2/2+Φ+w. It exposes entropy gradients as the thermodynamic source of a mismatch between Bernoulli function gradients and the vorticity term. In a steady adiabatic process, its projection along a streamline makes ε constant there.

Yudovich characteristic flow (X˙(t)=u(t,X(t)))

For a planar Euler velocity obtained from vorticity in Lloc∞​(dt;L1∩L∞), the planar vorticity velocity kernel gives a locally integrable speed bound and log-Lipschitz modulus. Spatial mollification gives existence of global trajectories; the Osgood uniqueness criterion gives uniqueness and continuous dependence. Measurability in time and an initial trace are needed. Bernoulli's principle (p/ρ+∣u∣2/2+Φ=B)

Words: 324 Articles: 1
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid OurBigBook.com
The Bernoulli principle relates pressure, kinetic energy per unit volume and Newtonian gravitational potential along a streamline of steady flow with inviscid flow. For constant mass density, the Bernoulli equation gives p/ρ+u2/2+Φ constant along that streamline. Constancy across different streamlines requires further hypotheses, such as irrotational flow.
Precisely, suppose a fluid of constant positive mass density obeys the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid, its velocity field and pressure are steady, and the body force per unit mass is −∇Φ. Then
B=ρp​+2∣u∣2​+Φ,u⋅∇B=0.
(1)
Every term has units of energy per unit mass. For a uniform gravitational field, Newtonian gravitational potential is Φ=gz, up to an additive constant. The pressure term represents work by surrounding fluid, so acceleration can exchange pressure work with kinetic energy. Dotting the steady Euler equations with u gives the displayed directional derivative: this is why constancy follows a streamline. [ref]
Continue the horizontal water constriction example from incompressible flow. Assume negligible viscous losses, steady flow and approximately uniform flow at both measurement sections, so the streamline speeds there equal the mean speeds 0.20 and 0.80ms−1. Equal height cancels the Newtonian gravitational potential terms. The ideal pressure drop is
p1​−p2​=2ρ​(U22​−U12​)=21000​(0.802−0.202)=300Pa.
(2)
This is the Venturi effect in its ideal limit: the gain in kinetic energy density is supplied by pressure work. Cambridge's treatment uses this relation to connect a measured pressure difference to volumetric flow rate. [ref]
The 300Pa result is conditional, rather than a prediction of the actual viscous pipe. Mass conservation fixes Q and the ratio of mean speeds even with viscosity, but nonuniform velocity profiles require an integrated energy balance, and wall friction dissipates mechanical energy. A pump would supply additional work. In particular, constant mean speed in a straight pipe does not imply constant pressure: Poiseuille flow needs a continuing pressure gradient to overcome viscosity. [ref]
