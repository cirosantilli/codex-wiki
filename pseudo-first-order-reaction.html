Pseudo-first-order reaction

OurBigBook.com Words: 123
In a second-order reaction A+B⟶P with rate law −d[A]/dt=k2​[A][B], a large excess of B can keep [B]≈[B]0​ during the observation window. Then
−dtd[A]​≈k1​[A],k1​=k2​[B]0​,[A](t)≈[A]0​e−k1​t.
(147)
This is a pseudo-first-order reaction: the approximate first-order reaction describes that window, while changing the excess B concentration changes k1​. Its units are s−1, distinct from the second-order reaction rate constant k2​. For one-to-one stoichiometry, the maximum fractional depletion of B is [A]0​/[B]0​; an original 100:1 initial excess bounds its change by 1%. A twofold excess permits 50% depletion and does not provide the same control.[ref]
