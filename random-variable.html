Random element (X:Ω→E)

Words: 70
Random variable OurBigBook.com
A random element is a measurable function from a probability space (Ω,F,P) to a measurable space (E,E). Its law is the pushforward measure PX​(A)=P(X−1(A)) for A∈E. Real-valued random variables and vector-valued random variables are examples; a stochastic sample path can also be a random element when its path-space sigma-algebra is specified. For a Polish space E, the usual choice is its Borel sigma-algebra. A large deviation principle concerns the laws of a family of such random elements.

Statistical dependence

Words: 46
Random variable OurBigBook.com
Two random variables are statistically dependent when their joint probability distribution differs from the product of their marginal probability distributions. Equivalently, some events determined by the two variables violate the product rule that defines independence. Nonzero covariance implies statistical dependence when the moments exist, but zero covariance does not in general imply independence.

Random sum (Z=∑j=1N​Yj​)

Words: 88 Articles: 1
Random variable OurBigBook.com
A random sum has a random number N of summands, usually with N a nonnegative integer-valued random variable. The empty sum is zero. The dependence between N and the summands must be stated; independent summands do not by themselves imply independence from N.

Transform composition for an independent random sum (MZ​(t)=GN​(MY​(t)))

Words: 47
Random sum OurBigBook.com
For independent identically distributed summands, independent also of the count N, conditioning on N=n gives E[etZ∣N=n]=MY​(t)n. The law of total expectation gives the composition of the count's probability generating function with the summand's moment-generating function. This identity is valid as an extended nonnegative expectation; finiteness near zero requires additional exponential-integrability assumptions.

Discrete random variable

Words: 41
Random variable OurBigBook.com
A random variable supported on a finite or countable subset of the real line. Its law is specified by a probability mass function. Countable support need not consist of integers: a variable constantly equal to 3/2 is discrete. Integer-indexed tail-sum formulas therefore require a separate integer-valued hypothesis.

Random vector

Words: 22
Random variable OurBigBook.com
A random vector is a random variable whose values are vectors or, equivalently, an ordered tuple of scalar random variables on one probability space.

Hilbert-space-valued random variable

Words: 241 Articles: 8
Random variable OurBigBook.com
A Hilbert-space-valued random variable is a measurable map into a Hilbert space. It is square-integrable when E∥X∥2<∞, in which case its mean is defined by the Bochner integral.

Covariance operator (CX​)

Words: 188 Articles: 6
Hilbert-space-valued random variable OurBigBook.com
For a square-integrable centered Hilbert-space-valued random variable X, its covariance operator is
CX​h=E[⟨X,h⟩X]=E[X⊗X]h.
(1)
It is a positive self-adjoint trace-class operator, and trCX​=E∥X∥2.

Covariance kernel (cX​(s,t))

Words: 94 Articles: 3
Covariance operator OurBigBook.com
When a covariance operator on a function space is an integral operator, its covariance kernel satisfies (CX​f)(s)=∫cX​(s,t)f(t)dt. For a centered process, cX​(s,t)=E[X(s)X(t)] whenever point evaluation is meaningful.
Brownian covariance kernel
Words: 43 Articles: 1
Covariance kernel OurBigBook.com
The covariance kernel of standard Brownian motion on [0,1] is c(s,t)=min(s,t). Its integral operator has eigenfunctions 2​sin((k−21​)πt) and eigenvalues ((k−21​)π)−2.
Brownian covariance from an integrated orthonormal basis (ki​(t)=⟨1[0,t]​,hi​⟩⟹K(s,t)=s∧t)
Words: 22
Brownian covariance kernel OurBigBook.com
For a complete orthonormal basis of L2(R+​), the Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis identifies the sum of the feature products with ⟨1[0,s]​,1[0,t]​⟩=min(s,t). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives absolute convergence.
Brownian bridge covariance kernel
Words: 24
Covariance kernel OurBigBook.com
The covariance kernel of a standard Brownian bridge on [0,1] is c(s,t)=min(s,t)−st. Its integral operator has eigenfunctions 2​sin(kπt) and eigenvalues (kπ)−2 for k≥1.

Empirical covariance operator (C)

Words: 67 Articles: 1
Covariance operator OurBigBook.com
For observations X1​,…,Xn​ and a chosen center m, the empirical covariance operator is Cm​=n−1∑i​(Xi​−m)⊗(Xi​−m). Its expectation equals the population covariance plus the rank-one operator formed from the centering error.
Pooled covariance operator
Words: 35
Empirical covariance operator OurBigBook.com
A pooled covariance operator averages the within-group empirical covariance operators from several samples that are assumed to share one population covariance. Centering within each group prevents differences between group means from contaminating the covariance estimate.

Gaussian random element

Words: 24
Hilbert-space-valued random variable OurBigBook.com
A random element G of a Hilbert space is Gaussian when every continuous linear functional of G has a normal distribution. Its mean and covariance operator determine its distribution.

Independent random variables

Words: 626 Articles: 17
Random variable OurBigBook.com
Random variables X1​,…,Xn​ are independent when their generated sigma-algebras are independent sigma-algebras. Equivalently, their joint distribution is the product of their marginal distributions.

Pairwise independent random variables

Words: 31
Independent random variables OurBigBook.com
Random variables are pairwise independent when every two distinct members are independent. This is weaker than joint independence, but it is enough to make all off-diagonal covariances vanish for square-integrable variables.

Conditional independence (X⊥⊥Y∣Z)

Words: 531 Articles: 13
Independent random variables OurBigBook.com
Random variables X and Y are conditionally independent given Z when their conditional joint distribution factorizes into their two conditional marginal distributions almost surely.

Covariance induced by a shared latent variable (Cov(A,B)=Var(g(T)))

Words: 51
Conditional independence OurBigBook.com
Let A,B be indicator random variables with conditional independence given a random variable T, and suppose E[A∣T]=E[B∣T]=g(T). Then the law of total expectation gives E[AB]=E[g(T)2] and E[A]=E[B]=E[g(T)]. Thus their covariance is the variance of g(T) and is nonnegative. The result explains correlated component evolutionary states in a coeval binary population without correlation between initial masses.

Conditional independence graph

Words: 323 Articles: 7
Conditional independence OurBigBook.com
For a random vector indexed by vertices V, its conditional independence graph is the undirected graph with u−v absent exactly when Zu​⊥Zv​∣ZV∖{u,v}​. In a nonsingular multivariate normal distribution, its edges are the nonzero off-diagonal entries of the precision matrix.
Nodewise regression
Words: 151 Articles: 3
Conditional independence graph OurBigBook.com
Fit the conditional law of each variable given all others and use its predictor support to estimate its neighbours. In a Gaussian graphical model, this is linear regression with coefficients given by conditional regression coefficients from a Gaussian precision matrix. The Nodewise Lasso supplies sparse fits; the OR rule for nodewise graph selection and AND rule for nodewise graph selection combine possibly disagreeing directional supports into an undirected graph.
Profiling intercepts in nodewise regression (ak​=Xk​−X−kT​θ−k,k​)
Words: 43
Nodewise regression OurBigBook.com
Minimizing the squared error over each free regression intercept gives the displayed formula. Substitution replaces all data columns by centred columns. The regression intercept is generally not the marginal mean of the response when predictor means are nonzero; the distinction matters in conditional regression coefficients from a Gaussian precision matrix.
AND rule for nodewise graph selection ({j,k}∈E⟺θjk​=0 and θkj​=0)
Words: 29
Nodewise regression OurBigBook.com
The intersection rule retains an edge only when both directions of nodewise regression select it. It is more conservative than the OR rule for nodewise graph selection; the distinction matters because separate finite-sample fits can disagree.
OR rule for nodewise graph selection ({j,k}∈E⟺θjk​=0 or θkj​=0)
Words: 33
Nodewise regression OurBigBook.com
The union rule retains an edge whenever either of the two nodewise regression fits selects the other variable. It is more inclusive than the AND rule for nodewise graph selection and makes an undirected edge decision from potentially asymmetric estimated neighbourhoods.
Gaussian graphical model (Ω=Σ−1,Ωjk​=0⟺{j,k}∈/E)
Words: 48
Conditional independence graph OurBigBook.com
For a nondegenerate multivariate normal distribution, the conditional independence graph is read from zeros in its precision matrix. Conditional independence of two components given all others is equivalent to a zero cross term in their conditional Gaussian quadratic density, hence to Ωjk​=0. It does not generally correspond to a zero in the covariance matrix.
Neighbourhood selection
Words: 36
Conditional independence graph OurBigBook.com
For Gaussian data, regress each variable on all remaining variables using Lasso. Nonzero coefficients estimate its neighbours in the conditional independence graph. Symmetrize the separate regressions using either the union or the intersection of their selected directed neighbourhoods.
Moral graph equals the conditional independence graph under faithfulness
Words: 48
Conditional independence graph OurBigBook.com
If D-separation and conditional independence are equivalent for a Directed acyclic graph, its moral graph is its conditional independence graph. After conditioning on all other vertices, an active path can only be a direct edge or a two-edge collider through a common child: two adjacent interior vertices cannot both be colliders. These are exactly the moral edges.

Contraction axiom for conditional independence

Words: 23
Conditional independence OurBigBook.com
The contraction axiom says that
X⊥Y∣ZandX⊥W∣(Y,Z)⟹X⊥(Y,W)∣Z.
(2)
It follows directly by multiplying the corresponding conditional-density factorizations.

Decomposition axiom for conditional independence

Words: 29
Conditional independence OurBigBook.com
The decomposition axiom says that X⊥(Y,W)∣Z implies both X⊥Y∣Z and X⊥W∣Z. It follows by marginalizing the joint conditional distribution over the discarded variable.

Generalized covariance measure statistic (T)

Words: 81 Articles: 1
Conditional independence OurBigBook.com
To test X⊥⊥Y∣Z, regress X and Y on Z and average the products of their residuals. Dividing the scaled average by its empirical second moment produces the generalized covariance measure statistic. Small mean-square nuisance-regression errors and a product-rate condition make it asymptotically pivotal.
Studentization of the generalized covariance measure statistic
Words: 36
Generalized covariance measure statistic OurBigBook.com
If the numerator of the generalized covariance measure statistic obeys a central limit theorem with variance E(ε2ξ2) and its empirical residual-product second moment converges in probability to the same positive quantity, the Slutsky theorem makes the studentized statistic converge in distribution to N(0,1).

Orthogonality of independent centered Hilbert-space random variables

Words: 27
Independent random variables OurBigBook.com
If Xi​ are independent centered square-integrable random variables in a Hilbert space, then E⟨Xi​,Xj​⟩=0 for i=j, and hence
E∥∑i​Xi​∥2=∑i​E∥Xi​∥2.
(3)

Independent and identically distributed random variables

Words: 15
Independent random variables OurBigBook.com
A family of independent random variables is independent and identically distributed when every variable has the same probability distribution.
