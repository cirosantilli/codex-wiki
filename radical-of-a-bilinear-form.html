Radical of a bilinear form (radB)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Bilinear form Degenerate bilinear form
OurBigBook.com Words: 219 Articles: 2
For a symmetric bilinear form or an alternating bilinear form, the radical is the linear subspace
radB={v∈V:B(v,w)=0 for every w∈V}.
(27)
These vectors are invisible to all pairings, and the bilinear form is nondegenerate exactly when this linear subspace is {0}. In coordinates, radB=kerA for both kinds of bilinear form, since AT=A or AT=−A. Thus diag(1,0) has radical of a bilinear form span{(0,1)}.
Passing to the quotient vector space V/radB removes precisely the invisible directions. The rule B([v],[w])=B(v,w) is well-defined because adding a vector in the radical of a bilinear form to either input changes no pairing, and the resulting bilinear form is nondegenerate. For B((x,y),(s,t))=xs, this quotient vector space retains just the x direction and its form is ordinary multiplication.[ref]
For a general bilinear form, distinguish the left radical of a bilinear form from the right radical of a bilinear form; they can be different linear subspaces even though their dimensions agree in finite dimensions.
