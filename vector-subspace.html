Vector subspace

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 631 Articles: 12
A vector subspace is a nonempty subset of a vector space closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication, and is therefore itself a vector space. Equivalently, whenever u,v belong to the subset, so does au+bv for all allowed scalars a,b. In particular it contains the zero vector.
For example, the plane W={(x,y,z):x+y+z=0}⊆R3 is a vector subspace, with basis (1,−1,0),(1,0,−1). Indeed every element is (−y)(1,−1,0)+(−z)(1,0,−1). The parallel plane x+y+z=1 misses the zero vector and is an affine subspace instead. Homogeneous constraints produce vector subspaces: W is the kernel of the linear map (x,y,z)↦x+y+z. This lets us describe all solutions of a homogeneous linear equation through a basis of its kernel.
