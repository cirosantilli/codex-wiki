Seven-triangle Dirichlet transplantation

 ... Geometry and topology Differential geometry Riemannian geometry Spectral geometry Isospectral manifolds Transplantation theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 211
Take seven copies of a scalene triangle, with sides labelled a,b,c opposite its three vertices, and reflect adjacent copies across their glued side. For the first planar domain, the glued pairs are a:(2,3),(6,7), b:(4,6),(5,7), c:(1,5),(3,7); for the second they are a:(1,3),(5,7), b:(2,6),(3,7), c:(4,5),(6,7). Both coloured trees have central tile 7. For triangles sufficiently close to equilateral the interiors do not overlap. If its three angles are distinct and between π/4 and π/2, the only reentrant vertices are the original central vertices, with angles four times the corresponding triangle angles. They force any proposed isometry to preserve the labelled central triangle, which is incompatible with the different attachments on its arms.
For the spectral proof index the nonzero vectors and covectors of F23​ by 1,…,7, using binary coordinates. The Fano plane incidence matrix is By,x​=1y⋅x=0​. It satisfies BTB=2I+J, so is invertible, and intertwines the point and line permutations of each of the three elementary transvections. Put D=diag(1,1,−1,1,−1,−1,1). The signed reflection matrices for the Dirichlet boundary condition are −DPs​D and −DQs​D, hence C=DBD intertwines them. Its polar orthogonal factor gives a unitary equivalence of the Dirichlet Laplacians, preserving both the L2 inner product and the Dirichlet energy of the piecewise functions. This produces a three-parameter family of noncongruent isospectral planar domains.
