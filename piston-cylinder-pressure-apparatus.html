Piston-cylinder pressure apparatus (p−pext​≃F/A)

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology
OurBigBook.com Words: 544 Articles: 1
In a functional model, a driven piston reduces the chamber's volume. A surrounding cylinder carries the wall stress; a deformable packing or gasket closes a potential leak path around a moving or removable closure. A sample and a separate pressure sensor occupy the chamber. A transmitting liquid can load the sample on all sides; the sensor's signal passes to an external readout. This is a division of experimental functions, not a scale reconstruction of one historical machine.
For a slowly moving frictionless drive piston of effective surface area A, external pressure pext​ and inward applied force F, Newton's second law at mechanical balance gives
F+pext​A−pA=0,p−pext​=F/A.
(30)
Thus 20kN on a stipulated 20mm2 effective surface area corresponds ideally to 1GPa of differential pressure. These are illustrative numbers, not apparatus specifications. The effective surface area is the one associated with the pressure difference, not necessarily an external ram's visible cross section. With resisting friction Ff​ during inward motion, the balance becomes p−pext​=(F−Ff​)/A. During outward motion the sign of the friction contribution reverses. An applied-load reading can therefore show hysteresis even when the chamber's actual pressure is well defined.
A resistance pressure gauge instead supplies an internal signal. In a generic electrical readout, measure an electric potential difference Ve​ with a known electric current I and use Ohm's law to infer electrical resistance R=Ve​/I. This describes a possible readout principle; the lecture passage cited below identifies the sensor material and calibration landmarks without specifying this wiring.
