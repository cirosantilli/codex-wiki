If this holds, it holds after complexification; applying it to a complex eigenvector gives λkv→0, so ∣λ∣<1. Conversely, the complex Jordan normal form expresses each power as a finite sum of terms (rk​)λk−r from its blocks. Every term tends to zero when all eigenvalues have modulus below one. Thus
Convergence for every starting vector⟺ρ(G)<1.​

The convergence criterion for a stationary iteration needs complex triangularization or Jordan normal form. The paper's assertion that every real matrix is similar to a real upper-triangular matrix is false when nonreal eigenvalues occur; complex triangularization supplies the intended argument. In the Gauss-Seidel method, with A=D+L+U split into diagonal, strictly lower and strictly upper parts, M=D+L, N=−U​.
For n≥4, eliminating the ghost values gives the symmetric pentadiagonal matrix

7U1​−4U2​+U3​=h4f1​,−4U1​+6U2​−4U3​+U4​=h4f2​,

For n=3 the matrix is ​7−41​−46−4​1−47​​, for n=2 it is (7−4​−47​), and for n=1 it is (8) because both ghost contributions meet the same unknown. The diagonal entries are positive in every case, so the lower triangular matrix D+L is invertible and the Gauss-Seidel iteration is well defined.
An eigenvalue λ of G=−(D+L)−1U makes H(λ)=λ(D+L)+U singular. Let ℓi​ and ri​ be the sums of absolute lower and upper entries in row i. The row's diagonal-dominance margin is
∣λ∣di​−∣λ∣ℓi​−ri​.
(33)
Interior full rows have di​=6, ℓi​=ri​=5, giving margin ∣λ∣−5. Boundary rows have larger margins; for instance the first gives 7∣λ∣−5, and the second gives at least 2∣λ∣−5. Therefore ∣λ∣>5 would make H(λ) a strictly diagonally dominant matrix, hence nonsingular, a contradiction.
At ∣λ∣=5 it is weakly diagonally dominant with strict boundary rows. Its nonzero nearest-neighbour entries make it an irreducibly diagonally dominant matrix. The nonsingularity theorem for that class again contradicts singularity. For the small matrices above all rows are already strict at this value. Hence
Every iteration eigenvalue has ∣λ∣<5; equality is impossible.​
(34)
There is also a stronger conclusion. For T=tridiag(−1,2,−1),
A=T2+2e1​e1T​+2en​enT​,
(35)
including the n=1 overlap. Since T is positive definite, A is a symmetric positive-definite matrix. The Gauss-Seidel convergence from diagonal dominance and positive definiteness theorem consequently gives the stronger bound ρ(G)<1.
