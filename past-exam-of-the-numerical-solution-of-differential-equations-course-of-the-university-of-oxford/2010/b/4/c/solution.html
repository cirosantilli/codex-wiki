Use the fine matrix Ah​ and right-hand side from part (b), with diagonal Dh​. Starting from an iterate v, take ν weighted Jacobi smoothing steps
v←v+ωDh−1​(bh​−Ah​v),0<ω<1.
(22)
Form the residual rh​=bh​−Ah​v. For coarse interior indices j=1,…,m/2−1, use full-weighting restriction
(Rrh​)j​=41​(r2j−1​+2r2j​+r2j+1​).
(23)
The prolongation operator matrix P is linear interpolation of a coarse correction: (Pe)2j​=ej​ and (Pe)2j+1​=(ej​+ej+1​)/2, with zero endpoint corrections. Thus R=PT/2. Choose the Galerkin coarse-grid operator Ac​=RAh​P, solve Ac​ec​=Rrh​ exactly, and correct v←v+Pec​. The entries of Ah​, R and P above specify every matrix in the cycle. A rediscretized coarse operator is another standard choice if used consistently.
For a nonsingular coarse operator, the fine-grid iteration matrix of this pre-smoothed two-grid cycle is
ETG​=(I−PAc−1​RAh​)(I−ωDh−1​Ah​)ν.​
(24)
The purpose of under-relaxation is to damp fine-grid oscillations, leaving smoother error for coarse-grid correction. For the diffusion-dominated one-dimensional operator, ω=2/3 is the usual effective choice; the high-frequency symbol 1−ω(1−cosξ) then has modulus at most 1/3 for π/2≤∣ξ∣≤π. Coefficients and convection can change the best parameter, so this is a motivated choice rather than a universal optimum. Under the diagonal-dominance condition, the unweighted Jacobi matrix already has norm less than one in the maximum norm, and its convex combination with the identity remains convergent for 0<ω<1.
