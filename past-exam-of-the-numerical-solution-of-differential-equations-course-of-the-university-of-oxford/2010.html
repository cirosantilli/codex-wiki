Substitute a smooth exact solution into the linear multistep method and expand about xn​. Its residual is
(1+a+b)y+h(3+a−1)y′+h2(9/2+a/2−2)y′′+O(h3).
(1)
Consistency of a numerical method requires the coefficients of y and hy′ to vanish. Therefore 1+a+b=0 and 3+a=1, giving the unique choice
a=−2,b=1.​
(2)
For this choice the next coefficient is 9/2−1−2=3/2=0, so the residual is 23​h2y′′+O(h3). The normalized local truncation error, obtained by dividing the residual by h, is O(h). Hence the method has order one.

A zero-stable has a uniformly bounded response to small perturbations of its starting values and accumulated residuals on a fixed time interval as h→0. Equivalently, its first characteristic polynomial satisfies the root condition for a multistep method: every root has modulus at most one, and each root on the unit circle is simple.
For the consistent parameters, the recurrence for y′=0 is yn+3​−2yn+1​+yn​=0, with characteristic polynomial
ρ(z)=z3−2z+1=(z−1)(z2+z−1).
(3)
Let r=(−1−5​)/2. Choose y1​=r and y2​=r2; together with y0​=1 these generate exactly yn​=rn. Since ∣r∣=(1+5​)/2>3/2,
∣yn​∣=(21+5​​)n≥(3/2)n.​
(4)
This parasitic mode violates the root condition and grows without a bound independent of the number of steps. Scaling its starting amplitudes gives the same unbounded amplification for arbitrarily small initial perturbations. Thus the only consistent method in the family is not zero-stable.

A method has numerical convergence if it approximates the exact solution uniformly at mesh points on every fixed finite interval as h→0, whenever its starting approximations converge to the corresponding exact starting values, for sufficiently smooth solutions of a well-posed initial-value problem.
The Dahlquist equivalence theorem states that a fixed-coefficient linear multistep method is convergent if and only if it is consistent and zero-stable, under the usual Lipschitz condition on the differential equation and convergent starting data. In this family numerical consistency forces the parameters found in part (a), and part (b) proves that choice is not zero-stable. All other parameter choices are inconsistent. Therefore no member of the family is convergent.

Introduce v=u′ and solve the initial-value system
u′=v,v′=p(x)v+q(x)u−f(x,u),u(0)=α,v(0)=s.
(18)
The unknown initial slope s is the shooting method parameter. Integrate to one and solve the scalar residual equation R(s)=u(1;s)−β=0, using a secant iteration or a bracketed root finder when suitable data are available.
For a Newton update, integrate the sensitivity equations along the current shot:
w′=z,z′=p(x)z+[q(x)−fu​(x,u)]w,w(0)=0,z(0)=1.
(19)
Then R′(s)=w(1) and the update is s←s−R(s)/w(1) when this derivative is nonzero. After a root has been found, the corresponding shot satisfies both endpoint conditions. This formulation does not presume that a nonlinear boundary-value problem necessarily has a unique solution.

Use the fine matrix Ah​ and right-hand side from part (b), with diagonal Dh​. Starting from an iterate v, take ν weighted Jacobi smoothing steps
v←v+ωDh−1​(bh​−Ah​v),0<ω<1.
(22)
Form the residual rh​=bh​−Ah​v. For coarse interior indices j=1,…,m/2−1, use full-weighting restriction
(Rrh​)j​=41​(r2j−1​+2r2j​+r2j+1​).
(23)
The prolongation operator matrix P is linear interpolation of a coarse correction: (Pe)2j​=ej​ and (Pe)2j+1​=(ej​+ej+1​)/2, with zero endpoint corrections. Thus R=PT/2. Choose the Galerkin coarse-grid operator Ac​=RAh​P, solve Ac​ec​=Rrh​ exactly, and correct v←v+Pec​. The entries of Ah​, R and P above specify every matrix in the cycle. A rediscretized coarse operator is another standard choice if used consistently.
For a nonsingular coarse operator, the fine-grid iteration matrix of this pre-smoothed two-grid cycle is
ETG​=(I−PAc−1​RAh​)(I−ωDh−1​Ah​)ν.​
(24)
The purpose of under-relaxation is to damp fine-grid oscillations, leaving smoother error for coarse-grid correction. For the diffusion-dominated one-dimensional operator, ω=2/3 is the usual effective choice; the high-frequency symbol 1−ω(1−cosξ) then has modulus at most 1/3 for π/2≤∣ξ∣≤π. Coefficients and convection can change the best parameter, so this is a motivated choice rather than a universal optimum. Under the diagonal-dominance condition, the unweighted Jacobi matrix already has norm less than one in the maximum norm, and its convex combination with the identity remains convergent for 0<ω<1.

The coefficient signs alone do not imply the assertion as printed. Two interior nodes can have rows Lh​ψ1​=ψ2​−ψ1​ and Lh​ψ2​=ψ1​−ψ2​, with a separate boundary node. Taking interior values one and boundary value zero satisfies every stated coefficient inequality and Lh​ψ=0, but its maximum is not on the boundary. Connectedness of the geometric domain does not ensure connectedness of this stencil graph.
The usual extra assumption is that every interior node has a path of positive stencil coefficients to a prescribed boundary node. Under this assumption let M≥0 be the global maximum and suppose an interior node p attains it. Then
0≤Lh​ψp​=∑r=p​cp,r​(ψr​−M)+(∑r=p​cp,r​−cp,p​)M≤0.
(25)
All summands are nonpositive, so equality forces ψr​=M at every neighbor with cp,r​>0. Propagate along a path to the boundary to find a boundary node with value M. This proves the discrete maximum principle under boundary-reaching stencil connectivity, and exhibits why the literal hypotheses need that qualification.

With zero boundary values eliminated, the diagonal of Lh​ is −4I/h2. The Jacobi method is therefore
J=I+41​h2Lh​,
(33)
which averages the four neighboring components. The sine addition identity shows that the supplied product vectors are eigenvectors, with

b OurBigBook.com
The endpoint α=0 in the printed nonnegative-parameter hypothesis must be excluded. We first establish the intended result for α>0, then describe that endpoint exactly.
Write X,Y for the two directional matrices; they commute because they act on separate tensor-product coordinates. At a limit V, the intermediate vector also has a limit V. Subtract the two limiting equations in the form that cancels XV and YV; this gives 2α(V−V)=0. Hence V=V for α>0. Substitution then gives (X+Y)V=h2f, precisely the required discrete equation.
Eliminating the intermediate vector gives the alternating direction implicit iteration matrix
G=(αI−Y)−1(αI+X)(αI−X)−1(αI+Y).
(36)
The sine-product basis simultaneously diagonalizes X,Y, with eigenvalues −ξp​ and −ηq​, where
ξp​=4sin2(pπh/2)>0,ηq​=4sin2(qπh/2)>0.
(37)
Thus
λpq​(G)=α+ξp​α−ξp​​α+ηq​α−ηq​​.​
(38)
For α>0, each factor has modulus strictly less than one. Hence ρ(G)<1, proving convergence from every starting vector to the unique discrete solution.
At α=0 the two factors are both −1, so G=I. The forcing terms cancel in the composed iteration as well, and Vk+1=Vk for every starting vector. An incorrect initial vector therefore remains incorrect forever, even though the sequence is constant and has a limit. This disproves both the literal fixed-point implication and the claimed general convergence-to-solution assertion at zero; strict positivity of α is the necessary repair.
