Tensile modulus experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Linear elasticity Young's modulus
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
MIT's Spring 2004 laboratory assignment Properties of Heat Treated Steel gives a concrete tensile testing protocol. Record transducer channels and calibration factors; measure specimen diameter and length, and the extensometer gauge length with its locking pin inserted. Zero the load cell, pin the threaded grips into their adapters, and attach the extensometer centrally. Apply the specified seating load, record actuator position, then acquire readings every second while moving at about 0.05 cmmin−1. The handout derives engineering stress from load cell force, engineering strain from extensometer deformation, and Young's modulus by linear regression below the proportional limit; it warns that actuator-derived strain includes apparatus deformation.[ref]
For a modulus-focused measurement, select a load window after seating, hold temperature and loading rate consistent, and record force, local extension and crosshead displacement together. Fit F=F0​+kg​ΔLg​ within that window and calculate E=kg​Lg​/A0​. Inspect the regression residuals for curvature; repeat at a lower maximum load and include unloading to check elastic deformation. These are diagnostic additions to the cited protocol, whose full test continues through failure. The handout's seating load is specific to its specimens and apparatus, rather than a universal preload.
An extensometer measures the change between its contact points; crosshead displacement measures travel of the machine's moving member. The manufacturer Instron explicitly distinguishes local strain from travel that includes machine deformation and machine compliance.[ref] The gauge length Lg​ can be shorter than the uniform length L between grips. Thus even a perfectly rigid machine generally has ΔLg​=x: the local instrument sees only its own segment. Do not divide one channel's extension by the other channel's reference length.
Grips must transmit axial force without slip. Misalignment adds bending moment and makes opposite sides experience different axial strains; an instrument on just one side may then report a biased modulus. A changing extension offset, hysteresis between loading and unloading, or load-dependent fitted slope is evidence to investigate seating, slip or material response. A straight line over a short interval alone does not identify which is responsible.
