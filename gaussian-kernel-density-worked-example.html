Gaussian kernel density worked example (f​(0)=0.337949…)

Take the realized statistical sample (−1,0,1), the Gaussian density kernel ϕ(u)=(2π)−1/2e−u2/2 and smoothing bandwidth h=1/2. Then
f​(x)=32​[ϕ(2x+2)+ϕ(2x)+ϕ(2x−2)].
(4)
At x=0, the three contributions are 0.03599398, 0.26596152, and 0.03599398, because ϕ(0)=0.39894228 and ϕ(2)=e−2/2π​=0.05399097. Their sum is
f​(0)=32π​2(1+2e−2)​=0.33794948….
(5)
This is a probability density function value, not the probability of observing exactly zero. Each of the three component curves has area 1/3, despite having a different height at zero.
Let Φ be the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution. The fitted probability of the interval [−1/2,1/2] is its area under the kernel density estimator:
∫−1/21/2​f​(x)dx​=31​a∈{−1,0,1}∑​[Φ(1−2a)−Φ(−1−2a)]=3[Φ(3)−Φ(1)]+[Φ(1)−Φ(−1)]+[Φ(−1)−Φ(−3)]​=30.15730536+0.68268949+0.15730536​=0.33243340….​
(6)
The shaded area in the diagram is this fitted probability. Its agreement to a few digits with 1/3 is incidental: the empirical measure gives this interval mass 1/3 exactly, whereas kernel density estimation redistributes that mass into neighboring intervals.
