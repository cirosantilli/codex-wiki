Use the computational basis in the stated order and let the rightmost operation act first. The controlled phase gate is ∣0⟩⟨0∣⊗I+∣1⟩⟨1∣⊗Z. From the rotation definition,
Ry​(θ)=(cos(θ/2)sin(θ/2)​−sin(θ/2)cos(θ/2)​),Ry​(π/2)ZRy​(−π/2)=X.
(1)
The control-zero block remains the identity, and the control-one block becomes X. Thus
Ry(2)​(π/2)UCP​Ry(2)​(−π/2)=UCNOT​.​
(2)
This proves the relation for arbitrary superpositions, rather than only for computational-basis probabilities.
For the second relation write S=USW1/2​. Its central block is (ab​ba​), with a=(1+i)/2, b=(1−i)/2. Because a2=i/2, b2=−i/2 and ab=1/2, direct multiplication gives
SRz(1)​(π)S=diag(−i,1,−1,i).
(3)
Meanwhile Rz(1)​(π/2)Rz(2)​(−π/2)=diag(1,−i,i,1). Multiplying these diagonal matrices and the overall factor i gives
iRz(1)​(π/2)Rz(2)​(−π/2)SRz(1)​(π)S=diag(1,1,1,−1)=UCP​.​
(4)
The supplied S is indeed a square root of the SWAP gate: S2 exchanges ∣01⟩ and ∣10⟩ while fixing the other basis states. It acts as one on the triplet subspace and as i on the singlet subspace.
For an isotropic exchange interaction with Hamiltonian H=J(t)σ1​⋅σ2​/4, define φ=∫J(t)dt/ℏ. Triplet and singlet energies differ by J, so
U(φ)=e−iφ/4(PT​+eiφPS​).
(5)
An exchange pulse with φ=π/2 therefore implements e−iπ/8S. Two such pulses, interspersed with the specified local rotations, implement the controlled phase gate; the initial and final target rotations then turn it into a CNOT gate. Their common overall phase has no effect on an isolated gate's outcome probabilities; a subsequently controlled version must keep track of conditional phases.
The construction converts a physically available two-spin interaction into a standard entangling gate, which together with arbitrary one-qubit rotations supplies universal quantum computation. Practical operation needs calibrated exchange pulse area and timing, individually controllable rotations, and reproducible phase references. Charge noise can change J; magnetic-field gradients add unwanted single-spin evolution, and anisotropic exchange modifies the assumed Hamiltonian. Pulses must suppress leakage into unwanted orbital states while finishing before excessive decoherence. Readout, preparation, cross-talk and the extra operations in this decomposition all affect the achieved quantum gate fidelity. The algebraic identity alone does not establish a fault-tolerant quantum computation implementation.
