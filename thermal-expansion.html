Thermal expansion

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 417 Articles: 2
Thermal expansion is a change in a body's dimensions as its temperature changes under a specified mechanical constraint. A heated gas under a freely moving loaded piston usually increases its volume at fixed pressure; heating the same gas in a rigid vessel instead raises its pressure. The coefficient of thermal expansion quantifies the first response. Expansion can be negative, so the name does not assert that every substance grows on heating.
For an ideal gas at fixed pressure and particle number, V=NkB​T/P gives V2​/V1​=T2​/T1​. Heating from 300K to 330K therefore increases the volume by exactly 10% within the ideal gas model. For a solid, specify whether the measurement concerns length or volume: for uniform expansion in all three directions, the volumetric coefficient is three times the linear thermal expansion coefficient, because V∝L3. Direction-dependent expansion requires the separate dimensional responses. [ref]
