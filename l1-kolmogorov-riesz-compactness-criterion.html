L1 Kolmogorov-Riesz compactness criterion (S⋐L1(R)⟺S bounded, uniformly tight and translation continuous)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Lp space
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
A subset of L1(R) is relatively norm compact exactly when it is bounded, its absolute-integral tails tend to zero uniformly, and supf∈S​∥f(⋅+h)−f∥1​→0 as h→0. Necessity follows by taking a finite norm net and using the tail decay and translation continuity in Lp of its finitely many centers; the error contributes at most twice the net radius. For sufficiency, convolve with a triangular unit-integral kernel of width δ. Translation continuity gives uniform approximation in L1, while boundedness gives a common supremum bound and a common Lipschitz constant for the smoothed family. The original tail condition controls its tails outside a slightly enlarged interval. On that interval Arzelà-Ascoli theorem gives a finite uniform net. Extend its centers by zero outside the interval to obtain a finite L1 net, and combine the smoothing and tail errors. Completeness of L1 turns total boundedness into relative compactness.
