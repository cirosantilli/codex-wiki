Thévenin terminal response (V=Voc​−IRth​)

 Home Electrical engineering Thévenin's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
With the passive sign convention applied to the network, its entering electric current is J=−I, and the same relation becomes V=Voc​+JRth​. The electrical load uses the passive sign convention with entering electric current I, so it absorbs power VI. A positive delivered electric current therefore produces a voltage drop for positive Rth​; changing the reference direction without changing the equation would give the wrong sign.
The reason is linearity of Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's current law, Kirchhoff's voltage law, and the constitutive relations of any linear dependent electrical sources. Solve once at I=0 with the independent electrical sources present. Subtract this solution from a solution with prescribed delivered electric current I: the fixed independent electrical sources cancel, leaving a homogeneous linear network whose terminal voltage change is proportional to I. This is a short superposition principle argument for an affine function; the fixed offset need not vanish.
The qualification about a well-defined response matters. An ideal voltage source alone has Rth​=0. An ideal nonzero current source alone constrains I and does not have a finite open-circuit voltage or a representation V=Voc​−IRth​ with finite coefficients. Linear dependent electrical sources can give negative resistance, and singular ideal networks may fail to have a unique finite response. Neither a finite nor a strictly positive passive Rth​ follows merely from calling an electrical circuit linear. [ref]
