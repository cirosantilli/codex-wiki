Molecule bookkeeping

 Home Chemistry Molecule
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
Four water molecules contain eight hydrogen and four oxygen chemical atoms, twelve constituent chemical atoms altogether. Counting the constituents does not mean they have been separated by breaking chemical bonds. The same sample has different counts because the specified entities differ.
For N water molecules, their amount of substance is n=N/NA​, while the constituent hydrogen and oxygen inventories correspond to 2n and n respectively. The exact Avogadro constant is NA​=6.02214076×1023mol−1. The mole applies to specified entities, including chemical atoms, molecules, and ions.[ref] Thus four water molecules correspond to approximately 6.6422×10−24mol of water, but twice that amount of substance of constituent hydrogen chemical atoms. A macroscopic conversion through measured mass additionally needs the relevant molar mass, as in water mass to molecular count.
