Quadratic minor null Lagrangian (Kkγ​ϵαβγ​ϵijk​ui,α​uj,β​)

Words: 65
Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
For constant K, this quadratic combination of two-by-two gradient minors is a divergence. Indeed,
Kkγ​ϵαβγ​ϵijk​ui,α​​uj,β​=∂α​(Kkγ​ϵαβγ​ϵijk​ui​​uj,β​),
(1)
because the term containing uj,βα​ vanishes by antisymmetry of the Levi-Civita symbol. Its volume integral vanishes for homogeneous displacement boundary data. It also vanishes pointwise on rank-one matrices, since να​νβ​ is symmetric. Consequently one can strengthen an elastic energy density by such a term without changing fixed-boundary incremental energies or acoustic tensors.

Brachistochrone problem

Words: 78
Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
The brachistochrone problem asks for the curve along which a particle released from rest reaches a specified target fastest under a uniform gravitational field, without friction. With downward depth u, conservation of energy gives speed 2gu​ and travel time ∫(1+u′2)/(2gu)​dx. The Beltrami identity makes u(1+u′2) constant, leading to a cycloid. If only the horizontal endpoint coordinate x0​>0 is fixed, the natural boundary conditions for a free endpoint select the half arch ending at its deepest point, with depth 2x0​/π and minimum time πx0​/g​.

Travel-time minimization with speed proportional to squared radius

Words: 84
Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
For speed v=kr2 in the punctured plane, the travel-time element is ds/(kr2). Under the inversion in a circle with radial coordinate u=1/r, this becomes k−1du2+u2dθ2​, the arc length in Euclidean space divided by k. Thus a shortest travel-time route corresponds to a straight line segment in the inverted plane, whenever that segment avoids the origin and lies in the allowed region. Between (a,0) and (0,a) the inverted segment has equation u(cosθ+sinθ)=1/a. The route is r=a(cosθ+sinθ), and its minimum travel time is 2​/(ka). The triangle inequality for arc length proves global minimality.

Gamma-convergence (Fn​Γ​F)

Words: 64
Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
A sequence of functionals Gamma-converges in a specified topology when every convergent un​→u satisfies F(u)≤liminfn​Fn​(un​), and each u has a recovery sequence un​→u with Fn​(un​)→F(u). With appropriate compactness of energy sublevels, limits of approximate global minimizers minimize F, and the minimum values converge. This is suited to variational approximation such as the Ambrosio–Tortorelli approximation; it does not describe arbitrary local minima.

Variational problem

Words: 60
Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
A variational problem seeks a stationary point or minimizer of a functional on a specified function space. For a symmetric coercive bilinear form a and bounded linear functional ℓ on a real Hilbert space, minimizing J(v)=a(v,v)/2−ℓ(v) is equivalent to the weak formulation a(u,v)=ℓ(v) for every test vector. The Lax-Milgram theorem gives a unique solution, and J(u+w)−J(u)=a(w,w)/2 proves unique minimization. Specifying the admissible space and its boundary constraints is part of the problem.

Direct method in the calculus of variations

Calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
The direct method takes a minimizing sequence, extracts a subsequence using compactness, and uses lower semicontinuity to show that its limit attains the infimum.

Weak lower semicontinuity of convex gradient energies (uk​⇀u in H1⟹∫F(Du)≤liminfk​∫F(Duk​))

Words: 65
Direct method in the calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
Suppose the domain has finite measure and F∈C1 is convex, with ∣F(p)∣≤C(1+∣p∣2) and ∣DF(p)∣≤C(1+∣p∣). The supporting inequality F(p)≥F(q)+DF(q)⋅(p−q), integrated with q=Du, gives ∫F(Duk​)≥∫F(Du)+∫DF(Du)⋅(Duk​−Du). The last term tends to zero by weak convergence of the gradients and the fixed L2 test field DF(Du). This proves weak lower semicontinuity without requiring pointwise convergence of gradients.

Symmetric elliptic Dirichlet energy with a nonpositive potential (J(u)=21​∫(A∇u⋅∇u−qu2)+∫fu)

Words: 76
Direct method in the calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
For a bounded domain, bounded symmetric uniformly elliptic A, q≤0, and f∈L2, minimizing J on ψ+H01​ solves div(A∇u)+qu=f. The nonnegative quadratic terms are weakly lower semicontinuous, and Poincaré inequality makes the gradient norm coercive on the zero-boundary part. The first variation gives the weak equation with the forcing sign displayed above. If q is positive, coercivity may fail at a Dirichlet eigenvalue; orthogonality to the homogeneous kernel is then needed for solvability.

Screened sine-Gordon energy (E[u]=∫R3​(21​∣∇u∣2+21​u2+1−cosu−fu)dx)

Words: 176 Articles: 1
Direct method in the calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
For real f∈L2(R3), this energy functional on H1(R3) is coercive and attains its minimum by the direct method in the calculus of variations. The nonnegative potential term is weakly lower semicontinuous by local Sobolev compactness gives lower semicontinuity of a nonnegative integral. Its Euler-Lagrange equation is −Δu+u+sinu=f in the weak solution sense. The extra linear restoring term screens the Sine-Gordon equation nonlinearity. The scalar potential V(s)=s2/2+1−coss is convex because V′′(s)=1+coss≥0; hence any weak critical point is a minimizer. The elliptic regularity estimate for −Δ+1, together with ∣sinu∣≤∣u∣, gives u∈H2. The Sobolev inequality then puts u in W1,6, so Morrey's inequality and uniformly continuous integrable functions vanish at infinity give a continuous representative tending to zero.

Failure of the source-size bound for the screened sine-Gordon equation (∥u∥∞​>∥f∥∞​)

Words: 82
Screened sine-Gordon energy OurBigBook.com
Take a=3π/2, L=10 and u(x)=aexp(−∣x∣2/L2) on R3. Set f=−Δu+u+sinu. Both functions are smooth and belong to L2 space, and u∈H1. The strictly increasing reaction h(s)=s+sins has 0≤h(u)≤h(a)=a−1, while
∣Δu∣=L2a​∣4q−6∣e−q≤L26a​,q=∣x∣2/L2.
(2)
Consequently ∥f∥∞​≤a−1+6a/L2<a=∥u∥∞​. This u is a minimizer of the screened sine-Gordon energy: it solves the Euler-Lagrange equation and that energy is convex. Thus even the minimizing and smooth hypotheses do not justify the stronger source-size bound. The appropriate general estimate is maximum bound for a monotone reaction term.

Bounded slope condition

Words: 42
Direct method in the calculus of variations OurBigBook.com
Boundary data g satisfy the bounded slope condition with constant K when every boundary point admits affine lower and upper supporting functions of Lipschitz constant at most K. Comparison with these barriers gives a global Lipschitz bound for convex variational problems.

Comparison principle for convex variational integrals

Words: 28

For suitable convex integral functionals, ordered boundary values of two minimizers imply the same ordering in the domain. Affine minimizers can therefore serve as upper and lower barriers.

Quadratic functional (I(v)=B(v,v)−2ℓ(v)+c)

Words: 44
Functional OurBigBook.com
A quadratic functional on a real vector space combines a quadratic form B(v,v), a linear functional ℓ(v) and a constant. If B is a symmetric bounded bilinear form, its first variation is DI(v)[w]=2B(v,w)−2ℓ(w). For a strictly positive B, an existing stationary point is the unique minimizer by the quadratic variational principle for a symmetric positive operator.

Critical point of an energy functional (δE=0)

Words: 59
Energy functional OurBigBook.com
A field configuration is critical for an energy functional E if its first variation vanishes for every admissible infinitesimal field variation. For E[ϕ]=∫(21​∣∇ϕ∣2+U(ϕ)), integration by parts against compactly supported variations gives δE=∫(−Δϕ+U′(ϕ))δϕ, so stationary field configurations satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation Δϕ=U′(ϕ). A constrained target instead allows only tangent variations, changing that field equation. Criticality is stationarity, not necessarily energetic stability or a local minimum.

Sharp radial Dirichlet energy inequality (∫01​[rg′(r)2+g(r)2/r]dr≥1)

Words: 100
Dirichlet principle OurBigBook.com
For g(1)=1 and finite displayed energy, the angular mode g(r)cosθ has Dirichlet energy equal to π times that integral. The harmonic function rcosθ minimizes the energy with the same boundary data by the Dirichlet principle, giving the bound. Finite energy forces g(r)→0 as r→0: Cauchy–Schwarz bounds changes of g2 by twice the product of the two energy tails, so g2 has a limit, and a nonzero limit would make ∫g2/r divergent. Consequently completing the square gives the exact identity ∫01​[rg′2+g2/r]=1+∫01​r(g′−g/r)2. Equality occurs exactly for g(r)=r. This identity also covers finite-energy functions whose polar expression is not classically smooth at the centre.

Noether second theorem (∑a​[Ea​Ra​−∂μ​(Ea​Raμ​)]≡0)

Words: 106 Articles: 1
Noether theorem OurBigBook.com
Suppose an action is invariant under a local transformation δΦa​=Ra​α+Raμ​∂μ​α for every compactly supported function α. Variation and integration by parts give the off-shell identity
∑a​(Ea​Ra​−∂μ​(Ea​Raμ​))≡0.
(11)
Thus arbitrary local symmetry parameters imply dependencies among the field equations. In gauge theory, these identities accompany constraints rather than independent physical charges for each function. This complements the conserved-current statement of Noether theorem.

Conjugate point

A conjugate point marks a nonzero endpoint-vanishing Jacobi variation and the loss of positive definiteness of the second variation.

Multiplicity of a conjugate point (dim{J:J(0)=J(t0​)=0})

Words: 66
Conjugate point OurBigBook.com
Along a nonconstant affinely parametrized geodesic, the multiplicity of a conjugate point at positive time t0​ is the dimension of the space of Jacobi fields vanishing at both endpoints. The tangential component of such a Jacobi field is affine and vanishes at both endpoints, hence is zero. Uniqueness for the Jacobi field equation embeds this space into the (n−1)-dimensional normal space by J↦Dt​J(0). Thus its dimension is at most n−1.

Nonpositive sectional curvature excludes conjugate points ((∣J∣2)′′≥0)

Words: 64
Conjugate point OurBigBook.com
For a Jacobi field along a geodesic with nonpositive sectional curvature, the curvature convention with positive round-sphere curvature gives
(∣J∣2)′′=2∣Dt​J∣2−2⟨R(J,γ˙​)γ˙​,J⟩≥0.
(14)
Thus ∣J∣2 is nonnegative and convex. If it vanishes at both ends of a segment, convexity forces it to vanish everywhere. No nonzero Jacobi field can vanish at both ends, so the segment has no conjugate points. Only curvature of planes containing the geodesic tangent is needed, and completeness is unnecessary.
