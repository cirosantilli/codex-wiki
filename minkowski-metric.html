Minkowski metric (ημν​=diag(−1,1,1,1))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Minkowski spacetime
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 15
With the mostly-plus metric signature (−,+,+,+) and coordinates Xμ=(ct,x,y,z), the Minkowski metric is the nondegenerate bilinear form
ημν​=diag(−1,1,1,1),U⋅V=−U0V0+U1V1+U2V2+U3V3.
(50)
It is a symmetric bilinear form, but not a positive-definite bilinear form. For example, U=(1,1,0,0) is nonzero yet U⋅U=0, making it a null vector. This does not make the Minkowski metric degenerate: some other vectors have nonzero pairing with U.
Applied to infinitesimal displacements, the Minkowski metric gives
ds2=−c2dt2+dx2+dy2+dz2.
(51)
For a timelike curve, a clock records dτ=−ds2​/c. For a null curve, ds2=0. For spacelike separation, the positive Minkowski interval measures a different kind of separation. A Lorentz transformation preserves this form, expressed in matrix notation by ΛTηΛ=η, although it need not preserve ordinary Euclidean distance in the four-component coordinate list.
