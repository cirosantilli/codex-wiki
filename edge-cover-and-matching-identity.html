Edge cover and matching identity (ρ(G)+ν(G)=∣V(G)∣)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Matching (graph theory)
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
If a finite graph has no isolated vertex, its minimum edge cover cardinality ρ(G) and matching number ν(G) sum to its number of vertices.
Proof. Start with a maximum cardinality matching M. Its unmatched vertices in a matching form an independent set, since an edge between two of them could be added. Add one incident edge for each such vertex. They are distinct, giving an edge cover of size ∣M∣+∣V∣−2∣M∣=∣V∣−ν(G).
Conversely, in any inclusion-minimal edge cover F, every edge has an endpoint of vertex degree one in (V,F); otherwise it could be removed. Thus each component is a star graph: two vertices of vertex degree at least two in one component would produce a graph path containing an edge with both endpoints of vertex degree at least two. If there are t components, ∣F∣=∣V∣−t. Selecting one edge from each star graph gives a matching in a graph of size t, so ν(G)≥t and ∣F∣≥∣V∣−ν(G). A minimum-cardinality edge cover is inclusion-minimal, completing the proof. The constructive first half also gives a polynomial-time algorithm once a maximum cardinality matching is available.
