Raman sideband cooling experiment of 1995

 ... Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Ion trap Trapped-ion quantum control Resolved sideband cooling
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
In 1995, Christopher Monroe and collaborators cooled one 9Be+ ion in a ring/endcap Paul trap. They used 313 nm Doppler cooling and optical pumping into ∣F,mF​⟩=∣2,2⟩. Red-sideband Raman transitions transferred population to ∣1,1⟩, followed by dissipative recycling. Repeating across three directions yielded a reported three-dimensional ground state population of about 92%.[ref]
A variable-frequency Raman transition probe, an exchange pulse and a cycling detection beam converted state populations into fluorescence collected through f/2 optics onto a position-sensitive photomultiplier. Red/blue asymmetry inferred motional occupation; varying probe duration tested the thermal assumption. Off-resonant processes and independently observed heating limited cooling.[ref]
