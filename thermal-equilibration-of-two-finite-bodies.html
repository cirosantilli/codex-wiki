Thermal equilibration of two finite bodies (Tf​=(C1​T1,0​+C2​T2,0​)/(C1​+C2​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermal equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 267
Consider two rigid bodies forming an isolated system, with fixed composition and constant whole-body heat capacity at constant volume values C1​=1kJK−1 and C2​=3kJK−1. Their initial temperatures are T1,0​=300K and T2,0​=400K. Assume each body remains internally uniform, their contact stores negligible energy, no phase transition occurs and no work is done or extracted. These are stipulated model values, not material measurements.
Because dUi​=Ci​dTi​, write Ui​=ai​+Ci​Ti​ with arbitrary fixed reference constants ai​. The conserved relative internal energy is
E=U1​+U2​−a1​−a2​=C1​T1​+C2​T2​=1500kJ.
(1)
This is an energy reference convention, not a claim about actual heat capacities down to zero temperature. At thermal equilibrium the two temperatures equal Tf​, so the first law of thermodynamics gives
Tf​=C1​+C2​C1​T1,0​+C2​T2,0​​=375K,ΔU1​=75kJ,ΔU2​=−75kJ.
(2)
The larger heat capacity pulls the common temperature closer to its initial value: body 1 warms by 75K, while body 2 cools by 25K.
To predict a timescale, stipulate a constant contact conductance G=1/Rth​=0.1kJs−1K−1, the reciprocal of the contact's thermal resistance. The rate of heat transfer into body 1 is G(T2​−T1​), giving
C1​T˙1​=G(T2​−T1​),C2​T˙2​=−G(T2​−T1​).
(3)
Adding proves conservation of E. Subtracting gives exponential decay of the temperature difference, with time constant
τ=G(C1​+C2​)C1​C2​​=7.5s,T2​−T1​=100Ke−t/τ,
(4)
and therefore
T1​(t)=375K−75Ke−t/τ,T2​(t)=375K+25Ke−t/τ.
(5)
Exact equality is approached as t→∞ in this ideal model. Changing G changes the rate but not Tf​: equilibrium and the speed of reaching it are separate predictions. Real bodies can have internal temperature gradients, changing heat capacities and contact losses, so the plotted 7.5s is an assumed model timescale.
