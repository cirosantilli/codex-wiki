Simply transitive group action (∀x,y ∃!g: gx=y)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action Transitive group action
OurBigBook.com Words: 296
A group action on a nonempty set X is simply transitive when exactly one group element sends any chosen point to any other:
∀x,y∈X∃!g∈G:g⋅x=y.
(34)
Equivalently it is both a transitive group action and a free group action; this is also a regular group action. Transitivity supplies a transport, and freeness makes it unique. Conversely, putting y=x in the uniqueness condition shows that every stabilizer subgroup is {e}, since the identity element already fixes x. In a transitive group action, checking that just one stabilizer subgroup is trivial suffices: all the others are conjugate subgroups.
The cyclic group C3​ in the diagram under transitive group action is simply transitive. More generally, the left regular action of any group G on itself is simply transitive: the equation gx=y has the unique solution g=yx−1, using the inverse element of x. This shows why the property is useful: every position has a unique label relative to a chosen starting position.
Choose a point x0​∈X. Its orbit map
ϕx0​​:G⟶X,g⟼g⋅x0​
(35)
is a bijection and an equivariant map from the left regular action: ϕx0​​(hg)=h⋅ϕx0​​(g). This identifies X with a copy of G, but X does not come with a preferred identity element. If x1​=a⋅x0​, then ϕx1​​(g)=ϕx0​​(ga): changing the chosen point composes this identification with right multiplication by a. An old label g therefore becomes the new label ga−1, which also specifies the side of multiplication for a noncommutative group.
The transport from x to y remains independent of the chosen point. Writing x=b⋅x0​ and y=c⋅x0​, it is cb−1. With x1​=a⋅x0​, the new labels are ba−1 and ca−1, and
(ca−1)(ba−1)−1=cb−1.
(36)
For a finite group, simple transitivity implies ∣X∣=∣G∣. Conversely, a transitive group action with ∣X∣=∣G∣<∞ is simply transitive by the orbit-stabilizer theorem. Equality of cardinalities alone is insufficient without transitivity: the cyclic group C2​ can fix both points of a two-point set, giving two group orbits and two possible self-transports at each point.
