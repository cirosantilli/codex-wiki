Fermat number (Fn​=22n+1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 652 Articles: 4
A Fermat number is the positive integer Fn​=22n+1, indexed by a nonnegative integer n. The first five are 3,5,17,257,65537, all prime numbers; the next is the composite number F5​=4294967297=641⋅6700417. This family offers a compact lesson in number theory: a very persuasive finite pattern can fail, while an exact divisibility relation settles a gigantic case with little computation.
Why does the exponent itself have the form 2n? Suppose m=ab is a positive integer with an odd integer factor b>1. The polynomial factorization xb+1=(x+1)(xb−1−xb−2+⋯−x+1) shows that 2a+1 is a proper divisor of 2m+1. Consequently, if 2m+1 is a prime number, m must be a power of two. That is a necessary condition, rather than a promise that the surviving candidates are prime.
The Fermat number product identity makes distinct Fermat numbers coprime integers. Thus every term has a prime factor absent from all earlier terms, whether or not that term is itself a prime number. This gives an explicit route to infinitely many different prime numbers, without establishing that infinitely many terms of this particular sequence are prime. Meanwhile the prime divisor of a Fermat number criterion filters possible factors through a small multiplicative order calculation.
The family also links arithmetic to Euclidean geometry: a constructible regular polygon can have a number of sides equal to a power of two times a product of distinct Fermat primes. The Gauss-Wantzel theorem gives the precise criterion. For instance, 15=3⋅5 and 17 qualify, but 9=32 does not. The word "distinct" matters. Carl Pomerance describes this connection in Primality testing: then and now by Carl Pomerance (slide 18).
Historically, Leonhard Euler records Pierre de Fermat's proposed primality pattern, then refutes it by finding the factor 641 of F5​. His paper was presented in 1732 and published in 1738; those are different events, not competing dates for one publication.[ref]
A short modern check needs only two representations of 641:
641=5⋅27+1=54+24.
(6)
In modular arithmetic these give 54228≡1 and 54≡−24(mod641). Substitution yields 232≡−1(mod641), so 641∣F5​. This verifies the factor directly; it does not claim to reconstruct Euler's search process.
