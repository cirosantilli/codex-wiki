Coefficient of thermal expansion (α=V−1(∂V/∂T)P,N​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermal expansion
OurBigBook.com Words: 209
The volumetric coefficient of thermal expansion is the fractional volume increase per temperature increase at fixed pressure and composition:
α=V1​(∂T∂V​)P,N​=−ρ1​(∂T∂ρ​)P,N​,ρ=Vm​.
(50)
The mass density form requires fixed mass m. Its units are K−1; αT​ is another notation for this coefficient. For small temperature changes θ at fixed pressure,
V(T0​+θ)≃V(T0​)(1+α(T0​)θ),ρ(T0​+θ)≃ρ(T0​)(1−α(T0​)θ).
(51)
For a finite change in a single thermodynamic phase, integrate the possibly varying coefficient: ln(V2​/V1​)=∫T1​T2​​α(T,P,N)dT. A constant-coefficient approximation gives an exponential, while the displayed linear formula additionally requires ∣αθ∣≪1.
For an ideal gas at fixed particle number,
α=T1​,α(300K)=3001​K−1≃3.333×10−3K−1.
(52)
Integrating 1/T recovers the exact fixed-pressure thermal expansion ratio V2​/V1​=T2​/T1​. Combining expansion with isothermal compressibility gives the local response of any smooth simple compressible system of fixed composition,
VdV​=αdT−κT​dP,(∂T∂P​)V,N​=κT​α​.
(53)
The ratio has units PaK−1. For the one-mole ideal gas state in isothermal compressibility, it is P0​/T0​=333.33PaK−1: a 1K increase at fixed volume raises the pressure by about 333.33Pa. Maxwell relations identify this same ratio as the entropy slope with respect to volume and give CP​−CV​=TVα2/κT​.
Under the Boussinesq approximation, the small fractional mass density change produces buoyancy while mass density is otherwise treated as constant. A positive temperature excess θ gives reduced gravity g′=gαT​θ in the convention used in natural ventilation. The coefficient therefore converts a heat budget into a buoyancy budget. The same measurable expansion response supplies entropy changes and heat capacity differences through Maxwell relations, connecting fluid motion to equilibrium thermodynamics.
