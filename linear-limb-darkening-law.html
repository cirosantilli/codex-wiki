Linear limb-darkening law (I(μ)=Ic​[1−u(1−μ)])

 ... Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiance Spectral radiance Limb darkening
OurBigBook.com Words: 221 Articles: 1
The linear limb-darkening law parametrizes angular specific intensity by a stipulated limb-darkening coefficient u:
I(μ)=Ic​[1−u(1−μ)],Ic​=I(1),0≤u≤1.
(20)
Here I denotes specific intensity at one frequency, or radiance integrated over a specified interval of frequency or wavelength; it does not denote radiant intensity. Within this stipulated range, u=0 gives a uniform disk and I(0)/Ic​=1−u. The linear limb-darkening law is linear in μ, while its radial profile I(r)=Ic​[1−u+u1−(r/R⋆​)2​] is curved. In the linear source function example above, u=bν​/(aν​+bν​) when aν​,bν​≥0 and aν​+bν​>0.
For the illustrative choice u=0.6, the specific intensity at r/R⋆​=0, 0.8, and the limiting edge is respectively Ic​, 0.76Ic​, and 0.4Ic​. These are synthetic values, not measurements of the Sun. Real optical spectra and stellar atmospheres need not obey a linear limb-darkening law; more flexible profiles can be necessary for accurate exoplanet transit photometry. [ref]
