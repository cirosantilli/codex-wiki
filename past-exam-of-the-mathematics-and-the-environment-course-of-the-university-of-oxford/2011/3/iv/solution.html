For supercritical flow F>1, the waves propagate upstream. They grow if βe(F2−1)>3δq1​; at leading order in small δ, this holds for fixed positive β,e away from critical flow. They are antidunes, upstream-migrating bedforms coupled to the free surface. Indeed H=−U and S≃−(F2−1)U, so surface height H+S≃−F2U is in phase with the bed perturbation in this regime.
For clarity, neglecting δ completely gives the exact leading-order expressions
Reλ=F2−1βe​γ2+k2k2​,vph​=−F2−11​(q1​+γ2+k2βeγ​).

These also identify the missing qualification in the source: the unstable upstream branch requires F>1. For F<1 this leading model gives decay and downstream propagation. The near-critical case F2−1≃0 needs a different balance; it is not covered by the expansion. Small nonzero ϵ perturbs the slow bedform branch and adds fast water modes, so the stated result concerns the leading slow morphodynamic branch. This is the high-wavenumber antidune dispersion calculation.
