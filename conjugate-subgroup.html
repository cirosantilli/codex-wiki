Conjugate subgroup (gHg−1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 273
For a subgroup H of a group G and g∈G, the conjugate subgroup is the conjugate subset
gHg−1={ghg−1:h∈H}.
(26)
It is a subgroup because conjugation is a group automorphism. Restricting that group automorphism gives a group isomorphism from H to gHg−1, so conjugate subgroups have the same group order. This describes the same operations after moving their reference configuration. Conjugacy of subgroups is an equivalence relation. [ref]
For an equilateral triangle with vertices of a polygon labeled 1,2,3 counterclockwise, its symmetry group is the symmetric group S3​, also the dihedral group of group order six. Let r=(123) be the counterclockwise rotation through 120 degrees, and let s=(12) be the reflection fixing vertex of a polygon 3. Products apply the rightmost permutation first. The cyclic subgroup K=⟨s⟩={e,(12)} contains the identity element and one reflection. Relabeling a transposition by conjugation gives
r(12)r−1=(r(1) r(2))=(23),r2(12)r−2=(13).
(27)
Thus the conjugation action moves K among the three subgroups ⟨(12)⟩, ⟨(23)⟩, and ⟨(13)⟩. Geometrically, the rotation moves the axis of the reflection to the next axis. Each reflection fixes a different vertex of a polygon, although group isomorphisms relate all three subgroups.
The normalizer tells when this change of reference leaves the subgroup unchanged. For any group G and subgroup H≤G, there is a bijection
{gNG​(H):g∈G}⟶{gHg−1:g∈G},gNG​(H)⟼gHg−1.
(28)
Two choices g,k give the same conjugate subgroup exactly when k−1g∈NG​(H). Consequently the index of a subgroup [G:NG​(H)] counts the distinct conjugate subgroups, even for an infinite group. For a finite group the count is ∣G∣/∣NG​(H)∣. These are cosets; they need not form a quotient group, since NG​(H) need not be a normal subgroup of G. In the triangle example there are 6/2=3 conjugate subgroups, while the cyclic subgroup ⟨r⟩ has only one. [ref]
