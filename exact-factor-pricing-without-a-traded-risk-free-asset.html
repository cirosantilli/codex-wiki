Exact factor pricing without a traded risk-free asset (Er=λ0​1+Bλ)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Arbitrage Arbitrage pricing theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 115
Suppose asset returns have the exact representation r=a+Bf, allowing unrestricted long and short portfolios. A position v with 1Tv=0 and BTv=0 costs zero and has constant financial payoff aTv. Absence of arbitrage therefore forces aTv=0, since either sign of v is allowed. The orthogonal complement identity gives a∈span(1,colB). Taking expectations proves the displayed pricing relation, with λ equal to the coefficients of B in a plus Ef. When [1 B] has full column rank, λ0​ is the return of a unit-cost zero-exposure portfolio and λj​ is the expected return of a zero-cost portfolio with exposure one to factor j and zero to the others. Without that rank condition the coefficients need not be unique, and a traded zero-exposure portfolio need not exist.
