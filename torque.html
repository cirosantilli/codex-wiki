Torque (τO​=r×F)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Angular momentum
OurBigBook.com Words: 525 Articles: 3
Torque measures a force's turning effect about a specified origin. The geometry in the angular momentum diagram applies with force replacing momentum: transverse forces give positive or negative torque, while a radial force gives zero. A longer perpendicular lever arm makes the same force more effective, explaining why pushing near the end of a handle is useful.
For a force F applied at position r from a fixed origin O in an inertial frame, its torque, or moment of force, is
τO​=r×F,∣τO​∣=rFsinϕ=Fℓ.
(91)
Here F=∣F∣, ϕ is the angle between position and force, and ℓ=rsinϕ is the perpendicular lever arm. Direction follows the right-hand rule; in the xy plane τz​=xFy​−yFx​, positive counterclockwise as viewed from +z. The International System of Units unit is Nm=kgm2/s2. Although torque and energy have the same physical dimensions, torque is an axial vector and energy is a scalar; their roles differ.
For r=(2,0,0)m and F=(0,3,0)N, τz​=+6Nm. Applying the same force at (1,0,0)m gives +3Nm; reversing it gives a negative torque; directing it along r gives zero. More generally, sum ri​×Fi​ to find the total torque about one common origin.
For a constant-mass point mass, the product rule and Newton's second law show why net torque changes angular momentum:
dtdLO​​=v×(mv)+r×dtdp​=r×Fnet​=τOnet​.
(92)
The first cross product vanishes because its factors are parallel. For a collection with canceling internal torques, the remaining sum is external torque, giving conservation of angular momentum when it vanishes.[ref]
A nonzero torque can turn the direction of angular momentum as well as change its magnitude. Indeed, the dot product gives d∣LO​∣2/dt=2LO​⋅τO​: a perpendicular torque changes direction without instantaneously changing the magnitude. This is the geometry behind gyroscope precession. For a rapidly spinning symmetric top with a fixed tip, centre of mass distance d from the tip, mass M, and spin-dominated angular momentum magnitude L≃Iω, the slow steady precession approximation gives Ω≃Mgd/L, where g is gravitational acceleration. [ref] This is an approximation with Ω≪ω; the uniform precession of a heavy symmetric top retains contributions omitted by the spin-dominated estimate.
