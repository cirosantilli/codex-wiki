Trapped-ion internal and motional states (∣g,n⟩=∣g⟩⊗∣n⟩)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Ion trap Trapped-ion quantum control
OurBigBook.com Words: 992 Articles: 6
An internal qubit uses two distinguishable electronic or hyperfine structure quantum states, denoted ∣g⟩,∣e⟩. The ion's centre-of-mass motion has a different Hilbert space. In one approximately harmonic direction, a number state ∣n⟩ has energy En​=ℏν(n+1/2), where ν denotes a motional angular frequency, not ordinary frequency. The tensor product of quantum systems gives ∣g,n⟩. Cooling towards n=0 prepares motion; it does not automatically prepare the internal qubit. Nor does the ground state mean a stationary point particle: zero-point energy and position spread remain.
For several ions, Coulomb forces couple their displacements into collective normal modes. Each selected normal mode has its own quantum harmonic oscillator number states, while each ion retains internal qubit states. A pulse addressing one ion can change a shared mode. This supplies a route to correlations between distant internal quantum states; it also makes residual motional excitation and unwanted excitation of other normal modes relevant. A single-ion pulse calculation alone establishes no experimental multi-ion quantum gate performance.
