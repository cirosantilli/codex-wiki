For a nonsingular curve y2=x3+Ax+B with integers A,B, the Nagell–Lutz theorem states that a finite rational torsion point has integer coordinates and satisfies either y=0 or
y2∣4A3+27B2.​
(19)
The point at infinity is the identity and is treated separately. Integrality is the result allowed in the source. To prove the remaining divisibility, suppose y=0. Then the doubled point is finite and also torsion, so its coordinates are integral. Its tangent slope is m=(3x2+A)/(2y) and its abscissa is m2−2x. Thus m2 is an integer. A rational number whose square is an integer is itself an integer, so 2y divides 3x2+A. In particular y2 divides ψ1​(x)=(3x2+A)2. It also divides ψ2​(x)=x3+Ax+B=y2. Evaluating the permitted polynomial identity at the integer x therefore proves the divisibility of 4A3+27B2 by y2.
For y=0, the point equals its negative and has order two. The theorem gives a finite set of torsion candidates, but the divisibility condition by itself does not assert that every candidate is torsion.
