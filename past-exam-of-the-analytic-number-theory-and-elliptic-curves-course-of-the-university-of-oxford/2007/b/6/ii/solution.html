For a short Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve with coefficients in the p-adic integers, a point with nonintegral x has valuations vp​(x)=−2m and vp​(y)=−3m for some integer m≥1: the term x3 strictly dominates Ax+B in the equation, so 2vp​(y)=3vp​(x). Its formal parameter T=−x/y therefore has valuation m≥1. These points, together with the identity, form the formal neighborhood E1​(Qp​) of the identity, with parameter in pZp​ and the elliptic formal group law over the p-adic integers. For a point with x∈Zp​, the equation gives y∈Zp​ too.
The formal neighborhood has no nontrivial torsion. Multiplication by an integer coprime to p has series [m](T)=mT+O(T2) and preserves ∣T∣p​, so any torsion there must have p-power order. If p≥3, part (i) gives ∣T∣p​≥p−1/(p−1)>p−1, contradicting T∈pZp​.
The printed hypotheses also allow p=2. In that case, a nontrivial torsion point in the formal neighborhood would have order a power of two and hence yield a point of order two in that neighborhood. But every nonidentity point of order two on a short Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve has y=0 and x a root of the monic cubic over the p-adic integers X3+AX+B. Such a root lies in the p-adic integers, so it cannot belong to the formal neighborhood, whose nonidentity points have nonintegral coordinates. This excludes the remaining case. This is the torsion-free formal subgroup for a short Weierstrass equation. Thus every affine torsion point satisfies
∣x∣p​≤1,∣y∣p​≤1.​
(29)
With good reduction, the reduction homomorphism E(Qp​)→E(Fp​) has kernel exactly this torsion-free formal neighborhood. Its restriction to the torsion subgroup is therefore injective, proving the required subgroup isomorphism. The short model over the p-adic integers is essential to the prime-two argument; no blanket torsion-injectivity assertion for arbitrary models is needed.
