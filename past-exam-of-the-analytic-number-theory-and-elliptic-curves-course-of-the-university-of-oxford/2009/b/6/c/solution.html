Let P=(1,1) and solve ϕ​(u,v)=P. The first coordinate gives v2=4u2. Since u=0, the equation of D then gives

The second coordinate selects the signs. The two rational preimages are exactly
Q1​=(α,−2α),Q2​=(α+4,2(α+4)).
(57)
Substituting into the dual formula checks that both map to (1,1). Their coordinates are nonexceptional under the assumptions, and the degree-two map has no further preimages.
The two-torsion square-class homomorphism αD​ has kernel ϕ(C(Q)), and on these two points it is their nonzero u-coordinate modulo squares. Thus Q1​ is in the image of ϕ exactly when α is a rational square, and Q2​ is in that image exactly when α+4 is a rational square.
If P=2R, then ϕ(R) is one of the two preimages, because ϕ​ϕ(R)=2R=P. Conversely, if Qj​=ϕ(R), applying the dual shows P=2R. Therefore
P∈2C(Q)⟺α∈Q2 or α+4∈Q2.

Finally, if an integer equals (a/b)2 in lowest terms, then b2∣a2 forces b=1. Thus rational squareness of these integer parameters is exactly integer squareness, and
(1,1)∈2C(Q)⟺α or α+4 is an integer square.​
(59)
This is the two-isogeny halving criterion for the alpha family.
