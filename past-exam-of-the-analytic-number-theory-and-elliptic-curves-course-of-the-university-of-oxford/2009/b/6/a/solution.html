We use two-isogeny descent, stating the arithmetic results involved. For
E:y2=x(x2+ax+b),E′:Y2=X(X2−2aX+a2−4b),
(44)
define the two-torsion square-class homomorphism αE​:E(Q)→Q×/Q×2 by αE​(o)=1, αE​((0,0))=b, and αE​((x,y))=x otherwise. Its kernel is the image of the dual two-isogeny formula; the analogous map αE′​ has kernel the image of the forward two-isogeny formula. The two-isogeny rank formula is
2r=4∣αE​(E(Q))∣∣αE′​(E′(Q))∣​.
(45)
The images have signed squarefree representatives supported on primes dividing b and a2−4b, respectively. This prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent follows by valuation comparison: if a prime does not divide b, the valuations of x at that prime must be even, whether positive or negative. A candidate squarefree class d is represented precisely when its quartic covering in a two-isogeny descent
N2=dU4+aU2V2+(b/d)V4
(46)
has the corresponding rational solution, apart from the separately included identity and two-torsion cases; clearing denominators permits coprime integers U,V.
Here a=5,b=−5, so
E′:Y2=X(X2−10X+45).
(47)
On E the possible classes are 1,−1,5,−5. They all occur: the identity gives one, T=(0,0) gives −5, and (−1,3) gives −1. Since the image is a group, their product also gives five. Thus its image has order four.
On E′, the quadratic factor (X−5)2+20 is strictly positive for real X. Every nonzero rational X-coordinate is therefore positive. The prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent restricts its image to 1,3,5,15. The identity gives one and (0,0) gives 45, that is class five.
Class three is impossible. Its quartic covering in a two-isogeny descent would require
N2=3U4−10U2V2+15V4,gcd(U,V)=1.
(48)
Modulo three this is N2≡2U2V2, so three divides N and at least one of U,V. They cannot both be divisible. If 3∣U and 3∤V, the right side has valuation exactly one, from 15V4. If 3∣V and 3∤U, it again has valuation exactly one, from 3U4. Either case contradicts its being a square. Class fifteen is then impossible too, since multiplying it by the known image class five would give class three.
Hence the second image is exactly {1,5}, of order two. The rank formula gives
2r=44⋅2​=2,r=1.​
(49)
This proves the rank-one elliptic curve with coefficients five and minus five calculation, including the local obstruction that removes the spurious extra descent classes.
