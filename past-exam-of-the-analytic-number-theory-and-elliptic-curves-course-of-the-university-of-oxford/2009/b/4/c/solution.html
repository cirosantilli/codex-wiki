In the elliptic curve method, addition is attempted modulo the composite number. A denominator that is not a unit in a ring supplies a factor through its greatest common divisor with that number.
First double P using the elliptic-curve addition formula. Its tangent slope is
m=2⋅13⋅12+5​=4,x(2P)=42−2=14,y(2P)=4(1−14)−1=−53.
(24)
These calculations are valid modulo 221 since the only initial denominator is two, which is invertible. Thus 2P=(14,−53), equivalently (14,168) modulo 221.
To form 3P=P+2P, the chord slope would require
14−1−53−1​=13−54​(mod221).
(25)
The denominator has
gcd(13,221)=13,221=13⋅17.​
(26)
It cannot be inverted, and thirteen is the requested proper factor. The failure has a geometric explanation: modulo thirteen, 2P=(1,−1)=−P, so 3P is the point at infinity. Modulo seventeen that chord denominator remains nonzero. The differing behavior at the two primes is exactly what the elliptic curve method detects.
