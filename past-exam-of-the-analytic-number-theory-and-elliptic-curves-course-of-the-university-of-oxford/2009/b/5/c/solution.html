Since neither n nor −n is a square, both cubics have exactly one rational root, zero. Thus each torsion point of an elliptic curve group has exactly one nonidentity 2-torsion, namely T=(0,0).
Neither curve has a rational point of order four. Indeed on Em​:y2=x3+mx the duplication formula gives
x(2P)=4y(P)2(x(P)2−m)2​.
(41)
If 2P=T, then P is neither the identity nor a 2-torsion, so the denominator is nonzero and x(P)2=m. Thus m must be a rational square; an integer that is a rational square is an integer square. Applying this with m=n or m=−n contradicts the hypotheses.
If 3∤n, both curves have good reduction of an elliptic curve at three and exactly four points over F3​. To see this, x3=x there, so the equation is y2=(1+m)x. For m=1 the right side runs through all residues, and for m=−1 it is always zero; either way there are three affine points. Injection of torsion point of an elliptic curve at this odd good prime bounds each torsion element order by four. An order-four torsion subgroup with just one nonzero 2-torsion would contain an order-four point, which was excluded. Therefore in this case both torsion subgroups equal {o,T}.
The remaining case is 3∣n. The literal hypothesis 15∤n then ensures 5∤n, so both curves have good reduction of an elliptic curve at five. Direct square counting gives
mmod5#Em​(F5​)​14​22​310​48.​​
(42)
For example the square residues are 0,1,4; substituting x=0,…,4 gives this table, including the point at infinity. The pair m=n,−n has residues (1,4) or (2,3) in some order. In either pair at least one reduction group has order two or four. The same injection and absence of order-four points then force that curve's torsion point of an elliptic curve to have order two. Thus
at least one of E(Q)tors​,F(Q)tors​ is {o,(0,0)}.​
(43)
This torsion alternative for opposite quartic-twist coefficients uses only 15∤n; it does not strengthen the source hypothesis to coprimality with fifteen.
