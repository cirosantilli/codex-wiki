Let ωG​=hG​(T)dT. The pullback through the formal group homomorphism is
f∗ωG​=hG​(f(T))f′(T)dT.
(34)
The identity f(F(X,T))=G(f(X),f(T)) and the chain rule show that this pullback is invariant under F: translation by T before f becomes translation by f(T) afterward. By part (i) it is a scalar multiple of ωF​. Its coefficient at zero is hG​(0)f′(0)=f′(0), so
f∗ωG​=f′(0)ωF​.​
(35)
Apply this to the multiplication-by-p endomorphism q(T)=[p](T). Since the linear coefficient of F is X+Y, repeated addition gives q′(0)=p. If ωF​=h(T)dT with h(0)=1, the pullback formula gives
h(q(T))q′(T)=ph(T),q′(T)∈pR[[T]],
(36)
because h(q(T)) is a unit in a ring. Write q(T)=∑m≥1​am​Tm. Then mam​∈pR for every m. If p∤m, choose integers u,v with um+vp=1. It follows that
am​=u(mam​)+vpam​∈pR.
(37)
Choose bm​∈R with am​=pbm​ for those m not divisible by p, and define
f(T)=∑p∤m​bm​Tm,g(T)=∑j≥1​apj​Tj.
(38)
Coefficient comparison now proves
[p](T)=pf(T)+g(Tp).​
(39)
No cancellation of p was used, so this prime-multiplication coefficient splitting for formal groups remains valid when p is a zero divisor in R.
