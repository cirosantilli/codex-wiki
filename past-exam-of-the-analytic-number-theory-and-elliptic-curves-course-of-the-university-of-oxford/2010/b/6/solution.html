Complete the square-class map at the identity by q(O)=1; at the finite point T=(0,0) its value is the class of b. For another rational point write
x=r/s2,y=t/s3,gcd(r,s)=1.
(32)
These denominator powers follow because a negative valuation of x forces the leading cubic term to dominate, so that valuation is even and the valuation of y is three halves of it. The equation gives
t2=r(r2+ars2+bs4).
(33)
If a prime ℓ∤b divides r, it does not divide the second factor, since ℓ∤s. Thus vℓ​(r) is even. All denominator valuations are even too. Every image class therefore has a representative which is a signed squarefree divisor of b, proving that imq is finite.
Use the standard 2-isogeny with kernel {O,T} and dual isogeny satisfying ϕ​ϕ=[2], ϕϕ​=[2]. Away from the exceptional points one may take
ϕ(x,y)=(x+a+xb​, y(1−x2b​)).
(34)
Applying the analogous isogeny to E′ gives a curve with coefficients 4a,16b; scaling its coordinates by 1/4,1/8 gives the dual map back to E. The analogous descent map q′ on D has kernel ϕ(C) and finite image. The sequence
D/ϕ(C) ϕ​​ C/2C⟶C/ϕ​(D)⟶0
(35)
is exact at the last two terms. Its outside groups are finite, identified with the two finite descent images, so C/2C is finite.
For Ep​:y2=x(x2−p2), the rational 2-torsion is O,(0,0),(p,0),(−p,0), of order four. The first descent image is exactly {1,−1,p,−p}: these classes occur on the 2-torsion, and the divisor-support bound permits no others. The isogenous curve is Ep′​:y2=x(x2+4p2). Its real points have x≥0, so the second image lies in the four positive classes {1,2,p,2p}.
Here the kernel {O,(0,0)} of the dual is contained in ϕ(C), since ϕ(±p,0)=(0,0). Thus the displayed map D/ϕ(C)→C/2C is injective, and exactness gives
∣C/2C∣=∣imq∣∣imq′∣=4∣imq′∣.
(36)
The allowed rank formula makes the left side 2R+2, so
2R=∣imq′∣≤4,R≤2.​
(37)
Now consider the quartic relation. Normalize a nontrivial integer solution so that p does not divide both x,y, by dividing them by p and z by p2 whenever necessary. If p∤x, reduction modulo p gives x4=2z2, with z=0 modulo p, making 2 a quadratic residue. If p∣x, then p∤y and p∣z; write x=pX, z=pZ, divide by p2, and reduce to get y4=2Z2 modulo p. Again 2 is a square. Therefore
(p2​)=1,p≡±1(mod8).​
(38)
If the class 2 occurred in imq′, write the corresponding coordinate as x′=2u2/v2. The curve equation then gives
u4+p2v4=2z2,z=4uy′v3​.
(39)
Here u,v are nonzero coprime integers and z is rational. Since 2z2 is an integer, its reduced denominator must be one, so z is an integer too. For p≡3,5(mod8) this contradicts the quartic obstruction. Hence class 2 is absent. A subgroup of {1,2,p,2p} omitting 2 has order at most two, so 2R≤2 and R≤1.
For p=5, direct substitution verifies the nonintegral point
(x,y)=(425​,875​).​
(40)
A rational torsion point would have integral coordinates by the preceding theorem, so this point has infinite order. Thus R≥1, and together with the upper bound,
rankE5​(Q)=1.​
