Finite-thickness Meissner screening (Bx​(z)=B0​cosh(z/λL​)/cosh(d/(2λL​)))

To isolate finite size from quantum vortex penetration, keep the same weak-field London equations but replace the half-space by an infinite slab −d/2≤z≤d/2. Put equal tangential magnetic fields B0​x at its two faces. With z=0 now at the slab center, the even solution and its signed current density are
Bx​(z)=B0​cosh(d/(2λL​))cosh(z/λL​)​,Jy​(z)=μ0​λL​B0​​cosh(d/(2λL​))sinh(z/λL​)​.
(140)
The hyperbolic cosine profile has equal boundary values, while the electric currents on the two sides have opposite signs. In particular,
B0​Bx​(0)​=cosh(d/(2λL​))1​,B0​Bx​​=dB0​1​∫−d/2d/2​Bx​(z)dz=d2λL​​tanh(2λL​d​).
(141)
For the stipulated thickness d=2λL​, the center retains about 0.648B0​ and the mean is about 0.762B0​, even though this is a Meissner state. For d≫λL​, the center magnetic field is exponentially small and the mean is about 2λL​B0​/d, reflecting the two surface layers. For d≪λL​, the mean tends to B0​: little screening is possible across such a short distance. These are analytic model examples, not material measurements. Real finite objects also need their demagnetizing field and edge geometry accounted for; the slab calculation only isolates the effect of thickness.
