Read the coefficient inside the limit as μk​​(n); the printed omission of its index would otherwise make that condition independent of the sequence. Weak-star convergence immediately implies convergence of every coefficient by testing e−inx. Conversely coefficient convergence gives convergence against every trigonometric polynomial. Since ∥μk​∥=1, the dense-test argument yields weak-star convergence to μ.
The limit is necessarily a probability. Testing the constant function gives μ(T)=1. For every nonnegative continuous f, ∫fdμ=limk​∫fdμk​≥0. A Radon measure whose integrals against all such functions are nonnegative is positive, by the positive case of the Riesz representation theorem (or continuous approximation of Borel indicators). Thus μ is nonnegative and has total mass one.
