The absolute continuity of measures condition is
ν≪κ⟺κ(E)=0⟹ν(E)=0(E∈M).
(1)
The Radon-Nikodym theorem says that if both positive measures are sigma-finite measures, then this condition is equivalent to the existence of a nonnegative measurable function h, unique up to equality κ-almost everywhere, such that
ν(E)=∫E​hdκ(E∈M).​
(2)
One writes h=dν/dκ, the Radon-Nikodym derivative. Under these hypotheses h is finite κ-almost everywhere. If ν is finite, then h∈L1(κ). A finite signed measure absolutely continuous with respect to a sigma-finite positive measure likewise has an integrable signed Radon-Nikodym derivative.
The sigma-finiteness hypothesis on the dominating measure cannot simply be omitted. On the Borel subsets of [0,1], let κ be counting measure and ν Lebesgue measure. Absolute continuity holds because the only κ-null set is empty. A Radon-Nikodym derivative would satisfy h(x)=ν({x})=0 at every point, contradicting ν([0,1])=1. Here counting measure is not sigma-finite.
