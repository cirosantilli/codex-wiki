Trapped-electron spin-jump readout

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Ion trap Penning trap
OurBigBook.com Words: 235
Hans Georg Dehmelt describes a 4 K, 5 T Penning trap, with positive ring and negative endcaps 8 mm apart. Axial RF emission tracked an injected seven-electron bunch losing particles; reducing the drive retained the last electron. A weak magnetic bottle converted spin changes into axial-frequency changes. At successive probe frequencies, counted spin flips traced a resonance.[ref]
The inference can be explained without inventing a detector calibration. Let a weak bottle have on-axis field Bz​≃B0​+bz2. The magnetic dipole potential energy adds −μz​bz2 to the axial harmonic oscillator potential energy, so
ωz,μ2​≃ωz2​−m2μz​b​.
(29)
A change in the magnetic dipole moment therefore shifts the monitored axial angular frequency. This is a simplified readout model, not a quantitative reconstruction of the historical bottle. It illustrates how a transition in one degree of freedom becomes a detectable change in another.
For the positive electron spin g-factor convention, uniform-field spin precession and free cyclotron motion give
ωs​=2gs​​meB​,ωc​=meB​,ae​=2gs​−2​=ωc​ωs​−ωc​​,e>0.
(30)
Thus an anomalous magnetic moment measurement compares frequencies rather than requiring an equally precise absolute magnetic field calibration. The free ωc​ must first be inferred from trapped motion with the necessary corrections. A spin-flip count versus probe frequency supplies evidence for a transition resonance; it does not directly measure a spatial electron radius. Nor is this radio readout an optical fluorescence record: different experiments require different preparations and detectors. In a precision comparison, repeat scans with changed drive strength and monitor magnetic field drift, motional excitation and detector back-action; these are experimental-design checks, not additional historical observations asserted here.
