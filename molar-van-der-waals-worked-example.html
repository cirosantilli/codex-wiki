vm,c​=120.0cm3mol−1,Tc​≃320.7263K,pc​=8.333333MPa.

At T=360K and vm​=240cm3mol−1, first convert vm​ to 2.40×10−4m3mol−1. The repulsive-volume term and attractive correction are
vm​−bm​RT​=14.966033MPa,vm2​am​​=6.250000MPa,

so p=8.716033MPa. The ideal gas law would instead give RT/vm​=12.471694MPa. The excluded volume raises pressure relative to that ideal value, but attraction more than offsets the increase here. Neither correction alone describes this example.
For nmol​=0.500mol, the same state occupies V=nmol​vm​=120cm3. The total-volume formula gives nmol​bm​=20cm3 and the same 8.716033MPa. Halving the sample has halved both its physical volume and its excluded volume, while retaining its pressure. Conversely, if the two critical measurements Tc​,pc​ are used to determine model constants, inversion gives
