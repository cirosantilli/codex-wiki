Gaussian-process characterization of Brownian motion (E[Bs​Bt​]=min(s,t))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process Brownian motion
OurBigBook.com Words: 115
A centered Gaussian process with almost surely continuous sample paths and covariance E[Bs​Bt​]=min(s,t) is standard Brownian motion. Here Gaussian process means that every finite-dimensional distribution is a multivariate normal distribution; having separate normal distributions at each time would not suffice. This characterization is stated in Pitman, Basic Properties of Brownian Motion, Definition 15.2.
To compute the Brownian covariance kernel directly, suppose 0≤s≤t. Since Bt​=Bs​+(Bt​−Bs​) and the added Brownian increment is centered and independent of Bs​,
Cov(Bs​,Bt​)=EBs2​+E[Bs​(Bt​−Bs​)]=s+0=s.
(53)
Symmetry gives min(s,t) for arbitrary nonnegative times. For instance, Cov(B1​,B4​)=1 and their correlation is 1/1⋅4​=1/2. Positions are therefore related even though disjoint Brownian increments are independent: both positions contain the displacement accumulated during the first unit of time. Conversely, taking differences of min(s,t) gives zero covariance for disjoint increments; their joint multivariate normal distribution turns this into independence.
