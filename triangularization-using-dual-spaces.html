Triangularization using dual spaces ([T′]B′​=[T]BT​)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Characteristic polynomial Triangularization over an algebraically closed field
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
A linear map on a finite-dimensional vector space over an algebraically closed field has both an upper triangular matrix and a lower triangular matrix in suitable bases. One proof uses invariant subspaces and annihilators to make the induction step entirely in the dual space.
Choose an eigenvector v and let U=span{v}. The dual map preserves the annihilator of a vector subspace U0, of dimension one less. By induction, choose a basis of U0 making the restriction upper triangular. Extend this to a basis of the whole dual space; its matrix C remains upper triangular because U0 is an invariant subspace. In the corresponding primal basis, the matrix is CT, which is lower triangular. Reversing the primal basis makes it upper triangular. Dimension zero and one start the induction. This also allows the original eigenvector to be the first vector of the resulting upper triangular basis.
