Linearity of integration (∫(af+bg)dμ=a∫fdμ+b∫gdμ)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Integral
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
For integrable real-valued functions f,g on a measure space (X,μ) and fixed real numbers a,b, their linear combination af+bg is integrable, and the Lebesgue integral satisfies
∫X​(af+bg)dμ=a∫X​fdμ+b∫X​gdμ.
(15)
The same rule applies to any finite summation: ∫X​∑j=1n​aj​fj​dμ=∑j=1n​aj​∫X​fj​dμ for integrable functions fj​ and fixed scalars aj​. Finite signed values matter: integrability ensures all these integrals are finite, so no undefined ∞−∞ occurs.
This is the distributive property of finite summation carried into integration: a fixed factor can be pulled out, and a finite sum can be integrated term by term. For simple functions, distribute the finite sums of value times measure; approximation extends additivity to nonnegative measurable functions, and splitting into a positive part and negative part gives the integrable signed case. See Lecture 12: Lebesgue Integrable Functions, the Lebesgue Integral and the Dominated Convergence Theorem (Proposition 122: linearity for integrable functions). For example, ∫01​(2x−3)dx=2∫01​xdx−3∫01​1dx=−2. With a probability measure, linearity of integration gives linearity of expectation.
