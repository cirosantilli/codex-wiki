Ellipse (x2/a2+y2/b2=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 955 Articles: 12
An ellipse is the locus of points whose Euclidean distances from two fixed foci of a conic have constant sum 2a. If the distance between the foci of a conic is d<2a, the semi-major and semi-minor axes are a and 4a2−d2​/2. Writing c=d/2 gives the conventional relation b2=a2−c2.[ref]
For an original calculation, choose a=5, b=3, and hence c=4. The ellipse
25x2​+9y2​=1​
(39)
has foci of a conic F−​=(−4,0) and F+​=(4,0). The point P=(3,12/5) lies on it since 9/25+16/25=1. Its focal distances are
∣P−F−​∣=537​,∣P−F+​∣=513​,∣P−F−​∣+∣P−F+​∣=10.
(40)
Its eccentricity of a conic is e=4/5. The matching directrix of a conic for F+​ is x=a/e=25/4, and the perpendicular distance from P to that straight line is 13/4. Therefore the ratio of focal distance to directrix distance is (13/5)/(13/4)=4/5, agreeing with e.
The constant sum can be turned into a construction. Fix a pin at each focus of a conic and pull a closed string loop taut with a pencil. In this example the segment between the pins has length 8, so a loop of length 18 leaves 10 for the two pencil-to-pin segments. Moving the pencil while keeping that loop taut traces the ellipse. This is a geometric prediction from the length constraint, not an assertion about a measured drawing.
The parametrization x=5cost, y=3sint maps the unit circle onto this ellipse. It makes plotting easy, but is an affine map rather than an isometry: horizontal and vertical lengths have different scale factors. An ellipse is not a circle under the original Euclidean metric merely because this map exists. Its two semiaxes and eccentricity of a conic retain the metric information lost by that equivalence.
A useful proof outline begins with ∣X−F−​∣+∣X−F+​∣=2a. Isolate one square root, square, and then repeat; the resulting quadratic equation is x2/a2+y2/(a2−c2)=1. Since a>c, the quantities isolated before squaring have the required signs, so the reverse implication introduces no extra branch. The condition a>c is essential: at equality the focal-distance locus becomes the line segment between the foci of a conic.
