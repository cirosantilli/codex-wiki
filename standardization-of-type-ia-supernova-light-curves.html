Standardization of Type Ia supernova light curves (mB,corr​=mB​+α(s−1))

The stretch method rescales the light curve's time axis by s(1+z) and corrects peak apparent magnitude by α(s−1), fitting α jointly with cosmology.[ref]
Here is a deliberately stipulated local model that exposes the signs. Let tobs​ be observer time in days, tmax​ the observed peak time and T(u) a dimensionless template with T(0)=1. In a single photometric passband, write
u=s(1+z)tobs​−tmax​​,F(tobs​)=Fmax​T(u).
(59)
Here u is rest-frame time in days; the template's argument carries those units. For illustration, assign the intrinsic relation MB​(s)=MB,0​−α(s−1) with α>0: broader events are intrinsically brighter. Define the sign of the K correction by mobs​=MB​(s)+μ+AB​+K, where AB​ is interstellar extinction in magnitudes. Then
mB,corr​=mobs​−K−AB​+α(s−1)=MB,0​+μ.
(60)
Removing attenuation makes the magnitude smaller; standardizing a broad, intrinsically bright event makes it larger. The fitted cosmological time dilation must be removed before comparing intrinsic durations. This schematic factorization explains the bookkeeping; an actual multicolour fit must also model changing intrinsic colours and correlated uncertainties.
For two equally standardized sources, cancellation of their common absolute magnitude gives an especially clean result:
μ2​−μ1​=mB,corr,2​−mB,corr,1​,dL,1​dL,2​​=10(μ2​−μ1​)/5.
(61)
A stipulated residual of +0.20 mag therefore means a distance ratio 100.04=1.09648 and a radiative flux ratio 10−0.08=0.83176 relative to the comparison prediction, provided luminosity and attenuation have been correctly standardized. This is a conversion of a hypothetical residual, not a measured survey value.
