At zero applied field, time-reversal symmetry interchanges M and −M without changing the free energy, so the analytic expansion contains only even powers. The term −μ0​HM breaks this symmetry at fixed nonzero H. Stationarity and local stability require
μ0​H=a(T)M+bM3,a(T)+3bM2>0.​
(24)
For T>Tc​ at small field, the cubic term is higher order and M≃μ0​H/a(T). Thus the susceptibility as defined in the question is
χ=(∂H∂M​)T​H→0​a(T)μ0​​.​
(25)
The three requested free-energy sketches and their minima are given separately above.
For the plotted isotherms, divide the equation of state by nonzero M and rearrange:
M2=bμ0​​MH​−ba(T)​.​
(26)
This is an Arrott plot: its slope determines b, and the isotherm through the origin identifies the mean-field Curie temperature.[ref] Under the paper's stated uniform-field Landau model, use H as supplied on its axis.
The ordinate unit is (104Am−1)2=108A2m−2. Reading the original graph gives slopes about 0.076–0.080 ordinate units per unit H/M, so the physical slope is approximately 7.8×106A2m−2. Consequently
b≃1.6×10−13JmA−4.​
(27)
Approximate horizontal intercepts and the corresponding a=μ0​(H/M)M2=0​ are:
  • 4.2K: intercept −62, giving a≃−7.8×10−5Hm−1.
  • 13K: intercept −40, giving a≃−5.1×10−5Hm−1.
  • 19K: intercept −15, giving a≃−1.9×10−5Hm−1.
  • 25K: intercept +17, giving a≃+2.2×10−5Hm−1.
The sign change brackets the transition between 19 and 25 K. A linear fit of a(T)=α(T−Tc​) across the four read-offs gives approximately α=4.8×10−6Hm−1K−1 and
Tc​≃22K.​
(28)
Interpolating only the two nearest isotherms also gives about 22 K. These are graph estimates, not exact recovered measurements.
Below Tc​ at zero field, the stable branches have M02​=−a/b. Substitution gives the free-energy density relative to the omitted smooth background:
Fmin​=−4ba(T)2​=−4bα2(T−Tc​)2​.
(29)
The equilibrium excess heat-capacity density is cH​=−T∂T2​Fmin​=Tα2/(2b) below Tc​ and zero above it. Therefore
ΔcH​=2bTc​α2​>0.​
(30)
The all-isotherm linear fit gives about 1.5×103Jm−3K−1. Estimating the slope only between the 19 K and 25 K curves instead gives α≃6.9×10−6Hm−1K−1 and a jump near 3×103Jm−3K−1. The lines are not exactly parallel and the intercepts are not perfectly linear in T, so a jump of order a few 103Jm−3K−1 is supported; finer precision is not. This is per volume because F is a density; no sample volume is given to convert it to a total heat capacity.
If b<0, the quartic truncation is unbounded below. Add a positive sixth-order term cM6/6 with c>0. At zero field the coexistence conditions give
Mjump2​=−4c3b​,atransition​=16c3b2​>0.
(31)
Thus the zero-field transition is generically first order, with a discontinuous spontaneous magnetisation and possible metastability. It occurs before the quadratic coefficient reaches zero. The case b=0 is the tricritical boundary of this mean-field model; no value of c is invented.
