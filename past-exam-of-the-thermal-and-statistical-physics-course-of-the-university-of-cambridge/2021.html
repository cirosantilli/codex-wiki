The fixed common temperature, rigid external container and fixed particle numbers on either side make the total Helmholtz free energy the appropriate thermodynamic potential. Let the barrier have cross-sectional area A, and let increasing x expand the left compartment. Then dV1​=Adx and dV2​=−Adx. At fixed temperature and particle number, dFj​=−pj​dVj​, so
F(x)=F1​(T,V1​(x),N1​)+F2​(T,V2​(x),N2​),dxdF​=A(p2​−p1​).
(1)
Thus mechanical equilibrium, p1​=p2​, makes F stationary. The physical force on the barrier towards increasing x is f=A(p1​−p2​)=−F′(x). If the barrier is displaced slightly from x∗​, strict mechanical stability requires a restoring force:
f(x∗​+δx)=A(∂x∂p1​​−∂x∂p2​​)x∗​​δx+O(δx2),f′(x∗​)<0.
(2)
Consequently
F′′(x∗​)=A(∂x∂p2​​−∂x∂p1​​)x∗​​>0.​
(3)
The Taylor expansion F(x∗​+δx)=F(x∗​)+21​F′′(x∗​)δx2+O(δx3) proves that thermodynamic equilibrium is a strict local minimum of the Helmholtz free energy, rather than merely a stationary point.
For individually stable gases, the positive isothermal compressibilities κT,j​=−(1/Vj​)(∂Vj​/∂pj​)T,Nj​​ make the sign especially transparent:
∂x∂p1​​=−V1​κT,1​A​,∂x∂p2​​=V2​κT,2​A​,F′′=A2(V1​κT,1​1​+V2​κT,2​1​)>0.
(4)
If these inequalities hold throughout the accessible interval, F is a strictly convex function and an interior stationary point is the unique global minimum of the Helmholtz free energy. Stability at one point alone proves only the local statement. A vanishing restoring derivative describes marginal stability and requires examining higher-order terms.

J1122​mJ​−1+1−2+2​E/ϵ0204​​
(5)

Z=∑J=1,2​e−βϵJ(eβϵJ+e−βϵJ)=2+e−2βϵ+e−4βϵ.​
(6)

One can make the state labels independent by writing s=mJ​/J=±1, but then E(J,s)=ϵJ(1+s) contains a coupling between them. Hence the proposed J and mJ​ subsystems cannot be treated as noninteracting independent subsystems. The literal two-value reading of the PDF's endpoint notation has been used. (7)

A phase transition is a qualitative change of thermodynamic phase as control parameters such as temperature, pressure or chemical potential are varied. A genuine equilibrium singularity is a nonanalyticity of the appropriate thermodynamic potential in the thermodynamic limit; finite systems generally show rounded crossovers instead.
An order parameter is a macroscopic quantity that distinguishes the phases. It often vanishes in a symmetric disordered phase and becomes nonzero after spontaneous symmetry breaking, although density-driven transitions need not have that simple symmetry interpretation. It can be a scalar, vector or tensor. At a first-order phase transition an appropriate first derivative of the free energy is discontinuous, and the order parameter commonly jumps; at a continuous phase transition the order parameter can approach zero continuously while a susceptibility or a higher derivative becomes singular.
Three examples are:
  • A ferromagnet below its Curie temperature has a nonzero spontaneous magnetization M in zero applied magnetic field. Above the transition, the equilibrium magnetization vanishes in zero field. The direction of M selects a broken-symmetry state.
  • Near a liquid-gas critical point, the density contrast ρliquid​−ρgas​ distinguishes the coexisting liquid and gas. It tends to zero at the critical point; below that point, changing the conjugate chemical potential through coexistence switches between low- and high-density phases.
  • At an isotropic-to-nematic transition of a liquid crystal, the nematic order parameter is a symmetric traceless tensor, for example Qij​=⟨ui​uj​−δij​/3⟩, where u is a molecular orientation. It vanishes in the isotropic phase and is nonzero when the molecules preferentially align in a nematic liquid crystal.
These examples also illustrate why an order parameter must reflect the physical distinction between phases rather than merely be any quantity that changes with temperature.

For attractive nearest-neighbour interactions, J>0, the transformed model is a ferromagnetic Ising model. In three dimensions and at T<Tc​, its infinite-volume spontaneous magnetization has two branches. As the effective field crosses zero, the equilibrium mean spin changes discontinuously from a negative value to a positive value. Since (∂h/∂μ)T​=1/2, varying the physical chemical potential crosses this same first-order phase transition.
The particle-hole-symmetric coexistence line is
h=0⟺μcoex​(T)=−2qJ−kB​Tlnλ3b​.​
(41)
The mean Ising spin is a rescaled occupation density. Writing n=⟨N⟩/V for the physical number density,
mI​=M1​α∑​⟨σα​⟩=2M⟨N⟩​−1=2bn−1.​
(42)

ngas​=2b1−m0​(T)​,nliquid​=2b1+m0​(T)​,nliquid​−ngas​=bm0​(T)​.
(43)

At T=Tc​ the density contrast vanishes continuously; above Tc​, sweeping the chemical potential gives no coexistence jump. A true discontinuity requires the thermodynamic limit; a finite sum over finitely many cells remains analytic.

For the infinite system with outward drift, total probability remains one, but probability at a fixed distance from the root eventually decreases as the walker moves away. The question concerns this local decay, not a loss of total probability.
The independent dimensions of the continuum coefficients are [D]=L2/t and [C]=L/t. The drift-diffusion decay time and its associated length are, up to numerical factors:
ℓ∗​∼∣C∣D​,τ∗∼C2D​.​
(58)
Equivalently, equating the drift distance ∣C∣t to the diffusive width of order Dt​ gives this crossover scale. Rescaling x=(D/∣C∣)y and t=(D/C2)s removes the dimensional coefficients from the equation.
With the conventional coefficients used in the requested continuum derivation,
C2D0​​=2(z−2)2τz2​,τ∗∼τ(z−2)2z2​,z>2,​
(59)
where the last expression suppresses the numerical factor as requested. The elementary drifted Gaussian distribution on the full line has
P(x,t)=4πDt​1​exp[−4Dt(x+Ct)2​].
(60)
At fixed x, its exponential decay factor is e−C2t/(4D), so an exponential e-folding time would conventionally be called 4D/C2, consistent with the same dimensional scale. The reflecting half-line changes the spatial form and algebraic prefactor. The substitution P=e−Cx/(2D)ψ gives ∂t​ψ=D∂x2​ψ−(C2/4D)ψ, displaying the same exponential scale for outward drift.
For z=2, C=0 and no finite drift-induced exponential decay time exists: the probability near the root decays only algebraically. For the finite-bias, deterministic-step Gaussian envelope discussed in (b), the corresponding dimensional expression is instead Dfixed​/C2=2τ(z−1)/(z−2)2. These continuum estimates are not exact return-probability decay rates for the lattice walk at arbitrary z; in particular, a diffusion approximation need not describe far-tail return events accurately at strong bias.
