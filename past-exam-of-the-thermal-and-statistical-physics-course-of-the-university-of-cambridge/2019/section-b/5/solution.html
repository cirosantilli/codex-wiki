Availability and total entropy. The reservoir changes by dUR​=−dU and dVR​=−dV. Its particle number and the system's particle number are fixed. The reservoir's first law of thermodynamics therefore gives
dSR​=TR​dUR​+pR​dVR​​=−TR​dU+pR​dV​.
(27)
Add the system's entropy change. Because TR​,pR​ are constant,
dStot​=dS−TR​dU+pR​dV​=−TR​1​d(U+pR​V−TR​S).
(28)
Thus define the availability
A=U+pR​V−TR​S,ΔStot​=−TR​ΔA​.​
(29)
An additive constant in A is immaterial; its value above the equilibrium reference is the exergy. The Second law of thermodynamics gives ΔStot​≥0, hence ΔA≤0. A stable equilibrium maximizes total entropy over the allowed variations and therefore minimizes A. The environmental TR​,pR​ enter this definition even when the system has not yet reached their values. Because both thermal and mechanical exchanges are allowed, equilibrium here includes mechanical balance; equality of temperatures alone does not ensure the minimum.
Probability and curvature. For a fixed value of a macroscopic coordinate x, let S(x) count the compatible system microstates. The product of the system and reservoir multiplicities is
W(x)∝exp[kB​S(x)​−kB​TR​U(x)+pR​V(x)​]=exp[−kB​TR​A(x)​].
(30)
All factors independent of x enter a normalization constant, giving
P(x)=ℵe−A(x)/(kB​TR​),ℵ−1=∫e−A(x)/(kB​TR​)dx.​
(31)
Here A(x) is the constrained or effective availability for this coordinate, including the multiplicity of any eliminated variables. A nonuniform coordinate measure must likewise be retained or incorporated in that effective availability.
At a stable interior minimum, A′(x0​)=0 and A′′(x0​)>0. Its Taylor expansion gives
A(x)=A(x0​)+21​A′′(x0​)(x−x0​)2+⋯.
(32)
Keeping the quadratic term yields a normal distribution,
P(x)≃2πkB​TR​A′′(x0​)​​exp[−2kB​TR​A′′(x0​)(x−x0​)2​],⟨(x−x0​)2⟩≃A′′(x0​)kB​TR​​.​
(33)
This is availability curvature and equilibrium fluctuations. The quoted fluctuation formula holds at quadratic order in the Gaussian fluctuation approximation; it is not an exact identity for an arbitrary nonlinear A(x). Higher terms can shift the mean away from its most probable value x0​.
Bubble equilibrium. Keep the number of air molecules fixed and include the surface energy in the system's total internal energy. For constant surface tension Γ, this contribution is ΓΩ=ΓαV2/3, with no surface entropy under the stated approximation. Its differential is
dU=TdS−pair​dV+ΓdΩ.
(34)
Hence
dA=(T−TR​)dS+[pR​−pair​+32​ΓαV−1/3]dV.
(35)
Independent thermal and volume variations give T=TR​ and
pair​−pR​=32​ΓαV0−1/3​,V0​=[3(pair​−pR​)2Γα​]3.​
(36)
The air pressure is evaluated at V0​; since the bubble is closed it generally depends on volume, so this equation is implicit until an air equation of state is supplied. For a sphere, α=(36π)1/3, Ω=4πR2, and the pressure jump becomes 2Γ/R, the Young–Laplace equation for a single gas–liquid interface. There is no second surface as in a thin soap film.
Isothermal volume fluctuations. With the air maintained at TR​, eliminate its entropy in favor of its Helmholtz free energy:
AT​(V)=Fair​(TR​,V,N)+pR​V+ΓαV2/3.
(37)
The identity (∂Fair​/∂V)T,N​=−pair​ gives
AT′′​(V0​)=−(∂V∂pair​​)TR​,N,V0​​−92​ΓαV0−4/3​=V0​1​[κT−1​−92​ΓαV0−1/3​].
(38)
Using the curvature result,
⟨ΔV2⟩≃κT−1​−92​ΓαV0−1/3​kB​TR​V0​​.​
(39)
These are the volume fluctuations of a closed gas bubble. Stability requires the denominator to be positive. The negative surface contribution reflects the concavity of V2/3; gas compression supplies the positive restoring curvature. For ideal air, κT−1​=pair​, and mechanical equilibrium reduces the denominator to
D=pR​+94​ΓαV0−1/3​>0.​
(40)
The units are consistent: D is pressure, so kB​TR​V0​/D has units m6, the units of a volume variance. Holding pair​ externally fixed while varying V would remove the gas-compression term and change this stability problem; that is not the fixed-particle-number situation here.
Numerical size of the fluctuations. The volume conversion is important:
V0​=10−3mm3=10−12m3,V01/3​=10−4m,TR​=298.15K.
(41)
Use the specified α=4.84, Γ=0.072Nm−1 and pR​=105Pa. Then
pair​−pR​=32​(0.072)(4.84)(104)Pa=2323.2Pa,
(42)
D=105+94​(0.072)(4.84)(104)=101548.8Pa.
(43)
With the Boltzmann constant kB​=1.380649×10−23JK−1,
kB​TR​=4.1164×10−21J,⟨ΔV2⟩≃4.05×10−38m6.
(44)
Thus the fractional root mean square fluctuation and fractional variance are respectively
V0​⟨ΔV2⟩​​≃2.01×10−7,V02​⟨ΔV2⟩​≃4.05×10−14.​
(45)
The absolute root mean square fluctuation is 2.01×10−19m3. The very small fractional value supports the local Gaussian fluctuation approximation. This estimate neglects exchange of dissolved air, variations of surface tension, shape fluctuations and temperature fluctuations, consistently with the prescribed fixed-number, isothermal bubble model.
