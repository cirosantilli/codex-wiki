Measure single-particle energy from the lowest state and write εk​=ckα, with c>0. In a homogeneous system, the number of wavevector states in a thin spherical shell is proportional to kd−1dk. Changing variables from k to ε gives the density of states per unit spatial volume,
D(ε)=(2π)dαSd−1​​c−d/αεd/α−1,

where Sd−1​ is the area of the unit sphere in d dimensions; an additional internal state degeneracy would multiply this expression by a constant.
The Bose-Einstein distribution has μ≤0. At saturation, μ→0−, the maximum excited-state number density is
nexmax​(T)=∫0∞​eβε−1D(ε)​dε,β=kB​T1​.
(6)
Only the lower endpoint can cause divergence. There the integrand is proportional to
βεεd/α−1​∝εd/α−2.

The improper integral converges precisely when its power exceeds −1. Thus
d>αis the condition for Tc​>0.​
(8)
When d=α, the divergence is logarithmic; when d<α, it is a power divergence. In either case the excited states can accommodate any fixed finite density at every positive temperature, so there is no bulk Bose-Einstein condensation at nonzero temperature.
For completeness, putting s=d/α>1 and using the Gamma function and Riemann zeta function gives
nexmax​(T)=(2π)dαSd−1​​c−sΓ(s)ζ(s)(kB​T)s.
(9)
At fixed density n, the right-hand side reaches n at Tc​; below it the excess particles occupy the lowest state, with condensate fraction 1−(T/Tc​)s. This is Bose condensation with power-law dispersion in the homogeneous thermodynamic limit. A finite vessel or a trapping potential changes the low-energy state count and must not be silently substituted for that setting.
