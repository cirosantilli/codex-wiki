For a single quantum state, the equilibrium occupations are
nˉF​(ϵ)=e(ϵ−μ)/(kB​T)+11​,nˉB​(ϵ)=e(ϵ−μ)/(kB​T)−11​.​
(13)
Both reduce to e(μ−ϵ)/(kB​T) when ϵ−μ≫kB​T.
For one three-dimensional quadratic branch, the density of states per unit volume is
g(ϵ)=4π21​(ℏ22m​)3/2ϵ​.
(14)
With λT​=2πℏ2/(mkB​T)​, changing integration variable to x=ϵ/(kB​T) gives
nex​(μ=0)=λT3​ζ(3/2)​∝T3/2,nclassical​=λT3​eμ/(kB​T)​.​
(15)
A degeneracy factor multiplies both densities if additional equivalent branches are present. The first expression is the noncondensed density: a macroscopically occupied ground state must be counted separately.
Bose-Einstein condensation occurs when the excited states can no longer hold all particles and the ground-state occupation becomes macroscopic. At fixed total density,
Tc​=mkB​2πℏ2​[ζ(3/2)n​]2/3∝n2/3.​
(16)
For field-induced triplon condensation, write Δ=νn−η(H−Hg​), so ϵ(k)=ℏ2k2/(2m)+Δ. At fixed reference n, the three requested dispersions have gaps νn+ηHg​, νn and zero respectively. Their curvature is unchanged. Because the actual thermodynamic chemical potential is zero, the Bose distribution requires Δ≥0. At Hc​=Hg​+νn/η, the minimum reaches zero and the noncondensed population saturates, permitting macroscopic occupation.
For the triplon condensation field scaling, the critical density at onset is nT​=ζ(3/2)/λT3​. Therefore
Hc​(T)−Hg​=ην​ζ(3/2)(2πℏ2mkB​T​)3/2∝T3/2.​
(17)
The low-temperature phase boundary rises from (T,H)=(0,Hg​); the condensed phase lies on the higher-field side.
For H>Hc​, simply continuing the gap below zero would give unphysical Bose occupations. For the condensate density in a shifted-parabola triplon model, added condensate density keeps the minimum at zero: ν(n0​+nT​)=η(H−Hg​). At fixed temperature this gives
n0​=νη​[H−Hc​(T)](H>Hc​),​
(18)
with n0​=0 below onset. At zero temperature it becomes n0​=η(H−Hg​)/ν within that model.
This prefactor is conditional on continuing the stated single-density mean-field shift into the condensed phase. A microscopic interacting Bose condensate has collective Bogoliubov excitations Ek​=ϵk0​(ϵk0​+2gn0​)​, with ϵk0​=ℏ2k2/(2m), which are linear at small k. The normal-state shift alone does not fix the condensed interaction closure or identify g with ν. The gap closes and the condensate density grows linearly with excess field in the simple mean-field description; the detailed collective dispersion needs that additional interaction model. A dilute-boson treatment of field-induced magnetic ordering makes this normal/condensed distinction explicitly.[ref]
