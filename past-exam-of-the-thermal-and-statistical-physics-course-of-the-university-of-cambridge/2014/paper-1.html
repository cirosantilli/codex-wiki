For one oscillator, set x=ℏΩ/(kB​T). Its quantum-oscillator energy and heat capacity are
U=ℏΩ(21​+ex−11​),CV​=kB​(ex−1)2x2ex​.​
(1)
At low temperature, U→ℏΩ/2 and CV​∼kB​x2e−x→0: excitations freeze out but zero-point energy remains. At high temperature, U∼kB​T and CV​→kB​, recovering equipartition. The zero-point term does not contribute to heat capacity.

For N identical classical noninteracting particles, use ZN​=Z1N​/N!. At fixed N,
lnZN​=NlnV+23N​lnT+constant,
(3)
so the canonical partition-function identities give
p=kB​T(∂V∂lnZN​​)T​=VNkB​T​,U=kB​T2(∂T∂lnZN​​)V​=23​NkB​T.
(4)
Thus, for one mole,
pV=RT,CV,m​=23​R.​
(5)
The supplied T3/2 dependence includes only three translational degrees of freedom; additional thermally active internal modes would change the heat capacity.

a OurBigBook.com
For one Cartesian velocity component of a free Brownian particle, the inertial Langevin equation is
mv˙=−ζv+ξ(t),⟨ξ(t)⟩=0,⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=2ζkB​Tδ(t−t′).
(6)
The random force and viscous drag obey the fluctuation-dissipation relation. With a thermal equilibrium velocity distribution, the relaxation time is τ=m/ζ and
⟨v(t)v(0)⟩=mkB​T​e−∣t∣/τ.
(7)
Integrating velocity twice gives
⟨[x(t)−x(0)]2⟩=2ζkB​T​[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)].​
(8)
For t≫τ, this is Einstein's diffusion law, ⟨Δx2⟩=2Dt, with D=kB​T/ζ. In three dimensions ⟨∣Δr∣2⟩=6Dt. At t≪τ the motion is ballistic, ⟨Δx2⟩≃(kB​T/m)t2. A sphere in the Stokes-drag regime has ζ=6πηa, giving D=kB​T/(6πηa).

Landau theory expands the free energy in an order parameter. For a scalar with sign-reversal symmetry and zero conjugate field, a useful sextic even Landau potential is
f(m)=f0​+2r​m2+4b​m4+6c​m6,r=a(T−T0​),a,c>0.
(9)
Equilibrium selects the global minimum; other local minima can be metastable.
For b>0, the minimum changes continuously from m=0 to m≃±−r/b​ as r passes below zero. This is a second-order transition at T0​: the order parameter is continuous, there is no latent heat, and thermodynamic derivatives such as heat capacity can be discontinuous.
For b<0, the sixth-order term stabilizes the free energy. Equality of the ordered and disordered minima, together with f′(m)=0, gives
mt2​=−4c3b​,rt​=16c3b2​>0.​
(10)
The order parameter jumps at Tt​=T0​+rt​/a. The entropy generally jumps too, giving latent heat; metastable branches and barriers permit hysteresis, though equilibrium coexistence itself does not require hysteresis. The special case b=0 is tricritical. Mean-field exponents can fail where fluctuations dominate.

At T≪TF​, Pauli exclusion restricts thermally changed occupations to an energy interval of order kB​T about the Fermi energy. The affected fraction is of order T/TF​; their typical excitation energy is of order kB​T, so the thermal energy correction is proportional to T2 and the heat capacity is linear in T.
More precisely, with g(EF​) the total density of one-particle states per unit energy, including spin,
CV​=3π2​g(EF​)kB2​T+O(T3).​
(11)
For a three-dimensional ideal gas with quadratic dispersion, g(EF​)=3N/(2EF​), hence
CV​=2π2​NkB​TF​T​,TF​=EF​/kB​.​
(12)
The fixed-particle-number Sommerfeld expansion gives U=3NEF​/5+(π2/4)N(kB​T)2/EF​+⋯. The temperature dependence of the chemical potential must be included when obtaining this expression; holding it artificially fixed changes the intermediate energy correction.

For a single quantum state, the equilibrium occupations are
nˉF​(ϵ)=e(ϵ−μ)/(kB​T)+11​,nˉB​(ϵ)=e(ϵ−μ)/(kB​T)−11​.​
(13)
Both reduce to e(μ−ϵ)/(kB​T) when ϵ−μ≫kB​T.
For one three-dimensional quadratic branch, the density of states per unit volume is
g(ϵ)=4π21​(ℏ22m​)3/2ϵ​.
(14)
With λT​=2πℏ2/(mkB​T)​, changing integration variable to x=ϵ/(kB​T) gives
nex​(μ=0)=λT3​ζ(3/2)​∝T3/2,nclassical​=λT3​eμ/(kB​T)​.​
(15)
A degeneracy factor multiplies both densities if additional equivalent branches are present. The first expression is the noncondensed density: a macroscopically occupied ground state must be counted separately.
Bose-Einstein condensation occurs when the excited states can no longer hold all particles and the ground-state occupation becomes macroscopic. At fixed total density,
Tc​=mkB​2πℏ2​[ζ(3/2)n​]2/3∝n2/3.​
(16)
For field-induced triplon condensation, write Δ=νn−η(H−Hg​), so ϵ(k)=ℏ2k2/(2m)+Δ. At fixed reference n, the three requested dispersions have gaps νn+ηHg​, νn and zero respectively. Their curvature is unchanged. Because the actual thermodynamic chemical potential is zero, the Bose distribution requires Δ≥0. At Hc​=Hg​+νn/η, the minimum reaches zero and the noncondensed population saturates, permitting macroscopic occupation.
For the triplon condensation field scaling, the critical density at onset is nT​=ζ(3/2)/λT3​. Therefore
Hc​(T)−Hg​=ην​ζ(3/2)(2πℏ2mkB​T​)3/2∝T3/2.​
(17)
The low-temperature phase boundary rises from (T,H)=(0,Hg​); the condensed phase lies on the higher-field side.
For H>Hc​, simply continuing the gap below zero would give unphysical Bose occupations. For the condensate density in a shifted-parabola triplon model, added condensate density keeps the minimum at zero: ν(n0​+nT​)=η(H−Hg​). At fixed temperature this gives
n0​=νη​[H−Hc​(T)](H>Hc​),​
(18)
with n0​=0 below onset. At zero temperature it becomes n0​=η(H−Hg​)/ν within that model.
This prefactor is conditional on continuing the stated single-density mean-field shift into the condensed phase. A microscopic interacting Bose condensate has collective Bogoliubov excitations Ek​=ϵk0​(ϵk0​+2gn0​)​, with ϵk0​=ℏ2k2/(2m), which are linear at small k. The normal-state shift alone does not fix the condensed interaction closure or identify g with ν. The gap closes and the condensate density grows linearly with excess field in the simple mean-field description; the detailed collective dispersion needs that additional interaction model. A dilute-boson treatment of field-induced magnetic ordering makes this normal/condensed distinction explicitly.[ref]

Use v for molar volume to distinguish it from the fluctuating parcel volume below. The equation is p=RT/(v−b)−a/v2. The parameter b>0 models excluded volume, while a>0 models the reduction in pressure from attractive interactions.
Below the critical temperature, a raw isotherm has a loop. The positive-slope segment is mechanically unstable and surrounding metastable portions do not give the stable equilibrium mixture. Replace the loop by a constant coexistence pressure ps​ satisfying the Maxwell equal-area condition,
∫vl​vg​​[p(v,T)−ps​],dv=0.
(19)
The endpoints are liquid and vapour molar volumes; intermediate mean volumes correspond to phase mixtures with changing proportions. Above the critical temperature, liquid-like and gas-like states connect continuously without a coexistence plateau.
In the pressure-temperature diagram, the liquid-vapour coexistence line ends at the critical point. Below it, the low-pressure side is vapour and the high-pressure side liquid. “Gas” conventionally denotes the region above the critical temperature, where compression alone cannot cross a liquid-vapour transition. The model has no solid phase.
The critical isotherm has an inflection: pv​=pvv​=0. Thus
−(vc​−b)2RTc​​+vc3​2a​=0,(vc​−b)32RTc​​−vc4​6a​=0,
(20)
which give
vc​=3b,Tc​=27Rb8a​,pc​=27b2a​.​
(21)
For fluctuations about a stable equilibrium value x0​, the system plus reservoir has probability proportional to e−A(x)/(kB​T). Expanding availability,
A(x)=A(x0​)+21​A′′(x0​)(x−x0​)2+⋯,
(22)
gives a Gaussian distribution and, to this order, ⟨x⟩=x0​ with
⟨(x−⟨x⟩)2⟩≃A′′(x0​)kB​T​.​
(23)
This requires positive curvature and sufficiently small fluctuations; it is not exact when higher-order terms matter.
The whole cylinder's volume is fixed. For the volume fluctuations of a small fluid parcel, interpret the small quantity as a material parcel containing s moles, whose volume can fluctuate against the much larger remainder. Its mean volume at the critical density is Vˉ=svc​=3sb. At fixed temperature its availability is A(V)=Fs​(V,T)+pbath​V, so
AVV​=−(∂V∂p​)T,s​=−s1​(∂v∂p​)T​.
(24)
On the critical isochore, approached from T>Tc​,
(∂v∂p​)vc​,T​=−4b2R(T−Tc​)​.
(25)
Consequently
⟨δV2⟩≃R(T−Tc​)4kB​sb2T​=3R(T−Tc​)4kB​bVˉT​.​
(26)
Equivalently, ⟨δV2⟩=kB​TκT​Vˉ, with κT​=4b/[3R(T−Tc​)]. The mean-field variance diverges as (T−Tc​)−1, so its critical exponent is 1; the root-mean-square fluctuation has exponent 1/2. A fixed spatial observation box instead has number fluctuations, not fluctuating geometric volume. Finite size and non-Gaussian terms cut off the literal divergence; the approximation fails exactly at the critical point.
