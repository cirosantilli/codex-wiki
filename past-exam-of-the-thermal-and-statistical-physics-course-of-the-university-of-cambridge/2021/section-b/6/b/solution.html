The one-step master equation for the biased random walk is
P(x,t+τ)=w+​P(x−a,t)+w−​P(x+a,t),t=Nτ.
(46)
Expand in a Taylor series and retain the first time derivative and the first two space derivatives. Since w+​+w−​=1 and w−​−w+​=2ϵ,
τ∂t​P=2aϵ∂x​P+2a2​∂x2​P+⋯.
(47)
Thus the usual continuum truncation gives the requested Fokker-Planck equation,
∂t​P=D0​∂x2​P+C∂x​P,D0​=2τa2​,C=τ2aϵ​.​
(48)
The diffusion coefficient has units length2/time, while C has units of velocity. The physical drift velocity is v=−C=a(w+​−w−​)/τ, because the standard advection term is −v∂x​P. A rightward bias therefore means ϵ<0, C<0, and v>0.
The approximation must be stated carefully. The truncation is a controlled diffusion limit of a weakly biased random walk when a→0, τ∝a2, and ϵ∝a, keeping D0​ and C finite. It is also the spatial diffusion approximation for a continuous-time jump process with exponentially distributed waiting times of mean τ.
If the original fixed-step-time walk has a finite bias, its exact variance from (a) instead gives the diffusion coefficient of the Gaussian envelope about the drifting mean:
Dfixed​=2τa2​(1−4ϵ2)=τ2a2w+​w−​​.​
(49)
One obtains the same correction by retaining 21​τ2∂t2​P in the time expansion and using the leading advection equation to replace ∂t2​P by v2∂x2​P. The result is Dfixed​=D0​−v2τ/2. In particular, a deterministic one-way walk has zero variance, as this expression requires. Thus D0​ is the usual intended first-time-derivative approximation, not an exact variance-growth coefficient at arbitrary bias. The later continuum estimates use that convention, with the fixed-time alternative identified where relevant.
