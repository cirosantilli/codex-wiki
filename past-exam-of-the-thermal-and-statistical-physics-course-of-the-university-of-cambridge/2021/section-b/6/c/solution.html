For this radial random walk on a regular tree, the drawing is an infinite regular tree of degree z, not a planar lattice with closed loops. The radial coordinate is the number of edges in the unique path to the root. A non-root vertex has one neighbour inward and z−1 neighbours outward, so equal probabilities for its z edges give
w−rad​=z1​,w+rad​=zz−1​.
(50)
Let r be radial distance measured in edge units. For r>1, probability arriving in shell r comes from shell r−1 by an outward step or from shell r+1 by an inward step:
P(r,N+1)=zz−1​P(r−1,N)+z1​P(r+1,N),r>1.​
(51)
This has precisely the same bulk master equation as the one-dimensional biased random walk, with
ϵ=2w−rad​−w+rad​​=2z2−z​.​
(52)
The outward bias is geometric: beyond the first shell, the number of vertices is Mr​=z(z−1)r−1, so there are more outward than inward choices even though no edge direction is dynamically preferred. P(r,N) is the probability summed over an entire shell, not the probability at one specified vertex; no additional shell-size factor belongs in the boxed recurrence.
The equality concerns the evolution equation away from the root, not equality to the unrestricted full-line binomial distribution at all positions. The root imposes a different boundary rule.
