A nonzero superconducting energy gap suppresses the population of thermally excited quasiparticles. When kB​T≪Δ, their Boltzmann factor produces an approximately activated electronic heat capacity,
Ce​(T)≃A(T)exp(−kB​TΔ​),
(8)
where the prefactor A(T) varies algebraically rather than exponentially. For the rough estimate requested, neglect its change between the two quoted temperatures. Taking a ratio eliminates its unknown magnitude:
Ce​(2K)Ce​(3K)​≃14=exp[kB​Δ​(2K1​−3K1​)].
(9)
Therefore
kB​Δ​≃6ln14K=15.8K≈16K.​
(10)
The corresponding excitation energy is Δ≃2.19×10−22J≃1.36meV. The units mJmol−1K−1 of the two heat-capacity readings cancel in their ratio.
This is a heat-capacity activation estimate, not an exact determination of a gap from two data points. More generally, if Ce​=ATpe−Δ/(kB​T), the same measurements give
kB​Δ​=6[ln14−pln23​]K.
(11)
For example, the low-temperature BCS theory form for a fully gapped isotropic superconductor has p=−3/2 and would give about 19.5K. The stated approximate data and constant-gap assumption support the simpler 16K estimate; they do not specify a detailed density of states or heat-capacity prefactor. Here Δ means the minimum quasiparticle excitation energy, rather than automatically twice that value.
