Landau theory expands a thermodynamic potential in an order parameter, constrained by symmetry. For a scalar with sign-reversal symmetry, a useful free-energy density is
f=f0​+2a(T−Tc​)​η2+4b​η4−hη,a,b>0.
(9)
Here h is the conjugate field. At h=0, minimizing rather than merely finding stationary points gives
ηeq​=0(T>Tc​),ηeq​=±ba(Tc​−T)​​(T<Tc​).​
(10)
The symmetry-related minima represent spontaneous symmetry breaking, as for a simple uniaxial ferromagnet with magnetization as order parameter. An infinitesimal field selects one sign. Above Tc​, the susceptibility is 1/[a(T−Tc​)]; below it the stable-branch susceptibility is 1/[2a(Tc​−T)]. Minimization lowers f by −a2(T−Tc​)2/(4b) below Tc​, producing a heat-capacity density jump Tc​a2/(2b) with continuous entropy.
A negative quartic coefficient requires a stabilizing +cη6/6 with c>0. It can produce a first-order transition before the quadratic coefficient changes sign: coexistence occurs at a(T−Tc​)=3b2/(16c) and η2=−3b/(4c). Odd terms are allowed when the assumed symmetry is absent. Landau theory predicts mean-field exponents; spatial fluctuations can change critical behaviour, especially close to a critical point in low dimensions. A gradient term extends the model to nonuniform order parameters but does not by itself supply dynamical equations.
