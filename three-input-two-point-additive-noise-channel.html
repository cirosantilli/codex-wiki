Three-input two-point additive-noise channel

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Coding theory Discrete memoryless channel
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
Let the input alphabet be {0,1,2} and let the output be Y=X+Z, where independent noise is uniform on two distinct integers. Translating the output alphabet reduces the noise to {0,d}, d≥1. The reachable output alphabet has size 3+min(d,3): it is the union of two three-element intervals shifted by d.
For d=1, every row of the transition matrix has conditional information entropy one bit, so I(X;Y)≤log2​4−1=1. Input probabilities (1/2,0,1/2) make the output uniform and attain one bit per use. For d≥3, the two translated input alphabets are disjoint and every output identifies its input. A uniform input then attains log2​3 bits per use.
For d=2, reflection symmetry and concavity of information entropy permit restricting to input probabilities (s/2,1−s,s/2). Conditional on output parity, outputs whose labels are even integers have probabilities (1/4,1/2,1/4) and outputs whose labels are odd integers have probabilities (1/2,1/2). Consequently I(X;Y)=h2​(s)+s/2. Its strictly negative second derivative and stationary equation log2​((1−s)/s)+1/2=0 give s=2​/(1+2​) and channel capacity log2​(1+2​). All three values follow from overlap of translated input alphabets, rather than from output alphabet size alone.
