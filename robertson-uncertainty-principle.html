Robertson uncertainty principle (ΔAΔB≥21​∣⟨[A,B]⟩∣)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 11
The Robertson uncertainty principle bounds the product of the standard deviations of two observables in the same quantum state. For a normalized pure state ∣ψ⟩ and self-adjoint operators A,B, write their expectation values and variances as
⟨A⟩=⟨ψ∣A∣ψ⟩,(ΔA)2=∥(A−⟨A⟩I)ψ∥2=⟨A2⟩−⟨A⟩2,
(31)
and similarly for B, where I is the identity operator. The quantum uncertainties ΔA,ΔB then obey
ΔAΔB≥21​∣⟨[A,B]⟩∣,[A,B]=AB−BA.
(32)
Here [A,B] is the commutator, and every expectation value is taken in the same ∣ψ⟩. These standard deviations describe the outcome spreads when A and B are measured on separate ensembles prepared in that same quantum state. They are not instrument errors or the change caused by a preceding measurement in quantum mechanics. The definition and bound are developed in Barton Zwiebach, Uncertainty Principle and Compatible Observables, 2013, §§1–2.
For unbounded self-adjoint operators, the operator domains matter. Assume ψ∈D(A)∩D(B), so that both second moments are finite; ⟨A2⟩ here means the second moment of the measured probability distribution, equal to ∥Aψ∥2, even if A2ψ is undefined. To use the displayed operator commutator, also assume ψ∈D(AB)∩D(BA). Without these product-domain assumptions, the always meaningful version on D(A)∩D(B) uses ∣Im⟨Aψ∣Bψ⟩∣ as its right-hand side. These qualifications prevent a formal commutator identity from being applied to an inadmissible quantum state.
The brief proof is Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to u=(A−⟨A⟩I)ψ and v=(B−⟨B⟩I)ψ:
ΔAΔB=∥u∥∥v∥≥∣⟨u∣v⟩∣≥∣Im⟨u∣v⟩∣=21​∣⟨[A,B]⟩∣.
(33)
The final equality uses self-adjointness and the product-domain assumptions. Thus a geometric statement about inner products becomes a constraint on experimental spreads. For nonzero ΔA,ΔB, equality requires u,v to be proportional with a purely imaginary proportionality factor; the equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation turns this condition into a Gaussian wave packet.
The bound is useful because computing a single commutator and its expectation value can constrain two entire outcome distributions. Its right-hand side depends on the quantum state: the spin uncertainty state dependence example gives both a positive saturated bound and a zero bound for the same noncommuting observables. Robertson's paper, titled "The Uncertainty Principle", appeared on 1 July 1929 in Physical Review, volume 34, starting at page 163. [ref].
