Configurational entropy counts the arrangements compatible with a macroscopic configuration: S=kB​lnΩ. Let x=L/(Na), N↓​=N(1+x)/2 and N↑​=N(1−x)/2. The multiplicity is N!/(N↓​!N↑​!). Stirling's approximation therefore gives
S=NkB​[ln2−21+x​ln(1+x)−21−x​ln(1−x)],∣x∣≤1.
(21)
With downward L positive, the gravitational potential energy is −mgL. Minimizing the Helmholtz free energy F=−mgL−TS gives artanhx=mga/(kB​T), hence
L=Natanh(kB​Tmga​).​
(22)
Raising temperature reduces the extension: the entropic preference for many up/down arrangements opposes alignment by the load. For small loads, L≃Na2mg/(kB​T).
For the positive-feedback entropic chain with replacement force f(L)=f0​L/a, f0​>0, the conservative potential is U(L)=−∫0L​f(s)ds=−f0​L2/(2a). Using −f(L)L would overcount it by a factor two. The stationary equation becomes
x=tanh(TTc​​x),Tc​=kB​Naf0​​.​
(23)
Expansion about x=0 gives
F=F(0)+2NkB​​(T−Tc​)x2+12NkB​T​x4+O(x6).
(24)
For T>Tc​ the stable extension is zero. Below Tc​, nonzero minima appear, with x≃±3(Tc​−T)/T​ close to the transition and ∣x∣→1 as T→0. Restricting to downward extensions selects the positive branch. The onset is continuous in this model, with a nonanalytic mean-field change at Tc​; a finite jump in L does not follow from the printed force law, despite the question's discontinuity wording. A finite chain has rounded fluctuations rather than a literal thermodynamic singularity.
This is the same self-consistency equation as the zero-field mean-field treatment of the Ising model: the segment orientation corresponds to a spin, extension to magnetization, and the extension-dependent force to the self-consistent molecular field. The competition between alignment energy and binary configurational entropy explains the shared temperature dependence.
