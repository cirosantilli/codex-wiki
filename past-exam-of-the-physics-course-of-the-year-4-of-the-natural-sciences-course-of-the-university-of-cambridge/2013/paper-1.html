a OurBigBook.com
In a photoelectric effect experiment, illuminate a clean metal photocathode with monochromatic light and use a retarding voltage to measure the maximum emitted-electron kinetic energy. The relation eVstop​=hν−ϕ has a frequency threshold and a slope h. Increasing intensity chiefly changes the number emitted, while increasing frequency increases the available energy per emission. This supports energy transfer in units hν, with ϕ the work function. The retarding-potential measurement distinguishes the electron energy from the photocurrent; the two respond differently to changes of the light.

b OurBigBook.com
In electron diffraction, accelerate electrons through a known voltage and measure the angular scattering pattern from a crystal. Distinct diffraction maxima occur where the periodic atomic arrangement gives constructive interference. Their positions give a wavelength through the crystal spacing and diffraction geometry, agreeing with the de Broglie wavelength λ=h/p, with p≃2me​eV​ in the nonrelativistic regime. Varying the accelerating voltage shifts the maxima in the predicted way. A localized classical projectile model alone would not explain this wavelength-dependent interference pattern.

A2 OurBigBook.com
For independent Poisson random variables with known expected counts μi​, the model variance is μi​. The Pearson chi-squared statistic is therefore
χP2​=∑i​μi​(ni​−μi​)2​=20121​+35121​+44144​+3649​+24144​+11144​≃33.23.​
(1)
There are six independently specified bins and no parameters fitted from them, giving approximately six degrees of freedom. This is far above its expected value six; the approximate chi-squared distribution tail probability is 9.5×10−6. The discrepancies are much too large for a good Poisson-noise fit. Replacing model variances by the observed-count estimates gives the alternative statistic 30.92, with the same qualitative conclusion. There is no fitted normalization to justify subtracting a degree of freedom or rescaling the model to the observed total.

For equally nondegenerate levels, the partition function is Z=e−βϵ(1+e−βΔ). The mean energy per particle is U=ϵ+Δ/(1+eβΔ), so the Schottky anomaly has heat capacity
C=kB​(kB​TΔ​)2[1+eΔ/(kB​T)]2eΔ/(kB​T)​.​
(3)
At low temperature, C∼kB​(Δ/kB​T)2e−Δ/(kB​T) because excitation is suppressed. At high temperature, C∼Δ2/(4kB​T2) because the two populations approach equality and the energy saturates. The peak is about 0.439kB​ at kB​T≃0.417Δ. The common offset ϵ does not affect heat capacity. Multiply by particle number for the total heat capacity, or divide by the corresponding mass for a mass-specific quantity.

The narrow instantaneous beam sweeps around with the particle; over a whole orbit the emission forms a thin fan about the orbital plane. With E≫me​c2, γ≃5000/0.5=104. Expand the propagation distance at small angles:
r≃L+2Lx02​+y02​​−Lx0​x+y0​y​+2Lx2+y2​.
(14)
The first two terms supply an aperture-independent phase. If the aperture quadratic phase is small, k(x2+y2)/(2L)≪1, it too may be dropped from the integrand. The remaining Fraunhofer diffraction amplitude is the aperture's Fourier transform at transverse wavevector (kx0​/L,ky0​/L), as required.
For slit width w and center spacing d, adding the two shifted top-hat transforms gives the normalized intensity
I(0)I(X)​=[πwX/(λL)sin(πwX/(λL))​]2cos2(λLπdX​).​
(15)
The aperture outer half-width is 17.5μm; its maximum quadratic phase is only about 1.5×10−3 radians at the given wavelength and distance, so the Fraunhofer approximation is excellent. Fringe spacing is λL/d=25.28mm and the first single-slit envelope zero is at λL/w=63.2mm.
The side maximum of a finite-width double-slit pattern shifts slightly inward from 25.28mm. Differentiating the full intensity gives, away from zeros,
λLπw​[cotu−u1​]=λLπd​tanv,u=λLπwX​,v=λLπdX​.
(16)
Its first side maximum is at X≃23.88mm with I/I(0)≃0.592. The expected rate is therefore 59.2s−1. Poisson shot noise gives signal-to-noise ratio N/N​=N​, so one needs N=100 counts:
t≃100/(59.2s−1)=1.69s.​
(17)

Dark counts, background subtraction and uncertainty in the reference rate are excluded by the ideal stated noise model.

B3 OurBigBook.com
An ideal linear polarizer projects onto eθ​=(cosθ,sinθ)T. Thus its Jones matrix is Pθ​=eθ​eθT​, immediately giving the specified entries. For time convention e−iωt, a birefringent plate of thickness d produces relative phase 2π(ny​−nx​)d/λ. A quarter-wave thickness d=λ/[4(ny​−nx​)] gives, after removing a common phase, Q=diag(1,i) when x is fast.
For incident linear state E=E0​(cosθ,sinθ)T, the original order gives
QPπ/4​E=2E0​​(cosθ+sinθ)(i1​),I/I0​=cos2(θ−π/4).​
(18)
The transmitted state is circular wherever its amplitude is nonzero. Reversing the components gives
Pπ/4​QE=2E0​​(cosθ+isinθ)(11​),I/I0​=21​,​
(19)
and the output is linearly polarized at 45∘. The incident circular state E∝(1,i)T, called left-circular in the paper's convention, becomes (1,−1)T after Q and is extinguished by Pπ/4​. Explicit Jones vectors fix the handedness convention without relying on an ambiguous viewing direction.
With the stated normal mirror after the original system, an arbitrary incident field is first projected onto 45∘ and then made circular. In fixed laboratory transverse axes, the mirror contributes a common reflection phase, and the reciprocal return plate contributes another Q. The round-trip matrix is consequently proportional to
Pπ/4​Q2Pπ/4​=Pπ/4​(10​0−1​)Pπ/4​=0.
(20)
The returned field is orthogonal to the polarizer and is blocked. This suppresses the stipulated specular return toward a source, useful for avoiding optical feedback. The result concerns that mirror and return geometry; a reciprocal circular-polarizer assembly does not provide the general nonreciprocal two-port property of a Faraday optical isolator.

Configurational entropy counts the arrangements compatible with a macroscopic configuration: S=kB​lnΩ. Let x=L/(Na), N↓​=N(1+x)/2 and N↑​=N(1−x)/2. The multiplicity is N!/(N↓​!N↑​!). Stirling's approximation therefore gives
S=NkB​[ln2−21+x​ln(1+x)−21−x​ln(1−x)],∣x∣≤1.
(21)
With downward L positive, the gravitational potential energy is −mgL. Minimizing the Helmholtz free energy F=−mgL−TS gives artanhx=mga/(kB​T), hence
L=Natanh(kB​Tmga​).​
(22)
Raising temperature reduces the extension: the entropic preference for many up/down arrangements opposes alignment by the load. For small loads, L≃Na2mg/(kB​T).
For the positive-feedback entropic chain with replacement force f(L)=f0​L/a, f0​>0, the conservative potential is U(L)=−∫0L​f(s)ds=−f0​L2/(2a). Using −f(L)L would overcount it by a factor two. The stationary equation becomes
x=tanh(TTc​​x),Tc​=kB​Naf0​​.​
(23)
Expansion about x=0 gives
F=F(0)+2NkB​​(T−Tc​)x2+12NkB​T​x4+O(x6).
(24)
For T>Tc​ the stable extension is zero. Below Tc​, nonzero minima appear, with x≃±3(Tc​−T)/T​ close to the transition and ∣x∣→1 as T→0. Restricting to downward extensions selects the positive branch. The onset is continuous in this model, with a nonanalytic mean-field change at Tc​; a finite jump in L does not follow from the printed force law, despite the question's discontinuity wording. A finite chain has rounded fluctuations rather than a literal thermodynamic singularity.
This is the same self-consistency equation as the zero-field mean-field treatment of the Ising model: the segment orientation corresponds to a spin, extension to magnetization, and the extension-dependent force to the self-consistent molecular field. The competition between alignment energy and binary configurational entropy explains the shared temperature dependence.

In the Sz​ basis α,β, Sx​=(ℏ/2)(01​10​). Its normalized eigenstates are (α+β)/2​ and (α−β)/2​, with eigenvalues +ℏ/2 and −ℏ/2. Expanding the initial state in this basis and evolving the two phases gives, with Ω=λB,
∣ψ(t)⟩=cos(Ωt/2)α−isin(Ωt/2)β,P(Sz​=+ℏ/2)=cos2(Ωt/2).​
(25)
The sign of Ω fixes rotation sense; the probability is insensitive to that sign.
In the Stern-Gerlach experiment, a collimated atomic beam passes through a strongly nonuniform magnetic field. The force component Fz​≃μz​∂z​Bz​ separates magnetic-moment projections spatially. Silver atoms provide an effective spin-half moment, yielding two resolved components rather than a continuous distribution of deflections. To test the rotation above, use a first analyzer and aperture to prepare +z, pass that selected beam through a controlled approximately uniform x-directed field, and analyze it with a second z-oriented Stern-Gerlach magnet. Varying field strength or interaction time changes the two output populations as the calculated cosine and sine squares. A narrow velocity distribution and controlled field-entry geometry preserve the contrast by reducing variations of interaction time and unwanted spin evolution.

For charge per length λq​, Gauss's law gives Er​=λq​/(2πϵ0​r) between the conductors. Integration gives V=λq​ln(b/a)/(2πϵ0​). Similarly Ampère's law gives Bϕ​=μ0​I/(2πr); integrating the magnetic energy per length and setting it equal to LI2/2 gives

Internal magnetic energy in conductors with appreciable current penetration adds internal inductance; it cannot be determined fully from an outer conductor with unspecified wall geometry.
The distributed series resistance and inductance, with shunt capacitance and negligible leakage, give the telegrapher's equations

k≃ωLC​+i2R​LC​​,vph​≃LC​1​=c,ℓamp​=R2​CL​​.​
