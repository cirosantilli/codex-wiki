A1 OurBigBook.com
For an infinite cubic potential well of side L, the one-particle energy eigenvalues are Enx​ny​nz​​=E0​(nx2​+ny2​+nz2​), where E0​=π2ℏ2/(2mL2) and each quantum number is a positive integer. The lowest spatial orbital is (1,1,1), with energy 3E0​ and orbital degeneracy one. The next energy is 6E0​, with three orbitals given by permutations of (2,1,1).
Spin-zero identical particles are bosons. Both can occupy the lowest orbital, so the two-boson ground state is nondegenerate. The first excited total energy is 9E0​: one particle occupies the lowest orbital and one an excited orbital. Symmetrizing the two-particle wavefunction gives one state for each of the three excited orbitals, so the first-excited degeneracy is three.
Spin-half identical particles are fermions, so the total state must be antisymmetric. Two particles in the same lowest orbital have a symmetric spatial wavefunction and must form the unique spin singlet state. Thus the fermion ground state is also nondegenerate. At the first excited energy, each choice of excited spatial orbital permits a symmetric spatial state with a singlet state, or an antisymmetric spatial state with a three-dimensional triplet state. Therefore
gground​=1,gfirst​=3(1+3)=12for spin 21​.​
(1)
The spin multiplicity is essential: counting only the three orbital choices would omit allowed triplet states. The result assumes ordinary spin-statistics, an unsplit spin degeneracy and the hard-wall cube model.

For a homogeneous ideal gas in a two-dimensional box of area A, with dispersion ϵ=p2/(2m) and one internal state, the density of states is g(ϵ)=Am/(2πℏ2). The excited-particle density at temperature T>0 is
nex​=2πℏ2m​∫0∞​z−1eϵ/(kB​T)−1dϵ​=−2πℏ2mkB​T​ln(1−z),z=eμ/(kB​T)<1.
(6)
As μ→0−, this diverges. Hence at every fixed positive temperature the excited states can accommodate any finite areal density with μ<0; they never saturate at a finite critical density. There is no finite-temperature Bose-Einstein condensation in the homogeneous two-dimensional ideal gas thermodynamic limit.
The low-energy logarithmic divergence is the reason, not merely the small number of spatial dimensions. A finite box has a low-energy cutoff and a crossover; a trap changes the density of states and can change the conclusion. Condensation at zero temperature and an interacting gas's Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition are separate issues. The proof assumes the uniform quadratic-dispersion model, rather than every conceivable two-dimensional trapping potential.

Three experimental tests probe different consequences of special relativity. The Michelson-Morley experiment compares light round trips along perpendicular interferometer arms while changing the apparatus orientation. Its small observed orientation-dependent fringe shift contradicts the simple stationary-aether prediction and supports orientation-independent round-trip light speed. It does not, alone, exclude every alternative theory.[ref]
In the muon storage-ring time-dilation experiment, fast circulating muons live longer in laboratory time than muons at rest. Measuring decay counts versus elapsed laboratory time and independently determining the beam Lorentz factor tests τlab​=γτ0​. Circular motion includes acceleration, so this also tests the applicability of accumulated proper time along the stored trajectory.[ref]
Bertozzi's electron speed-energy experiment measures electron time of flight and deposited kinetic energy independently. An electrostatic accelerator and a linear accelerator increase the energy while the measured speed approaches the light-speed limit. Calorimetry checks that the additional beam energy is real, rather than inferring it only from an assumed voltage-to-speed relation. The result supports relativistic K=(γ−1)mc2 rather than extending K=mv2/2 to arbitrarily large speeds.[ref]

The physical origin is a rotational Doppler effect and angular-momentum exchange. In the stated convention, the first fixed quarter-wave plate changes the 45-degree input into circular polarization of spin +ℏ per photon. The rotating half-wave plate flips it to spin −ℏ, transferring 2ℏ to the rotating plate. Maintaining its rotation takes mechanical energy 2ℏΩ from each photon, agreeing with the redshift. The final fixed plate restores linear polarization; fixed plates can exchange angular momentum with their supports without the rotating element's corresponding mechanical work. The instantaneous Jones treatment assumes slow rotation compared with optical and transit-time scales and ideal retardances over the small shifted bandwidth.
