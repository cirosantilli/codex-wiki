A time-phasor convention must be changed consistently with the plate matrices, rather than used alone to change a physical redshift into a blueshift.
The physical origin is a rotational Doppler effect and angular-momentum exchange. In the stated convention, the first fixed quarter-wave plate changes the 45-degree input into circular polarization of spin +ℏ per photon. The rotating half-wave plate flips it to spin −ℏ, transferring 2ℏ to the rotating plate. Maintaining its rotation takes mechanical energy 2ℏΩ from each photon, agreeing with the redshift. The final fixed plate restores linear polarization; fixed plates can exchange angular momentum with their supports without the rotating element's corresponding mechanical work. The instantaneous Jones treatment assumes slow rotation compared with optical and transit-time scales and ideal retardances over the small shifted bandwidth.
