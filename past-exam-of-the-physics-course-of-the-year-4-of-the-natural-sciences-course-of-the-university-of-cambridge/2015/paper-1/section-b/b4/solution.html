Three experimental tests probe different consequences of special relativity. The Michelson-Morley experiment compares light round trips along perpendicular interferometer arms while changing the apparatus orientation. Its small observed orientation-dependent fringe shift contradicts the simple stationary-aether prediction and supports orientation-independent round-trip light speed. It does not, alone, exclude every alternative theory.[ref]
In the muon storage-ring time-dilation experiment, fast circulating muons live longer in laboratory time than muons at rest. Measuring decay counts versus elapsed laboratory time and independently determining the beam Lorentz factor tests τlab​=γτ0​. Circular motion includes acceleration, so this also tests the applicability of accumulated proper time along the stored trajectory.[ref]
Bertozzi's electron speed-energy experiment measures electron time of flight and deposited kinetic energy independently. An electrostatic accelerator and a linear accelerator increase the energy while the measured speed approaches the light-speed limit. Calorimetry checks that the additional beam energy is real, rather than inferring it only from an assumed voltage-to-speed relation. The result supports relativistic K=(γ−1)mc2 rather than extending K=mv2/2 to arbitrarily large speeds.[ref]
