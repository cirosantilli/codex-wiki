The electric displacement field is D=ϵ0​E+P. A homogeneous linear isotropic dielectric has P=ϵ0​χe​E, so D=ϵ0​ϵr​E. At a free-charge-free interface the normal component of D is continuous, while electrostatics requires continuity of tangential E.
At half immersion the conductor centre lies in the flat air-liquid interface. Set ϕ=C/r in both media, with potential zero at infinity. The sphere is an equipotential, and the potential is continuous across the liquid plane. On that plane the radial E field is tangential, so tangential E agrees while both normal D components vanish. Each half-space also satisfies the source-free potential equation. The fields outside the sphere are

C=2πϵ0​(1+ϵr​)Q​,V=2πϵ0​(1+ϵr​)aQ​.​

The half-immersed conducting sphere in a dielectric has radial E lines with equal field strength at equal radius on either side, whereas the D flux density is ϵr​ times larger in the liquid. The corresponding free surface charge is larger on the lower hemisphere. The unchanged field direction at this particular interface follows from its tangential geometry, not from a claim that electric fields never refract at dielectric interfaces.
The outward electrostatic pressure is pe​=E⋅D/2. Integrating its vertical component over the two hemispheres uses ∫upper​cosθdA=πa2 and the negative of that integral below. Thus

This uses the ideal flat-interface, linear-dielectric model with surface tension and interface deformation neglected. If ϵr​=1, there is no electric force of this kind, consistently preventing this mechanism from pulling an initially less-than-half-immersed sphere down to the specified level.
