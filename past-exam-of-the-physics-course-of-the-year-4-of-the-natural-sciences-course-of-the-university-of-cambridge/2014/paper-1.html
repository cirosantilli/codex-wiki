For the photopion threshold on a photon background, write M=mp​c2 and m=mπ​c2 in energy units. For a head-on photon of energy ϵ and a highly relativistic proton of total energy E, the invariant squared centre-of-momentum energy is approximately M2+4Eϵ. At threshold the final proton and pion are at rest relative to one another, so
M2+4Eth​ϵ=(M+m)2,Eth​=4ϵm(2M+m)​.​
(3)
Using a typical CMB photon energy ϵ≃2.70kB​T=6.28×10−4eV gives
Eth​≃1.1×1020eV.​
(4)
Thus photopion losses suppress the arrival of protons of order 1020eV from sufficiently distant sources. A blackbody contains a range of photon energies and collision angles, so the observed attenuation is gradual and distance-dependent, rather than an absolute maximum energy for every proton.

Zero-point energy is the ground-state energy above the classical minimum, remaining even at zero temperature. For a harmonic oscillator it is ℏω/2; simultaneous vanishing position and momentum fluctuations is forbidden by the uncertainty principle.
A concrete test is the Casimir interaction: changing conducting boundaries changes electromagnetic ground-state modes and produces a measurable force. Ideal parallel plates have pressure
PC​=−240a4π2ℏc​.​
(7)
A torsion-pendulum experiment measured the attraction of closely spaced conducting surfaces, testing this boundary-dependent interaction.[ref] Such measurements test changes in ground-state energy, not an arbitrary absolute additive vacuum energy; real-material and thermal corrections must be included when comparing precision data.

3p4s:1P1​,3P0,1,2​.​

B1 OurBigBook.com
For an isolated system with equally probable accessible microstates, Boltzmann entropy is S=kB​lnΩ. The second law states that an isolated system's total entropy cannot decrease; it remains constant in a reversible process and increases in an irreversible one.
The insulated mixture exchanges no heat with its surroundings. At constant pressure, with only pressure-volume work, its enthalpy is conserved. Constant and equal specific heat capacities therefore give Tf​=(T1​+T2​)/2. Calculate each entropy change along a hypothetical reversible temperature path:
ΔS=McP​lnT1​Tf​​+McP​lnT2​Tf​​=2McP​ln[2T1​T2​​T1​+T2​​].​
(16)
The surroundings gain no entropy through heat exchange; ideal mechanical work carries no entropy. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives ΔS≥0, with equality only for T1​=T2​. Mixing unequal temperatures is irreversible even though total energy is conserved.

Perturbation theory expands known eigenvalues and eigenfunctions about a solvable Hamiltonian. Small corrections require appropriate energy-gap and matrix-element scales; degeneracies must first be diagonalized within their subspace. The variational method instead minimizes a normalized trial state's energy expectation. It gives an upper bound on the ground energy without requiring a small perturbation; excited-state bounds need orthogonality constraints or an appropriate symmetry subspace.
Normalize the unperturbed oscillator states:
∣ψ0​∣2=π​α​e−α2x2,∣ψ1​∣2=π​2α3​x2e−α2x2.
(17)
For the Gaussian perturbation of a quantum harmonic oscillator, the first-order shifts are its expectation values:
ΔE0​=α2+β2​V0​α​,ΔE1​=(α2+β2)3/2V0​α3​.​
(18)
For β≫α, they scale as V0​α/β and V0​(α/β)3 respectively: a narrow central perturbation couples weakly to the odd state's central node. For β≪α, both approach V0​ because the perturbation is nearly constant over the wavefunctions. The leading corrections are V0​[1−β2/(2α2)] and V0​[1−3β2/(2α2)].
For an improved variational estimate, replace the fixed width α by an adjustable positive width s in each even/odd Gaussian. Their energy functionals are
Eeven​(s)=4mℏ2s2​+4s2mω2​+s2+β2​V0​s​,
(19)
Eodd​(s)=4m3ℏ2s2​+4s23mω2​+(s2+β2)3/2V0​s3​.
(20)
Minimize each separately. At s=α they recover the unperturbed energy plus the first-order shift, so optimization cannot raise those trial estimates. For this even one-dimensional potential the lowest odd state is the first excited state, making its odd-sector minimum an upper bound on E1​. Enlarging each parity-preserving trial basis can improve the estimates further.

For noninteracting particles, a many-particle configuration has E=∑j​nj​ϵj​ and N=∑j​nj​. Summing independently over allowed occupations therefore factorizes the grand partition function:
Θ=∏j​Θj​,Φ=−kB​Tj∑​lnΘj​.​
(21)
At fixed T,V, differentiating the original state sum gives ∂μ​lnΘ=β⟨N⟩ and ∂μ2​lnΘ=β2VarN, so
⟨N⟩=−∂μ​Φ,VarN=−kB​T∂μ2​Φ=kB​T∂μ​⟨N⟩.​
(22)
For one level, write q=e−β(ϵ−μ). Fermions allow n=0,1, giving ΘF​=1+q and nˉ=q/(1+q). Bosons allow all nonnegative integers, giving ΘB​=1/(1−q) and nˉ=q/(1−q), with q<1. Thus
nˉF​=eβ(ϵ−μ)+11​,nˉB​=eβ(ϵ−μ)−11​.​
(23)
The grand-canonical single-level occupation variance follows from the same derivative identity applied to one level:
Varn=kB​T∂μ​nˉ={nˉ(1−nˉ),nˉ(1+nˉ),​fermions,bosons.​​
(24)
In the high-temperature nondegenerate regime, nˉ≪1 and both approach classical Poisson-like fluctuations, Varn≃nˉ. At low temperature, nearly filled or empty fermion levels have strongly suppressed fluctuations; only the Fermi-surface window changes occupation. Highly occupied low-energy boson levels can instead have Varn∼nˉ2, showing bosonic enhancement. This last statement depends on occupation and reservoir chemical potential, not temperature alone; grand-canonical condensate fluctuations must not be confused with a fixed-total-number ensemble.

The method of images replaces conductors by fictitious charges outside the physical solution domain. Choose images so the real-plus-image potential obeys the specified conductor boundary values. The electrostatic uniqueness theorem then identifies that potential in the physical domain; fields inside the replaced conductor are not physical predictions.
Put the cylinder axis at the origin, real line at (2a,0) and image at (a/2,0). Let r1​,r2​ be distances from these lines. The exterior potential can be written
ϕ=2πϵ0​λ​lnr1​2r2​​.​
(30)
On the surface, r12​=a2(5−4cosθ) and r22​=r12​/4, so ϕ=0 everywhere as required. The additive constant is essential for a grounded cylinder; the two-dimensional potential need not also be zero at infinity.
The physical line feels the image field at separation 3a/2. Its force per unit length is attractive:
LF​=−3πϵ0​aλ2​x^.​
(31)
Measure θ from the radius toward the real line, as in the original diagram. The radial field just outside the surface is
Er​=2πϵ0​λ​[a2(5−4cosθ)a−2acosθ​−(a2/4)(5−4cosθ)a−(a/2)cosθ​].
(32)
Since the interior field is zero,
σ(θ)=ϵ0​Er​=−2πa(5−4cosθ)3λ​.​
(33)
Integrating aσ(θ) around the circumference gives total induced charge −λ per unit length.

A normal mode is a motion in which all generalized coordinates oscillate at one frequency with fixed amplitude ratios. Small oscillations about stable equilibrium in a time-independent conservative system give Mθ¨+Kθ=0, with positive mass matrix and symmetric stiffness matrix, allowing a generalized normal-mode eigenproblem.
For the three-to-one mass double-pendulum modes, the angles are absolute displacements from vertical, as printed. To quadratic order,
K=2mℓ2​(4θ˙12​+2θ˙1​θ˙2​+θ˙22​),U=2mgℓ​(4θ12​+θ22​).
(34)
For θ=Acosωt and q=ω2ℓ/g, the determinant is
det(4−4q−q​−q1−q​)=3q2−8q+4=(3q−2)(q−2).
(35)
Thus
ω−2​=3ℓ2g​,(θ1​,θ2​)∝(1,2);ω+2​=ℓ2g​,(θ1​,θ2​)∝(1,−2).​
(36)
The low mode has the strings leaning together, with the lower angle twice as large; the high mode has opposite angular signs. Both reverse together after half their respective periods.
For the specified release, decompose (α,α)=(3α/4)(1,2)+(α/4)(1,−2). Therefore
θ1​=4α​(3cosω−​t+cosω+​t),θ2​=2α​(3cosω−​t−cosω+​t),
(37)
and the lower mass's horizontal displacement is
xm​≃ℓ(θ1​+θ2​)=4αℓ​(9cosω−​t−cosω+​t),sup∣xm​∣=25​∣α∣ℓ.​
(38)
This is the supremum of two incommensurate harmonic modes, conventionally described as the maximum-amplitude estimate. Strictly, ω+​/ω−​=3​ is irrational, so the opposite cosine extrema are approached arbitrarily closely but never coincide at a finite time. The quoted value is a supremum of the ideal linearized motion.

Fresnel diffraction retains quadratic aperture-dependent propagation phases and generally depends on both aperture and screen distance. Fraunhofer diffraction neglects those phases and gives the Fourier transform of the aperture in angle space, either sufficiently far away or in a suitable lens's focal plane.
The calculations below use the Fraunhofer, paraxial approximation. For an overall aperture scale D∼1000λ, free propagation requires L≫D2/λ∼106λ. The printed L≫1000λ alone only makes the separation small relative to distance; it is not by itself the full far-field condition.
