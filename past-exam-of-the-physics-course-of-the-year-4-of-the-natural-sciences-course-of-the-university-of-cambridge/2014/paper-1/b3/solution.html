For noninteracting particles, a many-particle configuration has E=∑j​nj​ϵj​ and N=∑j​nj​. Summing independently over allowed occupations therefore factorizes the grand partition function:
Θ=∏j​Θj​,Φ=−kB​Tj∑​lnΘj​.​
(21)
At fixed T,V, differentiating the original state sum gives ∂μ​lnΘ=β⟨N⟩ and ∂μ2​lnΘ=β2VarN, so
⟨N⟩=−∂μ​Φ,VarN=−kB​T∂μ2​Φ=kB​T∂μ​⟨N⟩.​
(22)
For one level, write q=e−β(ϵ−μ). Fermions allow n=0,1, giving ΘF​=1+q and nˉ=q/(1+q). Bosons allow all nonnegative integers, giving ΘB​=1/(1−q) and nˉ=q/(1−q), with q<1. Thus
nˉF​=eβ(ϵ−μ)+11​,nˉB​=eβ(ϵ−μ)−11​.​
(23)
The grand-canonical single-level occupation variance follows from the same derivative identity applied to one level:
Varn=kB​T∂μ​nˉ={nˉ(1−nˉ),nˉ(1+nˉ),​fermions,bosons.​​
(24)
In the high-temperature nondegenerate regime, nˉ≪1 and both approach classical Poisson-like fluctuations, Varn≃nˉ. At low temperature, nearly filled or empty fermion levels have strongly suppressed fluctuations; only the Fermi-surface window changes occupation. Highly occupied low-energy boson levels can instead have Varn∼nˉ2, showing bosonic enhancement. This last statement depends on occupation and reservoir chemical potential, not temperature alone; grand-canonical condensate fluctuations must not be confused with a fixed-total-number ensemble.
