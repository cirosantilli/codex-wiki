The method of images replaces conductors by fictitious charges outside the physical solution domain. Choose images so the real-plus-image potential obeys the specified conductor boundary values. The electrostatic uniqueness theorem then identifies that potential in the physical domain; fields inside the replaced conductor are not physical predictions.
Put the cylinder axis at the origin, real line at (2a,0) and image at (a/2,0). Let r1​,r2​ be distances from these lines. The exterior potential can be written
ϕ=2πϵ0​λ​lnr1​2r2​​.​
(30)
On the surface, r12​=a2(5−4cosθ) and r22​=r12​/4, so ϕ=0 everywhere as required. The additive constant is essential for a grounded cylinder; the two-dimensional potential need not also be zero at infinity.
The physical line feels the image field at separation 3a/2. Its force per unit length is attractive:
LF​=−3πϵ0​aλ2​x^.​
(31)
Measure θ from the radius toward the real line, as in the original diagram. The radial field just outside the surface is
Er​=2πϵ0​λ​[a2(5−4cosθ)a−2acosθ​−(a2/4)(5−4cosθ)a−(a/2)cosθ​].
(32)
Since the interior field is zero,
σ(θ)=ϵ0​Er​=−2πa(5−4cosθ)3λ​.​
(33)
Integrating aσ(θ) around the circumference gives total induced charge −λ per unit length.
