For an elastic eccentric impact of a thin disc, take upward velocity as positive and let J be the short upward impulse. The disc's moment of inertia about a horizontal diameter is I=Ma2/4. With impact offset d=a/2,
vC​=−u+J/M,ω=Jd/I.
(3)
The contact point has upward velocity vC​+dω. Unit restitution against the fixed post requires that velocity to be +u, so
J(M1​+Id2​)=2u.
(4)
Since I=Md2, J=Mu and the centre's instantaneous speed is zero. The disc rotates with ω=2u/a; its rotational kinetic energy Iω2/2=Mu2/2 equals the entire initial energy. Gravity's impulse during the impact is neglected. Using the polar-axis moment of inertia would describe the wrong rotation axis.

The photon wavelength is λ=hc/E=3.5424×10−11m. The exact half-period zone radii for incident plane waves are chosen so that the path to the axial focus increases by λ/2 per zone:
f2+rn2​​−f=2nλ​,rn​=nfλ+4n2λ2​​≃nfλ​.​
(8)
Transmit alternate zones and block the others, with r0​=0. For f=1.00cm, r1​=0.5952μm. The number of zones is a design choice: 100 zones give outer radius 5.952μm and outermost width about 29.8nm. Adjacent open zones then arrive in phase at the principal focus.
The blocking material must be sufficiently opaque at 35 keV; no attenuation coefficient or fabrication thickness is specified. A binary amplitude plate also has zero-order transmission and other odd-order foci, so an aperture/order stop may be needed. The principal focal length is chromatic, varying as 1/λ for fixed radii.

A8 OurBigBook.com
Prefer molecular rotational spectroscopy or rovibrational spectroscopy, because nuclear masses enter their reduced mass directly. For an isotopologue with nuclear reduced mass μN​,
Erot​∝μN​Re2​1​,ωvib​≃μN​K​​.
(12)
Isotope substitution therefore produces appreciable shifts and distinct sets of lines, assuming the same electronic potential and approximately unchanged bond length and force constant. A suitable heteronuclear molecule can provide electric-dipole rotational/vibrational spectra; other molecules may require Raman spectroscopy.
Atomic electronic energies instead depend on the electron-nucleus reduced mass me​M/(me​+M), whose fractional change is suppressed by me​/M. High-resolution atomic spectroscopy can still detect isotope shifts, including nuclear-size and many-electron mass effects; it is not intrinsically incapable of identifying isotopes. Molecular spectra generally offer the stronger mass sensitivity for this purpose.

vr​=u(1−Rc2​/r2)cosθ,vθ​=−u(1+Rc2​/r2)sinθ.

B1 OurBigBook.com
A normal mode is a small motion in which all coordinates oscillate at one common angular frequency with fixed relative amplitudes. For conservative linearized motion, these are eigenvectors of the mass-weighted stiffness matrix.
All three springs are initially stretched from l to 2l, giving equal tension T0​=kl and zero resultant force on each mass. This prestress is essential for the transverse modes found below.
For the final mixed-axis trajectory of a prestressed spring pair, the initial displacements excite only the symmetric mode in each direction. Therefore, to first order in ϵ/l,
x1​(t)=2l+ϵcos(k/m​t),y1​(t)=ϵcos(k/(2m)​t).​
(15)
The second mass has the same displacements about 4l. Over the first transverse period Ty​=2π2m/k​, write s=t/Ty​:
ϵx1​−2l​=cos(2π2​s),ϵy1​​=cos(2πs),0≤s≤1.
(16)
The trajectory is not closed after Ty​, because the frequency ratio is 2​. It ends at approximately (−0.858,1) in these scaled coordinates rather than (1,1).

B2 OurBigBook.com
The first-order grating equation is dsinθ=λ, so d=2.879μm, about 347 lines per millimetre. For N equally illuminated lines,
I(θ)∝I1​(θ)​sin(δ/2)sin(Nδ/2)​​2,δ=2πdsinθ/λ.
(20)
Principal maxima stay at the same angles, their peaks scale as N2 for fixed illumination per line, and their angular widths shrink as 1/N. The resolving power of a diffraction grating is λ/Δλ=mN. Thus first order needs N=1000 and illuminated width Nd≃2.88mm.
The grating dispersion follows by differentiation: dθ/dλ=1/(dcosθ). With a detector centred on the chief ray, one pixel corresponds approximately to
Δλpixel​=fwdcosθ​=2.27nm.​
(21)
This exceeds the nominal 0.5nm grating width. The practical resolution scale is therefore a few nanometres; two-pixel sampling gives about 4.5nm. Exact resolvability also depends on slit width and image sampling. Taking the detector coordinate as ftanθ about the original axis instead changes the single-pixel estimate only to 2.20nm.
For ideal photon counting, Poisson distribution statistics give SNR =Nγ​​. Hence Nγ​=104 and tmin​=104/(2×105)=0.050s.
Use a lock-in amplifier for phase-sensitive detection: chop or otherwise modulate the source before the entrance slit, retaining a synchronous reference. At each grating setting amplify the photodiode output, multiply it by a reference and low-pass filter. For a signal scos(ωr​t+ϕ) and reference 2cosωr​t, the low-pass output is scosϕ; quadrature demodulation also recovers ssinϕ. Choose the modulation frequency above slow drift while within the source/detector bandwidth and away from strong electrical interference. Average for many cycles before changing the grating setting.
This rejects steady backgrounds and reduces low-frequency noise through narrow-band detection. It does not remove random dark-current/stray-light shot noise within that bandwidth, nor a background modulated coherently with the source. The chopping geometry must therefore distinguish wanted light from stray paths.[ref]

B3 OurBigBook.com
With microstate energies held fixed when differentiating,
⟨E⟩=−ZZ′​=−∂β​lnZ,⟨E2⟩=ZZ′′​,σE2​=∂β2​lnZ.​
(22)
Since dβ/dT=−1/(kB​T2),
σE2​=kB​T2CV​.​
(23)
The canonical ensemble fixes volume and particle number, and any other external Hamiltonian parameters. Thus the derivative is CV​; pressure-fixed fluctuations would naturally involve enthalpy in a different ensemble.
The translational and two-level energy variance follows because translation and internal states factorize for this dilute classical gas:
Z=Ztr​(1+e−βϵ)N,lnZtr​=constant−23N​lnβ.
(24)
Differentiating twice yields
σE2​=23​N(kB​T)2+Nϵ2[1+eϵ/(kB​T)]2eϵ/(kB​T)​.​
(25)
The terms are independent translational and two-level binomial variances. At kB​T≪ϵ, excitation freezes out exponentially and translation dominates. At kB​T≫ϵ, the internal variance tends to Nϵ2/4 while the translational variance grows as T2; the internal heat capacity consequently tends to zero. Fractional total-energy fluctuations are of order N−1/2 for large N. The low-temperature statement presumes translation is still nondegenerate; at strictly zero temperature the classical-gas model fails.

Use energy units m=mp​c2, M=mA​c2, e=Eγ​ and P=pc=E2−m2​. The soft-photon resonant capture threshold follows from four-momentum conservation:
M2=m2+2e(E−Pcosθ),E−Pcosθ=2E0​,E0​=4eM2−m2​.
(26)
For fixed proton energy the left side is largest at head-on incidence. Its threshold is E+P=2E0​. Combining that with (E−P)(E+P)=m2 gives
Emin​=E0​+4E0​m2​,mEmin​​=mE0​​+4E0​m​.​
(27)
Equality is allowed for a head-on photon. Here e=kB​T=2.3482×10−4eV, so
E0​=6.786×1020eV,Emin​/m≃7.23×1011.​
(28)
A very soft photon needs an enormous proton Lorentz factor to reach the specified invariant mass. A true thermal photon distribution and a finite-width resonance would smear this monoenergetic threshold.
For θ=0, E−P=m2/(E+P)≤m, whereas 2E0​≫m, so production is impossible. For each nonzero angle, sufficiently large energy is possible, and P≃E gives
E≃1−cosθ2E0​​.​
(29)

tanϕ=P+ecosθesinθ​≃2E0​e​sinθ(1−cosθ).
(30)
Writing x=cosθ, maximization gives (1−x)(2x+1)=0 and the interior maximum x=−1/2. Therefore, to the same extraordinarily accurate ultrarelativistic order,

(31)
The neglected relative corrections are of order m2/E02​ and e/E0​, both around 10−24. The final particle is effectively collinear with the proton despite a large photon incidence angle.

In the weak-field Zeeman effect, spin-orbit coupling keeps L,S,J good and a level shifts by μB​gJ​BmJ​. At fields large compared with fine-structure coupling but still within the linear electronic Zeeman approximation, L and S decouple: the Paschen-Back effect gives shifts μB​B(mL​+2mS​). Electric-dipole transitions obey ΔmL​=0,±1, ΔmS​=0 in that limit, recovering an orbital triplet pattern. Intermediate fields mix the coupled states.
For the weak-field sodium D-line Zeeman pattern, the given Landé g-factor formula gives
g(2S1/2​)=2,g(2P1/2​)=2/3,g(2P3/2​)=4/3.
(32)
Allowed photon shifts are δE=μB​B(gu​mu​−2ml​) with mu​−ml​=0,±1. Thus
D1​: δE/(μB​B)=±2/3,±4/3,D2​: δE/(μB​B)=±1/3,±1,±5/3.​
(33)
These are the four- and six-component patterns. Both π and σ components are represented, so an observation strictly along the field, which removes π emission, would not show this complete pattern. Hyperfine structure is neglected.
To determine the magnetic field from a Zeeman diagram, the unsplit separation provides its energy scale:
ΔED​=hc(1/λ2​−1/λ1​)=2.1315×10−3eV.
(34)
The smallest adjacent split spacing sE​=2μB​B1​/3 is approximately 0.070 of that separation in the sketch. Therefore
B1​≃2μB​3(0.070)ΔED​​≃3.9T≈4T.​
(35)
This is a graphical estimate; the drawing does not warrant high precision. Its splitting energy is small compared with ΔED​, consistent with the weak-field approximation.
For Doppler resolution of a Zeeman pattern, thermal velocities give Gaussian Doppler broadening with width
νΔνD​​=mNa​c28kB​Tln2​​,mNa​=NA​0.0230​kg.
(36)
At 350K, the photon-energy FWHM near 589nm is 5.88×10−6eV. Equating the smallest adjacent Zeeman spacing to this width gives B∼0.15T for loss of individually resolved components. There is no sharp Gaussian resolution threshold; using whole-pattern width instead gives a smaller field of order 0.03–0.04T for complete disappearance into a single envelope. Instrumental and collisional broadening would also affect the estimate.

For a normal from medium 1 to 2, the magnetic boundary conditions are
n⋅(B2​−B1​)=0,n×(H2​−H1​)=Kfree​.​
(41)
Without a free surface current, tangential H is continuous. To obtain the magnetization of a permeable sphere of radius R and permeability μ0​(1+χ), choose magnetic scalar potentials
φin​=−Hi​rcosθ,φout​=−H0​rcosθ+Ccosθ/r2.
(42)
Tangential continuity gives Hi​=H0​−C/R3, and normal B continuity gives (1+χ)Hi​=H0​+2C/R3. Hence
Hi​=1+χ/3H0​​,M=1+χ/3χH0​​.​
(43)
The fields are uniform inside. For negative susceptibility, magnetization is antiparallel to the applied field; the scalar formula retains its negative sign.
For a small weakly magnetic body, the induced magnetic energy is Um​=−χVB2/(2μ0​). The factor one-half accounts for building the induced moment. The vertical force is Fz​=χVBB′/μ0​. Balancing gravity therefore gives
BB′=χμ0​ρg​.​
(44)

BB′=−z3(B0​r0​)2​,z0​=[μ0​ρg−χ(B0​r0​)2​]1/3=0.0902m≃9.0cm.​
