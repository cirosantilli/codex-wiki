Two applications of optical interferometry are precision displacement or strain measurement, and Fourier transform spectroscopy, which recovers a spectrum from an interferogram.
For the round-trip phase in a Michelson interferometer, take a lossless 50:50 beam splitter with t=1/2​ and r=i/2​, and mirror reflection amplitude −1. At the depicted detector, each returning path has one beam-splitter transmission and one reflection. Their amplitudes are −iA0​e2ikD/2 and −iA0​e2ik(D+δ)/2. Consequently,
I(δ)=2∣A0​∣2​[1+cos(2kδ)]=∣A0​∣2cos2(kδ).​
(35)
The factor 2 in the phase is the return journey: the labelled δ is a one-way arm-length difference. The other output port has the complementary intensity. The printed ∣A0​∣2(1+2coskδ) cannot be an intensity: it becomes negative, and does not match the depicted path difference. No choice of normalization fixes that negative minimum. The expression above is the physically consistent result for the drawn output port.
For mutually incoherent spectral components with incident power density S(k),
I(δ)=21​∫S(k)[1+cos(2kδ)]dk.
(36)
The plot labels are λ1​=589.59nm and λ2​=590.00nm; these printed centres are used here. In the narrow-line, equal-power limit, each line has incident intensity J and
I=J[1+cos((k1​+k2​)δ)cos((k1​−k2​)δ)].​
(37)
There are rapid carrier fringes with approximately 0.2949μm mirror-displacement spacing and a slowly varying Interferometric visibility envelope. The first visibility zero is at 0.2121mm, and successive visibility revivals are separated by
Δδvis​=∣k1​−k2​∣π​=2∣λ2​−λ1​∣λ1​λ2​​=0.4242mm.​
(38)
For the unequal-line visibility in a spectral doublet, with incident line powers J1​,J2​, replace the intensity by [J1​+J2​+J1​cos(2k1​δ)+J2​cos(2k2​δ)]/2. The visibility becomes
V(δ)=J1​+J2​J12​+J22​+2J1​J2​cos(2(k1​−k2​)δ)​​.
(39)
The visibility minima rise from zero to ∣J1​−J2​∣/(J1​+J2​). The finite widths drawn in the spectrum also make coherence decay with path difference. For example, Gaussian lines of common wave-number standard deviation σk​ multiply the oscillatory term by e−2σk2​δ2; the actual linewidth is not specified numerically. Thus the narrow-line plot gives the beat positions, while the true long-delay oscillations die away.[ref]
The common-arm phase cancellation in a balanced interferometer means there is no smallest positive common mirror distance D in a balanced interferometer. With δ=0 the common factor e2ikD cancels from every intensity, and both paths interfere constructively at this detector for every D>0. Their infimum is zero. The 0.4242mm beat length describes changes of arm difference, not of the common arm length. It therefore cannot answer the final request as printed.
