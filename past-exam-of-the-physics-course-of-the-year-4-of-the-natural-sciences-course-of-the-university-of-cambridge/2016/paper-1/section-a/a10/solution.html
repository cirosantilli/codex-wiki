The fastest clearly resolved late oscillations occupy roughly 0.004–0.005s per cycle when compared with the 0.05s major-tick spacing. Thus fmax​ is of order 200–250Hz and the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem requires
fs​>2fmax​∼400–500sampless−1.​
(14)
A sampling rate of order 500sampless−1 is the estimated lower boundary for the plotted signal bandwidth; practical acquisition needs anti-alias filtering and margin. This is not a determination of LIGO's actual acquisition rate.[ref]
The bit-depth requirements for waveform digitization need a specified precision. The numerical bit depth is underdetermined by the figure. If the digitizer covers a full-scale range VFS​ and the required quantization step is at most Δ, then
b≥⌈log2​ΔVFS​​⌉.​
(15)
For a uniform quantizer its ideal quantization-noise rms is approximately Δ/12​. No permitted quantization error, noise criterion or calibrated digitizer range is specified. The plotted reconstruction band does not itself define one. Therefore no unique finite bit count, or exact lossless digitization of an arbitrary real-valued waveform, follows from the supplied data.
