Symmetric-square proof of the naive height parallelogram estimate (hx​(P+Q)+hx​(P−Q)=2hx​(P)+2hx​(Q)+O(1))

The biquadratic morphism for elliptic sums and differences has no common zero, so the local coefficient norms give
h([A:B:C])=2h(x(P))+2h(x(Q))+O(1),
(31)
uniformly in P,Q. Upper bounds use the fixed coefficients. Lower bounds follow from the absence of common zeros over every local field; the constants are 1 outside finitely many places, where the integral morphism still has no base point after reduction.
For the binary quadratic with roots u,v∈P1(K), the projective height of its coefficient vector is h(u)+h(v)+O(1). At nonarchimedean places the coefficient maximum norm of a product of linear polynomials is the product of their maximum norms by the multiplicativity of the nonarchimedean polynomial norm. At archimedean places the same norms are comparable up to a fixed factor for degree two. This also includes roots at infinity. Combining the comparisons yields hx​(P+Q)+hx​(P−Q)=2hx​(P)+2hx​(Q)+O(1). After dividing the identity for 2nP,2nQ by 4n and taking limits, it gives the exact height parallelogram identity for the canonical height of an elliptic curve.
