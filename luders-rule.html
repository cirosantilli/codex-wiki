Lüders rule (ρi​=Pi​ρPi​/Tr(Pi​ρ))

The Lüders rule specifies the ideal quantum instrument for a projective measurement. Conditional on an outcome with nonzero probability, it gives
ρi​=Tr(Pi​ρ)Pi​ρPi​​,∣ψi​⟩=⟨ψ∣Pi​∣ψ⟩​Pi​∣ψ⟩​
(7)
for a density operator and a pure state, respectively. The second formula applies only to a pure state input. If the result is discarded, the output is the nonselective projective measurement ∑i​Pi​ρPi​.
The crucial feature for a degenerate eigenvalue is preservation of quantum coherence within its eigenspace. For a qutrit with orthonormal basis ∣0⟩,∣1⟩,∣2⟩, consider
A=0P0​+1P1​,P0​=∣0⟩⟨0∣+∣1⟩⟨1∣,P1​=∣2⟩⟨2∣.
(8)
On ∣χ⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, outcome zero has probability one and the Lüders rule leaves ∣χ⟩ unchanged. Measuring all three rank-one projectors and then forgetting whether the label was zero or one instead gives
ρ​=21​∣0⟩⟨0∣+21​∣1⟩⟨1∣.
(9)
Both procedures always report the coarse value zero, but a subsequent test with projector ∣χ⟩⟨χ∣ succeeds with probability one for the first and one half for the second. Resolving extra information destroyed coherence. This explains why a quantum nondemolition measurement of a degenerate quantity must specify what information the apparatus actually records.
Gerhart Lüders proposed this state-change rule in 1951, explicitly discussing degenerate quantities and distinguishing selected subensembles from the aggregate measured ensemble. Kirkpatrick's translation was first posted in 2004; equations (7) and (8) give the unnormalized selective update and the nonselective update. [ref].
Repeatability follows briefly from Pj​Pi​=δij​Pi​: after outcome i, Tr(Pj​ρi​)=δij​. This assumes a second application of the same ideal instrument without intervening dynamics. An instrument with the same effects can rotate or reset the output and need not satisfy this conclusion; see POVM does not determine the post-measurement state.
