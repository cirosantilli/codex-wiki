Receiver-pessimal stable matching

 ... Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Matching (graph theory) Stable matching in a bipartite graph Gale-Shapley algorithm Proposer-optimal stable matching
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
For fixed strict preferences, the same output of the Gale-Shapley algorithm gives each receiver its worst partner obtainable in any stable matching, treating unmatched as last. "Worst" is restricted to stable possibilities: a receiver might still receive its favourite acceptable partner if every stable matching assigns it that partner. Reversing the proposing side gives receivers their best stable partners instead.
For a short proof, let M be a proposer-optimal stable matching and S any other stable matching. If a receiver r preferred M(r)=ℓ to S(r), then proposer optimality would make ℓ prefer r=M(ℓ) to S(ℓ), since the partners differ and preferences are strict. The pair (ℓ,r) would block S. Therefore no receiver prefers its partner in M to its partner in S. In the worked example, right-side ranks in ML​ are 2,2,3 for X,Y,Z, whereas MR​ gives them 1,1,2. Neither outcome is universally best for both sides.
