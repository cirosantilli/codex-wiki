Fluorescence microscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fluorescence
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 22
A fluorescence microscope maps photon emission to an image. Excitation light passes through a fluorescence excitation filter and reflects from a wavelength-selective beam splitter into the objective optical lens. Returning fluorescence passes through that beam splitter and a fluorescence emission filter to the photodetector. Rejecting excitation makes faint emission visible.[ref]
An ordinary image records emission-band counts at each pixel. Adding pulse-delay measurements gives a fluorescence lifetime map; scanning emission wavelength gives spectral information instead. Confocal microscopy adds spatial rejection and confocal optical sectioning, as developed in its own branch. An unlabeled specimen under the same illumination reveals intrinsic fluorescence and excitation leakage; excitation-blocked frames reveal detector background. A blank cannot perfectly predict background in a differently prepared specimen.
