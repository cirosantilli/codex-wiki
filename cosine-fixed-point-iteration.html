Cosine fixed-point iteration (xn+1​=cosxn​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fixed-point theorem Contraction mapping theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 264
Use radians and take T(x)=cosx on the closed interval [0,1]. This is a complete metric space with distance ∣x−y∣, and T([0,1])=[cos1,1]⊂[0,1]. The mean value theorem and ∣T′(x)∣=sinx≤sin1 give a contraction mapping with L=sin1≈0.841471<1. Both the interval and its preservation matter; a small derivative by itself would not ensure that all subsequent approximations stay in the chosen space.
Starting at x0​=0 gives x1​=1, x2​≈0.540302, x3​≈0.857553, and x4​≈0.654290. The cosine is decreasing here, so successive iterates alternate around the unique fixed point x∗​≈0.739085133215161. The contraction iteration error bound needs no prior knowledge of this numerical value. At step 40, x40​≈0.739085078689123 and the latest-step bound is below 7.20×10−7, certifying error below one millionth for exact iteration. The initial-step bound requires 91 iterations for the same tolerance. The observed step-40 error is only about 5.45×10−8; the guarantees allow less favorable behavior than this example actually exhibits.
This is the beauty of the contraction mapping theorem: a uniform local improvement controls the entire infinite process. Completeness is essential. On the incomplete interval (0,1), the contraction mapping T(x)=x/2 keeps every iterate inside but has no fixed point there; its missing limit is zero. Banach's 1922 paper developed abstract methods for different spaces of functions with applications to integral equations, a motivation still visible whenever repeated approximation is used to construct a function.[ref]
