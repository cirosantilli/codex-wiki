Kernel of a graph Laplacian (dimkerL=number of connected components)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Laplacian matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
The kernel of the Graph Laplacian consists exactly of vectors constant on each connected component of a graph. Indeed, Lx=0 implies xTLx=0, so the Graph Laplacian quadratic form forces equality at the endpoints of every edge; equality then propagates along graph paths. Conversely, these equalities make every entry of Lx zero. Thus the dimension of the kernel, equivalently the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue, counts the connected components of a graph.

The two displayed vectors form a basis of the kernel. The other two eigenvectors (1,−1,0,0)T and (0,0,1,−1)T each have eigenvalue two, so the full list of eigenvalues, including multiplicities, is 0,0,2,2. Separate connected components of a graph allow independent constant levels; a single global constant is only one of these possibilities.
