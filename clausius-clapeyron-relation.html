Clausius-Clapeyron relation (dp/dT=L/(TΔv))

 ... Thermodynamics Thermodynamic potential Thermodynamic free energy Gibbs free energy Phase coexistence curve Latent heat
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
The Clapeyron equation connects the slope of a phase coexistence curve to the latent heat and the change in volume during a first-order phase transition. A steep boundary can indicate a large latent heat, a small change in volume, or both. This lets a measured boundary constrain caloric properties and predicts how changing pressure shifts a melting temperature.
For a pure substance in bulk phase coexistence, label the initial and final thermodynamic phases α and β. Both have the same absolute temperature T, pressure p, and chemical potential. With s, h, and v denoting entropy, enthalpy, and volume on one common basis, define Δs=sβ​−sα​, Δh=hβ​−hα​, and Δv=vβ​−vα​. Then
dTdp​=ΔvΔs​=TΔvL​​,L=Δh=TΔs.
(18)
The derivative follows the phase coexistence curve; it is not a partial derivative within one thermodynamic phase. Use molar volume vm​ with molar latent heat Lm​ in Jmol−1, or specific volume v with latent heat L in Jkg−1. Both give dp/dT in PaK−1. For molar mass M, Lm​=ML and Δvm​=MΔv, so the predicted slope is independent of the chosen basis. This formulation assumes a simple compressible system, fixed composition, and negligible interface contributions; a curved interface can instead require the Young–Laplace equation.
For positive latent heat in the stated conversion direction, the sign of the slope is the sign of Δv. Converting liquid to gas normally increases volume and gives a positive slope. The negative melting-point slope of water illustrates the opposite sign. At a thermodynamic critical point, the coexisting gas and liquid merge: both L and Δv vanish, so their ratio needs a limiting analysis rather than substitution of zero into the Clapeyron equation.[ref]
A short derivation uses the molar Gibbs free energy gm​, equal to the chemical potential for a pure substance. Equality persists along the boundary, so dgm,α​=dgm,β​ with dgm​=−sm​dT+vm​dp. Therefore −Δsm​dT+Δvm​dp=0. Equality of gm​=hm​−Tsm​ also gives Δhm​=TΔsm​. Eliminating Δsm​ yields the Clapeyron equation.
