For the classical wave equation ytt​=c2yxx​ on an effectively infinite string, put g(x)=Ae−ax2/2, a>0. Initial data y(x,0)=g(x) and yt​(x,0)=−cg′(x)=caxg(x) give the single right-moving pulse y=g(x−ct). The same displacement with zero initial velocity instead gives y=[g(x−ct)+g(x+ct)]/2, two oppositely moving half-amplitude pulses. Both satisfy their initial data by direct differentiation. Their total energies differ because their initial kinetic energies differ; the split solution initially stores only elastic energy.
