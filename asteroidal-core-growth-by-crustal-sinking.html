Asteroidal core growth by crustal sinking (b3−b03​∼3R2Js​(t−t0​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Geophysics Planetary ice shell Stefan problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 71
At zero liquid superheat, dense solid released by viscous overturn of a dense crust can sink and remain solid. For a thin crust on an asteroid of fixed radius R, efficient deposition and the equal-density volume approximation give b3−b03​=3R2Js​(t−t0​). If surface gravity scales as R, the crustal flux scales as R1/3 and the core radius as R7/9t1/3. The approximation ends when remaining liquid is exhausted or the renewed crust is no longer thin.
