Kamerlingh Onnes's 1908 helium liquefaction experiment

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature Cryogenics Helium liquefaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
On 10 July 1908, Heike Kamerlingh Onnes's Leiden laboratory circulated purified helium through liquid air and evaporatively cooled liquid-hydrogen precooling, a regenerative heat exchanger, and an expansion valve. Returning helium cooled incoming helium; liquid collected in a transparent vacuum vessel. Reflected light revealed its liquid meniscus, crossed by thermocouple wires. Subsequent mass density and spectroscopy checks tested contamination.[ref]
A constant-volume helium gas thermometer below the valve supplied the temperature indication. An oxygen boiling reference check disagreed with its expected reading; the real gas correction to thermodynamic temperature remained provisional. A temperature plateau suggested immersion before the liquid meniscus was recognized.[ref]
The measurement principles separate two questions. A visible liquid meniscus establishes an interface; a gas thermometer estimates temperature through an equation of state. A stationary temperature trace can also result from a steady state without phase coexistence, so it cannot replace the interface observation. Conversely, seeing a liquid does not calibrate a thermometer. Thermometer calibration, the gas thermometer dead-space correction, departures from the ideal gas law and incomplete thermal equilibrium all matter when assigning an absolute temperature. A check far from the intended measurement range tests the instrument but does not by itself bound every low-temperature error.
