Local continuation proof of the monodromy theorem

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Holomorphic function Analytic continuation Monodromy theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
Along one path, analytic continuation is a chain of function elements on overlapping open discs, each agreeing with the preceding germ of a holomorphic function near the crossing point. The identity theorem makes this chain unique along that fixed path. Equality at a crossing means equality on a neighbourhood, not just equality of a single value.
For the deformation step, fix one intermediate path t↦H(s0​,t) and its continuation. By compactness of [0,1], choose a finite chain of representative open discs and subdivide the parameter so that each subarc fits in its assigned open disc, with consecutive junctions in overlap neighbourhoods where the representatives agree. Joint continuity of H ensures that every sufficiently nearby intermediate path uses that same chain and those same overlaps. The endpoints stay fixed, so the terminal germ of a holomorphic function stays unchanged. Existence along every intermediate path supplies this argument at each s0​; local constancy and connectedness of [0,1] give equality at s=0 and s=1. This proves the monodromy theorem without assuming that every analytic germ projection is a covering map.
