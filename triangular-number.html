Triangular number (Tn​=n(n+1)/2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Figurate number
OurBigBook.com Words: 685 Articles: 7
A triangular number counts rows of 1,2,…,n objects, with n a nonnegative integer:
Tn​=∑k=1n​k=2n(n+1)​,T0​=0.
(1)
The first values are 0,1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36,45,…. The empty arrangement contributes zero, by the empty sum convention. Adding one more row gives the triangular number recurrence Tn​−Tn−1​=n for n≥1. The row lengths form an arithmetic progression, while their running totals are triangular numbers.
Pair the rows with a reversed copy: 1+n=2+(n−1)=⋯=n+1. There are n such pairs, so two copies contain n(n+1) dots. Dividing by two proves the formula. This rectangle picture explains the factor one-half without requiring a formula for a long summation. For example, one hundred rows contain T100​=5050 dots.
The same count appears whenever every pair of different objects must be considered once. Five participants make ten possible handshakes; five sensors have ten different pairwise comparisons; a complete graph on five vertices has ten edges. These are the triangular number pair-counting identity, with one fewer row than objects. The distinction prevents a common error: T5​=15 counts all pairs from six objects, whereas five objects give T4​=10.
The triangular number square-complement identity says that two neighboring triangular numbers fill a square. Combining that identity with the one-row difference makes the difference of squares Tn2​−Tn−12​ equal to n3. It leads to Nicomachus's theorem: the sum of the first n cubes of numbers is the square of a number Tn2​. A change in the way one groups objects exposes a relation between summation, multiplication and geometry.
This geometric view is already visible in Nicomachus's Introduction to Arithmetic by Nicomachus (Hahn teacher edition): Book II, Chapter 8 describes growing triangular rows, Chapter 12 combines successive triangular counts into squares, and Chapter 20 groups successive odd integers into cubes of numbers.[ref] The linked edition uses Martin Luther D'Ooge's translation, revised with modern teaching material by Nathanael Hahn. Our empty-row index T0​=0 is a modern convention; the historical passage starts from unity and calls three the first actual triangle.
