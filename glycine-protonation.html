Glycine protonation

The three principal aqueous glycine forms differ by loss of one proton at each step:
H3​N+−CH2​−COOH+H2​OH3​N+−CH2​−COO−+H2​O​⇌H3​N+−CH2​−COO−+H3​O+,⇌H2​N−CH2​−COO−+H3​O+.​
(3)
Call these glycine forms G+, G0 and G−, respectively: a cation, a zwitterion and an anion, with net electric charges +e, 0 and −e, where e is the elementary charge. G0 specifically denotes the zwitterion, not an uncharged amino/carboxyl drawing. The first dissociation removes the carboxyl proton; the second removes a proton from the positively charged nitrogen. The chemical equilibrium changes are reversible, not three permanently separate kinds of glycine. [ref]
For all calculations and both diagrams here, explicitly stipulate the pKa values pKa1​=2.35 and pKa2​=9.78. These are illustrative model inputs, not measurements from the glycine titration experiment or a claim of universal constants. Actual acid dissociation constants depend on temperature and on the convention used for thermodynamic activity; apparent concentration-based values also depend on ionic strength. For comparison, the inspected textbook tabulates 2.34 and 9.60 for glycine, which would give a different isoelectric point. [ref]
