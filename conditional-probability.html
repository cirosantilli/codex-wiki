Conditional probability (P(A∣B))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 17
For events A,B with P(B)>0, conditional probability is
P(A∣B)=P(B)P(A∩B)​.
(71)
Restrict attention to the outcomes in B, then renormalize their probability measure so its total mass becomes one. The resulting rule A↦P(A∣B) is itself a probability measure. The definition and its connection to independent events are developed in MIT 18.05, Reading 3: Conditional Probability, Independence and Bayes' Theorem.
For the two-dice model, let A mean the sum is at least nine and B mean the first result is six. Of the six outcomes in B, four belong to A: the second die can be 3, 4, 5, or 6. Thus P(A∣B)=2/3. Reversing the information gives a different answer: four of the ten outcomes in A have first result six, so P(B∣A)=2/5. Both ratios use the same overlap; their denominators describe different information.
The definition requires positive conditioning mass. For a continuously distributed random variable X, it cannot directly define P(A∣X=x) when P(X=x)=0. A regular conditional distribution or conditional density supplies the appropriate extension, with the usual almost everywhere qualification. Nor does conditioning by itself justify causal inference: changing which outcomes are considered is different from physically changing how the experiment is generated.
