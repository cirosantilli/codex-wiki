Under single-length dynamical scaling of binary-fluid coarsening, mass density ρ, dynamic viscosity η and surface tension σ determine the displayed unique length and time scales by dimensional analysis. Writing L=L0​f(t/t0​) reduces the force-density estimate to αf′′+βf′2/f=γf′/f2+δ/f2. Resistive and driving terms need opposite signed geometric coefficients. Viscous hydrodynamic coarsening has f∼u and inertial hydrodynamic coarsening has f∼u2/3. These follow respectively from density-independent and viscosity-independent powers in L∝η2−3pρp−1σ2p−1tp. They require thin interfaces, statistically self-similar bicontinuous domains, dominant advection and no additional relevant macroscopic scales. A degeneracy in the signed inertial coefficients can invalidate the pure-power force balance without invalidating the dimensional calculation.
