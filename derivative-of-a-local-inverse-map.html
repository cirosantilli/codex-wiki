Derivative of a local inverse map (Dg(y)=[Df(g(y))]−1)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Differentiable map Inverse function theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 95
For the continuously differentiable inverse function g supplied by the inverse function theorem, the chain rule applied to f(g(y))=y gives
Df(g(y))Dg(y)=I,Dg(y)=[Df(g(y))]−1.
(164)
Here I is the identity matrix. The matrix inverse is taken at the recovered input g(y), not at the output y. In one dimension this is the inverse function rule g′(y)=1/f′(g(y)).
The formula also describes sensitivity. For a small output perturbation h, differentiability gives g(y+h)−g(y)=Dg(y)h+o(∥h∥). A large operator norm of Dg(y) permits large amplification in some directions. In the square root example, g′(y)=1/(2y​) grows without bound as positive y approaches zero. Existence of a local inverse function therefore does not imply that recovery is insensitive to measurement error.
