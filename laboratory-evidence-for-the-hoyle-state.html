Laboratory evidence for the Hoyle state

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Stellar astrophysics Stellar nuclear fusion Fowler nuclear astrophysics
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
In 1953, David Noel Ferguson Dunbar and collaborators used accelerated deuterons on nitrogen-14, populating carbon-12 through 14N(d,α)12C. Magnetic analysis resolved outgoing alpha particle groups. At 620keV incident energy and laboratory angle 90 degrees, they reported excitation 7.68±0.03MeV, with yield 6% of the 4.43MeV group's yield. The stronger group supplied an internal comparison. The paper's opening explicitly relates the search to Fred Hoyle's abundance argument. This is evidence for a nuclear level from a different entrance reaction than stellar fusion.[ref]
The inference uses energy and momentum conservation: at fixed incident energy and observation angle, more energy retained as carbon-12 excitation leaves less outgoing kinetic energy. A resolved particle group therefore identifies a nuclear level. This laboratory nuclear line supports an input required by a stellar synthesis model. It does not reproduce core helium burning, measure a complete stellar carbon yield, or by itself determine the stabilizing decay probability.
