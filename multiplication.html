Multiplication (×)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic
OurBigBook.com Words: 674 Articles: 19
Multiplication combines two factors into their product of numbers. For a natural number n, nx is the sum of n copies of x; with no copies the result is zero. This repeated-addition interpretation directly explains integer grouping, while multiplication of arbitrary real numbers also describes scaling by noninteger factors.
A rectangle of side lengths 3 and 6 has area 18. Split the second side into lengths 2 and 4. The two areas are 6 and 12, giving the visible identity
3(2+4)=3⋅2+3⋅4=18.
(35)
Rotating the same rectangle interchanges the sides without changing its area, explaining 3⋅6=6⋅3. These pictures illustrate distributivity and commutativity for ordinary positive numbers; matrix multiplication and other operations require their own rules.
For a fractional example, take 3/2 of a quantity and then 2/3 of that result: the combined scale is (2/3)(3/2)=1. The two fractions are multiplicative inverses. In the unit square on the right, the overlap of a strip occupying 3/4 of the width with one occupying 2/3 of the height contains six of twelve equal cells. Its area is (3/4)(2/3)=1/2. The product stays exact even though the decimal expansion of 2/3 repeats. [ref]
