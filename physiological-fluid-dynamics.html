Physiological fluid dynamics

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 24
Physiological fluid dynamics studies the motion of fluids in biological systems, including blood flow in elastic arteries, flow through deformable vessels, and liquid layers lining airways. Wall compliance couples pressure to geometry, so wave propagation and mechanical feedback are as important as viscosity.

Lubrication model of a compliant translating plug (px​=−6μU/h2+12μQ/h3)

For a freely translating compliant body separated from a cylindrical wall by a thin lubrication gap h, the body frame has wall speed −U and body surface speed zero. The local axial flow is w=−U(1−y/h)+px​y(y−h)/(2μ), so its flux per circumference is −Uh/2−px​h3/(12μ)=−Q. Rearranging proves the displayed pressure gradient. A linear compliance p=p0​+α(r0​−r) couples this flow to deformation. Specified end pressures and zero net axial body force close the problem.

Peristaltic pumping

Words: 105 Articles: 1
Physiological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
Peristaltic pumping transports fluid by a travelling deformation or travelling external pressure acting on a tube. With wave coordinate X=x−ct, mass conservation makes the relative flux Q=α(u−c) constant. The laboratory flux is αu=cα+Q, so a negative relative flux does not rule out forward mean pumping.

Collapsible tube flow

Words: 519 Articles: 8
Physiological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
A one-dimensional collapsible tube model couples area α, mean axial velocity u and internal pressure p: αt​+(αu)x​=0 and ut​+uux​=−px​−R(α)u. A wall relation specifies internal minus external pressure as a function of area. Decreasing area generally increases the friction coefficient R, while positive wall stiffness gives real small-amplitude wave speed.

Choking by wall thickness in an elastic tube (hc​/R=1+λ/4−5λ1/5/4)

Words: 83
Collapsible tube flow OurBigBook.com
For steady uniform-density inviscid flow with pressure law p=p0​+k(r−R) and a prescribed thickness h, mass conservation and the Bernoulli equation give h/R=1−s−1/2+λ(1−s2)/4, where s=u/V and λ=2ρV2/(kR). The function has a unique maximum at s=λ−2/5, producing the displayed choking bound. Below it there are two positive roots; continuity from the upstream state selects the lower-speed root for λ<1 and higher-speed root for λ>1. At λ=1, the maximum is zero and any positive prescribed thickness is incompatible with this model.

Subcritical tube flow (0<u<c)

Words: 37
Collapsible tube flow OurBigBook.com
A positive collapsible tube flow is subcritical when its characteristic speeds u+c and u−c have opposite signs. A downstream boundary can then send information upstream. For a quadratic pressure-area law and fixed dimensionless flux q, this corresponds to A/A0​>q​.

Supercritical tube flow (u>c)

Words: 38
Collapsible tube flow OurBigBook.com
A positive collapsible tube flow is supercritical when both characteristic speeds u±c are positive relative to the wall. Information then propagates downstream on both branches. For a quadratic pressure-area law and fixed dimensionless flux q, the condition is A/A0​<q​.

An elastic jump is a short transition from a fast, narrow supercritical collapsible tube flow to a slower, wider subcritical flow. Neglecting body force and distributed friction across the jump conserves flux and the momentum flux shown above. Distinct upstream and downstream areas therefore obey α23​+α1​α22​+α12​α2​=3q2/α1​. The flux function has its unique minimum at α=q​, so an upstream state below this value has one conjugate state above it. Let R(α)∝α−n, with n>0, and use disturbances ei(kx−ωt) about uniform velocity U. The intrinsic frequency is ν=ω−kU. Linearized mass conservation and the decrease in friction as area grows give the displayed dispersion relation. For nonzero k and positive flow, one branch grows exactly when nU>c0​. Arterial pressure wave

Words: 425 Articles: 6
Physiological fluid dynamics OurBigBook.com
A small pressure disturbance in an elastic artery propagates through the coupled inertia of blood and compliance of the wall. If A is the undisturbed area, ρ the mass density and c the wave speed, the linearized equations give c2=A/ρdp/dA. A travelling pressure amplitude p+​ carries flow amplitude Ap+​/(ρc); the oppositely travelling wave has the opposite flow sign.

At a lossless vascular junction, pressure continuity and volumetric flow rate balance give 1+r=t and Y1​(1−r)=Yd​t, where Yd​ is the sum of daughter characteristic admittances of an arterial wave. Eliminating transmission amplitude t gives the displayed reflection coefficient. A decrease in total area yields positive reflection if parent and daughter wave speeds are equal; differing stiffnesses require comparison of the admittances rather than areas alone.

Pressure-flow phase lag from one arterial reflection (ωΔt=arg[1−r2+2irsinϑ])

Words: 87
Bifurcation reflection of an arterial pressure wave OurBigBook.com
For incident and reflected harmonic arterial pressure waves with real 0<r<1, the local pressure and flow phasors are proportional to 1+re−iϑ and 1−re−iϑ. Their maximum-time difference is the displayed phase lag, on the branch 0<ϑ<π. Here ϑ=2ωd/c is the round-trip phase to the reflector. Its derivative has the sign of cosϑ. Raising the wave speed reduces the lag when 0<ϑ<π/2, but increases it when π/2<ϑ<π. This conditional harmonic model does not establish an age trend for arbitrary vascular geometry or measurement stations beyond the sole reflector.

A hydraulic resistance R imposes q=p/R. Combining this with forward and backward arterial pressure waves, p=p+​+p−​ and q=Y(p+​−p−​), gives the displayed reflection coefficient. A matched load RY=1 has no reflected pressure wave, while a closed end has reflection coefficient one. At a network junction the combined downstream input admittance replaces 1/R.

Wave transmission through a resistively loaded arterial loop

Words: 93
Wave transfer matrix of an arterial segment OurBigBook.com
Consider two identical two-segment paths from an inlet to a distal junction, with equal resistive beds at the two intermediate junctions and the distal junction. The segment transfer matrix and junction flow balances determine all pressures. If one inlet branch is removed and the beds are weakly loading the waves, the remaining segments form a three-segment chain with an effectively closed far end. The altered reflection pattern can raise peripheral oscillatory flow amplitudes even though a branch was lost; the incident wave amplitude, rather than total inlet pressure or flow, is the prescribed forcing.

Characteristic admittance of an arterial wave (Y=A/(ρc))

Words: 39
Arterial pressure wave OurBigBook.com
The characteristic admittance of an arterial wave relates the volumetric flow rate of a travelling arterial pressure wave to its pressure. For forward and backward amplitudes, p=p+​+p−​ and q=Y(p+​−p−​). It is the reciprocal of characteristic pressure-to-flow impedance, and should not be confused with the reciprocal hydraulic resistance of a steadily resistive segment.
