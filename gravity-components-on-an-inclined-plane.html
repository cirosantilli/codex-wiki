Gravity components on an inclined plane (W∥​=mgsinθ,N=mgcosθ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Inclined plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 259
Choose a unit vector ed​ pointing downhill and a perpendicular unit vector en​ pointing outward from the inclined plane. The gravitational weight points vertically downward. Its scalar projections onto these directions give
W=mgsinθed​−mgcosθen​.
(1)
The angle between the downward vertical and the inward normal is θ: the inward component uses cosine, and the downhill component uses sine. These are two components of one force. They replace its vertical arrow in a component calculation; adding them to that same arrow would count gravitational weight twice.
Continued contact with a stationary straight inclined plane gives zero perpendicular acceleration. The perpendicular component of Newton's second law therefore gives
N−mgcosθ=0,N=mgcosθ.
(2)
This is why the normal force generally differs from mg: it balances the inward component, rather than the whole gravitational weight. An extra lifting force, a curved surface, or motion of the support would require a new perpendicular balance.[ref]
The same geometry explains the ramp's usefulness. A frictionless block pulled uphill at constant velocity needs a parallel force P=mgsinθ. Travelling a distance L raises it through height h=Lsinθ, so the pulling work is PL=mgh, its gain in gravitational potential energy. The smaller force costs a larger displacement; the work-energy theorem keeps the accounting consistent.
