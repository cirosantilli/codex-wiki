Simple random sampling (P(A=a)=(nN​)−1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Probability sampling
OurBigBook.com Words: 807 Articles: 2
Here simple random sampling means a random unordered subset A of exactly n distinct labels from {1,…,N}, with every size-n subset equally likely. The sample size is fixed in advance, 1≤n≤N, and sampling without replacement prevents duplicate units. This is SRSWOR; some texts also use "simple random sampling" for independent uniform draws with replacement, which is a different design.[ref]
Every label has inclusion probability n/N, but equal inclusion probabilities alone are insufficient. For example, from six labels choose either {1,2,3} or {4,5,6} with probability 1/2. Each label is included with probability 1/2, yet eighteen of the twenty size-three subsets are impossible: this is a two-group cluster sampling design. Unequal probability sampling deliberately changes inclusion probabilities, often to improve precision for a particular estimand, and generally requires an appropriately weighted estimator rather than the plain sample mean.
Consider the original mathematical population with labelled values (2,4,6,8,10,12), and let n=2. The fifteen equiprobable subsets, followed by their sample means, are

Their sample means sum to 105, giving expected value 105/15=7. Their squared deviations from 7 sum to 70, giving variance 70/15=14/3. This is an enumeration over hypothetical selections from six stipulated numbers, not measured survey data.
With independent uniform sampling with replacement, the 62=36 ordered outcomes each have probability 1/36. The two orders (2,4) and (4,2) are distinct outcomes, and (2,2) is now possible. Forgetting order does not make the resulting unordered multisets equiprobable: {2,4} has probability 2/36 but {2,2} has probability 1/36. The thirty-six sample means still have expected value 7, but their squared deviations sum to 210, giving variance 210/36=35/6. Avoiding duplicate labels improves precision for this fixed population.
