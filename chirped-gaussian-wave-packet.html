A chirped Gaussian wave packet adds a quadratic quantum phase to the zero-mean Gaussian wave packet already evaluated above. Here σ>0 and κ is a real dimensionless parameter. Its probability density function ∣ψσ,κ​(x)∣2 is unchanged for every κ, so ⟨x⟩=0 and (Δx)2=σ2. Nevertheless, applying the momentum operator gives
pψσ,κ​=2σ2ℏ(κ+i)x​ψσ,κ​,⟨p⟩=0,(Δp)2=∥pψσ,κ​∥2=4σ2ℏ2(1+κ2)​.
(43)
The sign of the quantum covariance follows directly, without assigning a classical trajectory to the wavefunction:
⟨xp⟩=2ℏ​(κ+i),Cxp​=Re⟨xp⟩=2ℏκ​,r=ΔxΔpCxp​​=1+κ2​κ​.
(44)
Here r is a normalized symmetrized quantum covariance, analogous to a correlation coefficient. Positive κ gives positive Cxp​, and reversing κ reverses Cxp​ while leaving both variances unchanged. In the negative-exponent Fourier transform convention used above, the momentum density is
∣ψ​σ,κ​(p)∣2=πℏ2(1+κ2)2σ2​​exp(−ℏ2(1+κ2)2σ2p2​).
(45)
Consequently the quantum uncertainty product and the covariance matrix determinant tell different stories:
ΔxΔp=2ℏ​1+κ2​,detΣ=4ℏ2(1+κ2)​−4ℏ2κ2​=4ℏ2​.
(46)
Every member of this pure state family saturates the Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation. Only κ=0 saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty relation for this fixed pair of position and momentum axes. Thus being a Gaussian wave packet alone does not imply a minimum ΔxΔp.
For a concrete comparison, choose a reference length L, put X=x/L, P=pL/ℏ, and set σ=L. The choices κ=0,+2,−2 all have (ΔX)2=1, but give (ΔP)2=1/4,5/4,5/4 and CXP​=0,+1,−1, respectively. The two nonzero cases have r=±2/5​ and product 5​/2; all three covariance matrices have determinant 1/4.
The phase space curves in the figure satisfy zTΣ−1z=1 in the dimensionless coordinates, where z=(X,P)T. They display second moments, not observed simultaneous position and momentum pairs. Their common area is πdetΣ​=π/2, although their coordinate projections differ.
This distinction is useful for predicting focusing. Under free particle evolution with mass m, x(t)=x(0)+tp(0)/m, so
(Δx(t))2=σ2+m2t​Cxp​+m2t2​(Δp)2.
(47)
A negative chirp initially narrows the position distribution; positive chirp initially broadens it. For κ<0, the minimum occurs at t∗​=−2mσ2κ/[ℏ(1+κ2)]>0 and has variance σ2/(1+κ2). At that time the quantum covariance vanishes, and the unchanged momentum spread gives Δx(t∗​)Δp=ℏ/2. The extra initial product was a usable position-momentum correlation, not extra measurement backaction.
