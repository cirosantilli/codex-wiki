For every q∈L, the line pq meets the nonsingular conic in two points counted with multiplicity by the Bézout theorem. One is p; define α(q) as the second, using α(q)=p for the unique tangent line at p. Conversely, for r∈C∖{p}, intersect the line pr with L, and send p itself to the intersection of its tangent with L. Since p∈/L, each intersection with L is unique. These constructions are inverse, so α:L→C is a bijection preserving the stated collinearity. This is parametrization of a conic by projection.
For the particular conic take p=[1:0:1] and L={x=0}. If q=[0:s:t], points on pq have coordinates [a:bs:a+bt]. Substitution into the conic gives
b[−a(s+2t)+b(s2−t2)]=0.
(18)
Taking the second intersection gives the explicit map
α([0:s:t])=[s2−t2:s(s+2t):s2+st+t2].​
(19)
The three coordinates do not vanish simultaneously. If s+2t=0, the formula gives p, as required for the tangent direction.
For rational points, put r=s/t when t=0. The full affine list is
x=r2+r+1r2−1​,y=r2+r+1r(r+2)​,r∈Q,​
(20)
together with the parameter r=∞, which gives (x,y)=(1,1). The denominator is (r+1/2)2+3/4>0 for rational r. Conversely, every rational point other than p determines a rational joining line and hence a rational point of L; the point p is obtained at r=−2. Thus this lists all rational solutions.
