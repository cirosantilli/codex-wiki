For the surface of revolution, the arc-length condition on its profile gives E=1, F=0, G=f(u)2. Thus the geodesic equations reduce to
u′′=ff′(v′)2,dsd​(f2v′)=0.
(15)
Consequently
f(u)2v′=constant,​
(16)
the Clairaut first integral for a surface of revolution.
On a meridian, take v=b and u=±s+u0​. Both equations hold, so every meridian is a geodesic. On a cross-section u=c, arc length requires v′=±1/f(c). The first equation then holds exactly when f′(c)=0, while the second holds automatically. Hence
u=c is a geodesic⟺f′(c)=0.​
