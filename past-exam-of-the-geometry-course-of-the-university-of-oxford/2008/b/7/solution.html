For a degree-d nonconstant holomorphic map from a compact connected Riemann surface of genus g to the projective line, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula is
2g−2=−2d+p∈X∑​(ep​−1),​
(25)
where ep​ is the local degree. A meromorphic function defines a map to the sphere by giving its ordinary finite values away from poles and value infinity at each pole. The reciprocal function is holomorphic in a neighborhood of a pole, proving holomorphicity there. For use in Riemann-Hurwitz the function must be nonconstant; a nonzero constant still defines a holomorphic map, but of degree zero.
Let X=C/Λ. The double pole of ℘ gives a degree-two map to the sphere. The torus has genus one, so total ramification is four. One simple ramification point is zero, above infinity. At the three nonzero half-period classes hi​, evenness and periodicity give ℘′(hi​)=0. Each is a ramification point of local degree two, and these account for all remaining ramification. Put ei​=℘(hi​). They are distinct, since a degree-two fiber cannot contain two points each of local degree two.
At each hi​, ℘−ei​ has a double zero and ℘′ has a simple zero. Consequently the elliptic quotient
R(z)=(℘(z)−e1​)(℘(z)−e2​)(℘(z)−e3​)℘′(z)2​
(26)
has removable singularities there and no zeros or poles elsewhere except possibly zero. Near zero, ℘=z−2+O(z2) and ℘′=−2z−3+O(z), so R→4. Thus R is holomorphic on the compact torus and constant, proving
℘′2=4(℘−e1​)(℘−e2​)(℘−e3​).​
(27)
The printed additional requirement that all three ei​ be nonzero is false. For the square lattice Λ=Z+iZ, invariance of the defining series under multiplication by i gives ℘(iz)=−℘(z). At h=(1+i)/2, one has ih=h−1, so periodicity gives ℘(h)=℘(ih)=−℘(h) and hence ℘(h)=0. This is one of the three branch values. Distinctness, which is all the cubic construction needs, remains valid.
Define
Φ(z)=[℘(z):℘′(z):1](z∈/Λ),Φ(0)=[0:1:0].
(28)
The image lies on Y2Z=4(X−e1​Z)(X−e2​Z)(X−e3​Z). Distinct roots make its affine part smooth, and the point at infinity is smooth because the derivative of its equation with respect to Z is nonzero there. Near zero the projective coordinates X/Y=℘/℘′∼−z/2 and Z/Y=1/℘′∼−z3/2 extend holomorphically. Every finite X/Z value has two preimages under ℘ except at a branch value. Evenness pairs those preimages as z,−z, and oddness of ℘′ distinguishes them unless they are the same half-period class. Therefore the two coordinates together give injectivity and reach both signs of Y, proving surjectivity onto the cubic. Thus Φ is a bijective holomorphic map to a nonsingular cubic, including when one branch value is zero.
