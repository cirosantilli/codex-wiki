An inflection point is a smooth point where the tangent line has intersection multiplicity at least three with the curve. For a nonsingular plane curve of degree n≥3, these are the intersections with its Hessian curve, whose degree is 3(n−2). The original curve is not a component of its Hessian: otherwise in each graph chart the tangent direction would have zero derivative identically, making the curve locally a line and hence a line globally. Bézout therefore gives the upper bound 3n(n−2), counting multiplicity. A nonsingular conic has no inflection points; lines are excluded from this finite-contact definition.
Take F=y2z−x3+xz2. Its Hessian determinant is
detD2F=8(3xy2−3x2z−z3).
(22)
At infinity the curve has only O=[0:1:0], which is an inflection point: its tangent is z=0 and the restricted equation is −x3=0. In the chart z=1, substitute y2=x3−x into the Hessian equation to obtain
3x4−6x2−1=0,x2=1±3​2​.
(23)
Thus the full answer is
O=[0:1:0],[x:y:1] with x2=1±2/3​,y=±x3−x​.​
(24)
The four complex values of x are distinct and none is 0,1,−1, so each gives two distinct nonzero values of y. Together with O these are all nine inflection points, exhausting the Bézout bound.
