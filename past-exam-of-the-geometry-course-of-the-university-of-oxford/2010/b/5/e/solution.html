The curves have no common component, since their intersection is finite. Let R=0 define the irreducible degree-m curve E. Choose p∈E outside C∩D, and select (λ,μ)=(0,0) with λP(p)+μQ(p)=0. The polynomial H=λP+μQ is not identically zero, since P,Q are not proportional.
The degree-n curve H=0 meets E at the prescribed nm base points and also at p. If R did not divide H, irreducibility of R and the Bézout theorem would bound the number of distinct intersections by nm, contradicting these nm+1 points. Thus
H=RS,degS=n−m.
(34)
At every remaining common zero of P,Q lying off E, R is nonzero and hence S=0. Therefore
F={S=0} contains all remaining n(n−m) points,degF≤n−m.​
(35)
This is residual intersection through a curve pencil. The existence of a point p outside the finite base locus is automatic on the projective curve E.
