For a nonconstant holomorphic map of degree d between compact connected Riemann surfaces,
2gX​−2=d(2gY​−2)+p∈X∑​(ep​−1),​
(2)
where the analytic ramification index ep​ is the exponent in local coordinates w∘f=zep​ after coordinate changes. A meromorphic function maps its poles to infinity; the target coordinate 1/w is then holomorphic and vanishes at a pole, so this defines a holomorphic map to the Riemann sphere. A pole of order m has local degree m.
Let Λ be the period lattice. The induced function ℘:T=C/Λ→CP1 has just one pole, of order two, so its degree is two. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives total ramification four. The pole contributes one; every analytic ramification index is at most two, so there are exactly three further ramification points, each simple. Evenness of ℘, obtained by reindexing its lattice sum, identifies them with the three nonzero half-periods. Their values e1​,e2​,e3​ are distinct: two points of local degree two cannot lie in one degree-two fiber.
The functions ℘′2 and ∏i​(℘−ei​) have double zeros at these same three points and a pole of order six at zero. Their ratio is a holomorphic nowhere-zero function on the compact torus, hence constant. From ℘(z)=z−2+O(z2) and ℘′(z)=−2z−3+O(z) its value is four. Therefore
℘′2=4(℘−e1​)(℘−e2​)(℘−e3​),ei​=ej​ (i=j).​
(3)
The printed additional assertion that all ei​ are nonzero is false. The square-lattice Weierstrass function has a zero branch value: for Z+iZ, reindexing gives ℘(iz)=−℘(z). At the half-period h=(1+i)/2, one has ih=h−1, so periodicity gives ℘(h)=−℘(h) and hence ℘(h)=0. Thus one branch value is zero, while the three remain distinct.
The derivative has a single triple pole, so deg℘′=3, and the Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives total ramification ∑(ep​−1)=6. The number of distinct ramification points requires a further distinction. The absent constant term in the Laurent expansion gives e1​+e2​+e3​=0, so write ℘′2=4℘3−g2​℘−g3​. Differentiation yields ℘′′=6℘2−g2​/2. If g2​=0, each of the two values ±g2​/12​ has two distinct preimages under ℘ and none is a branch value, since a common zero of the cubic and its derivative would be a repeated cubic root. Thus there are four simple finite ramification points of ℘′ and its triple pole, giving five distinct points. If g2​=0, the nonsingularity of the cubic forces g3​=0. The two simple zeros of ℘ make ℘′′ vanish to order two, so each has analytic ramification index three for ℘′. Together with its triple pole there are three distinct points. This second case occurs for a hexagonal lattice: its sixth-root rotational symmetry forces the z2 Laurent coefficient, and hence g2​, to vanish. The weighted count is six in both cases.
