Orthogonal vectors (⟨u,v⟩=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Inner product
OurBigBook.com Words: 270 Articles: 3
Two vectors are orthogonal when their inner product is zero.
For real Euclidean vectors, the inner product is the dot product, so (3,4) and (4,−3) are orthogonal: 3(4)+4(−3)=0. For nonzero Euclidean vectors, this means their angle is 90∘. The zero vector is orthogonal to every vector, although it has no direction or defined angle.[ref]
In an abstract inner product space, orthogonality always refers to its chosen inner product. For example, the weighted inner product ⟨u,v⟩w​=4u1​v1​+u2​v2​ makes (1,1) and (1,−4) orthogonal, since 4−4=0. Their ordinary dot product is −3, so their arrows are not perpendicular in the usual Cartesian coordinate system. The algebraic definition also applies to functions, where arrows need not be available.
