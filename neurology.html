Diaschisis

Words: 31
Neurology OurBigBook.com
Dysfunction in a region remote from a lesion because of disrupted interactions with the damaged region. Reduced local activity after stroke can therefore reflect more than destruction of the locally measured tissue.

Dementia

Words: 29
Neurology OurBigBook.com
A syndrome of cognitive decline affecting everyday functioning, arising through different diseases. Age at reported symptom onset, age at diagnosis and the incidence of new cases are distinct outcomes.

Stroke

Words: 33 Articles: 1
Neurology OurBigBook.com
A sudden neurological injury caused by disruption of cerebral blood supply or bleeding. Vascular anatomy can make damage to neighboring tissue and pathways covary, limiting attempts to infer one-to-one mappings from brain lesions to computations.

Parkinson's disease

Words: 36
Neurology OurBigBook.com
A neurodegenerative disorder with motor and nonmotor manifestations, including motivational difficulties in some patients. Comparisons across dopamine-related medication states can inform mechanisms but do not selectively manipulate a single cognitive process. The disease name commemorates James Parkinson.

Cognitive neurology

Words: 435 Articles: 13
Neurology OurBigBook.com
The study of how neurological disease and injury affect cognition, using clinical patterns and experiments to constrain explanations of normal cognitive function. A brain lesion can disrupt connected regions and several computations, so a syndrome is evidence to explain rather than a complete processing model.

Apathy

Words: 87 Articles: 2
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
Reduced goal-directed activity or motivation relative to a person’s previous functioning. Initiation, valuation, effort and emotional experience require separate assessment; inactivity is not automatically anhedonia or motor incapacity.

Abulia

Words: 61 Articles: 1
Apathy OurBigBook.com
A marked reduction of spontaneous initiative or willful activity. Preserved behavior after prompting can help distinguish impaired self-initiation from inability to execute the same action.
Auto-activation deficit
Words: 36
Abulia OurBigBook.com
A severe reduction of spontaneous mental and behavioral activity that improves with external stimulation or instruction. Clinical descriptions after bilateral basal ganglia injury motivated experiments separating self-activation, incentive translation and motor capacity.[ref]

Semantic dementia

Words: 36
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
A progressive syndrome with deterioration of conceptual knowledge, often accompanied by fluent speech, anomia and poor word comprehension. Nonverbal semantic tests are important for distinguishing broad conceptual impairment from an isolated language-output problem.[ref]

Visual agnosia

Words: 92 Articles: 2
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
Impaired recognition of visually presented objects not adequately explained by elementary visual loss or a general inability to respond. Testing other sensory routes and nonverbal knowledge helps locate the difficulty.

Associative visual agnosia

Words: 34
Visual agnosia OurBigBook.com
A visual-recognition disorder attributed to difficulty accessing meaning from a relatively adequate visual representation. Copying can provide evidence about perception, but line-by-line copying alone does not demonstrate normal integration.[ref]

Apperceptive agnosia

Words: 28
Visual agnosia OurBigBook.com
A visual-recognition disorder in which constructing or integrating an adequate perceptual representation is impaired. The specific perceptual demands must be established rather than inferred from recognition failure alone.

Anosognosia

Words: 24
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
Impaired awareness of a neurological deficit. Its assessment must distinguish the underlying deficit, a person’s report and evidence that they understand the relevant questions.

Apraxia

Words: 78 Articles: 2
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
Difficulty performing learned actions not adequately explained by elementary weakness, sensory loss or failure to understand the instruction. Different clinically named forms need not share one processing mechanism.

Dressing apraxia

Words: 22
Apraxia OurBigBook.com
Difficulty organizing dressing actions that can involve relationships between clothing, the body and space. Motor limitations, understanding and neglect require separate assessment.

Constructional apraxia

Words: 28
Apraxia OurBigBook.com
A traditional clinical term for impaired copying, drawing or assembly of spatially organized material. Failures can involve spatial representations, neglect and planning rather than one general motor-program deficit.

Optic ataxia

Words: 31
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
Inaccurate visually guided reaching not adequately explained by elementary visual loss or weakness. Comparing gaze, hand and target conditions helps identify the affected visuomotor demands.[ref]

Hemispatial neglect

Words: 44
Cognitive neurology OurBigBook.com
A disproportionate failure to attend or respond to events on one side of space, often contralesional to brain injury. It may affect external space, the body or internally generated scenes and can occur without an equivalent primary visual-field loss.[ref]

Neurovascular coupling

Words: 25
Neuroimaging OurBigBook.com
The relationship between neural activity and local vascular responses underlying many functional imaging measurements. Disease-related changes in this relationship complicate direct comparisons of activation magnitude.

Calcium imaging

Words: 24
Neuroimaging OurBigBook.com
Imaging a calcium-sensitive signal as an indirect measure related to cellular activity. Indicator kinetics, neuropil contributions and movement can affect its relationship to spikes.

Fibre photometry

Words: 30
Neuroimaging OurBigBook.com
Optical measurement of a fluorescent signal collected from a tissue volume. It can report population calcium or transmitter-sensor dynamics depending on the reporter; it is not automatically a single-neuron spike train.

Event-related potential (ERP)

Words: 47
Electroencephalography OurBigBook.com
An electrical response measured relative to a specified event, commonly estimated from repeated scalp recordings. Its timing and distribution constrain processing hypotheses, but scalp averages are not direct recordings from an isolated neural generator. Differences between conditions can overlap in time without demonstrating strictly separate serial stages.

Contralateral delay activity

Words: 39
Electroencephalography OurBigBook.com
A sustained lateralized electrophysiological signal during visual-memory delays, typically measured as a difference between signals contralateral and ipsilateral to the attended visual field. Its load dependence and relation to behavioral capacity constrain models without fixing a universal storage count.

Migraine

Words: 26
Neurology OurBigBook.com
Migraine is a neurological disorder that can be an outcome in a clinical trial or an observational study. Its statistical association with an exposure does not alone establish a causal effect.
