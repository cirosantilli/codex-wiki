Krull principal ideal theorem

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Krull dimension Height of an ideal
OurBigBook.com Words: 434 Articles: 5
In a Noetherian ring, every prime ideal minimal over a proper principal ideal has height at most one.
For a proof, suppose instead that p0​⊊q⊊p and that p is minimal over (a). Quotient by p0​ and localize at p; one may assume that R is a Noetherian local domain, 0⊊q⊊p with p maximal, and p is the only prime containing (a). For the symbolic powers
q(n)=qnRq​∩R,
(14)
each q(n) is q-primary. The quotient R/(a) is a zero-dimensional Noetherian ring and hence Artinian, so the descending chain (q(n)+(a))/(a) stabilizes. For large n, writing an element of q(n) modulo q(n+1) and using a∈/q gives
q(n)=q(n+1)+aq(n).
(15)
The Nakayama lemma applied to q(n)/q(n+1) gives q(n)=q(n+1). Localizing at q would therefore make the powers of the nonzero maximal ideal qRq​ stabilize at a nonzero ideal, contradicting the Krull intersection theorem for the Noetherian local domain Rq​.
