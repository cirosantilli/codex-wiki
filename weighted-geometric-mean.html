Weighted geometric mean (Gw​=∏i​xiwi​​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Geometric mean
OurBigBook.com Words: 197
For positive real numbers xi​ and nonnegative weights with ∑i​wi​=1, the weighted geometric mean is
Gw​=∏i​xiwi​​=exp(∑i​wi​logxi​).
(10)
It takes a weighted mean of logarithms. A zero weight excludes that input; equal weights give the ordinary geometric mean. If xi​ occurs mi​ times in a list of total length M, its correct weight is wi​=mi​/M. For the list (12,3,3,3),
Gw​=121/433/4=32​≈4.24264,
(11)
and replacing all four entries by Gw​ preserves the product 324.
There is also an optimization interpretation. A logarithmic transformation turns multiplicative discrepancies into additive ones. For a positive representative c, the weighted mean minimizes squared deviations identity gives
∑i​wi​(logxi​−logc)2=∑i​wi​(logxi​−logGw​)2+(logc−logGw​)2.
(12)
Thus c=Gw​ uniquely minimizes weighted quadratic loss in logarithmic coordinates. The weighted arithmetic mean minimizes squared discrepancies in the original coordinates. These two objectives explain why different choices of what constitutes an error select different summaries.
The strictly concave function logx and the reversed Jensen's inequality give Gw​≤Aw​, where Aw​=∑i​wi​xi​ is the weighted arithmetic mean. Apply the same reasoning to the multiplicative inverses to get Hw​≤Gw​ for the weighted harmonic mean. Thus Hw​≤Gw​≤Aw​; equality holds exactly when all positive-weight inputs agree. For this list, Hw​=48/13, Gw​=32​, and Aw​=21/4. The weights specify which inputs are represented and must stay the same across the comparison.
