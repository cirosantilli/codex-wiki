Joukowski map from the upper half-disc to the upper half-plane (J(w)=−(w+w−1))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Joukowski map
OurBigBook.com Words: 119
The holomorphic function J(w)=−(w+w−1) maps D+​={w:∣w∣<1, Imw>0} conformally and bijectively onto the upper half-plane. Its imaginary part is Imw(∣w∣−2−1)>0, and its derivative w−2−1 never vanishes there.
Equality of two values implies (w1​−w2​)(1−1/(w1​w2​))=0; since ∣w1​w2​∣<1, this proves injectivity. For any target ζ in the upper half-plane, the quadratic equation w2+ζw+1=0 has two roots with product one and neither on the unit circle. Exactly one therefore has modulus below one; the imaginary-part formula forces that root to lie in D+​, proving surjectivity.
In particular, z↦eiz maps {0<Rez<π, Imz>0} bijectively to D+​, and composition gives the conformal map z↦−2cosz onto the upper half-plane. The two sides of the half-strip map to the outer real rays, while its bottom edge maps to the segment [−2,2].
