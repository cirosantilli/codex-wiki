Photodiode

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Photodetector
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
A photodiode is a semiconductor diode producing photocurrent from photon absorption. Exciting an electron across the band gap leaves an electron hole. The depletion region's electric field separates the charge carriers; others can arrive by diffusion before electron-hole recombination. Contacts complete the electrical circuit.[ref]
The useful bridge is from light to a measurable electric current: spectral responsivity converts incident radiant flux into photocurrent, while quantum efficiency counts collected signal charge carriers per incident photon. These answer different questions: how much electric current one watt produces, and how many incident photons contribute to the signal. The worked calculation below makes their relationship explicit.
