Epigenesis (biology)

Words: 24
Embryology OurBigBook.com
Progressive formation and differentiation of an organism during biological development, historically contrasted with preformationism. It must not be confused with the modern concept of epigenetics.

Preformationism

Words: 17
Embryology OurBigBook.com
The historical view that embryonic development enlarges an already formed organism rather than generating its organization progressively.

Maternal-to-zygotic transition

Words: 21
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
The transition from predominant reliance on maternally supplied products to zygotic gene expression, accompanied by regulated turnover or redeployment of maternal molecules.

Implantation

Words: 19
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Attachment and embedding of a mammalian conceptus in maternal tissue. Embryonic and extraembryonic tissues undergo coordinated morphogenesis during this transition.

Plant development

Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Formation and differentiation of plant tissues and organs, continuously influenced by internal state and the environment.

Root nodule

Plant development OurBigBook.com
A specialized plant organ hosting symbiotic nitrogen fixation in compatible associations. Nodule initiation, bacterial infection and effective nitrogen fixation are separable outcomes.

Nodulation

Formation of root nodules. More nodules do not necessarily imply more effective nitrogen fixation or greater host benefit.

Heart development

Words: 168 Articles: 9
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com

Myocardium

Words: 18
Heart development OurBigBook.com
The muscular tissue of the heart. Regional myocardial differentiation and growth are distinct from endocardial or supporting-cell development.

Atrioventricular canal

Words: 21
Heart development OurBigBook.com
The region connecting atrial and ventricular compartments during cardiac development. Its regulatory programme differs from that of rapidly expanding chamber myocardium.

Chamber myocardium

Words: 12
Heart development OurBigBook.com
myocardium acquiring chamber-associated growth, structural and electrophysiological properties, distinct from specialized non-chamber territories.

Cardiac looping

Words: 18
Heart development OurBigBook.com
Three-dimensional bending and torsion of the early heart tube. Geometry, left–right patterning, tissue growth and regional cell differentiation contribute distinct aspects.

Heart tube

Words: 53 Articles: 2
Heart development OurBigBook.com
An early tubular cardiac structure whose construction includes tissue convergence, differentiation and continued addition of cells at its poles.

Venous pole of the heart

Words: 20
Heart tube OurBigBook.com
The inflow end of the developing heart, with progenitors contributing to inflow and atrial regions under species- and stage-specific conditions.

Arterial pole of the heart

Words: 14
Heart tube OurBigBook.com
The outflow end of the developing heart, receiving progenitor contributions on a stage-specific schedule.

Cardiac crescent

Words: 15
Heart development OurBigBook.com
An early arrangement of cardiogenic tissue before formation of the linear heart tube in the mammalian embryo.

First heart field

Words: 31
Heart development OurBigBook.com
An early cardiac progenitor domain that differentiates into much of the initial myocardium, with major contributions to the left ventricle in mouse. Its boundaries and contributions require stage- and species-specific fate mapping.

Homeotic transformation

Words: 25
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Replacement of one position-specific developmental identity by another. Demonstrating it requires positive evidence for the alternative identity as well as loss of the original features.

Developmental timing

Words: 22
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
The timing and ordering of specification, differentiation, tissue movement and maturation. Equal chronological age need not imply equal developmental stage after perturbation.

Fetal breathing movement

Lung development OurBigBook.com
Movement before air breathing that contributes to fluid-related mechanical stimulation of the developing lung. Physical loading and mature respiratory function are distinct.

Alveolar epithelium

Words: 40 Articles: 2
Lung development OurBigBook.com
The epithelial lining of pulmonary gas-exchange units, containing differently specialized cell types.

Type II alveolar cell

Words: 12
Alveolar epithelium OurBigBook.com
An alveolar epithelial cell with secretory functions including surfactant production and context-dependent progenitor activity.

Type I alveolar cell

Words: 16
Alveolar epithelium OurBigBook.com
A thin, extended epithelial cell contributing to the gas-exchange surface. Flattening and molecular differentiation should be measured separately.

Feather placode

Words: 22
Skin development OurBigBook.com
A localized epithelial primordium of a feather. Its molecular identity and underlying dermal aggregation can be coupled through mechanical and chemical signals.

Developmental competence

Words: 29
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
The ability of a cell or tissue to respond to an instructive signal at a particular stage and location. The same signal can have different effects after competence changes.

Neural development

Words: 91 Articles: 4
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Formation and organization of nervous-system cells and their connections.

Neural progenitor cell

Words: 50 Articles: 1
Neural development OurBigBook.com
A precursor able to produce one or more neural cell types. multipotency is a property of an individual progenitor under tested conditions, not merely of a mixed population.

Radial glial cell

Words: 24
Neural progenitor cell OurBigBook.com
A neural progenitor with radial processes and context-dependent neuronal or glial outputs. cell morphology, molecular state and traced descendants provide complementary evidence for its role.

Neuronal differentiation

Words: 23
Neural development OurBigBook.com
Acquisition of neuronal identity and function. Commitment, subtype specification, maturation and survival should be measured separately rather than inferred from one neuronal marker.

Axon guidance

Words: 9
Neural development OurBigBook.com
Processes directing growing axons to appropriate regions or targets.

Epicardium

Words: 20
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
The outer tissue layer covering the heart, with developmental contributions to supporting cells and signals affecting myocardial and coronary development.

Endocardium

Words: 15
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
The inner endothelial lining of the heart, which also provides developmental signals to neighbouring myocardium.

Second heart field

Words: 26
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
A population of cardiac progenitors that contributes cells to the developing heart after initial heart-tube formation. Retaining a proliferative reservoir allows progressive addition of differentiated myocardium.

Regeneration

Words: 46 Articles: 1
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Replacement of lost biological structures through organized growth and differentiation. Recovery of mass alone need not demonstrate restoration of the original pattern or function.

Blastema

Words: 22
Regeneration OurBigBook.com
A regeneration-associated accumulation of progenitor cells. A blastema need not be a homogeneous pluripotent population; contributors can retain tissue and positional restrictions.

Positional information

Words: 55 Articles: 1
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Information allowing a cell to respond according to its location within a developing or regenerating structure. Establishing a positional value and interpreting it as a differentiated outcome are distinct steps.

Positional memory

Words: 25
Positional information OurBigBook.com
Persistence of a position-associated cellular identity after the original spatial context or instructive signal changes. It is distinct from simply retaining a differentiated tissue identity.

Intestinal development

Words: 38 Articles: 1
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Formation, regionalization and maturation of the intestine, including its epithelial surface, supporting tissues and renewing cell populations.

Villus morphogenesis

Words: 21
Intestinal development OurBigBook.com
Formation of epithelial villi through interactions among growth, tissue constraints and local signalling. The ordering of these events differs between species.

Mesenchyme

Words: 24
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
A tissue organization with cells dispersed through extracellular matrix, rather than arranged as a continuous polarized epithelium. Mesenchymal populations differ in origin and developmental potential.

A posterior limb-bud signalling region involved in anterior–posterior patterning. The fate of its labelled cells and the identities of adjacent anatomical digits are related but distinct observations.

ABC model of flower development

Words: 41
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
A model relating overlapping regulatory functions to floral organ identities. B- and C-function activity contributes to male organ identity, whereas C activity without B contributes to female identity, with additional partners required. Its simple domains are not universal across all angiosperms.

Developmental modularity

Words: 24
Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Organization of biological development into relatively separable components. It can allow one structure or deployment to evolve with fewer effects on others, without implying complete independence.

Developmental bias

Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Unequal accessibility of phenotypic variants caused by the organization of biological development. Repeated outcomes can reflect this structure as well as repeated natural selection; accessibility and selective advantage are different questions.

Serial homology

Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Correspondence among repeated structures within an organism, such as repeated appendages or segments. Repeated structures can subsequently specialize through divergent developmental regulation.

Heterotopy

An evolutionary change in the spatial deployment of a developmental process. Altering where a regulatory input is expressed can change the resulting pattern.

Deep homology

Developmental biology OurBigBook.com
Shared inherited developmental machinery underlying structures whose final organization can differ substantially. A shared regulator alone does not establish that two entire organs have the same historical origin.

Recruitment of an existing gene or gene regulatory network to a new developmental deployment or function. Changing the location of gene expression can generate a new structure using inherited cellular activities.

Evolutionary change in the amount or level of a developmental process, structure or expression pattern. A quantitative difference still requires a causal molecular and developmental explanation.

Accessibility in a model system does not establish the historical precursor.
