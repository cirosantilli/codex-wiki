Bacteria

 Home Biology Organism Prokaryote
OurBigBook.com Words: 343 Articles: 6
Bacteria are a major evolutionary lineage of organisms with prokaryotic cellular organization. Escherichia coli and cyanobacteria illustrate its variety: the same broad cellular plan supports different kinds of metabolism. Most bacteria have a cell wall containing peptidoglycan; archaea do not have peptidoglycan walls. A cell wall and a cell membrane are distinct structures, and some bacteria lack a cell wall. See OpenStax Biology 2e, 22.2: bacterial envelopes.
The usefulness of recognizing bacteria extends beyond naming: it identifies a shared evolutionary background for comparing genes, proteins and cellular mechanisms. A familiar shape or the absence of a cell nucleus alone cannot distinguish bacteria from archaea; molecular comparisons, including ribosomal RNA, supply additional evidence.
