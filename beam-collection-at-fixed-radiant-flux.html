Beam collection at fixed radiant flux (Φcollected​=Pmin(1,a2/b2))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiant flux
OurBigBook.com Words: 230
Consider an ideal parallel circular beam with uniform irradiance inside radius b, zero outside, and total radiant flux P=2W. A centred circular collector of radius a, perpendicular to the light rays, intercepts
Eb​=πb2P​,Φcollected​=Eb​πmin(a,b)2=Pmin(1,b2a2​).
(4)
For b=1cm and a=0.5cm, Eb​=20000/πWm−2≃6366.20Wm−2 and the collected radiant flux is 0.50W. Expanding the beam to b=2cm while keeping P fixed divides irradiance and the same collector's radiant flux by four: Eb​≃1591.55Wm−2 and Φcollected​=0.125W. A collector covering either entire beam still receives 2W.
Tilting a flat collector of actual area A by an angle θ to the beam direction gives Φcollected​=Eb​Acosθ if its entire projected area remains inside the uniform beam. At 60∘ the small collector's radiant flux halves. A collector large enough to intercept the whole beam after tilting still receives P: the footprint on its surface grows as 1/cosθ and cancels the irradiance factor. This model ignores diffraction, beam-edge structure, and changes in absorption with incidence angle; it counts incident rather than absorbed power.
