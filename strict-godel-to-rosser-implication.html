Strict Gödel-to-Rosser implication (T⊢G→R,T⊬R→G)

 ... Mathematical logic First-order logic First-order theory Diagonal lemma Gödel sentence Rosser sentence
OurBigBook.com Words: 76
For the standard Gödel sentence and Rosser sentence, the formalized Rosser unprovability theorem and G↔Con(T) yield T⊢G→R. If T+Con(T) is consistent, the converse is unprovable under the usual formalized incompleteness hypotheses. A proof of R→G would give □¬G→□¬R. Formalized negative unprovability identifies ¬□¬R with Con(T) and ¬□¬G with Con(T+Con(T)). The strengthened theory would then prove its own consistency, contradicting the Gödel second incompleteness theorem. The consistency assumption belongs to this strictness conclusion.
