Noninjective linear measurement (T(x)=T(x′)⟺x−x′∈kerT)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Kernel of a linear map
OurBigBook.com Words: 216
When a measurement model is a linear map T, its kernel records changes that its readout cannot distinguish. For example, the rectangular kernel and solution-fibre example measures only x+2y and y+z. Even exact readouts (3,2) leave the whole fiber u0​+kerA possible. Repeating either readout with greater precision cannot distinguish those inputs: their outputs agree exactly. This ambiguity follows from a nontrivial kernel, rather than from measurement error.
Additional information can remove a null direction of a linear map. In that example, adding a third linear functional that reads y distinguishes u0​+tv, because its third readout is t. More generally, additional linear functionals c1​,…,ck​ give unique recovery of all inputs exactly when
kerT∩kerc1​∩⋯∩kerck​={0}.
(5)
This follows by applying the trivial kernel criterion to the combined linear map x↦(Tx,c1​(x),…,ck​(x)). Merely adding repeated linear equations does not meet this condition. On a finite-dimensional input vector space, each added scalar readout can increase the matrix rank by at most one, so at least dimkerT extra independent readouts are needed for recovery of unrestricted inputs.
Restrictions on the possible inputs can also make recovery unique. If the allowed inputs form a set D, two allowed inputs are indistinguishable exactly when their difference is in the kernel. Thus T is injective on D precisely when no difference of distinct points of D is a kernel vector. Such a restriction is extra information about the input; the original readout alone determines only its fiber.
