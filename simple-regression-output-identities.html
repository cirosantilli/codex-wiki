Consider ordinary least squares with an intercept, one nonconstant predictor, n>2 cases, Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2>0 a nonconstant response, and a positive residual sum of squares. With the usual common-variance standard error, put s2=RSS/(n−2). Then
yˉ​=α+β​xˉ,SE(β​)=s/Sxx​​,F=tslope2​=1−R2(n−2)R2​.
(11)
The least-squares normal equations give zero residual sum and zero residual inner product with the centred predictor, proving the first identity and β​=Sxy​/Sxx​. The fitted centred sum of squares is β​2Sxx​. Dividing it by s2 gives the one-predictor F statistic and the squared zero-slope t statistic. Dividing fitted and residual sums of squares by their total gives the last expression. If 0≤R2<1, the adjusted coefficient of determination is 1−(1−R2)(n−1)/(n−2).
These are algebraic consistency identities for one specified fit; the mean identity needs no normal-error assumption. A normal linear model supplies the usual finite-sample t and F reference distributions. Heteroscedasticity-robust standard errors need not satisfy the displayed F=t-squared relationship to the classical sums-of-squares F statistic. Rounding can create small discrepancies, while different analysed records or a changed model can create larger ones; neither should be silently treated as the same fit.[ref]
