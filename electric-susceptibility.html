Electric susceptibility (χe​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Permittivity Dielectric
OurBigBook.com Words: 522 Articles: 5
The dimensionless quantity relating induced electric polarization to the internal electric field through P=ϵ0​χe​E in the static scalar model. It describes the material contribution; relative permittivity includes the vacuum term as well, ϵr​=1+χe​. Zero electric susceptibility gives ϵr​=1, not zero permittivity.
The scalar χe​ assumes a local response with linearity and isotropy, settled electric polarization, fixed material conditions, and no residual electric polarization at zero electric field. It relates P to the averaged total internal electric field, which already includes the field of the bound electric charges. It does not relate P to the field the external electrodes would produce in vacuum. The distinction is essential for a self-consistent calculation.[ref]
Substitute the scalar relation into the definition of electric displacement field:
D=ϵ0​E+P=ϵ0​(1+χe​)E.
(5)
Comparison with D=ϵE proves ϵ=ϵ0​(1+χe​) and ϵr​=1+χe​. Both D and P have units C/m2, so χe​ is a dimensionless quantity. In a stipulated static model with ϵr​=3, χe​=2: at a prescribed internal electric field, the material contribution P is twice ϵ0​E. This is a hypothetical response, not a measured material constant. The electric susceptibility is a convenient bridge from microscopic dielectric polarization to a macroscopic capacitance measurement, but it is not a measure of electrical conductivity.
