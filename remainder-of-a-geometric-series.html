Remainder of a geometric series (RN​=arN/(1−r))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Geometric series
OurBigBook.com Words: 204
For ∣r∣<1, let SN​ contain the first N terms, with S0​=0. The remainder after those terms is
RN​=∑j=N∞​arj=1−rarN​,​1−ra​−SN​​=∣1−r∣∣a∣∣r∣N​.
(86)
This is the exact absolute error. A convenient bound replaces the denominator by 1−∣r∣, giving ∣RN​∣≤∣a∣∣r∣N/(1−∣r∣) through the triangle inequality. The exact denominator is preferable when the sign or complex phase of r is known.
For a>0 and 0<r<1, the omitted fraction of the entire sum is RN​/(a/(1−r))=rN. Capturing at least 99 percent therefore means rN≤0.01. Taking logarithms and applying the ceiling function gives the smallest integer N:
N=⌈logrlog(0.01)​⌉.
(87)
This gives 7,21,90, and 459 terms for r=0.5,0.8,0.95, and 0.99, respectively. The difference is a concrete reason to care about the common ratio, even when all four series converge.
An absolute target has a different scale. For a=1, r=0.8, and required absolute error at most 0.01, the total sum is 5 and
∣RN​∣=5(0.8)N≤0.01.
(88)
The first acceptable term count is N=28: the remainder is approximately 0.0096714, while at N=27 it is approximately 0.0120893. Counting terms and checking the tail avoids an ambiguous instruction to stop when a term merely looks small.
