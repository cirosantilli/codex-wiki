Interferometric baseline (b, b⊥​/λ)

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Observational astronomy Radio astronomy Aperture synthesis Radio interferometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 480 Articles: 4
An interferometric baseline is the displacement b=r2​−r1​ between two antennas. For a unit line of sight s0​ toward the phase centre, its projection onto the plane of the sky is
b⊥​=b−(b⋅s0​)s0​,∣τg​∣=c∣b⋅s0​∣​.
(54)
Here c is the speed of light. The signed delay depends on which receiver is delayed; the geometric path difference's magnitude does not. If eθ​ is a unit tangent in the direction being imaged, the dimensionless coordinate u=b⋅eθ​/λ is a spatial frequency, measured in cycles per radian of small sky offset. A single pair gives fringes of angular period 1/∣u∣ along that tangent. This period specifies one measured oscillation; it does not specify a whole image's angular resolution or eliminate ambiguous source arrangements.
The left panel separates the component producing the reference delay from the component measuring angular structure. The right panel shows a possible Earth-rotation aperture synthesis track, with its opposite complex conjugate track. Those opposite samples provide the symmetry required by a real brightness distribution; they are not independent extra observations.
