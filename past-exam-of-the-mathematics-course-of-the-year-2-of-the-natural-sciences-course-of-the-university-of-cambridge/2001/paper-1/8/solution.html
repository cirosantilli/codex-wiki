Adapted homogeneous solutions. First assume real κ=0. The functions u(x)=cosh(κx) and v(x)=cosh(κ(1−x)) solve z′′−κ2z=0 and obey u′(0)=v′(1)=0. Their Wronskian is
W=uv′−u′v=−κ[cosh(κx)sinh(κ(1−x))+sinh(κx)cosh(κ(1−x))]=−κsinhκ.
(57)
It is independent of x and nonzero.
Green function. For 0<ξ<1, continuity at x=ξ and the jump condition Gx​(ξ+,ξ)−Gx​(ξ−,ξ)=1 follow by matching the Dirac delta function source. In particular a jump in G itself would produce a derivative of the Dirac delta function, which is absent. Write G=A(ξ)u(x) for x<ξ and G=B(ξ)v(x) for x>ξ. Solving the continuity and derivative-jump equations gives A=v(ξ)/W, B=u(ξ)/W. Hence the Neumann Green function for the modified Helmholtz equation on an interval is
G(x,ξ)=−κsinhκ1​{cosh(κx)cosh(κ(1−ξ)),cosh(κξ)cosh(κ(1−x)),​x≤ξ,x≥ξ.​​
(58)
The two pieces coincide at the source. Their derivative difference is W/W=1 and their endpoint derivatives vanish. The same expression has continuous one-sided limits as the source approaches an endpoint; the interior-source construction is what is needed in the integral solution.
Solution for a general source. By superposition, y(x)=∫01​G(x,ξ)f(ξ)dξ. Splitting the integral at ξ=x gives
y(x)=−κsinhκcosh(κx)∫x1​f(ξ)cosh(κ(1−ξ))dξ+cosh(κ(1−x))∫0x​f(ξ)cosh(κξ)dξ​.​

No nonzero homogeneous solution satisfies both endpoint conditions when κ=0: the condition at zero eliminates sinh(κx), and the condition at one then eliminates cosh(κx). Thus the Green function gives the unique solution.
The printed zero-parameter case. The statement permits real κ, but κ=0 is exceptional. Integrating a putative equation G′′=δ(x−ξ) would give G′(1)−G′(0)=1, contrary to the two zero derivatives. Thus this Green function does not exist at zero. For y′′=f with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, solvability instead requires ∫01​f=0, and a compatible solution has an arbitrary additive constant. This is the Fredholm solvability condition associated with the constant zero mode.
Explicit forcing. For f(x)=x and κ=1, the particular solution is −x. Write y=Ccoshx+Dsinhx−x. Then y′(0)=0 gives D=1 and y′(1)=0 gives C=(1−cosh1)/sinh1=−tanh(1/2). Therefore
y(x)=sinhx−tanh(1/2)coshx−x=cosh(1/2)sinh(x−1/2)​−x.​
(60)
Direct differentiation gives y′=cosh(x−1/2)/cosh(1/2)−1, which vanishes at both endpoints, and y′′−y=x, verifying the solution independently of the Green function integration.
