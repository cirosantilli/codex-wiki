Harmonic parts. Write the holomorphic function as f=u+iv. The Cauchy-Riemann equations say ux​=vy​ and uy​=−vx​. Equality of mixed partial derivatives therefore gives
uxx​+uyy​=vyx​−vxy​=0,vxx​+vyy​=−uyx​+uxy​=0.
(12)
Thus both the real and imaginary parts are harmonic functions and satisfy two-dimensional Laplace's equation.
Single-valued logarithm. A convenient choice is
f(z)=Log(1+z)−Log(1−z),f(0)=0,
(13)
using the principal logarithm for each term. Its branch cuts in the z plane are (−∞,−1] and [1,∞). The branch points are −1 and 1: going once around the first adds 2πi, while going once around the second subtracts 2πi. Removing the two exterior rays makes this a consistent branch of a multivalued function and in particular leaves the whole open unit disk available.
Potential inside the cylinder. Put z=reiθ with r<1. Multiplication by 1−z gives
1−z1+z​=1−2rcosθ+r21−r2+2irsinθ​.
(14)
The denominator is positive and the real part of the numerator is positive. Thus its argument lies strictly between −π/2 and π/2, and
Imf(reiθ)=arctan1−r22rsinθ​.
(15)
As r↑1, this tends to π/2 on the upper open semicircle and −π/2 on the lower open semicircle. Scale the imaginary part to obtain
Φ(r,θ)=π2V0​​arctan1−r22rsinθ​,0≤r<1.​
(16)
It is a harmonic function, has the required boundary values, and gives Φ(0)=0. The two joining points of the boundary arcs have discontinuous boundary data, so no common continuous boundary value is demanded there. Along the real diameter, including radial approaches to either joining point, the displayed solution is zero. This is the disk potential with two semicircular boundary values.
For a second check, the Taylor series of the logarithms gives f(z)=2∑j≥0​z2j+1/(2j+1). Taking the imaginary part yields the Fourier series
Φ(r,θ)=π4V0​​∑j=0∞​2j+1r2j+1sin((2j+1)θ)​,
(17)
whose boundary limit is exactly the required alternating pair of constant semicircle values.
