Explicit diffusion scheme. Take xj​=jh, h=1/N, tn​=nτ, and let Ujn​ approximate ψ(xj​,tn​). Combining the forward Euler method in time with D4,h​ in space gives
Ujn+1​=Ujn​+12h2τ​(−Uj+2n​+16Uj+1n​−30Ujn​+16Uj−1n​−Uj−2n​),Uj0​=f(jh).​
(69)
To check the error, insert the exact solution. The heat equation gives ψt​=ψxx​, while the time Taylor expansion and the spatial formula give the one-step defect
ψ(xj​,tn​+τ)−ψ(xj​,tn​)−τD4,h​ψ(xj​,tn​)=2τ2​ψtt​+90τh4​ψxxxxxx​+O(τ3+τh6).
(70)
Thus its unnormalized local truncation error is
O(τ2)+O(τh4),​
(71)
as requested. Dividing this defect by τ gives the normalized residual O(τ)+O(h4); these are distinct error conventions.
Boundary implementation. The centered stencil requires two ghost values at each end. An even-reflection closure for Neumann diffusion imposes
U−1n​=U1n​,U−2n​=U2n​,UN+1n​=UN−1n​,UN+2n​=UN−2n​.​
(72)
The reflected first centered difference at either endpoint is zero. Apply the same evolution formula at the endpoints after substituting these ghost values; for example,
U0n+1​=U0n​+12h2τ​(−2U2n​+32U1n​−30U0n​).
(73)
The ghost values also close the stencil at j=1 and j=N−1. They are determined auxiliary values, not new physical data. For a smooth solution compatible with the Neumann boundary conditions, differentiating those conditions in time and using the heat equation gives ψxxx​=ψxxxxx​=⋯=0 at the endpoints. Thus even reflection preserves the smoothness needed for fourth-order spatial accuracy. If the initial data fail the required higher compatibility conditions, a uniform smooth error expansion through t=0 cannot be assumed.
For completeness, the reflected spatial operator has cosine eigenvectors cos(ℓπj/N) and eigenvalues
λℓ​=−h24​sin22Nℓπ​(1+31​sin22Nℓπ​).
(74)
The most negative one is −16/(3h2). Requiring ∣1+τλℓ​∣≤1 gives the forward Euler method stability restriction τ/h2≤3/8​ in the natural trapezoid-weighted discrete norm. A fourth-order spatial stencil does not remove the explicit diffusion time-step restriction.
Implicit alternative. The Crank-Nicolson diffusion scheme using the same spatial operator and boundary closure is
(I−2τ​D4,h​)Un+1=(I+2τ​D4,h​)Un.​
(75)
It has second-order time accuracy and fourth-order spatial accuracy for smooth compatible solutions; its one-step defect is O(τ3)+O(τh4). Each spatial eigenvalue λ≤0 has amplification factor (1+τλ/2)/(1−τλ/2) of magnitude at most one, so it removes the explicit restriction for this boundary closure.
The price is solving a banded linear system at every step; the same matrix factorization can be reused for fixed h and τ. Large time steps still reduce accuracy. Moreover, the amplification factor tends to −1 as τ∣λ∣→∞, so short spatial wavelengths can alternate sign with little numerical damping. Thus unconditional linear stability does not guarantee a monotone or well-resolved transient. Boundary accuracy and initial compatibility remain necessary with either time discretization.
