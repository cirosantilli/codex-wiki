Definitions. A group homomorphism φ:G1​→G2​ preserves multiplication, φ(xy)=φ(x)φ(y). A group isomorphism is a bijective group homomorphism. The kernel of a group homomorphism is
K=kerφ={x∈G1​:φ(x)=e2​}.​
(55)
Preserving multiplication implies φ(e1​)=e2​ and φ(x−1)=φ(x)−1: the first follows by cancelling φ(e1​) from its square, and the second follows by applying φ to xx−1=e1​.
Kernel subgroup. The identity belongs to K. For x,y∈K, φ(xy)=e2​e2​=e2​, so xy∈K. Also φ(x−1)=e2−1​=e2​, so x−1∈K. Thus K≤G1​​.
Normality. For g∈G1​ and k∈K,
φ(gkg−1)=φ(g)φ(k)φ(g)−1=φ(g)e2​φ(g)−1=e2​.
(56)
Hence every gkg−1 is again in K, proving K⊴G1​​. Applying the same statement with g−1 gives equality gKg−1=K, and therefore gK=Kg.
Group of cosets. Define the product on the left cosets by
(aK)(bK)=(ab)K.​
(57)
To check that this does not depend on representatives, replace a,b by ak1​,bk2​. Then
(ak1​)(bk2​)=ab(b−1k1​b)k2​∈abK,
(58)
because normality puts b−1k1​b in K. Thus the product is well defined. It is associative because multiplication in G1​ is associative; its identity is K=e1​K, and the inverse of aK is a−1K. Consequently the cosets form the quotient group G1​/K​.
The map aK↦φ(a) is well defined and gives an isomorphism G1​/K≅imφ. If the original mapping is onto G2​, this becomes G1​/K≅G2​. No surjectivity is needed for the kernel of a group homomorphism to be a normal subgroup or for the quotient group to exist.
