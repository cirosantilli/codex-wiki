The original PDF has a typographical omission: its displayed equation has λw(x) rather than λw(x)y(x) on the right. Taken literally, it is an inhomogeneous boundary value problem, not the stated Sturm-Liouville problem. The quotient and the later eigenfunction expansion identify the intended equation, which the following derivation produces explicitly.
Choose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions y(a)=y(b)=0 and a nonzero real admissible y. Then G[y]>0 because w>0. For an endpoint-zero variation η, integration by parts gives
δF=2∫ab​[py′η′+qyη]dx=2∫ab​[−(py′)′+qy]ηdx,δG=2∫ab​wyηdx.
(88)
The first variation of the Rayleigh quotient is thus
δΛ=G[y]2​∫ab​[−(py′)′+qy−Λ[y]wy]ηdx.
(89)
By the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations, stationarity is equivalent to
−(py′)′+qy=λwy,λ=Λ[y],y(a)=y(b)=0.​
(90)
Conversely, multiply this corrected eigenvalue equation by y and integrate. The same boundary cancellation gives F[y]=λG[y], so the first variation vanishes. This proves the requested equivalence for the intended equation. Homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, or a Dirichlet condition at one end and a Neumann condition at the other, also eliminate the boundary term. General Robin boundary conditions would require the corresponding boundary contribution in the numerator.
