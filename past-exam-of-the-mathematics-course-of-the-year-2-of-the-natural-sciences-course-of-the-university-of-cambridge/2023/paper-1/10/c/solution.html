Take γ>0 and set A=π/2. The Rayleigh quotient of the trial for the quantum harmonic oscillator is
R(γ)=∫−A/γA/γ​cos2(γx)dx∫−A/γA/γ​[γ2sin2(γx)+x2cos2(γx)]dx​.
(94)
Its denominator is π/(2γ). The kinetic numerator is γ2 times that denominator. For the remaining integral, put t=γx and use
∫−π/2π/2​t2cos2tdt=24π3​−4π​=24π(π2−6)​,
(95)
obtained from cos2t=(1+cos2t)/2 and integration by parts. Thus the compact cosine trial bound for the quantum harmonic oscillator is
R(γ)=γ2+12γ2π2−6​.​
(96)
Differentiation gives R′=2γ−(π2−6)/(6γ3), so the minimizing parameter satisfies
γopt4​=12π2−6​.​
(97)
The quotient tends to infinity at either end of γ>0, and the two positive terms are equal at this stationary point, proving it is the global minimum. Therefore
λ0​≤3π2−6​​≈1.13572.​
(98)
For comparison, the exact ground state has value 1. The trial is admissible for the energy quadratic form: it is continuous, has compact support, and its weak derivative is square-integrable. Its first derivative jumps at the support endpoints, so it need not belong to the smaller twice-differentiable operator domain; this does not invalidate the Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle bound.
