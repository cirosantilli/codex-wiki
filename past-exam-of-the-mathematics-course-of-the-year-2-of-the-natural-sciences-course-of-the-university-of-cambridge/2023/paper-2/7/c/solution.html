For the stated geometry and numerical masses, the inertia and stiffness matrices are
M=diag(2,41​,252​),K=​5−20​−23−1​0−11​​.
(58)
A normal mode θ=veiωt requires (K−ω2M)v=0. Writing z=ω2, its characteristic equation is
det(K−zM)=−1004z3−108z2+613z−600​=0.
(59)
At the claimed z=6, this determinant is −27/50=0. Hence the printed claim that 6​ is a normal frequency is false for the stated data, and there is no corresponding nonzero normal mode.
The complete geometry-based answer is obtained from the three positive roots zj​:
4zj3​−108zj2​+613zj​−600(ωlow​,ωmiddle​,ωhigh​)​=0,≃(1.111471775, 2.491790287, 4.422172730).​​
(60)
An exact closed form for the squared angular frequencies is
zj​=9+3359​​cos[31​arccos3593/227453​​−32πj​],j=0,1,2.
(61)
They are approximately 19.555611657, 6.209018836 and 1.235369507, respectively. For any of these roots, a corresponding eigenvector can be chosen as
v(z)=​21​[(3−z/4)(1−2z/25)−1]1−2z/251​​.​
(62)
The last two rows of (K−zM)v=0 give this expression directly; the first is the characteristic equation. These three modes span all motion, with arbitrary sine and cosine amplitudes. Positivity of M and of the spring potential energy ensures that all squared angular frequencies are positive.
If instead one takes the PDF's printed I1​=m1​a2/6 as a stipulated inertia, then M=diag(1/2,1/4,2/25) and
det(K−zM)=−2002z3−69z2+626z−1200​.
(63)
The corresponding angular frequencies are approximately
(1.616170898, 3.315716400, 4.570997307).​
(64)
The same formula for v(z) applies, because its derivation used only the second and third rows. Here the determinant at z=6 is −63/25=0, so following the printed inertia does not repair the frequency claim. The PDF therefore needs a further correction to its numerical data or asserted frequency; the intended correction cannot be inferred uniquely.
