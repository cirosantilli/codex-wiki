Apply the Laplacian in polar coordinates twice to the proposed separated function. For any real exponent m,
∇2(rmcos2θ)=(m2−4)rm−2cos2θ,
(1)
so the biharmonic equation becomes
∇4(rmcos2θ)=(m2−4)((m−2)2−4)rm−4cos2θ=0.
(2)
Its four distinct roots are m=−2,0,2,4. The separated polar solutions of the biharmonic equation therefore give the complete radial family in this angular sector,
ψ=(Ar−2+B+Cr2+Dr4)cos2θ.
(3)
Equivalently, its radial equation is a fourth-order Cauchy-Euler equation; four distinct powers supply four linearly independent solutions, so no logarithmic terms are required.
Interior. Continuity with value zero at the origin removes both r−2cos2θ and cos2θ. The latter depends on the approach direction even though it is bounded. The two boundary conditions at unit radius then give C+D=0 and 2C+4D=1. Consequently
ψin​(r,θ)=21​(r4−r2)cos2θ,0≤r≤1.​
(4)
This extends smoothly through the origin: r2cos2θ=x2−y2 and r4cos2θ=(x2+y2)(x2−y2) are polynomials. Its radial derivative at r=1 is (2r3−r)cos2θ=cos2θ.
Exterior. The condition on the radial derivative at infinity excludes the r2 and r4 terms. A nonzero Bcos2θ is permitted, since the required decay concerns the radial derivative. Now A+B=0 and −2A=1, giving
ψout​(r,θ)=21​(1−r−2)cos2θ,r≥1.​
(5)
Indeed ∂r​ψout​=r−3cos2θ→0, and its value at unit radius is zero. These are particular interior and exterior solutions for the biharmonic streamfunction; no condition requiring ψ itself to vanish at infinity was imposed.
