For a piecewise continuous function of exponential order on t≥0, its unilateral Laplace transform is
F(p)=∫0∞​e−ptf(t)dt.​
(32)
The Bromwich inversion formula, at a point of continuity, is
f(t)=2πi1​T→∞lim​∫γ−iTγ+iT​eptF(p)dp.​
(33)
The Bromwich contour is a straight vertical line, traversed upwards, with γ to the right of the abscissa of convergence and hence of all singularities of the transform. Under the usual piecewise smoothness assumptions, at a jump the symmetric inversion limit gives the mean of the two one-sided values.
In the transform in the question, put λ=e−t. Then dλ=−e−tdt and
H(s)=∫0∞​e−sth(e−t)dt.
(34)
Thus the restricted Mellin transform on the unit interval is the Laplace transform of f(t)=h(e−t). Apply the Bromwich inversion formula with t=−logλ, where 0<λ<1, to obtain
h(λ)=2πi1​T→∞lim​∫γ−iTγ+iT​λ−sH(s)ds.​
(35)
Here γ lies in the convergence half-plane of H. At a discontinuity this formula has the same mean-value interpretation. The real natural logarithm defines λ−s unambiguously.
