Integral identity and representation. Define L=∇2+k2 and take n to be the outward unit normal. The product rule gives
∇⋅(ϕ∇ψ−ψ∇ϕ)=ϕ∇2ψ−ψ∇2ϕ=ϕLψ−ψLϕ.
(6)
The k2ϕψ terms cancel. Integrating and applying the divergence theorem proves the Helmholtz equation version of Green's second identity:
∫V​(ϕLψ−ψLϕ)dV=∫S​(ϕ∂n​ψ−ψ∂n​ϕ)dS.​
(7)
Choose the Dirichlet Green function with the sign convention
Lx​G(x,x′)=δ(x−x′),G(x,x′)=0(x∈S),
(8)
where δ is the three-dimensional Dirac delta. Substitute ψ=G, use Lϕ=f, and evaluate the Dirac delta integral. The boundary term containing G vanishes and ϕ=g on S, leaving
ϕ(x′)−∫V​G(x,x′)f(x)dV=∫S​g(x)∂n​G(x,x′)dS.
(9)
Hence
ϕ(x′)=∫V​G(x,x′)f(x)dV+∫S​g(x)∂n​G(x,x′)dS.​
(10)
The choice of sign in the Green function equation matters: it fixes both the source normalization and these signs. For a bounded region this construction presumes that the chosen k2 is not a Dirichlet eigenvalue of −∇2; at such an eigenvalue a Green inverse for arbitrary data does not exist.
Infinite space. Put r=∣x−x′∣. Away from the source, spherical symmetry reduces the Helmholtz equation to
G′′+r2​G′+k2G=0,(rG)′′+k2(rG)=0.
(11)
For k=0 its general radial solution is
G(r)=rAcoskr+Bsinkr​.
(12)
Integrate LG=δ over a ball of radius ε about the source. The divergence theorem yields
4πε2G′(ε)+k2∫r<ε​GdV=1.
(13)
Since G=A/r+Bk+O(r), the volume term is O(ε2) and the flux tends to −4πA. Therefore A=−1/(4π), while B remains free:
G(r)=−4πrcoskr​+Brsinkr​,r>0.​
(14)
The B term extends regularly through the source and solves the homogeneous Helmholtz equation, so it cannot change the unit Dirac delta normalization. A condition at infinity is needed to select it. For example, with time dependence e−iωt, the outgoing Green function for the three-dimensional Helmholtz equation is −eikr/(4πr), corresponding to B=−i/(4π).
Zero wavenumber. For fixed finite B, sin(kr)/r→0 as k→0, and
G(r)⟶−4πr1​,∇2G=δ.​
(15)
This is the decaying Green function of the Laplacian. Directly at k=0, the general radial solution is A/r+C; source normalization fixes A=−1/(4π), and decay at infinity removes C. If the free parameter depends on k as B(k)∼C/k, the limit instead includes that arbitrary homogeneous constant. Thus the usual Laplace limit holds for a fixed parameter, or after a suitable condition at infinity has been selected.
