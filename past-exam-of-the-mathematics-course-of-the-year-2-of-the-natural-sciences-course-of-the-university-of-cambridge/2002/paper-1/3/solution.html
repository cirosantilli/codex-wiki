Upper half-plane solution. Take the Fourier transform in x only, writing Φ(k,y)=ϕ​(k,y). Laplace's equation becomes Φyy​−k2Φ=0. For k=0, discard the exponentially growing solution and use Φ(k,0)=1, the Fourier transform of the Dirac delta function. This gives Φ(k,y)=e−∣k∣y. The value at k=0 can be set to its continuous limit one. Pointwise decay of ϕ as y→∞ does not require this individual transformed value to decay: the total integral in x remains one while the profile spreads.
The inverse Fourier transform now yields, for y>0,
ϕ(x,y)=2π1​∫−∞∞​e−∣k∣yeikxdk=π1​∫0∞​e−kycos(kx)dk.

This is the Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane. Its boundary condition is a distributional convergence, rather than an ordinary value at (0,0): for a smooth compactly supported test function g,
∫−∞∞​ϕ(x,y)g(x)dx=π1​∫−∞∞​1+t2g(yt)​dt⟶g(0).

The dominated convergence theorem applies because g is bounded and (1+t2)−1 is integrable. Also 0≤ϕ(x,y)≤1/(πy), proving the required decay as y→∞.
