For Cartesian tensors, let R be the orthogonal matrix taking old vector components to new ones, vi′​=Rij​vj​. A rank-n tensor transforms by one copy of R on each index:
Ti1​⋯in​′​=Ri1​j1​​⋯Rin​jn​​Tj1​⋯jn​​.​
(41)
Repeated indices are summed according to Einstein notation. In general coordinates a contravariant tensor index instead carries the corresponding Jacobian matrix ∂x′i/∂xj, and a covariant tensor index carries its inverse; in orthonormal bases of Cartesian axes their component laws coincide with the displayed form.
An isotropic tensor has identical components after every proper orthogonal transformation, so T′=T for every R with RTR=I and detR=1. Requiring reflection invariance as well is a stronger convention for odd tensor ranks. The Kronecker delta is invariant under all orthogonal transformations, while the Levi-Civita symbol is invariant under proper rotations and acquires a determinant sign under reflections.

A normal mode is a fixed displacement pattern whose components share a common harmonic time dependence in the equations for small oscillations. Its normal frequency is the associated oscillation frequency; below, ω denotes the angular frequency, so the frequency in cycles per unit time is ω/(2π). A normal coordinate is the amplitude of one such pattern, chosen so that the linear equations decouple. For mass matrix M and stiffness matrix K, the patterns solve Kv=ω2Mv. Zero eigenvalues describe free rigid motions rather than periodic restoring oscillations.

The matrix group. Work throughout in the finite field F5​, so every nonzero entry has a multiplicative inverse. Denote a matrix by A(a,b,c), with a,c=0. Ordinary matrix multiplication, interpreted using modular arithmetic, gives
A(a,b,c)A(d,e,f)=A(ad,ae+bf,cf).

Every element belongs to its own conjugacy class, by taking g=e. To prove uniqueness, suppose x=gag−1=hbh−1 belongs to both Cl(a) and Cl(b). Then
b=(h−1g)a(h−1g)−1.
(67)
For every t∈G, this makes tbt−1 a conjugate of a as well. Hence Cl(b)⊆Cl(a), and reversing the argument gives the other inclusion. Two conjugacy classes that meet are therefore equal, proving that the classes partition G.
Abelian case. If G is an abelian group, then gag−1=agg−1=a for every g, so Cl(a)={a}​ for each a.
Centre. An element has a singleton conjugacy class precisely when gzg−1=z for every g, equivalently gz=zg for every g. Thus the definition in the paper agrees with the usual centre of a group:
Z(G)={z∈G:gz=zg for every g∈G}.
(68)
The identity lies in Z(G). If z,w∈Z(G), then for any g, gzw=zgw=zwg, so zw∈Z(G). Also gz=zg implies z−1g=gz−1, so z−1∈Z(G). This proves it is a subgroup. Any two of its elements commute because either one commutes with every element of G. Consequently Z(G) is an abelian subgroup of G​.
Square symmetry. Use the paper's notation D4​ for the eight-element dihedral group of a square. Let r be a quarter-turn and s a reflection, so r4=s2=e and srs=r−1. (69)
and rj−1=rj+1. Therefore

Definitions and labels. A complex group representation on a vector space V is a group homomorphism ρ:G→GL(V), so ρ(gh)=ρ(g)ρ(h) and ρ(e)=I. It is an irreducible representation if V=0 and its only invariant subspaces are 0 and V.
Read permutation matrices by their action on column basis vectors. The labels in the original PDF are

Multiplication and conjugacy. Matrix products act on columns with the right factor first. For example A2=B, AD=C, and DA=E. The complete multiplication table, with row factor multiplied by column factor, is

Dimensions. The sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions equals the group order, which here is six. A three-dimensional irreducible representation would already contribute nine, so the displayed representation is reducible. There are three inequivalent complex irreducible representations, one for each conjugacy class, including the trivial representation of dimension one. If their dimensions are 1,d2​,d3​, then d22​+d32​=5, whose positive integer solution is {d2​,d3​}={1,2}. Hence
irreducible dimensions: 1,1,2.​
