Choose an orthonormal basis with n=e3​. A half-turn about n kills all mixed axial-transverse components. Commutation with a quarter-turn makes the transverse block a scalar identity matrix plus an antisymmetric matrix. The resulting oriented-axis invariant Cartesian tensor, in coordinate-free form, is
σij​=a(δij​−ni​nj​)+dni​nj​+hϵikj​nk​,​
(59)
with real constants a,d,h. The Kronecker delta, the outer product ni​nj​, and the displayed Levi-Civita symbol contraction are invariant under every proper rotation about n, so this form is also sufficient. In the chosen basis,
σ=​ah0​−ha0​00d​​.
(60)
The antisymmetric part must be retained unless further symmetry is imposed.
Decompose the electric field as E=E⊥​+E∥​, where E∥​=(n⋅E)n. The electric current density is
J=aE⊥​+dE∥​+hn×E⊥​.
(61)
For a nonzero real electric field, the alignment of current and field in an axisymmetric conductor gives the complete classification of collinearity:
  • If E⊥​=0, then J=dE​, with no additional symmetry required.
  • If E∥​=0, then J∥E ⟺ h=0​. Within this family h=0 is exactly the condition that the conductivity tensor is symmetric; then J=aE. An additional half-turn symmetry about a transverse axis is one way to force h=0.
  • If both components are nonzero, then J∥E ⟺ h=0 and a=d​. These conditions give the isotropic second-rank tensor σ=aI, and then J=aE for every direction.
For completeness, if E⊥​=0 and J=λE, taking the dot product with n×E⊥​ forces h=0. The transverse equation then gives λ=a, while a nonzero axial component gives λ=d. This proves there are no missing directions. Zero current is included as the zero scalar multiple; if “parallel” requires a direction for J, exclude cases where that scalar is zero. For a strictly anisotropic crystal the last possibility is absent.
