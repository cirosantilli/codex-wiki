The eight matrices ±I2​,±A,±B,±C are distinct. Therefore the group homomorphism is injective, and the representation is faithful; equivalently its kernel is {I}.
The character of a representation is the trace of its representing matrix. Consequently
χ(I)=2,χ(−I)=−2,χ(±i)=χ(±j)=χ(±k)=0.​

To see completeness, Lagrange's theorem allows only orders 1,2,4,8. The only element of order two is −I, which fixes the order-two subgroup. Every noncentral element has order four and generates the corresponding listed cyclic subgroup. Any order-four subgroup contains such an element, because there are only two central elements; it must equal the cyclic subgroup generated by that element. This accounts for all possibilities.
