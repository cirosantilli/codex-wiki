Taking the conjugate transpose term by term in the convergent matrix exponential gives
U†=eA†=e−A.
(38)
The matrices A and −A commute, so the matrix exponential product law gives U†U=e−AeA=I and likewise UU†=I. Hence U is unitary​.
For V, use the inverse and product rules for the conjugate transpose:
V†=[(I+A)−1]†(I−A)†=(I−A)−1(I+A).
(39)
The factors I±A commute, as do their inverses, since they are expressions in the same matrix. Consequently
V†V=(I−A)−1(I+A)(I−A)(I+A)−1=I,
(40)
and similarly VV†=I. Therefore V is unitary​. This rational construction is the skew-Hermitian form of the Cayley transform of a Hermitian matrix, obtained by writing A=iH with H a Hermitian matrix.
