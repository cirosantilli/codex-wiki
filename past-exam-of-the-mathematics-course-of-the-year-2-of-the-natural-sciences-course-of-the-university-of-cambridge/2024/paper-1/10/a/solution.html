Use the standard regular Sturm-Liouville problem hypotheses: positive weight w, real coefficients, and real self-adjoint boundary conditions. The positive weight and boundary-domain assumptions are implicit in the requested real orthonormal basis; real differential coefficients alone would not guarantee them for arbitrary complex boundary conditions.
Multiply the eigenfunction equation by yn​​ and integrate. By self-adjointness, the number ∫ab​yn​​Lyn​dx is real, whereas
∫ab​yn​​Lyn​dx=λn​∫ab​w∣yn​∣2dx.
(91)
The second integral is strictly positive, so λn​∈R​. Because the coefficients and boundary conditions are real, the real and imaginary parts of any complex eigenfunction separately obey the same equation and conditions. At least one is nonzero, so a real eigenfunction can be chosen. More generally, each eigenspace has a real basis, which can be orthonormalized using the weighted inner product.
The orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions, with normalization chosen to one, is
∫ab​w(x)ym​(x)​yn​(x)dx=δmn​.​

For the real choice, omit the complex conjugate.
