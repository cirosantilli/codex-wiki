A group homomorphism preserves multiplication, ϕ(ab)=ϕ(a)ϕ(b). A group isomorphism is a bijective homomorphism. An isomorphism from a group to itself is a group automorphism.
The automorphism group contains the identity map. Composition is associative, and if ϕ,ψ are automorphisms, (ϕ∘ψ)(ab)=ϕ(ψ(a)ψ(b))=(ϕ∘ψ)(a)(ϕ∘ψ)(b); composition is also bijective. For u=ϕ(a) and v=ϕ(b), ϕ−1(uv)=ϕ−1(ϕ(ab))=ab=ϕ−1(u)ϕ−1(v), proving the inverse bijection is a homomorphism. These facts establish all group axioms.
A finite cyclic group is G=⟨g⟩={e,g,…,gn−1} with g of order n. The map gk↦e2πik/n is well defined modulo n, bijective onto the roots of unity, and preserves multiplication. It identifies G with the multiplicative group of nth roots of unity.
For prime n, Lagrange's theorem leaves only subgroup orders one and n: there is no nontrivial proper subgroup. The trivial subgroup {e} is nevertheless proper when n>1, so that qualification is necessary in the printed claim. The characterization of groups with no proper nontrivial subgroups makes the distinction explicit.
For composite n, choose a prime divisor p and take H0​={e},H1​=⟨gp⟩​, of orders 1 and n/p. They are distinct proper subgroups, with H1​ nontrivial. If the question intended two nontrivial proper subgroups, that demand is not always possible: a cyclic group of order p2 has only the one of order p. The subgroups and quotients of a cyclic group are controlled by its divisors. The order-one group is a separate trivial case, with no proper subgroups.

A group representation is a homomorphism D:G→GL(V). It is a reducible representation if there is a nonzero proper invariant subspace of V preserved by every D(g), and an irreducible representation otherwise. With S invertible,
D(gh)=SD(g)D(h)S−1=(SD(g)S−1)(SD(h)S−1),
(23)
so changing basis gives another representation. Its character of a representation is χ(g)=trD(g), and cyclicity of the trace gives χ​(g)=tr(SD(g)S−1)=χ(g).
A conjugacy class consists of hgh−1 as h varies. Since (hgh−1)k=hgkh−1, a power equals the identity exactly when gk does. Thus conjugation preserves element order, including infinite order.
For the square, let r be rotation by π/2 and s reflection in a horizontal symmetry axis. The dihedral group has the eight elements
e,r,r2,r3,s,rs,r2s,r3s,r4=s2=e,srs=r−1.
(24)
The conjugacy classes of a finite dihedral group specialize to
{e},{r2},{r,r3},{s,r2s},{rs,r3s}.​
(25)
The two central rotations are singletons. Reflection conjugation reverses rotations, while conjugation by r shifts a reflection exponent by two, giving the two reflection classes.
For finite groups over C, character orthogonality and completeness make the irreducible characters a basis of the class functions. The latter space has one independent value per conjugacy class. Hence the square group has five inequivalent irreducible complex representations. The sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions is eight, forcing four one-dimensional representations and one two-dimensional representation. The four scalar choices r↦±1, s↦±1 supply the former; the ordinary action on the plane supplies the latter.
