A unitary matrix satisfies U†U=I over the complex numbers; a real orthogonal matrix satisfies QTQ=I. Their inverses are their conjugate transposes and transposes, respectively.
With d=−a, the coordinate e2​=(0,1,0) has eigenvalue c. In the x,z plane, the traceless symmetric two-by-two matrix has eigenvalues ±s, where s=a2+b2​. If s>0, choose θ with cos2θ=a/s and sin2θ=b/s. Then an orthonormal eigenbasis is
λ+​=s: v+​=(cosθ,0,sinθ),λ−​=−s: v−​=(−sinθ,0,cosθ),λ2​=c: e2​.​

This includes the degenerate cases that a division by b would miss.

There is also a global maximality proof for simple fences in the chosen half-plane, without assuming competitors are graphs. Complete the candidate minor arc with the complementary major arc of the same circle, which lies below the wall. Attach this same fixed major arc to any competing fence of length 2l.
