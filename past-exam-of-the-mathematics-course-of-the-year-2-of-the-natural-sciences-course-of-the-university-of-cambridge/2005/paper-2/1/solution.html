For a bounded region R with piecewise smooth boundary and a continuously differentiable vector field, the divergence theorem gives ∫R​∇⋅FdV=∫∂R​F⋅ndS, with outward unit normal n. Apply it to F=u∇v−v∇u. The cross terms cancel, giving the Green's second identity
∫BR​​[u(Δ−m2)v−v(Δ−m2)u]dV=∫∂BR​​(u∂n​v−v∂n​u)dS.
(1)
The m2uv terms cancel identically. Sending R→∞ proves the whole-space identity provided the boundary integral tends to zero. The boundary term at infinity in Green's second identity requires adequate derivative decay as well as field decay; mere pointwise convergence u,v→0 does not suffice. The screened kernels used below satisfy the required decay.
Take m>0. Apply the identity with u=ϕ and v=G(⋅,r), treating the source through its Dirac delta function, or excising a small source sphere and taking its radius to zero. It gives ϕ(r)−∫G(r′,r)ρ(r′)dV′=0. Symmetry of the radial Green function then yields the requested representation.
For separation s=∣r−r′∣>0, the modified Helmholtz equation reduces to (sG)′′−m2(sG)=0. Decay selects G=Ce−ms/s. Integrating the equation over a small sphere gives 4πs2G′→1, so C=−1/(4π). Thus
G(r,r′)=−4π∣r−r′∣e−m∣r−r′∣​,ϕ(r)=∫G(r,r′)ρ(r′)dV′.​
(2)
For ρ=e−βr/r with β>0, put h=rϕ. Then h′′−m2h=e−βr. When β=m, decay removes emr, while h(0)=0 removes an additional point source at the origin. Hence
ϕ(r)=r(β2−m2)e−βr−e−mr​.​
(3)
Since G has a minus sign, the positive Yukawa kernel convolution is
4π1​∫R3​∣r−r′∣e−m∣r−r′∣​r′e−βr′​dV′=−r(β2−m2)e−βr−e−mr​.​
(4)
The coincident-rate limit β=m is e−mr/(2m) for the convolution, and the origin limit for general rates is 1/(m+β). Both are finite; the apparent denominators in the distinct-rate formula are removable limits.
