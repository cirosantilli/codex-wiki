If a permutation has decompositions into r and s transpositions, they act as (−1)rf and (−1)sf. This polynomial is nonzero, for example at pairwise distinct real inputs, so the signs must agree. Thus all decompositions have the same parity, and an even decomposition forces every decomposition to be even. This defines the sign of a permutation; its kernel is the alternating group, linking the parity argument to the normal-kernel result above.
