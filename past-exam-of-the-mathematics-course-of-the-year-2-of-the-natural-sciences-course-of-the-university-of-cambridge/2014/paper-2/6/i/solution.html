For a constant orthogonal Cartesian change of coordinates xi′​=Qij​xj​, the tensor component transformation law for order n is
Ti1​⋯in​′​=Qi1​j1​​⋯Qin​jn​​Tj1​⋯jn​​.​
(43)
Repeated indices are summed. In Cartesian orthonormal coordinates the metric identifies upper and lower indices, so the same orthogonal matrix acts on every index.
An isotropic tensor has the same numerical components after every proper orthogonal change of axes: Q⊗nT=T for every Q with QTQ=I and detQ=1. For example, δij​ is isotropic because Qik​Qjk​=δij​. Some conventions require invariance under all orthogonal transformations, including reflections; this stronger convention must be distinguished for odd-order tensors, notably the Levi-Civita symbol, which is invariant under proper rotations but changes sign under its ordinary three-index transformation for a reflection.
