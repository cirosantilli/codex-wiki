Conversely, multiplication of an eigenfunction equation by ψ and integration by parts gives N=λD, hence Λ[ψ]=λ​. General Robin data require their boundary-energy contributions unless these endpoint products happen to vanish.
Use an eigenbasis orthonormal with respect to the weight: ∫ab​wψi∗​ψj​dx=δij​. For complex coefficients use the Hermitian version of the quotient, with absolute squares. Self-adjointness gives
Λ[ψ​]=∑i≥0​∣ci​∣2∑i≥0​∣ci​∣2λi​​≥λ0​.​
(71)
When c0​=0, dividing numerator and denominator by ∣c0​∣2 yields
λ=1+∑i≥1​∣ai​∣2λ0​+∑i≥1​∣ai​∣2λi​​,ai​=ci​/c0​.​
(72)
If c0​=0, these ratios are undefined, but the unscaled formula and upper-bound conclusion remain valid. The assumptions are a problem with a self-adjoint operator bounded below and a complete eigenbasis, with the trial function in its energy-form domain. The Rayleigh-Ritz method minimizes this quotient over an admissible trial family, obtaining an upper bound on λ0​. Higher eigenfunctions are stationary directions and need not be unrestricted extrema.
