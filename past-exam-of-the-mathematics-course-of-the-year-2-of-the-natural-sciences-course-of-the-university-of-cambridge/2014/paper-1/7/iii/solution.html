The logarithmic singularity at a lies inside the excluded disk, since d−a<R. The other singularity, at −a, lies outside the right half-plane. Thus the real logarithmic modulus is harmonic throughout the required region and has exactly the two specified boundary values.
Local branches of the complex logarithm justify harmonicity; its real part is globally single-valued even though the complex logarithm need not be single-valued around the excluded disk. The displayed solution is bounded and tends to zero at infinity. A boundedness or corresponding far-field condition supplies uniqueness in this unbounded domain; finite boundary values alone do not rule out unbounded additional harmonic solutions. The same geometry appears in cylinder-plane image charges.
