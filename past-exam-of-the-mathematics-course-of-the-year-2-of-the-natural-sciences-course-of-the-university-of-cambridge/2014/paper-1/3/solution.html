Kernel conditions. The clamped-beam Green function must obey G(0,ξ)=Gx​(0,ξ)=G(1,ξ)=Gx​(1,ξ)=0. Away from x=ξ, Gxxxx​=0. At the load point, G,Gx​,Gxx​ are continuous and
Gxxx​(ξ+,ξ)−Gxxx​(ξ−,ξ)=1.​
(14)
A jump in any lower derivative would create a derivative of the Dirac delta, rather than the single point load required by Dx4​G=δ(x−ξ).
Construction. A particularly simple representation is
G(x,ξ)=ax2+bx3+6(x−ξ)+3​​.
(15)
It already enforces the left-end conditions and the correct source jump. The right-end conditions give a+b=−(1−ξ)3/6 and 2a+3b=−(1−ξ)2/2, hence
a=2ξ(1−ξ)2​,b=−6(1−ξ)2(1+2ξ)​.
(16)
For x<ξ this is already factored; for x>ξ, expanding and refactoring gives
G(x,ξ)=⎩⎨⎧​6x2(1−ξ)2​[3ξ−(1+2ξ)x],6ξ2(1−x)2​[3x−(1+2x)ξ],​x≤ξ,x≥ξ.​​
(17)
The formulas agree at the junction. The forced displacement is obtained by integrating this kernel against f; for continuous loads it satisfies the equation and all four end conditions. Uniqueness follows because a cubic with zero value and slope at both ends is identically zero.
Reciprocity. In terms of x<​=min(x,ξ) and x>​=max(x,ξ), the same expression is
G(x,ξ)=6x<2​(1−x>​)2​[3x>​−(1+2x>​)x<​].
(18)
It is visibly symmetric under interchange of x and ξ. Thus the reciprocity of the clamped-beam Green function gives
y1​(x2​)=G(x2​,x1​)=G(x1​,x2​)=y2​(x1​).​
(19)
Equivalently, two integrations by parts show self-adjointness of Dx4​ under the clamped conditions, with bilinear form ∫u′′v′′dx.
