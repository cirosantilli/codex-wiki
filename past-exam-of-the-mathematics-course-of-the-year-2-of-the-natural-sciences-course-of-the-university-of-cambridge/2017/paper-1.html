A causal Green function vanishes before the impulse. For t>τ it solves the homogeneous linear ordinary differential equation, whose characteristic roots are −q±2i. Thus it has the form
G(t,τ)=e−q(t−τ)[Acos2(t−τ)+Bsin2(t−τ)].
(14)
Continuity at t=τ is necessary to avoid a delta derivative in Gtt​, so A=0. Integrating its forced equation across the impulse gives the jump condition for an impulse, Gt​(τ+,τ)−Gt​(τ−,τ)=1. The derivative from below is zero and the derivative from above is 2B, giving
G(t,τ)=21​Θ(t−τ)e−q(t−τ)sin[2(t−τ)].​
(15)
Here Θ is the Heaviside step function. The zero initial data exclude any additional homogeneous response. By the superposition principle, the output for a general locally integrable input is
x(t)=∫0t​G(t,τ)f(τ)dτ=21​∫0t​e−q(t−τ)sin[2(t−τ)]f(τ)dτ.​
(16)
The upper endpoint causes no unwanted term in the first derivative because G(t,t)=0; in the second derivative its unit derivative jump reproduces the input.

For an integrable applied voltage, the convolution theorem gives
I(ω)=[(γ1​+iω)2+p12​a1​p1​​+(γ2​+iω)2+p22​a2​p2​​]V(ω).
(26)
The integrated electric current is the zero-frequency transform, Q=I(0). Since V(0) can be arbitrary, zero total electric charge for every such input is equivalent to
γ12​+p12​a1​p1​​+γ22​+p22​a2​p2​​=0.​
(27)
For nonzero p1​,p2​, this can be written
a2​a1​​=−p1​(γ22​+p22​)p2​(γ12​+p12​)​.​
(28)
Because both amplitudes are positive, nonzero p1​,p2​ must have opposite signs. This is zero-frequency cancellation of a causal response kernel. The phrase arbitrary voltage is understood within a class for which the net-charge integrals exist; a permanent DC input need not have finite total charge.

An n×n matrix is diagonalizable over a chosen field exactly when it has a basis of n linearly independent eigenvectors. Equivalently, P−1AP is diagonal for some invertible P. Distinct eigenvalues suffice but are not necessary. For example,
J=(10​11​)
(29)
is not diagonalizable: its only eigenvalue is 1 and its eigenspace is the one-dimensional span of (1,0)T. It is a nontrivial Jordan block.
A Hermitian matrix satisfies A†=A, where the dagger denotes conjugate transpose. If Av=λv with v=0, then
λ=v†vv†Av​,(v†Av)∗=v†A†v=v†Av.
(30)
The denominator is positive real, so
λ∈R.​
(31)
For Av=λv and Aw=μw, Hermiticity also gives
μv†w=v†Aw=(Av)†w=λv†w.
(32)
Thus
λ=μ⟹v†w=0.​
(33)
This is the necessary qualification to the PDF's orthogonality wording: arbitrary eigenvectors belonging to a repeated eigenvalue need not be orthogonal. Within each repeated eigenspace, Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization produces an orthonormal basis; together the eigenspaces give an orthonormal eigenbasis by the finite-dimensional spectral theorem. For example, (1,0)T and (1,1)T are nonorthogonal eigenvectors of the identity matrix.

Suppose first that AB=BA, and take an eigenvector vj​ of A, with Avj​=aj​vj​. Commutation gives
A(Bvj​)=BAvj​=aj​Bvj​.

Since the eigenvalue aj​ is simple, its eigenspace is one-dimensional, so Bvj​=bj​vj​ for some scalar bj​. Thus every vector in an eigenbasis of A is also an eigenvector of B. (45)
