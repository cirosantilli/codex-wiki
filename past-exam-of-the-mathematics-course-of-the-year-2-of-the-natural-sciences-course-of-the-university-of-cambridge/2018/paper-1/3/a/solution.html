For x=ξ, the homogeneous solutions are ex,e−x. The left boundary condition selects sinhx, while boundedness at infinity selects e−x on the right. Thus write G=A(ξ)sinhx for x<ξ and G=B(ξ)e−x for x>ξ.
A unit Dirac delta distribution in the second derivative requires continuity of G and a derivative jump of one:
Asinhξ=Be−ξ,−Be−ξ−Acoshξ=1.
(19)

G(x,ξ)=−sinh(min{x,ξ})e−max{x,ξ}=−21​(e−∣x−ξ∣−e−(x+ξ))​.
(20)
The second expression is also a method of images construction. Applying the Green's function to the forcing gives
y(x)=−e−x∫0x​sinhξf(ξ)dξ−sinhx∫x∞​e−ξf(ξ)dξ​.

For bounded forcing this is bounded, vanishes at zero and solves the equation; a bounded homogeneous solution vanishing at zero is zero, proving uniqueness. More general forcing is allowed when the integrals converge and the resulting function has the required growth. Arbitrary unbounded forcing need not admit a bounded solution; this distinction is relevant in part (b).
