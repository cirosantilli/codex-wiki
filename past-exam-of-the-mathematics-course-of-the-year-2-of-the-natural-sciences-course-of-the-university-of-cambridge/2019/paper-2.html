An order-n Cartesian tensor has 3n components whose values in two orthonormal Cartesian frames satisfy
Ti1​…in​′​=Ri1​j1​​⋯Rin​jn​​Tj1​…jn​​,RTR=I,
(56)
with repeated indices summed as in Einstein notation. It is an isotropic tensor when its components are unchanged by every proper rotation, R∈SO(3).
For rank two, a half-turn about any coordinate axis changes the sign of each component having exactly one index along that axis. Invariance therefore makes every off-diagonal component zero. Quarter-turns interchanging two coordinate axes then require T11​=T22​=T33​. Thus, without assuming symmetry in advance,
Tij​=aδij​​.
(57)
Conversely, the Kronecker delta is invariant because Rik​Rjk​=δij​, so every tensor of this form is isotropic.
For rank three the general proper-rotation invariant form is
Tijk​=bϵijk​​,
(58)
where ϵijk​ is the Levi-Civita symbol. The convention matters: the isotropic rank-three tensor and reflections distinction says that invariance of a polar tensor under all R∈O(3) would force b=0. A nonzero ϵijk​ is invariant under proper rotations, and is a pseudotensor under the full orthogonal group. The shell integrals below are invariant under reflections as well, so their odd moments vanish.

A group is a set G equipped with a binary operation satisfying the group axioms: closure; associativity, (gh)k=g(hk); an identity element e with eg=ge=g; and an inverse element g−1 for every g, with gg−1=g−1g=e.
A subgroup H is a subset that is a group under the restricted operation. Equivalently, it is nonempty and contains xy−1 whenever x,y∈H. A generating set of a group is a set whose elements and inverses can be multiplied in finite words to produce every group element.
The group order is its number of elements, ∣G∣. The order of a group element g is the least positive integer r with gr=e, or infinity if there is none. Lagrange's theorem states that for a finite group and subgroup H, ∣H∣ divides ∣G∣ and ∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣. In particular, every finite element order divides the group order.
A cyclic group is generated by one element, so every element is a power of that generator. A group isomorphism is a bijective group homomorphism: a bijection ϕ:G→G′ satisfying ϕ(gh)=ϕ(g)ϕ(h) for all g,h.
