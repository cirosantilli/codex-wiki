For a separated axisymmetric harmonic function Ψ=R(r)Θ(θ), multiplying the Laplace equation by r2/(RΘ) gives
R(r2R′)′​=−sinθΘ(sinθΘ′)′​=λ.
(13)
The two sides depend on different variables, hence both equal a constant. Put u=cosθ in the angular equation. Since sinθΘ′=−sin2θdΘ/du, it becomes the Legendre differential equation
dud​[(1−u2)dudΘ​]+λΘ=0.
(14)
Regularity on both parts of the polar axis restricts λ=ℓ(ℓ+1), with ℓ=0,1,…, and selects the Legendre polynomial Pℓ​(u). The other angular solution is singular at an endpoint.
The radial Euler differential equation is
r2R′′+2rR′−ℓ(ℓ+1)R=0.
(15)
Trying R=rs gives s(s+1)=ℓ(ℓ+1), so s=ℓ or s=−ℓ−1. Superposition of these separated harmonic functions gives
Ψ(r,θ)=ℓ=0∑∞​(Aℓ​rℓ+Bℓ​r−ℓ−1)Pℓ​(cosθ)​.
(16)
The Legendre polynomials form the angular expansion for regular data with rotational symmetry about the polar axis; the radial domain and the boundary conditions determine which coefficients survive and where the expansion converges.
