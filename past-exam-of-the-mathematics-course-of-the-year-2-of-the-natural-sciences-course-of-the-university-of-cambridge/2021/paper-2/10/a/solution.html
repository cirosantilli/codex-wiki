Work over the complex numbers, the usual scalar field for the finite-group character theory used below. A group representation on a nonzero vector space V is a homomorphism D:G→GL(V), so D(gh)=D(g)D(h) and D(e)=I.
A faithful representation is injective: distinct group elements have distinct representing maps. Equivalently, its kernel is just {e}.
An irreducible representation has no nonzero proper invariant subspace: if W⊆V and D(g)W⊆W for every g∈G, then W={0} or W=V. Irreducibility concerns invariant subspaces, whereas faithfulness concerns whether any group element is represented by the identity.
