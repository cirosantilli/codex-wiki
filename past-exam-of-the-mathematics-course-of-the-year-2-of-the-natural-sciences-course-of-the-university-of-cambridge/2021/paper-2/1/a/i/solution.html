Two integrations by parts give the boundary form of the Sturm-Liouville operator:
⟨f,Lg⟩−⟨Lf,g⟩=[p(f′∗g−f∗g′)]ab​.
(1)
The interior terms cancel because p,q are real. Thus the required endpoint condition is
[p(f′∗g−f∗g′)]ab​=0for every f,g in the operator domain.​
(2)
For a regular interval, a standard self-adjoint domain is specified by one real homogeneous Robin boundary condition at each endpoint:
Aa​y(a)+Ba​p(a)y′(a)=0,Ab​y(b)+Bb​p(b)y′(b)=0,
(3)
where neither pair of real constants is identically zero. At each end, these conditions force the two boundary data vectors of f,g to be proportional, making the displayed form zero. Dirichlet boundary conditions and Neumann boundary conditions are the cases B=0 and A=0. Coupled conditions also work, for example y(a)=y(b) and p(a)y′(a)=p(b)y′(b), whose two endpoint contributions cancel. Self-adjointness uses the maximal common domain with these conditions, not an arbitrary smaller symmetric restriction.
The weighted inner product must be nondegenerate. The usual interpretation is w>0 almost everywhere, allowing isolated endpoint zeros as in part (b), with the operator defined on its appropriate weighted domain. Merely allowing a weight that is zero on an entire interval would not by itself define the stated operator or a positive-definite inner product on ordinary functions.
