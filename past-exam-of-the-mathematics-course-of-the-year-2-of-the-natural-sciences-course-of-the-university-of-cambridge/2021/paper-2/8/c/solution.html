Direct multiplication gives T2=P2=I, TP=PT=−I, and
TB(x)T=PB(x)P=B(−x).
(76)
Thus conjugation by each generator of G′ preserves G; so does conjugation by every product of the generators. Therefore G⊴G′​.
Using TB(x)=B(−x)T and its analogue for P, every group word can be reduced to B(x)S with S∈{I,T,P,TP}. Consequently the quotient has at most four cosets. They are distinct: a nonidentity diagonal matrix among T,P,−I cannot equal B(x), since a diagonal B(x) requires sinhx=0 and hence B(x)=I. Therefore
G′/G={G,TG,PG,TPG}.
(77)
The three nonidentity cosets all square to G, and TG and PG commute because T,P commute. Hence
G′/G≅C2​×C2​,the Klein four-group.​
(78)
Equivalently, G′ is the full real O(1,1) group and G its identity component. This is the component quotient of the real Lorentz group in two dimensions; the quotient records the four components, not the continuous boost parameter.
