Use the standard regular self-adjoint assumptions on the Sturm-Liouville problem, including positive weight w. The normalized eigenfunctions obey orthogonality
∫ab​w(x)ym​(x)∗yn​(x)dx=δmn​.
(1)

w(ξ)n=1∑∞​yn​(ξ)∗yn​(x)=δ(x−ξ).​
(2)
If zero is not an eigenvalue, divide each term by λn​. Since Lyn​=λn​yn​, application of L recovers the displayed delta expansion, proving
G(x;ξ)=w(ξ)n=1∑∞​λn​yn​(x)yn​(ξ)∗​.​
(3)
The factor w(ξ) is required because the source equation uses an ordinary, unweighted delta. It distinguishes the normalized Sturm-Liouville spectral Green kernel from a kernel integrated against weighted source measure. If there is a zero eigenvalue, an inverse for arbitrary forcing does not exist; the unqualified division by that eigenvalue is invalid.
For L=−d2/dx2+ω2 with real ω and zero endpoints on [0,1], the weight is one, yn​=2​sin(nπx) and λn​=n2π2+ω2>0. Substitution yields

The series is absolutely and uniformly convergent, although applying the differential operator is understood distributionally at its source point.

Set Φ=R(r)P(θ) and multiply the Laplace equation by r2/(RP). Separation of variables gives

For 0<a<b, the outer data have expansion b2cos2θ=b2/3+(2b2/3)P2​(cosθ), while the inner data are pure P0​. Thus only degrees zero and two are needed. A radial interpolation matching each pair of boundary values gives

Let the source be x0​=(a,b,c) with all three coordinates positive. Reflect it independently in the three coordinate planes: the eight points are xϵ​=(ϵ1​a,ϵ2​b,ϵ3​c), with ϵj​∈{1,−1}. Assign strength sϵ​=ϵ1​ϵ2​ϵ3​. Then
G(x,x0​)=−4π1​ϵ∈{±1}3∑​∣x−xϵ​∣ϵ1​ϵ2​ϵ3​​,N=7.​
(10)
The all-positive point is the original source. On any boundary plane, two terms differing only in that plane's sign have equal distances and opposite strengths, so cancel. All seven additional points lie outside the positive octant, leaving exactly the required interior source. This is the octant Dirichlet Green function.

Take the source at the origin and write ρ2=y2+z2. Repeated reflection in x=±1 places the sources at (2n,0,0) with strengths (−1)n, n∈Z. There are countably infinitely many images; n=0 is the original source. A convergent prescription groups adjacent opposite charges:
G(x,y,z;0)=−4π1​m∈Z∑​[(x−4m)2+ρ2​1​−(x−4m−2)2+ρ2​1​].​
(13)
Each bracket is O(∣m∣−2) on compact sets away from the source, so the paired series converges absolutely. The individual unsigned point-charge terms would not be absolutely summable. On x=1, reflection pairs the index n with 1−n; on x=−1, it pairs n with −1−n. Their distances agree and signs oppose. Symmetric finite cutoffs leave only a vanishing far-tail contribution, proving both zero boundary values. All nonzero-index sources lie outside the slab. This is the centered-source version of the Dirichlet Green function between parallel planes, selected to decay at large transverse distance.

Cauchy's integral theorem states that a holomorphic function on a simply connected domain has zero contour integral around every closed piecewise smooth curve in that domain. One proof begins with the Cauchy theorem for a triangle. Subdivide a triangle into four equal triangles. Their oriented boundary integrals sum to the original integral because internal edges cancel, so choose a nested subtriangle carrying at least one quarter of the preceding integral's modulus. At stage n, its perimeter and diameter are P/2n,d/2n, and the nested triangles converge to a point z0​. Complex differentiability gives f(z)=f(z0​)+f′(z0​)(z−z0​)+ϵ(z)(z−z0​), with ϵ(z)→0. The affine terms have primitives and integrate to zero. Therefore ∣In​∣≤sup∣ϵ∣Pd/4n, while ∣I0​∣≤4n∣In​∣. Letting n→∞ proves I0​=0. Triangulation cancels internal edges for polygonal contours; local primitives and deformation through the domain extend the result to the stated piecewise smooth curves. Simple connectedness ensures the necessary contractions do not pass through a missing point.
Apply the theorem to e−az2 on the rectangle between heights zero and ρ, with vertical sides at Rez=±R. On either vertical side,
∣e−az2∣=e−aR2+a(Imz)2≤e−aR2+aρ2.
(14)
The modulus of each side integral is at most ∣ρ∣e−aR2+aρ2→0. Both horizontal integrals converge absolutely. The two horizontal orientations are opposite, so the limit gives I(ρ,a)=I(0,a), also for negative ρ. This is Gaussian contour translation.
Now complete the square:
−ax2+iνx=−a(x−2aiν​)2−4aν2​.
(15)
The substitution z=x−iν/(2a) moves the path to height −ν/(2a). Its Gaussian integral equals the real-axis integral by the preceding result. Hence
∫−∞∞​e−ax2+iνxdx=aπ​​e−ν2/(4a),a>0, ν∈R.​
(16)
This also gives the Fourier transform of a Gaussian in the convention with no prefactor in the forward transform.

An isotropic tensor has unchanged components under every proper rotation of the Cartesian axes. Every rank-zero scalar is isotropic, and cδij​ is a rank-two example. The zero rank-one tensor is isotropic, but no nonzero vector is: a half-turn about an axis perpendicular to it reverses its sign.
For the inertia tensor, write u=x−G and X=P−G, so x−P=u−X. Expand its defining integrand. Every cross term has an integral containing ∫B​uρd3x=0 by the definition of the centre of mass. The remaining terms give the tensor parallel-axis theorem:
Iij​(P)−Iij​(G)=M(∣X∣2δij​−Xi​Xj​).​
(21)
For a uniform unit ball, rotational symmetry gives ∫ρui​uj​d3u=cδij​. Its trace is ∫ρ∣u∣2d3u=3M/5, so c=M/5 and I(G)=2MI/5. Therefore
I(P)=M[(52​+∣X∣2)I−XXT].​
(22)
Assuming positive mass, it is isotropic exactly when P=G. For X=0, its eigenvalues and directions are
λ∥​=52M​,e∥​=X/∣X∣;λ⊥​=M(52​+∣X∣2)on X⊥.​
(23)
To give an explicit orthonormal eigenbasis when X12​+X22​>0, take e1​=(−X2​,X1​,0)/X12​+X22​​ and e2​=e∥​×e1​. If X is a nonzero vector along the third axis, take the first two coordinate unit vectors instead. At X=0, all three eigenvalues are 2M/5 and every orthonormal basis works. The inertia tensor of a displaced uniform ball describes the same result for an arbitrary radius.

Measure the three coordinates as displacements from a configuration with both springs unstretched. The kinetic energy is 21​x˙12​+21​Mx˙22​+21​x˙32​, and the potential energy is 21​(x2​−x1​)2+21​(x3​−x2​)2. Expansion gives

For M>0, direct substitution yields the three normal modes:
translationantisymmetric outer motionsymmetric outer motion​v(1,1,1)T(1,0,−1)T(1,−2/M,1)T​ω011+2/M​​​.​
(26)
The three vectors are independent and orthogonal in the mass inner product vTTw, so they exhaust the motion. Their squared mass norms are M+2,2,2+4/M; division by the corresponding square roots gives normalized modes. This is the unit-spring, unit-outer-mass case of the normal modes of three masses connected by two equal springs.
As M→∞, the central displacement in the symmetric oscillatory mode tends to zero and both nonzero frequencies tend to one. The two limiting shapes (1,0,1) and (1,0,−1) span independent motions of the outer masses against an effectively fixed centre; each unit mass has its own unit spring frequency. The translational mode remains at zero frequency. Under a bounded kinetic-energy condition its common velocity tends to zero as M−1/2, while a static common displacement is still an equilibrium shift. One must specify that energy condition before concluding that an arbitrary freely translating heavy mass remains stationary.

A group homomorphism preserves multiplication: Φ(gh)=Φ(g)Φ(h). Its group kernel K={g:Φ(g)=eH​} is a subgroup, and Φ(gkg−1)=eH​ for k∈K. Thus gKg−1=K, proving it is a normal subgroup. The map gK↦Φ(g) is well-defined because two representatives of a coset differ by an element of K. It is a homomorphism, has trivial kernel, and is surjective when Φ is. This proves the first isomorphism theorem, G/K≅H. This proof does not use finiteness and therefore also applies below.
The identity matrix belongs to SL2​(C). The determinant is multiplicative, giving closure, and matrix multiplication is associative. The inverse of a determinant-one matrix A=(ac​bd​) is (d−c​−ba​), also of determinant one. Thus this special linear group is a group.
The fractional linear maps must be interpreted on the Riemann sphere, including infinity: a finite pole is sent to infinity and infinity is sent to a/c when c=0. As literal everywhere-defined maps of the complex plane they would not form the asserted group. Substitution into the two rational expressions gives fA​∘fB​=fAB​. Matrix identities and inverses therefore give the identity and inverse transformations, proving that the Möbius group is a group and that Φ:A↦fA​ is a surjective homomorphism.
For fA​ to be the identity, (az+b)/(cz+d)=z on all finite points where defined. Hence cz2+(d−a)z−b is the zero polynomial, so b=c=0, d=a, and a2=1 from the determinant. Therefore
kerΦ={I,−I},M≅SL2​(C)/{±I}=PSL2​(C).​
(27)
The kernel is central and hence normal. In particular Φ is not an isomorphism, since A and −A represent the same transformation. The quotient is the projective special linear group in this complex two-dimensional case.

For a subgroup H of a finite group G, distinct left cosets are disjoint and partition G. Each coset gH has exactly ∣H∣ elements because h↦gh is a bijection. Counting the partition proves Lagrange's theorem:
∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣.​
(28)
A cyclic group is generated by a single element. In a group of order four, an element of order four generates the whole group, giving C4​. Otherwise all three nonidentity elements have order two. Choose two of them a,b; cancellation makes ab=c the third. Since c−1=c, (ab)−1=b−1a−1=ba=ab. The remaining products then give the Klein four-group. This proves that there are precisely two groups of order four up to isomorphism, distinguished by whether an element of order four exists.
For the square, take r to be counterclockwise rotation by 90∘ and s reflection in the horizontal axis. They satisfy r4=s2=e and sr=r−1s. The dihedral group here is D4​ of order eight, with elements e,r,r2,r3,s,rs,r2s,r3s. Both generators are necessary: one generator would give a cyclic, hence abelian, group, while r and s do not commute. In the following Cayley table, the row acts after the column; the formula (rasb)(rcsd)=ra+(−1)bcsb+d, with exponents reduced modulo four and two, computes every entry.
err2r3srsr2sr3s​eerr2r3srsr2sr3s​rrr2r3er3ssrsr2s​r2r2r3err2sr3ssrs​r3r3err2rsr2sr3ss​ssrsr2sr3serr2r3​rsrsr2sr3ssr3err2​r2sr2sr3ssrsr2r3er​r3sr3ssrsr2srr2r3e​​.
(29)
The largest cyclic subgroup is ⟨r⟩={e,r,r2,r3}, of order four. Every reflection has order two and there is no element of order eight. The other subgroups of order four are
H0​={e,r2,s,r2s},H1​={e,r2,rs,r3s}.​
(30)
Both are Klein four-groups, generated by the commuting involutions r2 and one reflection. For completeness, any order-four subgroup containing r or r3 equals ⟨r⟩. Otherwise it contains a reflection f; the product of two distinct reflections is a nonidentity rotation, which must be r2 if odd powers of r are excluded. This forces the subgroup to be {e,r2,f,r2f}, giving exactly the two displayed choices. It is the square case of the classification of subgroups of a dihedral group.

The Klein four-group has multiplication table
1abc​11abc​aa1cb​bbc1a​ccba1​​.
(31)
Since it is abelian, every conjugacy class is a singleton: {1},{a},{b},{c}. An irreducible representation is a nonzero representation whose only invariant subspaces are zero and the whole representation space. For a finite group, the number of inequivalent complex irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes, so there are four here.
A one-dimensional representation sends the involutions a,b to signs, independently. All four choices are
χpq​(1)=1,χpq​(a)=(−1)p,χpq​(b)=(−1)q,χpq​(c)=(−1)p+q,p,q∈{0,1}.
(32)
They are distinct irreducible representations, exhausting the four possibilities. The character table of the Klein four-group is
χ00​χ01​χ10​χ11​​11111​a11−1−1​b1−11−1​c1−1−11​​.​
(33)
The specified diagonal matrices obey D(a)2=D(b)2=I, commute, and satisfy D(a)D(b)=D(c), so the group relations prove that D is a group representation. Its coordinate axes are invariant lines carrying respectively χ01​,χ10​,χ11​. Therefore
D≅χ01​⊕χ10​⊕χ11​.​
(34)
The trivial representation χ00​ is absent. If H denotes the four-row character matrix above, direct row products give HHT=4I. This verifies character orthogonality:
41​∑g∈V​χpq​(g)​χrs​(g)=δpr​δqs​.
