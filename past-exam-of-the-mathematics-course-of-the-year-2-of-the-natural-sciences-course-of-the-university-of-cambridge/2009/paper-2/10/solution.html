The Klein four-group has multiplication table
1abc​11abc​aa1cb​bbc1a​ccba1​​.
(31)
Since it is abelian, every conjugacy class is a singleton: {1},{a},{b},{c}. An irreducible representation is a nonzero representation whose only invariant subspaces are zero and the whole representation space. For a finite group, the number of inequivalent complex irreducible representations equals the number of conjugacy classes, so there are four here.
A one-dimensional representation sends the involutions a,b to signs, independently. All four choices are
χpq​(1)=1,χpq​(a)=(−1)p,χpq​(b)=(−1)q,χpq​(c)=(−1)p+q,p,q∈{0,1}.
(32)
They are distinct irreducible representations, exhausting the four possibilities. The character table of the Klein four-group is
χ00​χ01​χ10​χ11​​11111​a11−1−1​b1−11−1​c1−1−11​​.​
(33)
The specified diagonal matrices obey D(a)2=D(b)2=I, commute, and satisfy D(a)D(b)=D(c), so the group relations prove that D is a group representation. Its coordinate axes are invariant lines carrying respectively χ01​,χ10​,χ11​. Therefore
D≅χ01​⊕χ10​⊕χ11​.​
(34)
The trivial representation χ00​ is absent. If H denotes the four-row character matrix above, direct row products give HHT=4I. This verifies character orthogonality:
41​∑g∈V​χpq​(g)​χrs​(g)=δpr​δqs​.

The character of D is (3,−1,−1,−1), whose inner products with the four rows are 0,1,1,1, independently confirming the decomposition. All these irreducibles are real as well: commuting real involutions can be simultaneously diagonalized into their real sign eigenspaces.
