Choose homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, y(a)=y(b)=0, and nonzero admissible functions with finite F and positive G. Variations obey the same zero endpoint values. At a stationary Rayleigh quotient, set λ=F[y]/G[y]. Then
0=GδΛ=δF−λδG=2[py′η]ab​+2∫ab​[−(py′)′+qy−λwy]ηdx.
(43)
The boundary term vanishes, and the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations gives the Sturm-Liouville equation −(py′)′+qy=λwy. Thus stationary functions are eigenfunctions. Conversely, multiply that equation by y and integrate by parts to obtain F=λG; its first variation then vanishes by the same calculation. This proves that stationary values are the corresponding eigenvalues. Homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions also make the displayed boundary terms vanish; general Robin boundary conditions can require a boundary contribution to the quadratic form.
For p=1, q=0, w=x on (0,π), the polynomial trial function y=x(π−x) satisfies the endpoint conditions. Its Rayleigh quotient uses
F=∫0π​(π−2x)2dx=3π3​,G=∫0π​x3(π−x)2dx=60π6​,
(44)
so the polynomial estimate is λpoly​=20/π3≃0.64503​. The trigonometric trial function y=sinx gives
F=2π​,G=∫0π​xsin2xdx=4π2​,
(45)
and hence λtrig​=2/π≃0.63662​. Both are upper bounds on the lowest eigenvalue, by the Rayleigh-Ritz method; neither trial is an exact eigenfunction, so the inequalities are strict.
Their closeness has a useful explanation beyond their similar single-arch shapes. Both trial functions are symmetric under x↦π−x, forcing ∫0π​xy2dx=(π/2)∫0π​y2dx. The reflection-symmetric trials for a linearly weighted Rayleigh quotient therefore reduce this problem to a constant multiple of the ordinary Dirichlet energy quotient. The sine is the best trial within this symmetric class, and the polynomial's value is only a factor 10/π2≃1.0132 larger. The unrestricted lowest eigenfunction can be asymmetric because the weight x is asymmetric.
