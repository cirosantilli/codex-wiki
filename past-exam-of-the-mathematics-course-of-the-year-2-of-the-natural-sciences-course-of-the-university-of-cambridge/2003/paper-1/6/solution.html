For the normalized second-order linear differential equation y′′+P(z)y′+Q(z)y=0, an ordinary point z0​ has analytic P,Q near z0​. At a regular singular point, which is not ordinary, (z−z0​)P(z) and (z−z0​)2Q(z) are analytic. Ordinary points admit two independent convergent power series solutions. Regular singular points instead call for the Frobenius method, which permits leading powers and, in some cases, logarithms.
Here zero is an ordinary point. Substitute y=∑n≥0​an​zn into the Hermite differential equation and equate coefficients:
(n+2)(n+1)an+2​+(λ−2n)an​=0,an+2​=(n+2)(n+1)2n−λ​an​.​
(26)
The even and odd initial coefficients are independent, giving
y=a0​[1−2λ​z2+24λ(λ−4)​z4+⋯]+a1​[z+62−λ​z3+120(2−λ)(6−λ)​z5+⋯].
(27)
These are the power series solutions of the Hermite differential equation, with the present parameter equal to twice the parameter in that article. The ratio of successive terms within each nonterminating parity is asymptotically 2∣z∣2/n, so both series have infinite radius of convergence.
For a nonzero polynomial of degree m, its highest coefficient in −2zy′+λy forces λ=2m. Conversely at that value the matching parity terminates. Hence
λ=2m,m=0,1,2,….​
(28)
The polynomial solutions are scalar multiples of the Hermite polynomials. Two examples are λ=0: y=1;λ=2: y=z​; direct substitution verifies both. The other independent solution generally remains nonpolynomial.
