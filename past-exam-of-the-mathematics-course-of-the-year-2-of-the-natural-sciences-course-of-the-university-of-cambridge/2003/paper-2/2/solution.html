Expand the divergence of Φ∇Ψ−Ψ∇Φ. The cross terms cancel, leaving ΦΔΨ−ΨΔΦ. The divergence theorem gives Green's second identity,
∫V​(ΦΔΨ−ΨΔΦ)dV=∫S​(Φ∂n​Ψ−Ψ∂n​Φ)dS.
(5)
Use the Green function convention Δx​G(x,x0​)=δ(x−x0​). Integrating over a bounded domain containing x0​ yields ∫S​∂n​GdS=1​. Thus zero Neumann boundary data would be incompatible with this unit Dirac delta source. A suitable Neumann Green function with constant boundary flux instead has ∂n​G=1/∣S∣, where ∣S∣ is the boundary area. Substituting Ψ=G, ΔΦ=σ, ∂n​Φ=f gives
Φ(x0​)=∫V​GσdV−∫S​GfdS+∣S∣1​∫S​ΦdS.​
(6)
The last term is an undetermined constant. The data must obey the Neumann Poisson problem compatibility condition ∫V​σdV=∫S​fdS, obtained by integrating the original equation. Under this condition an additive constant in G cancels from the first two terms.
For the upper half-space, reflect the source to x0∗​=(x0,1​,x0,2​,−x0,3​). The method of images with equal-sign sources gives
G(x,x0​)=−4π1​(∣x−x0​∣1​+∣x−x0∗​∣1​).​
(7)
The reflected singularity lies outside the domain; on z=0 the two normal derivatives cancel, so ∂n​G=−∂z​G=0. Its total unit flux passes through a large hemisphere at infinity, where G∼−1/(2πR). This is why the bounded-domain condition on the boundary flux cannot be applied to the infinite plane alone.
With zero interior source and the given point boundary source, the boundary representation is Φ(x0​)=−G((0,0,0),x0​). The terms at infinity vanish for this resulting 1/R field. Therefore
Φ(x,y,z)=2πx2+y2+z2​1​,z>0.​
(8)
To check the singular boundary condition, −∂z​Φ=z/[2π(x2+y2+z2)3/2] is the Poisson kernel for the upper half-space. Its integral over a horizontal plane is one and its mass concentrates at the origin as z↓0, giving ∂n​Φ=δ(x)δ(y) in the distributional sense. This is the Neumann point source on a half-space boundary.
