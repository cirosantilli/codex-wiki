Multiply the equation for yn​ by ym​ and the equation for ym​ by yn​, then subtract. The common h terms cancel, and integration by parts gives the Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator:
(λn​−λm​)∫ab​wyn​ym​dx=[g(yn​ym′​−ym​yn′​)]ab​.
(22)
At each endpoint the two pairs (yn′​,yn​) and (ym′​,ym​) satisfy the same nontrivial homogeneous linear equation. They therefore lie on the same one-dimensional line, and their determinant vanishes. The boundary expression is zero. Since the eigenvalues are distinct, division gives the required orthogonality of Sturm-Liouville eigenfunctions:
∫ab​w(x)yn​(x)ym​(x)dx=0.​
(23)
For complex-valued eigenfunctions of a real self-adjoint problem one may choose real eigenfunctions, or use the conjugated version as a weighted inner product.
