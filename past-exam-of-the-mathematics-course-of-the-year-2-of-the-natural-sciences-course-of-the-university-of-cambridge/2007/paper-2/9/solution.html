Write Bn​=(10​n1​). Direct matrix multiplication gives Bn​Bm​=Bn+m​, B0​=I and Bn−1​=B−n​. Consequently these matrices form the integer shear group, a subgroup of GL(2,R). It is an infinite cyclic group, generated by B1​, since B1n​=Bn​ and no nonzero integer power is the identity.
The map n↦Bn​ is a bijective group homomorphism from the additive group of integers, proving the required group isomorphism. There are proper subgroups: for example {B2n​:n∈Z} omits B1​. In fact every subgroup is {Bmn​:n∈Z} for an integer m≥0, by the classification of subgroups of an infinite cyclic group; m=0 gives the trivial subgroup and m=1 the whole group.
