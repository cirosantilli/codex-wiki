Write ΔV=V2​−V1​>0 and γi​=(1−vi2​)−1/2, the initial Lorentz factor. The conserved energy from part (a) is E=mγi​+V1​. To emerge on the higher plateau with positive speed, the particle needs E>m+V2​. Hence
γi​>1+mΔV​,vi​>vth​=1−(1+mΔV​)−2​.​
(93)
Above this threshold, conservation of energy gives γf​=γi​−ΔV/m>1. The transmitted velocity is positive, so
vf​=+1−[1−vi2​​1​−mΔV​]−2​,vi​>vth​.​
(94)
Below threshold there is a turning point within the steep transition, where x˙=0 and V=E−m<V2​. The particle returns to the lower plateau. Its final potential energy equals its initial value, so its final Lorentz factor equals γi​ but its direction is reversed:
vf​=−vi​,0<vi​<vth​.​
(95)
At exact threshold the limiting speed on the upper plateau is zero. This is the marginal case, not passage with positive outgoing speed. The ideal discontinuous potential does not specify the detailed trajectory at equality; a smooth transition can make the particle approach the top asymptotically. The final-speed formulas above cover the strictly transmitted and reflected regimes requested.
