Put w=z−1 and a=1−i. Partial fraction decomposition gives
(z−i)(z+3)1​=3+i1​(w+a1​−w+41​).
(73)
Both terms admit a geometric series at w=0. The local Laurent series is actually a Taylor series, since the center is regular:
(z−i)(z+3)1​=3+i1​m=0∑∞​(−1)m[(1−i)m+11​−4m+11​](z−1)m,∣z−1∣<2​.​

The nearest pole is z=i, a distance ∣1−i∣=2​ from the center; the other pole is at distance 4. Thus the requested local radius of convergence is R=2​​.
For completeness, specifying a Laurent series without a region can also mean either of the other maximal annuli. Expanding the first fraction in inverse powers and the second in positive powers gives
3+i1​[∑m=0∞​wm+1(−a)m​−∑m=0∞​4m+1(−1)mwm​],2​<∣w∣<4.
(75)
Expanding both fractions in inverse powers instead gives
3+i1​∑m=0∞​wm+1(−a)m−(−4)m​,∣w∣>4.
(76)
These are different Laurent series on different annuli, rather than extensions of the local Taylor series beyond its radius of convergence.
