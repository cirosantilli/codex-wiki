The disk still has only three degrees of freedom and hence three normal modes: two centre coordinates and one in-plane rotation. Five springs alter the restoring quadratic form but add no disk coordinates. The symmetric group S3​ is generated by transpositions, each of order two. A group homomorphism to Z3​ must map every transposition to zero, because the only target element annihilated by two is zero. Hence the map to Z3​ is necessarily trivial.
For S3​→Z2​, the 3-cycles map to zero because their order divides three. All transpositions are conjugate group elements, and a map to an abelian group takes conjugate elements to the same value. This representation is irreducible over the complex numbers: the two distinct rotation eigenspaces are exchanged by the reflection, so neither is invariant under the whole group. Its character is 2 on the identity, −1 on the two 3-cycles and 0 on the three transpositions. Squaring maps each transposition to the identity and permutes the 3-cycles. Its Frobenius-Schur indicator is therefore
ν=62+3(2)+2(−1)​=1.​
(46)
If “cyclic” were intended literally, no two-dimensional complex irreducible representation would exist, since an irreducible representation of a finite abelian group is one-dimensional.

The requested two-dimensional irreducible representation does not exist for the Vierergruppe. The Klein four-group V4​ is an abelian group in which every nonidentity element has order two. Its complex irreducible representations are all one-dimensional. The same holds over the real numbers here: the commuting representing involutions are simultaneously diagonalizable with real eigenvalues ±1, so every two-dimensional representation has an invariant line. Each genuine irreducible character satisfies χ(g2)=χ(1)=1, and hence has Frobenius-Schur indicator ν=1.
The printed notation C4​ suggests a different possible interpretation, which must not be conflated with the Klein four-group. For the cyclic group of order four, a real two-dimensional irreducible representation is generated by
J=(01​−10​).
(47)
Its character on 1,J,−I,−J is (2,0,−2,0), giving ν=(2−2+2−2)/4=0. Over the complex numbers this representation splits into two conjugate one-dimensional characters, so it is not a two-dimensional complex irreducible representation. These alternatives explain what can be computed without silently changing the printed group or the ground field.

For the symmetric group representation in part (a), the identity and three transpositions contribute 4I to A=∑g​D(g2). The two 3-cycles contribute D(r)+D(r2)=−I. Thus
A=3I=26⋅1​I.​
(48)
More generally, the central average of representation matrices at squares commutes with the representation, because conjugation permutes the summands. For a complex irreducible representation, Schur's lemma therefore gives A=cI. Taking the trace yields cdimD=∣G∣ν, proving the claimed formula under its correct hypotheses.
For the Klein four-group there is no two-dimensional irreducible representation, as established in part (b). Its genuine one-dimensional irreducibles have A=4 and ν=1, so A=(4⋅1/1)I.
If the intended alternative is the real quarter-turn representation of the cyclic group of order four, its squared matrices sum to I−I+I−I=0. Thus A=0=(4⋅0/2)I in that example. This equality is a direct calculation; the complex version of Schur's lemma cannot be applied to its reducible complexification.
