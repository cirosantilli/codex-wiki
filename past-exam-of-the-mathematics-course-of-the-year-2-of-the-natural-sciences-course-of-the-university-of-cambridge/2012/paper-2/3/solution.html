∫BR​​(uLH​v−vLH​u)d3r=∫∂BR​​(u∂n​v−v∂n​u)dS.

Thus the whole-space integral vanishes when this boundary term tends to zero. For example, uniform bounds u,v=O(R−1−ϵ) and ∇u,∇v=O(R−1−ϵ), with ϵ>0, make it O(R−2ϵ).
Pointwise vanishing alone, as stated in the source, is insufficient. Smooth fields agreeing with u=sin(k0​r)/r and v=cos(k0​r)/r outside a ball both tend to zero, but their distant boundary integral is −4πk0​. One may smooth them inside the ball without changing that flux. The requested zero identity therefore needs sufficient decay or boundary/radiation conditions that actually eliminate the boundary term.
