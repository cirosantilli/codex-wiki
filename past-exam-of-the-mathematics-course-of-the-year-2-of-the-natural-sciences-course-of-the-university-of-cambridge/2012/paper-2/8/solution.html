The group order ∣G∣ is the number of elements of the finite group. A normal subgroup H≤G satisfies gHg−1=H for every g∈G, equivalently its left and right cosets agree.
For the square, let R be counterclockwise rotation by π/2, and s reflection in the horizontal axis. The dihedral group is
D4​={I,R,R2,R3,s,Rs,R2s,R3s},R4=s2=I,sRs=R−1.
(45)
The first four elements rotate by 0,90,180,270 degrees. The other four are Euclidean reflections in the horizontal axis, the line y=x, the vertical axis and the line y=−x, respectively, for a square centred at the origin with sides parallel to the axes.
The eight nontrivial proper subgroups are
{I,R2},{I,Rjs}(j=0,1,2,3),⟨R⟩={I,R,R2,R3},{I,R2,s,R2s},{I,R2,Rs,R3s}.​​
(46)
To check completeness, Lagrange's theorem allows proper orders one, two and four. The order-two subgroups are generated by the five involutions displayed. An order-four subgroup containing R or R3 is the rotation subgroup. Otherwise it must contain reflections; their product is a rotation, which must be R2, so its reflections differ by that half-turn, giving the two listed Klein subgroups. The trivial subgroup {I} is an additional proper subgroup. Thus the PDF's count of eight uses “proper” to exclude the trivial subgroup; with the usual inclusive definition there are nine proper subgroups.
A normal subgroup of order two is central: conjugation fixes its identity and must also fix its only other member. The half-turn R2 commutes with every square symmetry, whereas the reflection subgroups are exchanged by conjugation. Hence the unique order-two normal subgroup is
{I,R2}.​
(47)
For the general polygon, write Dn​={Rj,Rjs:0≤j<n} with Rn=s2=I and sRs=R−1. A rotation Rj commutes with s exactly when Rj=R−j, or 2j≡0(modn). A reflection cannot commute with R when n≥3, because
R(Rjs)R−1=Rj+2s=Rjs.
(48)
Therefore the center of a group here is {I} for odd n, and {I,Rn/2} for even n. The order-two normal subgroups correspond exactly to nonidentity central involutions, proving
{no order-two normal subgroup,{I,Rn/2} is the unique one,​n odd,n even.​​
(49)
This agrees with the involutions in an even dihedral group and uses the convention ∣Dn​∣=2n throughout.
