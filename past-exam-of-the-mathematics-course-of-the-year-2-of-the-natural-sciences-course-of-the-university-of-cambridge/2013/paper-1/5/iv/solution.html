Multiplying the defining equation on the right by I+A gives (I+U)A=I−U. Consequently the only possible answer is
A=(I+U)−1(I−U)=(I−U)(I+U)−1.​
(34)
The factors commute because they are rational functions of U. Its conjugate transpose, using the unitarity U†=U−1, is
A†​=(I+U−1)−1(I−U−1)=(I+U)−1U[−U−1(I−U)]=−A.​
(35)
Thus A is Skew-Hermitian. Moreover I+A=2(I+U)−1 and I−A=2U(I+U)−1, so substitution recovers U and establishes existence as well as uniqueness. The unitary matrix U=−I is a counterexample to invertibility of I+U; more generally the obstruction is an eigenvalue −1.
