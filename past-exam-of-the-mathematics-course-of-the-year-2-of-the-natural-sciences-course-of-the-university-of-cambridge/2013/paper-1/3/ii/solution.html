The interior singular point requires a qualification. This is a Green kernel with a vanishing Wronskian at an interior singular point, with a 1/ξ singularity in its source variable. It is not an ordinary integral inverse for unrestricted forcing. For smooth forcing f(ξ)=ξq(ξ), however, the product Gf extends continuously at zero and the integral is well defined. This includes part (iii). We use solutions with two continuous derivatives across zero, interpreting the equation there after multiplication by x. For such a solution, expansion at zero forces f(0)=0; arbitrary nonzero forcing at zero cannot satisfy this regularity. Merely imposing one continuous derivative would leave a piecewise homogeneous ambiguity, so the original singular coefficients alone do not specify an unrestricted boundary-value inverse.
