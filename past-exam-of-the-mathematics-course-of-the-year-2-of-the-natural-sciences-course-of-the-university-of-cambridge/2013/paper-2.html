The only possible singularity of f(z)/(z−z0​) inside the contour is at z0​, with residue f(z0​). It is at most a simple pole; when f(z0​)=0, it is removable. Thus the residue theorem gives the Cauchy integral formula,
∮C​z−z0​f(z)​dz=2πif(z0​),​
(30)
for a counterclockwise simple contour containing z0​ once, with f analytic on a neighbourhood of the contour and its interior. More generally the answer is 2πiInd(C,z0​)f(z0​), where the winding number accounts for multiple turns or reversed orientation. A clockwise simple contour has the opposite sign.

The order of a group element g is the least positive integer q such that gq=e, or infinity if there is no such integer. In a finite group such a q exists, because two powers among e,g,g2,… must coincide.
The powers e,g,…,gq−1 are distinct: a repetition with exponents differing by less than q would contradict its minimality. Division of any exponent by q shows that these are all the elements of the cyclic subgroup ⟨g⟩. Thus ∣⟨g⟩∣=q. Applying Lagrange's theorem gives
ord(g)=∣⟨g⟩∣ ∣ ∣G∣.​
(69)
In particular the identity has order one, and every nonidentity element has order greater than one. The divisibility assertion here concerns finite group order.

For a group representation D:G→GL(V), a faithful representation is injective, equivalently D(g)=I only for g=e. Thus no distinct group elements are represented by the same linear map.
Two equivalent representations are related by one fixed invertible change of basis: E(g)=S−1D(g)S for every g. The same S must work simultaneously for all group elements.
An irreducible representation acts on a nonzero vector space and has no invariant subspace other than {0} and the whole space. An invariant subspace is one carried into itself by every representing matrix.
The character of a representation is
χD​(g)=trD(g).​
(79)
The cyclic property of the trace makes it invariant under equivalence and constant on conjugacy classes. Its value at the identity is the dimension of the representation.

Write the finite cyclic group as Cn​=⟨g:gn=e⟩, the source's Zn​. It is Abelian, so hgh−1=g and every conjugacy class is a singleton: there are n such classes.
Over the complex numbers, all its irreducible representations are one-dimensional. One direct proof is that the matrix representing g has an eigenvector over C; its line is preserved by every power of that matrix and hence by the whole group. Irreducibility forces the line to be the whole representation space. Its eigenvalue must be an nth root of unity.
