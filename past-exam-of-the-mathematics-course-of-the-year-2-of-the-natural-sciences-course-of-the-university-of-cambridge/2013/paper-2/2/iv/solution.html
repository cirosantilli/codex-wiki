A positive matching root x∗​ gives s=x∗2​/R2. Substituting the root value requested by the question gives the intended expression
sintended​=R2π2​.​
(18)
After the index correction in part (iii), this is indeed the lowest mode in the ℓ=1 sector, because its condition is j0​(x)=sinx/x=0 and its first positive root is π. It is also the minimum over ℓ≥1.
However, the literal scalar problem includes ℓ=0, for which matching gives cosx=0. Its first positive root is π/2, so the actual minimum for the written scalar equations and stated boundary conditions is
sscalar,min​=4R2π2​.​
(19)
An explicit counterexample to the larger proposed minimum is Bin​=j0​(πr/(2R)), Bout​=2R/(πr), with no angular dependence. At R, both values are 2/π and both radial derivatives are −2/(πR).
To verify minimality, put u=rFℓ​. The radial problem is −u′′+ℓ(ℓ+1)u/r2=su, u(0)=0, u′(R)+ℓu(R)/R=0. Its Rayleigh quotient is
s=∫0R​∣u∣2dr∫0R​[∣u′∣2+ℓ(ℓ+1)∣u∣2/r2]dr+ℓ∣u(R)∣2/R​.
(20)
For ℓ=0 this is the Dirichlet-Neumann interval problem, whose lowest eigenvalue is π2/(4R2). The additional nonnegative terms increase with ℓ, proving the scalar minimum and the minimum-at-ℓ=1 statement for the restricted family. A monopole-free magnetic multipole interpretation would exclude ℓ=0 and yield the intended value, but that extra restriction is not supplied by the stated scalar model. The claim about the first zero is not valid for every fℓ​ as printed.
