Start from the three-index transformation law and contract the first two Cartesian indices:
Ck′​=i∑​Siik′​​=i,a,b,c∑​Qia​Qib​Qkc​Sabc​=a,b,c∑​δab​Qkc​Sabc​=c∑​Qkc​a∑​Saac​=Qkc​Cc​.​
(52)
Orthogonality supplies ∑i​Qia​Qib​=δab​, where δ is the Kronecker delta. The remaining factor is exactly the vector transformation rule. Therefore Ck​ is a vector.
This tensor contraction removes two indices by pairing them through the Euclidean metric. In general nonorthonormal coordinates, contraction must use the appropriate metric or pair a covariant and a contravariant index; the bare repeated-lower-index sum relies on Cartesian orthonormal coordinates.
