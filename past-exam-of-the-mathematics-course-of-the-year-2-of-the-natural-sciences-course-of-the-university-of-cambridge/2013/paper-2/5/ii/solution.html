Under regularity and integrability conditions permitting the limits to pass through the integral, apply the difference quotient directly in time:
Fx​[ut​](k,t)​=h→0lim​∫R​hu(x,t+h)−u(x,t)​e−ikxdx=h→0lim​hu(k,t+h)−u(k,t)​=∂t​u(k,t).​
(40)
For the spatial derivative, translate the integration variable in the first term:
Fx​[ux​](k,t)​=h→0lim​∫R​hu(x+h,t)−u(x,t)​e−ikxdx=h→0lim​heikh−1​u(k,t)=iku(k,t).​
(41)
Consequently
Fx​[ut​]=∂t​u,Fx​[ux​]=iku.​
(42)
The second calculation is the Translation property of the Fourier transform applied to a difference quotient. These are the Fourier transform of a derivative identities. The stated decay removes boundary terms in the alternative integration-by-parts derivation; rigorous interchange of limits also requires suitable control, for example integrable derivatives. The formulas extend to tempered distributions.
