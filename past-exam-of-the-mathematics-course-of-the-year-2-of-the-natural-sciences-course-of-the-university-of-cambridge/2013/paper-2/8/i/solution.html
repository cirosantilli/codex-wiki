Interpret the functions as maps on R∖{0}, so every reciprocal is defined. Let e(x)=x, a(x)=−x, b(x)=1/x and c(x)=−1/x. With the left factor applied last, direct function composition gives the Cayley table
∘eabc​eeabc​aaecb​bbcea​ccbae​​.​
(65)
The table proves closure and identifies e as the identity. Every element is its own inverse. Associativity is inherited from composition of maps, so the set is a group. The three nonidentity elements are commuting involutions.
Its subgroups are
{e},{e,a},{e,b},{e,c},{e,a,b,c}.​
(66)
There are no others: a subgroup containing two distinct nonidentity elements also contains their product, the third, and hence is the whole group; a subgroup containing just one has order two.
The abstract dihedral group D2​ has commuting generators R,m with R2=m2=I, for example a rectangle's half-turn and reflection in one axis. The assignment I↦e, R↦a, m↦b, Rm↦c respects products and is bijective. Thus
F≅D2​≅C2​×C2​.​
(67)
This is the group generated by sign reversal and reciprocation, a realization of the Klein four-group. The nonzero domain is essential; treating these as functions defined at zero would not give the stated group.
