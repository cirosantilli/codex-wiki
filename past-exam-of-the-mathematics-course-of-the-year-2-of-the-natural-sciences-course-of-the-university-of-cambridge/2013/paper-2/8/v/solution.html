For the intended finite nontrivial case, let G=⟨g⟩ have composite order N. Choose a prime divisor p of N, with 1<p<N. Then
H=⟨gN/p⟩​
(71)
is a nontrivial proper cyclic subgroup of order p. Indeed (gN/p)p=e, and no smaller positive power is the identity because g has order N. Thus 1<∣H∣=p<N.
If the source also allows an infinite cyclic group, ⟨g2⟩ is a proper cyclic subgroup: its powers have even exponents and do not include g. The trivial group, of order one, is an exception to the literal formulation “not of prime order”: it has no proper subgroup at all. The nontriviality assumption is therefore necessary for that general wording. These facts are instances of subgroups and quotients of a cyclic group.
