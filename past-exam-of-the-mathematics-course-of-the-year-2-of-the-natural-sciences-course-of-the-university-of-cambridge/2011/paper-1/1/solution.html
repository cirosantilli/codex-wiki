For a twice continuously differentiable vector field in Cartesian coordinates, the curl and divergence are
∇×v=(∂y​vz​−∂z​vy​, ∂z​vx​−∂x​vz​, ∂x​vy​−∂y​vx​),∇⋅v=∂x​vx​+∂y​vy​+∂z​vz​.
(1)
Put ω=∇×v. Using the Levi-Civita symbol, the Kronecker delta contraction and the product rule,
[∇×(v×ω)]i​​=∂j​(vi​ωj​−vj​ωi​)=ωj​∂j​vi​−vj​∂j​ωi​−ωi​∂j​vj​+vi​∂j​ωj​.​
(2)
The last term vanishes because the divergence of a curl is zero: the symmetric mixed derivatives contract against an antisymmetric symbol. Thus the curl of a cross product identity becomes
∇×(v×ω)=(ω⋅∇)v−(v⋅∇)ω−ω(∇⋅v).​
(3)
For orthogonal coordinates qi​ with position vector r(q1​,q2​,q3​), the scale factors of orthogonal coordinates and local unit vectors are
hi​=​∂qi​∂r​​,ei​=hi​1​∂qi​∂r​,dr=∑i​hi​ei​dqi​.
(4)
Orthogonality gives ds2=∑i​hi2​dqi2​, and right-handedness fixes e1​×e2​=e3​. In cylindrical coordinates, r=(ρcosϕ,ρsinϕ,z), so
hρ​=1,hϕ​=ρ,hz​=1,eρ​=(cosϕ,sinϕ,0),eϕ​=(−sinϕ,cosϕ,0),ez​=(0,0,1).​
(5)
The radial vector points away from the axis; the azimuthal vector is tangent in the direction of increasing ϕ; ez​ is the Cartesian positive z direction. The azimuthal basis is undefined on the coordinate axis itself.
For the axial velocity profile, the cylindrical coordinates formula gives
ω=∇×u=−∂ρ​uz​eϕ​=2u0​ρeϕ​.​
(6)
The associated cross product is
u×ω=−2u02​ρ(1−ρ2)eρ​.​
(7)
This field has only a radial component depending on ρ, so its curl is zero. Also ∇⋅u=∂z​uz​=0. The identity above therefore yields
(ω⋅∇)u=(u⋅∇)ω.​
(8)
In fact both directional derivatives vanish: the first differentiates uz​ez​ only in the azimuthal direction, and the second differentiates ω only in the z direction. All physical vector fields extend smoothly through ρ=0, despite the coordinate-basis singularity.
