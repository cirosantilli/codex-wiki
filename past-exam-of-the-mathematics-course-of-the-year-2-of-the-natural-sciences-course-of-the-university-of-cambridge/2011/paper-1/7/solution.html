The Cauchy-Riemann equations are ux​=vy​ and uy​=−vx​. A holomorphic function is smooth, so equality of mixed partial derivatives gives
Δu=(vy​)x​−(vx​)y​=0,Δv=−(uy​)x​+(ux​)y​=0.​

Thus its real and imaginary parts are harmonic functions.
An entire function is holomorphic at every finite point of the complex plane. The globally convergent power series for the exponential implies that ez and e−z are entire. Their difference therefore gives
sinhz=2ez−e−z​,(sinhz)′=coshz

at every complex z, proving that the hyperbolic sine is entire. For powers, the base case f1​(z)=z has derivative one everywhere. If zn is entire with derivative nzn−1, the product rule gives (zn+1)′=zn+znzn−1=(n+1)zn everywhere. Induction proves that every positive integer power is entire.
