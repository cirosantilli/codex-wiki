Let Q be the orthogonal matrix taking old components to new ones, so xi′​=Qip​xp​. With repeated indices summed, the tensor component transformation law is
tij′​=Qip​Qjq​tpq​,uijkl′​=Qip​Qjq​Qkr​Qls​upqrs​.​
(39)
An isotropic tensor has the same components after every proper rotation. Since Qip​Qjp​=δij​, the Kronecker delta is invariant. Applying this identity to each factor shows that aδij​ and every linear combination bδij​δkl​+cδik​δjl​+dδil​δjk​ are invariant as well.
To show that an isotropic second-rank tensor must be scalar, start with any matrix t, without assuming symmetry. Invariance under quarter-turns about axes 1 and 2 implies invariance under their squares, the half-turns
Q12​=diag(1,−1,−1),Q22​=diag(−1,1,−1).
(40)
The first forces t12​,t21​,t13​,t31​ to vanish. The second also forces t23​,t32​ to vanish. The matrix is therefore diagonal. A quarter-turn about axis 1 interchanges its 22 and 33 entries; one about axis 2 interchanges its 11 and 33 entries. All three must agree, giving t=aI.
A rotation preserves the ball VR​, ∣x∣2 and the volume element. Changing integration variables by that rotation shows that A and B are isotropic tensors. Write Aij​=aδij​. Taking its tensor contraction gives
3a=Aii​=∫VR​​∣x∣4dV=4π∫0R​r6dr=74πR7​,
(41)
so a=4πR7/21. Every permutation of the indices of B leaves its integrand unchanged. Its complete symmetry therefore makes all three isotropic coefficients equal, say d:
Bijkl​=d(δij​δkl​+δik​δjl​+δil​δjk​).
(42)
Double contraction gives 15d=Biikk​=∫VR​​∣x∣4dV=4πR7/7. Thus the isotropic fourth moment of a ball and its radial contraction are
Aij​=214πR7​δij​,Bijkl​=1054πR7​(δij​δkl​+δik​δjl​+δil​δjk​).​
(43)
Put d=4πR7/105, so that a=5d. Contract B with the prescribed unit vector twice:
Cij​=Aij​+Bijkl​nk​nl​=d(6δij​+2ni​nj​).
(44)
This has the spectrum of a scalar matrix plus a rank-one projector. In the direction n, the outer product nnT acts as the identity; on the plane perpendicular to n, it acts as zero. Therefore
Cn=10532πR7​n,Cv=358πR7​vfor v⋅n=0.​
(45)
Any nonzero multiple of n is a parallel eigenvector, and any nonzero vector in the perpendicular plane is a transverse eigenvector. The transverse eigenvalue has multiplicity two; an orthonormal basis of that plane completes a set of principal axes.
