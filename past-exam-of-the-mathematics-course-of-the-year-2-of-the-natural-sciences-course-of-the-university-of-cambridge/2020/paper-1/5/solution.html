Company A. The relevant functional is JA​=∫(y′2+y2−2xy)dx. The Euler-Lagrange equation gives
2(y−x)−2y′′=0,y′′−y=−x.
(56)
Its general solution is y=x+c1​coshx+c2​sinhx. Adding and subtracting the endpoint equations gives c1​=0 and c2​=−a/sinha. Thus
yA​(x)=x−asinhasinhx​.​
(57)
This curve is odd, negative on the left half and positive on the right half; company A has no lower-half-plane restriction. It is the unique global minimum. Indeed, for any endpoint-zero variation η, integration by parts and the differential equation cancel all linear terms, leaving
JA​[yA​+η]−JA​[yA​]=∫−aa​(η′2+η2)dx>0
(58)
unless η=0.
Company B. Minimize ∫ydx under the length constraint. Introduce a constant Lagrange multiplier λ and apply the Euler-Lagrange equation to y+λ1+y′2​:
1−λ(1+y′2)3/2y′′​=0.
(59)
The curvature is constant, so the required lower arc is a circular arc. Writing its radius as R≥a and imposing the endpoints gives
yB​(x)=R2−a2​−R2−x2​.​
(60)
Since 1+yB′2​​=R/R2−x2​, its length determines the radius:
L=2Rarcsin(a/R).​
(61)
Equivalently, introduce 0<θ≤π/2 with a=Rsinθ. Then L/(2a)=θ/sinθ, which increases strictly from 1 to π/2. The stipulated range of L therefore selects one minor arc. At L=πa it becomes the lower semicircle, with vertical endpoint tangents; it is still a single-valued finite-length graph.
Global minimality follows from the convexity of the augmented slope cost with λ=R. For a competitor y=yB​+η,
R(1+y′2​−1+yB′2​​)≥1+yB′2​​RyB′​​η′=xη′.
(62)
Consequently the augmented cost difference is at least ∫(η+xη′)dx=[xη]−aa​=0. Competitors have the same length, so the multiplier contribution cancels and the original cost is minimized. This proves that a circular arc maximizes area below a fixed chord: minimizing negative signed height maximizes the area between the fence and the chord.
Company C. The variable cost is JC​=∫yds=∫y1+y′2​dx. The augmented integrand is (y+λ)1+y′2​. Since it has no explicit x dependence, the Beltrami identity gives
1+y′2​y+λ​=b.
(63)
The hanging branch with b>0 integrates to y+λ=bcosh[(x−x0​)/b]. Equal endpoint heights force x0​=0, and the endpoint value fixes λ=bcosh(a/b). Thus
yC​(x)=b[cosh(x/b)−cosh(a/b)],b>0.​
(64)
Its length is
L=∫−aa​cosh(x/b)dx=2bsinh(a/b).​
(65)
Writing q=a/b, the equation L/(2a)=sinhq/q has one positive solution for every L>2a: its derivative has numerator qcoshq−sinhq>0. The curve is a catenary, is even, and lies strictly below the chord except at the endpoints.
A direct argument proves this is a global minimum, rather than merely an extremum of an augmented functional. Parametrize any competitor by arc length s∈[−L/2,L/2], from its left endpoint to its right endpoint. Then x′2+y′2=1, ∫x′ds=2a, and integration by parts gives
JC​=−∫sy′ds=2ab−∫(bx′+sy′)ds≥2ab−∫−L/2L/2​b2+s2​ds.
(66)
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the last step. Equality requires x′=b/b2+s2​ and y′=s/b2+s2​, whose integrals are precisely the displayed catenary with the stated length. Thus it attains a lower bound valid for every competitor. This proves that a catenary minimizes the integral of height along a fixed-length curve.
The sketch uses a=1 and L=2.7 for the two fixed-length cases. The vertical scale in the company A panel is enlarged to show its small odd deflection.
