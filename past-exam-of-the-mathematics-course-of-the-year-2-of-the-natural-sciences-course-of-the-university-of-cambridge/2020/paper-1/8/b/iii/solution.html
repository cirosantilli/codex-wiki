Reality requires σ(ω,r)=σ(−ω,r)∗. The way it constrains the amplitudes depends on whether the spatial wave number is real or imaginary.
For ∣ω∣>μ, k is real and even. Complex conjugation interchanges the two exponentials, so comparing independent spatial factors gives
A(ω)=B(−ω)∗(∣ω∣>μ).​
(95)
The companion equation B(ω)=A(−ω)∗ is the same constraint with frequency reversed and conjugated. Neither oscillatory spatial branch grows as r→∞.
For ∣ω∣<μ, put k=iκ with κ>0. Then
σ=A(ω)e−κr+B(ω)eκr.
(96)
Boundedness excludes the growing exponential, and reality then pairs the remaining amplitudes:
B(ω)=0,A(ω)=A(−ω)∗(∣ω∣<μ).​
(97)
These are the two independent low-frequency requirements. At threshold boundedness eliminates the separate linear-in-r solution discussed in (ii). The constraints are understood for a Fourier transform representation in which spatial mode amplitudes are well defined; sufficient amplitude integrability also ensures the spectral integral itself is bounded.
