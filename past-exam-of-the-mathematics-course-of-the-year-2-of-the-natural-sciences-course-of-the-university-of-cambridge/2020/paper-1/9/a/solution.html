Label the equilibrium vertices in the order +x,−x,+y,−y,+z,−z, and write their displacements as ui​(t)∈R3. Their eighteen Cartesian components are generalized coordinates. Linearize the axial Hookean springs about the unstressed equilibrium. For an edge with unit direction nij​, its first-order extension is nij​⋅(ui​−uj​), so the quadratic potential energy is
V=2k​∑⟨ij⟩​[nij​⋅(ui​−uj​)]2.
(100)
Three zero modes are translations: for each Cartesian unit vector eα​, take ui​=eα​ at every vertex. Three more are infinitesimal rotations ui​=eα​×ri​. Explicitly, in the stated vertex order, their displacement vectors are
Rx​:(0,0,ez​,−ez​,−ey​,ey​),
(101)
Ry​:(−ez​,ez​,0,0,ex​,−ex​),
(102)
Rz​:(ey​,−ey​,−ex​,ex​,0,0).
(103)
Translations leave each displacement difference zero. For rotations,
nij​⋅[Ω×(ri​−rj​)]=0
(104)
because the equilibrium edge vector is parallel to nij​. Hence every spring has zero first-order extension in all six patterns, and the stiffness matrix annihilates them.
The translations have nonzero summed displacement, while all the rotations have zero sum. The three rotational patterns are themselves independent because the vertices do not lie on a single line. Thus
there are at least six independent zero modes: three translations and three rotations.​
(105)
These are the rigid-motion zero modes of a spring framework. The rotational displacements describe infinitesimal rigid rotations; an arbitrary finite rotation would require its nonlinear displacement field.
