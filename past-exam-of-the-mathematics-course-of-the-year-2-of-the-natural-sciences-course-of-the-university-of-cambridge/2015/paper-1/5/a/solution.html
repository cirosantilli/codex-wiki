Let Avj​=aj​vj​. Commutation gives A(Bvj​)=B(Avj​)=aj​Bvj​, so B preserves each eigenspace of A. Since the eigenvalues of A are nondegenerate, each eigenspace is one-dimensional and Bvj​=bj​vj​. The spectral theorem for a Hermitian matrix lets us choose these vectors as an orthonormal eigenbasis. With U=(v1​,…,vn​),
A=UΛA​U†,B=UΛB​U†,U†U=I.​
(27)
This is simple spectrum gives simultaneous diagonalization. The common eigenvectors can be chosen up to their phases and ordering. In fact degeneracy does not prevent a common eigenbasis for commuting Hermitian matrices, but the one-dimensional argument already suffices here.
The convergent matrix exponential series respects similarity, so eA=UeΛA​U† and likewise for B. Scalar exponentials multiply on each diagonal entry, giving
eAeB=UeΛA​eΛB​U†=UeΛA​+ΛB​U†=eA+B.​
(28)
The essential hypothesis is commutation; Hermiticity supplies this convenient diagonalization proof.
