Let R[y]=F[y]/G[y], where G[y]>0 for a nonzero admissible real function. Writing λ=R[y], its first variation is
δR=G2​{[py′δy]αβ​+∫αβ​[−(py′)′+qy−λwy]δydx}.
(64)
Choose boundary conditions making the boundary term vanish, for example homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions, or a corresponding mixed pair. Arbitrary interior variations then give the Sturm-Liouville problem
−(py′)′+qy=λwy.​
(65)
Multiplying this equation by y and integrating by parts, with [pyy′]αβ​=0, yields
λ=∫αβ​wy2dx∫αβ​[p(y′)2+qy2]dx​=R[y].​
(66)
Thus eigenfunctions are stationary directions of the quotient. The lowest eigenvalue is its global minimum; higher eigenfunctions need not be unrestricted maxima or minima. General Robin boundary conditions require including their boundary energy in the quotient unless the displayed endpoint products also vanish.
The Rayleigh-Ritz method chooses admissible trial functions depending on finitely many coefficients and minimizes this quotient over them. Expansion in a complete orthonormal basis with weight w shows R[y]≥λ0​, so the trial minimum is an upper bound on the true lowest eigenvalue. In a finite basis, the minimization becomes a generalized eigenvalue problem for the stiffness and weighted mass matrices. Enlarging the trial space can only lower or preserve this upper bound.
