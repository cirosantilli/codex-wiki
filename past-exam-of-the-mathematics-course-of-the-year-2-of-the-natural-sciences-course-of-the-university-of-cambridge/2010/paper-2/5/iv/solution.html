For the Gaussian initial data for the heat equation, substitute the initial profile into the heat-kernel solution. Completing the square in y gives
a0​(y−x0​)2+4t(x−y)2​=(a0​+4t1​)(y−μ)2+1+4a0​ta0​(x−x0​)2​,μ=1+4a0​t4a0​tx0​+x​.
(35)
The remaining Gaussian integral yields
θG​(x,t)=1+4a0​t​A0​​exp[−1+4a0​ta0​(x−x0​)2​].​
(36)
The centre stays at x0​ and the squared width grows linearly with time.
For the interval pulse, the convolution reduces to B∫ab​G(x−y,t)dy. Using u=(y−x)/(2t​) and the error function gives
θI​(x,t)=2B​[erf(2t​x−a​)−erf(2t​x−b​)],t>0.​
(37)
This is the translated and scaled heat equation with interval-indicator initial data. At its jump points the t↓0 limits are B/2; away from them the initial data are recovered pointwise.
For fixed a<x<b at late time, both error function arguments are small. Since erfu=2u/π​+O(u3),
θI​(x,t)=4πt​B(b−a)​+O(t−3/2)(t→∞).​
(38)
The estimate is uniform for x∈[a,b]. Equivalently, on this fixed interval the Gaussian in the convolution is 1+O((b−a)2/t).
The spreading distance is more apparent on the growing spatial scale. For the Gaussian pulse the exponent is order one when ∣x−x0​∣=O(t​). For the interval pulse, set x=c+ξt​ with c=(a+b)/2 and fixed ξ. Uniformly for bounded ξ, its convolution gives
θI​(c+ξt​,t)∼4πt​B(b−a)​e−ξ2/4.
(39)
Thus both nonzero pulses spread over a distance O(t​), while their heights are O(t−1/2) and their total integrals remain constant. More quantitatively, the variances of their normalized profiles are 1/(2a0​)+2t and (b−a)2/12+2t, respectively, confirming the same width law.
