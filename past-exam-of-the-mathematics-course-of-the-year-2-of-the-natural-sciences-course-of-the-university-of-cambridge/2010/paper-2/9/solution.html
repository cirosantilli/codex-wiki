Two elements are conjugate group elements if h=xgx−1 for some x∈G. The conjugacy class of g is {xgx−1:x∈G}. Conjugacy is an equivalence relation: it is reflexive using x=e, symmetric by replacing x with x−1, and transitive because
k=yhy−1,h=xgx−1⟹k=(yx)g(yx)−1.
(71)
Thus the classes partition the group: every element belongs to its own class, and any two classes that intersect are equal. This proves that every element belongs to exactly one class.
If G is Abelian, then xgx−1=g, so every class is a singleton. In general the elements whose classes are singletons are precisely those commuting with every group element. Thus the center of a group is
Z(G)={z∈G:zg=gz for every g∈G}.
(72)
It contains e. If a,b∈Z(G), then ab−1 commutes with every g, since b−1 does and a does. The subgroup test therefore proves that Z(G) is a subgroup. Any two of its elements commute with each other, so it is an abelian subgroup.

These exhaust the symmetries: the image of one vertex has four choices and the image of an adjacent vertex has two choices, determining the entire square. For an explicit pair of nonconjugate elements take R2 and S. Both have order two, but their determinants are +1 and −1. A similarity transformation preserves the determinant, so R2 and S are not conjugate.
To find the centre, use SR=R−1S. A rotation Rj commutes with S exactly when Rj=R−j, so j is even. No reflection RjS commutes with R, since (RjS)R=Rj−1S while R(RjS)=Rj+1S, and R2=I. Therefore
Z(D4​)={I,R2}.​
