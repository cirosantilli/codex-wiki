For two functions obeying the endpoint conditions, integration by parts gives the Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator

At R, both u and v vanish. At 1, the factor x(x−1) tends to zero while the functions and their first derivatives remain bounded. Thus the boundary form vanishes at both ends.
This gives the regular self-adjoint differential operator realization: its singular-end condition is limx↓1​x(x−1)v′=0, together with v(R)=0. The bounded classical eigenfunctions satisfy exactly this no-log choice. More precisely the operator domain is the maximal differential-operator domain in the weighted space with these separated endpoint conditions; the preceding calculation verifies its self-adjoint boundary form, rather than merely the differential expression's formal symmetry.
