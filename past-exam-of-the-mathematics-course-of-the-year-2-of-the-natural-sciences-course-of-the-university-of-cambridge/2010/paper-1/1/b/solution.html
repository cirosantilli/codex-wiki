Away from the axis, cylindrical coordinates put the field in the line vortex form
v=2πρκ​eϕ​.
(5)
The only potentially nonzero component of its curl is
(∇×v)z​=ρ1​∂ρ∂​(ρvϕ​)=ρ1​∂ρ∂​2πκ​=0.
(6)
The other components vanish because there is no axial dependence and no radial or axial velocity. Thus the curl is zero for ρ>0; the field is singular on the axis, so a classical curl there is not defined.
For a spanning surface avoiding that axis, the smoothness hypotheses of Stokes theorem hold and give ∮C​v⋅dr=0. If the enclosed planar region contains the axis, the singularity prevents applying that theorem directly. Excise a small disk about the origin. The resulting annulus has zero curl, so its outer circulation equals the counterclockwise circulation on the small circle:
∮C​v⋅dr=∫02π​2πρκ​ρdϕ=κ.​
(7)
This sign assumes positive, counterclockwise orientation. Clockwise orientation gives −κ; more generally the value is κ times the winding number about the origin. A curve through the origin is excluded because its integral is singular. In the distribution interpretation the missing vorticity is concentrated on the axis as κδ(x)δ(y)ez​, explaining the nonzero circulation despite zero classical curl away from it.
