In monic form the Chebyshev differential equation has P(x)=−x/(1−x2) and Q(x)=p2/(1−x2). Both are analytic near zero, so it is an ordinary point. At x=1, (x−1)P=x/(x+1) and (x−1)2Q=−p2(x−1)/(x+1) are analytic; at x=−1, (x+1)P=x/(x−1) and (x+1)2Q=−p2(x+1)/(x−1) are analytic. Thus both endpoints are regular singular points.
Insert yq​=∑n≥0​an​xn+q and equate coefficients. The recurrence relation is
an+2​=(n+q+2)(n+q+1)(n+q)2−p2​an​,q=0,1.​

Explicitly the factors are (n2−p2)/[(n+2)(n+1)] for q=0 and [(n+1)2−p2]/[(n+3)(n+2)] for q=1. The lowest equation for q=1 forces a1​=0. For q=0, one may choose a1​=0 to select an even solution, independent of the odd q=1 solution. The leading coefficient a0​ is free in either chosen parity branch.
For a nonterminating branch the consecutive parity-term ratio tends to x2. Hence the ratio test gives convergence for ∣x∣<1, and radius of convergence one. Terminating branches are polynomials with infinite radius. For p=m≥0 an integer, choose q of the same parity as m: the recurrence stops once n+q=m, producing a degree-m Chebyshev polynomial. Normalization at 1 gives all nonzero coefficients through degree three:
T0​=1,T1​=x,T2​=2x2−1,T3​=4x3−3x.​

Set Y(θ)=y(cosθ). Direct differentiation gives Y′′=sin2θy′′−cosθy′, so the equation becomes Y′′+m2Y=0. For m>0, Y=Acos(mθ)+Bsin(mθ). A polynomial y has finite derivative at x=1, and therefore Y′(0)=−sin0y′(1)=0, forcing B=0. Also Y(0)=Tm​(1)=1, so A=1. For m=0, the transformed solution is affine in θ and the same derivative condition removes its linear term. Thus
Tm​(x)=cos(marccosx),−1≤x≤1,m=0,1,2,….​
(49)
The endpoint values follow by continuity. The finite-derivative condition is what excludes the independent nonpolynomial branch, rather than normalization alone.
