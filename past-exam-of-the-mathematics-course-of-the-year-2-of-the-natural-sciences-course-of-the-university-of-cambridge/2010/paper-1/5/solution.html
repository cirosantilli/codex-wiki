An eigenvalue λ and associated eigenvector e=0 satisfy Ae=λe. The characteristic polynomial det(A−λI) has degree equal to the positive matrix size. The fundamental theorem of algebra gives a complex root, at which A−λI is singular and therefore has a nontrivial kernel. Hence every nonempty square complex matrix has an eigenvector.
A nondiagonalizable example is the Jordan block (00​10​): its only eigenvalue is zero, and its eigenspace is one-dimensional. A Hermitian matrix satisfies A†=A and has an orthonormal eigenbasis by the spectral theorem. One proof finds an eigenvector, notes that its orthogonal complement is invariant, and repeats. Its eigenvalues are real because
