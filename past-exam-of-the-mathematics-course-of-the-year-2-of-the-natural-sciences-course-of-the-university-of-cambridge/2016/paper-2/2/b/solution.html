The only nonzero angular Fourier mode is cos(2σ). Its radial equation is T′′−4T=0. A solution vanishing at τ=a is proportional to sinh[2(τ−a)]; making its value one at b fixes the constant. The Dirichlet problem between bipolar coordinate circles therefore gives
Φ(σ,τ)=sinh[2(b−a)]sinh[2(τ−a)]​cos(2σ).​
(14)
Its second derivatives in σ and τ cancel, and the endpoint values are respectively zero and cos(2σ). The difference of any two solutions would be a harmonic function with zero Dirichlet boundary conditions, so the maximum principle proves uniqueness.
Geometric wording. In the printed bipolar coordinates, the circle radius 1/sinhτ decreases with τ. For 0<a<b, the b circle is contained inside the a circle. Thus the source’s “inner” and “outer” labels are interchanged geometrically. The solution above follows the explicit coordinate boundary values, which unambiguously specify the problem.
