Since a2=I, a representation satisfies D(a)2=I2​. Orthogonality gives D(a)−1=D(a)T, while the involution relation gives D(a)−1=D(a). Hence D(a) is a symmetric matrix, so γ=δ.
Its entries obey
α2+γ2=1,β2+γ2=1,γ(α+β)=0.
(73)
If γ=0, then β=−α; putting α=cosθ and γ=sinθ gives a reflection. If γ=0, the diagonal entries are independently ±1: the opposite-sign cases are already included among these reflections, while equal signs give I2​ or −I2​. Faithfulness excludes I2​, since a=I. Therefore the orthogonal involutions of the plane give
D(a)=−I2​orD(a)=(cosθsinθ​sinθ−cosθ​).​
(74)
The first is rotation by π. The second is reflection in the line making angle θ/2 with the first axis: the unit vector along that line is fixed, and its perpendicular is negated. These are all the nonidentity orthogonal transformations of order two in the plane.
