The inverse Fourier transform is
f(t)=2π1​∫R​ω−i−ieiωt​dω.
(38)
For t>0, close in the upper half-plane, where eiωt decays. The single enclosed pole has residue −ie−t, so counterclockwise integration gives f=e−t. For t<0, close clockwise in the lower half-plane; there is no enclosed pole and the result is zero. Thus
f(t)=e−tH(t),f(t)=0 (t<0),f(t)=e−t (t>0).​
(39)
Here H is the Heaviside step function. At the jump, symmetric Fourier inversion gives f(0)=1/2, the average of the one-sided limits. The transform itself cannot fix a function’s value at a single point, so this is the conventional representative rather than extra frequency information.
