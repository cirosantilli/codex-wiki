There are also the two carrier Dirac delta lines πδ(ω∓3/4). The last panel of the preceding figure shows this spectrum and the retained central portion. The prescribed strict cutoff removes the carriers and keeps
ρ​(ω)=⎩⎨⎧​π/4,2π​(1/4+∣ω∣),0,​∣ω∣≤1/4,1/4<∣ω∣<3/4,∣ω∣≥3/4.​​

The values of the ordinary function at individual endpoints do not affect its inverse transform. To handle the carrier lines, one must additionally assign zero filter gain to those lines, as the carrier-rejecting prescription indicates. A discontinuous mask has no canonical product with a Dirac delta at its discontinuity; a different physical boundary gain would leave a corresponding multiple of cos(3t/4).
The retained spectrum is real and even. Its inverse Fourier transform is
ρ(t)=41​∫01/4​cos(ωt)dω+21​∫1/43/4​(1/4+ω)cos(ωt)dω.
(26)
Integrating the linear factors yields
ρ(t)=2tsin(3t/4)​+2t2cos(3t/4)−cos(t/4)​,t=0.​
(27)
The removable value is ρ(0)=1/4​, obtained either by expansion or by integrating ρ​/(2π). Because the carrier frequency is below the message bandwidth, this is Fourier filtering of overlapping amplitude-modulation sidebands; the usual picture of two isolated bands in single-sideband modulation does not apply without that qualification.
