To make the directions explicit, set z=x+iy and D=(2+sinxcoshy)2+cos2xsinh2y. Multiplying by the complex conjugate of the denominator gives the logarithmic flow in a sine-mapped half-strip:
U=Dcosx(2coshy+sinx)​,V=Dsinhy(coshy+2sinx)​.​
(50)
In the open strip U>0. The vertical component is negative near the lower left wall where coshy+2sinx<0, and positive above that curve. On the bottom the flow is rightward. On the right wall it is upward, whereas on the left wall it points downward below z∗​ and upward above z∗​. The field is tangent to the boundaries except at the source and vanishes at the two bottom corners. At large y, U−iV→−i, so it tends to a uniform upward field (0,1).
Near z∗​, G′(z)∼1/(z−z∗​), showing a radial boundary source. The streamlines are the curves ImG=arg(2+sinz)=constant; the figure shows their orientation without reusing the exam's graphics.
