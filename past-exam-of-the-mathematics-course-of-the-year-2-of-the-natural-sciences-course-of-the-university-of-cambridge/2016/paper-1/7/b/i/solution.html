Choose the branch of (ζ+1)(ζ−1)​ holomorphic in the upper half-plane whose boundary value at zero is +i. Then
f′(ζ)=1−ζ2​1​,f(0)=0,
(44)
and integration gives z=arcsinζ,ζ=sinz​ on this branch. The normalization and intended upper-half-plane image fix the otherwise ambiguous square-root sign.
To see the image, put z=x+iy. For −π/2<x<π/2 and y>0,
Imsinz=cosxsinhy>0.
(45)
The bottom edge maps monotonically onto [−1,1]. The right wall maps by sin(π/2+iy)=coshy onto [1,∞), and the left by sin(−π/2+iy)=−coshy onto (−∞,−1]. These boundaries describe the conformal map of a vertical half-strip by sine.
For completeness, the map is one-to-one in the interior: equality of two sine values forces arguments differing by 2πm or summing to (2m+1)π, and neither alternative yields two distinct points in this half-strip. It is onto the upper half-plane as well. Setting w=eiz gives w2−2iζw−1=0; the root product is −1, and no root lies on ∣w∣=1 because that would make ζ real. Thus exactly one root has ∣w∣<1. The identity Imζ=(Rew)(∣w∣−2−1)/2 then gives Rew>0, so z=−ilogw belongs to the half-strip. The closure gives the stated boundary-inclusive region; holomorphy is understood in its open interior.
In this region the transformed function is G(z)=log(2+sinz). Its only finite intrinsic branch point in the closure occurs where 2+sinz=0:
z∗​=−2π​+iarcosh2=−2π​+ilog(2+3​),ζ∗​=−2.​
(46)
It lies on the left boundary. There are no singularities in the open half-strip. The points ζ=±1, where f′ diverges, map to the bottom corners; G is holomorphic through those corners since 2+sinz=0 there.
For the principal logarithm, the boundary segment z=−π/2+iy, y≥arcosh2, is the preimage of its cut ζ≤−2. Points on that cut above z∗​ admit local analytic continuation after changing branch; z∗​ itself is the genuine logarithmic branch point.
