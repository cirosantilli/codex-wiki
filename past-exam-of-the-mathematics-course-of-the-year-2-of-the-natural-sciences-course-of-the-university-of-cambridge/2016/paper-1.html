A complex function is holomorphic at a point if it is complex differentiable throughout some open neighbourhood of that point. Writing ζ=ξ+iη and ψ=P+iQ, a convenient sufficient and necessary criterion under continuously differentiable real components is the Cauchy-Riemann equations
Pξ​=Qη​,Pη​=−Qξ​.​

For the normalized second-order equation, x0​ is an ordinary point if both coefficient functions p and q are holomorphic near x0​. If it is singular but (x−x0​)p(x) and (x−x0​)2q(x) are holomorphic there, it is a regular singular point. Thus p may have at most a simple pole and q at most a double pole.
The distinction tells us which local series method is justified. At an ordinary point, two independent solutions are represented by ordinary Taylor series. At a regular singular point, the Frobenius method uses (x−x0​)r∑n≥0​an​(x−x0​)n; the indicial equation determines the exponents, and logarithmic terms may appear in a second solution. Singularities worse than these are irregular and need not have this Frobenius form.

There is an important exception to an unconditional radius-one answer. If k2=m2 for an integer m≥0, the series of parity m terminates at degree m and is a multiple of a Chebyshev polynomial. Its radius of convergence is R=∞​; the other independent parity solution still has radius one. For m=0 the even solution is constant. Any nonzero combination containing the nonterminating solution has radius one. These are the full power series of the Chebyshev differential equation, including the terminating cases.

δF=N⟨δϕ,(H−F[ϕ])ϕ⟩+⟨(H−F[ϕ])ϕ,δϕ⟩​=N2Re⟨δϕ,(H−F[ϕ])ϕ⟩​.
(68)

F[ψn​+η]−En​=1+2Re⟨ψn​,η⟩+∥η∥2⟨η,(H−En​)η⟩​.

(H−F[ϕ])ϕ=0at a stationary ϕ.​
