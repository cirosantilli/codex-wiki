Use the ordinary real inner product ⟨u,v⟩=∫ab​uvdx on the domain of functions obeying the two separated boundary conditions. Integration by parts gives the Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator
⟨u,Lv⟩−⟨Lu,v⟩=[p(vu′−uv′)]ab​.
(1)
At a, the two boundary-value vectors (u(a),u′(a)) and (v(a),v′(a)) satisfy the same homogeneous linear relation. If A2​=0, both function values vanish; if A2​=0, their derivatives are the same constant multiple −A1​/A2​ of their values. Either way vu′−uv′=0. The same argument applies at b. Hence
⟨u,Lv⟩=⟨Lu,v⟩.​
(2)
This proves symmetry, and the regular separated Sturm-Liouville problem has the same adjoint domain, so L is self-adjoint. To see the domain point, require the boundary form to vanish against every admissible u: the independently variable endpoint data force an adjoint-domain function to obey those same endpoint relations.
As usual, each endpoint pair (A1​,A2​) and (B1​,B2​) must be nonzero, and the coefficients must have the regularity needed for these operations. An identically zero pair would impose no condition at that endpoint and would not give the claimed self-adjoint operator. The related operator ω−1L is self-adjoint in the weighted inner product ∫ab​ωuvdx.

The only nonzero angular Fourier mode is cos(2σ). Its radial equation is T′′−4T=0. A solution vanishing at τ=a is proportional to sinh[2(τ−a)]; making its value one at b fixes the constant. The Dirichlet problem between bipolar coordinate circles therefore gives

The quotient group G/N consists of the cosets gN, with multiplication
(gN)(hN)=(gh)N.​

The four displayed matrices fail closure: for example σx​σy​=iσz​ is not one of them. Thus they do not form a group by themselves.
Let σ0​=I and μ4​={1,−1,i,−i}. The enlarged set is the single-qubit Pauli group
P1​={ησa​:η∈μ4​, a∈{0,x,y,z}}.​
(62)
There are 16 distinct matrices: I,σx​,σy​,σz​ are linearly independent, so two different ones cannot be scalar multiples, and the four phases are distinct for each nonzero matrix.
The Pauli matrices satisfy
σa​σb​=δab​I+iϵabc​σc​(a,b∈{x,y,z}),σa2​=I.
(63)
Here δ is the Kronecker delta and ϵ the Levi-Civita symbol. Thus any product of two Pauli matrices is another Pauli matrix or the identity times a phase in μ4​. Multiplying by the two original phases preserves membership in μ4​, proving closure of all 16 matrices.
Associativity is inherited from matrix multiplication, the identity is I, and
(ησa​)−1=η−1σa​∈P1​.
(64)
This proves every group axiom. The resulting group is non-Abelian, since reversing σx​σy​ changes its sign.

For every positive integer m, the group laws give (g−1)m=(gm)−1. Consequently
gm=e⟺(g−1)m=e.
(65)
The sets of positive exponents returning the two elements to the identity element are identical. If this set is nonempty, its least member is the order of a group element in both cases. If it is empty, neither element has finite order. Therefore, including the infinite-order case,
ord(g−1)=ord(g).​
(66)
For a direct divisibility argument in the finite case, if g has order n, then (g−1)n=e, so its inverse’s order divides n. Reversing the roles shows n divides that order, forcing equality. This is the general fact that an element and its inverse have the same order; it also reflects ⟨g⟩=⟨g−1⟩ as cyclic subgroups.
