Huber location response to one outlier (T(M))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Robust statistics Huber location estimator
OurBigBook.com Words: 213
Hold (0,1,2,3,4) fixed and move only the sixth observation M. For M≥5, the sample mean is (10+M)/6, the sample median is 2.5, and the Huber location estimator with fixed s=1,k=2 stays at 2.5. Once M−2.5≥2, its Huber score is already 2; moving it farther changes the Huber loss but leaves the root unchanged.
For a closer view of the transition, the exact Huber location estimator over 0≤M≤40 is
T(M)={(8+M)/5,2.5,​0≤M≤4.5,4.5≤M≤40.​
(22)
To verify the first branch, when 0≤M≤2 the score of 4 is saturated and the other five scores sum with it to 8+M−5θ. When 2≤M≤4.5, the score of 0 is saturated instead, giving the same expression. At M=4.5, the moving observation saturates too, and the equation becomes 10−4θ=0. The piecewise formula is continuous at the transition.
The rising sample mean comes from an unbounded linear score, the flat distant-outlier response from a bounded Huber score. This is a finite-data response curve with a specified scale and threshold, rather than a population influence function or a general claim about arbitrary numbers of outliers.
