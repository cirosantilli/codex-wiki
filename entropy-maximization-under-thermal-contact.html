Entropy maximization under thermal contact (∂Stot​/∂E1​=1/T1​−1/T2​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermal equilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 340
At fixed volumes and material inventories, two internally equilibrated bodies that exchange only heat within an isolated system have fixed total internal energy E=E1​+E2​. Their total entropy is
Stot​(E1​)=S1​(E1​)+S2​(E−E1​).
(20)
Using 1/Ti​=(∂Si​/∂Ei​)Vi​,Ni​​, an interior stationary point obeys
dE1​dStot​​=T1​1​−T2​1​=0.
(21)
Thus entropy stationarity requires matching thermodynamic temperatures. Positive values of heat capacity at constant volume make this a strict local maximum because
dE12​d2Stot​​=−C1​T12​1​−C2​T22​1​<0.
(22)
For bodies with positive heat capacities throughout the accessible range, this concavity gives at most one interior maximum. This is the stability check behind the equal-temperature condition; see entropy concavity and positive heat capacity. [ref]
For thermal equilibration of two finite bodies, entropy from heat capacity gives
ΔStot​=C1​lnT1,0​Tf​​+C2​lnT2,0​Tf​​=(ln300375​+3ln400375​)kJK−1≃0.02952799kJK−1>0.
(23)
Body 1 gains about 0.22314355kJK−1 of entropy, while body 2 loses about 0.19361556kJK−1. Total energy is conserved even though total entropy increases. The entropy calculation uses reversible comparison paths between the endpoint states; actual exchange across a finite temperature difference is irreversible.
To construct the plot, vary T1​ while enforcing the energy constraint, rather than holding T2​ fixed. In numerical units of kelvin, T2​/K=(1500−T1​/K)/3, and the plotted function is
S(T1​)=C1​ln300KT1​​+C2​ln400KT2​(T1​)​,dT1​dT2​​=−C2​C1​​.
(24)
Here S means Stot​−Stot,initial​, so its zero is a reference choice. The chain rule yields
S′(T1​)=T1​C1​​−T2​C1​​,S′′(T1​)=−T12​C1​​−C2​T22​C12​​<0.
(25)
The unique maximum is at T1​=T2​=375K. The physical trajectory starts at T1​=300K and moves toward that maximum; the rest of the curve compares other possible energy partitions, not later times in the trajectory.
The contact model also proves the direction of change directly:
S˙tot​=G(T2​−T1​)(T1​1​−T2​1​)=T1​T2​G(T2​−T1​)2​≥0.
(26)
For G>0 and positive temperatures, equality holds exactly at thermal equilibrium. The appealing link is that a dynamical heat transfer law moves the system uphill on the constrained entropy curve, while conservation of energy keeps it on that curve. This predicts both why the endpoint is stable and why the reverse separation into hot and cold bodies cannot happen spontaneously in the isolated system.
