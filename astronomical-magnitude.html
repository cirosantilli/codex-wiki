Astronomical magnitude

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 21
Astronomical magnitude expresses brightness on a logarithmic scale: equal steps in astronomical magnitude represent equal ratios of received radiative flux. The scale runs backwards, so a brighter source has a smaller astronomical magnitude. A difference of five magnitudes means exactly a factor of 100 in flux; one magnitude means a factor of 1001/5≃2.511886, rather than exactly 2.5. This compresses the enormous range of astronomical brightnesses into manageable numbers. [ref].
Apparent magnitude describes received brightness, while absolute magnitude compares sources at a shared reference distance. Both require a specified photometric passband and magnitude zero point. A magnitude difference turns a ratio of positive radiative fluxes into a difference of numbers; it does not measure an additive change in emitted luminosity.
