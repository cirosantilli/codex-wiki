Virtual image

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Image formation
OurBigBook.com Words: 660 Articles: 1
A virtual image is the apparent origin of an outgoing diverging bundle: the backward extensions of its light rays intersect at the image point. Those extensions are geometric constructions, not backward-travelling light. A screen placed there cannot intercept the outgoing bundle to display that virtual image. An eye or a second optical lens receiving the actual outgoing light rays can nevertheless form a subsequent real image. “Virtual” describes this imaging stage, not whether the image can be seen or photographed. [ref].
For a converging thin lens with 0<u<f, the same equations give v<0 and m=f/(f−u)>1: the virtual image is upright and enlarged. For the illustrative choice f=10cm, u=7.5cm, ho​=0.30cm,
v=−30cm,m=4,hi​=1.20cm.
(19)
The image appears farther from the optical lens than the object. A diverging thin lens with a real object instead has v<0 and 0<m<1, so a virtual image need not be enlarged. A plane mirror provides another virtual image, located behind the reflecting surface by backward extensions of the reflected light rays. [ref].
For a direct viewing test, bring the target to u=7.5cm, search for a sharp screen image on the outgoing side, then remove the screen and look through the optical lens toward the target. Expect an upright enlarged view if the eye receives the bundle and can focus. Record the eye position: 5cm beyond the optical lens gives a predicted apparent distance 35cm. This adapts the Northern Virginia laboratory's comparison. [ref].
For a position measurement, use parallax localization of a virtual image. Place a movable pencil tip or narrow comparison marker beside the apparent image, and view that marker directly beside or above the optical lens, while viewing the image through it. Move the head a little sideways and move the marker along the bench until relative lateral motion vanishes. Read its coordinate xm​; the estimate is v=xm​<0. Deliberately place the marker nearer and farther to check that relative motion reverses on opposite sides of the match. This follows Orhan Aytür's lens laboratory, which uses a pencil to locate a virtual image. [ref].
Keep target and optical lens fixed, use one eye and the central image detail, and ensure the marker itself is not viewed through the optical lens. A marker blocked by the target, or a head movement that loses the bundle through the aperture, invalidates that trial. Repeat the match from both directions and record its depth range. This measures apparent depth; comparing apparent sizes at different depths does not directly measure transverse magnification. Near u=f, apparent depth becomes very large and the match is poorly constrained. A failed screen search alone therefore needs the distance check and the viewing test.
