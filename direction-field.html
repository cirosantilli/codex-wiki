Direction field (y′=f(t,y))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 183
A direction field for y′=f(t,y) places a short segment with slope f(t,y) at each sampled point where f is defined. A function graph of a solution of a differential equation follows these segments: its tangent line has exactly the prescribed slope. Segment length is a drawing choice, rather than a speed; arrows, if used, point toward increasing t. An isocline collects points with one common slope.
For Newton's law of cooling, y′=−0.1(y−20) with time in minutes and temperature in degrees Celsius, the slopes at y=80,40,20,10 are respectively −6,−2,0,1 degrees Celsius per minute. Each horizontal row is an isocline, because this autonomous differential equation has no explicit time dependence. Above 20 the solutions of a differential equation descend, below 20 they rise, and y=20 is an equilibrium point.
The highlighted initial condition y(0)=80 selects one curve from the family. The picture reveals approach to the equilibrium point before any algebra is done. Under the hypotheses of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem, distinct function graphs of solutions cannot cross or even meet in the time-state plane. A direction field alone does not guarantee uniqueness: several curves can share a tangent line at the same point when those hypotheses fail. See the delayed-start example under initial value problem.
This geometric interpretation is developed in Direction Fields, Isoclines, Integral Curves.
