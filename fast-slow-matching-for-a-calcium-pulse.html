Fast-slow matching for a calcium pulse (UX​=s[U−UB​+γ(V−VB​)],sVX​=f(U,V))

 Home Biology Calcium in biology Calcium wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
In a two-variable calcium-wave model, the fast front scale can reduce to the displayed dynamical system. Its equilibria are intersections of the kinetic nullcline V=g(U) and a line of slope −1/γ. At a simple intersection with fV​<0, the Jacobian determinant is fV​(1+γg′); a negative determinant identifies a saddle point. Matching a rising front requires a heteroclinic orbit from one saddle to another, selected by a shooting method in the speed s. A falling connection must use the same speed. Between fronts the slow equation is s(1+γg′)U′=μ−U. Recovery on the two outer rising branches requires the resting value μ to lie between the low landing value and the high takeoff value. The nullcline shape supplies this geometry but does not by itself prove existence of both connections.
