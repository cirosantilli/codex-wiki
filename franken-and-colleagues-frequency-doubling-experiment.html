Franken and colleagues frequency-doubling experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Nonlinear optics Second-harmonic generation
OurBigBook.com Words: 178
In 1961, Peter Alden Franken, Alan Hill, C. Wilbur Peters and Gabriel Weinreich focused a pulsed ruby laser through a red optical filter into quartz. The filter suppressed xenon-flash background. A quartz-prism spectrograph used red-insensitive Eastman Type 103 plates. A single shot recorded a feature near 347.2nm from 694.3nm input. Removing the quartz or substituting glass removed the feature; changing polarization of waves and orientation followed their predicted pattern. These controls supported second-harmonic generation; photographic density gave an estimated yield.[ref]
A spectrograph turns output frequency into a recording position. Its spectral separation makes a faint new component distinguishable from bright input light. The reported rounded wavelengths do not constitute an independent precision measurement of the ratio two. Nor do these observations demonstrate perfect phase matching or complete conversion. The experiment and Nicolaas Bloembergen's later theoretical account are distinct contributions to nonlinear optics.
