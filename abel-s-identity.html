Abel's identity (W(x)=W(x0​)exp(−∫x0​x​p(s)ds))

 ... Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Linear ordinary differential equation Second-order linear differential equation Wronskian
OurBigBook.com Words: 192
For y′′+py′+qy=0, the Wronskian satisfies W′=−pW.
More precisely, let y1​,y2​ be solutions of a differential equation for the same homogeneous linear differential equation
y′′+p(x)y′+q(x)y=0
(18)
on an interval I where p,q are continuous functions. For any x0​∈I, Abel's identity gives
W(y1​,y2​)(x)=W(y1​,y2​)(x0​)exp(−∫x0​x​p(s)ds).
(19)
Only the coefficient of y′ enters the evolution of the Wronskian. In an unnormalized equation A(x)y′′+B(x)y′+C(x)y=0, first use p=B/A on an interval where A=0 and the coefficient ratios are continuous functions.[ref]
The proof uses cancellation. Differentiating the Wronskian by the product rule and substituting the common equation gives
W′​=(y1​y2′​−y1′​y2​)′=y1​y2′′​−y1′′​y2​=y1​(−py2′​−qy2​)−(−py1′​−qy1​)y2​=−pW.​
(20)
Multiplying this first-order linear differential equation by the integrating factor μ(x)=exp(∫x0​x​p(s)ds) gives (μW)′=0. Since μ(x0​)=1, the constant is W(x0​), proving Abel's identity. This argument also covers W(x0​)=0 without dividing by W.
The exponential function is positive for real p, so a real Wronskian either vanishes everywhere or has a fixed nonzero sign throughout I. In the preceding example, p=−1 and W(0)=3 give W(x)=3ex immediately. If p=0, every Wronskian of two solutions of a differential equation for that same equation is constant, regardless of q. Thus Abel's identity can certify linear independence and find the denominator used in variation of parameters without explicit formulas for both solutions of a differential equation.
