Vanishing two-to-one forcing coefficient for Stokes convection (β=0)

 ... Branches of physics Dynamical systems Bifurcation theory Amplitude equation Landau amplitude equation Spatially forced convection amplitude equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 78
In the free-slip Stokes flow temperature model, let the critical temperature be g=sinπz and vertical velocity f=sg, s=kc2​+π2. A forced positive second harmonic (W,Θ)e2ikc​x couples to the negative critical harmonic. Its temperature solvability condition has integrand
g(2f′Θ+fΘ′+W′g/2+Wg′)=(sg2Θ+g2W/2)′.
(44)
The identity follows by substituting f=sg and differentiating. Its integral is zero because g vanishes on both plates, regardless of the forced boundary value of Θ. Thus the first resonant forcing coefficient vanishes. A generic nonzero conjugate-amplitude term cannot be inferred from wave-number matching alone.
