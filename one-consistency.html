One-consistency (1-consistency)

 ... Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Mathematical logic First-order logic First-order theory Soundness of an arithmetic theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 115 Articles: 1
An arithmetic theory is one-consistent if it does not prove ∃xδ(x) while proving every ¬δ(n), for a bounded arithmetic formula δ and all standard natural numbers n. If the theory proves all true bounded sentences, this is equivalent to Sigma-1 soundness: a false existential theorem has every standard instance refuted by bounded completeness; conversely a true witness and its provable negation contradict the assumption of a consistent first-order theory.
