Respiratory control ratio (RCR=vADP​/vafter​)

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Energy metabolism Electron transport chain Respiratory control
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
The respiratory control ratio divides the oxygen consumption rate during ADP-stimulated respiration by that after supplied ADP has been used. It compares reaction rates, not ATP formed per oxygen.[ref]
A large ratio means strong stimulation under the comparison's conditions; it is not a universal measure of all mitochondrial functions.
For a constructed example, choose constant suspension volume V=2mL and oxygen concentration declines of 0.030 and 0.006mmolL−1min−1 during the two comparison windows. Assume no gas exchange or dilution and a correctly calibrated readout. A derivative converts each concentration slope into an amount of substance consumption rate:
v=−VdtdcO2​​​,vADP​=60nmolmin−1,vafter​=12nmolmin−1,RCR=5.
(15)
The common volume cancels in the ratio, so dividing the positive slope magnitudes gives the same answer. These are chosen inputs, not the 1955 observations. If an uncorrected influx of 3nmolmin−1 of oxygen occurred in both windows, the apparent rates would instead be 57 and 9, giving 19/3≈6.33. The same absolute error can inflate a ratio because it removes a larger fraction of its smaller denominator.
