Optical trap drag calibration (k⟨x⟩=ζu)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical tweezers Harmonic optical trap
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
Translate the liquid past a bead held in a stationary harmonic optical trap. At steady mean position, take u as the local fluid velocity relative to the fixed trap and x positive downstream. Averaging the overdamped force balance gives
0=−k⟨x⟩+ζu,⟨x⟩=kζ​u,k=d⟨x⟩/duζ​.
(33)
For the invented optical trap Langevin calibration example, u=50.0μm/s predicts ⟨x⟩=23.562nm, V−V0​=0.23562V, and ζu=k⟨x⟩=0.47124pN. Reversing u reverses the mean shift but leaves the ideal thermal variance unchanged. A symmetric speed sweep tests the offset and the linear force range; the slope determines ζ/k if position is calibrated.
Near a wall, ζ=6πηa is not the appropriate drag without a boundary correction; record bead height and direction of motion. Stage velocity must correspond to the local fluid velocity after transients have settled. Escape under increasing flow estimates a maximum holding force in that geometry, whereas small-displacement drag calibration estimates the local spring constant. They are different quantities. The atomic force microscopy similarly needs a calibrated spring constant to convert a measured deflection to force, but its cantilever is a physical spring rather than this optical field.
