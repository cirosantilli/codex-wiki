Primitive Fermat bound from abc (zn−7/2≤C1/6​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Diophantine equation abc conjecture
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
For a primitive positive solution of xn+yn=zn with n≥4, the three powers are pairwise coprime and their radical is at most xyz<z3. The abc conjecture with ϵ=1/6 gives the displayed inequality, hence z≤C1/62​ uniformly in n. The inequality zn≤2max(x,y)n then bounds n. Thus there are finitely many primitive triples and exponents. Homogeneity would produce infinitely many unscaled solutions from any one nonzero solution, so a primitive arithmetic height bound alone must not be described as finite many arbitrary multiples.
