Greedy two-approximation for maximum-weight matching (w(Mopt​)≤2w(Mgreedy​))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Matching (graph theory) Maximum-weight matching
OurBigBook.com Words: 151
For nonnegative weights on a finite graph, scan edges in nonincreasing weight order, selecting each when it shares no endpoint with an already selected edge. This greedy matching is a factor-two approximation algorithm for maximum-weight matching.
Proof. Charge each optimal edge to itself if selected, and otherwise to an earlier selected edge that blocked it. The receiving edge weighs at least as much. Each selected edge receives at most two charges, since its two endpoints each meet at most one optimal edge. Summing proves the bound. Sorting takes O(∣E∣log∣E∣) time, followed by a scan that records which vertices are used.
The factor is sharp even for strictly positive weights. On a four-vertex path graph with consecutive weights 1,1+ε,1, where 0<ε<1, the greedy matching weighs 1+ε and the optimal matching in a graph weighs two. Their ratio tends to two as ε↓0. For infinitely many sizes, use k disjoint copies with ε=1/k, k≥2. This example isolates the obstruction: a locally attractive edge can block two compatible edges.
