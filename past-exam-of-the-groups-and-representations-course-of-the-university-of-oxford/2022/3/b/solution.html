Write X=(AC​BD​). Commuting with all diag(U1​,U2​) requires
AU1​=U1​A,DU2​=U2​D,BU2​=U1​B,CU1​=U2​C.
(27)
For n1​,n2​≥2, the defining complex representations of each factor are irreducible. Schur lemma gives A=zIn1​​ and D=wIn2​​. The two block vector spaces are inequivalent representations of the product group, even when n1​=n2​: the first factor acts nontrivially on just the first block. Thus their off-diagonal intertwiners vanish, B=C=0. Unitarity and the unit determinant give
CG​(H)={diag(zIn1​​,wIn2​​):∣z∣=∣w∣=1, zn1​wn2​=1}.​
(28)
The assertion that this full group is always a circle needs correction. Put d=gcd(n1​,n2​), a=n1​/d, b=n2​/d. The continuous group homomorphism
(z,w)⟼zawb
(29)
maps this centralizer onto the dth roots of unity. Its kernel is the identity component of a Lie group, parametrized injectively by eiθ↦(eibθ,e−iaθ). Coprimality of a,b proves injectivity; a Bezout identity proves that this parametrization exhausts the kernel. Extending the primitive integer row (a,b) to a determinant-one integer matrix gives coordinates in which the constraint is simply td=1. Consequently
CG​(H)≅U(1)×Zd​,CG​(H)0≅U(1).​
(30)
For a concrete counterexample, take n1​=n2​=2. The constraint is (zw)2=1, giving two separate circles zw=1 and zw=−1. The element diag(I2​,−I2​) lies in the second component, so this centralizer cannot be isomorphic as a Lie group to the connected circle group.
For the intended specialization of this centralizer of two special unitary blocks, d=gcd(2,3)=1 and therefore the full centralizer really is a circle group:
Uc​(1)={eiθXc​:θ∈R/2πZ},Xc​=diag(3,3,−2,−2,−2).​
(31)
We use this primitive integer U(1) charge normalization below. If exactly one ni​ equals 1 and the other is at least 2, the same block-scalar formula still holds. If n1​=n2​=1, both factors are trivial and the centralizer is all of SU(2), another exception to the printed claim.
