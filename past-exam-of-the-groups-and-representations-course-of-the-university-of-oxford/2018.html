A group is a nonempty set G with an operation G×G→G, (g,h)↦gh, satisfying associativity, an identity element e with eg=ge=g, and an inverse element g−1 with gg−1=g−1g=e for every g. These are the group axioms. An Abelian group additionally satisfies gh=hg for every pair. A subgroup is a subset that is itself a group under the restricted operation; equivalently, it is nonempty and closed under hk−1.
Let R:G→GL(V) be a nonzero, finite-dimensional complex irreducible representation of an Abelian group. For each fixed g, commutativity gives
R(g)R(h)=R(h)R(g)(h∈G).
(1)
Thus R(g) is an intertwining operator from R to itself. By Schur's lemma over the complex numbers, R(g)=λg​IV​. Every line in V is therefore an invariant subspace. Since R is irreducible, this forces
dimC​V=1.​
(2)
This is the finite-dimensional convention used throughout the paper. Without it, the literal assertion for arbitrary Abelian groups needs qualification. A concrete counterexample is G=C(t)× acting by multiplication on the complex vector space V=C(t) of rational functions. Any nonzero invariant complex vector subspace containing f contains gf for every nonzero rational g, hence contains every rational function. This is an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation. The scalar conclusion of Schur's lemma used above depends on finite dimensionality.

In the complexification of a Lie algebra, define
L1​=iK1​,L2​=iK2​,L3​=J.
(32)
The relations in a become
[L1​,L2​]=L3​,[L2​,L3​]=L1​,[L3​,L1​]=L2​.

The special unitary Lie algebra su(2) has real basis Si​=−iσi​/2, where the Pauli matrices satisfy [σi​,σj​]=2iϵijk​σk​. Its commutators are the same: [Si​,Sj​]=ϵijk​Sk​. The complex-linear map Li​↦Si​ therefore preserves the bracket and maps a basis to a basis. Consequently
so(1,2)C​≅su(2)C​≅sl2​(C).​
(34)
The real Lie algebras are not isomorphic: multiplying the boosts by i changes their real form. For example, the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra evaluated at K1​ has real eigenvalues 0,1,−1, whereas the corresponding nonzero compact SU(2) generators have purely imaginary adjoint eigenvalues.
Every complex Lie algebra representation of the real algebra extends uniquely by complex linearity to its complexification of a Lie algebra. The finite-dimensional complex irreducible representations are therefore classified by a nonnegative integer highest weight m, just as for SU(2). There is one equivalence class for each
m=0,1,2,…,dimVm​=m+1=2j+1,j=m/2.​
(35)
These are statements about Lie algebra representations. They do not assert positive-definite unitarity for the noncompact real form, or automatic descent to the full Lorentz group. In particular, half-integer j gives a sign under a 2π spatial rotation, so its integration needs a covering group.

The spinor basis in c is compatible with the supplied gamma matrices. The rotation generator r(J) is anti-Hermitian and commutes with γ0​=−iσ1​. A colour triplet has the color charge transformation law of a quark, an antitriplet that of an antiquark, and a colour singlet is colour-neutral, as a lepton is. Thus both parent representations permit quark-like and antiquark-like colour sectors; the vector has one neutral sector and the spinor two. These are colour assignments only. They do not determine particle spin, statistics, electric charge, weak quantum numbers or a full generation of the Standard Model. In a real parent field the conjugate colour sectors may describe one complex colour field and its conjugate, rather than independent particle species.
Global group qualification. The printed chain is correct at the Lie algebra level, su(3)⊂su(4)≅so(6)⊂so(7). Globally its simply connected form is SU(3)⊂SU(4)≅Spin(6)⊂Spin(7). The standard map of SU(4) into SO(7) through 6⊕1 has a nontrivial kernel: the concrete central element −I4​ acts as +I6​ on Λ24 and trivially on the singlet representation. Its image is SO(6)≅SU(4)/{±I4​}, rather than an injective copy of SU(4) under this map.
The global form obstruction for the Spin7 spinor is explicit: the central element −1 of the Spin group Spin(7) maps to the identity in SO(7) but acts as −I8​ on the eight-dimensional spin representation. Equivalently −I4​ acts as −I on both 4 and 4 in its restriction. Therefore the spinor does not descend to a genuine SO(7) group representation. A theory containing that spinor needs the Spin group global form, or must understand the source's notation as specifying only its Lie algebra. The displayed colour branchings remain valid with this correction.
