A subgroup N≤G is a normal subgroup if gNg−1=N for every g∈G. A group homomorphism f:G→G satisfies f(gh)=f(g)f(h) for every g,h∈G. In particular f(e)=e and f(g−1)=f(g)−1. A group representation on a vector space V is a group homomorphism R:G→GL(V) into the general linear group of invertible linear maps on V.
For the kernel of a group homomorphism, put K=kerf={g:f(g)=e}. It contains e, and if k,ℓ∈K then f(kℓ−1)=f(k)f(ℓ)−1=e, so K is a subgroup. For g∈G and k∈K,
f(gkg−1)=f(g)f(k)f(g)−1=e.
(1)
Thus gKg−1⊆K. Applying this inclusion with g−1 gives the reverse inclusion, proving that kerf is a normal subgroup.
For a normal subgroup N, define multiplication of left cosets by
(gN)(hN)=(gh)N.​
(2)
To check independence of representatives, replace g,h by gn,hm, with n,m∈N. Then
(gn)(hm)=gh(h−1nh)m∈ghN,
(3)
because normality gives h−1nh∈N. Hence the operation is well-defined. Associativity follows from (gh)k=g(hk) in G; the identity is N=eN; and the inverse of gN is g−1N. These verify the group axioms, giving the quotient group G/N. The map g↦gN is a surjective group homomorphism with kernel of a group homomorphism equal to N.

Nonzero scalar matrices form a subgroup N of the general linear group, since products and inverses remain nonzero scalar matrices. Moreover, for every invertible g,
g(λIn​)g−1=λIn​.
(4)
Thus N lies in the center of a group and is a normal subgroup. The quotient group is the complex projective linear group PGLn​(C), whose elements identify invertible matrices differing by a nonzero scalar.
The canonical group homomorphism π:g↦[g] obeys [g][h]=[gh]. Composing it with the given group representation gives
R(gh)=[R(gh)]=[R(g)R(h)]=[R(g)][R(h)]=R(g)R(h).
(5)
Consequently
R=π∘R:G⟶PGLn​(C) is a group homomorphism.​
(6)
This is the projective representation associated with R: a nonzero scalar acts trivially on each line in the projective space of V. Its kernel of a group homomorphism consists of those g for which R(g) is scalar, which can be larger than the kernel of a group homomorphism of R itself.

First the group homomorphism is surjective. For any W∈U(n), choose z∈U(1) with zn=detW and set U=z−1W. Then U is unitary and detU=z−ndetW=1, so W=f(z,U).

K={(ζk,ζ−kIn​):k=0,1,…,n−1}≅Zn​.​
(27)

U(n)≅(U(1)×SU(n))/K.​
(28)
Explicitly the isomorphism sends the coset [(z,U)] to zU. The n possible choices of the root z differ precisely by multiplication by elements of K, which explains why the product before quotienting is not a unique factorization.
