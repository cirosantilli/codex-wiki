A group is a nonempty set G with an operation G×G→G, (g,h)↦gh, satisfying associativity, an identity element e with eg=ge=g, and an inverse element g−1 with gg−1=g−1g=e for every g. These are the group axioms. An Abelian group additionally satisfies gh=hg for every pair. A subgroup is a subset that is itself a group under the restricted operation; equivalently, it is nonempty and closed under hk−1.
Let R:G→GL(V) be a nonzero, finite-dimensional complex irreducible representation of an Abelian group. For each fixed g, commutativity gives
R(g)R(h)=R(h)R(g)(h∈G).
(1)
Thus R(g) is an intertwining operator from R to itself. By Schur's lemma over the complex numbers, R(g)=λg​IV​. Every line in V is therefore an invariant subspace. Since R is irreducible, this forces
dimC​V=1.​
(2)
This is the finite-dimensional convention used throughout the paper. Without it, the literal assertion for arbitrary Abelian groups needs qualification. A concrete counterexample is G=C(t)× acting by multiplication on the complex vector space V=C(t) of rational functions. Any nonzero invariant complex vector subspace containing f contains gf for every nonzero rational g, hence contains every rational function. This is an infinite-dimensional irreducible representation. The scalar conclusion of Schur's lemma used above depends on finite dimensionality.
