A group representation on a vector space V over a field K is a group homomorphism R:G→GL(V). In particular, R(gh)=R(g)R(h), R(e)=I and R(g−1)=R(g)−1. Choosing a basis identifies it with a matrix representation.
Two group representations R on V and S on W are equivalent representations when an invertible linear map T:V→W satisfies TR(g)=S(g)T for every g. Thus S(g)=TR(g)T−1; they differ only by a choice of basis.
A nonzero group representation is a reducible representation if there is a vector subspace U with 0=U=V and R(g)U⊆U for every g. It is an irreducible representation if it has no such invariant subspace. Reducibility alone does not assert the existence of an invariant complementary vector subspace; that stronger conclusion says that the representation is a semisimple representation.
The Schur lemma states that an intertwiner T between irreducible representations is either zero or an isomorphism. Consequently an intertwiner between inequivalent irreducible representations is zero. For a finite-dimensional irreducible representation over the complex numbers, every commuting endomorphism is scalar:
TR(g)=R(g)T(∀g)⟹T=λI.​
(1)
For the first assertion, the kernel and image of T are invariant subspaces, so a nonzero T is injective and surjective. For the scalar conclusion, choose an eigenvalue λ of T. The intertwiner T−λI has a nonzero kernel, so it must vanish. The finite-dimensional complex hypothesis matters: the scalar conclusion need not hold over the real numbers, as a real planar rotation through π/2 is an irreducible representation of the cyclic group of order four, while the rotation itself is a commuting nonscalar endomorphism.

The commuting direction in solution provides the correct candidate Lie algebra for a gauge group, su(3)c​⊕su(2)L​⊕u(1)Y​. This is a necessary structural condition for the Standard Model, but it does not by itself establish a viable grand unified theory.
The representations in solution do not give a Standard Model fermion generation with the correct weak hypercharges. Use Qelectric​=T3​+Y. Identifying (3,2)1​ with the left-handed quark doublet, whose weak hypercharge is 1/6, fixes Y=X/6. But then the doublet in (1,3) has Y=1/6, whereas the left-handed lepton doublet requires Y=−1/2. Its singlet has Y=−1/3, whereas a right-handed electron requires Y=−1. The (3,1)0​ is a further concrete mismatch: right-handed quarks written as left-handed antiquarks require Y=−2/3 or 1/3, and a rescaling cannot turn zero into either value. These comparisons use the electroweak representation and hypercharge table.
The charge-−3 doublet in (1,8) does have the required lepton weak hypercharge under this normalization, but the same Adjoint representation also brings a doublet of the opposite charge and an extra triplet. Therefore merely adding that representation does not supply the missing chiral generation without additional states and a mechanism for their masses.
Furthermore SU(3)×SU(3) is a product of two Simple Lie groups, permitting two independent gauge couplings; it does not enforce a common strong and electroweak gauge coupling. Thus the embedding exists, but the displayed matter content does not furnish a viable minimal unification of the Standard Model. A more elaborate model would require additional representations, consistent chiral matter, anomaly cancellation, and a specified spontaneous symmetry breaking mechanism. The calculations here do not prove that every possible extension using this product group is impossible.

Its coefficient matrix is traceless and has squared norm one. In particular, ccˉ by itself is not a vector in the traceless 15; its coefficient matrix has trace one.
To relate these two descriptions, let S4​=(uuˉ+ddˉ+ssˉ+ccˉ)/2 be the normalized SU(4) singlet and S3​=(uuˉ+ddˉ+ssˉ)/3​ the old SU(3) singlet. Then
S3​=23​​S4​+21​η15​,ccˉ=21​S4​−23​​η15​.​
(41)
This orthonormal change of basis shows explicitly why the complete tensor product contains two light-flavour singlets but adds only one state to the old light-flavour singlet sector.
