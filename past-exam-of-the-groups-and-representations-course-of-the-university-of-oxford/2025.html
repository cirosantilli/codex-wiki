A group representation on a vector space V is a group homomorphism R:G→GL(V), so R(gh)=R(g)R(h) and R(e)=I. A nonzero group representation is an irreducible representation if its only invariant subspaces are 0 and V. Here the representations and characters are finite-dimensional over the complex numbers.
The character of a representation is χR​(g)=trR(g). Three useful properties are:
  • It is a class function: χR​(hgh−1)=χR​(g), by the cyclic property of the trace.
  • It is additive on a direct sum and multiplicative on a tensor product of group representations: χR⊕S​=χR​+χS​ and χR⊗S​=χR​χS​.
  • The irreducible characters obey character orthogonality:
    ⟨χr​,χs​⟩=∣G∣1​∑g∈G​χr​(g)∗χs​(g)=δrs​.
    (1)
In particular, χR​(e)=dimV and χR​(g−1)=χR​(g)∗, the latter following from unitarization of a finite-group representation. The character inner product ⟨χr​,χR​⟩ counts the multiplicity of an irreducible representation r in R.

R(h)Pv=∣G∣1​∑g∈G​R(hg)v=∣G∣1​∑k∈G​R(k)v=Pv.
(2)

For a curve O(t)=I+tX+O(t2) in the special orthogonal group, differentiate O(t)TO(t)=I at the identity. This gives XT+X=0. Conversely, the matrix exponential of any real skew-symmetric matrix is orthogonal and has determinant detetX=ettrX=1. Hence
so(N)={X∈MN​(R):XT=−X},[X,Y]=XY−YX.​
(14)
A basis is Lij​=Eij​−Eji​ for 1≤i<j≤N, where Eij​ is a matrix unit. If Lji​=−Lij​ and Lii​=0, their Lie brackets are
[Lij​,Lkl​]=δjk​Lil​−δjl​Lik​−δik​Ljl​+δil​Ljk​.
(15)

dimso(N)=2N(N−1)​,rankSO(N)=⌊2N​⌋.​
(16)
The maximality follows by simultaneously decomposing commuting real skew-symmetric matrices into rotation two-planes, with at most one remaining fixed axis.

The unbroken group is the stabilizer subgroup of the vacuum expectation value. For a nonzero vector vacuum expectation value, an orthogonal transformation puts ⟨ϕ⟩=veN​. A special orthogonal transformation fixing it has the block form diag(A,1) with A∈SO(N−1). Consequently
SO(N)⟶SO(N−1)(v=0).​
(17)
The zero vacuum expectation value leaves the entire special orthogonal group unbroken.
For a real symmetric tensor write its vacuum expectation value as a real symmetric matrix Q. The transformation is Q↦OQOT. The spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices gives an orthonormal basis in which
Q=diag(q1​Im1​​,…,qp​Imp​​),qi​=qj​ (i=j),∑i​mi​=N.
(18)
Because OQOT=Q is equivalent to OQ=QO, an unbroken transformation preserves each eigenspace. The complete unbroken group is therefore
H=S(O(m1​)×⋯×O(mp​))={(A1​,…,Ap​):i∏​detAi​=1}.​
(19)
Its identity component is H0=∏i​SO(mi​). This symmetric tensor vacuum stabilizer shows why the representation alone does not determine a unique breaking pattern:
  • If Q=qI, all of SO(N) survives, even when q=0.
  • If all eigenvalues are distinct, H≅(Z/2Z)N−1, consisting of diagonal sign changes with total sign +1; no continuous symmetry survives.
  • If the two eigenvalue multiplicities are m and N−m, then H=S(O(m)×O(N−m)), with identity component SO(m)×SO(N−m).
For example a rank-one tensor Q=veN​eNT​ preserves O(N−1) embedded by A↦diag(A,detA). Its continuous unbroken group is SO(N−1), but it also allows reversal of the distinguished axis. Restricting to a traceless symmetric tensor imposes ∑i​mi​qi​=0 and otherwise leaves the same classification.

The complexification of a Lie algebra gives so(2n,C) of type Dn root system and so(2n+1,C) of type Bn root system. For Dn​, the simple roots αi​=ei​−ei+1​ for 1≤i<n and αn​=en−1​+en​ give a chain 1,…,n−2 with two terminal nodes n−1,n joined to n−2. All edges are single. For Bn​, use αi​=ei​−ei+1​ for i<n and αn​=en​. The Bn Dynkin diagram is a chain with a double final edge and an arrow pointing to the short node n.
The usual family diagrams apply to Dn​ for n≥4 and Bn​ for n≥2. The small cases are D3​≅A3​, D2​≅A1​⊕A1​, B1​≅A1​; so(2) is one-dimensional and Abelian.
To fix Casimir normalization for orthogonal vector representations, use Hermitian Lie algebra generators tij​=i(Eij​−Eji​)/2​, so the defining representation has Dynkin index c(N)=1. With long roots of squared length two, the quadratic Casimir operator has Casimir eigenvalue
C2​(λ)=21​(λ,λ+2ρ)=21​λTG(λ+21),
(20)
where ρ is the Weyl vector and λ in the final expression is the column of Dynkin labels. The defining fundamental representation has highest weight (1,0,…,0) in the stated family conventions.
For Dn​, the row supplied in the source has sum n−1 and first entry one, so
C2​(2n)=21​{1+2(n−1)}=22n−1​.
(21)
For Bn​, the supplied row has sum n−21​, giving
C2​(2n+1)=21​{1+2(n−21​)}=n.
(22)
Taking the trace of ∑a​ta​ta​=C2​(R)IR​ gives the relation between the Dynkin index and Casimir eigenvalue, c(R)dimg=C2​(R)dimR. Therefore
C2​(N)=2N−1​,c(N)=1.​
(23)
There is also a direct check: each coordinate appears in N−1 planes and tij2​=(Eii​+Ejj​)/2, so ∑i<j​tij2​=(N−1)I/2.
If the Casimir operator is instead defined by C(λ)=(λ,λ+2ρ), every Lie algebra generator is larger by 2​ and the corresponding answers are C(N)=N−1 and c(N)=2. These are the same Lie algebra in a different normalization. At the exceptional small ranks the family Dynkin labels of the vector change, but the direct matrix computation remains valid.

One generation of left-handed Standard Model fermions fits into 5⊕10 of an SU(5) grand unified theory. With hypercharge defined by Q=T3​+Y, their restriction of a representation to SU(3)c​×SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​ is
510​⟶(3,1)1/3​⊕(1,2)−1/2​⟶(3,2)1/6​⊕(3,1)−2/3​⊕(1,1)1​​​carrying dc,L,carrying Q,uc,ec.​
(48)
Here Q in the multiplet list denotes the left-handed quark doublet, and the superscript c labels left-handed charge-conjugate fields of the corresponding right-handed fermions.
The results of c therefore give
6⊕15⟶one ordinary generation5⊕10​​⊕additional multiplets1⊕5​​.​
(49)
Thus the required matter representations are present. The extra singlet has Standard Model representation (1,1)0​ and can describe the left-handed charge-conjugate field of a right-handed neutrino. Its gauge singlet character permits a Majorana mass term; a Yukawa interaction with a lepton doublet and a suitable Higgs field can then supply neutrino masses. The branching alone does not fix those interactions or their scales.
The extra 5 has
5⟶(3,1)−1/3​⊕(1,2)1/2​.
(50)
If it describes Weyl fermions, these are additional coloured and electroweak fermions in the conjugate Standard Model representations to the ordinary 5. With an additional 5, they can form a vectorlike gauge spectrum and acquire invariant masses. Pairing them with the only ordinary 5 would also remove that generation's dc,L fields, so a realistic heavy pairing needs extra matter. If the 5 instead describes a scalar field, it has the quantum numbers of the usual SU(5) Higgs field: a weak doublet and a coloured triplet. A single fermionic 15 does not turn its extra fermions into a scalar Higgs field merely by branching.
There is a further consistency condition if these are the entire chiral matter content of an SU(6) gauge theory. Normalize the cubic anomaly coefficient by A(6)=1. The dual representation has A(6)=−1, and the two-index antisymmetric representation has A(15)=6−4=2. Thus one copy of each has nonzero gauge anomaly, Atotal​=1. Their inclusion of the desired SU(5) multiplets is therefore not, by itself, a complete consistent chiral unified theory.
For example, anomaly cancellation holds for 15⊕26, whose restriction of a representation is
15⊕26⟶(10⊕5)⊕(5⊕5)⊕21.​
(51)
This leaves one ordinary generation, a potentially massive vectorlike gauge spectrum, and two possible singlet neutrinos. This anomaly-free matter choice is also used in explicit SU(6) model constructions.[ref] These interpretations require additional choices of matter statistics, spontaneous symmetry breaking, and interactions; the weight systems determine the multiplet content.
