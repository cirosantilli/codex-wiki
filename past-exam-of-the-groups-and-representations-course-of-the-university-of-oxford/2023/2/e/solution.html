Use the realization R3​=W⊂C4 from solution. Put x=(ϕ1​,ϕ2​,ϕ3​,−ϕ1​−ϕ2​−ϕ3​), which identifies the three-dimensional scalar field with W. The polynomial invariants of the standard S4 representation include the symmetric power sums
I2​=∑i=14​xi2​,I3​=∑i=14​xi3​,
(21)
because every permutation merely rearranges their summands. In these coordinates,
I2​=ϕ12​+ϕ22​+ϕ32​+(ϕ1​+ϕ2​+ϕ3​)2,I3​=−3(ϕ1​+ϕ2​)(ϕ2​+ϕ3​)(ϕ3​+ϕ1​).​
(22)
Neither polynomial vanishes identically: at (ϕ1​,ϕ2​,ϕ3​)=(1,1,1) they equal 12 and −24. Hence coefficients multiplying I2​ and I3​ provide nonzero invariant quadratic and cubic terms. A change of basis transports these polynomial invariants to any equivalent realization of R3​. For a real scalar field, both are real. For a complex scalar field, a real scalar potential may contain λk​Ik​+λk∗​Ik∗​; the quadratic Hermitian form ∑i​∣xi​∣2 is another invariant.
As an independent character check, the symmetric square of a group representation has character [χ(g)2+χ(g2)]/2, and the symmetric cube of a group representation has character [χ(g)3+3χ(g)χ(g2)+2χ(g3)]/6. With χ=χ3​ these are (6,2,2,0,0) and (10,2,−2,1,0). Their character inner products with χ0​ are both one, so each degree has exactly one independent polynomial invariant.
