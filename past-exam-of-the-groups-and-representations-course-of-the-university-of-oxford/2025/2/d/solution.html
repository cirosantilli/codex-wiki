In the normalization c(8)=1 of c, the Adjoint representation of D4​ has highest weight λadj​=(0,1,0,0), the highest root. The supplied Gram matrix has second row (1,2,1,1), whose sum is five. Hence
C2​(adj)=21​(2+2⋅5)=6.
(24)
The Adjoint representation has the same dimension as the Lie algebra, so its Dynkin index is also c(adj)=6. The k Weyl fermions form a direct sum of k vector representations; additivity of the trace gives c(rW​)=k.
Substitution into the given one-loop renormalization-group beta function yields
β(g)=−16π2g3​(22−32k​)=−24π233−k​g3.​
(25)
For positive g, asymptotic freedom at this order requires a negative beta function, equivalently
k<33;for a nonnegative integer number of fermions, k=0,1,…,32.​
(26)
Indeed, if b0​=22−2k/3>0, integrating μdg/dμ=−b0​g3/(16π2) gives
g2(μ)1​=g2(μ0​)1​+8π2b0​​logμ0​μ​,
(27)
so the gauge coupling decreases towards zero as the energy scale increases. At k=33 the one-loop coefficient vanishes and the given equation cannot decide asymptotic freedom; k>33 has the opposite one-loop running.
With the alternative convention C(λ)=λTG(λ+21) described in c, c(8)=2, c(adj)=12, and the same source formula reads β(g)=−(44−4k/3)g3/(16π2). Rescaling the gauge coupling together with the Lie algebra generators relates the conventions; the bound k<33 is identical.
