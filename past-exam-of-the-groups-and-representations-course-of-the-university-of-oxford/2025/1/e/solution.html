Choose the doublet bases so that the column of barred fields B=(ψˉ​L1​,ψˉ​L2​)T and R=(ψR1​,ψR2​)T both transform by the doublet matrices in d. This choice follows the source's assignment of the barred doublet itself. Since H transforms in ε, its Yukawa coupling must multiply a fermion bilinear in ε.
There is exactly one such bilinear in 2⊗2: the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients in d give B1​R2​−B2​R1​. Equivalently, writing the upper block of the Yukawa matrix as A, invariance requires
D(r)TAD(r)=A,D(s)TAD(s)=−A.
(12)
The first equation sets A11​=A22​=0, because α2=1; the second sets A21​=−A12​.
A mixed doublet-singlet bilinear remains a doublet after multiplication by H, so all four mixed entries vanish. Finally ψˉ​L3​ψR3​ transforms in ε⊗1=ε, and multiplication by H makes it invariant. Therefore
λ=​0−a0​a00​00b​​,a,b∈C.​
(13)
The allowed Yukawa interaction is H{a(ψˉ​L1​ψR2​−ψˉ​L2​ψR1​)+bψˉ​L3​ψR3​}, together with its Hermitian conjugate when forming a real Lagrangian density.
The displayed texture depends on basis. If instead the unbarred left doublet is assigned D in the same ordering as the right doublet, its barred doublet has the dual representation D∗ and the equivalent upper block is adiag(1,−1). Exchanging the two barred components converts between these descriptions. In either convention there are exactly two independent complex Yukawa couplings.
