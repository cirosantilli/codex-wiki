The commuting direction in solution provides the correct candidate Lie algebra for a gauge group, su(3)c​⊕su(2)L​⊕u(1)Y​. This is a necessary structural condition for the Standard Model, but it does not by itself establish a viable grand unified theory.
The representations in solution do not give a Standard Model fermion generation with the correct weak hypercharges. Use Qelectric​=T3​+Y. Identifying (3,2)1​ with the left-handed quark doublet, whose weak hypercharge is 1/6, fixes Y=X/6. But then the doublet in (1,3) has Y=1/6, whereas the left-handed lepton doublet requires Y=−1/2. Its singlet has Y=−1/3, whereas a right-handed electron requires Y=−1. The (3,1)0​ is a further concrete mismatch: right-handed quarks written as left-handed antiquarks require Y=−2/3 or 1/3, and a rescaling cannot turn zero into either value. These comparisons use the electroweak representation and hypercharge table.
The charge-−3 doublet in (1,8) does have the required lepton weak hypercharge under this normalization, but the same Adjoint representation also brings a doublet of the opposite charge and an extra triplet. Therefore merely adding that representation does not supply the missing chiral generation without additional states and a mechanism for their masses.
Furthermore SU(3)×SU(3) is a product of two Simple Lie groups, permitting two independent gauge couplings; it does not enforce a common strong and electroweak gauge coupling. Thus the embedding exists, but the displayed matter content does not furnish a viable minimal unification of the Standard Model. A more elaborate model would require additional representations, consistent chiral matter, anomaly cancellation, and a specified spontaneous symmetry breaking mechanism. The calculations here do not prove that every possible extension using this product group is impossible.
