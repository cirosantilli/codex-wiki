Use upper indices for the defining representation of SU(3) and lower indices for its dual representation. A Young diagram with row lengths (ℓ1​,ℓ2​) has highest weight Dynkin labels (p,q)=(ℓ1​−ℓ2​,ℓ2​); a full column of height three is a determinant representation and can be removed for SU(3). The Weyl dimension formula reduces to
dim(p,q)=2(p+1)(q+1)(p+q+2)​.
(21)
The six irreducible representations of smallest dimensions are 1,3,3,6,6,8. The following box arrays draw their Young diagrams; filling boxes by tensor-slot labels gives representative Young tableaux:

Their associated tensors, in the same order, can be taken as follows:
  • 1: a scalar s, unchanged by SU(3).
  • 3: a vector vi, with vi↦Uij​vj.
  • 3: an antisymmetric tensor A[ij], equivalently a covector wk​=21​ϵkij​Aij, with wi​↦wj​(U−1)ji​.
  • 6: a symmetric tensor S(ij), with Sij↦Uik​Ujℓ​Skℓ.
  • 6: a symmetric covariant tensor S(ij)​, with Sij​↦Skℓ​(U−1)ki​(U−1)ℓj​. In the four-upper-index Young tableau description, use two antisymmetric index pairs with symmetry under exchange of pairs, and convert each pair to a lower index with the invariant ϵ tensor.
  • 8: a mixed tensor Tij​ with Tii​=0, transforming as T↦UTU−1. This is the Adjoint representation. Its nine components obey one independent trace constraint.
These low-dimensional SU3 tensor representations have dimensions 1,3,3,6,6,8 by direct component counting. To check that none were missed, if p+q≥3 then (p+1)(q+1)≥p+q+1, so the displayed dimension is at least (p+q+1)(p+q+2)/2≥10. The pairs with p+q≤2 are precisely the six listed pairs.
