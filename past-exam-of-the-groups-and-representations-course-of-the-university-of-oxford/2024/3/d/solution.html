Let ρ3​(P)=P⊗P⊗P act on the alternating coefficient tensor φ, and set
H={P∈SO(7):ρ3​(P)φ=φ}.
(40)
The coefficient convention here is the one in the source's hint; using the usual covariant pullback gives the same stabilizer subgroup, since an invertible transformation preserves a differential form if and only if its inverse does. The identity matrix fixes φ. If P,Q fix it, then ρ3​(PQ)φ=ρ3​(P)ρ3​(Q)φ=φ. Multiplying ρ3​(P)φ=φ by ρ3​(P−1) shows that P−1 fixes it as well. Thus H is a subgroup of SO(7). Its defining equations are polynomial, so it is closed and hence a Matrix Lie group.
For P(t)=I+tT+O(t2)∈H, differentiating PTP=I gives TT=−T. Differentiating the three factors in ρ3​(P(t))φ=φ gives
h={T∈so(7):n∑​(Tkn​φnlm​+Tln​φknm​+Tmn​φkln​)=0 for all k,l,m}.​
(41)
The skew-symmetric matrix condition records the ambient special orthogonal group; it must be included when differentiating that group's definition.
These linear conditions are sufficient as well as necessary. On the tensor product, the infinitesimal action is
D(T)=T⊗I⊗I+I⊗T⊗I+I⊗I⊗T.
(42)
Its three terms commute, so ρ3​(etT)=etD(T). If D(T)φ=0, every positive power of D(T) annihilates φ, giving etD(T)φ=φ. Also T∈so(7) implies etT∈SO(7). Therefore etT∈H, proving the asserted Lie algebra description. For the given positive three-form this is the compact G2 Lie algebra g2​.
