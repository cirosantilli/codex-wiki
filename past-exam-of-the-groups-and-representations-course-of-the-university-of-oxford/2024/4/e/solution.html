Among the representations in c, the 10 of SU(5) occurs in the 15 of SU(6), while the simplest source of 5ˉ is 6ˉ. Thus the economical direct sum containing the prescribed family is
R=6ˉ⊕15↓SU(5)(5ˉ⊕10)⊕5⊕1.​
(60)
The extra SU(5) multiplets are one 5 and one trivial representation. Calling R a representation does not require it to be an irreducible representation; none of the five individual choices in c contains both required SU(5) summands.
There is a further physical qualification if this is to be the entire chiral matter content of a consistent grand unified theory. Merely containing one Standard Model family does not guarantee cancellation of the cubic SU(6) gauge anomaly. In the convention A(6)=1, one has A(6ˉ)=−1 and A(15)=2, so the displayed sum has A(R)=1. With these types of representations, the minimal anomaly-free completion is instead
Raf​=26ˉ⊕15↓SU(5)(5ˉ⊕10)⊕(5⊕5ˉ)⊕21.​
(61)
Beyond one family this adds a vectorlike gauge spectrum 5⊕5ˉ and two trivial representations. The conjugate pair can receive a mass after the appropriate symmetry breaking, without removing the remaining family.[ref] The first boxed answer addresses representation containment; the second includes the extra consistency condition for a chiral gauge theory.
