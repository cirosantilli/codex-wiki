The matrices obey Q5=P2=I and PQP=Q−1. Moving every P to the right therefore reduces every product to Qk or QkP, with 0≤k<5. The five diagonal matrices Qk are distinct because α is a primitive fifth root of unity; the five QkP are distinct and off-diagonal. Thus
G={Qk,QkP:0≤k<5},∣G∣=10.​
(1)
This is the dihedral group of the regular pentagon, with Q a rotation and P a reflection.
Conjugation by P interchanges Qk and Q−k, while conjugation by a power of Q fixes rotations. Also
Qj(QkP)Q−j=Qk+2jP.
(2)
Since multiplication by 2 permutes the residues modulo 5, all five reflections form one conjugacy class. The full list of conjugacy classes is
{I},{Q,Q4},{Q2,Q3},{P,QP,Q2P,Q3P,Q4P}.​

To prove the representation is irreducible over C, any proper nonzero invariant subspace would be a line invariant under Q. Its two distinct eigenvalues force such a line to be one of its coordinate eigenspaces. But P exchanges those two lines, so neither is G-invariant. Hence the given complex representation is irreducible.

Let vH​=⟨H⟩ be the vacuum expectation value in the convention M=vH​λ. For canonically normalized fermion kinetic terms, physical charged lepton masses are the nonnegative values in the singular value decomposition of M, equivalently the square roots of the eigenvalues of M†M.

M†M=∣vH​∣2diag(∣y∣2,∣y∣2,∣y3​∣2),{m1​,m2​,m3​}=∣vH​∣{∣y∣,∣y∣,∣y3​∣}.​

(q1​,q2​)=(q,−q)(modn),q∈Zn​.​

The complexification of a Lie algebra in the Adjoint representation of SU(5) consists of traceless endomorphisms of V=A⊕B. Splitting an endomorphism into its four blocks gives traceless endomorphisms of A and B, one relative block-scalar direction, and the two off-diagonal spaces Hom(B,A) and Hom(A,B).
The first three pieces transform as (3,1)0​, (1,8)0​ and (1,1)0​. The off-diagonal pieces have U(1) charges equal to output minus input charge, namely 3−(−2)=5 and −2−3=−5. Thus
24↓=(3,1)0​⊕(1,8)0​⊕(1,1)0​⊕(2,3)5​⊕(2,3)−5​.​
(36)

For the embedding in solution, restriction retains the first four Dynkin label coordinates, since the simple coroots of A4​ are the first four of A5​. In a coordinate-free version of the same projection, let ηi​ be the five defining weights of a representation of A4​. Restriction gives εi​↦ηi​ for 1≤i≤5, while ε6​↦0.
For the fundamental representation, its first five weights of a representation therefore form a 5 and the sixth is the zero weight of a representation of a trivial representation. Negating the weights of a representation gives the conjugate representation:
6↓SU(5)​=5⊕1,6↓SU(5)​=5⊕1.​
(49)
For the two-index antisymmetric representation, split the fifteen weights of a representation into the ten with i<j≤5 and the five with j=6. Their restricted values are ηi​+ηj​ and ηi​, respectively. These are precisely the weight systems of 10 and 5. Negating again gives
15↓SU(5)​=10⊕5,15↓SU(5)​=10⊕5.​
(50)
For instance the highest A5​ weight of a representation (0,1,0,0,0) restricts to (0,1,0,0) for the 10, while (1,0,0,0,−1) restricts to (1,0,0,0) for the 5. The restricted weight systems are disjoint unions of multiplicity-one sets in these cases, and their dimensions check as 6=5+1 and 15=10+5.

One left-handed Standard Model family requires the SU(5) representation content 10⊕5. No single irreducible representation in solution contains both. At the level of containing the required fields, the smallest choice is
15⊕6↓=(10⊕5)⊕5⊕1.​
(51)
The additional SU(5) multiplets are a 5 and a trivial representation. Another minimal choice using only two of the listed irreducible representations is
15⊕15↓=(10⊕5)⊕(10⊕5).
(52)
Its additional multiplets form the conjugate representation of a family. More generally, for multiplicities (m6​,m6ˉ​,m15​,m15​), branching of a representation gives
(m6​+m15​)5⊕(m6ˉ​+m15​)5⊕m15​10⊕m15​10⊕(m6​+m6ˉ​)1.
(53)
Thus containing a family requires m15​≥1 and m6ˉ​+m15​≥1.
For a consistent chiral grand unified theory, containment alone is insufficient. Normalize the cubic gauge anomaly coefficient by A(6)=1. For the special unitary group SU(N), A(Λ2N)=N−4, so for SU(6) it is 2. To verify this directly, take a traceless diagonal generator with entries xi​: its exterior square has entries xi​+xj​, and
∑i<j​(xi​+xj​)3=(N−1)∑i​xi3​+3∑i=j​xi2​xj​=(N−4)∑i​xi3​.
(54)
The conjugate representation reverses this cubic gauge anomaly. The combination 15⊕6 has coefficient 2−1=1, so cannot alone be a quantum-consistent chiral SU(6) gauge theory. The minimal anomaly-free choice with one net chiral gauge spectrum of a family is instead
15⊕26↓=(10⊕5)⊕(5⊕5)⊕21.​
(55)
The additional nonsinglet fields form a vectorlike gauge spectrum that can acquire an SU(5)-invariant mass, and the two gauge singlets may serve as sterile neutrinos. More generally, precisely one net chiral gauge spectrum of a family with vectorlike extra nonsinglets requires m15​−m15​=1 and m6ˉ​−m6​=2; these conditions also cancel the cubic SU(6) gauge anomaly. The 15⊕15 choice above is anomaly-free but has zero net chirality.
