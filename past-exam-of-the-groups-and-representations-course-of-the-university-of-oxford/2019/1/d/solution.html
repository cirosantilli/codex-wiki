Use the matrices of solution. Their projective classes satisfy [−M]=[M], since −I2​∈N. The group homomorphism is therefore explicitly
R(±I2​)=[I2​],R(±A)=[A],R(±B)=[B],R(±C)=[C].​
(10)
These four classes are distinct: the two diagonal representatives are not scalar multiples of one another, the two off-diagonal representatives are not scalar multiples of one another, and a diagonal representative cannot be a nonzero scalar multiple of an off-diagonal one.
Although A and B anticommute as matrices, their projective classes commute:
[A][B]=[AB]=[−BA]=[BA]=[B][A].
(11)
Furthermore [A]2=[B]2=[I2​] and [C]2=[−I2​]=[I2​]. Thus every nonidentity element of the image has order two. An explicit group isomorphism to the Klein four-group is
ImR⟶Z2​×Z2​,[AaBb]⟼(a,b),a,b∈{0,1}.​
(12)
Indeed reordering products introduces only a sign, which disappears in the projective linear group, so this map turns multiplication into addition modulo two in each coordinate. Equivalently, kerR=G∩N={±I2​} and the first isomorphism theorem gives
G/{±I2​}≅ImR≅Z2​×Z2​.
(13)
This projective image of the square symmetry group illustrates how a noncommutative group can have an Abelian projective image: its scalar commutator becomes the identity class.
