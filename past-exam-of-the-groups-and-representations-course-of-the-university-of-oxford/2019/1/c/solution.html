Write A=g1​, B=g2​ and C=AB. Direct multiplication gives
A2=B2=I2​,BA=−AB,C=(0−1​10​),C2=−I2​.
(7)
The relation BA=−AB moves all occurrences of A to the left of all occurrences of B. Removing even powers reduces every product of the generators to ±AaBb with a,b∈{0,1}. The resulting eight matrices are distinct, so
G={±I2​, ±A, ±B, ±C},∣G∣=8.​
(8)
Explicitly, the four pairs are
±(10​01​),±(10​0−1​),±(01​10​),±(0−1​10​).
(9)
Since AB=−BA and AB=0, the group is noncommutative. More precisely, C has order four, A has order two and ACA=C−1, identifying G with the dihedral group of a square. This fixes the convention unambiguously: it is the dihedral group of group order eight, irrespective of whether it is denoted D4​ or D8​.
