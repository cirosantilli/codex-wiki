Let S=s=(01​10​). Entrywise complex conjugation interchanges the diagonal entries of r and fixes s, so
SR2​(r)∗S−1=R2​(r),SR2​(s)∗S−1=R2​(s).
(20)
Since r,s generate the group, these identities hold for every element. Thus S is an invertible intertwiner, proving
R2∗​≅R2​.​
(21)
The character of a tensor product representation is the product of the factor characters. Hence χR2​⊗R2​​=(4,1,0). Its character inner products with the three rows of solution give multiplicities (1,1,1), so
R2​⊗R2​≅1⊕ε⊕R2​.​
(22)
For explicit Clebsch-Gordan decomposition of group representations bases, let e+​,e−​ be the coordinate basis. The symmetric tensor e+​⊗e−​+e−​⊗e+​ spans 1; the antisymmetric tensor e+​⊗e−​−e−​⊗e+​ spans ε; and the ordered pair e−​⊗e−​,e+​⊗e+​ carries R2​, since r acts by diag(α,α2) and s exchanges them. In particular S2R2​=1⊕R2​ and Λ2R2​=ε.
Multiplying by the sign representation preserves the character of R2​, because its character is zero on all three reflections. Thus ε⊗R2​≅R2​, and associativity gives
R2⊗3​​≅(1⊕ε⊕R2​)⊗R2​≅R2​⊕R2​⊕(1⊕ε⊕R2​).​
(23)
Therefore
R2​⊗R2​⊗R2​≅1⊕ε⊕3R2​.​
(24)
The dimensions give 8=1+1+3⋅2. Its two one-dimensional summands can also be seen directly: e+⊗3​+e−⊗3​ and e+⊗3​−e−⊗3​ transform as 1 and ε, respectively.
