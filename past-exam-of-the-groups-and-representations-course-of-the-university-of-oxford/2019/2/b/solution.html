The matrix r has distinct eigenvalues α,α2. Any one-dimensional invariant subspace for the defining representation would therefore have to be one of its two coordinate eigenspaces. But s exchanges those lines, so neither is invariant under the whole group. Hence R2​ is an irreducible representation over the complex numbers.
There are three complex irreducible representations up to equivalence of group representations, because there are three conjugacy classes. The trivial representation and the sign representation already give two inequivalent one-dimensional representations. Together with R2​, their dimensions satisfy the sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions:
12+12+22=6=∣G∣,dimensions 1,1,2.​
(17)
Thus there are no further irreducible representations. Alternatively the character of R2​ is (2,−1,0), and its character inner product with itself is (4+2)/6=1, independently confirming irreducibility.
