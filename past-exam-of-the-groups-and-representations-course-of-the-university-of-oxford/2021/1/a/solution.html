Let T(v⊗w)=w⊗v on the tensor product V⊗V. The linear map R(g)⊗R(g) commutes with T, so preserves its +1 and −1 eigenspaces, namely the symmetric square S2V and the exterior square ∧2V. Restricting the tensor product representation to these invariant subspaces defines S2R and ∧2R. The representation identity follows by restricting (R(g)⊗R(g))(R(h)⊗R(h))=R(gh)⊗R(gh).
For a finite group, g has finite order m, so R(g)m=I. Over the complex numbers, the polynomial tm−1 has distinct roots; hence R(g) is diagonalizable. Choose an eigenbasis v1​,…,vn​ with eigenvalues λ1​,…,λn​. A basis of the symmetric square consists of vi​⊗vi​ and vi​⊗vj​+vj​⊗vi​ for i<j. A basis of the exterior square consists of vi​⊗vj​−vj​⊗vi​ for i<j. Their eigenvalues are respectively λi2​,λi​λj​ and λi​λj​. Taking traces,
χS2R​(g)=∑i​λi2​+∑i<j​λi​λj​,χ∧2R​(g)=∑i<j​λi​λj​.
(1)
But χ(g)2=∑i​λi2​+2∑i<j​λi​λj​ and χ(g2)=∑i​λi2​. Thus the characters are
χS2R​(g)=21​[χ(g)2+χ(g2)],χ∧2R​(g)=21​[χ(g)2−χ(g2)].​
(2)
At g=e, these also give the dimensions n(n+1)/2 and n(n−1)/2.
