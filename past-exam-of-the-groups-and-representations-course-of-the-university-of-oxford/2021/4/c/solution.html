An unbroken subgroup H must fix the vacuum expectation value itself. Thus a nonzero H-invariant vacuum expectation value can exist only if the restriction of a representation to H contains a trivial representation. Applying the singlet criterion for an unbroken subgroup to solution gives
R4615​can preserve H2​noyesyes​can preserve H3​yesnoyes​​​
(45)
The two “no” entries are forbidden for every nonzero vacuum expectation value. Each “yes” means that the indicated subgroup can remain unbroken, subject to the scalar potential choosing such a vacuum. It does not by itself prove that the indicated subgroup is the entire stabilizer.
For exact spontaneous symmetry breaking to the stated subgroup, the distinction matters. A complex 4 admits an exact H3​ stabilizer; a complex 6 admits an exact H2​ stabilizer. A single 15 preserving H2​ or H3​ necessarily preserves an additional continuous U(1), as computed in solution, so neither exact endpoint is possible with that single field. Also, if the 6 scalar field is constrained to the real structure of the SU4 exterior square, its nonzero stabilizer is larger than H2​. The paper does not specify whether this reality constraint is imposed, so both cases are distinguished below.
