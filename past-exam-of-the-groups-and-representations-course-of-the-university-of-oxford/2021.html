Write the two Pauli matrices as
A=(01​10​),B=(10​0−1​),C=AB=(01​−10​).
(3)
They satisfy A2=B2=I, BA=−AB and C2=−I. Moving every A to the left of every B reduces each word to one of eight distinct matrices. Consequently
G={I,−I,A,−A,B,−B,C,−C},∣G∣=8.​
(4)
Taking r=C, s=A gives r4=s2=I and srs=r−1, identifying the group with the dihedral group of a square.
The elements ±I are central. Conjugating A by B changes its sign, and similarly for B and C. No conjugation mixes these pairs: modulo the central subgroup {±I} the quotient group is the Klein four-group and hence Abelian. Thus the conjugacy classes are precisely
{I},{−I},{A,−A},{B,−B},{C,−C}.​
(5)
There are five complex irreducible representations, one for each conjugacy class. In a one-dimensional representation, the images of A,B are signs s,t∈{1,−1}. Since they commute, −I maps to 1, and all four choices of s,t satisfy the relations. These are four inequivalent one-dimensional representations. The sum of squares of irreducible representation dimensions leaves 8−4=4 for the fifth, so the dimensions are
1,1,1,1,2.​

First choose the same orthonormal basis for the left and right copies of the two-dimensional unitary representation D. Then the family representation is F=D⊕ε. Because the barred fermion transforms with F(g)† and the scalar field H is a trivial representation, invariance of the Yukawa interaction requires
F(g)†λF(g)=λ,equivalentlyλF(g)=F(g)λ(g∈S3​).
(19)
Write λ in a 2+1 block form. The off-diagonal blocks would be intertwiners between inequivalent irreducible representations D and ε, so the Schur lemma makes them zero. The doublet block must be a scalar matrix, while the singlet block is arbitrary. Therefore
λ=​a00​0a0​00b​​,a,b∈C.​
(20)
Equivalently, the allowed Yukawa interaction is H[a(ψˉ​L1​ψR1​+ψˉ​L2​ψR2​)+bψˉ​L3​ψR3​], together with its Hermitian conjugate when constructing a real Lagrangian. The tensor product rules explain the two free parameters: both D∗⊗D and ε∗⊗ε contain one trivial representation, while the mixed products contain none.
There is a useful basis qualification. The basis used in solution is not orthonormal: its Gram matrix is K=(21​12​), and D(g)†KD(g)=K. If the fields are expressed in exactly those coordinates, with the barred components defined by ordinary conjugate transpose, the invariant doublet coefficient is aK, rather than aI2​. Canonically normalizing the kinetic term returns the boxed form. Independently chosen left and right orthonormal bases replace aI2​ by aS, where S is their fixed unitary intertwiner; the physical degeneracy is unchanged.

After the scalar field obtains a vacuum expectation value h, the fermion mass matrix in a canonically normalized basis is M=hλ. Physical masses are its singular values, not arbitrary entries or signed eigenvalues. Thus
me​=mμ​=∣ha∣,mτ​=∣hb∣.​
(21)
The independent Yukawa coupling b can fit the tau lepton mass relative to the doublet scale, but the electron and muon are predicted to have equal masses. The values supplied by the paper instead give mμ​/me​≃105.7/0.511≃207. Consequently exact unbroken S3​ with this representation assignment cannot reproduce all three masses. Complex phases of a,b, or changes of basis, cannot split the two equal singular values. Additional breaking of the symmetry or a different field content would be needed; the discrepancy cannot be removed merely by choosing different invariant Yukawa couplings.

The antisymmetric case. With ϵ=(0−1​10​), take
⟨A⟩=(aϵ0​0bϵ​).
(47)
Since VϵVT=ϵ for V∈SU(2), this antisymmetric tensor is fixed by H2​ for every a,b. Its transformation is A↦UAUT, so any stabilizing U also preserves
AA†=diag(∣a∣2I2​,∣b∣2I2​).
(48)
If ∣a∣=∣b∣, its two eigenspaces force U=diag(V,V) with unitary two-by-two blocks. For each nonzero block, VϵVT=(detV)ϵ forces the block determinant to be one. If one block is zero, the SU(4) determinant condition forces the other block determinant to be one as well. Hence
∣a∣=∣b∣⟹Stab(⟨A⟩)=H2​.​
(49)
The unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix shows that a generic complex 6 has two distinct paired singular values; choosing an SU(4) rather than a U(4) basis merely leaves an allowed overall phase in a,b. Thus its generic stabilizer is conjugate to H2​.
If instead ∣a∣=∣b∣=0, the form is proportional, up to unitary coordinates and phases, to a nondegenerate standard symplectic form. Its stabilizer is the compact symplectic group USp(4), of dimension ten, containing H2​ properly. The explicit choice a=b=1 is a concrete counterexample to inferring an exact H2​ stabilizer from the existence of two singlets alone. For a real 6, the real structure of the SU4 exterior square imposes b=aˉ in this form, so these equal magnitudes are compulsory. Every nonzero real 6 therefore has the larger compact symplectic group stabilizer. The zero vacuum expectation value preserves all of SU(4).
The adjoint cases. The H2​-fixed and H3​-fixed traceless matrices have respectively the forms
X2​=adiag(1,1,−1,−1),X3​=adiag(1,1,1,−3),a=0.
(50)
For a Hermitian adjoint scalar field, choose a real. More generally a complex a gives the same stabilizers. The repeated eigenvalues imply
Stab(X2​)=S(U(2)×U(2)),Stab(X3​)=S(U(3)×U(1)).​
(51)
Here S means that the product of the block determinants is one. These groups contain respectively H2​ and H3​, together with an additional continuous U(1). Explicit extra transformations are diag(eitI2​,e−itI2​) and diag(eitI3​,e−3it). They commute with X2​ and X3​ for every t, proving that neither exact endpoint H2​ or H3​ occurs for a single adjoint vacuum expectation value.
Nor do these larger subgroups survive for a generic Hermitian traceless matrix: four distinct eigenvalues leave only the maximal torus U(1)3. For a generic complex adjoint matrix, there need not even be a continuous stabilizer; generically its unitary centralizer is just the centre of a group Z4​. Thus the displayed degenerate adjoint directions are special. All these statements concern the stabilizer of a possible vacuum expectation value; no unspecified scalar potential can be assumed to select a particular direction or a particular reality condition.
