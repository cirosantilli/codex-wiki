A sphere of radius R settling in a stationary cylindrical tube of radius R+d, with d≪R and zero net through-flow, has leading speed
U∼27π82​​μΔρgd2​Rd​​.
(43)
The neck gap is H(z)=d+z2/(2R). In the sphere frame, leading bypass flux per circumferential length is q≃UR/2; wall-motion transport is smaller by d/R. The pressure-driven flux through a parabolic lubrication gap gives Δp=9πμUR2R​/(4d5/2). Pressure dominates the sphere drag, F≃πR2Δp, and balancing F against 4πR3Δρg/3 proves the speed. Tube end conditions allowing appreciable net flow change the bypass balance and can give other drag regimes.
