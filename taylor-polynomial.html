Taylor polynomial (Tn,a​(x)=∑k=0n​f(k)(a)(x−a)k/k!)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 336 Articles: 2
A Taylor polynomial replaces a function near a chosen point by a polynomial with matching derivatives. If f has derivatives through order n at a, define
Tn,a​(x)=∑k=0n​k!f(k)(a)​(x−a)k.
(12)
The factorial denominator makes Tn,a(j)​(a)=f(j)(a) for 0≤j≤n. This determines a unique polynomial of degree of a polynomial at most n. Order zero matches the value, order one also matches the tangent line, and order two also incorporates the second derivative. Matching more local information is useful because polynomials are easy to evaluate, differentiate, and integrate. The distinction between the finite Taylor polynomial and the infinite Taylor series is developed in OpenStax, Taylor and Maclaurin series.
For a computed example, let f(x)=1+x​ and expand at zero. Its first three derivatives give
f(0)=1,f′(0)=21​,f′′(0)=−41​,T2,0​(x)=1+2x​−8x2​.
(13)
At x=0.1, the linear approximation is 1.05 and the quadratic approximation is 1.04875, compared with 1.1​≈1.048808848. The quadratic correction captures the downward bending of the square root and reduces the absolute difference from about 0.00119115 to 0.0000588482.
The Taylor remainder turns this convenient approximation into a controlled calculation. On [0,0.1], f′′′(x)=3/[8(1+x)5/2] is positive and at most 3/8. The Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder therefore gives
0<1.1​−1.04875≤3!(3/8)(0.1)3​=160001​.
(14)
Consequently 1.04875<1.1​≤1.0488125 follows without using the displayed decimal evaluation as evidence. The Taylor polynomial and the Taylor remainder play different roles: the former supplies an answer that is easy to compute, while the latter specifies what accuracy is justified. Farther from the expansion point, the same low-degree polynomial need not remain accurate.
