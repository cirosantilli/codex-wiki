Euler characteristic of a torus (χ(T2)=1−2+1=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Homology (mathematics) Simplicial homology Euler characteristic
OurBigBook.com Words: 160
Identify opposite sides of a square by translations to obtain a torus. All four corners become one vertex; the horizontal sides give one topological cell a and the vertical sides one topological cell b. The open square supplies one two-dimensional topological cell. Thus (c0​,c1​,c2​)=(1,2,1) and χ=0. The cellular boundary of each one-dimensional topological cell is zero because its endpoints agree. Traversing the face boundary gives the group commutator aba−1b−1, whose signed counts of both a and b vanish. Hence its cellular boundary is also zero, and (b0​,b1​,b2​)=(1,2,1).[ref]
The boundary of a tetrahedron is a two-sphere, with four vertices, six edges and four triangular topological cells: χ=4−6+4=2. Its rational Betti numbers are (1,0,1) by homology of a sphere. The torus's two independent one-dimensional homology classes cancel its degree-zero and degree-two contributions. The torus and annulus both have Euler characteristic zero, yet different homology groups; one integer cannot recover all of homology.
