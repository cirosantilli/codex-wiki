Polar form of a complex number (z=reiθ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Complex number
OurBigBook.com Words: 264 Articles: 2
A nonzero complex number has polar form
z=r(cosθ+isinθ)=reiθ,r=∣z∣>0,
(107)
where r is its modulus and θ is an argument. The equality uses Euler's formula; sine and cosine recover the imaginary part and real part from polar coordinates. For z=3+4i, take r=5 and θ=arctan(4/3)≈0.927295 radians, so cosθ=3/5 and sinθ=4/5.
If w=seiϕ=0, then
zw=rsei(θ+ϕ),wz​=sr​ei(θ−ϕ).
(108)
Thus multiplication multiplies moduli and adds arguments modulo 2π; division divides moduli and subtracts arguments modulo 2π. Since 1+i=2​eiπ/4, multiplying 3+4i by 1+i gives −1+7i, of modulus 52​ and argument θ+π/4≈1.712693 radians. One formula simultaneously describes its new size and direction.
Repeated multiplication now becomes especially simple: (reiθ)n=rneinθ for a positive integer n. This explains the evenly spaced arguments of the roots of a complex number. For example, (3+4i)2=−7+24i has modulus 25 and principal argument 2θ≈1.854590 radians. The Cartesian calculation and the polar form provide independent checks on both its location and its size. Choose polar form when the task concerns multiplication, division, or repeated turns; use real parts and imaginary parts for addition.
