A rejection of inevitable, universal warfare between science and religion is well supported; a claim that there has never been conflict is not. Those propositions have different logical forms. Evidence of collaboration or of a religious investigator doing important work defeats a claim of necessary hostility. It does not negate a documented instance of coercion over a claim about nature. A useful historiography of science must be able to describe both kinds of case without forcing them into a universal pattern.
The categories themselves require historical care. Early modern investigators did not generally choose between membership of a modern scientific profession and membership of a religious community. Natural philosophy, theology, medicine and practical arts overlapped in personnel, education and sources of support. To call a dispute one between science and religion can therefore conceal disagreement among people who all regarded themselves as religious, or a struggle over which institution had authority to settle a particular question. This warning changes the explanation of a controversy; it does not show that the controversy never occurred.
Galileo Galilei's case makes the difference concrete. Robert Bellarmine's 1615 letter both allowed hypothetical calculation and restricted absolute affirmation of heliocentrism. Its conditional acceptance of a future demonstration complicates an account of simple hostility to reasoning. Yet in June 1633 the official sentence judged Galileo Galilei vehemently suspected of heresy for holding and defending the condemned position, prohibited his dialogue and imposed punishment and abjuration. This is a documented conflict over a natural claim and the authority to defend it, even if political relationships, procedural history and standards of proof also help explain the outcome.[ref][ref]
His own Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina by Galileo Galilei further resists the binary. He argued that Scripture could not be false when rightly understood and that demonstrated physical conclusions should assist its interpretation. He was contesting a use of religious authority from within a religious account of truth, rather than declaring all religious commitments meaningless. Two parties can agree in principle that truths must be compatible while disagreeing sharply over what has been demonstrated and who may reinterpret an authoritative text. A shared commitment to ultimate harmony can coexist with an immediate institutional conflict.[ref]
There is equally clear evidence against necessary warfare. The very programme of Robert Boyle's The Christian Virtuoso by Robert Boyle was to argue that experimental natural philosophy could assist Christian belief. Its 1690 title states that purpose explicitly. That is evidence of a participant understanding religious and experimental commitments as mutually supportive; it is not proof that no tension ever affected his reasoning or that all religious institutions agreed with him. It also does not make his experimental conclusions true merely because they served an apologetic purpose.[ref]
A historian should therefore specify the object and mechanism of a conflict. Was it a claim about the Earth, a rule of biblical interpretation, access to an office, the use of funds, or a restriction on publishing? What actions followed, and which sources document them? Likewise, a claim of support should identify an actual contribution: training, scientific instruments, funding, an explanatory commitment or an argument. Broad labels lose value when they substitute for those causal particulars.
The strongest revision of a warfare narrative is thus methodological rather than a reverse universal. It rejects the assumption that two permanent, internally homogeneous forces have always confronted one another. It permits intellectual cooperation, personally integrated commitments, and episodes of real suppression. The 1633 sentence is a counterexample to never; The Christian Virtuoso by Robert Boyle is a counterexample to necessarily. Neither single case provides the whole history. This position preserves the explanatory gains of contextual history while refusing to make contextual complexity a reason to deny what the surviving documents actually record.
