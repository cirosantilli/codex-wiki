Mechanical input and thermal storage in a calorimeter

OurBigBook.com Words: 242
The UCSB demonstration uses an aluminum cylinder turned by a crank, with a wrapped rope supporting a hanging mass. An embedded electrical thermometer connects through sliding contacts to an electrical resistance meter; a supplied electrical resistance–temperature table or fitted equation converts the readout. A counter records turns. Record the initial temperature, crank steadily, then stop and convert the minimum recorded electrical resistance to the final temperature.[ref]
The proposed control is to start below room temperature and finish comparably above it, approximately balancing incoming and outgoing environmental heat. Neglected accessory heat capacities and rough temperature estimates limit accuracy. The mechanical input and thermal response test an energy balance; warming alone does not establish quantitative equivalence.[ref]
This is a modern companion to Joule's paddle-wheel experiment, with a different apparatus. Choose the cylinder as the closed system. When endpoint bulk mechanical energy changes are negligible, a model for its thermal storage is
ΔU≃Ceff​(Tf​−Ti​)=Won​+Qroom​,Wby​=−Won​,
(15)
where Ceff​ is its participating heat capacity, approximately constant over the run, and Qroom​ is signed environmental heat. The cylinder is not an isolated system, since mechanical work enters. The temperature rise estimates a change in internal energy, rather than an amount of externally supplied heat. This boundary distinction connects a measured thermal response to the same stored quantity used in two reversible ideal-gas paths between the same states.
