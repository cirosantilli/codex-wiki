Limits of Meissner screening

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic field London equations Meissner effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 175
For a bulk type-II superconductor with negligible demagnetizing field, raising the applied magnetic field intensity past the lower critical value Hc1​ favors a superconducting mixed state containing quantum vortex lines; superconductivity survives until the upper critical value Hc2​. The magnetic field extends around each vortex over a penetration scale, not solely inside its core. Thus a nonzero average magnetic flux density can coexist with superconductivity. Below Hc1​, a type-II superconductor also supports a Meissner state; a vortex-free state above the equilibrium entry field may instead be metastable. [ref]
Real preparation histories may leave quantum vortex lines held in place by material inhomogeneities, retaining magnetic flux and producing hysteresis. This flux pinning restricts access to equilibrium: it is different from the arbitrary frozen interior magnetic field of the ideal perfect conductor model. Harvard's vortex-imaging work illustrates strong pinning to bulk disorder in a type-II superconductor. [ref] Separately, flux conservation in a superconducting loop and fluxoid and flux quantization in a superconductor explain why a hole can retain magnetic flux. Neither bore retention, vortex penetration nor weak screening in a thin sample should be silently identified with the equilibrium Planar London screening calculation.
