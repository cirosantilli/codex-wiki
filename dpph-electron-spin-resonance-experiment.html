DPPH electron spin resonance experiment

 Home Chemistry Radical (chemistry) Stable radical 2,2-Diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl
OurBigBook.com Words: 312
The University of Iowa's Electron Spin Resonance, University of Iowa laboratory protocol (revised July 2007, Experiment and Resonance Data Analysis, printed pp. 2–6) specifies DPPH on cotton in a tube inside an RF coil, between Helmholtz coils. A bridge monitors RF absorption; an oscilloscope records this output and the voltage across a 1Ω current-monitor resistor. Sweeping the coil current passes through the resonant magnetic field. The protocol describes two dips when the sweep revisits resonance; these are repeated crossings, not two distinct spectral lines.
Its calibration replaces the sample with a Hall effect probe, maps the magnetic field in 2mm steps, and accounts for sample dimensions. At least four RF frequencies test the resonance relation. Sweep speed is adjusted to obtain comparable passages. Only a local peak-plus-background fit is prescribed, because the full shape is not Gaussian. The protocol also flags unexplained trailing spikes and possible electronics contributions. These controls constrain field and sweep errors; an empty-tube/cotton blank would be an additional background control, not a reported measurement in this protocol.[ref]
For an isolated effective spin one-half with dimensionless positive factor g, the basic resonance model is
ΔE=gμB​∣B∣=hν,g=μB​∣Bres​∣hν​.
(3)
Here μB​ is the Bohr magneton, h is the Planck constant, ν is the applied frequency, and Bres​ is the calibrated resonant magnetic field. A change in frequency should shift the resonance field proportionally in this model. The factor g describes the electronic magnetic moment coupling, not the species' net electric charge.
A resonance supports the presence of detectable paramagnetism. It does not, by itself, identify an unknown radical: other species exhibiting paramagnetism, impurities, overlapping responses, and interactions with atomic nuclei can complicate interpretation. Conversely, absence of a detectable resonance need not mean absence of free radicals; a low concentration, a short lifetime, or an unsuitable measurement range can conceal them. The occupancy and chain diagrams are original bookkeeping illustrations; neither is a measured or simulated DPPH spectrum.
