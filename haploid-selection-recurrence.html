Haploid selection recurrence (p′=wp+(1−p)wp​)

 Home Biology Evolution Natural selection
OurBigBook.com Words: 289
Consider an explicitly idealized haploid population with two inherited types A,B, one allele per individual at the tracked genetic locus, and discrete, nonoverlapping generations. Assume faithful type transmission, no mutation or gene flow, a constant environment, and reproductive contributions independent of type frequency. Let the expected values of offspring surviving to the next census be mA​,mB​>0, with relative genotype fitness w=mA​/mB​.
For A frequency p, expected offspring contributions are proportional to pmA​ and (1−p)mB​. Normalizing gives the deterministic large-population mathematical model
p′=pmA​+(1−p)mB​pmA​​=wp+(1−p)wp​.
(102)
The denominator is mean relative genotype fitness. For w=1.2 and p0​=0.2, p1​=0.24/1.04=3/13≈0.23077. Equivalently, 200 A and 800 B parents with mA​=1.2,mB​=1 contribute expected counts 240 and 800. Fractional per-parent expected values are averages, not literal fractional offspring. The ratio of these expectations is a deterministic approximation, not generally the expectation of a random ratio.
The odds multiply particularly simply:
1−pt+1​pt+1​​=w1−pt​pt​​,pt​=1−p0​+wtp0​wtp0​​.
(103)
Thus p10​≈0.60753: repeated modest advantage can substantially change composition. For w>1 and 0<p<1, p′−p=(w−1)p(1−p)/(1+(w−1)p)>0. The deterministic boundary p=0 remains unchanged: natural selection needs existing variation.
A distinct stochastic extension fixes N offspring and samples their types independently using a binomial distribution: K∼Binomial(N,q), where q=wp/(wp+1−p), then p′=K/N. Its conditional expected value is q and conditional variance is q(1−q)/N. With w=1, the conditional expected value remains p, yet realized frequencies wander through genetic drift. With w>1, genetic drift can still lower p or lose A entirely. Over several generations, the deterministic curve need not equal the mean stochastic trajectory, because the recurrence is nonlinear.
