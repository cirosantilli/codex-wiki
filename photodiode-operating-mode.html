Photodiode operating mode

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Photodetector Photodiode
OurBigBook.com Words: 187
Photovoltaic mode uses no external reverse bias: a transimpedance amplifier can hold zero terminal electric potential difference, while an open electrical circuit develops a photovoltage. Photoconductive mode biases the diode cathode positive relative to the diode anode, generally reducing junction capacitance and increasing speed and dark current.[ref]
A simple calculation separates the readout from the conversion. If a linear transimpedance amplifier has feedback electrical resistance Rf​=1.00MΩ, the 650nm model above produces an output change of magnitude Rf​Iph​=0.393195V. The output sign depends on the connection. Increasing Rf​ increases that electric potential difference, but does not create more collected charge carriers.
For a reverse-biased photodiode feeding an electrical load RL​, Ohm's law gives a readout magnitude VL​≃Iph​RL​ while the response remains linear. With negligible dark current and other series drops, the remaining reverse bias is VR​≃Vb​−Iph​RL​, where Vb​ is the supply magnitude. Thus the chosen electrical load can consume the available bias: a larger readout does not automatically reduce measurement uncertainty. For the same constructed example, Vb​=5V and RL​=1MΩ leave about 4.60680V; this algebra alone does not establish a real device's linear range.
