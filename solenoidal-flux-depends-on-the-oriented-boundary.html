Solenoidal flux depends on the oriented boundary (∫S1​​F⋅dS=∫S2​​F⋅dS)

 ... Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Vector field Solenoidal vector field
OurBigBook.com Words: 72
Suppose two oriented surfaces have the same oriented boundary and S1​∪(−S2​) bounds a volume inside a domain where the vector field F is smooth and solenoidal. The divergence theorem gives zero flux through their closed union, proving the displayed equality. If a global vector potential F=∇×G exists on the relevant surfaces, the Stokes theorem proves the equality directly from their common boundary. The domain condition matters: a singularity inside the joining volume can obstruct the divergence argument.
