Inductance (L)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 20
Inductance relates electric currents to the magnetic flux linkage they produce. For a single linear coil with fixed geometry and magnetic response, its self-inductance L is the constant in
Λ=LI.
(20)
Here Λ is magnetic flux linkage from the coil's own electric current I. The SI unit of inductance is the henry. Knowing inductance lets us predict the induced electromotive force when electric current changes, and the magnetic energy stored by an inductor. [ref].
For example, a coil of self-inductance 0.5H carrying electric current 3A has magnetic flux linkage 1.5Wb. If its 100 turns link the same magnetic flux, each turn has magnetic flux 0.015Wb. Keeping the geometry and magnetic response fixed while doubling electric current doubles magnetic flux linkage and quadruples the inductor's magnetic energy.
The comparison with capacitance is useful: a linear capacitor has Q=CV and stores capacitor energy CV2/2 in its electric field, whereas a linear inductor has Λ=LI and stores magnetic energy LI2/2 in its magnetic field. The RLC circuit brings both forms of energy storage together.
