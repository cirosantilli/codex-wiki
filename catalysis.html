Catalysis

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 32
Catalysis accelerates a chemical reaction by providing an alternative reaction mechanism with a more accessible barrier through its transition states. A catalyst participates in the route and is regenerated. At fixed temperature, catalysis changes how quickly chemical equilibrium is reached without changing the equilibrium constant; it does not make an unfavourable overall change in Gibbs free energy favourable. [ref][ref]
In his 1909 lecture, Wilhelm Ostwald recalled the introduction of the term catalysis by Jöns Jacob Berzelius and emphasized measurable acceleration rather than merely naming an unexplained force. He also discussed reaction intermediates and participation of the catalyst in the steps whose sum gives the net chemical reaction. This is a participant's retrospective account, not evidence that every catalytic reaction mechanism is the same.[ref]
