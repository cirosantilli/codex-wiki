Escherichia coli

 Home Biology Organism Prokaryote Bacteria
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
Escherichia coli, often abbreviated E. coli, is a bacterial species widely used to study DNA replication and gene expression. Its chromosome is in a nucleoid, and its ribosomes work in the cytoplasm, outside any cell nucleus. A result from one laboratory strain should not automatically be generalized to every member of the species.
The original 1997 report by Blattner and colleagues presented the K-12 MG1655 genome sequence as a circular chromosome of 4,639,221 base pairs. See Blattner laboratory: original K-12 sequence and 1997 paper. As a bookkeeping example, one complete round of DNA replication from one such chromosome produces two daughter sequence copies totaling 2×4,639,221=9,278,442 base pairs. This is twice the copied material, not twice the length of the reference genome; it does not specify the instantaneous DNA content of a rapidly growing E. coli cell.
