For steady equal-pressure flow, mass conservation gives Aw=A0​w0​ and the kinematic boundary condition gives (Dw​)z​=γ/(2μw​), where D=Re​−Ri​. Therefore finite-height closure occurs exactly when
2μw0​​γ​∫0z∗​​w(z)​dz​=A0​​−D0​.
(64)
For positive velocity the integral is increasing. If it diverges, closure is finite; if its limiting value lies below the right-hand threshold, there is no finite closure. Quadratic freely falling profiles have logarithmically divergent integrals, while sufficiently strongly drawn exponentially accelerating profiles can have finite integrals. Thus nozzle speed alone is insufficient to guarantee closure: the axial tension or downstream condition matters.
