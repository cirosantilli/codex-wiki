Every subset of a cofinite space is compact

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Cofinite topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 179
Every subset A of a cofinite topological space is a compact subset. To see the finite reduction directly, take an open cover of a nonempty A by open sets of X and choose one member U containing a point of A. Since U has finite complement, the set A∖U is finite. Choose a covering member for each of those remaining points. Those members together with U are a finite subcover. The empty set is already compact.
Equivalently, the subspace topology on A is itself cofinite: for finite F⊆X, the trace of X∖F is A∖(F∩A). The argument applies again inside A. A concrete open cover {X∖{k}:k∈X} of X=N>0​ needs only the two members X∖{1} and X∖{2} when these labels are distinct: each fills the other's single gap.
On X=N>0​, the subset of even integers is compact but not closed, because it is infinite and proper. A compact subset of a Hausdorff space is guaranteed to be closed, whereas compactness without the Hausdorff space assumption does not suffice. Changing the topology changes compactness: the same infinite set with its discrete topology has the singleton open cover, which has no finite subcover. The finite-exception argument is special to finite complements; in the cocountable topology, compact subsets of a cocountable space must instead be finite.
