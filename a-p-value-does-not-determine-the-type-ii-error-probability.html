A p-value does not determine the type II error probability (β(δ)=Φ(c−δ)−Φ(−c−δ))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical hypothesis test Power function of a statistical test
OurBigBook.com Words: 84
Consider a two-sided test rejecting when ∣Z∣>c, with Z having a normal distribution of mean δ and variance one. Here Φ is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution. Under a nonzero alternative δ, the type II error probability is the displayed function. It tends to the nonrejection probability under the null as δ→0, and to zero as δ→+∞. A particular observed Z=z has positive density under all these alternatives. Its null-calibrated p-value therefore does not select one value of β. The power function describes repeated-sampling performance at specified alternatives.
