Four-slit Fraunhofer diffraction

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fraunhofer diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 76
For four identical long slits of width a centred at x=±D/2,±3D/2, the aperture Fourier transform gives
ψ(q)∝2asinc(qa/2)[cos(qD/2)+cos(3qD/2)],q=λ2πsinθ​.
(39)
Here sincx=sinx/x. Normalized to the on-axis light intensity,
I(q)/I(0)=sinc2(qa/2)cos2(qD/2)cos2(qD).
(40)
Principal array directions have qD=2πm; a single-slit diffraction envelope zero cancels such an order when ma/D is a nonzero integer. The closest central array zeros satisfy qD=±π/2. Finite slit width generally shifts the actual maxima of the complete product slightly away from the nominal principal array directions.
