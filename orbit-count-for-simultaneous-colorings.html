Orbit count for simultaneous colorings

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Burnside's lemma
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
Suppose a finite group acts on two finite position sets A and B. Color the first set using r colors and the second using s colors, with both colorings moved by the same group element. If cA​(g) and cB​(g) are the cycle counts of its two induced permutations, the number of simultaneous colorings fixed by g is rcA​(g)scB​(g): each cycle must be monochromatic, and different cycles can be colored independently. Burnside's lemma therefore gives
∣G∣1​∑g∈G​rcA​(g)scB​(g)
(41)
for the number of orbits. When the two position actions are equivariantly identified, this simplifies to the usual coloring count with rs combined colors. For example, a tetrahedron's face is paired with its opposite vertex, and this pairing commutes with every symmetry.
