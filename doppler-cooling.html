Doppler cooling

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Laser cooling
OurBigBook.com Words: 733 Articles: 2
Consider equal opposing laser beams along the x axis and an atom with velocity v. Let ωL​ be their angular frequency, ω0​ the atomic resonance angular frequency, k=2π/λ>0 their wavenumber, and δ=ωL​−ω0​ the laser detuning. Red detuning means δ<0. To first order in v/c, the atom sees
ω+′​=ωL​−kv,ω−′​=ωL​+kv,δ+​=δ−kv,δ−​=δ+kv.
(44)
For small positive v, the beam travelling towards negative x is closer to resonance and causes more photon absorption; its momentum kicks point against v. The beam travelling towards positive x is further from resonance. Reversing v exchanges the roles of the two beams. This is a Doppler effect on the absorption rates, rather than a need to follow each atom with a moving beam.
An absorbed photon gives the atom momentum ±ℏk, where ℏ=h/(2π) is the reduced Planck constant. Subsequent spontaneous emission gives a recoil opposite to the emitted photon. For an emission pattern symmetric under reversal of direction, that recoil has zero expected value, but nonzero variance. Its mean therefore leaves the directional absorption force intact, while its randomness adds recoil energy. Random absorption times and choices of beam add further momentum fluctuations. Zero mean recoil does not mean zero heating.
