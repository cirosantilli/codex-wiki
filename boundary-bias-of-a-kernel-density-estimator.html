If the unknown probability distribution is supported on [0,∞), an ordinary Gaussian density kernel still puts some fitted probability below zero. For positive observations Xi​ and the standard normal distribution cumulative distribution function Φ, the displayed formula gives the total leakage. For the statistical sample (0.1,0.4,0.9) and smoothing bandwidth h=0.5, it is
3Φ(−0.2)+Φ(−0.8)+Φ(−1.8)​=30.42074029+0.21185540+0.03593032​=0.22284200….
(8)
Thus about 22.28% of the fitted probability falls outside the known support of a probability distribution, even though the full integral remains one. The leakage panel in smoothing bandwidth shades this area. Simply deleting the negative part leaves area 0.777158…; renormalizing it makes another probability density function but does not by itself establish that the bias of an estimator has been corrected.
The issue also appears in the expectation of the kernel density estimator. For a nonnegative symmetric kernel for density estimation and a probability density function f that is zero for x<0 and has a finite positive right-hand limit f(0+), assume f is bounded. Then, for fixed K as h↓0,
Ef​h​(0)=∫0∞​K(u)f(hu)du⟶21​f(0+).
(9)
Half the averaging window lies on the impossible side of the boundary. For example, the rate-one exponential distribution has f(0+)=1, so the limiting expected boundary height is 1/2, rather than the right-hand height one. This refers to estimating the right-hand limit; changing a probability density function at a single point does not change its probability distribution. Decreasing the smoothing bandwidth narrows the affected region but does not remove this boundary-height error. This is why the interior second-order pointwise bias bound for kernel density estimation cannot be applied across a jump at the boundary.
