Newton's law of cooling (y′=−k(y−Ta​))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Linear ordinary differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 364 Articles: 4
Newton's law of cooling models an object's temperature y using a positive constant rate k and ambient temperature Ta​. The displayed ordinary differential equation assumes one temperature represents the object, the environment remains at Ta​, and the proportional rate stays fixed over the modeled range. It also describes warming when y<Ta​. These are modeling assumptions: [ref] explain the proportional law through a tangent line approximation and caution about large temperature differences.
Dimensional analysis requires k to have units of inverse time. The product k(t−t0​) in the exponential function solution is therefore dimensionless. The equilibrium point y=Ta​ attracts both warmer and colder starting states: the excess temperature undergoes exponential decay. See initial value problem for computed cooling times, direction field for the family of trajectories, and separable differential equation for a derivation.
