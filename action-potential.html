Action potential

 Home Biology Neuroscience Neuron
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 8
An action potential is a brief regenerative excursion of membrane potential, produced by changing electrical resistance and conductance through voltage-gated ion channels. In a typical fast neuronal action potential, sodium entry raises membrane potential, activating further sodium channels. This feedback produces the rapid rising phase. Inactivation of sodium channels and increased conductance to potassium then lower membrane potential again; persisting potassium conductance can produce an undershoot below the resting level. See Purves and colleagues: the ionic basis of action potentials.
For an illustrative electrical calculation, write inward sodium electric current as INa​=gNa​(ENa​−V). At V=−50mV, taking ENa​=60mV and gNa​=10nS gives INa​=1.1nA inward. A potassium current IK​=gK​(EK​−V) with EK​=−80mV and gK​=20nS gives −0.6nA, outward. These illustrative values expose opposing driving forces; a waveform requires the conductances to evolve with membrane potential and time, as in the Hodgkin-Huxley model.
An action potential propagates along an axon because local electric currents bring neighboring regions of cell membrane into the regime where their voltage-gated ion channels regenerate the signal. It is a traveling pattern of excitation, not a packet of sodium ions traversing the entire axon. Regeneration allows an action potential to travel without the passive attenuation of a small local voltage change. See Ion Channels and the Electrical Properties of Membranes.
The neuronal refractory period constrains how soon another action potential can follow. Under comparable conditions, successive action potentials have roughly stereotyped shapes, making event count and timing useful for neural coding; waveform details can nevertheless change with channel state and conditions. The leaky integrate-and-fire model abstracts each action potential to a threshold event and reset. Its reset jump is a mathematical rule, not the actual regenerative waveform produced by sodium and potassium currents.
