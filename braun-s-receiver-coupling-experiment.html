Braun's receiver coupling experiment

OurBigBook.com Words: 156
In his 1909 lecture, Karl Ferdinand Braun recalled two experimental stations on Strasbourg forts in summer 1902. Known capacitances and calculated inductances set the transmitter tuning; the receiver searched for its two coupled oscillations. Its receiving coil fed a second coil in a tuned indicator circuit. Separating these coils increased both the sharpness of resonance and excitation intensity, until further separation reduced the response. The varied coil spacing was the control; transmitter coupling need not be loose for this receiver effect. For intensity measurements, Braun described an air-thermometer readout in the related resonant measuring apparatus.[ref]
The comparison tests receiver coupling, not an unrestricted law that looser coupling always helps.
