Domain with a nonzero idempotent maximal ideal (0=m=m2)

OurBigBook.com Words: 132
Let
R=⋃n≥0​Q[un​],un​=t1/2n,un​=un+12​.
(40)
This ring consists of finite sums of rational multiples of nonnegative dyadic rational powers of t, interpreted as functions on t>0. Every pair of elements lies in one polynomial ring Q[un​], so R is an integral domain. The constant term defines a surjective ring homomorphism R→Q. Its kernel of a ring homomorphism m is nonzero and is a maximal ideal, by the maximal ideal quotient criterion.
Every f∈m has the form un​g(un​) for some n and some polynomial g. The identity
f=un+1​(un+1​g(un+12​))
(41)
expresses it as a product of two elements of m. Therefore m=m2, and mn=m for all n≥1. This shows that strict descent of powers of an ideal can fail in an integral domain, even for a nonzero maximal ideal. By the determinant trick, such an ideal cannot be finitely generated, so this ring is not a Noetherian ring.
