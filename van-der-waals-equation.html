Van der Waals equation

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Equation of state
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
The Van der Waals equation is a simple real gas equation of state that can also describe a liquid-gas critical point. With particle count N fixed, write the volume per particle as v=V/N:
p=v−bkB​T​−v2a​,v>b,a>0,b>0,
(155)
or equivalently (p+aN2/V2)(V−Nb)=NkB​T. Here p is pressure, T is absolute temperature, and kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. In SI, v and b have units m3 per particle, a has units Pam6=Jm3, and kB​ has units JK−1; the particle count is dimensionless. The pole at v=b excludes smaller volume values from this model.
The b correction represents excluded volume; the attraction term lowers pressure at fixed temperature and particle number density 1/v. This is a mean-field theory, replacing detailed intermolecular structure by two parameters. At v≫b with a/v2≪kB​T/v, it approaches the ideal gas law pv=kB​T. It supplies a useful qualitative route from a dilute gas to phase coexistence, without a claim of quantitative accuracy for every fluid.[ref]
Do not mix this particle convention with the molar volume convention elsewhere in this article. With amount of substance nmol​=N/NA​, Avogadro constant NA​, and molar gas constant R=NA​kB​, one has vm​=NA​v, bm​=NA​b, and am​=NA2​a, so p=RT/(vm​−bm​)−am​/vm2​. The molar parameters have units m3mol−1 and Pam6mol−2, respectively.
Johannes Diderik van der Waals introduced this equation of state in 1873, connecting molecular attraction with departures from the dilute ideal gas law. Its historical significance is the unified treatment of gases and liquids, rather than a universally exact fit.[ref]
