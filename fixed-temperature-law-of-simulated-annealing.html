Fixed-temperature law of simulated annealing (πT​(x)=ZT−1​e−E(x)/T)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Heuristic optimization Simulated annealing
OurBigBook.com Words: 285
On a finite state space F with finite objective function values, the fixed-T target is a Boltzmann distribution:
πT​(x)=ZT​e−E(x)/T​,ZT​=∑z∈F​e−E(z)/T.
(17)
The partition function ZT​ normalizes the weights but cancels from the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability. A short detailed balance proof explains the target. With symmetric q and x=y,
πT​(x)PT​(x,y)=ZT​q(x,y)​min{e−E(x)/T,e−E(y)/T}=πT​(y)PT​(y,x).
(18)
Summing over x gives πT​PT​=πT​, so this is a stationary distribution. On a finite state space, a connected graph of positive symmetric proposals makes the accepted Markov chain an irreducible Markov chain. Convergence from any starting state additionally requires an aperiodic Markov chain; one positive self-transition suffices under irreducibility. Replacing q by (I+q)/2 ensures this. For asymmetric q, require connectivity through bidirectional proposals: one-way proposals are rejected.[ref]
In the five-state barrier example for simulated annealing, T∗​=1/ln2 gives exact Boltzmann weights 2−E(x). Multiplying by 16 gives
πT∗​​=331​(4,8,1,4,16),ZT∗​​=1633​.
(19)
For the uphill edge, detailed balance is visible numerically:
πT∗​​(1)PT∗​​(1,2)=338​161​=661​=331​21​=πT∗​​(2)PT∗​​(2,1).
(20)
Thus the global minimum has the largest individual stationary probability, 16/33, but is far from certain at this positive T∗​. At T∗​/2 the exact stationary distribution is (16,64,1,16,256)/353.
For any finite F, let M be the set of global minima and Emin​ the smallest objective function value. Cancelling e−Emin​/T from numerator and denominator shows that each nonminimal state's weight tends to zero as T↓0, while every state in M has equal weight. Therefore πT​ tends to the uniform distribution on M. This is a statement about a family of equilibrium probability distributions; it does not prove that a cooling Markov chain follows that family.
