Dilute-gas Bose-Einstein condensation

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Quantum ideal-gas statistics Bose-Einstein statistics Bose-Einstein distribution Bose-Einstein condensation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 12
In a dilute gas, Bose-Einstein condensation makes a single one-particle state hold a substantial fraction of the bosons. This is a population effect: cooling reduces the number that the excited energy eigenstates can accommodate at thermal equilibrium. The surplus accumulates in a Bose-Einstein condensate. For an ideal gas the occupied mode is the trap ground state; interactions can deform that mode, as described by the Gross–Pitaevskii equation. Dilute does not mean classical: a small number density can still give overlapping thermal de Broglie wavelengths at sufficiently low temperature.
The appeal is that changing the trap changes a calculable Quantum Hamiltonian, while absorption imaging makes its population distribution visible. The Bose condensation in a three-dimensional harmonic trap below turns density of states into a prediction for a critical temperature and condensate fraction. These are separate questions from measuring matter-wave coherence or superfluidity.
Eric A. Cornell and Carl E. Wieman's joint lecture emphasizes the competition between useful elastic collisions, which restore thermal equilibrium during evaporative cooling, and collisions that remove or heat the trapped sample. A long trap lifetime alone is insufficient: reequilibration must keep up with evaporation.[ref]
