Kinetic friction (∣Ffr​∣=μN)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newtonian mechanics Friction
OurBigBook.com Words: 566 Articles: 2
Kinetic friction is friction during nonzero contact slip. For one-dimensional motion along a surface, choose right as positive and write v for the body's contact-point velocity and U for the surface's contact-point velocity, both measured in the same inertial frame. The signed relative velocity is w=v−U. The ideal Coulomb law for the tangential force on the body is
fk​=−μk​Nsgn(v−U),v−U=0.
(11)
Here N≥0 is the magnitude of the normal force, μk​≥0 is the coefficient of kinetic friction, and sgn is the sign function. Thus kinetic friction opposes contact slip, which can differ from the body's direction of motion. In several tangential directions the same law reads fk​=−μk​Nw/∣w∣ for a nonzero tangential relative velocity w. The magnitude law is documented in Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §6.2: static and kinetic friction.
For a stipulated toy model, take a nonrotating box with mass m=1kg, normal force N=10N, coefficient of static friction μs​=0.5, and coefficient of kinetic friction μk​=0.3. These are assumed model parameters, rather than measured properties of any particular pair of materials; the prescribed normal force is consistent with horizontal support under g=10m/s2 and no other vertical forces. On stationary ground, a box already sliding right has fk​=−3N. With an applied horizontal force F=6N to the right, Newton's second law gives a=(F+fk​)/m=3m/s2, for as long as rightward contact slip persists.
Direction is clearer by comparing two instantaneous states of this same model. If the box has v=1m/s on stationary ground, U=0 and w=+1m/s, so kinetic friction acts left with magnitude 3N. If the box still has v=1m/s but a belt beneath it has U=2m/s, then w=−1m/s: kinetic friction acts right with magnitude 3N, even while the box moves right.
In the belt case, the power delivered to the box is Pbox​=fk​v=+3W. With no other horizontal force, its kinetic energy therefore increases by the work-energy theorem. By Newton's third law, the box exerts −fk​ on the belt, so the contact power delivered to the belt is Pbelt​=−fk​U=−6W. Their sum is −3W: frictional heating produces thermal internal energy at 3W. An ideal motor maintaining the prescribed belt velocity supplies +6W against this contact load, neglecting other losses, so its input divides into 3W of box kinetic energy and 3W of frictional heating. These are instantaneous rates, since the box's velocity subsequently changes. This balance follows directly from power as force times velocity and conservation of energy; the power law is given in Ling, Sanny and Moebs, OpenStax University Physics 1, §7.4: power.
When contact slip vanishes, including a transition to rolling without slipping, the nonzero-slip law no longer applies. Determine the required static friction from the no-slip condition and the equations of motion, then test its bound. Substituting sgn(0)=0 into the sliding law would incorrectly exclude a nonzero static friction.
