Eigenspace (Eλ​=ker(A−λI))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Eigenvalue
OurBigBook.com Words: 342 Articles: 4
For an eigenvalue λ of a linear operator A:V→V, its eigenspace is
Eλ​=ker(A−λI)={v∈V:Av=λv}.
(54)
It contains all eigenvectors for λ and the zero vector. Including zero makes this a linear subspace: if v,w∈Eλ​, then A(av+bw)=λ(av+bw) for any scalars a,b. Every nonzero vector in this linear subspace has the same scaling under A. Its dimension is the geometric multiplicity of the eigenvalue. If λ is not an eigenvalue, the shifted kernel is only {0}.
For the symmetric matrix A=(21​12​) from eigenvector,
E3​=span{(1,1)T},E1​=span{(1,−1)T}.
(55)
Each eigenspace is a line. An eigenspace can also be a plane or the whole vector space: for B=diag(2,2,5), E2​={(x,y,0)T:x,y∈R} is a plane of dimension two, while for C=2I every vector belongs to E2​. A repeated eigenvalue therefore need not prevent diagonalizability.
The useful unit of information is the whole eigenspace, rather than a particular choice of eigenvectors inside it. On Eλ​, A is simply multiplication by λ. Distinct eigenspaces fit together as a direct sum, and a finite-dimensional linear operator is diagonalizable exactly when they fill the whole vector space. For the example above, every vector splits uniquely as
(xy​)=2x+y​(11​)+2x−y​(1−1​).
(56)
This decomposition makes the independent scalings visible without multiplying out the original matrix again.
