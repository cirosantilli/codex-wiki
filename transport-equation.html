Transport equation (ut​+b⋅∇u=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 75
A transport equation carries a quantity along the flow of a velocity field b. For a smooth solution of ut​+b⋅∇u=0, the value of u is constant along each characteristic curve. With constant speed c, the chain rule gives dtd​u(x0​+ct,t)=ut​+cux​=0, hence u(x,t)=g(x−ct). This is exact translation: it preserves the shape rather than smoothing it, as the periodic heat and transport comparison shows.

Periodic heat and transport comparison (uH​=1+e−tcosx+0.4e−9tcos(3x),uT​=g(x−t))

Words: 236
Transport equation OurBigBook.com
Use dimensionless x and t, the periodic domain [0,2π], and the same initial condition
g(x)=1+cosx+0.4cos(3x).
(1)
With zero PDE source term, compare the heat equation ut​=uxx​ and the transport equation ut​+ux​=0. Their exact solutions are
uH​(x,t)=1+e−tcosx+0.4e−9tcos(3x),uT​(x,t)=1+cos(x−t)+0.4cos(3(x−t)).
(2)
Each cosine satisfies ∂x2​cos(nx)=−n2cos(nx), so the heat equation reduces its amplitude according to An′​=−n2An​. The superposition principle combines the constant and two Fourier modes. The transport equation shifts the argument of the entire profile. Both formulas meet the initial condition and matching periodic boundary conditions exactly.[ref]
At t=1, the heat amplitudes are approximately 0.368 and 0.0000494: the small ripples disappear much sooner than the broad variation. The transport equation retains amplitudes 1 and 0.4, shifting the profile right by one unit with wraparound. Both preserve spatial average 1, but only the heat equation approaches the uniform profile. This gives diffusion its usefulness for smoothing uneven temperatures, while the transport equation models a profile carried by a flow. The signed profile can represent a scaled temperature deviation.
To restore units, let physical position be X=ℓx and time be T=τt. For UT​=DUXX​, the dimensionless coefficient is Dτ/ℓ2, with D measured in length squared per time. For UT​+cUX​=0, it is cτ/ℓ, with c measured in length per time. Both plotted coefficients equal one when D=ℓ2/τ and c=ℓ/τ. Thus the example compares specified models on the same scales, rather than identifying diffusivity with velocity.

Elliptic characteristics of a planar transport equation

Words: 111
Method of characteristics OurBigBook.com
For the linear partial differential equation 2yux​−xuy​=0, the method of characteristics gives x˙=2y, y˙​=−x, u˙=0. Thus ϕ=x2+2y2 is constant on each characteristic curve, whose nonzero level sets are ellipses. On a domain containing entire such ellipses, the solutions have the form u=F(ϕ), subject to the desired regularity at the origin.
For the inhomogeneous equation 2yux​−xuy​=xy(2y2−x2), a particular solution is up​=x2y2/2, so the general solution is u=x2y2/2+F(x2+2y2). Prescribing u(x,1)=x2 fixes F(s)=(s−2)/2 only for s≥2. The inner ellipses s<2 never intersect that data line, so the boundary values leave their solution values undetermined. Smooth extensions must match the prescribed F at s=2; choosing the same linear formula everywhere gives one polynomial extension.

Characteristic components of a hyperbolic first-order equation (yfx​+xfy​=0)

Words: 117
Method of characteristics OurBigBook.com
The characteristic ordinary differential equations x˙=y, y˙​=x preserve u=x2−y2. Away from the origin, u=x2−y2, v=2xy have nonzero Jacobian determinant 4(x2+y2), and the chain rule reduces the displayed equation to 2(x2+y2)fv​=0. Hence locally f=F(u).
Globally, a level set of u can have disconnected characteristic components, permitting different profiles on its different branches. A smooth counterexample on the whole plane is f=exp[−1/(x2−y2)2] where x>0 and x2>y2, and f=0 elsewhere. The exponential is flat on the wedge boundary, so this extension is smooth and satisfies the equation. Nevertheless the points (1,0) and (−1,0) have equal u and different function values. One global profile F(u) therefore needs an additional branch-identification condition, rather than following from the local change of variables alone.

Riemann problem (U(x,t)=U(x/t))

Words: 248 Articles: 2
System of conservation laws OurBigBook.com
A Riemann problem for a one-dimensional system of conservation laws ∂t​U+∂x​f(U)=0 has two constant initial states, UL​ for x<0 and UR​ for x>0. The data and equation are invariant under (x,t)↦(λx,λt). When the physically admissible entropy solution is unique, this invariance makes it a self-similar solution depending on x/t. For ideal-gas compressible flow, the characteristic speeds are u−c,u,u+c. The two acoustic families carry shock waves or rarefaction waves, while the middle family carries a contact discontinuity. Sufficiently strong separating velocities can instead create a vacuum region.

Single-rarefaction matching conditions for an ideal-gas Riemann problem (Δu=2σΔc/(γ−1))

Words: 83
Riemann problem OurBigBook.com
Two positive uniform ideal-gas states can be joined by one nontrivial rarefaction wave precisely when their specific entropy agrees, pL​/ρLγ​=pR​/ρRγ​, and for one σ∈{±1} they satisfy uR​−uL​=2σ(cR​−cL​)/(γ−1) with σ(cR​−cL​)>0. These equations are the constant-entropy and Riemann invariant conditions of the self-similar ideal-gas rarefaction. They are sufficient because its affine u,c profiles match the two states at ξL​=uL​+σcL​ and ξR​=uR​+σcR​, and ξR​>ξL​. The left family has cR​<cL​, while the right family has cR​>cL​. The inequality uR​>uL​ alone does not impose the two matching equalities.

Contact discontinuity ([u]=[p]=0)

Words: 86
Riemann problem OurBigBook.com
A contact discontinuity in one-dimensional gas dynamics moves with the common fluid velocity s=uL​=uR​ and has continuous pressure, while mass density and specific entropy can jump. The mass jump condition s[ρ]=[ρu] is automatic with common u=s; momentum conservation s[ρu]=[ρu2+p] then requires [p]=0. The energy jump condition is also satisfied. Thus a contact is a material interface, not a compression shock. A smooth self-similar positive-density flow cannot spread this zero-speed-relative-to-the-fluid family into a fan: u=x/t on an interval would require both du/d(x/t)=1 and, from continuity, du/d(x/t)=0.

For a differentiable scalar conservation law ut​+f(u)x​=0, a sonic point of a scalar flux is a state s where its characteristic speed f′(s) vanishes. If f is convex and this zero is unique, f′<0 on the left and f′>0 on the right, so s is the unique minimum of the flux. Uniformly convex scalar flux (F′′≥κ>0)

Words: 64

In the common PDE convention, a uniformly convex scalar flux is a C2 flux satisfying F′′(z)≥κ>0 throughout the specified state interval, with one constant κ. Equivalently, F(z)−κz2/2 is a convex function. This guarantees strict inequality below nontrivial chords and strict ordering of the two endpoint derivatives around the chord slope. Radiative shock

Words: 119 Articles: 1
Shock wave OurBigBook.com
A shock wave with significant radiation losses in its downstream evolution. Often a narrow dissipative jump initially heats the gas according to adiabatic Rankine-Hugoniot conditions, followed by a much longer radiative cooling layer. The gas's specific entropy increases at the jump and may then fall as radiation carries entropy away. If both distant states have equal temperature, the combined mass and momentum balances give an isothermal shock; the gas's energy flux is not conserved across the whole radiating region.

The Rankine-Hugoniot condition expresses conservation laws across a moving discontinuity of a weak solution. Across a discontinuity x=σt in ut​+f(u)x​=0, with left and right states u−​ and u+​, conservation requires
σ(u+​−u−​)=f(u+​)−f(u−​).
