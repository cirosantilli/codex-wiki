Isotope scaling of diatomic vibration (ν′/ν≃μ/μ′​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Center of mass Reduced mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 499 Articles: 1
Near a stable equilibrium bond length re​, a Taylor expansion of a diatomic molecule's potential energy gives U(r)≃U(re​)+k(r−re​)2/2, with k=U′′(re​)>0. Treat the internal stretch as a harmonic oscillator of reduced mass, initially omitting molecular rotation. If replacing an isotope leaves this local potential energy curvature unchanged, then
νν′​≃μ′μ​​,νharm​=2πc1​μk​​.
(256)
Here c is the speed of light and νharm​ a spectroscopic wavenumber; use consistent length units for c and νharm​. The quantum harmonic oscillator gives adjacent energy eigenvalues separated by hν, with h the Planck constant. Consequently a vibrational photon absorption can probe the same frequency scale, subject to electric-dipole selection rules.
For an explicitly approximate isotope example, assign the atomic masses of 1H, 2H, 35Cl, and 37Cl the mass-number values 1u,2u,35u,37u, respectively, with u the unified atomic mass unit. These are stipulated approximations to individual atomic masses, not measured masses of molecules or natural-abundance averages. They give
μH35Cl​=3635​u,μD35Cl​=3770​u,νH35Cl​νD35Cl​​≃7237​​≈0.716860,νH35Cl​νH37Cl​​≃666665​​≈0.999249.
(257)
The symbol D means deuterium, 2H. Doubling the light partner's approximate atomic mass therefore produces a large predicted shift, whereas changing the already heavy chlorine partner by two mass units produces a much smaller one. A quantitative comparison with infrared spectroscopy requires actual isotope-specific atomic masses, fitted band parameters, and uncertainties, rather than identifying any arbitrary peak with νharm​.
