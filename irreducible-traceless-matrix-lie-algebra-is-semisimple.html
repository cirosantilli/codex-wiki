Irreducible traceless matrix Lie algebra is semisimple (L≤sl(V),V irreducible ⟹ radL=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Diagonal dominance Lie theory Lie algebra Semisimple Lie algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
Let V be a finite-dimensional complex vector space and let L be a Lie subalgebra of its traceless endomorphisms acting irreducibly. Its solvable radical R is simultaneously triangularizable by Lie's theorem. The resulting finite set of diagonal characters is invariant under conjugation by etx for x∈L, because this conjugation preserves the ideal R and gives an equivalent representation. Each character varies continuously with t in a finite set, hence is constant; differentiation gives λ([x,r])=0.
A nonzero common R-eigenspace exists by Lie's theorem. The displayed vanishing makes it invariant under all of L: r(xv)=λ(r)xv+λ([r,x])v=λ(r)xv. Irreducibility therefore makes it all of V. Thus R acts by scalars, and the zero-trace condition forces those scalars to vanish. The matrix representation is faithful, so R=0. The traceless condition matters: adjoining scalar matrices to an irreducible semisimple algebra gives a reductive algebra with nonzero centre.
