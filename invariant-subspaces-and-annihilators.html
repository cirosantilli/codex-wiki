Invariant subspaces and annihilators (T(U)⊆U⟹T′(U0)⊆U0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Dual space Annihilator of a vector subspace
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
For a linear map T:V→V, an invariant subspace U has an annihilator of a vector subspace U0 invariant under the dual map T′. Indeed, for φ∈U0 and u∈U,
(T′φ)(u)=φ(Tu)=0.
(10)
For a finite-dimensional vector space, the converse also holds. If T′(U0)⊆U0, every φ∈U0 vanishes on Tu for each u∈U. The dual basis description of U0 shows that the common zero set of its linear functionals is exactly U, so Tu∈U. Thus
U is invariant under T⟺U0 is invariant under T′​.
(11)
The dimension identity dimU0=dimV−dimU exchanges a one-dimensional invariant subspace with an invariant hyperplane in the dual space.
