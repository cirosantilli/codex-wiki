Normal incidence (θi​=0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Refraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 58
At normal incidence, the incident light ray follows the surface normal vector. Snell's law gives θt​=0 for positive refractive indices, so the transmitted light ray does not bend. Nevertheless, its phase velocity and wavelength change if the refractive index changes. A single axial light ray therefore cannot measure apparent depth: neighboring light rays and their directions supply that information. A reflected component can still be present; no bending of the transmitted light ray does not imply complete transmission.
