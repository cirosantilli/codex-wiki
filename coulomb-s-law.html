Coulomb's law (F2←1​=kq1​q2​r/∣r∣3)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrostatics
OurBigBook.com Words: 940 Articles: 7
Coulomb's law gives the mutual electric force between stationary point charges in vacuum. Put electric charges q1​ and q2​ at r1​ and r2​, and define the displacement from charge 1 to charge 2 by r=r2​−r1​, with separation r=∣r∣>0 and unit vector r=r/r. In SI units, the force on charge 2 due to charge 1 is
F2←1​=kq1​q2​∣r∣3r​=r2kq1​q2​​r,k=4πε0​1​.​
(5)
Here ε0​ is the vacuum permittivity. The magnitude is k∣q1​q2​∣/r2. If q1​q2​>0, the force on charge 2 points away from charge 1; if q1​q2​<0, it points toward charge 1. Interchanging the charges gives F1←2​=−F2←1​, consistent with Newton's third law in this electrostatic setting. [ref].
For a fully computed example, stipulate q1​=+2nC, q2​=−3nC, r1​=0, r2​=0.20mx and the approximate value k=8.99×109Nm2/C2. Then
F2←1​=(0.20)2(8.99×109)(2×10−9)(−3×10−9)​Nx=−1.3485×10−6Nx.
(6)
The force is attractive, of magnitude 1.3485×10−6N. The source electric field at charge 2 is E1​(r2​)=449.5N/Cx, pointing away from the positive source. Multiplication by the negative q2​ reverses the direction to give the attractive force. This field excludes charge 2's own self-field.
Holding the electric charges fixed while changing the separation from R to 2R or 3R gives the normalized inverse-square law values
∣F(R)∣∣F(R)∣​=1,∣F(R)∣∣F(2R)∣​=41​,∣F(R)∣∣F(3R)∣​=91​.
(7)
For the stipulated example, separations 0.20, 0.40 and 0.60m give magnitudes 1.3485×10−6, 3.37125×10−7 and 1.498333…×10−7N. The diagram computes these ratios and uses one common arrow-length scale for both sign choices.
The inverse-square law has a geometric explanation through Gauss's law: a source point charge gives fixed total electric flux through a concentric sphere, whose surface area is 4πr2. Dividing that flux by the area gives a radial electric field proportional to 1/r2. This connects a pairwise force calculation to a global conservation-like accounting of field sources. For several stationary point charges, the superposition principle adds the individual electric fields as vectors, and Fi​=qi​Eother​(ri​) uses all sources except charge i itself. The Coulomb potential energy gives an alternative route when the desired quantity is work rather than instantaneous force.
