Use natural units c=1 and the Minkowski metric with signs (+,−,−,−). In the ideal centre-of-momentum frame, assume a known initial four-momentum P=(s​,0), no unaccounted radiation and exact visible reconstruction. Define
Pmiss​=P−pe​−pμ​=(Emiss​,pmiss​),Mmiss2​=Pmiss2​=Emiss2​−∣pmiss​∣2.
(47)
The missing four-momentum in electron-positron collisions is a subtraction of measured quantities. It is not itself a particle detector hit. With Meμ2​=(pe​+pμ​)2, expansion gives the useful identity
Mmiss2​=s+Meμ2​−2s​(Ee​+Eμ​).
(48)
For any collection of future-directed massless invisible four-momenta qi​=Ei​(1,ni​),
Mmiss2​=2∑i<j​Ei​Ej​(1−ni​⋅nj​)≥0.
(49)
Thus a positive missing invariant mass excludes exactly one massless invisible particle under the stated assumptions. It does not exclude one massive invisible particle, identify a neutrino flavor, or determine the number of massless invisible particles beyond that lower bound. Several collinear massless particles can even have zero combined invariant mass. Detector resolution and unaccounted radiation invalidate a literal event-by-event interpretation of these ideal inequalities.
For an original exact example in natural units, choose total energy 6GeV and visible massless four-momenta, in GeV,
pe​=(1;1,0,0),pμ​=(1;0,1,0),Pmiss​=(4;−1,−1,0),Mmiss2​=14GeV2.
(50)
Two possible invisible massless four-momenta are (2;−1/2,−1/2,14​/2) and (2;−1/2,−1/2,−14​/2). Splitting either into two positive collinear parts gives a three-particle interpretation with identical recoil. Alternatively, one invisible particle of mass 14​GeV has that recoil. This example tests the ambiguity of the subtraction, without asserting any of these alternatives is a tau lepton decay.
