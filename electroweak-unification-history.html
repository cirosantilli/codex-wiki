Electroweak unification history

OurBigBook.com Words: 225
Sheldon Lee Glashow's 1979 lecture, Towards a Unified Theory: Threads in a Tapestry, recalls his 1961 SU(2)×U(1) construction: a photon, a massive neutral intermediary and a mixing angle. It also stresses its unresolved mass problem: symmetry-breaking masses had been inserted, rather than generated by the Higgs mechanism. Establishing the gauge structure and explaining the gauge boson masses were complementary steps.[ref]
Steven Weinberg's A Model of Leptons (1967) builds a spontaneously broken gauge theory for the electron-type leptons, using a scalar doublet to give masses while retaining a massless photon. It derives neutral and charged couplings and discusses neutrino-electron scattering. Its closing question about renormalizability is explicitly unresolved; the paper is neither a proof of that property nor a complete treatment of quarks.[ref]
Abdus Salam's 1979 lecture, Gauge Unification of Fundamental Forces, describes his and Steven Weinberg's completion of the spontaneously broken SU(2)×U(1) formulation for leptons. He separates that construction from the subsequent establishment of renormalizability. Its role is to connect the mass mechanism with predictive neutral and charged couplings, not to identify a photographed event. The Gargamelle evidence subsequently tested the predicted neutral current.[ref]
