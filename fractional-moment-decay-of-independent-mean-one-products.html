Fractional moment decay of independent mean-one products (E[Ynr​]=(E[X1r​])n,0<r<1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale Product martingale from independent mean-one factors
OurBigBook.com Words: 106
Suppose (Xn​) are identically distributed independent random variables with Xn​≥0, EXn​=1, and X1​ not constant almost surely. For each 0<r<1, strict concavity of tr gives
0<q:=E[X1r​]<1.
(15)
The moment is finite because tr≤1+t on [0,∞). Let Yn​=∏k=1n​Xk​. Independence and Markov's inequality give
E[Ynr​]=qn,P(Yn​>ε)≤ε−rqn.
(16)
The series of the latter probabilities is finite. The First Borel-Cantelli lemma, applied for the countable choices ε=1/m, proves Yn​→0 almost surely. This proof requires neither existence nor integrability of logX1​.
Although this nonnegative martingale has EYn​=1 at every time, its terminal expected value is zero. It is therefore not uniformly integrable. The contrast is useful: bounded expected values ensure almost sure convergence here, but do not allow preservation of the expected value through the limit.
