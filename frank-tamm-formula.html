Frank-Tamm formula (d2N/(dxdλ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Cherenkov radiation
OurBigBook.com Words: 494 Articles: 1
For constant particle speed and electric charge q=ze, in a transparent, linear, nonmagnetic medium with isotropy, the Frank-Tamm formula gives the produced photon density:
dxdλd2N​=λ22παz2​(1−β2n2(λ)1​),βn(λ)>1.
(64)
Set it to zero below threshold. Here e is the positive elementary charge, x is track length, λ=2πc/ω is vacuum wavelength, and α is the fine-structure constant. With SI lengths in metres, the density has units m−2; multiplying by dxdλ gives a dimensionless expected count. The explicit SI convention is
α=4πϵ0​ℏce2​,dxdωd2N​=cαz2​(1−β2n2(ω)1​).
(65)
These conventions agree.[ref]
To check the convention, start from E=ℏω=hc/λ and d2N/(dxdE)=αz2sin2θ/(ℏc). Changing variables uses the positive density Jacobian ∣dE/dλ∣=hc/λ2; since h/ℏ=2π, the coefficient becomes 2παz2. No factor of the material refractive index belongs in this Jacobian: λ is not the in-medium wavelength. Multiplication by ℏω gives the corresponding energy loss density in this SI nonmagnetic convention:
dxdωd2W​=4πϵ0​c2z2e2ω​(1−β2n2(ω)1​).
(66)
For an original constant-index example, stipulate n=1.50, β=0.90, z=1 and α=1/137.036. Integration from 400 to 700nm gives
LN​=2πα(1−1.3521​)(400×10−9m1​−700×10−9m1​)≃2.217×104m−1.
(67)
Thus a stipulated 1cm track produces about 222 photons in this band in the ideal model. This is an emitted count before absorption, collection losses and detector response, not a brightness measurement or a measured water yield.
