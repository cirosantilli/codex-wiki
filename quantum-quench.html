A quantum quench changes a quantum system's control parameters rapidly and studies the subsequent unitary time evolution. In the ideal sudden limit, write an initial energy eigenstate ∣mi​⟩ using a complete final orthonormal eigenbasis:
∣mi​⟩=∑n​cn​∣nf​⟩,cn​=⟨nf​∣mi​⟩,∣ψ(t)⟩=∑n​cn​e−iEnf​t/ℏ∣nf​⟩.
(28)
The Born rule probabilities for a nondegenerate final energy measurement are ∣cn​∣2, with ∑n​∣cn​∣2=1. For a degenerate energy eigenvalue, sum the probabilities over its orthonormal energy eigenstates. Subsequent relative quantum phases can alter other measurement probabilities even though these final energy populations stay fixed. A quench need not begin in an energy eigenstate; the same expansion applies to any initial pure state.
