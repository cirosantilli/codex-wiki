Use decimal expansion, B=10, n=4, m=2, and q=100. The Karatsuba operand splitting is 1234=12⋅100+34, 5678=56⋅100+78. The Karatsuba three-product identity gives
p2​p0​ps​hxy​=12⋅56=672,=34⋅78=2652,=(12+34)(56+78)=46⋅134=6164,=6164−672−2652=2840,=672⋅10000+2840⋅100+2652=7,006,652.​
(52)
The third product has a three-digit operand. A small-input base case can evaluate these three products directly, or a smaller threshold can continue recursion. Either choice preserves the identity; only the operation count changes.
The same Karatsuba operand splitting handles 123⋅45: choose n=3, m=2, and write 123=1⋅100+23, 45=0⋅100+45. Then p2​=0, p0​=1035, ps​=24⋅45=1080, and h=45, giving 4500+1035=5535. A zero high block and unequal lengths require no new algebraic rule.
