Stern-Gerlach expectation estimation

 ... Quantum mechanics Quantum system Quantum state Observable Expectation value Estimating a quantum expectation value
OurBigBook.com Words: 349
A concrete idealized implementation uses a Stern-Gerlach experiment twice: a preparation filter along n=(sinθ,0,cosθ) passes only its plus spin channel, and a z analyzer resolves both output channels for many fresh atoms. This prepares the spin one-half along an axis state ∣ψθ​⟩. The authored apparatus description uses slit collimation, a nonuniform magnetic field with a pointed pole, and counters at the separated outputs.[ref] The two-channel spin one-half preparation and analyzer are described separately; the source's recombining “improved” apparatus is explicitly idealized, so the setup here is a proposed estimation model, not a report of observed counts.[ref]
Count every accepted analyzer event in its calibrated +z or −z channel and compute μ​N​ from the count difference. The magnetic field gradient separates trajectories through their magnetic moments; calibrated channel labels convert positions into spin eigenvalues. The readout is a population imbalance, not an intermediate trajectory carrying spin ℏcosθ/2. Beam position also depends on flight time and field geometry, so averaging raw positions alone does not establish a calibrated spin expectation value.
As controls, feed known ∣+z⟩ and ∣−z⟩ preparations to check the channel assignment and leakage, hold the analyzer axis fixed, and repeat short blocks to check preparation drift. Measure background counts and relative channel efficiencies. With efficiencies η+​ and η−​ and negligible background, the detected plus fraction is
p+det​=η+​p+​+η−​(1−p+​)η+​p+​​.
(37)
Unequal efficiencies bias the naive sample mean, even for arbitrarily many counts. For example, p+​=3/4, η+​=1, and η−​=1/2 give detected fraction 6/7 and naive mean 5ℏ/14, rather than ℏ/4. Background, overlapping beams, detector saturation, angle misalignment, unwanted internal states, and preparation correlations require a better quantum measurement model. Increasing N only reduces the independent sampling contribution; it cannot remove these systematic errors. The selected preparation beam is the population being estimated, so losses in preparing it must be distinguished from losses in reading its two outcomes.
