Couette flow with a slipping lower wall (u(z)=U(z+b)/(H+b))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Navier slip boundary condition Slip length
OurBigBook.com Words: 262
Consider steady flow of a Newtonian fluid in incompressible flow with constant dynamic viscosity μ>0 between parallel plates at z=0 and z=H. The bottom plate is stationary and obeys the Navier slip boundary condition with slip length b. The top plate moves at velocity U>0 and obeys the no-slip boundary condition. There is no streamwise pressure gradient or body force. The Couette flow solution is
u(z)=UH+bz+b​,u(0)=H+bUb​,τ=μu′(z)=H+bμU​.
(18)
Here τ is the constant shear stress magnitude. The lower wall's Navier slip boundary condition holds exactly because bu′(0)=bU/(H+b)=u(0). At the top, u(H)=U is exactly the prescribed no-slip boundary condition; no finite upper-wall slip length is imposed in this example.
For a numerical example, stipulate H=10μm, b=2μm, U=1mms−1 and μ=10−3Pas. The bottom velocity is 0.167mms−1 and the shear stress is 0.0833Pa, compared with 0.100Pa when both plates obey the no-slip boundary condition. The extrapolated profile reaches zero at z=−2μm.
For fixed H, U and μ, b→0 recovers u=Uz/H. As b→∞, u→U throughout the gap and τ→0: the moving upper wall selects the limiting uniform velocity, while the lower wall approaches a stress-free boundary condition. The dynamic viscosity stays positive; the disappearing shear stress comes from the disappearing velocity gradient.
A short derivation uses unidirectional flow: the convective acceleration vanishes and the Navier-Stokes equation reduces to μu′′=0. Write u=Az+B. The bottom condition gives B=bA and the top condition gives A(H+b)=U.
