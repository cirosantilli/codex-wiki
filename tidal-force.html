Tidal force

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Tidal tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 960 Articles: 9
Differential gravitational acceleration, multiplied by the affected mass, across a spatially extended body. In the linear approximation the acceleration difference is TΔr, where T is the tidal tensor. Stellar tidal forces limit the domain of a circumplanetary orbit; sufficiently strong ones can produce tidal disruption.
To isolate a tidal force, subtract the gravitational acceleration shared by the body as a whole. Let a reference test particle follow R¨=g(R), and let another have position R+x. In a translating accelerated reference frame with parallel, nonrotating axes,
x¨=g(R+x)−g(R)=T(R)x+O(R4μ∣x∣2​)
(15)
for a source point mass, when ∣x∣/R≪1. Here μ=GM is its standard gravitational parameter and the last term uses Big O notation. This Taylor expansion removes the common inward pull and keeps the spatial variation. The near side has an excess pull toward the source; the far side has a deficit of inward pull, which is an outward relative acceleration. Both sides can stretch apart while both still accelerate toward the source.[ref]
For a material element of mass m, the residual external force in this translating accelerated reference frame is m[g(R+x)−g(R)]. Actual deformation also depends on elasticity, pressure and self-gravity. A rigid body approximation suppresses deformation but can still have internal forces that resist the tidal force. The point-mass tidal stretching and squeezing pattern describes freely falling, noninteracting test particles, rather than promising the same shape for every solid, star or ocean. A rotating reference frame introduces centrifugal acceleration and Coriolis acceleration, which must be included separately.
