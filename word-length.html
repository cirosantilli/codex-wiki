Word length (∣g∣S​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Geometric group theory Cayley graph Word metric
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
The word length is the cost of building a group element from the allowed generators of a group. For a symmetric generating set of a group S of a group G, define
∣g∣S​=min{n≥0:g=s1​⋯sn​, si​∈S}.
(25)
The empty word over an alphabet represents the identity element and has length zero. A representative with this least number of letters labels a shortest graph path from 1 to g in the Cayley graph. Thus word length minimizes over all representatives, rather than counting the letters in whichever expression we happened to write down.[ref]
For the additive integers with S={−1,1}, the expression 1+1−1 has three letters but represents 1, whose word length is one. With T={−2,−1,1,2}, the integer 5 has word length three, realized by 2+2+1. The Cayley graphs of the integers visualize why these are minimal. A freely reduced word is always shortest in a free group with its free generators and their inverses; free reduction alone need not give a shortest representative in a general group.
Concatenating representatives and reversing their letters with inverse elements give
∣gh∣S​≤∣g∣S​+∣h∣S​,∣g−1∣S​=∣g∣S​,∣g∣S​=dS​(1,g).
(26)
These make word length a length function. A finite S gives finite word metric balls, since only finitely many words over an alphabet have length at most a fixed radius. The definition itself also works for infinite symmetric generating sets of a group; the bounded-metric example in equivalence of finite word metrics shows what finiteness adds.
