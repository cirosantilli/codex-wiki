Normal vector

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 716 Articles: 7
A normal vector at a point of an embedded smooth manifold is orthogonal to every tangent vector in its tangent space, using the surrounding dot product. Its direction describes departure from the local tangent geometry. For a regular level set F(x)=c with nonzero gradient, ∇F is a normal vector: differentiation of F along any curve on the level set gives ∇F⋅r′=0.
For a plane curve, the normal direction is a straight line perpendicular to its unit tangent vector; the two unit normals differ by sign. For a curve in three-dimensional Euclidean space, the orthogonal directions form a two-dimensional plane. A principal normal vector selects one of them using curvature, so it must be distinguished from an arbitrary normal vector.
