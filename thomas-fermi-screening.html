Thomas-Fermi screening

 ... Quantum mechanics Quasiparticle Exciton Wannier-Mott exciton Relative permittivity Dielectric screening
OurBigBook.com Words: 137 Articles: 1
In the long-wavelength static response of a degenerate conductor, a small electric potential ϕ changes the electron density by δn=eg(EF​)ϕ, where g(EF​) is the spin-summed density of states per volume per energy. The induced charge is therefore −e2g(EF​)ϕ. With background permittivity εb​, Poisson equation becomes
(∇2−kTF2​)ϕ=−ρext​/εb​,kTF2​=e2g(EF​)/εb​.
(21)
A point charge Q then has ϕ(r)=Qe−kTF​r/(4πεb​r). Larger electronic density of states gives stronger screening. This local response approximation does not capture the microscopic oscillatory tail of Friedel oscillations.[ref]
