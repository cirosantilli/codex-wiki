Circular-aperture diffraction laboratory experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Point spread function Airy disk
OurBigBook.com Words: 328
An actual teaching setup is described in Laboratory Exercise No. 4: Diffraction, MIT 6.237, Fall 2024, for MIT's 6.237 Modern Optics Project Laboratory, Fall 2024. Preparation includes predicting the aperture-size dependence and the distinction between Fraunhofer diffraction and Fresnel diffraction. A helium–neon laser supplies 632.8nm light; the far-field setup uses a planar incident wavefront, a diffracting object and a distant screen, with beam expansion when needed. Figure 1 also marks an optional CCD camera. Exercise 4.7 asks students to sketch the circular-aperture pattern, measure the Airy disk diameter between correctly identified boundaries, and infer the aperture size. No measured values are supplied. As a regime comparison, exercise 4.9 varies the circular opening's position near a microscope objective optical lens, observes near-field patterns and compares them with the far-field case. This tests whether a change in propagation geometry changes the pattern. The coherent illumination measures one aperture's response, rather than the discrimination of two mutually incoherent optical point sources.[ref]
For a quantitative interpretation, let L be the aperture-to-screen distance and dspot​ the first-zero diameter on the screen. In the paraxial approximation, the independent calculation is
dspot​≃2Lθ1​=2πj1,1​​DλL​,D≃2πj1,1​​dspot​λL​.
(13)
For an explicitly hypothetical measurement, L=1.80m and dspot​=5.60mm at 632.8nm would imply D≃0.496mm. The scale D2/λ≃0.389m is smaller than L, but only by a factor of about 4.6; this alone gives limited confidence in a high-precision Fraunhofer diffraction approximation. It is not a reported MIT measurement.
Additional recommended controls are to keep wavelength fixed, record L, centre and uniformly illuminate the aperture, and repeat with another D. Compare dspot​/L, which should vary inversely with D. A camera readout should include blocked-beam background and avoid saturation. A scaled repetition at larger L tests residual Fresnel diffraction; nonuniform illumination, optical aberrations, detector sampling and uncertainty in locating a dark ring limit the inferred D. The screen pattern's apparent bright edge depends on display brightness and is not a substitute for the first-zero boundary.
