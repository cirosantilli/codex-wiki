Arcsine quantiles and moments (Q(p)=sin2(πp/2))

Inverting F(x)=p gives the quantile function
Q(p)=sin2(2πp​),0≤p≤1.
(11)
Consequently, inverse transform sampling uses X=sin2(πU/2) with U uniform on (0,1). The quartiles are (2−2​)/4, 1/2 and (2+2​)/4.[ref]
The first and third quartiles are about 0.1464 and 0.8536, so the central half of the probability spans about 0.7071 of the unit interval. Under a uniform distribution, its width would be 0.5.
The symmetric probability distribution gives E[X]=1/2. The angle representation gives a short calculation of the second moment:
E[X2]=π2​∫0π/2​sin4θdθ=83​,Var(X)=83​−41​=81​.
(12)
Use sin4θ=(3−4cos2θ+cos4θ)/8 to evaluate the integral. A uniform distribution on [0,1] has variance 1/12; the larger variance 1/8 quantifies the greater spread toward the ends.
More generally, for a nonnegative integer n, the beta function yields
E[Xn]=B(1/2,1/2)B(n+1/2,1/2)​=4n(n2n​)​.
(13)
For n=0 this is one; for n=1,2 it reproduces the values above. The central binomial coefficient thus appears in an exact moment calculation for a continuous probability distribution.
