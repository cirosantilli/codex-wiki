Keller four-roller polymer experiment

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymer physics Coil-stretch transition
OurBigBook.com Words: 246 Articles: 1
Pierre-Gilles de Gennes's 1991 lecture describes Andrew Keller and collaborators using four rollers to deform a dilute polymer solution. A well-chosen exit trajectory kept coils under sustained extension. Above a threshold flow rate the solution became birefringent, revealing a change in chain orientation and conformation. The experiment connected an optical readout to a mechanically driven molecular response.[ref]
This original functional diagram shows a central flow idealization, rather than historical dimensions or a photograph. A probe of birefringence detects different optical responses along different directions; it does not directly photograph a chain or establish its extension without an optical model. Comparing a flowing solution with its quiescent state and controlling temperature and concentration are useful proposed checks, not an assertion that this is the complete historical protocol.
