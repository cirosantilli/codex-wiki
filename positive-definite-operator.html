Positive-definite operator (⟨Av,v⟩>0(v=0))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Self-adjoint operator
OurBigBook.com Words: 129 Articles: 1
A self-adjoint operator A is positive definite when ⟨Av,v⟩>0 for every nonzero vector v in its domain. In finite dimension it is invertible and has positive eigenvalues. In infinite dimension, strict positivity of individual quadratic-form values need not give a bounded inverse; the stronger uniform bound ⟨Av,v⟩≥c∥v∥2 with c>0 supplies coercivity.
For maps β:U→V and γ:V→W between finite-dimensional inner product spaces, with imβ=kerγ, the operator ββ∗+γ∗γ is positive definite. Its quadratic form is ∥β∗v∥2+∥γv∥2, whose zero set is imβ∩(imβ)⊥={0}. This uses the adjoint operator relation kerβ∗=(imβ)⊥.
