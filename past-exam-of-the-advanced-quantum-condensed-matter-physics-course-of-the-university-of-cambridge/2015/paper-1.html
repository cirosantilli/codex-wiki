The Hartree-Fock method minimizes the expectation of an interacting-electron Hamiltonian over normalized Slater determinants of orthonormal one-particle spin orbitals. It is a variational, self-consistent mean-field approximation. The determinant enforces fermionic antisymmetry exactly within that class, while each orbital responds to the mean charge density and a nonlocal exchange operator generated by the occupied orbitals.
For an electron gas, this accounts for kinetic pressure, the average electrostatic field, same-spin exchange and the associated exchange hole, and possible spin polarization. It does not account for general dynamical electron correlation, such as the correlated avoidance of opposite-spin electrons, and cannot capture all screening or collective fluctuations merely by optimizing one determinant. Uniform plane waves are an additional restriction, not the definition of Hartree-Fock itself.
In the energy functional, the one-body term is the sum of electron kinetic energy and ionic-potential energy. The direct two-body term is the Hartree energy: the classical interaction of the mean electron density with itself. Its factor 1/2 avoids counting every pair twice. The negative Fock term comes from exchanging the two indistinguishable electron labels in the antisymmetric state. For a spin-independent interaction, spin orthogonality makes this exchange act only between equal spin components. For an orbital interacting with itself, direct and exchange contributions cancel exactly; the exchange term removes the spurious one-electron self-interaction. Ionic background terms and their prescribed cancellation must be included consistently with the direct term.

The central limitation is that a Hartree-Fock electron gas includes exchange but omits the correlation energy that is not representable by one determinant. Opposite-spin electrons can lower their repulsion by avoiding one another in a correlated unpolarized state. Hartree-Fock misses that avoidance while retaining the exchange advantage of aligned spins, so it need not place a magnetic instability at a reliable interaction strength. The relevant energy differences between competing spin states can be much smaller than their total energies. Correlation calculations of the electron gas explicitly address this competition.[ref]
There are further model restrictions. A constant contact coupling discards the momentum dependence of the Coulomb interaction and the resulting nontrivial screening. Real materials have a lattice, band structure and material-dependent effective interactions. Spatial magnetic textures or other competing states are excluded by a comparison restricted to uniform Fermi spheres. Even before those changes, part c's two endpoints do not find the continuous polarization onset.
The symbol rs​ also needs care. Here it is an energy ratio proportional to γn1/3. The usual Dimensionless Wigner-Seitz radius of a three-dimensional Coulomb electron gas is rsWS​=[3/(4πn)]1/3/aB​, proportional to n−1/3. They are not the same density parameter. A quoted experimental value of the latter cannot be compared numerically to 1.175 without a model-specific mapping. The contact interaction, missing correlations, restricted variational class and parameter convention prevent the number 1.175 from being a quantitative experimental prediction. The paper supplies no particular experimental threshold, so none is invented here.

Landau quasiparticles describe the long-lived low-energy excitations of an interacting normal Fermi liquid. Starting from a free Fermi gas, imagine switching on interactions without changing the phase or destroying the one-to-one labelling of low-energy states. The resulting excitation carries the quantum numbers of a dressed particle but has a renormalized energy and interactions with other excitations. Its microscopic wavefunction need not resemble a bare electron state. The occupied momenta define a Fermi surface, and the quasiparticle energy measured from the chemical potential vanishes on that surface.
The Landau quasiparticle energy functional expresses the change near equilibrium in terms of occupation changes:
δ(E−μN)=∑pσ​ξp∗​δnpσ​+2V1​∑pσ,p′σ′​fσσ′​(p,p′)δnpσ​δnp′σ′​.
(12)
Differentiation gives the energy of one quasiparticle in the distorted distribution. This is a phenomenological expansion around the true interacting ground state, not the assertion that its total energy equals that of independent particles. For an isotropic system, spin-symmetric and spin-antisymmetric components of f are expanded in Legendre polynomials. Multiplication by the appropriate Fermi-surface density of states defines dimensionless Landau parameters Fls​,Fla​. The original formulation relates a change in the distribution to the energy shift.[ref]
Pauli blocking makes the low-energy excitations sharp. The phase space for a quasiparticle to scatter while creating another particle–hole pair shrinks as the Fermi surface is approached, giving the regular three-dimensional low-energy Fermi-liquid quasiparticle lifetime
τ−1∝(ϵ−μ)2+(πkB​T)2.
(13)
Thus at zero temperature its width is small compared with its excitation energy. The single-particle spectral function has a pole with quasiparticle residue Z, generally less than one, and an incoherent remainder. In a conventional Fermi liquid the occupation distribution has a discontinuity of height Z at the Fermi surface, rather than a unit free-particle step.
For an isotropic three-dimensional system, define N∗(0) to include both spins. The quasiparticle density of states determines the low-temperature electronic heat capacity, CV​/V=(π2/3)kB2​N∗(0)T. In the usual Landau-parameter normalization, the spin response is modified by 1/(1+F0a​) and the compressibility by 1/(1+F0s​). In a Galilean-invariant continuum, m∗/m=1+F1s​/3; that particular relation must not be imposed unchanged on a crystal band. A denominator approaching zero signals a possible instability of the assumed normal state.
The same interaction functional and a kinetic equation describe collective zero sound when the excitation frequency exceeds the collision rate. Ordinary first sound is the collision-dominated hydrodynamic limit. The theory organizes thermodynamics, response and transport through a small set of measured effective parameters while explaining why interactions coexist with long-lived fermionic excitations. It is not applicable without qualification in phases where the Fermi-liquid quasiparticle pole disappears or a gap opens over the relevant Fermi surface.

A Wannier-Mott exciton is a bound conduction-band electron and valence-band hole whose envelope extends over many lattice cells. That large radius permits an effective mass description and dielectric screening of the electron–hole attraction. With electron and hole masses me∗​,mh∗​, define reduced mass μ=me∗​mh∗​/(me∗​+mh∗​) and center-of-mass mass M=me∗​+mh∗​. The bulk relative-motion Hamiltonian is hydrogenic with an appropriate relative permittivity ϵr​:
Hrel​=−2μℏ2∇2​−4πϵ0​ϵr​re2​,aX​=μe24πϵ0​ϵr​ℏ2​,RX​=2(4πϵ0​ϵr​)2ℏ2μe4​.
(14)
A semiconductor quantum well confines the electron and hole perpendicular to the plane while leaving approximately free in-plane center-of-mass motion. If the well width is small compared with the bulk exciton Bohr radius, their relative motion becomes quasi-two-dimensional. The ideal zero-width, same-dielectric limit is
Hρ​=−2μℏ2∇ρ2​​−4πϵ0​ϵr​ρe2​,En​=−(n−1/2)2RX​​,n=1,2,….​
(15)
Its ground-state binding is 4RX​, compared with RX​ in three dimensions, and the ground envelope decays on the scale aX​/2. Stronger binding follows because confinement prevents the pair from separating in the third direction.
For a finite-width quantum-well exciton, average the Coulomb attraction over the normalized electron and hole confinement envelopes:
Veff​(ρ)=−4πϵ0​ϵr​e2​∫dze​dzh​ρ2+(ze​−zh​)2​∣χe​(ze​)∣2∣χh​(zh​)∣2​.
(16)
This smooths the strict planar singularity and changes the binding. The ideal factor four is a useful reference, not a universal value for every real well. Finite barriers, dielectric mismatch, several confinement subbands and nonparabolic valence bands can require a coupled treatment.
Optical absorption creates a nearly zero in-plane center-of-mass wavevector exciton. For the lowest confinement subbands, its line lies approximately at
ℏω=Eg​+Ee,z​+Eh,z​−Eb​+2Mℏ2K∥2​​.
(17)
The discrete bound-state peaks occur below the free electron–hole continuum. Dipole-allowed envelope states have nonzero relative wavefunction at zero separation; the absorption strength also contains the confinement overlap ∣∫χe∗​(z)χh​(z)dz∣2. Confinement can increase the overlap and the binding, making excitonic optical structure more prominent. A perpendicular electric field shifts and separates the confinement envelopes, changing the transition energy and usually reducing their optical overlap: this is the quantum-confined Stark effect.
Quantum wells tune both the continuum threshold and the bound-state energy; an optical line shift is not automatically a change in binding alone. The hydrogenic envelope model requires a Wannier-sized state, rather than an exciton localized to one lattice cell.

Experimental tests of BCS theory compare several properties of the same superconducting state. In the conventional weak-coupling setting, phonon-mediated attraction pairs opposite momenta and spins, and the BCS quasiparticle spectrum is Ek​=ξk2​+Δ2(T)​. Agreement with a single energy scale and its temperature dependence is more discriminating than observing zero resistance alone.
The superconducting isotope effect tests the lattice's role. Replacing one isotope by another changes ionic masses while leaving the chemical valence nearly unchanged. Phonon frequencies scale approximately as M−1/2, so a weak-coupling phonon mechanism predicts Tc​∝M−α with α≃1/2 when other parameters stay fixed. The shift was measured in mercury before the microscopic pairing theory was developed.[ref] Deviations from 1/2 can result from Coulomb, anharmonic and strong-coupling effects; the isotope observation supports lattice-mediated pairing without uniquely proving every assumption of the simplest model.
Thermodynamic measurements test the gapped excitations and condensation energy. An isotropic fully gapped superconductor has exponentially small low-temperature electronic heat capacity, rather than the normal linear law. At the transition the weak-coupling BCS thermodynamic ratios are
kB​Tc​2Δ(0)​=2πe−γE​≃3.528,Cn​(Tc​)Cs​(Tc−​)−Cn​(Tc​)​=7ζ(3)12​≃1.426.​
(18)
Here Cn​ means the electronic contribution after subtracting the lattice background. The gap closes at Tc​, and the same framework predicts the temperature dependence of penetration depth and thermodynamic critical field. These quantities were quantitative comparisons in the original theory.[ref]
Electron tunnelling measures the excitation spectrum more directly. A normal-metal–insulator–superconductor junction has, at sufficiently low temperature and nearly constant tunnelling matrix element, dI/dV proportional to the superconducting density of states at E=eV:
Nn​(0)Ns​(E)​=ReE2−Δ2​∣E∣​.
(19)
The suppression below the gap and enhancement at its edges test the BCS electronic density of states. Giaever's thin oxide-barrier junction measurements established such energy-gap structure.[ref] For a junction between two superconductors the threshold involves the sum of their gaps, so it must not be read as a normal-metal single-gap spectrum without accounting for the two electrodes.
Nuclear spin-lattice relaxation supplies a phase-coherence-sensitive test. In the conventional s-wave case, the Hebel-Slichter coherence peak just below Tc​ is followed by a falling rate at lower temperature. Density of states alone is not enough: the BCS coherence factors enter the transition matrix elements. The contrast between nuclear relaxation and ultrasonic attenuation was explained using the paired state, unlike a simple two-fluid population model.[ref]
Finally, magnetic flux quantization in units h/(2e) and Josephson effects support a coherent charge-2e condensate. Those observations establish pairing and phase coherence more broadly; their existence alone does not specify a phonon mechanism or an isotropic weak-coupling gap. The converging isotope, spectroscopic, thermodynamic and coherence-factor tests support conventional BCS superconductivity, with explicit limits to its simplest universal ratios.

The first term assigns energy ϵi​ to occupying the localized orbital at site i. The hopping term moves an electron between neighbouring sites, producing kinetic delocalization and band dispersion; Hermitian conjugate hops make the Hamiltonian Hermitian. The final term costs U>0 only when both spin states occupy the same local orbital. This is a Hubbard model with an on-site Coulomb penalty, not a classical interaction between magnetic dipoles.
At one electron per site and large U, a real hop is energetically costly, but a virtual hop into a doubly occupied intermediate state is possible. Opposite-spin states can exploit these virtual processes and lower their energy, while parallel spins cannot occupy the same one-orbital site. The resulting singlet–triplet splitting produces antiferromagnetic Hubbard kinetic exchange.
There is a terminology qualification. The paper calls this overlap-mediated process “direct exchange” because the hopping is directly between neighbouring magnetic orbitals. In the stricter distinction between direct Coulomb exchange versus kinetic exchange, the displayed Hamiltonian omits an explicit intersite Coulomb exchange integral. The antiferromagnetic t2/U mechanism derived below is kinetic exchange; a genuine intersite direct exchange term is separate and can compete with it. This distinction also separates the model from ligand-mediated superexchange in part f.

Expand the field operator in orthonormal localized orbitals, ψ^​(r)=∑i​ϕi​(r)ci​. For a one-electron continuum Hamiltonian h0​=p^​2/(2m∗)+V(r), projection gives
H0​=∑ij​hij​ci†​cj​,h12​=∫d3rϕ1∗​(r)[2m∗p^​2​+V(r)]ϕ2​(r).

The coefficient in the displayed lattice Hamiltonian is −t12​. Thus the projected hopping matrix element and sign convention require
−t12​=h12​=K12​+V12​,K12​=∫d3rϕ1∗​2m∗p^​2​ϕ2​.​
(21)
Orthogonality states ∫ϕ1∗​ϕ2​=0; it does not imply ∫ϕ1∗​Vϕ2​=0 for a spatially varying potential. If the off-diagonal potential is assumed zero, for example when it is a constant, the hopping coefficient is h12​=K12​. This is the kinetic integral requested by the paper, but it equals −t12​ under the paper's own leading-minus convention. A convention writing the hopping term with a plus sign would instead call h12​ itself t12​.
The printed positive equality is not a consequence of the stated orthogonality assumption and the displayed sign convention. Orthogonality does not diagonalize a varying potential gives an explicit counterexample to the underlying inference. The general projected relation above states the supported result; setting V12​=0 still leaves −t12​=K12​ in the question's convention. Rephasing an orbital changes both h12​ and the coefficient of the corresponding operator together; it does not remove this algebraic sign mismatch in a fixed convention. The spectrum in the remaining two-site calculation depends on ∣t∣2, so those parts are unaffected.

c2↑†​c1↑​∣1001⟩=c2↑†​∣0001⟩=∣0011⟩.

An overlap estimate must use nearest magnetic-ion separations. For the face-centered cubic lattice of Mn, dMn​=aMn​/2​=3.147 A˚; for the body-centered cubic lattice of Fe, dFe​=3​aFe​/2=2.477 A˚. The supplied radial functions have inverse decay lengths κZ​=Z/(3aB​). Their intersite overlap therefore contains approximately e−κZ​d, up to polynomial and angular factors. The decisive exponent difference is
κMn​dMn​−κFe​dFe​=3(0.529)25(4.45/2​)−26(3​2.86/2)​≃8.99.
(30)
The exponential orbital-tail estimate of exchange thus gives a hopping suppression approximately e−8.99≃1.25×10−4 before polynomial corrections. Keeping the supplied Z7/2r2 radial prefactor in a midpoint overlap proxy gives
ϕFe​(dFe​/2)2ϕMn​(dMn​/2)2​∼(2625​)7(dFe​dMn​​)4e−8.99≃2.5×10−4.
(31)
This proxy is not an evaluated hopping integral, but confirms that the polynomial factors do not undo the exponential loss. If comparable Coulomb denominators and nonexponential prefactors are assumed, a kinetic-exchange scale proportional to t2/U is suppressed by roughly 10−8–10−7. Direct intersite Coulomb exchange likewise contains products of the small tails. Coordination-number differences cannot compensate for many orders of magnitude.
By contrast, the measured ordering-temperature ratio supplied in the question is 122/1043≃0.117, not an exponentially tiny number. Within this overlap model, direct Mn–Mn exchange cannot explain MnO's substantial magnetic ordering scale by extrapolating the Fe mechanism. Another exchange path is required.
This is an order-of-magnitude argument, not an exact relation between an itinerant Curie temperature and a localized-spin Néel temperature. The hydrogenic functions use the printed bare Z values; actual solid-state orbitals are screened and hybridized. Their literal absolute exchange scales should not be treated as quantitative predictions for Fe or MnO.

Use an oxygen-mediated superexchange path between two magnetic metal orbitals. A minimal one-active-orbital three-site charge-transfer superexchange Hamiltonian is clearest in a hole representation:
H=Δ∑σ​npσ​+Ud​∑i=1,2​ndi↑​ndi↓​+Up​np↑​np↓​−tpd​∑i=1,2;σ​(diσ†​pσ​+pσ†​diσ​).
(32)
Here di​ describes a metal hole, p a ligand hole, and Δ>0 is the energy needed to create the virtual ligand hole. The low-energy configuration has one hole on each metal orbital and no ligand hole, corresponding to a filled bridging ligand orbital in the electron picture. Ud​ and Up​ penalize double hole occupancy on the corresponding sites.
Virtual metal–ligand hopping transfers charge through oxygen without producing a real mobile carrier. Eliminating the ligand gives an effective metal–metal hopping tddeff​∼tpd2​/Δ. Combining it with the large metal repulsion gives
JAF​∼Δ2Ud​4tpd4​​>0.​
(33)
A ligand double-occupancy channel adds a contribution of the same sign for the simple half-filled straight-bond model, often organized as J∼(4tpd4​/Δ2)[1/Ud​+2/(2Δ+Up​)]. The precise coefficients depend on the orbitals and hopping conventions; the essential mechanism is virtual charge transfer plus spin-selective access to intermediate states. The effective spin interaction favors antiparallel alignment while the material can remain insulating.
For MnO's rock salt structure, the strong approximately 180∘ Mn–O–Mn path joins next-nearest Mn sites. It is not the nearest fcc Mn–Mn separation used in the direct-overlap estimate of part e. The oxygen-mediated path avoids relying on the tiny direct overlap of the two distant 3d tails. A realistic model includes several Mn 3d orbitals, Hund coupling and the high-spin Mn ion; the single-orbital Hamiltonian above isolates the antiferromagnetic exchange channel. Approximately 90∘ paths and different orbital occupancies need not have the same sign.
