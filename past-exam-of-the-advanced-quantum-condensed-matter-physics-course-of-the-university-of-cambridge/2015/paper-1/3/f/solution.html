Use an oxygen-mediated superexchange path between two magnetic metal orbitals. A minimal one-active-orbital three-site charge-transfer superexchange Hamiltonian is clearest in a hole representation:
H=Δ∑σ​npσ​+Ud​∑i=1,2​ndi↑​ndi↓​+Up​np↑​np↓​−tpd​∑i=1,2;σ​(diσ†​pσ​+pσ†​diσ​).
(32)
Here di​ describes a metal hole, p a ligand hole, and Δ>0 is the energy needed to create the virtual ligand hole. The low-energy configuration has one hole on each metal orbital and no ligand hole, corresponding to a filled bridging ligand orbital in the electron picture. Ud​ and Up​ penalize double hole occupancy on the corresponding sites.
Virtual metal–ligand hopping transfers charge through oxygen without producing a real mobile carrier. Eliminating the ligand gives an effective metal–metal hopping tddeff​∼tpd2​/Δ. Combining it with the large metal repulsion gives
JAF​∼Δ2Ud​4tpd4​​>0.​
(33)
A ligand double-occupancy channel adds a contribution of the same sign for the simple half-filled straight-bond model, often organized as J∼(4tpd4​/Δ2)[1/Ud​+2/(2Δ+Up​)]. The precise coefficients depend on the orbitals and hopping conventions; the essential mechanism is virtual charge transfer plus spin-selective access to intermediate states. The effective spin interaction favors antiparallel alignment while the material can remain insulating.
For MnO's rock salt structure, the strong approximately 180∘ Mn–O–Mn path joins next-nearest Mn sites. It is not the nearest fcc Mn–Mn separation used in the direct-overlap estimate of part e. The oxygen-mediated path avoids relying on the tiny direct overlap of the two distant 3d tails. A realistic model includes several Mn 3d orbitals, Hund coupling and the high-spin Mn ion; the single-orbital Hamiltonian above isolates the antiferromagnetic exchange channel. Approximately 90∘ paths and different orbital occupancies need not have the same sign.
