The next heavy-hole and split-off thresholds are about 2.2224 and 2.3674 eV, outside the interval. Each threshold marks a continuum onset or shoulder, not the stronger bound-exciton peak below it. The six heavy- and light-hole onsets follow the six paired peak groups in the supplied diagram. Their first members lie roughly 20 and 12 meV below their respective onsets, as the common solution above explains.
A qualification is necessary for the split-off branch. Its p-like j=1/2 bloch states have nonzero px​ matrix elements to the s-like conduction states, so in-plane polarization does not generally forbid them. For example, a j=1/2 state has a nonzero px​±ipy​ orbital component with the appropriate spin, which couples to an s-like state through px​. The same-envelope rule therefore allows the two split-off thresholds in the table. The first nearly coincides with the third heavy-hole edge, while the second would give an additional onset near 2.086 eV. The supplied figure and twelve peak energies appear to retain only the heavy- and light-hole series; they do not specify a split-off-series oscillator strength or identify its extra features. Omitting the split-off transitions would require an additional approximation beyond the four-band information and polarization given. Their expected ideal two-dimensional first-state peaks would be near 1.9010 and 2.0701 eV; these are predictions of the stated model, not additional listed data.
