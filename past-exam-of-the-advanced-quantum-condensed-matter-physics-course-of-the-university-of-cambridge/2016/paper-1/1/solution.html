The absorptive dielectric function counts optically accessible electron-hole pairs. In the electric dipole approximation the perturbation is proportional to a⋅p^​, with a the light polarization and p^​ the electronic momentum operator. The squared matrix element between occupied valence and empty conduction states is the transition strength; it includes polarization and band-symmetry selection rules. The sums enumerate initial and final subbands. photon momentum is negligible on the crystal-momentum scale, so the transition is approximately vertical in the in-plane wavevector k.
For each subband pair, C is the constant-transition-energy contour in the two-dimensional Brillouin zone. Its line element is dl, and the reciprocal gradient weights the states satisfying energy conservation. Specifically, the level-set representation of the joint density of states is
∫d2kδ(En′​(k)−En​(k)−ℏω)=∫C​∣∇k​(En′​−En​)∣dl​.
(1)
A small energy gradient means many nearly resonant states; a vanishing gradient can produce a Van Hove singularity or an edge. The displayed expression suppresses overall optical prefactors and assumes the momentum matrix element is approximately constant along each contour. More generally it stays inside that integral. Occupation factors fv​−fc​ are also implicit; at low temperature in an undoped well the relevant valence states are full and the conduction states empty. This is the independent-particle continuum contribution from Fermi's golden rule. Bound excitons add optical resonances below the corresponding free-pair thresholds.
The GaAs four-band model is rooted in bonding and spin-orbit coupling. Near the zone centre, the conduction edge is predominantly s-like and antibonding, while the upper valence manifold is predominantly p-like. Combining orbital angular momentum l=1 with spin s=1/2 splits the six p-derived states into a j=3/2 quartet and a lower j=1/2 doublet. The latter is the split-off valence band. Within the upper quartet, the mj​=±3/2 and mj​=±1/2 branches, defined relative to a propagation or quantization direction, have different curvatures and are called heavy holes and light holes. They coincide at the unstrained bulk zone centre but need not remain degenerate in a well. The conduction edge is a spin doublet. Thus “four bands” counts conduction, heavy-hole, light-hole and split-off branches, including their spin multiplicities; it does not count four individual states. Positive hole masses describe the downward valence curvature, whereas the conduction curvature is upward.

The approximately 18-meV and 11-meV pair splittings support the two-dimensional series and are incompatible with the three-dimensional series at the supplied masses and permittivity. Equivalently, for a two-dimensional pair with energies E1​,E2​, the inferred edge is E2​+(E2​−E1​)/8; the observed pair splittings predict ground-state bindings around 20 and 12 meV, close to 4Rj∗​.
Absolute agreement should not be overstated. For example, the stated square-well parameters give the first heavy-hole edge 1.56390 eV and the first light-hole edge 1.59161 eV. Their ideal two-dimensional peak predictions are approximately (1.54415,1.56170) eV and (1.57950,1.59027) eV, respectively. The listed peaks are lower by about 1–2 meV across the families; rounded parameters and this simple effective-mass model do not reproduce every absolute energy exactly. No gap, width or mass has been silently adjusted to fit them. The peak-spacing comparison remains useful because it avoids a common edge offset. The split-off transitions predicted by the four-band data are discussed separately in part (c), rather than assigned fictitious extra peaks.
At 300 K, kB​T≃25.9 meV, whereas the ideal ground-state binding energies are only about 19.7, 12.1 and 15.4 meV; excited states are still more weakly bound. None of these series is expected to remain a clearly resolved, robust bound-exciton peak at room temperature in this simple estimate. Thermal ionization and phonon broadening work against their visibility. This is a scale argument, not a theorem that the exciton population or all Coulomb-enhanced absorption becomes exactly zero: actual observability also requires temperature-dependent gaps, linewidths, screening and carrier density, which are not supplied.
