Start from electrons moving in the periodic equilibrium ionic potential. Displace ion l by ul​ and expand its contribution as V(r−Rl​−ul​)≃V(r−Rl​)−ul​⋅∇V(r−Rl​). Quantizing the harmonic normal modes expresses ul​ as a sum of creation and annihilation operators with displacement scale ℏ/(2ρion​Vωq​)​ for a single simple branch. Expanding the electronic field in bloch states then gives a band-projected Fröhlich Hamiltonian
H=H0​+Ve-ph​,H0​=∑kσ​ϵk​ckσ†​ckσ​+∑q​Ωq​bq†​bq​,
(11)
Ve-ph​=∑kqσ​Mq​ck+q,σ†​ckσ​(bq​+b−q†​),Ωq​=ℏωq​.
(12)
Here c,c† obey canonical anticommutation relations and remove/create an electron with crystal momentum k and spin σ; b,b† are boson operators for lattice modes. The matrix element Mq​ contains the derivative of the ionic potential, phonon polarization, displacement normalization and electronic wavefunction overlaps. With phases chosen appropriately, M−q​=Mq∗​ ensures a Hermitian Hamiltonian. The displayed one-band, one-branch notation suppresses additional branch and band labels. “Fröhlich Hamiltonian” here means the linear electron-phonon model used in the question; it is not a requirement to use specifically the polar-optical-phonon coupling of a polar semiconductor. A harmonic lattice, small displacements and a weak enough coupling to organize perturbation theory are the underlying approximations.
The Fröhlich canonical transformation removes real linear phonon operators from the electron-only effective problem to first order. Choose an anti-Hermitian S with [S,H0​]=−Ve-ph​. For Akqσ​=ck+q,σ†​ckσ​ and Δkq​=ϵk+q​−ϵk​, one possible generator is
S=∑kqσ​Mq​Akqσ​(Δkq​−Ωq​bq​​+Δkq​+Ωq​b−q†​​).
(13)
Its Hermitian conjugate is its negative after relabelling momenta. The Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula gives
eSHe−S=H0​+21​[S,Ve-ph​]+O(M3).
(14)
The commutator describes virtual emission and absorption of a phonon. normal ordering yields an electron-electron term as well as one-electron energy renormalizations and phonon terms. Retain the former, absorb single-particle shifts into ϵk​ and eliminate the explicit phonon sector. For elastic pair scattering the two virtual time orderings have the combined amplitude
Veff​(q,Δ)=∣Mq​∣2(Δ−Ωq​1​−Δ+Ωq​1​)=Δ2−Ωq2​2∣Mq​∣2Ωq​​.
(15)
Using the usual factor one-half to count unordered pairs gives the coefficient in the printed Hamiltonian, with Δ=ϵk​−ϵk−q​. The two outgoing momenta are k−q and k′+q, so total momentum and the spins are conserved. A fully off-shell effective operator can instead use symmetrized denominators for both external legs; the displayed expression is the usual elastic-scattering reduction. Eliminating nearly resonant real phonons requires more care than this second-order construction.
For ∣Δ∣<Ωq​ the denominator is negative: virtual phonon exchange is attractive in this low-energy channel. Physically, an electron changes the ionic density and a second electron can gain energy by responding to the delayed distortion. This is a retarded interaction, not an unscreened instantaneous attraction at every energy transfer. At large transfer the sign changes, and screened Coulomb repulsion must also be included when deciding the net low-energy pairing strength. The connection between lattice dynamics and superconductivity is supported by the isotope effect, but the mathematical attraction alone does not determine a measured transition temperature.[ref]
For the pair calculation, make the model restrictions explicit. The filled Fermi sea is inert, and ∣0⟩ denotes that sea with no added particles, not an empty electronic vacuum. Project the interaction onto the previously empty shell 0≤ξk​≤D, where ξk​=ℏ2k2/(2m)−EF​ and D=ℏωD​. Measure the added-pair energy relative to 2EF​; this is the negative E in the final formula. If absolute one-electron energies are retained in the preceding eigenvalue equation, its eigenvalue must instead be Eabs​=2EF​+E. A shell-restricted state would not in general be an exact eigenstate of the completely unprojected, all-momentum contact Hamiltonian.
Write g=γ2>0 and ∣k⟩=c−k↓†​ck↑†​∣0⟩. Within the zero-total-momentum pair subspace the two opposite-spin terms cancel the interaction's factor one-half, while same-spin s-wave terms cancel by fermionic antisymmetry. Thus the projected Hamiltonian acts as
Hpair′​∣k⟩=2ξk​∣k⟩−g∑⟨p⟩​∣p⟩.
(16)
For ∣Ψ⟩=∑⟨k⟩​φk​∣k⟩ and C=∑⟨k⟩​φk​, matching each basis coefficient gives
(E−2ξk​)φk​=−gC,φk​=−E−2ξk​γ2C​.​
(17)
This is the stated coefficient formula in the consistent Fermi-level energy convention. Its even dependence on k produces an even orbital state with a spin singlet. The constant C is fixed up to an overall phase by normalization; for a bound state, ∣C∣−2=g2∑⟨k⟩​(E−2ξk​)−2. It cannot be set to zero for the nontrivial bound solution.
Put E=−B with B>0 and impose the sum defining C:
1=g∑⟨k⟩​2ξk​+B1​≃gρ(EF​)∫0D​2ξ+Bdξ​=2gρ(EF​)​logB2D+B​.
(18)
Here ρ(EF​) counts orbital states for one spin species, as appropriate to the unrestricted k sum in this pair basis, and is approximately constant over the thin shell. If a two-spin total density of states is used, it must be divided by two in this integral. State-counting and the volume convention in g and ρ must be consistent. With ξ=gρ(EF​) as the paper's dimensionless coupling, inversion yields
E=−e2/ξ−12ℏωD​​≃−2ℏωD​e−2/ξ(ξ≪1).​
(19)
The first approximation is the constant-density-of-states, restricted-shell model; the final exponential additionally assumes weak attraction. The binding energy is nonanalytic at zero coupling, so a finite-order power expansion in g cannot produce it.
Cooper's problem asks whether two electrons added above an inert Fermi sea bind despite Pauli exclusion of lower states. An arbitrarily weak net attraction binds a zero-momentum singlet below the free-pair threshold, because the near-Fermi-surface integral diverges logarithmically as B↓0. Its wavefunction draws on many time-reversed momentum states and becomes dominated by a narrow region near the Fermi surface at weak coupling; the corresponding pair is spatially extended. This establishes a Cooper instability of the unpaired reference state.[ref] The full superconducting ground state needs a coherent many-electron treatment in which overlapping pairs and the reorganized sea are included. BCS theory supplies that step; the two-electron binding scale alone is neither a calculation of the many-body gap nor a derivation of zero resistance.[ref]
