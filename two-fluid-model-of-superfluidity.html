Two-fluid model of superfluidity (ρ=ρs​+ρn​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Superfluid Normal component of a superfluid
OurBigBook.com Words: 445 Articles: 4
The two-fluid model of superfluidity gives the normal component of a superfluid and the superfluid independent velocities.
An elementary uniform-flow example makes the distinction operational. Write the mass flux per unit area and the ideal entropy flux as
j=ρs​vs​+ρn​vn​,jS​=ρsvn​,ρ=ρs​+ρn​.
(16)
Here s is specific entropy, in Jkg−1K−1, and ρs​,ρn​ are response mass densities. The mass density ρs​ used here gives the three-dimensional phase stiffness coefficient ρs​(ℏ/m)2 when vs​=(ℏ/m)∇θ is substituted into the kinetic energy density ρs​vs2​/2. For a stipulated ρs​=3ρ/4, ρn​=ρ/4 and vn​=3u along a channel, zero net mass flux requires vs​=−u. Yet jS​=3ρsu is positive. Opposing velocities can therefore transport entropy while transporting no net mass.
This example explains why excellent apparent heat transport need not be ordinary diffusion of heat through a stationary liquid. In the ideal zero-net-mass-flux limit, the heat flux associated with this entropy transport is TjS​. Viscous losses, quantum vortices and external heat exchange require additional terms. The model also supports second sound.
