Integral basis of a pure quartic field with radicand three modulo four (OQ(4d​)​=Z[4d​])

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Algebraic number theory Integral basis
OurBigBook.com Words: 180
For a nonzero squarefree integer d≡3(mod4), set θ=4d​. The power basis 1,θ,θ2,θ3 is an integral basis, with discriminant −256d3. The polynomial is Eisenstein at every prime dividing d; for d=−1, shift X4+1 by one to obtain an Eisenstein polynomial at two. The discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice restricts any power-basis index prime to 2d. An Eisenstein prime cannot divide the index: the norm of rθi/p has valuation i−4<0 for p∤r and i<4, while θ4/p is integral; multiplying a putative denominator-p integral element successively by powers of θ forces each numerator coefficient divisible by p.
It remains to exclude two. A putative integral element (a+bθ+cθ2+eθ3)/2 has relative norm to Q(d​) with coefficients
4a2+dc2−2dbe​,42ac−b2−de2​.
(17)
The quadratic field has ring of integers Z[d​], so both coefficients are integers. The first congruence forces a,c to have the same parity. If they are odd, the second requires b2+3e2≡2(mod4), impossible. If they are even, the second forces b,e to have the same parity and the first forces be even, so both are even. Thus no nontrivial denominator-two element exists, proving index one.
