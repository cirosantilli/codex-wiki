Unimodular change of lattice basis (B2​=B1​U,U∈GLn​(Z))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Unimodular matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
Let B1​,B2​ be real square invertible matrices whose columns are lattice bases. They generate the same Euclidean lattice precisely when
B1​Zn=B2​Zn⟺B2​=B1​U for a unimodular matrix U.​
(31)
[ref]
If B2​=B1​U, multiplication by U merely permutes the integer coordinate vectors, so the two generated sets agree. Conversely, equality of the generated sets means every column of either basis is an integer linear combination of the other. Hence B2​=B1​U and B1​=B2​V for integer matrices U,V. Substitution followed by multiplication by B1−1​ gives UV=I, so the integer inverse criterion for a unimodular matrix applies.
Equal real linear spans are a weaker condition: any two real square invertible matrices span the same ambient vector space. Even equal covolumes do not force the same Euclidean lattice. The requirement that the change matrix and its inverse matrix have integer entries provides the missing arithmetic information.
