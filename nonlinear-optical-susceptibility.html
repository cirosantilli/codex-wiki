Nonlinear optical susceptibility (χ(2))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Nonlinear optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 504 Articles: 5
Choose one driving electric field component and one responding electric polarization component. A schematic local, instantaneous expansion is
P=ϵ0​(χ(1)E+χ(2)E2+χ(3)E3+⋯).
(57)
Here ϵ0​ is the vacuum permittivity, χ(1) is the linear electric susceptibility, and the higher coefficients describe nonlinear response. In SI, P has units Cm−2, E has units Vm−1, and χ(2) has units mV−1. The selected scalar coefficient depends on polarization of waves and crystal orientation; it is not a universal material number independent of geometry.
For a chosen monochromatic wave E(t)=E0​cosωt, the quadratic contribution is exactly
P(2)(t)=2ϵ0​χ(2)E02​​(1+cos2ωt).
(58)
The cosine identity separates a constant contribution, associated with optical rectification, from an oscillation at twice the angular frequency, the source for second-harmonic generation. A term oscillating at 2ω is a local driving source; efficient outgoing light additionally requires propagation and phase matching.
For a material with optical dispersion, the scalar instantaneous expansion is only a teaching model. The actual quadratic response is a tensor with output and input frequency arguments: χijk(2)​(2ω;ω,ω) describes the harmonic, whereas χijk(2)​(0;ω,−ω) describes the constant response. Their coefficients need not coincide. The cosine calculation establishes which frequencies are available, without assigning measured coefficients to any crystal.
