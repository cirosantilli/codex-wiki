Linear rigid rotor (EJ​=ℏ2J(J+1)/(2I))

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation Rigid rotor
OurBigBook.com Words: 754 Articles: 4
For a diatomic molecule, let m1​,m2​ be the two masses and r their fixed separation. The distances from the centre of mass are r1​=m2​r/(m1​+m2​) and r2​=m1​r/(m1​+m2​). A perpendicular axis through the centre of mass therefore has moment of inertia
I=m1​r12​+m2​r22​=μr2,μ=m1​+m2​m1​m2​​.
(2)
The reduced mass μ here is not the electric dipole moment, for which we use d. For a longer linear molecule, replace μr2 by ∑i​mi​ρi2​, where ρi​ is each distance along the axis from the centre of mass. In the point-mass idealization, the axial moment of inertia is zero; turning about that axis changes no nuclear positions, so no independent axial rotational degree of freedom is included.
For the field-free linear rigid rotor, assume a fixed internal quantum state with no electronic angular momentum or electronic spin, and omit couplings to nuclear spin. The rotational Hamiltonian is Hrot​=L2/(2I). Its orientation wavefunctions are spherical harmonics YJM​, and its angular momentum eigenstates satisfy
L2YJM​=ℏ2J(J+1)YJM​,Lz​YJM​=ℏMYJM​,EJ​=2Iℏ2​J(J+1)=hBJ(J+1),
(3)
where J=0,1,2,… and M=−J,−J+1,…,J. Each EJ​ belongs to 2J+1 angular momentum eigenstates distinguished by the magnetic quantum number M, an instance of degenerate energy levels. The Planck constant is h=2πℏ; B is the frequency-valued rotational constant. Notice that the energy eigenvalues scale as J(J+1), not J2.
Subtracting consecutive energy eigenvalues gives
νJ→J+1​=hEJ+1​−EJ​​=2B(J+1).
(4)
Thus increasing level gaps produce a regularly spaced series of lines at 2B,4B,6B,…, with spacing 2B. Allowed positions do not guarantee that every line is visible: electric-dipole selection rules, occupation of the initial quantum state, and instrument response also matter.
