Inverse-adjoint representation (D♯(g)=D(g−1)†)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Group representation
OurBigBook.com Words: 80
For a complex finite-dimensional group representation, the displayed matrices give another group representation: taking inverses and taking the conjugate transpose each reverse multiplication order, so their composition preserves it. This operation is involutive. With the standard Hermitian inner product, an invariant vector subspace W for D♯ has orthogonal complement invariant for D, because D(g)†W=D♯(g−1)W⊆W. Thus ⟨w,D(g)v⟩=⟨D(g)†w,v⟩=0 for v∈W⊥,w∈W. It follows that D♯ is an irreducible representation exactly when D is. No finiteness assumption on the group or unitarity assumption on the group representation is needed.
