Cryogenic radiometer

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Electrical substitution radiometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 394 Articles: 1
A cryogenic radiometer uses low-temperature operation to improve electrical substitution radiometry through particular material and thermal properties. Lower heat capacity of a copper receiver permits a substantial cavity without proportionately slow relaxation; cold shields reduce unwanted thermal radiation, and heater leads with superconductivity can suppress resistive lead heating. These features do not make radiometric absorptance exactly one or abolish optical-electrical heating non-equivalence.[ref]
Cold surroundings matter as much as a cold receiver. In a simplified gray-surface model, net outward thermal radiation is ϵσA(T4−Ts4​), using emissivity ϵ, area A, shield temperature Ts​ and the Stefan-Boltzmann constant σ. Cold shields reduce the incoming term as well as the receiver's emission; cooling the receiver alone while leaving it exposed to warm surroundings does not remove incoming radiation. An entrance window still provides an optical and thermal path. Vacuum reduces gas-mediated heat transfer, while an intentional radiometer thermal link remains necessary. This model explains specific losses, rather than promising a universal improvement factor or perfect accuracy.
