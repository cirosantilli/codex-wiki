Cyclicity of a product of two finite cyclic groups (Cm​×Cn​ cyclic ⟺ gcd(m,n)=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Direct product of groups
OurBigBook.com Words: 183
For positive integers m,n, two finite cyclic groups combine into one cyclic group precisely when their group orders are coprime:
Cm​×Cn​≅Cmn​⟺gcd(m,n)=1.
(6)
The reason is that both cyclic counters must return to their identity elements simultaneously. If a,b are generators of a group for the two factors, the order of a direct-product element gives ord(a,b)=lcm(m,n). The pair generates all mn elements exactly when this least common multiple is mn.
For C2​×C3​, repeated addition of (1,1) visits
(0,0),(1,1),(0,2),(1,0),(0,1),(1,2),(0,0).
(7)
All six pairs appear before the return, so (1,1) is a generator. In contrast, in C2​×C2​ the same step gives only (0,0),(1,1),(0,0). Every nonidentity element has order of a group element two, so none can generate all four pairs. This is the Klein four-group; there is no group isomorphism to C4​ even though both have four elements.
For a brief proof of the converse, each component order of a group element divides the corresponding factor group order. Every pair's order therefore divides lcm(m,n). If gcd(m,n)>1, this is smaller than mn, so no pair can generate the whole product. The Chinese remainder theorem gives the explicit isomorphism in the coprime case: tmodmn↦(tmodm,tmodn).
