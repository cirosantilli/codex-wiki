Galvanometer calibration by half deflection (Rg​=RS/(R−S))

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current Galvanometer Moving-coil galvanometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 291
NCERT's teaching experiment uses a moving-coil galvanometer, a 0–6 V supply, a 0–10 kΩ series electrical resistance box R, a 0–200 Ω parallel electrical resistance box S, two switches and a voltmeter. With the parallel path open, close the supply switch and record the pointer deflection. Keep R fixed, close the parallel path, and adjust S until the deflection halves. The guide requests repeated settings, clean tight contacts and constant supply electromotive force; it introduces high R before energizing to protect the winding. [ref]
Write the winding electrical resistance as Rg​, the supply electromotive force as E, and assume negligible supply internal electrical resistance. Ohm's law gives the two winding electric currents
I1​=R+Rg​E​,I2​=R(Rg​+S)+Rg​SES​.
(18)
If proportional deflection gives I2​=I1​/2, solving yields Rg​=RS/(R−S), requiring R>S. The shortcut Rg​≃S requires R≫Rg​; closing the parallel path generally changes the total supply electric current. For chosen values R=9900Ω and S=99.0Ω, the exact result is Rg​=100Ω, rather than 99Ω. The manual gives this exact relation and asks how finite supply internal electrical resistance affects it. [ref]
As an additional calibration control, record the supply-off zero, apply several small positive and negative electric currents, and recheck zero after each sequence. With Rg​ known, calculate each electric current from the measured supply electric potential difference and the total series electrical resistance; plot settled deflection against that electric current. Equal-magnitude reversed readings should lie equally far from a stable zero. A fitted relation θread​=θ0​+SI​I separates zero offset from galvanometer current sensitivity. The manual's “figure of merit” is electric current per scale division, not the mechanical torsional spring constant k used above. These reversal and zero checks extend the documented procedure; they are proposed controls, not reported measurements.
