Quasi-long-range order

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Critical phenomenon Lower critical dimension Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
Quasi-long-range order means that correlations decay algebraically rather than approaching a nonzero constant. It occurs in the low-temperature phase of the two-dimensional XY model, where spin waves destroy true long-range order but bound vortex pairs preserve power-law correlations.
For a dimensionless Gaussian action F/(kB​T)=(KR​/2)∫d2r∣∇θ∣2, the phase-difference variance at a≪r≪L is log(r/a)/(πKR​) up to bounded cutoff terms. Gaussian phase averaging then gives
g(r)=⟨ei[θ(r)−θ(0)]⟩∝(r/a)−η,η=2πKR​1​.
(7)
Every positive η makes g(r)→0, so there is no conventional long-range order, even when the phase stiffness is finite. This is how an accumulation of gentle, cheap twists destroys global alignment without making every local twist free.
