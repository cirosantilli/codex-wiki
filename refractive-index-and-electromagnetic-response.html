Refractive index and electromagnetic response (n2=εr​μr​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Refractive index
OurBigBook.com Words: 203
For a uniform linear medium with isotropy and local constitutive response, Maxwell equations give k2=ω2ε(ω)μ(ω). Here ε is permittivity and μ is magnetic permeability. Since c−2=ε0​μ0​, the relative permittivity εr​=ε/ε0​ and the relative magnetic permeability μr​=μ/μ0​ obey
n2=εr​μr​.
(17)
If both responses are real and positive with negligible loss, and the ordinary positive-index branch is intended, then n=+εr​μr​​. If additionally μr​≃1, then n≃εr​​. All these responses must be evaluated at the electromagnetic wave's frequency; a static relative permittivity cannot simply be substituted at an optical frequency. [ref]
With loss, use a complex index n=n′+iκ and explicitly fix the phase convention for complex amplitudes. For E∝exp[i(kz−ωt)] with k=nω/c, its wave amplitude has factor exp(−κωz/c). A forward attenuating branch has κ≥0, while its wave phase is controlled by n′. The complex relation n2=εr​μr​ alone leaves a choice of sign: the physical solution also needs causality and the appropriate radiation or decay condition. In negative-index media the physically selected real part can be negative. The positive square root is therefore not a universal rule for complex or negative-index media. Direction and polarization of an electromagnetic wave also matter in a plane wave in an anisotropic dielectric, for which one scalar index need not describe every propagation branch.
