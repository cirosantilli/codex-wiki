Angular momentum and the escape energy threshold (ε=21​r˙2+2r2h2​−rμ​)

In the isolated model, conservation of energy and angular momentum conservation reduce the exterior test particle's motion to
ε=mE​=2r˙2​+2r2h2​−rμ​,h=∣r×v∣,μ=GM.
(20)
Here ε is specific orbital energy and h is specific angular momentum. The effective potential is Φeff​=h2/(2r2)−μ/r. A zero radial velocity is a radial turning point, and need not mean zero total speed: at the periapsis or apoapsis of a nonradial Kepler orbit, tangential velocity remains. Thus the vertical gap between ε and −μ/r represents total kinetic energy per unit mass, whereas the gap above Φeff​ represents its radial part.
For h>0, the orbital eccentricity obeys e2=1+2εh2/μ2. Negative ε gives an elliptic orbit, including a circular orbit at its minimum allowed energy; zero ε gives a parabolic trajectory; positive ε gives a hyperbolic Kepler orbit. The effective potential tends to zero from below at infinity, so its outer region creates no extra positive escape barrier. This explains why escape velocity depends on energy even though angular momentum changes the path. The h=0 case is radial motion, rather than a nondegenerate parabola or hyperbola; inward radial motion can end in collision.
