Subgroups generated by circle rotations (Hα​={e2πikα:k∈Z})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Topological group Compact group
OurBigBook.com Words: 309
Fix a real angle fraction α, and repeatedly multiply by g=e2πiα in the circle group. The generated cyclic subgroup is Hα​={gk:k∈Z}. Equivalently, the angle fractions are the fractional parts of kα, with one full turn identified with zero.
If α=p/q is a rational number in lowest terms, with q>0 and p,q coprime, then gq=1 and no smaller positive power is 1. Hence Hα​ is a finite cyclic group of order q, consisting of equally spaced points. For α=2/7, the successive angle fractions, starting at k=0, are
0,72​,74​,76​,71​,73​,75​,0.
(7)
The subgroup is finite, so it is a closed subgroup of the circle group; it is also a discrete subgroup. It is not an open subgroup, since every ambient neighbourhood of any of its points contains an arc, rather than only these seven points.
If α is an irrational number, no nonzero integer k satisfies kα∈Z, so the powers never repeat. The stronger statement is that Hα​ is a dense set in the circle group: every nonempty open arc contains a power gk. Thus its closure is all of T, while Hα​ itself is a countable proper subgroup and is not a closed subgroup or a discrete subgroup. See irrational rotation and everywhere interval frequency under an irrational rotation for the corresponding motion and its long-term frequencies.

This example shows why algebraic generation and closure must be distinguished: one irrational rotation generates a countable cyclic subgroup, yet its closure is the whole uncountable compact group.
