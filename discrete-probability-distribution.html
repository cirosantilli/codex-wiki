Benford law (P(D=d)=log10​(1+1/d))

Words: 178 Articles: 3
Discrete probability distribution OurBigBook.com
Benford law assigns leading base-ten digit d∈{1,…,9} probability log10​(1+1/d). This distribution arises when the fractional part of a base-ten logarithm is uniform: digit d corresponds to the interval [log10​d,log10​(d+1)).

Scale invariance of decimal significands

Words: 59
Benford law OurBigBook.com
A decimal significand lies in [1,10), obtained by removing the integer power of ten from a positive number. Multiplication by a constant rotates the fractional logarithm modulo one. Invariance under every such rotation makes the fractional logarithm uniform and hence implies Benford law. This is an invariant significand distribution, not a proper scale-invariant distribution for the entire positive number.

Benford law from logarithmic uniformity (P(D=i)=log10​(1+1/i))

Words: 42
Benford law OurBigBook.com
The leading decimal digit is determined by the fractional part of log10​X. A uniform fractional logarithm gives Benford law by the lengths of the intervals [log10​i,log10​(i+1)). A log-uniform distribution whose logarithmic span is an integer has this property. Arbitrary partial-decade truncations generally do not.

Benford frequencies for powers of an integer

Words: 46
Benford law OurBigBook.com
If a≥2 is an integer with irrational log10​a, then the leading digit d of an has limiting frequency log10​(1+1/d). Indeed the logarithmic fractional parts are the orbit of zero under an irrational rotation of the circle, so everywhere interval frequency under an irrational rotation applies. Thus for a=2, digit seven has frequency (log8−log7)/log10.

Estimating Bernoulli variance (ϕ=θ(1−θ))

Words: 76
Bernoulli distribution OurBigBook.com
For n≥2 independent Bernoulli observations with sum K, the extended maximum-likelihood estimator is ϕ​=(K/n)(1−K/n). Its unbiased improvement is K(n−K)/[n(n−1)]=nϕ​/(n−1), obtained by conditioning X1​(1−X2​) on K. The Rao-Blackwell theorem gives strict variance reduction for 0<θ<1. With a uniform prior on θ and squared-error loss for ϕ, the Bayes estimator is (K+1)(n−K+1)/[(n+2)(n+3)]. For parameter space strictly (0,1), samples with K=0 or n have only a boundary likelihood supremum, not an attained maximum.

A random experiment with two outcomes, success and failure, whose success indicator has a Bernoulli distribution. Geometric distribution

Words: 496 Articles: 7
Discrete probability distribution OurBigBook.com
A geometric distribution models the number of independent Bernoulli trials needed to obtain the first success. On the convention supported by 1,2,…, its mass is (1−p)k−1p and its mean is 1/p. On the failures-before-first-success convention supported by 0,1,…, its mass is (1−p)kp and its mean is (1−p)/p. Both conventions have variance (1−p)/p2.

Geometric return count before absorption (K∼Geom(Pi​(absorption before return to i)))

Words: 73
Geometric distribution OurBigBook.com
Start a Markov chain at a nonabsorbing state i. If each departure from i has probability p>0 of absorption before the next return, the Strong Markov property makes the number of visits to i, including the initial one, a positive-integer geometric random variable of parameter p. For a continuous-time Markov chain whose holding times at i are independent rate-λi​ exponentials, independently of the embedded jump chain, the total occupation time at i is a sum of K such holding times and has expected value 1/(pλi​).

Binomial proportion (p​=D/n)

Zero-event binomial upper confidence bound (pU​=1−α1/n)

Words: 62
Binomial distribution OurBigBook.com
If X∼Bin(n,p) and X=0 is observed, the maximum-likelihood estimate of p is zero. A one-sided 1−α upper confidence bound solves PpU​​(X=0)=(1−pU​)n=α, so pU​=1−α1/n. For large n, pU​≃−log(α)/n, giving approximately 3/n at 95% confidence. This describes uncertainty after no observed events, rather than proving that the underlying probability is zero. It assumes independent observations with a common event probability; clustered data require a different uncertainty calculation.

Constrained Poisson and multinomial likelihood equivalence

Words: 72
Multinomial distribution OurBigBook.com
For cell means μj​ with ∑j​μj​=n, the independent Poisson distribution likelihood and the multinomial distribution likelihood with probabilities μj​/n differ by a parameter-independent factor. Thus a maximum-likelihood estimator for the unrestricted Poisson model whose fitted total is n is also an estimator for the constrained multinomial model. Equality of named parameter estimates presumes identifiability and uniqueness; without these, the conclusion concerns the sets of maximizers. A free intercept in a log-linear Poisson model enforces the fitted-total equation.

Finite-sample bias in a negative multinomial probability estimator

Words: 64
Negative multinomial distribution OurBigBook.com
For n independent one-stop negative multinomial distribution observations, the natural estimates are α=SX​/(n+SX​+SY​) and β​=SY​/(n+SX​+SY​). Their sum is g((SX​+SY​)/n) with strictly concave g(z)=z/(1+z), so Jensen's inequality makes its expectation strictly below α+β. Multinomial distribution OurBigBook.com

At uniform probabilities on k categories, Pearson-standardized deviations have covariance equal to the orthogonal projection onto the (k−1)-dimensional subspace of zero-sum vectors. Uniform balls-in-bins allocation ((B1​,…,Bm​)∼Multinomial(n;1/m,…,1/m))

Words: 145 Articles: 1
Multinomial distribution OurBigBook.com
Place n labelled balls independently into m labelled bins, each with probability 1/m. The vector of bin counts has a multinomial distribution, and each count separately has the binomial distribution Bin(n,1/m). Distinct bin counts have covariance −n/m2, so they are dependent for n>0 and m>1. Counts of bins of a specified size are sums of indicator variables; their expectations follow from the linearity of expectation, without any independence between bins.

Poisson limit for occupancy fractions (E[Nj​]/m→e−λλj/j!)

Words: 79
Uniform balls-in-bins allocation OurBigBook.com
In a uniform balls-in-bins allocation with m→∞ and n/m→λ∈(0,∞), a fixed bin count has convergence in distribution to a Poisson distribution variable of mean λ by the Poisson limit theorem. If Nj​ counts bins containing exactly j balls, symmetry and the linearity of expectation give E[Nj​]/m=P(B1​=j)→e−λλj/j! for each fixed j. In particular the expected empty fraction tends to e−λ, and the expected fraction with at least two balls tends to 1−(1+λ)e−λ. These are statements about expectations; convergence of the random fractions requires an additional argument.
