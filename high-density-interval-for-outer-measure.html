High-density interval for outer measure (m∗(E∩I)>λ∣I∣,0<λ<1)

For any E⊆R with positive Lebesgue outer measure and any 0<λ<1, some bounded nonempty open interval I satisfies the displayed inequality. The set E need not be a Lebesgue measurable set.
To prove this, first choose a bounded portion F=E∩[−N,N] with 0<a=m∗(F)<∞, using countable subadditivity. Choose a countable open-interval cover (Ij​) of F with ∑j​∣Ij​∣<a/λ. If m∗(E∩Ij​)≤λ∣Ij​∣ for every j, then
a≤∑j​m∗(F∩Ij​)≤λ∑j​∣Ij​∣<a,

a contradiction. For measurable E this gives the high-density interval in a positive-measure subset of the real line without requiring the full Lebesgue density theorem.
