Maximum of a downward-biased random walk (P(M=j)=(1−p/q)(p/q)j,j≥0)

 ... Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Random walk Random walk on a graph Biased random walk
OurBigBook.com Words: 94
For a nearest-neighbour random walk with upward probability 0<p<q=1−p, start at S0​=0 and put M=supn≥0​Sn​. Its probability distribution is geometric on the nonnegative integers, with the displayed mass and expected value p/(q−p). To prove this, the exponential martingale of a biased simple random walk Zn​=(q/p)Sn​ has expectation one. Stop at the first hit Hk​ of k. Before that hit, Zn∧Hk​​≤(q/p)k; the strong law of large numbers gives Sn​→−∞ on paths without a hit. Dominated convergence theorem therefore gives 1=(q/p)kP(Hk​<∞). Taking differences of P(M≥k)=(p/q)k yields the mass. If time zero is excluded, the maximum can equal −1 with probability q−p and does not have this nonnegative geometric distribution.
