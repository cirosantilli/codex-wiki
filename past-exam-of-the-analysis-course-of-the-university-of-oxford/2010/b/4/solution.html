The Baire category theorem states that a nonempty complete metric space cannot be a countable union of closed nowhere dense sets. The Uniform boundedness principle says that if V is a Banach space, W a normed vector space, and T⊆B(V,W) is pointwise bounded, meaning supT∈T​∥Tx∥<∞ for every x∈V, then supT∈T​∥T∥<∞.
To prove it, let En​={x:supT∈T​∥Tx∥≤n}. Each set is closed by continuity of the individual operators, and their union is V. The Baire category theorem gives a ball B(x0​,r) inside some EN​. For ∥h∥<r, both x0​+h and x0​ are in EN​, so ∥Th∥≤∥T(x0​+h)∥+∥Tx0​∥≤2N for every T. Scaling gives ∥T∥≤2N/r uniformly. The zero-space and empty-family cases are immediate.
For the first family on a Hilbert space, fix x. The functionals y↦⟨y,Tx⟩ have operator norm ∥Tx∥ by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and equality on the direction of Tx. Their values are pointwise bounded by the hypothesis, after taking complex conjugates if necessary. Apply the Uniform boundedness principle to obtain supT∈E​∥Tx∥<∞. Apply it a second time to the operator family itself to conclude supT∈E​∥T∥<∞.
For the diagonal hypothesis, the answer depends on the scalar field. Over C, let qT​(x)=⟨Tx,x⟩, using the convention that the inner product is linear in its first variable. The complex polarization identity gives
4⟨Tx,y⟩=qT​(x+y)−qT​(x−y)+iqT​(x+iy)−iqT​(x−iy).
(18)
The assumed bounds at these four fixed vectors make every matrix coefficient uniformly bounded, so the preceding argument applies. Thus the complex-space answer is yes, even without assuming self-adjointness.
Over R, take X=R2 and
J=(01​−10​),F={nJ:n≥1}.
(19)
Every diagonal value ⟨nJx,x⟩ is zero, whereas ∥nJ∥=n. Thus the real-space answer is no in general. Real diagonal values do not detect the skew-symmetric part of an operator. This is diagonal boundedness of operator families over real and complex Hilbert spaces.
