For a positive operator, ⟨Tx,x⟩≥0. We show directly that every nonreal or negative real λ lies in the resolvent set.
If Imλ=0, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and reality of ⟨Tx,x⟩ give
∥(T−λI)x∥∥x∥≥∣⟨(T−λI)x,x⟩∣≥∣Imλ∣∥x∥2.
(22)
Thus T−λI is bounded below, hence injective with closed range: a convergent sequence of outputs has Cauchy preimages, whose limit maps to that output. Its adjoint is T−λˉI, which has trivial kernel by the same estimate. The identity Ran(A)⊥=kerA∗, proved by the same inner-product calculation as in (i), makes its range dense. Being closed and dense, it is all of X, and the lower bound makes the inverse bounded.
If λ<0 is real, positivity gives ⟨(T−λI)x,x⟩≥(−λ)∥x∥2, so the same argument applies with lower bound −λ. Here the operator is self-adjoint, so its range is dense because its own kernel is zero. Hence
σ(T)⊆[0,∞).​
(23)
