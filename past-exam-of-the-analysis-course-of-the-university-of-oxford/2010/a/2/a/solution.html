Equip the continuous dual space X′ with the operator norm
∥f∥=sup∥x∥≤1​∣f(x)∣.

Continuity of a linear functional makes this finite; homogeneity, definiteness and the triangle inequality follow directly from the supremum formula.
For completeness of the dual space, let (fn​) be Cauchy in this norm. For each x∈X, ∣fn​(x)−fm​(x)∣≤∥fn​−fm​∥∥x∥, so (fn​(x)) is Cauchy in R. Define f(x)=limn​fn​(x). Passing to the limit in the linearity identities makes f linear. Since a Cauchy sequence is bounded, supn​∥fn​∥≤C and ∣f(x)∣≤C∥x∥, so f∈X′.
Given ε>0, choose N with ∥fn​−fm​∥≤ε for n,m≥N. Let m→∞ pointwise; then ∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣≤ε∥x∥ and therefore ∥fn​−f∥≤ε. Thus X′ is a Banach space. This proof in fact works even if X itself is not complete.
