Define f♯(s)=f(−s)​. Interchanging the integrals and substituting variables gives
⟨Tf​g1​,g2​⟩=⟨g1​,Tf♯​g2​⟩.
(24)
Moreover Cauchy-Schwarz in the inner integral gives
∣⟨Tf​g1​,g2​⟩∣​≤∫R​∣f(s)∣​∫R​g1​(t−s)g2​(t)​dt​ds≤∥f∥1​∥g1​∥2​∥g2​∥2​.​
(25)
Taking the supremum over unit g2​ proves ∥Tf​g1​∥2​≤∥f∥1​∥g1​∥2​, so Tf​ is bounded with norm at most ∥f∥1​, and the adjoint identity is justified.
It remains to ensure necessity is a condition on the kernel itself. If Th​=0 for some h∈L1, convolve h with a smooth compactly supported approximate identity ηε​∈L1∩L2. Since Th​ηε​=0, all these convolutions vanish. But h∗ηε​→h in L1, by continuity of translations in L1, so h=0 almost everywhere. Thus the map from kernels to convolution operators is injective. Applying this to h=f−f♯ yields the exact criterion
Tf​=Tf∗​⟺f(s)=f(−s)​ for almost every s.​
(26)
For real-valued kernels this reduces to evenness; real-valuedness by itself is insufficient.
