The norm-preserving form of the Hahn-Banach theorem states: if M is a linear subspace of a real or complex normed vector space X and f:M→K is a bounded linear functional, there exists F∈X∗ with F∣M​=f and ∥F∥=∥f∥.
For a real separable X, put C=∥f∥. The case C=0 is immediate. To extend over one vector x0​∈/M, choose a real number c between
supm∈M​(f(m)−C∥m−x0​∥)andinfn∈M​(C∥n+x0​∥−f(n)).
(4)
The lower endpoint is no greater than the upper: for any m,n∈M,
f(m)+f(n)=f(m+n)≤C∥m+n∥≤C∥m−x0​∥+C∥n+x0​∥.
(5)
The endpoints are finite because testing zero bounds them between finite numbers. Define F(m+tx0​)=f(m)+tc. The upper inequality ensures F(z)≤C∥z∥ for vectors with positive x0​ coefficient, and the lower inequality gives it for negative coefficient; it already holds on M. Applying the same bound to −z gives ∣F(z)∣≤C∥z∥. Thus the extension preserves the norm.
Now add a countable dense sequence of vectors one at a time. The consistent extensions define a bounded functional of norm C on the union of the increasing domains. That union is dense in X, so extension by continuity gives the desired functional on all of X. This proves the real separable case without a maximality argument.
For the density application, suppose the closed linear span M were proper. The Hahn-Banach theorem supplies a nonzero continuous linear functional annihilating M. By duality of sequence spaces, it has the form f(z)=∑j≥1​aj​zj​ with a∈ℓ1​, not identically zero. Let m be the first index with am​=0. Writing tn​=(n+1)−1, annihilation of xn​ gives
0=tn−m​f(xn​)=am​+∑j>m​aj​tnj−m​.
(6)

span​{xn​:n≥1}=c0​.​
(7)
This is geometric sequences with accumulating ratios span c0.
