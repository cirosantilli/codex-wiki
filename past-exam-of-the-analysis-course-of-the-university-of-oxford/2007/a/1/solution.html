A norm on a complex vector space is a map ∥⋅∥:X→[0,∞) that vanishes only at zero, satisfies ∥λx∥=∣λ∣∥x∥, and obeys the triangle inequality. The space is Banach when every Cauchy sequence in the resulting metric converges in X.

The series consisting of yn1​​ followed by these successive differences is absolutely summable in norm, so converges by hypothesis; its partial sums are exactly this subsequence. A Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges to the same limit, proving the absolute-series criterion for completeness.
