For the centered ball, the Hahn-Banach theorem supplies a norm-one real functional with f(y)=∥y∥>r. Hence f(z)≤r on the ball, and any t∈(r,∥y∥) separates it strictly from y.
For the general center, apply the same construction to y−x: choose ∥f∥=1 and f(y−x)=∥y−x∥. Then
f(z)≤f(x)+r(z∈B),f(y)=f(x)+∥y−x∥>f(x)+r.
(14)
For example,
t=f(x)+2r+∥y−x∥​​
(15)
gives f(z)<t<f(y) for every z∈B. The strict gap uses exactly the assumption that y lies outside the closed ball.
