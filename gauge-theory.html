Gauge theory

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Classical field theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 132 Articles: 1
A classical field theory is a gauge theory when its description has a local redundancy represented by gauge transformations. A gauge field defines the covariant derivative that compares matter fields at different points, and its gauge field strength measures the curvature of that comparison. Electromagnetism is the familiar Abelian example; Yang-Mills theory generalizes it to a non-Abelian internal group. Both classical equations and their quantum field theory can have this structure. Physically equivalent gauge descriptions must give the same observables, even though the gauge potential changes.
