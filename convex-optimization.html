Pari-mutuel expected-return allocation (xi​=(pi​si​/τ​−si​)+)

Words: 76
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
For positive existing stakes si​ and budget b, maximizing ∑i​pi​xi​/(si​+xi​) is equivalent to minimizing ∑i​pi​si​/(si​+xi​). A positive multiplier τ chosen so that ∑i​xi​=b gives the displayed allocation. Each coordinate minimizes pi​si​/(si​+x)+τx on x≥0, which proves global optimality by Lagrangian sufficiency theorem. Its active set is ordered by pi​/si​. Zero existing stakes require a separate payoff convention and can cause nonattainment; the positive-stake formula should not be extended through undefined ratios.

Separation oracle

Words: 112 Articles: 1
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
A separation oracle either certifies that a supplied point belongs to a convex feasible set or returns a violated linear inequality whose half-space contains the whole feasible set. Its running time can be small even when the set has exponentially many defining inequalities. Together with rational encoding bounds and an outer bound, this permits polynomial time ellipsoid method feasibility and optimization.

Path-constraint separation by shortest paths (∑e∈p​xe​≥1(p:s⇝t))

Words: 57
Separation oracle OurBigBook.com
After testing nonnegative edge coordinates and any explicit box or budget inequalities, treat those coordinates as lengths. A shortest path of length below one supplies a violated path inequality; a minimum length at least one certifies all path inequalities. If no source-to-target path exists, all those inequalities are vacuous. Thus a polynomial time shortest path problem calculation separates an exponentially constrained linear program.

Chambolle–Pock algorithm

Words: 126 Articles: 1
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
For a saddle point problem minu​maxv​{F(u)+⟨Du,v⟩−G(v)}, one update ordering is
uk+1=proxτF​(uk−τD∗vk),vk+1=proxσG​(vk+σD(2uk+1−uk)).
(1)
The proximal operators treat both nonsmooth convex terms, while the extrapolation 2uk+1−uk couples the primal and dual variables. The primal objective is F(u)+G∗(Du), with G∗ the convex conjugate. A dual-first ordering is obtained by exchanging roles; consistent indexing is needed when comparing the formulas.

Convergence of primal-dual hybrid gradient (τσ∥D∥2<1)

Words: 68
Chambolle–Pock algorithm OurBigBook.com
For proper lower semicontinuous convex functions on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, a nonempty saddle point set, and constant positive steps satisfying the displayed inequality, the primal-dual hybrid gradient method with extrapolation parameter one converges to a saddle point. The strict condition controls the bilinear coupling by the operator norm. It does not remove the need for existence of a saddle point. The fixed-step theorem and assumptions are recalled in Malitsky and Pock, A first-order primal-dual algorithm with linesearch, Section 1.

Convex positively one-homogeneous functional (J(tu)=tJ(u)(t≥0))

Words: 198 Articles: 1
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
This is a proper convex function that has degree-one positive homogeneity, with J(0)=0. It need not be even or nonnegative; linear functionals are examples. Its subdifferential satisfies
p∈∂J(u)⟺⟨p,u⟩=J(u) and ⟨p,z⟩≤J(z) for every z.
(2)
The two tests z=0 and z=2u prove equality; the subgradient inequality then gives the global upper bound. Conversely that bound and equality prove the subgradient condition. Hence a subgradient at u also supports every nonnegative multiple of u. Requiring J(−u)=J(u) gives an absolutely one-homogeneous functional.

Generalized eigenfunction in the forward-operator metric (λK∗Ku∈∂J(u))

Words: 123
Convex positively one-homogeneous functional OurBigBook.com
A nonzero u satisfying this relation, with Ku=0, generalizes the eigenfunction of an absolutely one-homogeneous functional to the quadratic geometry induced by the forward map. Pairing with u yields λ=J(u)/∥Ku∥2. With unit data norm it is a generalized singular vector. If λ>0, 0≤αλ≤1 and the exact data are Ku, the vector (1−αλ)u minimizes the quadratic variational regularization objective. Substitute its raywise subgradient into the subgradient optimality condition. Every minimizing branch has the same predicted data, and injectivity of K gives equality of vectors. The Bregman distance along this ray is zero even though the reconstruction is shrunk. For nonnegative J the branch continues as (1−αλ)+​u; nonnegativity supplies 0∈∂J(0) for the threshold argument.
Background on these generalized singular vectors: Benning and Burger, Ground States and Singular Vectors of Convex Variational Regularization Methods.

Conic optimization

Words: 1k Articles: 17
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
Optimization of a linear function subject to affine constraints and membership in a closed convex cone. Choosing the positive semidefinite cone gives semidefinite programming; other choices include copositive optimization and completely positive optimization. Dual cones produce scalar-product bounds on feasible objectives.

Exactly one of the two displayed systems is feasible. Closedness of finitely generated cones and the Fenchel-Moreau theorem applied to a cone's indicator functional provide a proof through its polar cone. It yields a nonnegative multiplier certificate when a finite system of linear inequalities is inconsistent.

Normalized integer Farkas infeasibility gap (bTλ≥[(n+1)U]−(n+1))
Words: 130
Farkas certificate for linear inequalities OurBigBook.com
Suppose A,b have integer entries of absolute value at most U≥1, A has n columns, and Ax≥b is infeasible. A Farkas certificate for linear inequalities can be chosen with λ≥0, ATλ=0, 1Tλ=1 and the displayed lower bound. Normalize an infeasibility certificate to obtain the last equality, then maximize bTλ over the resulting compact linear polyhedron. A maximizing extreme point has at most n+1 positive entries: otherwise the augmented columns (AiT​,1) on its support would be dependent, allowing feasible perturbations in both directions. For its k≤n+1 independent supported columns, select k independent equations. Cramer's rule expresses the supported entries with a common nonzero integer determinant denominator. That determinant has absolute value at most k!Uk≤[(n+1)U]n+1. The positive value bTλ therefore has a positive integer numerator over a denominator of at most this size, proving the bound.

Interior-point method

Words: 553 Articles: 8
Conic optimization OurBigBook.com
An interior-point method solves constrained mathematical optimization problems by maintaining variables in the interiors of their feasible cones or inequality domains. Barrier derivatives are defined there, and damped Newton methods keep subsequent iterates in their domains. A central path gives a family of barrier-regularized optima whose duality gap approaches zero; a conic phase-I problem can supply a strict feasible starting point.

Homogeneous self-dual embedding of a linear program (xTs+τκ=(n+1)θρ)

Words: 138
Interior-point method OurBigBook.com
For a linear program min{cTx:Ax=b, x≥0} and its dual linear program, write e=1, bˉ=b−Ae, cˉ=c−e, and zˉ=cTe+1. Introduce x,s≥0, τ,κ,θ,ρ≥0, and a free multiplier λ satisfying
Ax−bτ+bˉθ=0,−ATλ+cτ−s−cˉθ=0,bTλ−cTx−κ+zˉθ=0,−bˉTλ+cˉTx−zˉτ+(n+1)ρ=0.
(3)
The point x=s=e, τ=κ=θ=ρ=1, λ=0 is strictly positive in all constrained coordinates. Multiplying the first three equations by λT,xT,τ respectively and adding gives the displayed identity. Minimizing θ has value zero: primal-dual optimal solutions or certificates from Farkas' lemma give zero-θ solutions after selecting positive ρ and scaling. At θ=0, τ>0 gives an optimal primal-dual pair by division by τ, while κ>0 gives a primal infeasibility or dual infeasibility certificate. A point with τ=κ=0 is not decisive; choosing a relative interior point of the optimal face of a linear program avoids that degeneracy because the face contains a point with τ+κ>0.

Conic phase-I problem

Words: 65
Interior-point method OurBigBook.com
An auxiliary conic optimization problem searches for strict feasibility before following a central path. Given e∈intK, minimizing t subject to Ax−b+te∈K and t≥−1 has a strict feasible start for sufficiently large t. Any feasible solution with t<0 certifies Ax−b∈intK. If the original problem is strictly feasible, a small negative t is feasible. Equality constraints and dual feasibility require their corresponding auxiliary procedures.

Self-concordant barrier (F,ν)

Words: 298 Articles: 5
Interior-point method OurBigBook.com
A convex three-times differentiable barrier F is self-concordant when
∣D3F(x)[h,h,h]∣≤2(D2F(x)[h,h])3/2.
(4)
A barrier of parameter ν additionally satisfies ∣DF(x)[h]∣≤νD2F(x)[h,h]​ and diverges at the domain boundary. Logarithmically homogeneous cone barriers satisfy F(tx)=F(x)−νlogt. The orthant barrier −∑i​logxi​ has parameter equal to the dimension, while the Lorentz-cone barrier −log(t2−∥z∥2) on t>∥z∥ has parameter two. Their Hessians define Dikin ellipsoids and control interior Newton steps.

For a logarithmically homogeneous barrier on a proper cone, the convex conjugate with reversed argument defines a barrier on the interior dual cone. The inverse gradient relation makes y=−μ∇F(s) equivalent to s=−μ∇F†​(y). This gives a joint primal-dual central path without assuming the barrier is identical on a self-dual cone.

Central path (μ>0)

For a strictly feasible primal-dual conic optimization problem and a logarithmically homogeneous barrier, the central path consists of solutions
s=Ax−b∈intK,A⊤y=c,y∈intK∗,y=−μ∇F(s),μ>0.

Central-path Newton system
Words: 65
Central path OurBigBook.com
At a target barrier parameter μ, let rp​=Ax−b−s, rd​=A⊤y−c, rc​=y+μ∇F(s). The Newton direction solves
AΔx−Δs=−rp​,A⊤Δy=−rd​,Δy+μ∇2F(s)Δs=−rc​.
(7)
Full column rank of A and a positive-definite barrier Hessian give a positive-definite reduced matrix. Backtracking must keep both cone variables interior; solving the linear equations alone does not guarantee that a full step stays inside the cones.

For minx​c⊤x subject to Ax−b∈K, the Lagrangian dual problem is maxy​b⊤y subject to A⊤y=c and y∈K∗. At feasible primal-dual points, the gap is ⟨Ax−b,y⟩≥0. A self-dual cone has K∗=K, but self-duality alone does not imply feasibility or strong duality.

Completely positive optimization

Words: 47
Conic optimization OurBigBook.com
Conic optimization using the completely positive cone. For a real symmetric matrix Q, the trace-normalized program min{⟨Q,X⟩F​:X∈CPn​, trX=1} minimizes a weighted average of nonnegative-unit-vector Rayleigh quotients. The weights are the squared norms of the factors in X=∑j​xj​xjT​. Hence a minimizing rank-one factor attains the same value as the original orthant minimum.

Let α=min{xTQx:x≥0, ∥x∥2​=1} for a real symmetric matrix Q. Compactness gives attainment. Homogeneity shows Q−λI is a copositive matrix exactly when λ≤α, proving
α=max{λ:Q−λI∈COPn​}.

Robust optimization

Words: 179 Articles: 2
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
Robust optimization chooses a decision while accounting for every parameter in a prescribed uncertainty set. A worst-case maximization takes the form supx​infr∈U​F(x,r) with the decision chosen before the uncertain parameter. With affine dependence on the uncertainty, the support function and linear programming duality often replace the inner optimization by explicit constraints.

Robust linear optimization over the probability simplex

Words: 70
Robust optimization OurBigBook.com
Worst-case linear revenue over a polyhedral uncertainty set is optimized by the linear program
maxx,α,β​ r0T​x−1T(α+β),x∈Δn​,α,β≥0,PT(α−β)=x.
(9)
Here Δn​ is the probability simplex. Finite decisions are exactly its intersection with rangePT. If this intersection is empty, all decisions have worst-case value −∞ and the displayed linear program is infeasible; its supremum over the empty feasible set is also −∞. Full column rank of P suffices to make all simplex decisions finite.

Polyhedral uncertainty set (U={r:∥P(r−r0​)∥∞​≤1})

A polyhedral uncertainty set is specified by finitely many linear inequalities. The displayed centered example is the inverse image of a box under a linear map. It is nonempty because it contains r0​, and contains every line r0​+td with d∈kerP. Consequently it need not be bounded when P is rank deficient. Its supremum norm constraint is equivalent to −1≤P(r−r0​)≤1.

If two nonnegative finite-valued convex functions are defined on all of a finite-dimensional space, their infimal convolution is finite, convex and continuous everywhere. Convex perturbation duality then gives strong duality and dual attainment at every argument. It does not require or imply attainment of the primal decomposition infimum.

Proportional fairness

Words: 261 Articles: 4
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
A rate allocation is proportionally fair when the sum of proportional changes toward any feasible competitor is nonpositive. If route r has nr​ identical active flows, this condition is ∑r​nr​(xr​−xr​)/xr​≤0 and is equivalent to maximizing weighted logarithmic utility subject to capacity constraints.

Inactive routes in proportional fairness

Words: 59
Proportional fairness OurBigBook.com
When a route has zero active flows, its term is absent from weighted logarithmic utility. Its aggregate allocation is taken to be zero; a per-flow rate on it has no operational meaning and need not be uniquely determined. One must not divide by its zero population. Empty-network and empty-group cases can therefore be treated separately while preserving uniqueness of active-flow rates.

Proportionally fair allocation on a four-cycle

Words: 129 Articles: 1
Proportional fairness OurBigBook.com
For the four routes consisting of adjacent pairs of a unit-capacity four-cycle, put P=n1​+n3​, Q=n2​+n4​, and N=P+Q. Every active route in the first group has total service P/N, and every active route in the second has total service Q/N. Its per-flow rate is this total divided by its active flow count.

Opposite-route reduction for a proportionally fair four-cycle (max(z1​,z3​)+max(z2​,z4​)≤1)

Words: 73
Proportionally fair allocation on a four-cycle OurBigBook.com
Write zr​=nr​xr​ for aggregate route service. The four resource constraints are all sums of one odd and one even route service, so they are equivalent to the displayed maximum constraint. For fixed maxima, increasing each active service to its group's maximum improves weighted logarithmic utility. Maximizing Plogp+Qlogq with p+q≤1 gives p=P/(P+Q) and q=Q/(P+Q), where P=n1​+n3​, Q=n2​+n4​. Inactive routes are omitted; the same formula covers states where one group is empty.

Weighted logarithmic utility (∑r​nr​logxr​)

Words: 33
Proportional fairness OurBigBook.com
The utility ∑r​nr​logxr​ sums logarithmic utilities over individual flows. Its first-order condition gives proportional fairness. Its negative is a strictly convex function on active flow rates, giving uniqueness of those rates on a convex set of feasible allocations.

Semidefinite programming

Words: 263 Articles: 4
Convex optimization OurBigBook.com
A semidefinite program optimizes a linear function subject to equalities between affine functions and positive semidefinite matrix inequalities. It generalizes linear programming: a diagonal matrix is a positive semidefinite matrix exactly when its diagonal entries are nonnegative. Matrix trace expresses the objective as ⟨C,X⟩=tr(CX) for real symmetric matrices.

Semidefinite relaxation of slab-constrained quadratic maximization (max{trX:X⪰0, aiT​Xai​≤1})

Replacing xxT by a general positive semidefinite matrix relaxes maximization of ∥x∥22​ subject to ∣aiT​x∣≤1. The relaxed value is an upper bound because xxT has trace ∥x∥22​ and satisfies the same quadratic constraints. Both problems are unbounded if the ai​ fail to span the ambient space. Use an orthogonal eigendecomposition X=VΛVT and a vector ξ of independent Rademacher random variables. Water-filling algorithm

Firmly nonexpansive mapping

Words: 27
Monotone operator OurBigBook.com
A mapping T on an inner product space is firmly nonexpansive when
∥T(v)−T(w)∥2≤⟨T(v)−T(w),v−w⟩.
(31)
Every firmly nonexpansive mapping is nonexpansive, and the resolvent of a maximal monotone operator is firmly nonexpansive.

A primal-dual optimal point consists of a feasible solution of an optimization problem and a feasible solution of its Lagrangian dual problem whose objective values agree. Under differentiability and suitable convexity, such points satisfy the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions.
