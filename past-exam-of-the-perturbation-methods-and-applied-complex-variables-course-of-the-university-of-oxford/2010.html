For the left inner coordinate X=x/δ, the equation becomes YXXX​−YX​+δ2XY=0. At leading order the solution that matches a bounded outer expansion is Y0​=A+De−X; the growing exponential is excluded. The boundary value gives A+D=1. Since y′(0)=1 is finite, the possible O(δ−1) derivative must vanish, so D=0 and A=1.
At the next order, Y1/2​=B+Ee−X. Its boundary value is zero, giving E=−B, while its physical derivative at zero is −E=1. Hence B=1, and the left inner expansion is 1+δ(1−e−X)+O(δ2).
At the right endpoint put ξ=(1−x)/δ. The equation is Yξξξ​−Yξ​=δ(1−δξ)Y. Matching the outer expansion at one and imposing Y(0)=1 give
Y0​=e​+(1−e​)e−ξ.
(4)

Y1/2​=e​(1−ξ)+[21−e​​ξ−e​]e−ξ.
(5)
Indeed the operator D3−D sends −e​ξ to e​ and sends ξe−ξ to 2e−ξ.
Add the two decaying inner corrections to the outer solution, subtracting their common matching parts. Since δξ=1−x, the composite asymptotic expansion is
yc​(x)=​ex2/2(1+δ)−δe−x/δ+e−(1−x)/δ[(e​−1)(2x​−23​)−δe​].​​
(6)
It reproduces all three boundary conditions to the retained algebraic orders, with exponentially small interactions between endpoints. The right endpoint has an O(1) boundary layer in y. The left correction has only amplitude O(ϵ​) in y, but its derivative is O(1) and is necessary for the derivative boundary condition. Thus the left layer is a weak derivative boundary layer; if “genuine” refers specifically to an order-one change in the function itself, only the right layer is genuine in that sense. Both nonzero corrections belong in the expansion through O(ϵ​).

Write A=aeiϕ. The modulation equation gives aT​=0 and ϕT​=−5a2/3. Matching the leading initial conditions gives a=1, ϕ(0)=0. Therefore
U0​=2cos(T0​−5T/3),u(t)∼2cos[(1−5ϵ2/3)t].​

i OurBigBook.com
At a vertex with exterior turning angle βj​π, the interior angle is (1−βj​)π. A local conformal map from a half-plane therefore behaves as f(Z)−zj​∼cj​(Z−Xj​)1−βj​, so its derivative has local exponent −βj​.
On each real boundary interval between prevertices, argf′ is constant because the image is a straight side. Across a prevertex its change is the exterior turning angle. Taking arg(Z−Xj​)∈(0,π), the product ∏j​(Z−Xj​)−βj​ has exactly these changes. Dividing f′ by that product removes the corner singularities and leaves a holomorphic nonzero function with constant boundary argument. Schwarz reflection and the regular behavior at infinity make it constant. This gives the Schwarz-Christoffel formula
f′(Z)=Cj=1∏n​(Z−Xj​)−βj​.​
(23)
There are three freely normalizable real prevertices, because an automorphism of the upper half-plane has three real parameters. After those are fixed, the remaining n−3 prevertex parameters are determined by the polygon's shape through the side-length conditions. They are not further independent normalization choices.
If the last prevertex is at infinity, absorb its constant scale into C and omit its finite factor:
f′(Z)=Cj=1∏n−1​(Z−Xj​)−βj​.​
(24)
For a bounded polygon, ∑j​βj​=2, so this derivative behaves like Zβn​−2, consistent with the corner described in the local coordinate 1/Z.

To verify the image and global injectivity, factor the construction. Squaring maps the upper half-plane bijectively onto C∖[0,∞). Thus 1−Z2 ranges over C∖(−∞,1]. The positive-real-part square root maps this onto the right half-plane with the interval (0,1] removed; reciprocation maps it onto the right half-plane slit along [1,∞). This also identifies the slit tip as a boundary point; the strict inequality in the printed slit description must be understood with that boundary convention.

In particular Z=−1 maps to the lower infinite prime end A, Z=0 to the tip B=1, and Z=1 to the upper infinite prime end C. The real-axis point at infinity maps to zero on the vertical wall.

The complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow maps the fluid region to the strip 0<Imw<1. Its lower boundary is the wall, with B at w=0; upstream is Rew→−∞ and downstream is Rew→+∞. The free streamline is the upper strip boundary.
Write q=w′=u−iv. The horizontal wall gives positive real velocities running from q=1 to the stagnation value q=0 at B. The vertical wall gives negative imaginary velocities from zero to −i. On the free surface ∣q∣=1, with the angle turning continuously from zero to −π/2. Thus the hodograph plane domain is the fourth-quadrant quarter of the unit disk, bounded by those two radii and the unit-circle arc.
The following successive conformal maps straighten it. First r=q2 maps to the lower half unit disk. Next s=(r−1)/(r+1) maps to the third quadrant, since Res=(∣r∣2−1)/∣r+1∣2<0 and Ims=2Imr/∣r+1∣2<0. Finally s2 maps onto the upper half-plane. The three wall landmarks q=1,0,−i go to 0,1,∞ respectively.
The strip map eπw also maps onto the upper half-plane, with these same landmarks 0,1,∞. The unique half-plane automorphism fixing all three is the identity. Therefore
eπw=(q2+1q2−1​)2,q=w′.​
(32)
The free surface maps to the negative real axis in either construction, as required by its streamfunction value one. This completes the right-angle free-streamline hodograph map construction.

For the standard canonical Wiener-Hopf factorization, first assume the nonzero kernel has Index of a Wiener-Hopf kernel zero and a continuous analytic logarithm ℓ=logF tending to zero at both ends of the strip, with sufficient decay for the Cauchy projections. Choose α<a<b<β. Let Γ+​ be the line Imζ=a oriented left to right, with its factor used above that line; let Γ−​ be the line Imζ=b oriented left to right, with its factor used below that line.
For a<Imz<b, closing the rectangle between the two lines and using the Cauchy integral formula gives
logF+​(z)−logF−​(z)=2πi1​(∫Γ+​​−∫Γ−​​)ζ−zℓ(ζ)​dζ=ℓ(z).
(33)
Thus F=F+​/F−​. Multiplying Fw+​=w−​ gives F+​w+​=F−​w−​ in the overlap. The canonical factors are analytic, nonzero, and normalized at infinity in their respective half-planes, so part (a) yields
F+​w+​=F−​w−​=constant.​
(34)
Contours may be shifted within the strip without changing their factors on the appropriate side. When necessary, a subtracted Cauchy kernel fixes convergence and the common normalization constant.
The printed hypotheses omit the index and factor-growth qualifications; F→1 alone does not ensure them. An explicit counterexample is F(z)=(z+i)/(z−i) on −1/2<Imz<1/2, with w+​=(z−i)/(z+i) and w−​=1. Both w functions are analytic and bounded at infinity in their prescribed half-planes, and the relation holds. If admissible analytic factors made their common product a nonzero constant, w−​=1 would force F−​ constant and hence F+​ proportional to F, impossible because F has a pole at i in the plus half-plane. The natural factors F+​=z+i, F−​=z−i instead give the nonconstant common polynomial z−i. Thus the boxed constant conclusion is the intended index-zero result, not a consequence of the literal assumptions alone.

There is also a far-field qualification in the source. The formula is the intended bounded mixed-boundary solution, but it does not tend to zero along every path to infinity. This can be checked explicitly: in parabolic coordinates x=a2−b2, y=2ab, with a,b>0, the mixed-boundary convection-diffusion solution in parabolic coordinates is
