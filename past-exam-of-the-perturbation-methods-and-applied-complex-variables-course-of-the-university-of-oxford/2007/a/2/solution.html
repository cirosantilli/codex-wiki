Fast trajectories are horizontal. Their equilibria lie on the cubic y=h(x); the transverse linearization is −h′(x)=1−x2, so the outer branches ∣x∣>1 attract and the middle branch repels. On a horizontal line, arrows point right when y>h(x) and left when y<h(x).
The source prints the slow time as T=k/t. The slow variable needed here is T=t/k. With this correction,
k−2dTdx​=y−h(x),dTdy​=λ−x.
(7)
At leading order y=h(x) and
(x2−1)dTdx​=λ−x.
(8)
The reciprocal printed variable would give −T2yT​=λ−x, rather than the autonomous slow equations used in the stated period formula.
For −1<λ<1, the left attracting branch runs from x=−2 to the fold x=−1, where y=2/3. The horizontal fast jump at this height lands at x=2. The right branch then runs from 2 to the fold 1, where y=−2/3, followed by a fast jump to −2. The equilibrium at x=λ is on the repelling middle branch. The two slow segments dominate the period:
T∼k[∫−2−1​λ−xx2−1​dx+∫12​x−λx2−1​dx].​
(9)
The jump times are negligible on this O(k) scale. Substituting u=−x in the first integral also gives the compact value
T∼k[3+(λ2−1)log1−λ24−λ2​].
(10)
For λ=1/2, the negative slow segment takes k[1−43​log(5/3)], and the positive one takes k[2−43​log3]. Thus x(t) rises slowly from −2 to −1, jumps to 2, decreases more slowly to 1, then jumps back to −2; the jumps are nearly vertical in the time trace.
For λ>1 the fast horizontal field and attracting branches are unchanged. The right slow branch has arrows toward x=λ from both directions; the left branch still moves toward −1 and jumps right. The equilibrium (λ,h(λ)) is stable, so the large relaxation cycle is replaced by approach to this equilibrium. If λ>2, a landing at 2 moves right toward λ, rather than left.
