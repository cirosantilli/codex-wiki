Choose each factor with its argument between zero and π in the upper half-plane. On an interval between consecutive real prevertices, each factor has a constant boundary argument, so f′ has constant argument and the image is a straight side. Crossing Xj​ from left to right decreases arg(Z−Xj​) by π, hence increases argf′ by βj​π, giving the prescribed exterior turn. Locally,
f(Z)−zj​∼Cj​(Z−Xj​)1−βj​,
(22)
so an upper half-disc maps to angle (1−βj​)π, the required interior angle. The overall constant rotates and scales the polygon; integration adds translation. For a general prescribed polygon, the prevertex positions must also satisfy the side-length conditions: the derivative formula fixes angles but arbitrary choices do not fix arbitrary side lengths.
For the slit domain, the slit tip has interior angle 2π, so its exterior parameter is −1. The two vertices at infinity give the factors at ±1, leading to f′(Z) proportional to Z(1−Z2)−3/2. Integrating and imposing the tip value gives
z=f(Z)=1−Z2​1​.​
(23)
The square root is the principal one: for ℑZ>0, 1−Z2 never lies on the nonpositive real axis. Thus ℜf>0. Boundary points −1<X<1 map to the two banks of the slit, with X=0 at the tip; the outside intervals map to the imaginary axis. The derivative is nonzero in the open half-plane. Conversely each point of the slit domain determines exactly one root Z of Z2=1−z−2 with positive imaginary part, proving bijectivity.
The Möbius map (Z−1)/(Z+1) takes the upper half-plane to itself, so its principal argument lies in (0,π). The imaginary part of its analytic logarithm is harmonic, equals π on (−1,1) and zero on the other real intervals. Thus
U(X,Y)=π1​argZ+1Z−1​.
(24)
It lies between zero and one and has the required boundary limits away from the two discontinuities. Pulling this harmonic function back by the inverse conformal map yields
u(x,y)=π1​argZ(z)+1Z(z)−1​,Z(z)2=1−z−2,ℑZ(z)>0.​
(25)
This specifies all branches and remains bounded even as z tends to either end at infinity.
To exhibit nonuniqueness without the growth restriction, put
H(z)=ℑ1−Z(z)2Z(z)​.
(26)
This is harmonic in the domain. On its boundary the corresponding Z is real and the rational expression is real, except at ±1, which represent infinity. At Z=∞, representing z=0, the expression tends to zero. Thus H has zero finite boundary data. It is nonzero, since for Z=iY its value is Y/(1+Y2)>0, and it is unbounded as Z approaches either pole ±1 non-tangentially. Therefore u+cH gives distinct solutions with the same finite boundary data when boundedness at infinity is removed.

The potential plane is the strip 0<ψ<1. The wall maps to its lower boundary, with upstream ϕ→−∞, the corner at w=0, and downstream ϕ→+∞. The free surface maps to its upper boundary.
Write q=w′=u−iv. Far upstream q=1; far downstream the flow points vertically down and q=i. On the two wall rays, q runs along the positive real and positive imaginary axes. The reentrant fluid corner has angle 3π/2, so its potential behaves locally as (z−B)2/3 and its speed diverges like ∣z−B∣−1/3. Thus the corner corresponds to q=∞, not q=0. The free surface has ∣q∣=1, giving the hodograph domain
ℜq>0,ℑq>0,∣q∣>1.
(30)
It is the exterior of a quarter unit disc in the first quadrant.
Now s=q2 maps this domain to the part of the upper half-plane outside the unit disc. The map
t=s+1s−1​
(31)
takes it to the first quadrant: its real and imaginary parts are positive because ∣s∣>1 and ℑs>0. Finally η=t2 maps that quadrant to the upper half-plane. The corresponding strip map is η=eπw. Both give upstream η=0, corner η=1, and downstream η=∞, so the three boundary normalizations fix the remaining real Möbius freedom. Consequently

A second contour starts at i and follows ζ=i−t, 0≤t<∞, choosing a continuous branch along the path. For ℜz>0 it converges at infinity; at the branch endpoint its integrable singularity is t−1/2. In both cases the boundary term contains 1+ζ2​ and vanishes at branch endpoints. The second integral has a nonzero logarithmic divergence as z→0 through positive real values, because its large-t integrand is a nonzero multiple of e−zt/t. It is therefore independent of the entire first solution; analytic continuation supplies solutions elsewhere away from the equation's singular point.
Strictly, nonzero w and an arbitrary contour alone do not prove the printed necessity for g. Necessity also needs injectivity of the chosen Laplace representation, while the vanishing-integrand argument above is the usual sufficient construction. The added entire integrand integrates to zero. Expanding the original weight on this circle gives integral 2πiJ0​(z), which is nonzero and solves the equation, although the modified weight is not a constant multiple of the printed one. The standard weights and contours above still give the requested independent solutions.

There are two distinct inconsistencies in the printed formulas. First the initial Cauchy integral has coefficient 1/(2π), whereas the stated boundary limits require 1/(2πi). Second the final integral is a solution of a difference jump problem, although the intervening equation for W is a sum jump problem. We derive the valid identities, show precisely where that final deduction fails, and give the corrected construction. Take c>0 and, initially, a Hölder continuous density on the interior with integrable endpoints, so the boundary limits and principal values exist.
Define the Cauchy principal value by
pv∫0c​t−xf(t)​dt=limδ↓0​(∫0x−δ​t−xf(t)​dt+∫x+δc​t−xf(t)​dt).

The subtracted integral has the common principal-value limit, while integration of the remaining constant density produces boundary arguments differing by 2π. This proves the Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem
(Cf​)±​=±21​f+2πi1​pv∫0c​t−xf(t)​dt.

In particular f=(Cf​)+​−(Cf​)−​ and (Cf​)+​+(Cf​)−​=(πi)−1pv∫f(t)/(t−x)dt. With the literal printed integral w=iCf​ instead, the limits are ±if/2+(2π)−1pv∫f/(t−x), and the next stated boundary equation would have right side iγ.
