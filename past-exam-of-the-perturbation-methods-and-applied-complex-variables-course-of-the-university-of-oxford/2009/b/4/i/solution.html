Write the complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow as w=ϕ+iψ, and the complex velocity as q=w′=u−iv. The lower solid wall has ψ=0. The upper solid wall and its continuing free streamline have constant ψ equal to the far-field volume flux per unit depth, namely a. Consequently the potential domain is the infinite strip
0<Imw<a.​
(32)
The points A,E correspond to ϕ→−∞, B,C to ϕ→+∞, and D is w=ia by the chosen potential normalization.
In the hodograph plane, the horizontal wall has q real positive, the vertical wall has q imaginary positive because the physical velocity is downward, and the fluid free boundary has ∣q∣=1. The upstream reservoir velocity tends to zero, the outgoing velocity is one, and the velocity at D is i. Thus the hodograph is the first-quadrant unit quarter disk, with A,E at q=0, B,C at q=1, and D at q=i.
