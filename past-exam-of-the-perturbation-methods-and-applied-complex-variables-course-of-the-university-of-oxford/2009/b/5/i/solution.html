Orient the contour so that the plus side is on the left; for a positively oriented simple closed contour this is its interior. If ζ(s) is a smooth arclength parametrization with ζ(0)=t, define the Cauchy principal value by deleting a symmetric interval ∣s∣<η and taking η↓0. This pairs the two singular sides at equal local arclength.
Near t, write f(ζ)=f(t)+[f(ζ)−f(t)]. The second term divided by ζ−t is regular because f is analytic there. For the constant term, indent the contour on either side of t by a small semicircle. Its limiting contribution to the Cauchy integral is ±f(t)/2, while the remaining contour tends to its principal value. This proves the Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem:
