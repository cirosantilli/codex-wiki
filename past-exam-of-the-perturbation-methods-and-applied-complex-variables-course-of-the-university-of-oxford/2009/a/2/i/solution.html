Set x=δX and write y(x)=Y(X). The equation becomes
δ2ϵ​Y′′+δα−1XαY′+Y=0.
(10)
A dominant balance must compare all three coefficients, rather than just the two derivative terms.
For −1<α<1, balancing diffusion and drift gives δ=ϵ1/(α+1). Their common coefficient tends to infinity, so the reaction term is smaller and the leading inner equation is
Y′′+XαY′=0,Y′=Cexp[−Xα+1/(α+1)].
(11)
This derivative is integrable at infinity, allowing an ordinary localized endpoint adjustment. Thus the thickness is ϵ1/(α+1) in this range.
For α=1, δ=ϵ​ makes all three terms comparable. Keep all of them:
Y′′+XY′+Y=0.
(12)
One decaying solution is e−X2/2, and reduction of order gives the other. This is a square-root-thickness layer with all terms retained.
For α>1, the first variation scale is δ=ϵ​. Keep diffusion and reaction, and drop drift at this scale:
Y′′+Y=0.
(13)
This leading motion is oscillatory, not a monotone decaying boundary-layer profile. To describe localization one must also resolve the slower damping envelope. Removing the first derivative in the original equation by
y=exp[−2ϵ(α+1)xα+1​]v
(14)
gives
v′′+[ϵ1​−2ϵαxα−1​−4ϵ2x2α​]v=0.
(15)
Near the endpoint the wavelength is O(ϵ​), while the envelope changes on x=O(ϵ1/(α+1)). Hence an oscillatory endpoint region is possible, but it has distinct wavelength and envelope scales; calling the diffusion-drift balance alone its leading equation would incorrectly discard the reaction term. This generalizes the oscillatory endpoint with a quadratically vanishing drift.
For α≤−1, the diffusion-drift balance would require δα+1=ϵ, which gives no shrinking positive power scale. At every shrinking scale the singular drift dominates diffusion, so no regular finite-amplitude diffusion boundary layer of the preceding kind occurs. The coefficient itself is singular at zero; singular endpoint solutions are a different issue from a bounded boundary-layer correction.
