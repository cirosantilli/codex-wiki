The only nondegenerate stationary point of a phase of the phase t2 is the endpoint zero. Its usual leading stationary phase method term is proportional to the amplitude there, but tan2(0)=0. Thus the nominal x−1/2 stationary contribution vanishes.
Expand one order further in the local amplitude: tan2t=t2+O(t4). The rescaling t=u/x​ then gives a first nonzero stationary contribution of order x−3/2, with coefficient −2π​/8 in the real integral, as in part (i). Meanwhile the upper endpoint is nonstationary and has nonzero amplitude; its integration by parts contribution is 2sin(π2x/16)/(πx), of order x−1.
Consequently the nonstationary endpoint dominates the first nonzero saddle contribution. A stationary phase method formula retaining only the usual leading saddle term would miss the answer. The full stationary phase method, with the vanishing-amplitude correction and the endpoint term, agrees with method of steepest descent. This is quadratic stationary endpoint with quadratically vanishing amplitude.

Near a prevertex of a Schwarz-Christoffel map Xj​, a conformal map to an interior angle (1−βj​)π has local form
f(Z)−f(Xj​)∼cj​(Z−Xj​)1−βj​.
(34)
Its derivative therefore has the factor (Z−Xj​)−βj​. On each real interval between prevertex, the argument of the product of these factors is constant, so its integral traces a straight polygon side. Passing a prevertex of a Schwarz-Christoffel map changes the argument by the prescribed exterior angle βj​π. Thus
f′(Z)=Cj=1∏n​(Z−Xj​)−βj​​
(35)
has exactly the required straight sides and corner angles, with the branches chosen in the upper half-plane. Equivalently f′′/f′ has simple poles −βj​/(Z−Xj​); removing them leaves the constant normalization determined by behavior at infinity. The additive integration constant translates the polygon and C rotates and scales it. This is the Schwarz-Christoffel formula.
The automorphisms of the upper half-plane are real Möbius transformations with positive determinant, modulo a common nonzero scalar. They have three real degrees of freedom, so three prevertex of a Schwarz-Christoffel map positions may be assigned independently. The remaining positions are constrained by the side-length ratios.
If Xn​=∞, omit its finite factor:
f′(Z)=Cj<n∏​(Z−Xj​)−βj​.​
(36)
Since ∑j​βj​=2, the derivative behaves like Zβn​−2 at infinity; in the coordinate U=1/Z, this is exactly the required corner exponent there. Thus the missing angle is encoded by behavior at infinity.
