Dropping the small highest-derivative term gives yo′​+xyo​=0. The outer solution satisfying the right endpoint condition is
yo​(x)=e(1−x2)/2.
(16)
It fails the left endpoint condition, so a boundary layer is needed at x=0. If X=x/ϵγ, diffusion and first derivative balance when ϵ1−2γ=ϵ−γ, giving γ=1.
With y(x)=Y(X), the exact inner expansion after multiplication by ϵ is
YXX​+YX​+ϵ2XY=0.
(17)
Its leading solution is Y=A+Be−X. The zero boundary value gives B=−A, and asymptotic matching condition as X→∞, x→0 fixes A=e1/2. Thus
Y(X)=e1/2(1−e−X).
(18)
The common overlap region value is e1/2. Add the outer and inner approximations and subtract that overlap region:
yc​(x)=e(1−x2)/2−e1/2−x/ϵ.​
(19)
The decaying inner exponential function can accommodate the left condition, whereas a right-end inner coordinate would give a mode that grows into the interval. There is no right boundary layer. The composite asymptotic expansion satisfies the left condition exactly and the right one up to the exponentially small term e1/2−1/ϵ; the remaining approximation error is algebraic and vanishes as ϵ↓0.
