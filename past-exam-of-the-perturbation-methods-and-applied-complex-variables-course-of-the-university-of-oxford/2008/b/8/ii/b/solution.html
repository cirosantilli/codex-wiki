Substituting their real-axis boundary values directly verifies the boxed integral transform relation for both signs of k, so the qualification can be checked without relying only on formal factorization.
There is also homogeneous corner freedom:
uh​=Cr−1/2sin(θ/2)
(88)
satisfies zero Dirichlet boundary condition data on the positive axis, zero Neumann boundary condition data on the negative axis and decay at infinity. Its negative trace has integral transform CΓ(1/2)(ik)−1/2, corresponding to the term A/k​. Higher singular modes r−(n+1/2)sin((n+1/2)θ) would also satisfy the homogeneous boundary conditions for integers n≥1, but are excluded if the negative trace is required to be locally integrable. Thus the one-constant formula belongs to a specified corner-growth class. The original boundary conditions alone, without that class, do not assert uniqueness. These facts form the finite-part normal trace for mixed quarter-power boundary data.
