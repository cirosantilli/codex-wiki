First state the normalization needed for the displayed logarithmic factorization: choose a continuous holomorphic function branch L=logF in the strip that tends to zero at both horizontal ends and has sufficient decay for its Cauchy integrals. In particular the index of the kernel along a horizontal line must be zero.

The source hypotheses require a qualification: nonvanishing in the strip and F→1 do not by themselves force index zero or a logarithm tending to zero at both ends. For example
F(z)=z+iz−i​
(72)
is analytic and nonzero for ∣Imz∣<1/2, tends to one at both ends, but winds once around zero along the real line. A logarithm continued along that line changes by 2πi, so it cannot have the stated normalization at both ends. One must first separate the index factor, or allow appropriately subtracted factors and a correspondingly different common-function growth class. The displayed constant conclusion follows under the canonical zero-index assumptions above; it is not an unconditional consequence of the two printed properties of F. This is the issue measured by the Index of a Wiener-Hopf kernel.
