Use the branch of 1−Z2​ that is positive for Z=iY, Y>0. The right-half-plane slit map
f(Z)=(1−Z2)−1/2​
(25)
is holomorphic in the upper half-plane, with derivative Z(1−Z2)−3/2, the required Schwarz-Christoffel formula form. Its derivative has no interior zero.
To verify the image and global injectivity, factor the construction. Squaring maps the upper half-plane bijectively onto C∖[0,∞). Thus 1−Z2 ranges over C∖(−∞,1]. The positive-real-part square root maps this onto the right half-plane with the interval (0,1] removed; reciprocation maps it onto the right half-plane slit along [1,∞). This also identifies the slit tip as a boundary point; the strict inequality in the printed slit description must be understood with that boundary convention.
Approaching the real axis from above gives
Zf(Z)​(−∞,−1)negative imaginary axis​(−1,0)lower slit bank​(0,1)upper slit bank​(1,∞)positive imaginary axis.​​
(26)
In particular Z=−1 maps to the lower infinite prime end A, Z=0 to the tip B=1, and Z=1 to the upper infinite prime end C. The real-axis point at infinity maps to zero on the vertical wall.
