The left side is analytic in the lower domain up to Imk<1: its apparent pole at zero is removable. The right side is analytic for Imk>0. By the supplied boundedness of kf​ and g​, both sides tend to zero at infinity in their half-planes. The gluing and Liouville theorem argument therefore makes their common value zero. Consequently
g​=−k​ic​,f​=kk−​i(c−k−​)​,u(k,0)=kk−i​ic​.

Choose the inversion contour Γ={s+iη:s∈R} oriented left to right, with any fixed 0<η<1. It passes above the pole at zero and below the branch point at i. Fourier inversion gives exactly
u(x,y)=2π1​∫Γ​kk−i​ie−iπ/4​e−k2−ik​ye−ikxdk.​

There is also a far-field qualification in the source. The formula is the intended bounded mixed-boundary solution, but it does not tend to zero along every path to infinity. This can be checked explicitly: in parabolic coordinates x=a2−b2, y=2ab, with a,b>0, the mixed-boundary convection-diffusion solution in parabolic coordinates is
u(x,y)=erfcb,b=2x2+y2​−x​​.
(41)
Indeed Δu=ubb​/[4(a2+b2)] and ux​=−bub​/[2(a2+b2)]; the identity ubb​=−2bub​ proves the differential equation. At b=0 the boundary value is one, and at a=0 its normal derivative is zero. Its traces are f(x)=erfc−x​ for x<0 and g(x)=−1/πx​ for x>0, whose one-sided transforms are the expressions derived above.
At a fixed angle away from the positive x axis, b→∞ and u→0. But at fixed y>0 and x→+∞, b∼y/(2x​) and u→1. Hence the radial decay statement cannot hold uniformly as printed; the intended decay is away from the downstream boundary layer. The exact expression also identifies its thickness as O(x​).
