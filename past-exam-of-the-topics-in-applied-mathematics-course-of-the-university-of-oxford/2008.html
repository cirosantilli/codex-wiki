For an autonomous scalar differential equation, linear stability of a simple equilibrium point Ne​ is determined by the derivative fN​(Ne​,a)=P′(Ne​): a negative derivative attracts nearby solutions and a positive derivative gives repulsion in a dynamical system of nearby solutions.

An unbiased random walk of bacteria leads, on the continuum scale, to diffusion with coefficient D, represented by DBXX​. The cell proliferation rate νGB is proportional both to the available biological growth factor concentration and to the bacterial density. Consumption of biological growth factor at rate μGB gives its negative reaction term.
There is no diffusion term for G because the biological growth factor is attached to the substrate rather than freely transported. There is also no chemotactic drift in this model: the specified motion is random, while the biological growth factor changes cell proliferation rather than directional movement. Both reaction terms vanish if either bacteria or biological growth factor are absent.

A fixed point x∗​ satisfies f(x∗​,μ)=x∗​. A perturbation obeys un+1​=mun​+O(un2​), where the periodic-orbit multiplier is m=fx​(x∗​,μ). It is linearly attracting for ∣m∣<1 and repelling for ∣m∣>1; at m=±1 nonlinear terms decide Lyapunov stability. A crossing through +1 can produce a saddle-node, transcritical or pitchfork bifurcation, depending on the mathematical model parameter dependence and symmetry. A crossing through −1 generically gives a period-doubling bifurcation. A real one-dimensional function cannot have the complex conjugate pair needed for a Neimark–Sacker bifurcation.
For a genuine period-two orbit, study the return map f∘f at either point. By the chain rule its periodic-orbit multiplier is
m2​=fx​(x1​,μ)fx​(x2​,μ).​
(50)
Its modulus determines linear stability by the same criterion, now per two iterates.
For the real quadratic iteration map, the fixed-point equation gives
x±​=2−1±1+4μ​​,μ≥−41​.
(51)
The derivative is −2x. The lower branch x−​ is repelling whenever μ>−1/4. The upper branch satisfies ∣−2x+​∣<1 exactly for
−41​<μ<43​.​
(52)
At μ=−1/4 the two fixed points merge in a saddle-node bifurcation. With u=x+1/2 at that mathematical model parameter, u′=u−u2, so the point attracts from the right and is repelling from the left. At μ=3/4, x+​=1/2 has periodic-orbit multiplier −1 and undergoes a period-doubling bifurcation. At the threshold itself u′=−u−u2 and the two-step return is u′′=u−2u3−u4, proving weak asymptotic stability there despite the inconclusive linear test.
Factor the second-iterate equation:
f(f(x),μ)−x=−(x2+x−μ)(x2−x+1−μ).
(53)
Removing the fixed-point factor yields the periodic orbit points
x1​=21−4μ−3​​,x2​=21+4μ−3​​.
(54)
They are distinct and exchanged by f exactly when μ>3/4; at equality they coincide with the fixed point. Their product is 1−μ, hence their periodic-orbit multiplier is 4(1−μ). Thus
the two-cycle exists for μ>43​,and is linearly attracting for 43​<μ<45​.​
(55)
It is repelling for μ>5/4 and reaches its next period-doubling bifurcation threshold at μ=5/4. At that exact endpoint it is weakly attracting: if f2 is expanded about either periodic orbit point as v′=−v+Av2+Bv3+O(v4), then A=2μ−6xi2​, B=−4xi​, and A2+B=20±102​>0. Its second return is v′′=v−2(A2+B)v3+O(v4).
At μ=2 put h(y)=4sin2(πy)−2=−2cos(2πy). The double-angle identity gives
f(h(y),2)=2−4cos2(2πy)=−2cos(4πy)=h(2ymod1).
(56)
This verifies the printed relation to the Bernoulli shift. Precisely, it is a semiconjugacy: h(y)=h(1−y), so it is not invertible on the circle. To obtain a genuine interval topological conjugacy, use the homeomorphism H(u)=−2cos(πu) from [0,1] onto [−2,2], and the full tent map
T(u)={2u,2−2u,​0≤u≤1/2,1/2≤u≤1.​
(57)
The same identity proves f(H(u),2)=H(T(u)).
The full tent map has dense periodic points, is topologically mixing and has sensitive dependence on initial conditions. To see these properties directly, its nth iterate has 2n monotone branches on dyadic intervals of length 2−n, each mapping onto [0,1]. Every nonempty open interval contains such a branch domain, and a later iterate covers all of [0,1], proving topological mixing. On any branch, the function graph of the iterate has a fixed point by the intermediate value theorem, so periodic points occur in every open interval. Given any initial point and any neighborhood, some iterate of that neighborhood covers the interval; one can choose a nearby point whose image differs from the initial point's image by at least a fixed positive distance. This proves sensitive dependence on initial conditions. Uniform continuity of the inverse homeomorphism transfers these properties to f on [−2,2].
Consequently the real quadratic iteration map is chaotic on its invariant interval in the Devaney sense: topological transitivity, dense periodic points and sensitive dependence on initial conditions, with all these orbits bounded. There are also aperiodic orbits: periodic points form a countable union of finite sets of roots of an equation of the equations fn(x)=x, whereas the interval is uncountable. The semiconjugacy alone would not justify treating h as invertible; the tent-map argument supplies the missing rigorous transfer.
