For the Ricker population map H(u)=rue−u, fixed points satisfy u=0 or re−u=1. Thus zero exists for all r>0, while u∗​=logr is positive exactly when r>1. Since H′(u)=re−u(1−u), the respective periodic-orbit multipliers are r and 1−logr.
Consequently zero is linearly attracting for 0<r<1 and repelling for r>1; the positive biological population is linearly attracting for 1<r<e2 and repelling for r>e2. The periodic-orbit multiplier of the positive point is positive below r=e and negative above it, distinguishing monotone from alternating local convergence. At r=1, the coincident zero state has unit periodic-orbit multiplier but 0<H(u)<u for every u>0, giving nonlinear attraction from the physical right side. At r=e2 the periodic-orbit multiplier is −1 and linearization is inconclusive. Writing u=2+η, the second iterate has expansion η↦η−η3/3+O(η4), so the critical point is weakly attracting; beyond it a period-doubling instability occurs.

IT​=rIS−aI,ST​=−(r+p)IS,VT​=pIS,RT​=aI.​

An equilibrium is asymptotically stable when it is Lyapunov stable and attracts every initial state in some neighbourhood. Lyapunov stability means that sufficiently small initial displacement stays small for all future times; attraction additionally requires convergence to the equilibrium. A Lyapunov function is a differentiable positive-definite scalar function near the equilibrium whose derivative along solutions is nonpositive. Strict negativity, or an appropriate invariant-set argument for a non-strict derivative, gives asymptotic stability.
For ν=−1, equilibria satisfy y=0 and x+x3=0, so only (0,0) is real. Its Jacobian matrix is (0−1​10​), with eigenvalues ±i for every κ. It is a linear centre equilibrium, so linearization alone cannot decide nonlinear attraction.
Use the positive proper energy
E=21​y2+21​x2+41​x4,E˙=κx2y2.​
(31)
For κ<0, energy sublevel sets are compact and invariant. The zero-derivative set is the union of the axes, but its only invariant subset is the origin: on x=0,y=0 one immediately leaves that axis, and on y=0,x=0 one has y˙​=−x−x3=0. LaSalle's invariance principle therefore proves global asymptotic stability. At κ=0 the conserved energy levels are closed periodic orbits, giving a nonlinear centre equilibrium. For κ>0, time reversal together with y↦−y converts the system to the attracting negative-κ case, so the origin is a nonlinear repeller and is unstable. Nonzero forward trajectories cannot remain bounded; this does not assert existence for all time if finite-time escape occurs.

A fixed point z∗​ of a map F:R2→R2 satisfies F(z∗​)=z∗​. Its linear perturbations evolve by the Jacobian matrix DF(z∗​): both eigenvalues must lie strictly inside the unit disk for linear asymptotic stability. One inside and one outside gives a saddle; both outside give a repeller. Unit-modulus eigenvalues require nonlinear analysis, unlike the continuous-time test using signs of real parts.
Here a fixed point has x=y=p and 2p=a(p−p3/3). Therefore
p=0 for all a>0,p=±3−6/a​ for a>2.​
(37)
At zero the characteristic equation is λ2+λ−a=0. Its negative root is less than −1 for every a>0, while its positive root crosses +1 at a=2. Thus zero is a saddle for 0<a<2 and a real repeller for a>2, with a pitchfork of fixed-point branches at a=2.
At either nonzero point the characteristic equation is λ2+λ+2a−6=0, whose discriminant is 25−8a. For 2<a<3 the fixed points are saddles. At a=3 their periodic-orbit multipliers are 0,−1, giving a flip threshold. For 3<a<25/8 they are attracting real nodes; at 25/8 repeated-eigenvalue degenerate nodes; and for 25/8<a<7/2 attracting foci. The Jury stability criterion conditions reduce to a>3 and a<7/2, confirming the full stable interval.
At a=7/2 the periodic-orbit multipliers are e±2πi/3. For larger a they form a repelling focus equilibrium. This is a unit-circle crossing at a strong resonance of a planar map with ratio 1:3; linearization alone does not justify asserting a generic nonresonant Neimark-Sacker bifurcation. The period-two branch at the flip threshold is derived next, including an additional family absent from the source's proposed quadratic.

For an equilibrium at zero, the local stable manifold consists of nearby points whose forward trajectories remain nearby and tend to zero; the local unstable manifold is defined by backward trajectories with the analogous property. For a smooth system z˙=Az+f(z) with f(0)=0, Df(0)=0 and no zero-real-part eigenvalues, the stable-manifold theorem gives manifolds tangent to the negative- and positive-real-part eigenspaces. Their dimensions are the corresponding eigenvalue counts. Real distinct eigenvalues alone are insufficient if one is zero; then a centre equilibrium direction requires additional analysis. In the saddle case each manifold is a curve, found by imposing invariance on a power-series graph through the relevant eigenvector.
In this system, the x nullcline is y=x2+2x and the y nullclines are x=0 and y=x−3. Substituting the first into the second gives x(x2+x+3)=0. Since the quadratic has discriminant −11, the origin is the only real equilibrium. Its Jacobian matrix (−23​10​) has eigenvalues −3,1, with eigenvectors (1,−1) and (1,3). It is therefore a hyperbolic saddle.
Set y=h(x) for either manifold. Invariance requires
h′(x)[−2x+h(x)−x2]=3x+xh(x)−x2.
(42)
For h=sx+bx2+cx3+O(x4), matching the linear coefficient gives s=−1 or s=3. The quadratic and cubic coefficients respectively satisfy
(3s−4)b=2s−1,(4s−6)c=b−2b(b−1).
(43)
For the stable slope s=−1, this yields b=3/7, c=−9/98; for the unstable slope s=3, it gives b=1, c=1/6. Thus
Ws: y=−x+73​x2−989​x3+O(x4),Wu: y=3x+x2+61​x3+O(x4).​

Substituting back into x˙ gives the reduced dynamics
x˙∣Ws​=−3x−74​x2−989​x3+O(x4),x˙∣Wu​=x+61​x3+O(x4).​

The signs of the linear terms confirm attraction and repulsion along the respective curves.
In the phase portrait, x˙ is positive above its parabolic nullcline and negative below it; y˙​ has sign x(3+y−x). The stable branches approach the saddle and the unstable branches leave it in opposite directions. There can be no periodic orbit, since such an orbit would require total enclosed equilibrium index +1, while the only equilibrium has index −1. There is no homoclinic excursion either: eliminating y gives x¨+(2+x)x˙=x(x2+x+3), so a positive turning point is a strict local minimum and a negative one a strict local maximum. A loop returning to zero would require the opposite extremum somewhere. Hence bounded forward trajectories lie on the stable manifold and approach zero; other trajectories eventually leave compact regions, without a claimed universal rate or infinite-time existence at infinity.
