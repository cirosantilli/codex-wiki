Period-doubling cascade universality concerns the continuation of this period-doubling cascade in smooth unimodal maps with a quadratic maximum. After suitable rescalings their repeated two-step maps approach the same limiting shape; successive parameter spacings have the Feigenbaum ratio approximately 4.6692 and the spatial rescaling has magnitude approximately 2.5029. These are asymptotic statements involving later doublings, not numbers that can be established from the first two thresholds alone. The accumulation near μ≃3.56995 is outside the first interval considered here. One should not claim chaos throughout the stable two-cycle interval.
Four useful dynamical properties to distinguish are sensitivity to initial conditions, topological transitivity or mixing, dense periodic points, and aperiodic orbits with persistent complex evolution. They concern the invariant set, not an assertion that every orbit must be aperiodic. A positive Lyapunov exponent for typical initial data is an additional quantitative diagnostic.

Every branch doubles interval length until it crosses a fold; the finite binary cylinders have length 2−n and map onto the whole interval after n steps. Thus every open interval eventually has an iterate covering the full interval: the map is topologically mixing and sensitive. Repeating a finite left/right branch word gives a periodic itinerary; arbitrarily long prescribed prefixes locate such periodic points in arbitrarily small cylinders, proving density. Nonperiodic binary itineraries yield uncountably many aperiodic orbits. Conjugacy transfers these properties to F4​, proving chaotic behaviour while retaining its dense special periodic orbits.
As a metric check, its invariant measure has density 1/[πx(1−x)​] and its typical Lyapunov exponent is log2>0. This is a typical-orbit assertion, not a claim about the derivative along every exceptional critical or preperiodic orbit. The explicit trigonometric solution and tent-map conjugacy explain why deterministic iteration of a map can nevertheless amplify uncertainty.
