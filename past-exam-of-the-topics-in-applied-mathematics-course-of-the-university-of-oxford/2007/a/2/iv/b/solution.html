After an initial layer of duration O(ϵ) in slow time, v≃u/(u+K) while u changes only slightly. The reduced substrate equation is
u′≃ηu−u+Kλu​=u+Kηu(u−ue​)​.​
(8)
Below ue​ it decreases to zero; above ue​ it increases without bound. The exact threshold for initial v=0 is the intersection of the saddle equilibrium's stable manifold with that axis. For small ϵ it is
u∗​≃ue​=k0​s0​k2​e0​​−k1​s0​k−1​+k2​​.​
(9)
In dimensional initial-substrate units the leading threshold is s0​u∗​≃k2​e0​/k0​−(k−1​+k2​)/k1​. Comparing the actual initial value u(0)=1 to this threshold determines its fate; the reference scale can be held fixed when varying the initial concentration.
The initial sequestration produces an O(ϵ) correction if desired. Write the stable manifold as u=ue​+ϵϕ(v)+O(ϵ2). The invariance equation at leading order gives ϕ′(v)=F(ue​,v)/G(ue​,v)=−(1−η) and ϕ(ve​)=0. Hence
u∗​=ue​+ϵ(1−η)ve​+O(ϵ2),​
(10)
for fixed parameters away from degenerate limits. The positive correction reflects the substrate consumed in building complexes from the initially enzyme-free-complex state.
The physical strip u≥0, 0≤v≤1 is invariant, and the off-diagonal derivatives are nonnegative there, so ordered initial substrate values remain ordered. There are only the attracting origin and the saddle equilibrium. With Dulac function 1/u, the divergence of the weighted vector field is −(K−λ)v/u2−(1+K/u)/ϵ<0, excluding periodic orbits in its interior. The saddle equilibrium separatrix therefore gives the unique threshold on the initial axis: below it solutions tend to extinction, at it they tend to the saddle equilibrium, and above it they escape to infinity. Growth is at most exponential, so this is u→∞ as time tends to infinity, not a finite-time blowup. This is autocatalytic enzyme clearance with a saddle threshold.
