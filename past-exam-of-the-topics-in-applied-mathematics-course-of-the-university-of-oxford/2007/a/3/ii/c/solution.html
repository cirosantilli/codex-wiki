The top edge points down by (a), the sloping edge points upward in the inward normal sense by (b), and on a=η the velocity a′=b≤0 points left. At a=0 the region contains only the equilibrium (0,0). Thus the closed triangle is forward invariant.
The given unstable saddle equilibrium branch enters its interior from (η,0) and stays in this compact region. Along it a′=b≤0, so a decreases and has a limit. An interior limiting orbit with b=0 would keep changing a, while an interior point with b=0, 0<a<η has b′<0 and is not an equilibrium. Hence the only possible forward limit is (0,0). Ordinary-differential-equation uniqueness prevents reaching and leaving an equilibrium in finite profile coordinate. The whole orbit therefore joins (η,0) at y=−∞ to (0,0) at y=+∞ with a≥0 and a′=b≤0.
Consequently a nonnegative monotone front exists for every c>2η​. This proves the requested strict-speed result without claiming an unproved conclusion at the repeated-root threshold. It is a trapping triangle for a saturating monostable invasion front.
