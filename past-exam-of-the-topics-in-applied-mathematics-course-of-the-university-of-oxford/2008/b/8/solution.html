For 0<a<2c, the determinant of the planar block is a(a−2c)<0, so the origin has one positive and two negative eigenvalues and is a saddle equilibrium. At a=c, the planar eigenvalues are explicitly ±c. For a>2c, the planar trace c−a is negative and its determinant positive, so the origin is asymptotically stable. To distinguish its type put

For a>a∗​ they are nonreal with negative real part, giving a stable spiral in the planar block together with the stable third direction. This refines the requested node-versus-saddle classification.
The origin cannot have a Hopf bifurcation: a planar imaginary pair would require trace zero, namely a=c, but the determinant there is −c2<0, so the pair is real and of opposite signs.

At a transverse eigenvalue crossing, a generic nonzero first Lyapunov coefficient gives a Hopf bifurcation. The displayed radical is the spectral threshold; it does not justify an unconditional generic-Hopf claim for all positive mathematical model parameters, nor does it by itself exclude a degenerate Hopf bifurcation point. For example b>17c/8 yields no real candidate at all. The cubic Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion gives local Lyapunov stability on the side with A>0 and AB−C>0, where

Thus the origin is always a saddle equilibrium for nonzero a,c in that mathematical model parameter range. The nonzero equilibrium points now satisfy y=x, z=2c and x2=2bc, so they are
