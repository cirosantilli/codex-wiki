The four vertices of the unit square map to (0,0),(2,1),(3,2),(1,1), the vertices of an area-one parallelogram. To reduce it modulo one, cut it by the integer grid. For each piece in the cell [j,j+1]×[k,k+1], translate by (−j,−k). Up to boundary identifications these translated pieces partition the unit square, because the inverse integer matrix gives exactly one torus preimage to every point. Finally, if v is fixed by Fp, then (Ap−I)v=m for an integer vector m. The eigenvalues of Ap−I are λ+p​−1 and λ−p​−1, neither zero.
