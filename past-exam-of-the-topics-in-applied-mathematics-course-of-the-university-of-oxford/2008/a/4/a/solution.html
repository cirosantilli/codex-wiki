The term α∇2q is cell diffusion arising from random movement. Chemotaxis up the concentration gradient gives the cell flux βq∇c, whose negative divergence is the transport contribution −β∇⋅(q∇c). The reaction term q(1−q) gives growth governed by the logistic differential equation with dimensionless carrying capacity one.
The chemoattractant itself diffuses, is produced by cells at rate κηq, and decays at rate κc. The homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions suppress the normal diffusive flux of each quantity. They also suppress the normal chemotactic cell flux because the normal derivative of c vanishes. Thus the rectangular boundary is impermeable, and the spatially integrated cell count changes through cell proliferation rather than transport across that boundary.
