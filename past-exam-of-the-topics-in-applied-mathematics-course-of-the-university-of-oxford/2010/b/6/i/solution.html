The Lorenz vector field has constant divergence ∇⋅F=−σ−1−b. Liouville formula for a fundamental matrix for the derivative of the flow therefore gives
detDΦt​=e−(σ+1+b)t,V(t)=V(0)e−(σ+1+b)t.​
(34)
This concerns transported three-dimensional phase volume, not the Euclidean distance between each pair of trajectories.
A quasiperiodic orbit with the usual smooth two-frequency torus closure would lie on an invariant embedded two-torus. That torus bounds a solid torus of positive finite volume in R3. Since the vector field is tangent to its boundary, its outward flux is zero, contradicting the divergence theorem, whose volume integral is −(σ+1+b)V<0. This negative-divergence obstruction to invariant tori excludes such quasiperiodic tori. The argument uses global negative divergence and the enclosed volume; saying only that an attracting torus has zero volume would not exclude it.
At an equilibrium the sum of eigenvalues is −(σ+1+b)<0, so they cannot all have positive real parts: no equilibrium is a fully repelling source. For a periodic orbit of period T, one Floquet multiplier equals one in the flow direction. The product of the other two is e−(σ+1+b)T<1, so they cannot both have modulus greater than one: no periodic orbit is fully repelling either. Saddle equilibria and saddle periodic orbits remain possible; contraction of volume does not imply contraction in every direction.
