The first equilibrium equation gives y=x, and the third gives z=x2/b. The second then becomes x(r−1−x2/b)=0. Hence the equilibria are
O=(0,0,0),C±​=(±b(r−1)​,±b(r−1)​,r−1)(r>1).​
(35)
At the origin, one eigenvalue is −b and the remaining two solve λ2+(σ+1)λ+σ(1−r)=0. Their discriminant is (σ−1)2+4σr>0. For r<1 both are negative, so the origin is linearly stable, with all eigenvalues real and negative. For r>1 one is positive and one negative, so it is a saddle equilibrium with one unstable and two stable directions. At r=1 its eigenvalues are 0,−(σ+1),−b: linearization has a neutral direction and cannot alone prove nonlinear stability. Given the source's global attraction for r<1, there are no nontrivial limit cycles or persistent chaotic invariant dynamics in that regime; all initial states tend to the origin.
To establish the local pitchfork, one needs the simple eigenvalue to cross zero, the two symmetry-related branches to exist and acquire the expected local stability, and nondegeneracy of the nonlinear coefficient. The derivative of the crossing eigenvalue is σ/(σ+1)>0. At C±​, the characteristic polynomial is
λ3+(σ+b+1)λ2+b(σ+r)λ+2σb(r−1)=0.​
(36)
For r>1 sufficiently close to one, all coefficients are positive and (σ+b+1)b(σ+r)>2σb(r−1), so the cubic Routh-Hurwitz criterion makes both new equilibria stable. Together with the symmetry (x,y,z)↦(−x,−y,z) and the square-root branches, this yields the supercritical pitchfork. A local centre-manifold coordinate proportional to x has leading normal form q˙​=σ+1σ​(r−1)q−b(σ+1)σ​q3+⋯, explicitly displaying the nonzero stabilizing cubic coefficient. At r=1 that cubic also gives weak local nonlinear attraction along the centre direction.
