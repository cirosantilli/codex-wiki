First assume ω0​=0 and regard θ modulo 2π. The section θ=0 has return time T=2π/∣ω0​∣. Solving the resonantly forced oscillator from this section gives
x(t)=x0​cos(ω0​t)+ω0​y0​​sin(ω0​t)+2ω0​γ​tsin(ω0​t).
(26)
At t=T, the sine vanishes and the cosine is one, while differentiating the secular term adds γT/2 to y. Hence the Poincaré map is
P(x,y)=(x,y+γT/2).​
(27)
For γ>0, every iterate has yn​=y0​+nγT/2→∞. Thus every oscillator trajectory is unbounded, regardless of its initial amplitude. For an arbitrary section phase θ0​, the return map translates by (γTsinθ0​/(2ω0​),γTcosθ0​/2), which is still nonzero. At γ=0 the map is the identity, and all (x,y) motions are bounded harmonic oscillations, including the zero-amplitude equilibrium.
The zero-frequency case allowed by the printed parameter range is different. If ω0​=0, then θ=θ0​ is constant and x=x0​+y0​t+21​γcosθ0​t2. There is no phase-crossing Poincaré section. For γ>0, choosing cosθ0​=0 and y0​=0 gives bounded equilibria, contradicting an unqualified “all trajectories” statement. For γ=0 at zero frequency, free-particle motion is unbounded unless y0​=0. The resonance conclusion therefore requires the natural nonzero-frequency assumption.
