At Ω=−2, the origin has stable and unstable eigenvalues 1−2​ and 1+2​; the two nonzero equilibria are repelling foci with eigenvalues (1±i7​)/2. A compatible portrait has one attracting closed orbit surrounding all three equilibria. Its total enclosed index is +1+1−1=+1, as a periodic orbit requires. Its half-turn image is also attracting, so uniqueness of the attracting cycle makes that cycle half-turn symmetric. Orbits spiral away from either focus equilibrium and approach the surrounding cycle; the stable separatrices approach the saddle instead. The figure integrates representative trajectories and shows the local saddle directions.
These trajectories cannot escape to infinity. A Liénard boundedness function supplies one explicit boundedness argument using the Liénard coordinate z=y+F(x) with F(x)=x3/3−2x and g(x)=x3−x. Then x˙=z−F(x) and z˙=−g(x). Put V=x4/4−x2/2 and W=V+z2/2−xz. This function is coercive after addition of a constant. Its derivative obeys

In the displayed portrait, generic non-equilibrium initial states tend to the cycle; points on the saddle's stable manifold tend to the origin; and initial data exactly at a repelling focus equilibrium remain there. The Poincaré-Bendixson theorem excludes a chaotic compact limit set for this smooth planar flow. Strictly, the assumption gives uniqueness only of an attracting periodic orbit: it does not by itself exclude exceptional nonattracting periodic or separatrix limit sets. Those would need additional global information if one wanted a classification stronger than the compatible portrait requested.
