For this concave recruitment map, the extinction state is invariant because g(0)=0. Near extinction, Nt+1​=g′(0)Nt​+O(Nt2​) with multiplier above one, so small positive populations initially grow. The strict concavity g′′<0 means marginal recruitment decreases as population increases. More precisely, for N>0,
dNd​Ng(N)​=N2Ng′(N)−g(N)​<0,
(1)
since g(N)=∫0N​g′(s)ds>Ng′(N). Thus per-capita recruitment decreases and the graph bends downwards from an initial slope greater than that of the identity line.
A positive fixed point, if present, is the unique positive intersection with g(N)=N: the strictly decreasing ratio can cross one at most once. The sketch shows the usual saturating example g(N)=3N/(1+N), which crosses at N=2.
The three assumptions alone do not guarantee this crossing. For example g(N)=2N+1−e−N satisfies all three and remains strictly above N for every N>0. An extra large-population restriction, such as g(N)<N somewhere, supplies existence. Keeping this distinction avoids inventing a carrying capacity that is not implied by the printed hypotheses.
