Positive equilibria are intersections of P(N) with the horizontal line E. A lower stable branch in (0,a1​) exists exactly for 0<E<ET​. A pair in (a2​,a3​) exists exactly for 0<E<EM​: its smaller member has P′>0 and is unstable; its larger member has P′<0 and is stable. At a positive root the linear eigenvalue is NP′(N), which proves these claims. Extinction has eigenvalue ET​−E, becoming stable for E>ET​.
The harvested bistable population thresholds determine the behaviour. If EM​<ET​, the upper stable population and its unstable separator first disappear in a saddle-node bifurcation. Populations then tend to the remaining lower stable state until that branch reaches extinction at ET​; beyond it extinction attracts every positive initial state. If ET​<EM​, the lower stable branch reaches extinction first. For ET​<E<EM​, extinction and the upper positive state are both stable, separated by the middle unstable root. Increasing effort through EM​ then destroys the remaining positive attractor, causing a sudden collapse to extinction. If ET​=EM​, both events occur at the same effort at different populations.
At E=ET​, near zero the leading term is P′(0)N2<0, so extinction attracts from the physical right side algebraically, despite its zero linear eigenvalue. At E=EM​, the double positive root is semistable: locally N˙≃−21​NM​∣P′′(NM​)∣(N−NM​)2, attracting from above and repelling from below. These critical cases matter for exact initial data.
