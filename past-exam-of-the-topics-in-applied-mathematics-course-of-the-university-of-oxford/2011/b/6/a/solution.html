For uniform nearest-neighbor bonds of strength K0​ on a ring with N≥3, retain the normalization of the given model:
θ˙i​=ωi​+NK0​​[sin(θi−1​−θi​)+sin(θi+1​−θi​)],​
(45)
with indices interpreted modulo N. On an open chain the interior equations are the same, while
θ˙1​=ω1​+NK0​​sin(θ2​−θ1​),θ˙N​=ωN​+NK0​​sin(θN−1​−θN​).
(46)
For nonuniform bonds, replace each K0​ by the corresponding Kij​. A convention without the factor 1/N is obtained by rescaling the bond strengths; it is not silently substituted here.
For all-to-all coupling define the Kuramoto order parameter
reiψ=N1​j=1∑N​eiθj​.​
(47)
It is the centroid of unit phase vectors: r measures phase coherence and ψ the mean phase. Multiplying by e−iθi​ and taking imaginary parts gives
θ˙i​=ωi​+Krsin(ψ−θi​).​
(48)
Thus only the two components of the Kuramoto order parameter mediate interaction.
For the symmetric stationary continuum branch, choose the rotating frame with ψ=0. Oscillators with ∣ω∣≤Kr lock at θ=arcsin(ω/(Kr)), choosing cosθ>0 for stability. Those with ∣ω∣>Kr drift, with stationary phase density proportional to ∣ω−Krsinθ∣−1. Their real contribution to the order parameter vanishes, since the integral of cosθ/(ω−Krsinθ) over a period is the change of a periodic logarithm. Their imaginary contributions cancel between ω and −ω because g is symmetric.
The locked oscillators therefore give the real self-consistency relation
r=∫−KrKr​1−K2r2ω2​​g(ω)dω.
(49)
Set ω=Krsinθ and divide by r>0 to obtain
1=K∫−π/2π/2​cos2θg(Krsinθ)dθ.​
(50)
This is the Kuramoto self-consistency equation for partial synchronization. The incoherent state r=0 must be considered before this division. If g is continuous at zero with 0<g(0)<∞, the branch meets incoherence at Kc​=2/[πg(0)].
