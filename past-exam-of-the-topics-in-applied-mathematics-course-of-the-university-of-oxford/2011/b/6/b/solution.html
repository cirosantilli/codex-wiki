Put x=z, y=z˙. The system is
x˙=y,y˙​=x3−x−Ay−y3.
(51)
Its equilibria are (0,0),(1,0),(−1,0)​. At (±1,0), the eigenvalues are (−A±A2+8​)/2, of opposite signs, so these are saddles for every A.
At the origin, the eigenvalues are (−A±A2−4​)/2. It is a stable focus for 0<A<2, a stable node for A>2, an unstable focus for −2<A<0, and an unstable node for A<−2. At A=±2 it is the corresponding repeated-eigenvalue improper node. At A=0, linearization gives a center, but nonlinear damping matters: with
E=2y2​+2x2​−4x4​,E˙=−Ay2−y4,​
(52)
small closed energy sublevels are invariant and E˙=−y4 forces convergence to the origin. Thus it is a weak attracting focus, not a nonlinear center.
Local bifurcation. Write a=−A. Averaging the radial equation near the origin, or eliminating nonresonant cubic terms, gives
R˙=2a​R−83​R3+O(aR3+R5).
(53)
The x3 restoring term has zero average radial contribution, while ⟨y4⟩=3R4/8. Thus A=0 is a supercritical Hopf bifurcation as a increases: a stable cycle appears for small a>0, with leading amplitude R≃4a/3​.
Global changes. Two further bifurcations must be retained. Define ah​ as the parameter where the upper unstable branch from (−1,0) reaches (1,0). Central symmetry supplies the lower connection in the opposite direction, forming a heteroclinic cycle. Define af​>ah​ as the subsequent collision of an inner attracting and an outer repelling periodic orbit. The complete phase portraits for ∣A∣<1 are:
  • 0<A<1: an attracting origin between the two saddles; no periodic orbit, since E˙<0 off the zero-velocity axis.
  • 0<a<ah​: a repelling origin surrounded by one attracting cycle. Appropriate inward saddle branches approach that cycle; trajectories outside its basin escape the central potential well.
  • a=ah​: the attracting cycle persists inside a repelling two-saddle heteroclinic loop.
  • ah​<a<af​: an inner attracting cycle and an outer repelling cycle coexist. The outer cycle separates the bounded oscillations from escape.
  • a=af​: the two cycles merge into a semistable cycle, attracting from its inside and repelling from its outside.
  • af​<a<1: no periodic orbit remains; generic nonstationary trajectories escape, with the saddle stable manifolds as exceptional trajectories.
Here escape means leaving the central well and becoming unbounded; it is not an additional finite equilibrium. The softening Rayleigh-Duffing phase portrait in the figure includes the two critical cases.
The return-map calculation underlying this classification is useful because the two global critical parameters are very close. On the section x=0,y>0, let Ha​(p) be minus the velocity at the next crossing of x=0, so a symmetric cycle is a root of Ha​(p)−p. Any periodic orbit must have its extrema inside −1<x<1, since at a positive maximum y=0 requires x3−x<0. Its index is one, so it surrounds the origin and neither saddle. Every such cycle is centrally symmetric: its reflected image cannot be a distinct nonintersecting cycle of equal enclosed area. Thus this half-return test detects all central cycles. A fixed point with Ha′​<1 is attracting and one with Ha′​>1 repelling; the full-return multiplier is (Ha′​)2.
For the saddle connection, let pL​(a) be the upper velocity at x=0 from the left saddle's unstable branch and pR​(a) the upper velocity at x=0 on the right saddle's stable branch. While y>0, their shooting equation is
dxdy​=yx3−x​+a−y2.
(54)
Comparison shows pL​ increases with a and pR​ decreases. At a=0, energy dissipation gives pL​<1/2​<pR​. At a=1, the lower comparison curve y=(x+1)/2 on [−1,0] gives pL​>1/2, while the upper comparison curve y=(1−x2)/2 on [0,1] gives pR​<1/2. The latter follows from
[yx3−x​+1−y2−y′]y=(1−x2)/2​=4(1−x)(x3+x2−x+3)​>0,
(55)
and the stable-branch expansion y=(1−x)−3(1−x)2/4+⋯ near x=1. Hence there is a unique ah​∈(0,1) with pL​=pR​.
At that connection the saddle eigenvalues are λu​=(ah​+ah2​+8​)/2 and λs​=(ah​−ah2​+8​)/2, so ∣λs​∣/λu​<1. The loop is therefore repelling. In particular, the attracting Hopf cycle cannot simply disappear in that connection. Near the upper endpoint pR​, the half-return map has
Ha​(p)=pL​(a)−C[pR​(a)−p]ν+⋯,0<ν=∣λs​∣/λu​<1.
(56)
For a just above ah​, this produces a second, repelling fixed point near the endpoint, in addition to the attracting inner one. Continuing the two roots of Ha​(p)−p makes their collision criterion explicit:
Haf​​(pf​)=pf​,Haf​′​(pf​)=1.​
(57)
This is a saddle-node bifurcation of periodic orbits, not a disappearance of the equilibrium points.
For a reproducible numerical check of the global sketches, event-based integration of this half-return map and its variational equation gives
ah​≃0.33144619,af​≃0.33147066,pf​≃0.68733571.
(58)
These values are computed by shooting the stated equations; their precision is unnecessary for the requested sketches. They explain why a coarse parameter sweep can miss the two-cycle interval entirely. The three bifurcation types are therefore Hopf at A=0, a two-saddle heteroclinic connection at A=−ah​, and a fold of cycles at A=−af​. There is no pitchfork of equilibria because their locations remain fixed for every A.
