The positive quadrant is invariant. The non-axis nullclines are u+v=1 and u=1/β. Below u+v=1, u increases; above it, u decreases. To the left of u=1/β, v increases; to the right, it decreases.
For β>1, the stable manifold of the interior saddle separates trajectories tending to (1,0) from those for which u→0 and v→∞. Exactly on that manifold, the trajectory tends to the saddle. For the illustrated r=1,β=2, the separating manifold is exactly v=u, since the vector field preserves that diagonal.
For β<1, every strictly positive initial state ultimately has u→0 and v→∞. Indeed u˙≤ru(1−u) implies limsupu≤1, so eventually 1−βu is bounded below by a positive constant. Then v grows exponentially, forcing u down. At β=1, a positive v eventually drives u below one; thereafter v increases. A finite positive limit for v would force u→1, inconsistent with u˙→−rv<0, so the same exclusion outcome holds.
There are no interior periodic orbits: the Bendixson-Dulac criterion with multiplier 1/(uv) gives
