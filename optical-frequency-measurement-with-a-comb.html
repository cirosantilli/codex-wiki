Optical frequency measurement with a comb (fL​=nfr​+f0​+b)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Optical frequency comb
OurBigBook.com Words: 431 Articles: 1
Mix an unknown single-frequency laser with a nearby comb tooth on a photodiode. Define the signed beat b=fL​−fn​, so
fL​=nfr​+f0​+b.
(18)
The beat arises from the cross term in light intensity:
​Ae2πifL​t+Be2πifn​t​2=∣A∣2+∣B∣2+2Re(AB∗e2πibt).
(19)
An electronic detector can follow this slow difference even though it cannot resolve either optical oscillation. The square-law expression also shows why a real-valued intensity trace by itself loses the sign of b. A small known upward shift of the chosen tooth decreases ∣b∣ when the unknown lies above it and increases ∣b∣ when it lies below, provided the tooth assignment remains unchanged.
For an original example, stipulate
fr​=250MHz,f0​=20MHz,b=+30MHz,n=2,000,000.
(20)
Then fn​=500,000.020GHz and fL​=500,000.050GHz. Choosing the opposite sign would give 499,999.990GHz instead. The stipulated optical frequency is countable because the large optical value is expressed using the integer n and three electronic frequencies; the diagram's two controls establish the comb, and this additional beat places the unknown against it.
Both comb readouts use one calibrated microwave reference. Feedback fixes fr​ and f0​, determining each tooth once n is identified. A frequency counter measures the slow beat, transferring the calibration to the optical frequency.[ref]
