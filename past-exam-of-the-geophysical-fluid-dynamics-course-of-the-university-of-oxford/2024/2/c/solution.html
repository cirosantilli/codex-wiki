Let Vb​ be the northward speed in the western boundary current, whose zonal width δ is much less than its meridional scale. Its dominant relative vorticity is ξ≃vx​∼Vb​/δ. Neglecting local wind stress curl and relative-vorticity advection in the narrow return region, the linear stationary vorticity equation becomes
βv≃−rξ≃−rvx​.
(26)
Consequently
δ∼βr​,v(x,y)∝e−x/δ.​
(27)
This decay into the interior from the western wall explains why the Stommel boundary layer lies on the west. The sign also matters: a northward return has vx​<0, so its negative relative vorticity supplies a positive drag contribution −rξ to balance βv. Here r has units s−1; no extra factor of f0​ belongs in r/β with the paper's definition of linear drag.
For example, at fixed y the leading correction to the interior ocean transport streamfunction is
ΨB​=LV(y)e−x/δ,HvB​=∂x​ΨB​=−δLV(y)​e−x/δ.
(28)
It cancels the western value of ΨI​ and carries ∫0∞​HvB​dx=−LV=−T. Exponentially small eastern-wall corrections and the turning regions near the northern and southern walls do not affect the leading width estimate.
Using one year =3.156×107 s, the representative β of 2(b), and H=103 m gives
r=3.17×10−8 s−1,δ≃1.58×103 m=1.6 km.​
(29)
To return the 25 Sv interior volume flux, conservation of mass requires
Vb​∼Hδ∣T∣​≃15.8 ms−1.​
(30)
For the exponential profile this is the boundary speed associated with its effective transport width δ. It is much narrower and faster than the familiar large-scale western boundary currents, whose energetic cores span tens of kilometres and have speeds of order 1 ms−1. The simplified constant-depth linear drag model with a one-year decay time therefore does not give a realistic quantitative boundary structure. Lateral viscosity, eddies, stratification and inertia can all affect a real current.
The corresponding relative vorticity scale is
∣ξ∣∼δVb​​≃9.96×10−3 s−1,∣f0​∣∣ξ∣​∼100for ∣f0​∣∼10−4 s−1.​
(31)
Thus relative vorticity cannot be discarded relative to planetary vorticity in this predicted return. Even a more realistic 1 ms−1 across 50 km gives ∣ξ∣∼2×10−5 s−1, an appreciable fraction of ∣f0​∣.
Magnitude alone does not prove that its material advection is large: a perfectly parallel current v=v(x), u=0 has u⋅∇ξ=0 exactly. For a gyre return varying over meridional scale B, however, incompressibility gives u∼δVb​/B, and both generic contributions to u⋅∇ξ scale as Vb2​/(Bδ). Relative to βVb​, this is
βVb​∣u⋅∇ξ∣​∼βBδVb​​≃1.6×102(B=π×106 m).
(32)
Absent special cancellations, the assumed neglect of relative-vorticity advection is therefore not self-consistent for these parameters. Its natural inertial width is instead δI​∼VI​/β​, where VI​=∣T∣/(HL)≃5×10−3 ms−1; this gives about 16 km. That comparison diagnoses the missing inertia rather than supplying a complete nonlinear boundary-current solution.
