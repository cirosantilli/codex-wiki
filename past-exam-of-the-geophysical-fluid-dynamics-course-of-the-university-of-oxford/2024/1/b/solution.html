Use a plane wave with phase kx+mz−ωt, and take N>0, k,m=0. The positive-frequency branch of the given hydrostatic dispersion relation is ω=N∣k∣/∣m∣. The wavevector is K=(k,m). Differentiating with respect to its components gives the group velocity, which carries the wave packet and its energy:
cg​=∇K​ω=(kω​,−mω​).​
(5)
The normal phase velocity, which follows a crest in its normal direction, is
cp​=k2+m2ω​(k,m).​
(6)
Consequently,
K⋅cg​=0,cp​⋅cg​=0,cg,x​cg,z​​=−mk​.
(7)
Thus the group velocity lies along the constant-phase lines z=−(k/m)x+constant, perpendicular to the phase velocity. For k,m>0, crests move upward and rightward, whereas energy moves downward and rightward. Choosing m<0 gives upward and rightward energy propagation and downward and rightward normal phase propagation. Conjugate or oppositely directed components supply the other outgoing directions from a localized source.
The speeds are
∣cg​∣=∣m∣N​1+m2k2​​,∣cp​∣=∣m∣k2+m2​N∣k∣​.​
(8)
The hydrostatic limit is ∣k/m∣≪1. In that limit energy propagates almost horizontally, with ∣cg,x​∣=N/∣m∣ and ∣cg,z​∣=N∣k∣/m2, while normal phase propagation is almost vertical and has speed approximately N∣k∣/m2. The horizontal speed at which a crest intersects a fixed height is ω/k=cg,x​; this is not the horizontal component of the normal phase velocity vector. For example, N=10−2 s−1, vertical wavelength 2π/∣m∣=10 km and horizontal wavelength 2π/∣k∣=1000 km give ∣cg,x​∣≃15.9 ms−1 and ∣cg,z​∣≃0.159 ms−1.
These are local speeds in a uniform medium, without a background current. The non-rotating approximation requires ∣f∣≪ω, while the hydrostatic approximation requires ω≪N. At lower frequencies, rotation changes the dispersion relation to that of inertia-gravity waves.
