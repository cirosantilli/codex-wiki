Use eastward x, northward y, pressure coordinate p increasing downward, and geopotential Φ=gz. Horizontal derivatives are taken on an isobaric surface. The horizontal geostrophic balance and vertical hydrostatic balance are

where R is the specific gas constant of dry air and the final equality uses the ideal gas law. At fixed latitude f is independent of p. Cross-differentiating gives
f∂p​ug​=−∂y​Φp​=pR​Ty​,f∂p​vg​=∂x​Φp​=−pR​Tx​.

Consequently the pressure-coordinate thermal wind equations are
∂lnp∂ug​​=fR​∂y∂T​,∂lnp∂vg​​=−fR​∂x∂T​.​
(22)
Equivalently, fez​×∂lnp​ug​=R∇p​T. Notice the sign: increasing eastward velocity upward means decreasing eastward velocity with lnp, and hence poleward-decreasing temperature in the Northern Hemisphere.
For the stratospheric polar vortex estimate, the original diagram must be read on its logarithmic pressure axis. Near 60∘ in the winter hemisphere, adjacent 10 ms−1 wind contours suggest an increase of roughly 20 ms−1 through a pressure decade surrounding 10 mb: for example, about 10 ms−1 near 30 mb and 30 ms−1 near 3 mb. These are coarse graphical readings, not a sounding. Thus
∂lnp∂ug​​≃ln(3/30)30−10​ ms−1≃−8.7 ms−1.
(23)
With ∣f∣=2Ωsin60∘≃1.26×10−4 s−1 and R≃287 Jkg−1K−1, the poleward temperature gradient is approximately
∂ypole​∂T​≃R∣f∣​∂lnp∂ug​​≃−3.8×10−6 Km−1≃−0.42 K per degree latitude.
(24)
If the vortex edge is taken to span roughly 50∘ to 70∘, its width is a(20π/180)≃2.2×106 m, giving
T(70∘)−T(50∘)≃−8.5 K,a poleward drop of order 10 K.​
(25)
The estimate depends on the width assigned to the edge and the graphical vertical shear; a range of several to roughly fifteen kelvin is more justified than a precise value. The sign remains a cold winter pole in either hemisphere. The diagram does not by itself prescribe a unique pair of edge latitudes.
At low latitudes, horizontal free-atmospheric temperature gradients are usually much smaller than at the winter vortex edge. For a comparable finite wind vertical shear, the rearranged thermal-wind balance, Ty​=(f/R)ulnp​, is consistent with weaker temperature gradients as ∣f∣ decreases. However, geostrophic balance loses validity near the equator because the Coriolis parameter vanishes; division by f cannot be used there to derive a prediction. The weak temperature gradient approximation is better motivated by rapid horizontal internal gravity wave adjustment, together with tropical convection and the Hadley cell. Thermal-wind balance describes the balanced extratropical relation and its tendency approaching the tropics; it is not a complete causal explanation of equatorial thermal uniformity.
