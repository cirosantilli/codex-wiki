Using the constants as printed, with volume flux measured in Sverdrups, gives
A=(7×106)(2×10−7)(15)=21 Sv,B=(7×106)(8×10−7)(35)=196 Sv.​
(28)
In particular the two small expansion coefficients should not be silently replaced by different oceanographic values.
For a positive steady volume flux, the equation in 2(a) is q2−Aq+BE=0. For a negative steady volume flux, it is q2−Aq−BE=0, with only its negative root admissible. Therefore for E>0 the branches are
q±​(E)=2A±A2−4BE​​(0<E≤4BA2​),qrev​(E)=2A−A2+4BE​​<0.​
(29)
The upper positive branch q+​ is stable, the lower positive branch q−​ is unstable, and the reversed branch qrev​ is stable under the usual salt budget dynamics with fixed ΔT. Thus there is bistability below the positive-branch fold. At the fold the two positive equilibria merge with a zero restoring rate; the point is not an ordinary asymptotically stable interior point of either branch. The negative algebraic root of the positive-q quadratic, or positive root of the negative-q quadratic, must never be added as extra solutions.
At E=0.3 Sv, the three steady volume fluxes are
q+​=17.67 Sv,q−​=3.327 Sv,qrev​=−2.502 Sv.​
(30)
The present thermally dominated, northward-upper-flow Atlantic meridional overturning circulation corresponds to q+​≃17.7 Sv. These algebraic data alone also allow a stable reversed state, so selecting the present branch uses the stated circulation direction. On the large positive branch, the predicted salinity contrast is ΔS=ES0​/q+​≃0.594 gkg−1, consistent with the reference-salinity approximation. The weaker branches give larger contrasts, approximately 3.16 and 4.20 gkg−1, so the approximation is less accurate there.
The saddle-node bifurcation occurs at
Ec​=4BA2​=0.5625 Sv,qc​=2A​=10.5 Sv.​
(31)
For E>Ec​, only the reversed steady volume flux remains. At E=Ec​ there are still two distinct values, the double positive root and the negative root; “single-valued” holds strictly above this threshold.
Slowly increasing E from the present value causes the stable positive volume flux to weaken. Its salinity contrast then increases because salt exchange is weaker, reducing the cold-box density advantage further: this is the salt-advection feedback. At the fold the positive steady state disappears, producing an abrupt transition to the reversed branch in the quasistatic limit. At the same Ec​ that branch has
qrev​(Ec​)=2A​(1−2​)≃−4.35 Sv.
(32)
For a finite slow ramp there can be a small dynamical delay near the fold. In this model subsequently reducing E while keeping it positive follows the stable reversed branch instead of automatically restoring the positive branch, an example of hysteresis. The two small branches approach q=0 as E→0+. At E=0 this is an equal-density, no-exchange equilibrium with ΔS=αΔT/β=3.75 gkg−1, whereas the positive branch tends to q=A with ΔS=0. One must use the original density relation at this endpoint, rather than divide by ∣q∣=0.
The reversal is thermally opposed but salinity driven: freshwater makes the cold box sufficiently fresh that the warm, salty box becomes denser. A real weakening would reduce the northward ocean heat flux and alter regional climate and ocean ventilation. The fold and full reversal here describe the ideal two-box overturning model; its numerical threshold is not a forecast for the real ocean, whose other controls appear in 2(c).
