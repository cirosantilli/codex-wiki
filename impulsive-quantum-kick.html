Impulsive quantum kick (Ukick​=e−iK/ℏ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Sudden approximation Quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step
OurBigBook.com Words: 181 Articles: 2
An impulsive quantum kick is the limit of a pulse with finite integrated Hamiltonian operator, rather than a bounded finite step. For a bounded self-adjoint operator K with units of action, a rectangular pulse Hτ​=K/τ lasting τ has exact unitary time evolution e−iK/ℏ for every τ. In the limiting notation H(t)=Kδ(t), δ is the Dirac delta function and
∣ψ(0+)⟩=e−iK/ℏ∣ψ(0−)⟩.
(27)
The integral estimate for quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step no longer tends to zero: the pulse height diverges. A bounded background Hamiltonian operator contributes vanishing evolution during this limit. A more general pulse with noncommuting generators can require time ordering; integrated area alone then need not specify the result.
For example, K=ℏϕσy​/2, with a y Pauli matrix, sends a qubit in ∣0⟩ to cos(ϕ/2)∣0⟩+sin(ϕ/2)∣1⟩. At ϕ=π, the Born rule predicts ∣1⟩ with probability one, however short the regularized pulse is. Its finite rotation is precisely what a bounded finite step cannot accomplish at the switching instant.
