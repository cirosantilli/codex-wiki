Pareto top shares and the 80/20 rule (T(q)=q1−1/α,α>1)

For a nonnegative random variable with finite positive mean, the share of its total amount carried by the largest fraction q of outcomes can be defined by dividing their contribution to the expected value by the whole expected value. For a Pareto distribution, let 0<q≤1 and choose xq​=βq−1/α, so that P(X≥xq​)=q. If α>1, direct integration gives
T(q)=E[X]E[X1{X≥xq​}​]​=αβ/(α−1)αβαxq1−α​/(α−1)​=q1−1/α.
(23)
This is a share of amount, distinct from the fraction of outcomes. It depends on α, but not on the unit of measurement β.[ref]
For the chosen α=2, the top 20% starts at x0.2​=105​≃22.361 and carries T(0.2)=0.2​≃44.72% of the amount. To make the top 20% carry exactly 80%, solve
(1/5)1−1/α=4/5⟹α=log4log5​≃1.160964.
(24)
The resulting Pareto distribution has a finite mean and infinite variance. The 80/20 rule selects one shape parameter; it is not a universal law, or even a property of every Pareto distribution. For α≤1, the population-share definition above is unavailable because its denominator is infinite; substituting such an α into the finite-mean formula is invalid.
The left panel compares three quantiles in units of the minimum. The right panel shows T(0.2) only where the mean is finite, and marks the chosen 80/20 value. The thresholds α=1 and α=2 separate the moment regimes; they do not separate valid and invalid probability distributions.
A straight-looking empirical log-log plot does not by itself establish a Pareto distribution or a reliable tail exponent; estimating an exponent and assessing goodness of fit are separate tasks.[ref] If a Pareto distribution is used only above a fitted lower threshold, T(q) describes that conditional tail population, not automatically the whole population. A genuine finite upper cutoff gives a truncated distribution with finite nonnegative moments; its survival function, quantiles and top shares must be recalculated. Truncation is a modelling change, distinct from merely observing a finite sample. None of the chosen numbers here estimates an actual wealth distribution.
