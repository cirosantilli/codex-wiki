A standing wave has a fixed spatial amplitude profile and oscillates in time, with stationary nodes of a standing wave. It arises from equal-amplitude waves of the same frequency travelling in opposite directions. For a uniform string with linear mass density μ, the wave speed is c=T/μ​ and a separated mode has Y′′+k2Y=0.
The fixed boundary requires Y(0)=0, while the free boundary requires TY′(L)=0. Thus Y=Asinkx and coskL=0, giving
kn​=L(n+1/2)π​,yn​(x,t)=An​sin(kn​x)cos(ckn​t+ϕn​),n=0,1,2,….​
(9)
The fixed end is a node of a standing wave and the free end an antinode. Arbitrary small motions are superpositions of these normal modes; the tension alone determines their frequencies only when μ is also specified.

For small horizontal displacement xi​≃lθi​, the gravitational restoring force is −mgθi​≃−(mg/l)xi​. A spring between adjacent bobs has extension equal to their displacement difference; Hooke's law pulls each bob towards its neighbour's displaced position. (15)
The small-angle approximation makes the bob motion horizontal to first order; its vertical shift changes spring geometry only at higher order. Multiplying all force equations by minus one puts the positive restoring coefficients on the displayed right-hand side.

The mixed partial derivative fxy​ measures how the slope fy​ changes as x varies while y is fixed:
fxy​(x,y)=limh→0​hfy​(x+h,y)−fy​(x,y)​,
(28)

An interior stationary point has fx​=fy​=0. Assuming twice continuous differentiability nearby, the two-dimensional Taylor series about it is
f(p+h)=f(p)+21​hTHh+o(∣h∣2),H=(AB​BC​),
(29)
where A=fxx​, B=fxy​ and C=fyy​. Let D=AC−B2. The second-derivative test gives a strict local minimum if D>0,A>0, a strict local maximum if D>0,A<0, and a saddle point if D<0.
For example, if A=0, the quadratic form is A(hx​+Bhy​/A)2+(D/A)hy2​, which proves these sign tests. If D=0, the quadratic part is inconclusive: x4+y4 has a minimum while x4−y4 has a saddle at the origin, although both have zero Hessian matrix there. Higher-order terms or direct inequalities must then be examined.

c OurBigBook.com
Writing r=x2+y2​, the absolute lower bound is cosr≥−1, attained exactly when
x2+y2=[(2n+1)π]2,n=0,1,2,….​
(30)
Every point on these circles is a global and local minimum, but not a strict minimum: neighbouring points on the same circle have exactly the same value. At such a point the radial Hessian eigenvalue is one and the tangential eigenvalue is zero, illustrating why a degenerate second-derivative test does not exclude a minimum.
There are no other local minima: between successive multiples of π, moving radially changes the value monotonically. The positive even-radius circles are maxima. At the origin the apparently singular radial expression is smooth, since cosx2+y2​=1−(x2+y2)/2+O(r4), and the origin is a strict local maximum.

The gradient equations are y=x3 and x=y3. Their real solutions are (0,0), (1,1) and (−1,−1). The Hessian matrix is (−12x24​4−12y2​), so the origin has eigenvalues ±4 and is a saddle point, with value zero. At either other point the eigenvalues are −8,−16, giving strict maxima with value two. They are global maxima because
4xy−x4−y4≤4xy−2x2y2=2−2(xy−1)2≤2,
(31)
and equality occurs precisely at those two points.
In polar coordinates, the zero contour is
0=2r2sin2θ−r4(cos4θ+sin4θ).
(32)
Besides the saddle r=0, its nonzero branches satisfy
r2=cos4θ+sin4θ2sin2θ​=3+cos4θ8sin2θ​.​
(33)
The denominator is positive; real radii require sin2θ≥0. The branches form two closed lobes in the first and third quadrants, meeting at the origin. Positive contours lie inside these lobes around the maxima, and negative contours extend outside. Along the coordinate axes the function is negative away from the saddle, whereas along y=x it is initially positive; these signs show the crossing geometry in the sketch.
