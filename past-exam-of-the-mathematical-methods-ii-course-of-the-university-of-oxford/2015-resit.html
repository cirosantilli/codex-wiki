A ray leaving focus A and reaching a boundary point P has incoming unit direction uA​. By the law of reflection, its normal component reverses and its tangential component stays fixed. The result just derived shows that the reflected direction is −uB​, pointing exactly from P to focus B. Thus all such in-plane rays from one focus converge to the other after one reflection, in the ideal geometrical optics model.
Moreover, every reflected path has the same total length AP+PB=s. In a uniform medium their propagation times are equal as well, so different reflection points do not introduce a geometrical path-length spread. Finite wavelength, surface errors and incomplete reflecting coverage limit a real focus; the ideal argument concerns rays in the plane of the ellipse. It does not assert that an arbitrary three-dimensional surface with only one elliptical section focuses every out-of-plane ray.

Un​(t)=4Tlkn2​An2​​cos2(ωn​t+ϕn​)​.
(54)
Since ρωn2​=Tkn2​, the sum is time independent:
En​=Kn​+Un​=4Tlkn2​An2​​=4ln2π2TAn2​​​.
(55)
For the released shape, A1​=A and A2​=A/2, so
E1​=E2​=4lπ2TA2​,Etotal​=2lπ2TA2​​.
(56)
The second mode's smaller displacement is offset by its doubled spatial wavenumber and angular frequency. orthogonality of distinct sine and cosine spatial modes eliminates cross terms in the integrated energies, so the total is their sum. Initially the kinetic energy is zero and all the energy is stored in the string's deformation; during the motion the total remains constant.
