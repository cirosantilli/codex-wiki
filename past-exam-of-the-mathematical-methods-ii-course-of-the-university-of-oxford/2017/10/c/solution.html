Each light-string round trip combines wall reflection −1 with junction reflection −1/3, multiplying the remaining pulse amplitude by 1/3. The initial directly reflected heavy-string pulse has amplitude Ai​/3. The subsequent outgoing heavy pulses have amplitudes
An​=−98​(31​)n−1Ai​,n=1,2,….
(25)
They all have width w in the same heavy medium. For nonoverlapping pulses their energy fractions therefore sum to
Ei​Eout​​=91​+8164​n=0∑∞​(91​)n=91​+98​=1.​
(26)
The first transmission leaves energy fraction 8/9 in the light segment; each return encounter leaves only 1/9 of the previous stored energy there, so this residual tends to zero.
For reflection from a finite string segment with a fixed far end, if pulses overlap, squared amplitudes cannot simply be added because of interference. The same result follows frequency by frequency: with round-trip phase q=e2ikl​Ll​, Ll​=2m, the total amplitude coefficient is
R(q)=31​−1−q/38q/9​=1−q/31/3−q​.
(27)
For real frequencies ∣q∣=1, so ∣1/3−q∣=∣1−q/3∣ and ∣R∣=1. Thus the ideal lossless fixed-ended segment returns all incident finite-pulse energy even when individual echoes overlap; their relative phases must be retained.
