vg​=vp​+kdkdvp​​=vp​−λdλdvp​​.

Apply the product-to-sum identity directly to the stated modulation:
A=acos(ωt−kx)+2b​cos[(ω+ωm​)t−kx]+2b​cos[(ω−ωm​)t−kx].​
(15)
The carrier has angular frequency ω and the two modulation sidebands have angular frequencies ω±ωm​, each with amplitude b/2.
Propagation qualification. In the literal formula, all three terms have the same spatial wavenumber. That identity is correct, but a time-only amplitude applied everywhere is not generally a freely propagating wave at speed c. For k=ω/c, applying ∂t2​−c2∂x2​ to the stated field gives A0′′​cos(ωt−kx)−2ωA0′​sin(ωt−kx), which at x=t=0 equals −bωm2​=0 when the modulation is nontrivial.
A physical modulation launched at a boundary and travelling without dispersion has envelope a+bcos[ωm​(t−x/c)]. The same expansion then has sideband wavenumbers k±​=(ω±ωm​)/c, while its value at x=0 agrees with the given time-modulated signal. This distinguishes the requested frequency decomposition from an unsupported fixed-wavenumber propagation claim.

At a smooth interior stationary point, define D=t11​t22​−t122​. The second-derivative test gives a minimum for D>0,t11​>0, a maximum for D>0,t11​<0, and a saddle point for D<0; D=0 is inconclusive.

Thus the stationary path is a strict minimum, not merely a candidate of unspecified type. In fact the same positivity holds for all finite crossing positions, so the time function is strictly convex and the minimum is unique.

Take downward displacement as positive. At equilibrium, the lower spring carries mg and extends by mg/k, while the upper spring supports both masses and extends by 2mg/k. Write x1​,x2​ for the masses' downward displacements from these equilibrium positions. The extra upper-spring extension is x1​ and the extra lower-spring extension is x2​−x1​. Applying Newton's second law and cancelling the equilibrium forces gives
mx¨1​=−2kx1​+kx2​,mx¨2​=kx1​−kx2​.​
(21)
The gravitational terms disappear because the constant equilibrium spring forces already balance the weights. Gravity sets the equilibrium extensions, rather than the coefficients of the linear oscillation equations for ideal springs.

For an element dx, the vertical components of the approximately constant tension are Tyx​ at its two ends. Their difference is Tyxx​dx to first order in the small slope. Its inertial response is ρdxytt​. Thus Newton's second law gives

The components of the curl of a gradient are differences of mixed second derivatives:
∇×∇ϕ=(ϕzy​−ϕyz​, ϕxz​−ϕzx​, ϕyx​−ϕxy​).
(38)
If ϕ has continuous second partial derivatives in a neighborhood, equality of mixed derivatives makes every component vanish. Thus
∇×(∇ϕ)=0.​
(39)
The C2 condition is a sufficient regularity hypothesis; no simple-connectivity assumption is needed for this local identity. Topology matters for the converse assertion that every curl-free field has a global potential.

For the given polynomial vector field, direct differentiation gives
(∇×G)x​=x2−x2=0,(∇×G)y​=(2xy−1)−(2xy−1)=0,(∇×G)z​=(3+2xz)−(3+2xz)=0.

An exact differential is a differential one-form equal to dϕ=ϕx​dx+ϕy​dy+ϕz​dz for a single-valued scalar function. This field is smooth on the simply connected domain R3, so its vanishing curl is sufficient for exactness. More concretely,
