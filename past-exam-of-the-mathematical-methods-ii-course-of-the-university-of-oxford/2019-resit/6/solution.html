For an oriented piecewise smooth surface S, a continuously differentiable vector field F on a neighborhood of it, and its compatibly oriented boundary C=∂S, Stokes theorem states
∮C​F⋅dr=∬S​(∇×F)⋅ndS.​
(18)
Here n is the chosen unit normal vector, dS is the unsigned surface area element, and dr is the tangent displacement along C. The right-hand rule relates the boundary direction to n.
The supplied field has curl ∇×F=(2z,2x,2y). Parametrize the plane by r(x,y)=(x,y,1−x−y) over the triangle D={x,y≥0:x+y≤1}. Its upward oriented surface area element is (1,1,1)dxdy, giving
∬S​(∇×F)⋅dS=∫D​2(x+y+z)dxdy=2area(D)=1​.
(19)
For the boundary line integral, take the positively oriented vertices A=(1,0,0), B=(0,1,0), C=(0,0,1). Their projections run counterclockwise in the horizontal plane. With 0≤t≤1, the three edges are
rAB​=(1−t,t,0),rBC​=(0,1−t,t),rCA​=(t,0,1−t).
(20)
On each edge, direct substitution gives F(r(t))⋅r′(t)=t2+2t−1. Hence
∮C​F⋅dr=3∫01​(t2+2t−1)dt=3(31​)=1.​
(21)
The two independent computations agree, including the orientation sign.
