Use the outward normal on the spherical patch. It is the quarter of the spherical surface lying below both the horizontal plane and the plane y=0, with both signs of x included. Close it by the two half-discs Dy​ in y=0,z≤0 and Dz​ in z=0,y≤0.
The divergence of the polynomial vector field is ∇⋅F=4xy. Its volume integral over the enclosed quarter-ball vanishes by oddness in x. Thus the divergence theorem gives ΦS​+ΦDy​​+ΦDz​​=0.
On Dy​, the outward normal is +y^​ and Fy​=−3z4. On Dz​, it is +z^ and Fz​=x3+y2; the odd term integrates to zero. The needed half-disc moments for radius R are
∫Dy​​z4dxdz=(∫0R​r5dr)(∫π2π​sin4ϕdϕ)=16πR6​,
(32)
and
∫Dz​​y2dxdy=(∫0R​r3dr)(∫π2π​sin2ϕdϕ)=8πR4​.
(33)
At R=4, the cap fluxes are −768π and 32π, respectively. Therefore the flux through a spherical quadrant by planar caps is
ΦS​=768π−32π=736π.​
(34)
The source does not explicitly specify a normal for this part; the boxed answer adopts the usual outward spherical orientation. Choosing the inward normal reverses its sign.
