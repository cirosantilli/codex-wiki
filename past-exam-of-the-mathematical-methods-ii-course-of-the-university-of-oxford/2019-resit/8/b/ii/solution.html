On an outward-oriented sphere of radius R>0, the electric field is normal with constant magnitude A/R2. Thus its flux is
∬SR​​E⋅dS=R2A​(4πR2)=4πA=ϵ0​Q​.​
(29)
It is independent of R. There is no contradiction with solution: the classical divergence theorem cannot be applied to a full ball while pretending that E is smooth at its center. On a punctured ball the outer flux and the inward-normal inner flux cancel, in agreement with zero ordinary divergence there.
The three-dimensional Dirac delta distribution δ(3)(r) is supported at the origin and satisfies the sampling identity
∫R3​δ(3)(r)φ(r)dV=φ(0)
(30)
for smooth compactly supported test functions φ. It is not an ordinary function with a finite value at zero; its total integral is one, and translating it moves the point at which the test function is sampled.
The globally valid source equation is
∇⋅E=4πAδ(3)(r)=ϵ0​Q​δ(3)(r).​
(31)
For a direct weak verification, excise a ball of radius ε and integrate −E⋅∇φ outside it. Since the ordinary divergence vanishes there, the result is A∫S2​φ(εr^)dΩ→4πAφ(0). This is precisely the displayed distributional divergence and restores the divergence theorem with the point charge included.
