For a real propagating positive-frequency branch, require ω2>a2 and c=0. If a is the usual nonnegative cutoff, the source condition ω>a gives this branch. With an arbitrary real negative a, that inequality alone is insufficient: a=−2 and ω=1 satisfy it but make the displayed square root imaginary.
On the positive-frequency real branch, the phase velocity vP​=ω/k gives
vP​=ω2−a2​cω​,k=cω2−a2​​.
(19)
Differentiate the dispersion relation to obtain the group velocity:
dωdk​=cω2−a2​ω​,vG​=dkdω​=ωcω2−a2​​.
(20)
Therefore
vG​=vP​c2​,vP​vG​=c2​.
(21)
For c>0 and a nonzero cutoff, vP​>c while vG​<c; when a=0, both equal c. The constant c is the velocity scale in this assumed medium, and need not independently be identified with the vacuum speed of light. A group velocity describes a narrow-band wave packet on the chosen differentiable branch, rather than automatically fixing a signal front velocity in every dispersive model.
