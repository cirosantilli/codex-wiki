Work on a domain with x=0, and suppose f is differentiable at u=y/x. The multivariable chain rule gives
∂x∂w​=f′(u)∂x∂u​=−x2y​f′(u),∂y∂w​=f′(u)∂y∂u​=x1​f′(u).
(8)
Multiplying the first equation by x and the second by y cancels their common terms:
x∂x∂w​+y∂y∂w​=0​.
(9)
The geometry is that y/x stays fixed along a radial dilation (x,y)↦(λx,λy). Thus w is constant along each admissible radial ray, and the differential operator x∂x​+y∂y​ differentiates along that ray. This is the degree-zero case of Euler homogeneous function theorem. The displayed quotient does not define a function at x=0 without additional extension data.
