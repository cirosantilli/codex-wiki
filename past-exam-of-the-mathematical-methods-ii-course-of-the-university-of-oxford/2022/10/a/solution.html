Write A(s)=s(s2−4), so z=A(x)A(y) and
zx​=(3x2−4)y(y2−4),zy​=(3y2−4)x(x2−4).
(19)
Solving these simultaneously gives thirteen stationary points: the nine grid points with x,y∈{−2,0,2} and the four points x,y∈{−2/3​,2/3​}. There are no mixed extra cases, because A and A′ have no common root.
At a grid point the Hessian matrix has zero diagonal and off-diagonal entry A′(x)A′(y)=0, so its determinant is negative: all nine are saddle points. At the other four points the Hessian matrix is −16xyI. Thus the equal-sign points are local maxima with value 256/27, and the opposite-sign points are local minima with value −256/27.
In the requested nonnegative quadrant, the stationary points are the four boundary saddle points (0,0),(0,2),(2,0),(2,2) and the interior local maximum (2/3​,2/3​). The zero contours are x=0,2 and y=0,2. The gradient is perpendicular to regular contours and points toward increasing z. A boundary point's classification above refers to the function on the whole plane; its behavior restricted to the quadrant can be one-sided. The interior local maximum is not a global maximum on the unbounded quadrant, since z→+∞ along x=y→∞.
