For a continuously differentiable vector field on a bounded region with suitably piecewise smooth boundary, the divergence theorem gives
∮S​F⋅n^dS=∫V​∇⋅FdV,
(1)
where the normal vector points outward. In three dimensions, the position vector is r=(x,y,z) and ∇⋅r=1+1+1=3. Therefore
∮S​r⋅dS=3V.​
(2)
The result depends only on the enclosed volume, not on the shape or its location relative to the origin. Reversing the surface orientation reverses the sign.

a OurBigBook.com
For a differentiable function at an interior stationary point, both first partial derivatives vanish. If the second derivatives are continuous, form the symmetric Hessian matrix
H=(fxx​fxy​​fxy​fyy​​),D=fxx​fyy​−fxy2​.
(15)
The second-derivative test gives a strict local minimum if D>0 and fxx​>0, a strict local maximum if D>0 and fxx​<0, and a saddle point if D<0. If D=0, higher-order terms must be examined. For example, x4+y4, −x4−y4 and x4−y4 all have zero Hessian matrix at the origin but respectively a minimum, maximum and saddle. These are local interior criteria; boundary extrema require separate treatment.

Choose both waves as sinusoids with a common zero phase and amplitude A. The trigonometric sum identity gives
Acos[(k+δ)x−(ω+ϵ)t]+Acos[(k−δ)x−(ω−ϵ)t]=2Acos(δx−ϵt)cos(kx−ωt).
(17)
The slowly varying wave envelope has constant phase when δx−ϵt is constant. Its speed is ϵ/δ, and in the close-frequency limit the group velocity is
vg​=dkdω​.​
(18)
It describes the motion of a narrow-band envelope in a medium where the constituent waves retain a coherent packet interpretation; it is not an unconditional signal-speed theorem in strongly absorbing or anomalously dispersive media. The carrier phase velocity is vp​=ω/k. In a nondispersive medium, ω=vk with constant v, so vg​=v=vp​.

The quadratic total mechanical energy is
E=21​θ˙TTθ˙+21​θTVθ,dtdE​=θ˙T(Tθ¨+Vθ).
(23)
For this frictionless conservative double pendulum, the Euler-Lagrange equations for L=K−U give
Tθ¨+Vθ=0,​
(24)
or 8θ¨1​+2θ¨2​+(4g/l)θ1​=0 and 2θ¨1​+θ¨2​+(g/l)θ2​=0. Substitution immediately verifies energy conservation.
Logical qualification. Energy conservation does not uniquely determine equations of motion: the scalar equation dE/dt=0 alone does not determine both equations. For example, replacing their right side by Gθ˙ with any nonzero antisymmetric matrix G preserves the same energy because θ˙TGθ˙=0, while changing the dynamics. Such a gyroscopic force is absent from the specified pendulum. The conservative-force structure, supplied here by the Lagrangian, is the additional information needed to justify the displayed equations rather than infer two independent equations from one scalar identity.

b OurBigBook.com
This is the velocity field of solid-body rotation about the axis parallel to ω through the origin. A fluid-following body's centre moves around that axis at angular speed ∣ω∣, keeping its axial coordinate and perpendicular distance fixed. Its orientation rotates at the same angular velocity. The symmetric part of the velocity-gradient tensor is zero, so the prescribed flow produces no local strain or change of shape. Indeed, the separation d between two fluid points obeys d∣d∣2/dt=2d⋅(ω×d)=0.
This is a kinematic prediction for an entrained body or material object. A finite inertial body initially at rest cannot instantaneously acquire the prescribed velocity and rotation without forces and torques; its transient after placement would also require density, drag and coupling data. Neglecting gravity alone does not specify that transient. On the rotation axis its centre can remain fixed while its orientation still rotates.
