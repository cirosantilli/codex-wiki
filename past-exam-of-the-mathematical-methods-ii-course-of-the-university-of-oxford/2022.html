i OurBigBook.com
For a sufficiently smooth vector field on an oriented surface S with compatible boundary orientation, Stokes theorem states
∮∂S​F⋅dr=∬S​(∇×F)⋅ndS.
(15)
Apply it to F=(P,Q,0) in the plane. The closed line integral is the area integral of Qx​−Py​. Thus Py​=Qx​ makes it vanish whenever a spanning surface lies within the smooth domain. In a simply connected domain this implies path independence: join one path from A to B to the reverse of another, and their difference is a zero closed line integral.
The domain qualification is essential. On the punctured plane, P=−y/(x2+y2), Q=x/(x2+y2) satisfy Py​=Qx​, but the line integral around the unit circle is 2π. A disk spanning that circle passes through the singularity. Hence the printed implication requires an appropriate domain assumption; zero curl alone is not a global path independence theorem.

Let F′=f and G′=g on the relevant intervals. The chain rule gives
d{F(xy)+G(y/x)}=[yf(xy)−x2y​g(y/x)]dx+[xf(xy)+x1​g(y/x)]dy.
(16)
Thus the one-form is explicitly an exact differential. Fixing its potential to vanish at (1,1) determines it uniquely on the connected domain:
ϕ(x,y)=∫1xy​f(s)ds+∫1y/x​g(t)dt.​
(17)
Continuity of f,g on the integration ranges suffices. For arbitrary smooth data defined throughout x>0, that half-plane also supplies the simply connected domain in the previous argument.
For the specialization f(s)=s−2, the potential based at (1,1) is defined in the positive quadrant; it cannot be continued through xy=0 using this formula. Taking a,b>0 keeps both endpoints in that component. Their ratios y/x are both b/a, so the G term cancels and
ϕ(ab,b2)−ϕ(a,b)=∫abab3​s−2ds=ab3b2−1​.​
(18)
No condition on g beyond existence on the traversed ratios enters this difference.

a OurBigBook.com
Write A(s)=s(s2−4), so z=A(x)A(y) and
zx​=(3x2−4)y(y2−4),zy​=(3y2−4)x(x2−4).
(19)
Solving these simultaneously gives thirteen stationary points: the nine grid points with x,y∈{−2,0,2} and the four points x,y∈{−2/3​,2/3​}. There are no mixed extra cases, because A and A′ have no common root.
At a grid point the Hessian matrix has zero diagonal and off-diagonal entry A′(x)A′(y)=0, so its determinant is negative: all nine are saddle points. At the other four points the Hessian matrix is −16xyI. Thus the equal-sign points are local maxima with value 256/27, and the opposite-sign points are local minima with value −256/27.
In the requested nonnegative quadrant, the stationary points are the four boundary saddle points (0,0),(0,2),(2,0),(2,2) and the interior local maximum (2/3​,2/3​). The zero contours are x=0,2 and y=0,2. The gradient is perpendicular to regular contours and points toward increasing z. A boundary point's classification above refers to the function on the whole plane; its behavior restricted to the quadrant can be one-sided. The interior local maximum is not a global maximum on the unbounded quadrant, since z→+∞ along x=y→∞.

First solve the supplied equations as a linear system, with ω02​=g/l and α>0. A normal mode x=aeiωt obeys
ω02​ω2​a=Da,D=(1+2α−α​−αα​).
(22)
Its characteristic polynomial is λ2−(1+3α)λ+α(1+α). Hence
ω±2​=2lg​[1+3α±1+2α+5α2​].​
(23)
Both are positive, since the trace and determinant of D are positive.
There is a physical discrepancy in the source. The horizontal-coordinate mass matrix of an unequal-mass double pendulum follows from the stated horizontal displacements, which give
T2​=2m​x˙12​+2αm​x˙22​,V2​=2lmg​[x12​+αx12​+α(x2​−x1​)2].
(24)
The Euler-Lagrange equations reproduce the supplied upper equation, but give x¨2​=−(g/l)(x2​−x1​), without its extra α. The true normal modes of a double pendulum consequently have ω±2​=(g/l)[1+α±α(1+α)​]. The boxed answer and the remaining parts below concern the explicitly supplied linear system. The two models agree at α=1, but not for a general mass ratio.

Assume V0​=0. Linear independence of the Fourier sine basis makes a flat string equivalent to vanishing of all three temporal coefficients. The fundamental requires sin(ω1​t)=0, which automatically zeros the other two. Thus the earliest positive return is
tflat​=ω1​π​=cL​.​
(32)
The velocity need not have returned to its initial value at that time; full recurrence of the displacement-and-velocity data occurs at 2L/c.
A nonzero pure fundamental profile would require sin2ω1​t=sin3ω1​t=0. The common zeros have ω1​t an integer multiple of π, so the fundamental vanishes too. A pure second or third harmonic would already require the fundamental to vanish, again making the whole string flat. The string never has a nonzero pure sine profile. If V0​=0, it remains identically zero and there is no earliest positive isolated return.
