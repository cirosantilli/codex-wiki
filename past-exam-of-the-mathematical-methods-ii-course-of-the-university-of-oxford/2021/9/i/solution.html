Let S be a compact piecewise smooth oriented surface, n its chosen unit normal vector, and ∂S its entire oriented boundary. Let A be a continuously differentiable vector field on a neighborhood of S. Stokes theorem states
∬S​(∇×A)⋅ndS=∮∂S​A⋅dr.​
(37)
Here ∇×A is the curl, dS is the surface area element, and dr is the directed line integral element. The boundary follows the right-hand rule relative to n; equivalently the surface lies to the left when walking along its boundary with one's head toward n.
The topological requirement is orientability, together with the regularity and boundary assumptions above. Simple connectivity is unnecessary. An annulus or cylindrical side is allowed, provided every boundary component is included with its induced direction. For a concrete counterexample to a disk-only restriction, take the planar annulus 1≤x2+y2≤4 and A=(−y/2,x/2,0). The curl is ez​, the surface integral is 3π, and the outer counterclockwise and inner clockwise line integrals sum to 4π−π=3π.
For S in the xy plane with upward normal vector and A=(P,Q,0), its curl has vertical component Qx​−Py​. Thus Stokes theorem becomes Green's theorem:
∮∂D​(Pdx+Qdy)=∬D​(∂x∂Q​−∂y∂P​)dxdy.​
(38)
The outer boundary is counterclockwise and any inner boundaries are clockwise.
