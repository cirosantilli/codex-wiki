The arc length of the helix follows from its parametrization:
r′(t)=(−10sin(t−π/2), 10cos(t−π/2), 5),∣r′(t)∣=100+25​=55​.

Thus ℓh​=15π5​m. Its endpoint is (0,20,15π), at height h=15πm.
Under the usual intended interpretation that the descending quarter of a circle drops through one radius, its radius is R=h. Its arc length is ℓc​=πR/2=15π2/2m, giving
ℓh​+ℓc​=15π5​+215π2​=179.39425397…m≃179m.​
(10)
The printed smoothness condition is inconsistent. The terminal tangent vector of the helix is r′(3π)=(−10,0,5). Every tangent vector to a curve confined to the yz plane has zero x component, so no such regular curve can join this helix with a common tangent line. Moreover, the helix is still rising at that endpoint. Thus no smooth ride satisfying all the literal conditions exists.
Even after allowing a corner, a quarter of a circle reaching the ground does not alone determine its radius: the orientation of its endpoint radii must also be specified. For example, in the yz plane, the circular arc
y=20+R(cosθ−cosθ0​),z=h+R(sinθ−sinθ0​),θ0​−π/2≤θ≤θ0​,
(11)
traversed toward decreasing θ, descends monotonically for 0≤θ0​≤π/2 and reaches z=0 when R=h/(sinθ0​+cosθ0​). Choosing θ0​=π/4 gives R=h/2​, whereas θ0​=π/2 gives R=h. The numerical answer above is conditional on the intended R=h geometry; it does not repair the impossible smooth join.
