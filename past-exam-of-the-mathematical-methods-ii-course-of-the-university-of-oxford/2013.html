a OurBigBook.com
Assume the stated continuous derivatives on an open simply connected region. If Nx​−My​=0, Green's theorem gives zero circulation around every simple closed curve and its enclosed region; a piecewise smooth closed curve can be decomposed into such loops, giving the same conclusion.
Conversely, suppose every closed-curve integral vanishes. Apply Green's theorem to an arbitrarily small disk about any interior point P. Its integral of Nx​−My​ is zero. Divide by the disk area and shrink its radius to zero; continuity makes the average approach (Nx​−My​)(P), so that value must vanish. Thus
∮C​Mdx+Ndy=0 for every closed C⟺Nx​=My​.​
(29)
Simple connectivity is needed for the global sufficiency statement, ensuring that a loop does not enclose a missing portion of the domain. Local necessity already follows from small disks.

Along a path avoiding the origin, polar coordinates give

d OurBigBook.com
There is no contradiction. The domain on which the field is smooth is the punctured plane, which is not simply connected. A loop enclosing the origin cannot be spanned by a planar region entirely inside that domain, so the smoothness hypothesis needed to apply Green's theorem to its full interior fails. A loop that bounds a region avoiding the origin does have zero circulation.
Locally the field is the gradient of the polar angle, but that angle cannot be made a single-valued continuous potential on the whole punctured plane. The nonzero period 2π around the hole measures this global obstruction, despite the field having zero local curl.

A local-wave description requires an identifiable smooth phase Θ(x,t) with kˉ=Θx​ and ωˉ=−Θt​. Real propagating wavenumber and frequency must satisfy the chosen local branch of ωˉ2=c2kˉ2+a. In a geometrical optics or slowly modulated wavepacket approximation, these quantities and the amplitude vary on scales large compared with a local wavelength and period; for example ∣∂x​kˉ∣≪kˉ2 and ∣∂t​ωˉ∣≪ωˉ2 away from degenerate points.
The phase compatibility in geometrical optics means the local fields cannot be specified independently: equality of mixed phase derivatives requires kˉt​+ωˉx​=0, derived as wave conservation below. These phase and scale conditions justify the local dispersion relation; they alone are not a guarantee of an arbitrary-amplitude exact PDE solution. Amplitude transport and higher-order envelope terms must also be satisfied.

On a locally right-moving branch with kˉ,ωˉ>0, a spatial phase increase of 2π counts one wave. The local number of waves per unit length and the number passing a fixed point per unit time are

For reflection between the foci of an ellipse, assume s>∣A−B∣ and define Φ(P)=RA​(P)+RB​(P). From part (b),
∇Φ=uA​+uB​,uA​=∣P−A∣P−A​,uB​=∣P−B∣P−B​.

d OurBigBook.com
In a homogeneous medium and the short-wavelength ray approximation, a smooth reflecting elliptical boundary sends a ray from focus A to focus B after one specular reflection, by the equal-angle property just proved. Moreover, every such route has the same path length AP+PB=s, hence the same propagation time s/cs​ for constant sound speed cs​.
An ideal two-dimensional elliptical reflector, or the corresponding ellipsoidal geometry, therefore concentrates sound near the other focus; equal travel times also support coherent arrival for a monochromatic source with a consistent reflection phase. Finite wavelength, imperfect surfaces, absorption and three-dimensional room geometry limit the focusing. The result predicts a finite physical focal region, not an infinite intensity at a mathematical point.
