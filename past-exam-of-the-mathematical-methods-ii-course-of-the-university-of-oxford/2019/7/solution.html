Work in the small-slope model of a transverse wave on a taut string. Let T be the constant tension, ρ the linear mass density, and take a right-moving sinusoidal displacement y=Acos(κx−ωt), with wave number κ=ω/v. The energy flux of a transverse string wave through a cut is the transverse force times transverse velocity:
P(x,t)=−T∂x∂y​∂t∂y​=TκωA2sin2(κx−ωt).
(16)
A cycle average gives ⟨sin2⟩=1/2. Using T=ρv2, obtained from the string wave equation, yields
P=21​TκωA2=21​ρvω2A2.​
(17)
The sign in the flux formula makes the power positive for propagation in the positive x direction.
The energy per unit equilibrium length is the sum of the kinetic and elastic terms:
E=21​ρyt2​+21​Tyx2​,⟨E⟩=21​ρω2A2.
(18)
For a segment of mass M=ρℓ containing many wavelengths, the spatial average agrees with the cycle average up to a small endpoint correction. Thus E≃21​Mω2A2. Convert the given quantities to SI units: M=0.080 kg, A=1.6×10−4 m, and ω=2π(120) s−1. Then
E≃21​(0.080)(240π)2(1.6×10−4)2=5.82×10−4 J.​
(19)
The many-wavelength condition explains why no phase of the finite segment is needed. The result is exact as a cycle average, or for a segment containing an integer number of wavelengths.
