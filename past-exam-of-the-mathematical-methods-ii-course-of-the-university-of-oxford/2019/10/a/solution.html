Use s for cylindrical radius, to distinguish it from spherical radius r. In cylindrical coordinates (s,ϕ,z), the azimuthal angle ϕ is measured from positive x in the horizontal plane:
x=scosϕ,y=ssinϕ,z=z,s≥0.​
(36)
Here 0≤ϕ<2π and z∈R.
In spherical polar coordinates (r,θ,ϕ), take θ as the polar angle from positive z and use the same azimuthal angle ϕ:
x=rsinθcosϕ,y=rsinθsinϕ,z=rcosθ.​
(37)
The ranges are r≥0, 0≤θ≤π and 0≤ϕ<2π. The radial direction has horizontal projection of length rsinθ, which explains the extra factor in x,y. The azimuth is undefined on the polar axis, and both angles are nonunique at the origin; these are coordinate degeneracies rather than geometric singularities.
