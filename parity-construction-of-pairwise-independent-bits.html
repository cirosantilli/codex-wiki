Parity construction of pairwise independent bits (X=U, Y=V, Z=U⊕V)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Pairwise independent events
OurBigBook.com Words: 387
The parity construction of pairwise independent bits turns two independent fair bits into three pairwise independent random variables. With X=U, Y=V, and Z=U⊕V, their allowed triples are 000,011,101,110, each of probability 1/4. Every coordinate has the Bernoulli distribution with parameter 1/2. Every pair has the same joint probability mass function:
(a,b)P(X=a,Y=b)P(X=a,Z=b)P(Y=a,Z=b)​(0,0)1/41/41/4​(0,1)1/41/41/4​(1,0)1/41/41/4​(1,1)1/41/41/4​​
(4)
All twelve pair cells equal the product of the corresponding probabilities in their marginal distributions. This establishes pairwise independent random variables, a requirement involving every pair of value-events, rather than merely one chosen event per coordinate. For general real-valued random variables Ri​, the requirement is
P(Ri​∈D,Rj​∈E)=P(Ri​∈D)P(Rj​∈E)(i=j)
(5)
for every pair of Borel sets D,E. For these binary random variables, checking all four cells of each pair suffices: any value-event is a union of the checked cells. In fact, for binary random variables, independence of their one-value events also gives the other cells by taking complements. Siegrist gives the all-set definition and its connection to indicator random variables in Random Services: independence of random variables.
The full joint probability mass function tells a different story:
(x,y,z)P(X=x,Y=y,Z=z)P(X=x)P(Y=y)P(Z=z)​0001/41/8​00101/8​01001/8​0111/41/8​10001/8​1011/41/8​1101/41/8​11101/8​​
(6)
The integer parity of X+Y+Z is always even. Any two bits determine the third: Z=X⊕Y, X=Y⊕Z, and Y=X⊕Z. Each coordinate is independent of either other coordinate individually, but is dependent on the pair together. For example, the conditional probabilities satisfy P(Z=1∣X=1)=1/2 and P(Z=1∣X=1,Y=1)=0. See also pairwise independence does not imply joint independence for the general distinction.
The constraint changes the sum's probability distribution without changing its expected value or variance. Let S=X+Y+Z. Only 000 gives S=0; each other allowed triple gives S=2. Direct calculation gives
P(S=0)=41​,P(S=2)=43​,E[S]=0⋅41​+2⋅43​=23​,E[S2]=4⋅43​=3,Var(S)=3−(23​)2=43​.
(7)
The same variance follows from the variance of a sum: each Bernoulli distribution has variance 1/4, and pairwise independent random variables have zero cross covariances. Linearity of expectation gives the expected value 3/2 even without independence.
For comparison, the sum T of three mutually independent fair bits has the binomial distribution Binomial(3,1/2):
tP(T=t)​01/8​13/8​23/8​31/8​​E[T]=23​,Var(T)=43​.
(8)
Both sums have identical expected values and variances, but S never takes the values one or three. Even their third moments differ: E[S3]=6, whereas E[T3]=27/4. Pairwise independence preserves these first two sum summaries while allowing a different joint probability distribution and a different sum law. The calculation is an exact finite example.
