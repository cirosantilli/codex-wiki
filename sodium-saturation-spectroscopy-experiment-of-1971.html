Sodium saturation spectroscopy experiment of 1971

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Laser spectroscopy Doppler-free saturation spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
Theodor W. Hänsch, I. S. Shahin and Arthur Leonard Schawlow reported pulsed dye laser saturation spectroscopy of sodium D lines in 1971. They resolved hyperfine structure; delaying the probe revealed persistent changes in ground-level distributions of velocities from optical pumping. Added argon allowed observation of collisional recovery. These measurements probe population memory as well as line positions.[ref]
The two-level saturation population model explains depletion in a closed transition; the delayed multilevel optical pumping signal needs additional populations and time dependence. A measured Doppler-free saturation spectroscopy feature can still contain natural linewidth, power broadening, pressure broadening, finite interaction time, imperfect alignment and laser spectral width. The word “Doppler-free” removes a particular thermal broadening mechanism, not every limit on resolution.
Arthur Leonard Schawlow's 1981 recognition concerned laser spectroscopy.[ref] Theodor W. Hänsch received a distinct award in 2005 for precision laser spectroscopy.[ref]
