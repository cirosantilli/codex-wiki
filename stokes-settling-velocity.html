Stokes settling velocity (Us​=2(ρp​−ρf​)ga2/(9μ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Suspension (chemistry) Settling velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 321
Take downward as positive and let gravity have magnitude g. For a rigid sphere of density ρp​ in otherwise stationary fluid of density ρf​, its weight minus buoyancy is 34​πa3(ρp​−ρf​)g. At terminal velocity, this balances the Stokes drag law:
6πμaUs​=34​πa3(ρp​−ρf​)g,Us​=9μ2(ρp​−ρf​)ga2​.
(1)
This signed downward settling velocity is negative for a buoyantly rising sphere and zero for equal densities. For fixed density contrast and dynamic viscosity, the submerged weight grows as a3 while linear drag at fixed speed grows as a; their balance gives the settling-velocity radius scaling Us​∝a2. Meanwhile,
Red​=μ2aρf​∣Us​∣​=9μ24ρf​∣ρp​−ρf​∣ga3​.
(2)
Thus doubling the radius multiplies the predicted Stokes settling speed by four and the particle Reynolds number by eight. This makes the formula useful for separating particles by size, while also providing its own check against extrapolation to large particles.
For a computed water example, take ρf​=1000 kgm−3, μ=1.00×10−3 Pas, ρp​=2500 kgm−3, g=9.81 ms−2, and a=10.0 μm. These are specified rounded inputs rather than a claim of exact water properties at every temperature. They give
Us​=3.27×10−4 ms−1=0.327 mms−1,Red​=10−31000(3.27×10−4)(20.0×10−6)​=0.00654.
(3)
The diameter is 20.0 μm, and both the net downward weight and the upward drag force are 6.16×10−11 N. At this terminal velocity, falling 1.00 mm takes 3.06 s. At a=20.0 μm the prediction is 1.308 mms−1 with Red​=0.05232; at a=50.0 μm it is 8.175 mms−1 with Red​=0.8175, which is no longer a convincing small-Reynolds number case. The figure labels these as predictions of the Stokes settling velocity formula and shows where its inertia check fails.
The calculation describes the eventual terminal velocity, not the release transient governed by particle acceleration and possibly the Unsteady Stokes equation. Walls, hydrodynamic interactions, departures from a rigid sphere, or a different surface condition require a different resistance. For sufficiently small particles, Brownian motion can obscure individual settling trajectories; the formula still specifies the gravitational drift within its assumptions.
