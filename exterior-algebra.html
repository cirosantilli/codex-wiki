Exterior algebra (Λ∙V)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 15
The exterior algebra of a vector space V combines scalars, vectors, and higher-dimensional oriented elements in one graded algebra. In a finite-dimensional vector space it is
Λ∙V=⨁k=0dimV​ΛkV
(13)
with multiplication given by the exterior product. Degree zero is the scalar field, degree one is V, and degree two is the space of bivectors. Multiplication adds degrees: combining two vectors produces a bivector, rather than another ordinary vector.[ref]
The essential rule is v∧v=0. Repeating the same direction cannot produce a nonzero oriented area. Bilinearity then gives u∧v=−v∧u: expand (u+v)∧(u+v)=0 and remove the two zero self-products. The alternating rule remains part of the definition in characteristic two, where sign reversal alone would not force a self-product to vanish.
If V=R2 with standard basis e1​,e2​, every element of its exterior algebra has the form
a+be1​+ce2​+d(e1​∧e2​).
(14)
There are four independent coefficients: one scalar, two vector components, and one area component. In R3, the respective degree dimensions are 1,3,3,1, giving eight components in total. More generally, the exterior power basis gives total dimension ∑k=0n​(kn​)=2n.
This language is useful when direction and orientation matter together. Determinants measure the action on top-degree exterior powers; differential forms use exterior powers of a dual space to describe quantities integrated along paths, surfaces, and higher-dimensional regions. The Grassmann algebra viewpoint also makes the sign changes in anticommuting generators systematic. A single multiplication rule connects geometry and algebra.
