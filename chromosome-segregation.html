Chromosome segregation

 Home Biology Genetics Chromosome
OurBigBook.com Words: 160 Articles: 3
Chromosome segregation allocates chromosomes to the products of division. Mitosis separates sister chromatids, preserving ploidy in completed daughter cell nuclei. Meiosis I separates homologous chromosomes with their sister chromatids still associated, reducing diploid to haploid ploidy; meiosis II separates the sister chromatids. Only the first of the two divisions reduces the number of complete sets.[ref]
For example, correct chromosome segregation of the stipulated three homologous chromosome pairs gives each meiosis I product one A, one B and one D, each replicated. Giving one product both A members and the other none would preserve the total DNA across products while failing to give each a complete set. Conservation of material alone does not prove correct chromosome segregation.
