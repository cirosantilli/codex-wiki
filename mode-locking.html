Mode locking

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Optical frequency comb
OurBigBook.com Words: 337 Articles: 3
Mode locking fixes relative phases between longitudinal normal modes of a laser, allowing short optical pulses. The same optical spectrum with wandering relative phases would not supply that stable pulse train.[ref]
Here is an original finite-mode model, with the convention that a positive frequency has time dependence e2πift:
E(t)=∑n=2440​an​e2πi(nfr​+f0​)t,an​=∑j=2440​e−(j−32)2/18e−(n−32)2/18​.
(14)
These field coefficients follow a Gaussian function and have aligned phases at t=0. Choose f0​=fr​/4 and write T=1/fr​. Since e2πin=1 for every integer n,
E(t+T)=e2πif0​TE(t)=iE(t),∣E(t+T)∣2=∣E(t)∣2.
(15)
The pulse light intensity repeats every T although the field needs four periods to repeat. At the pulse centers, E(0)=1, E(T)=i, E(2T)=−1 and E(3T)=−i. A detector of light intensity alone therefore cannot distinguish this offset from zero. This model has far fewer teeth than a practical optical comb; its normalized time and frequency scales are chosen to make the carrier oscillations visible.
A short proof of the converse uses the Fourier series. If E(t+T)=eiΔϕE(t), then E(t)e−iΔϕt/T is periodic with period T. Its Fourier series has frequencies n/T; restoring the removed factor shifts every tooth by Δϕ/(2πT). A finite observation time broadens the spectral peaks; it does not change this ideal infinite-train relation.
