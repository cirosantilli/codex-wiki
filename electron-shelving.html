Electron shelving

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Ion trap Trapped-ion quantum control
OurBigBook.com Words: 305 Articles: 1
Electron shelving distinguishes an internal quantum state outside a cycling optical transition from one that scatters many photons. In the mercury-ion example in David J. Wineland's lecture, S↔P excitation produces 194 nm fluorescence; transfer to a metastable atomic level D suppresses it. A photomultiplier records bright/dark intervals. Coherent 282 nm interrogation precedes detection to avoid perturbation by the detection beam.[ref]
“Dark” means weak scattering under a particular detection setting. The motional ground state is dark to an ideal red motional sideband for a different reason: there is no lower number state. It can still fluoresce strongly on a cycling detection transition. Shelving's population readout also need not preserve a quantum superposition's quantum coherence; scattered photons carry information about the internal state, and their recoil can disturb motion.
