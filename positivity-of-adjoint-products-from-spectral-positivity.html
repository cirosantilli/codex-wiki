Positivity of adjoint products from spectral positivity (a∗a≥0)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Banach algebra C-star algebra Positive element of a C-star algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 127
Define positivity spectrally: a self-adjoint element has nonnegative spectrum. First, addition preserves it. For p,q≥0 and s=∥p∥+∥q∥, functional calculus gives ∥s1−p−q∥≤s, forcing every real spectral value of p+q to be nonnegative. The cone is proper because a self-adjoint element with spectrum contained in {0} has norm zero.
For h=a∗a, let h−​=max(−h,0) by the continuous functional calculus, and set c=ah−1/2​. Then c∗c=−h−2​ is nonpositive. The nonzero spectra of products in opposite orders imply that cc∗ is nonpositive too. Write c=u+iv with u,v self-adjoint. Then c∗c+cc∗=2(u2+v2) is positive, since self-adjoint squares have nonnegative spectrum. It is also nonpositive, hence zero. It follows that c∗c=−cc∗ is both positive and nonpositive, hence zero. The C-star identity gives c=0, so h−2​=0 and h−​=0. Thus h≥0, without assuming positivity of adjoint products in an earlier step.
