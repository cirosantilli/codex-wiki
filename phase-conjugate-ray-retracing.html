Phase-conjugate ray retracing

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Phase conjugation Phase-conjugate mirror
OurBigBook.com Words: 263
Consider an optical point source at (z,x)=(0,0), a uniform transparent medium, and a flat return surface z=L. A light ray with slope s reaches (L,sL). A phase-conjugate mirror reverses its full direction and returns it to (0,0). An ordinary mirror reverses only its normal direction; its returning light ray reaches x=2sL at z=0. Thus rays with s=−1/4,0,1/4 at L=3 return to x=−3/2,0,3/2 with ordinary specular reflection, but all return to x=0 under ideal phase conjugation. These are stipulated geometric coordinates, not laboratory measurements.
Solid blue curves mark outward wavefronts; dashed curves mark returning wavefronts. In the phase-conjugate mirror panel the returning curves coincide with the outward ones, and the arrows reverse on the same light rays. The diagram uses equal spatial scales. In geometrical optics, reversing each light ray in an unchanged medium with wave reciprocity retraces its route. At the optical point source itself, the ray construction becomes singular and diffraction sets the physical focal size.
A wave-level proof outline applies to a stationary scalar medium with real refractive index n(r) and no wave absorption. Writing c for the vacuum speed of light, its Helmholtz equation is
∇2U+k02​n(r)2U=0,k0​=ω/c.
(19)
All coefficients are real, so complex conjugation gives the same Helmholtz equation for U∗. The conjugated outgoing boundary data select the incoming solution. This explains the connection between phase conjugation and retracing, provided the required incoming field is supplied over the necessary aperture. It also exposes the limits: wave reciprocity alone does not imply absence of wave absorption; conjugation of complex material coefficients would describe a different medium. Loss, an incomplete aperture, a changing medium or failure of wave reciprocity can prevent exact reconstruction. Intensity-dependent material changes need a separate analysis and are not covered by this linear proof.
