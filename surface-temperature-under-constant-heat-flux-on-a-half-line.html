Surface temperature under constant heat flux on a half-line (u(0,t)=kπ​2Qκt​​)

For thermal diffusivity κ>0 and thermal conductivity k>0, a semi-infinite conductor satisfies ut​=κuxx​ for x>0. Take zero initial temperature and constant incoming heat flux −kux​(0,t)=Q. Select the physical solution by boundedness at spatial infinity together with sufficient growth control for the time Laplace transform.
Writing the time transform as U gives Uxx​=(p/κ)U. The decaying branch is U=A(p)e−xp/κ​, where Rep​>0. The transformed flux condition gives A=Qκ​/(kp3/2). Since L[2t​]=π​p−3/2, inversion gives the displayed surface temperature. More generally the full profile is
u(x,t)=kQ​[2πκt​​e−x2/(4κt)−xerfc(2κt​x​)].
(16)
Differentiation verifies the heat equation and the applied flux; for every x>0 it tends to zero at initial time and decays at spatial infinity. Its surface temperature grows as t​, because the depth significantly heated by diffusion has the same scale.
