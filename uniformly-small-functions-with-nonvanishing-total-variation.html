Uniformly small functions with nonvanishing total variation (fn​(t)=n−1sin(2πnt),V01​(fn​)=4)

For each positive integer n, put fn​(t)=sin(2πnt)/n on [0,1]. Its supremum norm is exactly 1/n, so this sequence of functions converges to zero by uniform convergence. Nevertheless,
V01​(fn​)=4for every n≥1.​
(84)
Each of its n complete sine oscillations contributes 4/n: rise from zero to 1/n, fall through zero to −1/n, then return to zero. Taking a partition of an interval through all extrema attains 4. Every further partition refinement merely subdivides monotone function segments, so the same reasoning as in partition example of total variation proves the upper bound too.
For a continuously differentiable function on a closed bounded interval, total variation also satisfies
Vab​(f)=∫ab​∣f′(t)∣dt.
(85)
Applied here, the derivative is 2πcos(2πnt), and integrating its absolute value over n periods gives 4. The differentiability hypothesis matters: the partition definition applies much more widely than this derivative formula.[ref] Small pointwise size and small accumulated movement are different approximation goals.
