University of Florida HCl and DCl FTIR experiment

The University of Florida's Rotation Vibration Spectrum of the HCl Molecule, University of Florida laboratory protocol, printed pp. 2–3 and 10–11, uses a scanning Michelson interferometer for Fourier transform spectroscopy. An air-filled silicon-window cell supplies the background. Supervised preparation adds concentrated hydrogen chloride liquid; the cleaned cell subsequently receives DCl liquid. Acquisition settings are identical.
The readout is transmission or absorbance against spectroscopic wavenumber, resolving molecular rotation alongside stretching. Background addresses instrument and cell contributions; cleaning addresses carryover. Excessive absorption impairs analysis. Assigning isotopes and rotational branches precedes fitting band parameters to test isotope scaling of diatomic vibration. These are prescribed measurements, not numerical observations supplied here.[ref]
For a transparent ideal model, add the linear rigid rotor to the harmonic vibrational energy levels and assume the same frequency-valued rotational constant B in both vibrational states:
Ev,J​=V0​+hνharm​(v+1/2)+hBJ(J+1).
(33)
For a spin-free diatomic molecule with no electronic angular momentum, electric-dipole selection rules give ΔJ=±1 for this vibrational absorption. Subtracting initial from final energy eigenvalues yields
νR​(J)νP​(J)​=νharm​+2B(J+1),=νharm​−2BJ,​​J=0,1,2,…,J=1,2,3,…,B=B/c.​
(34)
These are two rotational branches about the vibrational band origin, not a single stretch line. For stipulated dimensionless values νharm​=100 and B=1, the first three R-branch positions are 102,104,106, and the first three P-branch positions are 98,96,94. There is no J=0→−1 transition. The numbers illustrate level subtraction; they are not HCl measurements. Real upper and lower rotational constants differ, and centrifugal distortion shifts lines further.
