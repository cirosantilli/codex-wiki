For an equally populated ideal pair of angular momentum eigenstates, the magnetic moment component takes two values, ±μB​, and the displacements at fixed velocity are ±d(v). Compare a specified classical alternative: every chemical atom carries a permanent magnetic moment of fixed magnitude μB​, with independently isotropic initial orientations and no dissipative alignment. If u=cosθ, the sphere's area element is 2πdu, so u has a uniform distribution on [−1,1]. With precession preserving this component in the ideal geometry, the displacement d(v)u fills the interval [−d(v),d(v)] continuously. A pair of bands therefore tests allowed components rather than merely proving that silver is magnetic. Other classical preparation assumptions would be different models and must be stated.

Now choose a uniform distribution of forward velocities from 400 to 600ms−1, explicitly as an example rather than the oven's actual distribution. Average either conditional probability density function over that interval. The positive-channel displacement ranges from 0.1776 to 0.3995mm, since slower chemical atoms spend longer accelerating and drifting. The two components broaden into bands even though their internal magnetic moment values remain discrete. The corresponding classical profile continues to populate the center. Conversely, a broad distribution of velocities need not create a new internal component: it spreads each existing component through the v−2 dependence. Further instrumental limits include collisions, aperture clipping of strongly deflected trajectories, asymmetric pole geometry, and nonlinear film development. Field-off widths and a mapped magnetic field gradient are needed to separate such effects. For a modern counting experiment, channel calibration and efficiency corrections belong to Stern-Gerlach expectation estimation; spatial separation alone does not supply those statistics.
