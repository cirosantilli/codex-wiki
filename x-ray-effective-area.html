X-ray effective area (Aeff​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Observational astronomy X-ray astronomy
OurBigBook.com Words: 541 Articles: 1
X-ray effective area is the area that converts an incident photon arrival rate per unit area into an accepted count rate. For a fixed direction and a sufficiently narrow energy interval, write
Aeff​=Ageom​Tηϵs​,Ns​=FAeff​t.
(47)
Here Ageom​ is projected geometric area, T is transmittance through windows or optics, η is quantum efficiency, ϵs​ is the probability that a detected source event passes event selection, and t is live observing time. Ns​ is an expected value, not a guarantee of an integer count. Multiplication follows by counting incident photons over area and time, then applying the successive conditional acceptance probabilities. Live time already excludes intervals when the instrument cannot record events; multiplying by a live-time fraction again would count the same loss twice.
For an invented narrow-band example, choose Ageom​=40cm2, T=0.8, η=0.75, and ϵs​=5/6. Then Aeff​=20cm2. A source with F=0.30photonscm−2s−1 observed for t=100s gives Ns​=600. F here counts photons; converting it into observed radiative flux requires their energies. If all have energy E0​, the corresponding radiative flux is E0​F.
A broad spectrum instead requires
Ns​=t∫FE​(E)Aeff​(E)dE,
(48)
where FE​ is the differential photon arrival rate per area and energy. A single band-averaged X-ray effective area depends on the assumed spectrum. This is why a count rate alone cannot give an assumption-free radiative flux.
