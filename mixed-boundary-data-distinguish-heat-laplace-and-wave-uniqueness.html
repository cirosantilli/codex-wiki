Mixed boundary data distinguish heat, Laplace and wave uniqueness (u(x,0)=u(0,y)=ux​(1,y)=0)

On the unit square, these data determine only the zero solution of the heat equation uy​=uxx​. The squared L2 norm decreases because its derivative is −2∫ux2​dx, and its initial value is zero. For Laplace's equation, sin(πx/2)sinh(πy/2) is a nonzero solution with the same three data; adding zero Dirichlet data on the top edge restores uniqueness by Green's first identity. For the wave equation uyy​=uxx​, sin(πx/2)sin(πy/2) is a counterexample; the missing datum is initial velocity. Adding uy​(x,0)=0 restores uniqueness by conservation of 21​∫(ux2​+uy2​)dx. Thus the type of equation determines what information is needed on an unprescribed edge.
