Ball (mathematics, Bd​(a,r))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Topological analysis Metric space
OurBigBook.com Words: 172
In a metric space (X,d), an open ball of radius r>0 about a is Bd​(a,r)={x∈X:d(a,x)<r}. A closed ball instead uses d(a,x)≤r. The strict inequality matters: a point at distance exactly r belongs to the closed ball and is excluded from the open ball. In particular, (1,0) is excluded from all three open unit balls above, and included in all three closed unit balls.
The three closed unit balls centred at zero are ∣x1​∣+∣x2​∣≤1, x12​+x22​≤1, and max{∣x1​∣,∣x2​∣}≤1. Their shapes are a diamond (a rotated square), a disk with a circle as its boundary, and an axis-aligned square. A ball need not look round! The corresponding open balls are the interiors of these shapes. At (0.8,0.8), Manhattan distance from zero is 1.6 and Euclidean distance is 1.28​≈1.131, whereas Chebyshev distance is 0.8: only the square's open unit ball contains this point.
These shapes illustrate B1​(0,1)⊆B2​(0,1)⊆B∞​(0,1), with either the open ball or closed ball convention used consistently. Their outlines are included in the displayed closed balls. In an arbitrary metric space, however, points at distance exactly r need not form the topological boundary; the discrete metric below shows why this distinction matters.
