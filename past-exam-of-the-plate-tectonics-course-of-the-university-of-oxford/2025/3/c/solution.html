Test geological associations and their chronology. The most persuasive hypothetical record would combine rigid-block relative motion, creation of oceanic lithosphere, its destruction at a coherent margin, and repeated assembly and dispersal of continents. I would compile independently dated rock and structural occurrences in age bins, map their associations, and distinguish primary formation from later emplacement. The aim is to evaluate alternative tectonic regimes, not simply to collect the oldest example of a familiar name.
Oceanic lithosphere and sutures. An ophiolite association containing residual peridotite, gabbro, sheeted dikes and submarine basalt could demonstrate construction of an oceanic section. Its case for plate tectonics strengthens if it is associated with an oceanward sedimentary succession, a metamorphic sole, an accretionary prism or mélange, and eventual emplacement along a suture zone between formerly distinct blocks. Date construction, metamorphic heating and emplacement separately. A dismembered collection of ultramafic and mafic rocks need not be a complete ophiolite; an isolated volcanic pile or greenstone belt can also arise in a plateau or vertical-overturn setting. Hotter early mantle may produce oceanic sections unlike a modern template.
Cold burial beside a hot arc. Blueschist and eclogite with well-constrained low temperature/pressure ratios would support unusually deep burial before substantial heating. Coesite or metamorphic diamond in a suitable continental crust protolith could establish ultrahigh-pressure metamorphism, consistent with deep continental burial and return. Reconstruct pressure-temperature-time paths using mineral assemblages, zoning, reaction textures and radiometric dating; a peak pressure without its timing and burial geometry gives a weaker constraint. Eclogite alone only indicates appropriate pressure-temperature-composition conditions and may also form at the base of thick crust.
An approximately coeval, spatially organized pair of low thermal-gradient and high thermal-gradient metamorphic belts would be stronger evidence for juxtaposition of a cold descending plate and a hotter volcanic arc or hinterland. Test the original spatial relation rather than pairing unrelated rocks brought together much later. A statistical study of metamorphic temperature/pressure distributions illustrates how gradual widening and bimodality can be used to evaluate changing tectonic behavior. The ratio is an apparent metamorphic thermal gradient, not necessarily a directly measured steady geothermal gradient.[ref]
The absence of old blueschist is not a decisive absence of old subduction. A hotter thermal regime may suppress its production, and later heating can transform it. The test should therefore include the distribution of pressure-temperature-time paths predicted by early-planet models, rather than demanding the youngest modern mineral assemblages at all ages.
Kinematic structures and basin evolution. Long, coherent thrust belts, imbricated sediments, folds, regionally consistent shear sense, major strike-slip faults, and suture zones can identify lateral shortening and relative displacement. A reconstructed ocean-facing volcanic arc, forearc basin and accretionary prism with a common polarity is more compelling than isolated shortening. Mountain building alone is not exclusive to plate tectonics: vertical instabilities and impacts can also deform rock. The length scale, sequence, strain partitioning and linkage between structures matter.
For repeated opening and closing, seek a dated progression from continental rifting, through a subsiding passive margin and an intervening oceanic assemblage, to convergence, continental collision and renewed rifting. Matching stratigraphy, sedimentary provenance and structural restoration on opposing blocks can test whether a reconstructed Wilson cycle is geometrically possible. Paleomagnetism from independently dated, demonstrably primary magnetizations could show differential latitudinal motion and rotation of separate blocks. Relative motion between blocks is more useful than motion of one block, which could include true polar wander. Remagnetization, inclination shallowing and longitude ambiguity must be assessed.
Secular changes require an archive model. Compile the incidence, spatial scale and recurrence of these associations through time, normalized where possible by exposed rock area, sampling effort and dating opportunities. Include detrital zircon and sedimentary provenance where they preserve information from eroded source rocks, while recognizing zircon fertility and sedimentary sorting biases. Increased abundance of plate-like associations in several independent regions could support their transition from local episodes to a widespread regime. A sharp break is only one possible outcome; a progressive increase in persistence or geographical coverage would support gradual establishment.

with additional redistribution between age bins if ages are reset or misassigned. This is not a calibrated dating law: it shows why an observed first appearance need not equal a true first formation. Subduction consumes oceanic lithosphere; erosion, metamorphism, tectonic reworking and burial selectively remove or obscure evidence. The oldest archive is especially incomplete, and some apparently diagnostic assemblages only become likely as the planet cools. An age peak may measure enhanced preservation during continental collision rather than an increased production rate.
State the chronological inference precisely. If an unambiguous assemblage forms at time te​, then the required process was operating by te​; an onset time measured forward from planetary formation obeys tonset​≤te​. If both are expressed as ages before present, the inequality reverses: the onset age is at least as old as the assemblage age. Constraining how much earlier onset could have occurred requires well-sampled older records and a credible prediction that the sought evidence would have formed and survived. Several coeval, independent records consistent with sustained horizontal recycling give a stronger inference of widespread plate tectonics than the oldest ambiguous occurrence of any single rock type. Combined with geodynamic modelling and geochemistry, the defensible answer would report a range for first local activity and a potentially different range for establishment of a persistent global system.
