The supplied plot gives surface heat flux, not a whole-crust mean temperature. At young ages its scatter is substantial. Representative readings suitable for an order-of-magnitude conductive calculation are qJ​≃0.20Wm−2 near 5 Ma and qN​≃0.070Wm−2 near 50 Ma; plausible graphical ranges are roughly 0.12–0.23 and 0.06–0.09Wm−2, respectively. These are approximate choices within the plotted population, not uniquely determined statistical means or a digitization of its red curve.
Assume the same normal oceanic crustal thickness hc​=7 km, seafloor temperature Ts​≃0∘C, negligible internal crustal heating and a linear conductive profile. These are additional assumptions; the paper supplies conductivity but not thickness. With depth z positive downward and q the magnitude of the upward flux, Fourier's law gives
T(z)=Ts​+kqz​,Tc​=hc​1​∫0hc​​T(z)dz=Ts​+2kqhc​​.
(3)
This is the crustal mean temperature from conductive heat flow, not the basal temperature, which is twice the excess above Ts​. Substitution of k=1.3Wm−1K−1 gives
TJ​≃2×1.30.20×7000​=538∘C,TN​≃2×1.30.070×7000​=188∘C.
(4)
A sensible rounded answer is therefore about 540∘C for the young crust and 190∘C for the old crust, with a representative contrast ΔTc​≃350 K. The corresponding basal values are about 1080 and 380∘C. The quoted graphical ranges instead yield mean temperatures about 320–620 and 160–240∘C, at the same assumed thickness.
The temperatures scale linearly with hc​/k. Hydrothermal circulation can redistribute heat, especially in young permeable crust, and measurements through sediment need not equal the purely conductive cooling-model flux everywhere. The supplied study explicitly addresses the resulting oceanic heat-flow deficit.[ref] Thus the calculation gives plausible incoming-crust averages under a stated model, not the entire plate's temperature or its later temperature at depth in the subduction zone.
