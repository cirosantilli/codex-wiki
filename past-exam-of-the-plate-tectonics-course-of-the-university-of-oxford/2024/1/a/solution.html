A useful idealization is a thin sedimentary cover over a felsic upper continental crust, an intermediate middle continental crust, and a more mafic lower continental crust, ending at the Mohorovičić discontinuity at about 35–40 km. The boundaries within the continental crust are approximate compositional divisions, not three universal sharp interfaces.
  • At the surface, a variable, commonly kilometre-scale sedimentary cover contains sediments and sedimentary rocks. Exposed crystalline basement can instead occur at the surface.
  • In the upper roughly 15 km, representative rocks are granite, granodiorite and compositionally related gneiss, with quartz and feldspar. Sedimentary rocks, metamorphic rocks and volcanic rocks make this layer heterogeneous.
  • At roughly 15–25 km, intermediate gneiss and amphibolite-facies assemblages become important. Diorite to granodiorite compositions and amphibole- and plagioclase-bearing rocks are representative.
  • At roughly 25–38 km, mafic granulite and gabbro-derived metamorphic rocks are a useful average, with plagioclase, pyroxene and sometimes garnet. Some provinces instead have substantially intermediate or felsic lower continental crust.
  • Below the Mohorovičić discontinuity is peridotite of the mantle, dominated by olivine and pyroxene. This is not a fourth crustal layer.
The important trend is increasing average mafic character with depth, rather than a change from granite directly to peridotite. Granulite describes a metamorphic facies, not a unique chemical composition. The synthesis in Nature and composition of the continental crust: A lower crustal perspective by Roberta L. Rudnick and David M. Fountain combines seismic and petrological evidence for these distinctions.
