The expected broad contrast is a less mature, commonly thinner early juvenile continental crust, with more mafic material and abundant sodic tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite, rather than the more differentiated, potassium-rich granitoid component characteristic of much Phanerozoic continental crust. This is a secular tendency, not a single stratified column applicable throughout the Archean.
Archean terrains commonly expose tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite gneiss associated with greenstone belts containing basalt, komatiite and metasedimentary rocks. Many later granitoids are richer in potassium, including granite and granodiorite. The proportions changed during the Archean: a reconstruction from trace element ratios in sedimentary rocks infers a markedly mafic upper continental crust before about 3.0 Ga and a more felsic upper continental crust by 2.5 Ga. Thus “all Archean continental crust was mafic” would be wrong. A preserved tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite-dominated nucleus can be strongly felsic, including at depth; its composition is not identical to that of its original juvenile precursor.[ref]
Thickness needs a stated comparison. An influential seismic compilation found preserved Archean crust about 27–40 km thick, commonly without the thick high-velocity basal layer of its 40–55 km thick Proterozoic comparison provinces. That supports a relatively thin endmember, but is not a measurement of every original Archean continental crust, nor a demonstration that every Archean region was thinner than today's 35–40 km reference column. Tectonic thickening, magmatic underplating, erosion, delamination and preservation bias all alter what survives. The very thick lithospheric mantle beneath a craton must also not be mistaken for thick continental crust.[ref]
The transferable answer is therefore more mafic early juvenile precursors, widespread sodic tonalite-trondhjemite-granodiorite production, and variable thickness evolving through time; late Archean felsic nuclei can already resemble substantial modern continental crust.
