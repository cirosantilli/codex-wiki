Two-particle relative coordinate (r=r2​−r1​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Center of mass Reduced mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 300
Choose positions in one inertial frame and let M=m1​+m2​. The centre of mass and two-particle relative coordinate are
R=Mm1​r1​+m2​r2​​,r=r2​−r1​,r1​=R−Mm2​​r,r2​=R+Mm1​​r.
(59)
The lighter body is farther from the centre of mass. Reconstructing both positions from these equations is part of solving the two-body problem; r alone describes their separation, not either body's laboratory position.[ref]
Substitute the reconstructed velocities into the kinetic energy. The result is the exact two-particle instance of kinetic-energy decomposition about the centre of mass:
T=21​m1​∣r˙1​∣2+21​m2​∣r˙2​∣2=21​M∣R˙∣2+21​μ∣r˙∣2.
(60)
The mixed dot product has coefficient −m1​m2​/M+m2​m1​/M=0; the coefficient of ∣r˙∣2 is (m1​m22​+m2​m12​)/M2=μ. This algebra needs constant positive masses but no particular force law. In the stationary centre of mass frame, the fraction of internal kinetic energy carried by body 1 is m2​/M, and by body 2 is m1​/M: the lighter body carries more.
For a closed pair with no external forces, write F for the force on body 2 and −F for that on body 1, as required by Newton's third law. Adding and subtracting their Newton's second law equations yields
MR¨=0,r¨=(m2​1​+m1​1​)F,μr¨=F.
(61)
Thus the centre of mass moves uniformly while the two-particle relative coordinate behaves like one body of reduced mass. Equal-opposite internal forces suffice for these equations. For an internal central force, F=f(r)r with r=∣r∣>0, the internal torque vanishes and conservation of angular momentum gives constant Lrel​=μr×r˙. If the central force comes from a time-independent potential energy U(r), then f(r)=−dU/dr and the internal mechanical energy Erel​=μ∣r˙∣2/2+U(r) is conserved. The kinetic energy split alone does not establish either conservation law.
