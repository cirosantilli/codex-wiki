Mathematical logic

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics
OurBigBook.com Words: 25k Articles: 518
Mathematical logic studies formal languages, formal proofs, model theory, computability theory, and the foundations of mathematics. It makes the rules of reasoning explicit enough that reasoning itself becomes an object of mathematics. Proof theory studies deductions; model theory studies interpretations in which statements hold.
Three questions organize the subject: is an expression a well-formed first-order formula, is it true in a specified first-order structure, and does it have a formal proof from specified axioms? These questions are distinct. For example, ∃n(n+n=1) is a first-order sentence in an appropriate first-order language; it is false in the natural numbers but true in the rational numbers, with n=1/2.
The beauty of mathematical logic is that the same logical connectives and rules of universal quantification and existential quantification organize arguments across mathematics. Classical propositional logic uses two truth values; Intuitionistic propositional logic interprets logical implication through constructions on proofs, without unrestricted law of excluded middle. This proof-based framework is developed in Frank Pfenning, Natural Deduction, sections 1–3.
