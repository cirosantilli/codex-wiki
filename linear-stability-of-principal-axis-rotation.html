Linear stability of principal-axis rotation

For 0<I1​<I2​<I3​, the transverse linear stability rates about steady rotation with signed component Ω=0 are
ν1​=∣Ω∣I2​I3​(I2​−I1​)(I3​−I1​)​​,ν3​=∣Ω∣I1​I2​(I3​−I2​)(I3​−I1​)​​,λ2​=∣Ω∣I1​I3​(I2​−I1​)(I3​−I2​)​​.
(12)
Axes one and three have transverse eigenvalues ±iν1​ and ±iν3​, producing bounded oscillations. Axis two has transverse eigenvalues ±λ2​, giving growing and decaying exponential functions. A neutral longitudinal perturbation corresponds to a small change of Ω. Generic intermediate-axis disturbances contain a growing component; a precisely chosen decaying direction is exceptional. For the normalized intermediate-axis model with ∣Ω∣=1, the values are ν1​=1/3​, ν3​=1 and λ2​=1/3​.
Proof outline: the linearization of the Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body about the third principal axis gives transverse perturbations obeying
ω˙1​=I1​I2​−I3​​Ωω2​,ω˙2​=I2​I3​−I1​​Ωω1​,
(13)
and hence
ω¨1​=−Ω2I1​I2​(I3​−I2​)(I3​−I1​)​ω1​.
(14)
Cyclic permutation gives the other rates. For nonlinear Lyapunov stability of the extreme rotations, use the torque-free rigid-body invariants: the conserved quadratic forms L2−2I1​E and 2I3​E−L2 have strictly positive transverse coefficients about axes one and three respectively. They bound transverse angular velocity, while E bounds its longitudinal change near nonzero rotation. Imaginary transverse eigenvalues alone would not establish this nonlinear conclusion.[ref]
The practical lesson is to choose a stable principal axis when using rotation to maintain an instrument's pointing direction. Both extreme principal moments of inertia are stable in the ideal torque-free model; if internal damping lowers rotational kinetic energy while approximately preserving angular momentum, the minimum energy E=L2/(2I3​) instead favors the largest principal moment of inertia. A control system must account for torque, changing mass distributions and flexibility before applying this ideal result.
