A discovery becomes more intelligible when its motivating question, proposed explanation and actual measurement can be followed together. The photoelectric effect connects Albert Einstein's light-quantum reasoning with the Millikan photoelectric experiment. The Schrödinger equation and the Davisson–Germer experiment connect mathematical wave mechanics with a laboratory test of matter waves.
The Nobel Prize in Physics provides one route through these subjects. Following the original papers and the people who built the apparatus also brings collaborators into view: Lester Halbert Germer appears alongside Clinton J. Davisson in the Davisson–Germer experiment. The history belongs in the articles on those people and concepts as well as in award records.
