Nuclear shell closure

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Nuclear shell model Magic number
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
Filling the accessible one-species nuclear shell model states below a substantial energy gap. A closed spherical j level has all mj​ values occupied, so their sum is zero. In the independent-particle picture, a closed core therefore contributes zero total angular momentum; a single particle outside it, or a single missing particle inside it, supplies the leading spin assignment. Residual interactions can change this simple picture.
The three-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator alone has one-species shell capacities (q+1)(q+2) for oscillator index q=0,1,…, including spin. Summing gives closures 2,8,20,40,70,112,…, rather than the complete traditional magic number sequence. Strong nuclear spin-orbit splitting can separate a high-j level from its former neighbors. Realistic radial potential energies and residual interactions also matter: a chosen oscillator plus an arbitrary single constant is not a demonstrated explanation of every real nuclear gap.
Traditional nuclear shell closures can weaken along isotope chains far from stability, as in neutron-rich magnesium.[ref]
The relevant test is the actual energy gap and the correlated nuclear quantum states, rather than membership in an immutable numerical list.
