Foil capacitor dielectric experiment

Southern Methodist University's teaching lab, Introduction to the Parallel Plate Capacitor Lab, makes the response tangible with a capacitor assembled from aluminium foil, paper and taped wire contacts. Its instructions specify approximately 10cm×10cm foil rectangles, aligned with the paper completely separating them. A multimeter in capacitance mode supplies the readout; a ruler measures plate dimensions and a micrometer measures specimen thickness. The lab replaces the paper with other sheets, including cardstock, plastic wrap and wax paper. It asks students to keep foil area and alignment consistent, measure thickness at several positions, estimate thickness uncertainty, and wait for the capacitance reading to stabilize. These are published preparation and measurement instructions, not measured results reported here.[ref]
The inference uses each specimen's own thickness t, not just a comparison of raw meter readings. With corrected capacitance Cs​ and overlapping area A, the ideal estimate is ϵr​=Cs​t/(ϵ0​A). A thicker sheet can give a smaller capacitance despite larger relative permittivity. This is why changing material while letting the plate gap change does not isolate electric susceptibility by itself.
Useful additional controls are to keep leads in the same positions, use capacitance measurement calibration, repeat one sheet after the others to check drift, and repeat the assembly to assess contact reproducibility. An air-gap reference must have known spacers maintaining the same separation; removing a supporting sheet and letting the foil touch is not such a reference. Record the instrument's test frequency and measurement mode when available. A capacitance display alone does not establish negligible dielectric loss or a strictly static permittivity. These are proposed controls extending the published teaching instructions.
Air gaps produce a particularly revealing systematic error. Suppose a uniform sheet of thickness t and relative permittivity ϵr​ is separated from the electrodes by air layers of total thickness g. Approximate air permittivity by vacuum permittivity, neglect the fringing field of a capacitor and use the same area in each layer. The common normal electric displacement field has magnitude D=Q/A, so integrating the electric field through the layers gives

ϵr​=ϵ0​ACs​t​=1+ϵr​g/tϵr​​.
(16)
For the stipulated model ϵr​=3 and g=t/10, this gives ϵr​=30/13≃2.308, not three. These values are calculated examples, not measured sheet properties. If total plate separation d=t+g is used instead, the effective estimate is (t+g)/(g+t/ϵr​); it still mixes the sheet and the air. Thus measuring a thickness accurately cannot compensate for an unrecognized layer.
The fringing field of a capacitor and additive lead capacitance further limit a foil measurement. Keep lateral plate dimensions large relative to separation, and use a guard electrode of a capacitor in a suitable fixture for more demanding work.[ref] A family of thickness measurements can help distinguish material response from an additive offset, but air gaps can vary with the assembly. A large, repeatable reading supports a capacitance change; extracting a material electric susceptibility requires the geometry, gaps, instrument response and dielectric loss assumptions to survive these checks. No measured relative permittivity is claimed for any of the lab's sheets.
