Euler characteristic of a cube boundary and its interior (χ(∂[0,1]3)=2,χ([0,1]3)=1)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Homology (mathematics) Simplicial homology Euler characteristic
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
The boundary of a cube has eight vertices, twelve edges and six square faces. Treating each open face as a two-dimensional topological cell gives
χ(∂[0,1]3)=8−12+6=2.​
(23)
This agrees with its being a two-sphere: stretching the hollow polyhedral surface changes its geometry but not its topology. The interior is excluded from this space.
Now include the solid interior. The cube has the same lower-dimensional topological cells, together with one open three-dimensional topological cell. The cell-count formula for Euler characteristic becomes
χ([0,1]3)=8−12+6−1=1.​
(24)
The filled cube is contractible, so its homology agrees with that of a point. The term −1 is essential: V−E+F by itself is the full count only for a two-dimensional CW complex.
Adding a diagonal within one square face replaces that face by two triangular faces and introduces one edge. Thus the Euler characteristic changes by −1+1=0, as in subdivision invariance of Euler characteristic. Including an interior is a different operation: it adds points to the space, rather than giving the same space a finer description. This distinction matters whenever a geometric model could mean either a surface or the solid it encloses.
