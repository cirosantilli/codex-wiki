Conditional expectation preserving a distribution (L(E[X∣G])=L(X)⟹X=E[X∣G] a.s.)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 105
For a real integrable random variable X, a conditional expectation Y=E[X∣G] with the same probability distribution as X must equal X almost surely. No second moment is needed. For any real c, the nonnegative random variable Dc​=∣X−c∣−sign(Y−c)(X−c) has expected value zero, by conditioning and equality of the two absolute moments. Thus Y>c implies X≥c, Y<c implies X≤c, and Y=c implies X=c, outside a null set. Using a countable family of rational c separates any unequal real values and proves the claim. Equality of the probabilities of X=c and Y=c also gives equality of their zero sets and hence equality of the two sign functions for every fixed c.
