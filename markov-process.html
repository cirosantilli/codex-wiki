Markov process

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 24k Articles: 425
A Markov process is a stochastic process whose present state contains all the information in its observed past needed to predict its future. Precisely, if Fs​ is the natural filtration generated by observations up to time s, then, for t>s and any measurable set A of states,
P(Xt​∈A∣Fs​)=P(Xt​∈A∣Xs​).
(1)
The equality is between conditional probabilities and holds almost surely. It expresses the Markov property: the past and future have conditional independence given the present. It does not assert independence of consecutive states. In the sunny/rainy example below, tomorrow's sunny probability is 0.8 after sunshine but 0.4 after rain, so knowing today's state matters.
The memory assumption concerns the chosen state space. If an equipment model's failure probability depends on its age, recording only “working” or “failed” may lose relevant information; recording age as part of the state can retain it. Similarly, a dependence on two previous observations can sometimes be represented by using their ordered pair as the current state. Whether such a description fits observations is a modeling question.
Transition Markov kernels describe the conditional distribution of a state at a later time. Successive kernels and the initial probability distribution determine every finite-dimensional distribution, through repeated conditional probability. The time-homogeneous Markov property is an additional assumption: the kernel depends on elapsed time rather than calendar time. The attraction is compression: prediction can proceed from a current state instead of an entire recorded history. Brownian motion and continuous-time Markov chains illustrate that a Markov process need not evolve in discrete time.

Random affine recursion (Xn​=An​Xn−1​+Bn​)

Words: 287 Articles: 2
Markov process OurBigBook.com
A random affine recursion updates a real random variable by Xn​=An​Xn−1​+Bn​. Assume the coefficient pairs (An​,Bn​) are independent and identically distributed random variables, independent of the initial value. The two coordinates within a pair may be dependent. Fresh coefficients make the recursion a time-homogeneous Markov process: its next conditional distribution depends on the current value alone.
The competition between the random multiplier and the fresh input determines whether initial conditions are forgotten. A synchronous coupling makes this particularly visible by using the same coefficient sequence for two initial values.

Exponential invariant law of a random affine recursion (Xn​=Un​(Xn−1​+En​),X∞​∼Exp(λ))

Words: 137
Random affine recursion OurBigBook.com
Let Un​ have the uniform distribution on [0,1] and let En​ have an exponential distribution of rate λ>0, with all these inputs independent. The random affine recursion Xn​=Un​(Xn−1​+En​) has an invariant exponential law of the same rate. Indeed, for independent exponential X,E, beta-gamma independence says that V=X/(X+E) is uniform and independent of W=X+E. Thus an independent uniform U gives UW the same law as VW=X.
For any deterministic real initial value c, couple the recursion to a stationary copy. Synchronous contraction of a random affine recursion gives an expected difference 2−nE∣c−X0​∣≤2−n(∣c∣+λ−1). Hence the one-time laws converge to Exp(λ), independently of c. An invariant initialization keeps every one-time law unchanged while the chain continues to fluctuate.

Synchronous contraction of a random affine recursion (E∣Xn​−Yn​∣=ρnE∣X0​−Y0​∣)

Words: 72
Random affine recursion OurBigBook.com
Drive two copies of a random affine recursion with the same coefficient pairs, independent of their initial coupling. Then Xn​−Yn​=An​(Xn−1​−Yn−1​). If ρ=E∣A1​∣<1 and E∣X0​−Y0​∣<∞, independence of random variables gives
E∣Xn​−Yn​∣=ρnE∣X0​−Y0​∣⟶0.
(2)
This proves convergence in L1 and hence convergence in probability of the difference. If one copy starts in a stationary distribution, Slutsky's theorem shows that the other copy converges to that distribution. The argument compares paths before taking their one-time probability distributions; it does not require the paths ever to become exactly equal.

A Markov process following a deterministic flow of a vector field between random jumps, with jump hazard function α(Xt−​) and destination transition kernel Kt​(Xt−​,dy). An insertion state in a profile hidden Markov model emits characters between successive match states. A bypass allows zero inserted characters and a self-transition allows several. Under a fixed loop probability r<1, the length of an insertion run conditional on entering has a geometric distribution on positive integers with exit probability 1−r.

A match state in a profile hidden Markov model emits one aligned character at a particular profile position whenever it is visited. “Match” names its alignment role, not a requirement to emit one deterministic character or to match another sequence exactly.

A profile hidden Markov model represents the position-dependent structure of a sequence alignment using an ordered series of match states and optional insertion states, commonly with silent deletion states as well. If deletion states are forbidden, an alignment column containing gaps cannot be a compulsory match state.
