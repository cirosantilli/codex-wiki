For the perpetual American call option, substitute a power Sq into the stationary Black-Scholes equation with continuous stock dividends. The characteristic equation is
F(q)=21​σ2q(q−1)+(r−y)q−r=0.
(17)
Its two roots have negative product −2r/σ2, so one is negative and one positive. The condition at zero excludes the negative power. Since F(1)=−y<0 and F(q)→∞ as q→∞, the positive root satisfies p>1; explicitly,
p=σ2−(r−y−σ2/2)+(r−y−σ2/2)2+2rσ2​​.
(18)
Value matching gives C(S)=(S^−K)(S/S^)p. The smooth fit condition gives p(S^−K)/S^=1, hence
S^=p−1pK​,C(S)={(S^−K)(S/S^)p,S−K,​0≤S<S^,S≥S^.​​
(19)
As y↓0, the positive root tends to 1 from above, so p→1, S^→∞​. For fixed S, the value tends to S. In the dividend-free perpetual limit no finite threshold attains that supremum; this agrees with the finite-maturity no early exercise of a call without dividends.
