For completeness, the natural limiting condition is limS↓0​H(S,t)=e−r(T−t), with 0≤H(S,t)≤e−r(T−t). A positive geometric Brownian motion cannot hit zero in finite time, and from a very small starting value the survival and below-strike terminal events have probability tending to one. If the barrier has already been hit, its value is zero regardless of the current stock price.
For K>B, the terminal payoff tends to one from inside at S=B, while an already hit barrier pays zero. Thus there is a terminal-boundary corner discontinuity; the terminal condition is imposed only for S<B, not as a requirement of continuous extension at (B,T).
