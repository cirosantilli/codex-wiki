The barrier-triggered put exercises at the particular stopping time τB​∧T. The American put option optimizes over all allowed exercise times, so
EB​(S,t)≤A(S,t;K).​
(26)
It can be strictly smaller, but can never exceed the American value. Equality is possible if immediate exercise is already triggered and optimal; a fixed barrier is not generally the optimal finite-maturity exercise rule.
The comparison with the European put option can go in either direction. By the early exercise premium for a fixed barrier,
EB​(S,t)−P(S,t;K)=EQ​[e−r(τB​−t){K−B−P(B,τB​;K)}1{τB​≤T}​∣St​=S].
(27)
Thus an early payment gains interest but sacrifices remaining put time value; the expression inside braces need not have a fixed sign for arbitrary barriers.
For a concrete general construction take current spot S≥K, r>0, σ>0, and t<T. Set γ=2r/σ2 and b∞​=Kγ/(1+γ), the perpetual put option exercise boundary. For 0<B<b∞​, the perpetual value at B equals K−B and strictly dominates the finite-maturity European value. The premium formula and positive probability of hitting B therefore give EB​>P. Strictness follows from the perpetual verification argument: before a fixed positive expiry, its discounted value loses drift rK whenever the stock is below b∞​, with positive expected occupation time when started at B<b∞​.
Conversely, for B<K sufficiently close to K, every possible payment of the exploding put is at most K−B, whether paid at touch or on surviving to expiry. Hence 0≤EB​≤K−B→0, while P(S,t;K)>0. This gives EB​<P for such barriers, and also strict inequality below the American value.
These examples establish both possibilities in general, not a claim that every fixed initial state admits both. If S<b∞​ and only alive barriers B<S are allowed, all those barriers are in the same perpetual stopping region and the premium comparison is nonnegative.
