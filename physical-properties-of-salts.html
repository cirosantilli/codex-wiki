Physical properties of salts

 Home Chemistry Chemical bond Ionic bonding
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 20
Many ionic crystals have high melting temperatures compared with small molecular solids, because reorganizing their strongly interacting ions costs energy. But melting leaves a dense liquid with interactions; it does not perform the complete separation defining a lattice energy. At fixed pressure, the transition occurs when the Gibbs free energies of the solid and liquid match. The relevant enthalpy and entropy differences both matter, so lattice energy alone is not a universal ordering of melting temperatures. Strong covalent bonds in extended networks can also give high melting temperatures.[ref]
