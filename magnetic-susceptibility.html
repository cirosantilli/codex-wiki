Magnetic susceptibility (χ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 10
Magnetic susceptibility describes how readily a magnetic field changes a material's magnetization. The field variable and normalization matter: volume magnetic susceptibility in the International System of Units is dimensionless, whereas a derivative of total magnetic moment with respect to B has units of JT−2. A slope measured against the applied magnetic field intensity also depends on the sample's demagnetizing field.
For an isotropic, reversible linear response through the origin, write
M=χv​Hint​,Bint​=μ0​(Hint​+M)=μ0​(1+χv​)Hint​.
(85)
Here M is magnetization, or magnetic moment per volume; Hint​ is the internal magnetic field intensity; Bint​ is the internal magnetic flux density; and μ0​ is the vacuum permeability. Both M and H have units Am−1. The magnetic permeability is μ0​(1+χv​). [ref]
The independent-moment magnetic response below shows why one number cannot describe every field: magnetization approaches a saturation magnetization, while its differential magnetic susceptibility approaches zero. Anisotropy requires a magnetic susceptibility tensor; hysteresis requires specifying the preparation and sweep branch as well as the bias magnetic field, temperature, and timescale. The Gouy balance turns a small response into a measurable force without presuming that its readout is already the intrinsic volume magnetic susceptibility.
