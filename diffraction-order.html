Diffraction order (m)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Diffraction grating
OurBigBook.com Words: 295
A diffraction order is the integer m labeling a principal constructive interference direction of a diffraction grating: adjacent elements differ in path length by m wavelengths. In the normal-incidence transmission geometry described above,
sinθm​=dmλ​,​dmλ​​≤1.
(90)
Positive and negative diffraction orders lie on opposite sides of the forward normal. The condition expresses the range of the sine of a real angle, so it also limits the propagating diffraction orders.
For λ=600nm and d=2μm, sinθm​=0.3m. Taking the inverse sine gives
m=0m=±1m=±2m=±3​:θ0​=0∘,:θm​=±17.458∘,:θm​=±36.870∘,:θm​=±64.158∘.​
(91)
These are seven allowed propagating diffraction orders. Order m=4 would require sinθ4​=1.2, and m=−4 would require −1.2, so neither has a propagating far-field direction. The allowed diffraction orders are equally spaced in sinθ, while their actual angles become farther apart toward grazing propagation.
Each principal peak has finite width. For the ideal uniform coherent array, the nearest zeros around order m obey
sinθm,±​=dmλ​±Ndλ​.
(92)
Thus the one-sided distance from the order direction to the first zero is Δ(sinθ)=λ/(Nd). Near the first diffraction order in our example, N=10 puts the zeros at 0.27 and 0.33, while N=40 puts them at 0.2925 and 0.3075. The respective one-sided widths are 0.03 and 0.0075; the full zero-to-zero widths are 0.06 and 0.015. These are calculated coherent peak widths, with the slit envelope omitted, and do not describe a measured instrument response. [ref].
Four times as many illuminated slits therefore give a fourfold narrowing in this coordinate without moving the diffraction order. That sharper directional selection helps distinguish nearby wavelengths in a spectrograph. Actual performance also depends on illumination, optical coherence, and source width; the separate slit-limited resolving power of a grating describes the contribution of the entrance slit.
