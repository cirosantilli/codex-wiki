Magnitude difference (Δm)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Astronomical magnitude
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
For two positive measurements F1​,F2​ in the same photometric passband, using the same magnitude zero point, units, and weighting convention, their apparent magnitudes obey
Δm=m2​−m1​=−2.5log10​(F1​F2​​),F1​F2​​=10−0.4Δm.
(18)
The logarithm takes a dimensionless ratio. An energy-weighted measurement must be compared with another energy-weighted measurement, and a photon-counting measurement with another consistently calibrated photon-counting measurement. Changing photometric passband or magnitude zero point requires the corresponding calibration, as in a colour index. [ref]. [ref].
For example, F2​=100F1​ gives Δm=−5: source 2 is brighter. Conversely, Δm=+5 gives F2​/F1​=0.01, and Δm=+1 gives F2​/F1​≃0.398107. Even fractional steps work: Δm=+0.1 gives F2​/F1​≃0.912011, an approximately 8.80% decrease. These are illustrative ratios, independent of any particular star catalogue.
