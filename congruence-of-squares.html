Congruence of squares (a2≡b2(modN))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Integer factorization
OurBigBook.com Words: 458 Articles: 1
A congruence of squares says that two integers a,b have the same square modulo a positive integer N. Factoring their difference of squares gives
a2≡b2(modN)⟹N∣(a−b)(a+b).
(4)
A useful integer factorization results when gcd(a−b,N) or gcd(a+b,N) is a proper divisor of N. Divisibility of a product does not generally force divisibility of either factor by all of N: different prime factors of N can divide different parts of that product.
For N=77, 202−132=231=3⋅77. Therefore
gcd(20−13,77)=gcd(7,77)=7,gcd(20+13,77)=gcd(33,77)=11.
(5)
Thus 77=7⋅11. The prime seven divides the difference, while eleven divides the sum. These computations already give a complete prime factorization because seven and eleven are prime numbers.
The Chinese remainder theorem explains the hidden sign choices. The four congruence classes solving z2≡15(mod77) have representatives 13,20,57,64. Modulo seven each is 1 or −1; modulo eleven each is 2 or −2. Two independent sign choices produce four roots. The inputs thirteen and twenty agree modulo seven and disagree by sign modulo eleven, allowing their sum and difference to separate the two prime factors.
For distinct odd prime numbers p,q, N=pq and gcd(b,N)=1, a solution a2≡b2(modN) with a≡b and a≡−b modulo N yields both proper factors by the two greatest common divisors. For a general composite integer, a proper divisor need not itself be prime; further factoring can remain. If a≡b or a≡−b(modN) in the coprime case, the two calculations return N and one instead. A factor base helps generate relations, but does not ensure every resulting relation is useful.
For historical context, Pomerance describes Kraitchik's 1920s enhancement of the difference-of-squares approach associated with Fermat: seek a difference that is a multiple of N, rather than exactly N. His account also notes antecedents in Gauss and Seelhoff, so this is not an attribution of the whole idea to a single inventor. [ref].
