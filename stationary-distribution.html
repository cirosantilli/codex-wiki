Stationary distribution (πP=π)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Markov process Markov chain
OurBigBook.com Words: 792 Articles: 6
A stationary distribution is a probability distribution π preserved by a Markov chain's evolution. For a Discrete-time Markov chain in the row convention,
πP=π,πi​≥0,∑i​πi​=1.
(74)
Thus π is a normalized nonnegative left eigenvector of the transition matrix for eigenvalue one. Starting with X0​ distributed as π makes every Xn​ have that same probability distribution. For a time-homogeneous Markov chain, the path probabilities are also unchanged by shifting all observation times together: the process has strict stationarity. This does not mean its individual paths are constant. See Hao Wu's MIT lecture 2, stationary distributions.
For the sunny/rainy transition matrix, write π=(a,1−a). The sunny component of πP=π gives a=0.8a+0.4(1−a), so 0.6a=0.4 and
π=(32​,31​),πP=(158​+152​,152​+153​)=π.
(75)
Moreover,
πS​PSR​=32​51​=152​=31​52​=πR​PRS​.
(76)
These equal stationary flows verify detailed balance and show that the Markov chain is reversible. The diagonal detailed balance equations hold automatically. Only 1/3 of the stationary days are rainy, but 0.6 of the days following a rainy day are rainy: stationarity permits dependence.
A finite Markov chain that is an irreducible Markov chain has a unique stationary distribution. If it is also an aperiodic Markov chain, μ0​Pn→π for every initial probability distribution. The sunny/rainy example satisfies both conditions: both directions are possible and each state has a positive holding probability of a Markov chain. See Hao Wu's MIT lecture 4, convergence theorem. For an infinite state space, the additional positive recurrence hypothesis in the countable-state Markov chain convergence theorem matters.
Existence of a stationary distribution alone does not give this convergence. Consider the deterministic two-cycle
C=(01​10​),π=(21​,21​).
(77)
Here πC=π, but starting in the first state gives alternating probability distributions (1,0),(0,1),(1,0),…. Its period of a state in a Markov chain is two. The running fraction of visits to either state nevertheless tends to 1/2, illustrating the Markov chain ergodic theorem. Starting with π instead keeps the one-time probability distributions stationary even though every path alternates.
