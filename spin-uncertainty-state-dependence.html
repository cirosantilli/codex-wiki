Spin uncertainty state dependence (Δσx​Δσz​≥∣⟨σy​⟩∣)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Robertson uncertainty principle
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
For a qubit, use the dimensionless Pauli matrices as observables A=σx​, B=σz​. Their Pauli matrix multiplication law gives
[σx​,σz​]=−2iσy​,σx2​=σy2​=σz2​=I,Δσx​Δσz​≥∣⟨σy​⟩∣.
(36)
These observables have eigenvalues ±1; the physical spin components are Sj​=(ℏ/2)σj​, where ℏ is the reduced Planck constant, so their standard deviations acquire a factor ℏ/2.
In the computational basis, prepare ∣+y⟩=(∣0⟩+i∣1⟩)/2​, an eigenstate of σy​ with eigenvalue +1. Direct multiplication gives
⟨σx​⟩=⟨σz​⟩=0,⟨σy​⟩=1,Δσx​=Δσz​=1.
(37)
Both Pauli measurements return +1 and −1 with equal probabilities, and the Robertson uncertainty principle is saturated: 1⋅1=1.
Instead prepare ∣+z⟩=∣0⟩. Now the expectation values are ⟨σx​⟩=⟨σy​⟩=0, ⟨σz​⟩=1, so Δσx​=1, Δσz​=0. The Pauli measurement of σz​ is certain, and both sides of the bound vanish. The commutator is still nonzero as an operator: noncommuting observables do not guarantee a positive lower bound independent of the quantum state. This example is useful for distinguishing the algebra of the observables from the expectation values in a particular preparation.
