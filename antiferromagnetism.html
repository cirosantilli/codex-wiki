Antiferromagnetism

Antiferromagnetism is ordered compensation of magnetic moments. In the collinear two-sublattice model, MA​=−MB​=0: net magnetization vanishes but staggered magnetization remains. Louis Néel developed this possibility.[ref]
For an original local energy check, perturb the antiparallel pair in exchange interaction by writing θ=π−δ. Then
E=−JS2cosδ=−JS2+21​JS2δ2+O(δ4).
(121)
For J>0, a small relative rotation costs positive energy. This verifies a pair's preference; it does not prove a bulk ordering temperature. A whole crystal must satisfy many bonds simultaneously, and thermal fluctuations and quantum effects matter. The Heisenberg antiferromagnet and antiferromagnetic Ising model specify different assumptions about those collective degrees of freedom.
Why is the distinction useful? A measurement of zero magnetization cannot decide between an ordered alternating arrangement, a disordered ensemble, and oppositely magnetized magnetic domains. A spatial probe such as magnetic neutron diffraction tests the pattern directly. The manganese monoxide neutron diffraction experiment is a particularly clear example: the order adds a new periodic scattering signal even though the net magnetic moment cancels.
