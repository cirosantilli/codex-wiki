Bayesian confirmation (P(H∣E,K)>P(H∣K))

 Home Philosophy Philosophy of science Scientific confirmation
OurBigBook.com Words: 273 Articles: 2
Evidence E confirms H relative to background K when it increases its conditional probability. With 0<P(H∣K)<1 and positive evidence probability, Bayes' theorem gives
P(H∣E,K)>P(H∣K)⟺P(E∣H,K)>P(E∣¬H,K).
(1)
Indeed, writing p=P(H∣K), a=P(E∣H,K) and b=P(E∣¬H,K) gives the posterior probability pa/(pa+(1−p)b); comparison with p reduces to a>b. Thus scientific confirmation depends on the alternatives and background, not just on a statement's logical form.
