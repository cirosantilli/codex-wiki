Poisson algebra ({fg,h}=f{g,h}+g{f,h})

Words: 122 Articles: 1
Algebra over a field OurBigBook.com
A Poisson algebra is a commutative associative algebra with a Lie bracket that is a derivation in each argument. Smooth functions on a symplectic manifold are its standard example. The linear span of moment-map components may form a finite-dimensional Lie subalgebra, while closing those components under multiplication normally produces a much larger Poisson algebra.

Let h be central and a non-zero-divisor in an associative algebra, with commutative quotient modulo h. Dividing commutators by h and reducing modulo h is independent of the lifts, since changing a lift by hc changes the quotient by a commutator, zero modulo h. Antisymmetry, the Leibniz rule and the Jacobi identity descend from the corresponding commutator identities. Division algebra

Words: 370 Articles: 6
Algebra over a field OurBigBook.com
A division algebra over a field k is a division ring equipped with a unital algebra structure over k, with k mapping into its center of an associative algebra. Every nonzero element is invertible; it need not be finite-dimensional over k.

Finite-dimensional real division-algebra classification (D≅R,C,H)

Words: 118
Division algebra OurBigBook.com
A finite-dimensional associative division algebra over R is one of the three displayed algebras. A nonreal element has an irreducible quadratic minimal polynomial and yields i2=−1. Its centralizer is a division algebra over central C, hence is C by splitting minimal polynomials. Decompose the whole algebra into the plus and minus eigenspaces of conjugation by i. If the minus space is zero, the algebra is C; otherwise it is Cj, since multiplying by j−1 takes that space into the centralizer. One has jz=zj and j2∈R. Division forces j2<0, which can be rescaled to −1, yielding H. Of these, only R,H have center R.

Central division algebra (Z(D)=K)

Words: 25
Division algebra OurBigBook.com
A central division algebra over K is a finite-dimensional division algebra whose center is exactly K. It is a central simple algebra. Every Brauer group class has a unique central division algebra representative up to isomorphism.

Finite-dimensional division algebra over an algebraically closed field (D=k)

Words: 40
Division algebra OurBigBook.com
Let D be a finite-dimensional division algebra over an algebraically closed field k. Left multiplication by a∈D has an eigenvalue λ∈k and a nonzero eigenvector v. Thus (a−λ)v=0; multiplying by v−1 gives a=λ. Every element is scalar, so D=k.

A quaternion is a+bi+cj+dk with real coefficients, where i2=j2=k2=ijk=−1. They form a noncommutative real division algebra. The norm squared is a2+b2+c2+d2, and every nonzero quaternion has inverse its conjugate divided by that squared norm. The unit quaternions form the Lie group S3.

Infinite cotangent space obstructs finite algebra generation (dimk​(I/I2)=∞⟹A is not finitely generated)

Words: 101
Finitely generated algebra OurBigBook.com
Let A be a commutative algebra over a field k, with a homomorphism A→k restricting to the identity on k, and let I be its kernel. If A is generated by finitely many elements, subtracting their images in k gives finitely many generators in I. Every polynomial in those generators, modulo I2, is a linear combination of their classes. Thus I/I2, the algebraic cotangent space at this point, is finite-dimensional. For A=k+xk[x,y] and I=xk[x,y], the classes xyj, j≥0, form an infinite basis modulo I2=x2k[x,y]. This is a subalgebra of a polynomial ring which is not a finitely generated algebra.

A vector bundle E with a fibrewise quadratic form Q defines a bundle whose fibre over x is the Clifford algebra Cl(Ex​,Qx​).
