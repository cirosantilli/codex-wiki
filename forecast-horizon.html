Forecast horizon (nforecast​≈λ−1log(Δ/ε0​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Devaney chaos Butterfly effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
A forecast horizon is the duration over which a prediction meets a specified error tolerance. If an initial uncertainty ε0​ grows approximately as ε0​eλn with positive Lyapunov exponent λ, then solving for when it reaches a tolerance Δ gives the displayed estimate. Here log is the natural logarithm and λ is measured per step of the discrete dynamical system; for a flow map, use the exponent per unit time instead. The estimate assumes the growth approximation remains valid up to the chosen tolerance and excludes additional model errors.
Reducing ε0​ by a factor ten buys approximately (log10)/λ more steps, rather than multiplying the forecast duration by ten. For typical logistic map orbits at r=4, λ=log2, so one extra decimal digit buys roughly 3.32 steps in this approximation. Better measurements help, but their benefit is logarithmic. A forecast of a probability distribution or a stable long-term average asks a different question from predicting each individual state.
