Jordan decomposition of a function of bounded variation (f=f(a)+P−N,Vat​(f)=P(t)+N(t))

Every real function on [a,b] has bounded variation if and only if it is a difference of two nondecreasing functions. For a normalized version, let v(t)=Vat​(f) and v(a)=0. Its accumulated positive and negative variations are
P(t)=2v(t)+f(t)−f(a)​,N(t)=2v(t)−f(t)+f(a)​.
(87)
Both are nondecreasing functions, P(a)=N(a)=0, and
f(t)=f(a)+P(t)−N(t),v(t)=P(t)+N(t).
(88)
This is the Jordan decomposition of a function of bounded variation.[ref] Equivalently one may write f=f+​−f−​ with f+​=(v+f)/2 and f−​=(v−f)/2; those functions need not vanish at a.
For the piecewise linear function in partition example of total variation, the successive values of P are 0,2,2,6,6, while those of N are 0,0,3,3,5. At t=4, the total rise is 6 and the total fall is 5: their difference gives the net change 1, and their sum gives the total variation 11. A declining segment increases N while P stays constant; an ascending segment does the opposite.
For a short proof outline, total variation is additive over adjacent intervals, because any two partitions of an interval have a common partition refinement. Thus v(t)−v(s)=Vst​(f)≥∣f(t)−f(s)∣ for s<t. Substituting this inequality into the formulas for P and N shows their increments are nonnegative. Conversely, if f=g−h for two nondecreasing functions, the triangle inequality bounds each partition sum by g(b)−g(a)+h(b)−h(a). This proves finite total variation without restricting the number of changes of direction.
