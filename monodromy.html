Monodromy

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 405 Articles: 4
Monodromy records how a germ of a holomorphic function changes after analytic continuation around a closed path. The source point returns to itself, but the continued germ of a holomorphic function need not. For z(t)=eit, 0≤t≤2π, starting with holomorphic square root value 1 gives w(t)=eit/2 and hence w(2π)=−1; starting with complex logarithm value 0 gives L(t)=it and hence L(2π)=2πi. After m counterclockwise turns the values are (−1)m and 2πim. Thus monodromy exchanges the two square-root branches of a multivalued function, whereas it shifts through infinitely many logarithm branches of a multivalued function. [ref]
The three panels use the same parameter t. They show analytic continuation through local holomorphic patches, including across a chosen branch cut; repeatedly evaluating the principal logarithm would instead reset its imaginary part at the cut. Monodromy makes the obstruction to a global branch of a multivalued function visible and motivates the use of Riemann surfaces as domains on which continued values stay distinct.
