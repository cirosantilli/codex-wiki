Gradient descent (xk+1​=xk​−η∇f(xk​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 21
Gradient descent is an iterative method for minimizing a differentiable function f: choose an initial vector x0​ and repeatedly move opposite its gradient.[ref]
With a fixed positive step size η, the update is
xk+1​=xk​−η∇f(xk​),k=0,1,2,….
(1)
The direction and the step size play different roles: the gradient supplies local information about increase, while η determines how far that information is trusted. A varying step size gives ηk​ instead; exact line search for a positive-definite quadratic chooses it by minimizing along the current direction.
Why is this the steepest direction? For a unit vector v in the Euclidean norm, the directional derivative is ⟨∇f(x),v⟩. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds it below by −∥∇f(x)∥2​, attained by v=−∇f(x)/∥∇f(x)∥2​ when the gradient is nonzero. This is steepest descent relative to the Euclidean norm; changing the norm can change the steepest direction. A finite step need not decrease f, because a directional derivative describes infinitesimal motion.
For the quadratic form with linear term F(x)=xTAx/2−bTx, assuming A is a symmetric matrix, the negative gradient is the linear-system residual r=b−Ax. Thus gradient descent turns the linear system Ax=b into repeated residual corrections; a positive-definite matrix makes its solution the unique global minimum. This is useful when applying the linear map x↦Ax is affordable but directly solving the linear system is costly.
The attraction is that a gradient evaluation gives an entire update without solving a new linear system. The difficulty is choosing a step size that progresses along gently curved directions without overshooting steep ones. Fixed-step gradient descent on a positive-definite quadratic makes this tension exact: every eigenvector direction has its own multiplier, determined by its eigenvalue. The resulting condition number dependence explains why a narrow valley can slow even an inexpensive iterative method.
The simplicity of the gradient update and its sensitivity to the condition number of the Hessian matrix are central practical features.[ref]
