Infinity-corrected microscope

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Optical microscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 158 Articles: 1
An optical microscope whose microscope objective, with the specimen at its designed front focal plane, sends out a parallel bundle of light rays from each specimen point. Different specimen points generally give different bundle directions. A tube lens then forms the finite intermediate microscope image; the microscope eyepiece views it. This parallel region permits suitable optical accessories to be inserted. Finite-conjugate and infinity-corrected components must be matched to their intended system.[ref]
In the ideal paraxial approximation, an object height ho​ at objective focal length fo​ gives bundle slope −ho​/fo​. A tube lens of focal length ft​ maps that slope to hi​=−ft​ho​/fo​, so
mo​=−fo​ft​​.

This is a two-assembly result. Substituting infinity into the single-objective transverse magnification −vo​/uo​ would not describe the finite recorded image. Infinity correction concerns the region after the objective; relaxed-eye viewing concerns the region after the eyepiece. They are distinct conditions.
