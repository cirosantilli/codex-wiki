Interval arithmetic operations (X+Y,X−Y,XY,X/Y)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Interval arithmetic
OurBigBook.com Words: 654 Articles: 4
For closed intervals X=[a,b] and Y=[c,d], exact addition, subtraction and multiplication give
X+YX−YXY​=[a+c,b+d],=[a−d,b−c],=[min{ac,ad,bc,bd},max{ac,ad,bc,bd}].​
(12)
These interval arithmetic operations describe all results u∘v for (u,v) in the Cartesian product X×Y: the two inputs are allowed to vary independently. Independence here means freedom to choose either input anywhere in its closed interval; it does not assert anything about probability. For example, [1,2]+[3,5]=[4,7] and [1,2]−[3,5]=[−4,−1].
All four endpoint products matter when signs change. For X=[−2,3] and Y=[−4,1], the endpoint products are 8,−2,−12,3, so XY=[−12,8]. Taking just ac and bd, even after sorting them, would give [3,8] and lose valid negative products.
For division, if 0∈/Y, the multiplicative inverse gives 1/Y=[1/d,1/c] and X/Y=X(1/Y). Thus [1,2]/[3,4]=[1/4,2/3]. This ordinary finite-closed interval rule requires a denominator that excludes zero. When the denominator contains zero, division is undefined for some pairs and can become unbounded near zero; these formulas cannot supply a finite interval enclosure of all quotients. The operations above use exact endpoints; a computer implementation must also apply outward rounding.
