Again put α=m1​​ and β=m2​​. The ring of integers of each of the three quadratic subfields has integral basis 1,α, 1,β, or 1,αβ, respectively: their square-free radicands are respectively congruent to 3,2,2 modulo four. The field traces of the algebraic integer γ to those subfields are
a+bα,a+cβ,a+dαβ,a=2r, b=2s, c=2t, d=2u.
(15)
They are algebraic integers, so comparison with each integral basis gives
a,b,c,d∈Z​.
(16)
This use of all three subfields is needed to control d as well as the other coefficients.
The field norm to Q(α) is
NL/Q(α)​(γ)=4a2+m1​b2−m2​c2−m1​m2​d2​+2ab−m2​cd​α.
(17)
Its two coefficients must be integers. The second condition says ab is even, since m2​ is even. Reducing the numerator of the first coefficient modulo four gives
a2−b2−2(c2+d2)≡0(mod4).
(18)
Reduction modulo two first gives a≡b(mod2). Since ab is even, both a and b are even. The same congruence now gives c2+d2≡0(mod2), or c≡d(mod2). Therefore every algebraic integer has the form
γ=2a​+2b​α+2c−d​β+dw,w=2β+αβ​,
(19)
with four integer coefficients.
For the converse, 1,α,β are algebraic integers, and
w2=4m2​(1+m1​)​+2m2​​α∈Z[α].
(20)
Here 4∣1+m1​ and 2∣m2​, so both coefficients are integers. The monic polynomial X2−w2 and integral dependence is transitive show that w is an algebraic integer. The four displayed generators are rationally linearly independent, since their coefficient matrix in 1,α,β,αβ has determinant 1/2. Hence the integral basis for coprime biquadratic radicands congruent to three and two is
{1,m1​​,m2​​,2m2​​+m1​m2​​​}​.
(21)
In this formula m1​m2​​ denotes the product αβ of the chosen square roots; this convention also handles negative radicands.
