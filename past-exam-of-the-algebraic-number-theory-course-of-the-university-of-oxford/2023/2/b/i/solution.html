By the correspondence theorem for ideals, an ideal of R/Pn has the form J/Pn with Pn⊆J⊆R. If J=R, apply unique factorization of ideals in a number field to J. Every prime ideal Q dividing J contains J and hence Pn. Primality gives P⊆Q, and maximality gives Q=P. Thus J=Pi for some i≥1.
The inclusion Pn⊆Pi forces i≤n: if i>n, cancellation using the invertible fractional ideal Pn would give R⊆Pi−n, which is impossible. The zero ideal corresponds to i=n, while the unit ideal corresponds to i=0. Consequently the nonzero proper ideals are exactly
Pi/Pn,1≤i<n​.
(34)
When n=1, there are none, in agreement with R/P being a field.
