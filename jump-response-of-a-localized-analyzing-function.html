Jump response of a localized analyzing function (cψ​(H(⋅−x0​);a,b)=a​∫(x0​−b)/a∞​ψ(u)​du)

Let ψ be an integrable function with at least one vanishing moment. For a unit jump discontinuity f(x)=H(x−x0​), where H is the Heaviside step function, substitution into the coefficient gives
cψ​(f;a,b)=a​∫θ∞​ψ(u)​du,θ=ax0​−b​.
(40)
The signed integral of the whole analyzing function is zero, but the jump discontinuity cuts through its support of a function and usually leaves an unbalanced partial integral. At fixed relative position θ with nonzero partial integral, the coefficient decays as a​, slower than the aq+1/2 Taylor remainder bound on a smooth region. “Edges survive” means this relative contrast: the normalized coefficients still tend to zero as a→0.
For an original exact comparison, use the smooth polynomial f(x)=x+x2 at b=0. The Haar wavelet h and the second-difference analyzing function η give
ch​(f;a,0)=−4a3/2+a5/2​,cη​(f;a,0)=6​2​a5/2.
(41)
The linear term cancels for η; it remains for h. For the fixed unit jump discontinuity H(x), choose b=−a/4 so that θ=1/4 at every scale. Then
ch​(H;a,−a/4)=−4a​​,cη​(H;a,−a/4)=−46​a​​.
(42)
Position matters. If an analyzing function's support of a function lies entirely on one constant side of the jump discontinuity, its coefficient is zero. Even a crossing can cancel: η has zero partial integral at θ=3/2. A jump discontinuity on a dyadic interval boundary can likewise escape finer Haar wavelets whose supports do not cross it. These examples describe particular functions and normalizations, not a universal ranking of wavelets for signal processing.
