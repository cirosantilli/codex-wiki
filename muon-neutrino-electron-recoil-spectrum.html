Muon-neutrino electron recoil spectrum (dσν​/dy=K[gL2​+gR2​(1−y)2])

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Weak interaction Neutral current
OurBigBook.com Words: 396
For an especially simple neutral current example, scatter a muon neutrino elastically from an initially stationary unpolarized electron:
νμ​e−⟶νμ​e−,y=Eν​Te​​,sW2​=sin2θW​,gL​=−21​+sW2​,gR​=sW2​.
(15)
Here Te​ is the final electron kinetic energy, Eν​ is the incident neutrino energy, and θW​ is the Weinberg angle. These are electron chiral couplings in the g/cosθW​ vertex convention of neutral-current vector and axial couplings. The chosen muon neutrino elastic channel has no additional W boson exchange amplitude; the electron-neutrino elastic channel would have one. Other charged-current final states can become kinematically accessible at sufficiently high incident energies and are not part of this elastic model.
Use natural units ℏ=c=1, retain only tree-level Feynman diagrams, and take the contact limit ∣q2∣≪mZ2​ with Eν​≫me​. Neglect electron-mass terms relative to Eν​, radiative corrections and detector response. Then
K=π2GF2​me​Eν​​,dydσν​​=K[gL2​+gR2​(1−y)2],dydσνˉ​​=K[gR2​+gL2​(1−y)2],0≤y≤1.
(16)
GF​ is the Fermi constant. The approximate endpoint y=1 replaces the exact elastic endpoint ymax​=2Eν​/(me​+2Eν​). The omitted leading mass-dependent interference term in dσν​/dy is −KgL​gR​me​y/Eν​. Thus the approximation is not appropriate for arbitrarily soft neutrinos.
The shape follows from the two chiral contributions to the massless scattering amplitude. The spin sum gives terms proportional to s2 and u2 in Mandelstam variables; in this limit u/s=−(1−y), giving the square (1−y)2. The two electron chiralities do not interfere when the relevant mass corrections are dropped. Replacing the neutrino by its antineutrino exchanges which coupling multiplies each kinematic term. This is the same chiral angular weighting that appears in neutrino scattering on an antiquark, now with a target whose couplings are particularly simple.
Integrating the two elementary terms gives
∫01​(1−y)2dy=31​,σν​=K(gL2​+3gR2​​),σνˉ​=K(gR2​+3gL2​​).
(17)
For an original teaching example choose sW2​=1/4, so gL​=−1/4 and gR​=1/4. This is a stipulated parameter, not a measurement of the Weinberg angle. Then
K1​dydσν​​=161+(1−y)2​,Kσν​​=121​,⟨y⟩=4/3∫01​y[1+(1−y)2]dy​=167​.
(18)
The normalized probability density function is p(y)=3[1+(1−y)2]/4, not the unnormalized differential cross section. Its endpoints have ratio two, and the fraction with y>1/2 is 13/32. The antineutrino spectrum happens to coincide at this chosen parameter because gL2​=gR2​; this equality is not a general prediction for arbitrary Weinberg angles.
Unlike a visible electron recoil, the scattered neutrino is unrecorded. A measured recoil spectrum must therefore be compared with incident neutrino flux, energy distribution, acceptance, detection efficiency and backgrounds. A shape or rate tests a specified theoretical model only after these experimental ingredients have been supplied.
