Sine power kernel integrability (∫0∞​∣sinx∣x−pdx<∞⟺1<p<2)

For p>0, the function x↦sinx/xp is Lebesgue integrable on (0,∞) exactly when 1<p<2. Near zero, sinx/x→1, so absolute integrability is equivalent to that of x1−p, which requires p<2. At infinity, p>1 gives the upper bound x−p. For 0<p≤1, on each interval [kπ,(k+1)π],
∫kπ(k+1)π​xp∣sinx∣​dx≥((k+1)π)p2​,

and the corresponding P-series diverges.
The signed improper integral nevertheless converges for every 0<p<2: near zero the same local comparison applies, and at infinity the Dirichlet test for improper integrals uses the bounded antiderivative of sine and the decreasing factor x−p→0. Thus 0<p≤1 gives conditional convergence, while 1<p<2 gives absolute integrability. In particular, substituting t=1/x turns ∫01​∣x−1sin(1/x)∣dx into the divergent tail ∫1∞​∣sint∣/tdt.
