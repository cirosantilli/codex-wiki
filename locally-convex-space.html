Locally convex space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Topological vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 26
A locally convex space is a topological vector space with a neighbourhood basis at zero consisting of convex sets. Equivalently, its topology is a seminorm topology generated by some family P of seminorms. We allow non-Hausdorff spaces here; a separating family of seminorms gives precisely the Hausdorff case. Every normed vector space is locally convex, but smooth convergence generally requires a whole family of measurements.
The geometric and measurement descriptions fit together: seminorm sublevel sets are convex, while the Minkowski functional of an open, convex zero neighbourhood invariant under all scalar multiplications with ∣λ∣≤1 is a continuous seminorm. Such neighbourhoods can be chosen as a neighbourhood basis. [ref].
Local convexity makes Hahn-Banach theorem and separation of a point and an open convex set available. It turns geometric distinctions into continuous linear functionals, connecting convex sets to the continuous dual space.
