In the Laplace integral method for a differential equation, differentiate the integral three times with respect to z, and replace zezζ by ∂ζ​ezζ. Integration by parts gives

These are the conditions obtained by eliminating the transformed interior term and the endpoint term separately. As usual for this method, differentiation and integration by parts need convergence assumptions; the contours constructed next make every such step valid locally uniformly for all complex z. A literal necessity claim for arbitrary weights and contours would additionally require uniqueness of the chosen representation by a Laplace integral; a null weight in a contour Laplace representation can otherwise be added without changing the solution.
Let
uj​=ei(π/4+jπ/2),j=0,1,2,3.
(34)
The quartic decay sectors for a contour integral contain the four rays ζ=ruj​. Along each ray, the quartic term is ζ4/4=−r4/4. For j=1,2,3, choose Γj​ to travel from infinity on ray u0​ to zero, and then from zero to infinity on ray uj​. Set A=1 and define
wj​(z)=∫Γj​​eζ4/4+zζdζ,j=1,2,3.​

On any compact set of z values, the integrand and each of its z derivatives are bounded by a constant times rme−r4/4+Mr for some M. These bounds are integrable at infinity, so differentiation under the integral sign is justified and the functions are entire. The endpoint term vanishes at both infinite ends. There is no extra term at zero because the two pieces join with the same integrand value and opposite endpoint orientations. Therefore all three functions solve the differential equation.

The coefficient matrix is a Vandermonde matrix at four distinct fourth roots of −1, and hence is invertible. All cj​ vanish. There is also a concrete reason for the earlier qualification about necessity. Deform the incoming ray of Γ1​ to angle π/3 and its outgoing ray to angle 2π/3, within their respective quartic decay sectors. The quartic integral is unchanged and remains nonzero. On this deformed contour, the extra entire weight eζ2 contributes zero for every z: throughout the closing wedge, Re(ζ2)≤−r2/2, so the large closing arc tends to zero and the Cauchy integral theorem gives a zero integral. Thus g=eζ4/4+eζ2 represents the same entire solution and has vanishing endpoint terms, although it does not satisfy g′=ζ3g. The canonical weight equation is the standard sufficient construction, rather than an unconditional uniqueness assertion about contour representations.
Thus the three contours give linearly independent nonzero solutions, and they form a fundamental system of solutions for this third-order linear equation. This proves independence directly instead of inferring it merely from the presence of three different contours.
