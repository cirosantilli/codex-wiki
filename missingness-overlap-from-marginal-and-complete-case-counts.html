Missingness overlap from marginal and complete-case counts (mXY​=mX​+mY​−(N−nXY​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Missing data
OurBigBook.com Words: 128
For N observations, let mX​ and mY​ count missing values of two variables, and let nXY​ count observations on which both are available. The number missing at least one variable is N−nXY​. Applying the inclusion-exclusion principle to the two missing-value sets gives the displayed number missing both. The disjoint counts are consequently mX​−mXY​ missing only X, mY​−mXY​ missing only Y, mXY​ missing both, and nXY​ complete cases. Their nonnegativity is a necessary consistency check on the supplied totals.
This identifies an aggregate missingness pattern without reconstructing individual records. It does not identify a missing-data mechanism, establish missing completely at random, or justify treating a substantive response such as an explicit unknown category as a missing observation. Counts from different cohorts cannot be inserted into the identity without first establishing a common denominator.
