Distance between two uniform points in a three-dimensional ball (fR​(r)=3r2/R3−9r3/(4R4)+3r5/(16R6))

For two independent random variables taking values in a three-dimensional ball with constant volume probability density, the density of their distance is the displayed expression for 0≤r≤2R, and zero otherwise. Both independence and the common radius R>0 are essential. To derive it, put u=∣r1​∣, v=∣r2​∣ and integrate the product volume differential form over orientations. The law of cosines gives the angular Jacobian determinant v/(ur), leaving fR​(r)=9r∫uvdudv/(2R6) over 0≤u,v≤R, ∣u−v∣≤r≤u+v. With x=u+v, y=u−v, the integrand becomes (x2−y2)dxdy/8. Direct integration gives ∫uvdudv=2R3r/3−R2r2/2+r4/24. An independent geometric derivation uses the overlap volume of two radius-R balls displaced by r, namely π(4R+r)(2R−r)2/12, multiplied by 4πr2 and divided by the square of 4πR3/3. The density integrates to one and has mean 36R/35.

One-big-jump polynomial lower bound (P(Sn​≥na)≥P(X1​≥na))

Words: 70
Pareto distribution OurBigBook.com
For nonnegative independent identically distributed summands with polynomial upper tail P(X1​≥x)=x−β for x≥1, positivity gives P(Sn​≥na)≥(na)−β for fixed a>0 and large n. Since this probability is also at most one, its logarithm divided by n tends to zero. No positive exponential moment exists, so the exponential-moment form of the Cramér theorem cannot be applied. The bound proves a logarithmic rate and does not assert an exact tail asymptotic.

A hypoexponential distribution is the distribution of a sum of independent variables with exponential distributions. For two positive unequal rates λ,μ, convolution of independent random variables gives the probability density function
f(s)=μ−λλμ​(e−λs−e−μs),s≥0.
(1)

Sum of exponential variables with linearly increasing rates (∑i=1N​Ti​=dmaxi≤N​Ei​)

Words: 110
Hypoexponential distribution OurBigBook.com
If the independent exponential random variables Ti​ have rates iλ, then
∑i=1N​Ti​ =d max1≤i≤N​Ei​,P(∑i=1N​Ti​≤t)=(1−e−λt)N(t≥0),
(2)
where the Ei​ are independent exponential random variables of rate λ. To see this, the first of N independent exponential clocks rings at rate Nλ; after it rings, the memoryless property leaves N−1 independent rate-λ clocks. The successive order statistic gaps are therefore independent with rates Nλ,(N−1)λ,…,λ. Their sum has the same distribution as ∑Ti​. In particular, for x>0, the tail at Nx has logarithm −λNx+o(N), since 1−(1−e−λNx)N∼Ne−λNx. The sum divided by N has a large deviation principle with speed N and rate function λx on x≥0, infinity otherwise.

Connectivity of three acute-angle sphere points (P(connected)=(π+2)/(4π))

Words: 80
Central angle between independent uniform sphere points OurBigBook.com
Join three independent uniform points on a sphere when their central angle is acute. Given angle γ between the first pair, the third point's two favorable hemispheres overlap in a spherical lune of area fraction (π−γ)/(2π). If γ<π/2, connection requires membership in their union, of probability 1/2+γ/(2π). If γ>π/2, it requires membership in their intersection, of probability 1/2−γ/(2π). Integrating these against the central-angle density sinγ/2 proves the displayed probability. Connectivity is weaker than all three pairwise angles being acute.

Shifted gamma distribution (V=k+Y,Y∼Gamma(α,ν))

Words: 55
Gamma distribution OurBigBook.com
A gamma distribution translated by a real constant k. With shape α>0 and rate ν>0, its support is (k,∞) and its first three cumulants are k+α/ν, α/ν2, and 2α/ν3. Its skewness is 2/α​. Beta-gamma independence (X/(X+Y)⊥(X+Y))

Words: 67
Gamma distribution OurBigBook.com
If X and Y are independent random variables with Gamma distributions of shapes a,b>0 and the same rate θ>0, then U=X/(X+Y) has the Beta distribution with parameters (a,b), and V=X+Y has the Gamma distribution of shape a+b and rate θ. Moreover U and V are independent. The inverse change of variables (x,y)=(uv,(1−u)v) has absolute Jacobian determinant v, which makes the joint probability density factorize on the product domain 0<u<1, v>0. The common rate is essential for this factorization.

Laplace prior for sparse images (π0​(u)∝e−λ∥u∥1​)

Words: 102
Laplace distribution OurBigBook.com
Independent centered Laplace distributions for pixel intensities give the prior distribution
π0​(u)=(λ/2)de−λ∑j=1d​∣uj​∣,λ>0.
(22)
This prior favors small intensities through its sharp peak at zero, while its tails permit isolated appreciable pixels. Its negative log density is an L1 norm penalty, so with a Gaussian likelihood a maximum a posteriori estimate minimizes a sum of squared residuals plus that penalty. A draw from this continuous prior has no exactly zero coordinate with positive probability; sparsity of a maximizing estimate and concentration of prior mass near zero are different statements. The product prior also does not encode spatial contiguity. Inverse transform sampling produces each coordinate from an independent uniform distribution by λ−1log(2U) for U≤1/2 and −λ−1log(2(1−U)) otherwise, ignoring the probability-zero endpoints.
