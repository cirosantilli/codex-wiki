Gravitational wave

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 39
A gravitational wave is a propagating disturbance of spacetime curvature produced by changing mass-energy multipole moments. In general relativity, its two transverse gravitational wave polarizations alternately stretch and squeeze freely falling separations. The leading radiation from a slowly moving source is governed by its mass quadrupole moment, rather than by its total mass alone.
The experimental breakthrough was GW150914: observed on 14 September 2015 and published on 11 February 2016. The LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo Collaboration reported a merging pair of black holes, using the two LIGO detectors at Hanford and Livingston; Virgo was being upgraded and did not observe this event.[ref]
The 2017 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized Rainer Weiss, Barry C. Barish and Kip S. Thorne for contributions to LIGO and gravitational wave observation. Rainer Weiss developed interferometric detection and its noise analysis; Barry C. Barish led the project into construction and operation; Kip S. Thorne developed its theoretical and astrophysical foundations. The experiment depended on a large collaboration.[ref]
