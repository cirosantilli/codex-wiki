Invariant subspaces of a quarter-turn in three dimensions ({0}, Rn, n⊥, R3)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Group representation Invariant subspace
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
For a quarter-turn rotation T of R3 about a unit axis vector n, the only real invariant subspaces are {0}, the axis Rn, its perpendicular plane n⊥, and all of R3.
Indeed T2 fixes the axis and negates the perpendicular plane. Hence P=(I+T2)/2 is the linear projection onto the axis, while I−P is the linear projection onto the perpendicular plane. An invariant subspace L is preserved by these polynomials in T, so
L=(L∩Rn)⊕(L∩n⊥).
(11)
The first intersection is either zero or the axis. If the second contains a nonzero v, it also contains Tv. These are perpendicular nonzero vectors, so they span the perpendicular plane. This proves the classification.
Over the complex numbers the situation changes: the rotation matrix has eigenvalues 1,i,−i, giving three one-dimensional eigenspaces. The absence of real eigenvalues on the perpendicular plane explains why it has no real one-dimensional invariant subspace.
