A practical design uses one common exon-4 forward PCR primer with an exon-specific reverse PCR primer for each transcript isoform. Candidate sequences, all written 5′ to 3′, are
F4​R5​R6​​:GCGCTTTGGCCTTGATCAAC,:AGCTTCCATCTGAAGAGGGT,:CTTGTATGTAGCGAACTCCG.​
(14)
Use F4​/R5​ for TCF-long and F4​/R6​ for TCF-short: R5​ recognizes exon 5, while R6​ recognizes exon 6. The displayed mature reference sequences predict approximately 130- and 67-base-pair products, respectively. Check actual splice products because a transcript with unexpected exon combinations could defeat the assumed specificity. The simple melting-temperature estimates are approximately 62, 60 and 60∘C.
Prepare matched RNA, remove contaminating DNA, carry out reverse transcription and validate each RT-qPCR assay with known isoform standards, a single-product check, a no-template control and a no-reverse-transcriptase control. The primers span an intron in the genomic arrangement but do not themselves cross a splice junction, so the DNA control remains important. Calibrate amplification efficiency and fluorescence response before comparing molecule numbers between the two assays. A redesigned junction-specific assay is another option once additional suitable flanking sequence is available.
