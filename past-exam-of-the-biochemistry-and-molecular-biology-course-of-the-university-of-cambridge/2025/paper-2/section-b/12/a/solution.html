The ADP addition contains
nADP​=(10×10−6L)(20×10−3molL−1)=0.200μmol.
(8)
During the initial stimulated interval, the plotted oxygen concentration falls from about 240 to 207μM. The chamber volume after this addition is 1.900+0.050+0.050+0.010=2.010mL, giving
ΔnO2​​≃33μmolL−1×0.002010L=0.0663μmol.
(9)
The conventional phosphorylation-to-oxidation ratio counts ATP formed per oxygen atom reduced, so its denominator is twice the number of oxygen molecules:
P/O≃2(0.0663)0.200​≃1.5.​
(10)
This assumes the added ADP is phosphorylated and uses the displayed total oxygen loss over the pulse; it is an apparent experimental ratio. A separate correction for background respiration or proton leak would change the denominator and must be specified explicitly. Dividing by oxygen molecules without the factor of two would instead give an ATP:O2​ ratio near 3.
