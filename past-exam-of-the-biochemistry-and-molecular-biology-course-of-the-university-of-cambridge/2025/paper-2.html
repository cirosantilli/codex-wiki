1 OurBigBook.com
For a primary sigma factor of the σ70 family, distinguish the following regions. Region 1.1 restrains inappropriate DNA binding by free sigma factor and must move out of the downstream-DNA channel during formation of the open RNA polymerase complex; region 1.2 contributes to recognition around the discriminator downstream of the −10 element. Domain 2 binds the RNA polymerase core, recognizes the −10 gene promoter element and helps melt the duplex and stabilize the exposed non-template strand. Domain 3 recognizes an extended −10 element; its region 3.2 projects toward the active-centre channel, helps position template and initiating substrates, and must be displaced as the nascent RNA grows. Domain 4 recognizes the −35 element through a helix-turn-helix motif and contacts the RNA polymerase beta flap and some transcription activators. Domains 2 and 4 supply the principal −10 and −35 sequence recognition, with the other regions controlling opening and productive initiation. This domain map concerns the σ70 family, rather than every structurally distinct bacterial sigma factor.[ref]

2 OurBigBook.com
Incubate a labelled precursor messenger RNA containing an authentic 3′-processing signal with a HeLa nuclear extract, then resolve the products by denaturing polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. With ATP, the upstream cleavage product acquires a heterogeneous poly(A) tail. Replace ATP with an appropriate non-incorporable analogue, such as alpha,beta-methylene-ATP: a discrete product ending at the normal cleavage site still appears, but the extended tail does not. Thus cleavage can occur when polyadenylation is blocked; it is not cleavage brought about by synthesis of the tail.[ref]
Conversely, supply an RNA substrate already ending at the normal cleavage site: addition of ATP and the appropriate poly(A) polymerase machinery can extend its free 3′ hydroxyl without any new endonucleolytic cut.[ref] Compare precursor, pre-cleaved substrate, no-extract and no-ATP controls, and map the product ends to distinguish correct processing from non-specific degradation. These are separable reactions, normally coordinated in one processing complex. Their separability does not imply that they use completely independent recognition factors.

A detectable burst kinetics phase requires rapid formation and detection of an initial reaction product, followed by a slower step that regenerates competent enzyme. A common hydrolysis mechanism is
E+S⇌ESk2​​E∗+P1​k3​​E+P2​,
(1)
where E∗ can be a covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate. With excess substrate, rapid binding and k2​≫k3​, the first turnover rapidly releases P1​ and traps much of the enzyme as E∗. Subsequent turnovers wait for the slow regeneration step. The measured product must report the fast step: monitoring only P2​ need not reveal a burst. Fast mixing and sufficient time resolution are needed, while spontaneous substrate hydrolysis, depletion and enzyme inactivation must be excluded.
For an illustrative saturated two-step cycle, initially unmodified enzyme concentration ET​ gives
[P1​](t)=k2​+k3​ET​k2​k3​​t+ET​(k2​+k3​k2​​)2(1−e−(k2​+k3​)t).
(2)
The second term approaches a burst amplitude near ET​ when k2​≫k3​, followed by the slower steady-state slope. A covalent intermediate is a useful example, not a logical requirement for every possible burst mechanism.

The enzyme adenylate kinase rapidly interconverts the adenine nucleotides:
2ADP⇌ATP+AMP,K=[ADP]2[ATP][AMP]​.
(3)
For approximately fixed conditions and near-equilibrium activity ratios,
[ATP][AMP]​=K([ATP][ADP]​)2.
(4)
The AMP:ATP ratio responds quadratically to ADP:ATP. Doubling ADP:ATP therefore quadruples AMP:ATP. In working skeletal muscle, ATP is also buffered by creatine kinase and continuing ATP production, so its fractional change is relatively small. The initially smaller AMP pool can undergo a much larger fractional increase, providing a sensitive signal of energetic stress to regulators such as AMP-activated protein kinase. This concerns sensitivity of relative changes; it does not mean that the absolute AMP concentration exceeds ATP.

5 OurBigBook.com
The mitochondrial fission-fusion cycle is continuous remodeling of a mitochondrial population by division and merger. In mammalian biological cells, mitofusins promote fusion of outer mitochondrial membranes, while OPA1 supports fusion of inner mitochondrial membranes. Fusion mixes matrix contents and permits functional complementation between mitochondria. Genetic disruption and rescue of mitofusins demonstrated their requirement for normal mitochondrial fusion.[ref]
For fission, recruited Drp1 assembles at constrictions and uses GTP-dependent remodeling to help divide the organelle.[ref] The balance controls network shape, distribution and quality control. Division can help distribute mitochondria to daughter biological cells and segregate damaged portions for mitophagy; fusion can restore mixed contents. These are regulated competing events in a network, not an obligatory synchronized sequence that every mitochondrion completes on a fixed timetable.

6 OurBigBook.com
A steroid hormone crosses the lipid bilayer and binds an intracellular nuclear receptor. For a cytoplasmic glucocorticoid receptor, ligand binding alters association with molecular chaperones, exposes nuclear-targeting functions and promotes accumulation in the cell nucleus. Other steroid hormone receptors already have substantial nuclear localization. The activated receptor binds specific hormone response elements in DNA, often as a dimer, and changes recruitment of transcription coactivators or transcription corepressors. These factors alter local chromatin and recruitment or activity of the RNA polymerase II machinery. The result is selective activation or repression of target genes, followed by altered messenger RNA and protein abundance; the hormone itself does not act as an RNA polymerase or directly rewrite the DNA sequence.

Fucci uses two fluorescent protein fusions carrying cell-cycle-regulated degradation signals: a red/orange Cdt1 fragment accumulates mainly in G1 phase, whereas a green geminin fragment accumulates through S phase, G2 phase and much of M phase. Opposing activities of SCF ubiquitin ligase and anaphase-promoting complex regulate reporter degradation through the ubiquitin-proteasome system; the fluorophores report those regulated stabilities rather than simply measuring total endogenous Cdt1 and geminin.[ref]
Live imaging follows individual biological cells through red, overlap and green states, allowing measurements of phase timing, spatial patterns and responses to perturbation without repeatedly fixing the sample. Flow cytometry can separate populations by reporter color. The basic two-color version does not independently resolve every phase, and a color transition is an indirect readout of ubiquitin ligase activity. Validate timing with DNA content or a DNA replication marker and control for altered reporter expression, fluorophore maturation and drug effects on degradation.

An antibody-drug conjugate couples an antibody against a tumor-associated surface antigen to a potent cytotoxic payload through a suitable chemical linker. Binding enriches delivery to biological cells displaying the target. Many designs undergo receptor-mediated endocytosis, trafficking through endosomes to a lysosome, and release of an active drug through linker cleavage or degradation of the antibody. The released payload can disrupt microtubules or damage DNA, killing the target biological cell. Specificity comes from targeted delivery combined with controlled payload release. It is relative rather than absolute: normal biological cells may share the antigen, and a membrane-permeable released payload can also affect neighboring biological cells. Successful action therefore depends on antigen abundance, accessibility, internalization, linker stability and payload sensitivity.

The hydrogenosomes and mitosomes are specialized mitochondrion-related organelles found in various anaerobic or oxygen-limited eukaryotes. Typical hydrogenosomes support anaerobic ATP production, converting substrates such as pyruvate into products including acetate, carbon dioxide and molecular hydrogen. Substrate-level phosphorylation supplies ATP, while hydrogenase disposes of reducing equivalents as hydrogen; oxygen is not the terminal acceptor of a conventional respiratory chain in this pathway.[ref]
The mitosomes retain selected essential metabolic functions but do not generate ATP by oxidative phosphorylation. In Giardia, their role includes iron-sulfur cluster assembly and protein maturation, demonstrated using organelle markers and biochemical reconstitution.[ref] Their functions are lineage-dependent: Entamoeba histolytica mitosomes compartmentalize a sulfate activation pathway.[ref] Thus neither organelle class should be described simply as a nonfunctional mitochondrion.

The catalytic serine residue supplies a nucleophile: its side-chain oxygen attacks the carbonyl carbon of the beta-lactam ring. This generates a tetrahedral intermediate, followed by opening of the ring and formation of a covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate. c OurBigBook.com
Hexokinase consumes exported ATP in the reaction
glucose+ATP⟶glucose 6−phosphate+ADP.
(11)
The adenine nucleotide translocator imports regenerated ADP, permitting ATP synthase to use the proton-motive force. Increased proton return reduces the opposing gradient and allows faster activity of the electron transport chain and oxygen consumption: this is respiratory control. The first hexokinase additions therefore increase demand and respiration. By about the fourth addition, demand no longer limits the measured flux: the respiratory and transport system has reached its available capacity, so more hexokinase cannot increase it. This does not imply that hexokinase ceases functioning, and its zero incremental control at maximal demand does not make it dispensable for maintaining that demand.

At low ATP demand, the proton-motive force is high and much of the remaining oxygen consumption supports proton leak. Increasing leak conductance then creates a substantial extra demand for proton pumping and strongly changes respiratory flux. At high phosphorylation demand, proton return through ATP synthase is large, the proton-motive force is lower, and leak contributes a much smaller part of total proton return. Control of oxygen consumption shifts toward substrate supply and the respiratory machinery, so the leak flux control coefficient becomes small. The mitochondrial dicarboxylate carrier is an inner-membrane transport protein that exchanges suitable dicarboxylates, including succinate and malate, with counter-substrates such as phosphate. In this experiment, it supplies external succinate to the matrix side of respiratory complex II; the inner mitochondrial membrane does not simply allow unrestricted diffusion of the charged substrate. The reported coefficients add to
0.29+0.04+0.33+0.17+0.03+0=0.86.
(12)
For a complete set of independently scaled activities controlling a steady-state flux, the flux summation theorem gives ∑i​CiJ​=1, where CiJ​=∂lnJ/∂lnEi​. One way to see this is to scale all reaction capacities by the same factor: steady-state concentrations can remain the same while all fluxes scale by that factor, so the summed logarithmic sensitivities equal one. The listed processes therefore leave a net coefficient of about 0.14 unaccounted for under the model assumptions. Measurement uncertainty, imperfect inhibitor specificity or incompatible measurements could also contribute to the discrepancy. Control is distributed; neither the sum nor the largest individual coefficient identifies a single universal rate-limiting step.
