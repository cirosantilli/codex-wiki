RF-averaged trapping potential (Ueff​=Q2∣Eamp​∣2/(4mΩ2))

Write the RF electric field as Eamp​(r)cosΩt, where Eamp​ is a real peak-amplitude vector with a common drive phase. Decompose the trajectory into a slow position R and fast displacement ξ. Over one short RF cycle, hold R fixed. The leading fast equation and solution are
mξ¨​=QEamp​(R)cosΩt,ξ=−mΩ2QEamp​(R)​cosΩt.
(22)
The homogeneous slow part is absorbed into R. Expand the electric field to first order in ξ and average, using ⟨cos2Ωt⟩=1/2:
⟨F⟩=−2mΩ2Q2​(Eamp​⋅∇)Eamp​=−∇(4mΩ2Q2∣Eamp​∣2​).
(23)
The second equality uses zero curl in the quasistatic RF electric field. This derives an effective potential energy, not an extra static electric potential. Its Q2 dependence confines either sign of electric charge towards a field-amplitude minimum. Using an RMS field instead would give a coefficient 1/(2mΩ2) multiplying Q2∣Erms​∣2.
The approximation needs fast displacement small compared with the field's variation length and slow angular frequencies small compared with Ω. In the stated electric quadrupole model, small ∣qi​∣ and ai​ of order qi2​ provide a controlled regime, away from stability boundaries. Add QΦdc​ when U=0. For U=0 the result is
Ueff​=4mΩ2d4Q2V2​(x2+y2+4z2),ωi​≃2Ω​ai​+2qi2​​​.
(24)
For the pure RF case, ωz​≃2ωx​=2ωy​. The square-root condition describes leading local restoring curvature; it is not a substitute for full Mathieu equation stability. Collisions, departures from the ideal electric quadrupole, and a displaced RF null require further analysis.
