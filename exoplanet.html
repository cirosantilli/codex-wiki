Methods of detecting exoplanets

Words: 43
Exoplanet OurBigBook.com
Methods of detecting exoplanets infer or resolve a planet through its effects on stellar light or its own emission. They include Doppler spectroscopy, transit photometry, gravitational microlensing and direct imaging. An exoplanet secondary eclipse measures the loss of planetary light when the planet passes behind its star.

Words: 33
Exoplanet OurBigBook.com
A super-Earth is an exoplanet more massive than Earth but below the ice-giant mass range. The label does not guarantee a rocky exoplanet interior, a specific radius, or habitability. Radius-based usage can overlap the sub-Neptune population.

Teq4​=4a21−AB​​(RA2​TA4​+RB2​TB4​).
(1)

Secondary planetary atmosphere

Words: 41
Exoplanet atmosphere OurBigBook.com
A secondary planetary atmosphere is supplied or replenished after the original nebular-envelope stage, through outgassing, volatile delivery and processing. The present atmospheres of Earth, Venus and Mars are predominantly secondary. The distinction from a primary planetary atmosphere concerns origin, not merely current molecular weight.

Primary planetary atmosphere

Words: 36
Exoplanet atmosphere OurBigBook.com
A primary planetary atmosphere is a retained gas envelope acquired from the protoplanetary nebula, commonly rich in molecular hydrogen and helium. The solar-system giant planets retain such envelopes, while escape and subsequent processing can substantially change their composition.

Planetary troposphere

Words: 79 Articles: 1
Atmospheric pressure-temperature profile OurBigBook.com
The lower part of a planetary atmosphere where temperature generally decreases outward and convection can carry intrinsic or surface heat. Its upper boundary is a tropopause. Stellar irradiation, composition gradients and clouds affect the extent of this region; pressure alone does not universally identify it.
Tropopause
Words: 35
Planetary troposphere OurBigBook.com
The transition above a planetary troposphere, usually near a temperature minimum or the change to a weakly varying or outward-increasing temperature profile. The overlying planetary stratosphere is stable against ordinary thermal convection. Its pressure is planet- and location-dependent.

Square-root exponential atmospheric profile (P=P0​eαT−T0​​)

Words: 128 Articles: 1
Atmospheric pressure-temperature profile OurBigBook.com
For P0​>0, α=0, T≥T0​ and physical T>0, the atmospheric pressure-temperature profile P=P0​exp(αT−T0​​) is equivalent to
T=T0​+α2log2(P/P0​)​,αlog(P/P0​)​≥0.
(2)
The branch restriction is essential: squaring does not create a second physical branch. Its temperature gradient is dT/dlogP=2T−T0​​/α. Thus, in hydrostatic equilibrium, α<0 gives an atmospheric thermal inversion, while α>0 gives outward cooling. At T=T0​ the inverse branch has zero derivative; α=0 gives constant pressure and cannot describe a nontrivial hydrostatic vertical interval.

∇=dlogPdlogT​=αT2T−T0​​​>∇ad​=γγ−1​.
(3)

An irradiated planetary atmosphere receives stellar energy from above while internal heat emerges from below. Their competition creates radiative, convective, and sometimes thermally inverted regions.

Greenhouse effect

Words: 122 Articles: 2
Irradiated planetary atmosphere OurBigBook.com
An atmosphere that absorbs and reemits thermal radiation can reduce outgoing energy at a given surface temperature, requiring a warmer surface to balance absorbed stellar energy. The planetary equilibrium temperature refers to the radiation budget, not the resulting surface temperature. Water, carbon dioxide and clouds can alter this balance.
Greenhouse gas
Words: 78 Articles: 1
Greenhouse effect OurBigBook.com
A greenhouse gas absorbs and emits planetary thermal radiation and therefore contributes to the greenhouse effect. Its climatic impact depends on absorption spectrum, concentration, lifetime and chemical effects. Carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour are important examples.
Global warming potential (GWPH​)
Words: 44

Global warming potential compares the time-integrated radiative forcing of equal-mass emission pulses of a greenhouse gas and carbon dioxide, over a stated time horizon H. It is not an instantaneous temperature ratio, and its numerical value depends on the horizon and included chemical and climatic responses.

Dry adiabatic lapse rate (Γdry​=g/Cp​)
Words: 103 Articles: 1
Adiabatic temperature gradient OurBigBook.com
For a homogeneous dry ideal gas in a hydrostatic adiabat, dT/dz=−g/Cp​, where z increases upwards and Cp​ is the specific heat capacity at constant pressure per unit mass. It follows from dT/dP=1/(ρCp​) and hydrostatic equilibrium. The altitude version of the Schwarzschild criterion is dT/dz<−g/Cp​ for instability; equality is marginal. Moisture, composition gradients and nonideal equations of state modify the criterion.
Adiabatic parcel gradient in a hydrostatic environment (dTg​/dz=−(g/Cp​)Tg​/Tenv​)
Words: 52
Dry adiabatic lapse rate OurBigBook.com
A dry parcel in rapid pressure balance with an ambient ideal gas has dTg​/dP=Tg​R/(PCp​). Ambient hydrostatic equilibrium gives dP/dz=−Pg/(RTenv​), hence dTg​/dz=−(g/Cp​)Tg​/Tenv​. At a temperature-matched launch point this equals the dry adiabatic lapse rate, supplying the local linear stability test. A substantially hotter parcel does not exactly follow the ambient density's hydrostatic adiabat.

Annulus model for transmission spectroscopy
Words: 111 Articles: 1

δλ​=A(1−e−τλ​),A=R⋆2​(Rp​+z)2−Rp2​​≃R⋆2​2Rp​z​.
(7)

δλ​=R⋆2​2​∫Rp​∞​b[1−e−τλ​(b)]db.
(8)
The annulus model replaces this varying slant opacity by an effective constant. The atmospheric signal scales with optical depth when optically thin and saturates at the annular area when opaque.

A stabilizing composition gradient can suppress large overturning motions while permitting double-diffusive convection in small layers separated by interfaces. If heat loss is less efficient than in a single well-mixed adiabat, the planet retains more entropy and contracts more slowly. This is a proposed delayed-cooling contribution to hot-Jupiter radius inflation, with an effectiveness that depends on the gradient and layer structure.
Heating of an inflated giant planet

Hot-Jupiter radius inflation OurBigBook.com
A giant planet can also remain inflated when power is deposited sufficiently deep to alter its internal energy budget. Candidate sources include tidal heating, Joule heating by wind-driven currents, and dissipation of circulation-driven mechanical energy. The depth and efficiency of deposition matter, as discussed in models of hot-Jupiter heating.

Heating of an inflated giant planet OurBigBook.com
Atmospheric winds across a magnetic field can induce electric currents. Their Ohmic heating converts mechanical energy into heat, at local rate q=J⋅E=J2/σ in the simplest resistive description. To maintain hot-Jupiter radius inflation, current paths and deposited heat must influence the deep interior; dissipation restricted to optically thin upper layers need not maintain the planet's entropy.
