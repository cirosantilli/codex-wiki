Logarithmic energy gain from a Peierls distortion (ΔE=−πt4δ2​log∣δ∣t​+O(δ2/t))

Fill the lower energy band of the dimerized tight-binding chain with both spin states. Take zero on-site energy and measure ground-state energy per doubled primitive unit cell. State counting in a Bloch band and q=ka give
E(δ)=−π8​∫0π/2​t2cos2q+δ2sin2q​dq,E(0)=−π8t​.
(44)
Equivalently, for ∣δ∣≤t, E(δ)=−(8t/π)E(1−δ2/t2​), where E(k) is the complete elliptic integral of the second kind in the modulus convention.
For fixed t>0 and ∣δ∣/t→0, the change is
E(δ)−E(0)=−πt4δ2​log∣δ∣t​+O(δ2/t).​
(45)
The logarithm comes from states near the edge of the Brillouin zone. Put u=π/2−q. To leading order the square root there is t2u2+δ2​. For a fixed small cutoff η>0, direct integration gives
∫0η​[t2u2+δ2​−tu]du=2tδ2​log∣δ∣t​+O(δ2/t).
(46)
The rest of the band, and the higher-order corrections near its edge, contribute only O(δ2/t); multiplying by −8/π gives the displayed asymptotic expansion. Here the remainder uses Big O notation. The negative logarithmic term exceeds any fixed quadratic elastic cost Kδ2, K>0, for sufficiently small nonzero δ. This explains the zero-temperature instability to a Peierls distortion in the ideal chain model.
