Linear left inverse criterion (ST=IV​⟺kerT={0})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Linear map
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
A linear map T:V→W between finite-dimensional vector spaces admits a linear left inverse S:W→V if and only if its kernel of a linear map is {0}, equivalently if and only if it is injective.
Necessity follows from Tv=0⟹v=STv=0. For sufficiency, choose a basis v1​,…,vn​ of V. Triviality of the kernel of a linear map implies that Tv1​,…,Tvn​ are linearly independent. By basis extension, they extend to a basis Tv1​,…,Tvn​,wn+1​,…,wm​ of W. Define S(Tvi​)=vi​ and S(wj​)=0, and extend by linearity. Then STvi​=vi​ on a basis, proving ST=IV​.
The freedom in S lies in its values on a direct-sum complement of the image of a linear map. For example, for T(x,y)=(x,y,0), every linear map S(u,v,w)=(u+aw,v+bw) is a left inverse, with arbitrary scalars a,b. A right inverse instead requires T to be a surjective linear map.
