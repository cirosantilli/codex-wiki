Simulated annealing

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Heuristic optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Simulated annealing accepts worse feasible solutions to escape a local minimum. Minimize an objective function E(x), analogous to energy. Effective temperature T>0 has the same units as E, absorbing the Boltzmann constant; it controls exploration, rather than a material's thermodynamic temperature.[ref]
Given current feasible point x, draw y from a proposal distribution q(x,y). For a symmetric proposal distribution, q(x,y)=q(y,x), use the Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability
ΔE=E(y)−E(x),aT​(x,y)=min{1,exp(−ΔE/T)}.
(13)
Accept with this conditional probability; on rejection retain x. Every downhill or equal-cost proposal is accepted. Draw a fresh uniform random variable U on (0,1) and accept if U<aT​(x,y). The off-diagonal transition probability PT​(x,y)=q(x,y)aT​(x,y) includes proposing y.[ref]
For a maximization problem with objective function f, set E=−f: every improving proposed neighbor is accepted, and a worsening move of loss Δ=f(x)−f(y)>0 has conditional probability exp(−Δ/T) of acceptance, under the same symmetry assumption. Likelihood-power annealing uses precisely this sign convention for a log-likelihood. Retain the best feasible solution seen, since the current state can worsen even after a global minimum has been visited.
If the proposal distribution is asymmetric, use the full Metropolis–Hastings acceptance probability, for q(x,y)>0,
aT​(x,y)=min{1,exp(−TE(y)−E(x)​)q(x,y)q(y,x)​}.
(14)
A missing reverse proposal gives zero acceptance. Even a downhill proposal need not be accepted with probability one in this case. Choosing uniformly among a variable number of neighbors is generally asymmetric: q(x,y)=1/d(x) and q(y,x)=1/d(y) give the correction d(x)/d(y), where d is vertex degree.[ref]
The useful idea is to separate exploring from insisting on improvement: an uphill move can open a route to a better feasible solution. Its usefulness depends on the proposal distribution, the scale of the objective function, and the cooling schedule for simulated annealing. Finite practical schedules carry no general guarantee of finding a global minimum.
