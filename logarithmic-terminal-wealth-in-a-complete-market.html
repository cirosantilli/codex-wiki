Logarithmic terminal wealth in a complete market (u(x)=logx−ElogZ)

 ... Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance Utility function Expected utility hypothesis Expected utility maximization Utility duality with martingale deflators
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
In a complete market with a unit cash asset and normalized strictly positive state-price density Z, the optimal positive terminal portfolio wealth is x/Z whenever it is replicable in the admissible class. The state-price budget constraint is an equality: E[Z(x/Z)]=x. The utility conjugate inequality gives ElogH≤logx−ElogZ for every affordable positive claim. This value can be infinite, because E(logZ)+<∞ while E(logZ)− need not be finite. Even completeness for bounded claims suffices to approximate the value: set bn​=E[Z/(Z∨n−1)] and Hn​=x/[bn​(Z∨n−1)]. Each claim is bounded and costs x, while its expected logarithmic utility tends to the displayed value by monotone convergence after an integrable lower bound is subtracted.
