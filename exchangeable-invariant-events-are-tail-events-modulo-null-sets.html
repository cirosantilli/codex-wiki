Exchangeable invariant events are tail events modulo null sets

 ... Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Joint probability distribution Exchangeable random variables Invariant sigma-algebra of an exchangeable sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
For a real sequence of exchangeable random variables, every event A in its invariant sigma-algebra of an exchangeable sequence agrees almost surely with an event of its tail sigma-algebra. Put F=1A​. Approximate F in L1 by its conditional expectation on the first n+1 coordinates. Swap that block with the next n+1 coordinates. Invariance of A and the law shows that the conditional expectation on the second block is an equally good approximation. It is measurable with respect to the tail after index n. The L1 contraction of conditional expectation then bounds ∥F−E[F∣Gn​]∥1​ by twice the approximation error. The reverse martingale convergence theorem gives F=E[F∣⋂n​Gn​] almost surely. Thresholding the latter at 1/2 supplies the required tail event. Together with the reverse inclusion, the two sigma-algebras have identical probability completions.
