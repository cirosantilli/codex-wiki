Gel electrophoresis size calibration (d=a−blog10​(L/L0​))

 Home Biology Gel electrophoresis Agarose gel electrophoresis
OurBigBook.com Words: 233
A gel electrophoresis size calibration relates migration distance to known fragment lengths on the same gel. Here is an explicitly chosen model for linear double-stranded DNA, not measured data or a universal law. Let L be the numerical length in base pairs and d the numerical migration distance in millimetres from the wells:
d=55−12log10​(L/100).
(31)
The logarithm has a dimensionless argument: L/100 is the length divided by 100 base pairs. The chosen DNA ladder fragments L=100,250,500,1000 give d=55.00,50.22,46.61,43.00, respectively. The gel bands therefore appear in the reverse order of length along the direction of migration.
To invert the calibration, rearrange the logarithm:
L=10010(55−d)/12.
(32)
Our chosen unknown L=375 gives d=48.1116248…, between the 250-base pair and 500-base pair references. Inserting that distance into the inverse recovers 375; retaining only d=48.11 gives about 375.12 base pairs. The extra digits reflect rounding in this model, not the resolving power of a real gel.
For linear interpolation between those two reference gel bands, use the logarithm of length. Here the fraction of the distance from the 500-base pair reference toward the 250-base pair reference is t=log10​(500/375)/log10​2≈0.4150, giving L=500(250/500)t=375. Direct linear interpolation of lengths instead gives approximately 396.24 base pairs. This difference explains why the choice of calibration coordinates matters. A real calibration must be checked over its usable range; folded RNA or circular DNA does not automatically share the linear double-stranded DNA calibration.
