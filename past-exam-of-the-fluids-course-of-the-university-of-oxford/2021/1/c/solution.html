The upper condition is a moving-wall no-penetration boundary condition, not zero normal velocity in the laboratory frame. At the hinge the velocity tends to zero. With the arbitrary constant of the streamfunction chosen so that ψ(r,0)=0, these conditions become

A changing angle cannot give a literally steady flow in fixed coordinates. We interpret the stated steadiness as the quasi-steady Stokes approximation at each instantaneous α.
The printed expression is not biharmonic. To test it without reproducing the source formula, write its bracket as Gp​(θ) and its streamfunction as ψp​=−Ωr2Gp​/2. For any angular function G, the Laplacian gives
∇4(r2G)=r2G(4)+4G′′​.

Gp(4)​+4Gp′′​=π2/4−1−16πcos2θ+32sin2θ​.

For example, at the interior point θ=π/8,
∇4ψp​=(π2/4−1)r242​(π−2)Ω​=0(Ω=0).​
(11)
The printed bracket does satisfy Gp​(0)=Gp′​(0)=Gp′​(α)=0 and Gp​(α)=1, so its boundary velocities would require Ω=ωh​. Satisfying these boundary conditions does not cure its nonzero biharmonic operator. The stated series is likewise not a general biharmonic basis: its r2θsin2θ term already fails the displayed test. The separated polar solutions of the biharmonic equation contain additional radial powers; for the required r2 dependence, the angular basis is 1,θ,sin2θ,cos2θ.
The corrected slowly opening Stokes wedge similarity solution follows by setting ψ=−ωh​r2G(θ)/2. The biharmonic equation gives G(4)+4G′′=0. The two lower-wall conditions reduce its general solution to
G=A(cos2θ−1)+B(sin2θ−2θ).
(12)
The upper-wall condition G′(α)=0 fixes the ratio of A and B, and G(α)=1 fixes their scale. An expression that also remains finite at α=π/2 is
G(θ)=2(sinα−αcosα)sinα(1−cos2θ)+cosα(sin2θ−2θ)​,ψ=−21​α˙r2G(θ).​
(13)
For 0<α<π, its denominator is positive: the derivative of sinα−αcosα is αsinα>0. Direct differentiation verifies all four boundary conditions and the biharmonic equation. The resulting velocity components are vr​=−α˙rG′/2 and vθ​=α˙rG.
For a fixed angle of order one, U(r)∼∣α˙∣r, so the local Reynolds number is Rer​∼∣α˙∣r2/ν. One breakdown length is therefore
l1​∼∣α˙∣ν​​.​
(14)
A second is the geometric length l2​∼min(L,W), where L is the distance to the radial ends and W the spanwise width. At r∼l2​, matching to the surrounding bath or three-dimensional end flow matters. Thus r≪min(l1​,l2​) is required for this local two-dimensional Stokes flow, with angle-dependent factors in the inertial estimate. Ideal infinite plates remove the geometric cutoff but not the inertial one.
There is also a temporal restriction: the relevant viscous diffusion time must be short compared with the time Ts​ over which the shape and its velocity field change appreciably. Using r as a conservative diffusion length gives r≪νTs​​. For order-one angles and constant α˙, Ts​∼∣α˙∣−1 gives the same scale as l1​; narrow angles require using their actual gap and angular factors rather than extrapolating this order-one-angle estimate.
