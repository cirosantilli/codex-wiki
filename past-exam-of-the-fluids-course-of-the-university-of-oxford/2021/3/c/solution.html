The gravity-capillary drop with a meridional curvature approximation uses the stipulated κ=−h′′ for this calculation. At equilibrium the thin-film equation gives
rh3(ρgh′−γh′′′)=C.
(40)
Regularity at r=0 forces C=0. In the wetted interior h>0, the equilibrium pressure gradient therefore vanishes, giving
h′′′−l2h′​=0,l=ρgγ​​.​
(41)
This is the capillary length. Integrating the constant-coefficient ordinary differential equation gives
h=C0​+C1​cosh(r/l)+C2​sinh(r/l).
(42)
The central boundary condition h′(0)=0 sets C2​=0, and contact at r=R gives C0​=−C1​cosh(R/l). Write C1​=−αl. The prescribed contact angle fixes
h′(R)=−αsinh(R/l)=−tanθc​,α=sinh(R/l)tanθc​​,h(r)=αl[cosh(R/l)−cosh(r/l)].​
(43)
Here α is the dimensionless coefficient of this drop solution, unrelated to the hinge angle in question 1.
The axisymmetric volume integral is V=2π∫0R​rh(r)dr. Substituting ξ=r/l gives
1=V2πl3α​∫0R/l​[cosh(R/l)−coshξ]ξdξ.​

For numerical evaluation, with s=R/l, the integral is
I(s)=2s2​coshs−ssinhs+coshs−1.
(45)
Together with α=tanθc​/sinhs, this determines R for fixed V and θc​.
The Bond number R2/l2=ρgR2/γ distinguishes the dominant balances. For R≪l, surface tension dominates gravity and the surface is nearly a small-slope constant-curvature cap:
h(r)≈2Rtanθc​​(R2−r2),V≈4πtanθc​​R3.
(46)
For R≫l, gravity flattens the interior, while surface tension bends the edge over the capillary length. The stipulated model gives
h(0)≈ltanθc​,V≈πR2ltanθc​,
(47)
up to relative corrections of order l/R. The contact angle fixes the edge slope and therefore the height scale; conservation of mass then fixes the lateral extent. Consistency of lubrication theory requires small slopes, in particular tanθc​≪1.
The stated approximation differs from axisymmetric free-surface curvature. For an axisymmetric graph with the same sign convention, the sum of principal curvatures is
κ=−r1​drd​(1+h′2​rh′​)≈−(h′′+rh′​).
(48)
The omitted h′/r term is generally of the same order as h′′. For example, h=H(1−r2/R2) gives stipulated κ=2H/R2 but axisymmetric small-slope κ=4H/R2. Thus the required hyperbolic-cosine profile solves the explicitly prescribed reduced model, not the full axisymmetric capillary equilibrium. Keeping both small-slope terms instead gives
h(r)=I1​(R/l)ltanθc​​[I0​(R/l)−I0​(r/l)],
(49)
where I0​,I1​ are modified Bessel functions. The qualitative capillary length and Bond number balances remain valid.
