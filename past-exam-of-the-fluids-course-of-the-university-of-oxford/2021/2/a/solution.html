Compressing a small region raises its mass density and, for positive compressibility, its pressure. The outward pressure gradient force accelerates neighbouring fluid elements. Their outward motion compresses fluid farther away, carrying the disturbance outward. The original compressed region relaxes; inertia allows the fluid motion to continue beyond zero excess pressure, producing a rarefaction and a restoring pressure gradient force. Elastic compression and inertia therefore support a propagating longitudinal wave.
