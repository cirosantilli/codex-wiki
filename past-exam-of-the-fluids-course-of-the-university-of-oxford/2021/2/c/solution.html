Use a plane wave proportional to ei(kx−ωt). Substitution into the convected acoustic wave equation gives the dispersion relation
(ω−U0​k)2=c02​k2,ω=(U0​±c0​)k.​
(24)
The two branches have signed phase velocities and group velocities U0​±c0​. On a branch with ω−U0​k=sc0​k, s=±1, the linearized Euler equation gives
p1​=c02​ρ1​,v1​=sρ0​c0​​ρ1​.
(25)
In subsonic flow with 0≤U0​<c0​, the absence of wave reflection selects the outgoing branch on each side:
ρ1​(x,t)=ρ0​Re[Aei(k±​x−ωt)],k+​=U0​+c0​ω​ (x>0),k−​=U0​−c0​ω​ (x<0).
(26)
For U0​=0, take A real and positive by choosing the source phase, and put k0​=ω/c0​. At ωt=2nπ, the requested spatial profiles are
ρ1​=ρ0​Acos(k0​x),p1​=ρ0​c02​Acos(k0​x),v1​=c0​Asgn(x)cos(k0​x)(x=0).​
(27)
For complex A, retain Re[Aeik0​∣x∣] instead. The normalized sound pressure and acoustic density perturbation lie on the same even cosine curve. The acoustic particle velocity follows that curve on the right and its negative on the left, as in the figure in part 2(a). Compressed fluid moves away from the source on both sides. These are two outgoing travelling waves, not a standing wave. The ideal point loudspeaker supplies the discontinuity of velocity at x=0; the source-free equations apply on either side of it.
