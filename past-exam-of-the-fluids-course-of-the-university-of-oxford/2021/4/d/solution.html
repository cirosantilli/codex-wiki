Use the Joukowski map with ∣c∣<a, in particular its intended range 0≤c<a/2. On the original circle,
X=(a+ac2​)cosθ,Y=(a−ac2​)sinθ.
(61)
Define the exterior inverse
f(Z)=2Z+Z2−4c2​​,
(62)
where the holomorphic square root is chosen so that Z2−4c2​∼Z at infinity. Then f(Z)∼Z is the branch mapping the exterior of the ellipse to ∣z∣>a. Composition of the zero-circulation complex potential from part 4(c) with this inverse gives
W(Z)=βeiαf(Z)2+f(Z)2βa4e−iα​.​
(63)
The ellipse is a streamline because its inverse image is the circle, and W(Z)∼βeiαZ2 at infinity. An additive constant in the complex potential has no effect on the velocity.
The transformed complex velocity obeys the chain rule:
dZdW​=dZ/dzdw/dz​=1−c2/z22βeiαz−2βa4e−iαz−3​.
(64)
Since ∣c∣<a, the denominator is nonzero on and outside ∣z∣=a. The four stagnation points therefore come from
z4=a4e−2iα,zn​=aeiθn​,θn​=−2α​+2nπ​,n=0,1,2,3.
(65)
Their physical locations are
Zn​=(a+ac2​)cosθn​+i(a−ac2​)sinθn​,n=0,1,2,3.​
(66)
They all lie on the ellipse, and their positions are independent of β>0, which only sets the overall speed scale.
The printed restriction that c is real and c<a/2 is insufficient literally: c=−2a satisfies it but makes dZ/dz=0 at z=2a in the exterior, so there is no exterior conformal map of the claimed type; the stated minor semiaxis would also be negative. The intended positive-parameter range, or more generally ∣c∣<a, is needed for the above solution. The reference to a sphere in the source is a wording slip: the geometry is two-dimensional flow around the mapped cylinder's elliptical cross-section.
