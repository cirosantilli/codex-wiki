The Reynolds number compares convective acceleration with viscous acceleration in the Navier-Stokes equation:
Re=νUL​=μρUL​.
(1)
At large Reynolds number, inertia dominates in the bulk; viscous boundary layers can still control wall shear stress, separation and drag. Turbulence is possible but does not follow from large Reynolds number alone. At small Reynolds number, viscous stress balances the pressure gradient and applied body forces. In the quasi-steady Stokes approximation, the velocity field responds instantaneously to the boundary motion and has kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow. Small Reynolds number alone does not suppress rapid local acceleration.
For a swimmer, take a characteristic length L∼1m and speed U∼1ms−1. The supplied kinematic viscosity gives
Re∼10−61×1​=106≫1.​
(2)
Human swimming is therefore an inertia-dominated fluid flow, rather than Stokes flow.

Given an admissible velocity field, recover the pressure gradient from the Stokes equation, ∇p=μ∇2v, and integrate it, fixing the additive constant by matching to the bath pressure near the open ends. The biharmonic equation ensures the compatibility of this integration. For a freely moving hinge, the laboratory velocity field also contains the unknown rigid translation and rotation. Solve the interior and exterior Stokes flows with these wall velocities, then impose zero total external force and torque on the complete apparatus if its motor supplies only internal actuation. The pinned solution alone cannot determine that free motion.
At a fixed angle, the actuation velocity scale is U∼L∣α˙∣. The Stokes equation gives Δp∼μU/L∼μ∣α˙∣. Integrating normal traction over a plate of length L gives a force per unit width of scale
F=μLα˙C(α),​
(16)
with a dimensionless signed coefficient depending on geometry and exterior matching. Normal viscous stress has the same dimensional scale and is included in C. The force reverses when the actuation reverses. A narrow gap can make ∣C∣ large.
More specifically, the corrected local streamfunction gives, up to a reference pressure,
p=μα˙K(α)log(r/r∗​)+constant,K(α)=sinα−αcosα2cosα​.
(17)
Indeed, inserting v=(−α˙rG′/2,α˙rG) into the vector Laplacian gives pr​=−μα˙(G′′′+4G′)/(2r)=μα˙K/r and pθ​=0. For α<π/2, opening produces reduced pressure toward the hinge when matched at r∗​∼L. The logarithmic singularity is integrable: ∫0L​log(L/r)dr=L, consistent with the stated force scale. A real hinge regularizes the singularity; the local formula is not a full end-matched solution.
The scallop theorem applies to a single periodically actuated hinge in a stationary Newtonian fluid with negligible fluid and body inertia. At each shape, Linearity of Stokes flow makes its rigid velocity proportional to α˙. Closing retraces the sequence of shapes and reverses the corresponding motion, irrespective of different opening and closing rates. The hinge may move during each half-cycle, but
net rigid displacement and rotation over a reciprocal cycle=0.​
(18)
A motor can generate instantaneous forces and consume energy through viscous dissipation without producing sustained swimming. Additional independently controlled shape changes, appreciable inertia, or a fluid with memory would require a different analysis.

Compressing a small region raises its mass density and, for positive compressibility, its pressure. The outward pressure gradient force accelerates neighbouring fluid elements. Their outward motion compresses fluid farther away, carrying the disturbance outward. The original compressed region relaxes; inertia allows the fluid motion to continue beyond zero excess pressure, producing a rarefaction and a restoring pressure gradient force. Elastic compression and inertia therefore support a propagating longitudinal wave.
In a local planar description with acoustic displacement ξ(x,t),
ρ1​=−ρ0​∂x​ξ,p1​=c02​ρ1​,ρ0​∂t2​ξ=−∂x​p1​,
(19)
so ξtt​=c02​ξxx​. The speed of sound is a propagation speed, whereas the acoustic particle velocity is the small back-and-forth motion of the medium. In linear acoustics, energy travels with the disturbance without first-order net transport of the fluid over a wave period.

Take the wind to blow toward positive x, so U0​≥0. In subsonic flow, both stationary observers record the source angular frequency ω and, for the imposed density boundary data, the same acoustic density perturbation amplitude ρ0​∣A∣. Their spatial records distinguish the wavelengths and directions:
λdown​=ω2π(c0​+U0​)​,λup​=ω2π(c0​−U0​)​.​
(28)
The downstream pattern moves at c0​+U0​ and the upstream pattern at signed velocity U0​−c0​. Thus the downstream observer sees longer wavelengths while the upstream observer sees shorter ones. The interval containing multiple wavelengths lets each measure the spatial spacing and its direction of travel. The arrival delays at ±L are L/(c0​+U0​) and L/(c0​−U0​) respectively; after arrival their temporal traces differ by these propagation phases, not by temporal frequency.
In supersonic flow, both characteristic curves have positive slopes dx/dt=U0​±c0​. With an initially undisturbed upstream region, the observer at −L sees no source-generated disturbance. The observer at +L can see two downstream travelling waves, with
k+​=U0​+c0​ω​,k−​=U0​−c0​ω​,ρ1​=ρ0​Re[e−iωt(A+​eik+​x+A−​eik−​x)].
(29)
The faster signal arrives first, at L/(U0​+c0​); the slower one arrives at L/(U0​−c0​). Once both are present, their different wavenumbers can produce spatial modulation, while the time dependence at a fixed position remains harmonic with angular frequency ω.
There is a qualification to the source data: the single prescribed value of ρ1​(0+,t) fixes only A+​+A−​=A. It does not determine the two complex amplitudes separately. A second source condition, such as its downstream acoustic particle velocity with amplitude c0​(A+​−A−​), or a specified mass/force source model, is needed. Consequently two downstream branches are allowed; the stated density data alone do not require both to have nonzero amplitude. Extending a formally upstream-going subsonic formula into x<0 when U0​>c0​ would impose a signal arriving from upstream infinity and violate the causal source interpretation.
As U0​→c0​, one laboratory phase velocity tends to zero. Its wavelength tends to zero, its wavenumber diverges, and its arrival time diverges; a finite-time experiment never attains the assumed established upstream field at fixed L. Finite source size, viscosity, thermal losses and eventually molecular scales invalidate this singular inviscid flow limit. With effective acoustic diffusivity νa​, a loss estimate is νa​k2/ω∼νa​ω/(U0​−c0​)2, which diverges. Moreover the finite-amplitude change in the characteristic speed is of order c0​∣A∣, so uniform linearization requires ∣U0​−c0​∣≫c0​∣A∣. At exactly sonic background speed, the zero-speed branch cannot support a nonzero-ω harmonic solution. Nonlinear acoustics and losses must resolve the near-sonic regime; an unlimited spatial compression of the ideal wave is not physical.

The ellipse is a streamline because its inverse image is the circle, and W(Z)∼βeiαZ2 at infinity. An additive constant in the complex potential has no effect on the velocity.

dZdW​=dZ/dzdw/dz​=1−c2/z22βeiαz−2βa4e−iαz−3​.
(64)
