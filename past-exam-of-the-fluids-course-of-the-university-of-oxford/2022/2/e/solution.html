The Hiemenz flow profile changes on η=O(1), so its boundary layer thickness is
δBL​∼Aν​​.​
(29)
A specified edge convention, such as u/(Ax)=0.99, introduces an order-one numerical multiplier requiring the profile to be solved. Scaling alone does not determine that multiplier. The result has no separate dependence on x, y, ρ or p0​ at fixed kinematic viscosity. At fixed dynamic viscosity, it is η/(ρA)​.
The viscous diffusion time across a thickness δ is δ2/ν; the imposed strain has time scale A−1. Their balance sets the thickness. Increasing kinematic viscosity spreads the layer, while increasing the imposed velocity gradient presses the layer toward the wall. The outward tangential velocity increases as Ax, so the decreasing travel time per unit distance offsets the usual downstream broadening.
For a conventional estimate of transition to turbulence, form the local horizontal Reynolds number:
Rex​=ν(Ax)x​=νAx2​.
(30)
If transition is assumed to occur at an empirical value Recrit​, then
l∼ARecrit​ν​​.​
(31)
A rough borrowed threshold of order 105–106 would give l of order 102–103ν/A​. This is a heuristic estimate, not a universal stability prediction: the accelerating outer flow and favorable pressure gradient can delay transition, and disturbance amplitudes and wall roughness matter. Simply setting Rex​∼1 locates failure of the thin-layer approximation near the stagnation point, not onset of turbulence.
