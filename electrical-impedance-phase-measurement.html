Electrical impedance phase measurement (∣Z∣=RV1​/VR​,ϕ=2πΔt/T)

An actual teaching experiment turns the phasor definition into readings. Leipzig University's laboratory handout Measurements with an oscilloscope, experiment E 3e, uses a function generator, a two-channel oscilloscope, a known resistor and a capacitor. Figure 5 places the capacitor above the resistor, with a common return below the resistor. Channel 1 measures the applied terminal electric potential difference v1​; channel 2 measures vR​. The handout asks for peak-to-peak values and relative timing, then comparison of measured and calculated electrical impedances with R=3kΩ, C=0.47μF at 100 and 300Hz. It also asks students to check the oscilloscope time base against a digitally counted generator frequency. These are apparatus and procedural specifications, not reported measured results.[ref]
For this arrangement, Ohm's law gives i(t)=vR​(t)/R if probe loading and the sensing resistor's electrical reactance are negligible. Record both voltage magnitudes with the same convention: peak, peak-to-peak, or root mean square. After the transient settles, compare matched rising zero crossings t1​ of v1​ and tR​ of vR​, with period T=1/f. Then
∣Z∣=RVR​V1​​,Δt=tR​−t1​,ϕ=T2πΔt​,Z=∣Z∣(cosϕ+isinϕ).
(39)
The recovered electrical impedance includes the known resistor. For a separate unknown component in series with that resistor, subtract R from the inferred total electrical impedance. A current crossing earlier than the applied voltage crossing gives Δt<0, hence negative ϕ. For the stipulated series RC impedance example at 1000Hz, this predicts Δt=−0.160717ms; it is a calculated timing, not a lab observation. Timing supplies the complex argument that an amplitude ratio alone cannot recover.
As additional measurement controls, first feed the same sinusoid to both channels to check relative delay and polarity; then replace the reactive chain with a known resistor, expecting nearly zero phase difference. Keep both probe returns at the common return shown in figure 5. A reversed voltage polarity adds half a turn to its inferred wave phase. Match crossings with the same slope and choose −T/2<Δt≤T/2; adjacent cycles otherwise change ϕ by a full turn. A sweep continuous in frequency helps track that branch. These controls test the signed inference rather than assuming that the scope's delay readout has the desired sign.
Measure the loaded terminal electric potential difference on channel 1 at every frequency. The generator's output impedance can change the applied magnitude; its front-panel setting is not the measured terminal value. The generator's internal electrical impedance is outside the terminal ratio above, while the known sensing resistor is inside it. Probe loading, finite timing resolution, component tolerances and distorted waveforms limit the inference. A repeat at lower drive amplitude tests whether one linear electrical impedance still fits. Electrical reactance bench measurement develops these loading and reference-direction issues further.
