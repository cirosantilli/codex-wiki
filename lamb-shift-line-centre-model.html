Lamb shift line-centre model (ν0​=νd​−aBr​)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Lamb shift Lamb-Retherford experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 398
Consider an original illustrative resonance model, with all numerical settings chosen here. On one isolated branch approximate the transition frequency by ν(B)=ν0​+aB, with known slope a=20 MHz/mT and drive frequency νd​=1300 MHz. Choose ν0​=1100 MHz, hence resonance field Br​=10 mT. This local linear model omits hyperfine structure and nonlinear level mixing; it is not the historical hydrogen Zeeman splitting calculation.
Use a Lorentzian line shape for a weak-drive current dip,
I(B)=C−1+4(B−Br​)2/wB2​A​,wB​=∣a∣wν​​.
(2)
Choose baseline C=1, depth A=0.25 in arbitrary detector signal units, and full width at half maximum wν​=100 MHz, so wB​=5 mT. The half-depth points are Br​±wB​/2; this checks the factor of four. If an exponential decay with lifetime τ alone set this width, natural linewidth would give wν​=1/(2πτ) and τ≃1.59 ns. The chosen width is an explanatory input, not a measurement of a historical lifetime.
Generate 65 equally spaced fields from 2 to 18 mT and add independent Gaussian noise with known standard deviation σI​=0.004 signal units. A simultaneous nonlinear regression fit of C,A,Br​,wB​ gives Br​≃10.0007 mT, wB​≃4.9305 mT and ν0​≃1099.986 MHz. With residual derivatives Jik​=∂I(Bi​)/∂θk​ and θ=(C,A,Br​,wB​), the local covariance matrix estimate is
Cov(θ^)≃σI2​(JTJ)−1.
(3)
It gives centre standard deviation 0.01427 mT, or 0.285 MHz in the inferred intercept. This uses the stipulated noise variance and a local linear approximation, rather than treating the width as the centre error. Repeated noisy samples can locate a broad line's centre much more closely than its width.
Include a common magnetic calibration offset with standard deviation ub​=0.020 mT, a fractional gain error ug​=0.001, and independent drive-frequency error ud​=0.100 MHz. To first order the inferred variance is
u2(ν0​)≃a2u2(Br​)+a2ub2​+(aBr​)2ug2​+ud2​.
(4)
Here u denotes a standard uncertainty, not a frequency. The four contributions in MHz are 0.285, 0.400, 0.200, and 0.100; adding their squares gives u(ν0​)≃0.540 MHz. The calibration offset shifts all sampled fields together, so increasing the sample count cannot average it away. Uncertainty in a, unresolved components, asymmetric line distortion, and errors in the assumed field dependence would require additional terms or a richer model. The historical inference requires the appropriate Zeeman splitting branches, rather than simply drawing a straight line through one resonance.
