Entropy (S)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 18
Entropy is an extensive quantity and a state function of an equilibrium system, measured in JK−1. Differences between equilibrium states are calculated along a reversible thermodynamic process:
S2​−S1​=∫12​TδQrev​​.
(1)
Here heat entering the system is positive and T is its absolute thermodynamic temperature. The reversible path used for calculation need not be the path actually taken. Dividing the actual heat by a single temperature generally fails for an irreversible process.
For example, put two rigid bodies, each with constant heat capacity C=100JK−1, at 600K and 300K in an otherwise isolated system. Assume each body has a uniform internal temperature, no phase transition occurs, and no work is extracted. Their final thermal equilibrium is at 450K, because the internal energy lost by one equals that gained by the other. Using entropy from heat capacity,
ΔSh​=Clog(450/600)=−28.77JK−1,ΔSc​=Clog(450/300)=40.55JK−1,
(2)
so ΔStot​=Clog(9/8)=11.78JK−1. One body's entropy decreases while the total increases. [ref]
In statistical mechanics, Boltzmann's entropy formula counts equally likely microstates compatible with a macrostate; Gibbs entropy handles a specified probability distribution over microstates. If the number of equally likely microstates doubles, entropy increases by kB​log2. The logarithm makes multiplicative counts additive for independent systems. These are precise statements about states and probabilities, not a universal measure of visible messiness. Calling entropy merely "disorder" obscures the thermal calculation above and hides which microstates are being counted. [ref]
The usefulness of entropy is that measurable heat capacities determine its changes, and the Second law of thermodynamics turns those changes into constraints on processes. It also connects macroscopic measurements to microscopic descriptions without requiring us to track every particle.

Entropy generated by cooling a finite body in a reservoir (ΔStot​=C[ln(T0​/Ti​)+Ti​/T0​−1])

Words: 54
Entropy OurBigBook.com
For a body of constant heat capacity C cooling from Ti​ to a thermal reservoir at T0​>0, the body's entropy change is Cln(T0​/Ti​), while the reservoir gains C(Ti​−T0​)/T0​. Their sum is nonnegative because r−1−lnr≥0 for r=Ti​/T0​>0. The logarithmic body contribution is computed along a hypothetical reversible path; the actual finite-temperature-difference heat transfer is irreversible.

Residual entropy (S0​)

Words: 54
Entropy OurBigBook.com
Residual entropy is entropy remaining in the zero-temperature limit. An equilibrium ground-state degeneracy g0​ gives S0​=kB​lng0​ for the complete system; an extensive residual entropy requires a correspondingly large state count. Frozen configurational disorder can also leave an experimentally inferred residual entropy when a sample fails to equilibrate. The zero-entropy perfect-crystal form of the third law of thermodynamics assumes a unique, fully ordered equilibrium ground state.

Specific entropy (s=S/M)

Words: 33
Entropy OurBigBook.com
Specific entropy is entropy per unit mass. For a perfect gas with constant specific heat capacity cv​ at constant volume and specific-heat ratio γ, differences obey
s2​−s1​=cv​log[p1​p2​​(ρ2​ρ1​​)γ],
(3)
where p is pressure and ρ is mass density. Thus a homentropic flow has constant p/ργ.

Boltzmann's entropy formula (S=kB​logΩ)

Words: 1k Articles: 7
Entropy OurBigBook.com
Why does a gas spread through an available container? One part of the answer is counting: a nearly uniform macrostate can be realized by vastly more microstates than a macrostate with every particle crowded into one corner. Boltzmann's entropy formula turns that multiplicity into entropy. The useful insight is that a large entropy measures how many microscopic realizations fit a specified macroscopic description, rather than how untidy a picture looks.
For a specified macrostate M containing Ω(M) accessible, equally likely microstates, write
S(M)=kB​lnΩ(M),pi​=Ω(M)1​(i compatible with M).
(4)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, and ln is the natural logarithm, also written log in this article's title. The count Ω(M) is dimensionless; entropy has units of energy divided by temperature. Always specify the macrostate, the constraints and the model's microscopic resolution before counting. A microcanonical ensemble provides a familiar equilibrium setting for the equal-probability assumption. [ref]
The natural logarithm compresses an enormous count into a manageable number and makes products of counts into sums of entropies; see additivity of Boltzmann entropy. With this counting convention, one allowed microstate has zero Boltzmann entropy. This zero refers to the chosen model and resolution; it does not by itself assign an absolute entropy to a real material.

Microstate multiplier from photon absorption (Ωf​/Ωi​≃exp[hc/(λkB​T)])

Words: 47
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
A single photon thermalized in a macroscopic body adds energy hc/λ. If its heat capacity is sufficiently large that the temperature change is negligible, the entropy increase is hc/(λT). The Boltzmann entropy then gives the displayed ratio of accessible microstates. The exponential can be enormous even though the deposited energy is macroscopically tiny.

Configurational entropy (Sconf​=kB​lnΩconf​)

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
The part of entropy associated with the number of distinct spatial or occupancy arrangements consistent with chosen macroscopic constraints. For equally weighted configurations, the Boltzmann entropy formula gives the displayed expression. Unequal probabilities require −kB​∑i​pi​lnpi​ over configurations. For n indistinguishable empty sites among M otherwise occupied sites, Ωconf​=(nM​). Vibrational or internal degrees of freedom can contribute additional entropy even at fixed site arrangement.

Equiatomic configurational entropy (ΔSmix​=Rlnn)

Words: 39
Configurational entropy OurBigBook.com
Randomly distributing n species with equal fractions over N sites gives Ω=N!/[(N/n)!]n. Stirling approximation gives S=kB​Nlnn, or Rlnn per mole of sites. Configurational entropy alone does not decide whether a multicomponent alloy is a stable single thermodynamic phase.

Boltzmann entropy of a two-box occupancy (S(k)=kB​ln(kN​))

Words: 317
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
Take N=20 distinguishable, labeled particles and two boxes of equal volume. Retain only whether each particle is in the left or right box: use a bit 0 for left and 1 for right. Each length-20 string is one coarse microstate of this toy model. Assume the labels make independent side choices with equal probability, so the 220=1048576 strings are equiprobable. Equal volumes motivate these weights for noninteracting particles with no preference for either box; equal volumes alone would not establish them for an arbitrary interacting gas.
Now retain only the number k in the left box as the macrostate. Choosing which k labels are left gives a binomial coefficient, so
Ω(k)=(k20​)=k!(20−k)!20!​,S(k)=kB​ln(k20​),P(k)=220(k20​)​.
(5)
The factorials count assignments to labels; the probability of a macrostate sums the probabilities of its constituent microstates. This is the binomial distribution with parameters 20 and 1/2. The same binomial coefficient appears in the counting of distinguishable two-level systems, although here the macrostate records occupancy rather than energy. [ref]
The largest binomial coefficient occurs at k=10, the balanced macrostate. All-left means k=20 and supplies just one string. Thus
Ω(10)Ω(20)P(10)​=184756,=1,≈0.176197,​S(10)/kB​S(20)/kB​P(20)​=ln184756≈12.126791,=0,=2−20≈9.53674×10−7.​
(6)
There are 184756 balanced strings for every all-left string, even though each individual string has the same probability. Exact balance has about 17.6% of the total probability; equilibrium reasoning concerns the many macrostates near balance too, rather than certainty of exactly ten particles on each side. The diagram compares the counts with their natural logarithms.
Four fair, independent coins give a smaller worked example with the same binomial distribution. If k is the number of heads, the five macrostates k=0,1,2,3,4 contain respectively 1,4,6,4,1 microstates. Their probabilities are respectively 1/16,4/16,6/16,4/16,1/16. In particular, two heads is one macrostate containing six ordered coin sequences, with Boltzmann entropy kB​ln6 in this counting analogy. A prescribed ordered sequence has one realization. The next diagram enumerates the same sixteen strings as left/right assignments.

Boltzmann entropy when a restriction is removed (ΔS=kB​ln(Ωf​/Ωi​))

Words: 210
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
In Boltzmann entropy of a two-box occupancy, impose an initial restriction that all labels are left. There is one allowed coarse microstate. Remove that restriction, allowing all occupancy values: the allowed set is now the union of every occupancy macrostate, containing 2N strings. If the final description assigns equal probability to this whole set, its Boltzmann entropy change is
Ωi​=1,Ωf​=∑k=0N​(kN​)=2N,ΔS=kB​lnΩi​Ωf​​=NkB​ln2.
(7)
The counting identity is the binomial theorem. For twenty labels, ΔS=20kB​ln2≈13.862944kB​. This final description fixes neither box occupancy; it is coarser than the particular macrostate k=10, whose Boltzmann entropy is kB​ln184756. The two counts answer different questions, so substituting the balanced macrostate for the full allowed set would change the calculation.
For four labels, the initial all-left string is 0000; removing the restriction makes all sixteen strings available. The k=2 column has only six strings. Accordingly, the whole allowed set has Boltzmann entropy kB​ln16=4kB​ln2, while the balanced macrostate has Boltzmann entropy kB​ln6.
Removing a barrier changes the allowed set immediately, but it does not instantaneously randomize the actual probability distribution. Motion and equilibration must make those configurations accessible. The counting result illustrates the configurational contribution to free expansion; it does not describe the relaxation time or every contribution to the entropy of a real gas.

Additivity of Boltzmann entropy (ΩAB​=ΩA​ΩB​,SAB​=SA​+SB​)

Words: 133
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
For two subsystems with independently specified macrostates, suppose every allowed microstate of A can be paired with every allowed microstate of B. Their allowed set is a Cartesian product. With equal probability for each pair, Boltzmann's entropy formula gives
ΩAB​=ΩA​ΩB​,SAB​=kB​ln(ΩA​ΩB​)=SA​+SB​.
(8)
For example, take two independent copies of the toy model, each with two labels choosing between its two boxes. Each copy has four configurations and Boltzmann entropy kB​ln4. Combining their independent labels gives sixteen configurations and Boltzmann entropy kB​ln16=2kB​ln4. This explains why entropy adds even though microscopic counts multiply. [ref]
The product assumption matters. A constraint shared between the subsystems can forbid some pairs; for example, fixing only their combined energy generally requires summing over its possible divisions. See entropy maximization under thermal contact for how thermal equilibrium selects the dominant division. Statistical independence cannot be inferred merely from drawing a boundary between two parts.

Scope of Boltzmann's entropy formula

Words: 293
Boltzmann's entropy formula OurBigBook.com
The strings in Boltzmann entropy of a two-box occupancy are complete microstates only within the binary toy model. They retain a coarse-grained variable for each particle and omit its exact position within a box and its momentum. An actual classical gas instead occupies continuous phase space; obtaining a dimensionless count from a phase space volume requires a specified cell convention and the appropriate treatment of particle identity. A quantum state count must respect the allowed quantum states and quantum statistics. Thus 2N is not a general count of microstates for a gas obeying quantum ideal-gas statistics. [ref]
For a discrete probability distribution with unequal weights, use the existing Gibbs entropy article; its equal-weight case gives Boltzmann's entropy formula. Shannon entropy uses the corresponding information measure, with the logarithm base setting its units. A count alone cannot replace the specified probability distribution, as the Boltzmann distribution examples demonstrate. [ref]
Overwhelming multiplicity explains why thermodynamic equilibrium is overwhelmingly probable in macroscopic systems under the appropriate statistical assumptions. It does not make low-entropy thermal fluctuations logically impossible. Nor does counting alone derive irreversible macroscopic evolution: one also needs dynamics, an initial preparation, and a reason the system samples the allowed microstates on the timescale of interest. [ref]
Historically, Boltzmann's 1877 paper counted microscopic arrangements, called complexions, and related the natural logarithm of their number to entropy. The Sharp and Matschinsky translation appeared in Entropy 17 (2015), pages 1971–2009, DOI 10.3390/e17041971. Its notation differs from ours: Boltzmann's permutability measure Ω is already logarithmic, whereas here Ω is the count. [ref] Josiah Willard Gibbs's Elementary Principles in Statistical Mechanics (1902) developed the statistical ensemble framework; Chapter XIV compares several statistical definitions of entropy with thermodynamics. [ref] [ref]

Comoving entropy (Sc​=a3s)

Words: 51
Entropy density OurBigBook.com
Comoving entropy is the entropy density multiplied by the physical volume factor a3 for a fixed comoving region in an expanding homogeneous universe. In adiabatic expansion it is conserved. Separate decoupled radiation sectors conserve their own comoving entropy, so disappearance of massive species can heat the still-equilibrated sector relative to a decoupled species.

Entropy density at zero chemical potential (s=(ρ+P)/T)

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Entropy density OurBigBook.com
For an extensive equilibrium system, the thermodynamic Euler relation gives E=TS−PV+μN. At zero chemical potential, divide by V to obtain s=(ρ+P)/T. Equivalently, dP=(ρ+P)dT/T makes
d(T(ρ+P)V​)=Td(ρV)+PdV​.
(10)
The first law of thermodynamics identifies this as dS; the thermodynamic Euler relation fixes the entropy normalization. For adiabatic expansion satisfying the cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation, the numerator on the right vanishes, proving cosmological entropy conservation.

Nonrelativistic entropy at zero chemical potential (s∼gm3(2π)−3/2x−1/2e−x)

Words: 50
Entropy density at zero chemical potential OurBigBook.com
For a dilute nonrelativistic gas with x=m/T≫1 and zero chemical potential, the Bose-Einstein distribution approaches the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. Expanding E=m+p2/(2m)+⋯ in the number density integral gives n∼g(mT/(2π))3/2e−x. Since ρ=mn+3nT/2+⋯ and P=nT, the entropy density at zero chemical potential gives s=n(x+5/2+⋯) and hence the displayed leading expression. This includes the internal multiplicity g; a single real scalar field has g=1.
