Kruskal extension across a simple static horizon (r∗​=(2κ)−1log∣r−rH​∣+h(r))

For ds2=−fdt2+dr2/f+R2dΩ2, suppose f and R are analytic at rH​, f(rH​)=0, f′(rH​)=2κ>0 and R(rH​)>0. The tortoise coordinate obeys the displayed expansion with analytic h. For U=−e−κ(t−r∗​) and V=eκ(t+r∗​) in the right exterior, −UV=(r−rH​)e2κh(r) after fixing an additive constant. The inverse function theorem makes r analytic in UV through zero, while ds2=fdUdV/(κ2UV)+R2dΩ2 has a finite nonzero radial coefficient there. This supplies the local analytic extension and its bifurcate Killing horizon.
