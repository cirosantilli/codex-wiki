Linear independence (∑i​ai​vi​=0⟺a1​=⋯=an​=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 976 Articles: 8
In a vector space V over a specified field F, a finite family of vectors (v1​,…,vn​) is linearly independent when, for coefficients ai​∈F,
∑i=1n​ai​vi​=0⟺a1​=⋯=an​=0.
(8)
Here the left-hand zero is the zero vector, while the right-hand zeros belong to the field F. Thus linear independence excludes every nontrivial linear relation. For an infinite family, require this condition for every finite subfamily.[ref]
For v1​=(1,1) and v2​=(1,−1) over the real numbers, the linear relation av1​+bv2​=0 gives a+b=0 and a−b=0. Adding and subtracting yields 2a=2b=0, hence a=b=0. This verifies linear independence directly and explains why the linear combination giving (3,1) has exactly the coefficients (2,1).
A linearly independent family cannot contain the zero vector: a coefficient of 1 on that entry and 0 on every other entry would be a nontrivial linear relation. The empty family is linearly independent, because its empty sum is the zero vector and there are no coefficients that could be nonzero. Linear independence identifies a collection without redundant vectors; combined with a spanning set, it gives a basis. The uniqueness of linear combination coefficients makes its representations unambiguous.
