Higher-order test for separated extrema

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Hessian matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
For f(x,y)=g(x)+h(y), a degenerate Hessian matrix can be supplemented by the first nonzero term of each one-variable displacement. Two negative leading even powers give a strict local maximum, two positive ones a strict local minimum, and opposite signs give a saddle. Looking along the coordinate directions and bounding the separated terms avoids an inconclusive quadratic test.
For an explicit contrast, consider
f+​(x,y)=x4+y4,f−​(x,y)=x4−y4.
(22)
Both have zero gradient and the same zero Hessian matrix at the origin:
Hf+​​(0,0)=Hf−​​(0,0)=(00​00​).
(23)
Nevertheless, f+​ is positive at every nonzero point, so the origin is a strict global minimum. For f−​, the restrictions f−​(t,0)=t4>0 and f−​(0,t)=−t4<0 for t=0 show that the origin is a saddle point of a scalar function. Identical quadratic information can conceal different geometry when higher-order terms are decisive.
