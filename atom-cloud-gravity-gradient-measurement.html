Atom-cloud gravity-gradient measurement

 ... Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Tidal tensor Gravity gradiometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
A laboratory gravity gradiometry experiment used two vertically separated clouds of chemical atoms in vacuum, launched into free fall. Shared laser beams applied three pulses (π/2,π,π/2), splitting and recombining matter waves. Balanced photodiodes measured probe-light absorption to infer internal-state populations; scanning the last pulse's wave phase produced an interference fringe.[ref]
The difference of fringe wave phases obeys δϕ=keff​⋅Δgτ2, with effective wave vector keff​ and pulse spacing τ: shared reference motion cancels, leaving differential gravitational acceleration. Eight lead bricks, each approximately 12.5kg, were placed symmetrically around the lower chamber, about 0.2m from the fountain apex. The measured signal was (8.1±2.1)×10−9g, versus a calculated 8.2×10−9g, where g denotes terrestrial gravitational acceleration.[ref]
Controls deliberately shook the shared reference reflector and compared actively isolated and unisolated operation. Tilting tests exposed sensitivity to Coriolis acceleration and centrifugal acceleration. Thus shared translation is rejectable, while rotation and finite measurement uncertainty remain limitations. The brick signal detects spatially varying gravity; it does not establish a precision value of the gravitational constant.[ref]
