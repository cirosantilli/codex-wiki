On-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop (Bz​(z)=2(R2+z2)3/2μ0​IR2​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Biot-Savart law
OurBigBook.com Words: 432
A circular loop of radius R carrying electric current I has, on its axis,
B(z)=2(R2+z2)3/2μ0​IR2​z^,
(111)
where the sign of z^ is fixed by the right-hand rule. At its centre, B(0)=μ0​I/(2R).
Put the loop in the xy plane, centered at the origin, with positive electric current counterclockwise when viewed from +z. The right-hand rule then gives the central magnetic field along +z. Parameterize source positions by the angle ϕ and observe at (0,0,z):
r′=(Rcosϕ,Rsinϕ,0),dℓ′=R(−sinϕ,cosϕ,0)dϕ,R=(−Rcosϕ,−Rsinϕ,z).
(112)
Here R without bold type is the loop radius, whereas R is the source-to-observer displacement. Their cross product is
dℓ′×R=(Rzcosϕ,Rzsinϕ,R2)dϕ.
(113)
The denominator ∣R∣3=(R2+z2)3/2 is constant around the loop. The integrals of sine and cosine over a full turn vanish, while the axial contribution adds to 2πR2. Substitution into the Biot-Savart law proves the on-axis formula. Symmetry cancels transverse components; it does not cancel the axial components.
For an original one-turn example in the same vacuum approximation, choose R=0.04m and I=2A. With μ0​≈4π×10−7Tm/A,
B(0)=10πμT≈31.42μT,B(0)B(z)​=(1+R2z2​)−3/2.
(114)
At z=0.03m, the source-to-observer distance is 0.05m, so B(z)/B(0)=64/125=0.512 and B(z)≈16.08μT. At z=−0.03m, the magnetic field has the same magnitude and still points along +z. The point has crossed the loop plane, but the loop's electric current has not reversed. Reversing the electric current reverses the magnetic field on both sides.
For ∣z∣≪R, a Taylor expansion gives B(z)/B(0)=1−3z2/(2R2)+O(z4/R4): the center is a smooth maximum, with zero first derivative. For ∣z∣≫R,
B(z)∼2∣z∣3μ0​IR2​z=2π∣z∣3μ0​m​z,m=IπR2.
(115)
This is the axial magnetic dipole field of magnetic dipole moment m=IπR2z.[ref] A compact closed loop has an inverse-cube axial far field, in contrast to the inverse-distance field of the infinite straight wire. The SI units of the exact loop formula are μ0​IR2/(R2+z2)3/2∼μ0​I/length, hence tesla.
For N coincident turns carrying the same electric current, the superposition principle multiplies the ideal result by N. A real coil has radial width and axial length: if those are not negligible, integrate or sum over its individual turns. This is why a single measured coil diameter and a turn count may not determine the full spatial magnetic field precisely. A collection of loops becomes a solenoid; integrating their axial contributions gives the on-axis magnetic field of a finite solenoid.
