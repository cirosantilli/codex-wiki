Finite titrant response of a buffer

OurBigBook.com Words: 203
Prepare the same ideal monoprotic acid model at initial pH 5, with appropriate accompanying ions to balance electric charge. To predict the new pH after a finite signed addition Δb, solve Q(pH1​)−Q(5)=Δb using the chemical equilibrium expression above. For Δb=0.002molL−1 of added chemical base, the calculated final pH is 5.366798 at C=0.010molL−1, but 5.034749 at C=0.100molL−1. The initial local approximation gives changes of about 0.34605 and 0.034730, respectively; its accuracy decreases as the partner balance changes appreciably.
For the dilute example, additions beyond approximately 0.005molL−1 in either direction exceed the initial inventory of one partner. The full chemical equilibrium calculation still produces a finite pH; an inventory-only Henderson-Hasselbalch equation calculation would attempt to use a negative partner quantity. At +0.006molL−1 the dilute example reaches pH 10.99564, whereas the concentrated example reaches only 5.104698. This demonstrates a finite operating range, not an abrupt cessation of all buffer capacity.
