Hilbert space (H)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 93
A Hilbert space is a real or complex inner product space that is complete in the norm ∥v∥=⟨v,v⟩​. Completeness means that every Cauchy sequence has a limit in the space: if its terms become arbitrarily close in this norm, they converge to an allowed vector. The inner product supplies lengths and orthogonality; completeness ensures that limiting approximation procedures do not leave the space.
Finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces are examples, but the same geometry also works for sequences and functions. In the l2 sequence space,
⟨u,v⟩=∑n=1∞​un​vn​​,∥u∥2=∑n=1∞​∣un​∣2.
(1)
In the L2 space on [−1,1] with Lebesgue measure,
⟨f,g⟩=∫−11​f(x)g(x)​dx,∥f∥2=∫−11​∣f(x)∣2dx.
(2)
These formulas use the convention linear in the first argument. Elements of the L2 space are equivalence classes of square-integrable functions agreeing almost everywhere, so zero distance really identifies the same element. A vector can therefore be a whole function, and its squared length can measure an integrated squared error.
For example, approximating f(x)=x2 by a+bx is a nearest-point problem in this Hilbert space. The allowed approximations form the linear span of 1,x, and the orthogonal projection is the constant 1/3. Its residual x2−1/3 is orthogonal to both allowed directions because its integral and its inner product with x vanish. The geometry therefore identifies the best approximation without searching through every pair of coefficients.
This is useful whenever a complicated vector must be represented by a tractable collection of directions. Fourier series approximate functions with orthogonal oscillations; the Parseval identity relates the sum of squared coefficients to the squared norm. Least-squares solutions approximate attainable data using a linear operator. The Hilbert projection theorem explains why a nearest element exists and is unique for a nonempty closed convex set. Distinct application settings share the same geometry of useful components and orthogonal residuals. Casey Rodriguez's MIT lectures develop these connections between Hilbert spaces, Fourier series, and nearest-point problems.[ref]
A historical mathematical setting is David Hilbert's Grundzüge einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen (1912). Its preface states that it reprints six communications published during 1904–1910 on linear integral equations. This provides a concrete source for the integral-equation setting associated with the name, while the abstract definition above incorporates later developments.[ref]

Reducing subspace of a Hilbert-space operator (TM⊆M,T∗M⊆M)

Words: 63
Hilbert space OurBigBook.com
A closed subspace of a Hilbert space reduces a bounded operator when both the operator and its adjoint leave the subspace invariant. Equivalently, the orthogonal projection onto it commutes with the operator: the adjoint invariance makes its orthogonal complement invariant too. For a star-closed algebra of operators, the closed span of the orbit of any vector is reducing for the whole algebra. This enables an orthogonal direct-sum decomposition into cyclic actions when constructing Borel functional calculus for a normal operator.

Norm-compact unit ball criterion

Words: 52
Hilbert space OurBigBook.com
The closed unit ball of a Hilbert space is compact in norm exactly when the space is finite-dimensional. Finite-dimensional compactness follows from the Heine-Borel theorem. In infinite dimension, an orthonormal sequence has pairwise distance 2​ and no convergent subsequence. Restricting a compact operator that equals a nonzero multiple of the identity on a subspace therefore forces that subspace to be finite-dimensional.

Closed subspace of a Hilbert space (V=V)

Words: 41
Hilbert space OurBigBook.com
A vector subspace closed in the norm topology of a Hilbert space is complete in the inherited norm and inner product. Its orthogonal complement gives a decomposition of the ambient Hilbert space into an orthogonal direct sum. This closedness permits orthogonal projection and ensures limits used in range arguments remain in the subspace. Every finite-dimensional vector subspace is closed.

Unitary extension of a finite-dimensional isometry (⟨ui​,uj​⟩=δij​)

A linear isometry from a subspace of a finite-dimensional Hilbert space into the same ambient space extends to a unitary operator. Complete an orthonormal basis of the input subspace and its isometric image to full orthonormal bases, then map the first to the second. The equal complement dimensions make this possible. Orthogonality of the specified image columns, not normalization alone, is essential. In an infinite-dimensional space arbitrary isometries need not be surjective; the finite-dimensional same-space hypothesis cannot be dropped.

Best N-term wavelet approximation of piecewise Hölder functions

Words: 78
Best N-term approximation OurBigBook.com
For a bounded function on an interval with finitely many jumps and piecewise Cα bounds, localized orthonormal wavelets with sufficient vanishing moments give squared best N-term approximation error O(N−2α). There are O(2j) smooth-region coefficients of size O(2−j(α+1/2)), and only O(K) jump-crossing coefficients of size O(2−j/2) per level. Retain all coefficients through level J and the jump-crossing coefficients through L=⌈2αJ⌉ when α>1/2. This costs O(2J+KJ) and leaves squared tails O(2−2αJ+2−L). Choosing 2J comparable to N proves the rate by the Parseval identity.

Hilbert space completion

The Hilbert space completion of an inner product space V is a Hilbert space containing an isometric dense copy of V. It can be constructed from Cauchy sequences in V, identifying two sequences when the norm of their difference tends to zero.
The space of finitely supported sequences with the l2 sequence space norm illustrates the need for completion. Let u(n)=(1,1/2,…,1/n,0,…). For m>n,
∥u(m)−u(n)∥22​=∑k=n+1m​k21​≤∫n∞​x2dx​=n1​.
(3)
This is a Cauchy sequence, but its coordinatewise limit (1/k)k≥1​ is not finitely supported. It belongs to the l2 sequence space, which is the completion. The same construction permits infinitely many small components while controlling their total squared size. An infinite orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space consequently permits norm-convergent series; a Hamel basis represents each vector by a finite sum, so these two uses of "basis" must be distinguished.

Hilbert projection theorem

Words: 778 Articles: 9
Hilbert space OurBigBook.com
For every nonempty closed convex set C in a Hilbert space and every x in that space, there is a unique p∈C satisfying
∥x−p∥=infz∈C​∥x−z∥.
(4)
Closedness and completeness let a limiting approximation attain the infimum; convexity prevents two different minimizers. The proof idea is to choose a sequence approaching the infimum. The parallelogram identity and the admissible midpoint make it a Cauchy sequence; applying the same identity to two putative minimizers proves uniqueness.
For a closed subspace of a Hilbert space, this nearest-point map is an orthogonal projection and is linear. For a general closed convex set it need not be linear: projecting the real line onto [0,1] clips values below zero to zero and values above one to one. The theorem is therefore useful for both linear approximation and constrained nearest-point problems.[ref]
Closedness is essential even in familiar examples. For the open interval (0,1)⊆R and x=0, the distance infimum is zero but no nearest point exists. The same obstruction appears when approximating (1/k)k≥1​ by finitely supported sequences: the infimum is zero, but the limit lies outside that subspace until we take its Hilbert space completion.

For a bounded linear operator T:H→K between real or complex Hilbert spaces, its adjoint is the unique bounded linear operator T∗:K→H satisfying
⟨Tx,y⟩K​=⟨x,T∗y⟩H​(x∈H, y∈K).
(14)

A=(20​11​),x=(11​),y=(10​),Ax=(31​),A∗y=(21​).
