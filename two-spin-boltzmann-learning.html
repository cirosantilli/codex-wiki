Two-spin Boltzmann learning (Z(J)=4coshJ,ℓ′(J)=1/2−tanhJ)

OurBigBook.com Words: 176
Take two visible bipolar units, no hidden units, zero thresholds, β=1, and EJ​(x)=−Jx1​x2​. As original training data, assign probabilities 3/8 each to (+,+) and (−,−) and 1/8 each to (+,−) and (−,+). The data pair product averages to 1/2. Enumeration of all four states gives two weights eJ and two e−J, hence the partition function Z(J)=4coshJ and
⟨x1​x2​⟩−​=tanhJ,ℓ(J)=2J​−log(4coshJ).
(12)
Starting at J=0, the positive-minus-negative difference is 1/2−0. A gradient ascent step of size 1/2 gives J=1/4. Each aligned state now has probability 0.311230 and each opposed state 0.188770, bringing the model toward the data. The log-likelihood increases from −1.386294 to −1.292224. Solving ℓ′(J)=0 gives J∗​=(log3)/2, which reproduces the data exactly. Since ℓ′′(J)=−1/cosh2J<0, this is the unique maximum. These are exact enumerations and their decimal evaluations, with no sampling or laboratory measurements.
