Raoult's law (pi​=xi​pi∗​)

In the ideal solution and ideal gas vapor approximation, each component's partial pressure equals its liquid mole fraction times its pure-component vapor pressure at the same temperature:
pi​=xi​pi∗​(T),P=∑i​pi​=∑i​xi​pi∗​(T),yi​=Ppi​​.
(11)
These pressure formulas neglect the change in the pure-liquid reference between its saturation pressure and P; appreciable real gas behavior also requires a more general treatment. The ideal solution definition in terms of chemical potentials does not require either approximation.[ref]
A short derivation explains the reference choice. At vapor-liquid equilibrium, each component has the same chemical potential in the liquid and vapor. For an ideal gas mixture, chemical potential of an ideal gas gives
μiv​=μiv,∘​(T)+RTlnp∘pi​​,μi∗​(T,pi∗​)=μiv,∘​(T)+RTlnp∘pi∗​​,
(12)
where p∘ is a reference pressure. Approximate μi∗​(T,P) by μi∗​(T,pi∗​) and subtract the pure-component equation from the mixture equation:
RTlnxi​=RTlnpi∗​pi​​.
(13)
Taking the exponential function gives Raoult's law. Summing partial pressures and normalizing gives P and yi​; this last step uses the ideal gas vapor assumption separately from the liquid model.
The law is named after François-Marie Raoult, who measured vapor pressures and freezing behavior of chemical solutions.[ref]
