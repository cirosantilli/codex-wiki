Chinese remainder theorem (Z/MZ≅∏j​Z/mj​Z)

 Home Mathematics
OurBigBook.com Words: 674 Articles: 6
The Chinese remainder theorem says that remainders modulo pairwise coprime positive integers can be specified independently. For m1​,…,mr​ pairwise coprime and M=m1​⋯mr​, every prescribed list a1​,…,ar​ determines one congruence class modulo M satisfying x≡aj​(modmj​) for all j. It determines a class, rather than one unrestricted integer: adding a multiple of M preserves every remainder.
For example, combine x≡2(mod3) and x≡3(mod5). Write x=2+3k from the first condition. The second becomes 3k≡1(mod5). Since three has modular inverse two modulo five, k≡2(mod5), giving x≡8(mod15). The integers 8, 23 and −7 all work. The fifteen possibilities modulo fifteen correspond to the fifteen pairs in a three-by-five grid.
The theorem also preserves arithmetic: reducing an addition or a multiplication modulo each mj​ gives the same tuple as performing that operation on the corresponding congruence classes. In the language of quotient rings, reduction is a ring isomorphism
Z/MZ[x]M​​⟶j=1∏r​Z/mj​Z,⟼([x]m1​​,…,[x]mr​​).​
(1)
One large residue is thus an exact encoding of several smaller residues. This is useful for splitting arithmetic into smaller calculations and then reconstructing the result; MIT's modular arithmetic notes develop both the reconstruction and the product representation. [ref] For a concrete multiplication, 7 corresponds to (1,2) and 8 to (2,3) modulo (3,5). Their componentwise product is (2,1), which reconstructs to 11(mod15), agreeing with 56≡11(mod15).
A constructive formula explains the independence. For mj​>1, put Mj​=M/mj​ and choose uj​ with Mj​uj​≡1(modmj​), using a modular multiplicative inverse. Then ej​=Mj​uj​ is one modulo mj​ and zero modulo every other mi​. Therefore
x≡∑j=1r​aj​ej​(modM)
(2)
has the desired remainders. A modulus equal to one imposes no restriction and can be omitted. Uniqueness follows because a difference divisible by every mj​ is divisible by their product when they are pairwise coprime. This short construction gives a reusable algorithm as well as an existence statement.
The coprime hypothesis explains why every cell in the left grid is attainable. With integer moduli six and eight, a pair must agree modulo their greatest common divisor, two. There are twenty-four compatible pairs, rather than forty-eight arbitrary pairs; each corresponds to a class modulo the least common multiple, twenty-four. The Chinese remainder theorem for noncoprime moduli states the resulting compatibility rule.
