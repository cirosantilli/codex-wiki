Floor and ceiling functions (⌊x⌋,⌈x⌉)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
The floor function and ceiling function turn a real number into directed integer bounds. They answer two different questions: which integer is the largest one that does not exceed x, and which is the smallest one that is not less than x?
⌊x⌋≤x<⌊x⌋+1,⌈x⌉−1<x≤⌈x⌉.
(2)
Both outputs are integers. These are exact mathematical operations; they need not choose the closest integer, and they do not refer to roundoff error in floating-point arithmetic.
For example,
x2.72−2.7−2​⌊x⌋22−3−2​⌈x⌉32−2−2​{x}0.700.30​​
(3)
The fractional part is {x}=x−⌊x⌋. The negative example matters: discarding the digits after the decimal point would give −2, which is truncation toward zero, rather than the floor function's value −3. At an integer, the floor function and ceiling function agree and the fractional part vanishes.
The usefulness is a bridge from continuous quantities to discrete decisions. An integer count in a closed interval uses both bounds. A ceiling count for fixed-size batches counts how many containers or pages are needed; a floor function counts how many complete groups fit. In Euclidean division, the same bounds specify the quotient, while the fractional part records the scaled remainder. A small amount of notation removes many cases that would otherwise have to be stated separately.
The reflection identity
⌈x⌉=−⌊−x⌋
(4)
follows by reflecting the ordered integers across zero: the largest one below −x becomes the smallest one above x. For every integer m, translation gives ⌊x+m⌋=⌊x⌋+m and ⌈x+m⌉=⌈x⌉+m. These formulas turn upper bounds into lower bounds and move examples between neighboring intervals.
For a historical example of the notation in use, Kenneth E. Iverson's 1962 conference paper A Programming Language lists the floor function, ceiling function and residue operation alongside the ordinary arithmetic operations. It states the two defining inequalities above, then extends operations component by component to vectors and matrices. This shows their role as tools for describing algorithms, rather than merely conventions for writing decimal numbers. [ref]
