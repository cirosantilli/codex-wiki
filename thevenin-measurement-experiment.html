Thévenin measurement experiment

 Home Electrical engineering Thévenin's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 403
An authored teaching experiment uses an adjustable steady-voltage supply, a digital multimeter, fixed resistors, and a 10kΩ potentiometer or resistance decade box. Its original circuit uses a 10V supply and 3.3, 6.8, and 4.7kΩ resistors; the student compares 8.2 and 2.2kΩ electrical loads. The readout is load voltage, recorded against prediction and deviation. The student then removes the electrical load to measure the open-circuit voltage, disconnects the supply and substitutes a wire to measure the Thévenin resistance with the multimeter's resistance mode, and restores the supply for an independent method: adjust the variable electrical load until the voltmeter reads half the unloaded voltage. Finally the student builds the equivalent and repeats both load measurements. Its tables are blank recording forms, not reported observations. [ref]
The half-voltage inference assumes negligible voltmeter loading and positive Rth​. A finite voltmeter input electrical resistance Rm​ actually gives an apparent unloaded reading
Vm​=Voc​Rth​+Rm​Rm​​.
(39)
When the same voltmeter remains connected across a variable electrical load RL​, the network sees Rp​=RL​Rm​/(RL​+Rm​). Setting its reading to Vm​/2, rather than to the true Voc​/2, gives
RL​=Rth​+Rm​Rth​Rm​​.
(40)
Thus the uncorrected half-reading method underestimates positive Rth​. In the original 12V example, a stipulated Rm​=10kΩ gives Vm​=120/17V≈7.059V and half-reading load RL​=20/17kΩ≈1.176kΩ, instead of 34​kΩ. These are model predictions for a deliberately loading meter, not the specification or observed readout of the source experiment's instrument.
Finite readings can also estimate the terminal line without a physical short. For two distinct load settings with corrected delivered electric currents I1​,I2​ and terminal voltages V1​,V2​,
Rth​=I2​−I1​V1​−V2​​,Voc​=V1​+I1​Rth​.
(41)
If the voltmeter remains across each electrical load, the delivered electric current is Ik​=Vk​(1/RL,k​+1/Rm​), including the meter branch. Omitting it biases the inferred slope.
Useful additional controls are to measure each resistor separately before assembly, verify terminal polarity, repeat an earlier load setting to detect supply drift, and compare a third setting with the inferred Thévenin terminal response. These are proposed controls, not reported outcomes of the source experiment. Closely spaced electric currents make the slope estimate sensitive to voltage resolution and measurement error; widely separated settings may trigger source current limiting or Joule heating that changes electrical resistance. Lead and contact electrical resistance, meter input uncertainty and rounding limit the inference. An ohmmeter injects its own test electric current; its reading on a powered network is not the suppressed-network ratio. Measurements with independent sources physically removed do not characterize an active dependent electrical source whose control or supply has also been disconnected.
