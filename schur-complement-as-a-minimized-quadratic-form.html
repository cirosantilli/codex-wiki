Schur complement as a minimized quadratic form (minx​q(x,y)=yTSy)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Schur complement
OurBigBook.com Words: 192
Let M=(ABT​BD​) be a real symmetric matrix with A a positive-definite matrix. Its quadratic form satisfies the completing the square identity
q(x,y)=xTAx+2xTBy+yTDy=(x+A−1By)TA(x+A−1By)+yT(D−BTA−1B)y.
(123)
For each fixed vector y, the unique minimizing vector is x=−A−1By, so the minimized value is yTSy. Also, M is a positive-definite matrix exactly when S is a positive-definite matrix, under the stated assumption on A.[ref]
An original scalar example is
q(x,y)=2x2+2xy+3y2=2(x+y/2)2+25​y2.
(124)
Here A=2, B=1, D=3, and the Schur complement is 5/2. Holding x=0 would give 3y2, but letting x relax to −y/2 reduces the quadratic form to (5/2)y2. At y=2 the respective values are 12 and 10. Eliminating a variable therefore changes the effective cost of the retained variable.
This interpretation supports quadratic optimization and the positive-definite symmetric matrix tests used in Cholesky decomposition. In a multivariate normal distribution, a covariance matrix also gives a conditional covariance through a Schur complement; the required probabilistic hypotheses are supplied by conditional multivariate normal distribution, rather than by the algebra alone.
