Planar rotation as a one-parameter subgroup (Rθ​=exp(θJ))

Differentiate the planar rotation matrix at angle zero:
J=dθdRθ​​​θ=0​=(01​−10​),J2=−I.
(9)
The even and odd powers in the matrix exponential therefore give
exp(θJ)=∑n=0∞​n!θnJn​=cosθI+sinθJ=Rθ​.
(10)
This exact formula gives the whole one-parameter subgroup. Taking X=J means unit angular velocity: at t=π/2, exp(tJ)=J sends (1,0)T to (0,1)T, a counterclockwise quarter-turn. At t=π, it equals −I; at t=2π, it returns to I. Choosing X=2J reaches the quarter-turn at t=π/4. Changing the generator changes the speed, while preserving the circular path.
For a moving vector v(t)=Rt​v(0), the ordinary differential equation v′(t)=Jv(t) says its velocity is perpendicular to its position. The beautiful compression is that one constant matrix specifies every angle of this endless family of rotations.
