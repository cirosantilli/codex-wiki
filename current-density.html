Current density (J)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 19
The current density J(r,t) describes the amount and direction of local electric charge transport per area per time. Its SI unit is A/m2. Through a small area element with unit normal n, the contribution to electric current is dI=J⋅ndA: the dot product selects transport through the surface. A current density tangent to the surface contributes zero. If current density is uniform and perpendicular to a wire's transverse slice, its magnitude j=∣J∣ satisfies
I=jA,j=AI​with the normal chosen along conventional current.
(20)
Here A is the wire cross-sectional area. For nonuniform current density, I/A is only the average normal component, and the surface integral is needed. [ref].
Consider two separate uniform-flow cases, each carrying electric current I=2A. A wire cross-sectional area of 1mm2=10−6m2 gives j=2×106A/m2; doubling the wire cross-sectional area to 2mm2 gives j=106A/m2. Equal electric current can therefore mean different local transport intensity. This distinction matters for Joule heating, whose local power per volume depends on current density and electric field.
For a toy electron transport model, stipulate mobile electron number density n=8×1028m−3 and drift velocity vd​=−10−4m/sx, with the positive unit normal x. Each electron has electric charge −e, where the elementary charge e=1.602176634×10−19C is exact in SI. These are defined model inputs, rather than measured parameters of a particular material. [ref] The current density and its magnitude are
J=n(−e)vd​=jx,j=ne∣vd​∣=1.2817413072×106A/m2.
(21)
For wire cross-sectional area A=1mm2 and constant transport over time 10s, this gives
I=jA=1.2817413072A,ΔQcross​=IΔt=12.817413072C.
(22)
The two minus signs make conventional current point opposite to the electron drift velocity. A small drift velocity can carry an appreciable electric current because many charge carriers contribute; it does not specify the propagation speed of an electrical disturbance.
