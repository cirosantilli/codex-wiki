Relativistic Sagnac delay on a circular ring (Δτ=4ΩA/(c21−Ω2R2/c2​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Sagnac effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 348
Consider an ideal circular vacuum path of laboratory radius R, a limit of closely spaced mirrors constraining light around the circle at laboratory speed c. The source and detector rotate uniformly together with signed angular velocity Ω, where ∣Ω∣R<c, in flat spacetime. Let L=2πR be the laboratory circumference and A=πR2 the positive area. The + pulse travels counterclockwise and the − pulse clockwise, independently of the sign of Ω. Both depart from the detector at t=0.
Unwrap the circle into a laboratory arc coordinate. The two first-return conditions are
ct+​=L+ΩRt+​,ct−​=L−ΩRt−​.
(47)
Consequently the exact laboratory arrival times and their difference are
t+​=c−ΩRL​,t−​=c+ΩRL​,Δt=c2−Ω2R24ΩA​.
(48)
The denominators c∓ΩR are closing rates in these laboratory equations; they are not local speed of light measurements by a rotating observer. The two arrival spacetime events lie on the same detector world line. Its constant speed is ∣Ω∣R, so proper time along that world line gives
Δτ=1−c2Ω2R2​​Δt=c21−Ω2R2/c2​4ΩA​.
(49)
Thus laboratory coordinate-time and rotating-detector proper time delays agree only to leading order at small ∣Ω∣R/c. The radius and circumference here are laboratory quantities, not lengths obtained by laying comoving rods around the rim. Finite mirror polygons, transparent media, and optical dispersion require their own path and propagation assumptions before using an exact formula.
An exact dimensionless check takes ΩR/c=1/5. Relative to L/c, the laboratory arrival times are 5/4 and 5/6, giving Δt/(L/c)=5/12. The detector instead records Δτ/(L/c)=6​/6≃0.408248. The leading Sagnac area law would give Δt/(L/c)≃2/5. These are model values chosen to make the relativistic distinction visible, not attainable parameters of the historical apparatus.
The rotating loop does not constitute a universal measurement of the one-way speed of light. It compares completed circuits at a single readout; assigning a velocity to each remote leg additionally requires a clock synchronization convention. Local Einstein synchronisation around a rotating rim does not close into a single globally synchronized clock network: the accumulated mismatch is another expression of the rotating-path asymmetry. Local comoving vacuum measurements still give c in both directions. Special relativity predicts a nonzero closed-loop delay because the receiver moves during propagation, while no single comoving inertial frame covers the whole circuit.
