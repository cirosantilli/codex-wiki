Multiplicity bias from a detector trigger (Pr(N=n∣trigger)∝A(n)Pr(N=n))

A trigger changes which particle populations are common in the recorded sample. Let N be the number of particles in an event, and stipulate an independent Bernoulli distribution for each particle's ability to produce an accepted telescope signal, with success probability ϵ. If any successful particle suffices, the event acceptance is
A(n)=1−(1−ϵ)n.
(24)
This is a deliberately simplified model. Its ϵ includes the joint geometrical acceptance and response of the whole telescope; it is not an assumed universal single-counter efficiency. Actual shower directions, spatial correlations and shared electronics can violate the independence assumption.
The conditional probability formula gives
Pr(N=n∣trigger)=∑m​A(m)Pr(N=m)A(n)Pr(N=n)​.
(25)
For chosen underlying fractions Pr(N=1)=0.90, Pr(N=5)=0.10 and ϵ=0.15, the two acceptances are 0.15 and 0.5563. Five-particle events consequently form about 29.18% of recorded events. A population that is only 10% of incoming events can occupy nearly 30% of the photographs. This is selection bias, not evidence that the source population changed.
