Conditioning and differential entropy

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Conditional entropy Conditioning reduces entropy
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
With appropriate probability density functions and finite differential entropies, h(X)−h(X∣Y)=I(X;Y)≥0, with equality iff independence. Differential entropy does not reverse the inequality; infinite or undefined quantities cannot justify subtraction.[ref]
However, differential entropy can be negative and depends on the choice of coordinates. For X with a uniform distribution on an interval of length a>0, h(X)=log2​a; in particular a=1/2 gives −1. Rescaling X by a constant c>0 adds log2​c to both h(X) and h(X∣Y), whenever they are finite, leaving the mutual information unchanged.[ref]
If a continuously distributed X is revealed exactly, its conditional distribution is a Dirac measure with no ordinary probability density function. The discrete identity H(X∣X)=0 has no finite differential entropy counterpart. Mutual information, defined through relative entropy, is infinite here.[ref]
