Volume magnetic susceptibility (χv​=M/Hint​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Magnetic susceptibility
OurBigBook.com Words: 132
Volume magnetic susceptibility is the dimensionless ratio of longitudinal magnetization to internal magnetic field intensity in a linear regime through the origin. Positive response gives paramagnetism. Always distinguish magnetization M=M/V from total magnetic moment M: dividing by volume changes the normalization. Mass-normalized and mole-normalized values likewise require the mass density or the volume per amount of substance before comparison with χv​.
In the existing Pauli paramagnetism formula, M denotes total magnetic moment, and g(EF​) is the total spin-summed density of states. Relabel that response as
χBtot​=(∂B∂M​)T​=μB2​g(EF​),χBvol​=VχBtot​​.
(63)
Here μB​ is the Bohr magneton. If its B is a prescribed vacuum/applied magnetic flux density B0​=μ0​Happ​, the dimensionless apparent slope is μ0​χBvol​. It agrees approximately with intrinsic χv​ only when the demagnetizing field correction and magnetic feedback are negligible. The existing Curie's law formula also uses a total-magnetic moment, B-derivative convention; its N counts moments rather than giving a number density.
