A relatively low yield stress increases the thickness needed to carry pressure, but a favorable toughness-to-yield-strength ratio can give a much larger leak-before-break upper bound. This tradeoff often makes a tough structural steel more attractive than choosing the highest strength alone.
For Arctic service, the ductile-to-brittle transition is crucial. A room-temperature toughness cannot be assumed to persist below the transition, and welding, constraint and loading rate affect the comparison. A suitably qualified low-temperature steel can be practical; the word mild alone does not establish that qualification.
