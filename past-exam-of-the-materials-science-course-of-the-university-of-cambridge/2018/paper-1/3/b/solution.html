Sample A is the Cu–Au alloy. At the indicated copper-rich composition, the supplied Cu–Au diagram permits one copper-rich solid-solution phase. The Cu–Ag diagram instead gives two equilibrium solid phases at room temperature. A single consistent set of FCC peaks therefore identifies A under the stated equilibrium assumptions; similar pure-metal lattice parameters alone would not distinguish gold from silver.
Use Vegard's law as an approximate atomic-fraction interpolation:
a≃(1−xAu​)aCu​+xAu​aAu​.
(18)
With the higher-angle estimate from part(a),
xAu​=0.4078−0.36150.363857−0.3615​≃0.0509.
(19)
This is about 5.1 atomic percent, not weight percent. Using rounded molar masses MAu​≃197 and MCu​≃63.55gmol−1,
wAu​=xAu​MAu​+(1−xAu​)MCu​xAu​MAu​​≃0.143,about 14wt% Au.​
(20)
Using the first peak alone instead gives roughly 12.6wt%. The justified conclusion is therefore about 13–14wt% gold, with uncertainty from angle rounding, calibration and the approximate linear lattice-composition relation.
