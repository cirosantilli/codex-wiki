At height z above the free lower end, a uniform vertical filament carries the weight of length z. With cross-sectional area A,
F(z)=ρAgz,σ(z)=ρgz,σmax​=ρgL.​
(40)
The maximum stress is at the top and is independent of diameter. The diameter cancels because both mass and load-bearing area scale with A. Neglecting self-heating gradients, evaporation and initial fabrication stress, this top point sets the first creep-strain limit.
For a clearly labelled external reference estimate, take ρ=1.93×104kgm−3. Then σmax​=1.89×104Pa=0.0189MPa; the area is 3.14×10−8m2 and the total suspended mass is about 6.06×10−5kg. The density is independently tabulated, not invented as a supplied 2016 value.[ref]
Use the original map's top temperature axis at 2500∘C and its right-hand tensile-stress axis, so no uncertain shear-stress conversion or additional shear modulus is required. The point lies in C, between the 10−9 and 10−8s−1 contours, nearer 10−8. A logarithmic interpolation of the scanned contours gives about 6×10−9s−1, with only graphical precision. Thus
tf​≃ε˙max​0.05​≃8×106s≃2.3×103h,
(41)
or of order three months in this steady-rate, reference-density map estimate. Reading only the bracketing contours gives the appropriately broad interval 5×106 to 5×107s; the graph does not support an exact service-life number.
For the exact prescribed input, the answer remains
tf​≃ε˙(2500∘C,ρgL)0.05​​.
(42)
This estimate uses the cited reference density and the grain-size assumptions of the supplied map. Thermal expansion lowers density, and an evolved area or tertiary material creep changes the rate; a steady initial-rate estimate is not a guaranteed lifetime. In particular, a different filament grain size could require a different map. Other failure mechanisms could shorten useful life.
