At equilibrium at room temperature the phases are pure Ag and pure Cu, with mass fractions 0.80 and 0.20. A representative solidification structure contains primary Ag-rich grains or dendrites surrounded by a finer two-phase eutectic aggregate. Subsequent equilibrium solute rejection modifies both constituents as they cool.
The 42.1% primary-α and 57.9% eutectic-constituent fractions calculated at the eutectic arrest describe how the liquid froze. They are not the final pure-Ag and pure-Cu phase fractions. For example, just below the arrest the total copper-rich phase fraction is (20−9)/(92−9)=11/83=0.1325; it rises to 0.20 as solubility is lost on further equilibrium cooling. The distinction separates a microstructural constituent from a thermodynamic phase.
Faster solidification usually refines the dendrite and eutectic spacings. If solid-state diffusion cannot keep up, composition gradients, supersaturated solids and delayed precipitation can persist. Their exact fractions then depend on the thermal history; faster cooling does not change the alloy's overall 20% copper inventory. Complete long-term equilibration would restore the same equilibrium phase compositions and mass fractions.
