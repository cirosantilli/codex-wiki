The cooling curve first falls in the entirely liquid field. On crossing the liquidus, primary Ag-rich α begins to grow. Latent heat release makes the descent shallower; the remaining liquid becomes richer in copper and approaches the eutectic composition.
At 779∘C, the remaining liquid solidifies as α+β, giving an isothermal arrest under the ideal equilibrium, heat-extraction model. After the liquid is exhausted, temperature falls through the two-solid field. Continued loss of solid solubility causes additional redistribution and precipitation; it does not require another invariant liquid-freezing plateau.
