The fluorite structure consists of a face-centered cubic lattice of calcium sites with all eight tetrahedral interstitial sites occupied by fluoride. In fractional coordinates of the conventional cubic unit cell, the calcium basis is (0,0,0),(0,21​,21​),(21​,0,21​),(21​,21​,0), and fluoride occupies all combinations (x,y,z) with x,y,z∈{41​,43​}. The projection therefore superposes two fluoride sites at each quarter-coordinate position. Labels in the original diagram give fractional depth along [001]; periodic boundary sites are displayed on both edges.
Counting shared sites gives 8/8+6/2=4 calcium sites and eight interior fluoride sites: four formula units per conventional cell.

b OurBigBook.com
Each calcium cation has eight fluoride neighbours at the vertices of a cube: cubic coordination. Each fluoride anion has four calcium neighbours in tetrahedral coordination. Both unlike-neighbour Euclidean distances are 3​a/4, with a the conventional cubic lattice parameter.
Apply the Goldschmidt packing principle at its limiting eight-coordinate geometry: the eight surrounding anions just touch one another and also touch the central cation. The anions form a cube of edge a/2, so 2ra​=a/2 and ra​+rc​=3​a/4. Eliminating a gives
rc​ra​​=3​−11​=23​+1​≃1.366.​
(1)
Equivalently, rc​/ra​=0.732. This is a limiting radius ratio rule, rather than a measured ionic-radius ratio. It does not assume that the calcium sublattice consists of mutually touching spheres: the calcium positions have face-centred cubic symmetry, but that symmetry alone does not specify which ions touch. A different construction with touching calcium spheres and fluoride just filling their tetrahedral interstitial sites would instead give ra​/rc​=3/2​−1=0.225; that is the opposite contact limit, not the eight-coordinate anion-contact ideal used here.

Using the contact model of (b), ra​=a/4 and rc​=(3​−1)a/4. Divide the volume of four cation spheres and eight anion spheres by a3 to obtain the packing fraction:
η=3a34π​(4rc3​+8ra3​)=12π​[(3​−1)3+2]=6π​(33​−4)≃0.6263.​
(2)
For one-radius cubic close-packed structure, face-diagonal contact gives 4r=2​a, hence ηccp​=4(4πr3/3)/a3=π/(32​)≃0.7405. The limiting fluorite value is lower than the one-radius close-packed value. The actual ionic radii need not equal the limiting ratio, and electronic charge distributions are not rigid sphere boundaries. In the same unlike-contact hard-sphere model, put q=ra​/rc​ and use a=4(ra​+rc​)/3​ to get

A larger barium cation expands the fluorite structure and can widen the constrictions through which fluoride ions move. A plausible explanation is therefore a smaller ionic migration barrier and greater fluoride mobility. In a simple dilute-carrier description, σion​=n∣q∣μ, or nq2D/(kB​T) when an uncorrelated Nernst-Einstein relation applies. fluoride vacancies and interstitial defects, including those generated as Frenkel pairs, provide possible carriers.
More open migration pathways can raise the conductivity, but ion size by itself is not a proof: defect formation free energies set n, migration free energies set mobility, and correlated hopping can alter the simple relation. A controlled comparison would use crystals with comparable impurity content, thermal history and electrode conditions, and measure conductivity over temperature to separate plausible activation behaviour from extrinsic defect effects.

Take the longitudinal scalar piezoelectric coefficient appropriate to the load and electrode direction. For a uniformly stressed slab of face area A and thickness t, the source charge is ΔQ=dΔF=dAΔσ. Its capacitance is C=ϵ0​κA/t, so the ideal initially uncharged, electrically isolated sensor gives
​ΔσΔV​​=ϵ0​κdt​.​
(4)
The sign depends on the poling direction and electrode convention. Equivalently, the incremental constitutive law is ΔD=dΔσ+ϵ0​κΔE; an ideal open circuit keeps free electrode charge, hence D, fixed and gives ΔE=−dΔσ/(ϵ0​κ). area cancels because both generated charge and capacitance scale with area. Thickness does not cancel: the material comparison is through d/κ only when geometry is fixed. Here κ must be the relative permittivity under the relevant mechanical and frequency conditions.

d OurBigBook.com
The sensor must survive the stress range and repeated loading without fracture, fatigue or excessive mechanical compliance. The tensor character of the piezoelectric coefficient makes crystal orientation important; a ceramic's poling quality and domain stability also matter. Compare linearity, hysteresis, reproducibility, aging and the usable frequency bandwidth, including resonance and dynamic response.
Electrical design is equally important: leakage resistance and readout input impedance determine low-frequency loss, and cable/input capacitance loads a voltage output. A charge amplifier may favour a different material ranking from a passive open-circuit voltage measurement. temperature changes can alter the coefficients, produce a pyroelectric signal in a polar material and depole a ferroelectric near its transition. Humidity, electrode bonding, electrical breakdown, manufacturability, cost and environmental constraints such as lead content also affect a practical choice. Calibration should separate applied stress from temperature drift and readout loading.

ferroelectric poling is essential. An initially unpoled ceramic has differently oriented crystal grains and domains whose charge responses largely cancel. An applied electric field favours suitably oriented domains and leaves a remanent macroscopic polarization after removal; it does not rotate all crystal grains into a single crystal.
In the linear coefficient model the added source polarization is
ΔPsource​=dΔσ=(80×10−12)10−410(9.81)​=7.85×10−5Cm−2.​
(7)
This is the stress-generated contribution at fixed electric field, equivalent to the short-circuit charge per face area. Under open-circuit conditions the induced field supplies an opposing dielectric contribution, so ΔD=0; the source quantity must not be confused with a change of total free electrode charge. For scale, room-temperature single-domain barium titanate has spontaneous polarization of order 0.26Cm−2: the calculated increment is about 3.0×10−4 of that value, a small response rather than wholesale reversal of the spontaneous polarization.[ref]
For the requested displacement estimate, assign the whole additional dipole moment of one cell to movement of its single Ti4+ ion relative to a fixed background. Then ΔP=(4e)Δx/(a2c), giving
Δx=4(1.6022×10−19)(7.848×10−5)(0.3994×10−9)2(0.4033×10−9)​=7.88×10−15m.​
(8)
This formal-charge estimate is only an equivalent displacement: real polarization changes involve oxygen and barium displacements, electronic polarization and Born effective charges, as well as possible domain-wall motion. The coefficient supplied for the ceramic cannot uniquely determine an actual microscopic titanium trajectory.

a OurBigBook.com
A heated cathode emits electrons, accelerated through a potential difference onto a metal target. Deceleration produces a bremsstrahlung continuum with λmin​=hc/(eVacc​); inner-shell ionization followed by electronic relaxation produces characteristic lines. The pictured instrument is consistent with the copper-target TEL-X-Ometer used for teaching diffraction. Its copper Kα​ radiation has wavelength about 0.154nm, comparable with atomic plane spacings. A nickel filter preferentially suppresses copper Kβ​, while slits collimate the beam; filtering does not remove every continuum photon.
A Geiger-Müller counter converts photon-induced gas ionization into countable electrical pulses. A scintillator with photomultiplier is another detector option. A detector-arm scan records count rate against scattering angle; coupled crystal rotation preserves the usual reflection geometry. These instrument features are documented by its manufacturer.[ref]
For the numerical calculations below use λ=0.154nm, explicitly identifying the characteristic line assumed. The photograph alone does not give a measured wavelength or angular travel limit; conclusions depending on these parameters are stated conditionally. A different selected line changes the angles but not a crystallographic extinction.

For a cubic crystal, dhkl​=a/h2+k2+l2​ and first-order bragg diffraction requires 2dhkl​sinθ=λ. The face-centred lattice sum is
Lhkl​=1+eiπ(k+l)+eiπ(h+l)+eiπ(h+k).
(9)
It is four if h,k,l are all even or all odd, and zero for mixed parity. With the NaCl basis displaced by (21​,0,0), the allowed amplitudes are Fhkl​=4[fCl​+(−1)h+k+lfNa​], up to the arbitrary choice of origin/species. Both allowed parity classes survive because the two ionic scattering factors are not identical. Intensities depend on ∣F∣2, geometry, absorption and scattering vector.
There are three separate tests: nonzero structure factor, accessible Bragg angle, and accessible plane orientation. In a symmetric reflection scan the scattering vector is normal to the specimen face, selecting the surface-parallel family and its allowed orders. Other families need a different mounting, independent orientation control or transmission/another facet. The original diagrams distinguish these geometric requirements; angles in the reciprocal-space panel are between plane normals, not Bragg angles.

c OurBigBook.com
Randomly oriented crystallites supply planes with their normals in each required scattering-vector direction. Of the five families considered, (111), (200) and (220) can therefore give accessible copper Kα​ powder peaks; (300) remains absent and (640) remains beyond a roughly 130∘ detector limit. Other allowed families also appear if their Bragg angles lie inside the instrument range; orientation averaging does not restrict the pattern to these five examples.
Only a small fraction of randomly oriented crystal grains satisfy the Bragg condition for a given detector setting, and each family scatters around a cone rather than concentrating all its intensity into one single-crystal spot. A narrow detector aperture therefore receives a relatively weak signal from a small powder specimen compared with a well-oriented large single crystal. Adequate crystal grain statistics, longer counting, suitable powder packing and detector acceptance matter. The odd-parity peaks additionally involve the difference of ionic scattering factors. No absolute single-crystal/powder intensity ratio follows without illuminated volumes, absorption and instrumental geometry.

b OurBigBook.com
At fixed transformed volume, an equilibrium solid-state precipitate seeks a low total free energy, including the orientation-dependent interfacial energy and elastic strain energy associated with misfit and applied stress. Low-energy interface orientations occupy large faces. The Wulff construction captures surface-energy anisotropy when strain and other contributions can be neglected; a solid-state precipitate generally also pays coherency strain energy, and its optimum shape can change with size.
During formation, growth kinetics and diffusion may prevent the equilibrium shape being reached. Coherent, semicoherent and incoherent interfaces have different bonding and defect structures, so their energies and attachment rates differ. The disc shape is consistent with much cheaper broad faces than edges, but morphology alone does not uniquely measure the energies or prove equilibrium.

c OurBigBook.com
Choose the orientation relation (0001)hcp​∥(111)ccp​ with aligned close-packed rows, for example [112ˉ0]hcp​∥[11ˉ0]ccp​. The two phases then share matching triangular layers at the broad faces. The stacking changes from ABCABC… to ABAB… across the phase region, so broad coherent crystal interfaces can be made by a local change of stacking, with small mismatch if the in-plane atomic spacings are similar.
At the cylindrical edge, different layer sequences terminate against one another. Registry cannot generally be maintained everywhere, so ledges, stacking defects and possibly misfit dislocations are needed; this interface is less coherent and often more costly. Thus a low-energy basal/(111) face and a higher-energy edge favour a thin plate. An ideal same-element stacking change need not imply zero edge energy or exactly zero face energy.

A heat engine must reject some absorbed heat to a colder reservoir. For heat addition and rejection at absolute temperatures Th​ and Tc​, the Carnot bound is
η≤1−Th​Tc​​.
(25)
At fixed Tc​, increasing Th​ increases this upper limit and can increase useful specific work in an appropriately designed gas-turbine cycle. Thus a higher hot-side temperature permits more efficient conversion of heat to work, rather than merely heating the exhaust. Real combustion occurs over a temperature range, and actual efficiency also depends on pressure ratio, losses and cycle design: peak temperature alone does not determine it. The materials incentive is to sustain higher turbine inlet temperatures while maintaining strength, oxidation resistance and acceptable creep life.

At turbine operating temperatures, grain boundaries provide paths for rapid diffusion, grain-boundary sliding and cavity formation, encouraging creep rupture. A single-crystal turbine blade removes these boundaries, especially boundaries unfavourably oriented across the tensile load. It retains dislocations and can still creep; eliminating boundaries is useful in this high-temperature regime even though fine crystal grains can strengthen other materials at lower temperatures.
Use directional solidification of the cast alloy through a controlled thermal gradient. A suitably oriented seed or a narrow grain selector lets one crystal survive and grow into the blade mould while suppressing competing nucleation. A spiral selector is a common way to exclude competing crystal grains. The blade is generally oriented near a ⟨001⟩ axis for the intended anisotropic properties. crystal grain selection is experimentally verified by orientation mapping, rather than assuming a directionally elongated casting is automatically a single crystal.[ref]

c OurBigBook.com
The nickel-rich γ matrix is a disordered substitutional solid solution on a face-centered cubic lattice. The ordered γ′ phase has the L12 structure, approximately Ni3​Al: aluminium occupies cube corners and nickel occupies face centres. Its occupied-site geometry is often called ordered FCC, but its chemically labelled Bravais lattice is primitive cubic, with a four-atom basis. A face-centre translation exchanges chemical sublattices and is not a symmetry of the ordered crystal.
Each Al atom has twelve nearest-neighbour Ni atoms at Euclidean distance a/2​. Each Ni atom has twelve nearest neighbours too, but these are four Al and eight Ni. The chemical distinction matters for slip. In disordered γ, the shortest perfect Burgers vector is
bγ​=2a​⟨110⟩,∣bγ​∣=a/2​.​
(26)
For the ordered primitive cubic translation lattice, the shortest order-preserving perfect vector is
bγ′,min​=a⟨100⟩,∣b∣=a.​
(27)
This does not imply that ⟨100⟩ slip must dominate. A vector must lie in a favourable glide plane and have an accessible core structure and activation stress. Matrix dislocations commonly glide on {111}⟨110⟩ systems. A single a⟨110⟩/2 passage through γ′ leaves an antiphase boundary; a second matching passage restores order, giving total vector a⟨110⟩. Such a superdislocation has a longer total vector than a⟨100⟩, yet its constituent dislocations can move on the favourable close-packed planes and interact with the matrix. Paired motion balances line and antiphase-boundary energies, so the shortest abstract lattice translation alone does not select the active slip system.[ref]

The main strengthening architecture is a high volume fraction of fine, thermally stable ordered gamma prime phase precipitates in the ductile nickel-rich matrix. Their nearly matching crystal parameters allow coherent crystal interfaces, but coherence does not make them transparent to a moving dislocation: cutting disrupts chemical order and costs antiphase-boundary energy. Pairing of cutting dislocations and their interactions with narrow matrix channels raise the stress needed for deformation. For particles that are bypassed, bowing and high-temperature climb are also impeded by the precipitate spacing and interface dislocation structures.
coherency strain and differences in elastic response provide additional obstacles. Refractory substitutional solutes contribute solid-solution strengthening and can slow diffusion-controlled deformation. Single-crystal manufacture suppresses grain-boundary creep and grain-boundary cavitation. Together these mechanisms permit useful strength at temperatures where an unstrengthened nickel matrix would deform much faster. Protective oxide scales and coatings preserve load-bearing material against environmental attack; they complement, rather than replace, the internal strengthening mechanisms.
A concrete microscopic observation is the paired dislocation loops around fine precipitates in the Cambridge C263 creep micrograph: the observed defect structure supports the importance of precipitate–dislocation interactions, though one image does not by itself separate every strengthening contribution.[ref] In-situ diffraction during stress-jump tests of a rafted AM1 single crystal also resolves evolving load partition and deformation in matrix channels, demonstrating why channel width and interface interactions matter.[ref] Long exposure can coarsen or change the precipitate morphology and alter channel widths, so high strength is conditional on temperature, time and microstructural stability.
