i OurBigBook.com
A periodic crystal cannot have a fivefold crystallographic rotation axis. The crystallographic restriction theorem permits rotation orders 1,2,3,4,6 for a periodic Bravais lattice. A 72∘ rotation cannot preserve such a lattice. This restriction concerns exact symmetry of the infinite crystal structure; an isolated finite cluster with fivefold symmetry is a different object.

ii OurBigBook.com
A motif can have fivefold symmetry. For example, repeat an identically oriented regular pentagonal molecular motif at every point of a square translation lattice. Each isolated motif has fivefold rotational symmetry, while the crystal structure as a whole does not: rotating about one motif fails to map the other motif centres onto lattice points. The symmetry of an atomic basis of a crystal need not be a symmetry of its Bravais lattice.

Each Ti has coordination number six, with oxygen neighbours forming a distorted octahedron. Each oxygen has coordination number three, with titanium neighbours forming a planar triangle. For oxygen at (u,u,0), the nearest Ti positions can be taken as (0,0,0) and (1/2,1/2,±1/2); all three lie in the plane x=y. The triangle is not generally equilateral. Equal Ti–O bond lengths in the following calculation do not make every angle ideal.

The fluorite structure has Ce on the corner and face-centre sites of a face-centered cubic lattice. Oxygen occupies all eight positions (x,y,z) with each coordinate independently 1/4 or 3/4. In projection, there are four oxygen positions (1/4,1/4),(1/4,3/4),(3/4,1/4),(3/4,3/4), each representing two heights. Accounting for shared boundary sites gives four Ce and eight O per conventional unit cell.

Each missing oxide ion leaves two units of excess positive effective charge, compensated by reducing two Ce4+ ions to Ce3+. c OurBigBook.com
Choose an objective aperture around a precipitate superlattice reflection, such as 100, which is absent from the disordered aluminium matrix. The selected beam then makes precipitates bright in dark-field transmission electron microscopy, while the matrix gives little intensity in that beam. Tilt the grain to place the chosen reflection close to its Bragg diffraction condition and preferably approach a two-beam geometry. This reduces excitation error and can increase precipitate brightness; the exact maximum also depends on foil thickness, strain and dynamical scattering. Selecting a shared fundamental reflection would give much poorer chemical-order selectivity.

i OurBigBook.com
At 700∘C the undercooling below the eutectoid temperature is small, so the free-energy driving force for forming pearlite is weak and nucleation is difficult. At 300∘C the driving force is larger, but carbon diffusion is slow, limiting the coupled growth of ferrite and cementite. Their competition produces the nose of the isothermal transformation diagram near 550∘C and 1s. A roughly linear quench from 740–800∘C must pass left of this nose, requiring a rate of order 2×102–3×102Ks−1, then continue below the martensite-start region near 240∘C. The tangent to the full start curve, rather than just the nose, refines this estimate; a true continuous-cooling diagram would be preferable. Martensite forms by a coordinated lattice-shape change without long-range carbon partitioning, so its formation is a displacive transformation. The tetragonal-to-monoclinic transformation of zirconia is a nonmetallic example.

b OurBigBook.com
Solution
Words: 86
ii OurBigBook.com
As-quenched martensite generally has greater hardness and strength, but lower ductility and toughness, than ordinary pearlitic steel. Its supersaturated carbon and distorted lattice impede dislocation glide; quench stresses also promote cracking. Pearlite combines hard cementite lamellae with more deformable ferrite, with finer spacings generally giving greater strength. Austenitize, quench to martensite, then temper to obtain a useful compromise between indentation resistance and resistance to fast fracture. Tempering below the austenitizing range relieves stresses and develops a fine carbide-containing microstructure. It trades some maximum hardness for improved toughness; the temperature and time must suit the required balance.

c OurBigBook.com
The lamellae are ferrite and cementite, together forming pearlite. The displayed quantity is surface height in an atomic force microscopy image. Nital preferentially attacks the ferrite, leaving it lower and therefore darker; the narrow raised cementite lamellae appear bright. This is not the same contrast mechanism as brightness in an optical micrograph. Faster cooling that still permits pearlite formation causes transformation at greater undercooling and generally gives finer lamellar spacing. Cooling sufficiently rapidly to bypass diffusional transformation instead produces other products, potentially martensite; it does not merely keep making pearlite indefinitely finer.

a OurBigBook.com
The exponential in the Peierls stress makes a large core-width-to-Burgers vector ratio w/b favourable for easy dislocation glide. Densely packed slip planes often have wider cores because the shear distortion can spread over more atomic rows, reducing the lattice-periodic barrier. Close-packed directions provide short lattice translations and hence small allowed Burgers vectors; their line energies, approximately proportional to Gb2, are also relatively low. These effects favour dense planes and directions, but the relation between packing and core width is a physical argument rather than a consequence of substituting arbitrary independent w,b into the formula. Body-centred cubic metals have densely packed directions without a truly close-packed plane, and their screw-core behaviour can require additional qualifications.

At small load the deformation is elastic: ε∥​=σ/E, and, using isotropic elasticity with E=2G(1+ν), ν=0.30 gives lateral contraction ε⊥​=−0.30ε∥​. At maximum load,
σmax​=π(2mm)212.6kN​=1.003GPa​.
(12)
For the specified slip system, the tensile direction is proportional to (−2,−3,5), the slip direction to (1,−1,1) and the plane normal to (1,0,−1). Hence the Schmid factor is
m=38​3​∣6∣​38​2​∣−7∣​=0.4512,∣τmax​∣=0.452GPa​.

Schmid's law predicts glide when this reaches the critical resolved shear stress. Before that, unloading reverses the strain; after activation, glide produces permanent strain and may generate work hardening and additional slip. The supplied data do not fix the yield load. Neither the adjective “ultra-strong” nor E/G supplies a critical shear stress or an absolute modulus. Other slip systems must also be checked before claiming which activates first. In particular, the specimen remains elastic through the maximum load if every relevant system stays below its activation threshold.

Burgers vector conservation gives two candidate reactions, depending on the relative signs of the approaching segments:
b1​+b2​=a[001],b1​−b2​=a[11ˉ0].
(14)
Initially b12​+b22​=3a2/2. The sum product has b2=a2, while the difference product has b2=2a2. Thus the a[001] product is favoured by Frank's rule; the a[11ˉ0] product is disfavoured. The result is not annihilation, because the two supplied vectors are not opposite. The same ordering survives a simple character correction: with ν=0.30, line energy proportional to Gb2[cos2χ+sin2χ/(1−ν)] gives the two parent segments a combined coefficient 1.786a2, whereas any character of the sum product has coefficient between a2 and 1.429a2 and any character of the difference product at least 2a2, comparing equal lengths and common logarithmic factors. A favourable dislocation junction can obstruct further glide and harden the crystal. Its actual mobility requires the junction line and allowed glide plane: the second screw can occupy several {110} planes containing [1ˉ11], so geometry is not unique. For instance, its (101) plane intersects (101ˉ) along [010]; an a[001] junction on that line would glide geometrically on (100), not either original plane. Dislocation cross-slip, junction unzipping or bypass can compete under load. An energy inequality alone is not a universal proof of a permanently sessile product.

The fixed support supplies the end shear force and the largest bending moment, FL. In elementary beam bending, the greatest longitudinal tensile and compressive stresses lie on opposite outer faces of the section at the fixed end, with zero longitudinal bending stress on the neutral axis. For a downward tip load on a horizontal cantilever, the upper face is in tension and the lower face in compression. Transverse shear stress has a different distribution, largest near the neutral axis for a rectangular section; “highest force” should therefore be interpreted with the stress component specified.

On a logarithmic strength–density materials selection chart, constant MA​ gives σf​=MA​ρ, a guideline of slope one; constant MB​ gives σf​=MB2​ρ2, of slope two. Moving each guideline upwards selects better candidates. A suitably qualified longitudinal carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer is a promising candidate for both designs. d OurBigBook.com
Check stiffness and tip deflection using Young's modulus, failure from existing cracks using fracture toughness or the supplied critical energy-release rate, local crushing at supports, shear failure, buckling, fatigue and joint design. In a simple linear-elastic crack model, Kc2​≃E′Gc​, so toughness cannot be inferred from strength alone. Wood also has grain-direction dependence, moisture sensitivity, natural defects, property scatter and time-dependent creep; the design must control grain alignment and compression-side stability. Metals raise issues of corrosion, mass, joining, machining, residual stress and heat-treatment state. Cost, availability, reproducibility, service temperature and end-of-life treatment can decide between otherwise feasible designs. A high merit index is a screening result, not a complete safety specification.

A diamond anvil cell concentrates force onto tiny opposing culets, reaching very high pressure with small samples and useful optical access through diamond; its small volume, pressure gradients, alignment demands and difficulties with some heating or sample environments can limit experiments. A multi-anvil press supports a much larger sample and is useful for high-temperature synthesis and bulk measurements, but usually reaches lower ultimate pressures and has more restricted optical access. Hydrostaticity, attainable temperature and calibration depend on the actual apparatus and pressure medium; neither design guarantees uniform pressure.

c OurBigBook.com
Within each cubic phase, increasing pressure decreases a, so dhkl​=a/h2+k2+l2​ decreases and Bragg's law moves a given reflection to higher 2θ. Near 2.55GPa, peaks of the rock salt structure disappear as those of the CsCl structure appear, potentially with a coexistence interval. The former has an FCC translation selection rule, with h,k,l all even or all odd; mixed-parity reflections vanish kinematically. The latter has primitive-cubic translations and basis factor fK​+(−1)h+k+lfCl​, so it has no FCC extinction rule. Odd-sum reflections may nevertheless be weak because the two ionic scattering factors are similar. Peak spacings, relative intensities and the cell volume per formula unit change discontinuously. A simple uniform shift of the entire original pattern cannot describe the structural transition.

The coordination number rises from six in the rock salt structure to eight in the CsCl structure. In the elementary hard-sphere ionic bonding model, accommodating eight opposite neighbours favours a larger cation/anion radius ratio: the geometrical limiting ratios are 2​−1 for sixfold and 3​−1 for eightfold coordination. The high-pressure change is therefore consistent with rK​/rCl​ increasing under compression. Since
dPd​lnrCl​rK​​=κr,Cl​−κr,K​,κr​=−dPdlnr​,
(24)
the chloride ion is inferred to be more compressible than the potassium ion in this radius-ratio interpretation. This is a qualitative structural argument; real ionic radii depend on coordination and electronic structure, and the transition does not uniquely determine separate microscopic compressibilities.
