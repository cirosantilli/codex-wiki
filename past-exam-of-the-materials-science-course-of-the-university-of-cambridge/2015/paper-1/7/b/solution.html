Assume the two thermodynamic phases have similar atomic volumes, so use fV​≃0.15. Model the stated average pitch L=1 μm by one sphere per representative cubic volume L3. If the sphere diameter is d,
fV​=L3(π/6)d3​,d=L(π6fV​​)1/3≃0.66 μm,r≃0.33 μm.​
(45)
Using the more literal lever-rule reading0.145 gives about0.65micrometres instead, within graphical uncertainty. The central estimate is therefore a diameter of roughly two-thirds of a micrometre.
A random distribution does not make a nearest-neighbor mean distance exactly equal to the cube root of inverse number density. The representative cubic spacing is an explicit geometric closure, not evidence that the precipitates actually form a cubic lattice. Unequal molar volumes or a different spacing statistic would change the inferred size.
