A Shockley partial dislocation in an FCC slip plane has Burgers vector of the family a⟨112⟩/6. Its vector must lie in the specified plane, and the two vectors must sum to the perfect dislocation vector.
For the first plane normal n1​=[1ˉ11], one allowed dissociation is
2a​[101]=6a​[211]+6a​[11ˉ2].​
(24)
The vectors add to a[303]/6, and n1​⋅[211]=−2+1+1=0, n1​⋅[11ˉ2]=−1−1+2=0.
For n2​=[111],
2a​[1ˉ10]=6a​[2ˉ11]+6a​[1ˉ21ˉ].​
(25)
Their sum is a[3ˉ30]/6, and both have zero dot product with [111]. Interchanging leading and trailing labels does not change these vector identities.
Each perfect vector has squared magnitude a2/2, whereas the sum of the two partial squared magnitudes is a2/3. The line-energy estimate proportional to Gb2 therefore favors separation, opposed by the stacking-fault energy of the ribbon between the partials.
