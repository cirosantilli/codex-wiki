Its dot product with the resultant Burgers vector is zero, so the junction is edge in character. A plane containing both line and Burgers vector has normal [100], rather than an FCC close-packed {111} glide normal. The resulting stair-rod dislocation is therefore sessile for ordinary glide and can form a Lomer-Cottrell lock. It obstructs further dislocation motion and promotes work hardening.
This energetic comparison assumes comparable line lengths and elastic prefactors. Geometry, line character and stacking-fault geometry affect a complete junction-energy calculation, but the small resultant identifies the favorable candidate requested by the vector model.
