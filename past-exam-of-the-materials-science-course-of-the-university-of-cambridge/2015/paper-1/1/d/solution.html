For indexing a diamond-cubic powder pattern, dhkl​=a/h2+k2+l2​ and Bragg's law gives sin2θ=λ2(h2+k2+l2)/(4a2). Using half of each printed scattering angle, the ratios to the first peak are approximately
1, 2.66658, 3.66665, 5.33319, 6.33322.
(9)
Multiplying by3 gives the allowed sequence 3,8,11,16,19. Thus
111,220,311,400,331.​
(10)
The numerical calculation, expressed without assuming a historical Data Book wavelength, is
2θ (deg)28.52447.44356.29469.35176.629​hkl111220311400331​h2+k2+l238111619​a/λ3.515343.515403.515353.515383.51537​​
(11)
Therefore a≃3.51537λ. As a separately identified reference convention, choosing Cu Kα1​ with λ≃0.15406 nm gives
a≃0.54158 nm.​
(12)
That wavelength follows from the copper KL3​ transition near 8047.8227 eV in the NIST transition database, using λ=hc/E.[ref]
The paper says Cu Kα but does not give the line-selection convention or an explicit wavelength. A rounded unresolved-doublet wavelength, for example 0.15418 nm, would instead give about 0.54200 nm. These are conditional wavelength evaluations, not claims about the unavailable historical Data Book. The angles are internally consistent with one cubic parameter; they should not be replaced by literature peak positions simply to obtain a familiar silicon value.
