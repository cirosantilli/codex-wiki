The materials interpretation is a cooling-rate gradient in a near-eutectoid steel. Before transformation, the parent thermodynamic phase is austenite. A small section can cool rapidly enough to suppress diffusional decomposition and form martensite, a supersaturated, highly strained transformation product. A much thicker interior cools more slowly and can transform diffusively to pearlite: alternating ferrite and cementite lamellae grouped into colonies. Bainite or mixed products are also possible for intermediate histories.
The likely contrast is therefore a martensite-rich rapidly cooled region and a more pearlitic slowly cooled interior. Brittle fracture is promoted by the highly strained martensitic state, residual stresses and local stress concentration. A larger intact interior need not share the same microstructure or local loading. The observations are consistent with this contrast, not a measurement proving pure thermodynamic phases everywhere.
