Solubility product (Ksp​)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Precipitation (chemistry)
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 8
The solubility product is the equilibrium constant for dissolution of a specified solid into its dissolved ions. For a pure bulk AB solid at the chosen temperature, pressure and standard states,
AB(s)⇌A+(aq)+B−(aq),Ksp​=aA​aB​.
(5)
The pure bulk solid's thermodynamic activity is one in this convention. Coexisting solid and chemical solution at chemical equilibrium give this equality. If all available solid dissolves, the final chemical solution may instead be undersaturated; do not impose equality merely because a salt was initially added.[ref]
Using the thermodynamic activity definition ai​=γi​ci​/c∘, with activity coefficients γi​ and reference molar concentration c∘, gives the independent conversion
Ksp​=γA​γB​(c∘)2cA​cB​​,Ksp,c​=γA​γB​Ksp​(c∘)2​.
(6)
Thus Ksp​ is dimensionless, while the conditional concentration form Ksp,c​ has units M2 for this one-to-one model. Treating Ksp,c​ as constant requires a justified fixed or approximately fixed activity coefficient product. Changing ionic strength can change that product even at fixed temperature; changing temperature can change the equilibrium constant itself. Specify the solid thermodynamic phase too: different crystal polymorphs need not have the same solubility product.
