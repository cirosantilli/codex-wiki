Finite-bin photon correlations (g​j(2)​=Cj​/(r1​r2​Tj​Δτ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum optics Photon counting
OurBigBook.com Words: 223
For stationary continuous detection, let Cj​ count all cross-channel pairs with t2​−t1​ in a bin of full width Δτ, and let r1​,r2​ be observed single-count rates. Let Tj​ be their joint live observation time at that delay, approximately the common duration Tobs​ when delays are short. Independent streams have expected pair count r1​r2​Tj​Δτ, giving the displayed estimator. It estimates a bin average of second-order optical coherence, further blurred by detector timing response. For a finite uninterrupted run the exact overlap weighting is Tobs​−∣τ∣ inside the delay integral, rather than a constant at long delays.
For an original numerical example, choose r1​=2000s−1, r2​=3000s−1, Tj​=100s, and Δτ=10ns. The independent reference is six pairs per bin. Chosen counts Cj​=3,6,12 give estimates 0.5,1,2; they are teaching inputs with large counting fluctuations, not historical measurements. For pulsed sources, compare properly normalized same-pulse and separate-pulse coincidences instead of assuming this continuous-source baseline.
Detector dead time suppresses nearby same-detector counts and can manufacture an apparent dip in a single-detector record. Two independent detector channels avoid that particular same-channel exclusion, but shared electronics can still reject pairs. Timing spread and wide bins wash out narrow features. A device recording only the first stop after each start measures a waiting-time distribution; interpreting it as all-pair correlations requires a sufficiently low stop probability over the relevant delay range.
