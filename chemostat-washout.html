Chemostat washout (Dc​=μ(sin​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Chemostat
OurBigBook.com Words: 128
Chemostat washout means the culture's biomass concentration tends to zero because growth cannot sustain replacement of the cells leaving the vessel. In the ideal Monod equation chemostat with D>0, its equilibrium is (s,x)=(sin​,0). Its Jacobian matrix has eigenvalues −D and μ(sin​)−D: a small inoculum grows when D<μ(sin​), whereas chemostat washout is locally asymptotically stable when D>μ(sin​). For nonnegative initial concentrations, chemostat washout occurs for D≥μ(sin​); the boundary case also loses biomass asymptotically.
At D=Dc​ the positive chemostat steady state meets the chemostat washout equilibrium, and one eigenvalue is zero, so linearization alone does not decide the boundary case. On the invariant nutrient-equivalent balance s+x/Y=sin​, small positive x obeys x˙=−[μ′(sin​)/Y]x2+O(x3): nutrient consumption lowers the specific growth rate below Dc​, leading to slow decay rather than a positive biomass concentration. The continued mathematical branches exchange stability in a transcritical bifurcation; the positive-biomass branch becomes physically inadmissible beyond Dc​.
