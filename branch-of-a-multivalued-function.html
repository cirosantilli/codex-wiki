Branch of a multivalued function

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Branch point
OurBigBook.com Words: 240
A branch of a multivalued analytic expression is a single-valued holomorphic function on a specified domain that consistently selects one of the expression's possible values. A branch cut is a convenient way to choose such a domain, rather than an intrinsic locus of singularities. On D=C∖(−∞,0], the principal logarithm supplies two square-root branches
B+​(z)=exp(21​Logz),B−​(z)=−B+​(z),B±​(z)2=z.
(102)
For z=i, these give B+​(i)=eiπ/4=(1+i)/2​ and B−​(i)=−(1+i)/2​. Their derivatives follow by differentiating B±2​=z: B±′​=1/(2B±​), so B+′​(i)=(1−i)/(22​). There are exactly two branches on D: for any other holomorphic square root B, the ratio B/B+​ is continuous, takes values in {1,−1}, and hence is constant on the connected domain.
The cut can move. Choosing 0<θ<2π defines a holomorphic logarithm L+​(z)=log∣z∣+iθ on D+​=C∖[0,∞). At −1 this gives L+​(−1)=iπ and exp(L+​(−1)/2)=i, both regular values despite −1 lying on the previous cut. In the complex upper half-plane, L+​ agrees with Log; in the lower half-plane it equals Log+2πi, so the corresponding square roots have opposite signs there. The overlap D∩D+​ has two components, exactly why the identity theorem cannot force agreement between them. [ref]
For example, the limits of B+​ at −r, r>0, are ir​ from above and −ir​ from below. This jump records the chosen branch cut; each point −r=0 still has a neighbourhood supporting a holomorphic square root. Moving the cut changes the bookkeeping, while the branch point at 0 and its monodromy persist. Specifying a branch of a multivalued function is therefore essential before differentiating or performing contour integration with holomorphic square roots, complex logarithms or complex exponentiation.
