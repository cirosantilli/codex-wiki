Lamb shift quenching readout (Q=(Ioff​−Ion​)/(Ioff​−Ibg​))

 Home Physics Atomic physics Lamb shift Lamb-Retherford experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 175
For a simple original detector model, let ΦS​ be the incident rate of surviving 2S atoms, e the positive elementary charge, η the mean number of collected electrons per incident 2S atom, and Ibg​ a stable background current. Take current magnitudes as positive. If microwaves remove a fraction p before the target,
Ioff​=Ibg​+eηΦS​,Ion​=Ibg​+eηΦS​(1−p),Q=p.
(1)
The equality needs the same beam flux, background and collection efficiency in both measurements. It is an ideal interpretation of a normalized current loss, not a claim that an actual galvanometer directly counts individual transitions. Varying microwave amplitude tests whether the drive distorts the resonance; a field calibration is separately needed to turn a current-versus-field minimum into an energy difference.
The metastable atomic level matters because an electric-dipole selection rule suppresses the direct 2S→1S electric-dipole interaction, whereas the opposite-parity 2S↔2P transition can be driven. A long lifetime can store the prepared population until interrogation. After transfer to 2P, a short lifetime supplies the loss mechanism and a broad natural linewidth. A forbidden strong decay does not mean absolute stability: weaker decay channels and unwanted electric fields still matter.
