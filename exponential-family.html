Curved exponential family

Words: 124 Articles: 1
Exponential family OurBigBook.com
A (p,q) curved exponential family, with q<p, restricts a p-parameter canonical family to a smooth rank-q parameter surface, usually a nonaffine surface. For example, N(μ,μ2) with μ>0 has sufficient statistics (Y,Y2) and natural parameters (1/μ,−1/(2μ2)), constrained by η2​=−η12​/2. Canonical concavity in the ambient parameters need not imply concavity along the curved parameter surface.

Conditional scale density in a curved Gaussian family (f(v∣a;μ)∝μ−nvn−1e−nq2v2/(2μ2)+nqv/(aμ))

Words: 67
Curved exponential family OurBigBook.com
For independent N(μ,μ2) observations with μ>0, the sufficient statistics are their sum and squared norm. Condition on a=n​∥y∥/∑i​yi​>0 and write v=μ​=∥y∥/(qn​), where q=(1+4a2​+1)/(2a). Polar coordinates supply radial measure ∥y∥n−1d∥y∥, while the conditional angular factor does not depend on μ. This proves the displayed shape. Its normalization depends on a,n but not μ after the substitution v=μz, and agrees with the normalized P-star approximation.

Minimal exponential family

Words: 47
Exponential family OurBigBook.com
An order-p exponential family is minimal if no nonzero linear combination of its p canonical sufficient statistics is almost surely constant under the base measure. Equivalently, its sufficient-statistic support lies in no proper affine hyperplane. Its cumulant function then has positive-definite Hessian matrix on the interior natural domain, because that Hessian is a covariance matrix.

Natural exponential family (fθ​(y)=h(y)eθTy−κ(θ))

Words: 267 Articles: 4
Exponential family OurBigBook.com
A natural exponential family uses its observation vector itself as the canonical sufficient statistic. Its cumulant function is κ(θ)=log∫h(y)eθTydν(y) and its natural parameter space consists of parameters for which this integral is finite. On the interior, differentiating gives mean parameter of an exponential family ∇κ and covariance matrix ∇2κ.

Convex support of an exponential family (C=conv(suppν)​)

Words: 44
Natural exponential family OurBigBook.com
The convex support is the closed convex hull of the canonical sufficient-statistic support. For a minimal exponential family it has nonempty interior. Means at finite interior natural parameters lie in that interior, because a supporting hyperplane at a boundary mean would force the sufficient statistic itself onto that hyperplane.

Regular natural exponential family

Words: 41
Natural exponential family OurBigBook.com
A natural exponential family is regular when its parameter space is open. In a full regular family, differentiation under the normalizing integral is justified locally by exponential moments in a neighbourhood. Regularity does not guarantee that every possible data set has a finite maximum-likelihood estimator.

Full natural exponential family

Words: 147 Articles: 1
Natural exponential family OurBigBook.com
A natural exponential family is full when its parameter set is the entire natural parameter space, with no restrictions on the allowable canonical parameters beyond finiteness of its normalizing integral. Fullness must not be confused with minimality or regularity.

Maximum likelihood existence in a full regular natural exponential family (∇κ(θ)=Yˉ)

Words: 113
Full natural exponential family OurBigBook.com
In a full regular minimal exponential family, a finite maximum-likelihood estimator exists precisely when the observed sufficient-statistic mean is in the interior of the convex support of an exponential family. It is unique because the log likelihood is strictly concave. For an interior mean, positive base-measure mass near finitely many support points surrounding the mean makes κ(θ)−θTYˉ grow at least linearly as ∥θ∥→∞. At a finite boundary of the full open natural domain, the normalizing integral diverges. Thus the likelihood attains its maximum in the domain. On a boundary face, no finite parameter can have the required mean. A regular restriction should instead use the exact mean-image criterion. These full-family conventions are also discussed in Geyer's exponential-family lecture notes.

Normal natural-mean parameter ratio estimator (ϕ​1​=Xˉ/v)

Words: 52
Exponential family OurBigBook.com
For independent N(μ,v) observations with unknown mean and variance, the first natural parameter is ϕ1​=μ/v. Its maximum-likelihood estimator is Xˉ/[n−1∑i​(Xi​−Xˉ)2]. The delta method and independent asymptotic mean/variance errors give limiting variance v−1+2μ2v−2 for n​(ϕ​1​−ϕ1​). Knowing v removes the second contribution and makes the estimator exactly normal with variance 1/(nv).

Log-density domination in an exponential family

Words: 44
Exponential family OurBigBook.com
On a bounded parameter set, logfθ​(x)=θx−K(θ)+logf0​(x) has an integrable envelope of a function class whenever K is bounded and ∣X∣+∣logf0​(X)∣ is integrable under the sampling law. A compact subset of the interior natural-parameter domain bounds the cumulant function of an exponential family and provides moments under model sampling; log-base-density integrability is an additional condition.

Normal-gamma distribution (τ∼Gamma(α,β),μ∣τ∼N(μ0​,(Kτ)−1))

Words: 64
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
A normal-gamma conjugate prior for the mean μ and precision parameter τ of a Gaussian distribution specifies a Gamma distribution τ∼Gamma(α,β) in the shape-rate convention and a conditional Gaussian distribution μ∣τ∼N(μ0​,(Kτ)−1). Its joint probability density function is proportional to τα−1τ​exp[−βτ−Kτ(μ−μ0​)2/2], with α,β,K>0. For n independent observations with Gaussian distribution N(μ,τ−1), multiplication by the likelihood and completing the square gives
Kn​=K+n,μn​=(Kμ0​+nxˉ)/(K+n),αn​=α+n/2,
(1)
and βn​=β+∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2/2+Kn(xˉ−μ0​)2/[2(K+n)]. The identity behind conjugacy is K(μ−μ0​)2+∑i​(xi​−μ)2=(K+n)(μ−μn​)2+∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2+Kn(xˉ−μ0​)2/(K+n).

Gamma rate gamma conjugacy (Θ∣x∼Gamma(A+nr,B+∑i​xi​))

Words: 68
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
Conditionally independent gamma distributions with known shape r and unknown rate Θ have likelihood proportional to Θnre−Θ∑i​xi​. A gamma distribution prior of shape A and rate B therefore updates to shape A+nr and rate B+∑i​xi​. When A+nr>1, the posterior mean of the conditional claim mean r/Θ is r(B+∑i​xi​)/(A+nr−1). For A=rk+1 and B=kμ, this is a credibility estimate with weight n/(n+k). It is the reciprocal-rate version of gamma scale inverse-gamma conjugacy.

Uniform-Pareto conjugacy (θ∣y∼Pareto(α+n,max(β,maxy)))

Words: 72 Articles: 1
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
A Pareto distribution prior for the upper limit of independent uniform distributions remains Pareto after observing data. The likelihood adds the sample size to the shape and replaces the lower bound by the larger of the prior bound and sample maximum.

Uniform-Pareto model evidence (p(y∣α,β)=αβα/[(α+n)mα+n])

Words: 35
Uniform-Pareto conjugacy OurBigBook.com
For positive observations and m=max(β,maxj​yj​), the Bayesian model evidence follows by integrating the uniform distribution likelihood against a Pareto distribution prior. For independent groups with shared fixed hyperparameters, these evidences multiply. The resulting hyperparameter log likelihood is Ilogα−∑i​log(α+ni​)−α∑i​log(mi​/β) up to a constant.

Gamma scale inverse-gamma conjugacy (Θ∣x∼InvGamma(k+nα,λ+∑j​xj​))

Words: 76 Articles: 1
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
For conditionally independent gamma distribution observations of known shape α and unknown scale Θ, an inverse-gamma distribution prior with shape k and scale λ gives an inverse-gamma distribution posterior with shape k+nα and scale λ+∑j​xj​. The posterior mean of the conditional claim expected value αΘ is α(λ+∑j​xj​)/(k+nα−1) when the denominator is positive.

Exact Bühlmann credibility for gamma claims (mB​=mBayes​)

Words: 32
Gamma scale inverse-gamma conjugacy OurBigBook.com
With the gamma scale inverse-gamma conjugacy model and prior shape k>2, the Bühlmann model has v/a=(k−1)/α. Its Bühlmann credibility premium equals α(λ+∑j​Xj​)/(k+nα−1), exactly the Bayes estimator under squared error loss of αΘ. Equality holds because the posterior mean is already affine in the observed sample mean.

Normal-inverse-gamma prior

Words: 50
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
For a normal linear model with coefficient vector η and residual variance σ2, a normal-inverse-gamma prior has η∣σ2∼N(m,σ2V) and σ2∼IG(a,b), with V a positive-definite matrix and a,b>0. The inverse-gamma density is proportional to (σ2)−a−1e−b/σ2. Multiplying by the normal linear model likelihood function and completing the square preserves the family, giving an analytic posterior and evidence integral.

Natural conjugate prior

Words: 199 Articles: 2
Conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
For an exponential family with density proportional to exp{θTT(x)−A(θ)}, its natural conjugate prior has density proportional to exp{θTλ1​−λ2​A(θ)}. Observations update λ1​ by adding sufficient statistics and update λ2​ by adding sample size.

Natural conjugate credibility identity (E[m(Θ)∣x]=(kμ+∑i​xi​)/(k+n))

Words: 167 Articles: 1
Natural conjugate prior OurBigBook.com
For a positive-support exponential family f(x∣θ)=p(x)e−θx/q(θ), normalization gives m(θ)=−q′(θ)/q(θ). The natural conjugate prior proportional to q(θ)−ke−kμθ has logarithmic derivative k(m(θ)−μ). If its endpoint density values vanish, integration gives prior expected value E[m(Θ)]=μ. Observations update k to k+n and μ to (kμ+∑i​xi​)/(k+n). The endpoint control for a Laplace-family conjugate posterior justifies the same mean identity after updating, giving an exact credibility estimate with credibility factor n/(n+k).
Endpoint control for a Laplace-family conjugate posterior
Words: 111
Natural conjugate credibility identity OurBigBook.com
In the positive-support exponential family q(θ)=∫p(x)e−θxdx, a proper natural conjugate prior with zero endpoint density values has mean parameter strictly inside the essential convex support (a,b). Updating that mean by a positive-weight average with supported observations keeps it inside (a,b). At finite parameter endpoints, prior vanishing forces q→∞, so the increased posterior power q−k−n still vanishes. At positive infinity, positive base-measure mass below a point d smaller than the updated mean bounds q(θ) below by Ce−dθ, giving exponential decay of the posterior kernel. Mass above the updated mean gives the analogous bound at negative infinity. Thus the updated density is proper and has the boundary condition required to integrate its score.

Interior moment matching in a finite exponential family (∇log∑n​eθ⋅n=μ)

Words: 37

For a finite support whose convex hull has full dimension, every interior target mean is realized by a unique natural parameter. Minimize the coercive function log∑n​eθ⋅n−θ⋅μ; its gradient is the mean discrepancy and its Hessian matrix is the positive-definite covariance matrix of the support.

Inverse Gaussian shape estimation and nuisance orthogonality (Iψλ​=0,Iψψ​=1/(2ψ2))

Words: 69
Inverse Gaussian distribution OurBigBook.com
For the Inverse Gaussian distribution parametrized by shape ψ and mean λ, the joint maximum-likelihood estimators are λ=Yˉ and ψ​=(Y−1−1/Yˉ)−1. With known λ, the shape estimator is instead (Y−1−2/λ+Yˉ/λ2)−1. Parameter orthogonality gives the same Wald test formula n(ψ​−ψ0​)2/(2ψ​2) in both fits. The two estimators differ by Op​(n−1) under regular model sampling, so the test statistics agree to first order under the null; they need not be numerically equal in finite samples.

Inverse Gaussian sum closure (Sn​∼IG(ϕ,n2λ))

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Inverse Gaussian distribution OurBigBook.com
In the parametrization with density proportional to y−3/2exp[−(λ/y+ϕy)/2], the Inverse Gaussian distribution has cumulant-generating function K(t)=λϕ​−λ(ϕ−2t)​. For n independent copies, the average has this same family with parameters (nϕ,nλ), while the sum has parameters (ϕ,n2λ). These assertions follow by multiplying moment-generating functions and scaling the density. The more usual mean parameter is μ=λ/ϕ​, so the sum has mean nμ and shape n2λ.

Chi-squared transform of an inverse Gaussian variable (λ(X−μ)2/(μ2X)∼χ12​)

Words: 31

For X∼IG(μ,λ), h(X)=λ(X−μ)2/(μ2X) has the chi-squared-one law. Its two inverse branches contribute weights μ/(μ+x−​) and μ/(μ+x+​) to the transformed density, and reciprocal roots make those weights sum to one.

Reciprocal-root inverse Gaussian sampler (h(x)=μ2xλ(x−μ)2​)

Words: 82 Articles: 1
Inverse Gaussian distribution OurBigBook.com
For a chi-squared-one variable Y, the positive inverse branches of h(x)=λ(x−μ)2/(μ2x) have product μ2. Weight a branch value x by μ/(μ+x); the two weights sum to one. Multiplying the chi-squared density by this weight and the branch Jacobian gives the inverse Gaussian density on both sides of μ.

Scaled deviance (D∗=D/ϕ)

Words: 69
Exponential-family deviance OurBigBook.com
For an exponential dispersion family with a common dispersion parameter ϕ, scaled deviance compares a fitted model with the saturated statistical model at the same dispersion:
D∗=2{ℓ(θ;ϕ)−ℓ(θ;ϕ)}=ϕD​,D=2∑i​{yi​(θi​−θi​)−b(θi​)+b(θi​)}.
(9)
For a normal distribution with variance σ2, unscaled deviance is the residual sum of squares and scaled deviance is that sum divided by σ2. For the binomial distribution, ϕ=1 and the two coincide. Unknown Gaussian dispersion requires variance estimation; raw deviance differences should not be treated as chi-squared statistics without the scale.

Underdispersion
