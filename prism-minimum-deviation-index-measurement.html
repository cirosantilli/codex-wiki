Prism minimum-deviation index measurement (nrel​=sin[(A+δmin​)/2]/sin(A/2))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Refractive index
OurBigBook.com Words: 556
A prism minimum-deviation index measurement infers a transparent sample's relative refractive index from two angles: its known apex angle A and the smallest deviation δmin​ of a selected spectral line. A spectrometer combines a source and slit, a collimator, a rotating optical prism table, and a viewing optical telescope with an angular readout. The collimator supplies a nearly parallel beam; the optical telescope locates its emergent direction. This turns an otherwise difficult measurement of the phase velocity of light into a measurement of angles.
For the symmetric path through a uniform optical prism, with the same external medium at both faces and positive refractive indices,
r1​=r2​=2A​,i=e=2A+δmin​​,nrel​=next​nprism​​=sin(A/2)sin[(A+δmin​)/2]​.
(18)
The angles i,e,r1​,r2​ are measured from face normals. This is the minimum deviation relation, not a formula for an arbitrary prism orientation. An absolute refractive index additionally requires next​.
For example, stipulate A=60∘ and nrel​=3/2. Then i=e=arcsin(3/4)≃48.5904∘ and δmin​≃37.1808∘. Substitution recovers nrel​=1.5. These synthetic values illustrate the geometry; they are not readings for the prism in the laboratory protocol below.
A Michigan State University teaching experiment uses a mercury lamp, slit, collimator, prism table and optical telescope, with a subsidiary angular scale read to one arcminute. First focus the optical telescope on a distant object; with the prism removed, align its crosshair to a narrow vertical slit image, focus the collimator, and establish the direct-beam zero. Insert and centre the prism. Select the green mercury spectral line, tabulated at 546.1nm, then rotate the prism while tracking its image with the optical telescope. Locate the turning point where continued prism rotation reverses the image motion, and read the deviation. Re-establish the minimum separately for each other spectral line. The protocol assumes A=60∘; that assumed apex angle is not an independently measured result. [ref]
For an uncertainty-aware implementation, repeat the turning-point search from both prism-rotation directions, recheck the direct-beam reference, and record individual readings before averaging. Independently determine A or include its calibration uncertainty; record the chosen spectral line, sample identity, temperature and surrounding-air conditions. These are proposed controls deduced from the inference, not claimed additional instructions or results of that manual. Finite slit width, unresolved spectral lines, imperfect collimation, table tilt, angular calibration and sample nonuniformity can limit the inferred refractive index. One arcminute readout resolution alone does not establish one arcminute total measurement uncertainty. A single selected wavelength gives one index, not a measured optical dispersion curve.
A short geometric derivation uses r1​+r2​=A and δ=i+e−A. Reversibility exchanges entrance and exit. At the symmetric stationary path, i=e and r1​=r2​; applying Snell's law gives the displayed index relation. The plotted calculation checks that this stationary point is a minimum on the chosen transmitting branch. To assess local sensitivity, differentiate the inferred index using angles in radians:
nrel​dnrel​​=21​cot(2A+δmin​​)dδmin​+21​[cot(2A+δmin​​)−cot(2A​)]dA.
(19)
This first-order relation exposes both angular contributions; it is not an instruction to add signed systematic errors as independent random errors. In the synthetic example, a deviation error of +1 arcminute at fixed A changes the inferred relative refractive index by approximately +1.92×10−4.
