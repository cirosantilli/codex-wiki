A linearly increasing radiative forcing produces a fast adjustment over about four years superposed on a slow deep-ocean response over about four hundred years. Once forcing stops increasing at year 70, surface warming continues and approaches the final 3K equilibrium. The right panel of solution gives a model response normalized to the supplied 2K endpoint and 3K equilibrium.
Write u=t−70 after stabilization and 3−Ts​=Af​e−u/4+As​e−u/400, with Af​+As​=1K. For a 70-year forcing ramp, the normalized step response is 1−bf​e−t/4−bs​e−t/400, bf​+bs​=1. The ramp endpoint implies
1=3[bf​704​(1−e−70/4)+bs​70400​(1−e−70/400)].
(87)
This gives bs​≃0.322, Af​≃0.116K and As​≃0.884K. Thus
T˙s​=4Af​​e−u/4+400As​​e−u/400≃0.0045, 0.0028, 0.0023Kyr−1
(88)
at u=10,15,20 years respectively. The usual order estimate after the fast tail has largely disappeared is T˙s​∼(3−2)/400≃0.0025Kyr−1​, with a still visible fast contribution near year 10.
To hold Ts​=T∗​ fixed, the deep layer obeys Ts​−Td​∝e−γu/Cd​ and the surface equation requires F=λT∗​+γ(T∗​−Td​). Hence F˙=−γ2(T∗​−Td​)/Cd​. Using bf​≃λ/(λ+γ) gives γ/λ≃0.475 and γ/Cd​≃1/(400bf​)≃0.00369yr−1. With T∗​=2K and initial forcing F0​=3λ, the initial fractional decline is
−F0​F˙​≃3γ/Cd​​≃0.00123yr−1≃0.12% per year.​
(89)
The rate declines slowly as the ocean contrast disappears. A coarser estimate treating the post-fast warming as an effective equilibrium change gives 0.0025/3≃0.08% per year; distinguishing the instantaneous surface feedback from the equilibrium feedback explains that difference.
Wording qualification: the printed text says a “1% increase … over 70 years”. The conventional transient climate response experiment uses 1% per year, which nearly doubles concentration in 70 years. A literal total 1% increase would not define the usual equilibrium climate sensitivity or transient climate response. The supplied 3K and 2K are used here as the endpoint normalizations, and a logarithmically uniform concentration growth gives the assumed linear forcing ramp.
