Equating marginal mitigation cost to the previous marginal damage gives
EM0​W​=r−gκγD0​WTγ−1​,E=κγD0​Tγ−1M0​(r−g)​.
(92)
Using T˙=κE from the cumulative-emissions approximation,
Tγ−1T˙=γD0​M0​(r−g)​,T(t)γ=T(t0​)γ+D0​M0​(r−g)​(t−t0​).​
(93)
Thus T∝t1/γ at late times and E∝t−(γ−1)/γ. Warming slows but never stops, and cumulative emissions remain unbounded. This follows from a cost model in which zero emissions is infinitely costly and from a marginal-damage rule with constant positive r−g.
The approximation eventually fails if cumulative emissions leave the range of constant TCRE, if large damages suppress economic growth, if technological change alters M0​, or if fossil resources and mitigation options impose different limits. The slowly varying-temperature approximation also needs T˙/[T(r−g)]≪1. Thresholds, adaptation, uncertainty and unequal impacts are omitted; the formula is a property of this model rather than a universal policy trajectory.
