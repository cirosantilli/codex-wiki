The planetary equilibrium temperature is the effective planetary temperature at which absorbed starlight balances emitted thermal radiation, neglecting internal power. With uniform reradiation over the full surface,
(1−α)πRp2​σTeff4​d2R⋆2​​=4πRp2​ϵσTeq4​.
(102)
Cancelling areas gives
Teq​=Teff​(2dR⋆​​)1/2(ϵ1−α​)1/4.​
(103)
Using the supplied Bond albedos and taking the thermal emissivity ϵ=1 yields Teq,⊕​≃255K,Teq,J​≃103K​. “Perfect black bodies” must here mean perfect infrared emitters, since a perfect absorber at all wavelengths would instead have α=0, inconsistent with retaining the supplied reflected fractions. If both albedos were set to zero, the corresponding values would be about 279 and 122K.
Earth's mean surface temperature of about 288K exceeds its emission temperature mainly because of the greenhouse effect. Jupiter also radiates substantial internal energy from cooling and contraction, including interior differentiation processes, so absorbed sunlight alone underpredicts its emission. Jupiter has no solid surface; the quoted 150K is a representative atmospheric level and is not identical to an effective radiating temperature.
