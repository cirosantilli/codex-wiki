At full phase, reflected-light contrast is
F⋆,λ​Fp,λrefl​​=Ag,λ​(dRp​​)2,​
(104)
where Ag​ is the geometric albedo, and other phases multiply this by a phase function normalized to one at full phase. Thermal contrast for uniform blackbody emitters is
F⋆,λ​Fp,λth​​=(R⋆​Rp​​)2Bλ​(Teff​)Bλ​(Tp​)​.​
(105)
The corresponding bolometric thermal ratio is (Rp​/R⋆​)2(Tp​/Teff​)4. If zero internal heating and equilibrium are assumed, energy balance also gives a bolometric reflected ratio α(Rp​/d)2/4=(αϵ/(1−α))(Rp​/R⋆​)2(Tp​/Teff​)4 for the luminosity ratio; it is not a directional full-phase contrast.
Earth's thermal emission peaks around 10μm, and solar emission around 0.5μm. At 0.5μm the thermal contrast for Tp​=288K is negligible, about 5×10−46; reflected light dominates. Taking illustrative visible Ag​≃0.3 gives C0.5​∼5×10−10​. A Bond albedo does not uniquely determine a spectral geometric albedo; a Lambert sphere with Bond albedo 0.3 would instead have Ag​=0.2, giving 3.6×10−10. At 20μm, the Planck function gives C20​≃1.0×10−6​ for 288K; using 255K gives about 7×10−7. These are order estimates, since the terrestrial spectrum is not a uniform blackbody.
