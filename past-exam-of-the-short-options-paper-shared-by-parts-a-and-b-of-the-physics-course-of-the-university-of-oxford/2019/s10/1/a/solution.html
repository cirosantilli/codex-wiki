After injection, the radiopharmaceutical circulates and accumulates according to its biological targeting and clearance. Technetium-99m undergoes an isomeric nuclear transition, producing approximately 140keV gamma rays; its roughly six-hour half-life permits useful counting with limited persistence. Some photons undergo absorption or Compton scattering in the patient. A lead collimator in the gamma camera accepts a narrow range of directions so the accepted event retains positional information.
An accepted gamma ray deposits energy, commonly in a NaI(Tl) scintillator, by photoelectric effect or Compton scattering. The resulting visible particle scintillation light is collected by photomultipliers. Their relative pulse sizes determine an event position and their summed pulse estimates its energy. A pulse-height analyser accepts an energy window around the photopeak to reject many scattered events. Position and uniformity corrections are applied; accepted events increment a two-dimensional pixel histogram. After acquisition the computer displays this count map as a planar projection of tracer activity. Finite counting statistics, attenuation, scattering and the collimator limit the image's quantitative accuracy and spatial resolution.[ref]
