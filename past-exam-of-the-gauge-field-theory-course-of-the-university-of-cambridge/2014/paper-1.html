In spontaneous symmetry breaking, the action respects a symmetry while a selected vacuum state is invariant only under a proper subgroup. A nonzero vacuum expectation value can identify that choice. For a local gauge theory, gauge transformations are redundancies rather than transformations between physically different states; a gauge-fixed scalar expectation value describes the Higgs mechanism and its massive excitations. It should not be interpreted as breaking an observable local redundancy.
Write the adjoint scalar field as Φ=Φ0​+φ, with Hermitian gauge generators normalized by tr(tatb)=δab/2. Since Φ0​ is constant, the part of the covariant derivative linear in the gauge field is Dμ​Φ0​=igAμa​[ta,Φ0​]. Both ta and Φ0​ are Hermitian, so their commutator is anti-Hermitian. The scalar kinetic term therefore contributes
Lmass​=−g2Aμa​Abμtr([ta,Φ0​][tb,Φ0​])=21​Aμa​(m2)abAbμ.
(1)
Matching coefficients gives the adjoint scalar gauge-boson mass matrix
(m2)ab=−2g2tr([ta,Φ0​][tb,Φ0​]).​
(2)
It is positive semidefinite: for real coefficients xa​, its quadratic form is 2g2tr(C†C)≥0, where C=[xa​ta,Φ0​]. Its null space consists of generators commuting with the vacuum, the Lie algebra of its centralizer.
For a diagonal vacuum Φ0​=diag(λ1​,λ2​,λ3​), the matrix unit Eij​ obeys [Eij​,Φ0​]=(λj​−λi​)Eij​. The two normalized Hermitian generators built from Eij​,Eji​ each acquire
mij2​=g2(λi​−λj​)2.
(3)
The diagonal generators t3,t8 commute with every such vacuum and remain massless. Orthogonality of these root generators also shows that the mass matrix is diagonal in the supplied Gell-Mann matrices basis. Take g>0 and v=0 below; v=0 restores the full SU(3) group.

For eigenvalues (v,v,−2v), the first two entries coincide. Consequently the 12 generators t1,t2, as well as t3,t8, commute with the vacuum. The 13 and 23 eigenvalue differences both have magnitude 3∣v∣, giving
m1,2,3,8​=0,m4,5,6,7​=3g∣v∣.​
(4)
There are four massless and four massive gauge bosons. The centralizer of a Hermitian matrix in SU(n) preserves the degenerate two-dimensional eigenspace, so the unbroken group is
H=S(U(2)×U(1))≅Z2​SU(2)×U(1)​≅U(2).​
(5)
At the Lie algebra level this is su(2)⊕u(1), generated by t1,2,3 and t8. The finite quotient specifies the actual subgroup inside SU(3); simply writing SU(2)×U(1) suppresses that global identification. The four real massive fields combine into a complex SU(2) doublet and its conjugate. Unbroken SU(2) rotations relate its two components, explaining the equal masses of the 13 and 23 sectors in this SU(3) gauge theory with an adjoint Higgs field.

For eigenvalues (v,−v,0), all entries are distinct. The 12 difference has magnitude 2∣v∣, while both the 13 and 23 differences have magnitude ∣v∣. The adjoint scalar gauge-boson mass matrix therefore gives
m3,8​=0,m1,2​=2g∣v∣,m4,5,6,7​=g∣v∣.​
(6)
Only the two diagonal generators commute with the vacuum. Its unbroken group is the maximal torus
H={diag(eiα,eiβ,e−i(α+β))}≅U(1)×U(1).​
(7)
The two real fields belonging to each ij pair combine into oppositely charged conjugate vector states with equal mass. The further equality between the 13 and 23 masses comes from the equally spaced vacuum eigenvalues; the surviving Abelian group alone does not force distinct root-charge sectors to have equal masses. Thus a fourfold massive degeneracy occurs in both examples, but its relation to the unbroken continuous symmetry differs. The scalar potential has not been specified, so no additional symmetry of that potential or physical scalar mass spectrum can be inferred.

The Standard Model is a chiral gauge theory: left-chiral quarks and leptons form SU(2) weak doublets, while the corresponding right-chiral charged fields are weak singlets. The covariant kinetic term contains gW​Qˉ​L​γμtaQL​Wμa​. With W±=(W1∓iW2)/2​, its off-diagonal generators give the weak charged current
Lcc​⊃2​gW​​bˉL​γμVtb∗​tL​Wμ−​+h.c.=22​gW​​bˉγμ(1−γ5)Vtb∗​tWμ−​+h.c.
(8)
An overall interaction sign depends on the convention for the covariant derivative and has no effect on this decay rate. The chiral projector PL​=(1−γ5)/2 explains the vector-minus-axial combination. The Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix comes from the different left-handed rotations that diagonalize up- and down-type fermion mass matrices. Set Vtb​=1 and mb​=0 as stipulated.
Let Pμν​=−gμν​+p3μ​p3ν​/mW2​, the massive vector polarization sum. Sum over final spins and average over the two equally populated initial top quark spins. The fermion spin sum and the Dirac adjoint of the vertex give
∣M∣2​=16gW2​​Pμν​tr[p2​γμ(1−γ5)(p1​+mt​)γν(1−γ5)].
(9)
The mt​ term vanishes because (1−γ5)γν(1−γ5)=0. Anticommuting γ5 through the remaining gamma matrices, using (1−γ5)2=2(1−γ5), then using cyclicity of the matrix trace and the symmetry of Pμν​ yields
∣M∣2​=8gW2​​Pμν​tr[(1−γ5)p1​γμp2​γν].​
(10)
The extra factor two from the chiral algebra compensates the initial spin-average factor. The term with γ5 is antisymmetric in μ,ν and vanishes upon contraction with Pμν​. The remaining gamma-matrix trace is
4[p1μ​p2ν​+p1ν​p2μ​−(p1​⋅p2​)gμν].
(11)
Its contraction gives
∣M∣2​=2gW2​​(p1​⋅p2​)+mW2​gW2​​(p1​⋅p3​)(p2​⋅p3​).
(12)
Here p1​=p2​+p3​, p12​=mt2​, p22​=0 and p32​=mW2​, so
p1​⋅p2​=p2​⋅p3​=2mt2​−mW2​​,p1​⋅p3​=2mt2​+mW2​​.
(13)
Therefore
∣M∣2​=4mW2​gW2​​(mt2​−mW2​)(mt2​+2mW2​).
(14)
The two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space has rest-frame momentum p∗=(mt2​−mW2​)/(2mt​). Substitution into Γ=p∗∣M∣2​/(8πmt2​) gives the two-body top-quark decay width
Γ(t→bW+)=64πmW2​gW2​mt3​​(1−mt2​mW2​​)2(1+2mt2​mW2​​).​
(15)
The squared threshold factor combines the vanishing final momentum with the corresponding kinematic factor in the squared scattering amplitude. The longitudinal W boson polarization contributes the high-mass enhancement proportional to mt3​/mW2​. The result is at tree level, for an on-shell W boson and mt​>mW​; finite bottom mass and radiative corrections change it. Restoring quark mixing multiplies the width by ∣Vtb​∣2.

A relativistic quantum field describes both creation and annihilation of excitations; a one-particle wavefunction alone is insufficient when interactions can change particle number. For a free complex scalar field, one conventional mode expansion of a free field is
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​[ap​e−ip⋅x+bp†​eip⋅x],Ep​=p2+m2​.
(16)
The apparently negative-frequency term creates a positive-energy antiparticle; it is not a physical particle of unbounded negative energy. The normal-ordered Hamiltonian and conserved global phase charge have the forms
H=∫(2π)3d3p​Ep​(ap†​ap​+bp†​bp​),Q=q∫(2π)3d3p​(ap†​ap​−bp†​bp​).
(17)
Particles and antiparticles have equal mass and opposite additive internal charges. Including both frequency sectors is also essential to the local field commutator: the positive- and negative-frequency terms combine so that spacelike-separated scalar observables commute, respecting microcausality.
A Dirac field similarly contains particle annihilation operators multiplying u spinors and antiparticle creation operators multiplying v spinors. Its canonical anticommutation relations implement Fermi-Dirac statistics, whereas scalar modes use commutators and Bose-Einstein statistics. These assignments are tied to the Spin-statistics theorem under the usual relativistic locality and positivity assumptions. Conserved charge constrains particle creation and annihilation, but particle and antiparticle numbers need not be separately conserved in an interacting theory.
An antiparticle need not be a distinct species. A neutral real scalar field is self-conjugate; the photon is also its own antiparticle. A Majorana fermion obeys a self-conjugacy condition and cannot carry an unbroken additive charge that distinguishes it from its antiparticle. Electric neutrality alone does not force self-conjugacy when other conserved quantum numbers distinguish the states.

Loop corrections in perturbative quantum field theory integrate over unbounded internal four-momentum. For example, after a Wick rotation, a four-dimensional integral with large-momentum behavior
∫Λ(kE2​+m2)2d4kE​​∼∫ΛkE​dkE​​
(18)
has a logarithmic ultraviolet divergence. This is sensitivity to short distances, distinct from a possible low-momentum infrared divergence.
Regularization in quantum field theory first makes the expression mathematically well defined, for example with a cutoff Λ or dimensional regularization in d=4−2ϵ. A regulator is an intermediate prescription rather than a measured physical parameter; a hard momentum cutoff can obscure gauge identities, so their preservation or restoration must be checked. Introduce bare fields and parameters, such as ϕ0​=Zϕ1/2​ϕ, and split the regulated Lagrangian density into a renormalized part and local counterterms. For instance, wavefunction, mass and coupling counterterms cancel the corresponding divergent parts of Green functions order by order.
Renormalization conditions fix finite parameters through specified observables or a subtraction prescription. Once these physical inputs are held fixed, predictions must not depend on the arbitrary regulator. In a renormalizable quantum field theory, a finite set of operator coefficients suffices to absorb ultraviolet divergences at every perturbative order, with the allowed structures constrained by the theory's symmetries. Gauge theories impose relations among counterterms through identities such as the Ward-Takahashi identity; one cannot choose every diagram's subtraction independently.
The remaining dependence on a chosen renormalization scale describes running couplings and is organized by the renormalization group. An effective field theory can instead include higher-dimension operators suppressed by a high scale. Infinitely many are allowed in principle, but only finitely many contribute at a fixed accuracy in the low-energy expansion. Renormalization is therefore a controlled relation between parameters and predictions at different scales, rather than an instruction to discard every infinity without fixing physical inputs.

The left- and right-chiral components of a charged Standard Model fermion transform in different electroweak group representations. A bare Dirac mass term −mfˉ​L​fR​+h.c. is therefore not invariant under the full electroweak gauge group. The Higgs doublet supplies the missing transformation properties. With H=iσ2​H∗, the gauge-invariant Yukawa interactions are
LY​=−Qˉ​L​Yd​HdR​−Qˉ​L​Yu​HuR​−LˉL​Ye​HeR​+h.c.,
(19)
where each Yf​ is a flavor-space Yukawa matrix. For example, hypercharges in the down-quark term add to −1/6+1/2−1/3=0, while those in the up-quark term add to −1/6−1/2+2/3=0. Thus the Higgs and conjugate Higgs are required in different terms.
After electroweak symmetry breaking, choose unitary gauge with H=(0,(v+h)/2​)T. The fermion mass matrices become
Md​=2​v​Yd​,Mu​=2​v​Yu​,Me​=2​v​Ye​.​
(20)
A singular value decomposition UfL†​Mf​UfR​=diag(mf,1​,mf,2​,mf,3​) defines nonnegative physical tree-level masses. The mismatch UuL†​UdL​ is the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix in the weak charged current. The corresponding physical Higgs boson couples diagonally as −(mf​/v)hfˉ​f in the minimal one-doublet theory. This relates its coupling strength to each mass, but the measured Yukawa couplings remain independent input parameters: the minimal theory does not predict their hierarchy.
The minimal renormalizable Standard Model contains no right-handed neutrino, so it has neither a neutrino Dirac Yukawa term nor a gauge-invariant renormalizable left-handed Majorana mass term. Its neutrinos are massless. Observed neutrino oscillations require going beyond that minimal field content or operator set: adding right-handed singlets permits Dirac masses, while the dimension-five Weinberg operator gives a Majorana neutrino mass of order v2/Λ after symmetry breaking. These alternatives should be distinguished from the charged-fermion Yukawa mechanism.
