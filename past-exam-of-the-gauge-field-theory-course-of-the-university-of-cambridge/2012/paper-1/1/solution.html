The gauge fields require their own Yang-Mills Lagrangian, −Wμνa​Waμν/4. The non-Abelian field strength contains a term quadratic in the gauge potentials, so squaring it produces three-vector and four-vector self-interaction vertices, with derivative momentum factors in the cubic vertex and SU(2) structure constants in both. They exist in either scalar phase and must be added to the scalar-generated vertices. A gauge-fixed quantum calculation also has gauge-fixing and, where applicable, ghost terms; those are not extra physical boson species.
For μ2>0, the global phase transformation Φ↦eiαΦ leaves both the scalar potential and the covariant kinetic term unchanged while leaving Wj​ fixed. Its Noether charge counts scalar particles minus antiparticles. Thus ϕa​ϕb​→ϕa​ϕb​ and ϕa​ϕa∗​→W3​W3​ are compatible with this charge, whereas ϕa​ϕb​→Wj​Wk​ is forbidden: its initial charge is two and final charge zero. Charge conservation is necessary, not sufficient, for a particular amplitude to be nonzero; other quantum numbers and kinematics still apply.

The observed charged weak interaction couples left-chiral fermions within SU(2) doublets. In the Standard Model, QL​=(uL​,dL​) and LL​=(νL​,eL​) are doublets, while uR​,dR​,eR​ are singlets.
