A bare Dirac mass couples left and right chirality, so the distinct electroweak representations prevent writing it as a gauge-invariant term before symmetry breaking. Instead the Yukawa interactions are
LY​=−Qˉ​L​Yd​dR​Φ−Qˉ​L​Yu​uR​Φ−LˉL​Ye​eR​Φ+Hermitian conjugate,Φ=iτ2​Φ∗.
(15)
Weak indices are contracted to singlets. Substituting the vacuum value gives mass matrices Mf​=Yf​v/2​ and a physical Higgs coupling −fˉ​L​Mf​fR​σ/v plus its conjugate. Biunitary diagonalization defines mass eigenstates; a mismatch between the up- and down-quark left rotations appears in the CKM matrix. Gauge-boson masses arise from the Higgs kinetic term, whereas these fermion masses arise from the Yukawa term.
With only the minimal field content there is no right-handed neutrino, so no renormalizable neutrino Yukawa term. Adding a gauge-singlet NR​ permits both a Dirac scale mD​=yν​v/2​ and a Majorana scale MR​. For one family the symmetric mass matrix in a left-chiral basis is
(0mD​​mD​MR​​).
(16)
Its signed eigenvalues are (MR​±MR2​+4mD2​​)/2. Rephasing gives positive physical masses, and for ∣MR​∣≫∣mD​∣ the seesaw mechanism yields
mlight​≃∣mD​∣2/∣MR​∣,mheavy​≃∣MR​∣.​
(17)
For mD​∼100GeV and MR​∼1014GeV, the light scale is about 0.1eV. Integrating out the heavy state generates the dimension-five Weinberg operator, whose Higgs vacuum insertion gives the same suppression. Alternatively a Dirac neutrino can have a very small Yukawa coupling with lepton number conserved. A tiny observed neutrino mass does not by itself distinguish these mechanisms.
