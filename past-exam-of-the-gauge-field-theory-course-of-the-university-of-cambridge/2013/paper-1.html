Use natural units and the Minkowski metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). Varying the Proca action with respect to Aν​ gives ∂L/∂Aν​=m2Aν and ∂L/∂(∂μ​Aν​)=−Fμν. The Euler-Lagrange equation is therefore
∂μ​Fμν+m2Aν=0.​
(1)
Taking a divergence annihilates the antisymmetric field strength, since commuting derivatives are symmetric in their indices. For m=0 this gives the Lorenz constraint in Proca theory ∂ν​Aν=0, and hence (□+m2)Aν=0. The constraint follows from dynamics; it is not an additional gauge fixing condition for this massive theory.
For four-momentum pμ=(E,0,0,p), longitudinal polarization of a massive vector boson lies in the time–z plane, has p⋅ε=0 and is normalized by ε⋅ε=−1. Taking
εLμ​=m1​(p,0,0,E)​
(2)
satisfies both requirements because E2−p2=m2. It can also be obtained by boosting the rest-frame unit spatial polarization along the direction of motion. Together with two transverse polarizations it gives the three physical spin states of a massive spin one particle.
In the Higgs boson rest frame, choose pμ=(E,0,0,p∗) and p′μ=(E,0,0,−p∗), with E=mH​/2 and p∗2=mH2​/4−mW2​. Consistent normalized longitudinal polarization vectors are
εL​(p)=mW​1​(p∗,0,0,E),εL​(p′)=mW​1​(p∗,0,0,−E).
(3)
Their Lorentz contraction is (p∗2+E2)/mW2​=(2p∗2+mW2​)/mW2​. The Higgs boson coupling to W bosons consequently gives
∣MLL​∣2=4mW4​v2g4​(2p∗2+mW2​)2=v2mH4​​(1−2x)2,x=mH2​mW2​​.
(4)
Using the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space, p∗=mH​1−4x​/2 and v−2=2​GF​ yields the longitudinal partial decay width
Γ(H→WL+​WL−​)=8π2​GF​mH3​​1−4x​(1−2x)2,mH​>2mW​.​
(5)
The charged W bosons are distinguishable, so there is no identical-particle factor of 1/2. This expression includes the entire angular phase space but only the specified longitudinal final polarizations. The Higgs decay to two W bosons summed over all polarizations instead has the factor 1−4x+12x2; the additional transverse contribution is 8x2. At mH​≫mW​ the longitudinal channel dominates and the leading width grows as GF​mH3​.

Below mH​=2mW​, the on-shell two-body decay channel is kinematically closed. An imaginary p∗ in the preceding formula is therefore a sign that this formula no longer applies. Slightly below threshold, the physical process can instead contain an on-shell W boson and an off-shell one, H→WW∗→Wffˉ​′, followed by decay of the remaining W boson. Its rate requires the virtual-vector propagator, its allowed fermionic decay channels and the corresponding multiparticle Lorentz-invariant phase space. The nonzero decay width of the W boson smooths the idealized threshold. A polarization-resolved rate for two on-shell longitudinal vectors must not be analytically continued and treated as that multiparticle rate.

In the stronger limit mH​≪mW​, both intermediate W bosons must be virtual. Their heavy propagators suppress the four-fermion decay amplitude, while the small available phase space suppresses its rate further. At energies small compared with mW​, integrating out the two vectors gives a local interaction proportional to GF​HJμ+​J−μ/v. Its dimension-seven coefficient implies a rate of order GF3​mH7​ when all four final fermions can be treated as massless, with channel-dependent constants. This is a low-energy scaling statement, not the on-shell longitudinal-width formula. Other accessible Higgs boson decay channels, such as a pair of fermions or loop-induced two-photon decay, must then be compared using their actual masses and couplings.

The Standard Model uses the local gauge group SU(3)c​×SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​. Its Higgs field is a complex Higgs doublet of hypercharge Y=1/2. A bare mass term for a weak gauge boson, or for a charged fermion joining inequivalent left- and right-handed gauge representations, would not respect the unbroken electroweak construction. Instead introduce a gauge-invariant Higgs potential
V(Φ)=−μ2Φ†Φ+λ(Φ†Φ)2,λ>0,μ2>0.
(6)
Its minima satisfy Φ†Φ=v2/2, v2=μ2/λ. Choosing a representative ⟨Φ⟩=(0,v)T/2​ leaves Q=T3​+Y unbroken. In this description electroweak symmetry breaking is SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​→U(1)em​; the underlying local gauge symmetry remains a redundancy of the theory. A vacuum representative is not a physical explicit violation of gauge invariance.
In unitary gauge, write Φ=(0,v+H)T/2​. The gauge-covariant kinetic term ∣Dμ​Φ∣2 produces
mW​=2gv​,mZ​=2v​g2+g′2​,mγ​=0,
(7)
with tanθW​=g′/g and e=gsinθW​. Three angular scalar directions supply the longitudinal gauge-boson polarizations of W+,W−,Z, while one real radial Higgs boson survives with mH2​=2λv2. Counting physical degrees of freedom checks the Higgs mechanism: the electroweak gauge sector and scalar doublet have 4×2+4=12 before the rearrangement and 3×3+2+1=12 afterwards.
The Higgs potential also fixes the scalar self-interactions:
V=constant+2mH2​​H2+2vmH2​​H3+8v2mH2​​H4.
(8)
The color gauge group is unbroken, so gluons remain massless. The same electroweak kinetic term fixes
L⊃v2mW2​​HWμ+​W−μ+vmZ2​​HZμ​Zμ+v2mW2​​H2Wμ+​W−μ+2v2mZ2​​H2Zμ​Zμ.
(9)
Gauge-invariant Yukawa couplings such as −yd​Qˉ​L​ΦdR​−yu​Qˉ​L​ΦuR​+h.c. give mf​=yf​v/2​ and the physical coupling −mf​Hfˉ​f/v. These relations predict stronger Higgs couplings to heavier massive particles; they do not determine the Yukawa parameters themselves. The minimal Standard Model does not supply observed neutrino masses. The surviving Higgs boson is neutral and has spin zero; its mass and self-coupling are tied to the curvature of the Higgs potential, not fixed by the gauge couplings alone. Higgs exchange also provides the Higgs cancellation in longitudinal vector scattering, maintaining the required high-energy behavior of weak amplitudes.
At a proton collider, important production mechanisms are gluon-fusion Higgs production through a heavy-quark loop, vector-boson fusion with two outgoing quark jets, associated WH or ZH production, and production with a top quark pair. At an electron–positron collider, Higgsstrahlung e+e−→ZH is a clean example. Tree-level decays include Higgs decay to a fermion pair and, when allowed, Higgs decay to two W bosons or Higgs decay to two Z bosons. Below an on-shell vector-pair threshold, one or both vectors can be virtual. The gg and γγ channels arise through loops; neutral colorless H has no corresponding direct tree-level gauge coupling. Rates and branching fractions depend on both couplings and kinematics, so “heaviest available particle” is a guide rather than a complete rule.
The Higgs boson discovery in 2012 supplied an experimental test. The ATLAS experiment and CMS experiment reconstructed two-photon and four-lepton invariant masses in Large Hadron Collider proton collisions. Electromagnetic calorimeters measure photon energies, while tracking and muon systems identify charged leptons; narrow reconstructed peaks can be distinguished from smooth backgrounds with control samples and a likelihood analysis. The ATLAS discovery paper reported a new resonance near 126.5GeV. An observed mass peak supports the existence of a new state; production channels, decay rates and angular distributions test whether it has the particular Higgs couplings and quantum numbers.[ref]
Finally, the hierarchy problem asks why the electroweak scalar mass scale remains much smaller than a putative heavy physical scale without delicate cancellations. A cutoff estimate suggests corrections δmH2​∼Λ2/(16π2) times couplings, but the quadratic cutoff term itself depends on the regularization convention. The physical concern persists when a heavy state of mass M couples to the Higgs field and produces threshold corrections of order its coupling times M2/(16π2). Matching a much smaller Higgs mass may then require cancellation between independent parameters. Supersymmetry, compositeness and other protective structures propose different resolutions; renormalizability of the Standard Model does not by itself resolve this sensitivity or establish that new particles must occur at one particular mass.

Interactions produce Feynman diagrams with internal lines whose independent loop momenta are integrated without an upper bound. Their large-momentum contributions can have an ultraviolet divergence. Two explicit examples in a scalar quartic theory are a one-loop tadpole diagram contributing to the two-point function and a one-loop four-point bubble diagram. After a Wick rotation, a spherical cutoff Λ gives the representative integrals
I1​=∫∣kE​∣<Λ​(2π)4d4kE​​kE2​+m21​=16π21​[Λ2−m2ln(1+m2Λ2​)],
(10)
I2​(0)=∫∣kE​∣<Λ​(2π)4d4kE​​(kE2​+m2)21​=16π21​[ln(1+m2Λ2​)+Λ2+m2m2​−1].
(11)
Combinatorial factors and couplings multiply these integrals in a particular diagram. The first is quadratically divergent by cutoff power counting; the second is logarithmically divergent. Other schemes package the divergent pieces differently.
In renormalization, introduce a regularization, separate bare parameters into renormalized parameters and counterterms, and fix the latter by stated normalization conditions. Local divergent pieces of the scalar two- and four-point functions are absorbed into mass, field normalization and quartic-coupling counterterms, with all other operators allowed by the chosen symmetries included as needed. A perturbatively renormalizable quantum field theory requires this finite counterterm family to remain closed at all orders. It does not require every individual bare diagram to be finite. For gauge theories, identities expressing gauge symmetry and absence of a gauge anomaly are essential in addition to superficial counting.
The action is dimensionless in natural units, so in four spacetime dimensions [L]=4 and [∂μ​]=1. The engineering dimensions of relativistic fields follow from matching the free kinetic terms:
2[ϕ]+2=4,2[ψ]+1=4,2[Aμ​]+2=4,
(12)
and hence
[ϕ]=1,[ψ]=23​,[Aμ​]=1.​
(13)
For a local operator O of mass dimension dO​, its coupling has dimension 4−dO​. Operators with dimension at most four are candidates for ordinary perturbative power-counting renormalizability. This test presupposes consistent kinetic terms, symmetries and the full set of induced counterterms; it is not a proof of physical consistency for an arbitrary vector interaction.

The operator (ϕ†ϕ)2 has mass dimension four, so [λ]=0. It is a power-counting renormalizable scalar quartic interaction. It describes scalar self-interactions, including the quartic term in the Higgs potential for the appropriate scalar representation. Its sign in the potential must be chosen for a stable large-field theory; dimension counting alone does not ensure stability.

b OurBigBook.com
The operator (ϕ†+ϕ)ψˉ​ψ has mass dimension 1+3=4, so [g]=0. It is a power-counting renormalizable Yukawa interaction between a real scalar combination and a fermion bilinear. A scalar vacuum expectation value contributes to a Dirac mass term; its fluctuations couple to the fermion pair. This is a schematic scalar–fermion theory. In a chiral gauge theory, representations must be combined gauge-invariantly, as in the Standard Model Higgs–fermion interactions. Without additional protecting symmetries, all allowed scalar counterterms must be included as well.

The operator ϕ†ϕψˉ​ψ has mass dimension 2+3=5, giving [g]=−1. It is not power-counting renormalizable as a fundamental interaction treated to arbitrary energy. It is a perfectly usable dimension-five effective operator below its suppression scale. Successive orders in that effective field theory require a derivative and operator expansion rather than one finite set of parameters valid at every order.

The operator Aμψˉ​γμ​ψ has mass dimension 1+3=4, so [e]=0. It is the renormalizable electromagnetic current coupling when completed as quantum electrodynamics: a massless Abelian gauge field couples minimally to the conserved Dirac current, with consistent gauge fixing and Ward identities. Indeed, replacing ∂μ​ by a gauge covariant derivative in the Dirac equation produces this interaction. The gauge structure, rather than dimension counting alone, ensures the necessary cancellations.[ref]

The operator Aμψˉ​∂μ​ψ has mass dimension 1+3+1=5, so [e]=−1. It is not power-counting renormalizable. Hermiticity may require a suitable conjugate or a symmetrized derivative, and electromagnetic gauge invariance would require a further consistent completion; neither repair changes its engineering dimension. Such derivative interactions can occur in an effective field theory, subject to its symmetries and operator redundancies.

Solution (Fermi interaction)

Words: 104
3 OurBigBook.com
Each Dirac current ψˉ​γμψ has mass dimension three, so their product has dimension six. Its coefficient therefore has dimension −2:
[GF​]=−2.​
(14)
The Fermi interaction is not power-counting renormalizable, but its success at low energies is explained by effective field theory. Exchange of a heavy W boson between the appropriate chiral weak currents has a propagator that expands as 1/(mW2​−q2)=mW−2​[1+q2/mW2​+⋯]. The leading term is a local four-fermion interaction and further terms are suppressed by powers of q2/mW2​. The complete spontaneously broken electroweak gauge theory resolves the pointlike approximation at higher energies. The source's vector-current product illustrates the operator dimension; the actual weak interaction also has its specific flavors and chiral structure.
