Inertia tensor (Iij​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics Moment of inertia
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 16
The inertia tensor packages the moments of inertia about every axis through a specified point O into one symmetric matrix. An axis still needs a direction: if n is a unit vector, its scalar moment of inertia is IO,n​=nTIO​n. The same mass layout can therefore be easy to spin about one direction and harder about another. The inertia tensor also determines rotational kinetic energy and relates angular velocity to angular momentum in the appropriate rigid body model.
Take position vectors r=(x,y,z) relative to O, expressed in an orthonormal basis. For point masses ma​, or a continuous distribution with mass density ρ and dm=ρdV,
(IO​)ij​=∑a​ma​(∣ra​∣2δij​−rai​raj​),(IO​)ij​=∫(∣r∣2δij​−ri​rj​)dm.
(21)
Here δij​ is the Kronecker delta. Equivalently, using the identity matrix 1 and an outer product,
IO​=∫(∣r∣21−rrT)dm=∫​y2+z2−xy−xz​−xyx2+z2−yz​−xz−yzx2+y2​​dm.
(22)
Every entry has SI units kgm2. The diagonal entries are axial moments of inertia; the off-diagonal entries carry a minus sign in this convention. Some texts instead define the integrals without the prefixed minus sign ∫xydm, ∫xzdm, ∫yzdm as their named off-diagonal quantities and put the minus signs into the displayed matrix. Check that convention before combining formulas. [ref] [ref]
The symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite, because its quadratic form is
uTIO​u=∫(∣u∣2∣r∣2−(u⋅r)2)dm=∫∣u×r∣2dm≥0.
(23)
The dot product and cross product identify this as a sum of squared distances when u is a unit vector. A zero moment of inertia occurs precisely when all the mass lies on that axis, up to sets of zero mass. A genuine three-dimensional body with positive mass density in a volume has strictly positive axial moments of inertia. Do not confuse this inertia tensor with the mass second-moment tensor S=∫rrTdm: they obey IO​=tr(S)1−S, where tr denotes the matrix trace.
If O is fixed in an inertial frame and each velocity is v=ω×r, summing the individual kinetic energies and angular momenta gives
LO​=IO​ω,K=21​ωTIO​ω=21​ω⋅LO​.
(24)
These formulas also describe angular momentum and rotational kinetic energy relative to the centre of mass G of a translating rigid body. In that case the total kinetic energy in the inertial frame is Ktotal​=21​M∣VG​∣2+21​ωTIG​ω. For a general moving point O, the body's actual velocities include VO​, so IO​ω alone need not be its angular momentum about O. [ref] [ref]
Changing the orthonormal basis changes components, rather than the physical distribution: if the columns of an orthogonal matrix Q are the new basis vectors expressed in the old basis, then IO′​=QTIO​Q. Changing the reference point is a different operation, governed by the tensor parallel-axis theorem. Both principal axes and principal moments of inertia must therefore be quoted with their reference point.
