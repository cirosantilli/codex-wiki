With charge density ρ, current density J, and speed of light c=(ϵ0​μ0​)−1/2, the four Maxwell equations in vacuum are
∇⋅E∇×E​=ρ/ϵ0​,=−∂t​B,​∇⋅B∇×B​=0,=μ0​J+c−2∂t​E.​​
(49)
The electric potential and magnetic vector potential determine the electric field and magnetic field by
E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A,B=∇×A.​
(50)
These definitions satisfy Gauss's law for magnetism and Faraday's law identically. The Lorenz gauge condition is
∇⋅A+c−2∂t​ϕ=0.​
(51)
Substituting into Gauss's law and using the Lorenz gauge condition gives
ϵ0​ρ​=−∇2ϕ−∂t​(∇⋅A)=−∇2ϕ+c−2∂t2​ϕ.
(52)
For the Ampère-Maxwell equation, the double-curl identity gives
∇(∇⋅A)−∇2A=μ0​J−c−2∇∂t​ϕ−c−2∂t2​A.
(53)
The two gradient terms cancel by the Lorenz gauge condition. Defining the d'Alembert operator with the sign convention □=∇2−c−2∂t2​, the remaining equations are
□ϕ=−ρ/ϵ0​,□A=−μ0​J.​
(54)
Conversely, if the electric potential and magnetic vector potential satisfy these wave equations and the Lorenz gauge condition, the same substitutions in reverse give Gauss's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation. The other two Maxwell equations follow identically from the definitions, proving equivalence within the Lorenz gauge.
