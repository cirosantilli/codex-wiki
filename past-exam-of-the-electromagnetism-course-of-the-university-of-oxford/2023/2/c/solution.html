Orient the unit normal n from R1​ to R2​ and write H for the magnetic field strength. For static electric fields, E=−∇ϕ; the static Ampère's law gives ∇×H=Jf​. The curl product rule therefore gives the local Poynting flux and terminal power identity
P=−∇ϕ×H=ϕJf​−∇×(ϕH).
(45)
Integrate over an oriented piece ΣR​ of the separating surface and apply Stokes theorem:
∫ΣR​​P⋅ndS=∫ΣR​​ϕJf​⋅ndS−∮∂ΣR​​ϕH⋅dℓ.
(46)
Once all the wires are included, the first surface integral is ∑k​ϕk​Ik​: the electric potential is constant on each perfect conductor and the integral of its normal current density is its signed electric current. Equivalently, remove the wire cross-sections and apply Stokes theorem in the surrounding insulator. Each resulting hole has negative boundary orientation, and the magnetostatic Ampère law around it gives the positive contribution ϕk​Ik​.
For a closed separating surface the boundary term is absent. For a noncompact separating surface, take R→∞ with the usual isolated-circuit condition ∮∂ΣR​​ϕH⋅dℓ→0. In either case,
∫Σ​P⋅ndS=k=1∑N​ϕk​Ik​.​
(47)
For a steady closed circuit the signed electric currents sum to zero, so shifting the reference of electric potential does not change the terminal power.
For a fully general noncompact surface, the hypotheses as printed need an additional condition at infinity. The exact result before imposing it is
P1→2​=k=1∑N​ϕk​Ik​−R→∞lim​∮∂ΣR​​ϕH⋅dℓ.​
(48)
Time independence alone does not remove this boundary term or ensure a finite Poynting flux; for example, spatially constant crossed electric fields and magnetic fields carry a constant Poynting vector through an infinite plane. The stated terminal-power formula holds with the boundary condition specified above.
