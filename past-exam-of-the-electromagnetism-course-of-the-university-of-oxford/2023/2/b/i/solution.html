Work in cylindrical coordinates and use the assumed independence of φ and z. In each material region, the zero curl of the static electric field gives
dρdEz​​=0,dρd(ρEφ​)​=0.
(32)
The tangential electric field is zero at a perfect conductor. The dielectric-interface boundary conditions make its tangential components continuous at each material interface, so Ez​=Eφ​=0 throughout the gap. They also vanish in the empty interior and exterior for the regular solution with no applied external electric field. Hence
E=Eρ​(ρ)eρ​.​
(33)
This specifies an axis of direction: the sign of Eρ​ depends on the sign of V.
For the static magnetic field, Gauss's law for magnetism implies
ρ1​dρd(ρBρ​)​=0.
(34)
Thus ρBρ​ is constant in each layer. Continuity of the normal magnetic field across every interface makes that constant global, and regularity at the axis makes it zero. Next use ∇×H=0 away from the conducting surfaces. Its φ component gives dHz​/dρ=0. There is no azimuthal surface current density, so Hz​ is continuous across the conducting surfaces and the material interface. The decay of the exterior magnetic field at infinity therefore forces this global constant to zero. The only remaining component is
B=Bφ​(ρ)eφ​.​
(35)
The magnetostatic Ampère law fixes its sign: positive inner electric current in the +z direction produces positive Bφ​ for positive magnetic permeability.
