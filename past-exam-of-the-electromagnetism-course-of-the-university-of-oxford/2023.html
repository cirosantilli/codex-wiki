Put k=b/a>1 and ez​=(0,0,1). The complete construction of image charges between concentric grounded spheres consists of two families, indexed by every integer:
qn​q​n​​=qkn,=−r0​qa​kn,​rn​rn​​=r0​k2nez​,=r0​a2​k2nez​,n∈Z.​​
(11)
The term (q0​,r0​) is the physical point charge; every other term is an image charge. In the first family n≤−1 lies inside r=a and n≥1 lies outside r=b. In the second family n≤0 lies inside r=a and n≥1 lies outside r=b. Thus no image charge lies in the physical gap.
To derive and verify these families, reflection of an axial point charge (Q,z) in a grounded sphere of radius R replaces it by the image charge (−RQ/z,R2/z). Reflection in a sends (qn​,rn​) to (q​−n​,r−n​); reflection in b sends it to (q​1−n​,r1−n​). Successive reflections therefore generate exactly the listed families. The method of images gives
ϕ(r)=4πϵ0​1​n∈Z∑​(∣r−r0​k2nez​∣qkn​−∣r−(a2/r0​)k2nez​∣(qa/r0​)kn​),a<r<b.​
(12)
The physical point charge itself is excluded when evaluating this formula. On r=a, pair the first-family term n with the second-family term −n; their electric potentials cancel by the single-sphere distance identity. On r=b, pair n with 1−n instead. These reindexings are justified by absolute convergence, as follows.
For n→−∞, both source positions tend to the origin while their electric charges have size O(kn). On the closed gap their distances are eventually at least a/2, so both electric potential terms are O(kn). For n→+∞, both distances are at least a positive constant times k2n and the electric charges are O(kn), giving O(k−n) terms. Both tails are bounded by convergent geometric series. This proves absolute convergence and uniform convergence of the tails on the closed gap. On any compact set avoiding the physical point charge, the differentiated tails obey the same type of bounds: for example, the large positive tail of the electric field is O(k−3n). Thus termwise differentiation is valid and the sum is a harmonic function except at the physical point charge, where it has the correct Coulomb's law singularity. The boundary cancellations and uniqueness of Poisson equation complete the construction.

Qa​=−q(a+h)da(d−h)​⟶−dq(d−h)​.

Use the constitutive relations D=ϵE and H=B/μ, where D is the electric displacement field, H the magnetic field strength, ϵ the permittivity, and μ the magnetic permeability. Let ρf​ be the charge density of free charge and Jf​ its current density. In this homogeneous medium, Gauss's law and the magnetostatic Ampère law are, respectively,
∇⋅D=ρf​,∮∂W​D⋅ndS=Qf​(W)=∫W​ρf​d3r,​
(24)
∇×H=Jf​,∮∂S​H⋅dℓ=If​(S)=∫S​Jf​⋅ndS.​
(25)
Here W is a volume with closed boundary ∂W, and S is an oriented spanning surface whose boundary orientation follows the right-hand rule. Thus the electric field form is ∇⋅E=ρf​/ϵ and ∮∂W​E⋅ndS=Qf​/ϵ; the magnetic field form is ∇×B=μJf​ and ∮∂S​B⋅dℓ=μIf​.
The remaining static Maxwell equations are
∇⋅B=0,∮∂W​B⋅ndS=0,∇×E=0,∮∂S​E⋅dℓ=0.
(26)
These express Gauss's law for magnetism and the static limit of Faraday's law. The volume and circulation forms follow from the divergence theorem and Stokes theorem. The electric charge and electric current on the right of the macroscopic laws are free sources; material electric polarization and magnetization are already included in D and H.

With charge density ρ, current density J, and speed of light c=(ϵ0​μ0​)−1/2, the four Maxwell equations in vacuum are
∇⋅E∇×E​=ρ/ϵ0​,=−∂t​B,​∇⋅B∇×B​=0,=μ0​J+c−2∂t​E.​​
(49)
The electric potential and magnetic vector potential determine the electric field and magnetic field by
E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A,B=∇×A.​
(50)
These definitions satisfy Gauss's law for magnetism and Faraday's law identically. The Lorenz gauge condition is
∇⋅A+c−2∂t​ϕ=0.​
(51)
Substituting into Gauss's law and using the Lorenz gauge condition gives
ϵ0​ρ​=−∇2ϕ−∂t​(∇⋅A)=−∇2ϕ+c−2∂t2​ϕ.
(52)
For the Ampère-Maxwell equation, the double-curl identity gives
∇(∇⋅A)−∇2A=μ0​J−c−2∇∂t​ϕ−c−2∂t2​A.
(53)
The two gradient terms cancel by the Lorenz gauge condition. Defining the d'Alembert operator with the sign convention □=∇2−c−2∂t2​, the remaining equations are
□ϕ=−ρ/ϵ0​,□A=−μ0​J.​
(54)
Conversely, if the electric potential and magnetic vector potential satisfy these wave equations and the Lorenz gauge condition, the same substitutions in reverse give Gauss's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation. The other two Maxwell equations follow identically from the definitions, proving equivalence within the Lorenz gauge.
