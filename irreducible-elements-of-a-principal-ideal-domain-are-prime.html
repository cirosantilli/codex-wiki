Irreducible elements of a principal ideal domain are prime

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Integral domain Principal ideal domain
OurBigBook.com Words: 77
Let π be an irreducible element of a principal ideal domain R, and suppose π∤a. The ideal (π,a)=(d) has generator d dividing π. Irreducibility makes d a unit or an associate of π; the latter would imply π∣a. Hence (π,a)=R, giving uπ+va=1 for some u,v∈R. If π∣ab, multiply this identity by b: both uπb and vab are divisible by π, so π∣b. This is precisely the prime element property.
