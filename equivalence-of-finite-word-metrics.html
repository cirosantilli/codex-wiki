Equivalence of finite word metrics

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Geometric group theory Cayley graph Word metric
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
Changing a finite generating set of a group changes individual distances, but it cannot introduce arbitrarily large relative distortion. For two finite symmetric generating sets of a group S,T of the same group, set
K=max({1}∪{∣s∣T​:s∈S}),L=max({1}∪{∣t∣S​:t∈T}).
(29)
Each S-letter can be replaced by at most K T-letters, and conversely each T-letter by at most L S-letters. Applying these substitutions to shortest representatives of g−1h gives
L1​dS​(g,h)≤dT​(g,h)≤KdS​(g,h).
(30)
Thus the identity map is a bilipschitz equivalence between the two word metrics, and hence a quasi-isometry. The constants depend on the chosen generating sets of a group.[ref]
In the Cayley graphs of the integers example, S={−1,1} and T={−2,−1,1,2} give K=1 and L=2: a unit move costs one in either alphabet, and a move of size two costs two unit moves. This recovers dT​≤dS​≤2dT​. The diamond and square in word metric balls in the integer lattice obey the same comparison, because max{∣m∣,∣n∣}≤∣m∣+∣n∣≤2max{∣m∣,∣n∣}.
Finiteness cannot simply be dropped from this universal statement. For the additive integers, take the infinite symmetric generating set of a group U=Z∖{0}. Every nonzero displacement is a single allowed move, so dU​(a,b)=1 whenever a=b, whereas dS​(0,n)=∣n∣ for S={−1,1}. There is no finite L with dS​≤LdU​: choosing an integer n>L violates it. This is a counterexample to unrestricted equivalence, not a claim that every infinite generating set of a group fails; two such sets with uniformly bounded generator lengths can still satisfy the same substitution bounds.[ref]
