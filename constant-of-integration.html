Constant of integration (∫f(x)dx=F(x)+C)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Integral Antiderivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 341 Articles: 1
On a connected open interval, if F′=g, every antiderivative of g has the form F+C, where C is a constant function. Indeed, two solutions satisfy (G−F)′=0: the zero derivative on a connected open set principle makes their difference constant. In one dimension, the mean value theorem proves this on any closed subinterval. [ref]
An initial condition or boundary condition can determine C. If a particular antiderivative is F0​ and F(x0​)=y0​, then
C=y0​−F0​(x0​).​
(25)
For g(x)=x2, F0​(x)=x3/3 and F(1)=2, this gives C=5/3. Without that reference value, choosing C=0 simply selects one member of the family.
The freedom has a physical meaning in a simple kinematic model. A velocity v(t)=at, with constant acceleration a, determines position only as x(t)=at2/2+C. The initial condition x(0)=x0​ selects C=x0​; changing the starting position does not change the prescribed velocity.
For the differential equation T′=−B/r2 on r>0, the antiderivative is T=B/r+C. The boundary condition T(Rs​)=Ts​ gives C=Ts​−B/Rs​. Discarding the constant of integration therefore needs a specified condition or an approximation that quantifies its effect. On a domain with several connected components, the freedom is larger: antiderivatives on disconnected domains require a separate constant on each component.
