Thermodynamic phase

Words: 57
Thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
A thermodynamic phase is a form of macroscopic equilibrium characterized by its structure, symmetries and thermodynamic response. Away from phase transitions, its free-energy density varies smoothly with the control parameters. Different phases can share a phase coexistence boundary. An ordered magnetic phase is distinguished from a disordered phase by its spontaneous order parameter; positive and negative ordered states are different pure thermodynamic phases related by spin inversion symmetry.

Laws of thermodynamics

Words: 87 Articles: 1
Thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
The laws organize the equilibrium and process constraints of thermodynamics: the zeroth law of thermodynamics makes thermal equilibrium transitive, the first law of thermodynamics expresses energy balance, the Second law of thermodynamics restricts entropy changes, and the Third law of thermodynamics describes the zero-temperature limit. Their analogy with horizon mechanics becomes quantitative through Hawking temperature and Bekenstein-Hawking entropy.

Zeroth law of thermodynamics

Words: 48
Laws of thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. Equilibrium classes can therefore be labelled by a common thermodynamic temperature. The corresponding Zeroth law of black-hole mechanics states constancy of surface gravity over an equilibrium horizon; Hawking radiation identifies that quantity with physical temperature up to its universal conversion factor.

Compressibility (κX​=−V−1(∂V/∂p)X​)

Thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
Compressibility measures fractional volume reduction per increase in pressure, with another thermodynamic variable held fixed. Holding temperature fixed gives isothermal compressibility; holding entropy fixed gives adiabatic compressibility for a reversible process. Its reciprocal is the bulk modulus. The constraint matters: these responses need not agree.

Maxwell relations

Words: 45
Thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
A Maxwell relation equates mixed derivatives of a thermodynamic potential. For dF=−SdT−PdV, equality of mixed derivatives gives (∂S/∂V)T​=(∂P/∂T)V​. For dG=−SdT+VdP, it gives (∂S/∂P)T​=−(∂V/∂T)P​. These identities connect experimentally accessible response functions to heat capacities and compressibility.

Diffusion (j=−D∇C)

Words: 151 Articles: 2
Thermodynamics OurBigBook.com
Diffusion is the spreading of heat or material by microscopic random motion. A concentration gradient produces flux down that gradient; with uniform diffusion coefficient D, conservation gives the diffusion equation Ct​=D∇2C. In a moving carrier, advection must also be included. Physical diffusion is a transport mechanism, distinct from the general probabilistic class of Markov diffusion.

Molecular diffusion

Words: 57
Diffusion OurBigBook.com
Molecular diffusion transports material through random molecular motion rather than coherent fluid advection. Its continuum limit gives a concentration flux −κ∇χ, with molecular diffusivity κ, and a diffusion equation when no bulk motion is present. Molecular diffusion acts on the small scalar gradients produced by stretching, even when the macroscopic effective transport is much faster than molecular transport.

Rotational diffusion (Dr​)

Rotational diffusion randomizes orientation. Its coefficient has units inverse time. It differs from the translational diffusion coefficient, whose units are length squared per time.

PB​PF​​=exp[−(F2​−F1​)/(kB​T)].
(1)
This is the path version of detailed balance. A time-even order parameter is read backward without changing sign, while a transport flux changes sign. The Onsager--Machlup path probability implements this reversal in a coarse-grained stochastic description.

Inverse temperature (β=1/(kB​T))

Temperature OurBigBook.com
Inverse temperature is β=1/(kB​T), where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and T is the absolute temperature. A canonical ensemble weights an energy E by its Boltzmann factor e−βE. Calling couplings dimensionless means absorbing this factor β into their energy coefficients. Boltzmann suppression (e−m/T)

Boltzmann suppression is the exponential reduction of an equilibrium population when creating it costs energy much greater than the temperature. For a nonrelativistic species with negligible chemical potential, the leading factor is e−m/T, multiplied by its momentum phase-space volume. It concerns the equilibrium abundance; freeze-out can preserve a larger population than the instantaneous equilibrium value at later times.

Molecular formation, photodissociation, optical depth and density-dependent level populations control the rate. This allows some small systems below the atomic virial temperature threshold to form stars.

A universal density-independent function of temperature alone is not generally sufficient for irradiated gas.

Two-phase scalar coexistence endpoint conditions (V′=V′′=V(3)=0,V(4)>0)
Words: 107
First-order phase transition OurBigBook.com
A smooth scalar Landau free energy can end a two-phase phase coexistence curve by merging two minima and their intervening maximum. At a generic stable endpoint, its first three derivatives vanish and its fourth derivative is positive. A field shift and two mixed control parameters reduce the local form to a quartic Landau free energy; the order-parameter jump then vanishes and the magnetic susceptibility diverges. This conclusion assumes there is no competing third phase and that the endpoint lies inside the control-parameter domain. It is not a general theorem about every first-order line: a sextic potential with fixed negative quartic coefficient has a symmetry-related ordered coexistence line that ends at a triple point with a finite jump.
