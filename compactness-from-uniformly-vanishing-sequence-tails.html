Compactness from uniformly vanishing sequence tails

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Sequentially compact space
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
Let am​≥0 and am​→0. The set K={x=(xm​):xm​∈R, ∣xm​∣≤am​} is a compact metric space for the supremum norm metric. Every member is a sequence tending to zero.
For any sequence in K, successively extract a convergent subsequence in each compact interval [−am​,am​], then take diagonal indices. The coordinate limits define x∈K. For any M, coordinatewise differences in the tail are bounded by 2supm>M​am​, independently of the sequence index. This tends to zero as M→∞, while finitely many initial coordinates converge together. Hence the diagonal subsequence converges in the supremum norm. This proves sequential compactness, equivalent to compactness for metric spaces.
