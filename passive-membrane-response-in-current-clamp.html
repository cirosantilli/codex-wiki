Passive membrane response in current clamp (τm​=Rm​Cm​)

 Home Biology Neuroscience Patch clamp Current clamp
OurBigBook.com Words: 177
Model a spatially uniform cell membrane as a capacitor of membrane capacitance Cm​ in parallel with membrane electrical resistance Rm​ and a fixed leak reversal potential EL​. Exclude active ion channels, seal leak current and pipette capacitance. For electric current I injected into the biological cell, electric charge conservation yields
Cm​dtdV​=I−Rm​V−EL​​.
(11)
Starting at V(0)=EL​ and applying constant I gives the ordinary differential equation solution
V(t)=EL​+IRm​(1−e−t/τm​),τm​=Rm​Cm​.
(12)
The steady state rise is IRm​; the time constant is Rm​Cm​. With I=50pA, Rm​=200MΩ, Cm​=50pF and EL​=−70mV, the rise is 10mV and τm​=10ms. At one time constant the rise is 10(1−e−1)mV≈6.321mV. The figure's uncompensated pipette voltage adds IRs​=1mV for Rs​=20MΩ. The membrane voltage is continuous at step onset in this model, while the pipette voltage immediately acquires that access drop.
Checking the units is instructive: electrical resistance times capacitance has units ΩF=s, and electric current times electrical resistance has units of voltage. Unlike the passive voltage clamp transient under patch-clamp series resistance, this ideal current clamp membrane time constant is set by Rm​Cm​, not Rs​Cm​. Strong current steps can recruit voltage-gated ion channels; the passive model then fails and cannot generate an action potential. Small pulses of both signs, stable access and repeated baseline measurements help test whether the passive approximation is appropriate.
