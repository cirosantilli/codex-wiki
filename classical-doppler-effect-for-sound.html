Classical Doppler effect for sound (fo​=fs​(c−uo​)/(c−us​))

 Home Physics Wave Doppler effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 381
Work in the material's rest reference frame, with constant speed of sound c>0. For one-dimensional propagation, choose the positive x direction along the travelling wave from its emission point toward the receiver. Define us​=dxs​/dt and uo​=dxo​/dt as the signed source and receiver velocities along that same axis. Both use the same sign convention: positive us​ moves the source toward the receiver ahead, whereas positive uo​ moves that receiver away from the incoming wave. Assume ∣us​∣<c and ∣uo​∣<c, constant velocities, and no source-receiver crossing during the interval considered. Then
fo​=fs​c−us​c−uo​​.
(22)
To derive this, let Ts​=1/fs​ be the emission wave period. During Ts​, one wave crest travels cTs​ while the source advances us​Ts​. Successive wavefronts along the chosen direction therefore have wavelength
λ=(c−us​)Ts​=fs​c−us​​.
(23)
They overtake the receiver at relative speed c−uo​, so counting wave crests gives fo​=(c−uo​)/λ. This separates the source's effect on wavelength from the receiver's effect on crossing rate. Even with a moving source, a stationary receiver obeys fo​λ=c because the wave speed through the material remains c.
For a stationary receiver and a source approaching at speed v, set uo​=0 and us​=v: fo​=fs​c/(c−v)>fs​. For recession choose the positive axis along the departing wave toward the receiver again, so the source has us​=−v: fo​=fs​c/(c+v)<fs​. For the deliberately chosen values c=343ms−1, v=20ms−1 and fs​=500Hz, the two predictions are
fapproach​=500323343​Hz≃530.96Hz,frecede​=500363343​Hz≃472.45Hz.
(24)
The corresponding wavelengths are 0.646m and 0.726m, compared with c/fs​=0.686m for a stationary source. These are calculations for chosen parameters, not reported measurements.
A receiver approaching a stationary source at 20ms−1 instead has us​=0, uo​=−20ms−1, giving fo​=500(363/343)Hz≃529.15Hz. Moving away gives fo​=500(323/343)Hz≃470.85Hz. The equal closing speeds do not make these equal to the moving-source values. If both source and receiver move together with us​=uo​, however, the numerator and denominator cancel and fo​=fs​. For speeds small compared with c, the first-order shift is (fo​−fs​)/fs​≃(us​−uo​)/c; the difference between moving-source and moving-receiver experiments appears beyond this first order.
