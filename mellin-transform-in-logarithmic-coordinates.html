The factor dx=eudu changes xs−1 to esu; missing this factor would shift every exponent by one. With hσ​(u)=eσuf(eu) and the Fourier transform convention h(ω)=∫R​h(u)e−iωudu, the identity is

Choosing σ inside the Mellin fundamental strip makes hσ​ a Lebesgue integrable function. The minus sign in the Fourier transform argument follows from the stated convention. For the Mellin transform of an interval indicator with [b,c]=[1,4] and σ=0, h0​=1[0,log4]​: the Fourier transform of an interval indicator is literally the same computation in a different coordinate.
Splitting the real line at u=0 also connects the Mellin transform to two unilateral Laplace transforms. If k+​(v)=f(ev) and k−​(v)=f(e−v) for v≥0, then
Mf(s)=Lk+​(−s)+Lk−​(s)
(14)
where both integrals converge absolutely. Their Laplace convergence regions impose opposite endpoint conditions, explaining the two sides of the Mellin fundamental strip. Finally, f(aeu)=f(eu+loga) shows that a change of scale is a translation in u. Weighted Fourier transforms of this translation give exactly the factor a−s in the scaling property of the Mellin transform. For a smooth function with compact support inside (0,∞), the Mellin inversion theorem follows from Fourier inversion; the existing formula states the corresponding hypotheses.
