Semi-random method

Words: 157 Articles: 1
Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
The semi-random method constructs a combinatorial object in many small random rounds, discarding incompatible proposals and updating the remaining constraints after each round. Independent proposals give calculable expected changes; concentration and deterministic corrections preserve the statistics needed for subsequent rounds. Unlike a single random sample, this combines repeated randomness with a structured evolving state. The Rödl nibble is a matching in a hypergraph version of this method.

Rödl nibble

Words: 96
Semi-random method OurBigBook.com
For a nearly D-regular s-uniform hypergraph, sample each edge independently with probability α/D, where α is small. Retain the isolated sampled edges as a matching in a hypergraph, and delete every vertex incident to any sampled edge before the next round. A vertex survives with probability (1−α/D)d(v)≈e−α; a sampled edge meets another sampled edge with probability at most sα(1+o(1)). Thus accepted edges cover order α of the vertices and collision waste is order α2. Small hypergraph codegrees control the overlap terms in residual-degree calculations. Repeating a bounded number of rounds while tracking near-regularity is the characteristic Rödl nibble mechanism.

Probabilistic method

Words: 170 Articles: 2
Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
The probabilistic method proves that a combinatorial object exists by defining a random object and showing that the desired property has positive probability. No efficient procedure for finding the object is required.

Triangle deletion in the alteration method

Words: 82
Probabilistic method OurBigBook.com
If a graph has E edges and T triangles, successively delete one edge from a remaining triangle. No new triangle is created and at most T deletions are needed, so a triangle-free subgraph has at least E−T edges. A random-graph expectation lower bound on E−T therefore proves existence of a large triangle-free graph. With edge probability n−1/2, the expectation is (n2−1)/(3n​); this is a useful existence bound but is not within a constant factor of the sharp quadratic Mantel theorem bound.

Dense integer sets with only logarithmic-length progressions

Words: 56
Probabilistic method OurBigBook.com
For any fixed density strictly below one, random subsets of an integer interval can have that density yet avoid arithmetic progressions longer than a constant times the logarithm of the interval length. A union bound over possible progressions bounds their occurrence, while concentration of the random cardinality supplies a dense realization. Passing to a subset gives an exact prescribed cardinality.

Subcritical component bound for a binomial random graph

Words: 166 Articles: 3
Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
For every fixed ε>0, every component of G(n,(1−ε)/n) has Oε​(logn) vertices with high probability. A breadth-first exploration is dominated by a branching process of mean 1−ε; its total progeny has an exponentially decreasing tail.

Sharp subcritical largest-component scale (L1​∼logn/(λ−1−logλ),0<λ<1)

Words: 57
Subcritical component bound for a binomial random graph OurBigBook.com
For fixed 0<λ<1 in G(n,λ/n), the largest component of a graph has the displayed order with high probability. A dominating Galton-Watson process gives the tail bound P(T≥j)≤e−(λ−1−logλ)(j−1) by the exponential Markov inequality. For the lower bound, the tree-component expectation in the Erdős-Rényi model diverges at j=⌊(1−η)logn/(λ−1−logλ)⌋. The disjoint-set covariance factor and the exclusion of overlaps give relative variance tending to zero, so the second moment method supplies a tree component of that order.

Hamiltonicity-to-pancyclicity sprinkling principle

Words: 49
Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
For a decreasing probability sequence p(n), if G(n,p(n)) contains a Hamilton cycle with high probability, then G(n,Cp(n)) is pancyclic with high probability for every fixed sufficiently large C; three independent rounds suffice. One first exposes a Hamiltonian round and uses the independent sprinkled edges to create cycles of every shorter length.

Lovász local lemma

Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
For bad events with a dependency graph of maximum degree D, the symmetric Lovász local lemma guarantees positive probability that none occurs whenever each event has probability at most p and ep(D+1)≤1.

Local lemma Ramsey lower bound (R(k)≥(2​/e+o(1))k2k/2)

Words: 69
Lovász local lemma OurBigBook.com
In an independent random two-colouring of KN​, a given k-vertex set is monochromatic with probability 21−(2k​). Events on sets intersecting in at most one vertex use disjoint edges. Thus dependency degree is at most (2k​)(k−2N−2​). The Lovász local lemma gives a colouring avoiding all monochromatic Kk​ when e21−(2k​)(1+(2k​)(k−2N−2​))≤1. For N=ck2k/2 the expression is O(k3/2(ec/2​)k), by Stirling's approximation. Taking c tending sufficiently slowly to 2​/e proves R(k)≥(2​/e+o(1))k2k/2.

Dependency-degree obstruction on a rooted tree (pd​=dd/(d+1)d+1)

Words: 105
Lovász local lemma OurBigBook.com
Let Bv​ be independent Bernoulli random variables on a rooted d-ary tree, and put Av​={Bv​=1, Bw​=0 for every child w}. Events at nonadjacent vertices use disjoint variables, giving a dependency graph of events of maximum degree d+1. Assign leaf success probability p and successive probabilities rj+1​=p/(1−rj​)d. If p>max0≤r≤1​r(1−r)d=pd​, the recursion eventually reaches one. Set the final root success probability to one. Each event has probability at most p, but following success vertices down from the root must reach an occurring event. Thus the bad events cover the sample space. This gives an upper bound for uniform avoidance thresholds without assuming a converse to the Lovász local lemma.

Compactness extension of the Lovász local lemma

Words: 62
Lovász local lemma OurBigBook.com
For countably many constraints on finite-valued variables, each involving finitely many variables, apply the finite Lovász local lemma to every finite constraint subfamily. Partial satisfying assignments on increasing finite variable sets form a finitely branching tree with nonempty levels. The König infinity lemma gives an infinite branch, producing one assignment satisfying all constraints. This existence argument does not assert positive probability of simultaneous avoidance in an infinite product space.

Dependency graph of events

Words: 102 Articles: 1
Lovász local lemma OurBigBook.com
A dependency graph of events joins potentially dependent events. Each event must be independent of the sigma-algebra generated by all its nonneighbors, a joint condition stronger than pairwise independence. In a product probability space, joining events whose sets of underlying independent variables overlap gives such a graph. This supplies the hypothesis of the Lovász local lemma.

Independence digraph of events

Words: 53
Dependency graph of events OurBigBook.com
For events Ai​, a directed graph is an independence digraph if Ai​ is independent of the sigma-algebra generated by the events indexed by its non-outneighbors, excluding i. The direction records which joint independence condition is available. A cyclically oriented triangle imposes pairwise independence; it does not impose independence of all three events together.

Dependent random choice

Words: 321 Articles: 6
Probabilistic combinatorics OurBigBook.com
Dependent random choice finds a large vertex set whose small subsets have large common neighbourhoods. In a bipartite graph with parts A,B, choose random vertices of B with repetition and take their common neighbourhood X⊆A; convexity gives a lower bound for E∣X∣, while counting subsets of X with small common neighbourhood allows their deletion.

Dense bipartite common-neighbour lemma

Words: 69
Dependent random choice OurBigBook.com
In a bipartite graph with parts of sizes m,n and density δ>0, a neighbourhood V′ of size at least δm/2 can be chosen so that at least a proportion 1−2ϵ/δ2 of its ordered pairs have at least ϵn common neighbours. Choose a random vertex on the other side, use the second moment of its degree, and penalize pairs with few common neighbours. This is a simple dependent random choice tool for controlling restricted sumsets.

Irregular pair of vertex sets

Words: 97
Regular pair of vertex sets OurBigBook.com
For disjoint nonempty vertex sets U,V of a graph, an ε-irregular pair is one that is not an ε-regular pair of vertex sets. Equivalently there are witnesses U′⊆U, V′⊆V with ∣U′∣≥ε∣U∣, ∣V′∣≥ε∣V∣ and ∣d(U′,V′)−d(U,V)∣>ε. Splitting both cells by these witnesses increases their contribution to the regularity energy by at least ε4∣U∣∣V∣/n2: the witness rectangle has relative weight at least ε2, its mean discrepancy has magnitude greater than ε, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds the weighted sum of squared discrepancies below by the rectangle weight times its squared mean. This is the local increment in the energy-increment proof of Szemerédi regularity.

Regular clique counting lemma (#Kr​≥δnr)

Words: 129 Articles: 1
Regular pair of vertex sets OurBigBook.com
For fixed r and positive density threshold λ, sufficiently regular pairwise dense bipartite graphs between r disjoint equal-sized vertex classes contain at least δnr transversal cliques, for some δ>0. A greedy embedding maintains common neighbourhoods and discards the small set of vertices with atypical degrees into them. The constants depend only on r,λ.
Regular triangle counting lemma (#K3​≥(1−2ε)(d−ε)3L3)
Words: 72
Regular clique counting lemma OurBigBook.com
If three disjoint vertex classes have size L, and their three pairs are ε-regular pairs of vertex sets of edge density of a bipartite graph at least d, where 0<ε≤d/2 and ε<1/2, they contain at least the displayed number of transversal triangles in a graph. All but 2εL vertices in the first class have at least (d−ε)L neighbours in both other classes. Regularity between those two neighbour sets supplies the third edge. This strengthens a mere triangle embedding lemma for regular pairs to a cubic lower count.

Bipartite Szemerédi regularity lemma
Words: 216
Regular pair of bipartite partitions OurBigBook.com
For every ε>0 there is a positive integer K(ε) such that every finite bipartite graph has an ε-regular pair of partitions with at most K(ε) cells on each side.
To prove this, give X×Y the uniform probability measure, let f be the indicator function of the edge set, and for partitions P,Q define their energy by
E(P,Q)=∥E(f∣P⊗Q)∥22​=∑i,j​∣X∣∣Y∣∣Xi​∣∣Yj​∣​d(Xi​,Yj​)2.
(10)
This conditional expectation is an orthogonal projection, so 0≤E≤∥f∥22​≤1. If the partitions are not ε-regular, choose witnesses Aij​⊆Xi​ and Bij​⊆Yj​ in every irregular cell pair and refine each Xi​ by all its Aij​ and each Yj​ by all its Bij​. The refined energy exceeds the old energy by
∥E(f∣P′⊗Q′)−E(f∣P⊗Q)∥22​.
(11)
On Aij​×Bij​ the absolute value function of the mean of the difference is greater than ε, while this rectangle occupies at least an ε2 fraction of Xi​×Yj​. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality therefore contributes more than ε4∣Xi​∣∣Yj​∣/(∣X∣∣Y∣) from that cell pair. Irregular pairs have total weight greater than ε, so every refinement raises the energy by more than ε5. The process stops after at most ⌈ε−5⌉ refinements. If the current cell counts are r,s, the construction gives at most r2s,s2r new cells, so a finite iterated bound depending only on ε supplies K(ε).

Szemerédi regularity lemma OurBigBook.com
For every ε>0, all sufficiently large finite graphs admit a set partition into at most M(ε) nonempty cells, each of size at most εn, with the displayed ordered irregular-pair weight bound. Start with small cells. Use regularity energy including diagonal rectangles, refine simultaneously by the witness subsets of all irregular pairs, and apply the refinement variance identity for regularity energy. Each failing round raises energy by more than ε5, while energy lies in [0,1]. The recurrence k↦k2k bounds the final cell count. Equitability is unnecessary for this weighted version.

Energy-increment proof of Szemerédi regularity (Δq>ε5/4,#refinements<⌈4ε−5⌉)
Words: 137
Szemerédi regularity lemma OurBigBook.com
Use equitable regularity energy with exceptional vertices retained as singleton cells. The refinement variance identity for regularity energy shows that splitting along an irregular pair's witnesses raises energy by more than ε4∣Vi​∣∣Vj​∣/n2. More than εk2 irregular ordered pairs therefore raise energy by more than ε5/4 when the exceptional set occupies at most half the vertices.
For equalization with a controlled exceptional set, cut each of the at most k2k witness atoms into pieces of size ⌊L/4k⌋ and put leftovers into singleton exceptional cells. This is a refinement, so it cannot decrease energy. At most n2−k vertices are lost. Start with enough cells that this loss, over ⌈4ε−5⌉ rounds, is at most εn/2; the initial exceptional set uses the other half. The number of cells grows by a bounded recurrence depending only on ε and the prescribed minimum. Energy lies in [0,1], so the process terminates.

Equitable regularity energy (q(P))
Words: 271 Articles: 2
Szemerédi regularity lemma OurBigBook.com
For a set partition P of the vertices of a graph of order n, define
q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∣A∣∣B∣d(A,B)2,
(13)
where the sum is ordered, the adjacency indicator defines the density also on diagonal pairs, and exceptional vertices are treated as singleton parts. Refinement cannot decrease this energy, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. An irregular pair of equal cells of size m, with witnesses of relative size at least ε and density discrepancy greater than ε, increases the energy by more than ε4m2/n2 for each orientation. Equalization with a controlled exceptional set
Words: 100

In a proof of the Szemerédi regularity lemma, suppose k equal classes have size L and witness splitting creates at most 2k atoms per class. Divide every atom into blocks of size ℓ=⌊L/4k⌋, putting leftovers into the exceptional set. At most k2kℓ≤kL2−k vertices are lost. When L≥2⋅4k, at most 2k4k blocks remain. For equitable regularity energy defined by omitting exceptional vertices, the loss from discarding a fraction r is at most 2r: only ordered pairs incident with discarded vertices are removed, and every squared edge density of a bipartite graph is at most one. This provides a quantitative way to restore equal class sizes after each energy increment.
