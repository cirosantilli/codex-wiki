Boltzmann distribution in a four-state system (Z=(1+q)2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Boltzmann distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 176
Consider two distinguishable, noninteracting two-level systems, each with energies 0 and Δ>0. Their four microstates (0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1) have total energies 0,Δ,Δ,2Δ. Thus the three energy macrostates have state degeneracies 1,2,1. This is a stipulated finite model, not a measured material.
Set q=e−βΔ. The four individual Boltzmann factors are 1,q,q,q2, so the partition function and aggregate probabilities are
Z=1+2q+q2=(1+q)2,(P(0),P(Δ),P(2Δ))=(1+q)2(1,2q,q2)​.
(71)
Each of the two middle microstates has probability q/Z, while their shared energy macrostate has probability 2q/Z.
At the temperature satisfying Δ=kB​Tln2, the Boltzmann factor is q=1/2 and the partition function is Z=9/4. Therefore
(p00​,p01​,p10​,p11​)=(94​,92​,92​,91​),(P(0),P(Δ),P(2Δ))=(94​,94​,91​).
(72)
The middle energy is as likely as the ground state energy because its state degeneracy is twice as large. Yet each individual middle microstate is only half as likely as the ground state microstate. The diagram places the four energies beside their individual probabilities, then shows the sums within each macrostate.
The expected value of energy follows directly by weighting the three possible energies:
⟨E⟩=0P(0)+ΔP(Δ)+2ΔP(2Δ)=(1+q)22Δq+2Δq2​=1+q2Δq​.
(73)
At q=1/2 this is 2Δ/3. An individual energy measurement still returns 0, Δ or 2Δ; the expected value describes the ensemble average, rather than a fourth possible measured energy.
