Logarithmic lubrication resistance of a sphere near a wall

 ... Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Lubrication theory Couette-Poiseuille flow in a thin gap Moving-boundary lubrication flux Reynolds equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 180 Articles: 1
A sphere of radius a at gap aϵ translates at Uex​ and rotates at ωey​ above a stationary wall in a right-handed coordinate system. At leading order its gap is h=aϵ+r2/(2a). In its translating frame the lower and upper tangential velocities are −Uex​ and −aωex​. The Reynolds lubrication equation gives
p=56μ​(U+aω)h2x​.
(50)
Integrating the upper-boundary traction, including pressure on the sloping surface, and its moment about the sphere centre gives the sphere-on-fluid force and torque
(Fx​Gy​/a​)∼54πμa​log(1/ϵ)(4−1​−14​)(Uaω​).
(51)
The off-diagonal symmetry is required by the Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow. A torque-free sphere therefore has aω=U/4 and leading drag 3πμaUlog(1/ϵ). Compare the rigid-wall terms of Bertin et al., equations (4.4)–(4.5); those forces act on the sphere and have the opposite sign.
