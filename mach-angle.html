Mach angle (μ=arcsin(M−1))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Compressible flow Normal shock wave Oblique shock
OurBigBook.com Words: 455 Articles: 1
The Mach angle of a supersonic flow with Mach number M>1 is μ=arcsin(M−1). Infinitesimal pressure disturbances lie on the corresponding Mach cone, and a weak oblique shock approaches this angle as its strength tends to zero.
The Mach angle is the half-angle between a Mach cone generator and its backward motion axis for a steady point source in a uniform three-dimensional medium with isotropy and constant sound speed. It obeys
sinμ=Uc​=M1​,0<μ<2π​,M>1.
(29)
The relation concerns weak acoustic waves in linear acoustics. It is not the angle of a finite oblique shock produced by finite turning of the fluid flow; that angle also depends on the deflection and the gas properties.[ref]
For a short proof, choose an old emission in the moving-point acoustic wavefronts construction and set s=−τ>0. Its circle has center (−Us,0) and radius cs. A tangent line from the present source at the origin meets the circle at a right angle to its radius. The resulting right triangle has hypotenuse Us and opposite side cs, giving the displayed sine ratio. The emission age cancels: every wavefront has the same tangent directions, so a continuum of expanding spheres produces one simple Mach cone.
In an original example, stipulate uniform gas with sound speed c=340ms−1 and a source moving through it at U=680ms−1. Then M=2 and μ=arcsin(1/2)=30∘. An emission one second earlier is now a sphere centered 680m behind the source with radius 340m. The selected sound speed defines this example; it is not a constant for air at every temperature. The Mach angle decreases as M increases: its derivative is −1/(MM2−1​) in radians, and its limits are 90∘ as M↓1 and 0 as M→∞.
