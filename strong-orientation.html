Strong orientation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Orientation of a graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 355 Articles: 1
A strong orientation is an orientation of a graph with strong connectivity: for every ordered pair (u,v) of vertices, there is a directed path from u to v. A length-zero directed path permits u=v. Ordinary connectivity of the underlying undirected graph is necessary, but does not ensure this two-way reachability after directions are chosen. The acyclic D1​ is not a strong orientation; the cyclic D2​ is, since a journey can continue around its directed cycle.
Attach a new vertex D to that triangle in a graph by the single edge CD. This edge is a bridge in a graph. If oriented C→D, no directed path leaves D for the triangle in a graph; if oriented D→C, no directed path from the triangle in a graph reaches D. Neither choice gives a strong orientation, even when the triangle in a graph itself is cyclically oriented. By contrast, the four-vertex cycle graph has the strong orientation
A→B→C→D→A.
(11)
Following this directed cycle reaches any requested vertex in at most three steps. Removing any one of its undirected edges leaves a graph path through all four vertices, so it has no bridge in a graph.
For more than one vertex, a strong orientation cannot also be an acyclic orientation: directed paths from u to v and back give a nonempty closed directed walk, from which removing repeated portions leaves a directed cycle. A directed graph containing one directed cycle need not have strong connectivity, as the attached D shows.
