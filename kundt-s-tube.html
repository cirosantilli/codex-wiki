Kundt's tube

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Linear acoustics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
A Kundt's tube turns an invisible standing wave into a measurable spatial pattern. A driven air column supports a longitudinal wave; measuring its frequency and the spacing of repeated nodes of a standing wave gives the speed of sound. The same drive can compare the wavelengths in a gas and a vibrating solid. August Kundt's 1866 paper introduced acoustic dust figures; its footnote explicitly counts successive nodes of a standing wave as half a wavelength.[ref]
The attractive feature is the separation of measurements: a clock or frequency counter measures repetition in time, while a ruler measures repetition in space. Their product supplies a propagation speed. The pressure pattern and the fluid-motion pattern are different, however; identifying which one a detector responds to prevents a quarter-wavelength mistake.
