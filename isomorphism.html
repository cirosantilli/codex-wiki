Isomorphism

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Morphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 349 Articles: 2
An isomorphism is a morphism with a two-sided inverse that is also a morphism. Two objects are isomorphic when such a map exists between them. For groups, rings, and vector spaces, a bijective structure-preserving map has a structure-preserving inverse; in other settings the inverse condition needs separate attention, as for a homeomorphism between topological spaces.
For example, f(k)=2k is a group isomorphism from the additive integers to the additive even integers. It is bijective onto that target, satisfies f(a+b)=f(a)+f(b), and has inverse 2k↦k. The two groups have the same addition pattern even though they are different sets. It is not a ring isomorphism for their inherited multiplications, since f(ab)=2ab while f(a)f(b)=4ab.
