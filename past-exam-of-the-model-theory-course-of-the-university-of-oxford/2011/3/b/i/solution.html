A filter on a set I means a proper family F⊆P(I) with I∈F, ∅∈/F, closure under finite intersections, and upward closure under inclusion. An ultrafilter is a maximal proper filter; equivalently, for every X⊆I, exactly one of X and I∖X belongs to it. A principal ultrafilter is {X⊆I:i∈X} for some i∈I. A nonprincipal ultrafilter is one not of that form, equivalently one containing no finite set.
To prove the ultrafilter lemma using Zorn's lemma, order all proper filters containing F by inclusion. The union of any chain is a proper filter: finitely many members lie together in one filter of the chain, and no chain member contains the empty set. Thus every chain has an upper bound, and Zorn's lemma gives a maximal member U.
For completeness, its decision property follows from maximality. If X∈/U, adjoining X cannot generate a proper filter. Hence some F∈U has F∩X=∅. Upward closure gives I∖X∈U. Both complements cannot be members, since their intersection is empty. Therefore F⊆U for an ultrafilter U​. Properness is essential; a family containing the empty set cannot extend to an ultrafilter.
