Past exam of the model theory course of the University of Oxford / 2011 / 3 / b / iv / Solution

 ... Model theory course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the model theory course of the University of Oxford 2011 3 b iv
OurBigBook.com Words: 301
Let P1​ and P3​ be the sets of primes congruent to one and three modulo four. Both are infinite. For P1​, if a finite list contained them all, put N=(2p1​⋯pr​)2+1. Any prime divisor q of this odd number is new and has minus one as a square modulo q, hence belongs to P1​ by the given criterion. For P3​, use N=4p1​⋯pr​−1. It is three modulo four, so at least one of its odd prime divisors is three modulo four; none of the listed primes divides N. These are elementary Euclidean proofs of infinitely many primes in each odd residue class modulo four.
On the set of all primes, the cofinite sets together with P1​ have the finite intersection property; so do the cofinite sets together with P3​. Extend the generated filters to ultrafilters U1​ and U3​ using the ultrafilter lemma. They are nonprincipal because they contain every cofinite set, including the complement of any singleton. Form
F1​=p∏​Fp​/U1​,F2​=p∏​Fp​/U3​.​
(31)
Every sentence of Σ has a cofinite truth set, hence belongs to both ultrafilters. The Łoś theorem shows F1​,F2​⊨Σ. In particular they are fields, since each field axiom holds in every factor.
For each fixed positive integer n, the sentence n⋅1=0 holds in Fp​ unless p divides n. Its truth set is cofinite, so both fields satisfy it. Therefore charF1​=charF2​=0​.
Finally the sentence ∃x(x2+1=0) holds at exactly the primes in P1​∪{2}. This set belongs to U1​, whereas its complement contains P3​ and belongs to U3​. Łoś therefore gives
F1​⊨∃x(x2+1=0),F2​⊨¬∃x(x2+1=0).​
(32)
Thus minus one is a square in the first characteristic-zero first-order model of Σ and is not a square in the second. The construction also shows why eventual truth over all primes need not decide a sentence whose truth splits along two infinite prime subsequences.
