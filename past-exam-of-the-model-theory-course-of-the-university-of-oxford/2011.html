For the additive zero singleton, x+x=x is already a quantifier-free formula, so it is also an existential formula and a universal formula with an empty quantifier block. If a nonempty block is required, add a dummy ∃y or ∀y; first-order domains are nonempty.
For the two definable multiplicative singletons, existential formulas are
0:1:​∃y(x⋅x=x ∧ x⋅y=y),∃y(x⋅x=x ∧ x⋅y=x).​​
(7)
In the integers, the equation x2=x restricts x to zero or one. In the first line, one never satisfies the inequality, whereas zero does with y=1. In the second, zero never satisfies it, whereas one does with y=0. The respective universal formulas are ∀y(xy=x) and ∀y(xy=y), as already verified.
Neither multiplicative singleton has a quantifier-free formula. In this constant-free, multiplication-only language, every term in the sole free variable x is xk for a positive integer k. Every atomic formula xr=xs is true at both zero and one. Every Boolean combination consequently has the same truth value at those two points, and cannot separate their singletons. This is the fact that zero and one have the same quantifier-free type in the multiplicative integers.
Thus additive zero admits all three forms; multiplicative zero and one admit existential and universal forms, but no quantifier-free form. All remaining cases were excluded from definability itself by structure automorphisms.

An aleph-zero-categorical theory has, up to isomorphism, exactly one first-order model of cardinality ℵ0​.
The Ryll-Nardzewski theorem says that, for a complete theory T in a countable first-order language, with infinite first-order models, the following conditions are equivalent: T is aleph-zero-categorical; for every positive integer n, its type space Sn​(T) of complete types over the empty set is finite; and, for every n, there are only finitely many L-formulas in n free variables modulo equivalence over T. Here φ and ψ are equivalent over T when T⊨∀x(φ↔ψ).

ii OurBigBook.com
A partial type p(x) is a set of L-first-order formulas in a fixed tuple x=(x1​,…,xn​) jointly consistent with T. A complete type, the usual meaning of an n-type here, is maximal among such consistent sets: for each first-order formula φ(x) it contains exactly one of φ,¬φ.
Add new constants c and consider
T∪{φ(c):φ∈p}.
(8)
Every finite subset is satisfiable by consistency of the type. The compactness theorem gives a first-order model of the full set. Its L-reduct is a first-order model of T, and the interpretations of c realize p. This proof applies to partial as well as complete types.
For a complete theory in a countable language, the types realized in every first-order model are exactly the isolated types. An isolated type has a consistent first-order formula θ∈p such that T⊨∀x(θ→φ) for every φ∈p. Completeness forces T⊨∃xθ, so every first-order model realizes p; the converse is the omitting types theorem. Countability is a hypothesis of that converse, not of the preceding compactness realization argument. For a partial type the corresponding criterion is an isolating consistent first-order formula, together with its existence in every first-order model; it need not itself belong to the partial type.
If T is not complete, no one complete n-type can be realized in every first-order model of T: a complete type decides sentences, and two completions of T disagree on some sentence. Thus the completeness and countability hypotheses must be retained when using the isolated-type characterization.

For the additive first-order structure, let
φn​(x):=∃y(x=n termsy+⋯+y​​),n≥1.
(9)
It defines nZ. These sets are pairwise distinct: if m>n>0, the integer n belongs to nZ but not to mZ. Thus there are infinitely many inequivalent one-variable first-order formulas modulo the complete theory of the additive integers. The Ryll-Nardzewski theorem implies that first-order theory is not aleph-zero-categorical.
For the multiplicative first-order structure define divisibility by d(y,x):=∃z(yz=x). For k≥1, the first-order formula
ψk​(x):=∃y1​⋯y2k​(⋀i<j​yi​=yj​ ∧ ⋀i=12k​d(yi​,x))

A complete theory is a consistent first-order theory deciding every sentence in its language:
T⊨σorT⊨¬σfor every L-sentence σ.​
(11)
Equivalently, every two first-order models of T are elementarily equivalent. Consistency excludes the vacuous situation in which a first-order theory has no first-order models and entails both alternatives.

A first-order theory has quantifier elimination if every first-order formula is equivalent over the first-order theory to a quantifier-free formula with the same free variables:
for each φ(x) there is quantifier-free θ(x) with T⊨∀x(φ↔θ).​
(12)
Logical truth and falsity may be treated as the empty Boolean combinations when considering sentences in a language without closed terms. The assertion concerns first-order formulas with free variables, not merely sentences.

A model-complete theory is a first-order theory for which every structure embedding between two of its first-order models is an elementary embedding. Thus, if f:A↪B is such an embedding, then for every first-order formula φ(x) and tuple a from A,
A⊨φ(a)⟺B⊨φ(fa).​
(13)
first-order model completeness does not assert that every two first-order models are elementarily equivalent: it constrains embeddings that exist between first-order models, whereas completeness constrains all first-order models.

i OurBigBook.com
If T is model-complete, the inclusion of a substructure of a first-order structure A⊆B, both first-order models of T, is a structure embedding and hence elementary. Therefore A⪯B.
Conversely, assume every such inclusion is elementary, and take any structure embedding f:A→B between first-order models of T. Its image f[A], with the induced first-order structure, is a substructure of B isomorphic to A, so it is itself a first-order model of T. By hypothesis f[A]⪯B. The isomorphism A→f[A] preserves all first-order formulas, and composing it with this elementary inclusion shows that f is an elementary embedding. Thus T is model-complete⟺(A⊆B⊨T⇒A⪯B)​.

A structure embedding preserves and reflects all atomic formulas, hence all quantifier-free formulas by induction on Boolean combinations. If T has quantifier elimination, replace any φ(x) by a quantifier-free θ(x) equivalent over T. For A⊆B⊨T and a tuple a from A,
A⊨φ(a)⟺A⊨θ(a)⟺B⊨θ(a)⟺B⊨φ(a).

Write
S(x):=∀y(E(x,y)→y=x),R(x):=∃y(E(x,y)∧y=x).
(15)
These are first-order formulas in the original language, not new predicate symbols. An axiomatization consists of the equivalence relation axioms for E, the bound
∀x∀y∀z((E(x,y)∧E(x,z))→(y=z∨y=x∨z=x)),
(16)
and, for every n≥1, the two sentences
∃x1​⋯xn​(⋀i​S(xi​)∧⋀i<j​xi​=xj​),
(17)
∃x1​⋯xn​(⋀i​R(xi​)∧⋀i<j​¬E(xi​,xj​)).
(18)
The first scheme supplies infinitely many singleton classes. The second supplies infinitely many distinct non-singleton classes; the size bound makes these doubletons.
Every countable first-order model has countably infinitely many classes of each size. A bijection between singleton classes, and another between doubleton classes followed by a bijection inside each matched pair, gives an isomorphism between any two countable first-order models. To infer completeness, suppose a sentence had different truth values in two arbitrary first-order models. The Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem supplies countable elementary substructures of those first-order models, which would still disagree on the sentence, contradicting the isomorphism just constructed. Thus all first-order models satisfy the same sentences. Since the original first-order structure satisfies the axioms, these axioms form a complete axiomatization of its first-order theory. This is the theory of infinitely many singleton and doubleton equivalence classes.

Take a countable elementary substructure B′ of A′ containing the named constant, using the Downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem. Its reduct B has infinitely many singleton and doubleton classes. In particular it contains the partner of each of its non-singleton elements, and its interpretation of P is exactly the singleton-class predicate.
Choose new distinct elements u,v∈/B. Define
C=B∪{u},D=B∪{u,v}.
(19)
In C, make u a new singleton class. In D, make {u,v} a new doubleton class; keep every old class of B unchanged. All three reducts are first-order models of the complete theory, and B⊆C⊆D.
Expand C,D by interpreting P as singleton-class membership and retaining the original named constant from B. The resulting countable first-order structures C′,D′ are each isomorphic to B′: all have infinitely many classes of each size, and the bijections of classes can be chosen to preserve the constant and its class. Hence they are first-order models of Th(A′). Both inclusions B′⊆C′ and B′⊆D′ are genuine structure embeddings, since no class meeting B was changed. The given quantifier elimination, together with the preceding implication to first-order model completeness, yields
B′⪯C′,B′⪯D′.
(20)

C⊨¬∃y(E(u,y)∧y=u),D⊨∃y(E(u,y)∧y=u),
(21)
so C⋠D​. There is no conflict with quantifier elimination in the expanded language: P(u) changes truth value, so C′ is not a substructure of D′.

An omega-saturated model realizes every complete one-type over every finite subset of its domain that is consistent with the first-order structure's first-order theory with those named parameters. Equivalently, every partial type in a finite tuple over finitely many parameters that is finitely satisfiable in the first-order structure has a realization there. The equivalence follows by extending a partial type to a complete type; finite tuples can be handled one coordinate at a time.
For an example, take a countably infinite set in the language of equality alone. Over finitely many named elements, a complete one-type either requires equality with one of them or inequality with all of them. Both possibilities are realized. quantifier elimination for an infinite pure set follows by choosing a quantified variable either equal to one of the finitely many named values or distinct from all of them. Indeed every permutation fixing the parameters is a structure automorphism, and all other elements have the same type, so there are no further one-types. This first-order structure is omega-saturated.
For a counterexample, take (N;0,S), where S(n)=n+1. The parameter-free partial type
p(x)={x=Sn(0):n=0,1,2,…}​
(22)
is finitely satisfiable: choose an integer beyond the finitely many excluded numerals. It is not realized in the first-order structure, because every natural number is one of those numerals. Any complete extension of this partial type is therefore omitted as well. Thus the standard successor first-order structure is not omega-saturated.

Here fixed means fixed setwise: σ[D]=D for every structure automorphism σ, not that each point must be fixed individually. A parameter-free definable set has this invariance by automorphism invariance of parameter-free definability.
For the converse, first establish the needed saturation implies homogeneity step. In a countable omega-saturated model, a finite partial elementary map can be extended to include any prescribed additional element in its domain. Transfer the element's complete type over the old finite domain along the map. Each finite part of this transported type has a witness because the old map is elementary; the omega-saturated model realizes the full transported type. The inequalities in the type maintain injectivity. Apply the same construction to the inverse map to enlarge the range. Alternating these steps through an enumeration of the countable domain is the back-and-forth method, giving a structure automorphism. In particular, any two elements with the same complete type over the empty set are related by a structure automorphism.
Assume now that D is finite and invariant, and fix a∈D. All realizations in A of pa​=tp(a/∅) belong to D, by the preceding homogeneity and the invariance assumption. Suppose that no parameter-free first-order formula θa​∈pa​ has all its realizations in D. Then every finite subset of

(23)

A filter on a set I means a proper family F⊆P(I) with I∈F, ∅∈/F, closure under finite intersections, and upward closure under inclusion. An ultrafilter is a maximal proper filter; equivalently, for every X⊆I, exactly one of X and I∖X belongs to it. A principal ultrafilter is {X⊆I:i∈X} for some i∈I. A nonprincipal ultrafilter is one not of that form, equivalently one containing no finite set.
To prove the ultrafilter lemma using Zorn's lemma, order all proper filters containing F by inclusion. The union of any chain is a proper filter: finitely many members lie together in one filter of the chain, and no chain member contains the empty set. Thus every chain has an upper bound, and Zorn's lemma gives a maximal member U.
For completeness, its decision property follows from maximality. If X∈/U, adjoining X cannot generate a proper filter. Hence some F∈U has F∩X=∅. Upward closure gives I∖X∈U. Both complements cannot be members, since their intersection is empty. Therefore F⊆U for an ultrafilter U​. Properness is essential; a family containing the empty set cannot extend to an ultrafilter.

The Łoś theorem states that, for every first-order formula φ(x) and product representatives a1,…,ar,
i∏​Ai​/U⊨φ([a1],…,[ar])⟺{i:Ai​⊨φ(ai1​,…,air​)}∈U.​
(28)
It extends the defining relation-symbol rule to arbitrary first-order formulas, including quantified ones.
Identify the countable index set with N. A nonprincipal ultrafilter on it contains every cofinite set. Let p(x) be a type over a finite parameter set in the ultraproduct M, finitely satisfiable in M. Because the language and parameter set are countable, enumerate it as φ0​(x),φ1​(x),…, with chosen product representatives for the parameters. By finite satisfiability and Łoś,
Xn​={i:Ai​⊨∃x⋀j≤n​φj​(x)}∈U.
(29)
Make these large sets decrease while forcing their intersection to be empty:
Yn​={i:i≥n}∩⋂k≤n​Xk​∈U.
(30)
For each index i, let r(i) be the largest n≤i with i∈Yn​, if one exists. Choose bi​∈Ai​ satisfying the first r(i)+1 first-order formulas; this is possible because i∈Xr(i)​. If no such n exists, choose any bi​.
For each fixed n, every i∈Yn​ has r(i)≥n, so bi​ satisfies φn​. Its coordinate truth set contains Yn​ and belongs to U. Another use of Łoś gives M⊨φn​([b]) for every n. Thus [b] realizes the type. This proves the countable saturation of an ultraproduct over omega, and in particular the ultraproduct is omega-saturated. The construction chooses a finite number of requirements at each coordinate; it does not presume the whole infinite type has a witness in any factor.

F1​=p∏​Fp​/U1​,F2​=p∏​Fp​/U3​.​
(31)
Every sentence of Σ has a cofinite truth set, hence belongs to both ultrafilters. The Łoś theorem shows F1​,F2​⊨Σ. In particular they are fields, since each field axiom holds in every factor.
For each fixed positive integer n, the sentence n⋅1=0 holds in Fp​ unless p divides n. Its truth set is cofinite, so both fields satisfy it. Therefore charF1​=charF2​=0​.
Finally the sentence ∃x(x2+1=0) holds at exactly the primes in P1​∪{2}. This set belongs to U1​, whereas its complement contains P3​ and belongs to U3​. Łoś therefore gives
F1​⊨∃x(x2+1=0),F2​⊨¬∃x(x2+1=0).​
(32)
Thus minus one is a square in the first characteristic-zero first-order model of Σ and is not a square in the second. The construction also shows why eventual truth over all primes need not decide a sentence whose truth splits along two infinite prime subsequences.
