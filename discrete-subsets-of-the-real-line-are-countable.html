Discrete subsets of the real line are countable (B⊆R discrete⇒∣B∣≤ℵ0​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Discrete space Discrete subset
OurBigBook.com Words: 92
Enumerate all intervals with rational endpoints, using the countability of the rational numbers and listing pairs of enumeration indices by increasing sum. If every point b of B is an isolated point, some such interval contains b and no other point of B. Assign to b the least index of such an interval. Distinct points cannot have the same index, so this gives an injective function into N and proves that B is a countable set. The argument uses isolation in the subspace topology, not closedness: the discrete subset {1/n:n≥1} illustrates that an accumulation point outside B is allowed.
