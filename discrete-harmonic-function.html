Discrete harmonic function (Lh=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Harmonic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 205 Articles: 3
A real function h on the vertices of a graph of an unweighted locally finite graph is a discrete harmonic function at u when deg(u)h(u)=∑v∼u​h(v), equivalently Lh(u)=0 for the Graph Laplacian. For positive degree this says its value is the average over its graph neighbours; at an isolated vertex of a graph the Laplacian condition is vacuous. On an electrical network the corresponding equation uses conductance weights and the Weighted graph Laplacian. Voltages away from sources and sinks, and hitting probabilities away from their prescribed boundaries, are discrete harmonic functions.
