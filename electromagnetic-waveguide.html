Electromagnetic waveguide

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 555 Articles: 7
An electromagnetic waveguide confines an electromagnetic wave across its axis while allowing it to travel along the axis. Conducting walls or changes of refractive index select transverse normal modes: reproducible cross-sectional field patterns with their own longitudinal wavenumbers. A hollow metal pipe and an optical fiber are different realizations of this principle. Their usefulness is to deliver electromagnetic energy along a chosen route instead of letting it spread freely.
For a uniform vacuum-filled conducting guide, write a normal mode's axial and time dependence as ei(βz−ωt). Here ω>0 is angular frequency, β is the longitudinal wavenumber, and k⊥2​ is the transverse eigenvalue selected by the wall boundary conditions. The electromagnetic wave equation separates into
β2=c2ω2​−k⊥2​.
(2)
The geometry consumes part of the available squared wavenumber: when ω/c<k⊥​, this branch is an evanescent wave rather than a propagating normal mode. The waveguide cutoff frequency is therefore a property of a specified branch and geometry. A transverse electromagnetic mode in a suitable two-conductor geometry can have k⊥​=0 and no positive geometric cutoff. An optical fiber needs its own dielectric boundary conditions, rather than the metal-wall formulas below.
The rectangular electromagnetic waveguide makes these ideas explicit with a single sine profile. The scalar-potential conducting waveguide modes give a broader construction. At an open aperture, a confined field can radiate into the electromagnetic aperture angular spectrum; it need not remain one guided normal mode.
