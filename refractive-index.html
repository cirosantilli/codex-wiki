Refractive index (n)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 8
The refractive index compares the phase velocity of light in a medium with the vacuum speed of light c. For a transparent, uniform medium with isotropy and a positive-index propagation branch,
n(ω)=vph​(ω)c​=ωck(ω)​.
(14)
Here ω>0 is the angular frequency, k>0 is the wavenumber, and vph​=ω/k is the speed of a surface of constant wave phase. The refractive index is a dimensionless quantity; it is a property of a specified material response at a specified frequency, rather than a universal number attached to the word “glass”. More general absorbing or negative-index media require the conventions discussed in refractive index and electromagnetic response. [ref]
For an original model example, choose n=3/2 at vacuum wavelength λ0​=600nm. The phase velocity is vph​=2c/3≃1.999×108m/s, the frequency is f=c/λ0​≃4.997×1014Hz, and the medium wavelength is 400nm. These are stipulated model values, not measured properties of glass or water. A value at this single frequency does not determine a pulse's group velocity.
The usefulness of refractive index is that one measured response connects several different observations: Snell's law predicts refraction, optical path length predicts accumulated wave phase, and changes with frequency describe optical dispersion. Even at normal incidence, where a light ray does not bend, a changed refractive index changes wavelength and wave phase.
