Frozen scalar fields and the Friedmann constraint

For a homogeneous canonical scalar field, the cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation reduces to ϕ˙​(ϕ¨​+3Hϕ˙​+V′)=0. It implies the inflaton equation of motion wherever ϕ˙​=0, and by continuity at isolated turning points. It cannot by itself exclude a frozen field on a time interval. Indeed, choose ϕ=ϕ0​ with V(ϕ0​)>0 but V′(ϕ0​)=0; a de Sitter scale factor in flat slicing with H2=V(ϕ0​)/(3MPl2​) satisfies the homogeneous Friedmann equation and Friedmann acceleration equation, but not the scalar equation. Varying the inflaton action in an expanding universe supplies the missing equation also for a frozen field, requiring V′(ϕ0​)=0.
