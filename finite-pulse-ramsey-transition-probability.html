Finite-pulse Ramsey transition probability (Pg→e​(δ;τ,T))

Use the Driven two-level Rabi model in the ordered basis (∣e⟩,∣g⟩), with Pauli matrices satisfying σz​=diag(1,−1). Let the Rabi detuning be δ=ω0​−ω=2π(ν0​−ν), in angular frequency, while ν0​ and ν are ordinary frequencies in hertz. Assume two identical square pulses, equal drive oscillation phases, constant positive Rabi angular frequency Ω, constant δ during pulses and flight, and no quantum decoherence or loss. The rotating-wave approximation and isolation of the chosen two-level system are required, just as for the Driven two-level Rabi model. Free flight means no transverse drive; the reference oscillator continues running.
The pulse and free-flight Hamiltonian operators in the rotating frame are
Hp​=2ℏ​(δσz​+Ωσx​),Hf​=2ℏδ​σz​,q=Ω2+δ2​,cp​=cos(qτ/2),sp​=sin(qτ/2).
(59)
The Pauli matrix multiplication law gives (δσz​+Ωσx​)2=q2I. Thus the exact special unitary group SU(2) pulse matrix for this effective Hamiltonian operator, and the flight matrix, are
Up​=(cp​−i(δ/q)sp​−i(Ω/q)sp​​−i(Ω/q)sp​cp​+i(δ/q)sp​​),Uf​=(e−iδT/20​0eiδT/2​).
(60)
The sequence is Up​Uf​Up​. Starting in ∣g⟩, matrix multiplication and the Born rule give
Pg→e​=4q2Ω2​sp2​[cp​cos2δT​−qδ​sp​sin2δT​]2.​
(61)
This retains the tilted rotation axis and quantum phase accumulated during each finite pulse. At resonance it gives Pg→e​(0)=sin2(Ωτ); with no flight it reduces to the single square-pulse response for duration 2τ. Both checks would fail if arbitrary ideal π/2 rotations were silently substituted at nonzero Rabi detuning.
For resonant pulse area Ωτ=π/2, ∣δ∣/Ω≪1 and τ≪T, the central fringe approaches
Pg→e​≃cos2(δT/2)=cos2[π(ν−ν0​)T].
(62)
Its half-height points are ∣ν−ν0​∣=1/(4T), so the central peak's full width at half maximum is 1/(2T) in hertz, or π/T in angular frequency. This width assumes an effectively flat pulse envelope across that central peak, one flight time and unit contrast; it is neither a universal finite-pulse width nor a frequency uncertainty. Longer flight lets a smaller frequency difference accumulate the same observable quantum phase.
The two pulses here have the same sign and the plotted outcome is transfer to ∣e⟩. The earlier Ramsey interferometry example uses an inverse analysis pulse and reports return to its initial label. Specifying the measured outcome and pulse oscillation phases reconciles the two conventions. In a beam the lengths and speed determine τ and T; in the Quantronium Ramsey interference experiment electronics set the pulse times for a stationary circuit.
