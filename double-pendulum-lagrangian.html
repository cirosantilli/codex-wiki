Double pendulum Lagrangian (L=T−V)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Lagrangian mechanics Double pendulum
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
Choose Cartesian coordinates with origin at the upper pivot, x to the right and y upward. The two position vectors are
r1​=ℓ(sinθ1​−cosθ1​​),r2​=ℓ(sinθ1​+sinθ2​−cosθ1​−cosθ2​​).
(15)
These expressions satisfy the rod holonomic constraints ∣r1​∣=ℓ and ∣r2​−r1​∣=ℓ automatically. This is the practical advantage of using the two angles as coordinates: the rod tensions need not be unknowns in the motion calculation.[ref]
Differentiate these position vectors to obtain the velocities. The dot product between their two rod contributions uses cos(θ1​−θ2​)=cosθ1​cosθ2​+sinθ1​sinθ2​, giving
∣r˙1​∣2=ℓ2θ˙12​,∣r˙2​∣2=ℓ2(θ˙12​+θ˙22​+2cos(θ1​−θ2​)θ˙1​θ˙2​).
(16)
Adding the two kinetic energies and measuring gravitational potential energy from the downward equilibrium gives
TVL​=2mℓ2​(2θ˙12​+θ˙22​+2cos(θ1​−θ2​)θ˙1​θ˙2​),=mgℓ[2(1−cosθ1​)+(1−cosθ2​)],=T−V.​
(17)
The coefficient 2 has a geometric meaning: the upper rod raises both masses, and its motion contributes to both kinetic energies. The coupling in T is present because the lower mass has a moving pivot. The zero chosen for V changes L by a constant and leaves the Euler-Lagrange equations unchanged.[ref]
The principle of stationary action applies to S=∫Ldt. Its fixed-endpoint first variation, after integration by parts, is
δS=∫∑i=12​(∂θi​∂L​−dtd​∂θ˙i​∂L​)δθi​dt.
(18)
Independent allowed variations therefore give one Euler-Lagrange equation for each angle. Stationary action alone does not assert a minimum.[ref]
