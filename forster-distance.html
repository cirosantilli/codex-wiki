Förster distance (R0​)

The Förster distance is the separation giving FRET efficiency E=1/2 for the specified pair and conditions. It is a property of the optical pair and its environment, rather than a universal length. In the usual pair model,
R06​∝κ2QD​Jn−4,

where κ2 is the FRET dipole orientation factor, QD​ the FRET donor quantum yield, J the FRET spectral overlap integral, and n the medium's refractive index. The omitted proportionality constant depends on the unit convention for J.[ref]
If FRET efficiency is known and those assumptions hold, inversion gives
r=R0​(E−1−1)1/6,dEdlogr​=−6E(1−E)1​.
(10)
The derivative explains why errors become troublesome near E=0 or E=1. An illustrative pair with R0​=4 nanometres and another with R0​=6 nanometres both give E=1/2 at their different Förster distances: knowing FRET efficiency alone does not determine r.
A heterogeneous population adds another ambiguity. In a synthetic example with equal weights per FRET donor excitation, efficiencies 0.1 and 0.9 average to 0.5, but their separations are 91/6R0​ and 9−1/6R0​. Their average separation is approximately 1.068R0​, whereas inverting the average FRET efficiency gives R0​. A measured emission ratio has additional detection weights; it need not equal even this excitation-weighted average. The transferable lesson is that an average readout does not uniquely specify a distribution of molecular separations.
