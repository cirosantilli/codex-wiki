Binary symmetric channel capacity (C=1−h2​(p))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Channel capacity Weakly symmetric channel capacity
OurBigBook.com Words: 634 Articles: 2
A binary symmetric channel independently flips each transmitted bit with crossover probability p. Its channel capacity is
C=1−h2​(p),0≤p≤1,
(103)
in bits per use, where h2​ is binary entropy and the endpoint convention is 0log2​0=0. Knowing the input leaves just the uncertainty of the flip, so H(Y∣X)=h2​(p) for every input probability distribution. Since the output is a bit, H(Y)≤1, and thus I(X;Y)≤1−h2​(p). A fair input produces a fair output and reaches the bound. This is the two-symbol case of weakly symmetric channel capacity; Eytan Modiano, MIT 16.36, Channel Capacity and Coding, slides 4–5 gives this calculation.
For crossover probabilities p=0.01,0.1,0.25, the resulting channel capacities are approximately 0.91920686, 0.53100441, and 0.18872188 bits per use. At p=0, the communication channel is noiseless; at p=1/2, the output is independent of the input and the capacity is zero. At p=1, every bit is inverted, a known reversible rule, and capacity is one again. More generally C(p)=C(1−p), because inverting every received bit exchanges the two crossover probabilities. The existing mutual information figure shows this full symmetric curve.
An uncoded transmission illustrates why the raw bit-error probability is not itself channel capacity. At p=0.1, transmitting 100 uncoded bits succeeds on all of them with probability 0.9100≈0.00002656. The probability of at least one error is therefore approximately 0.99997344. A suitable block code changes which received words count as recoverable evidence of a message; simply repeating uncoded transmission at rate one does not obtain the reliability promised at rates below C.
