Metric embedding (f:(X,dX​)↪(Y,dY​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 33
A metric embedding represents the points of one metric space (X,dX​) inside another (Y,dY​) by an injective function f:X→Y. The geometric question is how faithfully the representation preserves pairwise distances. For example, binary strings can be treated as vectors so that tools from linear algebra become available, but the chosen norm determines what their separation means. A drawing of the points alone does not specify a metric embedding: both metrics matter.
An isometric embedding preserves every distance exactly. A metric embedding that is a bi-Lipschitz map allows multiplicative error while keeping its bi-Lipschitz distortion bounded over all pairs. A coarse embedding instead controls separation through possibly nonlinear functions of distance. These are different requirements: the Hamming cube as an L1 and L2 metric below preserves Hamming distance exactly in one representation and takes its square root in another.
The usefulness is that a complicated distance comparison becomes a calculation with familiar vectors and norms. This also helps assess a visualization: nearby points should not acquire arbitrarily large separations, and far-apart points should not collapse together. Matoušek's 2013 lecture notes describe how motivations from computer science, especially approximation algorithms, accelerated the study of metric embeddings from the 1990s onward. [ref]
