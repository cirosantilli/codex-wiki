Angular momentum (L=r×p)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 37
Angular momentum measures how a point mass's momentum passes around a chosen origin. Motion straight toward or away from that origin contributes zero; transverse momentum contributes more when it passes farther away. The picture shows the sign convention before the calculation.
Fix an origin O and nonrotating axes in an inertial frame. For a nonrelativistic point mass of constant mass m>0, let r be its position from O and p=mv its mechanical momentum in that same reference frame. Its angular momentum is the cross product
LO​=r×p,∣LO​∣=rpsinϕ.
(11)
Here r=∣r∣, p=∣p∣, and ϕ∈[0,π] is the angle between them. The right-hand rule gives the direction: curl from r toward p, and the thumb points along LO​. Thus angular momentum is an axial vector, perpendicular to both position and momentum. Its International System of Units unit is kgm2/s. A point mass need not follow a circle to have angular momentum.[ref]
With x rightward, y upward, and +z out of the page, the cross product gives Lz​=xpy​−ypx​. Take
r=(2,0,0)m,p=(0,3,0)kgm/s.
(12)
Then LO​=(0,0,6)kgm2/s. Reversing the momentum makes Lz​=−6kgm2/s; radial momentum p=(3,0,0)kgm/s gives LO​=0. The factor rsinϕ is the lever arm to the line through the particle parallel to its momentum.
For motion in the xy plane, plane polar coordinates give v=r˙r+rθ˙θ, with r×θ=z. Consequently
Lz​=mr2θ˙,r2θ˙=mLz​​.
(13)
The signed angular velocity θ˙ is positive counterclockwise viewed from +z toward the plane, with θ measured in radians. Radial velocity does not enter the cross product. Under conservation of angular momentum, this relation predicts faster angular motion at smaller r and constant areal velocity A˙=Lz​/(2m) for consistently oriented swept area. That is the geometric content of Kepler's second law.
For several point masses, add their angular momenta about the same origin: LO​=∑i​ri​×pi​. The origin matters: angular momentum under an origin shift explains why the same moving particle can have different values about two fixed points. The central axis of a momentum system and angular momentum decomposition about the centre of mass make this dependence useful.
