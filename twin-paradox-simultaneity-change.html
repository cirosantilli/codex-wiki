Twin paradox simultaneity change

 ... Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor Time dilation Twin paradox
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
During either inertial leg, the traveller can correctly say that the moving home clock runs slowly. The missing step is deciding which distant home-clock reading is simultaneous with turnaround. By the Lorentz transformation, the outward inertial frame's constant-time line through P obeys t−vx/c2=constant. At x=0 it meets the home world line at
tH−​=2T​−2c2v2T​.
(9)
For the return inertial frame, use t+vx/c2=constant through the same spacetime event P; its home-clock assignment is
tH+​=2T​+2c2v2T​,tH+​−tH−​=c2v2T​.
(10)
The dashed lines in the spacetime diagram illustrate this relativity of simultaneity. For v=0.6c and T=10yr, they assign 3.2yr and 6.8yr to the home clock. The difference of 3.6yr is a change in the traveller's distant-time assignment, not a sudden physical advance of the home clock.
On each leg, the traveller's 4yr of proper time corresponds to 4/γ=3.2yr of home-clock advance according to that leg's inertial frame. Including the twin paradox simultaneity change gives 3.2+3.6+3.2=10yr, exactly the reading at reunion. Actual received light signals remain continuous across a physical turnaround; their arrival times are distinct from the distant spacetime events assigned as simultaneous by Einstein synchronisation.
