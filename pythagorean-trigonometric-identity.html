Pythagorean trigonometric identity (sin2x+cos2x=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Trigonometric function
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
For every real angle θ,
sin2θ+cos2θ=1.​
(60)
It is the equation of the unit circle written in terms of its sine and cosine coordinates. For an acute angle, dividing the Pythagorean theorem by the square of the hypotenuse gives the same relation in a right triangle. The circle interpretation explains why it remains true outside that first-quadrant triangle.
For example, if sinθ=3/5, the identity gives cos2θ=16/25, so cosθ is either 4/5 or −4/5. The sign needs the angle's location: it is positive in the first quadrant and negative in the second. Thus the identity determines the magnitude of the missing coordinate without determining the direction.
The relation is useful for eliminating one squared trigonometric function, checking that a coordinate pair has unit length, and checking that the columns of a rotation matrix are unit vectors. It also holds for complex arguments under the complex exponential definitions, although then the squares are algebraic squares rather than squared moduli.
