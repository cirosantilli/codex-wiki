Influence function of a Huber location estimator (IF(x;T,F)=sψk​((x−θ)/s)/A)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Robust statistics Influence function
OurBigBook.com Words: 273
Fix a scale s>0 and cutoff k>0. Define the population Huber location estimator by the root
EF​ψk​(sX−θ​)=0,θ=T(F),A=EF​ψk′​(sX−θ​)>0.
(29)
Here ψk​ is the Huber score. It has Lipschitz continuity, so dominated convergence justifies differentiation when F puts no mass at θ±ks. Then A=PF​(∣X−θ∣<ks), and the nonzero derivative identifies the local population root. The implicit function theorem applies away from the contaminating score's kinks; its Lipschitz continuity also gives the first-order expansion at those kinks. Differentiating its contaminated equation yields
0=−sA​IF(x;T,F)+ψk​(sx−θ​),IF(x;T,F)=Asψk​((x−θ)/s)​.
(30)
The normalization matters: the Huber score alone is not the influence function. The gross-error sensitivity is sk/A, while the rejection point of an influence function is infinite because the clipped tails stay nonzero. The original clipped estimating criterion and its derivative normalization also appear in the population analysis of M-estimators.[ref]
Scale sets what counts as a large residual. For a normal distribution N(μ,σ2) with fixed s=σ, symmetry gives θ=μ and A=2Φ(k)−1, where Φ is the standard normal distribution function. If the scale is instead a statistical functional S(F), differentiation of the joint equation adds the scale contribution:
IF(x;T,F)=Asψk​(rx​)−BIF(x;S,F)​,rx​=sx−θ​,B=EF​[rX​ψk′​(rX​)].
(31)
Under a symmetric baseline and an odd Huber score, B=0, so scale estimation drops out at first order. This cancellation needs regularity of S and does not protect finite contamination changes: an inflated standard deviation enlarges the physical cutoff ks. A robust choice, such as a suitable Scale M-estimator, needs its own influence function and breakdown point checked. Fixing a numeric cutoff in raw units also sacrifices scale equivariance when units change.[ref]
