Finite-population model of raven confirmation (Lnonblack​=(1−1/M)−1,Lraven​=(1−1/r)−1)

 Home Philosophy Philosophy of science Scientific confirmation Bayesian confirmation
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
Fix r>1 ravens and M>1 nonblack objects in a finite population. Under H0​, all ravens are black; under H1​, exactly one is nonblack. The total numbers r,M are held fixed under both hypotheses, so the classification of one nonraven also changes between hypotheses. This is a deliberately specified model for Bayesian confirmation, not a universal rule about how bird observations supply evidence.
A uniform draw among nonblack objects returns a nonraven with probabilities 1 under H0​ and 1−1/M under H1​. A uniform draw among ravens returns a black object with probabilities 1 and 1−1/r. The respective likelihood ratios for H0​ over H1​ are therefore (1−1/M)−1 and (1−1/r)−1. When M>r, the former is closer to one, so supplies less support under these assumptions.
With equal prior probabilities, Bayes' theorem gives posterior probabilities 1/(2−1/M) and 1/(2−1/r). More generally, with prior probability p for H0​, replace the two expressions by p/[p+(1−p)(1−1/M)] and p/[p+(1−p)(1−1/r)]. Previously known information or a different sampling protocol requires different likelihoods; selecting an object already known to be a nonraven need not supply new evidence.
