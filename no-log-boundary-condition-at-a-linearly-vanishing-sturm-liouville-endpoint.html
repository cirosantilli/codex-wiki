No-log boundary condition at a linearly vanishing Sturm-Liouville endpoint (limx↓a​p(x)y′(x)=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Sturm-Liouville theory Sturm-Liouville problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
Suppose p(x)=c(x−a)+O((x−a)2) with c>0, and the other coefficients in (py′)′+(λw−q)y=0 extend continuously to a, with w(a)>0. A bounded eigenfunction has (py′)′=O(1), hence py′=C+O(x−a). A nonzero C would give y′∼C/[c(x−a)] and an unbounded logarithmic singularity. Thus C=0, and the regular branch has bounded first derivative. The second local branch carries a logarithm. The title condition excludes that branch.
For two regular eigenfunctions, p(uv′−u′v)→0. This removes the singular-end contribution to the Lagrange identity for a Sturm-Liouville operator. Together with a separated self-adjoint condition at the other endpoint, it selects the regular self-adjoint differential operator realization. The precise operator domain uses the maximal differential-operator domain and the no-log endpoint condition; boundedness of classical eigenfunctions is its convenient local expression, not a replacement for specifying an operator domain.
