Rayleigh scattering

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
Rayleigh scattering is elastic scattering of electromagnetic radiation by particles much smaller than the incident wavelength. In the stationary particle approximation, the scattered electromagnetic radiation keeps the incident frequency: its direction changes, rather than its color. The incident electric field induces an oscillating electric dipole moment, which produces electric dipole radiation. This is a useful example of how a microscopic response can explain a familiar large-scale sight, why the sky is blue. [ref].
The smallness condition for Rayleigh scattering by a small sphere of radius a is ka≪1, where the wavenumber is k=2π/λ and λ is the surrounding medium's wavelength. For ordinary materials with modest refractive index, the incident electric field then varies little across the particle, making the electric-dipole approximation for radiation useful. For very large refractive index, also check the internal size ∣m∣ka≪1 before applying the simple electric dipole model. Once these conditions fail, use Mie scattering for a sphere instead of extrapolating the Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence.
Historically, the 1871 paper [ref] connected the color and polarization of an electromagnetic wave of scattered skylight with particles smaller than the wavelength. Its dimensional analysis anticipates scattered light intensity proportional to particle volume squared and to λ−4. The original mechanical picture is historical; the modern explanation uses induced electric dipole radiation.
