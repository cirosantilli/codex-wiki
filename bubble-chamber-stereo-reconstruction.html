Bubble-chamber stereo reconstruction

OurBigBook.com Words: 312
Stereoscopy supplies depth because different cameras see different projections of the same track. A functional reconstruction first matches the same interaction vertex and track segments across views. A calibrated lens and window model maps each measured image coordinate back to a ray inside the liquid. Refraction at the windows must be included. Intersecting the rays, or finding their closest agreement when measurements have errors, estimates three-dimensional coordinates. Several points then determine a trajectory, rather than only a projected arc.
An original ideal-camera model makes the geometry explicit. Take two parallel pinhole cameras with centers (0,0,0) and (b,0,0), focal length f, and optical axes along z. Use virtual image planes in front of the pinholes so that image coordinates have the same orientation as the scene. A point (X,Y,Z) then gives
uL​=ZfX​,uR​=Zf(X−b)​,vL​=vR​=ZfY​.
(36)
Subtracting the horizontal image coordinates gives disparity d=uL​−uR​=fb/Z, hence
Z=dfb​,X=fZuL​​,Y=fZvL​​.
(37)
For stipulated b=0.20m, f=0.050m, uL​=0.010m, uR​=0.005m and vL​=0.002m, the reconstructed point is (0.40,0.08,2.0)m. These are ideal optical inputs, not dimensions of Alvarez's apparatus. This model omits refraction and lens distortion; actual calibration replaces its simple ray geometry. Since ∣dZ/dd∣=fb/d2, the same disparity error gives worse depth precision for distant points.
The Lorentz force then supplies a momentum scale. With magnetic field B uniform and negligible stopping power over a fitted segment, bubble-chamber track readout gives p⊥​=∣q∣Br. The local three-dimensional helix tangent supplies the missing direction: if α is its angle to the field, p=p⊥​/sinα. In practical units,
GeV/cp⊥​​=0.299792458​eq​​TB​mr​.
(38)
Here e is the elementary charge and c the speed of light. A short, almost straight arc poorly constrains its radius of curvature; a visually sharp track can therefore have poor momentum precision.
