Essential infimum and essential supremum (essinf, esssup)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Infimum and supremum
OurBigBook.com Words: 468 Articles: 1
The essential infimum and essential supremum describe the lower and upper bounds of a measurable function after disregarding null sets for a specified measure. A pointwise infimum or supremum sees every input; an essential bound sees whether values below or above a proposed threshold occur on a set of positive measure. Thus "essential" means insensitive to changes on a null set, rather than insensitive to unusual values. This distinction makes essential bounds compatible with Lebesgue integrals and the equivalence classes used in Lp spaces. [ref].
For a real measurable function f on a measure space (X,Σ,μ), the definitions are
essinfμ​f=sup{a∈R:μ({x:f(x)<a})=0},esssupμ​f=inf{b∈R:μ({x:f(x)>b})=0}.
(1)
These can take values in the extended real number line. On a space of positive total measure, finite essential bounds obey essinfμ​f≤f≤esssupμ​f almost everywhere. If s=esssupμ​f is finite, every threshold b<s has μ({f>b})>0; the corresponding lower-edge statement is μ({f<a})>0 for every a>essinfμ​f. These are the measure-based counterparts of the approximation property of a supremum and approximation property of an infimum.
Here is a two-sided example on [0,1] with Lebesgue measure:
g(x)=⎩⎨⎧​−3,4,x,​x=1/4,x=3/4,otherwise.​
(2)
The pointwise bounds are infg=−3 and supg=4, while the essential bounds are essinfg=0 and esssupg=1. Both exceptional singletons are null sets. But for 0<a<1, the inputs where g<a still have measure a, and those where g>a have measure 1−a: deleting or adding two individual inputs cannot change those lengths. Hence neither essential bound can be moved inward.
The choice of measure is part of the answer. The same g has essential bounds −3 and 4 under counting measure, because every nonempty set has positive measure. Under the Dirac measure at 1/4, both essential bounds equal −3. A null set for one measure need not be a null set for another.
Nor must the exceptional inputs be isolated. The Dirichlet function, equal to one at rational numbers and zero at irrational numbers, has pointwise supremum one on [0,1], but essential supremum zero for Lebesgue measure: the rational numbers form a countable set that is a null set, even though they are dense. Conversely, a value ten on an interval of length 0.001 affects the essential supremum just as decisively as it would on half the domain. Small positive measure is not zero measure.
