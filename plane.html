Plane (P)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 809 Articles: 9
A plane is a two-dimensional affine subspace: a point a together with every linear combination of two linearly independent direction vectors u,v,
P={a+su+tv:s,t∈R}.
(51)
In three-dimensional Euclidean space, a Cartesian equation of a plane fixes one linear equation n⋅x=d, with a nonzero normal vector n. The two free directions are orthogonal to n. A plane extends indefinitely; a rectangular patch in a drawing marks only part of it, and does not define an edge of the plane.
For example, the plane 2x−y+2z=3 contains a=(1,1,1). The vectors u=(1,2,0) and v=(0,2,1) are linearly independent and satisfy (2,−1,2)⋅u=(2,−1,2)⋅v=0. Hence its points are exactly (1+s,1+2s+2t,1+t): substitution gives 2x−y+2z=3 for every real s,t, and every solution has s=x−1, t=z−1.
The description separates position from direction. A translation of the plane changes a and possibly d, while retaining its parallel directions. A plane through the origin is a linear subspace; the displayed example is not, since the zero vector fails its linear equation. This distinction matters when applying orthogonal projection: subtract an anchor point before projecting onto the direction linear subspace.
Historically, Euclid's Euclid's Elements by Euclid, Book I Definition 7, describes a plane surface through the straight lines lying on it. David E. Joyce's accompanying guide notes that this older use can include a bounded square or circular figure. That usage differs from the whole unbounded affine subspace used here.[ref]
