Germ plasm inheritance places germline-promoting material into selected biological cells; induction establishes germline identity through signalling to competent biological cells. Both must protect the future germline from inappropriate somatic differentiation. They differ in how that programme begins and in how broadly early transcription is repressed. primordial germ cells are precursors of future gametes, not simply biological cells expressing one widely shared germ-cell marker.
In Drosophila melanogaster, maternal oskar messenger RNA is transported to the posterior oocyte and locally translated. Oskar organises germ plasm containing RNA and proteins, including Vasa, Tudor and Nanos. Posterior cell nuclei encounter this specialised cytoplasm and cellularise as pole cells. Necessity and positional sufficiency are distinguishable: loss of Oskar disrupts assembly, while experimentally mislocalised oskar messenger RNA can induce anterior pole cells. The latter result shows that the organising material can redirect where the programme begins; it does not mean that Oskar acts without the other cellular components.[ref]
Assembly and inheritance also should not be equated with one universal granule mechanism. A germ plasm is a spatially organised collection of functional components; the visible granule and the whole regulatory activity are not necessarily identical. Test assembly by tracking recruitment order, deleting candidate organisers and rescuing them at physiological levels. Test specification separately by permanent lineage tracing and later contribution to the gonad and, where experimentally feasible, functional gametes. Disrupting a visible aggregate without abolishing the relevant biochemical activity is not evidence that all germline specification has failed.
In fly pole cells, a direct route to transcriptional quiescence is the small Pgc protein, translated from inherited germ-plasm RNA. It binds P-TEFb, a protein kinase complex needed for productive RNA polymerase II elongation, and prevents its normal recruitment to transcriptional sites. Reduced phosphorylation of the polymerase's C-terminal domain at serine 2 accompanies repression. Loss, ectopic expression, physical interaction and recruitment assays connect Pgc protein to this mechanism rather than merely correlating a silent biological cell with a granule.[ref]
The nematode Caenorhabditis elegans illustrates another inherited route. Maternal PIE-1 segregates into the germline blastomeres and inhibits the somatic transcription programme. A repression domain with similarity to the RNA polymerase II C-terminal repeat provided a mechanistic link to the transcriptional apparatus; mutations that damage repression also weaken in-embryo rescue. Thus distinct inherited repressors can converge on productive RNA polymerase II activity. It is unnecessary to claim that PIE-1 and Pgc protein are the same molecule or that every inherited germline component acts by this mechanism.[ref]
In the house mouse, primordial germ cells are induced from competent postimplantation epiblast rather than assigned by an already localised maternal germ plasm. BMP4 from extraembryonic biological tissues supplies an essential inductive input. BMP4 null embryos lack primordial germ cells, and chimera analysis identified an extraembryonic source requirement; fewer founders in heterozygotes showed a dosage-sensitive effect on establishment rather than simply later expansion.[ref] Necessity of this signal does not imply that every exposed epiblast biological cell becomes germline: competence, position and competing inputs constrain the response.
The induced programme uses BLIMP1, PRDM14 and TFAP2C to repress somatic regulatory networks and establish germline identity and epigenetic reprogramming. BLIMP1 binds and represses somatic and proliferation-associated targets, while the factors also activate germline-associated programmes. This is the crucial contrast in interpreting quiescence: an induced germline must transcribe its specification programme, so selective repression of somatic genes is not equivalent to a universal shutdown of transcription. Chromatin and DNA methylation are subsequently remodelled; a hypomethylated state by itself is not evidence that the genome is transcriptionally inactive.[ref]
Induction also differs among mammals. Human primordial germ cell-like cells require SOX17 as an important upstream specifier, with BLIMP1 helping suppress alternative somatic programmes. This comes from experimentally induced biological cells compared with available germ-cell references; it should not be presented as direct observation of every event in an intact human embryo. It demonstrates why the exact mouse hierarchy cannot simply be renamed human.[ref]
For either mode, measure nascent RNA incorporation together with serine-2-phosphorylated RNA polymerase II, total polymerase and multiple somatic and germline transcripts. Maternal messenger RNA persistence makes total RNA abundance an unreliable measure of current synthesis. Include somatic biological cells in the same embryo as positive controls, a validated transcription-inhibited sample as a low-signal control, and viability measurements. Timed perturbation after specification distinguishes a requirement for establishing identity from one for maintaining quiescence. Rescue should recover appropriate molecular identity and later lineage function, not merely lower a staining signal. These experiments would reveal the shared aim of suppressing inappropriate differentiation while preserving the different transcriptional requirements of inheritance and induction.
