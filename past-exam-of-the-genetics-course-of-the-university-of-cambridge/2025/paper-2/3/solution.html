Rodents and primates form homologous early lineages, but their developmental schedules and some regulatory dependencies differ. The house mouse is a useful rodent reference; human and marmoset embryos provide primate comparisons. Neither one rodent nor one primate represents every member of its group. Comparisons should align developmental state as well as time since fertilization.
Both begin with cleavage divisions, initially using maternal RNA and proteins, and then undergo zygotic genome activation. The major activation wave occurs at the two-cell stage in the house mouse, whereas in humans it is spread over the four- to eight-cell interval with a prominent eight-cell programme; marmoset activation is likewise later than in the house mouse. Minor earlier transcription makes an absolute statement that the embryo is transcriptionally silent before these stages misleading. Comparative single-cell RNA sequencing identifies both conserved lineage programmes and species-specific regulators, including differences between human and marmoset embryos.[ref]
The timing difference can be demonstrated with stage-resolved nascent-RNA labeling, intronic RNA measurements and transcription inhibition, alongside maternal-transcript decay measurements. Increased total transcript abundance alone cannot distinguish new synthesis from altered stability. An inhibitor-induced cleavage arrest is informative only if uptake, target engagement and viability are checked; maternal stores may sustain divisions after inhibition. Species-specific rates of division also mean that two-cell versus eight-cell activation cannot be translated into a fixed hour difference without direct measurement.
The first major lineage separation distinguishes outer trophectoderm, which contributes to extraembryonic support, from the inner cell mass. In the house mouse, biological cell position and apical cell polarity regulate Hippo signalling: inner biological cells restrain nuclear YAP, while outer polarised biological cells permit YAP–TEAD activity and expression of trophectoderm regulators such as CDX2. OCT4, Sox2 and NANOG participate in pluripotent identity. Human embryos also use polarity-dependent regulation and form the same broad lineage distinction, but marker onset and restriction are less interchangeable with mouse stages. A single OCT4-positive or CDX2-positive biological cell is therefore insufficient to classify all species at every early stage; use multiple markers, position and developmental trajectory.[ref]
The inner cell mass subsequently separates into epiblast, which supplies the embryo proper, and hypoblast, also called primitive endoderm, which supplies extraembryonic endodermal biological tissues. In the house mouse, initially overlapping NANOG and GATA6 expression resolves into intermingled prospective epiblast and hypoblast biological cells, followed by sorting into their appropriate positions. Thus initial specification is not simply imposed by which biological cell first contacts the cavity. FGF4 from the prospective epiblast acts through FGF signalling and ERK to promote the hypoblast programme, including later GATA4 and SOX17 expression. Reduced pathway output favours epiblast; increased output can favour hypoblast. Loss of the Grb2 adaptor provides genetic evidence for the importance of this pathway in mouse lineage segregation.[ref]
For the evidence available at the June 2025 examination, the central contrast was that hypoblast markers persisted in human embryos exposed to FGF receptor or MEK inhibitors under conditions that strongly redirected rodent inner cell mass allocation. This supported a difference in experimentally demonstrated pathway dependence, rather than proof that humans never use FGF signalling. Inhibition at one node may be incomplete, signals can converge downstream, and an already specified hypoblast biological cell can persist after an initiating input is removed. The original comparative inhibitor experiments should therefore be interpreted in their stage and culture context.[ref]
There are additional primate features. Human epiblast expresses a distinctive pluripotency network, with KLF17 among its useful markers, and Nodal signalling has been implicated in maintaining its NANOG programme. Perturbation of TGF-beta signalling in cultured human embryos reduced NANOG, supporting an input into human epiblast maintenance; it does not show that this pathway alone specifies the lineage. Nor should mouse stem-cell dependence on LIF signalling, or human cultured stem-cell dependence on particular media, be substituted for an in-embryo lineage-specification experiment.[ref]
Morphogenesis also diverges. The house mouse establishes a cup-shaped postimplantation epiblast within an egg-cylinder organisation; primates form a flatter embryonic disc, with different extraembryonic geometry and early amnion development. Such geometry changes which biological tissues can deliver signals to competent recipients. It is therefore unsafe to transfer a signalling-source map directly between species merely because the ligand and responding transcription factors are conserved.
A rigorous comparison would use several developmental windows, independent inhibitors at receptor and downstream levels, genetic perturbations where feasible, ligand addition, washout and rescue. Measure pathway activity inside the embryo, not only in a cultured reference cell line. Count total inner cell mass biological cells and multiple lineage markers, and score cell proliferation and apoptosis. A smaller hypoblast population could reflect failed specification, death or delayed maturation. Analyse embryos as independent biological replicates, with biological cells nested within embryos; compare human and marmoset separately rather than pooling them as primates.
A later result sharpens the caveat. Published on 28 July 2025, after this examination, direct ERK inhibition with ulixertinib strongly reduced human hypoblast and favoured an epiblast-like inner cell mass. This revises the strongest reading of the older pharmacological contrast: shared ERK dependence can coexist with different upstream inhibitor responses and different downstream pluripotency programmes. It is a subsequent scientific update, not evidence a June candidate was expected to know.[ref]
