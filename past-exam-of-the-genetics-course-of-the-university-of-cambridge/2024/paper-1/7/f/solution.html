The structural implication is a spectrum of arrangements, not a universal regular fibre. Local nucleosome packing, irregular accessible chains, compact domains and interactions between differently modified regions can coexist, and their balance depends on preparation and cellular context. Nucleosome breathing changes the sites available for intermolecular contacts and hence the connectivity of an array assembly; it is not merely a uniform change in the size of a rigid bead.[ref]
Reconstituted-array experiments separate contributions that are difficult to disentangle in nuclei. Fluorescent arrays, microscopy, droplet fusion and molecular exchange measurements test condensation and dynamics; changing tails, linker organization or acetylation tests the interaction mechanism. Bryan A. Gibson and colleagues found that histone H1 and nucleosome spacing modulate condensation, while acetylation and reader proteins can produce different condensed states.[ref]
In cells, combine imaging with contact maps and accessibility assays. Hi-C can reveal preferential contacts and chromatin compartments, but does not establish liquid behaviour. Fluorescence recovery after photobleaching measures recovery kinetics, but recovery alone does not prove equilibrium liquid-liquid demixing. Conversely, an in-vitro droplet is not proof that every nuclear domain forms by the same mechanism. The strongest case combines material behaviour with controlled molecular perturbations and measurements of the resulting organization.
