Overmodulation (m>1)

 Home Electrical engineering Amplitude modulation
OurBigBook.com Words: 147
For this single-tone AM model, overmodulation means m>1. The signed factor q(t) becomes negative whenever cos(Ωt)<−1/m. On such intervals,
Aq(t)cos(ωc​t)=A∣q(t)∣cos(ωc​t+π).
(7)
The sign reversal is a π radian shift of the carrier's wave phase. At each zero of q, the wave envelope vanishes and that phase is undefined. The waveform itself is continuous: the formula has not clipped it or created extra spectral lines.
At m=1.4=7/5, sign changes occur at message phases Ωt=arccos(−5/7) and 2π−arccos(−5/7) within each cycle. At the trough Ωt=π, the desired signed factor is −0.4, whereas its nonnegative wave envelope is 0.4A. Ideal envelope recovery followed by subtraction of A would give −0.6A there, rather than the desired message voltage −1.4A. This is loss of polarity in the detector, not loss of the message from the full modulated signal. Actual transmitter clipping is a separate signal distortion that can generate additional spectral components.
