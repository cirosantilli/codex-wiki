One-independent bond percolation

Words: 231 Articles: 2
Dependent percolation OurBigBook.com
A bond percolation law is one-independent if the bond states in any two sets with disjoint endpoint sets are independent as collections. In particular, states of bonds in a matching in a graph are mutually independent. The law need not be invariant under translations or positively associated.

Separated-block percolation renormalization

Words: 86
One-independent bond percolation OurBigBook.com
Place side-2n square lattice boxes around the points 3nv, v∈Z2. The width-2n rectangle joining adjacent boxes has length 5n. Declare a coarse bond open when its long rectangle is crossed and each endpoint box is crossed in the transverse direction. Nonincident coarse bonds use disjoint fine-bond sets, while incident good bonds give connected open paths inside their common endpoint box. When fixed-aspect crossing probabilities tend to one, these coarse bond probabilities tend to one. The Peierls circuit bound for one-independent percolation then yields an infinite fine-lattice open cluster.

Peierls circuit bound for one-independent percolation (P(specified closed dual circuit of length ℓ)≤qℓ/4)

Words: 104
One-independent bond percolation OurBigBook.com
If each bond has closed probability at most q in one-independent bond percolation on the square lattice, a set of ℓ bonds contains a matching in a graph of size at least ℓ/4: divide horizontal bonds by the parity of their left endpoint and vertical bonds by the parity of their lower endpoint. Each of the four classes is a matching. Mutual independence in the largest class proves the circuit bound. There are at most 4ℓ3ℓ dual circuits of length ℓ surrounding the origin. For sufficiently small q, the sum of these circuit probabilities is less than one, so the origin has a positive chance of belonging to an infinite percolation cluster.

Monochromatic line-graph percolation counterexample

Words: 141
Dependent percolation OurBigBook.com
Color the vertices of a periodic graph independently red or blue with equal probabilities, and declare a vertex of its line graph open exactly when the corresponding edge has equal-colored endpoints. The resulting site percolation has marginal 1/2 and is a finite-range dependent random field: sets at line-graph distance greater than one use disjoint color inputs. Red and blue open clusters cannot join. If the original graph has an infinite red component and an infinite blue component, its line graph consequently has at least two infinite percolation clusters. One explicit amenable graph with that property is obtained by replacing each square-lattice vertex by a complete graph on ten vertices, joining neighboring blocks completely. Blocks containing both colors have independent probability 511/512; a Peierls argument proves that they percolate, and their infinite cluster carries both a red and a blue component. Thus finite-range dependence, even with all site marginals nondegenerate, does not substitute for finite energy in percolation uniqueness.

Cluster weight in the random-cluster model (q)

Words: 44
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
The multiplicative factor q assigned to each connected component of a graph in a finite random-cluster measure. At fixed edge probability, the weight of a configuration with k components includes qk. For q=1 this reduces to independent bond percolation; increasing q at fixed p, for q≥1, suppresses increasing events by random-cluster weight monotonicity.

Random-cluster critical probability (pc​(q))

Words: 72 Articles: 1
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
The threshold for the origin to have an infinite percolation cluster under a consistently chosen infinite-volume random-cluster measure, conventionally the wired measure: pc​(q)=inf{p:θw(p,q)>0}. The definition alone makes no assertion about percolation exactly at the threshold.
Monotonicity of random-cluster critical probability in cluster weight (q1​≤q2​⟹pc​(q1​)≤pc​(q2​))
Words: 39
Random-cluster critical probability OurBigBook.com
The finite-volume random-cluster weight monotonicity passes to consistently wired or free infinite-volume measures for finite increasing connection events. Decreasing these events to origin-to-infinity connectivity yields θ(p,q1​)≥θ(p,q2​) for q1​≤q2​. The corresponding supercritical parameter sets are nested, and hence pc​(q1​)≤pc​(q2​).

Random-cluster weight monotonicity (q1​≤q2​⟹μp,q1​​⪰μp,q2​​)

Words: 51
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
For fixed p∈(0,1) and 1≤q1​≤q2​, the random-cluster measure at q1​ stochastically dominates that at q2​. In Holley's condition, Bernoulli factors cancel. Put a=k(S)−k(S∪T) and b=k(S∩T)−k(T); supermodularity of graph component count gives b≥a≥0, and the weight ratio is (q2​/q1​)aq2b−a​≥1. The comparison also holds with the same boundary wiring in both measures.

Self-dual parameter of the random-cluster model (psd​(q)=q​/(1+q​))

Planar random-cluster duality relates edge odds by [p/(1−p)][p∗/(1−p∗)]=q. The fixed point p=p∗ has odds q​, giving the displayed parameter. A self-dual parameter does not by itself establish a critical threshold on an arbitrary planar graph. At this parameter the symmetric cluster weight at the self-dual parameter makes the primal-dual cluster counts explicit.

Random-cluster single-edge conditional probability (ϕ(ω(e)=1∣ωE∖e​)∈{p, p/(p+q(1−p))})

Words: 56
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
For an edge with endpoints already connected without that edge, including any boundary wiring, the conditional open probability is p. Otherwise opening the edge merges two components, so the conditional probability is p/(p+q(1−p)). For q≥1, this conditional probability increases when more other edges are open or more boundary vertices are identified. This is the local mechanism of boundary monotonicity of the random-cluster measure.

Random-cluster boundary condition (ξ partitions ∂Λ)

Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
A boundary condition for a finite-box random-cluster measure is a partition of the boundary vertices. All vertices in one block are identified before counting open components; the identifications carry no Bernoulli edge factors. The weight is po(ω)(1−p)∣E∣−o(ω)qkξ​(ω). A coarser partition makes more identifications. General partitions need not have a planar realization.
Boundary monotonicity of the random-cluster measure (ξ⪯ζ ⟹ ϕΛ,p,qξ​⪯ϕΛ,p,qζ​(q≥1))
Words: 75
Random-cluster boundary condition OurBigBook.com
Here ξ⪯ζ means each block of ξ lies in a block of ζ, and the measure order is stochastic domination of probability measures. The random-cluster single-edge conditional probability is increasing in both exterior open edges and wiring for q≥1. Couple two heat-bath Markov chains by identical update edges and uniform random variables, starting from ordered states. Wired random-cluster boundary condition (ξ={∂Λ})
Words: 189 Articles: 3
Random-cluster boundary condition OurBigBook.com
All boundary vertices are identified into one vertex for component counting. This is the most wired random-cluster boundary condition on a fixed boundary, and maximizes increasing-event probabilities when q≥1 by boundary monotonicity of the random-cluster measure.
Infinite-volume wired random-cluster measure (ϕp,q1​)
Words: 162 Articles: 2
Wired random-cluster boundary condition OurBigBook.com
For q≥1, finite random-cluster measures with all boundary graph vertices identified have a weak infinite-volume limit of a sequence. On a regular lattice it is invariant under translations and lattice reflections and has positive association of the random-cluster model. Its conditional single-edge open probability lies between p/(p+q(1−p)) and p. Connection events can be approximated by increasing finite-volume path events, so their positive-association inequalities pass to the limit of a sequence.

Reflection comparison of random-cluster connections
Words: 66
Random-cluster inverse correlation length OurBigBook.com
Choose a graph vertex x=(n,x2​,…,xd​) on the boundary of a lattice box. Translation and reflection invariance identify the probabilities of 0↔x and x↔2ne1​. Their intersection implies 0↔2ne1​, so positive association of random variables gives C2n​≥ϕ(0↔x)2. A union bound and lattice symmetries choose such an x with probability at least the box-boundary connection probability divided by the number of boundary graph vertices. This polynomial factor disappears in the exponential rate, identifying boundary and axial inverse correlation lengths.
Free random-cluster boundary condition (ξ={{v}:v∈∂Λ})

Random-cluster boundary condition OurBigBook.com
The boundary partition has singleton blocks, so no different boundary vertices are identified. Open component counting is the ordinary counting in the finite graph, including isolated vertices. This is the least wired random-cluster boundary condition.

Positive association of the random-cluster model

Words: 25
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
For 0≤p≤1 and q≥1, the FKG inequality implies that increasing functions of a random-cluster configuration have nonnegative covariance. The conclusion can fail for 0<q<1.

Uniform connected-subgraph limit of the random-cluster model

Words: 42
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
On a finite connected graph, fixing p=1/2 and sending q↓0 makes the random-cluster model converge to the uniform law on connected spanning subgraphs: the factor qk(ω) selects the minimum possible component count k=1, while all surviving configurations have equal remaining weight.

Uniform spanning-tree limit of the random-cluster model

Words: 43
Random-cluster model OurBigBook.com
If p,q↓0 with q/p→0, the random-cluster model on a finite connected graph converges to the uniform spanning tree. Relative to a tree, an acyclic configuration with k>1 components pays a factor (q/p)k−1, while every additional cycle pays a factor asymptotic to p.

The Edwards-Sokal coupling assigns one common random spin to every random-cluster component. For q=2 it couples the random-cluster model to the Ising model.

Finite-range dependent random field
