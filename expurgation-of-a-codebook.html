Expurgation of a codebook

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Coding theory Channel coding Channel code
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
Expurgation discards messages with unusually high conditional decoding error. If a codebook with 2M equally likely messages has average error at most ϵ, at least M messages have individual error at most 2ϵ, by Markov's inequality. Retaining those messages and restricting the decoder's candidates gives a codebook with maximal error at most 2ϵ, provided removal of candidates cannot spoil a previously successful decoding. This is the case for bounded-distance decoding with errors on ambiguity, and for a decoder that retains its former decisions whenever the decoded message remains.
For a sequence of block lengths n, halving a book changes its logarithmic rate by 1/n, which tends to zero. Thus a vanishing average-error bound from a random coding construction can be converted into a vanishing maximal decoding error probability at the same limiting rate. The probabilistic construction first supplies a deterministic book with sufficiently small average error; expurgation is a subsequent deterministic step.
