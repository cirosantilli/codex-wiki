Internal-force cancellation in system momentum (dP/dt=Fext​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Force Newton's laws of motion Newton's third law
OurBigBook.com Words: 211
For a fixed collection of two constant-mass bodies in an inertial frame, separate their mutual forces from the resultant external force on each:
dtdpA​​=FB on A​+FA,ext​,dtdpB​​=FA on B​+FB,ext​.
(19)
Adding the two Newton's second law equations and using Newton's third law gives
P=pA​+pB​,dtdP​=FA,ext​+FB,ext​.
(20)
This is the proof of internal-force cancellation in system momentum. For more bodies, each internal force cancels with its partner once both appear in the sum. A boundary that excludes one interacting body leaves its force in the external force sum. A massive connecting spring must also be included, or its own changing momentum accounted for; its two endpoint forces are not automatically one third-law interaction pair between the carts.
Zero resultant external force gives momentum conservation; complete absence of external forces is sufficient but unnecessary. For constant total mass M, the centre of mass has velocity V=P/M and obeys MdV/dt=Fext​. Internal interactions can move the parts apart while leaving the centre of mass moving uniformly.
Additional external forces do not invalidate the identified mutual pair. At the 4N instant above, suppose a hand also exerts +3N on A. The mutual forces remain −4N and +4N, but the net forces become −1N on A and +4N on B. Consequently aA​=−1m/s2, aB​=+2m/s2, dP/dt=+3N, and the centre of mass has acceleration +1m/s2. Pair equality by itself does not promise constant total momentum.
