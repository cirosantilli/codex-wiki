Negative part of a finite convex function is Lipschitz (∣f−(x)−f−(y)∣≤L∣x−y∣)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Convex function
OurBigBook.com Words: 131
For a finite convex function f:R→R, its negative part f−=max(−f,0) is globally Lipschitz continuous. The negative sublevel set is an interval. If bounded, local Lipschitz continuity of finite convex functions bounds the slopes on its closure. If it is a left ray, any negative slope there would force f positive far to the left by the supporting-line inequality; all slopes on that ray are therefore nonnegative and bounded above near its finite endpoint. For a right ray the analogous slopes are nonpositive and bounded below. If f is negative everywhere, convexity and boundedness above force it to be constant. These cases bound the slopes of f− globally, including its zero extension outside the interval. In particular, integrability of f(Z) implies integrability of the negative part of every fixed translate f(Z+x).
