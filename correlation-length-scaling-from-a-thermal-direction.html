Correlation-length scaling from a thermal direction (ν=1/yt​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Critical phenomenon Renormalization group
OurBigBook.com Words: 310
Add a thermal scaling coordinate t, with its critical value subtracted, satisfying
dℓdt​=yt​t,t(ℓ)=byt​t0​,yt​>0.
(52)
This is a relevant direction of a fixed point. A thermal scaling coordinate agrees to leading order with a nonzero multiple of reduced temperature; in a general model it can mix several bare parameters. An ordering conjugate field is held at its critical value. The combined stability matrix at (g,t)=(ϵ,0) has eigenvalues −ϵ and yt​. Attraction along g therefore does not make the critical point attractive against thermal detuning. The critical surface in this example is t=0.
Let X=ξ/a denote the correlation length in microscopic lattice units. A renormalization-group transformation of length factor b gives
X(t0​,g0​)=bX(byt​t0​,g(logb)).
(53)
Indeed, a tail with exponential envelope exp(−r/ξ) becomes exp[−r′/(ξ/b)] under r′=r/b. Choose the stopping scale b∗​=∣t0​∣−1/yt​ so that the transformed thermal coordinate is +1 or −1. If the stopped correlation length tends to a finite nonzero value C±​ as the irrelevant coupling approaches g∗​, then
aξ​∼C±​∣t0​∣−1/yt​,ν=yt​1​.
(54)
The limit requires ∣t0​∣≪1 and enough room between a, ξ, and the sample size. Singular dependence on a dangerously irrelevant coupling or a marginal operator needs separate analysis. On an ordered branch, use the connected correlation function in a pure thermodynamic phase.
For a completely specified illustrative scaling model choose yt​=3/2 and X=C+​t−2/3 for t>0. Then t=10−3 gives X=100C+​, whereas t=10−6 gives X=104C+​. With b=4, t′=8t and X(t′)=X(t)/4. Models with C+​=1 and C+​=2 have different lengths but identical curves after plotting X/C+​ against t. This collapse checks the chosen scaling law; agreement of real data would test that law, rather than follow from this example. Neither ϵ=1/5 nor yt​=3/2 is being fitted to a three-dimensional Ising model.
