Failure-probability budget (P(⋂i​Fic​)≥max{0,1−∑i​δi​})

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Boole's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 342
A failure-probability budget assigns nonnegative numbers δi​ to a finite or countable family of failure events Fi​ in a common probability space, with verified bounds P(Fi​)≤δi​. The union bound guarantees that the probability of any failure is at most min{1,∑i​δi​}. By De Morgan's laws and the complement rule of probability, the probability that every requirement succeeds obeys
P(⋂i​Fic​)=1−P(⋃i​Fi​)≥max{0,1−∑i​δi​}.
(66)
Thus ∑i​δi​≤δ guarantees simultaneous success with probability at least 1−δ, even when all failure events depend on the same observations. The numbers δi​ are upper allowances; adding them is not a calculation of the exact failure probability.
For three requirements, give each a failure-probability budget δi​=1/20. The total budget is 3/20, so simultaneous success has probability at least 17/20. On a sample space of twenty equally likely outcomes, let F1​={1}, F2​={2}, and F3​={3}. These disjoint events attain the budget and the success bound. If instead all three failure events equal {1}, their individual probabilities are unchanged, but exact failure has probability 1/20 and simultaneous success has probability 19/20. This second model shows how overlap can leave the guarantee conservative.
For n requirements, an equal allocation δi​=δ/n is convenient; unequal allocations also work when some requirements are harder to satisfy. For infinitely many requirements indexed by i≥1, the allocation δi​=δ/2i uses total budget δ by the geometric series. A fixed positive allowance for every requirement instead gives an infinite sum and no positive simultaneous-success guarantee from the union bound. The individual probability bounds must cover all the failure events being combined: omitting possible failure modes leaves the resulting guarantee about the listed requirements only. The same addition of error allowances underlies the Bonferroni correction in simultaneous hypothesis tests.
