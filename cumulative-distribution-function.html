Cumulative distribution function (FX​(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 15
The cumulative distribution function of a real random variable X is
FX​(x)=P(X≤x).
(21)
It records the probability accumulated up to a threshold. It is a nondecreasing function and is right-continuous; its limits are zero at −∞ and one at +∞. These properties follow from ordered threshold events, continuity from above of a measure, and continuity from below of a measure. A cumulative distribution function determines the entire probability distribution on the real line. [ref].
For a<b,
P(a<X≤b)=FX​(b)−FX​(a),P(X=x)=FX​(x)−FX​(x−),
(22)
where FX​(x−)=limt↑x​FX​(t) is its left-hand limit. The second formula measures a jump. Thus P(a≤X≤b)=FX​(b)−FX​(a−); changing an endpoint is harmless only when its point probability is zero.
For the finite example under probability distribution, FX​(x) is 0 below zero, 0.2 on [0,1), 0.7 on [1,3), and 1 on [3,∞). In particular FX​(1)=0.7, but FX​(1−)=0.2, so the jump gives P(X=1)=0.5. For the density example, FY​(y)=y2 on [0,1], and its increase from 1/4 to 3/4 is 9/16−1/16=1/2.
When an absolutely continuous probability distribution has probability density function f, its cumulative distribution function is F(x)=∫−∞x​f(t)dt and F′=f almost everywhere. A continuous cumulative distribution function by itself does not guarantee such a density: the Cantor distribution is an important exception.
