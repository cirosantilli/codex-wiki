Rotational monitoring of deuterium exchange

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation Rotational spectroscopy Fourier transform microwave spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 226
A distinct experiment in An Automated, Highly Selective Reaction Monitoring Instrument Using Molecular Rotational Resonance Spectroscopy by Ann Adele Byars and others (Figure 1 and Figure 3) tracked exchange of hydrogen for deuterium in ethyl acetoacetate mixed with deuterated methanol. A pump circulated chemical solution through a valve; a calibrated loop injected aliquots into a temperature-controlled inlet, held at room temperature for this exchange experiment. Neon carried the vaporized sample to a cavity spectrometer. Separately recorded broadband spectra and fitted rotational Hamiltonians identified transitions for the four isotopic forms before targeted monitoring.[ref]
The readout was each assigned line's signal over reaction time, normalized by the sum of the four signals. Transitions with the same quantum numbers reduced response-factor differences. A solvent wash addressed observed sample carryover. The signals distinguished the unsubstituted, two singly substituted, and doubly substituted forms. Only one gas-phase structural form was detected; the authors explicitly caution that their readout does not recover the structural chemical equilibrium in chemical solution.[ref]
This example uses nonlinear molecules, so neither the single-constant linear rigid rotor nor the synthetic comb above is a fit to its spectrum. Its connection to the model is the sensitivity of moments of inertia to isotope placement. Line position helps assign a species; signal size additionally depends on sample transfer, initial quantum state populations and instrument response. Matching quantum numbers is a useful control, not a proof of identical response factors. These distinctions explain why resolving species and determining their concentrations are separate inference steps.
