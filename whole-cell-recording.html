Whole-cell recording

 Home Biology Neuroscience Patch clamp
OurBigBook.com Words: 740 Articles: 5
Whole-cell recording establishes intracellular electrical access by rupturing the membrane under a sealed patch pipette while retaining the gigaohm seal. Under adequate voltage clamp, the measured electric current combines contributions from accessible ion channels, membrane capacitance and seal leak current. Current clamp instead follows membrane potential while supplying a chosen electric current. Whole-cell recording is useful for linking a biological cell's total electrical response to its ion channels, but neither mode automatically controls distant dendrites or preserves the original cytosol.
In the 1981 methods demonstration, a bovine chromaffin biological cell was tested before and after patch rupture. Small voltage clamp steps produced a much larger capacitive transient after break-in. Integrating it gave 5pF for a 13μm cell; its decay indicated patch-clamp series resistance below 20MΩ. Depolarizing commands then revealed ionic electric currents. Separate current clamp recordings showed spontaneous action potentials. The comparison established access to the cell interior, rather than a claim that every pipette command equals membrane potential. Slower transients sometimes indicated incomplete rupture, and these small-cell results do not establish spatial control of a branched neuron.[ref]
