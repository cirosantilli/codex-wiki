First specify the perturbation: take fixed gas density, fixed ionic abundance and the same temperature-independent heating, so thermal stability requires dC/dT>0 at an equilibrium. Let θ=ΔE/kB​, ne​ be the collider density and nA​=n1​+n2​ the atomic density. A two-level optically thin balance is
n1​ne​q12​=n2​(A21​+ne​q21​),C=n2​A21​ΔE.
(14)
The critical density of an atomic transition is approximately A21​/q21​. At low density, each rare collisional excitation is followed by an escaping photon, so
C≃nA​ne​q12​ΔE∝T−1/2e−θ/T,dlnTdlnC​=−21​+Tθ​.
(15)
The isochoric thermal stability of a collisionally excited line therefore gives
T<2θ: stable;T>2θ: unstable.​
(16)
At T=2θ the linear restoring coefficient vanishes; the cooling maximum is marginal to first order and is not robust two-sided stability.
At high density, collisional de-excitation competes successfully with radiative decay, and collisions establish local thermodynamic equilibrium level populations. Detailed balance gives n2​/n1​=ge−θ/T, where g=g2​/g1​. The cooling is then
C=nA​A21​ΔEfu​(T),fu​=1+ge−θ/Tge−θ/T​,dlnTdlnfu​​=Tθ​(1−fu​)>0.
(17)
Thus the high-density fixed-density branch is stable for finite T, becoming weakly restoring as the upper fraction saturates at high temperature. It is incorrect to retain the low-density factor T−1/2 in the radiative cooling law once populations are thermalized.[ref]
The isobaric thermal stability of a two-level coolant is a distinct question. If the intended heating is a fixed rate per particle, then n∝T−1 and stability tests the cooling per particle. The low-density boundary becomes T<2θ/3; the high-density cooling per particle remains proportional to fu​, so remains stable. If instead heating is constant per volume, the isobaric low-density boundary is T<2θ/5, and the high-density stability condition is θ(1−fu​)/T>1. The source gives no pressure constraint or explicit heating normalization: the boxed 2θ result is the fixed-density answer, with these conditional alternatives stated to avoid asserting a universal boundary.
