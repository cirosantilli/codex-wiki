A spiral density wave is an organized density and gravitational-potential perturbation through which stars and gas can move; it is not generally a permanently labelled set of stars. Differential rotation winds a material arm rapidly. In a wave description, a perturbation has phase mϕ+∫kdr−ωt, with pattern speed Ωp​=ω/m distinct from the local stellar angular speed.
For a thin fluid disk the local tightly wound dispersion relation is
(ω−mΩ)2=κ2+cs2​k2−2πGΣ∣k∣.
(1)
Rotation and pressure resist self-gravity. A collisionless stellar disk requires its stellar response function instead of simply replacing cs​ by a dispersion; the familiar local axisymmetric stability parameter is Q⋆​=σR​κ/(3.36GΣ). This is distinct from the gas Qg​=cs​κ/(πGΣ).
Waves exchange angular momentum at Lindblad resonances and near corotation radius. Swing amplification can strengthen a leading disturbance as shear turns it into a trailing one. Bars or companions can force a coherent spiral; recurrent self-excited disturbances also occur. Thus a single indefinitely steady wave is one model, not a description established for every spiral galaxy. Gas compression and shocks along arms can organize star formation, while gravitational torques redistribute angular momentum. Arm brightness, recent star formation and stellar mass density need not trace precisely the same pattern.[ref]
