Across galactic disks the empirical Kennicutt–Schmidt law relates star formation rate surface density to gas surface density, ΣSFR​∝ΣgN​. A slope near 1.4 is a familiar integrated total-gas fit, while molecular-gas relations in ordinary disks can be closer to linear; the slope and normalization depend on tracer, averaging scale and environment. The gas-depletion time tdep​=Mg​/Ψ is the corresponding fuel-consumption scale, not a prediction that inflow and recycling cease.
Massive stars supply ionizing radiation, winds and supernova energy and momentum. This stellar feedback can disperse clouds, sustain turbulence and drive outflows, regulating rather than simply extinguishing all future star formation. Fresh accretion and recycled gas replenish the reservoir. UV and recombination-line emission trace young stars, while infrared emission traces absorbed and re-radiated light; extinction, stellar populations and the assumed initial mass function affect inferred rates. A galactic rate reflects supply, cloud formation and feedback together, rather than only the free-fall time of its densest gas.[ref]
