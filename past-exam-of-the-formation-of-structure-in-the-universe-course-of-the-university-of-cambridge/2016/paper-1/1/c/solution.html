Only the photon energy above the hydrogen ionization threshold initially becomes photoelectron kinetic energy. The stated mean therefore supplies
Eheat​=⟨hν⟩−hνLye​=kB​T∗​.
(11)
Let R be the local number of ionizations per volume per time. Photoionization equilibrium makes this equal to the recombination rate. If ⟨Erec​(T)⟩ is the mean kinetic energy removed per recombination, thermal balance reduces to
RkB​T∗​=R⟨Erec​(T)⟩,⟨Erec​(Teq​)⟩=kB​T∗​.​
(12)
Neither the ionization threshold nor the density remains in this event-by-event balance. The threshold energy is stored as atomic ionization energy and returned in recombination radiation; it is not a net thermal input. This separation between photoelectron heating and kinetic energy lost by recombining electrons is the relevant cooling balance.[ref]
In the elementary mean-thermal-energy approximation, a captured electron removes 23​kB​T. This gives the concise estimate
Teq​≃32​T∗​if ⟨Erec​⟩=23​kB​T.​
(13)
That coefficient is a stated approximation, not a universal recombination identity. Real recombination weights the electron distribution by its velocity-dependent capture rate. If the chosen cooling model instead writes ⟨Erec​⟩=ηrec​(T)kB​T, the precise answer is ηrec​(Teq​)Teq​=T∗​. The source supplies no recombination cooling coefficient or capture kernel from which a unique more accurate coefficient can be computed. Its emitted-photon average is also being used as the average of the absorbed ionizing photons; spectral filtering would require a different weighted average.
