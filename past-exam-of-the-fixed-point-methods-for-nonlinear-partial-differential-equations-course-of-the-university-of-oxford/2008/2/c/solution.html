Work in the Banach space C([0,1]) with its supremum norm, and take the nonempty closed bounded convex set M={u:100≤u(t)≤121 for all t}. Define
(Tu)(t)=101+∫0t​(∣u(s)∣​+1)ds.
(6)
For u∈M the absolute value is unnecessary, and 101≤Tu(t)≤101+12=113≤121​. Thus T:M→M. Also, for a,b≥100,
∣a​−b​∣=a​+b​∣a−b∣​≤201​∣a−b∣,
(7)
so ∥Tu−Tv∥∞​≤201​∥u−v∥∞​ and in particular T is continuous.
The image is uniformly bounded, and ∣Tu(t)−Tu(s)∣≤12∣t−s∣, so it is equicontinuous. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem says that a uniformly bounded equicontinuous family in C([0,1]) has relatively compact closure in the uniform norm. Hence T is compact, and the Schauder fixed-point theorem supplies u=Tu​, the required solution. In fact the displayed contraction estimate also lets the contraction mapping theorem give uniqueness within M and convergence of the corresponding Picard iteration. The initial constant here is the printed integer 101.
