Let S+n−1​ be the nonnegative portion of the unit sphere specified in the paper. If Ax=0 for some x∈S+n−1​, then this nonzero vector is an eigenvector with eigenvalue zero, which already proves the assertion.
Otherwise Ax=0 for every x∈S+n−1​, and
T(x)=∣Ax∣Ax​
(12)
is a continuous map from S+n−1​ to itself. Indeed, every entry of Ax is nonnegative because A is a nonnegative matrix and every entry of x is nonnegative. By the supplied homeomorphism h:Bn−1→S+n−1​, the continuous map h−1Th maps the finite-dimensional closed unit ball into itself. The Brouwer fixed-point theorem supplies a fixed point, hence T(x∗​)=x∗​ for some x∗​∈S+n−1​. Therefore
Ax∗​=λx∗​,x∗​=0,λ=∣Ax∗​∣>0.​
(13)
Together with the zero case this proves that A has a nonnegative eigenvalue, with a nonnegative eigenvector. For n=1, the auxiliary closed unit ball is the one-point space B0 and the argument remains valid. No claim of strict positivity of every entry of the eigenvector is needed.
