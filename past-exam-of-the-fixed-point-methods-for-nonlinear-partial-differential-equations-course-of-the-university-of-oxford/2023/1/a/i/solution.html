The Banach fixed-point theorem states that a contraction mapping T:X→X of a nonempty complete metric space (X,d) has a unique fixed point, and iterations of a map xn+1​=T(xn​) converge to it from every x0​∈X. Precisely, assume a single constant q∈[0,1) satisfies d(Tx,Ty)≤qd(x,y) for every x,y∈X.
Induction gives d(xn+1​,xn​)≤qnd(x1​,x0​). The triangle inequality and the geometric series therefore give, for m>n,
d(xm​,xn​)≤∑j=nm−1​qjd(x1​,x0​)≤1−qqn​d(x1​,x0​).
(1)
Thus (xn​) is a Cauchy sequence. By completeness it converges to some x∗​∈X. The contraction mapping is a continuous map, so Tx∗​=limn​Txn​=limn​xn+1​=x∗​. If y∗​ is another fixed point, then d(x∗​,y∗​)≤qd(x∗​,y∗​), forcing x∗​=y∗​. Letting m→∞ in the estimate also gives the contraction iteration error bound:
Tx∗​=x∗​,d(xn​,x∗​)≤1−qqn​d(x1​,x0​).​
(2)
If q=0, T is constant and the sequence reaches its unique fixed point after the first step; this also removes any ambiguity about 00 in the displayed estimate at n=0.
