We apply the closed-bounded-domain version of the Schauder fixed-point theorem in the Banach space L2(Ω). Write m=∣Ω∣ and use G,T,CP​ from part (b)(ii). Choose
a=CP2​m1/2,b=CP2​m1/4,R=max{1,2a,4b2},C={w∈L2(Ω):w≥0 a.e., ∥w∥2​≤R}.
(27)
The set C is nonempty, bounded, closed and a convex set. For closedness of its positivity condition, if wj​≥0 and wj​→w in L2, then pointwise w−≤∣w−wj​∣, whence ∥w−∥2​→0.
The weak maximum principle already gives Tw≥0. Our estimates give, for w∈C,
∥Tw∥2​≤CP2​(m1/2+m1/4∥w∥21/2​)≤a+bR​≤R/2+R/2=R.
(28)
Thus T(C)⊂C.
To prove continuity, use the pointwise square root estimate ∣s​−t​∣2≤∣s−t∣ for s,t≥0. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then gives
∥w​−z​∥22​≤∥w−z∥1​≤m1/2∥w−z∥2​,∥Tw−Tz∥2​≤CP2​m1/4∥w−z∥21/2​.
(29)
Hence T is a continuous map on C, even though the square root need not have a bounded derivative near zero.
Finally,
∥∇Tw∥2​≤CP​(m1/2+m1/4R​)(w∈C).
(30)
The Rellich-Kondrachov theorem says that H01​(Ω) embeds compactly into L2(Ω) on a bounded smooth domain. For n>2, 2<2n/(n−2), and for n=2 every finite target exponent is available, so the required compact embedding holds in every dimension in the question. Thus T(C) is relatively compact in L2(Ω).
All hypotheses of the Schauder fixed-point theorem are met. It gives u=Tu∈C, and part (b)(ii) converts this fixed point into the required weak solution:
There exists u∈H01​(Ω), u≥0 a.e., with ∫Ω​∇u⋅∇vdx=∫Ω​(1+u​)vdx∀v∈H01​(Ω).​
(31)
Only existence is needed. The construction does not assume that Ω is small or that T is a contraction mapping.
