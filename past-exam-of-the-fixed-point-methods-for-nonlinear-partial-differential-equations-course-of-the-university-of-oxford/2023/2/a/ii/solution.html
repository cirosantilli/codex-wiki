Let E=affK be the affine hull and put k=dimE≤n. If k=0, K is a singleton and is homeomorphic to B0. Suppose k>0. Choose k+1 affinely independent points of K spanning E. Their simplex is contained in K because K is a convex set, and its barycentre a has a neighbourhood in E contained in that simplex. Identifying E−a with Rk, put D=K−a. For some 0<r≤R<∞,
rBk⊂D⊂RBk.
(15)
Here the inner inclusion follows from that neighbourhood, and the outer one follows from the compactness of K.
Use the Minkowski functional
p(z)=inf{t>0:z∈tD}.
(16)
The inclusions imply ∣z∣/R≤p(z)≤∣z∣/r. The fact that D is a convex set implies p(z+w)≤p(z)+p(w): if z∈sD and w∈tD, then (z+w)/(s+t) is a convex combination of points of D. Also p(tz)=tp(z) for t≥0. Consequently
p(z)−p(w)≤p(z−w)≤∣z−w∣/r,∣p(z)−p(w)∣≤∣z−w∣/r.
(17)
Thus p is a continuous function. Since D is closed and contains zero, D={p≤1}: the forward inclusion is immediate, and for the reverse inclusion one takes a limit of z/tj​∈D with tj​→p(z)≤1, using positive homogeneity and the fact that D is a convex set. For z=0, p(z)>0, so this limiting argument has no division by zero.
Define a radial continuous map by
h(0)=a,h(tθ)=a+p(θ)t​θ(0<t≤1, ∣θ∣=1).
(18)
Each ray meets D in exactly {sθ:0≤s≤1/p(θ)}. Hence h is a bijection from Bk to K. Away from zero its continuity follows from that of p, and at zero ∣h(tθ)−a∣≤Rt. A continuous map that is a bijection from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism. Therefore
K≅Bk,k=dim(affK).​
(19)
Using the affine hull is essential: K can have empty interior in the ambient Rn.
