The contraction mapping theorem states that a self-map T of a nonempty complete metric space with a uniform contraction constant q<1 has a unique fixed point p. For every starting point, the iteration xn+1​=Txn​ converges to p, with d(xn​,p)≤qnd(x1​,x0​)/(1−q).
For the cosine fixed-point iteration, any real x0​ gives x1​∈[−1,1]. This closed interval is complete, and cosine maps it into [cos1,1]⊆[−1,1]. The mean value theorem gives
∣cosx−cosy∣≤sin(1)∣x−y∣(x,y∈[−1,1]),
(1)
with sin(1)<1. Apply the contraction mapping theorem to the tail starting at x1​. Therefore xn​→p,p=cosp​ for every real initial value. Numerically p≈0.739085. The global real-line derivative bound is only one; entering the invariant interval supplies the strict uniform bound needed by the theorem.

The Brouwer fixed-point theorem says that every continuous map T:BR​(0)→BR​(0) has a fixed point. To use the supplied outward vector field zero theorem, first extend the construction to all of Rn. Let
PR​(x)={x,Rx/∣x∣,​∣x∣≤R,∣x∣>R,​g(x)=x−T(PR​(x)).
(2)
The radial retraction onto a closed ball PR​ is continuous, so g is a continuous vector field on the required whole space. On ∣x∣=R, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
g(x)⋅x=R2−T(x)⋅x≥R2−∣T(x)∣R≥0.
(3)
The supplied theorem gives g(x0​)=0 with ∣x0​∣≤R. There PR​(x0​)=x0​, hence T(x0​)=x0​​. This explicit extension avoids presuming that the original map T was defined outside the ball.

A nonlinear compact operator is a continuous map that sends bounded sets of its domain to relatively compact subsets of the target space; linearity is not part of this general definition.
For the specified diagonal operator on sequence space on l2 sequence space,
∥Tx∥22​=∑i≥1​i2∣xi​∣2​≤∑i≥1​∣xi​∣2,
(4)
so it is well-defined, linear and bounded. Let TN​ retain only its first N output coordinates. This is a finite-rank operator, and
∥T−TN​∥=N+11​⟶0.​
(5)
The upper bound follows by estimating each tail multiplier by 1/(N+1); equality follows on the unit coordinate vector eN+1​. Finite-rank operators between Banach spaces are compact, and a limit of compact linear operators in operator norm is compact, the norm-closedness of compact operators. Therefore T is compact​.

The Schauder fixed-point theorem states that a continuous compact self-map of a nonempty closed bounded convex set in a Banach space has a fixed point. Equivalently in this bounded-domain version, it suffices for the image of the map to be relatively compact. The theorem guarantees existence, without requiring a contraction estimate or promising uniqueness.

Work in the Banach space C([0,1]) with its supremum norm, and take the nonempty closed bounded convex set M={u:100≤u(t)≤121 for all t}. Define
(Tu)(t)=101+∫0t​(∣u(s)∣​+1)ds.
(6)
For u∈M the absolute value is unnecessary, and 101≤Tu(t)≤101+12=113≤121​. Thus T:M→M. Also, for a,b≥100,
∣a​−b​∣=a​+b​∣a−b∣​≤201​∣a−b∣,
(7)
so ∥Tu−Tv∥∞​≤201​∥u−v∥∞​ and in particular T is continuous.
The image is uniformly bounded, and ∣Tu(t)−Tu(s)∣≤12∣t−s∣, so it is equicontinuous. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem says that a uniformly bounded equicontinuous family in C([0,1]) has relatively compact closure in the uniform norm. Hence T is compact, and the Schauder fixed-point theorem supplies u=Tu​, the required solution. In fact the displayed contraction estimate also lets the contraction mapping theorem give uniqueness within M and convergence of the corresponding Picard iteration. The initial constant here is the printed integer 101.

One-dimensional Sobolev embedding gives a continuous representative for each v∈H01​(0,l), with zero endpoint values, so the pointwise obstacle condition is meaningful. The positive part g+​=max{g,0} is Lipschitz and vanishes near both endpoints because g(0),g(l)<0. It therefore belongs to H01​ and satisfies g+​≥g, proving that M is nonempty.
If v,w∈M and t∈[0,1], then (1−t)v+tw≥g, so M is a convex set. If vn​→v in H01​, the estimate ∥vn​−v∥∞​≤l​∥vn′​−v′∥2​ gives uniform convergence. Passing to the limit in vn​(x)≥g(x) shows v∈M. Thus the admissible set is nonempty, closed and convex.
