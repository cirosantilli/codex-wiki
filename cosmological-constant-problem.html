Cosmological constant problem

 Home Physics Branches of physics Cosmology Dark energy Cosmological constant
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
The cosmological constant problem is the unexplained smallness of the gravitationally observable vacuum energy compared with the contributions expected from quantum field theory. In units with ℏ=c=1, an ultraviolet cutoff ΛUV​ generically gives contributions of order ΛUV4​, whereas the observed dark-energy density is of order 10−47GeV4. The Einstein equation depends on the sum of a bare cosmological constant and these contributions; subtracting a constant from a nongravitational Hamiltonian does not remove its gravitational effect. Exact global supersymmetry gives zero energy to a supersymmetric vacuum, but supersymmetry breaking generally reintroduces a much larger positive scale.
