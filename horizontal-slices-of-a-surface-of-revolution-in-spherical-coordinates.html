Horizontal slices of a surface of revolution in spherical coordinates (dV=π[r(θ)sinθ]2∣r′(θ)cosθ−r(θ)sinθ∣dθ)

A meridian r=r(θ) has cylindrical radius ρ=rsinθ and height z=rcosθ. On a branch with monotone height and filled horizontal disk sections, the volume between nearby horizontal planes is dV=πρ2∣dz∣, giving the formula. The conditions matter: a meridian that folds in height or bounds annular sections requires separate branches and inner-radius subtraction. A spherical angular wedge instead has volume (2π/3)r(θ)3sinθdθ when the solid fills each radial segment. Both describe volumes, but they partition the solid differently.
