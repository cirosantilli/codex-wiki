Eulerian trail

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Eulerian graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 279 Articles: 1
An Eulerian trail, also called an Eulerian path, is a trail in a graph traversing every edge exactly once. The name Eulerian path does not require a graph path: vertices may repeat. A Hamiltonian path instead visits every vertex exactly once, and may omit edges. An Eulerian trail may omit isolated vertices, so these are different coverage requirements.
For a finite undirected multigraph with at least one edge, allowing self-loops, an Eulerian trail exists exactly when the edge support of a graph is a connected graph and there are zero or two vertices of odd vertex degree. In the two-odd-vertex case they must be the two distinct endpoints; in the zero-odd-vertex case every Eulerian trail is an Euler circuit. Count a self-loop twice in the vertex degree but once in the trail, and count each parallel edge separately.[ref]
The parity obstruction is local: each intermediate visit pairs an arriving edge with a departing edge. Only the distinct endpoints can have an unpaired incidence. The handshaking lemma, ∑v∈V​deg(v)=2∣E∣, also shows that the number of vertices with odd vertex degree is even. Sufficiency in the two-endpoint case reduces to the Euler circuit criterion: add one extra edge between the odd vertices, construct an Euler circuit, rotate its record to start with the extra edge, then delete that edge. The remaining trail has the required endpoints.
For the Seven Bridges of Königsberg multigraph, the vertex degrees at A,B,C,D are (5,3,3,3), so no Eulerian trail exists. Remove the unique edge AD: the vertex degrees become (4,3,3,2), and a six-edge Eulerian trail runs from B to C. Label the parallel edges p1​,p2​ between A,B and q1​,q2​ between A,C; label BD by r and CD by s. A complete record is B,p1​,A,q1​,C,s,D,r,B,p2​,A,q2​,C. It revisits A,B,C while using every remaining edge once.
