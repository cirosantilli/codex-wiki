Slip length (b=ℓs​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Navier slip boundary condition
OurBigBook.com Words: 797 Articles: 2
The slip length measures tangential wall mobility in the Navier slip boundary condition. Where the wall shear rate is nonzero,
b=∂n​uurel​​.
(17)
The numerator is a velocity and the denominator has units s−1, so b has units of length. Geometrically, take a local inward normal coordinate s, with the wall at s=0. The tangent line to the relative velocity profile is
urel,lin​(s)=urel​(0)+s∂n​u(0)=(b+s)∂n​u(0).
(18)
It reaches zero at s=−b, a fictitious distance b inside the solid. This extrapolation does not assert that fluid exists there. At a moving wall, extrapolate to the wall's velocity, rather than to zero laboratory velocity. If both the wall shear rate and relative velocity vanish, their ratio cannot determine b.
The useful comparison is b/H, where H is the channel gap. A small absolute slip length can noticeably change volumetric flow rate in a micrometre-scale channel. Measured effective slip length may summarize unresolved interfacial structure; it need not mean literal molecular sliding at every point. The review distinguishes intrinsic, apparent and effective slip and discusses how surface conditions affect measurements. [ref]
