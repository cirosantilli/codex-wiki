Shot noise counting model (Var(iT​)=qI/T)

 Home Electrical engineering Signal processing Signal Noise (signal processing) Shot noise
OurBigBook.com Words: 206
Let q>0 be the magnitude of each transferred electric charge, and let arrivals form a Homogeneous Poisson process of rate λ. Extend the stream over past and future times when considering stationary filtering. For electrons, q=e, the elementary charge. Choose the positive electric current direction to follow these transfers, so I=qλ>0 is its mean magnitude. If NT​ counts arrivals in a gate of duration T>0, then
NT​∼Poisson(λT),E[NT​]=Var(NT​)=λT.
(42)
The gate's transferred electric charge is QT​=qNT​, and its average electric current is iT​=QT​/T. Scaling the expected value and variance gives
E[iT​]=I,Var(QT​)=qIT,Var(iT​)=TqI​,IVar(iT​)​​=λT​1​.
(43)
More arrivals increase the absolute fluctuation but decrease its size relative to the mean. For an original count example with λT=4, the mean count and its variance are both 4, the standard deviation is 2, and the relative standard deviation is 1/2. The probability of no transfer is e−4≈0.01832; the probability of four is 44e−4/4!≈0.19537. A constant arrival rate does not prescribe four arrivals in every gate.
These are fluctuations of a time average, not the variance of an ideal instantaneous electric current. A model of point transfers has arbitrarily sharp impulses; it acquires a finite measured variance only after specifying a gate or linear filter. Actual collection pulses have finite duration and electrical circuits have finite response times.
