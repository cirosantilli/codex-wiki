Vapor-compression refrigeration

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Refrigerator
OurBigBook.com Words: 101
Vapor-compression refrigeration circulates a working fluid through cold and hot coils. In the cold coil, liquid becomes gas while absorbing heat from the refrigerated region. Compression supplies work and raises the pressure and temperature; the hot coil releases heat as the gas returns to liquid. A restriction reduces the pressure before the next cold-side passage. Changing the pressure changes the temperature of the phase transition, letting one circulating fluid receive and release heat at different temperatures. This physical implementation serves both refrigerators and heat pumps; its ordinary dissipative operation differs from the reversible Carnot cycle used to bound performance. [ref] [ref]
