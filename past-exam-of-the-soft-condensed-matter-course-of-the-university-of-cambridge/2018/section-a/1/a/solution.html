OurBigBook.com Words: 124
Assume steady fully developed laminar flow of a Newtonian fluid in a long circular tube, with no slip at a rigid wall. The Hagen-Poiseuille equation gives the volume flow rate
Q=8ηℓπR4Δp​,
(1)
where R is the lumen radius, ℓ the tube length, η the dynamic viscosity and Δp the pressure drop. If Δp, ℓ and η remain fixed, then
Qold​Qnew​​=(Rold​Rnew​​)4.
(2)
Thus doubling the flow requires
Rnew​=21/4Rold​≃1.189Rold​,Rold​ΔR​≃18.9%​.
(3)
This estimate neglects pulsation, arterial compliance, entrance losses, the detailed shape of a stenosis and changes in the rest of the circulatory resistance. It treats blood as a homogeneous Newtonian fluid and assumes that enlarging the artery does not change its applied pressure drop. These assumptions supply the intended fourth-power scaling; an actual circulation need not keep that pressure drop fixed.
