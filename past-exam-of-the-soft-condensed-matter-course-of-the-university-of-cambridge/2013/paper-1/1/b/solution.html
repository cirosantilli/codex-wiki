The tension is supplied by Stokes drag on the free bead. With bead radius b=0.5μm, the stipulated isolated-sphere approximation gives
f=6πηbU=2.83pN.​
(1)
The trap also balances drag on the trapped bead, but that bead's drag is not an additional force transmitted through the chain. The polymer contour length is L=Na=6μm. Use a room-temperature estimate T≃300K, consistent with the water-viscosity approximation imported from part (a); the expressions below retain the dependence on T.
For a theta solvent, Gaussian chain elasticity gives Fel​=3kB​TR2/(2Na2) and hence
Rθ​=3kB​TfNa2​=0.819(T300K​)μm.​
(2)
This is the mean extension along the force, rather than the unforced end-to-end distance of a polymer chain aN​=60nm. At 300K, fa/(kB​T)=0.410; the finite-link freely jointed chain result R=L[coth(fa/kB​T)−kB​T/(fa)] is 0.810μm, close to the Gaussian estimate.
The good-solvent force-extension scaling changes the elasticity. In the tensile blob regime, a blob has size ξt​=kB​T/f, contains g∼(ξt​/a)5/3 segments, and the aligned string of N/g blobs has length
Rgood​∼gN​ξt​=Na(kB​Tfa​)2/3≃3.31(T300K​)2/3μm.​
(3)
Only the scaling and order of magnitude of the good-solvent value are determined: solvent-dependent swelling amplitudes are not supplied. The nonlinear force law is the essential correction to extrapolating a weak-force coil spring.[ref]
To expose the unrealistic extrapolation, the unforced good-solvent size estimate is R0​∼aN3/5=151nm. Treating this coil as a linear spring with spring constant 3kB​T/R02​ predicts R≃5.17μm at 300K, although its required condition fR0​≪kB​T fails by a factor of about 103. Dropping the order-one factor three, as in the rough estimate k∼kB​T/R02​, even gives R∼15.5μm>L. An extension beyond the polymer contour length is an artefact of extending the coil's linear response outside its range. The tensile blob estimate avoids that violation but here ξt​≃1.46nm and g∼4.4, so it is only marginally within a many-segment-blob regime. finite extensibility of a polymer chain, chain drag and flow disturbance by both large beads further limit quantitative realism. The specified neglect of chain drag is a model assumption, not evidence that it is physically negligible.
