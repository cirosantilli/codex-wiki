Let ε=ε0​εr​ be the permittivity, β=(kB​T)−1, e>0 the elementary charge, and c the bulk salt formula-unit number density. The Boltzmann distribution gives
nMg​(r)=ce−2βeϕ(r),nCl​(r)=2ceβeϕ(r).
(44)
Consequently the exterior Poisson-Boltzmann equation is
r21​drd​(r2drdϕ​)=−ε2ec​(e−2βeϕ−eβeϕ),r>a.
(45)
For small Q, meaning 2βe∣ϕ(a)∣≪1, linearize the exponentials. The Debye–Hückel approximation gives
ρion​≃−6βe2cϕ,r21​(r2ϕ′)′=κ2ϕ,κ2=εkB​T6e2c​,λD​=κ−1.​
(46)
This is also κ2=βe2∑i​zi2​ni,∞​/ε: the divalent cation contributes 4c and the two monovalent anions contribute 2c. In terms of molar salt concentration C in molm−3, replace c by NA​C; if C is in molL−1, use c=103NA​C. The molar ionic strength is 3C in the same concentration units.
The boundary conditions are ϕ(∞)=0 and, by Gauss's law,
−εϕ′(a+)=4πa2Q​.
(47)
Here the surface charge is uniform and the spherically symmetric interior field is zero. Set u=rϕ; the radial equation reduces to u′′=κ2u. Decay excludes the growing exponential, so write ϕ=Be−κ(r−a)/r. Then ϕ′(a)=−B(1+κa)/a2, giving
ϕ(r)=4πεr(1+κa)Q​e−κ(r−a).​
(48)
It solves the linearized Poisson-Boltzmann equation, not the full nonlinear equation at finite Q. The radial ion profiles, consistently to first order, are
nMg​(r)≃c[1−2βeϕ(r)],nCl​(r)≃2c[1+βeϕ(r)].​
(49)
The exponential Boltzmann distribution formulas above express the full mean-field relation, but substituting a linearized potential into them is accurate only to the retained order. A useful electroneutrality check is
4π∫a∞​ρion​r2dr=−4πε[r2ϕ′]a∞​=−Q.
(50)
The result is the Debye-Hückel potential of a charged sphere.
