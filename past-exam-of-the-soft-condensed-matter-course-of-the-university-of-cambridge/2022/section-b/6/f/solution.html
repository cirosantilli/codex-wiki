For dissolved, nonadsorbing polyethylene glycol, the usual new interaction is a depletion force. Polymer centres are excluded from a layer of thickness of order their radius of gyration Rg​ around each particle. When the particles approach, their exclusion layers overlap. This increases the volume accessible to polymer and its translational entropy, lowering the colloid pair free energy. Equivalently the surrounding polymer osmotic pressure pushes the particles together.
In the dilute-polymer Asakura-Oosawa model, let h=R−2a be the surface gap and Re​=a+Rg​. Then
Udep​(R)=−Πp​Vov​(R),Vov​(R)=12π​(4Re​+R)(2Re​−R)2​

for 2a≤R<2Re​, and Udep​=0 for R≥2Re​. Here Πp​≃np(polymer)​kB​T is the reservoir polymer osmotic pressure. The overlap-volume formula follows by adding two equal spherical cap volumes with height Re​−R/2. Thus the added attraction has range about 2Rg​ and grows with polymer concentration. The total interaction is approximately hard exclusion plus screened electrostatic repulsion plus Van der Waals force and depletion attractions.
Increasing polymer concentration therefore creates a family of phase diagrams, rather than a unique shift determined by polymer size alone. Where the attraction overcomes repulsion, colloid-poor fluid can coexist with a colloid-rich phase, and fluid–crystal coexistence generally broadens. For sufficiently long-ranged attraction, stable fluid–fluid separation can occur. For the stipulated small polymer, the attraction is short-ranged: fluid–fluid separation can be metastable to crystallization, and a rapid attraction increase can instead produce an arrested colloidal gel. Residual long-ranged repulsion can oppose bulk separation. Simulations and free-volume calculations confirm that longer-ranged electrostatic repulsion can counteract depletion-driven separation.[ref]
The nonadsorption assumption matters. If the polyethylene glycol adsorbs to the particles, a dense attached layer can give steric stabilization, whereas chains attaching to two particles can produce polymer bridging. The paper does not specify surface affinity or polymer concentration, so depletion is the standard conditional answer, not a prediction of an inevitable particular final phase.
