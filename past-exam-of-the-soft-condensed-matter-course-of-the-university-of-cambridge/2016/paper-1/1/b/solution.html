For an equilibrium contact angle measured through the liquid, Young's equation balances the tangential interfacial forces:
γSV​−γSL​=γLV​cosθ.
(2)
The pure-ethanol measurement therefore fixes the solid–vapour surface energy as
γSV​=(21.0+21.4cos23∘)×10−3=40.699×10−3Nm−1.
(3)
The inferred surface energy is 40.7mJm−2, equivalently 40.7mNm−1. The equality of these units follows because creating interfacial area costs energy.
For the equal-fraction mixture, the stated linear relations give γSL​=20.5mNm−1 and γLV​=28.5mNm−1. Holding the solid–vapour surface energy fixed,
cosθ1/2​=28.540.699−20.5​=0.70873,θ1/2​≃44.9∘.​
(4)
The small decrease in γSL​ increases the numerator of Young's equation, but the increase in γLV​ has the larger relative effect and reduces cosθ. Consequently the contact angle increases and the mixture wets the surface less well. This inference uses equilibrium wetting on an unchanged smooth solid; a changing surface composition or contact-angle hysteresis would require additional measurements.
