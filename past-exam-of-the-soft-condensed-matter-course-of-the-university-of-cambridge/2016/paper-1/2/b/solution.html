The Flory interaction parameter χ measures the excess cost of unlike segment contacts. In a simple lattice with coordination number z, contact energies ϵAA​,ϵBB​,ϵAB​ and temperature T,
χ=kB​Tz​[ϵAB​−2ϵAA​+ϵBB​​].
(8)
A positive Flory interaction parameter penalizes unlike contacts; a negative value favors them. Mixing still depends on the balance with entropy of mixing, rather than the sign of χ alone.
For two polymers of degrees of polymerization NA​,NB​, let ϕ be the volume fraction of species A. The binary Flory-Huggins polymer blend has mixing free energy per lattice site
kB​Tf(ϕ)​=NA​ϕ​lnϕ+NB​1−ϕ​ln(1−ϕ)+χϕ(1−ϕ).
(9)
The logarithmic terms favor mixing. Their factors 1/NA​ and 1/NB​ arise because an entire chain translates as one object; connecting many segments greatly reduces the mixing entropy per segment. Hence long polymers can demix for a much smaller positive Flory interaction parameter than small molecules.
For composition-independent χ, the local stability criterion is
kB​Tf′′(ϕ)​=NA​ϕ1​+NB​(1−ϕ)1​−2χ.
(10)
Positive curvature means stability to small composition fluctuations, zero curvature locates the spinodal, and negative curvature means spontaneous growth of fluctuations. Actual two-phase coexistence is determined by the common-tangent construction, or equality of both species' chemical potentials; its boundary is the binodal. A locally stable but globally unfavourable homogeneous mixture can lie between the binodal and spinodal.

ϕc​=NA​​+NB​​NB​​​,χc​=21​(NA−1/2​+NB−1/2​)2.

For the symmetric Flory-Huggins polymer blend, ϕc​=1/2 and χc​=2/N. For a polymer solution with NB​=1 and long chains of species A, χc​→1/2, consistent with the theta solvent limit. An effective Flory interaction parameter can depend on temperature and composition; in the simple energetic case χ∝1/T, heating encourages mixing, but that temperature trend is not universal.
The original illustration uses stipulated NA​=NB​=100. At χ=0.01 the mixing free energy is convex, whereas at χ=0.03 a central unstable region appears. The horizontal common-tangent construction in the latter case joins the symmetric coexisting compositions; its contacts differ from the spinodal boundaries. These are model curves, not supplied experimental data.
