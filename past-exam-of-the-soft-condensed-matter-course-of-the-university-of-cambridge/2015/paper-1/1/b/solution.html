solvent quality changes the balance of segment–segment and segment–solvent contacts. In a good solvent, excluded-volume repulsion swells a sufficiently long isolated chain, with RG​∝Nν, ν≃0.588 in three dimensions; the Flory approximation gives ν=3/5. At theta conditions the effective two-segment excluded volume vanishes and the ideal exponent 1/2 is recovered for long chains. A poor solvent favors a compact polymer globule, whose large-chain size scales approximately as N1/3 at fixed internal density. solvent and temperature can also change local persistence length and hence the prefactor. These different conformations need not have the same radius at the same molar mass.
