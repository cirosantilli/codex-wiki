polymers at interfaces balance favorable surface contacts against lost conformational entropy and interactions among neighboring chains. At an attractive solid–liquid interface, an adsorbed polymer train consists of consecutive bound segments. An adsorbed polymer loop leaves the surface and returns to it, while an adsorbed polymer tail extends to a free chain end. A chain can contain all three, so adsorption does not imply a completely flattened molecule. Surface affinity, solvent quality, persistence length, chain length and concentration control the balance and the resulting segment-density profile.
adsorption gains energy of order ϵkB​T per bound segment but reduces the number of accessible chain configurations. Weak adsorption can be described through adsorption blobs whose thickness exceeds the statistical segment length; each blob loses an energy of order kB​T through confinement. Stronger attraction generally creates more contacts and a more compact layer, while loops and tails can still dominate its outer extent. A measurement of adsorbed mass alone therefore does not determine layer thickness or conformation.
End-grafted chains are different: their attachment is fixed at one end. At grafting density σg​, the mean lateral spacing is D∼σg−1/2​. At low coverage the chains behave as isolated polymer mushrooms. The polymer mushroom-to-brush crossover occurs when σg​RG2​∼1. At higher coverage in a good solvent, crowding makes chains extend away from the surface. A free-energy balance per chain,
kB​TF​∼Nb2H2​+HvN2σg​​,
(7)
combines stretching entropy with repulsive excluded volume v>0. Minimization gives H3∼vN3b2σg​, or
H∼Nb(σg​b2)1/3when v∼b3.​
(8)
This is a scaling result with an undetermined order-one coefficient, valid for overlapping chains stretched below their contour length. In a compact poor-solvent layer, a roughly fixed internal polymer volume fraction instead gives H∝Nσg​.
Small-angle neutron scattering on end-grafted polydimethylsiloxane layers on porous silica has distinguished the good- and poor-solvent density exponents. The chemical system and scattering contrast matter: changing solvent quality should be separated from changing graft coverage, chain molar mass or adsorption strength.[ref]
An interface can also localize chains of a block copolymer when its blocks prefer different media. Such layers change interfacial tension and can stabilize dispersed droplets or particles through steric repulsion. Overlapping layers in a good solvent cost osmotic and conformational free energy; incomplete adsorption can instead produce polymer bridging between particles. Nonadsorbing chains can cause depletion attraction. Thus the presence of a polymer does not by itself guarantee stabilization.
