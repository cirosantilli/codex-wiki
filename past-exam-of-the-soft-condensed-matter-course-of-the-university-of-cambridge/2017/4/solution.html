Meaning of the mixing free energy. The supplied expression is the Flory–Huggins solution theory mixing free energy per lattice site, or per solvent-sized reference cell. Although the question calls it “per particle,” it cannot mean per polymer molecule: one chain occupies N sites. Let the site volume be v0​, the total number of sites be M=V/v0​, and the chain and solvent counts be np​,ns​, so
M=Nnp​+ns​,ϕ=MNnp​​,Fmix​=MkB​Tg(ϕ),
(32)
where
g(ϕ)=Nϕ​lnϕ+(1−ϕ)ln(1−ϕ)+χϕ(1−ϕ).
(33)
The first term is the polymer's translational entropy of mixing. Its factor 1/N expresses that N connected segments translate as one molecule. The second is the solvent's translational mixing entropy. Both are negative in the mixing free energy for 0<ϕ<1, favoring mixing.
The final term is a mean-field excess contact contribution. The Flory interaction parameter χ measures the relative preference for unlike polymer-solvent contacts compared with like contacts. In the simplest lattice model with coordination number z and pair energies ϵps​,ϵpp​,ϵss​,
χ=kB​Tz​[ϵps​−2ϵpp​+ϵss​​].
(34)
Positive χ penalizes unlike contacts and promotes separation; negative χ favors them. Effective real-solution values can also include non-enthalpic effects, so their temperature dependence need not be exactly a constant divided by T. The subsequent derivatives assume composition-independent χ as in the supplied model.
Osmotic pressure and the special value one half. At fixed chain count, ∂ϕ/∂V=−ϕ/V. Differentiating Fmix​=(V/v0​)kB​Tg(ϕ) with respect to volume, relative to the pure-solvent reference, gives
π=v0​kB​T​[ϕg′(ϕ)−g(ϕ)].
(35)
The resulting osmotic pressure in Flory-Huggins solution theory is
kB​Tπv0​​=−ln(1−ϕ)−(1−N1​)ϕ−χϕ2=Nϕ​+(21​−χ)ϕ2+3ϕ3​+O(ϕ4).​
(36)
The leading term is the ideal osmotic pressure of chains, since their number density is ϕ/(Nv0​). At χ=1/2 the second-virial term vanishes: effective two-segment attraction and excluded-volume repulsion balance. This is the theta solvent condition for a sufficiently long dilute chain; the positive third-order contribution remains, so a finite-concentration solution is not a completely noninteracting gas. The site-volume factor is necessary to give physical pressure units.
For χ<1/2, the effective pair excluded volume is positive. In a good solvent, the isolated chain expands relative to a Gaussian coil, with R∼bNν, ν≃0.588 or 3/5 in the Flory approximation. For χ>1/2, attraction wins in a poor solvent and a sufficiently long chain collapses toward a dense polymer globule, with asymptotic size R∝N1/3. Close to the theta condition, finite-chain crossover effects matter. At χ=1/2, R∼bN1/2 to leading scaling order. This theta criterion is not the exact finite-N phase-separation critical point; the lattice model has ϕc​=1/(1+N​) and χc​=(1+1/N​)2/2.
Overlap concentration. The polymer overlap concentration is reached when there is roughly one isolated coil in its own pervaded volume. If the coil size is R and a repeat unit occupies volume v0​,
ϕ∗∼R3Nv0​​.
(37)
With R∼bNν, the general scaling law is
ϕ∗∼b3v0​​N1−3ν.​
(38)
The usual single-segment-volume estimate v0​∼b3 gives ϕ∗∼N1−3ν: N−1/2 in a theta solvent, approximately N−4/5 in a good solvent, and order one for compact globules. A coil has no sharp surface, so choosing a radius of gyration or a geometric coil volume changes numerical prefactors.
For the stated polystyrene at N=1500 and χ=1/2, the conventional scaling estimate is
ϕ∗∼N−1/2=1500−1/2=0.0258≃2.6%.​
(39)
This is a volume-fraction estimate, not a molar concentration. It uses the implicit site-volume approximation; the question does not provide an independent repeat-unit volume to determine a more precise prefactor.
Semidilute length scale. Beyond overlap, a semidilute polymer solution is characterized locally by a concentration blob size ξ. Suppose one blob contains g statistical units. At the theta condition the subchain is Gaussian, so ξ∼bg​, while matching its internal concentration to the bulk gives ϕ∼gv0​/ξ3. Eliminating g gives the concentration-blob size in a theta solvent:
ξ∼b2ϕv0​​∼ϕb​when v0​∼b3.​
(40)
Using the supplied statistical step length b=0.67nm and ϕ=0.05,
ξ∼0.050.67​nm=13.4nm≃13nm.​
(41)
The same convention gives g∼ϕ−2=400<N=1500, and the whole ideal chain has R∼bN​≃26nm. Thus the blob is smaller than the chain. Because ϕ/ϕ∗≃1.94 is only modestly above overlap, the value is a scaling estimate in a crossover region, not a precision asymptotic measurement. A statistical step length is not automatically a physical monomer diameter: if v0​ differs from b3, the more general estimates are ϕ∗∼(v0​/b3)N−1/2 and ξ∼v0​/(b2ϕ), with the missing prefactor left explicit.
