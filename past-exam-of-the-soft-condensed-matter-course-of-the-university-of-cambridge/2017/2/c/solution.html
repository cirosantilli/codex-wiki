The requested model is the standard linear solid model: a spring of modulus E0​>0 in parallel with a Maxwell branch consisting of a spring E1​>0 in series with a dashpot of viscosity η>0. Both parallel branches have the same strain; their stresses add. In the Maxwell branch the spring and dashpot have the same stress and their strains add. The equilibrium spring prevents indefinite flow at long times.
For harmonic strain ϵ(t)=Re[ϵ0​eiωt], define the dynamic modulus by σ=E∗(ω)ϵ. The Maxwell branch has compliance 1/E1​+1/(iωη). Defining τ=η/E1​ therefore gives
E∗(ω)=E0​+E1​+iωηiωηE1​​=E0​+E1​1+iωτiωτ​.​
(14)
The same mechanical model can represent a shear modulus if the appropriate shear stresses and strains are used consistently.
Writing E∗=E′+iE′′, its storage modulus and loss modulus are
E′=E0​+E1​1+(ωτ)2(ωτ)2​,E′′=E1​1+(ωτ)2ωτ​.​
(15)
With this time convention the physical stress is ϵ0​[E′cosωt−E′′sinωt] and leads the strain by the loss angle. The dissipated energy per unit volume per cycle is πE′′ϵ02​≥0. Thus E′ describes elastic storage and E′′ describes dissipation.
At low frequency, ωτ≪1, the dashpot flows during a cycle and relaxes the Maxwell spring, leaving E′→E0​, E′′∼E1​ωτ. At high frequency, ωτ≫1, the dashpot hardly deforms within a cycle, so both springs carry elastic load: E′→E0​+E1​, while E′′∼E1​/(ωτ). The loss modulus is maximal at ωτ=1, with peak value E1​/2.
The equivalent constitutive equation is
σ+τσ˙=E0​ϵ+τ(E0​+E1​)ϵ˙.
(16)
After a sudden strain ϵs​ is imposed and held, the stress relaxes as σ(t)=ϵs​[E0​+E1​e−t/τ]. It falls from the instantaneous modulus E0​+E1​ to the nonzero relaxed modulus E0​. This single-time relaxation is the simplest improvement over a purely elastic solid or a Maxwell fluid with zero long-time modulus.
The loss tangent is tanδ=E′′/E′. Its maximum need not coincide with the loss-modulus maximum: the peak frequencies in a standard linear solid give ωτ=E0​/(E0​+E1​)​ for the loss tangent. The model captures one viscoelastic crossover between two finite elastic plateaux, with dissipation concentrated near the relaxation frequency.
