Local compositional stability requires positive curvature of the mixing free energy. Differentiating twice at fixed temperature, with χ independent of composition as in the given model, gives
kB​T1​∂ϕ2∂2f​=Nϕ1​+1−ϕ1​−2χ.
(33)
The spinodal occurs where this vanishes:
χs​(ϕ)=21​[Nϕ1​+1−ϕ1​].​
(34)
Between the two spinodal compositions at sufficiently large χ, f′′<0 and a homogeneous mixture is unstable to infinitesimal composition fluctuations. The binodal is instead fixed by the common-tangent construction, and encloses a larger coexistence region. Between binodal and spinodal the mixture is locally stable but metastable.
A phase diagram with a conventional upper critical temperature additionally assumes that χ decreases with T, for example χ=C/T with C>0. Then high temperature gives a uniform mixture; cooling produces an asymmetric coexistence dome whose critical composition is on the dilute-polymer side. The spinodal lies inside the binodal dome. An arbitrary χ(T) need not give this orientation, so the free-energy formula alone does not specify a unique temperature axis.
