Without stabilization, the potential energy is predominantly attractive outside the molecular core and drops into a deep primary minimum. With the charging treatment, the DLVO theory sum develops a positive barrier between distant particles and that primary minimum. The barrier must be large compared with kB​T to inhibit aggregation on the experimental timescale. A shallow secondary minimum can remain at larger separation, depending on parameters.
The horizontal axis is the surface gap, so the molecular exclusion boundary is at its origin. Both interactions tend to zero at large separation. Raising salt concentration shortens the Debye–Hückel screening length and can reduce the barrier, allowing particles to stick again.
