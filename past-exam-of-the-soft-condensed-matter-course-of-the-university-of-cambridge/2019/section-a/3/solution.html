Two examples are sodium dodecyl sulfate, with a negatively charged sulfate head and a hydrocarbon tail, and cetrimonium bromide, with a positively charged ammonium head and a hydrocarbon tail. Both are amphiphiles: one region is hydrophilic and another is hydrophobic.
In water, the hydrophobic effect favors association of the tails into a core, reducing their exposed area while the hydrated heads remain outside. This can lower the total Gibbs free energy despite loss of translational entropy. Head-group repulsion, interfacial cost and chain-packing constraints oppose unlimited growth, so finite micelles are favored under suitable conditions above the critical micelle concentration.
