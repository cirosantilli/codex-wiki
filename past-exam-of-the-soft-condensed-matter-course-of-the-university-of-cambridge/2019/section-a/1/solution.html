The Reynolds number compares inertial and viscous effects:
Re=ηρUL​,
(1)
where ρ is fluid mass density, U a characteristic swimming speed, L a characteristic body length, and η the dynamic viscosity. Both swimmers are in the same fluid, so the fluid properties cancel from the ratio:
Rewhale​Rebacterium​​=Uw​Lw​Ub​Lb​​.
(2)
For an order-of-magnitude estimate, take Lb​=2μm, Ub​=20μms−1, Lw​=10m and Uw​=5ms−1. With water values ρ≃103kgm−3 and η≃10−3Pas, these give
Reb​≃4×10−5,Rew​≃5×107,
(3)
and hence
Rew​Reb​​≃8×10−13∼10−12​.
(4)
The dimensions and swimming speeds are estimates, so this ratio is not a universal species-independent constant. Its physical significance is robust: viscous forces dominate the bacterium's motion, whereas fluid inertia is crucial for the whale.
