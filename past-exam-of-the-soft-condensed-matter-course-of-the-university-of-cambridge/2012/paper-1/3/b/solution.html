Use polymer volume fraction ϕ horizontally and the Flory interaction parameter χ vertically. Lower χ gives better solvent quality. The simplest Flory–Huggins solution theory assigns the dimensionless mixing free energy per lattice site
f(ϕ)=Nϕ​lnϕ+(1−ϕ)ln(1−ϕ)+χϕ(1−ϕ),veff​≃a3(1−2χ).
(16)
Thus χ<1/2 gives positive excluded volume and a good-solvent coil, χ=1/2 is the long-chain theta condition, and χ>1/2 is the poor-solvent side. It is the net two-segment interaction that vanishes at theta, not every molecular force.
At fixed good-solvent quality, the polymer overlap volume fraction ϕ∗∼Na3/Rg3​ separates isolated coils from a semidilute polymer solution. Above overlap, many chains interpenetrate, but there is still solvent on the segment scale. As ϕ approaches order unity the solution is concentrated; these concentration-regime boundaries are crossovers, not first-order phase transitions. The overlap fraction increases from order N−4/5 in a strong good solvent toward order N−1/2 at theta. The crossover can be expressed as ϕ∗∼N−4/5(1−2χ)−3/5 while 1−2χ≫N−1/2; it saturates at the theta scaling when that condition fails.
On the poor-solvent side there can instead be coexistence of polymer-poor and polymer-rich phases. The binodal follows a common tangent of f; the spinodal follows
f′′(ϕ)=Nϕ1​+1−ϕ1​−2χ=0.
(17)
The critical point of a Flory–Huggins polymer solution is
ϕc​=1+N​1​,χc​=21​(1+N−1/2)2.​
(18)
For finite N, χc​ lies above 1/2. Therefore poor solvent does not automatically mean demixing at every concentration, and an overlap line must not be mistaken for a coexistence boundary.
