Electric current (I=dQcross​/dt)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 49
An electric current measures how quickly signed electric charge crosses a chosen fixed oriented surface S. Choose its unit normal n; positive electric charge crossing along n counts positively, while crossing against it counts negatively. If Qcross​(t) is the accumulated signed electric charge transported through S, then
IS​(t)=dtdQcross​​=∫S​J(r,t)⋅ndA,Qcross​(t2​)−Qcross​(t1​)=∫t1​t2​​IS​(t)dt.
(70)
Here J is the current density; the surface integral collects its local normal contributions. Reversing the unit normal reverses IS​. The electric current is a signed scalar associated with the whole chosen oriented surface, whereas current density is a vector at each point. The SI unit is the ampere, one coulomb per second. [ref]; [ref].
For a constant electric current of 2A over a time interval of 3s, the transported electric charge is 6C. This need not be electric charge accumulating in the wire: in steady transport, electric charge enters and leaves. For a closed oriented surface with outward unit normal, electric charge conservation instead gives Iout​=−dQinside​/dt. Keeping transported and enclosed electric charge distinct connects electrical circuit bookkeeping to the local continuity equation.
