Daniell cell

 Home Chemistry Electrochemistry Galvanic cell
OurBigBook.com Words: 978 Articles: 1
A Daniell cell uses the zinc/Zn2+ and Cu2+/copper pairs. In the separated-compartment teaching version, a zinc electrode contacts an aqueous chemical solution containing Zn2+, and a copper electrode contacts one containing Cu2+. A salt bridge supplies an ionic connection; a wire and load supply an electronic connection. During discharge,
anode, oxidation:cathode, reduction:net:​Zn(s)⟶Zn2+(aq)+2e−,Cu2+(aq)+2e−⟶Cu(s),Zn(s)+Cu2+(aq)⟶Zn2+(aq)+Cu(s).​
(87)
The zinc electrode loses material and copper is deposited at the cathode. Each half-reaction conserves chemical atom counts and electric charge: the first has net charge zero on each side, the second +2e−2e=0 on the left and zero on the right. The net reaction has +2e on both sides, with no Electrons left in its chemical equation. The Zn/Cu teaching cell and these half-reactions are described in Florida State University, Redox Reactions in Voltaic Cells: Construction of a Potential Series, lab background.
Creating one mole of Zn2+ adds 2F of positive electric charge to the anode compartment's ionic inventory; consuming one mole of Cu2+ removes 2F of positive electric charge from the cathode compartment. Here F is the Faraday constant. In an ideal bookkeeping example using a potassium nitrate salt bridge, two moles of nitrate anions entering the anode compartment compensate the first change, and two moles of potassium cations entering the cathode compartment compensate the second. Other ions in the cell can share the actual transport. The two compensating inventory changes do not mean that 4F flowed through the load: the same series electric current transports 2F through each cross-section of the circuit per mole of net reaction. See salt bridge.
Under standard-state conditions near 298K, tabulated reduction potentials give a cell electromotive force of approximately 1.10V. This is a reversible reference value, not a promised loaded reading; actual composition matters through thermodynamic activity. The tabulated copper and zinc values and their subtraction are given in Purdue University, Electrochemical Reactions: standard-state reduction potentials. For the net chemical equation as written, the molar Gibbs free energy change obeys
Δr​G=−2FE.
(88)
Thus E=1.10V corresponds to about −212kJmol−1 of net reaction. At fixed temperature and pressure, −Δr​G is the maximum reversible electrical work per mole for this cell reaction. It is not automatically all the heat released by a finite-current cell. See 17.4 Potential, Free Energy, and Equilibrium (§17.4: cell potential and free energy).
