Mean absorption time of a lazy symmetric random walk (Ek​[T]=k(n−k)/(1−q))

OurBigBook.com Words: 121
Consider a random walk on {0,1,…,n} with absorbing states 0,n. At each interior state it stays in place with probability q, and moves one position in either direction with probability (1−q)/2, where 0≤q<1. Its mean absorption time from k is
Ek​[T]=1−qk(n−k)​.​
(2)
Proof. A sequence of at most n consecutive leftward moves ensures absorption, with probability at least ((1−q)/2)n>0. Dividing time into blocks of length n gives a geometric series bound on the survival probabilities, so the expected value is finite. Let ek​=Ek​[T]. The law of total expectation and first-step analysis give
ek​=1+qek​+21−q​(ek+1​+ek−1​),e0​=en​=0.
(3)
Thus ek+1​−2ek​+ek−1​=−2/(1−q). A particular solution of this linear recurrence relation is −k2/(1−q); its homogeneous solutions are A+Bk. The two boundary conditions give A=0, B=n/(1−q), proving both the formula and its uniqueness. Holding slows the usual symmetric random walk by the factor 1/(1−q).
