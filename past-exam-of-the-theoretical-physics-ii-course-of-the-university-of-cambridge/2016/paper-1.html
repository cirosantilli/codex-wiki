A matrix R belongs to the special orthogonal group in four dimensions precisely when
R∈M4​(R),RTR=I,detR=1.​
(24)
Differentiating the orthogonality relation along a path R(ϵ)=I+ϵX+O(ϵ2) gives XT+X=0. Thus its Lie algebra consists of real antisymmetric four-by-four matrices. Such a matrix has one free entry for each unordered coordinate pair, giving dimension (24​)=6.
Each displayed generator has a single two-coordinate antisymmetric block. The K matrices cover planes (1,2),(1,3),(1,4); the L matrices cover (3,4),(2,4),(2,3), with their printed orientations. These are all six distinct planes. Therefore the generators are linearly independent and span the full antisymmetric space: they form a basis of so(4).
Conversely, exponentiating any real antisymmetric X gives (eX)TeX=I and det(eX)=etrX=1. This verifies that the infinitesimal matrices generate proper rotations rather than reflections.

[Δ,Xs​]=2Xs​,[Δ,Ys​]=−2Ys​.

A Klein-Gordon solution is second order in time: both Ψ and ∂t​Ψ are needed as initial data. Its free modes have both signs of frequency,
E=±c2p2+m2c4​.
(54)
This differs from the first-order Schrödinger evolution of a fixed one-particle wavefunction.
Moreover, ∣Ψ∣2 does not generally obey the required probability continuity equation. The conserved Klein-Gordon density is proportional to
ρKG​=i(Ψ∗∂t​Ψ−(∂t​Ψ∗)Ψ),
(55)
up to a positive conventional constant. A pure positive-frequency mode gives +2ω∣Ψ∣2 and a negative-frequency mode gives −2ω∣Ψ∣2. This density is not positive definite and cannot be a universal position probability density.
For a free field, one can choose a positive-frequency subspace with a positive integrated inner product. Time-dependent backgrounds generally mix it with the negative-frequency subspace, so simply deleting the latter is not dynamically consistent. In quantum field theory the complex Klein-Gordon field instead has particle and antiparticle sectors; its conserved current is interpreted as charge current, and creation or annihilation of pairs need not fit a fixed-particle-number wavefunction. These are limitations of the naive single-particle interpretation, not a failure of the relativistic field equation.

For an exactly instantaneous vector-potential step in three spatial dimensions, at large k the mixing behaves as vk​∼eA0​⋅k/(2ℏk). Thus ∫d3k∣vk​∣2 diverges in the ultraviolet. In a finite quantization box, a finite momentum cutoff makes the product a normalized Fock-space vector; a sufficiently smooth finite-duration pulse removes this particular instantaneous-quench pathology. The per-mode distribution remains the result above, while the unregulated infinite product should not be asserted to have a finite nonzero global normalization.
