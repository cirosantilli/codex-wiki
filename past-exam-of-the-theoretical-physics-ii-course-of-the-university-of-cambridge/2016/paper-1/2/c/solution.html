A matrix R belongs to the special orthogonal group in four dimensions precisely when
R∈M4​(R),RTR=I,detR=1.​
(24)
Differentiating the orthogonality relation along a path R(ϵ)=I+ϵX+O(ϵ2) gives XT+X=0. Thus its Lie algebra consists of real antisymmetric four-by-four matrices. Such a matrix has one free entry for each unordered coordinate pair, giving dimension (24​)=6.
Each displayed generator has a single two-coordinate antisymmetric block. The K matrices cover planes (1,2),(1,3),(1,4); the L matrices cover (3,4),(2,4),(2,3), with their printed orientations. These are all six distinct planes. Therefore the generators are linearly independent and span the full antisymmetric space: they form a basis of so(4).
Conversely, exponentiating any real antisymmetric X gives (eX)TeX=I and det(eX)=etrX=1. This verifies that the infinitesimal matrices generate proper rotations rather than reflections.
