Each matrix is purely imaginary and antisymmetric. Let T have these four basis kets as columns in the computational basis. The infinitesimal generators of the standard rotation UA​=exp[−iθn⋅σA​/2] are

Using the displayed actions gives the phase-convention-specific identities
Gx​=−Lx−​,Gy​=Ly−​,Gz​=−Lz−​.​
(32)
They are real antisymmetric matrices. A finite SU(2) rotation on A therefore acts on the magic-basis coefficient vector as b↦Ob, with O∈SO(4). Consequently
q′=(Ob)T(Ob)=bTOTOb=bTb=q.
(33)
It preserves concurrence and hence preserves the reduced eigenvalues and entanglement entropy. A general U(2) operation adds a common phase to b; that changes q by a phase of twice the angle but leaves ∣q∣ unchanged. The real-rotation description of local transformations is a characteristic property of the magic basis.[ref]
As an independent direct check, a local unitary sends ρA​ to UA​ρA​UA†​, which is a unitary similarity transformation and cannot change its spectrum. This local-unitary invariance of entanglement entropy also explains why merely rotating one subsystem cannot create entanglement.
