Take ω>0 and T=2π/ω. A full spin one-half rotation gives R(T)=−I, an essential sign. The cyclic phase of a spin in a rotating field has factors and total quantum phases
eiΦ±​=−e∓iπΩ/ω,Φ±​=π∓ωπΩ​(mod2π)​.

The assumption ω≪B suffices for adiabatic following; near degeneracy it must not be silently dropped.[ref]

For elastic scattering by a real short-range scalar potential, write C=m/(2πℏ2), ki​=k and ∣kf​∣=k. In the far field, ∣r−r′∣=r−r⋅r′+O(r−1) and kf​=kr. The outgoing spherical-wave coefficient is
f(kf​,ki​)=−C∫d3r′e−ikf​⋅r′V(r′)ψki​​(r′).
(15)
Substitution of the incident plane wave inside this integral gives
f(1)=−CV(q),dΩdσ(1)​=C2∣V(q)∣2​,V(q)=∫e−iq⋅rV(r)d3r.
(16)
The incoming and outgoing flux factors cancel because their speeds agree. Here q=kf​−ki​ is a wavevector transfer; the actual momentum transfer is ℏq. This distinction preserves dimensions. The approximation requires the scattered correction to the interior wave to be small, and does not in general apply to an unscreened long-range potential.

There is a state-assumption issue. For a number-conserving Gaussian quantum state with no anomalous contractions, put ψi​=ψ(ri​), ni​=⟨ψi†​ψi​⟩ and the one-body density matrix gij​=⟨ψi†​ψj​⟩. Wick's theorem pairs each creation operator with an annihilation operator. The direct pairing gives n1​n2​; the exchanged pairing gives ϵg12​g21​, with ϵ=+1 for bosons and −1 for fermions. Hence
C12​=n1​n2​+ϵ∣g12​∣2​.
(23)
normal ordering removes the equal-position delta-function self-counting term. Fermionic occupation-number product states are Slater determinants and obey this Wick theorem. Independent thermal bosonic modes also obey it, with Nα​ understood as mean occupations.
A bosonic Fock product state does not generally obey the asserted identity. The bosonic number-state corrections to Gaussian density correlations follow from ⟨aα†2​aα2​⟩=Nα​(Nα​−1) and give
C12Fock​=n1​n2​+∣g12​∣2−α∑​Nα​(Nα​+1)∣φα​(r1​)∣2∣φα​(r2​)∣2​.
(24)
For one occupied mode this is N(N−1)∣φ1​∣2∣φ2​∣2, whereas the uncorrected Gaussian expression gives 2N2∣φ1​∣2∣φ2​∣2. This counterexample disproves the asserted identity for arbitrary bosonic number-product states. The formulas below give both the Gaussian result, with its additional state assumption, and the exact bosonic number-state alternative. The ensemble distinction is also explicit in a later primary treatment of ideal-gas correlations.[ref]

The three-point density correlation of an ideal quantum gas follows first for a number-conserving Gaussian quantum state. There are six pairings, one for each permutation of three annihilation operators. The identity gives the product of densities; each transposition has sign ϵ; both three-cycles have positive sign. Thus
C123(3)​=​n1​n2​n3​+ϵ[n1​∣g23​∣2+n2​∣g13​∣2+n3​∣g12​∣2]+g12​g23​g31​+g13​g32​g21​.​​
(26)
The last two terms are complex conjugates. They equal 2Re(g12​g23​g31​), not an additional signed pair term. This is the permanent of a matrix (gij​) for bosons and its determinant for fermions. Wick's theorem supplies the six terms only with the state assumption stated in part (a).
For completeness an exact bosonic Fock-state alternative can be given without that assumption. Let ℓ be a mode-occupation decrement with ∑α​ℓα​=3. Define Aℓ​(1,2,3) as the sum of ∏j=13​φαj​​(rj​) over all distinct ordered triples having these mode multiplicities. Acting three annihilation fields on the number state leaves mutually orthogonal states for different ℓ. Therefore
C123(3),Fock​=ℓ:∑ℓα​=3∑​[α∏​Nαℓα​​​]∣Aℓ​(1,2,3)∣2​.
(27)
Here Nℓ​=N!/(N−ℓ)!, with zero when ℓ>N. This expression includes repeated-mode occupation corrections absent from the Gaussian permanent; for a single mode it reduces to N(N−1)(N−2)∏j​∣φ(rj​)∣2.

A constant electrostatic energy offset is removed locally by the quantum phase substitution ψ=e−iV0​t/ℏψ0​, since (iℏ∂t​−V0​)ψ=e−iV0​t/ℏiℏ∂t​ψ0​. Therefore the two frequency branches become

Assume a positive-energy incoming particle, E>mc2, and a nonzero upward step V0​>0. Set M=mc2 and k=E2−M2​/(ℏc)>0. With the common time factor e−iEt/ℏ removed, take ϕ<​(x)=eikx+re−ikx and ϕ>​(x)=teiqx. The right-hand wavenumber satisfies q2=[(E−V0​)2−M2]/(ℏc)2; its physical branch is specified in part (c). r=k+qk−q​,t=k+q2k​​.
(39)

The Noether current of scalar electrodynamics, in a convenient common normalization for a stationary mode, is ρQ​=(E−V)∣ϕ∣2/(mc2) and jQ​=(ℏ/m)Im(ϕ∗∂x​ϕ). For a real q, its charge-current ratios are R=∣r∣2 and T=(q/k)∣t∣2, with R+T=1. The same statement uses Req for a decaying solution. The electrostatic potential couples through the time derivative; it is not a Lorentz-scalar mass shift. A zero step is recovered separately with q=k, r=0 and t=1.

Let z=E−V0​+i0. The outgoing analytic branch of Klein-Gordon step momentum is
q(z)=ℏcz−M​z+M​​​,
(40)
where each square root is continued from the upper half-plane. It behaves as z/(ℏc) at large positive or negative real z. The branch points are E=V0​±M; replacing this product by a principal square root of z2−M2 can choose the wrong sign below the lower threshold.
I. For E−V0​>M, q>0 and both transmitted kinetic energy and group velocity are positive. Thus 0≤R<1 and T=4kq/(k+q)2>0; the transmission decreases to zero on approaching the upper threshold from above.
II. For ∣E−V0​∣<M, write q=iκ with κ=M2−(E−V0​)2​/(ℏc)>0. The right wave decays, r=(k−iκ)/(k+iκ) has modulus one, and T=0. There is an evanescent tail but no flux transmitted to infinity. Its reflection quantum phase changes continuously between the two thresholds.
III. For E−V0​<−M, the analytic continuation gives q=−Q, where Q=(E−V0​)2−M2​/(ℏc)>0. The group velocity c2ℏq/(E−V0​) is again rightward, while the charge density and transmitted charge current are negative. Therefore
R=(k−Q)2(k+Q)2​>1,T=−(k−Q)24kQ​<0,R+T=1​.
(41)
Choosing q=+Q would describe a wavepacket arriving from the right, inconsistent with the chosen incoming channel. As the lower threshold is approached, q→0− and R→1 from above. This Klein-Gordon step amplification has a real denominator zero at E=V0​/2 when V0​>2M: there Q=k, and no finite stationary solution with unit incoming amplitude satisfies both matching equations. The resulting formal pole must not be described as an infinite single-particle probability. Finite switching, wavepackets and the treatment of the external source require a multiparticle analysis.[ref]

The negative-kinetic-energy branch is interpreted as an antiparticle channel. In the overlap mc2<E<V0​−mc2, particles can escape to the left and oppositely charged antiparticles to the right. This interval exists only when V0​>2mc2. The external electrostatic source supplies the energy for pair creation; the reflected particle flux can exceed the incident one because the outgoing state need not contain only the incident particle.
The conserved quantity is signed charge current, not a positive one-particle probability in both regions. Negative T denotes rightward transport of opposite charge, so it is consistent with R>1 and R+T=1. A quantized charged field with particle and antiparticle creation operators gives the appropriate interpretation of this Klein paradox. This bosonic result must not be replaced by a fermionic reflection law or interpreted as a failure of charge conservation.[ref]
