There is a state-assumption issue. For a number-conserving Gaussian quantum state with no anomalous contractions, put ψi​=ψ(ri​), ni​=⟨ψi†​ψi​⟩ and the one-body density matrix gij​=⟨ψi†​ψj​⟩. Wick's theorem pairs each creation operator with an annihilation operator. The direct pairing gives n1​n2​; the exchanged pairing gives ϵg12​g21​, with ϵ=+1 for bosons and −1 for fermions. Hence
C12​=n1​n2​+ϵ∣g12​∣2​.

normal ordering removes the equal-position delta-function self-counting term. Fermionic occupation-number product states are Slater determinants and obey this Wick theorem. Independent thermal bosonic modes also obey it, with Nα​ understood as mean occupations.
A bosonic Fock product state does not generally obey the asserted identity. The bosonic number-state corrections to Gaussian density correlations follow from ⟨aα†2​aα2​⟩=Nα​(Nα​−1) and give
C12Fock​=n1​n2​+∣g12​∣2−α∑​Nα​(Nα​+1)∣φα​(r1​)∣2∣φα​(r2​)∣2​.
(24)
For one occupied mode this is N(N−1)∣φ1​∣2∣φ2​∣2, whereas the uncorrected Gaussian expression gives 2N2∣φ1​∣2∣φ2​∣2. This counterexample disproves the asserted identity for arbitrary bosonic number-product states.
