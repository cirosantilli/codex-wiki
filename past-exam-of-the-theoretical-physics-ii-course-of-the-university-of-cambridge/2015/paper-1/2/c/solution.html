Multiply each time-sliced path weight by exp[−∑j​V(xj​)Δt]. A nonnegative finite V is a local killing rate: the Feynman-Kac weight attenuates paths in proportion to the time spent in regions where V>0. Let that rate tend to infinity outside the allowed interval. Paths that exit are removed, leaving Brownian motion killed at its first boundary hit.
Consequently the required kernel is the Gaussian path measure restricted to surviving paths, equivalently
K∣x∣<L​=∫xi​xf​​Dxe−∫[x˙2/(4D)+V(x)]dt,​

with the infinite-barrier interpretation stated above. It solves ∂t​K=D∂x2​K in the interior with absorbing Dirichlet boundary conditions at ±L. A reflecting boundary condition for a diffusion would describe a different process.
Strictly, this is a transition probability density in the final position, jointly with survival; a probability for an interval of final positions includes an integral over xf​. Its integral over the whole allowed interval is at most one. It is not the normalized endpoint density conditional on survival, which would require division by the survival probability. In these diffusion units V and the eigenvalues of H have units of inverse time.
