Outside a finite-range scattering potential, the stationary Schrödinger equation becomes the free Helmholtz equation. The specified incident state is a plane wave along +z. The scattered disturbance must be outgoing: the scattering experiment has an incoming incident beam but no independently imposed spherical wave arriving from infinity. At large radius its angular pattern is encoded by a scattering amplitude fk​(θ,ϕ):
ψk​(r)∼eikz+fk​(θ,ϕ)reikr​.​
(22)
An outgoing three-dimensional wave has amplitude proportional to 1/r because its probability current spreads over area proportional to r2. Indeed its radial current is (ℏk/m)∣fk​∣2/r2 to leading order, while the incident current density is ℏk/m. Therefore dσ/dΩ=∣fk​∣2. The sign e+ikr assumes the stationary time factor e−iEk​t/ℏ.
