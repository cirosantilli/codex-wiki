Word metric (dS​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Geometric group theory Cayley graph
OurBigBook.com Words: 891 Articles: 5
The word metric measures how many allowed moves are needed to change one group element into another. For a finite symmetric generating set of a group S with 1∈/S, it is the graph distance between vertices of the Cayley graph, equivalently
dS​(g,h)=∣g−1h∣S​.
(19)
Here word length counts letters in a shortest representative of g−1h. The path metric on the entire Cayley graph also permits points inside edges; the word metric is its restriction to the group itself.[ref]
The word metric is left invariant:
dS​(ag,ah)=∣(ag)−1(ah)∣S​=∣g−1h∣S​=dS​(g,h).
(20)
This corresponds to the label-preserving group action on the Cayley graph. Symmetry follows by reversing a shortest graph path; concatenating routes gives the triangle inequality.[ref]
Right invariance can fail in a non-abelian group, because right multiplication replaces g−1h by a−1g−1ha through conjugation. In the free group F(x,y) with S={x,x−1,y,y−1}, we have dS​(1,y)=1, whereas dS​(x,yx)=∣x−1yx∣S​=3: the latter freely reduced word has three letters. In an abelian group, conjugation fixes each element, so right invariance does hold. Cayley graphs of the integers and word metric balls in the integer lattice give examples where the word metric can be calculated explicitly.
