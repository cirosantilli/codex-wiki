Exact Newton error identity for a quadratic (ek+1​=ek2​/[2(xk​−a)])

For the quadratic polynomial f(x)=(x−a)2−b2 with b>0, consider the Newton root-finding iteration towards the polynomial root x∗​=a+b. If xk​=a, its signed error ek​=xk​−x∗​ satisfies
ek+1​=2(xk​−a)ek2​​.
(5)
Indeed the iteration for uk​=xk​−a is uk+1​=(uk​+b2/uk​)/2, and subtracting b yields (uk​−b)2/(2uk​). This exact identity explains quadratic convergence near the simple polynomial root. If an initial error is asymptotic to Cεm and xk​−a→b=0 as ε→0, one step changes its leading power to 2m and its coefficient to C2/(2b).
