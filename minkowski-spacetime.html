Minkowski spacetime (R1,3)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 21
Minkowski spacetime is the flat four-dimensional spacetime of special relativity: one time coordinate and three spatial coordinates, equipped with a Minkowski metric. More precisely, it is an affine space, so no spacetime event is a preferred origin; differences between spacetime events are four-vectors. Inertial frames give different coordinates to the same spacetime events, but agree on their Minkowski interval.
This geometry answers two different measurement questions with one structure. Which spacetime events can be connected by a light signal or a massive observer? The light cone classifies them. How much time does a moving clock record? Its world line and the Minkowski metric determine its proper time. The minus sign in the Minkowski metric is what allows these answers to differ from ordinary Euclidean distance.
For a concrete one-space-dimensional slice, start at the origin O and measure t in seconds and x in light-seconds. The spacetime event A at (t,x)=(4s,3ls) can be reached by an observer moving at 0.75c, whereas B=(2s,3ls) would require 1.5c to be reached directly from O. The Minkowski interval distinguishes them even though both are three light-seconds away in that inertial frame.
Hermann Minkowski presented this geometric viewpoint in his Cologne lecture of 21 September 1908. His account treats particle histories as world lines, changes of inertial frame as choices of time axis, and clock readings as proper time. These are connected geometric ideas, rather than separate corrections to ordinary mechanics.[ref]
