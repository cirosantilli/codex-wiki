Complex quadratic Schur criterion (∣ab−cb∣≤∣a∣2−∣c∣2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Schur stability criterion
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
For a complex polynomial p(z)=az2+bz+c, assume ∣a∣>∣c∣ and put D=∣a∣2−∣c∣2 and E=ab−cb. Both roots of a polynomial lie in the closed unit disk if and only if ∣E∣≤D. Strict inequality puts both roots in the open disk. Under the strict product assumption ∣c/a∣<1, any unit-modulus root is automatically simple.
To prove the strict version, define p#(z)=z2p(1/z)​ and q=ap−cp#=z(Dz+E). On the unit circle ∣p#∣=∣p∣, so the Rouche theorem shows that a stable p and q have the same two interior zeros. Conversely Dp=aq+cq#, and the same Rouche theorem implies that two interior zeros of q give two interior zeros of p. The nonzero root of q is −E/D, proving the criterion. The closed version follows by continuity, replacing b by (1−ε)b in the sufficiency direction. In the necessity direction with a boundary root, replace p(z) by p(z/r) for r<1 close to one and let r→1. The product of the two roots of a polynomial excludes two unit-modulus roots and excludes a repeated unit-modulus root.
