Tetrahedron centroid-to-midpoint sum of squares

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Polyhedron Tetrahedron
OurBigBook.com Words: 60
Place the centroid of a tetrahedron at the origin, so its four vertex vectors satisfy r1​+r2​+r3​+r4​=0. Midpoints of opposite edges have opposite vectors, hence equal distances from the centroid. For the three opposite-edge pairs, choose midpoint vectors (r1​+rj​)/2, j=2,3,4. Expanding their squared lengths and using ∑j=24​rj​=−r1​ gives
∑j=24​​2r1​+rj​​​2=41​∑i=14​∣ri​∣2.
(16)
The argument uses no regularity or equal-edge assumption on the tetrahedron.
