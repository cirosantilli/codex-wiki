Parametrization of a conic by projection

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
For a nonsingular conic with a point p and a projective line L not containing p, send q∈L to the second intersection of pq with the conic, counted with multiplicity. The tangent direction gives p itself. The inverse sends any other conic point to its joining line's intersection with L, and sends p to the tangent's intersection. The Bézout theorem makes these maps inverse. With data over a field, the construction preserves rationality: a known rational root of the restricted quadratic equation leaves its other root rational too. A conic with a rational point is therefore rationally parametrized by a projective line.
