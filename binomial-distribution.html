Binomial distribution (Bin(n,p))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 923 Articles: 14
The binomial distribution counts successes in a fixed number n of independent Bernoulli trials with common success probability p. Equivalently,
K=B1​+⋯+Bn​,Bi​∼Bernoulli(p),P(K=k)=(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k,k=0,…,n.
(42)
Here n is a nonnegative integer and 0≤p≤1. The binomial coefficient (kn​)=n!/[k!(n−k)!] counts placements of the k successes; each particular arrangement has probability pk(1−p)n−k. The binomial theorem makes the masses sum to [p+(1−p)]n=1. At p=0 all mass is at zero, and at p=1 all mass is at n; these endpoint laws can be read directly without interpreting 00.
The linearity of expectation and addition of variances for independent random variables give
EK=np,Var(K)=np(1−p).
(43)
The fixed trial count, common success probability, and joint independence are substantive model assumptions. Sampling uniformly without replacement from a finite population instead leads to a hypergeometric distribution. Independent trials with different success probabilities generally give a Poisson binomial distribution. Common marginal success probabilities alone are insufficient: if all ten indicators equal the same Bernoulli random variable with p=0.3, their sum is zero with probability 0.7 or ten with probability 0.3. Its mean is still three, but its variance is 100(0.21)=21. The standard construction is given in Penn State STAT 504, binomial distribution.
For an exact constructed example, let each of ten independent trials choose uniformly from ten labelled tokens, with replacement, and mark three tokens as successes. Then p=3/10 and K∼Bin(10,0.3). Exactly three successes have probability
P(K=3)=(310​)(0.3)3(0.7)7=120(0.027)(0.0823543)=0.266827932.
(44)
The mean is 10(0.3)=3, the variance is 10(0.3)(0.7)=2.1, and the standard deviation is 2.1​≈1.4491. Zero successes have probability 0.710=0.0282475249, so at least one success has probability 1−0.710=0.9717524751. A single count calculation replaces enumeration of all 1010 equally likely token sequences.
Each bar's height is one probability mass. Three is the most likely count, but its mass is only about 26.7%: a most likely outcome need not occur most of the time.
