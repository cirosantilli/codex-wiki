Thermopile radiative measurement

OurBigBook.com Words: 276
PASCO's documented lamp experiment connects a TD-8553 thermopile sensor to a millivoltmeter and places it level with a lamp filament along a meter stick. Lamp and detector axes are aligned. With the lamp off, readings at several positions establish an average ambient thermal radiation background. With the lamp on at a fixed supply setting, detector readings at different distances are compared after background subtraction. Between readings, a reflective shield prevents the detector warming; its shutter also protects the reference junction when idle. Moving the shutter can move the sensor, so fixed geometry matters. The manual's lamp limits must be followed.[ref]
This protocol tests whether the background-corrected electric potential difference approximately follows the inverse-square law over a suitable distance range. It is a relative electromagnetic radiation measurement; those readings alone do not calibrate absorbed power in watts. The manual treats the filament as an approximate point source.[ref]
For an absolute calibration, independently known absorbed power and a monitored reference temperature would be needed. A useful additional control is to repeat a fixed detector position before and after a distance sweep: disagreement reveals possible drift. These additions test the measurement chain rather than assuming that background subtraction fixes every error. A finite source, changing illuminated area, reflections, changing reference temperature, and insufficient settling can alter the measured dependence. In particular, the time constant in thermopile thermal response explains why a quick reading and a settled reading may differ. The detector infers its own absorber temperature difference; inferring the source's temperature additionally requires its emissivity, optical geometry and an appropriate thermal radiation model.
