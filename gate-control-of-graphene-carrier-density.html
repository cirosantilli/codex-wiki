Gate control of graphene carrier density (Vg​−V0​=en/cg​+EF​/e)

 Home Chemistry Chemical substance Chemical element Carbon Graphene
OurBigBook.com Words: 305 Articles: 1
Consider an ideal uniform graphene sheet connected to an electron reservoir, with an insulated gate. Let cg​>0 be geometric capacitance per unit area, e>0 the elementary charge, and V0​ the neutral-point offset. Neglect temperature, trapped electric charge and spatial inhomogeneity. A change in gate voltage pays for both the electrostatic potential drop, en/cg​, and the change in the sheet's Fermi energy, EF​/e. Here EF​ is measured relative to the band crossing; it is negative for excess electron holes.
Combine this relation with the carrier density in a graphene cone. Write u=∣EF​∣/e, measured in volts, V=∣Vg​−V0​∣, and α=e3/[π(ℏvF​)2cg​]. Then
V=u+αu2,u=1+1+4αV​2V​,n=sgn(Vg​−V0​)π(ℏvF​)2e2u2​.
(25)
The positive root is unique because u+αu2 is strictly increasing for u≥0. The often useful geometric approximation n≃cg​(Vg​−V0​)/e neglects the Fermi energy term. It overestimates ∣n∣ in this model, especially near neutrality where relatively few states are available.
For a deliberately chosen model with cg​=0.010F/m2, vF​=106m/s, V0​=0 and Vg​=1V, the formulas give EF​≃0.252eV and n≃4.67×1012cm−2. The purely geometric value would be 6.24×1012cm−2. These are chosen-model predictions, not measurements of the historical devices. Numerical conversion uses the exact Planck constant and elementary charge.[ref]
