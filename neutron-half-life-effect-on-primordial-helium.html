Neutron half-life effect on primordial helium (Yp​≃1+rf​2rf​​e−Δt/τn​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Cosmology Big Bang nucleosynthesis Primordial helium mass fraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 86
After cosmological weak freeze-out, the surviving neutron fraction is xn​=rf​(1+rf​)−1e−Δt/τn​, where rf​ is the initial neutron-to-proton ratio and τn​=t1/2​/ln2 is the mean lifetime. Baryon number is conserved because a decaying neutron becomes a proton. If almost all surviving neutrons enter helium-4, neutron-limited helium synthesis gives the displayed primordial helium mass fraction. Increasing the half-life increases survival. If the longer lifetime also represents uniformly weaker weak interactions, it raises the temperature of cosmological weak freeze-out and the initial neutron fraction, reinforcing the helium increase. That last conclusion requires specifying how the weak rates change, rather than treating lifetime alone as a complete microscopic model.
