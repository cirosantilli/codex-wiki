Superadditivity and nonempty core do not imply Shapley stability

 ... Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Game theory Cooperative game theory Transferable utility game Superadditive coalitional game
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
On three players, let v(S)=1 exactly when S contains player 1 and at least one of players 2 and 3, and zero otherwise. This simple cooperative game is superadditive: disjoint coalitions cannot both contain player 1, so at most one can have value one. Its core of a cooperative game is exactly {(1,0,0)}, since singleton nonnegativity and the two winning-pair constraints force the other two coordinates to zero. In a random player ordering, player 1 is pivotal unless it comes first, whereas each other player is pivotal in just one of the six orders. Thus its Shapley value is (2/3,1/6,1/6), outside the core: coalition {1,2} receives only 5/6<1. Convex cooperative games have the stronger property that their Shapley value belongs to the core of a convex game.
