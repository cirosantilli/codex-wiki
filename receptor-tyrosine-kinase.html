Receptor tyrosine kinase

 Home Biology Protein Receptor (biochemistry) Cell-surface receptor
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
A receptor tyrosine kinase combines extracellular ligand recognition with an intracellular protein kinase protein domain. A membrane-spanning segment connects these parts across the cell membrane: the signal changes the intracellular machinery without requiring the ligand to enter the cytosol. The protein kinase transfers a phosphate group from ATP to selected tyrosine residues on proteins, including the receptor itself.[ref]
This architecture makes a reception event useful in two ways: it can change catalytic activity and assemble a local group of signaling proteins. Trans-autophosphorylation can create phosphotyrosine residues recognized by SH2 domains. Recruitment positions a downstream enzyme or relay protein near its partners. Neither every modified site nor every recruited protein has the same effect; association is a specific molecular connection, rather than a universal command to activate everything nearby.[ref]
Compare the epidermal growth factor receptor with the insulin receptor. Their common topology does not impose one activation geometry or make assembly equivalent to activity. The receptor phosphorylation assay consequently needs more than evidence that two protein chains associate.
