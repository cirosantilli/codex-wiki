Genotype

 Home Biology Genetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 430 Articles: 11
A genotype records the inherited alleles carried by an individual at one or more genetic loci. At a two-copy diploid genetic locus with alleles A,a, the possible unordered genotypes are AA,Aa,aa. The first and last are homozygotes; the middle is a heterozygote. The notation records inherited states, without yet asserting their phenotypes. See NHGRI: genotype.
An unordered Aa combines the two parental orders Aa and aA. This small bookkeeping point explains the factor two in Hardy-Weinberg proportions and in an Aa×Aa cross. If parental origin or arrangement across several genetic loci matters, a phase-known genotype retains extra information about the two haplotypes.
