Unattainability of absolute zero

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Third law of thermodynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
In a regular thermodynamic formulation, a state at absolute zero cannot be reached from positive temperature by finitely many cooling operations with finite control resources. The entropy-difference form of the third law of thermodynamics makes regular entropy curves approach a common limiting entropy. An isentropic process starting above that entropy cannot intersect the common zero-temperature endpoint.
For an illustrative pair of settings with S−S0​=a1​T and S−S0​=a2​T, 0<a1​<a2​, an isothermal entropy-reduction step followed by reversible adiabatic cooling gives Tn+1​=(a1​/a2​)Tn​. Every finite number of stages leaves Tn​>0. The model illustrates the entropy geometry; a general finite-time impossibility statement additionally needs assumptions about available interactions and cooling rates.
