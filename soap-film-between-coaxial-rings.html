Soap film between coaxial rings

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Second fundamental form Mean curvature Soap film pressure balance
OurBigBook.com Words: 391
Two equal circular rings of radius R in parallel planes at z=±L provide a boundary for an open film. In the ideal axisymmetric model, a stationary connected film has the catenoid profile
r(z)=acosh(z/a),R=acosh(L/a).
(37)
The catenoid existence threshold for equal rings explains why this equation can have two solutions, one solution, or none. The cancellation of principal curvatures holds for both solutions whenever they exist; the mean curvature equation alone does not select a stable minimal surface.
This ideal geometry uses R=1 and L/R=0.60. The two profiles have different waist radii a, although both have H=0. For either profile the surface area follows by integration:
Acat​=2πa2(U+sinhUcoshU),U=L/a.
(38)
At this illustrated separation both profiles have greater surface area than the two disconnected disks, whose total is 2πR2. This comparison disproves a claim of global area minimization in a class allowing disconnected competitors; it does not determine the sign of the second variation for small variations of a connected film.
A reported experiment used coaxial wire rings of radius 4cm, drawn from a surfactant solution and slowly separated until collapse. The solution contained tetradecyltrimethylammonium bromide in deionized water, with glycerol added to vary viscosity. Fluorescein enabled fluorescence imaging under cyan LED arrays; a Phantom V641 camera recorded 4,000 or 5,600 frames per second. Extracted contours gave neck radius, attachment angle, and inferred surface area. They showed neck narrowing and eventual breakup, rather than indefinite persistence of a stationary catenoid. Comparing solutions across viscosities helped distinguish liquid dissipation from the surrounding air's inertia, but the ring displacement beyond the last stable state was not precisely controlled. These are collapse observations, not direct measurements of equilibrium H. [ref]
A proposed classroom measurement would instead hold two equal rings coaxial on a translation stage, leave both sides open to the same gas, and photograph the settled silhouette beside a scale. Fit r(z)=acosh(z/a) and inspect fit residuals before inferring cancellation of the principal curvatures. Repeat at several separations and with the camera rotated about the axis to test rotational symmetry. Keep solution composition, ring wetting, and elapsed drainage time comparable, and shield the film from drafts. Film weight, drainage, evaporation, surfactant gradients, and rupture can invalidate the ideal soap film pressure balance. A silhouette fit alone neither measures the pressure jump nor tests all possible perturbations of a stable minimal surface.
