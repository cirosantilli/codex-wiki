Two-pion kaon event reconstruction (Mππ2​=(E+​+E−​)2−∣p+​+p−​∣2)

A two-track invariant mass and a direction test answer different questions. In units c=1, assign each track the pion mass mπ​ and reconstruct its momentum before magnetic deflection. With p±​=∣p±​∣ and opening angle α,
E±​=p±2​+mπ2​​,Mππ2​=2mπ2​+2(E+​E−​−p+​p−​cosα).
(12)
This follows by expanding the squared sum of the two four-momenta. The angle θ between the reconstructed parent momentum P=p+​+p−​ and the neutral particle beam direction n^ satisfies
cosθ=∣P∣P⋅n^​.
(13)
A fully reconstructed two-body decay channel gives the parent mass and θ≃0, within experimental resolution. A three-body decay channel with an unseen neutrino generally misses both constraints; an accidental correct invariant mass alone is insufficient. Notice that the track opening angle α need not be small even when the parent direction angle θ vanishes.
The 1964 analysis selected 490<Mππ​<510 MeV and remeasured events with cosθ>0.9995, comparing adjacent mass bands.[ref]
For an original dimensionless example, take mπ​=1, n^=(0,0,1) and p±​=(±3​,0,3). Each energy is 13​; the opening angle is 60 degrees since cosα=(9−3)/(9+3)=1/2. Yet P=(0,0,6) points exactly along the beam, and Mππ2​=4(13)−36=16. Thus a parent of mass 4 passes both ideal tests. These chosen numbers illustrate four-momentum conservation, not detector performance.
