Emissivity (ϵ)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Stefan–Boltzmann law
OurBigBook.com Words: 674 Articles: 6
Emissivity compares thermal emission from a surface with emission from an ideal blackbody at the same absolute temperature, using the same wavelength and angular averaging convention. For ordinary passive surfaces in the far field at local thermal equilibrium, the ratio lies between zero and one. A blackbody has emissivity one. Unless a spectral or directional qualification is given, the ϵ in M=ϵσT4 is total hemispherical emissivity: a ratio of emitted radiant exitances, not of all outgoing radiant flux including reflected radiation.[ref]
Emissivity belongs to a specified surface state, not just a bulk material name. Coating, oxidation and surface finish can change it; wavelength, viewing direction and temperature also matter. A surface that looks white in visible light need not have low infrared emissivity. This distinction is useful when choosing a radiator coating or interpreting a radiation-based temperature measurement: a narrow-band detector needs the appropriate spectral emissivity, and its signal can include reflected surrounding radiation. The grey body and diffuse thermal emitter assumptions are separate simplifications, addressing wavelength and direction respectively.
