Uniform derivative convergence with an anchored value

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Uniform convergence
OurBigBook.com Words: 181
Let fn​ be continuously differentiable functions on an interval I. If fn′​→g by uniform convergence on I and fn​(c)→A at one fixed c∈I, then fn​ converge locally uniformly to a continuously differentiable function f with f′=g. If I is bounded, the convergence is uniform on all of I.[ref]
The anchor fixes the additive constant that a derivative cannot see. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, define
f(x)=A+∫cx​g(t)dt,∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣≤∣fn​(c)−A∣+∣x−c∣∥fn′​−g∥∞​.
(85)
The uniform limit theorem makes g a continuous function, so the fundamental theorem of calculus gives f′=g. The displayed estimate proves uniform convergence wherever ∣x−c∣ has a common finite bound, and therefore proves locally uniform convergence on any interval.
For example, on [0,2] take fn​(x)=3+1/n+x2/2+x/n. Its derivative is x+1/n, with supremum error 1/n from g(x)=x, and fn​(0)→3. The reconstructed uniform limit is f(x)=3+x2/2; the exact supremum error is 3/n, equal to the bound above. Without an anchor, the constant functions fn​(x)=n have zero derivatives but no finite pointwise limit. On the real line, fn​(x)=x/n has convergent anchored values and uniformly convergent derivatives, but its supremum error from zero is infinite. The bounded-distance condition has a real role.
