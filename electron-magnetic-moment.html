Electron magnetic moment (μs​=−gs​μB​S/ℏ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic vector potential Magnetic dipole moment
OurBigBook.com Words: 862 Articles: 4
The electron magnetic moment couples an electron's angular momentum to a magnetic field. Its intrinsic spin contribution is
μs​=−gs​μB​ℏS​,μB​=2me​eℏ​,e>0.
(92)
Here μB​ is the Bohr magneton, and gs​ is the positive electron spin g-factor under this sign convention. The minus sign makes magnetic dipole moment antiparallel to spin angular momentum. An electron in a chemical atom can also contribute an orbital magnetic dipole moment; the measured response of a whole atomic level combines these contributions.
Measuring a tiny departure of gs​ from 2 tests how well a theory predicts an electron's interaction with its surroundings. The Dirac equation gives gs​=2 before radiative corrections; the anomalous magnetic moment makes a small correction experimentally accessible.[ref] A useful strategy is to compare two atomic energy levels whose orbital and spin contributions have different weights, rather than trying to calibrate an absolute magnetic field more accurately.
