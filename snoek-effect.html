Snoek effect

 ... Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Rheology Non-Newtonian fluid Viscoelasticity Anelasticity
OurBigBook.com Words: 118
The Snoek effect is anelasticity caused by stress-induced redistribution of mobile interstitial defects among differently oriented sites in a crystal. An applied stress favors orientations whose local distortion best accommodates it. At low frequency the atoms can track that bias and provide additional relaxed compliance; at high frequency their populations are nearly frozen. Dissipation is largest between these regimes. For a weak single relaxation, the loss tangent has the approximate shape Δωτ/[1+(ωτ)2], with τ=τ0​eEa​/(kB​T) for thermally activated hopping. The peak is near ωτ=1. This describes a standard mechanism for interstitial impurities in body-centred cubic metals, not every damping mechanism in every alloy. [ref]
