Root bracket ([a,b])

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Bracketing method
OurBigBook.com Words: 62
A root bracket is a closed interval known to contain at least one zero of a function. For a real-valued continuous function on [a,b], a strict change of sign between f(a) and f(b) certifies such an enclosure by the intermediate value theorem. An endpoint with value zero already supplies a zero of a function. For example, [1,2] is a root bracket for the polynomial f(x)=x2−2, since f(1)=−1 and f(2)=2. A root bracket need not contain a unique zero of a function.
