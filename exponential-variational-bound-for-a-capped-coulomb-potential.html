Exponential variational bound for a capped Coulomb potential (VR​(r)=−κ/max(R,r))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Ground state Ground-state energy Quantum variational principle
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
For κ>0 and the radial exponential trial state e−αr, α>0, put a=ℏ2/(mκ). Direct radial integration gives the Rayleigh quotient
E(α)=κ[2aα2​−R1−(1+αR)e−2αR​].
(71)
The kinetic expectation is ℏ2α2/(2m); the potential expectation follows by splitting its radial integral at R. The common prefactor cancels from the stationary condition, giving aα=(1+2αR)e−2αR. Its positive solution is unique, since the left side increases and the right side decreases. For ε=R/a→0, it satisfies aα=1−2ε2+O(ε3).
The quantum variational principle makes E(α) an upper bound for the ground energy. The explicit choice α=1/a gives E0​(R)≤(κ/a)[1/2−{1−(1+ε)e−2ε}/ε], whose first correction to −κ/(2a) is 2κR2/(3a3). The true energy is no lower than the point-Coulomb value because capping makes the potential less attractive.
