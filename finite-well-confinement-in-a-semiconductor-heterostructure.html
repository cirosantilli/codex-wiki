Consider an original one-dimensional effective mass model with V(x)=0 for ∣x∣<a and V(x)=V0​>0 outside. Take the same positive effective mass m∗ everywhere, an abrupt interface, and no interactions. The wavefunction represents a transverse envelope; free motion parallel to the layer is outside this calculation. A double heterostructure laser need not be thin enough for this quantization to dominate its operation.

For the even bound state, the interior solution is Acos(zs) and the exterior solution is Acosze−w(∣s∣−1). For the odd bound state, replace cos(zs) by sin(zs) and cosz by sgn(s)sinz. The physical wavefunction is ψ(x)=ϕ(x/a)/a​.
Wavefunction normalization includes both exterior tails, not just the active layer. Direct integration gives
Aeven−2​=1+2zsin(2z)​+wcos2z​,Aodd−2​=1−2zsin(2z)​+wsin2z​.

The first two terms are the interior integral; the last is the total exterior integral. Thus the interior probability is Pin​=A2[1±sin(2z)/(2z)], with the upper sign for even parity.
Choose z0​=2. There is one even root in (0,π/2) and one odd root in (π/2,2); higher branches start beyond z0​. Each left-hand side increases on its stated interval while 4−z2​ decreases, proving uniqueness there. Numerically,

These are calculated values of the chosen finite square well. The excited bound state lies closer to the barrier and has a longer exterior tail: being bound does not mean having unit probability inside the well.
This model demonstrates electronic confinement, not optical refractive index confinement or a complete laser. Unequal effective masses, band bending, interface disorder and interactions require a more detailed model. In particular, continuity of the ordinary derivative above uses the chosen constant effective mass assumption.
