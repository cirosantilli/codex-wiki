Constant-coefficient Riccati equation (y′+(y−a)(y−b)=0,y=z′/z)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis WKB approximation Liouville-Green exponential ansatz Riccati equation Linearization of a Riccati equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
The linearization of a Riccati equation turns this equation into z′′−(a+b)z′+abz=0. For distinct real roots, every nonzero linearizing solution is z=Aeax+Bebx and y=(aAeax+bBebx)/(Aeax+Bebx) on intervals with nonzero denominator. The constants y=a,b arise when one coefficient vanishes. With A=0, the relative coefficient B/A can have either sign; replacing it by a fixed-sign real exponential would omit a real family of solutions.
For a=b, the solutions are z=(A+Bx)eax and y=a+B/(A+Bx). The equilibrium y=a occurs at B=0. Zeros of a nontrivial z give poles of a Riccati solution from zeros of its linearizing solution, so the corresponding y is defined separately between them. Multiplying both coefficients by the same nonzero scalar changes z but not y.
