Hess's law

 Home Chemistry Thermochemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
Hess's law states that compatible chemical reaction enthalpies add when their equations add. Reversing a chemical reaction reverses its enthalpy change; multiplying the entire chemical reaction equation by a factor multiplies that change by the same factor. The reason is that enthalpy is a state function: intermediate states cancel from the differences. A cycle supplies one independent balance relation; it cannot determine two unknown inputs without further information.
The law is named after Germain Henri Hess.
For specified initial and final thermodynamic states, the change is ΔH=Hfinal​−Hinitial​. Because enthalpy is an extensive variable, a quoted reaction ΔH belongs to the amount of substance specified for each species in its chemical equation. Reversing the equation changes the sign; scaling every coefficient scales ΔH; adding compatible equations adds their ΔH values. A per-mole value must say which chemical equation and normalization it uses. Match temperature, pressure, thermodynamic phases, chemical solution compositions and reference states before cancellation; the same formula in a gas and a liquid represents different states.[ref]
The value of Hess's law is that an inaccessible reaction enthalpy can be obtained from measurable transformations. The algebra does not require those transformations to be steps of the actual reaction mechanism. Neither an activation energy nor a reaction rate follows from the endpoint difference: a catalyst may change the reaction rate while preserving the compatible initial and final enthalpies.
