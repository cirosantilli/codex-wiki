Separable differential equation (y′=a(t)b(y))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 287 Articles: 2
A first-order ordinary differential equation is separable if its explicit form factors as y′=a(t)b(y). On an interval where b(y)=0, write dy/b(y)=a(t)dt and take antiderivatives to obtain an implicit solution:
∫b(y)dy​=∫a(t)dt+C.
(34)
An initial condition determines C and selects a branch and domain. The useful simplification is that the two integrals each involve only one variable, even when the original equation is nonlinear.
For Newton's law of cooling, division by y−Ta​ gives dy/(y−Ta​)=−kdt. To keep the logarithm dimensionless, integrate relative to the initial excess temperature:
log​y0​−Ta​y(t)−Ta​​​=−k(t−t0​).
(35)
When y0​=Ta​, continuity preserves the sign of the excess temperature until any potential zero, giving y−Ta​=(y0​−Ta​)e−k(t−t0​). This formula never reaches zero at finite time. Taking its derivative verifies the ordinary differential equation, and substitution at t0​ verifies the initial condition.
Always check what division removed. Each zero b(y∗​)=0 gives a constant solution of a differential equation y=y∗​. Cooling therefore also has the equilibrium point y=Ta​, omitted by division but included in the final explicit formula. For the nonunique initial value problem y′=∣y∣​, division by ∣y∣​ only describes the moving pieces: a valid solution may remain zero for an arbitrary time before joining one. Separation is a method for finding solutions, not a uniqueness theorem.
