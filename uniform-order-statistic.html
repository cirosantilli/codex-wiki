Uniform order statistic (U(r)​∼Beta(r,n+1−r))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Order statistic
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
For independent and identically distributed random variables U1​,…,Un​ with the continuous uniform distribution on (0,1), the rth order statistic has Beta distribution
U(r)​∼Beta(r,n+1−r),1≤r≤n.
(67)
Its probability density function, mean, and variance are
fr​(t)=(r−1)!(n−r)!n!​tr−1(1−t)n−r,0<t<1,
(68)
E[U(r)​]=n+1r​,Var(U(r)​)=(n+1)2(n+2)r(n+1−r)​.
(69)
The probability density function is zero outside [0,1]; values assigned to its endpoints do not change any probability. Peter Kempthorne's MIT lecture notes derive the uniform order statistic law and these moments. [ref].
For n=5, the minimum, sample median, and sample maximum correspond to r=1,3,5. Their probability density functions are 5(1−t)4, 30t2(1−t)2, and 5t4, respectively. The sample median therefore has Beta distribution Beta(3,3), mean 1/2, and variance 1/28≈0.0357143. Its cumulative distribution function is
P(U(3)​≤t)=10t3(1−t)2+5t4(1−t)+t5=10t3−15t4+6t5,0≤t≤1.
(70)
For instance, P(U(3)​≤1/4)=53/512≈0.103516. The mean of the minimum is 1/6, and the mean of the sample maximum is 5/6. The fixed observed sample median 0.4 in the order statistic example is one possible outcome; 1/2 is the average over repeated statistical samples from this uniform distribution.
Each curve has area one. The extreme order statistics favor the ends, while the sample median favors the center. The endpoint density height 5 is legitimate: the probability density function gives area per unit length, rather than point probability. In particular, a continuous uniform distribution has zero probability of producing an endpoint.
For a short proof, the binomial distribution counting identity under order statistic gives P(U(r)​≤t)=∑k=rn​(kn​)tk(1−t)n−k. Taking its derivative cancels consecutive terms, leaving the displayed probability density function. Recognize its normalizing factorial coefficient as that of the Beta distribution, then substitute α=r, β=n+1−r into the Beta distribution mean and variance formulas. Although the original observations are independent, their order statistics generally are dependent; see the positive covariance of two uniform order statistics.
