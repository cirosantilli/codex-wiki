Adiabatic invariant (I)

Words: 290 Articles: 5
Hamiltonian mechanics OurBigBook.com
A quantity conserved to leading order when a parameter varies slowly compared with the natural motion period. The action variable of a regular periodic Hamiltonian orbit is a standard example, as explained by adiabatic invariance of the action.

Circular-shell adiabatic contraction (ri​Mi​(ri​)=rf​Mf​(rf​))

Particle-speed invariant between slowly approaching walls (vℓ≈constant)

Words: 69
Adiabatic invariant OurBigBook.com
An elastic collision with a wall moving toward the particle at speed V changes the particle speed by approximately 2V when recoil is negligible. The collision rate is approximately v/ℓ for v≫V, and ℓ˙=−2V, yielding v˙/v=−ℓ˙/ℓ. H=21​(px2​+py2​)+V(x,y),V(x,y)=21​(x2+y2)+x2y−31​y3.
(1)
Its Hamilton's equations give x¨=−x−2xy and y¨​=−y−x2+y2. Its potential energy combines an isotropic quadratic minimum with a cubic interaction.

Hénon-Heiles potential

Words: 139
Hénon-Heiles system OurBigBook.com
The potential energy V(x,y)=21​(x2+y2)+x2y−y3/3 in the Hénon-Heiles system has the following expression in polar coordinates:
V=21​r2+31​r3sin(3θ),
(2)
so it is invariant under rotation through 2π/3. Solving ∇V=0 gives a strict local minimum at the origin and three saddle points, at (0,1) and (±3​/2,−1/2). All three saddle points have value 1/6. Their common level set factors exactly:
6V−1=(2y+1)[3x2−(y−1)2].
(3)
Thus this level set is the union of the three lines y=−1/2 and y=1±3​x. They bound an equilateral triangle containing the local minimum. The Hessian matrix is the identity at the origin; at each of the other three stationary points it has eigenvalues 3 and −1. For energies 0≤E<1/6, the central accessible region is bounded; above that value the three saddle passages allow paths from the centre to arbitrarily large radius. This follows directly from the radial potential: in each direction with sin(3θ)<0, its radial maximum occurs at r=−1/sin(3θ), with value 1/[6sin2(3θ)]≥1/6.

Inverted quartic oscillator phase portrait (K=x2+y2−y4)

For Φ(R,ϕ−Ωt,z) with constant pattern angular velocity Ω, ordinary specific energy satisfies E˙=∂t​Φ=−Ω∂ϕ​Φ and specific axial angular momentum satisfies L˙z​=−∂ϕ​Φ. Isolating integral

Words: 196 Articles: 1
Integral of motion OurBigBook.com
An isolating integral is a regular single-valued integral of motion whose fixed value restricts accessible phase space to a lower-dimensional level set. Functional independence of several such integrals means their differentials are linearly independent on the region considered. A central potential has specific orbital energy and the three components of specific angular momentum as four generically independent integrals. A Kepler orbit has a fifth independent integral supplied by its fixed apsidal direction, encoded by the Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector; its vector components obey two constraints with energy and angular momentum. Circular and radial orbits are degenerate cases of this count.

Isolating integrals and orbital closure dimension (N=6−d)

Words: 112
Isolating integral OurBigBook.com
In three-dimensional autonomous mechanics, a regular common level set of N independent isolating integrals has dimension 6−N in phase space. If an orbit is dense in a d-dimensional invariant torus and the integrals characterize its closure, N=6−d. A generic three-frequency regular orbit has three integrals; a spherical nonclosed rosette has energy and three components of specific angular momentum, with two-dimensional closure and four integrals. A periodic nonstationary orbit has one-dimensional closure and can be isolated by five independent orbit labels on the invariant family. The maximum is five wherever the Hamiltonian vector field is nonzero, since all integral differentials annihilate that field. A single resonant orbit need not yield an additional smooth single-valued integral on an open neighborhood; global superintegrability is stronger than orbital closure.
