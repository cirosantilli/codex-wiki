Work in a two-level sudden quench (⟨W⟩=(Δi​−Δf​cosθ)/2)

The drive can supply or extract work while the quantum state stays approximately fixed. For a differentiable bounded Hamiltonian operator, the Schrödinger equation gives
dtd​⟨H(t)⟩=⟨∂t∂H(t)​⟩.
(31)
The state-evolution terms cancel, leaving the external drive's power. For a finite ideal step the change in mean energy is ⟨ψ∣Hf​−Hi​∣ψ⟩. Thus conservation of energy for a closed system with a time-independent Hamiltonian operator does not assert conservation of the driven subsystem's energy during switching.
Specify work operationally by two energy measurements: one before the switch and one after. For the prepared initial ground state, the first outcome is definite; the second samples the new Born rule probabilities. This protocol gives a distribution of measured energy differences, rather than generally the measurement distribution of the single operator Hf​−Hi​.[ref]
Wg​=2Δi​−Δf​​with probability c2,We​=2Δi​+Δf​​with probability s2,
(32)
and hence
⟨Hf​⟩=−2Δf​​cosθ,⟨W⟩=2Δi​−Δf​cosθ​,Var(W)=4Δf2​​sin2θ.
(33)
For Δi​=Δf​=Δ and θ=π/3, the work is 0 or Δ, with probabilities 3/4 and 1/4; its expectation is Δ/4 and its variance is 3Δ2/16. The initial mean energy −Δ/2 becomes −Δ/4. Using different initial and final scalar offsets would also shift the measured work; the displayed Hamiltonian operators fix that convention explicitly.
