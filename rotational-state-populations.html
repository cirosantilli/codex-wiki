Rotational state populations (PJ​=(2J+1)e−hBJ(J+1)/(kB​T)/Qrot​)

For the spin-free linear rigid rotor in thermal equilibrium at temperature T>0, assume all integer J≥0 are allowed and ignore nuclear-spin restrictions. Each J level has 2J+1 quantum states sharing one energy eigenvalue, an instance of degenerate energy levels, so the Boltzmann distribution gives
Qrot​=∑J=0∞​(2J+1)e−hBJ(J+1)/(kB​T),PJ​=Qrot​(2J+1)e−hBJ(J+1)/(kB​T)​.
(11)
Here Qrot​ is a rotational partition function, h the Planck constant, kB​ the Boltzmann constant, and B a frequency in Hz. Thus hB/(kB​T) is dimensionless. The 2J+1 factor counts magnetic quantum numbers; it does not mean that a whole level is one quantum state. For the stipulated B=5GHz, the most populated levels are J=2 at 3K and J=7 at 30K.
Population alone is insufficient to predict intensity. In weak, unsaturated absorption, the integrated absorption coefficient includes the transition frequency, the squared electric dipole moment matrix element, and the difference of lower and upper substate populations because stimulated emission returns energy to the field. A pulsed FTMW spectroscopy signal additionally depends on excitation, quantum coherence, cavity tuning, and receiver response.[ref]
For an isotropic sample with polarization of an electromagnetic wave along z, write dz​=dcosθ. Normalized spherical harmonics give
∣⟨J+1,M∣cosθ∣J,M⟩∣2=(2J+1)(2J+3)(J+1)2−M2​,∑M=−JJ​∣⟨J+1,M∣cosθ∣J,M⟩∣2=3J+1​.
(12)
The last equality follows from ∑M=−JJ​M2=J(J+1)(2J+1)/3. Combining this sum with the per-substate Boltzmann distribution and subtracting stimulated emission gives relative integrated absorption areas
AJ​∝Qrot​νJ​(J+1)​e−hBJ(J+1)/(kB​T)(1−e−hνJ​/(kB​T)),νJ​=2B(J+1).
(13)
The common factor includes d2/3 and cancels upon normalization. This formula describes integrated weak absorption in a thermal, spin-free linear rigid rotor, not arbitrary peak heights. The example's strongest integrated lines originate at J=3 and J=13 for 3 and 30K, respectively: they differ from the most populated levels. A missing line can reflect population, coupling, coverage, or sensitivity; it does not prove that the corresponding quantum state is absent.
