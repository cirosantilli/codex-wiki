Kurtosis (κ=E[(X−EX)4]/Var(X)2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 725 Articles: 4
For a real random variable X with mean μ=EX, finite positive variance 0<σ2=Var(X)<∞, and finite fourth moment, kurtosis is the fourth central moment divided by the square of the variance:
κ(X)=σ4E[(X−μ)4]​=E[Z4],Z=σX−μ​.
(2)
The standard score Z measures distance from the mean in standard deviations. Its fourth power gives a value ten standard deviations away a weight 104=10000 inside the expected value. Thus a small probability mass far from the mean can strongly affect kurtosis. It is useful for describing the contribution of extreme standard scores, but does not by itself describe the height or shape of a probability density function near its peak.[ref]
The normal distribution has kurtosis 3. A random scale can increase kurtosis even when the conditional distribution is normal. A constant random variable has zero variance, so this ratio is undefined. If the variance is finite and positive but the fourth central moment is infinite, the ratio can be written +∞ in an extended sense; it is not a finite kurtosis. If the variance itself is infinite or the mean is undefined, the displayed normalization is unavailable.
