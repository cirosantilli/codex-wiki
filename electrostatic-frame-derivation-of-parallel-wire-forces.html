Electrostatic-frame derivation of parallel-wire forces

OurBigBook.com Words: 153
Consider lab-neutral parallel wires with stationary positive line density +λ and electron line density −λ. Let wire 1's electrons move at v1​ along the wire and wire 2's at v2​. The conventional currents are Ij​=−λvj​. In the rest frame of wire 2's electrons, wire 1's transformed total linear charge density is λ1′​=γ2​[0−v2​I1​/c2]=γ2​λv1​v2​/c2. Its radial electric field is λ1′​/(2πϵ0​d).
A wire-2 electron at rest there feels only an electric force. Transforming its transverse force back to the lab divides it by γ2​, and multiplying by the lab electron count per length λ/e gives
f⊥​/ℓ=−2πϵ0​c2dλ2v1​v2​​=−2πdμ0​I1​I2​​,
(44)
with positive radial direction away from wire 1. The lab-stationary positive charges feel zero net electric force because wire 1 is neutral in their frame. Equal current directions attract; opposite directions repel. Transforming force on each carrier and then counting carriers in the specified frame avoids an incorrect extra length-contraction factor in force per unit length.
