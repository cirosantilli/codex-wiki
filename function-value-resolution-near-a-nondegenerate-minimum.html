Function-value resolution near a nondegenerate minimum (δx≍δf/∣f′′(x∗​)∣​)

Near a smooth local minimum x∗​ with f′(x∗​)=0 and f′′(x∗​)>0, Taylor's theorem gives
f(x∗​+h)−f(x∗​)=21​f′′(x∗​)h2+o(h2).
(29)
If evaluated function values have absolute uncertainty of order δf, differences on that scale cannot reliably identify smaller displacements than order δf/f′′(x∗​)​. In ordinary floating-point arithmetic, when f(x∗​)=0 and evaluations have relative error of order ϵ, this becomes
∣h∣∼ϵf′′(x∗​)∣f(x∗​)∣​​.
(30)
For unit-scale values and curvature, this is the square root of machine epsilon, rather than machine epsilon itself. It explains why a derivative-free golden-section search can lose reliable comparisons while candidate positions remain representably different.[ref]
This is a precision limit of the evaluated values, not an invariant property of the minimizer. Accurately subtracting a known constant, obtaining values at higher precision, or locating a zero of an accurately evaluated derivative can change the attainable position accuracy. A zero minimum with relative error controlled on its small values also needs separate analysis.
