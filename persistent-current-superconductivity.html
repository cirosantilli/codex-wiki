Persistent current (superconductivity, I)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 221
A superconducting persistent current is an electric current circulating in a closed path with superconductivity after the external driving electromotive force has ceased. Its associated magnetic field makes the circulation observable without inserting a measuring instrument into the path. This article concerns macroscopic circulation in superconductivity. A finite experiment establishes persistence over its observation window with a stated measurement error, rather than directly observing an infinite lifetime.
The ideal circuit connection is flux conservation in a superconducting loop. In a fixed loop with self-inductance L, a single circulating electric current I, and externally imposed linked magnetic flux Φext​, the lumped circuit approximation gives
LdtdI​+RI=−dtdΦext​​.
(1)
When the electrical resistance R vanishes, integrating gives LI+Φext​=constant. Removing the external linked magnetic flux therefore changes the circulating electric current; holding it fixed afterwards preserves that electric current. This is a circuit approximation with a specified self-inductance, not a derivation of the microscopic state of superconductivity. For thin paths where the carrier contribution matters, fluxoid and flux quantization in a superconductor supplies the more careful description.
For constant L>0, constant effective electrical resistance R≥0, and no changing external linked magnetic flux, the RL circuit instead predicts
I(t)=I0​e−Rt/L,τ=RL​(R>0).
(2)
This exponential decay follows by separating dI/I=−(R/L)dt. A long relaxation time tests extremely small electrical resistance without trying to resolve an equally tiny steady electric potential difference. The stored magnetic energy is U=LI2/2, so the same model gives dU/dt=−RI2, exactly the Joule heating loss. The finite energy stored in the loop supplies the magnetic readout; the circulating electric current is not a continuously powered source of useful work.
