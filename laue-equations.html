Laue equations (ai​⋅(sout​−sin​)=mi​λ)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering X-ray diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 248
For incident and outgoing unit propagation vectors sin​ and sout​, wavelength λ>0, and primitive lattice translation vectors a1​,a2​,a3​, the Laue equations are
a1​⋅(sout​−sin​)=hλ,a2​⋅(sout​−sin​)=kλ,a3​⋅(sout​−sin​)=lλ,(h,k,l)∈Z3.
(30)
All three must hold for the same wavelength and outgoing direction. The integers label a reciprocal lattice vector; they need not be coprime. For wavevectors kin,out​=(2π/λ)sin,out​, equivalently
G=kout​−kin​=hb1​+kb2​+lb3​,ai​⋅bj​=2πδij​.
(31)
This uses angular wavenumbers; the crystallographic convention without 2π instead uses s/λ.[ref]
For a short proof, translate a scatterer through ai​. Its incident wave phase changes by kin​⋅ai​ and propagation to the same distant observation direction contributes −kout​⋅ai​. The relative wave phase factor is therefore exp(−iG⋅ai​). It equals one for all lattice translations exactly when G belongs to the reciprocal lattice. A finite crystal lattice structure factor broadens the ideal maxima; the crystal basis structure factor can cancel them. Geometric phase matching is necessary for a sharp reflection, but does not guarantee a visible spot.
For elastic scattering, the additional constraint is
∣kin​+G∣2=∣kin​∣2,2kin​⋅G+∣G∣2=0.
(32)
It is an Ewald sphere centered at −kin​ in the space of transfer vectors G. The parent elastic Bragg scattering condition uses q=kin​−kout​=−G, so its displayed condition has the opposite sign. Both give Bragg's law for the same lattice planes.
