PCR controls

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics Polymerase chain reaction PCR specificity
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
PCR controls are scientific controls that separate different explanations for a readout. A no-template reaction contains the PCR primers and copying reagents but replaces the added template with water or sample buffer. Amplification there can indicate carried-over DNA or a primer dimer; a clean result does not exclude contamination confined to one sample.[ref]
Different comparisons answer different questions. A known amplifiable template tests whether the PCR primers, DNA polymerase and thermal cycler can produce the expected amplicon. An extraction blank follows sample preparation without adding the sampled material. A separate amplifiable interval in the sample can test whether recovered DNA supports copying, although success there does not guarantee success at the target. A length standard calibrates gel electrophoresis; it is not a control for the copying reaction. These comparisons are an analytical design extension to the teaching example below, not a claim that its student protocol includes every comparison.
