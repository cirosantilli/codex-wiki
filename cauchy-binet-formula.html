Cauchy–Binet formula

For matrices A∈Rr×m and B∈Rm×r with r≤m,
det(AB)=∑S⊆{1,…,m}∣S∣=r​​detA:,S​detBS,:​,
(24)
where the indices of S are taken in increasing order. To prove it, write the jth column of AB as ∑q​Bqj​A:,q​ and expand its determinant by multilinearity. Terms with a repeated q vanish by alternation. For each remaining index set S={s1​<⋯<sr​}, collect all permutations qj​=sσ(j)​. The factor from A is sgn(σ)detA:,S​; the sum of the products sgn(σ)∏j​Bsσ(j)​,j​ is detBS,:​. This proves the formula. Applying it to selected rows and columns proves that a product of compatible totally nonnegative matrices is totally nonnegative.
