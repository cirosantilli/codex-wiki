Chemostat

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology
OurBigBook.com Words: 996 Articles: 6
A chemostat maintains a well-mixed culture at constant volume V by feeding fresh nutrient solution and removing culture at the same volumetric flow Q. Its dilution rate is D=Q/V. Mixing makes the outflow's nutrient concentration and biomass concentration equal to those inside the vessel; continuous replacement supplies nutrient and also removes growing cells.
The simplest one-nutrient chemostat uses nutrient concentration s(t), biomass concentration x(t), constant feed nutrient concentration sin​>0, and constant biomass yield Y>0. Assume a cell-free feed, one growth-limiting nutrient, constant volume, and no cell retention, death or additional nutrient consumption for maintenance. With specific growth rate μ(s), its ordinary differential equations are
dtds​=D(sin​−s)−Yμ(s)x​,dtdx​=(μ(s)−D)x.
(2)
The nutrient balance contains inflow Dsin​, outflow Ds, and consumption μ(s)x/Y; the biomass balance contains production μ(s)x and removal Dx. If s is in grams of nutrient per litre and x in grams of dry biomass per litre, Y is grams of dry biomass produced per gram of nutrient consumed. Thus both balances have units of their respective concentration per time. The Monod equation supplies an empirical choice of μ(s); the balances themselves express the stated production, consumption and transport assumptions.
The appeal of a chemostat is that, at a positive chemostat steady state, adjusting the pump sets the specific growth rate to D. The residual nutrient concentration then adjusts to that rate, and the available nutrient together with biomass yield sets the biomass concentration. This gives a controlled way to compare cultures at selected growth rates and a simple quantitative warning of chemostat washout.
Two 1950 publications on continuous culture are Novick and Szilard's Description of the Chemostat, Science 112(2920), 715-716, dated 15 December, and Monod's La technique de culture continue. Théorie et applications, Annales de l'Institut Pasteur 79, 390-410. The latter's original bibliographic details are reproduced in the contents of Monod's collected papers.
