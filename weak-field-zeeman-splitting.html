Weak-field Zeeman splitting (ΔE=μB​gJ​mJ​B)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Zeeman splitting
OurBigBook.com Words: 317 Articles: 1
Use the leading nonrelativistic magnetic coupling for an electronic level with total angular momentum operator J, and choose the z axis along a uniform external magnetic field B=Bz^, B≥0. Write its magnetic quantum number as mJ​, so Jz​∣J,mJ​⟩=ℏmJ​∣J,mJ​⟩. There are 2J+1 possibilities from −J to J. With Landé g-factor gJ​, the first-order shift is
ΔE=μB​gJ​mJ​B,μB​=2me​eℏ​,Δνul​=hμB​B​(gu​mu​−gl​ml​).
(5)
Here e>0 is the magnitude of the electron electric charge, me​ its mass, h the Planck constant, and μB​ the Bohr magneton. Both gJ​ and mJ​ are dimensionless; with B measured in tesla and μB​ in joules per tesla, ΔE is in joules. The photon frequency shift Δνul​ is in hertz, not angular frequency; indices u,l denote upper and lower levels. NIST states this weak-field convention and its orbital/spin assumptions explicitly.[ref]
The sign follows directly from magnetic dipole potential energy. Within this electronic J multiplet, the projected magnetic dipole moment is μ=−gJ​μB​J/ℏ. Therefore HZ​=−μ⋅B has the displayed positive coefficient. Subtracting the shifts of the two levels and using photon energy hνul​=Eu​−El​ gives the transition formula. For the orbital case, μB​/h=e/(4πme​) gives the same leading frequency spacing as the classical charged oscillator. This explains the historical sensitivity to charge divided by mass; the quantum state derivation here is modern.
“Weak” compares interaction energies: mixing with neighbouring fine structure levels must be small, so J remains a useful quantum number. The formula keeps the term linear in B and neglects quadratic shifts. It also omits hyperfine structure; if that is resolved and controls the coupling, the appropriate total electronic-plus-nuclear angular momentum and splitting factor must replace J,gJ​. Weak compared with fine structure does not automatically mean weak compared with hyperfine structure.
