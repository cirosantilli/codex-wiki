Sub-Doppler cooling

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods Laser cooling
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
Real atoms have multiple internal quantum states. Optical pumping and spatially varying electromagnetic polarization can remove kinetic energy by additional mechanisms, producing sub-Doppler cooling below the ideal Doppler cooling limit. In polarization-gradient cooling, state-dependent energy shifts from the Stark effect and delayed optical pumping make atoms repeatedly climb potential energy hills. This mechanism needs the internal-state structure omitted by the independent-beam two-level system above. [ref].
William D. Phillips's team tested unexpectedly cold sodium molasses with time-of-flight atom detection. A probe above the cloud initially saw no atoms; deliberately heating them verified that the detector worked. Moving the probe below the cloud produced arrival signals consistent with temperatures below the Doppler cooling limit. Independent release/recapture checks used sharply apertured beams and included gravity; uncertain cloud and probe geometry still limited the inferred temperature. These controls tested the thermometer as well as the cooling theory. [ref].
