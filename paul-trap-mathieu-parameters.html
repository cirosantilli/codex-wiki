Paul trap Mathieu parameters (ax​=4QU/(md2Ω2), qx​=2QV/(md2Ω2))

OurBigBook.com Words: 190
Specify the convention before quoting a stability parameter. Choose the ideal, rotationally symmetric electric potential
Φ(r,t)=2d2U−VcosΩt​(x2+y2−2z2),m>0,d>0,Ω>0.
(20)
Here U is a static voltage coefficient, V is a peak RF voltage coefficient, d is the length entering this displayed normalization, m is mass, Q is signed electric charge, and Ω is the drive angular frequency. The coefficient V is neither a peak-to-peak voltage nor an RMS voltage. An apparatus's ring-to-cap voltage requires matching its electrode surfaces to this electric potential; electrode radius alone does not define our d. The minus sign fixes the RF phase at t=0.
With τ=Ωt/2, Newton's second law gives a Mathieu equation for each Cartesian coordinate ui​:
dτ2d2ui​​+(ai​−2qi​cos2τ)ui​=0,(ay​,qy​)=(ax​,qx​),(az​,qz​)=(−2ax​,−2qx​).
(21)
Thus a three-dimensional Paul trap needs simultaneous bounded solutions in all three directions. Changing the sign of V reverses qi​ and shifts the RF phase by half a cycle; it does not change the ideal infinite-time stability regions. Rescaling the voltage or length convention changes the numerical parameters. Floquet theory determines stability of the full Mathieu equation; the small-parameter approximation below does not establish stability for arbitrary qi​. Bounded mathematical motion can still hit an electrode if its amplitude exceeds the available space.
