Double cover of the sphere with four branch values (d=2,Rf​=4,gX​=1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Riemann surfaces Riemann-Hurwitz formula
OurBigBook.com Words: 232
Suppose a nonconstant holomorphic map f:X→P1 from a connected compact Riemann surface has degree of a holomorphic map two and exactly four distinct branch values of a holomorphic map. The valency theorem forces each of their fibres to contain one ramification point with ep​=2, a simple ramification point of a holomorphic map. Hence
2gX​−2=2(−2)+4(2−1)=0,gX​=1.
(14)
This determines the genus without recognizing a torus in a drawing.
A concrete realization is the compact Riemann surface obtained by adding the two points at infinity to the even-degree hyperelliptic model given by the algebraic curve
w2=P(z)=(z+2)(z+1)(z−1)(z−2).
(15)
Projection to z has degree of a holomorphic map two. At a simple zero a of P, w is a local coordinate and z−a=w2/P′(a)+O(w4), so ep​=2. At infinity set u=1/z and s=w/z2: then s2=(1−4u2)(1−u2). There are two points with u=0, s=±1, each unramified since u is a local coordinate. The four finite roots are therefore all the branch values of a holomorphic map.[ref]
Choose a triangulation of a surface on the Riemann sphere with these four branch values of a holomorphic map as vertices, combinatorially the boundary of a tetrahedron. Its counts are V=4,E=6,F=4. Upstairs, each vertex has one preimage and each open edge and face has two lifts: VX​=4,EX​=12,FX​=8, giving Euler characteristic 4−12+8=0. This is a cell schematic, not a geometric immersion of X. The Triangulation proof of the Riemann-Hurwitz formula applies the same counting idea after refining a general triangulation of a surface around the branch values of a holomorphic map.[ref]
