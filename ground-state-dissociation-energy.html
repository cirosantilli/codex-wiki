Ground-state dissociation energy (D0​)

 Home Chemistry Chemical bond Bond-dissociation energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
The ground-state dissociation energy starts at the lowest vibrational quantum state, with the lowest allowed rotational quantum state and the fragment states explicitly specified. For a diatomic molecule whose fragments are ground-state chemical atoms, omitting any additional rotational correction,
D0​=De​−EZP​,
(29)
where EZP​ is the vibrational zero-point energy measured upward from the well minimum. The initial molecule already has this energy, so less additional energy is needed to reach the same final limit.[ref]
For fragments that are themselves molecules, include their zero-point energies too. Define De​ as the electronic energy difference between separately relaxed products and reactant; then, with consistent rotational conventions,
D0​=De​+∑fragments​EZP​−EZP,parent​.
(30)
Thus “subtract the parent's zero-point energy” is sufficient for atomic products but generally incomplete for molecular products. An excited final quantum state raises its channel threshold by its excitation energy; that threshold does not become the ground-product D0​ just because a detector selects it.
