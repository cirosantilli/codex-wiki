An energy has λ-displacement convexity when the displayed inequality holds along every displacement interpolation between endpoints in its domain. The case λ=0 is displacement convexity; λ>0 gives uniform convexity along these metric geodesics. This is distinct from convexity along ordinary linear mixtures of probability measures. On subintervals, the inequality is equivalent to convexity of t↦E(μt​)−λW2​(μ0​,μ1​)2t2/2; if the energy is twice differentiable, it is equivalent to the corresponding second derivative lower bound. Without that regularity the lower bound is interpreted using distributions.
