Latent heat (L=TΔS=ΔH)

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Thermodynamic potential Thermodynamic free energy Gibbs free energy Phase coexistence curve
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 10
Latent heat is the heat absorbed or released in a first-order phase transition through its entropy jump. For an equilibrium conversion from thermodynamic phase α to thermodynamic phase β at fixed pressure, evaluate both thermodynamic phases at the same temperature and pressure on their phase coexistence curve:
L=Ttr​(Sβ​−Sα​)=Hβ​−Hα​.
(188)
Use one normalization throughout: L, S and H may all be per mole, giving L in Jmol−1, or all per unit mass, giving L in Jkg−1. Here S is entropy and H is enthalpy on that basis. Equal Gibbs free energies of the coexisting thermodynamic phases give ΔH=Ttr​ΔS. Identifying this enthalpy difference with supplied heat assumes a closed system and an equilibrium conversion with only pressure-volume work. [ref]
For a pure substance with positive latent heat per unit mass L, let x be the phase fraction of the higher-enthalpy thermodynamic phase, measured by mass. While both thermodynamic phases coexist at the fixed pressure,
Q=mL(x2​−x1​),T=Ttr​.
(189)
Adding heat changes x rather than the temperature; this creates the horizontal portion of a heating curve. Once x=1, additional heat raises the temperature according to specific heat capacity at constant pressure. Reversing the conversion releases heat. The plateau represents changing enthalpy, rather than a finite single-thermodynamic phase heat capacity satisfying Q=Cp​ΔT. [ref]
For a constructed fixed-pressure model, take m=1kg, initial temperature 280K, transition temperature Ttr​=300K, solid specific heat capacity at constant pressure cs​=2kJkg−1K−1, latent heat L=100kJkg−1, and liquid specific heat capacity at constant pressure cl​=3kJkg−1K−1. These are stipulated model parameters, not empirical properties of a named material. Heating the solid to 300K takes 40kJ of sensible heat; converting it fully to liquid takes another 100kJ of heat; heating the liquid to 320K takes 60kJ. The total is 200kJ. After a total heat input of 90kJ, x=(90−40)/100=1/2 and the temperature is still 300K. The corresponding per-unit-mass entropy jump is L/Ttr​=31​kJkg−1K−1.
A continuous phase transition has no latent heat. Its heat capacity can have a jump or a singularity without a finite interval in which added heat converts coexisting thermodynamic phases at a constant temperature. A heating plateau therefore requires the stated phase coexistence conditions; it is not a feature of every phase transition. [ref]
