Pulsar period folding and alias checks (ϕ=frac[(t−t0​)/Ptrial​])

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Stellar astrophysics Stellar evolution Neutron star Pulsar
OurBigBook.com Words: 179
Phase folding of a light curve collects samples at equal trial phase. Consider a noiseless illustrative pulsar with one pulse every P=1.4s and arrival times tn​=nP, after pulsar dedispersion. At Ptrial​=P, all arrivals fold to one phase. But P/2 also puts all arrivals at one phase: folding discarded the fact that alternate proposed cycles contain no pulse. At 2P, the arrivals instead occupy two phases, 0 and 1/2. Thus a sharp folded profile alone need not identify the fundamental period.
Check the original time sequence, adjacent arrival intervals and pulse occupancy as well as the folded profile. With N arrivals and a slightly wrong trial period, the unwrapped drift is
δϕN−1​=(N−1)(Ptrial​P​−1).
(31)
For N=100 and Ptrial​=1.401s, this is about −0.07066 cycle. Splitting the observation into early and late pieces reveals the drift that one full folded average can blur. Separately, regular discrete sampling can create aliasing: observing at a spacing comparable with the pulse width can miss narrow pulses even when the recurrence period is much longer. Neither this toy exercise nor one sharp profile replaces time-standard calibration and propagation corrections.
