P-adic number (Qp​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Non-Archimedean analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 815 Articles: 6
For a prime number p, a p-adic number is an element of the field Qp​ obtained by completing the rational numbers for the metric dp​(x,y)=∣x−y∣p​. Here ∣⋅∣p​ is the p-adic absolute value. The Cauchy-sequence construction of a metric completion supplies limits for every Cauchy sequence, and addition, multiplication, and the p-adic absolute value extend to this complete metric space. The rational numbers form a dense subset. [ref]
For a concrete example with p=2, consider the partial sums of a geometric series:
sn​=∑k=0n−1​2k=2n−1,∣sn​−(−1)∣2​=∣2n∣2​=2−n.
(43)
The first values 1,3,7,15,31 have distances 1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16,1/32 from −1 in the 2-adic absolute value. Consequently
−1=∑k=0∞​2kin Q2​.
(44)
This is a statement about convergence in a metric space with the 2-adic absolute value. In the ordinary real number absolute value, the same partial sums grow without bound. Choosing a metric changes which approximation errors shrink.
More generally every nonzero P-adic number has a unique digit expansion
x=∑k=m∞​ak​pk,m∈Z,am​=0,ak​∈{0,1,…,p−1}.
(45)
Only finitely many negative powers occur, while positive powers can continue indefinitely. Written with the highest powers on the left, the digits extend infinitely to the left; for example the P-adic number −1 at p=2 is written …1112​. The p-adic integers are exactly the elements with ∣x∣p​≤1, whose digit expansions use only nonnegative powers. [ref]
The construction can be understood through modular arithmetic. Successively choose the low-order digits to agree modulo p, p2, and higher powers. After fixing digits below pn, the remaining error has p-adic absolute value at most p−n; the partial sums form a Cauchy sequence, so completeness supplies a limit. If two expansions first differ at position j, their difference has P-adic valuation j, proving uniqueness. This turns compatible finite congruence classes into one exact number. In particular, Hensel's lemma upgrades suitable approximate roots of a polynomial modulo powers of p into roots in the p-adic integers.
