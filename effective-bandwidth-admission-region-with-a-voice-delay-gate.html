Effective-bandwidth admission region with a voice delay gate

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Queueing theory Multiclass single-server queue Priority queue with a shared buffer
OurBigBook.com Words: 168
Assume independent stationary Compound Poisson process inputs, preemptive-resume priority for voice, first come first served within each class, capacity C, a shared buffer of B units of work, packet sizes bounded by smax​<B, and voice delay gate D>0. Write B0​=B−smax​ and K=CD. For nv​,nd​ flows with per-flow effective bandwidths av​,ad​, the continuous-time exponential workload bound gives
pd​≤e−θT​B0​,pv​≤e−θT​B0​+e−θV​K,
(2)
provided nv​av​(θT​)+nd​ad​(θT​)≤C and nv​av​(θV​)≤C. To prove this, couple with the unthinned infinite-buffer aggregate and voice queues driven by the same offered packets. Dropping work reduces both the total and voice workloads under this service discipline. A buffer-fit failure implies total workload greater than B0​, and a voice-gate failure implies voice workload at least K. Apply the two bounds and a union bound; Poisson arrivals see time averages converts stationary prearrival probabilities to offered-packet loss fractions. For target losses ℓd​,ℓv​, choose 0<ηB​<min(ℓd​,ℓv​) and ηV​=ℓv​−ηB​, then set
θT​=B0−1​log(1/ηB​),θV​=K−1log(1/ηV​).
(3)
The two effective bandwidth constraints, together with strict aggregate mean-load stability, define a sufficient integer admission region. Voice loss includes the shared-buffer term, even though data packets have lower service priority.
