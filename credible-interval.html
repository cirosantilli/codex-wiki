Credible interval (P(Θ∈C(d)∣D=d)=1−α)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Bayesian statistics
OurBigBook.com Words: 610 Articles: 4
A credible interval is an interval C(d) containing a prescribed amount 1−α of posterior probability for an unknown scalar statistical parameter Θ. For a continuous posterior distribution with posterior density p(θ∣d), its endpoints satisfy
P(a≤Θ≤b∣D=d)=∫ab​p(θ∣d)dθ=1−α.
(1)
The conditioning includes the chosen statistical model and prior distribution. For a discrete posterior distribution, exact equality may be impossible because of its probability masses; an interval can instead be required to contain at least the requested posterior probability. An interval compresses the full posterior distribution into a range whose uncertainty can be communicated in the parameter's own units. See MIT 18.05, Class 16: probability intervals.
For the eight-head, two-tail example in Bayesian statistics, the posterior density is 2860θ9(1−θ)3 on [0,1]. Its cumulative distribution function, obtained by integration of this polynomial, is
F(t)=286t10−780t11+715t12−220t13,0≤t≤1.
(2)
Solving F(a)=0.025 and F(b)=0.975 by the bisection method gives
a≈0.461868461,b≈0.909079605,F(b)−F(a)=0.95.
(3)
These are numerical approximations to the exact quantiles defined by those equations; the endpoints were computed from the cumulative distribution function, rather than read off a plot. The resulting equal-tailed credible interval leaves 2.5% posterior probability on each side. Its shaded area is 95% of the total area under the posterior density.
Credible intervals are not unique. The one-sided credible interval [0,F−1(0.95)]≈[0,0.887334219] also has 95% posterior probability, but leaves all 5% outside it on the right. A highest density region instead selects points above a density threshold; it need not be an equal-tailed credible interval, and when the posterior density has separated peaks it need not be one interval at all. Thus a reported credible interval should specify its construction.
A 95% credible interval makes a statement about the statistical parameter conditional on these observed data and the Bayesian model. A 95% confidence interval instead refers to the coverage probability of a procedure under repeated sampling at a fixed parameter value. These are different conditioning statements: 95% posterior probability does not by itself imply 95% coverage probability for every fixed parameter. The confidence interval article develops that sampling interpretation.
