Permanent as probability of distinct independent choices (P(X1​,…,Xn​ all distinct)=permP)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Permanent (mathematics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 196
Let X1​,…,Xn​ be independent random variables, each choosing a label from {1,…,n}. Put their probabilities in the matrix P=(pij​), where pij​=P(Xi​=j). Each row sums to one. All choices are distinct precisely when their ordered list is one of the permutations of the n labels. These are mutually exclusive events, and independence makes the probability of one list σ equal to ∏i​pi,σ(i)​. Adding the list probabilities gives the permanent of a matrix:
P(X1​,…,Xn​ all distinct)=permP.
(74)
In particular, this permanent lies between zero and one.
For a two-person example,
P=(3/41/5​1/44/5​).
(75)
The distinct outcomes (1,2) and (2,1) have probabilities 3/5 and 1/20. Therefore
permP=53​+201​=2013​=0.65.
(76)
The colliding outcomes (1,1) and (2,2) have probabilities 3/20 and 1/5, totaling 0.35. All four outcomes are shown below; their probabilities sum to one.
If every choice has the uniform distribution on a finite set of n labels, then pij​=1/n and the distinct-choice probability is n!/nn. For three choices and three labels it is 6/27=2/9. This counts a subset of all nn ordered outcomes, not independent pairwise collision events. Independence of the original choices is essential: without it, the individual rows of P do not determine the joint probabilities.
