The Dahlquist equivalence theorem states that, for fixed-coefficient linear multistep methods on the usual class of initial value problems with a right-hand side continuous in x and uniformly Lipschitz continuous in y, convergence of a numerical method for every such problem and every convergent set of starting values is equivalent to consistency of a numerical method and zero-stability. The statement is on fixed finite intervals where the exact solution exists, with implicit updates well defined. Its order estimate says that a zero-stable method with Tn​(h)=O(hp) and starting errors O(hp) has global error O(hp) under the corresponding solution smoothness and Lipschitz continuity assumptions; merely convergent starting values do not ensure the order-p rate.
For this application, sufficient assumptions are a unique solution y∈C3([x0​,x0​+T]), a uniform Lipschitz continuity bound L in y on a neighborhood of its graph, and the nearby solution branch of each implicit update. For sufficiently small h, the map
z⟼34​Yn+1​−31​Yn​+32​hf(xn+2​,z)

is a contraction mapping with factor at most 2hL/3<1 on a suitable small closed neighborhood. The Banach fixed-point theorem supplies local solvability of the nearby update. With starting errors
∣Y0​−y(x0​)∣+∣Y1​−y(x0​+h)∣=O(h2),
(21)

max0≤nh≤T​∣Yn​−y(xn​)∣≤CT​(∣Y0​−y(x0​)∣+∣Y1​−y(x0​+h)∣+maxn​∣Tn​(h)∣)=O(h2).
