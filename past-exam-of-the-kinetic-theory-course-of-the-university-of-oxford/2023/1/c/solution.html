Put zi​=(xi​,vi​) and dzi​=d3xi​d3vi​. Indistinguishability gives an exchangeable ensemble. Define unit-normalized marginal distributions
p1​(z1​,t)=∫ϱdz2​⋯dzN​,p2​(z1​,z2​,t)=∫ϱdz3​⋯dzN​.
(5)
Integrating the dissipative Liouville equation over z2​,…,zN​ removes their boundary fluxes. The N−1 identical pair terms remain:
∂t​p1​+v⋅∇x​p1​+(N−1)∇v​⋅∫K(x,x⋆​)(v⋆​−v)p2​(z,z⋆​,t)dz⋆​=0.
(6)
This is the first equation of a BBGKY hierarchy, with no independence assumed yet.
The additional physical assumption is a mean-field closure for velocity alignment: neglect pair correlations, p2​(z,z⋆​,t)≃p1​(z,t)p1​(z⋆​,t). Initial independence alone does not make this exact at later times, because the particles interact. In the usual number convention, f=Np1​ and the two-particle distribution function is f2​=N(N−1)p2​. Consequently the closed equation at finite N is
∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f+(1−N1​)∇v​⋅(fL[f])≃0,L[f](z)=∫K(x,x⋆​)(v⋆​−v)f(z⋆​,t)dz⋆​.
(7)
Neglecting the relative 1/N correction gives the intended mean-field alignment equation:
∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f+∇v​⋅(fL[f])=0.​
(8)
Equivalently, the closure f2​≃ff⋆​ incorporates this large-N approximation. It cannot be an exact finite-N identity with ∫f=N, since ∫f2​dz⋆​=(N−1)f whereas ∫ff⋆​dz⋆​=Nf.
There is also a consistent finite-N convention yielding the boxed equation directly under the independence closure: define f​=(N−1)p1​ instead. Its total normalization of a probability distribution is N−1, rather than the physical particle number N. Thus the displayed source equation needs a stated normalization of a probability distribution convention, or the large-N approximation, in addition to neglect of correlations. In the remaining parts we use the displayed mean-field alignment equation itself; its moment identities do not depend on the total normalization of a probability distribution.
