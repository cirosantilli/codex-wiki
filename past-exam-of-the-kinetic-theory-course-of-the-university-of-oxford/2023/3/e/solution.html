Apply the chain rule to f0​=f0​(E) with E=H0​(J):
k⋅∂J​f0​=f0′​(E)k⋅Ω.
(48)
On the resonance selected by the Dirac delta function, k⋅Ω=kϕ​Ωp​. Thus
T=π(2π)3Ωp​k∑​kϕ2​∫∣Ψk​∣2[−f0′​(E)]δ(k⋅Ω−kϕ​Ωp​)d3J≥0.​
(49)
Every weight is nonnegative, and −f0′​(E)>0. Strict positivity additionally requires a populated nonaxisymmetric resonance with nonzero coupling on a set of positive surface measure. The unconditional statement is nonnegativity, rather than positive definiteness. As a concrete counterexample to strict positivity of this formula, retain one nonzero conjugate harmonic pair with kϕ​=0 and choose its smooth coefficient Ψk​ supported in an open region ∣Δk​∣>d>0. The perturbation is nonzero but has zero resonant coupling, so T=0 even when f0′​(E)<0. The even simpler zero-perturbation case also gives zero. Whether a particular physical stellar bar has nonzero resonant overlap is additional information.
For a coupled stellar bar and halo, the halo gains positive angular momentum and exerts the opposite torque on the stellar bar. If the halo mass is Mh​ and the rigid stellar bar has constant positive moment of inertia Ib​, then
Ib​dtdΩp​​=−Mh​T≤0.​
(50)
This bar-halo angular-momentum transfer is a form of dynamical friction: a resonantly coupled stellar bar tends to slow down while the dark matter halo spins up. Actual long-term evolution changes both the stellar bar pattern and the halo distribution function, so the fixed-pattern response must then be updated. If the stellar bar changes its moment of inertia, the general relation is d(Ib​Ωp​)/dt=−Mh​T; positive moment of inertia alone would not fix the sign of dΩp​/dt without that rigidity assumption.
