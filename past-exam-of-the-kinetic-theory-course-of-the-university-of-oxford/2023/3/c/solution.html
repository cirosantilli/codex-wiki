L˙z​=−∂θϕ​∂H​=−∂θϕ​∂δΦ​.​

For the spherical unperturbed Hamiltonian, H0​=H0​(Jr​,L); in these particular action-angle variables the third component Ωϕ​=0 describes the fixed orientation of the orbital plane. It should not be confused with the physical azimuthal angular velocity of a particle around its orbit.
The conserved normalization of a probability distribution ∫fd3θd3J=1 makes the expected value of specific angular momentum equal to ∫Lz​fd3θd3J. Its time derivative is the torque per unit halo mass:
T(t)=−∫f(θ,J,t)∂θϕ​∂δΦ​d3θd3J.​
(39)
This follows either by averaging the single-particle equation or by integrating the Collisionless Boltzmann equation against Lz​, with vanishing boundary fluxes.
The term f0​∂θϕ​​δΦ integrates to zero over the periodic angle variable. Thus the first nonzero torque is quadratic in the perturbation. Use the Fourier series expansions of b; the source's reference here to part (a) is a numbering slip. Integrating the product over all three angle variables yields
T(t)​=−(2π)3k,ℓ∑​iℓϕ​∫δfk​δΦℓ​δk+ℓ,0​d3J=i(2π)3k∑​kϕ​∫δfk​(J,t)δΦk∗​(J,t)d3J.​​
(40)
Reality of the potential energy per unit mass gives δΦ−k​=δΦk∗​; likewise δf−k​=δfk∗​. Conjugate harmonics make the total torque real. The sum is over all integer vectors, so no extra factor of two should be inserted.
