Δk​(J)=k⋅Ω(J)−mΩp​.

Substitute δΦk​=Ψk​e−imΩp​t into the linear response in action-angle variables, then set τ=t−t′:

Pair k with −k. Because B−k​=−Bk​, Δ−k​=−Δk​, and ∣Ψ−k​∣2=∣Ψk​∣2, the imaginary cosine terms cancel. The finite-time torque is
T(t)=−(2π)3k∑​m∫Bk​∣Ψk​∣2Δk​sin(Δk​t)​d3J.​

This is a leading, second-order response in the perturbation amplitude.
For the long-time limit, assume a continuum of occupied action variables, sufficiently smooth integrable weights, convergent harmonic sums, and regular resonance surfaces ∇J​Δk​=0 wherever a contributing surface intersects the distribution. Assume also that the perturbation is weak enough to neglect nonlinear trapping and secular changes in f0​, and that the prescribed stellar bar pattern and its coefficients stay fixed. Then phase mixing removes nonresonant transients in the integrated response. The relevant long-time resonance kernel is the distributional convergence
xsin(tx)​⟶πδ(x)(t→+∞).
(45)
It is not a pointwise limit: at x=0 the kernel equals t. To justify its use under the integral over action variables, resolve the integral into detuning surfaces; the coarea formula gives
∫W(J)δ(Δk​(J))d3J=∫Δk​=0​∣∇J​Δk​∣W(J)​dS.
(46)
Smoothness and decay of this surface weight reduce the limit to the one-dimensional long-time resonance kernel. Equivalently, a causal convergence factor e−ητ gives Re∫0∞​e−ητ−iΔτdτ=η/(η2+Δ2)→πδ(Δ) as η↓0.
Therefore the resonant torque from a rotating perturbation is
T=−π(2π)3k∑​kϕ​∫∣Ψk​∣2k⋅∂J∂f0​​δ(k⋅Ω−kϕ​Ωp​)d3J.​
(47)
Only orbital resonances contribute to this steady integrated torque. The limit is a formal weak-perturbation limit, or an intermediate-time approximation after phase mixing but before substantial secular redistribution. It does not prove an unchanged halo can absorb angular momentum at this rate forever. A discrete set of orbital frequencies, singular resonance surfaces, or strong trapping can invalidate the stated steady limit.
