Write Kij​=K(xi​,xj​) and P=∑i​vi​. The unit particle masses make this sum the total momentum. Exchange the two indices and use the symmetry Kij​=Kji​:
P˙=∑i,j​Kij​(vj​−vi​)=21​∑i,j​Kij​[(vj​−vi​)+(vi​−vj​)]=0​.
(1)
Thus every interacting pair transfers equal and opposite momentum, even though the velocity alignment dissipates kinetic energy.
For the total kinetic energy EN​=21​∑i​∣vi​∣2, apply the same exchange of indices:
E˙N​​=i,j∑​Kij​vi​⋅(vj​−vi​)=21​i,j∑​Kij​[vi​⋅(vj​−vi​)+vj​⋅(vi​−vj​)]=−21​i,j∑​Kij​∣vj​−vi​∣2≤0​.​
(2)
The dissipated kinetic energy is that of relative motion. Since the total momentum is constant, EN​−∣P∣2/(2N)=21​∑i​∣vi​−P/N∣2 also decreases. Equality holds when every pair with Kij​>0 has the same velocity; the hypotheses permit disconnected interacting groups, so they alone do not guarantee eventual global velocity alignment.

Put zi​=(xi​,vi​) and dzi​=d3xi​d3vi​. Indistinguishability gives an exchangeable ensemble. Define unit-normalized marginal distributions
p1​(z1​,t)=∫ϱdz2​⋯dzN​,p2​(z1​,z2​,t)=∫ϱdz3​⋯dzN​.
(5)
Integrating the dissipative Liouville equation over z2​,…,zN​ removes their boundary fluxes. The N−1 identical pair terms remain:
∂t​p1​+v⋅∇x​p1​+(N−1)∇v​⋅∫K(x,x⋆​)(v⋆​−v)p2​(z,z⋆​,t)dz⋆​=0.
(6)
This is the first equation of a BBGKY hierarchy, with no independence assumed yet.
The additional physical assumption is a mean-field closure for velocity alignment: neglect pair correlations, p2​(z,z⋆​,t)≃p1​(z,t)p1​(z⋆​,t). Initial independence alone does not make this exact at later times, because the particles interact. In the usual number convention, f=Np1​ and the two-particle distribution function is f2​=N(N−1)p2​. Consequently the closed equation at finite N is
∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f+(1−N1​)∇v​⋅(fL[f])≃0,L[f](z)=∫K(x,x⋆​)(v⋆​−v)f(z⋆​,t)dz⋆​.
(7)
Neglecting the relative 1/N correction gives the intended mean-field alignment equation:
∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f+∇v​⋅(fL[f])=0.​
(8)
Equivalently, the closure f2​≃ff⋆​ incorporates this large-N approximation. It cannot be an exact finite-N identity with ∫f=N, since ∫f2​dz⋆​=(N−1)f whereas ∫ff⋆​dz⋆​=Nf.
There is also a consistent finite-N convention yielding the boxed equation directly under the independence closure: define f​=(N−1)p1​ instead. Its total normalization of a probability distribution is N−1, rather than the physical particle number N. Thus the displayed source equation needs a stated normalization of a probability distribution convention, or the large-N approximation, in addition to neglect of correlations. In the remaining parts we use the displayed mean-field alignment equation itself; its moment identities do not depend on the total normalization of a probability distribution.

The two real branches start at ω=±ωpe​, have zero initial slope, and approach the lines ω=±kVe​ from outside the populated parallel-velocity region. The original plot solves the degenerate-plasma dielectric response numerically; the shaded wedge shows where particles with resonant parallel velocities exist.
The distinction from a nondegenerate gas with a Maxwellian Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution is its tail. A Maxwellian has resonant particles at every finite phase velocity. Its long-wavelength Langmuir waves have exponentially weak Landau damping, but at shorter wavelengths that damping becomes strong. The ideal degenerate cutoff instead permits this undamped branch to persist continuously into degenerate-plasma zero-point sound. The coefficient 3Ve2​/5 in c is also different from the classical thermal dispersive coefficient.
A natural additional branch is a degenerate ion-acoustic wave. For singly charged ions with equal electron and ion number densities, degenerate electron pressure supplies a restoring force while ion mass supplies inertia. The electron equation of state has dPe​/dne​=me​Ve2​/3, suggesting, at long wavelengths,
ω≃±kcs​,cs2​≃3mi​me​​Ve2​.​
(31)
Both species are degenerate here, so Vi​=(me​/mi​)Ve​. The hierarchy Vi​≪cs​≪Ve​ explains the damping: there are no resonant ions, but there are resonant electrons inside their parallel marginal distribution. That electron marginal distribution has a small nonzero slope at u=cs​, even though the full three-dimensional occupation is constant inside the Fermi sphere. One therefore expects weak electron Landau damping, of relative order me​/mi​​, rather than exact absence of damping. Ion degeneracy pressure adds a relatively small correction to cs2​. This reasoning concerns an electrostatic ion-acoustic wave; transverse or magnetized branches require additional field dynamics or a background magnetic field.

The unconditional statement is nonnegativity, rather than positive definiteness. As a concrete counterexample to strict positivity of this formula, retain one nonzero conjugate harmonic pair with kϕ​=0 and choose its smooth coefficient Ψk​ supported in an open region ∣Δk​∣>d>0. The perturbation is nonzero but has zero resonant coupling, so T=0 even when f0′​(E)<0. The even simpler zero-perturbation case also gives zero. Whether a particular physical stellar bar has nonzero resonant overlap is additional information.
For a coupled stellar bar and halo, the halo gains positive angular momentum and exerts the opposite torque on the stellar bar. If the halo mass is Mh​ and the rigid stellar bar has constant positive moment of inertia Ib​, then
Ib​dtdΩp​​=−Mh​T≤0.​
(50)
This bar-halo angular-momentum transfer is a form of dynamical friction: a resonantly coupled stellar bar tends to slow down while the dark matter halo spins up. Actual long-term evolution changes both the stellar bar pattern and the halo distribution function, so the fixed-pattern response must then be updated. If the stellar bar changes its moment of inertia, the general relation is d(Ib​Ωp​)/dt=−Mh​T; positive moment of inertia alone would not fix the sign of dΩp​/dt without that rigidity assumption.
