The long-wavelength limit in d is resonant heating. Most electrons screen the slowly varying spatial field. The noise nevertheless excites collective Langmuir waves, whose sharp temporal spectrum lies near ω=±ωpe​. Electrons with v≃ω/k remain in phase with a wave and exchange energy with it. A stable tail absorbs net wave energy through Landau damping. The heating is initially deposited in narrow tail regions of velocity space, even though the volume-integrated rate equals that in c.
The short-wavelength limit in c is stochastic heating in the terminology intended here. With ϵ≃1, the imposed electric field is essentially unscreened. Its temporally uncorrelated kicks cause a random walk of electron velocity, so ⟨(Δv)2⟩=2D0​Δt. Electrons throughout the thermal population contribute to the resulting kinetic energy increase; no narrow collective wave peak selects a small tail population.
Both mechanisms are driven by white noise, and the formal condition ω=kv enters the stationary quasilinear diffusion calculation in both cases. The physical distinction is the sharp collective resonance in d versus the broad direct random forcing in c, rather than the mere presence of randomness. In collisionless theory this heating is an increase of the averaged kinetic energy; subsequent collisions can establish thermal equilibrium.
