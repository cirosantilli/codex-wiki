Let A0​=f0​(0), δ=v2​−v1​, V=v0​+δ and K=k2λDe2​. Use the Debye length convention of the paper, λDe​=v0​/ωpe​. On the candidate intervals in a, the Landau contour contributes no imaginary part. The piecewise constant slopes give
I(u)​=∫v−uf0′​(v)​dv=v0​A0​​[∫−v0​0​v−udv​−∫0v1​​v−udv​−∫v2​V​v−udv​]=v0​A0​​[2ln∣u∣−ln∣v0​+u∣−ln∣v1​−u∣−ln∣V−u∣+ln∣v2​−u∣].​
(24)
Since n0​=v0​A0​, setting the dielectric function to zero yields
ln∣v0​+u∣∣v1​−u∣∣V−u∣u2∣v2​−u∣​=K.​
(25)
The absolute values express the real response on the allowed intervals. Solving this real equation inside a sloping portion of f0​ would not satisfy the imaginary part of the dielectric function.
An exact algebraic form will also make the number of undamped branches clear. Define
A=v02​+δ(v0​−v1​),B=v0​v1​V,q=1−e−K.
(26)
On each allowed interval, the quantity inside the logarithm equals u2(u−v2​)/[(u+v0​)(u−v1​)(u−V)]. Exponentiating and using
(u+v0​)(u−v1​)(u−V)=u2(u−v2​)−Au+B
(27)
gives the cubic equation
qu2(u−v2​)−Au+B=0.
(28)
For every k>0, the logarithm runs from 0 to +∞ on (−∞,−v0​), from +∞ to −∞ on (v1​,v2​), and from +∞ to 0 on (V,∞). The intermediate value theorem supplies a root in each interval, and the cubic equation permits at most three. Thus there is exactly one undamped branch in each interval.
