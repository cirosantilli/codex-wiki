Subdifferential under scalar affine composition (∂[h(cTx+b)]=c∂h(cTx+b))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex optimization Subdifferential
OurBigBook.com Words: 78
For a finite convex function h:R→R, the function f(x)=h(cTx+b) is convex. The subgradient inequality gives c∂h(u)⊆∂f(x), where u=cTx+b. Conversely, every subgradient g of f annihilates kercT, because f is constant on lines in those directions. For c=0, write g=cv and test the subgradient inequality at y=x+(t−u)c/∥c∥22​; this proves v∈∂h(u). For c=0, f is constant and both sides are {0} since a finite convex function on the real line has a nonempty subdifferential everywhere.
