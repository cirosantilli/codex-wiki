Two-level sudden quench (pg​=cos2(θ/2),pe​=sin2(θ/2))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Sudden approximation Quantum quench
OurBigBook.com Words: 543 Articles: 2
Take an isolated two-level system, or a spin one-half qubit, with Pauli matrices σx​,σz​. Let σz​∣0⟩=∣0⟩ and σz​∣1⟩=−∣1⟩. The initial and final Hamiltonian operators are
Hi​=−2Δi​​σz​,Hf​=−2Δf​​(cosθσz​+sinθσx​),Δi​,Δf​>0.
(9)
The energy gaps are Δi​,Δf​. Prepare the initial ground state ∣0⟩, with energy eigenvalue −Δi​/2. A final orthonormal eigenbasis is
∣gf​⟩=c∣0⟩+s∣1⟩,∣ef​⟩=−s∣0⟩+c∣1⟩,c=cos(θ/2),s=sin(θ/2).
(10)
Direct matrix multiplication gives Hf​∣gf​⟩=−Δf​∣gf​⟩/2 and Hf​∣ef​⟩=+Δf​∣ef​⟩/2. The unchanged initial quantum state is ∣0⟩=c∣gf​⟩−s∣ef​⟩. Its new probability amplitudes are the inner products c,−s; the Born rule gives pg​=c2,pe​=s2. The angle between the real amplitude vectors is θ/2, whereas the Hamiltonian axis on the Bloch sphere rotates through θ.
At θ=π/3, c=3​/2 and s=1/2, so the final energy measurement gives −Δf​/2 with probability 3/4 and +Δf​/2 with probability 1/4. At θ=0 the initial ground state remains the final ground state; at θ=π it is the final excited energy eigenstate. These statements are about the new energy labels, not an instantaneous physical rotation of the quantum state.
