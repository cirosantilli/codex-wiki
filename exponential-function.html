Exponential function (ex)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 44
The real exponential function exp(x)=ex converts addition into multiplication:
exp(x+y)=exp(x)exp(y),exp(0)=1,dxd​exp(x)=exp(x).
(16)
Its values are positive, and it defines a bijection from the real numbers to the positive real numbers. The natural logarithm is its inverse function. These properties connect arithmetic with calculus: adding an amount to the input multiplies the output by a fixed factor, and the local rate of change equals the current value. The exponential series gives one construction; an equivalent characterization is the unique solution of a differential equation y′=y, y(0)=1.
For a concrete example, y(t)=10e0.2t starts at 10 and satisfies y′=0.2y. The absolute derivative increases with y, while the relative rate y′/y stays 0.2. At t=5, y=10e≈27.1828 and y′≈5.43656. The exponential function therefore models accumulation proportional to what is already present, rather than addition of a fixed amount at every step. A positive constant rate gives exponential growth; a negative one gives exponential decay.
Every positive real base b=1 fits the same construction through bx=exlnb. Thus its derivative is (lnb)bx: base e is special because lne=1. The exponential function is also unchanged in form by differentiation and integration, helping explain its repeated appearance in linear ordinary differential equations. Its real definition, inverse relationship, and derivative rules are developed in OpenStax, integrals, exponential functions, and logarithms.
