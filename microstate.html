Microstate

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics State degeneracy
OurBigBook.com Words: 97
A microstate is a complete microscopic specification within the chosen model. For two distinguishable, noninteracting two-level systems, label the units A,B and their states 0,1, with energies 0,Δ and Δ>0. The four microstates are
(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1),
(50)
with total energies 0,Δ,Δ,2Δ. Distinguishability matters: (0,1) and (1,0) describe different assignments to the labeled units. Noninteraction matters too: their energies add without an extra coupling term.
For a classical particle model, a microstate can instead be a point in phase space, specifying every position and momentum. In the quantum canonical ensemble, the sum uses energy eigenstates; a general quantum state may also contain quantum coherence, which an energy label alone does not specify.
