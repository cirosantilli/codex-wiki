Incompleteness of finitely supported sequences (∥x−x(N)∥1​=2−N)

 ... Analysis Functional analysis Normed vector space Banach space l-p sequence space Finitely supported sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 163
Equip the vector space c00​ of finitely supported sequences with the norm inherited from the absolutely summable sequence space. Consider the truncations
x(N)=(2−1,2−2,…,2−N,0,0,…).
(4)
For M>N, the geometric series gives
∥x(M)−x(N)∥1​=∑k=N+1M​2−k=2−N−2−M<2−N.
(5)
Therefore (x(N)) is a Cauchy sequence. Any possible norm limit must have coordinate k equal to 2−k, since ∣xk​−yk​∣≤∥x−y∥1​. That forces infinitely many nonzero coordinates, so no limit lies in c00​. There is nothing wrong with the approximations: the space is missing their limit.
Inside the absolutely summable sequence space, the limit is x=(2−k)k≥1​ and its exact truncation error is 2−N. Four retained coordinates leave error 1/16; ten leave 1/1024. More generally, truncating any element of the absolutely summable sequence space leaves a tail whose norm tends to zero. Together with completeness, this identifies that Banach space as the completion of a normed space c00​ with this norm. Finite storage and an infinite mathematical object fit together through a controlled error.
