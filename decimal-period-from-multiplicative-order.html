Decimal period from multiplicative order (ordq​(10))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Multiplicative order
OurBigBook.com Words: 391
For a reduced fraction p/q with 0<p<q, q>1, and q,10 coprime integers, its decimal representation repeats immediately after the decimal point. The shortest repeating block has length
L=ordq​(10),L∣φ(q).
(263)
Here ord denotes multiplicative order, and φ denotes Euler's totient function. Changing the numerator preserves the decimal period as long as the fraction remains reduced.[ref]
For example, long division of 1/7 gives the remainders 1,3,2,6,4,5,1, with emitted digits 1,4,2,8,5,7. Consequently
71​=0.142857,ord7​(10)=6.
(264)
Each remainder determines both the next digit and the next remainder; returning to one restarts the same six steps.
More generally, initialize long division with r0​=p. At each step, division with remainder gives
10rj−1​=qdj​+rj​,0≤dj​≤9,0≤rj​<q,
(265)
so rj​≡p10j(modq). Since p,q are coprime integers, the modular inverse of p shows that rL​=r0​ exactly when 10L≡1(modq). To see why the digit block cannot repeat sooner than the remainders, a purely repeating decimal with a block of L digits represents D/(10L−1) for an integer D. A reduced fraction p/q with that value forces q∣10L−1. Hence the shortest digit repeat and the multiplicative order coincide.
Knowing the decimal period predicts how many digits must be computed and stored: one block determines the whole repeating decimal. It also constructs the block directly. With L=ordq​(10), the integer
D=qp(10L−1)​
(266)
is the block, padded on the left with zeros to L digits. For 1/11, L=2 and D=9, so the block is 09 and 1/11=0.09. Reduction matters: 7/21=1/3=0.3 has decimal period one, whereas ord21​(10)=6.
If the reduced denominator contains prime factors two or five, ten has no multiplicative order modulo that whole denominator. Write
q=2u5vm,gcd(m,10)=1,k0​=max(u,v).
(267)
The decimal preperiod has exactly k0​ digits. If m>1, the remaining repeating decimal has decimal period ordm​(10). For instance, 28=22⋅7 gives 1/28=0.03571428: two prefix digits, then six repeating digits. If m=1, the decimal representation is a terminating decimal with exactly k0​ fractional places. We use the representation ending in zeros, rather than its alternative ending in nines; for example 1/40=0.025 terminates after three places.
A short derivation explains both cases. After k digits, multiplying the reduced fraction by 10k and discarding its integer part leaves a reduced fraction whose denominator is q/gcd(q,10k). This denominator first becomes coprime to ten at k=k0​. It then equals m, giving an immediate repeat when m>1, or an integer when m=1. The same long division argument works for positional notation in any base b≥2: for a reduced denominator q>1 coprime to b, the shortest repeating block has length ordq​(b).
