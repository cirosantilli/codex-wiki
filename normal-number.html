Normal number (limN→∞​FN​(w)=K−r)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A real number is normal in an integer base K≥2 when every finite word of digits occurs in its positional notation with the same limiting digit block frequency as every other word of the same length. Write its fractional part as x=0.d1​d2​… in base K, using the expansion that is not eventually all K−1 digits when two expansions are possible. For every length r≥1 and every word w∈{0,…,K−1}r, the requirement is
limN→∞​N−r+1∣{1≤j≤N−r+1:(dj​,…,dj+r−1​)=w}∣​=K−r.
(9)
Occurrences overlap: the word 11 occurs twice in 111. There are Kr possible words of length r, so K−r is the equal share for each. In a decimal representation, this means frequency 1/10 for each digit, 1/100 for each pair, and 1/1000 for each triple, continuing through every finite length. The definition agrees with that used by Copeland and Erdős in their work on explicit normal numbers. [ref]
The appeal is that a precise, deterministic real number can have the same limiting digit block frequencies as repeated draws from a uniform distribution on a finite set, with independence between draws. Champernowne constant is an explicit example. Normality is useful for studying digit distribution, but balanced digits alone establish only a simply normal number, and a finite sample cannot establish any of these infinite limits.
Émile Borel introduced normal numbers and established their prevalence in his 1909 paper applying probability to number theory; the stronger all-base statement is discussed under absolutely normal number. [ref]
