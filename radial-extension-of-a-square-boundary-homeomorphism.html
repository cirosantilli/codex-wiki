Radial extension of a square boundary homeomorphism

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Homeomorphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
A homeomorphism g:∂Q→∂Q of the boundary of a closed square Q extends to a homeomorphism of Q by fixing its centre c and setting H(c+r(p−c))=c+r(g(p)−c) for p∈∂Q and 0<r≤1. Each noncentral point lies on a unique ray from c meeting the boundary once, so the formula is well defined and its inverse is obtained from g−1. Both maps are continuous off the centre by the radial parametrization, and at the centre because their distances from c are bounded by r times the radius of the square. It follows that complements of any two closed boundary arcs are homeomorphic: first map the arcs to one another by a boundary homeomorphism, then extend radially.
