Sliding acceleration on an inclined plane (a=g(sinθ−μk​cosθ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Inclined plane
OurBigBook.com Words: 403
First test rest. With no other tangential forces, the required uphill static friction is fs​=mgsinθ. Its maximum is μs​N, where μs​ is the coefficient of static friction. Thus
mgsinθ≤μs​mgcosθ⟺tanθ≤μs​,θc​=arctanμs​.
(5)
The tangent function is increasing on this angle range, and its inverse tangent determines the last permitted resting angle θc​. At equality, the ideal model still permits limiting rest; a larger angle excludes it. Below the threshold, setting fs​=μs​N would incorrectly impose the maximum static friction even when less is required. Static friction on an inclined plane and inclined-plane measurement of static friction develop this onset criterion further.
For a block already sliding downhill, uphill kinetic friction has magnitude fk​=μk​N, where μk​ is the coefficient of kinetic friction. Taking downhill as positive, Newton's second law becomes
ma=mgsinθ−μk​mgcosθ,a=g(sinθ−μk​cosθ).
(6)
Every term in the force balance scales with mass; division by m>0 removes it. Doubling mass doubles both gravitational weight and the contact forces, while leaving this acceleration unchanged if the coefficients remain unchanged. A mass-independent result is a prediction of these assumptions, rather than a universal property of every ramp.
For an original numerical model, stipulate m=3kg, g=9.8m/s2, μs​=0.45 and μk​=0.30. The critical angle is θc​≃24.23∘. At 20∘, the normal force is 27.627N; rest requires 10.055N of uphill static friction, below its 12.432N bound. A resting block can stay there. If it is already sliding downhill, however, the kinetic friction is only 8.288N, giving downhill acceleration a≃0.5891m/s2. Specifying the initial state matters.
At 30∘, the downhill component of gravitational weight is 14.700N, while N≃25.461N and the static friction bound is only 11.458N. Rest is impossible. During downhill sliding,
fk​≃7.638N,Fnet​≃7.062N,a≃2.3539m/s2.
(7)
Released from rest on a sufficiently long ramp, this ideal block covers its first 1.00m in t=2L/a​≃0.9218s, reaching speed v=2aL​≃2.1697m/s. As an independent energy calculation, the lost gravitational potential energy is mgLsinθ=14.700J and kinetic friction removes fk​L≃7.638J of mechanical energy. The remaining 7.062J equals mv2/2 by the work-energy theorem.
At θ=arctanμk​, downhill sliding has zero acceleration and therefore constant velocity. Below that angle, a downhill-moving block slows; when it stops, test the static friction bound again. For uphill sliding, kinetic friction points downhill, so the signed downhill acceleration instead is g(sinθ+μk​cosθ). The sign follows contact slip, and the downhill formula cannot be used unchanged after a reversal.
