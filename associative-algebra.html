Primitive ideal (P=AnnA​(E),E simple)

Words: 66
Associative algebra OurBigBook.com
A primitive ideal of a unital associative algebra is the annihilator of a module for a nonzero simple module. Every maximal proper two-sided ideal is primitive: its quotient is a simple ring, and any simple module over that quotient has zero annihilator there. In a unital Banach algebra, a simple module can be realized as A/L for a maximal left ideal; this ideal is closed, making the representation a bounded algebraically irreducible action on a Banach space.

Ore extension (A[t;σ,δ],ta=σ(a)t+δ(a))

Words: 73
Associative algebra OurBigBook.com
An Ore extension uses a unital endomorphism σ of A and a σ-derivation δ, satisfying δ(ab)=σ(a)δ(b)+δ(a)b. Its elements have unique expressions ∑i​ai​ti and multiplication is determined by the displayed relation. Applying the relation to t(ab) shows why the endomorphism and derivation conditions are exactly the compatibility conditions for associativity. A differential Ore extension has σ=id. Adjoining ordinary partial derivatives successively to a polynomial ring gives the ordered-monomial construction of a Weyl algebra.

Algebra involution (x↦x∗)

Words: 41

An algebra involution on a complex associative algebra is a conjugate-linear map satisfying (xy)∗=y∗x∗ and (x∗)∗=x. It fixes the identity when one exists. A star algebra is an algebra equipped with such a map. These algebraic identities do not themselves impose norm continuity or the C-star identity.

Hermitian element of a star algebra (h∗=h)

Words: 50

A Hermitian algebra element of a star-algebra is an element fixed by its algebra involution. The identity and zero are Hermitian algebra elements. For every x, both xx∗ and x∗x are Hermitian algebra elements. A real spectrum for all such elements is an extra condition in a general Banach algebra, rather than a consequence of the involution identities alone.

Star-subalgebra

Star-algebra OurBigBook.com
A linear subalgebra closed under the involution. In bounded operators, this means closure under the adjoint operator; neither norm closure nor operator-topology closure is part of the definition. These distinctions matter in the Von Neumann double commutant theorem and the Kaplansky density theorem.

Cocenter of a finite-dimensional algebra (C(A)=A/[A,A])

Words: 198 Articles: 1
Associative algebra OurBigBook.com
The cocenter of a finite-dimensional algebra is the quotient by the vector subspace spanned by additive commutators ab−ba; this subspace need not be an ideal. For a group algebra, its basis is indexed by conjugacy classes: every commutator of basis elements is a difference of conjugates, and every difference of conjugates is a commutator. For Mn​(k), off-diagonal matrix units and Eii​−Ejj​ show that the quotient is one-dimensional, identified by the trace.

Frobenius map on an algebra cocenter (F([a])=[ap])

Words: 132
Cocenter of a finite-dimensional algebra OurBigBook.com
In characteristic p>0, the displayed map on the cocenter of a finite-dimensional algebra is additive and Frobenius-semilinear. Expand a pth power into words: nonconstant cyclic-word orbits have length p and cancel modulo commutators. Also (ab)p and (ba)p have the same cocenter class, so the map kills additive commutators and is well defined.
Over a perfect field with split semisimple quotient, the stable image of this map has dimension equal to the number of simple modules of the algebra. Indeed, the kernel of C(A)→C(A/J(A)) has representatives in the nilpotent Jacobson radical, so an iterate kills it. On the semisimple algebra quotient, each matrix-block coordinate is a field trace, and tr(ap)=tr(a)p. For a split semisimple quotient, Frobenius is bijective on every coordinate. In a group algebra over an algebraically closed field, the stable image has the p-regular element conjugacy classes as its basis, proving the simple-module counting theorem.

For a finitely generated associative algebra, choose matrices for its generators and impose its polynomial relations. This defines an affine variety. With prescribed orthogonal idempotents 1=∑i​ei​, require their images to be the standard projections on ⨁i​kni​. The resulting variety is RepA​(n), acted on by ∏i​GLni​​(k). The unit group E× is a nonempty open subset of the affine space E, detected by the determinant of left multiplication. It is therefore an irreducible connected linear algebraic group. Its map to (E/J(E))× has kernel 1+J(E), a closed unipotent subgroup since the radical is nilpotent.

The free associative algebra has a basis of finite words in its generators, including the empty word as identity. Multiplication concatenates words without commutativity relations. It is the path algebra of the quiver with r loops. With one generator it is a polynomial algebra.

Formal associative deformation (a∗b)

Associative algebra OurBigBook.com
A formal associative deformation is a unital k[[t]]-bilinear product continuous for the adic topology on A[[t]] of the form a∗b=ab+∑r≥1​trμr​(a,b). Each coefficient μr​ is a k-bilinear map A⊗A→A, and the whole product satisfies associativity. For an infinite-dimensional algebra, A[[t]] is the completed tensor product lim​n​A⊗k[t]/(tn) and generally differs from the ordinary A⊗k[[t]].

First-order associative deformation (a∗b=ab+εf(a,b),ε2=0)

The order-ε part of associativity is af(b,c)−f(ab,c)+f(a,bc)−f(a,b)c=0, so f is a Hochschild cocycle. A change of coordinates T=1+εg changes f to f+δg. Formal rigidity from vanishing second Hochschild cohomology

If HH2(A,A)=0, every formal associative deformation on the adic completion of a module A[[t]] is trivial. After killing lower coefficients, associativity makes the order-r coefficient a Hochschild cocycle μr​=δgr​. Transport by id+trgr​ subtracts δgr​ and kills it. The successive transformations stabilize modulo each tn and converge to an invertible change of coordinates. Completeness, and equivalence congruent to the identity modulo t, are essential to this argument.

For a unital star-subalgebra A of the bounded operators on a complex Hilbert space, its strong operator topology and weak operator topology closures equal its double commutant of an operator algebra. Kaplansky density theorem
Words: 73
Von Neumann double commutant theorem OurBigBook.com
For a unital star-subalgebra A⊆B(H), its unit ball is dense in the unit ball of A′′ in the strong operator topology. The same holds for self-adjoint unit balls and for positive unit balls, and the full unit balls may be approximated strongly together with their adjoints. Passing first to the norm closure allows continuous functional calculus; resolvent identities prove strong continuity of continuous functions vanishing at infinity, even for unbounded approximating nets of self-adjoint operators. A cutoff function supplies the norm bound, and a two-by-two self-adjoint matrix removes the self-adjoint restriction.

Double commutant theorem for star-algebras (A′′=A)

Words: 119
Commutant of an operator algebra OurBigBook.com
A unital subalgebra A⊆End(V) closed under the adjoint operator, on a finite-dimensional real or complex inner product space, equals its double commutant of an operator algebra. Here is a proof that does not require a classification of algebras. For N=dimV, choose a basis (v1​,…,vN​) and let W={(av1​,…,avN​):a∈A}⊆VN. The diagonal action of A preserves W and W⊥, so the orthogonal projection onto W commutes with that action. Each matrix entry of this projection belongs to A′. Hence the diagonal action of b∈A′′ commutes with the projection and preserves W. Since 1∈A, the tuple (v1​,…,vN​) belongs to W, and therefore (bv1​,…,bvN​)=(av1​,…,avN​) for one a∈A. This implies b=a. Without the unital hypothesis the conclusion is A′′=A+F1.

Then the algebra acts by the full matrix algebra on each simple factor, while its commutant acts by the full matrix algebra on each multiplicity factor. Taking the commutant twice recovers the original image. This is the algebraic basis of Schur–Weyl duality.

A block of a finite-dimensional associative algebra R is a two-sided ideal Re associated with a primitive idempotent e in the center of an associative algebra. Ext-connected components determine blocks

Words: 129
Block of a finite-dimensional algebra OurBigBook.com
For a finite-dimensional associative algebra, form a graph on its simple modules, joining S,T when Ext1(S,T) or Ext1(T,S) is nonzero. Its connected components are exactly the simple modules belonging to each block of a finite-dimensional algebra. They are also the components generated by sharing a Jordan–Hölder factor occurrence in an indecomposable representation that is a projective module.
Indeed, Ext separation of finite-length modules would split the regular module of a block into canonical summands if that block had two graph components. Right multiplication preserves these summands, so its projection supplies a nontrivial central idempotent, contradicting the definition of a block. An indecomposable projective belongs to one block. Finally, a nonsplit extension 0→U→V→W→0 of simple modules is a quotient of the projective cover of W: a lift onto W must contain U, since otherwise the extension splits. Thus its two endpoints share factors in an indecomposable projective.

The second term has this order because the algebra is commutative. Derivations of smooth functions are vector fields provide a geometric example.
