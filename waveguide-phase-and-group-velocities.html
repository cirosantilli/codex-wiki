Waveguide phase and group velocities (vph​vg​=c2)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic wave Rectangular electromagnetic waveguide Waveguide cutoff frequency
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
For a propagating branch of an ideal vacuum-filled rectangular electromagnetic waveguide, the phase velocity tracks constant wave phase along the axis, whereas the group velocity describes a sufficiently narrow, weakly distorted wave packet. Using ω2=c2β2+ωc2​ gives
vph​=βω​=1−(ωc​/ω)2​c​,vg​=dβdω​=ωc2β​=c1−(ωc​/ω)2​.
(7)
Thus vph​>c, 0<vg​<c, and vph​vg​=c2. Differentiating the dispersion relation proves the second formula; dividing it by β2 proves the first. This is the same mathematical structure as Klein-Gordon packet velocities. In the stipulated f=1.25fc​ example,
vph​=35​c,vg​=53​c.
(8)
Over 0.30m, the corresponding narrow-envelope delay is (0.50m)/c, approximately 1.668ns. Timing a carrier wave crest would give 0.6004ns; it does not time the arrival of a newly introduced message.
There is an independent energy check. For the forward TE10​ fields, the average axial Poynting vector is ⟨Sz​⟩=β∣Ey​∣2/(2μ0​ω). Integrating across the guide gives average power P and average electromagnetic energy per unit length U′:
P=4μ0​ωabβE02​​,U′=4ϵ0​abE02​​,U′P​=ωc2β​=vg​.
(9)
The transverse sine and cosine squared each integrate to a/2; β2+(π/a)2=ω2/c2 then makes the integrated electric and magnetic contributions to U′ equal. The axial energy transport therefore agrees with the group velocity in this ideal model.
As ω↓ωc​, vg​→0 and vph​→∞; exactly at cutoff ω/β is undefined. Strong wave dispersion close to cutoff also limits the narrow-packet approximation. Neither a large phase velocity nor a pulse peak establishes superluminal information velocity; front velocity asks a separate causal question. In a uniform filling with constant positive permittivity ε and magnetic permeability μ, replace c by u=1/εμ​, giving vph​vg​=u2. Frequency-dependent material response or significant loss requires differentiating the actual dispersion relation, so this product is not universal.
