Hyperbola (x2/a2−y2/b2=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Conic section
OurBigBook.com Words: 281 Articles: 3
A hyperbola is a conic section with two unbounded branches. In suitable coordinates its equation is x2/a2−y2/b2=1 with a,b>0. The unit hyperbola is parametrized by x=cosht, y=sinht on its right branch, relating it to the hyperbolic functions.
For example, x2/9−y2/16=1 has foci of a conic at (±5,0), because 52=32+42. Its focal-distance difference has absolute value 6, while its eccentricity of a conic is 5/3>1. At the point (3,0), the focal distances are 8 and 2, verifying that difference. The asymptotes are y=±4x/3; a distant point approaches those directions while remaining on one of the two branches. The focal-distance difference, rather than a constant sum, distinguishes this hyperbola from an ellipse.
