Cumulative hazard function (H(t))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Survival function Hazard function
OurBigBook.com Words: 401 Articles: 3
Suppose T≥0 has an absolutely continuous probability distribution, with survival function S(0)=1. On an interval where S(t)>0, its cumulative hazard is
H(t)=∫0t​h(u)du=−logS(t),S(t)=e−H(t).
(7)
Here h is the hazard function; its integral is dimensionless because the units of rate and time cancel. The identity follows by integrating the derivative of logS, so mere continuity of the survival function is not enough: the absolute continuity assumption supplies a probability density function. If S reaches zero, interpret −logS as +∞. The normalization S(0)=1 also matters; an initial probability mass at zero would require an initial term −logS(0).
Accumulating hazard functions adds on the H scale and multiplies survival factors on the S scale. For S(a)>0 and b>a,
P(a<T≤b∣T>a)=1−e−[H(b)−H(a)].
(8)
For instance, a cumulative hazard increment of 2 gives event probability 1−e−2≈0.864665, rather than a probability of 2. With h(t)=0.2 per year, H(5)=1 and S(5)=e−1≈0.367879. With the increasing hazard function h(t)=0.04t per year when t is expressed in years, H(t)=0.02t2, so H(5)=0.5 gives S(5)=e−0.5≈0.606531. Its coefficient has units of inverse years squared.
The constant hazard function produces an exponential distribution; the increasing example is a Weibull distribution. Their survival functions both decrease, although one hazard function stays constant and the other increases. At year ten both have H(10)=2 and S(10)=e−2 despite different earlier histories. The accumulation identity is described in Lu Tian's Stanford notes, Section 1.1.
For a discrete hazard qj​ at each possible event time tj​, the exact counterpart is S(t)=∏tj​≤t​(1−qj​). Taking its negative logarithm sums −log(1−qj​), rather than qj​. Therefore exponentiating a Nelson–Aalen estimator does not give exactly the Kaplan–Meier estimator; small-jump comparison of cumulative hazard estimators quantifies the difference.
