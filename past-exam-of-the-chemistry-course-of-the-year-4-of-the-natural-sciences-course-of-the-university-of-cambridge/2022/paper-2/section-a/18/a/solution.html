A is poly(norbornene); B is the corresponding sequential block copolymer with a methyl-ester-substituted second block. The process is ring-opening metathesis polymerization. Each strained bicyclic alkene becomes a cyclopentane unit whose two backbone attachment positions are joined to neighbouring units through −CH=CH− links. The second block retains its CO2​Me substituent on the cyclopentane framework. Thus a useful connectivity representation is end−[CH=CH−C5​H8​]n​−[CH=CH−C5​H7​(CO2​Me)]m​−end, with the cyclopentane attachment positions at 1 and 3. This representation specifies connectivity rather than a single backbone alkene geometry.
The ruthenium alkylidene reacts with a monomer alkene through a metallacyclobutane, then fragments with the other pair of bonds broken. A new ruthenium alkylidene remains at the growing end, allowing propagation and then addition of the second monomer. Relief of the bicyclic monomer's ring strain supplies the principal enthalpic driving force; the loss of monomer translational entropy opposes polymerization. Sequential addition yields blocks only if the first population of active ends survives until the second addition.
