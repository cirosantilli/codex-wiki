Let R=CH2​CH(Ph)−O−TEMPO, so B is HO−R. Its hydroxyl group initiates ring-opening polymerization of lactide, giving
C=H−[OCH(Me)CO]2n​−O−CH2​CH(Ph)−O−TEMPO.​
(4)
Each lactide contributes two lactyl repeat units. Dry monomer, exclusion of water and an alcohol-compatible catalyst such as tin(II) 2-ethylhexanoate under heating provide a practical route. Alcohol/metal-alkoxide exchange followed by lactide coordination and acyl–oxygen cleavage extends the ester chain while retaining a terminal hydroxyl group.
Heating C under oxygen-free conditions homolytically cleaves its benzylic C–O bond to the nitroxide, releasing a benzylic radical and TEMPO. The radical adds styrene repeatedly; reversible trapping by TEMPO controls the active radical concentration. D is therefore a poly(lactide)–polystyrene block copolymer with junction PLA−OCH2​CH(Ph)−[CH2​CH(Ph)]m​−O−TEMPO and the hydroxyl end of PLA retained. This is nitroxide-mediated polymerization; a temperature around 120–130 °C is a representative condition for a TEMPO/styrene system, rather than an exact universal optimum.
