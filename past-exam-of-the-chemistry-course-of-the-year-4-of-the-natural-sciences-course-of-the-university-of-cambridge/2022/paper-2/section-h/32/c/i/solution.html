C and the dimer of D have the same composition, Cu2​Li2​(TMP)4​, but different metal ordering around an eight-membered metal–nitrogen ring. C has adjacent Cu sites and adjacent Li sites; in the conventional Gilman dimer D2​, Cu and Li alternate around the four metal positions. D is represented by Li[Cu(TMP)2​] per formula unit. The salt by-product is LiCl.
A TMP anion has formula C9​H18​N and mass about 140.25. Therefore the mass per empirical unit is 2(140.25)+63.55+6.94≃350.99, giving Mr​≃702 for the dimer, consistent with 699±5. A monomer would be near 351 and is excluded. In C, inequivalent Cu/Cu, Cu/Li and Li/Li amide surroundings, combined with the constrained TMP orientations, permit the three methyl environments in the observed 1:2:1 integral ratio. D2​ has the higher exchange-averaged methyl equivalence needed for one methyl signal. One signal need not imply that all instantaneous solid-state methyl environments are identical.
The remaining formulae follow from atom and charge balance:
E=CuTMP,F=Li[Cu(Ph)(TMP)],G=Li2​Cu2​(Ph)(TMP)3​.​
(45)
E can itself aggregate; its formula here specifies the copper-amide unit. F is a mixed organyl/amido Gilman cuprate, and G combines one F unit with one D unit. The distinct metal-ordering isomers and these mixed aggregates have experimental precedent.[ref]
