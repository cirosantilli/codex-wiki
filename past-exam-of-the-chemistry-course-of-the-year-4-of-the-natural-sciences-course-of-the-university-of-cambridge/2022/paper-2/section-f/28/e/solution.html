 ... Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 4 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge 2022 Paper 2 Section F 28 e
OurBigBook.com Words: 211
The secondary alcohol of H condenses with the aldehyde under acid catalysis to form a hemiacetal, then loses water to an oxocarbenium ion. Its tethered E alkene attacks the aldehyde-derived carbon through a chair-like six-membered arrangement: the alkene carbon farther from the alcohol forms the new C–C bond, placing a carbocation at the nearer alkene carbon. Trifluoroacetate captures that cation; the basic methanolic work-up cleaves the trifluoroacetate ester to reveal the ring hydroxyl group. This is a Prins cyclization.
Number the product ring from oxygen through the aldehyde-derived carbon as C2, then the alkene-derived methyl-bearing carbon C3, the hydroxyl-bearing carbon C4, the intervening methylene C5 and the original alcohol carbon C6. A chair keeping the bulky C2 side chain and C6 hydroxymethyl group equatorial predicts 2,6-cis substitution. Suprafacial alkene capture preserves the E-alkene-derived relationship, giving 2,3-trans between the C2 side chain and C3 methyl. Preferred equatorial trapping of the C4 cation gives 3,4-trans, hence the 2,4,6-cis arrangement of the substituent-bearing bonds in the favoured chair. In the printed flat-ring orientation, draw the C2 side chain, C4 hydroxyl and C6 hydroxymethyl behind the plane, and C3 methyl in front. Existing stereocentres in the aldehyde side chain and at C6 are retained; they anchor the absolute face of this assignment.
