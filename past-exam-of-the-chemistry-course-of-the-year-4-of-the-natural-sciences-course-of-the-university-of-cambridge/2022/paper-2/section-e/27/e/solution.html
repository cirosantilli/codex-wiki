The initial slope near one is the short-observation regime T(T)∼T/2. Successively longer observations include trajectories that enter progressively longer-lived traps before reaching the absorbing minimum. The four marked shoulders represent successive relaxation timescales, not four distinct equilibrium phases. A plateau appears when the contributing faster modes have largely completed but the next slow component has not yet substantially changed the conditional mean. The ultimate plateau is the true mean first-passage time, approximately e32.3 in the plotted time units.
To estimate a rate, locate the rounding timescale at which that mode stops growing with the observation window, using νl​T∼1; the final completion of a crossover requires several lifetimes. Graphical transitions lie approximately around lnT∼8, 23, 29 and 35, giving only order-of-magnitude estimates
νl​∼e−8, e−23, e−29, e−35 in inverse plot-time units.​

These are roughly 3×10−4, 10−10, 3×10−13 and 6×10−16, with factors of several uncertainty from reading broad crossovers. Plateau heights are weighted sums ∑Al​/νl​ divided by completed probability, not individually 1/νl​; in particular the final plateau at lnT≃32.3 does not require the slowest lifetime to be e32.3.
