In electrophoresis, an applied electric field drives a charged particle and its associated diffuse ionic atmosphere relative to the liquid. Electric forcing is balanced by viscous stresses, giving a steady velocity v=μe​E in the linear regime. The relevant electrokinetic potential is the zeta potential at the slipping plane, rather than necessarily the solid surface potential.
For a thin electric double layer, the surrounding layer develops an electro-osmotic flow and the Smoluchowski electrophoretic mobility is μe​=ϵζ/η. The sign of ζ fixes the direction. This transport is different from dielectrophoresis, which can move a neutral polarizable particle in a nonuniform field.
