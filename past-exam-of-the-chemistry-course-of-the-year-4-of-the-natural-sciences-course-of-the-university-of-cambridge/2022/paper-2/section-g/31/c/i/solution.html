The hard-core chain does not undergo an energetic low-temperature collapse. For allowed configurations the only finite energy is bond stretching. If kσ2≫kB​T, bond lengths remain near σ and temperature mostly changes the small bond-length fluctuations, not the distribution of angular conformations. The end-to-end distance therefore fluctuates over an expanded self-avoiding-coil distribution even at low temperature, with an approximate scale σ(N−1)ν and ν≃0.588 for sufficiently long three-dimensional chains.
For N=10, the scaling is qualitative and finite-chain effects matter. An equilibrated distance-versus-sweep trace is stationary, with correlated excursions; a histogram of that trace is a distinct plot, not a curve of probability density against simulation step. Neither the temperature in kelvin nor the spring constant is specified, so an exact histogram cannot be calculated. The illustrative figure compares like-for-like coil traces rather than asserting a unique numerical simulation result.
