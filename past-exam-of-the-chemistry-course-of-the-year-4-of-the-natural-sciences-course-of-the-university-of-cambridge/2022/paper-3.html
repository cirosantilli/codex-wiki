The first equivalent of titanocene methylidene methylenates the ester: the carbonyl oxygen and titanium form an oxatitanacyclobutane, whose fragmentation gives PhC(OMe)=CH2​ and a titanium–oxygen product. The ester's phenyl-substituted carbon lies opposite Ti; the two new methylenes originate from the two methylidene equivalents.

The C–OMe bond breaks, OMe transfers to Ti, one Ti–C bond becomes the new C=C bond, and the other Ti–C bond remains as the allylic nucleophile. One valid asymmetric route uses N-Boc-(R)-proline as a chiral starting material. Convert its acid to the Weinreb amide with N,O-dimethylhydroxylamine and a coupling reagent such as EDC, then add phenylmagnesium bromide at low temperature. chelation of the magnesium tetrahedral intermediate prevents a second addition; aqueous workup releases the phenyl ketone. Reduce it with sodium borohydride, separate the resulting alcohol diastereomers, and retain the one with R configuration at both pyrrolidine C2 and the benzylic carbinol. This is an asymmetric synthesis through a pre-existing enantiopure centre, even though the final reduction need not be perfectly diastereoselective. If the other protected alcohol is isolated, Mitsunobu esterification with a carboxylic acid followed by ester hydrolysis inverts just the carbinol centre. Keeping nitrogen Boc-protected during that inversion avoids neighbouring-amine participation. Mild Boc removal followed by reductive benzylation with benzaldehyde and sodium triacetoxyborohydride gives D, the (R,R) amino alcohol. Use conditions that avoid epimerizing the amino ketone.
For ring expansion, activate D's alcohol with methanesulfonyl chloride and a tertiary amine base. Its own tertiary nitrogen attacks the benzylic carbon from the back, displacing mesylate with inversion and making a fused aziridinium ion. Acetate, supplied as a soluble acetate salt, attacks the original pyrrolidine C2 from the opposite face and breaks the old C2–N bond. The new N–benzylic-C bond is retained, incorporating that side-chain carbon into the six-membered ring. Inversion at the two successively attacked carbons gives the cis acetate/phenyl piperidine E in the displayed orientation. Attack at the benzylic carbon instead would simply open the three-membered ion back to a five-membered-ring derivative; specifying the attacked carbon is the crucial regiochemical step.

When ∣eV∣ reaches a vibrational quantum, a tunnelling electron can lose energy by exciting that vibration. Opening an inelastic channel changes dI/dV at both voltage polarities. If the conductance increases in each direction, d2I/dV2 has a positive feature at positive threshold and a negative feature at the corresponding negative threshold. The three energy scales are 8, 48 and 375 meV, corresponding to about 65, 387 and 3025 cm−1. A multilayer perceptron usually connects every input unit of a layer to every output unit. For n inputs and m outputs its dense affine layer has nm+m parameters, including biases.
