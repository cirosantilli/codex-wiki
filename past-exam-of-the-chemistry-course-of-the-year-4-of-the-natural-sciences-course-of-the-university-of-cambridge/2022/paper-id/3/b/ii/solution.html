Excited O(1D) has two competing fates. Collisional deactivation returns ground-state oxygen, which reforms ozone rapidly; reaction with water instead gives net ozone loss and two OH radicals. The chemical branching fraction for the latter is
fw​=k2​[M]+k3​[H2​O]k3​[H2​O]​=6.38×108+3.30×1073.30×107​=0.0491803.
(18)
Thus only about 4.92% of these ozone photolyses directly lead to the specified net chemical loss; treating every photodissociation as permanent removal would overestimate the sink. The corresponding first-order rate is J1​fw​=9.8361×10−7 s−1. Converting the given hydroperoxyl mixing ratio to a concentration gives [HO2​]=20×10−12[M]=4.4×108 cm−3, so k4​[HO2​]=9.6800×10−7 s−1.
For a well-mixed marine atmospheric boundary layer of depth H=2×105 cm, the downward surface flux is vdep​[O3​]. Dividing by the layer volume per unit area gives a first-order dry deposition rate vdep​/H=7.5×10−7 s−1. The air density cancels on expressing the entire balance in ozone mixing ratio χ:
dtdχ​=Pχ​−(J1​fw​+k4​[HO2​]+Hvdep​​)χ.
(19)
At steady state, with Pχ​=0.25/3600 ppbs−1,
χO3​​=2.70161×10−60.25/3600​=25.7 ppb.​
(20)
The effective removal time is about 4.28 days. This calculation assumes a uniform layer of the diagnosed depth, fixed chemical conditions and a local time-independent balance.
