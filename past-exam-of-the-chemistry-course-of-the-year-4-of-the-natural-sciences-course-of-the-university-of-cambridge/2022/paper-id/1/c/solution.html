The global warming potential compares equal-mass pulses, while the tabulated greenhouse gas radiative efficiencies are per ppb. For a fixed emitted mass, the initial mixing ratio is inversely proportional to molar mass. The molecular formulas give approximately Mr​=151.39 gmol−1 for C2​H2​Cl3​F and M11​=137.36 gmol−1 for CCl3​F. Thus the ratio of mass-based greenhouse gas radiative efficiencies is (0.24/0.26)(M11​/Mr​).
In the standard one-box interpretation, the two quoted removal times represent independent first-order sinks acting on the burden. Their rates add, giving
τr−1​=τtrop−1​+τstrat−1​,τr​=(0.751​+201​)−1=0.72289 yr.

For exponential pulse removal, the time-integrated abundance is proportional to τ(1−e−H/τ). Dividing the replacement's forcing integral by the CFC-11 integral cancels the unknown carbon-dioxide reference integral:
GWP100,r​​=51600.260.24​151.39137.36​55(1−e−100/55)0.72289(1−e−100/0.72289)​≃67.8≃68.​​
(4)
The finite-horizon factor for CFC-11 is important: 1−e−100/55=0.8377, not one. Omitting the molar-mass correction would incorrectly compare equal-ppb rather than equal-mass pulses.
This is about 76 times smaller than the stated CFC-11 value, because rapid tropospheric removal outweighs the fairly similar per-ppb greenhouse gas radiative efficiencies. It still represents appreciable warming per emitted mass. If the shorter lifetime alone is used as the troposphere-dominated approximation, the result is GWP100​≃70.4, close to the combined-rate result. If the two given lifetimes instead describe separate local compartments, an exact two-box calculation would also require exchange rates and burden partitioning; those are not supplied. The boxed result states the usual one-box assumption explicitly.
