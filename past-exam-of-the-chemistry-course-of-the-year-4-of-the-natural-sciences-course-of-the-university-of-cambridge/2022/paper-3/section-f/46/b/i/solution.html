Choose a reaction coordinate s(q) that is negative in the reactant region and positive in the product region; s=0 defines a dividing surface. With unnormalized canonical phase-space averaging, define
Cfs​(t)=(2πℏ)f1​∫dqdpe−βH(q,p)δ[s(q)]s˙(q,p)Θ[s(qt​)].
(30)
The signed initial flux δ(s)s˙ counts both directions, while the later side function tests product commitment. The rate is k=Cfs​(tpl​)/Qr​ on a plateau after microscopic recrossings have died away but before reactant depletion and global equilibration become important. The t→0+ limit instead gives the transition-state-theory positive flux and can overcount trajectories that later return. A suitable dividing surface changes that early flux but not a correctly evaluated committed plateau rate. This correlation formulation provides the dynamical correction to a transition-state estimate.[ref]
