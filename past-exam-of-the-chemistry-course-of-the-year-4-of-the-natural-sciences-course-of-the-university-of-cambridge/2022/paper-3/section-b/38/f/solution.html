The initial nitrogen environment loses intensity on heating, and a lower-binding-energy component becomes prominent near the highest temperatures. A change of surface chemical species accompanies loss or conversion of the original adsorbate. Plausible processes include thermally activated decomposition with nitrogen-containing fragments remaining on copper, desorption of intact material or fragments, and rearrangement of the coordination environment. The extra components at intermediate temperatures suggest coexistence rather than a rigid, uniform shift of every molecule.
The individual curves have vertical offsets, so their baseline height is not a coverage measurement. Integrated, sensitivity-corrected N and C peak areas, a clean heated substrate control, and evolved-gas mass spectrometry would help distinguish loss of material from chemical conversion. binding energy alone cannot establish a unique fragment structure: local charge, metallic screening and coordination all affect it.
