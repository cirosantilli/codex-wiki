Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 4 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge / 2022 / Paper 3 / Section G / 48 / f / Solution

 ... Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 4 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge 2022 Paper 3 Section G 48 f
OurBigBook.com Words: 220
Manganese(III) removes one electron from the enol/enolate of H, and loss of its hydroxyl proton gives a carbon-centred radical at the tether-bearing alpha carbon of the resulting 1,3-dicarbonyl system. The enolic OH becomes the second carbonyl oxygen. This radical attacks the terminal CH2​ of the tethered isopropenyl group, forming a new C–C bond and a six-membered ring. The residual radical is on the substituted carbon bearing methyl, which is more stable than the primary radical from the alternative regiochemical closure.
Dioxygen traps that radical as a peroxyl intermediate. reduction/proton transfer and O–O cleavage in the ensuing oxygenation/workup sequence give the new tertiary alcohol on the methyl-bearing carbon. The drawn net transformation specifies that oxygenated product, not a persistent hydroperoxide; individual electron/proton transfers require the reaction medium and workup and cannot be fixed uniquely from the abbreviated reagent line alone. The rigid cage and folded tether select the closure face and therefore the relative configuration of the new ring junction.
Finally the new hydroxyl oxygen attacks silicon of TBSCl, with imidazole assisting deprotonation and chloride departure, giving the tertiary silyl ether I. The hindered pre-existing hydroxyls remain as shown. Track the two key atom changes: the terminal alkene carbon becomes a methylene in the new ring, and the former substituted alkene carbon acquires oxygen and retains its methyl group. Calling the first step a two-electron aldol reaction would miss both the single-electron oxidant and the role of dioxygen.
