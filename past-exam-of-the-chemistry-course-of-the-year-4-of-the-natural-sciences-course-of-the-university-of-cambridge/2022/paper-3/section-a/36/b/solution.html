Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 4 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge / 2022 / Paper 3 / Section A / 36 / b / Solution

 ... Past exam of the chemistry course of the year 4 of the natural sciences course of the University of Cambridge 2022 Paper 3 Section A 36 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 172
The first equivalent of titanocene methylidene methylenates the ester: the carbonyl oxygen and titanium form an oxatitanacyclobutane, whose fragmentation gives PhC(OMe)=CH2​ and a titanium–oxygen product. The second equivalent adds across this electron-rich double bond. The initial adduct is a titanacyclobutane with cyclic connectivity Cp2​Ti−CH2​−C(Ph)(OMe)−CH2​−, closing from the last methylene to titanium. The ester's phenyl-substituted carbon lies opposite Ti; the two new methylenes originate from the two methylidene equivalents.
Beta-methoxy elimination opens that metallacycle to B, Cp2​Ti(OMe)[CH2​C(Ph)=CH2​], the allyltitanium reagent. The C–OMe bond breaks, OMe transfers to Ti, one Ti–C bond becomes the new C=C bond, and the other Ti–C bond remains as the allylic nucleophile. The cyclic adduct is therefore a precursor to the opened reagent B; treating B as merely the one-equivalent enol ether loses the second reagent carbon.
The aldehyde oxygen coordinates to titanium. Allylic carbon transfer to its carbonyl carbon, accompanied by movement of the C=O pi electrons to oxygen, gives the titanium alkoxide PhCH2​CH2​CH(O−Ti)CH2​C(Ph)=CH2​. Acidic aqueous workup cleaves O–Ti and protonates oxygen, giving PhCH2​CH2​CH(OH)CH2​C(Ph)=CH2​. A six-membered allyl-transfer arrangement is a convenient mechanistic model. No enantioselectivity is imposed by these achiral reagents.
