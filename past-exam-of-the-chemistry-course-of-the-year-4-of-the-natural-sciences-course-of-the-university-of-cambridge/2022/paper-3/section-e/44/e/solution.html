The acid initially protonates 1 and switches the system into the lithium-bound reporter state, so 512 nm intensity rises rapidly. The resulting carboxylate then decarboxylates irreversibly. Its carbanion removes the proton from 1, giving neutral waste 5 and restoring the original zinc/lithium distribution. Thus the 512 nm signal is a transient pulse followed by recovery to its initial low baseline; no repeated oscillations follow from one fuel addition.
For first-order decarboxylation the surviving fuel-derived carboxylate fraction is e−kt with k=ln2/(75 min). If proton exchange and metal redistribution are fast, background is subtracted, and the population of the switched state is proportional to remaining protonated 1, the illustrative recovery is
I512​(t)=Ilow​+(Ihigh​−Ilow​)2−t/(75min)​,t>0.
(20)
Half the initial excess signal remains after 75 min and one quarter after 150 min in that limiting model. Actual binding equilibria can make the intensity nonlinear in remaining fuel; the supplied half-life fixes the fuel decay, not an exact reporter-intensity law. Monitor 1H NMR fuel consumption and waste formation alongside the optical trace to distinguish dissipation-driven recovery from photobleaching or simple dilution.
