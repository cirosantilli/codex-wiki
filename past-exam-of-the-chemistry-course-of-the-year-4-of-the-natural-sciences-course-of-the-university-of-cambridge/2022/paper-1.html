Gel permeation chromatography separates by solution hydrodynamic volume, rather than molar mass alone. At equal molar mass, a more extended chain accesses fewer pores and elutes earlier. The relatively rigid, conjugated poly(3-hexylthiophene) is the natural first-eluting candidate, compared with the more flexible chains, provided all samples are molecularly dissolved.
This is a structural prediction, not an unconditional ordering: solvent quality, polymer aggregation and column interactions affect retention, and the hydrogen-bonding polyamide may not dissolve adequately in tetrahydrofuran. An insoluble sample cannot be assigned an ordinary solution gel permeation chromatography elution time.
A differential refractive index detector can detect all four polymers when their refractive-index contrasts are nonzero. A single ultraviolet detector is unsuitable as the universal detector because polypropylene lacks the strong chromophores present in the aromatic and conjugated products.

Formal ionic counting gives La(III), N(−III), and therefore Re(VI), with a 5d1 configuration. An isolated localized d1 center would carry a saturation magnetic moment of order 1μB​, much larger than the value quoted. It is not a d0 ion made diamagnetic simply by an octahedral ligand field.

M≃μB2​g(EF​)B

In the Asakura-Oosawa model, nonadsorbing polymers are treated as ideal, mutually penetrable particles, but their centers cannot enter a layer around a hard colloid. The polymer osmotic pressure is Π=ρkB​T in the ideal dilute limit, with the appropriate number density in the space dimension considered.
For fixed polymer number, reducing the inaccessible volume increases the available volume and lowers the polymer Helmholtz free energy. Equivalently, at fixed polymer chemical potential, the polymer contribution to the effective colloid potential is
Udep​=ΠΔVexcluded​=−ΠVoverlap​.​
(17)
When the excluded layers overlap, their union occupies less space than two separate layers. More polymer configurations become available, so the resulting depletion force attracts the colloids. It is an entropy-driven effective interaction, even though each microscopic colloid–polymer interaction is purely repulsive.

This is an Ireland–Claisen rearrangement. Number the ester carbonyl carbon C0​, its enolizable carbon Cα​, and the three allylic ring carbons C1​−C2​=C3​, starting at the carbon bearing the ester oxygen.
lithium hexamethyldisilazide removes an α proton and trimethylsilyl chloride traps the oxygen of the resulting ester enolate, giving a silyl ketene acetal. Heating permits a six-electron, [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement through the array
Cα​=C0​−O−C1​−C2​=C3​.
