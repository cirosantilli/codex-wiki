This is an Ireland–Claisen rearrangement. Number the ester carbonyl carbon C0​, its enolizable carbon Cα​, and the three allylic ring carbons C1​−C2​=C3​, starting at the carbon bearing the ester oxygen.
lithium hexamethyldisilazide removes an α proton and trimethylsilyl chloride traps the oxygen of the resulting ester enolate, giving a silyl ketene acetal. Heating permits a six-electron, [3,3]-sigmatropic rearrangement through the array
Cα​=C0​−O−C1​−C2​=C3​.
(27)
The electron-pair movements are π(Cα​=C0​)→C0​=O, σ(O−C1​)→π(C1​=C2​), and π(C2​=C3​)→σ(C3​−Cα​). Acidic work-up cleaves the silyl ester to the carboxylic acid. The benzyl ether remains a protecting group; it is not the migrating bond.
The suprafacial reaction transfers the starting allylic stereochemistry to C3​, where the new side chain is delivered from the original oxygen face. In the product's printed orientation, the new C3​–Cα​ bond is behind the ring and the C3​ methyl group is toward the viewer. At that ring star, the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules orders the groups as side chain, alkenyl ring direction, saturated ring direction, methyl, giving R.
The second star is Cα​. Its priorities are carboxyl, substituted ring carbon, CH2​CH2​OBn, H. A chair-like transition state normally places the bulky side chain pseudoequatorial. However, its absolute configuration also depends on the E/Z geometry of the trapped silyl ketene acetal: opposite geometries give opposite configurations at this star under the same suprafacial ring transfer. The printed conditions do not state solvent or the ketene-acetal geometry. Thus the secure assignment is the ring-star R configuration and conditional α-star stereochemistry, rather than an unqualified absolute assignment to both stars. Drawing the assumed ketene-acetal geometry and its chair is necessary before selecting one α epimer.
