The diffusion coefficient relates particle flux to concentration gradient through Fick's first law, j=−D∇c. For unbiased isotropic motion in d dimensions it can equivalently be defined by the long-time mean squared displacement:
D=t→∞lim​2dt⟨∣r(t)−r(0)∣2⟩​.​
(53)
An uncorrelated random walk with jump length a and total jump rate Γ has ⟨r2⟩=Γa2t, hence D=Γa2/(2d); correlations introduce a multiplicative correlation factor. The factor d counts the directions sharing the total displacement. It does not mean that the same material's single-coordinate diffusivity changes merely by choosing to observe fewer coordinates: ⟨x2⟩=2Dt in every Cartesian direction of the same isotropic system.
At 500 K, silver iodide is in its fast-ion-conducting α phase. For an order-of-magnitude estimate, take a jump length a∼3 Å and an effective successful jump rate Γ∼1011 s−1. Then
D∼6(3×10−10)2(1011)​∼1.5×10−9 m2s−1.​
(54)
Thus D of order 10−9 m2s−1 is a reasonable estimate for this superionic conductor. The length and rate are explicitly assumed material scales; the question supplies neither a measured conductivity nor jump data, so an exact numerical coefficient cannot be deduced from temperature alone. The estimate is not presented as an experimentally measured value.
