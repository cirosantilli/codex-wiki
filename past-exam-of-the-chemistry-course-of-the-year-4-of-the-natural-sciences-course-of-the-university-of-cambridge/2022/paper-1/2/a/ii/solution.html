At high temperature, χT tends towards the nearly constant Curie constant of weakly correlated S=21​ centers. On cooling, χT falls because neighboring spins increasingly correlate antiparallel. The broad maximum in χ marks the crossover from the high-temperature Curie-like regime to substantial short-range order.
At still lower temperature, the Magnetic susceptibility becomes small because the antiferromagnetic ladder favors a singlet-like ground state and has few thermally accessible magnetic excitations. An even-leg spin ladder can have a spin gap, which reinforces this suppression. A broad Magnetic susceptibility maximum and falling χT support short-range antiferromagnetic correlations; they do not establish a three-dimensional Néel transition. Such a transition would require independent evidence, rather than identifying the broad maximum itself as the ordering temperature.
