For the tropospheric OH budget, let P be hydroxyl radical production per unit volume and K its total first-order loss frequency. The present hydroxyl radical steady state has
P=1 yr10−6(2.5×1019)​=7.922×105 moleculescm−3s−1,[OH]0​=1.3P​=6.094×105 moleculescm−3.
(13)
Here one year is 365.25 days; using it consistently makes the reported lifetimes in years independent of that conversion convention. Methane's chemical lifetime is τ0​=1/(k2​[OH]0​), giving
τ0​=8.254 yr.​
(14)
The current combined loss frequency already includes existing hydrogen, so add only the incremental hydrogen sink:
ΔK=k1​(2.0−0.5)10−6[M]=0.25125 s−1,K1​=1.55125 s−1.
(15)
Holding production and the other losses fixed then gives [OH]1​=5.107×105 moleculescm−3 and
τ1​=9.849 yr,τ1​/τ0​=1.19327.​
(16)
The rapid hydroxyl radical adjustment time, 1/K of order a second, supports its steady-state approximation in this box model. Subsequent methane growth would itself change hydroxyl radical consumption, so the computed increase is not a fully coupled long-term atmospheric prediction. Detailed molecular hydrogen modelling treats changes in methane, ozone and stratospheric water alongside reductions in other precursor emissions.[ref]
