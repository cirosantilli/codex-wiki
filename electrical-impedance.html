Electrical impedance (Z(ω)=V/I)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 18
Electrical impedance is the ratio of terminal electric potential difference and electric current phasors at one specified positive angular frequency ω. For an electrical circuit modeled as a linear time-invariant system, use the sinusoidal steady state after any decaying transient has settled. With the passive sign convention, positive electric current enters the terminal assigned positive electric potential difference. Define peak complex amplitudes by
v(t)=Re(Veiωt),i(t)=Re(Ieiωt),Z(ω)=IV​,I=0.
(31)
The physical v(t) and i(t) are real; the complex numbers encode their amplitudes and wave phases. The unit of electrical impedance is the ohm, 1Ω=1V/A. Both phasors may instead use root mean square magnitudes, with the same ratio Z. A nonlinear diode can generate additional frequencies; a single Z(ω) then does not describe the whole response.
Write the electrical impedance in Cartesian and polar forms as
Z=R+iX=∣Z∣eiϕ,R=ReZ,X=ImZ,∣Z∣=R2+X2​,ϕ=argZ=argV−argI.
(32)
Here R is the resistive real part, X the signed electrical reactance, and ϕ the voltage-minus-current phase difference, defined modulo a full turn. The Pythagorean theorem gives the modulus from a right triangle in the complex plane. The complex argument gives the rotation from the current phasor to the voltage phasor. Thus Vrms​=∣Z∣Irms​, while negative ϕ means electric current leads terminal electric potential difference. For R>0, ϕ=arctan(X/R) lies between −π/2 and π/2; outside that case, use the quadrant of the complex number rather than a bare inverse tangent. The geometric magnitude and phase difference are the two pieces of the AC relation.[ref]
Multiplication by Z acts on phasors, not on instantaneous physical signals. For example, when i(t)=I0​cosωt,
v(t)=I0​(Rcosωt−Xsinωt).
(33)
The second term is in phase quadrature with electric current; it cannot be represented by pointwise multiplication v(t)=Zi(t) when X=0. This is the useful compression: one complex multiplication replaces separate amplitude and phase difference calculations, while the real part restores the physical signal.
An ideal resistor, inductor and capacitor have ZR​=R, ZL​=iωL and ZC​=1/(iωC). Their electrical impedances add in a series RLC circuit, giving Z=R+i(ωL−1/(ωC)). The current amplitude is V0​/∣Z∣, so cancellation of the imaginary parts explains the resonant series RLC response. The component-law derivation and opposite complex exponential convention are in reactance phase convention.
