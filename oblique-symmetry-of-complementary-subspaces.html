Oblique symmetry of complementary subspaces (T(u+w)=u−w,V=U⊕W)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Order of a group element Involution Eigenspace decomposition of a linear involution
OurBigBook.com Words: 131
For complementary vector subspaces U,W of a real vector space, the rule T(u+w)=u−w is a well-defined linear map satisfying T2=I. It fixes U pointwise and reverses W. In a basis formed by adjoining bases of U and W, its matrix is diag(I,−I). Conversely every real linear map with T2=I has this form, with U=ker(T−I) and W=ker(T+I), as follows from v=(v+Tv)/2+(v−Tv)/2.
In a Euclidean space, such a linear map is an orthogonal transformation if and only if U and W are perpendicular. Necessity follows from ⟨u,w⟩=⟨Tu,Tw⟩=−⟨u,w⟩; sufficiency follows by expanding the squared Euclidean norm of u+w. In three dimensions, a fixed plane with reversed complementary line is a possibly oblique reflection, and a fixed line with reversed complementary plane is a possibly oblique axial symmetry. Their diagonal forms alone do not imply a Euclidean reflection in a hyperplane or a Euclidean half-turn.
