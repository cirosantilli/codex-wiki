Lateral mobility of membrane proteins (⟨r2⟩=4Dt)

 Home Biology Biological cell Cell membrane Membrane protein
OurBigBook.com Words: 467 Articles: 1
Lateral mobility of membrane proteins is movement along a membrane surface. It differs from membrane transport through the boundary and from reversing membrane topology. Attachment, crowding and barriers can restrict diffusion; the fluid mosaic model does not require every membrane protein to wander freely.[ref]
An original local model treats a small, approximately flat patch as an unbounded plane with uniform diffusion coefficient D, no drift and no trapping. Represent the two displacement coordinates by independent Brownian motions, each with zero expected value and variance 2Dt. Since r2=x2+y2, addition of the two coordinate expectations gives
⟨r2⟩=4Dt,rrms​=4Dt​.
(2)
This is a root-mean-square displacement, not distance traveled along a jagged trajectory or the displacement of every protein. For a stipulated D=0.08μm2s−1 and t=5s, ⟨r2⟩=1.6μm2 and rrms​≈1.265μm. Reaching a stipulated root-mean-square scale L=2μm gives t=L2/(4D)=12.5s. These model values are not measured diffusion coefficients or mixing times from the historical experiment.
Multiplying the target displacement scale by two multiplies the model time by four. This quadratic scaling is a useful way to see why local encounters and redistribution over an entire biological cell need not happen on the same timescale. A curved, bounded cell membrane, restricted paths and different mobile populations require more than this flat-patch model; disappearance of an initial pattern alone does not determine D.
