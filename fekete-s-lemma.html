Fekete's lemma (limxn​/n=infk≥1​xk​/k)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 108
For a real subadditive sequence (xn​)n≥1​, the normalized sequence has the extended-real limit of a sequence
limn→∞​nxn​​=infk≥1​kxk​​∈[−∞,∞).
(15)
No lower-bound hypothesis is needed for this conclusion. The limit of a sequence is finite precisely when the ratios are bounded below. The example xn​=−n2 is a subadditive sequence with normalized limit of a sequence −∞.
To prove the result, fix k≥1 and, for n≥k, write n=qk+r with q≥1 and 0≤r<k. Iterating subadditivity gives xn​≤qxk​+Ck​, where Ck​=max(0,x1​,…,xk−1​) and C1​=0. Hence limsupn​xn​/n≤xk​/k for every k. Every ratio is at least their infimum. If that infimum is finite these bounds identify the limit of a sequence; if it is −∞, choosing k with an arbitrarily negative ratio gives convergence to −∞.
