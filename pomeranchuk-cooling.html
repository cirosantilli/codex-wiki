Pomeranchuk cooling

OurBigBook.com Words: 397 Articles: 1
Low-temperature solid helium-3 can have greater entropy than the liquid through disordered nuclear spins, enabling compression cooling. [ref]
The sign follows from the Clapeyron equation. Let Sl​,Ss​ be molar entropies and Vl​,Vs​ molar volumes of liquid and solid, respectively, on their phase coexistence curve. Equality of their chemical potentials, with dμ=−SdT+VdP, gives
dTdP​=Vl​−Vs​Sl​−Ss​​.
(23)
With Ss​>Sl​ and Vl​>Vs​, the slope is negative. Reversibly freezing one mole at fixed temperature requires heat into the sample,
Ql→s​=T(Ss​−Sl​)>0,Ls→l​=T(Sl​−Ss​)<0.
(24)
Here Ls→l​ uses the melting convention for latent heat of fusion. This is the opposite sign from ordinary ice melting. The inequalities specify the regime; freezing does not universally cool a substance. In an approximately isolated sample, the growing higher-entropy phase draws on the sample's thermal energy instead of an external heater. Irreversible losses and incoming heat oppose the cooling.
