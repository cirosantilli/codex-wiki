Prime field (Q or Fp​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Field
OurBigBook.com Words: 157
The prime field of a field K is its smallest subfield: the arithmetic forced upon us by the identity element 1K​. Every subfield must contain 1K​, its repeated sums, their additive inverses, and the multiplicative inverses of nonzero results. In characteristic zero these operations produce a copy of the rational numbers Q. In positive characteristic of a field p, they produce a copy of Fp​=Z/pZ, with addition and multiplication given by modular arithmetic.
For example, inside the field with four elements, repeated addition of 1 gives 0,1,0,1,…. Thus its prime field is {0,1}, and the other two elements cannot be reached by repeatedly adding 1. This is why a finite field need not consist entirely of ordinary integer residues: a larger finite field extends the arithmetic of its prime field with additional elements.
The positive characteristic of a field must be a prime number. If its least vanishing multiple of 1K​ were rs with 1<r,s<rs, then (r1K​)(s1K​)=0 would exhibit zero divisors. The absence of zero divisors rules this out. The prime field supplies the scalars when viewing K as a vector space; its basis then explains the possible sizes of a finite field.
