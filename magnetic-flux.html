Magnetic flux (ΦB​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic field
OurBigBook.com Words: 246 Articles: 1
Magnetic flux measures the signed amount of magnetic field crossing an oriented surface S. Choose a unit normal n and use the oriented surface element dS=ndA. The defining surface integral is
ΦB​=∫S​B⋅dS=∫S​B⋅ndA.
(144)
For a uniform magnetic field across a flat surface of area A, the dot product reduces this to
ΦB​=BAcosθ,
(145)
where θ is the angle between the magnetic field and the chosen unit normal, rather than the plane of the surface. The SI unit is the weber, 1Wb=1Tm2. [ref].
For a stipulated magnetic field B=2T and area A=3m2, the magnetic flux is 6Wb at θ=0, 3Wb at θ=π/3, and zero at θ=π/2. In the last case the magnetic field lies in the plane of the surface, so it does not cross it. Reversing the chosen unit normal reverses the sign of magnetic flux without changing the physical magnetic field; at θ=π the same example gives −6Wb.
The geometric idea is projected area: tilting a flat surface reduces the area seen perpendicular to the magnetic field by the factor cosθ. This explains how rotation alone can change magnetic flux even when the magnetic field is constant; Faraday's law of induction turns such changes into an electromotive force. [ref].
