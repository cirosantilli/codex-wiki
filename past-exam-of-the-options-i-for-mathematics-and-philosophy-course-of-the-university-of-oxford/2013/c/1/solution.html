Euler's totient function is
φ(n)=#{1≤r≤n:gcd(r,n)=1},n≥1.
(5)
Euler's theorem states that gcd(a,n)=1 implies aφ(n)≡1(modn)​. For n>1, let r1​,…,rφ(n)​ be the invertible residue classes modulo n. Multiplication by a permutes these classes, since it has an inverse modulo n. Multiplying the resulting congruences gives
aφ(n)r1​⋯rφ(n)​≡r1​⋯rφ(n)​(modn).
(6)
Every factor is a unit, so the product can be cancelled, proving the theorem. The case n=1 is immediate. For distinct prime numbers p,q, removing multiples of either prime from 1,…,pq gives
φ(pq)=pq−q−p+1=(p−1)(q−1).​
(7)
For the RSA cryptosystem, choose distinct large primes p,q, put N=pq, and choose e coprime to φ(N). Compute d with ed≡1(modφ(N)). The public key is (N,e); the private exponent d is retained secretly. Represent a message by 0≤m<N, encrypt by c≡me(modN), and decrypt by m′≡cd(modN).
To prove correct decryption even for messages not coprime to N, write ed=1+k(p−1)(q−1). Modulo p, if p∣m then med≡m≡0; otherwise Fermat's little theorem gives med≡m. The same reasoning holds modulo q. The Chinese remainder theorem therefore gives med≡m(modN), and the chosen representative recovers m. Thus decryption works for every message residue, not merely the units.
