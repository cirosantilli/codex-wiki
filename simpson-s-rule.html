Simpson's rule integrates the quadratic interpolant through two endpoints and their midpoint. It is exact also on cubic polynomials. On [0,2], define the raw kernel K(t)=L[(x−t)+3​], where L is integral minus the rule. Then K(t)=−t3(4−3t)/12 for 0≤t≤1 and K(t)=−(2−t)3(3t−2)/12 for 1≤t≤2. It is strictly negative inside the interval and integrates to −1/15. The Peano kernel theorem therefore gives the sharp bound ∣L(f)∣≤∥f(4)∥∞​/90; f(x)=x4/24 attains equality. Scaling to endpoint spacing h gives h5/90. The normalized Peano kernel is K/3!, so the external factorial must be omitted when using that normalization.
