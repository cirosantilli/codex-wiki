Moment of inertia (I)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 21
The moment of inertia about a specified axis measures how a mass distribution resists changes of angular velocity about that axis. For point masses mi​ at perpendicular distances r⊥i​, or a continuous distribution with mass density ρ,
I=∑i​mi​r⊥i2​,I=∫r⊥2​dm=∫r⊥2​ρdV.
(197)
Its SI unit is kgm2. The distance is to the entire axis, not to one point on it: moving a point mass along the axis leaves its contribution unchanged. The moment of inertia depends on both the distribution and the chosen axis; it is not determined by total mass alone. About an axis fixed in an inertial frame, a rigid body with larger moment of inertia has smaller angular acceleration under the same net axial torque, and greater rotational kinetic energy at the same angular speed.[ref]
For a simple model, put four equal point masses of total mass M on light rigid spokes at radius a. Their moment of inertia is Ma2. Doubling a quadruples the moment of inertia without changing M. The illustrated placements a=R/3 and a=R give MR2/9 and MR2. Actual spokes and finite-sized weights contribute their own moments of inertia; the point mass model neglects them. Repositioning weights during rotation also breaks the fixed-distribution assumption, so it requires a separate work and angular momentum calculation.
The moment of inertia of a uniform disc follows by dividing it into concentric annuli: dm=(2M/R2)rdr and I=∫0R​r2(2M/R2)rdr=MR2/2. For an ideal thin ring every mass element is at R, so I=MR2. A ring of finite radial thickness instead uses the moment of inertia of a uniform annulus, I=M(R2+b2)/2, with inner radius b. A moment of inertia is thus a compact summary of radial placement rather than a complete description of shape. For several joined pieces, their moments of inertia add only when each is calculated about the same axis.[ref]
A physical pendulum makes the moment of inertia measurable through timing. Martín Monteiro, Cecilia Stari, Cecilia Cabeza and Arturo C. Martí used a metal bar with suspension holes 1.0cm apart and an attached smartphone. They report bar length 1.199m, bar mass 0.2518kg and phone mass 0.1311kg. Changing the suspension hole changes the pivot axis. Their readouts were phone gyroscope angular velocity records, analyzed by autocorrelation or a fit to a sinusoid, and an independent photogate; the logged records for the sinusoid fits lasted 10s. They observed agreement between the three timing methods and the geometry-based model, including a minimum of oscillation period as the suspension point varied. These are reported apparatus details and observations, not measurements made for this article.[ref]
To infer a moment of inertia from such a measurement, determine the total mass Mtot​ including every attached part, locate the assembly's centre of mass G, and measure its distance h>0 below the horizontal pivot O. Record successive crossings in the same direction, or the elapsed time tN​ for N complete oscillations, giving oscillation period P=tN​/N. The physical pendulum model with a fixed pivot, a uniform gravitational field, negligible friction and the small-angle approximation gives
IO​θ¨=−Mtot​ghsinθ,P≃2πMtot​ghIO​​​,IO,inferred​=Mtot​gh(2πP​)2.
(198)
The crossing times, masses and geometric distances are the measured inputs; IO,inferred​ is an inference conditional on this model, while an integral over the assumed shape supplies an independent model prediction. The parallel axis theorem converts the inferred pivot moment of inertia to one about a parallel axis through G. The controls and interpretation of that comparison are described there. An axial measurement determines one scalar moment of inertia, not the complete inertia tensor needed for general rotation.
