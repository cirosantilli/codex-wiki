Opaque barrier approximation (T∼V02​16E(V0​−E)​e−2κa)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Potential barrier Quantum tunnelling Finite rectangular barrier matching Rectangular barrier transmission probability
OurBigBook.com Words: 156
For fixed 0<E<V0​ and κa≫1, the exact rectangular barrier transmission probability gives
T∼V02​16E(V0​−E)​exp[−ℏ2a2m(V0​−E)​​].
(77)
This is the leading term of an asymptotic expansion: the wavefunction amplitude scale is e−κa, and the transmission probability scale is e−2κa, with an interface prefactor. Increasing width by Δa multiplies this approximation by e−2κΔa. At fixed incident energy, increasing barrier height increases the tunnelling decay constant as V0​−E​; heavier nonrelativistic particles also have stronger suppression. For the numerical example in rectangular barrier transmission probability, the approximation gives 0.01397, compared with the exact 0.01388.
The regime condition matters. Near E=V0​ at fixed width, κa tends to zero and the opaque barrier approximation fails; use the exact threshold value. For κa≪1, expansion of the hyperbolic sine gives
T≃[1+2ℏ2EmV02​a2​]−1.
(78)
This tends to one as a→0 at fixed E,V0​, but the condition κa≪1 alone does not imply nearly perfect transmission if the remaining factor is large. At E>V0​, use the oscillatory formula and its scattering resonances, rather than an exponential decay law.
