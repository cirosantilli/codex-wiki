Methane chlorination chain

 Home Chemistry Radical (chemistry) Radical chain reaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 215
Light initiates methane chlorination by photodissociation of Cl2​ through homolysis. The elementary bookkeeping is
initiation:propagation:​Cl2​hν​2Cl∙,Cl∙+CH4​⟶HCl+CH3​∙,CH3​∙+Cl2​⟶CH3​Cl+Cl∙.​
(3)
Here hν denotes the photon energy. The first propagation step replaces a chlorine radical with a methyl radical; the second regenerates a chlorine radical. Adding the two propagation equations cancels both free radicals and gives CH4​+Cl2​⟶CH3​Cl+HCl: chloromethane and hydrogen chloride are made while the chain carrier is regenerated.[ref]
For example, recombination 2CH3​∙⟶C2​H6​ of methyl radicals makes ethane and a covalent bond from two unpaired electrons, removing two carriers. Recombination of two Cl∙ also terminates carriers; in the gas phase, stabilization of the product requires disposal of released energy, for example to a third collision partner. The figure omits that partner. Termination competes with propagation, and further chlorination can consume chloromethane, so this cycle alone does not predict a pure product or a measured reaction rate.[ref]
