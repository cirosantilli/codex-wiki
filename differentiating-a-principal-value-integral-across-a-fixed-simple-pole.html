Differentiating a principal-value integral across a fixed simple pole (dxd​PV∫a(x)b(x)​q(t)dt=q(b)b′−q(a)a′)

Suppose q(t)=A/(t−c)+r(t), where r is continuous, the pole c is fixed, and differentiable endpoints obey a(x)<c<b(x) locally. Then
PV∫a(x)b(x)​q(t)dt=Aln∣a(x)−c∣∣b(x)−c∣​+∫a(x)b(x)​r(t)dt.
(44)
Apply the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus to get
dxd​PV∫a(x)b(x)​q(t)dt=q(b(x))b′(x)−q(a(x))a′(x).​
(45)
The same boundary expression as in the Leibniz integral rule thus applies to this explicitly specified Cauchy principal value. If A=0, the two ordinary one-sided improper integrals diverge, so their sum is not an ordinary integral. A finite boundary derivative alone does not make that divergent integral meaningful.
