For a nonrotating force-free sphere moving with speed U in a stationary tube, use its moving frame: the tube wall has velocity −U. In the parabolic lubrication gap h=h0​+x2/(2a), h0​=ϵa(1+λcosθ), moving-boundary lubrication flux gives q=−h3px​/(12μ)−Uh/2. The wall-shear and pressure-drop balance in a tube rules out an O(ϵ−3/2) net pressure jump. Applying this common pressure recovery to each angular strip gives q=−2Uh0​/3. The next balance yields the displayed positive localized pressure drop. Meanwhile the integrated leading shear stress on the sphere vanishes in each strip; this is consistent with negligible leading torque, and does not mean the local shear or pressure force vanishes.
