Isolated attosecond pulse

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Attosecond physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
An isolated attosecond pulse has one dominant short burst within the observation window, with any satellite emission quantified. A broad optical spectrum alone cannot distinguish one burst from several. Selecting a generation interval and an appropriate spectral band can suppress neighboring bursts, but the resulting temporal contrast must still be tested.
For a simple calculation independent of any apparatus, let E(t)=f(t)+reiθf(t−Δ), with two nonoverlapping copies of a short optical pulse. Its optical spectrum has intensity
∣E(Ω)∣2=∣f​(Ω)∣2[1+r2+2rcos(ΩΔ+θ)].
(39)
A satellite produces spectral fringes of angular spacing 2π/Δ when the two bursts are coherent and resolved. For chosen field-amplitude ratio r=0.2, the satellite/main energy ratio is r2=0.04, while fringe visibility is 2r/(1+r2)≃0.3846. Weak satellite energy can thus leave substantial fringes. Averaging an uncontrolled θ removes the interference term without removing the satellite: absence of fringes alone does not prove isolation.
