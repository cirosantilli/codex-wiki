Ring of functions (kX)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Ring Direct product of rings
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
For a commutative ring k and a set X, the set kX of all functions X→k is a commutative ring under pointwise addition and multiplication. It is the direct product of rings with one copy of k for each x∈X.
If k is a field and X is finite, every ideal is generated by some of the singleton indicator functions ex​. Indeed, whenever f in the ideal has f(x)=0, multiplication by ex​/f(x) puts ex​ in the ideal. Every function in that ideal is a finite sum of its coordinate multiples of these ex​.
If X is infinite and k is a nonzero commutative ring, the functions with finite support form an ideal without a finite generating set of an ideal. A finite collection of such functions vanishes outside one finite subset, as does the ideal it generates, whereas a singleton indicator function outside that subset belongs to the ideal of all finitely supported functions. Thus, for a field k, kX is a Noetherian ring exactly when X is finite.
