Orthogonal projection (P)

The orthogonal projection PF​ onto a closed subspace of a Hilbert space F sends x to its component in the orthogonal decomposition
x=PF​x+r,PF​x∈F,r∈F⊥.
(8)
The residual r is orthogonal to every permitted direction. For any z∈F, the Pythagorean theorem gives
∥x−z∥2=∥r∥2+∥PF​x−z∥2.
(9)
Thus PF​x uniquely minimizes the distance to F. It is a linear map, is idempotent, and is nonexpansive: ∥PF​x−PF​y∥≤∥x−y∥; taking y=0 gives ∥PF​x∥≤∥x∥.
In R2, project v=(1,3) onto the line spanned by u=(2,1). The inner product gives
PF​v=⟨u,u⟩⟨v,u⟩​u=55​u=(2,1),r=(−1,2).
(10)
The check ⟨r,u⟩=−2+2=0 confirms orthogonality, and ∥v∥2=10=5+5=∥PF​v∥2+∥r∥2. Projecting again changes nothing, since the result already lies on the line.
For a finite orthonormal basis e1​,…,em​ of F, the same operation is PF​x=∑j​⟨x,ej​⟩ej​, using the convention linear in the first argument. Replacing coordinate vectors by functions gives exactly the same approximation principle. For f(x)=x2 in the real L2 space on [−1,1] and F=span{1,x}, the directions are orthogonal, with squared norms 2 and 2/3. Their inner products with f are 2/3 and zero, so
PF​f=22/3​⋅1+2/30​⋅x=31​.
(11)
Direct integration gives the squared error ∥x2−1/3∥2=8/45. For any competing affine polynomial, the orthogonal residual yields
∫−11​(x2−a−bx)2dx=458​+2(a−31​)2+32​b2.
(12)
Every deviation from a=1/3, b=0 adds a nonnegative error. The best function need not pass through any selected data point: it minimizes an integrated error across the whole interval.
