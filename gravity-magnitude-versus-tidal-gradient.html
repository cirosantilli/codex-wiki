Gravity magnitude versus tidal gradient (g=μ/R2,λr​=2μ/R3)

 ... Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field Tidal tensor Tidal force
OurBigBook.com Words: 199
For a fixed source point mass, the magnitude g=μ/R2 of the gravitational acceleration measures the local pull; the radial eigenvalue λr​=2μ/R3 of the tidal tensor measures how that pull changes across space. Doubling R divides g by four but λr​ by eight. For a fixed small radial separation L, the linear relative acceleration is Δa≃λr​L, so
gΔa​≃R2L​.
(28)
A small body can therefore experience strong common gravitational acceleration with a comparatively small tidal force. A measurement of g alone does not determine the tidal tensor: sources at different distances can have the same g but different spatial variation.
The local stretching time scale is λr−1/2​=R3/(2μ)​: it marks when an initially resting separation could change by order itself if the tidal tensor stayed fixed. During free fall, R changes, so this scale is not a complete trajectory or a universal disruption time. Tidal disruption additionally compares the tidal force with binding by self-gravity and material forces; g by itself supplies no such comparison.
