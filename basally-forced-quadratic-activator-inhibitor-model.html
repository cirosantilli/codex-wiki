Basally forced quadratic activator-inhibitor model ((u∗​,v∗​)=(1+r,(1+r)2))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Diffusion equation Reaction–diffusion system
OurBigBook.com Words: 313 Articles: 2
A quadratic activator can have both basal production and activation inhibited by a second species. A normalized example has reaction rates f(u,v)=1+ru2/v−u and g(u,v)=q(u2−v), with r,q>0 and positive concentrations. At its spatially homogeneous equilibrium, v=u2, so u∗​=1+r and v∗​=(1+r)2. Writing a=(r−1)/(r+1), its reaction Jacobian matrix is J=(a2q(1+r)​−r/(1+r)2−q​). Thus trJ=a−q and detJ=q>0, giving linear asymptotic stability when q>a and instability when q<a. Basal production makes a change sign at r=1; a positive activation feedback needed for a conventional Turing instability requires r>1.
