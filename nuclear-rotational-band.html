Nuclear rotational band (EI​−E0​=ℏ2I(I+1)/(2J))

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus
OurBigBook.com Words: 548 Articles: 2
A sequence of nuclear energy levels interpreted as different total angular momenta built on approximately the same intrinsic structure. For an axially symmetric, positive-parity, K=0 ground state band of an even-even nucleus, the allowed sequence is I=0,2,4,…. The simple constant-inertia rotor has
Hrot​=2JI2​,I2∣IM⟩=ℏ2I(I+1)∣IM⟩,EI​−E0​=2Jℏ2​I(I+1).
(30)
Here J is an effective moment of inertia, and ℏ the reduced Planck constant. The even-I restriction belongs to this particular intrinsic symmetry and band, rather than to all nuclear excitations. The angular momentum operator eigenvalue supplies I(I+1), rather than the classical replacement I2.
Set ϵ=E2​−E0​. Since 2(2+1)=6, the model predicts
ϵE4​−E0​​=620​=310​,ϵE6​−E0​​=642​=7.
(31)
For a chosen model scale ϵ=90keV, the excitation energies are 0,90,300,630keV. The corresponding successive gamma ray energies are approximately 90,210,330keV, neglecting nuclear recoil. Level energies and emitted line energies are different lists. These numbers illustrate the formula and are not a measured isotope spectrum.
The fitted J=3ℏ2/ϵ is a response parameter, not automatically the moment of inertia of a classical rigid charge distribution. Nuclear pairing changes this response; Ben Roy Mottelson connected observed inertias with correlations beyond independent-particle motion.[ref]
At larger angular momentum, changing intrinsic structure or nuclear pairing can invalidate a single constant J. A useful diagnostic is to compare (EI​−E0​)/[I(I+1)] across several levels: constancy tests the simple formula, while a drift identifies the need for additional physics. A successful ratio alone neither specifies every microscopic motion nor measures a literal solid-body rotation.
