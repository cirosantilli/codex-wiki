Carbon-dioxide viewing-cell phase-transition experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamic limit
OurBigBook.com Words: 182
The institutional account CO2 viewing cell describes an optical cell at the University of Stuttgart: a fixed-volume carbon dioxide sample cools from above its liquid-gas critical point into phase coexistence. The apparatus supplies a view through the cell and displayed temperature and pressure readings. Near roughly 31∘C, the account reports critical opalescence: enhanced scattering of light makes reflected light appear whitish and transmitted light dark. This readout reveals growing density thermal fluctuations; it is not a direct count of molecules or a measurement of an infinite correlation length. [ref]
Fixed volume constrains the experimental path; the pressure varies and is a readout, not an independently held control. The account explicitly identifies the clip as part of a longer cooldown and says its endpoints are not steady states. To test an equilibrium interpretation more closely, one would hold temperature until the readout settles, compare cooling and warming, and check optical background away from the liquid-gas critical point; these are proposed controls, not measurements reported on that page. The account does not provide cell dimensions, filling number density, calibration uncertainties or such control runs. The sample is finite: its striking appearance motivates the thermodynamic limit as a model of macroscopic sharpness, rather than demonstrating a literal equilibrium singularity.
