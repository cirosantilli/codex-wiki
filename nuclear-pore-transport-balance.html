Nuclear pore transport balance (R=Cn​/Cc​)

 Home Biology Biological cell Cell nucleus Nuclear envelope Nuclear pore complex
OurBigBook.com Words: 272
Nuclear accumulation should compare concentrations, not just total cargo. In an original constructed example, a cell nucleus occupies one volume unit and the accessible cytosol occupies nine. Of 130 tracer units, 40 are nuclear and 90 are cytosolic. With nn​,nc​ the amounts and Vn​,Vc​ the accessible volume, the concentration ratio is
Cn​=Vn​nn​​=40,Cc​=Vc​nc​​=10,R=Cc​Cn​​=4.
(8)
Only 40/130=4/13≈30.8% of the cargo is nuclear, yet nuclear concentration is four times cytosolic concentration. A majority outside the cell nucleus does not rule out nuclear enrichment.
For comparison, freely exchanging nonbinding cargo at equal concentrations would have nn​=13,nc​=117: the nuclear fraction would equal the nuclear fraction of accessible volume, 1/10. These are constructed amounts, not measurements from the nuclear pore localization experiment. Binding, unequal recovery or cargo degradation would require additional accounting before interpreting an assay.
A minimal two-compartment ordinary differential equation makes the distinction precise. Assume fixed accessible volume, no synthesis or degradation, and effective first-order exchange rates a,b>0:
dtdnn​​=anc​−bnn​,nc​=N−nn​,dtdnc​​=−dtdnn​​.
(9)
Total cargo N=nn​+nc​ is conserved. Setting the derivative to zero gives
nn∗​=a+baN​,Cc∗​Cn∗​​=bVn​aVc​​.
(10)
For symmetric passive exchange with conductance P, a=P/Vc​ and b=P/Vn​, so the concentration ratio is one even when the amounts differ. Choosing instead a=4/9 and b=1 in reciprocal model-time units gives nn∗​=40 for N=130,Vn​=1,Vc​=9. The exact ordinary differential equation solution is
nn​(t)=nn∗​+(nn​(0)−nn∗​)e−(a+b)t.
(11)
Substitution verifies the solution, and a+b>0 makes departures decay. This effective model can describe an asymmetric exchange balance, but does not by itself identify a nuclear localization sequence, transport receptor or source of energy.
The usefulness of the nuclear pore complex is precisely this combination of separation and exchange: a cell nucleus can maintain a distinctive protein population while receiving new cargo and exporting RNA. The nuclear pore localization experiment asks what feature controls entry; the nuclear pore transport balance asks what an accumulation readout actually means.
