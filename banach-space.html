Finite codimension in a Banach space (codimX​Y=dim(X/Y)<∞)

Words: 67
Banach space OurBigBook.com
For a closed linear subspace Y of a Banach space X, finite codimension means the vector quotient X/Y has finite dimension. This definition does not subtract two infinite dimensions. A local stable manifold has the codimension of its tangent subspace. If the linearized phase space has a direct sum decomposition X=Es⊕Eu with dimEu=1, then X/Es is isomorphic to Eu, so a local stable manifold tangent to Es has codimension one.

Banach-Saks property

Words: 34
Banach space OurBigBook.com
A Banach space has the Banach-Saks property if every bounded sequence contains a subsequence whose arithmetic averages converge in norm. The weak Banach-Saks property requires this only for weakly convergent sequences. The weak Banach–Saks theorem in a Hilbert space establishes that weak version in Hilbert spaces.

Complemented subspace

Words: 123 Articles: 1
Banach space OurBigBook.com
A closed vector subspace Y of a Banach space X is complemented if a bounded linear operator P:X→X satisfies P2=P and P(X)=Y. Equivalently, X is the topological direct sum of Y and a closed subspace: a projection gives X=Y⊕kerP, and the coordinate projection of such a decomposition is bounded.

Pełczyński decomposition method

Words: 79
Complemented subspace OurBigBook.com
For a Banach space E satisfying E≅(⨁n≥1​E)p​, a complemented subspace W is absorbed by E: write E≅W⊕V, expand the countable sum, and absorb one additional copy of W into the countably many W summands. Hence E⊕W≅E. If Z is complemented in E and contains a complemented copy of E, write Z≅E⊕W with W complemented in E; the preceding argument gives Z≅E. The decompositions are Banach space isomorphisms, not merely algebraic decompositions.

Hereditarily indecomposable Banach space

Words: 120 Articles: 1
Banach space OurBigBook.com
An infinite-dimensional Banach space is hereditarily indecomposable if no closed infinite-dimensional subspace is a topological direct sum of two infinite-dimensional closed subspaces. Equivalently, for any two infinite-dimensional closed subspaces U,V and any ε>0, some unit vectors u∈U,v∈V satisfy ∥u−v∥<ε. A positive lower bound on such distances would make the addition map from U⊕V an isomorphism onto a closed subspace.

Gowers dichotomy theorem

Words: 56
Hereditarily indecomposable Banach space OurBigBook.com
Every infinite-dimensional Banach space contains either an unconditional basic sequence or a hereditarily indecomposable Banach space. For a space with a basis and no unconditional basic sequence, apply the block Ramsey theorem to finite sequences whose odd and even spans contain nearby unit vectors. A diagonal sequence of successively sharper games gives a subspace in which every pair of infinite-dimensional subspaces has angle zero.

Quotient Banach space (X/Y)

Words: 152 Articles: 2
Banach space OurBigBook.com
For a closed vector subspace Y of a Banach space X, the quotient vector space X/Y is complete with its quotient norm. Closedness makes the quotient seminorm a norm. Completeness follows by choosing representatives of a rapidly Cauchy subsequence whose successive differences have summable norms in X.

Approximate weakly null lifting through a quotient

Words: 73
Quotient Banach space OurBigBook.com
If a Banach space X has separable dual and q:X→X/Y is a quotient by a closed subspace, every weakly null sequence in the quotient's closed unit ball has a subsequence with approximate weakly null lifts of norm at most 3. Choose representatives of norm below 3/2, pass to a common bidual weak-star limit, and subtract convex blocks whose quotient images tend to zero in norm. Convex-block cancellation of a weak-star limit gives weak nullity and the triangle inequality gives the bound 3.

Quotient norm (∥x+Y∥=infy∈Y​∥x+y∥)

Words: 38
Quotient Banach space OurBigBook.com
The quotient norm measures the smallest norm among representatives of a coset. Its infimum need not be attained. Nevertheless, for every ε>0, an element z∈X/Y has a representative v∈X with ∥v∥<∥z∥+ε. The quotient map is contractive.

Convex block (un​=∑i=pn​qn​​ai​xi​)

Words: 93 Articles: 1
Banach space OurBigBook.com
A convex block of a sequence in a Banach space is a sequence of convex combinations supported on successively disjoint finite intervals, with pn​<qn​<pn+1​, ai​≥0, and ∑i=pn​qn​​ai​=1. Zero coefficients can fill gaps and ensure strict endpoint inequalities. By the Mazur theorem, every weakly null sequence has convex blocks tending to zero in norm.

Convex-block cancellation of a weak-star limit

Words: 45
Convex block OurBigBook.com
If a bounded sequence (yn​) in X converges weak-star to ϕ∈X∗∗ under the canonical embedding into the bidual, then any convex blocks (un​) have the same scalar limit on every f∈X∗. Thus yn​−un​ is a weakly null sequence, even when ϕ does not belong to the embedded space X.

Duality mapping (J(x)=∂(21​∥x∥2))

Words: 55
Banach space OurBigBook.com
The duality mapping takes a vector to its supporting dual functionals for half its squared norm. Equivalently p∈J(x) when ∥p∥∗​=∥x∥ and ⟨x,p⟩=∥x∥2. This follows from the dual-norm inequality and equality in the conjugate relation for squared norms. In a Hilbert space, the Riesz representation theorem identifies it with the identity; in a general Banach space it can be set-valued.

Bounded scalar functions on an index set (ℓ∞​(Γ))

Words: 94 Articles: 1
Banach space OurBigBook.com
For an arbitrary set Γ and scalar field F, this is the space of bounded functions u:Γ→F with the supremum norm ∥u∥=supγ​∣u(γ)∣. Uniform limits of Cauchy sequences prove it is a Banach space. For Γ=N it is the l-infinity sequence space; for the empty index set it is the zero space, with norm zero.

Coordinate functional representation of an operator into bounded indexed functions (∥T∥=supγ​∥fγ​∥)

Words: 40
Bounded scalar functions on an index set OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator T:X→ℓ∞​(Γ) is exactly a uniformly bounded family of bounded linear functionals fγ​∈X∗ through (Tx)(γ)=fγ​(x). Evaluation shows ∥fγ​∥≤∥T∥; conversely the family bound gives ∥Tx∥≤(sup∥fγ​∥)∥x∥, proving equality. Taking all functionals in the dual unit ball gives an isometric embedding by the Hahn-Banach theorem.

Separable Banach space

Words: 29
Banach space OurBigBook.com
A Banach space is separable when it has a countable dense subset for its norm topology. It then has a countable base of norm balls and a countable norming family. Its continuous dual space need not be norm separable.

Absolutely summable sequence space (ℓ1={x:∑n​∣xn​∣<∞})

Words: 45
l-p sequence space OurBigBook.com
The Banach space ℓ1 consists of absolutely summable scalar sequences, with norm ∥x∥1​=∑n​∣xn​∣. Its continuous dual is ℓ∞, under the pairing ∑n​xn​yn​. Finitely supported sequences are norm dense, which identifies a linear functional from its values on coordinate vectors. The space of sequences converging to zero inside this dual is a norming subspace of infinite codimension.

Generalized limit (liminfxn​≤L(x)≤limsupxn​)

Words: 56
l-infinity sequence space OurBigBook.com
A generalized limit on real bounded sequences is a positive linear functional of operator norm one that extends the ordinary limit of a sequence. Such a functional can be chosen by the Hahn-Banach theorem: extend the limit on the convergent sequence space while dominating it by the sublinear functional p(x)=limsupn​xn​. Applying the same domination to −x gives the displayed lower bound. A Banach limit additionally has invariance under the left shift on bounded sequences.

Banach limit (L:ℓ∞(N;R)→R)

Words: 196 Articles: 2
l-infinity sequence space OurBigBook.com
A Banach limit is a positive linear functional on the real l-infinity sequence space that extends the ordinary limit on the convergent sequence space, has operator norm one, and is invariant under the left shift on bounded sequences. The Hahn-Banach theorem constructs one by separating the constant sequence 1 from the subspace (I−S)ℓ∞, whose distance from 1 is one. The functional is generally not unique.

Cesàro construction of a Banach limit (p(x)=limsupN​N−1∑j=1N​xj​)

Words: 73
Banach limit OurBigBook.com
The displayed sublinear functional on the real l-infinity sequence space agrees with the ordinary limit on convergent sequences. A dominated extension by the Hahn-Banach theorem therefore defines a generalized limit bounded between the lower and upper limits of the Cesaro means. For the left shift on bounded sequences S, the averages of Sx−x equal (xN+1​−x1​)/N, which tend to zero. Domination applied to both signs gives L(Sx−x)=0, hence shift invariance. Positivity, agreement on constant sequences and the operator norm one follow from the bounds, so L is a Banach limit.

Ultrafilter construction of a Banach limit (L(x)=limn→U​n1​∑j=1n​xj​)

Words: 73
Banach limit OurBigBook.com
A nonprincipal ultrafilter on the natural numbers gives a Banach limit by taking the ultralimit of the Cesaro means of each bounded sequence. Compactness of a closed bounded interval, or disk for complex sequences, supplies the limit. Finite intersections in the filter give linearity; nonnegative averages give positivity; constants give norm one. The Cesaro theorem for convergent sequences gives agreement with ordinary limits because the ultrafilter contains every cofinite set. For the left shift on bounded sequences, the two means differ by (xn+1​−x1​)/n, which tends to zero, proving shift invariance.

Riesz's lemma

Words: 65 Articles: 1
Banach space OurBigBook.com
If Y is a proper closed subspace of a normed space X and 0<α<1, there is a unit vector x∈X whose distance from Y exceeds α.

Compact unit ball characterizes finite-dimensional normed spaces

Words: 36
Riesz's lemma OurBigBook.com
A normed vector space has compact closed unit ball if and only if it is finite-dimensional. In an infinite-dimensional space, repeated application of Riesz lemma produces unit vectors separated pairwise by a fixed positive distance, contradicting sequential compactness.

Nearest point in a uniformly convex Banach space

Words: 69
Uniformly convex Banach space OurBigBook.com
Every nonempty closed convex subset of a uniformly convex Banach space has a unique nearest point to each vector. A minimizing sequence is Cauchy: otherwise uniform convexity would put some midpoint strictly closer than the infimum. Completeness and closedness give its limiting point. In ℓp, p≥2, the Clarkson inequality makes this quantitative: the distance between two minimizing vectors tends to zero after subtracting the midpoint's lower distance bound. Nonemptiness is required.

Uniform boundedness principle

Words: 145 Articles: 2
Banach space OurBigBook.com
If a family of bounded linear operators from a Banach space to a normed space is pointwise bounded, then their operator norms are uniformly bounded.

Weak boundedness implies norm boundedness

Words: 76
Uniform boundedness principle OurBigBook.com
A subset of a normed space is norm bounded if it is bounded under every continuous linear functional. Apply the Uniform boundedness principle on the complete dual space to the evaluation family f↦f(x). Hahn-Banach theorem identifies the evaluation norm with ∥x∥, even when the original normed space is not complete. As a consequence, two norms with exactly the same continuous dual as sets must be equivalent: apply this criterion to either norm's unit ball in the other norm.
