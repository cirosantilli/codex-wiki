Meissner screening current (Jy​(z)=−B0​e−z/λL​/(μ0​λL​))

In SI units, the magnetostatic Ampère law gives the current density supporting Planar London screening:
J=μ0​1​∇×B=−μ0​λL​B0​​e−z/λL​y​.
(24)
Here μ0​ is the vacuum permeability. The sign follows from (∇×B)y​=∂z​Bx​ in the chosen coordinates. Thus B0​>0 requires electric current along −y​. Its own magnetic field opposes the imposed magnetic field inside the material. The electric current is distributed over a finite layer; its depth integral is the effective surface current density
K=∫0∞​J(z)dz=−μ0​B0​​y​.
(25)
The units are Am−2 for J and Am−1 for K. Shrinking λL​ at fixed B0​ increases the surface current density, while this integrated surface current density stays fixed. The current sheet approximation is the large-length-scale limit of this layer, not a claim that an actual sample carries an infinitely thin electric current. It reproduces the magnetic-field jump across a surface current between the exterior B0​x and a field-free interior, with outward unit normal −z.
