Sedimentation scale height (H=kB​T/(ΔρVg))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process Brownian motion Perrin sedimentation experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 348
Take identical rigid spheres of radius a, particle density ρp​ and displaced liquid density ρf​, at uniform absolute temperature T. Let z increase upwards from a reference layer and write Δρ=ρp​−ρf​>0. The buoyancy correction gives the effective gravitational mass
V=34πa3​,mb​=(ρp​−ρf​)V,U(z)−U(0)=mb​gz.
(7)
The actual grain mass is ρp​V; the displaced liquid subtracts ρf​V from the effective gravitational mass. In a dilute, noninteracting particle suspension, the Boltzmann distribution therefore gives
n(0)n(z)​=exp(−kB​Tmb​gz​)=e−z/H,H=mb​gkB​T​=ΔρVgkB​T​.
(8)
Here n is number density and kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. Equivalently, the dilute osmotic pressure Π=nkB​T supports the grains above it: dΠ/dz=−nmb​g. Dividing by n and integrating yields the same exponential function. No dynamic viscosity appears in this equilibrium argument. If ρp​<ρf​, the population instead increases upwards; at equal densities this gravitational model predicts uniform n.
A microscope counts a finite optical slab, not an infinitesimal plane. For equal viewed area A and slab thickness δ, an ideal uniform detection window centred at z gives
C(z)=A∫z−δ/2z+δ/2​n(s)ds=2AHn(0)sinh(2Hδ​)e−z/H.
(9)
Thus equal optical volumes preserve the exponential function count ratio exactly, even for finite δ, provided the windows lie wholly inside the accessible chamber and have the same detection response. Unequal depth response or overlapping a boundary can corrupt this inference. Repeated observations estimate the mean count; closely spaced visits need not be independent because grains remain in view.
For an original chosen model, set a=0.250μm, ρp​=1200kgm−3, ρf​=1000kgm−3, T=298K and g=9.81ms−2. These are illustrative inputs, not Jean Baptiste Perrin's measurements. Using kB​=1.380649×10−23JK−1 gives
V≃6.5450×10−20m3,mb​≃1.3090×10−17kg,H≃32.04μm.
(10)
If equal observation effort gives expected count 400 in the reference layer, the expected counts 20, 40 and 60μm higher are approximately 214.3, 114.8 and 61.49. Fractions describe expected values over observations, not fractional grains. Using the actual grain mass instead of its buoyancy-corrected value would make the inferred Avogadro constant six times too small for these chosen densities.
