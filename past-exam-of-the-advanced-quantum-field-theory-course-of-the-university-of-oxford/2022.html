There are two one-loop Feynman diagrams: the two orders of the labelled photons around a top quark fermion loop, with one Yukawa interaction insertion for the outgoing Higgs boson. They are added, rather than identified by a Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. Each has the same color charge trace Nc​=3.
In the first routing, traverse the fermion loop from the p2​ insertion to the p1​ insertion with momentum l, then to the Higgs boson insertion with momentum l+p1​, then back to the p2​ insertion with momentum l−p2​. Conservation at the Higgs boson insertion gives ph​=p1​+p2​. The second routing exchanges (p1​,μ) and (p2​,ν): its three internal momenta are l, l+p2​ and l−p1​. Reversing the orientation is another way to represent this second ordering. Two vector insertions give no Furry theorem cancellation.

Use Minkowski metric g=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and the convention Sfi​=δfi​+i(2π)4δ(4)(pf​−pi​)Mfi​. Put m=mt​, y=λt​/2​, Q=et​=2/3 and
D0​=l2−m2+i0,D1​=(l+p1​)2−m2+i0,D2​=(l−p2​)2−m2+i0.
(1)

Tμν​(l)=tr[γμ​(l+m)γν​(l−p2​+m)(l+p1​+m)].
(2)
Here p=γρpρ​ is Feynman slash notation. The closed fermion loop sign is −1, the three interaction vertices contribute (−iy)(−ieQ)2, and the three Dirac propagators contribute i3. Their product, including the color charge trace, is −Nc​ye2Q2. Thus one ordering has

The displayed four-dimensional Feynman integral is formal until regularized; subsequent manipulations use dimensional regularization, which permits the required loop momentum shifts.

Set s=(p1​+p2​)2=mh2​, so p12​=p22​=0 and p1​⋅p2​=s/2. The physical polarization vectors obey ϵ1​⋅p1​=ϵ2​⋅p2​=0. Write Xμν​≃Yμν​ when they agree after contraction with these polarization vectors; terms proportional to p1,μ​ or p2,ν​ may then be discarded.
The gamma matrix trace identities kill the terms with an odd number of gamma matrices. The remaining three two-momentum terms and the term with three factors of m are
4mTμν​​=​m2gμν​+lμ​(l−p2​)ν​+(l−p2​)μ​lν​−gμν​l⋅(l−p2​)+lμ​(l+p1​)ν​+(l+p1​)μ​lν​−gμν​l⋅(l+p1​)+gμν​(l−p2​)⋅(l+p1​)−(l−p2​)μ​(l+p1​)ν​+(l+p1​)μ​(l−p2​)ν​.​
(5)
In the gμν​ coefficient the terms linear in l⋅pi​ cancel, leaving m2−l2−s/2. Among the other terms, the surviving mixed products of l and pi​ also cancel. Consequently
Tμν​≃4m[4lμ​lν​+p2,μ​p1,ν​+(m2−s/2−l2)gμν​].
(6)
In the second Feynman diagram, exchange the external labels and change the dummy loop momentum l↦−l. Its projected numerator and denominator become identical to those of the first Feynman diagram. Hence
Mμν′​=8iNc​ye2Q2m∫(2π)4d4l​D0​D1​D2​4lμ​lν​+p2,μ​p1,ν​+(m2−s/2−l2)gμν​​.​
(7)
With the common factor 8m extracted, the primitive integer coefficients are
A=4,B=1,C=1,prefactor=8iNc​2​λt​​e2et2​mt​.​
(8)
Equivalently, extracting only m gives (A,B,C)=(32,8,8) and prefactor iNc​λt​e2et2​mt​/2​. A common rescaling of all three coefficients changes the prefactor inversely. The physical scattering amplitude is ϵ1μ​ϵ2ν​Mμν′​; the projected integrand is not an identity for an arbitrary uncontracted off-shell tensor.

Continue the loop momentum to D=4−ε dimensions, keeping tr1=4 and the external physical polarization vectors four dimensional. Introduce Feynman parameters x,y≥0, x+y≤1, denoting this integration triangle by T. Completing the square gives
D0​D1​D2​1​=2∫T​dxdy(k2−Δ+i0)31​,k=l+xp1​−yp2​,Δ=m2−sxy.
(9)
Odd terms in k integrate to zero. Lorentz invariance of dimensional regularization gives kμ​kν​↦gμν​k2/D. The general projected numerator therefore becomes, under the Feynman integral,
gμν​[(DA​−C)k2+C(m2−s/2+sxy)]+(B−Axy)p2,μ​p1,ν​.
(10)
Only the k2 term can give an ultraviolet divergence. Define, with the conventional scale factor implicit,
Jn​(Δ)=μRε​∫(2π)DdDk​(k2−Δ+i0)−n,I2​=J2​+ΔJ3​.
(11)
The gamma function formula gives
J2​=16π2i​ε2​+O(1),J3​=−32π2Δi​+O(ε).
(12)
Thus the pole of the numerator integral, including 2∫T​dxdy=1, is
gμν​(4A​−C)8π2εi​,A=4C.​
(13)
The coefficients (4,1,1) satisfy this condition. A nonzero local divergence would have to be removed by a counterterm. The Ward identity forbids a local hAμ​Aμ term, and the first electromagnetic gauge-invariant coupling with one Higgs boson and two photons has the form hFμν​Fμν, of engineering dimension five. It is not an independent counterterm of the Standard Model, a renormalizable quantum field theory. The top quark contribution is separately electromagnetic gauge-invariant; no additional W boson loop Feynman diagram is needed to cancel this pole.
The finite remainder must still be retained. For A=4C,
DA​−C=4−εCε​,limε→0​(DA​−C)I2​=32π2iC​.
(14)
Putting D=4 before evaluating the divergent Feynman integral would incorrectly set this finite contribution to zero.

Use the Feynman parameters and J3​ defined in Solution. With A=4C, the finite term just found is −CΔJ3​. Combining it with the remaining gμν​ coefficient gives
C[−Δ+m2−s/2+sxy]J3​=−2sC​(1−4xy)J3​.
(15)
The other coefficient is (B−4Cxy)J3​. Thus, writing Iμν​ for the Feynman integral without the overall prefactor,
Iμν​=2∫T​dxdyJ3​(Δ)[−2sC​(1−4xy)gμν​+(B−4Cxy)p2,μ​p1,ν​].
(16)
For Tμν​=2s​gμν​−p2,μ​p1,ν​, the two coefficients must have the corresponding ratio. The mismatch is simply (B−C)J3​, independent of xy. Therefore the required relation is
B=C,​
(17)
which is satisfied. In the primitive normalization the result is
Iμν​=16π2i​∫01​dx∫01−x​dym2−sxy−i01−4xy​Tμν​,
(18)
and the full top quark scattering amplitude, in our stated S convention, is
M=−2π2Nc​ye2Q2m​ϵ1μ​ϵ2ν​Tμν​∫01​dx∫01−x​dym2−sxy−i01−4xy​.​
(19)
Indeed p1μ​Tμν​=p2ν​Tμν​=0: replacing either polarization vector by its photon momentum makes the scattering amplitude vanish. This is the Ward identity, expressing electromagnetic gauge invariance. The result also is symmetric under exchange of the two photons. The finite term in Solution is essential for this Ward identity.
As an independent low-energy check, for s/m2→0 the Feynman parameter integral is
m21​∫T​(1−4xy)dxdy+O(s/m4)=3m21​+O(s/m4).
(20)
The coefficient is consequently −Nc​ye2Q2/(6π2m) at leading order. It has the inverse-mass dimension required for an effective hFμν​Fμν coupling. If y=m/v, this coefficient stays finite in the large-m limit at fixed v; that limit increases the Yukawa coupling, so it is not a fixed-coupling heavy-field decoupling limit.

At the stated order the renormalization-group beta function is separable:
∫αi​αf​​α2dα​=b0​∫μi​μf​​μdμ​.
(21)
Consequently
αf​1​=αi​1​−b0​lnμi​μf​​,αf​=1−b0​αi​ln(μf​/μi​)αi​​.​
(22)
For a positive initial running coupling, this expression is used on the connected interval containing μi​ where its denominator is positive and perturbation theory remains valid.
If b0​<0, the running coupling decreases at high renormalization scale and tends to zero: this is asymptotic freedom. Decreasing the renormalization scale increases the running coupling, with a formal infrared pole at Λ=μi​exp[1/(b0​αi​)]. Near Λ, perturbation theory fails; the pole alone is not a proof of confinement. Both QCD and the SU(2) weak gauge coupling have this sign for the Standard Model particle content above the relevant thresholds.
If b0​>0, the running coupling decreases towards low renormalization scale and grows towards high renormalization scale. The formal ultraviolet Landau pole is μL​=μi​exp[1/(b0​αi​)]. The pole signals failure of this perturbative evolution, rather than a controlled physical infinity. The hypercharge gauge coupling and ordinary QED with charged matter have this sign.
If b0​=0, the running coupling is constant at this order; higher-order terms may still make it run. No Standard Model gauge factor has a vanishing one-loop order coefficient for the usual particle content.
For precise normalization, take αi​=gi2​/(4π) and Q=T3​+Y. The Standard Model one-loop gauge coefficients then give
(b0Y​,b02​,b03​)=2π1​(641​,−619​,−7).
(23)
These coefficients use d/dlnμ, not d/dlnμ2. Threshold matching changes the active particle content, so the single constant-b0​ expression is applied piecewise across thresholds.

There is one tree-level Feynman diagram: annihilation of the up quark and its antiquark into an s-channel gluon, followed by production of the down quark and its antiquark. A gluon interaction vertex preserves quark flavor, so a t-channel line joining the incoming up quark to the outgoing down quark is impossible.
Let i,j,k,l label the initial quark, initial antiquark, final quark, final antiquark color charges. Up to an irrelevant common sign, the scattering amplitude is
Ms​=sg2​(Ta)ji​(Ta)kl​[vˉ(p2​)γμu(p1​)][uˉ(p3​)γμ​v(p4​)].
(24)
The Mandelstam variables are s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​−p3​)2, u=(p1​−p4​)2, with s+t+u=0 in the zero-mass limit. The spin sum uses ∑uuˉ=∑vvˉ=p, and the initial spin average contributes 1/4. The resulting two gamma matrix traces contract to
41​tr(p2​γμp1​γν)tr(p3​γμ​p4​γν​)​=8[(p2​⋅p3​)(p1​⋅p4​)+(p2​⋅p4​)(p1​⋅p3​)]=2(t2+u2).​
(25)
The initial color average contributes 1/Nc2​. Summing final color charges gives the distinct-flavor quark scattering color factor
Kss​=Nc2​1​∑a,b​tr(TaTb)tr(TaTb)=4Nc2​Nc2​−1​.
(26)
Therefore
∣Ms​∣2​=g42Nc2​Nc2​−1​s2t2+u2​=94g4​s2t2+u2​,C=94g4​.​
(27)
Equivalently C=64π2αs2​/9 for the QCD coupling αs​=g2/(4π). There is no initial gluon spin average: the gluon is internal.

The two Bhabha scattering tree-level Feynman diagrams are the two panels of Solution with a photon mediator: s-channel annihilation with internal momentum p1​+p2​, and t-channel exchange with internal momentum p1​−p3​. Each interaction vertex preserves the electron species. There is no independent u-channel Feynman diagram, since the outgoing electron and Positron are distinguishable.
Removing the color charge factor from Solution and replacing g by e gives
∣Ms​∣2​=2e4s2t2+u2​.
(28)
Crossing symmetry interchanges s and t for the exchange Feynman diagram, giving
∣Mt​∣2​=2e4t2s2+u2​.
(29)
Squaring the sum of the two scattering amplitudes also produces the interference term 2ReMs​Mt∗​​. The given third term must be this cross term: it has both propagator denominators st, whereas a squared individual Feynman diagram has s2 or t2. With the correct relative fermionic sign, its value is 4e4u2/(st). Thus
∣M∣2​=2e4[s2t2+u2​+t2s2+u2​+st2u2​].​
(30)
The coefficient of the last displayed fraction is positive, but s>0 and t<0 in the physical scattering region, so this interference is destructive. The relative sign originates in the two possible external fermion contractions; changing an external-state phase changes both scattering amplitudes together.[ref]

Both s-channel annihilation and t-channel gluon exchange are now allowed. Their spin sum factors are the Bhabha scattering factors with e↦g. Their color charge tensors, with the external indices of Solution, are
cs​=(Ta)ji​(Ta)kl​,ct​=(Ta)ki​(Ta)jl​.
(31)
Each squared term has Kss​=Ktt​=(Nc2​−1)/(4Nc2​). The cross term instead has the color average
Kst​=Nc2​1​∑a,b​tr(TaTbTaTb).
(32)
To evaluate it, use the SU(N) fundamental completeness relation
∑a​(Ta)ij​(Ta)kl​=21​(δil​δjk​−Nc​1​δij​δkl​).
(33)
This follows by completing the orthogonal basis of traceless Hermitian matrices with the identity. Since trTb=0, it gives ∑a​TaTbTa=−Tb/(2Nc​), and hence
Kst​=−4Nc3​Nc2​−1​=−Nc​1​Kss​.​
(34)
Multiplying the two squared Bhabha scattering terms by Kss​ and its cross term by Kst​ gives
∣M∣2​=g42Nc2​Nc2​−1​[s2t2+u2​+t2s2+u2​−Nc​2​stu2​].​
(35)
For the SU(3) gauge group, this is
∣M∣2​=94g4​[s2t2+u2​+t2s2+u2​]−278g4​stu2​.​
(36)
The negative color charge overlap reverses the sign of the QED interference. Since st<0, this cross contribution is positive in the physical region. No additional spinor calculation is needed; all the new information is in the SU(N) fundamental completeness relation.

The Goldstone theorem states that spontaneous breaking of a continuous internal global symmetry in field theory in a local relativistic quantum field theory produces a zero-mass Goldstone boson for each independent broken generator, under the usual positive-state-space and conserved-current hypotheses. For G→H there are dimG−dimH symmetry-required Goldstone bosons. This count assumes a Lorentz-invariant vacuum state; nonrelativistic counting can differ.
At the classical level, invariance of the scalar potential gives ∂i​V(Taϕ)i​=0. Differentiating at a stationary vacuum expectation value ϕ0​ yields
(M2)ij​(Taϕ0​)j​=0,(M2)ij​=∂i​∂j​V∣ϕ0​​.​
(37)
These are the Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix. Broken generators have Taϕ0​=0, so their independent directions have zero quadratic mass with a canonical kinetic term. The theorem guarantees these modes; it does not exclude further accidental zero-mass modes. A local gauge symmetry is a redundancy and does not meet the physical global-symmetry hypothesis: the Higgs mechanism can instead supply longitudinal particle polarizations of massive gauge bosons.

Put r2=ϕi​ϕi​. Completing the square in the scalar potential gives
V=4λ​(r2−λμ2​)2−4λμ4​.
(38)
Since λ>0, its global minima form the vacuum manifold
r=v,v=λ​μ​.​
(39)
Here μ denotes the positive square root of the parameter μ2. The origin is a local maximum, not a ground state. Choose ⟨ϕ⟩=(v,0,0). Its stabilizer subgroup is the SO(2) subgroup of rotations in the (2,3) plane, so the stated SO(3) global symmetry in field theory is spontaneously broken to SO(2).
Write ϕ1​=v+h, ϕ2​=π2​, ϕ3​=π3​. The kinetic terms stay canonically normalized, while the scalar mass matrix is
(M2)ij​=(−μ2+λϕ2)δij​+2λϕi​ϕj​,M2​(v,0,0)​=diag(2μ2,0,0).
(40)
Equivalently, the quadratic scalar potential is μ2h2. Therefore the particle masses are
mh​=2​μ,mπ2​​=mπ3​​=0.​
(41)
The two angular Goldstone bosons match dimSO(3)−dimSO(2)=3−1=2. The radial displacement changes r and costs potential energy, giving the massive Higgs mode. These are tree-level masses; the Goldstone theorem protects the angular zero-mass modes in the exact global theory.

For any fixed r≥0, the source contribution obeys cϕ1​≥cr, because c<0 and ϕ1​≤r. Equality occurs only at (ϕ1​,ϕ2​,ϕ3​)=(r,0,0) when r>0. Thus the global ground state lies on the positive first axis. Minimize there:
f(r)=−21​μ2r2+41​λr4+cr,f′(r)=λr3−μ2r+c.
(42)
For 0≤r≤v=μ/λ​, f′(r)<0. For r>v, f′′(r)=3λr2−μ2>0, so f′ increases strictly from c to +∞. There is exactly one root v~>v, and it is the unique global minimum. Therefore
⟨ϕ⟩=(v~,0,0),λv~3−μ2v~+c=0,v~>λ​μ​>0.​
(43)
This argument selects the true ground state even if the full cubic has additional negative stationary roots.
Expand ϕ1​=v~+h, ϕ2​=π2​, ϕ3​=π3​. The source is linear, so it shifts the expansion point but has zero Hessian matrix. The scalar mass matrix is diagonal at the new vacuum expectation value:
mh2​=−μ2+3λv~2,mπ2​2​=mπ3​2​=−μ2+λv~2.
(44)
Eliminating λ with the stationary equation gives
mh2​=2μ2−v~3c​,mπ2​2​=mπ3​2​=−v~c​.​
(45)
All three squared masses are positive. The masses themselves are the positive square roots of these expressions.
The source explicitly reduces the stated SO(3) symmetry to SO(2); the selected vacuum state preserves that remaining SO(2). There is consequently no spontaneously broken exact continuous global symmetry in field theory to which the Goldstone theorem applies. For small ∣c∣, the two angular excitations are pseudo-Goldstone bosons. Their squared masses vanish as c→0−, while v~→μ/λ​ and mh2​→2μ2.

In unitary gauge the quadratic action is that of a Proca field. Use Minkowski metric (+−−−) and add a source Jμ​Aμ. Integration by parts, dropping the boundary term, gives
S[A]=21​∫d4xAμ​KμνAν​,Kμν=gμν(□+M2)−∂μ∂ν.
(46)
With Fourier transform convention A(x)=∫d4ke−ik⋅xA(k)/(2π)4, its kernel is
Kμν(k)=(M2−k2)gμν+kμkν.
(47)
For k2=0, introduce the transverse and longitudinal momentum projectors
PTμν​=gμν−k2kμkν​,PLμν​=k2kμkν​.
(48)
They obey PT​PL​=0 and PT​+PL​=1 as mixed-index linear operators. Hence
K=(M2−k2)PT​+M2PL​,K−1=M2−k2PT​​+M2PL​​=−k2−M2g−kk/M2​.
(49)
The final expression is regular at k2=0; the apparent singularities of the individual projectors have cancelled.
The normalized Gaussian functional integral is evaluated by completing the square with A↦A+K−1J:
Z[0]Z[J]​=∫DAexp{i21​AKA}∫DAexp{i(21​AKA+JA)}​=exp[−2i​∫(2π)4d4k​Jμ​(−k)(K−1)μν(k)Jν​(k)].
(50)
The source-independent functional determinant cancels in this ratio. Two source derivatives insert two fields, so the covariant Feynman propagator is iK−1, with the Feynman i-epsilon prescription at the physical pole:
Dμν​(k)=k2−M2+i0−i​(gμν​−M2kμ​kν​​),Dμν​(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4k​e−ik⋅(x−y)Dμν​(k).​
(51)
This is the Proca propagator. Its pole residue has the three physical particle polarizations of a massive spin-one gauge boson. No additional gauge-fixing term is needed to invert this already gauge-fixed massive quadratic action. In a full interacting Higgs mechanism calculation, any field-dependent unitary gauge measure belongs to the interacting theory; the free quadratic gauge boson propagator above is unaffected.
