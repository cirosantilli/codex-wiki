Use the Feynman parameters and J3​ defined in Solution. With A=4C, the finite term just found is −CΔJ3​. Combining it with the remaining gμν​ coefficient gives
C[−Δ+m2−s/2+sxy]J3​=−2sC​(1−4xy)J3​.
(15)
The other coefficient is (B−4Cxy)J3​. Thus, writing Iμν​ for the Feynman integral without the overall prefactor,
Iμν​=2∫T​dxdyJ3​(Δ)[−2sC​(1−4xy)gμν​+(B−4Cxy)p2,μ​p1,ν​].
(16)
For Tμν​=2s​gμν​−p2,μ​p1,ν​, the two coefficients must have the corresponding ratio. The mismatch is simply (B−C)J3​, independent of xy. Therefore the required relation is
B=C,​
(17)
which is satisfied. In the primitive normalization the result is
Iμν​=16π2i​∫01​dx∫01−x​dym2−sxy−i01−4xy​Tμν​,
(18)
and the full top quark scattering amplitude, in our stated S convention, is
M=−2π2Nc​ye2Q2m​ϵ1μ​ϵ2ν​Tμν​∫01​dx∫01−x​dym2−sxy−i01−4xy​.​
(19)
Indeed p1μ​Tμν​=p2ν​Tμν​=0: replacing either polarization vector by its photon momentum makes the scattering amplitude vanish. This is the Ward identity, expressing electromagnetic gauge invariance. The result also is symmetric under exchange of the two photons. The finite term in Solution is essential for this Ward identity.
As an independent low-energy check, for s/m2→0 the Feynman parameter integral is
m21​∫T​(1−4xy)dxdy+O(s/m4)=3m21​+O(s/m4).
(20)
The coefficient is consequently −Nc​ye2Q2/(6π2m) at leading order. It has the inverse-mass dimension required for an effective hFμν​Fμν coupling. If y=m/v, this coefficient stays finite in the large-m limit at fixed v; that limit increases the Yukawa coupling, so it is not a fixed-coupling heavy-field decoupling limit.
