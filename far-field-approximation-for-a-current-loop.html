Far-field approximation for a current loop (B≃μ0​[3(m⋅r)r−m]/(4πr3))

OurBigBook.com Words: 167
Far outside a localized steady source of size a, at distance r≫a, its multipole expansion starts with the magnetic dipole moment if that moment is nonzero. In vacuum, using SI units,
Adip​(r)=4πμ0​​r3m×r​,Bdip​(r)=4πr3μ0​​[3(m⋅r)r−m],
(237)
where μ0​ is the vacuum permeability, r=r/r, and the magnetic field is the curl of the magnetic vector potential. The magnetic dipole field falls as r−3, and the magnetic vector potential as r−2. The localized steady-current moment identities remove the putative r−1 term in the magnetic vector potential. No magnetic monopole is needed. If different current loops cancel the total magnetic dipole moment, a higher term of the multipole expansion supplies the leading distant magnetic field. [ref].
The approximation is not valid at the center of a finite current loop. For a circular current loop of radius R and electric current I, the exact on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop and the distant approximation are
Bz​(z)=2(R2+z2)3/2μ0​IR2​,Bz,dip​(z)=2∣z∣3μ0​IR2​.
(238)
Their ratio is Bz​/Bz,dip​=(1+R2/z2)−3/2: at ∣z∣=10R, the approximate magnetic field exceeds the exact magnetic field by about 1.50% relative to the exact value. Near the source, retain its geometry rather than replacing it with a point source specified only by its magnetic dipole moment.
