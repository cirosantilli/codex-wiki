Equality in data processing inequality (I(X;Z)=I(X;Y)⟺I(X;Y∣Z)=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Mutual information Data processing inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
Under the finite Markov chain hypothesis X→Y→Z, the gap identity in the data processing inequality gives
I(X;Z)=I(X;Y)⟺I(X;Y∣Z)=0.
(41)
By the zero criterion for conditional mutual information, equality means that X and Y have conditional independence given Z. After seeing the processed output, the original observation provides no additional information about X. Similarly, equality in the other bound, I(X;Z)=I(Y;Z), holds exactly when I(Y;Z∣X)=0.
Equality does not require retaining every detail of Y. Let X,W be independent fair bits, put Y=(X,W), and let the processed output be Z=X, the first coordinate of Y. This is a Markov chain, and
I(X;Y)=H(X)−H(X∣X,W)=1−0=1,I(X;Z)=I(X;X)=1.
(42)
Also I(X;Y∣Z)=H(X∣X)−H(X∣X,W)=0. The output discards the entire unrelated bit W, reducing H(Y)=2 to H(Z)=1, while preserving all mutual information about X. This is what makes equality useful for choosing a compact observation that retains all information about the quantity of interest.
