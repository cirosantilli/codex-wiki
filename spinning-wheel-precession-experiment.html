Spinning-wheel precession experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Momentum Angular momentum Gyroscope
OurBigBook.com Words: 472
Purdue's Bicycle Wheel Gyroscope demonstration uses a bicycle wheel on a long axle, with a ball at one end seated in a freely rotating socket on a heavy stand. Spin the wheel by hand or with the supplied motor, seat the ball, and release the nearly horizontal axle. The readout is the axle sweeping around the vertical while the wheel continues spinning. Compare with the stopped wheel, which falls when one end is released; reverse the spin with the same support and the precession reverses. These are the page's documented apparatus, presentation, and reversal checks. [ref]
An alternative in Physics Demonstrations by Julien Clinton Sprott (section 1.3, Materials and Procedure) suspends one axle end by stainless steel wire and a fishing-line swivel. The swivel allows precession without winding up the suspension. Sprott describes the axle bobbing as nutation and precession becoming faster as spin decreases. A suspended support can move, whereas the calculation here assumes a fixed O; use the socket version for the closest realization. [ref]
A proposed quantitative extension is to record an overhead view of an axle marker against a fixed angle scale, together with a side view for θ(t). Time successive overhead passages to estimate Ω=2π/T; count passages of a rim mark to estimate ∣ω3​∣=2πf. Record the spin sense too. Measure M and the centre of mass offset d of the whole moving assembly, and determine its axial moment of inertia I3​ independently. Wheel, axle, support, release angle, and observation interval should stay the same between spin trials. Measuring both Ω and θ(t) distinguishes azimuthal precession from nutation; an overhead movie alone can miss the latter. These camera readouts and parameter checks are proposed here, not reported measurements from the demonstration pages.
For slow precession, compare ΩI3​ω3​ with Mgd, and test whether reversing ω3​ reverses Ω with nearly unchanged magnitude. A brief interval minimizes spin loss from friction; repeat at several starting spin rates rather than interpreting an entire slowing run as one steady state. A suspension restoring torque governed by its torsion constant, socket friction, support motion, a misplaced centre of mass, and a push at release can all supply extra torque or change the initial conditions. The swivel reduces the twist that activates the suspension's torsion constant; it does not demonstrate zero torque.
Steady precession keeps θ constant; nutation changes θ while the axle may also precess. Sudden release generally excites both. A small initial drop is consistent with conservation of energy and conserved vertical angular momentum: some gravitational potential energy supplies the motion around the vertical, and the axial contribution to vertical angular momentum adjusts. Bearing friction may damp the bobbing, but an ideal undamped heavy symmetric top need not settle into steady precession. Chapter 20: Rotation in space explicitly distinguishes uniform precession from release transients and nutation (section 20-3, paragraphs following equation 20.15).[ref]
