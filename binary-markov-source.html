Binary Markov source

Take the alphabet {0,1}, fair initial bit X1​, and transition matrix
P=(1−qq​q1−q​),0≤q≤1.
(166)
The stationary distribution is (1/2,1/2). Every emitted symbol is a fair bit, while the next symbol flips the previous one with probability q. For q<1/2, repeating the previous symbol is the better one-step prediction; for q>1/2, predicting its opposite is better. The resulting minimum next-symbol error probability is min(q,1−q), compared with 1/2 when no history is used.
Given either state, the next-symbol information entropy is h2​(q). Thus
H(Xn​)=1,H(Xn​∣X1​,…,Xn−1​)=h2​(q)(n≥2),h(X)=h2​(q).​
(167)
At q=1/8, the best next-symbol prediction succeeds with probability 7/8, and the entropy rate is about 0.54356444 bits per symbol. The adjacent symbols share mutual information I(X1​;X2​)=1−h2​(q)≃0.45643556 bits. This is useful structure even though the single-symbol counts reveal no bias.
At q=0, the random initial bit is repeated forever; at q=1, it alternates forever. Both have H(X1​,…,Xn​)=1 and zero entropy rate. At q=1/2, the symbols are independent and identically distributed fair bits, giving entropy rate one. Zero rate therefore need not mean that the initial choice was deterministic.
