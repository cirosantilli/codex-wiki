Dense subspace (D=X)

Words: 46
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A dense subspace of a topological vector space X is a linear subspace D whose closure is all of X. It permits continuous operators to be determined or extended from values on D. For a normed space, every vector in X is a limit of vectors from D.

Sequential continuity criterion in a metrizable vector space

Words: 66
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A linear map from a metrizable space carrying a vector-space topology is continuous if it takes every null sequence to a null sequence. If continuity fails, choose a point in each shrinking basic neighborhood whose image stays outside a fixed neighborhood of zero. These points converge to zero but their images do not. For a Fréchet space described by increasing seminorms pj​, a discontinuous functional admits pj​(fj​)≤1/j and ∣u(fj​)∣≥1.

Bounded set in a topological vector space

Words: 62
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A subset B of a topological vector space is bounded when every neighborhood U of zero absorbs it: B⊆tU for all sufficiently large positive t. In a locally convex space defined by seminorms pλ​, this is equivalent to supx∈B​pλ​(x)<∞ for every λ. In particular, a bounded subset of the Schwartz space has a uniform bound for every Schwartz seminorm; it need not have uniformly bounded supports.

Strict inductive limit topology

Words: 40
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A strict inductive limit topology on an increasing union E=⋃n​En​ is the finest locally convex topology compatible with the inclusions of the stages En​. A linear map from E is continuous exactly when each restriction to En​ is continuous.

Metrizable topological vector space

Words: 33
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A topological vector space is metrizable when its topology is induced by a metric. At the origin it then has a countable neighborhood basis, so continuity of a linear map can be tested on sequences.

Finite-dimensional vector-space topology

Words: 125 Articles: 2
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
Every finite-dimensional real vector space has a unique Hausdorff topology making it a topological vector space. Choosing a basis identifies it with ordinary Euclidean space; every linear map between such spaces is continuous.

Equivalence of norms in finite dimensions

Words: 92 Articles: 1
Finite-dimensional vector-space topology OurBigBook.com
On a finite-dimensional vector space over a complete valued field, any two norms induce the same topology. Choosing a basis reduces the claim to comparison with the maximum of the absolute values of the coordinates.

Locally convex space

Words: 850 Articles: 20
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A locally convex space is a topological vector space whose topology is generated by a family of seminorms. Its continuous dual consists of the linear functionals bounded by a finite maximum of those seminorms.

Compact metrizable convex set

Words: 348 Articles: 6
Locally convex space OurBigBook.com
Here the set is a nonempty compact metrizable convex subset of a Hausdorff locally convex space. The ambient space can be infinite-dimensional. Continuous affine functions separate its points, and weak-star compact dual balls with separable preduals provide examples. This general setting is needed for Choquet theorem.

Choquet theorem

Words: 142 Articles: 2
Compact metrizable convex set OurBigBook.com
Every point of a compact metrizable convex subset of a Hausdorff locally convex space is the barycenter of a Borel probability measure concentrated on its extreme points. The theorem is an existence assertion; uniqueness requires additional hypotheses such as a simplex structure.
Threshold Choquet representation in L-infinity (Ht​(s)=21{t≤(1+f(s))/2}​−1)

Choquet theorem OurBigBook.com
For a real function in the L∞ unit ball, push uniform measure on t∈[0,1] forward by the displayed sign-valued functions. In the weak-star topology this is a Borel probability concentrated on the extreme points, and Fubini's theorem shows that its barycenter is f. Weak-star compactness and separability of the predual L1 place the example within Choquet theorem.
Choquet's theorem by strict convexity
Words: 56
Choquet theorem OurBigBook.com
Maximize the integral of a continuous strictly convex function among probability measures with a fixed barycenter. The supporting measure lemma for affine upper envelopes implies that the maximizing measure has zero integral of the nonnegative envelope gap. Strict convexity makes this gap positive at every nonextreme point, so the measure is concentrated on the extreme boundary. Metrizability makes that boundary Borel and supplies the continuous strictly convex function.

Affine upper envelope (f​(x)=inf{a(x):a∈A(K), a≥f})

Words: 105 Articles: 1
Compact metrizable convex set OurBigBook.com
For a bounded real function on a compact convex set, this envelope is the infimum of its continuous affine majorants. It is finite, concave and upper semicontinuous, and dominates the function. For continuous functions it describes the maximal integral among probability measures with a specified barycenter. Its homogeneity, subadditivity and affine-translation identities give the supporting measure lemma for affine upper envelopes.
Supporting measure lemma for affine upper envelopes (ν(f)=μ(f​),ν(g)≤μ(g​) ∀g∈C(K))
Words: 55
Affine upper envelope OurBigBook.com
The sublinear functional p(g)=μ(g​) has a linear supporting functional taking the value p(f) at a specified continuous f, by the Hahn-Banach theorem. It is positive and normalized because p has these values on constants. The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem gives the probability ν. Testing affine functions and their negatives shows that μ and ν have the same barycenter.

Barycenter (a(bμ​)=∫adμ(a continuous affine))

Words: 57
Compact metrizable convex set OurBigBook.com
The barycenter of a probability measure on a compact convex set is the point satisfying the displayed affine integral identities. It exists by approximating the measure by finitely supported measures and using compactness of their finite convex combinations. It is unique because continuous affine functions separate points. In a Banach-space setting it agrees with the appropriate vector integral when that integral is defined.

Minkowski functional

For an absorbing convex set U containing zero, its Minkowski functional is pU​(x)=inf{t>0:x∈tU}. If U is open and convex, then pU​ is sublinear and U={x:pU​(x)<1}.

Norm from a bounded symmetric convex neighbourhood (pU​(x)=inf{t>0:x∈tU})

Words: 50
Minkowski functional OurBigBook.com
For a bounded convex open set U containing zero and invariant under x↦−x, its Minkowski functional is a norm. Inner and outer Euclidean balls give positive finite bounds; symmetry and scaling give absolute homogeneity; convexity gives the triangle inequality. Openness makes U={pU​<1}, while boundedness prevents nonzero vectors from having zero gauge.

Dual pair

Words: 174 Articles: 3
Locally convex space OurBigBook.com
A dual pair (E,F) consists of a real vector space E and a vector space F of linear functionals that separates the points of E. Its weak topology σ(E,F) is the coarsest topology making every f∈F continuous, and its continuous dual is exactly F.

Continuous dual of a weak topology ((E,σ(E,F))′=F)

Words: 55
Dual pair OurBigBook.com
For a separated dual pair, the continuous linear functionals for its weak topology are exactly evaluations by members of the paired space. Continuity bounds a functional on a neighbourhood defined by finitely many evaluations; scalar rescaling makes it vanish on their common kernel. It then factors through their finite-dimensional evaluation map and is a finite linear combination of them.

Compact norming dual-pair criterion

Words: 55
Dual pair OurBigBook.com
Let Z⊆X∗ separate points of a Banach space X. If the closed unit ball of X is compact for σ(X,Z) and Z has the norm inherited from X∗, then the evaluation map X→Z∗ is an isometric isomorphism. Goldstine theorem makes its unit-ball image weak-star dense, while the assumed compactness makes that image closed.

Continuous linear operator

Words: 1k Articles: 24
Topological vector space OurBigBook.com
A continuous linear map is a linear map that is continuous for the topologies on its domain and codomain. Between normed vector spaces, continuity is equivalent to boundedness.

Completely continuous operator (xn​⇀0⟹∥Txn​∥→0)

Words: 73
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator between Banach spaces is completely continuous when it maps weakly convergent sequences to norm-convergent sequences. Equivalently, it maps weakly compact sets to norm-compact sets: the Eberlein-Šmulian theorem gives the forward direction, while a weakly convergent sequence together with its limit is weakly compact and gives the reverse direction. The inclusion C(T)→L2(T) for finite Lebesgue measure is completely continuous by pointwise convergence, the Uniform boundedness principle and the dominated convergence theorem, but is not a compact operator because of the orthogonal Fourier modes.

Approximate surjectivity with geometric correction (Tx=y,∥x∥≤R∥y∥/(1−k))

Words: 76
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
Let T:V→W be a bounded linear operator with V a Banach space. If every y∈W has an approximate preimage x with ∥x∥≤R∥y∥ and ∥Tx−y∥≤k∥y∥, where k<1, then every y has an exact preimage with the displayed norm bound. Repeatedly correct the residual: the corrections have norms at most Rkj∥y∥ and form an absolutely convergent series; its image under T equals y. This proves surjectivity but does not provide a bounded linear right inverse.

2-summing operator (π2​(T))

Words: 142 Articles: 1
Absolutely p-summing operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator T:E→F is 2-summing if ∑j​∥Txj​∥2≤C2supϕ∈BE∗​​∑j​∣ϕ(xj​)∣2 for every finite family; π2​(T) is the least C. For operators between Hilbert spaces, this is equivalent to being a Hilbert-Schmidt operator, with π2​(T)=∥T∥HS​. The upper bound follows by applying the Hilbert-Schmidt norm ideal inequality to the column operator of the family; testing finite orthonormal families gives the reverse bound.
2-summing norm of a finite-dimensional identity (π2​(IE​)=dimE​)

For a finite-dimensional normed vector space E, represent a finite family by its column operator A:ℓ2m​→E. Its operator norm is the family's weak square norm. Since A=AQ for the orthogonal projection onto (kerA)⊥, summing ∥Aej​∥2≤∥A∥2∥Qej​∥22​ proves the upper bound dimE​. In coordinates adapted to a maximal inscribed Euclidean or Hermitian ellipsoid, the John contact decomposition gives ∑r​cr​ur​ur∗​=I and ∑r​cr​=dimE. The vectors cr​​ur​ have total squared norm dimE and weak square norm one, giving equality. This holds over both real and complex scalars.

Pietsch factorization theorem

Words: 105
Absolutely p-summing operator OurBigBook.com
For an absolutely p-summing operator T:E→F, there is a probability measure μ on the dual unit ball with its weak-star topology such that ∥Tx∥≤πp​(T)(∫∣ϕ(x)∣pdμ(ϕ))1/p. Thus T factors through the closed span of the evaluation functions in Lp(μ), with the final operator having norm at most πp​(T). Conversely this factorization implies the summing inequality by summing under the integral. Existence of μ follows from finite-dimensional Hahn-Banach separation theorem: failure for finitely many vectors would contradict the summing inequality for a nonnegative weighted family. Compactness of probability measures then combines the finite constraints. The final operator need only be defined on the indicated closed subspace, not on all of Lp(μ).

Absolutely summing operator (π1​(T))

Words: 37
Absolutely p-summing operator OurBigBook.com
An absolutely summing operator is an absolutely p-summing operator with p=1. The identity on the infinite-dimensional l2 sequence space is not absolutely summing: the first n coordinate vectors have sum of norms n, while their weak 1-norm is n​.

Strictly singular operator

Words: 66
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator is strictly singular if it is bounded below on no infinite-dimensional subspace. Equivalently, every infinite-dimensional subspace contains unit vectors with arbitrarily small images. On a hereditarily indecomposable Banach space, an operator which is arbitrarily small on unit vectors in every finite-codimensional subspace is strictly singular: construct an infinite basic span on which its operator norm is small, then use the zero-angle property of hereditary indecomposability.

Positivity-preserving linear operator on L1 (u≥0⟹Tu≥0)

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator T:L1(Ω)→L1(Ω) preserves positivity when it takes nonnegative functions to nonnegative functions. By linearity it also preserves the pointwise order: u≤v implies Tu≤Tv. For example, integration against a nonnegative kernel preserves positivity, as does the identity operator. This is order positivity, which differs from a positive quadratic form on a Hilbert space.

Mean control for positive imaging operators (uΩ​∥T1∥1​≤∥Tu∥1​+CΩ​∥T∥TV(u))

On a bounded connected Lipschitz domain, let u≥0 belong to the BV space, and let T be a positivity-preserving operator with T1=0. Writing u=c1+(u−c1), c=uΩ​≥0, the triangle inequality and Poincaré inequality for total variation give c∥T1∥1​≤∥Tu∥1​+∥T∥∥u−c∥1​≤∥Tu∥1​+CΩ​∥T∥TV(u). Thus forward-image and variation bounds control the missing constant mode, and hence the full BV norm.

Schur test (∥A∥ℓ2→ℓ2​≤RC​)

Words: 58
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
For a matrix with nonnegative entries, row sums at most R and column sums at most C imply an ℓ2 operator norm at most RC​. Indeed the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives ∣∑j​Aij​xj​∣2≤(∑j​Aij​)∑j​Aij​∣xj​∣2. Summing over i bounds the result by RC∑j​∣xj​∣2. Integral kernels and positive weight functions give analogous forms. If a linear operator on real continuous functions preserves every zero, evaluate f−f(x)1 at x. Contractive linear operator (∥T∥≤1)

Words: 32
Continuous linear operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator between normed spaces is contractive if its operator norm is at most one. This norm property is distinct from a strict contraction mapping, whose Lipschitz constant must be less than one.

A continuous linear functional is a continuous linear map from a topological vector space to its scalar field. The collection of all such functionals is the continuous dual space.

Linear functional with closed kernel (l continuous⟺kerl closed)

Words: 64
Continuous linear functional OurBigBook.com
A linear functional on a normed vector space is continuous exactly when its kernel is closed. The forward direction follows because zero is closed in the scalar field. Conversely, if l=0, choose v with l(v)=1 and write M=kerl. Closedness gives d=dist(v,M)>0. Since x/l(x)−v∈M when l(x)=0, ∣l(x)∣≤∥x∥/d. The zero functional is continuous separately. Thus the criterion does not require completeness or the closed graph theorem.

Sequential continuity criterion for a linear map on a metrizable topological vector space

Words: 41

A linear map from a metrizable topological vector space is continuous exactly when it maps every sequence converging to zero to a sequence converging to zero. If continuity fails, a decreasing countable neighborhood basis supplies a null sequence whose images stay outside one fixed neighborhood of zero.
