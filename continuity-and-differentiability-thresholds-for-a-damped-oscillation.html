Continuity and differentiability thresholds for a damped oscillation (Fa​(x)=∣x∣ah(x),Fa​(0)=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differentiable function
OurBigBook.com Words: 151
Suppose h is bounded by one in absolute value on a punctured neighbourhood of zero. Suppose also that there are positive sequences pn​,qn​→0 with h(pn​)=1 and h(qn​)=0. Extend Fa​(x)=∣x∣ah(x) by Fa​(0)=0. Then
Fa​ is continuous at zero⟺a>0,Fa​ is differentiable at zero⟺a>1.
(80)
When the derivative exists, it equals zero. For continuity, the bound ∣Fa​(x)∣≤∣x∣a gives the epsilon-delta definition of continuity with δ=ε1/a when a>0. If a≤0, the values Fa​(pn​)=pna​ remain at least one for sufficiently large n, contradicting continuity at zero.
For differentiability, the difference quotient is bounded in absolute value by ∣x∣a−1, so it tends to zero when a>1. Conversely its values along qn​ are zero, forcing any possible derivative to be zero. Its values along pn​ equal pna−1​ and fail to tend to zero when a≤1.
For example, h(x)=sin(πsin(1/x)) has the required positive sequences pn​=1/(2πn+π/6) and qn​=1/(2πn). This separates the suppression needed for continuity from the stronger suppression needed for differentiability.
