Numerical analysis

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 66k Articles: 706
Numerical analysis designs algorithms that approximate mathematical answers while controlling cost and error. Its central question is how much of a computed answer can be trusted. Finding a zero of a function, evaluating an integral, solving a linear system, and evolving an ordinary differential equation all demand choices about accuracy, work, and the information available. MIT's numerical-methods course explicitly treats performance and accuracy as joint concerns.[ref]
Three questions organize error analysis. Truncation error measures the approximation introduced by an algorithm even in exact arithmetic; roundoff error comes from finite precision; the condition number measures sensitivity inherent in the problem. Numerical stability asks how the chosen algorithm propagates perturbations. These distinctions suggest different remedies: refine an approximation, change precision or arithmetic, or improve the accuracy of the input.
For example, the square root s(a)=a​ has relative condition number ∣as′(a)/s(a)∣=1/2 for a>0: a small relative change in a produces about half that relative change in s(a). In contrast, g(a)=1/(1−a) has relative condition number ∣a/(1−a)∣. Changing a=0.99 to 0.9901 changes g(a)=100 to about 101.010101: an input change of about 0.0101% produces an output change of about 1.0101%. This sensitivity exists even with exact arithmetic. MIT's conditioning lecture separates the condition number from roundoff error in precisely this way.[ref]
