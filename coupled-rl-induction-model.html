Coupled RL induction model (Li˙+Ri=v)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Maxwell equations Faraday's law of induction Faraday induction-ring experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 521 Articles: 3
Take two electrical circuits containing coupled inductors. Let their constant self-inductances be L1​,L2​>0, mutual inductance M, and positive electrical resistances R1​,R2​. Choose current directions so that the off-diagonal entries of the inductance matrix are +M. Assume a linear, quasistatic model, with no capacitive effects or core hysteresis. The magnetic flux linkages are λ1​=L1​i1​+Mi2​ and λ2​=Mi1​+L2​i2​. Combining their time derivatives with resistive electric potential differences gives
L1​i˙1​+Mi˙2​+R1​i1​=V(t),Mi˙1​+L2​i˙2​+R2​i2​=0.
(15)
The secondary is a closed, undriven electrical circuit. Require M2<L1​L2​, making L a positive-definite matrix. The chosen current convention fixes the signs; reversing the secondary's reference direction changes the sign assigned to M and i2​ without changing the physical circuit.
There is an essential switching assumption: the primary loop remains closed when V becomes zero. An ideal voltage source then imposes zero electric potential difference, allowing the current to decay. Literally opening an inductive circuit requires additional physics to determine the interruption, including possible capacitance and an electric arc. This closed-return model explains transient induction while keeping that distinction from Faraday's historical making and breaking of contact explicit.
