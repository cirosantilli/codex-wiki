Greedy Egyptian fraction expansion

 ... Area of mathematics Number theory Rational number Fraction Unit fraction Egyptian fraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
For a positive proper residual fraction n/d, with 0<n<d, choose q=⌈d/n⌉, append the unit fraction 1/q, and replace the residual by (nq−d)/(dq) in lowest terms. This is a greedy algorithm: the chosen unit fraction is the largest one not exceeding the residual.
The inequalities q−1<d/n≤q imply 0≤nq−d<n. Thus the numerator of the reduced residual strictly decreases while positive, proving algorithm termination by a nonnegative-integer loop variant. The loop invariant is that the accumulated sum plus the residual is the original rational number. Since q≥2 and n/d<1/(q−1), the new residual is less than 1/(q(q−1))≤1/q. If it remains positive, its next chosen denominator is therefore strictly greater than q; this expansion also satisfies the distinct-denominator convention. The proof does not bound the denominators by the input's fixed-width range. Arbitrary-precision arithmetic, checked conversion, or a different representable expansion can therefore be necessary in code.
