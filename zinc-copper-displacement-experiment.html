Zinc-copper displacement experiment

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Redox
OurBigBook.com Words: 303
An inspected university demonstration makes the paired changes visible. The 2021 Chemical Demonstrations Booklet hosted by Edith Cowan University describes two cut square zinc strips, each immersed in a separate glass beaker. One contains blue copper(II) sulfate chemical solution, the other colourless zinc sulfate chemical solution. The published readouts are the appearance of a brown copper deposit on the first strip and fading of its blue chemical solution; after thirty minutes more deposit is reported. The comparison strip in zinc sulfate shows no visible change. The documented preparation is cutting the strips; surface polishing is not specified.[ref]
The reported interpretation is conversion of dissolved Cu2+ to metallic copper, accompanied by conversion of metallic zinc to dissolved Zn2+. The sulfate anions balance bulk electric charge and cancel from the net chemical equation. The comparison shows that merely immersing a zinc strip in a sulfate chemical solution does not reproduce the visible coating.[ref]
Appearance is evidence for the proposed conversion, rather than a quantitative measurement of the two-electron ratio. Fading alone could also reflect dilution; deposit colour alone does not independently establish its composition. A stronger follow-up would compare the copper content of the chemical solution before and after, identify the solid, and check dissolved zinc. These are proposed analytical checks, not results reported in the booklet. Comparable starting chemical solutions, surface condition, immersion and observation time help interpret a comparison; an unchanged-looking strip does not rule out slow or invisible chemical reactions.
The two-electron interpretation comes from the species identities and electric charge balance, supported by the paired observations. It does not imply that the coating's mass equals the mass of zinc consumed: the same amount of substance of different chemical elements generally have different molar masses. Nor does it resolve microscopic surface sites or reaction pathways. The same net redox can be organized into separated compartments in a Daniell cell to obtain electrical work.
