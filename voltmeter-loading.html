Voltmeter loading

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric field Electric potential Voltmeter
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
Voltmeter loading is the change in an electric potential difference caused by the electric current drawn by the measuring voltmeter.
For a loaded voltage divider in steady direct current, a voltmeter connected across the lower resistor places its finite input electrical resistance Rm​ in parallel with the lower arm's electrical resistance R2​ and any intended electrical load of electrical resistance RL​. With positive, fixed electrical resistances, an upper resistor of electrical resistance R1​, and an ideal voltage source supplying electric potential difference Vin​, Kirchhoff's current law and Ohm's law give
R1​Vin​−Vm​​=R2​Vm​​+RL​Vm​​+Rm​Vm​​,Rp​=(R2​1​+RL​1​+Rm​1​)−1,Vm​=Vin​R1​+Rp​Rp​​.
(18)
Here Vm​ is the connected circuit's electric potential difference across the voltmeter; omit 1/RL​ if there is no intended electrical load. The parallel connection and need for large input electrical resistance are standard voltmeter operating principles.[ref]
Voltmeter loading is a bias relative to the target electrical circuit with the voltmeter absent and any intended electrical load still present. Voltage divider loading error instead compares the attached-load output with the wholly unloaded output. An accurate readout of Vm​, supported by measurement calibration, can still differ from either target: calibration accuracy concerns the indication of the connected electric potential difference, whereas voltmeter loading changes that electric potential difference itself.
