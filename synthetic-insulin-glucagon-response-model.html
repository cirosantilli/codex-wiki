Synthetic insulin-glucagon response model (τI​I˙=I∞​(g)−I,τH​H˙=H∞​(g)−H)

 Home Biology Physiology Homeostasis
OurBigBook.com Words: 308
This deliberately synthetic differential equation model illustrates response direction and lag. Every plotted number is chosen for teaching; none is a biological measurement or a fitted human parameter. Let externally prescribed, dimensionless g>0 represent a glucose stimulus relative to a reference. Let I and H be normalized insulin and glucagon response variables, with independently chosen time constants τI​=4 and τH​=6 in arbitrary time units:
I∞​(g)=1+g4g4​,H∞​(g)=1+g41​,τI​I˙=I∞​(g)−I,τH​H˙=H∞​(g)−H.
(6)
Here I∞​ rises and H∞​ falls with g. Their targets add to one by construction; the evolving I+H need not equal one because their time constants differ. Start with g=1 and I=H=1/2. Set g=1.6 from t=10 to 40, g=0.65 from t=40 to 70, and g=1 otherwise. Within a constant-input interval beginning at t0​, the exponential decay solution is
I(t)=I∞​+[I(t0​)−I∞​]e−(t−t0​)/τI​,H(t)=H∞​+[H(t0​)−H∞​]e−(t−t0​)/τH​.
(7)
Each response is a continuous function of time even when the imposed stimulus jumps. The chosen starting values stay between zero and one, since each solution is a weighted arithmetic mean of its interval's starting value and target.
For an illustrative downstream comparison, define release tendency R=0.3+0.6H/(1+I) and uptake tendency U=(0.25+0.5I)g, in the same arbitrary flux units. At the reference target, R=U=0.5. At the high-stimulus targets, R≈0.343 and U≈1.094; at the low-stimulus targets, R≈0.742 and U≈0.212. These chosen functions encode greater uptake with insulin and greater release with glucagon, moderated by insulin; they are not established enzyme kinetics. The g trace is imposed externally, so the figure does not demonstrate self-correction of blood glucose. A closed model would also need a conservation of mass equation, nutrient input, finite glycogen stores, and additional tissue interactions. The qualitative restoring loops are shown separately in the insulin circuit.
