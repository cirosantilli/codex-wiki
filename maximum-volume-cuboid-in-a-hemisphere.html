Maximum-volume cuboid in a hemisphere (Vmax​=4a3/(33​))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Sphere Hemisphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
For a closed hemisphere of radius a>0, take a rectangular parallelepiped with a face on the flat base, base half-widths x,y>0 and height z>0. Its curved-boundary constraint is x2+y2+z2=a2 and its volume is 4xyz. A Lagrange multiplier gives x=y=z=a/3​, yielding the displayed maximum. The full edge lengths are 2a/3​,2a/3​,a/3​.
This bound remains valid when arbitrary orientations are allowed. Write a cuboid's half-edge lengths as u1​,u2​,u3​, its center components in its orthonormal edge basis as vi​, and the vertical unit-vector components as ni​. Containment above the base plane requires ∑ni​vi​≥∑∣ni​∣ui​. Hence ∣vj​∣≥uj​ for at least one j: otherwise every contributing term would be strictly smaller than its required bound. Its farthest vertex has squared distance
∑i​ui2​+∑i​vi2​+2∑i​ui​∣vi​∣≥∑i=j​ui2​+4uj2​.
(15)
Containment in the sphere bounds the right side by a2. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality then gives 2u1​u2​u3​≤a3/(33​), so the full volume 8u1​u2​u3​ cannot exceed the displayed value. The constructed cuboid attains it. With strict containment and no boundary contact, the same value is a supremum.
