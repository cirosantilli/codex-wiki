Heat transfer

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat
OurBigBook.com Words: 807 Articles: 14
Heat transfer asks how quickly heat crosses a boundary and how the temperature varies along its path. Its principal mechanisms are thermal conduction, convection and thermal radiation. A warm wall can conduct heat through its interior while exchanging heat with moving air and emitting thermal radiation at its surfaces. Identifying these paths lets us estimate heating requirements and cooling times.
Distinguish the accumulated heat Q from its rate Q˙​=dQ/dt, a power measured in W. The heat flux q is the rate per unit area, measured in Wm−2: a uniform normal heat flux across area A gives Q˙​=Aq. The steady bar in thermal conduction carries q=400Wm−2 through A=0.01m2, hence Q˙​=4W; maintained for 60s, it transfers Q=240J. This distinction makes a surface's intensity and a whole object's heating load easy to compare.
