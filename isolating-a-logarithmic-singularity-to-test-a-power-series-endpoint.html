Isolating a logarithmic singularity to test a power-series endpoint (f(z)=Alog(1−z/R)+H(z))

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Power series Radius of convergence
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
Suppose A=0 and R>0, and H is an analytic function on a disk of radius strictly larger than R. The Taylor series at zero of f(z)=Alog(1−z/R)+H(z) has radius of convergence R. Its coefficients are the sum of −A/(nRn) and the more rapidly decreasing coefficients of H. At x=−R, the logarithmic part is an alternating harmonic series and converges conditionally; at x=R it diverges. The power series for H converges absolutely at both endpoints and therefore cannot change either conclusion. The real convergence interval is [−R,R).
For f(z)=log(2−ez), take R=log2 and H(z)=log[(2−ez)/(1−z/R)], with its branch normalized by H(0)=0. The quotient has nonzero removable value 2R at z=R. Its next zeros are at R±2πi, so a holomorphic logarithm exists on a larger disk. This shows why the Taylor series converges at x=−log2 even though its singular positive endpoint is excluded.
