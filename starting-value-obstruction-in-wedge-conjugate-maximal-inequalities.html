Starting-value obstruction in wedge conjugate maximal inequalities (P(0)=0 does not imply ImP(iα)=0)

 ... Probability theory Martingale Continuous-time martingale Local martingale Continuous local martingale Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities
OurBigBook.com Words: 114
Real and imaginary parts of an analytic Brownian image, after subtracting their starting values, have equal quadratic variation. The Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities compare their centered maximal moments with constants depending only on p. A boundary-point normalization P(0)=0 does not remove the starting imaginary value. For P(z)=iz on a wedge of opening απ<π/2, sup∣Imz∣≤tan(απ)sup∣Rez∣. If αp<1 both relevant moments are finite, so an uncentered comparison requires a constant at least cotp(απ); no bound depending only on p can hold over all such wedges. For fixed α>0, polynomials with finite real-part maximal moment have degree d<1/(αp) by the wedge radial-maximum tail. They form a finite-dimensional vector space, and equivalence of norms in finite dimensions gives a valid constant depending on p,α.
