Noncompactness after collapsing a countable real subset (A⊆R countable⟹R/A not compact)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Quotient topology Collapsing a subset to a point
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
Choose bn​∈(n,n+1/2)∖A, possible because each interval is uncountable. Every tail Kn​={bk​:k≥n} is closed and disjoint from A. Therefore Un​=R∖Kn​ is an open saturated set for collapsing a subset to a point, so its image in the quotient topology is open. These increasing images cover the quotient, but no finite selection covers it, because the largest selected image still misses an infinite tail of distinct singleton classes. This is an open cover with no finite subcover, proving noncompactness. The argument does not require the countable subset to be closed or nondense. For the empty subset, read the quotient as the original line.
