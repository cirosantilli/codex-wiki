Birefringent walk-off (ρ=ϕ−θ)

 ... Maxwell equations Maxwell equations in matter Linear anisotropic dielectric Birefringence Uniaxial optical medium Extraordinary wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
The extraordinary wave's Poynting vector can differ in direction from its wavevector. The latter specifies wave phase propagation, while the former specifies energy flux. This angular separation is walk-off.[ref]
In the lossless, nondispersive model above, take the gradient of ω=ckx2​/ne2​+kz2​/no2​​ to obtain the group velocity, parallel to the Poynting vector. If its angle to the optic axis is ϕ, this gives tanϕ=(no2​/ne2​)tanθ. Consequently the extraordinary wave generally carries energy away from its wavevector direction. An internal wavevector normal to a slab face therefore does not guarantee a normal energy path. Birefringent walk-off vanishes at θ=0 and θ=π/2; the latter direction still has unequal refractive indices. This is a useful waveplate geometry: overlapping components can acquire phase retardation without spatial splitting. A thin specimen, insufficient imaging resolution, or excitation of only one linear polarization can also hide a double image, for different reasons.
