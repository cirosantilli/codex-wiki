Projective measurement (Pi​Pj​=δij​Pi​,∑i​Pi​=I)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Measurement in quantum mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 875 Articles: 11
A projective measurement has self-adjoint orthogonal projections Pi​ satisfying Pi​Pj​=δij​Pi​ and ∑i​Pi​=I. Measuring an observable with discrete spectral decomposition A=∑i​ai​Pi​ reports eigenvalue ai​ with Born rule probability pi​=Tr(Pi​ρ). A projector can have rank greater than one: the outcome then identifies an eigenspace, rather than a particular eigenvector inside it. Projectors specify the outcome statistics; the usual ideal conditional update is the additional Lüders rule.
In the qubit example from measurement in quantum mechanics, Z=∣0⟩⟨0∣−∣1⟩⟨1∣. At θ=π/3, the zero and one probabilities are 3/4 and 1/4, regardless of the relative quantum phase ϕ. Under the Lüders rule, obtaining zero prepares ∣0⟩; obtaining one prepares ∣1⟩, up to global phase. Repeating the same measurement returns the same label with certainty, provided no intervening evolution or operation changes the measured sector. Repeatability concerns the conditional output, and does not mean the first outcome was predetermined.

p±​=∣⟨±∣ψ⟩∣2=21​​cos(θ/2)±eiϕsin(θ/2)​2=21±sinθcosϕ​.

At θ=π/3 and ϕ=0, p+​=(2+3​)/4≃0.9330 and p−​≃0.0670. At ϕ=π/2, both are 1/2; at ϕ=π, the first pair is exchanged. The Z statistics remain 3/4,1/4 throughout. This is the same addition-and-cancellation principle as wave interference: relative quantum phase changes how amplitudes reinforce or cancel in another basis.
The beauty of this basis dependence is that information invisible to one measurement becomes accessible to another. A Z measurement of an ensemble estimates its quantum state populations, while X probes one component of its quantum coherence. These are experiments on separate identically prepared copies. Sequentially measuring Z and then X on one copy instead probes the disturbed post-measurement state. Quantum state tomography combines suitable ensemble statistics to reconstruct the original state.
