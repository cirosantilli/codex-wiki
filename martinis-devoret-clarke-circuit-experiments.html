Martinis-Devoret-Clarke circuit experiments

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity Josephson junction Current-biased Josephson phase dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 491 Articles: 1
The 1985 spectroscopy experiment used niobium-based tunnel Josephson junctions on silicon, thermally anchored with final bias low-pass filters to a dilution refrigerator. Calibrated germanium and cobalt thermometers measured temperature. A filtered coaxial cable injected microwaves; repeated switches to nonzero electric potential difference measured escape with irradiation on and off. Bias scans at fixed microwave frequency resolved separate enhancements matching calculated unequal energy level spacings. Raising temperature merged the structure toward classical response. Junction parameters were calibrated in the classical regime; reducing the superconducting critical current with a magnetic field checked thermal readout. Parameter uncertainties limited absolute resonance positions.[ref]
The companion escape experiment mounted a tunnel Josephson junction on an attenuating coaxial cable, cold-anchored its bias filters, ramped the electric current, and counted switches to nonzero electric potential difference. Rapid current shutoff limited heating. Classical escape and microwave resonance calibrated the superconducting critical current, capacitance and environmental loading. Low-temperature escape agreed with the quantum prediction using these calibrated parameters. A magnetic field reduced the superconducting critical current to keep the same apparatus thermally activated at lower temperature, testing excess noise. Frequency-dependent loading contributed uncertainty; the predicted damping correction was smaller than combined errors, so the experiment did not resolve that correction.[ref]
Together these tests connect spectral structure and escape of the same kind of collective coordinate. They do not constitute a demonstration of coherent gate operations on a qubit, or prove that every macroscopic coordinate retains quantum behavior under arbitrary environmental coupling. The attraction is more concrete: a fabricated electrical circuit admits both an independently measurable classical model and quantitative quantum tests of its phase dynamics.
