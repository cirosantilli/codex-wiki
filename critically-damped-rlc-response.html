Critically damped RLC response (α=ω0​,R=2L/C​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit RLC circuit Series RLC circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
A critically damped RLC response is the boundary between underdamped RLC response and overdamped RLC response. The free series RLC circuit has a repeated characteristic root −ω0​, with general solution
q(t)=(A+Bt)e−ω0​t.
(74)
For initial conditions q(0)=q0​, i(0)=0,
q(t)=q0​(1+ω0​t)e−ω0​t,i(t)=−q0​ω02​te−ω0​t.
(75)
In the plotted normalization, q(1)=2/e≈0.735759 and i(1)=−1/e≈−0.367879. The electric charge returns without ringing; the polynomial factor 1+ω0​t matters, so a single exponential function does not give the whole response.
For fixed L,C and these initial conditions, critical damping avoids ringing while giving a faster asymptotic return than any larger R: the overdamped RLC response has a root closer to zero. This makes critical damping a useful tuning target for settling after switching. An MIT laboratory exercise makes the distinction observable by asking students to compare all three cases with L=47mH and C=47nF. Those values give Rcrit​=2L/C​=2000Ω and ω0​≈21276.6s−1. [ref].
