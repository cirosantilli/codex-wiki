Fizeau experiment path-length convention (L=2ℓ)

OurBigBook.com Words: 371
Use the schematic below to count the water traversals explicitly. Each stationary tube has laboratory length ℓ. In the displayed setting, upper-tube water flows right and lower-tube water flows left, each at speed u>0. Beam A goes right through the upper tube, exchanges tubes at the far optics, and returns left through the lower tube: it follows the flow twice. Beam B goes right through the lower tube and returns left through the upper tube: it opposes the flow twice. Each complete beam path therefore contains total water length L=2ℓ, not ℓ or 4ℓ. The connecting air paths and fixed optics are assumed to contribute no flow-dependent phase difference.
Let v+​ and v−​ be positive phase velocity magnitudes along and against the water flow:
v+​=1+u/(nc)c/n+u​,v−​=1−u/(nc)c/n−u​,Δt=tB​−tA​=L(v−​1​−v+​1​).
(38)
For a laboratory source of frequency ν=c/λ0​, where λ0​ is its vacuum wavelength, define the signed interference fringe offset N by the propagation phase difference Δϕ=2πνΔt=2πN. This is an equivalent phase delay between steady beams; no stopwatch resolves their transit-time difference. Subtract the zero-flow baseline and expand to first order:
Δt≃c22Lu​(n2−1)=c24ℓu​(n2−1),N(u)≃cλ0​2Lu​(n2−1)=cλ0​4ℓu​(n2−1).
(39)
Thus one shifted fringe corresponds to one cycle of phase difference. If the stationary pattern has spatial interference fringe spacing d, a simple readout model is I(x)=I0​[1+Vcos(2πx/d+2πN)], where V is the interference visibility. The maxima shift by −dN with this choice of screen coordinate. A sign assigned to “rightward” motion depends on the image orientation; the magnitude in fringe units does not.
Reversing both flows gives N(−u)=−N(u). The displacement between equal and opposite settings therefore has magnitude
∣N(u)−N(−u)∣=2∣N(u)∣≃cλ0​8ℓ∣u∣​(n2−1).
(40)
The factor 2 from comparing opposite flows is additional to the factor 2 already included in L=2ℓ. Repeating a round trip in just one flowing tube would instead put a beam with the flow once and against it once; that geometry does not produce the two same-sign contributions drawn here.
