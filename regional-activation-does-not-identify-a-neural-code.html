Regional activation does not identify a neural code

 Home Biology Neuroscience Cognitive neuroscience Neural representation
OurBigBook.com Words: 71
Consider two response vectors x=(3,1,3,1) and y=(3,3,1,1) over the same four measured locations, relative to a zero baseline. Both are positive everywhere and have mean two. Their centered vectors xc​=(1,−1,1,−1) and yc​=(1,1,−1,−1) satisfy xc​⋅yc​=0. Hence identical positive regional means can accompany orthogonal patterns. This counterexample shows why overlapping regional activation does not imply a shared neural representation. It does not assert that real neural codes are necessarily orthogonal or that unsuccessful decoding proves their independence.
