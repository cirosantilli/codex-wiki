Machine compliance (Cm​)

 ... Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Linear elasticity Young's modulus Tensile modulus experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 704 Articles: 2
Machine compliance is the extra load-path displacement per applied force attributed to the apparatus in a specified measurement model. Its units are mN−1, the reciprocal units of a spring constant. Here Cm​ includes the frame, load cell, adapters and grips insofar as their deformation contributes to the recorded crosshead displacement x. It is a property of that configuration and readout, rather than a universal constant of the testing machine.
Assume a uniform specimen with initial length L, constant A0​ and Young's modulus E, small axial elastic deformation, quasistatic loading, no slip, and a linear, length-independent apparatus response over the chosen force window. Equilibrium makes the same force act through specimen and apparatus, while their extensions add. The two springs in series therefore give
x=x0​+EA0​FL​+Cm​F,q(L)=dFdx​=EA0​L​+Cm​,kobs​=q(L)1​.
(14)
The offset x0​ describes the displacement origin after seating; the slope q describes total compliance. Fitting an intercept avoids forcing an arbitrary zero through the origin, but cannot repair slip or a curved response.
If all of x−x0​ is incorrectly attributed to specimen extension, the inferred modulus is
Eapp​=A0​q(L)L​=1+EA0​Cm​/LE​.
(15)
For positive Cm​, this is too small. The fractional bias is most serious for a short, thick, stiff specimen because its own compliance L/(EA0​) is small. A local extensometer instead has d(ΔLg​)/dF=Lg​/(EA0​) in this ideal model, excluding deformation outside its contacts.
A shaped specimen requires more care. With varying initial cross-sectional area A(s) and uniform E, its axial compliance is ∫ds/[EA(s)] in the one-dimensional approximation. Shoulder and end deformation must be included explicitly or remain identical between tests and be absorbed into an effective intercept. Changing only the extensometer gauge length on an unchanged specimen does not change its total compliance and cannot perform a two-length machine compliance separation.
