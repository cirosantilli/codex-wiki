Diamond anvil culet (Ac​=πdc2​/4)

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Pressure measurement High-pressure experimental metrology Diamond anvil cell
OurBigBook.com Words: 205
A diamond anvil culet is the small flat contact face at the tip of a diamond anvil. Its diameter dc​ determines its nominal surface area Ac​. The gasket hole has a different surface area Ah​; neither should be confused with the much larger drive mechanism. For an ideal static axial balance, F is the compressive force on either anvil. The two equal opposing loads do not produce 2F through a single contact.
For an original chosen geometry, F=1000N and dc​=0.300mm give
Ac​=7.0686×10−8m2,qˉ​=F/Ac​=14.147GPa.
(34)
Here qˉ​ is the mean axial contact stress over the culet, not a measured chamber pressure. Using a 0.100mm hole instead would give F/Ah​=127.324GPa, nine times larger. That substitution falsely assigns the entire applied force to the hole.
To see the missing load path, idealize the culet as a coplanar hole region plus a surrounding contact annulus. With a hydrostatic chamber pressure p and axial resultant Fg​ carried by the gasket, its force balance is
F=pAh​+Fg​.
(35)
If the stipulated chamber pressure is 5.40GPa, the hole carries only 42.4N and the gasket carries 957.6N. These are model loads, not measurements. Real gasket deformation and anvil elasticity alter the local contact stress; the scalar balance cannot predict p without additional information. This is why internal pressure calibration matters even when applied force is accurately known.
