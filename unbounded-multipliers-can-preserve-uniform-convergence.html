Unbounded multipliers can preserve uniform convergence (g(x)=1/x,0<x≤1/2)

An unbounded function can multiply a series while preserving uniform convergence. On E=(0,1/2], let g(x)=1/x. The geometric series gives
∑n=0∞​xng(x)=x(1−x)1​,supx∈E​​x(1−x)1​−∑n=0N​xn−1​=21−N⟶0.

The equality follows because xN/(1−x) increases up to x=1/2. Both the first term and the sum are unbounded, while their difference after sufficiently many terms is uniformly small. Uniform convergence controls approximation errors; boundedness of each individual function is a separate property.
