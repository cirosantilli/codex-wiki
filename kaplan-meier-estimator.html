Kaplan–Meier estimator (S(t)=∏tj​≤t​(1−dj​/nj​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
At distinct observed event times t1​<t2​<⋯, let nj​ be the risk set size immediately before tj​, and let dj​ count events at tj​. The Kaplan–Meier estimator of the survival function is
S(t)=∏tj​≤t​(1−nj​dj​​).
(19)
The empty product is one. Each factor estimates a conditional survival probability among those still observed and event-free; their product joins the successive steps. This is nonparametric statistics: no specified shape for the survival distribution is imposed. Use independent censoring and the appropriate risk sets to interpret the result as an estimate for the intended population.
The Kaplan–Meier estimator is a right-continuous function with downward jumps at event times. Right censoring changes later denominators without causing a jump. A tick on the curve marks right censoring, rather than a failure. For a tied group of dj​ events, use the one factor (nj​−dj​)/nj​; subjects right-censored at that recorded time remain in nj​ for this factor. The fraction dj​/nj​ estimates a discrete hazard, not a continuous hazard function in inverse-time units. With no censoring, the factors telescope to the fraction of the original cohort whose observed survival times exceed t, the complement of its empirical distribution function.
Edward L. Kaplan and Paul Meier published [ref]. Their paper addresses lifetime estimation when observation ends before failure and explicitly calls attention to the assumption of independence between lifetime and potential observation loss. The date 1958 is the original publication year; the publisher's later online-posting date is not the discovery date.
In his [ref], Kaplan traces Meier's interest to cancer-duration data in 1952 and his own to the lifetimes of vacuum tubes in undersea telephone-cable equipment a year later. The journal encouraged them to combine their manuscripts. The shared mathematical question was how to estimate a survival function when some lifetimes are still running or disappear from observation.
