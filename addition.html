Addition (+)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 13
Addition combines two numbers into their sum. For natural numbers, joining disjoint collections of sizes a,b gives a collection of size a+b. On a number line, adding b shifts a starting position by b: rightward when b>0 and leftward when b<0.
Starting at 2 and adding 3 reaches 5. Starting there and adding −3 returns to 2, because 3+(−3)=0. Additive inverses turn this return move into an algebraic rule. With real numbers, addition is commutative and associative: exchanging two shifts or grouping several shifts differently leaves the final position unchanged.
The bottom panel applies x↦−3x/2. Input 2 becomes −3, and input −2 becomes 3. Multiplication by a negative real number rescales distances from zero and reverses direction, giving a reflection in mathematics as well as a scale change. This is a different operation from shifting every input by one fixed amount.
