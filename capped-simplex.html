Capped simplex

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex set
OurBigBook.com Words: 100
For an integer 0≤k≤n, the capped simplex
Pk​={v∈Rn:0≤vi​≤1, ∑i​vi​=k}
(34)
is the convex hull of the zero-one vectors having exactly k entries equal to one. It is a convex polytope whose extreme points are precisely these vectors.
If a feasible vector has a fractional coordinate, the integer coordinate sum forces at least two fractional coordinates. Perturbing one upward and the other downward by a sufficiently small amount gives two distinct feasible vectors whose midpoint is the original vector. It is therefore not an extreme point. Conversely, a zero-one vector cannot be a nontrivial convex combination of points of [0,1]n, since each coordinate is already at a bound.
