Dual space (V∗=HomF​(V,F))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 29
The dual space of a vector space V over a field F is the vector space of all linear functionals φ:V→F. Think of a vector as an input and a linear functional as a scalar-valued measurement of that input. For V=R2, the rules (x,y)↦x, (x,y)↦y, and (x,y)↦2x−y are different elements of the dual space. None needs a notion of length or angle.
Its operations combine measurements pointwise:
(φ+ψ)(v)=φ(v)+ψ(v),(aφ)(v)=aφ(v).
(1)
The dual basis explains the finite-dimensional structure: specifying a linear functional's values on a basis determines its value on every vector. Thus an n-dimensional vector space has an n-dimensional dual space. The spaces have the same dimension, but their elements play different roles: an input and a rule that measures an input. [ref].
This separation is useful when changing coordinates. The dual pairing φ(v) must stay the same while the component lists for v and φ change in opposite ways. Covector transformation under a change of basis makes the compensation explicit. An inner product on a finite-dimensional real vector space gives one way to represent each linear functional by a vector, but that representation uses the chosen inner product; the dual space itself does not require one.
Here dual space means the algebraic dual: all linear functionals. For a topological vector space, the continuous dual space keeps only the continuous linear functionals. In infinite dimension, this can be a proper vector subspace; discontinuous functional detected by vanishing sequence tails gives an example. The distinction matters when a scalar-valued measurement must respond continuously to small input changes.
