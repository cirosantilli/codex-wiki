Let N be the vector subspace of integrable functions that vanish almost everywhere. The Lebesgue space is the quotient vector space L1(R)=L1(R)/N: its elements are equivalence classes of functions equal almost everywhere. Addition and scalar multiplication are [f]+[g]=[f+g] and c[f]=[cf].
Set ∥[f]∥1​=∫∣f∣. This is well-defined because replacing f by an equal-almost-everywhere representative does not change the integral. It is nonnegative, homogeneous, and obeys the triangle inequality by ∣f+g∣≤∣f∣+∣g∣. Part (i) says its only zero element is the zero class. Hence it is a norm on L1.
