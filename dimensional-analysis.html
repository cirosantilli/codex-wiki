Dimensional analysis

 Home Physics Mathematical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 651 Articles: 9
Dimensional analysis checks the physical dimensions of quantities and identifies combinations unchanged by the choice of units. It catches impossible equations and reveals scaling before a full dynamical calculation. Adding two quantities requires the same physical dimension; replacing metres by centimetres must change numerical values consistently while preserving physical predictions.
For the ideal simple pendulum, suppose its oscillation period T depends only on mass m, length ℓ, gravitational acceleration g, and dimensionless angular amplitude θ0​. Write T=maℓbgcF(θ0​). Since [m]=M, [ℓ]=L, and [g]=LT−2, matching physical dimensions gives a=0, b+c=0, and −2c=1. Consequently
T=gℓ​​F(θ0​).
(8)
The angular amplitude is dimensionless when expressed in radians. Dimensional analysis establishes the length/gravity scaling and the absence of a mass factor under these assumptions, but cannot determine F. The harmonic oscillator equation gives F→2π at small amplitude; the full simple pendulum supplies its finite-amplitude correction. A model with drag or a finite massive support has additional parameters, so the same dimensional argument does not automatically apply.
The result is useful for comparison: at fixed angular amplitude and g, lengths in the ratio 1:4 give periods in the ratio 1:2. The ratio Tg/ℓ​ should be the same for both in this ideal model. A failure to collapse onto the same value points to measurement error or an omitted parameter. Correct physical dimensions are a necessary consistency check, while the coefficient and the adequacy of the model require more information.
