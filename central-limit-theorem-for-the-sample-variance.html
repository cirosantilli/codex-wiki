Central limit theorem for the sample variance (n​(Sn2​−v)⇒N(0,μ4​−v2))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Jackknife bias correction Sample variance
OurBigBook.com Words: 91
Let Xi​ be real independent and identically distributed random variables, with mean m, variance v and finite fourth central moment μ4​=E[(Xi​−m)4]. For the sample variance Sn2​=(n−1)−1∑i​(Xi​−Xn​)2, the displayed convergence in distribution holds.
Indeed n−1∑i​(Xi​−Xn​)2=n−1∑i​(Xi​−m)2−(Xn​−m)2. The central limit theorem for the sample mean implies that n​(Xn​−m)2 tends to zero in probability. Changing the divisor from n to n−1 also has negligible effect at this scale, by the law of large numbers. Apply the central limit theorem to the independent variables (Xi​−m)2, whose variance is μ4​−v2, and use Slutsky's theorem. Standardization gives a standard normal distribution limit when μ4​>v2. The familiar special case 2v2 holds for the normal distribution because then μ4​=3v2.
