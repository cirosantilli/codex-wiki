Lazy plume (Γ>1)

Words: 55
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
A buoyant plume with insufficient momentum for the corresponding pure plume balance. In the constant-entrainment top-hat model it has plume balance parameter Γ>1, unlike the momentum-excess forced plume. The equations accelerate the plume and drive the balance parameter toward one downstream. The definition depends on the chosen flux and entrainment convention; it is not merely a synonym for slow vertical speed.

Filling box model

Words: 307 Articles: 3
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
A filling box model describes a turbulent plume rising through a confined fluid and spreading beneath its ceiling. Fluid entrainment transfers initially unmodified ambient fluid into the plume; the discharged fluid forms a descending filling-box first front. A maintained heat source can have negligible source volume flux while maintaining nonzero buoyancy flux. A finite injected fluid flux requires an equal exhaust if the container volume is fixed.

Finite-source filling-box front with a side opening (Ac​z˙=−πQ(z) above;Ac​z˙=−π[Q(z)−Qs​] below)

Words: 79
Filling box model OurBigBook.com
For a source in pure plume balance, Q(z)=CFs1/3​(z+zv​)5/3 and Qs​=Q(0). A single ideal exhaust passes the source volume flux. Above the exhaust height, it removes unprocessed lower fluid and the filling-box first front descends at rate −πQ/Ac​. Below it, processed fluid leaves and only entrained volume grows the processed region. The floor is reached in finite time if the opening is at the floor; otherwise the final approach is exponential. A finite-height aperture supporting bidirectional exchange requires a different model.

Filling-box first front (h(t))

Words: 170 Articles: 1
Filling box model OurBigBook.com
In a filling box model, the first front separates fluid already modified by plume discharge from the initially unmodified fluid below. If the plume occupies a negligible fraction of horizontal area A, its volume flux across the front is P(h), and the ceiling exhaust is F, volume conservation gives Ah˙=F−P(h). For a closed box with a negligible-volume buoyancy source, F=0. For a finite injected source flux Q with ceiling overflow Q, F=Q. The first front need not delimit a uniformly mixed upper layer throughout the unventilated transient.

Symmetric line-plume filling-box front (h(t)=He−αw∗​t/R)

Words: 81
Filling-box first front OurBigBook.com
An ideal two-sided line plume has half-width b=αz, constant velocity w∗​=(B/(2α))1/3 and volume flux q=2αw∗​z. In a closed box of width 2R, neglecting the plume's occupied area gives 2Rh˙=−q(h) for the filling-box first front, initially at ceiling height H. With negligible diffusion the front is a material interface; its ambient buoyancy jump retains the initial ceiling-discharge value B/q(H)=w∗2​/H. The contemporaneous plume reduced gravity w∗2​/h(t) is a different quantity. The upper ambient generally becomes stratified rather than remaining one uniformly mixed layer.

Forced plume

Words: 129 Articles: 3
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
A forced plume has both imposed momentum flux and positive buoyancy flux. Its near-source region behaves like a jet, while its far field approaches a pure plume. The crossover is measured by a jet length.

Jet length (LJ​)

Words: 25
Forced plume OurBigBook.com
For source kinematic momentum flux m0​ and buoyancy flux f0​>0, dimensional analysis gives LJ​=m03/4​/f01/2​. Under constant Batchelor entrainment, the actual turning length also contains an entrainment coefficient factor of order α−1/2.

Inclined forced plume

Words: 74 Articles: 1
Forced plume OurBigBook.com
An inclined forced plume curves upward under buoyancy. In a stationary homogeneous ambient, its horizontal momentum flux is conserved and its vertical momentum flux increases. For kinematic plume fluxes, mx′​=0 and mz′​=fq/m, where m=mx2​+mz2​​ and the derivative is with respect to centreline arc length.

Horizontal forced-plume trajectory

Words: 38
Inclined forced plume OurBigBook.com
For zero source volume flux, constant Batchelor entrainment, and a horizontal source, the near-source trajectory is cubic: z∼απ​f0​x3/(3m03/2​). Far from the source the centreline approaches a vertical asymptote, with its horizontal distance from that asymptote proportional to z−1/3.

Buoyancy flux (B)

Words: 26
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
The buoyancy flux through a section is the integral of velocity times reduced gravity. In a Boussinesq pure plume in a uniform ambient it is conserved.

Batchelor entrainment hypothesis

Words: 67 Articles: 1
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis takes the inward mean edge velocity of a turbulent plume to be ve​=αw in the Boussinesq approximation. Its density-corrected non-Boussinesq form is ve​=αwρ/ρ0​​, where ρ and ρ0​ are plume and ambient mass densities and α is the entrainment coefficient.

Entrainment coefficient (α)

Words: 30
Batchelor entrainment hypothesis OurBigBook.com
The entrainment coefficient is the dimensionless constant in the Batchelor entrainment hypothesis, so the mean speed at which ambient fluid crosses a plume edge is α times the plume's characteristic axial speed.

Top-hat plume model

Words: 2k Articles: 42
Turbulent plume OurBigBook.com
A top-hat plume model takes velocity and reduced gravity to be uniform across the plume and zero outside it. Integral volume, momentum, and buoyancy fluxes then depend only on plume width and these uniform values.

Confined plume with compensating return flow (Q=b2w,v=−Q/(R2−b2))

Words: 157 Articles: 1
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
In an impermeable cylinder, an axisymmetric turbulent plume with negligible source volume flux requires a downward return flow. With uniform upward plume velocity w and ambient velocity v, volume conservation gives b2w+(R2−b2)v=0. An entrainment coefficient α based on the relative speed gives Q′=2αb(w−v). A specified pressure closure is essential for momentum conservation. If the departure from ambient hydrostatic pressure and wall force are neglected in the whole-section integral, the total specific momentum flux J=b2w2+(R2−b2)v2 obeys J′=b2g′. Homogeneous unmodified ambient and no buoyancy loss give b2wg′=Bs​. These are leading integral-model closures; restoring the ambient dynamic pressure can change the singularity criterion.

Confined-plume momentum fold (b=R/2​,Ma​=M)

Words: 66
Confined plume with compensating return flow OurBigBook.com
For the pressure-neglecting confined plume with compensating return flow, define specific plume momentum flux M=b2w2 and area fraction s=Q2/(R2M). Then J=M/(1−s) and
(1−2s)M′=MBs​Q​(1−s)2−R22Q​Q′.
(1)
The branch issuing from a pure plume reaches a fold at s=1/2, where plume and ambient have equal areas, opposite velocities and equal specific momentum fluxes. The numerator is nonzero on this branch, so M′ diverges although M,Q,w remain finite. This signals failure of the steady uniform-profile description, not infinite physical velocity.

Source-volume length of a turbulent plume (LQjet​=Q0​/(cM0​​),LQplume​=(Q0​/CQ​)3/5)

Words: 57
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
Source volume flux is negligible only when entrained volume greatly exceeds it. In the jet limit of the top-hat plume model, this requires z≫Q0​/(cM0​​). In a pure plume with Q=CQ​z5/3, it requires z≫(Q0​/CQ​)3/5, where CQ​=(3c/5)(9cF0​/20)1/3 for full-cross-section fluxes and c=2αe​π​. These scales differ from the jet length which measures imposed momentum relative to buoyancy. Neglecting source volume does not automatically permit neglecting source momentum.

Jet limit of the top-hat plume model (M=M0​,Q=2αe​πM0​​z,r=2αe​z)

Words: 68
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
With zero source volume flux and zero buoyancy flux but positive source momentum flux, the axisymmetric top-hat plume model reduces to a turbulent round jet. For full-cross-section kinematic fluxes, put c=2αe​π​. Entrainment gives Q′=cM​, while momentum conservation gives M=M0​. Hence Q=cM0​​z, speed w=M0​​/(cz) and radius r=cz/π​. A finite source flux replaces z by z+Q0​/(cM0​​). The ideal zero-flux source has singular speed and is a far-field model, not a finite nozzle.

Weak hyperbolicity of the unsteady top-hat plume (At​+wAz​+Awz​=⋯,wt​+wwz​=⋯)

Words: 70
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
For plume area A and reduced gravity g′, the principal matrix in variables (A,w,g′) is ​w00​Aw0​00w​​. At A>0 it has a triple real speed w but only two independent eigenvectors. Thus the model is weakly hyperbolic, not strongly or strictly hyperbolic. Real speeds alone do not provide a complete set of characteristic fields or a uniform high-frequency estimate. Shape factors representing nonuniform radial profiles can alter this degeneracy.

Unsteady top-hat line-plume balances

Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
Use half-width b, upward speed w, constant inertial reference density R, and positive buoyant reduced gravity g′. Half-section fluxes are Q=Rbw, M=Rbw2 and F=Rg′bw. With two-sided entrainment ue​=αw, the Boussinesq balances are
(Q2/M)t​+Qz​=RαM/Q,Qt​+Mz​=FQ/M,(FQ/M)t​+Fz​=−N2Q.
(2)
Full-width fluxes are twice these definitions. The shape coefficients differ from triangular transverse profiles, so their unsteady balances cannot be used unchanged here.

Separable decaying top-hat line plume (b=αz/2,w=2z/(3τ),g′=2z/(3τ2))

Words: 83
Unsteady top-hat line-plume balances OurBigBook.com
In a homogeneous ambient fluid, the displayed nonsteady power-law ansatz solves the unsteady top-hat line-plume balances for τ=t+t∗​>0. It gives Q=Rαz2/(3τ), M=2Rαz3/(9τ2) and F=2Rαz3/(9τ3). The solution is singular at zero age and carries zero source flux at its unshifted ideal origin. It describes a separable branch away from a source; a finite-width boundary, virtual-origin shift or matching region is needed to supply nonzero physical source flux. It is distinct from the steady constant-flux line plume, whose top-hat half-width grows as αz.

Merger of two equal pure plumes (Qc​=2Q,Mc​=2M,Fc​=2F,Γc​=2​)

Words: 122 Articles: 1
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
If two equal pure plumes merge while keeping their common velocity and reduced gravity, conservation of cross-sectional area gives radius 2​ times the individual radius. All three integral fluxes double. The plume balance parameter increases by 23/25/2=2​, so the immediate combined plume is a lazy plume. Similarity attraction may restore pure balance farther downstream, but instantaneous pure balance does not follow from adding two pure plumes.

Fixed-speed pure-plume merger and flux conservation (bp​=b,πbp2​=21​πbc2​)

Words: 63
Merger of two equal pure plumes OurBigBook.com
For a merged pair held at the original velocity and reduced gravity, pure plume balance requires the original individual radius, not the larger area-preserving radius. The required pure area is half the combined area. Shrinking to that area at unchanged velocity and reduced gravity would halve volume, momentum and buoyancy fluxes, contradicting conservation of the merged fluxes. A conservative merger must change its properties or begin away from pure balance.

Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient (Qz​=2αM​,Mz​=FQ/M,Fz​=−N2Q)

Words: 297 Articles: 5
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
With common factors of π removed, steady kinematic fluxes satisfy Qz​=2αM​, Mz​=FQ/M and Fz​=−N2Q. The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis supplies volume, the integrated buoyancy force supplies momentum, and the environmental mass density gradient reduces buoyancy flux. This uses the Boussinesq approximation.

Plume similarity in power-law stratification ((q,m,f)=(3+β/2,4+β,4+3β/2))

For N2=Kzβ and positive pure-power fluxes, the similarity solution exponents are q=3+β/2, m=4+β and f=4+3β/2. Increasing volume and momentum and decreasing positive buoyancy flux require −4<β<−8/3. At fixed nonzero K the endpoint coefficient limits are singular.

For prescribed N2=−G2<0, the steady Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient has kinematic fluxes Q=α2Gz3/9, M=α2G2z4/36, and F=α2G3z4/36. Balancing powers in Qz​=2αM​, Mz​=FQ/M, and Fz​=G2Q gives exponents (3,4,4) and these positive amplitudes. Amplitudes and physical branches of a power-law stratified plume (b=4αz/(p+6),w2=−2Czp+2/[(p+4)(3p+8)])
Words: 88
Plume similarity in power-law stratification OurBigBook.com
For the Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient with N2=Czp, the exact pure-power solution has the displayed radius and velocity, and g′=−2Czp+1/(3p+8). With positive entrainment, radius, upward speed and reduced gravity, stable C>0 requires −4<p<−8/3, whereas unstable C<0 requires p>−8/3. The stable branch has divergent buoyancy flux at zero height, so it is not a finite-flux point-source solution. The unstable branch has zero limiting source buoyancy flux and extracts buoyancy from its maintained unstable ambient. The cases p=−6,−4,−8/3 are singular at nonzero C; C=0 instead admits the ordinary Boussinesq point-source plume.

Non-Boussinesq top-hat plume equations

Words: 166 Articles: 1
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
For a turbulent plume in a homogeneous ambient of density ρ0​, define actual sectional mass flux, momentum flux and density-weighted buoyancy flux by Q=πb2ρw, M=πb2ρw2 and F=πb2wg(ρ0​−ρ). With non-Boussinesq Batchelor entrainment ve​=αwρ/ρ0​​, their balances are
∂t​(Q2/M)+∂z​Q=2απρ0​M​,∂t​Q+∂z​M=QF/M,∂t​(QF/M)+∂z​F=0.
(3)
Here Q2/M is sectional mass, QF/M is sectional density-deficit buoyancy, and the entrained ambient initially has zero vertical momentum. The last equation follows by subtracting mass conservation from ρ0​ times volume conservation. Scase, Caulfield, Dalziel and Hunt's time-dependent plume model derives the equivalent system with the common factor π suppressed.

Pure plume

Words: 1k Articles: 23
Top-hat plume model OurBigBook.com
A pure plume is a similarity regime in which the source supplies buoyancy flux but no dynamically persistent source volume or momentum flux. Its finite source region is represented by a plume virtual origin.

Pure plume balance (M5/2=5FQ2/(8α))

Words: 266 Articles: 4
Pure plume OurBigBook.com
For the constant-entrainment axisymmetric plume equations Q′=2αM​, M′=FQ/M with F>0, pure balance means M5/2=5FQ2/(8α) at every height. This is a similarity relation between fluxes, not a requirement that the physical source have zero radius. A finite source on this curve is represented by a plume virtual origin. For fluxes normalized by pi and top-hat fields, the same relation is g′b/w2=8α/5.
Plume flux-balance invariant (M5/2−5FQ2/(8α)=C)
