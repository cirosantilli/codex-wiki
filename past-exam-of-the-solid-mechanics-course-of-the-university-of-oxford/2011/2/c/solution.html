For R=∣x∣>0, DR=θ and Dθ=(I−θ⊗θ)/R. Differentiating the spherically symmetric deformation gives
F=r′(R)θ⊗θ+Rr(R)​(I−θ⊗θ).​
(20)
For an orientation-preserving spherically symmetric deformation with positive radius, r>0 and r′>0. The radial direction and its perpendicular plane are eigenspaces of this positive symmetric matrix, so
λ1​=r′(R),λ2​=λ3​=r(R)/R.​
(21)
At a positive diagonal matrix D, every diagonal rotation with two minus signs commutes with D. The supplied equivariance relation therefore forces T(D) to be a diagonal matrix: each off-diagonal entry changes sign under one such rotation while D stays fixed. Varying a positive diagonal entry shows that the diagonal entries of T(D) are Φ,j​. Permutation symmetry of Φ implies Φ,2​=Φ,3​ when λ2​=λ3​.
Choose a rotation whose first column is θ. Applying equivariance to F=QDQT now gives
T=Φ,1​θ⊗θ+Φ,2​(I−θ⊗θ),​
(22)
with the derivatives evaluated at (r′,r/R,r/R). Thus the stress has one radial and one repeated tangential principal value, independently of the choice of tangent basis.
