We first derive the stress and energy facts used in both parts. The Cauchy traction s=Tn is force per unit area in the deformed configuration. The first Piola-Kirchhoff traction sR​=TR​N is force per unit reference area. Their force vectors are measured in the same spatial coordinates, but the area measures and the normals used to specify the surface are different.
Parametrize a reference surface by x=x(u,v). Its oriented area vector is (xu​×xv​)dudv. Under deformation, the two tangents become Fxu​,Fxv​. The cross-product identity
(Fa)×(Fb)=(cofF)(a×b)=JF−T(a×b)
(1)
therefore proves Nanson's formula nda=JF−TNdA. Equality of the physical force on corresponding surface patches now gives
TR​NdA=Tnda=JTF−TNdA.
(2)
Since this holds for every reference normal,
TR​=JTF−T,T=J−1TR​FT.​
(3)
The first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor is generally not symmetric, whereas the Cauchy stress tensor is symmetric in the absence of couple stresses.
For the stated energy on orientation-preserving gradients, differentiation gives
TR​=DW(F)=μ(F−J−2cofF)=μ(F−J−1F−T).
(4)
A stress-free state satisfies FFT=J−1I. Taking determinants yields J2=J−3, so J=1 and FFT=I: precisely F∈SO(3). These states are also the global energy minimizers. If vi​>0 are the singular values, the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives ∣F∣2≥3J2/3, with equality only when the singular values are equal. The function 3J2/3/2+J−1 decreases up to J=1 and increases thereafter, with minimum 5/2. Thus W≥5μ/2, with equality precisely at rotations. A constant shift of the energy can set this minimum to zero without changing the stress.
For the given simple shear,
F=​100​110​001​​,F−T=​1−10​010​001​​,TR​=μ​010​100​000​​.
(5)
For a unit normal N, ∣TR​N∣2=μ2(N12​+N22​)=μ2(1−N32​). Hence every reference plane whose normal lies in the e1​,e2​ plane is maximizing:
N=(cosα,sinα,0),x1​cosα+x2​sinα=0,max∣sR​∣=μ.​
(6)
There is a family of maximizing planes, rather than one distinguished normal.

T=TR​FT=μ​110​100​000​​.

Its eigenvalues are μ(1+5​)/2, μ(1−5​)/2, and zero. Because it is symmetric, its largest traction magnitude on a unit spatial normal is the largest absolute eigenvalue. Put φ=(1+5​)/2. The positive eigenvalue has the strictly largest modulus, with normal

The question asks for the plane in the reference configuration, so this spatial normal must be pulled back. Nanson's formula gives N parallel to FTn, and
FT(φ,1,0)T=(φ,φ+1,0)T=φ(1,φ,0)T.
(9)

A dead-load traction is prescribed force per unit reference area whose vector is independent of the deformation. It is imposed through TR​N=tR​ on the reference boundary. It does not rotate or rescale with the current surface as a follower load would.
Elastic incompressibility imposes detF=1 and adds a scalar Lagrange multiplier to the constitutive law. With the pressure sign convention used here,
TR​=DW(F)−pcofF=DW(F)−pF−T,T=DW(F)FT−pI.
(11)
The pressure field is determined by equilibrium and boundary conditions, rather than by the strain energy density alone.
For the diagonal homogeneous deformation, F=diag(v1​,v2​,v3​) and v1​v2​v3​=1. Isotropy makes DW(F) diagonal here, with entries Φi​, so
(TR​)ii​=Φi​−vi​p​.
(12)
The six face conditions are exactly TR​ei​=τei​. Thus Φi​−p/vi​=τ for each i, or
v1​Φ1​−τv1​=v2​Φ2​−τv2​=v3​Φ3​−τv3​=p,v1​v2​v3​=1.​
(13)
Necessity follows from the boundary conditions. Conversely these equations give a constant pressure and constant stress satisfying those conditions, while DivTR​=0 because the stress is constant and there is no body force. This proves sufficiency as well.
For the particular energy, Φi​=μ(vi​−vi−2​). Put S=v1​+v2​+v3​ and Q=v1​v2​+v1​v3​+v2​v3​. Elastic incompressibility gives vi−1​=vj​vk​, hence
vi​Φi​−τvi​=μ(vi2​−vj​vk​)−τvi​=(μS−τ)vi​−μQ.
(14)
Equality for every pair is therefore equivalent to (μS−τ)(vi​−vj​)=0. If all stretches are equal, their positive product one gives v1​=v2​=v3​=1, with p=−τ. Otherwise S=τ/μ. Conversely any positive triple with this sum and product one solves the equations, with p=−μQ. Thus the complete solutions within the stated homogeneous diagonal ansatz are the unit triple and the stated sum-product family.
By the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, S≥3, with equality only at the unit triple. Consequently τ≤3μ admits only that triple, including equality, when the two descriptions coincide.
To prove infinitely many for s=τ/μ>3, choose v1​=t near one and set
v2,3​=2s−t±(s−t)2−4/t​​.
(15)
At t=1 the discriminant is (s−1)2−4>0; it stays positive on an interval about one, and both roots remain positive. Every such t gives a different admissible triple. Hence
τ≤3μ: one triple;τ>3μ: infinitely many triples.​
(16)
The degeneracy has an energy explanation: under the product constraint the strain energy density is μS2/2, and the total potential under dead loading is μS2/2−τS. It depends on the three stretches only through their sum. Its minimum over attainable S≥3 moves from S=3 to the whole level set S=τ/μ when the load exceeds 3μ.

For a hyperelastic material TR​(F)=DW(F). Material frame indifference of this first Piola-Kirchhoff law means
TR​(QF)=QTR​(F)(Q∈SO(3)).​
(17)
The spatial force components rotate under a superposed rigid rotation, whereas the reference normal does not.
If W(QF)=W(F), differentiate with respect to F in direction H to obtain DW(QF):(QH)=DW(F):H, hence the displayed stress transformation. Conversely, assume that stress transformation. For fixed Q, the derivative of W(QF)−W(F) with respect to F vanishes. The positive-determinant matrix domain is connected, as follows from polar decomposition and the connectedness of SO(3) and of the positive-definite cone. Thus W(QF)−W(F)=c(Q), independent of F. Iterating gives
W(QnF)=W(F)+nc(Q)(n∈Z).
(18)
The assumed lower bound on W forces c(Q)=0: either nonzero sign would tend to minus infinity for one direction of integer n. This proves the required equivalence and explains the use of the lower-bound hypothesis.
In the frame-indifferent setting, isotropy means invariance under reference rotations as well: W(FQ)=W(F) for Q∈SO(3). The distinction matters: left rotations encode changes of observer, and right rotations encode material symmetry.
An orientation-preserving singular value decomposition is F=Rdiag(v1​,v2​,v3​)S, with R,S∈SO(3) and vi​>0. Material frame indifference and isotropy remove R,S, leaving a function Φ of the stretches. Permuting the stretches using signed permutation rotations does not change the value, so Φ is symmetric. Conversely a symmetric stretch function is unchanged by both left and right rotations, in particular is isotropic.
The eigenvalues of B=FFT are vi2​. Its three principal invariants are
I=trB=∑i​vi2​,II=21​[(trB)2−tr(B2)]=∑i<j​vi2​vj2​,III=detB=∏i​vi2​.
(19)
They determine the unordered positive eigenvalues as the roots of λ3−Iλ2+IIλ−III=0. Therefore a symmetric stretch function defines a well-defined h(I,II,III) on the admissible invariant triples. Conversely a function of these invariants is a symmetric stretch function, proving the equivalence of all three descriptions.
The printed domain of Φ must be (0,∞)3, since it takes three arguments. Also isotropy alone as right invariance would not imply the spectral representation without material frame indifference: tr(AFFT) for a fixed nonscalar symmetric positive-definite A is right invariant but not generally objective. The equivalence here uses the frame-indifferent constitutive setting.
For the specific energy, Newton identities for the three eigenvalues gives ∑i​vi6​=I3−3III+3III, while v1​v2​v3​=J=III​. Thus
W=αI+β(I3−3III+3III)+γIII−1/2.​
(20)
To differentiate it, use
DF​I=2F,DF​II=2(IF−BF),DF​III=2JcofF.
(21)
These follow from I=∣F∣2, the displayed trace formula for II, and III=J2. The three partial derivatives of h are hI​=α+3βI2−3βII, hII​=−3βI and hIII​=3β−γIII−3/2/2. The chain rule therefore gives
TR​=(2α−6βII)F+6βIBF+(6βJ−γJ−2)cofF.
(22)
Finally −6βII=−3β(trB)2+3βtr(B2), and BF=FFTF. This is precisely the requested expression, with the coefficient of F separated from the BF and cofactor terms. The positive sign of the 6βJ term comes from the 3βIII contribution in the energy.

Use a temperature-dependent strain energy density W(F,ϑ) with different minimizing deformation gradients in the two crystal phases. Above the transition temperature its wells describe austenite; below it the wells are unions SO(3)Ui​ of martensitic variants. Left rotational orbits reflect material frame indifference, while the crystal's discrete material symmetries relate the variants. Minimizing the integral of this elastic energy, together with loading and boundary constraints, models the change of lattice shape. Compatibility restricts which low-energy gradients can meet continuously; fine compatible mixtures can accommodate macroscopic boundary shapes.
For the jump condition, write the two affine pieces as Ax+c+​ and Bx+c−​. Continuity on the interface means
(A−B)x+(c+​−c−​)=0(x⋅N=k).
(23)
Taking differences along the interface gives (A−B)t=0 for every tangent vector t with t⋅N=0. Decompose any vector v as t+(v⋅N)N. Then
(A−B)v=(v⋅N)(A−B)N.
(24)
With a=(A−B)N this proves
A−B=a⊗N.​
(25)
Conversely this rank-one connection permits continuity by choosing c+​−c−​=−ka. A coherent twin interface is such a compatible planar boundary between different martensitic variants, possibly after a rigid rotation of one variant. The rank-one condition is therefore the geometric compatibility condition for a simple twin.
For the unrotated pair, direct subtraction gives
U1​−U2​=2δe3​⊗e1​,
(26)
so R=I is one solution, with reference interface normal e1​.
Put d=1+δ2, c=(1−δ2)/d and s=2δ/d. Since c2+s2=1, the stated R is a proper rotation. Multiplication gives
RU2​=​c+δs0s−δc​010​−s0c​​=​10δ​010​−s0c​​.
(27)
Here c+δs=1 and s−δc=δ. Consequently
U1​−RU2​=​000​000​s01−c​​=1+δ22δ​(e1​+δe3​)⊗e3​.​
(28)
This is the second rank-one connection, now with normal e3​. Because δ>0, the two rotations are distinct. The permitted assertion that there are at most two proves completeness.
Finally suppose QU1​ is rank-one connected to RU2​, with Q∈SO(3). Left multiplication of their difference by QT preserves rank and gives U1​−QTRU2​. The classification just proved forces QTR to be either I or R, hence Q=R or Q=I. The two requested matrices are therefore
U1​andRU1​.​
(29)
Their connections are explicit: the first was calculated above, and RU1​−RU2​=2δ(Re3​)⊗e1​. If desired the second matrix is
