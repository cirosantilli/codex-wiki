The matrix exponential is the absolutely convergent series etA=∑n=0∞​tnAn/n!, with derivative AetA and inverse e−tA. If KT=−K, then (etK)TetK=e−tKetK=I. Its determinant is continuous in t, is never zero, and starts at one, so it is one throughout: etK∈SO(3). Conversely, differentiating (etK)TetK=I at zero gives KT+K=0. Thus
etK∈SO(3) for all real t⟺KT=−K.​
(1)
The quantifier matters: membership at one isolated time alone would not imply skewness.
Write P(F)=DW(F) and J=detF>0. For any skew matrix K, differentiate frame indifference along etKF:
0=P(F):(KF)=(P(F)FT):K.
(2)
A matrix orthogonal to every skew matrix is symmetric. Therefore P(F)FT and the Cauchy stress tensor T=J−1P(F)FT are symmetric.
Let C=D2W(I) and assume P(I)=0. Differentiating symmetry of P(F)FT at I in direction H shows C[H] is symmetric for every H. Hence cijkl​=cjikl​. As a Hessian, C also has pair-interchange symmetry cijkl​=cklij​. Combining the two gives cijkl​=cijlk​. Equivalently C annihilates infinitesimal rotations. This proves both requested minor symmetries and shows exactly where the stress-free assumption enters. It is stress-free linearization of an objective energy.
For the particular energy, ∑i​vi2​=tr(FTF) and ∏i​vi​=J, so
P(F)=4αF+h′(J)cofF.
(3)
At I this is [4α+h′(1)]I. Therefore the reference is stress-free exactly when h′(1)=−4α​. To differentiate again, use cofF=JF−T. The determinant and inverse derivatives at I give
D(cof)(I)[H]=(trH)I−HT,​
(4)
which also proves the supplied hint. It follows that
C[H]=4αH+h′′(1)(trH)I−4α[(trH)I−HT].
(5)
For a symmetric e this becomes
Ce=8αe+[h′′(1)−4α](tre)I=2μe+λ(tre)I,μ=4α, λ=h′′(1)−4α.​
(6)
No additional positivity of the bulk modulus follows from the stated smoothness of h alone; this calculation identifies the moduli rather than imposing extra material assumptions.
