The material symmetry group consists of invertible admissible reference transformations H for which W(FH)=W(F) for every admissible F. Its rotational part lies in SO(3); a determinant-one reference convention restricts the full group to the corresponding special linear group. This right action is distinct from the spatial left action W(QF)=W(F) of material frame indifference.
The proper cube-rotation group has 24​ elements. Choose the image of one outward face normal in six ways, then the image of an adjacent perpendicular normal in four ways; orientation fixes the third direction. No reflections are included. For an isotropic material every proper rotation belongs to the material symmetry group.
Combine material isotropy with the physical frame-indifference condition. The singular value decomposition may be written F=Rdiag(v1​,v2​,v3​)Q with R,Q∈SO(3) because detF>0. Left and right rotational invariance remove R,Q. Signed permutation rotations permit any permutation of the three diagonal stretches, so
W(F)=Φ(v1​,v2​,v3​),Φ symmetric in its three arguments.​
(15)
Right invariance alone, without the usual material frame indifference assumption, would not suffice to remove spatial orientation.
The strict Baker-Ericksen inequalities say that larger distinct principal stretches carry larger principal Cauchy stresses:
(σi​−σj​)(vi​−vj​)>0if vi​=vj​,σi​=J−1vi​Φi​.​
(16)
A natural state of a hyperelastic material has zero stress. If two stretches differed, their equal zero stresses would contradict the strict inequality. Thus all three equal some v>0, and polar decomposition gives F=vQ​, Q∈SO(3). A weak non-strict inequality alone would not prove this conclusion, so its strict convention is explicit here.
For the radial deformation put f(R)=r(R)/R, R=∣x∣, θ=x/R. Since ∂j​R=θj​,
Dy=fI+Rf′θ⊗θ=Rr​I+(r′−Rr​)θ⊗θ.​
(17)
This symmetric tensor has radial eigenvalue r′ and the repeated tangential eigenvalue r/R. Both are positive in the open annulus under the stated monotonicity/nonnegativity conditions. Therefore these are its principal stretches. The two cases below establish radial zero-energy rigidity by different arguments.
