For a hyperelastic material TR​(F)=DW(F). Material frame indifference of this first Piola-Kirchhoff law means
TR​(QF)=QTR​(F)(Q∈SO(3)).​
(17)
The spatial force components rotate under a superposed rigid rotation, whereas the reference normal does not.
If W(QF)=W(F), differentiate with respect to F in direction H to obtain DW(QF):(QH)=DW(F):H, hence the displayed stress transformation. Conversely, assume that stress transformation. For fixed Q, the derivative of W(QF)−W(F) with respect to F vanishes. The positive-determinant matrix domain is connected, as follows from polar decomposition and the connectedness of SO(3) and of the positive-definite cone. Thus W(QF)−W(F)=c(Q), independent of F. Iterating gives
W(QnF)=W(F)+nc(Q)(n∈Z).
(18)
The assumed lower bound on W forces c(Q)=0: either nonzero sign would tend to minus infinity for one direction of integer n. This proves the required equivalence and explains the use of the lower-bound hypothesis.
In the frame-indifferent setting, isotropy means invariance under reference rotations as well: W(FQ)=W(F) for Q∈SO(3). The distinction matters: left rotations encode changes of observer, and right rotations encode material symmetry.
An orientation-preserving singular value decomposition is F=Rdiag(v1​,v2​,v3​)S, with R,S∈SO(3) and vi​>0. Material frame indifference and isotropy remove R,S, leaving a function Φ of the stretches. Permuting the stretches using signed permutation rotations does not change the value, so Φ is symmetric. Conversely a symmetric stretch function is unchanged by both left and right rotations, in particular is isotropic.
The eigenvalues of B=FFT are vi2​. Its three principal invariants are
I=trB=∑i​vi2​,II=21​[(trB)2−tr(B2)]=∑i<j​vi2​vj2​,III=detB=∏i​vi2​.
(19)
They determine the unordered positive eigenvalues as the roots of λ3−Iλ2+IIλ−III=0. Therefore a symmetric stretch function defines a well-defined h(I,II,III) on the admissible invariant triples. Conversely a function of these invariants is a symmetric stretch function, proving the equivalence of all three descriptions.
The printed domain of Φ must be (0,∞)3, since it takes three arguments. Also isotropy alone as right invariance would not imply the spectral representation without material frame indifference: tr(AFFT) for a fixed nonscalar symmetric positive-definite A is right invariant but not generally objective. The equivalence here uses the frame-indifferent constitutive setting.
For the specific energy, Newton identities for the three eigenvalues gives ∑i​vi6​=I3−3III+3III, while v1​v2​v3​=J=III​. Thus
W=αI+β(I3−3III+3III)+γIII−1/2.​
(20)
To differentiate it, use
DF​I=2F,DF​II=2(IF−BF),DF​III=2JcofF.
(21)
These follow from I=∣F∣2, the displayed trace formula for II, and III=J2. The three partial derivatives of h are hI​=α+3βI2−3βII, hII​=−3βI and hIII​=3β−γIII−3/2/2. The chain rule therefore gives
TR​=(2α−6βII)F+6βIBF+(6βJ−γJ−2)cofF.
(22)
Finally −6βII=−3β(trB)2+3βtr(B2), and BF=FFTF. This is precisely the requested expression, with the coefficient of F separated from the BF and cofactor terms. The positive sign of the 6βJ term comes from the 3βIII contribution in the energy.
