We first derive the stress and energy facts used in both parts. The Cauchy traction s=Tn is force per unit area in the deformed configuration. The first Piola-Kirchhoff traction sR​=TR​N is force per unit reference area. Their force vectors are measured in the same spatial coordinates, but the area measures and the normals used to specify the surface are different.
Parametrize a reference surface by x=x(u,v). Its oriented area vector is (xu​×xv​)dudv. Under deformation, the two tangents become Fxu​,Fxv​. The cross-product identity
(Fa)×(Fb)=(cofF)(a×b)=JF−T(a×b)

therefore proves Nanson's formula nda=JF−TNdA. Equality of the physical force on corresponding surface patches now gives
TR​NdA=Tnda=JTF−TNdA.

The first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor is generally not symmetric, whereas the Cauchy stress tensor is symmetric in the absence of couple stresses.

A stress-free state satisfies FFT=J−1I. Taking determinants yields J2=J−3, so J=1 and FFT=I: precisely F∈SO(3). These states are also the global energy minimizers.
