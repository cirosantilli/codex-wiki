Let Wr​,Wθ​,Wϕ​ denote derivatives of the strain energy density with respect to its three principal stretches. The principal stress formula for incompressible hyperelasticity is
tr​=λr​Wr​−p,tθ​=λθ​Wθ​−p,tϕ​=λϕ​Wϕ​−p.
(21)
It follows by applying T=F(∂F​W)T−p1 in principal axes. Equal tangential principal stretches and material isotropy give Wθ​=Wϕ​ at (λ−2,λ,λ). Differentiating the reduced strain energy density by the chain rule gives
h′(λ)​=−2λ−3Wr​+Wθ​+Wϕ​=2(Wθ​−λ−3Wr​)=λ2​(tθ​−tr​).​
(22)
The unknown pressure cancels in this difference. Substituting in part (i), and then using the supplied derivative of λ, gives
drdtr​​=rλh′(λ)​=Rh′(λ)​,drdλ​=R1−λ3​.
(23)
Consequently the desired ordinary differential equation is
dλdtr​​=1−λ3h′(λ)​.​
(24)

Take the inner surface to be free of traction, as appropriate to an empty cavity with no surface tension. At the outer surface the imposed pressure is compressive when positive. The outward normal vectors are −er​ at r=a and +er​ at r=b, giving
tr​(a)=0,tr​(b)=−P.
(25)
Integrating the ordinary differential equation from part (ii) between the two surfaces gives
−P=∫λa​λb​​1−λ3h′(λ)​dλ.
(26)
The outer stretch follows from incompressibility:
λb3​=B3b3​=1+B3A3​(λa3​−1).
(27)
Hence an expression entirely in terms of the inner stretch and the reference radii is
P(λa​)=∫λa​[1+(A/B)3(λa3​−1)]1/3​λ3−1h′(λ)​dλ.​
(28)
Equivalently P=−∫λb​λa​​h′(λ)/(λ3−1)dλ. For an expanding shell, 1<λb​<λa​; when h′>0 on this range, P<0, correctly describing an outward tensile loading. If an inner pressure Pi​ were present, the same derivation would give P−Pi​ on the left. Thus the empty-cavity condition specifies the pressure reference needed for the requested answer.

First let A↓0 at fixed positive cavity radius a and fixed reference outer radius B. Then
λa​=Aa​⟶∞,λb​⟶(1+B3a3​)1/3,
(29)
and the pressure sustaining a finite cavity is
P(a)=−∫(1+a3/B3)1/3∞​λ3−1h′(λ)​dλ.
(30)
The onset of cavitation is the additional limit a↓0. The outer stretch then tends to one, and the signed critical pressure is
Pcrit​=−∫1∞​λ3−1h′(λ)​dλ.​
(31)
When this improper integral is positive and finite before the minus sign, the tensile loading magnitude is −Pcrit​>0. Thus the cavity opens under tension, even though the load variable is named pressure. The critical value is independent of the original sphere radius B.
For A=0 and a>0, the deformation map is smooth away from R=0 but singular at the original centre: r=(R3+a3)1/3 approaches a, rather than zero, as R↓0. This is the elastic cavitation mechanism; it cannot be represented by a smooth deformation map through the centre.

For general α>0, put S(λ)=λ−4+2λ2. The reduced strain energy density and its derivative are
h(λ)=2μ​[S(λ)α−3α],h′(λ)=2μαS(λ)α−1(λ−λ−5).

The same factorization as in part (i) yields
λ3−1h′(λ)​=2μαS(λ)α−1(λ−2+λ−5).
(37)
This form removes the apparent singularity at λ=1: its limit is 4μα3α−1, finite for every fixed α>0. Thus only the upper endpoint can obstruct convergence of the improper integral.
As λ→∞, S(λ)∼2λ2, giving
λ3−1h′(λ)​∼μα2αλ2α−4.
(38)
For μ>0, the comparison test with a power integral implies convergence exactly when 2α−4<−1. Therefore
0<α<23​​
(39)
is precisely the range with finite critical pressure, the cavitation criterion for power-law strain energy. At α=3/2 the divergence is logarithmic; for larger α it is a power divergence. These materials require an unbounded tensile loading magnitude in this ideal model. The qualification μ>0 excludes the degenerate zero-energy material.

a OurBigBook.com
The first equation is the local balance of linear momentum. Use M,L,T for the dimensions of mass, length, and time, to distinguish dimensional time from the Cauchy stress tensor T.
  • x(X,t) is the current position of the particle labelled by reference position X; both have dimension L. The deformation map specifies the particle's trajectory.
  • x¨=∂t2​x∣X​ is its material acceleration, with dimension LT−2. The dots follow the particle and are not spatially fixed time derivatives.
  • ρ is current mass density, with dimension ML−3. For a homogeneous material with incompressibility, mass conservation makes it constant in space and time.
  • b is body force per unit mass, such as gravitational acceleration, with dimension LT−2. Hence ρb is force per unit volume.
  • T is the Cauchy stress tensor, mapping a unit normal vector n to surface traction Tn. Each component has dimension ML−1T−2, or force per unit area.
  • div differentiates in the current configuration, with dimension L−1. In Cartesian coordinates, (divT)i​=∂Tij​/∂xj​. Thus divT, ρb, and ρx¨ all have dimension ML−2T−2.
  • The superscript T denotes the dimension-preserving matrix transpose. The relation T=TT expresses angular momentum conservation in a continuum without body couples or couple stresses.
Here t has dimension T. The hypothesis ∂t​b=0 means that the prescribed spatial body force field is the same for every member of the static family and the resulting motion.

Restore the particle label suppressed in the question, writing the static family as x0​(X;τ). At each fixed τ, its Cauchy stress tensor satisfies
divT0​(τ)+ρb=0.
(40)
This dynamic continuation of a static incompressible elastic deformation sets τ=t to form a motion. At every time its deformation gradient is the same as that of the corresponding static member. For an elastic material the elastic part of the Cauchy stress tensor depends on that deformation gradient, while incompressibility leaves an arbitrary scalar constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress. Therefore write
T0​=S(F)−p0​1,T=S(F)−p1,T−T0​=(p0​−p)1.
(41)
Here 1 is the identity matrix. Subtracting static balance of linear momentum from dynamic balance of linear momentum gives
ρx¨0​=∇(p0​−p).
(42)
Since the homogeneous incompressible material has constant mass density, define ξ=(p−p0​)/ρ. Necessarily
x¨0​(X,t)=−∇ξ(x0​(X,t),t).​
(43)
Conversely, suppose this acceleration potential exists. Choose p=p0​+ρξ. Then
divT+ρb=divT0​+ρb−ρ∇ξ=ρx¨0​.
(44)
The added isotropic stress is symmetric, so angular momentum conservation remains satisfied. This proves both directions for the field equations and the elastic constitutive equation. The surface tractions for the motion are those of the adjusted stress; identical prescribed static tractions would impose an additional boundary restriction on ξ.

For a twice continuously differentiable scalar potential, mixed second partial derivatives commute. Taking the spatial curl of the equation from part (i) gives
∇×x¨=−∇×∇ξ=0.​
(47)
For example, its first component is −∂2​∂3​ξ+∂3​∂2​ξ=0. The curl acts on the spatial representation of the material acceleration, not directly on its expression in reference coordinates.
This is a necessary local condition. It is sufficient locally, and sufficient globally on a simply connected domain under the usual smoothness assumptions. On a domain with holes, a global scalar potential additionally requires every closed-loop line integral of the material acceleration to vanish. The homogeneous motion in part (c) admits an explicit global scalar potential, so this topological qualification causes no obstruction there.

Take two time derivatives at fixed reference position X:
x¨=F¨X+c¨.
(48)
The constraint detF=1 ensures that the deformation gradient is invertible, so X=F−1(x−c). Thus the spatial material acceleration is the affine vector field
x¨=A(t)(x−c(t))+c¨(t),A=F¨F−1.
(49)
Its spatial gradient is A, and its curl is
∇×x¨=(A32​−A23​, A13​−A31​, A21​−A12​).
(50)
Part (b)(iii) therefore requires
F¨F−1=(F¨F−1)T.​
(51)
Equivalently, multiplying on the left by FT and on the right by F gives
FTF¨=F¨TF.​
(52)
The translation c(t) contributes no spatial curl and is unrestricted by this condition. A useful equivalent first integral is
dtd​(FTF˙−F˙TF)=0:
(53)
the antisymmetric matrix in parentheses must be constant, and need not vanish. The separate constraint detF=1 is still required; differentiating it also gives tr(F˙F−1)=0.
The condition is understood in the setting of part (b), where a static family already exists under the prescribed body force. For a homogeneous elastic deformation gradient, the elastic stress S(F) is spatially constant. Static balance of linear momentum consequently gives ∇p0​=ρb, ensuring that b has a scalar potential. If the static-family assumption were dropped and an arbitrary time-independent body force allowed, the general necessary condition would instead be ∇×x¨=∇×b.
