Cauchy's stress hypothesis says that traction across a cut depends locally on its point, time and oriented normal, rather than on the global shape of the cut. Here all areas and normals are referential: E is a fixed material region in reference coordinates, ρR​ its mass density per reference volume, v the current particle velocity, b body force per unit mass, and sR​ force per unit reference area. The time derivative on the left is total material momentum rate; the two right terms are contact and body forces.
A shrinking pillbox gives sR​(−N)=−sR​(N). In a shrinking tetrahedron the volume contributions scale as its linear size cubed, whereas face forces scale as size squared. Their leading balance expresses traction on an arbitrary face as the normal-component-weighted sum of tractions on three coordinate faces. Defining those three tractions as tensor columns gives sR​(N)=TR​N​. Applying the divergence theorem to momentum balance and localizing over arbitrary E then yields
ρR​v˙=DivTR​+ρR​b.​
(5)
The referential tensor is the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress and need not be symmetric; it should not be silently identified with the spatial Cauchy stress.
Incompressibility is detF=1​. For a homogeneous hyperelastic material the referential constitutive relation becomes
TR​=DW(F)−pcofF=DW(F)−pF−T(detF=1),​
(6)
where the undetermined scalar pressure p enforces the constraint. Homogeneity removes explicit reference-position dependence from W, not the possible spatial variation of that pressure.
For the neo-Hookean energy, DW=2αF. For the positive diagonal ansatz the nominal principal stresses are ti​=2αvi​−p/vi​. Since F is constant, equilibrium requires cofF∇p=0, so p is constant. Stress-free endfaces give p=2αv32​>0​. The side normals include (0,−1,0) and ±(3​/2,1/2,0), so their prescribed tractions force t1​=t2​=T​.
For fixed positive p, the scalar function 2αv−p/v is strictly increasing on v>0. Thus equality of the two nominal stresses implies v1​=v2​=v. Incompressibility gives v3​=v−2, and substitution gives
T=2α(v−v−5),p=2αv−4.​
(7)
The load function has derivative 2α(1+5v−6)>0, tends to minus infinity as v↓0 and to infinity as v→∞. It therefore determines exactly one positive v for every specified load, with v≥1 for outward tensile T≥0. The resulting constant stress satisfies equilibrium and every prescribed face traction, proving existence and uniqueness within the requested positive diagonal class. It does not claim uniqueness among all possible nonhomogeneous deformations. This is the equibiaxial dead loading of a neo-Hookean prism.
