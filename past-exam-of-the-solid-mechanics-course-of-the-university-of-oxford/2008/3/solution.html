Put n=x/R and q=r(R)/R. Differentiating y=qx uses ∇R=n and ∇q=(r′−q)n/R. Hence
F=qI+(r′−q)n⊗n.​
(8)
Its radial eigenvalue is r′ and its two tangential eigenvalues are q. They are positive by hypothesis, so they are also the principal stretches:
v1​=r′,v2​=v3​=q.​
(9)
For an isotropic stored energy the nominal stress has the same radial/tangential projectors,
P=DW(F)=Φ1​n⊗n+Φ2​(I−n⊗n),
(10)
where symmetry of Φ gives equal tangential derivatives. Since Div(n⊗n)=2n/R and the scalar coefficients depend only on R,
DivP=[dRdΦ1​​+R2​(Φ1​−Φ2​)]n.
(11)
Thus equilibrium is exactly dΦ1​/dR=2(Φ2​−Φ1​)/R​.
For the particular energy define δ=r′q2=detF>0. Its two derivatives are
Φ1​=μ+h′(δ)q2,Φ2​=μ+h′(δ)r′q.
(12)
Also q′=(r′−q)/R. Differentiating the first formula and substituting the equilibrium equation gives
h′′(δ)δ′q2+2h′(δ)qq′=2h′(δ)q(r′−q)/R=2h′(δ)qq′.
(13)
The last terms cancel. Strict positivity of h′′ and q yields δ′=0​, so r2r′=DR2 for one positive constant D. Integration gives r3=DR3+C.
The displacement data mean r(1)=λ1​ and r(2)=2λ2​, not r(2)=λ2​. Solving the two scalar equations gives
D=78λ23​−λ13​​,C=78(λ13​−λ23​)​,r=(DR3+C)1/3.​
(14)
The given inequality makes D>0. Since r3 increases from λ13​>0 to 8λ23​, it is positive on the whole shell, and r′=DR2/r2>0. Thus the formula is smooth and admissible and satisfies equilibrium. Conversely every admissible radial equilibrium had to have this constant determinant and these two integration constants, proving uniqueness. This is a constant-Jacobian radial equilibrium for a stretch-sum energy.
