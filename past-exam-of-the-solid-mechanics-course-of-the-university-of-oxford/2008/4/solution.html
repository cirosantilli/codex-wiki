On each half-space the deformation is affine. Continuity on the separating plane makes the two affine constants equal and gives (A−B)t=0 for every tangent vector t⊥N. Decomposing any x into its tangential part and (x⋅N)N gives
(A−B)x=[(A−B)N](N⋅x),A−B=a⊗N,a=(A−B)N.​
(15)
This is the Hadamard jump condition. Since B=A−a⊗N=A(I−A−1a⊗N), the matrix determinant lemma gives
detB=detA(1−N⋅A−1a).
(16)
Both determinants are positive, hence A−1a⋅N<1​.
Write a1​=η1​, a2​=η2​, S=a12​+a22​ and Δ=a22​−a12​=0. The two proposed angles have c=2a1​a2​/S and s=κΔ/S, and c2+s2=1. Therefore the displayed rotations lie in SO(3). Direct multiplication gives
RU2​−U1​=SΔ​​−a2​κa1​0​−κa2​a1​0​000​​=SΔ​(−a2​,κa1​,0)⊗(1,κ,0).​
(17)
This is a rank-one connection for each sign. With a unit normal one may choose N=(1,κ,0)/2​ and jump vector a=2​Δ(−a2​,κa1​,0)/S. The two rotations are distinct because Δ=0; the permitted at-most-two result therefore proves these are all solutions. Flipping both a and N changes no connection.
It remains to test the material plane. Because y(0)=0, the affine constants vanish. Let t=(−p2​,p1​,0), so the plane is spanned by t and e3​. Both deformation gradients send e3​ to a1​e3​. If the plane is not the twin plane, it cuts the two half-spaces; its two image halves are coplanar exactly when the in-plane vectors Bt and At are parallel. Put d=(−a2​,κa1​) and n=(1,κ) in their two-dimensional sections. Then
2det​(Bt,At)​=SΔ​2det​(Bt,d)(n⋅t)=Sκa1​a2​Δ​(p1​−κp2​)2​.​
(18)
Its prefactor is nonzero, so coplanarity requires p1​=κp2​, precisely p∥(1,κ,0)∥N. Conversely, on that twin plane (A−B)x=0, so the two affine maps agree and its image is a plane. Thus Π remains plane⟺p∥N​. All other allowed planes acquire a kink along their intersection with the twin. This is the planarity test across a cubic-to-tetragonal twin.
