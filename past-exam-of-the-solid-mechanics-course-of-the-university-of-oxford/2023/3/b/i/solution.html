Restore the particle label suppressed in the question, writing the static family as x0​(X;τ). At each fixed τ, its Cauchy stress tensor satisfies
divT0​(τ)+ρb=0.
(40)
This dynamic continuation of a static incompressible elastic deformation sets τ=t to form a motion. At every time its deformation gradient is the same as that of the corresponding static member. For an elastic material the elastic part of the Cauchy stress tensor depends on that deformation gradient, while incompressibility leaves an arbitrary scalar constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress. Therefore write
T0​=S(F)−p0​1,T=S(F)−p1,T−T0​=(p0​−p)1.
(41)
Here 1 is the identity matrix. Subtracting static balance of linear momentum from dynamic balance of linear momentum gives
ρx¨0​=∇(p0​−p).
(42)
Since the homogeneous incompressible material has constant mass density, define ξ=(p−p0​)/ρ. Necessarily
x¨0​(X,t)=−∇ξ(x0​(X,t),t).​
(43)
Conversely, suppose this acceleration potential exists. Choose p=p0​+ρξ. Then
divT+ρb=divT0​+ρb−ρ∇ξ=ρx¨0​.
(44)
The added isotropic stress is symmetric, so angular momentum conservation remains satisfied. This proves both directions for the field equations and the elastic constitutive equation. The surface tractions for the motion are those of the adjusted stress; identical prescribed static tractions would impose an additional boundary restriction on ξ.
