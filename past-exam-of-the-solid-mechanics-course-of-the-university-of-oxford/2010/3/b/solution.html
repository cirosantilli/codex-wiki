Use −π<θs​<π, with the crack along the negative radial axis. In the coefficient convention used here, the leading crack-tip expansion is
u(s)=ureg​+ks​Rs1/2​sin(θs​/2),∇ureg​=o(Rs−1/2​).​
(23)
The square-root term is harmonic and has zero angular derivative on both crack faces. Regular contributions may include a constant and bounded-gradient terms under smooth loading.
If E(s) is the minimized elastic-plus-load potential, the energy release rate is G(s)=−dE(s)/ds​. With this particular square-root coefficient normalization,
G(s)=4πμ​ks2​.​
(24)
For example, the small-circle energy flux is ∫[Wn1​−μur​ux1​​]Rs​dθs​. The singular term gives W=μks2​/(8Rs​), ur​=ks​sin(θs​/2)/(2Rs​​) and ux1​​=−ks​sin(θs​/2)/(2Rs​​). Integration over (−π,π) produces the stated factor. Other stress-intensity normalizations change that factor.
For toughness Gc​>0, local stability against infinitesimal extension means G(s)≤Gc​​, since the derivative of E(s)+Gc​(s+1) is nonnegative. Global energetic stability against every extension is the stronger condition E(s)≤E(r)+Gc​(r−s) for all r≥s.

Tlin=λtr(e)I+2μe.​

Ahead of the tip, θs​=0, the singular gradient is (0,ks​/(2Rs​​)). Thus
∣Tlin∣θs​=0​=2​μ∣ks​∣​Rs−1/2​+o(Rs−1/2​).​
