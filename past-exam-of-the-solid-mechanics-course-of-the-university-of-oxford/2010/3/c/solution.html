For a differentiable crack path, the local quasistatic Griffith laws with constant positive toughness are
ℓ˙(t)≥0,G(ℓ(t),t)≤Gc​,ℓ˙(t)[Gc​−G(ℓ(t),t)]=0.​
(28)
They express irreversibility, local stability, and energy balance during growth. A strictly subcritical release rate forces the crack to stop.
Fix a crack parameter s and let u(s,t) denote the equilibrium displacement with the prescribed time-dependent loads. The scalar equilibrium problem is linear and has a unique solution for its fixed Dirichlet data. Both body force and boundary displacement scale by t, so
u(s,t)=tu(s,1).​
(29)
The minimized potential energy is quadratic under this scaling: the strain term scales by t2 and so does the load term −f(t)u(s,t). Hence E(s,t)=t2E(s,1) and
G(s,t)=t2G(s,1).​
(30)
Equivalently the crack-tip coefficient scales by t and its squared energy-release formula gives the same result.
Put s0​=ℓ(0). Finiteness and continuity of G(s,1) make it bounded by some finite M≥0 on a neighborhood of s0​. Continuity of ℓ supplies a positive interval on which ℓ(t) stays in that neighborhood. Shorten it to [0,t0​] so that t02​M<Gc​. Then G(ℓ(t),t)≤t02​M<Gc​, and complementarity forces ℓ˙(t)=0 throughout. Therefore
ℓ(t)=ℓ(0)(0≤t≤t0​).​
(31)
No differentiating an unknown crack path or assuming its length already constant is needed; its continuity first gives the local release-rate bound.
