Control-point influence on a curve (ΔC(t)=bi​(t)h)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Computer aided geometric design Control point
OurBigBook.com Words: 413
For a parametric curve represented by fixed scalar functions bj​ and control points Pj​ in Euclidean space, write
C(t)=∑j​bj​(t)Pj​,Pi​=Pi​+h,Pj​=Pj​(j=i).
(4)
The influence function of this handle is bi​: the resulting displacement vector is ΔC(t)=C(t)−C(t)=bi​(t)h, and its norm is ∣bi​(t)∣∥h∥. These are exact finite changes, not only a first-order estimate. Where bi​ is negative the displacement points opposite to h. Where bi​(t)=0, the curve is unchanged at that parameter.
For the cubic Bézier curve above, the Bernstein basis influence function is b1,3​(t)=3t(1−t)2. Its derivative is
b1,3′​(t)=3(1−t)(1−3t).
(5)
It increases up to t=1/3 and decreases thereafter on [0,1]. Consequently the largest displacement at corresponding parameters is
max0≤t≤1​∥C(t)−C(t)∥=94​∥h∥,C(1/3)=(2734​,34​),C(1/3)=(2734​,916​).
(6)
For h=(0,1) it is 4/9. This is a comparison at the same parameter. A geometric closest-distance comparison, as in a closest-point search on a subdivision curve, instead minimizes over another parameter:
d(C(t),C([0,1]))=min0≤s≤1​∥C(s)−C(t)∥≤∥C(t)−C(t)∥.
(7)
The closest parameter s can differ from t, so 4/9 is not automatically a maximum closest-distance separation of the two curves. Neither parameter is assumed to be arc length.
The support of a function bi​ is the closure of the parameter values where bi​=0. It bounds where a nonzero handle move can act, but may include boundary points where the actual displacement is zero. Here the support of a function b1,3​ is all of [0,1] relative to the parameter domain, while the endpoints are unaffected. In contrast, a B-spline of order k has support within [ti​,ti+k​] in its spline knot sequence, so its handle affects only that parameter range; the support and Gram bandwidth of B-splines describes the same locality in another application. Changing the spline knots or the basis along with the handle requires a different comparison.
When the basis functions form a partition of unity, ∑j​bj​(t)=1, each evaluation is an affine combination of the control points. Applying an affine map T(x)=Ax+a to all handles gives
∑j​bj​(t)T(Pj​)=AC(t)+a=T(C(t)).
(8)
This is affine equivariance of a geometric basis, also called affine covariance: affine maps preserve affine combinations. In particular, translating every handle by h translates the whole curve by h, whereas moving just one handle produces bi​(t)h. The Bernstein basis has this partition of unity property.[ref] Nonnegative weights further make evaluations convex combinations, giving the convex hull bound illustrated by the control polygon; nonnegativity is not needed for affine covariance.
For a proof outline of the influence formula, subtract the two basis representations: every unchanged coefficient cancels, leaving bi​(t)(Pi​+h)−bi​(t)Pi​=bi​(t)h. The same subtraction with several moved handles gives ΔC(t)=∑i​bi​(t)hi​. No claim about interpolation, closest-point search on a subdivision curve or endpoint motion follows until the actual basis values are examined.
