Higgs field

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Electroweak interaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 46
The Standard Model Higgs field is a complex SU(2)L​ doublet of hypercharge 1/2. Its nonzero vacuum expectation value triggers the Higgs mechanism.
The Higgs field is a scalar field: its components carry internal weak isospin and hypercharge, but no spacetime spin. A complex Higgs doublet contains four real field components. The vacuum expectation value is a property of the lowest-energy state, not a gas of Higgs bosons. A Higgs boson is an excitation about that state.
Why introduce this field? A bare mass term for a gauge field generally fails to respect the local gauge symmetry. A bare Dirac mass term for a charged Standard Model fermion also joins left- and right-handed fields with different Standard Model representations. A Higgs doublet permits gauge-invariant interactions whose expansion about a nonzero vacuum expectation value supplies both masses and definite Higgs boson interactions. The field therefore links the mass problem to experimentally testable coupling relations, as illustrated by the Higgs boson coupling to Z bosons.
