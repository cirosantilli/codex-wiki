Laplace median robustness example

OurBigBook.com Words: 178
Choose the five numbers 1,2,2,5,10 as an original illustration. Their sample median is 2 and their sample mean is 4. The sum of absolute losses at the sample median is
S(2)=∣1−2∣+∣2−2∣+∣2−2∣+∣5−2∣+∣10−2∣=1+0+0+3+8=12.
(78)
If these numbers were modeled as an independent and identically distributed Laplace distribution sample, the joint maximum-likelihood estimates would therefore be μ​=2 and b=12/5=2.4. Using the sample mean instead gives S(4)=14, so it does not maximize this likelihood function.
Now replace only 10 by 100. The sample median stays at 2, while the sample mean becomes (1+2+2+5+100)/5=22. The minimized sum of absolute losses rises to S(2)=102, and the fitted scale rises to b=102/5=20.4. Stability of the fitted location therefore does not mean stability of every fitted parameter.
The left panel subtracts each objective's minimum so the two absolute loss curves can be compared despite the different totals. The right panel tracks the sample mean and sample median as the largest chosen value changes. This is an outlier sensitivity illustration, not actual observations or a check that a Laplace distribution fits any population. In applications, regression residuals and the data-generating assumptions need separate examination; a stable sample median alone does not validate a statistical model.
