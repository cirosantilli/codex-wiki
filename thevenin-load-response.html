Thévenin load response (VL​=Voc​RL​/(Rth​+RL​))

 Home Electrical engineering Thévenin's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 169
A positive resistive electrical load obeys Ohm's law, VL​=IRL​. Its resistive load line intersects the Thévenin terminal response at
I=Rth​+RL​Voc​​,VL​=Rth​+RL​Voc​RL​​,
(38)
provided Rth​+RL​=0. For the Thévenin divider example, RL​=1kΩ gives I=24/7mA and VL​=24/7V; RL​=4kΩ gives I=1.5mA and VL​=6V. At RL​=Rth​=34​kΩ, the voltage is 4V, half the open-circuit voltage.
For this positive Rth​, increasing RL​ reduces the drawn electric current and raises VL​ toward Voc​. This explains voltmeter loading: a voltmeter itself is an electrical load. The equivalence also applies to a nonlinear electrical load through intersection with its terminal relation, but an arbitrary load need not give a unique operating point. If negative Rth​ cancels positive RL​, the displayed formula is singular; a real active circuit's limits and dynamics must then be considered.
