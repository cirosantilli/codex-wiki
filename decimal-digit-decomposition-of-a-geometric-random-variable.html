Decimal digit decomposition of a geometric random variable (N=bY+X)

The same argument works for any integer base b≥2. Suppose N has geometric distribution P(N=n)=(1−q)qn, n≥0, with 0<q<1. Its Euclidean division N=bY+X gives 0≤X<b and Y≥0. Their joint probability mass function is
pX,Y​(x,y)=(1−q)qx+by=1−qb(1−q)qx​(1−qb)qby.
(23)
The factors are normalized probability mass functions, so the final digit X and quotient Y are independent. The quotient again has geometric distribution, with success probability 1−qb. If N is the integer part of an exponential random variable of rate λ, take q=e−λ.
