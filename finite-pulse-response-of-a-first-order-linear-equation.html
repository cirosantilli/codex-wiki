Finite-pulse response of a first-order linear equation (y(t)=A[λeλ(t−a)−1​H(t−a)−λeλ(t−b)−1​H(t−b)])

OurBigBook.com Words: 102
Let 0<a<b and y′−λy=A[H(t−a)−H(t−b)], with zero initial data. For λ=0, the Laplace transform time-shift rule gives the displayed response, since the transform of the input is A(e−as−e−bs)/s and the equation supplies the factor (s−λ)−1. Equivalently, integrate the impulse response eλtH(t) over the active pulse. Before a the solution is zero; during the pulse it is A(eλ(t−a)−1)/λ; after b it is Aeλt(e−λa−e−λb)/λ. The solution is continuous at both switching times. At λ=0 take the limit, giving linear growth during the pulse and the constant A(b−a) afterward. Positive λ gives growing memory, while negative λ gives decay after forcing stops.
