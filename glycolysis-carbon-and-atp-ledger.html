Glycolysis carbon and ATP ledger

 Home Chemistry Biochemistry Metabolic pathway Glycolysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 330
Follow one glucose through canonical glycolysis, with no diversion of intermediates into other metabolic pathways. The investment stage uses two ATP to prepare the six-carbon substrate. Cleavage and rearrangement provide two glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate molecules, each containing three carbon chemical atoms. Each then yields one three-carbon pyruvate, one NADH, and two ATP in the payoff stage.[ref]
The material check is 6=2×3: all six carbon chemical atoms remain in the two pyruvates. No carbon dioxide has been released within this boundary. In the ATP ledger, each three-carbon route contains two ATP-forming transfers, so
gross ATP=2×2=4,net ATP=4−2=2.
(9)
The first pair of payoff transfers only restores the initial ATP inventory; the second pair creates the net gain. Four newly formed ATP is therefore compatible with only two additional ATP remaining.
For an original scaled example, stipulate complete conversion of 5μmol of glucose. The initial 30μmol of carbon chemical atoms becomes 10μmol of pyruvate, still containing 30μmol of carbon chemical atoms. The pathway spends 10μmol of ATP, forms 20μmol of ATP, and adds 10μmol to the ATP inventory before any other ATP use. It also reduces 10μmol of NAD+. These are calculated ideal amounts, not observed yields; side routes or incomplete conversion change the outcome.
A common biochemical net chemical equation is
glucose+2ADP+2Pi​+2NAD+⟶​2pyruvate+2ATP+2NADH+2H++2H2​O.​
(10)
Here Pi​ is inorganic phosphate. The names group biochemical protonation and metal-binding states; the displayed proton and water terms use a common shorthand rather than a unique specification of every chemical species. The robust ledger records two net ADP-to-ATP conversions and two NAD+-to-NADH conversions. Summing the individual chemical equations cancels two invested ATP against four formed ATP and cancels the internal substrates, giving those coefficients.
