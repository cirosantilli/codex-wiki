An exact P(k)=A/k3 has divergent unsubtracted point variance. Its finite isotropic covariance difference is the displayed logarithm, unchanged by simultaneous dilation of r,r0​. Thus the nonzero-mode scaling statement has a natural subtraction interpretation. Exact dilation invariance of a continuous finite-variance isotropic covariance itself would instead force a constant covariance, corresponding only to a zero-mode spectral measure. Cutoffs or a zero-mode subtraction must accompany the nontrivial spectrum.
