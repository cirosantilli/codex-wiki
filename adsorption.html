Adsorption

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 26
Adsorption is accumulation of a substance at an interface. Binding a solute to a packing surface can delay its passage through a chromatography column. Ideal size-exclusion chromatography separates through SEC pore access without such binding; extra retention from adsorption requires a different interpretation.
For a gas contacting a solid, adsorption concentrates molecules at the boundary; absorption takes material into the bulk. A porous solid can have a large internal surface area: binding on pore walls is still adsorption, even though those walls lie inside the particle. Both processes can occur together. Desorption returns adsorbed material to the surrounding phase. The useful question is therefore not just how much material disappeared from the gas, but where it went and whether it returns when the pressure falls.
An adsorption site model turns this spatial distinction into counting. With twelve identical adsorption sites and three bound molecules, the surface coverage is θ=3/12=1/4. Filling six gives θ=1/2; filling twelve gives a complete monolayer. These are configurations illustrating the count, rather than measured arrangements or claims that all real surfaces have twelve sites.
