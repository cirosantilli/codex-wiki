The displayed oscillator expansion and the displayed action require two separate corrections to describe one conventional positive-energy theory. First compute what the oscillator algebra literally implies. Assume the two oscillator species commute with each other and annihilate the vacuum. Same-charge contractions vanish, so
⟨Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)⟩0​=⟨Tϕ∗(x)ϕ∗(y)⟩0​=0.​
(41)
For the two mixed contractions the printed phases give the same kernel, with z=x−y:
Clit​(z)​=θ(z0)∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​e+ip⋅z+θ(−z0)∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​e−ip⋅z=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2−i0−ie−ip⋅z​.​
(42)
Thus ⟨Tϕ(x)ϕ∗(y)⟩0​=⟨Tϕ∗(x)ϕ(y)⟩0​=Clit​(z). In D dimensions replace d3p and d4p by dD−1p and dDp; the source's explicit three-dimensional spatial measure is the D=4 specialization. The covariant integral is manifestly Lorentz invariant. With
DF​(z)=∫(2π)DdDp​p2−m2+i0ie−ip⋅z​,
(43)
we have Clit​(z)=DF​(z)∗. This reversed-frequency scalar contraction has the opposite pole prescription to the usual positive-energy Feynman propagator. For a single zero-momentum mode at positive time separation, the printed contraction is e+imt/(2m), while DF​ is e−imt/(2m): this is a concrete phase counterexample, rather than an immaterial change of integration variable.
There is also a normalization of a complex scalar propagator conflict independent of those phases. The displayed Lagrangian density gives the canonical momentum πϕ​=ϕ˙​∗/2. Substituting the printed modes yields
[ϕ(t,x),πϕ​(t,y)]=−2i​δ(3)(x−y),
(44)
instead of the canonical +iδ(3). Reversing the mode phases repairs the sign but leaves +iδ(3)/2, so it does not repair the normalization. The complex scalar field convention with unit mixed propagator normally has no 1/2 in its quadratic action.[ref]
To retain the actual action in the paper, use ϕ=ϕ1​+iϕ2​ with canonically normalized real components, or equivalently replace its mode expansion by
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)D−1Ep​​dD−1p​(ap​e−ip⋅x+bp†​eip⋅x).
(45)
The corresponding path integral with the usual Feynman i-epsilon prescription has
C(x,y)≡⟨Tϕ(x)ϕ∗(y)⟩0​=⟨Tϕ∗(x)ϕ(y)⟩0​=2DF​(x−y),​
(46)
with the two same-charge propagators still zero. This action-consistent kernel is used in b and c. Keeping conventional unit modes instead would require removing the quadratic factors 1/2 from the action as well as reversing the phases. Those are distinct theories unless the field and its coupling are rescaled together.
