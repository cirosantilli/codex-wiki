Divide the infinitesimal variation by its constant parameter α, so the transformation generator is (δϕ1​,δϕ2​)=(ϕ2​,−ϕ1​). The variation of the kinetic term is
∂μ​ϕ1​∂μϕ2​−∂μ​ϕ2​∂μϕ1​=0,
(1)
and that of ϕ12​+ϕ22​ is 2ϕ1​ϕ2​−2ϕ2​ϕ1​=0. Thus the Lagrangian density is invariant pointwise, without a nonzero boundary current. The finite transformation is a rotation of the two real scalar fields, giving an SO(2) global symmetry in field theory.
Noether's theorem supplies the parameter-independent Noether current
jμ=ϕ2​∂μϕ1​−ϕ1​∂μϕ2​.​
(2)
Its divergence is ∂μ​jμ=ϕ2​□ϕ1​−ϕ1​□ϕ2​, since the first-derivative terms cancel. Both fields obey the same Klein-Gordon equation, so this vanishes on solutions. With spatial boundary conditions giving zero flux at infinity, the Noether charge is
Q=∫d3x(ϕ2​ϕ˙​1​−ϕ1​ϕ˙​2​),Q˙​=0.​
(3)
A finite box with periodic boundary conditions works as well. The spatial integral is conserved because the local continuity equation converts its time derivative into a boundary flux; neither vanishing local current nor constant individual fields is required. Equivalently, Φ=(ϕ1​+iϕ2​)/2​ transforms as Φ↦e−iαΦ, exhibiting the equivalent global U(1) symmetry and fixing the sign of the generator.

Take the free squared mass m2≥0, as for the ordinary massive free theory. The quartic part of the scalar potential can be written
V4​=λ(ϕ12​−ϕ22​)2+2(λ+κ)ϕ12​ϕ22​.
(10)
It is nonnegative for every real field value exactly when
λ≥0,λ+κ≥0.​
(11)
Necessity follows from the axis ϕ2​=0 and the diagonal ϕ1​=ϕ2​: a negative coefficient on either ray makes V tend to minus infinity under rescaling. The displayed sum proves sufficiency, since m2(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)/2 is also nonnegative. This is a copositive matrix condition on the quadratic form in the nonnegative variables (ϕ12​,ϕ22​), rather than positive definiteness on all independent signed variables.
The strict inequalities λ>0, κ>−λ make the quartic part grow in every nonzero direction. They are not necessary for a stable vacuum when m2>0. For example, λ=κ=0 gives two stable free massive fields; λ>0, κ=−λ has a flat quartic diagonal but a confining quadratic term. If m2=0, boundary cases have flat vacuum directions and need not have a unique isolated minimum. If one instead allows negative squared mass, a flat quartic ray makes the full potential unbounded below; the stability conditions must then be strengthened. This distinguishes boundedness from strict quartic coercivity.
Under the generator of a, the variation is
δV4​=4(λ−κ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(12)
It vanishes identically if and only if κ=λ. In that case V4​=λ(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2 preserves the entire global U(1) symmetry. More explicitly, the interacting Euler-Lagrange equations give
∂μ​jμ=−δV4​=4(κ−λ)ϕ1​ϕ2​(ϕ12​−ϕ22​).
(13)
Hence
Q is a conserved operator of the interacting theory exactly when κ=λ.​
(14)
A special solution lying on an axis does not establish an operator conservation law. Among the stable couplings, the symmetry-preserving line is κ=λ≥0.

Expand the normalized Euclidean path integral with weight e−SE​ and apply Wick's theorem. Use labelled external momenta and a fixed Fourier transform convention throughout. A line carrying momentum q contributes (q2+m02​)−1. Each 2k-valent interaction vertex contributes −λ2k​=−μdk​g2k​: its (2k)! possible contractions cancel the factorial in the Lagrangian density. A vertex also carries (2π)Dδ(D)(∑q) with all incident momenta incoming. Integrate each independent loop momentum with dDq/(2π)D and divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor for fixed external labels. There are no Minkowski factors of i.
For the full N-point Green's function, include every graph in which all vertices belong to components attached to external insertions. In particular, disconnected components carrying external insertions must be included, and the zeroth-order result is the sum of free pairings. Pure vacuum bubbles cancel against the denominator. The connected Green's function restricts to connected graphs, while a one-particle-irreducible vertex imposes the stronger restriction that removing one internal line cannot disconnect the graph. These are different objects. Odd N vanishes in the symmetric vacuum because every interaction has even valence. At fixed perturbative order, multiply by the appropriate power of the coupling and sum all allowed graph topologies and external attachments.
If the overall momentum-conservation delta function is removed by convention, report the remaining function as its coefficient. The ordinary unamputated Green's function includes external scalar propagators; dividing them out defines an amputated function. The definition in c performs this amputation but does not, by itself, impose the usual one-particle-irreducibility condition.

A phi-fourth theory interaction has mass dimension 4−D, so its bare coefficient must contain a renormalization scale factor μ4−D=(μ2)2−D/2 when expressed through a dimensionless renormalized coupling. The tree-level four-leg interaction vertex is linear in that coupling. At the next order, two quartic vertices form the three one-loop bubble diagram graphs shown in c, with external pairings 12∣34, 13∣24 and 14∣23.
For each pairing there are two identical internal lines and Feynman-diagram symmetry factor 2. The loop integral has the large-momentum behaviour
∫(2π)DdDq​q2(q+P)21​∼∫∞dqqD−5.
(30)
It is logarithmically divergent in four dimensions. Dimensional regularization represents that local divergence by a simple pole proportional to 1/(D−4). Its residue is independent of external momenta, so a local quartic counterterm cancels it. The three graphs sum to one constant coefficient; this argument establishes its structure without requiring its value.
The relation consequently has the form
λ0​=μ4−D[g+D−4Cg2​+O(g3)].​
(31)
The sign of C incorporates the chosen pole denominator and vertex convention. In particular, a negative C here corresponds to a positive coefficient of 1/(4−D) in the bare coupling. Finite pieces depend on the renormalization scheme; a pure-pole relation is natural in minimal subtraction scheme. One-loop two-point tadpole diagrams contribute mass renormalization, but not the momentum-dependent field renormalization that could change this coupling relation at order g2. The four-point object relevant here is the amputated one-particle-irreducible vertex; external-leg self-energy insertions are dealt with separately.

The tree term has Feynman-diagram symmetry factor 1 and is −iλ∫dDzP(z).
At second order, a connected graph has two quartic vertices, two internal lines and one loop. The three bubble integrals are
Bs​=Bt​=Bu​=​∫z,w​C(x1​,z)C(x2​,z)C(z,w)2C(w,y1​)C(w,y2​),∫z,w​C(x1​,z)C(z,y1​)C(z,w)C(w,z)C(x2​,w)C(w,y2​),∫z,w​C(x1​,z)C(z,y2​)C(z,w)C(w,z)C(x2​,w)C(w,y1​).​
(55)
The oriented complex-scalar quartic graph factors distinguish the channels. In Bs​ the two internal lines have the same orientation and can be interchanged, giving symmetry factor Ss​=2. In each mixed channel the two orientations are opposite; interchanging them is not an oriented graph automorphism, so St​=Su​=1. Equality of the numerical scalar kernels C(z,w)=C(w,z) does not erase the field-slot distinction used in counting contractions.
There are also four connected external-leg tadpole diagrams. They must be included because a connected correlation function is neither amputated nor restricted to one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams. Representative integrals, with the other two obtained by the indicated external permutations, are
Tx1​​=Tx2​​=Ty1​​=Ty2​​=​∫z,w​C(x1​,z)C(z,z)C(z,w)C(x2​,w)C(w,y1​)C(w,y2​),Tx1​​​x1​↔x2​​,∫z,w​C(x1​,w)C(x2​,w)C(w,y2​)C(w,z)C(z,z)C(z,y1​),Ty1​​​y1​↔y2​​.​
(56)
Each oriented external-leg graph has symmetry factor 1: a directed self-loop has no independent reversal symmetry. Altogether,
Gc(4)​=​−iλ∫dDzP(z)+(−iλ)2[21​Bs​+Bt​+Bu​+Tx1​​+Tx2​​+Ty1​​+Ty2​​]+O(λ3).​​
(57)
These expressions retain every external propagator. The physical normalization is explicit: each C is 2DF​, not DF​. Equivalently set χ=ϕ/2​; its quadratic action has unit mixed propagator and its interaction is −λ∣χ∣4, giving vertex −4iλ. Multiplying a four-χ correlator by four converts it to the four-ϕ correlator, confirming the powers of two.
Completeness can also be seen without a picture. With two vertices and four external legs, I=(8−4)/2=2. Connectivity allows either two intervertex lines with a two-and-two external split, or one bridge and one self-loop with a one-and-three split. The charge assignments give exactly the three bubble attachments and four leg choices above. Direct labelled Wick contraction counting gives respectively 16,32,32,32,32,32,32 contractions. Dividing by the expansion denominator 2!42=32 reproduces 1/2,1,1,1,1,1,1. Vacuum factors cancel, and there are no additional connected topologies at this order.
