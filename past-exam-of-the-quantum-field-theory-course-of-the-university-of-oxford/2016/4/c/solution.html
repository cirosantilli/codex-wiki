It is convenient to evaluate the one-particle irreducible vertices using the Euclidean action and then translate the counterterms back to the Minkowski spacetime Lagrangian density. For the tadpole integral, analytic continuation of solution gives
ID,1​=(4π)2−ϵ/2Γ(−1+ϵ/2)​(m2)1−ϵ/2=−8π2ϵm2​+O(1).
(42)
Indeed Γ(−1+ϵ/2)=Γ(ϵ/2)/(ϵ/2−1)=−2/ϵ+O(1). The negative pole is a property of analytic continuation, not the value of a convergent positive integral.
The Euclidean space inverse two-point vertex has the one-loop correction
ΓE(2)​(pE​)=pE2​+m2+2λ0​​ID,1​+O(λ02​),ΓE(2)​​pole​=−16π2ϵλ0​m2​.
(43)
The factor 1/2 is the symmetry factor weight of the tadpole diagram. A mass counterterm δm2=λ0​m2/(16π2ϵ) cancels this pole. There is no momentum-dependent pole at this order, and hence no one-loop wave-function renormalization.
For the four-point vertex, the three bubble diagrams give
ΓE(4)​=λ0​−2λ02​​(Bs​+Bt​+Bu​)+O(λ03​),ΓE(4)​​pole​=−16π2ϵ3λ02​​.
(44)
One can check the relative minus sign independently from the one-loop effective action: its 21​Trlog(1+Gλ0​ϕ2/2) term has a positive term linear in λ0​ϕ2 and a negative term quadratic in it. Thus δλ=3λ02​/(16π2ϵ) cancels the quartic pole.
In the Euclidean action, these two counterterms are +δm2ϕ2/2+δλϕ4/4!. Returning to the Minkowski spacetime Lagrangian density gives
Lct​=−21​16π2ϵλ0​m2​ϕ2−4!1​16π2ϵ3λ02​​ϕ4.​
(45)
For a direct sign check with Feynman rules, the two amputated loop pole insertions are +iλ0​m2/(16π2ϵ) and +i3λ02​/(16π2ϵ), while the counterterm insertions are −iδm2 and −iδλ.
As usual, the displayed pole residues suppress renormalization scale powers: [λ0​]=ϵ away from four dimensions. The next part restores these powers explicitly with a dimensionless renormalized coupling. Replacing the tree coupling by its renormalized counterpart inside a one-loop residue changes only higher-loop terms.
