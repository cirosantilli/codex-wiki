Write S=S0​+SI​, where S0​ contains the quadratic kinetic term and mass term, and SI​=−λ∫dDxϕ(x)3/3!. Then
eiS=eiS0​∑r=0∞​r!(3!)r(−iλ)r​∫∏a=1r​dDxa​∏a=1r​ϕ(xa​)3.
(28)
Let Z0​=∫DϕeiS0​ and let ⟨⋅⟩0​ denote the unit-normalized free Gaussian vacuum expectation value. The unnormalized numerator NN​ is consequently
Z0​NN​​=∑r=0∞​r!(3!)r(−iλ)r​∫∏a=1r​dDxa​⟨Tϕ(y1​)⋯ϕ(yN​)∏a=1r​ϕ(xa​)3⟩0​.
(29)
The Minkowski path integral is understood with the usual scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription, as a formal perturbation series. A real cubic potential energy is unbounded below; no nonperturbative stable vacuum is being assumed here.
Set Δ(x−z)=⟨Tϕ(x)ϕ(z)⟩0​, Ki​(x)=Δ(yi​−x), and t=Δ(0), with a common regularization understood for coincident points. The zero-order one-point term has one field and vanishes. At first order, Wick's theorem gives three choices for the contraction with the external field, leaving a tadpole diagram:
⟨Tϕ(y1​)ϕ(x)3⟩0​=3K1​(x)t.
(30)
The second-order term contains seven fields, so it vanishes by the vanishing odd correlators of a free Gaussian field. Hence
N1​=Z0​[2−iλ​∫dDxK1​(x)t+O(λ3)].​
(31)
The coefficient is 3/3!=1/2, or symmetry factor S=2 when each diagram is weighted by 1/S. No spacetime integral has been evaluated. The one-point tadpole diagram is shown in the first panel of the following figure; its other panels organize solution.
