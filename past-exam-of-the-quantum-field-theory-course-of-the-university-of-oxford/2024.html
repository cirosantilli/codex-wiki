Assume mψ​>0, as required to have a rest frame. Choose the Lorentz boost Λ for which Λp=(mψ​,0), and write its unitary operator as U. The stipulated one-particle state normalization gives U∣ψ,p⟩=∣ψ,0⟩ without a momentum-dependent prefactor. A Lorentz scalar at the origin satisfies Uϕ(0)U†=ϕ(0), and the vacuum state satisfies U∣Ω⟩=∣Ω⟩. Inserting U†U therefore proves
⟨Ω∣ϕ(0)∣ψ,p⟩=⟨Ω∣ϕ(0)∣ψ,0⟩.​
(13)
With a noncovariant normalization of momentum eigenstates, the assumed boost law would instead contain a square-root energy factor, so this equality is specifically tied to the paper's assumption.
Let qμ=(Eqχ​,q) denote the four-momentum of the other energy eigenstate. Translation covariance first gives
⟨χ,q∣ϕ(x)∣ψ,p⟩=ei(q−p)⋅x⟨χ,q∣ϕ(0)∣ψ,p⟩=ei(q−p)⋅x⟨Uχ,q∣ϕ(0)∣ψ,0⟩.
(14)
Thus a suitable ket, including the phase whose complex conjugate appears in its bra, is
∣Ψ⟩=e−i(q−p)⋅xU∣χ,q⟩.​
(15)
Here ⟨Uχ,q∣ means (U∣χ,q⟩)†. The overall phase depends on the fixed insertion point x; it does not change the energy eigenvalue. It cannot simply be dropped if the equality of complex matrix elements is to hold exactly.
The transformed state has four-momentum q′=Λq. Lorentz invariance of the Minkowski inner product gives
p⋅q=(Λp)⋅(Λq)=mψ​q′0,EΨ=mψ​p⋅q​=mψ​Ep​Eqχ​−p⋅q​.​
(16)
There is no boost to a rest frame for a nonzero massless four-momentum, so the rest-state statements require the positive-mass assumption.

Interpret the repeated species label in the Yukawa interaction as summed over i=1,2: each Feynman vertex is diagonal in species and equals −ig. Define the invariant scattering amplitude by S−1=i(2π)4δ(4)(Pf​−Pi​)M. There is one tree-level Feynman diagram, an s-channel scalar exchange. In the left panel, j=2 for this part; the right panel only occurs in d. Arrows show fermion-number flow, so an antifermion arrow opposes its physical momentum.
Writing k=p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​ and s=k2, the momentum-space Feynman rules give
iM=[vˉs2​(p2​)(−ig)us1​(p1​)]s−M2+i0i​[uˉs3​(p3​)(−ig)vs4​(p4​)],M=−s−M2+i0g2[vˉ2​u1​][uˉ3​v4​]​.​
(24)
The scalar carries no spin label. Average over the 2×2 incoming spin states and sum the four outgoing combinations. For the real scalar Yukawa interaction, the conjugate of vˉ2​u1​ is uˉ1​v2​. The fermion spin sums then yield
∣M∣2​=4(s−M2)2g4​tr[(p2​−m)(p1​+m)]tr[(p3​+m)(p4​−m)].
(25)
The bar denotes the initial spin average, not a Dirac adjoint. The gamma matrix trace identities give
tr[(p2​−m)(p1​+m)]=4(p1​⋅p2​−m2)=2(s−4m2),
(26)
and the second trace has the same value because p3​+p4​=p1​+p2​ and all external masses are m. Therefore
∣M∣2​=g4(s−M2)2(s−4m2)2​=g4(M2−s4m2−s​)2.​
(27)
This is the fixed-order expression away from s=M2. If that pole is accessible, a physical scattering resonance requires a scalar self-energy and decay width; the tree formula alone is not finite there.

For a single species both panels of the figure in c contribute, now with j=1. They are the s-channel annihilation and t-channel scalar exchange, where the Mandelstam variables are s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​−p3​)2, u=(p1​−p4​)2 and s+t+u=4m2. Fix the external-state order consistently with c. The exchange of identical external fermionic operators gives a relative minus sign:
M=−g2(s−M2+i0(vˉ2​u1​)(uˉ3​v4​)​−t−M2+i0(uˉ3​u1​)(vˉ2​v4​)​).​
(28)
Changing the convention for ordering all external states can change the overall sign, but not this relative fermionic sign.
The exchange p1​↔−p4​ must include crossing symmetry the associated external spinors, as well as continuing the four-momenta. It interchanges s and t, leaves u fixed, and interchanges the two spinor pairings. Hence the analytically continued invariant scattering amplitude is antisymmetric:
M⟼−M,∣M∣2​(s,t)=∣M∣2​(t,s).​
(29)
The first relation is understood with consistently chosen spinor crossing phases. It is not a substitution of negative-energy arguments into the original positive-energy u spinors without changing external states.
Without repeating the spin sum calculation, c fixes the square of the first diagram. Crossing symmetry fixes the second by s↔t. Consequently the strongest immediate inference is
∣M∣2​=g4[(s−M2)2(s−4m2)2​+(t−M2)2(t−4m2)2​+(s−M2)(t−M2)C(s,t)​],C(s,t)=C(t,s).​
(30)
The interference function is not determined by the separate diagram squares. In particular adding the two squares and dropping interference is unjustified. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the arrays of spin amplitudes also gives ∣C(s,t)∣≤2∣(s−4m2)(t−4m2)∣ on the physical scattering region.
For completeness, its value can be checked with one contracted trace, without another diagram calculation. The cross product is
K=41​tr[(p2​−m)(p1​+m)(p3​+m)(p4​−m)]=−21​st−2m2(s+t)+8m4.
(31)
The relative minus in the invariant scattering amplitude gives C=−2K. Thus the fully evaluated result, beyond the symmetry inference alone, is
∣M∣2​=g4[(s−M2)2(s−4m2)2​+(t−M2)2(t−4m2)2​+(s−M2)(t−M2)st+4m2(s+t)−16m4​].​
(32)
Every term respects the inferred s,t symmetry. In the massless limit the interference numerator reduces to st, which is nonzero at a generic scattering angle.

In 2+1 spacetime dimensions, the dimensionless action gives [L]=3 and [∂μ​]=1. The scalar kinetic term requires 2[ϕ]+2=3, whereas the fermion kinetic term requires 2[ψ]+1=3. Thus the engineering dimensions of relativistic fields are
[ϕ]=21​,[ψ]=[ψˉ​]=1.
(33)
Matching each interaction to mass dimension three gives
[g1​]=3−(2[ϕ]+2[ψ])=0,[g2​]=3−4[ϕ]=1,[g3​]=3−6[ϕ]=0.​
(34)
Here the brackets mean exponents of mass in natural units. The coefficients g1​,g3​ are marginal couplings by classical power counting, and g2​ is a relevant coupling.

Let Ib​,If​ count internal scalar and fermion lines and L count independent loops in a connected amputated Feynman diagram. At large loop momentum, the scalar propagator falls as k−2 and the Dirac propagator falls as k−1, including its numerator. Each loop integration is d3k. The nonderivative interaction vertices add no powers. Therefore
ω=3L−2Ib​−If​.
(35)
Count endpoints of each kind of line and use the connected-graph loop order identity:
2Ib​+b=2n1​+4n2​+6n3​,2If​+f=2n1​,L=Ib​+If​−(n1​+n2​+n3​)+1.
(36)
Eliminating L,Ib​,If​ gives
ω​=Ib​+2If​−3(n1​+n2​+n3​)+3=(n1​+2n2​+3n3​−2b​)+2(n1​−2f​)−3(n1​+n2​+n3​)+3=3−2b​−f−n2​.​​
(37)
The cancellation of n1​,n3​ reflects their dimensionless couplings; every insertion of the positive-dimensional g2​ improves the superficial degree of divergence by one.
The printed exact homogeneity is shorthand for ultraviolet power counting, not an identity for a massive integrated correlation function. For example, even the free amputated scalar two-point vertex Γ(2)(p)=p2−M2 obeys Γ(2)(λp)=λ2p2−M2, which differs from λ2Γ(2)(p) when M=0. Logarithmic divergences, actual numerator cancellations and ultraviolet subdivergences also require separate treatment.

Use one-particle-irreducible correlation functions to identify independent local counterterms. The exact internal symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ excludes odd b, and Dirac fermion number conservation requires equal numbers of ψ and ψˉ​, so f is even. From b, the complete list of potentially overall divergent vertices with external legs is:
  • Γϕϕ​: (b,f)=(2,0), ω=2−n2​, potentially divergent for n2​≤2.
  • Γϕ4​: (b,f)=(4,0), ω=1−n2​, potentially divergent for n2​≤1.
  • Γϕ6​: (b,f)=(6,0), ω=−n2​, potentially divergent only for n2​=0.
  • Γψˉ​ψ​: (b,f)=(0,2), ω=1−n2​, potentially divergent for n2​≤1.
  • Γϕ2ψˉ​ψ​: (b,f)=(2,2), ω=−n2​, potentially divergent only for n2​=0.
The zero-leg vacuum diagrams, with ω=3−n2​, may also require a vacuum energy density subtraction. Four-fermion vertices have ω≤−1 and do not require a new four-fermion counterterm. These are superficial bounds: a particular allowed diagram can vanish or be convergent.
Locality and Lorentz invariance restrict the divergent parts to a finite basis. The scalar two-point vertex can contain a constant and p2; there is no Lorentz scalar linear in a single momentum. The fermion two-point vertex can contain I and p. The remaining listed vertices have no allowed derivative divergence. A complete counterterm Lagrangian, up to total derivatives, is therefore
Lct​=​−δΛ+2δZϕ​​(∂μ​ϕ)(∂μϕ)−2δM2​ϕ2+δZψ​ψˉ​iγμ∂μ​ψ−δmψˉ​ψ−2δg1​​ϕ2ψˉ​ψ−4!δg2​​ϕ4−6!δg3​​ϕ6.​​
(38)
The coefficients supply wavefunction renormalization, mass counterterms and renormalizations of the three interactions already present. Recursive subtraction of ultraviolet subdivergences then leaves only these local overall divergences. This finite counterterm closure is why the complete theory is expected to have perturbative renormalizability. It does not mean every listed coefficient first becomes nonzero at one loop, or that setting an allowed coupling to zero is stable under renormalization.

Set the renormalized g3​ to zero. For a six-scalar vertex, b gives ω=−n2​, so a new overall logarithmic divergence can only occur in graphs with n2​=0. With b=6,f=0, the line counts become If​=n1​ and Ib​=n1​−3, hence L=n1​−2.
At one loop this forces n1​=3 and Ib​=0: the only candidate is a closed triangle Feynman diagram with two external scalar legs at each vertex. Its numerator at large loop momentum is proportional to
tr[(k)3]=k2tr(k)=0.
(39)
This vanishes for the specified two-component gamma matrices, each of which is traceless. Replacing one leading k by the fermion mass or an external momentum lowers the superficial degree of divergence from zero to at most −1. Thus the apparent logarithm cancels. Graphs with a g2​ vertex already have negative overall degree; the pure-scalar one-loop triangle, for instance, has ω=−3. There is no one-loop ultraviolet subdivergence inside these proper one-loop vertices. Therefore
δg3one loop​=0.​
(40)
The six-point correlation function need not vanish: finite terms are allowed. Nor is it correct to say that all odd gamma-matrix traces vanish in 2+1 dimensions. In this representation,
γ0γ1γ2=−iI2​,tr(γ0γ1γ2)=−2i=0.
(41)
The cancellation above follows from repeating the same leading loop momentum, not from a false four-dimensional trace rule.
At two loops, n1​=4, If​=4 and Ib​=1 are possible. A closed fermion box with a scalar line joining two of its vertices leaves six external scalar legs. It has L=2, ω=0, and four leading gamma matrices; the one-loop odd-numerator argument no longer applies.
A background-field check demonstrates that its logarithm is actually nonzero. Put ϕ=v+η with constant v. Define the background fermion mass μ=m+g1​v2/2, the background scalar squared mass B2=M2+g2​v2/2, and the induced three-leg Yukawa coupling y=g1​v. The two-loop quantum effective potential contains the sunset diagram with two fermion lines and one scalar line. After a Wick rotation, its relevant integral, apart from a nonzero overall combinatorial factor, is
y2∫(2π)6d3kd3l​(k2+μ2)(l2+μ2)((k−l)2+B2)2(−k⋅l+μ2)​.
(42)
Here the dots and gamma-matrix traces are Euclidean. With A=k2+μ2, B=l2+μ2, C=(k−l)2+B2, its numerator is exactly
2(−k⋅l+μ2)=−A−B+C+4μ2−B2.
(43)
The first three terms reduce to products of one-loop tadpole integrals, which have no logarithmic pole near three dimensions in dimensional regularization. The remaining term is
y2(4μ2−B2)H,H=∫(2π)6d3kd3l​ABC1​.
(44)
For positive regulator masses, the large-momentum region of H has a positive, nonzero logarithmic divergence. One can see this directly by restricting the relative angles and the ratio ∣k∣/∣l∣ to compact intervals away from zero: the remaining common radial integral is proportional to ∫Λdr/r. Its proper one-loop subgraphs are ultraviolet convergent.
Finally, the coefficient multiplying H contains
y2(4μ2−B2)=g12​v2[4m2−M2+(4mg1​−2g2​​)v2+g12​v4].
(45)
The two-loop sextic counterterm in quartic Yukawa theory follows because its v6 coefficient is g14​=0 for a nonzero coupling. Other two-loop vacuum topologies reduce to products of finite-in-dimensional-regularization tadpoles and do not cancel this logarithmic pole. Thus
δg3two loops​ is generically nonzero and proportional to g14​.​
(46)
A sextic counterterm is required even if the renormalized sextic coupling is set to zero at a chosen renormalization scale. A theory constrained to g3​=0 at every scale lacks counterterm closure when g1​=0. The decoupled exception g1​=0 is the three-dimensional scalar quartic interaction, which needs no sextic counterterm by power counting.
