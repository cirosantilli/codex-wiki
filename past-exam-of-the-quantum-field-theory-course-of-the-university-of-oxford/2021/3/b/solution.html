Let L be the number of independent loop momenta in a connected Feynman diagram. Every scalar propagator falls as k−2, and every loop momentum integration contributes three powers, so ω=3L−2e. Counting line ends and using connectedness gives
4n4​+6n6​=2e+n,L=e−n4​−n6​+1.
(26)
Eliminating L and e yields
ω=3(e−n4​−n6​+1)−2e=3−2n​−n4​.​
(27)
Thus increasing the sextic interaction vertex count does not worsen the superficial degree of divergence; adding a quartic interaction vertex improves it. Only finitely many external-line structures can have an overall ultraviolet divergence, even though arbitrarily high loop order is possible.
After subtracting ultraviolet subdivergences, locality and Lorentz invariance restrict the divergent counterterms to a constant vacuum energy density, a mass counterterm, a wave-function renormalization, and quartic and sextic field interaction terms. Odd powers are forbidden by the ϕ↦−ϕ Z2 symmetry. In particular, n=2 permits a constant and p2, n=4 permits only a constant, and n=6 permits only a constant. This is perturbative renormalizability, not a guarantee that every superficially allowed divergence actually occurs or that every unrenormalized graph with ω<0 lacks divergent subgraphs.
