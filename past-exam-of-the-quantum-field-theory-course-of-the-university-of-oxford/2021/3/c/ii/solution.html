To include all momentum-dependent contributions to the two-point function, put one external line at each interaction vertex. Let r be the number of lines connecting the vertices, and tA​,tB​ their numbers of tadpole diagrams. For vertex valences vA​,vB​∈{4,6},
r+2tA​=vA​−1,r+2tB​=vB​−1.
(30)
The integer r is odd, and r=1 gives a one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram and is excluded. Thus the complete list is
  • vA​=vB​=4: r=3, tA​=tB​=0, the sunset diagram.
  • vA​=4,vB​=6: r=3, tA​=0,tB​=1, the sunset diagram with one tadpole diagram.
  • vA​=vB​=6: r=3, tA​=tB​=1, the sunset diagram with one tadpole diagram at each vertex.
  • vA​=vB​=6: r=5, tA​=tB​=0, the five-line banana integral topology.
The coefficient of the mixed graph includes the reflected external attachment as well as its reciprocal Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. If both external lines attach to the same interaction vertex, no external momentum enters the connecting internal lines, and that Feynman diagram cannot contribute to wave-function renormalization.
For incoming external momentum p, assign the connecting lines momenta k1​,…,kr−1​,p−∑j​kj​. Define
Br,M​(p)=∫∏j=1r−1​(2π)3d3kj​​[∏j=1r−1​D(kj​)]D(p−∑j=1r−1​kj​),r=3,5.
(31)
The momentum space Feynman rules give, in the order listed,
​C44​(−ig1​)2B3,M​(p),C46​(−ig1​)(−ig2​)TM​B3,M​(p),C66,3​(−ig2​)2TM2​B3,M​(p),C66,5​(−ig2​)2B5,M​(p).​​
(32)
Each C is a reciprocal Feynman-diagram symmetry factor, including any equivalent external attachments. There are respectively 2,3,4,4 independent loop momenta.
If “contribute to wavefunction renormalization” means requiring a divergent kinetic counterterm after subtraction of all subdivergences, only the last topology qualifies. The first three still have nontrivial external momentum dependence and give finite contributions to a normalization condition; their raw divergent factors arise from mass counterterms and interaction vertex counterterms. Keeping all four here makes this distinction explicit. The ultraviolet divergence analysis in d establishes it directly.
