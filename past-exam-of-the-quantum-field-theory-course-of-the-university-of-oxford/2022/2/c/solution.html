It follows by crossing symmetry from the identical-fermion scattering amplitude in i, or directly by the two inequivalent external contractions of g2(ψˉ​ψ)2/2. The displayed answer in the source is false. At threshold, pi​=(m,0), hence s=4m2 and t=u=0. Then vˉu=uˉv=0, while the spin sums give ∣M∣2​=16g4m4. The printed expression instead gives 20g4m4. This is also a counterexample in the continuous threshold limit from physical nonzero incoming momenta. At m=0 there is a further discrepancy: the correct result is g4(s2+t2+u2)/2, rather than the printed g4(3s2+3t2−u2)/2. Changing a common phase of the scattering amplitude cannot change either comparison.
The requested s↔t symmetry nevertheless holds for the correct answer. Crossing symmetry exchanges the two ways of pairing the four external fermion lines; the momentum-independent auxiliary-field propagator supplies no channel-dependent denominator. The relative fermionic sign can reverse the crossed scattering amplitude, but disappears in its squared modulus. With u=4m2−s−t, the boxed expression is manifestly symmetric under s↔t. This is an analytic crossing relation, not a statement that positive physical s and negative physical t have interchangeable scattering kinematics.
