Define the constant Dirac gamma matrix combinations αk=γ0γk and β=γ0. The Hamiltonian operator is
H=∫d3yψb†​(y)(−iαbck​∂yk​+mβbc​)ψc​(y).
(34)
Evaluate its commutator with n(x) using the bilocal result in a. The mass term cancels immediately when z=y, because its two Dirac delta distributions coincide. For the derivative term first keep the two points distinct:
C(x)=−i∫d3yαbck​∂zk​{[δ(3)(x−y)−δ(3)(x−z)]ψb†​(y)ψc​(z)}​z=y​.
(35)
The terms in which the derivative acts on ψc​ cancel against each other; the derivative of the second Dirac delta distribution remains. Thus
C(x)=i∫d3yψ†(y)αkψ(y)∂yk​δ(3)(x−y)=−i∂xk​[ψ†(x)αkψ(x)].
(36)
The last equality is integration by parts, or the defining derivative rule for a distribution. Consequently the literal conserved current whose divergence equals C is
C=∇⋅J,Jk=−iψ†αkψ=−iψˉ​γkψ.​
(37)
Because a commutator of two Hermitian operators is anti-Hermitian, this J includes −i. The physical Hermitian Dirac current is instead jk=ψˉ​γkψ, so C=−i∇⋅j. The Heisenberg equation of motion now gives
∂t​n=i[H,n]=−iC=−∇⋅j,jμ=ψˉ​γμψ.​
(38)
This establishes the continuity equation directly from the required commutator, without assuming the Dirac equation or invoking the Noether theorem as a substitute for the computation.
