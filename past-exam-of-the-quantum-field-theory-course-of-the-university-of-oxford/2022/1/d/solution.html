The second supplied expansion is incorrect. Write A=p2−m2. Direct multiplication of the two denominators gives (A+k2)2−4(p⋅k)2, so the correct Taylor expansion is
[(p−k)2−m2][(p+k)2−m2]1​=A21​(1−A2k2​+A24(p⋅k)2​+O(k4)).

A concrete counterexample is p=0, m=1, k=(ϵ,0): the exact expression is (1−ϵ2)−2=1+2ϵ2+O(ϵ4), whereas the printed expansion gives 1+ϵ2/2+O(ϵ4). We therefore compute the self-energy without that identity.
For Euclidean external momentum P, define the scalar bubble integral
B(P)=∫(2π)6d6qE​​(qE2​+m2)((qE​+P)2+m2)1​.
(7)
A Feynman parameter x combines its denominators into one squared denominator with K=m2+x(1−x)P2. Applying the radial result above and integrating ∫01​x(1−x)dx=1/6 yields
B(P)​div​=C(Λ2−2m2LΛ​−3P2​LΛ​).

One can instead expand with the ultraviolet cutoff fixed on the unshifted loop momentum: the P2 coefficient of the integrand is −(qE2​+m2)−3+32​qE2​(qE2​+m2)−4, using rotational averaging in six dimensions. Its leading radial term is −1/(3qE6​), giving the same logarithm. Shifting a sharp integration boundary changes finite terms in this scalar bubble integral, and does not change the displayed divergent coefficients.
To fix all signs and symmetry factors together, expand the one-loop Euclidean effective action around a background scalar field:
Γ1,E​[ϕ]=21​Trlog(D+gϕ),D=−∂E2​+m2,

Γ1,E​[ϕ]−Γ1,E​[0]=2g​Tr(D−1ϕ)−4g2​Tr(D−1ϕD−1ϕ)+6g3​Tr(D−1ϕ)3+⋯.

These terms are the tadpole diagram, the two-point bubble diagram, and the triangle Feynman diagram. The tadpole diagram has factor 1/2; the two-point bubble diagram also has factor 1/2 after two differentiations with respect to the background scalar field. The local three-point interaction vertex is g3I3​. Thus their divergent contributions to the one-, two-, and three-point Euclidean one-particle irreducible vertices are respectively
2g​I1​,−2g2​B(P),g3I3​.

δh​​div​δm​​div​δZ​​div​δg​​div​​=−2Cg​(2Λ4​−m2Λ2+m4LΛ​),=2Cg2​(Λ2−2m2LΛ​),=−6Cg2​LΛ​,=−Cg3LΛ​.​​
