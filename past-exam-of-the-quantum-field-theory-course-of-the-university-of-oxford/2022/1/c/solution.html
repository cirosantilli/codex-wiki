Set g=g1​ and g2​=0. The superficial degree of divergence is then ω=6−2n, independently of the number of cubic interaction vertices. After subtracting ultraviolet subdivergences, the divergent part of a one-particle irreducible Feynman diagram is a local polynomial in external momenta of degree at most ω. Lorentz invariance and momentum conservation leave the following possibilities:
  • n=0: a constant vacuum energy density.
  • n=1: a linear tadpole counterterm. The single external momentum is zero, so derivatives cannot generate further independent terms.
  • n=2: a mass counterterm and a wave-function renormalization, since the polynomial has the form A+Bp2.
  • n=3: a constant cubic interaction vertex.
  • n≥4: no overall ultraviolet divergence, although ultraviolet subdivergences still require their lower-point counterterms.
Thus a finite set of counterterms closes under perturbation theory. In terms of renormalized coefficients, write
L+Lct​=21+δZ​​(∂ϕ)2−2m2+δm​​ϕ2−3!g+δg​​ϕ3−δh​ϕ−δE​.​
(3)
Here δm​ is a coefficient of squared mass, rather than a correction to m itself. The tadpole counterterm fixes the chosen expansion point, for example ⟨ϕ⟩=0; it cannot be omitted merely because the original Lagrangian density lacks a linear term. The vacuum energy density term cancels out of normalized Green functions, but is required if the unnormalized vacuum functional is also to be renormalized. This establishes perturbative renormalizability; the unbounded scalar potential of a real cubic scalar field theory does not by itself define a stable nonperturbative vacuum state.
