Treat ϕ and ϕ∗ as independent variables in the Euler-Lagrange equations. Write s=ϕ∗ϕ, and assume the scalar potential f(s) is real and differentiable. Variation with respect to ϕ∗ gives
∂ϕ∗∂L​=−f′(s)ϕ,∂(∂μ​ϕ∗)∂L​=∂μϕ.
(1)
Consequently the equations for the complex scalar field and its complex conjugate are
□ϕ+f′(ϕ∗ϕ)ϕ=0,□ϕ∗+f′(ϕ∗ϕ)ϕ∗=0.​
(2)
No polynomial assumption is needed: only the first derivative of the scalar potential enters. For f(s)=m2s, both reduce to the free Klein-Gordon equation.

b OurBigBook.com
For a constant real parameter, ∂μ​(eiαϕ)=eiα∂μ​ϕ. The phases therefore cancel separately in the kinetic term and in ϕ∗ϕ, leaving any f(ϕ∗ϕ) unchanged. This is the global phase symmetry of a complex scalar field, with circle group U(1).
Use infinitesimal variations per unit parameter, δϕ=iϕ, δϕ∗=−iϕ∗. The Lagrangian density is exactly invariant, so no total-derivative correction is needed in Noether's theorem. The Noether current is
Jμ=i(∂μϕ∗)ϕ−iϕ∗∂μϕ.​
(3)
It depends only on derivatives of the Lagrangian density with respect to field derivatives, so the derivative-free scalar potential cannot alter its form. Direct differentiation cancels the mixed first-derivative terms:
∂μ​Jμ=i(□ϕ∗)ϕ−iϕ∗□ϕ=−if′(s)ϕ∗ϕ+if′(s)ϕ∗ϕ=0.
(4)
Thus it is conserved on solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equations. Its spatial integral is a conserved Noether charge when the flux through the boundary vanishes. Its overall sign is fixed here by δϕ=+iϕ; reversing the orientation of the phase transformation reverses the current.

c OurBigBook.com
Let ∫p​=∫d3p/(2π)3. The conjugate momentum fields are πϕ​=ϕ˙​† and πϕ†​=ϕ˙​. Inserting the given mode expansion of a free field into i∫d3x(ϕ˙​†ϕ−ϕ†ϕ˙​) produces spatial Dirac delta factors. The b†c† and cb terms vanish after integration: they contain ωp​−ωq​ on δ3(p+q), and ω−p​=ωp​. The number-conserving terms instead add their two frequency factors, cancelling the mode normalization. The un-subtracted ordered expression is
Qraw​=∫p​(cp​cp†​−bp†​bp​)=∫p​(cp†​cp​−bp†​bp​)+∫p​(2π)3δ3(0).
(5)
The last term is an infinite vacuum constant, not a finite physical vacuum charge. Choosing the Fock vacuum to have zero Noether charge, equivalently using normal ordering, gives
Q=∫p​(cp†​cp​−bp†​bp​)=Nc​−Nb​.​
(6)
A state containing nc​ quanta created by c† and nb​ by b† has Noether charge nc​−nb​. Thus the two families describe opposite unit Noether charges; with this source's current the c† quantum has positive Noether charge and the b† quantum negative Noether charge. This is an internal Noether charge; identifying it with electric charge requires a specified electromagnetic coupling. The vacuum subtraction of a complex scalar charge leaves all commutators unchanged.[ref]
Scope of the expansion: a fixed-frequency expansion with time-independent oscillator coefficients is the free-field or asymptotic-field form. For arbitrary interacting f, it can be used as canonical oscillator coordinates at a chosen time, rather than as the exact interacting time dependence. For example, f(s)=m2s+λs2 gives (□+m2)ϕ+2λϕ∗ϕϕ=0, which a generic free plane-wave superposition does not satisfy. The Noether charge and its generator action remain valid in the interacting theory; the symmetry does not depend on using a free Hamiltonian.

The two oscillator families commute with each other. Their canonical commutation relations give
[Q,bp​]=+bp​,[Q,cp†​]=+cp†​,[Q,bp†​]=−bp†​,[Q,cp​]=−cp​.
(7)
Both terms in the mode expansion of a free field therefore acquire the same factor, giving
[Q,ϕ(x)]=ϕ(x),[Q,ϕ†(x)]=−ϕ†(x).​
(8)
The Noether charge is the generator of the internal symmetry. In the active-operator convention U(α)=eiαQ, repeated commutators give
U(α)ϕ(x)U(α)−1=eiαϕ(x).​
(9)
The sign agrees with part b. A negative sign for [Q,ϕ] would correspond to the opposite current convention, not to the current used here. Subtracting a multiple of the identity from Q to fix the vacuum Noether charge has no effect on this generator relation.

In d spacetime dimensions, [ddx]=−d and a dimensionless action requires [L]=d. Since [∂μ​]=1, the canonical kinetic term fixes 2+2[ϕ]=d. The cubic and quartic terms then give
[ϕ]=2d−2​,[κ]=26−d​,[λ]=4−d.​
(10)
These brackets denote mass dimensions. The cubic coupling is dimensionless at d=6, whereas the quartic coupling is dimensionless at d=4. Hence there is no single dimension in which both nonzero couplings are dimensionless. For example, at d=4 the cubic coupling still has mass dimension one.[ref]

Let Dxy​=⟨0∣Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩0​ be the free Feynman propagator, and abbreviate v3​=−iκ, v4​=−iλ. Expand the normalized Dyson series, so vacuum bubbles cancel between numerator and denominator, and retain the connected correlation function. Wick's theorem yields
Gc​(x,y)=​Dxy​+2v4​​∫ddzDxz​Dzz​Dzy​+2v32​​∫ddzddwDxz​Dzw2​Dwy​+2v32​​∫ddzddwDxz​Dzy​Dzw​Dww​+O(two loops).​
(11)
These are respectively the free line, the quartic tadpole diagram, the two-cubic-vertex bubble diagram, and an tadpole contribution to a cubic-theory two-point function, attached by a bridge to the vertex carrying both external legs. The last graph is connected but is a one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram; removing its bridge disconnects it. It must be included when all connected graphs are requested. It disappears if an additional vanishing-one-point-function renormalization condition and its corresponding tadpole subtraction are imposed; that convention was not specified here.
The factors can be counted directly. The quartic term has 4⋅3=12 attachments for the two labelled external legs, divided by 4!=24. At second order in the cubic interaction the expansion denominator is 2!(3!)2=72. There are 36 Wick contractions for the bubble, and 36 for the attached-tadpole graph, giving 1/2 for each. Thus each loop topology shown has symmetry factor S=2. Disconnected products of one-point functions and vacuum bubbles are not part of Gc​.[ref]
The one-loop cubic-quartic scalar vertex classification follows without truncating arbitrarily in powers of the couplings. A connected graph with E external legs, V3​ cubic vertices, V4​ quartic vertices and loop number L satisfies
V3​+2V4​=E+2L−2.
(12)
For E=2,L=1, the only possibilities are (V3​,V4​)=(2,0) or (0,1), exactly those just listed.

For the amputated one-particle-irreducible vertex, every internal bridge is forbidden. Define the Feynman integrals
D(k)=k2−m2+i0i​,∫k​=∫(2π)dddk​,
(13)
I2​(P)=∫k​D(k)D(k+P),I3​(P,Q,R)=∫k​D(k)D(k+P)D(k−R),P+Q+R=0.
(14)
For E=3,L=1, the counting identity in part b gives V3​+2V4​=3. The only one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams are a triangle of three cubic vertices and a two-propagator bubble joining one cubic and one quartic vertex. Write V3​ for the amputated Feynman vertex, including its overall factor of i. With all external momenta incoming,
V3,1-loop​(p1​,p2​,p3​)=v33​I3​(p1​,p2​,p3​)+2v3​v4​​i=1∑3​I2​(pi​).​
(15)
The triangle has S=1 for labelled external legs. The mixed bubble has S=2 because its two internal lines may be interchanged. There are three distinct terms, according to which external leg is attached to the cubic vertex; the other two attach to the quartic vertex. No tadpole attached by a bridge is one-particle irreducible.
The requested position-space three-point correlation function is obtained by restoring the external propagators and a Fourier transform:
G1PI,1-loop(3)​(x,y,z)=∫p1​​∫p2​​∫p3​​(2π)dδd(p1​+p2​+p3​)e−i(p1​x+p2​y+p3​z)D(p1​)D(p2​)D(p3​)V3,1-loop​.
(16)
The tree-level contribution would replace the last factor by v3​. This explicitly distinguishes the amputated vertex from the correlation function while giving all loop integrations in momentum space.

Let V4​=iΓ(4) denote the amputated Feynman vertex, with tree term v4​=−iλ. A one-loop one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram has V3​+2V4​=4, allowing three families: two quartic vertices, two cubic vertices and one quartic vertex, or four cubic vertices. Define
I4​(P,Q,R,S)=∫k​D(k)D(k+P)D(k+P+Q)D(k−S),P+Q+R+S=0.
(17)
Using I2​,I3​ from part c and writing Pij​=pi​+pj​, the complete one-loop result is
V4,1-loop​=​2v42​​[I2​(P12​)+I2​(P13​)+I2​(P14​)]+v32​v4​1≤i<j≤4∑​I3​(Pij​,pk​,pl​)+v34​[I4​(p1​,p2​,p3​,p4​)+I4​(p1​,p2​,p4​,p3​)+I4​(p1​,p3​,p2​,p4​)].​​
(18)
In each term of the middle sum, k,l are the two complementary indices. Their order does not change the integral after a loop-momentum redefinition.
The first line contains three bubble channels, one for each partition of the four labelled external legs into two pairs. Each has S=2 from interchanging its two identical internal lines. In the second line, the quartic vertex carries a chosen pair of external legs, giving (24​)=6 mixed triangles. Each has S=1. In the last line, cyclic orderings of four labelled external legs are identified under rotation and reversal, leaving (4−1)!/2=3 boxes, again with S=1 each. The figure in part b shows one representative of each family and its multiplicity. External-leg corrections and tadpoles connected by bridges are excluded because Γ(4) is a one-particle-irreducible vertex. All integrals are left unevaluated, as appropriate here.

A free contraction must connect one ϕ and one ϕ∗ field. In momentum space,
⟨Tϕ(p)ϕ∗(−p)⟩0​=p2−m02​+i0i​,⟨Tϕϕ⟩0​=⟨Tϕ∗ϕ∗⟩0​=0.​
(19)
Represent the nonzero propagator by a directed line so the two conjugate ends remain distinguishable; an undirected line alone would incorrectly allow same-charge contractions. Choose one consistent arrow convention, for example pointing from the starred end to the unstarred end.
The interaction contains two identical ϕ fields and two identical ϕ∗ fields. Their 2!2! assignments cancel the coefficient 1/4, giving
vϕϕϕ∗ϕ∗​=−iλ0​.​
(20)
The vertex has two incoming and two outgoing arrows. Include (2π)dδd(∑pi​) at every vertex, integrate ddk/(2π)d for each independent loop, and divide by the oriented graph's symmetry factor. There is no fermion-loop sign.

Use renormalized propagators of mass m and tree vertex −igμϵ. The interaction counterterms have Feynman factors −iδm2​ for a two-leg mass insertion and −iμϵgδg​ for a four-leg insertion. At one loop the two-point one-particle-irreducible vertex is
iΓ(2)(p)=i(p2−m2)−iδm2​+(−igμϵ)∫k​k2−m2+i0i​+O(g2).
(25)
The directed tadpole has S=1: interchanging its two ends would reverse the field labels, so the real-scalar tadpole's factor 1/2 does not apply. It is independent of external momentum, so no wave-function counterterm is required at this order.
For the four-point vertex, label the two unstarred incoming fields by 1,2 and the starred ones by 3,4, with all momenta incoming. The tree contact, the three oriented bubbles and the contact counterterm give
iΓ(4)=​−igμϵ−iμϵgδg​+(−igμϵ)2[21​I2​(p1​+p2​)+I2​(p1​+p3​)+I2​(p1​+p4​)]+O(g3).​
(26)
The first bubble has two equivalent arrows along the same direction between its vertices, giving S=2. In the other two channels the arrows oppose one another, and interchanging the lines does not preserve their oriented attachments, giving S=1. This is the oriented complex-scalar quartic graph factors rule.
The mass tadpole is order gm2, while the four-point loop is order g2. In the multiplicative coupling convention of the source,
δm2​=O(gm2/ϵ),δg​=O(g/ϵ),gδg​=O(g2/ϵ).​
(27)
The distinction between δg​ and the whole additive four-leg coefficient gδg​ matters for the requested order counting.

Source correction: equation 11 on the original page has Γ(α−1/2). The dimensionally regulated integral instead contains Γ(α−d/2). This is not an interchangeable convention: for α=2 and d=4−ϵ, the correct factor Γ(ϵ/2) has a logarithmic ultraviolet pole, whereas the printed Γ(3/2) is finite.
To obtain the correct expression, shift the loop momentum and perform a Wick rotation. For positive Euclidean M2, introduce a Schwinger parameterization:
∫(2π)dddqE​​(qE2​+M2)α1​​=Γ(α)1​∫0∞​dssα−1e−sM2∫(2π)dddqE​​e−sqE2​=(4π)d/2Γ(α)Γ(α−d/2)​(M2)d/2−α.​
(28)
The derivation first holds in the convergent range and defines the result elsewhere by analytic continuation. For integer α, rotating back gives
∫(2π)dddq​(q2+2q⋅p−m2+i0)α1​=(4π)d/2i(−1)α​Γ(α)Γ(α−d/2)​(p2+m2)d/2−α,​
(29)
with the branch inherited from i0. This is the Lorentzian continuation of the Euclidean massive loop integral.
Set p=0, α=1, d=4−ϵ. Since Γ(−1+ϵ/2)=−2/ϵ+O(1),
∫k​k2−m2+i01​=8π2ϵim2​+O(1).
(30)
Consequently the directed tadpole in part d gives
iΓloop(2)​​div​=(−igμϵ)i∫k​k2−m2+i01​​div​=8π2ϵigm2​.
(31)
The counterterm is −iδm2​, so cancelling only the pole in the minimal subtraction scheme requires
δm2​=8π2ϵgm2​.​
(32)
This is the quartic complex-scalar mass renormalization result. The scale factors and logarithms contribute finite terms at this order and do not change the displayed pole coefficient. A real-field check gives a tadpole coefficient 21​(3g/2+g/2)=g, in agreement with the oriented complex-field computation.

At fixed external momenta, fixed regulator and fixed bare coupling, define the total scale derivative
D=μ∂μ∂​+β(g)∂g∂​.
(36)
The bare vertex has no independent dependence on the arbitrary renormalization scale. Differentiating Γ(n)=Zϕn/2​Γ0(n)​ therefore gives
DΓ(n)=2n​(μdμdlogZϕ​​)bare​Γ(n).
(37)
Defining the two scale-response functions as in the source yields
[μ∂μ​+β(g)∂g​−2n​γ(g)]Γ(n)=0.​
(38)
This is the Callan-Symanzik equation. Its minus sign and factor n/2 follow from the specified Zϕn/2​ multiplying the bare vertex. In particular, the source's γ=μdlogZϕ​/dμ is twice the field anomalous dimension defined using logZϕ1/2​. A convention change must be made consistently in both definitions and equation.

A(ϵ)=ϵa1​​+a2​+a3​ϵ+⋯,C(ϵ)=ϵz1​​+z2​+z3​ϵ+⋯.

β(g)γ(g)​=−ϵg0​(1+2Ag0​)+O(g03​)=−ϵg−ϵAg2+O(g3),=−ϵg0​1+Cg0​C​+O(g02​)=−ϵCg+O(g2).​

Retain the specified leading poles and write A=a1​/ϵ, B=b1​/ϵ2. Solving g=g0​+Ag02​+Bg03​ recursively gives
g0​=g−Ag2+(2A2−B)g3+O(g4).
(47)
The scale derivative must again be reexpressed in the renormalized coupling:
β(g)​=−ϵg0​(1+2Ag0​+3Bg02​)=−ϵg−ϵAg2+2ϵ(A2−B)g3+O(g4)=−ϵg−a1​g2+ϵ2(a12​−b1​)​g3+O(g4).​​
(48)
The leading-pole consistency under coupling reparametrization shows that a finite renormalization-group beta function requires the two-loop double-pole consistency condition
b1​=a12​.​
(49)
The leading two-loop pole is fixed by the one-loop simple pole; it is not an independent parameter. This is an instance of the simple-pole formulas for minimal-subtraction renormalization group functions and their higher-pole consistency relations.
The source's leading-pole truncation does not fix the finite two-loop coefficient of the beta function. For example, adding the omitted term c1​g03​/ϵ leaves the double-pole condition unchanged but contributes −2c1​g3 to the finite beta function. Thus cancelling the displayed 1/ϵ term is not a claim that the physical two-loop running vanishes.
