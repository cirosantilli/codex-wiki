Distinct gamma matrices anticommute, and (γ0)2=I, (γj)2=−I for j=1,2,3. Moving a particular γμ through the other three factors in γ5 gives three minus signs. Therefore
{γ5,γμ}=0.​
(1)
Pairing equal factors in the square involves 3+2+1=6 interchanges. The Clifford algebra consequently gives
(γ5)2=(−1)6μ=0∏3​(γμ)2=−I.​
(2)
In particular (γ5)−1=−γ5; replacing it by the conventional chirality matrix would change this answer.
The trace is unchanged by a similarity transformation. Since (γ0)−1=γ0 and γ0γ5γ0=−γ5, cyclic property of the trace gives trγ5=−trγ5. Also
tr(γ5γμ)=tr(γμγ5)=−tr(γ5γμ).
(3)
Thus
trγ5=tr(γ5γμ)=0.​
(4)

The mass dimensions are [ϕ]=1, [ψ]=3/2, [g]=0. Up to total derivatives, power counting allows local counterterms of mass dimension at most four. A convenient basis for the counterterm Lagrangian compatible with the actual parity symmetry in quantum field theory and Dirac fermion number conservation is
Lct(+)​=​−δΛ+δZψ​ψˉ​iγμ∂μ​ψ−δMψˉ​ψ+21​δZϕ​∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ−21​δm2ϕ2−δgϕψˉ​γ5ψ−4!δλ​ϕ4.​​
(5)
The optional constant δΛ renormalizes the vacuum energy density. The wavefunction renormalization, mass counterterms and Yukawa coupling renormalization correct the terms already present. The quartic interaction must also be included: the fermion loop with four external bosonic legs is logarithmically divergent, even if the renormalized quartic coupling is set to zero at one chosen renormalization scale. The displayed original interactions alone do not form a closed set of counterterms.
If “most general” is interpreted before imposing the parity symmetry in quantum field theory examined in d, Lorentz invariance, a Hermitian action and Dirac fermion number conservation also allow
Lct(−)​=​−δtϕ−3!δκ​ϕ3−δyϕψˉ​ψ−δM5​ψˉ​γ5ψ+δZ5​ψˉ​γμγ5∂μ​ψ.​
(6)
Here all coefficients can be real in the paper's gamma matrix convention; the last term is Hermitian in the action up to a total derivative. It is an axial kinetic normalization, which could alternatively be removed by a suitable perturbative field redefinition. Every term in Lct(−)​ is odd under the actual parity symmetry in quantum field theory, so its coefficient vanishes in a symmetry-preserving renormalization scheme. Listing these terms makes explicit which assumption excludes a tadpole counterterm, a cubic scalar term, a scalar Yukawa interaction, a pseudoscalar Dirac mass term and an axial kinetic term.

Expanding eiSint​ gives the Feynman vertex
Vab​=−ig(γ5)ab​.​
(7)
There is one scalar leg and a continuous oriented fermion line; no extra factor i is absorbed into γ5. The Dirac propagator is SF​(k)=i(k+M)/(k2−M2+i0), where k=γμkμ​. Each closed fermion loop supplies a minus sign.
The tadpole diagram is proportional to

(8)
because both numerator traces vanish by a. This is zero before the loop momentum integration.
For either orientation of the triangle Feynman diagram, let a,b,c be its three internal four-momenta. Its numerator trace is
N​=tr[γ5(a+M)γ5(b+M)γ5(c+M)]=tr[(a−M)(b+M)γ5(c+M)]=0.​

(10)

Writing xP​=(t,−x), the standard parity transformation of a Dirac field is
ψ′(x)=ηP​γ0ψ(xP​),ψˉ​′(x)=ηP∗​ψˉ​(xP​)γ0,∣ηP​∣=1.​
(11)
Since γ0γ5γ0=−γ5, the fermion bilinear ψˉ​γ5ψ changes sign. Invariance of the pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction therefore requires
ϕ′(x)=−ϕ(xP​).​
(12)
Thus ϕ is a pseudoscalar, despite having the usual scalar-form kinetic term and mass term. In this convention (γ5)†=−γ5, but ψˉ​γ5ψ is Hermitian because of the additional γ0 in the Dirac adjoint. A real g is consequently consistent with a Hermitian Lagrangian density.
In perturbation theory about the parity-invariant vacuum state, renormalization can preserve this non-anomalous parity symmetry in quantum field theory. Every local counterterm must then be parity even, at every loop order. The allowed terms are precisely Lct(+)​ in b; all coefficients in Lct(−)​ are zero. In particular, no ϕ or ϕ3 counterterm is generated, whereas ϕ4 is allowed and required. The one-point and three-point pure-boson vertices also vanish to all orders: a parity-odd Lorentz scalar cannot be formed from their at most two independent external four-momenta. This last reasoning is specific to these low-point vertices; it does not assert that parity symmetry in quantum field theory is an independent ϕ↦−ϕ internal symmetry with the fermions held fixed.

Independent global phase symmetries would rotate ϕ↦e−iαϕ and Φ↦e−iβΦ. The cubic interaction vertex preserves only 2α+β=0 modulo 2π. The continuous internal symmetry of a classical field theory is therefore the circle group
U(1):ϕ↦e−iαϕ,Φ↦e2iαΦ.​
(13)
Normalize the Noether charge so that qϕ​=1. Then qΦ​=−2, qϕˉ​​=−1 and qΦˉ​=2. The Noether current is
jμ=iϕ†∂μϕ−2iΦ†∂μΦ,∂μ​jμ=0,​
(14)
where A∂B=A∂B−(∂A)B. For example, the Euler-Lagrange field equations give
∂μ​(iϕ†∂μϕ)=ih(ϕ2Φ−ϕ†2Φ†),∂μ​(iΦ†∂μΦ)=2ih​(ϕ2Φ−ϕ†2Φ†),
(15)
which cancel with the relative coefficient −2. The quartic interaction vertex separately preserves both original phase rotations.
There is also a discrete charge conjugation symmetry (ϕ,Φ)↦(ϕ†,Φ†) for these real couplings. Including it, the internal symmetry is U(1)⋊Z2​≅O(2), with charge conjugation reversing the Noether charge. The connected symmetry and its conserved current are the U(1) above.

⟨Tϕϕ†⟩(p)=p2−m2+i0i​,⟨TΦΦ†⟩(p)=p2−M2+i0i​.​

Vϕϕ†ΦΦ†​=−ig,VϕϕΦ​=−ih,Vϕ†ϕ†Φ†​=−ih.​

The two identical light legs cancel the 1/2! in each cubic interaction vertex; there is no remaining factor 1/2 in its Feynman rule. Multiply each Feynman vertex by (2π)4δ(4)(∑p), and integrate an internal unconstrained four-momentum with d4p/(2π)4.
The arrows in the figure follow positive Noether charge: a light arrow carries one unit and a heavy arrow two. Consequently a heavy particle Φ travels opposite its charge arrow, whereas its antiparticle Φˉ travels along it. At a ϕ2Φ Feynman vertex, two unit-charge arrows enter and a two-unit arrow leaves; the conjugate Feynman vertex reverses every arrow. The quartic Feynman vertex has one incoming and one outgoing arrow of each weight. These rules prevent attaching a propagator between two unconjugated fields.

I2​=8πE∗​p∗​​=8πβ​,E∗​=2M​.

(24)

For g=0, the invariant scattering amplitude from the tree-level Feynman diagram in i has a pole at s=M2. The intermediate heavy antiparticle can go on shell and live for a time of order its mean lifetime. Near this scattering resonance, inserting a single self-energy is no longer a uniformly small correction. Dyson resummation gives, to leading accuracy in a narrow decay width,
M(s)≃−s−M2+iMΓphys​h2​.​
(26)
Here M is understood as the renormalized resonance mass; the real self-energy shifts the original pole position. The imaginary term removes the unphysical divergence of the fixed-order invariant scattering amplitude.
For equal incoming and outgoing light masses, the two-body momentum ratio cancels in the cross section. The angle-independent invariant scattering amplitude, integrated over the full solid angle with the identical final-state symmetry factor 1/2!, gives σ=∣M∣2/(32πs). Thus the Breit-Wigner resonance cross section is
σ(s)≃32πs[(s−M2)2+M2Γphys2​]h4​,Γphys​=32πMh2β​.​
(27)
At s≫M2 it reproduces h4/(32πs3). At the resonance peak,
σ(M2)≃M2β232π​≃M232π​(M≫m).​
(28)
This is also 8π/k∗2​ for k∗​=Mβ/2, the elastic identical-boson S wave partial-wave unitarity limit when this is the only open decay channel. The approximation assumes Γphys​≪M and a resonance sufficiently far from threshold for the width to vary slowly across it.
If one mechanically uses the unsymmetrized Γprinted​=2Γphys​ from d, the same displayed Breit-Wigner resonance cross section would have that doubled width and predict a peak 8π/(M2β2). This is the literal-width substitution, rather than the consistent physical result: the given high-energy cross section already includes the identical-final-particle factor that the printed decay width integral omits.

For three odd fermionic operators, the identity
[A,BC]={A,B}C−B{A,C}
(29)
converts the commutator with a quadratic Hamiltonian into the given canonical anticommutation relations. Writing a mode label as i=(p,s), this yields
[ai​,aj†​aj​]=δij​aj​,[bi†​,bj†​bj​]=−δij​bj†​.
(30)
The opposite-species number terms commute with each fermionic creation operator or fermionic annihilation operator, because two anticommutations give a positive sign. Restoring the continuous normalization, the Dirac delta distribution in the mode brackets cancels d3q/(2π)3. Consequently
[aps​,H]=Ep​aps​,[bps†​,H]=−Ep​bps†​.​
(31)
Substitute these into the mode expansion of a Dirac field. The result is
[ψ(x),H]=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​∑s​Ep​[e−ip⋅xaps​us(p)−eip⋅xbps†​vs(p)].
(32)
Since i∂t​e−ip⋅x=Ep​e−ip⋅x and i∂t​eip⋅x=−Ep​eip⋅x, this is exactly
i∂t​ψ(t,x)=[ψ(t,x),H].​
(33)
The Heisenberg equation of motion uses an ordinary commutator with the even Hamiltonian, despite the odd fermionic operators obeying anticommutators among themselves.

For i<j, interchange the entries in slots i,j while preserving the order of all intermediate fermionic creation operators. Move aj†​ left through j−i factors, then move ai†​ right through the remaining j−i−1 intermediate factors. The canonical anticommutation relations give the overall fermionic sign
(−1)(j−i)+(j−i−1)=(−1)2(j−i)−1=−1.
(35)
Hence
∣1,…,j,…,i,…,n⟩=−∣1,…,i,…,j,…,n⟩.​
(36)
More generally any permutation π gives
∣π(1),…,π(n)⟩=sgn(π)∣1,…,n⟩.​
(37)
The sign of a permutation is independent of its decomposition into adjacent interchanges. Thus the n-particle sector is an exterior power of the one-particle state space, the defining antisymmetry of fermionic Fock space. Repeated complete mode labels make the state zero, so they satisfy the same identity trivially.

The corrected expression in d is a vacuum two-point function, or Wick contraction, of the free Dirac field. It equals the negative spinor transpose of the usual Dirac propagator with reversed spacetime arguments. It supplies the internal fermion lines in Feynman diagrams, rather than a position-space detection probability.
For x0>y0, ψ(y) creates an antiparticle and ψˉ​(x) annihilates it. The vvˉ term describes positive-energy antiparticle propagation from y to x. For y0>x0, ψˉ​(x) creates a particle and ψ(y) annihilates it. The uuˉ term describes positive-energy particle propagation from x to y, with the minus sign required by exchanging the odd operators in fermionic time ordering. Neither term describes a physical negative-energy excitation.
The Heaviside step functions select these chronological alternatives. The factors e∓ip⋅z are on-shell plane waves with p0=Ep​>0, and dΠp​ is the positive-energy Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure. The sum over s includes the two spin states. With standard relativistic spinor normalization, the fermion spin sums are
∑s​us(p)uˉs(p)=p+μ,∑s​vs(p)vˉs(p)=p−μ.
(43)
They encode the spinor structure and solve the corresponding on-shell Dirac equations. In particular, the corrected answer can also be written
Cab​(x,y)=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−μ2+i0i(p−μ)ba​​e−ip⋅(x−y).
(44)
The Feynman i-epsilon prescription specifies the temporal boundary conditions of the Green function; its positive- and negative-frequency residues reproduce the two terms and their relative minus sign. With SF​ defined in d,
(i∂−μ)SF​(w)=iδ(4)(w)I.
(45)
The contact term is fixed by the equal-time canonical anticommutation relations. Choosing a value for θ(0) does not change the unequal-time sign demonstrated above.
Finally, microcausality requires field anticommutators to vanish at spacelike separation; the time-ordered vacuum two-point function itself need not vanish there. These facts are compatible with relativistic causality. The plus-plus expression printed in the paper lacks the fermionic sign needed for this standard Dirac propagator interpretation.
