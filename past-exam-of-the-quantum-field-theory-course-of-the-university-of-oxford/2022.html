A dimensionless action in six spacetime dimensions requires the Lagrangian density to have mass dimension six. The kinetic term then fixes 2+2[ϕ]=6, and each field interaction term must have the same mass dimension. Therefore
[ϕ]=2,[g1​]=0,[g2​]=−2.​
(1)
Equivalently, in powers of a length unit L, ϕ has units L−2, g1​ has units L0, and g2​ has units L2. Thus literal length exponents would be (−2,0,2); the d's in the supplied superficial degree of divergence formula must instead mean mass dimensions, namely (2,0,−2). This distinction resolves the wording's convention mismatch.
To check the convention independently, let I,V3​,V4​,ℓ count internal lines, cubic interaction vertices, quartic interaction vertices, and independent loop momenta. A connected Feynman diagram obeys 2I+n=3V3​+4V4​ and ℓ=I−V3​−V4​+1. Hence its superficial degree of divergence is 6ℓ−2I=6−2n+2V4​, agreeing with the supplied formula only with the mass dimensions above.

c OurBigBook.com
Set g=g1​ and g2​=0. The superficial degree of divergence is then ω=6−2n, independently of the number of cubic interaction vertices. After subtracting ultraviolet subdivergences, the divergent part of a one-particle irreducible Feynman diagram is a local polynomial in external momenta of degree at most ω. Lorentz invariance and momentum conservation leave the following possibilities:
  • n=0: a constant vacuum energy density.
  • n=1: a linear tadpole counterterm. The single external momentum is zero, so derivatives cannot generate further independent terms.
  • n=2: a mass counterterm and a wave-function renormalization, since the polynomial has the form A+Bp2.
  • n=3: a constant cubic interaction vertex.
  • n≥4: no overall ultraviolet divergence, although ultraviolet subdivergences still require their lower-point counterterms.
Thus a finite set of counterterms closes under perturbation theory. In terms of renormalized coefficients, write
L+Lct​=21+δZ​​(∂ϕ)2−2m2+δm​​ϕ2−3!g+δg​​ϕ3−δh​ϕ−δE​.​
(3)
Here δm​ is a coefficient of squared mass, rather than a correction to m itself. The tadpole counterterm fixes the chosen expansion point, for example ⟨ϕ⟩=0; it cannot be omitted merely because the original Lagrangian density lacks a linear term. The vacuum energy density term cancels out of normalized Green functions, but is required if the unnormalized vacuum functional is also to be renormalized. This establishes perturbative renormalizability; the unbounded scalar potential of a real cubic scalar field theory does not by itself define a stable nonperturbative vacuum state.

Continue with the cubic scalar field theory of c, and use precisely its counterterm signs. Take m2>0 to separate ultraviolet divergences from infrared divergences. Make a Wick rotation and impose the rotationally invariant cutoff regularization ∣qE​∣<Λ. Put
C=2(2π)6area(S5)​=128π31​,LΛ​=logm2Λ2​,Ir​=∫∣qE​∣<Λ​(2π)6d6qE​​(qE2​+m2)−r.
(4)
For example, spherical coordinates give I1​=C∫0Λ2​t2/(t+m2)dt. Polynomial division and then differentiation with respect to m2 give the divergent parts
I1​​div​I2​​div​​=C(2Λ4​−m2Λ2+m4LΛ​),=C(Λ2−2m2LΛ​),I3​​div​=CLΛ​.​
(5)
The derivative relations are I2​=−∂m2​I1​ and I3​=21​∂m22​I1​; differentiation also generates finite terms, which are irrelevant here. The first supplied integral identity is consistent with these divergent parts after Wick rotation.
The second supplied expansion is incorrect. Write A=p2−m2. Direct multiplication of the two denominators gives (A+k2)2−4(p⋅k)2, so the correct Taylor expansion is
[(p−k)2−m2][(p+k)2−m2]1​=A21​(1−A2k2​+A24(p⋅k)2​+O(k4)).
(6)
A concrete counterexample is p=0, m=1, k=(ϵ,0): the exact expression is (1−ϵ2)−2=1+2ϵ2+O(ϵ4), whereas the printed expansion gives 1+ϵ2/2+O(ϵ4). We therefore compute the self-energy without that identity.
For Euclidean external momentum P, define the scalar bubble integral
B(P)=∫(2π)6d6qE​​(qE2​+m2)((qE​+P)2+m2)1​.
(7)
A Feynman parameter x combines its denominators into one squared denominator with K=m2+x(1−x)P2. Applying the radial result above and integrating ∫01​x(1−x)dx=1/6 yields
B(P)​div​=C(Λ2−2m2LΛ​−3P2​LΛ​).
(8)
One can instead expand with the ultraviolet cutoff fixed on the unshifted loop momentum: the P2 coefficient of the integrand is −(qE2​+m2)−3+32​qE2​(qE2​+m2)−4, using rotational averaging in six dimensions. Its leading radial term is −1/(3qE6​), giving the same logarithm. Shifting a sharp integration boundary changes finite terms in this scalar bubble integral, and does not change the displayed divergent coefficients.
To fix all signs and symmetry factors together, expand the one-loop Euclidean effective action around a background scalar field:
Γ1,E​[ϕ]=21​Trlog(D+gϕ),D=−∂E2​+m2,
(9)
Γ1,E​[ϕ]−Γ1,E​[0]=2g​Tr(D−1ϕ)−4g2​Tr(D−1ϕD−1ϕ)+6g3​Tr(D−1ϕ)3+⋯.
(10)
These terms are the tadpole diagram, the two-point bubble diagram, and the triangle Feynman diagram. The tadpole diagram has factor 1/2; the two-point bubble diagram also has factor 1/2 after two differentiations with respect to the background scalar field. The local three-point interaction vertex is g3I3​. Thus their divergent contributions to the one-, two-, and three-point Euclidean one-particle irreducible vertices are respectively
2g​I1​,−2g2​B(P),g3I3​.
(11)
The Euclidean counterterm Lagrangian is δZ​(∂E​ϕ)2/2+δm​ϕ2/2+δg​ϕ3/6+δh​ϕ+δE​. Cancelling each divergent one-particle irreducible vertex therefore gives
δh​​div​δm​​div​δZ​​div​δg​​div​​=−2Cg​(2Λ4​−m2Λ2+m4LΛ​),=2Cg2​(Λ2−2m2LΛ​),=−6Cg2​LΛ​,=−Cg3LΛ​.​​
(12)
For example, the two-point sum is δZ​P2+δm​−g2B(P)/2 and its divergent coefficients cancel separately. Finite counterterms depend on the chosen renormalization conditions. The zero-mass limit can use a positive subtraction scale in the logarithms after infrared regularization.
If unnormalized vacuum functionals are retained, specify the determinant measure by
V1,E​(0)=21​∫∣qE​∣<Λ​(2π)6d6qE​​logμ2qE2​+m2​.
(13)
Expanding its radial integrand at large qE​ fixes the additional vacuum energy density counterterm:
δE​​div​=−2C​[3Λ6​logμ2Λ2​−9Λ6​+2m2Λ4​−2m4Λ2​+3m6​LΛ​].​
(14)
The purely mass-independent terms depend on the determinant normalization. Normalized Green functions eliminate this entire constant.

The auxiliary field σ has quadratic kernel −1 and no kinetic term. Inverting that kernel, rather than introducing an artificial scalar mass, gives the momentum space Feynman rules
σ line: −i,ψ line: p2−m2+i0i(p+m)​,σψˉ​ψ vertex: ig.​
(15)
The auxiliary-field propagator is independent of momentum; in position space it is −iδ(4)(x−y). At each interaction vertex attach (2π)4δ(4)(∑p) for momentum conservation. Integrate independent loop momenta with d4p/(2π)4, respect fermion flow, include the closed fermion loop sign −1, and use the appropriate symmetry factor. Incoming and outgoing fermions carry us​(p) and uˉs​(p); incoming and outgoing antiparticles carry vˉs​(p) and vs​(p). Exchanging external fermions introduces the fermionic sign. Here p=γμpμ​ is Feynman slash notation.
For a dimensional check, [ψ]=3/2, [σ]=2, [g]=−1 in mass dimensions. Eliminating σ consequently produces a four-fermion interaction of negative coupling mass dimension, not a propagating scalar degree of freedom.

Label the incoming fermion momenta p1​,p2​ and outgoing fermion momenta p3​,p4​, with p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​. There are two tree-level Feynman diagrams: the t channel connects 1 to 3 and 2 to 4, with exchanged momentum qt​=p1​−p3​=p4​−p2​; the u channel connects 1 to 4 and 2 to 3, with qu​=p1​−p4​=p3​−p2​. Both internal lines are auxiliary-field propagators. The upper row in the figure gives these labelled connections; the outgoing labels are exchanged in its second panel to avoid a visually unnecessary crossing.
Each tree-level Feynman diagram contains two factors ig and one factor −i, giving ig2. The exchange of the two identical outgoing fermions gives the relative fermionic sign. Up to a common external-state phase, the corresponding scattering amplitude is
Mff​=g2[(uˉ3​u1​)(uˉ4​u2​)−(uˉ4​u1​)(uˉ3​u2​)].​
(16)
There is no annihilation channel: both incoming particles carry the same fermion number, whereas a σψˉ​ψ interaction vertex couples one fermion to one antiparticle when both are incoming.

Use incoming fermion p1​ and antiparticle p2​, outgoing fermion p3​ and antiparticle p4​. The two tree-level Feynman diagrams are the t channel, with qt​=p1​−p3​=p4​−p2​, and the s channel, with qs​=p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​. They occupy the lower row of the figure. In the t channel the fermion flow follows 1→3 and 4→2; in the s channel it follows 1→2 at the incoming interaction vertex and 4→3 at the outgoing interaction vertex.
The two contractions again have a relative fermionic sign. With the same harmless common-phase convention as i, their sum is
Mffˉ​​=g2[(uˉ3​u1​)(vˉ2​v4​)−(vˉ2​u1​)(uˉ3​v4​)].​
(17)
The auxiliary-field propagator is the same constant −i in both channels. There is no u channel preserving the external fermion number assignments.

Write ui​=usi​(pi​) and vi​=vsi​(pi​). With Sfi​=δfi​+i(2π)4δ(4)(p1​+p2​−p3​−p4​)Mfi​, choose
Mp1​,p2​,p3​,p4​s1​,s2​,s3​,s4​​=g2(T−A),T=(uˉ3​u1​)(vˉ2​v4​),A=(vˉ2​u1​)(uˉ3​v4​).​
(18)
The relative minus sign is fixed by the canonical anticommutation relations, not by the Mandelstam variables. It follows by crossing symmetry from the identical-fermion scattering amplitude in i, or directly by the two inequivalent external contractions of g2(ψˉ​ψ)2/2. For an explicit sign check, fix incoming state order b1†​d2†​∣0⟩ and outgoing order b3†​d4†​∣0⟩. The direct contraction contains (b3†​b1​)(d2​d4†​), whose coefficient in the ordered product b3†​d4†​d2​b1​ is −1; the annihilation contraction (b3†​d4†​)(d2​b1​) has coefficient +1. The common sign can change with the external-state convention, while their relative minus sign cannot.
Let r=m2. The external particles are on shell, so s+t+u=4r and
p1​⋅p2​=p3​⋅p4​=2s​−r,p1​⋅p3​=p2​⋅p4​=r−2t​,p1​⋅p4​=p2​⋅p3​=r−2u​.
(19)
Use the supplied spin sums and gamma matrix trace identities. The two diagonal terms in the initial spin average are
41​∑∣T∣2=41​Tr[(p3​+m)(p1​+m)]Tr[(p2​−m)(p4​−m)]=(t−4r)2,
(20)
41​∑∣A∣2=41​Tr[(p2​−m)(p1​+m)]Tr[(p3​+m)(p4​−m)]=(s−4r)2.
(21)
For the interference term, the spin sum is a single Dirac trace:
R:=∑TA∗=Tr[(p3​+m)(p1​+m)(p2​−m)(p4​−m)].
(22)
Terms with an odd number of Dirac gamma matrices vanish. Expanding the remaining terms gives
4R​=​(p3​⋅p1​)(p2​⋅p4​)−(p3​⋅p2​)(p1​⋅p4​)+(p3​⋅p4​)(p1​⋅p2​)+r(p3​⋅p1​−p3​⋅p2​−p3​⋅p4​−p1​⋅p2​−p1​⋅p4​+p2​⋅p4​)+r2.​
(23)
Substituting the Mandelstam variables reduces this to
R=s2+t2−u2+16ru−16r2=(s−4r)2+(t−4r)2−(u−4r)2.
(24)
It is real. The initial spin average therefore yields
∣M∣2​=g4[(t−4r)2+(s−4r)2−2R​],
(25)
∣M∣2​=2g4​[(s−4m2)2+(t−4m2)2+(u−4m2)2].​
(26)
The displayed answer in the source is false. At threshold, pi​=(m,0), hence s=4m2 and t=u=0. Then vˉu=uˉv=0, while the spin sums give ∣M∣2​=16g4m4. The printed expression instead gives 20g4m4. This is also a counterexample in the continuous threshold limit from physical nonzero incoming momenta. At m=0 there is a further discrepancy: the correct result is g4(s2+t2+u2)/2, rather than the printed g4(3s2+3t2−u2)/2. Changing a common phase of the scattering amplitude cannot change either comparison.
The requested s↔t symmetry nevertheless holds for the correct answer. Crossing symmetry exchanges the two ways of pairing the four external fermion lines; the momentum-independent auxiliary-field propagator supplies no channel-dependent denominator. The relative fermionic sign can reverse the crossed scattering amplitude, but disappears in its squared modulus. With u=4m2−s−t, the boxed expression is manifestly symmetric under s↔t. This is an analytic crossing relation, not a statement that positive physical s and negative physical t have interchangeable scattering kinematics.

Set B(x)=ψˉ​(x)ψ(x), a Grassmann-even Dirac scalar bilinear. Completing the square gives
−21​σ2+gσB=−21​(σ−gB)2+2g2​B2.
(27)
Introduce external Grassmann sources η,ηˉ​ for the Dirac field, but no source for σ. The generating functional is
Z[η,ηˉ​]=∫Dψˉ​DψDσexp{i∫d4x[ψˉ​(i∂−m)ψ−21​(σ−gB)2+2g2​B2+ηˉ​ψ+ψˉ​η]}.
(28)
At a finite regulator the change of variable σ′=σ−gB has unit Jacobian: it translates a bosonic integration variable by a Grassmann-even expression independent of that variable. Its oscillatory Gaussian integral can be defined with a convergence prescription and then continued. It is a constant N independent of ψ,η,ηˉ​. Thus
Z[η,ηˉ​]=N∫Dψˉ​Dψexp{i∫d4x[ψˉ​(i∂−m)ψ+2g2​(ψˉ​ψ)2+ηˉ​ψ+ψˉ​η]}.
(29)
The factor N cancels in Z[η,ηˉ​]/Z[0,0]. Every normalized Green function with only external fermion lines is a Grassmann source derivative of this same normalized generating functional, so
Gauxiliary(n)​=Gfour-fermion(n)​.​
(30)
This is an exact Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation in reverse, rather than just an approximation based on tree-level Feynman diagrams. At the diagrammatic level the contact auxiliary-field propagator −iδ(4)(x−y) collapses each exchanged line into the same local four-fermion interaction. The equality uses a common regulator and does not assert perturbative renormalizability of that four-dimensional four-fermion interaction.

For arbitrary linear operators, expansion of the anticommutators gives
{X,Y}Z−Y{X,Z}=(XY+YX)Z−Y(XZ+ZX)=XYZ−YZX=[X,YZ].
(31)
Now use the commutator product rule [VX,W]=V[X,W]+[V,W]X, with W=YZ, twice. The result is
[VX,YZ]=V{X,Y}Z−VY{X,Z}+{V,Y}ZX−Y{V,Z}X.​
(32)
No grading assumption is needed for these algebraic identities.
For the Dirac field, supplement the supplied equal-time canonical anticommutation relations with {ψa​(x),ψb​(y)}={ψa†​(x),ψb†​(y)}=0. Define the fermion number density n(x)=∑a​ψa†​(x)ψa​(x). In the preceding identity take V=ψa†​(x), X=ψa​(x), Y=ψb†​(y), Z=ψc​(z) and sum over a. Only its first and last anticommutators survive, giving
[n(x),ψb†​(y)ψc​(z)]=[δ(3)(x−y)−δ(3)(x−z)]ψb†​(y)ψc​(z).​
(33)
This is an identity of operator-valued distributions. Point splitting, or smearing with test functions, gives a precise meaning to coincident bilinears. Normal ordering only subtracts a constant from the density and does not alter this commutator.

Define the constant Dirac gamma matrix combinations αk=γ0γk and β=γ0. The Hamiltonian operator is
H=∫d3yψb†​(y)(−iαbck​∂yk​+mβbc​)ψc​(y).
(34)
Evaluate its commutator with n(x) using the bilocal result in a. The mass term cancels immediately when z=y, because its two Dirac delta distributions coincide. For the derivative term first keep the two points distinct:
C(x)=−i∫d3yαbck​∂zk​{[δ(3)(x−y)−δ(3)(x−z)]ψb†​(y)ψc​(z)}​z=y​.
(35)
The terms in which the derivative acts on ψc​ cancel against each other; the derivative of the second Dirac delta distribution remains. Thus
C(x)=i∫d3yψ†(y)αkψ(y)∂yk​δ(3)(x−y)=−i∂xk​[ψ†(x)αkψ(x)].
(36)
The last equality is integration by parts, or the defining derivative rule for a distribution. Consequently the literal conserved current whose divergence equals C is
C=∇⋅J,Jk=−iψ†αkψ=−iψˉ​γkψ.​
(37)
Because a commutator of two Hermitian operators is anti-Hermitian, this J includes −i. The physical Hermitian Dirac current is instead jk=ψˉ​γkψ, so C=−i∇⋅j. The Heisenberg equation of motion now gives
∂t​n=i[H,n]=−iC=−∇⋅j,jμ=ψˉ​γμψ.​
(38)
This establishes the continuity equation directly from the required commutator, without assuming the Dirac equation or invoking the Noether theorem as a substitute for the computation.

The Hamiltonian operator is invariant under the constant global phase symmetry ψ↦eiθψ, ψˉ​↦ψˉ​e−iθ. The Noether current of this unitary group U(1) is the Dirac current obtained in b. For fields with vanishing flux at infinity, or periodic boundary conditions, the divergence theorem and that continuity equation imply
Q=∫d3x:ψ†ψ: ,[Q,H]=0,dtdQ​=0.​
(39)
The canonical anticommutation relations also give [Q,ψ]=−ψ and [Q,ψ†]=ψ†, hence eiθQψe−iθQ=e−iθψ. Reversing the parameter sign recovers the stated phase convention. Thus Q generates the global phase symmetry. With normal ordering, Q counts fermions minus antiparticles, rather than the sum of both populations. Its conservation law is Dirac fermion number conservation. A position-dependent phase produces derivative terms in this free Hamiltonian operator; the symmetry under discussion is global.

Combine the two equal-mass Dirac fields into Ψ=(ψ1,ψ2)T. A constant unitary matrix U acting on this flavour index commutes with the common Dirac Hamiltonian, so Ψ↦UΨ gives the unitary flavour symmetry of degenerate Dirac fields U(2). Its real Lie algebra has dimension four. A convenient Hermitian generator basis is T0=12​/2, Ta=τa/2, a=1,2,3, where τa are the Pauli matrices.
For each generator the canonical anticommutation relations give
ρA​=Ψ†TAΨ,jAk​=ΨˉγkTAΨ,[ρA​(x),H]=−i∂k​jAk​(x).
(40)
The calculation is exactly the one in b, since TA commutes with both the Dirac gamma matrices and the common fermion mass matrix. Thus
Four independent number-preserving vector currents:jAμ​=ΨˉγμTAΨ,A=0,1,2,3.​
(41)
Explicitly their densities are
ρ0​=21​(ψ1†ψ1+ψ2†ψ2),ρ3​=21​(ψ1†ψ1−ψ2†ψ2),
(42)
ρ1​=21​(ψ1†ψ2+ψ2†ψ1),ρ2​=21​(−iψ1†ψ2+iψ2†ψ1).
(43)
The two off-diagonal conserved currents are why counting only the individual fermion numbers misses two generators. The singlet generates U(1); the other three generate special unitary group SU(2). Unequal masses would break the off-diagonal conservation laws.
Scope of the count. Four is the count of currents of the displayed ψˉ​AγμTAB​ψB form. The completely free system also has an enhanced internal symmetry of free Dirac fields if mixing with charge-conjugate fields is admitted. For m=0, write its two Dirac fields as four equal-mass Majorana spinors χI. Their orthogonal group O(4) rotations have six continuous generators, with conserved currents proportional to iχˉ​IγμχJ for I<J; the four U(2) currents form a subgroup. This larger count is relevant if “similar” means all local internal currents of the free system, without preserving a chosen fermion number. If m=0, the usual number-preserving chiral flavour symmetry of massless Dirac fields is U(2)L​×U(2)R​ with eight currents; allowing charge conjugation mixing enlarges it to U(4) with sixteen. These counts concern internal currents, excluding spacetime Noether currents.
