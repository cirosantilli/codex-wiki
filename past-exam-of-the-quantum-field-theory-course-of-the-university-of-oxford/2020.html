Let p1​,p3​ label the incoming and outgoing fermion, and p2​,p4​ the incoming and outgoing antifermion. The bottom row of the figure gives the two tree-level Feynman diagrams:
  • The t channel has an open fermion line 1→3 and an open antifermion line with fermion flow 4→2. The scalar propagator carries qt​=p1​−p3​=p4​−p2​.
  • The s channel has initial particle-antiparticle annihilation into a scalar propagator with qs​=p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​, followed by creation of the final pair.
Thus the Mandelstam variables in the two denominators are
t=(p1​−p3​)2,s=(p1​+p2​)2.​
(4)
The two scattering amplitude terms have a relative fermionic sign of minus when written with spinor products (uˉ3​u1​)(vˉ2​v4​) and (vˉ2​u1​)(uˉ3​v4​). The overall phase depends on the chosen ordering of the external states. No u channel joins an incoming fermion to an outgoing antifermion through a species-preserving Yukawa interaction.

Define M by removing the factor i(2π)4δ(4)(p1​+p2​−p3​−p4​) from the connected transition matrix element. The single tree-level Feynman diagram in i gives
iM=(−ig)2q2−m2+i0i​[uˉs3​​(p3​)us1​​(p1​)][uˉs4​​(p4​)us2​​(p2​)],q=p1​−p3​.
(5)
There is no identical-particle exchange term because the species differ. Each incoming fermion has two equally populated spin angular momentum states in an unpolarized beam. Their probabilities multiply to 1/4, giving a spin average over the four initial choices. The unobserved final spin angular momentum states are distinct outcomes, so their probabilities are added by a spin sum, with no final averaging:
∣M∣2​=41​∑s1​,s2​,s3​,s4​​∣M∣2.
(6)
Taking the complex conjugate of uˉ3​u1​ gives uˉ1​u3​. The fermion spin sum therefore converts each squared bilinear into a gamma-matrix trace:
∣M∣2​=4(q2−m2)2g4​tr[(p3​+m)(p1​+m)]tr[(p4​+m)(p2​+m)].
(7)
Using tr1=4, trγμ=0, tr(γμγν)=4ημν, and the mass shell conditions pj2​=m2, each gamma-matrix trace is
4(p1​⋅p3​+m2)=4(2m2−2q2​)=2(4m2−q2).
(8)
The second equality follows from q2=2m2−2p1​⋅p3​; momentum conservation gives the identical result for p2​,p4​. Hence
∣M∣2​=g4(m2−q24m2−q2​)2.​
(9)
The printed prefactor g2 is incorrect for the stated coupling. Each of the two Yukawa interaction vertices contributes one g to the scattering amplitude, so its square is proportional to g4. As a concrete counterexample, take g=2, m>0, and forward elastic kinematics p3​=p1​, p4​=p2​. Then q2=0 and the spin average is 16g4=256, whereas the printed expression gives 16g2=64.

For a connected amputated Feynman diagram, write L for the number of independent loop momenta. Each loop momentum integration scales as λ4, each internal scalar propagator as λ−2, and each internal Dirac propagator as λ−1 when both four-momenta and mass are scaled. The Yukawa coupling has mass dimension zero. Thus
dS​=4L−nf​−2ns​.
(10)
Counting the two fermion ends and one scalar end at each interaction vertex, and using connectedness, gives
2nf​=2nv​,2ns​+n=nv​,L=nf​+ns​−nv​+1.
(11)
Consequently nf​=nv​, L=ns​+1, and
dS​=4+2ns​−nv​=4−n.​
(12)
The requested exponent requires amputation and removal of the overall momentum delta function. An ordinary unamputated connected Green function has an additional n external scalar propagators, giving mass dimension 4−3n after stripping that delta function, or −3n if it is retained. For a concrete check, the free two-point scalar propagator scales as λ−2, not λ2. The exponent 4−n is the engineering result for the amputated object. A fixed renormalization scale introduces logarithms; the displayed homogeneity is a naive power counting statement rather than an exact quantum scaling law at fixed subtraction scale.
More generally, with Es​ external scalar ends and Ef​ external fermion ends, the same counting gives the superficial degree of divergence
D=4−Es​−23​Ef​.​
(13)
This is an upper bound on the overall ultraviolet divergence. Numerator cancellations and Lorentz invariance may lower it; an apparently convergent whole Feynman diagram can still have ultraviolet subdivergences. Local subtraction of those ultraviolet subdivergences is required first.
For pure scalar ends, Es​=0,1,2,3,4 can require counterterms. Lorentz invariance reduces the two-point terms to a mass counterterm and wave-function renormalization; derivatives of a single scalar field are total derivatives, and a one-derivative cubic scalar term cannot be a Lorentz scalar. With two external fermions, Es​=0 requires their kinetic term and mass counterterm, while Es​=1 requires Yukawa coupling renormalization. Four external fermions give D=−2, so no independent four-fermion counterterm is required.
A complete counterterm Lagrangian consistent with the species symmetry can therefore be written
Lct​=​2δZϕ​​(∂ϕ)2−2δms2​​ϕ2−δtϕ−3!δκ​ϕ3−4!δλ4​​ϕ4−δE+i=1∑2​ψˉ​i​(δZψ​i∂−δmf​)ψi​−δgϕi=1∑2​ψˉ​i​ψi​.​​
(14)
The vacuum energy density term matters for an unnormalized vacuum functional and cancels from normalized Green functions. The tadpole counterterm can impose ⟨ϕ⟩=0. There is no ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry of the massive Yukawa interaction, so linear, cubic and quartic scalar terms cannot be excluded. For instance, in a constant background the divergent fermion loop contribution contains (m+gϕ)4, exhibiting all these powers. This is counterterm closure of a massive Yukawa theory.
The two species have identical couplings, so their common unitary flavour rotations preserve the Lagrangian density and their counterterms can be chosen species independent. Equality of the scalar and fermion masses, however, is not protected: δms2​ and δmf​ are independent. Including the displayed finite set of operators yields a renormalizable quantum field theory. Restricting the bare Lagrangian density to the terms printed in the question is not closed under renormalization.

Write ∫p​=∫d3p/(2π)3 and dp​=ap​−bp​. The nonzero canonical commutation relations are
[ap​,aq†​]=[bp​,bq†​]=(2π)3δ(3)(p−q),

with cross-species brackets zero. The commutator product rule gives
[Q,aq​][Q,bq​][Q,aq†​][Q,bq†​]​=∫p​dp​[dp†​,aq​]=−dq​,=∫p​dp​[dp†​,bq​]=+dq​,=∫p​[dp​,aq†​]dp†​=dq†​,=∫p​[dp​,bq†​]dp†​=−dq†​.​
(16)
Linearity of the commutator now yields, for the real coefficient s,
[Q,aq​+sbq​][Q,aq†​+sbq†​]​=−(1−s)(aq​−bq​),=(1−s)(aq†​−bq†​).​​
(17)
Strictly, the printed Q contains an infinite constant in continuum Fock space. Work first with finitely many modes, or replace it by its normal ordering :Q:=∫p​dp†​dp​. Their difference is a constant, so every displayed commutator and every conjugation below is unchanged. The latter convention fixes the vacuum state phase without an infinite phase ambiguity.

For a Hermitian operator Q, the operator exponential Cλ​=eiλQ is a unitary operator, with Cλ†​=e−iλQ. Differentiation and [Q,Cλ​]=0 give
dλd​(Cλ​ACλ†​)=iQCλ​ACλ†​−iCλ​ACλ†​Q=iCλ​[Q,A]Cλ†​.
(18)
When [Q,A]=cA, this is the first-order ordinary differential equation A′(λ)=icA(λ) with A(0)=A. Multiplication by e−icλ makes its derivative zero, proving
A(λ)=eicλA.​
(19)
Equivalently, the commutator expansion for exponential conjugation has successive nested commutators crA, whose exponential series sums to this expression.

Use the combinations a±​=a±b at each momentum. Part a gives [Q,a+​]=0, [Q,a−​]=−2a−​, while their creation operators obey [Q,a+†​]=0, [Q,a−†​]=2a−†​. Applying b gives
Cλ​aCλ†​Cλ​bCλ†​​=21+e−2iλ​a+21−e−2iλ​b,=21−e−2iλ​a+21+e−2iλ​b.​
(20)
The creation operators transform by the Hermitian adjoint formulas with e+2iλ. At λ=π/2, both exponentials are −1, so
Cap​C†=bp​,Cbp​C†=ap​,Cap†​C†=bp†​,Cbp†​C†=ap†​,C=Cπ/2​.​
(21)
Substituting into the fixed-time mode expansion of a free field of the complex scalar field gives
Cϕ(x)C†=∫p​2Ep​​a−p†​+bp​​eip⋅x=ϕ†(x).
(22)
Thus C exchanges particles and antiparticles, as required for charge conjugation. To see the charge reversal explicitly, the conserved charge Qem​=∫p​(ap†​ap​−bp†​bp​) obeys CQem​C†=−Qem​. The generator Q in the question is distinct from this charge.
The two occupation terms in the Hamiltonian are exchanged, with the same relativistic energy Ep​, and therefore
CHC†=H.​
(23)
In fact [Q,H]=0 because its mixing terms exchange equal-energy modes. With normal ordering, :Q:=2∫p​c−,p†​c−,p​ for c−​=(a−b)/2​. Then C=eiπN−​ fixes the vacuum state and satisfies C2=1. The construction is a bosonic species-swap generator.

Take parity symmetry in quantum field theory to preserve free time evolution, so the conjugate initial data reflect along with the field. Substitute the mode expansion of a free field into the defining parity operator relation and change the integration variable p↦−p. Since E−p​=Ep​, coefficient comparison, or comparison of both the field and its conjugate initial data, gives the intrinsic-even convention
Pap​P†Pap†​P†​=a−p​,=a−p†​,​Pbp​P†Pbp†​P†​=b−p​,=b−p†​.​​
(24)
Here parity symmetry in quantum field theory reverses spatial momentum and preserves time. The creation operator formulas follow by taking Hermitian adjoints. The given field relation has fixed the intrinsic phase to +1.
For one real scalar field, let R act on one-particle wave functions by (Rf)(p)=f(−p). It is a Hermitian operator with R2=1, so P−​=(1−R)/2 is the orthogonal projector onto odd wave functions. Its second quantization is the odd-mode number operator
Nodd​=21​∫p​ap†​(ap​−a−p​)=41​∫p​(ap†​−a−p†​)(ap​−a−p​).
(25)
The full momentum space is integrated in both expressions; the factors prevent double counting the pairs {p,−p}. A suitable parity operator is
P=exp(iπNodd​)=exp[2iπ​∫p​ap†​(ap​−a−p​)].​
(26)
For verification, [Nodd​,ap​]=−(ap​−a−p​)/2. Thus the even combination ap​+a−p​ is unchanged, and the odd combination ap​−a−p​ changes sign under conjugation by eiπNodd​, exactly reproducing ap​↦a−p​. The same holds for creation operators. The odd-mode number operator has integer eigenvalues, so P2=1, and P∣0⟩=∣0⟩. Multiplying by an overall constant phase would not change its conjugation action. This is parity as odd-mode number parity.

Keep λ for the cubic coupling and j for the external source. Expanding the interaction part of the scalar field path integral yields
Z[λ;j]=∑V=0∞​V!1​(−3!iλ​)V∫∏v=1V​d4yv​∫Dϕ∏v=1V​ϕ(yv​)3eiS0​[ϕ]+i∫jϕ.
(27)
A functional derivative of the source exponential inserts iϕ(y). In particular, δj​(y)3ei∫jϕ=−iϕ(y)3ei∫jϕ, so −iλϕ(y)3 can be replaced by λδj​(y)3. The functional derivatives commute and can be taken outside the free path integral. Resumming the exponential series gives
Z[λ;j]=exp[3!λ​∫d4yδj(y)3δ3​]Z[0;j].​
(28)
This is a formal perturbation series; a real cubic scalar potential is unbounded and the expression does not assert a stable nonperturbative vacuum state.
To evaluate the free Gaussian path integral, use integration by parts to write
S0​[ϕ]+∫jϕ=21​∫ϕKϕ+∫jϕ,K=−□−m2+iϵ.
(29)
The inverse kernel is
K−1(x,y)=D(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+iϵe−ip⋅(x−y)​.
(30)
Completing the square with ϕ=χ−K−1j changes the exponent to i∫χKχ/2−i∫jK−1j/2. The translation-invariant functional measure makes the remaining Gaussian path integral precisely Z[0;0], hence
Z[0;j]=Z[0;0]exp[−2i​∫d4xd4y∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+iϵj(x)j(y)e−ip⋅(x−y)​].​
(31)
For ϵ>0, the exponent includes −ϵ∫ϕ2/2, which damps the regulated finite-dimensional integrals. The Feynman i-epsilon prescription displaces the positive-energy pole below the real p0 axis and the negative-energy pole above it, selecting the time-ordered vacuum expectation value. Indeed the Feynman propagator is ΔF​=iD. Thus iϵ specifies both convergence and the vacuum state boundary conditions, with ϵ→0+ taken after integration.

For the interacting vacuum state defined perturbatively by the same Feynman i-epsilon prescription, the normalized time-ordered product is
⟨Tr=1∏m​ϕ(xr​)⟩=Z[λ;0]1​im1​δj(x1​)⋯δj(xm​)δmZ[λ;j]​​j=0​.​
(32)
Each functional derivative inserts iϕ, accounting for i−m. The denominator implements cancellation of vacuum bubbles, while disconnected pieces carrying external insertions still contribute. The lower limit i=i in the printed product is a typographical error; the product here runs from 1 to m.
At order V in the cubic scalar field interaction term, the expansion of the generating functional supplies 1/[V!(3!)V]. The V! counts permutations of indistinguishable integrated interaction vertices, and 3! counts permutations of the three field slots at each interaction vertex. Applying the Wick theorem pairs fields into Feynman propagators. Several pairings produce the same Feynman diagram; their number NG​ multiplies the expansion coefficient. For fixed labelled external points, the net combinatorial weight is
wG​=V!(3!)VNG​​=SG​1​.​
(33)
Here SG​ is the order of the graph's automorphism group when the external labels are fixed, with permutations of identical parallel lines and reversal of a self-loop included as applicable. This is the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor convention in which a Feynman diagram is divided by SG​. Each cubic interaction vertex then contributes −iλ, each line contributes ΔF​, and each internal vertex is integrated over its position. External labelled points are not divided by m!.

Hold the external endpoints fixed and distinguish the three Feynman diagrams by their left-to-right order in the source. Write S for the divisor and w=1/S for the multiplying combinatorial weight. Each line below includes external Feynman propagators; amputation leaves the same w.
Left: two-point bubble. There are two cubic interaction vertices and two parallel internal lines. Interchanging those lines preserves the labelled external endpoints, giving S=2. Direct Wick contraction counting gives two assignments of the external endpoints to the vertices, 32 choices of their field slots, and 2! pairings of the remaining slots:
Nleft​=2⋅32⋅2=36,wleft​=2!(3!)236​=21​.​
(34)
With Δ=ΔF​ and external points x1​,x2​, its contribution is
2(−iλ)2​∫d4yd4zΔ(x1​−y)Δ(y−z)2Δ(z−x2​).
(35)
Middle: three-point triangle. The three labelled external endpoints fix their three adjacent cubic interaction vertices, so no nonidentity graph automorphism remains. Counting gives 3! assignments of endpoints to vertices, 33 choices of external field slots, and 23 assignments of remaining slots to the two specified neighbours:
Nmiddle​=3!3323=1296,wmiddle​=3!(3!)31296​=1.​
(36)
Its contribution is
(−iλ)3∫∏r=13​d4yr​[∏r=13​Δ(xr​−yr​)]Δ(y1​−y2​)Δ(y2​−y3​)Δ(y3​−y1​).
(37)
The apparent rotational symmetry of an unlabelled triangle does not survive fixing three distinct external endpoints.
Right: two-point graph with a chord. There are four cubic interaction vertices: the left and right attachment vertices A,B and the two chord ends C,D. Its internal edges are AC,AD,BC,BD,CD. Reflection interchanges C,D, fixes A,B, and preserves the two external labels. This is the only nontrivial graph automorphism, so S=2.
For a separate Wick contraction count, choose the ordered external attachment vertices in 4⋅3 ways and their field slots in 32 ways. Assign their two remaining slots to C,D in 22 ways. At each of C,D, choose the slot joining A and then the slot joining B in 3⋅2 ways; the last slots join each other. Thus
Nright​=12⋅9⋅4⋅62=15552,wright​=4!(3!)415552​=21​.​
(38)
Its contribution is
2(−iλ)4​∫d4Ad4Bd4Cd4DΔ(x1​−A)Δ(B−x2​)Δ(A−C)Δ(A−D)Δ(B−C)Δ(B−D)Δ(C−D).
(39)
Consequently the three multiplying weights are (1/2,1,1/2)​, or divisors (2,1,2)​. The central chord is a single internal line joining two vertices, not a vertex at the centre of the circle.
