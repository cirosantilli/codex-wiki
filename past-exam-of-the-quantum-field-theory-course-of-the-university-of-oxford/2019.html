A real scalar field suffices to describe one neutral species of bosonic quasiparticle. Let π(x) be its canonical momentum. In three spatial dimensions, choose the positive classical Hamiltonian
H[ϕ,π]=21​∫d3x[π2+κ2(∇2ϕ)2+m2ϕ2].​
(1)
The same construction works in any number of spatial dimensions by replacing d3x appropriately. Under a spatial Fourier transform, ∇2ϕ becomes −∣p∣2ϕ​, so the Hamiltonian is a collection of independent quantum harmonic oscillators with squared angular frequency ωp2​=κ2∣p∣4+m2. The usual reality condition pairs opposite momenta; it does not introduce a second species.
The canonical action is S=∫dt[∫d3xπϕ˙​−H]. Varying π gives π=ϕ˙​; eliminating it by the Legendre transform gives
S[ϕ]=21​∫dtd3x[(∂t​ϕ)2−κ2(∇2ϕ)2−m2ϕ2].​
(2)
This Lagrangian density is local: only the field and finitely many derivatives at the same point occur. It contains second spatial derivatives but only first time derivatives, so no extra canonical time data are needed. A square root of a differential operator in the classical Hamiltonian is unnecessary. The fourth-order spatial term breaks Lorentz invariance while preserving spatial rotations and translations. This is a free scalar field with quartic spatial dispersion.

For a Lagrangian density depending on ϕ, ∂t​ϕ, ∂i​ϕ and ∂i​∂j​ϕ, direct variation gives
δS=∫dtd3x[Lϕ​δϕ+Lϕ˙​​∂t​δϕ+L∂i​ϕ​∂i​δϕ+L∂i​∂j​ϕ​∂i​∂j​δϕ].
(3)
Take δϕ to have compact support, so every boundary term vanishes. One integration by parts for the first-derivative terms and two for the second-derivative term yield the Euler-Lagrange field equation with second derivatives
Lϕ​−∂t​Lϕ˙​​−∂i​L∂i​ϕ​+∂i​∂j​L∂i​∂j​ϕ​=0.
(4)
The plus sign on the last term is essential. For the action in a, it is particularly transparent to vary the Laplacian directly:
δS=∫dtd3x[ϕ˙​∂t​δϕ−κ2∇2ϕ∇2δϕ−m2ϕδϕ]=−∫dtd3x[ϕ¨​+κ2∇4ϕ+m2ϕ]δϕ.
(5)
Thus the classical equation of motion is
∂t2​ϕ+κ2∇4ϕ+m2ϕ=0.​
(6)
Here ∇4=(∇2)2 is the biharmonic operator. Substitution of the plane wave ϕ=Ae−iωt+ip⋅x gives
[−ω2+κ2∣p∣4+m2]A=0,ω=±κ2∣p∣4+m2​.​
(7)
A real classical solution combines complex-conjugate plane waves. Quantization assigns the positive branch to the excitation energy, giving the required dispersion relation. The negative-frequency branch supplies the creation operator part of the field, rather than negative excitation energy. Independently, the Hamiltonian in a gives ϕ˙​=π, π˙=−κ2∇4ϕ−m2ϕ, confirming the same sign.

Define Ep​=κ2∣p∣4+m2​ and ∫p​=∫d3p/(2π)3. The Hermitian quantum field has the mode expansion of a free field
ϕ(t,x)=∫p​2Ep​​1​[ap​e−iEp​t+ip⋅x+ap†​eiEp​t−ip⋅x],​
(8)
where the annihilation operators and creation operators obey the bosonic canonical commutation relations
[ap​,aq†​]=(2π)3δ(3)(p−q),[ap​,aq​]=[ap†​,aq†​]=0.​
(9)
Because π=ϕ˙​, its mode expansion of a free field is
π(t,x)=−i∫p​2Ep​​​[ap​e−iEp​t+ip⋅x−ap†​eiEp​t−ip⋅x].
(10)
The two nonzero cross commutators then give
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=2i​∫p​[eip⋅(x−y)+e−ip⋅(x−y)]=iδ(3)(x−y),
(11)
and the equal-time field-field and momentum-momentum commutators vanish. This verifies the factor 1/2Ep​​ without requiring Lorentz invariance.
Substitution into the Hamiltonian, followed by normal ordering, yields
:H:=∫p​Ep​ap†​ap​.​
(12)
Consequently [:H:,ap†​]=Ep​ap†​: one application of a creation operator adds precisely the required quasiparticle energy. Before normal ordering there is the usual constant zero-point vacuum energy. If m=0, the exactly zero-momentum mode has E0​=0 and is a free canonical coordinate, not an oscillator with a normalizable oscillator vacuum state; in a finite box handle that mode separately or introduce a small positive mass. The formula applies directly to all modes with Ep​>0.

Use the Fourier transform convention ϕ(x)=∫d4p/(2π)4e−ip⋅xϕ​(p), and similarly for Φ. All four-momenta at an interaction vertex are incoming. The quadratic terms are diagonal, so the scalar propagators are
Dϕ​(p)=p2−m2+i0i​,DΦ​(p)=p2−M2+i0i​,DϕΦ​=0.​
(13)
The field interaction terms are λϕΦ2/2 and gΦ∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ/2. The two identical field slots cancel the factor 1/2 at either cubic interaction vertex. Expanding exp(iSint​) therefore gives
ϕ(p)Φ(k)Φ(ℓ):Φ(k)ϕ(p)ϕ(q):​iλ,p+k+ℓ=0,−igp⋅q,k+p+q=0.​​
(14)
In the second rule p,q are specifically the two ϕ four-momenta; there is no derivative acting on the Φ leg. Indeed the two derivatives give (−ipμ​)(−iqμ)=−p⋅q, fixing the minus sign. This is a mixed scalar derivative cubic vertex. For arbitrary off-shell legs one may equivalently write −ig(k2−p2−q2)/2, but may not replace the squared four-momenta by squared masses unless those legs are on their mass shells.
Each interaction vertex also carries (2π)4δ(4)(∑p) for momentum conservation. Integrate independent loop momenta with d4p/(2π)4 and divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. An amputated external scalar leg has factor 1. The Feynman i-epsilon prescription fixes the propagator poles. As a dimensional check in four spacetime dimensions, [ϕ]=[Φ]=1, [λ]=1 and [g]=−1 in mass dimensions; the two powers of momentum in the derivative rule restore mass dimension one for its cubic interaction vertex.

Let p1​,p2​ denote the incoming ϕ,Φ four-momenta and p3​,p4​ the outgoing ϕ,Φ four-momenta. Thus p1​+p2​=p3​+p4​, p12​=p32​=m2 and p22​=p42​=M2. Define the Mandelstam variables and exchanged four-momenta by
s=(p1​+p2​)2=qs2​,u=(p1​−p4​)2=qu2​,qs​=p1​+p2​,qu​=p1​−p4​=p3​−p2​.
(15)
The leading connected four-point scattering amplitude uses two cubic interaction vertices. There are four tree-level Feynman diagrams: s and u exchange of Φ with two λ vertices, and s and u exchange of ϕ with two g vertices.
There is no term proportional to λg: one vertex of each kind has three field slots of each species altogether, whereas the external state uses two of each. This leaves one ϕ and one Φ to contract internally, and their mixed scalar propagator is zero. There is also no t channel: grouping both external ϕ legs at one vertex would require a g vertex with an internal Φ, while grouping both external Φ legs at the other would require a λ vertex with an internal ϕ.
Define iM after removing the overall momentum conservation delta function. The nonderivative exchange gives
iMλ2​=(iλ)2[s−M2+i0i​+u−M2+i0i​].
(16)
For the derivative s channel, the incoming ϕ four-momenta at the first vertex are p1​,−qs​, and at the second they are qs​,−p3​. The two interaction vertices are therefore igp1​⋅qs​ and igp3​⋅qs​. The same reasoning with qu​ applies to the u channel, giving
iMg2​=−ig2[s−m2+i0(p1​⋅qs​)(p3​⋅qs​)​+u−m2+i0(p1​⋅qu​)(p3​⋅qu​)​].
(17)
The mass shell conditions and momentum conservation imply
p1​⋅qs​=p3​⋅qs​=2s+m2−M2​,p1​⋅qu​=p3​⋅qu​=2u+m2−M2​.
(18)
Combining the four Feynman diagrams therefore yields
M=​−λ2[s−M2+i01​+u−M2+i01​]−4g2​[s−m2+i0(s+m2−M2)2​+u−m2+i0(u+m2−M2)2​].​​
(19)
Each displayed tree-level Feynman diagram has Feynman-diagram symmetry factor 1 for fixed external species and labels. The result is of order λ2,g2; if both couplings are scaled by a common parameter ε, the next connected corrections begin at order ε4. A four-point cubic Feynman diagram has 3V=2I+4, so its vertex count V is even. The internal lines remain off shell throughout: imposing an on-shell relation on an exchanged four-momentum would incorrectly change the derivative vertices.

With η=diag(1,−1), the Clifford algebra gives (γ0)2=1, (γ1)2=−1, and γ0γ1=−γ1γ0. Hence
γ5γ0=−γ1,γ0γ5=γ1,γ5γ1=−γ0,γ1γ5=γ0,
(20)
so {γ5,γ0}={γ5,γ1}=0. Its square is
(γ5)2=γ0γ1γ0γ1=−(γ0)2(γ1)2=1.
(21)
Choose the following gamma matrices, expressed using the Pauli matrices:
γ0=σ1​=(01​10​),γ1=−iσ2​=(01​−10​),γ5=σ3​=(10​0−1​).​
(22)
These satisfy (γ0)†=γ0, (γ1)†=−γ1 and (γ5)†=γ5. The chirality eigenvalues are therefore +1,−1​, and we order the spinor as ψ=(ψR​,ψL​)T throughout. Its Dirac adjoint is ψˉ​=(ψL†​,ψR†​). No additional factor of i multiplies γ0γ1 in this spacetime dimension. This realizes a Dirac field in two spacetime dimensions.

Variation of the action with respect to the independent Dirac adjoint gives (iγ0∂t​+iγ1∂x​−m)ψ=0. The gamma matrices chosen in a make the Dirac equation
(−mi(∂t​+∂x​)​i(∂t​−∂x​)−m​)(ψR​ψL​​)=0.​
(23)
Multiplication by γ0 gives the equivalent Dirac Hamiltonian form
i∂t​ψ=(−iσ3​∂x​+mσ1​)ψ.
(24)
For a plane wave ψ=ue−iEt+ipx, where p is the signed one-dimensional momentum, the equation is
(E−p−m​−mE+p​)u=0.
(25)
A nonzero spinor exists precisely when the determinant vanishes:
E2−p2−m2=0,E=±p2+m2​.​
(26)
For m=0, one possible unnormalized spinor solution is u=(E+p,m)T. Alternatively, (pσ3​+mσ1​)2=(p2+m2)1 follows from the Pauli matrix multiplication law, proving the same relativistic energy-momentum relation. Acting on the Dirac equation with iγμ∂μ​+m also shows that each component satisfies (∂t2​−∂x2​+m2)ψ=0, the Klein-Gordon equation.

In the chirality basis of a, the two rows of the Dirac Hamiltonian equation give
i(∂t​+∂x​)ψR​=mψL​,i(∂t​−∂x​)ψL​=mψR​.​
(27)
The Dirac mass term thus couples the two opposite chirality components. In particular, the Lagrangian density is
L=iψR†​(∂t​+∂x​)ψR​+iψL†​(∂t​−∂x​)ψL​−m(ψL†​ψR​+ψR†​ψL​).
(28)
For m=0, the coupling disappears and the massless Dirac equation splits into the independent transport equations
(∂t​+∂x​)ψR​=0,(∂t​−∂x​)ψL​=0.​
(29)
These are the two chiral transport equations in two spacetime dimensions. The chiral projectors are PR​=(1+γ5)/2 and PL​=(1−γ5)/2; in this basis they simply select the upper and lower components.

Continue with m=0. Along a characteristic x=x0​+t, the chain rule gives
dtd​ψR​(t,x0​+t)=(∂t​+∂x​)ψR​=0.
(30)
Thus the initial wave packet ψR​(0,x)=f(x) evolves as ψR​(t,x)=f(x−t). Similarly, along x=x0​−t, the left component is constant, giving
ψR​(t,x)=f(x−t),ψL​(t,x)=h(x+t).​
(31)
A feature initially at x0​ therefore appears at x0​+t for the right component and x0​−t for the left component. The profiles translate without changing shape at the speed of light, which is 1 in these units. For positive-energy plane waves, the right component has E=p>0 and the left component has E=−p>0, another way to identify their propagation directions.
The conclusion concerns the massless Dirac equation. For m=0, the components couple as in c, and a positive-energy packet has group velocity p/p2+m2​, rather than a universal speed ±1. For example, a massive positive-energy rest solution has p=0 and ψR​=ψL​, so neither component alone describes an independently moving light-speed packet.

A continuous Lorentz transformation in one spatial dimension must preserve t2−x2. In the component connected to the identity, take the image of the time axis to have velocity v, with ∣v∣<1, and write Bv​(1,0)T=γ(1,v)T. Preservation of the Minkowski norm gives γ2(1−v2)=1, so γ=(1−v2)−1/2. The spacelike column must be orthogonal to the timelike column and have squared Minkowski norm −1; continuity fixes it to γ(v,1)T. Thus
x′=Bv​x,Bv​=1−v2​1​(1v​v1​).​
(32)
Here x=(t,x)T denotes the coordinate pair. This is the positive off-diagonal convention in the source; describing a passive frame moving at +v instead reverses the sign of v.
Define the rapidity α=artanhv. The hyperbolic function identities give
coshα=1−v2​1​,sinhα=1−v2​v​,B(α)=(coshαsinhα​sinhαcoshα​).​
(33)
In light-cone coordinates, t′+x′=eα(t+x) and t′−x′=e−α(t−x). Therefore the derivatives transform oppositely:
∂t′​+∂x′​=e−α(∂t​+∂x​),∂t′​−∂x′​=eα(∂t​−∂x​).
(34)
Use the field-transformation convention ψ′(x′)=S(α)ψ(x). The component action in c is invariant under
ψR′​(x′)=eα/2ψR​(x),ψL′​(x′)=e−α/2ψL​(x),ΛR​(α)=eα/2,ΛL​(α)=e−α/2.​
(35)
Indeed, the right-component kinetic term gains eα/2e−αeα/2=1, and the left-component kinetic term gains e−α/2eαe−α/2=1. The two factors in each Dirac mass term cancel as well. Finally detB=1, so dt′dx′=dtdx and the whole action is unchanged. These are the chiral boost weights in two spacetime dimensions.
In spinor notation, S(α)=exp(αγ5/2) satisfies S−1γμS=Bμν​γν and S†γ0S=γ0, so ψˉ​′=ψˉ​S−1. These identities directly verify covariance of the Dirac equation and the Lorentz invariance of its action. The matrices S are not unitary with respect to the ordinary component norm; the Dirac adjoint supplies the invariant bilinear.
The statement about there being only Lorentz boosts applies to the identity component of the One-dimensional Lorentz group. The full Lorentz group also has discrete transformations: the concrete spatial reflection diag(1,−1) preserves the Minkowski metric but has determinant −1, so it cannot equal any displayed B(α), whose determinant is +1.
