The additional six-leg interaction vertex has Feynman rule −ig6​. Since both interactions lack derivatives, the graph identity still gives ω=2(1−V), with V=V4​+V6​. The difference is that a one-vertex contraction of the phi-six theory can leave four external legs: choosing the contracted pair gives (26​)=15. Its quartic interaction correction is
6!g6​​15TΛ​ϕ4=4!1​2g6​TΛ​​ϕ4.
(10)
Consequently a one-loop quartic interaction counterterm is now necessary:
δg4(1)​=−2g6​​TΛ​=−8πg6​​log(1+m2Λ2​).​
(11)
The one-loop mass counterterm from c is unchanged. Contracting four legs of a single six-leg interaction vertex gives (26​)3=45 double contractions, hence a two-loop raw self-energy g6​TΛ2​/8. Its subtraction must include the quartic interaction counterterm insertion, rather than subtracting this raw expression in isolation.
All divergent field-dependent coefficients are most simply organized by the normal-ordering subtraction scheme in two dimensions. With T=TΛ​, express a potential normal ordered at mass m in ordinary fields:
VB​(ϕ)=24g4​​(ϕ4−6Tϕ2+3T2)+720g6​​(ϕ6−15Tϕ4+45T2ϕ2−15T3).
(12)
Thus its divergent subtraction coefficients, in this scheme, are
δg4​=−2g6​T​,δm​=−2g4​T​+8g6​T2​,δg6​=δZ​=0.​
(13)
Indeed the two-loop self-energy terms are g6​T2/8+δg4​T/2=−g6​T2/8, which the displayed mass counterterm cancels. The constant terms only affect vacuum energy. After removing all one-vertex self-contractions, every remaining subgraph is ultraviolet finite. Additional finite on-shell renormalization scheme adjustments are subject to the same qualification as d.
