For nonzero real g, the continuous internal symmetry of a classical field theory is the global U(1) symmetry
ϕ⟼e−iαϕ,Φ⟼e−2iαΦ,α constant.
(14)
The kinetic terms and mass terms are invariant separately, while the phases in ϕ2Φ† cancel. Independent phase rotations would instead require 2αϕ​−αΦ​=0 modulo 2π, leaving one connected circle. Simultaneous complex conjugation is also a discrete symmetry, reversing the continuous charge; including it gives U(1)⋊Z2​≅O(2) for generic parameters. At g=0 the continuous symmetry enlarges to U(1)×U(1).
The Noether current with particles assigned positive charges is
jμ=i(ϕ†∂μϕ−(∂μϕ†)ϕ)+2i(Φ†∂μΦ−(∂μΦ†)Φ).​
(15)
For example, put A=ϕ2Φ† and B=ϕ†2Φ. The Euler-Lagrange equations give ∂μ​jϕμ​=ig(A−B) and ∂μ​jΦμ​=ig(B−A), with the factor 2 already included in jΦμ​. Their sum vanishes. The Noether charge Q=∫d3xj0 is conserved and, after normal ordering, counts
Q=Nϕ​−Nϕˉ​​+2(NΦ​−NΦˉ​).
(16)
Thus a Φ particle carries two units of the same conserved charge carried by a ϕ particle; their antiparticles carry the negatives. The interaction permits conversion of one Φ particle into two ϕ particles when allowed by conservation of energy, while preserving this weighted number.
