The momentum space Feynman rules use the scalar propagator and two Dirac propagators
Δϕ​(p)=p2−m2+i0i​,Sχ​(p)=p2−m2+i0i(p+m)​,SΨ​(p)=p2−M2+i0i(p+M)​.​
(1)
The cubic scalar field interaction vertex is −iλ. Each of the two flavour-changing Yukawa coupling interaction vertices, ϕΨˉχ and ϕχˉ​Ψ, has factor −igδab​ in Dirac spinor indices. A fermion flow arrow continues through each interaction vertex, but its species changes. There is no ϕΨˉΨ or ϕχˉ​χ interaction vertex.
Every interaction vertex carries (2π)4δ(4)(∑p) with inward four-momenta. Integrate each independent loop momentum with d4ℓ/(2π)4, divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor, and include a minus sign and a gamma-matrix trace for each closed fermion loop. For an amputated scattering amplitude, incoming fermions supply us(p) and outgoing fermions supply uˉs(p); incoming antifermions supply vˉs(p) and outgoing antifermions supply vs(p). An external real scalar field particle supplies 1. Use the standard relativistic normalization of a one-particle state; the external factors 1/2E​ belong to that normalization and the Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure, rather than an additional factor in the invariant scattering amplitude.

For Ψ(p1​)→χ(p2​)+ϕ(k1​)+ϕ(k2​), the sole tree-level Feynman diagram has a Yukawa coupling interaction vertex producing an off-shell scalar field particle of four-momentum q=p1​−p2​=k1​+k2​, followed by its cubic scalar field interaction vertex. Its invariant decay amplitude is
iM=(−ig)(−iλ)uˉχ​(p2​)uΨ​(p1​)(k1​+k2​)2−m2+i0i​.​
(5)
Two successive Yukawa coupling interaction vertices would change the fermion species twice and leave a Ψ, so they cannot produce this final state. Interchanging the two identical outgoing bosons does not create another Feynman diagram; the cubic factorial is already included in the Feynman rule. In the convention of iii, the invariant decay amplitude is Ms1​s2​​=−guˉχs2​​(p2​)uΨs1​​(p1​). Including the common overall i in the definition changes no squared decay amplitude. An unpolarized ensemble is an incoherent equal mixture of the two initial spin states; hence take a spin average over s1​ and a spin sum over the unobserved final s2​. Different final spin states are orthogonal outcomes, so their probabilities, rather than their decay amplitudes, add.
Using the Dirac adjoint and the fermion spin sum gives
∣M∣2​​=2g2​s1​,s2​∑​(uˉ2​u1​)(uˉ1​u2​)=2g2​tr[(p2​+m)(p1​+M)]=2g2(p1​⋅p2​+mM).​

m2=(p1​−p2​)2=M2+m2−2p1​⋅p2​,p1​⋅p2​=2M2​.

Put Q=M−3m. For Q<0 the decay width is zero by conservation of energy. Just above the three-particle decay threshold, all three daughters are nonrelativistic. The decay amplitude of iv remains finite: the internal scalar propagator denominator approaches (2m)2−m2=3m2, and the Dirac scalar bilinear does not vanish at rest. Thus, for nonzero fixed g and λ, the leading suppression comes entirely from three-body threshold phase space.
Use the spatial Dirac delta function to eliminate one daughter momentum. The remaining integral has six independent momentum coordinates and one conservation of energy constraint,
∫d3pd3qδ(Q−2mp2+q2+(p+q)2​).
(12)
Rescaling both momenta by mQ​ gives Q3 from the measure and Q−1 from the Dirac delta function. The smooth factors 1/(2E) do not change the power. Consequently
Γ=C(M,g,λ)(M−3m)2[1+O((M−3m)/M)]as m↑M/3,​
(13)
with a finite positive leading coefficient when gλ=0; Γ=0 for m≥M/3. The 1/2! for the identical bosons changes only this coefficient. Equivalently, at fixed M the opening is quadratic in M/3−m. If gλ=0, this leading tree-level Feynman diagram vanishes, so the stated leading coefficient is zero.

b OurBigBook.com
Let L be the number of independent loop momenta in a connected Feynman diagram. Every scalar propagator falls as k−2, and every loop momentum integration contributes three powers, so ω=3L−2e. Counting line ends and using connectedness gives
4n4​+6n6​=2e+n,L=e−n4​−n6​+1.
(26)
Eliminating L and e yields
ω=3(e−n4​−n6​+1)−2e=3−2n​−n4​.​
(27)
Thus increasing the sextic interaction vertex count does not worsen the superficial degree of divergence; adding a quartic interaction vertex improves it. Only finitely many external-line structures can have an overall ultraviolet divergence, even though arbitrarily high loop order is possible.
After subtracting ultraviolet subdivergences, locality and Lorentz invariance restrict the divergent counterterms to a constant vacuum energy density, a mass counterterm, a wave-function renormalization, and quartic and sextic field interaction terms. Odd powers are forbidden by the ϕ↦−ϕ Z2 symmetry. In particular, n=2 permits a constant and p2, n=4 permits only a constant, and n=6 permits only a constant. This is perturbative renormalizability, not a guarantee that every superficially allowed divergence actually occurs or that every unrenormalized graph with ω<0 lacks divergent subgraphs.

To include all momentum-dependent contributions to the two-point function, put one external line at each interaction vertex. Let r be the number of lines connecting the vertices, and tA​,tB​ their numbers of tadpole diagrams. For vertex valences vA​,vB​∈{4,6},
r+2tA​=vA​−1,r+2tB​=vB​−1.
(30)
The integer r is odd, and r=1 gives a one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram and is excluded. Thus the complete list is
  • vA​=vB​=4: r=3, tA​=tB​=0, the sunset diagram.
  • vA​=4,vB​=6: r=3, tA​=0,tB​=1, the sunset diagram with one tadpole diagram.
  • vA​=vB​=6: r=3, tA​=tB​=1, the sunset diagram with one tadpole diagram at each vertex.
  • vA​=vB​=6: r=5, tA​=tB​=0, the five-line banana integral topology.
The coefficient of the mixed graph includes the reflected external attachment as well as its reciprocal Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. If both external lines attach to the same interaction vertex, no external momentum enters the connecting internal lines, and that Feynman diagram cannot contribute to wave-function renormalization.
For incoming external momentum p, assign the connecting lines momenta k1​,…,kr−1​,p−∑j​kj​. Define
Br,M​(p)=∫∏j=1r−1​(2π)3d3kj​​[∏j=1r−1​D(kj​)]D(p−∑j=1r−1​kj​),r=3,5.
(31)
The momentum space Feynman rules give, in the order listed,
​C44​(−ig1​)2B3,M​(p),C46​(−ig1​)(−ig2​)TM​B3,M​(p),C66,3​(−ig2​)2TM2​B3,M​(p),C66,5​(−ig2​)2B5,M​(p).​​
(32)
Each C is a reciprocal Feynman-diagram symmetry factor, including any equivalent external attachments. There are respectively 2,3,4,4 independent loop momenta.
If “contribute to wavefunction renormalization” means requiring a divergent kinetic counterterm after subtraction of all subdivergences, only the last topology qualifies. The first three still have nontrivial external momentum dependence and give finite contributions to a normalization condition; their raw divergent factors arise from mass counterterms and interaction vertex counterterms. Keeping all four here makes this distinction explicit. The ultraviolet divergence analysis in d establishes it directly.

There are three independent nonfactorized momentum integrals, namely a tadpole integral, the three-line sunset diagram integral, and the five-line banana integral. The six displayed Feynman diagrams involve six complete integral expressions:
T,T2,B3​(P),TB3​(P),T2B3​(P),B5​(P).​

The distinction between independent building blocks and their products removes an ambiguity in counting “different momentum integrals”. For the strict divergent-wave-function renormalization interpretation of ii, the graphs in c reduce to T,T2,B5​, comprising three full integral expressions and two independent nonfactorized building blocks.
Make a Wick rotation and use m>0 to separate ultraviolet divergences from infrared divergences. Write DE​(k)=1/(k2+m2), and let T,Br​(P) be the Euclidean versions of the integrals above, with their harmless overall factors of i removed. A spherical ultraviolet cutoff gives
TΛ​=2π21​∫0Λ​k2+m2k2dk​=2π21​[Λ−marctan(Λ/m)]=2π2Λ​−4πm​+O(Λ−1).
(34)
Thus the one-vertex quartic graph is linearly divergent, and the one-vertex sextic graph is quadratically divergent as TΛ2​.
For the other two building blocks, the spatial Fourier transform is especially transparent. The three-dimensional Euclidean scalar propagator is
G(x)=∫(2π)3d3k​k2+m2eik⋅x​=4π∣x∣e−m∣x∣​.
(35)
The convolution theorem converts the r parallel lines into G(x)r. Angular integration gives, with a short-distance cutoff a=Λ−1,
Br(a)​(P)=(4π)r4π​∫a∞​ρ2−re−rmρPρsin(Pρ)​dρ,P=∣PE​∣.
(36)
This is a different convenient cutoff regularization for the connected block; power-divergent coefficients depend on the cutoff convention, whereas the conclusions about powers and the presence of a kinetic logarithm do not. The sinc function is understood to be 1 at P=0.
For r=3, the integrand starts as ρ−1+O(1), so
B3(a)​(P)=16π21​logma1​+O(1).
(37)
Its divergent part is independent of external momentum. In particular,
B3​(P)−B3​(0)=16π21​∫0∞​ρe−3mρ​[Pρsin(Pρ)​−1]dρ=−864π2m2P2​+O(P4).

vertices and integral4: T6: T24,4: B3​4,6: TB3​6,6: T2B3​6,6: B5​​(n4​,n6​)(1,0)(0,1)(2,0)(1,1)(0,2)(0,2)​ω120122​leading unrenormalized growthΛΛ2logΛΛlogΛΛ2logΛΛ2​​
