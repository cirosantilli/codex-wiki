For g=0, the invariant scattering amplitude from the tree-level Feynman diagram in i has a pole at s=M2. The intermediate heavy antiparticle can go on shell and live for a time of order its mean lifetime. Near this scattering resonance, inserting a single self-energy is no longer a uniformly small correction. Dyson resummation gives, to leading accuracy in a narrow decay width,
M(s)≃−s−M2+iMΓphys​h2​.​
(26)
Here M is understood as the renormalized resonance mass; the real self-energy shifts the original pole position. The imaginary term removes the unphysical divergence of the fixed-order invariant scattering amplitude.
For equal incoming and outgoing light masses, the two-body momentum ratio cancels in the cross section. The angle-independent invariant scattering amplitude, integrated over the full solid angle with the identical final-state symmetry factor 1/2!, gives σ=∣M∣2/(32πs). Thus the Breit-Wigner resonance cross section is
σ(s)≃32πs[(s−M2)2+M2Γphys2​]h4​,Γphys​=32πMh2β​.​
(27)
At s≫M2 it reproduces h4/(32πs3). At the resonance peak,
σ(M2)≃M2β232π​≃M232π​(M≫m).​
(28)
This is also 8π/k∗2​ for k∗​=Mβ/2, the elastic identical-boson S wave partial-wave unitarity limit when this is the only open decay channel. The approximation assumes Γphys​≪M and a resonance sufficiently far from threshold for the width to vary slowly across it.
If one mechanically uses the unsymmetrized Γprinted​=2Γphys​ from d, the same displayed Breit-Wigner resonance cross section would have that doubled width and predict a peak 8π/(M2β2). This is the literal-width substitution, rather than the consistent physical result: the given high-energy cross section already includes the identical-final-particle factor that the printed decay width integral omits.
