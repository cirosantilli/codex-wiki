Holomorphy from polynomial growth of arithmetic partial sums (∑n≤x​an​=O(xα) ⟹ ∑an​n−s holomorphic for Res>α)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Analytic number theory Dirichlet series

Let S(x)=∑n≤x​an​ satisfy ∣S(x)∣≤Cxα for x≥1. The Abel summation formula gives A(s)=s∫1∞​S(t)t−s−1dt when Res>α, since its finite-sum boundary term tends to zero. Split this integral into blocks Im​(s)=∫mm+1​S(t)t−s−1dt. For every δ>0, the estimate ∣Im​(s)∣≤Cm−1−δ holds uniformly on Res>α+δ. Each block is an entire function, by differentiation under the finite-range integral on compact sets. The Weierstrass M-test makes their sum locally uniformly convergent and therefore holomorphic; multiplication by s preserves holomorphy.
This proof distinguishes uniform convergence of the block sum on a whole half-plane from local uniform convergence after multiplication by s. The latter is all that holomorphy needs. In particular, a bound ∑n≤x​μ(n)=O(xα) gives a holomorphic continuation of the reciprocal Riemann zeta function to this half-plane and excludes zeta zeros there, with the pole at one treated by cancellation.
