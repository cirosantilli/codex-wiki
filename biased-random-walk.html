Biased random walk (ESn​=n(2p−1),p=21​)

 ... Probability theory Markov process Markov chain Random walk Random walk on a graph Simple random walk on the integer line
OurBigBook.com Words: 478 Articles: 5
A biased random walk on the integer line takes unit steps right and left with unequal probabilities p and 1−p, using independent and identically distributed random variables as increments. For S0​=0, the expected value is n(2p−1): positive for p>1/2 and negative for p<1/2. Changing p to 1−p reverses the preferred direction. The exact endpoint probability mass function is given under simple random walk on the integer line.
For example, with p=0.65 each step has expected value 0.30 and variance 0.91. After 80 steps,
ES80​=24,Var(S80​)=72.8,σ80​=72.8​≈8.532.
(85)
The displacement of the expected value from zero is about 24/8.532≈2.813 standard deviations, yet individual sample paths still move left as well as right, as the figure under simple random walk illustrates. Bias lets this elementary stochastic process model an average directional tendency while retaining fluctuations. Boundary and return questions have their own treatments under gambler's ruin and the specialized children below.
