Preparation uncertainty

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Robertson uncertainty principle Heisenberg uncertainty relation
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
Preparation uncertainty describes the outcome spread intrinsic to a specified quantum state preparation. Operationally, prepare many copies of the same quantum state, measure position on one group and momentum on another, and estimate the two variances. Ideal measurements still have the spreads predicted by the Born rule. No earlier position measurement need occur in the momentum group. The Heisenberg uncertainty relation constrains these two distributions belonging to the same preparation.
Measurement backaction concerns how a particular measurement in quantum mechanics changes a quantum state. Instrument resolution concerns how its reported outcomes relate to the intended observable. Those questions require a model of the instrument and its interaction with the system. Substituting an instrument error and an induced change for Δx and Δp in the Heisenberg uncertainty relation does not follow from its proof. The practical value of preparation uncertainty is that it bounds achievable input spreads before any such instrument model is chosen.
