Tangent vector

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
A tangent vector at a point p of a smooth manifold without boundary records an instantaneous velocity through p. Its base point is part of the data; measuring its length additionally requires a Riemannian metric. Intrinsically, a smooth curve γ with γ(0)=p defines its tangent vector by how every local smooth function changes along it:
v(f)=dtd​f(γ(t))​t=0​.
(1)
This is a derivation at a point. Two smooth curves through p represent the same tangent vector if these derivatives agree for every such smooth function; equivalently, their coordinate velocity vectors agree in one manifold chart. The chain rule makes that agreement independent of the chosen manifold chart. [ref]
For an embedded submanifold of Euclidean space, the intrinsic tangent vector is represented by the ordinary derivative γ′(0), with its base point p retained. A drawn arrow starting at p represents that tangent vector; its endpoint p+γ′(0) need not lie on the embedded submanifold. Nor is a tangent vector required to be a unit tangent vector: a stationary smooth curve gives the zero vector, which belongs to the tangent space but has no direction to normalize.
Here are explicit calculations. In R2, γ(t)=(t,t2) and η(t)=(t,2t2) have different routes but the same tangent vector (1,0) at (0,0). For f(x,y)=3x+y, both give v(f)=3. For a circle of radius R, c(t)=(Rcos(ωt),Rsin(ωt)) has tangent vector (0,Rω) at (R,0): changing ω changes the speed or reverses travel without changing the circle.
A reparametrization β(s)=γ(h(s)) with h(0)=0 gives, by the chain rule, β′(0)=h′(0)γ′(0). A regular change of curve parameter has h′(0)=0. The singular choice h(s)=s3 instead gives
γ(t)=(t,t2),β(s)=(s3,s6),γ′(0)=(1,0),β′(0)=(0,0).
(2)
Both describe the same parabola, whose tangent line at the origin is still the horizontal axis. The zero velocity diagnoses this singular change of curve parameter, whose inverse is not differentiable at zero; it does not show that the geometric tangent line is absent. The regular curve γ supplies its nonzero direction.
The diagram is an analytical illustration of these exact parametric curves, with equal parameter increments marked on each route. It separates geometry from traversal: the tangent line depends on the route, while a velocity vector also depends on how the curve parameter advances.

Derivation at a point (v:Cp∞​(M)→R)

Words: 46
Tangent vector OurBigBook.com
At p on a smooth manifold, a derivation at that point is a real-linear map v:Cp∞​(M)→R on germs of smooth functions with v(fh)=f(p)v(h)+h(p)v(f). It defines a tangent vector. The local factorization f−f(p)=∑i​(xi−xi(p))fi​ gives v(f)=∑i​v(xi)∂i​f(p), so the coordinate derivations form a basis of the tangent space.

Tangent space (Tp​M)

Words: 992 Articles: 7
Tangent vector OurBigBook.com
At a fixed point p of an n-dimensional smooth manifold M without boundary, the tangent space Tp​M is the real vector space of all tangent vectors at p. In manifold charts x=(x1,…,xn) and y=(y1,…,yn), a tangent vector has components
v=∑i=1n​vxi​∂xi∂​​p​,vya​=∑i=1n​∂xi∂ya​​p​vxi​.
(3)
The coordinate basis transforms inversely, so the tangent vector is unchanged. This invertible Jacobian matrix also preserves vector addition and scalar multiplication, giving a vector space of dimension n independently of coordinates. [ref]
For example, at (x,y)=(1,0) in R2, use the alternative manifold chart u=x, w=y+x2. The Jacobian matrix sends (vx​,vy​)=(2,3) to (vu​,vw​)=(2,7). Indeed, the smooth curve (x(t),y(t))=(1+2t,3t) gives w(t)=1+7t+4t2. Conversely, ∂u​∣p​=∂x​∣p​−2∂y​∣p​ and ∂w​∣p​=∂y​∣p​, so 2∂u​∣p​+7∂w​∣p​=2∂x​∣p​+3∂y​∣p​. The numbers change; their action on every smooth function does not.
For an embedded submanifold M⊆RN, the tangent space can be identified with the vector subspace
Tp​M={γ′(0):γ:(−ε,ε)→M is smooth, γ(0)=p}.
(4)
A proof outline explains why every coordinate vector is possible: in a manifold chart x, lift the short straight path x(p)+ta using x−1. Its tangent vector has components a. For a regular local parametrization X:U⊆Rn→M, the same chain rule gives Tp​M=imDXq​, where X(q)=p. This is the tangent space from a local parametrization.
For the sphere SR2​={p∈R3:p⋅p=R2}, differentiating that equation gives p⋅v=0. Conversely, for any nonzero v with p⋅v=0, the smooth curve
γv​(t)=pcos(R∥v∥t​)+R∥v∥v​sin(R∥v∥t​)
(5)
lies on the sphere and has γv′​(0)=v; use a constant smooth curve for v=0. Thus Tp​SR2​={v:p⋅v=0}, not merely a subset of that plane. At p=(0,0,R) this vector subspace is vz​=0, while the affine tangent plane drawn through p is z=R.
This also explains a mechanical constraint without requiring a force calculation. In the ideal mathematical model of a particle confined to a stationary sphere, its allowed velocities at p are exactly Tp​SR2​: the radial component must vanish, but either independent tangential direction is available. For a general stationary holonomic constraint F(q)=0 with independent equations, the regular level set theorem gives Tq​M=kerDFq​. These are instantaneous kinematic possibilities; they do not determine the actual subsequent motion. On a general smooth manifold, tangent spaces at different base points cannot be identified canonically; an affine connection supplies parallel transport when such comparisons are needed.

Tangent plane

Words: 404
Tangent space OurBigBook.com
For an embedded regular surface M⊆R3 and p∈M, the affine tangent plane is
p+Tp​M={p+v:v∈Tp​M}.
(6)
It is a set of points through p, whereas the tangent space Tp​M is a vector space of displacements with zero vector. Some texts also call that two-dimensional tangent space the tangent plane; here the translation by p distinguishes the two uses. For a regular parametric surface X(u,v) with p=X(u0​,v0​),
Tp​M=span{Xu​,Xv​},p+Tp​M={p+aXu​+bXv​:a,b∈R},
(7)
where the partial derivatives are evaluated at (u0​,v0​). Regularity means that these tangent vectors are linearly independent, equivalently Xu​×Xv​=0. Their cross product is a normal vector. [ref]
For an implicit regular surface F(x,y,z)=0, require a smooth function F with ∇F(p)=0. Then
Tp​M=kerDFp​={v:∇F(p)⋅v=0},p+Tp​M={r:∇F(p)⋅(r−p)=0}.
(8)
Differentiating F(γ(t))=0 proves that every surface velocity lies in the displayed kernel of a linear map; the implicit function theorem gives a local graph with two independent coordinates, proving equality. The gradient is the normal vector with respect to the ambient dot product. [ref]
For the sphere F(r)=r⋅r−R2, ∇F(p)=2p=0, and the affine tangent plane has equation p⋅r=R2. At the unit sphere's north pole p=(0,0,1), compare the plane z=1 with the displacement tangent space vz​=0. The regular parametric surface X(u,v)=(u,v,1−u2−v2​), for u2+v2<1, gives Xu​(0,0)=(1,0,0) and Xv​(0,0)=(0,1,0), confirming the same answer.
This analytical illustration shows a unit sphere and a vertical cross-section. The smooth curves γ1​(t)=(sint,0,cost) and γ2​(t)=(0,sint,cost) give the two displayed tangent vectors at p. The straight point p+tv for any nonzero v∈Tp​S2 is outside the sphere when t=0, since ∥p+tv∥2=1+t2∥v∥2. A tangent plane therefore captures first-order motion, not an exact patch of the regular surface.
The same idea makes local approximation useful. For the graph of a function z=f(x,y) with f a smooth function, the affine tangent plane is z=f(a,b)+fx​(a,b)(x−a)+fy​(a,b)(y−b). [ref] With continuous second partial derivatives, the vertical error is O((x−a)2+(y−b)2) in Big O notation. On the unit sphere near its north pole, 1−u2−v2​=1−21​(u2+v2)+O((u2+v2)2): the tangent plane loses quadratic bending while preserving all first-order velocities.
A failed regularity test needs interpretation. Replacing the sphere equation by G(r)=(r⋅r−1)2=0 makes ∇G=0 everywhere on the same perfectly regular sphere, so this defining smooth function cannot recover its tangent plane. Similarly, the usual spherical parametric surface loses matrix rank at the poles, while the graph parametrization above remains regular there. A vanishing gradient or degenerate parametrization invalidates that calculation; it alone does not prove that the underlying geometry has no tangent plane.

Tangent space by point derivations (Tx​M=Derx​(Cx∞​(M),R))

Words: 42
Tangent space OurBigBook.com
A tangent vector is a linear map on germs of smooth functions satisfying D(ab)=a(x)D(b)+b(x)D(a). In a manifold chart, first-order expansion shows Dh=∑i​D(ui)∂i​h(x). Thus the coordinate derivations form a basis, and the dimension equals the manifold dimension. Composition of germs with a smooth map defines its differential of a smooth map without choosing charts.

Cotangent space (Tp∗​M)

Words: 91 Articles: 1
Tangent space OurBigBook.com
The cotangent space at p is the dual vector space Tp∗​M=(Tp​M)∗. Its elements are covectors on the tangent space. In a manifold chart x, the basis dxi∣p​ is dual to ∂i​∣p​. Under a coordinate change to y, the coefficients of α=ai​dxi=bj​dyj obey bj​=∑i​ai​∂xi/∂yj. The disjoint union of these spaces is the cotangent bundle.

Inner product on exterior powers of a cotangent space (⟨ξ1​∧⋯∧ξp​,ζ1​∧⋯∧ζp​⟩=det(⟨ξi​,ζj​⟩))

Words: 37
Cotangent space OurBigBook.com
A Riemannian metric induces a dual inner product on each cotangent space. On decomposable covectors define ⟨ξ1​∧⋯∧ξp​,ζ1​∧⋯∧ζp​⟩=det(⟨ξi​,ζj​⟩) and extend bilinearly. Increasing wedges of an orthonormal coframe form an orthonormal basis. This positive-definite inner product is the one used to define the Hodge star operator.

Tangent space from a local parametrization

Words: 43
Tangent space OurBigBook.com
If ϕ:U⊆Rd→M⊆Rn is a local parametrization with ϕ(u)=p, then
Tp​M=imDϕu​.
(9)
The derivative has rank d, so this is a d-dimensional vector subspace. A change of parametrization composes Dϕu​ with an invertible coordinate-change derivative, leaving its image unchanged.

Coordinate basis (∂/∂xμ)

Words: 48
Tangent space OurBigBook.com
A manifold chart with coordinates xμ induces a smooth local frame of the tangent bundle, with tangent vectors ∂μ​=∂/∂xμ forming a basis of each tangent space. Their Lie brackets of vector fields vanish because mixed partial derivatives of smooth functions commute. Under a change of coordinates they transform by the chain rule: ∂/∂yν=(∂xμ/∂yν)∂/∂xμ, with coefficients given by the Jacobian matrix.

Unit tangent vector (T)

Words: 108
Tangent vector OurBigBook.com
For a regular curve r(t) with nonzero velocity vector, its unit tangent vector is
T(t)=∥r′(t)∥r′(t)​.
(10)
The Euclidean norm in the denominator removes the traversal speed, leaving a unit vector that records direction. A positive change of traversal speed leaves this direction unchanged; reversing the direction of travel reverses T. In arc length coordinates, T=dr/ds.
For the parabola r(t)=(t,t2), the velocity vector is (1,2t) and the speed is 1+4t2​. At t=1, T=(1,2)T/5​. The tangent vector (1,2)T and the unit tangent vector point in the same direction, but only the latter has Euclidean norm one. The principal normal vector is a further choice based on how this direction changes, where the curvature is nonzero. [ref]
