The pressure is 10−8mbar=10−6Pa. The molecular arrival flux from an ideal gas is
J=2πmkB​T​P​.
(2)
Because the residual-gas composition, temperature and sticking probability are not specified, the answer is an order-of-magnitude monolayer-equivalent exposure time, not a unique coverage prediction. Take T=300K, a nitrogen-like molecular mass m=28u=4.65×10−26kg, and unit sticking probability. Then
J≃2.88×1016m−2s−1.
(3)
Use the given 0.5nm molecular-layer scale as an approximate intermolecular spacing, giving Ns​∼(0.5nm)−2=4×1018m−2. Thus
τML​∼sJNs​​≃s1.4×102​s.​
(4)
For s=1, this is a few minutes; assuming instead 1019m−2 sites gives about 350s, the same rough scale. A clean-looking vacuum pressure does not imply a contamination-free surface. Desorption and a small sticking probability can greatly lengthen net accumulation, while residual water or hydrocarbons can dominate the chemistry. Full saturation in a site-blocking adsorption model is not exactly the same as one monolayer-equivalent incident fluence.
