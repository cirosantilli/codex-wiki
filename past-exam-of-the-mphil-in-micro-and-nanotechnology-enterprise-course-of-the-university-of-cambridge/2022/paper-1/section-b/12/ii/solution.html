A generator couple has a p-type and an n-type thermoelement joined electrically at the hot face, with their other ends connected through a load at the cold face. The legs are electrically in series and thermally in parallel. Electrically insulating, thermally conducting plates couple them to the hot source and cold sink; metal interconnects carry current. The Seebeck effect provides an open-circuit electromotive force E=αnp​ΔT, where the magnitude of αnp​ is the difference between the two leg Seebeck coefficients.
For fixed junction temperatures, constant properties and internal resistance R,
PL​=I2RL​=(R+RL​)2(αnp​ΔT)2RL​​.
(31)
Differentiate with respect to load resistance:
dRL​dPL​​=(αnp​ΔT)2(R+RL​)3R−RL​​.
(32)
The derivative changes from positive to negative at RL​=R, so
RL​=R,IPmax​​=2Rαnp​ΔT​,Pmax​=4R(αnp​ΔT)2​.​
(33)
The load voltage is then half the open-circuit voltage. The heat source and sink must sustain the temperature difference; an isolated hot object cools as energy is extracted. Maximum power and maximum conversion efficiency are different operating points: the efficiency-optimal load is generally larger than R.
