A lithium–oxygen system can have much greater theoretical active-material specific energy because it avoids the heavy transition-metal host and carbon host of the preceding insertion cell. Oxygen can be taken from the surroundings, but a mass comparison must say whether that oxygen is counted.
The peroxide reaction transfers two electrons per Li2​O2​ unit. If oxygen is counted, the reactant mass per two electrons is
M=2(6.94)+32.00=45.88gmol−1,
(29)
compared with 2(169.93)=339.86g for two electrons in the idealized lithium-ion reaction. At the same average voltage, the active-material specific energy would therefore be larger by 339.86/45.88≃7.4. If only stored lithium is counted, the ideal mass advantage is larger still, but this excludes the oxygen and is not a like-for-like closed-cell mass basis.
The source supplies no lithium-peroxide formation free energy or oxygen-electrode potential, so it does not determine an absolute lithium–oxygen voltage or specific energy. Real devices require an oxygen electrode, gas-management and electrolyte; insulating discharge products, parasitic reactions, charging overpotential and mass transport reduce the practical advantage. The question's reaction is chemical oxygen reduction, not a biological energy pathway.
