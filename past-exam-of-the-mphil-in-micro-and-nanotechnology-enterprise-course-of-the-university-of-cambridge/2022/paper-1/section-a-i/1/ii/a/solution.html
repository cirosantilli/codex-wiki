OurBigBook.com Words: 126
Use conventional bright-field transmission electron microscopy, with nearly parallel illumination, a suitably small objective aperture and low magnification covering the whole cross-section. More strongly scattering material sends more intensity outside the selected central beam and therefore looks darker. Tilt crystalline layers away from strong Bragg diffraction conditions to reduce diffraction contrast, and use a near-focus image so that electron phase contrast does not dominate.
The nominally uniform 150nm thickness helps separate composition from thickness variations, but does not make the contrast purely compositional: density, atomic number and multiple scattering still matter. Choose an accelerating voltage sufficient to transmit this thickness, commonly 200–300kV for a robust inorganic film, subject to its damage limit. High-angle annular dark-field imaging in scanning transmission electron microscopy is a useful alternative for approximately monotonic mass-thickness contrast, but is a different acquisition mode and still requires thickness and detector-angle control.
