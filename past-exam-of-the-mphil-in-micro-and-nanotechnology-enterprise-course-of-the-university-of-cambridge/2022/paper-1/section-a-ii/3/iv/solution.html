A negative photoresist is removed at low dose and retained at high dose. Its remaining-thickness curve rises from zero above a gel or retention threshold and approaches one above a larger full-retention threshold. A useful schematic model is
f−​(D)=⎩⎨⎧​0ln(Db​/Da​)ln(D/Da​)​1​D≤Da​,Da​<D<Db​,D≥Db​.​
(5)
The developed profile is t(x)/t0​=f−​[τI(x)]. A central intensity maximum therefore leaves a central ridge, with cleared regions towards the darker sides. Halving exposure narrows and can lower the retained ridge; doubling it broadens the ridge and can raise its shoulders. A saturated central plateau cannot become thicker than the original film in this simple model. If the half-dose peak is below Da​, no ridge survives; if it lies between Da​ and Db​, the centre survives only partially.
The negative-resist thresholds are not supplied, so there is no unique numerical profile or linewidth.
