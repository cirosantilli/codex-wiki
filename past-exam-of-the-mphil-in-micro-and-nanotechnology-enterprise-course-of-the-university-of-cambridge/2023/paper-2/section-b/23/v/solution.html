Define γSV​, γSL​ and γLV​ as the solid-vapor, solid-liquid and liquid-vapor interfacial free energies per actual area. For the corresponding planar material, Young's contact-angle equation is
γSV​−γSL​=γLV​cosθ.
(42)
Consider advancing the macroscopic liquid footprint over projected area A, while retaining the specified wetting only to each sphere's equator. The newly wetted upper hemispheres have actual area 2ϕA. The gaps occupy projected area (1−ϕ)A, across which a liquid-vapor interface remains instead of solid-liquid contact.
The microscopic free-energy change, apart from the outer macroscopic droplet surface, is
AΔF​=2ϕ(γSL​−γSV​)+(1−ϕ)γLV​.
(43)
Equating this with −γLV​cosθ∗, the macroscopic wetting contribution, yields
cosθ∗=2ϕcosθ−(1−ϕ)=ϕ(2cosθ+1)−1.​
(44)
The factor 2 comes from the curved hemisphere area; the negative gap term is the trapped liquid-vapor interface. This combines Wenzel roughness on the wetted solid fraction with Cassie-type air gaps. It differs from both homogeneous Wenzel wetting and a Cassie formula that treats the solid contacts as flat.
For ϕ=0.9069 and θ=68∘, this gives cosθ∗≃0.5864 and θ∗≃54.1∘. The hybrid state is less wettable than the fully penetrated hemispherical model, but still not hydrophobic. The result is conditional on the assumed equatorial meniscus and retained air: pinning or metastability may be required, and allowing further penetration can change the equilibrium geometry.
