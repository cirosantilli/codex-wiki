Absorption generates electrons and holes in the perovskite. After relaxation toward the band edges, their nonequilibrium populations are described by separate electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels. Selective contacts collect electrons through an electron-transport layer and holes through a hole-transport layer, while suppressing the opposite carriers.
For ideal contacts at open circuit,
qVOC​≃EFn​−EFp​​.
(47)
This quasi-Fermi-level splitting is not the band gap itself or simply the equilibrium built-in voltage. At equilibrium there is one common Fermi level and no extractable photovoltage. Under illumination at open circuit, generation is balanced by recombination while a nonzero population splitting persists.
Important parasitic paths include trap-assisted Shockley-Read-Hall recombination in the bulk, at grain boundaries and at interfaces, and recombination at poorly selective contacts. They lower the steady carrier populations and their quasi-Fermi splitting. Auger recombination can matter at high carrier densities but need not dominate an ordinary one-sun device. Radiative recombination is an unavoidable detailed-balance contribution, rather than an avoidable defect loss of the same kind.
An approximate illuminated diode relation is
J(V)=JSC​−J0​[eqV/(nid​kB​T)−1],VOC​=qnid​kB​T​ln(1+J0​JSC​​).
(48)
Increased nonradiative recombination generally increases the dark recombination current and reduces VOC​; it can also change ideality and fill factor. Shunts add a leakage path. Series resistance mainly reduces voltage under load and fill factor: its simple IRs​ drop vanishes at zero terminal current, so it should not be invoked as an automatic direct subtraction from open-circuit voltage.
