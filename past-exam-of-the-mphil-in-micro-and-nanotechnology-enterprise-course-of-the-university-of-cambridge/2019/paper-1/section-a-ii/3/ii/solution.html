On a graph of normalized remaining thickness against log10​D, both positive photoresist curves remain near unity below D0​ and fall to zero by Dc​. Their transition widths are log10​(Dc​/D0​)=1/γ: 0.752 decades for the lower-contrast material and 0.177 decades for the higher-contrast material. The latter therefore has a much steeper transition. The curves can be aligned at a common illustrative clearing dose; their absolute horizontal placement cannot be inferred from photoresist contrast alone.
The higher-contrast material distinguishes bright and dark regions even when diffraction has reduced their intensity modulation. The lower-contrast material requires a much stronger aerial-image modulation and loses small features earlier. Finite resist thickness, standing waves, acid diffusion, development conditions and photon statistics can limit actual performance beyond this threshold model.
