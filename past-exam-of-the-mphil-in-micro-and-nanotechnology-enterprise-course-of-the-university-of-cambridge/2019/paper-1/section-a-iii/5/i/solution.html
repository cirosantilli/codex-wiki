charge stabilization of colloids supplies repulsion when like-charged surfaces and their electric double layers approach. steric stabilization uses attached or adsorbed molecular layers whose overlap costs conformational and osmotic free energy. Combining both gives electrosteric stabilization; a solvated coating must remain attached and well solvated. A viscous medium slows encounters but does not by itself create an equilibrium repulsive barrier.
DLVO theory adds screened electrostatic repulsion to Van der Waals force. A representative close-gap form is
U(h)≃Be−κh−12hAH​a​,
(5)
for equal spheres of radius a, with Hamaker constant AH​ and inverse Debye–Hückel screening length κ. The formula is schematic near molecular contact, where hydration, surface roughness and finite ion size matter. A repulsive barrier many kB​T high produces kinetic stability even if the primary attractive minimum is deeper than the dispersed state. Entry into that primary minimum generally gives strongly bound aggregation; a shallow secondary minimum can give reversible flocculation.
Adding electrolyte increases κ, shortens the repulsion and can remove the barrier, enabling fast diffusion-limited aggregation. The critical coagulation concentration is the approximate salt concentration at which this rapid-coagulation regime begins under specified conditions. It is not a universal property of the salt alone. Test the prediction using time-resolved dynamic light scattering or turbidity at controlled particle concentration, pH and temperature; check that added ions do not specifically adsorb or change the surface chemistry.
