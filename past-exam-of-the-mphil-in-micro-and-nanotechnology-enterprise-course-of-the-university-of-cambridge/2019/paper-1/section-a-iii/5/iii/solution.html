In the high-surface-potential Schulze-Hardy rule, the critical coagulation concentration scales approximately as z−6 with the counterion valence. Hence
cAl​=cCa​(32​)6=0.0172964​=8.78×10−4 molL−1​.
(6)
The more highly charged counterion therefore requires about eleven times less concentration in this idealized estimate. This assumes the cations are the counterions of a negatively charged surface, the surface-potential regime is appropriate, and the only changing mechanism is electrostatic stabilization. Specific adsorption and hydrolysis of aluminium ions can invalidate this scaling; matching ionic strengths instead is not the same prediction.
