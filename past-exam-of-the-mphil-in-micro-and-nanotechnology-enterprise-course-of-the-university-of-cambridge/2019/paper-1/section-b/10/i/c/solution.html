A Pickering emulsion is stabilized by particles adsorbed at the liquid–liquid interface. A partially wetting sphere removes a patch of that interface. The smaller detachment barrier into either bulk phase is approximately
ΔE=πrp2​γow​(1−∣cosθ∣)2,
(15)
where θ is the three-phase contact angle. At approximately neutral wetting, θ=90∘, the stated particle radius gives
ΔE=π(20×10−9)2(0.050)=6.28×10−17 J≃1.53×104kB​T​
(16)
at 25∘C. Such a large attachment energy makes thermal detachment unlikely. A sufficiently covered interface resists drop coalescence, and a water-wet particle preference supports an oil-in-water dispersion. However, radius alone does not guarantee stabilization: completely water-wet or oil-wet particles have a vanishing attachment barrier in this model, and sufficient particle coverage is necessary. Sedimentation and droplet ripening are separate failure modes. The classical particle-stabilized-emulsion experiments provide the historical basis of the terminology.[ref]
