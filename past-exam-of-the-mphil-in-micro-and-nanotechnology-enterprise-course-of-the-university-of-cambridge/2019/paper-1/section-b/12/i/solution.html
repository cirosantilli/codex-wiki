A thermoelectric generator uses the Seebeck effect and needs a spatial temperature gradient: a couple develops V≃(Sp​−Sn​)ΔT between hot and cold junctions. It can deliver continuous power under a steady thermal gradient and has no moving parts, but requires sustained heat flow and adequate hot- and cold-side thermal contacts. Thermal conduction, internal electrical resistance and the material's thermoelectric figure of merit limit conversion.
A pyroelectric generator responds to a time-dependent spontaneous electric polarization. Under approximately uniform heating or cooling, I=pAdT/dt when the electrical boundary condition allows charge flow. It can harvest temporal thermal fluctuations without a persistent spatial gradient. However, at a constant uniform temperature its ordinary pyroelectric current stops; practical extraction needs cycling, low-leakage electronics and a suitable load. A large open-circuit voltage is not evidence of a large available average power. Temperature-driven nanowire measurements provide a primary experimental example of this distinction.[ref]
