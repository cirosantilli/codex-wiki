From the illuminated side downward, the p-type gallium arsenide cap provides a low-resistance route to the front metal contact. It should remain beneath the contact grid but be removed from the optical aperture to reduce parasitic absorption. The wider-band gap p-type aluminium gallium arsenide window transmits much of the useful light and reduces front-surface recombination of carriers in the adjacent absorber. The optical-window and reduced-recombination rationale has a primary experimental basis in early heteroface devices.[ref]
The p-type gallium arsenide emitter and n-type gallium arsenide base form the p-n junction. Absorbed photons generate electrons and electron holes; diffusion and the junction's electric field direct them toward the appropriate contacts. The n-type aluminium gallium arsenide back-surface-field layer provides a barrier to minority holes moving toward the substrate and reduces rear recombination. The n-type gallium arsenide substrate supports the epitaxial layers and conducts majority carriers to the rear contact.
The front metal must collect current with limited shading and the back metal provides the return contact. Interpreting the patterned hatched region as the optical antireflection coating, it reduces reflected light over the exposed window. Carrier selectivity, low recombination, low series resistance and low shading must work together; the equilibrium junction field alone is not a source of electrical energy.
