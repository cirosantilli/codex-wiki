For cubic lattice planes, dhkl​=a/h2+k2+l2​. The face-centred cubic selection rule permits indices that are all odd or all even. Thus
d111​=0.2336nm,d200​=0.2023nm,d220​=0.1430nm.
(13)
The first two spacings exceed the quoted 0.19 nm real-space resolution, whereas d220​ does not. This restriction is for the resolved image and its transform; a diffraction instrument need not have the same cutoff.
A natural candidate is a beam near a local ⟨110⟩ zone axis. For example, the [110] zone law h+k=0 includes four {111} reflections and two {200} reflections, all within that real-space cutoff. Their reciprocal-space length ratio is g200​/g111​=2/3​=1.1547. The angle between (11ˉ1) and (11ˉ1ˉ) reciprocal vectors is cos−1(1/3)=70.53∘. In contrast, a [001] view gives a fourfold arrangement of the resolvable {200} family, while the lowest ordinary [111] zone reflections are {220} and would not be resolved at the stated cutoff.
In a five-domain decahedral interpretation, neighboring tetrahedral fcc regions meet on {111} twin planes around a common near-⟨110⟩ axis. Five unstrained wedges supply 5×70.53∘=352.64∘; elastic distortion accommodates the remaining 7.36∘. Their rotated diffraction contributions need not resemble a single six-spot [110] pattern. Near-⟨110⟩ fcc domains in a multiply twinned particle are a defensible orientation proposal, not a unique full indexing. The printed transform has no reciprocal-space scale or indexed spot list, so it cannot establish every domain rotation or exclude an overlapping-particle contribution. Calibrated spacings and transforms from individual regions would settle those distinctions. The five-domain geometry is independently observed in atomic-resolution gold-particle measurements.[ref]
