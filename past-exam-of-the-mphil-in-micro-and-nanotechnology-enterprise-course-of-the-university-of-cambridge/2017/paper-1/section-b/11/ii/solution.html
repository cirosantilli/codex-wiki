ηa​=0.295log10​(200/1)=0.6788V.

Assume the quoted limiting current uses the same cathodic area convention. A steady diffusion-film model gives cs​/cb​=1−j/jL​=1/41. The Nernst equation then makes the cathodic concentration overpotential magnitude
∣ηc,conc​∣=2FRT​ln41=0.04765V.

Relative to the local surface equilibrium potential, the cathode also needs an activation overpotential. Neglecting anodic concentration polarization, the separated-loss accounting is
Vapp​≃1.9265V+∣ηc,act​∣​.
(23)
To calculate that activation term, adopt a single effective two-electron Butler-Volmer equation with constant transfer coefficients satisfying αa​+αc​=1, no anodic concentration polarization and reduction proportional to the surface Cu2+ concentration. The supplied anodic Tafel slope then determines the reverse slope through
αa​=2FbRTln10​≃0.10016,αc​=1−αa​≃0.89984.
(24)
With f=2F/(RT) and cathodic overpotential ηc​ referenced to bulk equilibrium,
j0​jc​​=exp(αa​fηc​)−cb​cs​​exp(−αc​fηc​).
(25)
Solving at jc​=−200Am−2 gives ηc​≃−0.12854V, including both concentration and activation effects. Thus
Vapp​≃1.20+0.67880+0.12854=2.0073V≃2.01V.​
(26)
The 0.04765 V concentration loss must not be added again. The kinetic assumption matters: the supplied slope is for copper oxidation, and an exchange current density alone does not specify every cathodic current-potential relation. A multistep copper reaction need not obey the effective single-step relation used for this estimate.
