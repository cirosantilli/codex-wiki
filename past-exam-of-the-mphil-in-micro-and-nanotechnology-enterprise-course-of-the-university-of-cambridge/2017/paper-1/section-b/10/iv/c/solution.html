Let Psat​ denote the flat-interface saturation pressure at the same temperature, PV​ the surrounding equilibrium vapor pressure, and Vl​ the incompressible liquid molar volume. Equating liquid and ideal-vapor chemical potentials to their common reference at Psat​ gives
Vl​(pl​−Psat​)=RTlnPsat​PV​​.
(17)
Thus the pressure contribution to the attractive normal force on a wetted circular area is
Fp​=πa2(PV​−pl​)=πa2[Vl​RT​lnPV​Psat​​+PV​−Psat​].
(18)
For capillary pressures large compared with the vapor-pressure difference, and a bridge wetting essentially the plate area,
F≃Vl​πa2RT​lnPV​Psat​​​.
(19)
Here F is the magnitude of the external separating force balancing attraction, and PV​<Psat​. A finite contact angle adds a contact-line force 2πaγsinθ at each plate's rim, counted once in the force on that plate, while the exact pressure also retains both curvatures. The rim term is normally smaller than the narrow-gap pressure term by order d/a for appreciable wetting; it cannot be neglected uniformly near θ=90∘. For complete wetting the leading equivalent expression is F≃2πa2γ/d. The corresponding equilibrium humidity is PV​/Psat​≃exp[−2γVl​/(RTd)].
These are quasistatic, equilibrium expressions for an existing bridge, neglecting viscous suction, contact-angle hysteresis, disjoining forces and deformation of the plates. The wetted radius must replace a if the liquid does not reach the plate edge. During actual separation a bridge can shrink, depin or break, so this local force balance is not a promise of a constant force for arbitrary travel.
