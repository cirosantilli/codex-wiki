A tightly focused laser produces a strong intensity gradient. A small polarizable particle experiences a gradient force toward increasing intensity when the real polarizability has the appropriate positive sign; radiation pressure from scattering and absorption pushes predominantly along the beam. A high-numerical-aperture objective creates lateral confinement and a sufficiently strong opposing axial gradient for a three-dimensional optical trap. Equilibrium is generally shifted from the intensity maximum by radiation pressure.

Viscous drag opposes motion, Brownian forcing produces fluctuations, and gravity/buoyancy are normally small at this scale.
For absorbing gold, use its complex wavelength-dependent permittivity: scattering, absorption and heating can frustrate trapping. The existence of an ideal dielectric gradient-force trap does not guarantee stable trapping of every metal nanoparticle at every wavelength or power.[ref]
