The electron-optical column contains an electron gun, an accelerating supply, a condenser lens system, an objective aperture, an electron objective lens, scan coils and alignment/stigmation controls. The electron gun may use thermionic emission or field emission. The condenser lens system sets the incident probe current and source demagnification; the electron objective lens focuses the probe on the specimen. A movable specimen stage controls position, tilt and working distance. The column and specimen chamber need a vacuum system, appropriate pumps and specimen-transfer hardware. Detectors collect secondary electrons, backscattered electrons or characteristic X-rays; amplification and synchronized raster acquisition assign a measured signal to each image pixel. The first two signals provide different imaging information, while characteristic X-rays allow elemental analysis. A source, probe-forming optics, raster scan, specimen stage, detectors, acquisition electronics and vacuum system form the essential chain.[ref]

Model the AFM cantilever as a driven damped harmonic oscillator. If the drive stays at the free-space resonance and the spring constant k and effective mass m are unchanged, its steady amplitude is
A=(k−mω2)2+(γω)2​Fd​​,A(ω0​)=(γ0​+γts​)ω0​Fd​​.
(2)
Here γ0​ is intrinsic damping and γts​ the added dissipative interaction with the surface. At large average separation there is no tapping, so γts​≃0 and A=Afree​. As the lower turning point reaches the surface, intermittent contacts dissipate energy and the amplitude decreases. With further approach the oscillation becomes strongly constrained, eventually approaching a continuously contacting, nearly quenched response. The damping-only sketch has a free-amplitude plateau followed by a descending amplitude on approach. The rounded transition in the sketch is illustrative; adhesion and actual contact mechanics determine its shape. The deliberately omitted force-gradient-induced resonance shift can make real approach curves more complicated.

ii OurBigBook.com
An uncorrected amplitude image has a useful vertical range only across the transition between free oscillation and strongly suppressed oscillation. If the free motion is a few tens of nanometres peak-to-peak, its one-sided amplitude is half that excursion. The relevant vertical height range is therefore of the order of the oscillation excursion, only tens of nanometres, rather than micrometres. It is not a sharp universal limit equal to the quoted peak-to-peak value: the chosen mean gap, contact law and usable sensitivity determine the actual range. A sufficiently high feature can quench the motion or produce destructive contact; a sufficiently deep depression leaves the probe oscillating freely with essentially no height sensitivity. This argument limits height tracking, not the lateral width of a feature. Lateral resolution instead depends on AFM tip convolution, sampling and the probe-surface interaction.

In tapping-mode atomic force microscopy, demodulate the cantilever displacement to obtain its amplitude and compare it with a setpoint below Afree​. A closed-loop control system drives a piezoelectric scanner in z to restore the setpoint as the raster crosses raised or depressed regions. The interaction remains in its sensitive amplitude range while the scanner follows heights much larger than the oscillation amplitude. The principal height image is the calibrated feedback z displacement at each lateral pixel. The amplitude-error and phase channels are supplementary signals, rather than interchangeable height measurements. Maximum height is now bounded by scanner travel, clearance and stable tracking. Steep features require adequate feedback bandwidth and sufficiently slow scanning; trace/retrace disagreement tests tracking, while a changed AFM probe tests geometric broadening. Surface-dependent dissipation can move the setpoint height even on a geometrically flat specimen.

A sacrificial layer temporarily supports the intended moving structure and determines its clearance above the substrate. One workable surface micromachining sequence deposits silicon dioxide on silicon, opens anchor windows in that oxide, deposits a structural polysilicon film and patterns the beam and release openings. The structural film contacts the substrate at the anchors. Selective removal of the oxide beneath the beam leaves the beam suspended while retaining its attachment. An access opening or exposed perimeter is essential; an etchant cannot instantly reach sealed material.
The release needs substantial lateral removal, so an isotropic etch with high etch selectivity is useful. Hydrofluoric-acid oxide release is compatible with a suitable polysilicon structure; a directional reactive-ion etching step alone would mainly clear exposed vertical paths and leave material beneath the beam. Choose the structural material, anchor design, sacrificial material and etchant together, and allow for transport-limited undercut and structural residual stress. Wet release followed by ordinary evaporation can pull a compliant structure onto the substrate through capillary force and cause stiction. critical point drying or a compatible vapor-phase release etch avoids the receding liquid interface. Neither method removes the need to check residues, selectivity and anchor survival. Pattern the support and structure first, then selectively undercut the support while preserving the anchors.[ref]

Oxygen-plasma ashing, preferably a remote or downstream arrangement when low damage matters, removes organic photoresist chemically. Feed oxygen through a plasma source energized by radiofrequency or microwave power. Electron-impact dissociation generates reactive oxygen species; neutral radicals reach the wafer in a separate downstream region. Keep the wafer off a strongly biased driven electrode so energetic ion bombardment is much smaller than in directional reactive-ion etching. A barrel asher with shielded wafers is another chemical-stripping implementation.
O2​+e−⟶2O+e−,Cx​Hy​+(2x+y/2)O⟶xCO2​+(y/2)H2​O.
(3)
These equations represent radical production and an ideal complete-oxidation balance; actual exhaust can also contain CO and fragments. Pumping removes volatile products. Directional shape fidelity is unnecessary for blanket stripping, while access around surface relief and high chemical removal selectivity are useful. Monitor endpoint and wafer temperature, and verify removal with a witness surface. oxygen can oxidize exposed materials, charging can still damage sensitive structures, and inorganic or metal-containing residues may require a compatible separate clean. Ashing removes organic contamination; it is not a universal residue-removal step.[ref]

A thermoelectric generator maintains hot and cold junctions of two materials with different Seebeck coefficients. Mobile charge carriers redistribute in the temperature gradient, producing an open-circuit voltage of magnitude ∣V∣=∣Sp​−Sn​∣ΔT for constant coefficients. A load permits electric current; pairs of p-type and n-type legs are commonly connected electrically in series and thermally in parallel. Maintaining the output requires continuous heat transfer from the hot reservoir and rejection of heat to the cold reservoir. The thermoelectric figure of merit ZT=S2σT/κ expresses the favorable combination of large Seebeck coefficient, large electrical conductivity and small thermal conductivity. Contact losses, Joule heating and parasitic heat leaks reduce practical efficiency.
A pyroelectric generator instead exploits a change of spontaneous electric polarization with temperature. If the relevant component of the pyroelectric coefficient is p=dPs​/dT, an electrode of area A receives a short-circuit electric current approximately I=pAdT/dt. A heating stroke and a cooling stroke produce opposite charge changes. Rectification or a synchronized electrical cycle can transfer energy to storage. A spatial temperature difference drives the thermoelectric device; a temporal temperature change drives the pyroelectric device. Uniform, constant temperature gives no continuing current from pyroelectricity even if the material is hot. Reported nanowire generators deliberately use changing temperature, rather than infer continuous generation from a static hot specimen.[ref]
Both approaches can be compact, silent and free of macroscopic moving parts. The thermoelectric generator gives continuous output under a sustained gradient, but needs two effective thermal contacts and sufficient heat flow; very small ΔT gives a small voltage and thermal contacts can consume much of the available gradient. Thin films exhibiting pyroelectricity can respond to intermittent heating without a permanently maintained hot/cold separation across their thickness, but require repeated thermal cycling, low electrical leakage and suitable charge extraction. Their thermal time constant limits useful cycling frequency, and sensible heating/cooling can dominate the heat budget. A steady hot pipe with an available cold sink favors a thermoelectric generator; periodic radiative heating can favor a pyroelectric generator. Neither converts equilibrium thermal motion at a single fixed temperature into sustained useful work.

In transmission electron microscopy, the electron objective lens forms an intermediate real-space image and intermediate/projector lenses enlarge it onto a screen or camera. The total optical magnification is the product of the relevant stage magnifications. The user changes imaging-lens excitations to select magnification, while separately controlling illumination and focus. Display resizing introduces an additional enlargement, so the recorded pixel calibration matters.
In scanning transmission electron microscopy, a focused probe is rastered across the specimen and a detector signal is assigned to each probe position. The displayed magnification is display width divided by scanned specimen width. The user increases magnification by reducing raster-deflection amplitude, independently of choosing a smaller incident probe with the probe-forming lenses. TEM changes image-forming lens magnification; STEM changes the raster field of view. A smaller field or more pixels does not create finer physical resolution than the probe, specimen scattering and detection permit. A calibrated real-space scale is more transferable than the apparent size on a monitor.[ref]

Choose cyclic reciprocal units, so the incident and elastically scattered wavevectors have magnitude 1/λ. Place the reciprocal lattice origin O at the end of the incident vector from the sphere centre C. A reciprocal vector g is excited when its endpoint G lies on the Ewald sphere, because
ks​=k0​+g,∣ks​∣=∣k0​∣=1/λ.
(12)
A specimen tilt rotates the reciprocal lattice relative to this construction. The very short electron de Broglie wavelength makes the sphere nearly flat across several ordinary reciprocal-lattice spacings. In a thin foil, finite thickness t broadens reciprocal points into reciprocal lattice rods approximately along the foil normal, with characteristic longitudinal extent 1/t. A rod may intersect the sphere even when its ideal point is slightly off it. Consequently several reflections can appear simultaneously near a zone axis, and thinning changes the excitation tolerance. A small particle has finite-size broadening in other directions too. This geometrical excitation condition does not guarantee a particular spot intensity: crystal basis structure factors, specimen thickness and dynamical diffraction still matter.

Indexed electron diffraction ring positions from a polycrystalline deposit identify candidate phases and give lattice parameters after camera-length calibration. Spot patterns from a small selected area establish zone axis directions, epitaxial orientation relationships or crystal twinning. Nanobeam mapping can reveal grain orientation changes, while systematic spot shifts can measure relative strain if lens distortions and scan drift are controlled. Spot or ring broadening can reflect finite coherent size, but also strain and instrumental broadening, so it is not an unqualified particle-diameter measurement. Use a calibration standard and an empty support region, vary dose to check electron beam damage, and combine spacing information with elemental analysis when chemically distinct phases have similar lattice parameters.

The resolved periodic contrast comes principally from electron phase contrast: transmitted and diffracted electron waves interfere after passing through the image-forming optics. The resulting intensity depends on specimen thickness, orientation, defocus and the contrast transfer function; bright dots cannot automatically be labelled gold-atom positions without image simulation. Finite numerical aperture and diffraction remove high spatial frequencies from a mask image. Neighboring features modify the aerial intensity, and the photoresist threshold turns this blurred intensity into biased edges, shortened lines and rounded corners. optical proximity correction deliberately predistorts the mask: line-end extensions and small serifs can compensate for local loss of exposure, while other edges are moved back where exposure spreads too far. The correction is calibrated for illumination, resist and focus; it does not restore arbitrarily high lost spatial frequencies.
double patterning separates a dense target pattern into two sparser patterns, each printable within the single-exposure process window. Two exposures with an intervening pattern-fixing or transfer step combine their features on the wafer. For regular lines, alternating line assignment can halve the final pitch while each exposure retains a larger pitch. optical proximity correction corrects local image bias; double patterning divides the density burden between exposures. Overlay error and the minimum printable isolated feature still constrain the result. Simply exposing the same unmodified resist twice without an appropriate process separation does not give the same independent thresholding.

Treat the letter-shaped regions as openings to be etched, using positive-tone resist; the clear mask areas therefore correspond to those openings. Label the two L features L1​,L2​ from left to right. An alternating assignment is mask A: H, L1​, O; mask B: E, L2​. Each full letter remains on one mask, avoiding unnecessary stitch boundaries across its stems. Nearest-neighbor interletter gaps now occur between different exposures, rather than having to be resolved within one exposure. Verify that each individual letter's internal spaces are still printable; without dimensions, this partition is a process proposal rather than proof of a particular numerical minimum linewidth.
A robust sequence is litho-etch-litho-etch double patterning into a durable hard mask. Deposit an oxide hard mask on silicon; expose and develop mask A and transfer those openings through the hard mask. Strip resist, apply fresh resist, align and expose mask B, then transfer its openings into the same hard mask, the second resist covering mask-A openings during that second oxide transfer. Remove resist and perform one final deep silicon etch through the union of the openings. An inductively coupled deep reactive-ion etching process with alternating fluorine etching and sidewall passivation gives much more vertical, high-aspect-ratio features than an isotropic etch. Mask thickness and etch selectivity must survive the required depth. Control sidewall scalloping, aspect-ratio-dependent etch rate, charging, microloading and the combined overlay/etch-bias budget.
A spacer-based process can produce very fine regular pitches, but arbitrary letter junctions and terminations require additional cut/block patterning; it is not automatically a two-mask solution for this target. Use two independently transferred sparse masks, then a selective directional deep etch. The schematic gives the assignment and a generic corner-correction example without reproducing the supplied pattern.

A La Mer diagram plots the concentration of growth species against time. Label the equilibrium solubility Cs​ and a higher effective nucleation threshold Cn​. Generate growth species until the concentration briefly exceeds Cn​; a short burst nucleation interval then creates seeds. Consumption brings the concentration below Cn​ while leaving it above Cs​, allowing existing particles to grow without substantial continuing nucleation. Rapid mixing, a short seed-formation interval and a common growth period help produce a narrow particle size distribution. Suppress secondary nucleation and aggregation, then quench or consume the remaining growth species when the desired size is reached. The threshold is an operational kinetic scale, not a sharp new equilibrium phase boundary.
Reducing the solid-liquid interfacial tension lowers the classical barrier, which scales as γ3 at fixed supersaturation. More seeds can then form at a smaller supersaturation; at fixed total precipitated mass, more seeds generally mean a smaller mean final particle size. A narrower distribution is not guaranteed: if nucleation continues throughout growth, particles have different ages and the distribution can broaden. A brief well-separated burst and uniform subsequent growth are the controlling requirements, not low interfacial tension alone. The historical model concerns separated nucleation/growth in sulfur hydrosols, rather than a universal demonstrated mechanism for every nanoparticle synthesis.[ref]

A material exhibiting piezoelectricity converts cyclic mechanical stress into electric charge; its mechanical and electrical boundary conditions jointly set the extractable energy. piezoelectric ceramics such as lead zirconate titanate are stiff and often have large charge coefficients and electromechanical coupling, but are brittle and can be difficult to match to large-strain flexible motion. piezoelectric polymers such as polyvinylidene fluoride are compliant, light and compatible with flexible large-area films. Their lower charge coefficient is partly offset in voltage-sensitive applications by lower permittivity: the voltage coefficient is g=d/ϵ.
Ceramics often favor strong charge generation and coupling; polymers favor flexibility and strain accommodation. The larger open-circuit voltage of a low-capacitance film does not itself imply more useful power. For a simple fixed-stress comparison the electrical energy scale involves d2/ϵ, while coupling also depends on mechanical compliance. At fixed imposed strain, different stiffnesses change the available mechanical energy. resonance tuning, fatigue, mechanical mounting, rectifier losses and load matching can reverse a simplistic material ranking; compare complete devices under the same actual vibration source.
