The thin scanning transmission electron microscopy foil restricts scattering to a much smaller projected specimen volume than bulk SEM at 30kV. It reveals the nanoscale shape and continuity of the gold/NiP interface, the narrow low-density opening and the fine heterogeneous region underneath the gold, which the larger SEM interaction volume and surface-sensitive collection obscure.
The enlarged view resolves structure on the tens-of-nanometres scale: local interface roughness, small bright and dark domains and the relationship of the gold layer to the damaged or porous-looking NiPy​ region. This helps distinguish an interface-associated defect from merely a change between nominal alloy layers. It still does not identify a crystalline phase or quantify composition without diffraction or spectroscopic analysis. Since the SEM surface and focused ion beam milling lamella need not cut precisely the same volume, correlations should allow for section position.
