The main characteristic peaks identify nickel and phosphorus: the spectrum has a strong Ni Kα peak near 7.48keV, the Ni L region near 0.85keV and P Kα near 2.01keV. Weaker associated lines, including Ni Kβ near 8.26keV, can accompany them. The continuous background is bremsstrahlung from decelerated electrons.
Small carbon and oxygen signals at low energy can arise from contamination, surface oxide or preparation materials; a copper contribution can arise from the adjacent substrate within the finite interaction volume. The Cu L region lies close to Ni L, so overlap is particularly troublesome at low energy. A weak Cu Kα contribution near 8.04keV is better separated, though still close to Ni Kβ. At 30kV the beam can excite these K lines.
Peak height is not an atomic fraction: ionization cross-sections, fluorescence yields, absorption, detector response, background and line overlap require correction. The large nickel peak therefore supports the nickel-rich assignment but does not directly give the nickel-to-phosphorus atomic ratio.
