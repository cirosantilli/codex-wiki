One design is an anthracene–methylene–aza-15-crown-5 sensor, with the tertiary amine of the aza-crown linked through CH2​ to anthracene. The crown supplies the sodium-binding oxygen/nitrogen cavity, the amine supplies an electron donor and anthracene supplies the fluorophore. A sufficiently rigid sodium-sized cryptand could improve discrimination further.
Without a bound cation, photoinduced electron transfer from the amine can quench excited anthracene. sodium coordination lowers the electron-donating ability of that lone pair, suppressing this quenching route and increasing fluorescence. The methylene spacer allows electronic communication by electron transfer while limiting direct conjugation between the binding site and fluorophore.
A cavity appropriate to sodium is preferable to an 18-crown-6-like potassium-sized cavity. Selectivity must be tested, not inferred perfectly from cavity size. Potassium binding, including possible sandwich complexes of flexible crowns, can still compete. At the intended concentrations the design should satisfy KNa​[Na+]≫KK​[K+] and should give a fluorescence change on sodium binding. Rigidity, cavity size and suppression of competing stoichiometries are useful design controls. fluorescence calibration in the actual potassium-containing solvent establishes the usable detection range.
