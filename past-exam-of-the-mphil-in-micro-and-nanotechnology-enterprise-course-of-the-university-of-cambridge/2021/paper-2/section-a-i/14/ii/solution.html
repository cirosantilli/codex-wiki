Let gl​−gs​ be the liquid-minus-solid free energy density at the same temperature. Near the bulk coexistence temperature Tm​, linearization gives
gl​−gs​≃ΔsV​(Tm​−T)=Tm​ΔHV​​ΔT.
(3)
For a sphere, an interface displacement changes area and volume with dA/dV=2/r. Equality of the bulk driving force and the capillary cost at a curved solid–liquid interface therefore gives gl​−gs​=2γ/r∗. Equivalently, differentiating 4πr2γ−(4π/3)r3(gl​−gs​) gives the same stationary radius. Combining the expressions yields
ΔT=r∗ΔHV​2γTm​​.​
(4)
This form of the Gibbs-Thomson effect assumes isotropic interfacial energy, a spherical interface, bulk-like latent heat per volume and a near-coexistence free energy expansion. More detailed free-particle models must distinguish solid–vapor, liquid–vapor and solid–liquid energies.
