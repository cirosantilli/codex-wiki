This is transverse magnetic focusing. The depleted gates form reflecting boundaries and two constrictions. A narrow quantum point contact collimates carriers into cyclotron trajectories with cyclotron radius rc​=m∗vF​/(e∣B∣)=ℏkF​/(e∣B∣). When an integer number of semicircular skipping arcs links the two openings, the collector couples particularly strongly to the injector population. A floating voltage contact then changes its electrochemical potential to satisfy zero net current, producing a peak in the measured nonlocal voltage. Sweeping the field changes the arc diameter, moving the focused population into or away from the collector.
The connection condition is L=2jrc​ for integer j≥1, with the higher orders requiring specular reflections. The effect needs ballistic electron transport, sufficiently narrow openings and a smooth boundary. Electron phase coherence can add interference structure, but the basic geometrical focusing condition is semiclassical.[ref]
There is a polarity qualification in the printed geometry. The electron charge is negative: for a field out of the page, an electron launched upward through the left opening bends left, not toward the right opening. A direct left-opening-to-right-opening focusing path in the upper region therefore requires the field into the page; the printed outward field supports the reverse connection. Reversing conventional current reverses the occupation perturbation and voltage sign, but does not reverse the curvature of an individual electron. Thus the standard injector-to-collector explanation describes the focusing mechanism, while the asserted signed voltage increase requires a consistent field/lead convention. The calculated results below are peak-field magnitudes; taking outward field positive gives negative fields for direct left-to-right electron injection.
