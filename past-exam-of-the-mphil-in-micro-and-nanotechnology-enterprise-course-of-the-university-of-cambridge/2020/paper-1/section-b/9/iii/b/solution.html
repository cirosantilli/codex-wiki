Clean the exposed silicon and grow a thin, high-quality SiO2 gate dielectric by controlled dry thermal oxidation. Deposit polycrystalline silicon, conventionally by low-pressure chemical vapour deposition from silane, dope it in situ or by subsequent implantation and activation, and pattern/etch it to define the two gates. Phosphorus or arsenic gives n+ polysilicon; boron gives p+ polysilicon where the selected gate-work-function process requires it. A traditional single-poly process may use a common gate doping instead; gate and source/drain doping need not be identical.
The patterned gates subsequently serve as self-alignment masks for donor-doped nMOS source/drain and acceptor-doped pMOS source/drain regions. Annealing activates these dopants. Keep the oxide thin and uniform enough for strong electrostatic control but free from pinholes and excessive leakage. A high-permittivity dielectric deposited by atomic layer deposition with a metal gate is a modern alternative, rather than the conventional polysilicon/SiO2 stack drawn.
