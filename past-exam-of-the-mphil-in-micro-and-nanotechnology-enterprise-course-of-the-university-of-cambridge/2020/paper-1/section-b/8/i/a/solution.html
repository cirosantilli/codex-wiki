One image takes 512s if 1Hz means one completed scan line per second. The slow-axis speed is 10/512=0.01953μms−1. Recording a wire spanning 1μm along that axis therefore takes 51.2s. Meanwhile the fast-axis drift is 20/3600=0.005556μms−1, giving a transverse displacement of 0.2844μm. The apparent straight-line length is
Lapp​=12+0.28442​=1.040μm.​
(8)
Its slow-axis projection remains 1μm; the overestimate refers to length along the slanted trace.
With the wire along the fast axis and drift along the slow axis, its length is acquired within one line, so Lapp​≃1.000μm. Taking a forward sweep of 1s across 10μm, the wire is crossed in 0.1s and the perpendicular displacement is only 0.556nm. The corresponding length change is about 1.5×10−7μm, far below the 19.5nm pixel pitch. For the convention in which one hertz includes a forward and reverse sweep, that already negligible displacement halves. The slow-axis calculation still uses one completed line per second.
