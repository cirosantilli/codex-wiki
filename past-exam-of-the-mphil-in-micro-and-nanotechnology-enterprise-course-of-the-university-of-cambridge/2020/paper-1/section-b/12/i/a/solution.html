Specific energy is usable energy divided by device mass, commonly Whkg−1; specific power is deliverable power divided by mass, commonly Wkg−1. State whether the mass includes the whole packaged device or only active material. Volumetric energy density uses volume instead.
A conventional capacitor separates charge across a dielectric and stores U=CV2/2, enabling rapid charge/discharge but relatively little energy. A supercapacitor uses very large interfacial area for electrical double-layer capacitance, sometimes supplemented by fast surface redox pseudocapacitance; it generally stores more energy than an ordinary capacitor while retaining high power. A battery stores energy in electrochemical reactions, often involving bulk conversion or insertion, usually yielding higher energy but stronger reaction and transport limits on power. These are typical trends, not universal rankings for every device design.
