At low carrier concentration, ∣S∣ can be large but electrical conductivity is too small. Increasing the carrier concentration usually increases σ=neμ, while ∣S∣ decreases. Electronic thermal conductivity rises approximately as κe​=LσT by the Wiedemann-Franz law; lattice conduction supplies an additional background. Thus neither an insulator nor an ordinary metal usually maximizes Z.
Heavily doped semiconductors and semimetals often give the useful compromise, near a maximum of S2σ/κ as doping is varied. The optimum is material- and temperature-dependent; there is no universal optimal concentration.
