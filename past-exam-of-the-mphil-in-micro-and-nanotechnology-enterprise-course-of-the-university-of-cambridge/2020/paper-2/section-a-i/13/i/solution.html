The carbon nanotube chiral vector identifies equivalent sites of graphene that become one point on rolling:
Ch​=na1​+ma2​,∣a1​∣=∣a2​∣=a=3​b,d=πa​n2+nm+m2​.
(1)
The primitive vectors meet at 60∘, b is the C–C bond length, and one can choose 0≤m≤n. The carbon nanotube chiral angle satisfies tanθ=3​m/(2n+m). Armchair tubes have (n,n) and θ=30∘; zigzag tubes have (n,0) and θ=0. Other pairs are chiral, with mirror-related left- and right-handed forms. Armchair and zigzag tubes themselves have mirror symmetry.
The circumferential boundary condition requires k⋅Ch​=2πℓ. In nanotube zone folding, an allowed wavevector line intersects one of the Dirac valleys in graphene precisely when n−m is divisible by three. Thus all armchair tubes are metallic in this approximation. Zigzag tubes are metallic for n divisible by three and otherwise semiconducting; general chiral tubes follow the same modular rule. Curvature can open small gaps in nominally metallic non-armchair tubes. Strain, interactions and broken symmetries can modify the ideal result; metallic band structure does not imply zero device resistance.
