Across a radial section, the narrow-gap indium gallium nitride layer lies between wider-gap n-GaN and p-GaN. A suitable type-I band alignment has a downward conduction-band offset and upward valence-band offset in the indium gallium nitride, confining electrons and holes to the active layer. The original band sketch isolates those offsets; the complete junction also has doping-induced band bending.
At thermal equilibrium the Fermi level is common throughout. Forward bias separates the electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels, injects carriers from the n- and p-regions and permits electron-hole recombination in the thin active layer. A full radial line through the wire crosses two copies of the shell well; a core-to-exterior radial coordinate crosses one.
The 5 nm indium gallium nitride shell is the quantum well active region, not a metallic contact. Finite band offsets, confinement and polarization fields affect its transition energy; the bulk gap alone is insufficient.
A plausible role for the SiN mask is selective-area growth: restrict nucleation to exposed windows and suppress parasitic deposition between wires. Retained insulating material can also help prevent unwanted electrical paths during later processing. Which role dominates depends on the actual fabrication sequence. The cited source concerns core-shell single-wire LEDs and contact engineering, so a qualitative band sketch does not reproduce its complete device model.[ref]
