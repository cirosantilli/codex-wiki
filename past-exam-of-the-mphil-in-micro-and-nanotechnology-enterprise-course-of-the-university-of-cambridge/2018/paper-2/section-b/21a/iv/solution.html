The literal u(r)=−C4​/r4 curve is monotone increasing from −∞ at r→0+ toward zero from below as r→∞:
u′(r)=4C4​/r5>0.
(32)
Taken literally at a zero gap, it predicts an unbounded collapse energy and force. That extrapolation is outside the far-field dipole approximation and cannot describe actual touching matter.
Finite atomic/ionic dimensions, electronic overlap and Pauli repulsion supply short-distance constraints. Solvent and hydration layers, steric coatings and surface chemistry can add further contributions. An illustrative regularized model is utoy​=A/r12−C4​/r4 with A>0; it has a finite minimum at r=(3A/C4​)1/8 and becomes repulsive at shorter distances. This is a schematic regularization, not a derived complete ion–sphere contact law.
Separately, correcting the center-distance convention to R=a+r already removes this particular r=0 divergence from the far-field expression, but does not make a dipole approximation accurate at contact. Near-field multipoles and microscopic repulsion still require a better model. Calibrated colloidal probe technique measurements can test where a long-range force model ceases to agree with actual approach curves.[ref]
