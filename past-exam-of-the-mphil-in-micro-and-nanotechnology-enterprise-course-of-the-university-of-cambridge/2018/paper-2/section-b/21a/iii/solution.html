For α>0, the given model predicts attraction proportional to r−5 in force, independent of the ion's charge sign; the energy is proportional to −r−4 and vanishes at large separation. A larger electric polarizability gives a stronger interaction.
This is induction by a charged object. Ordinary nonretarded London interaction between two small neutral polarizable objects instead has an R−6 energy dependence; geometry-integrated colloidal dispersion laws can have other powers. Calling the specified r−4 law a generic dispersion force would conceal its charge-dependent origin.
Attraction can favor association or adsorption. A stable suspension normally requires an opposing free-energy barrier, such as double-layer or steric repulsion, relative to Brownian motion. Salt, surface coatings and solvent quality alter that balance. However, one ion–particle pair potential alone does not establish the phase behavior or aggregation rate of a many-particle suspension; concentrations, diffusion, surface chemistry and the remaining interactions also matter.
