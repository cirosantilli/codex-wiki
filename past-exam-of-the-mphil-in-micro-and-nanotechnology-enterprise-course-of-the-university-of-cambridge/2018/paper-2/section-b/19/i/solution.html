A common implementation uses a gallium arsenide/aluminium gallium arsenide heterostructure with a two-dimensional electron gas near the interface. electrons supplied by remote donors occupy quantized normal-direction states, while an undoped spacer reduces ionized-impurity scattering. Surface metallic gates, insulated from the conducting layer, deplete selected regions when negatively biased.
Leave a small conducting island bounded by depleted regions and connect it weakly to source and drain reservoirs through two tunable constrictions. A plunger gate controls the island potential/population; barrier gates control tunnel coupling. Ohmic contacts connect the reservoirs to the measurement circuit. Vertical heterostructure confinement plus lateral depletion force defines the dot. A 2DEG without lateral gates would remain extended in its plane.
The original device diagram labels the gates, island, tunnel barriers and reservoirs. Charge stability and spectroscopy versus gate/bias should verify that an isolated weakly coupled island has actually formed; a lithographic outline alone is insufficient.
