Taking the printed reference to part(iii) literally, measure an equilibrium water contact angle with a calibrated sessile-drop goniometer on a smooth homogeneous surface. With independently constrained interfacial tensions, test γSV​−γSL​=γLV​cosθC​. Advancing and receding measurements reveal pinning and hysteresis; contact angle alone cannot independently determine both unknown solid-interface energies.
If the intended reference was instead the polymer-size prediction in the preceding part, use static light scattering or small-angle neutron scattering at both temperatures on appropriately dilute solutions. At small scattering wave number q, the Guinier approximation
I(q)≃I(0)exp(−q2Rg2​/3)
(24)
gives Rg​ from the slope of lnI against q2. Control concentration, aggregation and scattering contrast, and compare the extracted radii. Dynamic light scattering normally measures a hydrodynamic size rather than directly the same radius of gyration. Answering both readings avoids silently correcting the printed cross-reference.
