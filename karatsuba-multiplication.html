Karatsuba multiplication

 Home Computer science Algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
Karatsuba multiplication is a divide-and-conquer algorithm for multiplication of integers. Splitting each operand into two blocks seems to require four smaller products; the Karatsuba three-product identity recovers the two cross terms together from just three products. Repeating that saving through recursion changes the time complexity from quadratic to O(nlog2​3), where n counts digits in a fixed positional notation. The exponent is about 1.585: the benefit comes from the algorithm, even though each splitting step needs more addition and subtraction.
The Karatsuba worked multiplication below computes 1234⋅5678=7,006,652 using 12⋅56, 34⋅78, and 46⋅134. The last operand already shows why “half-size” needs care: Karatsuba carry handling and Karatsuba recursion termination account for the extra digit. Karatsuba digit-operation cost states the operation model; Karatsuba practical crossover explains why the asymptotic saving alone does not select a useful threshold.
The original 1962 paper presents a binary numeral system automaton construction: its Theorem 2 and proof reduce multiplication to taking squares of numbers, then reduce a larger square of a number to three smaller squares of numbers. Its footnote explicitly addresses the extra digit in a sum of halves. The sequential time complexity analysis below uses a different operation model from that paper's automaton size and depth bounds, including its Boolean circuit realization without feedback.[ref]
