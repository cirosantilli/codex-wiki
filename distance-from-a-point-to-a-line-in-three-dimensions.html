Distance from a point to a line in three dimensions (D=∣(r1​−r0​)×(r2​−r0​)∣/∣r2​−r1​∣)

For distinct points r1​,r2​, the cross product in the numerator has magnitude twice the area of the triangle with third vertex r0​. The same double area is the base length ∣r2​−r1​∣ times the perpendicular height from r0​, proving the displayed Euclidean distance. Equivalently, let u=r2​−r1​ and project r0​−r1​ onto the line direction. The squared distance is ∣r0​−r1​∣2−[u⋅(r0​−r1​)]2/∣u∣2; Lagrange's identity gives the same cross-product formula.
