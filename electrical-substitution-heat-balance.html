Electrical substitution heat balance (Pabs​=Ph,d​−Ph,l​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Electrical substitution radiometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 643 Articles: 2
An explicit lumped model makes the cancellation understandable. Treat the receiver as having uniform temperature T, heat capacity C, heater power Ph​ deposited in the receiver, net parasitic input Ppar​, and outward heat transfer Qout​(T,Tb​) to a reference at temperature Tb​. With incident radiant flux Pin​ and radiometric absorptance a, conservation of energy gives
CdtdT​=Ph​+aPin​+Ppar​−Qout​(T,Tb​).
(30)
The dark state has no admitted beam. Compare settled dark and light states, denoted d and l; each has dT/dt=0. Subtracting their balances gives
Pabs​=aPin​=ΔPh​+ΔQout​−ΔPpar​,ΔPh​=Ph,d​−Ph,l​,
(31)
where ΔQout​=Qout,l​−Qout,d​ and ΔPpar​=Ppar,l​−Ppar,d​. At identical T and Tb​, with unchanged surroundings and parasitic heating, both correction differences vanish. Thus Pabs​=ΔPh​, and Pin​=ΔPh​/a. Notice the signs: extra stray thermal radiation admitted with the beam also reduces heater power, so it must be subtracted from that reduction before attributing the result to the intended beam.
Measure the heater's own terminal voltage Vh​ and electric current Ih​: for steady electrical heating, Ph​=Vh​Ih​=Ih2​Rh​, where Rh​ is its electrical resistance. A supply voltage that includes wire voltage drops counts Joule heating in the leads too; only the portion deposited in the receiver belongs in this balance. Changes in that deposited lead heat belong in ΔPpar​. Optical-electrical heating non-equivalence adds a further instrument-specific correction when the uniform-temperature model is insufficient.[ref]
