Impossibility of universal identical-basis perfect correlations

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Bell state Reduced density matrix Entangled state
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
For two systems of equal dimension d≥2, no joint pure state guarantees equal outcome labels whenever both systems undergo the same arbitrary basic measurement. Equal outcomes in the computational basis would force ∣Ψ⟩=∑j​cj​∣jj⟩. On any two coordinates j,k, changing both bases to the real sum/difference pair makes the different-outcome amplitude (cj​−ck​)/2, so all coefficients must agree. Changing both to the imaginary-phase pair makes a different-outcome amplitude (cj​+ck​)/2, so those coefficients must also be negatives. They must all vanish, contradicting normalization. The one-dimensional case is an exception.
The requirement of all complex bases matters. A maximally entangled vector d−1/2∑j​∣jj⟩ does produce matching labels if one party uses a basis and the other its coordinatewise complex conjugate basis. Conjugate-state steering from a maximally entangled pair gives this conjugation explicitly. Identical real bases are a special case, not evidence for identical arbitrary complex bases.
