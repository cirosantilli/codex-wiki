Complex number (C)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 18
A complex number is z=x+iy, where x,y are real numbers and the imaginary unit satisfies i2=−1. The real part is x and the imaginary part is y: for 3+4i they are 3 and 4, respectively. Equality requires both coordinates to agree. Thus the complex plane gives each complex number a concrete position, while ordinary addition and multiplication become geometric operations.
For z=3+4i and w=1+i, addition works coordinate by coordinate, whereas multiplication uses i2=−1:
z+w=4+5i,zw=(3+4i)(1+i)=3+7i−4=−1+7i.
(150)
These answers encode different motions: addition by w is a translation, while multiplication by w is a rotation combined with a uniform change of size. Keeping these pictures distinct prevents a common beginner's confusion.
The complex numbers form a field: every nonzero z has a multiplicative inverse, computable using its complex conjugate and modulus. They also supply the missing solutions of a quadratic equation such as u2+1=0, namely u=±i. The surprising payoff is that extending arithmetic also makes Euclidean geometry easier to calculate. For a systematic introduction to these definitions, see Jeremy Orloff, MIT 18.04, topic 1, sections 1.3–1.5.
