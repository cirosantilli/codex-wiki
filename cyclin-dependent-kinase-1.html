Cyclin-dependent kinase 1

OurBigBook.com Words: 958 Articles: 5
Cyclin-dependent kinase 1, abbreviated CDK1 and historically called Cdc2, is the catalytic protein kinase in the CDK1–cyclin B assembly. CDK1 transfers a phosphate group from ATP to a protein substrate; cyclin B supplies a regulatory binding partner. Binding and phosphorylation regulate catalytic competence, so detecting CDK1 protein alone does not establish activity. The distinction makes a durable catalytic component reusable as the cell cycle changes its regulatory inputs.[ref]
The cell-cycle activation feedback uses CDK1 activity as both an output and a regulatory input: activity promotes Cdc25 activation and suppresses Wee1, reinforcing release from inhibitory phosphorylation. The already described cell-cycle hysteresis experiment tests how that output depends on history at matched cyclin B input; the histone H1 kinase assay below explains what its output measures. A binding diagram alone cannot establish hysteresis.
