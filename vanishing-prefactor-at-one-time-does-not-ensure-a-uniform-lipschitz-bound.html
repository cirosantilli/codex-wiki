Vanishing prefactor at one time does not ensure a uniform Lipschitz bound (f(x,y)=x∣y∣2/3)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Picard-Lindelöf theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
A coefficient that vanishes at the initial time can hide failure of the uniform Lipschitz condition required for an initial value problem. For f(x,y)=x∣y∣2/3, the map y↦f(0,y) is identically zero. But every rectangle of positive widths around (0,0) contains some x=0, and
∣y∣∣f(x,y)−f(x,0)∣​=∣x∣∣y∣−1/3⟶∞.
(50)
No Lipschitz constant works uniformly on the rectangle. Actual uniqueness also fails: y=0 and y=x6/216 are distinct differentiable solutions with initial value zero. Substitution gives y′=x5/36=x∣y∣2/3 for the second solution. A condition at the single initial time cannot replace control on a neighbourhood.
