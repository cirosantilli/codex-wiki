Schauder fixed-point theorem (T(K)⊂K⟹∃u∈K:T(u)=u)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fixed-point theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 397 Articles: 4
Every continuous map from a nonempty compact convex set K in a Banach space to itself has a fixed point. A useful equivalent form takes a nonempty closed bounded convex set C and a continuous map T:C→C whose image is relatively compact. To obtain this version from the compact-domain version, restrict T to convT(C)⊂C and use closed convex hull of a compact set.
For a proof on K, the Schauder approximation lemma gives continuous maps Tε​:K→K∩Lε​ with finite-dimensional Lε​ and ∥T−Tε​∥∞​≤ε. The Brouwer fixed-point theorem on the nonempty compact convex set K∩Lε​ gives xε​=Tε​xε​, so ∥Txε​−xε​∥≤ε. A convergent subsequence as ε↓0 and the continuity of T give an exact fixed point.
