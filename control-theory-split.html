Control theory

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics
OurBigBook.com Words: 12k Articles: 126
Control theory studies how to choose control inputs for a dynamical system so that its plant output follows a reference signal, despite control disturbances and imperfect knowledge of the controlled plant. A controller turns the available information into a control signal. The design must consider both the resulting response and stability theory: a rule that corrects a small mismatch can still produce growing deviations if the interconnection is badly chosen. [ref]
The central distinction is informational. Open-loop control executes planned commands; feedback control uses the measured output of the actual controlled plant to update those commands. A reference signal can describe a destination, while control disturbances describe influences that the controller did not command. A model can predict where the controlled plant should be; measurement tells the controller what actually happened. The authors provide a free full-book PDF through [ref].
For a concrete comparison, all examples below use the same ideal scalar controlled plant:
x˙(t)=u(t)+w(t),y(t)=ym​(t)=x(t),r(t)=1,x(0)=0.
(1)
Here x is an illustrative position, u the control input, w a control disturbance, and ym​ the measured output. Both x and t are dimensionless quantities; the numbers are chosen to expose the mechanism, with no empirical interpretation. The ordinary differential equation says that the present control input and control disturbance accumulate into x. The measured output equals the entire state only in this ideal example; a general plant output need not reveal the entire state.
