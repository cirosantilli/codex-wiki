Suppose a diagonalizable matrix has a simple eigenvalue λ∗​ uniquely closest to a shift μ∈/spec(A). If the starting vector has a nonzero coefficient c∗​ in the target eigenbasis direction, fixed-shift inverse iteration converges to that eigenvector line. In fact, the eigenvalues of (A−μI)−1 are (λj​−μ)−1, so after k inverse applications, division by c∗​(λ∗​−μ)−k leaves the target eigenvector plus

This proves direction error O(qμk​) in Big O notation. The multiplicative constant depends on the eigenbasis and starting coefficients, and no uniform reduction at the first step follows for a nonsymmetric matrix. Over the real numbers, λ∗​<μ produces alternating signs. A nonzero ordinary inner product with a right eigenvector is not generally the same as a nonzero coefficient in the eigenbasis; the latter is the required condition.
