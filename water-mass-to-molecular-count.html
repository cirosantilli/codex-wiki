Water mass to molecular count

 Home Chemistry Molar mass
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
Choose approximate atomic weights Ar​(H)=1.008 and Ar​(O)=15.999, and use Mu​≈1gmol−1. The chemical formula H2​O then gives water molar mass
M(H2​O)≈(2(1.008)+15.999)gmol−1=18.015gmol−1.
(13)
These are deliberately chosen rounded inputs, not exact atomic weights or a universal value for every water sample. Different isotopic abundances, notably those in heavy water, change the mean molar mass; chemical formula alone does not specify them. Adding atomic weights also neglects the tiny mass change associated with chemical bond energies, immaterial at this rounding precision.
For an illustrative pure water sample of mass 4.50375g, this convention gives
n≈18.015gmol−14.50375g​=0.250000mol,N≈1.50553519×1023.
(14)
The count refers to water molecules. Their hydrogen chemical atom count is approximately 2N=3.01107038×1023, and their oxygen chemical atom count is approximately N. The mean mass per water molecule is M/NA​≈2.99146×10−26kg. Extra displayed digits expose the arithmetic; they do not imply that the sample composition or mass was measured that precisely.
The same conversion makes stoichiometry a mass calculation. For the chemical equation 2H2​+O2​→2H2​O, complete conversion of 0.250000mol of molecular hydrogen and 0.125000mol of molecular oxygen gives 0.250000mol of water. With the chosen approximate atomic weights, the reactant masses are 0.50400g and 3.99975g, summing to 4.50375g. The 2:1:2 ratio belongs to molecule counts and amount of substance, not to masses; this bookkeeping neglects the much smaller reaction energy contribution to mass.
