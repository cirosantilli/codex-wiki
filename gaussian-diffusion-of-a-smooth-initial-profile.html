For diffusion with a constant diffusion coefficient D>0 and no sources, the model flux is J=−D∇u. A continuity equation gives ut​=−∇⋅J=DΔu. This proportionality is a constitutive assumption about transport; the continuity equation expresses conservation. If D varies with position, it must remain inside the divergence, giving ut​=∇⋅(D∇u) instead.[ref]
An exact constructed heat equation example on the whole plane uses dimensionless coordinates with D=1 and the smooth initial Gaussian function u(x,y,0)=e−r2. Set s=1+4t. For t≥0,
u(x,y,t)=s1​e−r2/s,r2=x2+y2.
(44)
Direct differentiation verifies both the initial condition and
ut​=Δu=s34(r2−s)​e−r2/s.
(45)
The center decreases, while the region r2>s increases at that instant. The sign-change circle expands with radius s​. A fixed outer location can initially increase and later decrease as this circle passes it.
At time t, the peak is 1/s, and the radius where the profile is 1/e times its peak is s​. The width grows as the height falls. Integration in polar coordinates gives
∫R2​u(x,y,t)dxdy=2π∫0∞​sr​e−r2/sdr=π.
(46)
Thus a decreasing peak is compatible with a conserved spatial integral. Dividing u by π makes a probability density function with coordinate variances s/2=1/2+2t, connecting the same spreading to Brownian motion with diffusion coefficient one. Equivalently the profile is evolved by the Gaussian heat kernel with time parameter 2t, since that article uses generator 21​Δ.
