Horner's method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
Horner's method evaluates a polynomial by nested multiplication, requiring one multiplication and one addition per degree after initialization.
For p(X)=an​Xn+⋯+a0​, start with bn​=an​ and compute bj​=xbj+1​+aj​ for j=n−1,…,0. The final value is b0​=p(x). Equivalently,
p(x)=a0​+x(a1​+x(a2​+⋯+xan​)).
(6)
This reorganization reuses intermediate results, avoiding separate computation of every power. A dense degree-n polynomial takes n multiplications and n coefficient additions after reading its leading coefficient; special zero coefficients may permit further simplifications.
For our cubic, the coefficient list in descending order is 1,−3,0,4. The zero is important: it occupies the missing X term's position. At x=2, the accumulated values are
1 ⟶ 2⋅1−3=−1 ⟶ 2(−1)+0=−2 ⟶ 2(−2)+4=0.
(7)
At x=3, the same operations give 1,0,0,4, confirming p(3)=4.
The intermediate values do more than evaluate. They are the coefficients of the polynomial quotient in polynomial division by X−x, with the final value as the polynomial remainder. At x=2, the first three values give X2−X−2, so
p(X)=(X−2)(X2−X−2)+0.
(8)
At x=3, they give X2, so p(X)=(X−3)X2+4. This links an efficient numerical calculation with the exact factor theorem.
William George Horner's 1819 paper concerns solving numerical equations by successive approximation. The Royal Society archive records the manuscript and its reading on 1 July 1819; it provides a historical reference for the method's name. [ref]
