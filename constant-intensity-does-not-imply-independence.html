Constant intensity does not imply independence

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Point process Intensity measure of a point process
OurBigBook.com Words: 500 Articles: 2
The same intensity measure can belong to very different point processes. A Poisson point process with a locally finite intensity measure μ adds two requirements: each finite-intensity window count has the Poisson distribution of mean μ(W), and counts in pairwise disjoint observation windows are mutually independent. These requirements determine its probability law; they do not follow from the formula for μ.[ref]
A randomly shifted lattice point process, a unit-rate Poisson point process, and the shared random rate gives dependent point counts construction all satisfy μ(A)=∣A∣ on the real line. Nevertheless, their counts in [0,1) have respectively variance zero, one, and two. Counts in the disjoint observation windows [0,1/2) and [1/2,1) have respectively covariance −1/4, zero, and 1/4. These exact calculations show why measuring mean density cannot identify a spacing or statistical dependence model.
