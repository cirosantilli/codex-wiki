Optical fiber coupling and bend experiment

OurBigBook.com Words: 160
MIT's Optical Waveguides and Fibers, Laboratory Exercise No. 7 (Fall 2005), describes a teaching apparatus. A cleaned, flat-ended multimode optical fiber receives helium–neon laser light through a microscope objective optical lens; a micropositioner aligns the input. The launch deliberately overfills the fibre's cone. A beam splitter sends a sample to a reference photodetector to track laser fluctuations. The fixed output faces a screen or a photodetector on a rotation stage with angular measurement calibration. Readings every two degrees, divided by simultaneous reference readings, give an angular profile. Repeating with an underfilled launch is a control: too few excited normal modes underestimate numerical aperture. Optional index-matching fluid removes cladding-carried light. Bending the fibre reveals leakage and reduced output on an oscilloscope. The handout supplies no measured trace or absolute detector measurement calibration, and this angular scan does not determine loss per kilometre.[ref]
