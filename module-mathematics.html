Module (mathematics, M)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Module theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 32
A left R-module consists of an abelian group (M,+) and scalar multiplication R×M→M, written (r,m)↦rm, where R is a ring with identity element 1. For every r,s∈R and m,n∈M, require
r(m+n)=rm+rn,(r+s)m=rm+sm,(rs)m=r(sm),1m=m.
(17)
The module elements can be added to one another and multiplied by scalars; a product of two module elements is not part of the definition. These conventions agree with [ref]. For a noncommutative ring, the side matters: a right module instead has (mr)s=m(rs). Over a commutative ring, the two conventions agree.
Over a field k, these are exactly the vector space axioms. A nonzero a∈k has a multiplicative inverse, so am=0 implies m=a−1(am)=0. Over the integers, 2 has no multiplicative inverse: 2x=1 has no solution in Z. In the module Z/6Z, division can also lose uniqueness: 21=24=2. A ring element which is a unit still acts invertibly on every module; the difficulty lies with scalars which are not units.
