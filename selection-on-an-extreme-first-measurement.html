Selection on an extreme first measurement (E[Y∣X>a]=0.6ϕ(a)/(1−Φ(a)))

Select units with first reading X>1, keep that membership fixed, and measure the same units again. The first reading now has a truncated normal distribution. For a general finite threshold a, write ϕ for the standard normal density and Φ for the standard normal distribution function. Since ϕ′(x)=−xϕ(x),
E[X∣X>a]=1−Φ(a)∫a∞​xϕ(x)dx​=1−Φ(a)ϕ(a)​.
(21)
The tower property of conditional expectation lets us average the line E[Y∣X]=0.6X over the selected group.[ref] At a=1 this gives
P(X>1)E[X∣X>1]E[Y∣X>1]E[Y−X∣X>1]​≈0.158655,≈1.525135,=0.6E[X∣X>1]≈0.915081,≈−0.610054.​
(22)
The average second reading is below the selection threshold even though every selected first reading exceeded it. This is a group expectation; any finite generated group's sample mean fluctuates around it. Selecting again on Y>1 would define a different group and answer a different question.
The latent variable decomposition makes the apparent drop especially clear. Gaussian conditioning gives E[T∣X=x]=0.6x and E[ε1​∣X=x]=0.4x. Thus
E[T∣X>1]≈0.915081,E[ε1​∣X>1]≈0.610054,E[ε2​∣X>1]=0.
(23)
The first measurement error has zero unconditional mean, but positive selected mean. The second measurement error stays centered because it is independent of selection on X. The persistent signal T never changed.
