For an exactly soluble example, set L1​=L2​=L, R1​=R2​=R, and 0<M<L. Start with zero electric currents, and apply a constant electric potential difference V0​>0 at t=0. The sums s=i1​+i2​ and d=i1​−i2​ obey separate first-order linear differential equations:
(L+M)s˙+Rs=V0​,(L−M)d˙+Rd=V0​.
(16)
Their time constants are τ+​=(L+M)/R and τ−​=(L−M)/R. Thus, for t≥0,
i1​(t)=2RV0​​(2−e−t/τ+​−e−t/τ−​),i2​(t)=2RV0​​(e−t/τ−​−e−t/τ+​).
(17)
Since τ−​<τ+​, i2​(t)<0 for t>0, but i2​(t)→0 as t→∞. The primary approaches V0​/R. The secondary response opposes the rise of linked magnetic flux, in accordance with Lenz's law. Removing mutual inductance by setting M=0 makes the two exponentials equal and gives zero secondary electric current.
For stipulated L=0.1H, M=0.06H, R=10Ω and V0​=1V, the two time constants are 16ms and 4ms. Setting i˙2​=0 gives its largest negative magnitude at
t∗​=τ+​−τ−​τ+​τ−​​logτ−​τ+​​≃7.394ms,i2​(t∗​)≃−23.62mA.
(18)
The continuing primary electric current eventually reaches 100mA while the secondary returns to zero. These numbers belong to the stated model, not the 1831 apparatus.
