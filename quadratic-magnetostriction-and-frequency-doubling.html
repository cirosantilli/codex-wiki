Quadratic magnetostriction and frequency doubling (εzz​=κH2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Continuum mechanics Elasticity (physics) Magnetostriction
OurBigBook.com Words: 254
As an original stipulated low-field model, take the axial magnetic field intensity H in A/m and prescribe
q(H)=κH2,εzz​=q(H),εxx​=εyy​=−2q(H)​,κ=5×10−14m2/A2.
(4)
All strain components are dimensionless, and their trace vanishes. This is an even, quasistatic, unsaturated toy law with a fixed measuring axis. It is distinct from the fixed-magnitude directional model in saturated isotropic magnetostrictive strain. In particular, one must not replace local unit magnetization by normalized net magnetization in a many-domain specimen and assume this law follows.
For H(t)=H0​sin(2πft), H0​=20kA/m, and f=50Hz, a trigonometric identity gives
q(t)=2κH02​​[1−cos(4πft)]=10−5[1−cos(2π(100Hz)t)].
(5)
The axial strain ranges from zero to 20×10−6, with mean 10×10−6 and a 100Hz component of amplitude 10×10−6. The cosine coefficient is negative with the chosen time origin. For L=100mm the length ranges from zero to 2μm relative to H=0. There is no 50Hz term in this unbiased model.
A static bias Hb​ changes the conclusion. Expanding κ[Hb​+H0​sin(2πft)]2 produces a fundamental-frequency term 2κHb​H0​sin(2πft) as well as the doubled-frequency term. This is an incremental linear response about a state with a chosen magnetic axis; it does not contradict isotropic linear magnetostriction vanishes at an unbiased isotropic reference. Real hysteresis, thermal expansion from Joule heating, and mechanical resonance can add further components or change their phases. Frequency alone cannot identify the cause of motion: Joule heating also contains an even-in-electric current contribution.
