Parallel-plate capacitor (C=ϵA/d)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Capacitor
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 7
A parallel-plate capacitor consists of two parallel electrical conductors with overlapping area A and separation d. If the gap is small compared with both plate widths and is filled by a uniform material of positive permittivity ϵ, its capacitance is approximately
C≃dϵA​.
(5)
This neglects the fringing field of a capacitor near the plate edges. The interior electric field points from the plate of positive electric charge to the plate of negative electric charge, and is approximately uniform. [ref].
For a worked vacuum example, stipulate A=0.01m2, d=0.001m, and vacuum permittivity ϵ0​≈8.854×10−12F/m. This rounds the value in Fundamental Physical Constants — Complete Listing by National Institute of Standards and Technology and others. Within the approximation,
C≃0.001(8.854×10−12)(0.01)​F=8.854×10−11F=88.54pF.
(6)
At terminal electric potential difference V=10V, the electric field magnitude is E≃V/d=104V/m, the plate electric charge magnitude is Q=CV=0.8854nC, and the capacitor energy is U=CV2/2=4.427nJ. These numbers use the stipulated rounded vacuum permittivity; finite plates also have corrections from the fringing field of a capacitor.
The formula makes design changes easy to predict: doubling the overlapping area doubles the capacitance, and halving the gap doubles the capacitance, with permittivity fixed and the fringing field of a capacitor neglected. Doubling permittivity also doubles capacitance if the material uniformly fills the gap and remains linear. This lets a compact device store more separated electric charge at the same terminal electric potential difference.
The short derivation uses surface charge density σ=Q/A. In the interior, Gauss's law gives electric field magnitude E≃σ/ϵ=Q/(ϵA). The electric potential difference is V≃Ed=Qd/(ϵA), so dividing Q by V gives C≃ϵA/d. A narrow curved capacitor gap has the same leading local approximation when its width is much less than the curvature radius.
