Composite statistics of neutral atoms

 ... Quantum mechanics Tensor product of quantum systems Multiparticle quantum state Particle exchange operator Indistinguishable particles Spin-statistics theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Exchanging two identical bound atoms exchanges their constituent fermions. An atom with mass number A and atomic number Z contains A nucleons and Z electrons, so its exchange sign is (−1)A+Z when its internal state is preserved. An odd count gives a composite fermion; an even count gives a composite boson. This is equivalently the integer-versus-half-integer total-spin distinction in the Spin-statistics theorem. For example, neutral lithium-6 has nine fermionic constituents and is a fermion, while neutral lithium-7 has ten and is a boson. Counting nucleons alone instead classifies the nucleus, not the neutral atom. This effective composite-particle description requires energies and separations where the bound atom remains intact.
