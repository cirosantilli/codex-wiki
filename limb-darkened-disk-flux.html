Limb-darkened disk flux (I=Ic​(1−u/3))

For a circular stellar disk whose specific intensity is independent of azimuth, its mean specific intensity over projected area is
I=πR⋆2​1​∫disk​IdAproj​=2∫01​I(μ)μdμ.
(21)
The polar coordinates element is dAproj​=2πrdr after angular integration, and rdr=−R⋆2​μdμ. Substitution of the linear limb-darkening law gives
I=2Ic​∫01​(1−u+uμ)μdμ=Ic​(1−u/3).
(22)
For a distant observer at distance D≫R⋆​ receiving light normally to the detecting plane, the observed radiative flux is
fν​≃D2πR⋆2​​Iν​=π(DR⋆​​)2Iν,c​(1−uν​/3).
(23)
It is spectral irradiance, with units Wm−2Hz−1 when Iν​ is per hertz. The local outward radiative flux per actual surface area is instead Fν​=2π∫01​Iν​(μ)μdμ=πIν​, a spectral radiant exitance. In a spherical model with the same stellar atmosphere everywhere, the luminosity is Lν​=4πR⋆2​Fν​ and fν​=Lν​/(4πD2), consistent with the inverse-square law. Compare disks at fixed Ic​ or fixed total observed radiative flux explicitly: increasing u lowers the total in the first comparison, while the second requires a larger Ic​.
