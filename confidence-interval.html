Confidence interval (C(X)=[L(X),U(X)])

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 11
A confidence interval is a random interval constructed from a statistical sample to cover a fixed unknown statistical parameter with a prescribed coverage probability. For a scalar target θ, a procedure with nominal confidence 1−α satisfies
Pθ​{L(X)≤θ≤U(X)}≥1−αfor every admissible θ.
(45)
The subscript records which probability distribution generates the repeated samples. The endpoints are statistics, so they vary from sample to sample; the target is fixed. Equality gives exact nominal coverage probability, while the inequality also permits conservative procedures. For a general estimand ψ(θ), replace the target inside the interval by ψ(θ).
A realized confidence interval communicates the precision of an estimate in the target's own units. Reporting 1.2 alone conceals the difference between an estimate with standard error 0.5 and one with standard error 0.05. A narrow confidence interval indicates greater precision for the stated statistical model, rather than a guarantee that its assumptions describe the experiment correctly.
Once x is observed, C(x) either contains the fixed target or does not. The 95% statement concerns the random procedure C(X), not a posterior probability of 0.95 that this particular interval contains θ. A credible interval instead assigns specified posterior probability to a region under Bayesian inference. These interpretations can differ even when the numerical endpoints agree. The repeated-sampling interpretation is explained in NIST's confidence-limits teaching reference.
Jerzy Neyman's 1937 paper formulates the fixed-target, random-endpoint construction in Section II, especially equation (20) on page 348. Its confidence coefficient must hold across the allowed statistical parameters, rather than only at the value suggested by one sample. See Neyman, Outline of a Theory of Statistical Estimation Based on the Classical Theory of Probability and the original paper scan.
