For an ideal gas with specific internal energy cv​T and Fourier's law heat flux q=−k∇T, subtraction of the kinetic-energy balance from total energy gives ρcv​DT/Dt+p∇⋅u=∇⋅(k∇T). The thermodynamic entropy satisfies TDS/Dt=cv​DT/Dt+p∇⋅u/ρ, so
ρDtDS​=T1​∇⋅(k∇T)=∇⋅(Tk∇T​)+T2k∣∇T∣2​.

The divergence describes entropy transport by heat; the final term is local irreversible production. For T>0 and k≥0 it is nonnegative. In a fixed impermeable thermally insulated container, boundary entropy flux vanishes and mass conservation gives d∫ρSdV/dt=∫k∣∇T∣2/T2dV. Insulation conserves total energy while permitting entropy to increase as temperature gradients relax.
