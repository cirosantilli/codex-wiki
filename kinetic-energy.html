Kinetic energy (K=21​mv2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 8
Kinetic energy is the energy associated with motion. For a nonrelativistic point mass of mass m>0, measured in a specified inertial frame,
K=21​mv⋅v=21​mv2,v=∣v∣.
(9)
Here v is velocity and v is speed; T is another common symbol for kinetic energy. The dot product makes K nonnegative regardless of the direction of velocity. At fixed mass, doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy: a 2kg point mass at 2m/s has 4J, whereas at 4m/s it has 16J. The SI units are kgm2/s2=J. Different reference frames can assign different velocities and therefore different kinetic energies. [ref]
A short outline explains why kinetic energy has this formula. In a nonrelativistic inertial frame, constant mass and Newton's second law give, using the chain rule,
dtdK​=mv⋅dtdv​=Fnet​⋅v.
(10)
Integrating over time proves the work-energy theorem, Wnet​=Kf​−Ki​. The net force is the sum of all forces, and Wnet​ is their total work, not merely the work of a chosen driving force. This route to final speed is useful when the forces as functions of position are known but the full motion as a function of time would be harder to find. [ref]
In the running example, a 2kg point mass starts at rest and receives a constant net 3N force through 4m. Its net work is 12J, so
Kf​=12J,vf​=m2Kf​​​=12​m/s≈3.4641m/s.
(11)
At any intermediate distance x≥0, K(x)=(3N)x and v(x)=2Fx/m​: kinetic energy is a linear function of distance, while speed is proportional to the square root of distance. If a net opposing 3N braking force subsequently acts for 4m, it does −12J of work and brings this point mass to rest. Negative net work decreases kinetic energy; it does not make kinetic energy negative. Beyond the stopping point the motion must be reconsidered, since the point mass cannot keep moving forward with a negative K.
Kinetic energy also makes the exchange with potential energy intuitive: a falling body's loss of gravitational potential energy can appear as increased kinetic energy. The formula above applies when speed is small compared with the speed of light; at higher speeds, special relativity changes the expression. [ref]
