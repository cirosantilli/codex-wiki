Torus knot (Tp,q​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Knot theory Knot
OurBigBook.com Words: 580 Articles: 2
For nonzero coprime integers p,q, the torus knot Tp,q​ lies on a standard unknotted torus in the three-sphere and winds p times meridionally and q times longitudinally. Here a meridian of a solid torus goes around the small cross-section of the doughnut, while the longitudinal direction goes around its central hole. The integers count signed turns, so changing one sign gives the mirror of a link of the torus knot. In this article p is always the meridional count and q the longitudinal count.
An explicit parametric curve in three-dimensional Euclidean space is
γp,q​(t)=((R+rcos(pt))cos(qt), (R+rcos(pt))sin(qt), rsin(pt)),0≤t<2π,
(3)
where R>r>0. Its cylindrical radius ρ=x2+y2​ satisfies (ρ−R)2+z2=r2, the equation of the supporting torus. Its meridional angle is pt and its longitudinal angle is qt: this checks the convention directly rather than relying on the picture. Its squared speed is
∥γp,q′​(t)∥2=r2p2+q2(R+rcos(pt))2>0.
(4)
If two parameter values give the same point, their difference δ satisfies pδ,qδ∈2πZ. Bézout's identity for the coprime integers p,q then gives δ∈2πZ. Thus the loop closes and is an injective function on the parameter circle, with nonzero derivative; it is a smooth embedding.
For example, R=2, r=0.7, p=2, q=3 produces a trefoil knot. During one circuit the meridional angle changes by 4π and the longitudinal angle by 6π. The supporting torus is a guide for construction, not a constraint on later ambient isotopies: the knot may move off it. This family makes arithmetic visible as geometry: the coprime integers determine whether a single traversal visits the whole loop without repetition.
The coprime integers hypothesis matters. If the greatest common divisor is d=gcd(∣p∣,∣q∣)>1, the same parametric curve retraces a single component d times; it does not automatically draw d different components. The corresponding torus link is instead the full set of points on the torus whose angles satisfy qϕ−pθ∈2πZ, with meridional angle ϕ and longitudinal angle θ. It has d disjoint components, each with winding counts (p/d,q/d). For instance, the torus link T2,2​ consists of two parallel (1,1) loops and is a Hopf link, whereas substituting (2,2) into γp,q​ simply traces one of those loops twice.
