Exclusion versus particle position

Exclusion constrains complete one-particle states, not a collection of little spatial boxes. In particular, two opposite-spin electrons in the same spatial orbital have
Ψ(r1​,s1​;r2​,s2​)=ϕa​(r1​)ϕa​(r2​)2​χ↑​(s1​)χ↓​(s2​)−χ↓​(s1​)χ↑​(s2​)​.
(8)
The spatial factor is symmetric; the spin-one-half singlet state supplies the required antisymmetry. At r1​=r2​, this wavefunction need not vanish for opposite spin labels. For equal spin labels it does vanish. In a continuous position description, an exact coincident point has zero measure anyway; the useful comparison concerns joint probability densities or detection in finite regions. Equal positions do not imply equal complete quantum states.
The fermionic occupation numbers nα​=0,1 refer to a chosen orthonormal basis of one-particle states. A quantum superposition need not have definite occupation numbers in that basis, and an expectation value ⟨nα​⟩ can be fractional. For example, one electron in (u+v)/2​ has mean occupation number 1/2 in each of two orthogonal spin orbitals, without splitting into two particles. The canonical anticommutation relations give (aα†​)2=0 and make aα†​aα​ a projector, so its expectation value stays between zero and one in every normalized quantum state.
