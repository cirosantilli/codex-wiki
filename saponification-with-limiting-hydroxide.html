Saponification with limiting hydroxide

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Hydrolysis Saponification
OurBigBook.com Words: 141
Consider a hypothetical closed, well mixed chemical solution initially containing 20mmol of ethyl acetate, 12mmol of hydroxide, and no acetate ions or ethanol. One millimole is 10−3 mole. Assume complete conversion as far as the one-to-one chemical equation allows, with no competing chemical reactions. This is an ideal stoichiometry calculation, not a measured yield.
Let the extent of reaction be ξ, expressed in millimoles. The four values of amount of substance, in the order ethyl acetate, hydroxide, acetate ion, ethanol, are
(20−ξ,12−ξ,ξ,ξ)mmol,0≤ξ≤12.
(34)
The upper bound follows because both reactant quantities must be nonnegative. Thus hydroxide is the limiting reagent, and the ideal endpoint ξ=12 leaves
8mmol ethyl acetate,0mmol hydroxide,12mmol acetate,12mmol ethanol.
(35)
The carboxylate formed equals the hydroxide consumed, while unreacted ester remains. Adding more hydroxide raises the stoichiometry bound; merely waiting longer does not change that bound. To convert these values of amount of substance into concentrations, a volume is also required. The endpoint does not specify the reaction rate or time needed to approach it.
