Ligand-dependent membrane phosphorylation experiment

 ... Biology Protein Receptor (biochemistry) Cell-surface receptor Receptor tyrosine kinase Receptor phosphorylation assay
OurBigBook.com Words: 203
A motivating experiment used isolated membranes from A-431 biological cells, which supplied abundant EGFR. Separate membrane aliquots were incubated at 0∘C with ATP carrying radioactive phosphorus in its terminal phosphate group, plus magnesium or manganese ions. Radioactivity in material recovered by chemical acid-induced precipitation supplied the readout. Adding EGF beforehand enhanced incorporation relative to the unstimulated preparation; this placed a ligand-dependent biochemical response in a cell-free membrane system.[ref]
The follow-up comparisons separated increased addition from suppressed removal: phosphate removal was rapid but unaffected by EGF, and anti-EGF antibody reversed activation by removing the ligand. These observations support reversible regulation of membrane-associated protein kinase activity.[ref] Total incorporation alone cannot identify which protein or amino acid residue received the label, prove intrinsic receptor catalysis, or predict an intact biological cell's response.
The same account records an instructive identification error: receptor phosphorylation was initially assigned to the wrong amino acid residue because released modified amino acids migrated together in the electrophoretic electric field. Reanalysis identified phosphotyrosine residues. Retained radioactivity is evidence about the assay's separation and detection, not unique chemical identity.[ref]
