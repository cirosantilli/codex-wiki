Step-growth polymerization

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
In step-growth polymerization, suitable reactive groups on molecules of different chain lengths can join. The linear count and its assumptions are given in polymerization.
A one-unit monomer, a two-unit chain, and a twenty-unit chain can all be joining partners if their functional groups are compatible and accessible. Schematically, Pj​+Pk​⟶Pj+k​: the subscripts count monomeric units, and end-group chemistry is suppressed. In chain-growth propagation, by contrast, Pj∗​+M⟶Pj+1∗​ requires a free monomer and a polymerization-active site. An arbitrary pair of existing chains is not a propagation pair in that mechanism. The practical consequence of step-growth polymerization is that most monomers can have joined something while the average chain is still short.
Whether a joining step releases a small molecule is a separate question. Polycondensation releases small products during the joining chemistry, as in the Nylon 6,10 demonstration in polymerization. Polyaddition joins suitable chains without that elimination: reacting a diol with a molecule bearing two isocyanate groups can build a polyurethane. Thus step-growth polymerization and polycondensation are not synonyms.[ref]
