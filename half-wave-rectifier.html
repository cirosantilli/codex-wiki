Half-wave rectifier (vL​(t)=max(0,vS​(t)))

A half-wave rectifier with one diode and a resistor load passes one half of each input cycle.[ref]
For an original ideal example, let an ideal voltage source supply the sinusoid vS​(t)=Vp​sin(ωt), with Vp​=5V and angular frequency ω>0. Connect its positive reference terminal to the diode anode, the diode cathode to the upper terminal of a load resistor with electrical resistance RL​=1kΩ, and the lower terminal to the source return. Let vL​ be the upper-minus-lower load electric potential difference, and iL​ the downward load electric current. Assume the ideal diode model, zero source electrical resistance, and no filter capacitor, inductor or switching delay.
The Kirchhoff cycle law gives vS​=VD​+vL​, and Ohm's law gives vL​=RL​iL​. If vS​>0, the blocking assumption would give VD​=vS​>0, contradicting the ideal diode model, so the diode conducts with VD​=0. If vS​<0, conduction would give iL​=vS​/RL​<0, also forbidden, so the diode blocks. At the crossings both values vanish. Thus
vL​(t)=max{0,Vp​sin(ωt)},iL​(t)=RL​vL​(t)​.
(9)
The peak load electric current is 5mA. The output is unidirectional but still varies in time, reaching zero throughout every negative half-cycle. Over a period of a function 2π/ω, write the wave phase as θ=ωt. Its average value of a function and root mean square follow from two integrals:
vL​=2π1​∫0π​Vp​sinθdθ=πVp​​=1.59155V,vL,rms​=(2π1​∫0π​Vp2​sin2θdθ)1/2=2Vp​​=2.500V.
(10)
Consequently the average load power is vL,rms2​/RL​=6.25mW, not vL2​/RL​. The ideal diode has zero loss; the resistor converts supplied energy into Joule heating. The same switching argument predicts vL​=min(0,vS​) when the diode is reversed. Directional selection changes the waveform and its average value of a function; it does not by itself produce a constant output.
