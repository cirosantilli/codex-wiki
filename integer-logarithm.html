Integer logarithm (⌊logb​n⌋)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Exponential function Logarithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 162
For integers b>1 and n>0, the integer logarithm is the largest nonnegative integer i such that bi≤n. Equivalently,
bi≤n<bi+1,i=⌊logb​n⌋.
(25)
The equivalence follows from the monotonicity of exponentiation in its exponent and the definition of the floor function. For example, 34=81≤100<243=35, so the base-three integer logarithm of 100 is 4.
A multiplication loop maintains p=bi≤n and accepts another factor b while pb≤n. Repeated squaring makes the search faster: precompute b2k starting at k=0, and consider these factors in decreasing k. Include a factor when the accumulated product remains at most n. If L=⌊logb​n⌋, the loop invariant before considering k is i≤L<i+2k+1. Accepting 2k exactly when i+2k≤L halves that uncertainty, proving the greedy algorithm returns L. This takes O(1+log(1+logb​n)) arithmetic operations; it is not the same claim as this bound on bit complexity.
For positive operands in bounded integer arithmetic, test p≤nDIVq before multiplying by q. Integer division makes this equivalent to pq≤n while preventing integer overflow in a rejected candidate.
