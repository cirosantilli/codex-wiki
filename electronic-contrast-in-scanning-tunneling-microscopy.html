Electronic contrast in scanning tunneling microscopy (z=h+lnρ/(2κ)+constant)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Potential barrier Quantum tunnelling Tunnel junction Scanning tunneling microscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 368
Separate geometry from electronic response in an original one-dimensional toy tunnel junction. Write the geometric surface height as h(x), tip height as z(x), and positive gap as s(x)=z(x)−h(x). Assume low fixed tip-sample electric potential difference: the sampled energy window is narrow enough that the tip density of states is featureless and the sample local density of states varies little across that window. Neglect thermal broadening, changes of tip shape and lateral averaging. Let ρ(x)>0 be the effective sample local density of states divided by a positive reference value, so it is dimensionless. Choose a spatially constant effective barrier energy Φ>0, a positive coefficient C with units of electric current, and
I(x,z)=Cρ(x)exp[−2κ(z−h(x))],κ=ℏ2me​Φ​​>0.
(82)
Here me​ is the electron mass, ℏ the reduced Planck constant, and κ the tunnelling decay constant. This is an illustrative low-bias model, not a universal calibration formula. At fixed I=I0​>0, taking a logarithm gives
ln(I0​/C)=lnρ−2κ(z−h),z(x)=h(x)+2κlnρ(x)​+s0​,s0​=2κln(C/I0​)​.
(83)
The logarithms have dimensionless arguments. Only constant ρ makes the recorded height differ from geometric height by a constant. Multiplying ρ by r>0 raises the apparent height by lnr/(2κ) without moving the geometric surface.
For a concrete ambiguity, choose κ=10nm−1, a=0.020nm, lateral period L=1nm, s0​=0.50nm, and q(x)=cos(2πx/L). Compare
hA​(x)hB​(x)​=aq(x),=0,​ρA​(x)ρB​(x)​=1,=exp(2κaq(x)).​
(84)
Both produce exactly z(x)=s0​+aq(x) at I0​=Ce−2κs0​=Ce−10. Substitution checks this without identifying ρ with geometric height: case A has constant gap s0​, while case B has gap s0​+aq(x) and a compensating electronic factor. The chosen ρB​ ranges from e−0.4 to e0.4, approximately 0.6703 to 1.4918; every gap remains positive. The chosen κ corresponds to Φ≃3.81 electronvolts, a positive effective barrier.
At fixed tip height zf​=s0​, both cases instead give
I0​I(x,zf​)​=exp[0.4q(x)].
(85)
Their maximum-to-minimum electric current ratio is e0.8≃2.226. Thus even adding this fixed-height scan at the same electric potential difference cannot distinguish these deliberately chosen surfaces within the toy model. Independent structural information or additional electronic measurements are required. Changing electric potential difference can supply such information, but also changes the assumptions used to define C and ρ.
