Autocatalytic enzyme clearance with a saddle threshold (u′=ηu−u+uv+(K−λ)v,ϵv′=u−uv−Kv)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Enzyme kinetics Michaelis-Menten kinetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 119
Substrate replication competes with saturating enzyme clearance in this fast-slow model, where K>λ>0 and ϵ≪1. The physical strip u≥0, 0≤v≤1 is invariant. If 0<η<λ/K, the origin is attracting and a positive saddle exists at ue​=λ/η−K, ve​=1−ηK/λ. Eliminating the fast complex fraction gives u′≃ηu(u−ue​)/(u+K), separating extinction from runaway. For initially zero complexes, the stable-manifold threshold is u∗​=ue​+ϵ(1−η)ve​+O(ϵ2), away from singular parameter limits. To derive the correction, write the stable manifold as u=ue​+ϵϕ(v); its invariance equation gives ϕ′=−(1−η) and ϕ(ve​)=0. A Dulac factor 1/u has strictly negative weighted divergence, excluding a periodic alternative to the two basins. Saturated clearance and autocatalytic growth can therefore create an unstable concentration threshold rather than a stable nonzero balance.
