Motional electromotive force (Emot​=∮C​(v×B)⋅dl)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current Electromotive force
OurBigBook.com Words: 413 Articles: 2
Motional electromotive force is the contribution ∮C​(v×B)⋅dl from an electrical conductor moving with local velocity v through a magnetic field. The cross product comes from the magnetic part of the Lorentz force. For a straight rod of length ℓ, perpendicular to both its velocity and a uniform magnetic field, its magnitude is Bℓv. The sign depends on the direction of integration along the rod. [ref].
For a computed example, take ℓ=0.50m, velocity v=2.0xm/s to the right, and magnetic field B=0.40zT out of the page. The rod lies along y. Then
v×B=−0.80y​V/m,∫topbottom​(v×B)⋅dl=Bℓv=0.40V.
(75)
Positive electric charge is driven downward. For an isolated rod after charge separation has equilibrated, E=−v×B inside it, and the electric potential difference is Vbottom​−Vtop​=+0.40V; the bottom is positive. This open-circuit statement does not imply a sustained electric current.
Close the rod's path with stationary U-shaped rails and an electrical resistance R>0 at the left. With out-of-page unit normal, the positive loop direction is counterclockwise, hence upward along the rod. If the rod's horizontal position is x(t), the enclosed area is A=ℓx and the external magnetic flux is Φext​=Bℓx. Thus
ECCW​=−Bℓx˙=−0.40V,ICCW​=−R0.40V​.
(76)
Here the resistive approximation neglects self-inductance. The negative electric current is clockwise, downward along the rod, consistent with the charge-separation calculation and Lenz's law.
The closed-loop result illustrates why the moving-circuit form of Faraday's law of induction includes motional electromotive force: the magnetic field is constant in time, so the electric field circulation alone would miss this drive. Its mechanical consequence is electromagnetic braking of a sliding rod.
