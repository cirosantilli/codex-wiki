Measurable-factor rule for conditional expectation (E[HY∣G]=HE[Y∣G])

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
Let Y be an integrable random variable, let H be a finite G-measurable random variable, and suppose HY is an integrable random variable. Then a known factor can be taken outside the conditional expectation:
E[HY∣G]=HE[Y∣G]a.s.
(25)
For a bounded H, first verify the defining integral identity when H is the indicator function of a measurable set in G, extend by linearity to simple functions, and then use bounded approximation and the dominated convergence theorem. For nonnegative H,Y, truncating H and using the conditional monotone convergence theorem proves the identity with extended nonnegative conditional expectations. In particular,
E[∣H∣E[∣Y∣∣G]]=E∣HY∣<∞.
(26)
For general H,Y under the stated integrability assumptions, the conditional Jensen inequality bounds ∣HE[Y∣G]∣ by that integrable random variable. Apply the bounded-factor result to truncations of H, then use the dominated convergence theorem on the products and the L1 contraction of conditional expectation. This proves the identity without assuming H itself is an integrable random variable.
The product-integrability condition is essential to the ordinary finite conditional expectation statement. An unbounded factor cannot be moved through an expected value simply because its partner is an integrable random variable.[ref]
