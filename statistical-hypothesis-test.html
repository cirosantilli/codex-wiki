False alarm rate (FAR)

Words: 23
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
The expected rate of detections exceeding a stated threshold under a specified background-only model. A time rate and a per-trial significance level require different normalizations.

Statistical hypothesis

Words: 38
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A statistical hypothesis is a specified set of possible data-generating probability distributions, usually expressed as a restriction on model parameters. A simple statistical hypothesis specifies one distribution; a composite statistical hypothesis permits several. A hypothesis test compares a null hypothesis with an alternative hypothesis using a statistic calibrated under the null.

Sequential probability ratio test (St​=∑s=1t​log[f1​(Ys​)/f0​(Ys​)])

Words: 47
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A sequential probability ratio test accumulates the log-likelihood ratio for two specified simple statistical hypotheses until it reaches an upper or lower boundary. An upper crossing favors the alternative, a lower crossing favors the null, and observations continue between them. Error-calibrated boundaries are approximately log[(1−β)/α] and log[β/(1−α)]; discrete overshoot means these approximations need calibration.

Statistical significance

Words: 191 Articles: 2
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A result is statistically significant at a specified significance level α when its valid p-value is at most α and the corresponding null hypothesis is rejected. Significance does not measure effect size, establish practical importance, or give the probability that the null is true. Software stars encode thresholds for p-values; they do not supply an additional statistic.

Poisson tail discovery probability (p=P(N≥n∣μb​)=1−e−μb​∑k=0n−1​μbk​/k!)

Words: 108
Statistical significance OurBigBook.com
For a known background rate b observed for exposure T, independent events give a Poisson distribution with mean μb​=bT. An excess-count test of the background-only null hypothesis uses the upper-tail P-value in the title. The tail, rather than the probability of exactly n, counts all outcomes at least as discrepant in the chosen direction.
This probability is conditional on the null model; it is not the posterior probability that the null model is true. A stipulated discovery threshold is met only when the tail is below that threshold. Uncertainty in the background rate or selecting among many searches needs additional modelling rather than being absorbed into the same known-mean formula.

Two-sided hypothesis test

Words: 42
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A two-sided test detects departures in either direction from the null hypothesis, for example θ=θ0​. With a continuous symmetric null statistic centred at zero, its p-value is P0​(∣T∣≥∣tobs​∣)=2P0​(T≥∣tobs​∣). This is the usual coefficient test printed by Gaussian regression summaries, unlike a directional one-sided comparison.

One-sided hypothesis test

Words: 56
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A one-sided test targets deviations in one specified direction, such as θ>θ0​. If larger values of a statistic T indicate this direction, its p-value is an upper-tail null probability P0​(T≥tobs​). The direction should be chosen from the scientific question before examining the data. An upper normal test uses 1−Φ(z); a lower test uses Φ(z).

Alternative hypothesis (H1​)

Words: 64
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
The alternative hypothesis specifies the parameter values or distributions against which a null hypothesis is tested. If the null is θ∈Θ0​, the alternative is θ∈Θ1​ with Θ0​∩Θ1​=∅. For example an upper one-sided comparison uses θ>θ0​, while a two-sided comparison uses θ=θ0​. The alternative determines the relevant rejection tail. A p-value is computed under the null; it is not the probability that the alternative is false.

Hotelling's T-squared statistic (T2=n(Xˉ−μ0​)TS−1(Xˉ−μ0​))

Words: 172 Articles: 3
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
For independent multivariate normal observations with positive-definite covariance, this statistic is the maximum squared studentized mean contrast. With unbiased sample covariance matrix S and n>p, its null distribution obeys (n−p)T2/(p(n−1))∼Fp,n−p​. This follows from independence of the Gaussian sample mean and the residual Wishart distribution.

Affine invariance of Hotelling's statistic (TY2​=TX2​)

Words: 53
Hotelling's T-squared statistic OurBigBook.com
Under a nonsingular affine transformation Y=AX+b, transform the null mean by the same map. Then the centered sample mean becomes A(Xˉ−μ0​) and the sample covariance matrix becomes ASAT. Since (ASAT)−1=A−TS−1A−1, the quadratic form in Hotelling's T-squared statistic is unchanged. Its value and test decision are consequently invariant under changes of units, origin and nonsingular coordinates.

At equally spaced distinct times, a mean profile is affine exactly when its consecutive second differences vanish. These form p−2 independent contrasts. At unequal times, compare adjacent slopes instead: (μi+1​−μi​)/(ti+1​−ti​)−(μi​−μi−1​)/(ti​−ti−1​). A Hotelling test of linear hypotheses tests these restrictions jointly with unknown covariance.

McNemar's test (X2=(b−c)2/(b+c))

Words: 269 Articles: 2
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
For paired binary observations (Ci​,Mi​), let b count pairs (1,0) and c pairs (0,1). Under the null hypothesis of equal marginal event probabilities, these discordant outcomes have equal probabilities. Conditional on b+c, b has the binomial distribution Bin(b+c,1/2), giving an exact test. For enough discordant pairs, (b−c)/b+c​ has an approximate standard normal distribution, or its square has an approximate one-degree-of-freedom chi-squared distribution. Concordant pairs provide no information about the direction of the marginal difference. If there are no discordant pairs, there is no directional information and the divided statistic is not computed. The pairing and independence between pairs are essential; the usual independent-two-sample formula is not a paired test.

Equality criterion for paired and unpaired allele tests

Words: 65
McNemar's test OurBigBook.com
Let a paired binary table have cells a,b,c,d and n=a+b+c+d. The uncorrected McNemar test is (b−c)2/(b+c), while the ordinary independent-sample chi-squared test on the two marginal allele columns is 2n(b−c)2/[(2a+b+c)(2d+b+c)]. For nonzero difference and positive denominators, they coincide exactly when (2a+b+c)(2d+b+c)=2n(b+c). Such numerical coincidence does not justify discarding pairing; altering concordant counts can change the unpaired statistic while leaving McNemar's statistic unchanged.

Paired binary sample size calculation (n≃{z1−α/2​q​+z1−β​q−δ2​}2/δ2)

Words: 98
McNemar's test OurBigBook.com
For a paired binary difference D=C−M, set δ=P(C=1)−P(M=1) and q=P(C=M). Then ED=δ and Var(D)=q−δ2. Under equal marginal probabilities the variance is q. The McNemar test has approximate upper rejection boundary z1−α/2​nq​ for the sum of differences. Requiring that the alternative mean nδ exceed this boundary by z1−β​n(q−δ2)​ gives the displayed planning size for a positive alternative, neglecting its very small lower-tail rejection probability. Use z1−α​ for a pre-specified one-sided test. The marginal probabilities alone do not determine q: co-occurrence of positive outcomes must be specified or bounded. Discreteness and clustered data can change achieved statistical power.

Significant and nonsignificant results need not differ significantly

Words: 56
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
For independent effect estimates 1.01 with standard error 0.50, and 0.99 with standard error 0.51, one two-sided Wald test is significant at five percent and the other is not. But their difference is 0.02 with standard error 0.502+0.512​≈0.714, giving a negligible test statistic. Hence different significance labels do not establish an interaction or between-study heterogeneity; compare the effects directly.

Generate independent replicated datasets under a fully specified null hypothesis and calculate the same predictive discrepancy statistic for them and the observation. Their null exchangeability gives a finite-simulation rank test. A measurable summary of a possible dataset is chosen to detect disagreement with a specified predictive model. Compare its observed value with its distribution under replicated datasets. Scan statistic (T=maxS∈S​TS​)

Words: 153 Articles: 2
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A scan statistic searches a family of candidate locations or subsets and reports the largest local statistic. Under a null hypothesis, a union bound converts a tail estimate for each candidate into a bound for the maximum; no independence between local statistics is required. Structured alternatives often permit a chi-squared testing lower bound using the overlap geometry of two candidates.

Cyclic interval overlap bound (P(S∩T=∅)≤2k/d)

Words: 63
Scan statistic OurBigBook.com
For independent uniformly placed cyclic intervals of length k<d, an overlap is possible at at most 2k−1 starting-point offsets, or at all d offsets if that number exceeds d. Thus the overlap probability is at most min{1,(2k−1)/d}. If R=∣S∩T∣, then 0≤R≤k and E(eaR/k−1)≤(2k/d)(ea−1) for a≥0. The overlap law is not the hypergeometric distribution for unrestricted random subsets.

Scan statistic OurBigBook.com
A quadratic scan statistic maximizes a directional empirical second moment over candidate unit vectors. For a Gaussian random vector sample with a rank-one covariance spike in one of those directions, the matching statistic has its scale multiplied by the spike's eigenvalue. A chi-squared concentration inequality controls each direction.

Least-favourable null configuration

Words: 42
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A least-favourable configuration maximizes a test procedure’s Type I error probability over allowed null hypothesis parameters. For one-sided normal comparisons with a fixed covariance matrix, coupling by common centered variables shows monotonicity in the means. For the familywise error rate, mixed true and false null configurations must also be checked.

Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity (X2=∑(O−E)2/E)

Words: 251 Articles: 3
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
The Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity tests whether several independent categorical samples have the same category probabilities. Under the null, expected cell counts equal the product of the corresponding row and column totals divided by the total sample size. The Pearson chi-squared statistic for contingency tables ∑(O−E)2/E has an asymptotic chi-squared distribution with (r−1)(c−1) degrees of freedom, when expected counts are sufficiently large. The sampling design differs from a Pearson chi-squared test of independence, though the statistic has the same form.

Empty groups in a binomial homogeneity test

Words: 67
Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity OurBigBook.com
For independent Ri​∼Bin(ni​,pi​), a group with ni​=0 necessarily has Ri​=0 and contributes likelihood one. Its probability parameter is unidentifiable and supplies no restriction to test. If k groups have positive sample sizes, the unrestricted model has k probability parameters and the common-probability model has one; the asymptotic homogeneity test therefore has k−1 degrees of freedom. Zero expected-count rows must be removed rather than divided by in the Pearson statistic.

Pearson chi-squared statistic for contingency tables (X2=∑ij​(Oij​−Eij​)2/Eij​)

Words: 117 Articles: 1
Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity OurBigBook.com
For observed counts Oij​ and positive expected counts Eij​ in a contingency table, the statistic is X2=∑ij​(Oij​−Eij​)2/Eij​. Under common category probabilities or statistical independence, estimate Eij​ by the product of the row and column totals divided by the grand total. With large expected counts, this gives the test statistic for the Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity or the Pearson chi-squared test of independence, with asymptotic chi-squared distribution on (r−1)(c−1) degrees of freedom.

Yates's correction for continuity (XY2​=∑i,j​(max{∣Oij​−Eij​∣−1/2,0})2/Eij​)

Words: 58
Pearson chi-squared statistic for contingency tables OurBigBook.com
For a two-by-two contingency table, subtracting one half from each absolute observed-minus-expected count difference, with truncation at zero, gives the displayed corrected Pearson chi-squared statistic. Expected counts use the independence model Eij​=Oi+​O+j​/N. The subtraction compensates approximately for using a continuous chi-squared distribution for discrete counts. It can make the test conservative. The corrected statistic should be distinguished from the ordinary uncorrected Pearson chi-squared statistic.

Statistical test (φ(x)∈[0,1])

Words: 124 Articles: 1
Statistical hypothesis test OurBigBook.com
A statistical test is a measurable rule for deciding whether to reject a null hypothesis. Allowing randomisation, it is represented by a function φ(x)∈[0,1] giving the conditional rejection probability after observing x. A deterministic test takes only values zero and one. Under a simple null, E0​φ is its size of a statistical test; under an alternative, E1​φ is its statistical power. Randomisation on a likelihood ratio equality set allows a Neyman-Pearson lemma threshold test to attain a prescribed size even for discrete data.

Most powerful test

Words: 51
Statistical test OurBigBook.com
At level α, a most powerful test against a specified simple alternative maximises statistical power among statistical tests whose size of a statistical test is at most α. The Neyman-Pearson lemma constructs it by a likelihood ratio threshold. A uniformly most powerful test is most powerful simultaneously against every member of the specified alternative family; maximising power at one alternative alone need not give uniform optimality.

For an independent sample from a normal distribution, maximization of the likelihood over the unknown variance gives the likelihood ratio for a fixed mean against an unrestricted mean as displayed, where t=n​(Xˉ−μ0​)/S and S2=∑i​(Xi​−Xˉ)2/(n−1). Regression coefficient test power ignores nuisance coefficients

Words: 50
Student's t-test OurBigBook.com
In a fixed full-rank normal linear model, the studentized estimator of one coefficient has a noncentral t-distribution with noncentrality equal to that coefficient divided by its standard deviation. The fitted and residual orthogonal projections of the Gaussian noise are independent. Therefore the statistical power of its exact Student's t-test does not depend on the other regression coefficients, with design and variance fixed.

For two independent binomial counts, form the Fisher exact test rejection region from their conditional null hypergeometric distribution. Posterior odds are monotone under a monotone likelihood ratio

Monotone likelihood ratio OurBigBook.com
For a family with a monotone likelihood ratio in T, a prior density π and cutoff a, the posterior odds of θ>a against θ≤a are nondecreasing in T. To prove it, write the unnormalized masses as A(t) and B(t). For t2​>t1​, the difference A(t2​)B(t1​)−A(t1​)B(t2​) is the double integral over θ>a≥η of π(θ)π(η)[fθ​(t2​)fη​(t1​)−fθ​(t1​)fη​(t2​)]. Each integrand is nonnegative by the likelihood-ratio ordering. Common factors in the full-data likelihood cancel from the odds, so the same argument works when T is a statistic rather than the complete observation. Interpret zero denominators by extended odds whenever the posterior is defined.

Monotone test

An upper-tail monotone test in a real statistic T has rejection probability ϕ=1T>c​+η1T=c​, with 0≤η≤1; the constant tests correspond to infinite cutoffs. For a family with positive densities on a common support and a monotone likelihood ratio in T, its power function can cross that of an arbitrary competing test only from below to above as the parameter increases. If r=fθ1​​/fθ0​​ and θ1​>θ0​, choose a positive number k between the ratios on opposite sides of the cutoff. The signs of ϕ−ϕ′ and r−k agree away from T=c, and r=k at a boundary with positive mass. Therefore Eθ1​​(ϕ−ϕ′)≥kEθ0​​(ϕ−ϕ′). In particular strict positivity at θ0​ propagates to θ1​.
