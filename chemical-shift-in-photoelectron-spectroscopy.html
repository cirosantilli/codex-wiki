Chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy (ΔEB​)

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Photoemission spectroscopy X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 587 Articles: 1
A chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy is a difference in binding energy for the same kind of core electron in different chemical surroundings, using comparable energy references. Identifying a chemical element assigns its family of lines; resolving chemical shifts within that family asks how its chemical atoms are bonded. For example, distinguishing a metal from its chemically modified surface requires more than detecting that element in both places.
The removal energy compares the initial electronic state with the state left after emission. Redistribution of the remaining electrons changes that final state's energy; a measured shift therefore includes relaxation as well as initial bonding effects.[ref]
This makes chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy useful but conditional. Compare the same electron shell, the same photoelectron binding energy reference, and compatible sample conditions. Agreement with a chemically appropriate reference supports an assignment; a line position by itself need not uniquely determine an oxidation state. An unresolved sum of two chemical components can even have a centroid where neither component lies.
