Bernoulli polarization martingale (Xn+1​=Xn​+α(Bn+1​−Xn​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
Let 0<α<1, X0​=x∈[0,1], and let Bn+1​ be a binary random variable satisfying E[Bn+1​∣Fn​]=Xn​. Suppose Xn​ is Fn​-measurable and Bn+1​ is Fn+1​-measurable. The update
Xn+1​=(1−α)Xn​+αBn+1​
(1)
remains in [0,1] and defines a bounded martingale. For example, take independent uniform random variables Un+1​ independent of the current filtration and set Bn+1​=1{Un+1​≤Xn​}​.
The binary identity Bn+12​=Bn+1​ gives
E[(Xn+1​−Xn​)2∣Fn​]=α2Xn​(1−Xn​),
(2)
and hence
E[Xn+1​(1−Xn+1​)∣Fn​]=(1−α2)Xn​(1−Xn​).
(3)
The L2-bounded martingale convergence theorem gives a terminal random variable X∞​ with almost sure convergence and convergence in L2. Iteration followed by the bounded convergence theorem yields
E[X∞​(1−X∞​)]=limn​x(1−x)(1−α2)n=0.
(4)
Thus X∞​ takes only 0 and 1, and preservation of its expected value gives a Bernoulli distribution with parameter x. The terminal conditional expectation representation gives the exact mean squared error
E[(X∞​−Xn​)2]=x(1−x)(1−α2)n.
(5)
The finite-time paths can stay strictly inside (0,1) while their limit lies entirely at the endpoints. The positive conditional variance in the interior makes permanent convergence to an interior value impossible.
