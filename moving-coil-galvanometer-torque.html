Moving-coil galvanometer torque (τm​=NIAB)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric charge Electric current Galvanometer Moving-coil galvanometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 165
For a rectangular winding with N turns, active side length ℓ, side separation w, and per-turn area A=wℓ, the magnetic force on a current-carrying wire gives equal and opposite side forces of magnitude N∣I∣ℓB. Their force couple has lever arm w. Choosing the positive rotation to match positive electric current in the illustration gives the signed torque and spring balance
τm​=NIAB,τs​=−kθ,NIAB−kθ=0,θ=kNAB​I.
(17)
Here B is the working-gap magnetic flux density, k>0 is the torsional spring constant in Nm/rad, and θ is the deflection angle in radians from the no-current zero. The balance holds after motion has settled, within the radial-field and linear elasticity ranges. Do not identify θ with the angle between a magnetic dipole moment and a fixed uniform magnetic field: that different geometry has torque magnitude NIAB∣sinα∣. [ref]
For an original illustrative design, choose N=120, A=3.00×10−4m2, B=0.250T, and k=3.00×10−6Nm/rad. Then NAB=9.00×10−3Nm/A. At I=+40.0μA, the magnetic torque is +3.60×10−7Nm and θ=+0.120rad≃+6.88∘. Reversing the electric current reverses both the torque and the deflection, giving −6.88∘ without changing the magnetic field.
