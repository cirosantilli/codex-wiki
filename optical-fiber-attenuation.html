Optical fiber attenuation (αdB​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Optical fiber
OurBigBook.com Words: 570 Articles: 1
Optical fiber attenuation is the reduction of transmitted optical power along an optical fiber. Define a positive loss in decibels by comparing optical power P(0) already launched into the guide with surviving optical power P(z):
A(z)=10log10​(P(z)P(0)​)dB.
(34)
The factor ten applies to a power ratio; the factor twenty applies to a wave amplitude ratio only when power is proportional to its square under the same conditions.[ref]
For a uniform illustrative model with constant loss coefficient αdB​ in dB/km and distance z in kilometres,
A(z)=αdB​z,P(0)P(z)​=10−A(z)/(10dB).
(35)
For αdB​=0.2dB/km over 10km, the loss is 2dB and
P(0)P(10km)​=10−0.2≃0.630957.
(36)
Thus about 63.1% of the launched optical power survives and 36.9% is lost. This is an illustrative coefficient, not a property of all optical fibers, and is independent of the earlier illustrative refractive indices.
The exponential decay follows from assuming each equal distance interval transmits the same fraction. For two such intervals, power ratios multiply; their logarithms add, so the losses in decibels add. Two 5km sections each transmit 10−0.1≃0.794328; together they transmit (0.794328)2≃0.630957. The loss curve is straight in decibels and curved on a linear power scale.
Wave absorption and scattering of light can remove guided optical power even in a straight fibre, while bends can let it escape. Entrance losses, joints and connectors contribute additional losses; they belong separately from a uniform distance coefficient. This is why a measured end-to-end power ratio need not equal the straight-fibre model.[ref]
