Second fundamental form (II)

OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 90
For an embedded submanifold M⊆N of a Riemannian manifold, the vector-valued second fundamental form is
A(X,Y)=(∇X​Y)⊥.
(1)
Here ∇ is the ambient Levi-Civita connection and the superscript denotes projection onto the normal bundle. For a hypersurface in Euclidean space with unit normal N, the scalar form is II(X,Y)=⟨A(X,Y),N⟩=⟨−dN(X),Y⟩. In a surface parametrization its coefficients are e=N⋅Xuu​, f=N⋅Xuv​, and g=N⋅Xvv​.

Totally umbilic hypersurface (Kab​=λhab​)

Words: 130 Articles: 1
Second fundamental form OurBigBook.com
At every point its second fundamental form is a scalar multiple of its induced metric. Equivalently its shape operator is scalar, so all principal normal curvatures agree. For a three-dimensional hypersurface, K=3λ and Kab​Kab=3λ2. The sign of its intrinsic scalar in a vacuum Lorentzian ambient space then follows from the Gauss equation for a nonnull hypersurface.

Scalar curvature of an umbilic vacuum hypersurface ((3)R=6ελ2)

Words: 78
Totally umbilic hypersurface OurBigBook.com
For a timelike unit normal with squared norm epsilon and vanishing ambient Ricci tensor, contraction of the Gauss equation for a nonnull hypersurface gives the displayed formula. In signature (+−−−) the timelike normal has epsilon plus one, so the scalar of the induced negative-definite metric is nonnegative. In signature (−+++) the positive-definite spatial metric instead has scalar −6λ2. Replacing the induced metric by its negative reverses the scalar, so the inequality must not be transferred between these conventions without adjustment.

Zero second fundamental form implies planar image (II=0⟹dN=0)

Words: 70
Second fundamental form OurBigBook.com
Differentiate the orthogonality of the unit normal to the two independent coordinate tangent vectors. If the second fundamental form vanishes, each derivative of the normal is orthogonal to both tangent vectors; differentiating its unit length also makes it orthogonal to the normal. Thus both normal derivatives vanish. On a connected parameter domain the normal is constant, and differentiating the position's scalar product with that constant normal proves the surface lies in a plane.

Gauss–Codazzi equations

Words: 2k Articles: 45
Second fundamental form OurBigBook.com
The Gauss–Codazzi equations relate the intrinsic curvature and connection of an embedded submanifold to its second fundamental form and the ambient curvature. The Gauss equation controls tangential curvature; the Codazzi equation controls the covariant derivative of the second fundamental form. The Gauss formula is the tangential-normal splitting used to derive these relations.

Assume the trace Ricci curvature satisfies Ric≥(n−1)kg, with k>0. If points p,q have Riemannian distance D=π/k​, the balls B(p,r) and B(q,D−r) are disjoint. For a connected Riemannian manifold of dimension n≥2, assume geodesic completeness and Ric≥(n−1)κg for a constant κ>0. Complete positively curved paraboloid (g=(1+4r2)dr2+r2dθ2,K=4/(1+4r2)2)
Words: 70
Myers's theorem OurBigBook.com
The graph z=x2+y2 in Euclidean three-space has the displayed induced Riemannian metric and positive Gaussian curvature. It is smooth at its vertex; the polar-coordinate singularity is only a coordinate artifact. The metric dominates the Euclidean plane metric, so upward stability of Riemannian completeness proves completeness. It has infinite diameter. In dimension two Ricci curvature equals Gaussian curvature times the metric, so this is a counterexample to compactness under merely pointwise positive Ricci curvature. Its curvature tends to zero at infinity.
Finite fundamental group from a uniform positive Ricci bound (Ric≥(n−1)κg ⟹ ∣π1​(M)∣<∞)

Myers's theorem OurBigBook.com
If a connected complete Riemannian manifold has Ric≥(n−1)κg with κ>0, its universal cover with the lifted metric is complete and satisfies the same bound. The Bonnet-Myers theorem makes that cover compact. A covering fiber is closed and discrete and hence finite; the number of its elements equals the order of the fundamental group. Applying the diameter theorem only to the base would not prove this conclusion: the positive lower bound must also be used on the cover.

Ricci-flat obstruction for a closed three-manifold times a line
Words: 79
Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem OurBigBook.com
If a connected closed manifold M3 has a noncontractible universal cover, then M×R has no complete metric making it a Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold. The product has two ends and hence contains a line in a Riemannian manifold for any complete metric. The Cheeger-Gromoll splitting theorem would split it isometrically as N3×R. Its three-dimensional factor is Ricci-flat and therefore flat by three-dimensional curvature from the Ricci tensor. The complete simply connected cover of the product would be Euclidean four-space. But that cover is M×R, which retracts onto M, contradicting noncontractibility.

On a compact flat Riemannian manifold, the integrated Bochner identity for Killing vector fields makes every Killing vector field parallel. Thus the six orientable closed flat three-manifold types have identity-component isometry dimensions three for the torus, one for each cyclic nontrivial holonomy, and zero for the Hantzsche-Wendt manifold.
Bieberbach theorem
Words: 73
Flat manifold OurBigBook.com
The theorem classifies compact flat manifolds through their crystallographic covering groups.
For a compact flat Riemannian manifold Rn/Γ, the translations in Γ form a normal full-rank Euclidean lattice of finite index. The quotient is its finite linear holonomy. Affine conjugacy of the torsion-free crystallographic groups classifies these manifolds up to affine diffeomorphism. An abelian Γ must consist entirely of translations: commuting with every lattice translation forces each linear part to fix a spanning set of Rn.

The closed orientable flat Riemannian manifold with holonomy C2​×C2​ can be presented by Euclidean screws α(x,y,z)=(x+1/2,−y,−z) and β(x,y,z)=(−x,y+1/2,−z+1/2). Short-vector cancellation for flat tori
Words: 90
Flat torus OurBigBook.com
Let Λ1​,Λ2​⊂Rd be full-rank Euclidean lattices, and choose ε smaller than every nonzero vector length in either. The shortest vectors of Λi​⊕εZk are exactly the new coordinate vectors and their negatives. Any isometry between the associated flat tori lifts to an affine Euclidean isometry, whose orthogonal part maps the lattices. It must preserve the span of the shortest vectors, hence also its orthogonal complement, and therefore maps Λ1​ to Λ2​. Consequently a nonisometric pair remains nonisometric after adding sufficiently short equal circle factors. The spectrum of a flat torus shows that adding the same factors preserves isospectral manifolds.
Spectrum of a flat torus (λw​=4π2∣w∣2)

Two-dimensional lattice reconstruction from vector lengths (G=(a2a2b2−A2​​a2b2−A2​b2​))
Words: 100

The vector-length multiset of a rank-two Euclidean lattice determines it up to an orthogonal map. Let a be the shortest nonzero length. Removing two copies of each positive integer multiple of a removes one primitive lattice line; the shortest remaining length b is the shortest independent-vector length. This proves two-dimensional spectral rigidity for a flat torus through its dual lattice.
