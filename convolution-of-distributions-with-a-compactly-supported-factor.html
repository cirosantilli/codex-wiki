Convolution of distributions with a compactly supported factor (u∗v)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Convolution
OurBigBook.com Words: 82
If v is a compactly supported distribution and u is any distribution, define ⟨u∗v,ϕ⟩=⟨ux​,⟨vy​,ϕ(x+y)⟩⟩. The inner function is smooth and supported in suppϕ−suppv, hence is a test function for u. Finite-order estimates prove continuity, while the tensor product of distributions proves commutativity after inserting compact cutoffs. For every test function ψ, (u∗v)∗ψ=u∗(v∗ψ); evaluating at zero against reflected test functions proves uniqueness. If both distributions have compact support, supp(u∗v)⊂suppu+suppv and the convolution theorem gives u∗v=uv. Without a compact factor or another support/growth condition, arbitrary distributional convolution is not generally defined.
