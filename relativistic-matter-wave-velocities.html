Relativistic matter-wave velocities (vph​=E/p,vg​=dE/dp)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Wave function Matter wave
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
For a one-dimensional free particle with m>0 and p>0, take the positive total energy from the relativistic energy-momentum relation:
E(p)=m2c4+p2c2​,ω=E/ℏ,k=p/ℏ.
(4)
A fixed plane wave quantum phase obeys kx−ωt=constant. Its phase velocity is therefore
vph​=kω​=pE​=vc2​>c.
(5)
For a narrow wave packet centered at p0​, expand its dispersion relation as E(p)≃E(p0​)+E′(p0​)(p−p0​). In the Fourier inversion integral building the wave packet, the envelope then depends on x−E′(p0​)t. Thus its group velocity is
vg​=dkdω​=dpdE​=Epc2​=v<c,vph​vg​=c2.
(6)
The superluminal phase velocity follows a repeating phase marker; it does not describe a localized signal or superluminal transport of energy. Higher derivatives of the dispersion relation produce wave packet spreading, so the translated-envelope argument is a narrow-band approximation. For v=0.6c, the Lorentz factor is 1.25, p=0.75mc and E=1.25mc2: the group velocity is 0.6c while the phase velocity is 5c/3.
The total-energy convention matters. Define ψ=eimc2t/ℏΨ to remove the common rest energy from a wavefunction Ψ with total-energy time dependence. Then
K=E−mc2≃2mp2​,ωNR​=K/ℏ,kωNR​​≃2mp​,dkdωNR​​≃mp​.
(7)
This is exactly the free-particle momentum convention used by the Schrödinger equation here. Removing a common time-dependent global phase changes the reported phase velocity, while leaving the de Broglie wavelength, relative spatial quantum phases and group velocity unchanged. Adding mc2 again to a total energy would count the rest energy twice.
