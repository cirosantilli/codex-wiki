Corrections to a pulling measurement of kinetic friction (μk​=(Fcosα−ma)/(mg−Fsinα))

 ... Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newtonian mechanics Friction Kinetic friction Coefficient of kinetic friction
OurBigBook.com Words: 221
For a block sliding right on a stationary horizontal plane, take rightward acceleration a as positive. A gauge reading F for a pull at angle α above horizontal has horizontal component Fcosα and upward component Fsinα. With no vertical acceleration or other forces, Newton's second law gives
N=mg−Fsinα,Fcosα−∣fk​∣=ma,
(5)
and therefore
μk​=mg−FsinαFcosα−ma​.
(6)
This inference requires continued contact with N>0 and rightward contact slip, so its numerator must be nonnegative. An upward pull reduces normal force; a downward pull, represented by α<0, increases it. The horizontal constant-velocity formula is the special case α=0, a=0.
For the original 2kg, μk​=0.30 model, an upward pull at α=30∘ maintaining constant velocity needs
F=cosα+μk​sinαμk​mg​≃5.7873N,N≃16.7064N.
(7)
Dividing this gauge reading by mg would give about 0.2953, rather than 0.30: the gauge reads the full inclined force, and the pull has changed the normal force.
Even with a horizontal pull, an accelerating measurement needs the ma correction: μk​=(F−ma)/(mg). In the same model, a horizontal 7.88N pull produces a=1m/s2 while the block slides right; subtracting ma=2N recovers the 5.88N kinetic friction. Dividing the uncorrected reading by 19.6N instead gives about 0.4020. Measure acceleration in an inertial frame, along the sliding direction, over the same interval as the gauge reading. These corrections follow from the force balance, independently of whether the resulting coefficient of kinetic friction is constant across runs.
