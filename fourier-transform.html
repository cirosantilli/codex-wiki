Inverse Fourier transform (F−1g(x)=2π1​∫R​g(k)eikxdk)

With the Fourier transform convention f​(k)=∫R​f(x)e−ikxdx, the inverse Fourier transform is the displayed linear operator. The integral is defined directly for an integrable function g. The Fourier inversion theorem ensures F−1f​=f under its hypotheses, in particular for Schwartz functions, and for integrable f and f​ at continuity points of f. The operation also extends to tempered distributions; the normalization and sign depend on the chosen Fourier transform convention.

Spectral leakage

Words: 62

A spectrally sharp component therefore contributes to nearby frequencies rather than remaining a single ideal line.
