Low-degree evaluation rank on a uniform layer (rankFp​​M≤(sn​))

 ... Area of mathematics Combinatorics Extremal set theory Set family Uniform set family Uniform layer of the Boolean cube
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
Let 0≤s≤k≤n and form the integer matrix with rows indexed by S⊆[n], ∣S∣≤s, columns indexed by k-subsets T, and entry MS,T​=1S⊆T​. Its matrix rank over any finite field Fp​ is at most (sn​). Over the rational numbers, the row of a j-subset S is (s−jk−j​)−1 times the sum of the rows of all s-subsets containing S. Thus the rows of degree exactly s span, giving the bound over the rational numbers. Every larger square minor therefore has integer determinant zero, and remains zero modulo p. The matrix rank cannot increase under this reduction. This argument avoids dividing by an integer that might vanish in the finite field, and gives the sharp uniform-layer step in the Frankl-Wilson theorem.
