Rotation matrix

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
A rotation matrix is an orthogonal matrix with determinant 1.
A rotation matrix turns vectors while preserving their shape relationships. For column coordinate vectors in an orthonormal basis, its defining conditions are
RTR=I,detR=1,
(108)
where RT is the transpose and I the identity matrix. In the usual Cartesian basis of the plane, an active counterclockwise planar rotation through an angle θ uses
Rθ​=(cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​),v′=Rθ​v.
(109)
Here the vector moves and the basis stays fixed: Rπ/2​(1,0)T=(0,1)T. Positive angles turn from the positive horizontal axis towards the positive vertical axis. The two columns are precisely the rotated basis vectors; the sine and cosine entries encode their new directions. Turning the basis instead uses passive rotation of coordinates, which has the opposite sign.
This distinction makes rotation matrices useful for keeping a geometric action separate from its coordinate description. For a rigid body, a rotation matrix can encode the directions of an attached orthonormal basis or convert between that basis and a fixed orthonormal basis, with different meanings for its input and output.[ref]
The orthogonal matrix condition preserves every dot product:
(Ru)⋅(Rv)=(Ru)T(Rv)=uTRTRv=u⋅v.
(110)
Consequently Euclidean norms and angles between nonzero vectors are preserved, as is the Euclidean norm of the difference between any two endpoints. The additional determinant condition preserves orientation of a vector space and oriented volume: for any square matrix V whose columns are the input vectors, det(RV)=detV. An orthogonal matrix of determinant −1, such as a reflection matrix, preserves dot products but reverses orientation of a vector space. The matrix inverse is especially simple: R−1=RT.[ref]
