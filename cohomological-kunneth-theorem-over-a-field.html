Cohomological Künneth theorem over a field (H∗(X×F;k)≅H∗(X;k)⊗k​H∗(F;k))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Cohomology Künneth theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 96
If k is a field and H∗(F;k) has finite total dimension, the cohomology cross product gives the displayed graded-ring isomorphism for arbitrary spaces. Over a field, every chain complex splits into its homology with zero differential and contractible summands. The Eilenberg–Zilber theorem thus replaces the fiber chain complex by a bounded finite-dimensional complex; dualizing its tensor product with C∗​(X;k) gives a finite direct sum of shifted cochain complexes of X. Its cohomology is the displayed tensor product. Finiteness prevents the infinite-product obstruction that can occur for two infinite discrete spaces. The standard product formula and its finiteness hypothesis are discussed in pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch3.pdf, Theorem 3.15.
