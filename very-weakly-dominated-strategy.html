Very weakly dominated strategy (ui​(si​,s−i​)≤ui​(σi​,s−i​) for every s−i​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Game theory Dominated strategy
OurBigBook.com Words: 137
A pure strategy is very weakly dominated by another strategy when its payoff is no greater against every opposing pure strategy profile, with no strict advantage required anywhere. The definition permits payoff-equivalent strategies and even domination by the original strategy itself. A weakly dominated strategy instead has a strict inequality for at least one opposing strategy profile.
In a binary game, suppose σi​ very weakly dominates si​ and assigns positive probability to a different pure action. At every opposing strategy profile where si​ pays one, all actions in the support of a mixed strategy must also pay one. Where si​ pays zero, their payoffs are automatically no smaller. Therefore every distinct supported action very weakly dominates si​. If the mixed domination is weak with a strict improvement somewhere, choose a supported action paying one at a profile where si​ pays zero; that action also supplies a strict improvement. The requirement of nontrivial support is indispensable for the very-weak version.
