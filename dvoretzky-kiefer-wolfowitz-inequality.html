For independent and identically distributed real random variables with cumulative distribution function F, the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality gives, for every n≥1 and ε>0,
P(supx∈R​∣Fn​(x)−F(x)∣>ε)≤2e−2nε2.
(26)
The supremum is the Kolmogorov distance: a single event controls all thresholds simultaneously. The bound holds for discrete, continuous, and mixed probability distributions.[ref]
For one fixed threshold, Hoeffding's inequality gives the same expression as an upper bound on P(∣Fn​(x)−F(x)∣>ε). That fixed-threshold result alone does not justify moving the supremum inside the probability. The DKW inequality is the stronger statement needed for a simultaneous empirical distribution confidence band.
