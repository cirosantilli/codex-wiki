Finite rectangular barrier matching

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Potential barrier Quantum tunnelling
OurBigBook.com Words: 616 Articles: 4
Take a nonrelativistic particle of constant mass m with energy E>0, and choose the same zero of potential energy on both sides of a rectangular potential barrier:
V(x)={V0​,0,​0<x<a,x<0 or x>a,​V0​>0,a>0.
(72)
The equality of the exterior potential energies matters: it gives the same exterior wavenumber k=2mE​/ℏ and makes T=∣t∣2. Here ℏ=h/(2π) is the reduced Planck constant. This model has a finite jump in potential energy, no delta potential, and no absorbing term.
For 0<E<V0​, the time-independent Schrödinger equation gives the spatial wavefunction
ψ(x)=⎩⎨⎧​eikx+re−ikx,Aeκx+Be−κx,teikx,​x<0,0<x<a,x>a,​κ=ℏ2m(V0​−E)​​.
(73)
The full stationary state is Ψ(x,t)=ψ(x)e−iEt/ℏ. We set the incident probability amplitude to one and impose no incoming wave from the right; this scattering wavefunction is not a normalizable wavefunction. The tunnelling decay constant κ describes exponential decay, rather than an oscillatory wavenumber in the barrier. Both exponential functions are needed on the finite interval: the growing term is not excluded by a condition at infinity.
The wavefunction and its derivative are continuous at each face, giving four linear matching conditions:
1+rAeκa+Be−κa​=A+B,=teika,​ik(1−r)κ(Aeκa−Be−κa)​=κ(A−B),=ikteika.​
(74)
Their solution supplies the transmission amplitude
t=cosh(κa)+i2kκκ2−k2​sinh(κa)e−ika​.
(75)
A short derivation is to use the two right-face equations to express Aeκa and Be−κa in terms of t, then substitute into the left-face equations. Taking the squared modulus gives the rectangular barrier transmission probability.
