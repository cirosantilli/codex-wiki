Kernel of a group homomorphism (kerφ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group homomorphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 110 Articles: 1
The kernel of a group homomorphism is the group version of a kernel. For φ:G→H, it is
kerφ={g∈G:φ(g)=eH​}.
(53)
It is a normal subgroup of the domain: for n in the group kernel, φ(gng−1)=φ(g)eH​φ(g)−1=eH​. The group kernel describes inputs whose effect disappears after mapping. For reduction modulo three from Z/6Z, it is {0,3}; for the sign of a permutation on S3​, it is A3​. A group homomorphism is injective exactly when its group kernel contains only the identity element.
