Two-factor tensor rank (rank⊗​(t)=rankC(t))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Tensor product
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
The two-factor tensor rank is the smallest number of pure tensors whose sum equals t, with zero allowed as the empty sum. For two finite-dimensional vector spaces over the same field, it equals the matrix rank of the tensor-product coefficient matrix. A sum of r outer products has column space contained in the span of their r first factors, so its matrix rank is at most r. Conversely, choose a basis u1​,…,ur​ for the column space, express each column in that basis, and collect the coefficients into row vectors wkT​. This yields C=∑k=1r​uk​wkT​, hence a sum of r pure tensors.
The pure tensor (2,1)⊗(−1,3) therefore has two-factor tensor rank one, whereas e1​⊗f1​+e2​⊗f2​ has two-factor tensor rank two. Both have two factors: the number of factors and the minimum number of product summands are different counts. Over the real numbers, the singular value decomposition supplies a useful decomposition C=∑k​σk​uk​vkT​, with one nonzero term per matrix rank. The two-factor rank equality does not extend to three or more factors by merely choosing one matrix layout.
