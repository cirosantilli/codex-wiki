Harmonic analysis

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 19
Harmonic analysis decomposes functions into elementary oscillations, generalizing Fourier series and the Fourier transform. On a locally compact Abelian group the elementary oscillations are continuous unitary characters, and Haar measure supplies translation-invariant integration.

Carleson measure (μ(S(I))≤Cm(I))

Words: 197 Articles: 2
Harmonic analysis OurBigBook.com
A positive Borel measure near a domain boundary is a Carleson measure when its mass in boundary neighborhoods is bounded by a constant times their boundary measure. On the unit disc, this becomes the displayed bound for every Carleson box. The equivalent hyperbolic geodesic cap version follows from the geodesic-cap and Carleson-box comparison. The Carleson embedding theorem on the disk explains why this geometric growth condition controls integration of analytic Hardy spaces.

Carleson embedding theorem on the disk (H1(D)↪L1(μ)⟺μ is Carleson)

A positive Borel measure on the unit disc satisfies ∫∣f∣dμ≤N∥f∥1​ for every H1 space on the unit disk function exactly when it is a Carleson measure, with comparable optimal constants. The normalized squared Cauchy kernel tests necessity. For sufficiency, the boundary shadow of every point with ∣f(z)∣>t lies inside a component arc of {Nf>t}. The geodesic-cap and Carleson-box comparison bounds the measure of each shadow region by its component length, giving μ{∣f∣>t}≤C∥μ∥Carl​m{Nf>t}. The layer cake representation and H1 non-tangential maximal inequality on the disk give the embedding. The broader kernel and covering mechanism is discussed in Terence Tao's harmonic-analysis notes, Exercise 2 after Lemma 4.1; the analytic endpoint here uses the fractional-power argument just given.

Carleson box (S(I)={rξ:ξ∈I, 1−m(I)<r<1})

Words: 50
Carleson measure OurBigBook.com
A boundary neighborhood over an arc I of the unit circle, with radial depth equal to its normalized angular length m(I). Changing that depth by any fixed positive factor gives an equivalent Carleson measure condition. Large arcs and central mass can be handled separately by finitely many fixed neighborhoods covering the unit disc.

Non-tangential maximal function (Fa∗​(x)=supy>0, ∣x−x′∣≤ay​∣F(x′,y)∣)

Words: 118 Articles: 1
Harmonic analysis OurBigBook.com
This supremum function takes a supremum over a cone approaching the boundary point x; the positive number a is its aperture. For an analytic upper-half-plane H1 function, it belongs to L1. To prove this without first assuming boundary absolute continuity, shift the function upwards, apply subharmonic Poisson comparison to its square root, and use the strong L2 bound for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function. Fatou lemma removes the shift.

Hardy space (Hp)

Harmonic analysis OurBigBook.com
For 0<p<∞, an analytic Hardy space controls the integral means of a holomorphic function as it approaches a domain boundary. On the disk, Hp requires sup0<r<1​∫∣F(reiθ)∣pdθ<∞; on the upper half-plane it requires supy>0​∫R​∣f(x+iy)∣pdx<∞. For p=∞ use a supremum norm. These analytic spaces differ from the harmonic Hardy space of the upper half-space; the Hardy space of the circle is the boundary Hilbert-space realization of the disk H2 space.

Fatou theorem for bounded holomorphic functions

Words: 84
H-infinity space OurBigBook.com
A bounded holomorphic function on the unit disc has finite nontangential limits almost everywhere on its boundary. To outline a proof, bounded real and imaginary harmonic parts have L∞ boundary representations: apply the Banach-Alaoglu theorem to their circular traces and pass to the limit in the Poisson integral on the unit disk. Almost every boundary point is a Lebesgue point. The fact that a Poisson integral converges nontangentially at Lebesgue points gives the desired limits. Their L∞ norm equals the interior supremum norm, and the Poisson integral recovers the original function. Exceptional points can lack even radial limits; exp(iLog(1−z)) is a bounded example at the boundary point one.

F. and M. Riesz theorem

Words: 90
Hardy space OurBigBook.com
If a finite complex Borel measure μ on the circle has Fourier coefficients μ​(n)=0 for all n<0, then it is absolutely continuous with respect to arc length. The reflected Fourier convention gives the equivalent theorem with positive coefficients vanishing. The Poisson integral on the unit disk is analytic because of the one-sided Fourier spectrum. Subharmonic comparison for its square root and an L2 maximal estimate produce an integrable radial maximum. This maximum dominates the weak-star boundary measure, forcing absolute continuity of measures. The same proof on the real line says that a finite measure with Fourier transform of a finite measure supported in [0,∞) has a Lebesgue density.

Calderón–Zygmund decomposition (f=g+∑Q​bQ​)

Words: 82
Harmonic analysis OurBigBook.com
For f∈L1(Rd) and a positive threshold λ, take maximal dyadic cubes with mean of ∣f∣ exceeding λ. They are disjoint, have total volume at most ∥f∥1​/λ, and their means are at most 2dλ. Replace f inside each selected cube by its mean to obtain g; write bQ​=(f−fQ​)1Q​. Then ∥g∥∞​≤2dλ, ∥g∥1​≤∥f∥1​, ∫bQ​=0, and ∑Q​∥bQ​∥1​≤2∥f∥1​. The outside bound follows from the Lebesgue differentiation theorem. This separates a bounded part from localized cancellation errors.

Positive-definite function (∑i,j​zi​zj​​p(xi​xj−1​)≥0)

A complex-valued function on a group is positive-definite when the displayed inequality holds for every finite choice of points and coefficients. This includes positive semidefinite, rather than necessarily strictly positive definite, Gram matrices. In particular p(e)≥0 and ∣p(x)∣≤p(e). On an Abelian group, every convolution square u∗u∗ with u∈Cc​(G) is continuous and positive-definite: its quadratic form is the integral of ∣∑i​zi​u(t+xi​)∣2.

Cyclic unitary representation of a positive-definite function (p(x)=⟨Ux​v,v⟩)

Words: 64
Positive-definite function OurBigBook.com
For an Abelian group, on finite formal sums of group points, define ⟨[x],[y]⟩=p(xy−1), with the first variable linear. Quotient by the null space of this positive semidefinite form and complete to a Hilbert space. Left translation is unitary, and v=[e] generates a dense span of translated vectors. For continuous p, the equality ∥Ux​[y]−[y]∥2=2(p(e)−Rep(x)) gives strong continuity. This realizes p as a matrix coefficient without assuming its integral representation.

A positive-definite function r on the discrete group Z is the sequence of Fourier coefficients of a unique finite positive Borel measure on the circle, with total mass r(0). Wiener Fourier coefficient lemma (limN→∞​N−1∑n<N​∣σ(n)∣2=∑t​σ({t})2)
Words: 68
Bochner–Herglotz theorem OurBigBook.com
For a finite positive Borel measure on the circle, averaged squared Fourier coefficients tend to the sum of squares of its atomic masses. Express each squared coefficient as a double integral and average the circle character. The averaged character is bounded by one, tends to one on the diagonal and to zero elsewhere, so the dominated convergence theorem gives the diagonal mass. In particular an atomless measure has vanishing averaged absolute coefficients by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Reversing p,q and using uniqueness gives the displayed identity. It makes the local quotients μp​/p​ consistent wherever p​>0.
