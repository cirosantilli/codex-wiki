Bivector (w∈Λ2V)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Exterior algebra
OurBigBook.com Words: 367 Articles: 1
A bivector is an element of the second exterior power Λ2V. A simple bivector is one exterior product u∧v; in real Euclidean space, it records the oriented area generated by those two vectors. A general bivector is a sum of such elements and need not describe just one plane. Covariant counterparts are differential two-forms, which take two vectors as inputs rather than belonging to Λ2V.
In R3, choose
u=(1,2,0),v=(0,1,3).
(15)
Expanding by bilinearity gives
B=u∧v=e1​∧e2​+3e1​∧e3​+6e2​∧e3​.
(16)
The three coefficients are signed areas of the projections onto the xy, xz, and yz coordinate planes. They are three components of one bivector, not the three coordinates of either input vector.
With the usual dot product and a fixed orientation of a vector space, the cross product represents this area element by the normal vector (6,−3,1). The order of the components matters: e1​∧e2​ corresponds to e3​, while e1​∧e3​ corresponds to −e2​. The Gram determinant checks the full area:
∥u∧v∥2=∥u∥2∥v∥2−(u⋅v)2=5⋅10−22=46.
(17)
Here the norm on bivectors is the one induced by the Euclidean inner product. The exterior product exists without that metric and in arbitrary dimension; the familiar cross product description relies on the additional three-dimensional geometry.
