Comb tooth index ambiguity

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser Optical frequency comb Optical frequency measurement with a comb
OurBigBook.com Words: 234
One beat magnitude does not tell us the integer n. In the example, every proposed optical frequency n(250MHz)+50MHz produces the same signed beat with its corresponding tooth. A coarse independent optical measurement can select the integer: once the beat sign is known, an error bound below fr​/2 suffices if the candidate is selected by nearest distance.
There is also a useful two-spacing check. Keep the unknown laser and f0​ fixed, track the same tooth continuously, and increase fr​ by a stipulated 5Hz. Its frequency rises by 10MHz, so the signed beat falls from 30 to 20MHz. Hence
n=−Δfr​Δb​=510,000,000​=2,000,000.
(21)
This inference fails if the unknown drifts appreciably, the offset changes unnoticed, the beat sign is lost, or the tracker jumps to another tooth. The assumed tooth motion stays well below half the spacing in this example. If f0​ also changes by a measured amount, use n=−(Δb+Δf0​)/Δfr​.
For a fixed verified tooth number, the first-order frequency error is δfL​=nδfr​+δf0​+δb. Errors in these readings can be correlated because they share a reference. A small beat uncertainty therefore does not imply the same absolute optical accuracy: the reference's calibration and the factor n remain in the error budget. A wrong tooth number is a separate discrete error, not a small correction. The beauty of the comb is that two controlled parameters organize millions of optical reference points, while the unknown's position is read electronically.
