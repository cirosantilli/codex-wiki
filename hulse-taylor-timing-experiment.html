Hulse-Taylor timing experiment

 ... Stellar astrophysics Stellar evolution Neutron star Pulsar Binary pulsar PSR B1913+16
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
The surprise was a pulsar whose apparent period would not stay fixed. Russell Alan Hulse's Arecibo search used a multichannel receiver, pulsar dedispersion and computer searches over period and pulse width. Follow-up at higher time resolution retained the changing period, excluding inadequate sampling as its explanation. The observed variation exceeded the expected terrestrial Doppler shift; a predicted reversal from decreasing to increasing period appeared on 16 September 1974.[ref]
The 1975 analysis fitted an eccentric Kepler orbit to Doppler shifts inferred from about 200 five-minute period measurements over ten days. That discovery fit was not yet the later precision arrival-time experiment.[ref] An orbit explains both signs of the apparent period change without requiring the stellar rotation to alternate between speeding up and slowing down.
