Lozi fixed points and the alternating-sign two-cycle (x1​=(a+1−b)/(a2+(1−b)2),x2​=(1−b−a)/(a2+(1−b)2))

The positive fixed point exists when b<1+a and equals (1,b)/(1+a−b); the negative one exists when b>1−a and equals (1,b)/(1−a−b). Equality on either boundary does not give a branch, because its fixed-point equation becomes 0=1. For a genuine two-cycle and a2=(1−b)2, subtracting its scalar equations rules out equal signs of the two first coordinates. Solving with x1​>0>x2​ gives the displayed coordinates and second coordinates y1​=bx2​, y2​=bx1​. Their signs require exactly a>∣1−b∣. The cycle is unique up to interchanging its two points. Degenerate boundary parameter lines can support additional period-two families and are outside this statement.
