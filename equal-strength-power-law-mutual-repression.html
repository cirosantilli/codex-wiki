Equal-strength power-law mutual repression (f(y)=λ(1+y)−n,g(x)=λ(1+x)−m)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Gene regulatory network Mutual repression
OurBigBook.com Words: 125
For positive m,n, this mutual repression system can display bistability for some λ exactly when both m>1 and n>1. If either exponent is at most one, it has a unique stable equilibrium for every λ. To prove the less obvious case m≤1<n, put a=x/(1+x) and b=y/(1+y) at equilibrium. Equality of the two expressions for λ gives a≤b(1−b)n−1. The loop gain is then bounded by mnb2(1−b)n−1, whose maximum is 4mn(n−1)n−1/(n+1)n+1<1. Swap the two components for the other case. If both exponents exceed one, sufficiently large λ yields two stable equilibria with (x,y)∼(λ,λ1−m) and (x,y)∼(λ1−n,λ). The weaker condition mn≤1 is sufficient to exclude multistability, but does not exhaust all exclusions when the same production prefactor is imposed.
