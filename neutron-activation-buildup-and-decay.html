Neutron activation buildup and decay (N˙=R−λN)

 ... Subatomic particle Hadron Baryon Nucleon Neutron Neutron activation
OurBigBook.com Words: 336
Let N be the expected value of the number of radioactive product atomic nuclei, initially zero, produced at constant rate R. Let their nuclear decay constant be λ>0. Assume one product species, negligible target depletion and negligible capture by the product. During irradiation, production adds Rdt while radioactive decay removes λNdt in expectation. Multiplying the resulting ordinary differential equation by eλt gives
dtd​(eλtN)=Reλt,N(t)=λR​(1−e−λt),A(t)=R(1−e−λt).
(27)
The expected radioactive activity A=λN tends to the production rate R. This saturation is a balance of continuing production and radioactive decay, not exhaustion of target atomic nuclei.
If irradiation ends at ti​, let s denote elapsed time afterward. Setting the production term to zero gives
Nafter​(s)=λR​(1−e−λti​)e−λs,Aafter​(s)=R(1−e−λti​)e−λs.
(28)
For detection efficiency ε, constant background count rate b, delay d and counting-window duration Δ, integrating the expected count rate gives
E[C]=bΔ+ελR​(1−e−λti​)e−λd(1−e−λΔ).
(29)
This assumes at most one recorded pulse per product transformation and negligible counter losses. Detector response, branching fractions and background must be calibrated independently. Subtracting an estimated background does not remove its counting uncertainty. Several products give a sum of these terms with distinct nuclear decay constants; a single exponential decay fit can then mislead.
For a wholly stipulated example, choose R=120min−1, half-life 2min and ti​=4min. Then λ=log2/(2min), A(ti​)=90min−1 and N(ti​)≃259.69. Choose ε=0.25, b=2min−1 and d=Δ=2min. The result is
E[C]−bΔ=log2/20.25×120​×43​×21​×21​≃16.23,E[C]≃20.23.
(30)
The background contributes four expected counts. Multiplying the initial net count rate in that window by its duration would give 22.5 net counts, overestimating the integrated 16.23 because the product decays appreciably during counting.
