Radiation pressure of a directed beam (p⊥​=(a+2R)Icos2θ/c)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Pressure Radiation pressure
OurBigBook.com Words: 376
For normal incidence in vacuum, a uniform beam delivers energy IAΔt to an illuminated surface area A during time Δt. A perfect absorber receives momentum IAΔt/c, so force divided by surface area gives pabs​=I/c. A perfect stationary mirror sends the photons back with reversed momentum, so momentum conservation gives twice the normal transfer, prefl​=2I/c. These are time-averaged pressures; the microscopic arrivals need not be uniform.
For a stipulated model light intensity I=1000Wm−2 and the exact SI speed of light c=299792458ms−1,
pabs​=3.335640952×10−6Pa≃3.3356μPa,prefl​=6.671281904×10−6Pa≃6.6713μPa.
(2)
These are computed predictions, not measured data or a universal sunlight value. If the beam fully covers a normally oriented surface area A=0.010m2, the intercepted radiant flux is Φ=IA=10W and the absorber's force is Φ/c≃33.356nN. Using the total source power would be wrong if only part of its beam reaches the receiver. The absorption prediction assumes complete photon absorption, while the reflection prediction assumes complete specular reflection; transmission, scattering of light and directed thermal photon emission require their own outgoing momentum accounting.
Measure the angle of incidence θ between the incoming path and the surface's normal line, with 0≤θ<π/2. There are two separate cosine factors. A fixed actual surface area A intercepts only its projected area Acosθ, so Φ=IAcosθ. Each incoming photon supplies normal momentum (Eγ​/c)cosθ on absorption, or twice that on specular reflection. Thus, for radiometric absorptance a and reflected power fraction R,
F⊥​=(a+2R)cΦ​cosθ,p⊥​=AF⊥​​=(a+2R)cI​cos2θ.
(3)
This model assumes transmitted light continues straight in the same medium and carries away its original momentum. It omits thermal recoil and assumes a stationary receiver. At θ=60∘, fixed beam light intensity therefore gives one quarter of the normal-incidence pressure. For fixed intercepted radiant flux Φ, however, the normal force has only one cosine factor. Equivalently, if the given quantity is receiving-surface irradiance Einc​=Icosθ, then p⊥​=(a+2R)Einc​cosθ/c. The distinction matters when interpreting an experiment's angle scan. [ref]
Absorption also transfers a tangential momentum component at oblique incidence. It contributes a tangential force, rather than normal pressure. Ideal specular reflection preserves the tangential momentum component, so it transfers only normal momentum.
