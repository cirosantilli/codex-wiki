Hardy's integral inequality (∥Hf∥p​≤p−1p​∥f∥p​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Hardy's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 556 Articles: 3
Let 1<p<∞ and let f be a real or complex measurable function in Lp space Lp((0,∞),dx), with Lebesgue measure dx. The Hardy operator, denoted here by H, takes the running average
Hf(x)=x1​∫0x​f(t)dt,x>0.
(1)
Hölder's inequality ensures ∫0x​∣f(t)∣dt≤x1−1/p∥f∥p​<∞, so every such average exists. Hardy's integral inequality states
∫0∞​∣Hf(x)∣pdx≤(p−1p​)p∫0∞​∣f(x)∣pdx.
(2)
The real signed and complex extensions of the nonnegative statement follow from the triangle inequality ∣Hf∣≤H∣f∣. Changing f on a null set changes neither average nor Lp norm.[ref]
Averaging over an expanding interval spreads a localized input across the whole positive half-line. Hardy's integral inequality bounds the total Lp norm of that spread. The Hardy operator is consequently a bounded linear operator on this Lp space. The factor p/(p−1) bounds its operator norm; its pth power is the constant in the displayed integral bound. These are different constants: at p=2, the L2 norm bound is two and the squared L2 norm bound is four.
