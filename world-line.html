World line

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 219 Articles: 2
A world line is an object's history as a curve of spacetime events. In an inertial frame, a particle at rest has constant spatial coordinates, so its world line is vertical in a spacetime diagram with ct upward. A particle at constant velocity v along one axis has x=x0​+vt, a straight sloping world line. The coordinate representation changes with the inertial frame, while the physical history is the same.
A massive observer follows a future-directed timelike curve. Along a differentiable world line with velocity v(t), its clock accumulates
dτ=dt1−c2∣v(t)∣2​​,Δτ=∫t0​t1​​1−c2∣v(t)∣2​​dt.

The Minkowski metric therefore measures clock time along the curve, not just the difference between two coordinate readings. In the straight O-to-A example, v=0.75c for 4s, giving 41−0.752​s=7​s. If the observer changes velocity along the path, use the integral rather than assigning the straight-segment formula to the whole path.
