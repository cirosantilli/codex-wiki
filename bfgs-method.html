BFGS method

OurBigBook.com Words: 265 Articles: 1
A quasi-Newton method that maintains a symmetric curvature approximation for differentiable optimization. For a positive-definite symmetric matrix H approximating inverse curvature, step s, gradient change y and ρ=1/(yTs)>0, its inverse update is
H+​=(I−ρsyT)H(I−ρysT)+ρssT.
(65)
Multiplying by y gives H+​y=s, the inverse form of the Quasi-Newton secant equation. The update is a symmetric matrix. For any nonzero v,
vTH+​v=[(I−ρysT)v]TH[(I−ρysT)v]+ρ(sTv)2>0.
(66)
Both terms are nonnegative, and both can vanish only if sTv=0 and then v=0. Thus positive definiteness is preserved when yTs>0, making −H+​∇f a descent direction away from a stationary point. Full updates use quadratic storage in the dimension; the method does not require explicit Hessian matrix evaluations. Superlinear convergence requires additional local smoothness and line search assumptions.[ref]
