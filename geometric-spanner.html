Geometric spanner (dG​(u,v)≤t∥u−v∥)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 90
A geometric spanner is a weighted graph on points in Euclidean space, with edge weights equal to their Euclidean distances, in which every pair has a graph path of length at most t times its direct distance. Its stretch factor is the largest ratio dG​(u,v)/∥u−v∥, where dG​ is shortest-path distance. A square's four perimeter edges, without diagonals, give stretch factor 2​: adjacent points have ratio one, and diagonal pairs have route length 2 instead of 2​. The aim is to retain short routes with fewer edges than a complete graph. [ref].
