Lambertian reflectance

Words: 42
Optics OurBigBook.com
Lambertian reflectance is the ideal diffuse-reflection model with direction-independent outgoing radiance, the frequency-integrated specific intensity. A surface with hemispherical reflectance ρ receiving irradiance F has outgoing radiance ρF/π. The observed radiant intensity still acquires the projected-area cosine factor described by Lambert's cosine law.

Lambert's cosine law (I(θ)=I(0)cosθ)

Words: 44
Optics OurBigBook.com
For an ideal diffuse emitting or reflecting planar surface, the radiant intensity in a direction at angle θ to its normal is proportional to cosθ. The factor comes from projected area. Lambertian reflectance instead describes the corresponding direction-independent radiance; intensity and radiance are distinct quantities.

Optical lattice (V(x)=sER​sin2(πx/d))

Words: 157 Articles: 2
Optics OurBigBook.com
An optical lattice is a periodic optical trap produced by interfering light fields. Its periodic potential gives atomic Bloch states and energy bands. For the displayed one-dimensional lattice, the recoil energy is ER​=ℏ2π2/(2md2) and s measures the depth in recoil units.

Condensate compressibility in an optical lattice (κ−1=n(∂μ/∂n)s​)

Words: 92
Optical lattice OurBigBook.com
Here n is average number density and μ the ground-state chemical potential. This commonly used condensate response has units of inverse energy; thermodynamic isothermal compressibility is instead κ/n. In a weakly interacting repulsive condensate, the normalized one-cell ground state fs​, with average ∣fs​∣2=1, gives μ=E0​(s)+UnI(s)+⋯, I(s)=d−1∫0d​∣fs​∣4dx. Localization raises I and reduces κ at fixed n,U,d. In deep harmonic wells I∝s1/4, hence κ∝s−1/4. Recoil energy is the kinetic energy gained by an initially stationary atom absorbing a photon of momentum pR​. In a standing-wave optical lattice of spacing d, the photon wavenumber is π/d, giving ER​=ℏ2π2/(2md2).

Spatial light modulator (SLM)

Words: 46
Optics OurBigBook.com
A spatial light modulator applies an electronically controlled spatially varying optical phase, amplitude, or polarization change. In a 4f pulse shaper, frequency maps to transverse position, allowing the pixels to implement a calibrated spectral mask. A phase-only modulator generally needs additional polarization optics or another modulation stage for independent amplitude control.

Spectral pulse shaping (Eout​(ω)=M(ω)Ein​(ω))

Words: 113 Articles: 1
Optics OurBigBook.com
Spectral pulse shaping changes an optical pulse by applying a complex amplitude-and-phase mask M(ω) to its frequency components. The output in time is the inverse Fourier transform of the masked spectrum. A passive device cannot amplify unavailable spectral components; finite bandwidth and spectral resolution constrain the realizable waveforms and time window.

4f pulse shaper (Grating–lens–mask–lens–grating)

Words: 67
Spectral pulse shaping OurBigBook.com
A 4f pulse shaper uses a diffraction grating to disperse an input pulse, a lens to focus its frequencies at different positions in a shared Fourier plane, a spectral mask or spatial light modulator there to modify them, and a second lens and grating to recombine them. In the symmetric idealization the distances are f,f,f,f, with the lenses separated by 2f. Shaping the spatially resolved spectrum implements spectral pulse shaping rather than a direct time-domain mask.

Optical tweezers

Words: 515 Articles: 8
Optics OurBigBook.com
An optical trap uses the forces from a focused light field to confine or manipulate a small particle. In an overdamped one-dimensional model, a translating localized potential exerts F(x−vT​t) and balances linear drag.

Optical momentum efficiency (F=QP/c)

Words: 79
Optical tweezers OurBigBook.com
An optical momentum efficiency Q expresses the net axial momentum transferred to a bead relative to incident power P divided by vacuum light speed. It absorbs medium-index factors and the incident/outgoing ray geometry. Refractive-index contrast alone does not determine Q for a transparent bead illuminated by a focused beam. The estimate Q∼m−1 is a weak-contrast scale, not a universal force formula. An effective reflector with power reflectance R instead gives Q≃2R in the simple vacuum-momentum convention.

Passage through a translating optical trap (Δt,Δx)

Words: 406 Articles: 6
Optical tweezers OurBigBook.com
For a force profile supported on an interval of width w, set u=F/ζ. If a trap of speed v overtakes an overdamped particle with v−u>0, then Δt=∫dy/(v−u) and Δx=vΔt−w. These expressions follow by changing to the trap-frame coordinate, whose velocity is u−v. Smooth zeroes of v−u can prevent finite passage.

High-speed passage expansion of a localized trap (Δx=∑m≥1​vT−m​∫(F/ζ)mdy)

Words: 72
Passage through a translating optical trap OurBigBook.com
If the maximum force-induced speed is smaller in magnitude than vT​, expand the passage denominator as a uniformly convergent geometric series. For a localized conservative well with equal outside potential levels, the first force moment vanishes and displacement starts at order vT−2​. An odd force has no cubic moment, giving the next correction at order vT−4​. Compact force support alone does not guarantee a vanishing first moment or positive displacement.

Sufficient escape condition for a translating trap (vT​>sup(F/ζ))

Words: 64
Passage through a translating optical trap OurBigBook.com
In the trap frame, a particle under linear drag has relative speed F(y)/ζ−vT​. A uniform strictly negative relative speed guarantees finite passage through a compact support. For a bounded continuous force, vT​>supF/ζ supplies that condition. For ordinary smooth forces, an encountered zero of the relative speed is an equilibrium or a passage bottleneck, not a finite crossing. Negative forces do not impede forward trap passage.

Repeated kicks from a circular optical trap (fp​=fT​Δx/(L+Δx))

Words: 167 Articles: 2
Passage through a translating optical trap OurBigBook.com
For a passing optical trap on a circle of circumference L with nonoverlapping support, a particle that is stationary between encounters receives displacement Δx in each encounter. The trap revolution frequency fT​ and particle revolution frequency obey Lfp​=Δx(fT​−fp​). This corrects the naive independent-kick estimate when the particle motion changes the encounter rate.
Large-speed approximation for circular-trap kicks (fp​≃fT​Δx/(2πR))
Words: 67
Repeated kicks from a circular optical trap OurBigBook.com
The exact separated-kick response is fp​=fT​Δx/(C+Δx) for circumference C=2πR. Its relative correction to the independent-kick estimate is controlled by Δx/C. For a localized well with force-induced speed bounded by ϵvT​, Δx/C≤(w/C)ϵ2/(1−ϵ). Thus high speed is sufficient when the support width is below one circumference. The local straight-force approximation also requires a narrow trap on the curved track.
Triangular optical-trap response (fp​/fc​)
Words: 49
Repeated kicks from a circular optical trap OurBigBook.com
A triangular localized potential energy has forces +F behind and −F ahead of the trap centre. With vc​=F/ζ=Lfc​, α=2X0​/L and β=fT​/fc​, its circular response is fp​/fc​=αβ/(β2−1+α) for β>1. A trapped particle has fp​/fc​=β for 0≤β≤1, interpreting the discontinuous force at the cusp by a sticking or rounded-well limit.

Forward displacement from a translating localized potential (vΔx=∫x˙2dt)

Words: 46
Passage through a translating optical trap OurBigBook.com
For a translating potential energy profile with equal values at the two ends and linear drag force −ζx˙, a finite passing trajectory satisfies vΔx=∫x˙2dt≥0. Equivalently, since ∫Fdy=0, Δx=∫u2/[v(v−u)]dy. Compact support of the force alone is insufficient. At high speed the displacement is v−2∫(F/ζ)2dy+O(v−3).

Diffraction order (m)

Words: 19
Diffraction grating OurBigBook.com
A diffraction order labels a constructive interference maximum by the integer number of wavelengths in the path difference between adjacent grating elements.

Grating equation (mλ=d(sinα+sinβ))

Words: 175 Articles: 3
Diffraction grating OurBigBook.com
For a reflection diffraction grating with both ray directions measured on the same side of its normal, adjacent grooves have path difference d(sinα+sinβ). Constructive interference requires this to be an integer multiple mλ. Signed-angle conventions can turn the sum into a difference; the physical path difference is unchanged.
Grating dispersion (dβ/dλ=m/(dcosβ))
Words: 33
Grating equation OurBigBook.com
At fixed incidence angle, differentiating the grating equation gives dβ/dλ=m/(dcosβ). A camera with focal length f therefore has local focal-plane dispersion q=fm/(dcosβ) near its optical axis. With coordinate X=ftan(β−β0​), the full derivative is q=fsec2(β−β0​)m/(dcosβ).
Anamorphic magnification of a grating (∣dβ/dα∣=cosα/cosβ)
Words: 96 Articles: 1
Grating equation OurBigBook.com
At fixed wavelength, the grating equation gives dβ/dα=−cosα/cosβ. Thus an incident angular width is magnified by cosα/cosβ in the dispersion direction. Including the ratio of camera and collimator focal lengths gives the projected slit width. The ESO B&C operating manual, Appendix A explicitly includes this anamorphic factor.
Slit-limited resolving power of a grating (R=mλfcoll​/(xdcosα))
Words: 53
Anamorphic magnification of a grating OurBigBook.com
A slit of width x at the focus of a collimator subtends x/fcoll​. Its projected width is xfcam​cosα/(fcoll​cosβ). Dividing this width by the grating dispersion gives Δλ=xdcosα/(mfcoll​). Thus the spectral resolving power is R=mλfcoll​/(xdcosα). Omitting the anamorphic factor gives a denominator cosβ instead; these agree in the Littrow configuration.

The free spectral range is the wavelength or frequency spacing between adjacent orders or resonances of an optical instrument. It identifies when different orders can overlap.

Free spectral range of an echelle grating

Words: 121 Articles: 1
Free spectral range OurBigBook.com
At fixed incident and central outgoing angles, the grating equation gives mλm​=K=d(sinα+sinβ0​). Adjacent central wavelengths are separated by
λm​−λm+1​=m(m+1)K​∼mλm​​.
(2)
This is the usual adjacent-order free spectral range. A detector width of approximately qΔλFSR​, where q is the grating dispersion, covers one such interval.
Adjacent-order spacing versus nearest-axis echelle interval (Scentres​=K/[m(m+1)])
Words: 79
Free spectral range of an echelle grating OurBigBook.com
At a fixed camera-axis direction, the grating equation gives mλm​=K=d(sinα+sinβ0​), so adjacent central wavelengths differ by K/[m(m+1)]. This differs from the exact wavelength cell where an order is nearest the optical camera axis. For β0​=0 and x=ftanβ, equal distances of orders m and m+1 mean opposite diffraction angles; adding their diffraction grating equations gives λ=K/(m+1/2). The full order-m cell is bounded by K/(m+1/2) and K/(m−1/2), hence has width K/(m2−1/4) for m≥2 and accessible neighbouring orders. Both widths approach K/m2 at high order.

A photodetector bias frame is an exposure of effectively zero integration time used to estimate the electronic offset and read noise. For independent identical bias reads, the variance of their difference is twice the single-read variance; subtracting an estimated mean bias removes the offset but adds the uncertainty of that estimate.

Flat-field correction compensates stable multiplicative variations in photodetector response using calibrated illumination. Identically illuminated exposure ratios cancel the stable response in their mean, but their photon shot noise remains signal dependent.

An analogue-to-digital unit is a unit of a digitized photodetector output after electronic conversion. Its relation to collected Electrons is set by the detector conversion gain; an additive electronic offset must be removed before interpreting a count as a light signal.

Detector conversion gain (g)

Words: 161 Articles: 2

Detector conversion gain is the number of collected Electrons per analogue-to-digital unit. With independent Poisson distribution photoelectron counts, the shot-noise variance in count units is the mean count divided by the gain. Detected-photon and Electron conventions agree only when each detected photon contributes one Electron.
Gain estimation from flat-field ratios
Words: 126 Articles: 1

Let independent exposure sums A,B have mean N ADU at each detector pixel, with pure Poisson distribution noise and fixed gain g. The delta method gives A/B≃1+(A−N)/N−(B−N)/N, so Var(A/B)≃2/(gN) and g≃2/(NE2). For nonuniform detector pixel means Ni​, an unweighted spatial ratio variance is instead (2/g)⟨1/Ni​⟩. This high-count approximation requires linear unsaturated data, bias subtraction and negligible read noise.
Bias of pooled flat-field ratio gain (gnaive​/g=1/(⟨N⟩⟨1/N⟩))
Words: 73

For stable positive detector pixel means Ni​ and uniform detector conversion gain g, high-count independent Poisson distribution noise gives Var(Ai​/Bi​)≃2/(gNi​). Thus substituting a global mean in the equal-signal formula yields gnaive​=g/[⟨Ni​⟩⟨1/Ni​⟩]≤g, by the Jensen inequality. Equality holds for uniform means. The flat-field correction pattern cancels from the ratio mean but not from the signal-dependent noise. Local signal bins, or a variance fit after normalization of differences by the square root of their local mean, avoid this leading bias.

Read noise is uncertainty added by measuring detector charge or voltage. It is often expressed as an equivalent number of input electrons per pixel per read.

A charge-coupled device stores photoelectrons in potential wells and transfers the charge through clocked stages to an output amplifier. The transfer differs from independently addressed pixels in a hybrid infrared detector.

An electron-multiplying CCD adds a high-field multiplication register before the output amplifier. Impact ionization generates a large mean gain, reducing input-referred read noise but adding multiplication excess noise.
Multiplication excess noise (F)

Random multiplication increases the noise relative to deterministic gain. If the output from one input electron has mean g and variance g2, a Poisson distribution of input electrons with mean μ produces output variance 2g2μ, by the law of total variance. An energetic charge carrier creates an additional electron–hole pair. In a multiplication register this is stochastic, so the output charge fluctuates even when the input electron number is fixed.

Signal-to-noise ratio in photon counting

Words: 56
Photodetector OurBigBook.com
For source counts S and independent background counts B with Poisson distributions, the ideal counting standard deviation is S+B​, so the signal-to-noise ratio is S/S+B​. Detector noise and uncertainty in the estimated background add further variance. A larger sky aperture raises B, while better throughput raises S; both affect the faintness limit of a spectrograph.
