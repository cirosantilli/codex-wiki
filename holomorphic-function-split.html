Holomorphic function (f∈O(U))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 78
A complex-valued function f:U→C is holomorphic on an open set U when it has a complex derivative at every point of U. Requiring a derivative throughout a neighbourhood is much stronger than complex differentiability at a point. It forces locally convergent Taylor series and derivatives of every order, as follows from the Cauchy integral formula and Cauchy derivative formula.
To see the restriction in real coordinates, write z=x+iy and f(z)=u(x,y)+iv(x,y). The real and imaginary increment quotients must agree, giving the Cauchy-Riemann equations
ux​=vy​,uy​=−vx​,f′=ux​+ivx​.
(1)
Conversely, if u,v have continuous partial derivatives on U and satisfy the Cauchy-Riemann equations there, then f is a holomorphic function. The equations at a lone point, without a differentiability hypothesis for the real coordinate map, are insufficient. Their role is to force the Jacobian matrix to have the special form
Df=(ux​vx​​uy​vy​​)=(pq​−qp​),f′=p+iq.
(2)
Such a matrix represents multiplication by a complex number; it has only two free real entries. [ref]
For f(z)=z2, u=x2−y2 and v=2xy, so ux​=vy​=2x and uy​=−vx​=−2y. Directly,
f(z0​+h)−f(z0​)=2z0​h+h2,f′(z0​)=2z0​.
(3)
At z0​=1+i, multiplication by 2+2i scales lengths by its modulus 22​ and rotates by its argument π/4. A horizontal increment h=0.01 gives the exact displacement 0.0201+0.02i; a vertical increment h=0.01i gives −0.0201+0.02i. The first-order predictions 0.02+0.02i and −0.02+0.02i are perpendicular, and the errors have modulus 10−4.
At every nonzero z0​, this local rotation and scaling makes z2 a local conformal map. At zero, the first-order map vanishes: reiθ becomes r2e2iθ, so the argument doubles and the origin is a critical point. Globally z and −z have the same square. Local preservation of angles therefore coexists with global folding, a useful warning when using holomorphic functions as coordinate changes.
