Convolution theorem for Laplace transforms (L(f∗g)=LfLg)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Integral transform Laplace transform
OurBigBook.com Words: 80
For functions f,g on [0,∞) with e−σtf(t) and e−σtg(t) Lebesgue integrable functions, their unilateral convolution, defined almost everywhere if necessary, satisfies
L{f∗g}(s)=F(s)G(s),(f∗g)(t)=∫0t​f(τ)g(t−τ)dτ,Res=σ.
(29)
Taking a causal extension by zero of both functions gives the existing whole-line convolution definition. Absolute convergence and Fubini's theorem justify the product rule: put t=τ+u in the double integral, so e−st=e−sτe−su. For piecewise continuous functions of exponential order a and b, the hypotheses hold for Res>max(a,b). The Laplace transform article works out the concrete example h(t)=e−2t and the Heaviside step function input, whose output is (1−e−2t)/2. [ref].
