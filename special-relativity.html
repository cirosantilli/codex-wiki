Einstein's principle of relativity

Words: 40
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
The laws of physics have the same form in all inertial frames; uniform absolute motion cannot be identified by experiments confined to an inertial laboratory. The invariant vacuum speed of light is a separate postulate. Together they motivate the Lorentz transformations of special relativity.

Relativistic particle (uμuμ​=c2)

Words: 38
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
A relativistic massive particle is described by a timelike worldline with proper-time four-velocity uμ. In signature (+−−−) its normalization is uμuμ​=c2 and its momentum is pμ=muμ. The relativistic charged-particle action adds electromagnetic coupling, whose variation gives the Lorentz force.

Nonrelativistic particle

Words: 30
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
A massive particle is nonrelativistic when its speed is small compared with the speed of light. Its energy expansion is E=mc2+p2/(2m)+O(p4/(m3c2)). The leading momentum-dependent term is the classical kinetic energy.

Hyperbolic motion (x2−c2t2=c2t02​)

Words: 32
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
Hyperbolic motion in special relativity is rectilinear motion with constant proper acceleration. A representative positive-x world line is x=ct0​cosh(τ/t0​), t=t0​sinh(τ/t0​), with proper acceleration c/t0​. This describes a hyperbola in spacetime, not a hyperbolic Kepler orbit.

Proper length (ℓ)

Words: 32
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
Proper length is spatial length measured using the induced metric on a specified spacelike slice. For a moving extended object, specify the simultaneity convention; local rest lengths need not coincide with a coordinate length.

Spacetime

Words: 208 Articles: 4
Special relativity OurBigBook.com
Spacetime combines spatial position and time into one geometric setting for physical events. In special relativity it is modeled by Minkowski spacetime; in general relativity its geometry is dynamical.

Spacetime extension

Words: 159 Articles: 2
Spacetime OurBigBook.com
A spacetime extension is an isometric embedding of a spacetime as a proper open subset of a larger connected spacetime with a nondegenerate Lorentzian metric. The allowed differentiability class must be specified: analytic, smooth and low-regularity extensions are different questions. Divergent curvature can obstruct a regular extension even when an incomplete geodesic has finite affine length.

Maximal analytic extension

Words: 57
Spacetime extension OurBigBook.com
A maximal analytic extension of an analytic spacetime is an analytic spacetime extension admitting no further proper analytic extension as a regular spacetime of the same dimension. It is different from a maximal Cauchy development, which can end at a smooth Cauchy horizon. Additional global choices or quotients can affect uniqueness. The simply connected extension of positive-mass Schwarzschild spacetime through both horizons is the Kruskal spacetime; its curvature singularities remain boundaries.

Extendible spacetime

Words: 48
Spacetime extension OurBigBook.com
An extendible spacetime admits a spacetime extension in the specified regularity class. A coordinate horizon need not obstruct an extension: the exterior Schwarzschild chart extends through its horizon in regular null coordinates. Maximality refers to extensions of the given spacetime, not to the mere absence of a singularity in one coordinate chart.

Null vector

Special relativity OurBigBook.com
A nonzero spacetime vector k is null when its Minkowski norm vanishes, kaka​=0. Null tangent vectors describe the local directions of light rays.
Special relativity describes inertial frames using Lorentz transformations and Minkowski spacetime.

Sum of future-pointing null vectors ((A+B)2=−2ab(1−n⋅m))

Words: 64
Null vector OurBigBook.com
In Minkowski spacetime with metric signature (−,+,+,+), write two nonzero future-pointing null vectors as A=a(1,n) and B=b(1,m), where a,b>0 and n,m are unit vectors. Their Lorentzian inner product gives
(A+B)⋅(A+B)=−2ab(1−n⋅m)≤0.
(2)
Thus their sum is a future-pointing causal vector. It is a null vector exactly when the spatial directions coincide, and a timelike vector otherwise. The positivity of the time components is essential: adding past- and future-pointing null vectors can instead produce a spacelike vector.

Null condition (g(k,k)=0)

The null condition for a nonzero vector k in a Lorentzian metric is g(k,k)=0. Applied to the tangent of a curve it makes the curve a null curve. Nullity alone does not imply the geodesic equation; an additional zero or tangent-parallel covariant acceleration is required.

The twin paradox compares proper time along the different world lines of a travelling observer and a stay-at-home observer who reunite. Reciprocal inertial time dilation produces an apparent contradiction only if the traveller's change of inertial frame is ignored.

Local Lorentz transformation (e′Aμ​=ΛAB​(x)eBμ​)

Words: 66
Lorentz transformation OurBigBook.com
An orthonormal coframe in spacetime can be rotated by a position-dependent Lorentz matrix without changing the spacetime metric tensor. Its spin connection transforms as ω′=ΛωΛ−1−dΛΛ−1. A spinor field transforms through a chosen spin lift ψ′=S(Λ)ψ, not through the vector matrix Λ. With S−1γAS=ΛAB​γB, an infinitesimal parameter gives S=1+41​αAB​γAB+O(α2), where γAB=21​[γA,γB]. Consistent global transition lifts require a spin structure; a 2π spatial rotation lifts to minus the identity on spinors.

Relativity of simultaneity (Δt′=−γvΔx/c2(Δt=0))

Words: 177 Articles: 2
Lorentz transformation OurBigBook.com
Spatially separated simultaneous events in one inertial frame are generally not simultaneous in a relatively moving frame. The Lorentz transformation explicitly mixes position into time. This is essential when comparing distances between moving light pulses: one must compare their positions at the same time in the measuring frame.

Rod containment and simultaneity

Words: 74
Relativity of simultaneity OurBigBook.com
A moving rod can have both ends inside a shorter shed on a single constant-time slice of the shed frame. Lorentz transformation sends the two simultaneous endpoint events to different times in the rod frame; it does not imply simultaneous containment there. Entrance and first collision can be spacelike separated and occur in reversed time order. After impact, length changes must propagate through the rod causally; a perfectly rigid instantaneous stopping model is not relativistically admissible.

Photon separation under a collinear Lorentz boost (d′=d(c+v)/(c−v)​)

Words: 59
Relativity of simultaneity OurBigBook.com
For two photons emitted simultaneously a distance d apart in one frame and moving in the same positive direction, the world-line intercept x−ct transforms to γ(1+v/c)(x−ct). Their equal-time separation in the boosted frame is therefore the displayed constant d′. It is different from length contraction of the emitters, because the emission events are not simultaneous after the boost.

Lorentz invariance

Words: 36
Lorentz covariance OurBigBook.com
A physical quantity is Lorentz invariant when its value is unchanged under Lorentz transformations. Tensor equations can be Lorentz covariant while their components transform. Fixed-vector gauge choices need not make covariance manifest, although physical gauge-invariant predictions retain the symmetry.

Lorentz boost (t′=γ(t−vx),x′=γ(x−vt))

Words: 223 Articles: 4
Lorentz transformation OurBigBook.com
A Lorentz boost relates inertial frames moving at a constant relative velocity without an additional spatial rotation. In units with light speed one, the displayed transformation preserves t2−x2, with Lorentz factor γ=(1−v2)−1/2. Applying it to a static classical field-theory soliton produces a uniformly moving solution in a Lorentz-invariant theory.

Wigner rotation

Words: 101 Articles: 1
Lorentz boost OurBigBook.com
Two noncollinear Lorentz boosts produce a Lorentz transformation with both a boost and a rotation. In the SL(2,C) lift, the product of two positive Hermitian boost matrices generally ceases to be Hermitian. Its polar decomposition of an invertible complex matrix separates a positive Hermitian boost factor and a unitary rotation factor. The rotation depends on both boosts and their order, and is called the Wigner rotation.
Noncollinear boost obstruction from Pauli products (A′A−(A′A)†=2is′s(n′×n)⋅σ)
Words: 48
Wigner rotation OurBigBook.com
For boost matrices A=cI+sn⋅σ and A′=c′I+s′n′⋅σ, the Pauli matrix multiplication law gives the displayed anti-Hermitian part. If both rapidities are nonzero and the axes are noncollinear, it cannot vanish, while either lift of a pure boost is Hermitian. Thus an additional Wigner rotation is necessary. A zero boost is a degenerate exception.

Rapidity (θ=artanhv)

Words: 78 Articles: 1
Lorentz boost OurBigBook.com
For one spatial dimension and c=1, a massive particle has v=tanhθ, E=Mcoshθ and p=Msinhθ. A Lorentz boost adds a constant to all rapidities, so a rapidity difference is invariant. Rapidity is useful for factorized scattering, where two-body S-matrices depend only on that difference.
Iterated collinear boosts with equal rapidity (un​=ctanh(nα))
Words: 36
Rapidity OurBigBook.com
The relativistic velocity-addition formula becomes addition of rapidities for collinear Lorentz boosts. Starting from rest, applying the same positive rest-frame velocity increment ctanhα repeatedly gives un​=ctanh(nα). For increment c/2, α=21​log3 and un​=c(3n−1)/(3n+1). Every finite sequence remains below the speed of light.

The orthochronous Lorentz group consists of Lorentz transformations preserving the future sheet of the unit timelike hyperboloid. Equivalently, A00​>0. This is an open subgroup of O(1,3) and hence a full-dimensional submanifold.

Proper orthochronous Lorentz group

The proper orthochronous Lorentz group is the identity component of the Lorentz group. Its elements have determinant one and preserve the future light cone.
Every proper orthochronous Lorentz transformation is an exponential (L=expΩ)
Words: 95
Proper orthochronous Lorentz group OurBigBook.com
Use the Lorentz spinor double cover SL(2,C)→SO+(1,3). For a lift with distinct eigenvalues λ,λ−1, diagonalize and choose logarithms z,−z; this gives a traceless matrix logarithm. For a repeated eigenvalue, choose the sign of the lift so it is I+N, where N2=0, and use log(I+N)=N. The two lifts have the same Lorentz image. Exponentiating the differential of the covering map therefore expresses every proper orthochronous transformation as the exponential of a real element of the Lie algebra of the Lorentz group. This argument does not assert that every element of SL(2,C) itself has a traceless logarithm.
Lorentz spinor double cover (SL(2,C)⟶SO+(1,3))

Finite-dimensional unitary representations of the massless little group have trivial translations (N1​=N2​=0)
Words: 74
Massless particle little group OurBigBook.com
In a finite-dimensional unitary representation of ISO(2), its two commuting Hermitian translation generators have a finite joint spectrum. Conjugating by every planar rotation rotates that spectrum. A finite set invariant under all rotations contains only the origin, so both generators vanish. An irreducible representation is then a one-dimensional rotation character, giving a helicity state. Nontrivial translations instead require the infinite-dimensional continuous-spin representation. The same argument applies to the physical double cover of this little group.
