p-adic integer (Zp​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory P-adic valuation
OurBigBook.com Words: 899 Articles: 8
The p-adic integers retain arithmetic information modulo arbitrarily high powers of one prime number p. They form the subring
Zp​={x∈Qp​:∣x∣p​≤1}​
(41)
of the P-adic numbers. Their p-adic absolute value bounds the allowed denominators: 1/3 belongs to Z2​, while 1/2 does not. The name therefore describes a local arithmetic ring, not just the ordinary integers.[ref]
A concrete description gives an element by a compatible sequence of congruence classes:
Zp​=lim​n≥1​Z/pnZ,rn+1​≡rn​(modpn).
(42)
The inverse limit condition says that gaining more precision must preserve the digits already determined. Each rn​ is a residue modulo pn; choosing its least nonnegative integer representative gives 0≤rn​<pn. The ordinary integer 13 gives residues 1,1,5,13 modulo 2,4,8,16.
The useful feature is that one exact object organizes an unlimited family of finite modular arithmetic calculations. Addition and multiplication are performed modulo each pn, and the results remain compatible. A p-adic digit expansion makes this concrete:
x=∑j≥0​bj​pj,bj​∈{0,1,…,p−1},rn​=∑j=0n−1​bj​pj.
(43)
The expansion is unique and converges in the p-adic metric; its higher powers have smaller p-adic absolute value. The digit agreement and p-adic distance criterion tells exactly which finite digits a precision bound preserves.[ref]
