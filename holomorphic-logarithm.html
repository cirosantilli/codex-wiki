Holomorphic logarithm (eg=f,g′=f′/f)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 299
A holomorphic logarithm of a nowhere-zero holomorphic function f on a domain U is a holomorphic function g such that eg(z)=f(z) throughout U. Differentiating with the chain rule gives g′=f′/f. For f(z)=z, a holomorphic logarithm is a branch of the complex logarithm, and its derivative is 1/z.
The principal logarithm is holomorphic on the slit complex plane D=C∖(−∞,0], with
Logz=log∣z∣+iArgz,−π<Argz<π,(Logz)′=z1​.
(15)
Here Arg is the chosen complex argument, and ImLogz∈(−π,π). One may conventionally assign a principal value on the negative real axis, but that axis is excluded from this holomorphic domain. [ref] For example, 1+i=2​eiπ/4 gives Log(1+i)=21​log2+iπ/4 and (Log)′(1+i)=(1−i)/2; all its other complex logarithms differ by 2πik, k∈Z.
There is no holomorphic logarithm of z on the punctured complex plane C∖{0}. Such a function would be a holomorphic primitive of 1/z, so its contour integral around every closed path would vanish. On the counterclockwise unit circle, however,
∮∣z∣=1​zdz​=∫02π​eitieit​dt=2πi=0.
(16)
This is the period obstruction to a holomorphic antiderivative, and also exactly the logarithm change in the monodromy diagram.
Every nowhere-zero holomorphic function f on a simply connected domain U does admit a holomorphic logarithm. Choose a∈U and b∈C with eb=f(a), and define
g(z)=b+∫az​f(ζ)f′(ζ)​dζ.
(17)
The Cauchy integral theorem makes this contour integral independent of the path in U. Then g′=f′/f and (fe−g)′=e−g(f′−fg′)=0; its value at a is 1, proving eg=f. Any two choices on a connected domain differ by a constant 2πik, since their difference is continuous and takes values in the discrete set 2πiZ. [ref]
For a computed example, take f(z)=1+z on the unit disc, with a=b=0. Expanding f′/f=1/(1+z) as a geometric series and integrating gives
g(z)=∑n=1∞​n(−1)n−1zn​,g(1/2)=log(3/2),eg(1/2)=3/2.
(18)
The construction also produces holomorphic square roots: h=eg/2 satisfies h2=f, so here h(1/2)=3/2​. More generally, eαg gives a consistent branch of a multivalued function for complex exponentiation by α. A holomorphic logarithm converts multiplicative expressions into additive ones while making the domain restrictions explicit.
