Quadratic median-of-three selection family (C(n)=⌊n2/4⌋)

 Home Computer science Algorithm Selection algorithm Quickselect Median-of-three pivot selection
OurBigBook.com Words: 224
A distinct-input counterexample for Quickselect that chooses the first/middle/last median, reserves it at the last slot, scans lower values to the front, and finally swaps the pivot into place. Let An​ be the following permutation of 0,…,n−1. For n<4, take increasing order. For n≥4, let B=An−2​+2 componentwise and m=⌊n/2⌋, then set
An​(0)=1,An​(m)=0,An​(n−1)=B(m−2),An​(1)=B(n−3),
(1)
and put An​(j)=B(j−2) at every other index 2≤j≤n−2. These assignments use every entry of B once and add zero and one, so they really define a permutation.
The sampled values are 1,0,B(m−2), with the last at least two, so the pivot is one. After the initial pivot swap, the only lower value is the zero at index m. Moving it to the front places the displaced B(m−2) at its intended tail position. The final pivot swap moves B(n−3) from index one to the last position. Every other tail entry already has its intended position. Thus the complete partitioned result is exactly [0,1] followed by B.
Selecting the maximum consequently repeats the same construction on n−2 entries. If C(n) counts only pivot comparisons made by the partition scan, then C(n)=n−1+C(n−2), with C(1)=0 and C(2)=1. Hence C(n)=⌊n2/4⌋ and total runtime is Θ(n2). For example, A6​=[1,4,3,0,2,5]. This disproves a linear worst-case claim even when every value is distinct.
