Hypersurface orthogonality (n∧dn=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Distribution (differential geometry) Integrable distribution Frobenius theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 115 Articles: 1
A nowhere-zero differential one-form n is hypersurface orthogonal if locally n=Fdt with F=0. Its kernel consists of tangent vectors to the level hypersurfaces of t. Frobenius theorem makes this equivalent to n∧dn=0, where d is the exterior derivative. With a torsion-free Levi-Civita connection, this is n[α​∇β​nγ]​=0. For a timelike unit normal, projecting the derivative on both indices gives zero antisymmetric part, which explains why hypersurface orthogonality implies symmetric extrinsic curvature.
