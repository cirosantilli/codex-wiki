Put T=βℏ>0, the interval of imaginary time, and assume m,ω>0. For a variation x0​+ϵη with η(0)=η(T)=0, the first variation of the Euclidean action is
δS=m∫0T​(x˙0​η˙​+ω2x0​η)dτ=m[x˙0​η]0T​+m∫0T​(−x¨0​+ω2x0​)ηdτ.
(1)
The boundary term vanishes. The fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations therefore gives the Euler-Lagrange equation
x¨0​=ω2x0​.​
(2)
Its sign differs from the real-time harmonic oscillator equation because the Euclidean action contains positive kinetic energy and positive potential energy. The general solution is Acosh(ωτ)+Bsinh(ωτ). Applying the two endpoint conditions gives A=x and B=−xtanh(ωT/2). Equivalently, the equal-endpoint Euclidean oscillator path is
x0​(τ)=xcosh(ωT/2)cosh[ω(τ−T/2)]​.​
(3)
This form displays reflection about the midpoint and satisfies both endpoints immediately. On this path,
dτd​(x0​x˙0​)=x˙02​+x0​x¨0​=x˙02​+ω2x02​.
(4)
Thus the Euclidean action reduces to a boundary term. Since x˙0​(T)=ωxtanh(ωT/2) and x˙0​(0)=−ωxtanh(ωT/2),
S[x0​]=2m​[x0​x˙0​]0T​=mωx2tanh(2βℏω​).​
(5)
The positive quadratic form m∫0T​(η˙​2+ω2η2)dτ is the second variation, so this stationary path is the unique minimum, rather than merely an arbitrary stationary point.

After the shift x(τ)=x0​(τ)+y(τ), the endpoint values of y are both zero. Expanding the Euclidean action gives
S[x0​+y]=S[x0​]+S[y]+m∫0T​(x˙0​y˙​+ω2x0​y)dτ.
(6)
By integration by parts, the cross term is
m[x˙0​y]0T​+m∫0T​(−x¨0​+ω2x0​)ydτ=0.
(7)
The first term vanishes by the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, and the second by the Euler-Lagrange equation. The translation has unit Jacobian determinant in the time-sliced configuration-space path integral, so Dx=Dy. Consequently classical-path factorization of a quadratic path integral is exact here:
⟨x∣e−βH∣x⟩=e−S[x0​]/ℏZ,Z=∫y(0)=y(T)=0​Dyexp[−2ℏm​∫0T​(y˙​2+ω2y2)dτ].​
(8)
All dependence on the common endpoint x lies in
e−S[x0​]/ℏ=exp[−ℏmωx2​tanh(2βℏω​)],Z is independent of x.​
(9)
The endpoint domain and the integrand defining Z are the same for every x. This Z is a zero-endpoint Gaussian path integral; the canonical partition function instead integrates the entire diagonal kernel over x. The final sentence of the printed part omits /ℏ from its exponential: the physical Boltzmann factor has the dimensionless exponent −S/ℏ.
An optional normalization check uses the Dirichlet oscillator fluctuation determinant. Its eigenvalues are m[(nπ/T)2+ω2], n≥1. Relative to the free-particle Gaussian path integral, the hyperbolic-sine infinite product gives
m/(2πℏT)​Z​=∏n=1∞​[1+n2π2ω2T2​]−1/2=sinh(ωT)ωT​​.
(10)
Thus Z=mω/[2πℏsinh(βℏω)]​. Integrating the resulting diagonal thermal kernel of a quantum harmonic oscillator by a Gaussian integral yields Tre−βH=[2sinh(βℏω/2)]−1, including the zero-point energy.

Because S[x0​]=0 at x=0, the factor Z cancels from the ratio. Write z=βℏω and q=mω/ℏ​x. The exact answer is
R=exp[−q2tanh(z/2)].​
(11)
At high temperature, z≪1. Expanding the hyperbolic tangent gives
logR=−q2(2z​−24z3​+O(z5)),R≃exp(−2βmω2x2​).​
(12)
The displayed leading expression is the ratio of classical Boltzmann factors for potential energy V(x)=mω2x2/2 and V(0)=0. Indeed the classical canonical ensemble gives
pcl​(x) ∝ ∫−∞∞​dpe−β[p2/(2m)+V(x)] ∝ e−βV(x).
(13)
The momentum Gaussian integral is independent of x and cancels in the ratio. This is the classical phase-space limit of the thermal kernel. Here β=1/(kB​T), with kB​ the Boltzmann constant; the associated classical position variance is 1/(βmω2). At fixed x, R ultimately tends to one as temperature tends to infinity, while its width grows.
At low temperature, z≫1. Now tanh(z/2)=1−2e−z+O(e−2z), so at fixed q
R=e−q2[1+2q2e−z+O(e−2z)],R≃exp(−ℏmωx2​).​
(14)
Unlike the classical high-temperature expression, this limiting width remains nonzero at zero temperature, reflecting the ground state of the quantum harmonic oscillator.

Insert the complete orthonormal basis of energy eigenstates on either side of the Boltzmann factor. The spectral theorem gives
e−βH=∑n=0∞​e−βℏω(n+1/2)∣n⟩⟨n∣,⟨x∣e−βH∣x⟩=n=0∑∞​e−βℏω(n+1/2)∣φn​(x)∣2.​
(15)
At low temperature, the gap ℏω suppresses excited energy eigenstates. The harmonic oscillator eigenfunction of the ground state is
φ0​(x)=(πℏmω​)1/4e−mωx2/(2ℏ).
(16)
Therefore
⟨x∣e−βH∣x⟩∼e−βℏω/2∣φ0​(x)∣2,R⟶∣φ0​(0)∣2∣φ0​(x)∣2​=e−mωx2/ℏ.​
(17)
The common zero-point energy factor cancels. Dividing the kernel by its trace would give the position probability density of the canonical ensemble; at zero temperature this tends to ∣φ0​(x)∣2, whose variance is ℏ/(2mω). Thus zero temperature projects onto the ground state and retains its quantum spread, rather than concentrating at the minimum of the classical potential energy.
There is also a check on the first correction. With q=mω/ℏ​x, the first excited harmonic oscillator eigenfunction satisfies ∣φ1​(x)∣2=2q2∣φ0​(x)∣2 and φ1​(0)=0. Hence the spectral theorem gives R=e−q2[1+2q2e−z+O(e−2z)], in agreement with part (c).

Let V=L3 be the box volume. The number operator is the sum of the occupation numbers of all momentum eigenstates, including the zero mode:
N^=∫V​d3rψ†(r)ψ(r)=k∑​ψk†​ψk​.​
(18)
In the Fourier series for the contact interaction, the two creation operators supply phase e−i(k1​+k2​)⋅r and the two annihilation operators supply ei(k3​+k4​)⋅r. Fourier orthogonality with periodic boundary conditions gives
∫V​d3rei(k3​+k4​−k1​−k2​)⋅r=Vδk1​+k2​,k3​+k4​​.
(19)
The four normalization factors together contribute V−2, leaving the momentum-space contact-interaction Hamiltonian
Hint​=2Vu​k1​,k2​,k3​,k4​∑​δk1​+k2​,k3​+k4​​ψk1​†​ψk2​†​ψk3​​ψk4​​.​
(20)
The Kronecker delta enforces momentum conservation. An equivalent expression that resolves the momentum transfer is
Hint​=2Vu​∑k,p,q​ψk+q†​ψp−q†​ψp​ψk​.
(21)
Both are already in normal ordering; no extra commutator constant is introduced.

Taking the Hermitian adjoint gives φk​=ck​ψk​+sk​ψ−k†​. For nonzero wavevectors, the canonical commutation relations imply
[φk​,φq†​]​=ck​cq​[ψk​,ψq†​]+sk​sq​[ψ−k†​,ψ−q​]=(ck​cq​−sk​sq​)δk,q​.​
(22)
When the Kronecker delta is nonzero, k=q, so the hyperbolic Pythagorean identity ck2​−sk2​=1 gives
[φk​,φq†​]=δk,q​.​
(23)
For the two annihilation operators, the only potentially nonzero terms are
[φk​,φq​]​=ck​sq​[ψk​,ψ−q†​]+sk​cq​[ψ−k†​,ψq​]=(ck​sq​−sk​cq​)δk,−q​=0.​
(24)
Here q=−k implies equal magnitudes and hence equal c,s, because θk​ depends only on ∣k∣. Thus
[φk​,φq​]=0.​
(25)
The corresponding commutators of two creation operators vanish by the Hermitian adjoint. This verifies that the Bogoliubov transformation preserves the full bosonic canonical commutation relations.

Normalize the specified state by ⟨0∣0⟩=1 and write ⟨A⟩=⟨0∣A∣0⟩. Its zero mode is a coherent state, so any normally ordered product of zero-mode creation operators and annihilation operators can be evaluated using ψ0​↦N0​​ and ψ0†​↦N0​​. In particular ⟨(ψ0†​)2ψ02​⟩=N02​, rather than N0​(N0​−1), which would apply to a zero-mode number state.
Sort the momentum-space contact-interaction Hamiltonian by its number of zero-mode factors. Four such factors give uN02​/(2V). Three zero-mode factors and one nonzero momentum violate momentum conservation, so no such term survives. With two zero-mode factors there are three possibilities:
  • Two zero-mode creation operators leave the opposite-momentum pair ψk​ψ−k​.
  • Two zero-mode annihilation operators leave ψk†​ψ−k†​.
  • One zero-mode factor on each side leaves ψk†​ψk​. Choosing which of the two creation operators and which of the two annihilation operators are zero gives four placements.
The sums over k=0 include both members of each opposite-momentum pair, consistently with the overall factor 1/2. Terms with one zero-mode factor contain three nonzero-mode factors; their expected values vanish in the centered multimode squeezed vacuum. Indeed each ψ is linear in φ,φ†, and an odd product has zero vacuum expectation value. Terms with no zero-mode factors are independent of N0​ at fixed θk​. Therefore
⟨Hint​⟩=2VuN02​​+2VuN0​​k=0∑​[⟨ψk​ψ−k​⟩+⟨ψk†​ψ−k†​⟩+4⟨ψk†​ψk​⟩]+O(N00​).​
(26)
To evaluate these vacuum expectation values, use the inverse Bogoliubov transformation and its Hermitian adjoint:
ψk​=ck​φk​−sk​φ−k†​,ψk†​=ck​φk†​−sk​φ−k​.
(27)
Since φk​∣0⟩=0, the only nonzero two-factor vacuum expectation value is ⟨φk​φq†​⟩=δk,q​. The bosonic squeezed-vacuum contractions are consequently
⟨ψk†​ψq​⟩⟨ψk​ψq​⟩⟨ψk†​ψq†​⟩​=sk2​δk,q​,=−ck​sk​δk,−q​,=−ck​sk​δk,−q​.​​
(28)
In particular the three requested quantities are −sinhθk​coshθk​, −sinhθk​coshθk​ and sinh2θk​, respectively. The minus sign in the pair vacuum expectation values comes from the inverse Bogoliubov transformation and is essential to lowering the interaction energy. Substitution yields
⟨Hint​⟩=2VuN02​​+VuN0​​k=0∑​(2sk2​−ck​sk​)+O(N00​).​
(29)

The coherent state in the zero mode has mean occupation number N0​. The other occupation numbers follow from part (c), giving exactly
⟨N^⟩=N0​+D,D≡k=0∑​sinh2θk​.​
(30)
Impose the fixed mean particle number ⟨N^⟩=N by choosing
N0​=N−D.​
(31)
Here D is the quantum depletion of a Bose condensate. The approximation requires 0≤D≪N, so N0​ remains positive and macroscopically large. The specified coherent state and multimode squeezed vacuum are not exact eigenstates of the total number operator; N in this variational construction is their prescribed mean particle number.
Write P=∑k=0​sk​ck​. Substituting into the retained part of the interaction energy before truncating gives
⟨Hint​⟩​≃2Vu​(N−D)2+Vu​(N−D)(2D−P)=2VuN2​+VuN​(D−P)+Vu​(−23​D2+DP).​
(32)
The last two terms quadratic in D,P have no explicit large-N factor when the Bogoliubov transformation parameters are held fixed, and are discarded along with the nonzero-mode quartic contribution already omitted in part (c). This is the same truncation that underlies the number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian. Thus, defining the physical number density correctly as ρ=N/V=N/L3,
⟨Hint​⟩≃2uρ​N+uρk=0∑​(sinh2θk​−sinhθk​coshθk​).​
(33)
Replacing N0​ by N at the outset would give the wrong coefficient 2sinh2θk​: expanding the leading condensate interaction energy supplies the necessary subtraction.
The printed definition ρ=N/L is an error. In three dimensions the contact interaction coupling u has units of energy times volume, so uN/L does not even have units of energy. There is a direct counterexample with no depletion: when θk​=0, the specified coherent state has exactly ⟨Hint​⟩=uN2/(2L3). In fixed length and energy units, L=2, u=1 and N=100 give 625, whereas the printed uρN/2 with ρ=N/L gives 2500. The requested formula and the minimization in part (e) hold with ρ=N/L3.

Put ϵk​=ℏ2k2/(2m)>0 for k=0 and g=uρ, using ρ=N/L3. Since the zero mode has zero kinetic energy, the exact kinetic energy in this trial state is
⟨HKE​⟩=k=0∑​ϵk​sinh2θk​.​
(34)
Together with part (d), the fixed-mean-number Bogoliubov variational energy is
E[θ]≃2gN​+∑k=0​fk​(θk​),fk​(θ)=(ϵk​+g)sinh2θ−gsinhθcoshθ.
(35)
For repulsive contact interaction, u>0, each ϵk​+g>g>0. Using the double-angle hyperbolic-function identities,
fk​(θ)=2ϵk​+g​(cosh2θ−1)−2g​sinh2θ,
(36)
so
fk′​(θ)=(ϵk​+g)sinh2θ−gcosh2θ.
(37)
The stationary point therefore satisfies
tanh2θk​=ϵk​+gg​=uρ+ℏ2k2/(2m)uρ​.​
(38)
This is a minimum, not just a vanishing derivative. Define the positive Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion
Ek​=(ϵk​+g)2−g2​=ϵk​(ϵk​+2g)​.
(39)
At the displayed solution θk∗​, cosh2θk∗​=(ϵk​+g)/Ek​ and sinh2θk∗​=g/Ek​. Hence
fk​(θ)=2Ek​​cosh[2(θ−θk∗​)]−2ϵk​+g​,fk′′​(θk∗​)=2Ek​>0.
(40)
The hyperbolic cosine has its unique minimum at zero, proving global minimization of each retained term. Treating equal-magnitude wavevectors together simply multiplies the same function by their multiplicity. At a fixed ultraviolet cutoff, the resulting energy change from θ=0 is
Emin​−E[0]=21​k=0∑​(Ek​−ϵk​−g)<0(g>0).​
(41)
Indeed Ek​<ϵk​+g. More directly, for small positive θ,
fk​(θ)=−gθ+(ϵk​+g)θ2+O(θ3).
(42)
The pair vacuum expectation value lowers the interaction energy at first order in θ, whereas the nonzero occupation number and its kinetic energy cost start at second order. The two-mode squeezed vacuum admixes opposite-momentum pairs into the condensate, introducing spatial correlations that reduce the chance of two bosons coinciding. These correlations lower repulsive contact interaction enough to outweigh the added kinetic energy. A small quantum depletion of a Bose condensate can therefore lower the energy below the uncorrelated condensate value.
Two qualifications delimit this result. First, the paper does not state the sign of u. More generally a finite stable minimum exists only when ϵk​+g>∣g∣ for every retained nonzero mode; repulsion ensures this. For example, with g=−ϵk​ in some mode, fk​(θ)=ϵk​sinh(2θ)/2 tends to −∞ as θ→−∞, so an unconditional minimum claim for arbitrary u is false. Also the formula minimizes the truncated Bogoliubov theory energy, not the exact quartic Hamiltonian including its discarded terms, and it must remain in the regime of small quantum depletion of a Bose condensate.
Second, a three-dimensional unregulated contact interaction cannot give a finite absolute beyond-leading-order ground-state energy by this bare sum. For large k, Ek​−ϵk​−g=−g2/(2ϵk​)+O(ϵk−2​), whose three-dimensional sum has an ultraviolet divergence. A finite ultraviolet cutoff, or renormalization of a three-dimensional dilute-gas contact coupling matched to a physical scattering length, is needed. This does not alter the modewise minimization or the finite-cutoff lowering mechanism just derived.
