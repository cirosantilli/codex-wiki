The canonical anticommutation relations make (a†b†)2=0, so the proposed exponential function terminates exactly:
∣0⟩=A(∣0⟩+B∣11⟩).
(38)
Use the inverse Bogoliubov transformation c=ua−vb†, d=va†+ub, and the actions a∣11⟩=b†∣0⟩ and b∣11⟩=−a†∣0⟩. The two vacuum state conditions become
c∣0⟩=A(uB−v)b†∣0⟩=0,d∣0⟩=A(v−uB)a†∣0⟩=0.
(39)
Both therefore require B=v/u. Normalizing this quantum state gives 1=A2(1+B2). Choosing positive A, which fixes an irrelevant overall global phase, yields A=u because u2+v2=1. Thus the two-mode fermionic paired vacuum is
∣0⟩=u∣0⟩+va†b†∣0⟩,A=2EE+ε​​,B=E+εΔ​=E+εE−ε​​,E=ε2+Δ2​.​
(40)
The other sign for A describes the same physical quantum state. The positive relative coefficient is fixed by the paper's pairing order and the convention ∣11⟩=a†b†∣0⟩.
As a direct eigenvector check, Δv=(E−ε)u and Δu=(E+ε)v imply
(0−Δ​−Δ2ε​)(vu​)=(ε−E)(vu​).
(41)
It is therefore the unique ground state, as also follows from positive quasiparticle excitation energy E. It has definite even fermion parity but is not an eigenstate of the original total number operator: the empty-pair and occupied-pair probabilities are u2 and v2. In particular ⟨Na​⟩=⟨Nb​⟩=v2. The pair amplitude is ⟨ab⟩=−uv, consistently with ab∣11⟩=−∣0⟩.
For the limiting check Δ→0+ at fixed positive ε, A→1, B→0, so ∣0⟩→∣0⟩. For ε→0+ at fixed positive Δ, A→1/2​, B→1, giving equal amplitudes for the empty and occupied pair. These checks also fix which branch of the Bogoliubov transformation produces the ground state.
