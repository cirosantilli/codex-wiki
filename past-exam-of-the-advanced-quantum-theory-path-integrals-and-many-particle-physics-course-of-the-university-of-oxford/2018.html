With no pairing, the Hamiltonian is diagonal in the fermionic occupation numbers. The Pauli exclusion principle allows each momentum mode to be either empty or occupied once. Without a fixed fermion number constraint, occupying a mode lowers the energy precisely when its single-particle energy is negative. Therefore, with ∣0⟩ the Fock vacuum, a ground state is
∣GS⟩=p:ϵ(p)<0∏​cp†​∣0⟩,EGS​=p:ϵ(p)<0∑​ϵ(p).​
(1)
Choose a fixed ordering of the fermionic creation operators; changing it alters only the overall phase. Any zero-energy modes may be occupied or empty without changing the ground-state energy.
To identify the occupied modes, complete the square:
αϵ(p)​=(p2−2β​)2+μ−4β2​.
(2)
Here β is the dispersion parameter, not inverse temperature. If β2<4μ, every mode has positive energy and the unique ground state is the Fock vacuum, with EGS​=0. If β2=4μ, there are still no negative-energy modes, but p=±β/2​ have zero energy if they lie on the allowed momentum grid. Each such mode doubles the ground-state degeneracy.
For β2>4μ, define
p−2​=2β−β2−4μ​​,p+2​=2β+β2−4μ​​.
(3)
Both are positive, and the occupied Fermi sea consists of the two intervals p−​<∣p∣<p+​. With p=2πn/L, the requested finite-size ground-state energy is explicitly
EGS​=2αn∈Z>0​Lp−​/(2π)<n<Lp+​/(2π)​∑​[(L2πn​)4−β(L2πn​)2+μ].​
(4)
The factor two counts opposite momenta. Endpoints are excluded from this sum because they have zero energy; when an endpoint is allowed, its optional occupation adds ground-state degeneracy. A narrow negative-energy interval can contain no allowed finite-size momenta, in which case the sum is empty. The zero-momentum mode is always empty since ϵ(0)=αμ>0.

For real angles write up​=cosθp​ and vp​=sinθp​. For one independent pair p>0, the transformation and its conjugate read
cp​=up​αp​+ivp​α−p†​,c−p​=up​α−p​−ivp​αp†​.
(5)
Using the canonical anticommutation relations for the α modes gives
{cp​,cp†​}{c−p​,c−p†​}{cp​,c−p​}{cp​,c−p†​}​=up2​+vp2​=1,=up2​+vp2​=1,=−iup​vp​+ivp​up​=0,=0.​
(6)
The remaining anticommutators vanish for independent momentum pairs. The displayed two-by-two matrix is unitary and its inverse is obtained by changing i to −i.
When the same formula is indexed over both signs of momentum, its first row at −p must agree with the conjugate of its second row at p. Thus a consistent global convention requires
u−p​=up​,v−p​=−vp​,θ−p​=−θp​(mod2π).​
(7)
This is the odd-momentum fermionic Bogoliubov angle convention. If one checks only the first-row formulas, their cross anticommutator is isin(θp​+θ−p​); its vanishing gives the condition modulo π, with possible signs absorbable in the α modes. Requiring both rows to describe the same operators fixes the convention above. Merely checking a separate unitary matrix at each momentum misses this redundancy: choosing θp​=θ−p​=π/4 in both first rows would give {cp​,c−p​}=i.
At p=0, the annihilation operator and its conjugate belong to one mode. The full displayed formula is consistent only with v0​=0; choose θ0​=0 and α0​=c0​. This mode is not a two-mode pair.

Group the nonzero modes into independent pairs p>0. For one pair set ε=ϵ(p)=p2/(2m)+μ, and introduce the Nambu spinor Ψp​=(cp​,c−p†​)T. Normal ordering using c−p​c−p†​=1−c−p†​c−p​ gives
Hp​=Ψp†​(ε−iΔ​iΔ−ε​)Ψp​+ε.
(8)
This exactly contains both kinetic terms and the pairing terms once; there is no additional factor of one half because only p>0 is summed.
Insert Ψp​=Up​Ap​, where Ap​=(αp​,α−p†​)T and Up​ is the Bogoliubov transformation matrix. Direct matrix multiplication yields
Up†​hp​Up​=(εcos2θp​−Δsin2θp​−i(εsin2θp​+Δcos2θp​)​i(εsin2θp​+Δcos2θp​)−εcos2θp​+Δsin2θp​​).
(9)
Choose the diagonalizing branch
cos2θp​=Ep​ε​,sin2θp​=−Ep​Δ​,Ep​=ε2+Δ2​,p>0.​
(10)
Then the off-diagonal terms vanish and the upper diagonal entry is Ep​>0. Extend the angle to negative momenta as in part b; the sine relation just written applies to the positive representative of each pair. The BCS coherence factors can be chosen as
up​=2Ep​Ep​+ε​​,vp​=−2Ep​(Ep​+ε)​Δ​.
(11)
Here up​ denotes cosθp​, and vp​ denotes sinθp​; these expressions are nonsingular because μ>0 and m>0. The minus sign of vp​ for positive Δ is fixed by the pairing phase in this Hamiltonian.
A second use of the canonical anticommutation relations gives
Hp​=Ep​(αp†​αp​−α−p​α−p†​)+ε=Ep​(αp†​αp​+α−p†​α−p​)+ε−Ep​.
(12)
The zero-momentum term was never paired and is simply μα0†​α0​. Hence
H=p∑​E(p)αp†​αp​+C,C=p>0∑​[ϵ(p)−ϵ(p)2+Δ2​],​
(13)
with
E(0)=μ,E(p)=ϵ(p)2+Δ2​(p=0).​
(14)
The constant is the ground-state energy. At fixed L, its ultraviolet tail converges: ϵ(p)−E(p)=−Δ2/[ϵ(p)+E(p)]∼−mΔ2/p2 in one dimension. Replacing E(0) by μ2+Δ2​ would incorrectly pair a spinless mode with itself.

Since every quasiparticle energy is positive, the ground state is the vacuum of all the αp​ annihilation operators, including α0​=c0​. Its zero-momentum mode is therefore empty. For each positive momentum, the inverse Bogoliubov transformation gives
αp​=up​cp​−ivp​c−p†​,α−p​=up​c−p​+ivp​cp†​.
(15)
Both annihilate the normalized pair state (up​+ivp​cp†​c−p†​)∣0⟩. For example, applying αp​ produces the two cancelling terms iup​vp​c−p†​∣0⟩. Thus
∣Ω⟩=p>0∏​(up​+ivp​cp†​c−p†​)∣0⟩,c0​∣Ω⟩=0.​
(16)
The factors have even fermion parity and commute for disjoint momentum pairs. Each normalized factor is a superposition of zero and two fermions, whose pair-occupation probability is vp2​. Therefore
⟨Ω∣N∣Ω⟩=2p>0∑​vp2​=p>0∑​(1−Ep​ϵ(p)​).​
(17)
There is no zero-mode contribution. For fixed L, the sum converges since vp2​=O(p−4).
A normalized simple excited state is ∣k⟩=αk†​∣Ω⟩ for k>0. Its excitation energy is Ek​, and its total momentum is k: both terms in αk†​=uk​ck†​+ivk​c−k​ add momentum k. Acting on the k,−k pair gives
αk†​(uk​+ivk​ck†​c−k†​)∣0⟩=(uk2​+vk2​)ck†​∣0⟩=ck†​∣0⟩.
(18)
The affected pair now contains exactly one fermion instead of mean number 2vk2​; all other pairs are unchanged. Consequently
⟨k∣N∣k⟩=⟨N⟩Ω​+1−2vk2​=⟨N⟩Ω​+Ek​ϵ(k)​.​
(19)
This is the fermion number of a Bogoliubov excitation. Its change in mean fermion number lies between zero and one for μ>0, with the value one at Δ=0. The Bogoliubov quasiparticle coherently mixes adding a fermion and removing the opposite-momentum fermion; it is not an eigen-excitation of the original fermion number operator. Indeed,
[N,H]=2iΔ∑p>0​(cp†​c−p†​+c−p​cp​),
(20)
so fermion number is not conserved when Δ=0, while fermion parity is conserved. The generally noninteger change is an expectation value in a coherent paired background, rather than a fractional eigenvalue of the fermion number operator. In the weak-pairing limit at fixed k, it tends to one, as expected for an ordinary added fermion.

Let β=1/(kB​T) and use the two spin values +1,−1 as row and column labels. Define the spin-chain transfer matrix
Wst​=e−βE0​(s,t),s,t∈{+1,−1}.
(21)
It is a real symmetric matrix because E0​(s,t)=E0​(t,s); every entry is strictly positive for finite bond energies at T>0. The Boltzmann factor of a spin configuration factorizes into the product Wσ1​σ2​​⋯WσL​σ1​​. Summing the intermediate spin labels is exactly matrix multiplication, and the final closure imposes periodic boundary conditions. Thus
ZL​=σ1​,…,σL​∑​j=1∏L​Wσj​σj+1​​=TrWL.​
(22)
Writing W=(ab​bd​), its eigenvalues are
λ±​=2a+d±(a−d)2+4b2​​,ZL​=λ+L​+λ−L​.​
(23)
A positive-entry matrix need not have two positive eigenvalues; λ−​ can be negative. The Perron–Frobenius theorem ensures λ+​>∣λ−​∣ here. Therefore the Helmholtz free energy is FL​=−kB​TlnZL​, and its value per spin in the thermodynamic limit is −kB​Tlnλ+​.

For an open chain there are L−1 bonds, with both endpoint Ising spins independently summed. Retain the same spin-chain transfer matrix, but replace the trace by endpoint vectors. With 1=(1,1)T,
ZLopen​=1TWL−11.​
(24)
This follows directly by expanding the product of L−1 Boltzmann factors. If v±​ are orthonormal eigenvectors of the symmetric matrix W, the equivalent spectral decomposition is
ZLopen​=∑ν=±​(1Tvν​)2λνL−1​.
(25)
Fixed endpoint Ising spins replace the corresponding 1 by a basis vector; extra endpoint energies replace its entries by the corresponding Boltzmann factors. The leading bulk Helmholtz free energy per spin agrees with the periodic result, because the boundary changes only subextensive terms. In particular, L=1 gives Z1open​=1T1=2, as required for one free Ising spin.

Let M=L/2, so that the total number of Ising spins is 3M. Write K=βJ and group each triangle's two base spins and its apex into one cell. The Ising chain with alternating triangular decorations has a triangle bond followed by an undecorated connecting bond. Summing an apex Ising spin first gives a decorated-bond Boltzmann weight
Ast​=∑τ=±1​e−K[st+(s+t)τ]=2e−Kstcosh[K(s+t)].
(26)
The undecorated bridge has Boltzmann weight Bts′​=e−Kts′. In the basis +1,−1,
A=(eK+e−3K2eK​2eKeK+e−3K​),B=(e−KeK​eKe−K​).
(27)
Each cell contributes T=AB, so periodic boundary conditions give
ZL​=Tr(AB)M.​
(28)
The two matrices have common normalized eigenvectors (1,1)T/2​ and (1,−1)T/2​, expressing spin inversion symmetry. Their respective eigenvalues are
a+​=3eK+e−3K,a−​=e−3K−eK,b+​=e−K+eK,b−​=e−K−eK.
(29)
Consequently the cell transfer matrix has eigenvalues
λ+​λ−​​=(3eK+e−3K)(e−K+eK)=3+e−4K+e−2K+3e2K,=(e−3K−eK)(e−K−eK)=e−4K−e−2K−1+e2K,​
(30)
and the finite-size partition function is
ZL​=λ+L/2​+λ−L/2​.​
(31)
For K>0, λ−​≥0 and λ+​−λ−​=4+2e−2K+2e2K>0, so only λ+​ survives in the thermodynamic limit. Divide the Helmholtz free energy by 3M, the number of sites rather than the number of cells or base spins:
f(T)=−3kB​T​lnλ+​=−3kB​T​ln(3+e−4βJ+e−2βJ+3e2βJ).​
(32)
As a counting check, K=0 gives λ+​=8, λ−​=0, hence ZL​=8M=23M, exactly the number of independent Ising spin configurations. This model has separated decorated bonds and should not be confused with the corner-sharing sawtooth Ising chain, which has a different site count.

For low temperature, factor out the largest Boltzmann factor from the dominant eigenvalue:
λ+​=3e2K(1+e−2K+31​e−4K+31​e−6K).
(33)
Thus the Helmholtz free energy per site has the expansion
f=−32J​−3kB​T​ln3−3kB​T​e−2K+O(kB​Te−4K).
(34)
Differentiate at fixed J, remembering that K=J/(kB​T). The entropy per site is
s=3kB​​ln3+3kB​​(1+2K)e−2K+O(kB​(1+K)e−4K),T→0+lim​s(T)=3kB​​ln3.​
(35)
The nonzero residual entropy comes from an exponentially large ground-state degeneracy, generated by geometrical frustration. A triangle with antiferromagnetic energy J[st+(s+t)τ] has minimum energy −J: if s=t, the apex must have the opposite sign; if s=−t, either apex sign is permitted. The two all-equal configurations instead have energy 3J. Thus exactly one of a minimum-energy triangle's bonds is frustrated. The bridge can simultaneously minimize its energy at −J by requiring t=−s′, where s′ is the next cell's first base spin.
For fixed s and s′, the number of allowed apex choices is therefore two when s=s′ and one when s=−s′. The zero-temperature counting transfer matrix on successive first base spins is
G=(21​12​),gM​=TrGM=3M+1,EGS​=−2JM=−JL.​
(36)
The eigenvalues of G are 3 and 1. Dividing kB​lngM​ by the 3M sites and taking the thermodynamic limit gives kB​ln3/3, independently confirming the derivative calculation. This does not assert a unique ordered ground state: the extensive choice of frustrated bonds is precisely what leaves entropy at zero temperature. At high temperature, all Ising spins become independent and s→kB​ln2 per site.
