Differentiate the Lagrangian with respect to the generalized velocities:
pj​=∂x˙j​∂L​=mx˙j​+eAj​.​
(1)
The Legendre transform cancels the vector-potential energy term, giving H=p⋅x˙−L=m∣x˙∣2/2+V. Eliminating velocity with mx˙=p−eA therefore yields
H=2m∣p−eA∣2​+V.​
(2)
For the given gauge, set π1​=p1​+eBx2​/2, π2​=p2​−eBx1​/2, π3​=p3​. Hamilton's equations give x˙j​=πj​/m, p˙​1​=(eB/2)x˙2​, p˙​2​=−(eB/2)x˙1​, p˙​3​=0. Hence the conserved quantities are
p3​,K1​=p1​−2eB​x2​,K2​=p2​+2eB​x1​,H.​
(3)
The two transverse combinations are components of magnetic pseudomomentum. The printed conserved combinations omit the electric charge factor e. With the stated vector potential and general electric charge they need the factors displayed above; as printed they hold only if e=1 or if electric charge is already absorbed into the field convention.
The initial data give K1​=K2​=p3​=0. Thus p1​=eBx2​/2, p2​=−eBx1​/2 throughout, and
x˙1​=ωc​x2​,x˙2​=−ωc​x1​,ωc​=eB/m.
(4)
Solving with the initial point gives
x1​=asin(ωc​t),x2​=acos(ωc​t),x3​=0.​
(5)
Consequently x12​+x22​=a2: the guiding centre is the origin and the signed cyclotron frequency fixes the rotation direction. The energy is the constant e2B2a2/(2m).

En​=2ma2n2π2ℏ2​,Ψn​(x)=a2​​sinanπx​,n=1,2,….​

For a decaying bound state take α>0. Substituting F=fe−αr gives F′′=e−αr(f′′−2αf′+α2f). The term involving the energy cancels α2f, leaving the displayed equation with
A=ℏ22mK​.​
(9)
At the lowest power of a series from the Frobenius method f=∑k≥0​ak​rk+c, the indicial equation is c(c−1)−ℓ(ℓ+1)=0. Its roots are ℓ+1 and −ℓ. The latter makes F∼r−ℓ and the radial probability density integral ∫∣F∣2dr diverge for positive integral ℓ. Hence c=ℓ+1​.
Equating the remaining coefficients gives
ak+1​=(k+1)(k+2ℓ+2)2α(k+ℓ+1)−A​ak​.​
(10)
Unless the numerator vanishes, the regular confluent-hypergeometric series contains a large-radius growing branch with the exponential factor e2αr. Multiplication by the external e−αr then leaves exponential growth, so it cannot be a normalizable bound state. Equivalently, the recurrence at large k has ak+1​/ak​∼2α/k, indicating that growing exponential scale; regularity at the origin has already fixed the combination of solutions, leaving termination as the way to remove it.
If the polynomial ends at degree index N≥0, its last recurrence numerator gives A=2α(N+ℓ+1). With n=N+ℓ+1>ℓ,
α=2nA​,En​=−8mn2ℏ2A2​=−2ℏ2n2mK2​.​
(11)
For the separate angle-independent exponential, normalization includes the full solid angle:
1=4π∣N∣2∫0∞​r2e−2αrdr=4π∣N∣2(2α)32​=α3π∣N∣2​.
(12)
Thus one can choose N=α3/π​​, up to an overall phase. Angle independence corresponds to the ℓ=0 extension, distinct from the positive-ℓ family used in the indicial discussion.

A pure state is a unit vector in a complex Hilbert space, with vectors differing by a global phase representing the same physical state. An observable is a self-adjoint operator, with measurement probabilities determined by its spectral projections. For vectors in the relevant operator domains,
Eψ​[A]=⟨ψ,Aψ⟩,Δψ​[A]2=⟨ψ,A2ψ⟩−Eψ​[A]2=∥(A−Eψ​[A])ψ∥2.​
(13)
A squared norm vanishes exactly when its vector vanishes. Therefore zero dispersion is equivalent to Aψ=Eψ​[A]ψ.
In the position representation, (Xψ)(x)=xψ(x) and (Pψ)(x)=−iℏψ′(x). On a common differentiable invariant domain,
(XP−PX)ψ=−iℏxψ′+iℏ(xψ)′=iℏψ,
(14)
so [X,P]=iℏI​.
For two observables set a=∥Aψ∥2, b=∥Bψ∥2, c=Eψ​[i[A,B]]. For real t,
0≤∥(A−itB)ψ∥2=a−ct+bt2.
(15)
If b>0, nonpositivity of its discriminant yields 4ab≥c2​. Equality holds exactly when the minimum squared norm vanishes, namely
Aψ=itBψfor some real t,t=c/(2b).​
(16)
The degenerate case must also be included: if Bψ=0, then b=c=0 and equality holds for every Aψ. Thus the complete criterion is that Bψ=0, or that the displayed real-t relation holds. This includes Aψ=0 by choosing t=0 when b>0.
Apply the inequality to centred operators A=P−Eψ​[P]I, B=X−Eψ​[X]I. Their squared norms are the variances and their commutator is [P,X]=−iℏI, giving
Δψ​[P]2Δψ​[X]2≥4ℏ2​.​
(17)
An eigenvector of either operator would have its corresponding dispersion zero, contradicting this positive lower bound if the other dispersion were finite and the stated domain conditions held. In the usual L2(R) representation there are in fact no normalizable exact position or momentum eigenvectors; their generalized eigenvectors are not counterexamples. The equality condition above is the uncentered second-moment commutator inequality.

For identical bosons the full wave function is symmetric under the particle exchange operator:
ψ(x2​,x1​)=ψ(x1​,x2​).​
(18)
In the given position-only model this is the spatial parity under the particle exchange operator. If additional spin variables are present, the symmetry applies to the complete state including them.

For identical fermions the full wave function is antisymmetric:
ψ(x2​,x1​)=−ψ(x1​,x2​).​
(19)
In particular it vanishes when the two complete one-particle arguments coincide. Applying this to the position-only model assumes that no independent spin symmetry changes the required spatial parity.

The orthogonal coordinate change preserves the kinetic energy and takes the particle exchange operator to (x+​,x−​)↦(x+​,−x−​). Thus the spatial wave function is even in x−​ for bosons and odd for fermions.
The total potential energy and Hamiltonian separate as
V=2mω2​x+2​+2mω2(1+2κ)​x−2​,H=H+​(ω)+H−​(Ω),Ω=ω1+2κ​.
(20)
Assume 1+2κ>0 so the relative oscillator is confining. Its spectrum is En+​,n−​​=ℏω(n+​+1/2)+ℏΩ(n−​+1/2), and its parity in the two-particle relative coordinate is (−1)n−​.
The lowest boson state uses n+​=n−​=0, whereas the lowest fermion state uses n+​=0, n−​=1. Therefore
EB​=21​ℏω[1+1+2κ​],EF​=21​ℏω[1+31+2κ​].​
(21)
For an oscillator of frequency Ω, ⟨x−2​⟩n​=(n+1/2)ℏ/(mΩ). Since (x1​−x2​)2=2x−2​,
⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩B​=mΩℏ​,⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩F​=mΩ3ℏ​.​
(22)
The factor three is the extra relative-coordinate excitation required by antisymmetry. The supplied oscillator wave functions correspond to kinetic energy P2/(2m); the extra factor 1/2 printed before that term in the hint is inconsistent with those functions and the stated energies. The calculation uses the conventional kinetic energy dictated by particle mass m.

Use the normalized Rayleigh quotient R(ψ)=⟨ψ,Hψ⟩/⟨ψ,ψ⟩. Its real directional derivative in an arbitrary complex direction η is
δR=⟨ψ,ψ⟩2Re⟨η,(H−R)ψ⟩​.
(23)
It vanishes for every direction precisely when (H−R)ψ=0, since testing η=(H−R)ψ gives its squared norm. Conversely an eigenvector makes every derivative zero. Stationarity over all states is stronger than stationarity in a restricted trial family.
Normalize the Gaussian as ψα​=(α/π)1/4e−αx2/2, with α>0. Its moments are ⟨X2⟩=1/(2α), ⟨X4⟩=3/(4α2) and ⟨P2⟩=ℏ2α/2. The printed potential energy consequently gives
E(α)=4mℏ2α​+4αmω2​+256ℏα215m2ω3​.​
(24)
Put a=αℏ/(mω). Then E/(ℏω)=a/4+1/(4a)+15/(256a2), whose second derivative is 1/(2a3)+45/(128a4)>0. It diverges at both positive endpoints, so its sole stationary point is the unique global minimum. Its equation is
a3−a−3215​=0,a∗​≃1.18179610.​
(25)
The variational principle therefore yields the optimized upper bound
E0​≤E(α∗​)≃0.548944745ℏω,α∗​≃1.18179610ℏmω​.​
(26)
The printed minimizer is inconsistent with the printed quartic coefficient. At a=3/2 the derivative is 5/48>0, so this is not the stationary Gaussian for that potential energy. It is nevertheless a legitimate trial vector and gives the simple, weaker bound E0​≤109ℏω/192​. If the quartic coefficient were instead 5m2ω3/(16ℏ), four times the printed value, then a=3/2 would indeed minimize the family and its energy would be 31ℏω/48. This possible coefficient correction is identified explicitly rather than silently used. The reusable calculation is Gaussian trial for a quadratic-plus-quartic oscillator.

Let Ψ=0 and take the reduced positive operator ρ1​=Tr2​∣Ψ⟩⟨Ψ∣. Choose orthonormal eigenvectors ξj​ for its positive eigenvalues λj​. Define
vj​=(⟨ξj​∣⊗I)Ψ,cj​=λj​​,ηj​=vj​/cj​.
(27)
The defining identity of the partial trace gives ⟨vi​,vj​⟩=λj​δij​, so the ηj​ are orthonormal. Expansion in a full eigenbasis of ρ1​ yields Ψ=∑j​ξj​⊗vj​; zero-eigenvalue terms have zero squared norm and contribute nothing. Thus
Ψ=j∑​cj​∣ξj​⟩∣ηj​⟩,cj​>0.​
(28)
The zero vector has the empty decomposition. Unit normalization would give ∑j​cj2​=1, but no normalization is needed for the theorem itself.
For the given vector, the coefficient matrix is M=(101​011​). Its second reduced operator is M∗M=(21​12​), whose eigenvalues are 3,1 and eigenvectors η+​=(E1​+E2​)/2​, η−​=(E1​−E2​)/2​. The corresponding normalized first-factor vectors are
ξ+​=6​e1​+e2​+2e3​​,ξ−​=2​e1​−e2​​.
(29)
Their norms are one and their inner product is zero. Hence
ψ=3​∣ξ+​⟩∣η+​⟩+∣ξ−​⟩∣η−​⟩.​
(30)
Expanding the two terms returns the original three contributions. The vector has squared norm 4; if a normalized state is wanted, divide by two and the Schmidt coefficients become 3​/2 and 1/2.

The no-cloning theorem states that no fixed deterministic quantum operation can perfectly duplicate every unknown pure state. For a unitary copier with a fixed blank, suppose it cloned two normalized vectors ∣a⟩,∣b⟩. Preservation of inner products would give ⟨a∣b⟩=⟨a∣b⟩2, impossible for a nonorthogonal distinct pair with overlap strictly between zero and one in modulus. Including an environment does not help: its final overlap has modulus at most one, so one would require ∣⟨a∣b⟩∣≤∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2. Thus a universal perfect copier is impossible. Orthogonal known alternatives do not contradict this restriction.
Label the three qubits as input S, Alice's entangled qubit A and Bob's qubit B. Initially their joint state is
2​1​[α0​(∣000⟩+∣011⟩)+α1​(∣100⟩+∣111⟩)].
(31)
The controlled-NOT gate from S to A changes this to
2​1​[α0​(∣000⟩+∣011⟩)+α1​(∣110⟩+∣101⟩)].
(32)
Apply H∣0⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​ and H∣1⟩=(∣0⟩−∣1⟩)/2​ to the first qubit and group by Alice's two computational-basis outcomes. The result is
21​[∣00⟩∣ψ⟩+∣01⟩X∣ψ⟩+∣10⟩Z∣ψ⟩+∣11⟩XZ∣ψ⟩].​
(33)
In expanded form the last two Bob factors are α0​∣0⟩−α1​∣1⟩ and α0​∣1⟩−α1​∣0⟩. Their Alice labels must be 10 and 11; the repeated 00 labels in the source are printing errors.
Alice measures her two qubits and sends the two classical bits (a,b) to Bob. Each outcome has probability 1/4 and Bob's conditional state is XbZa∣ψ⟩. He applies ZaXb​ to recover the input, up to physically irrelevant global phase. Explicitly the corrections for 00,01,10,11 are I,X,Z,ZX. This is quantum teleportation: Alice's input is consumed by measurement, so no second surviving copy is created. Before her classical message, Bob's reduced density operator is I/2, independent of the input, so the protocol also supplies no faster-than-light communication.
