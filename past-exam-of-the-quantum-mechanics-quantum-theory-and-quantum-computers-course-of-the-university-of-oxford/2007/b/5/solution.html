The orthogonal coordinate change preserves the kinetic energy and takes the particle exchange operator to (x+​,x−​)↦(x+​,−x−​). Thus the spatial wave function is even in x−​ for bosons and odd for fermions.

The lowest boson state uses n+​=n−​=0, whereas the lowest fermion state uses n+​=0, n−​=1. Therefore
EB​=21​ℏω[1+1+2κ​],EF​=21​ℏω[1+31+2κ​].​

For an oscillator of frequency Ω, ⟨x−2​⟩n​=(n+1/2)ℏ/(mΩ). Since (x1​−x2​)2=2x−2​,
⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩B​=mΩℏ​,⟨(x1​−x2​)2⟩F​=mΩ3ℏ​.​

The factor three is the extra relative-coordinate excitation required by antisymmetry. The supplied oscillator wave functions correspond to kinetic energy P2/(2m); the extra factor 1/2 printed before that term in the hint is inconsistent with those functions and the stated energies. The calculation uses the conventional kinetic energy dictated by particle mass m.
