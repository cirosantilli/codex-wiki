The no-cloning theorem states that no fixed deterministic quantum operation can perfectly duplicate every unknown pure state. For a unitary copier with a fixed blank, suppose it cloned two normalized vectors ∣a⟩,∣b⟩. Preservation of inner products would give ⟨a∣b⟩=⟨a∣b⟩2, impossible for a nonorthogonal distinct pair with overlap strictly between zero and one in modulus. Including an environment does not help: its final overlap has modulus at most one, so one would require ∣⟨a∣b⟩∣≤∣⟨a∣b⟩∣2. Thus a universal perfect copier is impossible. Orthogonal known alternatives do not contradict this restriction.
Label the three qubits as input S, Alice's entangled qubit A and Bob's qubit B. Initially their joint state is

The controlled-NOT gate from S to A changes this to

Apply H∣0⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​ and H∣1⟩=(∣0⟩−∣1⟩)/2​ to the first qubit and group by Alice's two computational-basis outcomes. The result is
21​[∣00⟩∣ψ⟩+∣01⟩X∣ψ⟩+∣10⟩Z∣ψ⟩+∣11⟩XZ∣ψ⟩].​
(33)
In expanded form the last two Bob factors are α0​∣0⟩−α1​∣1⟩ and α0​∣1⟩−α1​∣0⟩. Their Alice labels must be 10 and 11; the repeated 00 labels in the source are printing errors.
Alice measures her two qubits and sends the two classical bits (a,b) to Bob. Each outcome has probability 1/4 and Bob's conditional state is XbZa∣ψ⟩. He applies ZaXb​ to recover the input, up to physically irrelevant global phase. Explicitly the corrections for 00,01,10,11 are I,X,Z,ZX. This is quantum teleportation: Alice's input is consumed by measurement, so no second surviving copy is created. Before her classical message, Bob's reduced density operator is I/2, independent of the input, so the protocol also supplies no faster-than-light communication.
