Take a normalized vector in the required common operator domains. Expanding the squared norm and using self-adjointness gives
∥(A−itB)ψ∥2=⟨ψ,(A+itB)(A−itB)ψ⟩=⟨A2⟩+t2⟨B2⟩−it⟨[A,B]⟩.
(18)
Thus its final term is ℏt⟨C⟩. Set a=∥Aψ∥2, b=∥Bψ∥2, c=⟨C⟩. If b>0, minimizing the nonnegative quadratic at t∗​=−ℏc/(2b) gives
4ab≥ℏ2c2.​
(19)
Equality holds precisely when the minimum norm vanishes, that is (A−it∗​B)ψ=0. If b=0, however, then Bψ=0 and c=0, so equality is automatic even when Aψ=0. The printed equality criterion needs this zero-vector exception. For example A=I, B=0, C=0 saturates the inequality but admits no such real t. The exact general criterion is linear dependence of Aψ,Bψ together with vanishing real part of their inner product, as in the uncentered second-moment commutator inequality.
Apply the same calculation to A−⟨A⟩I, B−⟨B⟩I, whose commutator is unchanged. This gives the Robertson uncertainty principle
4(ΔA)2(ΔB)2≥ℏ2⟨C⟩2.​
(20)
If ψ is an eigenvector of A, its dispersion is zero; finite dispersion of B then forces ⟨C⟩=0​.
In the position representation, Xψ=xψ and Pψ=−iℏψ′. The product rule gives [P,X]ψ=−iℏψ, hence [P,X]=−iℏI​. Since V(X) commutes with X,
[H,X]=2m1​{P[P,X]+[P,X]P}=−miℏ​P.​
(21)
For an energy eigenstate, ⟨[H,X]⟩=E⟨X⟩−E⟨X⟩=0, so ⟨P⟩=0​. For the oscillator [H,P]=iℏmω2X, and the same argument gives ⟨X⟩=0​. These expectation identities require the stated finite moments and commutator domains; formal position eigenvectors are not normalizable exceptions.
Apply the Robertson uncertainty principle to each angular-momentum pair:
4(ΔL1​)2(ΔL2​)2≥ℏ2⟨L3​⟩2.​
(22)
If ψ is an eigenvector of L3​, then ΔL3​=0. The inequalities for (L2​,L3​) and (L3​,L1​) force ⟨L1​⟩=⟨L2​⟩=0​, assuming the relevant moments exist.
