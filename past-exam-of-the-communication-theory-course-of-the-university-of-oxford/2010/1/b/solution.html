Put S=∑k=1a​2−lk​ and let lmax​ be the largest length. Expanding Sn sums 2−L over all strings of n source symbols, where L is the total code length. By unique decodability, the concatenations of length r in this sum are all distinct, so there are at most 2r of them. Since n≤r≤nlmax​,
Sn≤∑r=nnlmax​​2r2−r≤nlmax​.
(1)
Taking nth roots and sending n to infinity proves the Kraft–McMillan inequality
k=1∑a​2−lk​≤1.​
(2)
Now qk​=2−lk​/S is a probability distribution. With base-two logarithms, Gibbs inequality gives
0≤D(p∥q)=∑k​pk​log2​qk​pk​​=−H(X)+EL+log2​S.
(3)
Terms with pk​=0 contribute zero. Therefore
EL−H(X)=D(p∥q)−log2​S≥0.​
(4)
The normalization by S is important: the raw numbers 2−lk​ need not sum to one.
