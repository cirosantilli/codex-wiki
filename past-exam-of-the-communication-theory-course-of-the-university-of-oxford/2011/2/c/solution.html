Let Fk​=∑r<k​pr​ and let Jk​=[Fk​,Fk​+pk​) be the probability cell of symbol k. Its midpoint is Gk​=Fk​+pk​/2. Use the binary expansion terminating in zeros at a dyadic rational, so truncation to lk​ bits gives
ak​=2−lk​⌊2lk​Gk​⌋.
(23)
The chosen codeword corresponds to the dyadic interval Dk​=[ak​,ak​+2−lk​). It contains Gk​, and its width satisfies 2−lk​≤pk​/2. Consequently
ak​>Gk​−2−lk​≥Fk​,ak​+2−lk​≤Gk​+2−lk​≤Fk​+pk​.
(24)
Thus Dk​⊆Jk​. The probability cells are disjoint, so the code intervals are disjoint. If one binary codeword were a prefix of another, its dyadic interval would contain the other's interval, contradicting this disjointness. Hence the code is instantaneous.
Finally, lk​=⌈−log2​pk​⌉+1<−log2​pk​+2. Multiplying by pk​ and summing yields
EL<H(X)+2.​
(25)
This is the Shannon-Fano-Elias code: the extra bit makes the dyadic interval narrow enough to remain inside the cell around its midpoint.
