For any chosen input distribution, the chain rule and nonnegativity of conditional mutual information give
I(U;V,W)=I(U;V)+I(U;W∣V)≥I(U;V),
(34)
and likewise I(U;V,W)≥I(U;W). Choosing an input distribution attaining either marginal capacity proves K≥max{K1​,K2​}.
For the upper bound, conditional independence implies H(V,W∣U)=H(V∣U)+H(W∣U), while entropy subadditivity gives H(V,W)≤H(V)+H(W). Therefore
I(U;V,W)​=H(V,W)−H(V,W∣U)≤H(V)+H(W)−H(V∣U)−H(W∣U)=I(U;V)+I(U;W)≤K1​+K2​.​
(35)
Maximizing over the input law gives
max{K1​,K2​}≤K≤K1​+K2​.​
(36)
More precisely, under the conditional-independence assumption,
I(U;V,W)=I(U;V)+I(U;W)−I(V;W).
(37)
The outputs may be dependent through the common input, and this final term measures the information they share rather than add independently.
This is information from two conditionally independent observations.
