Every internal node of an optimal binary prefix tree has two children. If one had only one child, remove that one-edge detour from all descendant words. Prefix-freeness is preserved and at least one positive-probability word becomes shorter, strictly lowering the expected length. Thus the tree is full.
Take a leaf at maximum depth L. Its sibling exists because the tree is full. That sibling cannot be an internal node, since a descendant would be deeper than L. Hence it is another leaf at depth L, and the corresponding codewords differ only in their final bit. This is the deepest sibling property of an optimal prefix code.
A finite full binary tree satisfies Kraft equality, ∑x​2−ℓx​=1, by dividing mass equally down each branch from the root. If all 2n lengths were at least n, with at least one strictly greater, that sum would be less than 2n2−n=1, a contradiction. Since the code is not a block code, the alternative that all lengths equal n is excluded. Therefore some word has length below n. Also a longest word must have length above n: if all lengths were at most n and some shorter, the Kraft sum would exceed one.
