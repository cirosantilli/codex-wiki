Rank of an observation (ri​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Order statistic
OurBigBook.com Words: 188
The rank of an observation records where that observation lies among the order statistics of a statistical sample. When all values are distinct, ri​=1+#{j:xj​<xi​} and xi​=x(ri​)​. For (0.7,0.1,0.9,0.4,0.2), the ranks of observations in original order are (4,1,5,3,2): the first observation is fourth smallest, while the fourth observation is third smallest.
Ties require a convention. A tied block occupying sorted positions a,…,b can assign each member the average rank of an observation (a+b)/2, the minimum position a, or the maximum position b; an explicit rule can also break ties into distinct positions. R's official documentation describes these choices and uses average ranks by default. [ref].
For example, (0.1,0.4,0.4,0.9) has order statistics (0.1,0.4,0.4,0.9). The two 0.4 observations occupy positions 2 and 3; average ranks of observations are (1,2.5,2.5,4), whereas minimum-position ranks are (1,2,2,4). A fractional average rank is a summary of tied positions, rather than an index of one order statistic. Without omissions, distinct ranks and average ranks sum to n(n+1)/2, since averaging each tied block preserves its sum; minimum-position ranks need not.
Wilcoxon signed-rank tests rank the absolute nonzero paired differences, while Wilcoxon rank sum tests rank the pooled observations from two samples; these are different rankings.
