Pointwise convergence (fn​(x)→f(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 628 Articles: 4
For a sequence of functions fn​:X→R, pointwise convergence to a function f:X→R means
∀x∈X∀ε>0∃N∈N>0​∀n≥N:∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣<ε.
(47)
It is the ordinary limit of a sequence at each fixed input. The threshold N=N(x,ε) may depend on the input. Uniform convergence instead requires one threshold for the entire domain of a function.[ref]
Consider the polynomial functions fn​(x)=xn, for positive integers n, on the interval [0,1]. For 0≤x<1 the geometric sequence (xn) tends to zero, while fn​(1)=1. Thus
f(x)={0,1,​0≤x<1,x=1.​
(48)
Each fn​ is a continuous function, but its pointwise limit is discontinuous at one. Holding an input still hides what happens near that endpoint. The analogous even-power example on [−1,1] appears in the source.[ref]
For 0<x<1 and 0<ε<1, taking the logarithm of xn<ε and dividing by the negative real number logx reverses the inequality:
n>logxlogε​,N(x,ε)=⌊logxlogε​⌋+1.
(49)
The floor function followed by adding one handles the strict inequality even when the ratio is an integer. For tolerance ε=0.01, the first successful indices at x=0.5, 0.8, and 0.99 are respectively 7, 21, and 459. At x=0.5, 2−6=0.015625 fails and 2−7=0.0078125 succeeds. At x=0 and x=1, the error from this pointwise limit is already zero, so N=1 works. Nevertheless, N(x,ε) grows without bound as x→1 from below. A finite list of accurate test inputs cannot establish uniform convergence.
An even sharper warning concerns integration. On [0,1], define the continuous functions
gn​(x)=⎩⎨⎧​2n2x,2n−2n2x,0,​0≤x≤1/(2n),1/(2n)≤x≤1/n,1/n≤x≤1.​
(50)
Their supports are [0,1/n], with height n at 1/(2n) and value zero at both ends. Every fixed x>0 eventually lies beyond the support, and gn​(0)=0 always, so pointwise convergence gives the zero function. Nevertheless, each integral is the area of a triangle: ∫01​gn​(x)dx=21​(1/n)n=21​. Shrinking width compensates increasing height. Hence the limit of a sequence of these integrals differs from the integral of the pointwise limit; interchanging them requires additional conditions, such as uniform convergence preserves integrals on a compact interval.
