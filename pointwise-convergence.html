Pointwise convergence (fn​(x)→f(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series
OurBigBook.com Words: 459 Articles: 3
For a sequence of functions fn​:X→R and a function f:X→R on the same domain of a function, pointwise convergence to f means
∀x∈X∀ε>0∃N∈N>0​∀n≥N:∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣<ε.
(92)
At each fixed input, this is the ordinary limit of a sequence. The index may depend on both the input and the requested accuracy: N=N(x,ε). It therefore certifies each input separately, with no promise about the worst error across the domain of a function. This is a useful first way to identify a candidate pointwise limit: hold the input still and study a numerical sequence. See MIT 18.100A, Lecture 23, definition 7.
Consider the polynomial functions fn​(x)=xn, with positive integer index n, on the interval [0,1]. For 0≤x<1 the geometric sequence (xn) tends to zero, while fn​(1)=1. Thus its pointwise limit is
f(x)={0,1,​0≤x<1,x=1.​
(93)
Each fn​ is a continuous function, but this pointwise limit fails continuity at one: its nearby values from the left are zero while its value at one is one. At any fixed 0<x<1 and 0<ε<1, the logarithm gives the sufficient choice N>log(ε)/log(x). For example, at x=1/2 and ε=0.01, N=7 works because 2−7=0.0078125, whereas 2−6=0.015625. As the input approaches one from below, the required index grows without bound. A common tolerance needs increasingly late indices at different inputs.
An even sharper warning concerns integration. On [0,1], define the continuous functions
gn​(x)=⎩⎨⎧​2n2x,2n−2n2x,0,​0≤x≤1/(2n),1/(2n)≤x≤1/n,1/n≤x≤1.​
(94)
Their supports are [0,1/n], with height n at 1/(2n) and value zero at both ends. Every fixed x>0 eventually lies beyond the support, and gn​(0)=0 always, so pointwise convergence gives the zero function. Nevertheless, each integral is the area of a triangle: ∫01​gn​(x)dx=21​(1/n)n=21​. Shrinking width exactly compensates increasing height. Hence the limit of these integrals differs from the integral of the pointwise limit; interchanging them requires additional conditions, such as uniform convergence preserves integrals on a compact interval.
