Bragg's law (nλ=2dsinθ)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering Elastic Bragg scattering condition
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
Bragg's law selects directions of constructive interference in coherent elastic scattering from equally spaced lattice planes:
nλ=2dsinθ,n=1,2,3,….
(25)
Here d>0 is the perpendicular interplanar spacing, λ>0 the incident wavelength, n the positive integer diffraction order, and θ the Bragg angle, measured between the incoming beam and the lattice planes. The scattering deflection, between incoming and outgoing propagation directions, is 2θ. Thus a plotted detector angle 2θ must be halved before insertion into Bragg's law. The θ in the parent elastic Bragg scattering condition denotes the full deflection; the present θ denotes half of it. [ref]
The lattice planes organize wave interference throughout a crystal structure; they are not literal metal mirrors or separate reflecting surfaces. The picture assumes elastic scattering, hence an unchanged wavelength, and sufficiently stable relative wave phases across the contributing lattice planes. X-ray wavelengths comparable with atomic interplanar spacings make these directions experimentally accessible. The sine cannot exceed one: an order exists geometrically only if nλ/(2d)≤1. For λ>2d, even the first diffraction order is inaccessible. A geometrically possible direction need not have nonzero wave intensity; the crystal lattice structure factor and crystal basis structure factor also matter. [ref]
