Macrostate

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics State degeneracy
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
A macrostate groups microstates sharing prescribed coarse data. The grouping depends on what is retained: here retain only total energy. For the two distinguishable two-level systems, the macrostates E=0,Δ,2Δ have state degeneracies 1,2,1. Although two units are not macroscopic in size, they make this loss of information visible: knowing E=Δ does not tell us which unit is excited.
At inverse temperature βΔ=ln3, each unit's excited-state probability is p=1/4. Their independence gives microstate probabilities 9/16,3/16,3/16,1/16 in the order above. Adding within each macrostate gives
P(E=0)=169​,P(E=Δ)=166​,P(E=2Δ)=161​.
(60)
The total expected value of energy is therefore Δ(6/16)+2Δ(1/16)=Δ/2, matching two units each with expected value of energy Δ/4. Equal state degeneracies would give the wrong answer: macrostate probabilities combine counting with the Boltzmann factor. At very high positive temperature, all four microstates become equally likely, but these three macrostate probabilities approach 1/4,1/2,1/4.
