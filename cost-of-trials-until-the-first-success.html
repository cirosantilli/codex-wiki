Cost of trials until the first success (E[SN​]=3/p−2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Wald's equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 224
Consider a stipulated repeated-attempt model, with independent Bernoulli trials of success probability p∈(0,1]. An unsuccessful attempt costs three units of work, and a successful attempt costs one. Let Bi​ be its success indicator function, Xi​=3−2Bi​ its cost, and N=inf{i≥1:Bi​=1} the first success. Observing Xi​ reveals Bi​, so N is a stopping time for the costs' natural filtration. The geometric distribution gives P(N≥i)=(1−p)i−1 and E[N]=1/p, including the deterministic case p=1. Thus
E[X1​]=3−2p,E[SN​]=(3−2p)/p=3/p−2.​
(55)
At p=1/4, the average is four attempts and ten units of work. An independent check uses the pathwise identity SN​=3(N−1)+1: there are N−1 failures and one final success. No success probabilities or costs in this example are measured data.
Here N is not independent of the observed costs. In particular, conditioning on N=n forces every preceding cost to be three and the final cost to be one, so E[SN​∣N=n]=3n−2, generally different from nE[X1​]=(5/2)n at p=1/4. Nevertheless the unconditional identity holds. This distinction explains why conditioning on the total count as though the original cost law remained unchanged is a faulty proof.
