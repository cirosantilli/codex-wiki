Endomorphism of a finite cyclic group (fa​(k)=ak(modn))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Morphism Endomorphism
OurBigBook.com Words: 124
Every endomorphism of the additive finite cyclic group Cn​=Z/nZ, for a positive integer n, has the form fa​(k)=ak(modn), determined by a=fa​(1). This is the self-map case of homomorphism from a finite cyclic group: repeated addition forces the value at every element. Conversely, each choice of a respects addition and gives a well-defined map on congruence classes.
Let d=gcd(n,a), the greatest common divisor. The image of a group homomorphism is the cyclic subgroup generated by a, with size n/d by order of an element of a finite cyclic group. The kernel of a group homomorphism is
kerfa​={0,n/d,2n/d,…,(d−1)n/d},∣kerfa​∣=d.
(5)
Indeed, ak=0(modn) is equivalent to n/d dividing k, after cancelling d and using coprimality. Consequently fa​ is a group automorphism exactly when a and n are coprime. In that case a modular inverse b of a gives fa−1​=fb​. This connects reversible self-maps directly to the generators of a finite cyclic group.[ref]
