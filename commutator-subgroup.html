Commutator subgroup (G′=[G,G])

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
The commutator subgroup of a group G is the subgroup generated by all its group commutators:
G′=[G,G]=⟨x−1y−1xy:x,y∈G⟩.
(12)
Here and throughout, [x,y]=x−1y−1xy. A group commutator is the identity element precisely when x and y commute. The generated subgroup includes finite products of group commutators and their inverse elements; an element of G′ need not itself be a single group commutator. Thus G′={e} precisely when G is an abelian group.
The useful idea is to record exactly what must disappear to make multiplication commutative. The identity xy=yx[x,y] shows how a group commutator corrects a change in multiplication order. Setting every such correction equal to e produces the abelianization G/G′, while preserving every piece of information that a group homomorphism to an abelian group can detect. This connects a local comparison of two operations to a canonical quotient group of the whole group.
The commutator subgroup is a characteristic subgroup, hence a normal subgroup: a group automorphism α sends [x,y] to [α(x),α(y)]. For any normal subgroup N,
G/N is abelian⟺G′⊆N.
(13)
Indeed, the group commutator of xN and yN is [x,y]N. This makes G′ the smallest normal subgroup whose quotient group is an abelian group. See Conrad, Subgroup series I, section 4: commutators and abelian quotients.
