Compact-sublevel convergence of the residual method

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Inverse problem Regularization of an inverse problem Variational regularization Residual method for variational regularization
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
Suppose J is nonnegative and sequentially lower semicontinuous, and its sublevel sets are strongly sequentially compact. For a solvable exact equation Au=f, every sequence of residual-method minimizers with δ→0 has a strongly convergent subsequence, and every limit is a J-minimizing solution. Indeed, comparison with an exact penalty minimizer bounds all reconstructions in one compact sublevel set; the residual bound gives ∥Auδ​−f∥≤2δ. Continuity of A and lower semicontinuity of J identify each subsequential limit as an exact minimizer. Thus the distance to a set of exact penalty minimizers tends to zero. Uniqueness of the exact minimizer upgrades this to full strong convergence.
Uniqueness cannot be omitted: for A=0, fδ​=f=0, and J(u)=max{∣u∣−1,0} on R, the sublevel sets are compact intervals but the minimizers u1/k​=(−1)k do not converge.
