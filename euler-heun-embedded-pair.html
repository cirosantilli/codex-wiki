Euler-Heun embedded pair (d=h(k2​−k1​)/2)

The Euler-Heun 1(2) pair combines the explicit Euler method and Heun's method. Here 1(2) names their respective order of a numerical method; the accepted endpoint in this article is the second-order one. Its shared Runge-Kutta stages and endpoints are
k1​Y[1]d​=f(tn​,yn​),=yn​+hk1​,=Y[2]−Y[1]=2h​(k2​−k1​).​k2​Y[2]​=f(tn​+h,yn​+hk1​),=yn​+2h​(k1​+k2​),​01​0121​1​0021​0​​
(3)
The first output row in this Butcher tableau is Heun's method; the second is the explicit Euler method. Both outputs cost two evaluations of f together, rather than one for Euler method plus two for a separately computed Heun's method.
For the initial value problem y′=y, y(0)=1, take h=0.2. The first slope is k1​=1; the stage state is 1+0.2k1​=1.2, giving k2​=1.2. Therefore
Y[1]=1.2,Y[2]=1.22,d=0.02,y(0.2)=e0.2≈1.22140275816.
(4)
The exact exponential function gives signed local defects approximately 0.02140275816 for the explicit Euler method and 0.00140275816 for Heun's method. Their difference is exactly 0.02. Thus the pair difference estimates the larger Euler defect; calling it the actual Heun defect would miss by a factor about fourteen here.
More generally, a step from yn​ on y′=y has
ℓ1​ℓ2​d​=yn​(eh−1−h)=yn​(2h2​+6h3​+O(h4)),=yn​(eh−1−h−h2/2)=yn​(6h3​+O(h4)),=yn​2h2​.​
(5)
These original Taylor expansions separate three quantities: the accepted Heun's method has local truncation error O(h3) and global error O(h2) under suitable numerical stability, while the tested local error estimator is O(h2). For E=∣d∣/s with s held approximately fixed, use hnew​=ηhE−1/2. A cube-root update would model a different quantity, the actual higher-order local defect.
