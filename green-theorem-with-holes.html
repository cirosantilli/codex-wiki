Green theorem with holes

 ... Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Vector calculus Green theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 344
On the punctured plane R2∖{(0,0)}, consider the vector field
G(x,y)=(−x2+y2y​,x2+y2x​).
(78)
With r2=x2+y2, its partial derivatives satisfy
Qx​=r4y2−x2​,Py​=r4y2−x2​,Qx​−Py​=0.
(79)
Thus the field has zero scalar curl everywhere it is defined. Nevertheless, on any counterclockwise circle γR​(t)=(Rcost,Rsint) with R>0,
G(γR​(t))=R1​(−sint,cost),γR′​(t)=R(−sint,cost),∮γR​​G⋅dr=∫02π​1dt=2π.
(80)
The radius cancels exactly.
Applying Green theorem to the enclosed disk would be invalid: G is undefined at the origin, inside that disk. Zero curl on the path alone is insufficient.[ref]
Now take the annulus A={(x,y):1≤r≤2}. Its positive boundary orientation is counterclockwise on the outer circle and clockwise on the inner circle. The complete boundary line integral is therefore
∮∂A​G⋅dr=outer boundary2π​​+inner boundary(−2π)​​=0=∬A​(Qx​−Py​)dA.
(81)
Here Green theorem applies: an open set containing the closed annulus can avoid the origin. The inner contribution remains −2π even for an arbitrarily small positive inner radius. Decreasing the hole's area does not decrease this line integral.
The cancellation has a useful proof picture. Cut the annulus along two radial segments into two half-annuli. Each piece has a single simple closed curve as its boundary; along each cut, the two induced boundary orientations oppose, so those line integrals cancel when the two applications of Green theorem are added. Only the outer and inner circles remain.[ref]
Locally, a choice of angle θ in polar coordinates has gradient ∇θ=G. Globally, following a circle once increases that angle by 2π, so it cannot be a single-valued potential of a conservative vector field on the punctured plane. The nonzero closed-path line integral also rules out such a potential by the fundamental theorem for line integrals.
On a simply connected open region, a continuously differentiable planar vector field with zero scalar curl does have a global potential. The hole is what separates the local derivative condition from the global conclusion.[ref]
