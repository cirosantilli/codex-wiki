Radio interferometry

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Observational astronomy Radio astronomy Aperture synthesis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 19
Radio interferometry measures the spatial coherence of received radio waves. At two antennas, a receiver preserves the relative wave phase, compensates the geometric delay for a chosen phase centre, and averages a product of one signal with the complex conjugate of the other. In a complex-signal description this correlation is ⟨E1​E2∗​⟩. Its real and imaginary parts carry two complementary components of the complex visibility; measuring only the received powers discards the relative wave phase needed for general imaging.
A finite frequency band makes delay compensation essential. If the residual delay is Δτ, the residual wave phase varies by 2πΔνΔτ across a band of width Δν. Averaging these differently oriented complex contributions reduces the correlation. For an ideal rectangular band the multiplicative factor is sin(πΔνΔτ)/(πΔνΔτ). A phase correction at just the central frequency cannot remove this variation across the band. Long time averages can likewise smear a changing signal.
