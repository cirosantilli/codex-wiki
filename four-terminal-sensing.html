Four-terminal sensing (R=[V(+I)−V(−I)]/(2I))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity Resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
Four-terminal sensing uses one pair of contacts to carry electric current and a separate pair to sense electric potential difference. The sensing instrument draws negligible electric current, so the voltage drops along its own leads are negligible, and the current-contact drops outside the sensing interval are excluded. A measurement using just two shared leads includes their electrical resistance and contact drops, which can overwhelm the small sample signal in a resistance-versus-temperature experiment for superconductivity. Four contacts do not automatically remove offsets from the thermoelectric effect. [ref]
For a stable offset V0​, let I>0 denote the magnitude of the reversed electric current. The measured electric potential differences are V(+I)=IR+V0​ and V(−I)=−IR+V0​, so subtraction gives the displayed electrical resistance. For example, with stipulated I=1mA, R=0.2mΩ and V0​=3μV, the two readings are 3.2 and 2.8μV; their difference correctly recovers R. If the offset changes between readings, its change leaves a residual measurement error; the sample and measuring circuit must remain sufficiently stable during reversal.
