The regular level set theorem says that for a C1 map g:U⊆Rn→Rr, if Dg has rank r on g−1(c), then g−1(c) is an (n−r)-dimensional submanifold and
Tp​(g−1(c))=kerDg(p).
(45)
Here r=1 and the given rank condition makes the zero set a two-dimensional submanifold. It is nonempty because g(0)=f(0)=0. Part (c) gives
Dg(p)(1,1,1)=gx​(p)+gy​(p)+gz​(p)=0.
(46)
Consequently
(1,1,1)∈kerDg(p)=Tp​Mfor every p∈M.​
(47)
There is also a direct curve proof: g(p+t(1,1,1))=g(p)=0, so the entire line through p in this direction lies in M. Its velocity at zero is exactly the required tangent vector.
