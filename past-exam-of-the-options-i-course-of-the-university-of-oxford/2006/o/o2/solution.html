Assume each sampled response is truthful, card selection is independent of the student's status, and responses follow the usual independent sampling model. In randomized response, a yes answer occurs either for a user receiving the first question or for a nonuser receiving the complementary question. Its probability is
q=xp+(1−x)(1−p)=(1−x)+(2x−1)p​.
(51)
Consequently Y∼Bin(n,q), and the likelihood is
L(p)=(Yn​)[(1−x)+(2x−1)p]Y[x−(2x−1)p]n−Y,0≤p≤1.
(52)
For x=1/2, first maximize the binomial likelihood in the unrestricted probability q: its maximum occurs at Y/n. Inverting the affine relation gives
p​=n(2x−1)Y−n(1−x)​.
(53)
For an interior value 0<Y<n, the same result follows from the score
ℓ′(p)=(2x−1)q(1−q)Y−nq​.
(54)
The endpoint cases follow by monotonicity. This estimator has expected value p and variance
Ep​=p,Var(p​)=n(2x−1)2q(1−q)​=np(1−p)​+n(2x−1)2x(1−x)​​.
(55)
These identities follow immediately from EY=nq and Var(Y)=nq(1−q).
The printed claim needs a parameter-space qualification. The admissible yes probabilities lie in [min(x,1−x),max(x,1−x)], which may exclude Y/n. The constrained maximum-likelihood estimator is
p​ML​=min{1,max{0,p​}}​.
(56)
The displayed untrimmed estimator is the maximum-likelihood estimator only when it already lies in [0,1]. Its unbiased estimator property does not carry over to the clipped estimator in general. For example, if n=1, x=3/4 and p=0, the clipped estimate equals 1 exactly when the answer is yes, so its expectation is 1/4, not zero. At x=1/2, q=1/2 for every p: the parameter is unidentifiable and the printed quotient is undefined. Thus the unbiased formula requires x=1/2, and the constrained likelihood assertion requires the stated clipping.
