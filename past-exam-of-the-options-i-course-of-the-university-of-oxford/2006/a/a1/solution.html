Write the group action as (ω,g)↦ωg. A right group action satisfies ω1=ω and (ωg)h=ω(gh). Its orbit and stabilizer are
Orb(ω)={ωg:g∈G},H=Stab(ω)={g∈G:ωg=ω}.
(1)
The identity belongs to H. If g,h∈H, then ωgh=(ωg)h=ω, so gh∈H. Also ωg=ω implies ω=(ωg)g−1=ωg−1, so g−1∈H. Thus H is a subgroup.
For the orbit-stabilizer theorem, the map G→Orb(ω), g↦ωg, is onto, and
ωg=ωh⟺gh−1∈H⟺g∈Hh.
(2)
Its fibers are precisely the right cosets Hh, each containing ∣H∣ elements. Counting these disjoint fibers gives
∣Orb(ω)∣=∣G∣/∣H∣​.
(3)
For the symmetric group S3​, the six subgroups are
{1},⟨(12)⟩,⟨(13)⟩,⟨(23)⟩,A3​={1,(123),(132)},S3​.
(4)
The group action H↦g−1Hg has four orbits: each of {1}, A3​, and S3​ is fixed, while the three order-two subgroups form one orbit. Here an orbit is a collection of subgroups, rather than a collection of individual permutations.
