Use the right-action convention printed in the paper: ω1=ω and (ωg)h=ω(gh). The orbit of a group action and stabilizer are
Orb(ω)={ωg:g∈G},H=Stab(ω)={g∈G:ωg=ω}.
(1)
The identity belongs to H, products of elements of H fix ω, and ωg=ω implies ωg−1=ω. Thus H is a subgroup. For k∈G,
k∈Stab(ωg)⟺ωgkg−1=ω⟺gkg−1∈H,
(2)
proving Stab(ωg)=g−1Hg.
The rotational action on the eight vertices of a cube is transitive. Model the vertices as (±1,±1,±1). For any desired sign triple, choose a permutation whose parity makes the corresponding signed permutation matrix have determinant one; it maps (1,1,1) to that vertex and preserves the cube. A rotation fixing a vertex fixes the axis through that vertex, the centre and the opposite vertex. It cyclically permutes the three incident edges, giving precisely rotations by 0,2π/3,4π/3. Thus the stabilizer is cyclic of order three.
