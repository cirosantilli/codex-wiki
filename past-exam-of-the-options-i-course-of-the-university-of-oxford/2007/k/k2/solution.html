The Milne-Thomson circle theorem replaces a potential f analytic inside the circle of radius a by f(z)+f(a2/zˉ)​ outside it, when its original singularities are outside. The circle becomes a streamline, the imposed flow at infinity is preserved, and no extra circulation is introduced.
Use the convention that positive circulation is counterclockwise, so a vortex potential is Γlog(z−z1​)/(2πi) and its derivative is u−iv. With b=a2/zˉ1​, the circle construction gives
w(z)=2πiΓ​{log(z−z1​)−log(z−b)+logz}​
(30)
up to an additive constant and local logarithm branches. There is an image vortex of strength −Γ at b and a central vortex of strength Γ. Their zero net interior circulation is needed for the specified motion equation.
The original vortex moves with the regular velocity of the image flow, excluding its own singular velocity. Thus
dtdzˉ1​​=2πiΓ​{−z1​−a2/zˉ1​1​+z1​1​}=2πz1​(∣z1​∣2−a2)iΓa2​​.
(31)
The conjugation in this equation matches the printed source. Write z1​=reiθ and cancel e−iθ to obtain
r˙−irθ˙=2πr(r2−a2)iΓa2​.
(32)
Real and imaginary parts give
r˙=0,θ˙=−2πr2(r2−a2)Γa2​​.
(33)
The vortex moves uniformly on the circle determined by its initial radius, clockwise for positive Γ in this convention.
