A streamline is a curve whose tangent is parallel to the instantaneous fluid velocity field. In steady flow one may parametrize it by dr/ds=u(r). Dotting the steady Euler equations with u eliminates the cross-product term, since u⋅((∇×u)×u)=0. Hence
u⋅∇(p+21​ρ∣u∣2)=0,​
(30)
which is Bernoulli's principle along each streamline.
For the supplied field, xu+yv=0, so along a streamline
dsd​(x2+y2)=2xu+2yv=0.
(31)
Writing r2=x2+y2, the tangential speed is vθ​=−br+c/r and the radial speed is zero. Both r and ∣u∣2=(−br+c/r)2 are constant around each nonstagnant circular streamline. The Bernoulli quantity is constant there, so the pressure is constant on every such circle.
If a circle is wholly stagnant, the same conclusion follows directly from the steady Euler equations: on it u=0 and ∇∣u∣2=0, hence ∇p=0. Equivalently the radial momentum equation gives ∇p=ρ(−br+c/r)2er​/r and no angular pressure variation, including at zero tangential speed. Thus p is constant on x2+y2=a2 for every nonzero a, with no exception for a stagnation circle.
