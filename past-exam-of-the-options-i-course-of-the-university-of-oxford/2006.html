A1 OurBigBook.com
Write the group action as (ω,g)↦ωg. A right group action satisfies ω1=ω and (ωg)h=ω(gh). Its orbit and stabilizer are
Orb(ω)={ωg:g∈G},H=Stab(ω)={g∈G:ωg=ω}.
(1)
The identity belongs to H. If g,h∈H, then ωgh=(ωg)h=ω, so gh∈H. Also ωg=ω implies ω=(ωg)g−1=ωg−1, so g−1∈H. Thus H is a subgroup.
For the orbit-stabilizer theorem, the map G→Orb(ω), g↦ωg, is onto, and
ωg=ωh⟺gh−1∈H⟺g∈Hh.
(2)
Its fibers are precisely the right cosets Hh, each containing ∣H∣ elements. Counting these disjoint fibers gives
∣Orb(ω)∣=∣G∣/∣H∣​.
(3)
For the symmetric group S3​, the six subgroups are
{1},⟨(12)⟩,⟨(13)⟩,⟨(23)⟩,A3​={1,(123),(132)},S3​.
(4)
The group action H↦g−1Hg has four orbits: each of {1}, A3​, and S3​ is fixed, while the three order-two subgroups form one orbit. Here an orbit is a collection of subgroups, rather than a collection of individual permutations.

Since K is an F-vector subspace of the finite-dimensional vector space L, it has a finite F-basis u1​,…,um​. Any finite F-spanning set for L also spans L over K, so choose a finite K-basis v1​,…,vr​ of L.

Put E=F(a,b). The tower law for field extensions shows that both m and n divide [E:F]. Since m,n are coprime, their product divides [E:F], so [E:F]≥mn.
The minimal polynomial of b over F has degree n and is still an annihilating polynomial over F(a). The minimal polynomial over F(a) divides it, so
[E:F(a)]≤n,[E:F]=[E:F(a)]m≤mn.
(7)
The upper and lower bounds agree:
[F(a,b):F]=mn​.
(8)
Coprimality is the step forcing the lower bound to equal the product; without it the two generated fields can overlap substantially.

Use the agreed text encoding to split the plaintext into integer blocks mj​∈{0,…,1072}. RSA encrypts each block by modular exponentiation:
rj​≡mj11​(mod1073).
(9)
Because 1073=29⋅37 is a product of distinct primes, its Euler totient function is φ(1073)=28⋅36=1008. The inverse exponent follows from
11⋅275=3025=1+3⋅1008.
(10)
Thus a decryption exponent is 275, and the three recovered blocks are
mj​≡rj275​(mod1073),0≤mj​<1073,j=1,2,3​.
(11)

Solution

Words: 134
E1 OurBigBook.com
A complete metric space is a metric space in which every Cauchy sequence converges to a point of that space.
If xn​→x, choose N so that d(xn​,x)<ε/2 for all n≥N. The triangle inequality gives d(xm​,xn​)≤d(xm​,x)+d(x,xn​)<ε, proving the Cauchy sequence property.
Now let A be complete in the induced metric. If x∈A, choose an​∈A with d(an​,x)<1/n. The sequence converges to x in X, so is a Cauchy sequence. Completeness of A supplies a limit a∈A. Uniqueness of limits in a metric space gives a=x, hence A⊆A. Therefore a complete metric subspace is closed.
Completeness does not imply sequential compactness. In the complete space R, the sequence xn​=n has no convergent subsequence: a subsequence has strictly increasing indices nk​≥k, so xnk​​→+∞ and is unbounded, whereas every convergent real sequence is bounded.

A topological space is connected when it cannot be written as the union of two disjoint nonempty open sets. Equivalently, the only subsets that are simultaneously open and closed are the empty set and the whole space. For a subset, connectedness refers to its subspace topology.

i OurBigBook.com
Two states lie in the same communicating class when each is reachable from the other by transitions of positive probability. Inspecting the directed transitions gives three classes:
{1},{2,3,6,7},{4,5,8}.
(45)
State 1 has no incoming transition from another state and can leave, so its singleton class is open and transient. More explicitly, the probability of returning to 1 after time zero is only 0.1, since a departure can never be reversed.
States 2,3,6,7 communicate: 2→3→7→2 and 2→6→7 are positive-probability paths. The class is open because, for example, 2→8 and 6→4 leave it; no transition from the class {4,5,8} returns to it. Every state of {2,3,6,7} is transient. One can see eventual exit directly: from each of its four states there is a positive-probability path of at most three steps to 8; the minimum of these four path probabilities is positive, so survival through repeated three-step blocks has a geometrically decreasing upper bound.
Finally 4→8→5→4 shows that {4,5,8} communicates, and every outgoing transition stays inside it. It is a closed finite irreducible class, so all its states are positive recurrent. Therefore the first two classes are open and transient; the last is closed and recurrent.

Assume each sampled response is truthful, card selection is independent of the student's status, and responses follow the usual independent sampling model. In randomized response, a yes answer occurs either for a user receiving the first question or for a nonuser receiving the complementary question. Its probability is
q=xp+(1−x)(1−p)=(1−x)+(2x−1)p​.
(51)
Consequently Y∼Bin(n,q), and the likelihood is
L(p)=(Yn​)[(1−x)+(2x−1)p]Y[x−(2x−1)p]n−Y,0≤p≤1.
(52)
For x=1/2, first maximize the binomial likelihood in the unrestricted probability q: its maximum occurs at Y/n. Inverting the affine relation gives
p​=n(2x−1)Y−n(1−x)​.
(53)
For an interior value 0<Y<n, the same result follows from the score
ℓ′(p)=(2x−1)q(1−q)Y−nq​.
(54)
The endpoint cases follow by monotonicity. This estimator has expected value p and variance
Ep​=p,Var(p​)=n(2x−1)2q(1−q)​=np(1−p)​+n(2x−1)2x(1−x)​​.
(55)
These identities follow immediately from EY=nq and Var(Y)=nq(1−q).
The printed claim needs a parameter-space qualification. The admissible yes probabilities lie in [min(x,1−x),max(x,1−x)], which may exclude Y/n. The constrained maximum-likelihood estimator is
p​ML​=min{1,max{0,p​}}​.
(56)
The displayed untrimmed estimator is the maximum-likelihood estimator only when it already lies in [0,1]. Its unbiased estimator property does not carry over to the clipped estimator in general. For example, if n=1, x=3/4 and p=0, the clipped estimate equals 1 exactly when the answer is yes, so its expectation is 1/4, not zero. At x=1/2, q=1/2 for every p: the parameter is unidentifiable and the printed quotient is undefined. Thus the unbiased formula requires x=1/2, and the constrained likelihood assertion requires the stated clipping.

The Lagrange interpolation polynomial constructs existence explicitly:
ℓj​(x)=∏0≤k≤nk=j​​xj​−xk​x−xk​​,p(x)=∑j=0n​fj​ℓj​(x).
