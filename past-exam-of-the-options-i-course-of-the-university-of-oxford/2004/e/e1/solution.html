A metric space is a set X with a function d:X×X→[0,∞) satisfying d(x,y)=0⟺x=y, symmetry and the triangle inequality. A subset U is open if every x∈U has some open open ball B(x,r) contained in U.
For finitely many open sets U1​,…,Uk​ and x in their intersection, choose radii rj​>0 with B(x,rj​)⊆Uj​. The positive minimum radius gives a ball contained in the intersection. Thus every finite intersection is open; the empty intersection is X, also open.
For distinct points put δ=d(x1​,x2​)>0. The balls B(x1​,δ/3) and B(x2​,δ/3) are disjoint: a common point would make d(x1​,x2​)<2δ/3 by the triangle inequality. This proves the Hausdorff condition.
A singleton {x} is closed because each y=x has a ball of radius d(x,y)/2 avoiding it, making its complement open. Finite unions of closed sets are closed, as their complements are finite intersections of open sets. Hence every finite subset is closed, including the empty subset.
