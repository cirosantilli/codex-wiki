The quadratic reciprocity law states that distinct odd primes a,b satisfy
(ba​)(ab​)=(−1)((a−1)/2)((b−1)/2).
(4)
First suppose p=q. If the odd prime r equalled one of them, divisibility of p−q by r would force the other to equal r too, a contradiction. Thus reciprocity applies to r,p and r,q. The modular congruence modulo 4r gives both p≡q(modr) and p≡q(mod4). Therefore (p/r)=(q/r), and the reciprocity signs are identical. Rearranging the two reciprocity identities proves (r/p)=(r/q)​. If p=q, the equality is immediate, including a zero Legendre symbol when p=r.

(pn​)=∏j​(prj​​)ej​=∏j​(qrj​​)ej​=(qn​)​.
