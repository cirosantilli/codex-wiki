For the isolated line vortex, use polar coordinates z=reiθ to obtain
ϕ=2πΓ​θ,ψ=−2πΓ​logr,ur​=0,uθ​=2πrΓ​.
(42)
Thus for nonzero Γ the streamlines are circles and their counterclockwise circulation is 2πruθ​=Γ. If Γ=0 the fluid is stationary and the streamline description degenerates.
Place an opposite image vortex at z=−1. The method of images gives
w(z)=−2πiΓ​(log(z−1)−log(z+1))​.
(43)
On z=iy, the distances to the two vortex positions agree, so ψ=−(Γ/2π)log(∣z−1∣/∣z+1∣)=0. Hence the wall is a streamline. Directly the wall velocity has u=0 and
v(0,y)=−π(1+y2)Γ​.
(44)
For the stipulated steady flow, Bernoulli's equation in the irrotational fluid outside the vortex core gives
p(0,y)−p∞​=−21​ρv(0,y)2=−2π2(1+y2)2ρΓ2​.
(45)
The force exerted by the fluid on this wall points in the −x direction for positive pressure. Relative to uniform pressure p∞​, however, its change is in the +x direction. Using the supplied integral, the force change per unit span is
ΔFwall​=4πρΓ2​ex​​.
(46)
Thus the wall is attracted towards the vortex, with this magnitude if the other side remains at p∞​. Subtracting the uniform pressure is essential for an infinite wall. A freely moving vortex would translate parallel to the wall under its image-induced velocity; the calculation here is for the stated steady vortex configuration, with its position held fixed.
