By continuity of the cumulative distribution function and nonnegativity, F(0)=0; strict increase and F(x)→1 allow inversion for every 0<t<1. The probability integral transform follows from
P(F(X)≤t)=P(X≤F−1(t))=t.
(60)
Thus F(X) is uniformly distributed on (0,1).
The order statistics arrange the sample increasingly: X(1)​≤⋯≤X(n)​. Strict monotonicity means F(X(i)​) is the ith order statistic of n independent uniform variables. Its density is
(i−1)!(n−i)!n!​ti−1(1−t)n−i,0<t<1,
(61)
obtained by choosing the i−1 observations below t and the n−i above it. This is the beta distribution with parameters i,n+1−i, so
EF(X(i)​)=n+1i​​.
(62)
For the given log-logistic distribution, rearrangement gives
log1−F(x)F(x)​=(β−1)logx−(β−1)logα.
(63)
Consequently plot yi​=log(i/(n+1−i)) vertically against xi∗​=logX(i)​ horizontally. Under a good fit, replacing F(X(i)​) by its plotting position i/(n+1) gives an approximate straight line with
slope=β−1,intercept=−(β−1)logα​.
(64)
This is a diagnostic probability plot, rather than an assertion that the expectation of the nonlinear transformed order statistic equals that transformed plotting position. Positive observations occur almost surely under the model.
