(1)
In the action on ordered pairs there are exactly two orbits. One is the diagonal, transitive because the action on Ω is transitive. Faithfulness of the action is not required.

Eisenstein criterion says that a primitive integer polynomial is irreducible over Q if some prime divides all coefficients except the leading one, does not divide the leading coefficient, and its square does not divide the constant coefficient. The quadratic reciprocity law states that distinct odd primes a,b satisfy
(ba​)(ab​)=(−1)((a−1)/2)((b−1)/2).
(4)
First suppose p=q. If the odd prime r equalled one of them, divisibility of p−q by r would force the other to equal r too, a contradiction. Thus reciprocity applies to r,p and r,q. The modular congruence modulo 4r gives both p≡q(modr) and p≡q(mod4). Therefore (p/r)=(q/r), and the reciprocity signs are identical. Rearranging the two reciprocity identities proves (r/p)=(r/q)​. If p=q, the equality is immediate, including a zero Legendre symbol when p=r.
For general odd n>1, factor n=∏j​rjej​​. If p=q there is nothing to prove. Otherwise neither p nor q divides n, since any such divisor, together with 4n∣p−q, would force p=q. Each 4rj​ divides p−q, so the first result and multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol give
(pn​)=∏j​(prj​​)ej​=∏j​(qrj​​)ej​=(qn​)​.
(5)
For n=1, both symbols equal one.

A basis for a topology is a family of open sets such that every open set is a union of members of the family. Equivalently, for every point in an open set there is a basis member containing that point and contained in the open set.
For a basis B on Y, continuity is equivalent to f−1(B) being open for every B∈B. Necessity follows because basis sets are open. Conversely, taking preimages of a union of basis sets gives a union of open preimages, so every open set has an open preimage.

A subset is connected when it has no separation into two disjoint nonempty relatively open sets. It is path-connected when every pair of its points can be joined by a continuous path from [0,1] lying in the subset.
Path-connectedness implies connectedness; the reverse fails in general, for example for the topologist's sine curve. They are equivalent for open subsets of every Euclidean space Rn. More generally this holds for open subsets of locally path-connected spaces.

The Markov property says that conditional on the present state, the next-state law does not depend on the earlier history. For this time-homogeneous chain, P(Xn+1​=j∣X0​=i0​,…,Xn​=i)=pij​ whenever the conditioning event has positive probability.
States communicate when each is accessible from the other by a finite positive-probability path; the equivalence classes are the communicating classes. A class is closed if no one-step transition leaves it, and open otherwise. Probability nonnegativity adds the implicit restriction p≥0 to the printed upper bound.
At p=0, every state is absorbing, so the classes are the singletons, all closed. For 0<p≤1/2, every neighbouring pair communicates, so the whole state space is one closed class, making the chain irreducible. There are no open classes in either case.
For m=3 and p>0, detailed balance along the path gives 2pπ0​=pπ1​, π1​=π2​, and pπ2​=2pπ3​. After normalization,
π=(1,2,2,1)/6​.
(39)
For 0<p<1/2, the self-loops have positive probability; the finite irreducible chain is aperiodic, so Pi​(Xn​=j)→πj​ for all fixed starting states and targets. For p=1/2, every step changes parity. The chain has period two, and for each fixed i,j one parity of times has probability zero while πj​>0. Thus the stated convergence from a fixed starting state holds exactly when 0<p<1/2. This is the stationary law of a reflecting nearest-neighbor walk on a finite path.
