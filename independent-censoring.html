Independent censoring

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Survival analysis Censoring (statistics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 444 Articles: 5
A simple sufficient independent censoring assumption is that the potential observation-ending time C and survival time T are independent random variables. More generally, appropriate conditional independence given the covariates used in the analysis can suffice for analysis within those conditions. The requirement concerns the potential times, rather than independence of the observed duration Y=min(T,C) and event indicator δ: those recorded quantities ordinarily depend on each other.
For an ordinary pooled Kaplan–Meier estimator, the observed risk set must represent the future event experience of everyone still event-free at each time. Conditional independence given a covariate does not alone justify ignoring that covariate in a pooled calculation. Also, there must be a positive probability of remaining observed through the horizons being estimated. If follow-up ends at a common closing time, it supplies no observations to identify the later survival function. Independent subjects with a common survival distribution and independent censoring form the basic model in Lu Tian's STAT331 notes, Unit 3, pages 1–4.
For example, subjects who stop attending because their condition is deteriorating can produce informative censoring. A predetermined common closing date can instead give administrative censoring, provided calendar entry and other relevant conditions do not select future survival times. The reason for stopping observation must be justified from study design and context; the recorded event indicators alone cannot verify independent censoring.
