Direction arises from switching between mutually incompatible states, with feedback providing memory and regulated destruction resetting the system. Different transitions use related logic, but a steep response, a bistable switch and an irreversible molecular event are not identical. The cell cycle also requires checkpoints and once-per-cycle licensing so that the ordered biochemical states produce a correct sequence of physical events.
At the G1/S transition, growth signals promote cyclin-dependent kinase activity and relieve RB-mediated restraint on E2F. E2F-dependent expression of additional cell-cycle components can reinforce the transition. More generally, activation of a kinase together with removal of its inhibitor creates positive feedback. In yeast, multisite phosphorylation of Sic1 permits recognition by the Cdc4 ubiquitin-ligase system and degradation of an inhibitor of S-phase kinase. This supplies a threshold that prevents weak or premature signals from launching the next programme.[ref] The detailed regulators vary across organisms and biological cell types; the conserved point is a coordinated transition from an inhibited state to one that reinforces its own activation.
Mitotic entry gives an experimentally accessible example of bistability. cyclin B binding increases the available CDK1 complex, but Wee1-dependent inhibitory phosphorylation can keep it inactive. Cdc25 removes the inhibitory phosphate. Active CDK1 promotes Cdc25 activity and suppresses Wee1, giving a positive-feedback loop and a double-negative loop. Over a suitable input range, both a low-activity and a high-activity state can be stable. If cyclin input rises from interphase, activation occurs at a higher threshold than the input required to maintain a pre-existing mitotic state. These distinct thresholds constitute hysteresis and prevent small fluctuations from repeatedly reversing a committed transition.
In egg extracts, nondegradable cyclin could be supplied while CDK activity was approached from different initial states. The same cyclin input then supported different persistent activities depending on history, directly distinguishing hysteresis from a single steep but history-independent response.[ref] These are extract measurements, not universal numerical thresholds for every intact biological cell. Reset is achieved by coupling activation to delayed negative feedback and proteolysis. Mitotic kinase activity helps prepare activation of the anaphase-promoting complex with Cdc20, while the spindle checkpoint prevents productive progression until attachment conditions are met. Once this restraint is satisfied, APC/C-dependent ubiquitination promotes destruction of securin and cyclin B. securin loss allows separase to cleave cohesin, making sister-chromatid separation possible. Experiments identifying cohesin cleavage connected regulated proteolysis to a physical transition that simple reversal of a phosphorylation state cannot undo.[ref] Chromatids cannot be returned to a pre-anaphase chromosome merely by reducing separase activity.
cyclin B destruction lowers CDK1 activity, allowing phosphatases to reverse mitotic phosphorylation and promoting an interphase state. Activation of anaphase-promoting complex with Cdh1 at low kinase activity can help keep cyclins low, while high kinase activity opposes that state. Thus low-CDK and high-CDK programmes are mutually antagonistic. Cyclin synthesis eventually provides a new activating input. Early experiments in extracts showed the importance of cyclin synthesis and destruction, including failure of normal exit with nondegradable cyclin.[ref] A bistable activation module combined with an appropriate delayed reset can generate an ordered oscillation; a bistable module held at constant input need not oscillate on its own.
replication licensing enforces another essential directionality. At low CDK activity, origin recognition complex with Cdc6 and Cdt1 loads MCM2-7 double hexamers, establishing competence to replicate. In S phase, activating kinases promote origin firing while high CDK activity and additional controls prevent renewed licensing. In animal biological cells, geminin inhibition of Cdt1 and regulated degradation help separate these states. Purified-protein reconstitution demonstrated loading of the replication-helicase precursor as a distinct biochemical event.[ref] Separating licensing from firing allows many origins to be prepared but prevents fired regions from being copied repeatedly during the same cycle. Lowering mitotic kinase activity must eventually restore licensing competence for the next cycle, rather than permit uncontrolled rereplication during the current S phase.
Checkpoint pathways modify or hold these switches when DNA is unreplicated, damaged or chromosomes are improperly attached; they do not supply the entire oscillator. Energy-consuming phosphorylation cycles, ubiquitination, proteolysis and resynthesis make the system a driven process, while feedback stabilizes distinct states. Thresholds provide commitment, hysteresis provides memory, destruction and cleavage enforce selected transitions, and licensing/checkpoints couple those transitions to one correct round of replication and segregation.
