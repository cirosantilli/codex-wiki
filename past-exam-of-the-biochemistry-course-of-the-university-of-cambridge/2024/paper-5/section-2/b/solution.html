OurBigBook.com Words: 117
The detectable cytoskeleton effect is substantially reversible after washout. Depolarized actin patches reappear first, reaching roughly 85% of biological cells near 20 minutes. Polarized actin patches subsequently increase to about 80%, while detectable actin cables recover later, reaching roughly 70–75% by the end of the time course. This argues against irreversible destruction of all cellular capacity to assemble actin.
Recovery is not instantaneous, and the population measurements do not prove that every previously treated biological cell recovers. Nor do they distinguish reuse of the existing actin pool from a contribution of new protein synthesis. A time-matched vehicle culture and a continuously treated culture would separate washout-dependent recovery from changes caused by handling or time alone. Reversibility of visible structures also does not by itself establish recovery of every physiological function.
