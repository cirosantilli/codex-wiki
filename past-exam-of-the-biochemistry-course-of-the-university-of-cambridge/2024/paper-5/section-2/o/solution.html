Efficient and timely budding requires the normal actin network, but some initial polarized cell growth remains possible when visible actin filaments are greatly depleted. Around 40% of control biological cells have new buds by two hours, rising to nearly 60% by six hours. Drug-treated biological cells show almost no budding at two hours but roughly 15–20% at four to six hours, and the phase images confirm small buds despite loss of normal bright actin structures.
The result therefore supports a large delay and reduction, rather than an absolute block to every initiation event. Polarity cues or localized membrane-fusion machinery could remain spatially organized without normal long actin cables. The images and bud counts do not establish completion of a daughter-cell cycle, normal cytokinesis or indefinite proliferation. Residual actin filaments below detection remain an alternative contributor. This distinction between initial bud emergence and completion of growth was directly examined in a quiescence-exit study.[ref]
