 ... Biochemistry course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the biochemistry course of the University of Cambridge 2024 Paper 5 Section 2 l
OurBigBook.com Words: 164
Test whether cellular drug effects persist when actin is protected by a drug-resistant allele. Compare otherwise matched wild-type and act1-117 biological cells across a drug-concentration range, measuring growth or colony formation together with actin organization and a relevant cellular phenotype. Each strain needs a vehicle-only baseline because the mutant already has abnormal intrinsic assembly. If the resistant strain maintains its actin network and the normal actin-dependent functions but still develops another reproducible defect, that residual effect is a candidate off-target drug effect. Comparable intracellular exposure is important: resistance caused by lower uptake would not protect only the intended target.
Conversely, coordinated rescue of the actin and growth phenotypes supports an on-target explanation over that dose range, without proving the absence of every other molecular interaction. Reintroducing wild-type actin or using another independently resistant allele helps separate the actin substitution from unrelated genetic background effects. A candidate secondary target would need its own binding or functional assay and a selective rescue test. A second actin inhibitor giving the same phenotype is supportive but cannot alone exclude shared secondary effects.
