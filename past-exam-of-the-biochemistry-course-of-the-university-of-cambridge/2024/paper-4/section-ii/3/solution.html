Maturation best explains major changes in Golgi cisternal identity and early-to-late endosomal progression, but neither pathway is a conveyor belt with no stable domains or transport carriers. The useful comparison concerns what persists—membrane, cargo or resident machinery—and what the experiments actually follow.
In a stable-compartment model, each compartment retains its processing identity while cargo moves onward in vesicles or tubules. In cisternal maturation, cargo can remain within a cisterna while its resident enzymes and trafficking machinery change, turning an early cisterna into a later one. This requires selective recycling of resident machinery in the opposite direction. Both models therefore depend on membrane trafficking; observing vesicles alone does not distinguish them. A static image of separate cis and trans markers is likewise compatible with a population of maturing cisternae.
A decisive experiment follows an individual structure over time using early and late resident markers. In budding yeast, live imaging showed initially early-marker-positive Golgi cisternae losing that marker and acquiring a late marker. This is strong evidence for changing cisternal identity within the tracked object.[ref] A complementary experiment follows synchronized cargo together with residents. Cargo retention while residents are replaced favours maturation; cargo movement between objects whose residents remain stable favours transfer between persistent processing domains.
Large cargo makes a strong mechanistic test. A long procollagen assembly cannot simply be assumed to enter a small conventional coated vesicle. Maturation can move such cargo through the stack without packaging it into small forward carriers at every step. However, cargo size does not by itself prove that every soluble cargo uses the same route. Tubular connections, larger carriers and rapid exchange of small cargo can coexist with maturation. The geometry and spatial resolution of stacked mammalian Golgi also differ from those of dispersed yeast cisternae, so one should not transfer every kinetic detail between systems without testing it.
For the Golgi, the strongest synthesis is therefore a dynamic population of processing compartments with changing identity, coupled to resident recycling and cargo-dependent transport. COPI and other retrieval pathways can maintain enzyme gradients while COPII supplies new material from the endoplasmic reticulum. A named cis, medial or trans region can persist statistically even when its component membranes and proteins do not. Bulk transport, rapid cargo exchange and local stable zones need not be mutually exclusive.
The endocytic pathway poses a related but distinct problem. An early endosome receives material from the surface and separates recycling cargo from cargo retained for degradation. A strictly stable early-endosome model predicts that degradative material must leave in carriers while early identity persists. A maturation model predicts that a cargo-bearing domain changes into a late endosome, alongside removal of material that should recycle.
Time-resolved Rab5 and Rab7 measurements demonstrated replacement of an early Rab machinery by a late one on cargo-bearing endosomes. This Rab conversion provides direct evidence for maturation rather than inferring it solely from differently labeled endpoint compartments.[ref] The associated changes in lipids, acidity, motility, fusion machinery and internal membrane organization make this a functional transition, rather than a marker swap alone.
Yet recycling domains can bud or tubulate from a maturing endosome. Some cargos return to the plasma membrane; others use retromer-associated retrieval routes; still others enter intralumenal vesicles and continue toward lysosomes. A maturing degradative region can therefore coexist with relatively persistent sorting activities elsewhere in the same network. Endosome fusion and fission also mean that an object tracked by microscopy may exchange membrane extensively.
To discriminate mechanisms, combine live tracking of residents and cargo with pulse-chase kinetics, acute perturbations of retrieval machinery and quantitative membrane accounting. Apparent marker conversion caused by two unresolved objects crossing must be excluded. Conversely, failure to observe conversion at low time resolution is not proof of stability. The evidence favours maturation as a major organizing principle, with selective sorting, retrieval and exchange explaining how cells retain steady trafficking functions while individual compartments change.
