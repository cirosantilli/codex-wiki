Complex plane (C≅R2)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Complex number
OurBigBook.com Words: 482 Articles: 5
The complex plane identifies the complex number x+iy with the point (x,y) in Cartesian coordinates. The horizontal coordinate is its real part, the vertical coordinate its imaginary part. Arrows from zero identify the same points with vectors. Adding 1+i moves 3+4i to 4+5i: every point undergoes the same translation.
Multiplication has a richer geometry. Multiplying by the imaginary unit sends x+iy to −y+ix, a counterclockwise quarter-turn rotation. In particular i(3+4i)=−4+3i. Multiplying by 1+i rotates through π/4 and scales every Euclidean distance by 2​. More generally, multiplication by a+ib acts through the real matrix
(xy​)⟼(ab​−ba​)(xy​).
(159)
For a+ib=0, this preserves oriented angles between nonzero displacement vectors: every direction turns equally. Its determinant is a2+b2>0, while its common scale is a2+b2​. This is the elementary geometric reason complex numbers are so useful for conformal maps.
The name Argand plane recalls Jean-Robert Argand's geometric interpretation. In his 1813 article he explicitly reports publishing his Essai in 1806; the original article is available in Argand, Annales de mathématiques, volume 4, page 133. A [ref] preserves the essay and its geometric drawings. Reading these alongside a modern complex plane reveals how directions and lengths give arithmetic a visible meaning.
