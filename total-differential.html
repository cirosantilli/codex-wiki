Total differential (df)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus Partial derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 286
The total differential is the linear map that combines the first-order responses to simultaneous changes in all coordinates. For a differentiable real-valued function at a, it can be written
dfa​(h)=∑j=1n​∂j​f(a)hj​=∇f(a)⋅h,df=∑j=1n​∂xj​∂f​dxj​.
(12)
Here h is an input vector; dxj​ denotes the coordinate linear functional h↦hj​. The coordinate formula and the Fréchet derivative describe the same linear map. The defining approximation is
f(a+h)−f(a)=dfa​(h)+o(∥h∥)(h→0).
(13)
The little o notation says the remaining approximation error becomes small relative to the input norm, across every direction of approach.[ref]
For f=x2+xy+2y2 at a=(1,1), dfa​(h,k)=3h+5k. A simultaneous change (h,k)=(0.02,−0.01) gives the linear approximation
Δf≈3(0.02)+5(−0.01)=0.01​.
(14)
The exact algebra is
f(1+h,1+k)−4=3h+5k+h2+hk+2k2.
(15)
Consequently the exact change is 0.0104, and the approximation error is 0.0004. If both input changes are halved, the linear prediction becomes 0.005 and the error becomes 0.0001: the first-order response halves, while the quadratic error falls by a factor of four. The right panel under directional derivative displays this comparison as the displacement is scaled.
For a quick check of differentiability, ∣hk∣≤(h2+k2)/2 gives
∣h2+hk+2k2∣≤25​(h2+k2).
(16)
Dividing by h2+k2​ makes this bound tend to zero. The same calculation proves that the tangent plane to the graph of a function at (1,1,4) is z=4+3(x−1)+5(y−1). It approximates the entire local surface, rather than only two coordinate slices.
The total differential is useful for estimating the combined effect of small input changes without reevaluating a complicated function. For example, if ∣h∣≤0.02 and ∣k∣≤0.01, then ∣dfa​(h,k)∣≤0.11. The exact remainder bound from the displayed polynomial is at most 0.0008, so this example has ∣Δf∣≤0.1108 throughout that input rectangle. These are deterministic bounds; a variance calculation additionally needs a joint probability distribution and its covariances. A first-order estimate is also local: large changes require control of the remainder, not merely knowledge of the partial derivatives at one point.
