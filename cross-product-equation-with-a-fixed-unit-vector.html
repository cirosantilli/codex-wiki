Cross product equation with a fixed unit vector (r×g=f⟺r=g×f+tg,f⋅g=0)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Vector Cross product
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
For a fixed unit vector g∈R3, the linear map r↦r×g has kernel Rg and image the perpendicular plane g⊥. Thus r×g=f is solvable exactly when f⋅g=0, and then its full solution set is
r=g×f+tg,t∈R.
(22)
For the proof, a cross product with g must be perpendicular to g. Conversely the vector triple product identity gives g×(r×g)=r−(r⋅g)g, which determines the perpendicular orthogonal projection of r. The remaining parallel component is arbitrary. Finally (g×f)×g=f when f⋅g=0, verifying sufficiency.
Geometrically, the solution set is a straight line with direction g. Its nearest point to the origin is g×f and its distance from the origin is ∣f∣. For a general nonzero fixed vector a, the corresponding formula is r=(a×f)/∣a∣2+ta, again subject to f⋅a=0.
