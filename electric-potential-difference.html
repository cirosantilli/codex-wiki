Electric potential difference (VAB​=ϕ(A)−ϕ(B))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric field Electric potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 294
An electric potential difference compares electric potentials at two points. For a move from a to b, define ΔΦ=Φ(b)−Φ(a); the terminal convention VAB​=Φ(A)−Φ(B) instead means the potential at A relative to B, so the order must always be stated. In electrostatics, the line integral of the electric field gives
ΔΦ=−∫ab​E⋅dl,ΔU=qΔΦ,Wfield​=q∫ab​E⋅dl=q(Φ(a)−Φ(b))=−ΔU.
(115)
Here ΔU is the change in interaction potential energy of the test electric charge q with fixed sources, while Wfield​ is work done by the electric field. For an external agent moving the charge slowly without a change in kinetic energy, its work is ΔU. A positive electric charge gains kinetic energy when accelerated toward lower electric potential; a negative electric charge gains kinetic energy when accelerated toward higher electric potential. Both motions lower qΦ.[ref]
For example, fix Q=+2nC at the origin and compare distances ra​=0.20m and rb​=0.10m. With the stipulated approximation k=8.99×109Nm2/C2 and zero at infinity,
Φ(a)=ra​kQ​=89.9V,Φ(b)=rb​kQ​=179.8V,ΔΦ=89.9V.
(116)
Moving q=−3nC inward therefore gives
Ua​=−2.697×10−7J,Ub​=−5.394×10−7J,ΔU=−2.697×10−7J,Wfield​=+2.697×10−7J.
(117)
If only the electric force does work, conservation of energy makes the gain in kinetic energy 2.697×10−7J. Direct integration confirms the signs: ∫ra​rb​​kQdr/r2=kQ(1/ra​−1/rb​)=−89.9V, which multiplied by the negative test electric charge gives positive work. The electric potential difference is unchanged by choosing a different electrical reference potential.
In an electrical circuit, an electric potential difference is the voltage across a component's terminals. In an electrical network, a vertex voltage is specified relative to a reference vertex; its differences across edges drive current flow. For a uniform electric field, the integral reduces to ΔΦ=−E⋅(rb​−ra​). A field E=300V/mx across a displacement 0.04mx therefore gives ΔΦ=−12V, as in the interior approximation for a parallel-plate capacitor. The volt, measured in joules per coulomb, expresses energy transfer per unit electric charge, which makes it useful for comparing components without specifying the amount of charge transported.
