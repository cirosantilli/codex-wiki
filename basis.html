Basis

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space
OurBigBook.com Words: 677 Articles: 12
A basis is a linearly independent spanning set of vectors in a vector space. Every vector is a unique finite linear combination of basis vectors. Independence supplies uniqueness, and spanning supplies existence. An ordered basis turns the coefficients into a coordinate vector.
For B=(b1​,b2​)=((2,1),(1,2)), the matrix with these vectors as columns is
B=(21​12​),B−1=31​(2−1​−12​).
(12)
Hence the coordinate vector of v=(5,4) is [v]B​=B−1v=(2,1), and B[v]B​=v recovers the usual coordinates. The determinant 3 is nonzero, so this recovery works for every vector in R2. A basis need not have perpendicular or unit-length vectors; an orthonormal basis adds those geometric conveniences when an inner product is available.
The point of changing basis is to simplify the operation being studied. Diagonalization uses an eigenbasis to turn a coupled linear map into independent scalings. In a space of polynomials, the basis 1,x,x2 makes differentiation easy to encode, while orthogonal polynomials can make approximation errors easier to measure. Gilbert Strang's MIT lecture on independence, basis and dimension develops this connection between attainable columns, redundant columns and coordinates.[ref]
