NaCl conventional cell and coordination

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Crystal structure Atomic basis of a crystal
OurBigBook.com Words: 276
Ordinary solid sodium chloride has the rock salt structure. Choose a conventional cube of side a with chlorine anions at the corners and face centers, and sodium cations at the edge centers and cube center. Corner sites are shared by eight cubes, face sites by two and edge sites by four. The unit cell counts are therefore
NCl−​=8(81​)+6(21​)=4,NNa+​=12(41​)+1=4.
(10)
There are four formula units per conventional unit cell, rather than four separate NaCl molecules.[ref]
The Bravais lattice is a face-centered cubic lattice. One choice of primitive lattice translation vectors and atomic basis is
a1​=2a​(0,1,1),a2​=2a​(1,0,1),a3​=2a​(1,1,0),rCl−​=0,rNa+​=2a​(1,0,0).
(11)
The determinant gives a primitive unit cell volume of a3/4; that cell represents one cation and one anion. Four such volumes make the conventional cube. The union of all ion positions resembles a primitive cubic lattice of step a/2 only if the chemical labels are discarded: translation by a(1,0,0)/2 exchanges unlike ions and fails to preserve the chemical structure.
Each ion has six nearest unlike neighbors at distance a/2, along the positive and negative directions of the three cube axes. Thus the coordination number is six. Within either same-species face-centered cubic lattice, the nearest same-ion neighbors instead number twelve: their displacements are permutations of (±a/2,±a/2,0), all of length a/2​. Since a/2​>a/2, those twelve are a more distant shell in the complete sodium chloride structure. The number twelve describes the same-ion FCC lattice neighborhood; six describes the nearest unlike-ion neighborhood.
