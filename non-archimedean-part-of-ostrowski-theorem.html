Non-Archimedean part of Ostrowski theorem (∣x∣=p−cvp​(x),c>0)

 ... Area of mathematics Arithmetic Non-Archimedean analysis P-adic number p-adic absolute value Ostrowski's theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 72
For a nontrivial Non-Archimedean absolute value on Q, every integer has absolute value at most one. Some prime p must have value less than one, or the value would be trivial on every rational number. Two distinct such primes are impossible by a Bezout identity. Prime factorization then gives the displayed formula. Distinct primes give inequivalent topologies, since powers of p tend to zero only for the p-adic absolute value among these values.
