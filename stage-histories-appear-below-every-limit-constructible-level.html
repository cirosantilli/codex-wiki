For a transitive set X, let fξ​ be the history function η↦Lη​(X) on ξ+1 in the relative constructible hierarchy. By transfinite induction, fξ​∈Lξ+k​(X) for some finite k, possibly depending on ξ. The base and successor steps follow by forming finite ordered pairs and adjoining the next entry, using Lξ+1​(X)∈Lξ+2​(X). These operations require finitely many subsequent definable power sets.
At a nonzero limit ordinal λ, the inductive bounds put every earlier fη​ in Lλ​(X), since η+k<λ. This level satisfies finite relation closure for set-theoretic coding, independently of the availability of history functions. Consequently its coded relative constructible stage predicate computes the endpoints correctly. Finite pairing closure and the earlier histories show that
h={⟨η,B⟩∈Lλ​(X):(Lλ​(X),∈)⊨SX​(η,B)}

Thus h=fλ​↾λ is a definable subset of Lλ​(X) and belongs to Lλ+1​(X). Extracting its domain λ and adjoining ⟨λ,Lλ​(X)⟩ uses finitely many further operations, proving the induction step. Every nonzero limit α therefore contains all fξ​ with ξ<α, as required by relative constructible level recognition.
