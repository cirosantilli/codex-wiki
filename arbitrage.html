Arbitrage (V0​=0,VT​≥0 P-a.s.,P(VT​>0)>0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Mathematical finance
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 24
An arbitrage is an allowed self-financing portfolio with zero initial wealth, nonnegative terminal portfolio wealth almost surely under the physical probability measure P, and strictly positive terminal wealth with positive probability. In a finite model where every state has positive probability, nonnegativity means no loss in any state. Positive expected return alone does not establish arbitrage: losses may still occur.
For the binomial market example, suppose the European call option sells for 13 while its replicating portfolio costs 250/21. Sell the call, buy its replicating portfolio, and lend the surplus 13−250/21=23/21 in the bank account. The initial net cost is zero. At maturity the call obligation and portfolio cancel in either state, leaving (21/20)(23/21)=23/20. A price below 250/21 permits the reversed construction, using short selling and borrowing. This is the law of one price made operational; the assumed trading permissions are essential.
