Killing vector field (K)

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 34
A Killing vector field describes a direction in which geometry has a continuous symmetry: moving points together along its flow of a vector field preserves the metric tensor, including its pairing of tangent vectors. Such a symmetry gives a geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector, often a component of momentum or angular momentum. The direction may vary with position: rotation provides the basic example.
On a Riemannian manifold or Lorentzian manifold with metric tensor g, a smooth vector field K is a Killing vector field when its Lie derivative of g vanishes. If Φs​ is its local flow of a vector field, the equivalent statements are
LK​g=0⟺Φs∗​g=g⟺∇(μ​Kν)​=0.
(1)
The middle equality uses the pullback of a covariant tensor: the local flow maps are isometries on their domains. The last equality is the Killing equation, using the Levi-Civita connection. The infinitesimal condition holds throughout an open domain and integrates to exact local isometries; it is stronger than making the first derivatives of g vanish at a single point. See Tong, section 4.3.1, equation (4.29).
For example, on the interval 0<x<1 with Euclidean metric dx2, K=∂x​ translates points by x↦x+s. This is a local isometry, but a point reaches the end of the interval in finite flow parameter. A local flow of a vector field can be incomplete; see Killing fields on incomplete manifolds need not generate global isometries. The full-plane translation and rotation examples under Euclidean Killing vector field instead have flows for every real s.

Two-dimensional Killing algebra with a periodic generator is abelian ([T,Φ]=0)

Words: 70
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
Suppose the full Lie algebra of Killing fields has basis T,Φ and the flow of Φ has a nonzero period P. Write [T,Φ]=aT+bΦ with constant coefficients. The Adjoint representation of the periodic flow satisfies ePadΦ=1. Its real eigenvalues are −a and zero, so a=0. The remaining adjoint operator is nilpotent and its exponential is 1+PadΦ, forcing b=0. This proves commutation from genuine periodic axial symmetry, without differentiating mere asymptotic component limits.

Bochner identity for Killing vector fields (∫M​∣∇X∣2dV=∫M​Ric(X,X)dV)

Words: 62
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
On a closed Riemannian manifold, a Killing vector field X satisfies ∇∗∇X=Ric♯X for the nonnegative rough Laplacian. This follows by differentiating ∇i​Xj​+∇j​Xi​=0, contracting and commuting covariant derivatives; the contraction also gives divX=0. Taking the inner product with X and using integration by parts gives the displayed identity. If Ricci curvature is nonpositive, the nonnegative left side must vanish, so X is a parallel vector field. In particular every Killing vector field on a closed flat Riemannian manifold is a parallel vector field.

Killing fields on incomplete manifolds need not generate global isometries

Words: 67
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
On a geodesically complete Riemannian manifold, every Killing vector field is complete and integrates to a global one-parameter subgroup of its isometry group. On the incomplete interval (0,1) with its Euclidean metric, ∂x​ satisfies the Killing equation, but translations leave the interval in finite time. The full isometry group is only the identity and reflection. Local solutions of the Killing equation therefore need not equal the Lie algebra of global isometries without completeness.

Killing transport (∇V​ξa​=Lab​Vb,∇V​Lab​=−Rabc​dVcξd​)

Words: 140 Articles: 1
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
The Killing equation and the second covariant derivative of a Killing vector transport a Killing field and its antisymmetric derivative along any curve. Here Lab​=∇b​ξa​ and the curvature convention is the one in curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities. In a parallel frame the equations form a homogeneous linear system of ordinary differential equations. Initial value zero has unique zero continuation. Thus on a connected pseudo-Riemannian manifold, not just on a Riemannian manifold, the field is determined by its value and first derivative at one point.

Dimension bound for Killing vector fields (dimkill(M,g)≤n(n+1)/2)

Words: 69
Killing transport OurBigBook.com
On a connected n-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold, evaluation of a Killing vector field and its first derivative is injective by Killing transport. The value has n components and the Killing equation makes the derivative an antisymmetric two-tensor with n(n−1)/2 components. Their sum gives the bound. Flat metrics attain it through ξa​=Aa​+Bab​xb with Bab​=−Bba​. Connectedness is essential for a uniform global bound in terms of n: independent fields can be supported on different connected components.

Killing field is determined by its value and first covariant derivative (K(p),(∇K)p​)

Words: 65
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
On a connected Riemannian manifold, these data determine a Killing field uniquely, without a completeness assumption. The second covariant derivative of a Killing vector makes its restriction to every geodesic a Jacobi field. The difference of two fields with identical data has zero Jacobi initial values and vanishes in a convex normal neighborhood. The set on which both its value and covariant derivative vanish is closed by continuity and open by the same local argument, so connectedness makes it the whole manifold.

Killing-flow four-acceleration (aa​=−∇a​logKbKb​​)

With metric signature (+−−−), normalize a timelike Killing vector field by Ua=Ka/N, N2=KaKa​. The Killing equation gives Kb∇b​Ka​=−21​∇a​N2, while Kb∇b​N=0. Thus the four-acceleration of its normalized flow is the displayed gradient. With metric signature (−+++) the corresponding lower-index formula has the opposite sign.

Papapetrou electromagnetic field (F=dK♭)

Words: 44
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
For a Killing vector field K, its metric-dual differential one-form produces the electromagnetic field Fab​=2∇[a​Kb]​=2∇a​Kb​. The exterior derivative gives dF=0, while the second covariant derivative of a Killing vector gives ∇aFab​=−2Rbd​Kd. Thus this construction solves the source-free Maxwell equations in a vacuum spacetime with zero cosmological constant. The field is a test field: adding its stress-energy tensor would generally change the original metric tensor.

ηab​x¨b+ξ(∂c​Aa​−∂a​Ac​)x˙c=0.

Thus, with Fbc​=∂b​Ac​−∂c​Ab​,
x¨a=ξηab(∂b​Ac​−∂c​Ab​)x˙c=ξFac​x˙c.

Affine normalization in a Kaluza-Klein geodesic reduction

For ds52​=ηab​dxadxb+(dy+Aa​dxa)2, with A independent of y, an affine parameter λ makes ξ=dy/dλ+Aa​dxa/dλ and the tangent norm N=gAB​x˙Ax˙B constant. The four-dimensional interval satisfies
du2=(N−ξ2)dλ2.
(4)
Consequently the formula dλ2=du2/(1−ξ2) assumes the additional normalization N=1; affine parametrization alone does not set this value. Euclidean Killing vector field (K(x)=A+Bx,BT=−B)

Words: 229 Articles: 1
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
On Euclidean space, every Killing vector field is a constant translation generator plus a constant infinitesimal rotation generator:
K(x)=A+Bx,BT=−B.
(5)
Here A is a constant vector and B is an antisymmetric matrix. The second covariant derivative of a Killing vector vanishes for the Euclidean metric, so the components are affine functions of Cartesian coordinates; the Killing equation makes the linear coefficient antisymmetric.
In the Euclidean plane with metric tensor g=dx2+dy2, two useful examples are
Ktr​=∂x​,Krot​=−y∂x​+x∂y​.
(6)
The notation ∂x​ denotes a coordinate basis vector; dx denotes a differential one-form. The corresponding flow of a vector field maps are
Φstr​(x,y)=(x+s,y),Φsrot​(x,y)=(xcoss−ysins, xsins+ycoss).
(7)
For translation, all arrows have the same direction and length. For rotation, arrows are tangent to circles, have length x2+y2​, and vanish at the origin. The rotation flow keeps x2+y2 fixed, and its nonstationary integral curves of a vector field are circles. Both flows preserve the Euclidean metric. See Carroll's original notes, section 5, equations (5.45)-(5.46).
The circular integral curves of a vector field are symmetry orbits, whereas geodesics of the Euclidean plane are straight lines. A Killing vector field does not require its own orbits to be geodesics. The geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector pairs the field with the velocity of a separate geodesic; it does not require the velocity to equal the Killing vector field.

Helical orbit of a Euclidean Killing vector

Words: 45
Euclidean Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
An infinitesimal rotation in one coordinate plane plus a constant translation has integral curves of a vector field that rotate about a shifted centre in that plane and move uniformly in the remaining directions. Nonzero transverse drift gives helices; zero drift gives circles, with zero radius allowing straight or stationary curves.

Killing equation (∇(μ​Kν)​=0)

Words: 355 Articles: 4
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
The Killing equation tests whether a candidate vector field generates a local isometry, without needing to solve for its flow of a vector field. For the Levi-Civita connection of a metric tensor g, it is
∇(μ​Kν)​=21​(∇μ​Kν​+∇ν​Kμ​)=0,Kν​=gνρ​Kρ.
(8)
Parentheses mean averaging over the two index orders. The equation says the symmetric part of the covariant derivative vanishes, not that the entire covariant derivative vanishes. The Lie derivative of the metric tensor gives the coordinate form
(LK​g)μν​=Kρ∂ρ​gμν​+gρν​∂μ​Kρ+gμρ​∂ν​Kρ=∇μ​Kν​+∇ν​Kμ​.
(9)
The last two terms account for how tangent vectors move with the field; checking only Kρ∂ρ​gμν​ misses this. See Carroll, section 5, equations (5.33) and (5.41)-(5.42).
For the Euclidean Killing vector field K=−y∂x​+x∂y​, the Euclidean metric in Cartesian coordinates makes covariant derivatives ordinary partial derivatives. Its diagonal equations give ∂x​Kx​=∂y​Ky​=0, and its mixed equation gives ∂x​Ky​+∂y​Kx​=1−1=0. Thus a varying vector field can preserve a constant metric tensor. In contrast, K=x∂x​+y∂y​ gives (LK​g)xx​=2 and fails the Killing equation, despite the constant metric components.
If K=∂z​ is a coordinate basis vector, its components are constant and the coordinate form reduces to ∂z​gμν​=0. Hence independence of the metric components from z supplies a Killing vector field. This shortcut applies to that coordinate generator, not to an arbitrary varying vector field.

Curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities (ka;bc​=−Cabc​dkd​)

Words: 81
Second covariant derivative of a Killing vector OurBigBook.com
Let Cabcd​=∂d​Γbca​−∂c​Γbda​−Γeca​Γbde​+Γeda​Γbce​, with all other index positions obtained by the metric tensor. This convention is the negative of the convention [∇c​,∇d​]Va=Rabcd​Vb. The Ricci identity is ka;bc​−ka;cb​=Ca​dbc​kd​, with ka;bc​=∇c​∇b​ka​. For a Killing vector, the Killing equation makes this second derivative antisymmetric in a,b. Applying the commutator three times gives 2ka;bc​=(Ca​dbc​+Cc​dba​−Cb​dac​)kd​=−2Cabc​dkd​, where the last step uses the first Bianchi identity. Switching the sign of the Riemann curvature tensor switches the sign in this identity; moving a raised index by ordinary metric contraction does not switch it.

Killing vector preserves scalar curvature

Every Killing vector field K preserves the scalar curvature: LK​R=Kα∇α​R=0. One index proof rewrites this derivative as a doubly contracted curvature commutator acting on the antisymmetric tensor ∇[μ​Kλ]​; both terms vanish by symmetry of the Ricci tensor.

Geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector (CK​=g(K,U))

Words: 513 Articles: 3
Killing vector field OurBigBook.com
Each Killing vector field supplies a conserved quantity for freely moving test particles: it turns a geometric symmetry into a first integral of the geodesic equation. Let γ(λ) be a geodesic with affine parameter λ, tangent vector Uμ=dxμ/dλ, and ∇U​U=0. Wherever a Killing vector field K is defined along the geodesic,
CK​=g(K,U)=Kμ​Uμ,dλdCK​​=0.
(11)
The statement applies to Riemannian manifolds and to timelike geodesics, null geodesics and spacelike geodesics in a Lorentzian manifold. It requires an affine parameter; for a massive test particle, proper time is one choice. See Tong, section 4.3.2, equation (4.32).
For a worked example in the Euclidean plane, take γ(t)=(1+t,2−t) with affine parameter t and velocity U=(1,−1). The Euclidean Killing vector fields for translation and rotation give
Ctr​=vx​=1,Crot​=xvy​−yvx​=−(1+t)−(2−t)=−3.
(12)
The rotational field along the geodesic is Krot​(γ(t))=(t−2,1+t): it changes from (−2,1) at t=0 to (−1,2) at t=1, yet its pairing with U stays −3. With toy mass m=1 and t interpreted as time, the first constant is the x component of linear momentum, and the second is the signed z component of angular momentum about the origin. Their numerical values use toy units. The geodesic is straight; the rotation flow orbits are circles.
This conservation law follows from metric compatibility and the Killing equation:
dλd​(Kμ​Uμ)=UμUν∇μ​Kν​+Kν​(∇U​U)ν=UμUν∇(μ​Kν)​=0.
(13)
The geodesic equation kills the acceleration term, and the symmetric product UμUν selects the vanishing symmetric derivative. This tensor contraction is the whole proof outline; see Carroll, equation (5.43).
In a stationary spacetime, a chosen time-translation Killing vector field K gives Killing energy. With metric signature (−+++), c=1, and a massive test particle's four-momentum pμ=mUμ, the physical normalization is
E=−pμ​Kμ,Lz​=pμ​Φμ.
(14)
Here Φ generates the axial rotational symmetry when one exists. In coordinates with K=∂t​ and Φ=∂ϕ​, these are E=−pt​ and Lz​=pϕ​. Both are conserved quantities along the geodesic, even if the metric has a time-angle cross term. A future timelike K makes E positive for a future massive particle; the scale of E depends on the normalization of K. Not every spacetime has a global timelike Killing vector field, and a stationary field can become spacelike in an ergoregion of a stationary spacetime. Conservation on the field's domain does not imply a universal global positive energy; see Tong, sections 4.3.1-4.3.2 on the timelike-domain qualification and conserved charges.

For a geodesic tangent u and a stationary Killing vector field K, the Killing energy is E=−g(K,u). It is conserved along the geodesic. When K is future timelike and u is future causal, E>0 for a nonzero tangent; it need not be positive where K becomes spacelike.

With metric signature (+−−−) and a future timelike stationary Killing vector K, positive energy is E=g(P,K) for future physical momentum P. A common (−+++) convention instead uses −g(P,K). The normalization of K fixes the observer or asymptotic time scale.

Charged-particle conserved quantity from a Killing symmetry (mVa​ua−qΦ)

Words: 84
Geodesic conserved quantity from a Killing vector OurBigBook.com
Suppose V is a Killing vector field, F is an electromagnetic field tensor satisfying dF=0 and LV​F=0, and a particle of nonzero mass m obeys the Lorentz force equation ∇u​u=(q/m)Fab​ub. Cartan's magic formula gives d(ιV​F)=0, so the local potential of a closed differential one-form proof gives dΦ=ιV​F locally. The Killing equation eliminates uaub∇a​Vb​, and the force term gives d(V⋅u)/dτ=(q/m)dΦ/dτ. Therefore mV⋅u−qΦ is locally conserved. The sign assumes contraction in the first argument of F. A global potential additionally requires the closed one-form to have zero periods; invariance alone is insufficient without the homogeneous Maxwell equations.

Angular momentum conservation in an axisymmetric vacuum region (JS2​​=JS1​​)

Words: 68
Komar angular momentum OurBigBook.com
For an axial Killing vector ξ and zero cosmological constant, set Fab=∇bξa. The Killing equation and Ricci identity give ∇b​Fab=−Rab​ξb. In a vacuum region this vanishes, so Stokes theorem makes the Komar angular momentum equal on homologous enclosing surfaces. The region may contain gravitational waves with nonzero Weyl curvature: Ricci flatness, rather than complete flatness, is what removes the flux. This proves absence of axial angular-momentum transport by exactly axisymmetric gravitational radiation, without assuming that a radiating spacetime is stationary.
