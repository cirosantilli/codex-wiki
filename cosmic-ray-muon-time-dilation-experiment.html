Cosmic-ray muon time-dilation experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor Time dilation
OurBigBook.com Words: 165 Articles: 1
Atmospheric cosmic rays generate muons whose mean lifetime at rest is about 2.2μs. For a selected speed v and production-to-detection distance h in the terrestrial frame, special relativity predicts survival fraction e−h/(γvτ0​), where γ is the Lorentz factor. Without time dilation, the exponent would instead be −h/(vτ0​). Comparing altitude-dependent counts at controlled energies, allowing for acceptance and energy loss, tests these alternatives. In the muon rest frame the terrestrial distance is shortened to h/γ by length contraction, so the proper flight time and survival fraction agree with the terrestrial calculation. This frame comparison does not turn the same decay measurement into two independent tests.
