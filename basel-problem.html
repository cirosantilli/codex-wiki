Basel problem (∑n=1∞​n−2=π2/6)

The Basel problem asks for the exact sum of the reciprocal squares of the positive integers. The answer is
n=1∑∞​n21​=ζ(2)=6π2​​.
(60)
The integral test proves that the series converges, but does not identify this exact value. The Euler evaluation of even zeta values does: the equally spaced poles of cotangent connect reciprocal integer powers to Bernoulli numbers and π. This separates three different questions: whether a sum exists, how well a partial sum approximates it, and what its exact value is.
For example, the ten-term partial sum is about 1.549768, while the exact value is about 1.644934. The zeta series truncation error bounds the missing amount between 1/11 and 1/10; it is much larger than the first omitted term 1/121. More terms improve the approximation, but a numerical coincidence with π2/6 would not prove the identity.
In On the sums of series of reciprocals by Leonhard Euler, §2 identifies the reciprocal-square sum with one-sixth of the squared circumference of a unit-diameter circle. The translation's publication note dates the presentation to 5 December 1735 and publication to 1740.[ref] The cotangent coefficient proof of the even zeta values gives a short modern outline, while zeta series truncation error addresses the distinct approximation question.
