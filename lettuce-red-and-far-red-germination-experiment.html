Lettuce red and far-red germination experiment

 ... Biology Organism Eukaryote Plant Seed Germination
OurBigBook.com Words: 211
The 1952 experiment used Grand Rapids lettuce seeds on wetted blotters in covered dishes. After 16 hours in darkness, seven lots of 200 received successive alternating exposures: one minute of red light at 580–680nm, then four minutes above 700nm, with one lot removed after each exposure. Treated lots and a dark scientific control remained at 20∘C for two days; germinated seeds were counted. Separate wavelength tests used a two-glass-prism spectrograph.[ref]
Table 1 shows alternating promotion and suppression according to the final exposure. Red light could restore promotion after far-red light; suppression was incomplete. This reversibility supports a reversible sensing state rather than simple cumulative illumination. The paper did not isolate or identify the pigment; the response concerns this lettuce preparation and timing. Its readout was a two-day germination count, not subsequent seedling performance.[ref]
The modern phytochrome description distinguishes forms Pr​ and Pfr​ that interconvert under red and far-red light. This provides a concrete example of signal transduction: light changes a sensing state that affects development. The response should be distinguished from photosynthesis, which supplies chemical resources.[ref]
