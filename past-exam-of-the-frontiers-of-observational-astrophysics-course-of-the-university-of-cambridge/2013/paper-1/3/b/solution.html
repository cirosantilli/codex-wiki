The thermal Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect is inverse-Compton scattering of cosmic microwave background photons by hot electrons in the intracluster medium. Electron thermal energy shifts photons toward higher frequencies, changing the spectrum relative to neighboring sky. In the nonrelativistic, small-optical-depth limit its amplitude is controlled by the Compton parameter

It measures line-of-sight electron pressure. At low frequencies the apparent Rayleigh-Jeans temperature changes by ΔT/TCMB​≃−2y. The thermal spectral distortion crosses through zero near 217GHz for the usual CMB temperature and then becomes an increment; relativistic corrections matter for hot gas. Multi-frequency measurements distinguish this spectrum from dust, radio sources and an ordinary CMB temperature fluctuation.
The surface-brightness contrast has no ordinary (1+z)−4 dimming for the same cluster pressure integral: the incident CMB temperature and the observation's frequency redshift scale together. Angular extent and integrated flux still depend on distance. This makes the effect useful for selecting distant clusters and mapping their thermal pressure. The solid-angle integral Y=∫ydΩ scales with total thermal energy divided by angular-diameter distance squared, providing a mass proxy when its gas-physics calibration is specified.[ref]
X-ray surface brightness depends roughly on ∫ne2​Λ(Te​)dl, whereas y depends on ∫ne​Te​dl. Combining X-ray spectra, brightness and resolved SZ data constrains density and line-of-sight depth. With a justified geometry, comparison to angular size gives a distance estimate; clumping and elongation can bias it. Cluster abundances and their redshift evolution test cosmological structure growth after selection and mass calibration are modeled.
The kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect additionally probes bulk line-of-sight motion, with leading ΔT/TCMB​≃−τe​v∥​/c in a stated velocity-sign convention. Its approximately blackbody temperature spectrum differs from the thermal distortion. These are complementary measurements of thermal pressure and gas motion, requiring foreground and instrumental control.
