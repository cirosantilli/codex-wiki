Type Ia supernovae are standardizable candles. Repeated multiband photometry measures peak apparent magnitude, light-curve width and colour; spectra identify the supernova class and determine redshift, often from the host galaxy. Nearby objects calibrate the relation between light-curve shape, colour and absolute luminosity. Applying those relations produces a corrected apparent magnitude mcorr​, not an assumption that every event has identical luminosity. The distance modulus then gives

For fixed low-redshift calibration, a negative q0​ increases the second-order distance relative to a decelerating model. Objects at a given redshift are therefore fainter. A Hubble diagram spanning enough redshift can distinguish this curvature from the common magnitude intercept. In matter-plus-cosmological-constant models, q0​=Ωm​/2−ΩΛ​; fitting the distances can test whether q0​<0. The two original supernova teams found distances supporting an accelerating expansion in that framework.[ref][ref]
The comparison requires matched rest-frame passbands and K corrections, correction for redshift time dilation in the light curves, a common photometric calibration, and explicit uncertainty propagation. Dust extinction can imitate dimming; multicolour and infrared observations test reddening and the adopted extinction law. Evolution in the progenitor population or host-galaxy dependence can alter standardization, so one compares matched populations and fits host-dependent terms where warranted. Flux-limited discovery introduces Malmquist bias and other selection effects, which must be modelled through the survey detection efficiency. peculiar velocities matter at low redshift, while gravitational lensing and intrinsic scatter broaden the distant sample. Classification and outlier tests reduce contamination by other transient types.
Independent data reductions, nearby anchors, spectroscopic checks and a fit over the full redshift range distinguish an expansion-history signal from one instrumental offset. Complementary cosmological observations help separate matter density, curvature and dark-energy parameters. The acceleration inference comes from the relative distance-redshift relation, conditional on standardization and an expansion model. Distant supernovae integrate the past expansion history; they do not directly measure the instantaneous second time derivative of today's scale factor without such modelling.
