Weak transitions in dilute gas. A forbidden emission line is a transition absent in the electric-dipole approximation because of its selection rules, but permitted through weaker channels such as magnetic-dipole or electric-quadrupole radiation, sometimes aided by state mixing. It is not an impossible transition. Its upper atomic level is relatively long lived, so collisions can deactivate it before a photon is emitted in dense gas. Low-density astrophysical plasmas allow radiative decay to compete successfully.
In a schematic two-level statistical equilibrium,
nl​ne​qlu​=nu​(Aul​+ne​qul​),ϵul​=nu​Aul​hνul​.
(15)
The critical density is ncrit​=Aul​/qul​ for this simple level system; multiple radiative and collisional channels require summed rates. At ne​≪ncrit​ each collisional excitation is likely to produce radiation, so ϵul​∝nl​ne​qlu​hν. At high density collisional de-excitation suppresses the line relative to this low-density expectation. The rate need not decrease absolutely with increasing density; the suppression is relative to the naive excitation scaling.
A temperature-sensitive ratio. For a Maxwell-Boltzmann electron distribution,
qlu​(T)∝T−1/2Υlu​(T)exp[−ΔElu​/(kB​T)],
(16)
where Υ is an effective collision strength. Lines from upper levels with different excitation energies therefore have a strongly temperature-dependent ratio. Using one ion reduces sensitivity to abundance and ionization fraction compared with comparing unrelated species.
A standard example is doubly ionized oxygen: the 4959 and 5007 angstrom lines descend from 1D2​ toward the 3P ground term, while the 4363 angstrom line connects 1S0​ to 1D2​. The higher excitation threshold makes the auroral 4363 line relatively stronger in hotter gas. In the low-density Maxwellian approximation, with slowly varying collision strengths,
ROIII​=I4363​I4959​+I5007​​≃7.90exp(Te​32900 K​),Te​≃ln(ROIII​/7.90)32900 K​.​
(17)
The numerical form is a useful approximation, not a universal exact atomic law; a multilevel atomic calculation with current collision strengths is needed for precise work.[ref]
In an active galactic nucleus these lines commonly diagnose the relatively dilute narrow-line gas; dense broad-line gas can collisionally quench them. Measure and deblend the fluxes, subtract continuum and stellar features, correct differential extinction, and check density independently. The 5007/4959 ratio mainly tests radiative branching and calibration because both lines share one upper level; it is not the required temperature thermometer. Temperature gradients, multiple density components, shocks, ionization structure and departures from the Maxwellian electron distribution can bias a single inferred temperature. Without a measured ratio and density regime, no particular AGN temperature is numerically determined here.
