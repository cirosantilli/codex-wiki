annual stellar parallax uses the known Earth-orbit baseline, with d(pc)=1/ϖ(arcsec). Repeated positions separate parallax from proper motion; calibration offsets, binarity and noisy-parallax selection require checks. Nearby stars calibrate absolute luminosities. main-sequence fitting compares a cluster sequence with nearby calibrated stars; age, metallicity, reddening and unresolved binaries limit the match. Cepheid variables extend the range to nearby galaxies: measure periods and mean multiband magnitudes, calibrate the Cepheid period-luminosity relation, and correct for extinction, metallicity, crowding and pulsation mode. RR Lyrae stars and the tip of the red giant branch provide complementary population-II routes with their own composition and calibration dependence.
Some anchors bypass intermediate rungs. An eclipsing-binary distance starts from a double-lined eclipsing binary: velocities give asini; eclipses give inclination i and relative radii R/a, hence physical radii. An independently calibrated surface-brightness/colour relation gives angular radii and distance; alternatively corrected bolometric flux satisfies Fbol​=σTeff4​(R/d)2 for a component. temperature, extinction, third light and surface-brightness calibration remain systematic errors. Eight late-type binaries gave a 2.2%-accuracy LMC anchor in 2013.[ref]
A maser-disk geometric distance uses water masers orbiting in the nearly edge-on nuclear disk of NGC 4258. very-long-baseline interferometry, Doppler velocities, proper motions and accelerations constrain its orbit scale and distance. In an ideal circular, matched-radius example R=v2/aorb​ and d=R/θ; real fits account for disk warp and different maser locations. This geometric method calibrated an extragalactic distance independently of stellar luminosities.[ref] These overlapping anchors cross-check the nearby-galaxy ladder; no Hubble-flow fit is needed here.
