The detector is intended for arcsecond-scale astronomical seeing, rather than the telescope's diffraction limit. A circular aperture of this area has diameter 1.13m, giving a Rayleigh criterion angle about 0.11 arcsec at the observing wavelength. One-arcsecond pixels conventionally sample an astronomical seeing profile of roughly two arcseconds across with two samples; a one-arcsecond profile would be undersampled. The pixel scale alone does not determine an exact astronomical seeing width.
Use the supplied surface brightness as radiative flux integrated over the photometric passband: its units contain no inverse hertz. Treat hν as the representative photon energy, and assume unit throughput and quantum efficiency. A genuinely frequency-specific brightness would require a bandwidth. The pixel cut retains 51.6% of this total. It is an imposed isophotal photometry rule, not a fundamental limit: averaging annuli or fitting a justified profile can recover signal below the per-pixel cut.
In practice, sky background photons add shot noise even after subtraction of the mean sky. read noise, dark current, imperfect flat-field correction, throughput calibration and atmospheric extinction also matter. At large radius the dominant uncertainty can be a tiny error in the sky level, scattered light or the wings of the point spread function, rather than source photon statistics. Mask foreground stars and background objects, compare blank-sky regions and dithered exposures, and check profile recovery by inserting artificial extended sources. astronomical seeing and finite pixels convolve the intrinsic profile; model that response when extrapolating the total.
The luminosity convergence of an outer surface-brightness profile follows directly: I∝r−p contributes dF∝r1−pdr, hence F(<R)∝R2−p at large radius for p<2. For either shallow index considered, ever larger annuli dominate: extending the law to infinity gives an unphysical divergent luminosity. Such a law must eventually steepen or truncate. Over a finite range, however, substantial light can lie below a surface-brightness selection threshold and be mistaken for sky. This biases galaxy luminosities, the luminosity function and the inferred diffuse light budget; distant diffuse galaxies become especially incomplete because bolometric cosmological surface-brightness dimming scales as (1+z)−4. An assumed finite-aperture total is therefore a model-dependent quantity.
