2-adic absolute value (∣x∣2​=2−v2​(x))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Non-Archimedean analysis P-adic number p-adic absolute value
OurBigBook.com Words: 199 Articles: 1
The 2-adic absolute value is the p-adic absolute value for the prime number p=2. It is defined on the rational numbers by ∣x∣2​=2−v2​(x) for x=0 and ∣0∣2​=0, and extends to their P-adic number completion Q2​. For a nonzero integer, count factors of two until the remaining factor is odd; for a rational number, subtract the denominator's count from the numerator's count.
The associated metric reverses a familiar comparison:
d2​(5,13)=∣5−13∣2​=∣−8∣2​=81​,d2​(5,6)=∣−1∣2​=1.
(20)
Although 13 is eight units from 5 on the ordinary number line, it agrees with 5 modulo 8, whereas 6 already disagrees modulo 2. The 2-adic absolute value measures that agreement. The value ∣3/8∣2​=8 also shows why this Non-Archimedean absolute value must be distinguished from the ordinary absolute value ∣3/8∣=3/8.
In the left panel, differences farther from the diagonal grow in ordinary absolute value. In the right panel, nonzero even differences are closer than odd differences, and each further factor of two halves the 2-adic absolute value. The highlighted pairs have opposite distance rankings. The repeated pattern is the nested congruence class structure of an ultrametric space, visible without adding any new points.
