For g≥0, the Hamiltonian operator is nonnegative: its quadratic form is ℏ2∫∣ψ′∣2dx/(2m)+g(∣ψ(a)∣2+∣ψ(−a)∣2)≥0. Thus it has no negative-energy bound states. A nonnegative-energy exterior wavefunction cannot be square integrable unless it vanishes identically, so there are no other bound states either.
For g<0, set ν=−mg/ℏ2>0. The even equation is the zero of f(κ)=ν(1+e−2aκ)−κ. Since f(0)=2ν>0, f(κ)→−∞ and f′(κ)=−2aνe−2aκ−1<0, the intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity give exactly one positive equation root.
The odd equation is the zero of h(κ)=ν(1−e−2aκ)−κ. Here h(0)=0, h′(0)=2aν−1, h′′(κ)=−4a2νe−2aκ<0, and h(κ)→−∞. If 2aν≤1, strict concavity makes h(κ)<0 for every κ>0. If 2aν>1, h initially increases, has exactly one maximum, and then decreases through zero exactly once. This proves the bound-state count of an attractive symmetric double-delta potential:
N(g)=⎩⎨⎧​0,1,2,​g≥0,−2maℏ2​≤g<0,g<−2maℏ2​.​​
(32)
At the threshold g=−ℏ2/(2ma), the odd zero-energy solution is proportional to x inside (−a,a) and to asgnx outside. It is bounded but not a square-integrable function, so it is a zero-energy scattering resonance rather than a second bound state. Each negative-energy bound state is either even or odd by nondegeneracy of one-dimensional bound states; the two branches above exhaust the count.
