Write ∣↑⟩,∣↓⟩ for the two S3​ eigenstates, of eigenvalues ±ℏ/2. The spin-spin interaction can be evaluated using
S(1)⋅S(2)=S3(1)​S3(2)​+21​(S+(1)​S−(2)​+S−(1)​S+(2)​).
(4)
For ∣β∣≫∣α∣, take H0​=β(S3(1)​−S3(2)​)/ℏ and V=αS(1)⋅S(2)/ℏ2. The unperturbed energy eigenvalues are β,−β,0,0 on ∣↑↓⟩,∣↓↑⟩,∣↑↑⟩,∣↓↓⟩, respectively. For an isolated normalized energy eigenstate ∣n⟩, first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory gives ΔEn(1)​=⟨n∣V∣n⟩. The two antiparallel spins have expectation −α/4, so
E↑↓​=β−4α​+O(α2/∣β∣),E↓↑​=−β−4α​+O(α2/∣β∣).​
(5)
For the zero-energy degenerate eigenspace, degenerate perturbation theory requires diagonalizing the restriction of V. On the ordered basis (∣↑↑⟩,∣↓↓⟩) it is (α/4)I: the ladder operators cannot take one parallel state to the other. Indeed neither parallel state couples to anything else under H, so the remaining two levels are exactly α/4, retaining their twofold quantum degeneracy.
For ∣α∣≫∣β∣, take H0​=αS(1)⋅S(2)/ℏ2 and V=βD, where D=(S3(1)​−S3(2)​)/ℏ. The spin-one-half triplet state basis and spin-one-half singlet state are
∣t+​⟩=∣↑↑⟩,∣t0​⟩=2​∣↑↓⟩+∣↓↑⟩​,∣t−​⟩=∣↓↓⟩,∣s⟩=2​∣↑↓⟩−∣↓↑⟩​.
(6)
The identity S(1)⋅S(2)=(J2−3ℏ2/2)/2 gives unperturbed energy eigenvalues Et(0)​=α/4 and Es(0)​=−3α/4. Direct action gives
D∣t±​⟩=0,D∣t0​⟩=∣s⟩,D∣s⟩=∣t0​⟩.
(7)
Consequently the restriction of V to the entire triplet degenerate eigenspace is zero, so no first-order splitting is missed. The singlet expectation is also zero. In second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory, ΔEn(2)​=∑r=n​∣⟨r∣V∣n⟩∣2/(En(0)​−Er(0)​). Within the triplet, the corresponding second-order effective matrix is β2∣t0​⟩⟨t0​∣/α, already diagonal in this basis. Thus, through second order,
Et±​​=4α​,Et0​​=4α​+αβ2​,Es​=−43α​−αβ2​.​
(8)
The last two expressions have higher corrections beginning at O(β4/∣α∣3), as the exact calculation in part (d) also verifies. For positive α, coupling to the lower singlet raises the triplet's M=0 level and lowers the singlet; the signs are precisely the energy denominators above.
