Fano's inequality via an error indicator (H(X∣Y)≤h(pe​)+pe​log2​(∣JX​∣−1))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Conditional entropy Fano's inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 285
Under the hypotheses of Fano's inequality, let JX​=X, M=∣JX​∣, and let Z=1{X=f(Y)}​ be the indicator random variable of an error. Its probability of being one is pe​. The h in the displayed bound denotes binary entropy h2​ in bits. Since Z is a deterministic function of (X,Y), H(Z∣X,Y)=0. Applying the chain rule for conditional entropy in both orders gives the exact decomposition
H(X∣Y)​=H(X,Z∣Y)=H(Z∣Y)+H(X∣Z,Y).​
(17)
The first term is bounded by H(Z)=h2​(pe​), because conditioning reduces entropy. For the second term, fix a report Y=y: if Z=0, then X=f(y) is known; if Z=1, then X lies among at most M−1 other symbols. Maximum entropy on a finite alphabet bounds the latter uncertainty by log2​(M−1). Averaging over the reports and the error flag gives
H(X∣Z,Y)≤(1−pe​)⋅0+pe​log2​(M−1).
(18)
This proves Fano's inequality. Events of zero probability contribute zero to this average, so the proof also covers pe​=0,1 without assigning a conditional distribution to an impossible event.[ref]
The decomposition also explains equality. The first bound is exact when the indicator random variable Z and Y are independent random variables: knowing the report does not change the error probability. The second is exact when, for every report with positive error probability, the wrong-symbol conditional distribution is uniform on all M−1 alternatives. Both conditions hold in the four-symbol toy channel of Fano's inequality. At q=0, the second condition is vacuous; at q=1, the flag is constant but three alternatives remain, giving H(X∣Y)=log2​3. For M=2, there is just one alternative after an error, so the second term vanishes: all remaining uncertainty is whether the single alternative must replace the guess.
