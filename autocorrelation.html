Autocorrelation (ρ(h))

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Time series Stationary process Weakly stationary process Autocovariance
OurBigBook.com Words: 407 Articles: 5
The autocorrelation function of a real weakly stationary process with positive variance is its normalized autocovariance:
ρ(h)=γ(0)γ(h)​=Corr(Xt+h​,Xt​).
(9)
It is dimensionless, with ρ(0)=1, ρ(−h)=ρ(h), and ∣ρ(h)∣≤1 by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. It is unchanged by replacing Xt​ with aXt​+b for fixed a=0. This makes autocorrelation useful when comparing records measured on different scales. The ACF is a property of the model; the sample autocorrelation function estimates it and will generally not reproduce theoretical zeros exactly.
An AR(1) with coefficient ϕ=0.7 has autocorrelations 0.7,0.49,0.343 at lags one through three. With ϕ=−0.7 these become −0.7,0.49,−0.343: the same diminishing magnitude, alternating in sign. An MA(1) with θ=0.7 instead has lag-one autocorrelation 0.7/1.49=70/149≃0.4698 and zero at every higher lag. Equal numerical coefficients in an autoregressive model and a moving-average model do not mean equal dependence. See Bartlett, lecture 3: theoretical ACFs and prediction.
The bars show exact model autocorrelations, not estimates from the illustrated sample paths in time series. The AR(1) retains a diminishing trace of a disturbance at arbitrarily large lags; the MA(1) stops sharing driving noise after lag one. A zero autocorrelation means uncorrelated random variables, and does not generally imply independent random variables. For a Gaussian process it does imply independence of the two coordinates.
There is a direct prediction interpretation. Among affine predictions based on just Xt​, the best linear predictor of Xt+h​ is μ+ρ(h)(Xt​−μ), with mean squared prediction error γ(0)[1−ρ(h)2]. Thus negative autocorrelation is also useful predictive information. A prediction using the whole observed past can do better; best linear prediction from a finite past describes that problem. Interpreting the affine prediction as a conditional expectation requires additional assumptions, such as a joint normal distribution.
