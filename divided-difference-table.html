Divided difference table (Di,k​=f[xi​,…,xi+k​])

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Interpolation Polynomial interpolation Divided difference
OurBigBook.com Words: 269
A divided difference table arranges divided differences by starting interpolation node i and order k. Its base column is Di,0​=f(xi​); every later entry uses two neighboring entries from the previous column:
Di,k​=xi+k​−xi​Di+1,k−1​−Di,k−1​​,1≤k≤n,0≤i≤n−k.
(11)
The denominator spans all k+1 interpolation nodes of that entry, even though the numerator uses adjacent table entries. The interpolation nodes must be distinct; they need not be equally spaced or sorted.[ref]
Take the polynomial f(x)=x3−2x+1 at the interpolation nodes (−1,0,2,3), giving values (2,1,5,22). Its divided difference table is
xi​−1023​Di,0​21522​Di,1​−1217​Di,2​15​Di,3​1​​
(12)
The first divided differences are (1−2)/(0+1)=−1, (5−1)/(2−0)=2, and (22−5)/(3−2)=17. The second divided differences use the wider spans: (2+1)/(2+1)=1 and (17−2)/(3−0)=5. The third divided difference is (5−1)/(3+1)=1. Reading the top row gives the Newton polynomial coefficients (2,−1,1,1), rather than all the entries in any one column.
The resulting Newton polynomial grows through four partial interpolants:
p0​(x)p1​(x)p2​(x)p3​(x)​=2,=2−(x+1)=1−x,=2−(x+1)+(x+1)x=x2+1,=2−(x+1)+(x+1)x+(x+1)x(x−2)=x3−2x+1.​
(13)
At x=2, p1​(2)=−1 misses f(2)=5 by 6; the correction (x+1)x supplies exactly 6 there and vanishes at the earlier interpolation nodes −1,0. At x=3, p2​(3)=10 misses f(3)=22 by 12; the correction (x+1)x(x−2) supplies exactly 12 there and vanishes at all three earlier interpolation nodes. Each added term therefore preserves the previous agreements while satisfying the next one.
For n+1 interpolation nodes, the triangle contains n+(n−1)+⋯+1=n(n+1)/2 recurrence steps. Once the top row is known, nested evaluation of the Newton polynomial uses only n multiplications and n additions per argument. The triangle organizes the work; the factors explain why the construction works.
