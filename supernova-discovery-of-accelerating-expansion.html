The 1998 High-Z Supernova Search Team paper by Adam Riess, Brian Schmidt and collaborators presented supernova evidence for acceleration.[ref]
The 1999 Supernova Cosmology Project paper by Saul Perlmutter and collaborators independently analysed 42 distant events. Alexei V. Filippenko coauthored both papers.[ref]
The High-Z Supernova Search Team search used the CTIO Blanco 4-m telescope and a 2048×2048 charge-coupled device, comparing fields across dark runs. Difference images located rising candidates; Keck, MMT and ESO spectroscopy classified them and measured host redshifts. Follow-up photometry used filters approximating emitted B and V bands. Calibrated field stars transferred the magnitude zero point; late host images removed galaxy light, point spread function fits measured brightness, and inserted artificial stars tested recovery.[ref]
The Supernova Cosmology Project likewise discovered rising events with CTIO 4-m charge-coupled device imaging, then followed their light curves with several telescopes. Keck LRIS and ESO spectroscopy supplied classification and redshift, preferably from narrow host lines. Cross-filter K corrections connected received R/I observations to emitted B/V light.[ref]
Why does fainter mean accelerating? First it means a larger inferred luminosity distance only at fixed standardized luminosity and corrected attenuation. Next it favours acceleration only after a comparison family is specified: an FLRW metric, representative propagation of light, common nearby and distant standardization, and controlled measurement calibration and selection. For the usual general relativity models containing nonnegative pressureless matter, spatial curvature of an FLRW universe and a cosmological constant, the shape of the Hubble diagram constrains Ωm,0​,ΩΛ,0​ and thus q0​=Ωm,0​/2−ΩΛ,0​. A positive residual relative to one decelerating model rejects that prediction; it does not alone prove q0​<0 across arbitrary expansion histories or arbitrary opacity laws.
The essential advance is that a nearby sample fixes an intercept while a range of distant redshifts measures the shape. The two teams supplied independent observations and analysis, although common nearby calibrators and Type Ia supernova physics can produce shared uncertainties. The dark-energy acceleration criterion explains the interpretation within general relativity; the worked supernova distance comparison shows the size and sign of the effect without fitting any survey data.
