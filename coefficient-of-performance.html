Coefficient of performance (COP=Quseful​/W)

OurBigBook.com Words: 356
For the cyclic two-side energy balance in refrigerator, define the dimensionless cooling and heating coefficients by
COPr​=WQc​​,COPheat​=WQh​​=WQc​+W​=COPr​+1.
(21)
This identity applies even to irreversible machines, provided both coefficients refer to the same operating point, work input and boundary with no additional energy transfer or storage. It does not compare two separately operated appliances. Unlike thermal efficiency of a heat engine, a COP can exceed one: its numerator counts heat moved, including energy taken from another region. [ref]
To obtain temperature-only bounds, impose stronger assumptions: a cyclic machine exchanges heat only with two ideal thermal reservoirs maintained at uniform, fixed absolute thermodynamic temperatures 0<Tc​<Th​, and receives work through an ideal input carrying no entropy. Include the machine and the heat transfer interfaces in the entropy production accounting. Because its own entropy returns to its initial value each cycle, the Second law of thermodynamics gives
Sgen​=Th​Qh​​−Tc​Qc​​≥0.
(22)
Combining this inequality with Qh​=Qc​+W gives
COPr​≤Th​−Tc​Tc​​​,COPheat​≤Th​−Tc​Th​​​.
(23)
Equality requires a reversible thermodynamic process for the entire cycle and its contacts, as in a reversed Carnot cycle. These are Carnot's theorem bounds independent of the working substance. Use kelvin values, not degree Celsius readings, in the numerators; the temperature difference can be expressed in either unit. [ref] [ref]
The diagram fixes Tc​=280K and sets Th​=320K, so the reversible bounds are 280/40=7 and 320/40=8. The stipulated machine with COPr​=3 and COPheat​=4 satisfies both. Its reservoir entropy production per cycle is 4000/320−3000/280=25/14JK−1>0. Increasing the temperature lift ΔT=Th​−Tc​ at fixed Tc​ lowers both bounds. Equivalently, delivering fixed Qh​ needs at least
Wmin​=Qh​(1−Th​Tc​​).
(24)
These expressions reveal why a small temperature lift is attractive. Their divergence as Th​−Tc​→0+ is an ideal reversible limit, not a promise of infinite heating or cooling power. For finite heat transfer rates, the working fluid must be colder than the cold bath and hotter than the hot bath; these contact differences produce entropy and increase the internal temperature lift. Dissipative compression and the pressure-reducing restriction of vapor-compression refrigeration supply further departures from reversibility. Thus bath temperatures set an outer bound, not a prediction of real COP. [ref]
