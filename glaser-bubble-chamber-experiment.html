Glaser bubble-chamber experiment

 Home Physics Particle detection Particle detector Bubble chamber
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
Donald Arthur Glaser developed the bubble chamber in 1952 during experimental physics research at Michigan.[ref]
His 1960 lecture, Elementary particles and bubble chambers, describes evacuated capillary bulbs containing pure diethyl ether. Cooling one bulb reduced pressure; the other remained hot. A cobalt-60 source initiated boiling. Small glass chambers subsequently used hot-oil baths, hand-cranked pistons and high-speed photography. Automatic cycling with counter-controlled flashes produced finer tracks. Local ionization deposits energy that can initiate bubble nucleation.[ref]
Later metal-chamber photographs compared operation with and without a nearby radioactive source: wall boiling occurred without interior tracks; irradiation produced tracks before disruptive flow ended sensitivity.[ref]
These observations separate two questions: can radiation start boiling, and can a detector preserve the geometry of a passing charged particle? Whole-vessel boiling alone answers only the first. Wall background, finite sensitivity and growth before photography constrain the second.
