Hoeffding inequality (P(Sn​≥t)≤e−2t2/V)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Concentration inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
The Hoeffding inequality turns finite interval bounds and independence into exponentially small tail probabilities for a sum. It requires neither a normal distribution nor identical probability distributions. Let X1​,…,Xn​ be independent essentially bounded real random variables, with deterministic finite bounds ai​≤Xi​≤bi​ almost surely. Define the centered sum and its squared-width budget by
Sn​=∑i=1n​(Xi​−EXi​),V=∑i=1n​(bi​−ai​)2.
(20)
For V>0 and t>0,
P(Sn​≥t)≤e−2t2/V,P(Sn​≤−t)≤e−2t2/V.​
(21)
The union bound therefore gives P(∣Sn​∣≥t)≤min{1,2e−2t2/V}. If V=0, every Xi​ is constant almost surely, so Sn​=0 and both positive-threshold tail probabilities vanish. The equal-width version is stated in the authored Stanford notes.[ref]
The quantity V is a sum of squared interval widths, not the variance of Sn​. Centering changes the endpoints to ai​−EXi​ and bi​−EXi​, leaving each width unchanged. For example, independent random variables in [−2,1] and [4,5] have V=32+12=10, whatever their expected values or probability distributions. A deviation at least three above their total expected value has probability at most e−18/10≈0.165299. If all widths equal b−a>0, the sample mean Xn​=n−1∑i​Xi​ satisfies
P(∣Xn​−EXn​∣≥ε)≤min{1,2exp(−2nε2/(b−a)2)},ε>0.
(22)
Independence is essential to this general guarantee. Take one Bernoulli random variable B with success probability 1/2 and set every Xi​=B. Each lies in [0,1], but for every n the sample mean is either zero or one. Thus P(∣Xn​−1/2∣≥1/4)=1, whereas incorrectly inserting these dependent observations into the formula would give 2e−n/8, less than one for n≥6. Some special dependent models have their own valid concentration inequalities; arbitrary statistical dependence cannot be ignored.
For the short proof, apply Markov's inequality to eλSn​ with λ>0, factor the expectation using independence, and apply the Hoeffding lemma to each factor:
P(Sn​≥t)≤e−λt∏i​Eeλ(Xi​−EXi​)≤exp(−λt+λ2V/8).
(23)
Minimizing this quadratic polynomial at λ=4t/V gives the upper tail probability; applying it to −Sn​ gives the lower one. This is the Chernoff bound method. The original 1963 article is Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables by Wassily Hoeffding; its publisher record identifies the article, but its full text was unavailable during this source check. The argument above is a direct derivation rather than a claim to have inspected that article's proof.
