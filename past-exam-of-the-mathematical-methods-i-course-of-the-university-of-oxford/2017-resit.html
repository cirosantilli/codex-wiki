An orthogonal matrix satisfies ATA=I. Taking determinants and using detAT=detA gives (detA)2=1, so

For a two-dimensional complex orthogonal matrix, let ε=detA∈{+1,−1}. Since A−1=AT, comparison with the two-by-two matrix inverse formula forces the second row to be (−εb,εa) if the first is (a,b). Thus the complete family is
A=(a−εb​bεa​),a,b∈C,a2+b2=1,ε=±1.​
(8)
Conversely direct multiplication gives ATA=(a2+b2)I=I and detA=ε, proving sufficiency. The two parameters obey a bilinear constraint, not ∣a∣2+∣b∣2=1; replacing squares by absolute squares would incorrectly impose unitarity.

The hyperbolic functions satisfy cosh2θ−sinh2θ=1, so a useful family is
a=coshθ,b=isinhθ,A=(coshθ−εisinhθ​isinhθεcoshθ​).​
(9)
For real θ this gives a one-real-parameter family of complex orthogonal matrices, which is enough to exhibit the requested hyperbolic parametrization. It does not parametrize every matrix in part (ii). For example a=b=1/2​ obeys the constraint but cannot have a=coshθ with real θ, since then a≥1.

For the damped harmonic oscillator, the characteristic polynomial is r2+2γr+ω02​. When 0≤γ<ω0​, its roots are −γ±iΩ, with Ω=ω02​−γ2​. Thus
x(t)=e−γt(AcosΩt+BsinΩt).​

Divide the external force by m. Away from resonance a particular solution has the forcing frequency, so the general forced harmonic oscillator solution is
x(t)=Acosω0​t+Bsinω0​t+m(ω02​−ω2)F0​​cos(ωt−ϕ),ω=ω0​.​
(21)
The two homogeneous terms cannot be discarded in an undamped system: they do not decay. If the same initial conditions are imposed, then A=x0​−Ccosϕ, B=−C(ω/ω0​)sinϕ, where C=F0​/[m(ω02​−ω2)].
For ω≫ω0​, the forced displacement approaches −F0​cos(ωt−ϕ)/(mω2): its amplitude decreases quadratically and it is in antiphase with the force. This statement concerns the forced component; a freely excited component at ω0​ can remain.
At resonance the preceding denominator is zero and the appropriate solution is
x(t)=Acosω0​t+Bsinω0​t+2mω0​F0​t​sin(ω0​t−ϕ).​
(22)
Its amplitude grows linearly because the external force supplies energy coherently. For x(0)=x0​, x˙(0)=0, choose A=x0​ and B=F0​sinϕ/(2mω02​). Physical damping or nonlinear response ultimately limits the ideal resonant growth.

Write z=x+iy=0. Since z−1=(x−iy)/(x2+y2), the two loci are
(x−21​)2+y2=41​,x2+(y+21​)2=41​,z=0.​
(23)
Thus they are radius-1/2 circles centred at (1/2,0) and (0,−1/2), each missing the origin. Their circle equations intersect at (0,0) and (1/2,−1/2), but only the latter belongs to the original loci. At that point the radius vectors are (0,−1/2) and (1/2,0), so the tangents meet at a right angle.
Every point on either circle has distance at most 1/2+1/2=1 from the origin. Equality occurs only at (1,0) on the first or (0,−1) on the second. The unit circle is therefore internally tangent to both and crosses neither. The sketch marks the excluded origin explicitly, rather than treating it as a second intersection.

With a fixed unit normal n, the Householder reflection is P=I−2nnT. It is a linear map because both the identity and the map v↦n(nTv) are linear. Decompose v=v∥​+v⊥​, where v⊥​=(n⋅v)n and v∥​⋅n=0. Then
Pv=v∥​−v⊥​.
(32)
Thus the tangential component is unchanged and the normal component reverses. Its norm and absolute normal component are preserved, proving equality of the physical incidence and reflection angles measured against the appropriate normal. Angles of the incident and reflected directed vectors to one fixed oriented normal are instead supplementary; equality uses the conventional acute angle to the normal line, or reverses the incident direction.
Since (nnT)2=nnT, direct multiplication gives P2=I. Every eigenvalue therefore satisfies λ2=1. More specifically, the tangent plane is the +1 eigenspace and the normal line the −1 eigenspace. In the stated orthonormal basis, with the first two vectors tangent to the plane,
[P]{e1​,e2​,n}​=diag(1,1,−1).​
(33)
This also proves both signs occur, rather than merely showing that any eigenvalue must belong to {+1,−1}.
