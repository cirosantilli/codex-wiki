For a two-dimensional complex orthogonal matrix, let ε=detA∈{+1,−1}. Since A−1=AT, comparison with the two-by-two matrix inverse formula forces the second row to be (−εb,εa) if the first is (a,b). Thus the complete family is
A=(a−εb​bεa​),a,b∈C,a2+b2=1,ε=±1.​
(8)
Conversely direct multiplication gives ATA=(a2+b2)I=I and detA=ε, proving sufficiency. The two parameters obey a bilinear constraint, not ∣a∣2+∣b∣2=1; replacing squares by absolute squares would incorrectly impose unitarity.
