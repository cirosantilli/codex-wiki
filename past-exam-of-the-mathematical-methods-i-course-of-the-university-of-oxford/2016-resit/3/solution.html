Let R=∣α∣, S=∣β∣ and d=∣b−a∣. For distinct centres, the two spherical surfaces intersect exactly when
∣R−S∣≤d≤R+S.​
(6)
Strict inequalities give a nondegenerate circle; equality gives a single tangency point. If d=0, equal radii give coincident spheres and unequal radii give no intersection, so the circle-centre formula below does not apply.
Subtracting the squared sphere equations gives the radical plane
2(b−a)⋅r=∣b∣2−∣a∣2+α2−β2.
(7)
The centre of the circle of intersection of two spheres is the projection of either sphere centre onto this plane. Write c=a+ℓ(b−a)/d; substitution gives ℓ=(d2+α2−β2)/(2d) and hence
c=21​(a+b+∣b−a∣2(α2−β2)(b−a)​).​
(8)
The circle radius squared is R2−ℓ2, providing another check of the intersection conditions.
Its centre equals a precisely when ℓ=0, or β2=α2+d2​. For a positive first radius, the intersection is then a great circle of the first sphere. A triangle from its centre to an intersection point and then to the second centre is right-angled at a, explaining the Pythagorean theorem relation geometrically.
