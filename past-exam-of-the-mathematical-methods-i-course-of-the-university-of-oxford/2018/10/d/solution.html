Take the inner product with ui​ in a=∑j​aj′​wj​. The reciprocal basis condition gives ai′​=⟨ui​,a⟩. Expanding instead in the u basis proves the reciprocal-basis coordinate transformation
ai′​=j∑​Gij​aj​,Gij​=⟨ui​,uj​⟩.​
(34)
Thus G is the Gram matrix of the original basis. It is a positive-definite matrix, so this change of basis is invertible.
In the specified two-dimensional Cartesian frame,
u1​=(1,0),u2​=(21​,23​​).
(35)
Solving the four dot product conditions gives
w1​=(1,−3​1​),w2​=(0,3​2​).​
(36)
They are perpendicular to u2​ and u1​, respectively, with lengths adjusted so the other dot product is one. Equating the Cartesian components of a=a1​u1​+a2​u2​=a1′​w1​+a2′​w2​ gives
ax​=a1​+21​a2​=a1′​,ay​=23​​a2​=3​−a1′​+2a2′​​.
(37)
Consequently
(a1′​a2′​​)=(11/2​1/21​)(a1​a2​​),​
(38)
which agrees entry by entry with the Gram matrix computed directly from the two unit basis vectors.
