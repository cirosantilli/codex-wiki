Let Av=λv with v=0, using the standard complex Hermitian inner product. Then
v†Av=λv†v.
(15)
Taking the complex conjugate and using A†=−A yields λv†v=v†A†v=−λv†v. Since v†v>0,
λ=−λ,Reλ=0.​
(16)
Thus every eigenvalue of an anti-Hermitian matrix lies on the imaginary axis, including the possible zero eigenvalue; each nonzero eigenvalue is purely imaginary.
Now take Au=λu and Av=μv. On one hand u†Av=μu†v. On the other hand
u†Av=−(Au)†v=−λu†v=λu†v.
(17)
Consequently (μ−λ)u†v=0. Distinct eigenvalues therefore imply
u†v=0,​
(18)
which is the required orthogonality of the eigenvectors.
