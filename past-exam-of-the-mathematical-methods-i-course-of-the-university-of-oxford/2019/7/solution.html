Write the given matrix as A. Expanding its characteristic polynomial along the middle column gives
det(λI−A)​=(λ+2)[(λ−7)(λ+8)+54]=(λ+2)(λ2+λ−2)=(λ−1)(λ+2)2.​
(19)
Thus the eigenvalues are 1,−2,−2, counted with algebraic multiplicity.
For λ=1, solving (A−I)v=0 gives z=2x and y=−x. For λ=−2, solving (A+2I)v=0 gives z=3x, with y free. A convenient eigenbasis is therefore
λ1−2−2​eigenvector(1,−1,2)T(1,0,3)T(0,1,0)T​​​
(20)
The last two eigenvectors span the two-dimensional eigenspace for −2. The three columns have determinant −1, so they are linearly independent and form a basis of R3. In particular the repeated eigenvalue causes no failure of diagonalizability here.
