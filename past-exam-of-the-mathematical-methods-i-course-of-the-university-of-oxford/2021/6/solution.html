Use the Characteristic polynomials of AB and BA, which also account for the zero eigenvalue and algebraic multiplicities. The block determinant
det(I−B​tAI​)
(10)
equals both det(I+tAB) and det(I+tBA), by taking the two Schur complements. For λ=0, set t=−1/λ and multiply by λn to obtain
det(λI−AB)=det(λI−BA).​
(11)
Both sides are polynomials, so agreement away from zero proves equality there as well. Their roots, with multiplicity, are the same. The familiar eigenvector argument ABv=μv⇒BA(Bv)=μBv works directly only when μ=0, because then Bv=0; it should not be used to discard the zero case.
