Label a opposite x0​, b opposite x1​, c opposite x2​ and d opposite x3​. Since the tetrahedron is nondegenerate, its scalar triple product T=u1​⋅(u2​×u3​) is nonzero. Put s=T/∣T∣ to handle either orientation of a vector space. Then the required outward vectors are
abcd​=s(u2​−u1​)×(u3​−u1​),=−su2​×u3​,=−su3​×u1​,=−su1​×u2​.​​
(36)
Each cross product has magnitude twice the area of its triangular face. The signs are fixed geometrically: b⋅u1​=−∣T∣<0, so b points away from the opposite vertex; the same test with u2​ and u3​ handles c and d. For the remaining face, the displacement toward its opposite vertex is −u1​, and a⋅(−u1​)=−∣T∣<0, again outward.
Expand the first cross product:
a=s(u2​×u3​+u3​×u1​+u1​×u2​)=−b−c−d.
(37)
Therefore
a+b+c+d=0.​
(38)
This is closure of outward face area vectors, with every face area multiplied by the same factor two. Omitting s would orient all four faces inward when T<0.
