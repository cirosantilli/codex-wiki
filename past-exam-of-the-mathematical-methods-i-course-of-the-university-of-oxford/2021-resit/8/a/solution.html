Differentiation satisfies D(af+bg)=aD(f)+bD(g) for constant scalars a,b, so it is a linear map on suitable vector spaces of differentiable functions. An arbitrary set S need not even be a vector space, and an endomorphism interpretation also requires D(S)⊆S.
For a noninvertible example, take S=span{1,x}. Then D(a+bx)=b and the kernel of a linear map is the nonzero linear span of 1, so D:S→S is not injective. The matrix in this basis is (00​10​).
For an invertible example, take S=span{ex,e2x}. In this basis, D has matrix diag(1,2), and
