Let R=∣α∣, S=∣β∣ and d=∣b−a∣. For distinct centres, the two spherical surfaces intersect exactly when
∣R−S∣≤d≤R+S.​
(6)
Strict inequalities give a nondegenerate circle; equality gives a single tangency point. If d=0, equal radii give coincident spheres and unequal radii give no intersection, so the circle-centre formula below does not apply.
Subtracting the squared sphere equations gives the radical plane
2(b−a)⋅r=∣b∣2−∣a∣2+α2−β2.
(7)
The centre of the circle of intersection of two spheres is the projection of either sphere centre onto this plane. Write c=a+ℓ(b−a)/d; substitution gives ℓ=(d2+α2−β2)/(2d) and hence
c=21​(a+b+∣b−a∣2(α2−β2)(b−a)​).​
(8)
The circle radius squared is R2−ℓ2, providing another check of the intersection conditions.
Its centre equals a precisely when ℓ=0, or β2=α2+d2​. For a positive first radius, the intersection is then a great circle of the first sphere. A triangle from its centre to an intersection point and then to the second centre is right-angled at a, explaining the Pythagorean theorem relation geometrically.

Choose the cylinder centre's initial position as the coordinate origin, with downhill motion to the right and 0≤α<π/2. For rolling without slipping, the clockwise angular speed is ω=v/r. A point initially vertically above the axis therefore has
x(t)=vtcosα+asin(vt/r),y(t)=−vtsinα+acos(vt/r).​
(9)
A different origin adds constant coordinates without changing the following velocity test. Setting θ=vt/r gives x˙/v=cosα+(a/r)cosθ and y˙​/v=−sinα−(a/r)sinθ.
For strictly upward motion, x˙=0 and y˙​>0. The first condition fixes cosθ=−(r/a)cosα; choose the negative sine branch to maximize y˙​. Positivity then requires
(a/r)2−cos2α​>sinα,equivalentlya>r.​
(10)
At a=r the relevant point is momentarily stationary, not moving upward. Thus a material point inside or on an ordinary cylinder cannot move exactly vertically upward in this motion. For A′, the centre and constant remain a=(−1,0,0) and C=3. Its eigenvalues are 3,1,−1, so rotated coordinates put the surface in the form 3u2+w2−y2=3. This is a one-sheet hyperboloid. Since 3∥x∥2≥xTA′x=3, the nearest centre-to-surface distance is at least one, attained in the eigen-direction with eigenvalue three. The coordinate y is unbounded, because arbitrarily large ∣y∣ can be balanced by u or w. Thus the nearest distance is one, and there is no maximum value of y.
For the printed nonsymmetric A′′, use the symmetric part of a matrix:
S=2A′′+A′′T​=​201​011/2​11/22​​,rTA′′r=rTSr.

The skew-symmetric part contributes zero to this scalar. The same centre solves 2Sa+b=0, and again C=3. Sylvester's positive principal minors are 2,2,5/2, so this surface is an ellipsoid.

Assume k>0, m>0 and γ≥0. The spring constant k measures restoring force per displacement from the equilibrium position x0​. The term −γmv is linear drag, with damping rate γ in inverse-time units. Writing ω0​=k/m​, the equation is
x¨+γx˙+ω02​(x−x0​)=0.
(36)
For small positive damping it gives underdamped motion: oscillations about x0​ with a decaying amplitude. If γ=0, the motion is undamped simple harmonic motion.

x¨+γx˙+ω02​x=ω02​de−αt.
(40)
A particular solution is Fe−αt, where F=ω02​d/(α2−γα+ω02​). The denominator equals (α−γ/2)2+ωd2​>0, so it cannot vanish for real α under the given underdamping condition. Imposing both initial conditions gives
