Use the imaginary part of the finite complex sum:
∑n=0N​(nN​)sin(nθ+ϕ)=Im[eiϕ∑n=0N​(nN​)einθ]=Im[eiϕ(1+eiθ)N].
(22)
The identity 1+eiθ=2eiθ/2cos(θ/2) then gives
n=0∑N​(nN​)sin(nθ+ϕ)=2NcosN(θ/2)sin(Nθ/2+ϕ).​
(23)
Here N is a nonnegative integer. Keeping the signed cosine raised to the integer power makes the result valid even when cos(θ/2)<0; inserting an absolute value would lose the sign for odd N.
