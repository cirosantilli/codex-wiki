Measure the car's vertical displacement y from its static equilibrium, so gravity's constant contribution is already balanced. Both the spring and the viscous damper act on motion relative to the wheel. The base-excited oscillator therefore satisfies
My¨​=−k(y−y0​)−γ(y˙​−y˙​0​).
(10)
For dimensional clarity write the road profile as Bsin(qx); the printed notation sets the implicit wave number q=1 in the chosen length units. Constant forward speed gives forcing angular frequency ω=qv. With complex amplitudes Y,Y0​, the steady response is
Y0​Y​=k−Mω2+iγωk+iγω​,A(v)=∣B∣(k−Mq2v2)2+γ2q2v2k2+γ2q2v2​​.​
(11)
Set q=1 for the expression in the question's notation. Assuming M,k>0 and γ>0, the transient response decays, leaving this steady-state response.
For a useful sketch define ωn​=k/M​, r=ω/ωn​ and damping ratio ζ=γ/(2kM​). The displacement transmissibility is
∣B∣A​=(1−r2)2+4ζ2r21+4ζ2r2​​.
(12)
It starts at 1, rises to a finite maximum for finite positive damping, crosses 1 at r=2​, then tends to zero. Differentiation with respect to r2 gives the maximum at r2=2/[1+1+8ζ2​].
Very small damping permits large resonant oscillations near ωn​, producing discomfort and potentially large suspension travel. At exactly zero damping the assumed decay of transients fails, and resonant forcing produces secular growth. Very large damping makes the car follow the bumps over a wide range of speeds, sacrificing vibration isolation: at each fixed forcing frequency, Y/Y0​→1 as γ→∞. For any fixed finite damping the response still eventually decreases at sufficiently high frequency. These two limits should not be interchanged.

For a Hermitian matrix H, H†=H. Reversing the product of n identical factors has no effect, so
(Hn)†=(H†)n=Hn.
(35)
Hence every positive integer power is Hermitian. The identical-factor condition matters; a product of different Hermitian matrices need not be Hermitian.
