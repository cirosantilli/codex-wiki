In a real orthonormal basis, a matrix is a proper rotation matrix exactly when RTR=I and detR=1. The first condition preserves dot products, and the second preserves orientation of a vector space.
Put s=3​, p=(1+s)/3, q=(1−s)/3 and r=1/3, so the rows of the given matrix are (p,q,r),(r,p,q),(q,r,p). Their squared Euclidean norms and pairwise dot products are
p2+q2+r2=1,pq+qr+rp=0.
(68)
Thus RTR=I. For the sign of its determinant, use the circulant form:
detR=p3+q3+r3−3pqr=(p+q+r)(p2+q2+r2−pq−qr−rp)=1.
(69)
This proves that it is a rotation matrix. Its fixed axis is (1,1,1)T, because each row sums to one. The rotation trace formula gives cosθ=(trR−1)/2=3​/2; the skew part selects a positive angle θ=π/6 about the unit axis (1,1,1)T/3​.
The original triangle has edge vectors b−a=(−2,2,0)T and c−a=(−2,0,2)T. Its area is half the Euclidean norm of their cross product:
A=21​∥(b−a)×(c−a)∥=21​∥(4,4,4)T∥=23​.​
(70)
The rotated vertices are twice the three columns of R:
a′=32​​1+3​11−3​​​,b′=32​​1−3​1+3​1​​,c′=32​​11−3​1+3​​​.​
(71)
A rotation matrix preserves lengths and the angle between the two edge vectors, so it preserves their parallelogram area. More explicitly, (Ru)×(Rv)=R(u×v) for a proper rotation matrix, and R(1,1,1)T=(1,1,1)T, so the rotated edge cross product is again (4,4,4)T. Therefore
A′=23​.​
(72)
