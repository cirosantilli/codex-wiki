A negative value of either radius makes the intersection empty. Assume henceforth α,β≥0 and put D=∣d∣. If D=0, the two spheres are concentric: their intersection is the entire sphere when α=β>0, the origin when α=β=0, and empty otherwise.
For D>0, subtract their squared equations to obtain the plane
x⋅d=2D2+α2−β2​.
(24)
The centre of the circle of intersection of two spheres is x0​=sd, where
d=Dd​,s=2DD2+α2−β2​.
(25)
Every point of that plane can be written x=x0​+u with u⋅d=0. Its remaining equation is
∣u∣2=ρ2=α2−s2=4D2[(α+β)2−D2][D2−(α−β)2]​.
(26)
Thus the intersection is a circle of centre x0​ and radius ρ if ∣α−β∣<D<α+β; a single point x0​ at either equality; and empty otherwise. These include tangency to a zero-radius sphere. A negative s places the circle centre beyond the first sphere's centre on the side opposite d.
In Rn for n≥2, the same calculation gives an (n−2)-dimensional sphere in the perpendicular affine hyperplane when ρ2>0, a point when ρ2=0, and the empty set otherwise. In two dimensions the positive-radius case consists of two points. In one dimension the perpendicular hyperplane has only one point, so a nonconcentric intersection exists only when ρ2=0. Concentric equal-radius sets coincide in every dimension.
