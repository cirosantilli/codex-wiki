The characteristic equation of the damped harmonic oscillator is r2+γr+ω02​=0. In the underdamped case its roots are −γ/2±iωd​, where ωd​=ω02​−γ2/4​. Thus the exact real solution is y=Ae−γt/2cos(ωd​t+α). For γ≪ω0​, ωd​=ω0​+O(γ2/ω0​), giving the requested leading approximation
y(t)≃y0​e−γt/2cos(ω0​t+α).​
(6)
For mass m and spring constant mω02​, the mechanical energy is E=21​m(y˙​2+ω02​y2). Differentiating and using the differential equation gives E˙=−mγy˙​2. In particular, energy decay of a viscously damped harmonic oscillator is not an exact pointwise exponential decay: E˙=0 at a turning point. To leading order in γ/ω0​, the period average is
E(t)≃21​mω02​y02​e−γt,Ploss​≃γE.​
(7)
The quality factor is Q=2πE/(energy lost in one cycle). Using the period T≃2π/ω0​ and the small fractional loss γT gives Q≃ω0​/γ​. Equivalently, the energy-decay definition Q=ω0​E/Ploss​ yields the same leading result. Replacing ωd​ by ω0​ in the oscillatory phase is not uniform over arbitrarily long times; its phase error is O(γ2t/ω0​).

Write the matrix trace in Einstein summation convention:
tr(AB)=∑i,j​Aij​Bji​=∑j,i​Bji​Aij​=tr(BA)​.
(12)

Let Av=λv with v=0. A vanishing commutator gives A(Bv)=B(Av)=λBv, so Bv belongs to the eigenspace Eλ​. This is eigenspace invariance for commuting linear maps. A nondegenerate eigenvalue has a one-dimensional eigenspace, so Bv=βv for a scalar β; β=0 is allowed. Therefore v is also an eigenvector of B. Without the simple-eigenvalue condition, B can mix vectors inside a larger eigenspace, and an arbitrarily chosen eigenvector of A need not be an eigenvector of B.

The characteristic polynomial factors as det(λI−A)=(λ−1)(λ2−1). Thus λ=1 twice,λ=−1 once​ in algebraic multiplicity. An orthonormal basis of corresponding eigenvectors is
v1​=​100​​,v2​=2​1​​01−i​​(λ=1),v3​=2​1​​01i​​(λ=−1).
(15)
Direct multiplication verifies Av1​=v1​, Av2​=v2​, Av3​=−v3​. Their mutual Hermitian inner products vanish and each has norm one, which proves linear independence. For the repeated eigenvalue, Gaussian elimination gives
A−I=​000​0−1−i​0i−1​​ ⟶ ​000​100​−i00​​.

A plane through r0​ with nonzero normal vector n consists of the position vectors r satisfying (r−r0​)⋅n=0. Thus r⋅n=d, with d=r0​⋅n. When n is a unit vector, d is the signed distance from the origin in the direction n; for an unnormalized normal vector, that distance is d/∥n∥. The displacement within the plane is perpendicular to n.
The cross product of the two normal vectors is (2,6,−3)×(5,2,−1)=(0,−13,−26), so their intersection of two planes has direction (0,1,2). Eliminating 2y−z gives x=2, and substitution then gives z=2y−2. Thus the Cartesian constraints and parametric equation of a straight line are
x=2,z=2y−2;r=​20−2​​+t​012​​,t∈R.​
(20)
There is no valid division by a zero x component in a symmetric ratio form; retaining x=2 explicitly handles that component.

Substitute the power series and its termwise derivatives into the ordinary differential equation. The coefficient of xn, for n≥0, is
(n+2)(n+1)an+2​+[n(n−1)−6n+12]an​=0.
(22)

an+2​=−(n+1)(n+2)(n−3)(n−4)​an​.​
(23)

The jth column of the matrix representation of a linear map is the coordinate vector of the image of the jth basis vector:
ϕ(uj​)=∑i​Aij​ui​.
(25)
Define C by wj​=∑i​Cij​ui​, so its columns express the new basis vectors in the old basis. For every vector, [v]u​=C[v]w​. Applying ϕ in either coordinate system gives AC[v]w​=CA′[v]w​, proving the change of basis formula
A′=C−1AC.​
(26)
To make A′ diagonal, choose the columns of C to be linearly independent eigenvectors of A, expressed in the u basis, and put their corresponding eigenvalues on the diagonal in the same order. This is possible precisely when A is a diagonalizable matrix over the stated field; an eigenbasis need not exist over an arbitrary field.

Adopt the convention that an inner product is linear in its second slot. Over R, it is a symmetric bilinear form with ⟨v,v⟩≥0, equality only for v=0. Over C, a Hermitian inner product obeys
⟨u,av+bw⟩=a⟨u,v⟩+b⟨u,w⟩,⟨u,v⟩=⟨v,u⟩​,⟨v,v⟩>0(v=0).
(30)
These imply conjugate linearity in the first slot: ⟨au+bv,w⟩=a⟨u,w⟩+b⟨v,w⟩. The strict positivity condition distinguishes an inner product from a general Hermitian form.
