High-temperature superconductivity

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
High-temperature superconductivity is a historical name for superconductivity above earlier metallic benchmarks. The original Bednorz-Müller experiment operated at tens of kelvins, requiring deep cooling; cuprate superconductors opened a new materials family.[ref]
A higher superconducting transition temperature enlarges the available operating range, but useful transport also needs a sufficient superconducting critical current at the operating temperature and magnetic field. In a ceramic, connections between grains can limit the whole specimen even when individual grains support superconductivity. This connects the materials problem to superconducting weak links and the Josephson effect. The parallel-path model of a superconducting ceramic below isolates why connectivity matters.
