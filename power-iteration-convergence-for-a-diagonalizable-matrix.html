Power iteration convergence for a diagonalizable matrix (∣λ1​∣>∣λ2​∣,(V−1x0​)1​=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Numerical linear algebra Power iteration
OurBigBook.com Words: 315 Articles: 1
Suppose A∈Cn×n is a diagonalizable matrix, n≥2, with an eigenbasis v1​,…,vn​ and spectral decomposition
A=VΛV−1,V=(v1​ ⋯ vn​),Λ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​).
(27)
Assume the dominant eigenvalue λ1​ is a simple eigenvalue and
∣λ1​∣>∣λ2​∣≥⋯≥∣λn​∣,x0​=∑j=1n​cj​vj​,c1​=0.
(28)
Then the normalized power iteration approaches the one-dimensional eigenspace of λ1​. Its asymptotic direction-error bound has factor γ=∣λ2​/λ1​∣<1. This spectral gap is a gap in modulus: if g=∣λ1​∣−∣λ2​∣, then γ=1−g/∣λ1​∣. A small relative gap gives slow convergence.[ref]
The short proof is the exact eigenbasis identity
c1​λ1k​Akx0​​=v1​+∑j=2n​c1​cj​​(λ1​λj​​)kvj​.
(29)
The remaining sum has norm bounded by a constant times γk. This proves convergence of direction, allowing a changing sign or complex factor. A tiny c1​ or a poorly conditioned, nonorthogonal eigenbasis can delay visible convergence: the condition number of V affects constants, while γ controls the eventual rate. The bound need not be an equality; if the initial coefficient of the second-largest eigenvalue vanishes, the active spectrum of the power method gives a faster rate. When γ=0, all unwanted components disappear after one multiplication.[ref]
The hypothesis is c1​=(V−1x0​)1​=0, not an ordinary nonzero inner product with the right eigenvector. Let w1∗​ be the first row of V−1, a left eigenvector normalized so w1∗​vj​ is 1 for j=1 and 0 otherwise; ∗ denotes conjugate transpose. Then c1​=w1∗​x0​. For a Hermitian matrix, choose an orthonormal eigenbasis and the criterion becomes v1∗​x0​=0: the starting vector must be nonorthogonal to the dominant eigenvector. For a general diagonalizable matrix, the nonorthogonal eigenbasis makes these two tests different, as illustrated under power iteration failure cases.
