Gravitational potential of a sinusoidal sheet (Φ=−k2πGΣk​​cos(kx)e−k∣y∣)

A sinusoidal surface-density component Σk​cos(kx) on y=0, with k>0 and translational invariance in the remaining direction, produces the displayed gravitational potential. Laplace's equation away from the sheet separates into a cosine in x and a decaying exponential in ∣y∣. Continuity across the sheet excludes a potential jump, while integrating Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity across it gives [Φy​]0−0+​=4πGΣk​cos(kx). For a potential Ccos(kx)e−k∣y∣ that derivative jump is −2kCcos(kx), giving C=−2πGΣk​/k.
More generally a specified jump Jcos(kx) gives potential −Jcos(kx)e−k∣y∣/(2k). Each spatial Fourier mode decays on the length scale 1/k, so fine density structure has a shorter-range field. The sinusoid changes sign and is naturally interpreted as a density perturbation component. General sheet distributions are built by superposing their Fourier modes; decay alone does not restrict a harmonic potential to a single mode.
