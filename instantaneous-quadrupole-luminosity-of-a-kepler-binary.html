Instantaneous quadrupole luminosity of a Kepler binary (L=32G3M2μ2(v2−11r˙2/12)/(5c5r4))

For a Newtonian Kepler orbit, let M=M1​+M2​ be the total mass and μ=M1​M2​/M the reduced mass; put k=GM, n=r/r, u=r˙ and v⊥​=r˙−un, with v2=r˙⋅r˙. In the convention qij​=μ(3ri​rj​−r2δij​)/2, differentiating the acceleration r¨=−kr/r3 gives
q...​​ij​=r2μk​[u(δij​−3ni​nj​)−6(ni​v⊥j​+v⊥i​nj​)].
(11)
The two terms have zero cross tensor contraction, with squared norms 6u2 and 72v⊥2​. Therefore q...​​ij​q...​​ij​=72μ2k2(v2−11u2/12)/r4. Substitution into the quadrupole formula in this convention, L=4Gq...​​ij​q...​​ij​/(45c5), proves the luminosity. It is nonnegative since v2−11u2/12=v⊥2​+u2/12. The formula uses a weak gravitational field, slow motion and leading radiation order; orbital averaging gives a secular luminosity.
