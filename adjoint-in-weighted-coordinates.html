Adjoint in weighted coordinates ([T∗]=GH−1​A∗GK​)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Hilbert space Riesz representation theorem Adjoint operator
OurBigBook.com Words: 100
Suppose a linear map between finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces has matrix A, and their inner products in the chosen bases are ⟨x,z⟩H​=z∗GH​x and ⟨u,v⟩K​=v∗GK​u. The Gram matrices GH​,GK​ are positive-definite matrices. Writing A∗=AT for the ordinary conjugate transpose, the coordinate matrix of the adjoint operator is
[T∗]=GH−1​A∗GK​.
(21)
Indeed, the defining inner product identity says y∗GK​Ax=y∗[T∗]∗GH​x for all x,y. Thus simply transposing and conjugating is valid in orthonormal bases, where both Gram matrices are the identity matrix.
For the weighted inner product ⟨x,y⟩G​=y1​​x1​+4y2​​x2​ and the coordinate swap A=(01​10​), the adjoint operator has matrix (01/4​40​). With x=(1,0)T and y=(0,1)T, both inner products equal 4. The coordinate swap is its own ordinary transpose, but is not self-adjoint in this weighted inner product.
