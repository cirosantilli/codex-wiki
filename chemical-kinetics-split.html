Chemical kinetics

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 12k Articles: 95
Chemical kinetics studies how quickly chemical reactions proceed and how their reaction rates depend on composition and conditions. It connects a measured change in concentration to a rate law, then uses a rate equation to predict future concentrations. This makes it useful for choosing how long to wait for conversion and for testing a proposed reaction mechanism. Changing temperature, reactant concentrations, or the presence of a catalyst can change reaction rates; see Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.2: factors affecting reaction rates.
Chemical equilibrium specifies the eventual balance under given constraints; chemical kinetics determines how rapidly it is approached. At fixed temperature and pressure, a decrease in Gibbs free energy favours conversion but does not guarantee a fast chemical reaction: reaching a transition state can require crossing a substantial barrier. Thus a mixture can change imperceptibly over an observation period without being at chemical equilibrium. The distinction separates which direction is favoured from how long conversion takes. See 12.5 Collision Theory (§12.5: collision theory and reaction barriers) and Chemistry 2e, §16.1: spontaneity and speed.
