Phase conjugation (Uc​(r)=γU(r)∗)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
Phase conjugation creates a returning monochromatic wave whose spatial complex amplitude is proportional to the complex conjugate of the incident field. Fix the time convention throughout:
E(r,t)=Re{U(r)e−iωt},U(r)=A(r)eiϕ(r),Uc​(r)=γA(r)e−iϕ(r).
(18)
Here E is one scalar component of the electric field, A≥0 is the wave amplitude, ω>0 is the angular frequency, and γ is a spatially constant complex number setting the return strength and reference wave phase. The complex conjugation acts on U, including its spatial carrier, while the time factor remains e−iωt. Conjugating both factors would merely give another representation of the same real field. With γ=1, the new real field equals E(r,−t) in this ideal monochromatic wave model; that identity does not assert reversal of an arbitrary pulse or of the apparatus producing it.
The spatial wave phase becomes −ϕ+argγ, so the local wavevector changes from ∇ϕ to −∇ϕ. For example, U=Aei(kx​x+kz​z) with kz​>0 becomes γAe−i(kx​x+kz​z): both components of the wavevector reverse. In a transparent stationary medium with isotropy and positive refractive index, light rays follow the wavevector, giving counterpropagation. The same wavefronts now have opposite propagation normals. This is why phase conjugation can return a distorted beam toward its origin: it reverses the detailed spatial wave phase, rather than requiring that wave phase to be flat.
The attractive application is correction of optical aberrations accumulated on an outward trip. The return uses the distortion itself as the prescription for correction, as the original thin phase-plate cancellation by phase conjugation example makes explicit. An experimentally accessible implementation measures the spatial wave phase and uses a spatial light modulator to generate its negative; digital optical phase conjugation describes an inspected 2010 demonstration of recovery through multiple scattering.
