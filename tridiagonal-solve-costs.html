Tridiagonal solve costs (8n−7)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Tridiagonal matrix algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
For the displayed unpivoted Thomas algorithm with one right-hand side, count each scalar addition, subtraction, multiplication or division as one arithmetic operation. Forming mi​,pi​,gi​ takes five arithmetic operations per forward step. The last unknown takes one division; each remaining back substitution step takes three arithmetic operations. Thus
5(n−1)+1+3(n−1)=8n−7.
(191)
This is an operation-count model, excluding assignments, indexing, pivot checks and any special simplifications of the coefficients. It is not a timing benchmark. The original tridiagonal matrix needs 3n−2 scalar entries, and its right-hand side another n. Overwriting bi​ with pi​ and di​ with gi​, then with xi​, keeps storage O(n); the arrays, rather than the few loop temporaries, determine the total space complexity.
For repeated right-hand sides, retain the LU decomposition: computing mi​,pi​ once costs 3(n−1) arithmetic operations, and each subsequent pair of forward substitution and back substitution sweeps costs 5n−4. Hence r right-hand sides cost 3(n−1)+r(5n−4) arithmetic operations. This is the banded counterpart of Reusing LU factors for multiple right-hand sides.
