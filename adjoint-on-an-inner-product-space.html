Adjoint on an inner product space (⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T∗y⟩)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Hilbert space Riesz representation theorem Adjoint operator
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
On a complex inner product space V, with the inner product conjugate-linear in its first argument, an everywhere-defined linear map T:V→V admits an adjoint on V if there is a linear map T∗:V→V satisfying
⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,T∗y⟩(x,y∈V).
(20)
The adjoint, when it exists, is unique: the difference of two candidate values at y is orthogonal to every vector and hence to itself. In a finite-dimensional vector space, it always exists, and its matrix in an orthonormal basis is the conjugate transpose. In a space that is not complete, existence must instead be checked.
The identity ⟨Tx,y⟩=⟨x,Ty⟩ for every x,y∈V supplies such an adjoint directly, namely T∗=T. This algebraic statement should be distinguished from self-adjointness of a linear operator on a completed Hilbert space, which additionally compares the domains of an operator and its Hilbert-space adjoint. The Gaussian polynomial operator is a useful example where the identity holds on the entire polynomial inner product space and can be used on every finite-dimensional invariant polynomial subspace.
