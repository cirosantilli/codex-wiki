Variational characterization of trace distance

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Density matrix Trace distance
OurBigBook.com Words: 372 Articles: 1
The variational characterization of trace distance turns a matrix distance into the best possible binary experiment. For density operators ρ,σ on the same finite-dimensional vector space,
D(ρ,σ)=max0≤P≤I​Tr[P(ρ−σ)]=max0≤P≤I​∣Tr[P(ρ−σ)]∣.
(15)
An operator P with 0≤P≤I is an effect of a POVM: both P and I−P are positive operators. The Born rule assigns the first outcome probabilities Tr(Pρ) and Tr(Pσ). Their signed gap is Tr(PΔ), where Δ=ρ−σ. Since TrΔ=0, replacing P by I−P reverses this gap, explaining the equality with the absolute-value maximum.
One optimal P is the spectral projector P+​ onto the positive eigenspaces of Δ. In the spectral decomposition of Δ, this choice includes all positive eigenvalues and excludes all negative eigenvalues. Their positive sum equals the trace distance by the spectral-parts formula for trace distance. Any zero eigenspace can be assigned to either outcome without changing the gap. This is the effect selected by the equal-prior Holevo–Helstrom theorem. [ref]
The same optimum has a classical description. For any finite-outcome POVM {Ei​}, let pi​=Tr(Ei​ρ) and qi​=Tr(Ei​σ). Then
D(ρ,σ)=max{Ei​}​21​∑i​∣pi​−qi​∣=max{Ei​}​maxA​​∑i∈A​(pi​−qi​)​.
(16)
The expression 21​∑i​∣pi​−qi​∣ is the total variation distance of the entire outcome probability distributions; the expression for A is the gap for one event. Grouping outcomes in A gives the single effect PA​=∑i∈A​Ei​. Conversely, an effect P gives the binary POVM {P,I−P}, whose two probability gaps are opposites, so their total variation distance is ∣Tr(PΔ)∣. Thus the optimum over all POVMs is already attained by one binary projective measurement. [ref]
For quantum state discrimination with equal prior probabilities, declare ρ on outcome P and σ on outcome I−P. Directly averaging the two conditional success probabilities gives
Psuccess​(P)=21​Tr(Pρ)+21​Tr((I−P)σ)=21​(1+Tr(PΔ)).
(17)
Optimizing this expression gives (1+D)/2. For the diagonal example in trace distance, P=∣0⟩⟨0∣ gives a gap 0.8−0.3=0.5. For zero versus plus, that same effect gives only 1−1/2=1/2, while the effect in Helstrom measurement for the zero and plus states gives the larger gap 1/2​. The trace-distance-preserving binary measurement depends on the pair of preparations; a fixed measurement basis does not generally attain the trace distance for every pair.
