Normal equations for linear least squares (XTXθ=XTy)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Normal linear model Linear regression
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
Minimizing ∥y−Xθ∥2 gives the displayed equations by differentiation. Equivalently, the regression residual r=y−Xθ satisfies XTr=0: it is orthogonal to every column of the design matrix. If X has full column rank, the minimizer is unique and the fitted values are the orthogonal projection of y onto its column space. A rank-deficient design matrix can give multiple coefficient vectors, while the projected fitted vector is still unique.
For an intercept and one predictor, put Sxx​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2 and Sxy​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)(yi​−yˉ​). The equations give β​=Sxy​/Sxx​ and α=yˉ​−β​xˉ, provided Sxx​>0. In the five-point example in linear regression, xˉ=2, yˉ​=2.8, Sxx​=10 and Sxy​=10, giving β​=1 and α=0.8.
The normal equations describe the mathematical optimum, but explicitly forming and inverting XTX can amplify numerical problems. A QR decomposition computes the same least-squares solution through orthogonal coordinates; a singular value decomposition also exposes nearly dependent columns. This connects statistical fitting to numerical linear algebra and the choice of a stable algorithm. [ref]
