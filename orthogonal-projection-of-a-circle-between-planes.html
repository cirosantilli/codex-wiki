Orthogonal projection of a circle between planes (b/a=∣n1​⋅n2​∣)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Inner product Orthogonal projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace Orthogonal projection matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
Under orthogonal projection from one plane onto another, a circle of radius R becomes an ellipse with semiaxes R and R∣cosα∣, where α is the angle between the planes' unit normal vectors. To prove this, choose one in-plane unit vector parallel to the planes' intersection and the other perpendicular to it. The first vector is unchanged by the projection; the second has projected length ∣cosα∣ and remains perpendicular to the first. Parametrizing the circle in that orthonormal basis therefore gives projected coordinates (Rcost,Rcosαsint). Parallel planes have a circular image, and perpendicular planes give the degenerate case of a line segment. An arbitrary center simply projects to the center of the image.
