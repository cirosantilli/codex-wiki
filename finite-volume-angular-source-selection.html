Finite-volume angular source selection (px​(θ)=excosθ/∫−ππ​excosαdα,x=Nh/(kB​T))

Freeze the magnitude of an order parameter and retain just one common orientation θ for N rigidly aligned cells. Give this collective coordinate energy E(θ)=−Nhcosθ at positive temperature T. The Boltzmann distribution is the displayed normalized probability density function, where kB​ is the Boltzmann constant. Here h is energy per cell, a different normalization from the source density in continuous vacuum selection by an external source. This is a deliberately restricted model of selection among already ordered orientations: it does not derive alignment or an ordering critical temperature from a spatial interaction.
Its mean orientation is an elementary ratio of integrals:
mN​(h)=⟨cosθ⟩=∫−ππ​excosθdθ∫−ππ​cosθexcosθdθ​.
(8)
At h=0 every angle has equal probability, and the numerator vanishes exactly. For small x, expansion of the exponential gives mN​(h)=x/2+O(x3) because the angular mean of cos2θ is 1/2. For x=1 and 5 the means are approximately 0.446390 and 0.893383. At fixed h>0, increasing N concentrates the probability density function near θ=0; expanding cosθ=1−θ2/2+O(θ4) gives angular variance approximately 1/x. Consequently
limh↓0​limN→∞​mN​(h)=1,limN→∞​limh→0​mN​(h)=0.
(9)
The uniform angular average remains a symmetric mixture if one sets h=0 first. The other order selects a definite direction. This is the continuous-orientation version of the symmetry-breaking order of limits; it illustrates selection without replacing the separate problem of stability against spatial fluctuations.
