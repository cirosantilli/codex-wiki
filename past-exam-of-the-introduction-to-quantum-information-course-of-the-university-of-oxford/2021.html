Solutions to all three questions. We use the usual Pauli matrices, column state vectors, and the density operator convention ρ↦UρU† for a gate. The CHSH conclusions require independent, uniformly chosen settings and no communication between the devices during a run. In Question 3, a reliable unknown-basis transformation means one deterministic operation that works for every common input basis.

The Pauli multiplication identity gives (s⋅σ)2=∣s∣2I. Its eigenvalues are therefore ±∣s∣, and those of the density matrix are
λ±​=21±∣s∣​.
(1)
Hermiticity and trace one are automatic; positive semidefiniteness holds exactly for ∣s∣≤1. Thus physical qubit states form the Bloch ball, with pure states on the Bloch sphere and mixed states inside.
For the mixture of the two standard-basis states,
ρ=(p0​0​0p1​​),s=(0,0,p0​−p1​),p0​+p1​=1.​
(2)
These form the entire z-axis diameter, shown in the left panel of the diagram in part b. Generate the state by selecting ∣0⟩ with classical probability p0​ and ∣1⟩ with probability p1​, then discarding the classical selection record. A coherent superposition is generally a different state: the forgotten classical record removes off-diagonal coherence.

The six Pauli eigenstates have Bloch vectors ±ex​, ±ey​, ±ez​:
X:Y:Z:​∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​,∣±i⟩=(∣0⟩±i∣1⟩)/2​,∣0⟩, ∣1⟩.​
(3)
Classical convex combinations of density operators produce the corresponding convex combinations of their Bloch vectors. Their convex hull is the stabilizer octahedron:
∣sx​∣+∣sy​∣+∣sz​∣≤1.​
(4)
The six vertices are pure Pauli states; the faces and interior represent their mixtures. To verify the reverse inclusion, give the vertex in the sign of each coordinate weight ∣sj​∣ and use the leftover weight at the origin, itself the equal mixture of either opposite pair.

Conjugation by the Hadamard gate sends (X,Y,Z) to (Z,−Y,X). Therefore its Bloch vector action is
H: (sx​,sy​,sz​)⟼(sz​,−sy​,sx​).​
(5)
It is a rotation through π about (ex​+ez​)/2​: H=(X+Z)/2​ differs by a global phase from the corresponding spin rotation.
The S gate and T gate differ by global phases from z-axis rotations:
S: Rz​(π/2),T: Rz​(π/4).​
(6)
Indeed diag(1,eiθ)=eiθ/2e−iθZ/2, which sends (sx​,sy​,sz​) to (sx​cosθ−sy​sinθ,sx​sinθ+sy​cosθ,sz​).

(7)
Thus each generating Clifford gate conjugates every Pauli operator to another Pauli operator up to sign. Repeated conjugation proves the same for every product of generators and their inverses, hence for the Clifford group. For Hermitian Pauli observables the possible phases here are signs; arbitrary Pauli-group elements also include their conventional phases.
If P∣ψ⟩=λ∣ψ⟩, then (CPC†)C∣ψ⟩=λC∣ψ⟩. Therefore C∣ψ⟩ is another Pauli eigenstate. Every single-qubit Clifford circuit consequently permutes the six Pauli vertices of the stabilizer octahedron.

While the sequence is remembered, the conditional quantum state is the known pure endpoint C∣0⟩, so its Bloch vector is one of the six Pauli vertices. Partial forgetting leaves a probability distribution pj​ over possible endpoints, giving
ρ=∑j=16​pj​∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣,s=∑j​pj​vj​.
(8)
Hence the vector lies in the stabilizer octahedron. The mixing describes lost classical information, not a different unitary evolution of the physical qubit.
Under complete unbiased uncertainty over the six endpoints, pj​=1/6, the opposite vectors cancel and
s=0,ρ=I/2.​
(9)
This is the maximally mixed state at the center. Mere loss of the particular sequence does not mathematically force uniform probabilities: a known biased distribution of endpoint states would leave its corresponding noncentral mixture. The central conclusion uses the unbiased randomization implicit in complete forgetting in this setting.

f OurBigBook.com
Write ∣ψ⟩=a∣0⟩+b∣1⟩ and ω=eiπ/4. After the controlled-NOT gate, the joint state is
2​1​[a∣0⟩(∣0⟩+ω∣1⟩)+b∣1⟩(ω∣0⟩+∣1⟩)].
(10)
An ancilla measurement outcome zero leaves the unnormalized data state (a∣0⟩+ωb∣1⟩)/2​=T∣ψ⟩/2​. Outcome one leaves (ωa∣0⟩+b∣1⟩)/2​=ωT†∣ψ⟩/2​. Both outcomes have probability 1/2. The correction for outcome one is S, and ST†=T, so after correction the data is always T∣ψ⟩, up to an irrelevant global phase. This proves magic-state injection of a T gate.
The resource state's Bloch vector is (1/2​,1/2​,0), outside the stabilizer octahedron. Clifford gates alone preserve stabilizer structure and, with standard-basis preparation and measurement, admit efficient classical simulation by the Gottesman--Knill theorem. Adding the non-Clifford T gate supplies a universal quantum gate set together with Clifford gates: arbitrary finite-qubit unitaries can be approximated to arbitrary precision. The assertion is approximate universality, not exact representation of every unitary by a finite circuit.

a OurBigBook.com
Each local reduced density operator of the Bell state is I/2. Alice's unrecorded local measurement leaves Bob's reduced state unchanged: he still sees a fair random bit. Only after learning her outcome through a classical channel can he identify the corresponding conditional state. Alice cannot choose the random outcome to encode a bit, so the correlations obey the no-signalling theorem.
If the shared state is the trusted pure Bell state, measuring both sides in the Z basis gives identical, uniformly random raw bits. A pure joint state of Alice and Bob has no correlations with an external system, so these bits can form a shared secret. In an actual quantum key distribution protocol, the parties must verify the necessary state/device assumptions and authenticate their classical communication; correlation alone, without a security test, does not certify secrecy against an untrusted source.

The first three angle differences have cosine 1/2​, while the fourth has cosine −1/2​. The first three winning conditions require agreement and the fourth requires disagreement. Each consequently succeeds with the same probability
Ps​=21+1/2​​=cos2(π/8).​

Solution

Words: 174
e OurBigBook.com
For predetermined outcomes, the four products multiply to one:
(A1​B1​)(A1​B2​)(A2​B1​)(A2​B2​)=A12​A22​B12​B22​=1.
(15)
The four desired signs are (+1,+1,+1,−1), whose product is minus one. Thus at least one winning condition must fail for every deterministic assignment. Uniform independent settings give success at most 3/4 per assignment, and averaging over hidden assignments preserves the bound.
An observed rate near 0.85, with enough trials to exclude statistical fluctuation, therefore contradicts Eve's predetermined-local-outcome claim, provided the settings are independent of her devices and the devices cannot communicate during a run. It violates the local bound of the CHSH inequality while remaining consistent with the quantum optimum cos2(π/8)≈0.853553.
The observation is not by itself exact certification of a perfect two-qubit state. Maximal quantum CHSH correlations support CHSH self-testing only with the appropriate quantum/locality assumptions and up to local changes of representation and unused ancillary systems; a near-maximal finite data set requires a quantitative error analysis. An unrestricted communicating pair of black boxes can instead win every run, so the quantum bound is not a bound on arbitrary devices without these assumptions.

No. The independent-setting assumption can fail if Eve controls the random-number generators. Her hidden information can then include the settings that will be selected, and the outcome assignment can depend on that information. For each known setting pair she can prearrange its one tested winning relation, without having to satisfy all four counterfactual relations at once.
For example, arrange equal outputs for the first three setting pairs and opposite outputs for the fourth, conditioning the programmed outputs on the known pair. This can give success one with predetermined records. It does not contradict the 3/4 proof, which assumes the same hidden-assignment distribution for every independently selected pair. This is the measurement-independence loophole, so the claimed security conclusion is not established by such a compromised test.

The partial trace removes the other orthonormal factor in the Schmidt decomposition:
ρA​=k∑​pk​∣ak​⟩⟨ak​∣,ρB​=k∑​pk​∣bk​⟩⟨bk​∣.​
(16)
For instance, tracing ∣ak​bk​⟩⟨aℓ​bℓ​∣ over B gives ⟨bℓ​∣bk​⟩∣ak​⟩⟨aℓ​∣, which vanishes unless k=ℓ. Consequently the nonzero eigenvalues of the two reduced density operators agree, with multiplicity. If the subsystem dimensions differ, the orthonormal Schmidt families can be completed to full bases and the extra eigenvalues are zero.

By linearity, the isotropic qubit map acts as
Er​(ρ)=21​(I+rs⋅σ),s⟼rs.​

It contracts or expands every direction by the same factor and reverses the vector when r<0. More generally Er​(A)=rA+(1−r)tr(A)I/2, so the map preserves trace and Hermiticity for every real r.

The output of a state of Bloch radius u has eigenvalues (1±ru)/2. These are nonnegative for every 0≤u≤1 exactly when
−1≤r≤1.​
(18)
If ∣r∣>1, a pure input already gives a negative eigenvalue. Conversely, ∣r∣≤1 preserves the Bloch ball, hence positivity for states and, by positive scaling, for all positive operators. This is positivity of the map alone; physical implementation on part of an entangled system needs complete positivity.

The Bell state ∣Ω⟩ is an eigenvector of Jr​ with eigenvalue (1+3r)/2. Its three-dimensional orthogonal complement has eigenvalue (1−r)/2. The Choi criterion for complete positivity therefore gives
−31​≤r≤1.​

d OurBigBook.com
Write the unknown common eigenbasis as an unknown unit Bloch vector n. Then ρA​ has vector 0.8n, while ρB​ has vector −0.4n. A single deterministic channel working for every possible basis must therefore map 0.8n to −0.4n for every unit n.
By the basis-independent conversion of qubit Bloch radii, linearity forces that channel to be E−1/2​. Explicitly, add the conditions for n and −n to get E(I)=I, then use the three coordinate directions to fix its action on every Pauli matrix. But −1/2<−1/3, so part iv excludes complete positivity. Thus the forward conversion cannot be implemented reliably without knowing the basis.
The reverse conversion would require E−2​, which is not even positive by part ii. Therefore the reverse conversion is also impossible. Unitary operations interspersed with physically allowed depolarizing maps cannot evade these restrictions: their composition remains a physical quantum channel. The question concerns one basis-independent operation on the supplied qubit, not an additional resource of many copies for learning its basis.

e OurBigBook.com
With the basis known, choose a unitary operator that interchanges its two basis states. On the Bloch sphere this is a rotation by π about an axis perpendicular to n, sending n to −n. Follow it by E1/2​, which is completely positive. Then
0.8n⟼−0.8n⟼−0.4n.
(22)
Thus ρA​ can be converted to ρB​ using the allowed operations and a suitable physically available depolarizing parameter.
The reverse direction would have to increase Bloch radius from 0.4 to 0.8. Unitaries preserve radius, and every completely positive Er​ has ∣r∣≤1, so any composition of the allowed operations cannot increase it. Hence ρB​ still cannot be converted to ρA​ using these resources. This conclusion uses the stated unitary/depolarizing resources; an unrestricted state-preparation channel would be a different allowance.
