Thin phase-plate cancellation by phase conjugation (t(x)t(x)∗=1)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Phase conjugation
OurBigBook.com Words: 365
Model a thin transparent plate by a lossless reciprocal phase screen with transmission t(x)=eiψ(x) at the chosen angular frequency. Both passages multiply the local field by the same t(x): reversing travel does not by itself change the sign of an accumulated optical path length. Assume the plate is stationary, the two passages use corresponding transverse positions, and the plate is adjacent to the return surface or perfectly reimaged onto it. Ignore propagation between these reference surfaces, wave absorption, clipping and intensity-dependent changes. Take the incident boundary envelope to be U0​(x)=A(x)eiϕ0​(x). Outward transmission gives tU0​. Ideal phase conjugation and the second transmission then give
Ureturn​(x)=t(x)γ[t(x)U0​(x)]∗=γt(x)t(x)∗U0​(x)∗=γU0​(x)∗.
(20)
This is a complete short proof of cancellation in the specified phase screen model. For an ideal ordinary mirror with constant reflection factor r, the corresponding envelope is rt(x)2U0​(x): the extra wave phase is 2ψ(x), rather than zero. A constant reflection wave phase changes neither conclusion.
For an original toy optical aberration, let ξ=x/a on −1≤ξ≤1, take ϕ0​=0, and choose
ψ(ξ)=2π​ξ2+4π​ξ3.
(21)
The even term adds symmetric curvature and the odd term makes the two sides unequal. The outward wave phase is ψ; immediately after ideal phase conjugation it is −ψ; after the second passage it is zero. At ξ=−1,0,1, the outward values are π/4,0,3π/4, while an ordinary mirror's completed return has values π/2,0,3π/2. All wave phases here are unwrapped relative to the central ray and are otherwise equivalent modulo 2π. At a stipulated vacuum wavelength λ=532nm, these outward errors correspond to optical path length errors h=λψ/(2π) of 66.5,0,199.5nm. These are chosen values; they are not observations or physical plate thicknesses.
The wave phase plots make a subtle distinction visible: the field is still curved immediately after phase conjugation, and becomes flat only after crossing the same plate again. More generally, conjugation restores U0∗​, not a universally flat wavefront. If the plate instead has transmission t=α(x)eiψ(x) with real 0≤α≤1, the return is γα(x)2U0∗​. Its plate-induced wave phase cancels but its lost wave amplitude does not recover. If the return plate has changed to wave phase ψr​, the residual factor is ei(ψr​−ψ). Those two variations distinguish correction of a stationary optical aberration from universal reversal of loss or material evolution.
