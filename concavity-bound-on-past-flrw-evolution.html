In an FLRW metric with zero separate cosmological constant, ϱ+3p≥0 makes the Friedmann acceleration equation imply a¨≤0. If a0​=a(t0​)>0 and v0​=a˙(t0​)>0, then the scale factor is a concave function and
a(t)≤a0​−v0​(t0​−t)

throughout its regular past interval. Thus a regular positive scale factor cannot persist earlier than t0​−a0​/v0​. If the solution continues regularly whenever a>0, its first past zero occurs within a0​/v0​=H(t0​)−1, where H is the Hubble parameter.
The continuation condition matters: without restrictions on the equation of state, an earlier curvature singularity can occur at positive a. For example, in fixed units a(t)=1+t​, t>0, with k=0, defines energy density ϱ=3(a˙/a)2/(8πG) and pressure p=−ϱ/3−a¨/(4πGa). These satisfy the Friedmann equations and ϱ+3p>0, yet a→1 at the singular past boundary. Conversely the Milne model reaches zero without divergent curvature. A finite duration, a zero of the scale factor, and a curvature singularity are distinct conclusions.
