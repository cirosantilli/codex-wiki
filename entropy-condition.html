Entropy condition (∂t​η(u)+∂x​Q(u)≤0)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Conservation law
OurBigBook.com Words: 89
For a scalar conservation law ut​+q(u)x​=0, an entropy condition restricts its weak solutions to physically admissible ones. For every convex entropy function η, define an entropy flux by Q′=η′q′. The displayed inequality is imposed in the distributional sense. Positive diffusion explains this choice: for ut​+q(u)x​=ϵ[D(u)ux​]x​ with D>0, the entropy balance is η(u)t​+Q(u)x​=ϵ[η′(u)D(u)ux​]x​−ϵη′′(u)D(u)ux2​. The last term is nonpositive, and an appropriate vanishing-diffusion limit yields the entropy inequality. For a strictly concave flux and a single shock, this selects states with incoming characteristics, rather than a discontinuity whose characteristics spread outward.
