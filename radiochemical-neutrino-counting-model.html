Radiochemical neutrino counting model (μs​=ϵηλR​(1−e−λT)e−λd(1−e−λW))

 ... Stellar astrophysics Star Sun Solar neutrino Solar neutrino detection Homestake experiment
OurBigBook.com Words: 274
An idealized radiochemical neutrino counting model separates production, survival, recovery and readout. Suppose a freshly cleared target produces atoms at a constant rate R during exposure T. Each atom has nuclear decay constant λ=log2/h, where h is its half-life. The expected surviving population obeys
dtdN​=R−λN,N(0)=0,N(T)=λR​(1−e−λT).
(3)
The proof is to multiply the differential equation by eλt and apply integration. The expression also follows by summing the exponential decay survival probabilities of atoms produced at every earlier time. Production does not imply eventual recovery: atoms can decay during exposure.
Let η be the fraction recovered instantaneously at extraction, d the subsequent delay before counting, ϵ the probability of recording a decay, and W the counting duration. The expected signal pulses are
μs​=ϵηN(T)e−λd(1−e−λW).
(4)
With constant background rate b, the expected total is μ=μs​+bW. During counting, the expected instantaneous pulse rate is s(t)+b, with s(t)=ϵηλN(T)e−λ(d+t). Equal time windows therefore lose signal by the factor e−λΔt while a constant background stays unchanged. Fitting the time dependence helps distinguish radioactive decay from that background; it does not recover individual capture times.
For an original worked example choose Rh=20, T=W=h, d=0, η=0.8, ϵ=0.5 and bh=0.2. These are illustrative assumptions. Then N(T)=10/log2, and
μs​=log22​≃2.8854,μ≃3.0854.
(5)
Doubling the exposure does not double the surviving inventory: N(2h)/N(h)=3/2. A delay of one half-life halves the signal. At λT≪1, N(T)≃RT; at long exposure it saturates at R/λ. These limits explain why a large target and repeated extraction can matter more than simply waiting longer. The model neglects time-dependent production, finite extraction duration, residual atoms between runs and time-dependent backgrounds.
