Normal component of a superfluid (vn​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Superfluid
OurBigBook.com Words: 519 Articles: 5
The normal component of a superfluid describes the momentum and entropy carried by excitations rather than the phase-gradient condensate current. Its velocity need not equal the superfluid velocity. In phenomenological condensate equations, prescribing its motion supplies a reference flow for dissipation; a full two-component fluid theory would also evolve its momentum and energy.
These are two responses of one liquid, not separable kinds of atoms. Their fractions need not equal the Bose-Einstein condensation fraction.[ref]
