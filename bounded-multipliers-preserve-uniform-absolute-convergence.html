Bounded multipliers preserve uniform absolute convergence (∑n​∣fn​∣ uniformly convergent,supn,x​∣gn​(x)∣<∞)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Absolute convergence Uniform absolute convergence
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
Suppose a series of functions ∑fn​ has uniform absolute convergence on E and (gn​) is a uniformly bounded family of functions there, with common bound M. The triangle inequality gives
​∑n=p+1q​fn​(x)gn​(x)​≤M∑n=p+1q​∣fn​(x)∣.
(104)
The right-hand side tends uniformly to zero as q>p→∞. The Uniform Cauchy criterion proves uniform convergence of ∑fn​gn​. Applying the same estimate to ∑∣fn​gn​∣ proves uniform absolute convergence too.
The shared bound over both n and x matters: separate bounds for each gn​ can grow too fast. For example, the constant functions fn​(x)=2−n and gn​(x)=2n have a summable original series but products identically one.
