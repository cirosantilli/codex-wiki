A scalar field theory describes one or more Lorentz scalar fields through an action. The displayed Lagrangian density is a common classical normalization for a real scalar field; a complex scalar field has two real components. Canonical quantization turns these classical degrees of freedom into operators. The free real scalar field gives spin-zero particles, while interactions arise from the potential V.

Stability of a two-scalar quartic potential (λ≥0,μ≥−λ)

Words: 69
Scalar field theory OurBigBook.com
For real scalar fields with quartic potential energy density V4​=λ(ϕ14​+ϕ24​)+2μϕ12​ϕ22​, nonnegativity in every direction is equivalent to the displayed conditions. Necessity follows on an axis and on ϕ1​=ϕ2​. Sufficiency follows by writing V4​=λ(ϕ12​−ϕ22​)2+2(λ+μ)ϕ12​ϕ22​. With a positive quadratic mass term, equality cases still give a stable isolated classical vacuum at the origin. The quartic alone is strictly positive away from the origin precisely when λ>0 and μ>−λ. Rotation symmetry additionally requires μ=λ.

Canonical scalar spectral sum rule (∫0∞​dσρ(σ)=1)
