Characteristic function (φX​(t))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 22
The characteristic function of a real random variable X is
φX​(t)=E[eitX].
(1)
It determines the probability distribution of X.
Here t∈R and i2=−1. By Euler's formula, its real part and imaginary part are E[cos(tX)] and E[sin(tX)]. The complex exponential has absolute value one, so this expected value exists for every real random variable, even without finite absolute moments. Moreover,
φX​(0)=1,∣φX​(t)∣≤E[∣eitX∣]=1,φX​(−t)=φX​(t)​.
(2)
The bound uses the triangle inequality for a vector-valued integral; the last identity is complex conjugate symmetry.[ref] Consequently the real part is an even function and the imaginary part is an odd function.
For a discrete probability distribution with values xj​ and probabilities pj​, the characteristic function is ∑j​pj​eitxj​: a weighted average of points on the unit circle in the complex plane. For example, if B has the Bernoulli distribution with P(B=1)=1/3 and P(B=0)=2/3, then
φB​(t)=32​+31​eit,φB​(2π/3)=21​+63​​i,∣φB​(2π/3)∣=3​1​.
(3)
Each point being averaged has absolute value one, but their directions partially cancel. At t=π, the same average is 1/3; at t=2π, it returns to one. The characteristic function is thus neither a probability density function nor a probability mass function: its argument is a frequency parameter, and its values can be complex numbers. This Bernoulli distribution has no probability density function with respect to Lebesgue measure, yet its characteristic function is defined at every frequency.
For a probability density function f, the same definition becomes φX​(t)=∫R​eitxf(x)dx, the Fourier transform with the displayed sign convention. The Laplace transform of a nonnegative random variable instead uses e−sX for s≥0, a real decaying weight. For the Bernoulli distribution above, it is 2/3+e−s/3; neither transform is the probability density function. The uniqueness theorem for characteristic functions explains why the oscillating averages still retain the entire probability law, while the characteristic function of a sum of independent variables turns combining laws into multiplication. These two properties make the representation useful even when directly computing a sum's probability distribution is cumbersome.

Characteristic-function derivatives from finite absolute moments (φX(k)​(0)=ikE[Xk])

Words: 148
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
If m≥1 is an integer and E[∣X∣m]<∞, the characteristic function has continuous derivatives through order m, with
φX(k)​(t)=E[(iX)keitX],φX(k)​(0)=ikE[Xk],1≤k≤m.
(4)
Thus finite absolute moments justify recovering moments from derivatives.[ref]
For a short justification of differentiation under the integral sign, the difference quotient for the (k−1)st derivative has integrand (iX)k−1eitX(eihX−1)/h. Its absolute value is at most ∣X∣k, since ∣eiu−1∣≤∣u∣ for real u. Lower absolute moments are finite because ∣X∣k≤1+∣X∣m. Apply the dominated convergence theorem successively, then use the same integrable bound to obtain continuity of each derivative.
For the Bernoulli distribution with parameter 1/3, direct differentiation gives φB′​(0)=i/3 and φB′′​(0)=−1/3, agreeing with E[B]=E[B2]=1/3. Its variance is therefore 1/3−(1/3)2=2/9. Existence and continuity of a characteristic function alone do not authorize these moment calculations: the standard Cauchy distribution, whose expected value is undefined, still has a characteristic function. The stated finite absolute moment hypothesis is a sufficient condition for the derivative formula, not an assumption built into the definition of a characteristic function.

Average 1−cos(uy) over 0≤u≤1/λ. Multiplication by λ gives 1−sin(y/λ)/(y/λ), with value zero at y=0. This is at least 1−sin1 when ∣y∣≥λ. The bound converts control of the characteristic function near zero into tightness of probability measures and is useful in proving the Lévy continuity theorem.

Conditional characteristic function (E[eiθY∣G])

Words: 85
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
For a real random variable Y and a sigma-algebra G, the conditional characteristic function is E[eiθY∣G]. If it equals the nonrandom characteristic function of a probability law for every θ, then Y has that law and is independent of G. To verify the independence assertion, multiply the identity by any bounded G-measurable test variable and use the uniqueness theorem for characteristic functions on the resulting finite measures. Equality for rational θ extends by continuity, so simultaneous null sets can be arranged when needed.

Continuity of a characteristic function

Words: 143
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
Every characteristic function is uniformly continuous and satisfies φ(0)=1 and ∣φ(t)∣≤1. Continuity follows from the dominated convergence theorem applied to eitX.
More explicitly, if tn​→t, then eitn​X→eitX pointwise and ∣eitn​X∣=1. Apply the dominated convergence theorem to the real part and imaginary part separately. There is no finite moment hypothesis. For uniform continuity, the stronger estimate
supt∈R​∣φX​(t+h)−φX​(t)∣≤E[∣eihX−1∣]⟶0(h→0)
(5)
follows by factoring out eitX, whose absolute value is one. The integrand tends pointwise to zero and is bounded by two, so the dominated convergence theorem applies again. The bound is independent of t, which supplies uniform continuity on the entire real line.[ref]
If the first absolute moment is finite, ∣eiu−1∣≤∣u∣ additionally gives ∣φX​(t+h)−φX​(t)∣≤∣h∣E[∣X∣], a Lipschitz continuity estimate. The earlier argument matters because continuity survives even when this expected value is infinite. For convergence in distribution, the separate Lévy continuity theorem concerns limits of whole sequences of characteristic functions; the argument here establishes continuity of one characteristic function.

Irrational unit-modulus frequencies force a point mass

Words: 60
Unit modulus of a characteristic function OurBigBook.com
If u/v is irrational and ∣φX​(u)∣=∣φX​(v)∣=1, then X is almost surely constant. For independent copies X,X′, both u(X−X′) and v(X−X′) lie in 2πZ almost surely; irrationality makes the two corresponding lattices intersect only at zero, so X=X′ almost surely. Two independent identically distributed variables can agree almost surely only when their common law is a point mass.

Uniqueness theorem for characteristic functions

Words: 97 Articles: 1
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
Two finite Borel measures on R with the same characteristic function are equal. One proof convolves both measures with centered Gaussians, applies Fourier inversion to the smooth densities, and then lets the Gaussian variance tend to zero.

Characteristic function of a sum of independent variables

Words: 438 Articles: 2
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
Independence makes the characteristic function of a sum equal the product of the individual characteristic functions.
For mutually independent random variables X1​,…,Xn​, write Sn​=∑j=1n​Xj​. Then
φSn​​(t)=E[∏j=1n​eitXj​]=∏j=1n​E[eitXj​]=∏j=1n​φXj​​(t).
(10)
This is the expectation of a product of independent random variables applied to bounded complex exponentials; no moment hypothesis is needed.[ref]
Identical probability distributions are unnecessary. When the random variables are independent and identically distributed random variables, the product is φX​(t)n. A deterministic rescaling also follows directly from the definition: φaX+b​(t)=eitbφX​(at) for real a,b. Consequently the sample mean has characteristic function φX​(t/n)n, and a sum divided by n​ has characteristic function φX​(t/n​)n.
The independence assumption does real work. Let X take −1 and 1 with equal probabilities and set Y=X. Both characteristic functions are cost, but φX+Y​(t)=cos(2t), not cos2t. At t=π/2 they give −1 and zero respectively. Knowing the two marginal probability distributions does not determine the sum without their joint probability distribution.

Three Bernoulli variables through characteristic functions (φS​(t)=(2/3+eit/3)3)

Words: 174
Characteristic function of a sum of independent variables OurBigBook.com
Let B1​,B2​,B3​ be independent random variables, each with the Bernoulli distribution of parameter 1/3, and let S=B1​+B2​+B3​. Multiplying their characteristic functions and expanding by the binomial theorem gives
φS​(t)=(32​+31​eit)3=278​+2712​eit+276​e2it+271​e3it.
(11)
The coefficients are exactly the probability mass function of the binomial distribution with parameters 3,1/3: the sum takes 0,1,2,3 with probabilities 8/27,12/27,6/27,1/27. Direct outcome counting verifies them: an outcome with k ones has probability (1/3)k(2/3)3−k, and there are (k3​) such outcomes. The coefficients sum to one, recovering φS​(0)=1.
At t=π, φS​(π)=(1/3)3=1/27. This is also P(S even)−P(S odd)=(8+6−12−1)/27: the complex exponential now records parity. The moments agree by two routes: linearity of expectation gives E[S]=1, while the variance of a sum of independent random variables gives Var(S)=3(2/9)=2/3. Summing k2P(S=k) gives the second moment 5/3, also recovered as −φS′′​(0).

Characteristic functions of symmetric sign sums (cos(t/n)n→1,cos(t/n​)n→e−t2/2)

Words: 126
Characteristic function of a sum of independent variables OurBigBook.com
Let X1​,X2​,… be independent and identically distributed random variables taking −1 and 1 with equal probabilities, the Rademacher distribution. The two opposite complex exponentials give φX​(t)=(eit+e−it)/2=cost. Their expected value is zero and their variance is one. For Sn​=∑j=1n​Xj​, the characteristic functions of its sample mean and its variance normalization are respectively
φSn​/n​(t)=cos(t/n)n,φSn​/n​​(t)=cos(t/n​)n.
(12)
For each fixed t, the bases are positive for all sufficiently large n. The Taylor expansion logcosu=−u2/2+O(u4) at zero then gives
nlogcos(t/n)=−2nt2​+O(n−3)⟶0,nlogcos(t/n​)=−2t2​+O(n−1)⟶−2t2​.
(13)
Exponentiating proves the two displayed pointwise limits. The first limit is the characteristic function of the constant zero; the second is that of the standard normal distribution. The Lévy continuity theorem gives the corresponding convergence in distribution statements. Averaging has variance 1/n, whereas dividing the sum by n​ preserves variance one. This sign calculation illustrates why the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem use different scales; it is not a proof for arbitrary probability distributions.

Lévy continuity theorem

Words: 118 Articles: 2
Characteristic function OurBigBook.com
If the characteristic functions φn​ of probability distributions μn​ converge pointwise to a function φ that is continuous at zero, then φ is a characteristic function and μn​ converges in distribution to its distribution. Conversely, convergence in distribution implies pointwise convergence of the characteristic functions.

Characteristic-function tightness bound (μ(∣x∣≥2/h)≤h−1∫−hh​(1−Reφ(t))dt)

Words: 56
Lévy continuity theorem OurBigBook.com
For a probability measure on the real line with characteristic function φ, averaging 1−cos(tx) over [−h,h] gives 1−sin(hx)/(hx). This is at least 1/2 when ∣x∣≥2/h. Integrating gives the displayed bound. Consequently pointwise convergence of characteristic functions to a function continuous at zero implies tightness of probability measures, by the dominated convergence theorem on [−h,h] and a separate tail bound for the finitely many initial measures.

Weak law from a characteristic-function expansion

Words: 21
Lévy continuity theorem OurBigBook.com
If independent and identically distributed random variables have characteristic function φ(t)=1+iat+o(∣t∣) at zero, then their sample mean has convergence in distribution to a: its characteristic function is φ(t/n)n→eiat. This is also convergence in probability by convergence in distribution to a constant implies convergence in probability.
