Put k=β/γ. The nullclines of the space-clamped FitzHugh-Nagumo model are
v˙=0:w=f(v)+I,w˙=0:w=kv.
(17)
The first nullcline is a downward-leading cubic polynomial, shifted vertically by I; the second nullcline is a line through the origin with slope k>0. Their intersections satisfy
I=h(v),h(v)=kv−f(v)=v3−(1+α)v2+(α+k)v.
(18)
Writing M=(1−α+α2)/3, differentiation gives
f′(v)=−3(v−31+α​)2+M,h′(v)=k−f′(v).
(19)
If k>M, h increases strictly, so there is exactly one equilibrium. If k=M, h is still strictly increasing, although its derivative vanishes at one point, so the equilibrium is again unique. If 0<k<M, define
u±​=31+α±1−α+α2−3k​​,Ilow​=h(u+​),Ihigh​=h(u−​).
(20)
Here h has a local maximum at u−​ and a local minimum at u+​; therefore
Ilow​<I<Ihigh​:I<Ilow​ or I>Ihigh​:​three distinct equilibria,one equilibrium.​​
(21)
At either endpoint there are two distinct equilibria, one a double root. These are the saddle-node bifurcation boundaries omitted by the generic one-or-three wording. At k=M and I=h((1+α)/3) there is one triple root. This is the Cubic FitzHugh-Nagumo nullcline criterion.
