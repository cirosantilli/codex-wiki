Max-flow min-cut theorem (maxf​∣f∣=minS:s∈S,t∈/S​c(S,V∖S))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Flow network
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 12
For a finite flow network with finite nonnegative real flow network edge capacities, the greatest strength of a flow of a feasible flow equals the least cut capacity of a source-to-sink cut of a flow network:
maxf feasible​∣f∣=minS⊆V:s∈S,t∈/S​∑(u,v)∈E:u∈S,v∈/S​cuv​.
(16)
For a maximum flow, the vertices reachable from the source in the residual network define a minimum cut whose outgoing original edges are saturated and whose incoming original edges carry zero flow.[ref]
Why is a cut of a flow network an upper bound? Sum flow conservation on its source side S. Internal edge contributions cancel, leaving
∣f∣=∑(u,v)∈E:u∈S,v∈/S​fuv​−∑(u,v)∈E:u∈/S,v∈S​fuv​≤∑(u,v)∈E:u∈S,v∈/S​cuv​=c(S,V∖S).
(17)
Only directed edges leaving S contribute to cut capacity. Directed edges entering S are subtracted in net flow, so adding their capacities to the upper bound would lose the intended bottleneck. This inequality is weak duality; the theorem says the bound is attained, an instance of strong duality. Its beauty is that a routing plan and a separating boundary independently witness each other's optimality.
In the running flow network, the two internal vertices give four possible source sides. For S={s}, the cut capacity is csa​+csb​=3+2=5. For S={s,a} it is csb​+cab​+cat​=2+1+2=5. For S={s,b} it is csa​+cbt​=3+3=6; the incoming edge a→b contributes nothing to that cut capacity. Finally, S={s,a,b} gives cat​+cbt​=2+3=5. Thus the minimum cut has cut capacity 5, matching the explicitly computed maximum flow.
Ford and Fulkerson's 1956 paper opens with a railway-capacity question attributed there to T. Harris and proves a minimal-cut theorem as Theorem 1. Its original formulation uses graph paths carrying nonnegative amounts; the residual reachability certificate for maximum flow below gives the finite directed flow network proof used here.[ref]
