Positive second-moment matrix test for an inertia tensor (S=21​tr(I)1−I⪰0)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics Moment of inertia Inertia tensor
OurBigBook.com Words: 95
For a nonnegative mass distribution relative to an origin, the second-moment matrix is S=∫rrTdm and the inertia tensor is I=tr(S)1−S. Taking the trace gives the displayed inverse formula, which must be positive semidefinite. Conversely, any such S can be realized by pairs of point masses along its orthogonal principal directions. Thus this is also a realizability test when total mass and dimensions are unrestricted. In principal coordinates it is equivalent to the moment-of-inertia triangle inequalities: I2​+I3​−I1​=2S11​≥0, with cyclic counterparts. A symmetric positive definite matrix alone need not be a physical inertia tensor.
