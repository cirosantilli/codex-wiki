Geometric distribution (Geom(p))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 10
Consider successive independent Bernoulli trials with the same success probability 0<p≤1. A geometric distribution models waiting for their first success. Two conventions coexist, so always say what is counted. Let T count all trials up to and including the successful trial, and let F=T−1 count preceding failures. Their probability mass functions are
P(T=k)=(1−p)k−1p,k=1,2,…,P(F=j)=(1−p)jp,j=0,1,….
(19)
The factors describe failures followed by one success; independence permits multiplication. Summing the geometric series verifies that each law has total mass one. Their expected values differ by one, while shifting by a constant leaves the variance unchanged:
ET=p1​,EF=p1−p​,Var(T)=Var(F)=p21−p​.
(20)
The positive convention and these moments are also given by Penn State STAT 414.[ref]
For a constructed retry mechanism with independent attempts and p=0.3, success on attempt four has probability P(T=4)=0.73(0.3)=0.1029. More than four attempts are needed precisely when the first four fail: P(T>4)=0.74=0.2401. The expected values are ET=10/3 and EF=7/3, and the common variance is 70/9≈7.7778. The large spread explains why the average waiting count is not a deadline.
The two probability mass functions have identical heights with different labels: T=4 and F=3 describe the same event. For 0<p<1, consecutive masses have ratio 1−p, explaining the name geometric distribution. The support of a probability distribution is the positive integers for T and the nonnegative integers for F. At p=1, it collapses to T=1 and F=0, both with variance zero; interpret an empty product, including (1−p)0, as one. These counts concern attempts; converting them into elapsed time requires specifying the time spent per attempt.
The survival function makes the counting convention especially visible. Put q=1−p. For nonnegative integers n, and for k≥1,
P(T>n)=qn,P(T≤n)=1−qn,P(T≥k)=qk−1,P(F≥n)=qn,P(F>n)=qn+1.
(21)
The event T>n requires n failures, whereas T≥n requires only n−1 when n≥1. For the constructed p=0.3 mechanism, P(T≥4)=0.343 differs from P(T>4)=0.2401. The geometric success deadline uses the latter tail to control the chance of still waiting.
The striking memorylessness of the geometric distribution says that, for nonnegative integers m,r with P(T>m)>0,
P(T>m+r∣T>m)=(1−p)m(1−p)m+r​=(1−p)r=P(T>r).
(22)
After five failures, the conditional probability of requiring more than two additional attempts is still 0.72=0.49. The analogous zero-based identity uses P(F≥m+r∣F≥m)=P(F≥r); keeping the inequalities straight avoids an off-by-one error. This property follows from fresh independent trials with unchanged p; learning or changing conditions can destroy it. At p=0, success never occurs, so there is no proper finite waiting-count law.
