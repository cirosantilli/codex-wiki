For the spin separation at a smooth quantum Hall edge, linearize the confinement energy at a crossing. Compensating a spin energy difference by a potential-energy change gives
Δy≃∣V′∣EZ​​=3∣V′∣ℏωc​​.​
(3)
Here V′ is interpreted as the specified slope of electron potential energy. If it denotes electrostatic voltage per length instead, the denominator is e∣V′∣. The two edges of a neck and the two sides of an antidot have oppositely directed slopes; the formula is a positive local separation on any one edge. For local filling two, only the n=0 spin pair is occupied at the neck minimum: E0,+​+Umin​<EF​<E1,−​+Umin​. Raising both branches with the confinement produces two crossings on each side, as shown in the original neck sketch. This fixed-slope estimate precedes self-consistent electrostatic screening.
An antidot is a depleted potential hill. guiding centres circulate around its closed equipotentials, with chirality opposite to the outer sample edge. A phase-coherent round trip contributes magnetic phase 2πBS/Φ0​, with Φ0​=h/e. A localized orbit resonant with the incident energy can transfer an electron between extended channels. If the outgoing channel returns toward the injecting reservoir, the process is resonant reflection and reduces electrical conductance. If coupling instead connects an otherwise blocked incident channel to the far reservoir, it is resonant transmission and adds electrical conductance. Transmission peaks require that forward connectivity; a closed orbit by itself does not guarantee them. Interchannel scattering can change which orbit participates.[ref]
For the antidot resonance conductance bounds with one active spin-resolved resonance, write
L(E)=(E−ϵd​)2+(ΓL​+ΓR​)2/4ΓL​ΓR​​,0≤L≤1.
(4)
Here ΓL​,ΓR​ are energy broadenings; the corresponding escape rates are ΓL​/ℏ,ΓR​/ℏ. In a reflection-dominated configuration with two otherwise transmitted channels, G=(e2/h)(2−L): a 2e2/h background can dip to e2/h. In a transmission-dominated configuration with one spectator channel and the second otherwise blocked, G=(e2/h)(1+L): peaks rise from e2/h toward 2e2/h. These are the two ideal channel-count examples plotted below. More generally, G=(e2/h)(Nopen​−R) or (e2/h)(Ndir​+T); a different spectator count changes the integer offset. The constriction filling alone does not specify every tunnel coupling or resonance height. Equal couplings permit unit resonant transfer in the isolated-level model; asymmetry, temperature and dephasing reduce the contrast. Holding the effective orbit area approximately fixed gives ΔB≃h/(eS) for one branch, not a universal charging-corrected period.
In quantum-dot versus antidot resonance topology, a quantum dot is a potential well holding a finite electron island. Its closed guiding-centre circulation is reversed relative to an antidot at the same field. With two barriers in series, current follows source → dot → drain; off resonance there need not be a direct conducting path. A gate shifts a level or an addition electrochemical potential into alignment with the reservoirs and produces a electrical conductance peak. For an isolated coherent spin-resolved level, the zero-temperature peak is at most e2/h and reaches it only for equal couplings; unresolved noninteracting spin degeneracy doubles this bound. In weak sequential tunnelling, peak height depends on rates and temperature rather than being quantized. Charging produces Coulomb blockade peaks with spacing set by addition energy and gate lever arm. An antidot also supports discrete orbit/charging resonances, but can be side-coupled to already open channels and produce dips. Gate voltage changes both orbit area and confinement, whereas a magnetic sweep directly changes enclosed flux.
Coulomb interactions require a self-consistent confinement potential. A gradual decrease in electron density cannot leave arbitrary uncompensated donor charge at no cost. Partly occupied Landau levels have a large charge response: electrostatic screening flattens their effective energy near the Fermi energy over compressible quantum Hall edge strips. Between them, integer-filled incompressible quantum Hall edge strips have a gap, weak density response and most of the potential variation. In an idealized cross-section the effective branch thus alternates between flat, partially occupied portions and rising portions crossing a gap; its carrier density varies oppositely. The bare levels still have Landau level and spin splittings locally, but the common electrostatic potential is no longer an imposed straight slope. exchange interaction can additionally renormalize spin gaps. Widths must follow electrostatics and the density gradient, so the unscreened EZ​/∣V′∣ separation is not a quantitative screened-strip width.[ref]
