The protein fluctuation equation gives δP=(kP​δR+η​P​)/(γP​−iω). With the stated independent noises,
SP​(ω)=γP2​+ω2qP​​+(γP2​+ω2)(γR2​+ω2)kP2​qR​​.
(39)
Partial fractions and the supplied integral give the general variance
⟨δP2⟩=2γP​qP​​+2γR​γP​(γR​+γP​)kP2​qR​​.​
(40)
The specific count-noise formula in the question additionally uses the usual steady birth-and-death noise strengths qR​=2γR​R∗​ and qP​=2γP​P∗​. Then P∗​=kP​R∗​/γP​ reduces it to P∗​[1+kP​/(γR​+γP​)]. Arbitrary unspecified qR​,qP​ do not by themselves imply this particular Fano factor.
The requested rewriting is
F=1+1+ϕb​,b=γR​kP​​,ϕ=γR​γP​​=τP​τR​​.​
(41)
Here b is the translational burst size, the expected number of proteins produced during one RNA's mean lifetime. The ratio ϕ compares removal rates, equivalently RNA to protein mean lifetimes. Poisson molecule-number statistics have variance equal to mean, hence F=1.
When RNA is much less stable than protein, ϕ≪1 and F≃1+b. At fixed degradation rates it rises linearly with the rate of biological translation, with exact slope 1/(γR​+γP​). The formal intercept at zero rate is the limit from positive protein mean; at exactly zero production both mean and variance vanish and their ratio is undefined. A larger Fano factor need not imply a larger relative variance varP/P∗2​, because the mean also increases.
