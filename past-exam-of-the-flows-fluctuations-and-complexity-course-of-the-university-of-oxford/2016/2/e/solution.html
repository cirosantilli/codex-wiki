Define A(H)=(H−1)/H3 for positive thickness. Its derivative is A′(H)=(3−2H)/H4, so its maximum is attained at H=3/2 and equals 4/27. At θ=0, the equation requires A(H)=α, proving the necessary condition α≤4/27.
It is also sufficient for a continuous finite periodic branch. On 0<H≤3/2, A increases monotonically from −∞ to 4/27, so every value αcosθ in the required range has exactly one root on this branch. It joins H=1 where cosθ=0. The second large-thickness root on the ascending side does not extend to a finite periodic branch through those points.
Thus the limiting maximum thickness occurs at the ascending point of the critical film:
αc​=274​,Hmax​=23​,hmax​=2U3Q​.​
(20)
Subcritical films satisfy a strict thickness bound there. Equivalently the largest carried flux and critical absolute thickness are
Qc​=32​gνU3​​,hc​=gνU​​.
(21)
The mechanism is a transport capacity limit: wall transport grows only as Uh, while opposing gravitational drainage grows as h3. At the critical point, adding thickness no longer increases the upward flux. Indeed ∂Q/∂h=U(3/H−2) on the steady branch and vanishes at H=3/2. Excess liquid cannot be maintained as this globally wall-entrained film and must redistribute or drain.
The algebra establishes existence and the limiting branch of the steady lubrication model. A full temporal free-surface stability result requires its time-dependent equation and, when relevant, pressure and capillary corrections; the sign of ∂Q/∂h alone is not a proof of growth of perturbations.
