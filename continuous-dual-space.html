Continuous dual space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 35
The continuous dual X∗ of a topological vector space is the vector space of its continuous scalar-valued linear functionals. For a normed space these are exactly the bounded linear functionals.
This is the continuity-restricted subspace of the algebraic dual space; in infinite dimension the two can differ.

Norming functional (∥ℓ∥=1,ℓ(v)=∥v∥)

Words: 55
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
For a nonzero vector in a real or complex normed vector space, a norming functional is a continuous linear functional satisfying the displayed conditions. The Hahn-Banach theorem extends the functional sending av to a∥v∥ on its one-dimensional span. Consequently ∥w∥≥Reℓ(w), with equality at v, giving a supporting linear lower bound for the norm. At zero the zero functional can be used as a supporting functional.

Norming subspace of a dual space (c∥x∥≤supz∈Z, ∥z∥≤1​∣z(x)∣)

Words: 176 Articles: 2
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
A vector subspace Z⊆X∗ is norming if some c>0 satisfies the displayed inequality. It retains enough continuous linear functionals to control the norm of every vector. Every such vector subspace is dense for the weak-star topology: otherwise a nonzero evaluation linear functional would annihilate its weak-star closure, contradicting the inequality. In the real case its symmetric unit ball makes the absolute-value and signed supremum versions equivalent.

Vanishing sequences norm the summable sequence space (supy∈c0​, ∥y∥∞​≤1​∣⟨x,y⟩∣=∥x∥1​)

Words: 53
Norming subspace of a dual space OurBigBook.com
Finite truncations of the sign or phase sequence of x∈ℓ1 belong to c0​ and attain increasing partial sums of ∑n​∣xn​∣. Thus the space of sequences converging to zero is 1-norming for the absolutely summable sequence space. Its codimension in ℓ∞ is infinite: indicators of pairwise disjoint infinite subsets have linearly independent classes modulo c0​. Norming does not require finite codimension.

Finite-codimensional weak-star dense dual subspace is norming (c=dist(SX​,Y⊥)>0)

Words: 68
Norming subspace of a dual space OurBigBook.com
A norm-closed finite-codimensional vector subspace Y⊆X∗ that is weak-star dense is a norming subspace. Its annihilator F=Y⊥⊆X∗∗ is finite-dimensional and disjoint from JX. The positive distance d of the unit sphere from F gives a triple-dual linear functional vanishing on F and taking a value at least d at a unit vector. Apply the finite-dimensional interpolation form of Goldstine's theorem to the Banach space X∗, then normalize and take a supremum to obtain norming constant d.

Continuous-dual separation theorem for Hausdorff locally convex spaces (X∗ separates points of X)

Words: 55
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
In a Hausdorff locally convex space, a separating family of continuous seminorms has p(x)>0 for any chosen nonzero x and some p. Define a linear functional on its one-dimensional span attaining p(x) and extend it under that seminorm by the real Hahn-Banach theorem. The extension is continuous because it is seminorm-bounded, and distinguishes x from zero. The Hausdorff condition is essential.

Strong dual topology (β(E′,E))

Words: 55
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
For a locally convex space E, the strong dual topology on its continuous dual space E′ is generated by seminorms pB​(T)=supφ∈B​∣⟨T,φ⟩∣, where B runs through the bounded sets in a topological vector space E. Convergence therefore means uniform convergence of pairings on every such set. For the Schwartz space, the defining boundedness is boundedness of every Schwartz seminorm, rather than one norm or one common compact support.

Dual pairing (⟨f,x⟩)

Words: 27
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
The dual pairing evaluates a linear functional on a vector: ⟨f,x⟩=f(x). It is the basic pairing between a topological vector space and its continuous dual.

Positive linear functional

Words: 224 Articles: 4
Continuous dual space OurBigBook.com
A linear functional Λ on an ordered vector space of functions is positive when f≥0 implies Λ(f)≥0. Positivity implies monotonicity: f≤g gives Λ(f)≤Λ(g).

Jordan decomposition of a bounded functional on C(K) (ϕ=ϕ+−ϕ−,∥ϕ∥=∥ϕ+∥+∥ϕ−∥)

Words: 52
Positive linear functional OurBigBook.com
For a bounded real linear functional on the space of continuous functions on a compact space, define ϕ+(f)=sup0≤g≤f​ϕ(g) for f≥0. The decomposition g=min(g,f)+(g−f)+ for 0≤g≤f+h proves additivity on the positive cone; positive homogeneity is immediate. Extend by differences, and put ϕ−=ϕ+−ϕ. Both are positive linear functionals. Their norms sum to 2ϕ+(1)−ϕ(1)=sup∥g∥∞​≤1​ϕ(g)=∥ϕ∥, using the substitution g=2h−1.

Positive extension from a unital subspace of C(K)

Words: 60
Positive linear functional OurBigBook.com
A real positive linear functional on a vector subspace of C(K) containing 1 extends positively to all of C(K). Its norm is ϕ(1) by order bounds. If this is positive, apply the Hahn-Banach theorem to obtain an extension of the same norm, normalize it to value one at 1, and use the unital contraction positivity criterion. If ϕ(1)=0, the functional is zero and its zero extension suffices.

Norm of a positive functional on C(K) (∥ϕ∥=ϕ(1))

Words: 30
Positive linear functional OurBigBook.com
For a real positive linear functional on the space of continuous functions on a compact space, −∥f∥∞​1≤f≤∥f∥∞​1 gives ∣ϕ(f)∣≤ϕ(1)∥f∥∞​. Evaluation at 1 gives equality of the operator norm and ϕ(1). Thus positivity alone already implies continuity in the supremum norm.

Unital contraction positivity criterion (∥T∥≤1,T1=1⟹T is positive)

Words: 56
Positive linear functional OurBigBook.com
A real-linear contraction on C(X) that preserves 1 is positive: for 0≤f≤1, ∥1−Tf∥∞​≤1 forces Tf≥0, then scale. In the complex case, a unital norm-one functional is real on real functions, since ∣1+itϕ(g)∣≤∥1+itg∥∞​=1+O(t2) for both signs of t. The same positivity argument applies pointwise to ϕ(f)=Tf(x). This turns a unital contraction semigroup into a Feller semigroup.

Riesz-Thorin theorem

Words: 58
Operator norm OurBigBook.com
For a linear operator bounded from Lpi​ to Lqi​ with norms Mi​, i=0,1, interpolate reciprocals by 1/p=(1−θ)/p0​+θ/p1​ and 1/q=(1−θ)/q0​+θ/q1​. The intermediate operator norm is at most M01−θ​M1θ​. The complex interpolation proof first treats simple functions; completeness and continuity on the endpoint sum spaces identify the extension with the original operator. On finite counting spaces it also gives norm bounds between sequence spaces.

Operator norm duality (∥A∥=∥A∗∥)

Words: 48
Operator norm OurBigBook.com
For bounded operators between Hilbert spaces, write the norm as the supremum of ∣⟨Ax,y⟩∣ over unit vectors x,y. Moving the operator to the other slot proves equality of its norm with that of its adjoint operator. Applied to the exponential matrix, this makes the primal and dual analytic large sieve inequalities equivalent.

Euclidean logarithmic norm (μ2​(A)=λmax​((A+A∗)/2))

Words: 106 Articles: 1
Operator norm OurBigBook.com
The Euclidean logarithmic norm is the largest eigenvalue of a matrix's Hermitian part. Differentiating ∥y∥22​ along y′=Ay gives ∥etA∥2​≤etμ2​(A). It is the least exponent for such a bound with prefactor one, as a first-order expansion at zero shows. It equals the spectral abscissa for a normal matrix, but need not do so otherwise. A scalar rational stability function does not generally inherit a bound by its value at this real number.

Euclidean semigroup growth bounds (ets(A)≤∥etA∥2​≤etμ2​(A))

Words: 42
Euclidean logarithmic norm OurBigBook.com
The spectral radius of the matrix exponential gives the lower bound through the spectral abscissa s(A). Differentiating the squared Euclidean norm along q′=Aq and bounding its Hermitian quadratic form gives the upper bound through the Euclidean logarithmic norm μ2​(A). A non-normal matrix can have μ2​(A)>0 despite s(A)<0, allowing transient growth before its ultimate decay.

Submultiplicativity of the operator norm (∥AB∥≤∥A∥∥B∥)

Words: 27
Operator norm OurBigBook.com
For composable bounded linear operators, the operator norm satisfies ∥AB∥≤∥A∥∥B∥. Indeed, ∥ABx∥≤∥A∥∥Bx∥≤∥A∥∥B∥∥x∥, and taking the supremum over unit vectors proves the bound. It implies ∥[A,B]∥≤2∥A∥∥B∥ by the triangle inequality.

Completeness of the dual space

Words: 28

The continuous dual of every normed space is Banach because an operator-norm Cauchy sequence converges pointwise to a bounded linear functional and then uniformly on the unit ball.

Coordinate pairing gives (ℓp)∗=ℓq for 1<p<∞, (ℓ1)∗=ℓ∞, and (c0​)∗=ℓ1.

Duality of l1 and l infinity ((ℓ1)∗=ℓ∞)

Words: 136 Articles: 3

For y∈ℓ∞, the formula ϕy​(x)=∑n​xn​yn​ defines a bounded functional on ℓ1 with ∥ϕy​∥=∥y∥∞​. Conversely, every ϕ∈(ℓ1)∗ is represented this way by the bounded sequence yn​=ϕ(en​). This is an isometric isomorphism of normed spaces.

Schur property

Gliding hump argument
Words: 90 Articles: 1
Schur property OurBigBook.com
A gliding hump argument selects a subsequence and successive finite coordinate blocks so that each selected vector has little mass before and after its assigned block. A bounded dual vector can then align its signs independently on those disjoint blocks.
Schur property of l1 (ℓ1)
Words: 50
Gliding hump argument OurBigBook.com
The l-p sequence space ℓ1 has the Schur property. If a weakly null sequence stayed bounded below in norm, coordinatewise convergence and summability would select disjoint blocks containing almost all of successive terms. A sequence in ℓ∞ matching their signs on those blocks would pair uniformly positively with a subsequence, contradicting weak convergence through the duality of l1 and l infinity.

Closed-hyperplane proof of Lp duality (ℓ(f)=∫fg)

Words: 78
Duality of Lp spaces OurBigBook.com
Let 1<p<∞ and q=p/(p−1). For a nonzero bounded linear functional ℓ on an Lp space, choose f0​ with ℓ(f0​)=1 and a closest point h in its closed kernel. Put z=f0​−h. The variational condition is ∫∣z∣p−2zk=0 for every kernel element k. Since f−ℓ(f)z lies in the kernel, ℓ(f)=∫fg with g=∣z∣p−2z/∥z∥pp​∈Lq. Hölder's inequality gives the upper norm bound; testing against ∣g∣q−2g​ gives equality. Thus the representation is an isometric linear isomorphism when the pairing is bilinear.

Lp duality on an arbitrary measure space ((Lp)∗≅Lq,1<p<∞)

Words: 120 Articles: 1
Duality of Lp spaces OurBigBook.com
For conjugate exponents p,q∈(1,∞), every bounded linear functional on an Lp space is uniquely h↦∫hgdμ for g∈Lq, with functional norm ∥g∥q​. This holds on arbitrary measure spaces. Apply the Radon-Nikodym theorem on finite-measure pieces, then use support localization of an Lp functional to obtain one global density without assuming sigma-finiteness of the entire measure.

Support localization of an Lp functional (g=0 outside a sigma-finite S)

Words: 75
Lp duality on an arbitrary measure space OurBigBook.com
For 1<p<∞, local Radon-Nikodym derivatives gE​ representing an Lp functional on finite-measure sets have uniformly bounded Lq mass. Choose a sequence of finite-measure sets approaching the supremum of that mass, and glue their compatible densities on their union S. Any finite-measure set outside S must have zero local density, since otherwise it would increase the supremum. Every Lp space function is approximable by simple functions of finite-measure support, so the glued density represents the functional on the whole space.

Dual of L infinity is larger than L1 ((L∞)∗⊋L1)

Words: 55
Duality of Lp spaces OurBigBook.com
On Rn, integration embeds L1 isometrically into (L∞)∗, but this is not surjective. For example, the functional taking the limit at infinity on C1⊕C0​(Rn) extends to L∞ by the Hahn-Banach theorem; it vanishes on compactly supported functions and is one on the constant function, so no L1 density represents it.

Reflexivity of Lp spaces

Words: 26
Duality of Lp spaces OurBigBook.com
For 1<p<∞, the identifications (Lp)∗=Lq and (Lq)∗=Lp make Lp reflexive. On a non-atomic space such as Rn, neither L1 nor L∞ is reflexive.
