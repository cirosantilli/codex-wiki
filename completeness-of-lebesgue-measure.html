Completeness of Lebesgue measure

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Sigma-finite uniqueness theorem for measures Lebesgue measure
OurBigBook.com Words: 94
Every subset B of a Lebesgue null set is a Lebesgue measurable set and is itself a null set. In fact the same conclusion follows directly from m∗(B)=0, without assuming measurability in advance. For any A⊆R, monotonicity of Lebesgue outer measure gives m∗(A∩B)=0 and m∗(A∖B)≤m∗(A). Therefore
m∗(A∩B)+m∗(A∖B)≤m∗(A).
(53)
The reverse inequality follows from countable subadditivity of Lebesgue outer measure, applied to the union A=(A∩B)∪(A∖B). This is Carathéodory's criterion, proving measurability. If B is a subset of an outer-null N, its outer measure is zero by the same monotonicity. Thus the restriction of outer measure to measurable sets is a complete measure.
