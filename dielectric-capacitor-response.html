Dielectric capacitor response (C=ϵr​C0​)

Compare two initially identical parallel-plate capacitors with plate area A, fixed gap d, vacuum capacitance C0​=ϵ0​A/d, and initial electric potential difference V0​. Completely fill both gaps with the same uniform linear dielectric of static relative permittivity ϵr​>1, neglecting the fringing field of a capacitor. Keep one connected to an ideal voltage source and isolate the other. The geometric capacitance becomes C=ϵr​C0​ in both cases; the electrical constraint determines what else changes.[ref]

Thus the statement that a dielectric reduces the electric field needs the fixed-free-charge condition. With fixed electric potential difference, the voltage source supplies additional free plate electric charge, maintaining E=V0​/d despite the new bound electric charges.
For the stipulated ϵr​=3 example, the voltage source supplies ΔQ=2Q0​. At constant V0​, its delivered electrical work is V0​ΔQ=4U0​, whereas the increase in capacitor energy is 2U0​. In an ideal reversible quasistatic insertion, the remaining 2U0​ is mechanical work delivered by the assembly, as accounted for in fixed-voltage capacitor energy balance. For the isolated assembly, capacitor energy falls from U0​ to U0​/3, allowing 2U0​/3 of mechanical work in the same ideal limit. Actual losses reduce recoverable work; energy increases in the connected case do not arise from the dielectric supplying free energy.
