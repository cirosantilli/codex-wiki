Thermal diffusion time (L2/κ)

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Thermal conduction Thermal diffusivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 558 Articles: 2
The thermal diffusion time tD​=L2/α, with α=κ the thermal diffusivity, is a characteristic scale for smoothing a temperature variation over length L. It is the thermal instance of the diffusive time scale. Rescaling x=Lx and t=(L2/α)t removes L and α from the constant-coefficient heat equation. Doubling length therefore multiplies comparable internal equilibration times by four; increasing thermal diffusivity by four divides them by four.
This scale is not a universal time of complete thermal equilibrium. A nonzero Fourier mode decays exponentially and never becomes exactly zero at finite time. The geometry, boundary conditions, initial pattern and accepted residual determine the numerical factor. For example, an insulated bar's slowest nonuniform Fourier mode has decay time L2/(π2α), and reducing its amplitude to a fraction ε requires
tε​=π2αL2​logε1​,0<ε<1.
(26)
External heat transfer can impose a longer cooling time even when internal temperature differences smooth quickly. Thermal diffusivity alone does not determine cooling to the room's temperature.
