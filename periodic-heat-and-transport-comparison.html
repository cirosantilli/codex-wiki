Periodic heat and transport comparison (uH​=1+e−tcosx+0.4e−9tcos(3x),uT​=g(x−t))

OurBigBook.com Words: 236
Use dimensionless x and t, the periodic domain [0,2π], and the same initial condition
g(x)=1+cosx+0.4cos(3x).
(1)
With zero PDE source term, compare the heat equation ut​=uxx​ and the transport equation ut​+ux​=0. Their exact solutions are
uH​(x,t)=1+e−tcosx+0.4e−9tcos(3x),uT​(x,t)=1+cos(x−t)+0.4cos(3(x−t)).
(2)
Each cosine satisfies ∂x2​cos(nx)=−n2cos(nx), so the heat equation reduces its amplitude according to An′​=−n2An​. The superposition principle combines the constant and two Fourier modes. The transport equation shifts the argument of the entire profile. Both formulas meet the initial condition and matching periodic boundary conditions exactly.[ref]
At t=1, the heat amplitudes are approximately 0.368 and 0.0000494: the small ripples disappear much sooner than the broad variation. The transport equation retains amplitudes 1 and 0.4, shifting the profile right by one unit with wraparound. Both preserve spatial average 1, but only the heat equation approaches the uniform profile. This gives diffusion its usefulness for smoothing uneven temperatures, while the transport equation models a profile carried by a flow. The signed profile can represent a scaled temperature deviation.
To restore units, let physical position be X=ℓx and time be T=τt. For UT​=DUXX​, the dimensionless coefficient is Dτ/ℓ2, with D measured in length squared per time. For UT​+cUX​=0, it is cτ/ℓ, with c measured in length per time. Both plotted coefficients equal one when D=ℓ2/τ and c=ℓ/τ. Thus the example compares specified models on the same scales, rather than identifying diffusivity with velocity.
