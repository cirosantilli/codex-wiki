Gaussian short-rate model with a deterministic shift (rt​=g(t)+σWtQ​)

Under a risk-neutral measure Q, let the short rate be rt​=g(t)+σWtQ​, with deterministic locally integrable g and constant σ>0. For a zero-coupon bond paying one at T, the risk-neutral pricing value is
Pt​(T)=exp[−∫tT​g(s)ds−σ(T−t)WtQ​+6σ2(T−t)3​].
(24)
Indeed, conditional on Ft​, the random part of the integrated future short rate is σ∫tT​(T−u)dWuQ​, which is Gaussian with mean zero and variance σ2(T−t)3/3. Its exponential moment gives the formula. At points where g is continuous, the instantaneous forward rate is
ft​(T)=−∂T​logPt​(T)=g(T)+σWtQ​−21​σ2(T−t)2.
(25)
For merely locally integrable g, the same identity holds almost everywhere. The model allows negative short rates and gives explicit bond prices and forward curves.
