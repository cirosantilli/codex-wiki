The quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity perturbation combines relative vorticity, ∇h2​ψ′, with the stretching contribution f02​∂z​(N−2ψz′​). The latter measures how buoyancy surfaces and rotating fluid columns are displaced in a stratified fluid. Even a flow with no vertical dependence can have nonzero relative vorticity, while its explicit stretching term vanishes.
For a vertically uniform plane wave with horizontal wavenumbers (k,l), q′=−(k2+l2)ψ′. Substitution gives

Thus phase propagates westward relative to the current. Physically, northward parcel displacement carries lower background planetary vorticity into a region with larger f, requiring negative relative vorticity to conserve total potential vorticity; southward displacement requires positive relative vorticity. The velocity induced by that vorticity pattern shifts the displacement wave westward. This is a statement about phase motion, not a claim that every Rossby-wave packet has westward group velocity.
