Take S as the integrated line mass absorption coefficient, and q as the absorber mass fraction, assumed constant; for a pure absorbing gas set q=1. All spectral intervals below use the paper's wavenumber coordinate. The normalized Lorentz line shape is
κν​(p)=πS​(ν−ν0​)2+Δ(p)2Δ(p)​,κν0​​(p)=πΔ(p)S​.​
(11)
If instead an absorption coefficient per length is wanted, multiply by absorber density. Neglecting temperature dependence, pressure broadening gives Δ(p)=Δs​p/ps​. In the line wings, with d=∣ν−ν0​∣≫Δ(p),
κν​≃πps​d2SΔs​p​,τν​(p)≃2πgps​d2qSΔs​p2​.
(12)
Define the line-opacity parameter and characteristic wavenumber width
C=2πgΔs​qSps​​,w=Δs​C​.
(13)
At an emitting level τν​∼1, the wing estimate implies d/Δ(p)∼C​. Thus the strong-line limit relevant to outgoing radiation is
C≫1,equivalently qSps​/(gΔs​)≫1 up to fixed factors.​
(14)
It ensures that the opaque-to-transparent transition lies in the Lorentz wings, rather than the narrow central core, and the characteristic absorption width is much larger than Δs​. At a grazing ray angle the criterion is even easier to satisfy because of the larger optical depth. This is a criterion for the radiatively important wings, not a claim that collision broadening remains physical at arbitrarily small pressure.
