For a=0,21​,1,23​,2,…, put n=2a=0,1,2,3,4,…. Repeated integration by parts gives Γ(n+1)=n!. The hemispheric thick-limit results are

The second row assumes fixed surface temperature; the third assumes fixed absorbed radiant flux. In the vertical-ray approximation the first of these rows becomes 1,1/τ0​,2/τ02​,6/τ03​,24/τ04​, and the surface temperature factors are the inverse fourth roots.
Thus increasing a makes the atmosphere colder at every fixed 0<p<ps​, decreases outgoing radiation at fixed Ts​, and requires a warmer surface at fixed absorbed radiant flux. The case a=0 is isothermal and gives F=σTs4​ exactly, including finite optical depth.
There is an essential approximation limit. The extension of the integral to infinity is valid for fixed a as τ0​→∞, and is not uniform for arbitrarily large a at fixed τ0​. The vertical integrand peaks at t=2a, so one needs that peak well above the surface in optical depth, with a negligible tail beyond τ0​. For example, at τ0​=10 the thick formula predicts F/(σTs4​)=20!/(111020)≃0.00221 for a=10 but approximately 0.04773 for a=12. This artificial increase contradicts the exact monotonicity: in the finite-optical depth expression every atmospheric emission factor (t/τ0​)2a decreases with a. At fixed finite τ0​, the exact limiting outgoing radiant flux is the transmitted surface term 2σTs4​E3​(τ0​), rather than factorial divergence.
