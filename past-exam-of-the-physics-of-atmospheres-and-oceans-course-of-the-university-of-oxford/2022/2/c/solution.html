Write τ0​=τ∞​, n=2a, and t=τ0​(p/ps​)2. The integrated Planck function is B(T)=σT4/π by the Stefan–Boltzmann law, and
B(t)=πσTs4​​(τ0​t​)n.
(14)
The formal radiative transfer equation solution for upward radiance at the top is
I(0,μ)=πσTs4​​e−τ0​/μ+∫0τ0​​B(t)e−t/μμdt​,0<μ≤1.
(15)
The first term is transmitted surface emission; the second is emission from the atmosphere. Integrating radiance over the upward hemisphere gives the outgoing radiant flux per unit area,
F=2σTs4​[E3​(τ0​)+τ0−n​∫0τ0​​tnE2​(t)dt],Ej​(t)=∫01​μj−2e−t/μdμ.​
(16)
Here Ej​ is an exponential integral. For fixed a and τ0​→∞, surface transmission is negligible and the upper limit can be extended to infinity. Positivity permits interchanging the integrals; putting t=μx gives
∫0∞​tnE2​(t)dt=∫01​μn+1dμ∫0∞​xne−xdx=n+2Γ(n+1)​.
(17)
Consequently the exact hemispheric optically thick result is
F∼σTs4​τ0−2a​a+1Γ(2a+1)​.​
(18)
This is a dimensional scale times a dimensionless definite integral depending only on a. In the commonly used vertical-ray approximation, define Fv​=πI(0,1). Then
Fv​=σTs4​[e−τ0​+τ0−2a​∫0τ0​​t2ae−tdt]∼σTs4​τ0−2a​Γ(2a+1).
(19)
A vertical-ray approximation therefore has the same scaling but omits the angular factor 1/(a+1). Stating which quantity is used prevents a purely angular normalization discrepancy.
