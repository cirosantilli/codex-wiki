Use one spectral resolution element, Δν=0.5cm−1, and assume comparable angular resolution horizontally and vertically, so the pixel solid angle is Ω≃θ2=6.67×10−6sr. The question specifies only the vertical angle; without an assumption about horizontal field of view the diameter is not unique. Interpret throughput η=0.5 as dimensionless transmission. With primary collecting area Ap​=πd2/4, the power in that element is
Ps​=ηBν​ΔνAp​Ω=1.30×10−7Ap​W,
(37)
when Ap​ is in square metres. There is no extra factor of π multiplying the supplied spectral radiance.
For an integration time t=1 s, the order-of-magnitude effective noise bandwidth is Δf≃1/t=1 Hz. The root-mean-square noise-equivalent power is then Pn​=NEPΔf​=5×10−11 W.[ref] A signal-to-noise ratio of 10 requires Ps​=5×10−10 W, and therefore
Ap​≃3.84×10−3m2,d≃7.0cm.​
(38)
An ideal rectangular average of a one-sided white noise spectral density has Δf=1/(2t), giving d≃5.9 cm instead. A circular field with diameter θ changes the diameter by another order-unity factor. These convention choices all require an aperture substantially larger than the 9.5 mm diffraction estimate, so signal collection is the stronger constraint. The estimate treats the quoted NEP as applicable to the reconstructed channel; a detailed Fourier transform spectroscopy design would also specify scan weighting, background and how interferogram noise propagates into that channel.
