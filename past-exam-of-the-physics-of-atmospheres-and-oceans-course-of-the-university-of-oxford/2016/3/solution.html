The polar plot has equal forward and backward lobes, each with radius 3/2, and lateral values 3/4. A small nonabsorbing particle behaves as an induced electric dipole. As the size parameter 2πr/λ grows, Rayleigh scattering in the electric-dipole approximation no longer suffices. Higher multipoles and interference produce structure in the angular pattern, and large particles generally develop a strong narrow forward diffraction peak. Their g is normally positive, although detailed values and angular features depend on refractive index, size distribution and shape; they are not fixed by radius alone. Large spheres may show rainbows and backscattering structure, while nonspherical ice introduces further angular features.

The respective transport-depth factors are 0.15, 0.30 and 1. The water cloud scatters most strongly forward and returns the least light backward. All transmittances tend to zero in the infinite-depth limit. The strictly unscattered direct-beam transmittance is instead e−τ, identical in the three cases: the ranking concerns total transmitted radiation, including forward-scattered light, not that direct component alone. Full radiative transfer can also depend on higher angular moments beyond g.
