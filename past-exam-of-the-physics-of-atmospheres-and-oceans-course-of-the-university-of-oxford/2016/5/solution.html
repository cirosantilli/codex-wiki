Detector noise and graphical reading. Noise-equivalent power specifies the incident optical power producing signal-to-noise ratio one, with the electrical bandwidth and averaging convention stated. A spectral NEP has units WHz−1/2; a band-integrated NEP has units W. Specific detectivity normalizes the latter by detector area and noise bandwidth:
D∗​=NEPΔf​Ad​Δf​​=NEPspectral​Ad​​​​.
(31)
Its usual unit, the Jones, is cmHz1/2W−1. Removing the expected square-root area and bandwidth dependence helps compare different detector sizes and readout bandwidths, provided noise conditions and spectral response are comparable. This does not normalize away every background, temperature or implementation difference.
Reading the native embedded voltage plot gives approximate means VC​≃0.331 V, VS​≃0.406 V and VH​≃0.493 V. The one-second root mean square scatter is about 0.006 V; the plotted peak-to-peak excursions are larger and must not be substituted for an root mean square noise. Independent graphical digitization of the blue trace gives means near 0.331,0.406,0.493 V and scatters about 0.006–0.007 V, consistent with this estimate. These are readings from a drawing, not exact original data.
The supplied Planck approximation gives BC​≃0.08590 and BH​≃0.12836 in Wm−2sr−1(cm−1)−1. Thus the noise-equivalent radiance of one one-second sample is
NER≃VH​−VC​BH​−BC​​σV​≃1.6×10−3 Wm−2sr−1(cm−1)−1​.

Here the calibrating means are used to determine the response slope; their own uncertainty is included explicitly in the final scene estimate below.

σP​≃ηAd​ΩΔνNER≃0.6(1.9×10−7)(1)(1.6×10−3)≃1.8×10−10 W.
(34)
The wavenumber bandwidth is 1 cm−1 and must match the radiance's per-wavenumber unit; it is not an electrical noise bandwidth. For the common order-of-magnitude assignment Δf∼1 Hz to a one-second sample,
NEP∼1.8×10−10 WHz−1/2,D∗​∼5.6×108 Jones​.
(35)
Area in the last expression is 1 mm2=0.01 cm2. For a specified one-second boxcar with white noise, the one-sided equivalent noise bandwidth is instead 1/(2t)=0.5 Hz. That convention gives spectral NEP ≃2.5×10−10 WHz−1/2 and D∗​≃3.9×108 Jones. The source does not specify the electrical filter, so the two conventions should not be mixed or assigned spurious precision.
Two-point retrieval and its uncertainty. Assume linear, stable response for the three views. The radiometer two-point calibration is
