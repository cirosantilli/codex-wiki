Potential temperature and a parcel. For a dry ideal gas of fixed composition, the first law of thermodynamics per unit mass is
δq=cp​dT−ρdp​=cp​dT−Rd​Tpdp​.
(7)
A reversible adiabatic process has δq=0. Taking cp​,Rd​ constant gives dlnT=κdlnp, where κ=Rd​/cp​. Integrating to a fixed reference pressure p0​ defines the potential temperature
Θ=T(p0​/p)κ,DΘ/Dt=0for dry adiabatic motion​.
(8)
It is the temperature a parcel would have after reversible dry adiabatic process taking it to p0​. Equivalently ds=cp​dlnΘ under these assumptions, making potential temperature a thermodynamic entropy label useful for diagnosing stratification and air-mass motion. Latent heating, radiation, mixing and variable composition require modifying the conservation statement.
An air parcel is a small material volume followed with the flow. The parcel model treats it as internally approximately uniform, large compared with molecular scales and small compared with environmental variation scales. Rapid acoustic wave adjustment makes its pressure approximately equal to the surrounding pressure, while slower heat and mass exchange can be neglected during an idealized displacement. It is not a rigid container or an isolated molecule.
At the parcel's height, environmental hydrostatic pressure supplies an upward pressure force ρe​g per unit volume. Dividing by parcel density gives the upward acceleration
B=g(ρp​ρe​​−1)=g(Te​Tp​​−1)=gΘe​Θp​−Θe​​​.
(9)
The last steps use equal pressure, equal gas constants and the same reference pressure. Moisture-dependent density requires virtual temperature or its potential-temperature counterpart. Nonhydrostatic pressure perturbations and entrainment are excluded from this elementary buoyancy expression.
The convective available potential energy is the positive buoyancy work per unit mass between the level of free convection and the equilibrium level:
CAPE=∫zLFC​zEL​​B(z)dz=g∫zLFC​zEL​​Θe​Θp​−Θe​​dz​.

One sums positive-buoyancy layers when there is more than one. Negative work below free convection contributes convective inhibition rather than positive CAPE. In a lossless parcel estimate, conversion of CAPE to vertical kinetic energy gives wmax​≃2CAPE​; pressure work beyond this model, mixing and precipitation can change that speed.
Linear stability. Let ξ denote displacement from a neutrally buoyant reference altitude. Dry motion through an adiabatic process retains Θp​=Θe​(0), while Θe​(ξ)=Θe​(0)+ξΘe′​(0)+O(ξ2). The linear vertical momentum equation is therefore
ξ¨​+N02​ξ=0,N02​=Θe​g​dzdΘe​​​.
(11)
For N02​>0, ξ=CcosN0​t+DsinN0​t: stable buoyancy oscillations with period 2π/N0​. For N02​=0, ξ=C+Dt: neutral stratification with no restoring force. For N02​<0, growing and decaying exponentials occur, indicating convective instability. The growing solution is generic unless the initial data select only the decaying branch. These are local small-displacement results with an approximately constant environmental gradient.
Lapse-rate estimate. Define Γ=−dT/dz. Hydrostatic balance and the ideal gas law imply p′/p=−g/(Rd​T), so

In the free-parcel-oscillator interpretation, identifying the ridge encounter interval with the buoyancy period gives P=104/20=500 s and N0​=2π/P=0.01257 s−1. With T=293.15 K, g=9.81 ms−2 and cp​≃1004 Jkg−1K−1,
Γ=Γd​−gTN02​​≃5.1 Kkm−1​.
(13)
The frequency identification is an extra modeling assumption. A stationary mountain gravity wave generally obeys ω2=N02​kh2​/(kh2​+mz2​) without rotation, with ω=Ukh​. Ridge spacing fixes ω, not the vertical wavenumber mz​. For example, mz​=kh​ allows the same spacing and wind with N0​=2​ω, and a lapse rate about 0.3 Kkm−1. Thus 5.1 is the requested parcel-period estimate, not a uniquely identifiable lapse rate for every possible mountain-wave field; a resolved wave field needs its vertical structure as well.
