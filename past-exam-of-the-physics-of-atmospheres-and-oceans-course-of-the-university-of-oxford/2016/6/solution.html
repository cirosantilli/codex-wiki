Potential-vorticity dynamics. The quasi-geostrophic potential-vorticity equation expresses material conservation of a balanced potential vorticity by a horizontally nondivergent geostrophic flow. Its terms represent relative vorticity, planetary vorticity and the layer-thickness/free-surface contribution. The Rossby deformation radius Ld​ measures the scale at which that thickness contribution competes with the horizontal-vorticity term. The quasi-geostrophic approximation filters fast acoustic waves and gravity waves, making the slower balanced evolution accessible to early digital forecasts. The 1950 barotropic-vorticity forecasting study demonstrated practical twenty-four-hour forecasts from observed mid-tropospheric fields, while identifying discretization and omitted baroclinicity as error sources.[ref]

The polar-wave deformation-radius identifiability issue is that a unique Ld​ additionally requires a radial wavenumber or boundary condition. The quoted peak-to-peak meander is an amplitude, not a radial wavelength: multiplying the exact eigenfunction by a constant changes its amplitude without changing K,α or ω. Thus it cannot be silently converted into K=2π/Δr.

Different radial structures give different values: for example K=3.0×10−7 and 5.0×10−7 m−1 yield about 2.0×106 and 3.2×106 m, respectively, at the same measured phase speed. Their amplitudes remain free.
