Notation. The source uses ps​ for both the hypothetical dry surface pressure and the actual surface pressure after evaporation. Distinguish them as Pd​ and P, respectively, and interpret the specified pressure thickness Δp as fixed. This conserves the original hydrogen inventory and permits P to vary. Reading both occurrences as one fixed number would be inconsistent with adding water vapor to a closed atmospheric column mass budget.
Let E(T) denote the saturation pressure of water, let x be the mole fraction of water vapor in the mixed layer, and let Md​,Mv​ be the molar masses of hydrogen and water. Surface phase equilibrium and the ideal gas law give
x=PE(Ts​)​,pv​(p)=xp.
(1)
Because the layer has constant temperature, its relative humidity is
RH(p)=E(Ts​)pv​(p)​=Pp​​,P−Δp≤p≤P.
(2)
Thus every level strictly above the surface is subsaturated: hydrostatic balance makes p<P, whereas the saturation pressure stays constant. The result does not require a dilute water vapor approximation.
The Clausius-Clapeyron relation, neglecting the specific volume of liquid water and treating the water vapor as an ideal gas, gives
dTdlnE​=Rv​T2Lv​​,E(T)=Et​exp[Rv​Lv​​(Tt​1​−T1​)],Rv​=Mv​R​.
(3)
Here Tt​,Et​ are the triple point values, Lv​ is the specific latent heat, and R is the molar gas constant. All temperatures in these expressions are absolute. As throughout the question, this is a constant-latent heat, ideal gas model, not an extrapolation valid through the real critical point of water.
For a well-mixed ideal gas mixture, its mean molar mass and water vapor mass fraction are
M=Md​(1+ax),wv​=1+axϵx​,ϵ=Md​Mv​​≃9,a=ϵ−1≃8.
(4)
By hydrostatic balance, the mixed layer has total mass per unit area Δp/g, of which wv​Δp/g is water vapor. The remaining atmosphere contains no water vapor. Consequently conservation of hydrogen mass gives
gP​=gPd​​+gΔp​wv​,P=Pd​+ΔpP+aE(Ts​)ϵE(Ts​)​.
(5)
Equivalently the requested quadratic equation is
(P−Pd​)(P+aE)=ϵΔpE.​
(6)
For each Ts​, calculate E, take the positive root of this quadratic equation, and retain it only if 0≤x<1 and P>Δp. The last condition leaves a nonempty dry layer. In particular the dry constituent's surface partial pressure is not generally Pd​: Pd​ measures its column weight, and the hydrogen and water vapor have different molar masses.
