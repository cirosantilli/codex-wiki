Take a Northern-Hemisphere cyclonic vortex, with positive relative vorticity. For a fixed-depth incompressible layer, conservation of absolute vorticity gives
DtD​(ξ+f)=0,DtDξ​=−βv.
(44)
The anticlockwise flow on the east side moves parcels northward, increasing their planetary vorticity and creating negative relative vorticity anomalies. On the west side it moves parcels southward and creates positive relative vorticity anomalies. Initially,
∂t​ξ≃−βv,
(45)
so the positive anomaly grows on the western flank while the eastern flank loses positive vorticity. The cyclonic pattern consequently shifts westward. The induced anomalies themselves drive meridional flow and further relative vorticity anomalies, transmitting a Rossby wave pattern through the surrounding fluid. The restoring process is this exchange between planetary vorticity and relative vorticity, rather than a parcel executing a permanently westward translation.
The sketch shows the initial tendency, with the generated signs separated from the initial positive core. An isolated disturbance contains many wavevectors and disperses; westward phase velocity does not imply that all its wave energy travels westward. A Southern-Hemisphere cyclonic vortex reverses the signs of the initial and generated relative vorticities, while the same beta plane mechanism remains.
