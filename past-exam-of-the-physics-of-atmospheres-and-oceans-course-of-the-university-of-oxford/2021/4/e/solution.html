A calibrated multispectral filter radiometer is a natural choice for rapid thermal mapping: a few selected bands provide higher photon throughput and faster imaging than spreading the light over many spectrometer channels. Several bands can help separate temperature, dust emissivity and atmospheric effects. A spectrometer would be justified if identifying composition or resolving spectral structure were a central additional objective; one broadband radiance alone cannot generally determine both temperature and unknown emissivity.
Thermal infrared radiation is preferable to reflected visible light for measuring temperature. The Rayleigh criterion sets
1.22Dλ​≲h500​,λ≲12.0μm.​
(43)
A band near 10μm is therefore a reasonable compromise between useful planetary thermal emission and the required diffraction-limited resolution. At that wavelength, the nominal surface Rayleigh criterion separation is about 415m; at 15μm it is about 623m and fails the target. The exact bands must avoid or model strong atmospheric absorption and account for the dust spectrum. The 500m sampling pitch is still not a guarantee of faithful reconstruction of arbitrary 500m features: detector sampling and the point spread function must be considered together.
