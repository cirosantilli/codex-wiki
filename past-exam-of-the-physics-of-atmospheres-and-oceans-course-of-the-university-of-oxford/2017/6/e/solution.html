If one retains the Euler approximation A=I+Jδt, its exact norm matrix is
ATA=I+(J+JT)δt+JTJδt2.
(62)
The additional term can mix the instantaneous symmetric-part eigenvectors, so they no longer generally diagonalize finite-time error amplification. However, when δt2 is material, a reliable physical propagator must itself be calculated beyond first-order Euler: a large Euler-step amplification can be a numerical artifact. For constant J, the exact map is Φ=eJΔt; for a changing reference trajectory it solves Φ˙=J(t)Φ, Φ(0)=I.
Use its singular value decomposition, Φ=UΣVT. The initial orthonormal right singular vectors vi​ diagonalize ΦTΦ and satisfy
Φvi​=si​ui​,∥vi​∥2∥Φvi​∥2​=si2​.​
(63)
A singular value si​>1 gives finite-time norm growth, si​<1 decay, and the leading right singular vector is the initial perturbation with greatest amplification in the stated norm. The left singular vector describes the resulting final error pattern. Non-normal dynamics can give transient growth even if every Jacobian matrix eigenvalue has negative real part, so selecting observations from those eigenvalues alone is insufficient. Over a genuinely short interval, si2​=1+λi​δt+O(δt2), recovering the symmetric-part result. Initial observations should target the relevant growing right singular projections, with prior covariance and observational precision included when they are not isotropic.
