Work with the explicitly corrected reduced-pressure system in b. Write d=1/(2H) and use the complex wave amplitude, taking real parts at the end. The vertical factor satisfies (∂z2​−H−1∂z​)w=−(m2+d2)w, so the wave equation gives
ω2=k2+m2+d2N2k2​,c=kω​=k2+m2+d2​N​>0.​
(45)
For a Kelvin wave, continuity, zonal momentum and the buoyancy equation then determine all polarizations:
u=−km+id​w,Φ=kω​u=−k2ω(m+id)​w,p′=ρ0​Φ,b=−iωN2​w,v=0.​
(46)
In particular u,w,b,Φ have amplitude proportional to ez/(2H), whereas pressure p′ has amplitude proportional to e−z/(2H). Directly substituting these expressions into vertical momentum gives Φz​=−iω(m2+d2)w/k2=i(ω−N2/ω)w, verifying both the sign and the dispersion relation.
Meridional momentum is βyu+Φy​=0. Since Φ=cu, it integrates to a Gaussian exp[−βy2/(2c)]. Thus
LD2​=βc​=βk2+m2+1/(4H2)​N​.​
(47)
The eastward intrinsic branch c>0 is selected by decay away from the equator; choosing c<0 would make the meridional amplitude grow without bound. Although the squared dispersion relation has both frequency signs, a meridionally trapped Kelvin wave has this one phase-propagation direction.
For short vertical wavelengths, m2≫k2+d2, so ω2≃N2k2/m2, c≃N/∣m∣ and LD2​≃N/(β∣m∣). The wave is a slowly oscillating, hydrostatic internal gravity wave with strong vertical variation and relatively narrow trapping. Here u≃−(m/k)w and p′/ρ0​≃−(ωm/k2)w; the buoyancy expression above remains valid.
For long vertical wavelengths, ∣m∣≪k2+d2​, the exact limiting speed is c=N/k2+d2​. To obtain the stated height-controlled limit also requires long horizontal wavelength, ∣k∣H≪1. With both ∣m∣H≪1 and ∣k∣H≪1,
ω≃2NHk,LD2​≃β2NH​.​
(48)
The finite density scale height prevents the hydrostatic 1/∣m∣ phase speed from diverging as m→0. The polarizations tend to u≃−iw/(2Hk), Φ≃−iNw/k and b≃−iNw/(2Hk). Long vertical wavelength alone at fixed large kH would not justify c=2NH. In the opposite horizontally short limit the frequency approaches N instead. The growth ez/(2H) compensates decreasing reference density in wave-energy density; a radiating wave train is a local modal description, not a finite-total-energy eigenfunction over an infinite vertical domain.
