Apply the intended, corrected dispersion relation in c. A uniform mean zonal wind gives ωground​=ω+kU. Stationarity with U=−20ms−1 therefore requires eastward intrinsic phase speed ω/k=−U=20ms−1. With k=1/a,
∣m∣=[202N2​−a21​−4H21​]1/2≃4.975×10−4m−1,λz​=∣m∣2π​≃12.6km.​

The period determines Ω=2π/(24.4×3600s) and β=2Ω/a≃4.21×10−11m−1s−1. Hence
LD​=20/β​≃6.89×105m,LD​/a≃0.203rad≃11.6∘.​
(50)
A storm within roughly 12∘ of the equator has substantial projection on the Gaussian mode. There is no sharp latitude cutoff: ∣y∣=LD​ gives amplitude e−1/2 and the 1/e amplitude latitude is about 16.4∘. Forcing outside that core has progressively weaker overlap.
The intrinsic wave is eastward even though its observed ground pattern is stationary; westward advection cancels its phase propagation. Its intrinsic period is about 12.4 Earth days. The inferred ∣m∣≫1/(2H) puts it in the short-vertical-wavelength branch, well below the long-wave speed 2NH=200ms−1. For upward energy radiation, ∂ω/∂m must be positive, which selects m<0 for positive k,ω: vertical phase propagation is then downward. Dust absorption supplies thermal forcing with a zonally asymmetric component; the response does not imply that dust itself moves eastward at the intrinsic phase speed.
Southern spring to summer, the warmer perihelion-side dust season, is a reasonable climatological window for such a regional storm. This seasonal inference uses observed dust climatology, not the dispersion relation: the specified wind and wave parameters alone do not determine a unique season, and individual regional storms can occur outside that window.[ref]
