Use downward optical depth t measured from the top, with total D=τ∞​. This is the effective optical-depth/two-stream convention that gives an attenuation factor e−t. Constant absorption per unit mass and constant gravity give t=Dp/ps​. Write the locally emitted flux source as S(t)=πB(t)=S0​+bt.
The atmospheric contribution to upward outgoing longwave flux is
Fatm​=∫0D​S(t)e−tdt=S0​(1−e−D)+b[1−(D+1)e−D].
(11)
Because the source is linear, the effective emission pressure makes this exactly (1−e−D)S(teff​), where
teff​=1−e−D1−(D+1)e−D​=1−eD−1D​,prad​=Dps​​teff​.​
(12)
Thus for D≫1, teff​→1 and prad​≃ps​/D. For D≪1, expansion gives teff​=D/2−D2/12+O(D4), so prad​≃ps​/2. In the thin limit Fatm​≃DπB(ps​/2): it is the small atmospheric contribution, not the whole outgoing flux, which this level characterizes. Surface radiation transmitted through the column must be added separately.
Angular convention: these numerical coefficients use an effective optical depth. If t instead means literal vertical monochromatic optical depth and the angular integral is retained exactly, the flux weight is 2E2​(t), not e−t. Its thick-limit mean depth is 2/3, giving prad​≃2ps​/(3D) for a linear source. The coefficient one in the requested expression therefore specifies a two-stream/diffusivity convention; it is not an angle-independent identity. The thin-limit half-column result survives to leading order.
