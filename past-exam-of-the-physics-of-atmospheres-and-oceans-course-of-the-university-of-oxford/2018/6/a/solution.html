Linearize about a uniform horizontal flow U and a hydrostatic, vertically stratified basic state. The basic-flow material derivative is D0​=∂t​+U∂x​. The first two equations are the horizontal and vertical perturbation momentum balances: pressure gradients accelerate the disturbance, while the vertical equation retains the buoyancy force −gρ′/ρ0​ after the basic hydrostatic balance is subtracted.
The Boussinesq approximation neglects fractional density variations in inertia and enforces incompressibility, ux​+wz​=0, but retains them in buoyancy. Advecting the stable background density gradient gives D0​ρ′+wdρˉ​/dz=0. With N2=−(g/ρ0​)dρˉ​/dz>0, this is the fourth supplied equation. A displaced parcel then has a restoring buoyancy tendency.
Small amplitude allows products of perturbations to be discarded; two-dimensional motion removes y dependence and transverse flow. Rotation, viscosity, diffusion, diabatic heating and mean shear are neglected. ρ0​ is the reference inertial density, whereas the small background density gradient still sets N. In a compressible real atmosphere the appropriate stability measure involves potential temperature; this Boussinesq system is a local idealization that filters acoustic waves rather than a full compressible atmospheric model.
