The global mean absorbed solar flux is L∞​=0.8×584/4=116.8Wm−2. Using σ=5.6704×10−8Wm−2K−4 gives
Tg​≃240.5K,T(0)≃219.4K,T∞​≃179.1K.​
(26)
For the requested graphical parcel estimate, start a dry adiabat at the warm ground, Tad​(z)=240.5−4.4z with z in km, and intersect it with the radiative atmospheric profile 179.1[1+1.25e−z/10]1/4. Solving gives
zmix​≃9.9km,T(zmix​)≃197.1K.​
(27)
The original diagram below shows the intersection and the initial ground–air temperature discontinuity. A parcel heated towards the ground temperature remains buoyant until nearly one scale height in this model.
The atmospheric radiative lapse rate itself is only about 3.05Kkm−1 at the bottom and decreases with height, smaller than the stated adiabatic 4.4Kkm−1. Thus the interior radiative atmosphere is statically stable; the driver of this particular estimate is the hotter ground and discontinuity at the lower boundary, not a radiative lapse rate everywhere steeper than adiabatic. Convection would smooth that discontinuity and redistribute energy.
This is a diagnostic depth using the unadjusted radiative ground and atmospheric temperatures, not a fully energy-conserving radiative–convective equilibrium after adjustment. A self-consistent adjustment changes the surface temperature, infrared fluxes and boundary height. Mars also has spectral CO2​ absorption, dust, day–night variation, seasonal changes and geographically uneven heating; a gray, horizontally averaged, constant-scale-height column with fixed lapse rate captures none of those details. The calculation indicates the possible importance of a deep surface-driven mixed layer under the stated assumptions, not a universal Martian boundary-layer measurement.
