For l=0 put LD​=NH/f0​, η=kLD​ with k>0, and write the discriminant as
D(η)=(2η​−coth2η​)(2η​−tanh2η​)=1+4η2​−ηcothη.
(7)
The second factor is positive for every η>0, since x>tanhx. The first changes sign at the positive solution of x=cothx, x=η/2. Numerically xc​=1.19968, so ηc​=2.39936. The growing branch exists for 0<kLD​<ηc​, giving
λ>λmin​=ηc​2π​LD​=2.619LD​​.
(8)
Equality is neutral, so this is an infimum wavelength, rather than an attained shortest growing mode.
In the unstable interval,
cr​=2ΛH​,σ≡kci​=NΛf0​​−D(η)​​.
(9)
Both phase-speed branches have the same real part there; the imaginary parts have opposite signs. At and beyond the short-wave cutoff the growth rate is zero and the two real speeds are
ΛHc±​​=21​±ηD(η)​​.
(10)
They split continuously from 1/2 and approach the boundary-flow speeds zero and ΛH at large η. As η→0, D=−η2/12+O(η4), so σ→0 linearly with k, despite a finite limiting ci​=ΛH/12​. A zonally uniform disturbance has no exponential growth from this mechanism.
The maximum growth minimizes D. Its derivative is
D′(η)=2η​−cothη+ηcsch2η.
(11)
The positive interior minimum is at ηm​≃1.606, yielding
λm​=ηm​2π​LD​≃3.91LD​,σmax​=0.310NΛf0​​​.
(12)
The supplied phase-speed formula therefore predicts fastest growth at a wavelength of order four deformation lengths, not at the short-wave cutoff.
