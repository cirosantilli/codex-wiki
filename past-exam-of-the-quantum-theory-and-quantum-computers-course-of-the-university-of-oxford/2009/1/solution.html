Let U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ and take an observable with no explicit time dependence. In the Schrödinger picture, ψS​(t)=U(t)ψ0​ and AS​=A. In the Heisenberg picture, ψH​=ψ0​ and AH​(t)=U(t)†AU(t). For the interaction picture choose a time-independent splitting H=H0​+V, put U0​=e−iH0​t/ℏ, and define
ψI​=U0†​ψS​,AI​=U0†​AU0​,VI​=U0†​VU0​.
(1)
Then iℏψ˙​I​=VI​ψI​ and A˙I​=(i/ℏ)[H0​,AI​]. Unitarity gives the identical expectation in all three descriptions:
⟨ψS​,AψS​⟩=⟨ψ0​,AH​ψ0​⟩=⟨ψI​,AI​ψI​⟩.​
(2)
Different choices of the split interpolate between pictures; H0​=H makes the state constant, while H0​=0 gives the Schrödinger description.
Differentiate U†AU to obtain the Heisenberg equation of motion
A˙H​=ℏi​[H,AH​].​
(3)
For an explicitly time-dependent observable there is additionally U†(∂t​A)U. Thus HH​=H is constant. In a normalized energy eigenstate, ⟨[H,AH​]⟩=E⟨AH​⟩−E⟨AH​⟩=0, so the expectation of its Heisenberg derivative vanishes whenever the products are defined.
For the harmonic oscillator, the canonical commutator gives
X˙=P/m,P˙=−mω2X,dtd​(XP)=P2/m−mω2X2.​
(4)
The last identity follows from the operator product rule without commuting X and P. Although XP is not itself self-adjoint, the same calculation can be applied to the self-adjoint dilation observable (XP+PX)/2 and gives the same derivative.
Stationarity of the three expectations gives ⟨P⟩=⟨X⟩=0 and ⟨P2⟩=m2ω2⟨X2⟩. Substitution into E=⟨P2⟩/(2m)+mω2⟨X2⟩/2 yields the quantum virial theorem
⟨2mP2​⟩=2E​=21​mω2⟨X2⟩.​
(5)
The vanishing means make the variances equal to the second moments, so
(ΔP)2=mE,(ΔX)2=mω2E​,ΔXΔP=ωE​=(n+21​)ℏ≥ℏ/2.​
(6)
This satisfies the position-momentum Heisenberg uncertainty relation, with equality only for the ground state.
