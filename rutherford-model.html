Rutherford model

 Home Physics Atomic physics
OurBigBook.com Tags: Atomic nucleus Words: 276
The Rutherford model places a concentrated central electric charge inside a much larger chemical atom. In 1911, Ernest Rutherford argued that rare large deflections were predominantly single close encounters, rather than accumulated small deviations in a diffuse-charge atom. He treated the central electric charge as positive for calculation while noting that the scattering deductions did not establish its sign. He explicitly deferred atomic stability.[ref]
The physical inference is a compact region generating a strong electric field, not a miniature hard wall. Most impact parameters miss that region, while a small subset give large deflections. The model supplies a way to infer interior structure from outgoing angles, a central use of scattering throughout atomic physics and nuclear physics. This inference does not by itself specify electron quantum states or explain spectral lines.
The repulsive model also sets a probing scale. At closest approach, conservation of energy with zero radial velocity gives

For the numerical model above, even a head-on projectile turns at rmin​=2a=45.504fm. This is a calculated closest-approach distance, not a measured atomic nucleus radius. A point-charge fit can constrain how compact the scattering centre must be, but cannot directly resolve structure at distances the projectiles never reach.
The Rutherford differential cross-section must not be extrapolated to zero angle: the unscreened point model has a forward divergence, while real neutral chemical atoms exhibit the screening effect, finite beam acceptance, and accumulated small deflections. At very short separation, finite nuclear size and the nuclear force can change the interaction. Nuclear recoil changes the relation between laboratory and centre-of-mass angles for lighter targets; appreciable energy loss or repeated large deflections invalidates the simple thin-foil scattering counts. These limits delimit the calculation rather than supplying evidence for a nuclear contact collision in the early experiment.
