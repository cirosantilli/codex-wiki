Rotational kinetic energy (K=21​Iω2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Energy Kinetic energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 339
Rotational kinetic energy is the kinetic energy of a rigid body's rotation. For pure rotation about an axis fixed in an inertial frame,
Krot​=21​Iω2,I=∫r⊥2​dm,ω=∣ω∣.
(113)
Here r⊥​ is the perpendicular distance of a mass element dm from that axis, I is the moment of inertia about that same axis, and ω is the common angular speed, measured in radians per second. The rigid body assumption fixes the relative positions of the mass elements; they share angular velocity but have different speeds v=ωr⊥​. Summing their kinetic energies dmv2/2 gives the formula. This is a nonrelativistic model; deformation or internal motion can contribute additional kinetic energy.[ref]
For a computed example, take a uniform disc and an ideal thin ring, each of mass M=2kg and radius R=0.30m, rotating about its central perpendicular axis at angular speed 10rad/s. The moment of inertia of a uniform disc is MR2/2=0.090kgm2, while the ring has moment of inertia MR2=0.180kgm2. Their rotational kinetic energies are therefore 4.5J and 9.0J. The ring stores twice the energy at the same angular speed, because more mass lies farther from the axis. At the same nonzero rotational kinetic energy, its angular speed would instead be 1/2​ times the disc's. This makes radial placement useful for storing energy in a rotating wheel, although a real design must also withstand the stresses of rotation.
For fixed-axis rotation with constant moment of inertia, write the signed rotation rate as Ω=θ˙, with angle θ in radians. The axial component of net external torque obeys τaxis​=IΩ˙, so the work-energy theorem becomes
dtdKrot​​=τaxis​Ω,ΔKrot​=∫τaxis​dθ.
(114)
An opposing torque removes rotational kinetic energy; bearing friction must be included in the net torque. The signed Ω matters when the direction reverses. Rotation about a fixed axis need not be about a principal axis: the scalar energy formula still holds, but transverse bearing torques can be needed to maintain that axis.
For general rigid body motion, use the inertia tensor IG​ about the centre of mass G:
Ktotal​=21​M∣VG​∣2+21​ωTIG​ω,LG​=IG​ω.
(115)
This is the kinetic-energy decomposition about the centre of mass. In a principal body frame, the rotational term is 21​(I1​ω12​+I2​ω22​+I3​ω32​), using the principal moments of inertia. Away from a principal axis, angular momentum and angular velocity need not be parallel. A freely rotating asymmetric rigid body can change the direction of angular velocity relative to the body even while rotational kinetic energy remains constant; Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body govern that motion. One constant axial moment of inertia is then insufficient.[ref]
