Twin paradox with fixed reunion events (Δτ=∫0T​1−v(t)2/c2​dt)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor Time dilation Twin paradox
OurBigBook.com Words: 236
Work in Minkowski spacetime, neglect gravity, and choose the stay-at-home clock's inertial frame. Let departure be O=(t,x)=(0,0) and reunion be R=(T,0). The home clock follows xH​(t)=0. A traveller moves outward with velocity +v and returns with velocity −v, where 0<v<c and c is the speed of light. The ideal world line has a sharp turnaround at P=(T/2,vT/2):
xT​(t)={vt,v(T−t),​0≤t≤T/2,T/2≤t≤T.​
(9)
Each straight leg lies in an inertial frame, but no single traveller inertial frame covers both legs.
For any differentiable timelike curve in this home inertial frame, its clock accumulates proper time through the integral
Δτ=∫0T​1−c2v(t)2​​dt.
(10)
Apply it separately to the traveller's two legs. With the Lorentz factor γ=(1−v2/c2)−1/2,
ΔτH​=T,ΔτT​=γT​,ΔτH​−ΔτT​=T(1−γ1​).
(11)
For v=0.6c and T=10yr, the Lorentz factor is γ=1.25. The turnaround is 3 light-years away at home time 5yr. The traveller records 4yr outward and 4yr returning: the reunited clocks read 10yr and 8yr. These are illustrative model values, not experimental measurements.
The same integral gives a short geometric explanation: 1−v(t)2/c2​≤1, so every such competing timelike curve between these fixed spacetime events has Δτ≤T. Equality requires zero velocity throughout, apart from sets of zero duration. Thus the straight home world line maximizes proper time in this Minkowski spacetime problem. This statement concerns fixed endpoints and an inertial home clock; in a gravitational field, compare paths using the appropriate metric tensor.
