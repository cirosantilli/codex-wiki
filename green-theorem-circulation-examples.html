Green theorem circulation examples

OurBigBook.com Words: 152
Take the vector field F=(−2y,3x). Its scalar curl is the constant 3−(−2)=5, so Green theorem predicts that its positively oriented boundary line integral is five times the enclosed area. This one field makes both a round and a rectangular calculation transparent.
For the circle x2+y2=4, choose the counterclockwise parametric equations γ(t)=(2cost,2sint), 0≤t≤2π. Direct substitution in the line integral gives
∮C​(−2ydx+3xdy)=∫02π​(8sin2t+12cos2t)dt=20π.
(72)
The interior double integral is shorter: the disk has area 4π, hence ∬D​5dA=20π. Reversing the boundary orientation gives −20π.
For the rectangle D=[0,3]×[0,2], traverse its bottom, right, top, and left edges in that order. The four line integrals are respectively
∫03​0dx=0,∫02​9dy=18,∫30​(−4)dx=12,∫20​0dy=0.
(73)
Their sum is 30, agreeing with ∬D​5dA=5⋅3⋅2. In both examples the path geometry changes the separate contributions, while constant curl makes only area matter to the final answer.
