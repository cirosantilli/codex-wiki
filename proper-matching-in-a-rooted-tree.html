Proper matching in a rooted tree

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Matching (graph theory)
OurBigBook.com Words: 189 Articles: 1
A matching in a graph on a rooted tree has the sibling-support condition if every unmatched non-root vertex has its parent matched to one of its siblings in a rooted tree. This is the properness condition used here. The root has no parent, so either exempt an unmatched root or require it to be matched in a nontrivial instance; the unmatched-vertex bound for a proper tree matching holds under either convention. Properness is stronger than being maximal: on the path r−a−b−c rooted at r, selecting only ab gives a maximal matching in a graph, but unmatched c has parent b matched upward rather than to a sibling of c.
