Cones generated by rotation and dilation characteristics (r˙=r−k×r)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Transport equation Characteristic curve Method of characteristics
OurBigBook.com Words: 150
For a fixed real unit vector k, the linear ordinary differential equation r˙=r−k×r combines dilation with rotation about the axis Rk. Its solution from a line sℓ, with ∣ℓ∣=1, is
r(s,t)=set(κk+costv−sint(k×v)),κ=k⋅ℓ,v=ℓ−κk.
(7)
Since k⋅r=setκ and ∣r∣=∣s∣et, the union of these characteristic curves is given by ∣k⋅r∣=∣κ∣∣r∣. For 0<∣κ∣<1 it is a circular cone of semi-angle arccos∣κ∣: varying t supplies every azimuth and varying s supplies every radius on both halves. For κ=0 it is a plane; for ∣κ∣=1 it is only the axis. In the last case the line is itself a characteristic curve, so the initial data do not generate a two-dimensional surface. The cone is a smooth surface away from its vertex, and it is a graph of a function only on suitable patches. Invariance of a graph patch under characteristic curves holds only while the curve stays in its domain; global flow invariance requires continuation of that patch.
