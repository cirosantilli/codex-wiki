Asymptotic power series (f(x)−∑k=0N​ck​(x−a)k=o(∣x−a∣N))

OurBigBook.com Words: 399 Articles: 1
An asymptotic power series uses powers as its asymptotic sequence. Near a finite point a, the condition in the title applies along the specified domain for every fixed N; at infinity, inverse powers are convenient. It need not be a convergent power series. Its coefficients record the successive limiting responses, as the integral example below demonstrates.
The finite-order Taylor theorem supplies an asymptotic power series for a smooth function near a point, but smoothness does not imply that its Taylor series converges to the function. Define g(x)=e−1/x for x>0 and g(x)=0 for x≤0. Every right-hand derivative is an exponential function times a polynomial in 1/x, tending to zero as x↓0. Consequently g is a smooth function with every derivative zero at the origin. Its all-zero Taylor series misses g(x)>0 to the right. Zero and g also have the same all-zero asymptotic power series on that side, despite being different functions. An asymptotic expansion can determine all its own coefficients without determining the represented function uniquely.
