Beer-Lambert calibration model

Choose an ideal absorbing chemical species with ε=8000Lmol−1cm−1 at a fixed wavelength, and a cuvette with geometric length l=1cm. These are stipulated model parameters. A micromolar molar concentration is 1μM=10−6molL−1, so each additional 25μM gives ΔA=8000×1×25×10−6=0.2. Five chosen standards therefore predict

The absorbance graph is a straight line of slope 0.008(μM)−1, while the transmittance graph shows exponential decay. Equal increments of molar concentration multiply transmittance by 10−0.2≈0.63096; they do not subtract a fixed transmitted fraction.
Suppose a measurement returns the stipulated blank-normalized reading T=0.40 under those same conditions. The inferred absorbance and molar concentration are
A=−log10​(0.40)=log10​(2.5)≈0.3979400,c=8000×1log10​(2.5)​molL−1≈49.7425μM.
(22)
This lies slightly below the 50μM standard because 0.40 is slightly above 10−0.4≈0.39811. A calibration determined experimentally by linear regression may instead have A=mc+b. Then use c=(A−b)/m, keeping the slope m and intercept b in their measured units. A nonzero intercept calls for checking the reference blank and sample preparation; it does not uniquely identify the cause.
