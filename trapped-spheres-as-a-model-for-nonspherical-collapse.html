Trapped spheres as a model for nonspherical collapse (θ+​=r1−2M/r​,θ−​=−r2​)

A concrete example separates a trapped surface from a failure of coordinates. Use geometrized units G=c=1, metric signature (−,+,+,+), and the regular ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates of a Schwarzschild black hole with mass M>0:
ds2=−f(r)dv2+2dvdr+r2dΩ2,f(r)=1−r2M​.
(34)
For a round sphere, choose future null vector normals
k+​=∂v​+2f​∂r​,k−​=−∂r​,g(k+​,k−​)=−1.
(35)
The spherical null expansion in ingoing coordinates follows directly from A=4πr2: θk​=2k(r)/r. At r=3M, (θ+​,θ−​)=(1/(9M),−2/(3M)); at r=3M/2, it is (−2/(9M),−4/(3M)). Both future light fronts shrink in the second case. At r=2M, θ+​=0, so this sphere is marginally outer trapped surface, not a strict trapped surface. In the spherical model this boundary is an event horizon; that identification does not extend to arbitrary geometries.
Although k−​ is affine as written, k+​ generally obeys ∇k+​​k+​=(M/r2)k+​. These are valid initial normals for the sign test; use affine rescaling of a null normal before applying the affine Null Raychaudhuri equation. Regularity of the displayed metric tensor at r=2M shows why a divergence in another chart would not establish null geodesic incompleteness.
On a compact trapped surface, both null expansions have a uniform negative margin. Sufficiently small smooth perturbations controlling the relevant metric tensor and surface derivatives preserve trapping. This conditional robustness does not prove that every nonspherical gravitational collapse creates a trapped surface.[ref]
