Spherical wedge

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Sphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
A spherical wedge is the portion of a solid sphere between two meridional half-planes meeting at a dihedral angle β, with 0<β<2π. Put the common axis along z and its azimuthal bisector along x. For radius a, use spherical coordinates 0≤r≤a, 0≤θ≤π, −β/2≤φ≤β/2. Its volume is 2βa3/3.
For constant mass density, reflection symmetries put the centre of mass on the bisector. The first moment is

Dividing by volume gives xˉ=3πasin(β/2)/(8β). It decreases from the limiting value 3πa/16 for an infinitesimal wedge to zero for a full sphere.
The curved boundary is a spherical lune. Its outward vector area is πa2sin(β/2)ex​. Indeed, its x component has the same angular integrals as the first moment, multiplied by a2; the other components cancel by symmetry. Its scalar surface area is 2βa2, which is generally different from the magnitude of its vector area.
