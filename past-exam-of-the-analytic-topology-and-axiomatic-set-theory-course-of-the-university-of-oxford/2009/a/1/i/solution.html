Let H be the set of separately continuous real-valued functions on the plane. Take as a subbasis all sets h−1(U), where h∈H and U is open in the usual real line. Unions of finite intersections of these sets form a topology, and every h is continuous for it by construction. Conversely every topology making every h continuous must contain the subbasis, hence this entire generated topology. Thus the required coarsest topology exists and is unique: it is the initial topology of the family H.
The family is a set, being a subset of RR2, so this construction has no proper-class issue. The coordinate projections belong to H; consequently this topology contains the ordinary Euclidean topology. This last observation will also make convergence in the new topology imply ordinary coordinatewise convergence.
