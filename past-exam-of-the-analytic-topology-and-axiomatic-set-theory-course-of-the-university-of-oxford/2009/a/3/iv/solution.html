For the two ends of a real-line compactification, let An+​=h((n,∞))​ and An−​=h((−∞,−n))​. Each is nonempty compact and connected, being the closure of a connected image. The two nested families therefore have nonempty connected intersections C+ and C−. Since closure commutes with finite unions, Tn​​=An+​∪An−​. Moreover

For if a point fails both intersections, it fails one positive member and one negative member, hence both members at the larger index by nesting.
If the remainder were a finite three-point set, its Hausdorff subspace topology would be discrete. Each nonempty connected subset C± would then be a singleton, so their union would have at most two points. This contradiction proves there is no three-point Hausdorff compactification of the real line. The argument does not assert that every possible remainder is finite.
