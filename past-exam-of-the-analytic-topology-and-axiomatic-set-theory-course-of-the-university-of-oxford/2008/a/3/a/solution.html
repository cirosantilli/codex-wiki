A Hausdorff compactification of X is a pair (h,Y) in which Y is compact Hausdorff and h:X→Y is a topological embedding with dense image.
For a locally compact Hausdorff noncompact topological space, form X∗=X∪{∞} with one new point. Keep all open subsets of X, and take the neighborhoods of infinity to contain sets
{∞}∪(X∖K),K⊆X compact.
(14)
Compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed. Finite unions of compact sets are compact, so finite intersections of these basic neighborhoods are again of the same form; their intersections with ordinary open sets are open in X. This specifies a topology restricting to the original one on X.
It is compact: from an open cover choose a member containing infinity. Its complement is contained in some compact K, and finitely many more members cover that compact remainder. It is Hausdorff: two points of X are separated as before; to separate x∈X from infinity, choose an open neighborhood U of x lying inside a compact neighborhood K. The open set U and the infinity neighborhood X∗∖K are disjoint.
The inclusion of X is an embedding. Every basic neighborhood of infinity meets X, because otherwise its compact complement K would equal all of X, contrary to noncompactness. Thus X is dense in X∗. This proves that X∗ is the one-point compactification.
