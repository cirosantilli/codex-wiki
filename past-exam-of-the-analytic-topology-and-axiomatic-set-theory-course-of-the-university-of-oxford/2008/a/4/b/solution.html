For every ultrafilter H on X, its image is an ultrafilter on the compact space Y, so it has a limit. That limit is unique because Y is Hausdorff: distinct limits would have disjoint neighborhoods both belonging to the same proper filter. Therefore Λ is well-defined. Since f[X] is dense, every b∈Y lies in f[X]​. Part (a), with A=X, supplies an ultrafilter mapping to b, proving surjectivity.
Suppose disjoint subsets have disjoint image closures. If H=K, choose A∈H not in K; the ultrafilter criterion gives X∖A∈K. Part (a) gives
Λ(H)∈f[A]​,Λ(K)∈f[X∖A]​.
(21)
The two closures are disjoint by assumption, so the limits differ. Thus Λ is injective.
Conversely, suppose Λ is injective and disjoint A,B⊆X had a common point b in their image closures. Part (a) would supply ultrafilters H,K containing A,B respectively, both with image limit b. They must be distinct, since a proper filter cannot contain the disjoint sets A and B simultaneously. This contradicts injectivity. Hence
Λ injective⟺f[A]​∩f[B]​=∅ whenever A∩B=∅.​
(22)
