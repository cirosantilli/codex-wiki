König theorem for cardinal numbers states that if κi​<λi​ for each index i∈I, then
i∈I∑​κi​<i∈I∏​λi​.​
(35)
Represent each cardinal by its initial ordinal, let A={(i,α):i∈I,α<κi​}, and let B=∏i∈I​λi​.
First there is an injection A→B: encode (i,α) by the function which is zero away from i and takes value α+1 at i. The nonzero coordinate identifies i, and its value identifies α. It is valid because α+1≤κi​<λi​; an index with κi​=0 contributes no domain points.
For strictness suppose a surjection F:A→B existed. At coordinate i, the values F(i,α)(i) for α<κi​ occupy fewer than λi​ possibilities. Choose bi​ as the least ordinal below λi​ absent from that set. The resulting b=(bi​)∈B differs from F(i,α) at coordinate i for every (i,α)∈A, contradicting surjectivity.
An injection B→A would produce a surjection A→B by inverting on its image and assigning the zero function elsewhere. It is therefore impossible. The injection in the first direction and impossibility of the reverse give the strict cardinal inequality. This diagonal argument uses the canonical well-orders of the specified cardinals to choose missing coordinate values, rather than an unexplained arbitrary choice at that step.
