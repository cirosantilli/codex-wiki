Gershgorin bounds for symmetric matrices (mini​(aii​−ri​)≤λ≤maxi​(aii​+ri​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Gershgorin circle theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 115
For a real symmetric matrix, or more generally a Hermitian matrix, the finite-dimensional spectral theorem makes all eigenvalues real. Intersecting its Gershgorin discs with the real axis gives
λ∈⋃i=1n​[aii​−ri​,aii​+ri​],mini​(aii​−ri​)≤λ≤maxi​(aii​+ri​).
(74)
The union preserves gaps that the single outer interval loses. If every aii​−ri​>0, all eigenvalues are positive, so the matrix is a positive-definite matrix. This is the positive-diagonal strictly diagonally dominant matrix criterion. For the three-by-three example, 1.7≤λ≤9.4 gives a positive-definite matrix certificate and bounds its spectral condition number of a positive-definite matrix by 9.4/1.7≈5.529412; the computed value is about 4.544364.
Strictness matters. The symmetric matrix (1−1​−11​) has positive diagonal and is a diagonally dominant matrix, but its eigenvalues are 0 and 2: it is singular. For a general complex matrix, the condition Reaii​>ri​ only puts the eigenvalues in the open right half-plane. The Hermitian matrix assumption is what converts that statement into positive definiteness.
