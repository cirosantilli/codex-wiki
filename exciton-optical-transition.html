Exciton optical transition (Eopt​≃Eg​−EB​)

OurBigBook.com Words: 362 Articles: 2
For the ideal Wannier-Mott exciton in energy bands whose edges occur at the same crystal momentum, the pair excitation energy, measured from the unexcited crystal, is
EX,n​(K)=Eg​−n2R∗​+2Mℏ2K2​.
(3)
Here ℏK is the pair's total crystal momentum; the last term is center of mass kinetic energy. For a permitted photon absorption transition with negligible recoil energy, ℏω≃Eg​−EB,n​. An electric-dipole selection rule and a nonzero transition matrix element are still needed: a bound state need not produce an observable spectral line. If the relevant energy band edges have different values of crystal momentum, a phonon can also be needed to conserve crystal momentum; subtracting a binding scale from a band gap alone does not specify the optical threshold.
For example, the lowest internal bound state in this hypothetical model costs 1.489115eV to create optically, yet needs only 0.010885eV more to dissociate. The large creation energy and the small exciton binding energy answer different physical questions. An electron-hole recombination event can instead release excitation energy by photon emission or to other degrees of freedom; an emitted photon need not reproduce the original absorption energy if relaxation intervened.
