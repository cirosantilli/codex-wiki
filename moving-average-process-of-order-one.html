Moving-average process of order one (MA(1))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Time series Autoregressive moving-average model Moving-average model
OurBigBook.com Words: 426 Articles: 1
A moving-average process of order one has the form
Xt​=μ+εt​+θεt−1​,θ∈R,
(22)
with centered white noise of finite positive variance σ2. Each disturbance enters the current observation with weight 1 and the next observation with weight θ, then leaves the filter. It is a weakly stationary process for every real θ: the mean is μ and the autocovariance of an MA(1) process depends only on lag. Strong white noise additionally makes it a strictly stationary process. No ∣θ∣<1 restriction is needed for stationarity.
For a worked observation calculation, take μ=10, θ=0.7, and consecutive noise values εt−1​=−1, εt​=2, εt+1​=0. Then Xt​=11.3 and Xt+1​=11.4; the same disturbance 2 appears in both. If σ2=1, the variance is 1.49, the lag-one autocovariance is 0.7, and the lag-one autocorrelation is 70/149≃0.4698. All higher-lag autocorrelations vanish, as shown in autocorrelation.
An MA(1) is useful for brief dependence caused by overlapping disturbances or measurements. Its name refers to a weighted moving sum of noise; its weights need not be positive or sum to one, and it differs from the data smoother in seasonal extraction by a centred moving average. Under strong white noise, observations more than one interval apart depend on disjoint independent noise families and are independent. Under weak white noise, the same cutoff establishes only uncorrelated random variables.
Invertibility of a moving-average model is a separate question: can the specified driving noise be recovered by a stable linear filter of present and past observations? For an MA(1) the answer, with the absolutely summable filter convention, is exactly ∣θ∣<1:
εt​=∑j=0∞​(−θ)j(Xt−j​−μ).
(23)
Thus θ=2 gives a stationary MA(1) that is not invertible in this sense. At θ=±1 it is still stationary, but the inverse coefficients are not absolutely summable. The case θ=0 is simply shifted white noise. See Bartlett, lecture 5: MA(1) and stable invertibility.
