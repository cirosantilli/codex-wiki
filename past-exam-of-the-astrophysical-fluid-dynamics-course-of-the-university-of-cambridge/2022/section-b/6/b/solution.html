Write total pressure and gravitational potential as
p=p0​(r)+π(r,z,t),Φ=Φ0​(r)+ψ(r,z,t).
(48)
Here π is the Eulerian perturbation of a fluid variable applied to pressure, while ψ is the corresponding gravitational potential perturbation. They have units Pa and m2s−2, respectively. The constant interior mass density is ρ.
The Euler equations are
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−ρ1​∇(p0​+π)−∇(Φ0​+ψ).
(49)
Subtract the hydrostatic equilibrium relation ∇p0​/ρ+∇Φ0​=0 and discard terms quadratic in the perturbation. Mass conservation gives incompressible flow, and the gravitational Poisson equation has no bulk density perturbation. Thus, strictly inside the fluid,
∂t​u=−ρ∇π​−∇ψ,∇⋅u=0,∇2ψ=0.​
(50)
The last equation is Laplace equation, not a statement that self-gravity has disappeared. The displaced surface carries a first-order surface mass density ρη, where η is its radial displacement, and this supplies the source and matching conditions for ψ at the moving interface. The axisymmetric gravitational potential of a displaced cylindrical interface, expressed using modified Bessel functions, incorporates that boundary source.
It is useful to define W=ψ+π/ρ. Taking the divergence of the momentum equation gives ∇2W=0. For an axisymmetric Fourier mode with axial wavenumber k>0, regularity at r=0 selects radial dependence I0​(kr), the Modified Bessel function of the first kind, since K0​(kr) is singular there. In what follows k=∣kz​∣ can always be chosen nonnegative.
