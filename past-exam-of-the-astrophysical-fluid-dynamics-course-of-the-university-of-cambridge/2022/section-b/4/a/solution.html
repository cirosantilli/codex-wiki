The buoyancy frequency, also called the Brunt–Väisälä frequency, is the angular frequency of a small vertical displacement restored by buoyancy in a stably stratified fluid. Take z upward, gravitational acceleration −gz, and a background in hydrostatic equilibrium,
dzdp​=−ρg.
(15)
Displace a small gas element upward by ξ, sufficiently slowly for its pressure to match its surroundings but without heat exchange. For this adiabatic process, p/ργ is constant along the element's motion, so its mass density changes by ργ−1(dlnp/dz)ξ. The surrounding mass density changes by (dρ/dz)ξ. The element's excess mass density is consequently
Δρ=ρ(γ1​dzdlnp​−dzdlnρ​)ξ.
(16)
Its acceleration is ξ¨​=−gΔρ/ρ=−N2ξ. Thus
N2=g(γ1​dzdlnp​−dzdlnρ​).​
(17)
For a gas of fixed composition obeying the ideal gas law, ρ∝p/T. The adiabatic buoyancy frequency of an ideal gas is therefore
N2=g[T1​dzdT​−γpγ−1​dzdp​]=Tg​(dzdT​+cp​g​),​
(18)
where cp​=γR/(γ−1) is the specific heat capacity at constant pressure, and R=p/(ρT) is the specific gas constant. Here N2 has units s−2; N is an angular frequency, not a cyclic frequency. Positive N2 gives simple harmonic motion, negative N2 gives convective instability, and N2=0 is marginal. The temperature form assumes fixed composition; the preceding density form is more general.
