For ν>0 and positive Ψ, set f=logΨ, so the Cole-Hopf transformation is u=−2νfx​. Substitution into the viscous Burgers equation gives
ut​+uux​−νuxx​=−2ν∂x​(ft​−νfxx​−νfx2​)=−2ν∂x​(ΨΨt​−νΨxx​​)=0.
(35)
Hence the most general equation implied by this substitution is
Ψt​−νΨxx​=C(t)Ψ.
(36)
The time normalization in the Cole-Hopf transformation accounts for the arbitrary function C(t): multiplying Ψ by a nonzero function of time leaves ∂x​logΨ and therefore u unchanged. Replacing Ψ by e−∫tC(s)dsΨ sets C=0. With this conventional normalization,
∂t​Ψ=ν∂x2​Ψ,​
(37)
the heat equation.
For each j=1,2, the function Ψj​=eνκj2​t+κj​x obeys ∂t​Ψj​=νκj2​Ψj​=ν∂x2​Ψj​. By the superposition principle for this linear partial differential equation,
Ψ=eνκ12​t+κ1​x+eνκ22​t+κ2​x​
(38)
is a solution, positive for all real x,t,κ1​,κ2​. Here κj​ has units m−1 so both exponents are dimensionless. The positivity ensures that its logarithm and the resulting velocity are regular.
