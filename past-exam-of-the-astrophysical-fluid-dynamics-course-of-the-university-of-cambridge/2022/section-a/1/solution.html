A fluid particle follows a Lagrangian trajectory, so its position satisfies x˙=u(x,t) rather than the velocity evaluated permanently at its initial position. Assume ω=0, and write
x(t)=X0​+u0​a(t)+u02​b(t)+O(u03​),a(0)=b(0)=0.
(1)
Set α=kX0​ and θ=α−ωt. A Taylor expansion of the velocity about X0​ gives
u0​a˙+u02​b˙=u0​sinθ+ku02​acosθ+O(u03​).
(2)
Thus a˙=sinθ, and
a(t)=ωcosθ−cosα​.
(3)
At the next order,
b˙=ωk​(cos2θ−cosαcosθ),
(4)
so the required second-order trajectory in a sinusoidal velocity field is
x(t)=​X0​+ωu0​​(cosθ−cosα)+ωku02​​[2t​+4ωsin2α−sin2θ​−ωcosα(sinα−sinθ)​]+O(u03​).​​
(5)
Both corrections vanish at t=0. All retained terms have units of length. The secular contribution gives the Stokes drift
x˙=2ωku02​​+O(u03​),​
(6)
although the Eulerian time average of the given velocity is zero. The particle samples the wave at its displaced position, producing this second-order drift. The expansion is in the dimensionless parameter ∣ku0​/ω∣≪1, for fixed times; it ceases to be uniform when the accumulated drift changes the wave phase by order unity.
