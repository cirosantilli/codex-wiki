The Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a perfect gas express conservation of mass flux, normal momentum flux, and total energy flux through a steady planar shock wave in its rest frame. With mass flux j=ρ1​u1​=ρ2​u2​, momentum flux is ρu2+p and specific transported energy is u2/2+e+p/ρ. The last term is pressure work, so the energy equation contains specific enthalpy rather than only specific internal energy. Neglect gravity across the thin front, magnetic stresses and radiative energy loss for the adiabatic case.
For a calorically perfect ideal gas, e=p/[(γ−1)ρ] and cs2​=γp/ρ. Therefore
2u12​​+γ−1cs,12​​=2u22​​+γ−1cs,22​​.​
(15)
Put χ=ρ2​/ρ1​, so u2​=u1​/χ. Momentum conservation gives
p1​p2​​=1+γM12​(1−χ1​),M1​=cs,1​u1​​.
(16)
Insert this and cs,22​/cs,12​=(p2​/p1​)/χ into the energy equation. After multiplying out, its two factors are
(χ−1)[((γ−1)M12​+2)χ−(γ+1)M12​]=0.
(17)
The first root is the unchanged flow. The compressive shock branch is
χ=(γ−1)M12​+2(γ+1)M12​​=γ−1+2/M12​γ+1​.​
(18)
It has χ>1 for upstream supersonic flow. A strong shock approaches (γ+1)/(γ−1): four for a monatomic gas, or six for a fixed rotationally excited diatomic gas with γ=7/5. The assumed fixed heat capacity ceases to describe the gas when dissociation or ionization becomes important. Adiabatic here means no net heat lost from the shock-plus-flow energy budget; the irreversible front still increases entropy.
For an isothermal shock of unchanged composition, let a2=p/ρ=kB​T/mˉ be the common isothermal sound speed squared. Mass and momentum conservation give
u12​+a2=χu12​​+a2χ,(χ−1)(a2χ−u12​)=0.
(19)
The compressive branch is therefore
χ=MT,12​,MT,1​=u1​/a.​
(20)
Unlike the adiabatic ratio, it has no fixed upper bound as its upstream Mach number grows. Heat removal prevents the pressure increase that would otherwise resist compression. This approximation describes an efficiently cooled jump between equal-temperature far states, not an adiabatic shock with its energy equation left unchanged. For the same upstream physical temperature and velocity, MT,1​=γ​M1​; the two sound-speed conventions must not be confused.
Full dissociation changes the equation of state even when the temperature is held constant. With negligible electron and binding-energy mass corrections, upstream molecular hydrogen has a12​=kB​T/(2mH​) and downstream atomic hydrogen has a22​=kB​T/mH​=2a12​. The conserved quantity is mass flux; particle number flux doubles. Thus
χ=u2​u1​​,u12​+a12​=u1​u2​+2a12​u2​u1​​.​
(21)
Equivalently, the dissociating isothermal shock relation between temperature and the two velocities is
a12​=2u1​−u2​u1​u2​(u1​−u2​)​.​
(22)
Writing MT,1​=u1​/a1​ gives
2χ2−(MT,12​+1)χ+MT,12​=0.
(23)
The high-compression branch is
χ=4MT,12​+1+(MT,12​+1)2−8MT,12​​​.​
(24)
Its strong-shock limit is χ∼MT,12​/2, rather than MT,12​. The pressure per unit mass has doubled, reducing the compression available at a fixed upstream temperature and speed. A real compressive branch in this prescribed fully dissociated, equal-temperature model requires MT,1​≥1+2​; the high root has downstream speed no greater than the downstream isothermal sound speed. These mass and momentum conditions do not themselves establish that sufficient energy is available to dissociate every molecule. The chemical energy and any required radiative loss must also be included in a physical energy budget.
