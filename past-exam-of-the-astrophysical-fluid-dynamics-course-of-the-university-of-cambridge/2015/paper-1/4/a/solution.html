For a uniform sphere, M=4πρR3/3 and its gravitational potential energy is Ω=−3GM2/(5R). The ideal-gas kinetic energy is Ekin​=3MkB​T/(2mˉ). Equating these magnitudes using the virial theorem gives GM/R=5kB​T/mˉ. Eliminating R yields the uniform-sphere pressure-work collapse criterion
Mcrit​=(Gmˉ5kB​T​)3/2(4πρ3​)1/2.
(25)
In particular, for fixed composition,
MJ​∝T3/2ρ−1/2.​
(26)
The same Jeans mass scaling follows from cs2​k2∼4πGρ in the Jeans instability dispersion relation and a characteristic size k−1. Pressure communicates support in a sound-crossing time; sufficiently strong self-gravity wins when the gravitational time is shorter.
The numerical coefficient depends on the equilibrium, boundary and wavelength-to-cloud conventions. A sphere with both exactly uniform density and uniform temperature is not an exact hydrostatic self-gravitating ideal-gas equilibrium without additional stresses. The uniform-sphere argument establishes the characteristic mass scaling; it does not prove an exact stability boundary for that nonexistent equilibrium.
