For a barotropic fluid, define h(ρ)=∫dp/ρ. Dotting the steady Euler equation with u gives
u⋅∇(2u2​+h+ψ)=0.
(14)
Thus the Bernoulli function H=u2/2+h+ψ is constant along each streamline. Different streamlines can have different constants; steady flow alone does not imply one spatially uniform H.
In a quasi-one-dimensional flow through area A(z) with negligible gravity, the axial momentum equation and dp=cs2​dρ give
udzdu​=−ρcs2​​dzdρ​,u2dzdlnu​=−cs2​dzdlnρ​.
(15)
Meanwhile the mass flow rate M˙=ρuA is constant, so dlnρ=−dlnu−dlnA. Substitution proves the nozzle area-velocity relation
(u2−cs2​)dlnu=cs2​dlnA.​
(16)
A subsonic flow accelerates where A decreases, whereas a supersonic flow accelerates where A increases. A smooth accelerating transonic branch in a de Laval nozzle therefore crosses u=cs​ at a minimum-area throat. Suitable reservoir and downstream pressures are also required: a converging-diverging shape by itself does not exclude a wholly subsonic solution or a downstream shock.
For the stipulated constant-K polytropic flow, cs2​=γKργ−1. At the throat um​=cs,m​, so
M˙=Am​ρm​γKρmγ−1​​,ρm​=(Am​γK​M˙​)2/(γ+1).​
(17)
The specific enthalpy is h=γKργ−1/(γ−1)=cs2​/(γ−1). Evaluating H at the throat and substituting ρ=M˙/(Au) elsewhere gives
2u2​+γ−1γK​(AuM˙​)γ−1=2(γ−1)γ+1​cs,m2​.​
(18)
On the outward supersonic branch, A∼Cr2 lets the gas expand and its specific enthalpy tend to zero for γ>1. Consequently
u∞​=γ−1γ+1​​cs,m​,ρ∼Cu∞​M˙​r−2.​
(19)
For γ=5/3, u∞​=2cs,m​. Also T∝ργ−1∝r−2(γ−1). These are the adiabatic nozzle terminal speed and expansion laws for a lossless escaping branch; a subsonic branch could instead approach finite pressure with u∝A−1. A pressure-shaped astrophysical jet is being approximated by a nozzle here: the assumed geometry does not by itself explain the confinement or account for magnetic forces or relativistic motion in an actual active galactic nucleus.
