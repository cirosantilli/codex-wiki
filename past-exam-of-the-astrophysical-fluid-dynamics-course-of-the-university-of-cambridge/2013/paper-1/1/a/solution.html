Let x measure distance down the incline and y distance perpendicular to it, with the wall at y=0 and the free surface at y=h. A uniform steady falling film flow has u=u(y)ex​. Its convective acceleration vanishes because u⋅∇=u∂x​ and ∂x​u=0. A flat surface exposed to constant atmospheric pressure has
p(y)=patm​+ρgcosα(h−y).
(1)
Thus ∂x​p=0, and the downslope component of the Navier-Stokes equations reduces to 0=gsinα+(η/ρ)u′′. The printed pressure-free equation must be understood as this downslope projection: its full vector version would incorrectly omit the normal hydrostatic pressure gradient that balances gcosα.
The no-slip boundary condition gives u(0)=0. Neglecting air shear stress makes the surface shear-free, so u′(h)=0. Two integrations give the uniform gravity-driven Newtonian film profile
u′(y)=ηρgsinα​(h−y),u(y)=ηρgsinα​(hy−2y2​).
(2)
The velocity increases up to the free surface, where
umax​=2ηρgh2sinα​.​
(3)
This assumes a uniform laminar Newtonian fluid film, a stationary wall and negligible surface shear; a varying thickness or externally imposed downslope pressure gradient would alter the balance.
