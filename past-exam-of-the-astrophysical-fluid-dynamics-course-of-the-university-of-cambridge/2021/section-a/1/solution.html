Past exam of the astrophysical fluid dynamics course of the University of Cambridge / 2021 / Section A / 1 / Solution

The first argument mixes a material fluid element with a fixed spatial volume. For a material volume element following the fluid, its volume is generally time dependent:
DtD​δV=(∇⋅u)δV.
(1)
Therefore the material derivative cannot be taken outside the product with δV as in the proposed cancellation. Instead, mass conservation states
DtD​(ρδV)=0,DtDρ​=−ρ∇⋅u.
(2)
The momentum balance for a material fluid element, using Newton's second law, is consequently
DtD​(ρδVu)=(ρδV)DtDu​=−∇pδV,ρDtDu​=−∇p.​
(3)
For a fixed control volume, fluid crosses its boundary and carries momentum; a momentum flux term must be included. Its local conservative form is ∂t​(ρu)+∇⋅(ρu⊗u)=−∇p, equivalent to the displayed Euler equations after using the continuity equation. The erroneous equation omits the term ρu∇⋅u needed to convert a material derivative of momentum density into this conservative form.
For the second task, take a homogeneous fluid with constant mass density ρ, prescribed gravitational acceleration g, incompressible flow and inviscid flow. Its equations are
DtDu​=−∇(ρp​)+g,∇⋅u=0.
(4)
Under the replacement ρ↦ρ​=Cρ, the same velocity solves these equations if p↦p​=Cp, up to a spatially constant pressure offset. This is density scaling invariance of incompressible Euler flow:
The velocity field alone cannot determine the uniform fluid density.​
(5)
The comparison assumes the same geometry and compatible velocity or gravitational free surface conditions; an independently specified nonuniform boundary pressure would supply additional information. The gravitational field is taken as prescribed. If the fluid's own self-gravity must be included, its field changes with mass density, so the invariance need not hold. A single freely moving particle's density-independent gravitational acceleration does not justify neglecting that distinction.
