Take the outward wavenumber k>0; the absolute value in the source makes the two propagation directions equivalent. The phase velocity of the gravity-capillary wave obeys
cp2​=k2ω2​=kg​+ρσ​k.
(9)
Differentiate with respect to k:
dkd(cp2​)​=−k2g​+ρσ​=0⇒k∗​=σρg​​.
(10)
The second derivative is positive, and cp​→∞ at both ends of the positive-k range, so this is the global minimum. The minimum phase speed of a gravity-capillary wave is
cp,min​=(ρ4gσ​)1/4.​
(11)
For g=9.81 ms−2 and the supplied surface tension and mass density,
cp,min​=0.231 ms−1.​
(12)
The corresponding wavenumber is k∗​≃369 m−1, giving wavelength λ∗​=2π/k∗​≃1.70 cm. At this minimum, cg​=dω/dk=cp​+kdcp​/dk=cp​. Thus the group velocity and phase velocity agree for this particular ripple, although they differ for general gravity-capillary waves.
