Along a family of equilibria at fixed temperature, K and the dimensionless function Ψ are fixed. The boundary mass density changes through both ρc​ and the boundary coordinate ξ:

From part (c), N=Cρc−1/2​f(ξ), where C is constant at fixed temperature and f=ξ2Ψ′. Set its logarithmic variation to zero:
0=NδN​=−21​ρc​δρc​​+ff′​δξ.
(39)
The isothermal Lane-Emden equation gives f′=ξ2e−Ψ, so the fixed-N relation is
ρc​δρc​​=Ψ′2e−Ψ​δξ.​
(40)
Substitute back into the boundary-density variation:
δρ​N​=ρ(Ψ′2e−Ψ​−Ψ′)δξ.
(41)
For a nontrivial variation at a finite positive radius, its vanishing gives the fixed-mass turning point of an isothermal sphere:
[Ψ′(ξ)]2=2e−Ψ(ξ).​
(42)
This derivative follows a fixed-mass sequence of equilibria; it is not merely the spatial derivative of density inside one sphere. The corresponding radial variation is
rδr​=(ξ1​−Ψ′e−Ψ​)δξ.
(43)
At small ξ, Ψ′≃ξ/3: the boundary density increases while the radius decreases along increasing ξ, giving the stated negative (∂p/∂V)N,T​ of the stable branch. The first density turning point marks its first loss of radial stability; it need not be confused with a turning point of radius itself.
