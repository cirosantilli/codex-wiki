Write the initial mass density as ρ=mn. The ideal gas law gives pressure p=nkB​T, and the sphere has M=4πρR3/3. Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and G is the gravitational constant. First obtain the gravitational potential energy by adding concentric shells. The mass inside radius r is M(r)=Mr3/R3, so
W=−∫0R​rGM(r)​dM(r)=−∫0R​R3rGMr3​R33Mr2​dr=−5R3GM2​.
(1)
In a small contraction dR<0 at fixed total mass, the gravitational energy released is −dW=3GM2∣dR∣/(5R2). The positive work required against the gas pressure is −pdV=4πR2p∣dR∣. Equating these gives
5R23GM2​=4πR2nkB​T=mR3MkB​T​,RGMm​=5kB​T.
(2)
Use R=(3M/(4πmn))1/3 to eliminate the radius. The uniform-sphere pressure-work collapse criterion is
Mcrit​=(Gm5kB​T​)3/2(4πmn3​)1/2=4π375​​G3/2m2n​(kB​T)3/2​.​
(3)
The units are kilograms: (kB​T/(Gm))3/2 has units kg3/2m−3/2, and ρ−1/2 supplies kg−1/2m3/2. At fixed initial n and T, M>Mcrit​ makes gravity stronger than the pressure-work requirement in this model, allowing contraction.
This is a Jeans mass estimate for homologous contraction of an initially uniform sphere. It is not an exact stability limit for a hydrostatic gas sphere: uniform mass density and uniform temperature would give uniform pressure, rather than the gradient required by hydrostatic equilibrium. The numerical coefficient therefore differs from conventions based on a Jeans instability wavelength or a pressure-confined isothermal sphere. Subsequent contraction also depends on heat exchange; isothermal pressure support during gravitational collapse weakens relative to gravity as the radius decreases.

The exponential factor removes the first spatial derivative. Apply the chain rule explicitly:
∂z​Δρ=e−z/(2H)(Ψz​−2HΨ​),∂z2​Δρ=e−z/(2H)(Ψzz​−HΨz​​+4H2Ψ​),
(4)
while ∂t2​Δρ=e−z/(2H)Ψtt​. Substituting into the supplied density equation, the terms proportional to Ψz​ cancel, leaving
Ψtt​−c2Ψzz​+4H2c2​Ψ=0,ω0​=2Hc​.​
(5)
This is the atmospheric acoustic cutoff transformation. There is a sign error in the target equation in the original PDF, as well as in the transcription: the last term must be +ω02​Ψ for real positive ω0​. If the displayed minus sign is retained literally, coefficient matching would require ω02​=−c2/(4H2), so it would not define a real characteristic oscillation period.
For the corrected equation, a plane wave Ψ∝ei(kz−ωt) has dispersion relation
ω2=c2k2+ω02​.
(6)
Thus ω0​ is the acoustic cutoff frequency: vertically propagating acoustic waves have ∣ω∣≥ω0​. The scale height and sound speed have units metres and metres per second, respectively, so ω0​ has units s−1.
For an order-of-magnitude estimate in the lower terrestrial atmosphere, take H≃8.0 km and c≃340 ms−1. These are representative atmospheric values, not additional data specified by the paper. They give
ω0​≃2.1×10−2 s−1,ω0​2π​=c4πH​≃3.0×102 s≃5 min.​
(7)
The isothermal atmosphere approximation is local: a real atmospheric temperature profile and the thermodynamic response of the disturbance modify the precise cutoff and period.

For an inverted free surface, gravity promotes a displacement instead of restoring it. The surface tension term still resists curvature. Hence the gravity-capillary wave relation becomes, with q=∣k∣,
ω2=q(ρσq2​−g).​
(8)
For 0<q<kc​, where kc​=ρg/σ​, ω2<0. Writing the displacement as η∝est+ikx gives the Rayleigh-Taylor instability growth rate
s=gq−ρσq3​​,0<q<kc​.
(9)
Shorter wavelengths, q>kc​, remain oscillatory because surface tension dominates. The marginal scale is determined by the capillary length,
ℓc​=ρgσ​​=1000(9.81)7.2×10−2​​ m≃2.71 mm,λc​=2πℓc​≃1.70 cm.​
(10)
A sufficiently small aperture excludes the long unstable modes: this is finite-aperture stabilization of an inverted liquid surface. The estimate requested is therefore a container opening of order one centimetre, with 1.7 cm the marginal full wavelength rather than a geometry-independent maximum diameter.
The exact largest opening cannot be fixed from the stated infinite-surface dispersion relation alone. It requires the aperture shape and contact-line boundary conditions. For example, a one-dimensional span of width L whose contact line is pinned at both ends has a half-sine lowest mode with qmin​=π/L. In that simple model,
Lcrit​=πℓc​≃8.5 mm,L<Lcrit​ excludes growing modes.​
(11)
Different geometries change the numerical factor. This is a small-disturbance stability estimate with negligible air inertia and no leakage or contact-line failure; a guarantee that a real inverted container retains its water needs those additional boundary details.

Following a fluid element. Let X(t) be a pathline, satisfying X˙=u(X,t). The material derivative, also called the Lagrangian time derivative, is the rate observed while following that element. The multivariable chain rule gives
DtDϕ​=dtd​ϕ(X(t),t)=∂t∂ϕ​+u⋅∇ϕ.​
(12)
The partial derivative holds position fixed; the advection term accounts for motion through a spatially varying field. For a vector field, the same formula applies component by component in fixed Cartesian axes.
Momentum balance. For an inviscid flow, pressure is the only surface stress. A small material volume dV has net pressure force −∇pdV, obtained by the divergence theorem. Its mass is ρdV and its material acceleration is Du/Dt. Applying Newton's second law gives the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid,
ρDtDu​=ρ[∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u]=−∇p.​
(13)
This is the local momentum conservation equation when viscosity and physical body forces are absent. A prescribed body force per unit mass would add ρf on the right.
Changing to rotating coordinates. In a rotating frame with constant angular velocity Ω, the lab acceleration is the rotating-frame material acceleration plus 2Ω×u+Ω×(Ω×r). Therefore
ρ0​[∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u+2Ω×u]=−∇p−ρ0​Ω×(Ω×r).
(14)
For uniform rotation,
Ω×(Ω×r)=−∇(21​∣Ω×r∣2).
(15)
With constant ρ0​, this gives the Euler equation in a uniformly rotating frame,
ρ0​[∂t​u+2Ω×u+(u⋅∇)u]=−∇[p−2ρ0​​∣Ω×r∣2].​
(16)
The term subtracted from p is the equilibrium centrifugal contribution. Dividing the bracket by ρ0​ defines a reduced pressure Π=p/ρ0​−∣Ω×r∣2/2, with units m2s−2. The Coriolis acceleration remains a velocity-dependent term.
Linear disturbances. Linearize about rest in the rotating coordinates, with the equilibrium part of Π removed. The advective acceleration is quadratic in disturbance amplitude, so the linearized Euler equations and incompressible flow condition are
∂t​u+2Ω×u=−∇Π′,∇⋅u=0.
(17)
Taking the curl removes the pressure gradient. The identity
∇×(Ω×u)=Ω(∇⋅u)−(Ω⋅∇)u=−(Ω⋅∇)u
(18)
gives the inertial-wave vorticity equation,
∂t​(∇×u)−2(Ω⋅∇)u=0,∇⋅u=0.​
(19)
Plane-wave frequencies. Let k=0 and use the given plane wave convention. Incompressible flow requires k⋅u0​=0, and the vorticity equation gives
ωk×u0​=2i(Ω⋅k)u0​.
(20)
For a nonzero frequency, apply k× again, using k×(k×u0​)=−k2u0​. It follows that
ω2=k24(Ω⋅k)2​=4Ω2k2kz2​​,ω=±2Ωkkz​​.​
(21)
This is the inertial-wave dispersion relation. If θ is the angle of the wavevector to the rotation axis, the positive angular frequency is 2∣Ωcosθ∣. It is largest, 2∣Ω∣, for a parallel wavevector, decreases to zero for a perpendicular one, and is independent of the magnitude of the wavenumber at fixed direction. The two signs describe the two oscillatory polarization branches. When kz​=0, the zero-frequency result is obtained directly from the linear equations rather than by dividing by ω; it describes the nonoscillating balanced limit.

The local conservation laws. The first equation is the radial Euler equation: the material acceleration Du/Dt=∂t​u+u∂r​u is caused by the pressure gradient force per unit mass, −∂r​p/ρ. It neglects gravity and viscosity. The second equation is mass conservation in spherical coordinates,
∂t​ρ+r21​∂r​(r2ρu)=0.
(22)
The term 2ρu/r appears because the area of a spherical surface grows as r2.
To obtain the third equation, let e=p/[(γ−1)ρ] be the specific internal energy of an ideal gas with constant heat capacity ratio γ. The first law of thermodynamics for a smooth adiabatically moving element is
DtDe​+pDtD(1/ρ)​=0,DtDe​=ρ2p​DtDρ​.
(23)
Differentiating e and collecting terms gives
(γ−1)ρ1​DtDp​−(γ−1)ρ2p​DtDρ​=ρ2p​DtDρ​,
(24)
and hence
DtDp​=ργp​DtDρ​,(∂t​+u∂r​)(pρ−γ)=0.​
(25)
Thus the entropy parameter p/ργ is conserved along each smooth pathline; for fixed composition, specific entropy differs from cv​ln(p/ργ) only by a constant. This is local isentropic flow behind the shock. A physical shock wave itself generates entropy and must be treated with jump conditions, rather than by applying this smooth equation across the discontinuity.
The front-radius scaling. In the self-similar blast wave approximation there is no scale set by ambient pressure, cooling, gravity, or the initial source radius. Write R∝Eaρ0b​td. Using [E]=ML2T−2 and [ρ0​]=ML−3, dimensional analysis gives
a+b=0,2a−3b=1,−2a+d=0.
(26)
Thus a=1/5, b=−1/5, d=2/5 and
R=ξ0​(ρ0​E​)1/5t2/5.​
(27)
The dimensionless coefficient ξ0​ depends on γ and the resolved similarity profiles; dimensions alone do not fix it. Define the length scale ℓ(t)=(Et2/ρ0​)1/5, so ξ=r/ℓ and the front is at the fixed coordinate ξ=ξ0​.
Kinetic energy. Substitute ρ=ρ0​fρ​(ξ) and u=(r/t)fu​(ξ) into the volume integral. Since r=ℓξ and dr=ℓdξ,
Ekin​=t22πρ0​​∫0R​r4fρ​fu2​dr=t22πρ0​ℓ5​∫0ξ0​​ξ4fρ​fu2​dξ.
(28)
But ρ0​ℓ5/t2=E, so the first of the spherical blast-wave similarity energy integrals is
Ekin​=2πE∫0ξ0​​ξ4fρ​(ξ)fu​(ξ)2dξ=constant.​
(29)
The conclusion requires a fixed, integrable similarity profile, as in the finite-energy blast solution. Expansion increases the swept-up mass while reducing the characteristic squared speed, leaving this product constant.
Pressure and internal energy. The derivatives of the similarity variable are
∂t​ξ∣r​=−5t2ξ​,∂r​ξ∣t​=ℓ1​.
(30)
Writing a prime for differentiation with respect to ξ, the material acceleration becomes
∂t​u=t2r​[−fu​−52​ξfu′​],u∂r​u=t2r​[fu2​+ξfu​fu′​],
(31)
DtDu​=t2r​[fu2​−fu​+ξ(fu​−52​)fu′​].
(32)
The radial momentum equation therefore requires a pressure gradient on the scale ρ0​ℓ/t2. Integrating over radius gives pressure similarity in a spherical blast wave:
p(r,t)=ρ0​t2ℓ2​fp​(ξ)=E2/5ρ03/5​t−6/5fp​(ξ),​
(33)
where
fp′​=−ξfρ​[fu2​−fu​+ξ(fu​−52​)fu′​].​
(34)
The prefactor has units kgm−1s−2, as required for pressure. Strictly, the momentum equation alone permits an additional pressure uniform in space and arbitrary in time. In the scale-free strong-blast solution, shock matching and the remaining thermodynamic equation fix that freedom with the same similarity scaling; any allowed constant of integration is included in fp​. An independently imposed external pressure would introduce another scale and invalidate the simple dimensional ansatz.
For constant γ>1, the internal energy is consequently
Eint​=γ−14π​t2ρ0​ℓ5​∫0ξ0​​ξ2fp​(ξ)dξ,
(35)
Eint​=γ−14πE​∫0ξ0​​ξ2fp​(ξ)dξ=constant.​
(36)
If E is the total excess explosion energy, its normalization is
1=2π∫0ξ0​​ξ4fρ​fu2​dξ+γ−14π​∫0ξ0​​ξ2fp​dξ.
(37)
The two constant fractions need not be equal. No explicit solution for fp​ is needed to establish their time independence.

Hydrostatic equilibrium with self-gravity. A resting fluid obeys ∇p=−ρ∇Ψ. Spherical symmetry and the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity give
drdp​=−ρdrdΨ​,r21​drd​(r2drdΨ​)=4πGρ.
(38)
Eliminate the Newtonian gravitational potential derivative to obtain
drd​(ρr2​drdp​)=−4πGr2ρ.​
(39)
Equivalently, the shell theorem gives Ψ′=GM(r)/r2 and M′(r)=4πr2ρ, which leads to the same hydrostatic equilibrium equation.
A uniform-density liquid. With constant ρ, M(r)=4πρr3/3. There is no central point mass, so the interior gravitational acceleration magnitude is 4πGρr/3. Thus
drdp​=−34πGρ2​r.
(40)
Use p(R)=0 at a vacuum boundary, neglecting surface tension. The uniform-density planetary pressure profile is
p(r)=32πGρ2​(R2−r2),0≤r≤R.​
(41)
The central value can also be written
pc​=32πGρ2R2​=8πR43GM2​.​
(42)
It has units pascals and decreases smoothly from its maximum at the centre to zero at the surface.
An isothermal gas. Here c is the constant isothermal sound speed, with p=c2ρ. Try p=A/r2, so ρ=A/(c2r2). The two sides of the equilibrium equation become
drd​(ρr2​drdp​)=drd​(−2c2r)=−2c2,−4πGr2ρ=−c24πGA​.
(43)
Thus A=c4/(2πG), giving the singular isothermal sphere,
p(r)=2πGr2c4​,ρ(r)=2πGr2c2​,M(r)=G2c2r​.​
(44)
This does not describe a finite gaseous sphere in vacuum. Both pressure and mass density diverge at the centre, and p(R)>0 for every finite outer radius R, so it cannot meet p=0 there. Extending it to infinity removes the finite-radius pressure jump but makes the total mass infinite. It is useful as an idealized profile over an intermediate radial range, or as a truncated model with nonzero external pressure. The vacuum boundary obstruction for an isothermal sphere also applies to the regular, nonsingular isothermal solution; removing the central singularity does not produce a finite vacuum surface.
Steady outflow. In the wind calculation, take the central mass M to be fixed and neglect the wind's own self-gravity. Constant mass flow rate and the isothermal equation of state give
4πr2ρu=M˙,ρ1​drdρ​=−r2​−u1​drdu​,p=c2ρ.
(45)
The steady radial Euler equation is
udrdu​=−c2ρ1​drdρ​−r2GM​.
(46)
Substituting the density derivative gives
(u−uc2​)drdu​=r2c2​−r2GM​.​
(47)
This is the isothermal Parker wind equation. A smooth sonic point requires both sides to vanish when u=c, so
rs​=2c2GM​,u(rs​)=c.​
(48)
Maintaining constant temperature during expansion generally requires heat input; it is an isothermal model, not the adiabatic blast-wave model of Question 5.
The escaping branch at large radius. Integrate the wind equation, using dimensionless logarithm arguments:
2u2​−c2lncu​=2c2lnrs​r​+rGM​+CE​.
(49)
Set x=r/rs​ and w=u2/c2. Then
w−lnw=4lnx+x4​+C0​.
(50)
For the accelerating transonic branch, w(1)=1, which fixes C0​=−3. At x≫1 on its supersonic branch, w→∞ and lnw is smaller than w. Consequently
w∼4lnx,u(r)∼2cln(r/rs​)​.​
(51)
A first correction makes the ordering explicit:
w=4lnx+ln(4lnx)−3+o(1).
(52)
This is the large-radius asymptotics of an isothermal Parker wind. The leading expression also holds for other supersonic branches with finite C0​.
The source implicitly selects an escaping wind. Positive steady outward flow alone does not determine that branch: a subsonic isothermal breeze also satisfies the differential equation. Its w≪1 limit gives w∼e−C0​x−4, hence u∝r−2 and ρ→ρ∞​>0. Such a breeze needs nonzero pressure at infinity. Selecting a regular outflow from a subsonic base into vanishing ambient pressure instead gives the accelerating transonic wind used above.
The asymptotic speed has the same units as c; the logarithm is dimensionless. Its unlimited growth is a formal feature of indefinite isothermal heating and a prescribed point-mass potential. Real heating, surrounding pressure, and eventually the neglected wind mass limit that extrapolation.
