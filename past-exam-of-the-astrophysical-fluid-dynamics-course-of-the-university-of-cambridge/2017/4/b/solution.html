The surface must be a material surface S(t), moving with the fluid; its boundary moves with it as well. The stated barotropic vorticity transport law, with ω=∇×v, gives ∇⋅ω=0. Expanding its curl therefore yields
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)v−ω(∇⋅v).
(43)
A material tangent vector t obeys Dt/Dt=(∇v)t. Apply the product rule to the cross product of two such tangents to obtain material surface element evolution:
DtD(dS)​=[(∇⋅v)I−(∇v)T]dS.
(44)
Here (∇v)ij​=∂j​vi​. The stretching and area-change terms cancel exactly in the transported flux element:
DtD​(ω⋅dS)​=[(ω⋅∇)v−ω∇⋅v]⋅dS+ω⋅[(∇⋅v)I−(∇v)T]dS=0.​
(45)
Integrate over the fixed particle labels parameterizing the material surface. This proves the conservation of vorticity flux through a material surface:
dtd​∫S(t)​ω⋅dS=0.​
(46)
The conserved flux is also the circulation ∮∂S(t)​v⋅dℓ by the Stokes theorem, so this agrees with Kelvin's circulation theorem. The assumptions are smooth inviscid barotropic flow and conservative body forces. A fixed open surface generally exchanges vorticity flux with its surroundings and does not satisfy this conservation statement; compressibility by itself does not invalidate the material-surface result.
