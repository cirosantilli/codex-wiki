Following a parcel. Let X(t) be a fluid pathline, with X˙=u(X,t). The multivariable chain rule gives the material derivative,
DtDQ​=dtd​Q(X(t),t)=∂t∂Q​+u⋅∇Q.​
(24)
The first term measures change at fixed position; the advection term accounts for the parcel moving through spatial gradients. The derivative is also called the Lagrangian derivative.
The forces on a parcel. A material volume dV has mass ρdV. The pressure forces on its boundary sum to −∇pdV, while the gravitational acceleration is −∇ψ. Newton's second law therefore gives the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid,
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−ρ1​∇p−∇ψ.​
(25)
This neglects viscosity and other body forces. The left side is the parcel's acceleration, not just the time derivative at a fixed spatial location.
Bernoulli conservation. The claimed streamline result needs steady flow and a barotropic fluid, p=p(ρ), or an equivalent enthalpy relation along each isentropic streamline. These hypotheses are necessary; the unsteady Euler equation alone does not imply the stated conservation law. Define the barotropic enthalpy function
hb​(p)=∫pref​p​ρ(p′)dp′​,∇hb​=ρ∇p​.
(26)
Dot the steady Euler equation with u, using u⋅[(u⋅∇)u]=u⋅∇(u2/2). This gives
u⋅∇(21​u2+hb​+ψ)=0.
(27)
Thus the Bernoulli function is
H=21​u2+∫ρdp​+ψ=constant along each streamline.​
(28)
Every term has units of energy per mass, m2s−2. Different streamlines can have different constants.
The spherical accretion equation. Write the inward speed as u(r)>0, so the velocity vector is −u(r)r and the positive mass accretion rate is
M˙=4πr2ρu,drdlnρ​=−r2​−drdlnu​.
(29)
Assume a prescribed central potential ψ=−GM/r and negligible gas self-gravity. The steady radial Euler equation is
udrdu​=−ρ1​drdp​−r2GM​.
(30)
For a barotropic equation of state, dp/dr=cs2​dρ/dr, with cs2​=dp/dρ. Eliminate the density derivative to obtain
(u2−cs2​)drdlnu​=r2cs2​​−r2GM​=r2cs2​​(1−2cs2​rGM​).​
(31)
A smooth sonic point requires the right side to vanish where u=cs​, hence
rs​=2cs​(rs​)2GM​,u(rs​)=cs​(rs​).​
(32)
For a nonisothermal gas this is an implicit relation involving the local sound speed. In the isothermal case cs​ is constant and this is the Bondi sonic point.
The isothermal integral. For p=cs2​ρ, choose the enthalpy reference so that hb​=cs2​ln(ρ/ρ∞​). The reservoir is at rest at infinity and ψ(∞)=0, making its Bernoulli constant zero. Thus
21​u2+cs2​lnρ∞​ρ​−rGM​=0,
(33)
or
u2=2cs2​lnρρ∞​​+r2GM​.​
(34)
The logarithm has a dimensionless argument. Constant temperature during compression generally requires heat removal, so this is Isothermal Bondi accretion, rather than adiabatic accretion with γ>1.
The critical accretion rate. Select the smooth reservoir-connected transonic branch. Evaluate the last relation at us​=cs​ and GM/rs​=2cs2​:
cs2​=2cs2​lnρs​ρ∞​​+4cs2​,ρs​=e3/2ρ∞​.​
(35)
Mass conservation then gives the isothermal Bondi accretion rate,
M˙=4πrs2​ρs​cs​=πe3/2cs3​G2M2ρ∞​​.​
(36)
The units are kgs−1. Equivalently M˙=4πλG2M2ρ∞​/cs3​, with λ=e3/2/4≃1.12.
The inner-boundary selection of isothermal Bondi flow is important: a standard absorbing accretor inside the sonic radius selects this critical solution. The steady equations and ρ∞​ alone also admit subcritical subsonic branches supported by a different inner boundary; they do not by themselves fix a unique rate. The usual answer presumes transonic capture.
