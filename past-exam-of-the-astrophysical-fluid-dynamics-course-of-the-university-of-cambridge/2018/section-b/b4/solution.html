The background and the Jeans prescription. For a resting fluid, u0​=0 and continuity is automatic. The remaining hydrostatic equilibrium equations are
∇p0​=−ρ0​∇ψ0​,∇2ψ0​=4πGρ0​.​
(37)
An infinite uniform static medium with constant p0​ and ρ0​>0 would require ∇ψ0​=0 by force balance, but then its Laplacian would vanish, contradicting the Poisson equation. An isolated static uniform background is therefore not an exact equilibrium of this system.
The Jeans swindle suppresses the gravitational field of the uniform background and retains only the field sourced by perturbations. Formally it replaces the source by ρ−ρ0​ in the perturbation problem. This can be regarded as a specified compensating background or a local idealization; it does not prove that the original infinite isolated medium can be static.
Linearized equations. Put δρ=Δρ, δp=Δp, δψ=Δψ, and v=Δu. For uniform ρ0​,p0​, neglect products of perturbations. Continuity gives
∂t​δρ+ρ0​∇⋅v=0.​
(38)
The advective acceleration is quadratic, and the background pressure gradient vanishes, so the Euler equation becomes
∂t​v=−ρ0​1​∇δp−∇δψ.​
(39)
With the Jeans prescription and constant sound speed, the gravitational and pressure closures are
∇2δψ=4πGδρ,δp=cs2​δρ.​
(40)
The density correction to 1/ρ multiplying ∇δp is second order and is consistently omitted.
The longitudinal dispersion relation. Differentiate continuity in time and substitute the divergence of the linearized Euler equation:
∂t2​δρ=∇2δp+ρ0​∇2δψ=cs2​∇2δρ+4πGρ0​δρ.
(41)
For a plane wave proportional to ei(k⋅x−ωt), ∂t2​↦−ω2 and ∇2↦−k2. Therefore
ω2=cs2​k2−4πGρ0​=cs2​(k2−kJ2​),kJ​=cs​4πGρ0​​​.​
(42)
The physical Jeans length is
ℓJ​=kJ​2π​=cs​Gρ0​π​​.​
(43)
Since Gρ0​ has units inverse square seconds, kJ​ has units m−1 and ℓJ​ metres. This is the compressive density branch of the Jeans instability; transverse velocity disturbances have no pressure or gravity restoring force in this static nonrotating model and are neutral at linear order.
Which scales grow. For 0<k<kJ​, or wavelength ℓ>ℓJ​, ω2<0 and one temporal solution grows exponentially. The static Jeans growth-rate spectrum is
s(k)=4πGρ0​−cs2​k2​,δρ∝es(k)t.​
(44)
Its derivative is negative for 0<k<kJ​, so longer wavelengths grow faster. The supremum is s→4πGρ0​​ as k→0+: there is no selected finite fastest wavelength in this infinite-medium calculation. The exactly uniform k=0 density change is not an ordinary finite-potential Fourier perturbation with the fixed background subtraction. In a finite cloud, boundaries set the longest allowed scale.
For k>kJ​, the density perturbation oscillates as a stable acoustic wave. At k=kJ​ the linear equation is ∂t2​δρ=0, so the mode is marginal rather than exponentially growing.
Comparing acoustic and gravitational times. A sound-crossing estimate for a region of size ℓ is tsound​∼ℓ/cs​. Self-gravitational acceleration scales as Gρ0​ℓ, so collapse over a distance ℓ takes tgrav​∼(Gρ0​)−1/2. Equating these scales gives
ℓ∼Gρ0​​cs​​,
(45)
which agrees with the Jeans length up to its numerical factor π​. This is the physical competition between pressure communication and gravitational contraction.
The constants can be checked using the free-fall time of a uniform sphere. For a pressureless shell initially at radius a, enclosing fixed mass Ma​, energy conservation gives r˙2=2GMa​(1/r−1/a). Thus
tff​=∫0a​2GMa​(1/r−1/a)​dr​=22​π​GMa​a3​​=32Gρ0​3π​​,
(46)
where Ma​=4πρ0​a3/3. Consequently the Jeans length and gravitational time conventions satisfy
cs​ℓJ​​=Gρ0​π​​=332​​tff​≃3.27tff​.​
(47)
They agree as characteristic scales, not as identical exact times. Alternatively, using the length kJ−1​ gives 1/(cs​kJ​)=1/4πGρ0​​, exactly the gravitational inverse frequency entering the dispersion relation. The actual growth time of a marginal Jeans mode is infinite because pressure and gravity cancel there; it should not be confused with a pressureless free-fall time.
