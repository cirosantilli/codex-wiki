For a Newtonian circular orbit, vϕ2​=GM/r, so the specific orbital energy is e=vϕ2​/2−GM/r=−GM/(2r). Half of the released potential energy remains in circular orbital motion. The binding-energy estimate between the specified edges is therefore
Lbind​≃2GMm˙​(rin​1​−rout​1​)=2Rs​GMm˙​(61​−301​)=30m˙c2​.
(4)
Using M⊙​=1.98847×1030kg and one Julian year =3.15576×107s gives m˙=6.30×1022kgs−1. Thus the radiative luminosity scale is L∼1.9×1038W, approximately 1.9×1045ergs−1 or 4.9×1011L⊙​. The black-hole mass cancels because both edge radii scale with M. The specified inner edge is 6Rs​; it must not be silently replaced with a different relativistic orbit radius.
For a finite viscous disc, the precise radiated power also depends on boundary work: torque transport of energy in a steady accretion disk means that orbital binding-energy release need not all be radiated inside the two stated edges. If the intended model is a radiatively efficient steady Keplerian accretion disk with a zero-torque inner boundary condition, integrate the one-face flux F=3GMm˙[1−rin​/r​]/(8πr3) over both faces. It gives
Ldiss​=2rin​GMm˙​[1−3rout​rin​​+2(rout​rin​​)3/2]≃1.37×1038W​.
(5)
The difference from Lbind​ is the outward torque power at the outer edge. The question supplies neither boundary torque nor the external material-supply energy budget, so the two calculations state their assumptions explicitly: about 2×1038 W is the binding-energy estimate, while 1.37×1038 W is the finite-disc zero-inner-torque dissipation result. Both neglect radiation advection, winds and relativistic corrections as required for this model.[ref]
