Use the idealized fully developed, laminar cylindrical-flow model implied by the fixed cross-section and zero velocity at its sides. Take the jet length to be R, as for a central source reaching the cloud boundary; if the source is off-centre, the actual path length must replace R. Let s denote distance from the jet axis and write the axial velocity as u(s). Then (u⋅∇)u=0, the pressure gradient is dp/dz=−p0​/R, and the axial Navier-Stokes equation reduces to
0=Rp0​​+ρνs1​dsd​(sdsdu​).
(19)
Regularity at s=0 excludes a logarithmic term. The imposed no-slip boundary condition u(r0​)=0 gives the Hagen-Poiseuille flow profile
u(s)=4ρνRp0​​(r02​−s2)=Umax​(1−r02​s2​),Umax​=4ρνRp0​r02​​.
(20)
The kinetic energy crossing each area element per unit time is (ρu2/2)udA, so the velocity must be cubed before integration. Thus
P=21​ρ∫u3dA=πρUmax3​∫0r0​​(1−r02​s2​)3sds=8πρUmax3​r02​​.
(21)
Consequently
P=512ρ2ν3R3πp03​​r08​,A=512ρ2ν3R3πp03​​​.
(22)
The mean velocity is uˉ=Umax​/2. Replacing the profile by uˉ in 21​ρπr02​uˉ3 underestimates the flux by two; the kinetic energy correction factor is two for this profile. The radius dependence follows from u∝r02​ and cross-sectional area ∝r02​.
The specified P is outgoing bulk kinetic-energy flux, not the pressure-work rate p0​Q, which scales as r04​. In a fully developed section the profile is independent of z and pressure work is dissipated as heat; entrance acceleration and energy supply establish the profile. A turbulent or freely entraining astrophysical jet need not have this no-slip parabolic structure. The model assumes a long enough, laminar confined region and neglects entrance losses and gravity.
