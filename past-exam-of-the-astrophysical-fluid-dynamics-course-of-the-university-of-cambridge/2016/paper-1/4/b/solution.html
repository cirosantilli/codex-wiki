Treat the gas as a smooth quasi-one-dimensional flow through a rigid passage, neglecting axial body forces and heat input. It is the confining medium that is incompressible; the flowing gas must remain compressible. With area A(x) and axial speed u, continuity and inviscid momentum balance give
ρuA=M˙,udu=−ρdp​,dp=cs2​dρ.
(30)
Combining their logarithmic differentials yields the nozzle area-velocity relation
AdA​=(M2−1)udu​,M=cs​u​.
(31)
A smooth crossing of M=1 requires an area extremum. The given radius is positive and has derivative r′(x)=16r0​xexp[−16x2/L2]/L2, which vanishes only at x=0 within the slab and changes from negative to positive there. Thus this station is the unique throat, with rs​=r0​/2 and As​=πr02​/4. The specified subsonic inlet and supersonic outlet select the smooth accelerating branch, so
xs​=0​.
(32)
A throat by itself does not guarantee transonic flow for arbitrary reservoir and back pressures; that branch is specified here. Before the throat a converging section accelerates subsonic flow; beyond it a diverging section accelerates supersonic flow.[ref]
For the temperature task, evaluate the mass flux and sound speed at the throat using p=Kργ:
M˙=As​ρs​cs​=As​γK​ρs(γ+1)/2​,ρs​=(As​γK​M˙​)2/(γ+1).
(33)
The ideal-gas relation then gives
Txs​​=R∗μK​(πr02​γK​4M˙​)2(γ−1)/(γ+1)​.
(34)
This expresses the sonic temperature in terms of the entropy constant, geometry, mass flux and composition; no numerical value of K or M˙ is provided. Equivalently csγ+1​=γK(M˙/As​)γ−1 and Ts​=μcs2​/(γR∗). The slab length drops out because the sonic thermodynamic state depends on the minimum area, not on the axial distance over which a lossless smooth nozzle contracts. As a check, conserved stagnation enthalpy gives Ts​=2Tstag​/(γ+1).[ref]
The result assumes cross-sectional thermodynamic uniformity and negligible transverse kinetic energy. Exact multidimensional flow through a sharply varying passage can have a curved sonic surface; the radius profile alone does not solve that boundary-value problem.
