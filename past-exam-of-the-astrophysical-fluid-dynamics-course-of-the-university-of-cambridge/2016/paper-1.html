Let z increase upwards and let g>0 denote the downward gravitational acceleration. The environment obeys hydrostatic equilibrium, dp/dz=−ρg. Displace a small parcel by ζ while it remains in pressure balance with its surroundings and exchanges negligible heat. For fixed composition and adiabatic index γ, its density change at constant specific entropy satisfies dρpar​/dp=ρ/(γp). At the displaced position, its density excess over the environment is therefore
Δρ=(γpρ​dzdp​−dzdρ​)ζ+O(ζ2).
(1)
The buoyancy acceleration is ζ¨​≃−gΔρ/ρ. Hence
ζ¨​=−N2ζ,N2=ρg​(γpρ​dzdp​−dzdρ​).
(2)
Stability requires N2>0, equivalently
pγρ​dzdp​>dzdρ​​.
(3)
An upward-displaced parcel is then denser than its surroundings and falls back; a downward-displaced parcel is lighter and rises. Equality gives neutral stability in this parcel model. For a perfect gas with constant composition, ds=cv​dln(p/ργ) gives N2=(g/cp​)ds/dz, so a rising specific entropy is stable. This is the parcel form of the Schwarzschild criterion. Rapid pressure equilibration, negligible heat diffusion during displacement and no composition-driven buoyancy are the assumptions behind this test.

For a Newtonian circular orbit, vϕ2​=GM/r, so the specific orbital energy is e=vϕ2​/2−GM/r=−GM/(2r). Half of the released potential energy remains in circular orbital motion. The binding-energy estimate between the specified edges is therefore
Lbind​≃2GMm˙​(rin​1​−rout​1​)=2Rs​GMm˙​(61​−301​)=30m˙c2​.
(4)
Using M⊙​=1.98847×1030kg and one Julian year =3.15576×107s gives m˙=6.30×1022kgs−1. Thus the radiative luminosity scale is L∼1.9×1038W, approximately 1.9×1045ergs−1 or 4.9×1011L⊙​. The black-hole mass cancels because both edge radii scale with M. The specified inner edge is 6Rs​; it must not be silently replaced with a different relativistic orbit radius.
For a finite viscous disc, the precise radiated power also depends on boundary work: torque transport of energy in a steady accretion disk means that orbital binding-energy release need not all be radiated inside the two stated edges. If the intended model is a radiatively efficient steady Keplerian accretion disk with a zero-torque inner boundary condition, integrate the one-face flux F=3GMm˙[1−rin​/r​]/(8πr3) over both faces. It gives
Ldiss​=2rin​GMm˙​[1−3rout​rin​​+2(rout​rin​​)3/2]≃1.37×1038W​.
(5)
The difference from Lbind​ is the outward torque power at the outer edge. The question supplies neither boundary torque nor the external material-supply energy budget, so the two calculations state their assumptions explicitly: about 2×1038 W is the binding-energy estimate, while 1.37×1038 W is the finite-disc zero-inner-torque dissipation result. Both neglect radiation advection, winds and relativistic corrections as required for this model.[ref]

Immediately upstream, free fall from rest at infinity gives u12​=2GM0​/r0​. Treat the surface shock as locally planar and stationary, with negligible upstream pressure. The Strong-shock Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a monatomic gas, γ=5/3, give ρ2​/ρ1​=4 and u2​=u1​/4. momentum conservation then gives
p2​=ρ1​u12​−ρ2​u22​=43​ρ1​u12​,ρ2​p2​​=163​u12​.
(6)
Use the source's gas-constant convention p=ρ(R∗/μ)T. The immediate postshock temperature is
T2​=R∗μ​163​r0​2GM0​​=R∗μ​83​r0​GM0​​​.
(7)
Here adiabatic means that the jump does not lose appreciable heat to the surroundings; the shock is nevertheless irreversible and raises entropy. The gas still has velocity u1​/4 immediately downstream, so setting all upstream kinetic energy equal to downstream internal energy would give the wrong coefficient. Later settling, radiative cooling or changing ionization can alter the temperature; they are outside this immediate, fixed-composition perfect-gas jump.

The Rayleigh-Taylor instability extracts gravitational potential energy when a heavy fluid is supported above a lighter one. A small downward excursion of heavy material and upward excursion of light material can grow instead of experiencing restoring buoyancy. The Kelvin-Helmholtz instability instead extracts kinetic energy from a velocity difference across an interface or shear layer. The interface displacement and its induced flow reinforce one another by tapping the background shear; a reversed density ordering is not required.
A useful common model has two inviscid, incompressible, semi-infinite fluids separated at z=0, with lower density/velocity ρ1​,U1​, upper density/velocity ρ2​,U2​, downward gravity g, and surface tension σ. For a disturbance proportional to ei(kx−ωt), with k>0, the two-fluid interfacial gravity-shear dispersion relation is
(ω−kρ1​+ρ2​ρ1​U1​+ρ2​U2​​)2=ρ1​+ρ2​gk(ρ1​−ρ2​)+σk3​−(ρ1​+ρ2​)2ρ1​ρ2​​k2(U1​−U2​)2.
(8)
A negative right side gives exponential growth. With no shear or surface tension and ρ2​>ρ1​, the growth rate of the Rayleigh-Taylor instability is s=Agk​, where A=(ρ2​−ρ1​)/(ρ2​+ρ1​) is the Atwood number. With equal densities and no restoring force, the growth rate of the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability is s=k∣U1​−U2​∣/2. Heavy-over-light stratification drives Rayleigh-Taylor growth; velocity shear drives Kelvin-Helmholtz growth. A heavy lower fluid supplies gravitational stabilization, and surface tension stabilizes sufficiently short waves in this ideal interface model.
Finite shear-layer thickness, viscosity, compressibility and magnetic tension can change the growth rates and unstable wavelength range. In particular, unlimited growth with increasing k is a limitation of the zero-thickness, zero-surface-tension idealization. Density-interface overturning and jet-boundary mixing are appropriate physical applications of the two mechanisms, but the nonlinear mixed state is not determined by the linear growth rate alone.

A finite-amplitude compression can steepen because different parts of the wave travel at different characteristic speeds. For a right-moving simple wave in a perfect gas, one may write dx/dt=c0​+(γ+1)u/2 in terms of the local velocity perturbation u. Faster compressive characteristics catch slower ones. When the smooth inviscid description would become multivalued, a physical compression shock replaces it. A rarefaction normally spreads rather than forming an entropy-violating expansion shock.[ref]
Across a locally planar shock, use velocities relative to its moving front. The Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a perfect gas conserve mass, momentum and total energy flux:
ρ1​u1​=ρ2​u2​=j,p1​+ρ1​u12​=p2​+ρ2​u22​,h1​+21​u12​=h2​+21​u22​,
(9)
where h=γp/[(γ−1)ρ] is the specific enthalpy. For upstream normal Mach number M1​>1,
ρ1​ρ2​​=2+(γ−1)M12​(γ+1)M12​​,p1​p2​​=γ+12γM12​−(γ−1)​,M22​=γM12​−(γ−1)/21+(γ−1)M12​/2​.
(10)
Thus a normal compression shock increases pressure, density and temperature, reduces the normal velocity, and changes a supersonic upstream flow to subsonic downstream flow in the shock frame. In the strong-shock limit, compression approaches (γ+1)/(γ−1), which is four for a monatomic gas. A shock conserves total energy but generates entropy; an adiabatic shock is not an isentropic compression. For a calorically perfect gas, stagnation temperature is conserved while stagnation pressure falls.[ref]
Real dissipative processes resolve the narrow front; the jump conditions do not require its detailed internal profile. In a continuum-gas experiment a shock tube can produce a moving shock by releasing a pressure discontinuity. pressure probes separated along the tube measure arrival speed and the pressure jump; distinguishing the shock from the following contact surface is essential. A contact surface can have a density/entropy jump with continuous pressure and normal velocity, whereas a shock carries nonzero mass flux through its front.

A small pressure disturbance propagates because compression raises the pressure, the pressure gradient accelerates nearby fluid, and the induced convergence compresses the next region. For a uniform fluid at rest, linearizing mass and momentum conservation and using the adiabatic pressure-density response gives
∂t∂ρ′​+ρ0​∇⋅u′=0,ρ0​∂t∂u′​=−∇p′,p′=cs2​ρ′,cs2​=(∂ρ∂p​)s​.
(11)
Eliminating ρ′ and u′ gives ∂t2​p′=cs2​∇2p′. A plane wave has ω2=cs2​∣k∣2 and longitudinal velocity parallel to k. In a perfect gas,
cs​=γp0​/ρ0​​=γ(R∗/μ)T0​​​.
(12)
The adiabatic response is appropriate when heat cannot diffuse appreciably out of each compressed region during a period; an isothermal response would give a different derivative. In a moving uniform medium, the acoustic characteristics travel at U±cs​ along the flow, rather than at ±cs​ in the laboratory.
For a progressive wave, p′=ρ0​cs​u∥′​; pressure, density and longitudinal velocity are in phase. The acoustic energy density is the sum of kinetic and compression energies,
Eac​=21​ρ0​∣u′∣2+2ρ0​cs2​p′2​,⟨I⟩=ρ0​cs​prms2​​.
(13)
Sound transports energy while fluid elements execute small oscillations about their mean positions. The ideal uniform-fluid acoustic mode is nondispersive. Spherical spreading reduces pressure amplitude as 1/r when total power is conserved; viscosity and heat conduction attenuate waves, while sufficiently large compressions can steepen into shocks. In a stratified astrophysical gas the uniform result is local: wavelengths short compared with background scale heights justify it, whereas long waves can couple to buoyancy and acoustic-cutoff behavior.

Let e=cv​T be the specific internal energy of a calorically perfect gas and define its specific gas constant R=R∗/μ, so p=RρT. A reversible adiabatic change has no heat input and obeys
de+pd(1/ρ)=0⟹cv​dT−ρRT​dρ=0.
(14)
Using cp​−cv​=R and γ=cp​/cv​ gives
TdT​=(γ−1)ρdρ​,dlnp=dlnρ+dlnT=γdlnρ.
(15)
Integration yields p/ργ=K, or p=Kργ​, with K fixed on that isentrope. Equivalently s−sref​=cv​ln[(p/ργ)/Kref​], so K specifies entropy as well as composition. In a smooth nondissipative flow this means D(pρ−γ)/Dt=0 along each parcel. A single common K throughout the fluid additionally requires a homentropic flow. No heat transfer alone does not forbid entropy production: different sides of an adiabatic shock generally have different K values. Constant composition and heat capacities are needed for the fixed exponent used here.

Write ω=∇×u and use the vector identity (u⋅∇)u=∇(∣u∣2/2)−u×ω. Taking the curl of the momentum equation eliminates all pure gradients, including gravity and the ∇(∇⋅u) viscous contribution. Before invoking barotropy, the pressure term gives
∇×(−ρ1​∇p)=−∇(1/ρ)×∇p=ρ2∇ρ×∇p​.
(16)
This is baroclinic vorticity generation. For a barotropic fluid, p=p(ρ), so ∇p is parallel to ∇ρ and this source vanishes. Constant ν permits the curl and Laplacian to commute. The result is
∂t∂ω​=∇×(u×ω)+ν∇2ω​.
(17)
No incompressibility assumption was used. Since ∇⋅ω=0, an equivalent form is
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)u−ω∇⋅u+ν∇2ω.
(18)
The terms represent vortex stretching/tilting, compression/dilatation and viscous diffusion. In particular, removing the baroclinic source does not make the vorticity of a three-dimensional parcel constant. A spatially varying entropy-dependent K in p=Kργ need not define a single barotropic law; the common-pressure-law assumption matters.

Use the idealized fully developed, laminar cylindrical-flow model implied by the fixed cross-section and zero velocity at its sides. Take the jet length to be R, as for a central source reaching the cloud boundary; if the source is off-centre, the actual path length must replace R. Let s denote distance from the jet axis and write the axial velocity as u(s). Then (u⋅∇)u=0, the pressure gradient is dp/dz=−p0​/R, and the axial Navier-Stokes equation reduces to
0=Rp0​​+ρνs1​dsd​(sdsdu​).
(19)
Regularity at s=0 excludes a logarithmic term. The imposed no-slip boundary condition u(r0​)=0 gives the Hagen-Poiseuille flow profile
u(s)=4ρνRp0​​(r02​−s2)=Umax​(1−r02​s2​),Umax​=4ρνRp0​r02​​.
(20)
The kinetic energy crossing each area element per unit time is (ρu2/2)udA, so the velocity must be cubed before integration. Thus
P=21​ρ∫u3dA=πρUmax3​∫0r0​​(1−r02​s2​)3sds=8πρUmax3​r02​​.
(21)
Consequently
P=512ρ2ν3R3πp03​​r08​,A=512ρ2ν3R3πp03​​​.
(22)
The mean velocity is uˉ=Umax​/2. Replacing the profile by uˉ in 21​ρπr02​uˉ3 underestimates the flux by two; the kinetic energy correction factor is two for this profile. The radius dependence follows from u∝r02​ and cross-sectional area ∝r02​.
The specified P is outgoing bulk kinetic-energy flux, not the pressure-work rate p0​Q, which scales as r04​. In a fully developed section the profile is independent of z and pressure work is dissipated as heat; entrance acceleration and energy supply establish the profile. A turbulent or freely entraining astrophysical jet need not have this no-slip parabolic structure. The model assumes a long enough, laminar confined region and neglects entrance losses and gravity.

Write the polytropic law as p=Kρ1+1/n, with constant K and n>0. Its pressure potential, or polytropic enthalpy relative to zero density, is
H(ρ)=∫0ρ​ρ​1​dρ​dp​dρ​=(n+1)Kρ1/n.
(23)
Hydrostatic balance is dH/dr=−dψ/dr. If the surface has zero density and potential ψ0​, integration gives H=ψ0​−ψ. At the centre, Δψ≡ψ0​−ψc​=(n+1)Kρc1/n​>0. Therefore
θ=ΔψH​=(ρc​ρ​)1/n,r=αξ,α2=4πGρc​Δψ​.
(24)
Insert ψ=ψ0​−Δψθ and ρ=ρc​θn into the spherical Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity:
r21​drd​(r2drdψ​)=4πGρ⟹ξ21​dξd​(ξ2dξdθ​)=−θn​.
(25)
The central conditions are θ(0)=1 and θ′(0)=0; the latter follows from a regular spherically symmetric centre. This reproduces the supplied coordinate and potential scaling rather than introducing an unrelated normalization.
For the sound-speed task, the adiabatic sound speed of a perfect polytropic gas is
cs2​=dρdp​=γKργ−1=(γ−1)H=(γ−1)(ψ0​−ψ),n=γ−11​.
(26)
A diatomic gas with active rotational but frozen vibrational degrees of freedom has γ=7/5 and n=5/2. With the requested surface-potential gauge ψ0​=0,
cs​=−52​ψ​​.
(27)
The interior potential is then negative. Choosing zero potential at the surface is legitimate; for a finite isolated sphere it is a different additive gauge from setting potential to zero at infinity. Both choices cannot generally be imposed simultaneously.
For the mass-radius task, let ξ1​ be the first zero of the regular Lane-Emden function and Yn​=−ξ12​θ′(ξ1​). A finite-radius polytrope has
R=αξ1​,M=4πα3ρc​Yn​,RGM​=Δψξ1​Yn​​=(n+1)ξ1​Yn​​μR∗Tc​​.
(28)
Thus
M=ξ1​(n+1)Yn​​GμR∗Tc​​R​.
(29)
At fixed central temperature, fixed composition and fixed polytropic index, M∝R. These are homologous polytropes at fixed central temperature. They do not in general have constant K; imposing constant entropy instead gives a different polytropic mass-radius sequence. If n or μ varies between stars, the displayed dimensionless structure factor and composition factor must be retained: equal Tc​ alone is not a universal exact proportionality for arbitrary stellar models.

Treat the gas as a smooth quasi-one-dimensional flow through a rigid passage, neglecting axial body forces and heat input. It is the confining medium that is incompressible; the flowing gas must remain compressible. With area A(x) and axial speed u, continuity and inviscid momentum balance give
ρuA=M˙,udu=−ρdp​,dp=cs2​dρ.
(30)
Combining their logarithmic differentials yields the nozzle area-velocity relation
AdA​=(M2−1)udu​,M=cs​u​.
(31)
A smooth crossing of M=1 requires an area extremum. The given radius is positive and has derivative r′(x)=16r0​xexp[−16x2/L2]/L2, which vanishes only at x=0 within the slab and changes from negative to positive there. Thus this station is the unique throat, with rs​=r0​/2 and As​=πr02​/4. The specified subsonic inlet and supersonic outlet select the smooth accelerating branch, so
xs​=0​.
(32)
A throat by itself does not guarantee transonic flow for arbitrary reservoir and back pressures; that branch is specified here. Before the throat a converging section accelerates subsonic flow; beyond it a diverging section accelerates supersonic flow.[ref]
For the temperature task, evaluate the mass flux and sound speed at the throat using p=Kργ:
M˙=As​ρs​cs​=As​γK​ρs(γ+1)/2​,ρs​=(As​γK​M˙​)2/(γ+1).
(33)
The ideal-gas relation then gives
Txs​​=R∗μK​(πr02​γK​4M˙​)2(γ−1)/(γ+1)​.
(34)
This expresses the sonic temperature in terms of the entropy constant, geometry, mass flux and composition; no numerical value of K or M˙ is provided. Equivalently csγ+1​=γK(M˙/As​)γ−1 and Ts​=μcs2​/(γR∗). The slab length drops out because the sonic thermodynamic state depends on the minimum area, not on the axial distance over which a lossless smooth nozzle contracts. As a check, conserved stagnation enthalpy gives Ts​=2Tstag​/(γ+1).[ref]
The result assumes cross-sectional thermodynamic uniformity and negligible transverse kinetic energy. Exact multidimensional flow through a sharply varying passage can have a curved sonic surface; the radius profile alone does not solve that boundary-value problem.
