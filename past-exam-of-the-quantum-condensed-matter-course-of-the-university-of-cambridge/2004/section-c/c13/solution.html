For an isotropic free-electron gas at zero temperature, the Fermi wavevector is the radius of the occupied region in wavevector space. Its energy is EF​=ℏ2kF2​/(2m). The first Brillouin zone is the Wigner-Seitz cell of the reciprocal lattice; it contains one distinct crystal momentum per primitive cell and per band, with spin counted separately.
For a two-dimensional sample of area A, allowed wavevectors have density A/(2π)2. Including two spin states, filling a circle gives N=2[A/(2π)2]πkF2​. Dividing by area therefore yields
kF​=2πn​.​
(29)
The square lattice has one atom per area a2, so for valence z the free-electron density is z/a2. Its Brillouin zone has ∣kx​∣,∣ky​∣≤π/a. Thus
kF(1)​=a2π​​≃8.36×109m−1,kF(2)​=a4π​​≃1.18×1010m−1.​
(30)
The monovalent Fermi circle lies inside the square. The divalent circle crosses the faces but misses the corners; its area equals the zone's area. These are the free-electron contours before distortion by the periodic potential.
At a Bragg plane, the periodic potential mixes degenerate plane waves differing by a reciprocal lattice vector. The two standing-wave combinations have different charge densities relative to the ions and split in energy, opening the gap. In a two-wave approximation, the boundary energies are EB​±Eg​/2, with Eg​=2∣VG​∣.
The boundary wavevector magnitude is π/a along [10] and 2​π/a along [11]. The free-electron boundary energies are therefore
EX​=2ma2ℏ2π2​≃4.18eV,EM​=2EX​≃8.36eV.
(31)
Within the stated direction-independent gap picture, the upper band's lowest face-edge energy is approximately EX​+2eV=6.18eV, while the lower band's corner-edge energy is approximately EM​−2eV=6.36eV. There is consequently a small overlap, about 0.18eV, rather than a global insulating gap. The divalent Fermi level lies in that overlap, producing occupied upper-band states and unfilled lower-band states.
Thus the intended isotropic-gap approximation predicts a metal, with small electron and hole pockets. Positive direct gaps can coexist with band overlap. A fully specified square-lattice potential would require simultaneous treatment of the corner degeneracies; a numerical boundary splitting alone does not uniquely determine an arbitrary two-dimensional band structure. The conclusion here uses the given simplified gap picture, rather than electron count alone.
