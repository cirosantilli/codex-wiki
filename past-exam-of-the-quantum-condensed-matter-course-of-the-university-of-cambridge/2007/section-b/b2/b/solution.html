 ... Quantum Condensed Matter course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the quantum condensed matter course of the University of Cambridge 2007 Section B B2 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 152
In a heavy fermion material, interactions of narrow, often f-derived states with conduction electrons produce low-energy quasiparticles with very large thermodynamic effective mass. A coherent Kondo lattice can form a narrow low-energy band, so the large mass is an interaction-renormalized response rather than a larger bare electron mass.
Two concrete experimental signatures are an enormous linear electronic heat capacity coefficient and a strongly enhanced quadratic low-temperature electrical resistivity. Calorimetry separates C=γT+βT3 by plotting C/T against T2; the intercept measures the electronic coefficient rather than a lattice contribution. Four-terminal transport tests the Fermi liquid law ρ=ρ0​+AT2. The low-temperature calorimetry and resistivity of CeAl3 found γ=1.62Jmol−1K−2 and A=35μΩcmK−2 below 0.2K, making both signatures quantitative.[ref]
An enhanced nearly temperature-independent spin susceptibility and a large quantum-oscillation cyclotron effective mass provide additional tests. Not every heavy-fermion compound has the same ordered ground state; magnetism or superconductivity can intervene before a simple normal Fermi-liquid limit is reached.
