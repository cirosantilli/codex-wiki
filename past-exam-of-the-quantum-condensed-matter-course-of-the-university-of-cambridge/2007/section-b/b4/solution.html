Bloch's theorem states that an eigenfunction in a periodic potential can be written ψk​(r)=eik⋅ruk​(r), with uk​ lattice-periodic. In the orthogonal tight-binding approximation, choose localized orthonormal orbitals ∣j⟩ with on-site matrix element E0​ and real nearest-neighbour hopping integral t. The Bloch sum ∣k⟩=N−1/2∑j​eikja∣j⟩ is an eigenstate because each neighbour shift contributes a phase e±ika. Thus
E(k)=E0​+t(eika+e−ika)=E0​+2tcoska.​

The simple-cubic nearest-neighbour tight-binding band has six neighbours, giving E(k)=E0​+2t[cos(kx​a)+cos(ky​a)+cos(kz​a)]. The cosine sum ranges from −3 to 3, so the positive bandwidth is W=12∣t∣. The stated hopping formula is negative when E1​>0, so the printed 12t must be read as a bandwidth magnitude.
Near the zone centre, E=E0​+6t−ta2k2+O(k4). To leading order the constant-energy surfaces are spheres, and comparison with EΓ​+ℏ2k2/(2m∗) gives
m∗=−2ta2ℏ2​=4E1​a2(1+a/a1​)ℏ2ea/a1​​.​

This is the effective mass for overlap-weighted exponential hopping. With s=a/a1​, its logarithmic derivative is dlnm∗/ds=1−2/s−1/(1+s). The positive stationary point is s=1+3​, and it is a minimum. The formula grows at both very small and very large s; the small-separation divergence is a mathematical extrapolation into a regime where localized tight binding itself becomes unreliable.
