A direct band gap has its conduction minimum and valence maximum at the same crystal momentum. A photon can then connect the band-edge states with negligible momentum change. An indirect band gap requires an additional phonon to supply or remove momentum, making radiative recombination much less probable. Direct-gap materials such as gallium arsenide or gallium nitride are therefore usually better for a light-emitting diode than silicon.
For the graded junction, let q=∣e∣>0, εs​=ε0​εr​, and use electrostatic potential φ with Ex​=−φ′. Fully ionized dopants have charge density ρ=q(ND​−NA​)=qαx, so Poisson's equation is φ′′=−qαx/εs​. The paper's displayed plus sign corresponds to using the negative potential V=−φ, or a signed electron charge in that formula. The positive barrier magnitude and charge magnitude must be used consistently in the width below.
The intended compensation point is x=0, where ND​=NA​ and the net doping changes sign. A locally neutral model with comparable electron and hole mobilities has its lowest carrier conductivity near there. The depletion approximation neglects mobile-carrier conductivity throughout the layer, so it does not by itself locate an exact conductivity minimum; unequal mobilities or asymmetric carrier profiles can shift it.
