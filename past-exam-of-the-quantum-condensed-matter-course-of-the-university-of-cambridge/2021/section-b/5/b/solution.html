Let n=N/V and ce​=Ce​/V be the carrier density and electronic heat capacity per unit volume. In isotropic kinetic theory, the angular average ⟨vx2​⟩=vF2​/3 gives the electronic thermal conductivity
Ke​=31​ce​vF​ℓQ​=31​ce​vF2​τQ​,
(23)
where ℓQ​=vF​τQ​ is the mean free path associated with the heat-current relaxation time. With vF2​=2EF​/m∗,
Ke​=32A​m∗nkB2​TτQ​​.
(24)
The Drude model gives the electrical conductivity σ=ne2τσ​/m∗, where τσ​ is the momentum relaxation time. Therefore
σTKe​​=32A​(ekB​​)2τσ​τQ​​.
(25)
When the same approximately elastic collisions relax heat and charge currents, τQ​=τσ​. Using A=π2/2 gives the Wiedemann-Franz law
Ke​=L0​σT,L0​=3π2​(ekB​​)2≃2.44×10−8WΩK−2.​
(26)
L0​ is the Lorenz number. Degenerate electrons move at the Fermi velocity, not the classical thermal velocity; using classical equipartition would give the wrong coefficient.
