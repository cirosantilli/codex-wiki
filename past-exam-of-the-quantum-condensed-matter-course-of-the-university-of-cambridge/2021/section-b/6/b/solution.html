At equilibrium, majority electrons undergo diffusion from n to p, while majority electron holes diffuse from p to n. Their departure exposes positive donors on the n side and negative acceptors on the p side. The resulting electric field points from n to p and opposes further diffusion. Equilibrium is reached when the chemical potential is constant across the junction. The built-in potential is this electrostatic adjustment, not a discontinuity of the material's band gap.
Since electronic band energies shift by −eV(x), both the conduction band and valence band undergo band bending down from p to n by eVb​ at zero applied bias. Their separation remains the same gap. In the nondegenerate thermal equilibrium limit the built-in voltage can also be expressed using neutral-edge densities as eVb​=kB​Tlog(nn​/np​)=kB​Tlog(nn​pp​/ni2​).
Take the n-contact potential and its electrochemical potential as reference. Applying Vext​ to the p side changes the total depletion-layer drop to
U=Vb​−Vext​,Ec,p​−Ec,n​=Ev,p​−Ev,n​=eU.​
(32)
Thus Vext​<0 is reverse bias: it increases the barrier and widens the depletion region. The bands have horizontal tangents at the neutral edges in this approximation and a smooth cubic bend within the linearly graded layer.
A biased junction is not in global thermal equilibrium. Its contact electron electrochemical potentials obey
μp​−μn​=−eVext​.​
(33)
Under reverse bias the p-contact level is higher by e∣Vext​∣. A single horizontal equilibrium chemical potential cannot describe this state; electron and hole quasi-Fermi levels describe the nonequilibrium populations. The sketch marks the contact levels. Detailed spatial quasi-Fermi profiles require transport and recombination information beyond the electrostatic doping profile.
