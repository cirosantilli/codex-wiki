Let adjacent equal masses m be separated by a, with alternating strong and weak spring constants K and κ, K≫κ>0. The pattern repeats after 2a, even though the masses are identical. Thus the first Brillouin zone is
−2aπ​≤q≤2aπ​.​
(4)
Choose the strong bond inside each two-atom primitive unit cell. With displacement amplitudes (u,v)ei(2naq−ωt), the normal mode equations are
mω2(uv​)=(K+κ−K−κe2iqa​−K−κe−2iqaK+κ​)(uv​).
(5)
The alternating-spring chain dispersion relation is consequently
ω±2​(q)=mK+κ±(K−κ)2+4Kκcos2(qa)​​.​
(6)
The lower acoustic phonon branch has in-phase motion within the strongly bound pair and ω−​(0)=0. Its small-q slope is the speed of sound,
cs​=am(K+κ)2Kκ​​≃am2κ​​.
(7)
The upper optical phonon branch has opposite motion within each pair, with ω+​(0)=2(K+κ)/m​. Here “optical” names the gapped branch; equal neutral masses need not couple to light through an electric dipole.
In the strong-bond limit of the alternating-spring chain, the pairs act almost as rigid dimers in the low-frequency branch, while their internal stretch gives a high, nearly flat branch:
ω−2​≃m2κ​sin2(qa),ω+2​≃m2K​+m2κ​cos2(qa).
(8)
At the zone edge the two frequencies are 2κ/m​ and 2K/m​, separated by a large forbidden frequency interval. Neighbouring cells are out of phase there. Both branches have horizontal tangents at the edge.
