Use a representative conduction-electron density n∼1029m−3, roughly one mobile electron per atom in a dense metal, and the bare electron mass for this estimate. The plasma frequency is then
ωp​=me​ϵ0​ne2​​≃(9.109×10−31)(8.854×10−12)(1029)(1.602×10−19)2​​≃1.8×1016rads−1.​

This is an order-of-magnitude answer because no particular metal or carrier density is specified. It corresponds to an ordinary frequency fp​≃2.8×1015Hz, excitation energy ℏωp​≃12eV, and free-space wavelength near 1.1×10−7m.
The physical oscillation is collective motion of the electron gas against the positive ionic background. Displacing the electrons relative to the ions produces a restoring electric field. In the simplest uniform-displacement picture, x¨+ωp2​x=0: the frequency increases with the density of mobile electric charge and decreases with carrier inertia. Quantizing this longitudinal collective oscillation gives a plasmon.
The same scale controls the ideal collisionless Drude model optical response. Its cold collisionless plasma dielectric response is ϵr​(ω)=1−ωp2​/ω2. Below ωp​, the negative relative permittivity makes a transverse electromagnetic wave evanescent, producing strong reflection; above it, the ideal model admits propagation. Real-metal collisions, interband photon absorption and background polarization modify this simple optical edge. Thus ωp​ is a collective charge-response scale, not a single-electron scattering rate or a sharp transparency threshold for every real metal.
