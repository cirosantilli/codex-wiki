The Kuhn length lK​ is the segment length of an equivalent freely jointed chain chosen to match contour length L and the unperturbed mean-square end-to-end distance: ⟨R2⟩=LlK​. Since R≤L, its maximum finite-chain value is lK​=L, attained by a straight rigid rod. Independent steps of length b have L=Nb and ⟨R2⟩=Nb2, so
lK​=b.​
(15)
For a long freely rotating chain with fixed angle α between successive forward bond vectors, orientational correlations decay as cs with c=cosα. Summing them gives the Kuhn length of a freely rotating chain:
lK​≃b1−c1+c​.
(16)
Positive persistence increases the statistical segment length, while negative bond correlations can reduce it. A chemical internal bond angle is supplementary to this forward-vector angle, so the cosine convention matters. Near the perfectly aligned limit the finite-chain bound lK​≤L replaces the long-chain asymptotic formula.
