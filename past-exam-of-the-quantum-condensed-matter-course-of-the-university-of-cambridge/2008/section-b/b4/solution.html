Only states within an energy window of order kB​T around the Fermi energy can change occupation in a degenerate metal. A Sommerfeld expansion gives electronic energy above its zero-temperature value proportional to g(EF​)(kB​T)2, hence Ce​=γT. Three-dimensional linear-dispersion acoustic phonons have density of states proportional to ω2; integrating their Bose occupations gives thermal energy proportional to T4 and the Debye heat capacity Cmph​=βT3. Thus C/T=γ+βT2 at sufficiently low temperature in the normal state.
Reading the straight low-temperature fit gives approximately γ≃33 mJmol−1K−2 and β≃3.8 mJmol−1K−4. These estimates agree with the reported fit to this material in the original experiment.[ref] Treating the graph values as approximate, the molar density of states is
g(EF​)=π2kB2​3γ​≃5.3×1043 J−1mol−1≃14 eV−1 per formula unit.​
(30)
The stipulated two electrons per formula unit give n=2NA​/Vm​≃2.62×1027 m−3. For a single spin-degenerate parabolic band, the molar density of states is g(EF​)=3(2NA​)/(2EF​)=3NA​/EF​. Hence
EF​≃3.4×10−20 J≃0.21 eV,kF​=(3π2n)1/3≃4.3×109 m−1,m∗=2EF​ℏ2kF2​​≃3.2me​.​
(31)
These are model-dependent effective parameters inferred from the measured heat capacity and assumed carrier count, rather than an independent determination of a unique band structure.
Using the Debye prefactor stated in the question gives
ΘD​=(5β12π4NA​kB​​)1/3≃80 K.​
(32)
This prefactor counts three effective modes per formula unit. Consistently choosing the same count for the Debye wavevector gives kD​=(6π2NA​/Vm​)1/3≃4.3×109 m−1. The corresponding average sound speed is
vD​=ℏkD​kB​ΘD​​≃2.5×103 ms−1.​
(33)
There is a normalization choice here: a formula unit has 31 atoms. With the conventional full atomic-mode count, the molar Debye coefficient contains an additional factor 31, and both ΘD​ and kD​ increase by 311/3. That convention gives ΘD​≃251 K and kD​≃1.34×1010 m−1, while leaving the inferred sound speed unchanged. The mode count in the Debye model must be used consistently; mixing the printed temperature with an atomic-count wavevector gives an erroneous sound speed.
The upward curvature above about 3 K indicates low-energy vibrations missing from a pure acoustic T3 model. In this cage compound, the original analysis fits an additional localized mode with ℏΩ/kB​≃32 K, assigned to a rattling mode of a barium guest atom.[ref] At temperatures high compared with all relevant phonon energies, the full lattice approaches the Dulong-Petit law, Cmlattice​→3(31)R=93R≃773 Jmol−1K−1. The reduced three-mode model alone approaches 3R and omits the other branches. An approximately linear electronic term can still be added while the carriers remain degenerate, but cannot be extrapolated beyond their Fermi temperature.
