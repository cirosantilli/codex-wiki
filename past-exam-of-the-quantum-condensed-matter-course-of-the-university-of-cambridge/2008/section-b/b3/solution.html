Let α=ℏ2/(2m∗), so the initial conduction-electron kinetic energy is E1​=αk12​. In the normal momentum-conserving process with the final hole fixed at zero wavevector, the two final conduction electrons obey k1​=k2​+k3​. Their total kinetic energy satisfies
α(k22​+k32​)=2E1​​+2α​∣k2​−k3​∣2≥2E1​​.
(28)
Energy conservation for impact ionization is E1​=Eg​+α(k22​+k32​). Consequently E1​≥Eg​+E1​/2, giving
E1,min​=2Eg​.​
(29)
The bound is attained when k2​=k3​=k1​/2; opposite spins permit the two electrons to share that wavevector. Fixing the hole momentum is essential: a mobile final hole gives a different threshold. This is the threshold for impact ionization with a hole at zero momentum.
The two hot electrons subsequently redistribute energy by electron-electron collisions and transfer it to the lattice by electron-phonon scattering. Optical-phonon emission can provide large discrete losses while the electrons are hot; acoustic phonons dominate later small energy exchanges. Electron-electron collisions alone conserve the electron system's total energy and do not cool it into the lattice. The electron-hole pair eventually recombines radiatively or through nonradiative channels, such as defect-assisted recombination or Auger recombination. Recombination is distinct from the preceding intraband cooling.
