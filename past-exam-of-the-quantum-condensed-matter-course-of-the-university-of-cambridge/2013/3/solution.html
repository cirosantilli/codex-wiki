Dopant atoms and carrier type. Substitutional semiconductor doping places a small fraction of foreign atoms on host lattice sites. In a tetrahedrally bonded group-IV semiconductor, a group-V substitution such as phosphorus in silicon has an extra valence electron. A shallow donor level just below the conduction edge can release that electron, leaving a positively charged donor. In an n-type semiconductor, mobile electrons are the majority carriers and the chemical potential shifts toward the conduction band.
A group-III substitution such as boron in silicon has one fewer valence electron. An acceptor level just above the valence edge captures an electron from the valence states, leaving a mobile electron hole and a negatively charged acceptor. In a p-type semiconductor, electron holes are the majority carriers and the chemical potential shifts toward the valence band. The material can remain electrically neutral in both cases: mobile carrier electric charge is compensated by fixed ionized impurity electric charge. Dopant freeze-out, compensation and degeneracy qualify the simplest one-carrier-per-dopant picture.
Making the junction. Choose p material at x<0 and n material at x>0. Electrons initially diffuse from n to p, and electron holes from p to n, recombining near the interface. Negative ionized acceptors are exposed on the p side and positive ionized donors on the n side. They produce an electric field directed from n to p. The field opposes further majority-carrier diffusion until drift and diffusion balance and the equilibrium electrochemical potential is uniform.
The depletion regions are depleted of mobile majority carriers, not of the fixed dopant charges that create the space charge. At equilibrium the conduction and valence edges bend together, while the chemical potential is flat. The specified Δϕ0​ is a electric potential difference: the corresponding initial chemical-potential energy separation is eΔϕ0​. This convention is required by the quoted units and width formulas. Applied bias. Define positive V as forward bias, raising the p-side terminal potential relative to the n-side terminal. It lowers the remaining barrier from Δϕ0​ to Δϕ0​−V. Reverse bias has V<0 and raises that barrier. The same Poisson integration applies, with this replacement:
wn​(V)=eND​(NA​+ND​)2εs​NA​(Δϕ0​−V)​​,wp​(V)=eNA​(NA​+ND​)2εs​ND​(Δϕ0​−V)​​.​
(20)
Forward bias narrows the depleted region; reverse bias widens it. Under bias there need not be one equilibrium chemical potential across the device, so the equilibrium flat-level argument must not be reapplied unchanged. The barrier/width change is illustrated in the next figure.
Differential capacitance and electric charge sign. Put B=Δϕ0​−V>0 and Neff​=NA​ND​/(NA​+ND​). The signed p-side fixed electric charge per unit area is
Qp​=−eNA​wp​=−2εs​eNeff​B​.

CA​=dVdQp​​=2(NA​+ND​)(Δϕ0​−V)εs​eNA​ND​​​=wn​+wp​εs​​.​

The n-side electric charge is Qn​=−Qp​, so dQn​/dV=−CA​ instead. If Q denotes a positive space-charge magnitude, the positive capacitance is −dQ/dV for this forward-voltage convention. This resolves the sign implicit in the paper's positive dQ/dV. The total device capacitance is C=ACA​, where A is its cross-sectional area.

Cfd​=2dεs​A​≃5.31×10−13F=0.531pF.​
(25)
Thus the idealized piecewise sketch over the specified range is
C(V)=⎩⎨⎧​0.531pF,2A​0.5V−Vεs​eN​​,​−5.0≤V≤Vfd​,Vfd​<V<0.5V.​
(26)
It is continuous at full depletion, rises through 1.30pF at zero bias, and has a formal inverse-square-root divergence as V→0.5V from below.
There are two physical limits to this extension. Once all dopants are exposed, their fixed space charge no longer increases, but the terminal electrodes can still acquire incremental electric charge; differentiating only the fixed ionized electric charge would incorrectly predict zero capacitance. Near V=Δϕ0​, the depletion approximation itself fails as mobile carriers and forward injection become important, so the formal infinite endpoint is not a prediction of an actual infinite measured capacitance. The fully depleted plateau assumes ideal contacts and no breakdown, injection storage or extra series capacitance.
