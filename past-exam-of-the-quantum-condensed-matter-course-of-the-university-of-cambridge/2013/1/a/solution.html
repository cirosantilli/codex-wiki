Use the e−iωt convention, so passive photon absorption has positive imaginary permittivity. For a single Lorentz oscillator model resonance, write the photon energy as E=ℏω and
ϵr​(E)=ϵb​+E02​−E2−iΓES​,E0​=2eV,S>0,Γ>0.
(1)
Here S has units of energy squared, Γ=ℏγ is a damping energy, and ϵb​ is an approximately constant background. The corresponding real and imaginary parts are
ϵr′​(E)=ϵb​+(E02​−E2)2+Γ2E2S(E02​−E2)​,ϵr′′​(E)=(E02​−E2)2+Γ2E2SΓE​.
(2)
Thus the real part starts above the background, rises on the low-energy side of the resonance, drops rapidly through ϵr′​=ϵb​ at 2eV, and approaches the background from below at larger energy. The imaginary part starts at zero, has a positive photon absorption peak near 2eV, and decays above it. For weak damping its peak is close to the resonance, but not exactly fixed there for arbitrary damping. At asymptotically large energy the corrections scale as ϵr′​−ϵb​∼−S/E2 and ϵr′′​∼SΓ/E3.
The resonance position is fixed at 2 eV; peak height, width and background are not specified. The figure chooses an explicit finite damping and oscillator strength only to illustrate the shape over 0–5 eV. In particular, a negative real permittivity above resonance is possible for suitable strength and damping, but is not forced by the resonance energy alone. Setting damping exactly to zero would give a singular idealized response instead of a finite-width photon absorption peak.
