For equal-size molecules, let f be the mixing Helmholtz free energy per lattice site. Flory–Huggins solution theory reduces to the regular solution model:
kB​Tf​=ϕlnϕ+(1−ϕ)ln(1−ϕ)+χϕ(1−ϕ).​
(3)
The first two terms are minus the combinatorial entropy of mixing; the last is the excess cost of unlike contacts. A positive Flory interaction parameter penalizes such contacts. Additive terms linear in composition change the chosen pure-component reference but do not change stability.
A small composition fluctuation at fixed mean composition costs energy proportional to f′′. Thus local stability requires f′′>0, and the spinodal is
kB​Tf′′​=ϕ(1−ϕ)1​−2χ,χs​(ϕ)=2ϕ(1−ϕ)1​.​
(4)
Inside this curve, f′′<0 and arbitrarily small composition fluctuations grow. The minimum of χs​ is at ϕ=1/2, giving the critical value χc​=2. At the thermodynamic critical point, f(2)=f(3)=0 while f(4)>0.
For χ=0, f is strictly convex with its minimum at 1/2. At χ=2, the central quadratic term vanishes but a positive quartic term remains. At χ=2.5, there are two minima and a central maximum; the spinodal compositions are (1±1−2/χ​)/2≃0.2764,0.7236. The common-tangent construction gives coexisting compositions ϕb​≃0.1448 and 1−ϕb​, satisfying ln[ϕb​/(1−ϕb​)]+χ(1−2ϕb​)=0. Between a binodal endpoint and the spinodal, the homogeneous state is locally stable but metastable; global stability requires the convex envelope of f.
