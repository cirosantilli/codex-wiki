The force is determined by the total normal stress of the ionic liquid. The ions contribute osmotic pressure because confining an excess number density costs translational entropy. The osmotic stress in a symmetric electric double layer, relative to the bulk reservoir, is
Π=kB​T(n+​+n−​−2n0​)−2εE2​.

The second term is the normal electrostatic stress. The Poisson-Boltzmann equation makes this combination independent of position between planar surfaces. At the midpoint of identical equally charged surfaces, symmetry gives E=0, so
Πmid​=2n0​kB​T[coshkB​Tqψmid​​−1].​
(15)
Calculating only the repulsion of bare surface charges would omit both the screening ion cloud and its entropic stress. Evaluating the total stress at the midpoint packages their combined force in a particularly simple form.
For equal spheres, assume h≪a, κa≫1, weak surface potential and weak overlap, with prescribed ψ0​. Then ψmid​≃2ψ0​e−κh/2 gives Π≃2εκ2ψ02​e−κh. Integrating once gives the planar interaction energy per area, W=∫h∞​Π(s)ds≃2εκψ02​e−κh, and the Derjaguin approximation gives the repulsive sphere force. Combining it with the near-contact van der Waals attraction of spheres yields the DLVO theory approximation
F(h)≃2πεaκψ02​e−κh−12h2AH​a​.​
(16)
Positive force is repulsive. The exponential term can produce a repulsive barrier in the corresponding pair energy; the short-range attraction leads to a primary minimum, while a shallower secondary minimum may occur farther out. Adding salt shortens the repulsion and can destroy the barrier, allowing particle aggregation. Molecular contact requires a repulsive-core correction, and changing the charge-regulation boundary condition changes the electrostatic prefactor.
