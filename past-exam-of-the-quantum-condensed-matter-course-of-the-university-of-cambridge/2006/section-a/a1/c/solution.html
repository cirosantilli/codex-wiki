The fluctuation-dissipation theorem relates spontaneous fluctuations in thermal equilibrium to the dissipative part of the linear response to a weak conjugate force. For an overdamped trapped coordinate, the Langevin equation is
γx˙+Kx=f(t)+ξ(t),⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=2γkB​Tδ(t−t′).
(1)
The fluctuation-dissipation relation for a Langevin particle fixes the noise strength from the same drag coefficient γ that dissipates motion. With a drive proportional to e−iωt, the linear response function is χ(ω)=1/(K−iγω). The two-sided angular-frequency power spectral density then obeys
Sxx​(ω)=K2+γ2ω22γkB​T​=ω2kB​T​Imχ(ω).​
(2)
Its integral ∫Sxx​dω/(2π)=kB​T/K agrees with the equipartition theorem. Without a restoring force, the same noise-drag relation gives the diffusion-mobility relation D=kB​T/γ.
In thermal calibration of an optical trap, record a bead's spontaneous displacement fluctuations and compare their spectrum with its response to a weak calibrated oscillatory force. The overdamped harmonic-trap power spectrum and the dissipative response must be consistent with the same temperature and drag. The equilibrium and weak-drive conditions are essential: an actively driven bath need not obey this equilibrium relation.
