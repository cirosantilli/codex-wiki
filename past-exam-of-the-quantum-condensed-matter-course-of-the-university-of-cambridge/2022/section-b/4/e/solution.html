An unbound conduction electron has no local restoring force, so set ω0​=0 while retaining inertia and momentum relaxation time τ=1/γ. This is the Drude model:
ϵr​(ω)=1−ω(ω+iγ)ωp2​​.​
(18)
Separating the response gives
ϵr′​=1−ω2+γ2ωp2​​,ϵr′′​=ω(ω2+γ2)ωp2​γ​.​
(19)
For a good metal, γ≪ωp​. The real part is strongly negative at low frequency, crosses zero at ωp2​−γ2​≃ωp​, and approaches one from below. The imaginary part diverges as ωp2​/(γω) at low frequency, reflecting the finite dc electrical conductivity σ0​=ϵ0​ωp2​/γ, then decays as ω−3. The low-frequency imaginary response describes conduction loss, not a finite-frequency bound-electron absorption line. Core-electron and interband responses can add a background in a real metal.
