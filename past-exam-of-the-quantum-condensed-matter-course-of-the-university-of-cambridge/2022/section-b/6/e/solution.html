A current-carrying sample in a transverse magnetic field develops a transverse electric field because the Lorentz force deflects its charge carriers. In steady state this Hall voltage balances the transverse deflection. With Hall coefficient RH​=Ey​/(jx​Bz​), one isotropic electron band gives RH​=−1/(ne) and one hole band gives RH​=+1/(pe).
When electrons and electron holes both conduct, the weak-field two-carrier result is
RH​=e(pμh​+nμe​)2pμh2​−nμe2​​,σ=e(nμe​+pμh​).​
(49)
The two-carrier Hall coefficient weights each species by mobility squared. Its sign changes when
pμh2​=nμe2​.​
(50)
Temperature changes dopant ionization, intrinsic electron-hole generation, and the two charge carrier mobilities, so it can move the system across this condition. For example, a p-type semiconductor can be hole-dominated in its extrinsic regime but become negative in its intrinsic regime if n≃p and μe​>μh​. A sign change is possible, not mandatory: in many n-type semiconductors electrons dominate at both temperatures. The density majority alone does not determine the Hall sign in a two-carrier material.
