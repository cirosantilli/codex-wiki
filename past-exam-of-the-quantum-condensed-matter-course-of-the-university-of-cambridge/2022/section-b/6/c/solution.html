Ordinary doped semiconductor devices need a regime where their donor impurities or acceptor impurities supply mobile charge carriers while intrinsic thermal excitation remains sufficiently small.
At low temperature, donor impurities or acceptor impurities may no longer be ionized. Dopant freeze-out reduces the carrier density, raises electrical resistance and changes the intended carrier injection and p-n junction response. At high temperature the intrinsic carrier concentration grows as

The usual operating window lies between freeze-out and intrinsic conduction, with mobility and thermal management placing further practical limits. This is a statement about conventional doped-device operation, not a fundamental prohibition on engineered cryogenic or wide-bandgap devices.
