An ideal diffusion-dominated p-n junction obeys the Shockley diode equation
I(V,T)=Is​(T)[eeV/(ηkB​T)−1],​
(46)
with diode ideality factor η=1 in the simplest model. Forward bias gives exponential current growth; reverse bias gives a nearly constant −Is​ until diode reverse breakdown, outside this ideal expression.
For a long abrupt p-n junction in low carrier injection, the diode saturation current is
Is​=eAni2​(Lp​ND​Dp​​+Ln​NA​Dn​​)=C(T)ni2​.​
(47)
Here A is area, ND​,NA​ are ionized dopant densities, and D,L are minority-carrier diffusion coefficients and diffusion lengths in the appropriate regions. At each temperature, extract Is​ either from the reverse-saturation plateau below breakdown or from a forward exponential fit using its measured diode ideality factor.
Extracting a semiconductor band gap from diode saturation current requires correcting for the transport prefactor:
logC(T)T3Is​(T)​=constant−kB​TEg​​.​
(48)
A plot against 1/T has slope −Eg​/kB​ when the gap and effective masses are nearly temperature-independent. Alternatively divide the measured Is​ by a known C(T) to recover the full ni2​(T). The prefactor cannot generally be assumed constant over an arbitrarily broad range. Junction-generation or recombination-dominated current can scale as ni​ instead, so the diffusion regime should be identified from the current-voltage behaviour.
