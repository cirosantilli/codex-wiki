Noninteracting spectral line. Treat k as a wavevector, so physical crystal momentum is ℏk. Write Ωk​=εk​/ℏ; an energy zero at the chemical potential can be chosen throughout. The temporal factor of a noninteracting state is e−iΩk​t. With the Fourier transform convention ψ​(ω)=∫dteiωtψ(t),
ψ​k​(ω)=2πψk​(0)δ(ω−Ωk​).
(31)
Thus its normalized frequency spectral function is
A0​(k,ω)=δ(ω−Ωk​).​
(32)
One should not square the delta distribution as if it were an ordinary finite-time transition amplitude. More precisely, the fermionic retarded Green function is G0R​(k,t)=−iΘ(t)e−iΩk​t. Its Fourier transform is (ω−Ωk​+i0+)−1, whose negative imaginary part divided by π is the same delta peak.
Interactions and lifetime. Interactions change the dispersion through the real part of the self-energy and permit decay into other excitations through its imaginary part. In the simplest quasiparticle-pole model, exponential decay of the retarded amplitude gives
GR(k,t)=−iΘ(t)e−iΩk∗​t−Γk​t,Γk​>0.
(33)
Performing the causal integral,
GR(k,ω)=−i∫0∞​e[i(ω−Ωk∗​)−Γk​]tdt=ω−Ωk∗​+iΓk​1​.
(34)
The corresponding Lorentzian quasiparticle spectral peak is
A(k,ω)=−π1​ImGR=π1​(ω−εk∗​/ℏ)2+Γk2​Γk​​.​
(35)
Its total frequency integral is one. The exponential amplitude lifetime is 1/Γk​; the probability lifetime is 1/(2Γk​). Describing Γk​ as a scattering rate therefore requires the lifetime convention to be specified. The energy half-width is ℏΓk​.
A general interacting electron is more accurately described by GR=Zk​/(ω−Ωk∗​+iΓk​)+GincR​ near the pole. The coherent peak then integrates to the quasiparticle residue Zk​ and an incoherent continuum carries the remaining weight. In an energy-unit self-energy convention,
GR=ω−εk0​/ℏ−ΣR(k,ω)/ℏ1​,εk∗​=εk0​+ReΣR(k,εk∗​/ℏ).
(36)
Linearizing the real denominator gives Z=[1−ℏ−1∂ω​ReΣR]−1 and Γ=−ZImΣR/ℏ at the pole. The requested unit-weight Lorentzian is the normalized quasiparticle model, not the most general exact interacting spectrum.
Peak position and width. At fixed k, put γ=γ(k)>0 and Ω=ℏk2/(2m∗). Differentiation gives
∂ω∂A​=−π[(ω−Ω)2+γ2]22γ(ω−Ω)​.
(37)
It is positive for ω<Ω and negative for ω>Ω, so the only maximum is at Ω, with height 1/(πγ). Setting A to half this height gives (ω−Ω)2=γ2. Therefore
ωmax​=2m∗ℏk2​,HWHMω​=γ(k),FWHMω​=2γ(k).​
(38)
In energy units the maximum is ℏ2k2/(2m∗) and the half-width is ℏγ(k). Frequency dependence of the self-energy would require reconsidering both the shape and peak position.
Fermi gas and Fermi liquid. A noninteracting Fermi gas has a delta-function spectral line at its bare dispersion for every momentum. A normal three-dimensional Fermi liquid has a shifted coherent peak plus incoherent spectral weight; close to the Fermi surface its width scales as (ε−μ)2+(πkB​T)2. At zero temperature the peak becomes arbitrarily sharp at the Fermi surface, while it broadens farther away. Spectral functions include addition and removal weight; occupied-only photoemission contains an extra Fermi factor.
Photoemission measurement. A photon of known energy hν ejects an electron. Measure its kinetic energy Ekin​ and emission direction. For binding energy EB​≥0 below the Fermi level and the appropriate calibrated work function Φ, conservation of energy gives
Ekin​=hν−Φ−EB​.​
(39)
Conservation of momentum parallel to a periodic surface gives
ki,∥​+qγ,∥​=kout,∥​+G∥​,kout,∥​=ℏ2m0​Ekin​​​sinθ,
(40)
where G∥​ is a surface reciprocal-lattice vector. At suitable photon energies the photon momentum can be neglected; at higher energies it must be retained. The surface breaks perpendicular translation symmetry, so the perpendicular crystal momentum generally needs a final-state model. A two-dimensional band can be reconstructed directly from the parallel components.
Within the sudden approximation and electric-dipole approximation, photoemission intensity and spectral function are related by
I(k,E)∝∣M(k,hν,polarization)∣2f(E)AE​(k,E),AE​(k,E)=ℏ1​A(k,E/ℏ).
(41)
Here E is measured relative to the chemical potential. Instrumental resolution convolves the product and inelastic scattering adds background. Thus ARPES maps the occupied electron removal spectral function, weighted by a matrix element; it does not measure the full unoccupied spectrum or an unweighted spectral function automatically. Bright ridges give dispersions, and intrinsic widths give decay information after instrumental effects are removed.
Reading the supplied image. Its bright ridges form a graphene-like cone. The crossing near ky​=0 is the Dirac point, not in general the Fermi level. In the paper's plotted energy coordinates its energy is roughly ED​∼1.4–1.5eV. A velocity follows from a branch slope independently of the energy zero:
v=ℏ1​​dky​dE​​,1eVA˚/ℏ=1.519×105ms−1.
(42)
For example, the lower branch moves by about 0.3eV between ∣ky​∣∼0.03 and 0.11A˚−1. This gives dE/dk∼3.8eVA˚ and
vF​∼6×105ms−1as a rough linear-cone estimate.​
(43)
Reading other points changes the estimate by tens of percent. The original exam image has unequal energy increments on nearly equally spaced labelled ticks, so it does not justify a precise fitted velocity. The value is an estimate of the cone slope extrapolated to the Fermi crossing, rather than a measured derivative exactly at a visibly identified Fermi cutoff.
To determine a Fermi energy, first calibrate the Fermi edge, then subtract the chosen reference energy. Neither a Fermi-level marker nor an explicit energy-zero convention is supplied in this crop. If its upper termination near 1.9eV is intended to represent the Fermi cutoff, the conditional graph answer is EF​≃1.9eV on that axis, or EF​−ED​∼0.4eV relative to the Dirac crossing. Identifying the crossing itself as the Fermi energy would incorrectly assume undoped graphene.
The research article cited by the exam identifies this as linearly polarized graphene ARPES and independently states EF​−ED​≃0.4eV for the substrate-doped sample. That resolves the relative energy when this external calibration is admitted.[ref] The crop alone does not uniquely fix an absolute Fermi energy; the source-supported relative value and the conditional 1.9eV axis reading should be distinguished.
