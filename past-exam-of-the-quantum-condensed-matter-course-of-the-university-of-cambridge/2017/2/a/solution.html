Dielectric screening is the rearrangement of a medium's charges in response to an applied charge. In a metal, mobile electrons accumulate near a positive perturbation and reduce its long-range potential. Thomas-Fermi screening treats the electron gas locally as an equilibrium gas whose density responds to the local electrostatic potential, ignoring gradients of the density on the microscopic wavelength scale.
Take e>0, background permittivity εb​, and electrostatic potential ϕ. An electron's potential energy is −eϕ, so the local kinetic Fermi energy becomes μ+eϕ. For a three-dimensional spin-degenerate parabolic band at zero temperature,
n(ϕ)=3π21​[ℏ22m(μ+eϕ)​]3/2,δn≃eg(EF​)ϕ,
(11)
where g(EF​)=dn/dμ is the spin-summed density of states per volume per energy. The induced charge is ρind​=−eδn=−e2g(EF​)ϕ. Combining it with Poisson equation gives
(∇2−kTF2​)ϕ=−εb​ρext​​,kTF2​=εb​e2g(EF​)​.​
(12)
For a point external charge Q, spherical symmetry and decay at infinity select
ϕ(r)=4πεb​rQ​e−r/λTF​,λTF​=e2g(EF​)εb​​​.​
(13)
Thus the bare 1/r potential is cut off exponentially. Fourier transformation makes the same result transparent: ϕ(q)=ρext​(q)/[εb​(q2+kTF2​)], so the static longitudinal dielectric factor is 1+kTF2​/q2 relative to the background. For a free electron band, g(EF​)=3n/(2EF​) and λTF2​=2εb​EF​/(3ne2).
The local linear approximation requires a weak perturbation and slowly varying fields on the relevant microscopic scale. It omits nonlocal Friedel oscillations and atom-scale structure; a nonlinear local-density treatment is needed near a sufficiently strong charge. Finite temperature replaces g(EF​) by the appropriate compressibility derivative ∂n/∂μ. The nondegenerate limit gives classical screening rather than the degenerate Thomas-Fermi length. A larger density of states means that a smaller potential can move more carriers, hence stronger screening.
