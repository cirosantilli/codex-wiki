Use two plane waves, ∣k⟩ and ∣k−G⟩, as a basis near their Bragg degeneracy. The potential has Fourier coefficients V±G​; its zero Fourier coefficient vanishes. All other plane waves are off resonance and are neglected in the leading nearly-free electron model. With εk​=ℏ2k2/(2me​), the Schrödinger equation reduces to the Hermitian matrix problem
(εk​VG∗​​VG​εk−G​​)(c1​c2​​)=E(c1​c2​​).
(19)
Setting the characteristic polynomial equal to zero gives
(E−εk​)(E−εk−G​)−∣VG​∣2=0.
(20)
Solving this quadratic equation gives
E±​=2εk​+εk−G​​±(2εk​−εk−G​​)2+∣VG​∣2​.
(21)
For the real cosine potential its coefficient VG​ is real; the absolute-value form also allows an arbitrary choice of origin and corresponding Fourier phase.
At the Brillouin-zone boundary, k⋅G=G2/2, the free energies are equal. The two standing waves have different probability densities relative to the lattice potential and are split by
ΔE=2∣VG​∣.​
(22)
Repeating this avoided crossing at the reciprocal lattice boundaries joins smooth branches into energy bands. In one dimension a nonzero Fourier coefficient opens a gap at the corresponding crossing. In higher dimensions a direct gap at one wavevector need not give a common forbidden energy interval at every wavevector: different bands may still overlap in energy. The present two-dimensional example remains metallic even though the crossings are locally gapped.
For the rectangular lattice, choose real-space primitive vectors ax and by​. The reciprocal lattice vectors are
Gx​=a2π​x,Gy​=b2π​y​.
(23)
Define the half-widths of the first Brillouin zone by K=π/a and L=π/b. Their values and the zone are
K=2.41661nm−1,L=3.92699nm−1,−K≤kx​≤K,−L≤ky​≤L.​
(24)
A primitive reciprocal cell can be drawn with a corner at the origin; its centred translate is the first Brillouin zone here. Its area is 4π2/(ab)=37.9600nm−2.
Including both spin states, counting the occupied free-electron states per unit area gives
n2D​=2(2π)2πkF2​​=2πkF2​​=ab2​.
(25)
Consequently
kF​=ab4π​​=3.47607nm−1,EF(0)​=2me​ℏ2kF2​​=0.46036eV.​
(26)
The occupied disk has precisely the area of the first Brillouin zone, but their shapes differ: K<kF​<L. It protrudes through both vertical zone boundaries and does not reach the horizontal ones. Thus two electrons per cell do not imply a filled first band in the absence of a separating band gap.
Fold the protruding caps by ±Gx​. The original circle and the translated circle intersect at kx​=K, ky​=±kF2​−K2​. The potential mixes these states and replaces each crossing by an avoided crossing. The lower branch reconnects into two open sheets running across successive zones in the kx​ direction; the upper branch forms a closed lens-shaped pocket about a vertical zone boundary. In a reduced-zone sketch its two halves sit at opposite edges, but periodic identification makes them one closed sheet. This is rectangular-lattice Fermi-surface reconstruction.
The largest pocket in this weak-potential construction is the lens-shaped intersection of two disks enclosed by the intersecting free circles. Each has radius kF​ and their centres are separated by 2K. If θ=arccos(K/kF​), two sectors minus their two triangles give the lens bound on a reconstructed Fermi pocket:
Amax​=2kF2​θ−2KkF2​−K2​,Amax​=7.30680nm−2.​
(27)
Opening a gap raises the upper branch at fixed chemical potential and shrinks its pocket. The reported quantum oscillation area satisfies
0<Ak​=5.74nm−2<Amax​,Amax​Ak​​=0.786.​
(28)
It is therefore consistent with this closed sheet. Onsager quantization gives F=ℏAk​/(2πe)≃601T. An orbit on this closed contour can return to itself under the Lorentz force and occupy quantized Landau levels. The open sheets do not enclose a finite orbit area for a perpendicular field in this two-dimensional model. The observation is consistent with the reconstructed pocket, without uniquely determining a completely arbitrary band structure.
For a quantitative rough coupling estimate, use the supplied fixed-chemical-potential approximation and retain the two waves coupled by Gx​. Write
C=2me​ℏ2​=0.0380998eVnm2,v=∣VG​∣,kx​=K+q.
(29)
Their upper eigenvalue is
E+​(q,ky​)=C(K2+q2+ky2​)+4C2K2q2+v2​.
(30)
Setting it equal to CkF2​ gives the pocket boundary
ky2​=kF2​−K2−q2−4K2q2+(v/C)2​.
(31)
Its intersection with ky​=0 occurs at q=±qm​, where
qm2​=kF2​+K2−4kF2​K2+(v/C)2​.
(32)
The two-wave estimate of a Bragg-gap coupling from a pocket area therefore uses
A(v)=4∫0qm​​kF2​−K2−q2−4K2q2+(v/C)2​​dq.
(33)
At v=0 this reproduces the lens bound on a reconstructed Fermi pocket; the pocket disappears at v=C(kF2​−K2)=0.23786eV. Numerical integration gives A(0.075eV)=6.22nm−2 and A(0.100eV)=5.54nm−2. Matching the measured area gives v≃0.0931eV, so the justified rough result is
∣VG​∣∼0.1eV(ΔE∼0.2eV).​
(34)
This estimate neglects remote plane-wave admixtures and other Fourier coefficients. It also holds the chemical potential fixed as requested; an exact fixed-density calculation would determine it self-consistently after reconstruction. The numerical root is a way to implement the approximate model, not a measurement of VG​ to four significant figures.
