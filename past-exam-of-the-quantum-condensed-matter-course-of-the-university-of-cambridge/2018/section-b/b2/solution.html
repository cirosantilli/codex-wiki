Equilibrium junction. Put the p-type side on the left and n-type side on the right. When they are joined, electrons diffuse from n to p and electron holes diffuse from p to n. This leaves negative ionized acceptors on the p-side and positive ionized donors on the n-side of the depletion region. The resulting built-in electric field points from n to p, opposing further diffusion. At equilibrium there is one constant Fermi level, and drift and diffusion currents cancel for each carrier species.

Light emission. Forward bias lowers the barrier and injects minority carriers across the p-n junction. In a light-emitting diode, electron-hole recombination converts some of their energy into photons. A direct band gap permits efficient radiative transitions with nearly conserved crystal momentum; nonradiative channels compete. Near-edge emission has ℏω≃Eg​ and λ≃hc/Eg​. Material composition and confinement select the emission colour. Reverse-bias saturation is not the ordinary LED operating mode.
Reverse current. Take V=VR​>0 and IR​>0 to mean reverse voltage and reverse current magnitudes. The Shockley diode equation in this convention is
IR​=IS​(1−e−eVR​/(kB​T)).
(13)
The bias-independent IS​ term is the supply of thermally excited minority carriers from the neutral regions, swept across the junction by its electric field. The opposing injection current is reduced by the extra barrier energy eVR​ and is IS​e−eVR​/(kB​T). Their equality gives zero net current at zero bias; for eVR​≫kB​T, the reverse current approaches IS​ until leakage or breakdown invalidates this ideal law. The collecting field need not create the minority carriers; their thermal supply limits the diffusion-dominated current.
For an ideal long junction of area A, steady diffusion with recombination gives the diode minority-carrier diffusion current
IS​=eA(Ln​NA​Dn​ni2​​+Lp​ND​Dp​ni2​​),Ln​=Dn​τn​​,Lp​=Dp​τp​​.​
(14)
It depends on doping, geometry, carrier diffusion coefficients and lifetimes, and especially the intrinsic carrier concentration. Depletion-region generation current and surface leakage can add nonideal contributions to the measured reverse current.
Band-gap estimate from the data. Read a nearly flat region well above the small-bias knee and below the high-bias upturn, rather than estimating IS​ in the breakdown region. For a diffusion-dominated junction with nearly temperature-independent gap and effective masses,
ni2​=NC​NV​e−Eg​/(kB​T),NC​NV​∝T3,

The colder curve is a genuine limitation of the supplied data: the 0 to −45∘C pair gives only about 0.32eV with a constant prefactor, and the endpoint pair gives about 0.42eV. Thus all three cannot support one precise diffusion-only activation slope. A depletion-region generation current, approximately proportional to ni​ rather than ni2​, can have a roughly half-gap activation energy; other leakage and temperature-dependent transport can also alter the slope. The PDF alone does not identify their relative contributions. A reliable precision gap measurement would use more temperatures and verify the transport regime instead of forcing one fit through these three readings.
