Two atomic orbitals. In the linear combination of atomic orbitals approximation, use ∣ψ⟩=cA​∣A⟩+cB​∣B⟩, where the two basis functions are localized atomic orbitals. Projecting H∣ψ⟩=E∣ψ⟩ onto each orbital gives the generalized eigenvalue problem
(HAA​HBA​​HAB​HBB​​)(cA​cB​​)=E(1S∗​S1​)(cA​cB​​),det(H−ES)=0.​
(20)
Here the scalar overlap in the matrix is S=⟨A∣B⟩, while Hab​=⟨a∣H∣b⟩. Normalization requires c†Sc=1, with S now denoting the full overlap matrix. For equivalent real orbitals with diagonal energy Ea​, real hopping t, and real overlap s, the eigenstates and energies are
∣ψ±​⟩=2(1±s)​∣A⟩±∣B⟩​,E±​=1±sEa​±t​.
(21)
For the usual negative hopping convention, the symmetric combination is the lower bonding orbital and the antisymmetric combination is the higher antibonding orbital. In an orthonormal approximation, s=0 and the result reduces to a two-by-two Hermitian matrix eigenproblem.
Hybridized bands. At each k, take the two Bloch basis states to be orthonormal, as implicit in the stated band model, and write ϵk​=ℏ2k2/(2m). The Hamiltonian is
H(k)=(ϵk​V∗​VEd​​).
(22)
For ∣ψk​⟩=ak​∣ϕk​⟩+bk​∣χk​⟩, the eigenvalue condition is
(ϵk​−E)a+Vb=0,V∗a+(Ed​−E)b=0,
(23)
so (E−ϵk​)(E−Ed​)−∣V∣2=0. Thus band hybridization gives
E±​(k)=2ϵk​+Ed​​±4(ϵk​−Ed​)2​+∣V∣2​.​
(24)
For nonzero V, a convenient explicit normalized pair of eigenstates is
∣ψk,±​⟩=∣V∣2+(E±​−ϵk​)2​V∣ϕk​⟩+(E±​−ϵk​)∣χk​⟩​.​
(25)
A phase convention can make V real, but the eigenvalues depend only on its modulus. These are exact within the truncated two-band model, and approximate as a description of a material.
Dispersions and limits. For V=0, the parabola crosses the flat band at
k0​=2mEd​​/ℏ.
(26)
For V=0 the crossing is avoided, with a direct hybridization gap 2∣V∣ at k0​. The lower and upper branches exchange orbital character through this avoided crossing. At the origin their energies are
E±​(0)=2Ed​±Ed2​+4∣V∣2​​,
(27)
so for ∣V∣≪Ed​ the small-k expansions are
E−​(k)=−Ed​∣V∣2​+(1−Ed2​∣V∣2​)ϵk​+O(∣V∣4/Ed3​,∣V∣2ϵk2​/Ed3​),
(28)
E+​(k)=Ed​+Ed​∣V∣2​+Ed2​∣V∣2​ϵk​+O(∣V∣4/Ed3​,∣V∣2ϵk2​/Ed3​).
(29)
At large k, where ϵk​−Ed​≫∣V∣,
E−​(k)=Ed​−ϵk​−Ed​∣V∣2​+O((ϵk​−Ed​)3∣V∣4​),E+​(k)=ϵk​+ϵk​−Ed​∣V∣2​+O((ϵk​−Ed​)3∣V∣4​).​
(30)
The lower branch approaches Ed​ from below and becomes flat; the upper branch approaches the free-electron parabola from above. At zero coupling the unsorted orbital bands remain exactly flat and parabolic. Sorting them by energy instead gives E−​=min(ϵk​,Ed​) and E+​=max(ϵk​,Ed​), which cross at k0​ without mixing.
Fermi wavevector and velocity. A partially occupied perfectly flat band has chemical potential Ed​. Thus before mixing, the occupied broad band has radius k0​ defined above. For spin degeneracy two, its particle density is n1​=k03​/(3π2); equal population of the flat sector gives total density n=2n1​.
For any nonzero mixing, E−​(k)<Ed​ at finite k, while E+​(k)>Ed​. Moreover E−​(k) increases monotonically. At zero temperature and this fixed density, a single occupied sphere in the lower branch therefore accommodates all particles, and the upper branch is empty. Fermi-volume conservation in two hybridized electron bands gives
3π2kF3​​=3π22k03​​,kF​=21/3k0​,ΔkF​=(21/3−1)k0​≃0.260k0​.​
(31)
The new radius is about 26% larger than the old broad-band radius. The strictly flat uncoupled band has no uniquely determined Fermi radius of its own; its prescribed population is what enters the count.
The radial group velocity is v−​(k)=ℏ−1dE−​/dk. Differentiating the exact dispersion gives
dϵk​dE−​​=21​[1−(ϵk​−Ed​)2+4∣V∣2​ϵk​−Ed​​].
(32)
At kF​, put δ=22/3−1, so ϵkF​​−Ed​=δEd​. Relative to the original broad-band Fermi velocity vF(1)​=ℏk0​/m,
vF(1)​vF​​=221/3​[1−δ2+4∣V∣2/Ed2​​δ​].
(33)
Expanding for ∣V∣≪Ed​ gives the flat-band hybridization Fermi-velocity suppression
vF(1)​vF​​=(22/3−1)221/3​Ed2​∣V∣2​+O(Ed4​∣V∣4​).​
(34)
The coefficient is about 3.65. The enlarged occupied volume coexists with a small velocity because the new Fermi surface lies on the almost-flat part of the lower branch. This result assumes fixed particle density, sufficient available states before any Brillouin zone boundary, and temperature low enough to resolve energy differences of order ∣V∣2/Ed​. The limit V→0 at a fixed nonzero temperature need not give this sharp redistribution. Giving the narrow band a finite dispersion can also leave both pockets occupied, so the result is specific to the ideal model.
