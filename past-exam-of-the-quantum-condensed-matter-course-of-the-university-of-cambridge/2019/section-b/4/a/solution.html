In the nearly-free electron model, a weak periodic potential has Fourier components VG​ that couple plane waves at k and k−G. Far from a degeneracy their admixture gives small perturbative energy shifts. At a Bragg plane, however,
ϵk​=ϵk−G​,
(8)
so degenerate perturbation theory is required. In one dimension the first such crossing is at k=±G/2, the boundary of the first Brillouin zone.
Within the degenerate pair the eigenvalues are ϵG/2​±∣VG​∣. Thus
energy gap at the Bragg crossing=2∣VG​∣.​
(9)
The eigenstates are standing-wave combinations of counterpropagating waves. Their charge densities sit differently relative to the ionic potential, giving different potential energies and removing the free-electron degeneracy. The resulting avoided crossing produces continuous allowed energy bands separated by a forbidden interval. A Fourier component or symmetry-allowed coupling is needed: geometry alone does not require every possible gap to be nonzero.
