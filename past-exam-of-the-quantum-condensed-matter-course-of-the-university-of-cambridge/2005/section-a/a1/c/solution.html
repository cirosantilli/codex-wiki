The storage modulus G′ measures the elastic component; the loss modulus G′′ measures dissipation. The mean power per volume is ωγ02​G′′/2, and the energy dissipated per cycle is πγ02​G′′, fixing the sign convention.
A Hookean solid has G∗=G0​, while a Newtonian fluid has G∗=iωη. The Maxwell model interpolates with G∗=G0​iωτR​/(1+iωτR​), giving G′=G0​(ωτR​)2/[1+(ωτR​)2] and G′′=G0​ωτR​/[1+(ωτR​)2]. It is fluid-like at low frequency and elastic on times short compared with relaxation time τR​.
A rheometer imposes small-amplitude oscillatory shear and resolves the torque amplitude and phase relative to the imposed motion. The torsional measurement of a complex shear modulus makes the conversion from torque and twist to G∗ explicit. A frequency sweep locates relaxation scales; keeping the strain small tests the linear-response assumption.
