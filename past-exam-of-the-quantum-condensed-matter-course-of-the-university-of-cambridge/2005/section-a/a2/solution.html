For a dilute suspended sphere in Stokes flow, the translational friction coefficient is fh​=6πηR, with dynamic viscosity η and hydrodynamic radius R. To derive the diffusion-friction relation, put particles in a weak conservative force F=−U′. Their drift velocity is F/fh​, while the diffusive flux is −Ddc/dx. At equilibrium the Boltzmann distribution gives c′=−cU′/(kB​T)=cF/(kB​T). Zero total flux therefore gives cF/fh​−DcF/(kB​T)=0, hence the Stokes–Einstein relation
D=fh​kB​T​=6πηRkB​T​.​
(4)
Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, T absolute temperature and D the translational diffusion coefficient. The sphere, no-slip continuum-fluid and dilute thermal-equilibrium assumptions enter through the drag law and flux balance.
At T=293.15K and the supplied viscosity, the predicted product is DR=2.1472×10−19m3s−1. The derived predictions and radii inferred from the measured diffusion coefficients are:

The ribonuclease diffusion coefficient is about 16% below the spherical prediction, corresponding to a larger frictional radius than the stated radius. Hydration, shape, a different operational radius and coarse measurement precision can matter; molar mass is not an independent parameter in the spherical diffusion law. A compact constant-density globule would have R∝M1/3, whereas collagen's large frictional radius relative to its mass is consistent with an extended nonspherical molecule. The inferred Stokes radius is a friction-equivalent radius, not automatically a geometric half-length.
In analytical ultracentrifugation, scan the radial protein concentration while a rotor spins at known angular speed ω. Optical absorbance or refractive-index interference records a moving sedimentation boundary and its diffusive broadening.[ref] With solvent density ρs​, partial specific volume vˉ and molar mass M in kgmol−1, the buoyant mass is mb​=M(1−vˉρs​)/NA​. Force balance gives fh​v=mb​ω2r, so the sedimentation coefficient is s=v/(ω2r)=mb​/fh​. A boundary trajectory gives s=ω−2dlnr/dt; diffusion gives fh​=kB​T/D.
Combining them gives the molar mass from sedimentation and diffusion:
M=D(1−vˉρs​)Rgas​Ts​,Rh​=6πηDkB​T​.​
(5)
Known mass and buoyancy can instead determine friction from s alone, and comparison with a compact sphere tests shape or hydration. In sedimentation equilibrium, the stationary radial profile obeys dlnc/d(r2)=mb​ω2/(2kB​T), providing buoyant mass without a friction model. This is a sedimentation-diffusion balance in a uniform solvent, rather than density-gradient banding at an isopycnic point.
