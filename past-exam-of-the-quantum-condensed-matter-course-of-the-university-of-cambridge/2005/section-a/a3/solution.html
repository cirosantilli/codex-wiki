An ideal chain can be treated as a random walk when effective segment orientations are independent and isotropic, segment length is fixed, and interactions such as long-range excluded volume are absent or effectively screened. A large number of segments is needed for the Gaussian continuum approximation. Write R=∑j=1N​aj​. Each Cartesian component has mean zero and variance Na2/3, so the central limit theorem gives the Gaussian limit of the end-to-end distribution of a freely jointed chain
p(R,N)≃(32πNa2​)−3/2exp[−2Na23R2​].​
(6)
Equivalently, one step has characteristic function sin(ka)/(ka); taking its Nth power and expanding its logarithm gives e−Na2k2/6, whose inverse Fourier transform is this density. It is a density per vector volume d3R. The scalar end-to-end-distance density is 4πR2p(R,N). Thus the printed expression must be interpreted as a vector density; it is also a large-N approximation, not the exact finite-chain distribution near R=Na.
