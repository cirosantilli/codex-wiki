In the Bloch wave sum, N is the number of sites, Rj​=ja the equilibrium position of site j, ϕ(r−Rj​) a localized atomic orbital there, and k the crystal wavevector. The phase factor gives the translation character of the Bloch state. The factor 1/N​ normalizes it when the site orbitals are treated as orthonormal; appreciable intersite overlap requires an overlap-dependent normalization.
For a tight-binding model, choose localized orbitals and calculate the on-site and intersite Hamiltonian matrix elements. Form Bloch sums for each orbital type and solve H(k)c=E(k)S(k)c at each k, where S(k) is the orbital-overlap matrix. In the orthogonal tight-binding approximation, S=I. The eigenvalues give the energy bands and the eigenvectors their orbital composition; the nonorthogonal nearest-neighbour tight-binding band illustrates how retained overlap modifies this procedure.
For one orthonormal s orbital per site, keep on-site energy ϵs​ and real nearest-neighbor hopping integral t=⟨ϕj​∣H∣ϕj+1​⟩. The two neighbors contribute phases e±ika, giving Es​(k)=ϵs​+2tcoska. The band-curvature definition 1/m∗=E′′(k)/ℏ2 gives
m∗(0)=−2ta2ℏ2​,m∗(±π/a)=2ta2ℏ2​,W=4∣t∣.​
(12)
With the usual bonding convention t<0 for these s orbitals, the center is a minimum with positive electron effective mass and the edge a maximum with negative electron effective mass. Hole masses near a filled-band maximum have the opposite sign.
For p orbitals aligned along the chain, the orbital phase and the sign of one-dimensional hopping reverse the usual center-versus-edge ordering. Choose every px​ orbital with its positive lobe on the right. Equal neighboring Bloch coefficients then bring opposite-sign lobes together between sites, giving an antibonding state at k=0. Alternating coefficients at k=π/a make those facing lobes have the same sign, producing the lower bonding state. Thus a nearest-neighbor model has Ep​(k)=ϵp​+2tσ​coska with the usual tσ​>0, maximum at zero and minima at the zone edges. This uses a fixed common orbital-phase convention at all sites.
