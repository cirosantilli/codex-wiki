Continuous vacuum selection by an external source (Uh​(ϕ)=λ(ϕ2−v2)2/4−hϕ1​)

Consider two real scalar fields ϕ=(ϕ1​,ϕ2​) with positive λ,v, standard kinetic terms, and the displayed scalar potential. At h=0, the circle group rotates one field into the other: this is a global symmetry in field theory, with the same rotation at every point in space. The vacuum manifold is the circle ϕ2=v2. Choose (v,0) and differentiate the scalar potential twice:
M2=(2λv20​00​).
(5)
The scalar mass matrix assigns a restoring force to the radial displacement, whereas its tangent is one of the Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix. Moving around the circle costs no potential energy; moving between neighbouring points in space can still cost gradient energy. Flat angular potential energy therefore means a soft collective excitation, not that every spatial pattern is free.
A positive source h explicitly selects the positive ϕ1​ direction. The minimum is (w,0), where w>v is the positive solution of λw(w2−v2)=h. The two eigenvalues of the scalar mass matrix there are
mradial2​=2λw2+wh​,mangular2​=wh​.
(6)
For example, λ=v=1 and h=0.15 give w≃1.06792, mradial2​≃2.42138 and mangular2​≃0.140459. The light angular excitation becomes a Goldstone boson as h↓0, while the radial mass stays finite. The source changes both the preferred direction and the radius; expanding about the old point (v,0) at nonzero h would miss that shift.
