Discovery of the first pulsar

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Stellar astrophysics Stellar evolution Neutron star Pulsar
OurBigBook.com Words: 356
Jocelyn Bell Burnell helped build Antony Hewish's radio telescope for finding compact sources through interplanetary scintillation in the solar wind. Operating it and inspecting its chart recorder output in 1967, she recognized an unusual recurring trace, checked earlier charts, and obtained fast recordings resolving pulses in late November. Her observation, recognition and persistent analysis were indispensable.[ref]
The 1968 paper by Antony Hewish, Jocelyn Bell Burnell (bylined S. J. Bell), J. D. H. Pilkington, P. F. Scott and R. A. Collins reported 1.337s spacing and proposed compact-star oscillations. Its radio telescope had 2,048 full-wave dipole antennas in sixteen rows. Four receivers selected declinations; two array halves formed an east-west interferometer. The 81.5MHz receivers passed 1MHz. Observations used 0.05s receiver time constant and Rapidigraph chart recorder with 0.03s time constant; radio second pips calibrated timing.[ref]
Antony Hewish checked sidereal time recurrence. Jocelyn Bell Burnell recalls that P. F. Scott and R. A. Collins reproduced the pulses with another telescope and receivers, while J. D. H. Pilkington measured pulsar dispersion delay: independent checks of instrument independence and intervening plasma.[ref]
A useful numerical comparison uses the approximate sidereal time day of 1436.07min. Relative to a 1440min schedule, the same sky direction transits about 3.93min earlier each day, or 39.3min after ten days. This compares successive passages through the receiving beam; it is a different clock from the roughly 1.3s pulse spacing within one passage. Sky recurrence alone cannot rule out every transmitter or reflection, and pulsar dispersion delay alone does not uniquely establish an emission mechanism. Their strength lies in independent constraints agreeing.
The discovery lesson is that an unexpected signal must survive changes of readout, instrument and observing date. Increasing chart recorder speed exposed structure hidden in a compressed trace; it did not make the source begin pulsing. The pulsar interpretation now separates the rotating clock, beam geometry, pulsar magnetosphere emission and pulsar dispersion delay. The initial observation established periodic arrival, not a direct measurement of the neutron star's surface motion or radius.
