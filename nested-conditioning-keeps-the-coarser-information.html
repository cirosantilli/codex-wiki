Nested conditioning keeps the coarser information (E[E[X∣G]∣H]=E[X∣H](H⊆G))

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation Law of total expectation
OurBigBook.com Words: 112
For integrable random variable X and nested sub-sigma-algebras H⊆G, the tower property says
E[E[X∣G]∣H]=E[X∣H].
(23)
The finer-information conditional mean can be averaged within each coarser group, recovering the coarser-information conditional mean. Conversely, conditioning an already H-measurable conditional mean on G leaves it unchanged. Both orders therefore recover E[X∣H] when the information is nested.[ref]
For the finite example in law of total expectation, exact knowledge of X gives the finer mean X itself. Averaging within the groups A,B recovers two and eight. Forgetting the group then gives 13/2. The nesting condition is essential; iterated conditional expectation over nonnested sigma-algebras describes why arbitrary successive conditioning cannot simply be replaced by conditioning on an intersection.
