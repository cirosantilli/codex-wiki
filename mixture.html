Mixture

 Home Chemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 94
A mixture contains two or more chemical substances combined in proportions that need not be fixed. A chemical compound instead has a definite composition: water has two hydrogen chemical atoms per oxygen chemical atom, whereas a sucrose and water mixture can contain different sugar proportions. A homogeneous mixture has uniform composition on the scale of representative samples; a heterogeneous mixture has distinguishable regions of different composition. A chemical solution is homogeneous, whereas sand dispersed in water is a particle suspension.[ref]
The distinction concerns the sample, not merely its ingredient list. A vessel with dissolved sodium chloride and excess undissolved sodium chloride contains a chemical solution and an ionic crystal thermodynamic phase. The liquid portion can be homogeneous while the whole vessel is heterogeneous. Similarly, molecular oxygen dissolved in water is part of a chemical solution; visible gas bubbles introduce a separate gas thermodynamic phase.

A short-range solvent-mediated interaction associated with water organization and hydration near surfaces. It is distinct from an ideal point-ion double layer and can contribute repulsion at separations where continuum far-field models fail.

A dispersion of droplets of one liquid in another immiscible liquid. Its interfacial energy and kinetic persistence depend on droplet size, interfacial tension and stabilizing species.

Droplet coalescence

Merging of droplets after their separating film fails. This differs from clustering of still-distinct droplets. Interfacial protection can delay merging without making the two bulk liquid phases molecularly miscible.

Emulsion stability versus molecular miscibility

A surfactant-stabilized dispersion can remain macroscopically uniform while its two liquids remain distinct phases. Lower interfacial tension reduces the work of making droplets, and repulsive interfacial films can slow droplet coalescence. Kinetic emulsion stability does not by itself establish equilibrium molecular miscibility or a thermodynamically stable microemulsion.

Colloidal coagulation

The distribution of particle dimensions in an ensemble, with number-, volume- or intensity-weighting specified. Broad size distributions can broaden a melting transition, frustrate colloidal crystallization and bias scattering-based size estimates. A mean size alone is then insufficient.

A spread of particle or molecular dimensions rather than a single size. A quantitative measure must state the weighting and statistic; particle-size variation and polymer chain-length variation are related examples but use different common indices.
Molar-mass dispersity (Mw​/Mn​)
Words: 47
Polydispersity OurBigBook.com
The weight-average molar mass divided by the number-average molar mass. Its conventional written symbol is Đ. With number weighting it equals 1+Var(M)/⟨M⟩2, so it is at least one and equals one only for a uniform molar mass. It measures one aspect of distribution breadth, not the full shape of a molar mass distribution.

Colloidal crystal

Colloid OurBigBook.com
An ordered arrangement of colloidal particles. Nearly monodisperse hard-sphere-like particles can order through packing entropy at sufficient concentration, while weak reversible attractions or templates can guide ordering. An irreversibly aggregated solid is not necessarily a colloidal crystal.

Colloidal stability

Colloid OurBigBook.com
Resistance of a dispersion to aggregation or irreversible separation. Electrostatic and steric interactions and solvent compatibility can all matter.

Gravitational Peclet number (Peg​)

For a sphere of radius a and buoyant density difference Δρ, define Peg​=vs​a/D=mb​ga/(kB​T) using radius as the transport length. With Stokes law and Stokes–Einstein diffusion, Peg​=4πΔρga4/(3kB​T). Sedimentation-Brownian crossover radius (ac​=[3kB​T/(4πΔρg)]1/4)
Words: 42
Gravitational Peclet number OurBigBook.com
Setting the gravitational Péclet number to one gives the displayed particle-scale estimate. A macroscopic sample instead requires its height to be small compared with the sedimentation scale height. These different observational criteria give different size thresholds; “no sedimentation” is not an exact universal cutoff.

Sedimentation length (ℓg​=kB​T/(mb​g))

Words: 45
Colloidal stability OurBigBook.com
For noninteracting particles with positive buoyant mass mb​ in a gravitational field, a dilute thermodynamic equilibrium profile is proportional to e−z/ℓg​ with ℓg​=kB​T/(mb​g). Uniformity over a vessel height H requires H≪ℓg​. Requiring only ℓg​ to exceed a particle radius is a weaker microscopic gravity-versus-diffusion condition.

Derjaguin approximation (F(h)=2πReff​W(h))

Words: 85
Colloid OurBigBook.com
For gently curved interacting surfaces whose range and closest gap are small compared with their radii, integrate the local planar interaction over nearly parallel surface elements. With W(s) the planar interaction free energy per area and Π(s)=−W′(s), the local gap for two spheres is s≃h+r2/(2Reff​), where Reff−1​=a1−1​+a2−1​. Thus F=2π∫0∞​rΠ(s)dr=2πReff​∫h∞​Π(s)ds=2πReff​W(h) when W(∞)=0. Equal spheres have Reff​=a/2; a sphere and plane have Reff​=a. Use consistent surface boundary conditions when supplying the planar interaction.

Near-contact van der Waals attraction of spheres (U(h)≃−AH​a/(12h))

Words: 71
Colloid OurBigBook.com
For two equal spheres of radius a separated by a surface gap h≪a, a positive Hamaker constant AH​ gives the nonretarded Van der Waals force approximation U=−AH​a/(12h) and F=−dU/dh=−AH​a/(12h2). The Derjaguin approximation derives the geometry factor from the planar interaction energy per area W=−AH​/(12πh2) and effective radius a/2. A sphere and plane instead have effective radius a, doubling the force at the same gap. Molecular repulsion, roughness and retardation limit these continuum formulas.

Particle aggregation

Words: 370 Articles: 5
Colloid OurBigBook.com
Particle aggregation is association of initially separate particles into larger clusters. For colloids, both the rate of Brownian motion encounters and the probability that an encounter produces attachment matter. Strong attraction with negligible repulsive barriers can give diffusion-limited cluster aggregation; a barrier that makes attachment unlikely can give reaction-limited cluster aggregation. Reversible association and bond rearrangement require additional kinetic assumptions.

Flocculation

Words: 37
Particle aggregation OurBigBook.com
Association of dispersed particles into larger clusters, often after repulsive stabilization is weakened. Salt screening, reduced surface charge, polymer bridging or depletion force can promote it. Clusters may sediment rapidly; preventing aggregation and preventing gravitational separation are distinct stability requirements.

Diffusion-limited aggregation (DLA)

Words: 54
Particle aggregation OurBigBook.com
In the standard single-seed model, individual particles execute random walks until first contact with a growing cluster, where they stick irreversibly. Exposed tips intercept more particles than screened inner regions, producing branched clusters. This differs from diffusion-limited cluster aggregation, in which whole existing clusters move and join one another. The two models need not share the same mass fractal dimension.

Reaction-limited cluster aggregation (RLCA)

Words: 81
Particle aggregation OurBigBook.com
In reaction-limited cluster aggregation, encounters occur frequently compared with successful attachment: an activation or interaction barrier gives a small sticking probability. Repeated unsuccessful approaches allow clusters to sample more relative configurations before bonding, commonly producing denser aggregates than diffusion-limited cluster aggregation. Typical three-dimensional isotropic irreversible cluster models give mass fractal dimension around 2.1. This structural contrast does not require previously formed bonds to break and restructure; it can arise from unsuccessful encounters alone. [ref]

Diffusion-limited cluster aggregation (DLCA)

Words: 144 Articles: 1
Particle aggregation OurBigBook.com
In diffusion-limited cluster aggregation, mobile clusters undergoing Brownian motion attach with probability close to one when they encounter another cluster. Irreversible first-contact sticking tends to trap particles in exposed outer branches, producing open aggregates. In the common three-dimensional isotropic cluster model, the mass fractal dimension is roughly 1.8. This is different from depositing individual random walkers onto one fixed cluster. Rotational motion, anisotropic attraction and bond restructuring can change the morphology.

For two dilute spherical species with absorbing contact and negligible interaction barriers, relative diffusion has coefficient D=D1​+D2​ and contact radius R=a1​+a2​. The steady concentration outside one target is c(r)=c∞​(1−R/r), solving the radial Laplace equation with zero contact concentration. Its inward diffusive flux is 4πDRc∞​, proving the displayed bimolecular rate coefficient. For identical particles, converting this encounter coefficient into a rate of loss of cluster number additionally needs the factor that prevents counting the same pair twice.

Colloidal glass

Words: 35
Colloid OurBigBook.com
A colloidal glass is a disordered dense particle assembly with structural relaxation too slow on the observation timescale. Crowding, repulsion or short-range attraction can impede rearrangement. Kinetic arrest must be distinguished from an equilibrium phase transition to a crystal.

Colloidal gel

Words: 42
Colloid OurBigBook.com
A colloidal gel is a mechanically persistent, space-spanning particle network arrested on the observation timescale. Attractions can produce such a network during aggregation or arrested phase separation. A gel is a dynamical, often nonequilibrium state; its appearance does not establish an additional equilibrium thermodynamic phase.

Polymer bridging

Words: 43
Colloid OurBigBook.com
Polymer bridging occurs when one polymer chain attaches to more than one particle, producing an effective attraction or network connection. Surface affinity and incomplete coverage are essential to this mechanism. It should be distinguished from both steric stabilization by densely coated particles and the depletion force produced by nonadsorbing chains.

Steric stabilization

Words: 92 Articles: 2
Colloid OurBigBook.com
Steric stabilization is resistance to particle aggregation supplied by attached or adsorbed molecular layers. Bringing two coated particles close compresses or overlaps the layers, increasing their free energy through conformational and osmotic effects. It differs from the depletion force of nonadsorbing dissolved chains.

Steric repulsion

Words: 31
Steric stabilization OurBigBook.com
Repulsion associated with overlap of surface-bound layers, often through chain confinement and osmotic free-energy costs. Layer thickness, solvent quality and grafting/adsorption state matter; it is not simply a bare hard-sphere collision.

Electrosteric stabilization

Words: 24
Steric stabilization OurBigBook.com
Stabilization by a surface-bound polymer layer that supplies both conformational/osmotic repulsion and electrostatic repulsion. Chain coverage, solvent quality and screening determine the resulting interaction barrier.

Depletion force (Udep​=−Πp​Vov​)

Words: 162 Articles: 1
Colloid OurBigBook.com
A depletion force is an effective interaction caused by exclusion of smaller particles or macromolecules from regions around larger objects. Overlap of the excluded regions increases the accessible volume for the smaller species. At fixed reservoir osmotic pressure Πp​, the associated pair free energy is −Πp​ times the overlap volume in the ideal dilute-depletant approximation. It is an entropic attraction for two large hard particles. Surface adsorption can introduce different interactions, so adding an arbitrary polymer does not by itself guarantee depletion attraction.

Asakura-Oosawa model (Udep​(R)=−Πp​π(4Re​+R)(2Re​−R)2/12)

Words: 86
Depletion force OurBigBook.com
The Asakura-Oosawa model treats dilute nonadsorbing polymer coils as mutually interpenetrable ideal particles excluded from a layer around each colloid. For colloid radius a and polymer exclusion thickness Rg​, two exclusion spheres of radius Re​=a+Rg​ overlap when 2a≤R<2Re​. Adding their two spherical cap volumes gives Vov​=π(4Re​+R)(2Re​−R)2/12. Multiplication by −Πp​ gives the displayed pair free energy, with zero depletion interaction beyond 2Re​. The range is controlled by polymer size and the depth by reservoir polymer concentration. At larger polymer concentration, polymer interactions and many-body overlap effects require care.

DLVO theory

Words: 342 Articles: 5
Colloid OurBigBook.com
DLVO theory combines screened electrostatic repulsion from overlapping electric double layers with attractive Van der Waals forces to model colloid stability. A repulsive barrier can inhibit aggregation even when a deep short-distance attractive minimum exists. Increasing electrolyte concentration reduces the screening length and can lower that barrier. Molecular surface forces and polymer-mediated interactions require additions to this description.

Primary and secondary DLVO minima

Words: 48
DLVO theory OurBigBook.com
A deep short-distance minimum can bind contacting particles, while a more distant shallow minimum can permit loose reversible aggregates. A repulsive barrier between them controls kinetic access to contact. Salt can lower that barrier through screening. Molecular-scale steric or hydration forces lie outside the simplest continuum dispersion-plus-double-layer model.

Debye screening of colloid repulsion (κ2=2ne2/(ϵkB​T))

Words: 55
DLVO theory OurBigBook.com
For a symmetric monovalent reservoir with number density n of each species, linearizing Poisson-Boltzmann theory gives the displayed inverse screening length. A planar potential decays exponentially; a spherical far field also has a geometrical inverse-distance factor. Increasing salt reduces the range of repulsion. Fixed surface potential, fixed charge and charge regulation need not give identical interaction amplitudes.

Charge stabilization of colloids

Words: 191 Articles: 2
DLVO theory OurBigBook.com
Like-charged colloids can resist aggregation because bringing their electric double layers together costs free energy. In DLVO theory, screened electrostatic repulsion competes with attractive Van der Waals forces. A repulsive barrier much larger than kB​T suppresses thermally activated aggregation, although the dispersion can remain metastable. Lower ionic strength generally lengthens the Debye–Hückel screening length, while multivalent counterions or added salt shorten it and can lower the barrier. The surface charge, charge regulation and solvent-dependent attraction also matter; screening length alone does not guarantee stability.
Critical coagulation concentration (CCC)
Words: 121 Articles: 1
Charge stabilization of colloids OurBigBook.com
The critical coagulation concentration is the approximate electrolyte concentration separating slow, barrier-limited aggregation from rapid aggregation of a charge-stabilized colloid. In the simplest DLVO theory, increasing salt shortens the Debye–Hückel screening length and lowers the repulsive barrier until rapid aggregation is possible. It depends on particle charge, solvent, temperature and particularly counterion valence. It is a kinetic crossover, not a universal salt concentration or necessarily an equilibrium phase boundary. Aggregation just below this crossover can be reaction limited; exactly at or above the rapid-aggregation threshold, proximity to the threshold alone does not imply a small sticking probability.
Schulze-Hardy rule
Words: 28
Critical coagulation concentration OurBigBook.com
Strong sensitivity of a lyophobic colloid's critical coagulation concentration to counterion valence. A high-surface-potential DLVO approximation gives cccc​∝z−6; specific adsorption and charge regulation can invalidate this simple scaling.

Screened Coulomb pair potential (U(R)∝e−κR/R)

Words: 58
Colloid OurBigBook.com
A screened Coulomb pair potential models the effective electrostatic interaction of particles in an electrolyte. For identical weakly charged spheres of radius a, charge Q, and Debye–Hückel screening length κ−1, a common pairwise approximation is U(R)=Q2e−κ(R−2a)/[4πε(1+κa)2R] for R≥2a. The near-contact interaction can require more detailed boundary conditions or nonlinear screening. Charge, screening length and temperature all matter to a resulting phase diagram.

Solution (chemistry)

Words: 5k Articles: 48
Mixture OurBigBook.com
A chemical solution is a homogeneous mixture in which designated solutes are distributed in a solvent. Representative equal-volume samples have the same composition after mixing. Individual molecules and ions remain distinguishable. The solute/solvent labels describe their roles in this mixture.[ref]
A dissolved solute is dispersed as molecules, chemical atoms, or ions, rather than as the larger solid particles of a particle suspension. For example, sucrose retains its molecular formula during dissolution in water; a chemical reaction is not required. Dissolved sodium chloride supplies sodium cations and chloride anions, surrounded by water molecules through solvation. Dissolved does not mean chemically destroyed or converted into water.[ref]
This makes a useful connection between microscopic identity and laboratory sampling. If a chemical solution is well mixed, the amount of substance in a measured portion follows from its concentration and volume. A scoop from a poorly mixed particle suspension need not represent the whole vessel. Visual clarity alone cannot establish composition or exclude a fine dispersion; the quantitative claim needs representative sampling.

Regular solution (f/(kB​T)=ϕlogϕ+(1−ϕ)log(1−ϕ)+χϕ(1−ϕ))

Words: 74
Solution (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
A regular solution keeps the ideal combinatorial entropy of mixing while allowing a nonzero energetic preference for unlike contacts. For a symmetric binary mixture with equal molecular sizes, the displayed free energy per site combines mixing entropy with a contact penalty χϕ(1−ϕ). Its spinodal satisfies 1/ϕ+1/(1−ϕ)−2χ=0. At the symmetric thermodynamic critical point, the third composition derivative also vanishes, giving ϕc​=1/2 and χc​=2. If χ=A/T, the critical temperature is A/2. This is the equal-size limit of Flory–Huggins solution theory.

Semidilute polymer solution

Words: 319 Articles: 4
Polymer solution OurBigBook.com
A solution above the polymer overlap concentration but below the concentrated regime, with interpenetrating chains. Local polymer structure is characterized by a correlation length or concentration-blob size. Scaling formulas describe a broad semidilute regime most reliably when the concentration greatly exceeds overlap while remaining small compared with one in volume fraction.
Semidilute osmotic pressure of a two-dimensional polymer solution (Π∼(kB​T/ℓ2)ϕ3)
Words: 83
Semidilute polymer solution OurBigBook.com
In a planar good-solvent solution, a correlation blob of g monomers has size ξ∼ℓg3/4. Its area fraction is ϕ∼gℓ2/ξ2∼g−1/2, giving g∼ϕ−2 and ξ∼ℓϕ−3/2. Each blob contributes order kB​T to the osmotic free energy, so the surface osmotic pressure is Π∼kB​T/ξ2∼(kB​T/ℓ2)ϕ3. This regime requires many blobs per chain and area fraction below close packing. In the dilute regime entire chains are the independently translating objects and Π∼kB​Tϕ/(Nℓ2). The two expressions match at the two-dimensional polymer overlap fraction ϕ∗∼N−1/2.

Concentration-blob size in a theta solvent (ξ∼v0​/(b2ϕ))

Concentration blob OurBigBook.com
For an ideal chain inside a blob, ν=1/2 and ξ∼bg​. Together with ϕ∼gv0​/ξ3, this gives the displayed concentration-blob size. Under the site-volume approximation v0​∼b3, ξ∼b/ϕ and g∼ϕ−2. Theta solvent

Words: 49
Polymer solution OurBigBook.com
A solvent under conditions where the leading effective pair excluded volume of a long dilute polymer vanishes. In the simplest Flory–Huggins solution theory this means χ=1/2. The chain is approximately an ideal chain, while three-segment interactions can still affect the osmotic pressure of a concentrated solution. A particular polymer-solvent pair often meets this condition only near its theta temperature.

Polymer overlap concentration (c∗∼M/(NA​Vcoil​))

Words: 241 Articles: 2
Polymer solution OurBigBook.com
The overlap concentration is the concentration at which distinct dilute polymer coils first substantially interpenetrate. It corresponds to roughly one chain per coil volume. If a chain contains N repeat units, has molar mass M and occupies a coil volume Vcoil​, then its chain number density is approximately 1/Vcoil​, its repeat-unit number density is N/Vcoil​, and its mass concentration is M/(NA​Vcoil​). A scaling convention uses Vcoil​∼R3; a commonly chosen geometric convention uses Vcoil​=4πRg3​/3. These give different order-one prefactors because a coil has no sharp surface. For an ideal chain, R∼bN1/2 and a site-volume estimate v0​∼b3 gives overlap volume fraction ϕ∗∼N−1/2. A good solvent chain with the Flory approximation R∼bN3/5 instead gives ϕ∗∼N−4/5.
Polymer overlap volume fraction (ϕ∗∼Nv0​/R3)
Words: 60
Polymer overlap concentration OurBigBook.com
The polymer overlap volume fraction counts the segment volume of one chain divided by its coil volume. If the segment volume is v0​ and R∼bNν, then ϕ∗∼(v0​/b3)N1−3ν. The site-volume convention v0​∼b3 gives N−4/5 for the three-dimensional Flory approximation and N−1/2 for an ideal chain. A geometric coil volume such as 4πRg3​/3 changes a prefactor; segment length and segment volume are not generally interchangeable without a model convention.
Two-dimensional polymer overlap fraction (ϕ∗∼Nℓ2/R2∼N1−2ν)
Words: 69
Polymer overlap concentration OurBigBook.com
For polymers restricted to an interface, overlap means roughly one coil per coil area, nc​R2∼1. A chain contains N monomers with typical occupied area ℓ2, so its overlap area fraction is ϕ∗∼Nℓ2/R2. With the planar good-solvent exponent ν=3/4 from the two-dimensional Flory approximation, this gives ϕ∗∼N−1/2. The same exponent happens to occur for a three-dimensional ideal chain's volume fraction, but its geometric origin is different.

Poor solvent

Words: 123 Articles: 1
Polymer solution OurBigBook.com
A poor solvent favors effective attraction between polymer segments relative to solvent contacts. A dilute flexible chain tends to contract, and sufficiently unfavorable conditions produce a compact polymer globule. Solvent quality depends on the polymer, solvent and temperature. The simple Flory–Huggins solution theory identifies the poor-solvent side of the theta condition with χ>1/2; this does not guarantee that every finite-density mixture is inside its spinodal.
Polymer globule (R∼bN1/3)
Words: 64
Poor solvent OurBigBook.com
A polymer globule is a compact conformation favored by effective segment attractions in a poor solvent. If its internal segment density is independent of chain length, its volume is proportional to N and a size measure scales as N1/3. The proportionality between physical volume and the cube of a radius of gyration depends on shape and density profile. Compactness alone supplies scaling, not a uniquely determined numerical monomer volume.

Theta temperature (Θ)

Words: 58

The theta temperature is the temperature at which the leading effective two-segment interaction of a dilute long polymer vanishes, so its large-scale size is approximately that of an ideal chain.
