For central potential V(r)=βrk and nonzero angular momentum J, a circular orbit satisfies βkr0k+2​=J2/m, so attraction requires βk>0. Twice differentiating the radial effective potential V+J2/(2mr2) gives Veff′′​(r0​)=(k+2)J2/(mr04​). Its harmonic approximation therefore has radial angular frequency ωr​=k+2​∣J∣/(mr02​), whereas ∣ωθ​∣=∣J∣/(mr02​). This proves the ratio and shows stability precisely when k>−2. At k=−2 the tuned effective potential is flat; at k<−2 the radial disturbance grows exponentially. Rationality of this local frequency ratio is only a necessary condition for all nearby orbits to close; Bertrand's theorem makes a stronger finite-amplitude assertion.
