Separation of variables in the central Coulomb potential gives ψnlm​=Rnl​(r)Ylm​(θ,ϕ). The radial wavefunction depends on n and l, independently of m. For the hydrogen radial wavefunction in the first excited shell, put ρ=r/a0​. The Bohr radius is a0​=4πϵ0​ℏ2/(μe2), where μ is the electron–proton reduced mass. Choose the real phases
R20​=22​a03/2​(2−ρ)e−ρ/2​,R21​=24​a03/2​ρe−ρ/2​.
(3)
Near the origin, R20​→1/(2​a03/2​), whereas R21​∼r/(24​a05/2​). More generally, regular hydrogen radial wavefunctions behave as rl. At large r, both displayed functions have a polynomial factor proportional to r multiplying e−r/(2a0​); R20​ approaches zero from below and R21​ from above with these phase choices.
The 2s radial wavefunction crosses zero once, at r=2a0​, and has its negative minimum at 4a0​. The 2p radial wavefunction has no radial node at positive r, and reaches its maximum at 2a0​. The count is n−l−1; neither the origin nor the limit at infinity is counted as a radial node. These are sketches of R, rather than of rR or the radial probability density r2∣R∣2.

For the Schrödinger equation with a real scalar potential and no vector potential, the probability current is
j=2miℏ​(Ψ∗∇Ψ−Ψ∇Ψ∗)=mℏ​Im(Ψ∗∇Ψ).
(4)
Together with the probability density ρ=∣Ψ∣2, it satisfies the continuity equation ∂t​ρ+∇⋅j=0. Substituting ∂x​Ψ=ikΨ gives
j=mℏk​∣A∣2,ρ(x)=∣A∣2,dP=∣A∣2dx.​
(5)
A complex wavefunction requires both its real and imaginary parts in the sketch. Choosing the irrelevant constant phase so that A is real, they are Acoskx and Asinkx, while the probability density is horizontal. It is the spatial phase gradient of a Schrodinger wavefunction ∂x​argΨ=k that determines the sign of the probability current. In particular, eikx and e−ikx have the same real part but opposite probability currents, so the slope of the real part cannot determine the direction.
A nonzero plane wave on the whole real line is not square integrable. The displayed density is understood with box normalization, generalized normalization, or as a local description of a broad wave packet; it does not define a normalized probability distribution on the whole line.

a OurBigBook.com
The spin Hilbert space is a tensor product of two two-dimensional spaces and therefore has dimension four. Angular momentum addition decomposes it as
S=1: MS​=1,0,−1;S=0: MS​=0.​
(18)
These are three orthogonal triplet states and one singlet state. For identical electrons the complete wavefunction must also satisfy fermionic exchange symmetry: a symmetric triplet spin factor accompanies an antisymmetric spatial factor, whereas the antisymmetric singlet spin factor accompanies a symmetric spatial factor. If both electrons occupy the same spatial orbital, only the singlet spin factor is allowed. The four-dimensional spin space does not assert four allowed spin states for that particular spatial occupation.

Write Ψ=(∣↓↑⟩+∣↑↓⟩)/2​. Each term has cancelling z components, so Sz​Ψ=0. In the formula for S2, the first three terms contribute (3/2−1/2)ℏ2Ψ=ℏ2Ψ. The two ladder products exchange the two terms and contribute another ℏ2Ψ. Therefore
S2Ψ=2ℏ2Ψ,S=1,MS​=0,P12​Ψ=+Ψ.​

This spin state is symmetric; the accompanying spatial state must be antisymmetric for two electrons. This does not prohibit opposite spins in different spatial orbitals. For example, with orthonormal orbitals ϕa​,ϕb​, the Slater determinant
2​1​[ϕa​(r1​)ϕb​(r2​)∣↓1​↑2​⟩−ϕb​(r1​)ϕa​(r2​)∣↑1​↓2​⟩]

For a conditional spin measurement of identical fermions addressing the z component in one spatial mode, the Born rule gives
P(↑)=21​.​
(23)
Conditioned on this result, the mode spin state is ∣↑a​↓b​⟩; the other outcome produces ∣↓a​↑b​⟩. The spin product is not a spin-exchange eigenstate. The measurement has selected an addressed mode and recorded an outcome, so this conditional spin description distinguishes the measured subsystem.
The full electron wavefunction still obeys fermionic exchange symmetry. For example, the conditional spin-up outcome in spatial mode a is represented in particle coordinates by
2​1​[ϕa​(r1​)ϕb​(r2​)∣↑1​↓2​⟩−ϕb​(r1​)ϕa​(r2​)∣↓1​↑2​⟩].
(24)
It is antisymmetric under full particle exchange. Thus the loss of a definite symmetry of the conditional spin product does not mean that measurement turns identical electrons into distinguishable particles. If the outcome is discarded, the mode spin state is instead the equal mixture of the two opposite-spin products. The measurement must specify which spatial mode is addressed for the conditional statement to be operationally meaningful.

⟨r⟩1s​=aq3​4​(2/aq​)43!​=23​aq​.

The unperturbed n=2 level is degenerate. The useful point is that a central perturbation is already diagonal within its usual orbital eigenbasis:
⟨nlm∣br∣nl′m′⟩=b(∫0∞​r3Rnl∗​Rnl′​dr)δll′​δmm′​.
(43)
Angular orthogonality removes the possible mixing of 2s and 2p, and also the mixing of different magnetic sublevels. Therefore the matrix required by degenerate perturbation theory needs no further diagonalization: its diagonal entries are precisely the expectation values above. It would be incorrect to justify the calculation by claiming that the unperturbed spectrum was nondegenerate.
For b=0 the energy needs only n. For a nonzero central confinement term the energy generally needs n,l, but remains independent of m. The magnetic quantum number is still needed to specify an individual orbital eigenstate, rather than just its energy.
