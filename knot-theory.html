Knot invariant

Words: 30
Knot theory OurBigBook.com
A knot invariant assigns the same value to isotopic knots. Examples include the knot group, Alexander polynomial of a knot, Seifert genus, knot signature and Jones polynomial. Different knot invariants can retain different information: isomorphic knot groups do not alone imply isotopic knots.

Prime decomposition of knots

Words: 53
Connected sum of knots OurBigBook.com
Every nontrivial oriented knot is a finite connected sum of knots of prime knots, uniquely up to permutation and oriented link isotopy. Existence follows by induction from additivity of Seifert genus. For uniqueness, compare complete decomposing sphere systems for a knot and remove their intersection circles by the prime-ball exchange lemma. Once a prime ball is disjoint from both systems, its factor can be removed from both; induction then matches all factors.

Decomposing sphere system for a knot
Words: 176 Articles: 1
Splitting sphere of a knot OurBigBook.com
A finite collection of disjoint splitting spheres of a knot encodes a connected sum of knots. Cut along every sphere and fill each spherical boundary with a three-ball containing a straight arc joining its two marked endpoints. The resulting oriented knots are the factors. The system is complete when its nontrivial factors are prime knots.
Prime-ball exchange lemma
Words: 133
Decomposing sphere system for a knot OurBigBook.com
In a complete decomposing sphere system for a knot, a disk inside a prime-factor ball that meets the knotted arc once cuts its factor into a connected sum of knots. One side must be an unknot. Replacing the ball by the side carrying the prime factor therefore preserves the unordered factor list. A disk missing the arc cuts off an empty ball and gives the same conclusion. After a small transverse perturbation, such a replacement removes an innermost intersection circle with another sphere system. If a chosen innermost disk contains both marked points, another innermost disk, or its complementary disk when it is the only intersection, contains neither. Iterating produces a prime-factor ball disjoint from both systems. The replacement changes only the location of a joining arc in the intervening punctured ball; capping the other boundaries still recovers the same oriented factors.

Torus knots are prime

Words: 77
Torus knot OurBigBook.com
For coprime p,q with absolute values at least two, the knot group is ⟨a,b∣ap=bq⟩. Its nontrivial central element z=ap=bq maps nontrivially to the abelianization. A meridian aubv, where qu+pv=1, has infinite order in the quotient C∣p∣​∗C∣q∣​. A composite knot group would be an amalgamated free product of proper factors over its meridian subgroup, whose center lies in that subgroup. Then z would be a meridian power, contradicting the infinite order of the meridian modulo z.

Seifert-matrix presentation of the Alexander module (AK​=coker(tV−VT))

Linking-dual generators for the homology of the cut-open piece give the positive and negative inclusion matrices V and VT in row convention. The gluing relations therefore have matrix tV−VT. Full-degree irreducible Alexander polynomial implies a prime knot (brΔK​=2gs​(K),ΔK​ irreducible ⟹ K prime)
Words: 56
Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus OurBigBook.com
Assume gs​(K)>0. A nontrivial connected sum of knots has positive genus summands, and additivity of Seifert genus applies. Its Alexander polynomial of a knot is the product of the summand polynomials. If the polynomial is irreducible, one summand has unit polynomial; the other must then have polynomial breadth 2gs​(K), exceeding twice its strictly smaller genus. This contradicts the Alexander breadth bound on Seifert genus. Irreducibility without the full-degree hypothesis alone does not imply primeness.

Nontrivial knot exteriors have incompressible boundary

Words: 97
Knot exterior OurBigBook.com
A sphere in a knot exterior bounds a ball on the side not containing the knot, so the exterior is irreducible. A compression disk for its boundary torus would cut the exterior to a manifold with sphere boundary; irreducibility makes that manifold a ball, and reversing the compression gives a solid torus exterior. Its meridian disk has boundary a preferred knot longitude, so adjoining the boundary annulus gives a spanning disk for the knot, which is then an unknot. The Loop theorem therefore makes the peripheral fundamental group injective for every nontrivial knot. In particular its knot group contains an injected copy of the torus group and cannot be cyclic.

Two-generator knot groups have cyclic Alexander modules
Words: 63
Knot group OurBigBook.com
The abelianization of a knot group is infinite cyclic. Nielsen changes of a two-generator free basis reduce its exponent vector to (1,0). In the cyclic-cover cellular chain complex, the first boundary is then (t−1,0), whose kernel is a free rank-one Laurent module. Quotienting by the lifted relator boundaries gives a cyclic module, regardless of the number of relators. A noncyclic Alexander module therefore obstructs every two-generator knot-group presentation.
