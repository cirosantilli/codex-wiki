Mass distribution principle (μ(B(x,r))≤Crs ⟹ dimH​E≥s)

 ... Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Geometric measure theory Hausdorff measure Hausdorff dimension
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
The mass distribution principle converts a diffuse measure into a lower bound on Hausdorff dimension. Let s>0 and let a Borel probability measure μ give a Borel set E full mass. If, for some C,r0​>0, every ball centered in E satisfies
μ(B(x,r))≤Crs(0<r<r0​),
(5)
then Hs(E)≥1/C>0 in the diameter-cost convention, and consequently dimH​E≥s.
The idea is to keep unit mass from being crowded into cheap covering pieces. For a piece U of positive diameter r meeting E, choose x∈U∩E. A ball of radius r+ε contains U; letting ε↓0 gives μ∗(U)≤Crs, where μ∗ is the associated outer measure. Zero-diameter pieces have zero mass by the same estimate. For any sufficiently small cover,
1=μ(E)≤∑j​μ∗(Uj​)≤C∑j​(diamUj​)s.
(6)
Thus every cover costs at least 1/C. This is the lower-bound counterpart to the explicit covers in scale-restricted Hausdorff content. The Cantor distribution supplies the needed mass in Hausdorff dimension of the Cantor set. [ref].
