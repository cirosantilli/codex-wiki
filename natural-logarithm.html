Natural logarithm (ln)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Exponential function Logarithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 144
The natural logarithm lnx is the logarithm with base Euler's number, and the inverse function of the real exponential function. A particularly geometric definition is
lnx=∫1x​tdt​,x>0.
(48)
For x>1, it is the area under the hyperbola y=1/t from 1 to x. For 0<x<1, the reversed integration limits give a negative value. The fundamental theorem of calculus gives (lnx)′=1/x, so the natural logarithm is increasing, while (lnx)′′=−1/x2 gives concavity.
The change of variables t=au shows ∫aab​dt/t=∫1b​du/u=lnb for a,b>0. Thus the intervals from 1 to 2 and from 2 to 4 each contribute area ln2, explaining ln4=2ln2. Equal ratios give equal increments on a logarithmic scale, even though the ordinary differences grow. This makes the natural logarithm useful for comparing multiplicative changes and solving for time in exponential growth or exponential decay. The integral construction and inverse function properties are discussed in OpenStax, the natural logarithm as an integral.
