Conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane (ϕ(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Upper half-plane (complex analysis)
OurBigBook.com Words: 318
Every conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane is a Möbius transformation with real coefficients and positive determinant, and conversely every such map is a conformal bijection of the upper half-plane to itself:
f(z)=cz+daz+b​,a,b,c,d∈R,Δ=ad−bc>0.
(20)
The real-coefficient and sign conditions matter. The complex Möbius transformation z↦iz sends i to −1 on the boundary, while the real Möbius transformation z↦1/z has negative determinant and sends i to −i in the lower half-plane. [ref]
For two concrete upper half-plane automorphisms, take
T(z)=z+1,AT​=(10​11​),S(z)=−z1​,AS​=(01​−10​).
(21)
Both matrices have determinant one. Here T(i)=1+i, S(i)=i, and S(1+i)=(−1+i)/2. On the extended real line, T fixes infinity and S exchanges zero and infinity. On the unit disc, the same maps become C∘T∘C−1 and C∘S∘C−1; the second is simply w↦−w.
Multiplying all four coefficients by a nonzero real scalar leaves f unchanged. Dividing them by Δ​ normalizes the determinant to one; the two remaining representatives A and −A give the same map. Thus the group under function composition is the projective special linear group
Aut(H)≅PSL2​(R)=SL2​(R)/{I,−I},
(22)
where SL2​(R) is the real special linear group of degree two. Composition of the Möbius transformations corresponds to multiplication of their matrices. [ref]
On the boundary,
f′(x)=(cx+d)2Δ​>0(cx+d=0).
(23)
So f preserves the cyclic boundary orientation of the extended real line; when c=0, its pole at −d/c prevents treating it as a single increasing function across that point on the ordinary real line. Three distinct boundary images determine a Möbius transformation uniquely, but an upper half-plane automorphism can realize only triples with matching cyclic boundary orientation.
A brief proof outline starts with the identities
Imf(z)=∣cz+d∣2ΔImz​,f−1(w)=−cw+adw−b​.
(24)
They show that f and its inverse function preserve the upper half-plane. For completeness of the classification, conjugate an arbitrary conformal bijection by the Cayley transform. Normalize its image of zero with an automorphism of the unit disk, then apply the Schwarz lemma to the resulting map and its inverse function; both inequalities force a rotation. Conjugating back gives the real Möbius transformation above.
Finally, ∣f′(z)∣=Δ/∣cz+d∣2 and the displayed imaginary part identity show that ∣df(z)∣/Imf(z)=∣dz∣/Imz. Every upper half-plane automorphism is therefore also an isometry of the Poincaré half-plane model. The same formulas describe both holomorphic coordinate symmetries and motions of the hyperbolic plane.
