Beltrami identity for a second-derivative functional (H=y′′Fy′′​+y′(Fy′​−(Fy′′​)′)−F,H′=−Fx​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Calculus of variations Euler-Lagrange equation for a second-derivative functional
OurBigBook.com Words: 58
For a smooth functional ∫F(x,y,y′,y′′)dx, let P=Fy′​ and Q=Fy′′​. Along an extremal, the Euler-Lagrange equation for a second-derivative functional is Fy​−P′+Q′′=0. Differentiating H=y′′Q+y′(P−Q′)−F by the chain rule gives
H′=y′(P′−Q′′−Fy​)−Fx​=−Fx​.
(1)
Consequently H is constant whenever F has no explicit dependence on x. This extends the Beltrami identity to a second-derivative variational problem. If F is independent of y′′, the expression reduces to y′Fy′​−F.
