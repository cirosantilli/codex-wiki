Geodesics on a circular cylinder (θ=θ0​+cs,z=z0​+ds)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential geometry Surface of revolution Circular cylinder Intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder
OurBigBook.com Words: 292 Articles: 1
In coordinates (θ,z) on a circular cylinder of radius a, the induced metric is a2dθ2+dz2. Its coefficients are constant, so the Christoffel symbols vanish and the affinely parametrized geodesic equation is θ′′=z′′=0. The nonconstant solutions are generators when c=0, circular parallels when d=0, and helices when both are nonzero. Unrolling the cylinder with coordinate u=aθ makes all these curves straight lines in the Euclidean metric. Unit-speed parametrization additionally requires a2c2+d2=1.
For a worked helix on a circular cylinder of radius 1, choose θ(t)=2πt,z(t)=2t for 0≤t≤1. Unrolling gives the straight segment (u,z)=(2πt,2t), so its arc length is 4π2+4​. Its acceleration in space points normally to the surface, consistent with the geodesic equation.
This geodesic is not globally shortest between its endpoints: they share the same angular position, and the vertical generator has length 2. The helix minimizes length among paths with the same endpoints and one full winding, because their unrolled lifts join (0,0) to (2π,2). In general, geodesics minimize sufficiently short subarcs; changing winding can produce a shorter route. Keeping this local/global distinction makes unrolling useful without mistaking every straight lift for an unrestricted shortest path.
