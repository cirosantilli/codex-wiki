Decimal representation (N=∑j=0n​dj​10j)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Integer Positional notation Decimal
OurBigBook.com Words: 247 Articles: 1
A decimal representation is positional notation with radix ten and numeral digits zero through nine. For an integer, the nonnegative part is a finite decimal expansion; a negative integer has a separate minus sign. For example, 205=2⋅100+0⋅10+5, whereas 250=2⋅100+5⋅10+0. The numeral digits alone do not determine the value: their positions do.
Fractional positions extend the same place value rule to the right of the decimal point, with successive weights 1/10,1/100,1/1000,…. [ref] For example,
205.375=205+103​+1007​+10005​=205+83​.
(7)
The matching binary representation is 11001101.0112​, since its fractional part is 0⋅2−1+1⋅2−2+1⋅2−3=3/8. These representations terminate in both bases; finiteness in one radix does not imply finiteness in another.
Leading zeros in the integer part and trailing zeros in the fractional part leave the value unchanged: 00205.3750=205.375. A zero appended to the right of an integer string has a different effect: 2050=10⋅205. Keeping the decimal point fixed prevents confusing these two operations.
Every integer has a finite decimal expansion, but a noninteger rational number may need infinitely many fractional numeral digits, as in 1/3=0.333…. A terminating decimal has a zero tail by convention. Infinite decimal strings have a further ambiguity that finite canonical integer strings do not: 0.999…=1, because the geometric series ∑j=1∞​9⋅10−j sums to one. An infinite decimal tail denotes a limit, not a sufficiently long rounded approximation.
