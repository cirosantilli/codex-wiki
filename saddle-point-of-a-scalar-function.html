Saddle point of a scalar function (∇f(x∗​)=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Critical point
OurBigBook.com Words: 301 Articles: 2
A saddle point of a scalar function is a stationary point with nearby values both greater and less than its value. An indefinite Hessian matrix proves this by choosing directions with opposite signs of the quadratic term. A degenerate Hessian matrix may require higher-order terms: x2−y4 has a saddle point of a scalar function at the origin despite a zero quadratic coefficient in the y direction. This is distinct from a saddle equilibrium of a dynamical system.
