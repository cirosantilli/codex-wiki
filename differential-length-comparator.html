Differential length comparator

 Home Physics Metrology Dimensional metrology
OurBigBook.com Words: 308 Articles: 1
An instrument for comparing two lengths through their difference. Suppose a test rod and reference both have length L0​ at temperature T0​, and have constant linear thermal expansion coefficients αs​ and αr​ over a small interval. Their first-order models give
Ls​(T)=L0​[1+αs​(T−T0​)],Lr​(T)=L0​[1+αr​(T−T0​)].
(1)
For the observed difference D(T)=Ls​(T)−Lr​(T), the derivative b=dD/dT therefore determines
αs​=αr​+L0​b​​.
(2)
The reference's independently calibrated linear thermal expansion coefficient is essential: the difference alone identifies αs​−αr​, not either coefficient separately.
For an original numerical example, stipulate L0​=1.00m, αs​=1.0×10−6K−1 and αr​=9.0×10−6K−1. Heating both by 10K gives sample and reference increases of 10μm and 90μm. Thus the sample expands even though its measured difference decreases by 80μm. These are chosen model parameters, not measured values for a historical Invar specimen or reference.
If αr​ and b have independent standard uncertainties 0.2×10−6K−1 and 0.3μmK−1, and uncertainty in L0​ is negligible, variance additivity for independent random variables gives
u(αs​)=u(αr​)2+L02​u(b)2​​≃0.36×10−6K−1.
(3)
Repeating the difference measurement can reduce its random contribution; it does not remove uncertainty in the reference. A temperature gradient or unequal rod temperatures also breaks the common-temperature model and can introduce a systematic error.
