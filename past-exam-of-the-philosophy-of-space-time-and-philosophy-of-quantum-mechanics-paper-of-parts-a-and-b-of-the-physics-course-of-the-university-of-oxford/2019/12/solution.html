The Pusey-Barrett-Rudolph theorem excludes overlapping preparation distributions for distinct pure states in an ontological model of a quantum system satisfying preparation independence and the specified quantum predictions. It excludes neither all hidden variables nor every informational use of a quantum state. The precise overlap criterion is essential: a psi-epistemic model lets at least two distinct pure-state preparations assign positive common probability mass to the same underlying states; a psi-ontic model has mutually singular measures for each distinct pair.[ref]
Let preparation x sample λ∈Λ from μx​. A fixed quantum measurement M has nonnegative response functions ξM​(j∣λ) with ∑j​ξM​(j∣λ)=1. Averaging over the preparation must reproduce the Born rule. The response is fixed by M and the complete underlying state, not by additional knowledge of the preparation label. No outcome determinism or Bell local causality is assumed; the joint measurement response need not factor into local responses. For independently operated preparations, preparation independence further identifies the joint state space with the appropriate product and assigns
μx1​…xn​​(λ1​,…,λn​)=∏k=1n​μxk​​(λk​).
(24)
Operationally independent devices do not logically entail this ontic factorization; it is an additional physical premise.[ref]
The two-qubit example can be checked directly. Set ∣±⟩=(∣0⟩±∣1⟩)/2​. In computational basis order 00,01,10,11, take the following normalized columns as an orthonormal basis:
ξ1​=2​(0,1,1,0)T​,ξ2​=2(1,−1,1,1)T​,ξ3​=2(1,1,−1,1)T​,ξ4​=2​(1,0,0,−1)T​.
(25)
Their pairwise inner products vanish. Direct multiplication shows that the respective preparations 00,0+,+0,++ have zero probability amplitude for respective outcomes 1,2,3,4. This is the PBR exclusion measurement for zero and plus: the measurement does not perfectly distinguish the preparations, but each outcome rules one out.
Suppose a common submeasure ν of μ0​ and μ+​ has mass ω>0; with densities one can take ν(λ)=min{μ0​(λ),μ+​(λ)}. Preparation independence makes ν⊗ν a common submeasure of all four product preparations, of mass ω2. The zero probability for outcome j under its excluded preparation and nonnegativity force
∫ξM​(j∣λ1​,λ2​)d(ν⊗ν)=0(j=1,2,3,4).
(26)
Summing and using normalization yields ω2=0, a contradiction. This measure formulation avoids treating a set with merely nonempty topological support as a positive-probability overlap.
For arbitrary distinct nonorthogonal pure states, write ∣ψ0,1​⟩=cos(θ/2)∣0⟩±sin(θ/2)∣1⟩, with overlap cosθ and 0<θ<π/2. The general proof constructs an exclusion measurement on n independently prepared copies when 21/n−1≤tan(θ/2). A finite n always exists. The same common-submeasure contradiction then excludes positive overlap. Orthogonal preparations are already disjoint if their perfectly distinguishing measurement is reproduced. The original paper also bounds residual overlap for imperfect exclusion data; exact disjointness concerns the ideal theorem, not a finite experiment's proof of exact zero.[ref]
The scope is consequently sharp. Bohmian mechanics is not excluded: its underlying state includes the wave function, making distinct pure preparations disjoint even though extra configuration variables remain. A psi-ontic model need not be complete with the wave function alone, and the theorem does not identify the wave function with a three-dimensional material field. Models abandoning preparation independence, or accounts not representing preparations as uncertainty over observer-independent states of this specified kind, are outside this conclusion. Epistemic uncertainty between mixed preparations is not banned. Nor does the theorem establish a collapse mechanism, choose between Bohmian and Everettian theories, or solve the measurement problem. Its achievement is a conditional impossibility theorem about a precise form of pure-state epistemic overlap.
