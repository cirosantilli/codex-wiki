Use the complex velocity q(z)=w′(z)=u−iv. For this steady, constant-mass density, irrotational flow with no body force, the applicable Bernoulli equation is
p+2ρ​∣q∣2=P∗​,
(21)
with a single constant P∗​ throughout the connected regular fluid region. The body's impermeability makes the velocity tangent to C. Thus qdz is real there, giving
qdz=qˉ​dzˉ,∣q∣2dzˉ=q2dz.
(22)
The complex force from part (a) is
Fx​−iFy​​=−i∮C​pdzˉ=2iρ​∮C​∣q∣2dzˉ=2iρ​∮C​q2dz,​
(23)
where the constant-P∗​ term vanishes. This proves the Blasius force theorem:
Fx​−iFy​=2iρ​∮C​(w′(z))2dz.​
(24)
For the moment, observe that
zdzˉ=(xdx+ydy)+i(ydx−xdy).
(25)
Therefore M=Re∮C​pzdzˉ. The constant pressure contribution has zero real part, because xdx+ydy=21​d(x2+y2). Substituting Bernoulli's principle and the same tangency identity gives the Blasius moment theorem:
M=−2ρ​Re∮C​z(w′(z))2dz.​
(26)
The real-part operation is essential: ∮zdzˉ itself need not vanish, but its real part does.
