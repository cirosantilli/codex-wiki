a OurBigBook.com
An irrotational flow has ∇×u=0. Hence a velocity potential ϕ exists locally, and globally on a simply connected domain, with u=∇ϕ. The exterior of a sphere in three dimensions has this property. Constant mass density reduces the continuity equation to ∇⋅u=0, so
∇2ϕ=0​.
(1)
Thus the velocity potential is a harmonic function. For the Euler equations, use the vorticity cross-product identity
(u⋅∇)u=∇(21​∣u∣2)−u×(∇×u).
(2)
The last term vanishes. Since ∂t​u=∇ϕt​, the Euler momentum equation becomes
∇(ϕt​+21​∣∇ϕ∣2+ρp​)=0.
(3)
On each connected fluid region, a function with zero gradient is spatially constant. Therefore the Unsteady Bernoulli equation is
ρp​+ϕt​+21​∣∇ϕ∣2=F(t)​.
(4)
The freedom to add a function of time to the velocity potential changes F(t) but leaves the velocity field unchanged. On a multiply connected region, zero vorticity alone guarantees only local potentials; a global single-valued velocity potential additionally requires zero circulation around every closed path.

∂r​(r2ϕr​)=0,ϕr​=r2A(t)​,ϕ=G(t)−rA(t)​.

ϕ(r,t)=G(t)−rR2R˙​,u=r2R2R˙​r​.
(6)
Equivalently, its volumetric flow rate through any concentric sphere is 4πr2ur​=4πR2R˙, the rate of change of the enclosed volume.

Use W=ϕ+iψ for the complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow, so W′(z)=u−iv. The Milne-Thomson circle theorem says that if f(z) is an incident complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow whose singularities lie outside ∣z∣=a, then
W(z)=f(z)+f(zˉa2​)​
(15)
makes ∣z∣=a a streamline. Indeed, on that circle a2/zˉ=z, so W=f+fˉ​ is real and the stream function is constant. This imposes the no-penetration boundary condition.
For the two-dimensional potential doublet, put M=meiα and f=M/(z−b), where b>a is real. The theorem gives
W(z)=z−bmeiα​+a2−bzme−iαz​​.
(16)
Up to an additive constant, this is equivalently
W(z)=z−bmeiα​−b2(z−a2/b)ma2e−iα​.
(17)
The method of images has placed the image two-dimensional potential doublet at a2/b, inside the obstacle. We use the solution with zero added circulation and no imposed far-field flow; the original doublet is the only singularity in the liquid.

The complex potential of a two-dimensional potential flow gives
q(z):=W′(z)=−(z−b)2meiα​+(bz−a2)2ma2e−iα​.
(18)
At a stagnation point, q=0. For m=0, taking the two square-root alternatives gives
bz−a2=σae−iα(z−b),σ∈{+1,−1}.
(19)
Therefore
zσ​=ab−σae−iαa−σbe−iα​,σ=±1​.
(20)
The denominators are nonzero because b>a. Moreover
∣a−σbe−iα∣2=a2+b2−2σabcosα=∣b−σae−iα∣2,
(21)
so both stagnation points satisfy ∣zσ​∣=a. They are distinct: equality would require 2e−iα(a2−b2)=0. Neither is a singularity of q. Conversely, the numerator of q is a quadratic polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient, so these are all its finite zeros. Thus there are exactly two boundary stagnation points and none in the open fluid region. If m=0, the flow instead vanishes everywhere, which is the degenerate exception to the assertion.

For q=W′=u−iv, the Blasius force theorem states that the force per unit length exerted by a steady incompressible flow and irrotational flow on an impermeable body is
X−iY=2iρ​∮C​q(z)2dz,
(22)
where C runs counterclockwise around the body and excludes external flow singularities. The sign corresponds to the force on the body.
Let c=a2/b and A=ma2e−iα/b2. The complex velocity may be written
q(z)=(z−c)2A​+H(z),H(z)=−(z−b)2meiα​.
(23)
Only the image point c lies inside the circular contour. The term A2/(z−c)4 has zero residue; H2 is regular there. In the cross term, the linear term of the Taylor expansion of H supplies
Resz=c​q2=2AH′(c)=b2(c−b)34m2a2​=−(b2−a2)34m2a2b​.
(24)

X−iY=−πρResz=c​q2,X=(b2−a2)34πρm2a2b​,Y=0​.
(25)
Since b>a, X>0: the obstacle is pulled toward the doublet, independently of α. This is a force per unit length perpendicular to the plane of flow.

Keep the prescribed orientation α and strength m fixed, and now let the doublet position b(t) be complex. The Milne-Thomson circle theorem applies instantaneously with
W(z,t)=z−bM​+a2−bˉzMˉz​,M=meiα.

b˙=(∣b∣2−a2)2ma2eiα​​.
(28)
Its direction is constant. Indeed Im(be−iα) is constant and, since b(0)=b0​>a is real, equals −b0​sinα. Writing b=reiθ in polar coordinates gives the path equation
rsin(θ−α)=−b0​sinα​,r(θ)=sin(α−θ)b0​sinα​​
(29)
where the quotient is used when sinα=0 and the branch through (r,θ)=(b0​,0) with r>a is selected. It is the straight line through b0​ parallel to eiα. An unambiguous parametrization, also covering the degenerate polar cases, is
b=b0​+seiα,s˙=(∣b0​+seiα∣2−a2)2ma2​,s(0)=0.

a OurBigBook.com
For a plane wave with amplitude α, wavenumber k>0 and angular frequency ω>0, the dispersion relation ω=ω(k) specifies which frequencies the governing equations permit. A point of fixed wave phase obeys kx−ωt=constant, so the phase speed is
cp​=kω​​.
(31)
It is the speed of individual crests. For a narrow wave packet, two nearby plane waves with differences Δk and Δω have a slowly varying wave envelope depending on Δkx−Δωt. Taking the limit gives the group velocity
cg​=dkdω​​.
(32)
This is the envelope's propagation speed and, in the present linear lossless wave model, the energy transport speed for a narrow packet.
A dispersive wave has phase speed dependent on wavenumber, so different components can change their relative phases and alter the packet's shape. A nondispersive wave on the chosen propagation branch has constant phase speed over the relevant band:
ω(k)=ck,cp​=cg​=c​.
(33)
Indeed dcp​/dk=(cg​−cp​)/k, so equality throughout a band is equivalent to constant phase speed there. Equality at an isolated wavenumber does not make the entire wave family nondispersive.

At the rigid bed, the no-penetration boundary condition gives
ϕz​=0at z=−h​.
(35)
The displaced surface is material, so taking the material derivative of z−η(x,t) gives the exact kinematic boundary condition for a free-surface graph:
ηt​+ϕx​ηx​=ϕz​at z=η(x,t)​.
(36)
With upward vertical coordinate z, the gravitational potential per unit mass is gz. The Unsteady Bernoulli equation, evaluated on the surface with the prescribed pressure p=Bηxxxx​, gives
ϕt​+21​(ϕx2​+ϕz2​)+gη+ρB​ηxxxx​=0at z=η(x,t)​.
(37)
Here the spatially uniform time-dependent Bernoulli term has been absorbed into the velocity potential. The pressure is measured relative to the constant exterior pressure. This dynamic boundary condition uses the specified massless bending-cover model, with no ice inertia or tension.

Linearize about the resting state η=0, ϕ=0. Introduce a dimensionless small parameter ϵ and write
η=ϵη1​+O(ϵ2),ϕ=ϵϕ1​+O(ϵ2),
(38)
holding the depth, bending stiffness and variation scales fixed. Thus both boundary displacement and induced velocity are small. For any first-order derivative Dϕ, a Taylor expansion at the moving surface gives
(Dϕ)(x,η,t)=ϵ(Dϕ1​)(x,0,t)+O(ϵ2).
(39)
The displacement correction to the leading field is ϵ2η1​∂z​(Dϕ1​)(x,0,t), so evaluating it on z=0 changes it only at second order. Also ϕx​ηx​=O(ϵ2) and ∣∇ϕ∣2=O(ϵ2). The first-order kinematic boundary condition and dynamic boundary condition are therefore
(η1​)t​=(ϕ1​)z​(x,0,t),(ϕ1​)t​(x,0,t)+gη1​+ρB​(η1​)xxxx​=0.
(40)
Dropping the subscript, the linearized boundary condition pair is
ηt​=ϕz​,ϕt​+gη+ρB​ηxxxx​=0at z=0​.
(41)
The fixed-bed condition remains ϕz​(x,−h,t)=0. Small height alone, with arbitrarily rapid spatial variation or a finite background disturbance, does not justify discarding the products.

Let χ=kx−ωt, with k>0. The surface elevation is η=αcosχ, so ηt​=αωsinχ. Use separation of variables in the interior Laplace equation:
ϕ=Acosh(k(z+h))sinχ.
(42)
The sinh(k(z+h)) term is excluded by the bed no-penetration boundary condition. The linear surface kinematic boundary condition determines
Aksinh(kh)=αω,ϕ(x,z,t)=ksinh(kh)αω​cosh(k(z+h))sin(kx−ωt)​.
(43)
An irrelevant additive constant is omitted. At z=0,
ϕt​=−kαω2​coth(kh)cosχ,ηxxxx​=αk4cosχ.
(44)
The linear dynamic boundary condition then reduces to
−kω2​coth(kh)+g+ρB​k4=0.
(45)
Consequently the dispersion relation of a floating elastic plate with zero plate inertia is
ω2=(gk+ρB​k5)tanh(kh),ω(k)=(gk+ρB​k5)tanh(kh)​​.
(46)
The negative-frequency branch gives propagation in the opposite direction. For signed wavenumber, replace k by ∣k∣ in the squared-frequency formula. Setting B=0 recovers the finite-depth surface gravity wave relation, a useful sign check.
