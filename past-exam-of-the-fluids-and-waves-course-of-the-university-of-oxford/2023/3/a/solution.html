For a plane wave with amplitude α, wavenumber k>0 and angular frequency ω>0, the dispersion relation ω=ω(k) specifies which frequencies the governing equations permit. A point of fixed wave phase obeys kx−ωt=constant, so the phase speed is
cp​=kω​​.
(31)
It is the speed of individual crests. For a narrow wave packet, two nearby plane waves with differences Δk and Δω have a slowly varying wave envelope depending on Δkx−Δωt. Taking the limit gives the group velocity
cg​=dkdω​​.
(32)
This is the envelope's propagation speed and, in the present linear lossless wave model, the energy transport speed for a narrow packet.
A dispersive wave has phase speed dependent on wavenumber, so different components can change their relative phases and alter the packet's shape. A nondispersive wave on the chosen propagation branch has constant phase speed over the relevant band:
ω(k)=ck,cp​=cg​=c​.
(33)
Indeed dcp​/dk=(cg​−cp​)/k, so equality throughout a band is equivalent to constant phase speed there. Equality at an isolated wavenumber does not make the entire wave family nondispersive.
