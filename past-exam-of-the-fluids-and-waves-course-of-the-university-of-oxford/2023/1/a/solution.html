An irrotational flow has ∇×u=0. Hence a velocity potential ϕ exists locally, and globally on a simply connected domain, with u=∇ϕ. The exterior of a sphere in three dimensions has this property. Constant mass density reduces the continuity equation to ∇⋅u=0, so
∇2ϕ=0​.
(1)
Thus the velocity potential is a harmonic function. For the Euler equations, use the vorticity cross-product identity
(u⋅∇)u=∇(21​∣u∣2)−u×(∇×u).
(2)
The last term vanishes. Since ∂t​u=∇ϕt​, the Euler momentum equation becomes
∇(ϕt​+21​∣∇ϕ∣2+ρp​)=0.
(3)
On each connected fluid region, a function with zero gradient is spatially constant. Therefore the Unsteady Bernoulli equation is
ρp​+ϕt​+21​∣∇ϕ∣2=F(t)​.
(4)
The freedom to add a function of time to the velocity potential changes F(t) but leaves the velocity field unchanged. On a multiply connected region, zero vorticity alone guarantees only local potentials; a global single-valued velocity potential additionally requires zero circulation around every closed path.
