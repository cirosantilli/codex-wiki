Linearize about fluid at rest, with mean free surface y=0 and mean bottom y=−d. Regard a, η and the perturbation velocity potential as first-order quantities. A Taylor expansion from y=η to y=0 produces products of first-order quantities, as do ϕx​ηx​ and ∣∇ϕ∣2. Discarding these second-order terms gives the linearized free-surface boundary conditions
ϕy​=ηt​,ϕt​+gη=0(y=0).​
(42)
Equivalently, differentiate the second condition in time and substitute the first to obtain ϕtt​+gϕy​=0 at y=0.
The bottom is also impermeable and moving. Its exact kinematic boundary condition follows from D(y−h)/Dt=0:
ϕy​=ht​+ϕx​hx​(y=h).
(43)
Here ht​=−akccos(k(x−ct)), while ϕx​hx​ and the change from evaluating ϕy​ at h to evaluating it at −d are second order. Hence
ϕy​(x,−d,t)=−akccos(k(x−ct)).​
(44)
The interior Laplace equation remains ϕxx​+ϕyy​=0 on −d<y<0. This linearization requires small slopes and small resulting excursions as well as a≪d; close to resonance a small prescribed bottom displacement can produce a response too large for it.
