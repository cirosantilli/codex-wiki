Let V(t) be a smoothly moving material volume, carried by a smooth velocity field u, and let f be continuously differentiable. The material-volume form of the Reynolds transport theorem states
dtd​∫V(t)​fdV=∫V(t)​(DtDf​+f∇⋅u)dV.​
(2)
Equivalently, the divergence theorem gives
dtd​∫V(t)​fdV=∫V(t)​∂t​fdV+∫∂V(t)​fu⋅ndS,
(3)
where n is the outward unit normal vector. The second integral measures the flux carried across the instantaneous boundary by its motion.
For the proof, use the particle map x=X(a,t) from V(0), with positive Jacobian determinant J=det(∂X/∂a). Euler's volume identity is
∂t​J=J∇⋅u.
(4)
It is the Jacobian evolution of a smooth flow: if F=∂X/∂a, differentiation gives Ft​=(∇u)F, and differentiation of the determinant gives Jt​=Jtr(F−1Ft​)=J∇⋅u. The change of variables formula now moves the integral to the fixed reference volume:
dtd​∫V(t)​fdV​=dtd​∫V(0)​f(X(a,t),t)Jda=∫V(0)​(DtDf​J+fJt​)da=∫V(t)​(DtDf​+f∇⋅u)dV.​
(5)
Finally, Df/Dt+f∇⋅u=∂t​f+∇⋅(fu), giving the equivalent boundary form.
