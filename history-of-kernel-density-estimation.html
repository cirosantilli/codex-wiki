History of kernel density estimation

OurBigBook.com Words: 166
Murray Rosenblatt's 1956 paper studied density estimation from a one-dimensional statistical sample, the unavoidable bias of an estimator under its stated conditions, and asymptotic mean squared error. It is an early foundation of kernel density estimation. [ref]. Emanuel Parzen's 1962 paper developed statistical consistency and asymptotic normality for probability density function estimates and studied estimation of the mode. [ref].
The authors' interests help explain the connection with smoothing. In a conversation recorded in 2006 and published in 2009, Rosenblatt recalled that his density estimation work grew out of estimation of the time-series spectral density and emphasized the connection with a smoothed histogram. This is his retrospective account of the motivation. [ref]. Parzen's original paper lists Stanford University as his affiliation; its opening frames the problem as recovering both a probability density function and its mode from independent and identically distributed random variables. [ref].
