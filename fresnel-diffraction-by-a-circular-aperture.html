Fresnel diffraction by a circular aperture

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fresnel diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 93
For a circular aperture of radius a, paraxial plane-wave illumination and propagation distance z, define NF​=a2/(λz). On axis, the radial Fresnel diffraction integral normalized by the unobstructed field gives
U0​Ua​​=1−eiπNF​,I0​Ia​​=4sin2(2πNF​​).
(3)
For a finite axial point source a distance s before the aperture and detector a distance r after it, replace z by sr/(s+r) in the phase. The formula assumes illumination amplitudes and obliquity factors approximately constant across the aperture. Odd and even numbers of Fresnel half-period zones give bright and dark axial extrema; large local intensity is compatible with global conservation of energy.
