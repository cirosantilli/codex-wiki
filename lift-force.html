Lift (force)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 4
Lift is the component of the fluid force perpendicular to the far-field flow relative to the body. It need not point vertically: a tilted flow changes the lift direction, and a banked wing can produce a sideways component. In a planar problem choose a unit vector eD​ along the incoming relative velocity U∞​ and a perpendicular unit vector eL​ pointing towards the chosen positive-lift side. Then
U∞​=∣U∞​∣,D=F⋅eD​,L=F⋅eL​,F=DeD​+LeL​.
(3)
Here D is drag and L is signed lift; L<0 means a force towards the opposite side. In three dimensions the plane perpendicular to U∞​ also admits a separate side-force direction, so a lift axis must be specified. Lift is also distinct from buoyancy, which can exist without a relative flow. [ref]
The force comes from adding local surface tractions, rather than assigning a single pressure to each side. Let Σ be the wetted body surface and n its unit normal vector pointing out of the body into the fluid. The pressure contribution pushes inwards; the viscous stress tensor also contributes, including tangential shear stress:
F=∫Σ​[−(p−p∞​)n+σvisc​n]dA,L=∫Σ​[−(p−p∞​)n+σvisc​n]⋅eL​dA.
(4)
Using a constant reference pressure p∞​ is legitimate on a closed surface because ∫Σ​ndA=0. This surface integral makes the direction explicit: a surface-normal force is generally neither pure lift nor pure drag. Pressure and skin shear stress both enter the resultant, although the pressure term usually dominates lift on an airfoil. [ref]
The lift coefficient isolates a useful scale. For a finite wing of reference area S (normally its projected planform area), and for a two-dimensional airfoil section of chord length c, respectively,
q∞​=21​ρ∞​U∞2​,CL​=q∞​SL​,cl​=q∞​cL′​.
(5)
Here q∞​ is dynamic pressure, ρ∞​ is far-field mass density, and L′ is lift per unit span, with units Nm−1. The signs of CL​ and cl​ follow the chosen lift axis. For stipulated values ρ∞​=1.20kgm−3, U∞​=20ms−1, S=0.50m2 and CL​=0.60, q∞​=240Pa and L=72N. Doubling U∞​ gives four times the lift only if CL​ stays fixed: changes in Reynolds number, Mach number, surface condition or angle of attack can change it. [ref]
A motivating experiment is to compare two independently observed quantities: the lift registered by a force balance, and the lift inferred from surface pressures. A low-speed wind tunnel holds the model fixed while air passes it at adjustable speed; a calibrated balance measures forces, while small flush holes on both surfaces connect by tubing to differential-pressure sensors referenced to p∞​. Iowa State's GA(W)-1 teaching model has 43 taps around a 101mm chord; its instructions give their coordinates and close the contour by repeating the first tap. A classroom comparison can combine this pressure-panel method with a balance on the same model; this is an experiment design, not a claim that those instructions report a combined measurement. [ref] [ref]
Before turning on the wind, apply known positive and negative loads to the balance through its resolving centre; use mg for the known load from a mass. Record all balance channels during loading and unloading, including response to drag and torque loads, to detect cross coupling, hysteresis and zero drift. Record a wind-off tare at each model orientation so its weight does not become an aerodynamic lift reading. Map tunnel flow direction and uniformity, rather than using the fan setting as a speed measurement. Covert's MIT guide describes these calibration, tare and flow-survey requirements. [ref]
For each pressure channel, apply zero and several known pressure differences spanning the expected range; determine the voltage-to-pressure relation and recheck it after a run. Inspect tubing for leaks and blocked taps, and wait for readings to settle before averaging: tubing can delay a pressure signal. This calibration principle is used in NASA's electronically scanned pressure systems, where several applied calibration pressures determine each sensor's response and repeated calibration tracks drift. A simple pressure standard can instead use a liquid-column height difference and the hydrostatic pressure law. [ref]
Measure far-field static and total pressure outside the model's fluid wake; in low-speed incompressible flow their difference gives q∞​ by the Bernoulli equation, so U∞​=2q∞​/ρ∞​​. Record temperature and ambient pressure to determine mass density using the ideal gas law. At fixed q∞​, step the angle of attack, repeat selected settings, and sweep back down to check reproducibility. Record balance lift, each tap's mean pressure, and fluctuation sizes. NASA describes pressure probes connected to differential transducers and emphasizes recording their reference pressure. [ref]
Convert each tap to a pressure coefficient Cp,i​=(pi​−p∞​)/q∞​. For consecutive taps bounding a panel of length Δsi​ with outward normal vector ni​, a trapezoidal rule estimate of the pressure force per unit span is
Fp′​≃−q∞​∑i​2Cp,i​+Cp,i+1​​ni​Δsi​,Lp′​=Fp′​⋅eL​.
(6)
Compare this with balance lift divided by span only when the spanwise flow is approximately uniform. Agreement tests the pressure-resultant picture; disagreement calls for checking unresolved leading-edge pressure variations, skin shear stress, support loads and three-dimensional end effects before rejecting it. A force balance senses the total load, while taps alone reconstruct the pressure part. Tunnel walls and model blockage alter the effective flow and require corrections; end plates or a model extending between walls approximate two-dimensional flow but do not guarantee it. Record the Reynolds number and Mach number with the result, and do not interpret tunnel lift coefficients as universal wing data. [ref] [ref]
