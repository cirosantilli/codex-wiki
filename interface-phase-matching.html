Interface phase matching (ki,∥​=kt,∥​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Refraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 214
At a stationary, uniform planar boundary, incident and transmitted plane waves have the same variation of wave phase along the interface and in time. The figure marks places where equal-wave phase wavefronts meet the boundary. Although their perpendicular spacing differs, their intersections must advance along the boundary together. This is the local mechanism behind Snell's law.[ref]
For a short derivation, let x run along the interface and z increase into the transmitted medium. Write each wave phase as Φj​=kj,x​x+kj,z​z−ωj​t+ϕj​. The electromagnetic boundary conditions must hold at every x,t on z=0; their matching requires ωi​=ωt​ and ki,x​=kt,x​. Constant offsets ϕj​ and wave amplitudes are determined separately. Since kj,x​=kj​sinθj​, this gives
cni​ω​sinθi​=cnt​ω​sinθt​.
(16)
For nonzero tangential wavenumber, the spacing of equal-wave phase intersections along the interface is λi​/sinθi​=λt​/sinθt​. It is not the perpendicular wavelength. At normal incidence, the tangential wavenumber vanishes and the interface is uniform in wave phase.[ref]
