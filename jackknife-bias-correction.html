Jackknife bias correction (TJ​=nTn​−(n−1)TJ​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference
OurBigBook.com Words: 521 Articles: 3
The jackknife bias correction uses jackknife resampling to estimate and subtract the bias of an estimator. Here Tn​ denotes the original estimator, T(−i)​ its version after deleting observation i, and TJ​=n−1∑i=1n​T(−i)​ the mean of those versions. For sample size n>1, define
Bn​=(n−1)(TJ​−Tn​),TJ​=Tn​−Bn​=nTn​−(n−1)TJ​.
(41)
The sign matters: a negative estimated bias increases the corrected value. The original estimator and all deletion versions must target the same estimand and use the same estimation rule at their respective sample sizes. The definition alone does not make TJ​ an unbiased estimator.
For an exact worked example, take the empirical variance
Tn​=n1​∑i=1n​(Xi​−Xn​)2.
(42)
On (1,2,3,4,10), the sample mean is 4 and T5​=50/5=10. Each deletion empirical variance is recomputed around its own deletion sample mean, with divisor 4, giving
(T(−1)​,…,T(−5)​)=(9.6875,11.25,12.1875,12.5,1.25),TJ​=9.375.
(43)
For instance, deleting 10 leaves (1,2,3,4), whose sample mean is 2.5 and whose empirical variance is 1.25. The correction on the full statistical sample is
B5​=4(9.375−10)=−2.5,TJ​=10−(−2.5)=12.5.
(44)
The same data thus give a jackknife variance estimator of 2.5 for the sample mean, and a corrected empirical variance of 12.5; these are different estimands.
The exact equality with the usual sample variance is not an accident of these numbers. For any data with n>1, write Q=∑i​(xi​−x)2=nTn​. Removing xi​ changes this sum to Q−n(xi​−x)2/(n−1), so
T(−i)​=n−1Q​−(n−1)2n(xi​−x)2​,TJ​=(n−1)2n(n−2)​Tn​,TJ​=n−1n​Tn​=sn2​.
(45)
For independent and identically distributed random variables with finite second moment and population variance σ2, the expectation is ETn​=(1−1/n)σ2. Its bias is exactly −σ2/n; jackknife bias correction removes it exactly in this example. Thus sn2​ is an unbiased estimator of σ2 under these assumptions, including n=2 where each deletion empirical variance is zero.
More generally, suppose the bias has a regular expansion at both sample sizes n and n−1, with coefficients a,b independent of n:
Bn​=ETn​−θ=na​+n2b​+O(n−3).
(46)
Under independent and identically distributed sampling, each deletion estimator has the same expectation as Tn−1​. Hence the corrected bias is
ETJ​−θ=nBn​−(n−1)Bn−1​=O(n−2),nna​−(n−1)n−1a​=0.
(47)
The leading O(n−1) term cancels; the remaining bias need not vanish. The O notation is big O notation. This argument presumes the expansion, rather than proving it for every statistic; the regularity conditions discussed for the jackknife variance estimator and statistical dependence still require care. Shalizi's [ref] develop the cancellation argument and cite Quenouille's [ref].
