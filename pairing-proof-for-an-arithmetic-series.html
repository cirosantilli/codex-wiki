Pairing proof for an arithmetic series (ak​+an+1−k​=a1​+an​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Arithmetic progression Arithmetic series
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
Reverse the finite list of sequence terms and add it to the original list. At position k,
ak​+an+1−k​=(a1​+(k−1)d)+(a1​+(n−k)d)=2a1​+(n−1)d=a1​+an​.
(40)
Every position therefore contributes the same value. Reversal preserves the sum, giving 2Sn​=n(a1​+an​) and the stated formula. The symmetry explains why the endpoint average represents the whole list; one does not need to add each entry separately.
The reversal argument works for both even and odd lengths. For 2,5,8,11,14, the two outer pairs each total sixteen and the middle entry is eight. Adding the list to its reverse counts that middle entry twice too, giving 2S5​=5×16 and S5​=40. No half-entry is required for an odd number of entries.
