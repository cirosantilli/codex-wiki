Endpoint mixture identity for a monotone weighted integral (∫ab​fg=f(a)∫ad​g+f(b)∫db​g)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Riemann integration Riemann integral Weighted mean value theorem for integrals
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
If f,g:[a,b]→R are continuous functions, g≥0, and f is a nondecreasing monotonic function, there is d∈[a,b] satisfying the identity in the title. It expresses the weighted integral as a mixture of the two endpoint values of f, with the split occurring in the accumulated weight.
Put G=∫ab​g and I=∫ab​fg. The monotonicity of the Riemann integral gives f(a)G≤I≤f(b)G. The function H(x)=∫ax​g is continuous; indeed, ∣H(y)−H(x)∣≤max[a,b]​∣g∣∣y−x∣ by the extreme value theorem and the triangle inequality for the Riemann integral. Therefore
K(x)=f(a)H(x)+f(b)(G−H(x))
(89)
is continuous, with K(a)=f(b)G and K(b)=f(a)G. The intermediate value theorem gives K(d)=I. If the endpoint values coincide, the bounds force I to have that common value and an endpoint works. The proof covers zero total weight and a degenerate interval without division.
