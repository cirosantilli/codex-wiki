Coarse-grid correction (uh​←uh​+PAH−1​R(bh​−Ah​uh​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Multigrid method
OurBigBook.com Words: 670 Articles: 1
A coarse-grid correction improves a fine-grid approximation by solving a smaller equation for the part of its approximation error representable on a coarse mesh. Use the signed convention eh​=uh∗​−vh​ and the linear-system residual rh​=bh​−Ah​vh​=Ah​eh​. A prolongation linear map P:Rm→Rn interpolates coarse corrections into fine-grid corrections, and a restriction linear map R:Rn→Rm transfers the linear-system residual. Solve AH​zH​=Rrh​ exactly or approximately and update vh​←vh​+PzH​. The plus sign follows from defining the approximation error as exact minus approximate. It is the correction equation's right-hand side Rrh​, rather than merely restricted solution values, that determines the update.
For the multigrid method model Ah​=h−2tridiag(−1,2,−1), take n=2m+1, so H=2h and the coarse nodes coincide with every second fine node. Use linear interpolation with zero boundary corrections z0​=zm+1​=0:
(Pz)2i​=zi​(1≤i≤m),(Pz)2i+1​=2zi​+zi+1​​(0≤i≤m).
(91)
Use full-weighting restriction for the interior linear-system residual:
(Rr)i​=4r2i−1​+2r2i​+r2i+1​​(1≤i≤m),R=21​PT.
(92)
Here PT is the matrix transpose in ordinary unweighted coordinates. With these specific transfers, the Galerkin coarse-grid operator is
AH​=RAh​P=H−2tridiag(−1,2,−1).
(93)
Thus it equals the rediscretized Poisson equation operator on this constant-coefficient one-dimensional mesh. The equality is special to the stated model and transfers. In particular, dropping h−2 from the fine matrix while using H−2 on the coarse mesh changes the correction unless the right-hand sides and transfers are rescaled consistently.
Take seven interior points, h=1/8, three coarse unknowns, and the deliberately constructed approximation error
z=(1,2,1)T,eh​=Pz=(1/2,1,3/2,2,3/2,1,1/2)T.
(94)
For example, choose bh​=0, so uh∗​=0 and vh​=−eh​. Direct matrix multiplication gives
rh​=Ah​eh​=(0,0,0,64,0,0,0)T,Rrh​=(0,32,0)T,AH​=16​2−10​−12−1​0−12​​.
(95)
The exact coarse solve returns zH​=z, so adding PzH​ removes the entire chosen approximation error. A broad triangular error can have a sharply localized linear-system residual: the latter measures discrete curvature, not error height. This is why the two should not be confused. A smooth sine mode instead has linear-system residual λk​(Ah​)eh​, so interpreting its magnitude also requires the eigenvalue and the equation's scaling.
The algebra explains the exact cancellation beyond this example. Assume P has linearly independent columns and choose an invertible Galerkin coarse-grid operator AH​=RAh​P. With an exact coarse solve, the iteration matrix for the remaining approximation error is
ehnew​=Ceh​,C=I−PAH−1​RAh​,CP=0,C2=C,RAh​C=0.
(96)
The short proof is to substitute RAh​P=AH​. Thus every vector in the column space of P is eliminated, and the corrected linear-system residual restricts to zero. These are exact projection identities, not a statement that every visually smooth vector belongs to that column space. If Ah​ is a symmetric matrix and a positive-definite matrix, and R=cPT with c>0, then AH​ is also a positive-definite matrix. Moreover PTAh​Ceh​=0: C is an orthogonal projection onto the complement of the column space of P in the energy norm ∥q∥Ah​​=qTAh​q​. Equivalently, the added correction is the best approximation to eh​ from that column space in this energy norm. Without these symmetry and transfer assumptions, the projection need not be orthogonal.[ref]
For an approximate coarse solve represented by a fixed linear map BH​, replace AH−1​ by BH​. Then
CB​=I−PBH​RAh​,CB​P=P(I−BH​AH​).
(97)
There is no exact cancellation of coarse-representable approximation error unless BH​AH​=I on the relevant directions. For the triangular example, deliberately returning half the exact correction, BH​=21​AH−1​, leaves eh​/2, as the figure shows. This controlled mathematical comparison illustrates an incomplete coarse solve; it is not a measured performance result or a prescription for a geometric multigrid V-cycle. More generally, if zH∗​=AH−1​Rrh​, then an approximate zH​ leaves Ceh​+P(zH∗​−zH​), separating the nonrepresentable remainder from the coarse-solve defect.
With pre- and post-relaxation iteration matrices Spre​ and Spost​, a two-grid error step has iteration matrix E=Spost​CB​Spre​. A geometric multigrid V-cycle supplies BH​ by one recursive coarse cycle from zero, with a direct solve only at the bottom. Effective smoothing, a coarse column space that approximates the surviving approximation error, and a sufficiently accurate coarse correction must work together. Coarsening alone supplies no convergence guarantee, and correcting an arbitrary fine-grid vector exactly would require a coarse column space large enough to contain it.
