Projective space represents invertible quotients (Pn(T)={OTn+1​↠L}/≅)

Words: 93
Projective space OurBigBook.com
With the quotient convention, projective space represents the functor of surjections from a rank-n+1 free sheaf to a line bundle, modulo isomorphisms of the target compatible with the map. On an affine base the target may be a nontrivial rank-one projective module, so a tuple of elements generating the unit ideal is only the description when that target is trivial. Where the image of the ith basis vector generates the target, divide the other images by it to get the usual affine coordinates. Infinitesimal variations of the quotient form Hom(kerq,L) and give the Euler sequence.

Morphisms from projective space to smaller projective space are constant

Words: 69
Projective space OurBigBook.com
A nonconstant morphism Pm→Pn with m>n would have a positive-dimensional fiber of a morphism by the fiber dimension theorem. A target hyperplane avoiding its image point meets the positive-dimensional projective image, so its pullback is a nonempty divisor in Pm disjoint from that fibre. Every positive-dimensional projective subvariety meets every hypersurface: on its affine cone, a homogeneous equation cuts a positive-dimensional zero set containing a nonzero cone point. This contradiction proves constancy.

Dual projective space (P(V∗))

Words: 189 Articles: 2
Projective space OurBigBook.com
Hyperplanes in P(V) are kernels of nonzero linear functionals modulo scalar multiplication. Their parameter space is therefore P(V∗), of the same dimension. In particular lines in a projective plane are parametrized by another projective plane.

Projective dual variety (Y∗⊂P(V∗))

Words: 154 Articles: 1
Dual projective space OurBigBook.com
For an irreducible projective variety Y⊂P(V), its projective dual is the closure of hyperplanes containing the embedded tangent space at some smooth point of Y. Over the complex numbers, biduality gives (Y∗)∗=Y. For a nondegenerate smooth curve in characteristic zero, the dual is an irreducible hypersurface; a general point corresponds to one ordinary tangency. Its defining incidence space consists of a point on the curve and a hyperplane containing its tangent line.

Projective biduality theorem ((Y∗)∗=Y)

Words: 81
Projective dual variety OurBigBook.com
Over the complex numbers, an irreducible projective variety is recovered by applying projective dual variety twice. In its conormal incidence variety, pairs (y,H) with H tangent at y become pairs (H,y) under exchange of the two projective factors. At general smooth pairs, differentiating the incidence equation shows that the hyperplane defined by y is tangent to the dual variety at H. Closure gives the same conormal variety with the factors exchanged, proving biduality. This is why the branch hypersurface of the Gauss map of a theta divisor determines a nonhyperelliptic canonical curve.

Projective coordinates over a local ring (Hom(SpecA,Pn)={(ai​):∃i, ai​∈A×}/A×)

Words: 62
Projective space OurBigBook.com
For a local ring A, every map from SpecA into projective space lands in one standard chart: the inverse image of a chart containing the closed point is the whole spectrum. This gives homogeneous coordinates with at least one unit entry, unique up to a common unit. For a nonlocal ring, coordinates may instead merely generate the unit ideal, and general maps can involve a nontrivial invertible sheaf quotient.

Quaternionic projective space (HPn)

Words: 250 Articles: 3
Projective space OurBigBook.com
Quaternionic projective space is the space of right quaternionic lines in Hn+1. It is S4n+3 modulo the right action of unit quaternions, with one cell in each dimension 0,4,…,4n. It is a compact smooth manifold of real dimension 4n; HP1≅S4. The quaternionic tautological line bundle and its sphere bundle give the multiplication in its cohomology ring of quaternionic projective space.

Complex cobordism ring of the quaternionic projective plane (MU∗(HP2)≅MU∗(pt)[q]/(q3),∣q∣=4)

Words: 62
Quaternionic projective space OurBigBook.com
For the quaternionic tautological line bundle regarded as a complex rank-two bundle, take q=c2MU​. Its ordinary image generates degree-four integral cohomology, and its square generates degree eight. The Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence collapses because the cells have dimensions 0,4,8 and the coefficients are even. The classes 1,q,q2 lift a free basis of the associated graded module, and q3=0 because the theory is connective and the space has dimension eight.

Complex inclusion into quaternionic projective space (i:CPn↪HPn,i∗u=x2)

Words: 87
Quaternionic projective space OurBigBook.com
Coordinate inclusion of complex vectors sends a complex line to its quaternionic span. If two such vectors span the same quaternionic line, a nonzero complex coordinate forces their scalar ratio to be complex, so this map is injective. The pulled-back quaternionic tautological line bundle splits as L⊕L with complex first Chern classes −x,x. Its top Chern class, and thus Euler class, is −x2. With u the negative Euler generator, i∗u=x2 and i∗(uk)=x2k. The map on infinite projective spaces is injective on cohomology; a finite inclusion loses the degrees with 2k>n.

Cohomology ring of quaternionic projective space (H∗(HPn;Z)=Z[u]/(un+1),∣u∣=4)

Words: 46
Quaternionic projective space OurBigBook.com
The cell structure gives one integral cohomology generator in each degree divisible by four through 4n. The Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle for the quaternionic tautological line bundle makes multiplication by its Euler class an isomorphism between successive such degrees. Choose u to be the negative of that Euler class; its powers generate the displayed cohomology ring.

General linear position

Words: 39
Projective space OurBigBook.com
Points in Pn are in general linear position when no k+1 of them lie in a projective subspace of dimension less than k for k≤n. Equivalently, every selection of at most n+1 representing vectors is linearly independent.

The degree-d Veronese map sends a projective point to the list of all degree-d monomials in its homogeneous coordinates. It embeds Pn into a higher-dimensional projective space.

Nonnegative hyperplane twist cohomology by restriction (h0(Pn,O(m))=(nn+m​),Hr>0=0)

Words: 55
Cohomology of twisting sheaves on projective space OurBigBook.com
For m≥0, these sheaf cohomology dimensions follow from the vanishing for O and the hyperplane exact sequence for twisting sheaves. Induct on dimension and then on m in 0→O(m−1)→O(m)→i∗​OPn−1​(m)→0. The long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology gives higher-degree vanishing and the recurrence hn0​(m)−hn0​(m−1)=(n−1n−1+m​). Starting with hn0​(0)=1 yields the formula; the corresponding homogeneous monomials form a basis of global sections.

Infinite negative-twist sum with zero global sections (⨁n≥1​OP1​(−1))

Words: 65
Cohomology of twisting sheaves on projective space OurBigBook.com
On the projective line, an infinite direct sum of copies of O(−1) is a quasi-coherent sheaf but not a coherent sheaf: at any point its residue-vector-space dimension is infinite. Its global sections vanish because each summand has no sections, and sections commute with direct sums for the finite two-chart equalizer. Its direct image sheaf on Speck is consequently zero and coherent. This shows that a nonaffine direct image can lose information needed to detect coherence.

Point-line duality (p∈ℓ⟺ℓ∗∈p∗)

Words: 333 Articles: 5
Projective plane OurBigBook.com
A point [a:b:c] of the projective plane corresponds to the dual line aX+bY+cZ=0. A line given by those coefficients corresponds back to the point [a:b:c]. Incidence is reversed: p∈ℓ if and only if ℓ∗∈p∗. Thus collinear point triples become concurrent line triples, preserving the essential combinatorics of incidence geometry.

Dual arrangement of a planar point set

Words: 283 Articles: 4
Point-line duality OurBigBook.com
Dualize a noncollinear finite point set in the real projective plane to projective lines. Their intersection vertices and consecutive line segments form an embedded graph. A vertex incident to k lines has graph degree 2k and corresponds to a primal line containing k points. Ordinary lines correspond to degree-four vertices. The faces are polygons with at least three sides.
Good edge of a dual line arrangement
Words: 170 Articles: 2
Dual arrangement of a planar point set OurBigBook.com
A good dual-arrangement edge has degree-six endpoints and two triangular adjacent faces. Other edges are bad. The Euler defect identity for a projective line arrangement bounds the number of bad edges by 16t2​: charge them to degree-four or higher-degree vertices and to nontriangular faces. This turns a count of ordinary lines into quantitative control of local triangular geometry.
Safe edge of a dual line arrangement
Words: 124 Articles: 1
Good edge of a dual line arrangement OurBigBook.com
A safe dual-arrangement edge is one for which every edge on a path of length at most two from either endpoint is a good dual-arrangement edge. This stronger local condition supplies a two-cell-thick triangular strip. Only a bounded multiple of the original bad edges can be unsafe: trace a shortest path to the first bad edge and reverse it through degree-six intermediate vertices.
Bounded-radius propagation of edge defects
Words: 66
Safe edge of a dual line arrangement OurBigBook.com
In an embedded arrangement graph, an edge whose fixed-radius neighbourhood meets a bad edge can be charged to the first such edge along a shortest path. All intervening edges are good dual-arrangement edges, so their endpoints have bounded degree. The number of possible reversed paths is bounded in terms of the fixed radius alone. Therefore thickening a defect set by a fixed radius preserves an O(t2​) edge count.
Euler defect identity for a projective line arrangement (t2​=3+∑k≥4​(k−3)tk​+∑j≥4​(j−3)fj​)
Words: 59
Dual arrangement of a planar point set OurBigBook.com
Let tk​ count vertices where k lines meet and fj​ count faces with j sides in a nonpencil projective line arrangement. Using Euler characteristic one, v=∑tk​, e=∑ktk​, and 2e=∑jfj​ gives t2​=3+∑k≥4​(k−3)tk​+∑j≥4​(j−3)fj​. Thus few double vertices control both high-multiplicity vertices and nontriangular faces. Discarding the face term gives the usual arrangement form of the ordinary-line inequality.

Fano plane

Words: 113 Articles: 2
Projective plane OurBigBook.com
The Fano plane is the projective plane over the finite field F2​. It has seven points and seven lines, each line contains three points, and two distinct lines meet in one point. A line-point incidence matrix N therefore satisfies NNT=2I+J, making it invertible over the real numbers and useful for the transplantation theorem.

Fano plane duality group (GL3​(2)⋊⟨τ⟩,∣G∣=336)

Words: 33
Fano plane OurBigBook.com
Adjoin the inverse-transpose automorphism to the collineation group of the Fano plane. Its maximal subgroups other than the collineation group are unordered incident-pair stabilizers, unordered nonincident-pair stabilizers and Sylow 7-normalizers, of orders 16, 12 and 42.

Fano point-line stabilizer intersection (∣Gp​∩GL​∣=8 or 6)

There are 21 incident point-line pairs and 28 nonincident pairs in the Fano plane, with GL3​(2) transitive on each type. Orbit-stabilizer theorem gives the two orders as 168/21=8 and 168/28=6.

Pole divisor avoiding a fiber

Words: 87
Projective variety OurBigBook.com
Suppose a nonconstant morphism of varieties f:X→Z has fiber Y over z0​, where X is normal, connected and projective. Choose an affine neighborhood U of z0​ and a regular function on U nonconstant on f(X)∩U. Its pullback is a nonconstant rational function on X, so its pole Weil divisor is nonzero. A pole-free rational function on a normal variety is regular, and global regular functions on a connected projective variety are constant. The pole divisor avoids f−1(U), hence avoids Y. No projectivity assumption on Z is needed.

Projective completion

A projective completion of an affine variety X is a projective variety containing X as a dense open subvariety. Homogenizing affine defining equations and taking their projective zero set gives the projective closure, although it may add singularities at infinity.

Two-affine cover of a smooth projective curve
Words: 42
Smooth projective curve OurBigBook.com
A nonconstant rational function on a smooth projective curve extends to a finite morphism to the projective line. The inverse images of its two standard affine charts are affine varieties covering the curve. The resulting Čech cochain complex has only degrees zero and one, so every coherent sheaf has zero sheaf cohomology in degrees at least two.
Genus of a smooth projective curve (g=dimk​H1(C,OC​))
Words: 98 Articles: 1
Smooth projective curve OurBigBook.com
For a smooth projective curve with H0(C,OC​)=k, its genus is dimk​H1(C,OC​), equivalently dimk​H0(C,ΩC1​) by Serre duality. It equals the arithmetic genus 1−χ(OC​). Without the condition on constants, the general Riemann-Roch theorem instead uses χ(OC​)=h0(OC​)−h1(OC​).
Rationality of a smooth projective genus-zero curve (g(C)=0⟹C≅P1)
Words: 62
Genus of a smooth projective curve OurBigBook.com
Over an algebraically closed field, choose a point P on the smooth projective curve. The Riemann-Roch theorem gives dimL(P)=2: the complementary canonical divisor term has negative degree. A nonconstant function in L(P) has exactly one simple pole, so it defines a degree-one morphism of algebraic curves to the projective line. A degree-one map between smooth projective curves is an isomorphism. The existence of a rational point is essential over a field which is not algebraically closed.
Smooth plane cubic in characteristic three (Y2Z−X3+XZ2=0)
Words: 66
Smooth projective curve OurBigBook.com
Over the algebraic closure of F3​, the displayed cubic is smooth. Its three partial derivatives are Z2, 2YZ, and Y2+2XZ; their simultaneous vanishing would give Z=Y=0, and the equation would then give X=0, impossible in projective space. Its affine equation y2=x3−x is irreducible because the odd-degree polynomial on the right is not a square in the rational function field.
Smooth rational curve
Words: 46
Smooth projective curve OurBigBook.com
Over an algebraically closed field, a smooth projective rational curve is isomorphic to P1. Its line bundles are determined by degree, its degree-zero line bundle is trivial, and H1(P1,O)=0. An integral projective curve of arithmetic genus zero is automatically smooth and rational by the normalization genus formula.

Local parameter on a smooth algebraic curve (t∈m∖m2)
Words: 68
Local ring of a smooth algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
At a point of a smooth algebraic curve, its local ring is a discrete valuation ring. A local parameter is a generator t of its maximal ideal, equivalently an element of valuation one. It measures the order of a zero of a function or pole by expressing functions as powers of t times units. Over the complex numbers its analytic counterpart is a local coordinate vanishing simply at the point. For an elliptic curve in a Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve, t=−x/y is the standard parameter at the identity.

Projection from a point on a smooth quadric ([x0​:x1​:x2​:x3​]↦[x1​:x2​:x0​−x3​])
Words: 65
Smooth quadric surface OurBigBook.com
For X={x0​x1​=x2​x3​} and P=[1:0:0:1], the displayed map is defined on X∖{P}. Its fibre over [u:v:w] has equation (u−v)λ+uw=0 on an affine line. Away from u=v the fibre is one reduced point. Picard group of a smooth affine quadric surface (Pic(Q∖C)≅Z)
Words: 47
Smooth quadric surface OurBigBook.com
Over an algebraically closed field, remove a smooth hyperplane conic C from a smooth quadric surface Q. The rulings of a smooth quadric surface generate Pic(Q)=Z2, and C has class (1,1). Picard-group localization on a smooth variety gives the quotient by Z(1,1). The restricted ruling bundle OQ​(1,0) is a generator, exhibiting a nontrivial line bundle on an affine surface.
Segre description of a smooth quadric surface (Q≅P1×P1)
Words: 103
Smooth quadric surface OurBigBook.com
Over an algebraically closed field, a smooth four-variable projective quadratic equation can be written x0​x3​−x1​x2​=0, including in characteristic two. Its quadratic form splits into two hyperbolic planes: choose an isotropic vector and its paired vector, adjust the latter to be isotropic, and repeat on the orthogonal complement. In characteristic two, smoothness forces the alternating polar form in dimension four to have full rank, since a positive-dimensional radical has a nonzero isotropic vector over the algebraically closed field and would give a singular point. The Segre embedding ([s0​:s1​],[t0​:t1​])↦[s0​t0​:s0​t1​:s1​t0​:s1​t1​] then gives the isomorphism. Each matrix of coordinates has rank one, which supplies the inverse pair of projective factors.
