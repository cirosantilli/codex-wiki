Black-hole mass measurement

Words: 44
Astrophysical black hole OurBigBook.com
An inference of a compact object's gravitating mass from specified tracers and a dynamical or radiation model. Resolved stellar or gas orbits, spectroscopic binary dynamics, and reverberation mapping supply complementary approaches with different distance, geometry and equilibrium assumptions. A short variability timescale constrains size but is not a unique mass measurement.

Black-hole sphere of influence (rinf​≃GM∙​/σ2)

Words: 48
Astrophysical black hole OurBigBook.com
The black-hole sphere of influence is a characteristic radius where the central black hole appreciably changes surrounding stellar dynamics. Equating its Newtonian gravitational potential scale GM∙​/r to the ambient squared velocity dispersion gives rinf​≃GM∙​/σ2. Dynamical mass measurements depend on angular resolution rinf​/D, tracer brightness, and degeneracies in the orbital distribution, not solely on black hole mass.

Black-hole spin (a∗​)

Words: 296 Articles: 5
Astrophysical black hole OurBigBook.com
Black-hole spin is the angular momentum of a black hole, commonly expressed by the dimensionless Kerr parameter a∗​=cJ/(GM2). It changes the innermost stable circular orbit, accretion efficiency, and relativistic frame dragging.

Black-hole spin measurement (a∗​=cJ/(GM2))

Words: 136 Articles: 2
Black-hole spin OurBigBook.com
An inference of black-hole angular momentum, usually quoted through a∗​. Accretion-based techniques use the dependence of the stable inner orbit and relativistic radiation transfer on spin in the Kerr black hole model. Disk truncation, inclination, illumination and atmosphere assumptions can imitate a change of spin and must enter the uncertainty model.

Black-hole reflection spectroscopy

Words: 38
Black-hole spin measurement OurBigBook.com
An inference from radiation reflected by an irradiated accretion disc, including iron-line and continuum features. Doppler effects, gravitational redshift and ray propagation determine its profile. Associating the innermost reflecting radius with the innermost stable circular orbit can constrain spin, conditional on illumination, ionization, abundance, inclination and disk structure.

Black-hole continuum fitting

Words: 51
Black-hole spin measurement OurBigBook.com
A fit of a thermal accretion-disk spectrum that infers the inner emitting radius and identifies it with the innermost stable circular orbit. A relativistic thin-disk model requires independently constrained mass, distance and inclination, together with spectral hardening and absorption. Use of a suitable disk-dominated state is an essential physical check, not merely a statistical cut.

Black-hole spin-up by coherent accretion

Words: 131 Articles: 1
Black-hole spin OurBigBook.com
Coherent prograde gas accretion increases the angular momentum of a black hole. For a zero-torque thin disc, the rest mass dm0​ entering from the innermost stable circular orbit changes the hole by dM=EISCO​dm0​ and dJ=ℓISCO​dm0​. Its dimensionless spin obeys da∗​/dlogM=cℓISCO​/(GMEISCO​)−2a∗​. Starting from zero spin and neglecting captured radiation, the formal extremal endpoint has Mf​=6​Mi​ and requires rest-mass supply approximately 1.8464Mi​. Photon capture leads instead to the Thorne spin limit.

Thorne spin limit (a∗​≃0.998)

Words: 63
Black-hole spin-up by coherent accretion OurBigBook.com
The Thorne spin limit is the equilibrium spin produced by competition between coherent thin-disc matter accretion and capture of disc photons by a Kerr black hole. Preferential capture of counterrotating photon angular momentum prevents the standard radiatively efficient model from reaching a∗​=1. The commonly quoted limit is approximately 0.998, with some dependence on emission and accretion assumptions. Thorne's original calculation derives this result.

Quasi-periodic oscillation (QPO)

Words: 48
Black-hole accretion OurBigBook.com
An oscillatory feature with finite width in a source's variability power spectrum, rather than a perfectly periodic clock. Orbital, precession and disc-mode interpretations connect different frequencies to the same compact object. Identifying an observed peak with an innermost stable circular orbit orbital frequency is therefore a model assumption, not a direct black-hole mass or spin measurement.

Collisionless black-hole capture

Words: 74
Black-hole accretion OurBigBook.com
Collisionless matter approaching a black hole generally cannot dissipate enough orbital energy to remain bound, so direct capture requires sufficiently small angular momentum. For nonrelativistic incident speed v, a Schwarzschild black hole has direct test-particle capture cross-section approximately 16πG2M2/(c2v2). A Newtonian pericentre estimate using the Schwarzschild radius instead gives 4πG2M2/(c2v2). Both show that capture at a given mass density is suppressed by order (v/c)2 relative to supersonic Bondi--Hoyle--Lyttleton accretion. Tidal disruption events provide a distinct way to accrete some stellar debris.

Monatomic Bondi accretion (γ=5/3,M˙max​=πG2M2ρ∞​c∞−3​)

Words: 101
Bondi accretion OurBigBook.com
For isentropic spherical accretion with this adiabatic index, define λ=M˙c∞3​/(4πG2M2ρ∞​) and x=rc∞2​/(GM). The steady-flow equation is λ​[M3/2/2+3M−1/2/2]=1+3x/2. Relativistic spherical accretion

Words: 66
Bondi accretion OurBigBook.com
Relativistic spherical accretion is steady radial gas inflow in Schwarzschild spacetime, with conserved rest-mass flux and relativistic Bernoulli function. A regular solution passes a finite sonic point outside the event horizon. For an isentropic gas with γ=5/3 and cold asymptotic sound speed c∞​≪c, the critical radius is approximately 3GM/(4cc∞​) and the leading mass accretion rate is πG2M2ρ∞​/c∞3​. Relativistic corrections resolve the zero-radius critical point of the Newtonian γ=5/3 model without eliminating accretion.

Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre

Words: 852 Articles: 7

The useful idea is to weigh an unseen object through its effect on a visible tracer. A curved stellar orbit carries more information than a single fast-moving star: its changing position and velocity constrain a common centre, a length scale and a clock. The Galactic-centre stellar-orbit experiments combine astrometry with Doppler spectroscopy; the Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model below shows how the two measurements complement one another.
Galactic-centre point-mass orbit model (μ=4π2a3/P2)

E−esinE=P2π(t−τ)​,
(5)
where τ is the time of periapsis. Coordinates in the orbital plane, with the point mass at the origin, are
r′(E)=a​cosE−e1−e2​sinE0​​,r˙′(E)=P(1−ecosE)2πa​​−sinE1−e2​cosE0​​.
(6)
The focus is the central mass; the ellipse's geometric centre is displaced by −ae along the first coordinate. The varying denominator makes the star move faster near periapsis, even though the mean anomaly increases uniformly.
Sky projection of the Galactic-centre orbit model (θ=(x/D,y/D))

Here i is orbital inclination, Ω the longitude of ascending node, and ω the argument of periapsis. For distance D≫a, the small-angle model gives angular positions θx​=x/D, θy​=y/D in radians. Define α=a/D, the angular scale of the deprojected fitted orbit. It is not generally the semi-major axis of the apparent ellipse on the sky. Projection also need not send a true focus to a focus of the apparent ellipse. Fitting the rotated orbit preserves that distinction; measuring a bounding ellipse alone loses it.

Galactic-centre stellar-orbit experiments (2002–2008)
Words: 134
Stellar orbits at the Galactic centre OurBigBook.com
Rainer Schödel, Reinhard Genzel and collaborators combined NTT imaging with VLT adaptive-optics infrared observations. Silicon-monoxide masers registered stellar images to the radio position of Sagittarius A*. Their 2002 observations included S2's closest approach; a fitted Kepler orbit constrained a concentrated central mass. Rejecting a particular extended cluster also required its density and survival assumptions.[ref]
Andrea Ghez and collaborators combined Keck speckle imaging and laser-guide-star adaptive optics with infrared spectra. Image fitting supplied angular positions; spectral lines supplied velocities. The 2008 analysis treated optical distortion, coordinate alignment and blended sources: a shifting photocentre can imitate motion. Joint angular and spectral fitting constrained both distance and mass, with assumptions about central radial motion affecting the result. Apparatus calibration and confusion tests are therefore part of the dynamical inference.[ref]
