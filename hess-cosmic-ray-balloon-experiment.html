Hess cosmic-ray balloon experiment

 ... Branches of physics Astrophysics Galaxy Milky Way Interstellar medium Cosmic ray
OurBigBook.com Words: 973 Articles: 2
Victor Franz Hess's 1912 series used two sealed, 3-mm-walled ionization chambers with Theodor Wulf's fiber electrometers. Karl Bergwitz suggested internal plating; Günther & Tegetmeyer improved focusing. Basket-mounted ground comparisons, insulation-loss measurements, simultaneous detectors and barograph-derived heights supplied checks. The highest flight reached 5350 m; its comparisons came from Vienna and after landing, without a launch-site baseline.[ref]
A third, thin-walled chamber admitted ambient air. Hess multiplied its readings by 750/b, with pressure b in millimeters of mercury, warning that vessel-wall contributions might violate this scaling. He recorded outside temperature but applied no absolute-temperature correction. Night flights, an eclipse and weather observations constrained alternatives to radiation from above; the eclipse argument addressed directly propagating solar radiation.[ref]
The electrometers read gradual electric potential loss caused by continuing air ionization. The low-altitude dip and strong high-altitude rise favored an extraterrestrial contribution, without measuring proton composition.[ref] An ionization chamber integrates electric charge: one energetic particle may create many ion pairs, several particles may contribute to one reading, and a photon can create charged particles in the gas or walls. A voltage-loss reading is therefore neither a count of primary cosmic rays nor a chemical analysis of them.
Domenico Pacini supplied independent evidence: in June 1911 he compared a copper-box-enclosed electroscope at the sea surface and 3 m underwater, more than 300 m offshore above an 8-m-deep seabed. Reduced underwater ionization, stable electrical loss between runs, and a lake comparison supported an external contribution independent of soil radioactive decay. Water provided shielding; this established a different constraint from Hess's altitude comparison.[ref]
