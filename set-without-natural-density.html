Set without natural density (A=⋃k≥0​([4k,2⋅4k)∩Z))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Natural density
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
Include the positive integers in [1,2),[4,8),[16,32),[64,128),…, and exclude the intervening integer intervals and zero. Equivalently, include [22k,22k+1)∩Z for k≥0. Each included block is large enough to raise the running proportion; the following excluded block lowers it again.
With CA​(N)=∣A∩{0,…,N−1}∣, the exact boundary counts follow from a geometric series:
CA​(4k)=∑j=0k−1​4j=34k−1​,CA​(2⋅4k)=∑j=0k​4j=34k+1−1​.
(16)
The first sum is empty at k=0. Dividing by the corresponding boundary, rather than using one denominator for both, gives
4kCA​(4k)​=31​−3⋅4k1​,2⋅4kCA​(2⋅4k)​=32​−6⋅4k1​.
(17)
For example, CA​(16)=1+4=5, whereas CA​(32)=1+4+16=21, yielding proportions 5/16 and 21/32. For 4k≤N≤2⋅4k, the exact count is (4k−1)/3+N−4k; for 2⋅4k≤N≤4k+1 it stays (4k+1−1)/3. Hence the proportion increases through each included block and decreases through each excluded block. These boundary subsequences give the actual limit inferior and limit superior:
liminfN→∞​NCA​(N)​=31​,limsupN→∞​NCA​(N)​=32​.
(18)
Since these differ, A has no natural density. Its upper asymptotic density still records its largest limiting proportion.
