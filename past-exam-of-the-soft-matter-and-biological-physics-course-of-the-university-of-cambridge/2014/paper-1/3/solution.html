The confined-polymer entry and recoil times have different length dependence; this entry graph is linear through the origin. Charge and drag both grow with inserted length, leaving a constant velocity. The same cancellation explains length-independent mobility in an ideal free-draining electrophoresis model. It does not include a capture waiting time or claim that every free-solution chain has identical hydrodynamic drag.
A measured superlinear entry time can arise when exterior-coil drag, tension propagation, wall adsorption or nonuniform friction adds length dependence absent from this model. For entropic-trap length separation, a reservoir/channel array can repeat length-dependent capture, confinement and relaxation delays, turning them into different mean residence times. The uniform inside-channel drift speed derived above is itself length independent, so that drift alone cannot separate lengths. Separation must retain length dependence of the barrier or of the capture/recoil dynamics and can be tuned with a forward-biased time-dependent field. For large bulk throughput, pressure-driven advection is preferable to diffusion alone; a pressure gradient can be combined with electric capture control. In the unobstructed continuum limit Q=πR4ΔP/(8ηℓc​). Excessive driving erases trapping differences, so throughput and resolution compete; the question supplies no geometry or adsorption law selecting a unique optimal drive.
