Use a force-tilted activation barrier for a generic one-coordinate escape model. A tensile force tilts a free-energy landscape to GF​(x)=G0​(x)−Fx. If xb​ is the initial minimum and xs​ the saddle, define Δx=xs​−xb​>0 and the zero-force barrier ΔG0‡​=G0​(xs​)−G0​(xb​). The relevant activation barrier is measured relative to the initial minimum, not an arbitrary absolute transition-state energy. To first order in force,
ΔG‡(F)=ΔG0‡​−FΔx,k(F)=k0​eF/F0​,F0​=ΔxkB​T​,k0​=νe−ΔG0‡​/(kB​T).​
(5)
The attempt prefactor ν is assumed force independent, and the saddle displacement is approximated as constant. This single-barrier approximation need not survive large landscape deformations or a change of escape pathway. For a generic force-ramp measurement, a calibrated atomic force microscope cantilever pulls an attachment while a stage changes separation. cantilever deflection d gives force kc​d, and the cantilever compliance correction subtracts d from imposed separation to give tether extension, with further corrections for other apparatus compliance. A prescribed stage speed does not automatically produce constant force loading rate when tether stiffness varies; the assumed F(t)=ft must be measured or controlled. The no-rebinding approximation is a physical escape assumption: after the link breaks, separation removes the partners from the capture region before appreciable reassociation. It requires the integrated return/association probability during that separation to be small. If the separated state stays nearby or the force is held without separation, setting k+​=0 can fail. Under the stipulated approximation, with the link initially intact,
dFdp​=−fk0​​eF/F0​p,p(F)=exp[−fk0​F0​​(eF/F0​−1)],F≥0.​
(6)
This survival under a linear force ramp differs from the probability density for rupture. It starts at one, decreases to zero and shifts to larger force as the loading rate increases. For small F/F0​, p≃e−k0​F/f; a slow ramp allows much escape before substantial force is reached.
The median rupture force under a linear ramp satisfies p=1/2, giving
F1/2​=F0​ln(1+k0​F0​fln2​).​
(7)
At high loading rate, the slope of F1/2​ against lnf tends to F0​; it is not exactly F0​ for a slow ramp. Fit the full expression across loading rates to recover F0​ and k0​. The rupture-force probability density is ρ(F)=k(F)p(F)/f, whose most probable rupture force under a linear ramp is F∗​=F0​ln[f/(k0​F0​)] if f>k0​F0​, and zero otherwise. median and mode are different observables and must not be interchanged when fitting.
At known temperature, F0​ yields the activation distance Δx=kB​T/F0​; 1/k0​ gives the zero-force mean lifetime in this exponential model. Absolute barrier height additionally requires the attempt prefactor, and neither equilibrium binding free energy nor an association rate follows from dissociation-only data.
