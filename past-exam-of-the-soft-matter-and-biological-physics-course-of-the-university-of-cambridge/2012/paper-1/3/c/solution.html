A front solution joins different asymptotic states in space. In a reaction–diffusion system, local nonlinear dynamics select possible states while diffusion couples neighboring points. A travelling wave with profile u(x,t)=U(ξ), ξ=x−ct, converts a partial differential equation into a boundary value problem for the shape and speed. A pulse instead returns to the same resting state on both sides and can contain leading and trailing fronts.
A useful nondimensional cubic fast-slow reaction-diffusion model, in a convention of the FitzHugh-Nagumo model, is
ut​=Duxx​+f(u)−w+I,wt​=ε(u−βw),f(u)=u(1−u)(u−α),0<α<1,
(12)
where D>0, ε≪1, and β>0. Here u is a fast activation variable, w a slow negative-feedback variable and I a generic drive; no physiological variable is assigned. Without spatial coupling, nullclines are w=f(u)+I and w=u/β. At I=0, the resting state (0,0) has Jacobian matrix
J=(−αε​−1−εβ​),trJ=−α−εβ<0,detJ=ε(1+αβ)>0.
(13)
It has linear stability. With appropriate parameter choices a sufficiently large perturbation makes a fast excursion to the other outer branch of the cubic, followed by slow feedback and return. Thus a stable rest state can be excitable without being linearly unstable. Other parameters or applied drive can yield multiple equilibria or oscillations; these behaviours should not be asserted for every parameter set.
The fast-subsystem front can be obtained explicitly by freezing w=I=0. For the bistable cubic reaction-diffusion equation, u=0,1 are stable and u=α is unstable. The decreasing logistic front of a bistable cubic equation is
U(ξ)=1+exp[ξ/2D​]1​,c=D/2​(1−2α).​
(14)
To check it, U′=−U(1−U)/2D​ and substitution into DU′′+cU′+f(U)=0 cancels every term. For α<1/2, the high state advances to the right; for α>1/2, the direction reverses. At α=1/2 the front is stationary. More generally, multiplying the travelling-wave equation by U′ and integrating yields
c∫−∞∞​(U′)2dξ=∫01​f(u)du=121−2α​.
(15)
This links propagation direction to the bias between the two stable states and recovers Maxwell balance for a scalar reaction-diffusion front at zero speed. It is a bistable selected-speed problem, rather than a universal Fisher-type minimum-speed formula.
For the coupled fast-slow system, travelling profiles satisfy
DU′′+cU′+f(U)−W+I=0,cW′+ε(U−βW)=0.
(16)
A slow recovery wake can support a return front behind the advancing fast front, producing a travelling pulse for suitable parameters. Its width reflects the slow recovery time and propagation speed, while each sharp edge has the faster diffusion–reaction scale. The frozen-recovery speed above is a controlled subsystem result, not an exact speed for every coupled pulse. A definite pulse speed requires parameters and connecting boundary condition; none are specified by this brief-note question.
