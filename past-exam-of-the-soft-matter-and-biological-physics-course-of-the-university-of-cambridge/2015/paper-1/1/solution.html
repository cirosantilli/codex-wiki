Write ε=ε0​εr​ and take n0​ to be the reservoir density of each monovalent ion species. Equal electrochemical potentials in equilibrium give the Boltzmann ion densities in a symmetric electrolyte,
n+​=n0​e−eψ/(kB​T),n−​=n0​eeψ/(kB​T),ρe​=e(n+​−n−​)=−2en0​sinhkB​Teψ​.
(1)
Combining this with the electrostatic Poisson equation gives the planar Poisson-Boltzmann equation,
ψ′′=ε2en0​​sinhkB​Teψ​,κ2=εkB​T2e2n0​​.​
(2)
The Debye length is κ−1. If the symbol n0​ instead denotes the combined density of both species, the factor two is absent; the per-species convention above makes the normalization explicit. At ∣eψ∣≪kB​T, the linearized planar screening solution obeys ψ′′=κ2ψ. With ψ(0)=ζ<0 and ψ(∞)=0,
ψ(x)=ζe−κx.​
(3)
The positive ions are counterions and the negative ions are co-ions. At weak potential,
n+​≃n0​(1−kB​Teζ​e−κx),n−​≃n0​(1+kB​Teζ​e−κx).
(4)
They approach n0​ from above and below respectively, with equal first-order excess and deficit. For stronger negative potential, interpret the source's strong-potential comparison as a statement about ∣eψ∣, since ψ itself is negative. Counterions become strongly concentrated near the wall, while co-ions are exponentially depleted and remain nonnegative. Linearization misses this asymmetry and would eventually predict unphysical negative co-ion density. Point-ion mean-field theory itself eventually needs finite-size and correlation corrections; it provides no steric cap at arbitrarily large counterion concentration.
For steady tangential electro-osmosis, with no imposed pressure gradient and negligible inertia, the Stokes equation is ηv′′+ρe​E=0. Since ρe​=−εψ′′,
ηv′′=εEψ′′.
(5)
The conditions v(0)=0 and v′(∞)=0 give
v(x)=ηεE​[ψ(x)−ζ]=U(1−e−κx),U=−ηεζE​.​
(6)
The printed constant speed is the bulk velocity outside the double layer, not the velocity at the no-slip wall. With negative ζ the positive counterion excess drives fluid along the applied field. The far-field value is the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski electro-osmotic velocity.[ref]
For the finite channel, first model the added wall as an ideally uncharged insulating boundary with no normal field on its outer side: ψ′(d)=0, while ψ(0)=ζ. The one-charged-wall electro-osmotic channel then has
ψ(x)=ζcosh(κd)cosh[κ(d−x)]​.
(7)
Integrating the same force balance and enforcing v(0)=v(d)=0 gives
v(x)=U[1−(1−sech(κd))dx​−cosh(κd)cosh[κ(d−x)]​].​
(8)
For U>0, the maximum occurs at
xmax​=d−κ1​arsinh[κdcosh(κd)−1​]≃κlog(κd)​(κd≫1).​
(9)
In that limit v/U≃1−x/d−e−κx: the velocity rises rapidly from zero at the charged wall, reaches a maximum a few screening lengths away, then falls approximately linearly to zero at the neutral wall. One charged wall does not give a symmetric plug across a channel with no-slip at the other wall.
An alternative electrode condition ψ(d)=0 means a wall held at reservoir potential; it is not the same as zero surface charge. Under that convention,
v(x)=U[1−dx​−sinh(κd)sinh[κ(d−x)]​],
(10)
with cosh[κ(d−xmax​)]=sinh(κd)/(κd). Both boundary conventions have the same leading thin-double-layer profile above. An externally imposed pressure gradient would add a pressure-driven profile; none is specified here.
For dilute nonadsorbing uncharged polymers in equilibrium or sufficiently weak flow, the entropic polymer depletion near nonadsorbing walls suppresses chain configurations near each wall. A depletion layer of order the coil radius of gyration is expected at both walls, with a bulk-like central region when d is much larger than the coil size. Strong confinement rounds this into a central enrichment. Neutrality removes a direct electrophoretic force, not excluded-volume entropy. Adsorption, concentration and strong-flow migration were not specified, so this is a conditional equilibrium sketch rather than a universal flowing-polymer profile.
