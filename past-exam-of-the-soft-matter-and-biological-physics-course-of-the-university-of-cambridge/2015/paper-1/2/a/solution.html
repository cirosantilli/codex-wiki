The generic instrument uses a magnetically responsive bead, a controllable external field and optical tracking of bead position or orientation. A field gradient supplies translational force; rotating an appropriate anisotropic field or bead moment can supply torque. For a permanent dipole,
U=−m⋅B,F=∇(m⋅B),τ=m×B.
(11)
For an isotropic unsaturated induced moment with effective susceptibility contrast Δχ and bead volume V, the induced magnetic bead force instead follows from
U=−2μ0​VΔχ​B2,F=2μ0​VΔχ​∇B2.​
(12)
The factor one-half accounts for inducing the moment. A spatially uniform field alone supplies no translational force; an isotropic induced moment parallel to the field supplies no orienting torque either. Saturation or magnetic anisotropy requires the corresponding magnetization model.
Position tracking needs a length calibration, and force calibration needs measured field gradients, a known reference load, or calibrated thermal fluctuations. For a generic tensioned tether of extension ℓ, small transverse displacement x costs energy Fx2/(2ℓ), so F=kB​Tℓ/⟨x2⟩ under equilibrium, small-angle and single-mode assumptions. Attachment geometry, bead rotation, wall drag and finite acquisition bandwidth can affect this calibration. These equations describe generic magnetic tweezers mechanics, without assigning a biological response.
