A useful original example fixes five active quark flavors and chooses Q0​=20GeV and α0​=αs​(Q0​)=0.16. These are illustrative inputs, not a measured determination. Integrating d(1/αs​)/dlnQ=β0​/(2π) with β0​=23/3 gives
αs−1​(Q)=6.25+6π23​ln20GeVQ​,Λ=Q0​exp(−β0​α0​2π​)≃0.1193GeV.
(24)
Consequently αs​(10GeV)≃0.1850, αs​(40GeV)≃0.1409, and αs​(100GeV)≃0.1217. Substituting Q=Q0​ returns the input, while differentiating returns the stated beta function. Rescaling all dimensional inputs by the same factor leaves the QCD coupling unchanged: only ratios enter. The reciprocal QCD coupling is a straight line against ln(Q/Q0​), so the calibration sets the intercept and nf​ sets the slope.
Changing the physical probe energy and changing an auxiliary renormalization scale are different operations. To isolate the latter, hold a hypothetical hard scale Qh​=20GeV fixed and define a(μ)=αs​(μ)/π, b=β0​/4, and the toy approximations
R1​(μ)=1+a(μ),R2​(μ)=1+a(μ)+ba(μ)2lnQh2​μ2​.
(25)
Using the one-loop order equation da/dlnμ2=−ba2 gives
dlnμ2dR1​​=−ba2,dlnμ2dR2​​=−2b2a3lnQh2​μ2​.
(26)
The explicit logarithm cancels the order-a2 scale drift. At μ=10,20,40GeV, respectively, R1​≃1.0589,1.0509,1.0449, whereas R2​≃1.0497,1.0509,1.0502. These are values of an invented dimensionless observable chosen to expose the cancellation; they are not a cross section prediction. Smaller residual scale variation does not bound the unknown constant at the next loop order.
