In thermal-diffusion units, let an imposed field point along c=(sinα,0,cosα) and consider a Fourier mode of wavevector (k,0,m). Put q=k2+m2 and d=ksinα+mcosα. The growth exponent s of its buoyant branch satisfies
q(s+q)[(s/σ+q)(s+ζq)+Qζd2]=Rk2(s+ζq),
(23)
where σ is the Prandtl number, ζ the magnetic-to-thermal diffusivity ratio, Q the Chandrasekhar number, and R the Rayleigh number. The resistive induction equation gives b=idu/(s+ζq), and thermal transport gives θ=w/(s+q). Projecting momentum perpendicular to the wavevector removes pressure; the vertical component of the projected buoyancy is R(k2/q)θ. Substitution gives the displayed polynomial. The stationary neutral curve is R=q3/k2+Qqd2/k2. No vertical boundary conditions are imposed in this plane-wave calculation.
