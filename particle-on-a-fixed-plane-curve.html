Particle on a fixed plane curve ((1+y′2)x¨=−gy′−y′y′′x˙2)

OurBigBook.com Words: 83
A point mass constrained to the fixed graph y=y(x) has velocity (x˙,y′x˙). Under uniform downward gravity, its Lagrangian is L=m(1+y′2)x˙2/2−mgy, so its canonical momentum is px​=m(1+y′2)x˙, not the horizontal mechanical momentum. The Euler-Lagrange equation gives the equation in the title, and the conserved energy is m(1+y′2)x˙2/2+mgy. A straight segment with constant slope s has constant horizontal acceleration −gs/(1+s2); zero curvature does not imply zero acceleration. A cart held to a track satisfies this mechanical constraint only while the assumed contact or retaining mechanism remains effective.
