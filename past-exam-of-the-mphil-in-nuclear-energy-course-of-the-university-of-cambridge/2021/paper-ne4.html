The distinction is between partitioning reusable fuel constituents from fission-product waste, and subsequently partitioning uranium from plutonium. In the first operation, chemical selectivity transfers selected actinides into a different stream while most fission products remain in the waste stream. In the second, differences in chemical speciation allow the two recovered elements to follow different streams. Neither operation separates uranium isotopes: uranium enrichment and chemical element separation are different tasks.
At a conceptual level, solvent extraction exploits unequal affinities of dissolved species for immiscible liquid phases. complexation changes those affinities, while a change in oxidation state can change an element's preferred phase without changing its identity. This explains why a stream bearing actinides can first be separated from much of its accompanying waste, and why uranium and plutonium need not subsequently remain together. Dissolved and undissolved residues, contaminated solvents and off-gases remain waste-management obligations; a recovery percentage alone is not a complete material balance.
Equipment must provide phase contact and phase disengagement within shielded, confined systems. Remotely maintained contactors and settlers are equipment categories serving those functions; sealed transfer systems, shielded cells and monitored off-gas handling limit contamination and exposure. Cooling manages residual decay heat, and nuclear material accountancy follows inventories and losses. criticality control in radioactive waste is essential because solvent extraction can redistribute fissile constituents. Scoped limitation: this answer gives the chemical principles and equipment functions; operational fissile-separation recipes, reagent sequences, contactor design details and process conditions are intentionally not supplied.

evaporation transfers water to a vapour stream and retains mainly nonvolatile solutes in a concentrate. It can give substantial volume reduction and works across many dissolved-solute compositions. Its disadvantages are heat demand, scaling and corrosion; aerosols, entrainment or volatile radioactive species can contaminate the condensate. Condensate must therefore be sampled or monitored rather than assumed releasable, while the concentrate requires radioactive waste conditioning. A demister limits droplet carryover but does not remove a genuinely volatile contaminant.
ion exchange transfers dissolved radioactive ions to an exchanger, often an ion-exchange resin. It is useful for polishing relatively dilute aqueous streams and can provide selective removal without evaporating the bulk water. Competing inactive ions consume capacity, suspended particles foul the bed, and a neutral or poorly retained species may pass through. Breakthrough measurements determine when the exchanger ceases to meet its target; exhausted resin or regeneration liquors become secondary radioactive waste. Separation performance must be established for the actual feed chemistry, not inferred from the name of the radionuclide alone.
Chemical precipitation with solid–liquid separation uses precipitation, coprecipitation and adsorption to carry contaminants into solids, followed by settling or filtration. It can treat substantial volumes and complex feeds at moderate equipment cost. Its disadvantages are a relatively bulky sludge, incomplete removal of soluble species, and sensitivity to pH, complexation and competing ions. The sludge must be dewatered and conditioned, and the clarified liquid checked for residual radioactive activity. Polishing by another method may still be needed.
In each case, measure both the treated stream and the residue so that the radioactive inventory balance can be checked. Treatment transfers or concentrates radioactive activity; only radioactive decay reduces the number of radioactive parent nuclei. These methods and the distinction between treatment and conditioning are set out in the institutional waste-management framework.[ref]

Synroc immobilises waste ions in selected durable crystalline hosts, rather than trying to put the whole inventory into one universal site. The underlying philosophy is that minerals retaining analogous elements over geological times suggest useful crystal structures for engineered containment. These natural analogues motivate the choice; their survival does not prove that every synthetic composition, damaged microstructure or groundwater environment will behave identically. This was the rationale of the original mineral-analogue proposal.[ref]
A representative Synroc-C assemblage includes zirconolite, CaZrTi2​O7​, as an actinide host; a hollandite waste-form phase, represented by BaAl2​Ti6​O16​, whose tunnels accommodate large cations such as caesium; and a perovskite waste-form phase, CaTiO3​, which can accept strontium on calcium sites and other constituents with suitable charge compensation in a crystal. Titanium-oxide phases provide additional accommodation and composition flexibility. The actual phases are solid solutions, not simply pure end-member minerals mixed with an inert waste powder.[ref]
Manufacture combines a characterised waste feed with selected oxide-forming precursors, converts the mixture to a suitable powder or calcination product, and consolidates it while forming the host phases. hot pressing and hot isostatic pressing are consolidation approaches; the latter uses pressure acting approximately equally in all directions. The objective is a dense ceramic with the intended phase assemblage and low connected porosity, contained in an appropriate radioactive waste package. Verify density and phase composition, use microscopy to find segregated constituents or pores, and test leaching of the actual product. Successful densification alone does not establish radionuclide retention.[ref]

The host is zirconolite, with ideal end-member composition CaZrTi2​O7​. In the proposed formula, the substitution is formally
Zr4+⟷Pu4+.
(24)
It is an isovalent substitution: the mean charge of the mixed zirconium/plutonium site remains +4, so
2+[0.2(4)+0.8(4)]+2(4)=14=7(2).
(25)
No compensating oxygen vacancy or aliovalent co-substituent is required by this particular charge account. Suitable oxidation state is essential; the same formula interpreted with only trivalent plutonium would not be charge balanced.
In zirconolite-2M the zirconium site has an oxygen coordination number of seven. The ionic radius of plutonium(IV) is larger than that of zirconium(IV), so increasing its occupancy changes local strain and the preferred arrangement of the cation layers. The observed progression in the investigated substitution series was zirconolite-2M → zirconolite-4M → pyrochlore structure, rather than unlimited substitution in one unchanged monoclinic lattice. The primary study found a 2M solid-solution limit near x=0.15, a narrow 4M range near x=0.41, and a pyrochlore composition at x=0.60 in CaZr1−x​Pux​Ti2​O7​. These are experimental observations for that series, not universal boundaries independent of preparation and oxidation state.[ref]
Consequently the nominal x=0.20 composition identifies a zirconolite-based waste-form design, but does not by itself prove a pure 2M product. Diffraction and phase-composition measurements are needed. At larger substitution, the fluorite-related pyrochlore arrangement accommodates a different cation distribution and coordination environment.

gadolinium is primarily a strong neutron absorber. The supplied thermal cross section for gadolinium-157 is very large, so capture removes neutrons that could otherwise sustain nuclear fission. This is a criticality-control role rather than a requirement to complete the host formula. The isotope-specific number must not be assigned unchanged to natural gadolinium.
hafnium supplies an additional neutron poison with favourable retention chemistry. Its supplied natural-abundance thermal cross section is much smaller than that of gadolinium-157 but still appreciable. Chemically, tetravalent hafnium resembles zirconium and can remain associated with a durable titanate host. Keeping the absorber with the fissile inventory during alteration is as important as its initial neutron capture strength: a highly absorbing constituent offers poor long-term protection if it is preferentially lost. These considerations explain using complementary absorbers rather than ranking them solely by their initial thermal cross sections.
uranium addition chiefly addresses isotopic dilution, including the long-term daughter inventory. plutonium-239 alpha decay produces uranium-235. Added uranium-238 lowers the uranium-235 fraction of that future uranium inventory; depleted uranium adds less uranium-235 initially than natural uranium. uranium-238 also absorbs some neutrons, but its small listed cross section does not make it a substitute for the strong absorbers. The long-term daughter issue and the retention of gadolinium and hafnium are explicit concerns in ceramic waste-form development.[ref]
These are complementary physical purposes, not proof of criticality control in radioactive waste from three cross-section values. neutron energy, isotopic composition, geometry, moderation, absorber distribution and their coupled evolution matter. Incorporation in the same durable waste form helps maintain association, but neither uranium addition nor chemical immobilisation makes deliberate element separation physically impossible.

Let c(t) be the fraction still crystalline. Under the direct-impact amorphization model, damage events occur randomly and independently; an event striking already amorphous material does not create that much additional amorphous volume. Only the crystalline fraction can be newly converted, so
dtdc​=−Rvc,c(0)=1,fa​(t)=1−c(t)=1−e−Rvt.
(26)
The product of the given event rate density and damage volume has units of inverse time:
k=Rv=(5.685×1014)(1.706×10−25)=9.69861×10−11s−1.
(27)
For 99% radiation-induced amorphization, c=0.01, giving
t99​=k−ln(0.01)​=4.74828×1010s=1504.64yr​.
(28)
Using 365.25 days per year, the corresponding calendar date is approximately 3555 CE, measured from disposal preparation in 2050.
A linear estimate fa​=Rvt misses repeated hits to already damaged regions and therefore predicts too rapid an approach to 99%. The exponential function model never reaches exactly 100% in finite time. Its assumptions include constant dose rate, fixed effective damage volume, no annealing and no spatial correlation of damage. Actual alpha decay generates recoil damage as well as the alpha particle's track; the stated damage volume must be understood as the effective volume per radioactive decay for the chosen model.
Amorphous is not synonymous with dissolved or failed containment. Radiation damage can change swelling, connected porosity and leach behaviour, so testing must compare structural evolution with element release. Accelerated self-irradiation and parallel dissolution measurements are useful precisely because crystallinity alone is not the performance endpoint.[ref]

A useful three-product account is a demonstrated site end state, recovered materials, and wastes requiring disposal or continuing management. It keeps the land outcome distinct from the material streams.
The first product is the land and any retained buildings in the specified nuclear decommissioning end state. Characterisation and final surveys must show that the remaining inventory is consistent with the intended reuse and any retained restrictions. Demolition is a possible means of reaching that state, not its definition. The associated records and final radiological evidence are part of the handover.
The second product is reusable or recyclable material and equipment. Segregation and, where appropriate, decontamination prevent clean bulk material from being unnecessarily mixed with contaminated material. Release from radiological control requires demonstrated radiological clearance; conventional chemical hazards still need their own management.
The third product is waste: both ordinary unrecoverable demolition debris and radioactive waste that remains under control. Activated structures, contaminated components and secondary cleaning wastes may require different treatment and packages. spent nuclear fuel, if still present, requires its own inventory and management route rather than being counted as ordinary scrap. Thus the material outputs can alternatively be tabulated as reusable material, conventional waste and radioactive waste, while the restored site is the overarching project deliverable. The source does not define a unique named three-product taxonomy.[ref]

The traditional threefold classification is immediate dismantling, deferred dismantling and entombment of a nuclear facility. It appears in the older IAEA WS-R-5 classification.[ref]
With immediate dismantling, decontamination and removal follow permanent shutdown without a prolonged safe-storage interval. Operating staff, drawings and knowledge remain available, the project can reach its end state sooner, and the site can return to use earlier. Against this, substantial funds and waste-management routes are needed early, and less time has elapsed for short-lived activation products to radioactive decay. Exposure and remote-handling requirements may therefore be greater for a particular task.
With deferred dismantling, the facility is put into a maintained safe condition before later dismantling. radioactive decay can reduce some later radiation fields and permit better tools or disposal routes to become available. However, deferral does not remove the waste or responsibility: surveillance, security, building maintenance and preservation of records continue. Deterioration or loss of institutional knowledge can offset the benefit. Long-lived constituents may obtain little useful reduction over a practicable delay.
entombment of a nuclear facility encases radioactive structures within a long-lived barrier. It can reduce the need for immediate intrusive work in exceptional circumstances, but leaves a persistent in-situ inventory, restricts site use and requires confidence in barriers, groundwater behaviour and long-term institutional arrangements. It transfers management obligations rather than simply completing ordinary unrestricted site release.
There is an important qualification to the historical wording: GSR Part 6, published in 2014 and therefore already applicable to interpreting a 2021 question, recognises the first two planned strategies and excludes entombment of a nuclear facility for planned permanent shutdown. Paragraph 1.10 allows it to be considered only exceptionally, such as after a severe accident. It would therefore be misleading to present all three historical labels as equally acceptable normal planned strategies.[ref]

Let ce​ be the measured mass concentration of element e, ce,b​ its blank concentration, V the leachate volume, fe​ the element's mass fraction in the original glass, and S the exposed glass surface area. For one closed test, the normalised elemental release is
NLe​=fe​S(ce​−ce,b​)V​​.
(29)
If concentration is in gL−1, volume in litres and area in square metres, NLe​ is in gm−2 of glass-equivalent release. Dividing by fe​ removes the trivial difference caused by loading different amounts of the measured element; dividing by S removes the first-order geometrical scale difference. Normalising only by initial element content or reporting raw solution concentration does not accomplish both corrections.
For repeated sampling with leachant replacement, use cumulative released mass: add the mass removed in all earlier aliquots to the mass present in the final remaining solution, with the corresponding blank correction. A flow-through experiment instead integrates Q(t)[ce,out​(t)−ce,in​(t)] over time. State how surface area was determined and whether it is held at its initial value in the reported normalisation; cracks, fines and alteration can change the genuinely reactive area.
Compare NLe​(t) for several constituents. In congruent glass dissolution, the different element-normalised curves coincide, provided the released elements remain in solution. Preferential early alkali release can indicate ion exchange, whereas lower dissolved silicon or actinide release can reflect an alteration layer, sorption or secondary-phase precipitation. boron is often useful as a relatively mobile dissolution tracer, but not if the leachant contains a large boron background or if it is itself retained. A low aqueous concentration of one constituent is therefore not automatically a low glass-dissolution rate.
A near-linear NLe​(t) interval gives an approximately constant normalised elemental release rate. A square-root time dependence can be consistent with diffusion-limited transport, and decreasing slopes can indicate saturation or a protective alteration layer; no one curve shape proves a unique mechanism. Compare solution inventories with the altered solid by microscopy, composition and phase analysis. Differences in pH, temperature, leachant composition, duration and surface-area-to-volume ratio remain even after this mass-and-area normalisation. Careful background subtraction and control of solution conditions are experimentally consequential, as demonstrated in primary flow-through measurements.[ref]

Use a single-pass flow-through leach test. A controlled leachant passes once over a known glass surface area, and successive effluent samples are analysed after the initial transient. temperature and pH are controlled, and fresh solution removes dissolved products so that they do not accumulate towards saturation with respect to the dissolving glass. This approaches the forward glass dissolution rate at those specified conditions.
At steady state, with Q expressed as volume per time,
re​=fe​SQ(ce,out​−ce,in​)​​.
(30)
Increase Q/S across a series of tests until the normalised rate becomes independent of further dilution, within analytical uncertainty. A genuine plateau supports approach to the dilute-solution limit; it should not be assumed from one arbitrarily chosen flow rate. Check blanks, inlet concentrations, temperature, pH, actual flow, fines and exposed surface area. At high flow the concentration increment becomes small, so an uncorrected background can manufacture an apparent rate increase.
The resulting maximum is the forward rate for the specified temperature, pH and glass, not an absolute maximum over every possible environment. It also differs from the lower residual rate that can develop in a static, product-rich leachate. Primary measurements demonstrate the method and the importance of background subtraction and flow variation.[ref]
