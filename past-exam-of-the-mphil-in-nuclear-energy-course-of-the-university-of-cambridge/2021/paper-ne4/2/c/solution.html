For six hours of operation at the maximum stated carbon-dioxide-equivalent flow, the daily input volume and untreated radioactive activity inventory are

For the three-hour guaranteed delay, every element of the incoming stream acquires the same radioactive decay factor. The radioactive activity released per operating day, attributing the delayed tail to its production day, is
Dd​=Dd,0​e−λ(10800)=1.712×109Bq​.
(18)
Even on the conservative assumption of operation on all 365 days,
Dy​=365Dd​=6.248×1011Bqyr−1​,
(19)
so the annual discharge is below the supplied 1012 Bq limit. For 250 operating days it is 4.279×1011 Bq. This sums radioactive activity inventories discharged over the year; it does not credit radioactive decay in the environment after release. It also retains the final delayed gas after production stops, rather than bypassing the retention system to empty it.
The residence-time qualification matters numerically. A single tank filled for six hours and released immediately, with radioactive decay during filling but no later hold, would contain
Afill​(21600)=6.502×1010Bq.
(20)
Repeating that operation on 250 days releases 1.625×1013 Bq, above the limit. A well-mixed or fill-and-dump system therefore cannot inherit the plug-flow compliance result just because its nominal volume divided by flow equals the selected delay time. The total nitrogen flow and any unretained radioactive carbon species must also be included in a real measured release account.
