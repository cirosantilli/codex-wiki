Use a single-pass flow-through leach test. A controlled leachant passes once over a known glass surface area, and successive effluent samples are analysed after the initial transient. temperature and pH are controlled, and fresh solution removes dissolved products so that they do not accumulate towards saturation with respect to the dissolving glass. This approaches the forward glass dissolution rate at those specified conditions.
At steady state, with Q expressed as volume per time,
re​=fe​SQ(ce,out​−ce,in​)​​.
(30)
Increase Q/S across a series of tests until the normalised rate becomes independent of further dilution, within analytical uncertainty. A genuine plateau supports approach to the dilute-solution limit; it should not be assumed from one arbitrarily chosen flow rate. Check blanks, inlet concentrations, temperature, pH, actual flow, fines and exposed surface area. At high flow the concentration increment becomes small, so an uncorrected background can manufacture an apparent rate increase.
The resulting maximum is the forward rate for the specified temperature, pH and glass, not an absolute maximum over every possible environment. It also differs from the lower residual rate that can develop in a static, product-rich leachate. Primary measurements demonstrate the method and the importance of background subtraction and flow variation.[ref]
