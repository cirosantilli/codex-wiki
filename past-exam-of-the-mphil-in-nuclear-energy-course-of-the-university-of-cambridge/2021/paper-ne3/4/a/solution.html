Mixed oxide fuel begins as a specified mixture of uranium and plutonium oxides. A fuel pin develops strong radial temperature gradients during operation. The components and oxygen need not remain spatially uniform: their chemical potentials depend on temperature, local composition and oxygen-to-metal ratio.
Actinide redistribution in oxide fuel can involve thermally driven solid-state diffusion and evaporation–condensation transport across pores or a central cavity. Oxygen redistributes as well, changing local oxidation states and the relative thermodynamic tendencies of uranium- and plutonium-bearing species. Pore migration, grain growth and central-void formation couple compositional redistribution to fuel restructuring. Differential local burnup also changes composition over time.
The direction and magnitude of plutonium migration cannot be inferred from a temperature gradient alone. In particular irradiated fast-reactor pins, enrichment around a central cavity has been observed, but the result depends on stoichiometry and operating history. M. Bober and colleagues investigated thermal-diffusion and evaporation–condensation effects using autoradiographs of sections of fuel irradiated in RAPSODIE.[ref]
Redistribution changes the radial heat generation rate and material properties. Concentrating more fissile material near an already hot region can raise local heat generation; changes in thermal conductivity and local melting behavior modify the temperature response. Gas release, pore formation and fuel–cladding contact can also change thermal resistance and mechanical loading. A spatially averaged composition can therefore underestimate a local temperature or chemical interaction.
Segregated fission products add another concern: mobile species can accumulate at colder interfaces or react with the cladding, while insoluble products form separate phases. Fuel performance must therefore track composition, temperature and microstructure together, rather than treating segregation as merely a cosmetic change in an otherwise fixed fuel.
