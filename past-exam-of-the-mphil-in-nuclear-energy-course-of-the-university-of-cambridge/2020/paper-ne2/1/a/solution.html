Take one-dimensional steady state thermal conduction, constant thermal conductivity k>0, and positive volumetric heat generation rate. Write q0​=q0′′′​ for brevity, and let x increase away from the illuminated face. Conservation of energy and Fourier's law give
dxd​(−kdxdT​)=q0​e−μx,T(0)=T(a)=T0​.
(1)
Thus T′′=−(q0​/k)e−μx<0: the profile is strictly concave and any stationary point is its unique maximum. Two integrations and the equal-surface boundary conditions yield the exponential slab heating solution
T(x)−T0​=kμ2q0​​[1−e−μx−(1−e−μa)ax​].​
(2)
Its derivative is
T′(x)=kμq0​​[e−μx−μa1−e−μa​].
(3)
Define β=μa and C=(1−e−β)/β. Setting T′(xm​)=0 gives the distance from the illuminated surface:
xm​=μ1​ln1−e−μaμa​=−μlnC​.​
(4)
For β>0, e−β<C<1, so 0<xm​<a. More specifically C=e−β/2sinh(β/2)/(β/2)>e−β/2, hence xm​<a/2: the peak is nearer the more strongly heated face.
The apparent singularity at μ=0 is removable. Expansion gives
axm​​=21​−24β​+O(β3),T(x)−T0​⟶2kq0​​x(a−x).
(5)
The uniform-heating limit therefore has its maximum at a/2, as symmetry requires.
