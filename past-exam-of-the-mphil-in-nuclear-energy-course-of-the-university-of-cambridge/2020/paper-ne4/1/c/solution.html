Treat the 5.3% recycled product as replacing the same uranium mass of ordinary 5.0% nuclear fuel: the additional uranium-235 compensates for the supplied uranium-236 penalty. It displaces fresh natural-uranium feed in the ratio calculated in part (b), hence

The remaining natural feed is 313.36245 tUyr−1, a 9.885% resource saving relative to the full-power once-through requirement. This credits one uranium recycle only; it adds no second-pass recycling or plutonium credit.
Separative work must be charged for producing the recycled nuclear fuel as well as credited for displaced fresh nuclear fuel. With uranium-235 mass fraction x, use the separative value function
V(x)=(1−2x)lnx1−x​,S=PV(xp​)+WV(xw​)−FV(xf​).
(9)

The recycled product still requires
Sr​=1000[Pr​V(0.053)+Wr​V(0.003)−Fr​V(0.0085)]=19726.2085 kg-SWUyr−1.
(12)
The net separative-work saving is consequently
ΔS=Savoided​−Sr​=1592.7590 kg-SWUyr−1​.
(13)
As a whole-cycle check, the fresh route requires 215668.0851 kg-SWU per year, while the mixed fresh-plus-recycled supply requires 214075.3261 kg-SWU. The net saving is only 0.7385% of the original work. The recovered stream has a higher uranium-235 assay than natural feed, but the higher required product assay uses most of the work credit. These equations are resource accounting and do not specify an uranium enrichment plant or its operation.
