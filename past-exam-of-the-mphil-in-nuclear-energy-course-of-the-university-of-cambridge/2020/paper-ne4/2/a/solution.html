A parent initially at rest emits an alpha particle and a recoiling heavy daughter. momentum conservation gives equal and opposite momenta, so their nonrelativistic kinetic energies satisfy
Tα​Td​​=Md​mα​​,Tα​=QMd​+mα​Md​​,Td​=QMd​+mα​mα​​.
(16)
Here Q is the kinetic energy available to this branch; excitation of the daughter reduces it and may be followed by photon emission. For an illustrative Q=5 MeV, mα​=4u and Md​=236u, the energies are 4.917 MeV in the alpha particle and 83.3 keV in the daughter. The heavy daughter receives much less energy, but that does not imply much less atomic damage.
The fast alpha particle loses most of its energy through electronic stopping: ionization and electronic excitation along a relatively long path. It also undergoes nuclear collisions, particularly towards the end of its range, and can produce displacement damage. The slower, heavy, highly charged recoil couples strongly to surrounding nuclei and generates a compact displacement cascade. Successive knock-on atoms create vacancies, interstitial defects and clusters. Some Frenkel pairs recombine as the cascade relaxes; the initial collision count is therefore not the number of defects that remain.
A quantitative illustration comes from brannerite rather than an assumed universal ceramic. Simulations in the primary natural-mineral study used a 4.5 MeV alpha particle and an 80 keV daughter recoil. The corresponding ranges were about 13 micrometres and 23 nanometres. At the study's assumed 50 eV threshold displacement energy, its tabulated results give roughly 560–571 recoil atomic displacements and 91–100 alpha particle atomic displacements per radioactive decay. The numbers depend strongly on the displacement threshold and material, but the shorter recoil path contains the dominant and much denser ballistic radiation damage.[ref]
In a susceptible ceramic, cascade cores can accumulate into an amorphous fraction; defects and associated relaxation can also change volume, thermal conductivity and mechanical response. molecular dynamics simulations of zirconolite found a strongly disordered core surrounded by more isolated point defects, supporting this distinction between local cascade damage and an entirely transformed specimen.[ref]
The alpha particle eventually becomes a helium atom after slowing and charge exchange. helium accumulation can have later microstructural effects distinct from the initial knock-on cascade. Material-dependent recombination and annealing limit surviving disorder; a change in diffraction or radiation-induced swelling is not automatically radiation-induced amorphization, and radiation-induced amorphization is not automatically dissolution or release of the contained radionuclide.
