Both terms concern time-dependent strain under stress. Here radiation-enhanced creep means irradiation accelerating a mechanism that also operates thermally, whereas radiation-induced creep emphasises deformation driven by irradiation-specific defect fluxes. The literature sometimes uses irradiation creep as an umbrella, so identifying the mechanism is more informative than treating the names as mutually exclusive experimental categories.
radiation-enhanced diffusion maintains mobile point-defect concentrations above their thermal equilibrium values. Under a load, this can accelerate diffusion-mediated deformation or dislocation climb and thereby enhance a creep mechanism. The effect depends on temperature, defect production, recombination and sinks; simply raising the specimen temperature by radiation heating is a different explanation and must be controlled.
radiation-induced creep can remain important where unirradiated creep is small. stress-induced preferential absorption gives differently oriented dislocations unequal capture of irradiation-produced defects, producing directional climb. Climb can also enable further glide by relieving obstacles. A schematic low-stress relation is

Compare stressed and unstressed specimens in and out of irradiation at matched actual temperature, and resolve longitudinal, transverse and volumetric strains. Unstressed irradiation growth or void swelling is not itself evidence of creep, and stress-enhanced void swelling must not be included automatically in a fitted creep strain. These controls distinguish a mechanical response to irradiation defects from ordinary creep or a different dimensional-change mechanism.
