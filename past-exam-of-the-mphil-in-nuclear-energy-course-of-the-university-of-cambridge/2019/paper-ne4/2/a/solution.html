helium changes cavity stability and ductility; void swelling changes bulk volume and component dimensions. These effects interact, but a helium bubble and a vacancy-rich void are not interchangeable descriptions.
neutron reactions produce helium from alloy constituents and impurities. For example, capture by boron-10 can produce helium through 10B+n⟶7Li+4He; the production per displacement dose depends on composition and neutron spectrum. Retained helium has low solubility and is trapped at defects or in bubbles. Bubble nucleation and growth at grain boundaries can assist intergranular cavitation, lowering tensile ductility and creep-rupture resistance, particularly when temperature permits the relevant transport. This is important even before a large bulk void swelling is measurable. A primary study linked high-temperature ductility loss to observed bubbles and grain-boundary weakening in irradiated stainless steel.[ref]
displacement damage continuously creates vacancies and self-interstitials. Their different capture efficiencies at dislocations and other sinks can leave a vacancy supersaturation available to cavities. A stable cavity grows when its net vacancy uptake exceeds vacancy emission and interstitial absorption. helium can help stabilise small nuclei, but the later volume increase is not simply the volume of gas inserted into an unchanged matrix. Changing helium content can also change cavity number, size and competition for point defects; a universal proportionality between helium concentration and void swelling is not justified.
For nuclear fuel cladding, void swelling can enlarge the diameter, alter the fuel–nuclear fuel cladding gap, reduce clearances and distort reactor coolant channels or nearby structures. Nonuniform void swelling generates additional stresses. Bubble-assisted embrittlement and stress-dependent creep then affect the ability of the wall to contain the fuel and internal pressure. The relevant measurements combine density or volume change, dimensional strain, cavity microscopy and mechanical tests. Pressurised-tube experiments explicitly separated void swelling, creep, plastic strain and precipitation strain; void swelling itself was approximately isotropic in that study.[ref]
