Write SA=S for the exposed glass area. If Ce​ is the blank-corrected dissolved mass concentration of element e, xe​ its initial mass fraction in the glass and V the leachate volume, then
NLe​=xe​(SA/V)Ce​​=xe​SACe​V​​.
(7)
The numerator is the released element mass measured in solution. Dividing by xe​ converts this to an equivalent mass of the original glass, while dividing by SA expresses that mass per exposed area. With Ce​ in gL−1 and SA/V in m2L−1, NLe​ has units gm−2 of glass-equivalent release. The fraction xe​ must be used as a fraction, not an unconverted percentage.
These corrections allow comparison of specimens of different size and composition without confusing a lower concentration with better durability. State how the area was measured; geometric area and a rough powder's accessible area need not coincide. For repeated sampling, include element mass removed in earlier aliquots and the volume remaining; for an initial blank in the leachant, subtract its corresponding inventory. Surface cracking and evolving porosity can make a rate based on initial area differ from a rate based on the true reactive area.
Under congruent glass dissolution, constituents enter solution in their bulk proportions, so their correctly normalised releases coincide. Preferential sodium release by ion exchange can instead give NLNa​>NLSi​, while silicon retention in a gel or secondary precipitate can depress its dissolved release. Noncoincident curves show nonstoichiometric aqueous release, but do not uniquely identify the mechanism. Conversely, solution coincidence does not by itself establish that no secondary reactions occurred. A complete inference compares solution inventories with altered solids.
Normalisation removes composition and first-order area/volume scaling; it does not make different pH, temperature, solution composition or contact histories equivalent. In a batch test, even the same initial water changes composition and pH as reaction proceeds. Reporting those conditions and time is essential for a transferable comparison rather than treating NLe​ as a condition-independent material constant.
