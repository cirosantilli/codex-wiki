Spherical polytropic flow with adiabatic exponent three halves

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Compressible flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
For steady spherical isentropic flow with p=Kρ3/2 around a point mass, put j=r2ρv>0 and E=v2/2+2c2−GM/r>0. Eliminating the mass density gives c2=[j(3K/2)2]2/5r−4/5M−2/5. With rs​=GM/(4E), x=(r/rs​)1/5, y=M2/5 and λ=[j(3K/2)2]2/5/(2Ers4/5​), the Bernoulli equation reduces to λF(y)=F(x), where F(t)=t4+4/t. Since F has its unique minimum at t=1, a regular sonic point requires λ=1 and r=rs​. One transonic branch is y=x, which has v=2E​, ρ∝r−2 and p∝r−3. The decreasing branch has inner free fall and an outer nearly static reservoir, giving Bondi accretion for inward fluid flow. A nonradiative stationary shock wave keeps j,E,rs​ fixed but raises K, so λ increases by the factor (K2​/K1​)4/5. The inward transonic solution is the spherical n=2, γ=3/2 specialization of transonic accretion in a power-law tube.
