SPEAR Mark I tau discovery experiment (1975)

 ... Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Standard Model fermion Lepton Tau (particle)
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
Counterrotating electron and Positron beams at SPEAR collided inside the Mark I magnetic particle detector. Lead–scintillator scintillation counters identified electrons through large shower pulses; small pulses plus hits beyond the iron return identified muons.[ref]
The selection required opposite-charge electron–muon tracks, no detected photons, momenta above 0.65GeV/c, and transverse noncoplanarity above 20∘. Multitrack hadron samples calibrated momentum-dependent misidentification; collinear lepton pairs checked identification losses. Photon and charge control samples tested backgrounds. Acceptance corrections depended on the proposed signal.[ref]
The 1975 claim was anomalous events without a conventional explanation; new-particle pair production was a possible interpretation.[ref] Martin Lewis Perl's lecture describes subsequent evidence for a charged lepton.[ref]
The particle identification problem matters as much as the apparent missing four-momentum in electron-positron collisions. A penetrating hadron can imitate a muon; a particle shower can imitate an electron; an undetected photon or a detector gap can imitate invisible recoil. An absence of additional recorded signals is conditional on detection efficiency. In a proposed signal model, the selected count has the schematic form
Nselected​=Lσε+Nbackground​,
(41)
where L is integrated collider luminosity, σ a cross section, and ε the combined acceptance and detection efficiency. A single count cannot determine all three factors. A control sample must probe the relevant momentum and angular range; its abundant events do not automatically calibrate a differently distributed signal.
