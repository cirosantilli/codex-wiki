Reaction rate (v)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 9
For one chemical reaction in a closed system of fixed volume V, with uniform concentrations and no competing reactions or appreciable accumulation of unlisted species, choose the chemical equation
aA+bB⟶cC,a,b,c>0.
(133)
Brackets denote amount of substance per volume, for example [A]=nA​/V. The stoichiometry gives one instantaneous reaction rate
v=−a1​dtd[A]​=−b1​dtd[B]​=c1​dtd[C]​=V1​dtdξ​,
(134)
where ξ is the existing extent of reaction. The minus signs make forward consumption positive; dividing by the coefficients makes measurements of different chemical species agree. Units can be molL−1s−1. See IUPAC Gold Book: rate of reaction and Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, §12.1: chemical reaction rates.
For the illustrative chemical equation 2A+B⟶3C, a measured d[C]/dt=0.060molL−1s−1 implies v=0.020molL−1s−1, d[A]/dt=−0.040molL−1s−1, and d[B]/dt=−0.020molL−1s−1. These numbers express stoichiometry, without supplying a rate law.
Each derivative is the slope of a concentration curve at one instant. Over a finite interval, −Δ[A]/(aΔt) instead gives an average reaction rate. For A converting reversibly to B, the net reaction rate is v=d[B]/dt=Jf​−Jr​; both directional reaction rates can remain positive when v=0 at chemical equilibrium.
