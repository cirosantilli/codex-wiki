Exact tidal acceleration between separated masses (Δa=2μL/[R3(1−L2/(4R2))2])

OurBigBook.com Words: 187
Stipulate a fixed source point mass M=1012kg, two equal test particles of mass m=1kg, and G=6.67430×10−11m3kg−1s−2. This is a chosen model, not a measurement of a particular object. Place the test particles on the outward radial axis at R−L/2 and R+L/2, with midpoint distance R=100m and separation L=2m. Neglect their mutual attraction, other sources and all supporting forces.
Each signed radial gravitational acceleration is inward:
a−​=−(R−L/2)2μ​≈−0.0068098153ms−2,a+​=−(R+L/2)2μ​≈−0.0065427899ms−2.
(16)
Their separation has outward relative acceleration
L¨=a+​−a−​=μ[(R−L/2)21​−(R+L/2)21​]=R32μL​(1−4R2L2​)−2.
(17)
The exact value is 2.6702540×10−4ms−2. The midpoint tidal tensor predicts 2μL/R3=2.6697200×10−4ms−2, an underestimate of 0.019999% relative to the exact value. For these equal 1kg masses, the difference of external forces has the same numerical value in the SI unit of force, the newton. It is not an additional mutual force between the test particles.
The exact formula requires L<2R and describes the instantaneous relative acceleration. Because the two sample positions are symmetric about the midpoint, the Taylor expansion error relative to the linear result starts at order (L/R)2; a one-sided comparison with a reference point generally has relative error of order L/R. If either test particle enters an extended source, use that source's interior Newtonian gravitational field instead of extrapolating the singular point mass model.
