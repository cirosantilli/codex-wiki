Quantum state continuity at a finite Hamiltonian step (∣ψ(0+)⟩=∣ψ(0−)⟩)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Sudden approximation
OurBigBook.com Words: 417 Articles: 3
For a fixed Hilbert space, a bounded self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator with a finite step Hi​→Hf​ at t=0 does not deliver an instantaneous rotation of the quantum state. Integrating the Schrödinger equation across a short interval gives
∣ψ(ϵ)⟩−∣ψ(−ϵ)⟩=−ℏi​∫−ϵϵ​H(t)∣ψ(t)⟩dt.
(25)
For a normalized quantum state and ∥H(t)∥≤M in operator norm, the difference has norm at most 2ϵM/ℏ, which tends to zero. The time derivative can jump even though the quantum state is continuous. Here ℏ is the reduced Planck constant. An instantaneous replacement of the eigenbasis changes coordinates, not the vector being represented.
For a real switch occupying 0≤t≤τ, remove any real scalar contribution a(t)I that produces only a global phase, and write ∣ψ​(t)⟩=ei∫0t​a(s)ds/ℏ∣ψ(t)⟩. The same integral argument gives the sufficient bounded-model estimate
​ψ​(τ)−ψ(0)​≤ε,ε=ℏ1​∫0τ​∥H(t)−a(t)I∥dt.
(26)
Thus ε≪1 controls the sudden approximation up to a global phase. For any normalized final energy eigenstate, its probability amplitude differs from the instantaneous-switch value by at most ε, and its Born rule probability differs by at most 2ε. These are sufficient error bounds, not necessary criteria. For the fixed-gap two-level sudden quench, any switching path H(t)=−Δn(t)⋅σ/2 with a unit vector n(t) has operator norm Δ/2, giving ε≤Δτ/(2ℏ).
This estimate is deliberately for bounded Hamiltonian operators. An unbounded energy spectrum does not supply a finite global M; relevant quantum states, operator domains and excited energy eigenvalues require separate control. The adiabatic theorem also requires hypotheses about the path and its spectral gaps; there is no universal switch-duration criterion covering all spectra. The cold-atom collapse and revival after a lattice quench illustrates how a single switch can be sudden for one degree of freedom and slow for another.
