Elliptical polarization (Ex2​/Ax2​+Ey2​/Ay2​−2Ex​Ey​cosδ/(Ax​Ay​)=sin2δ)

 Home Physics Wave Polarization (waves)
OurBigBook.com Words: 363
For an elliptically polarized wave, the tip of its transverse vector traces an ellipse at a fixed point during each wave period. For polarization of an electromagnetic wave, this is the electric field vector. With the real components in polarization of waves, assume Ax​,Ay​>0 and sinδ=0. Eliminating time gives
Ax2​Ex2​​+Ay2​Ey2​​−Ax​Ay​2Ex​Ey​​cosδ=sin2δ.
(57)
A short derivation uses ϕ=ωt and the cosine addition formula to obtain Ey​/Ay​=(Ex​/Ax​)cosδ−sinϕsinδ; squaring and using the sine and cosine identity sin2ϕ=1−cos2ϕ gives the displayed equation. Outside phase quadrature, the mixed term gives a tilted ellipse; Ax​,Ay​ are component wave amplitudes and need not be its semi-major and semi-minor axes.
In phase quadrature, δ=±π/2, the mixed term vanishes. For (Ax​,Ay​)=(2,1)V/m, the ellipse is Ex2​/4+Ey2​=1, its semi-major and semi-minor axes have lengths 2 and 1V/m, and its electric field magnitude varies between 1 and 2V/m. At ϕ=π/4 with δ=π/2, the electric field is (2​,−1/2​)V/m and has magnitude 5/2​V/m. Equal wave amplitudes instead give circular polarization, a special case of elliptical polarization with constant magnitude. As δ approaches 0 or π, the ellipse collapses to a line segment and gives linear polarization; this limit is degenerate rather than a nondegenerate ellipse.
Changing δ from π/2 to −π/2 reverses traversal of the same ellipse. In the displayed x,y plot, δ=π/2 moves from (Ax​,0) toward negative y as time increases. A label such as right- or left-handed circular polarization additionally requires a stated viewing direction and naming convention: viewing along propagation and viewing against propagation reverse the apparent rotation. [ref].
The ellipse records both the relative wave amplitudes and the phase difference of two transverse oscillations. This separates a change of polarization of waves from a change of overall wave amplitude: multiplying both components by the same positive factor enlarges the ellipse while preserving its shape and orientation.
A rotating ideal linear polarizer can probe this geometry. For the 2:1 ellipse in phase quadrature, orthogonal projection onto a pass axis at angle θ gives Eout​(t)=2cosθcosωt−sinθsinωt, in V/m. Averaging its square over a wave period gives (4cos2θ+sin2θ)/2, while the incoming squared electric field magnitude averages to 5/2. Thus the transmitted fraction of light intensity is (4cos2θ+sin2θ)/5: it is 4/5 at 0∘, 1/2 at 45∘ and 1/5 at 90∘. There is no single incident line of linear polarization to insert into Malus's law; projecting the electric field and averaging its square is the appropriate generalization. This measurement cannot distinguish δ=π/2 from δ=−π/2, because reversing the traversal leaves all these squared averages unchanged.
