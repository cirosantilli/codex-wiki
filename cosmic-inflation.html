Graceful exit from inflation (ϵH​→1)

Words: 51
Cosmic inflation OurBigBook.com
A graceful exit from inflation ends sustained accelerating expansion and connects it to a hot radiation-dominated universe. Crossing ϵH​=1 ends acceleration in a homogeneous expanding model; reheating requires an additional mechanism to transfer inflaton energy into other species. Pure exponential-potential power-law inflation keeps ϵH​ constant and its inflaton potential has no finite minimum, so neither an automatic endpoint nor oscillatory reheating follows from that potential alone.

Power-law inflation (a(t)∝tp,p>1)

Words: 169 Articles: 2
Cosmic inflation OurBigBook.com
Power-law inflation is cosmic inflation with a scale factor proportional to tp for constant p>1. Its Hubble parameter is H=p/t and its first Hubble slow-roll parameter is ϵH​=1/p. The comoving Hubble radius decreases as t1−p. A canonical inflaton with an exponential inflaton potential admits an exact example. Because ϵH​ is constant, an indefinitely maintained power law has no graceful exit from inflation.

Exponential-potential power-law inflation (V=V0​e−cϕ/mP​,a∝t16π/c2)

Words: 125 Articles: 1
Power-law inflation OurBigBook.com
Use natural units, the unreduced Planck mass mP​=G−1/2, V0​>0 and c=0. In a flat universe dominated by a canonical inflaton, ϕ=(2mP​/c)log(βt) and a∝tp solve the inflaton equation of motion and Friedmann equation exactly when p=16π/c2 and β2=V0​c2/[2mP2​(3p−1)]. The positive-potential scaling solution requires p>1/3, while cosmic inflation requires p>1. Its constant equation-of-state parameter is w=−1+2/(3p). In terms of the reduced Planck mass M=mP​/8π​, the exponential slope is λ=c/8π​ and the same exponent is p=2/λ2.

F-term hybrid inflation (W=gS(uv−ζ))

Words: 231 Articles: 3
Cosmic inflation OurBigBook.com
The bilinear hybrid superpotential couples an inflaton S to two waterfall fields u,v. In global supersymmetry, V=∣g∣2(∣uv−ζ∣2+∣S∣2(∣u∣2+∣v∣2)). The branch u=v=0 breaks supersymmetry and is transversely stable for ∣S∣2>∣ζ∣, while the zero-energy branch S=0, uv=ζ is supersymmetric. A canonical Kähler potential in supergravity slightly lifts the inflationary valley. It must not be described as two isolated stationary minima of the full potential.

Supersymmetric vacuum manifold of a bilinear hybrid superpotential (S=0,uv=ζ)

Words: 65
F-term hybrid inflation OurBigBook.com
For nonzero g and ζ, these field values give W=0 and all Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential zero, hence zero supergravity auxiliary fields and zero vacuum energy. With only the supplied chiral fields, the product condition gives a complex vacuum manifold rather than one isolated point. A specified gauged opposite-charge symmetry can add a D-flatness condition and a gauge quotient, but no such gauge sector should be invented from the field labels alone.

Waterfall instability in F-term hybrid inflation (m±2​≃∣g∣2(∣S∣2±∣ζ∣))

Words: 58
F-term hybrid inflation OurBigBook.com
In the small-field global approximation, the waterfall fluctuations on u=v=0 have the displayed masses. One becomes negative when ∣S∣2<∣ζ∣, driving the fields towards the supersymmetric vacuum branch. In the canonical supergravity model, the exact masses follow from expanding the exponential and Kähler-covariant derivatives; their Planck-suppressed corrections should not be discarded unless the corresponding field and symmetry-breaking scales are small.

Approximately constant positive Hubble parameter gives a nearly de Sitter spacetime expansion in flat slicing. Its scale factor can be approximated by a=−1/(Hτ) with negative conformal time increasing toward zero. Slow variation of the expansion rate limits the accuracy of this approximation.

Reheating

Words: 237 Articles: 4
Cosmic inflation OurBigBook.com
Reheating transfers the inflaton energy into other particles after cosmic inflation, eventually establishing a hot thermal bath. It requires interactions in addition to a background scalar potential. The duration and averaged equation-of-state parameter of reheating affect the number of e-folds between a chosen scale's horizon exit and the end of inflation.

Reheating temperature (TR​)

Words: 99 Articles: 1
Reheating OurBigBook.com
The reheating temperature characterizes the thermal radiation bath after energy from a nonthermal component has been transferred and thermalized. For sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic, TR​≃[3MPl2​Γ2/(c2​g⋆​)]1/4. It differs from the residual radiation temperature immediately before decay.

Sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic (ρϕ​≃3MPl2​Γ2)

Words: 95 Articles: 1
Reheating OurBigBook.com

Vacuum domination by a scalar field (ϕ˙​2/2≪V(ϕ))

Words: 57
Inflaton OurBigBook.com
A homogeneous canonical scalar field with positive scalar potential has energy density ρϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2+V and pressure Pϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2−V. When its potential energy dominates its kinetic energy, the equation-of-state parameter approaches −1. If other components and spatial curvature are negligible, the Friedmann acceleration equation gives accelerating expansion. Small initial kinetic energy is not enough to ensure long cosmic inflation; the field must also remain on a sufficiently flat part of the scalar potential, as measured by the potential slow-roll parameters.

Frozen scalar fields and the Friedmann constraint
