RC low-pass step response (vout​(t)=Vs​+(v0​−Vs​)e−t/τ)

The step response describes a sudden persistent input change. Suppose the input becomes a constant Vs​ at t=0, and the initial capacitor voltage is v0​. Finite electric current leaves that voltage continuous. Subtract Vs​ from the RC low-pass filter equation: the remaining error satisfies τde/dt+e=0. Separating variables gives
vout​(t)=Vs​+(v0​−Vs​)e−t/τ(t≥0).
(76)
For an initially uncharged capacitor and a 0 to 3V step in the constructed example,
vout​(t)=3(1−e−t/(103.4μs))V.
(77)
At one time constant, t=103.4μs, the voltage is 1.8964V, about 63.2% of its final value. The voltage error then continues its exponential decay. This is different from a pure delay: the output starts changing immediately, yet takes time to approach the target.
Define settling time relative to the total voltage change: require ∣vout​−Vs​∣≤ϵ∣v0​−Vs​∣ thereafter, with 0<ϵ<1. The exponential decay is monotone, so
tϵ​=τlnϵ1​,fc​tϵ​=2πln(1/ϵ)​.
(78)
For 1% settling, t0.01​=4.6052τ≈476.17μs; the 10% to 90% rise takes τln9≈227.19μs. Reducing the cutoff frequency by doubling C doubles both times. This reciprocal relation is the useful connection between signal bandwidth and response speed: stronger suppression of rapid noise comes with slower tracking of wanted changes.
