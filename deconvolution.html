Deconvolution (g=h∗f ⟼ f)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Convolution
OurBigBook.com Words: 99 Articles: 1
Deconvolution is the inverse problem of recovering an input f from a known response kernel h and measured output g=h∗f. The convolution theorem turns it into G=HF, but division by a small H amplifies noise and a zero of H loses information in the corresponding mode. A unique stable inverse requires assumptions on the operator and admissible inputs; a formal Fourier transform quotient alone does not establish either property.
