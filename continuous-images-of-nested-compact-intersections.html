Continuous images of nested compact intersections (f(⋂n​Kn​)=⋂n​f(Kn​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Compact space
OurBigBook.com Words: 208 Articles: 1
Let X be a Hausdorff space, let Kn​ be a nested sequence of nonempty compact subsets of X, and let f:X→Y be a continuous map into a T1 space. Then the displayed equality holds. The forward inclusion is true for every map. For the reverse, fix y∈⋂n​f(Kn​). Each Fn​=Kn​∩f−1({y}) is nonempty and closed in the compact set K1​: the Kn​ are closed by the Hausdorff assumption, while the fiber is closed because Y is T1 and f is continuous. The nested sets Fn​ have the finite intersection property, so compactness gives a point in their intersection. Its image is y.
The proof only needs the relevant fibers to be closed in K1​, rather than all of Y to be T1. A Hausdorff space as codomain is a familiar sufficient special case. Mere continuity into an arbitrary space does not suffice.
