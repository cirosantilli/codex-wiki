Lagrangian duality (supλ≥0,μ​infu​L(u,λ,μ))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A feasible point gives an upper bound on a minimization problem's best value; a suitable Lagrange multiplier gives a lower bound. Lagrangian duality searches for the strongest bound obtainable this way. Matching bounds certify a global minimum.
Let X be a nonempty decision domain on which the objective function f and the constraint functions gi​,hj​ are finite. Write the primal problem and its optimization Lagrangian as
p∗​=inf{f(x):x∈X, gi​(x)≤0, hj​(x)=0},L(x,λ,ν)=f(x)+∑i=1m​λi​gi​(x)+∑j=1r​νj​hj​(x).
(31)
The inequality Lagrange multipliers obey λi​≥0; the equality Lagrange multipliers νj​ are unrestricted real numbers. Define the Lagrange dual function and dual optimal value by
q(λ,ν)=infx∈X​L(x,λ,ν),d∗​=supλ≥0, ν∈Rr​q(λ,ν).
(32)
The inner infimum keeps X, but releases the displayed optimization constraints. It can be −∞, giving an uninformative lower bound. A different domain changes the bound.[ref]
Here is the entire weak duality argument. For any feasible point z and allowable Lagrange multipliers,
q(λ,ν)≤L(z,λ,ν)≤f(z).
(33)
The first inequality is the definition of an infimum; the second uses λi​gi​(z)≤0 and hj​(z)=0. Taking the infimum over feasible z, then the supremum over allowable Lagrange multipliers, gives d∗​≤p∗​. No convexity is used.
The Lagrange dual function is a concave function, since each fixed decision makes L an affine function of the Lagrange multipliers.[ref] Explicitly, write y=(λ,ν) and choose two points where q is finite. For 0≤t≤1 and every x∈X,
L(x,ty1​+(1−t)y2​)=tL(x,y1​)+(1−t)L(x,y2​)≥tq(y1​)+(1−t)q(y2​).
(34)
Taking the infimum over x proves the defining concavity inequality. Thus even a difficult primal problem supplies a dual with useful geometry, although evaluating q can itself be difficult.
For finite optimal values, strong duality means d∗​=p∗​, or zero duality gap. It need not imply dual attainment. For convex optimization, Slater's condition is a sufficient constraint qualification for both when p∗​ is finite.[ref] The Strong Lagrangian property requires an actual finite Lagrange multiplier achieving equality. Convex perturbation duality interprets such a bound as a supporting slope of the perturbed value function.
