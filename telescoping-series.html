Telescoping series (∑n=1N​(bn​−bn+1​)=b1​−bN+1​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Series (mathematics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 280 Articles: 1
A telescoping series has partial sums in which internal contributions cancel. The basic pattern is an​=bn​−bn+1​, for which finite summation gives
∑n=1N​(bn​−bn+1​)=(b1​−b2​)+(b2​−b3​)+⋯+(bN​−bN+1​)=b1​−bN+1​.
(29)
Only the two boundary terms survive. If bn​ tends to a finite limit of a sequence L, the series consequently sums to b1​−L. The cancellation is exact for each finite N; the infinite result then follows by a limit of a sequence.
For example, partial fraction decomposition gives
n(n+1)1​=n1​−n+11​,∑n=1N​n(n+1)1​=1−N+11​.
(30)
The first three terms are 1/2,1/6,1/12, with partial sum 3/4. The first five have partial sum 5/6. Taking the limit of a sequence gives the concise answer
n=1∑∞​n(n+1)1​=1​.
(31)
The appeal is that a long calculation becomes a boundary calculation. It works whenever the summand is a successive difference, not only for rational functions. For instance, an​=log((n+1)/n)=log(n+1)−logn gives partial sum log(N+1), so its series diverges despite the cancellation. A telescoping series therefore need not be a convergent series: one must still examine the surviving boundary term.
