Shared-uniform counterexample to the second Borel-Cantelli lemma

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Borel-Cantelli lemmas Borel-Cantelli second lemma
OurBigBook.com Words: 252
Choose one random variable U with the continuous uniform distribution on [0,1], and reuse it in every event:
An​={U≤n+11​},n≥1.
(67)
Each probability is 1/(n+1), so their harmonic series diverges, just as in summable and harmonic event probabilities. But the events are decreasing: An+1​⊆An​. For every outcome U=u>0, all indices with n+1>1/u fail. Only finitely many events occur on that outcome. The limsup of events is exactly {U=0}, a zero-probability event. This is a concrete closed-interval version of the shrinking-interval dependence example.[ref]
The missing assumption is visible already in two events. For i<j, their intersection is Aj​, hence
P(Ai​∩Aj​)=j+11​=(i+1)(j+1)1​=P(Ai​)P(Aj​).
(68)
Moreover, the whole tail union is just Am​, with probability 1/(m+1)→0. This calculation explains directly why divergence alone fails: overlapping events keep spending their individual probabilities on the same outcomes.
Let N=∑n≥1​1An​​ be the total number of occurrences, a sum of indicator random variables. Although N<∞ almost surely, Tonelli theorem gives E[N]=∑n≥1​1/(n+1)=∞. More explicitly, P(N≥k)=1/(k+1) for k≥1: very small positive values of U give very long, but finite, initial runs. Infinite expected value does not imply a positive probability of an infinite count.
