Repdigit (Rn​=db−1bn−1​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Integer
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
A repdigit is a positive integer whose base-b expansion repeats one nonzero digit d. For b≥2, 1≤d<b and n≥1, the finite geometric series gives Rn​=d(1+b+⋯+bn−1)=d(bn−1)/(b−1). For example, 5(10n−1)/9 has n decimal digits, all equal to five.
Every prime number not dividing b divides infinitely many such repdigits. If it divides d, every length works. Otherwise, if it does not divide b−1, the Fermat little theorem gives divisibility for lengths that are multiples of p−1, and division by b−1 is justified by its modular inverse. If p∣b−1, the sum is congruent to dn modulo p, so lengths divisible by p work instead. A prime dividing b but not d divides none, since Rn​≡d(modp). This distinction prevents invalid cancellation of the geometric-sum denominator.
