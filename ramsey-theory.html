Square-difference recurrence for finite colourings (ϕ(a)=ϕ(a+b2),a,b>0)

Words: 80
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
Every finite colouring of the positive integers contains a pair with nonzero square difference. One proof takes limits of the colour autocorrelations and represents the resulting positive-definite sequence by a positive spectral measure. Its atom at zero has mass at least the sum of squared colour densities. Averaging along squares kills irrational frequencies; a common multiple makes finitely many rational atomic frequencies equal to one. The remaining rational atomic mass can be made arbitrarily small, leaving a positive average correlation.

Multicolour Ramsey bound (Rk​(3)≤k(Rk−1​(3)−1)+2≤3k!)

Words: 68
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
Let Rk​(3) be the least order of a complete graph whose every k-edge-colouring has a monochromatic triangle in a graph. At a vertex of a graph of order k(Rk−1​(3)−1)+2, some colour joins at least Rk−1​(3) neighbours. An edge of that colour among these neighbours completes a triangle; otherwise the neighbours use at most k−1 colours and contain a triangle by induction. This proves the recurrence and the weaker explicit bound Rk​(3)≤3k!.

Homogeneous set for a colouring (χ↾[H]r is constant)

Words: 308 Articles: 3
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
For a colouring χ:[S]r→{0,…,k−1} of the r-element subsets of a set S, a subset H⊆S is homogeneous if all its r-element subsets receive the same colour. The infinite Ramsey theorem for r-sets guarantees an infinite homogeneous subset when S=N, r is positive and the number of colours is finite. This does not guarantee a computable set when the colouring is computable.

Computable colouring (χ:[N]r→k)

Words: 248 Articles: 2
Homogeneous set for a colouring OurBigBook.com
A colouring is computable when an algorithm sorts the finite input subset, computes its colour and terminates. Its colour classes are uniformly computable sets. The distinction between existence of an infinite homogeneous set for a colouring and effective construction of one is a basic phenomenon in computability theory.

Finite-injury computable colouring of pairs

Words: 124
Computable colouring OurBigBook.com
There is a two-colour computable colouring of pairs with no infinite computably enumerable set homogeneous for it. Enumerate these sets as We​. In stages, give each eligible requirement two markers from its enumerated set, avoiding markers of higher priority, and cancel all lower-priority markers whenever a new assignment is made. Colour (x,y) by the marker status of x at stage y: first markers have colour zero, second markers colour one, and unmarked points colour zero. Each requirement is injured only finitely often by induction on its index. If We​ is infinite it eventually has permanent distinct markers ae​,be​; for every sufficiently large y∈We​, the pairs (ae​,y) and (be​,y) have different colours. Simulating finitely many stages computes each pair's colour, proving both effectiveness and the obstruction.

Halting-stage colouring of triples

Words: 84
Computable colouring OurBigBook.com
Let Ks​ be an increasing uniformly computable finite approximation to the diagonal halting set. For a<b<c, colour the triple 0 if Kb​↾a=Kc​↾a, and 1 otherwise. No infinite homogeneous set for a colouring can have colour 1, since the finitely many bits below its first element eventually stabilize. Every infinite homogeneous set H of colour 0 computes the diagonal halting set: choose n<a<b in H and read membership of n in Kb​. Homogeneity and unboundedness make this answer final. Thus the colouring has no infinite computable homogeneous set.

Dyadic valuation and scale colouring (χ(n)=(v2​(n)mod2,⌊log2​n⌋mod2))

Words: 79
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
Colour n>0 by (v2​(n)mod2,⌊log2​n⌋mod2). No increasing infinite sequence can make all its sums xi​+xj​ and xi​+2xj​, i<j, one colour. If its 2-adic valuations are bounded, two terms of equal valuation and equal odd part modulo four give pair sums of different valuation parity. If they are unbounded, a later term divisible by a sufficiently large power of two prevents adding a fixed earlier term from crossing either dyadic boundary; the two resulting sums have adjacent logarithmic scale indices.

Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers ([N]ω)

Words: 2k Articles: 28
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
The space [N]ω, also written N(ω), consists of all infinite subsets of the positive integers. An element is identified with its strictly increasing enumeration. If s is finite, write s⊏X when s is the finite initial segment of this enumeration.

Finite stem of an infinite subset (s∈[N]<ω)

Words: 59
Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers OurBigBook.com
A finite stem is a finite initial segment s of an infinite subset of N. The associated neighbourhood in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets is [s]={X:s is an initial segment of X}. For an infinite tail A above maxs, the neighbourhood [s,A]={s∪B:B∈[A]ω} is basic in the Ellentuck topology. Finite stems allow a fusion argument to fix initial choices while successively thinning the remaining infinite tail.

Ramsey family in the homogeneous-cone sense (∃M∈[N]ω[M]ω⊆A or [M]ω∩A=∅)

Words: 143
Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers OurBigBook.com
A family A⊆[N]ω is Ramsey in the homogeneous-cone sense if there is an infinite M⊆N such that either [M]ω⊆A or [M]ω∩A=∅. This is the existence of one infinite homogeneous cone. Requiring such a refinement inside every infinite initial ground set gives the stronger definition used for a Ramsey set of infinite subsets; requiring a homogeneous refinement in every finite-stem neighbourhood gives a completely Ramsey set.
These quantifiers should be distinguished. For example, the existence of a homogeneous cone on the even numbers does not constrain a family's restriction to the odd numbers. If E is the finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring class on the odd numbers, the family consisting of E alone has a homogeneous cone disjoint from it on the even numbers, but no homogeneous cone on the odd numbers. Every open set in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets has homogeneous cones inside all infinite ground sets, as follows by deciding finite stems through fusion.

Ramsey cone topology (τcone​)

Words: 111 Articles: 1
Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers OurBigBook.com
This topology has basic open sets [A]ω for infinite ground sets A, without fixed finite stems. It is coarser than the Ellentuck topology and differs from the ordinary topology on infinite subsets, whose basic cylinders fix an initial segment. The symbol τ is sometimes used for either coarser convention, so it should be accompanied by a definition.

Meagreness of the Ramsey cone topology

Words: 59
Ramsey cone topology OurBigBook.com
The whole space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers is a meagre set in the Ramsey cone topology. For Dj​={X:minX=j}, the closure is {X:j∈X}, which has empty interior: every infinite ground set can be thinned to omit j. Thus all Dj​ are nowhere dense, while their countable union is the whole space. In contrast, the whole space is not meagre in either the Ellentuck topology or the ordinary topology on infinite subsets.

Ramsey set of infinite subsets

A set E⊆[N]ω is Ramsey if every infinite A has an infinite subset B such that [B]ω⊆E or [B]ω∩E=∅.

Open Ramsey theorem

Words: 80
Ramsey set of infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
Every open set O in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets is a Ramsey set of infinite subsets: some infinite B has [B]ω⊆O or [B]ω∩O=∅. Use acceptance and rejection of finite stems and fusion to decide every finite stem. If the empty stem is rejected, each rejected stem has only finitely many accepting one-point extensions. Successive avoidance of these finite obstructions gives an infinite set all of whose finite stems are rejected; openness then excludes every infinite subset from O. This concerns increasing enumerations of infinite subsets, rather than unrestricted sequences with repetitions.

Finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring (ε(A)=∣A△R∣mod2)

Words: 58
Ramsey set of infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
Choose one representative R for each equivalence class of infinite sets under finite symmetric difference. The colour ∣A△R∣mod2 changes on removing one point. Every cone [M]ω therefore contains opposite-coloured sets M and M∖{minM}. Either colour family is a non-Ramsey set of infinite subsets. Choice of representatives is essential to this construction, which does not claim a definable regular family.

Non-Ramsey set from transfinite selection

Words: 63
Ramsey set of infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
Using the axiom of choice, enumerate all infinite subsets Aα​ of the positive integers in order type 2ℵ0​. By transfinite recursion, choose two previously unused infinite subsets Xα​,Yα​ of each Aα​. This is possible because each [Aα​]ω has cardinality 2ℵ0​ and fewer choices have been made at stage α. The set {Xα​} is not a Ramsey set of infinite subsets, since every [Aα​]ω meets both it and its complement.

Completely Ramsey set

Words: 557 Articles: 7
Ramsey set of infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
A set E⊆[N]ω is completely Ramsey if every basic Ellentuck topology neighborhood [s,A] admits an infinite B⊆A with [s,B]⊆E or [s,B]∩E=∅. Keeping the finite stem s makes this stronger than being a Ramsey set of infinite subsets.
Stem-supported Ramsey family need not be completely Ramsey
Words: 65
Completely Ramsey set OurBigBook.com
Let E be the even positive integers, and take a finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring ε of [E]ω. The family {{1}∪X:X∈[E]ω, ε(X)=0} is Ramsey even under the every-ground-set convention, since every infinite ground set can be thinned to omit 1. It is not completely Ramsey: inside [{1},E], every infinite tail has two subfamilies of opposite parity. Its supporting neighbourhood is closed and nowhere dense in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets, but open in the Ellentuck topology.
Completely Ramsey-null set
Words: 158 Articles: 1
Completely Ramsey set OurBigBook.com
A set N is completely Ramsey-null when every Ellentuck topology neighborhood [s,A] has a refinement [s,B] disjoint from N, with the same finite stem s. Assuming open Ellentuck sets are completely Ramsey, every star-nowhere dense set is completely Ramsey-null: apply the open-set property to the dense complement of its closure. The Ellentuck meagre-set fusion lemma shows that countable unions retain this avoidance property.
Ellentuck meagre-set fusion lemma
Words: 102
Completely Ramsey-null set OurBigBook.com
Countably many completely Ramsey-null sets can be avoided simultaneously in a stem-preserving Ellentuck topology refinement. Select an increasing sequence bj​ with nested infinite remaining tails. At stage j, thin the tail to avoid the jth set for every stem s∪t, where t ranges over all subsets of the first j selected points. There are only finitely many such stems. Any infinite subset of the final selected sequence uses some such t before stage j and has all remaining points in the thinned tail. Thus it avoids every forbidden set. This explains why an argument checking only the full selected prefix is insufficient.
Fusion proof for open Ellentuck sets
Words: 298 Articles: 3
Completely Ramsey set OurBigBook.com
Every open set in the Ellentuck topology is a completely Ramsey set. Fix a finite stem s. An infinite tail accepts a finite extension a if its entire basic neighborhood [s∪a,A] lies in the open set; it rejects a if no infinite subtail accepts it. Decisions persist under thinning. A fusion chooses an infinite set deciding every finite extension. For any rejected extension, only finitely many possible next elements can yield accepted extensions, since infinitely many would themselves provide an accepting tail. A second thinning therefore makes all finite extensions rejected. Openness then rules out any point of the set in the resulting neighborhood.
Acceptance and rejection of finite stems
Words: 198 Articles: 2
Fusion proof for open Ellentuck sets OurBigBook.com
Relative to a family Y, an infinite reservoir accepts a finite stem s when its entire Ellentuck topology neighbourhood [s,A] lies in Y. It rejects s when no infinite refinement accepts it. Every reservoir has a refinement deciding a prescribed stem, and both outcomes are hereditary under further infinite thinning. These definitions turn open-set homogeneity into a fusion construction.
Finitely many accepting extensions of a rejected stem
Words: 72
Acceptance and rejection of finite stems OurBigBook.com
Suppose a reservoir rejects s and decides every extension s∪{a}. Only finitely many of those one-point extensions can have accepting tails. If infinitely many did, collect their new points into C; every infinite subset of C begins with an accepting successor, so [s,C] would lie in the family. That would make C accept s, contradicting rejection. This finite-obstruction fact lets a second fusion preserve rejection of every finite stem.
Deciding all finite stems by fusion
Words: 67
Acceptance and rejection of finite stems OurBigBook.com
Choose successive points and, after each choice, thin the unused reservoir to decide all subsets of the finite chosen prefix. There are only finitely many stems to handle at each stage. The final diagonal infinite set decides every one of its finite stems, because its relevant tail lies in the reservoir chosen when the stem's largest point was selected. The argument uses the hereditary decisions from acceptance and rejection of finite stems.

Ellentuck topology (τ∗)

Words: 469 Articles: 6
Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers OurBigBook.com
For a finite set s and an infinite set A lying above maxs, put
[s,A]={s∪B:B∈[A]ω}.
(1)
These sets form the basic neighborhoods of the Ellentuck topology, also called the star topology. It is finer than the ordinary topology on infinite subsets. Every basic neighborhood is a clopen set in the Ellentuck topology, and a closed set in the ordinary topology.

Ellentuck Borel sets are completely Ramsey

Words: 170 Articles: 1
Ellentuck topology OurBigBook.com
Every Borel set for the Ellentuck topology is completely Ramsey. First, an acceptance-and-rejection fusion makes every open set homogeneous after thinning the infinite reservoir while keeping its finite stem. Every nowhere-dense set is consequently completely Ramsey-null, and a fusion handling all subsets of each finite selected prefix shows that countable unions of such null sets remain null. Finally, sets differing from an open set by a meagre set form a sigma-algebra containing the open sets. Thus every Borel set has the required stem-preserving homogeneous refinement.
Baire-property reduction for completely Ramsey families
Words: 94
Ellentuck Borel sets are completely Ramsey OurBigBook.com
In the Ellentuck topology, suppose every open set is completely Ramsey and every meagre set is completely Ramsey-null. If E△U is contained in a meagre set for some open U, first refine a basic neighbourhood without changing its stem to avoid that exceptional set, and then refine again to be homogeneous for U. The resulting neighbourhood is homogeneous for E. Sets with such an open representative form a sigma-algebra: countable unions collect the meagre errors, and complements replace Uc by its interior, differing only on the nowhere-dense boundary of U. This proves that all star-Borel sets are completely Ramsey.

Cone on an infinite coinfinite ground set (EA​=[A]ω)

Words: 162 Articles: 1
Ellentuck topology OurBigBook.com
For an infinite coinfinite set A⊆N, let EA​=[A]ω in the space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers. This cone is a nonempty clopen set in the Ellentuck topology, because it is the basic neighbourhood [∅,A], and any X∈/EA​ has a finite initial segment containing a point outside A and hence a basic neighbourhood disjoint from EA​.
The cone is a closed set with empty interior in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets: membership of a point outside A witnesses its complement as an open set, while every ordinary finite-stem neighbourhood can be extended by an arbitrarily large point outside A. Thus EA​ is an ordinary nowhere dense set, but it is neither ordinary open nor Ellentuck nowhere dense. It provides both distinctions between these two topologies.
Ordinarily nowhere-dense sets need not be completely Ramsey
Words: 54
Cone on an infinite coinfinite ground set OurBigBook.com
The cone [E]ω on the even integers is nonempty and Ellentuck topology open, while it is closed and nowhere dense in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets. Choose a finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring on that cone and take one colour class. The class is still ordinarily nowhere dense because its closure lies in the cone, but every stem-free refinement inside the cone meets both colours. Hence it is not completely Ramsey.

Countable unions of Ellentuck clopen sets need not be closed

Words: 45
Ellentuck topology OurBigBook.com
In the space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers, the families Cn​={X:n∈/X} are clopen sets in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets and thus in the finer Ellentuck topology. Their union is [N]ω∖{N}. Every basic Ellentuck topology neighborhood of N contains N∖{n} for some sufficiently large n, so this union is not a closed set.

Baire property in the Ellentuck topology (E△U is star-meagre)

Words: 38

A subset of the space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers has this property when it differs from an Ellentuck topology open set by a meagre set in the same topology. By the complete-Ramsey characterization, these are precisely the completely Ramsey sets. Using meagreness from a different topology changes the definition.

The ordinary topology on [N]ω has basic sets [s]={X:s⊏X} for finite s. It is the product topology on increasing enumerations, and also the subspace topology on infinite-subset indicator functions in {0,1}N.

Dense countable family of cofinite infinite subsets (D={X⊆N:X is cofinite})

Words: 61
Ordinary topology on infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
In the ordinary topology on infinite subsets, the family D of cofinite sets is a countable set, a dense and a meagre set. Countability follows by indexing its members by their finite complements. Each singleton is closed with empty interior, while every basic cylinder with finite initial segment s contains s∪{n:n>maxs}. Consequently D is not a nowhere dense set. This example uses the ordinary cylinder topology, rather than the Ramsey cone topology.

Gap-doubling closed family of infinite subsets (an+1​>2an​)

Words: 138 Articles: 1
Ordinary topology on infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
The family D={{a1​<a2​<⋯}:an+1​>2an​ for all n} is ordinarily closed and nowhere dense. A failed gap is witnessed by a finite prefix, and any cylinder can be refined by adjoining consecutive large integers that fail the gap. Nevertheless D intersects every infinite-subset cone in continuum many sets: a binary tree chooses two sufficiently large next points inside its reservoir at each step.
Meagre set meeting every Ramsey cone in both colours
Words: 78
Gap-doubling closed family of infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
A closed nowhere dense family can meet every cone [M]ω in continuum many sets, as the gap-doubling closed family of infinite subsets does. Well-order the cones and choose two fresh candidates in each, permanently reserving one of each colour. The red family meets every cone and its complement contains every reserved blue candidate, so it is not Ramsey. As a subset of the closed nowhere dense family, it is still nowhere dense and has the Baire property in the ordinary infinite-subset topology. This recursion requires no regularity assumption on the continuum cardinal.

Baire property in the ordinary infinite-subset topology (Y△O is τ-meagre)

Words: 40
Ordinary topology on infinite subsets OurBigBook.com
A family Y⊆[N]ω is τ-Baire when Y△O is meagre for some ordinarily open family O. This is weaker than the corresponding regularity in the star topology. A meagre set meeting every Ramsey cone in both colours has the ordinary Baire property while failing to be a Ramsey set of infinite subsets.

Matching Ramsey number (R(rK2​,sK2​))

For r≥s≥1, the two-colour Ramsey number for disjoint edge matchings is
R(rK2​,sK2​)=2r+s−1.
(2)
Thus every red-blue colouring of K2r+s−1​ contains either a red matching of size r or a blue matching of size s, and the order is sharp.

Finite coloring

A finite coloring of a set X is a function c:X→C into a finite set C of colors. A subset is monochromatic when the restriction of c to it is constant.

Colour profile of a finite block ((c(b1​),…,c(bN​)))

Words: 54
Finite coloring OurBigBook.com
The colour profile of a length-N block b1​,…,bN​ under a finite colouring c is the ordered tuple (c(b1​),…,c(bN​)). There are at most rN profiles when c uses at most r colours. Equally profiled blocks reproduce every colour configuration at the same relative positions; this converts a colouring of points into a finite colouring of blocks in the colour-focusing proof of Van der Waerden theorem.

Colour class (c−1(i))

A colour class of a finite colouring c:X→{1,…,r} is a fibre c−1(i). Each colour class is a monochromatic set, and these fibres give a finite set partition of X after empty fibres are omitted.

Cyclic logarithmic coloring (⌊logb​x⌋modq)

Words: 61
Finite coloring OurBigBook.com
For b>1 and an integer q≥2, assign x≥1 the color ⌊logb​x⌋modq. The floor function partitions the domain into half-open geometric bins. If 1<logb​(y/x)<q−1, their bin indices differ by an integer in {1,…,q−1}, so their colors differ. For example, b=3/2 and q=3 separates every ratio in [1.9,2]. Composing this finite coloring with another logarithm can obstruct monochromatic multiplicative configurations whose logarithms have such ratios.

Refinement of a finite coloring

Words: 45
Finite coloring OurBigBook.com
A refinement of a finite coloring assigns different new colors whenever the original colors differ. A set monochromatic for the refinement is therefore monochromatic for the original coloring. Given colorings χ,ψ, the product coloring n↦(χ(n),ψ(n)) refines both. This makes it possible to impose finitely many coloring obstructions simultaneously.

Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
There is a finite coloring of the positive integers for which no infinite set has all pairwise sums and pairwise products in one color. Refining this coloring by the parity of the 2-adic valuation also prevents a constant infinite sequence from evading the obstruction.

Euclidean Ramsey set

Words: 349 Articles: 11
Ramsey theory OurBigBook.com
A finite set X⊆Rd is Euclidean Ramsey when, for every number of colors, some finite-dimensional Euclidean space has the property that every coloring of it contains a monochromatic isometric copy of X.

Spherical point set

A finite point set is spherical when it lies on a sphere. Every Euclidean Ramsey set is spherical. Whether every finite spherical point set is Euclidean Ramsey is open.

Product theorem for Euclidean Ramsey sets

Words: 54
Euclidean Ramsey set OurBigBook.com
If finite point sets X and Y are Euclidean Ramsey, then their orthogonal Cartesian product X×Y is Euclidean Ramsey. The proof first chooses a finite Ramsey witness for X, colors a witness for Y by the complete color pattern it induces on the first witness, and then applies the two Ramsey properties in succession.

Three-term unit arithmetic progression is not Euclidean Ramsey

Words: 46
Euclidean Ramsey set OurBigBook.com
The collinear set {0,1,2} is not a Euclidean Ramsey set. In every dimension, coloring x by ⌊2∥x∥2⌋ modulo ten prevents a monochromatic congruent copy; the parallelogram law would force the corresponding three integer parts to have a second difference strictly between two and six, which cannot be divisible by ten.

Edge Ramsey set

A finite Euclidean configuration is edge Ramsey when every finite edge coloring of a suitable finite Euclidean set contains a monochromatic isometric copy. The edge Ramsey configurations are exactly the equidistant sets, equivalently the vertex sets of regular simplices.

A-invariant coloring of a Cartesian power (A-invariant coloring of a Cartesian power)
Words: 55
Kriz theorem for cyclic transitive point sets OurBigBook.com
For A⊆X, a coloring of Xn is A-invariant when changing any coordinates from one member of A to another does not change the color. A product argument upgrades the existence of a copy of X on which A is monochromatic to an A-invariant copy of every finite Cartesian power Xn.
