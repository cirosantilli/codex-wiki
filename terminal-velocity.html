Terminal velocity (vT​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Position Velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 361 Articles: 1
Terminal velocity is a constant limiting velocity approached when a velocity-dependent resistance balances the other applied forces. A falling body may keep moving while its net force and acceleration tend to zero. For downward motion in a stationary medium, its limiting speed is set by balancing gravitational weight against drag force, including buoyancy if appreciable. The balance, rather than a universal numerical value, is the defining idea. [ref]
The terminal velocity under linear drag gives a simple example with an explicit approach to the limit. Real falling objects need an appropriate resistance model: linear drag is useful for slow motion of a small sphere in Stokes flow, whereas many faster falling objects require quadratic drag. Changing mass also changes the question: the existing accreting raindrop with quadratic drag has a speed-radius attractor of an accreting raindrop, rather than the fixed-mass balance used below.
