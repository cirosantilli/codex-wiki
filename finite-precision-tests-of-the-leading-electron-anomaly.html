Finite precision tests of the leading electron anomaly

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Perturbative quantum field theory Quantum electrodynamics Schwinger correction to the electron magnetic moment
OurBigBook.com Words: 190
A prediction becomes useful when its error and the competing models are explicit. Consider an original hypothetical readout a∗​=0.00116 with stipulated absolute tolerance 2×10−5. Hold the teaching input α=1/137 fixed. Compare three models: the Dirac equation baseline ae​=0, the leading QED value ae​=α/(2π), and a trial coefficient twice as large, ae​=α/π. Their absolute residuals are respectively 0.00116, approximately 1.7149×10−6, and approximately 0.00116343. Only the leading QED value lies inside this stipulated tolerance. The example tests both the existence and the size of a correction; it does not quote an actual observation or assign the tolerance a statistical interpretation.
At tighter precision, including more loop orders and propagating the uncertainty in the fine-structure constant becomes essential. The first omitted term has order α2, but the order symbol alone gives no numerical error bound. If α changes by a small δα, this leading prediction changes by δae​=δα/(2π). Thus a comparison must state which input determined α: extracting it from the very electron magnetic moment being tested would not supply an independent test of that input. Agreement with one leading term at one tolerance does not establish agreement to arbitrarily many digits.
