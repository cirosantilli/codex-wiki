Functional-group conversion in step growth (p)

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Step-growth polymerization Linear step-growth molecule count
OurBigBook.com Words: 154
In a balanced bifunctional feed there are initially N0​ A groups and N0​ B groups. Define the functional-group conversion in step growth by p=J/N0​: the reacted fraction of either group type. Each join consumes one of each. Therefore N=N0​(1−p). In the eight-AB example, three joins give p=3/8 and four give p=1/2. In the latter state only three of eight starting monomer molecules remain isolated: their disappearance fraction is 5/8, different from p=1/2. Neither this group-based p nor disappearance of isolated monomers is the incorporated-feed fraction in the ideal living polymerization count.
With unequal initial counts A0​≤B0​, this article always uses p=J/A0​, the conversion of the limiting A groups. The reacted fraction of B groups is J/B0​=rp, where r=A0​/B0​. The fraction of all initial functional groups consumed is 2J/(A0​+B0​)=2rp/(1+r). At nonzero conversion these three fractions coincide only at r=1. Naming the measured group type prevents an apparently correct equation from using the wrong conversion.
