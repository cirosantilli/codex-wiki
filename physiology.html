Chemoreceptor

Words: 47
Physiology OurBigBook.com
A chemoreceptor is a sensory cell or receptor system responding to chemical composition. In respiratory regulation, carbon-dioxide-related chemical signals contribute to feedback controlling ventilation. Blood transport and neural response introduce delays, so a lumped ventilation model may respond to past rather than present carbon-dioxide partial pressure.

Ventricular elastance (pLV​=ELV​(t)(VLV​−V0​))

Words: 194 Articles: 2
Left ventricle OurBigBook.com
Time-dependent elastance models the pressure generated by a cardiac ventricle at its current stressed volume. The reference V0​ is its zero-pressure volume. The inverse of elastance is compliance at fixed time. Rising elastance increases pressure without volume change during isovolumetric contraction; an approximately constant systolic value ES​ gives compliance Cs​=1/ES​ during a simplified ejection stage.
Rapid artery-ventricle pressure equilibration (p^​a​=[Ca​pa+​+V+−V0​]/(Ca​+1/Es​))
Words: 71
Ventricular elastance OurBigBook.com
If an open aortic valve equilibrates pressure much faster than peripheral outflow, the arteries and ventricle share a common pressure while their combined blood volume is conserved during the short adjustment. Let arterial vascular compliance be Ca​ and systolic ventricular elastance be Es​. Equating the initial and final arterial-plus-ventricular stored volumes gives the displayed pressure. Subsequent peripheral drainage sees the combined compliance Ca​+1/Es​; during filling a closed aortic valve removes the ventricular contribution.
Ventricular ejection pressure and stroke-volume approximation (p​a​=(Ca​pa+​+V+−V0​)/(Ca​+Cs​))
Words: 73
Ventricular elastance OurBigBook.com
After a rapid switch to systolic compliance Cs​, negligible valve resistance equalizes arterial and ventricular pressures. Neglecting runoff during this short stage conserves combined stored volume and gives the displayed pressure. Thereafter, with closed mitral valve, negligible venous pressure and constant Cs​, the pressure decays as p​a​e−t/[Rc​(Ca​+Cs​)]. At firing duration ΔtF​, the stroke volume is V+−V0​−Cs​p​a​e−ΔtF​/[Rc​(Ca​+Cs​)]. Each approximation follows from an explicit valve or time-scale separation condition.

Delayed carbon-dioxide ventilation feedback (KP˙=M−PG[P(t−τ)−P0​]+​)

Words: 190 Articles: 1
Pulmonary ventilation OurBigBook.com
A one-compartment carbon-dioxide balance couples metabolic production with pressure-dependent ventilatory removal. A positive-part response models a nonnegative threshold-linear respiratory drive, and a transport/response delay can destabilize negative feedback. With T=t/τ, P=P0​+[M/(GP0​)]p, the scaled law is p′=α[1−(1+μp)[p(T−1)]+​], where α=τGP0​/K and μ=M/(GP02​). Its positive equilibrium is 2/[1+1+4μ​]; 1/(1+μ) is only its first-order small-μ approximation.

Sigmoidal delayed ventilation threshold (τc​=arccos(−A/B)/B2−A2​,B>A>0)

Words: 132

Splitting the characteristic equation of a delay differential equation at an imaginary root yields ω2=B2−A2 and the displayed first critical delay. The root crosses into the unstable half-plane with real derivative ω2/[(1+Aτc​)2+ω2τc2​]>0. If A≪B, the period near onset is approximately 4τc​. For a Hill equation response, B/A=nθn/(θn+(p∗)n) identifies whether delayed gain can exceed instantaneous damping.
A longer circulation time increases the delay between gas exchange and its sensed feedback. The American Thoracic Society's documented case relates a change in breathing pattern during one night to a longer estimated circulation time; the simple scalar model isolates a possible feedback mechanism rather than representing every determinant of that observation.[ref]

Homeostasis

Words: 2k Articles: 40
Physiology OurBigBook.com
Homeostasis is the maintenance of usable internal conditions despite disturbances. It concerns selected regulated physiological variables, rather than keeping every part of an organism unchanged: core body temperature may remain near its usual range while skin temperature, sweating and blood pressure change. Regulation makes a relatively dependable environment for biological cells out of a changing external world. [ref]
A useful causal description separates a physiological sensor, a physiological integrator and a physiological effector. The return from the physiological effector's action to the sensed regulated physiological variable closes a feedback control loop. In negative feedback, that return opposes the disturbance. The insulin and glucagon articles already trace the blood glucose example; synaptic integration explains how several inputs can jointly influence a neuron. These are implementations and ingredients, not a requirement that every regulatory loop use one anatomical control center. [ref]
Homeostasis is useful precisely because internal activity can change: a nearly steady temperature can conceal a large increase in heat transfer. Conversely, observing a flat trace alone does not identify a regulatory mechanism. The environment might simply be constant, or the measuring instrument too insensitive; a perturbation and an independent response readout provide stronger evidence. The skin-temperature perturbation experiment below makes that distinction tangible.
The historical motivation was the puzzle of stability in changing living material. In his 1932 The Wisdom of the Body, Walter Bradford Cannon emphasized that an organism exchanges material with its surroundings and continually replaces its own structure, yet maintains relatively steady internal conditions. This authored excerpt presents homeostasis as active compensation for external and internal disturbances, rather than a static body. [ref]

(2)

This deliberately synthetic differential equation model illustrates response direction and lag. Every plotted number is chosen for teaching; none is a biological measurement or a fitted human parameter. Let externally prescribed, dimensionless g>0 represent a glucose stimulus relative to a reference. Let I and H be normalized insulin and glucagon response variables, with independently chosen time constants τI​=4 and τH​=6 in arbitrary time units:
I∞​(g)=1+g4g4​,H∞​(g)=1+g41​,τI​I˙=I∞​(g)−I,τH​H˙=H∞​(g)−H.
(6)
Here I∞​ rises and H∞​ falls with g. Their targets add to one by construction; the evolving I+H need not equal one because their time constants differ. Start with g=1 and I=H=1/2. Set g=1.6 from t=10 to 40, g=0.65 from t=40 to 70, and g=1 otherwise. Within a constant-input interval beginning at t0​, the exponential decay solution is
I(t)=I∞​+[I(t0​)−I∞​]e−(t−t0​)/τI​,H(t)=H∞​+[H(t0​)−H∞​]e−(t−t0​)/τH​.
(7)
Each response is a continuous function of time even when the imposed stimulus jumps. The chosen starting values stay between zero and one, since each solution is a weighted arithmetic mean of its interval's starting value and target.
For an illustrative downstream comparison, define release tendency R=0.3+0.6H/(1+I) and uptake tendency U=(0.25+0.5I)g, in the same arbitrary flux units. At the reference target, R=U=0.5. At the high-stimulus targets, R≈0.343 and U≈1.094; at the low-stimulus targets, R≈0.742 and U≈0.212. These chosen functions encode greater uptake with insulin and greater release with glucagon, moderated by insulin; they are not established enzyme kinetics. The g trace is imposed externally, so the figure does not demonstrate self-correction of blood glucose. A closed model would also need a conservation of mass equation, nutrient input, finite glycogen stores, and additional tissue interactions. The qualitative restoring loops are shown separately in the insulin circuit.

Circadian rhythm

Words: 1k Articles: 12
Physiology OurBigBook.com
A circadian rhythm is an endogenous biological oscillation with a period near 24 hours, persisting without a repeating external signal. A circadian clock times biological activities and anticipates daily changes without continual external instructions. A free-running circadian rhythm reveals its intrinsic period; entrainment of circadian rhythm coordinates it with an external cycle.[ref]
In 1994, Martha Hotz Vitaterna and colleagues screened offspring of mutagen-treated C57BL/6J mice (the house mouse). Each mouse lived in a cage with a running wheel, inside a ventilated, light-tight chamber with timer-controlled lighting. Wheel revolutions were recorded electronically or on an event recorder. The initial screen used 14:10 LD for the first 41 animals and 12:12 LD thereafter; LD denotes hours of light and darkness. Transfer to constant darkness, DD, omitted the next scheduled lights-on; recording continued for at least three weeks. The first 41 animals also received brief light pulses, so those records were not uninterrupted DD.[ref]
The subsequent-generation comparison in Figure 2 used 12:12 LD before DD. Animals without the Clock mutation and with mutant genotypes supplied the comparison: the B6 examples in panels A–C had initial DD periods of 23.6, 24.8 and 27.1 hours, respectively; the homozygote later lost its sustained circadian rhythm. Total activity remained comparable across genotypes in the interstrain comparison. The mutation therefore altered timing, beyond merely reducing running. Wheel activity is a behavioral readout; it does not directly measure every circadian clock or establish a human period.[ref]
Daily activity under LD alone cannot establish entrainment of circadian rhythm. Light can directly change activity without resetting the circadian clock: masking of circadian rhythm. Removing the light cycle helps distinguish these effects; they can coexist.[ref] In an experiment, other repeating inputs must also be controlled: a nominally dark room with daily handling is not automatically an environment without timing cues.

Free-running circadian rhythm (τ)

Words: 413
Circadian rhythm OurBigBook.com
A free-running circadian rhythm persists without an entraining cycle. Its period τ separates corresponding circadian phases, such as successive activity onsets, under specified constant conditions.[ref] A circadian phase tells where the system is within its cycle; a period tells how long one cycle takes. Choosing onset rather than peak as the reference changes the phase label, not the period of an ideal repeating signal.
Consider an original, deliberately idealized model with τ=23.5h. For seven days prescribe a 12:12 LD cycle with activity starting at hour 12 of each 24-hour day. Then omit the next scheduled dawn and keep the environment dark. Stipulate that the first DD onset retains hour 12, and that every later onset is exactly 23.5h after the previous one. Activity lasts eight hours after each onset. These are chosen model values, not recorded mouse or human data, and the fixed activity window represents an output of the model rather than the molecular mechanism of a circadian clock.
An actogram displays activity against time in successive rows. Here each row spans T=24h, regardless of the circadian rhythm's period. If t0​ is the first DD onset and n counts completed free-running cycles, then
tn​=t0​+nτ,tn+1​−tn​=τ,xn​=tn​−(t0​+nT)=n(τ−T).
(8)
The unwrapped onset displacement xn​ is therefore −0.5nh. After eight cycles the onset is four hours earlier than a 24-hour continuation would predict; after sixteen cycles it is eight hours earlier. Because these onsets remain within successive calendar rows in the displayed interval, their row positions are 12,11.5,11,…,4 hours. The visual slant comes from comparing two different periods, not from cessation of the oscillation.
An alternative phase oscillator description makes the same distinction explicit. Set the circadian phase to zero at t0​ and define
θ(t)=τ2π(t−t0​)​(mod2π),ω=τ2π​.
(9)
The angular frequency ω is constant in this model. Onset occurs whenever the unwrapped circadian phase completes another turn. A period longer than T would instead produce later onsets; equality would produce aligned rows even without entrainment of circadian rhythm. Thus aligned rows by themselves do not identify the cause of a daily pattern. Real activity can be noisy, and a free-running circadian rhythm's period can vary with conditions or with time after release; the stipulated straight line assumes a stationary period.

Entrainment of circadian rhythm

Words: 375 Articles: 1
Circadian rhythm OurBigBook.com
Entrainment of circadian rhythm synchronizes a circadian clock to a repeating cue, a zeitgeber. One-to-one entrainment of circadian rhythm gives one cycle per environmental cycle with a stable relative circadian phase.[ref] The stable relation need not put an activity onset exactly at lights-off: a constant offset also expresses phase locking.
For an original local model, let xn​ be the activity-onset offset from lights-off on environmental cycle n, with a negative value meaning an earlier onset. Choose an intrinsic period τ=23.5h and environmental period T=24h. Let D(x) be a stipulated daily delay, measured in hours; positive D moves the next onset later. The recurrence relation is
xn+1​=xn​+(τ−T)+D(xn​).
(10)
A fixed phase relationship x∗​ requires D(x∗​)=T−τ: here a half-hour delay balances the half-hour intrinsic advance each cycle. This equality alone describes balance; restoration after a small disturbance also requires fixed point stability for an iteration.
Choose the illustrative local law D(x)=0.5h−0.4x. Then
xn+1​=0.6xn​,x∗​=0,xn​=−3(0.6)nh
(11)
for the chosen starting offset x0​=−3h. The error shrinks by a factor of 0.6 each cycle, proving fixed point stability for an iteration in this model. After five cycles x5​=−0.23328h, about fourteen minutes early. At the fixed point, the imposed delay is 0.5h and the corrected onset interval is τ+D(x∗​)=24h, although the uncoupled period remains 23.5h.
The uncorrected comparison has D=0 and xn​=−3h+n(τ−T), so after five cycles it is 5.5 hours early. The two traces separate ongoing timekeeping from adjustment to a zeitgeber. The chosen linear delay law is only a local teaching construction: it is not a measured response to light, does not account for wrapping of circadian phase, and does not predict an organism's entrainment range. Nor does it include masking of circadian rhythm. In a real study, tracking several cycles after removal of the cue or after a timed perturbation supplies information that a single activity value under illumination cannot provide.
