Wien's displacement law

 Home Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Radiative transfer Planck function
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
Wien's displacement law says that the wavelength maximizing the Planck function per unit wavelength, Bλ​(T), moves inversely with absolute temperature:
λmax​T=b,b≃2.897771955×10−3mK.
(28)
Here b is the Wien displacement constant. Heating an ideal blackbody therefore shifts this peak toward shorter wavelengths, while also increasing its emission at every fixed wavelength. The peak identifies where equal narrow wavelength intervals carry the most power; it is not a single emitted wavelength. Planck's law supplies the full curve, and the Stefan–Boltzmann law supplies its integral.
Specify the spectral coordinate before finding a peak. The frequency form of Wien's displacement law maximizes Bν​, which is spectral radiance per unit frequency. Its maximum is not c/λmax​. The frequency and wavelength spectral radiance conversion changes the density as well as the horizontal coordinate; the existing frequency and wavelength peaks of Planck's law give a complementary numerical comparison.
In his 11 December 1911 Nobel lecture, Wilhelm Wien explained the displacement principle through a thought experiment: compress black-body radiation between reflecting moving walls. Work against radiation pressure raises the energy density, while reflection changes each frequency by the Doppler effect. The Second law of thermodynamics connects the resulting spectrum to one at a higher temperature. This determines how the spectrum scales between temperatures, without determining its complete shape. The Wien approximation is a separate, restricted spectral formula.[ref]
