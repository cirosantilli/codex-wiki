Chromatographic partition coefficient (K=cs​/cm​)

 Home Chemistry Chromatography
OurBigBook.com Words: 271
For a dilute solute distributed between two liquid regions at local chemical equilibrium, define the chromatographic partition coefficient as
K=cm​cs​​,n=cm​Vm​+cs​Vs​=cm​(Vm​+KVs​),fm​=ncm​Vm​​=Vm​+KVs​Vm​​.
(1)
Here cm​,cs​ are concentrations expressed in the same units, Vm​,Vs​ specify accessible volume in the chromatographic mobile phase and immobilized liquid chromatographic stationary phase, respectively, n is total solute amount of substance, and fm​ is the fraction of that amount of substance in the chromatographic mobile phase. Assume a constant chromatographic partition coefficient, rapid exchange, unchanged phases and temperature, and no chemical reaction. A chemical equilibrium population still exchanges molecules; it is not permanently divided into two sets.
The amount of substance balance proves the expression for fm​. Increasing either K or Vs​/Vm​ reduces the mobile fraction, illustrating why a concentration ratio alone cannot determine retention. For surface adsorption, specify an adsorption storage law instead of assigning an arbitrary liquid volume to the solid.
An original discrete model makes the separation visible. In each identical cell, first establish local chemical equilibrium, then advance only the mobile fraction by one cell; let the stationary fraction wait. Repeat twelve times, without any additional spreading. With Vs​=Vm​, stipulated chromatographic partition coefficients KA​=1/3 and KB​=3 give fA​=3/4 and fB​=1/4. Each cycle contributes mean displacement fm​ cells, so the mean positions after twelve cycles are nine cells and three cells. Redistribution itself broadens each band: the cell positions have a binomial distribution with parameters 12,fm​. These dimensionless chosen values illustrate transport, not measurements of any particular substances.
