Kundt tube experiment

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Linear acoustics Kundt's tube
OurBigBook.com Words: 305
PHYWE's implementation rubs a 1.60m rod supported 0.40m from each end. Its end disc drives a dusty glass tube; a movable tuning slide selects a clear pattern. A ruler measures repeated positions and a thermometer records gas temperature. The two supports select a rod wavelength equal to its length.[ref]
The University of Iowa also documents an electronic version: a loudspeaker drives the tube and a microphone on a plunger supplies an oscilloscope readout. This permits a spatial scan of sound pressure without relying on the dust pattern.[ref]
For a proposed pressure-scan measurement, keep the drive frequency fixed and move a small microphone through the tube. Record its position and fitted sinusoidal amplitude after each move; repeat the scan in reverse to reveal drift. Locate several consecutive maxima, rather than estimating a wavelength from a single pair. Reducing the drive checks whether the inferred spacing is independent of amplitude in the linear acoustics regime. A drive-off record estimates electrical background. These are controls for the proposed measurement, not reported observations from the cited demonstrations.
A microphone and its access opening can perturb the field. End compliance, losses and transverse modes also challenge the one-dimensional description. Dust traces identify a repeating structure, but the grains are not fluid parcels: the equations below describe sound pressure and acoustic particle velocity, rather than a detailed grain-transport model. The temperature and gas composition must remain sufficiently uniform to interpret a single fitted speed of sound.
