Waiting-time continuation at a non-Lipschitz equilibrium (yc​(t)=y∗​−[(1−p)(t−c)]+1/(1−p)​,0<p<1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Picard-Lindelöf theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
For the autonomous differential equation y′=−∣y−y∗​∣p, the displayed family stays at y∗​ until time c and then decreases below it. Here [z]+​=max(z,0). Direct differentiation verifies the equation on each side; since 1/(1−p)>1, the derivative tends to zero at departure, making the joined function continuously differentiable. Distinct departure times give different solutions from the same equilibrium initial condition. The ratio ∣f(y∗​+ε)−f(y∗​)∣/ε=εp−1 is unbounded, so the local Lipschitz continuity hypothesis of the Picard-Lindelöf theorem fails.
A solution starting above y∗​ reaches it in finite time. It can then use any of these continuations. For p=1/2, y∗​=−1, and initial value y(0)=0, arrival occurs at time two; departure at c≥2 gives y=−1−(t−c)2/4 afterwards. Waiting-time solutions of a sublinear Bernoulli equation give a related mechanism for departures in the opposite direction.
