Rice University's ELEC 332: In the Lab makes the model experimentally testable. Preparation resets the spectrum analyzer and observes an unmodulated generator output on it and an oscilloscope. A Wavetek function generator supplies external modulation. The specified 10MHz carrier and 10kHz message separate three peaks. Marker readouts give frequency and level; narrowing resolution bandwidth shows how the instrument broadens a line.[ref]
Part 3 uses an SA602 analog multiplier module. The message generator also triggers the oscilloscope; multiplier output feeds both displays. A no-offset module supplies a suppressed-carrier control. Replacing it with the offset-adding module restores the carrier; varying levels tests signal distortion. Finally a radio receives the conventional AM transmission, and varying message frequency checks that the heard tone belongs to this source. These are prescribed observations, not reported measurements.[ref]
Calibration matters: verify generator voltage at the connected input, distinguish peak from peak-to-peak amplitude and root mean square, and check analyser units and loading before comparing heights with Am/2 or power with A2m2/(8R). The unmodulated carrier is a reference control. Limited resolution can merge sidebands; generator harmonics can add peaks. Rice explicitly demonstrates both limitations in Part 1.[ref]
A complementary University of Kansas procedure, A.2 - Amplitude Modulation, calibrates the added carrier and the suppressed-carrier product separately to 4V peak-to-peak before recombining them. It holds the carrier fixed, varies modulation depth, reads envelope extrema and carrier-to-sideband power differences, then connects the demodulator and adjusts its tunable low-pass filter for recovery, comparing the output with the message. These are setup targets and comparison instructions, not measured results.[ref]
The inference combines time and frequency spectrum readouts: correct sideband spacing tests frequency translation, relative heights test modulation depth, and the recovered message tests the detector. Hardware clipping, receiver filtering and noise can spoil recovery even at m<1. Conversely failure of an envelope detector at m>1 does not demonstrate failure of a synchronized multiplier receiver.
