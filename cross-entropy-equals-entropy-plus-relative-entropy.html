Cross-entropy equals entropy plus relative entropy (H(P,Q)−H(P)=DKL​(P∥Q))

For finite probability mass functions, with qi​>0 whenever pi​>0, subtract the two expectations of logarithmic loss:
H(P,Q)−H(P)​=i:pi​>0∑​pi​(log2​pi​−log2​qi​)=i:pi​>0∑​pi​log2​qi​pi​​=DKL​(P∥Q).​
(9)
The Gibbs inequality makes the difference nonnegative and zero exactly when P=Q. If Q assigns zero probability to an outcome with positive probability under P, both the cross-entropy and the Kullback-Leibler divergence are infinite. The finite information entropy still leaves the displayed identity valid in the extended sense. In more general settings, undefined differences such as ∞−∞ cannot be used as a derivation.
