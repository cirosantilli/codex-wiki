Fitting subgroup (F(G))

Words: 196 Articles: 4
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
The largest nilpotent normal subgroup of a finite group G. If Op​(G) denotes its p-core, distinct p-cores commute, and F(G)=∏p​Op​(G). Every normal nilpotent group has Sylow subgroups which are characteristic subgroups, so it is contained in this product.

Fitting subgroup is self-centralizing in soluble groups

Words: 45
Fitting subgroup OurBigBook.com
In a finite soluble group, CG​(F(G))≤F(G). Put C=CG​(F) and D=C∩F=Z(F). If C>D, choose an elementary abelian minimal normal subgroup of G/D inside C/D, and let K be its preimage. Then K′≤D≤Z(K), so K is a nilpotent normal subgroup, contradicting K≤F.

Fitting subgroup centralizes the socle

Words: 59
Fitting subgroup OurBigBook.com
For a finite group, F(G) centralizes its group socle. A nonabelian minimal normal subgroup meets F(G) trivially: otherwise it is nilpotent, and its nontrivial characteristic center makes it abelian. Their group commutators therefore vanish. An abelian minimal normal subgroup M is an elementary abelian p-group; M≤Op​(G) and M∩Z(Op​(G))=1 by the fixed-point counting of a finite p-group on M. Minimal normality makes M≤Z(Op​(G)), and other p-cores commute with M.

Nilpotent normal subgroup

Words: 30
Fitting subgroup OurBigBook.com
A normal subgroup which is a nilpotent group. In a finite group its unique Sylow subgroups are characteristic subgroups and hence normal in the ambient group. Products of nilpotent normal subgroups are nilpotent groups: their normal Sylow subgroups of different prime characteristics commute.

p-core (Op​(G))

Words: 33
Fitting subgroup OurBigBook.com
The largest normal p-subgroup of a finite group. Products of normal p-subgroups are again normal p-subgroups, since ∣AB∣=∣A∣∣B∣/∣A∩B∣. Thus the subgroup generated by all such subgroups is itself a p-subgroup. It is a characteristic subgroup.

Classification of groups of order ten (C10​, D10​)

Words: 75
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
A group of order ten is either a cyclic group or the dihedral group of the pentagon. Lagrange's theorem and the fact that a group of exponent two has power-of-two order force an element of order five or ten. In the latter case the group is cyclic. In the former case its order-five cyclic subgroup is normal by index two; an outside involution acts on it either trivially or by inversion. The trivial action gives C5​×C2​≅C10​, and inversion gives D10​.

Heisenberg group over a prime field (UT3​(Fp​))

Words: 59
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
Upper-unitriangular three-by-three matrices over the prime finite field form the finite analogue of the real Heisenberg group. In coordinates their multiplication is (a,b,c)(a′,b′,c′)=(a+a′,b+b′,c+c′+ab′). The order is p3, and (a,b,c)k=(ka,kb,kc+(2k​)ab). For odd p, every nonidentity element has order p, but the group is nonabelian. For p=2 the exponent statement changes, so odd characteristic is essential for the nonisomorphic Gassmann equivalent regular subgroups construction.

Tetrahedral symmetry (T≅A4​,Td​≅S4​)

Words: 73
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
A regular tetrahedron has a proper rotational symmetry group T of order 12, isomorphic to the alternating group A4​ acting on its four vertices. Its full spatial symmetry group Td​ has order 24 and is isomorphic to the symmetric group S4​; the additional operations reverse orientation. Three-fold vertex/face-axis rotations and half-turns about opposite-edge midpoints generate the proper group. The distinction matters when a field symmetry uses only proper rotations, but a scalar density also admits reflections.

Group of order eight (∣G∣=8)

Words: 87
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
Up to group isomorphism, the groups of order eight are C8​, C4​×C2​, C23​, D8​ and Q8​, with D8​ here denoting the eight-element dihedral group. An element of order eight gives the first case. If every nonidentity element has order two the group is an elementary abelian group. Otherwise, after excluding elements of order eight, a cyclic subgroup of order four has index two; an element outside it either commutes with its generator or inverts it. In the latter case its square is either the identity or the central involution, giving the dihedral group or quaternion group.

Feit–Thompson theorem

Words: 33
Finite group theory OurBigBook.com
Every finite group of odd order is a solvable group. In particular, a nonabelian simple group has even order and therefore has an involution by Cauchy's theorem for finite groups. This is a deep theorem, not an elementary consequence of the Sylow theorems.

Maximal subgroups of A5 (A4​, C5​⋊C2​, S3​)

Words: 71
Alternating group OurBigBook.com
Every maximal proper subgroup normalizes one of its minimal normal subgroups. As A5​ has no proper nonabelian simple subgroup, that subgroup is elementary abelian. Its possible types are C2​,C22​,C3​,C5​. Their normalizers have orders 4,12,6,10 respectively. Automorphisms of alternating groups from triple supports (Aut(An​)≅Sn​(n≥5, n=6))

Words: 117
Alternating group OurBigBook.com
Except in degree six, the 3-cycles are characterized among order-three elements by the largest centralizer. Their cyclic subgroups correspond to three-element supports. Two different such subgroups commute exactly for disjoint supports; with one common point their generator products have order five, and with two common points those products have order two or three. Thus an automorphism induces an automorphism of J(n,3). The maximal cliques of a Johnson graph reconstruct a permutation of the points. After undoing that permutation, an automorphism fixes every cyclic 3-subgroup. Product orders force its choices of generator inversion to be consistent on the connected graph, and inversion of every 3-cycle would reverse a noncommuting product. Hence it fixes all 3-cycles, which generate An​.

Symmetric-group involution class-size collision (n!/[2kk!(n−2k)!]=(2n​)⟺k=1 or (n,k)=(6,3))

Words: 86
Automorphisms of the symmetric group OurBigBook.com
A permutation of order two is a product of k disjoint transpositions. Its conjugacy class has the displayed size. For k≥2, equality with the transposition class requires (n−2)!/(n−2k)!=2k−1k!. The left side grows strictly with n. At n=2k, its ratio to the right side is one only for k=3; at k=2 it jumps from below to above one between n=4 and n=5, and for k≥4 it is already greater than one. Thus degree six is the only possible exceptional degree for an group automorphism not preserving transpositions.

Oligomorphic permutation group

Words: 58
Permutation group OurBigBook.com
A permutation group on a set is oligomorphic when its diagonal action on ordered n-tuples has finitely many orbits for every positive integer n. For a countable model of a complete theory, an oligomorphic automorphism group makes every finite-arity type space finite: realized types are dense, and only finitely many are possible because types are constant on orbits.

A cycle and a transposition generate the symmetric group exactly at coprime separation

Words: 48
Symmetric group OurBigBook.com
For n≥2 and 1≤k<n, an n-cycle c=(12…n) and t=(11+k) generate Sn​ exactly when gcd(n,k)=1. Their conjugate transpositions connect labels differing by k modulo n. This graph is connected exactly at coprime separation, so transpositions on a connected graph generate the symmetric group. Otherwise residue classes modulo gcd(n,k) form a nontrivial block system preserved by both generators.

Permutation order and sign do not determine conjugacy
Words: 51
Cycle type OurBigBook.com
Conjugation preserves order of a group element, because (gxg−1)k=gxkg−1, and preserves the sign of a permutation, because sign is a group homomorphism into {1,−1}. These invariants do not determine cycle type. In S6​, (123) and (123)(456) both have order three and positive sign, but have three and zero fixed points respectively. Conjugation relabels cycles, so these permutations are not conjugate.
Permutation conjugate to its square (σ∼Sn​​σ2 ⟺ ord(σ) is odd)
Words: 62
Cycle type OurBigBook.com
Squaring an odd-length permutation cycle retains its length, whereas an even-length cycle splits into two cycles of half its length. Thus if any even cycle is present, the number of cycles strictly increases, so the cycle type changes. Since conjugate permutations in a symmetric group have the same cycle type, a permutation is conjugate to its square precisely when all its cycle lengths are odd, equivalently when its order of a group element is odd.
Transposition changes the cycle count by one (ℓ(τσ)=ℓ(σ)±1)
Words: 83
Cycle type OurBigBook.com
Left multiplication of a permutation by a transposition (a b) swaps the two target labels a,b. If they occur in different cycles, (a x1​…xp​)(b y1​…yq​) becomes the single cycle (a x1​…xp​ b y1​…yq​). If they occur in the same cycle, that formula reverses and splits the cycle in two. All other cycles are unchanged, so the total number of cycles, including fixed points, decreases or increases by exactly one. Empty intermediate lists yield one-cycles and require no exceptional case.

Minimal faithful permutation degree of the quaternion group (μ(Q8​)=8)

Words: 60
Quaternion group OurBigBook.com
The least size of a finite set on which the quaternion group acts faithfully is eight. Every nontrivial subgroup of Q8​ contains its central involution −1. In an action on fewer than eight points, the orbit-stabilizer theorem makes every stabilizer subgroup nontrivial, so −1 fixes every point. This rules out a faithful group action, even with several group orbits. The regular action on eight points is faithful by Cayley theorem.

Powerful p-group

Words: 363 Articles: 4
Finite p-group OurBigBook.com
A finite p-group is powerful if [G,G]≤Gp for odd p, or [G,G]≤G4 for p=2. Here powers denote generated subgroups. The same definition applies to pro-p groups using closed power and commutator subgroups. Its Frattini subgroup is Gp. For odd p, successive power layers admit linear correction of roots, which makes every element of Gp an actual pth power.

Minimal cyclic factorization criterion for powerful p-groups (G=⟨a1​⟩⋯⟨ad​⟩, d=d(G))

Words: 84
Powerful p-group OurBigBook.com
For an odd prime number p, a finite p-group that is a product of d(G) cyclic subgroups is a powerful p-group. Indeed, H=G/Gp has exponent of a finite group p and the same minimal generator number d. Its cyclic factorization gives ∣H∣≤pd, while its Frattini quotient has order pd. Thus Φ(H)=1, H is an elementary abelian group, and [G,G]⊆Gp. The condition on the number of factors is essential: the nonabelian Heisenberg group over Fp​ has exponent p and is a product of three cyclic subgroups, although its minimal generator number is two.

Subgroup generator bound for a powerful finite p-group (d(H)≤d(G))

The onto power map θ:V→W sends A into the image of Hp in K/Φ(K). Since Φ(K)≤Gp2, this image has dimension at least dimθ(A). Also Φ(K)≤Φ(H)≤K. Induction on ∣G∣, applied to the powerful subgroup Gp, gives d(K)≤dimW. Consequently
d(H)≤dimA+dimW−dimθ(A)≤dimW+dimkerθ=dimV=d(G).

Power lifting in a powerful p-group (Gp={xp:x∈G})

Words: 86
Powerful p-group OurBigBook.com
For odd p, set P0​=G and Pi+1​=Pip​. Powerful embedding gives [Pi​,G]≤Pi+1​. Modulo Pi+2​, commutators with Pi​ are central and have exponent dividing p, so (xy)p≡xpyp when y∈Pi​. Also the power map Pi​/Pi+1​→Pi+1​/Pi+2​ is a surjective homomorphism. First find a root modulo P2​, then multiply it by successive corrections in Pi​. The process terminates for a finite p-group, and converges for a pro-p group with separated power filtration. This proves that the generated power subgroup consists of actual powers.

A Sylow basis consists of one Sylow subgroup for each prime dividing the group order, with any two of them permutable as subgroups. Permutable means Pp​Pq​=Pq​Pp​, not elementwise commutation.
