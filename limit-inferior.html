Limit inferior (liminf)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence Limit inferior and limit superior
OurBigBook.com Words: 116
The limit inferior of a real sequence is
liminfn→∞​an​=supN​infn≥N​an​,
(55)
allowing a value in the extended real number line. For a finite value L, it has two complementary meanings: for every ε>0, all sufficiently late terms satisfy an​>L−ε, while arbitrarily late terms satisfy an​<L+ε. The first prevents persistent dips below L; the second prevents any larger number from being the true lower edge.
For an​=(−1)n(1+1/n), the lower bounds in limit inferior and limit superior approach −1. Every late term eventually exceeds −1−ε, and the subsequence at odd integers comes arbitrarily close to −1. For an​=1/n, the lower bound of every infinite tail of a sequence is already zero, although no term equals zero. Thus a limit inferior need not be the minimum of any tail.
