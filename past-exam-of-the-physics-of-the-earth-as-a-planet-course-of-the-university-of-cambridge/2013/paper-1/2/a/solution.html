Oceanic magnetic anomalies. Newly formed oceanic crust contains magnetic minerals. As it cools through their magnetic blocking temperatures, it acquires thermoremanent magnetization recording the ambient field polarity. Continuing seafloor spreading carries that record away from the ridge. Geomagnetic reversals therefore generate approximately paired marine magnetic stripes parallel to the ridge, with older crust farther from its axis. A magnetometer measures the superposed field above this magnetized material; subtracting a reference field reveals magnetic anomalies. The anomaly sign and shape depend on magnetization direction, latitude, source depth and geometry, so a positive measured anomaly is not universally identical to normal rock polarity.[ref]
Matching a sequence of stripes to an independently dated reversal chronology gives plate ages and spreading histories. A stripe of width w formed over interval Δt implies half-spreading rate v=w/Δt under steady symmetric spreading; full separation speed is 2v. Unequal widths on opposite flanks can reflect asymmetric spreading or later deformation. Alteration, remagnetization and overlapping magnetic sources complicate inversion. The sequence correlation is stronger evidence than an isolated stripe or a field-strength peak.
Rayleigh-Bénard convection. Consider a horizontal fluid layer of thickness d, hotter below by ΔT>0. The conductive state is motionless with a linear temperature gradient. A rising warm parcel is buoyant, but viscosity resists its motion and thermal diffusion erases its temperature anomaly. Above onset, buoyancy can sustain overturning rolls and heat transfer exceeds pure conduction, with Nusselt number Nu>1. Nonlinear advection eventually limits the disturbance; larger driving can produce time-dependent or turbulent states. The ideal Boussinesq problem does not require hexagonal cells: planform selection depends on boundaries and non-Boussinesq effects. Convection driven by surface tension at a free surface is a distinct mechanism. An adverse temperature gradient supplies driving, but overcoming diffusion and viscosity requires a finite threshold.
