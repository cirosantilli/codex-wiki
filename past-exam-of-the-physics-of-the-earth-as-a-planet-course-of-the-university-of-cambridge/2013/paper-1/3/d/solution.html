Use the flat two-layer model, with thickness h=30km, crustal shear speed β1​=3.5kms−1 and mantle speed β2​=4.5kms−1. For SH-wave travel times in a crustal waveguide, unfolding each reflected path gives all reflection orders at once:
tD​=3.5x​,tm​=3.5x2+(60m)2​​,m=1,2,…,​
(21)
with x in kilometres and times in seconds. The direct wave begins at the origin; reflected orders have intercepts 60m/3.5=17.14ms and approach the direct-wave slope at large distance.
The critical angle, measured from the vertical, is ic​=arcsin(3.5/4.5)≃51.1∘. Associated critical refraction and its multiples give
tH,m​=4.5x​+2mhβ1−2​−β2−2​​=4.5x​+10.77m,x≥xc,m​=2mhtanic​≃74.25mkm.​
(22)
Each head-wave line is tangent to its reflection curve at the critical distance. In particular the primary head wave exists before it becomes the first arrival; it overtakes the direct wave only around 170km.
The slope of a reflected travel-time curve is p=dtm​/dx=sini/β1​. For x<xc,m​, p<1/β2​, so the mantle supports outgoing shear radiation and successive reverberations leak energy. For x>xc,m​, 1/β2​<p<1/β1​: the mantle field is evanescent and ideal interface reflection is total. These leaking and trapped SH-wave reverberations explain the annotations in the original travel-time drawing below. A reflected ray is not itself a separate Love mode: constructive interference of the trapped contributions, including reflection phase, selects the normal modes.
For a horizontally propagating mode with β1​<c<β2​, define vertical wavenumbers
q=ωβ1−2​−c−2​,s=ωc−2−β2−2​​.
(23)
The free upper surface and welded interface impose the Love-wave dispersion condition
tan(qh)=μ1​qμ2​s​,qh=nπ+arctanμ1​qμ2​s​,n=0,1,2,…,
(24)
where μj​=ρj​βj2​ are shear moduli. Each phase-velocity branch falls towards 3.5kms−1 with increasing frequency. Higher branches join 4.5kms−1 at their low-frequency cutoffs. At cutoff s→0, so
fc,n​=2hβ1−2​−β2−2​​n​≃0.0928nHz.​
(25)
Thus the first three overtones have maximum periods about 10.77, 5.39 and 3.59s. The fundamental mode has n=0 and no positive-frequency cutoff in this flat layer-over-half-space model: it approaches c=β2​ as f→0, with ever-deeper penetration. The phase-velocity sketch uses an explicitly illustrative equal-density choice, because the question gives speeds but no density or rigidity ratio. That missing ratio changes detailed branch curvature, not the cutoff values or the requested branch topology.
For the actual finite spherical Earth, the very-long-period continuation must instead be described by discrete toroidal normal modes, not a flat infinite waveguide at arbitrary wavelength. The flat-model answer therefore does not assert a continuum of arbitrarily low-frequency global nonrigid oscillations. A global lowest oscillation frequency cannot be computed from the supplied crust-and-half-space data alone.[ref]
