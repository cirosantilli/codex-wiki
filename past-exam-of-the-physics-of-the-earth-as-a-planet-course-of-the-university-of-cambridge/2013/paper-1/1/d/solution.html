Ridge push is a horizontal pressure force integrated with depth, equivalently a difference in gravitational potential energy per horizontal area. It is not the weight of a slab multiplied by the surface slope at one chosen point.
For an explicit compensated-column derivation, let y measure depth below the ridge seafloor; the old seafloor is at y=h. Set b=ρm​αΔT. The hot column has greater pressure. Its pressure excess over the old column is
Δp(y)=⎩⎨⎧​g(ρm​−ρw​)y,2agb​(a−s)2,0,​0<y<h,y=h+s,0<s<a,y>h+a.​
(7)
The second line follows by integrating δρ(s)=b(1−s/a) upwards from the compensation level. The two expressions agree at y=h because (ρm​−ρw​)h=ba/2. Integrating this positive pressure excess gives the ridge-push force in a plate model, directed away from the ridge:
F=∫Δpdy=6ρm​gαΔTa2​+2g(ρm​−ρw​)h2​.
(8)
Consistently retaining only first order in thermal expansion gives
F≃2.60×1012Nm−1.​
(9)
If the topographic term is retained within the same linear-density column model, it adds 1.52×1011Nm−1, giving F≃2.75×1012Nm−1. This is a useful small correction, not a claim that all other second-order material effects have been included. Both values use the intended hot initial plate, not the inconsistent printed zero-age profile.
