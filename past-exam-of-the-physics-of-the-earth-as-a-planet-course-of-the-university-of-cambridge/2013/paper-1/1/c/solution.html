 ... Physics of the Earth as a Planet course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the physics of the Earth as a Planet course of the University of Cambridge 2013 Paper 1 1 c
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
Use the intended hot-ridge state and a linear law of thermal expansion about mantle density ρm​ at T1​. Let s be depth below the old seafloor. Its excess density is
δρ(s)=ρm​αΔT(1−s/a),0<s<a,
(5)
and its excess column mass is ∫0a​δρds=ρm​αΔTa/2. Isostasy requires equal pressure at a compensation depth below both columns. Lowering the old seafloor by h replaces rock by water, reducing column mass by (ρm​−ρw​)h. Therefore the isostatic ridge elevation in a plate model is
h=2(ρm​−ρw​)ρm​αΔTa​=3.90×103m=3.9km.​
(6)
Here αΔT=0.052; the result is a first-order estimate and neglects sediments, crustal-composition differences and flexural support. If one instead insists on the literal printed zero-age profile, its column is denser than the old plate and the formal relative elevation reverses sign, −3.9km to this order. That profile cannot describe the elevated hot ridge posited by the question.
