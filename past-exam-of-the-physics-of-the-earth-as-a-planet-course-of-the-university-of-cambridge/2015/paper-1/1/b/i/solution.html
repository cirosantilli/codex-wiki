Assume a stable, lossless elastic body with the usual symmetry of the elasticity tensor and boundary conditions that eliminate the surface terms in integration by parts, such as a traction-free outer surface. Its elastic stiffness operator is then self-adjoint. When density varies spatially, the precise eigenproblem is generalized:
H^uα​=ρωα2​uα​,⟨uα​,uβ​⟩ρ​=∫Ω​ρuα∗​⋅uβ​dV=δαβ​.
(2)
Here α abbreviates the pair of mode labels. Equivalently, ρ−1H^ is a self-adjoint operator in the mass inner product. Stability makes ωα2​ real and nonnegative. Modes at different eigenvalues are orthogonal, and an orthonormal basis can be selected within each degenerate eigenspace. Completeness permits expansion of a suitable displacement field in these mass-weighted elastic normal modes.
Insert the expansion and project onto uα∗​. Mass normalization diagonalizes both the acceleration term and the restoring term. For a symmetric stress glut tensor supported away from the boundary,
∫Ω​uα∗​⋅(−∇⋅Γ)dV=∫Ω​Γ:∇uα∗​dV=∫Ω​Γ:eα∗​dV.
(3)
The symmetry of the stress glut tensor removes the antisymmetric part of the displacement gradient, leaving the strain. The result is one independent forced oscillator per mode, with the stated excitation integral. If the modes instead have mass norm Nα​, that integral is divided by Nα​. A source touching the boundary requires the corresponding surface forcing to be retained rather than silently dropping it.
Rigid translations and rotations can be zero-frequency modes of a free body. Their strain vanishes, so an internal symmetric seismic moment tensor does not excite them through this coupling. Attenuation, radiative boundaries or other non-self-adjoint effects require modifications of the simple conservative expansion.
