Seismic surface-wave dispersion. Rayleigh waves combine vertical and longitudinal motion in the vertical plane of propagation; Love waves have horizontal transverse motion and can be guided by a low-shear-speed surface layer. A homogeneous elastic half-space supports a Rayleigh wave with a frequency-independent speed in the ideal elastic limit, but depth-dependent Earth structure makes the surface-wave eigenfunctions and their propagation speeds depend on frequency. This structural dispersion can occur even when each constituent material is itself nondispersive.
Distinguish phase velocity c=ω/k from group velocity U=dω/dk, which controls a narrow-band envelope in the appropriate weakly attenuating limit. Longer periods generally have deeper sensitivity, while shorter periods emphasize shallower structure. In a profile whose shear speed increases with depth this often makes long-period waves faster, but low-velocity zones, anisotropy and higher modes can change that trend. Frequency dependence need not be monotonic in every Earth model.
Measure phase delays or wave-packet arrival times across known source–receiver paths for many periods, accounting for source phase, cycle ambiguity, multipathing and mode identification. Finite bandwidth, depth averaging and correlated sensitivities mean that one dispersion curve does not uniquely determine a detailed Earth profile.
