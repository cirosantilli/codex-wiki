Chain-growth polymerization

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 16
In chain-growth polymerization, each growth step joins a monomer to a reactive site on a polymer chain and regenerates that reactive site. This is the precise mechanistic restriction in IUPAC's definition: growth occurs exclusively through these monomer–chain-site reactions. The word chain refers to the succession of site-regenerating chemical reactions. It does not merely mean that the product looks like a long chain. Chain-growth initiation starts this succession; chain-growth propagation continues it; chain-growth termination and chain transfer in chain polymerization can change its fate.[ref]
The contrast with step-growth polymerization concerns the permitted joining partners. There, suitable reactive groups can join two existing chains, for example a two-unit chain and a three-unit chain. In chain-growth propagation, a free monomer adds to a polymerization-active site. Release of a small byproduct is a separate classification question: IUPAC explicitly allows condensative chain polymerization.[ref] The useful consequence is that a few growing chains can incorporate many monomer molecules while much of the feed remains unreacted. Their degree of polymerization depends on how those units are shared among chains, as finite chain-growth bookkeeping makes explicit.
