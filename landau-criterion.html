Landau criterion (vL​=infp>0​ϵ(p)/p)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity Superfluid
OurBigBook.com Words: 762 Articles: 6
The Landau criterion asks whether creating an excitation can lower a moving superfluid's energy. For a weakly coupled, heavy obstacle moving through a uniform liquid initially at zero temperature, let ϵ(p)>0 be the excitation energy in the fluid rest frame, with momentum magnitude p. Transferring momentum p from an obstacle moving at velocity v supplies, to leading order in its negligible recoil, energy v⋅p. Emission requires
ϵ(p)−v⋅p≤0.
(19)
For an isotropic dispersion relation, the easiest direction is parallel to v, giving the necessary kinematic condition
v≥vL​,vL​=infp>0​pϵ(p)​.
(20)
An infimum includes cases in which the limiting slope occurs only as p→0. A finite-mass obstacle of mass M instead loses v⋅p−p2/(2M), so the corresponding emission inequality includes the extra positive recoil term p2/(2M) on the left. This is an energy and momentum balance, not a calculation of an emission rate.
The Landau criterion excludes spontaneous emission into the specified spectrum below its threshold. Above it, kinematic permission alone does not guarantee strong coupling or rapid decay. It is not a universal observed flow threshold: walls, channel geometry and quantum vortex nucleation can enable other decay routes, and finite temperature permits scattering from existing excitations. Using only bulk phonons and rotons leaves those processes outside the model.
Landau's 1941 energy and momentum argument used a roton approximation different from the finite-momentum minimum illustrated below.[ref]
Landau received his Nobel Prize in Physics in 1962 while hospitalized in Moscow after a car accident.[ref]
For a deliberately simple Bose-Einstein condensation comparison, stipulate an ideal free-particle excitation law ϵ=p2/(2m). Then ϵ/p=p/(2m) has infimum zero. Macroscopic one-particle occupation therefore supplies no positive emission threshold by this argument alone. By contrast a linear phonon law ϵ=cp yields vL​=c. The dispersion relation and the collective response contain information that the occupation criterion alone does not supply; Bose-Einstein condensation and thermodynamic superfluidity must be tested separately.
To check the picture algebraically, on the stipulated roton branch,
dqd​qe(q)​=q2qe′(q)−e(q)​,qe′(q)−e(q)=2q2−8.6.
(21)
Thus tangency occurs at qL​=4.3​, slightly above the roton energy minimum. At an energy minimum e′=0, but a tangent through the origin needs e′=e/q>0. Minimizing energy and minimizing the energy-to-momentum ratio are different operations. This geometric construction makes the criterion useful even when the full dispersion relation is supplied numerically rather than by a simple formula.
