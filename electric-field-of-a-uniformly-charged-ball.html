Electric field of a uniformly charged ball (Er​(r)=Qr/(4πε0​R3)(0≤r≤R))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Maxwell equations Gauss's law
OurBigBook.com Words: 113
Distribute a positive electric charge Q uniformly through a closed ball of radius R in vacuum, with no external field. Its volume charge density is 3Q/(4πR3). For 0<r<R, the enclosed fraction is (r/R)3. Applying Gauss's law on a concentric Gaussian surface gives
Er​(r)=⎩⎨⎧​4πε0​R3Qr​,4πε0​r2Q​,​0≤r≤R,r≥R.​
(24)
The electric field grows linearly inside and decays as an inverse square outside. With ER​=Q/(4πε0​R2), its values at r/R=1/2,1,2 are ER​/2,ER​,ER​/4. The field vanishes at the centre by spherical symmetry and is continuous at the surface because this ideal volume distribution has no singular surface layer. Its interior behavior differs from a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium, where mobile charge redistributes.
