Fidelity of quantum states (F(ρ,σ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum information theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 10
For density operators ρ,σ on the same finite-dimensional Hilbert space, quantum fidelity measures how close their quantum states are. This article uses the unsquared convention:
F(ρ,σ)=Trρ​σρ​​=∥ρ​σ​∥1​,0≤F(ρ,σ)≤1.
(10)
Each square root is the positive square root of an operator, and ∥⋅∥1​ is the trace norm. Quantum fidelity is symmetric in its inputs; it is one exactly when ρ=σ, and zero exactly when ρσ=0, meaning that the support of a positive operator for one state is orthogonal to that for the other. Larger quantum fidelity means greater similarity. The separate squared quantum fidelity is Fsq​=F2. [ref]
For two normalized pure states, quantum fidelity is the magnitude of their inner product, F=∣⟨ψ∣φ⟩∣. Thus ∣0⟩ compared with itself has F=1, while ∣0⟩ and ∣1⟩ have F=0. For ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, comparison with ∣0⟩ gives F=1/2​, Fsq​=1/2, and trace distance D=1/2​ by the pure-state trace distance and fidelity identity.
Why compare density operators rather than their entries? A change of coordinates should not make the same physical preparations look more or less alike. Unitary invariance of trace distance and fidelity makes quantum fidelity independent of a common basis choice. Multiplying a pure state vector by a global phase leaves its density operator unchanged, so its quantum fidelity with the original state is one. These features make quantum fidelity useful for checking whether quantum channels, state preparations, and Schumacher compression preserve a target quantum state.
The connection to Uhlmann's theorem supplies another beautiful interpretation: quantum fidelity is the largest absolute inner product between pure vectors obtained from a purification of a density operator for each state, using a sufficiently large common reference space. Uhlmann's 1976 paper extended transition probability to algebraic states, including those of a C-star algebra; for density operators its transition probability corresponds to our squared quantum fidelity. The measurement characterization of quantum fidelity gives a complementary description directly in terms of observable outcome statistics. [ref]
