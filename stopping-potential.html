Stopping potential (eVs​=Kmax​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Photon Photon absorption Photoelectric effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 243
A stopping potential Vs​≥0 is the magnitude of the retarding electric potential difference just sufficient to stop collection of even the fastest relevant electrons in an ideal collection geometry. Since the electron's electric charge is −e, the positive energy barrier is eVs​. After the photoelectric contact-potential correction,
Vs​=eh​ν−eΦ​.
(48)
The maximum here is an emission energy edge, not the mean kinetic energy. In an ideal planar retarding geometry, transmission instead selects the component normal to the plates; identifying its endpoint with the full edge requires collection of trajectories that attain that edge in the relevant direction.
To make a complete illustrative curve, choose a uniform distribution of outward normal kinetic energy K⊥​ on 0≤K⊥​≤Kmax​, complete collection at zero barrier, and zero background. This is a deliberately chosen distribution, not a prediction of the Einstein photoelectric equation. For an effective retarding barrier eVr​, the collected fraction is
F(Vr​)=⎩⎨⎧​1,1−Vr​/Vs​,0,​Vr​≤0,0<Vr​<Vs​,Vr​≥Vs​.​
(49)
Integrating that uniform probability density above eVr​ proves the middle line. Thus I(Vr​)=Isat​F(Vr​) has a common endpoint when incident light intensity changes, even though its vertical scale changes. Real emission distributions need not give straight segments.
