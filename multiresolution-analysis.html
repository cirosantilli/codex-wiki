Multiresolution analysis

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Wavelet
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 15
A multiresolution analysis organizes a function into successively finer approximation spaces. In the usual orthonormal construction on L2(R), these are closed linear subspaces (Vj​)j∈Z​ with
Vj​⊆Vj+1​,⋃j​Vj​​=L2(R),⋂j​Vj​={0}.
(51)
Rescaling relates the levels: f(x)∈Vj​ if and only if f(2x)∈Vj+1​. Integer translates of a scaling function form an orthonormal basis of V0​. The detail space is the orthogonal complement of Vj​ inside Vj+1​, giving Vj+1​=Vj​⊕Wj​. Coarse information and new details therefore fit together without overlap in squared L2 norm.
For the Haar wavelet, Vj​ consists of square-integrable functions constant on cells of length 2−j. Splitting every cell into two halves creates one new contrast per old cell, the Haar refinement identity. On the bounded example [0,1], the first three spaces have dimensions 1,2,4: a whole-interval mean, two half-interval means, and four quarter-interval means. The Haar projection diagram displays those three resolutions. This bounded construction starts at level zero; the displayed intersection condition refers to the full family on the real line.
The usefulness is a controlled way to increase resolution. Earlier coarse information remains available while new wavelet coefficients identify what the finer description adds. The wavelet approximation complexity connects this extra resolution to both approximation error and the number of coefficients being estimated. [ref]
