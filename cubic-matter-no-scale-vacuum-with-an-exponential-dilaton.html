Cubic-matter no-scale vacuum with an exponential dilaton (Vmin​=0,FT=0 on the nontrivial finite branch)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Supersymmetry Supergravity No-scale supergravity Matter-logarithm no-scale potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 194 Articles: 1
For W=C3+A+b, A=ae−αS, the potential is V=∣C3+b+(1+αs)A∣2/(st3)+3∣C∣4/(st2). A finite zero-energy vacuum requires C=0 and b+(1+αs)A=0. With a,b=0, such a solution exists exactly when 0<∣b/a∣≤2e−1/2: the magnitude equation is ∣b/a∣=(1+x)e−x/2, x=αs>0, whose maximum occurs at x=1. At that vacuum FS=FC=0 but FT=eK/2tWˉ=0. Both components of T are flat, while C is quartically lifted. If no root exists, the positive potential falls toward zero as t→∞ and has no finite minimum. If a=b=0, the C=0 family is supersymmetric instead.
