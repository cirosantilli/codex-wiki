Floating equilibrium (Vdisplaced​/Vbody​=ρbody​/ρf​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Buoyancy Archimedes' principle
OurBigBook.com Words: 259
A freely floating body at rest has no supporting contact or applied holding force. Neglecting surface tension and the buoyancy of air, its upward buoyancy balances its downward force from gravity:
Fb​=mg.
(43)
For a body of uniform mass density ρbody​ and total volume Vbody​, its mass is m=ρbody​Vbody​. Substitution into Archimedes' principle gives
Vbody​Vdisplaced​​=ρf​ρbody​​.
(44)
Partial immersion is possible when ρbody​<ρf​. A nonuniform body uses its mean mass density m/Vbody​ instead; for a hollow body this volume must count the enclosed space that excludes water. This explains how the amount of displaced water can support a vessel whose walls alone are denser than water. [ref]
For the 600 kgm−3 cube in water of mass density 1000 kgm−3, the displaced fraction is 600/1000=0.60: sixty percent of its volume lies below the surface. With a horizontal top and bottom, the immersed depth d satisfies Vdisplaced​=Ad, so
Vdisplaced​=0.000600 m3,d=0.0100.000600​=0.060 m.
(45)
Its remaining height is 0.040 m, and the buoyancy is 1000×9.81×0.000600=5.886 N, exactly balancing its downward force from gravity.
