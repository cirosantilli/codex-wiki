Chernoff information for complementary Bernoulli distributions (C(Bern(p),Bern(1−p))=−ln(2p(1−p)​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 293
Let P be the Bernoulli distribution with success probability 0.2 and Q the Bernoulli distribution with success probability 0.8. For 0≤s≤1,
Z(s)=0.2s0.81−s+0.8s0.21−s.
(66)
The two summands have product 0.16. Their arithmetic mean is at least their geometric mean, with equality at s=1/2. Thus
C(P,Q)=−ln0.8≃0.22314355 nats per observation.​
(67)
The same argument gives C=−ln(2p(1−p)​) for complementary parameters 0<p<1. At p=1/2 the laws agree and C=0.
With equal prior probabilities, count the successes K among n observations. The likelihood ratio is Q⊗n/P⊗n=42K−n, so choose Q when K>n/2 and P when K<n/2. If n is even, either decision or a fair tie randomization gives the same average error at K=n/2. Under P, K has the binomial distribution with parameters n,0.2. Symmetry therefore gives the exact optimal average error
pn∗​=∑k>n/2​(kn​)0.2k0.8n−k+21​1{n even}​(n/2n​)0.2n/20.8n/2.
(68)
The last term applies only for even n; it represents half the tie probability. Direct finite sums give p1∗​=0.2, p5∗​=0.05792 and p20∗​≃0.00157912. Twenty observations reduce the optimal average error to about 0.158%. The finite-sample Chernoff error bound at n=20 is 21​(0.8)20≃0.00576461, a guarantee rather than the exact error.
Reducing the exact error from p20∗​ to p50∗​≃1.29363×10−6 illustrates the value of repeated evidence. The example assumes a single fixed coin law throughout the experiment. If repeated sensor readings share an unmodelled common error, increasing their number need not supply this much independent information.
