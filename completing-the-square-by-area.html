Completing the square by area

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial Completing the square
OurBigBook.com Words: 149
Start with a square of side length x>0 and add two rectangles, each with side lengths x and 3. The total area is x2+6x. Place the rectangles along two adjacent sides of the square: the missing corner is a 3 by 3 square, of area 9. Filling it produces a square of side length x+3.
The area picture explains why the half-coefficient is squared. It also separates two operations that are easy to confuse: adding 9 changes x2+6x, whereas adding and subtracting 9 preserves the expression. When solving an equation instead, adding 9 to one side requires adding 9 to the other side too.
