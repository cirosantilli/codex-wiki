Chemical potential equality for particle exchange (μm,A​=μm,B​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Chemical potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 569 Articles: 1
Consider two internally equilibrated reservoirs A and B at the same imposed temperature and pressure. They may have different compositions or represent different phases. Transfer a small positive amount of substance δn of the same chemical species from A to B, holding the other inventories fixed. To first order, their total Gibbs free energy changes by
δGtotal​=−μm,A​δn+μm,B​δn=(μm,B​−μm,A​)δn.
(202)
Hence exchange toward lower chemical potential lowers Gibbs free energy. If transfer is allowed in both directions and the two reservoirs contain the exchangeable component, thermodynamic equilibrium requires equal chemical potentials for that component. See MIT 5.60, Lecture 14, pages 1–2.
An impermeable partition can maintain unequal chemical potentials; equality is a condition on an allowed exchange. Two pure samples of the same ideal gas already have equal chemical potentials at the same temperature and pressure. Different compositions at a common pressure can instead give different chemical potentials for one component through its thermodynamic activity.
Equality concerns the same exchangeable component in different places, not all different chemical species. For a permitted chemical reaction, the corresponding interior chemical equilibrium criterion is ∑i​νi​μm,i​=0, with signed stoichiometric vector entries νi​. The existing chemical equilibrium and constrained Gibbs minimization for chemical equilibrium sections develop those composition constraints.
