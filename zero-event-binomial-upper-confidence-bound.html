Zero-event binomial upper confidence bound (pU​=1−α1/n)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution Binomial distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 62
If X∼Bin(n,p) and X=0 is observed, the maximum-likelihood estimate of p is zero. A one-sided 1−α upper confidence bound solves PpU​​(X=0)=(1−pU​)n=α, so pU​=1−α1/n. For large n, pU​≃−log(α)/n, giving approximately 3/n at 95% confidence. This describes uncertainty after no observed events, rather than proving that the underlying probability is zero. It assumes independent observations with a common event probability; clustered data require a different uncertainty calculation.
