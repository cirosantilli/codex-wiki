Spin glass

Words: 854 Articles: 8
Spin model OurBigBook.com
A spin glass has frozen, irregular local spin magnetization without the periodic magnetic order of a simple ferromagnet or antiferromagnet. Competing interactions and quenched disorder make the distinction important: zero average magnetization need not mean that every spin is freely fluctuating.[ref]
The frustrated Ising triangle gives a small example of incompatible local preferences. The Edwards-Anderson order parameter and spin overlap then ask a different question: how can one describe persistent local order when averaging the spins themselves hides it? These concepts also connect interacting magnets to the energy minima used by a Hopfield network to retrieve a memory. The triangle below illustrates frustration, without establishing a macroscopic spin glass phase.

Frustrated Ising triangle (H=J(s1​s2​+s2​s3​+s3​s1​),J>0)

Words: 200
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
Take three Ising spins si​∈{−1,1}, with an antiferromagnetic interaction of energy scale J on each edge of a triangle. An individually satisfied bond has si​sj​=−1. But multiplying all three bond products gives (s1​s2​)(s2​s3​)(s3​s1​)=1, whereas three satisfied bonds would give −1. At least one bond must therefore remain unsatisfied. This is an exactly finite example of geometrical frustration.
There are six ground states with one unsatisfied bond and ground-state energy −J. The two all-aligned microstates have energy 3J. No interaction is switched off in a ground state: the system minimizes their sum by accepting one local disappointment. Allowing continuous three-component spins instead of Ising spins changes this optimization problem and permits the 120-degree arrangement described under geometrical frustration.
Writing β=1/(kB​T) for inverse temperature, the exact partition function and mean internal energy are
Z=6eβJ+2e−3βJ,⟨H⟩=6eβJ+2e−3βJ−6JeβJ+6Je−3βJ​.
(1)
At T>0 these expressions are smooth: a finite triangle has no thermodynamic phase transition. In zero magnetic field, spin inversion symmetry also gives ⟨si​⟩=0 at every site, even as the Boltzmann distribution concentrates on the six frustrated ground states.

Edwards-Anderson model (HJ​(s)=−∑{i,j}​Jij​si​sj​)

Words: 58

A common Ising version of the Edwards-Anderson model places Ising spins on a lattice with short-range, fixed random interactions Jij​. The displayed sum counts each interacting pair once; positive Jij​ favors equal spins, negative Jij​ favors opposite spins. Different samples retain different couplings. Quenched disorder describes this fixed-sample averaging.[ref]

Edwards-Anderson order parameter (qEA​=N−1∑i​⟨si​⟩α2​)

Words: 55
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
The Edwards-Anderson order parameter measures squared local spin magnetization within a selected equilibrium state α. Squaring prevents opposite local orientations from cancelling. The state selection and limits matter: the full finite, zero-field canonical ensemble has ⟨si​⟩=0 by spin inversion symmetry, so substituting that average would miss broken-state order.[ref]

Spin overlap (q(s,t)=N−1∑i​si​ti​)

Words: 124
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
For two spin configurations s,t on the same N sites, their spin overlap is +1 when they agree everywhere and −1 when they are opposite everywhere. Two independent canonical ensemble draws must use the same quenched disorder. Otherwise their comparison mixes changes of thermal state with changes of material sample.
An original finite example uses the six equally weighted ground states of the frustrated Ising triangle. For each first configuration, the second has one identical choice, one fully reversed choice, two choices differing at one site and two differing at two sites. Thus the probability distribution of spin overlap is
P(q=1)=P(q=−1)=61​,P(q=1/3)=P(q=−1/3)=31​.
(2)
It is symmetric, with ⟨q⟩=0 but ⟨q2⟩=11/27. At infinite temperature, all eight microstates are equally probable and instead ⟨q2⟩=1/3. This computable change in similarity statistics is a finite-model fact, not evidence for replica symmetry breaking.

Replica symmetry breaking

Words: 96
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
Giorgio Parisi's 1979 work replaced a single replica order parameter by an infinite hierarchy in the mean-field theory of spin glasses. The claim concerns a thermodynamic model, rather than inequivalence of the labels attached to two finite laboratory copies.[ref]
For equilibrium states with weights wα​ and local means miα​, define Qαγ​=N−1∑i​miα​miγ​. The distribution PJ​(q)=∑α,γ​wα​wγ​δ(q−Qαγ​) records their similarities. This state-based overlap distribution is distinct from the finite instantaneous spin overlap used in the triangle example.[ref]

Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model (Jij​=J0​/N+Jzij​/N​)

Words: 64
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
The Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model couples every distinct pair of Ising spins using independent standard normal random variables zij​ for i<j, with Jji​=Jij​. Here J0​ and J have units of energy. David Sherrington and Scott Kirkpatrick introduced this scaling in 1975; their original replica-symmetric calculation gave an unphysical negative low-temperature entropy. The model and that initial proposed solution must be distinguished.[ref]

Gold-iron alloy susceptibility experiment (1972)

Words: 113
Spin glass OurBigBook.com
V. Cannella and J. A. Mydosh measured low-field magnetic susceptibility of gold-iron alloys using a mutual inductance bridge. Moving samples into and out of the pickup arrangement separated their signal from the background. Cooling revealed sharp susceptibility cusps; changing concentration, sample shape and applied magnetic field tested their interpretation. Larger fields rounded the peaks. Their 1972 paper interpreted low-concentration ordering in antiferromagnetic terms: the cusp alone did not demonstrate Parisi's later theory.[ref]
This experiment measures a response to a small applied perturbation, rather than directly photographing frozen spins. Comparing field strengths is essential because the probing magnetic field itself changes the response.[ref]

Annealed disorder

Words: 52
Spin model OurBigBook.com
Annealed disorder allows the variables representing disorder to equilibrate along with the spins. With a specified normalized prior measure over couplings, its partition function is the average EJ​ZJ​. This differs from preparing samples with fixed couplings and averaging their measured thermodynamics, as in quenched disorder.[ref]

Quenched disorder is randomness held fixed while the other degrees of freedom equilibrate. For a sample with couplings J, first compute its partition function ZJ​ and Helmholtz free energy −kB​TlogZJ​, then average over samples. One must not silently replace EJ​logZJ​ by logEJ​ZJ​.[ref]

Two-spin quenched and annealed free energies (Fa​≤Fq​)

Words: 179
Quenched disorder OurBigBook.com
Choose two Ising spins with Hamiltonian H=−Js1​s2​, where J independently takes J0​ or 2J0​ with equal prior probabilities and J0​>0. This original finite example has no geometrical frustration and no phase transition. Its four spin assignments give ZJ​=4cosh(βJ). Therefore
Fq​=−2kB​T​{log[4cosh(βJ0​)]+log[4cosh(2βJ0​)]},Fa​=−kB​Tlog{2[cosh(βJ0​)+cosh(2βJ0​)]}.
(3)
The concave function log puts Fa​≤Fq​. For this two-value case the claim is just (Z1​+Z2​)/2≥Z1​Z2​​, obtained by squaring Z1​​−Z2​​. Thus no formal continuation of replicas is needed to see the distinction.
In the annealed disorder ensemble, the stronger bond has probability Z2J0​​/(ZJ0​​+Z2J0​​), approaching one at low temperature. A quenched disorder collection instead retains one half at each coupling. As T→0, Fq​→−3J0​/2 and Fa​→−2J0​: averaging samples and allowing the material variables to equilibrate describe different physics.

The classical Blume–Emery–Griffiths model uses spins σi​∈{−1,0,1} with both bilinear σi​σj​ and biquadratic σi2​σj2​ nearest-neighbour interactions, together with a single-site term proportional to σi2​. Classical Ising chain as a quantum particle (HQ​=−2Jsinhκ​dm2d2​+U(m))

Words: 281 Articles: 2
Transfer matrix for a classical spin chain OurBigBook.com
Hubbard-Stratonovich decoupling of an exponentially coupled Ising chain gives a scalar-field action with a nearest-neighbor gradient term and U(m)=tanh(κ/2)m2/J−log[2cosh(2m+h)]. Its continuum partition function is the Euclidean path integral of the displayed quantum particle. At zero field the potential can have two wells, but a finite barrier still permits quantum tunnelling and gives a unique symmetric ground state. The excitation gap produces a finite classical correlation length, so there is no finite-temperature spontaneous magnetization for fixed positive κ. One classical spatial coordinate becomes imaginary time; a d-dimensional classical model corresponds to a quantum model with d−1 spatial dimensions.

Auxiliary-field transfer operator for an Ising chain (T(m′,m)=exp[−D(m′−m)2−{U(m′)+U(m)}/2])

For a nearest-neighbor auxiliary-field representation of an Ising model, sharing each local potential between adjacent bonds gives the displayed symmetric integral operator. Its periodic partition function is the trace of its Nth power, up to Gaussian normalization constants. In the slowly varying continuum limit, a slice of length ϵ has kernel [M/(2πϵ)]1/2exp[−M(m′−m)2/(2ϵ)−ϵ{U(m′)+U(m)}/2]. Expanding its action on a smooth function gives 1−ϵH+O(ϵ2), with H=−(2M)−1∂m2​+U. Thus fixed endpoints give an imaginary-time transition kernel and periodic endpoints give a Euclidean path integral trace. Double-well threshold of an auxiliary Ising potential (J>21​tanh(κ/2))

Words: 97
Classical Ising chain as a quantum particle OurBigBook.com
The zero-field Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation of an exponentially coupled Ising model produces U(m)=Am2−log[2cosh(2m)], where A=tanh(κ/2)/J>0. Since U′′(0)=2A−4 and U tends to positive infinity, a symmetric double well exists exactly when A<2. Its nonzero stationary points solve Am=tanh(2m); the ratio tanh(2m)/m decreases from 2 to 0 for m>0, proving that there is exactly one positive minimum. At A=2 the minimum is a single quartic minimum. A small conjugate field adds −htanh(2m)+O(h2) and tilts the wells, while a sufficiently large field can remove the metastable well. A finite barrier permits quantum tunnelling; two classical minima alone do not establish spontaneous symmetry breaking.

Spin inversion symmetry

Words: 49
Spin model OurBigBook.com
Spin inversion symmetry means that simultaneous reversal of every spin leaves the probability distribution or energy unchanged. For a finite spin model with this symmetry, the weights of opposite configurations pair up, so every observable that changes sign under the reversal has zero expected value. An applied magnetic field generally breaks this symmetry.

Ising model

Words: 3k Articles: 40
Spin model OurBigBook.com
The Ising model is a spin model with an Ising spin σv​∈{−1,1} at every graph vertex v. For a finite graph with vertex set V and edge set E, a uniform coupling J and field parameter h give the Hamiltonian
H(σ)=−J∑{v,w}∈E​σv​σw​−h∑v∈V​σv​,β=kB​T1​.
(4)
Each edge is counted once. Here T>0 is temperature, kB​ is the Boltzmann constant, and β is inverse temperature; J and h have units of energy. For magnetic applications, h includes the magnetic moment multiplying the applied magnetic field. The canonical ensemble assigns the Boltzmann distribution
P(σ)=Ze−βH(σ)​,Z=∑τ∈{−1,1}V​e−βH(τ).
(5)
The partition function Z makes the probabilities sum to one. Positive J rewards equal neighboring Ising spins, modeling ferromagnetism; negative J rewards opposite neighbors, giving an antiferromagnetic Ising model. Positive h rewards +1, while negative h rewards −1.[ref]
A single local rule can organize a whole graph: rewarding agreement on each edge makes all-equal Ising spin patterns favorable even though no edge directly couples distant vertices. The four-spin Ising ring makes this visible with only sixteen microstates. It also separates three different questions: whether neighbors agree, whether the mean spin magnetization is nonzero, and how large its thermal fluctuations are.
A motivating experiment is the University of Colorado Boulder's Curie-temperature demonstration. An iron rod suspended from a twisting wire on a stand is initially held near a permanent magnet by ferromagnetism. A gas burner supplied from a propane cylinder heats the rod; the readout is its motion away from the magnet, followed by return as it cools. Repeated cooling supplies a reversible comparison on the same sample. The source explicitly calls for heating the rod while keeping the magnet cool, since heating the magnet can reduce its strength. The inference is that warming the sample weakens its magnetic attraction across the Curie temperature. This is a qualitative force readout, with no calibrated temperature or magnetization trace supplied: it motivates competition between alignment and thermal fluctuations, but does not identify J, measure individual Ising spins, or realize a four-spin Ising ring.[ref]
Ernst Ising's 1925 paper examined a linear chain of two-orientation magnetic elements with neighboring interactions. In its calculation for a large chain, the zero-field mean magnetization vanishes and the model has no spontaneous magnetization. The inspected English translation states this immediately after equation (8) in §2. This historical result concerns the chain; it does not establish the behavior of a two-dimensional Ising model.[ref]

For J>0 and Siz​=±1/2, a parallel bond has energy −J/2 while an antiparallel bond has +J/2. A wall therefore costs J, rather than the 2J of a differently normalized −J∑si​si+1​ model. Ising ring bond correlation (c1​=⟨σi​σi+1​⟩)

Words: 165

The Ising ring bond correlation is the neighboring two-point correlation function c1​=⟨σi​σi+1​⟩ in the four-spin Ising ring. All four edges are equivalent. At zero field each mean Ising spin is zero by spin inversion symmetry, so c1​ also equals the connected correlation function. Agreement gives bond product +1 and disagreement −1; thus P(σi​=σi+1​)=(1+c1​)/2.
Differentiating the finite partition function with respect to K=βJ inserts the sum of four bond products. Therefore
c1​=41​dKdlogZ​=cosh(4K)+3sinh(4K)​,U=⟨H⟩=−4Jc1​.
(8)
Here U is internal energy. At K=(ln2)/4, the class-weight calculation gives c1​=(4−1)/17=3/17 and U=−12J/17. The agreement probability for a selected bond is 10/17, despite the exactly zero mean spin magnetization.
A second derivative gives the energy variance, and hence the heat capacity CH​=dU/dT at fixed J and h=0:
J2Var(H)​=dK2d2logZ​=[cosh(4K)+3]216[1+3cosh(4K)]​,kB​CH​​=K2J2Var(H)​.
(9)
To see the variance identity directly, a derivative of an expected value under weights eKS inserts S−⟨S⟩, where S=∑i​σi​σi+1​=−H/J. The factor K2 follows from dK/dT=−K/T. At the same exact example, Var(H)/J2=1216/289. The heat capacity vanishes both as K→0 and as K→∞: at high temperature its dimensionless factor K2 vanishes, while at low temperature excited microstates become exponentially rare.

Ising ring magnetization fluctuations (Var(m)=⟨m2⟩,m=41​∑i​σi​)

Words: 297
Four-spin Ising ring OurBigBook.com
Define the instantaneous spin magnetization m=41​∑i​σi​. Global reversal sends m to −m without changing the zero-field energy. Pairing every microstate with its reversed partner proves ⟨m⟩=0 at every positive temperature, even when almost all probability lies on the two fully aligned patterns. A vanishing mean does not imply weak alignment: the limiting probability distribution is half at m=+1 and half at m=−1.
The Ising ring magnetization fluctuations measure this distinction through variance. In the d=0 class, both patterns have m2=1. Among the d=2 patterns, eight have m2=1/4 and four have m2=0; the two d=4 patterns also have m2=0. Consequently
Var(m)=⟨m2⟩=Z2e4K+2​.
(10)
It equals 6/17 at K=(ln2)/4, approaches 1/4 for four independent fair Ising spins at K→0, and approaches 1 as K→∞. Unlike the energy variance, the spin magnetization variance remains large in the low-temperature symmetric mixture.
The same thermal fluctuation controls the field response. Allow h to vary before setting it to zero; differentiating the Boltzmann distribution inserts β∑i​σi​=4βm, giving
∂h∂⟨m⟩​​h=0​=4βVar(m).
(11)
This is a spin-normalized magnetic susceptibility with respect to the energy-valued parameter h; conversion to physical magnetization and magnetic field requires the magnetic moment and sample volume factors.
For finite N, Z is a finite positive sum of exponential functions. Its free energy −kB​TlogZ is a real analytic function for T>0, so the smooth heat capacity peak here is not a critical phase transition. Spontaneous magnetization concerns the order of limits: first a thermodynamic limit, then removal of a symmetry-breaking field. The four-spin Ising ring alone establishes neither that limit nor any two-dimensional Ising model result.[ref]

Open-chain endpoint corrections for an exponential Ising kernel (mTJN−1​m=D∑j=1N−1​(mj+1​−mj​)2+A∑j=1N​mj2​+B(m12​+mN2​))

Exponentially coupled Ising inverse kernel OurBigBook.com
For N≥2, the open-chain Ising model coupling [JN​]ij​=Jρ∣i−j∣, 0<ρ<1, has a tridiagonal inverse. Its endpoint diagonal entries are 1/[J(1−ρ2)], its interior diagonal entries are (1+ρ2)/[J(1−ρ2)], and adjacent off-diagonal entries are −ρ/[J(1−ρ2)]. Direct matrix multiplication verifies the inverse because the geometric sequences cancel in every interior row. The displayed quadratic form follows with D=ρ/[J(1−ρ2)], A=(1−ρ)/[J(1+ρ)] and B=ρ/[J(1+ρ)]. For ρ=e−κ, D=(2Jsinhκ)−1 and A=tanh(κ/2)/J. Omitting the endpoint term requires an infinite chain, a correctly periodized coupling, or a bulk thermodynamic limit.

Ising boundary condition

Words: 133 Articles: 1
Ising model OurBigBook.com
A fixed Ising boundary condition pins the Ising spins on a specified boundary, leaving the interior distributed with the original interaction weights. The boundary can be the inner graph vertex boundary of a finite box, or an exterior neighboring set if that convention is explicitly chosen. Conditioning on pinned Ising spins preserves the FKG lattice condition of a ferromagnetic Ising model. The FKG inequality then makes interior expectations of increasing observables nondecreasing in the pinned boundary Ising spins.

Extremal infinite-volume Ising measures (μ0,μ1)

Words: 73
Ising boundary condition OurBigBook.com
For the ferromagnetic binary Ising model, all-zero boundary measures increase and all-one boundary measures decrease as boxes exhaust the lattice, in stochastic domination. The nearest-neighbor spatial conditional probability distribution reduces each domain comparison to a mixture of boundary conditions for the smaller box. Probabilities that a specified finite set is all one consequently converge; inclusion-exclusion gives convergence of every finite Ising spin pattern. Their consistent limits define the two extreme infinite-volume measures. Existence does not assert that the two limits coincide.

Antiferromagnetic Ising model (J>0)

Analyticity of the one-dimensional Ising free energy

Words: 69
Transfer matrix for the one-dimensional Ising model OurBigBook.com
At real field and nonzero temperature, every entry of the finite-range Ising transfer matrix is positive. Its largest eigenvalue is simple by the Perron–Frobenius theorem and depends smoothly on field and temperature. The thermodynamic free energy per site is its negative logarithm times kB​T. In the nearest-neighbour chain it is analytic and the zero-field magnetization vanishes, despite exponentially long low-temperature correlations. Finite-volume spin-inversion symmetry alone would not suffice to exclude spontaneous thermodynamic order.

Finite-ring parity qualification of an Ising quasiparticle gap
Words: 78

A bulk quasiparticle energy is not automatically the first excitation gap of a finite periodic spin chain. The Jordan–Wigner transformation fixes a boundary condition through fermion parity and restricts occupations to that same parity. At zero transverse field an Ising domain wall costs 2J, but periodic spin configurations contain an even number of walls; the first wall-pair excitation costs 4J. An ordered finite chain also has a distinct small symmetry-sector splitting at nonzero field. The thermodynamic dispersion and these finite-system scales answer different questions.
