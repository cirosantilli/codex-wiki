Dirichlet hyperbola method

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Dirichlet convolution
OurBigBook.com Words: 896 Articles: 5
The Dirichlet hyperbola method turns a long summation over a Dirichlet convolution into two short ones by covering the lattice points below a hyperbola with two overlapping strips. Its simplest application is an exact count: for a positive integer N, every positive integer divisor a of n≤N corresponds to the ordered pair (a,n/a). Thus the divisor summatory function counts precisely the ordered pairs of positive integers with ab≤N:
D(N)=∑n=1N​τ(n)=#{(a,b)∈Z>02​:ab≤N}=∑a=1N​⌊aN​⌋.
(64)
Here τ is the divisor function, and the floor function gives the number of allowed values of b for each a. The axes are excluded, while points on ab=N are included.
Set r=⌊N​⌋, using the floor function and the positive square root. Every counted pair has a≤r or b≤r. Otherwise ab≥(r+1)2>N. The first strip contains ∑a=1r​⌊N/a⌋ points; swapping the two coordinates gives the same count for the second. Their overlap contains all r2 pairs with 1≤a,b≤r, since r2≤N. The inclusion-exclusion principle therefore gives the exact identity
D(N)=2a=1∑r​⌊aN​⌋−r2,r=⌊N​⌋.​
(65)
The subtraction is for the whole overlap square, rather than only the diagonal. The same Dirichlet hyperbola method identity holds for real x≥1, with D(x)=∑n≤x​τ(n) and r=⌊x​⌋. [ref]
