Coronagraph

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 7
A coronagraph is an optical instrument that suppresses a bright source's light before detection while transmitting some light from nearby directions. Its purpose is to reveal faint structure buried in the bright source's point spread function, as in exoplanet direct imaging. A Lyot coronagraph combines a coronagraphic focal plane mask with a Lyot stop in a reimaged optical pupil; blocking the central image alone does not remove its diffraction rings. [ref].
The original motivation was the solar corona: hiding the bright photosphere would allow longer, repeated observations of faint surrounding light. In his 1930 paper, Lyot explained that short total eclipses restricted observing time and prevented some long exposures for spectral lines. The attraction is an instrument that makes the faint neighborhood accessible on demand. [ref].
