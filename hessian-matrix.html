Hessian matrix (Hessf)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
The Hessian matrix describes how the slope of a function changes in different directions. For a real-valued function f with continuous second partial derivatives on an open subset of Rn, it is
Hf​(x)=(∂xi​∂xj​∂2f​(x))i,j=1n​.
(17)
The symmetry of second derivatives makes Hf​ a symmetric matrix under these hypotheses. It is also the Jacobian matrix of the gradient: near x, a small displacement h changes ∇f by Hf​(x)h+o(∥h∥). Thus the gradient measures the current slope, while the Hessian measures its local variation. [ref]
For example, take the quadratic form
q(x,y)=x2+2xy+3y2.
(18)
Its gradient and Hessian matrix are
∇q=(2x+2y,2x+6y)T,Hq​=(22​26​).
(19)
The only critical point is the origin. Completing the square gives q=(x+y)2+2y2, so the origin is a strict global minimum. The eigenvalues 4−22​ and 4+22​ are both positive, recovering the same conclusion from the positive definiteness of the Hessian. Its eigenvectors identify the rotated directions in which the quadratic form has its smallest and largest bending.
For a fixed vector v, restriction to a straight line gives the directional interpretation
dt2d2​f(x+tv)​t=0​=vTHf​(x)v.
(20)
This is the quadratic form associated with the Hessian matrix. For a unit eigenvector v, it equals the corresponding eigenvalue. Opposite signs therefore mean that some directions bend upwards and others downwards. The second-derivative test turns this information into a classification when the gradient vanishes.
The same object is useful for computation. Newton's method in optimization chooses a displacement h by solving Hf​(x)h=−∇f(x) when the Hessian matrix is an invertible matrix; this makes the gradient of the local quadratic model vanish. For the displayed q, the equation gives h=−(x,y)T, so one exact step reaches the minimum from any starting point. For a general function, a Hessian with opposite-sign eigenvalues or a large condition number need not give a useful minimizing step, and the local minimum need not be a global minimum.
The name honors Otto Hesse. [ref] An older geometric use is the Hessian criterion for flexes of a plane cubic, connecting a determinant of second partial derivatives with inflection points of an algebraic curve. Hesse's 1844 paper Über die Wendepuncte der Curven dritter Ordnung is an early source on that geometric problem. [ref]
