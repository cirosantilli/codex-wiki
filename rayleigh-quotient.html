Rayleigh quotient (RA​(x)=x∗xx∗Ax​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 920 Articles: 12
The Rayleigh quotient measures the quadratic form of a self-adjoint operator per unit squared norm. For a nonzero vector x and a finite-dimensional Hermitian matrix A,
RA​(x)=x∗xx∗Ax​.
(72)
For a real symmetric matrix, replace the conjugate transpose x∗ by xT. More generally, for a self-adjoint operator H on a Hilbert space, use RH​(ψ)=⟨ψ,Hψ⟩/⟨ψ,ψ⟩ for nonzero ψ in its operator domain. On an eigenvector it returns the exact eigenvalue. Multiplying the vector by any nonzero scalar leaves the quotient unchanged, so it measures a direction rather than a chosen length.
For the symmetric matrix A=(21​12​) from the finite-dimensional spectral theorem example,
RA​(1,0)=2,RA​(1,1)=3,RA​(−1,1)=1.
(73)
The first value is not an eigenvalue: it is an average of the two directional responses. To see every direction, write the unit vector q(θ)=cosθe+​+sinθe−​ in the orthonormal eigenbasis. Then
RA​(q(θ))=3cos2θ+sin2θ=1+2cos2θ.
(74)
The largest value is 3 along e+​; the smallest is 1 along e−​. A direction halfway between them has quotient 2. In the left panel, the color on the unit circle represents the quotient; in the right panel, the same values are plotted against the angle measured from e+​.
The Hermitian Rayleigh quotient range explains why this is useful: evaluating one trial vector gives an upper bound on the smallest eigenvalue and a lower bound on the largest one, without solving a characteristic polynomial. At an extreme eigenvector, a small angular error changes the quotient only quadratically. Near e+​ in this example, 3−RA​(q(θ))=2sin2θ=2θ2+O(θ4). This is the geometric reason for quadratic Rayleigh-quotient improvement for the power method and motivates the Rayleigh-Ritz method.
There is a concrete historical link with normal modes. In The Theory of Sound (1877), Volume I, Sections 88–89, Lord Rayleigh studies the period of a constrained trial vibration. He shows that the period has a stationary point at a normal mode and that a trial type bounds the longest natural period. These energy ratios are a mechanical setting for the modern Rayleigh quotient and generalized Rayleigh quotient; the book describes the result through vibration periods, rather than today's matrix notation.[ref]
