Optical coherence

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 24
Optical coherence describes correlations between the wave amplitudes and wave phases of light sampled at different places or times. Its most direct classical test is wave interference: combine two samples of a field and see how much the detected light intensity changes when their relative wave phase is scanned. First-order optical coherence makes this statement quantitative; temporal optical coherence compares delayed samples, while spatial optical coherence compares different positions. These properties are relative to the samples and averaging procedure, rather than a single yes-or-no label for a source. [ref].
A useful surprise is that two copies of a fluctuating source can interfere strongly when they have matched delays: the copies share the fluctuations. Conversely, two individually steady-looking beams need not have correlated wave phases. The ideal diffraction grating calculation requires spatial optical coherence across its illuminated elements; unequal-arm measurements with a Michelson interferometer test temporal optical coherence.
