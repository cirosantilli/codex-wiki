In question 1, the repeated species index in the <Yukawa interaction> is summed over $i=1,2$. The spatial <mode expansion of a free field>[mode expansions] in question 2 are at a fixed time.

== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

For <four-momentum> $k$ flowing along the <fermion flow>, the <momentum space> <Feynman rules> are
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\text{scalar line: }&\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i0},\\
\text{fermion line of species }i\text{ to }j:\quad&\delta_{ij}\frac{i(\not k+m)}{k^2-m^2+i0},\\
\phi\bar\psi_i\psi_j\text{ vertex: }&-ig\delta_{ij}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The <scalar propagator> and <Dirac propagator> invert their respective quadratic kernels. The <interaction vertex> follows by expanding $\exp(i\int\mathcal L_{\rm int})$, fixing its sign. Species is conserved along each open <fermion> line; there is no vertex changing species 1 into species 2.

An <interaction vertex> carries $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\sum k)$ with inward <four-momenta>. Impose <momentum conservation> and integrate each independent <loop momentum> with $d^4k/(2\pi)^4$. Include the <closed fermion loop sign> $-1$ for every <fermion loop>, the relevant <fermionic signs> when rearranging external states, and the <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>. External incoming and outgoing <fermions> carry $u_s(p)$ and $\bar u_s(p)$; incoming and outgoing <antifermions> carry $\bar v_s(p)$ and $v_s(p)$. An external <real scalar field> leg has factor 1 in an amputated <scattering amplitude>. The <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> specifies the poles.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Label the initial species-1 and species-2 <fermions> by $(p_1,s_1)$ and $(p_2,s_2)$, and the final species-1 and species-2 <fermions> by $(p_3,s_3)$ and $(p_4,s_4)$. There is exactly one <tree-level Feynman diagram>: the $t$-channel <scalar propagator> joins the continuous species-1 line $1\to3$ to the continuous species-2 line $2\to4$. Its exchanged <four-momentum> $q_t$ determines $t$, in the notation of <Mandelstam variables>:
$$
\boxed{q_t=p_1-p_3=p_4-p_2,\qquad t=q_t^2.}
$$
The top row of the figure displays this <Feynman diagram>. A crossed attachment $1\to4$ would change species at an <interaction vertex> and is forbidden. There is no initial <antifermion> for an annihilation channel.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

With all four external <fermions> of species 1, both final attachments are possible. The middle row of the figure gives the two <tree-level Feynman diagrams>:

* The $t$ channel has lines $1\to3$ and $2\to4$, joined by a <scalar propagator> with $q_t=p_1-p_3=p_4-p_2$.
* The $u$ channel has lines $1\to4$ and $2\to3$, joined by a <scalar propagator> with $q_u=p_1-p_4=p_3-p_2$.

For a fixed ordering of external <fermion> states, the <fermionic sign> from exchanging the final identical <fermions> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M=-g^2\left[
\frac{(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar u_4u_2)}{t-m^2+i0}
-\frac{(\bar u_4u_1)(\bar u_3u_2)}{u-m^2+i0}
\right],\qquad u=q_u^2.}
$$
Here $u_j=u_{s_j}(p_j)$ are <Dirac spinors>. In particular, the <scattering amplitude> changes sign when the complete final labels $3$ and $4$ are exchanged, as required for identical <fermions>.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

Let $p_1,p_3$ label the incoming and outgoing <fermion>, and $p_2,p_4$ the incoming and outgoing <antifermion>. The bottom row of the figure gives the two <tree-level Feynman diagrams>:

* The $t$ channel has an open <fermion> line $1\to3$ and an open <antifermion> line with <fermion flow> $4\to2$. The <scalar propagator> carries $q_t=p_1-p_3=p_4-p_2$.
* The $s$ channel has initial <particle-antiparticle annihilation> into a <scalar propagator> with $q_s=p_1+p_2=p_3+p_4$, followed by creation of the final pair.

Thus the <Mandelstam variables> in the two denominators are
$$
\boxed{t=(p_1-p_3)^2,\qquad s=(p_1+p_2)^2.}
$$
The two <scattering amplitude> terms have a relative <fermionic sign> of minus when written with spinor products $(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar v_2v_4)$ and $(\bar v_2u_1)(\bar u_3v_4)$. The overall phase depends on the chosen ordering of the external states. No $u$ channel joins an incoming <fermion> to an outgoing <antifermion> through a species-preserving <Yukawa interaction>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Define $\mathcal M$ by removing the factor $i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_1+p_2-p_3-p_4)$ from the connected transition <matrix element>. The single <tree-level Feynman diagram> in <1/b/i> gives
$$
i\mathcal M=(-ig)^2\frac{i}{q^2-m^2+i0}
[\bar u_{s_3}(p_3)u_{s_1}(p_1)]
[\bar u_{s_4}(p_4)u_{s_2}(p_2)],
\qquad q=p_1-p_3.
$$
There is no identical-particle exchange term because the species differ. Each incoming <fermion> has two equally populated <spin angular momentum> states in an unpolarized beam. Their probabilities multiply to $1/4$, giving a <spin average> over the four initial choices. The unobserved final <spin angular momentum> states are distinct outcomes, so their probabilities are added by a <spin sum>, with no final averaging:
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac14\sum_{s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4}|\mathcal M|^2.
$$
Taking the complex conjugate of $\bar u_3u_1$ gives $\bar u_1u_3$. The <fermion spin sum> therefore converts each squared bilinear into a <gamma-matrix trace>:
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
=\frac{g^4}{4(q^2-m^2)^2}
\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_3+m)(\not p_1+m)]
\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_4+m)(\not p_2+m)].
$$
Using $\operatorname{tr}1=4$, $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu=0$, $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=4\eta^{\mu\nu}$, and the <mass shell> conditions $p_j^2=m^2$, each <gamma-matrix trace> is
$$
4(p_1\cdot p_3+m^2)=4\left(2m^2-\frac{q^2}{2}\right)
=2(4m^2-q^2).
$$
The second equality follows from $q^2=2m^2-2p_1\cdot p_3$; <momentum conservation> gives the identical result for $p_2,p_4$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
=g^4\left(\frac{4m^2-q^2}{m^2-q^2}\right)^2.}
$$
**The printed prefactor $g^2$ is incorrect for the stated coupling.** Each of the two <Yukawa interaction> vertices contributes one $g$ to the <scattering amplitude>, so its square is proportional to $g^4$. As a concrete counterexample, take $g=2$, $m>0$, and forward elastic kinematics $p_3=p_1$, $p_4=p_2$. Then $q^2=0$ and the <spin average> is $16g^4=256$, whereas the printed expression gives $16g^2=64$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a connected amputated <Feynman diagram>, write $L$ for the number of independent <loop momenta>. Each <loop momentum> integration scales as $\lambda^4$, each internal <scalar propagator> as $\lambda^{-2}$, and each internal <Dirac propagator> as $\lambda^{-1}$ when both <four-momenta> and <mass> are scaled. The <Yukawa coupling> has <mass dimension> zero. Thus
$$
d_S=4L-n_f-2n_s.
$$
Counting the two <fermion> ends and one scalar end at each <interaction vertex>, and using connectedness, gives
$$
2n_f=2n_v,\qquad 2n_s+n=n_v,\qquad
L=n_f+n_s-n_v+1.
$$
Consequently $n_f=n_v$, $L=n_s+1$, and
$$
\boxed{d_S=4+2n_s-n_v=4-n.}
$$
**The requested exponent requires amputation and removal of the overall momentum delta function.** An ordinary unamputated connected <Green function> has an additional $n$ external <scalar propagators>, giving <mass dimension> $4-3n$ after stripping that delta function, or $-3n$ if it is retained. For a concrete check, the free two-point <scalar propagator> scales as $\lambda^{-2}$, not $\lambda^2$. The exponent $4-n$ is the engineering result for the amputated object. A fixed <renormalization scale> introduces logarithms; the displayed homogeneity is a naive <power counting> statement rather than an exact quantum scaling law at fixed subtraction scale.

More generally, with $E_s$ external scalar ends and $E_f$ external <fermion> ends, the same counting gives the <superficial degree of divergence>
$$
\boxed{D=4-E_s-\frac32E_f.}
$$
This is an upper bound on the overall <ultraviolet divergence>. Numerator cancellations and <Lorentz invariance> may lower it; an apparently convergent whole <Feynman diagram> can still have <ultraviolet subdivergences>. Local subtraction of those <ultraviolet subdivergences> is required first.

For pure scalar ends, $E_s=0,1,2,3,4$ can require <counterterms>. <Lorentz invariance> reduces the two-point terms to a <mass counterterm> and <wave-function renormalization>; derivatives of a single scalar field are total derivatives, and a one-derivative cubic scalar term cannot be a <Lorentz scalar>. With two external <fermions>, $E_s=0$ requires their <kinetic term> and <mass counterterm>, while $E_s=1$ requires <Yukawa coupling> renormalization. Four external <fermions> give $D=-2$, so no independent four-<fermion> <counterterm> is required.

A complete <counterterm Lagrangian> consistent with the species symmetry can therefore be written
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\rm ct}={}&\frac{\delta Z_\phi}{2}(\partial\phi)^2
-\frac{\delta m_s^2}{2}\phi^2-\delta t\,\phi
-\frac{\delta\kappa}{3!}\phi^3-\frac{\delta\lambda_4}{4!}\phi^4-\delta\mathcal E\\
&+\sum_{i=1}^2\bar\psi_i(\delta Z_\psi i\not\partial-\delta m_f)\psi_i
-\delta g\,\phi\sum_{i=1}^2\bar\psi_i\psi_i.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The <vacuum energy density> term matters for an unnormalized <vacuum functional> and cancels from normalized <Green functions>. The <tadpole counterterm> can impose $\langle\phi\rangle=0$. There is no $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ symmetry of the massive <Yukawa interaction>, so linear, cubic and quartic scalar terms cannot be excluded. For instance, in a constant background the divergent <fermion loop> contribution contains $(m+g\phi)^4$, exhibiting all these powers. This is <counterterm closure of a massive Yukawa theory>.

The two species have identical couplings, so their common unitary flavour rotations preserve the <Lagrangian density> and their <counterterms> can be chosen species independent. Equality of the scalar and <fermion> masses, however, is not protected: $\delta m_s^2$ and $\delta m_f$ are independent. Including the displayed finite set of operators yields a <renormalizable quantum field theory>. Restricting the bare <Lagrangian density> to the terms printed in the question is not closed under <renormalization>.

=== a

==== Solution

Write $\int_{\mathbf p}=\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3$ and $d_{\mathbf p}=a_{\mathbf p}-b_{\mathbf p}$. The nonzero <canonical commutation relations> are
$$
[a_{\mathbf p},a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]
=[b_{\mathbf p},b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q),
$$
with cross-species brackets zero. The <commutator product rule> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[Q,a_{\mathbf q}]&=\int_{\mathbf p}d_{\mathbf p}[d_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,a_{\mathbf q}]=-d_{\mathbf q},\\
[Q,b_{\mathbf q}]&=\int_{\mathbf p}d_{\mathbf p}[d_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,b_{\mathbf q}]=+d_{\mathbf q},\\
[Q,a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]&=\int_{\mathbf p}[d_{\mathbf p},a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]d_{\mathbf p}^\dagger=d_{\mathbf q}^\dagger,\\
[Q,b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]&=\int_{\mathbf p}[d_{\mathbf p},b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]d_{\mathbf p}^\dagger=-d_{\mathbf q}^\dagger.
\end{aligned}
$$
Linearity of the <commutator> now yields, for the real coefficient $s$,
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
[Q,a_{\mathbf q}+sb_{\mathbf q}]&=-(1-s)(a_{\mathbf q}-b_{\mathbf q}),\\
[Q,a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger+sb_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]&=(1-s)(a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger-b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger).
\end{aligned}}
$$
Strictly, the printed $Q$ contains an infinite constant in continuum <Fock space>. Work first with finitely many modes, or replace it by its <normal ordering> $:Q:=\int_{\mathbf p}d_{\mathbf p}^\dagger d_{\mathbf p}$. Their difference is a constant, so every displayed <commutator> and every conjugation below is unchanged. The latter convention fixes the <vacuum state> phase without an infinite phase ambiguity.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For a <Hermitian operator> $Q$, the <operator exponential> $C_\lambda=e^{i\lambda Q}$ is a <unitary operator>, with $C_\lambda^\dagger=e^{-i\lambda Q}$. Differentiation and $[Q,C_\lambda]=0$ give
$$
\frac{d}{d\lambda}(C_\lambda A C_\lambda^\dagger)
=iQC_\lambda A C_\lambda^\dagger-iC_\lambda A C_\lambda^\dagger Q
=iC_\lambda[Q,A]C_\lambda^\dagger.
$$
When $[Q,A]=cA$, this is the first-order <ordinary differential equation> $A'(\lambda)=icA(\lambda)$ with $A(0)=A$. Multiplication by $e^{-ic\lambda}$ makes its derivative zero, proving
$$
\boxed{A(\lambda)=e^{ic\lambda}A.}
$$
Equivalently, the <commutator expansion for exponential conjugation> has successive nested <commutators> $c^r A$, whose <exponential series> sums to this expression.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the combinations $a_\pm=a\pm b$ at each <momentum>. Part <2/a> gives $[Q,a_+]=0$, $[Q,a_-]=-2a_-$, while their <creation operators> obey $[Q,a_+^\dagger]=0$, $[Q,a_-^\dagger]=2a_-^\dagger$. Applying <2/b> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
C_\lambda a C_\lambda^\dagger
&=\frac{1+e^{-2i\lambda}}2a+\frac{1-e^{-2i\lambda}}2b,\\
C_\lambda b C_\lambda^\dagger
&=\frac{1-e^{-2i\lambda}}2a+\frac{1+e^{-2i\lambda}}2b.
\end{aligned}
$$
The <creation operators> transform by the <Hermitian adjoint> formulas with $e^{+2i\lambda}$. At $\lambda=\pi/2$, both exponentials are $-1$, so
$$
\boxed{Ca_{\mathbf p}C^\dagger=b_{\mathbf p},\quad
Cb_{\mathbf p}C^\dagger=a_{\mathbf p},\quad
Ca_{\mathbf p}^\dagger C^\dagger=b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,\quad
Cb_{\mathbf p}^\dagger C^\dagger=a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,\qquad C=C_{\pi/2}.}
$$
Substituting into the fixed-time <mode expansion of a free field> of the <complex scalar field> gives
$$
C\phi(\mathbf x)C^\dagger
=\int_{\mathbf p}\frac{a_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger+b_{\mathbf p}}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}
 e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}
=\phi^\dagger(\mathbf x).
$$
Thus $C$ exchanges particles and <antiparticles>, as required for <charge conjugation>. To see the charge reversal explicitly, the conserved charge $Q_{\rm em}=\int_{\mathbf p}(a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger b_{\mathbf p})$ obeys $CQ_{\rm em}C^\dagger=-Q_{\rm em}$. The generator $Q$ in the question is distinct from this charge.

The two occupation terms in the <Hamiltonian> are exchanged, with the same <relativistic energy> $E_{\mathbf p}$, and therefore
$$
\boxed{CHC^\dagger=H.}
$$
In fact $[Q,H]=0$ because its mixing terms exchange equal-<energy> modes. With <normal ordering>, $:Q:=2\int_{\mathbf p}c_{-,\mathbf p}^\dagger c_{-,\mathbf p}$ for $c_-=(a-b)/\sqrt2$. Then $C=e^{i\pi N_-}$ fixes the <vacuum state> and satisfies $C^2=1$. The construction is a <bosonic species-swap generator>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Take <parity symmetry in quantum field theory> to preserve free time evolution, so the conjugate initial data reflect along with the field. Substitute the <mode expansion of a free field> into the defining <parity operator> relation and change the integration variable $\mathbf p\mapsto-\mathbf p$. Since $E_{-\mathbf p}=E_{\mathbf p}$, coefficient comparison, or comparison of both the field and its conjugate initial data, gives the intrinsic-even convention
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P a_{\mathbf p}\mathcal P^\dagger&=a_{-\mathbf p},&
\mathcal P b_{\mathbf p}\mathcal P^\dagger&=b_{-\mathbf p},\\
\mathcal P a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger\mathcal P^\dagger&=a_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger,&
\mathcal P b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger\mathcal P^\dagger&=b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Here <parity symmetry in quantum field theory> reverses spatial <momentum> and preserves time. The <creation operator> formulas follow by taking <Hermitian adjoints>. The given field relation has fixed the intrinsic phase to $+1$.

For one <real scalar field>, let $R$ act on one-particle <wave functions> by $(Rf)(\mathbf p)=f(-\mathbf p)$. It is a <Hermitian operator> with $R^2=1$, so $P_-=(1-R)/2$ is the <orthogonal projector> onto odd <wave functions>. Its <second quantization> is the odd-mode <number operator>
$$
N_{\rm odd}=\frac12\int_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger(a_{\mathbf p}-a_{-\mathbf p})
=\frac14\int_{\mathbf p}(a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger-a_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger)(a_{\mathbf p}-a_{-\mathbf p}).
$$
The full <momentum space> is integrated in both expressions; the factors prevent double counting the pairs $\{\mathbf p,-\mathbf p\}$. A suitable <parity operator> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal P=\exp(i\pi N_{\rm odd})
=\exp\left[\frac{i\pi}{2}\int_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger(a_{\mathbf p}-a_{-\mathbf p})\right].}
$$
For verification, $[N_{\rm odd},a_{\mathbf p}]=-(a_{\mathbf p}-a_{-\mathbf p})/2$. Thus the even combination $a_{\mathbf p}+a_{-\mathbf p}$ is unchanged, and the odd combination $a_{\mathbf p}-a_{-\mathbf p}$ changes sign under conjugation by $e^{i\pi N_{\rm odd}}$, exactly reproducing $a_{\mathbf p}\mapsto a_{-\mathbf p}$. The same holds for <creation operators>. The odd-mode <number operator> has integer <eigenvalues>, so $\mathcal P^2=1$, and $\mathcal P|0\rangle=|0\rangle$. Multiplying by an overall constant phase would not change its conjugation action. This is <parity as odd-mode number parity>.

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Keep $\lambda$ for the cubic coupling and $j$ for the external <source>. Expanding the interaction part of the <scalar field path integral> yields
$$
Z[\lambda;j]=\sum_{V=0}^\infty\frac1{V!}
\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{3!}\right)^V
\int\prod_{v=1}^V d^4y_v\int D\phi\,
\prod_{v=1}^V\phi(y_v)^3\,
 e^{iS_0[\phi]+i\int j\phi}.
$$
A <functional derivative> of the source exponential inserts $i\phi(y)$. In particular, $\delta_j(y)^3 e^{i\int j\phi}=-i\phi(y)^3e^{i\int j\phi}$, so $-i\lambda\phi(y)^3$ can be replaced by $\lambda\delta_j(y)^3$. The <functional derivatives> commute and can be taken outside the free <path integral>. Resumming the <exponential series> gives
$$
\boxed{Z[\lambda;j]=\exp\left[\frac\lambda{3!}\int d^4y\,
\frac{\delta^3}{\delta j(y)^3}\right]Z[0;j].}
$$
This is a formal <perturbation series>; a real cubic <scalar potential> is unbounded and the expression does not assert a stable nonperturbative <vacuum state>.

To evaluate the free <Gaussian path integral>, use <integration by parts> to write
$$
S_0[\phi]+\int j\phi=\frac12\int\phi K\phi+\int j\phi,
\qquad K=-\Box-m^2+i\epsilon.
$$
The inverse kernel is
$$
K^{-1}(x,y)=D(x-y)
=\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
<Completing the square> with $\phi=\chi-K^{-1}j$ changes the exponent to $i\int\chi K\chi/2-i\int jK^{-1}j/2$. The translation-invariant <functional measure> makes the remaining <Gaussian path integral> precisely $Z[0;0]$, hence
$$
\boxed{Z[0;j]=Z[0;0]\exp\left[-\frac i2\int d^4x\,d^4y
\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{j(x)j(y)e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)}}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}\right].}
$$
For $\epsilon>0$, the exponent includes $-\epsilon\int\phi^2/2$, which damps the regulated finite-dimensional integrals. The <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> displaces the positive-<energy> pole below the real $p^0$ axis and the negative-<energy> pole above it, selecting the time-ordered <vacuum expectation value>. Indeed the <Feynman propagator> is $\Delta_F=iD$. Thus $i\epsilon$ specifies both convergence and the <vacuum state> boundary conditions, with $\epsilon\to0^+$ taken after integration.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For the interacting <vacuum state> defined perturbatively by the same <Feynman i-epsilon prescription>, the normalized <time-ordered product> is
$$
\boxed{\left\langle T\prod_{r=1}^m\phi(x_r)\right\rangle
=\left.\frac{1}{Z[\lambda;0]}\frac1{i^m}
\frac{\delta^m Z[\lambda;j]}{\delta j(x_1)\cdots\delta j(x_m)}\right|_{j=0}.}
$$
Each <functional derivative> inserts $i\phi$, accounting for $i^{-m}$. The denominator implements <cancellation of vacuum bubbles>, while disconnected pieces carrying external insertions still contribute. The lower limit $i=i$ in the printed product is a typographical error; the product here runs from 1 to $m$.

At order $V$ in the cubic scalar <field interaction term>, the expansion of the <generating functional> supplies $1/[V!(3!)^V]$. The $V!$ counts permutations of indistinguishable integrated <interaction vertices>, and $3!$ counts permutations of the three field slots at each <interaction vertex>. Applying the <Wick theorem> pairs fields into <Feynman propagators>. Several pairings produce the same <Feynman diagram>; their number $N_G$ multiplies the expansion coefficient. For fixed labelled external points, the net combinatorial weight is
$$
\boxed{w_G=\frac{N_G}{V!(3!)^V}=\frac1{S_G}.}
$$
Here $S_G$ is the order of the graph's <automorphism group> when the external labels are fixed, with permutations of identical parallel lines and reversal of a self-loop included as applicable. This is the <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor> convention in which a <Feynman diagram> is divided by $S_G$. Each cubic <interaction vertex> then contributes $-i\lambda$, each line contributes $\Delta_F$, and each internal vertex is integrated over its position. External labelled points are not divided by $m!$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Hold the external endpoints fixed and distinguish the three <Feynman diagrams> by their left-to-right order in the source. Write $S$ for the divisor and $w=1/S$ for the multiplying combinatorial weight. Each line below includes external <Feynman propagators>; <amputation> leaves the same $w$.

**Left: two-point bubble.** There are two cubic <interaction vertices> and two parallel internal lines. Interchanging those lines preserves the labelled external endpoints, giving $S=2$. Direct <Wick contraction> counting gives two assignments of the external endpoints to the vertices, $3^2$ choices of their field slots, and $2!$ pairings of the remaining slots:
$$
N_{\rm left}=2\cdot3^2\cdot2=36,\qquad
\boxed{w_{\rm left}=\frac{36}{2!(3!)^2}=\frac12.}
$$
With $\Delta=\Delta_F$ and external points $x_1,x_2$, its contribution is
$$
\frac{(-i\lambda)^2}{2}\int d^4y\,d^4z\,
\Delta(x_1-y)\Delta(y-z)^2\Delta(z-x_2).
$$

**Middle: three-point triangle.** The three labelled external endpoints fix their three adjacent cubic <interaction vertices>, so no nonidentity <graph automorphism> remains. Counting gives $3!$ assignments of endpoints to vertices, $3^3$ choices of external field slots, and $2^3$ assignments of remaining slots to the two specified neighbours:
$$
N_{\rm middle}=3!\,3^3\,2^3=1296,\qquad
\boxed{w_{\rm middle}=\frac{1296}{3!(3!)^3}=1.}
$$
Its contribution is
$$
(-i\lambda)^3\int\prod_{r=1}^3 d^4y_r\,
\left[\prod_{r=1}^3\Delta(x_r-y_r)\right]
\Delta(y_1-y_2)\Delta(y_2-y_3)\Delta(y_3-y_1).
$$
The apparent rotational symmetry of an unlabelled triangle does not survive fixing three distinct external endpoints.

**Right: two-point graph with a chord.** There are four cubic <interaction vertices>: the left and right attachment vertices $A,B$ and the two chord ends $C,D$. Its internal edges are $AC,AD,BC,BD,CD$. Reflection interchanges $C,D$, fixes $A,B$, and preserves the two external labels. This is the only nontrivial <graph automorphism>, so $S=2$.

For a separate <Wick contraction> count, choose the ordered external attachment vertices in $4\cdot3$ ways and their field slots in $3^2$ ways. Assign their two remaining slots to $C,D$ in $2^2$ ways. At each of $C,D$, choose the slot joining $A$ and then the slot joining $B$ in $3\cdot2$ ways; the last slots join each other. Thus
$$
N_{\rm right}=12\cdot9\cdot4\cdot6^2=15552,\qquad
\boxed{w_{\rm right}=\frac{15552}{4!(3!)^4}=\frac12.}
$$
Its contribution is
$$
\frac{(-i\lambda)^4}{2}\int d^4A\,d^4B\,d^4C\,d^4D\,
\Delta(x_1-A)\Delta(B-x_2)
\Delta(A-C)\Delta(A-D)\Delta(B-C)\Delta(B-D)\Delta(C-D).
$$
Consequently the three multiplying weights are $\boxed{(1/2,1,1/2)}$, or divisors $\boxed{(2,1,2)}$. The central chord is a single internal line joining two vertices, not a vertex at the centre of the circle.
