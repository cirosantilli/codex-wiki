Solutions use <natural units> $\hbar=c=1$ and <Minkowski metric> $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$. The <d'Alembert operator> is $\Box=\partial_\mu\partial^\mu$. All four questions are covered, including the optional fourth question.

== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

For the <real scalar field>, the two derivatives entering the <Euler-Lagrange field equation> are
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}=\partial^\mu\phi,
\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi}=-m^2\phi-\frac{\lambda}{6}\phi^3.
$$
Consequently the <equation of motion> is
$$
\boxed{(\Box+m^2)\phi+\frac{\lambda}{6}\phi^3=0.}
$$
The <quartic interaction> therefore adds a local cubic term to the free <Klein-Gordon equation>. Below, write $F=(\Box+m^2)\phi+\lambda\phi^3/6$; thus $F=0$ means that the <field> is <on shell>.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Take a constant infinitesimal <translation> with fixed-coordinate <field variation> $\delta\phi=\epsilon^\nu\partial_\nu\phi$. The associated change in the <Lagrangian density> is $\delta\mathcal L=\epsilon^\nu\partial_\nu\mathcal L=\partial_\mu(\epsilon^\nu\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}\mathcal L)$. Substituting this total derivative into the <Noether current> gives $J^\mu=\epsilon^\nu T^\mu{}_{\nu}$, where
$$
\boxed{T^\mu{}_{\nu}=\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial_\nu\phi-\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}\left[\frac12\partial_\rho\phi\,\partial^\rho\phi-\frac12m^2\phi^2-\frac{\lambda}{24}\phi^4\right].}
$$
This is the <canonical stress-energy tensor>. The chosen sign of the <translation> parameter produces precisely this sign of the <Noether current>; reversing the parameter reverses the current without changing its conservation law.

To check <current conservation> directly, differentiate the <tensor>. The terms containing $\partial_\nu\partial_\rho\phi$ cancel against the derivative of the <kinetic term>, leaving
$$
\partial_\mu T^\mu{}_{\nu}=
\left(\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\frac{\lambda}{6}\phi^3\right)\partial_\nu\phi
=F\partial_\nu\phi.
$$
Thus $\partial_\mu T^\mu{}_{\nu}=0$ <on shell>. Raising the second <Lorentz index> gives $T^{\mu\nu}=T^{\nu\mu}$: this <scalar field> already has a symmetric <canonical stress-energy tensor>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

First take the <trace> in four <spacetime> dimensions:
$$
T^\mu{}_{\mu}=-(\partial\phi)^2+2m^2\phi^2+\frac{\lambda}{6}\phi^4,
\qquad
\Omega^\mu{}_{\mu}=T^\mu{}_{\mu}-3C\Box\phi^2.
$$
The <product rule> gives $\Box\phi^2=2(\partial\phi)^2+2\phi\Box\phi$. For the massless <equation of motion>, $\phi\Box\phi=-\lambda\phi^4/6$, so
$$
\left.\Omega^\mu{}_{\mu}\right|_{F=0,m=0}
=-(1+6C)(\partial\phi)^2+\lambda\left(\frac16+C\right)\phi^4.
$$
Both coefficients vanish for
$$
\boxed{C=-\frac16.}
$$
The minus sign follows from the order $\partial^\mu\partial^\nu-\eta^{\mu\nu}\Box$ used in the question's <stress-energy tensor improvement>. Commuting <partial derivatives> shows that this improvement has identically zero <divergence>, so it preserves the conservation law.

The precise <trace> identity, without using the <equation of motion>, is
$$
\boxed{\Omega^\mu{}_{\mu}=m^2\phi^2+\phi F.}
$$
Hence the massless <tensor> is traceless **on shell**. It is not traceless for arbitrary <off shell> configurations: a nonzero constant $\phi=A$ with $\lambda\ne0$ has $\Omega^\mu{}_{\mu}=\lambda A^4/6$. This is a classical result; it does not assert absence of a quantum <trace anomaly>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Under the finite <dilation>, the <volume element> transforms as $d^4x'=e^{4\alpha}d^4x$, whereas $\partial'_\mu\phi'(x')=e^{-2\alpha}\partial_\mu\phi(x)$. Thus the <kinetic term> and <quartic interaction> both acquire the factor $e^{-4\alpha}$. Their factors cancel the <Jacobian determinant>, proving
$$
\boxed{S[\phi']=S[\phi]\quad(m=0).}
$$
A <mass term> would instead acquire a net factor $e^{2\alpha}$ in the <action> and break this <scale invariance>.

For the <Noether current>, use the variation at the same coordinate, rather than comparing values at different coordinates:
$$
\delta_0\phi(x)=\phi'(x)-\phi(x)
=-\alpha\bigl(\phi+x^\nu\partial_\nu\phi\bigr).
$$
At $m=0$, the corresponding <Lagrangian density> variation is $\delta_0\mathcal L=-\alpha(4+x\cdot\partial)\mathcal L=-\alpha\partial_\mu(x^\mu\mathcal L)$. Thus $J_0^\mu=-x^\mu\mathcal L$, and the stated <Noether current> convention gives
$$
\boxed{j^\mu=-x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}-\phi\partial^\mu\phi.}
$$
The frequently used <dilatation current> $D^\mu=x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}+\phi\partial^\mu\phi$ is $-j^\mu$; this is solely an overall parameter convention. In the convention $D^\mu=x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}-V^\mu$, the <virial current> is $V^\mu=-\phi\partial^\mu\phi$.

Using <1/b/solution> and the <trace> computed in <1/c/solution> gives the useful identity
$$
\partial_\mu D^\mu
=T^\mu{}_{\mu}+x^\nu F\partial_\nu\phi+(\partial\phi)^2+\phi\Box\phi
=m^2\phi^2+\bigl(\phi+x^\nu\partial_\nu\phi\bigr)F.
$$
It follows that $\boxed{\partial_\mu j^\mu=0}$ in the massless theory <on shell>. Equivalently, $x_\nu\Omega^{\mu\nu}$ is a <conserved current>, since its <divergence> is the <trace> of the improved <stress-energy tensor> <on shell>. These are classical <scale invariance> statements; the nonzero <beta function> in <4/d/solution> explains why the interacting quantum theory need not retain them.

== 2
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $\int_{\mathbf p}=\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3$ and $E_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{\mathbf p^2+m^2}$. Insert the <mode expansion of a free field> for each quantity into the <commutator>. Only the mixed <creation operator> and <annihilation operator> terms survive. The <Dirac delta function> in their <canonical commutation relation> sets the two <momenta> equal, so the square-root factors multiply to $1/2$:
$$
[\phi(\mathbf x),\pi(\mathbf y)]
=\frac i2\int_{\mathbf p}\left[e^{i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}+e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}\right]
=\boxed{i\delta^3(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)}.
$$
In the second equality, each <Fourier transform> is the same <Dirac delta function>, since the integration domain is invariant under $\mathbf p\mapsto-\mathbf p$. This is the equal-time <canonical commutation relation>. The analogous calculation gives $[\phi(\mathbf x),\phi(\mathbf y)]=[\pi(\mathbf x),\pi(\mathbf y)]=0$, because their two mixed terms have opposite signs.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The unit-normalized <Fock vacuum> is characterized by
$$
a_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle=0\quad\hbox{for every }\mathbf p,
\qquad \langle0|0\rangle=1.
$$
Choose the <relativistic normalization of a one-particle state> from the outset:
$$
|\mathbf p\rangle=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}|0\rangle,
\qquad
|\mathbf p_1,\ldots,\mathbf p_n\rangle
=\prod_{r=1}^n\bigl(\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p_r}}\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf p_r}\bigr)|0\rangle.
$$
Since any two <creation operators> commute, any <permutation> of their order gives exactly the same <state vector>. Thus these <multiparticle quantum states> are symmetric under exchange and describe <bosons>, without a minus sign or any additional exchange phase.

The <Hilbert space> is the <bosonic Fock space>, the direct sum of the symmetric $n$-particle <Hilbert spaces>, including the zero-particle sector. Sharp-<momentum> kets are distributionally normalized; square-integrable <wave packets> provide ordinary <Hilbert space> vectors. For repeated occupation of a discrete mode, a unit-normalized <number state> uses $(a^\dagger)^n|0\rangle/\sqrt{n!}$. That factorial does not belong in the above labelled continuum-ket convention.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The <normal ordering> of both <linear maps> puts every <annihilation operator> to the right, so
$$
H|0\rangle=0,\qquad\mathbf P|0\rangle=\mathbf0.
$$
In particular the stated <Hamiltonian> has already subtracted the free <vacuum energy>. For a <one-particle state>, evaluate the <commutators> using $[AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B$:
$$
[H,a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}]
=\int_{\mathbf p} E_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf k)
=E_{\mathbf k}a^\dagger_{\mathbf k},
\qquad
[\mathbf P,a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}]=\mathbf k\,a^\dagger_{\mathbf k}.
$$
Acting on the <Fock vacuum> therefore gives
$$
\boxed{H|\mathbf k\rangle=E_{\mathbf k}|\mathbf k\rangle,
\qquad\mathbf P|\mathbf k\rangle=\mathbf k|\mathbf k\rangle.}
$$
The extra factor $\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf k}}$ in the chosen <relativistic normalization of a one-particle state> is a scalar multiplier and does not change either <eigenvalue>. Applying the same argument to a <multiparticle quantum state> gives additive total <energy> and <momentum>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Fock vacuum> condition and the <creation operator> <commutator> give
$$
\langle\mathbf p|\mathbf q\rangle
=\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}\,2E_{\mathbf q}}\,
\langle0|a_{\mathbf p}a^\dagger_{\mathbf q}|0\rangle
=\boxed{2E_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)}.
$$
Equality of the square-root product and $2E_{\mathbf p}$ holds on the support of the <Dirac delta function>.

The positive-energy <mass shell> has the <Lorentz invariant> measure
$$
d^4p\,\theta(p^0)\delta(p^2-m^2)=\frac{d^3p}{2E_{\mathbf p}}.
$$
Here both $p^2$ and the four-dimensional <volume element> are invariant, and positive <energy> is preserved by a proper orthochronous <Lorentz transformation>. Consequently the three-dimensional <Jacobian determinant> is $J=E_{\boldsymbol\Lambda p}/E_{\mathbf p}$. Transforming the <Dirac delta function> yields
$$
\delta^3(\boldsymbol\Lambda p-\boldsymbol\Lambda q)
=\frac{E_{\mathbf p}}{E_{\boldsymbol\Lambda p}}\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q),
$$
so its factor exactly cancels that of $2E_{\boldsymbol\Lambda p}$. Thus the displayed <inner product> is invariant under simultaneous transformation of both <momenta>. This is also why the one-particle completeness measure is $d^3p/((2\pi)^3 2E_{\mathbf p})$.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[Section 2.4.1, relativistic normalization and the positive-energy mass-shell measure]{ref}

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Heisenberg picture> evolution of the <annihilation operator> follows from $[H,a_{\mathbf p}]=-E_{\mathbf p}a_{\mathbf p}$. With $p^0=E_{\mathbf p}$, this gives the <mode expansion of a free field>
$$
\phi(x)=\int_{\mathbf p}\frac1{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf p}}}
\left(a_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot x}+a^\dagger_{\mathbf p}e^{ip\cdot x}\right).
$$
Define $\Delta^+(z)=\int_{\mathbf p}e^{-ip\cdot z}/(2E_{\mathbf p})$. The <time-ordered product> then gives the <Feynman propagator>
$$
\Delta_F(z)=\theta(z^0)\Delta^+(z)+\theta(-z^0)\Delta^+(-z)
=\int\frac{d^4p}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{i\,e^{-ip\cdot z}}{p^2-m^2+i0}.
$$
The last expression follows by closing the $p^0$ <contour integral> below for $z^0>0$ and above for $z^0<0$; the <scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription> supplies the required positive- and negative-frequency terms.

For $z^0\ne0$ every term obeys the homogeneous <Klein-Gordon equation>. At $z^0=0$, the equal-time <commutator> of the <fields> vanishes, so the <propagator> is continuous and there is no derivative of a <Dirac delta function>. Its first time derivative has the jump
$$
\left[\partial_{z^0}\Delta_F\right]_{0^-}^{0^+}
=\int_{\mathbf p}(-i)e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf z}
=-i\delta^3(\mathbf z).
$$
The second time derivative therefore supplies precisely the source:
$$
\boxed{(\Box_x+m^2)\Delta_F(x-y)=-i\delta^4(x-y).}
$$
Thus the defined <Feynman propagator> is a <Green's function> with source normalization $-i$; $i\Delta_F$ has source normalization $+1$. Keeping this factor avoids confusing the <vacuum expectation value> convention with conventions which define the <propagator> after dividing by $i$.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[Sections 2.7.1–2.7.2, time ordering, pole prescription and the inhomogeneous Klein-Gordon equation]{ref}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Let brackets denote <mass dimension>. In <natural units>, $[x]=-1$ and $[\partial_\mu]=1$, so a dimensionless <action> requires $[\mathcal L]=D$. The <kinetic term> fixes
$$
2+2[\phi]=D,
\qquad
\boxed{[\phi]=\frac{D-2}{2}.}
$$
The <cubic scalar field theory> interaction then requires $[\lambda]+3[\phi]=D$, giving
$$
\boxed{[\lambda]=\frac{6-D}{2},\qquad D_c=6,\qquad[\phi]_{D_c}=2.}
$$
The <coupling constant (physics)> is dimensionless at this <critical dimension of a monomial scalar interaction>. For $D<6$ it has positive <mass dimension>, while for $D>6$ it has negative <mass dimension>, the usual <power counting> distinction between super-renormalizable and non-renormalizable cubic interactions.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $S=S_0+S_I$, where $S_0$ contains the quadratic <kinetic term> and <mass term>, and $S_I=-\lambda\int d^Dx\,\phi(x)^3/3!$. Then
$$
e^{iS}=e^{iS_0}\sum_{r=0}^\infty\frac{(-i\lambda)^r}{r!(3!)^r}
\int\prod_{a=1}^r d^Dx_a\,\prod_{a=1}^r\phi(x_a)^3.
$$
Let $Z_0=\int D\phi\,e^{iS_0}$ and let $\langle\cdot\rangle_0$ denote the unit-normalized free <Gaussian> <vacuum expectation value>. The unnormalized numerator $\mathcal N_N$ is consequently
$$
\frac{\mathcal N_N}{Z_0}
=\sum_{r=0}^\infty\frac{(-i\lambda)^r}{r!(3!)^r}
\int\prod_{a=1}^r d^Dx_a\,
\left\langle T\phi(y_1)\cdots\phi(y_N)\prod_{a=1}^r\phi(x_a)^3\right\rangle_0.
$$
The Minkowski <path integral> is understood with the usual <scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription>, as a formal <perturbation series>. A real cubic <potential energy> is unbounded below; no nonperturbative stable <vacuum> is being assumed here.

Set $\Delta(x-z)=\langle T\phi(x)\phi(z)\rangle_0$, $K_i(x)=\Delta(y_i-x)$, and $t=\Delta(0)$, with a common <regularization> understood for coincident points. The zero-order one-point term has one <field> and vanishes. At first order, <Wick's theorem> gives three choices for the <contraction> with the external <field>, leaving a <tadpole diagram>:
$$
\langle T\phi(y_1)\phi(x)^3\rangle_0=3K_1(x)t.
$$
The second-order term contains seven <fields>, so it vanishes by the <vanishing odd correlators of a free Gaussian field>. Hence
$$
\boxed{\mathcal N_1=Z_0\left[\frac{-i\lambda}{2}\int d^Dx\,K_1(x)t+O(\lambda^3)\right].}
$$
The coefficient is $3/3!=1/2$, or <symmetry factor> $S=2$ when each diagram is weighted by $1/S$. No <spacetime> integral has been evaluated. The one-point <tadpole diagram> is shown in the first panel of the following figure; its other panels organize <3/c/solution>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the notation $\Delta_{12}=\Delta(y_1-y_2)$, $\Delta_{xz}=\Delta(x-z)$ and $\int_{xz}=\int d^Dx\,d^Dz$. The order-$\lambda$ term vanishes because it has five <fields>. At order $\lambda^2$, enumerate the eight-field <Wick contractions>, whose expansion denominator is $2!(3!)^2=72$.

If the two external <fields> contract with each other, the remaining <vacuum diagrams> are a pair of cubic <vertices> joined by three lines, with six <Wick contractions>, and a pair joined by one line with a <tadpole diagram> at each end, with nine <Wick contractions>. Define their combined integrated weight by
$$
V_2=\int_{xz}\left[\frac1{12}\Delta_{xz}^3+\frac18t^2\Delta_{xz}\right].
$$
Thus $Z/Z_0=1+(-i\lambda)^2V_2+O(\lambda^4)$. The numerator is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathcal N_2}{Z_0}
={}&\Delta_{12}\left[1+(-i\lambda)^2V_2\right]\quad+\\
&(-i\lambda)^2\int_{xz}\left[
\frac12K_1(x)K_2(z)\Delta_{xz}^2
+\frac12K_1(x)K_2(x)\Delta_{xz}t
+\frac14K_1(x)K_2(z)t^2\right]+O(\lambda^4).
\end{aligned}
$$
The three latter terms have respectively 36, 36 and 18 <Wick contractions>. Together with the six and nine <Wick contractions>, they exhaust all $7!!=105$ pairings.

Dividing by $Z/Z_0$ cancels the two <vacuum diagrams>, giving
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\langle T\phi(y_1)\phi(y_2)\rangle={}&\Delta_{12}+(-i\lambda)^2\int_{xz}\Bigl[
\tfrac12K_1(x)K_2(z)\Delta_{xz}^2\\
&+\tfrac12K_1(x)K_2(x)\Delta_{xz}t
+\tfrac14K_1(x)K_2(z)t^2\Bigr]+O(\lambda^4).
\end{aligned}}
$$
The first correction is the two-line <bubble diagram>, with <symmetry factor> $S=2$. The second is the connected <tadpole contribution to a cubic-theory two-point function>: both external lines meet one <vertex>, joined by a single line to a second <vertex> bearing a <tadpole diagram>; its <symmetry factor> is also $S=2$. Interchanging the two integrated <vertices> is already included in the 36 contractions and gives no extra factor.

The final term is the <disconnected Feynman diagram> consisting of two separate one-point <tadpole diagrams>, with coefficient $1/4$. It survives <cancellation of vacuum bubbles>, since each component touches an external point. Indeed it is precisely the leading $\langle\phi(y_1)\rangle\langle\phi(y_2)\rangle$. It would disappear from the connected <two-point correlation function>, but the question defines the full <correlation function>. No <normal ordering> or <tadpole subtraction> of the interaction was specified. All terms are depicted in <3/b/solution>, and all <spacetime> integrals remain unevaluated.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a connected cubic <Feynman diagram> with $V$ internal <vertices>, $I$ internal lines and three external lines, $3V=2I+3$. Hence $V$ is odd and the <loop order> is $L=I-V+1=(V-1)/2$. Through order $\lambda^3$, there is one <tree-level Feynman diagram> at $V=1$ and seven labelled <one-loop Feynman diagrams> at $V=3$:

* One triangle, with one external line at each <vertex>. Fixing the three external labels leaves no internal automorphism, so its <symmetry factor> is $S=1$.
* Three <bubble diagrams> on external branches of the tree, one for each choice of the distinguished external label $i$. The other two external lines meet the remaining tree <vertex>. Exchanging the two parallel lines gives <symmetry factor> $S=2$ for each labelled graph.
* Three <tadpole diagrams> at the end of a chain. The middle <vertex> carries the distinguished external label $i$, the other two external lines meet one end <vertex>, and the other end bears the loop. Reversing the two half-edges of the loop gives <symmetry factor> $S=2$ for each labelled graph.

\Image[2016-cubic-three-point-diagrams.png]
{title=All connected cubic three-point diagrams through third order: one tree and seven labelled one-loop graphs; a diagram of symmetry factor S has coefficient 1/S}

For an explicit check, use the <propagator> notation of <3/b/solution> and define, with $\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal T&=\int d^Dx\,d^Dz\,d^Dw\,
K_1(x)K_2(z)K_3(w)\Delta_{xz}\Delta_{zw}\Delta_{wx},\\
\mathcal B_i&=\int d^Dx\,d^Dz\,d^Dw\,
K_i(z)K_j(w)K_k(w)\Delta_{xz}^2\Delta_{xw},\\
\mathcal A_i&=\int d^Dx\,d^Dz\,d^Dw\,
K_i(z)K_j(w)K_k(w)\Delta_{xz}\Delta_{zw}\,t.
\end{aligned}
$$
There is no ordering of $j,k$ in this notation. The complete connected <correlation function> through this order is
$$
\boxed{G_{3,c}=(-i\lambda)\int d^Dx\,K_1(x)K_2(x)K_3(x)
+(-i\lambda)^3\left[\mathcal T+\frac12\sum_{i=1}^3(\mathcal B_i+\mathcal A_i)\right]+O(\lambda^5).}
$$
For completeness of the classification, a one-loop connected internal graph either has a three-edge cycle, a pair of parallel edges and a bridge, or a self-loop and two bridges. The cubic degree condition forces exactly the external attachments just listed. Direct <Wick contraction> counting gives 1296 pairings for the triangle and 648 for each of the six other labelled graphs, divided by $3!(3!)^3=1296$.

**Connected does not mean one-particle irreducible.** After <amputation of external propagators>, only the triangle is <one-particle irreducible> at this order; the six other loop graphs belong to the requested connected answer. The <tadpole diagrams> cannot be discarded without an additional subtraction prescription.

{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

For incoming <four-momenta>, the momentum-space <Feynman rules> in the chosen <Minkowski metric> convention are:

* Each internal line contributes the <scalar propagator> $i/(p^2-m^2+i0)$.
* Each quartic <interaction vertex> contributes $-i\lambda_0$ and $(2\pi)^D\delta^D(\sum p)$ for <momentum conservation>.
* Each independent <loop momentum> is integrated with $d^Dk/(2\pi)^D$; after eliminating redundant <Dirac delta functions>, one overall conservation factor remains.
* Each graph is divided by its <symmetry factor> $S$, with external labels held fixed.

There is one connected one-loop <one-particle-irreducible> two-point graph: a single quartic <vertex> with two external lines and one <tadpole diagram>. Its <symmetry factor> is $S=2$ from interchange of the two loop half-edges.

There are three connected one-loop <one-particle-irreducible> four-point graphs: two quartic <vertices> joined by two parallel internal lines, with external partitions $(12|34)$, $(13|24)$ and $(14|23)$. These are the $s$, $t$ and $u$ <scattering channels>; each <bubble diagram> has <symmetry factor> $S=2$. External-branch insertions are excluded by the <one-particle-irreducible> requirement.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The positive denominators in this part are **Euclidean**. Starting with the <Minkowski spacetime> <Feynman rules>, first perform a <Wick rotation>; a bare <Minkowski metric> integral without a pole prescription would not equal the displayed positive expression. Work initially with $m^2>0$ and $\Re n>D/2$, where the <Euclidean massive loop integral> converges.

Use <Schwinger parameterization> followed by the <Gaussian integral>:
$$
\frac1{(k^2+m^2)^n}=\frac1{\Gamma(n)}\int_0^\infty ds\,s^{n-1}e^{-s(k^2+m^2)},
\qquad
\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}e^{-sk^2}=\frac1{(4\pi s)^{D/2}}.
$$
The remaining <Gamma function> integral gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{D,n}(m^2)
&=\frac1{(4\pi)^{D/2}\Gamma(n)}\int_0^\infty ds\,s^{n-D/2-1}e^{-sm^2}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\Gamma(n-D/2)}{(4\pi)^{D/2}(n-1)!}(m^2)^{D/2-n}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The factorial form is for positive integral $n$. <Dimensional regularization> defines the result elsewhere by <analytic continuation>.

For the <scalar bubble integral>, combine the two denominators with a <Feynman parameter> $x$ and shift the integration variable to $\ell=k-(1-x)p_E$. Then
$$
B_D(p_E)=\int_0^1dx\,I_{D,2}(M_x^2),
\qquad M_x^2=m^2+x(1-x)p_E^2.
$$
For $D=4-\epsilon$ this is
$$
B_D(p_E)=\frac{\Gamma(\epsilon/2)}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon/2}}\int_0^1dx\,(M_x^2)^{-\epsilon/2}.
$$
The <Gamma function> recurrence gives $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)=2/\epsilon+O(1)$, and the parameter integral is $1+O(\epsilon)$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{B_D(p_E)=\frac1{8\pi^2\epsilon}+O(1).}
$$
This <ultraviolet divergence> is independent of the external <momentum>. The assumption $m^2>0$ avoids an <infrared divergence>. In particular the completely massless zero-momentum integral is a <scaleless integral> and vanishes in <dimensional regularization>; its ultraviolet and infrared poles must be separated before assigning it the displayed ultraviolet residue.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

It is convenient to evaluate the <one-particle-irreducible> vertices using the <Euclidean action> and then translate the <counterterms> back to the <Minkowski spacetime> <Lagrangian density>. For the <tadpole integral>, <analytic continuation> of <4/b/solution> gives
$$
I_{D,1}=\frac{\Gamma(-1+\epsilon/2)}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon/2}}(m^2)^{1-\epsilon/2}
=-\frac{m^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}+O(1).
$$
Indeed $\Gamma(-1+\epsilon/2)=\Gamma(\epsilon/2)/(\epsilon/2-1)=-2/\epsilon+O(1)$. The negative pole is a property of <analytic continuation>, not the value of a convergent positive integral.

The <Euclidean space> inverse two-point <vertex> has the one-loop correction
$$
\Gamma_E^{(2)}(p_E)=p_E^2+m^2+\frac{\lambda_0}{2}I_{D,1}+O(\lambda_0^2),
\qquad
\left.\Gamma_E^{(2)}\right|_{\rm pole}=-\frac{\lambda_0m^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
The factor $1/2$ is the <symmetry factor> weight of the <tadpole diagram>. A mass <counterterm> $\delta m^2=\lambda_0m^2/(16\pi^2\epsilon)$ cancels this pole. There is no momentum-dependent pole at this order, and hence no one-loop <wave-function renormalization>.

For the four-point <vertex>, the three <bubble diagrams> give
$$
\Gamma_E^{(4)}=\lambda_0-\frac{\lambda_0^2}{2}(B_s+B_t+B_u)+O(\lambda_0^3),
\qquad
\left.\Gamma_E^{(4)}\right|_{\rm pole}=-\frac{3\lambda_0^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
One can check the relative minus sign independently from the one-loop <effective action>: its $\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}\log(1+G\lambda_0\phi^2/2)$ term has a positive term linear in $\lambda_0\phi^2$ and a negative term quadratic in it. Thus $\delta\lambda=3\lambda_0^2/(16\pi^2\epsilon)$ cancels the quartic pole.

In the <Euclidean action>, these two <counterterms> are $+\delta m^2\phi^2/2+\delta\lambda\phi^4/4!$. Returning to the <Minkowski spacetime> <Lagrangian density> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\rm ct}=-\frac12\frac{\lambda_0m^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}\phi^2
-\frac1{4!}\frac{3\lambda_0^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}\phi^4.}
$$
For a direct sign check with <Feynman rules>, the two amputated loop pole insertions are $+i\lambda_0m^2/(16\pi^2\epsilon)$ and $+i3\lambda_0^2/(16\pi^2\epsilon)$, while the <counterterm> insertions are $-i\delta m^2$ and $-i\delta\lambda$.

As usual, the displayed pole residues suppress <renormalization scale> powers: $[\lambda_0]=\epsilon$ away from four dimensions. The next part restores these powers explicitly with a dimensionless <renormalized coupling>. Replacing the tree coupling by its renormalized counterpart inside a one-loop residue changes only higher-loop terms.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Because the quartic <coupling constant (physics)> has <mass dimension> $\epsilon=4-D$, introduce a <renormalization scale> $\mu$ and define the dimensionless <renormalized coupling> $g$ by <minimal subtraction scheme>:
$$
\boxed{\lambda_0=\mu^\epsilon\left[g+\frac{3g^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}+O(g^3)\right].}
$$
Here $\lambda_0$ is the fixed bare <coupling constant (physics)>. Equivalently the tree-level quartic term is $-\mu^\epsilon g\phi^4/4!$, and its one-loop <counterterm> is $-\mu^\epsilon3g^2\phi^4/(16\pi^2\epsilon\,4!)$. This states explicitly the scale convention implicit in <4/c/solution>.

Put $a=3/(16\pi^2)$. Differentiating the bare relation at fixed $\lambda_0$ gives
$$
0=\epsilon\left(g+\frac{ag^2}{\epsilon}\right)
+\widehat\beta(g)\left(1+\frac{2ag}{\epsilon}\right)+O(g^3).
$$
Write $\widehat\beta=-\epsilon g+b g^2+O(g^3)$. The coefficient of $g^2$ in the last identity is $a+b-2a$, so $b=a$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\widehat\beta(g)=-\epsilon g+\frac{3g^2}{16\pi^2}+O(g^3).}
$$
The first term is the <engineering term in a dimensionally continued beta function>. In four dimensions it disappears, leaving the positive <one-loop quartic scalar beta function> $3g^2/(16\pi^2)$. Thus the <renormalized coupling> increases with increasing <renormalization scale> near weak positive coupling. This quantum running is the obstruction to extending the classical <scale invariance> in <1/d/solution> to the interacting quantum theory.
