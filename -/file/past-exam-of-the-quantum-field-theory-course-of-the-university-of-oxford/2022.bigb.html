== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

A dimensionless <action> in six spacetime dimensions requires the <Lagrangian density> to have <mass dimension> six. The <kinetic term> then fixes $2+2[\phi]=6$, and each <field interaction term> must have the same <mass dimension>. Therefore
$$
\boxed{[\phi]=2,\qquad [g_1]=0,\qquad [g_2]=-2.}
$$
Equivalently, in powers of a length unit $L$, $\phi$ has units $L^{-2}$, $g_1$ has units $L^0$, and $g_2$ has units $L^2$. Thus literal *length exponents* would be $(-2,0,2)$; the $d$'s in the supplied <superficial degree of divergence> formula must instead mean <mass dimensions>, namely $(2,0,-2)$. This distinction resolves the wording's convention mismatch.

To check the convention independently, let $I,V_3,V_4,\ell$ count internal lines, cubic <interaction vertices>, quartic <interaction vertices>, and independent <loop momenta>. A connected <Feynman diagram> obeys $2I+n=3V_3+4V_4$ and $\ell=I-V_3-V_4+1$. Hence its <superficial degree of divergence> is $6\ell-2I=6-2n+2V_4$, agreeing with the supplied formula only with the <mass dimensions> above.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

In <momentum space>, the <Feynman rules> for the <real scalar field> are
$$
\boxed{\text{internal line: }\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
\text{cubic vertex: }-ig_1,\qquad
\text{quartic vertex: }-ig_2.}
$$
Each <interaction vertex> supplies $(2\pi)^6\delta^{(6)}(\sum p)$, with all its <momenta> counted inward. Impose <momentum conservation>, integrate each independent <loop momentum> with $d^6p/(2\pi)^6$, and divide by the <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>. For a connected amputated <scattering amplitude>, remove the external <scalar propagators> and retain a single overall <momentum conservation> factor. These signs follow by expanding $\exp(i\int\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}})$; the factorials in the <Lagrangian density> cancel the corresponding attachments in the <Wick theorem>. The <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> specifies the <scalar propagator> poles.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Set $g=g_1$ and $g_2=0$. The <superficial degree of divergence> is then $\omega=6-2n$, independently of the number of cubic <interaction vertices>. After subtracting <ultraviolet subdivergences>, the divergent part of a <one-particle-irreducible> <Feynman diagram> is a local <polynomial> in external <momenta> of degree at most $\omega$. <Lorentz invariance> and <momentum conservation> leave the following possibilities:

* $n=0$: a constant <vacuum energy density>.
* $n=1$: a linear <tadpole counterterm>. The single external <momentum> is zero, so derivatives cannot generate further independent terms.
* $n=2$: a <mass counterterm> and a <wave-function renormalization>, since the <polynomial> has the form $A+Bp^2$.
* $n=3$: a constant cubic <interaction vertex>.
* $n\geq4$: no overall <ultraviolet divergence>, although <ultraviolet subdivergences> still require their lower-point <counterterms>.

Thus a finite set of <counterterms> closes under <perturbation theory>. In terms of renormalized coefficients, write
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ct}}
=\frac{1+\delta_Z}{2}(\partial\phi)^2
-\frac{m^2+\delta_m}{2}\phi^2
-\frac{g+\delta_g}{3!}\phi^3-\delta_h\phi-\delta_\mathcal E.}
$$
Here $\delta_m$ is a coefficient of squared <mass>, rather than a correction to $m$ itself. The <tadpole counterterm> fixes the chosen expansion point, for example $\langle\phi\rangle=0$; it cannot be omitted merely because the original <Lagrangian density> lacks a linear term. The <vacuum energy density> term cancels out of normalized <Green functions>, but is required if the unnormalized <vacuum functional> is also to be renormalized. This establishes perturbative <renormalizability>; the unbounded <scalar potential> of a real cubic <scalar field theory> does not by itself define a stable nonperturbative <vacuum state>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Continue with the cubic <scalar field theory> of <1/c>, and use precisely its <counterterm> signs. Take $m^2>0$ to separate <ultraviolet divergences> from <infrared divergences>. Make a <Wick rotation> and impose the rotationally invariant <cutoff regularization> $|q_E|<\Lambda$. Put
$$
C=\frac{\operatorname{area}(S^5)}{2(2\pi)^6}
=\frac1{128\pi^3},\qquad L_\Lambda=\log\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2},\qquad
I_r=\int_{|q_E|<\Lambda}\frac{d^6q_E}{(2\pi)^6}(q_E^2+m^2)^{-r}.
$$
For example, <spherical coordinates> give $I_1=C\int_0^{\Lambda^2} t^2/(t+m^2)\,dt$. Polynomial division and then differentiation with respect to $m^2$ give the divergent parts
$$
\begin{aligned}
I_1\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=C\left(\frac{\Lambda^4}{2}-m^2\Lambda^2+m^4L_\Lambda\right),\\
I_2\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=C(\Lambda^2-2m^2L_\Lambda),\qquad
I_3\big|_{\mathrm{div}}=CL_\Lambda.
\end{aligned}
$$
The derivative relations are $I_2=-\partial_{m^2}I_1$ and $I_3=\tfrac12\partial_{m^2}^2I_1$; differentiation also generates finite terms, which are irrelevant here. The first supplied integral identity is consistent with these divergent parts after <Wick rotation>.

**The second supplied expansion is incorrect.** Write $A=p^2-m^2$. Direct multiplication of the two denominators gives $(A+k^2)^2-4(p\cdot k)^2$, so the correct <Taylor expansion> is
$$
\frac1{[(p-k)^2-m^2][(p+k)^2-m^2]}
=\frac1{A^2}\left(1-\frac{2k^2}{A}+\frac{4(p\cdot k)^2}{A^2}+O(k^4)\right).
$$
A concrete counterexample is $p=0$, $m=1$, $k=(\epsilon,\mathbf0)$: the exact expression is $(1-\epsilon^2)^{-2}=1+2\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^4)$, whereas the printed expansion gives $1+\epsilon^2/2+O(\epsilon^4)$. We therefore compute the <self-energy> without that identity.

For Euclidean external <momentum> $P$, define the <scalar bubble integral>
$$
B(P)=\int\frac{d^6q_E}{(2\pi)^6}
\frac1{(q_E^2+m^2)((q_E+P)^2+m^2)}.
$$
A <Feynman parameter> $x$ combines its denominators into one squared denominator with $K=m^2+x(1-x)P^2$. Applying the radial result above and integrating $\int_0^1x(1-x)\,dx=1/6$ yields
$$
B(P)\big|_{\mathrm{div}}
=C\left(\Lambda^2-2m^2L_\Lambda-\frac{P^2}{3}L_\Lambda\right).
$$
One can instead expand with the <ultraviolet cutoff> fixed on the unshifted <loop momentum>: the $P^2$ coefficient of the integrand is $-(q_E^2+m^2)^{-3}+\tfrac23q_E^2(q_E^2+m^2)^{-4}$, using rotational averaging in six dimensions. Its leading radial term is $-1/(3q_E^6)$, giving the same logarithm. Shifting a sharp integration boundary changes finite terms in this <scalar bubble integral>, and does not change the displayed divergent coefficients.

To fix all signs and <symmetry factors> together, expand the one-loop <Euclidean effective action> around a background <scalar field>:
$$
\Gamma_{1,E}[\phi]=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}\log(D+g\phi),\qquad D=-\partial_E^2+m^2,
$$
$$
\Gamma_{1,E}[\phi]-\Gamma_{1,E}[0]
=\frac g2\operatorname{Tr}(D^{-1}\phi)
-\frac{g^2}{4}\operatorname{Tr}(D^{-1}\phi D^{-1}\phi)
+\frac{g^3}{6}\operatorname{Tr}(D^{-1}\phi)^3+\cdots.
$$
These terms are the <tadpole diagram>, the two-point <bubble diagram>, and the <triangle Feynman diagram>. The <tadpole diagram> has factor $1/2$; the two-point <bubble diagram> also has factor $1/2$ after two differentiations with respect to the background <scalar field>. The local three-point <interaction vertex> is $g^3I_3$. Thus their divergent contributions to the one-, two-, and three-point Euclidean <one-particle-irreducible> vertices are respectively
$$
\frac g2 I_1,\qquad -\frac{g^2}{2}B(P),\qquad g^3I_3.
$$
The Euclidean <counterterm Lagrangian> is $\delta_Z(\partial_E\phi)^2/2+\delta_m\phi^2/2+\delta_g\phi^3/6+\delta_h\phi+\delta_\mathcal E$. Cancelling each divergent <one-particle-irreducible> vertex therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\delta_h\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=-\frac{Cg}{2}\left(\frac{\Lambda^4}{2}-m^2\Lambda^2+m^4L_\Lambda\right),\\
\delta_m\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=\frac{Cg^2}{2}(\Lambda^2-2m^2L_\Lambda),\\
\delta_Z\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=-\frac{Cg^2}{6}L_\Lambda,\\
\delta_g\big|_{\mathrm{div}}&=-Cg^3L_\Lambda.
\end{aligned}}
$$
For example, the two-point sum is $\delta_ZP^2+\delta_m-g^2B(P)/2$ and its divergent coefficients cancel separately. Finite <counterterms> depend on the chosen <renormalization conditions>. The zero-<mass> limit can use a positive subtraction scale in the logarithms after <infrared regularization>.

If unnormalized <vacuum functionals> are retained, specify the determinant measure by
$$
V_{1,E}(0)=\frac12\int_{|q_E|<\Lambda}\frac{d^6q_E}{(2\pi)^6}
\log\frac{q_E^2+m^2}{\mu^2}.
$$
Expanding its radial integrand at large $q_E$ fixes the additional <vacuum energy density> <counterterm>:
$$
\boxed{\delta_\mathcal E\big|_{\mathrm{div}}=-\frac C2\left[
\frac{\Lambda^6}{3}\log\frac{\Lambda^2}{\mu^2}-\frac{\Lambda^6}{9}
+\frac{m^2\Lambda^4}{2}-\frac{m^4\Lambda^2}{2}
+\frac{m^6}{3}L_\Lambda\right].}
$$
The purely <mass>-independent terms depend on the determinant normalization. Normalized <Green functions> eliminate this entire constant.

=== a

==== Solution

The <auxiliary field> $\sigma$ has quadratic kernel $-1$ and no <kinetic term>. Inverting that kernel, rather than introducing an artificial <scalar mass>, gives the <momentum space> <Feynman rules>
$$
\boxed{\sigma\text{ line: }-i,\qquad
\psi\text{ line: }\frac{i(\not p+m)}{p^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
\sigma\bar\psi\psi\text{ vertex: }ig.}
$$
The <auxiliary-field propagator> is independent of <momentum>; in position space it is $-i\delta^{(4)}(x-y)$. At each <interaction vertex> attach $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\sum p)$ for <momentum conservation>. Integrate independent <loop momenta> with $d^4p/(2\pi)^4$, respect <fermion flow>, include the <closed fermion loop sign> $-1$, and use the appropriate <symmetry factor>. Incoming and outgoing <fermions> carry $u_s(p)$ and $\bar u_s(p)$; incoming and outgoing <antiparticles> carry $\bar v_s(p)$ and $v_s(p)$. Exchanging external <fermions> introduces the <fermionic sign>. Here $\not p=\gamma^\mu p_\mu$ is <Feynman slash notation>.

For a dimensional check, $[\psi]=3/2$, $[\sigma]=2$, $[g]=-1$ in <mass dimensions>. Eliminating $\sigma$ consequently produces a <four-fermion interaction> of negative coupling <mass dimension>, not a propagating scalar degree of freedom.

==== i

===== Solution

Label the incoming <fermion> <momenta> $p_1,p_2$ and outgoing <fermion> <momenta> $p_3,p_4$, with $p_1+p_2=p_3+p_4$. There are two <tree-level Feynman diagrams>: the $t$ channel connects $1$ to $3$ and $2$ to $4$, with exchanged <momentum> $q_t=p_1-p_3=p_4-p_2$; the $u$ channel connects $1$ to $4$ and $2$ to $3$, with $q_u=p_1-p_4=p_3-p_2$. Both internal lines are <auxiliary-field propagators>. The upper row in the figure gives these labelled connections; the outgoing labels are exchanged in its second panel to avoid a visually unnecessary crossing.

Each <tree-level Feynman diagram> contains two factors $ig$ and one factor $-i$, giving $ig^2$. The exchange of the two identical outgoing <fermions> gives the relative <fermionic sign>. Up to a common external-state phase, the corresponding <scattering amplitude> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{ff}=g^2\left[(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar u_4u_2)-(\bar u_4u_1)(\bar u_3u_2)\right].}
$$
There is no annihilation channel: both incoming particles carry the same <fermion number>, whereas a $\sigma\bar\psi\psi$ <interaction vertex> couples one <fermion> to one <antiparticle> when both are incoming.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Use incoming <fermion> $p_1$ and <antiparticle> $p_2$, outgoing <fermion> $p_3$ and <antiparticle> $p_4$. The two <tree-level Feynman diagrams> are the $t$ channel, with $q_t=p_1-p_3=p_4-p_2$, and the $s$ channel, with $q_s=p_1+p_2=p_3+p_4$. They occupy the lower row of the figure. In the $t$ channel the <fermion flow> follows $1\to3$ and $4\to2$; in the $s$ channel it follows $1\to2$ at the incoming <interaction vertex> and $4\to3$ at the outgoing <interaction vertex>.

The two contractions again have a relative <fermionic sign>. With the same harmless common-phase convention as <2/b/i>, their sum is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{f\bar f}=g^2\left[(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar v_2v_4)-(\bar v_2u_1)(\bar u_3v_4)\right].}
$$
The <auxiliary-field propagator> is the same constant $-i$ in both channels. There is no $u$ channel preserving the external <fermion number> assignments.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $u_i=u^{s_i}(p_i)$ and $v_i=v^{s_i}(p_i)$. With $S_{fi}=\delta_{fi}+i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_1+p_2-p_3-p_4)\mathcal M_{fi}$, choose
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4}^{s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4}=g^2(T-A),\qquad
T=(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar v_2v_4),\quad A=(\bar v_2u_1)(\bar u_3v_4).}
$$
The relative minus sign is fixed by the <canonical anticommutation relations>, not by the <Mandelstam variables>. It follows by <crossing symmetry> from the identical-<fermion> <scattering amplitude> in <2/b/i>, or directly by the two inequivalent external contractions of $g^2(\bar\psi\psi)^2/2$. For an explicit sign check, fix incoming state order $b_1^\dagger d_2^\dagger|0\rangle$ and outgoing order $b_3^\dagger d_4^\dagger|0\rangle$. The direct contraction contains $(b_3^\dagger b_1)(d_2d_4^\dagger)$, whose coefficient in the ordered product $b_3^\dagger d_4^\dagger d_2b_1$ is $-1$; the annihilation contraction $(b_3^\dagger d_4^\dagger)(d_2b_1)$ has coefficient $+1$. The common sign can change with the external-state convention, while their relative minus sign cannot.

Let $r=m^2$. The external particles are <on shell>, so $s+t+u=4r$ and
$$
p_1\cdot p_2=p_3\cdot p_4=\frac s2-r,\qquad
p_1\cdot p_3=p_2\cdot p_4=r-\frac t2,\qquad
p_1\cdot p_4=p_2\cdot p_3=r-\frac u2.
$$
Use the supplied <spin sums> and <gamma matrix trace identities>. The two diagonal terms in the initial <spin average> are
$$
\frac14\sum|T|^2
=\frac14\operatorname{Tr}[(\not p_3+m)(\not p_1+m)]
\operatorname{Tr}[(\not p_2-m)(\not p_4-m)]
=(t-4r)^2,
$$
$$
\frac14\sum|A|^2
=\frac14\operatorname{Tr}[(\not p_2-m)(\not p_1+m)]
\operatorname{Tr}[(\not p_3+m)(\not p_4-m)]
=(s-4r)^2.
$$
For the interference term, the <spin sum> is a single <Dirac trace>:
$$
R:=\sum TA^*
=\operatorname{Tr}[(\not p_3+m)(\not p_1+m)(\not p_2-m)(\not p_4-m)].
$$
Terms with an odd number of <Dirac gamma matrices> vanish. Expanding the remaining terms gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac R4={}&(p_3\cdot p_1)(p_2\cdot p_4)
-(p_3\cdot p_2)(p_1\cdot p_4)
+(p_3\cdot p_4)(p_1\cdot p_2)\\
&+r(p_3\cdot p_1-p_3\cdot p_2-p_3\cdot p_4-p_1\cdot p_2-p_1\cdot p_4+p_2\cdot p_4)+r^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Substituting the <Mandelstam variables> reduces this to
$$
R=s^2+t^2-u^2+16ru-16r^2
=(s-4r)^2+(t-4r)^2-(u-4r)^2.
$$
It is real. The initial <spin average> therefore yields
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^4\left[(t-4r)^2+(s-4r)^2-\frac R2\right],
$$
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac{g^4}{2}\left[(s-4m^2)^2+(t-4m^2)^2+(u-4m^2)^2\right].}
$$
**The displayed answer in the source is false.** At threshold, $p_i=(m,\mathbf0)$, hence $s=4m^2$ and $t=u=0$. Then $\bar vu=\bar uv=0$, while the <spin sums> give $\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=16g^4m^4$. The printed expression instead gives $20g^4m^4$. This is also a counterexample in the continuous threshold limit from physical nonzero incoming <momenta>. At $m=0$ there is a further discrepancy: the correct result is $g^4(s^2+t^2+u^2)/2$, rather than the printed $g^4(3s^2+3t^2-u^2)/2$. Changing a common phase of the <scattering amplitude> cannot change either comparison.

The requested $s\leftrightarrow t$ symmetry nevertheless holds for the correct answer. <Crossing symmetry> exchanges the two ways of pairing the four external <fermion> lines; the momentum-independent <auxiliary-field propagator> supplies no channel-dependent denominator. The relative <fermionic sign> can reverse the crossed <scattering amplitude>, but disappears in its squared modulus. With $u=4m^2-s-t$, the boxed expression is manifestly symmetric under $s\leftrightarrow t$. This is an analytic crossing relation, not a statement that positive physical $s$ and negative physical $t$ have interchangeable scattering kinematics.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Set $B(x)=\bar\psi(x)\psi(x)$, a Grassmann-even <Dirac scalar bilinear>. Completing the square gives
$$
-\frac12\sigma^2+g\sigma B
=-\frac12(\sigma-gB)^2+\frac{g^2}{2}B^2.
$$
Introduce external Grassmann <sources> $\eta,\bar\eta$ for the <Dirac field>, but no source for $\sigma$. The <generating functional> is
$$
Z[\eta,\bar\eta]=\int\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\,\mathcal D\sigma\,
\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\left[\bar\psi(i\not\partial-m)\psi
-\frac12(\sigma-gB)^2+\frac{g^2}{2}B^2
+\bar\eta\psi+\bar\psi\eta\right]\right\}.
$$
At a finite regulator the change of variable $\sigma'=\sigma-gB$ has unit Jacobian: it translates a bosonic integration variable by a Grassmann-even expression independent of that variable. Its oscillatory <Gaussian integral> can be defined with a convergence prescription and then continued. It is a constant $\mathcal N$ independent of $\psi,\eta,\bar\eta$. Thus
$$
Z[\eta,\bar\eta]=\mathcal N\int\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\,
\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\left[\bar\psi(i\not\partial-m)\psi
+\frac{g^2}{2}(\bar\psi\psi)^2+\bar\eta\psi+\bar\psi\eta\right]\right\}.
$$
The factor $\mathcal N$ cancels in $Z[\eta,\bar\eta]/Z[0,0]$. Every normalized <Green function> with only external <fermion> lines is a Grassmann source derivative of this same normalized <generating functional>, so
$$
\boxed{G_{\mathrm{auxiliary}}^{(n)}=G_{\mathrm{four\text{-}fermion}}^{(n)}.}
$$
This is an exact <Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation> in reverse, rather than just an approximation based on <tree-level Feynman diagrams>. At the diagrammatic level the contact <auxiliary-field propagator> $-i\delta^{(4)}(x-y)$ collapses each exchanged line into the same local <four-fermion interaction>. The equality uses a common regulator and does not assert perturbative <renormalizability> of that four-dimensional <four-fermion interaction>.

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

For arbitrary <linear operators>, expansion of the <anticommutators> gives
$$
\{X,Y\}Z-Y\{X,Z\}=(XY+YX)Z-Y(XZ+ZX)=XYZ-YZX=[X,YZ].
$$
Now use the <commutator product rule> $[VX,W]=V[X,W]+[V,W]X$, with $W=YZ$, twice. The result is
$$
\boxed{[VX,YZ]=V\{X,Y\}Z-VY\{X,Z\}+\{V,Y\}ZX-Y\{V,Z\}X.}
$$
No grading assumption is needed for these algebraic identities.

For the <Dirac field>, supplement the supplied equal-time <canonical anticommutation relations> with $\{\psi_a(x),\psi_b(y)\}=\{\psi_a^\dagger(x),\psi_b^\dagger(y)\}=0$. Define the <fermion number> density $n(\mathbf x)=\sum_a\psi_a^\dagger(\mathbf x)\psi_a(\mathbf x)$. In the preceding identity take $V=\psi_a^\dagger(\mathbf x)$, $X=\psi_a(\mathbf x)$, $Y=\psi_b^\dagger(\mathbf y)$, $Z=\psi_c(\mathbf z)$ and sum over $a$. Only its first and last <anticommutators> survive, giving
$$
\boxed{[n(\mathbf x),\psi_b^\dagger(\mathbf y)\psi_c(\mathbf z)]
=\left[\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)-\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)\right]
\psi_b^\dagger(\mathbf y)\psi_c(\mathbf z).}
$$
This is an identity of operator-valued <distributions>. Point splitting, or smearing with test functions, gives a precise meaning to coincident bilinears. <Normal ordering> only subtracts a constant from the density and does not alter this <commutator>.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Define the constant <Dirac gamma matrix> combinations $\alpha^k=\gamma^0\gamma^k$ and $\beta=\gamma^0$. The <Hamiltonian operator> is
$$
H=\int d^3y\,\psi_b^\dagger(\mathbf y)
(-i\alpha^k_{bc}\partial_{y^k}+m\beta_{bc})\psi_c(\mathbf y).
$$
Evaluate its <commutator> with $n(\mathbf x)$ using the bilocal result in <3/a>. The <mass term> cancels immediately when $\mathbf z=\mathbf y$, because its two <Dirac delta distributions> coincide. For the derivative term first keep the two points distinct:
$$
C(\mathbf x)=-i\int d^3y\,\alpha^k_{bc}\left.
\partial_{z^k}\left\{[\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)-\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf z)]
\psi_b^\dagger(\mathbf y)\psi_c(\mathbf z)\right\}\right|_{\mathbf z=\mathbf y}.
$$
The terms in which the derivative acts on $\psi_c$ cancel against each other; the derivative of the second <Dirac delta distribution> remains. Thus
$$
C(\mathbf x)=i\int d^3y\,\psi^\dagger(\mathbf y)\alpha^k\psi(\mathbf y)
\partial_{y^k}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y)
=-i\partial_{x^k}[\psi^\dagger(\mathbf x)\alpha^k\psi(\mathbf x)].
$$
The last equality is <integration by parts>, or the defining derivative rule for a <distribution>. Consequently the literal <conserved current> whose <divergence> equals $C$ is
$$
\boxed{C=\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf J,\qquad
J^k=-i\psi^\dagger\alpha^k\psi=-i\bar\psi\gamma^k\psi.}
$$
Because a <commutator> of two <Hermitian operators> is anti-Hermitian, this $\mathbf J$ includes $-i$. The physical Hermitian <Dirac current> is instead $j^k=\bar\psi\gamma^k\psi$, so $C=-i\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf j$. The <Heisenberg equation of motion> now gives
$$
\boxed{\partial_t n=i[H,n]=-iC=-\boldsymbol\nabla\cdot\mathbf j,\qquad
j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi.}
$$
This establishes the <continuity equation> directly from the required <commutator>, without assuming the <Dirac equation> or invoking the <Noether theorem> as a substitute for the computation.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Hamiltonian operator> is invariant under the constant <global phase symmetry> $\psi\mapsto e^{i\theta}\psi$, $\bar\psi\mapsto\bar\psi e^{-i\theta}$. The <Noether current> of this <unitary group> $U(1)$ is the <Dirac current> obtained in <3/b>. For fields with vanishing flux at infinity, or periodic boundary conditions, the <divergence theorem> and that <continuity equation> imply
$$
\boxed{Q=\int d^3x\,:\!\psi^\dagger\psi\!:\ ,\qquad [Q,H]=0,\qquad \frac{dQ}{dt}=0.}
$$
The <canonical anticommutation relations> also give $[Q,\psi]=-\psi$ and $[Q,\psi^\dagger]=\psi^\dagger$, hence $e^{i\theta Q}\psi e^{-i\theta Q}=e^{-i\theta}\psi$. Reversing the parameter sign recovers the stated phase convention. Thus $Q$ generates the <global phase symmetry>. With <normal ordering>, $Q$ counts <fermions> minus <antiparticles>, rather than the sum of both populations. Its <conservation law> is <Dirac fermion number conservation>. A position-dependent phase produces derivative terms in this free <Hamiltonian operator>; the symmetry under discussion is global.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Combine the two equal-<mass> <Dirac fields> into $\Psi=(\psi^1,\psi^2)^T$. A constant <unitary matrix> $U$ acting on this flavour index commutes with the common <Dirac Hamiltonian>, so $\Psi\mapsto U\Psi$ gives the <unitary flavour symmetry of degenerate Dirac fields> $U(2)$. Its real <Lie algebra> has dimension four. A convenient Hermitian generator basis is $T^0=\mathbf1_2/2$, $T^a=\tau^a/2$, $a=1,2,3$, where $\tau^a$ are the <Pauli matrices>.

For each generator the <canonical anticommutation relations> give
$$
\rho_A=\Psi^\dagger T^A\Psi,\qquad
j_A^k=\bar\Psi\gamma^kT^A\Psi,\qquad
[\rho_A(\mathbf x),H]=-i\partial_k j_A^k(\mathbf x).
$$
The calculation is exactly the one in <3/b>, since $T^A$ commutes with both the <Dirac gamma matrices> and the common <fermion mass matrix>. Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{Four independent number-preserving vector currents:}\qquad
j_A^\mu=\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu T^A\Psi,\quad A=0,1,2,3.}
$$
Explicitly their densities are
$$
\rho_0=\tfrac12(\psi^{1\dagger}\psi^1+\psi^{2\dagger}\psi^2),\qquad
\rho_3=\tfrac12(\psi^{1\dagger}\psi^1-\psi^{2\dagger}\psi^2),
$$
$$
\rho_1=\tfrac12(\psi^{1\dagger}\psi^2+\psi^{2\dagger}\psi^1),\qquad
\rho_2=\tfrac12(-i\psi^{1\dagger}\psi^2+i\psi^{2\dagger}\psi^1).
$$
The two off-diagonal <conserved currents> are why counting only the individual <fermion numbers> misses two generators. The singlet generates $U(1)$; the other three generate <special unitary group> $SU(2)$. Unequal <masses> would break the off-diagonal <conservation laws>.

**Scope of the count.** Four is the count of currents of the displayed $\bar\psi^A\gamma^\mu T_{AB}\psi^B$ form. The completely free system also has an <enhanced internal symmetry of free Dirac fields> if mixing with <charge-conjugate fields> is admitted. For $m\ne0$, write its two <Dirac fields> as four equal-<mass> <Majorana spinors> $\chi^I$. Their <orthogonal group> $O(4)$ rotations have six continuous generators, with <conserved currents> proportional to $i\bar\chi^I\gamma^\mu\chi^J$ for $I<J$; the four $U(2)$ currents form a subgroup. This larger count is relevant if “similar” means *all* local internal currents of the free system, without preserving a chosen <fermion number>. If $m=0$, the usual number-preserving <chiral flavour symmetry of massless Dirac fields> is $U(2)_L\times U(2)_R$ with eight currents; allowing <charge conjugation> mixing enlarges it to $U(4)$ with sixteen. These counts concern internal currents, excluding spacetime <Noether currents>.
