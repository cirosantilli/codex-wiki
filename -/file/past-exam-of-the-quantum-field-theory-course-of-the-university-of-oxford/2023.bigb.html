=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Use $\hbar=c=1$, the <Minkowski metric> $\eta=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, and $p^2=(p^0)^2-(p^1)^2$. The momentum-space <Feynman rules> for the <real scalar field> are
$$
\boxed{\text{internal line: }\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon},\qquad
\text{four-leg vertex: }-ig_4.}
$$
At each <interaction vertex> impose two-dimensional <momentum conservation>; before removing the overall conservation factor this is $(2\pi)^2\delta^{(2)}(\sum_jp_j)$. Integrate each independent <loop momentum> with $d^2k/(2\pi)^2$, and divide a <Feynman diagram> by its <symmetry factor>. External lines carry the same <scalar propagator> in an unamputated <two-point correlation function>; they are removed in an amputated <scattering amplitude>.

The quadratic <action> is inverted with the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> to obtain the <scalar propagator>. Expanding the <Dyson series> gives $-ig_4/4!$ per insertion; the $4!$ attachments in the <Wick theorem> cancel the factorial. Fourier integration over the insertion point supplies the <momentum conservation> factor. Automorphisms that leave the labelled external attachments fixed account for the remaining <symmetry factor>. In two spacetime dimensions the <mass dimensions> are $[\phi]=0$ and $[g_4]=2$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The one-loop <one-particle-irreducible> correction is a <tadpole diagram>: two external lines meet one <quartic interaction> and the other two legs are joined. Interchanging the two ends of its loop gives the <symmetry factor> $2$.

\Image[2023-scalar-diagrams.png]
{title=Original scalar diagrams: the two-dimensional tadpole, charge-conserving light-scalar exchange, and heavy-scalar box amplitudes. Dashed lines carry charge two; solid lines carry charge one.}

Define the <self-energy> by an insertion $-i\Sigma(p^2)$ and a full <scalar propagator> $i/[p^2-m^2-\Sigma(p^2)+i\epsilon]$ before <counterterms>. The supplied <cutoff regularization> integral gives
$$
T_\Lambda:=\int_{|k_E|<\Lambda}\frac{d^2k_E}{(2\pi)^2}\frac1{k_E^2+m^2}
=\frac1{4\pi}\log\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right),
\qquad
-i\Sigma_1=\frac{-ig_4}{2}\int\frac{d^2k}{(2\pi)^2}\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i\epsilon}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\Sigma_1(p^2)=\frac{g_4}{2}T_\Lambda
=\frac{g_4}{8\pi}\log\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right).}
$$
It has no external <momentum> dependence. The correction to the unamputated <two-point correlation function> in <momentum space> is
$$
\Delta G(p)=\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}[-i\Sigma_1]
\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon}
=\frac{i\Sigma_1}{(p^2-m^2+i\epsilon)^2}.
$$
The positive sign of $\Sigma_1$ raises the unrenormalized squared <pole mass> for $g_4>0$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $\phi_B=Z^{1/2}\phi$, $Z=1+\delta_Z$, and define the <mass counterterm> $\delta_m$ as a squared-mass coefficient:
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm ct}=\frac12\delta_Z(\partial\phi)^2-\frac12\delta_m\phi^2,
\qquad \delta_m=Zm_B^2-m^2.
$$
With the <self-energy> convention in <1/b>, the inverse <scalar propagator> is $p^2-m^2+\delta_Zp^2-\delta_m-\Sigma(p^2)$. Requiring the <pole mass> to equal $m$ therefore gives the <on-shell renormalization scheme> condition
$$
\delta_m=m^2\delta_Z-\Sigma(m^2).
$$
At one <loop order>, $\Sigma_1'=0$; hence a unit <pole residue> also gives $\delta_Z^{(1)}=0$, and
$$
\boxed{\delta_m^{(1)}=-\frac{g_4}{8\pi}\log\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right),\qquad
m_B^2=m^2+\delta_m^{(1)}+O(g_4^2).}
$$
The <mass counterterm> <Feynman rule> is $-i\delta_m$, cancelling the <tadpole diagram>. A convention defining a positive mass subtraction by $m_B^2=m^2-\delta_m^{\rm sub}$ instead has $\delta_m^{\rm sub}=-\delta_m$; specifying this convention avoids a spurious sign disagreement.

There is no one-loop <wave-function renormalization> because the <tadpole diagram> is independent of <momentum>. Each one-loop four-point <bubble diagram> has two <scalar propagators>, so its large Euclidean <loop momentum> integral behaves as $\int d^2k_E/k_E^4$ and is <ultraviolet finite>. Thus no divergent <quartic interaction> <counterterm> is required. Finite coupling redefinitions remain possible. At higher <loop orders>, finite <wave-function renormalization> may be needed if unit <pole residue> is imposed; <power counting> only rules out its <ultraviolet divergence>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $I,L,V$ count internal lines, independent loops, and <interaction vertices> of a <connected Feynman diagram>. The graph identity $L=I-V+1$ and the two inverse powers from each <scalar propagator> give its <superficial degree of divergence>:
$$
\boxed{\omega=2L-2I=2(1-V).}
$$
The same formula applies to every connected subgraph. Graphs with $V\geq2$ have negative <superficial degree of divergence>, but this alone does not remove a possible <ultraviolet subdivergence>. A one-vertex subgraph with external legs in the <quartic scalar field theory> can diverge only by a <tadpole diagram>, whose subtraction was fixed in <1/c>. Subtract every such <tadpole diagram>; every remaining loop subgraph then has $V\geq2$ and negative <superficial degree of divergence>. Its <ultraviolet divergence> is absent. Equivalently, <normal ordering> the <quartic interaction> removes all self-contractions at a single <interaction vertex>. Thus **no new divergent field-dependent counterterm structure is needed**, and in a <normal-ordering subtraction scheme in two dimensions> the one-loop mass subtraction exhausts these divergent coefficients.

The literal assertion that there are no further <counterterm> contributions in the <on-shell renormalization scheme> is stronger and generally false. A concrete counterexample is the two-loop <sunset diagram>. After analytic continuation below its three-particle threshold, the magnitude of its <self-energy> at $p^2=m^2$ contains the strictly positive finite integral
$$
\left|\Sigma_{\rm sunset}(m^2)\right|
=\frac{g_4^2}{6(4\pi)^2m^2}
\int_{x,y,z\geq0}dx\,dy\,dz\,
\frac{\delta(1-x-y-z)}{xy+xz+yz-xyz}>0
\quad(g_4\ne0).
$$
The integrable corners do not spoil convergence. Its nonzero value shifts the <pole mass>, requiring an additional *finite* <mass counterterm> if $m$ is to remain the exact <pole mass>; its derivative can likewise change the <pole residue>. These are compatible with the <power counting> result. If <vacuum diagrams> are retained rather than divided out in normalized <correlation functions>, a separate constant <vacuum energy> <counterterm> is also required: the one-vertex double-loop <vacuum diagram> is already divergent. The claim about the mass subtraction concerns field-dependent <counterterms> and normalized <correlation functions>.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

The additional six-leg <interaction vertex> has <Feynman rule> $-ig_6$. Since both interactions lack derivatives, the graph identity still gives $\omega=2(1-V)$, with $V=V_4+V_6$. The difference is that a one-vertex contraction of the <phi-six theory> can leave four external legs: choosing the contracted pair gives $\binom62=15$. Its <quartic interaction> correction is
$$
\frac{g_6}{6!}\,15T_\Lambda\phi^4
=\frac1{4!}\frac{g_6T_\Lambda}{2}\phi^4.
$$
Consequently a one-loop <quartic interaction> <counterterm> is now necessary:
$$
\boxed{\delta g_4^{(1)}=-\frac{g_6}{2}T_\Lambda
=-\frac{g_6}{8\pi}\log\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right).}
$$
The one-loop <mass counterterm> from <1/c> is unchanged. Contracting four legs of a single six-leg <interaction vertex> gives $\binom62\,3=45$ double contractions, hence a two-loop raw <self-energy> $g_6T_\Lambda^2/8$. Its subtraction must include the <quartic interaction> <counterterm> insertion, rather than subtracting this raw expression in isolation.

All divergent field-dependent coefficients are most simply organized by the <normal-ordering subtraction scheme in two dimensions>. With $T=T_\Lambda$, express a potential normal ordered at mass $m$ in ordinary fields:
$$
V_B(\phi)=\frac{g_4}{24}(\phi^4-6T\phi^2+3T^2)
+\frac{g_6}{720}(\phi^6-15T\phi^4+45T^2\phi^2-15T^3).
$$
Thus its divergent subtraction coefficients, in this scheme, are
$$
\boxed{\delta g_4=-\frac{g_6T}{2},\qquad
\delta_m=-\frac{g_4T}{2}+\frac{g_6T^2}{8},\qquad
\delta g_6=\delta_Z=0.}
$$
Indeed the two-loop <self-energy> terms are $g_6T^2/8+\delta g_4T/2=-g_6T^2/8$, which the displayed <mass counterterm> cancels. The constant terms only affect <vacuum energy>. After removing all one-vertex self-contractions, every remaining subgraph is <ultraviolet finite>. Additional finite <on-shell renormalization scheme> adjustments are subject to the same qualification as <1/d>.

=== a

==== Solution

For nonzero real $g$, the continuous <internal symmetry of a classical field theory> is the <global U(1) symmetry>
$$
\phi\longmapsto e^{-i\alpha}\phi,\qquad
\Phi\longmapsto e^{-2i\alpha}\Phi,
\qquad \alpha\text{ constant}.
$$
The <kinetic terms> and mass terms are invariant separately, while the phases in $\phi^2\Phi^\dagger$ cancel. Independent phase rotations would instead require $2\alpha_\phi-\alpha_\Phi=0$ modulo $2\pi$, leaving one connected circle. Simultaneous <complex conjugation> is also a discrete symmetry, reversing the continuous charge; including it gives $U(1)\rtimes\mathbb Z_2\cong O(2)$ for generic parameters. At $g=0$ the continuous symmetry enlarges to $U(1)\times U(1)$.

The <Noether current> with particles assigned positive charges is
$$
\boxed{j^\mu=i\bigl(\phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\phi-(\partial^\mu\phi^\dagger)\phi\bigr)
+2i\bigl(\Phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\Phi-(\partial^\mu\Phi^\dagger)\Phi\bigr).}
$$
For example, put $A=\phi^2\Phi^\dagger$ and $B=\phi^{\dagger2}\Phi$. The <Euler-Lagrange equations> give $\partial_\mu j_\phi^\mu=ig(A-B)$ and $\partial_\mu j_\Phi^\mu=ig(B-A)$, with the factor $2$ already included in $j_\Phi^\mu$. Their sum vanishes. The <Noether charge> $Q=\int d^3x\,j^0$ is conserved and, after <normal ordering>, counts
$$
Q=N_\phi-N_{\bar\phi}+2(N_\Phi-N_{\bar\Phi}).
$$
Thus a $\Phi$ particle carries two units of the same <conserved charge> carried by a $\phi$ particle; their <antiparticles> carry the negatives. The interaction permits conversion of one $\Phi$ particle into two $\phi$ particles when allowed by <conservation of energy>, while preserving this weighted number.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The two <complex scalar fields> have <scalar propagators>
$$
\langle T\phi\phi^\dagger\rangle(p)=\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i\epsilon},\qquad
\langle T\Phi\Phi^\dagger\rangle(p)=\frac{i}{p^2-M^2+i\epsilon};
$$
contractions of two unconjugated fields or two conjugated fields vanish. Draw an arrow on each line in the direction of positive <conserved charge> flow: a solid line carries one unit and a dashed line two units. A particle arrow follows its physical <momentum>; an <antiparticle> arrow opposes it.

There are two conjugate three-leg <interaction vertices>, both with <Feynman rule> $-ig$: one has a charge-two arrow entering and two charge-one arrows leaving, and the other reverses every arrow. These describe the two conjugate terms $\phi^2\Phi^\dagger$ and $\phi^{\dagger2}\Phi$. The two identical $\phi$ attachments cancel the $1/2$ in the <Lagrangian density>. All crossed assignments are obtained by moving external lines between initial and final states while maintaining the arrows. There is no <interaction vertex> with three equal-sign charges.

Each <interaction vertex> conserves <four-momentum>, supplying $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_j)$ with all momenta entering. Integrate each independent <loop momentum> with $d^4k/(2\pi)^4$ and divide by the <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>. No minus sign is assigned to a closed scalar loop. These rules are for $i\mathcal M$ after amputating external lines and removing the overall <four-momentum conservation> factor.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Label the incoming <four-momenta> $p_1,p_2$ and the outgoing ones $p_3,p_4$. Define the <Mandelstam variables> $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$, $t=(p_1-p_3)^2$, $u=(p_1-p_4)^2$. Two incoming $\phi$ particles have <conserved charge> $+2$, so the only <tree-level Feynman diagram> is an $s$-channel $\Phi$ exchange, shown in panel (b) of <1/b>'s figure. Each end converts two charge-one lines into one charge-two line. There are no $t$- or $u$-channel exchanges: an incoming and an outgoing particle at such an end would transfer zero charge, for which this theory has no cubic <interaction vertex>.
$$
i\mathcal M_{\phi\phi}=(-ig)^2\frac{i}{s-M^2+i\epsilon},
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\phi\phi}=-\frac{g^2}{s-M^2+i\epsilon}.}
$$
The single <Feynman diagram> is already symmetric under exchanging the two outgoing particles. This does not add another copy of the <scattering amplitude>; the <identical final-state symmetry factor> enters the integrated <scattering cross-section> instead.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Take $\phi(p_1)+\bar\phi(p_2)\to\phi(p_3)+\bar\phi(p_4)$ with the <Mandelstam variables> of <2/c/i>. The allowed <tree-level Feynman diagram> is the crossed $u$-channel $\Phi$ exchange in panel (c) of <1/b>'s figure. At one end the incoming particle and outgoing <antiparticle> transfer charge $+2$; the other end absorbs that charge. The zero-charge $s$- and $t$-channel assignments are forbidden by the <interaction vertices>. Thus
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\phi\bar\phi}=-\frac{g^2}{u-M^2+i\epsilon}.}
$$
In the <center-of-mass frame> with $|\mathbf p|\ll m$, $s=4m^2+O(|\mathbf p|^2)$ and $u=O(|\mathbf p|^2)$. For $M\gg m$, both <scattering amplitudes> approach $g^2/M^2$. The elastic two-body <Lorentz-invariant phase space> and incident flux agree for the two experiments. Using
$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{|\mathcal M|^2}{64\pi^2s}
$$
for distinguishable final particles, and an extra factor $1/2!$ when integrating the identical-particle final state over the whole sphere, gives
$$
\sigma_{\phi\phi}\simeq\frac{g^4}{32\pi sM^4},\qquad
\sigma_{\phi\bar\phi}\simeq\frac{g^4}{16\pi sM^4},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\sigma_{\phi\phi}}{\sigma_{\phi\bar\phi}}\longrightarrow\frac12.}
$$
There is no additional division for identical incoming particles in the definition of a beam <scattering cross-section>. Before taking $M/m\to\infty$, the zero-<momentum> ratio is $\tfrac12[M^2/(M^2-4m^2)]^2$, provided the threshold is away from the heavy pole.

Near $s=M^2$, the bare $s$-channel <scalar propagator> makes fixed-order <perturbation theory> fail: repeated <self-energy> insertions contain powers of $\Sigma_\Phi(s)/(s-M^2)$. The $\Phi\to\phi\phi$ channel is open for $M>2m$, giving the leading <decay width>
$$
\Gamma_\Phi=\frac{g^2}{32\pi M}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^2}{M^2}}.
$$
Resumming the <self-energy> gives a finite <Breit-Wigner resonance cross section>, with denominator $s-M_{\rm pole}^2+iM_{\rm pole}\Gamma_\Phi$ near the pole, rather than an infinite <scattering cross-section>. Also, $s\simeq M^2$ with $M\gg m$ lies outside the assumed nonrelativistic incoming kinematics; the ratio $1/2$ is not a resonance prediction.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Four external $\Phi$ lines require four cubic <interaction vertices>, since each such <interaction vertex> contains only one heavy field. Their other eight endpoints must form four light internal lines. A <connected Feynman diagram> therefore has $V=I=4$ and $L=1$: the leading contribution is a one-loop <box diagram>, of order $g^4$, with four $\phi$ <scalar propagators>. Charge-two incoming and outgoing heavy lines attach alternately around the box, as shown in panel (d) of <1/b>'s figure. Each incoming heavy line supplies the two outgoing light-charge arrows at its corner, and each outgoing heavy line absorbs them. With external labels fixed there is one such box and its <symmetry factor> is one.

For $|\mathbf p|\ll M\ll m$, the external energies are also small compared with $m$, so expand the loop integrand in all external <four-momenta>. Its leading term is
$$
i\mathcal M_{\Phi\Phi}=g^4\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k^2-m^2+i\epsilon)^4}
+O\left(\frac{i g^4M^2}{m^6}\right).
$$
A <Wick rotation> and the <Euclidean massive loop integral> give
$$
\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k^2-m^2+i\epsilon)^4}
=i\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)^4}
=\frac{i}{96\pi^2m^4}.
$$
Hence, more precisely than just its requested scaling,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\Phi\Phi}=\frac{g^4}{96\pi^2m^4}
\left[1+O\left(\frac{M^2}{m^2},\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{m^2}\right)\right].}
$$
The <mass dimension> $[g]=1$ makes $g^4/m^4$ dimensionless, as a four-dimensional <scattering amplitude> should be. The leading amplitude has no extra identical-particle multiplicity.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Cross the second incoming heavy particle and one outgoing heavy particle in <2/d/i> to obtain $\Phi\bar\Phi\to\Phi\bar\Phi$. Panel (e) of <1/b>'s figure shows the resulting <box diagram>. Its four light-charge arrows again alternate source and sink corners; it is the only connected order-$g^4$ topology. There is no tree exchange because every cubic <interaction vertex> has only one heavy leg. With $|\mathbf p|\ll M\ll m$, crossing does not affect the zero-external-<momentum> loop integral, so
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\Phi\bar\Phi}=\frac{g^4}{96\pi^2m^4}
\left[1+O\left(\frac{M^2}{m^2},\frac{|\mathbf p|^2}{m^2}\right)\right].}
$$
An independent check is to integrate out the <complex scalar field> $\phi$ at constant $\Phi$. Its two real-component squared masses are $m^2\pm g|\Phi|$. The one-loop <quantum effective potential> is therefore
$$
V_1(\Phi)=\frac12\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}
\left[\log(k_E^2+m^2+g|\Phi|)+\log(k_E^2+m^2-g|\Phi|)\right].
$$
Expanding the logarithms to fourth order in $\Phi$ gives
$$
V_1\supset-\frac{g^4|\Phi|^4}{4}\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)^4}
=-\frac{g^4}{384\pi^2m^4}(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2.
$$
Thus the <effective Lagrangian> has the positive local coefficient $g^4/(384\pi^2m^4)$. Its $2!2!$ external attachments give the common <scattering amplitude> $g^4/(96\pi^2m^4)$ in both channels. This also checks the box multiplicity and sign.

=== a

==== Solution

In terms of the <Pauli matrices>, $\gamma^0=\sigma_3$, $\gamma^1=i\sigma_1$, $\gamma^2=i\sigma_2$. The <Pauli matrix multiplication law> implies $\sigma_j^2=I_2$ and $\sigma_j\sigma_k=-\sigma_k\sigma_j$ for $j\ne k$. Consequently
$$
(\gamma^0)^2=I_2,\qquad (\gamma^1)^2=(\gamma^2)^2=-I_2,
\qquad \gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu=-\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\quad(\mu\ne\nu).
$$
These diagonal and off-diagonal cases are exactly the <Clifford algebra> relation
$$
\boxed{\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}I_2.}
$$
The identity matrix is implicit on the right-hand side in the source. Useful conventions for the remaining parts are $(\gamma^0)^\dagger=\gamma^0$, $(\gamma^j)^\dagger=-\gamma^j$ for $j=1,2$, and $\gamma^{0*}=\gamma^0$, $\gamma^{1*}=-\gamma^1$, $\gamma^{2*}=\gamma^2$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

A <parity symmetry in quantum field theory> reverses spatial orientation, so its spatial matrix must lie in the determinant-$-1$ component of the <orthogonal group>. In two spatial dimensions, reversing both coordinates gives $-I_2$, which has determinant $+1$ and is a rotation through $\pi$ in the <special orthogonal group in two dimensions>. It cannot test a discrete orientation-reversing symmetry independent of ordinary rotations. Reflecting one coordinate, for example $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$, has determinant $-1$ and supplies the required <parity symmetry in quantum field theory>.

In three spatial dimensions, $-I_3$ has determinant $-1$, so simultaneous inversion of all three coordinates is the standard choice. A single-coordinate reflection also reverses orientation there, and differs from $-I_3$ by a proper rotation through $\pi$. Thus, in a rotationally invariant theory, it tests the same discrete symmetry. The word *is* in the source specifies conventional representatives: the essential distinction is orientation reversal, rather than a unique choice of axis. Both transformations preserve the ordinary spatial integration measure through the absolute value of their <Jacobian determinants>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Put $x_P=(t,-x,y)$ and $U_P=\gamma^1$. Since $U_P^\dagger=-\gamma^1$ and $\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^0=-\gamma^1$, the <Dirac adjoint> transforms as
$$
\bar\psi'(x)=\psi^\dagger(x_P)(-\gamma^1)\gamma^0
=\bar\psi(x_P)\gamma^1.
$$
The <chain rule> gives $\partial_\mu\psi(x_P)=r_\mu(\partial_\mu\psi)(x_P)$, with $(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(1,-1,1)$ and no sum in this formula. The <Clifford algebra> gives
$$
\gamma^1\gamma^0\gamma^1=\gamma^0,\qquad
\gamma^1\gamma^1\gamma^1=-\gamma^1,\qquad
\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^1=\gamma^2.
$$
Multiplication by $r_\mu$ therefore restores each <kinetic term>:
$$
\bar\psi'i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi'
=\bar\psi(x_P)i\gamma^\mu(\partial_\mu\psi)(x_P).
$$
However, the <Dirac scalar bilinear> changes sign because $(\gamma^1)^2=-I_2$:
$$
\bar\psi'\psi'=-\bar\psi(x_P)\psi(x_P).
$$
Changing variables in the <action> has unit absolute <Jacobian determinant>. Hence
$$
\boxed{S_m\xrightarrow{P}S_{-m}:\quad S_{\rm kin}\to S_{\rm kin},\quad
-m\int\bar\psi\psi\to+m\int\bar\psi\psi.}
$$
A nonzero <Dirac mass term> for this single two-component field breaks <parity symmetry in quantum field theory> with the specified transformation.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

The specified <time reversal> includes complex conjugating $i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu$; omitting that operation changes the answer. Put $x_T=(-t,x,y)$, $U_T=\gamma^2$, and $(r_0,r_1,r_2)=(-1,1,1)$. As in <3/c>, the <Dirac adjoint> in the source's transformed bilinear is $\bar\psi'(x)=\bar\psi(x_T)\gamma^2$. The conjugated <Dirac operator> has coefficient matrices
$$
(i\gamma^0)^*=-i\gamma^0,\qquad
(i\gamma^1)^*=+i\gamma^1,\qquad
(i\gamma^2)^*=-i\gamma^2.
$$
Combining these coefficients with the <chain rule> signs and the <Clifford algebra> gives, without summing over $\mu$,
$$
\gamma^2(i\gamma^\mu)^*\gamma^2r_\mu=i\gamma^\mu
\quad(\mu=0,1,2).
$$
For example the time coefficient is $\gamma^2(-i\gamma^0)\gamma^2(-1)=i\gamma^0$, and the second spatial coefficient is $\gamma^2(-i\gamma^2)\gamma^2=i\gamma^2$. Thus the <kinetic term> is invariant. The real <Dirac mass term> again reverses sign since $\bar\psi'\psi'=\bar\psi(\gamma^2)^2\psi=-\bar\psi\psi$. The spacetime integration has unit absolute <Jacobian determinant>, so
$$
\boxed{T:S_m\mapsto S_{-m},\qquad TP:S_m\mapsto S_m.}
$$
Each operation reverses the sign of the mass, while their composition reverses it twice and preserves the <kinetic term>. In an operator formulation the complex conjugation of coefficients expresses the <antiunitary operator> character of <time reversal>; the transformation must not be treated as an ordinary unitary substitution with all $i$'s held fixed. These statements concern the stated free <action>.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

First conjugate the <Dirac equation>: $(-i\gamma^{\mu*}\partial_\mu-m)\psi^*=0$. A matrix $\Gamma$ taking $\psi^*$ into another solution of the same <Dirac equation> must obey
$$
\gamma^\mu\Gamma=-\Gamma\gamma^{\mu*}.
$$
Because $\gamma^1$ anticommutes with the real $\gamma^0,\gamma^2$ and commutes with the purely imaginary $\gamma^1$, a unitary choice is
$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\gamma^1=i\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\psi_C=\gamma^1\psi^*.}
$$
Any constant overall phase is equally valid. To verify invariance of the <action>, rather than only of its equation, keep the <fermion> exchange signs. Define the <charge-conjugation matrix> $C=\Gamma\gamma^0=\sigma_2$. It obeys
$$
C^T=-C,\qquad C^{-1}=C,\qquad
C^{-1}\gamma^\mu C=-(\gamma^\mu)^T,
\qquad
\psi_C=C\bar\psi^T,\quad \bar\psi_C=-\psi^TC^{-1}.
$$
For a classical <Grassmann field>, interchanging two components contributes a minus sign; equivalently use <normal ordering> for quantum <fermionic operators>. Thus
$$
\bar\psi_C\psi_C=-\psi^T\bar\psi^T=\bar\psi\psi,
$$
and
$$
\bar\psi_Ci\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi_C
=i\psi^T(\gamma^\mu)^T(\partial_\mu\bar\psi)^T
=-i(\partial_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu\psi
=\bar\psi i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi
-i\partial_\mu(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi).
$$
The last term integrates to a boundary term, vanishing with the usual decay or compatible boundary conditions. Both the <kinetic term> and the <Dirac mass term> therefore leave $S$ invariant under <charge conjugation>. Treating the components as commuting numbers would give the wrong mass-bilinear sign; the <fermion> statistics is essential.
