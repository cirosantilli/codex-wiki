The three-dimensional <scalar field theory> in <3> has two spatial dimensions and one time dimension. Results are at the stated order of <perturbative quantum field theory>.

== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

The <momentum-space> <Feynman rules> use the <scalar propagator> and two <Dirac propagators>
$$
\boxed{\Delta_\phi(p)=\frac{i}{p^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
S_\chi(p)=\frac{i(\not p+m)}{p^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
S_\Psi(p)=\frac{i(\not p+M)}{p^2-M^2+i0}.}
$$
The cubic <scalar field> <interaction vertex> is $-i\lambda$. Each of the two flavour-changing <Yukawa coupling> <interaction vertices>, $\phi\bar\Psi\chi$ and $\phi\bar\chi\Psi$, has factor $-ig\,\delta_{ab}$ in <Dirac spinor> indices. A <fermion flow> arrow continues through each <interaction vertex>, but its species changes. There is no $\phi\bar\Psi\Psi$ or $\phi\bar\chi\chi$ <interaction vertex>.

Every <interaction vertex> carries $(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(\sum p)$ with inward <four-momenta>. Integrate each independent <loop momentum> with $d^4\ell/(2\pi)^4$, divide by the <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>, and include a minus sign and a <gamma-matrix trace> for each closed <fermion loop>. The $i0$ is the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription>.

For an amputated <scattering amplitude>, incoming <fermions> supply $u^s(p)$ and outgoing <fermions> supply $\bar u^s(p)$; incoming <antifermions> supply $\bar v^s(p)$ and outgoing <antifermions> supply $v^s(p)$. An external <real scalar field> particle supplies $1$. Use the standard <relativistic normalization of a one-particle state>; the external factors $1/\sqrt{2E}$ belong to that normalization and the <Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure>, rather than an additional factor in the invariant <scattering amplitude>.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Label the external <four-momenta> by $\Psi(p_1)+\chi(p_2)\to\Psi(p_3)+\chi(p_4)$. The only <tree-level Feynman diagram> exchanges a <scalar field> particle between the transitions $\Psi(p_1)\to\chi(p_4)$ and $\chi(p_2)\to\Psi(p_3)$. Its internal <four-momentum> is $q=p_1-p_4=p_3-p_2$. Thus its <scattering amplitude>, with a consistent external-state ordering, is
$$
i\mathcal M=(-ig)^2\,[\bar u_\chi(p_4)u_\Psi(p_1)]\,
\frac{i}{(p_1-p_4)^2-m^2+i0}\,
[\bar u_\Psi(p_3)u_\chi(p_2)].
$$
It is the crossed, or $u$-channel, exchange with these labels. A direct $t$-channel <scalar field> exchange would need a flavour-preserving <Yukawa coupling>, which the <Lagrangian density> does not contain. There is no annihilation <Feynman diagram> for two incoming <fermions>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

For $\Psi(p_1)+\phi(k_1)\to\Psi(p_2)+\phi(k_2)$, there are two <tree-level Feynman diagrams>, both with an internal $\chi$ <fermion>. Their internal <four-momenta> are $p_1+k_1$ and $p_1-k_2$. The <scattering amplitude> is
$$
i\mathcal M=(-ig)^2\bar u_\Psi(p_2)
\left[S_\chi(p_1+k_1)+S_\chi(p_1-k_2)\right]u_\Psi(p_1).
$$
These are the two possible absorption/emission orderings along the <fermion flow>. There is no third <tree-level Feynman diagram> using the cubic <scalar field> <interaction vertex>, since attaching its remaining <scalar field> line to the external $\Psi$ line would require a forbidden $\phi\bar\Psi\Psi$ <interaction vertex>.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

For $\Psi(p_1)\to\chi(p_2)+\phi(p_3)$, the sole <tree-level Feynman diagram> is a single flavour-changing <Yukawa coupling> <interaction vertex>, with $p_1=p_2+p_3$. Its invariant <decay amplitude> is
$$
\boxed{i\mathcal M_{s_1s_2}=-ig\,\bar u_\chi^{s_2}(p_2)u_\Psi^{s_1}(p_1).}
$$
The solid arrow passes from the incoming $\Psi$ to the outgoing $\chi$; the dashed outgoing line carries $p_3$.

==== iv
{scope}

===== Solution

For $\Psi(p_1)\to\chi(p_2)+\phi(k_1)+\phi(k_2)$, the sole <tree-level Feynman diagram> has a <Yukawa coupling> <interaction vertex> producing an off-shell <scalar field> particle of <four-momentum> $q=p_1-p_2=k_1+k_2$, followed by its cubic <scalar field> <interaction vertex>. Its invariant <decay amplitude> is
$$
\boxed{i\mathcal M=(-ig)(-i\lambda)\,
\bar u_\chi(p_2)u_\Psi(p_1)\,
\frac{i}{(k_1+k_2)^2-m^2+i0}.}
$$
Two successive <Yukawa coupling> <interaction vertices> would change the <fermion> species twice and leave a $\Psi$, so they cannot produce this final state. Interchanging the two identical outgoing <bosons> does not create another <Feynman diagram>; the cubic factorial is already included in the <Feynman rule>. The integrated <decay width> instead includes the <identical final-state symmetry factor> $1/2!$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

In the convention of <1/b/iii>, the invariant <decay amplitude> is $\mathcal M_{s_1s_2}=-g\bar u_\chi^{s_2}(p_2)u_\Psi^{s_1}(p_1)$. Including the common overall $i$ in the definition changes no squared <decay amplitude>. An unpolarized ensemble is an incoherent equal mixture of the two initial <spin> states; hence take a <spin average> over $s_1$ and a <spin sum> over the unobserved final $s_2$. Different final <spin> states are orthogonal outcomes, so their probabilities, rather than their <decay amplitudes>, add.

Using the <Dirac adjoint> and the <fermion spin sum> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
&=\frac{g^2}{2}\sum_{s_1,s_2}
(\bar u_2u_1)(\bar u_1u_2)\\
&=\frac{g^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_2+m)(\not p_1+M)]
=2g^2(p_1\cdot p_2+mM).
\end{aligned}
$$
The <gamma-matrix trace> identities used here are $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu)=4\eta^{\mu\nu}$. The <mass-shell conditions> $p_1^2=M^2$ and $p_2^2=p_3^2=m^2$, together with <four-momentum conservation>, imply
$$
m^2=(p_1-p_2)^2=M^2+m^2-2p_1\cdot p_2,
\qquad p_1\cdot p_2=\frac{M^2}{2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^2M(M+2m).}
$$
In the parent's rest frame this also says $E_2=E_3=M/2$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

**The printed integration labels need correction.** The parent's <four-momentum> is fixed, so the two <Lorentz-invariant phase-space measures> must integrate $p_2$ and $p_3$, rather than the printed $p_1$ and $p_2$. Write $P=p_1=(M,\mathbf0)$ and $p=p_2$, $k=p_3$. The physical <decay width> is
$$
\Gamma=\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}{2M}
\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_p}
\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3\,2E_k}
(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(P-p-k).
$$
The spatial <Dirac delta function> sets $\mathbf k=-\mathbf p$. Performing the angular integral in <spherical coordinates>, with $E=\sqrt{p^2+m^2}$, gives the integrated <two-body decay phase space>
$$
\Phi_2=\frac1{4\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{p^2\,dp}{E^2}\delta(M-2E).
$$
The positive root is $p_*=\sqrt{M^2/4-m^2}$. Since $d(2E)/dp=2p/E$, the <Dirac delta function> integration yields $\Phi_2=p_*/(4\pi M)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}\,p_*}{8\pi M^2}
=\frac{g^2(M+2m)}{16\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m^2}{M^2}},\qquad M>2m.}
$$
The two final particles have different species, so there is no <identical final-state symmetry factor>. The result tends to $g^2M/(16\pi)$ as $m\to0$ and vanishes at the two-particle <decay threshold>.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

Put $Q=M-3m$. For $Q<0$ the <decay width> is zero by <conservation of energy>. Just above the three-particle <decay threshold>, all three daughters are nonrelativistic. The <decay amplitude> of <1/b/iv> remains finite: the internal <scalar propagator> denominator approaches $(2m)^2-m^2=3m^2$, and the <Dirac scalar bilinear> does not vanish at rest. Thus, for nonzero fixed $g$ and $\lambda$, the leading suppression comes entirely from <three-body threshold phase space>.

Use the spatial <Dirac delta function> to eliminate one daughter <momentum>. The remaining integral has six independent <momentum> coordinates and one <conservation of energy> constraint,
$$
\int d^3p\,d^3q\,
\delta\!\left(Q-\frac{p^2+q^2+(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)^2}{2m}\right).
$$
Rescaling both <momenta> by $\sqrt{mQ}$ gives $Q^3$ from the measure and $Q^{-1}$ from the <Dirac delta function>. The smooth factors $1/(2E)$ do not change the power. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\Gamma=C(M,g,\lambda)\,(M-3m)^2[1+O((M-3m)/M)]\quad\text{as }m\uparrow M/3,}
$$
with a finite positive leading coefficient when $g\lambda\ne0$; $\Gamma=0$ for $m\geq M/3$. The $1/2!$ for the identical <bosons> changes only this coefficient. Equivalently, at fixed $M$ the opening is quadratic in $M/3-m$. If $g\lambda=0$, this leading <tree-level Feynman diagram> vanishes, so the stated leading coefficient is zero.

== 2
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $A=\mathbf p\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $E=\sqrt{\mathbf p^2+m^2}$. The <Pauli multiplication identity> gives $A^2=\mathbf p^2I_2$, and the orthonormal two-component <spinors> obey $\sum_s\xi^s\xi^{s\dagger}=I_2$. Taking the <Dirac adjoint> of the supplied <Dirac spinor> therefore gives, directly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_su^s(\mathbf p)\bar u^s(\mathbf p)
&=\begin{pmatrix}(E+m)I_2&-A\\ A&-A^2/(E+m)\end{pmatrix}\\
&=\begin{pmatrix}(E+m)I_2&-A\\ A&(m-E)I_2\end{pmatrix}
=\boxed{\not p+m}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the antiparticle deduction, specify the standard normalization $v^{r\dagger}(-\mathbf p)v^s(-\mathbf p)=2E\delta^{rs}$, just as $u^{r\dagger}u^s=2E\delta^{rs}$. **Spanning alone does not specify a normalization:** replacing every $v$ by $2v$ preserves the spanning property but multiplies its proposed <fermion spin sum> by four. The standard normalization is the convention implicit in the <mode expansion of a Dirac field> with the standard <canonical anticommutation relations>.

To use completeness correctly, introduce the <Hermitian> <Dirac Hamiltonian>
$$
h(\mathbf p)=\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf p+\beta m,
\qquad\alpha^i=\gamma^0\gamma^i,\quad\beta=\gamma^0.
$$
The <Dirac equation> makes $u^s(\mathbf p)$ and $v^s(-\mathbf p)$ its <eigenvectors> with <eigenvalues> $E$ and $-E$. Their mutual orthogonality and spanning property give complementary <orthogonal projectors>
$$
\frac1{2E}\sum_su^s(\mathbf p)u^{s\dagger}(\mathbf p)=\frac{E+h(\mathbf p)}{2E},\qquad
\frac1{2E}\sum_sv^s(-\mathbf p)v^{s\dagger}(-\mathbf p)=\frac{E-h(\mathbf p)}{2E}.
$$
Multiply the second equality by $2E\gamma^0$ on the right and replace $\mathbf p$ by $-\mathbf p$. For the positive-energy <four-momentum> $p=(E,\mathbf p)$,
$$
[E-h(-\mathbf p)]\gamma^0
=E\gamma^0-\boldsymbol\gamma\cdot\mathbf p-m=\not p-m,
\qquad\boxed{\sum_sv^s(\mathbf p)\bar v^s(\mathbf p)=\not p-m}.
$$

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The <canonical anticommutation relations> are
$$
\{a_{\mathbf p}^s,a_{\mathbf q}^{r\dagger}\}
=\{b_{\mathbf p}^s,b_{\mathbf q}^{r\dagger}\}
=(2\pi)^3\delta^{sr}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf p-\mathbf q),
$$
with the mixed and equal-type <anticommutators> zero. Insert the <mode expansion of a Dirac field>. The phases cancel at equal time, and <2/a> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\psi_\alpha(t,\mathbf y),\psi_\beta^\dagger(t,\mathbf x)\}
&=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,e^{i\mathbf p\cdot(\mathbf y-\mathbf x)}
\frac{\sum_s[u^s_\alpha(\mathbf p)u^{s*}_\beta(\mathbf p)
+v^s_\alpha(-\mathbf p)v^{s*}_\beta(-\mathbf p)]}{2E}\\
&=\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y).
\end{aligned}
$$
Since $\bar\psi\gamma^0=\psi^\dagger$, the requested componentwise answer is
$$
\boxed{\{(i\bar\psi\gamma^0)_\alpha(t,\mathbf x),\psi_\beta(t,\mathbf y)\}
=i\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf x-\mathbf y).}
$$
This is the <equal-time canonical anticommutator of a Dirac field>: the <canonical momentum> conjugate to $\psi_\alpha$ is $\pi_\alpha=i\psi_\alpha^\dagger$. It is the fermionic counterpart of the canonical field <canonical commutation relation>, ensuring the correct local <canonical quantization>. For this first-order <Lagrangian density>, the conjugate variables are constrained; the displayed relation is the standard result after handling that constraint. It does not assert a nonzero <anticommutator> between the fields at separated spatial points.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Take a spatial <Fourier transform> of the <mode expansion of a Dirac field>:
$$
\widetilde\psi(t,\mathbf p)=\int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\psi(t,\mathbf x)
=\frac1{\sqrt{2E}}\sum_r
\left[b_{-\mathbf p}^{r\dagger}v^r(-\mathbf p)e^{iEt}
+a_{\mathbf p}^ru^r(\mathbf p)e^{-iEt}\right].
$$
The <orthogonality> relations $u^{s\dagger}(\mathbf p)u^r(\mathbf p)=2E\delta^{sr}$ and $u^{s\dagger}(\mathbf p)v^r(-\mathbf p)=0$ isolate the <fermionic annihilation operator>. Projecting onto $v^s(-\mathbf p)$ instead isolates the <fermionic creation operator>:
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
a_{\mathbf p}^s&=\frac{e^{iEt}}{\sqrt{2E}}u^{s\dagger}(\mathbf p)
\int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\psi(t,\mathbf x),\\
b_{-\mathbf p}^{s\dagger}&=\frac{e^{-iEt}}{\sqrt{2E}}v^{s\dagger}(-\mathbf p)
\int d^3x\,e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}\psi(t,\mathbf x).
\end{aligned}}
$$
The opposite time phases and reversed antiparticle <momentum> are essential. Both denominators are $\sqrt{2E}$, because the <Dirac spinors> have norm squared $2E$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Read the unsuperscripted $a_{\mathbf p}$ in the printed <Hamiltonian> as $a_{\mathbf p}^s$: the repeated <spin> label is otherwise missing. Relabel $\mathbf p\mapsto-\mathbf p$ in its antiparticle term, and insert both projections from <2/c>. Keeping the original operator ordering gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
H&=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,
\widetilde\psi^\dagger(t,\mathbf p)
\left[\frac12\sum_s\left(u^s(\mathbf p)u^{s\dagger}(\mathbf p)
-v^s(-\mathbf p)v^{s\dagger}(-\mathbf p)\right)\right]
\widetilde\psi(t,\mathbf p)\\
&=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}\,
\widetilde\psi^\dagger(t,\mathbf p)
(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf p+\beta m)
\widetilde\psi(t,\mathbf p).
\end{aligned}
$$
The difference of the two <orthogonal projectors> in <2/a> is $h(\mathbf p)/E$, explaining the cancellation of the factors $E$. Under inverse <Fourier transformation>, $\mathbf p$ becomes $-i\boldsymbol\nabla$ acting on $\psi$. Hence
$$
\boxed{H=\int d^3x\,\mathcal H,\qquad
\mathcal H=\psi^\dagger(-i\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla+\beta m)\psi
=\bar\psi(-i\gamma^i\partial_i+m)\psi.}
$$
Spatial indices run from $1$ to $3$. This is the local <Hamiltonian density> of the free <Dirac field>. It also follows from $\mathcal H=\pi\dot\psi-\mathcal L$ with $\pi=i\psi^\dagger$.

The original antiparticle term is $-bb^\dagger$, so the result retains its infinite additive <vacuum energy>. If the <Hamiltonian> is <normal ordered>, its occupation-number expression becomes $\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3\,E\sum_s(a^{s\dagger}a^s+b^{s\dagger}b^s)$ and its local expression is $:\!\mathcal H\!:$, with the same subtraction. The displayed unsymmetrized <Hamiltonian density> is <Hermitian> after spatial integration under vanishing boundary terms; a manifestly <Hermitian> local form replaces the derivative term by $-\tfrac i2\psi^\dagger\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\overleftrightarrow{\boldsymbol\nabla}\psi$ and differs by a spatial <divergence>.

== 3
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Here three means the total spacetime dimension. A dimensionless <action> requires $[\mathcal L]=3$ in <mass dimensions>. The <kinetic term> gives $2+2[\phi]=3$, so $[\phi]=1/2$. Matching the <mass dimensions> of both <field interaction terms> gives
$$
\boxed{[g_1]=3-4[\phi]=1,\qquad [g_2]=3-6[\phi]=0.}
$$
Thus $g_1$ has units of <mass> and $g_2$ is dimensionless. The quartic interaction is superrenormalizable by <power counting>, and $g_2$ is a <marginal coupling> by <power counting>.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $L$ be the number of independent <loop momenta> in a connected <Feynman diagram>. Every <scalar propagator> falls as $k^{-2}$, and every <loop momentum> integration contributes three powers, so $\omega=3L-2e$. Counting line ends and using connectedness gives
$$
4n_4+6n_6=2e+n,\qquad L=e-n_4-n_6+1.
$$
Eliminating $L$ and $e$ yields
$$
\boxed{\omega=3(e-n_4-n_6+1)-2e=3-\frac n2-n_4.}
$$
Thus increasing the sextic <interaction vertex> count does not worsen the <superficial degree of divergence>; adding a quartic <interaction vertex> improves it. Only finitely many external-line structures can have an overall <ultraviolet divergence>, even though arbitrarily high <loop order> is possible.

After subtracting <ultraviolet subdivergences>, locality and <Lorentz invariance> restrict the divergent <counterterms> to a constant <vacuum energy density>, a <mass counterterm>, a <wave-function renormalization>, and quartic and sextic <field interaction terms>. Odd powers are forbidden by the $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ <Z2 symmetry>. In particular, $n=2$ permits a constant and $p^2$, $n=4$ permits only a constant, and $n=6$ permits only a constant. This is perturbative <renormalizability>, not a guarantee that every superficially allowed divergence actually occurs or that every unrenormalized graph with $\omega<0$ lacks divergent subgraphs.

All lines represent the same <real scalar field>. The lower row labels the number of parallel connecting lines; a small closed line at a vertex is a <tadpole diagram>.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

With a single quartic <interaction vertex>, the two unused line ends close into one <tadpole diagram>. With a single sextic <interaction vertex>, the four unused ends pair into two <tadpole diagrams>. These are the only one-vertex <one-particle-irreducible> <Feynman diagrams> for the <two-point function>.

Define the Minkowski <Feynman integrals>
$$
D(k)=\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i0},\qquad
T_M=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}D(k).
$$
The amputated <two-point function> contributions are
$$
\boxed{C_4(-ig_1)T_M,\qquad C_6(-ig_2)T_M^2,}
$$
where $C_4,C_6$ are the reciprocals of the corresponding <Feynman-diagram symmetry factors>. Both are independent of external <momentum>, and hence affect the <mass counterterm> rather than <wave-function renormalization>. No numerical <Feynman-diagram symmetry factors> are needed here.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

To include all momentum-dependent contributions to the <two-point function>, put one external line at each <interaction vertex>. Let $r$ be the number of lines connecting the vertices, and $t_A,t_B$ their numbers of <tadpole diagrams>. For vertex valences $v_A,v_B\in\{4,6\}$,
$$
r+2t_A=v_A-1,\qquad r+2t_B=v_B-1.
$$
The integer $r$ is odd, and $r=1$ gives a <one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram> and is excluded. Thus the complete list is

* $v_A=v_B=4$: $r=3$, $t_A=t_B=0$, the <sunset diagram>.
* $v_A=4,v_B=6$: $r=3$, $t_A=0,t_B=1$, the <sunset diagram> with one <tadpole diagram>.
* $v_A=v_B=6$: $r=3$, $t_A=t_B=1$, the <sunset diagram> with one <tadpole diagram> at each vertex.
* $v_A=v_B=6$: $r=5$, $t_A=t_B=0$, the <five-line banana integral> topology.

The coefficient of the mixed graph includes the reflected external attachment as well as its reciprocal <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>. If both external lines attach to the same <interaction vertex>, no external <momentum> enters the connecting internal lines, and that <Feynman diagram> cannot contribute to <wave-function renormalization>.

For incoming external <momentum> $p$, assign the connecting lines <momenta> $k_1,\ldots,k_{r-1},p-\sum_jk_j$. Define
$$
B_{r,M}(p)=\int\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}\frac{d^3k_j}{(2\pi)^3}
\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r-1}D(k_j)\right]D\!\left(p-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}k_j\right),\qquad r=3,5.
$$
The <momentum-space> <Feynman rules> give, in the order listed,
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
&C_{44}(-ig_1)^2B_{3,M}(p),\\
&C_{46}(-ig_1)(-ig_2)T_MB_{3,M}(p),\\
&C_{66,3}(-ig_2)^2T_M^2B_{3,M}(p),\\
&C_{66,5}(-ig_2)^2B_{5,M}(p).
\end{aligned}}
$$
Each $C$ is a reciprocal <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>, including any equivalent external attachments. There are respectively $2,3,4,4$ independent <loop momenta>.

**If “contribute to wavefunction renormalization” means requiring a divergent kinetic counterterm after subtraction of all subdivergences, only the last topology qualifies.** The first three still have nontrivial external <momentum> dependence and give finite contributions to a normalization condition; their raw divergent factors arise from <mass counterterms> and <interaction vertex> <counterterms>. Keeping all four here makes this distinction explicit. The <ultraviolet divergence> analysis in <3/d> establishes it directly.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

There are **three independent nonfactorized <momentum integrals>**, namely a <tadpole integral>, the three-line <sunset diagram> integral, and the <five-line banana integral>. The six displayed <Feynman diagrams> involve **six complete integral expressions**:
$$
\boxed{T,\quad T^2,\quad B_3(P),\quad T B_3(P),\quad T^2B_3(P),\quad B_5(P).}
$$
The distinction between independent building blocks and their products removes an ambiguity in counting “different momentum integrals”. For the strict divergent-<wave-function renormalization> interpretation of <3/c/ii>, the graphs in <3/c> reduce to $T,T^2,B_5$, comprising **three full integral expressions and two independent nonfactorized building blocks**.

Make a <Wick rotation> and use $m>0$ to separate <ultraviolet divergences> from <infrared divergences>. Write $D_E(k)=1/(k^2+m^2)$, and let $T,B_r(P)$ be the Euclidean versions of the integrals above, with their harmless overall factors of $i$ removed. A spherical <ultraviolet cutoff> gives
$$
T_\Lambda=\frac1{2\pi^2}\int_0^\Lambda\frac{k^2\,dk}{k^2+m^2}
=\frac1{2\pi^2}\left[\Lambda-m\arctan(\Lambda/m)\right]
=\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi^2}-\frac{m}{4\pi}+O(\Lambda^{-1}).
$$
Thus the one-vertex quartic graph is linearly divergent, and the one-vertex sextic graph is quadratically divergent as $T_\Lambda^2$.

For the other two building blocks, the spatial <Fourier transform> is especially transparent. The three-dimensional Euclidean <scalar propagator> is
$$
G(x)=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac{e^{ik\cdot x}}{k^2+m^2}
=\frac{e^{-m|x|}}{4\pi|x|}.
$$
The <convolution theorem> converts the $r$ parallel lines into $G(x)^r$. Angular integration gives, with a short-distance cutoff $a=\Lambda^{-1}$,
$$
B_r^{(a)}(P)=\frac{4\pi}{(4\pi)^r}\int_a^\infty
\rho^{2-r}e^{-rm\rho}\frac{\sin(P\rho)}{P\rho}\,d\rho,
\qquad P=|P_E|.
$$
This is a different convenient <cutoff regularization> for the connected block; power-divergent coefficients depend on the cutoff convention, whereas the conclusions about powers and the presence of a kinetic logarithm do not. The <sinc function> is understood to be $1$ at $P=0$.

For $r=3$, the integrand starts as $\rho^{-1}+O(1)$, so
$$
B_3^{(a)}(P)=\frac1{16\pi^2}\log\frac1{ma}+O(1).
$$
Its divergent part is independent of external <momentum>. In particular,
$$
B_3(P)-B_3(0)=\frac1{16\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-3m\rho}}\rho
\left[\frac{\sin(P\rho)}{P\rho}-1\right]d\rho
=-\frac{P^2}{864\pi^2m^2}+O(P^4).
$$
The finite difference and its finite derivative show why the pure three-line <sunset diagram> does not need a divergent <wave-function renormalization> in three dimensions.

For $r=5$, the small-distance <Taylor expansion> is
$$
\rho^{-3}e^{-5m\rho}\frac{\sin(P\rho)}{P\rho}
=\rho^{-3}-5m\rho^{-2}
+\left(\frac{25m^2}{2}-\frac{P^2}{6}\right)\rho^{-1}+O(1).
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{B_5^{(a)}(P)\big|_{\mathrm{div}}
=\frac1{256\pi^4}\left[\frac1{2a^2}-\frac{5m}{a}
+\left(\frac{25m^2}{2}-\frac{P^2}{6}\right)\log\frac1{ma}\right].}
$$
In particular, the nonzero $P^2\log\Lambda$ term requires a genuine divergent <wave-function renormalization>, in addition to the <mass counterterm>. The coefficients here refer to the displayed position-space cutoff and have not included <Feynman-diagram symmetry factors> or <bare couplings>.

The comparison with the <superficial degree of divergence> is
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{vertices and integral}&(n_4,n_6)&\omega&\text{leading unrenormalized growth}\\\hline
4:\ T&(1,0)&1&\Lambda\\
6:\ T^2&(0,1)&2&\Lambda^2\\
4,4:\ B_3&(2,0)&0&\log\Lambda\\
4,6:\ TB_3&(1,1)&1&\Lambda\log\Lambda\\
6,6:\ T^2B_3&(0,2)&2&\Lambda^2\log\Lambda\\
6,6:\ B_5&(0,2)&2&\Lambda^2
\end{array}
$$
Thus the actual leading power agrees with $\omega$ in every case. The additional logarithms in products come from <ultraviolet subdivergences>, which uniform <power counting> alone does not resolve.

For $TB_3$ and $T^2B_3$, differentiation with respect to $P^2$ removes the logarithmic divergence of $B_3$, but leaves divergent factors $T$ or $T^2$ multiplying a finite, nonpolynomial function of $P^2$. These are subdivergent contributions, not new nonlocal <counterterms>: subtracting each <tadpole diagram> subdivergence and the momentum-independent $B_3$ subdivergence removes them. For example, in a factorized subtraction scheme $TB_3\mapsto(T-T_{\mathrm{div}})(B_3-B_{3,\mathrm{div}})$, and likewise for $T^2B_3$. The remaining $B_5$ kinetic logarithm explains the strict interpretation in <3/c/ii>. A <dimensional regularization> scheme can suppress power divergences; it does not invalidate this cutoff-based comparison with $\omega$.
