== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Treat $\phi$ and $\phi^*$ as independent variables in the <Euler-Lagrange equations>. Write $s=\phi^*\phi$, and assume the <scalar potential> $f(s)$ is real and differentiable. Variation with respect to $\phi^*$ gives
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^*}=-f'(s)\phi,\qquad
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^*)}=\partial^\mu\phi.
$$
Consequently the equations for the <complex scalar field> and its <complex conjugate> are
$$
\boxed{\Box\phi+f'(\phi^*\phi)\phi=0,\qquad
\Box\phi^*+f'(\phi^*\phi)\phi^*=0.}
$$
No polynomial assumption is needed: only the first derivative of the <scalar potential> enters. For $f(s)=m^2s$, both reduce to the free <Klein-Gordon equation>.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For a constant real parameter, $\partial_\mu(e^{i\alpha}\phi)=e^{i\alpha}\partial_\mu\phi$. The phases therefore cancel separately in the <kinetic term> and in $\phi^*\phi$, leaving any $f(\phi^*\phi)$ unchanged. This is the <global phase symmetry of a complex scalar field>, with <circle group> $U(1)$.

Use infinitesimal variations per unit parameter, $\delta\phi=i\phi$, $\delta\phi^*=-i\phi^*$. The <Lagrangian density> is exactly invariant, so no total-derivative correction is needed in <Noether's theorem>. The <Noether current> is
$$
\boxed{J^\mu=i(\partial^\mu\phi^*)\phi-i\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi.}
$$
It depends only on derivatives of the <Lagrangian density> with respect to <field> derivatives, so the derivative-free <scalar potential> cannot alter its form. Direct differentiation cancels the mixed first-derivative terms:
$$
\partial_\mu J^\mu=i(\Box\phi^*)\phi-i\phi^*\Box\phi
=-if'(s)\phi^*\phi+if'(s)\phi^*\phi=0.
$$
Thus it is conserved on solutions of the <Euler-Lagrange equations>. Its spatial integral is a conserved <Noether charge> when the <flux> through the boundary vanishes. Its overall sign is fixed here by $\delta\phi=+i\phi$; reversing the orientation of the phase transformation reverses the current.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $\int_{\mathbf p}=\int d^3p/(2\pi)^3$. The <conjugate momentum> <fields> are $\pi_\phi=\dot\phi^\dagger$ and $\pi_{\phi^\dagger}=\dot\phi$. Inserting the given <mode expansion of a free field> into $i\int d^3x(\dot\phi^\dagger\phi-\phi^\dagger\dot\phi)$ produces spatial <Dirac delta> factors. The $b^\dagger c^\dagger$ and $cb$ terms vanish after integration: they contain $\omega_{\mathbf p}-\omega_{\mathbf q}$ on $\delta^3(\mathbf p+\mathbf q)$, and $\omega_{-\mathbf p}=\omega_{\mathbf p}$. The number-conserving terms instead add their two frequency factors, cancelling the mode normalization. The un-subtracted ordered expression is
$$
Q_{\mathrm{raw}}=\int_{\mathbf p}\left(c_{\mathbf p}c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger b_{\mathbf p}\right)
=\int_{\mathbf p}\left(c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger c_{\mathbf p}-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger b_{\mathbf p}\right)
+\int_{\mathbf p}(2\pi)^3\delta^3(0).
$$
The last term is an infinite <vacuum> constant, not a finite physical <vacuum> charge. Choosing the <Fock vacuum> to have zero <Noether charge>, equivalently using <normal ordering>, gives
$$
\boxed{Q=\int_{\mathbf p}\left(c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger c_{\mathbf p}-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger b_{\mathbf p}\right)=N_c-N_b.}
$$
A state containing $n_c$ quanta created by $c^\dagger$ and $n_b$ by $b^\dagger$ has <Noether charge> $n_c-n_b$. Thus the two families describe opposite unit <Noether charges>; with this source's current the $c^\dagger$ quantum has positive <Noether charge> and the $b^\dagger$ quantum negative <Noether charge>. This is an internal <Noether charge>; identifying it with <electric charge> requires a specified electromagnetic <coupling>. The <vacuum subtraction of a complex scalar charge> leaves all <commutators> unchanged.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[Section 2.5, complex scalar fields and their opposite-charge oscillator families; the phase convention fixes the overall sign]{ref}

**Scope of the expansion:** a fixed-frequency expansion with time-independent oscillator coefficients is the free-field or asymptotic-field form. For arbitrary interacting $f$, it can be used as canonical oscillator coordinates at a chosen time, rather than as the exact interacting time dependence. For example, $f(s)=m^2s+\lambda s^2$ gives $(\Box+m^2)\phi+2\lambda\phi^*\phi\phi=0$, which a generic free plane-wave superposition does not satisfy. The <Noether charge> and its generator <action> remain valid in the interacting theory; the <symmetry> does not depend on using a free <Hamiltonian>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

The two oscillator families commute with each other. Their <canonical commutation relations> give
$$
[Q,b_{\mathbf p}]=+b_{\mathbf p},\qquad
[Q,c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger]=+c_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,
\qquad
[Q,b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger]=-b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,\qquad
[Q,c_{\mathbf p}]=-c_{\mathbf p}.
$$
Both terms in the <mode expansion of a free field> therefore acquire the same factor, giving
$$
\boxed{[Q,\phi(x)]=\phi(x),\qquad [Q,\phi^\dagger(x)]=-\phi^\dagger(x).}
$$
The <Noether charge> is the generator of the internal <symmetry>. In the active-operator convention $U(\alpha)=e^{i\alpha Q}$, repeated <commutators> give
$$
\boxed{U(\alpha)\phi(x)U(\alpha)^{-1}=e^{i\alpha}\phi(x).}
$$
The sign agrees with part b. A negative sign for $[Q,\phi]$ would correspond to the opposite current convention, not to the current used here. Subtracting a multiple of the identity from $Q$ to fix the <vacuum> <Noether charge> has no effect on this generator relation.

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

In $d$ <spacetime> dimensions, $[d^dx]=-d$ and a dimensionless <action> requires $[\mathcal L]=d$. Since $[\partial_\mu]=1$, the canonical <kinetic term> fixes $2+2[\phi]=d$. The cubic and quartic terms then give
$$
\boxed{[\phi]=\frac{d-2}{2},\qquad[\kappa]=\frac{6-d}{2},\qquad[\lambda]=4-d.}
$$
These brackets denote <mass dimensions>. The cubic <coupling> is dimensionless at $d=6$, whereas the quartic <coupling> is dimensionless at $d=4$. Hence there is **no single dimension** in which both nonzero <couplings> are dimensionless. For example, at $d=4$ the cubic <coupling> still has <mass dimension> one.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[Section 3.1, dimensional analysis of scalar interactions]{ref}

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $D_{xy}=\langle0|T\phi(x)\phi(y)|0\rangle_0$ be the free <Feynman propagator>, and abbreviate $v_3=-i\kappa$, $v_4=-i\lambda$. Expand the normalized <Dyson series>, so <vacuum> bubbles cancel between numerator and denominator, and retain the <connected correlation function>. <Wick's theorem> yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
G_c(x,y)={}&D_{xy}
+\frac{v_4}{2}\int d^dz\,D_{xz}D_{zz}D_{zy}\\
&+\frac{v_3^2}{2}\int d^dz\,d^dw\,D_{xz}D_{zw}^2D_{wy}\\
&+\frac{v_3^2}{2}\int d^dz\,d^dw\,D_{xz}D_{zy}D_{zw}D_{ww}
+O(\text{two loops}).
\end{aligned}
$$
These are respectively the free line, the quartic <tadpole diagram>, the two-cubic-vertex <bubble diagram>, and an <tadpole contribution to a cubic-theory two-point function>, attached by a bridge to the vertex carrying both external legs. The last graph is connected but is a <one-particle-reducible Feynman diagram>; removing its bridge disconnects it. It must be included when all connected graphs are requested. It disappears if an additional vanishing-one-point-function <renormalization condition> and its corresponding <tadpole subtraction> are imposed; that convention was not specified here.

The factors can be counted directly. The quartic term has $4\cdot3=12$ attachments for the two labelled external legs, divided by $4!=24$. At second order in the cubic interaction the expansion denominator is $2!(3!)^2=72$. There are 36 <Wick contractions> for the bubble, and 36 for the attached-tadpole graph, giving $1/2$ for each. Thus each loop topology shown has <symmetry factor> $S=2$. Disconnected products of one-point functions and <vacuum> bubbles are not part of $G_c$.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[Sections 3.3–3.4, Wick contractions and diagrammatic perturbation theory]{ref}

The <one-loop cubic-quartic scalar vertex classification> follows without truncating arbitrarily in powers of the <couplings>. A connected graph with $E$ external legs, $V_3$ cubic vertices, $V_4$ quartic vertices and loop number $L$ satisfies
$$
V_3+2V_4=E+2L-2.
$$
For $E=2,L=1$, the only possibilities are $(V_3,V_4)=(2,0)$ or $(0,1)$, exactly those just listed.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For the amputated <one-particle-irreducible vertex>, every internal bridge is forbidden. Define the <Feynman integrals>
$$
D(k)=\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i0},\qquad \int_k=\int\frac{d^dk}{(2\pi)^d},
$$
$$
I_2(P)=\int_kD(k)D(k+P),\qquad
I_3(P,Q,R)=\int_kD(k)D(k+P)D(k-R),\quad P+Q+R=0.
$$
For $E=3,L=1$, the counting identity in part b gives $V_3+2V_4=3$. The only <one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams> are a triangle of three cubic vertices and a two-propagator bubble joining one cubic and one quartic vertex. Write $\mathcal V_3$ for the amputated Feynman vertex, including its overall factor of $i$. With all external <momenta> incoming,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal V_{3,\,1\text{-loop}}(p_1,p_2,p_3)
=v_3^3 I_3(p_1,p_2,p_3)
+\frac{v_3v_4}{2}\sum_{i=1}^3 I_2(p_i).}
$$
The triangle has $S=1$ for labelled external legs. The mixed bubble has $S=2$ because its two internal lines may be interchanged. There are three distinct terms, according to which external leg is attached to the cubic vertex; the other two attach to the quartic vertex. No tadpole attached by a bridge is <one-particle irreducible>.

The requested position-space three-point <correlation function> is obtained by restoring the external <propagators> and a <Fourier transform>:
$$
G^{(3)}_{\mathrm{1PI},\,1\text{-loop}}(x,y,z)
=\int_{p_1}\int_{p_2}\int_{p_3}(2\pi)^d\delta^d(p_1+p_2+p_3)
 e^{-i(p_1x+p_2y+p_3z)}D(p_1)D(p_2)D(p_3)
\mathcal V_{3,\,1\text{-loop}}.
$$
The tree-level contribution would replace the last factor by $v_3$. This explicitly distinguishes the amputated vertex from the <correlation function> while giving all loop integrations in <momentum> space.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $\mathcal V_4=i\Gamma^{(4)}$ denote the amputated Feynman vertex, with tree term $v_4=-i\lambda$. A one-loop <one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram> has $V_3+2V_4=4$, allowing three families: two quartic vertices, two cubic vertices and one quartic vertex, or four cubic vertices. Define
$$
I_4(P,Q,R,S)=\int_kD(k)D(k+P)D(k+P+Q)D(k-S),\quad P+Q+R+S=0.
$$
Using $I_2,I_3$ from part c and writing $P_{ij}=p_i+p_j$, the complete one-loop result is
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal V_{4,\,1\text{-loop}}={}&\frac{v_4^2}{2}
\left[I_2(P_{12})+I_2(P_{13})+I_2(P_{14})\right]\\
&+v_3^2v_4\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}I_3(P_{ij},p_k,p_l)\\
&+v_3^4\left[I_4(p_1,p_2,p_3,p_4)+I_4(p_1,p_2,p_4,p_3)+I_4(p_1,p_3,p_2,p_4)\right].
\end{aligned}}
$$
In each term of the middle sum, $k,l$ are the two complementary indices. Their order does not change the integral after a loop-<momentum> redefinition.

The first line contains three bubble channels, one for each partition of the four labelled external legs into two pairs. Each has $S=2$ from interchanging its two identical internal lines. In the second line, the quartic vertex carries a chosen pair of external legs, giving $\binom42=6$ mixed triangles. Each has $S=1$. In the last line, cyclic orderings of four labelled external legs are identified under rotation and reversal, leaving $(4-1)!/2=3$ boxes, again with $S=1$ each. The figure in part b shows one representative of each family and its multiplicity. External-leg corrections and tadpoles connected by bridges are excluded because $\Gamma^{(4)}$ is a <one-particle-irreducible vertex>. All integrals are left unevaluated, as appropriate here.

== 3
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

A free contraction must connect one $\phi$ and one $\phi^*$ <field>. In <momentum> space,
$$
\boxed{\langle T\phi(p)\phi^*(-p)\rangle_0=\frac{i}{p^2-m_0^2+i0},\qquad
\langle T\phi\phi\rangle_0=\langle T\phi^*\phi^*\rangle_0=0.}
$$
Represent the nonzero <propagator> by a directed line so the two conjugate ends remain distinguishable; an undirected line alone would incorrectly allow same-charge contractions. Choose one consistent arrow convention, for example pointing from the starred end to the unstarred end.

The interaction contains two identical $\phi$ <fields> and two identical $\phi^*$ <fields>. Their $2!2!$ assignments cancel the coefficient $1/4$, giving
$$
\boxed{v_{\phi\phi\phi^*\phi^*}=-i\lambda_0.}
$$
The vertex has two incoming and two outgoing arrows. Include $(2\pi)^d\delta^d(\sum p_i)$ at every vertex, integrate $d^dk/(2\pi)^d$ for each independent loop, and divide by the oriented graph's <symmetry factor>. There is no fermion-loop sign.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The two real <fields> have canonical normalization because
$$
\phi^*\phi=\frac{\phi_1^2+\phi_2^2}{2},\qquad
\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^2\partial_\mu\phi_a\partial^\mu\phi_a.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=\frac12\sum_{a=1}^2\left[(\partial\phi_a)^2-m_0^2\phi_a^2\right]
-\frac{\lambda_0}{16}\left(\phi_1^4+2\phi_1^2\phi_2^2+\phi_2^4\right).}
$$
The interaction is invariant under $O(2)$ rotations and reflections of $(\phi_1,\phi_2)$. The continuous rotations are the original complex-field $U(1)$ <symmetry>. The factor $1/16$ follows from both the original $1/4$ and the square of the $1/2$ in $\phi^*\phi$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The <propagator> carries a real-species label rather than a charge-flow arrow:
$$
\boxed{D_{ab}(p)=\frac{i\delta_{ab}}{p^2-m_0^2+i0}.}
$$
Differentiating the interaction with respect to its four external real <fields> gives the compact <tensor> rule
$$
\boxed{v_{abcd}=-\frac{i\lambda_0}{2}
(\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}+\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}+\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}).}
$$
Thus four $\phi_1$ legs or four $\phi_2$ legs give $-3i\lambda_0/2$, while two of each give $-i\lambda_0/2$. Vertices with an odd number of either species vanish. For example, $4!(\lambda_0/16)=3\lambda_0/2$, whereas $2!2!(2\lambda_0/16)=\lambda_0/2$. These factors already account for assignments at one vertex; graph <symmetry factors> must still be included separately. <Momentum> conservation and loop measures are the same as in part a.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Use renormalized <propagators> of <mass> $m$ and tree vertex $-ig\mu^\epsilon$. The interaction <counterterms> have Feynman factors $-i\delta_{m^2}$ for a two-leg <mass> insertion and $-i\mu^\epsilon g\delta_g$ for a four-leg insertion. At one loop the two-point <one-particle-irreducible vertex> is
$$
i\Gamma^{(2)}(p)=i(p^2-m^2)-i\delta_{m^2}
+(-ig\mu^\epsilon)\int_k\frac{i}{k^2-m^2+i0}+O(g^2).
$$
The directed tadpole has $S=1$: interchanging its two ends would reverse the <field> labels, so the real-scalar tadpole's factor $1/2$ does not apply. It is independent of external <momentum>, so no wave-function <counterterm> is required at this order.

For the four-point vertex, label the two unstarred incoming <fields> by $1,2$ and the starred ones by $3,4$, with all <momenta> incoming. The tree contact, the three oriented bubbles and the contact <counterterm> give
$$
\begin{aligned}
i\Gamma^{(4)}={}&-ig\mu^\epsilon-i\mu^\epsilon g\delta_g\\
&+(-ig\mu^\epsilon)^2\left[\frac12 I_2(p_1+p_2)+I_2(p_1+p_3)+I_2(p_1+p_4)\right]+O(g^3).
\end{aligned}
$$
The first bubble has two equivalent arrows along the same direction between its vertices, giving $S=2$. In the other two channels the arrows oppose one another, and interchanging the lines does not preserve their oriented attachments, giving $S=1$. This is the <oriented complex-scalar quartic graph factors> rule.

The <mass> tadpole is order $gm^2$, while the four-point loop is order $g^2$. In the multiplicative <coupling> convention of the source,
$$
\boxed{\delta_{m^2}=O(gm^2/\epsilon),\qquad \delta_g=O(g/\epsilon),\qquad g\delta_g=O(g^2/\epsilon).}
$$
The distinction between $\delta_g$ and the whole additive four-leg coefficient $g\delta_g$ matters for the requested order counting.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

**Source correction:** equation 11 on the original page has $\Gamma(\alpha-1/2)$. The dimensionally regulated integral instead contains $\Gamma(\alpha-d/2)$. This is not an interchangeable convention: for $\alpha=2$ and $d=4-\epsilon$, the correct factor $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)$ has a logarithmic <ultraviolet pole>, whereas the printed $\Gamma(3/2)$ is finite.

To obtain the correct expression, shift the <loop momentum> and perform a <Wick rotation>. For positive Euclidean $\mathcal M^2$, introduce a <Schwinger parameterization>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{d^dq_E}{(2\pi)^d}\frac1{(q_E^2+\mathcal M^2)^\alpha}
&=\frac1{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_0^\infty ds\,s^{\alpha-1}e^{-s\mathcal M^2}
\int\frac{d^dq_E}{(2\pi)^d}e^{-sq_E^2}\\
&=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha-d/2)}{(4\pi)^{d/2}\Gamma(\alpha)}(\mathcal M^2)^{d/2-\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The derivation first holds in the convergent range and defines the result elsewhere by <analytic continuation>. For integer $\alpha$, rotating back gives
$$
\boxed{\int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac1{(q^2+2q\cdot p-m^2+i0)^\alpha}
=\frac{i(-1)^\alpha}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha-d/2)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
(p^2+m^2)^{d/2-\alpha},}
$$
with the branch inherited from $i0$. This is the Lorentzian continuation of the <Euclidean massive loop integral>.

Set $p=0$, $\alpha=1$, $d=4-\epsilon$. Since $\Gamma(-1+\epsilon/2)=-2/\epsilon+O(1)$,
$$
\int_k\frac1{k^2-m^2+i0}=\frac{im^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}+O(1).
$$
Consequently the directed tadpole in part d gives
$$
\left.i\Gamma^{(2)}_{\mathrm{loop}}\right|_{\mathrm{div}}
=(-ig\mu^\epsilon)\left.i\int_k\frac1{k^2-m^2+i0}\right|_{\mathrm{div}}
=\frac{igm^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
The <counterterm> is $-i\delta_{m^2}$, so cancelling only the pole in the <minimal subtraction scheme> requires
$$
\boxed{\delta_{m^2}=\frac{gm^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}.}
$$
This is the <quartic complex-scalar mass renormalization> result. The scale factors and logarithms contribute finite terms at this order and do not change the displayed pole coefficient. A real-field check gives a tadpole coefficient $\tfrac12(3g/2+g/2)=g$, in agreement with the oriented complex-field computation.

=== f
{scope}

==== Solution

To first order, invert $m_0^2=m^2[1+g/(8\pi^2\epsilon)]$ and use $g=\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}+O(\lambda_0^2)$:
$$
\boxed{m^2=m_0^2\left[1-\frac{\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}}{8\pi^2\epsilon}\right]+O(\lambda_0^2).}
$$
The remainder is a perturbative statement at fixed regulator. When differentiating, hold the <bare mass> $m_0^2$ fixed as well as $\lambda_0$; otherwise the <mass> running would not be defined by a fixed bare theory. Since $\mu\,\partial_\mu\mu^{-\epsilon}=-\epsilon\mu^{-\epsilon}$,
$$
\mu\frac{d\log m^2}{d\mu}\bigg|_{\mathrm{bare}}
=\frac{\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}}{8\pi^2}+O(g^2)
=\frac{g}{8\pi^2}+O(g^2).
$$
Thus, with the source's extra factor of one half,
$$
\boxed{\gamma_m(g)=\frac{g}{16\pi^2}+O(g^2).}
$$
The factor $-\epsilon$ from the running dimensionless <bare coupling> cancels the <counterterm> pole. Differentiating as though $g$ were independent of $\mu$ would miss this finite result. Some conventions call $\mu\,d\log m^2/d\mu$ itself the <mass> <anomalous dimension>; that convention is twice the one defined here.

== 4
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

At fixed external <momenta>, fixed regulator and fixed bare <coupling>, define the total scale derivative
$$
\mathcal D=\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta(g)\frac{\partial}{\partial g}.
$$
The bare vertex has no independent dependence on the arbitrary <renormalization scale>. Differentiating $\Gamma^{(n)}=Z_\phi^{n/2}\Gamma_0^{(n)}$ therefore gives
$$
\mathcal D\Gamma^{(n)}=\frac n2\left(\mu\frac{d\log Z_\phi}{d\mu}\right)_{\mathrm{bare}}\Gamma^{(n)}.
$$
Defining the two scale-response functions as in the source yields
$$
\boxed{\left[\mu\partial_\mu+\beta(g)\partial_g-\frac n2\gamma(g)\right]\Gamma^{(n)}=0.}
$$
This is the <Callan-Symanzik equation>. Its minus sign and factor $n/2$ follow from the specified $Z_\phi^{n/2}$ multiplying the bare vertex. In particular, the source's $\gamma=\mu\,d\log Z_\phi/d\mu$ is twice the <field> <anomalous dimension> defined using $\log Z_\phi^{1/2}$. A convention change must be made consistently in both definitions and equation.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The dimensionless bare parameter is $g_0=\lambda_0\mu^{-\epsilon}$, so direct differentiation at fixed $\lambda_0$ gives
$$
\boxed{\beta_0(g_0)=\mu\frac{dg_0}{d\mu}\bigg|_{\lambda_0}=-\epsilon g_0.}
$$
The dimensionful bare vertex itself is scale independent. Reexpressing it as $\widehat\Gamma^{(n)}(p,g_0,\mu)=\Gamma_0^{(n)}(p,g_0\mu^\epsilon)$ introduces only the combination $g_0\mu^\epsilon$. Hence
$$
\left(\mu\partial_\mu-\epsilon g_0\partial_{g_0}\right)\widehat\Gamma^{(n)}=0.
$$
There is no multiplicative <field> rescaling in this bare parametrization, so
$$
\boxed{\gamma_0(g_0)=0.}
$$
This does not assert that the interacting renormalized <field> has zero <anomalous dimension>. It says that the unrescaled bare <field> has no additional normalization factor under this change of the arbitrary scale.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $\mathcal D_0=\mu\partial_\mu+\beta_0(g_0)\partial_{g_0}$. Allow a general bare coefficient $\gamma_0$ temporarily, so the starting equation is $(\mathcal D_0-n\gamma_0/2)\widehat\Gamma^{(n)}=0$. Substitute $\widehat\Gamma^{(n)}=Z_\phi(g_0)^{-n/2}\Gamma^{(n)}(p,g(g_0),\mu)$ and apply the product and chain rules:
$$
0=Z_\phi^{-n/2}\left[
\mu\partial_\mu+\beta_0\frac{dg}{dg_0}\partial_g
-\frac n2\left(\gamma_0+\beta_0\frac{d\log Z_\phi}{dg_0}\right)\right]\Gamma^{(n)}.
$$
Multiplying by $Z_\phi^{n/2}$ identifies
$$
\boxed{\beta(g(g_0))=\beta_0(g_0)\frac{dg}{dg_0},\qquad
\gamma(g(g_0))=\gamma_0(g_0)+\frac{\beta_0(g_0)}{Z_\phi(g_0)}\frac{dZ_\phi}{dg_0}.}
$$
Thus the <renormalization-group beta function> transforms by the <chain rule> under a <coupling> reparametrization, while the <field> coefficient also records the scale dependence of the finite or divergent <field> normalization. For the actual bare parametrization here, substitute $\beta_0=-\epsilon g_0$ and $\gamma_0=0$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

At fixed regulator abbreviate
$$
A(\epsilon)=\frac{a_1}{\epsilon}+a_2+a_3\epsilon+\cdots,\qquad
C(\epsilon)=\frac{z_1}{\epsilon}+z_2+z_3\epsilon+\cdots.
$$
The <coupling> expansion is $g=g_0+Ag_0^2+O(g_0^3)$, whose inverse is $g_0=g-Ag^2+O(g^3)$. Using part c before removing the regulator,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta(g)&=-\epsilon g_0(1+2Ag_0)+O(g_0^3)\\
&=-\epsilon g-\epsilon A g^2+O(g^3),\\
\gamma(g)&=-\epsilon g_0\frac{C}{1+Cg_0}+O(g_0^2)
=-\epsilon Cg+O(g^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore the regulator-free coefficients at the orders determined by the supplied expansions are
$$
\boxed{\beta(g)=-a_1g^2+O(g^3),\qquad\gamma(g)=-z_1g+O(g^2).}
$$
The canonical term $-\epsilon g$ vanishes, and the factors of $\epsilon$ cancel the simple poles. The finite coefficients $a_2,z_2$ and higher positive powers of $\epsilon$ do not enter these leading regulator-free terms. Inverting the <coupling> is essential: substituting $g_0=g$ too early would incorrectly give $-2a_1g^2$.

The calculation establishes finiteness only at the displayed perturbative orders. Higher-order pole cancellations require the higher-order coefficients, as the next part makes explicit.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

Retain the specified leading poles and write $A=a_1/\epsilon$, $B=b_1/\epsilon^2$. Solving $g=g_0+Ag_0^2+Bg_0^3$ recursively gives
$$
g_0=g-Ag^2+(2A^2-B)g^3+O(g^4).
$$
The scale derivative must again be reexpressed in the <renormalized coupling>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\beta(g)&=-\epsilon g_0\left(1+2Ag_0+3Bg_0^2\right)\\
&=-\epsilon g-\epsilon A g^2+2\epsilon(A^2-B)g^3+O(g^4)\\
&=\boxed{-\epsilon g-a_1g^2+\frac{2(a_1^2-b_1)}{\epsilon}g^3+O(g^4).}
\end{aligned}
$$
The <leading-pole consistency under coupling reparametrization> shows that a finite <renormalization-group beta function> requires the two-loop double-pole consistency condition
$$
\boxed{b_1=a_1^2.}
$$
The leading two-loop pole is fixed by the one-loop simple pole; it is not an independent parameter. This is an instance of the <simple-pole formulas for minimal-subtraction renormalization group functions> and their higher-pole consistency relations.

The source's leading-pole truncation does **not** fix the finite two-loop coefficient of the <beta function>. For example, adding the omitted term $c_1g_0^3/\epsilon$ leaves the double-pole condition unchanged but contributes $-2c_1g^3$ to the finite <beta function>. Thus cancelling the displayed $1/\epsilon$ term is not a claim that the physical two-loop running vanishes.
