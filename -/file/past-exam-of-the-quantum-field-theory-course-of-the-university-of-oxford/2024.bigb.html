Use <natural units> $\hbar=c=1$ and the mostly-minus <Minkowski metric>. Question 3 has two spatial dimensions: its <gamma matrices> are the specified two-by-two matrices. The printed $\sigma^\mu$ in its <kinetic term> is interpreted as those $\gamma^\mu$, rather than as three unmodified <Pauli matrices>. All <spin sums> in question 2 instead use four-component <Dirac spinors> in four spacetime dimensions.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Write $\int_{\mathbf p}=\int d^3\mathbf p/(2\pi)^3$. The <canonical commutation relations> and the product rule for a <commutator> give
$$
[a_{\mathbf p},a_{\mathbf q}^\dagger a_{\mathbf q}]=(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)a_{\mathbf q},\qquad
[b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger,b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger b_{\mathbf q}]=-(2\pi)^3\delta^3(\mathbf p-\mathbf q)b_{\mathbf q}^\dagger.
$$
The other-species terms vanish. Integrating the <Dirac delta distributions> in the <Hamiltonian> therefore gives
$$
[a_{\mathbf p},H]=E_p a_{\mathbf p},\qquad [b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger,H]=-E_p b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger,
$$
where $E_{-p}=E_p$. Consequently the <complex scalar field> satisfies
$$
[\phi(t,\mathbf x),H]=\int_{\mathbf p}\frac{E_p}{\sqrt{2E_p}}\left(-b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger e^{iE_pt}+a_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_pt}\right)e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}.
$$
Direct <differentiation> of the time factors gives the same expression, proving the <Heisenberg equation of motion>
$$
\boxed{i\partial_t\phi(t,\mathbf x)=[\phi(t,\mathbf x),H].}
$$

= Solution
{parent=b}

Both a <particle> and its <antiparticle> carry the <momentum> of their mode label. Thus the <normal-ordered> total <momentum operator> is
$$
\boxed{\mathbf P=\int_{\mathbf p}\mathbf p\left(a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}+b_{\mathbf p}^\dagger b_{\mathbf p}\right).}
$$
The <canonical commutation relations> give
$$
[a_{\mathbf p},\mathbf P]=\mathbf p\,a_{\mathbf p},\qquad
[b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger,\mathbf P]=-(-\mathbf p)b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger=\mathbf p\,b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger.
$$
The minus sign in the <antiparticle> mode label is essential: both terms in the printed <mode expansion of a free field> contain $e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}$. Hence
$$
[\phi,\mathbf P]=\int_{\mathbf p}\frac{\mathbf p}{\sqrt{2E_p}}\left(b_{-\mathbf p}^\dagger e^{iE_pt}+a_{\mathbf p}e^{-iE_pt}\right)e^{i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x}
=\boxed{-i\nabla\phi.}
$$

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

For a small real time displacement $\epsilon$, the <unitary time evolution> operator is $T_t(\epsilon)=1-i\epsilon H+O(\epsilon^2)$. The corresponding <Heisenberg-picture> field transformation is conjugation by its inverse:
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_t(\epsilon)^\dagger\phi(t,\mathbf x)T_t(\epsilon)
&=\phi+i\epsilon[H,\phi]+O(\epsilon^2)\\
&=\phi+\epsilon\partial_t\phi+O(\epsilon^2)
=\boxed{\phi(t+\epsilon,\mathbf x)+O(\epsilon^2).}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here <1/a> gives $[H,\phi]=-i\partial_t\phi$.

Likewise let $T_s(\mathbf a)=1+i\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf P+O(|\mathbf a|^2)$, with the paper's sign for its <spatial translation operator>. From <1/b>,
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_s(\mathbf a)^\dagger\phi(t,\mathbf x)T_s(\mathbf a)
&=\phi+i\mathbf a\cdot[\phi,\mathbf P]+O(|\mathbf a|^2)\\
&=\phi+\mathbf a\cdot\nabla\phi+O(|\mathbf a|^2)
=\boxed{\phi(t,\mathbf x+\mathbf a)+O(|\mathbf a|^2).}
\end{aligned}
$$
These are precisely the first-order <Taylor expansions>. Since $H$ and $\mathbf P$ commute in this translation-invariant theory, the finite result is
$$
\boxed{\phi(x)=e^{iP\cdot x}\phi(0)e^{-iP\cdot x},\qquad P\cdot x=Ht-\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf x.}
$$

Choose the zero of <energy> so that the translation-invariant <vacuum state> has $P^\mu|\Omega\rangle=0$. A <one-particle state> of <rest mass> $m_\psi$ obeys the <relativistic energy-momentum relation>
$$
\boxed{p^\mu=(E_{\mathbf p},\mathbf p),\qquad E_{\mathbf p}=\sqrt{m_\psi^2+|\mathbf p|^2},\qquad p^2=m_\psi^2.}
$$
The <translation covariance of a quantum field> in <1/c> remains valid for the full interacting <four-momentum operator>. Acting its <exponential functions> on the two <momentum eigenstates> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\Omega|\phi(x)|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle
&=\langle\Omega|e^{iP\cdot x}\phi(0)e^{-iP\cdot x}|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle\\
&=\boxed{e^{-ip\cdot x}\langle\Omega|\phi(0)|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle.}
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $p\cdot x=E_{\mathbf p}t-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf x$. A different additive <vacuum energy> must be subtracted consistently from both <energy eigenvalues> before identifying the physical $p^0$.

= Solution
{parent=e}

Assume $m_\psi>0$, as required to have a <rest frame>. Choose the <Lorentz boost> $\Lambda$ for which $\Lambda p=(m_\psi,\mathbf0)$, and write its <unitary operator> as $U$. The stipulated <one-particle state> normalization gives $U|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle=|\psi,\mathbf0\rangle$ without a momentum-dependent prefactor. A <Lorentz scalar> at the origin satisfies $U\phi(0)U^\dagger=\phi(0)$, and the <vacuum state> satisfies $U|\Omega\rangle=|\Omega\rangle$. Inserting $U^\dagger U$ therefore proves
$$
\boxed{\langle\Omega|\phi(0)|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle=\langle\Omega|\phi(0)|\psi,\mathbf0\rangle.}
$$
With a noncovariant normalization of <momentum eigenstates>, the assumed boost law would instead contain a square-root energy factor, so this equality is specifically tied to the paper's assumption.

Let $q^\mu=(E_{\mathbf q}^{\chi},\mathbf q)$ denote the <four-momentum> of the other <energy eigenstate>. Translation covariance first gives
$$
\langle\chi,\mathbf q|\phi(x)|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle
=e^{i(q-p)\cdot x}\langle\chi,\mathbf q|\phi(0)|\psi,\mathbf p\rangle
=e^{i(q-p)\cdot x}\langle U\chi,\mathbf q|\phi(0)|\psi,\mathbf0\rangle.
$$
Thus a suitable ket, including the phase whose complex conjugate appears in its bra, is
$$
\boxed{|\Psi\rangle=e^{-i(q-p)\cdot x}U|\chi,\mathbf q\rangle.}
$$
Here $\langle U\chi,\mathbf q|$ means $(U|\chi,\mathbf q\rangle)^\dagger$. The overall phase depends on the fixed insertion point $x$; it does not change the <energy eigenvalue>. It cannot simply be dropped if the equality of complex <matrix elements> is to hold exactly.

The transformed state has <four-momentum> $q'=\Lambda q$. <Lorentz invariance> of the <Minkowski inner product> gives
$$
p\cdot q=(\Lambda p)\cdot(\Lambda q)=m_\psi q'^0,
\qquad
\boxed{E^\Psi=\frac{p\cdot q}{m_\psi}=\frac{E_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf q}^{\chi}-\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf q}{m_\psi}.}
$$
There is no boost to a <rest frame> for a nonzero massless <four-momentum>, so the rest-state statements require the positive-mass assumption.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Using <Feynman slash notation>, the symmetric tensor $p_\mu p_\nu$ kills the antisymmetric <commutator> of the <gamma matrices>. The <Clifford algebra> gives
$$
\boxed{\not p\,\not p=\tfrac12p_\mu p_\nu\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=p^2I.}
$$
Consequently $(\not p)^{2r}=(p^2)^rI$ and $(\not p)^{2r+1}=(p^2)^r\not p$. For a scalar-coefficient <polynomial> $F(z)=\sum_nc_nz^n$, split its even and odd powers:
$$
\boxed{F(\not p)=A(p^2)\not p+B(p^2)I,\quad A(z)=\sum_rc_{2r+1}z^r,\quad B(z)=\sum_rc_{2r}z^r.}
$$
This reduction assumes scalar coefficients; arbitrary noncommuting matrix coefficients would not give the stated scalar functions $A,B$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Every positive-energy <Dirac spinor> obeys $(\not p-m)u^s(\mathbf p)=0$. Therefore the <fermion spin sum> satisfies
$$
(\not p-m)P=\sum_s[(\not p-m)u^s]\bar u^s=0.
$$
Since $\bar u^s=u^{s\dagger}\gamma^0$ and $(\gamma^0)^2=I$, the <trace> of each outer product is its contracted scalar. The prescribed normalization and the two <spin> states yield
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}(P\gamma^0)=\sum_su^{s\dagger}u^s=2(2E_p)=4E_p.}
$$
Now make exactly the stated <polynomial> assumption and apply <polynomial reduction of a slashed vector>. By <2/a>, on the <mass shell> $p^2=m^2$ write $P=A\not p+BI$. Then
$$
0=(\not p-m)P=(Am^2-mB)I+(B-mA)\not p.
$$
The <matrices> $I$ and $\not p$ are linearly independent for a physical nonzero $p$, so $B=mA$. The <gamma matrix trace identities>, $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^0=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^0)=4\eta^{\mu0}$, give
$$
4E_p=\operatorname{tr}[A(\not p+m)\gamma^0]=4AE_p.
$$
Thus $A=1$ and
$$
\boxed{P(\mathbf p)=\not p+m.}
$$
No explicit representation of the <Dirac spinors> has been used. The same reasoning covers $m=0$ provided $p^0>0$.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Interpret the repeated species label in the <Yukawa interaction> as summed over $i=1,2$: each <Feynman vertex> is diagonal in species and equals $-ig$. Define the <invariant scattering amplitude> by $S-1=i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(P_f-P_i)\mathcal M$. There is one <tree-level Feynman diagram>, an $s$-channel scalar exchange. In the left panel, $j=2$ for this part; the right panel only occurs in <2/d>. Arrows show fermion-number flow, so an <antifermion> arrow opposes its physical <momentum>.

\Image[2024-yukawa-scattering.png]
{title=Labelled scalar-exchange annihilation and elastic diagrams, with arrows following fermion-number flow}

Writing $k=p_1+p_2=p_3+p_4$ and $s=k^2$, the momentum-space <Feynman rules> give
$$
i\mathcal M=[\bar v^{s_2}(p_2)(-ig)u^{s_1}(p_1)]\frac{i}{s-M^2+i0}[\bar u^{s_3}(p_3)(-ig)v^{s_4}(p_4)],
\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal M=-\frac{g^2[\bar v_2u_1][\bar u_3v_4]}{s-M^2+i0}.}
$$
The scalar carries no <spin> label. Average over the $2\times2$ incoming <spin> states and sum the four outgoing combinations. For the real scalar <Yukawa interaction>, the conjugate of $\bar v_2u_1$ is $\bar u_1v_2$. The <fermion spin sums> then yield
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac{g^4}{4(s-M^2)^2}\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_2-m)(\not p_1+m)]\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_3+m)(\not p_4-m)].
$$
The bar denotes the initial <spin average>, not a <Dirac adjoint>. The <gamma matrix trace identities> give
$$
\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_2-m)(\not p_1+m)]=4(p_1\cdot p_2-m^2)=2(s-4m^2),
$$
and the second <trace> has the same value because $p_3+p_4=p_1+p_2$ and all external masses are $m$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^4\frac{(s-4m^2)^2}{(s-M^2)^2}=g^4\left(\frac{4m^2-s}{M^2-s}\right)^2.}
$$
This is the fixed-order expression away from $s=M^2$. If that <pole> is accessible, a physical <scattering resonance> requires a scalar <self-energy> and <decay width>; the tree formula alone is not finite there.

= Solution
{parent=d}

For a single species both panels of the figure in <2/c> contribute, now with $j=1$. They are the $s$-channel annihilation and $t$-channel scalar exchange, where the <Mandelstam variables> are $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$, $t=(p_1-p_3)^2$, $u=(p_1-p_4)^2$ and $s+t+u=4m^2$. Fix the external-state order consistently with <2/c>. The exchange of identical external <fermionic operators> gives a relative minus sign:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M=-g^2\left(\frac{(\bar v_2u_1)(\bar u_3v_4)}{s-M^2+i0}-\frac{(\bar u_3u_1)(\bar v_2v_4)}{t-M^2+i0}\right).}
$$
Changing the convention for ordering all external states can change the overall sign, but not this relative <fermionic sign>.

The exchange $p_1\leftrightarrow-p_4$ must include <crossing symmetry> the associated external <spinors>, as well as continuing the <four-momenta>. It interchanges $s$ and $t$, leaves $u$ fixed, and interchanges the two spinor pairings. Hence the analytically continued <invariant scattering amplitude> is antisymmetric:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M\longmapsto-\mathcal M,\qquad \overline{|\mathcal M|^2}(s,t)=\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}(t,s).}
$$
The first relation is understood with consistently chosen spinor crossing phases. It is not a substitution of negative-energy arguments into the original positive-energy $u$ spinors without changing external states.

Without repeating the <spin sum> calculation, <2/c> fixes the square of the first diagram. <Crossing symmetry> fixes the second by $s\leftrightarrow t$. Consequently the strongest immediate inference is
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^4\left[\frac{(s-4m^2)^2}{(s-M^2)^2}+\frac{(t-4m^2)^2}{(t-M^2)^2}+\frac{C(s,t)}{(s-M^2)(t-M^2)}\right],\quad C(s,t)=C(t,s).}
$$
The <interference> function is not determined by the separate diagram squares. In particular **adding the two squares and dropping <interference> is unjustified**. The <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> applied to the arrays of spin amplitudes also gives $|C(s,t)|\leq2|(s-4m^2)(t-4m^2)|$ on the physical scattering region.

For completeness, its value can be checked with one contracted <trace>, without another diagram calculation. The cross product is
$$
K=\frac14\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_2-m)(\not p_1+m)(\not p_3+m)(\not p_4-m)]
=-\frac12st-2m^2(s+t)+8m^4.
$$
The relative minus in the <invariant scattering amplitude> gives $C=-2K$. Thus the fully evaluated result, beyond the symmetry inference alone, is
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^4\left[\frac{(s-4m^2)^2}{(s-M^2)^2}+\frac{(t-4m^2)^2}{(t-M^2)^2}+\frac{st+4m^2(s+t)-16m^4}{(s-M^2)(t-M^2)}\right].}
$$
Every term respects the inferred $s,t$ symmetry. In the massless limit the <interference> numerator reduces to $st$, which is nonzero at a generic scattering angle.

= Solution
{parent=a}

In $2+1$ spacetime dimensions, the dimensionless <action> gives $[\mathcal L]=3$ and $[\partial_\mu]=1$. The scalar <kinetic term> requires $2[\phi]+2=3$, whereas the fermion <kinetic term> requires $2[\psi]+1=3$. Thus the <engineering dimensions of relativistic fields> are
$$
[\phi]=\frac12,\qquad[\psi]=[\bar\psi]=1.
$$
Matching each interaction to <mass dimension> three gives
$$
\boxed{[g_1]=3-(2[\phi]+2[\psi])=0,\quad[g_2]=3-4[\phi]=1,\quad[g_3]=3-6[\phi]=0.}
$$
Here the brackets mean exponents of mass in <natural units>. The coefficients $g_1,g_3$ are <marginal couplings> by classical <power counting>, and $g_2$ is a <relevant coupling>.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Let $I_b,I_f$ count internal scalar and fermion lines and $L$ count independent loops in a connected amputated <Feynman diagram>. At large <loop momentum>, the <scalar propagator> falls as $k^{-2}$ and the <Dirac propagator> falls as $k^{-1}$, including its numerator. Each loop integration is $d^3k$. The nonderivative <interaction vertices> add no powers. Therefore
$$
\omega=3L-2I_b-I_f.
$$
Count endpoints of each kind of line and use the connected-graph <loop order> identity:
$$
2I_b+b=2n_1+4n_2+6n_3,\qquad
2I_f+f=2n_1,\qquad
L=I_b+I_f-(n_1+n_2+n_3)+1.
$$
Eliminating $L,I_b,I_f$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega&=I_b+2I_f-3(n_1+n_2+n_3)+3\\
&=\left(n_1+2n_2+3n_3-\frac b2\right)+2\left(n_1-\frac f2\right)-3(n_1+n_2+n_3)+3\\
&=\boxed{3-\frac b2-f-n_2.}
\end{aligned}
$$
The cancellation of $n_1,n_3$ reflects their dimensionless couplings; every insertion of the positive-dimensional $g_2$ improves the <superficial degree of divergence> by one.

The printed exact homogeneity is shorthand for ultraviolet <power counting>, not an identity for a massive integrated <correlation function>. For example, even the free amputated scalar two-point vertex $\Gamma^{(2)}(p)=p^2-M^2$ obeys $\Gamma^{(2)}(\lambda p)=\lambda^2p^2-M^2$, which differs from $\lambda^2\Gamma^{(2)}(p)$ when $M\ne0$. <Logarithmic divergences>, actual numerator cancellations and <ultraviolet subdivergences> also require separate treatment.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Use <one-particle-irreducible correlation functions> to identify independent local <counterterms>. The exact internal symmetry $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ excludes odd $b$, and <Dirac fermion number conservation> requires equal numbers of $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$, so $f$ is even. From <3/b>, the complete list of potentially overall divergent vertices with external legs is:

* $\Gamma_{\phi\phi}$: $(b,f)=(2,0)$, $\omega=2-n_2$, potentially divergent for $n_2\leq2$.
* $\Gamma_{\phi^4}$: $(b,f)=(4,0)$, $\omega=1-n_2$, potentially divergent for $n_2\leq1$.
* $\Gamma_{\phi^6}$: $(b,f)=(6,0)$, $\omega=-n_2$, potentially divergent only for $n_2=0$.
* $\Gamma_{\bar\psi\psi}$: $(b,f)=(0,2)$, $\omega=1-n_2$, potentially divergent for $n_2\leq1$.
* $\Gamma_{\phi^2\bar\psi\psi}$: $(b,f)=(2,2)$, $\omega=-n_2$, potentially divergent only for $n_2=0$.

The zero-leg <vacuum diagrams>, with $\omega=3-n_2$, may also require a <vacuum energy density> subtraction. Four-fermion vertices have $\omega\leq-1$ and do not require a new four-fermion <counterterm>. These are superficial bounds: a particular allowed diagram can vanish or be convergent.

Locality and <Lorentz invariance> restrict the divergent parts to a finite basis. The scalar two-point vertex can contain a constant and $p^2$; there is no <Lorentz scalar> linear in a single <momentum>. The fermion two-point vertex can contain $I$ and $\not p$. The remaining listed vertices have no allowed derivative divergence. A complete <counterterm Lagrangian>, up to <total derivatives>, is therefore
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\rm ct}={}&-\delta\Lambda+\frac{\delta Z_\phi}{2}(\partial_\mu\phi)(\partial^\mu\phi)-\frac{\delta M^2}{2}\phi^2
+\delta Z_\psi\bar\psi i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi-\delta m\bar\psi\psi\\
&-\frac{\delta g_1}{2}\phi^2\bar\psi\psi-\frac{\delta g_2}{4!}\phi^4-\frac{\delta g_3}{6!}\phi^6.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The coefficients supply <wavefunction renormalization>, <mass counterterms> and renormalizations of the three interactions already present. Recursive subtraction of <ultraviolet subdivergences> then leaves only these local overall divergences. This finite <counterterm closure> is why the complete theory is expected to have perturbative <renormalizability>. It does not mean every listed coefficient first becomes nonzero at one loop, or that setting an allowed coupling to zero is stable under <renormalization>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Set the renormalized $g_3$ to zero. For a six-scalar vertex, <3/b> gives $\omega=-n_2$, so a new overall <logarithmic divergence> can only occur in graphs with $n_2=0$. With $b=6,f=0$, the line counts become $I_f=n_1$ and $I_b=n_1-3$, hence $L=n_1-2$.

At one loop this forces $n_1=3$ and $I_b=0$: the only candidate is a closed <triangle Feynman diagram> with two external scalar legs at each vertex. Its numerator at large <loop momentum> is proportional to
$$
\operatorname{tr}[(\not k)^3]=k^2\operatorname{tr}(\not k)=0.
$$
This vanishes for the specified two-component <gamma matrices>, each of which is traceless. Replacing one leading $\not k$ by the fermion mass or an external <momentum> lowers the <superficial degree of divergence> from zero to at most $-1$. Thus the apparent logarithm cancels. Graphs with a $g_2$ vertex already have negative overall degree; the pure-scalar one-loop triangle, for instance, has $\omega=-3$. There is no one-loop <ultraviolet subdivergence> inside these proper one-loop vertices. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\delta g_3^{\text{one loop}}=0.}
$$
The six-point <correlation function> need not vanish: finite terms are allowed. Nor is it correct to say that all odd <gamma matrix traces> vanish in $2+1$ dimensions. In this representation,
$$
\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2=-iI_2,\qquad\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2)=-2i\ne0.
$$
The cancellation above follows from repeating the same leading <loop momentum>, not from a false four-dimensional trace rule.

At two loops, $n_1=4$, $I_f=4$ and $I_b=1$ are possible. A closed fermion box with a scalar line joining two of its vertices leaves six external scalar legs. It has $L=2$, $\omega=0$, and four leading <gamma matrices>; the one-loop odd-numerator argument no longer applies.

A background-field check demonstrates that its logarithm is actually nonzero. Put $\phi=v+\eta$ with constant $v$. Define the background fermion mass $\mu=m+g_1v^2/2$, the background scalar squared mass $\mathcal B^2=M^2+g_2v^2/2$, and the induced three-leg <Yukawa coupling> $y=g_1v$. The two-loop <quantum effective potential> contains the <sunset diagram> with two fermion lines and one scalar line. After a <Wick rotation>, its relevant integral, apart from a nonzero overall combinatorial factor, is
$$
y^2\int\frac{d^3k\,d^3l}{(2\pi)^6}\frac{2(-k\cdot l+\mu^2)}{(k^2+\mu^2)(l^2+\mu^2)((k-l)^2+\mathcal B^2)}.
$$
Here the dots and <gamma matrix traces> are Euclidean. With $A=k^2+\mu^2$, $B=l^2+\mu^2$, $C=(k-l)^2+\mathcal B^2$, its numerator is exactly
$$
2(-k\cdot l+\mu^2)=-A-B+C+4\mu^2-\mathcal B^2.
$$
The first three terms reduce to products of one-loop <tadpole integrals>, which have no logarithmic pole near three dimensions in <dimensional regularization>. The remaining term is
$$
y^2(4\mu^2-\mathcal B^2)H,\qquad H=\int\frac{d^3k\,d^3l}{(2\pi)^6}\frac1{ABC}.
$$
For positive regulator masses, the large-momentum region of $H$ has a positive, nonzero logarithmic divergence. One can see this directly by restricting the relative angles and the ratio $|k|/|l|$ to compact intervals away from zero: the remaining common radial integral is proportional to $\int^\Lambda dr/r$. Its proper one-loop subgraphs are ultraviolet convergent.

Finally, the coefficient multiplying $H$ contains
$$
y^2(4\mu^2-\mathcal B^2)=g_1^2v^2\left[4m^2- M^2+\left(4mg_1-\frac{g_2}{2}\right)v^2+g_1^2v^4\right].
$$
The <two-loop sextic counterterm in quartic Yukawa theory> follows because its $v^6$ coefficient is $g_1^4\ne0$ for a nonzero coupling. Other two-loop vacuum topologies reduce to products of finite-in-dimensional-regularization tadpoles and do not cancel this logarithmic pole. Thus
$$
\boxed{\delta g_3^{\text{two loops}}\ \text{is generically nonzero and proportional to }g_1^4.}
$$
A sextic <counterterm> is required even if the renormalized sextic coupling is set to zero at a chosen <renormalization scale>. A theory constrained to $g_3=0$ at every scale lacks <counterterm closure> when $g_1\ne0$. The decoupled exception $g_1=0$ is the three-dimensional scalar <quartic interaction>, which needs no sextic <counterterm> by <power counting>.
