= Solution
{parent=a}

Make the <Wick rotation> $t'=-i\tau$, and write $T_E=\beta\hbar$ for the final <imaginary time>, where $\beta$ is <inverse temperature> and $\hbar$ is the <reduced Planck constant>. Then $dt'=-i\,d\tau$ and $dx/dt'=i\,dx/d\tau$. Thus the continued <action> is
$$
S=i\int_0^{T_E}\left[\frac m2\left(\frac{dx}{d\tau}\right)^2+V(x)\right]d\tau
=iS_{\mathrm{Euc}},
\qquad
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{Euc}}[x]=\int_0^{\beta\hbar}\left[\frac m2\dot x^2+V(x)\right]d\tau.}
$$
Both the <kinetic energy> and the <potential energy> enter this <Euclidean action> with positive signs relative to their real-time definitions. Also $e^{-iH(-i\beta\hbar)/\hbar}=e^{-\beta H}$, so the <Euclidean path integral> is
$$
\boxed{\langle x_f|e^{-\beta H}|x_i\rangle
=\int_{x(0)=x_i}^{x(T_E)=x_f}\mathcal D x\,
 e^{-S_{\mathrm{Euc}}[x]/\hbar}.}
$$
Its <boundary conditions> fix the two endpoint <positions>. They need not coincide: identifying and integrating over them would instead give the <thermal trace>.

The normalization of $\mathcal D x$ can be fixed without continuing a formal measure. Apply the <Trotter product formula> to $H=\hat p^2/(2m)+V(\hat x)$, divide $T_E$ into $M$ intervals of size $\epsilon=T_E/M$, and integrate the intermediate <positions>. The resulting <time-sliced configuration-space path integral> is
$$
\lim_{M\to\infty}
\left(\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{M/2}
\int_{\mathbb R^{M-1}}\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}dx_j\,
\exp\!\left[-\frac1\hbar\sum_{j=1}^M
\left\{\frac{m(x_j-x_{j-1})^2}{2\epsilon}+\epsilon V(x_{j-1})\right\}\right],
\quad x_0=x_i,\quad x_M=x_f.
$$
This also explains the upper limit $\beta\hbar$ rather than $\beta$ when the <reduced Planck constant> has not been set to one.

For a <free particle>, vary the <Euclidean action> with a variation $h$ obeying $h(0)=h(T_E)=0$. <Integration by parts> gives
$$
\delta S_{\mathrm{Euc}}=m\int_0^{T_E}\dot x\dot h\,d\tau
=-m\int_0^{T_E}\ddot xh\,d\tau.
$$
The <Euler-Lagrange equation> is therefore $\ddot x_0=0$. The fixed <boundary conditions> determine its unique solution:
$$
\boxed{x_0(\tau)=x_i+\frac{x_f-x_i}{\beta\hbar}\tau,
\qquad 0\leq\tau\leq\beta\hbar.}
$$
This <classical path> is also the unique minimum among paths with finite <Euclidean action>: the second variation is $m\int_0^{T_E}\dot h^2d\tau>0$ for every nonzero admissible variation. Its <Euclidean action> is
$$
\boxed{S_{\mathrm{Euc}}[x_0]=\frac{m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\beta\hbar}.}
$$

= Solution

Write $x=x_0+y$, so the fluctuation has homogeneous <Dirichlet boundary conditions> $y(0)=y(T_E)=0$. Because $\dot x_0$ is constant, the cross term in the <Euclidean action> vanishes exactly:
$$
m\int_0^{T_E}\dot x_0\dot y\,d\tau
=m\dot x_0[y(T_E)-y(0)]=0.
$$
Consequently
$$
S_{\mathrm{Euc}}[x_0+y]
=\frac{m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2T_E}+\frac m2\int_0^{T_E}\dot y^2d\tau.
$$
Translation of each intermediate integration variable has <Jacobian determinant> one in the <time-sliced configuration-space path integral>. This is an exact <classical-path factorization of a quadratic path integral>, giving
$$
\boxed{\langle x_f|e^{-\beta H}|x_i\rangle
=\mathcal N\exp\!\left[-\frac{m(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\beta\hbar^2}\right],
\quad
\mathcal N=\int_{y(0)=0}^{y(\beta\hbar)=0}\mathcal D y\,
\exp\!\left[-\frac{m}{2\hbar}\int_0^{\beta\hbar}\dot y^2d\tau\right].}
$$
The fluctuation <Euclidean action>, the homogeneous <boundary conditions>, and the measure normalization contain neither $x_i$ nor $x_f$. Hence **$\mathcal N$ is independent of both endpoint <positions>**. It depends on $\beta,m,\hbar$.

With the standard position-state normalization, the <Euclidean free-particle bridge amplitude> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal N=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi\beta\hbar^2}}.}
$$
For example, a <momentum> resolution gives a one-dimensional <Gaussian integral>,
$$
\langle x_f|e^{-\beta H}|x_i\rangle
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp}{2\pi\hbar}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{\beta p^2}{2m}+\frac{ip(x_f-x_i)}{\hbar}\right],
$$
whose evaluation gives the same exponent and prefactor. In particular, the result integrates to one over $x_f$ at fixed $x_i$, as required for its <heat kernel> interpretation.

= Solution
{parent=d}

The integrated <stochastic differential equation> reads
$$
Y(t):=y(t)-y(0)=\int_0^t\eta(s)\,ds.
$$
The <Gaussian white noise> has zero <expected value>, so
$$
\boxed{\langle y(t)-y(0)\rangle=0.}
$$
Its <Dirac delta> <covariance> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle Y(t)^2\rangle
&=\int_0^t\!ds\int_0^t\!ds'\,\langle\eta(s)\eta(s')\rangle\\
&=\Gamma\int_0^t\!ds\int_0^t\!ds'\,\delta(s-s')
=\Gamma t.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $\boxed{\langle[y(t)-y(0)]^2\rangle=\Gamma t}$, which is also the <variance> because the displacement has zero <expected value>. More generally its <covariance> is $\langle Y(s)Y(t)\rangle=\Gamma\min(s,t)$. Equivalently $Y(t)=\sqrt\Gamma\,W_t$ for standard <Brownian motion>, and the <diffusion coefficient> is $D=\Gamma/2$.

= Solution
{parent=e}

The <Brownian motion> displacement has a <normal distribution> with <variance> $\Gamma t$. Its transition <probability density function> is therefore
$$
p(y_f,t\mid y_i,0)
=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\Gamma t}}
\exp\!\left[-\frac{(y_f-y_i)^2}{2\Gamma t}\right].
$$
Comparison with the <free particle> <heat kernel> obtained in part (c) gives the required matching:
$$
\boxed{\Gamma t=\frac{\beta\hbar^2}{m}.}
$$
This matches whole path distributions after a linear reparametrization, not merely endpoint <variances>. To see this, divide the Brownian duration $t$ into $M$ slices of size $\delta t$. The <independent increments> have <normal distributions> with <variance> $\Gamma\delta t$, so their joint density contributes
$$
\prod_{j=1}^M\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\Gamma\delta t}}
\exp\!\left[-\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{(y_j-y_{j-1})^2}{2\Gamma\delta t}\right].
$$
For the <Euclidean path integral>, the corresponding expression is
$$
\prod_{j=1}^M\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi\hbar\delta\tau}}
\exp\!\left[-\sum_{j=1}^M\frac{m(x_j-x_{j-1})^2}{2\hbar\delta\tau}\right],
\qquad \delta\tau=\frac{\beta\hbar}{M}.
$$
The matching condition makes $\Gamma\delta t=\hbar\delta\tau/m$, so both the exponents and all normalization factors coincide. In particular, identifying Brownian time with <imaginary time>, $t=\beta\hbar$, requires $\Gamma=\hbar/m$, or <diffusion coefficient> $D=\hbar/(2m)$.

With both endpoints fixed, the normalized path law is a **<Brownian bridge>**. The <Euclidean path integral> itself is the endpoint density times that conditional path law. In the normalized time coordinate $s=\tau/(\beta\hbar)$, its <expected value> and <covariance> are
$$
\mathbb E[x(s)]=x_i+s(x_f-x_i),
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(x(s),x(s'))
=\frac{\beta\hbar^2}{m}\bigl[\min(s,s')-ss'\bigr].
$$
The <classical path> is therefore the bridge's <expected value>, while individual <Brownian bridge> paths fluctuate about it. Typical <Brownian motion> paths are continuous but nowhere differentiable: the kinetic <Euclidean action> is interpreted through the <time-sliced diffusion path integral>, rather than by assuming that a typical path has an ordinary velocity.

For $J>0$ each bond minimizes its <energy> when its two <Ising spins> are opposite, giving bond <energy> $-J$. An even ring allows every bond to do this simultaneously, including the closing bond required by the <periodic boundary conditions>. Its two <ground states> are
$$
\boxed{S_j=\sigma(-1)^{j-1},\quad\sigma=\pm1;
\qquad E_0=-NJ,\quad g_0=2.}
$$
The two configurations are related by <spin inversion symmetry>. There is no <geometrical frustration>.

{parent=a}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Let $k$ count parallel bonds. Then $N-k$ bonds are antiparallel and the <energy> is
$$
H=Jk-J(N-k)=-NJ+2Jk.
$$
The <periodic boundary conditions> impose
$$
\prod_{j=1}^N(S_jS_{j+1})=\prod_{j=1}^NS_j^2=1.
$$
Hence $N-k$ must be even. For odd $N$, $k$ is odd, so its minimum is one: one bond must be parallel. All other bonds alternate. Choosing that bond in $N$ ways and then choosing either value of $S_1$ determines every <Ising spin>, giving
$$
\boxed{E_0=-(N-2)J,\qquad g_0=2N.}
$$
This is an <odd antiferromagnetic Ising ring>: <geometrical frustration> forces one defect, whose location is unrestricted. The <ground-state degeneracy> includes both its possible locations and global <spin inversion symmetry>. The formula also includes the self-bond case $N=1$ under the stated <Hamiltonian> convention.

{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Set $K=\beta J$. The <transfer matrix for the one-dimensional Ising model> assigns a <Boltzmann factor> to each bond. In the <Ising spin> order $(+1,-1)$, it is
$$
T_{ss'}=e^{-Kss'},\qquad
T=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-K}&e^K\\e^K&e^{-K}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying these bond factors and summing every <Ising spin> implements the <canonical ensemble>. The <periodic boundary conditions> close the product into a <trace>:
$$
Z_N=\sum_{S_1,\ldots,S_N}\prod_{j=1}^NT_{S_jS_{j+1}}
=\operatorname{tr}T^N.
$$
The <eigenvectors> $|+\rangle=(1,1)^T/\sqrt2$ and $|-\rangle=(1,-1)^T/\sqrt2$ form an <orthonormal basis>, with <eigenvalues>
$$
\lambda_+=e^{-K}+e^K=2\cosh K,
\qquad
\lambda_-=e^{-K}-e^K=-2\sinh K.
$$
The negative second <eigenvalue> encodes alternating <correlations>. Defining $q=\lambda_-/\lambda_+=-\tanh K$, the exact <partition function> and total <Helmholtz free energy> are
$$
\boxed{Z_N=(2\cosh K)^N+(-2\sinh K)^N
=(2\cosh K)^N(1+q^N),}
$$
$$
\boxed{F_N=-\frac1\beta\ln Z_N
=-\frac N\beta\ln(2\cosh K)-\frac1\beta\ln(1+q^N).}
$$
The parity-dependent term must be retained for finite $N$; in particular it nearly cancels the leading term of $Z_N$ at low <temperature> when $N$ is odd.

Represent insertion of one <Ising spin> by $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. In the <eigenbasis> of $T$, this <matrix> swaps $|+\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$. Its diagonal entries in that <eigenbasis> vanish, giving the exact <one-point correlation function>
$$
\boxed{\langle S_1\rangle=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma T^N)}{Z_N}=0.}
$$
Equivalently, configurations pair with their opposites under <spin inversion symmetry>, with the same <Boltzmann factor> and opposite $S_1$.

Two insertions separated by $n$ bonds give the <two-point correlation function>
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle S_1S_{n+1}\rangle
&=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma T^n\Sigma T^{N-n})}{Z_N}\\
&=\frac{\lambda_-^n\lambda_+^{N-n}+\lambda_+^n\lambda_-^{N-n}}
{\lambda_+^N+\lambda_-^N}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the exact <finite antiferromagnetic Ising-ring correlation> is
$$
\boxed{C_N(n):=\langle S_1S_{n+1}\rangle
=\frac{q^n+q^{N-n}}{1+q^N},\quad q=-\tanh(\beta J),\quad0\leq n\leq N-1.}
$$
The two numerator terms represent the two arcs between the sites. The formula has $C_N(0)=1$ and $C_N(n)=C_N(N-n)$, as required by the <periodic boundary conditions>. Since the <one-point correlation function> is zero, this is also the <connected correlation function>. At $K=0$, every <Ising spin> is <independent>, so $C_N(n)=0$ for distinct sites and $C_N(0)=1$; powers with exponent zero are understood as one.

= Solution

For $0<K<\infty$, $|q|=\tanh K<1$. Taking the <thermodynamic limit> $N\to\infty$ at fixed separation $n$ removes $q^N$ and $q^{N-n}$ from the <finite antiferromagnetic Ising-ring correlation>. Therefore
$$
\boxed{C_\infty(n)=(-\tanh K)^n=(-1)^ne^{-n/\xi},
\qquad \xi=-\frac1{\ln\tanh(\beta J)}=\frac1{\ln\coth(\beta J)}.}
$$
The <correlation length> $\xi$ is measured in lattice spacings. If the lattice spacing is $a$, its physical value is $a\xi$. The factor $(-1)^n$ expresses <antiferromagnetism>, while $e^{-n/\xi}$ measures the decay of the staggered <two-point correlation function>.

At every positive <temperature>, $\xi$ is finite. At low <temperature>, $\tanh K=1-2e^{-2K}+O(e^{-4K})$, giving $\boxed{\xi\sim\tfrac12e^{2\beta J}}$. At infinite <temperature>, $K=0$, distinct <Ising spins> are uncorrelated; the limiting <correlation length> is zero.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Put $r=\tanh K$, so $r\to1^-$ as the <temperature> tends to zero. For even $N$, the <finite antiferromagnetic Ising-ring correlation> becomes
$$
C_N(n)=(-1)^n\frac{r^n+r^{N-n}}{1+r^N}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{\lim_{\beta J\to\infty}C_N(n)=(-1)^n\qquad(N\text{ even}).}
$$
The two perfectly alternating <ground states> give exactly the same product $S_1S_{n+1}=(-1)^n$; averaging them preserves its magnitude even though the <one-point correlation function> remains zero.

For odd $N$ the signs instead give
$$
C_N(n)=(-1)^n\frac{r^n-r^{N-n}}{1-r^N}.
$$
Both numerator and denominator vanish. Set $r=1-\delta$ and expand at fixed $N,n$:
$$
r^n-r^{N-n}=(N-2n)\delta+O(\delta^2),
\qquad1-r^N=N\delta+O(\delta^2).
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{\lim_{\beta J\to\infty}C_N(n)
=(-1)^n\left(1-\frac{2n}{N}\right)\qquad(N\text{ odd}).}
$$
There is a direct <ground state> interpretation. The mandatory parallel bond of an <odd antiferromagnetic Ising ring> is uniformly distributed among its $N$ bonds in the limiting <canonical ensemble>. Along the directed $n$-bond arc from site 1 to site $n+1$, the product is $(-1)^n$ if the defect lies outside that arc, and $-(-1)^n$ if it lies inside. The respective probabilities are $1-n/N$ and $n/N$, giving the displayed <two-point correlation function>. Global <spin inversion symmetry> doubles the number of <ground states> but leaves each product unchanged.

\Image[2025-ising-ring-parity.png]
{title=One unavoidable parallel bond changes the zero-temperature correlations of an odd antiferromagnetic ring}

The reduction on an odd ring survives at separations comparable with $N$, despite the vanishing defect density $1/N$. For fixed $n$, however, taking $N\to\infty$ after the zero-<temperature> limit recovers $(-1)^n$ for either parity, consistently with the diverging <correlation length>. The linear envelope at finite odd $N$ comes from one freely located frustration defect; it is distinct from the exponential decay caused by thermal defects in the <thermodynamic limit>.

= Solution

The <canonical anticommutation relations> imply $a^2=(a^\dagger)^2=0$. Thus the <number operator> satisfies
$$
N_a^2=a^\dagger aa^\dagger a
=a^\dagger(1-a^\dagger a)a=N_a.
$$
It is a <orthogonal projection>, so its <eigenvalues> obey $n_a^2=n_a$ and are $\boxed{n_a\in\{0,1\}}$. The same argument gives $\boxed{n_b\in\{0,1\}}$. This is the <Pauli exclusion principle> for each orbital. The two <number operators> commute, so they have simultaneous <eigenstates>.

Take the <vacuum state> to have unit norm and fix the double-occupation convention $|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle$. An <orthonormal basis> of the four-dimensional <fermionic Fock space> is
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{state}&n_a&n_b\\\hline
|00\rangle=|0\rangle&0&0\\
|10\rangle=a^\dagger|0\rangle&1&0\\
|01\rangle=b^\dagger|0\rangle&0&1\\
|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle&1&1
\end{array}}
$$
Each individual <number operator> has two-dimensional <eigenspaces>: for $N_a$ these are spanned by $\{|00\rangle,|01\rangle\}$ and $\{|10\rangle,|11\rangle\}$; for $N_b$ they are spanned by $\{|00\rangle,|10\rangle\}$ and $\{|01\rangle,|11\rangle\}$. Higher <occupation numbers> vanish because the square of a <fermionic creation operator> is zero. Reversing the order of the two <fermionic creation operators> changes only the sign convention for $|11\rangle$.

The <Bogoliubov transformation> and its <Hermitian conjugate> read
$$
a=uc+vd^\dagger,\quad a^\dagger=uc^\dagger+vd,
\qquad
b=-vc^\dagger+ud,\quad b^\dagger=-vc+ud^\dagger.
$$
Using the <canonical anticommutation relations> of $c,d$, the nonzero same-mode <anticommutators> are
$$
\{a,a^\dagger\}=u^2\{c,c^\dagger\}+v^2\{d^\dagger,d\}=u^2+v^2,
\qquad
\{b,b^\dagger\}=u^2+v^2.
$$
The equal-type <anticommutators> satisfy $\{a,a\}=\{b,b\}=0$, since every contributing equal-type or mixed-mode <anticommutator> of $c,d$ vanishes. The potentially nontrivial cross term cancels:
$$
\{a,b\}=-uv\{c,c^\dagger\}+uv\{d^\dagger,d\}
=-uv+uv=0.
$$
Also $\{a,b^\dagger\}=0$, and the other cross relations follow by <Hermitian conjugation>. Therefore the necessary and sufficient condition is
$$
\boxed{u^2+v^2=1.}
$$
The real transformation <matrix> is then an <orthogonal matrix>, with inverse
$$
\boxed{c=ua-vb^\dagger,\qquad d=va^\dagger+ub.}
$$
This preservation of the <canonical anticommutation relations> makes it a canonical <Bogoliubov transformation>.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Write $Q=c^\dagger c+d^\dagger d$ and $P=cd+d^\dagger c^\dagger$. Expanding the <number operators> and restoring the displayed order with the <canonical anticommutation relations> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^\dagger a+b^\dagger b
&=(u^2-v^2)Q+2v^2-2uvP,\\
ab+b^\dagger a^\dagger
&=2uv(Q-1)+(u^2-v^2)P.
\end{aligned}
$$
The minus sign in the first line follows from $c^\dagger d^\dagger+dc=-(cd+d^\dagger c^\dagger)$. Substitution into the <Hamiltonian operator> yields
$$
H=\bigl[\varepsilon(u^2-v^2)+2\Delta uv\bigr]Q
+\bigl[\Delta(u^2-v^2)-2\varepsilon uv\bigr]P
+2\varepsilon v^2-2\Delta uv.
$$
Let $E=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}>0$. A convenient real <Bogoliubov transformation> has
$$
\boxed{u=\sqrt{\frac{E+\varepsilon}{2E}},\qquad
v=\sqrt{\frac{E-\varepsilon}{2E}}
=\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2E(E+\varepsilon)}}.}
$$
These obey $u^2+v^2=1$, $u^2-v^2=\varepsilon/E$ and $2uv=\Delta/E$. Equivalently $u=\cos\theta$, $v=\sin\theta$ with $0<\theta<\pi/4$ and $\tan(2\theta)=\Delta/\varepsilon$. The coefficient of $P$ vanishes, while the coefficient of $Q$ is $E$. The constant is
$$
2\varepsilon v^2-2\Delta uv
=\varepsilon\left(1-\frac\varepsilon E\right)-\frac{\Delta^2}{E}
=\varepsilon-E.
$$
Thus the diagonalized <two-mode fermion pairing Hamiltonian> is
$$
\boxed{H=E(c^\dagger c+d^\dagger d)+\varepsilon-E,
\qquad \lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2},\quad
\mu=\varepsilon-\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}.}
$$
The two transformed <fermionic occupation numbers> independently take values zero or one. The <energy eigenvalues> and their <eigenspace> dimensions are therefore
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
n_c+n_d&\text{energy}&\text{degeneracy}\\\hline
0&\varepsilon-E&1\\
1&\varepsilon&2\\
2&\varepsilon+E&1
\end{array}}
$$
The positive <quasiparticle> <energy> $E$ makes the transformed <vacuum state> the unique <ground state>. The constant $\mu$ is essential for the correct <ground-state energy>.

As a separate sign and spectrum check, use the original <fermionic Fock space> basis. Both singly occupied states are <energy eigenstates> with <energy> $\varepsilon$, since the pairing terms annihilate them. In the ordered even-<fermion parity> basis $(|0\rangle,a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle)$,
$$
H_{\mathrm{even}}=
\begin{pmatrix}0&-\Delta\\-\Delta&2\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The off-diagonal signs follow from $b^\dagger a^\dagger=-a^\dagger b^\dagger$ and $ab\,a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle=-|0\rangle$. Its <characteristic polynomial> is $z^2-2\varepsilon z-\Delta^2$, giving the remaining two <eigenvalues> $\varepsilon\pm E$, in agreement with the <Bogoliubov transformation>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

The inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> gives $c=ua-vb^\dagger$ and $d=va^\dagger+ub$. Because $(a^\dagger b^\dagger)^2=0$ by the <canonical anticommutation relations>, the exponential truncates:
$$
Ae^{Ba^\dagger b^\dagger}|0\rangle
=A\bigl(|0\rangle+Ba^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle\bigr).
$$
The <fermionic signs> are fixed by
$$
a\,a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle=b^\dagger|0\rangle,
\qquad
b\,a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle=-a^\dagger|0\rangle.
$$
It follows that
$$
c|\tilde0\rangle=A(uB-v)b^\dagger|0\rangle,
\qquad
d|\tilde0\rangle=A(v-uB)a^\dagger|0\rangle.
$$
Both annihilation conditions hold precisely when $B=v/u$. Normalizing the <paired fermionic vacuum>, using the <orthogonality> of its two <Fock states>, gives $A^2(1+B^2)=1$. Choose the irrelevant overall phase so that $A>0$. The result is
$$
\boxed{A=u=\sqrt{\frac{E+\varepsilon}{2E}},\qquad
B=\frac vu=\frac{\Delta}{E+\varepsilon}
=\frac{E-\varepsilon}{\Delta},\qquad
E=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}.}
$$
Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{|\tilde0\rangle=u|0\rangle+va^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle.}
$$
Its positive pair coefficient follows from the specified order $ab+b^\dagger a^\dagger$ in the <Hamiltonian operator>. The state has even <fermion parity> and is a <quantum superposition> of zero and two original particles. In the limit $\Delta\to0^+$, $A\to1$ and $B\to0$, so it reduces to the original <vacuum state>, as expected for $\varepsilon>0$.
