= a

= Solution
{parent=a}

Take the inviscid limit of the <Navier-Stokes equation>, neglect gravity, and use <ideal magnetohydrodynamics>. The governing equations are
$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0,\qquad
\rho(\partial_t\mathbf v+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf v)=-\nabla P+\frac{(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\times\mathbf B}{\mu_0},\qquad
\partial_t\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B),\quad\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0.
$$
The <continuity equation> also gives $D_t\rho=0$. Write the first-order magnetic perturbation as $\mathbf b=\mathbf B'$ and linearize about the uniform stationary background:
$$
\rho_0\partial_t\mathbf v=-\nabla\left(P'+\frac{B_0b_z}{\mu_0}\right)+\frac{B_0}{\mu_0}\partial_z\mathbf b,
\qquad\partial_t\mathbf b=B_0\partial_z\mathbf v,
\qquad\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=\nabla\cdot\mathbf b=0.
$$
For a nonzero-<wavenumber> <plane wave> proportional to $e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$, the two divergence constraints imply $v_z=b_z=0$. The longitudinal momentum equation then gives $P'=0$ for that mode, and $\partial_t\rho'=0$ gives $\rho'=0$ for nonzero $\omega$. Thus the propagating <Alfvén wave> is transverse. Its two independent transverse directions have the same <dispersion relation>.

The <linear Alfvén wave equations> reduce to
$$
-i\omega\rho_0\mathbf v=\frac{ikB_0}{\mu_0}\mathbf b,\qquad-i\omega\mathbf b=ikB_0\mathbf v.
$$
Eliminating either <amplitude> gives
$$
\boxed{\omega^2=k^2v_A^2,\qquad v_A=\frac{B_0}{\sqrt{\mu_0\rho_0}},\qquad\mathbf b=-\frac{kB_0}{\omega}\mathbf v.}
$$
For $B_0>0$, the branch traveling along $+z$ has $\omega=kv_A$ and $\mathbf b=-\sqrt{\mu_0\rho_0}\mathbf v$. The opposite branch reverses that <amplitude> sign. Finite <viscosity> would additionally damp the motion; it is neglected in obtaining the real ideal <dispersion relation>.

= b
{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Subtract the constant equilibrium <magnetic energy> density. Since $\mathbf B_0\cdot\mathbf b=0$, the perturbation densities are
$$
\mathcal E_K=\frac12\rho_0|\mathbf v|^2,\qquad
\mathcal E_B=\frac{|\mathbf b|^2}{2\mu_0}.
$$
The traveling-wave <amplitude> relation from part <1/a> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_B=\mathcal E_K,\qquad\mathcal E=\mathcal E_K+\mathcal E_B=\rho_0|\mathbf v|^2=\frac{|\mathbf b|^2}{\mu_0}.}
$$
This is pointwise <Alfvén wave energy transport> for one traveling wave, not a claim that every superposition or <standing wave> has pointwise equipartition.

For the real fields written as $\mathbf v=\operatorname{Re}(\widehat{\mathbf v}e^{i(kz-\omega t)})$ and similarly for $\mathbf b$, the period averages are
$$
\langle\mathcal E_K\rangle=\frac{\rho_0}{4}|\widehat{\mathbf v}|^2,\qquad
\langle\mathcal E_B\rangle=\frac1{4\mu_0}|\widehat{\mathbf b}|^2,\qquad
\boxed{\langle\mathcal E\rangle=\frac{\rho_0}{2}|\widehat{\mathbf v}|^2.}
$$
The factors one half distinguish a sinusoid's peak <amplitude> from its mean squared value; the same complex-vector convention also covers circular <polarization of waves>.

= Solution
{parent=c}

In <ideal magnetohydrodynamics>, the first-order <electric field> is $\mathbf E_1=-\mathbf v\times B_0\mathbf e_z$. Retaining its product with both the background and first-order <magnetic field> gives the <Poynting vector>
$$
\mathbf S=\frac1{\mu_0}\mathbf E_1\times(B_0\mathbf e_z+\mathbf b)
=\frac{B_0^2}{\mu_0}\mathbf v-\frac{B_0}{\mu_0}(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf b)\mathbf e_z.
$$
The first term is transverse and oscillatory, with zero period average. The second is quadratic and transports wave <energy>. A potential quadratic ideal electric-field term $-\mathbf v\times\mathbf b$ vanishes here because the real traveling-wave fields are parallel or antiparallel.

For the $+z$ branch, $\mathbf b=-\sqrt{\mu_0\rho_0}\mathbf v$, so
$$
\boxed{\langle\mathbf S\rangle=\rho_0v_A\langle|\mathbf v|^2\rangle\mathbf e_z=v_A\langle\mathcal E\rangle\mathbf e_z.}
$$
For the opposite branch, its sign reverses. The averaged <Poynting flux> is the total wave <energy density>, including equal kinetic and magnetic shares, multiplied by the <group velocity>. This agrees with <Alfvén wave energy transport>. Although the flux in this expression is electromagnetic, it supplies the transport of both components of the coupled fluid-field wave.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}

In this question, the coefficient multiplying $\nabla^2\mathbf B$ is the <magnetic diffusivity>. Denote it $\eta_m$; it is the paper's $\eta$, with dimensions of length squared per time. If $\eta_{\rm Ohm}$ denotes <electrical resistivity>, then $\eta_m=\eta_{\rm Ohm}/\mu_0$. The two conventions must not be interchanged.

For an imposed real positive $\omega$, retain the momentum equation and use
$$
-i\omega\rho_0\mathbf v=\frac{ikB_0}{\mu_0}\mathbf b,\qquad
(-i\omega+\eta_mk^2)\mathbf b=ikB_0\mathbf v.
$$
Elimination gives the exact <resistive damping of an Alfvén wave> relation
$$
\boxed{\omega^2+i\eta_m\omega k^2-v_A^2k^2=0,\qquad
k^2=\frac{\omega^2}{v_A^2-i\eta_m\omega}.}
$$
Here $k$ is complex because the driving <frequency> is real. Introduce the dimensionless diffusion parameter $\epsilon=\eta_m\omega/v_A^2\ll1$. For propagation and attenuation along $+z$, choose the square-root branch with $\operatorname{Re}k,\operatorname{Im}k>0$. The <spatial damping of a driven Alfvén wave> is
$$
\boxed{k=\frac\omega{v_A}(1-i\epsilon)^{-1/2}=\frac\omega{v_A}\left(1+\frac{i\epsilon}{2}+O(\epsilon^2)\right).}
$$
With the convention $e^{i(kz-\omega t)}$, the <amplitude> decays as $e^{-\operatorname{Im}k\,z}$. Define its e-folding length $L_A=1/\operatorname{Im}k$ and the wavelength $\lambda=2\pi/\operatorname{Re}k$. Then
$$
\boxed{L_A\sim\frac{2v_A^3}{\eta_m\omega^2},\qquad\frac{L_A}{\lambda}\sim\frac1{\pi\epsilon}.}
$$
If damping length instead refers to wave <energy>, the squared <amplitude> decays twice as fast: $L_E=L_A/2$ and $L_E/\lambda\sim1/(2\pi\epsilon)$. Stating the convention avoids a factor-of-two ambiguity. The temporal damping rate $\eta_mk^2/2$ at fixed real $k$ is a different specification from this imposed-frequency problem.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Expand the given translating-sphere <Stokes flow> into its <Stokeslet> and <potential dipole> parts:
$$
\mathbf u=\frac{3a}{4}\left(\frac{\mathbf U}{r}+\frac{(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)\mathbf x}{r^3}\right)
+\frac{a^3}{4}\left(\frac{\mathbf U}{r^3}-\frac{3(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)\mathbf x}{r^5}\right).
$$
The second parenthesis is $\nabla[(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)/r^3]$ and has zero curl outside the sphere. For the first,
$$
\nabla\times\frac{\mathbf U}{r}=\frac{\mathbf U\times\mathbf x}{r^3},\qquad
\nabla\times\frac{(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)\mathbf x}{r^3}=\frac{\mathbf U\times\mathbf x}{r^3}.
$$
Thus the <vorticity of translating-sphere Stokes flow> is
$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf u=\frac{3a}{2}\frac{\mathbf U\times\mathbf x}{r^3}.}
$$
The result is independent of the irrotational finite-size dipole correction. It holds in the exterior $r>a$; the singular solid harmonics are used only outside their source.

= b
{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Use the <force flux at infinity in Stokes flow>. With an outward normal from a large sphere, the <force> on the body is $\mathbf F=\int_{S_R}\sigma\mathbf n\,dS$. Let $C=3a/4$. The leading far-field <velocity> and <pressure> are
$$
u_i=C\left(\frac{U_i}{r}+\frac{(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)x_i}{r^3}\right)+O(r^{-3}),\qquad
p=2\mu C\frac{\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x}{r^3}.
$$
Their <rate-of-strain tensor> is
$$
e_{ij}=C(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf x)\left(\frac{\delta_{ij}}{r^3}-\frac{3x_ix_j}{r^5}\right)+O(r^{-4}).
$$
In the <Newtonian fluid stress tensor> $\sigma_{ij}=-p\delta_{ij}+2\mu e_{ij}$, the isotropic pieces cancel. Consequently,
$$
\sigma\mathbf n=-\frac{6\mu C}{R^2}(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n+O(R^{-4}).
$$
The <second spherical moment integral> $\int n_in_j\,d\Omega=(4\pi/3)\delta_{ij}$ follows from rotational symmetry and $\sum_i n_i^2=1$. Since $dS=R^2d\Omega$, taking $R\to\infty$ gives the <Stokes drag law>:
$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=-6\mu C\frac{4\pi}{3}\mathbf U=-6\pi\mu a\mathbf U.}
$$
The omitted dipole contributes a surface integral of order $R^{-2}$ and vanishes in this limit. The sign refers to the fluid's drag on the moving sphere.

= Solution
{parent=c}

At the <terminal velocity>, buoyancy-corrected weight balances the <Stokes drag law>:
$$
\frac{4\pi a^3}{3}(\widetilde\rho-\rho)\mathbf g-6\pi\mu a\mathbf U=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{\mathbf U=\frac{2a^2(\widetilde\rho-\rho)}{9\mu}\mathbf g,\qquad
|\mathbf U|=\frac{2a^2|\widetilde\rho-\rho|}{9\mu}|\mathbf g|.}
$$
A denser sphere falls in the direction of $\mathbf g$; a lighter one rises. Equal densities give no sedimentation. The calculation assumes negligible fluid <Reynolds number>, an unbounded fluid, and negligible transient body <acceleration> after terminal settling. The fluid <mass density> enters through <buoyancy> and through the small-<Reynolds number> condition.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Take $z$ positive upward, let sphere A's centre be $Z(t)\mathbf e_z$, and write its downward <velocity> as $-U\mathbf e_z$, with $\dot Z=-U$. At B's centre the displacement from A is $\mathbf r=\ell\mathbf e_x-Z\mathbf e_z$. The <vorticity of translating-sphere Stokes flow> therefore gives
$$
\boldsymbol\omega_\infty=-\frac{3aU\ell}{2(\ell^2+Z^2)^{3/2}}\mathbf e_y.
$$
B is held in translation but has zero applied <torque>. By <Faxén's rotational law>, it rotates with half the incident <vorticity>:
$$
\boldsymbol\Omega_B=-\frac{3aU\ell}{4(\ell^2+Z^2)^{3/2}}\mathbf e_y.
$$
Integrating the <rotation induced by a passing Stokes sphere> along the complete path cancels $U$:
$$
\Delta\boldsymbol\theta=\frac{3a\ell}{4}\mathbf e_y\int_{+\infty}^{-\infty}\frac{dZ}{(\ell^2+Z^2)^{3/2}}
=\boxed{-\frac{3a}{2\ell}\mathbf e_y.}
$$
Its magnitude is $3a/(2\ell)$ radians, with direction about the negative $y$ axis by the right-hand rule. All instantaneous rotations have this same axis, so the integrated <angular velocity> gives the net angle without a noncommuting-rotation correction. The result is independent of B's radius in the prescribed incident-flow calculation.

\Image[2023-passing-sphere-rotation.png]
{title=Angular velocity and cumulative rotation from an isolated sphere passing a torque-free target}

The inequality $\ell>a+b$ ensures no overlap. It does not by itself make mutual <hydrodynamic interactions> negligible: the stated flow is the isolated-A solution, and a held finite B also disturbs A's flow. Thus the displayed angle is the result for that supplied incident flow, or the leading one-way approximation to separated bodies. A fully coupled two-sphere calculation uses the <method of reflections for Stokes flow> or an exact pair resistance solution.
