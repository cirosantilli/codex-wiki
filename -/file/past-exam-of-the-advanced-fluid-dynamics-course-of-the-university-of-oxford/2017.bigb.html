= Solution
{parent=a}

Choose two independent labels for the <magnetic field lines>, constant along each line. These are <Euler potentials>: $\mathbf B\cdot\nabla\alpha=\mathbf B\cdot\nabla\beta=0$. Where the two <gradients> are independent, the vector <Orthogonality>[perpendicular] to both is $\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta$, so initially write
$$
\mathbf B=f\,\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta.
$$
The <Solenoidal vector field>[solenoidal condition] gives $0=\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=(\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta)\cdot\nabla f$. Thus $f$ is constant along a <Magnetic field line>[field line] and is locally a function of its labels. Redefining one label, for example $\widetilde\beta(\alpha,\beta)=\int^\beta f(\alpha,b)\,db$, absorbs this factor. Dropping the tilde gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbf B=\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\beta.}
$$
This representation automatically has zero <divergence>, since it is also $\nabla\times(\alpha\nabla\beta)$.

For an <magnetic arcade>, assign the labels on one polarity of the footpoint surface and transport them along each line to its other footpoint. They specify both line identity and <magnetic flux>: on a transverse surface, flux coordinates can be chosen so that the <Magnetic flux>[directed flux] element is $d\alpha\,d\beta$. In particular,
$$
B_z(x,y,0)=\partial_x\alpha_0\,\partial_y\beta_0-
\partial_y\alpha_0\,\partial_x\beta_0.
$$
The <Euler potentials>[boundary labels] at paired footpoints must agree, so prescribed <Boundary condition>[boundary functions] encode a compatible <Magnetic field line>[footpoint mapping], not independently arbitrary values at the two ends.

The construction assumes a smooth family of nonintersecting lines that can be labelled from a transverse boundary patch, with suitable patches at nulls or coordinate singularities. A local <Euler potentials>[Euler-potential representation] does not establish the existence of two globally single-valued regular labels for every possible <Solenoidal vector field>[solenoidal field]; closed, recurrent or more complicated <Magnetic field line>[field topology] can obstruct such a global choice. The line-tied <magnetic arcade> geometry supplies the relevant simplifying assumption here.

Use the <magnetic energy> $E_B=(2\mu_0)^{-1}\int_V|\mathbf B|^2\,dV$. Vary the <Euler potentials> while keeping their boundary values fixed. The <Calculus of variations>[field variation] is
$$
\delta\mathbf B=\nabla\delta\alpha\times\nabla\beta+
\nabla\alpha\times\nabla\delta\beta
=\nabla\times\left(\delta\alpha\nabla\beta-\delta\beta\nabla\alpha\right).
$$
Let $\delta\mathbf A=\delta\alpha\nabla\beta-\delta\beta\nabla\alpha$. <Integration by parts> gives
$$
\delta E_B=\frac1{\mu_0}\int_V\delta\mathbf A\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\,dV,
$$
because $\delta\mathbf A=0$ on the boundary. Therefore
$$
\delta E_B=\frac1{\mu_0}\int_V\left[
\delta\alpha\,\nabla\beta\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B)
-\delta\beta\,\nabla\alpha\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\right]dV.
$$
The two interior <Calculus of variations>[variations] are independent. At a smooth <Local minimum>[minimum] their coefficients must vanish, so the <curl> is <Orthogonality>[perpendicular] to both potential <gradients> and hence parallel to $\mathbf B$ wherever $\mathbf B\ne0$:
$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda(\mathbf x)\mathbf B,
\qquad\mathbf J\times\mathbf B=0,
\quad\mathbf J=\frac1{\mu_0}\nabla\times\mathbf B.}
$$
This is a <force-free magnetic field>: the <Lorentz force density> vanishes. Taking the <divergence> also gives $\mathbf B\cdot\nabla\lambda=0$, so the proportionality factor is constant along a <Magnetic field line>[field line] but need not be the same on different lines.

The fixed-label <Calculus of variations>[variation] preserves the encoded <Magnetic field line>[field-line connection] rather than allowing arbitrary <Magnetic reconnection>[reconnection] during minimization. The argument proves a necessary condition for a smooth <Local minimum>[minimum], not that every <Force-free magnetic field>[force-free stationary field] is a stable energy <Local minimum>[minimum]. It also does not include <Pressure>[gas pressure] or <gravity> in the minimized <functional>.

= Solution

A <linear force-free magnetic field> has a spatially constant proportionality factor, $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda\mathbf B$. Taking its <curl> and using $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ gives
$$
-\nabla^2\mathbf B
=\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)
=\lambda\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda^2\mathbf B.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\mathbf B+\lambda^2\mathbf B=0.}
$$
The constant $\lambda$ has dimensions of inverse length in this convention. If it varied in space, the extra term $\nabla\lambda\times\mathbf B$ would remain, so the same <Helmholtz equation> would not follow merely from a <Force-free magnetic field>[nonlinear force-free condition].

= Solution

**Use the original scanned <boundary condition>.** The scan fixes the <Magnetic field>[normal-field amplitude] at the corner $x=z=0$. The supplied TeX drops the condition $x=0$ and would instead prescribe a constant normal field on the entire bottom. Those are different <Boundary value problem>[boundary-value problems]; the solution below uses the legible original scan.

Choose a positive integer $n$, put $k=n\pi/a$, and seek a <Separation of variables>[separated decaying mode]. A suitable <Cartesian magnetic flux function>[flux function] is
$$
A(x,z)=\frac{B_0}{k}\sin(kx)e^{-qz},\qquad
\mathbf B=(-\partial_z A,\lambda A,\partial_x A).
$$
This is a <decaying linear force-free magnetic arcade>. The first and third <Curl>[curl components] then satisfy the <Force-free magnetic field>[force-free equation] automatically; its second component requires $-(\partial_x^2+\partial_z^2)A=\lambda^2A$. Thus
$$
\boxed{q=\sqrt{k^2-\lambda^2},\qquad
\mathbf B=B_0e^{-qz}
\left(\frac qk\sin kx,\frac\lambda k\sin kx,\cos kx\right).}
$$
Direct <Derivative>[differentiation] gives $\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0$ and $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\lambda\mathbf B$. Both horizontal components vanish at $x=0,a$, and $B_z(0,0)=B_0$. The <magnetic field> on the bottom is $B_0\cos kx$, not a uniform $B_0$.

A strictly decaying real <normal mode> requires $q>0$, so
$$
\boxed{|\lambda|<n\pi/a.}
$$
For the fundamental, single-arcade choice $n=1$, this becomes $|\lambda|<\pi/a$. Equality gives no vertical <exponential decay>, while a larger magnitude gives an oscillatory rather than decaying vertical dependence for that <Normal mode>[mode]. The data do not explicitly fix the <Normal mode>[horizontal mode] number. If arbitrarily fine higher-mode arcades are permitted, every finite real $\lambda$ admits some integer $n$ with $n\pi/a>|\lambda|$. The fundamental restriction must therefore not be advertised as a necessary bound for every possible field satisfying only the stated pointwise and wall data.

For a fundamental <Magnetic field line>[field line], $A$ is constant along its projection in the $(x,z)$ <plane>. If $0<s<1$ denotes its normalized flux-function value, its arch is
$$
z=\frac1q\log\frac{\sin(kx)}s,
\qquad x_-=\frac{\arcsin s}{k},\quad
x_+=\frac{\pi-\arcsin s}{k}.
$$
The maximum height is $q^{-1}\log(1/s)$ at $x=a/2$. In the horizontal projection,
$$
\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{B_y}{B_x}=\frac\lambda q,
\qquad y-y_-=\frac\lambda q(x-x_-).}
$$
Thus the vertical projections are arches and the horizontal projections are straight sheared segments joining their paired footpoints. Positive $\lambda$ gives positive horizontal slope, negative $\lambda$ reverses the shear, and $\lambda=0$ gives an unsheared <Magnetic scalar potential>[potential arcade]. Higher $n$ repeats such cells across the width.

{title=Vertical arches and horizontal shear of a decaying linear force-free arcade}
{description=An original fundamental-mode example has lambda divided by k equal to 0.6 and q divided by k equal to 0.8. Paired colours identify the same projected field lines; arrows follow the magnetic field for positive B0. The horizontal segments display the slope lambda divided by q.}

The transcription's alternative uniform-bottom condition would be inconsistent with <exponential decay> and zero side flux for $B_0\ne0$: <Solenoidal vector field>[solenoidality] gives $d\int_0^aB_z\,dx/dz=-B_x(a,z)+B_x(0,z)=0$, whereas a uniform nonzero bottom would give a <Magnetic flux>[conserved flux] $aB_0$ that cannot decay to zero. This integral check is an independent reason not to silently solve that mistranscribed problem.

= Solution

Take $\mathbf R=\mathbf r_2-\mathbf r_1$ and label each <Hookean dumbbell model>[dumbbell] once. Locally assume its <probability density> and <number density> vary negligibly over a connector length. The <oriented connector crossing count> follows geometrically: a connector crosses a small <plane> element precisely when its midpoint lies within a slab of normal thickness $|\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n|$. For configurations in $d^3R$, the **unsigned** crossing number per unit area is therefore
$$
\boxed{n|\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n|\psi(\mathbf x,\mathbf R,t)\,d^3R.}
$$
The expression without the <absolute value> is an oriented crossing count, or an unsigned count restricted to $\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n\ge0$. It cannot be an unrestricted nonnegative number: a connector $\mathbf R=-R\mathbf n$ gives a negative printed expression despite physically intersecting the <plane>.

To obtain <traction>, keep the <force> orientation as well. For $\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n>0$, the <Hooke's law>[spring force] on the bead below the <plane> exerted by the bead above it is $H\mathbf R$. For the opposite orientation it is $-H\mathbf R$. Multiplying that <force> by the unsigned crossing count in either case gives
$$
d\mathbf t=nH\mathbf R(\mathbf R\cdot\mathbf n)\psi\,d^3R.
$$
Integrating over all labelled configurations yields
$$
\mathbf t=\left[nH\int\mathbf R\mathbf R^T\psi\,d^3R\right]\mathbf n,
\qquad
\boxed{\boldsymbol\sigma_{\rm spring}=nH\langle\mathbf R\mathbf R^T\rangle.}
$$
There is no extra factor of two: each <Hookean dumbbell model>[dumbbell] was labelled and counted once, and the orientation-dependent <force> has already supplied the sign. This is the local <Continuum mechanics>[continuum] <Cauchy stress tensor>[stress approximation]; resolving appreciable spatial <Calculus of variations>[variation] across a finite connector would require a nonlocal <stress> construction.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Let $p_T=nk_BT$, $C=\langle\mathbf R\mathbf R^T\rangle$, and $M=I+\alpha\sigma$, so the single-bead <Hydrodynamic mobility matrix>[mobility] is $M/\zeta$. Assume an <Incompressible flow>[incompressible solvent], constant $n,H,T,\zeta$, negligible <Inertia>[bead inertia], and a symmetric <Positive-definite matrix>[positive-definite] $M$. The last requirement makes the <Hydrodynamic resistance matrix>[resistance] invertible and <Diffusion>[Brownian diffusion] physically admissible. At a given position and time, $M$ depends on the moment $C$ but not on the individual configuration $\mathbf R$.

Use the source's <gradient> convention $K_{ij}=\partial_i u_j$, and write $G=K^T$ for the <Velocity gradient>[component-first gradient]. Then $\mathbf u(\mathbf r_2)-\mathbf u(\mathbf r_1)\simeq G\mathbf R$. The <Hooke's law>[spring forces] on the two beads are $\mathbf F_1=H\mathbf R$ and $\mathbf F_2=-H\mathbf R$. Their overdamped <Fokker-Planck probability current>[probability currents] are
$$
\mathbf j_i=\mathbf u_i\psi+\frac M\zeta
\left(\mathbf F_i\psi-k_BT\nabla_{\mathbf r_i}\psi\right).
$$
Here the <Brownian motion>[Brownian term] written as $-k_BT\nabla\psi$ is a <force> weighted by <probability density>. Its corresponding conditional <Osmotic pressure>[osmotic force] is $-k_BT\nabla\log\psi$; treating the given term as an unweighted deterministic <force> per bead would not produce the stated <Fokker-Planck equation>[Fokker–Planck equation].

With midpoint $\mathbf x=(\mathbf r_1+\mathbf r_2)/2$, the derivatives are $\nabla_{r_1}=\tfrac12\nabla_x-\nabla_R$ and $\nabla_{r_2}=\tfrac12\nabla_x+\nabla_R$. Subtracting the bead currents gives the <Fokker-Planck probability current>[configuration current]
$$
\boxed{\mathbf j_R=G\mathbf R\psi-
\frac{2H}{\zeta}M\mathbf R\psi-
\frac{2k_BT}{\zeta}M\nabla_R\psi.}
$$
The <Fokker-Planck probability current>[midpoint current] is $\mathbf u\psi$ after neglecting the specified spatial <Diffusion>[Brownian diffusion]. <Continuity equation>[Configuration probability conservation] therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\partial_t\psi+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla_x\psi+
\nabla_R\cdot\left(G\mathbf R\psi-\frac{2H}{\zeta}M\mathbf R\psi\right)
=\frac{2k_BT}{\zeta}\nabla_R\cdot(M\nabla_R\psi).}
$$
Since $G\mathbf R$ has components $R_j\partial_j u_i$, this agrees with the source's $\mathbf R\cdot\nabla\mathbf u$. The factor two in both spring relaxation and configuration <Diffusion coefficient>[diffusivity] comes from relative motion of both beads.

The <second moment> $C$ is the unnormalized <conformation tensor>. Now multiply by $R_iR_j$ and integrate over <configuration space>. Assume sufficiently decaying tails so every boundary term at infinite separation vanishes. The affine current gives $GC+CG^T$; spring drift gives $-(2H/\zeta)(MC+CM)$; two <integrations by parts> of <diffusion> give $(4k_BT/\zeta)M$. Hence
$$
D_tC-GC-CG^T=-\frac{2H}{\zeta}(MC+CM)+\frac{4k_BT}{\zeta}M,
\qquad D_t=\partial_t+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla_x.
$$
Because $\sigma=nHC-p_TI$, $M$ is a <polynomial> in $C$ and <Commuting operators>[commutes] with it. Thus
$$
C^{\nabla}\equiv D_tC-GC-CG^T
=-\frac{4H}{\zeta}M\left(C-\frac{k_BT}{H}I\right).
$$
This is the <Hookean dumbbell conformation equation with anisotropic mobility>, an exact <Moment closure>[second-moment closure] within the stated model; no <Gaussian distribution>[Gaussian approximation] to $\psi$ was needed.

The <upper-convected derivative> of the <Identity matrix>[identity] is $I^{\nabla}=-(G+G^T)$. Applying it to the <Stress>[polymer stress] therefore gives
$$
\sigma^{\nabla}=-\frac{4H}{\zeta}M\sigma+p_T(G+G^T).
$$
Consequently the coefficients and the closed <Constitutive equation>[stress equation] are
$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{\zeta}{4H},\qquad
\mu=\lambda p_T=\frac{n k_BT\zeta}{4H},\qquad
\lambda\sigma^{\nabla}+\sigma+\alpha\sigma^2
=\mu(K+K^T),}
$$
where $\sigma^{\nabla}=D_t\sigma-\sigma K-K^T\sigma$ in the printed convention. Here $\lambda$ is a <polymer relaxation time>, unrelated to the inverse-length parameter of question <1/c>; $\mu$ is the <Viscosity>[polymer viscosity] scale. The coefficient $\alpha$ has inverse-stress units. In the <dimensional mobility parameter in the Giesekus model>, the dimensionless factor is $b=\alpha p_T$, not the dimensional $\alpha$ printed in this problem.

The <Upper-convected derivative>[upper-convected rate] removes the affine <Conformation tensor>[stretching] and <Rotation>[rotation of material connectors]. If $\dot F=GF$, then a <tensor> transported purely as $C=FC_0F^T$ has $C^{\nabla}=0$. Under a time-dependent <Rotation>[rigid rotation] $Q$, the <Objective time derivative>[rate transforms] as $Q C^{\nabla}Q^T$: this is <material frame indifference>, not an ordinary componentwise time derivative. The resulting equation is the <Giesekus model> in these dimensional conventions.

The <Isotropic tensor>[isotropic subtraction] is fixed by **zero <Stress>[polymer extra stress] at <thermal equilibrium>**. With no flow and <Hydrodynamic mobility matrix>[positive mobility], the <stationary distribution> is
$$
\psi_{\rm eq}=\left(\frac{H}{2\pi k_BT}\right)^{3/2}
\exp\left(-\frac{H R^2}{2k_BT}\right),\qquad
C_{\rm eq}=\frac{k_BT}{H}I,
$$
so $\boxed{\sigma_{\rm eq}=0}$. The <constitutive equation> correspondingly has a zero-stress quiescent state and a <Newtonian fluid>[Newtonian low-rate limit] $\sigma\simeq\mu(K+K^T)$. Without subtracting exactly $p_TI$, the spring contribution would retain an equilibrium <Isotropic tensor>[isotropic stress] and the equation would acquire corresponding reference terms. Because <Hydrodynamic mobility matrix>[mobility] itself depends on $\sigma$, that reference cannot simply be shifted while retaining the same model. The separate <Pressure>[fluid pressure] enforcing <Incompressible flow>[incompressibility] is not included in this <Stress>[polymer extra stress].

= Solution
{parent=c}

For <steady uniaxial extension of the Giesekus model>, the component-first <velocity gradient> is <Diagonal matrix>[diagonal], $G=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma,-\gamma/2,-\gamma/2)$. For a <Steady state>[steady] spatially uniform <Cauchy stress tensor>[diagonal stress], the <material derivative> vanishes and the <Upper-convected derivative>[convected terms] are $-2\gamma\sigma_{11}$ and $+\gamma\sigma_{22}$. The off-diagonal equations vanish identically, and symmetry permits equal transverse components. The remaining equations are
$$
\boxed{\alpha\sigma_{11}^2+(1-2\lambda\gamma)\sigma_{11}-2\mu\gamma=0,\qquad
\alpha\sigma_{22}^2+(1+\lambda\gamma)\sigma_{22}+\mu\gamma=0.}
$$
Set $W=\lambda\gamma\ge0$, $b=\alpha p_T$ and $s_i=\sigma_{ii}/p_T$, with $\mu=\lambda p_T$. For $b>0$, the branches continuously connected to zero <stress> at zero rate are
$$
\boxed{s_1=\frac{2W-1+\sqrt{(1-2W)^2+8bW}}{2b},\qquad
s_2=\frac{-(1+W)+\sqrt{(1+W)^2-4bW}}{2b}.}
$$
A sufficient nondegenerate range for physical solutions at every nonnegative rate is $0<b<1$. Indeed $M=(1-b)I+bA$ with $A=HC/(k_BT)$, so positive <Conformation tensor>[conformation] implies <Hydrodynamic mobility matrix>[positive mobility]. The longitudinal <quadratic equation> is nonpositive at $s_1=0$ and has a nonnegative <root of a polynomial>. The transverse <quadratic equation> has values $b-1<0$ at $s_2=-1$ and $W\ge0$ at $s_2=0$, so its larger <root of a polynomial> lies in $(-1,0]$. Thus the displayed branches satisfy $s_i>-1$, giving $A_i=1+s_i>0$. The other <Root of a polynomial>[algebraic roots] do not provide a physical branch connected to the <Boltzmann distribution>[equilibrium distribution]. For constant positive <Diagonal matrix>[diagonal] $A$, the associated stationary configuration distribution is a <Multivariate normal distribution>[Gaussian with covariance] $C=(k_BT/H)A$.

For positive $\alpha$ on the physical branch, the <Stress>[stretching stress] remains finite through $W=1/2$. At that rate,
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{11}=\sqrt{\frac{p_T}{\alpha}}.}
$$
At large <Extensional strain rate>[extension rate],
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{11}\sim\frac{2\lambda\gamma}{\alpha},\qquad
\frac{\sigma_{11}}\gamma\longrightarrow\frac{2\lambda}{\alpha}.}
$$
Thus the <stress> continues to grow, but the <Extensional viscosity>[uniaxial extensional viscosity] $\eta_E=(\sigma_{11}-\sigma_{22})/\gamma$ approaches the finite plateau $2\lambda/\alpha$ rather than developing a finite-rate pole. The <Stress>[transverse stress] approaches $-p_T$ as its <conformation tensor> variance becomes small. At small rate the two <stresses> are $2\mu\gamma$ and $-\mu\gamma$, so $\eta_E\to3\mu$.

When $\alpha=0$, the physical <Steady state>[steady] branch instead is
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{11}=\frac{2\mu\gamma}{1-2\lambda\gamma},\qquad
\sigma_{22}=-\frac{\mu\gamma}{1+\lambda\gamma}.}
$$
The <Stress>[stretching stress] diverges as $W\to1/2$ from below. Above that threshold its algebraic continuation has $1+s_1=1/(1-2W)<0$ and cannot be a <Conformation tensor>[conformation] <Covariance matrix>[covariance]. There is no physical finite <Steady state>[steady] <Conformation tensor>[stretching] state. The transient equation
$$
\lambda\dot\sigma_{11}=(2W-1)\sigma_{11}+2p_TW
$$
gives linear growth at $W=1/2$ and exponential growth at larger rates, the <Hookean dumbbell model>[Hookean] <Coil-stretch transition>[coil-stretch] failure of the <Upper-convected Maxwell model>[upper-convected Maxwell limit].

**Parameter qualification.** Positivity of $\alpha$ alone, without a restriction on $b=\alpha nk_BT$, does not ensure real physical <Steady state>[steady] solutions for the entire <tensor>. For example $b=2$, $W=1$ makes the transverse equation $2s_2^2+2s_2+1=0$, which has no real root. Its <diffusion>/<Hydrodynamic resistance matrix>[resistance] interpretation is already inadmissible for some conformations. The physically positive-mobility regime stated above avoids that counterexample; the source's positive-$\alpha$ case must not be taken as a guarantee for arbitrary parameters.
