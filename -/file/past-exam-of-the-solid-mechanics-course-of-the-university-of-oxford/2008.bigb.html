= Solution
{parent=1}

By the <spectral theorem> a <symmetric positive definite matrix> $C$ has $C=Q\operatorname{diag}(c_i)Q^{\mathsf T}$, with $Q$ <orthogonal> and $c_i>0$. Define $S=Q\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{c_i})Q^{\mathsf T}$. It is <symmetric> <positive definite> and $S^2=C$. For <uniqueness>, any <symmetric> <positive definite> square root $T$ commutes with $C=T^2$, hence preserves each <eigenspace> of $C$. On its $c$-<eigenspace>, $T$ is <symmetric> <positive definite> and all its <eigenvalues> have square $c$, so all equal $\sqrt c$. Therefore $T=S$, including repeated <eigenspaces>.

For $\det F>0$, put
$$
\boxed{U=(F^{\mathsf T}F)^{1/2},\quad R=FU^{-1},\quad V=RUR^{\mathsf T}=(FF^{\mathsf T})^{1/2}.}
$$
Then $R^{\mathsf T}R=I$ and $\det R=\det F/\det U>0$, so $R\in SO(3)$; also $F=RU=VR$. Any other <polar decomposition in continuum mechanics>[right polar decomposition] has $F^{\mathsf T}F=U^2$, so <uniqueness> of the positive root fixes $U$ and then $R$. The left factor is consequently fixed too.

The principal stretches $v_i$ are the positive <eigenvalues> of $U$, equivalently the <singular values> of $F$. For a <unit vector> $N=\sum_i c_iN_i$ in an orthonormal <eigenbasis> of $U$,
$$
|FN|^2=N^{\mathsf T}U^2N=\sum_i v_i^2c_i^2\leq v_{\max}^2\sum_i c_i^2=v_{\max}^2.
$$
Thus $\boxed{\max_{|N|=1}|FN|=v_{\max}}$, attained precisely by the <unit vectors> in the <eigenspace> of $U$ belonging to $v_{\max}$. With a simple largest <eigenvalue> these are its two opposite unit <eigenvectors>; degeneracy allows every unit combination within that <eigenspace>.

For the <simple shear>,
$$
F=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad C=F^{\mathsf T}F=\begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma&0\\\gamma&1+\gamma^2&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
A line with nonzero direction $N$ keeps its length exactly when
$$
|FN|^2-|N|^2=\gamma N_2(2N_1+\gamma N_2)=0.
$$
Hence $\boxed{N_2=0\quad\text{or}\quad2N_1+\gamma N_2=0}$, with $N_3$ arbitrary. Every segment in the cube <linear dependence>[parallel] to one of these directions preserves length; there is no restriction on its starting point other than remaining in the cube.

The two in-<plane> stretches are $v_\pm=(\sqrt{4+\gamma^2}\pm\gamma)/2$, and the third is one. A maximizing direction for $\gamma>0$ is $N\parallel(1,v_+,0)$, since the $C$ <eigenvalue> equation gives $N_2/N_1=(v_+^2-1)/\gamma=v_+$. Therefore $\boxed{N\to\pm(1,1,0)/\sqrt2}$ as $\gamma\downarrow0$. At exactly zero shear all directions tie; the limit refers to the selected maximizing direction for positive shear. These are the <length-preserving directions and principal directions of simple shear>.

= Solution
{parent=2}

<Cauchy's stress hypothesis> says that <traction> across a cut depends locally on its point, time and oriented normal, rather than on the global shape of the cut. Here all areas and normals are referential: $E$ is a fixed material region in reference coordinates, $\rho_R$ its mass <density> per reference volume, $v$ the current particle velocity, $b$ <body force> per unit mass, and $s_R$ force per unit reference area. The time derivative on the left is total material momentum rate; the two right terms are contact and <body forces>.

A shrinking pillbox gives $s_R(-N)=-s_R(N)$. In a shrinking tetrahedron the volume contributions scale as its linear size cubed, whereas face forces scale as size squared. Their leading balance expresses <traction> on an arbitrary face as the normal-component-weighted sum of <tractions> on three coordinate faces. Defining those three <tractions> as tensor columns gives $\boxed{s_R(N)=T_RN}$. Applying the <divergence theorem> to <conservation of momentum>[momentum balance] and localizing over arbitrary $E$ then yields
$$
\boxed{\rho_R\dot v=\operatorname{Div}T_R+\rho_Rb.}
$$
The referential tensor is the <first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor>[first Piola-Kirchhoff stress] and need not be <symmetric>; it should not be silently identified with the spatial <Cauchy stress tensor>[Cauchy stress].

<Incompressibility> is $\boxed{\det F=1}$. For a <homogeneous hyperelastic material> the referential <constitutive equation>[constitutive relation] becomes
$$
\boxed{T_R=DW(F)-p\operatorname{cof}F=DW(F)-pF^{-\mathsf T}\quad(\det F=1),}
$$
where the undetermined scalar <pressure> $p$ enforces the <constraint>. Homogeneity removes explicit reference-position dependence from $W$, not the possible spatial variation of that <pressure>.

For the <neo-Hookean solid>[neo-Hookean] energy, $DW=2\alpha F$. For the positive diagonal ansatz the nominal principal stresses are $t_i=2\alpha v_i-p/v_i$. Since $F$ is constant, <equilibrium> requires $\operatorname{cof}F\nabla p=0$, so $p$ is constant. Stress-free endfaces give $\boxed{p=2\alpha v_3^2>0}$. The side normals include $(0,-1,0)$ and $\pm(\sqrt3/2,1/2,0)$, so their prescribed <tractions> force $\boxed{t_1=t_2=T}$.

For fixed positive $p$, the scalar function $2\alpha v-p/v$ is <strictly increasing> on $v>0$. Thus equality of the two nominal stresses implies $v_1=v_2=v$. <Incompressibility> gives $v_3=v^{-2}$, and substitution gives
$$
\boxed{T=2\alpha(v-v^{-5}),\qquad p=2\alpha v^{-4}.}
$$
The load function has derivative $2\alpha(1+5v^{-6})>0$, tends to minus infinity as $v\downarrow0$ and to infinity as $v\to\infty$. It therefore determines exactly one positive $v$ for every specified load, with $v\geq1$ for outward <tension>[tensile] $T\geq0$. The resulting constant stress satisfies <equilibrium> and every prescribed face <traction>, proving existence and <uniqueness> within the requested positive diagonal class. It does not claim <uniqueness> among all possible nonhomogeneous <deformations>. This is the <equibiaxial dead loading of a neo-Hookean prism>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Put $n=x/R$ and $q=r(R)/R$. Differentiating $y=qx$ uses $\nabla R=n$ and $\nabla q=(r'-q)n/R$. Hence
$$
\boxed{F=qI+(r'-q)n\otimes n.}
$$
Its <eigenvalue>[radial eigenvalue] is $r'$ and its two tangential <eigenvalues> are $q$. They are positive by hypothesis, so they are also the principal stretches:
$$
\boxed{v_1=r',\qquad v_2=v_3=q.}
$$
For an <isotropic> <strain energy density>[stored energy] the <nominal stress> has the same radial/tangential <projectors>,
$$
P=DW(F)=\Phi_1 n\otimes n+\Phi_2(I-n\otimes n),
$$
where symmetry of $\Phi$ gives equal tangential derivatives. Since $\operatorname{Div}(n\otimes n)=2n/R$ and the scalar coefficients depend only on $R$,
$$
\operatorname{Div}P=\left[\frac{d\Phi_1}{dR}+\frac2R(\Phi_1-\Phi_2)\right]n.
$$
Thus <equilibrium> is exactly $\boxed{d\Phi_1/dR=2(\Phi_2-\Phi_1)/R}$.

For the particular energy define $\delta=r'q^2=\det F>0$. Its two derivatives are
$$
\Phi_1=\mu+h'(\delta)q^2,\qquad\Phi_2=\mu+h'(\delta)r'q.
$$
Also $q'=(r'-q)/R$. Differentiating the first formula and substituting the <equilibrium> equation gives
$$
h''(\delta)\delta'q^2+2h'(\delta)qq'=2h'(\delta)q(r'-q)/R=2h'(\delta)qq'.
$$
The last terms cancel. Strict positivity of $h''$ and $q$ yields $\boxed{\delta'=0}$, so $r^2r'=D R^2$ for one positive constant $D$. Integration gives $r^3=DR^3+C$.

The displacement data mean $r(1)=\lambda_1$ and $r(2)=2\lambda_2$, not $r(2)=\lambda_2$. Solving the two scalar equations gives
$$
\boxed{D=\frac{8\lambda_2^3-\lambda_1^3}{7},\qquad C=\frac{8(\lambda_1^3-\lambda_2^3)}7,\qquad r=(DR^3+C)^{1/3}.}
$$
The given inequality makes $D>0$. Since $r^3$ increases from $\lambda_1^3>0$ to $8\lambda_2^3$, it is positive on the whole shell, and $r'=DR^2/r^2>0$. Thus the formula is <smooth> and <admissible> and satisfies <equilibrium>. Conversely every <admissible> radial <equilibrium> had to have this constant <determinant> and these two integration constants, proving <uniqueness>. This is a <constant-Jacobian radial equilibrium for a stretch-sum energy>.

= Solution
{parent=4}

On each half-space the <deformation> is affine. Continuity on the separating <plane> makes the two affine constants equal and gives $(A-B)t=0$ for every <tangent vector> $t\perp N$. Decomposing any $x$ into its tangential part and $(x\cdot N)N$ gives
$$
(A-B)x=[(A-B)N](N\cdot x),\qquad\boxed{A-B=a\otimes N,\quad a=(A-B)N.}
$$
This is the <Hadamard jump condition>. Since $B=A-a\otimes N=A(I-A^{-1}a\otimes N)$, the <matrix determinant lemma> gives
$$
\det B=\det A(1-N\cdot A^{-1}a).
$$
Both <determinants> are positive, hence $\boxed{A^{-1}a\cdot N<1}$.

Write $a_1=\eta_1$, $a_2=\eta_2$, $S=a_1^2+a_2^2$ and $\Delta=a_2^2-a_1^2\ne0$. The two proposed angles have $c=2a_1a_2/S$ and $s=\kappa\Delta/S$, and $c^2+s^2=1$. Therefore the displayed <rotations> lie in $SO(3)$. Direct multiplication gives
$$
RU_2-U_1=\frac\Delta S\begin{pmatrix}-a_2&-\kappa a_2&0\\\kappa a_1&a_1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
=\boxed{\frac\Delta S(-a_2,\kappa a_1,0)\otimes(1,\kappa,0).}
$$
This is a <rank-one connection> for each sign. With a unit normal one may choose $N=(1,\kappa,0)/\sqrt2$ and jump vector $a=\sqrt2\Delta(-a_2,\kappa a_1,0)/S$. The two <rotations> are distinct because $\Delta\ne0$; the permitted at-most-two result therefore proves these are all solutions. Flipping both $a$ and $N$ changes no connection.

It remains to test the material <plane>. Because $y(0)=0$, the affine constants vanish. Let $t=(-p_2,p_1,0)$, so the <plane> is spanned by $t$ and $e_3$. Both <deformation gradients> send $e_3$ to $a_1e_3$. If the <plane> is not the <twin plane>, it cuts the two half-spaces; its two image halves are coplanar exactly when the in-<plane> vectors $Bt$ and $At$ are <linear dependence>[parallel]. Put $d=(-a_2,\kappa a_1)$ and $n=(1,\kappa)$ in their two-dimensional sections. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\det_2(Bt,At)
&=\frac\Delta S\det_2(Bt,d)(n\cdot t)\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\kappa a_1a_2\Delta}{S}(p_1-\kappa p_2)^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Its prefactor is nonzero, so <coplanarity> requires $p_1=\kappa p_2$, precisely $p\parallel(1,\kappa,0)\parallel N$. Conversely, on that <twin plane> $(A-B)x=0$, so the two <affine maps> agree and its image is a <plane>. Thus $\boxed{\Pi\text{ remains plane}\iff p\parallel N}$. All other allowed <planes> acquire a kink along their intersection with the twin. This is the <planarity test across a cubic-to-tetragonal twin>.
