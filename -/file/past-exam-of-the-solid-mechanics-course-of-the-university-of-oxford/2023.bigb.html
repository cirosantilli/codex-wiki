Solutions to all three questions of the <solid mechanics course of the University of Oxford> Part C examination, sat on 8 June 2023. Source: https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/request_for_mathematics_examinat/response/3129786/attach/3/FOI%20202507%20740%20Data.pdf#page=313[original released PDF, inclusive bundle pages 313–316; paper pages 1–4]. The <torque> normalization in Question 1 follows the printed integral; the signed <pressure> in Question 2 is positive for compression.

= Solution
{parent=a}
{c}

Write the <first strain invariant> in components of the <deformation gradient>:
$$
I_1=\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf F\mathbf F^T)
=\sum_{i,I}F_{iI}^2.
$$
Here lower-case and upper-case indices refer to the <current configuration> and <reference configuration>, respectively. Differentiating gives $\partial I_1/\partial F_{iI}=2F_{iI}$. The <chain rule> therefore gives
$$
\frac{\partial W}{\partial F_{iI}}=2w'(I_1)F_{iI},
\qquad
\partial_{\mathbf F}W=2w'(I_1)\mathbf F.
$$
Substitution into the given <hyperelastic material> law, with its indicated <matrix transpose>, yields
$$
\boxed{\mathbf T
=\mathbf F(\partial_{\mathbf F}W)^T-p\mathbf1
=2w'(I_1)\mathbf F\mathbf F^T-p\mathbf1
=2\frac{\partial w}{\partial I_1}\mathbf B-p\mathbf1.}
$$
The <left Cauchy-Green deformation tensor> is $\mathbf B=\mathbf F\mathbf F^T$. The scalar $p$ is the <Lagrange multiplier> enforcing <incompressibility>, rather than a function fixed by the <strain energy density> alone. The <identity matrix> $\mathbf1$ makes this an isotropic <constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress>.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

Use separate moving <orthonormal bases> in the reference and current <cylindrical coordinate systems>. For <pure torsion of an incompressible cylinder>, the <deformation map> is
$$
\mathbf x(R,\Theta,Z)=R\mathbf e_r(\Theta+\tau Z)+Z\mathbf e_z.
$$
The <unit vectors> depend on the angle: $\partial_\theta\mathbf e_r=\mathbf e_\theta$ and $\partial_\theta\mathbf e_\theta=-\mathbf e_r$. Consequently
$$
\partial_R\mathbf x=\mathbf e_r,\qquad
\partial_\Theta\mathbf x=R\mathbf e_\theta,\qquad
\partial_Z\mathbf x=\tau R\mathbf e_\theta+\mathbf e_z.
$$
In particular, the last derivative includes the rotation of $\mathbf e_r$ along the cylinder. The material <gradient> has the reference coordinate scale factor $1/R$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf F
&=(\partial_R\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_R
+\frac1R(\partial_\Theta\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_\Theta
+(\partial_Z\mathbf x)\otimes\mathbf E_Z\\
&=\mathbf e_r\otimes\mathbf E_R
+\mathbf e_\theta\otimes\mathbf E_\Theta
+(\tau r\mathbf e_\theta+\mathbf e_z)\otimes\mathbf E_Z.
\end{aligned}
$$
The <outer products> specify the images of the three reference <unit vectors>. Thus, with current basis vectors indexing rows and reference basis vectors indexing columns,
$$
\boxed{\mathbf F=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&\tau r\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Its <determinant> is one, checking <incompressibility>. The radius is unchanged, so the current outer radius in part (d) is $a=A$. The formula at the coordinate axis is understood by continuity in <Cartesian coordinates>.

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

Multiplying the <deformation gradient> by its <matrix transpose> gives the <left Cauchy-Green deformation tensor> and its <trace>:
$$
\mathbf B=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0\\
0&1+\tau^2r^2&\tau r\\
0&\tau r&1
\end{pmatrix},\qquad I_1=3+\tau^2r^2.
$$
The derivative of the <strain energy density> is
$$
\hat w'(I_1)=\mu_1+\mu_2 I_1^n.
$$
For brevity put $g(r)=\mu_1+\mu_2(3+\tau^2r^2)^n$. Part (a) gives the <Cauchy stress tensor>, in the current <cylindrical coordinate system> <orthonormal basis>, as
$$
\boxed{\mathbf T=\begin{pmatrix}
2g-p&0&0\\
0&2g(1+\tau^2r^2)-p&2g\tau r\\
0&2g\tau r&2g-p
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
In particular, the end-face <shear stress> is $T_{\theta z}=2g\tau r$. The undetermined <pressure> $p$ affects the normal <stresses> but does not affect this <shear stress>. No normal <traction> conditions have been specified to determine $p$.

Using $a=A$ and the end-face <shear stress> from part (c), the printed <torque> normalization gives
$$
M(\tau)=2\tau\int_0^A\left[\mu_1+\mu_2(3+\tau^2r^2)^n\right]r^3\,dr.
$$
The <binomial theorem> expands the remaining <integral> into powers of $r$:
$$
(3+\tau^2r^2)^n
=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk3^{n-k}\tau^{2k}r^{2k}.
$$
Integrating each term gives an expression valid also at $\tau=0$:
$$
\boxed{M(\tau)=\frac{\mu_1A^4}{2}\tau
+\mu_2\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk
\frac{3^{n-k}A^{2k+4}}{k+2}\tau^{2k+1}.}
$$
For example, $n=1$ gives $M=(\mu_1+3\mu_2)A^4\tau/2+\mu_2A^6\tau^3/3$. The <torque> is an odd <polynomial> in the twist per unit reference length, as expected when reversing the twist reverses the end-face <shear stress>.

For a physical end-face <torque> $N$, integration over the azimuth normally gives
$$
N=\int_0^{2\pi}\!\int_0^A T_{\theta z}r^2\,dr\,d\theta=2\pi M.
$$
The original PDF explicitly defines $M$ without this $2\pi$, so the boxed answer retains its convention. Since $\mathbf T$ is the <Cauchy stress tensor>, its shear component here is spatial $T_{\theta z}$; the printed capital $Z$ in $T_{\theta Z}$ does not require a change to a <nominal stress tensor>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

Only the $k=0$ term of the <polynomial> contributes to the limit:
$$
\frac{M(\tau)}\tau
=\frac{A^4}{2}(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2)+O(\tau^2).
$$
Thus the requested <torsional stiffness per unit twist> is
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{A^4}{2}(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2).}
$$
The small-<strain> <shear modulus> is $G=2\hat w'(3)=2(\mu_1+3^n\mu_2)$. Therefore $\alpha=GA^4/4$, consistently with the usual circular-shaft <torque> relation after restoring the $2\pi$ factor. If the prescribed end rotation is the <angle> $\varphi=\tau L$, the physical <torsional stiffness> is $dN/d\varphi=2\pi\alpha/L$ at zero twist. This also explains why $L$ does not enter the requested $\alpha$.

= Solution
{parent=a}
{c}

The <radial unit vector in spherical coordinates> is $\mathbf e_R=\mathbf X/R$, so $\mathbf X\otimes\mathbf X/R^2=\mathbf e_R\otimes\mathbf e_R$. The <identity matrix> is the sum of the three basis <outer products>. Substitution in the given <deformation gradient> gives
$$
\mathbf F=f'(R)\mathbf e_R\otimes\mathbf e_R
+\frac{f(R)}R\left(\mathbf e_\Theta\otimes\mathbf e_\Theta
+\mathbf e_\Phi\otimes\mathbf e_\Phi\right)
=\operatorname{diag}\left(f'(R),\frac{f(R)}R,\frac{f(R)}R\right).
$$
The reference and current radial directions coincide for this <spherically symmetric deformation>. The positive <principal stretches> are therefore $\lambda_r=f'(R)$ and $\lambda_\theta=\lambda_\phi=f(R)/R$. <Incompressibility> imposes
$$
f'(R)\frac{f(R)^2}{R^2}=1,
\qquad \frac{d}{dR}f(R)^3=3R^2.
$$
Integrating and using $f(A)=a$ gives
$$
\boxed{r=f(R)=\left(R^3+a^3-A^3\right)^{1/3},\qquad
b^3=B^3+a^3-A^3.}
$$
In particular $b^3-a^3=B^3-A^3$, expressing conservation of shell <volume>. Writing $\lambda=r/R$ also gives $\lambda_r=\lambda^{-2}$ and $\lambda_\theta=\lambda_\phi=\lambda$.

= Solution
{parent=i}
{c}

The <Cauchy momentum equation> in <static elastic equilibrium>, with zero <body force>, is $\operatorname{div}\mathbf T=\mathbf0$. The <Cauchy stress tensor> is symmetric. <Material isotropy> and the equal tangential <principal stretches> imply $t_\theta=t_\phi$, so its radial equation is
$$
\boxed{\frac{dt_r}{dr}+\frac2r(t_r-t_\theta)=0,\qquad
\text{equivalently}\quad
\frac{dt_r}{dr}=\frac2r(t_\theta-t_r).}
$$
All <stresses> and spatial derivatives in this equation are measured in the <current configuration>.

= ii
{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

Let $W_r,W_\theta,W_\phi$ denote derivatives of the <strain energy density> with respect to its three <principal stretches>. The <principal stress formula for incompressible hyperelasticity> is
$$
t_r=\lambda_rW_r-p,\qquad
t_\theta=\lambda_\theta W_\theta-p,\qquad
t_\phi=\lambda_\phi W_\phi-p.
$$
It follows by applying $\mathbf T=\mathbf F(\partial_{\mathbf F}W)^T-p\mathbf1$ in principal axes. Equal tangential <principal stretches> and <material isotropy> give $W_\theta=W_\phi$ at $(\lambda^{-2},\lambda,\lambda)$. Differentiating the reduced <strain energy density> by the <chain rule> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
h'(\lambda)&=-2\lambda^{-3}W_r+W_\theta+W_\phi\\
&=2\left(W_\theta-\lambda^{-3}W_r\right)
=\frac2\lambda(t_\theta-t_r).
\end{aligned}
$$
The unknown <pressure> cancels in this difference. Substituting in part (i), and then using the supplied derivative of $\lambda$, gives
$$
\frac{dt_r}{dr}=\frac{\lambda h'(\lambda)}r=\frac{h'(\lambda)}R,
\qquad
\frac{d\lambda}{dr}=\frac{1-\lambda^3}{R}.
$$
Consequently the desired <ordinary differential equation> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{dt_r}{d\lambda}=\frac{h'(\lambda)}{1-\lambda^3}.}
$$
For completeness, the supplied derivative follows from $R^3=r^3-(a^3-A^3)$: $dR/dr=r^2/R^2=\lambda^2$, and differentiating $\lambda=r/R$ gives $(1-\lambda^3)/R$. At the undeformed value $\lambda=1$ the division must be interpreted by a limit; the <spherically symmetric deformation> with $a=A$ has $\lambda\equiv1$ and should be handled directly by the radial equilibrium equation.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iii}
{c}

Take the inner surface to be free of <traction>, as appropriate to an empty cavity with no <surface tension>. At the outer surface the imposed <pressure> is compressive when positive. The outward <normal vectors> are $-\mathbf e_r$ at $r=a$ and $+\mathbf e_r$ at $r=b$, giving
$$
t_r(a)=0,\qquad t_r(b)=-P.
$$
Integrating the <ordinary differential equation> from part (ii) between the two surfaces gives
$$
-P=\int_{\lambda_a}^{\lambda_b}\frac{h'(\lambda)}{1-\lambda^3}\,d\lambda.
$$
The outer stretch follows from <incompressibility>:
$$
\lambda_b^3=\frac{b^3}{B^3}
=1+\frac{A^3}{B^3}(\lambda_a^3-1).
$$
Hence an expression entirely in terms of the inner stretch and the reference radii is
$$
\boxed{P(\lambda_a)=
\int_{\lambda_a}^{[1+(A/B)^3(\lambda_a^3-1)]^{1/3}}
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.}
$$
Equivalently $P=-\int_{\lambda_b}^{\lambda_a}h'(\lambda)/(\lambda^3-1)\,d\lambda$. For an expanding shell, $1<\lambda_b<\lambda_a$; when $h'>0$ on this range, $P<0$, correctly describing an outward tensile loading. If an inner <pressure> $P_i$ were present, the same derivation would give $P-P_i$ on the left. Thus the empty-cavity condition specifies the pressure reference needed for the requested answer.

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

First let $A\downarrow0$ at fixed positive cavity radius $a$ and fixed reference outer radius $B$. Then
$$
\lambda_a=\frac aA\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
\lambda_b\longrightarrow\left(1+\frac{a^3}{B^3}\right)^{1/3},
$$
and the <pressure> sustaining a finite cavity is
$$
P(a)=-\int_{(1+a^3/B^3)^{1/3}}^\infty
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.
$$
**The onset of cavitation is the additional limit $a\downarrow0$.** The outer stretch then tends to one, and the signed critical <pressure> is
$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{crit}}=-\int_1^\infty
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}\,d\lambda.}
$$
When this <improper integral> is positive and finite before the minus sign, the tensile loading magnitude is $-P_{\mathrm{crit}}>0$. Thus the cavity opens under tension, even though the load variable is named <pressure>. The critical value is independent of the original sphere radius $B$.

For $A=0$ and $a>0$, the <deformation map> is smooth away from $R=0$ but singular at the original centre: $r=(R^3+a^3)^{1/3}$ approaches $a$, rather than zero, as $R\downarrow0$. This is the <elastic cavitation> mechanism; it cannot be represented by a smooth <deformation map> through the centre.

= Solution
{parent=i}
{c}

The <neo-Hookean solid> corresponds to $\alpha=1$, with positive <shear modulus> $\mu$. Substitution of the <principal stretches> gives
$$
h(\lambda)=\frac\mu2(\lambda^{-4}+2\lambda^2-3),\qquad
h'(\lambda)=2\mu(\lambda-\lambda^{-5}).
$$
The identity $\lambda^6-1=(\lambda^3-1)(\lambda^3+1)$ simplifies the integrand:
$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
=2\mu\left(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-5}\right).
$$
The two <improper integrals> are elementary:
$$
\boxed{P_{\mathrm{crit}}
=-2\mu\left(\int_1^\infty\lambda^{-2}\,d\lambda
+\int_1^\infty\lambda^{-5}\,d\lambda\right)
=-2\mu\left(1+\frac14\right)=-\frac52\mu.}
$$
Thus the critical tensile <stress> magnitude is $5\mu/2$. As a check on the finite-sphere limit, part (c) gives
$$
P(a)=-2\mu\left(\lambda_b^{-1}+\frac14\lambda_b^{-4}\right),\qquad
\lambda_b=(1+a^3/B^3)^{1/3},
$$
which tends to $-5\mu/2$ as the cavity radius tends to zero.

{parent=d}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

For general $\alpha>0$, put $S(\lambda)=\lambda^{-4}+2\lambda^2$. The reduced <strain energy density> and its derivative are
$$
h(\lambda)=\frac\mu2\left[S(\lambda)^\alpha-3^\alpha\right],\qquad
h'(\lambda)=2\mu\alpha S(\lambda)^{\alpha-1}(\lambda-\lambda^{-5}).
$$
The same factorization as in part (i) yields
$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
=2\mu\alpha S(\lambda)^{\alpha-1}
\left(\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-5}\right).
$$
This form removes the apparent singularity at $\lambda=1$: its limit is $4\mu\alpha3^{\alpha-1}$, finite for every fixed $\alpha>0$. Thus only the upper endpoint can obstruct convergence of the <improper integral>.

As $\lambda\to\infty$, $S(\lambda)\sim2\lambda^2$, giving
$$
\frac{h'(\lambda)}{\lambda^3-1}
\sim\mu\alpha2^\alpha\lambda^{2\alpha-4}.
$$
For $\mu>0$, the <comparison test> with a power <integral> implies convergence exactly when $2\alpha-4<-1$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{0<\alpha<\frac32}
$$
is precisely the range with finite critical <pressure>, the <cavitation criterion for power-law strain energy>. At $\alpha=3/2$ the divergence is logarithmic; for larger $\alpha$ it is a power divergence. These materials require an unbounded tensile loading magnitude in this ideal model. The qualification $\mu>0$ excludes the degenerate zero-energy material.

= Solution
{parent=a}
{c}

The first equation is the local <balance of linear momentum>. Use $\mathsf M,\mathsf L,\mathsf T$ for the dimensions of <mass>, <length>, and <time>, to distinguish dimensional time from the <Cauchy stress tensor> $\mathbf T$.

* $\mathbf x(\mathbf X,t)$ is the current position of the particle labelled by reference position $\mathbf X$; both have dimension $\mathsf L$. The <deformation map> specifies the particle's trajectory.
* $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\partial_t^2\mathbf x|_{\mathbf X}$ is its <material acceleration>, with dimension $\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}$. The dots follow the particle and are not spatially fixed time derivatives.
* $\rho$ is current <mass density>, with dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-3}$. For a homogeneous material with <incompressibility>, <mass conservation> makes it constant in space and time.
* $\mathbf b$ is <body force> per unit <mass>, such as gravitational <acceleration>, with dimension $\mathsf L\mathsf T^{-2}$. Hence $\rho\mathbf b$ is <force> per unit <volume>.
* $\mathbf T$ is the <Cauchy stress tensor>, mapping a unit <normal vector> $\mathbf n$ to surface <traction> $\mathbf T\mathbf n$. Each component has dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-1}\mathsf T^{-2}$, or <force> per unit <area>.
* $\operatorname{div}$ differentiates in the <current configuration>, with dimension $\mathsf L^{-1}$. In <Cartesian coordinates>, $(\operatorname{div}\mathbf T)_i=\partial T_{ij}/\partial x_j$. Thus $\operatorname{div}\mathbf T$, $\rho\mathbf b$, and $\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}$ all have dimension $\mathsf M\mathsf L^{-2}\mathsf T^{-2}$.
* The superscript $T$ denotes the dimension-preserving <matrix transpose>. The relation $\mathbf T=\mathbf T^T$ expresses <angular momentum conservation> in a continuum without body couples or couple <stresses>.

Here $t$ has dimension $\mathsf T$. The hypothesis $\partial_t\mathbf b=\mathbf0$ means that the prescribed spatial <body force> field is the same for every member of the static family and the resulting motion.

= i
{parent=b}

= Solution
{parent=i}
{c}

Restore the particle label suppressed in the question, writing the static family as $\mathbf x_0(\mathbf X;\tau)$. At each fixed $\tau$, its <Cauchy stress tensor> satisfies
$$
\operatorname{div}\mathbf T_0(\tau)+\rho\mathbf b=\mathbf0.
$$
This <dynamic continuation of a static incompressible elastic deformation> sets $\tau=t$ to form a motion. At every time its <deformation gradient> is the same as that of the corresponding static member. For an elastic material the elastic part of the <Cauchy stress tensor> depends on that <deformation gradient>, while <incompressibility> leaves an arbitrary scalar <constraint reaction in hyperelastic stress>. Therefore write
$$
\mathbf T_0=\mathbf S(\mathbf F)-p_0\mathbf1,\qquad
\mathbf T=\mathbf S(\mathbf F)-p\mathbf1,
\qquad
\mathbf T-\mathbf T_0=(p_0-p)\mathbf1.
$$
Here $\mathbf1$ is the <identity matrix>. Subtracting static <balance of linear momentum> from dynamic <balance of linear momentum> gives
$$
\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0=\nabla(p_0-p).
$$
Since the homogeneous incompressible material has constant <mass density>, define $\xi=(p-p_0)/\rho$. Necessarily
$$
\boxed{\ddot{\mathbf x}_0(\mathbf X,t)
=-\nabla\xi(\mathbf x_0(\mathbf X,t),t).}
$$
Conversely, suppose this <acceleration potential> exists. Choose $p=p_0+\rho\xi$. Then
$$
\operatorname{div}\mathbf T+\rho\mathbf b
=\operatorname{div}\mathbf T_0+\rho\mathbf b-\rho\nabla\xi
=\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0.
$$
The added isotropic <stress> is symmetric, so <angular momentum conservation> remains satisfied. This proves both directions for the field equations and the elastic <constitutive equation>. The surface <tractions> for the motion are those of the adjusted <stress>; identical prescribed static <tractions> would impose an additional boundary restriction on $\xi$.

The <pressure> adjustment from part (i) can be written
$$
\boxed{\mathbf T=\mathbf T_0(t)+q(\mathbf x,t)\mathbf1,\qquad
q=-\rho\xi+C(t).}
$$
Indeed, since $\operatorname{div}(q\mathbf1)=\nabla q$, the difference of the two <Cauchy momentum equations> is
$$
\nabla q=\rho\ddot{\mathbf x}_0=-\rho\nabla\xi.
$$
Thus $q+\rho\xi$ is constant in space on each connected component. The function $C(t)$ is fixed by any prescribed <pressure> or surface <traction> datum. In terms of the usual compressive <pressure> multiplier, $p=p_0-q=p_0+\rho\xi-C(t)$. The plus sign in $q\mathbf1$ therefore does not identify $q$ with the compressive multiplier $p$ itself.

= Solution
{parent=iii}
{c}

For a twice continuously differentiable <scalar potential>, mixed second <partial derivatives> commute. Taking the spatial <curl> of the equation from part (i) gives
$$
\boxed{\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}
=-\nabla\times\nabla\xi=\mathbf0.}
$$
For example, its first component is $-\partial_2\partial_3\xi+\partial_3\partial_2\xi=0$. The <curl> acts on the spatial representation of the <material acceleration>, not directly on its expression in reference coordinates.

This is a necessary local condition. It is sufficient locally, and sufficient globally on a <simply connected domain> under the usual smoothness assumptions. On a domain with holes, a global <scalar potential> additionally requires every closed-loop <line integral> of the <material acceleration> to vanish. The homogeneous motion in part (c) admits an explicit global <scalar potential>, so this topological qualification causes no obstruction there.

= Solution
{parent=i}
{c}

Take two time derivatives at fixed reference position $\mathbf X$:
$$
\ddot{\mathbf x}=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf X+\ddot{\mathbf c}.
$$
The constraint $\det\mathbf F=1$ ensures that the <deformation gradient> is invertible, so $\mathbf X=\mathbf F^{-1}(\mathbf x-\mathbf c)$. Thus the spatial <material acceleration> is the affine <vector field>
$$
\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A(t)(\mathbf x-\mathbf c(t))+\ddot{\mathbf c}(t),
\qquad \mathbf A=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}.
$$
Its spatial <gradient> is $\mathbf A$, and its <curl> is
$$
\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}
=(A_{32}-A_{23},\ A_{13}-A_{31},\ A_{21}-A_{12}).
$$
Part (b)(iii) therefore requires
$$
\boxed{\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}
=\left(\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}\right)^T.}
$$
Equivalently, multiplying on the left by $\mathbf F^T$ and on the right by $\mathbf F$ gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbf F^T\ddot{\mathbf F}=\ddot{\mathbf F}^{\,T}\mathbf F.}
$$
The translation $\mathbf c(t)$ contributes no spatial <curl> and is unrestricted by this condition. A useful equivalent first integral is
$$
\frac d{dt}\left(\mathbf F^T\dot{\mathbf F}-\dot{\mathbf F}^{\,T}\mathbf F\right)=\mathbf0:
$$
the antisymmetric <matrix> in parentheses must be constant, and need not vanish. The separate constraint $\det\mathbf F=1$ is still required; differentiating it also gives $\operatorname{tr}(\dot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1})=0$.

The condition is understood in the setting of part (b), where a static family already exists under the prescribed <body force>. For a homogeneous elastic <deformation gradient>, the elastic <stress> $\mathbf S(\mathbf F)$ is spatially constant. Static <balance of linear momentum> consequently gives $\nabla p_0=\rho\mathbf b$, ensuring that $\mathbf b$ has a <scalar potential>. If the static-family assumption were dropped and an arbitrary time-independent <body force> allowed, the general necessary condition would instead be $\nabla\times\ddot{\mathbf x}=\nabla\times\mathbf b$.

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

For this <homogeneous motion>, suppose the <matrix> $\mathbf A=\ddot{\mathbf F}\mathbf F^{-1}$ is symmetric. Put $\mathbf y=\mathbf x-\mathbf c(t)$. The <acceleration potential of a homogeneous motion> is
$$
\boxed{\xi(\mathbf x,t)
=-\frac12\mathbf y^T\mathbf A(t)\mathbf y
-\ddot{\mathbf c}(t)\cdot\mathbf y+g(t),}
$$
where $g(t)$ is arbitrary. When taking the spatial <gradient>, $t$, $\mathbf c$, $\ddot{\mathbf c}$, and $\mathbf A$ are fixed. Symmetry gives
$$
\nabla\xi=-\frac12(\mathbf A+\mathbf A^T)\mathbf y-\ddot{\mathbf c}
=-\mathbf A\mathbf y-\ddot{\mathbf c}
=-\ddot{\mathbf x}.
$$
This verifies the potential directly and also proves sufficiency of the <symmetric matrix> condition within the static-family setting. Its dimension is $\mathsf L^2\mathsf T^{-2}$, so the <pressure> correction $-\rho\xi$ has the dimension of <stress>. Combining with part (b)(ii), the corresponding dynamic <Cauchy stress tensor> is
$$
\mathbf T=\mathbf T_0(t)+
\left[\frac\rho2\mathbf y^T\mathbf A\mathbf y
+\rho\ddot{\mathbf c}\cdot\mathbf y+\widetilde C(t)\right]\mathbf1,
$$
where $\widetilde C(t)=C(t)-\rho g(t)$. Substituting its <divergence> into the <Cauchy momentum equation> reproduces $\rho[\mathbf A\mathbf y+\ddot{\mathbf c}]$, as required.
