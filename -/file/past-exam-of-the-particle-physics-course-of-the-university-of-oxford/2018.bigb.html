=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Work in the <W boson> <rest frame>, taking the spin-quantization axis along its laboratory motion. Neglect charged-lepton and <neutrino> <masses>. The <weak charged current> produces a <Helicity>[negative-helicity] $\ell^-$ and a <Helicity>[positive-helicity] <antineutrino>. Their <momenta> are opposite, so both <spin> <Vectors> point opposite to the <charged lepton>'s direction $\hat{\mathbf n}$; the final total <Spin>[spin projection] along $\hat{\mathbf n}$ is $-1$.

Let $\lambda_W=+1,0,-1$ denote the parent <Spin>[spin projection] along $z$. In the given <probability amplitude>, use $j=1$ and the <rotation> element $d^1_{-1,\lambda_W}(\theta)$. The supplied spin-one <rotation> elements give
$$
\boxed{|\mathcal M_{+1}|^2=m_W^2g^2\frac{(1-\cos\theta)^2}{4},\qquad
|\mathcal M_0|^2=m_W^2g^2\frac{\sin^2\theta}{2},\qquad
|\mathcal M_{-1}|^2=m_W^2g^2\frac{(1+\cos\theta)^2}{4}.}
$$
The transverse states have <Spin>[spin projections] $\pm1$; the longitudinal state has projection zero, not total <spin> zero. The original diagram below distinguishes <momenta> from <spins> and shows the corresponding normalized angular profiles.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

With relativistically normalized one-particle states, <Fermi's golden rule> becomes
$$
\Gamma=\frac1{2M}\int |\mathcal M|^2(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(P-p_1-p_2)
\frac{d^3p_1}{(2\pi)^3 2E_1}\frac{d^3p_2}{(2\pi)^3 2E_2}.
$$
In the parent <rest frame>, the three-momentum <Dirac delta function>[delta function] fixes $\mathbf p_2=-\mathbf p_1$. The <energy> <Dirac delta function>[delta function] fixes the common magnitude $p^*$; its radial <Jacobian determinant>[Jacobian] converts the remaining <Lorentz-invariant phase space>[phase-space] factor into
$$
\boxed{\Gamma=\frac{p^*}{32\pi^2M^2}\int|\mathcal M|^2\,d\Omega.}
$$
Only one <momentum> magnitude remains because the two final <momenta> are constrained, not independent. Explicitly,
$$
p^*=\frac{\sqrt{[M^2-(m_1+m_2)^2][M^2-(m_1-m_2)^2]}}{2M}.
$$
For leptonic <W boson> decay both final <masses> are small compared with $m_W$, including the <Tau lepton>[tau] <mass> at the precision requested, so $p^*\simeq m_W/2$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For the longitudinal state,
$$
\int |\mathcal M_0|^2d\Omega=m_W^2g^2\,2\pi\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1-z^2}{2}\,dz=\frac{4\pi}{3}m_W^2g^2.
$$
Insert this and $p^*=m_W/2$ into the <Lorentz-invariant phase space>[phase-space] formula:
$$
\boxed{\Gamma_\ell=\frac{g^2m_W}{48\pi}.}
$$
Rotational invariance makes the integrated <Decay rate>[decay probability] independent of the orientation of the initial <spin>. Both transverse <Particle polarization>[polarizations] therefore have the same rate. An unpolarized sample averages these three equal rates; it does not add three copies. Hence its <decay width>[width] into one specified charged-lepton <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] is also $g^2m_W/(48\pi)$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

At a hadron collider, select $W\to\ell\nu$ events and reconstruct the <transverse mass>
$$
m_T=\sqrt{2p_T^\ell p_T^\nu(1-\cos\Delta\phi)},
$$
using <missing transverse momentum> for the <neutrino>. Fit the shape, especially the region above the nominal <Jacobian determinant>[Jacobian] edge, with finite-width resonance templates convolved with detector resolution, recoil modelling and backgrounds. The <Breit-Wigner resonance cross section>[Breit-Wigner] <decay width>[width] controls this off-shell tail. Calibrated <momentum> and missing-momentum resolutions must be separated from the physical <decay width>[width]. This is a lineshape measurement; the <mean lifetime>[lifetime] is far too short for a direct flight-distance measurement.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

Assume only <Standard Model> decay channels, negligible final-state <masses>, three <lepton> families, three <quark> <Color charge>[colours] and a <Unitary operator>[unitary] <CKM matrix>. The <Top quark>[top-quark] channels are kinematically closed. Summing the allowed <quark> channels gives $3\sum_{i=u,c}\sum_{j=d,s,b}|V_{ij}|^2\simeq6$ times one leptonic <decay width>[width], while the <leptons> contribute three. Neglecting <QCD> and <Electroweak interaction>[electroweak] <radiative corrections>,
$$
\Gamma_W\simeq9\Gamma_\ell=\frac{9g^2m_W}{48\pi}.
$$
The source's rounded CKM entries give almost the same factor; their tiny nonunitarity is not physically significant. Consequently
$$
\boxed{g^2=\frac{48\pi\Gamma_W}{9m_W}=0.4356\pm0.0083,\qquad g=0.6600\pm0.0063.}
$$
The propagated <Measurement uncertainty>[uncertainty] uses $\delta g/g=\tfrac12\delta\Gamma_W/\Gamma_W$, taking $m_W$ as fixed. It is the measurement <Measurement uncertainty>[uncertainty] within the stated tree-level approximation; omitted <radiative corrections> are an additional modelling limitation. In particular <QCD> increases the hadronic <decay width>[width] and lowers the inferred <Gauge coupling>[coupling] when included.

The printed electromagnetic comparison $4\pi/137$ is $e^2$, not the <probability amplitude> <Gauge coupling>[coupling] $e$. Compare like quantities:
$$
\boxed{\frac{g^2}{e^2}\simeq4.75,\qquad e=\sqrt{4\pi/137}\simeq0.303,\qquad g/e\simeq2.18.}
$$
Equivalently $\alpha_W=g^2/(4\pi)\simeq0.0347$ exceeds $\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}\simeq1/137$. The <weak interaction> is weak at low <energy> primarily because of the massive <propagator>, rather than an exceptionally small fundamental <Gauge coupling>[coupling].

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

The <valence quark> distributions describe net <quark> content: $\int u_v(x)dx=2$ and $\int d_v(x)dx=1$ for a <proton>. Their <momentum> densities $xu_v,xd_v$ are small at very small $x$, peak at moderate <Parton momentum fractions>[momentum fractions], and vanish toward $x=1$, where one <parton> carrying almost all <proton> <momentum> is unlikely. The larger $u$ distribution reflects two valence <up quarks> and its comparatively harder shape; its pointwise value need not be exactly twice the down distribution.

The <sea quark> distribution is generated largely by <gluon> radiation and $g\to q\bar q$ splitting. It grows strongly toward small $x$ and falls at large $x$. The plotted sea curve is scaled by $0.05$, so it must be multiplied by twenty before numerical use. The total up-quark <Parton distribution function>[PDF] is
$$
\boxed{u(x)=u_v(x)+\bar u(x),}
$$
using the valence-up distribution and the up-sea distribution. The summed sea curve alone does not fix $\bar u$ without a <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] assumption. Under the later stated four-flavour symmetry, $\bar u=\Sigma/8$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

At leading order, neglect the <transverse momentum> of the incoming <partons> and of the produced <On shell>[on-shell] boson. In the hadron <Center-of-momentum frame>[centre-of-mass frame],
$$
p_+=\frac{\sqrt s}{2}(x_+,0,0,x_+),\qquad
p_-=\frac{\sqrt s}{2}(x_-,0,0,-x_-).
$$
Then $m_W^2=(p_++p_-)^2=x_+x_-s$, and
$$
y=\frac12\ln\frac{x_+}{x_-}.
$$
Solving these product and ratio equations gives
$$
\boxed{x_+=\sqrt\tau e^y,\qquad x_-=\sqrt\tau e^{-y},\qquad \sqrt\tau=\frac{80.4}{2000}=0.0402.}
$$
Over $-2<y<2$, each <parton momentum fraction>[momentum fraction] ranges between
$$
\boxed{0.00544<x_\pm<0.2970.}
$$
They are anticorrelated: increasing $y$ increases $x_+$ while decreasing $x_-$. Higher-order recoil changes the simple two-to-one <kinematics>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The leading subprocess is $u\bar d\to W^+$. There are two beam assignments, and <charge conjugation> relates <antiproton> <Parton distribution functions>[PDFs] to <proton> <Parton distribution functions>[PDFs]. Therefore
$$
\frac{d\sigma}{dy}\propto u_p(x_+)\bar d_{\bar p}(x_-)+\bar d_p(x_+)u_{\bar p}(x_-)
=u(x_+)d(x_-)+\bar d(x_+)\bar u(x_-).
$$
In a narrow-width leading-order <normalization> the prefactor is $\pi g^2/(12s)$ when $|V_{ud}|=1$; only the <Parton distribution function>[PDF] dependence is needed for the fractions below.

Write the common sea <Parton distribution function>[PDF] as $S=\bar u=\bar d=\bar s=\bar c=\Sigma/8$. The <Valence and sea decomposition of proton-antiproton W production> gives the three <parton> luminosities
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{VV}=u_v(x_+)d_v(x_-),\quad
\mathcal L_{VS}=u_v(x_+)S(x_-)+S(x_+)d_v(x_-),\quad
\mathcal L_{SS}=2S(x_+)S(x_-).}
$$
The factor two in the sea-sea term includes both beam assignments. The <antiproton>'s <Valence quark>[valence antiquarks] make valence-valence <Annihilation> possible.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

At $y=0$, both fractions equal $x=0.0402$. Reading the original plot near this value gives representative <momentum> densities
$$
U=xu_v\simeq0.42,\qquad D=xd_v\simeq0.24,\qquad 0.05x\Sigma\simeq0.08.
$$
These are approximate graphical readings, not a precision <Parton distribution function>[PDF] fit. Undo the sea-curve scale and divide by eight <Flavor eigenstates>[flavours]/antiflavours:
$$
S_x=xS=\frac{x\Sigma}{8}\simeq\frac{0.08}{0.05\times8}=0.20.
$$
All products have the same $1/x^2$ factor, which cancels in the fractions. The three relative weights are
$$
w_{VV}=UD=0.1008,\qquad w_{VS}=S_x(U+D)=0.1320,\qquad w_{SS}=2S_x^2=0.0800.
$$
Their sum is $0.3128$, giving
$$
\boxed{f_{VV}\simeq32\%,\qquad f_{VS}\simeq42\%,\qquad f_{SS}\simeq26\%.}
$$
Reading the central curves more finely changes these percentages by about a percentage point, well within what the plot supports. Omitting its factor $0.05$ would substantially underestimate the sea contribution.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

Use leptonic decays: an isolated high <transverse momentum> positive <electron> or <muon>, together with substantial <missing transverse momentum> from the <neutrino>, provides a clean $W^+$ signature. The <lepton> <transverse momentum> has a <W-decay Jacobian peak>[Jacobian] scale near $m_W/2$, and the <lepton>–missing-momentum <transverse mass> has an edge near $m_W$, broadened by <decay width>[width], recoil and resolution. <Lepton> identification, isolation and a prompt primary-vertex origin suppress <Jets> faking <leptons> and heavy-flavour decays. Missing-momentum quality requirements suppress mismeasured multijet events; a veto on a second oppositely <charged lepton> suppresses $Z\to\ell^+\ell^-$ when both are reconstructed. Hadronic $W$ decays have a much larger ordinary-QCD background.

==== Solution

The <Flavor eigenstates>[flavour states] $K^0=d\bar s$ and $\bar K^0=s\bar d$ have definite <Strangeness> and are distinguished by flavour-sensitive production or decay. The <mass eigenstates>[propagation states] diagonalize the <Effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing>[effective Hamiltonian] $H_{\mathrm{eff}}=M-i\Gamma/2$; their complex <eigenvalues> give a pole <mass> and a <decay width>. The common <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] combinations specified in the source assume the usual <CPT symmetry>[CPT-symmetric] two-state description and are normalized with $|p|^2+|q|^2=1$.

Choose the <phase> convention $CP|K^0\rangle=|\bar K^0\rangle$. If mixing conserves <CP>, $p=q$ can be chosen, and the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations are <CP> even and odd. More generally the equality must be understood with the corresponding flavour-state <phase> convention; $|p|=|q|$ alone is not a statement fixing an arbitrary written <phase>.

The almost <CP eigenstate>[CP-even] state decays rapidly to <Pion>[two pions], whereas the almost <CP eigenstate>[CP-odd] state is largely restricted to slower channels such as <Pion>[three pions]. Their very different <mean lifetimes>[lifetimes] are easier to distinguish than their tiny <mass> splitting, motivating the names $K_S^0$ and $K_L^0$. In the source's first notation the subscript $L$ means *light*, not *long-lived*: the light state is identified with the short-lived <kaon>, and the heavy state with the long-lived <kaon>. Below, $\ell,h$ label light/heavy to avoid that collision.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The two indistinguishable paths to the same final state must be added as <probability amplitudes>:
$$
\mathcal A_f(t)=A_f g_+(t)+\frac qp\bar A_f g_-(t)=A_f[g_+(t)+\lambda g_-(t)].
$$
Taking the <Complex modulus>[squared modulus] gives the time-dependent <decay rate>, with the common final-state <Lorentz-invariant phase space>[phase-space] <normalization> absorbed into the <probability amplitudes>:
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(K(t)\to f)=|A_f|^2\left[|g_+|^2+|\lambda|^2|g_-|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda g_-g_+^*)\right].}
$$
The cross term is the <interference> between direct decay and decay after <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] conversion. For reference, diagonal propagation gives $g_\pm=(e_\ell\pm e_h)/2$, where $e_j=e^{-im_jt-\Gamma_jt/2}$ and $t$ is <proper time>. The unfactored expression remains usable if $A_f=0$, when the ratio $\lambda$ itself is undefined.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Starting with an <Neutral antikaon>[antikaon] instead gives
$$
\bar{\mathcal A}_f(t)=\bar A_f g_++\frac pq A_f g_-=\bar A_f[g_++\lambda^{-1}g_-].
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to f)=|\bar A_f|^2\left[|g_+|^2+|\lambda^{-1}|^2|g_-|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\lambda^{-1}g_-g_+^*)\right].}
$$
This factored form assumes $A_f,\bar A_f\ne0$; otherwise use the preceding <probability amplitude>. The three mechanisms are separated below. Comparisons for distinct final states must pair $f$ with its <CP> conjugate $\bar f$.

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Direct <CP violation> means unequal magnitudes of CP-conjugate decay <probability amplitudes>:
$$
\boxed{|A_f|\ne|\bar A_{\bar f}|.}
$$
It changes <Decay rate>[decay probabilities] even at $t=0$, before any mixing. For a common <CP-eigenstate> final state, this reduces to $|A_f|\ne|\bar A_f|$. Usually it requires interfering decay contributions with different <Weak phase>[weak phases] and different <Absorptive phase of a decay amplitude>[strong phases]; it is not equivalent to a nonzero weak <phase> in one isolated <probability amplitude>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

<CP violation in neutral-meson mixing>[CP violation in mixing] occurs when
$$
\boxed{|q/p|\ne1.}
$$
The flavour-transition <probabilities> are proportional to $|q/p|^2|g_-|^2$ for $K^0\to\bar K^0$, and $|p/q|^2|g_-|^2$ for the reverse. Their inequality remains when decay <probability amplitudes> themselves conserve <CP>. The coefficients multiplying the mixed-decay terms in the rate equations encode this asymmetry.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

<Interference> <CP violation> concerns the relative <phase> between direct decay and decay after mixing. For a common <CP-eigenstate> final state, it can occur with both $|q/p|=1$ and $|\bar A_f/A_f|=1$, so neither of the first two mechanisms is needed. In that limit $|\lambda|=1$, but
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Im}\lambda\ne0}
$$
produces different <interference> terms for $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}=\lambda^*$. With $\Delta m=m_h-m_\ell$,
$$
g_-g_+^*=\frac14\left[e^{-\Gamma_\ell t}-e^{-\Gamma_h t}+2i e^{-(\Gamma_\ell+\Gamma_h)t/2}\sin(\Delta m t)\right].
$$
The term proportional to $\operatorname{Im}\lambda\sin(\Delta m t)$ consequently reverses sign. In the CP-conserving limit, $\lambda$ is the real <CP> <parity> of the final state in a consistent <phase> convention. For non-CP-eigenstate final states, compare the full coefficients in CP-conjugate rates rather than imposing this simplified criterion.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Introduce all four physical <probability amplitudes>: $A_f,\bar A_f,A_{\bar f},\bar A_{\bar f}$. Under exchange of the <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] and final-state labels, define
$$
\boxed{\bar\lambda=\frac pq\frac{A_{\bar f}}{\bar A_{\bar f}}.}
$$
This <CP-conjugate decay-amplitude ratio> gives the conjugate-channel rates
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to\bar f)&=|\bar A_{\bar f}|^2\left[|g_+|^2+|\bar\lambda|^2|g_-|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\bar\lambda g_-g_+^*)\right],\\
\Gamma(K(t)\to\bar f)&=|A_{\bar f}|^2\left[|g_+|^2+|\bar\lambda^{-1}|^2|g_-|^2+2\operatorname{Re}(\bar\lambda^{-1}g_-g_+^*)\right].
\end{aligned}}
$$
<CP> is a <Unitary operator>[unitary] discrete transformation, so exchanging state labels does not mean complex-conjugating every strong <phase> by fiat. Nor should one assume that CP-conjugate <probability amplitudes> are equal while studying <CP violation>.

The equality $\bar\lambda=\lambda^{-1}$ holds when
$$
\boxed{\frac{A_{\bar f}}{\bar A_{\bar f}}=\frac{A_f}{\bar A_f},}
$$
in particular when $\bar f$ and $f$ are the same <CP-eigenstate> ray, with a consistent state <phase>. It is not automatic for two distinct CP-conjugate final states. For example, $p=q$, $A_f=\bar A_{\bar f}=2$ and $\bar A_f=A_{\bar f}=1$ conserve the paired decay <probability amplitudes> but give $\lambda=\bar\lambda=1/2$, not inverse values.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

Assume the usual <Semileptonic decay>[semileptonic] <Flavor eigenstate>[flavour] selection rule $\Delta S=\Delta Q$, negligible wrong-sign decay <probability amplitudes>, and equal magnitudes for CP-conjugate allowed <probability amplitudes>. Then $f_+=\pi^-\ell^+\nu$ is reached directly only from $K^0$, and $f_-=\pi^+\ell^-\bar\nu$ only from $\bar K^0$. Put $|A_{f_+}|=|\bar A_{f_-}|=|A|$. The four rates are
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(K(t)\to f_+)&=|A|^2|g_+|^2,&\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to f_-)&=|A|^2|g_+|^2,\\
\Gamma(K(t)\to f_-)&=|A|^2|q/p|^2|g_-|^2,&\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to f_+)&=|A|^2|p/q|^2|g_-|^2.
\end{aligned}}
$$
There is only one path to each tagged <Semileptonic decay>[semileptonic] channel, so no direct/mixed decay <interference> survives. The <semileptonic wrong-sign mixing asymmetry> is
$$
\boxed{a_{\mathrm{SL}}=\frac{\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to f_+)-\Gamma(K(t)\to f_-)}{\Gamma(\bar K(t)\to f_+)+\Gamma(K(t)\to f_-)}
=\frac{1-|q/p|^4}{1+|q/p|^4}.}
$$
It isolates <CP violation in neutral-meson mixing>[CP violation in mixing] under the stated assumptions. Allowing direct <Semileptonic decay>[semileptonic] <CP violation> or wrong-sign <probability amplitudes> would spoil that isolation.<Review of Particle Physics by Particle Data Group (2022)>[<CP Violation> in the <Quark> Sector, §13.1.3, equations (13.22)–(13.24): mixing and <Semileptonic decay>[semileptonic] wrong-sign asymmetry]{ref}

==== Solution

Use dimensionless physical <helicity> $h=\mathbf S\cdot\hat{\mathbf p}$, with <spin> <eigenvalues> $\pm1/2$ in <natural units>. In the <Dirac representation of the gamma matrices>[Dirac representation],
$$
\boxed{h_D=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p}&0\\0&\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf p}\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The entries $1$ in the supplied transformation denote two-by-two <Identity matrix>[identities]. Since its <matrix> acts only on the upper/lower <spinor> blocks, it <Commuting operators>[commutes] with the repeated <Pauli matrices>[Pauli block]. Therefore $u^{-1}=u$ and
$$
\boxed{h_W=u h_Du^{-1}=h_D.}
$$
The <eigenvalues> remain the physical <Spin>[spin projection] along the direction of motion, $+1/2$ or $-1/2$. A convention omitting the factor one half instead labels them $\pm1$; this changes no measurement <probabilities>. A change of <Gamma matrices>[gamma-matrix representation] cannot change the <spin> observable.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The two <Weyl spinors> transform in the inequivalent left- and <Chirality>[right-chiral] representations of the proper <Lorentz group>. They are selected by $P_{L,R}=(1\mp\gamma^5)/2$ and have different <Lorentz boost>[boost] transformation laws; <parity> exchanges the two. A massive <Dirac equation> couples them, whereas in the massless limit they can propagate independently.

For the particular $u$ printed in this paper, transforming its supplied Dirac $\gamma^5$ gives
$$
\boxed{\gamma_W^5=u\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix}u^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&-I\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Thus the upper block is <Chirality>[right chiral] and the lower block <Chirality>[left chiral] in this convention. Many references use the opposite block order; neither convention changes physics, but they must not be mixed. <Chirality> is not generally the same as <helicity> for a massive <electron>. The weak <charged current> selects the <Chirality>[left-chiral] <electron> component, reducing to negative <helicity> at high <energy>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Put $r=p/(E+m)$ and assume $p>0$. The common <plane wave> has unit <Complex modulus>[modulus]. The component <norm> is
$$
\psi^\dagger\psi=|N|^2[(1+r)^2+4(1-r)^2+(1-r)^2+4(1+r)^2]=10|N|^2(1+r^2).
$$
For unit <spinor> <probability density> choose
$$
\boxed{N=\frac1{\sqrt{10(1+r^2)}}=\sqrt{\frac{E+m}{20E}},}
$$
where $E^2=p^2+m^2$ was used. This is <spinor> <normalization>, not square <normalization> of a <plane wave> over infinite space; a box or <Wave packet>[wavepacket] gives the corresponding spatial <normalization>. If the convention is instead $\psi^\dagger\psi=2E$, use $N=\sqrt{(E+m)/10}$.

For <momentum> along $+z$, the <Helicity>[positive-helicity] components are the first and third, while the second and fourth have negative <helicity>. Hence
$$
\boxed{P(h=+1/2)=2|N|^2(1+r^2)=\frac15,\qquad P(h=-1/2)=8|N|^2(1+r^2)=\frac45.}
$$
Both are independent of <speed>. In the source's chiral block order the exact <Chirality>[left-chiral] weight is $1/2+3p/(10E)$, tending to $4/5$ at high <energy>; <helicity> and <chirality> <probabilities> agree only in that limit.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Charged current>[charged-current] process is
$$
\boxed{e^-+\mu^+\longrightarrow\nu_e+\bar\nu_\mu,}
$$
with a $t$-channel $W^-$ emitted at the electron-to-neutrino vertex and absorbed at the antimuon-to-antineutrino vertex. It is not an $s$-channel <Annihilation> of the unlike-flavour <charged leptons> into one $W$.

At high <momentum transfer> the incident <electron>'s <Chirality>[left-chiral] fraction tends to $4/5$. In the collision <Center-of-momentum frame>[centre-of-mass frame], the incoming <antimuon> moves along $-z$ and its <Charged current>[charged-current] allowed right <helicity> has <spin> along $-z$. A rest-frame <spin> at <Angle> $\theta$ to $+z$ projects onto that <spin> direction with <probability> $\sin^2(\theta/2)$. Therefore, if $\sigma_0$ denotes the <cross section> for a pure <Helicity>[left-helicity] <electron> and fully selected <antimuon> <spin>,
$$
\boxed{\sigma(\theta)\simeq\frac45\,\sigma_0\sin^2\frac\theta2=\frac25\sigma_0(1-\cos\theta).}
$$
It vanishes at $\theta=0$ in the ultrarelativistic selection limit and is maximal at $\theta=\pi$. Finite-mass corrections can fill the suppressed orientation. The absolute <normalization> depends on beam <energy> and the exchanged-boson <propagator>; the spin-angle dependence is what is fixed here.

===== Solution

For an incoming $\mu^-$, a putative $e^-\mu^-\to\nu_e\nu_\mu$ process would change total <electric charge> from $-2$ to zero. It is forbidden: both negative <charged leptons> would have to emit a $W^-$, and a single exchanged charged boson cannot connect those two emission vertices. Ordinary <Elastic scattering>[elastic] $e^-\mu^-\to e^-\mu^-$ scattering remains possible through $t$-channel <photon> or $Z$ exchange, shown in the figure, but this is not the <Charged current>[charged-current] reaction of part d. The <Neutral current>[neutral-current] weak contribution is distinct from the electromagnetic one.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

A <proton> contains <up quarks> on which the <charged current> can act:
$$
\boxed{e^-u\longrightarrow\nu_e d}
$$
through $W^-$ exchange, weighted by $V_{ud}$. Spectator <quarks> complete the hadronic final state. At the exclusive nucleon level the charge assignment is $e^-p\to\nu_e n$; at the high <momentum> transfers specified, the generally dominant description is inclusive $e^-p\to\nu_e X$ with <proton> structure and hadronic breakup, not an unsuppressed pointlike proton-to-neutron transition. The <Quark>[quark-level] diagram in the figure displays this allowed charge flow. The <electron>'s <Chirality>[left-chiral] selection is the same as in part d.

==== Solution

A fast charged particle loses <energy> by <Ionization>[ionizing] and exciting <silicon>, creating <Electron-hole pair>[electron–hole pairs] along its path. In a reverse-biased <Silicon microstrip detector>[microstrip diode], the <electric field> separates and drifts the <electrons> and <holes> toward opposite <Electrode>[electrodes]. Their motion induces a <Electric current>[current] on the strips, which is integrated by the readout electronics to locate the hit.

The bias depletes the bulk of mobile equilibrium carriers, reducing conduction and <capacitance>, and supplies rapid directed charge collection before <recombination> or trapping. Without a collecting field, <diffusion> is slow and charge spreads or recombines, degrading signal and position measurement. The signal is generated by the traversing particle's <energy> deposition, not by the bias creating the <ionization> charge.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Treat the $p^+$ strips as much more strongly <Semiconductor doping>[doped] than the $n$ bulk, so the <Depletion region>[depletion layer] lies predominantly in the latter. Fully ionized <Donor impurities>[donors] leave uniform <space charge> $\rho=eN_D$. In one dimension,
$$
\frac{dE}{dx}=\frac{eN_D}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}},\qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}=\epsilon_r\epsilon_0.
$$
At the depletion boundary $x=d$, the minimum-bias solution has $E(d)=0$. Thus $E(x)=eN_D(x-d)/\epsilon_{\mathrm{Si}}$, and the magnitude of the potential drop is
$$
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{dep}}=\left|\int_0^dE(x)dx\right|=\frac{eN_Dd^2}{2\epsilon_r\epsilon_0}.}
$$
For $N_D=10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, $d=300\,\mu\mathrm m$, and $\epsilon_r=11.6$, this is
$$
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{dep}}\simeq70.2\,\mathrm V.}
$$
The applied <reverse bias> is approximately this value; more precisely the built-in <Built-in potential>[junction potential] contributes to the total <One-sided depletion voltage>[depletion voltage] and is subtracted from the required external bias. It is small on the scale of seventy volts and is not specified. Here the stated free-carrier density is used as the effective <Donor impurity>[donor density] of the neutral bulk before depletion.

==== Solution

For <binary strip position resolution>[binary strip readout], report the centre of the hit strip. With uniform impact position across pitch $w$, the position error is uniformly distributed on $[-w/2,w/2]$, so
$$
\sigma_x^2=\frac1w\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}x^2dx=\frac{w^2}{12},\qquad
\boxed{\sigma_x=\frac{100\,\mu\mathrm m}{\sqrt{12}}=28.87\,\mu\mathrm m.}
$$
This assumes no analogue charge sharing between strips.

The <Lorentz force>[magnetic force] is $d\mathbf p/dt=q\mathbf v\times\mathbf B$. Since relativistic <momentum> is parallel to <velocity>, $d(p^2)/dt=2\mathbf p\cdot q(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)=0$. A uniform transverse field therefore changes <momentum> direction but not magnitude. If the trajectory turns at angular <speed> $v/R$, then $|d\mathbf p/dt|=p_Tv/R$. Equating force magnitudes yields
$$
\boxed{R=\frac{p_T}{|q|B}.}
$$
This is the positive <radius>. The sign of $q$ determines the direction of <curvature>; the source's signed formula can describe an oriented <radius>. In practical tracking units,
$$
p_T[\mathrm{GeV}/c]=0.299792458\,|q/e|B[\mathrm T]R[\mathrm m].
$$

==== Solution

The source labels $L$ as the full chord from $P_1$ to $P_3$, with $L/2$ on each side of its midpoint. The <Right triangle> between the <Circle> centre, chord midpoint and endpoint gives
$$
(R-s)^2+(L/2)^2=R^2,\qquad
\boxed{R=\frac{L^2}{8s}+\frac s2\simeq\frac{L^2}{8s}.}
$$
For fixed geometry and field, the inverse <transverse momentum> is
$$
\frac1{p_T}\simeq\frac{8s}{0.299792458\,|q/e|BL^2}.
$$
If the <Measurement uncertainty>[uncertainty] is dominated by the central point, $\sigma_s\simeq\sigma_x$. Linear <propagation of uncertainty> gives
$$
\boxed{\sigma(1/p_T)\simeq\frac{8\sigma_x}{0.299792458\,|q/e|BL^2}.}
$$
Using $|q/e|=1$, $B=2\,\mathrm T$, $L=0.6\,\mathrm m$ and $\sigma_x=28.87\,\mu\mathrm m$,
$$
\boxed{\sigma(1/p_T)=1.070\times10^{-3}\,(\mathrm{GeV}/c)^{-1}.}
$$
The inverse-momentum resolution is approximately <momentum> independent in this spatial-error-only model; the relative <momentum> resolution grows as $p_T\sigma(1/p_T)$. Endpoint errors and <multiple scattering> are excluded by the stated assumptions.

For negligible <fermion> <masses>, the <Neutral current>[neutral-current] vertex connects a <fermion> and <antifermion> of opposite <helicity>. The two incoming choices are $e^-_Le^+_R$ and $e^-_Re^+_L$; the two outgoing choices are $f_L\bar f_R$ and $f_R\bar f_L$. Their combinations give four channels, denoted $LL,LR,RL,RR$, where each letter refers to the <fermion> rather than <antifermion>.

Take the incoming <electron> <momentum> along $+z$ and <positron> <momentum> along $-z$. The initial $L$ pair has both <spins> along $-z$, giving total projection $-1$; the $R$ pair has both along $+z$, giving projection $+1$. If the outgoing <fermion> moves along $\hat{\mathbf n}$, an outgoing $L$ pair has both <spins> along $-\hat{\mathbf n}$ and an $R$ pair both along $+\hat{\mathbf n}$. ==== Solution

Let $z=\cos\theta^\star$. Rotating the spin-one intermediate state between initial and final axes gives <probability amplitudes> proportional to $d^1_{\pm1,\pm1}$ for equal <fermion> <helicities> and $d^1_{\mp1,\pm1}$ for opposite ones. Thus, apart from a common positive <normalization>,

|| Channel | Angular weight \\
| $LL$ | $|g_L^eg_L^f|^2(1+z)^2$ \\
| $RR$ | $|g_R^eg_R^f|^2(1+z)^2$ \\
| $LR$ | $|g_L^eg_R^f|^2(1-z)^2$ \\
| $RL$ | $|g_R^eg_L^f|^2(1-z)^2$

The common factor $1/4$ from the <rotation> elements is absorbed into the <normalization>. Distinct external <helicities> are <Orthogonality>[orthogonal], so an unpolarized rate adds their squared <probability amplitudes> incoherently.

**The source interchanges the conventional left and right <electron> <Gauge coupling>[coupling] labels.** With $g_L=T_3-Q\sin^2\theta_W$ and $g_R=-Q\sin^2\theta_W$, the physical <electron> values are
$$
g_L=-\frac12+\sin^2\theta_W,\qquad g_R=\sin^2\theta_W.
$$
At $\sin^2\theta_W=0$, for example, the left <electron> still carries <weak isospin> $-1/2$, while the right <electron> is a <Singlet representation>[weak singlet]; the printed assignment gives the opposite result. This fixes how the helicity-channel table should be interpreted.<Electroweak Model and Constraints on New Physics by J. Erler and A. Freitas (2024)>[§10.1: <fermion> <Neutral current>[neutral-current] interaction and weak-isospin assignments]{ref}

For a <muon> final state, <lepton universality> gives the same pair of <Gauge coupling>[couplings]. Crucially, swapping both labels does not change the summed distribution. Put $L=g_L$, $R=g_R$; then
$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma}{dz}=\mathcal N\left[(L^4+R^4)(1+z)^2+2L^2R^2(1-z)^2\right]
=\mathcal N\left[(L^2+R^2)^2(1+z^2)+2(L^2-R^2)^2z\right].}
$$
Here $\mathcal N$ includes the resonance <propagator>, initial <spin> average and common <Gauge coupling>[coupling] <normalization>. Equivalently,
$$
\boxed{\frac1\sigma\frac{d\sigma}{dz}=\frac38(1+z^2)+\frac34\mathcal A_\ell^2z,\qquad
\mathcal A_\ell=\frac{L^2-R^2}{L^2+R^2}.}
$$
The source's simultaneous label interchange reverses $\mathcal A_\ell$ but leaves its square unchanged.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For the <Pole forward-backward asymmetry for charged leptons>, <Integration>[integrating] the normalized distribution over the two hemispheres cancels the even term in the numerator. Since $\int_{-1}^{1}(1+z^2)dz=8/3$ and $\int_0^1z\,dz-\int_{-1}^0z\,dz=1$,
$$
\boxed{A_{FB}=\frac34\left(\frac{L^2-R^2}{L^2+R^2}\right)^2
=\frac34\left[\frac{1-4\sin^2\theta_W}{1-4\sin^2\theta_W+8\sin^4\theta_W}\right]^2.}
$$
Within this single-$Z$, tree-level model, a nonzero asymmetry requires unequal left/right <Gauge coupling>[coupling] magnitudes, demonstrating <parity violation> in the vertices. For $g_L=g_R$ the odd angular term vanishes and $A_{FB}=0$; equal magnitudes with opposite signs also give zero. This inference is process-specific: a forward-backward asymmetry is not universally a parity-odd observable, since reversing both beam and outgoing <momenta> leaves their relative <Angle> unchanged. Purely electromagnetic <Bhabha scattering> is a concrete parity-conserving process with a forward-peaked, asymmetric distribution.

For the supplied $\sin^2\theta_W=0.23153$,
$$
\boxed{A_{FB}^{\mathrm{pred}}=0.01620.}
$$
The measurement differs by $0.0188-0.01620=0.00260$, or $0.79$ times its quoted <Measurement uncertainty>[uncertainty] of $0.0033$, so it is consistent with the prediction. Statistical fluctuations alone preclude exact equality; <photon> exchange, <initial-state radiation> and <Electroweak interaction>[electroweak] <radiative corrections> also modify the idealized pure-resonance prediction and provide physical reasons for small differences.
