==== i

===== Solution

A leading valence-level process is $u\bar d\to W^+\to e^+\nu_e$, with the <up quark> supplied by the <proton> and the <down antiquark> by the <antiproton>. The same <Feynman diagram> works with $e^+\nu_e$ replaced by $\mu^+\nu_\mu$. Both vertices are <weak charged current> vertices; the production <scattering amplitude> contains the <CKM matrix> element $V_{ud}$ or its conjugate according to the chosen fermion-flow convention. The intermediate <W boson> is produced near its <mass shell>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Neglect the participating <quark> and <lepton> <masses>, so that the <weak charged current> selects a left-<helicity> $u$ and a right-<helicity> $\bar d$. Choose $+z$ along the <proton> beam in the <parton model>[partonic] <centre-of-momentum frame>. Then the <up quark> moves along $+z$ with <spin angular momentum>[spin projection] $-1/2$, while the <down antiquark> moves along $-z$ with <helicity> $+1/2$ and also has <spin angular momentum>[spin projection] $-1/2$ along $+z$. Thus
$$
\boxed{J_z(W^+)=-1.}
$$
The collinear two-body initial state has no orbital projection about its collision axis. In this approximation the produced <W boson> therefore has the indicated transverse <polarization>; its longitudinal spin state is absent. The unpolarized <hadron> beams contain other constituent <spin angular momentum>[spin states], but these do not participate in this <massless particle>[massless] <weak charged current>[charged-current] subprocess.

In the final state the <positron> has <helicity> $+1/2$ and the <electron neutrino> has <helicity> $-1/2$. Along the outgoing <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] direction their combined angular-momentum projection is $+1$, because the oppositely moving <neutrino> has <spin angular momentum>[spin projection] $+1/2$ on that axis. Finite <masses>, noncollinear production and sea contributions modify the ideal configuration.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

Let $\theta^\star$ be the angle from the incoming <up quark>, along the <proton> direction, to the outgoing charged <antilepton> in the <W boson> rest frame. The initial and final <spin angular momentum>[spin projections] in <1/a/ii> are $-1$ and $+1$. The <Wigner small-d matrix> therefore gives
$$
\mathcal M(\theta^\star)\propto d^1_{-1,+1}(\theta^\star)
=\frac{1-\cos\theta^\star}{2}.
$$
After squaring and normalizing over $c=\cos\theta^\star$, the <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] <angular distribution> is
$$
\boxed{\frac1\Gamma\frac{d\Gamma}{dc}=\frac38(1-c)^2,\qquad -1<c<1.}
$$
Its integral is one since $\int_{-1}^{1}(1-c)^2dc=8/3$. The charged <antilepton> is preferentially emitted toward the <antiproton>. Measuring the angle from the incoming <down antiquark> instead replaces $c$ by $-c$ and produces $(1+c)^2$; the axis convention is part of the answer. Per unit solid angle the normalized density is $3(1-\cos\theta^\star)^2/(16\pi)$.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Take an on-shell <W boson> of fixed <mass> $M_W$, zero transverse recoil, negligible <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] <mass>, and unrestricted angular acceptance. In its rest frame the charged <lepton> has <momentum> magnitude $p_0=M_W/2$. A longitudinal <Lorentz boost> to the laboratory preserves <transverse momentum>, so
$$
p_T=p_0\sin\theta^\star,\qquad
c=\pm\sqrt{1-(p_T/p_0)^2}.
$$
Both angular roots contribute to the same $p_T$. Set $x=p_T/p_0$ and $a=\sqrt{1-x^2}$. The change-of-variable <Jacobian determinant> has $|dc/dp_T|=x/(p_0a)$, and the two-root angular weight is $\tfrac38[(1-a)^2+(1+a)^2]=\tfrac34(2-x^2)$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\frac1\Gamma\frac{d\Gamma}{dp_T}
=\frac{3}{4p_0}\frac{x(2-x^2)}{\sqrt{1-x^2}},\qquad
0<p_T<p_0,\qquad p_T^{\max}=\frac{M_W}{2}.}
$$
This is the ideal <W-decay Jacobian peak>. The divergence at the endpoint is integrable; it is a change-of-variable effect, not an infinite decay probability. Reversing the polar axis does not change this <transverse momentum> density, because the sum of the two roots removes the term odd in $c$.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

<Initial-state radiation> of <gluons> gives the <W boson> nonzero <transverse momentum>. Its transverse <Lorentz boost> then changes the <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] $p_T$, washing out the sharp $M_W/2$ edge and permitting events above it. The recoil must be modelled with <quantum chromodynamics> and constrained by measured <hadron>[hadronic] activity. Independently, the finite <W boson> <decay width> spreads its actual <invariant mass>, also smearing the fixed-mass endpoint. Either is a theoretical broadening mechanism; neither is a <particle detector>[detector-resolution] effect.

=== c
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

The useful modes are $\boxed{W^+\to e^+\nu_e\ \text{and}\ W^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu}$ and their charge conjugates. An <electron> gives an isolated track and localized <electromagnetic shower>; a <muon> gives an isolated penetrating track. Both allow a well-calibrated <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] <transverse momentum> and a <missing transverse momentum> measurement for the <neutrino>.

The <tau lepton> decays before detection, usually adding further <neutrinos>. Its visible <momentum> is only part of the original tau <momentum>, so the simple two-body endpoint is lost. For the direct <electron> and <muon> modes, the <transverse mass>
$$
M_T^2=2p_T^\ell p_T^\nu[1-\cos(\Delta\varphi)]
$$
also has an ideal endpoint at $M_W$ and avoids needing the unmeasured <neutrino> longitudinal <momentum>. Fits to this distribution or the <charged lepton>[charged-lepton] $p_T$ spectrum determine $M_W$.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Choose $W^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu$. Important backgrounds include <quantum chromodynamics> production of heavy-flavour <hadrons> with semileptonic decays, in-flight <pion> or <kaon> decays, and <hadrons> penetrating the calorimeters. These generally have accompanying jet activity; semileptonic decays can contain real <neutrinos>, while jet mismeasurement can fake <missing transverse momentum>. Heavy-flavour tracks can originate at a <displaced decay vertex>. Require a high-$p_T$ isolated <muon>, agreement between inner tracking and the external <muon> system, and substantial <missing transverse momentum> inconsistent with <particle detector>[detector] noise or a mismeasured jet.

The genuine electroweak backgrounds include $Z\to\mu^+\mu^-$ with one <muon> lost and $W\to\tau\nu\to\mu\nu\nu\nu$. Veto a second reconstructed opposite-sign <muon> and use the dimuon <invariant mass> when both are present to reject the <Z boson> channel. The tau-mediated spectrum is softer and contains extra missing <particles>; template fits and control samples estimate its residual contribution. <Detector acceptance> determines how much of the lost-muon background survives.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

For the selected <muon> channel, finite curvature resolution in the magnetic tracking system broadens the reconstructed <transverse momentum> spectrum. A high-$p_T$ track has small <sagitta>, so spatial measurement errors, alignment errors and magnetic-field calibration errors become important; at high <momentum> an approximately fixed uncertainty in $1/p_T$ gives $\delta p_T\propto p_T^2$. <Multiple Coulomb scattering> contributes at lower <momenta>. If the <transverse mass> is fitted, finite hadronic-recoil resolution additionally smears the inferred <missing transverse momentum>. These are experimental effects, distinct from the physical recoil and width in <1/b/ii>.

==== Solution

<Quadrupole magnets> provide transverse restoring forces proportional to displacement from the reference orbit. Their <magnetic field> increases linearly away from the axis, so an off-axis <particle> receives a kick that can bring its trajectory back toward the design orbit. A magnetic <quadrupole magnet>[quadrupole] focuses in one transverse plane and defocuses in the other; a single <quadrupole magnet>[quadrupole] cannot confine both planes.

An <alternating-gradient focusing> lattice alternates <quadrupole magnet>[quadrupole] orientations with drift spaces. With suitable strengths and separations, a focusing-defocusing sequence gives stable <betatron oscillations> in both planes. The focusing acts on transverse position, while the phase advance through the lattice converts an initial angular deviation into position and then a corrective kick. The beam is thus transported within a bounded envelope rather than allowed to drift into the pipe. Dipoles provide the main bending; the <quadrupole magnet>[quadrupole] lattice controls the small deviations.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $z=\sqrt\epsilon\,\omega\cos\psi$, with $\omega=\sqrt\beta>0$ and $\psi=\phi-\phi_0$. Differentiating twice gives
$$
\frac{z''+kz}{\sqrt\epsilon}
=[\omega''-\omega(\phi')^2+k\omega]\cos\psi
-[2\omega'\phi'+\omega\phi'']\sin\psi.
$$
The coefficients must vanish for arbitrary initial phase $\phi_0$. The sine coefficient gives
$$
(\omega^2\phi')'=0,\qquad \omega^2\phi'=C.
$$
The conventional <Courant-Snyder parameters> normalize the phase so that $C=1$; any positive constant $C$ can be absorbed into the scale assigned to $\beta$ and $\epsilon$. Explicitly, $\widetilde\beta=\beta/C$ and $\widetilde\epsilon=C\epsilon$ preserve the orbit while making $\widetilde\beta\phi'=1$. With this convention,
$$
\boxed{\phi'=\frac1\beta,\qquad
\omega''-\frac1{\omega^3}+k(s)\omega=0.}
$$
The second expression is the <Ermakov-Pinney equation> for the beam envelope. It follows by substituting $\phi'=1/\omega^2$ into the cosine coefficient.

**The printed equation omits the factor $\omega$ multiplying $k(s)$.** For a concrete counterexample, take the constant periodic focusing strength $k=1/16$ and the constant envelope $\omega=2$. Then $\phi'=1/4$ and $z=2\sqrt\epsilon\cos(s/4-\phi_0)$ solve $z''+z/16=0$. The correct envelope equation is $0-1/8+(1/16)2=0$, whereas the printed equation gives $-1/8+1/16=-1/16$.

The associated invariant is
$$
\epsilon=\gamma_Tz^2+2\alpha_Tzz'+\beta z'^2,
\qquad \alpha_T=-\beta'/2,\quad
\gamma_T=(1+\alpha_T^2)/\beta.
$$
Thus $\epsilon$ fixes the <phase space>[phase-space] ellipse of a trajectory; a beam's rms <geometric emittance> uses the corresponding second-moment convention. Factors of $\pi$ depend on whether emittance means ellipse area or area divided by $\pi$ and do not alter the envelope equation.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For a beam with rms <geometric emittance> $\epsilon_y$, its vertical size at the collision point is $\sigma_y^\star=\sqrt{\epsilon_y\beta_y^\star}$. Reducing $\beta_y^\star$ therefore concentrates the beam, increasing the number of <particles> encountering the opposite beam per transverse area. For identical Gaussian beams colliding head-on,
$$
\mathcal L=\frac{n_b f_{\rm rev}N_1N_2}{4\pi\sigma_x^\star\sigma_y^\star}.
$$
Keep bunch populations, horizontal size, collision frequency and emittances fixed. If both vertical beta values are decreased by a factor $f>1$, meaning $\beta_{y,\rm new}^\star=\beta_{y,\rm old}^\star/f$, both vertical beam sizes decrease by $1/\sqrt f$, and
$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathcal L_{\rm new}}{\mathcal L_{\rm old}}=\sqrt f.}
$$
The <collider luminosity>[luminosity] is inversely proportional to the common vertical size, not its square, because only one transverse dimension has changed. The same ratio follows from the unequal-beam overlap denominator $\sqrt{\sigma_{y1}^2+\sigma_{y2}^2}$.

A <final-focus quadrupole> system on either side of the interaction point forms a waist there. Strong <quadrupole magnet>[quadrupole] doublets or triplets, with suitable matching sections, focus both planes. At a symmetric waist $\beta'=0$ and the nearby drift gives $\beta(s)=\beta^\star+s^2/\beta^\star$, so small $\beta^\star$ requires large beam envelopes and strong optical gradients in the adjacent magnets. Chromatic correction is needed for off-momentum <particles>. The formula assumes negligible crossing-angle and hourglass reductions; a bunch length comparable to $\beta^\star$ changes the effective overlap.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a paraxial orbit $z'\simeq p_z/p_s$, where $p_z$ is transverse <momentum> and $p_s$ longitudinal <momentum>. Acceleration mainly increases $p_s$, so a given transverse kick produces a smaller angular deviation. In an ideal matched accelerator without scattering or radiation, the conserved transverse canonical <phase space>[phase-space] area becomes a smaller area in the coordinates $(z,z')$. This is <adiabatic damping of geometric emittance>, not loss of canonical <phase space>[phase-space] volume.

The invariant <normalized emittance> is
$$
\epsilon_n=\beta_{\rm rel}\gamma_{\rm rel}\epsilon
=\frac{p_s}{m}\epsilon,
\qquad
\boxed{\epsilon\propto p_s^{-1}.}
$$
Here $\beta_{\rm rel}$ is speed in units of $c$, distinct from the lattice beta function. Neglecting dilution mechanisms, each acceleration stage thus supplies a beam with smaller angular spread and <geometric emittance> for injection into the next stage.

A chain of increasingly energetic <particle accelerators> lets each ring operate over a manageable range of <magnetic rigidity> and radio-frequency settings. It also avoids injecting a large-emittance low-energy beam directly into the small-aperture high-field main ring. Higher injection <energy> reduces space-charge effects and improves capture and transmission. Emittance dilution, mismatching and collective instabilities can oppose the ideal damping; acceleration alone does not reduce <normalized emittance>.

==== Solution

Use the <orthonormality>[orthonormal] basis $|u\bar u\rangle,|d\bar d\rangle,|s\bar s\rangle$. The normalized <quark flavor>[flavour] <singlet representation>[singlet] is
$$
\boxed{|1\rangle=\frac{|u\bar u\rangle+|d\bar d\rangle+|s\bar s\rangle}{\sqrt3},
\qquad I=I_3=S=0.}
$$
The <SU(3)> invariant contraction pairs each fundamental <quark flavor>[flavour] with its conjugate. Applying any supplied raising operator to the <singlet representation>[singlet] gives zero because its <quark> and <antiquark> contributions cancel.

To fix the neutral <isospin> <triplet representation>[triplet] phase consistently with the source's charged states, apply $I_+$ to $-|d\bar u\rangle$:
$$
I_+[-|d\bar u\rangle]=|d\bar d\rangle-|u\bar u\rangle.
$$
Thus one missing member of the <meson octet> is
$$
\boxed{|T_0\rangle=\frac{|d\bar d\rangle-|u\bar u\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad I=1,\ I_3=S=0.}
$$
Indeed $I_+|T_0\rangle=\sqrt2|u\bar d\rangle$, the correct <ladder operator> strength for the $I=1$ <triplet representation>[triplet].

The other missing state is orthogonal to both $|1\rangle$ and $|T_0\rangle$. For coefficients $(a,b,c)$, <orthogonality> requires $a=b$ and $a+b+c=0$. Choose the convenient overall phase $c>0$ and normalize:
$$
\boxed{|8_0\rangle=\frac{2|s\bar s\rangle-|u\bar u\rangle-|d\bar d\rangle}{\sqrt6},
\qquad I=I_3=S=0.}
$$
It can also be obtained by adding the supplied ladder operations $V_+[-|s\bar u\rangle]=|s\bar s\rangle-|u\bar u\rangle$ and $U_+[-|s\bar d\rangle]=|s\bar s\rangle-|d\bar d\rangle$. The three displayed states are mutually <orthonormality>[orthonormal]. The <meson octet> here is the traceless adjoint component of $\bar3\otimes3$; the source's word “antisymmetric” does not impose antisymmetry on all its quark-antiquark <quark flavor>[flavour] wavefunctions.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Only the <singlet representation>[singlet] $|1\rangle$ and isoscalar <octet representation>[octet] member $|8_0\rangle$ can mix without violating <isospin>: they have the same $I=I_3=S=0$ and, for the vector multiplet, the same $J^{PC}=1^{--}$. The neutral <triplet representation>[triplet] member has $I=1$ and is excluded in the <isospin> limit.

Set $|\psi_a\rangle=|1\rangle$ and $|\psi_b\rangle=|8_0\rangle$, with precisely the phases in <3/a>. Define $|n\rangle=(|u\bar u\rangle+|d\bar d\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Taking overlaps gives
$$
|n\rangle=\sqrt{\frac23}|1\rangle-\frac1{\sqrt3}|8_0\rangle,
\qquad
|s\bar s\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt3}|1\rangle+\sqrt{\frac23}|8_0\rangle.
$$
Therefore the source's rotation convention is satisfied by
$$
\boxed{\cos\theta=\sqrt{\frac23},\qquad
\sin\theta=-\frac1{\sqrt3},\qquad
\theta=-\arctan\frac1{\sqrt2}\simeq-35.26^\circ.}
$$
This yields $|\omega\rangle=|n\rangle$ and $|\phi\rangle=|s\bar s\rangle$ exactly. Reversing the <octet representation>[octet] phase changes the quoted sign, with an irrelevant compensating phase on the physical phi state; exchanging the basis order gives the complementary angle. The physical statement of <ideal vector-meson mixing> is the pure strange and pure nonstrange content, rather than a convention-independent sign for the angle.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The observed dominance of <kaons> supports a predominantly $s\bar s$ <phi meson>. In a connected strong decay, the original strange <quark> and <antiquark> survive into different <kaons>, while a <gluon> produces a light $q\bar q$ pair:
$$
s\bar s\longrightarrow(s\bar q)(q\bar s),\qquad q=u,d.
$$
This gives $K^-K^+$ or $\bar K^0K^0$ and is allowed by the <OZI rule>. A pionic final state contains no strange valence constituents, so the initial $s\bar s$ pair must annihilate. The strong annihilation of a colour-singlet $1^{--}$ state requires at least three <gluons>: one <gluon> cannot carry the <singlet representation>[singlet] colour, and two have the wrong charge-conjugation quantum number. This disconnected <quark flavor>[flavour] topology is suppressed by the <OZI rule>, although it need not vanish.

\Image[2019-phi-decay-topologies.png]
{title=Original quark-level topologies for connected kaon production, three-gluon annihilation into pions, and electromagnetic annihilation into leptons. The hadronization blob is schematic.}

The leptonic mode proceeds through $s\bar s\to\gamma^*\to\ell^+\ell^-$ and is suppressed by <electromagnetic interaction>[electromagnetic couplings]; its width is proportional to $\alpha^2$ times the vector-meson overlap. Unlike a strong <hadron>[hadronic] decay, it cannot create its final <particles> through <quark> rearrangement. Hence a <branching fraction> of order $10^{-4}$ is qualitatively consistent with <electromagnetism>[electromagnetic] annihilation competing with strong modes.

Kaon dominance is especially suggestive because <phase space> is limited: using the supplied neutral-kaon and phi <masses>,
$$
\boxed{p_K=\sqrt{\frac{m_\phi^2}{4}-m_{K^0}^2}\simeq110\ \mathrm{MeV},\qquad
m_\phi-2m_{K^0}=24\ \mathrm{MeV}.}
$$
The two spin-zero <kaons> have <orbital angular momentum> $L=1$ to match the vector parent, so their <partial decay width>[partial width] has the additional near-threshold factor $p_K^3$. Pionic modes have more <phase space> yet remain subdominant because of their annihilation topology. The observations therefore support, but do not establish exact, <ideal vector-meson mixing>: a small nonstrange admixture, <electromagnetism>[electromagnetic] amplitudes and nonperturbative strong dynamics can all contribute to the pionic rate.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

<Quark mass> differences break <flavor symmetry>[flavour symmetry]. <Isospin> exchanges $u,d$, so its strong breaking is controlled by the small difference $m_d-m_u$. <U-spin> exchanges $d,s$, so its strong breaking involves the much larger $m_s-m_d$. Relative to the characteristic strong-interaction scale, the latter is much larger; this explains why <U-spin> is a substantially poorer symmetry.

Flavour-dependent interactions are a second source. <Electromagnetism> distinguishes $u$ from $d$ because $Q_u=2/3$ and $Q_d=-1/3$, and therefore breaks <isospin>. However, $d,s$ both have charge $-1/3$, so their <electromagnetic interaction>[electromagnetic couplings] alone do **not** break <U-spin> in the equal-mass limit. If two breaking mechanisms for each symmetry are required, the second common mechanism is the <weak interaction>: the <CKM matrix> and weak <quark flavor>[flavour] transitions distinguish the flavours and do not conserve either strong-interaction <flavor symmetry>[flavour symmetry]. The dominant difference in the quality of the two approximate strong symmetries is still the <mass> splitting.

=== a

==== Solution

The <CKM matrix> converts down-type <quark mass> eigenstates into the combinations entering the <weak charged current>. Its entry $V_{ij}$ controls a <weak charged current>[charged-current] transition between up-type <quark flavor>[flavour] $i$ and down-type <quark flavor>[flavour] $j$. In the three-generation <Standard Model>, $V=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}$ is a product of <unitary matrices> from diagonalizing the <quark mass> matrices, so $V^\dagger V=1$.

The <Wolfenstein parametrization> displays the hierarchy
$$
|V|\sim\begin{pmatrix}1&\lambda&\lambda^3\\\lambda&1&\lambda^2\\\lambda^3&\lambda^2&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus diagonal transitions dominate; first-second generation mixing is larger than second-third generation mixing, and direct first-third generation mixing is smallest. The parameters $A,\rho,\eta$ refine this order estimate. With nondegenerate <quark masses>, a nonzero irreducible weak phase permits <CP violation>; at leading order the <Jarlskog invariant> is $J\simeq A^2\lambda^6\eta$. Individual complex entries are not enough: the phase must survive allowed quark-field rephasings.

The exact physical <CKM matrix> is unitary, whereas its displayed cubic truncation is only approximately so. For example its first-row norm is $1+\lambda^4/4+A^2\lambda^6(\rho^2+\eta^2)$, which is not exactly one for nonzero $\lambda$. The omitted higher-order terms repair this; <unitarity> must be tested to the retained order.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The inner product of columns one and three is
$$
V_{ud}^*V_{ub}+V_{cd}^*V_{cb}+V_{td}^*V_{tb}
=A\lambda^3[(\rho-i\eta)-1+(1-\rho+i\eta)]+O(\lambda^5)=O(\lambda^5).
$$
The leading terms cancel, establishing compatibility with <unitarity> at cubic order. Direct multiplication of the printed truncation leaves $-A\lambda^5(\rho-i\eta)/2$, so it must not be treated as exactly unitary beyond its stated accuracy.

For the exact matrix, conjugate the closure relation and define
$$
a=V_{ud}V_{ub}^*,\quad b=V_{cd}V_{cb}^*,\quad c=V_{td}V_{tb}^*,\qquad a+b+c=0.
$$
Divide by $-b$ and let $z=-a/b=\bar\rho+i\bar\eta$. Then $a/(-b)=z$ and $c/(-b)=1-z$, giving a <CKM unitarity triangle> with vertices
$$
\boxed{(0,0),\qquad(1,0),\qquad(\bar\rho,\bar\eta).}
$$
At leading Wolfenstein order these are $(0,0),(1,0),(\rho,\eta)$. The barred coordinates define the exact triangle; they differ from the unbarred parameters beyond leading order.

\Image[2019-unitarity-and-b-decay.png]
{title=Original normalized unitarity triangle, top-dominated neutral-B mixing box, and the leading tree topology for a common charmonium-kaon final state. The triangle coordinates are illustrative.}

For positive $\bar\eta$, define $\gamma$ at the origin, $\beta$ at $(1,0)$, and $\alpha$ at the apex. Comparing the directions of the incident complex sides gives the rephasing-invariant relations
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\alpha&=\arg\left[-\frac{V_{td}V_{tb}^*}{V_{ud}V_{ub}^*}\right],\\
\beta&=\arg\left[-\frac{V_{cd}V_{cb}^*}{V_{td}V_{tb}^*}\right],\\
\gamma&=\arg\left[-\frac{V_{ud}V_{ub}^*}{V_{cd}V_{cb}^*}\right],\qquad
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=\pi.
\end{aligned}}
$$
To leading order $\gamma=\arg(\rho+i\eta)$ and $\beta=\operatorname{atan2}(\eta,1-\rho)$; the remaining angle follows by subtraction. Each ratio cancels the unphysical common phase of the corresponding column-orthogonality terms.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Use $B^0=d\bar b$ and $\bar B^0=\bar d b$. A <box Feynman diagram> with two <W bosons> and internal up-type <quarks> changes one neutral <quark flavor>[flavour] into the other. The <top quark> contribution dominates the dispersive <neutral B meson mixing>[mixing amplitude] and carries the squared CKM combination involving $V_{td},V_{tb}$. With a consistent meson phase convention,
$$
\frac qp\simeq\frac{V_{td}V_{tb}^*}{V_{td}^*V_{tb}}.
$$
The illustration in <4/b> shows this box and the dominant decay topology. A suitable common final state is $f=J/\psi\,K_S$, reconstructed for example through $J/\psi\to\mu^+\mu^-$ and $K_S\to\pi^+\pi^-$. The leading $\bar b\to\bar c c\bar s$ tree transition gives $B^0\to J/\psi K^0$, while the conjugate gives $\bar B^0\to J/\psi\bar K^0$. <Neutral kaon mixing> projects both kaon flavours onto the observed $K_S$.

The final state is approximately a <CP eigenstate> with $\eta_f=-1$: $J/\psi$ and $K_S$ each have CP approximately $+1$, and their relative <orbital angular momentum> is $L=1$ to couple the vector charmonium to the spin-zero parent. Neglecting the small kaon CP impurity and subleading <decay amplitude>[decay amplitudes],
$$
\boxed{\lambda_f=\frac qp\frac{\bar A_f}{A_f}\simeq-e^{-2i\beta},\qquad
\operatorname{Im}\lambda_f=\sin2\beta.}
$$
There are two indistinguishable paths, direct decay and mixing followed by decay, so their amplitudes interfere. For $\Delta\Gamma_d\simeq0$ and negligible direct <CP violation>, the <time-dependent neutral-B CP asymmetry> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{\Gamma(\bar B^0(t)\to f)-\Gamma(B^0(t)\to f)}
{\Gamma(\bar B^0(t)\to f)+\Gamma(B^0(t)\to f)}
=\sin2\beta\,\sin(\Delta m_d t).}
$$
Measure the production <quark flavor>[flavour] and proper decay time, fit the oscillation coefficient, and extract $\sin2\beta$. This determines $\beta$ up to the usual trigonometric ambiguities unless further information is used. The cancellation of the dominant decay phase is why this channel isolates the angle involving $V_{td}$ without needing $V_{ub}$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

In a proton-proton collision, associate the reconstructed neutral <B meson> with the accompanying opposite-side bottom <hadron>. A direct semileptonic decay $b\to c\ell^-\bar\nu$ tags a $b$ on that side and hence an initial $\bar b$, or $B^0=d\bar b$, on the signal side. The opposite <charged lepton> tags the conjugate assignment. Charged <kaons> from the opposite-side decay chain and same-side fragmentation tracks can supply additional statistical <flavour tagging> information; the common CP final state alone does not reveal the production <quark flavor>[flavour].

A magnetic spectrometer with precise silicon vertex tracking, charged-particle identification and <muon> or <electron> identification is suitable. A forward geometry can exploit the boosted bottom-hadron pairs at the <LHC>. It reconstructs displaced decay vertices, measures the signed tagging tracks, and infers the signal proper time from $t\simeq Lm_B/|\mathbf p_B|$. The supplied lifetime corresponds to a rest-frame mean flight length $c\tau\simeq0.45\ \mathrm{mm}$, enlarged by the <Lorentz boost>[boost].

Even a perfect <particle detector>[detector] cannot remove physical mistags. An opposite-side neutral bottom meson can oscillate before its semileptonic decay, reversing the <lepton> charge relative to its production <quark flavor>[flavour]. A cascade $b\to c\to\ell^+$ produces the charge opposite to a direct $b\to\ell^-$ decay. Additional bottom pairs or unrelated fragmentation <particles> can also make the chosen association incorrect. For mistag probability $w$, the measured asymmetry is diluted by $D=1-2w$; this is why the tagging calibration is part of a CP measurement.

=== a

==== Solution

The ordering of <Weyl spinor>[Weyl components] depends on the supplied basis transformation. In this paper use $u_W=Uu_D=(\xi,\eta)^{\mathsf T}$. Applying the printed $U$ to the printed Dirac matrices gives
$$
\gamma_W^0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\I&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma_W^i=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\sigma_i\\\sigma_i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\gamma_W^5=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&-I\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Thus $\xi$ is right chiral and $\eta$ left chiral in this ordering. The free <massless particle>[massless] <Weyl equations> are
$$
\boxed{i(\partial_t+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)\xi=0,
\qquad i(\partial_t-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol\nabla)\eta=0.}
$$
They describe <spin angular momentum>[spin-one-half] <massless particle>[massless] fields in the two inequivalent chiral <spinor representation of the Lorentz group>[spinor representations of the Lorentz group]. Their rotation generators agree, but their <opposite Weyl boost generators>[boost generators] have opposite signs; spatial parity interchanges the two. A positive-energy <particle> in the $\xi$ field has <helicity> $+1/2$, and one in the $\eta$ field has <helicity> $-1/2$. Their antiparticles have the opposite <helicities>, so a <Weyl field> is not a field containing only one <helicity> for both <particles> and antiparticles.

<Neutrinos> provide the observed ultrarelativistic approximation to a left-chiral <particle> and right-helicity antiparticle. However, <neutrino oscillation> establishes nonzero <neutrino mass> differences, so physical <neutrinos> are not all exactly <massless particle>[massless] Weyl <particles>. Chiral <Weyl fields> occur in the <Standard Model>, but an isolated exactly <massless particle>[massless] elementary Weyl <particle> is not established by <neutrino> observations. Whether <neutrino mass> eigenstates are Dirac or Majorana remains a separate question.<sources/neutrino-masses-mixing-and-oscillations-by-particle-data-group-2024>[Sections 14.2, 14.4 and 14.9.2: massive-neutrino extensions, mass-induced oscillations, and the Dirac–Majorana question]{ref}

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

With the <gamma matrices> derived in <5/a>, the momentum-space <Dirac equation> reads
$$
(E-\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p)\xi=m\eta,
\qquad (E+\boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\mathbf p)\eta=m\xi.
$$
For a positive-energy <particle> at rest, $E=m>0$ and $\mathbf p=0$, so $\xi=\eta$. The <Dirac adjoint> in this basis is $\bar u=(\eta^\dagger,\xi^\dagger)$, and the specified <normalization> gives
$$
\bar u u=\eta^\dagger\xi+\xi^\dagger\eta=2\xi^\dagger\xi=2m.
$$
Therefore, for any normalized two-component <spinor> $\chi$,
$$
\boxed{\xi(0)=\eta(0)=\sqrt m\,\chi,\qquad
u_W(0)=\sqrt m\begin{pmatrix}\chi\\\chi\end{pmatrix},\qquad \chi^\dagger\chi=1.}
$$
Equivalently, choosing $\chi_+=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and $\chi_-=(0,1)^{\mathsf T}$ gives the two independent rest-frame <spin angular momentum>[spin states]. A strictly <massless particle>[massless] <particle> has no rest frame; this step requires $m>0$.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Choose the active <Lorentz boost>[boost] producing $p_z=m\sinh\rho$ and $E=m\cosh\rho$. The supplied two-component <Lorentz boost>[boost] $L=\exp(-\rho\sigma_3/2)$ acts on the left-chiral block $\eta$; the right-chiral block $\xi$ transforms by its inverse. Applying them to <5/b> gives
$$
\boxed{\xi(p)=\sqrt m\,e^{\rho\sigma_3/2}\chi,
\qquad \eta(p)=\sqrt m\,e^{-\rho\sigma_3/2}\chi.}
$$
Use $me^{\pm\rho}=E\pm p_z$ and $\sigma_3\chi_\pm=\pm\chi_\pm$ to obtain
$$
\boxed{u_+(p)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{E+p_z}\\0\\\sqrt{E-p_z}\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u_-(p)=\begin{pmatrix}0\\\sqrt{E-p_z}\\0\\\sqrt{E+p_z}\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Both are <particle> <spinors>, with spin respectively parallel and antiparallel to the positive $z$ axis. A compact equivalent expression is $\xi=\sqrt{E+p_z\sigma_3}\chi$, $\eta=\sqrt{E-p_z\sigma_3}\chi$, using the positive matrix square roots.

The products of corresponding nonzero upper and lower components equal $\sqrt{E^2-p_z^2}=m$, so $\bar u_\pm u_\pm=2m$ and $u_\pm^\dagger u_\pm=2E$. Substitution also verifies $(E-p_z\sigma_3)\xi=m\eta$ and $(E+p_z\sigma_3)\eta=m\xi$. These checks fix which <Lorentz boost>[boost] belongs to which component and prevent silently exchanging the chiral ordering defined by the source's $U$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Take $p_z>0$ and $E\gg m$. Then $\sqrt{E+p_z}\simeq\sqrt{2E}$ while $\sqrt{E-p_z}=m/\sqrt{E+p_z}\simeq m/\sqrt{2E}$. Hence
$$
\boxed{u_+(p)\longrightarrow\sqrt{2E}\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
u_-(p)\longrightarrow\sqrt{2E}\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The <helicity> operator is $\widehat h=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_3,\sigma_3)$ for <momentum> along $+z$. It gives
$$
\boxed{h[u_+]=+\tfrac12,\qquad h[u_-]=-\tfrac12.}
$$
The positive-helicity state becomes entirely right chiral, while the negative-helicity state becomes entirely left chiral. The suppressed opposite-chirality amplitude is of relative order $m/(2E)$; at finite <mass> <chirality> is not the same as <helicity>. If $p_z<0$, <helicity> is measured along the reversed <momentum> and the labels of the two spin-axis states interchange.

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the leading-order <parton model>: scatter incoherently from an approximately on-shell collinear <quark> carrying nucleon <momentum> fraction $x$, neglect <quark> and <lepton> <masses>, use an unpolarized target, and neglect nuclear binding and higher-twist corrections. The condition $Q^2\gg m_p^2$ suppresses target-mass effects, while $Q^2\ll M_W^2$ permits the local <Fermi interaction> instead of the full W propagator. CKM-weighted accessible final flavours are approximated by their leading light-quark sums. The struck-quark <centre-of-momentum frame>[centre-of-mass] <energy> is $\widehat s\simeq xs$, with $s=(k+p)^2\simeq2m_NE_\nu$.

A <neutrino> scatters through $\nu d\to\mu^-u$ or $\nu s\to\mu^-c$, and through the corresponding crossed <antiquark> channels such as $\nu\bar u\to\mu^-\bar d$. The chiral <weak charged current>[charged current] makes the <massless particle>[massless] <quark> contribution independent of $y$ and the <antiquark> contribution proportional to $(1-y)^2$. The scattering probability is weighted by the <parton distribution functions>; the explicit factor $x$ comes from $\widehat s=xs$. In the usual convention $q(x)dx$ is a number probability density, while $xq(x)$ is a <momentum> density. Approximate <Bjorken scaling> leaves mainly $x$ dependence; <quantum chromodynamics>[QCD] evolution adds a logarithmic $Q^2$ dependence.

**The printed proton-density formula is not an isoscalar formula in the standard PDF convention.** For a <proton>, neglecting incoming charm and taking accessible CKM sums as unity, the conventional leading-order result is
$$
\frac{d^2\sigma^{\nu p}}{dxdy}=\frac{G_F^2s}{\pi}\,x\left[d_p+s_p+(1-y)^2\bar u_p\right].
$$
For an <isoscalar target> per nucleon, average <proton> and <neutron> and use <charge symmetry of nucleon parton distributions>: $d_n=u_p$, $\bar u_n=\bar d_p$, $s_n=s_p$. This gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2\sigma^{\nu N}}{dxdy}=\frac{G_F^2s}{2\pi}\,x
\left[u_p+d_p+2s_p+(1-y)^2(\bar u_p+\bar d_p)\right].}
$$
The standard <normalization> follows equivalently by inserting $F_2^{\nu p}=2x(d+s+\bar u)$ and $xF_3^{\nu p}=2x(d+s-\bar u)$ into the <weak charged current>[charged-current] structure-function formula.<sources/structure-functions-by-particle-data-group-2024>[Section 18.1, Eqs. (18.8) and (18.19): <weak charged current>[charged-current] <normalization> and <proton> <quark> combinations]{ref}

A concrete discrepancy remains even with a simple valence model: take $u_p=2h(x)$, $d_p=h(x)$ and all sea densities zero. The correctly averaged isoscalar bracket is $3h(x)$, while the printed bracket is only $h(x)$ with the same prefactor. Thus the intended <helicity> and $x$ dependence can be explained, but the literal printed isoscalar claim cannot be derived. If the symbols were instead defined as <momentum> densities, the extra explicit $x$ would also need removal.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For the stationary target, $p=(m_N,\mathbf0)$. The invariant definition immediately gives
$$
\boxed{y=\frac{p\cdot(k-k')}{p\cdot k}
=\frac{m_N(E_\nu-E_\mu)}{m_NE_\nu}
=1-\frac{E_\mu}{E_\nu}.}
$$
No <massless particle>[massless] approximation is needed for this laboratory identity when binding and target recoil conventions are neglected.

For the angular relation, specify the neutrino-parton <centre-of-momentum frame> and neglect <masses> there. The incoming and outgoing <leptons> then each have <energy> $\sqrt{\widehat s}/2$. Their <momentum> transfer satisfies
$$
Q^2=2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\widehat s}}2\right)^2(1-\cos\theta^\star)
=\frac{\widehat s}{2}(1-\cos\theta^\star).
$$
Since $Q^2=xys$ and $\widehat s=xs$, cancellation gives
$$
\boxed{y=\frac{1-\cos\theta^\star}{2},\qquad
1-y=\frac{1+\cos\theta^\star}{2}.}
$$
This identifies the <antiquark> weight $(1-y)^2$ with the corresponding squared angular factor. The relevant <massless particle>[massless] condition is sufficient <energy> in the lepton-parton subprocess; $E_\nu\gg m_\mu$ alone does not control every endpoint configuration.

The frame qualifier matters: the complete neutrino-nucleon <centre-of-momentum frame>[centre-of-mass] final state contains a variable-mass <hadron>[hadronic] system. In that frame, still neglecting <masses> but retaining $x$, direct two-body kinematics for $\mu+X$ gives instead
$$
y=\frac{1-\cos\theta_{\nu N}}{1-\cos\theta_{\nu N}+x(1+\cos\theta_{\nu N})}.
$$
For example $x=1/2$ and $\theta_{\nu N}=\pi/2$ give $y=2/3$, not $1/2$. Thus the simple angle-only relation implicitly uses the <parton model>[partonic] frame.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For a valence-dominated <isoscalar target>, $u_v^N(x)=d_v^N(x)=q_v(x)$. A left-chiral incident <neutrino> has unsuppressed scattering on a left-chiral <quark> and the $(1-y)^2$ weight for a right-chiral <quark>. Summing the up and down contributions yields
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2\sigma_{\rm NC}^{\nu N}}{dxdy}
=\frac{G_F^2s}{\pi}\,xq_v(x)
\left[G_L^2+G_R^2(1-y)^2\right],}
$$
where $G_L^2=(g_L^u)^2+(g_L^d)^2$ and $G_R^2=(g_R^u)^2+(g_R^d)^2$. The common <normalization> here uses the conventional Fermi coupling and <parton distribution functions>[PDFs] in <6/a>; the requested $y$ shape is independent of an overall <normalization> convention.

Put $w=\sin^2\theta_W$. With $I_3^u=+1/2$, $I_3^d=-1/2$, $Q_u=2/3$ and $Q_d=-1/3$,
$$
g_L^u=\frac12-\frac23w,\quad g_L^d=-\frac12+\frac13w,
\qquad g_R^u=-\frac23w,\quad g_R^d=\frac13w,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{G_L^2=\frac12-w+\frac59w^2,\qquad
G_R^2=\frac59w^2,\qquad
\frac{d\sigma_{\rm NC}}{dy}\propto
\left(\frac12-w+\frac59w^2\right)+\frac59w^2(1-y)^2.}
$$
Fit the relative constant and $(1-y)^2$ components to determine their coupling ratio, or use the <neutrino neutral-to-charged current ratio>[NC-to-CC cross-section ratio] to constrain the absolute combination. In this valence limit, integrating over $0<y<1$ gives
$$
\boxed{R_\nu=\frac{\sigma_{\rm NC}^{\nu N}}{\sigma_{\rm CC}^{\nu N}}
=G_L^2+\frac13G_R^2
=\frac12-w+\frac{20}{27}w^2.}
$$
The same valence <momentum> integral cancels between numerator and denominator. Experimentally, NC events have <hadron>[hadronic] activity without the <weak charged current>[charged-current] outgoing <muon>; known fluxes or CC <normalization>, <muon> identification and acceptance corrections are needed. Sea-quark contributions, charm thresholds, <quantum chromodynamics>[QCD] corrections and departures from isoscalarity must be included for a precision <weak mixing angle>[weak-mixing-angle] measurement.
