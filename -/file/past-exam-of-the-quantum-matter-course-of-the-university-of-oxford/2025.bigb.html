The <Hartree-Fock method> approximates the <ground-state> <wavefunction> by a single <Slater determinant>, minimizing the <expectation value> of the <Hamiltonian> over its occupied <orbitals>. It retains <fermionic exchange symmetry> exactly within that determinant but omits <electron correlation> beyond a single <Slater determinant>.

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

The <periodic boundary condition> requires $e^{ikM}=1$, so there are exactly $M$ independent <wavevectors> in a <Brillouin zone>:
$$
\boxed{k=\frac{2\pi l}{M},\qquad l=0,\ldots,M-1\quad(\bmod\ 2\pi).}
$$
The inverse <discrete Fourier transform>, with the sign convention of the paper, is
$$
c^\dagger_{\sigma,j}=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_k e^{-ikj}\widetilde c^\dagger_{\sigma,k},\qquad
c_{\sigma,j}=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_k e^{ikj}\widetilde c_{\sigma,k}.
$$
<Fourier orthogonality> gives the <tight-binding> <dispersion relation> $\epsilon_k=-2t\cos k$. For the interaction, first use the <canonical anticommutation relations> at distinct sites to write $n_{\sigma,j}n_{\sigma,j+1}=c^\dagger_{\sigma,j}c^\dagger_{\sigma,j+1}c_{\sigma,j+1}c_{\sigma,j}$. Summing its <discrete Fourier transform> over $j$ enforces <crystal momentum conservation>, giving
$$
\boxed{H=\sum_{\sigma,k}(-2t\cos k)\widetilde c^\dagger_{\sigma,k}\widetilde c_{\sigma,k}
+\frac VM\sum_{\sigma,k,p,q}e^{iq}\widetilde c^\dagger_{\sigma,k+q}\widetilde c^\dagger_{\sigma,p-q}\widetilde c_{\sigma,p}\widetilde c_{\sigma,k}.}
$$
Every <wavevector> is reduced modulo $2\pi$. The phase $e^{iq}$ comes from the displacement of the second site by one lattice spacing. Equivalently replace $e^{iq}$ by $\cos q$: exchanging the two incoming and the two outgoing <fermions> changes $q$ to $-q$ with two cancelling <fermionic signs>. Thus the usual interaction vertex is $2V\cos q$ accompanied by the factor $1/(2M)$.

For each <spin> let $\mathcal F_\sigma$ contain the $N_\sigma$ lowest <tight-binding> <energy levels>, and set $n_\sigma=N_\sigma/M$ and $C_\sigma=M^{-1}\sum_{k\in\mathcal F_\sigma}e^{ik}$. The normalized trial <Slater determinant> is
$$
|\mathcal F\rangle=\prod_{\sigma}\prod_{k\in\mathcal F_\sigma}\widetilde c^\dagger_{\sigma,k}|0\rangle.
$$
Its nearest-neighbour <one-body density matrix> is $\langle c^\dagger_{\sigma,j}c_{\sigma,j+1}\rangle=C_\sigma$. By <Wick's theorem>, <fermionic exchange symmetry> subtracts the squared off-diagonal <one-body density matrix> from the direct contribution
$$
\langle n_{\sigma,j}n_{\sigma,j+1}\rangle=n_\sigma^2-|C_\sigma|^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\langle H\rangle=M\sum_\sigma\left[-2t\operatorname{Re}C_\sigma+V(n_\sigma^2-|C_\sigma|^2)\right].}
$$
This formula also specifies the finite-$M$ answer exactly, including partially filled terminal <energy levels> through the chosen occupied set. For $N_\sigma$ consecutive allowed <wavevectors>, $|C_\sigma|=|\sin(\pi N_\sigma/M)|/[M\sin(\pi/M)]$; its phase locates the centre of that occupied interval.

Write $\tau=|t|$. In the large-$M$ limit the <Fermi sea> is centred at $k_0=0$ for $t>0$ and $k_0=\pi$ for $t<0$, with <Fermi wavevector> $k_{F,\sigma}=\pi n_\sigma$ measured from $k_0$. Consequently
$$
C_\sigma\longrightarrow e^{ik_0}\frac{\sin(\pi n_\sigma)}\pi,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\langle H\rangle}{M}=\sum_{\sigma=\uparrow,\downarrow}\left[-\frac{2\tau}{\pi}\sin(\pi n_\sigma)+V\left(n_\sigma^2-\frac{\sin^2(\pi n_\sigma)}{\pi^2}\right)\right].}
$$
The subtraction due to <fermionic exchange symmetry> is essential: replacing $\langle n_jn_{j+1}\rangle$ by $n^2$ would discard the suppression of neighbouring equal-<spin> occupation. A completely filled <spin> band has zero <kinetic energy> and interaction <energy> $MV$, as the formula also shows.

At $N=M$, the two <spin> occupations per site are $x$ and $1-x$. With $\tau=|t|>0$, the trial <energy> per site is
$$
\boxed{e(x)=\frac{\langle H\rangle}{M}=-\frac{4\tau}{\pi}\sin(\pi x)+V\left[x^2+(1-x)^2-\frac{2\sin^2(\pi x)}{\pi^2}\right],\quad 0\leq x\leq1.}
$$
Its <derivative> is $e'(x)=-4\tau\cos(\pi x)+V[4x-2-(2/\pi)\sin(2\pi x)]$. Since $x=0$ is a boundary point, its <local minimum> test is one-sided: $e'(0)=-4\tau-2V$. At $V=-2\tau$, where this <derivative> vanishes, its <Taylor series> begins $e(x)-e(0)=(2\pi^2\tau/3)x^3+O(x^4)>0$ for small positive $x$. Hence
$$
\boxed{x=0\text{ is a strict one-sided local minimum exactly when }V/\tau\leq-2.}
$$
The same applies to $x=1$ by <spin> interchange.

The <symmetry> $e(x)=e(1-x)$ makes $x=1/2$ a stationary point for every $V$. Here $e''(1/2)=4\pi\tau+8V$, so the <second derivative test> settles all cases except equality. Writing $y=x-1/2$, at $V=-\pi\tau/2$ the <Taylor series> is $e(1/2+y)-e(1/2)=(\pi^3\tau/6)y^4+O(y^6)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{x=\tfrac12\text{ is a strict local minimum exactly when }V/\tau\geq-\pi/2;\quad V/\tau<-\pi/2\text{ makes it a local maximum}.}
$$
**The minimizing branches interpolate continuously.** To find all of them, put $u=\pi(1/2-x)$ on $0<x<1/2$. An interior stationary point obeys
$$
\frac V\tau=-\frac{\pi\sin u}{u+\sin u\cos u},\qquad 0<u<\frac\pi2.
$$
The right side decreases strictly from $-\pi/2$ to $-2$, since
$$
\frac{d}{du}\frac{\sin u}{u+\sin u\cos u}
=\frac{\cos u\,(u-\sin u\cos u)}{(u+\sin u\cos u)^2}>0.
$$
The positivity follows from $u-\sin u\cos u=\int_0^u2\sin^2v\,dv$. There is therefore exactly one such stationary point on each side of $1/2$ when $-2<V/\tau<-\pi/2$. The signs of $e'$ show that both are <global minima>, whereas $1/2$ is then a <local maximum>. The complete answer is
$$
\boxed{x_{\min}=\begin{cases}
0\text{ or }1,&V/\tau\leq-2,\\
\tfrac12\mp u/\pi,&-2<V/\tau<-\pi/2,\quad V/\tau=-\pi\sin u/(u+\sin u\cos u),\\
\tfrac12,&V/\tau\geq-\pi/2.
\end{cases}}
$$
The pair merges through a <supercritical pitchfork bifurcation> at $V/\tau=-\pi/2$. Comparing only $e(0)$ and $e(1/2)$ would miss the intervening <ground states> with partial <spin polarization>.

For $V\gg|t|$, the leading objective is to avoid adjacent electrons of the same <spin>. The unpolarized <Fermi sea>, although optimal within the preceding restricted family, has interaction <energy> per site $V(1/2-2/\pi^2)>0$. Its cost grows linearly with $V$, while states with no forbidden neighbouring pairs have zero interaction <energy>.

There are two independent <spinless fermion t-V chains>, one for each <spin>. On an even ring, avoiding all neighbouring pairs allows at most $M/2$ particles in each chain. Since the total is $M$, both bounds must be saturated, and each chain must alternate occupied and empty sites. Their two independent choices give **four limiting occupation patterns**:
$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
\uparrow,\downarrow,\uparrow,\downarrow,\ldots;\qquad
\downarrow,\uparrow,\downarrow,\uparrow,\ldots;\\
\uparrow\downarrow,0,\uparrow\downarrow,0,\ldots;\qquad
0,\uparrow\downarrow,0,\uparrow\downarrow,\ldots.
\end{gathered}}
$$
The first two are <Néel states> with one electron per site; the last two are <charge density waves> alternating doubly occupied and empty sites. Opposite-<spin> double occupation costs nothing in this particular <Hamiltonian>. Each species has alternating occupation in all four patterns.

These are the exact zero-interaction <ground states> at $t=0$, and the limiting ordered configurations as $V/|t|\to\infty$. At large finite $V/|t|$, <virtual hopping> admixes configurations with forbidden pairs, producing an <energy> correction of order $-M|t|^2/V$. A finite ring can have <superpositions> of the ordered configurations, with possible <quantum tunnelling> splittings; four exactly degenerate product <ground states> are not implied at finite hopping. The four ordered choices describe the strong-coupling <thermodynamic limit>, with <spontaneous symmetry breaking> understood.

Measure single-particle <energy> from the <quantum harmonic oscillator> <ground-state> <energy> $\hbar\omega$, and write $\widetilde\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{physical}}-\hbar\omega\leq0$. The <Bose-Einstein distribution> and the supplied <density of states> give
$$
N_{\mathrm{ex}}(T,\widetilde\mu)=\int_0^\infty\frac{E\,dE}{(\hbar\omega)^2[e^{(E-\widetilde\mu)/(k_BT)}-1]}.
$$
This excited-state population increases with the <chemical potential> and has the finite saturation value
$$
N_{\mathrm{ex}}^{\max}(T)=\left(\frac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)^2\int_0^\infty\frac{u\,du}{e^u-1}
=\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(\frac{k_BT}{\hbar\omega}\right)^2.
$$
Any additional particles must occupy the <ground state>: this is <two-dimensional harmonic-trap Bose condensation>. The <critical temperature> and the <condensate fraction> are therefore
$$
\boxed{N\omega^2=\frac{\pi^2}{6}\left(\frac{k_BT_c}{\hbar}\right)^2,\qquad
k_BT_c=\frac{\hbar\omega\sqrt{6N}}\pi,\qquad
N_0(T)=\begin{cases}N[1-(T/T_c)^2],&T\leq T_c,\\0,&T>T_c.\end{cases}}
$$
Here $N$ is the total <particle number>, the integral of the inhomogeneous <number density> over the trap; there is no fixed uniform area in which $N$ itself is a <number density>. Equivalently $N\kappa=\pi^2m(k_BT_c)^2/(6\hbar^2)$ relates the trap population to the <critical temperature>. Harmonic confinement changes the low-<energy> <density of states> and permits this saturation in two spatial dimensions.

These formulas are leading large-$N$ results with $k_BT_c\gg\hbar\omega$. A sharp transition belongs to the trapped-gas <thermodynamic limit> $N\to\infty$, $\omega\to0$ at fixed $N\omega^2$; a finite trap has <finite-size rounding>. In particular, finite $N_0$ requires $\widetilde\mu=-k_BT\log(1+1/N_0)<0$, approaching zero in the condensation limit.

Write $G(r)=\langle\psi^\dagger(0)\psi(\mathbf r)\rangle$, $r=|\mathbf r|$, and $\ell^2=\hbar/(m\omega)$. At zero <temperature> all $N$ particles occupy $\phi_0(\mathbf r)=(\ell\sqrt\pi)^{-1}e^{-r^2/(2\ell^2)}$. Expanding the <bosonic annihilation operator> in single-particle <orbitals> gives
$$
\boxed{G(r)=N\phi_0^*(0)\phi_0(\mathbf r)=\frac{N}{\pi\ell^2}\exp\!\left(-\frac{r^2}{2\ell^2}\right)\quad(T=0).}
$$
This <one-body density matrix> is a <Gaussian function> of width $\ell$; it contains one <wavefunction> evaluated at $r$, so its exponent is half that of the spatial <number density> $N|\phi_0(r)|^2$. In particular,
$$
\boxed{G(0)=\langle\psi^\dagger(0)\psi(0)\rangle=n(0),}
$$
the total <number density> at the trap centre, including condensed and noncondensed particles.

At positive <temperature>, with occupations $\overline n_a$ of the trap <energy eigenstates>, the exact ideal-gas decomposition is
$$
G(r)=\frac{N_0}{\pi\ell^2}e^{-r^2/(2\ell^2)}+\sum_{a\ne0}\overline n_a\phi_a^*(0)\phi_a(\mathbf r).
$$
For $\hbar\omega\ll k_BT<k_BT_c$, the sketch has a broad <Bose-Einstein condensate> component on scale $\ell$ and a narrower thermal peak near the origin. On approaching $T_c$ the broad component loses its weight $N_0$, while the thermal part remains. Above $T_c$ there is no macroscopic broad <Bose-Einstein condensate> component; close to $T_c$, low <energy levels> can still give a shoulder, so a single narrow <Gaussian function> is only a high-<temperature> description. At $k_BT\lesssim\hbar\omega$ individual excited <energy levels> matter and the two-scale description need not apply.

For $T\gg T_c$ the <Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution> applies. The leading off-diagonal <one-body density matrix> is
$$
G(r)\simeq\frac{N\kappa}{2\pi k_BT}\exp\!\left(-\frac{mk_BT}{2\hbar^2}r^2\right),\qquad
\boxed{\sigma_r=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{mk_BT}}=\frac{\lambda_T}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\quad
\lambda_T=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi\hbar^2}{mk_BT}}.}
$$
Here $\sigma_r$ is the <Gaussian function> width defined by $e^{-r^2/(2\sigma_r^2)}$, and $\lambda_T$ is the <thermal de Broglie wavelength>. The thermal cloud itself extends over the much larger spatial scale $\sqrt{k_BT/\kappa}$; this is a <correlation> width, not the cloud radius.

For a normalized <orbital> $\int d^2r\,|\phi|^2=1$, the symmetric <Hartree product state> and its equivalent <Fock-space> form are
$$
\boxed{\Phi(\mathbf r_1,\ldots,\mathbf r_N)=\prod_{j=1}^N\phi(\mathbf r_j),\qquad
|N:\phi\rangle=\frac{(a_\phi^\dagger)^N}{\sqrt{N!}}|0\rangle,\quad
a_\phi^\dagger=\int d^2r\,\phi(\mathbf r)\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r).}
$$
There are exactly $N(N-1)/2$ interacting pairs, so the <expectation value> of the grand <Hamiltonian> is the <functional>
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_\mu[\phi]=N\int d^2r\left[\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}|\nabla\phi|^2+\frac\kappa2r^2|\phi|^2\right]
+\frac{gN(N-1)}2\int d^2r\,|\phi|^4-\mu N.}
$$
At fixed $N$ the term $-\mu N$ is constant. Enforce <wavefunction normalization> separately by varying $\mathcal E_\mu-N\eta(\int|\phi|^2-1)$ with respect to $\phi^*$. The resulting stationary <Gross–Pitaevskii equation> for the normalized <orbital> is
$$
\boxed{\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+\frac\kappa2r^2+g(N-1)|\phi|^2\right]\phi=\eta\phi.}
$$
The <Lagrange multiplier> $\eta$ enforces the norm; fixed-$N$ variation cannot identify it with the independently inserted <chemical potential> merely by differentiating the constant $-\mu N$.

For the standard large-$N$ convention put $\Psi=\sqrt N\phi$, so $n=|\Psi|^2$ and $\int n=N$, and replace $N-1$ by $N$. Varying the resulting grand <energy> <functional>
$$
\int d^2r\left[\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}|\nabla\Psi|^2+\left(\frac\kappa2r^2-\mu\right)|\Psi|^2+\frac g2|\Psi|^4\right]
$$
with unrestricted $\Psi$ gives
$$
\boxed{\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2+\frac\kappa2r^2+g|\Psi|^2\right]\Psi=\mu\Psi.}
$$
Equivalently, at fixed $N$ this same $\mu$ is chosen as the normalization <Lagrange multiplier>. This is the <chemical potential> convention used below. The <contact interaction> coupling $g$ has dimensions of <energy> times area in two dimensions.

In the large-$N$ <Gross–Pitaevskii equation>, dropping the <kinetic energy> gives $[\kappa r^2/2+gn(r)-\mu]\Psi(r)=0$. The physical nonnegative <Thomas–Fermi approximation for a condensate> is therefore
$$
\boxed{n(r)=\frac1g\left(\mu-\frac\kappa2r^2\right)_+,\qquad R=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}\kappa},}
$$
where $(z)_+=\max(z,0)$ and $R$ is the cloud radius. Its <particle number> is
$$
N=2\pi\int_0^Rr\,n(r)\,dr=\frac{2\pi}{g}\left(\frac{\mu R^2}2-\frac{\kappa R^4}8\right)
=\frac{\pi\mu^2}{g\kappa}.
$$
Thus the <two-dimensional Thomas–Fermi condensate> obeys
$$
\boxed{\mu=\sqrt{\frac{Ng\kappa}\pi},\qquad R=\left(\frac{4Ng}{\pi\kappa}\right)^{1/4},\qquad n(0)=\frac\mu g.}
$$
Here $N=\int n\,d^2r$ is a total <particle number>. Keeping the exact pair count in the fixed-$N$ <Hartree product state> would replace $g$ by $g(1-1/N)$ in these formulas expressed in terms of $n=N|\phi|^2$.

The approximation is **best at the centre, $r=0$, and worst near the edge, $r=R$**. For example its omitted <quantum pressure of a condensate>, evaluated on the interior profile, is
$$
Q(r)=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt n}
=\frac{\hbar^2}{2mR^2}\frac{2-r^2/R^2}{(1-r^2/R^2)^2}.
$$
It is smallest at the centre and diverges at the artificial sharp edge. The central <healing length> $\xi_0=\hbar/\sqrt{2m\mu}$ is much smaller than $R$ when $\mu\gg\hbar\omega$, since $R/\xi_0=2\mu/(\hbar\omega)$. This is the bulk validity condition; the full <Gross–Pitaevskii equation> smooths the edge and gives a decaying tail outside $R$.

The <Hartree product state> underlying the <Gross–Pitaevskii equation> has the factorized <one-body coherence of a Hartree condensate>. Choosing its nodeless <ground-state> <orbital> real gives
$$
\boxed{G_{\mathrm{GP}}(r)=N\phi^*(0)\phi(r)=\sqrt{n(0)n(r)}.}
$$
In particular, the <Thomas–Fermi approximation for a condensate> from part (d) predicts
$$
\boxed{G_{\mathrm{TF}}(r)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac\mu g\sqrt{1-r^2/R^2},&0\leq r<R,\\0,&r\geq R.\end{cases}}
$$
The curve starts at $\mu/g$, decreases with zero slope at the origin, and reaches zero with an infinite slope at $R$. Restoring the <kinetic energy> in the full <Gross–Pitaevskii equation> rounds that edge and produces a nonzero tail. This corrects the <Thomas–Fermi approximation for a condensate>, but the resulting <one-body density matrix> still factorizes.

**The additional missing physics is quantum depletion.** Interactions scatter pairs of <bosons> into <orbitals> outside the <Bose-Einstein condensate> even at zero <temperature>. In terms of the true <Bose-Einstein condensate> <orbital> $\varphi_0$ the exact <one-body density matrix> decomposes as
$$
G(r)=N_0\varphi_0^*(0)\varphi_0(r)+\rho_{\mathrm{ex}}(0,\mathbf r),\qquad N_0<N,
\qquad
G(0)=N_0|\varphi_0(0)|^2+n_{\mathrm{ex}}(0).
$$
The single-<orbital> <Hartree product state> sets $N_0=N$ and omits $\rho_{\mathrm{ex}}$ altogether. In a <weakly interacting Bose gas>, <Bogoliubov theory> adds a comparatively short-range noncondensed contribution near the origin, together with reduced weight in the broad <Bose-Einstein condensate> component. The central value remains the total <number density>, not just the <condensate density>. Its numerical shift relative to the original <Gross–Pitaevskii equation> solution also depends on the interaction-induced redistribution of <number density>; it is not fixed by the <condensate fraction> alone.
