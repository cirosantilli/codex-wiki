Write $h_0=-\hbar^2\nabla^2/(2m)+V(\mathbf r)$ for the one-particle <Hamiltonian operator>. The <second quantization> of the <kinetic energy>, external <potential energy> and repulsive <contact interaction> is
$$
\boxed{\hat H=\int d^3r\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)h_0\hat\psi(\mathbf r)
+\frac{U_0}{2}\int d^3r\,\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)^2\hat\psi(\mathbf r)^2.}
$$
The factor $1/2$ counts each unordered particle pair once. <Normal ordering> excludes self-interaction: the interaction acts on two distinct <bosons>. The fixed <particle number> sector has $\hat N=\int d^3r\,\hat\psi^\dagger\hat\psi=N$. In three dimensions, this <contact interaction> is understood as a regulated low-<energy> interaction with a matched coupling, as in <renormalization of a three-dimensional dilute-gas contact coupling>; the finite-mode calculation below uses its projected matrix elements.

The <quantized field> expansion in the complete <orthonormal basis> of <orbitals> is
$$
\boxed{\hat\psi(\mathbf r)=\sum_n\phi_n(\mathbf r)\hat a_n,\qquad
\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)=\sum_n\phi_n^*(\mathbf r)\hat a_n^\dagger.}
$$
The complex conjugation in the <creation operator> expansion is essential for complex <orbitals>. Indeed, $\hat a_n^\dagger=\int d^3r\,\phi_n(\mathbf r)\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)$ creates the <wavefunction> $\phi_n$. The <resolution of the identity in an orthonormal basis> gives
$$
[\hat\psi(\mathbf r),\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r')]
=\sum_n\phi_n(\mathbf r)\phi_n^*(\mathbf r')
=\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r-\mathbf r'),
$$
recovering the <canonical commutation relation> and the spatial <Dirac delta function>.

The <two-mode approximation for a Bose gas> retains the real localized <orbitals>:
$$
\boxed{\hat\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)\simeq\varphi_L(\mathbf r)\hat a_L^\dagger+\varphi_R(\mathbf r)\hat a_R^\dagger.}
$$
It is valid when the lowest doublet is well separated from higher <energy levels>, and the <temperature> and interaction-induced changes of the <orbitals> are small on that separation. More explicitly, if $\Delta$ is the <energy gap> to discarded <orbitals>, sufficient low-<energy> conditions are $k_BT,t,NU_0I\ll\Delta$, where $I=\int d^3r\,|\varphi_L|^4$. A deep <double-well potential> suppresses interwell interaction overlaps. Large $N$ alone does not justify the truncation: increasing $N$ at fixed coupling can violate the interaction condition.

Let $\epsilon=(E_0+E_1)/2$ and use the even and odd <eigenfunctions> to evaluate the projected one-particle <Hamiltonian operator>:
$$
\langle L|h_0|L\rangle=\langle R|h_0|R\rangle=\epsilon,\qquad
\langle L|h_0|R\rangle=\frac{E_0-E_1}{2}=-t.
$$
Thus
$$
\hat H_0=\epsilon(\hat n_L+\hat n_R)-t(\hat a_L^\dagger\hat a_R+\hat a_R^\dagger\hat a_L),\qquad
\boxed{t=\frac{E_1-E_0}{2}>0.}
$$
The same <quantum tunnelling> matrix element is $t=-\int d^3r\,\varphi_Lh_0\varphi_R$. Reflection symmetry makes $\int|\varphi_L|^4=\int|\varphi_R|^4=I$. Neglecting mixed <contact interaction> integrals leaves
$$
\hat H_{\rm int}=\frac{U_0I}{2}\sum_{j=L,R}(\hat a_j^\dagger)^2\hat a_j^2
=\frac{U_0I}{2}\sum_{j=L,R}\hat n_j(\hat n_j-1).
$$
The last step is the <normal-ordered boson pair count>, following from the <canonical commutation relation>. Consequently the <two-site Bose-Hubbard model> has precisely the requested coefficients
$$
\boxed{w=\frac{U_0}{2}\int d^3r\,|\varphi_L(\mathbf r)|^4,\qquad
\mu=w-\frac{E_0+E_1}{2},}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\hat H=-t(\hat a_L^\dagger\hat a_R+\hat a_R^\dagger\hat a_L)
+w(\hat n_L^2+\hat n_R^2)-\mu(\hat n_L+\hat n_R).}
$$
Here $\mu$ is the coefficient obtained by rewriting the fixed-<particle number> <Hamiltonian operator>, rather than an independently prescribed thermodynamic <chemical potential>. Adding a reservoir term $-\mu_{\rm res}\hat N$ would replace it by $\mu+\mu_{\rm res}$.

The negligible-overlap instruction applies to the mixed <contact interaction> integrals while the small <quantum tunnelling> matrix element is retained. Exactly disjoint smooth <orbitals> for a local $h_0$ would also give $t=0$; setting every overlap-related matrix element identically to zero would discard the physical splitting one is trying to describe.

The normalized <Hartree product state> and its equivalent <second quantization> form are
$$
\boxed{\Psi_{\theta,\chi}(\mathbf r_1,\ldots,\mathbf r_N)
=\prod_{j=1}^N\varphi_{(\theta,\chi)}(\mathbf r_j),\qquad
|\theta,\chi;N\rangle=\frac{(\cos\theta\,\hat a_L^\dagger+e^{i\chi}\sin\theta\,\hat a_R^\dagger)^N}{\sqrt{N!}}|0\rangle.}
$$
The product is already symmetric under exchanging <bosons>; no additional $1/\sqrt{N!}$ belongs in the coordinate <wavefunction>. The factor in the <second quantization> expression instead normalizes repeated <creation operators> acting on the <vacuum state>. This is a <fixed-number two-mode condensate>, with every <boson> in the same normalized <orbital>.

Set $c=\cos\theta$ and $s=\sin\theta$. Its <binomial distribution> of occupations gives
$$
\langle\hat n_L\rangle=Nc^2,\quad\langle\hat n_R\rangle=Ns^2,\quad
\langle\hat a_L^\dagger\hat a_R\rangle=Ncs\,e^{i\chi},\qquad
\langle\hat n_L^2+\hat n_R^2\rangle=N+N(N-1)(c^4+s^4).
$$
The <expectation value> of the <Hamiltonian operator> is therefore
$$
\boxed{E(\theta,\chi)=-tN\sin(2\theta)\cos\chi
+w\left[N+N(N-1)\left(1-\frac12\sin^2(2\theta)\right)\right]-\mu N.}
$$
Keeping $N(N-1)$ rather than replacing it by $N^2$ retains the exact pair count of the trial <quantum state>. Equivalently, with $\epsilon=w-\mu$, its interaction contribution is $wN(N-1)(c^4+s^4)$ and its one-particle contribution is $\epsilon N-tN\sin(2\theta)\cos\chi$.

Choose the nonredundant parameter range $0\leq\theta\leq\pi/2$, with $\chi$ modulo $2\pi$. For $t>0$, minimizing over the relative <phase> gives $\chi=0$. Putting $x=\sin(2\theta)\in[0,1]$, the remaining nonconstant <energy> is $-tNx-\tfrac12wN(N-1)x^2$, which decreases with $x$ for the repulsive coupling $w>0$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\theta=\frac\pi4,\qquad\chi=0\pmod{2\pi},\qquad
E_{\min}=-tN+\frac w2N(N+1)-\mu N.}
$$
The optimal <orbital> is the symmetric $\phi_0=(\varphi_L+\varphi_R)/\sqrt2$. Both the <quantum tunnelling> term and the repulsive interaction favor this balanced occupation within the <Hartree product state> family. This result uses a <variational method>: for strong repulsion relative to <quantum tunnelling>, the true many-body <ground state> can have smaller occupation fluctuations than this <fixed-number two-mode condensate>.

**The printed identity has the wrong sign for the specified relative phase.** Differentiating the <energy> found above and evaluating the <operator commutator> give
$$
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\chi}=tN\sin(2\theta)\sin\chi,\qquad
[\hat H,\hat n_L]=t(\hat a_L^\dagger\hat a_R-\hat a_R^\dagger\hat a_L),\qquad
i\langle[\hat H,\hat n_L]\rangle=-tN\sin(2\theta)\sin\chi.
$$
Thus the <phase derivative of a two-mode condensate energy> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{\partial E}{\partial\chi}=-i\langle[\hat H,\hat n_L]\rangle
=i\langle[\hat H,\hat n_R]\rangle.}
$$
A concrete counterexample to the printed sign is $N=100$, $\theta=\pi/4$, $\chi=\pi/2$ and $t>0$: its left side is $100t$, whereas its printed right side is $-100t$, independently of $w$ and $\mu$.

There is also a direct derivation without inserting the <Hamiltonian operator>. Since $|\theta,\chi;N\rangle=e^{i\chi\hat n_R}|\theta,0;N\rangle$, differentiating this <unitary operator> gives $\partial_\chi E=i\langle[\hat H,\hat n_R]\rangle$. Conservation of the total <particle number>, $[\hat H,\hat n_L+\hat n_R]=0$, then supplies the minus sign for the left well.

By the <Heisenberg equation of motion>, $\hbar\,d\langle\hat n_L\rangle/dt=i\langle[\hat H,\hat n_L]\rangle$. Define the <bosonic Josephson junction> <particle current> from left to right as $J_{L\to R}=-d\langle\hat n_L\rangle/dt$. The corrected identity means
$$
\boxed{J_{L\to R}=\frac1\hbar\frac{\partial E}{\partial\chi}
=\frac{tN}{\hbar}\sin(2\theta)\sin\chi.}
$$
The <energy> slope with respect to the relative <phase> drives particle transfer; an extremum with respect to that <phase> has zero instantaneous <particle current>. The printed sign would work with $e^{-i\chi}$ in the right-well <orbital>, or with $[\hat n_L,\hat H]$ in place of $[\hat H,\hat n_L]$.

For a uniform continuum gas with pair interactions depending on relative positions, a <Galilean boost> changes every particle <momentum> by $p_0\hat{\mathbf x}$ and leaves the interaction <energy> unchanged. Write $\hat P_x=\sum_j\hat p_{j,x}$. The transformed many-body <Hamiltonian operator> is
$$
\hat H'=\sum_{j=1}^N\frac{(\hat{\mathbf p}_j+p_0\hat{\mathbf x})^2}{2m}+\hat U
=\hat H+\frac{p_0}{m}\hat P_x+\frac{Np_0^2}{2m}.
$$
The resting <ground state> has $\langle\hat P_x\rangle=0$, so the <Galilean boost energy of an interacting gas> is
$$
\boxed{\Delta E=\frac{Np_0^2}{2m}.}
$$
The <mass> here is the bare particle <mass>, because this is an exact transformation of the microscopic <kinetic energy>. Substituting the <Fermi-liquid effective mass> $m^*$ would omit the change in interaction <energy> encoded by the <Landau quasiparticle energy functional>. This conclusion requires the <principle of Galilean relativity>; a gas in a lattice or a fixed external <potential energy> landscape need not obey it.

Let $A$ be the real-space area and use $\sum_{\mathbf p}\to C\int d^2p$, with $C=A/(2\pi\hbar)^2$. There is one state per <momentum> cell, since the <fermions> are spinless. An annulus between $p$ and $p+dp$ contains $C\,2\pi p\,dp$ states. Near the <Fermi surface>, the <quasiparticle> <dispersion relation> has $d\epsilon/dp=v_F^*=p_F/m^*$, so the total <density of states> is
$$
\boxed{D^*(\mu)=C\frac{2\pi p_F}{v_F^*}=\frac{Am^*}{2\pi\hbar^2}.}
$$
The <density of states> per unit area and the occupied-state count are, respectively,
$$
\boxed{g^*(\mu)=\frac{D^*(\mu)}A=\frac{m^*}{2\pi\hbar^2},\qquad
N=C\pi p_F^2=\frac{Ap_F^2}{4\pi\hbar^2}.}
$$
In particular, $D^*(\mu)=2Nm^*/p_F^2$. No factor of two for <spin degeneracy> is present. This counts <Landau quasiparticle> levels, so it is not multiplied by the <quasiparticle residue> of the microscopic particle <Green's function>.

The shifted <Fermi circle> satisfies $|\mathbf p-p_0\hat{\mathbf x}|=p_F$. Its radial boundary at polar angle $\theta$ is the positive solution of $p^2-2pp_0\cos\theta+p_0^2=p_F^2$. Thus, for $p_0<p_F$,
$$
p_F+\nu(\theta)=p_0\cos\theta+\sqrt{p_F^2-p_0^2\sin^2\theta},
$$
and its leading <Taylor expansion> is
$$
\boxed{\nu(\theta)=p_0\cos\theta+O(p_0^2/p_F).}
$$
Forward-pointing <momenta> gain occupied states and backward-pointing <momenta> lose them. With the paper's explicitly reversed <Heaviside step function> convention, $\Theta'(x)=-\delta(x)$; consequently
$$
\delta n(\mathbf p)=\nu(\theta_{\mathbf p})\delta(p-p_F)+O(p_0^2),
$$
with a positive leading sign.

For the later quadratic <energy> calculation it is useful to distinguish the first-order shape from exact <particle number> conservation. The <particle-conserving displacement of a Fermi circle> has
$$
\nu(\theta)=p_0\cos\theta-\frac{p_0^2}{2p_F}\sin^2\theta+O(p_0^4/p_F^3),\qquad
\delta N=C\int_0^{2\pi}\left[p_F\nu+\frac{\nu^2}{2}\right]d\theta=0.
$$
The second-order inward correction cancels the apparent area increase one would obtain by treating $\nu=p_0\cos\theta$ as exact.

Within a stable normal <Fermi liquid>, the low-<energy> states are described by long-lived <Landau quasiparticles> and <holes> in a thin shell about the <Fermi surface>; the <Pauli exclusion principle> suppresses their decay. Expanding the <energy> about the equilibrium occupations gives the one-<quasiparticle> <dispersion relation> at first order and the interaction between occupation changes at second order, namely the <Landau quasiparticle energy functional>. For a smooth interaction, radial deviations in that interaction may be neglected at leading order, leaving its angular dependence; higher occupation terms contribute higher powers of the small shell thickness. Collective occupation deformations are included in the same description. The sufficiency claim is conditional on the normal <Fermi liquid> regime, and on supplementing this static <energy functional> with dynamics when required: it is not a description of arbitrary low-<energy> phases such as a <superconductor> or <non-Fermi liquid>.

Use the total <density of states> $D^*=Am^*/(2\pi\hbar^2)$ and $C=A/(2\pi\hbar)^2$ from part (b). In the paper's double-sum convention, $f(\phi)$ has units of <energy> and scales as $A^{-1}$. Define its first angular moment by
$$
\overline f_1=\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,f(\phi)\cos\phi.
$$
This convention matters: the <Landau quasiparticle energy functional> written with an explicit $1/A$ instead uses the intensive kernel $\widetilde f=Af$.

At zero <temperature>, the displayed functional is the change in $E-\mu N$; a true <Galilean boost> preserves $N$, so $\Delta\mathcal F=\Delta E$. For a radial displacement $\nu(\theta)$, the first term in the <Landau quasiparticle energy functional> becomes
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm qp}
&=C\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_{p_F}^{p_F+\nu(\theta)}p\,dp\,\frac{p_F}{m^*}(p-p_F)\\
&=\frac{Cp_F^2}{2m^*}\int_0^{2\pi}\nu(\theta)^2\,d\theta+o(p_0^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
The oriented radial <integral> also works for negative $\nu$: removing occupied levels below the <chemical potential> costs positive <energy>. Substituting $\nu=p_0\cos\theta+O(p_0^2/p_F)$ and using $N=C\pi p_F^2$ gives
$$
\boxed{\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm qp}=\frac{Np_0^2}{2m^*}+o(p_0^2).}
$$
The exact second-order correction to the boundary is unnecessary here because the unperturbed excitation <energy> vanishes on the <Fermi surface>; it changes this term only beyond quadratic order. It must nevertheless be understood when identifying $\Delta\mathcal F$ with a fixed-<particle number> <energy> change.

For the quadratic interaction term, the first-order occupation change alone suffices. The radial <Dirac delta function> integrals give
$$
\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm int}=\frac{C^2p_F^2p_0^2}{2}
\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta\int_0^{2\pi}d\theta'\,
f(\theta-\theta')\cos\theta\cos\theta'+o(p_0^2).
$$
Put $\phi=\theta-\theta'$. The remaining angular <integral> uses
$$
\int_0^{2\pi}\cos(\theta'+\phi)\cos\theta'\,d\theta'=\pi\cos\phi,
$$
so only the first cosine component of the interaction survives. Since $D^*=2\pi Cm^*$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\mathcal F_{\rm int}
&=\frac{C^2p_F^2p_0^2\pi}{2}\int_0^{2\pi}f(\phi)\cos\phi\,d\phi+o(p_0^2)\\
&=\frac{Np_0^2}{2m^*}\,D^*\overline f_1+o(p_0^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Adding both contributions and matching the bare-<mass> result of part (a) yields the <two-dimensional Landau effective-mass relation>:
$$
\boxed{\Delta E=\frac{Np_0^2}{2m^*}\left(1+D^*\overline f_1\right)+o(p_0^2),\qquad
\frac{m^*}{m}=1+D^*\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,f(\phi)\cos\phi.}
$$
Equivalently, using the <density of states> per unit area $g^*=D^*/A$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{m^*}{m}=1+g^*\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}\,\widetilde f(\phi)\cos\phi.}
$$
If dimensionless <Landau parameters> are defined through the <Fourier series> $D^*f(\phi)=F_0+\sum_{\ell\geq1}F_\ell\cos(\ell\phi)$, then $D^*\overline f_1=F_1/2$ and
$$
\boxed{\frac{m^*}{m}=1+\frac{F_1}{2}.}
$$
Defining the cosine expansion with an extra factor of two would instead absorb that factor into $F_1$; the angular-<integral> formula above fixes the convention unambiguously. The physical content is that interaction changes accompanying a moving <Fermi sea> compensate the renormalized <quasiparticle> <velocity>, so the total boost <energy> is governed by the bare <mass>. For an interaction independent of angle, $\overline f_1=0$ and this consistency condition requires $m^*=m$.
