= Maria Skłodowska

A <physicist> and <chemist> whose investigation of <radioactivity> joined sensitive measurement to chemical separation. She moved from Warsaw to Paris to study, later becoming a professor of <physics>. The <Curie ionization-current measurement> shows the experimental craft behind the work: balancing a tiny <electric current> against a calibrated <electric charge>. She shared the 1903 <Nobel Prize in Physics> and received the 1911 prize in <chemistry>.<Nobel Lectures, Physics 1901–1921/Marie Curie - Biographical>[Nobel Lectures, Physics 1901–1921; Biographical identity and professional specialization]{ref}

= P. Curie
{c}

A <physicist> who investigated <magnetism>, <piezoelectricity> and <radioactivity>. He built delicate balances, <electrometers> and <piezoelectric crystals>; this work on instruments helped make weak electrical effects measurable. With <Marie Curie>, he investigated radioactive substances under difficult laboratory conditions. The <Curie ionization-current measurement> illustrates how such instrumentation can turn a small signal into a quantitative comparison.<Nobel Lectures, Physics 1901–1921/Pierre Curie - Biographical>[Nobel Lectures, Physics 1901–1921; Biographical identity and professional specialization]{ref}
