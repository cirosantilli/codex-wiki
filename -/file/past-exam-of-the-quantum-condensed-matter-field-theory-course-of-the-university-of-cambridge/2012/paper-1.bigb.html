For the <transverse-field Ising model>, use dimensionless <spin one-half> operators, so $S^x$ has eigenvalues $\pm1/2$. At zero transverse field every aligned bond contributes $-J$. The two <ground states> are
$$
\boxed{|X_\pm\rangle=\bigotimes_{n=1}^N|\!\pm x\rangle_n,\qquad E_0=-JN.}
$$
They span an exactly two-dimensional ground space on the finite periodic ferromagnetic chain. Any linear combination is also a ground state; parity eigenstates are the symmetric and antisymmetric combinations. Choosing one ordered product state in the thermodynamic limit is <spontaneous symmetry breaking>.

For large positive field the leading term is $-2Jg\sum_nS_n^z$. Its unique minimizing state is
$$
\boxed{|Z\rangle=\bigotimes_n|\!+z\rangle_n,\qquad E_0\sim-JgN\quad(g\to\infty).}
$$
The exchange has zero first-order expectation in this state and produces virtual spin-pair fluctuations at finite g. Thus the polarized product is the limiting wavefunction, not an exact finite-field ground state. Bulk perturbation theory gives $E_0/N=-Jg-J/(4g)+O(J/g^3)$. The field-polarized phase has no twofold ordered-state degeneracy.

Let $n_n=c_n^\dagger c_n$ and $P_n=\prod_{m<n}(1-2n_m)$. The <Jordan–Wigner transformation> gives $2S_n^x=P_n(c_n+c_n^\dagger)$ and $P_{n+1}=P_n(1-2n_n)$. The <canonical anticommutation relations> imply
$$
(c_n+c_n^\dagger)(1-2n_n)=c_n^\dagger-c_n.
$$
Consequently the <bondwise cancellation of a Jordan-Wigner string> yields
$$
4S_n^xS_{n+1}^x=(c_n^\dagger-c_n)(c_{n+1}^\dagger+c_{n+1}),\qquad
2S_n^z=1-2n_n.
$$
Substitution therefore gives
$$
\boxed{H=-J\sum_n\left[(c_n^\dagger-c_n)(c_{n+1}^\dagger+c_{n+1})+g(1-2n_n)\right].}
$$
The <finite-ring parity qualification of an Ising quasiparticle gap> matters here. For an exact periodic spin chain, the closing bond retains total <fermion parity>. The <parity-dependent boundary condition of the Jordan-Wigner chain> is $c_{N+1}=-Pc_1$, with $P=(-1)^{N_f}$. Fixing that parity determines the allowed Fourier grid, and the resulting many-body states must still have the chosen parity. Dropping this boundary issue preserves the thermodynamic energy density, but can lose a degeneracy or the correct finite-ring excitation gap. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§5.1, spin-chain fermionization and boundary sectors]{ref}

With the specified <Fourier transform> convention, <normal ordering> gives
$$
\sum_n(c_n^\dagger c_{n+1}-c_nc_{n+1}^\dagger)=2\sum_k\cos k\,c_k^\dagger c_k,
$$
where the possible constant $\sum_ke^{ik}$ vanishes on the complete lattice momentum grid. Antisymmetry of opposite-momentum pairs similarly gives
$$
\sum_nc_n^\dagger c_{n+1}^\dagger=-i\sum_k\sin k\,c_k^\dagger c_{-k}^\dagger,\qquad
\sum_nc_nc_{n+1}=i\sum_k\sin k\,c_kc_{-k}.
$$
Hence
$$
H=-JgN+2J\sum_k(g-\cos k)c_k^\dagger c_k
+iJ\sum_k\sin k\,(c_k^\dagger c_{-k}^\dagger+c_kc_{-k}).
$$
Use the <Ising-chain Nambu spinor> $\Psi_k=(c_k,-ic_{-k}^\dagger)^T$. Expanding its two diagonal terms uses $c_{-k}c_{-k}^\dagger=1-c_{-k}^\dagger c_{-k}$, retaining the constant. The result is
$$
\boxed{H=J\sum_k\Psi_k^\dagger\left[(g-\cos k)\sigma_z-\sin k\,\sigma_x\right]\Psi_k.}
$$
This is a full-zone sum. Its opposite-momentum components are redundant; treating each matrix eigenvalue as an independent physical excitation would miss the factor of two in the quasiparticle energy. Self-paired momenta have zero anomalous term and are ordinary number levels.

Put $d_k=\sqrt{(g-\cos k)^2+\sin^2k}$. The matrix in the <Ising-chain Nambu spinor> has eigenvalues $\pm d_k$. A canonical <Bogoliubov transformation>, with paired angles satisfying $\cos\vartheta_k=(g-\cos k)/d_k$ and $\sin\vartheta_k=-\sin k/d_k$, diagonalizes it. Accounting for <full-zone fermionic Nambu energy counting> gives
$$
\boxed{H=\sum_k\epsilon_k\left(\gamma_k^\dagger\gamma_k-\frac12\right),\qquad
\epsilon_k=2Jd_k.}
$$
Thus the bulk <Ising-chain ground-state energy> is
$$
\boxed{E_0=-\frac12\sum_k\epsilon_k,\qquad
\frac{E_0}{N}\longrightarrow-\frac{J}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sqrt{1+g^2-2g\cos k}\,dk.}
$$
Exact finite-chain states additionally obey their <fermion parity> sector.

The <Ising-chain quasiparticle dispersion> has bulk gap $2J|1-g|$, closing at the <Ising-chain quantum critical point>. Finite-system parity splittings and the bulk gap are distinct scales. The three requested limits are illustrated below.

The <Hellmann–Feynman theorem> applied to the source field normalization gives
$$
\frac{\partial E_0}{\partial g}=\left\langle\frac{\partial H}{\partial g}\right\rangle
=-2J\sum_n\langle S_n^z\rangle.
$$
Differentiating the <Ising-chain ground-state energy>, using $\partial_g\epsilon_k=4J^2(g-\cos k)/\epsilon_k$, therefore proves
$$
\boxed{\sum_n\langle S_n^z\rangle=\sum_k\frac{J(g-\cos k)}{\epsilon_k}.}
$$
This is the <Ising-chain field derivative and transverse polarization>, not its spontaneous x-order parameter. It tends to zero at g=0 and to N/2 at large positive field. The <critical transverse polarization of an Ising chain> is finite: in the thermodynamic critical limit this gives $\langle S^z\rangle=1/\pi$. A zero-energy isolated momentum is treated by the appropriate limiting state, rather than by assigning a value to a literal 0/0 summand.

Use dimensionless <spin> operators and nearest-neighbour exchange $J_H>0$ on a regular <bipartite graph> with N sites and <coordination number of a lattice> z. Count each bond once and define $\gamma_{\mathbf k}=z^{-1}\sum_\delta e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\delta}$, real for the inversion-symmetric examples considered here.

For the ferromagnetic <Heisenberg model>, $H_F=-J_H\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j$. Expanding the <Holstein–Primakoff transformation> about all spins along +z gives
$$
S_i^z=S-a_i^\dagger a_i,\qquad S_i^+\simeq\sqrt{2S}\,a_i,\qquad
H_F=E_{\rm cl}+J_HS\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}(a_i^\dagger-a_j^\dagger)(a_i-a_j),
$$
up to interactions of higher order in boson density, with $E_{\rm cl}=-NzJ_HS^2/2$. The <isotropic spin-wave vacuum energy comparison> starts with this normal ordering. The <linear spin-wave approximation> then gives
$$
\boxed{E_F(\mathbf k)=zJ_HS(1-\gamma_{\mathbf k}),\qquad
H_F=E_{\rm cl}+\sum_{\mathbf k}E_F(\mathbf k)a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}.}
$$
Each <ferromagnetic magnon> lowers total $S^z$ by one. The fully aligned state is an exact eigenstate and ground state; there is no harmonic vacuum depletion or added zero-point energy in this normal-ordered Hamiltonian. The global spin-rotation multiplet remains degenerate. On a hypercubic lattice of spacing a, $z=2d$ and $\gamma_{\mathbf k}=d^{-1}\sum_j\cos k_ja$, so $E_F\simeq J_HSa^2k^2$. The exact one-magnon sector agrees with this result even at finite S; interactions enter for multiple magnons.

For the <Heisenberg antiferromagnet>, take $H_A=+J_H\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j$ and expand around a <Néel state>. On A, $S^z=S-a^\dagger a$ and $S^+\simeq\sqrt{2S}a$; on B, $S^z=-S+b^\dagger b$ and $S^+\simeq\sqrt{2S}b^\dagger$. A bond becomes
$$
\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j\simeq-S^2+S(a_i^\dagger a_i+b_j^\dagger b_j+a_ib_j+a_i^\dagger b_j^\dagger).
$$
With N/2 momenta in the magnetic <Brillouin zone>,
$$
H_A=E_{\rm cl}+zJ_HS\sum_{\mathbf k\in{\rm MBZ}}\left[a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}+b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}+\gamma_{\mathbf k}(a_{\mathbf k}b_{-\mathbf k}+a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger)\right].
$$
The bosonic <Bogoliubov transformation> has $u_k^2-v_k^2=1$ and $\tanh2\theta_k=\gamma_k$. For example $a_k=u_k\alpha_k-v_k\beta_{-k}^\dagger$ and $b_{-k}=u_k\beta_{-k}-v_k\alpha_k^\dagger$. It gives two branches in this reduced zone,
$$
\boxed{E_A(\mathbf k)=zJ_HS\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\mathbf k}^2},\quad
E_{A,0}=E_{\rm cl}+\sum_{{\rm MBZ}}[E_A(\mathbf k)-zJ_HS],\quad
v_k^2=\frac12\left[\frac1{\sqrt{1-\gamma_k^2}}-1\right].}
$$
The <reduced-zone counting of bipartite spin waves> gives N oscillators in total, not 2N. The negative vacuum correction and <quantum depletion of Néel order>, $\delta S=(2/N)\sum_{{\rm MBZ}}v_k^2$, distinguish the antiferromagnet from the ferromagnet. At nonzero temperature replace $v_k^2$ by $v_k^2+(u_k^2+v_k^2)n_B(E_k)$. Hypercubic small-momentum modes have $E_A\simeq2J_HSa\sqrt d\,|k|$, hence linear physical frequency $E_A/\hbar$.

Self-consistency requires depletion much smaller than S and respects the <Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint>. In one dimension the antiferromagnetic vacuum depletion diverges logarithmically, invalidating ordered spin-wave theory; it does not alone prove an exact gap or its absence. At positive temperature, isotropic short-range models in d≤2 cannot sustain such magnetic order by the <Mermin-Wagner theorem>. In three dimensions the quadratic ferromagnetic modes give the low-temperature magnetization reduction proportional to $T^{3/2}$, whereas the antiferromagnetic thermal reduction scales as $T^2$. Anisotropy, fields, frustration and magnon interactions alter these conclusions. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§4.1, contrasting ferromagnetic and antiferromagnetic low-energy dynamics]{ref}

Insert position resolutions between short-time evolution factors for $H=p^2/(2M)+V(q)$. Integrating the momenta gives the <path integral>, with real-time slice factor $[M/(2\pi i\hbar\Delta t)]^{1/2}$ and weight $e^{iS[q]/\hbar}$. A real classical trajectory cannot pass through a barrier with insufficient energy, but the path integral includes nonclassical paths.

Continue $t=-is$, with s physical <imaginary time>. The damped kernel is
$$
K_E(q_f,q_i;\mathcal T)=\langle q_f|e^{-H\mathcal T/\hbar}|q_i\rangle
=\int\mathcal Dq\,e^{-S_E[q]/\hbar},\qquad
S_E=\int_0^{\mathcal T}ds\left[\frac M2\dot q^2+V(q)\right].
$$
The <Euclidean action> saddle obeys $M\ddot q=V'(q)$: motion in the inverted potential. For degenerate minima at $\pm a$, subtract their common potential energy. A trajectory joining them with zero Euclidean energy satisfies
$$
\frac M2\dot q^2=V(q),\qquad
\boxed{S_I=\int_{-a}^{a}dq\,\sqrt{2MV(q)}.}
$$
It is an <instanton>; reversing its direction gives an <anti-instanton>. Thus a crossing amplitude is nonperturbative, proportional to $e^{-S_I/\hbar}$. For the representative quartic well $V=M\omega_0^2(q^2-a^2)^2/(8a^2)$, direct integration gives
$$
q_I(s)=a\tanh\frac{\omega_0(s-s_0)}2,\qquad S_I=\frac23M\omega_0a^2.
$$
The arbitrary center $s_0$ is an <instanton translation zero mode>. Expanding $q=q_I+\eta$ gives a <Gaussian path integral> involving $L_I=-\partial_s^2+V''(q_I)/M$. Treating the zero mode as a collective coordinate rather than a zero Gaussian denominator produces the <instanton fluctuation prefactor>
$$
K=\sqrt{\frac{S_I}{2\pi\hbar}}\left[\frac{\det L_0}{\det' L_I}\right]^{1/2},\qquad
L_0=-\partial_s^2+\omega_0^2,\qquad \kappa=K e^{-S_I/\hbar}.
$$
Common endpoint regularization defines the <functional determinant> ratio; K and kappa have inverse-time units. The prime removes only the zero eigenvalue, while integrating the center supplies the total available time. Large $S_I/\hbar$ and $\kappa\ll\omega_0$ justify a <dilute instanton gas>.

Multiple crossings alternate direction. Integrating n ordered centers gives $\mathcal T^n/n!$. Return paths contain even n, and transfer paths contain odd n, so their common local-well factor multiplies $\cosh(\kappa\mathcal T)$ and $\sinh(\kappa\mathcal T)$ respectively. The two lowest levels are consequently
$$
\boxed{E_\pm=E_{\rm well}\mp\hbar\kappa,\qquad
\Delta E=2\hbar K e^{-S_I/\hbar}.}
$$
The lower state is the even combination of the two localized states. Local anharmonic corrections modify $E_{\rm well}\simeq\hbar\omega_0/2$ and higher semiclassical corrections modify K. The <thermal trace as a closed Euclidean path integral> gives $Z\simeq2e^{-\beta E_{\rm well}}\cosh(\beta\hbar\kappa)$ when higher local levels are negligible. This describes coherent level splitting in a stable well, not irreversible decay.

For a scattering barrier at energy E, the leading <WKB tunnelling exponent> gives an amplitude $\exp[-\int_{q_1}^{q_2}\sqrt{2M(V-E)}\,dq/\hbar]$ and a transmission probability with twice that exponent. Endpoint matching fixes prefactors. Metastable escape instead uses a <Euclidean bounce>, with a negative fluctuation mode and a decay rate. Neither its negative mode nor the probability/amplitude factor should be transplanted into the stable double-well splitting. <Gauge Theory by David Tong>[Chapter2, §§2.3.2–2.3.3, printedpp.56–60: quantum-mechanical tunnelling, fluctuation determinants and repeated instanton events]{ref}

Use the reduced attractive <reduced BCS pairing Hamiltonian> with volume V, $\xi_k=\varepsilon_k-\mu$ and attraction $\lambda>0$:
$$
\mathcal H=\sum_{k\sigma}\xi_kc_{k\sigma}^\dagger c_{k\sigma}-\frac\lambda V B^\dagger B,\qquad
B=\sum_kc_{-k\downarrow}c_{k\uparrow}.
$$
A specified pairing shell or an ultraviolet renormalization is part of the model. The trace is grand canonical. Insert <fermionic coherent states> into its time-sliced trace. The resulting <Grassmann variables> are antiperiodic, and with inverse-energy <imaginary time> $0\le\tau\le\beta$ the dimensionless action is
$$
\mathcal S=\int_0^\beta d\tau\left[\sum_{k\sigma}\bar\psi_{k\sigma}(\partial_\tau+\xi_k)\psi_{k\sigma}-\frac\lambda V\bar P P\right],\qquad
P=\sum_k\psi_{-k\downarrow}\psi_{k\uparrow}.
$$
This boundary condition is essential for <Fermi-Dirac statistics>; replacing it by a bosonic periodic trace changes the answer. A complex <Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation> uses the local identity
$$
e^{(\lambda/V)\bar P P}\propto\int d\Delta\,d\Delta^*\,
e^{-V|\Delta|^2/\lambda+\Delta\bar P+\Delta^*P},
$$
with time-slice factors understood. The pairing field renders the fermion action quadratic. For a uniform static field the independent <Nambu spinor> block $(\psi_{k\uparrow},\bar\psi_{-k\downarrow})^T$ has
$$
\mathcal G^{-1}(k,i\omega_n)=
\begin{pmatrix}-i\omega_n+\xi_k&-\Delta\\-\Delta^*&-i\omega_n-\xi_k\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\omega_n=(2n+1)\pi/\beta,\qquad E_k=\sqrt{\xi_k^2+|\Delta|^2}.
$$
Its determinant is $-(\omega_n^2+E_k^2)$. The phase-independent determinant sign cancels under consistent reference normalization. Integrating the fermions gives the <BCS pairing-field effective action>, $\mathcal S_{\rm eff}=\int_0^\beta V|\Delta|^2/\lambda\,d\tau-\operatorname{Tr}\ln\mathcal G^{-1}$, with the normal-ordering/reference term retained. The <fermionic pairing-block determinant normalization> uses one independent pairing block for every k; a fully doubled spin-particle-hole trace instead needs one half. The <subtracted grand potential of the reduced BCS model> is particularly unambiguous:
$$
\Omega(\Delta)-\Omega(0)=\frac{V|\Delta|^2}{\lambda}
-\frac1\beta\sum_{kn}\ln\frac{\omega_n^2+E_k^2}{\omega_n^2+\xi_k^2}.
$$
Performing the <Matsubara frequency> sum yields
$$
\Omega(\Delta)=\frac{V|\Delta|^2}{\lambda}
+\sum_k(\xi_k-E_k)-\frac2\beta\sum_k\ln(1+e^{-\beta E_k}),
$$
up to a common constant. At zero gap this reduces exactly to the two-spin free-gas result. The <Matsubara derivation of the BCS saddle> follows by differentiating with respect to $\Delta^*$ and using $\beta^{-1}\sum_n(\omega_n^2+E^2)^{-1}=\tanh(\beta E/2)/(2E)$ gives
$$
\boxed{1=\frac\lambda V\sum_k\frac{\tanh(\beta E_k/2)}{2E_k},\qquad
N=\sum_k\left[1-\frac{\xi_k}{E_k}\tanh\frac{\beta E_k}2\right].}
$$
The second equation fixes the <chemical potential> at specified number. This stationary action is BCS <mean-field theory>, not an exact integration of pairing-field fluctuations. With constant single-spin <density of states> $\nu_F$ per volume and a symmetric energy cutoff $\Lambda$, weak coupling gives $\Delta(0)\simeq2\Lambda e^{-1/(\lambda\nu_F)}$ and $k_BT_c\simeq(2e^{\gamma_E}/\pi)\Lambda e^{-1/(\lambda\nu_F)}$. The zero-temperature condensation grand-potential density is $-\nu_F\Delta^2/2$ to leading weak-coupling order; using a spin-summed density would change these displayed factors. The <BCS coherence factors> are $u_k^2=(1+\xi_k/E_k)/2$ and $v_k^2=(1-\xi_k/E_k)/2$, and the associated <BCS ground state> is the normalized product of empty and occupied pair states. <Lecture Notes for Quantum Matter by Steven H. Simon>[Chapter11, BCS variational state, coherence factors, gap and thermal occupations]{ref}

Allowing a slowly varying complex pairing field generates phase stiffness and, near the transition, the <Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity>. A neutral paired system has a gapless phase sound mode; in a charged bulk superconductor electromagnetic coupling produces the <Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor> and the <Meissner effect>. These collective fluctuations are distinct from the gapped fermionic excitations. A microscopic dynamical calculation is needed to fix collective-mode dispersion and damping; the static saddle alone does not determine them. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§§3.1–3.2, coherent-state construction; §2.3, pair-field integration and charged phase dynamics]{ref}

For the untwisted <quantum rotor>, periodicity on the angle circle requires $\psi(\phi+2\pi)=\psi(\phi)$. The normalized <energy eigenstates> and their <angular momentum> are
$$
u_n(\phi)=\frac{e^{in\phi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}},\qquad
\ell_n=\hbar n,\qquad E_n=\frac{\hbar^2n^2}{2I},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$
Taking the <trace> in this orthonormal basis therefore gives
$$
\boxed{Z=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\exp\left[-\frac{\beta\hbar^2n^2}{2I}\right].}
$$
The n=0 state is nondegenerate and each nonzero $|n|$ has two opposite-angular-momentum states. No flux or twisted angular boundary condition is present.

First lift the angle to the real line. The <time-sliced configuration-space path integral> for a free coordinate with inertia I has kinetic action $\int dt\,I\dot\phi^2/2$. The rotor kernel sums lifted endpoints separated by $\phi_F-\phi_I+2\pi m$, because all such endpoints represent the same final angle on the circle.

Continue real time as $t=-i\hbar\tau$. Then $e^{-iHt/\hbar}=e^{-H\tau}$ and the end of the thermal interval is $\tau=\beta$, in inverse-energy units. Thus
$$
\frac{S_E}{\hbar}=\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}\int_0^\beta d\tau\,\dot\phi^2.
$$
The <trace> integrates the initial angle over one copy of the circle, not the whole lifted line. It yields the <rotor winding-sector path integral>
$$
\boxed{Z=\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\int_{\phi(0)=\phi}^{\phi(\beta)=\phi+2\pi m}\mathcal D\phi\,
\exp\left[-\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}\int_0^\beta d\tau\,\dot\phi^2\right].}
$$
The integer m is temporal <winding number>, not the angular-momentum label n. Locally the measure has the free-kernel normalization $[I/(2\pi\hbar^2\Delta\tau)]^{1/2}$ per time slice. Summing topological sectors and retaining this normalization are necessary for the correct thermal trace.

In winding sector m write the lifted path as
$$
\phi(\tau)=\phi_0+\frac{2\pi m\tau}{\beta}+\eta(\tau),\qquad \eta(0)=\eta(\beta)=0.
$$
The cross term vanishes since $\int_0^\beta\dot\eta\,d\tau=0$. The action separates into $I(2\pi m)^2/(2\hbar^2\beta)$ and the same fixed-endpoint <Gaussian path integral> for every m. Neither term depends on the initial angle, so its integral supplies $2\pi$:
$$
\boxed{Z=2\pi Z_0\sum_m e^{-I(2\pi m)^2/(2\hbar^2\beta)},\qquad
Z_0=\int_{\eta(0)=\eta(\beta)=0}\mathcal D\eta\,
e^{-I\int_0^\beta\dot\eta^2d\tau/(2\hbar^2)}.}
$$
The <fixed-endpoint rotor fluctuation normalization> is
$$
\boxed{Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{I}{2\pi\hbar^2\beta}}.}
$$
It is the diagonal free-particle thermal kernel on the lifted line, or the amplitude density for a zero-displacement fluctuation bridge. It is not by itself the circle partition function or a separately countable angular zero mode. The integration over $\phi_0$ has already counted that mode. Quadraticity makes the saddle-plus-fluctuation evaluation exact.

Analytically continue the supplied free propagator using $M=I$ and $t=-i\hbar\beta$. This gives $Z_0=\sqrt{I/(2\pi\hbar^2\beta)}$ without an arbitrary measure constant.

Apply the <Poisson summation formula> to $h(x)=e^{-Ax^2}$, with $A=2\pi^2I/(\hbar^2\beta)$. Its <Gaussian integral> is
$$
\int_{-\infty}^\infty dx\,e^{-Ax^2+2\pi inx}=\sqrt{\frac\pi A}\,e^{-\pi^2n^2/A}.
$$
The prefactors cancel exactly because $2\pi Z_0\sqrt{\pi/A}=1$. Therefore the <momentum-winding duality of the quantum rotor> gives
$$
\boxed{2\pi Z_0\sum_me^{-Am^2}=\sum_ne^{-\beta\hbar^2n^2/(2I)},}
$$
in agreement with the spectrum. The <thermal winding dominance of a free rotor> interchanges between the two representations. At low temperature the momentum sum is dominated by n=0 and $Z\to1$. At high temperature the winding sum is dominated by m=0 and $Z\sim\sqrt{2\pi I/(\beta\hbar^2)}$, the classical angular phase-space integral. Winding and momentum labels are dual descriptions; keeping only m=0 at low temperature would be incorrect.

The <coupled quantum rotor chain> has a compact phase and conjugate integer number $n_j=\ell_j/\hbar$. Expanding the cosine gives
$$
H\simeq-NJ+\sum_j\left[\frac{\ell_j^2}{2I}+\frac J2(\phi_{j+1}-\phi_j)^2\right].
$$
In the phase-space <Euclidean path integral>, using inverse-energy time, each rotor has the action term $\ell_j^2/(2I)-i\ell_j\dot\phi_j/\hbar$. Completing its Gaussian square produces $I\dot\phi_j^2/(2\hbar^2)$. Equivalently, apply the short-time free-rotor kernel locally and retain winding sectors. The smooth-sector continuum limit at unit spacing is consequently
$$
\boxed{Z\simeq e^{\beta NJ}\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{-\mathcal S},\qquad
\mathcal S=\int_0^\beta d\tau\int dx\left[\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}(\partial_\tau\phi)^2+\frac J2(\partial_x\phi)^2\right].}
$$
The overall constant is omitted when the reference bond energy is subtracted. **With the same physical J as the Hamiltonian, the printed spatial coefficient has an extra factor $1/\hbar^2$.** The <imaginary-time normalization for a coupled rotor chain> requires $\hbar^2J/2$ inside a bracket with common prefactor $1/\hbar^2$. Setting $\hbar=1$ hides the discrepancy; it cannot establish the printed formula with explicit physical units.

Returning to physical time gives $I\partial_t^2\phi=J\partial_x^2\phi$. The <harmonic phase mode of a quantum rotor chain> therefore has
$$
\boxed{\omega(k)=2\sqrt{J/I}\,|\sin(k/2)|\simeq c|k|,\qquad
c=\sqrt{J/I},\qquad E_k=\hbar\omega(k).}
$$
This is a gapless sound-like collective phase mode, analogous to neutral <superfluid> sound. In <rotor-chain phase-number hydrodynamics>, number fluctuations supply the compressibility and the intersite coupling supplies <phase stiffness>. The mode is a phase-density oscillation, not a free single-rotor transition. The <global phase zero mode of a finite rotor chain> is a compact rotor with inertia NI, which should not be counted as an independent zero-frequency oscillator.

The <compactness qualification of a harmonic rotor chain> matters here. The smooth harmonic theory requires small neighbouring phase differences and cannot settle the entire strong-fluctuation phase diagram. In one dimension its zero-temperature phase variance grows logarithmically, so the gapless regime has algebraic phase correlations rather than a true condensate. Thermal fluctuations and compact <phase slips> require additional care; phase-slip proliferation can produce a gapped phase outside the Gaussian regime. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§5.2, rotor phase/number variables and limits of smooth superfluid theory]{ref}
