Use a real, even interaction $V_{-\mathbf k}=V_{\mathbf k}$. Let $\mathcal V=L^3$ and $N_{\rm ex}=\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}$. In the <Bogoliubov theory> expansion, keep terms with zero or two noncondensed operators. The zero-<momentum> term gives $V_0N_0(N_0-1)/(2\mathcal V)$. The terms with two noncondensed operators give
$$
\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left[\frac{N_0}{\mathcal V}(V_0+V_{\mathbf k})b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}+\frac{N_0V_{\mathbf k}}{2\mathcal V}\left(b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b^\dagger_{-\mathbf k}+b_{-\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}\right)\right].
$$
Here $V_0$ is the direct condensate interaction and $V_{\mathbf k}$ is its exchange contribution. At fixed total <particle number>, $N_0=N-N_{\rm ex}$, so
$$
\frac{V_0N_0^2}{2\mathcal V}=\frac{V_0N^2}{2\mathcal V}-nV_0N_{\rm ex}+O(N_{\rm ex}^2/\mathcal V).
$$
The second term cancels the direct normal term. Adding the <kinetic energy> yields the <number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian>
$$
\boxed{H_2=E_{\rm mf}+\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left[A_k b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}+\frac{B_k}{2}\left(b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b^\dagger_{-\mathbf k}+b_{-\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}\right)\right],\quad
E_{\rm mf}=\frac{\mathcal V V_0n^2}{2},\quad A_k=\epsilon_k+nV_k,\quad B_k=nV_k.}
$$
The constant is the condensate <mean-field theory> interaction <energy>; the normal term describes moving a particle out of the condensate, while the anomalous term creates or annihilates an opposite-<momentum> pair by exchanging two particles with the condensate. Total <particle number> remains fixed in the underlying theory; the condensate amplitudes in this notation are treated as classical amplitudes.

For <condensate fluctuation power counting>, put $\delta=N_{\rm ex}/N\ll1$. Formal interaction-amplitude counting gives $H_2-E_{\rm mf}\sim E_{\rm mf}\delta$, cubic terms $\sim E_{\rm mf}\delta^{3/2}$ and quartic terms $\sim E_{\rm mf}\delta^2$; these are not uniform bounds on individual modes. Thus the latter are down by $\sqrt\delta$ and $\delta$ relative to the quadratic interaction terms. The $N_0-1$ correction is relative $1/N$. Homogeneity eliminates terms linear in nonzero-<momentum> operators. Small depletion and weak interactions, not low <temperature> alone, justify the truncation. <Lecture Notes for Quantum Matter by Steven H. Simon>[§4.5, Bogoliubov expansion; fixed-number cancellation derived above]{ref}

For each opposite-<momentum> pair use the <Bogoliubov transformation>
$$
b_{\mathbf k}=u_k\alpha_{\mathbf k}-v_k\alpha^\dagger_{-\mathbf k},\qquad
u_k^2-v_k^2=1,\qquad u_k^2+v_k^2=\frac{A_k}{E_k},\qquad 2u_kv_k=\frac{B_k}{E_k}.
$$
Equivalently $u_k=\cosh\theta_k$, $v_k=\sinh\theta_k$, with $\tanh(2\theta_k)=B_k/A_k$. The identity $u_k^2-v_k^2=1$ preserves the <canonical commutation relation>. Vanishing anomalous terms require $B_k(u_k^2+v_k^2)-2A_ku_kv_k=0$, and hence the <Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion> is
$$
\boxed{E_k=\sqrt{A_k^2-B_k^2}=\sqrt{\epsilon_k(\epsilon_k+2nV_k)}.}
$$
Substitution and <normal ordering> give
$$
\boxed{H_2=E_{\rm mf}+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}(E_k-A_k)+\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}E_k\alpha^\dagger_{\mathbf k}\alpha_{\mathbf k}.}
$$
This is the requested form after putting each $E_k/2$ inside the oscillator term. By <momentum-pair counting in bosonic diagonalization>, all sums cover the full nonzero-<momentum> set; no further factor of two is needed. The <bosonic Bogoliubov zero-point shift> combines the two vacuum sums before taking a continuum limit.

If $V_k\to V_0>0$ smoothly, the low-energy excitations are gapless <phonons>:
$$
\boxed{E_k\simeq\hbar c|k|,\qquad c=\sqrt{nV_0/m}.}
$$
For contact repulsion, the <healing length> $\xi=\hbar/\sqrt{2mnV_0}$ separates this regime from the particle-like regime $E_k\simeq\epsilon_k+nV_0$. Stability requires $\epsilon_k+2nV_k>0$ for every nonzero mode. A general momentum-dependent interaction need not share the contact model's entire curve. An unregularized contact model also makes the vacuum-energy shift ultraviolet divergent; a physical finite-range interaction or scattering-length renormalization is needed for that absolute <ground-state energy>.

In the <Bogoliubov quasiparticle> vacuum, $\alpha_{\mathbf k}|0_\alpha\rangle=0$, but the inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> gives $\langle b^\dagger_{\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k}\rangle=v_k^2$. Consequently the <quantum depletion of a Bose condensate> is
$$
\boxed{n-n_0=\frac1{\mathcal V}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}v_k^2=\frac1{2\mathcal V}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left(\frac{\epsilon_k+nV_k}{\sqrt{\epsilon_k(\epsilon_k+2nV_k)}}-1\right).}
$$
**This expression assumes zero temperature.** At nonzero <temperature>, thermal <Bogoliubov quasiparticles> add to the depletion:
$$
 n-n_0=\frac1{\mathcal V}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left[v_k^2+\frac{A_k}{E_k}\frac1{e^{\beta E_k}-1}\right].
$$
Thus the printed formula is the quantum contribution, or a low-temperature approximation when the thermal contribution is negligible. It is nonnegative for a stable quadratic <Hamiltonian> and vanishes in the noninteracting zero-temperature limit.

A periodic <harmonic chain> with $N$ masses $m_0$, spacing $a$ and nearest-neighbour spring constant $K$ has
$$
H=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left[\frac{p_j^2}{2m_0}+\frac K2(u_{j+1}-u_j)^2\right],\qquad u_N=u_0.
$$
Hamilton's equations give $m_0\ddot u_j=K(u_{j+1}+u_{j-1}-2u_j)$. The <discrete Fourier transform> $u_j=N^{-1/2}\sum_k Q_ke^{ikja}$, $p_j=N^{-1/2}\sum_k P_ke^{ikja}$, with $k=2\pi l/(Na)$ in one <Brillouin zone>, diagonalizes the quadratic form:
$$
\boxed{\omega_k=2\sqrt{K/m_0}\,|\sin(ka/2)|,\qquad H=\sum_k\left[\frac{P_kP_{-k}}{2m_0}+\frac{m_0\omega_k^2}{2}Q_kQ_{-k}\right].}
$$
The <harmonic-chain normal-mode reality condition> means $Q_k^\dagger=Q_{-k}$, not that $Q_k$ and $Q_{-k}$ are independent real coordinates. For <canonical quantization>, impose $[u_j,p_l]=i\hbar\delta_{jl}$, equivalently $[Q_k,P_{k'}]=i\hbar\delta_{k,-k'}$. For $k\ne0$ set
$$
Q_k=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_0\omega_k}}(a_k+a^\dagger_{-k}),\quad
P_k=-i\sqrt{\frac{m_0\hbar\omega_k}{2}}(a_k-a^\dagger_{-k}),\quad
[a_k,a^\dagger_{k'}]=\delta_{kk'}.
$$
Then the <quantum harmonic chain> is a set of independent <phonon> oscillators:
$$
\boxed{H=\frac{P_{\rm tot}^2}{2Nm_0}+\sum_{k\ne0}\hbar\omega_k(a_k^\dagger a_k+1/2).}
$$
The <centre-of-mass zero mode of a harmonic chain> is the free coordinate $X=N^{-1}\sum_ju_j$, with $[X,P_{\rm tot}]=i\hbar$; it cannot be normalized by dividing by $\sqrt{\omega_0}$. Retaining it gives
$$
u_j=X+\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{k\ne0}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m_0\omega_k}}(a_ke^{ikja}+a_k^\dagger e^{-ikja}),
$$
with the corresponding momentum expansion and uniform term $P_{\rm tot}/N$. The nonzero modes alone give the site <commutator> $i\hbar(\delta_{jl}-1/N)$; the uniform term supplies the missing $1/N$. Each oscillator occupation is a nonnegative integer, and adding one <phonon> costs $\hbar\omega_k$ and crystal <momentum> $\hbar k$ modulo a reciprocal-lattice vector. <Applications of Quantum Mechanics by David Tong>[§4.1.1, printed pp.103–105, monatomic-chain normal modes and quantization]{ref}

In the <continuum limit of a harmonic chain>, $\rho=m_0/a$, $Y=Ka$ and
$$
L=\frac12\int dx\,[\rho\dot u^2-Y(\partial_xu)^2],\qquad
\Pi=\rho\dot u,\quad [u(x),\Pi(x')]=i\hbar\delta(x-x'),\qquad
\boxed{c=\sqrt{Y/\rho}=a\sqrt{K/m_0}.}
$$
The <wave equation> and linear low-$k$ <dispersion relation> follow. The lattice gives an ultraviolet cutoff; the acoustic approximation does not reproduce the zone-edge flattening.

With the centre of mass fixed, the oscillator <partition function> and <Bose-Einstein distribution> are
$$
Z_{\rm osc}=\prod_{k\ne0}\frac1{2\sinh(\beta\hbar\omega_k/2)},\qquad
\langle a_k^\dagger a_k\rangle=\frac1{e^{\beta\hbar\omega_k}-1}.
$$
For a long chain, above its finite-size level spacing and below the zone-edge scale, integrating the two acoustic propagation directions gives $C/(Na)=\pi k_{\rm B}^2T/(3\hbar c)$. At high <temperature> each retained oscillator instead contributes $k_{\rm B}$ to the <heat capacity>.

For $H=\hat p^2/(2m)+V(\hat x)$, divide elapsed time $t$ into $J$ slices $\Delta t=t/J$ and apply the <Trotter product formula>. Inserting complete position and momentum bases gives the regulated <phase-space path integral>
$$
K(x_f,x_i;t)=\lim_{J\to\infty}\int\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}dx_j\prod_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{dp_j}{2\pi\hbar}\exp\left\{\frac i\hbar\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left[p_j(x_{j+1}-x_j)-\Delta t\left(\frac{p_j^2}{2m}+V(x_j)\right)\right]\right\}.
$$
The endpoint values are $x_0=x_i$, $x_J=x_f$. Each <Gaussian momentum integration in a phase-space path integral> can be performed explicitly, leaving the <configuration-space path integral>
$$
\boxed{K=\int_{x_i}^{x_f}\mathcal Dx\,e^{iS[x]/\hbar},\qquad S[x]=\int_0^t ds\,[m\dot x^2/2-V(x)].}
$$
Its measure is the time-sliced limit of $(m/(2\pi i\hbar\Delta t))^{J/2}\prod_{j=1}^{J-1}dx_j$, with the usual infinitesimal convergence prescription. The integral sums amplitudes, not positive classical probabilities. Operator ordering remains part of the time-slice definition. Expanding a short-time kernel recovers the <Schrödinger equation>; composing kernels reproduces the <time-evolution operator>.

In the <classical limit>, rapid phase cancellation suppresses paths away from stationary <action>. Varying $S$ with fixed endpoints gives $m\ddot x=-V'(x)$. The semiclassical result sums the classical-path phases multiplied by fluctuation <functional determinants>; for a quadratic <Hamiltonian> the fluctuation integral is Gaussian and this expansion is exact.

For <quantum statistical mechanics>, continue $t=-i\tau$ by a <Wick rotation>. The <Euclidean action> and thermal <trace> give
$$
S_E=\int_0^{\beta\hbar}d\tau\,[m(\partial_\tau x)^2/2+V(x)],\qquad
\boxed{Z=\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}=\int_{x(\beta\hbar)=x(0)}\mathcal Dx\,e^{-S_E/\hbar}.}
$$
The trace closes position-space paths. In a many-particle trace one also sums over endpoint permutations with the appropriate exchange signs. In a <coherent state> field formulation, bosonic fields are periodic and <fermionic coherent states> instead give antiperiodic <Grassmann fields>, hence bosonic and fermionic <Matsubara frequencies> $2\pi n/(\beta\hbar)$ and $(2n+1)\pi/(\beta\hbar)$. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§§3.1–3.2, coherent-state time slicing and trace boundary conditions]{ref}

The <quantum-to-classical imaginary-time mapping> views a local $d$-dimensional quantum problem as a classical-looking field theory in $d+1$ dimensions, with imaginary-time extent $\beta\hbar$. A genuine classical probability interpretation additionally requires a nonnegative weight; fermion signs and Berry terms can obstruct it.

For a smooth potential in the high-temperature or small-$\hbar$ limit, imaginary-time paths fluctuate only over the <thermal de Broglie wavelength> $\lambda_T=\sqrt{2\pi\beta\hbar^2/m}$. If this is short compared with the potential's variation scale, the diagonal free kernel gives
$$
\boxed{Z\simeq\frac1{2\pi\hbar}\int dx\,dp\,e^{-\beta[p^2/(2m)+V(x)]}=\frac1{\lambda_T}\int dx\,e^{-\beta V(x)}.}
$$
This <classical phase-space limit of the thermal kernel> is the classical <phase space> partition function for one degree of freedom. A dilute identical-particle gas also needs its $1/N!$ factor; when exchange is appreciable, classical statistics fails. As a check, a single <quantum harmonic oscillator> gives $Z=[2\sinh(\beta\hbar\omega/2)]^{-1}\to1/(\beta\hbar\omega)$, precisely the classical phase-space integral.

For conventional <superconductivity>, an attractive interaction pairs opposite-spin <fermions>. A complex pairing <Hubbard–Stratonovich transformation> introduces a <Cooper pair> field $\Delta$. The fermionic fields then appear quadratically, giving the <pair-field effective action of a superconductor>
$$
S_{\rm eff}[\Delta,A]=\int\frac{|\Delta|^2}{g}-\operatorname{Tr}\ln G^{-1}[\Delta,A]+S_{\rm EM},\qquad g>0.
$$
The trace convention counts each independent spin-pair block once. The uniform saddle is the <BCS gap equation>; the fermionic excitations have $E_k=\sqrt{\xi_k^2+|\Delta|^2}$ in the fully gapped case. A slow-field expansion near the transition yields <Ginzburg-Landau theory of superconductivity>. Normalize its pair field $\Psi$ so $|\Psi|^2$ is an effective pair density, with pair mass $m_*$ and signed charge $q=-2e$:
$$
F=\int d^3x\left[a|\Psi|^2+\frac b2|\Psi|^4+\frac1{2m_*}|(-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf A)\Psi|^2+\frac{\mathbf B^2}{2\mu_0}\right],\qquad b>0.
$$
For $a<0$, the uniform minimum has $\rho_0=|\Psi|^2=-a/b$, with arbitrary constant phase. Writing $\Psi=(\sqrt{\rho_0}+h)e^{i\theta}$ separates the <amplitude mode> $h$ from the phase. The local potential has curvature term $2|a|h^2$ and coherence scale $\xi=\hbar/\sqrt{2m_*|a|}$. A neutral condensate has a gapless phase <Goldstone mode> when suitable dynamics is included.

The charged theory is invariant under
$$
\theta\mapsto\theta+q\chi/\hbar,\quad\mathbf A\mapsto\mathbf A+\nabla\chi,\quad\phi\mapsto\phi-\partial_t\chi.
$$
The <gauge-invariant condensate momentum> is $\hbar\nabla\theta-q\mathbf A$. Only $\hbar\nabla\theta-q\mathbf A$ and $\hbar\dot\theta+q\phi$ enter physical phase dynamics. At nearly constant amplitude, varying $F$ gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbf j=\frac{q\rho_0}{m_*}(\hbar\nabla\theta-q\mathbf A),\qquad
\lambda^{-2}=\frac{\mu_0q^2\rho_0}{m_*}.}
$$
For a smooth phase, taking the curl and using <Ampère's law> gives $\nabla^2\mathbf B=\mathbf B/\lambda^2$: the <Meissner effect> screens a static bulk <magnetic field> over the <London penetration depth> $\lambda$. In a gauge with $\theta=0$, the phase stiffness is a quadratic term in $\mathbf A$, the superconducting version of the <Higgs mechanism>.

To identify the dynamical mode, a simple lossless long-wave <superconducting phase-density action> is
$$
\mathcal L=\frac{\chi_p}{2}(\hbar\dot\theta+q\phi)^2-\frac{\rho_0}{2m_*}(\hbar\nabla\theta-q\mathbf A)^2+\frac{\epsilon_0\mathbf E^2}{2}-\frac{\mathbf B^2}{2\mu_0},\qquad \chi_p>0.
$$
Here $\chi_p$ is the pair compressibility coefficient. Eliminating the longitudinal electric potential gives
$$
\boxed{\omega^2=\omega_p^2+c_s^2k^2,\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{q^2\rho_0}{\epsilon_0m_*},\qquad c_s^2=\frac{\rho_0}{m_*\chi_p}.}
$$
Thus the neutral sound mode becomes a gapped longitudinal <plasma oscillation>; transverse electromagnetic waves acquire $\omega^2=c_{\rm vac}^2k^2+c_{\rm vac}^2/\lambda^2$ in this simple bulk model. This redistribution of the phase degree of freedom into electromagnetic collective modes is the <Anderson-Higgs mechanism in a superconductor>. Local <gauge symmetry> is a redundancy, not an independently observable symmetry whose breaking creates another massless particle. <Physics 215C: Quantum Field Theory, Part 3 by John McGreevy>[§2.3, superconductors, superfluids and gauged phase dynamics]{ref}

The static functional alone does not determine a frequency or damping rate. The displayed mode dispersions require the added dynamical assumptions; real screening, normal carriers, boundaries and dissipation modify them. The amplitude fluctuation is distinct from the phase mode and is not removed by the gauge choice.

Use $\hbar=k_{\rm B}=1$ and dimensionless spin operators. At one site, the <canonical anticommutation relations> imply the <on-site spin-square normal-ordering identity>
$$
\mathbf S_n^2=\frac34(n_n-2n_{n\uparrow}n_{n\downarrow}),\qquad
:\mathbf S_n^2:=\mathbf S_n^2-\frac34n_n=-\frac32n_{n\uparrow}n_{n\downarrow}.
$$
Indeed the empty and doubly occupied states have spin zero, while either singly occupied state has $\mathbf S^2=3/4$. Thus the interaction in normal-ordered form is **$3U\sum_n c^\dagger_{n\uparrow}c^\dagger_{n\downarrow}c_{n\downarrow}c_{n\uparrow}$**. There is no residual $3Un/2$ chemical-potential shift: that contraction has cancelled.

Insert <fermionic coherent state> resolutions between imaginary-time evolution factors. With $\xi_k=\epsilon_k-\mu$ and $D_n=\bar\psi_{n\uparrow}\bar\psi_{n\downarrow}\psi_{n\downarrow}\psi_{n\uparrow}$, the <grand-canonical coherent-state action> is
$$
\boxed{Z=\int_{\rm AP}\mathcal D(\bar\psi,\psi)\,e^{-S_0-3U\int_0^\beta d\tau\sum_nD_n},\qquad
S_0=\int_0^\beta d\tau\sum_{k\sigma}\bar\psi_{k\sigma}(\partial_\tau+\xi_k)\psi_{k\sigma}.}
$$
The independent barred and unbarred <Grassmann variables> satisfy antiperiodic boundary conditions at $0,\beta$. Adjacent time-slice labels in the normal-ordered symbol fix the continuum prescription and thermal normalization. The interaction coefficient is $3U$, not the $U$ of a differently normalized <Hubbard model>.

Expand the <Grassmann fields> in fermionic <Matsubara frequencies> $\omega_l=(2l+1)\pi/\beta$. For $U=0$, the <Grassmann Gaussian integral> factorizes into thermal <fermionic one-level thermal determinants>:
$$
Z_0=\prod_{k,\sigma}(1+e^{-\beta\xi_k})=\prod_k(1+e^{-\beta\xi_k})^2.
$$
Each factor counts the empty and occupied states of one spin orbital. Equivalently, the regularized <Matsubara sum> gives the logarithm of that factor; the raw infinite determinant requires <thermal normalization of a fermion determinant>. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\Omega_0=-\frac1\beta\ln Z_0=-\frac2\beta\sum_k\ln(1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_k-\mu)}).}
$$
**The printed $F$ is the grand potential $\Omega$**, because the trace contains $H-\mu\hat N$. The <Helmholtz free energy> at fixed mean <particle number> is $F_{\rm H}=\Omega+\mu\langle\hat N\rangle$.

Define the Grassmann spin density $\mathbf j_n=\bar\psi_n\boldsymbol\sigma\psi_n$. The <Pauli matrices> and Grassmann reordering give
$$
\mathbf j_n^2=-6D_n,\qquad 3UD_n=-\frac U2\mathbf j_n^2.
$$
For example $j_z^2=-2D_n$; each of the other two components gives the same result. For $U>0$, complete the square independently at each site and time slice:
$$
 e^{\Delta\tau U\mathbf j^2/2}=\left(\frac{\Delta\tau}{2\pi U}\right)^{3/2}\int_{\mathbb R^3}d^3M\,\exp\left[-\Delta\tau\left(\frac{\mathbf M^2}{2U}-\mathbf M\cdot\mathbf j\right)\right].
$$
This normalized <spin-vector Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling> yields
$$
\boxed{S=S_0+\int_0^\beta d\tau\sum_n\left[\frac{\mathbf M_n^2}{2U}-\sum_{\alpha\beta}\bar\psi_{n\alpha}\mathbf M_n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_{\alpha\beta}\psi_{n\beta}\right].}
$$
The auxiliary <bosonic> <quantum field> is real and periodic, while the fermionic fields remain antiperiodic. All three vector components are required to reproduce the original $3U$ interaction; replacing this exact transformation by a single-component identity would change its coefficient.

For the uniform <mean-field approximation> field $\mathbf M=M\hat{\mathbf e}_z$, the inverse fermion propagator is diagonal in <momentum>, frequency and spin: $-i\omega_l+\xi_k-\sigma M$. The <Grassmann Gaussian integral> therefore gives
$$
Z_{\rm uniform}=\int d\mu(M)\,e^{-\beta NM^2/(2U)}\prod_{k,\sigma=\pm1}\left[1+e^{-\beta(\xi_k-\sigma M)}\right].
$$
Here $d\mu(M)$ denotes the residual uniform-field measure; integration over its orientation only affects a nonextensive prefactor. Hence
$$
\boxed{S_{\rm eff}(M)=\frac{\beta NM^2}{2U}-\sum_{k,\sigma=\pm1}\ln\left[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_k-\mu-\sigma M)}\right].}
$$
The exchange field separates the two spin bands by $2M$. Integrating out the fermions is exact for a specified auxiliary field; restricting that field to be constant is the <mean-field approximation>.

Differentiate the uniform <effective action>:
$$
0=\frac1\beta\frac{\partial S_{\rm eff}}{\partial M}=\frac{NM}{U}-\sum_{k,\sigma}\sigma n_F(\epsilon_k-\sigma M).
$$
To reproduce the printed coefficient, define the **spin-summed density of states per site** by
$$
\nu(\epsilon)=\frac2N\sum_k\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon_k),\qquad
\frac1N\sum_k f(\epsilon_k)=\frac12\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)f(\epsilon).
$$
The <saddle-point approximation> then gives
$$
\boxed{M=\frac U2\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)\,\sigma n_F(\epsilon-\sigma M).}
$$
The <uniform spin field and magnetization> obey $M=U(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow)=2U\langle S_z\rangle$ for occupations per site. If a one-spin <density of states> were used instead, the factor $1/2$ would be absent. The spin field is an energy, rather than directly a magnetic moment.

= Solution

The constant term in the spin sum cancels. Linearizing the <saddle point> equation gives
$$
M=UM\chi_0+O(M^3),\qquad \chi_0=\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)[-\partial_\epsilon n_F(\epsilon)].
$$
The <paramagnetic auxiliary-field curvature> is
$$
\frac{S_{\rm eff}(M)-S_{\rm eff}(0)}{\beta N}=\frac12(U^{-1}-\chi_0)M^2+O(M^4).
$$
Consequently the <Stoner criterion> for the loss of local paramagnetic stability is
$$
\boxed{U>U_c,\qquad U_c\chi_0=1,\qquad U_c\nu(\mu)\simeq1\quad(T\to0).}
$$
The printed $\nu(0)$ uses energy measured from the <Fermi level>. For a smooth <density of states>, $-n_F'$ becomes a narrow unit-area peak at $\mu$; a singular density of states or appreciable temperature requires the integral form. The curvature establishes local instability, not the order or location of every possible thermodynamic transition.

With exact spin-rotation symmetry, <itinerant ferromagnetism> selects a magnetization direction. The two broken transverse generators form **one quadratic <type-B Goldstone boson>**, a long-wave <ferromagnetic magnon> with $E_q\sim Dq^2$. Longitudinal amplitude motion is not symmetry-protected to be gapless. In a metallic ordered state, particle-hole excitations remain near each surviving exchange-split <Fermi surface>; these are distinct from the collective magnon. The <itinerant spin-flip particle-hole continuum> starts at exchange scale $2|M|$ at $q=0$ in this simple mean-field spectrum, while its finite-$q$ boundaries differ. Spin anisotropy, spin-orbit coupling or an external field can gap the transverse mode; none is present in the stated model.
