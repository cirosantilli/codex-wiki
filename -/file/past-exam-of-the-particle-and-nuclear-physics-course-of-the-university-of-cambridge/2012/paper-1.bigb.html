The static <one-pion exchange> <propagator> gives a <Yukawa potential> proportional to $e^{-m_\pi cr/\hbar}/r$. Its characteristic range is therefore the <Reduced Compton wavelength>,
$$
\boxed{R\sim\frac{\hbar c}{m_\pi c^2}=\frac{197.3}{140}\,\mathrm{fm}\simeq1.4\,\mathrm{fm}}.
$$
This is an exponential falloff scale, not a sharp interaction cutoff. The ordinary <Compton wavelength> would introduce an unwanted factor $2\pi$.

Neglect atomic <binding energy> and take the target <electron> at rest. Use <natural units> temporarily. The final state contains three <electrons> and one <Positron>, so its threshold <invariant mass> is $4m_e$. For incident total <energy> $E$,
$$
s=(p_1+p_2)^2=2m_e^2+2m_eE=16m_e^2,
\qquad \boxed{E_{\rm th}=7m_ec^2=3.577\,\mathrm{MeV}\simeq3.58\,\mathrm{MeV}}.
$$
The corresponding incident <kinetic energy> is $6m_ec^2=3.066\,\mathrm{MeV}$. At threshold the products have no relative <momentum> in the <centre-of-momentum frame>; their common laboratory motion requires more <energy> than merely the pair's rest <energy>.

The <nuclear quadrupole vibration> has five one-phonon magnetic substates, $m=-2,-1,0,1,2$. <Bose-Einstein statistics> restrict three identical <phonons> to the <symmetric power> $S^3(V_2)$, of dimension $\binom{5+3-1}{3}=35$. Count occupation states $(n_{-2},\ldots,n_2)$ with $\sum n_m=3$ and $\sum mn_m=M$. For nonnegative $M$ the counts are
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrr}M&0&1&2&3&4&5&6&7\\\hline d_M&5&4&4&3&2&1&1&0\end{array}.
$$
Each spin-$J$ <irreducible representation> contributes one state at every projection from $-J$ to $J$. <magnetic-substate subtraction> therefore gives multiplicity $d_J-d_{J+1}$: one each at $J=0,2,3,4,6$, and none at $1,5$. Thus the <three-quadrupole-phonon multiplet> is
$$
\boxed{S^3(V_2)=V_0\oplus V_2\oplus V_3\oplus V_4\oplus V_6,\qquad J^P=0^+,2^+,3^+,4^+,6^+}.
$$
The dimension check is $1+5+7+9+13=35$. The <parity> is positive because each constituent <phonon> has positive <parity>. Pairwise angular-momentum triangle rules alone would include states incompatible with complete boson symmetrization.

A bare <gauge boson> mass would spoil the electroweak gauge structure. In the <Standard Model> a complex <Higgs doublet> instead has a symmetry-breaking potential. Choose the convention
$$
V=-\mu^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi+\lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2,\qquad
\Phi=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom{0}{v+h},\qquad v^2=\mu^2/\lambda.
$$
The <Higgs mechanism> rearranges three scalar components into the <longitudinal polarizations> of the massive <W bosons> and <Z bosons>; the unbroken <electromagnetic gauge symmetry> leaves the <photon> massless. One physical scalar excitation, the <Higgs boson> $h$, remains.<Broken Symmetries and the Masses of Gauge Bosons by Peter Higgs>[pp. 508–509, gauge-field mass generation in a symmetry-breaking scalar model]{ref} Using <tree-level Feynman diagrams>,
$$
\boxed{m_W=gv/2,\quad m_Z=\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}\,v/2,\quad m_h^2=2\lambda v^2,\quad m_f=y_fv/\sqrt2}.
$$
These equations use <natural units>. <Yukawa interactions> generate charged-fermion masses and Higgs couplings proportional to those masses; the minimal model does not account for observed <neutrino masses>. The gauge kinetic term supplies Higgs couplings to W and Z pairs. The physical Higgs exchange also cancels the otherwise excessive high-energy growth of longitudinal vector-boson scattering. The mechanism does not fix the <Yukawa couplings> or the Higgs self-coupling, and most <proton> mass arises from strong-interaction dynamics rather than directly from the light-quark Higgs masses.

The mass-generation mechanism is a statement about the field and its <vacuum expectation value>; the observable boson is the excitation about that vacuum.

<beta decay> is a <weak charged current> process. At <quark> level $d\to uW^{-*}\to ue^-\bar\nu_e$, with amplitude proportional to $G_FV_{ud}$ at low <momentum transfer>. A <neutron> in a <nucleus> can thereby become a <proton>:
$$
n\to pe^-\bar\nu_e,\qquad p\to ne^+\nu_e,\qquad pe^-\to n\nu_e.
$$
The second reaction denotes an energetically allowed nuclear conversion, not a decay of an isolated free <proton>. In a nuclear beta transition the <mass number> stays fixed and the <atomic number> changes by one. <baryon number> and <electric charge> are conserved; the <neutrino> or <antineutrino> supplies the missing <energy>, <momentum> and <angular momentum>. The three-body final state produces a continuous <electron> spectrum rather than a single <electron> <energy>. Neglecting <neutrino mass> and recoil, an allowed spectrum in <electron> total <energy> is
$$
\frac{d\Gamma}{dE_e}\propto F(Z,E_e)\,p_eE_e(E_0-E_e)^2
\left(g_V^2|M_F|^2+g_A^2|M_{GT}|^2\right).
$$
The <Fermi function> accounts for the <electron>'s <Coulomb interaction> with the daughter; $E_0$ is the <electron> total-energy endpoint. <forbidden beta decay> adds momentum-dependent nuclear shape factors. The <V-A interaction> violates <parity>: it couples left-chiral <fermion> fields, not an equal mixture of left and right currents.

For <allowed beta decay> the leading <leptons> have zero <orbital angular momentum>. <Fermi transitions> have $\Delta J=0$; <Gamow-Teller transitions> have $\Delta J=0,1$ with $0\to0$ excluded. Both preserve nuclear <parity>. Higher <lepton> partial waves permit forbidden transitions with extra suppression. Nuclear matrix elements and <phase space>, as well as these <selection rules>, determine lifetimes. Particle-physics examples include free-neutron decay and <pion beta decay>, $\pi^+\to\pi^0e^+\nu_e$. <semileptonic decays> test <quark> mixing and the universality of the <weak charged current>. **<beta decay> changes <quark flavour> through virtual W exchange; its continuous spectrum reflects an additional <lepton> in the final state.**

<nuclear radius scaling> follows approximately $R=r_0A^{1/3}$ with $r_0\simeq1.2\,\mathrm{fm}$ for a sharp matter-radius convention. Consequently the interior <nucleon> density is nearly independent of <mass number>: <nuclear forces> are short range and exhibit <nuclear matter saturation>. A diffuse <nuclear charge distribution> has a charge-weighted <root mean square> radius, not a hard surface. For a uniform sphere, $r_{\rm ch}=\sqrt{3/5}R$; charge radii and matter radii need not coincide, especially with <neutron> skins.

Elastic <electron scattering> measures the <nuclear charge form factor>. In a simple one-photon, spherical approximation, with $q$ a wave number,
$$
F(q)=\frac{1}{Ze}\int\rho_{\rm ch}(\mathbf r)e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\,d^3r,
\qquad F(q)=1-\frac{q^2\langle r^2\rangle}{6}+\cdots.
$$
The scattering <cross section> relative to a point-charge prediction is proportional to $|F|^2$, so its low-$q$ slope constrains the radius and <diffraction> structure constrains the radial distribution. Finite <momentum> coverage and <Coulomb distortion in electron scattering> limit reconstruction. In <Robert Hofstadter>'s experiment a <magnetic spectrometer> separated elastic and inelastic <electron> peaks; beam-charge normalization and closed-slit background checks prevented raw count structure from being mistaken for a <nuclear charge form factor>.<Electron Scattering and Nuclear Structure by Robert Hofstadter>[§III(b)(1), pp. 222–223; §IV(a), pp. 227–231]{ref}

A useful intrinsic-shape description is $R(\theta,\phi)=R_0[1+\sum_{\mu=-2}^{2}\alpha_{2\mu}Y_{2\mu}(\theta,\phi)+\cdots]$. An <intrinsic nuclear quadrupole moment> $Q_0=\int\rho_{\rm ch}(3z^2-r^2)\,d^3r$ distinguishes prolate and oblate positive-charge distributions by positive and negative signs. The laboratory <spectroscopic nuclear quadrupole moment> is state dependent: an $I=0$ state has zero laboratory quadrupole moment even when its intrinsic shape is deformed. <nuclear rotational bands> approximately follow $E_I\propto I(I+1)$; harmonic <nuclear quadrupole vibrations> instead have equally spaced <phonon> energies and multiphonon <spin> multiplets. Measured <electric quadrupole transitions> test the collective interpretation in addition to level energies. **Radius probes spatial extent; quadrupole observables and collective spectra probe shape.**

A <Feynman diagram> specifies a term in the perturbative <scattering amplitude>, not a measured microscopic trajectory. Assign external <spinors> or <polarization vectors>, an interaction factor to each <Feynman vertex> and a <propagator> to each internal line; impose <four-momentum> conservation and integrate over independent <loop momenta>. Sum amplitudes for indistinguishable alternatives, then square, sum final <spins> and average unpolarized initial <spins>. A <decay rate> uses $d\Gamma=|\mathcal M|^2d\Phi_n/(2M)$ in <natural units>; a <cross section> also divides by incident flux. <interference> means probabilities of individual diagrams must not simply be added.

The electromagnetic <fermion> vertex is $f\bar f\gamma$, with coupling proportional to $eQ_f\gamma^\mu$; it preserves <fermion> flavour. There is no elementary three-photon or four-photon vertex in <quantum electrodynamics>. The <weak charged current> has $W\ell\nu$ and $Wu_id_j$ vertices, with a left-chiral projector and a <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix> element at the <quark> vertex. The <weak neutral current> has $Zf\bar f$ vertices with vector and axial couplings; it includes <neutrinos> and is flavour diagonal at tree level. Electroweak gauge interactions also include $WW\gamma$, $WWZ$ and quartic $WWWW$, $WWZZ$, $WWZ\gamma$ and $WW\gamma\gamma$ vertices. Higgs-fermion and Higgs-vector interactions complete the mass-related scalar sector.<Electroweak Model and Constraints on New Physics by J. Erler and A. Freitas (2024)>[§10.1, Eqs. 10.2–10.7 and discussion of bosonic interactions]{ref}

<Quantum chromodynamics> has $q\bar qg$ vertices proportional to $g_s\gamma^\mu T^a$, plus three-gluon and four-gluon <Feynman vertices> because <gluons> carry <color charge>. A quark-gluon vertex preserves <quark flavour>. Internal particles need not be on their <mass shells>, but <electric charge> and <four-momentum> are conserved at each vertex. The figures below show representative leading flavour topologies; binding and <hadronization> are implicit, and additional strong-interaction contributions can modify hadronic amplitudes.

The <weak charged current> spectator topology is $c\to qW^{+*}$, $W^{+*}\to u\bar q'$, with the initial $\bar u$ a spectator. The pair $q\bar u$ forms the negative <meson> and $u\bar q'$ the positive <meson>. The amplitude contains $V_{cq}^*V_{uq'}$ in one common charged-current convention; complex conjugating all factors changes no rate classification. With $c_C=\cos\theta_C$ and $s_C=\sin\theta_C$, the CKM magnitudes are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{mode}&(q,q')&\text{amplitude order}\\\hline
\pi^+K^-&(s,d)&c_C^2\\
\pi^+\pi^-&(d,d)&s_Cc_C\\
K^+K^-&(s,s)&s_Cc_C\\
K^+\pi^-&(d,s)&s_C^2
\end{array}.
$$
Thus **$\pi^+K^-$ is favoured, both equal-flavour pairs are singly Cabibbo suppressed, and $K^+\pi^-$ is doubly suppressed**. The corresponding parametric rate hierarchy is $1:\tan^2\theta_C:\tan^4\theta_C$ after normalizing to the favoured mode. <Cabibbo suppression> counting alone does not rank the middle two: <phase space>, different matrix elements and <hadronic final-state interactions> matter. With identical constant matrix elements, the larger <pion> <phase space> would favour $\pi^+\pi^-$, whereas experimentally $K^+K^-$ is faster. The empirical decreasing order is
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(\pi^+K^-) > \Gamma(K^+K^-) > \Gamma(\pi^+\pi^-) > \Gamma(K^+\pi^-)}.
$$
As a later cross-check, <HFLAV>'s 2024 averages give approximately $4.00\%$, $0.411\%$, $0.149\%$ and $0.0138\%$ respectively. The exact middle ordering is an experimental input, not a consequence of the supplied masses and <Cabibbo angle> alone.<D0 hadronic branching fractions and Final State Radiation by HFLAV (2024)>[final branching-fraction average table and final doubly suppressed-mode estimate]{ref}

The initial <strangeness> is $-2$. The $\Lambda\pi^0$ final state has <strangeness> $-1$, so its allowed nonleptonic amplitude is weak. One representative topology is $s\to uW^{-*}$ followed by $W^{-*}\to d\bar u$; spectator $u,s$ combine with the produced $d$ into the $\Lambda$, while $u\bar u$ contributes to the neutral <pion>. The available rest <energy> is $1314.8-1115.7-135.0=64.1\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

The $\Lambda\bar K^0$ state would conserve <strangeness> and could have a strong vertex, but its threshold exceeds the parent mass by $1115.7+497.6-1314.8=298.5\,\mathrm{MeV}$. **It is kinematically forbidden.** Coupling strength cannot rescue a closed decay channel.

The radiative channel also changes <strangeness>. It is a <weak radiative hyperon decay>, not an ordinary electromagnetic decay between states of the same flavour. A representative two-quark contribution is W exchange $su\to ud$ with a <photon> attached to a charged line; the other allowed attachments, hadronic intermediate states and short-distance loop contributions must be included for a complete amplitude. Its extra electromagnetic coupling and hadronic dynamics suppress it relative to the nonleptonic channel. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(\Lambda\pi^0)\gg\Gamma(\Lambda\gamma)>\Gamma(\Lambda\bar K^0)=0}.
$$
<NA48 Collaboration> measured a radiative <branching fraction> of about $1.16\times10^{-3}$, corroborating the suppression rather than a purely electromagnetic interpretation.<Measurement of the Xi0 to Lambda gamma Decay Asymmetry and Branching Fraction by NA48 Collaboration>[abstract and branching-fraction result, 2004]{ref}

For the neutral quark-antiquark <mesons> here, $C=(-1)^{L+S}$. Thus $C(\eta')=C(\pi^0)=+1$, while $C(\rho^0)=C(\gamma)=-1$. The radiative $\rho^0\gamma$ mode conserves <charge conjugation> and admits a quark-line photon-emission diagram. Both components of the supplied $\rho^0$ <wavefunction> contribute: the charge-weighted flavour overlap is proportional to $Q_u-Q_d=1$, so it does not cancel. Its <photon> <energy> is $(m_{\eta'}^2-m_\rho^2)/(2m_{\eta'})\simeq165\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

The $\pi^0\gamma$ mode changes <charge conjugation> and, more decisively, a spin-zero particle cannot decay into another spin-zero particle and one real <photon>. In the parent rest frame the <photon> has <helicity> $\pm1$, while the opposite-moving <pion> has <helicity> zero; the <spin-zero two-body helicity selection> rule cannot be satisfied. **Its on-shell two-body amplitude vanishes even if a <weak interaction> violates C.** A virtual <photon> subsequently making another pair is a different final state.

For $\pi^0\rho^0$, angular-momentum conservation requires relative <orbital angular momentum> $L=1$. Its <parity> is then negative, matching the parent, but its charge-conjugation eigenvalue is $C_\pi C_\rho=-1$: the two distinct self-conjugate particles are not interchanged by C, so there is no additional $(-1)^L$ factor in this C eigenvalue. The decay is forbidden by strong and electromagnetic charge-conjugation conservation despite being above threshold. A weak contribution would require <CP violation> as well. At the strong/electromagnetic accuracy of the diagram classification,
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(\rho^0\gamma)>\Gamma(\pi^0\rho^0)=\Gamma(\pi^0\gamma)=0}.
$$
If extremely small weak CP-violating amplitudes are retained, $\pi^0\rho^0$ need not be exactly zero; the one-real-photon <spin> prohibition remains exact for $\pi^0\gamma$.

For <electron-positron annihilation into a muon pair> the gauge diagrams are s-channel <photon> and Z exchange. For <electron-positron annihilation into electron neutrinos> there is s-channel Z exchange and t-channel W exchange, with <interference>. For <electron-positron annihilation into W-boson pairs> there are s-channel <photon> and Z diagrams and a t-channel electron-neutrino diagram. All three gauge amplitudes are required for the proper high-energy cancellation. Electron-Yukawa-suppressed s-channel Higgs diagrams can also produce the massive <muon> or W pair; they vanish in the usual zero-electron-mass approximation used for the leading gauge comparison.

\Image[paper-1-lepton-topologies.png]
{title=Lepton and W-pair gauge diagrams}

There is **no energy-independent ordering**. For unpolarized beams and inclusive Born <cross sections> the useful regimes are:

* Below the <muon> threshold only the <neutrino> channel is open, ignoring its tiny mass threshold. Immediately above $2m_\mu$, <muon> <phase space> initially vanishes.
* Well above $2m_\mu$ but well below the weak-boson masses, <photon> exchange gives $\sigma_{\mu\mu}\simeq4\pi\alpha^2/(3s)$, whereas $\sigma_{\nu_e\bar\nu_e}=O(G_F^2s)$. Hence the <muon> channel dominates and real W-pair production is closed.
* Near the Z pole, W pairs are still closed and the Z resonance dominates both open channels. The ratio is approximately $\Gamma(Z\to\nu_e\bar\nu_e)/\Gamma(Z\to\mu^+\mu^-)\simeq2$, from their neutral-current couplings: <neutrinos> rank above <muons>. This is for one <neutrino> flavour.
* At $\sqrt s=2m_W\simeq160.806\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the on-shell W-pair channel opens from zero <phase space>. Its position in the ordering changes across threshold and must be obtained from the summed amplitudes, rather than inferred solely from a weak/electromagnetic label.
* Far above the weak masses, forward t-channel W exchange makes the inclusive electron-neutrino <cross section> approach a nonzero scale of order $g^4/m_W^2$. The W-pair <cross section> behaves as $O[g^4\log(s/m_W^2)/s]$ and the <muon> <cross section> as $O(g^4/s)$. Thus asymptotically the decreasing order is $\nu_e\bar\nu_e$, then $W^+W^-$, then $\mu^+\mu^-$. Fixed angular cuts, <beam spin polarization> and radiative corrections modify these comparisons.

The Z-pole ratio follows $[\tfrac12]^2/[(-\tfrac12+\sin^2\theta_W)^2+\sin^4\theta_W]\simeq1.99$. Treating every weak reaction as uniformly slower misses both the resonance and forward exchange.

For projectile velocity $v$ and a resting target, let $M=m_x+m_X$, $\mu=m_xm_X/M$, and $V=m_xv/M$. Subtract the <kinetic energy> of the centre of mass from the laboratory <kinetic energy>:
$$
T^*=\tfrac12m_xv^2-\tfrac12MV^2
=T_x\frac{m_X}{M}=\boxed{\frac{\mu}{m_x}T_x}.
$$
In the <centre-of-momentum frame> the two momenta are opposite with equal magnitude, so
$$
T^*=\frac{p^{*2}}{2m_x}+\frac{p^{*2}}{2m_X}=\frac{p^{*2}}{2\mu},
\qquad\boxed{p^*=\sqrt{2\mu T^*}}.
$$
For masses quoted in $\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$, use $p^*c=\sqrt{2(\mu c^2)T^*}$. These are the nonrelativistic relations stipulated for this question; no relativistic correction should be silently mixed into the numerical results.

The <partial decay width> $\Gamma_x$ is the <energy> width associated with decay of the <compound nucleus> back into the entrance channel $x+X$; by <time reversal> it also controls formation from that channel. The <partial decay width> $\Gamma_y$ describes the selected exit channel $y+Y$. The total <decay width> sums all energetically accessible channels, including these: $\boxed{\Gamma=\sum_i\Gamma_i=\hbar/\tau}$. The <branching fraction> for channel $i$ is $\Gamma_i/\Gamma$. Widths have <energy> units, whereas $\Gamma_i/\hbar$ is a decay probability per unit time. The isolated-resonance formula assumes approximately constant widths over the resonance and neglects a nonresonant interfering background.

An unstable isolated level has survival amplitude $a(t)=\theta(t)e^{-iE_0t/\hbar}e^{-\Gamma t/(2\hbar)}$, because its <survival probability> falls as $e^{-\Gamma t/\hbar}$. Its energy-domain amplitude is proportional to
$$
\int_0^\infty e^{i(E-E_0)t/\hbar}e^{-\Gamma t/(2\hbar)}dt
=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma/2-i(E-E_0)}.
$$
Taking the modulus squared gives the denominator of the <Breit-Wigner resonance cross section>
$$
\boxed{(E-E_0)^2+(\Gamma/2)^2}.
$$
At $E-E_0=\pm\Gamma/2$ the resonant factor is half its peak, so $\Gamma$ is the <full width at half maximum>. <exponential decay> is the isolated-level approximation; energy-dependent thresholds or overlapping levels need a more general lineshape.

Unpolarized projectile and target <spins> supply $(2J_x+1)(2J_X+1)$ equally weighted <spin> orientations. An isolated compound-nucleus level of <spin> $J$ has $2J+1$ degenerate magnetic substates. Sum over these resonance substates and average over the entrance <spins> to obtain
$$
\boxed{g=\frac{2J+1}{(2J_x+1)(2J_X+1)}}.
$$
This <resonance spin statistical factor> is a statistical <spin> weight, not always a probability smaller than one: <orbital angular momentum> also participates in forming the resonance. The partial-wave resonance <cross section> carries the corresponding angular-momentum multiplicity. Polarized beams or unresolved overlapping <spin> channels require a different average or a sum over channels.

The gamma width is $0.10\,\mathrm{eV}=10^{-7}\,\mathrm{MeV}$, so the remaining <proton> width is $\Gamma_p=0.20-0.15-10^{-7}=0.0499999\,\mathrm{MeV}$. At the peak of the same resonance the total-width denominator and gamma exit width cancel. <alpha particles> and <carbon-12> have <spin> zero; the <proton> and the stated nitrogen level each have <spin> $1/2$. Hence $g_\alpha/g_p=4$, independently of the unspecified resonance <spin>. The <proton> <reduced mass> and <momentum> are
$$
\mu_pc^2=879.223\,\mathrm{MeV},\qquad p_p^*c=\sqrt{2(879.223)(2.113770)}=60.967\,\mathrm{MeV}.
$$
The <same-resonance entrance-channel cross-section ratio> is therefore
$$
\boxed{\frac{\sigma_\alpha}{\sigma_p}
=\frac{g_\alpha}{g_p}\frac{p_p^{*2}}{p_\alpha^{*2}}\frac{\Gamma_\alpha}{\Gamma_p}
=4\frac{60.967^2}{205.790^2}\frac{0.15}{0.0499999}\simeq1.053}.
$$
The ratio compares each reaction at its own laboratory resonance <energy>. Factors of $\hbar^2$ restoring the <cross section>'s area units cancel. Only the supplied nitrogen <spin> magnitude is used: its printed positive <parity> is immaterial to this <spin> average, so no orbital-parity inference is made from that assignment.
