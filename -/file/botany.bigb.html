= Plant morphology
{parent=Botany}

Study of the form and organization of <plant> structures.

= Shoot
{parent=Plant morphology}

A <plant stem> with its associated <leaves> and <buds>.

= Peduncle
{parent=Plant morphology}

The stalk supporting an <inflorescence> or a solitary <flower>.

= Inflorescence
{parent=Plant morphology}

The arrangement of <flowers> on a flowering axis, or the flowering system itself.

= Flower head
{parent=Inflorescence}

An <inflorescence> of closely packed <flowers> on a common expanded support, as in the <common daisy>.

= Pedicel
{parent=Inflorescence}

The stalk of one <flower> within an <inflorescence>.

= Umbel
{parent=Inflorescence}

An <inflorescence> with stalks arising together at the top of a <peduncle>.<Glossary of Botanical Terms by PlantNET>[T–U, entry “umbel”]{ref}

= Compound umbel
{parent=Umbel}

An <inflorescence> whose primary branches arise together and each end in an <umbellet>.

= Umbellet
{parent=Compound umbel}

A secondary <umbel> within a <compound umbel>.

= Spadix
{parent=Inflorescence}

An <inflorescence> with a thickened axis bearing closely attached <flowers>, commonly associated with a <spathe>.

= Bract
{parent=Plant morphology}

A modified <leaf> associated with a <flower> or <inflorescence>.

= Involucre
{parent=Bract}

A ring or several rings of <bracts> surrounding a <flower> or <inflorescence>.<Glossary of Botanical Terms by PlantNET>[I–L, entry “involucre”, sense 1]{ref}

= Spathe
{parent=Bract}

A sheathing <bract> enclosing a <flower> or <inflorescence>, especially a <spadix>.<Glossary of Botanical Terms by PlantNET>[S, entry “spathe”]{ref}

= Bud
{parent=Plant morphology}

An undeveloped <shoot> or <flower>, often enclosed by protective <leaves>.

= Plant stem
{parent=Plant morphology}

An axis of a <plant> bearing <leaves> at <stem nodes>, separated by <internodes>. A <rhizome> is a specialized <plant stem>.

= Rhizome
{parent=Plant stem}

A persistent, usually horizontal <plant stem> at or beneath the ground surface, bearing <stem nodes>, <buds> and often reduced <leaves>. <Ginger> provides an example.<Ginger by the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew>[Plant description]{ref}

= Internode
{parent=Plant stem}

The portion of a <plant stem> between successive <stem nodes>.

= Stem node
{parent=Plant stem}

A point on a <plant stem> where a <leaf> is attached.

= Plant physiology
{parent=Botany}

Study of the functions of <plants>, including <photosynthesis>, transport, growth and <signal transduction>.

= Nitrogen assimilation
{parent=Plant physiology}

Incorporation of inorganic or organic <nitrogen> into cellular compounds. In <plants>, nitrate reduction and ammonium incorporation connect nitrogen uptake with carbon metabolism.

= Root pressure
{parent=Plant physiology}

Positive hydrostatic pressure associated with roots, capable under suitable conditions of producing sap exudation.

= Transpiration
{parent=Plant physiology}

= Flavonoid
{parent=Plant physiology}

A class of plant metabolites. Selected root-exuded flavonoids help induce rhizobial symbiotic signaling; this is one role among many.

= Phototropin
{parent=Plant physiology}

A blue-light-activated receptor kinase family controlling responses including chloroplast relocation, phototropism and stomatal opening.

= NRT1.1
{parent=Plant physiology}
{c}

An Arabidopsis nitrate transporter and sensor, also called CHL1 or NPF6.3. Its nitrate-regulated auxin transport connects local nutrient conditions to lateral-root development.

= Cryptochrome
{parent=Plant physiology}

A blue-light photoreceptor family with roles including plant development, circadian entrainment and photoperiodic responses.

= Photoperiodism
{parent=Plant physiology}

Dependence of a biological response on the daily pattern of light and darkness, often mediated by coincidence between light and an internally timed sensitive phase.

= CONSTANS
{parent=Photoperiodism}
{c}

An Arabidopsis transcriptional regulator whose timed expression and light-dependent protein regulation contribute to photoperiodic activation of <FLOWERING LOCUS T>.

= External coincidence model
{parent=Photoperiodism}

A model in which a light-sensitive internal phase produces a response when it coincides with the external light interval. Equal total light need not produce equal responses if timing differs.

= Guard cell
{parent=Plant physiology}

One of a pair of specialized epidermal cells controlling a <stoma>. Changes in solute content and <turgor pressure> alter pore aperture.

= Plant immunity
{parent=Botany}

= Growth-defense trade-off
{parent=Plant immunity}

Allocation and regulatory conflicts between growth and defence; the sign and magnitude of the relationship depend on environment and <genotype>.

= PAD4
{c}
{parent=Plant immunity}

A component of a <plant> immune signaling pathway whose evolutionary conservation and loss are coupled to other pathway members.

= EDS1
{c}
{parent=Plant immunity}

A component of a <plant> immune signaling pathway whose evolutionary conservation and loss are coupled to other pathway members.

= Hybrid necrosis
{parent=Plant immunity}

A deleterious hybrid <phenotype> that can arise from incompatible immune-gene interactions and inappropriate defence activation.

= Gene-for-gene interaction
{parent=Plant immunity}

A host resistance and pathogen avirulence specificity relationship; it is not the complete architecture of all <plant> disease resistance.

= Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat receptor
{parent=Plant immunity}

= NLR
{c}
{synonym}

An intracellular immune receptor class in <plants>, commonly shortened to <NLR>.

= Pan-NLRome
{parent=Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat receptor}

The species-wide collection of <NLR> genes and alleles across accessions, rather than the inventory in a single reference genome.

= NLR gene cluster
{c}
{parent=Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat receptor}

A genomic group of related receptor genes shaped by duplication, deletion and sequence exchange; not every <NLR> is clustered.

= Integrated decoy
{parent=Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat receptor}

A domain incorporated into an immune receptor that mimics an effector target and links its perturbation to immune recognition.

= Paired NLR
{parent=Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat receptor}

Functionally coupled receptors whose sensor and signaling activities may be partitioned between two genes. Physical linkage can help preserve their compatible inheritance.

= RRS1-RPS4 receptor pair
{c}
{parent=Paired NLR}

An <Arabidopsis thaliana> paired immune-receptor example associated with a WRKY integrated domain in RRS1.

= Pattern recognition receptor
{parent=Plant immunity}

= PRR
{c}
{synonym}

A receptor recognizing conserved microbial or damage-associated molecular patterns.

= Resistance gene
{parent=Plant immunity}

A host gene contributing to reduced disease; not all <resistance genes> encode <NLR> receptors.

{parent=Botany}

= Plant genetics
{parent=Botany}

= FLOWERING LOCUS T
{c}
{parent=Plant genetics}

= FT protein
{synonym}
{c}

A flowering regulator that can also link maternal temperature history in fruit tissues to progeny dormancy.

= Ph1 locus
{c}
{parent=Plant genetics}

A wheat locus promoting meiotic stability and limiting crossover between homoeologous chromosomes.

= Plant breeding
{parent=Plant genetics}

= Seed priming
{parent=Plant breeding}

A controlled preparatory treatment that changes subsequent germination or stress responses; benefits and storage costs depend on the <crop> and treatment.

= Linkage drag
{parent=Plant breeding}

Retention of unfavorable linked material during introgression of a desired allele.

= Somaclonal variation
{parent=Plant breeding}

Variation among <plants> regenerated from cultured somatic cells; it can have genetic or epigenetic causes.

= Karma epiallele
{c}
{parent=Somaclonal variation}

Methylation variation at an intronic <retrotransposon> associated with normal versus mantled oil-palm development; loss of methylation is associated with altered splicing and reduced yield.

= Plant epigenetics
{parent=Plant genetics}

= FWA
{c}
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

An <Arabidopsis thaliana> gene whose ectopic expression and flowering <phenotype> can be associated with a stably unmethylated promoter state.

= VTE3(1)
{c}
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

A <tomato> vitamin E biosynthetic gene whose expression can be influenced by methylation of a nearby promoter <retrotransposon>.

= Stress priming
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

A prior stimulus changes the response to a subsequent challenge. Persistence may involve metabolites, proteins, regulatory feedback or <chromatin>; priming alone does not establish <epigenetic inheritance>.

= HSFA2
{c}
{parent=Stress priming}

A heat-shock <transcription> factor involved in sustained transcriptional stress memory in <Arabidopsis thaliana>.

= Somatic stress memory
{parent=Stress priming}

Persistence of a response state within an individual after the initial stimulus has ended, distinguished from inheritance through reproduction.

= Vernalization
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

Acquisition of competence to flower after prolonged cold; the molecular implementation and persistence vary among <plant> lineages.

= PEP1
{c}
{parent=Vernalization}

The <Arabis alpina> <FLC> orthologue, whose seasonal reactivation contributes to perennial flowering behavior.

= FLOWERING LOCUS C
{c}
{parent=Vernalization}

= FLC
{c}
{synonym}

An <Arabidopsis thaliana> floral repressor whose stable silencing contributes to memory of prolonged cold.

= Nucleolar dominance
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

Epigenetic silencing of one parental set of ribosomal RNA genes in a hybrid; it is not simply the deletion of those genes.

= RNA-directed DNA methylation
{c}
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

= RdDM
{c}
{synonym}

A <plant> silencing pathway in which small RNAs guide methylation at homologous <chromatin>. Canonical <RdDM> involves <Pol IV>, <RDR2>, <DCL3>, Argonaute and <Pol V> scaffold <transcription>.

= Epigenome editing
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

Targeted alteration of an <epigenetic state>, distinguished from cutting or rewriting the target DNA sequence. Persistence after removal of the targeting factor is locus dependent.

= Epigenome-wide association study
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

= EWAS
{c}
{synonym}

An association analysis relating epigenomic measurements to a <phenotype>. Association alone does not establish causal direction or independence from genetic variation.

= Epigenetic recombinant inbred line
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

= epiRIL
{c}
{synonym}

A line from a mapping population segregating experimentally induced epigenetic differences in a comparatively uniform genetic background. New transposon insertions and residual sequence differences still need to be assessed.

= Epiallele
{parent=Plant epigenetics}

An alternative regulatory state of a locus associated with an epigenetic difference. Its stability and independence from DNA sequence variation must be tested.

= Epimutation
{parent=Epiallele}

A gain or loss of an inherited <epigenetic state> rather than a change in nucleotide sequence.
