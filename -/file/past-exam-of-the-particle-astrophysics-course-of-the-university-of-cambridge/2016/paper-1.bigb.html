= Solution
{parent=1}

The known active <neutrinos> are too light and too fast to supply the dominant <cold dark matter>. Their relativistic decoupling leaves substantial <free streaming>, which erases rather than seeds sufficiently small-scale structure; their allowed <mass> density is also inadequate for the dominant cold component. Changing a light active <neutrino> from Dirac to Majorana does not remove those problems. What can change the picture is an enlarged neutral-fermion sector containing sterile states with different <masses>, production and decay rates. A sufficiently long-lived <sterile neutrino> in a Majorana state can be a dark-matter candidate; Majorana character alone supplies neither stability nor the correct abundance.

For the <spinor> calculation, put $a=p_z/(E+m)$ and define $A=N(1-a)/2$, $B=N(1+a)/2$. Applying the given <chiral projectors> gives
$$
U_{1L}=A\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U_{1R}=B\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
V_{2R}=B\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\overline U_{1R}U_{1L}=2AB
=\frac{E+m}{2}\left(1-\frac{p_z^2}{(E+m)^2}\right)=m,
\qquad
\overline V_{2R}U_{1L}=2AB=m,
$$
where the last equality uses $E^2-p_z^2=m^2$. Same-chirality <Lorentz scalar> products instead vanish: for example $\overline U_{1L}U_{1L}=0$. The nonzero overlaps demonstrate the <Lorentz scalar> structures coupling left and right <chirality>. For $U$ to $U$ this is a particle-number-preserving Dirac coupling; for $U$ to the charge-conjugate $V$ sector it can describe a lepton-number-violating Majorana coupling.

There is a normalization subtlety: both displayed overlaps equal the on-shell <mass> parameter used to define the <spinors>. A <chiral scalar spinor overlap> verifies a coupling structure without fixing an independent Lagrangian coefficient $M_L$. Repeating the on-shell construction with mass parameter $M_L$ would instead give the corresponding overlap $M_L$; this is a different input, rather than a newly derived parameter. The independent coefficients of a <Dirac mass term> and a <Majorana mass term> are defined by
$$
\mathcal L_m=-m(\bar\nu_R\nu_L+\bar\nu_L\nu_R)
-\frac12\left(M_L\overline{\nu_L^c}\nu_L+
M_R\overline{\nu_R^c}\nu_R+\mathrm{h.c.}\right).
$$
The Majorana terms connect a field with its charge conjugate and violate the corresponding continuous <lepton> phase symmetry by two units. With only unbroken <Standard Model> <gauge symmetry>, a left-handed Majorana <mass> also requires <electroweak symmetry breaking> or an appropriate higher-dimensional operator; it is not an arbitrary gauge-invariant bare <mass> for a <lepton> doublet.

Use an all-left-handed Weyl <basis> $\mathbf n_L=(\nu_L,\nu_R^c)^T$. For real parameters the <one-family Dirac-Majorana mass matrix> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M=\begin{pmatrix}M_L&m\\m&M_R\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal L_m=-\frac12\overline{\mathbf n_L^{\,c}}\mathcal M\mathbf n_L+\mathrm{h.c.}}
$$
Writing $(\nu_{1L},\nu_{2L})^T=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}(\nu_L,\nu_R^c)^T$ gives an orthogonal diagonalization, with $\tan2\theta=2m/(M_R-M_L)$ up to the rotation-sign convention. Massive-particle <chirality> and <helicity> are not identical; the familiar left/right <helicity> language becomes an approximation in the relativistic regime.

The <characteristic polynomial> is
$$
\det(\mathcal M-\lambda\mathbf1)
=\lambda^2-(M_L+M_R)\lambda+M_LM_R-m^2.
$$
Its roots are
$$
\boxed{\lambda_\pm=\frac12\left[M_R+M_L\pm
\sqrt{(M_R-M_L)^2+4m^2}\right].}
$$
For Majorana <fermions> the nonnegative physical <masses>, as explained by <physical Majorana mass and signed eigenvalue>, are $|\lambda_\pm|$ after a possible field rephasing; a negative real <eigenvalue> is not a negative physical <rest mass>. When $M_R\gg |m|,|M_L|$,
$$
\lambda_{\rm light}\simeq M_L-\frac{m^2}{M_R},\qquad
\lambda_{\rm heavy}\simeq M_R+\frac{m^2}{M_R}.
$$
The light state is predominantly $\nu_L-(m/M_R)\nu_R^c$, while the heavy state is predominantly $\nu_R^c+(m/M_R)\nu_L$. Each <mass> <eigenstate> combines its Weyl component and charge conjugate to form a Majorana field.

For $M_R=10^{12}\,\mathrm{GeV}$, **the heavy <mass> is approximately $10^{12}\,\mathrm{GeV}$**. In the type-I limit $M_L\simeq0$,
$$
\boxed{m_{\rm light}\simeq10^{-3}
\left(\frac{m}{1\,\mathrm{GeV}}\right)^2\,\mathrm{eV}.}
$$
The <seesaw light-mass estimate needs a Dirac scale>: no unique numerical light <mass> follows because the Dirac <mass> and $M_L$ were not supplied. As an explicitly conditional illustration, choosing $m=100\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $M_L=0$ gives $10\,\mathrm{eV}$; this is an assumed Dirac scale, not additional exam data or a prediction from $M_R$ alone. A heavy seesaw state is also not automatically stable enough to be <dark matter>.

The conventional minimal <Pati-Salam model> <gauge group> is
$$
\boxed{SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R.}
$$
The group printed in this paper is different: it contains two $SU(4)$ factors and only one $SU(2)$. This is a discrepancy in the original PDF, not just in the transcription. The standard-model discussion below uses the conventional intended <Pati-Salam model> model. A literal theory with the printed product would require additional <fermion> representations and symmetry-breaking data that are not specified.

The <Pati-Salam model> unifies the three <quark> <color charges> with <lepton number> as a fourth <color charge>. A family forms $(4,2,1)$ left-handed and $(4,1,2)$ right-handed multiplets, including a <right-handed neutrino>. The extended <color charge> group contains quark-lepton <gauge bosons>, while the right-handed weak factor supplies right-handed charged currents after symmetry breaking. The <Pati-Salam charge embedding> is
$$
Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+\frac{B-L}{2}.
$$
The original paper explicitly develops the smaller local subgroup $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\times SU(4)_C$, as well as a larger underlying global structure; neither distinction licenses silently replacing the printed group.<Lepton number as the fourth color by Jogesh Pati and Abdus Salam>[§II.B, equation (2), printed p. 276; §II.C, charge assignments]{ref} The product group is a quark-lepton unification scheme rather than a simple <gauge group> with one automatically common coupling. Breaking the right-handed and $B-L$ symmetries can generate a large $M_R$ and light/heavy <Majorana neutrinos> through the seesaw. The <gauge group> alone does not uniquely fix their <masses> or guarantee a dark-matter lifetime.

The most important laboratory test of Majorana character is <neutrinoless double beta decay>,
$$
(A,Z)\longrightarrow(A,Z+2)+e^-+e^-,
$$
with no outgoing <neutrinos>. Under light-neutrino exchange, the amplitude involves
$$
m_{\beta\beta}=\left|\sum_iU_{ei}^{\,2}m_i\right|,\qquad
[T_{1/2}^{0\nu}]^{-1}=G_{0\nu}|\mathcal M_{0\nu}|^2
\left|\frac{m_{\beta\beta}}{m_e}\right|^2.
$$
The coherent squared mixing elements allow phase cancellations. A null result constrains this combination within the assumed mechanism, rather than proving that all <neutrinos> are Dirac.

The observable is a peak in summed electron <energy> at the decay <Q value>, unlike the continuous two-neutrino spectrum. Enriched source material, large exposure, good <energy> resolution, underground operation and radiopure construction suppress backgrounds. <germanium> arrays use the source as a semiconductor detector; <xenon> time-projection chambers combine <ionization>, light and event-position information; <bolometers> measure deposited <energy> through a <temperature> rise. The GERDA collaboration's Phase-I study is a concrete example of an enriched-germanium source-detector search with background suppression.<Results on neutrinoless double beta decay of 76Ge from GERDA Phase I by the GERDA Collaboration>[Abstract and Phase-I source-detector method, arXiv:1307.4720]{ref} <energy> calibration, resolution tails, radon and material backgrounds, cosmic-ray vetoes, signal-like event <topology> and sideband fits all need independent checks before a Q-value excess is convincing. Collider same-sign dilepton processes can test heavier Majorana states, but require a specified production and decay mechanism. **Lepton-number violation, absolute-mass measurements and oscillation data provide complementary information; oscillations alone do not settle Majorana character.**

<supersymmetry> relates <bosons> and <fermions> through fermionic symmetry generators. A main motivation is the <Lorentz scalar> <hierarchy problem>: related <boson> and <fermion> loop contributions cancel particular ultraviolet sensitivities in an exactly supersymmetric theory. <soft supersymmetry breaking> leaves residual sensitivity to the <superpartner> scale, so a very heavy spectrum does not automatically solve naturalness. Supersymmetric matter content can also improve gauge-coupling unification, and a stable neutral lightest <superpartner> can be a dark-matter candidate. None of these motivations is itself experimental evidence; <supersymmetry> must be broken if ordinary particles and their partners have different observed <masses>.

If <R-parity> is conserved, <superpartners> are produced in pairs and cascades end in a stable <lightest supersymmetric particle>. A <neutralino> LSP can escape the detector, giving <missing transverse momentum>. Colored <squarks> and <gluinos> can generate energetic <jets>, sometimes b-tagged <jets> and <leptons>, plus that imbalance. An <inclusive supersymmetry search> classifies broad event samples by <jet> and <lepton> multiplicities, <transverse momentum> sums and missing <momentum>, without requiring a fully reconstructed decay chain.

Background controls are part of the physics argument. Lost-lepton W and top events can be constrained using <lepton> <collider control regions>; invisible-Z production can be related to visible-Z or photon samples with appropriate transfer factors. Instrumental imbalance in multijet events needs detector-quality checks and data sidebands. An excess in a <collider signal region> should survive uncertainty in those extrapolations, efficiencies and <luminosity>, with independently defined validation regions and attention to the number of searches performed.

An <exclusive supersymmetry search> targets a more specific production mode and decay pattern. Electroweak chargino-neutralino production can yield three <leptons> plus missing <momentum>; a cascade through a <slepton> can yield a dilepton invariant-mass edge that constrains <mass> combinations. Such searches often have cleaner backgrounds but depend more strongly on branching fractions and intermediate <masses>. An endpoint does not necessarily determine every absolute <mass> without additional observables.

Different spectra require different strategies. For a <compressed supersymmetric spectrum>, small parent-daughter <mass> differences make the visible decay products soft. <initial-state radiation> can recoil against the pair and provide a trigger, supplemented by soft-lepton reconstruction. The ATLAS compressed-spectrum study explicitly uses small <neutralino> <mass> differences and <initial-state radiation> to define a dedicated search rather than applying a hard-lepton selection indiscriminately.<Search for electroweak production of supersymmetric states in scenarios with compressed mass spectra at 13 TeV with the ATLAS detector by the ATLAS Collaboration>[arXiv:1712.08119, abstract and compressed-spectrum search strategy; methodological illustration published after this exam]{ref} If <R-parity> is violated, the LSP can decay visibly, so high-multiplicity <jet> or <lepton> channels may matter more than large missing <momentum>. CMS separately studied hadronic and leptonic final states for this reason.<Searches for R-parity-violating supersymmetry in pp collisions at 8 TeV in final states with 0-4 leptons by the CMS Collaboration>[CMS-SUS-14-003, abstract and independent lepton-multiplicity analyses]{ref} Long-lived particles instead motivate displaced vertices, disappearing tracks, abnormal <ionization> or time-of-flight measurements. Thus **there is no single model-independent missing-energy cut that covers every supersymmetric spectrum**.

For the hypothetical relic-density calculation, use the supplied distribution and retain its prefactor exactly. Put $M=mc^2$, $\Theta=k_BT$ and $\Theta_0=k_BT_0$. Then
$$
n_f=6\left[\frac{M\Theta_f}{(\hbar c)^2}\right]^{3/2}e^{-P},\qquad
\Theta_f=\frac{M}{P}.
$$
<number density>, denoted $N$ in the paper, is written $n$ here to distinguish it from a total particle count. The <cosmological particle freeze-out> <temperature> in <energy> units is
$$
\boxed{\Theta_f=\frac{38}{36}\,\mathrm{GeV}=1.0556\,\mathrm{GeV}.}
$$
Assume a stable nonrelativistic relic, negligible further <annihilation> and $aT=\mathrm{constant}$ after <cosmological particle freeze-out>, using <sharp-freeze-out relic dilution>. Its comoving particle number is conserved, so
$$
n_0=n_f\left(\frac{a_f}{a_0}\right)^3
=n_f\left(\frac{\Theta_0}{\Theta_f}\right)^3
=\boxed{6\left(\frac{\Theta_0}{\hbar c}\right)^3P^{3/2}e^{-P}.}
$$
The particle <mass> cancels from this present <number density> under the stated approximation. Its present rest-energy density is $Mn_0$, not merely $n_0$:
$$
n_f=4.4193\times10^{34}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3},\qquad
n_0=4.8766\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3},
$$
$$
\boxed{\rho_0=38n_0=1.8531\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{GeV\,m}^{-3},\qquad
\frac{\rho_0}{\rho_c}=3.7062\times10^{-3}.}
$$
This is only **0.371% of the <critical density>**, not a universe-closing component. The correct further comparison is with the measured dark-matter fraction, not an assumption that all <critical density> must be <dark matter>. In standard <cosmology> this abundance would be a small subcomponent, despite a possible collider discovery.

The <exponential freeze-out sensitivity> makes the result highly sensitive to $P$. Also, thermal <entropy> release means $aT$ is not exactly constant through the whole cosmic history. If total comoving <entropy> is conserved, the above <number density> acquires a factor $g_{*s,0}/g_{*s,f}$, involving <entropy degrees of freedom>. Those degrees of freedom and a full <annihilation> history are not given, so no precise correction is justified from the supplied data.

Establishing the nature of <dark matter> would require more than measuring a missing particle's collider <mass>. Measure its spin, charges, interactions, decay lifetime or stability limits, and <annihilation> or coannihilation processes; compare the resulting relic calculation with cosmological observations. Seek compatible direct nuclear-recoil signals and indirect <annihilation> or decay products, while controlling astrophysical and detector backgrounds. A collider-invisible lifetime need not be cosmologically long, and nonthermal production could change the abundance. **A consistent particle identity, cosmological abundance and independent non-collider evidence are needed to connect the hypothetical discovery to the <dark matter>.**

<gravitational lensing> measures the gravitational influence of matter without requiring that matter to shine. In <general relativity> a <point mass> produces small-angle deflection
$$
\hat\alpha=\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.
$$
For a thin lens, with angular-diameter distances $D_d,D_s,D_{ds}$,
$$
\boldsymbol\beta=\boldsymbol\theta-
\frac{D_{ds}}{D_s}\hat{\boldsymbol\alpha}(D_d\boldsymbol\theta).
$$
Image multiplicity, distortion and magnification therefore constrain projected gravitating <mass>, with source and lens geometry measured separately.

Strong lensing gives multiple images, arcs and <Einstein rings>. Their positions and, where available, time delays constrain a central <mass> distribution. Weak lensing measures correlated small image distortions over a larger field. Using the <critical surface density for gravitational lensing> and <reduced gravitational shear>, the convergence and shear satisfy
$$
\kappa=\frac{\Sigma}{\Sigma_{\rm crit}},\qquad
\Sigma_{\rm crit}=\frac{c^2}{4\pi G}\frac{D_s}{D_dD_{ds}},\qquad
g_{\rm red}=\frac{\gamma}{1-\kappa}.
$$
Because intrinsic galaxy shapes are unknown, weak shear is inferred statistically using many sources. Point-spread-function anisotropy, shear calibration, photometric redshifts, intrinsic alignments and foreground contamination must be controlled.

An inferred lensing <mass> can be compared with the independently estimated stellar and hot-gas <mass>. The <bullet Cluster dark-matter separation> provides a particularly informative separation of components: the collisional X-ray-emitting gas is displaced from the galaxies, while lensing <mass> peaks approximately follow the less-collisional galaxy component. The primary analysis combined wide-field and Hubble imaging rather than inferring lensing <mass> from X-ray hydrostatic equilibrium.<A direct empirical proof of the existence of dark matter by Douglas Clowe and others>[arXiv:astro-ph/0608407, abstract and lensing/X-ray component comparison]{ref} This favors a dominant relatively collisionless unseen component. Translating a merger offset into a particle self-interaction <cross section> also requires a merger model and projection uncertainties; lensing does not directly reveal a <WIMP>'s <mass> or quantum numbers.

Microlensing tests a different possibility: <dark matter> in compact objects. A compact foreground lens can create an unresolved, time-dependent magnification of a background star. Event frequency and duration constrain the abundance and <mass> range of compact lenses only after survey cadence, detection efficiency, blending, source sizes and halo geometry are included. Achromaticity and the expected light-curve shape help distinguish simple lensing from intrinsic variability. A null survey result does not exclude every compact-object <mass> at every location.

Weak-lensing correlations and small-scale strong-lens structure also test the <matter power spectrum> and halo substructure, potentially distinguishing cold from warm free-streaming behavior. Baryonic feedback can change the same small-scale <mass> distribution. There are intrinsic inversion ambiguities: the mass-sheet transformation
$$
\kappa\mapsto\lambda\kappa+(1-\lambda),\qquad
\gamma\mapsto\lambda\gamma
$$
leaves <reduced gravitational shear> unchanged. Source-redshift information, absolute magnification or other data can restrict such degeneracies. **Lensing establishes where gravitating matter is and tests its distribution and interactions; identification of its particle species requires complementary evidence.**

Extra spatial dimensions offer mechanisms for unification and for the large separation between weak and gravitational scales. They are hypotheses about geometry and fields, not observations established by the <hierarchy problem> itself.

In a <Kaluza-Klein theory> construction, a compact extra coordinate can turn components of a higher-dimensional metric into four-dimensional gauge and <scalar fields>. For a field periodic on a circle of radius $R$, <momentum> in that direction is quantized. In units $\hbar=c=1$,
$$
m_n^2=m_0^2+\frac{n^2}{R^2},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$
The resulting tower explains why a small compact dimension can be hard to see at low <energy>: nonzero modes need <energy> of order $1/R$. A realistic model must also explain its matter representations, <chirality>, internal geometry and stabilization of <Lorentz scalar> moduli; a compact coordinate alone does not reproduce the <Standard Model>.

Large-extra-dimension models instead use volume to weaken effective gravity. With a fixed unwarped compact space of volume $V_d$, integrating the higher-dimensional <Einstein-Hilbert action> gives, in a consistent reduced-Planck-mass convention,
$$
\boxed{M_{\rm Pl}^2=M_D^{d+2}V_d.}
$$
The <Standard Model> can be localized on a <brane> while gravity propagates in the bulk. A large $V_d$ makes the observed four-dimensional gravitational scale large even if the fundamental higher-dimensional scale is much smaller. The proposal identifies gravitational dilution as a possible hierarchy mechanism, with deviations from ordinary gravity at sufficiently short distance.<The Hierarchy Problem and New Dimensions at a Millimeter by Nima Arkani-Hamed and others>[arXiv:hep-ph/9803315, abstract and volume-dilution mechanism]{ref} For approximately flat dimensions of common size $R$, gravity at distances much smaller than $R$ spreads through more directions and the force scales as $r^{-(2+d)}$, returning to $r^{-2}$ at large distance.

Warped models use a different mechanism. A five-dimensional metric
$$
ds^2=e^{-2k|y|}\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu+dy^2
$$
redshifts a <mass> on a distant <brane> by $m_{\rm IR}=e^{-kL}m_{\rm UV}$. An exponential hierarchy can therefore arise without a large flat compact volume. The original Randall–Sundrum construction makes this distinction explicit and predicts characteristic massive spin-two excitations.<A Large Mass Hierarchy from a Small Extra Dimension by Lisa Randall and Raman Sundrum>[arXiv:hep-ph/9905221, abstract and warped hierarchy mechanism]{ref} It still needs a consistent stabilization and phenomenology; the flat-volume formula and the warped redshift should not be treated as interchangeable explanations.

Extra dimensions also occur in string-theory constructions, where consistency and <compactification> can organize gravitational and gauge degrees of freedom. That is a framework motivation, not proof that a particular <compactification> describes nature. Laboratory inverse-square-law tests, collider missing-momentum channels from escaping bulk modes, massive-mode resonances, and astrophysical or cosmological energy-loss constraints probe different parameter ranges. Background estimates and dependence on field localization matter: a <brane> model with invisible bulk emission need not share the signatures of a model with visible resonant decays. **The useful claim is a testable mechanism for a specified hierarchy or unification problem, with <compactification> and stabilization assumptions stated.**

A <topological defect> can remain after <spontaneous symmetry breaking> when the field cannot be continuously put into the vacuum everywhere. If the <vacuum manifold> is $\mathcal M=G/H$, its <topology> classifies possible obstructions. Causally separated regions can settle into different local order-parameter configurations; joining them during a <phase transition> can trap defects. This is a formation mechanism, not an assertion that every transition creates every possible defect.

In three spatial dimensions the <homotopy classification of cosmological defects> gives are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{vacuum topology}&\text{configuration}&\text{spatial character}\\ \hline
\pi_0(\mathcal M)\ne0&\text{domain walls}&\text{surfaces}\\
\pi_1(\mathcal M)\ne0&\text{strings}&\text{lines}\\
\pi_2(\mathcal M)\ne0&\text{monopoles}&\text{points}\\
\pi_3(\mathcal M)\ne0&\text{textures}&\text{extended winding}
\end{array}
$$
Disconnected vacua can meet across a wall; winding around a line can obstruct filling its core; a sphere around a point can carry monopole charge. Textures differ from permanent localized cores: they can collapse and unwind when the field leaves the <vacuum manifold>. Local gauge and global symmetry breaking also differ in their long-range fields and <radiation>. The original cosmological analysis connects vacuum <topology> with domain and string formation and their subsequent evolution.<Topology of cosmic domains and strings by Thomas Walter Bannerman Kibble>[J. Phys. A 9, pp. 1387–1398 (1976), vacuum topology and domain/string evolution]{ref}

The <energy> budget determines cosmological importance. Nonrelativistic monopoles dilute approximately as $a^{-3}$, so a large primordial abundance can become unacceptable as <radiation> falls faster. Inflation can dilute an earlier population, provided the same defect-producing transition is not repeated afterwards. A slowly evolving wall network dilutes especially slowly and can dominate the <energy> budget or create excessive anisotropy.

Strings can lose <energy> by intercommutation and loop formation, with loops radiating <gravitational waves> or field quanta. A <horizon-scaling cosmic string network> whose characteristic separation is of order the horizon can have density $\rho_{\rm string}\sim\mu/t^2$ in natural units, keeping a limited fraction of the total rather than accumulating indefinitely. This differs from an ideal frozen noninteracting network's simple $a^{-2}$ dilution law. Similarly, a <horizon-scaling domain wall network> has $\rho_{\rm wall}\sim\sigma/t$, while a frozen wall network follows $a^{-1}$. The evolutionary assumptions must accompany any quoted scaling.

Defects can seed density wakes and other inhomogeneities, perturb the microwave background and generate non-Gaussian signatures. A sufficiently straight ideal <cosmic string> has conical spacetime and can create doubled background images; a cosmic-string network can generate gravitational-wave bursts and a stochastic background. Textures can produce localized microwave-background <temperature> features while collapsing. These signatures constrain defect abundance and symmetry-breaking scale; an isolated suggestive feature is not a unique defect detection.

The predictions depend on network evolution, reconnection, loop sizes, <radiation> channels and the relation between symmetry breaking and inflation. **<topology> identifies which configurations are possible; formation and evolution determine whether they survive and whether their gravitational or radiative effects are observable.** A defect's unseen <energy> does not automatically make it the dominant <cold dark matter>.
