= Bosonic string theory
{wiki}

Bosonic string theory quantizes a relativistic string using only bosonic worldsheet fields. Lorentz-invariant quantization requires 26-dimensional target spacetime and contains a tachyonic ground state.

= Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{title2=$4+N_f/2-26=0$}

Keeping the ordinary reparameterization <bc ghost system>, four embedding bosons require 44 additional real chiral fermions in each chirality to cancel the <worldsheet Weyl anomaly>. These are internal matter fields, not an assumed locally supersymmetric RNS construction. Their bilinear currents generate the chiral $\mathfrak{so}(44)$ current algebra, with $\binom{44}{2}=946$ currents. A full closed fermionic model also requires consistent spin-structure and modular projections; the central-charge count alone does not determine these choices.

= Zero- and two-fermion vertices of an internally fermionized bosonic string
{parent=Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string}
{title2=$M^2=-4/\alpha',\ -2/\alpha',\ 0$}

In the unprojected polynomial Neveu–Schwarz vertex sector, zero fermion insertions give the usual closed-string tachyon and the massless graviton, two-form and dilaton. Two insertions split into a left-right pair $\psi^i\bar\psi^j e^{ipX}$ of internal weights $(1/2,1/2)$ and a same-chirality current pair of weight one. The first has $M^2=-2/\alpha'$ and 44-squared scalar components. The second requires a bosonic derivative on the opposite side for level matching and gives the two sets of 946 massless gauge vectors. Four-fermion scalar-current products belong to a different insertion count. A chosen global projection may retain a subset, so these operators do not by themselves specify a projected string spectrum.

= Open string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{wiki}

An open string has a worldsheet with boundary. Its endpoint conditions may be <Neumann boundary conditions>, <Dirichlet boundary conditions>, or mixtures selected independently in target-space directions.

= Open-string oscillator degeneracy
{parent=Open string}
{title2=$F(w)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-w^n)^{-24}$}

For an ordinary critical <open string> with all <Neumann boundary conditions>, there are 24 independent transverse creation oscillators at each positive mode number $n$. Summing their nonnegative occupation numbers gives one factor $(1-w^n)^{-24}$. The coefficient $d_p=[w^p]F(w)$ counts oscillator polarizations at level $p$, excluding continuous center-of-mass momentum and additional endpoint multiplicities. <Cauchy coefficient formula> gives $d_p=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint F(w)w^{-p-1}dw$.

= Large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string
{parent=Open-string oscillator degeneracy}
{title2=$d_p\sim2^{-1/2}p^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt p}$}

The <Dedekind eta function> modular transformation gives $F(e^{-t})\sim(t/2\pi)^{12}\exp(4\pi^2/t-t)$. The <saddle-point approximation> for coefficient extraction has $t_0=2\pi/\sqrt{p-1}$, phase $4\pi\sqrt{p-1}$ and curvature $(p-1)^{3/2}/\pi$. Its prefactor $t_0^{12}$ supplies $(p-1)^{-6}$ and the Gaussian width supplies $(p-1)^{-3/4}/\sqrt2$. This explains both the exponential growth and the power $-27/4$.

= Open-string endpoint momentum flux
{parent=Open string}
{title2=$J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$}

For the <Nambu-Goto phase-space action>, $\dot P_m=\partial_\sigma J_m$ with $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Thus $\dot{\mathcal P}_m=J_m(\pi)-J_m(0)$. Free endpoint variations force $J_m=0$ individually. A <Dirichlet boundary condition> allows an external support or <D-brane> to absorb momentum in the fixed direction.

= Variational open-string boundary conditions
{parent=Open-string endpoint momentum flux}
{title2=$J_m\delta X^m|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

The spatial integration-by-parts term in the <Nambu-Goto phase-space action> pairs endpoint displacement with momentum flux $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Free endpoint displacements require zero flux. Fixed normal coordinates instead set those variations to zero, while tangential variations retain a <Neumann boundary condition>. Boundary conditions must also be preserved by the equations of motion. In a boundary-preserving gauge with $u=0$, mixed <Neumann boundary conditions> and <Dirichlet boundary conditions> implement a planar <D-brane>.

= Free-end string boundary condition
{parent=Open-string endpoint momentum flux}
{title2=$X'^m|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

Free endpoint variations set the <open string> flux $eT^2X'_m+uP_m$ to zero. In a boundary-adapted temporal gauge, freedom to vary time forces $u=0$ at the end. Nonzero lapse then gives $X'^m=0$, a <Neumann boundary condition>.

= Null motion of a free string endpoint
{parent=Free-end string boundary condition}
{title2=$\dot X^2|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

In a boundary-adapted <temporal gauge>, a free <open string> endpoint has $X'^m=0$. The <Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints> then imply $P^2=0$, while $\dot X=eP$ implies $\dot X^2=0$. For $X^0=t$, its spatial speed is one. This null boundary is compatible with a timelike interior <worldsheet>.

= Open-string mode expansion
{parent=Open string}

In <conformal gauge>, a free open-string coordinate with <Neumann boundary conditions> has a cosine expansion. Canonical quantization turns its nonzero Fourier coefficients into <string oscillators>.

= Integer-mode expansion with parallel D-brane endpoints
{parent=Open-string mode expansion}
{title2=$\cos n\sigma\ \text{for NN},\quad\sin n\sigma\ \text{for DD}$}

On $0\le\sigma\le\pi$, a coordinate with <Neumann boundary conditions> at both ends has a constant position, linear time <zero mode> and cosine oscillators. A coordinate with <Dirichlet boundary conditions> at both ends has a linear spatial interpolation between endpoint positions and sine oscillators, with no <momentum> <zero mode>. Both have <integer> frequencies. This differs from an <ND boundary condition>, which applies different conditions at the two ends of the same coordinate and gives half-integer frequencies.

= Oscillator symplectic form of an open string
{parent=Open-string mode expansion}
{title2=$\{\alpha_j^m,\alpha_k^n\}=-ij\eta^{mn}\delta_{j+k,0}$}

The Fourier kinetic term $\sum_{k>0}i\alpha_{-k}\cdot\dot\alpha_k/k$ supplies conjugate pairs, with $\alpha_{-k}=\alpha_k^*$. Inverting its <symplectic form> yields the displayed <Poisson brackets>. Nonzero modes are independent of the center-of-mass pair, while $\alpha_0=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p$ is the momentum zero mode. The imaginary factors are compatible with a real action up to a total derivative.

= ND boundary condition
{c}
{synonym}

A transverse <open string> coordinate has an ND boundary condition when one endpoint obeys a <Neumann boundary condition> and the other a <Dirichlet boundary condition>. On a strip of width $\pi$, its nonconstant spatial <eigenfunctions> are $\cos((n+1/2)\sigma)$ when the free endpoint is at zero. They solve the endpoint equations because their derivatives vanish at zero and their values vanish at $\pi$. Reversing the endpoints gives sine <eigenfunctions> with the same frequencies. The fixed endpoint removes the dynamical <worldsheet zero mode>.

= Bosonic ND boundary-changing conformal weight
{parent=Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition}
{title2=$h_{\mathrm{ND}}=1/16$}

= ND twist conformal weight
{c}
{synonym}

For one free boson, the mixed ND open-string sector has strip vacuum energy $E_0=1/48$. The strip-to-plane transformation relates this energy to the plane <Virasoro algebra> zero mode by $E_0=h-c/24$, with <central charge> $c=1$. Hence its ground <conformal weight> is $h=1/48+1/24=1/16$. For $d$ independent ND coordinates the weights add to $d/16$. At $d=24$, the plane matter ground weight is $3/2$, while the strip oscillator vacuum energy is $1/2$. The plane physical condition $L_0^{\mathrm{plane}}-1=0$ is therefore equivalent to $\alpha'p_\parallel^2+N+1/2=0$. The apparent different constants are coordinate/constraint conventions, not different mass spectra.

= Closed string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{wiki}

= Closed bosonic string
{synonym}

A closed string has a cylindrical worldsheet without boundary. Periodicity permits independent left- and right-moving oscillator sectors and, in a compact target direction, winding modes.

= Large-level degeneracy of a closed bosonic string
{parent=Closed string}
{title2=$g_N\sim\tfrac12(N-1)^{-27/2}e^{8\pi\sqrt{N-1}}$}

The left and right transverse oscillator sectors each have <generating function> $\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)^{-24}$. Its coefficient satisfies $d_N\sim2^{-1/2}(N-1)^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt{N-1}}$. <Closed-string level matching> requires equal levels but leaves their polarizations independent, so $g_N=d_N^2$. With $\alpha'M^2=4(N-1)$, the exponential is $e^{\beta_H M}$ with $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$. The smooth rest-mass density is proportional to $M^{-26}e^{\beta_H M}$, since $dN/dM=\alpha'M/2$. This distinguishes degeneracy per level from density per unit mass. The <polynomial> prefactor matters at the boundary of canonical convergence.

= Closed-string mode expansion
{parent=Closed string}

A free closed-string embedding coordinate is the sum of center-of-mass zero modes and independent periodic left- and right-moving oscillator expansions.

= Covariant quantization of the bosonic string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

Covariant quantization keeps target-spacetime Lorentz covariance manifest, imposes canonical commutation relations on every embedding coordinate and removes unphysical states with the <Virasoro constraints>.

= No-ghost theorem below the critical bosonic dimension
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}
{title2=$2\le d\le26,\quad a=1$}

For integer target dimension $2\le d\le26$, intercept $a=1$ and ordinary nonzero momentum, the <old covariant string quantization> conditions leave a positive-semidefinite physical inner product. Quotienting its physical <null string states> removes gauge degeneracy. A <DDF operator> basis displays $d-2$ positive transverse oscillators; below dimension 26 the remaining physical longitudinal sector can be organized as a unitary <Virasoro algebra> sector with central charge $26-d$ and highest weight zero. At $d=26$ this extra sector becomes null and the quotient reduces to the 24 transverse oscillators. The result concerns negative-norm states, not the removal of the bosonic <tachyon>, and it does not itself cancel the <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> of a subcritical matter theory.

= DDF oscillator
{c}
{synonym}

DDF operators are transverse physical <string oscillators> constructed as contour integrals of weight-one <string vertex operators>. Choose a null reference momentum $q$ and transverse polarizations $\epsilon_i$ orthogonal to it and to a reference on-shell momentum. The integrand is proportional to $\epsilon_i\cdot\partial X\,e^{inq\cdot X}$; its normalization and $q\cdot p$ are fixed so the operators obey the oscillator algebra in the title and commute with the positive-mode <Virasoro constraints>. For an open-string tachyon seed, one can use $2\alpha' q\cdot p=1$. Acting with negative modes shifts momentum and raises oscillator level while preserving the physical mass-shell condition. Their transverse <Fock space> has positive norm and makes the physical content of the <no-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string> explicit.

= No-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}

In flat 26-dimensional <bosonic string theory>, at ordinary nonzero momentum, take <physical string states> satisfying $L_{n>0}|\psi\rangle=0$ and $(L_0-1)|\psi\rangle=0$. The <inner product> restricted to these states is positive semidefinite. Quotienting by physical <null string states> removes the degeneracy; the resulting <Hilbert space> is represented by the 24 transverse <string oscillators>. This is a statement about negative-norm matter states, not about removing the auxiliary <Faddeev-Popov ghosts>. It does not remove the bosonic <tachyon>.

= Level-two open-string polarization decomposition
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}
{title2=$\zeta_{\mu\nu}=\frac{t}{20}(3q_\mu q_\nu+\eta_{\mu\nu})+q_\mu A_\nu+q_\nu A_\mu+c_{\mu\nu}$}

For $d=26$, $q^2=-2$ and the <physical string state> $[\zeta_{\mu\nu}\alpha_{-1}^\mu\alpha_{-1}^\nu+b_\mu\alpha_{-2}^\mu]|p\rangle$, the <Virasoro constraints> give $b_\nu=-q^\mu\zeta_{\mu\nu}$ and $t=\zeta^\mu{}_{\mu}=-2q\cdot b$. Every solution has the displayed decomposition, where $q\cdot A=0$ and $c$ is symmetric, traceless and transverse to $q$. The vector piece is $L_{-1}(2A\cdot\alpha_{-1}|p\rangle)$; the scalar piece is $t(L_{-2}+3L_{-1}^2/2)|p\rangle/10$. These are <null string states>, leaving the transverse traceless massive tensor as the physical polarization.

{synonym}

Time-coordinate oscillators produce negative norms in the covariant indefinite <Fock space>. A physical negative-norm state would violate probabilistic unitarity. Such ghosts differ from the anticommuting <worldsheet ghost fields> introduced by <gauge fixing>.

= Old covariant string quantization
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}
{title2=$(L_0-a)|\psi\rangle=0,\quad L_{n>0}|\psi\rangle=0$}

= Old covariant quantization
{synonym}

Old covariant quantization retains all target-space <string oscillators> and imposes $(L_0-a)|\psi\rangle=0$ and $L_n|\psi\rangle=0$ for $n>0$. Negative-mode constraints are adjoints, not further annihilation conditions. Quotienting physical <null string states> removes redundant polarizations.

= Level-two scalar in covariant string quantization
{parent=Old covariant string quantization}
{title2=$\langle S|S\rangle=\frac{2}{25}(D-1)(26-D)$}

At $a=1$, let $k=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p$, $k^2=-2$. The physical scalar $[\alpha_{-1}\cdot\alpha_{-1}+(D+4)(k\cdot\alpha_{-1})^2/10+(D-1)k\cdot\alpha_{-2}/5]|p\rangle$ has norm $2(D-1)(26-D)/25$. It is positive below 26, null at 26, and a <string ghost state> above 26. Matching the ordinary transverse spectrum removes this extra scalar at 26.

= Level-one intercept bound in covariant string quantization
{parent=Old covariant string quantization}
{title2=$a\leq1$}

A polarization obeys $p\cdot\epsilon=0$ and $p^2=(a-1)/\alpha'$. If $a>1$, spacelike momentum allows a negative-norm timelike polarization. Thus ghost absence requires $a\leq1$. At $a=1$ the <null string state> identification $\epsilon\sim\epsilon+\lambda p$ leaves $D-2$ polarizations.

= Physical string state
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}

A physical string state obeys the Virasoro mass-shell and positive-mode constraints. States differing by a null state represent the same physical polarization.

= Spurious string state
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}

A spurious string state is a positive-level Virasoro descendant orthogonal to all physical string states.

= Null string state
{parent=Spurious string state}

A null string state is both physical and spurious. It has zero norm and is quotiented from the physical state space.

= Null-state quotient of a string
{parent=Null string state}
{title2=$\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys}}/\operatorname{rad}\mathcal H_{\mathrm{phys} }$}

The physical state space of <old covariant quantization> is quotiented by its physical spurious states, which are orthogonal to all physical states. In a ghost-free sector these form the radical of the semidefinite inner product. The quotient retains positive physical polarizations.

= Level-two scalar Virasoro null state
{parent=Null string state}

For $L_0|p\rangle=-|p\rangle$, the descendant $Z=(L_{-2}+\tfrac32L_{-1}^2)|p\rangle$ obeys $L_1Z=0$ and $L_2Z=(D-26)|p\rangle/2$. It becomes physical and null at 26 and supplies the scalar quotient needed for the massive spin-two <light-cone gauge in string theory> spectrum.

The characteristic string length is $\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}$, with $T=1/(2\pi\alpha')$. Excited string states have masses of order $1/\ell_s$ and higher-derivative corrections become important at that scale.

A Regge trajectory is an approximately linear relation between angular momentum and squared mass. A classical rotating open string obeys $J=\alpha'M^2$, with quantum effects adding an intercept.

= Regge limit
{parent=Regge trajectory}
{c}
{title2=$s\to\infty,\quad t\text{ fixed}$}

The Regge limit of a scattering amplitude takes the center-of-mass energy invariant $s$ large at fixed momentum-transfer invariant $t$. A leading trajectory produces a power $s^{\alpha(t)}$, with signature and residue factors. For the bosonic <Virasoro-Shapiro amplitude> in dimensionless string variables, $\alpha(t)=2+2t$. This differs from fixed-angle high-energy scattering.

= Polyakov action
{c}
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{wiki}

The Polyakov action introduces an independent worldsheet metric $g_{\alpha\beta}$:
$$
S_{\rm P}=-\frac T2\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-g}\,
g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X\mathbin\cdot\partial_\beta X.
$$
Its metric equation makes $g_{\alpha\beta}$ conformal to the <induced worldsheet metric>, reducing it classically to the <Nambu–Goto action>.

= String sigma model in a spacetime metric
{parent=Polyakov action}
{title2=$S=-\frac T2\int\sqrt{-h}\,h^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}(X)\partial_aX^\mu\partial_bX^\nu$}

Replacing the flat target metric in the <Polyakov action> by a <spacetime metric> $g_{\mu\nu}(X)$ gives the displayed string sigma model. The embedding fields $X^\mu$ map the <worldsheet> into target spacetime, while $h_{ab}$ is an independent <worldsheet metric>. Classical <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl transformations> permit <conformal gauge>; quantum consistency additionally requires vanishing <sigma-model beta functions>.

= String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave
{parent=String sigma model in a spacetime metric}
{title2=$ds^2=-2,dX^+dX^--m^2|X|^2(dX^+)^2+dX^I dX^I$}

In <conformal gauge> and <light-cone gauge in string theory> $X^+=\kappa\tau$, the transverse part of the <string sigma model in a spacetime metric> reduces to
$$
S_\perp=\frac T2\int d\tau d\sigma\,\left(\dot X^I\dot X^I-X'^IX'^I-m^2\kappa^2X^IX^I\right).
$$
The omitted longitudinal term is a total time derivative. Each transverse coordinate obeys a massive <wave equation>. If the spatial period is $L$, the longitudinal momentum is $p^+=T\kappa L$ in this normalization. Keeping the gauge slope explicit prevents missing a factor of $p^+$ when changing conventions.

= Plane-wave string mode Hamiltonian
{parent=String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave}
{title2=$H_\perp=\sum_{I,a}\left(\frac{p_{Ia}^2}{2T}+\frac T2\omega_a^2q_{Ia}^2\right)$}

Expand every transverse <closed string> coordinate in a real orthonormal <Fourier basis> of the spatial circle. The <canonical momenta> are $p_{Ia}=T\dot q_{Ia}$, and spatial orthogonality reduces the field <Hamiltonian> to the displayed independent <harmonic oscillators>. Canonical quantization gives $\omega_a(a_{Ia}^\dagger a_{Ia}+1/2)$ for each nonzero-frequency mode; a zero-frequency mode retains the free particle term $p_{Ia}^2/(2T)$. The formal <zero-point energy> requires a regulator; it is not automatically the massless flat-background intercept. Opposite travelling-wave oscillator combinations diagonalize the translation generator and satisfy <closed-string level matching>.

= Plane-wave string mode frequency
{parent=String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave}
{title2=$\omega_n=\sqrt{(2\pi n/L)^2+m^2\kappa^2}$}

For a <closed string> in a <string sigma model in an isotropic plane wave>, insert $e^{-i\omega_n\tau+2\pi in\sigma/L}$ into the transverse <wave equation>. Periodicity requires integer $n$, giving the displayed <dispersion relation>. The zero spatial mode is an oscillator of frequency $|m\kappa|$; when this mass vanishes it becomes a free particle mode and needs the position-plus-linear-time expansion.

= Classical equivalence of Polyakov and Nambu–Goto actions
{parent=Polyakov action}
{title2=$h_{ab}=\Omega^2\gamma_{ab}$}

Varying the independent metric in the <Polyakov action> sets the <worldsheet stress tensor> to zero. For a nondegenerate timelike embedding in two <worldsheet> dimensions, the metric is proportional to the <induced worldsheet metric>. The conformal factor cancels from the action, which becomes the <Nambu–Goto action>. This elimination is classical; the quantum measure and <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> require separate treatment.

= Worldsheet stress-energy tensor
{parent=Polyakov action}
{title2=$T_{ab}$}

= Worldsheet stress tensor
{synonym}

Varying the independent <worldsheet metric> gives $T_{ab}=T[\partial_aX\cdot\partial_bX-\tfrac12\gamma_{ab}\gamma^{cd}\partial_cX\cdot\partial_dX]$. Its classical vanishing is the metric equation and produces the <Virasoro constraints>.

= Phase-space identification of the Polyakov metric
{parent=Polyakov action}
{title2=$\gamma_{ab}\sim\begin{pmatrix}u^2-e^2T^2&u\\u&1\end{pmatrix}$}

For positive lapse, eliminating momentum gives $(\dot X-uX')^2/(2e)-eT^2X'^2/2$. This is the <Polyakov action> for $\gamma_{ab}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}u^2-e^2T^2&u\\u&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, up to a <Weyl transformation>. Thus $e=1/T,u=0$ is <conformal gauge>.

= Polyakov path integral
{c}
{parent=Polyakov action}

The Polyakov path integral integrates over string embeddings and worldsheet metrics, divided by worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations. Gauge fixing introduces worldsheet ghosts and moduli integrals.

= Two-dimensional quantum-gravity interpretation of strings
{parent=Polyakov path integral}

The <Polyakov path integral> integrates over <string worldsheet> metrics as well as embedding fields, modulo <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl transformations>. Summing surface topologies gives perturbative <two-dimensional quantum gravity> coupled to the coordinate matter fields. Gauge fixing retains <worldsheet moduli> and introduces ghosts; a conformal gauge is locally flat, not a global flat metric on every genus. Cancellation of the <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> makes the critical bosonic version consistent.

= Worldsheet moduli
{parent=Polyakov path integral}
{title2=$\mathcal M_{g,n}$}

= Worldsheet modulus
{synonym}

Worldsheet moduli parameterize inequivalent conformal structures and marked insertion points after <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl invariance> are fixed. At each <genus>, <string perturbation theory> integrates over the corresponding moduli space.

= Worldsheet conformal Killing group
{parent=Worldsheet moduli}

The worldsheet conformal Killing group is the connected residual group of conformal automorphisms of the chosen compact <Riemann surface>. Its infinitesimal vectors satisfy the <conformal Killing equation>, or $P_1v=0$ for the <worldsheet diffeomorphism ghost operator>. Its complex dimensions are three on a sphere, one on a torus, and zero for <genus> at least two. The sphere group consists of <Möbius transformations>, while the torus's connected group is translation. Additional discrete automorphisms are handled by the moduli quotient rather than counted as continuous zero modes.

= Infinitesimal worldsheet modulus
{parent=Worldsheet moduli}
{title2=$h^{ab}t_{ab}=0,\quad\nabla^at_{ab}=0$}

= Infinitesimal worldsheet moduli
{synonym}

After quotienting infinitesimal <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl transformations>, choose a metric variation orthogonal to the gauge directions. Orthogonality to Weyl scaling makes its trace zero; the <formal adjoint of the worldsheet conformal Killing operator> makes its divergence zero. These are transverse-traceless variations of the two-dimensional metric. In a local conformal complex coordinate, their nonzero components satisfy $\partial_{\bar z}t_{zz}=0$ and its conjugate, linking them to <holomorphic quadratic differentials>.

= Torus modular invariance
{parent=Worldsheet moduli}

The torus worldsheet path integral is invariant under the <modular group> acting on its complex parameter $\tau$. Integrating over the <standard fundamental domain of the modular group> counts each inequivalent torus once and excludes the short-proper-time ultraviolet region.

= Closed-string scattering amplitude
{parent=Polyakov path integral}
{title2=$\mathcal A_{g,n}$}

A closed-string <scattering amplitude> is a worldsheet correlation function of external <string vertex operators>, integrated over their unfixed positions and <worldsheet moduli>, with the necessary <worldsheet ghost fields>.

= Worldsheet integration count after conformal gauge fixing
{parent=Closed-string scattering amplitude}
{title2=$d_g+n-k_g=3g-3+n$}

For a stable $n$-point <closed-string scattering amplitude> on a <genus>-$g$ surface, let $d_g$ count complex unmarked <worldsheet moduli> and $k_g$ count complex dimensions of the <worldsheet conformal Killing group>. Integrating the $n$ vertex positions and moduli, then fixing $k_g$ positions, leaves $d_g+n-k_g$ complex integrations. The <punctured Riemann surface moduli dimension> identifies this as $3g-3+n$. <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields> and antighost insertions implement the removed zero modes and the surviving modulus measure; they do not add independently integrated worldsheet coordinates.

= Sphere tachyon position integral
{parent=Closed-string scattering amplitude}

The tree-level <Polyakov path integral> for closed-string <tachyons> reduces in <conformal gauge> to their vertex-position integral on the <Riemann sphere>. Three positions are fixed by the residual <Möbius group>; the other $N-3$ complex positions are integrated. The embedding-field Gaussian integral produces <momentum conservation> and the <Koba-Nielsen factor>.

= Sphere gauge fixing for four string vertices
{parent=Closed-string scattering amplitude}

At genus zero the residual <Möbius transformation> group fixes three marked insertion points, leaving one complex position to integrate. The corresponding <worldsheet ghost fields> give $|z_{ij}z_{jk}z_{ki}|^2$ for the three fixed points. For on-shell weight-$(1,1)$ <string vertex operators>, these ghost factors cancel the coordinate dependence of the fixed-point normalization, including the limit at infinity. The remaining <Koba-Nielsen factor> for four tachyons is the integrand of the <complex beta integral>, producing the <Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude>.

= String dual resonance
{parent=Closed-string scattering amplitude}

A single <crossing-symmetric scattering amplitude> has equivalent pole expansions in different exchange channels. Its <scattering-amplitude factorization> reproduces the string spectrum and couplings in each channel without adding the channel expansions again.

= Worldsheet Green function
{parent=Polyakov path integral}

A worldsheet Green function inverts the scalar kinetic operator after its zero modes have been removed. In a locally flat complex coordinate it satisfies $-(\pi\alpha')^{-1}\partial\bar\partial G(z,w)=\delta^{(2)}(z-w)$ and is $G(z,w)=-(\alpha'/2)\log|z-w|^2$ up to harmonic and constant terms.

= String vertex operator
{parent=Polyakov path integral}

A string vertex operator inserts an asymptotic string state at a point of the worldsheet. Its conformal weights encode the physical-state and mass-shell conditions.

These insertions are described by the state-field correspondence of a <vertex operator algebra>; an individual insertion is not the whole algebra.

= Integrated string vertex operator
{parent=String vertex operator}

An integrated closed-string vertex is inserted as $\int d^2z\,V(z,\bar z)$. In conformal gauge its matter operator must have <conformal weights> $(1,1)$ so that the insertion is invariant under worldsheet coordinate changes.

= Massless closed-string vertex operator
{parent=Integrated string vertex operator}

At the first massless closed-string level, the matter vertex has the form $\epsilon_{\mu\nu}:\!\partial X^\mu\bar\partial X^\nu e^{ik\cdot X}\!:$, with null momentum and a polarization tensor transverse in both indices. Its symmetric trace-free, antisymmetric and trace sectors describe the graviton, <Kalb–Ramond field> and <dilaton>.

= Polarization tensor
{title2=$\epsilon_{\mu\nu}$}
{parent=Massless closed-string vertex operator}

= String-state gauge redundancy

= One-loop metric beta function of a string sigma model

= Leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition

= Dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity
{parent=Leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition}
{title2=$\Box_g\varphi-2|\nabla\varphi|^2=C$}

Diverging $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi=0$ and using the <contracted Bianchi identity> and <Ricci identity> gives $\nabla_b(R+4\Box_g\varphi-4|\nabla\varphi|^2)=0$. The additional dilaton anomaly coefficient fixes $C=(D-26)/(3\alpha')$ at leading order for the bosonic string without an antisymmetric background. In critical dimension it is zero; noncritical backgrounds have a central-charge-deficit term.

= Linear dilaton counterexample to zero integration constant

But $\Box\varphi-2|\nabla\varphi|^2=-2q^2$, which need not vanish. This demonstrates the integration constant left by the <contracted Bianchi identity>. Full quantum Weyl invariance fixes the allowed $q^2$ through the <central charge>, as in a <linear dilaton conformal field theory>; the metric equation alone does not.

= Pulsating circular string

A circular <closed string> contracts and re-expands with $X^0=Rt$ and $X^1+iX^2=R\cos t\,e^{i\sigma}$. Its <proper length> is $2\pi R|\cos t|$, while its <energy> remains $2\pi TR$. = Target-space Noether charges of a string

Translations and <Lorentz transformations> give <Noether charges> $\mathcal P_m=\int P_m\,d\sigma$ and $\mathcal J_{mn}=\int(X_mP_n-X_nP_m)\,d\sigma$. Their time derivatives are boundary fluxes, which vanish for a <closed string> or a free-ended <open string>.

The two constraints are $P^2+T^2X'^2=0$ and $P\cdot X'=0$. They are <first-class constraints> associated with <worldsheet diffeomorphisms>. Their <Lagrange multipliers> are arbitrary gauge functions rather than propagating fields.

= Chiral constraint algebra of a closed string
{parent=Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints}
{title2=$\mathrm{Diff}_1\oplus\mathrm{Diff}_1$}

For a <closed string>, the currents $J_\pm=P\pm TX'$ have vanishing mixed <Poisson brackets>. The densities $\mathcal H_\pm=J_\pm^2/(4T)$ therefore generate commuting constraint algebras. = Rotating Nambu–Goto string
{parent=Nambu–Goto action}

A straight rigidly rotating classical string has <speed of light> at a free endpoint. Its <energy> and <angular momentum> satisfy a <Regge trajectory>. A segment with one anchored and one free end has $J=E^2/(\pi T)$, twice the slope of the string with two free ends.

= Free-ended straight-string Regge relation
{parent=Rotating Nambu–Goto string}
{title2=$J=\alpha'M^2$}

With <string tension> $T=1/(2\pi\alpha')$, the free-ended <open string> $X^0=a\tau$, $X^1+iX^2=a\cos\sigma\,e^{i\tau}$, $0\leq\sigma\leq\pi$, is straight at every target time. Its <Nambu–Goto equations of motion> and <Virasoro constraints> hold, and its ends move at light speed. Integrating the <target-space Noether charges of a string> gives $M=\pi Ta$ and $J=\pi Ta^2/2$. Thus $J=\alpha'M^2$. Extending the same map to $0\leq\sigma\leq2\pi$ describes a double-covered folded <closed string>, with twice the mass and twice the <angular momentum>, hence $J=\alpha'M^2/2$. The different slopes record the different boundary topology.

= Rigid circular string in four spatial dimensions
{parent=Rotating Nambu–Goto string}
{title2=$X=A(t)\cos\sigma+B(t)\sin\sigma$}

If $A(t)$ and $B(t)$ are perpendicular four-dimensional spatial vectors of equal fixed magnitude, every constant-time slice is a planar circle. Opposite rotations in two orthogonal coordinate planes can produce a <Nambu–Goto action> solution with constant radius and a rotating plane. The <induced worldsheet metric> can be constant even though the embedding moves. Rotating Cartesian axes clarify its shape without removing its kinetic target-space energy.

= Straight rotating string with one fixed endpoint
{parent=Rotating Nambu–Goto string}
{title2=$\omega L=1$}

A <Nambu–Goto action> solution with $X^0=t$ is $\boldsymbol X=L\sin(\sigma/L)(\cos(t/L),\sin(t/L))$, for $0\leq\sigma\leq\pi L/2$. It solves the <wave equation>, has a fixed endpoint at the origin, and obeys the <free-end string boundary condition> at the other end. Its <proper length> is $L$ and its free endpoint has angular speed $1/L$ and spatial speed one.

= Two-dimensional conformal field theory
{title2=2D CFT}
{parent=Worldsheet}
{wiki}

A two-dimensional conformal field theory is invariant under local holomorphic and antiholomorphic conformal transformations.

= Global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}

For <primary operators> of holomorphic weights $h_i$ and a <Möbius transformation> invariant vacuum, infinitesimal translations, dilations and special conformal transformations give $\sum_i\partial_iG=0$, $\sum_i(z_i\partial_i+h_i)G=0$, and $\sum_i(z_i^2\partial_i+2h_i z_i)G=0$. Solving gives a constant one-point function only at $h=0$, a two-point function $Cz_{12}^{-2h}$ only for equal weights, and a three-point function $Cz_{12}^{h_3-h_1-h_2}z_{13}^{h_2-h_1-h_3}z_{23}^{h_1-h_2-h_3}$. Three-point covariance imposes no further equality of weights; dynamical selection rules may still force $C=0$. For more points, arbitrary functions of <conformal cross-ratios> remain.

= Liouville field theory
{c}
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}
{wiki}
{title2=$S_L=\frac1{4\pi}\int\sqrt h[(\nabla\phi)^2+QR\phi+4\pi\mu e^{2b\phi}]$}

<Liouville field theory> is a two-dimensional scalar theory with a curvature background charge and an exponential interaction. In the usual spacelike normalization, quantum conformal invariance gives $Q=b+b^{-1}$ and <central charge> $c_L=1+6Q^2$. When a noncritical <string worldsheet> conformal factor is retained as a dynamical field, its effective action has this form and its <central charge> combines with the matter and <worldsheet ghost field> charges. It is closely related to a <background-charge scalar field>, but its exponential potential supplies an additional interaction.

= Radial quantization
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}

<Radial quantization> treats circles around the origin as equal-time slices and logarithmic radius as Euclidean time. A local insertion at the origin prepares a state on a surrounding circle. Contours of the stress tensor act on that insertion, turning its singular <operator product expansion> into the <Virasoro algebra> action on the corresponding state.

= Quasi-primary operator
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}
{title2=$(h,\widetilde h)$}

A <quasi-primary operator> transforms with weights $(h,\widetilde h)$ under global conformal, or Möbius, transformations. A <primary operator> has this covariance under every locally invertible holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinate change. In <radial quantization>, quasi-primary states are annihilated by $L_1,\widetilde L_1$, whereas <primary states> are annihilated by every positive Virasoro mode. The stress tensor at nonzero <central charge> is quasi-primary but not primary because its transformation under a general coordinate change includes a Schwarzian term.

= Free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}
{title2=$h_\psi=1/2,\quad c=1/2$}

A real chiral <Majorana fermion> with contraction $\psi(z)\psi(w)\sim1/(z-w)$ has stress tensor $T=-:\psi\partial\psi:/2$. Fermionic <Wick contractions> give $T(z)\psi(w)\sim\psi(w)/(2(z-w)^2)+\partial\psi(w)/(z-w)$. Double contractions in $TT$ give $1/(4(z-w)^4)$, so the <central charge> is $1/2$. Fermion bilinears of different species are weight-one currents; equal-species undifferentiated bilinears vanish by antisymmetry.

= Ising Virasoro modules from a Majorana fermion
{c}
{parent=Free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory}
{title2=$h=0,\;1/2,\;1/16$}

A chiral <Majorana fermion> with modes $\{b_r,b_s\}=\delta_{r+s,0}$ and $b_r^\dagger=b_{-r}$ has $T=-:\psi\partial\psi:/2$ and <central charge> $1/2$. The even and odd fermion-number parts of its <Neveu–Schwarz sector> give unitary modules with lowest weights $0$ and $1/2$. In its <Ramond sector>, a choice of ground-state zero-mode eigenvalue gives the module of weight $1/16$; the other choice gives an isomorphic <Virasoro algebra> module. Their cyclic highest-weight submodules are irreducible: a positive-energy invariant proper submodule would have a lowest vector orthogonal to every descendant of the original highest vector, which is impossible in a positive inner-product cyclic module.

= Beta-gamma system
{title2=$\beta\gamma$ system}
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}

A beta-gamma system is a pair of commuting chiral fields with first-order action $\int\beta\bar\partial\gamma$. If $\gamma$ has weight $h$ and $\beta$ has weight $1-h$, its central charge is $2(6h^2-6h+1)$.

= Stress tensor of a commuting beta-gamma system
{parent=Beta-gamma system}
{title2=$T=(1-\lambda):\partial\beta\,\gamma:-\lambda:\beta\,\partial\gamma:,\quad c=12\lambda^2-12\lambda+2$}

Using the <radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions>, single contractions give $T(z)\beta(w)\sim\lambda\beta(w)/(z-w)^2+\partial\beta(w)/(z-w)$ and the corresponding weight $1-\lambda$ for $\gamma$. Put $A=:\partial\beta\,\gamma:$ and $B=:\beta\,\partial\gamma:$. Their double-contraction coefficients in $AA,BB,AB,BA$ are respectively $1,1,2,2$. The fourth-order pole of $TT$ is therefore $(1-\lambda)^2+\lambda^2-4\lambda(1-\lambda)=6\lambda^2-6\lambda+1$, which equals $c/2$. There is no anticommuting Wick sign.

= Radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions
{parent=Beta-gamma system}
{title2=$\beta(z)\gamma(w)\sim-(z-w)^{-1},\quad\gamma(z)\beta(w)\sim+(z-w)^{-1}$}

Commutation allows exchanging the factors at their existing arguments; it does not allow exchanging their arguments while retaining the same pole sign. With the displayed convention, differentiation produces $\partial\beta(z)\gamma(w)\sim(z-w)^{-2}$ and $\partial\gamma(z)\beta(w)\sim-(z-w)^{-2}$. These signs are essential for the <conformal weights> and <central charge> of a commuting <beta-gamma system>.

= Stress-tensor operator-product expansion

The fourth-order pole is the central extension, while the remaining poles express that $T$ has conformal weight two.

= Virasoro algebra

The Virasoro algebra is the central extension of the algebra of holomorphic vector fields on a circle. Its generators are the Laurent modes of the holomorphic stress-energy tensor, and its central term is controlled by the <central charge> $c$.

= Virasoro character
{c}
{parent=Virasoro algebra}

A <Virasoro character> records the graded dimensions of a positive-energy <Virasoro algebra> module. A state of weight $h+N$ contributes $q^{h+N-c/24}$, and its multiplicity is the dimension of that energy eigenspace. The shift accounts for the <Casimir energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory>. For a <Virasoro Verma module>, the character is $q^{h-c/24}\prod_{n\ge1}(1-q^n)^{-1}$, by counting the ordered descendant basis.

= Virasoro Verma module

The <Virasoro Verma module> $M(c,h)$ is the universal <highest-weight representation> with <central charge> $c$ and ground weight $h$. The <Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem> gives a basis $L_{-n_1}\cdots L_{-n_s}|h\rangle$ with $n_1\ge\cdots\ge n_s>0$. Its invariant Hermitian form is fixed by $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$ and the norm of the ground vector. Quotienting by its maximal proper submodule produces the irreducible highest-weight module; in the unitary cases this removes the null vectors.

= Level-two Virasoro descendant Gram matrix
{parent=Virasoro Verma module}
{title2=$G_2=\begin{pmatrix}4h+c/2&6h\\6h&4h(2h+1)\end{pmatrix}$}

For a normalized <highest-weight vector> with $L_0|h\rangle=h|h\rangle$, $L_{n>0}|h\rangle=0$ and $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$, the level-two descendants $L_{-2}|h\rangle,L_{-1}^2|h\rangle$ have the displayed <Gram matrix>. It follows from the <Virasoro algebra>: $\langle L_2L_{-2}\rangle=4h+c/2$, $\langle L_2L_{-1}^2\rangle=6h$ and $\langle L_1^2L_{-1}^2\rangle=4h(2h+1)$. At $h=-1,c=26$ its null direction is $(1,3/2)$, giving the <level-two scalar Virasoro null state>. Nullity of a descendant is a metric statement; satisfying all physical constraints must be checked separately.

= Unitary highest-weight Virasoro module

= Unitary highest-weight Virasoro representation
{synonym}

A <unitary highest-weight Virasoro module> is generated by a normalized vector satisfying the displayed relations, with a positive-definite <inner product> and $L_n^\dagger=L_{-n}$. Its <Virasoro descendants> have $L_0$ eigenvalues $h+N$ for nonnegative integral levels $N$. The commutator gives $\|L_{-1}|h\rangle\|^2=2h$, so unitarity requires $h\ge0$. Such a module is a quotient of a <Virasoro Verma module>; its <unitary Virasoro discrete series> describes the cases with <central charge> below one.

= Sugawara construction
{c}
{parent=Virasoro algebra}
{wiki}
{title2=$L_m=(2k+Q)^{-1}\sum_{a,p}:J^a_{m-p}J^a_p:$}

For a restricted representation of an affine current algebra with $[J_m^a,J_n^b]=if^{abc}J_{m+n}^c+km\delta^{ab}\delta_{m+n,0}$ and $f^{abc}f^{abd}=Q\delta^{cd}$, the displayed <normal ordering> construction is defined when $2k+Q\ne0$. Moving the currents past each other gives $[L_m,J_n^a]=-nJ_{m+n}^a$; the additional $Q$ in the denominator is the adjoint-Casimir reordering term. The resulting <Virasoro algebra> has <central charge> $c=k\dim\mathfrak g/(k+Q/2)$. On a compact unitary positive-energy module, $L_m^\dagger=L_{-m}$.

= Coset construction
{c}
{parent=Sugawara construction}
{wiki}
{title2=$L_n^{\mathfrak g/\mathfrak h}=L_n^{\mathfrak g}-L_n^{\mathfrak h}$}

For a current subalgebra $\mathfrak h\subset\mathfrak g$, its currents have conformal weight one under both <Sugawara construction> stress tensors. Their difference therefore commutes with all $\mathfrak h$ currents and with the $\mathfrak h$ stress tensor. It obeys the <Virasoro algebra> with <central charge> $c_{\mathfrak g}-c_{\mathfrak h}$. For a positive-level compact unitary vacuum representation, $\|L_{-2}^{\mathfrak g/\mathfrak h}|0\rangle\|^2=(c_{\mathfrak g}-c_{\mathfrak h})/2\ge0$. A <Cartan subalgebra> has $c_{\mathfrak h}=\operatorname{rank}\mathfrak g$, giving the lower bound on $c_{\mathfrak g}$ under these hypotheses.

= Unitary Virasoro discrete series

An irreducible unitary <highest weight> module of the <Virasoro algebra> with $c<1$ has integer $m\ge2$, $1\le r\le m-1$, $1\le s\le m$, and the displayed weights. = Virasoro generator
{c}
{parent=Virasoro algebra}
{title2=$L_n=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint dz\,z^{n+1}T(z)$}

<Virasoro generators> are the contour modes of the holomorphic stress tensor in a <two-dimensional conformal field theory>. Their commutators are $[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+c(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}/12$. = Virasoro zero-mode shift

= Free-boson Virasoro central term

For $d$ free target coordinates, <normal ordering> of $L_m=\sum_n:\alpha_{m-n}\cdot\alpha_n:/2$ gives a matter <central charge> $d$. = Canonical supercurrent bracket of free RNS matter

The <distributional derivative> identity $f(\sigma,\sigma')\delta'=f(\sigma,\sigma)\delta'+(\partial_{\sigma'}f)(\sigma,\sigma)\delta$, with a vanishing coincident Grassmann bilinear, converts this to $\pi\delta\theta_{++}$. Use the chiral <equation of motion> to identify $\partial_\sigma\psi_1=\partial_+\psi_1$. Quantum <normal ordering> adds a central contact term proportional to $\delta''$, the local form of the <N=1 super-Virasoro algebra> central extension.

= Linear dilaton conformal field theory

A linear dilaton conformal field theory improves the free-boson stress tensor by a second derivative, $T=-\alpha'^{-1}:\!\partial X\partial X\!:-q\partial^2X$. The improvement shifts the <central charge> to $c=1+6\alpha'q^2$ and corresponds to a dilaton linear in the target coordinate $X$.

= Background-charge scalar field

A background-charge scalar field is a free scalar coupled linearly to the worldsheet Ricci scalar. This curvature coupling produces the improved stress tensor of a <linear dilaton conformal field theory>.

For a noncompact free boson with target volume $V$, the torus partition function is $Z=V[2\pi\sqrt{\operatorname{Im}\tau}]^{-1}|\eta(\tau)|^{-2}$ in the normalization where the zero-mode Hamiltonian is $p^2/2$.

= Compact boson
{parent=Free boson conformal field theory}
{wiki}

A compact boson takes values on a circle, $X\sim X+2\pi R$. Its states carry quantized momentum $n/R$ and integer winding $wR$.

= Momentum and winding modes
{title2=$(n,w)$}
{parent=Compact boson}

For a compact boson of radius $R$, momentum number $n$ and winding number $w$ combine into left- and right-moving zero-mode momenta $p_L=n/R+wR$ and $p_R=n/R-wR$ in a common normalization.

= Exceptional massless ground states of a bosonic circle
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{title2=$R=|n|\sqrt{\alpha'}/2\ \text{or}\ R=2\sqrt{\alpha'}/|w|$}

In the critical <closed bosonic string>, compactification of one coordinate gives $M^2=n^2/R^2+w^2R^2/\alpha'^2+2(N+\widetilde N-2)/\alpha'$ and $\widetilde N-N+nw=0$. At oscillator level zero, level matching forces $nw=0$. Thus a pure <momentum> <ground state> becomes a massless <scalar field> at $R=|n|\sqrt{\alpha'}/2$, while a pure winding <ground state> does so at $R=2\sqrt{\alpha'}/|w|$. These are threshold states descended from the bosonic <tachyon>; they are additional to the neutral <graviton>, two-form, vectors, <dilaton> and radius modulus. At the <self-dual circle>, the four charges $(\pm2,0),(0,\pm2)$ contribute to the enhanced massless <scalar field> spectrum.

= Compact-circle closed-string level matching
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{title2=$\widetilde N-N+nw=0$}

For a compact string coordinate of radius $R$, choose $p_L=n/R+wR/\alpha'$ and $p_R=n/R-wR/\alpha'$, where the left-moving oscillators are denoted by tildes. The two classical zero-mode <Virasoro constraints> give $M^2=p_L^2+4\widetilde N/\alpha'=p_R^2+4N/\alpha'$. Their difference yields the displayed shifted <closed-string level matching> condition. Their average is $M^2=(n/R)^2+(wR/\alpha')^2+2(N+\widetilde N)/\alpha'$. Classically the center-of-mass momentum can be continuous; the integer momentum number belongs to compact-coordinate quantization. Quantum intercepts shift the mass formula but cancel out of level matching.

= Massless spectrum at the bosonic self-dual circle
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{title2=$2(N+\widetilde N-2)+n^2+w^2=0$}

The <self-dual circle> mass equation and <closed-string level matching> exhaust the possibilities at level sum zero, one or two. Besides the usual neutral $(N,\widetilde N)=(1,1)$ states, charges $(n,w)=(\pm1,\pm1)$ supply singly excited chiral states, with the sector fixed by level matching. Ground-state charges $(\pm2,0)$ and $(0,\pm2)$ also become massless. Their compact energy cancels the uncompactified <tachyon> mass. With $d$ physical transverse components, these families have $d^2+4d+4$ independent polarizations.

= T-duality
{c}
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{wiki}

T-duality identifies a compact boson of radius $R$ with one of reciprocal radius by exchanging momentum and winding numbers. Factors of $\alpha'$ restore the dimensional relation $R\leftrightarrow\alpha'/R$.

= T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions
{c}
{parent=T-duality}
{title2=$N\leftrightarrow D$}

= D-brane T-duality
{c}
{synonym}

For a compact <string embedding map> coordinate, <T-duality> preserves its left-moving part and reverses its right-moving part. Consequently $\partial_\tau\widetilde X=\partial_\sigma X$ and $\partial_\sigma\widetilde X=\partial_\tau X$. A <Neumann boundary condition> becomes a <Dirichlet boundary condition> and conversely. A <D-brane> wrapping the circle loses one spatial worldvolume dimension; a <D-brane> transverse to it gains one in the dual description.

= Brane separation becomes a Wilson line
{parent=T-duality of D-brane boundary conditions}
{title2=$A_i=\frac{y_i}{2\pi\alpha'}$}

Under <T-duality>, the positions $y_i$ of <D-branes> transverse to a circle of radius $R$ become diagonal <Wilson lines> on branes wrapping the dual circle $R'=\alpha'/R$. Their holonomies are $\exp(i2\pi R'A_i)=\exp(iy_i/R)$. An <open string stretched between D-branes> with displacement $y_j-y_i+2\pi Rw$ becomes a string with shifted compact momentum $w/R'+A_j-A_i$. The stretching contribution and the shifted-momentum contribution to its squared mass are identical.

= Global qualifications of Abelian T-duality
{parent=T-duality}

A local <Buscher procedure> sets a flat auxiliary connection equal to a coordinate derivative only on a simply connected patch. A full compact-circle <Polyakov path integral> must also sum flat-connection holonomies, match the period of the dual multiplier, and include <momentum and winding modes>. A free circle isometry then gives the radius relation $R'=\alpha'/R$. Fixed points, noncompact orbits and global identifications require additional analysis; a local spacelike <Killing vector> does not specify these data.

= Self-dual circle compactification
{parent=T-duality}
{title2=$R=\sqrt{\alpha'}$}

= Self-dual circle
{synonym}

A circle compactification is self-dual when the radius is unchanged by the <T-duality> map $R\mapsto\alpha'/R$. Momentum and winding then enter the <bosonic string mass spectrum> with equal weights. Special charge sectors can become massless at this radius.

= Self-dual circle current algebra
{parent=Self-dual circle compactification}
{title2=$SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$}

At $R=\sqrt{\alpha'}$, a chiral <compact boson> has <operator product expansion> $Y_L(z)Y_L(w)\sim-\alpha'\log(z-w)/2$. The currents $J^3=i\partial Y_L/\sqrt{\alpha'}$ and $J^\pm=:\!e^{\pm2iY_L/\sqrt{\alpha'}}\!:$ have <conformal weight> one. Contractions give $J^3(z)J^3(w)\sim1/[2(z-w)^2]$, $J^3(z)J^\pm(w)\sim\pm J^\pm(w)/(z-w)$ and $J^+(z)J^-(w)\sim1/(z-w)^2+2J^3(w)/(z-w)$. These are the level-one <SU(2) Lie algebra> currents. The right-moving copy supplies a second factor. Combining a current with a noncompact coordinate derivative gives a massless <string vertex operator> for each of the six vectors; four are additional to the generic two Cartan vectors.

= Radius deformation as a Higgs mechanism
{parent=Self-dual circle compactification}
{title2=$m_W^2=(R/\alpha'-1/R)^2$}

At the <self-dual circle>, left and right dimension-one currents generate $SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$. The compact-coordinate vertex $\partial Y_L\bar\partial Y_R$ is a Cartan–Cartan component of a <scalar field> in the $(3,3)$ representation. Its <expectation value> changes the circle radius and leaves $U(1)_L\times U(1)_R$ unbroken. The four extra charged vectors acquire the displayed mass, and four <scalar field> polarizations become their longitudinal modes. This realizes the <Higgs mechanism> geometrically. In a bosonic string the ever-present <tachyon> and additional <scalar field> instabilities prevent interpreting the whole family as a stable vacuum.

= Buscher procedure
{c}
{parent=T-duality}

The Buscher procedure derives T-dual backgrounds by replacing the derivative of an isometric target coordinate with an independent worldsheet one-form and imposing its flatness with a Lagrange multiplier. Integrating out either field yields the original or dual sigma model.

= First-order action for Abelian worldsheet duality
{parent=Buscher procedure}
{title2=$\tfrac12VA_\mu A^\mu+\widetilde z\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu A_\nu$}

= First-order worldsheet duality action
{synonym}

Replace the derivative of an isometric coordinate by an independent one-form $A$ and impose $dA=0$ with a <Lagrange multiplier>. In signature $(-,+)$ with $\epsilon^{\tau\sigma}=1$, its equation is $VA^\nu=\epsilon^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\widetilde z$. Eliminating $A$ from the full action, including the multiplier term, gives $\tfrac12V^{-1}\partial_\mu\widetilde z\partial^\mu\widetilde z$. Omitting the multiplier term during substitution would give the wrong sign. This derives the block-diagonal metric inversion in the <Buscher rules>.

= Buscher dilaton shift
{parent=Buscher procedure}
{c}
{title2=$\varphi'=\varphi-\frac12\log g_{yy}$}

The quantum measure in abelian <T-duality> changes the conventional <dilaton> by $\varphi'=\varphi-\frac12\log g_{yy}$ in alpha-prime-one units for a spacelike isometry. The regulated Gaussian integration over the auxiliary gauge field produces this curvature coupling. It is consistent with invariance of the string-frame density $e^{-2\varphi}\sqrt{\det g}$ under the local Buscher rules.

= Polar-coordinate T-dual background
{parent=Buscher dilaton shift}
{title2=$d\widetilde s^2=dr^2+r^{-2}d\widetilde z^2,\ \widetilde\Phi=-\log r$}

The flat polar metric $dr^2+r^2dz^2$ has a circle isometry away from $r=0$. Its local <Buscher rules> give the displayed dual metric and <dilaton>. The dual <Ricci tensor> has components $R_{rr}=-2/r^2$ and $R_{\widetilde z\widetilde z}=-2/r^4$, while the dilaton Hessian has components $1/r^2$ and $1/r^4$. Thus the <leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition> $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi=0$ holds despite nonzero curvature. Flat spectators complete a critical target; the scalar anomaly condition also holds because $|\nabla\Phi|^2=1/r^2$, $\Box\Phi=2/r^2$ and $R=-4/r^2$. The construction is local on $r>0$, excluding the degenerating circle.

= Buscher rules
{c}
{parent=Buscher procedure}

The Buscher rules invert the metric component along an Abelian isometry and transform the other metric and B-field components. The Gaussian functional determinant also shifts the dilaton; when the metric is block diagonal with $G_{zz}=V$, $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi-\tfrac12\log V$.

= Buscher dual of a metric with vanishing two-form
{parent=Buscher rules}
{c}
{title2=$g'_{ij}=g_{ij}-g_{yi}g_{yj}/g_{yy}$}

For a nondegenerate isometry direction $y$ and initially zero two-form, the local T-dual fields in alpha-prime-one units are $g'_{yy}=1/g_{yy}$, $g'_{yi}=0$, $g'_{ij}=g_{ij}-g_{yi}g_{yj}/g_{yy}$, $B'_{yi}=g_{yi}/g_{yy}$ and $B'_{ij}=0$. Gauging the isometry and eliminating its auxiliary connection derives these rules. The spectator metric is a <Schur complement>. The sign of the mixed two-form follows the multiplier and dual-coordinate orientation convention.

= String oscillator
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

The Fourier modes $\alpha_n^i$ and $\widetilde\alpha_n^i$ of the transverse closed-string coordinates obey harmonic-oscillator commutation relations. Modes with negative index create string excitations.

= Oscillator vacuum
{parent=String oscillator}
{title2=$\lvert0\rangle$}

= Oscillator vacua
{synonym}

An oscillator vacuum is annihilated by all positive-frequency oscillator annihilation operators. For a <Ramond sector> string this leaves a degenerate space on which the fermionic zero modes act through the <Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra>. Vacuum annihilation therefore does not select a unique spinor state.

= Half-integer open-string oscillator
{parent=String oscillator}
{title2=$\alpha_r^i,\quad r\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12$}

For a <Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition>, the transverse modes have frequencies $r\in\mathbb Z+1/2$. The <canonical commutation relations> give $[\alpha_r^i,\alpha_s^j]=r\delta^{ij}\delta_{r+s,0}$ and $\alpha_r^\dagger=\alpha_{-r}$. One derivation expands $X-y=\sum_{r>0}q_r\cos(r\sigma)$: spatial <orthogonality> reduces the <Polyakov action> to $(8\alpha')^{-1}\int\sum_r(\dot q_r^2-r^2q_r^2)d\tau$. These are <harmonic oscillators> of <mass> $1/(4\alpha')$, whose normalized <creation operators> satisfy $\alpha_{-r}=i\sqrt r\,a_r^\dagger$ in the conventional phased expansion.

= String level operator
{title2=$N$}
{parent=String oscillator}

The string level operator is the weighted oscillator number $N=\sum_{n>0}\alpha_{-n}\mathbin\cdot\alpha_n$. A closed string has independent operators $N$ and $\widetilde N$ for its two chiral sectors.

= Integer string oscillator level
{parent=String level operator}
{title2=$N=\sum_{k,i}k r_{ki}$}

The <canonical commutation relations> imply $[N,\alpha_{-k}^i]=k\alpha_{-k}^i$ for $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. With zero level assigned to the <oscillator vacuum>, a <Fock space> basis state containing $r_{ki}$ <creation operators> of each mode has level $\sum_{k,i}kr_{ki}$. The level is therefore a nonnegative integer; it counts oscillator excitation weighted by frequency, not simply the number of creation operators.

= Closed-string level matching
{parent=String oscillator}

Closed-string periodicity and the residual spatial constraint require equal left- and right-moving excitation levels, $N=\widetilde N$.

= Normal-ordering constant of a string
{title2=$a$}
{parent=String oscillator}

= String intercept
{synonym}

The normal-ordering constant is the zero-point shift in the quantum Virasoro generator. Closure of the target-space Lorentz algebra fixes $a=1$ and the spacetime dimension $D=26$ for the bosonic string.

= Critical-vector assumption in the string intercept argument
{parent=Normal-ordering constant of a string}
{title2=$M_1^2=(1-a)/\alpha'$}

In the usual critical Lorentz-invariant bosonic string, level-one transverse vector polarizations fit a massless representation, whereas a massive vector would require an extra polarization. This fixes the intercept to one. Polarization counting alone does not establish that conclusion in every dimension: in three dimensions the one transverse polarization transforms trivially under the transverse rotation group and can be interpreted as a scalar. The quantum consistency assumptions must accompany an argument that the first level must be massless.

= Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy
{c}
{parent=Normal-ordering constant of a string}
{title2=$E_0=d/48$}

Each of $d$ physical transverse <bosons> with a <Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition> contributes $\tfrac12\sum_{r>0}r=1/48$ by the <half-integer zeta-regularized mode sum>. Thus $L_0^\perp=N+d/48$ and the convention $L_0=\alpha'p^2+N-a$ has $a=-d/48$. For $d=24$, the vacuum shift is $+1/2$. The ordinary integer-moded open-string shift is instead $-d/24$; these endpoint sectors have different normal-ordering constants.

= Lowest levels of a fully transverse ND bosonic string
{parent=Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy}

With 24 physical <half-integer open-string oscillators>, the first three <string level operator> <eigenvalues> are $0,1/2,1$. Their states are the vacuum, $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}|0\rangle$, and $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}a_{1/2}^{j\dagger}|0\rangle$. The <creation operators> commute, so the third level is the <symmetric square> of the 24-dimensional transverse vector, of dimension $24\cdot25/2=300$. Its trace is a scalar and its trace-free part has dimension 299. Including the vacuum shift gives $\alpha'M^2=1/2,1,3/2$ when the two longitudinal directions are common directions with <Neumann boundary conditions>. The representation labels refer to the surviving ND rotation group, not full unbroken target Lorentz symmetry.

= Bosonic string mass spectrum
{parent=String oscillator}

For a closed bosonic string,
$$
M^2=\frac4{\alpha'}(N-a)=\frac4{\alpha'}(\widetilde N-a),
\qquad N=\widetilde N.
$$

= Closed-bosonic-string mass shell from primary weights
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$M^2=4(N-1)/\alpha',\quad N=\widetilde N$}

A matter vertex with left and right oscillator levels $N,\widetilde N$ and momentum $k$ has weights $N+\alpha'k^2/4,\widetilde N+\alpha'k^2/4$. An <integrated string vertex operator> must have weights $(1,1)$, giving the displayed mass shell and level matching with $M^2=-k^2$. Physical states are further identified modulo <null string states>, equivalently through <BRST cohomology>; merely counting all weight-one primaries would include gauge redundancies.

= Massive two-form at closed-string level two
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$M^2=8\pi T$}

Each level-two chiral sector of the critical <closed string> is the <symmetric traceless square> $S^2_0V$ of the massive <little group> vector space. The <tensor product> of its two sectors contains the <exterior square> $\Lambda^2V$: the equivariant map $(S,T)\mapsto ST-TS$ is onto the antisymmetric matrices. Compact-group complete reducibility realizes this quotient as a subrepresentation. It describes a <massive two-form field> with $M^2=8\pi T=4/\alpha'$.

= Massless first closed-string level
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$N=\widetilde N=1,\quad M^2=0$}

In the critical <bosonic string theory>, the states $\alpha_{-1}^i\widetilde\alpha_{-1}^j|0;p\rangle$ have $M^2=0$. Their coefficient matrix decomposes into a symmetric trace-free tensor, an antisymmetric tensor, and a scalar trace, representing the <graviton>, <Kalb–Ramond field>, and <dilaton>. Their transverse <particle polarization> counts add to $(D-2)^2$. The <normal-ordering constant of a string> is one.

= Open bosonic string mass spectrum
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$\alpha'M^2=N-a$}

The <string level operator> for a free-ended <open string> is $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. The quantum condition is $\alpha'M^2=N-a$, where $a$ is the <normal-ordering constant of a string>. Lorentz-consistent <bosonic string theory> has $a=1$.

= Lowest light-cone levels of an open bosonic string
{parent=Open bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$N=0,1,2$}

The first levels are the <oscillator vacuum>, $\alpha_{-1}^I|0\rangle$, and $\alpha_{-2}^I|0\rangle,\alpha_{-1}^I\alpha_{-1}^J|0\rangle$. At level two the transverse <vector representation> and <symmetric square> assemble into the massive <symmetric traceless square> of one extra rotation dimension.

= Critical dimension of string theory
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

= Critical dimension of the bosonic string
{synonym}

The critical dimension is the target-spacetime dimension in which quantum anomalies obstructing Lorentz or Weyl symmetry cancel. It is 26 for the bosonic string.

Here critical means cancellation of the string’s quantum anomalies; it does not mean the upper or lower critical dimension of a phase transition.

= Bosonic-string critical dimension from ghost descendants
{parent=Critical dimension of string theory}
{title2=$L_2L_{-2}|\Omega\rangle=(2\alpha_0^2+D/2-17)|\Omega\rangle$}

For the <FP ghost> vacuum annihilated by $b_{n\ge0}$ and $c_{n>0}$, the <FP ghost> level-one and level-two descendant coefficients are $-2$ and $-17$. Matter contributes $\alpha_0^2$ and $2\alpha_0^2+D/2$. <Virasoro closure from BRST nilpotence> at level one fixes the total highest weight to $\alpha_0^2/2-1$; level two then requires $D=26$. Imposing ground-state BRST closure gives $\alpha_0^2=2$ and the open bosonic <tachyon>. Interpret the coefficients with the stated <ghost zero-mode pairing>.

= Critical dimension of the RNS superstring
{parent=Critical dimension of string theory}
{title2=$D=10$}

Each of $D$ embedding <bosons> contributes one to the chiral <central charge>, and each real <worldsheet Majorana fermion> contributes one half. The reparameterization <bc system> contributes $-26$, and the <superconformal ghosts> contribute $+11$. Vanishing total <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> therefore requires $3D/2-26+11=0$, or $D=10$. This differs from the critical dimension 26 of the bosonic string, which has no worldsheet <fermions> or superconformal ghosts.

= Massless chiral RNS spectrum after GSO projection
{parent=Critical dimension of the RNS superstring}
{title2=$8_v\oplus8_{s\text{ or }c}$}

In one critical chiral <RNS string> sector, <light-cone gauge in string theory> leaves eight transverse directions. The <Neveu–Schwarz sector> massless states $b_{-1/2}^i|0;k\rangle$ give eight spacetime bosonic polarizations. The <Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra> gives sixteen ground-state polarizations, split into two eight-dimensional spinor chiralities. The supersymmetric <GSO projection> keeps one chirality, giving eight spacetime fermionic polarizations. Without that projection the massless counts do not agree. Counting a chiral sector is distinct from tensoring left and right sectors into closed-string states.

= Koba-Nielsen factor
{c}
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

The Koba-Nielsen factor is the product of powers of pairwise worldsheet insertion-point distances produced by contractions of string vertex operators.

= Möbius invariance of on-shell closed-string vertex integrals
{c}
{parent=Koba-Nielsen factor}
{title2=$\alpha'p_i^2=4$}

Under $z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)$ with $ad-bc=1$, the coordinate measure contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-4}$ at insertion $i$. The <Koba-Nielsen factor> contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-\alpha'\sum_{j\ne i}p_i\cdot p_j}$. <Four-momentum conservation> makes this exponent $\alpha'p_i^2$, so the factors cancel precisely on the displayed <tachyon> mass shell. The correlator alone is covariant; its product with all the coordinate measures is invariant.

= Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude
{c}
{parent=Koba-Nielsen factor}
{wiki}

The Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude is the crossing-symmetric tree-level four-point amplitude of closed-string tachyons. Its worldsheet integral is a complex beta integral, and its poles display the closed-string spectrum.

= Fixed-angle softness of the closed-string amplitude
{parent=Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude}
{title2=$\log|A|\sim-2s f(a)$}

For $t\simeq-as$, $u\simeq-(1-a)s$ with $0<a<1$, the smooth high-energy envelope satisfies $\log|A|\simeq-2s[-a\log a-(1-a)\log(1-a)]$. The <Gamma function> asymptotics cancel the leading $s\log s$ terms, leaving exponential suppression. Exact real-axis resonance poles must be excluded or treated by analytic continuation.

= Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude pole residue
{c}
{parent=Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude}
{title2=$s_n=4(n-1)/\alpha'$}

For $a=-1-\alpha's/4$, $b=-1-\alpha't/4$ and $a+b+c=1$, the amplitude has generic <simple poles> at $a=-n$. The <gamma function> <residue> and recurrence give
$$
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=s_n}A^{(4)}=-\frac{8\pi g_s^2}{\alpha'(n!)^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(b-j)^2.
$$
The <residue> polynomial has degree $2n$, consistent with closed-string exchange up to spin $2n$. When $a=-n+\delta_a$ and $b=-m+\delta_b$, the reciprocal factor $1/\Gamma(a+b)$ removes a putative double pole: the leading ratio is $\binom{n+m}{n}^2(1/\delta_a+1/\delta_b)$. Thus channel singularities must be counted using the kinematic constraint, not by multiplying independent numerator poles.

= CHY formalism

The Cachazo-He-Yuan formalism expresses tree-level scattering amplitudes as integrals localized on scattering equations.

= Scattering equation

\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{p_i\cdot p_j}{z_i-z_j}=0.
$$
