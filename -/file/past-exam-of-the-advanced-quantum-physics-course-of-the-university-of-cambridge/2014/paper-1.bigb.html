The normalized <quantum harmonic oscillator> <ground state> has $|A|^2=\sqrt{m\omega/(\pi\hbar)}$ and <energy> $E_0=\hbar\omega/2$. Its <classical turning points> are $x=\pm\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$. With $y=x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$, the <ground-state oscillator probability beyond the turning points> is$$
\boxed{P=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_1^\infty e^{-y^2}\,dy=1-\operatorname{erf}(1)\simeq0.16.}
$$
The stated rounded <error function> gives $0.16$; the more precise Gaussian integral is $0.15730$.

An impulse integrated across its full support produces the <impulsive quantum kick> $K=e^{-i\hat U/\hbar}$. Thus the leading transition amplitude is $-iU_{21}/\hbar$ and$$
\boxed{P_{1\to2}=\frac{|U_{21}|^2}{\hbar^2}+O(|U_{21}/\hbar|^4).}
$$
Here $U_{21}$ has units of action. The <off-diagonal two-state impulse> is exactly $P=\sin^2(|U_{21}|/\hbar)$ for a Hermitian two-state impulse with zero diagonal. No <energy> denominator occurs: the impulse is instantaneous.

For two equivalent $p$ <electrons>, antisymmetry permits$$
\boxed{{}^3P_{0,1,2},\qquad{}^1D_2,\qquad{}^1S_0.}
$$
Their $9+5+1=15$ states exhaust $\binom62$. <Hund's rules> first maximize <spin>, then orbital <angular momentum>; the less-than-half-filled $p$ shell puts the smallest $J$ lowest. Hence the ground level is **${}^3P_0$**. This is the <allowed LS terms of a p-squared configuration>.<Energy Levels of Neutral Carbon by National Institute of Standards and Technology>[Ground configuration2s²2p² and its³P,¹D,¹S levels]{ref}

The <postulates of quantum mechanics> assign a pure state to a normalized ray in a complex <Hilbert space>, and an observable to a <self-adjoint operator>. A spectral outcome projector $P_a$ has <Born rule> <probability> $p_a=\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle$. An ideal <projective measurement> updates the state to $P_a|\psi\rangle/\sqrt{p_a}$ when $p_a>0$; degeneracy requires the full outcome projector.

Between measurements, an isolated system obeys $i\hbar\partial_t|\psi\rangle=H|\psi\rangle$, giving unitary evolution. Composite systems use <tensor products>; identical particles additionally obey the appropriate exchange symmetry. Mixed preparations use a <density matrix>, with $p_a=\operatorname{tr}(\rho P_a)$. These postulates require a specified <Hamiltonian> and measurement model; they do not assign those automatically.<Quantum Mechanics by David Tong>[Quantum states, observables, measurement and Schrödinger evolution]{ref}

= Solution

The <central field approximation> replaces the anisotropic many-electron environment by an average spherical potential, giving one-electron orbitals labelled by $n,l,m$. Screening changes the effective radial attraction and removes the pure Coulomb degeneracy between different $l$.

The <Hartree method> improves the potential self-consistently. For normalized occupied orbitals,$$
\left[-\frac{\hbar^2\nabla^2}{2m_e}-\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}+\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\int\frac{|\phi_j(\mathbf r')|^2}{|\mathbf r-\mathbf r'|}\,d^3r'\right]\phi_i=\epsilon_i\phi_i.
$$
Guess orbitals, construct their mean fields, solve the <Hartree equations>, and iterate to convergence. The $j\ne i$ sum excludes self-interaction in this product-state model. A spherical average can enforce the central-field specialization. A <Hartree product state> omits fermionic exchange and dynamical correlation; <Hartree-Fock method> adds antisymmetry and exchange through a <Slater determinant>. Converged orbital <eigenvalues> must not simply be added as the total <energy>, because that double counts pair interactions.<Topics in Quantum Mechanics by David Tong>[Authored Atoms chapter,§7.3.1: Hartree self-consistency and product-state limitations]{ref}

A <laser> combines pumping, an amplifying medium and optical feedback. <Stimulated emission> adds photons to an occupied optical mode; net amplification requires <population inversion> between the relevant levels after accounting for their degeneracies. Pumping through additional levels and rapid lower-level relaxation can sustain inversion; a closed <two-level system> under steady incoherent resonant pumping cannot sustain it.

An <optical resonator> selects modes and feeds light back through the gain medium. For intensity gain coefficient $g$, internal intensity loss $\alpha_i$, length $L$ and mirror intensity reflectivities $R_1,R_2$, the <round-trip laser threshold condition> is$$
\boxed{R_1R_2e^{2(g-\alpha_i)L}=1,\qquad g_{\rm th}=\alpha_i+\frac1{2L}\log\frac1{R_1R_2}.}
$$
Above threshold, saturation limits gain and a partially transmitting mirror provides output. Typical properties are strong directionality, substantial coherence and narrow bandwidth; multiple modes and pulsed operation are possible, so perfect monochromaticity is not universal.<Fundamentals of Photonics and Quantum Electronics lecture notes by Franz X. Kärtner>[Chapter7,§§7.1–7.2 and <laser threshold>: feedback, gain and level schemes]{ref}

For <spin> one-half, $s_\pm=s_x\pm is_y=(\hbar/2)(\sigma_x\pm i\sigma_y)$, hence$$
\boxed{s_+=\hbar\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad s_-=\hbar\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
They raise or lower the <spin> projection by one unit of $\hbar$ and annihilate the corresponding endpoint. The exchange identity $\boldsymbol\sigma_1\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_2=\sigma_{1z}\sigma_{2z}+2(\sigma_{1+}\sigma_{2-}+\sigma_{1-}\sigma_{2+})$ preserves total <spin> projection.

Write $\Delta=\Delta E$, $u=\mu_0B(g_1+g_2)$ and $w=\mu_0B(g_1-g_2)$. In the **listed** order $(\uparrow\uparrow,\downarrow\downarrow,\uparrow\downarrow,\downarrow\uparrow)$, direct action gives$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}
\Delta/4-u/2&0&0&0\\
0&\Delta/4+u/2&0&0\\
0&0&-\Delta/4-w/2&\Delta/2\\
0&0&\Delta/2&-\Delta/4+w/2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
**The printed matrix instead uses $(\uparrow\uparrow,\uparrow\downarrow,\downarrow\uparrow,\downarrow\downarrow)$.** Permuting the listed indices to $(1,3,4,2)$ recovers its central block and diagonal entries. This is a basis-order mismatch in the source, not a different <Hamiltonian>.

The <two-spin exchange spectrum with unequal signed magnetic moments> is$$
\boxed{E_{\uparrow\uparrow}=\frac\Delta4-\frac u2,\quad E_{\downarrow\downarrow}=\frac\Delta4+\frac u2,\quad E_\pm=-\frac\Delta4\pm\frac12\sqrt{\Delta^2+w^2}.}
$$
Use the products $g_j\mu_0$ as the signed magnetic coefficients in the stated <Hamiltonian>. Opposite physical charges must be represented in those signs; they do not by themselves imply equal moment magnitudes. Here $\mu_0$ denotes the source's magneton, not vacuum permeability.

At zero field, the <spin-one-half triplet state> has <energy> $\Delta/4$ and degeneracy three; the <spin-one-half singlet state> has <energy> $-3\Delta/4$ and degeneracy one:$$
\boxed{E_t=\Delta/4\ (3),\qquad E_s=-3\Delta/4\ (1).}
$$
For $\Delta>0$ the singlet is lower; for $\Delta<0$ the triplet is lower. If $\Delta=0$, all four states coincide.

= Solution

For $|w|\gg|\Delta|$, the mixed states approach opposite-spin product states and$$
\boxed{E_\pm\simeq-\frac\Delta4\pm\frac{|w|}{2},\qquad E_{\uparrow\uparrow,\downarrow\downarrow}=\frac\Delta4\mp\frac u2.}
$$
The omitted mixed-branch correction is of order $\Delta^2/|w|$. The four levels are **generically nondegenerate**, apart from special moment choices or crossings.

For equal-and-opposite signed moments, $g_2=-g_1$, $u=0$ and the parallel-spin level remains exactly twofold degenerate at $\Delta/4$; the other two approach $-\Delta/4\pm|g_1\mu_0B|$. Equal moment magnitudes are an extra assumption, not implied by opposite charges alone. If $g_1=g_2$, the differential Zeeman term vanishes and the singlet/triplet-zero block stays unmixed by the field. This is the <strong-field limit of a two-spin exchange spectrum>.

The quoted expansion is <nondegenerate perturbation theory> for a known isolated eigenstate of a time-independent <Hamiltonian>. A useful small-mixing condition is $|\langle k|H_1|i\rangle|\ll|E_i-E_k|$ for coupled states. First order is a diagonal expectation value; second order describes virtual mixing. A degenerate or nearly degenerate coupled subspace must first be treated by diagonalization there. Symmetry can eliminate terms, and convergence still needs checking. Ground-state second-order shifts are nonpositive because all denominators are negative.<Quantum Mechanics by Richard Fitzpatrick>[Non-degenerate perturbation theory and its breakdown at coupled degeneracies]{ref}

For the <rotational Stark effect>, $H_1=-pE\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the **<polar angle> to the field**. The source calls it azimuthal, which is inconsistent with this coupling. Rotor states have <parity> $(-1)^J$, while $\cos\theta$ is odd, so every first-order <matrix element> within one fixed-$J$ manifold vanishes. The $m$ degeneracy therefore causes no first-order splitting.

Only $J\pm1$ with the same $m$ contribute in second order. Using their <energy> differences $2BJ$ and $-2B(J+1)$,$$
\delta E_{Jm}^{(2)}=\frac{(pE)^2}{2B}\left[\frac{J^2-m^2}{J(2J-1)(2J+1)}-\frac{(J+1)^2-m^2}{(J+1)(2J+1)(2J+3)}\right].
$$
For $J\ge1$ this simplifies to the <quadratic Stark shift of a polar linear rotor>,$$
\boxed{\delta E_{Jm}^{(2)}=\frac{(pE)^2[J(J+1)-3m^2]}{2BJ(J+1)(2J-1)(2J+3)}.}
$$
For $J=0$, only $J'=1,m=0$ contributes, with squared angular <matrix element> $1/3$ and denominator $-2B$:$$
\boxed{\delta E_{00}^{(2)}=-\frac{(pE)^2}{6B}.}
$$
Here $B$ is the rotational <energy> constant, unlike the <magnetic field> in Question3.

The unperturbed line gives $B/h=6.08\,\mathrm{GHz}$. The $J=1$ shifts are $(pE)^2/(10B)$ for $m=0$ and $-(pE)^2/(20B)$ for $m=\pm1$. Subtracting the common lower-state shift gives transition shifts $4(pE)^2/(15Bh)$ and $7(pE)^2/(60Bh)$. Their separation determines the <dipole moment from a rotational Stark doublet>:$$
\Delta\nu=\frac{3(pE)^2}{20Bh},\qquad\boxed{p=\frac hE\sqrt{\frac{20}{3}\frac Bh\Delta\nu}=2.39\times10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\simeq0.717\,\mathrm D.}
$$
The $\mathrm D$ unit is the <debye>. Using external $h=6.62607015\times10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}$ and $1\,\mathrm D=3.33564\times10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}$ gives $pE/B\simeq0.0593$, consistent with weak-field perturbation theory. The two shifted <frequencies> are about $12.165707$ and $12.162497\,\mathrm{GHz}$; <polarization> selects $\Delta m=0$ or $\pm1$. The inferred value is a magnitude; these shifts do not determine the dipole's sign.<CODATA recommended values of the fundamental physical constants: 2022 by National Institute of Standards and Technology>[Exact SI Planck constant]{ref}<Triatomic Spectral Database by National Institute of Standards and Technology>[Section5: <debye> conversion]{ref}
