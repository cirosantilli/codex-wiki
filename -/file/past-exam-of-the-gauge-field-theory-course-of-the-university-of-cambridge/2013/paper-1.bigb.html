= Solution
{parent=1}

Use <natural units> and the <Minkowski metric> $g_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$. Varying the <Proca action> with respect to $A_\nu$ gives $\partial\mathcal L/\partial A_\nu=m^2A^\nu$ and $\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu A_\nu)=-F^{\mu\nu}$. The <Euler-Lagrange equation> is therefore
$$
\boxed{\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}+m^2A^\nu=0.}
$$
Taking a divergence annihilates the antisymmetric field strength, since commuting derivatives are symmetric in their indices. For $m\ne0$ this gives the <Lorenz constraint in Proca theory> $\partial_\nu A^\nu=0$, and hence $(\Box+m^2)A^\nu=0$. The constraint follows from dynamics; it is not an additional <gauge fixing> condition for this massive theory.

For four-<momentum> $p^\mu=(E,0,0,p)$, <longitudinal polarization of a massive vector boson> lies in the time–$z$ plane, has $p\cdot\varepsilon=0$ and is normalized by $\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon=-1$. Taking
$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_L^\mu=\frac1m(p,0,0,E)}
$$
satisfies both requirements because $E^2-p^2=m^2$. It can also be obtained by boosting the rest-frame unit spatial <polarization> along the direction of motion. Together with two transverse <polarizations> it gives the three physical spin states of a massive <spin> one particle.

In the <Higgs boson> rest frame, choose $p^\mu=(E,0,0,p^*)$ and $p'^\mu=(E,0,0,-p^*)$, with $E=m_H/2$ and $p^{*2}=m_H^2/4-m_W^2$. Consistent normalized longitudinal <polarization vectors> are
$$
\varepsilon_L(p)=\frac1{m_W}(p^*,0,0,E),\qquad
\varepsilon_L(p')=\frac1{m_W}(p^*,0,0,-E).
$$
Their Lorentz contraction is $(p^{*2}+E^2)/m_W^2=(2p^{*2}+m_W^2)/m_W^2$. The <Higgs boson coupling to W bosons> consequently gives
$$
|\mathcal M_{LL}|^2=\frac{v^2g^4}{4m_W^4}(2p^{*2}+m_W^2)^2
=\frac{m_H^4}{v^2}(1-2x)^2,\qquad x=\frac{m_W^2}{m_H^2}.
$$
Using the <two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space>, $p^*=m_H\sqrt{1-4x}/2$ and $v^{-2}=\sqrt2G_F$ yields the longitudinal partial <decay width>
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(H\to W_L^+W_L^-)=\frac{G_Fm_H^3}{8\pi\sqrt2}\sqrt{1-4x}\,(1-2x)^2,\qquad m_H>2m_W.}
$$
The charged <W bosons> are distinguishable, so there is no identical-particle factor of $1/2$. This expression includes the entire angular phase space but only the specified longitudinal final <polarizations>. The <Higgs decay to two W bosons> summed over all <polarizations> instead has the factor $1-4x+12x^2$; the additional transverse contribution is $8x^2$. At $m_H\gg m_W$ the longitudinal channel dominates and the leading width grows as $G_Fm_H^3$.

= Solution
{parent=i}

Below $m_H=2m_W$, the on-shell two-body <decay channel> is kinematically closed. An imaginary $p^*$ in the preceding formula is therefore a sign that this formula no longer applies. Slightly below threshold, the physical process can instead contain an on-shell <W boson> and an off-shell one, $H\to WW^*\to Wf\bar f'$, followed by decay of the remaining <W boson>. Its rate requires the virtual-vector <propagator>, its allowed fermionic decay channels and the corresponding multiparticle <Lorentz-invariant phase space>. The nonzero <decay width> of the <W boson> smooths the idealized threshold. A polarization-resolved rate for two on-shell longitudinal vectors must not be analytically continued and treated as that multiparticle rate.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

In the stronger limit $m_H\ll m_W$, both intermediate <W bosons> must be virtual. Their heavy propagators suppress the four-fermion <decay amplitude>, while the small available <phase space> suppresses its rate further. At energies small compared with $m_W$, integrating out the two vectors gives a local interaction proportional to $G_FH J_\mu^+J^{-\mu}/v$. Its dimension-seven coefficient implies a rate of order $G_F^3m_H^7$ when all four final <fermions> can be treated as massless, with channel-dependent constants. This is a low-energy scaling statement, not the on-shell longitudinal-width formula. Other accessible <Higgs boson> decay channels, such as a pair of <fermions> or loop-induced two-<photon> decay, must then be compared using their actual masses and couplings.

= Solution
{parent=2}

The <Standard Model> uses the local <gauge group> $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$. Its <Higgs field> is a complex <Higgs doublet> of <hypercharge> $Y=1/2$. A bare <mass> term for a weak <gauge boson>, or for a charged <fermion> joining inequivalent left- and right-handed gauge representations, would not respect the unbroken electroweak construction. Instead introduce a gauge-invariant <Higgs potential>
$$
V(\Phi)=-\mu^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi+\lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2,\qquad\lambda>0,\quad\mu^2>0.
$$
Its minima satisfy $\Phi^\dagger\Phi=v^2/2$, $v^2=\mu^2/\lambda$. Choosing a representative $\langle\Phi\rangle=(0,v)^T/\sqrt2$ leaves $Q=T_3+Y$ unbroken. In this description <electroweak symmetry breaking> is $SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y\to U(1)_{\rm em}$; the underlying local <gauge symmetry> remains a redundancy of the theory. A vacuum representative is not a physical explicit violation of gauge invariance.

In <unitary gauge>, write $\Phi=(0,v+H)^T/\sqrt2$. The <gauge-covariant kinetic term> $|D_\mu\Phi|^2$ produces
$$
m_W=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad m_Z=\frac{v}{2}\sqrt{g^2+g'^2},\qquad m_\gamma=0,
$$
with $\tan\theta_W=g'/g$ and $e=g\sin\theta_W$. Three angular scalar directions supply the <longitudinal gauge-boson polarizations> of $W^+,W^-,Z$, while one real radial <Higgs boson> survives with $m_H^2=2\lambda v^2$. Counting physical <degrees of freedom> checks the <Higgs mechanism>: the electroweak gauge sector and scalar doublet have $4\times2+4=12$ before the rearrangement and $3\times3+2+1=12$ afterwards.

The <Higgs potential> also fixes the scalar self-interactions:
$$
V=\text{constant}+\frac{m_H^2}{2}H^2+\frac{m_H^2}{2v}H^3+\frac{m_H^2}{8v^2}H^4.
$$
The color gauge group is unbroken, so <gluons> remain massless. The same electroweak kinetic term fixes
$$
\mathcal L\supset\frac{2m_W^2}{v}HW_\mu^+W^{-\mu}+\frac{m_Z^2}{v}HZ_\mu Z^\mu
+\frac{m_W^2}{v^2}H^2W_\mu^+W^{-\mu}+\frac{m_Z^2}{2v^2}H^2Z_\mu Z^\mu.
$$
Gauge-invariant <Yukawa couplings> such as $-y_d\bar Q_L\Phi d_R-y_u\bar Q_L\widetilde\Phi u_R+\mathrm{h.c.}$ give $m_f=y_fv/\sqrt2$ and the physical coupling $-m_fH\bar f f/v$. These relations predict stronger Higgs couplings to heavier massive particles; they do not determine the Yukawa parameters themselves. The minimal <Standard Model> does not supply observed neutrino masses. The surviving <Higgs boson> is neutral and has <spin> zero; its <mass> and self-coupling are tied to the curvature of the <Higgs potential>, not fixed by the gauge couplings alone. Higgs exchange also provides the <Higgs cancellation in longitudinal vector scattering>, maintaining the required high-energy behavior of weak amplitudes.

At a proton collider, important production mechanisms are <gluon-fusion Higgs production> through a heavy-quark loop, <vector-boson fusion> with two outgoing quark jets, associated $WH$ or $ZH$ production, and production with a <top quark> pair. At an <electron>–positron collider, <Higgsstrahlung> $e^+e^-\to ZH$ is a clean example. Tree-level decays include <Higgs decay to a fermion pair> and, when allowed, <Higgs decay to two W bosons> or <Higgs decay to two Z bosons>. Below an on-shell vector-pair threshold, one or both vectors can be virtual. The $gg$ and $\gamma\gamma$ channels arise through loops; neutral colorless $H$ has no corresponding direct tree-level gauge coupling. Rates and <branching fractions> depend on both couplings and kinematics, so “heaviest available particle” is a guide rather than a complete rule.

The <Higgs boson discovery in 2012> supplied an experimental test. The <ATLAS experiment> and <CMS experiment> reconstructed two-<photon> and four-lepton <invariant masses> in <Large Hadron Collider> proton collisions. <Electromagnetic calorimeters> measure <photon> energies, while tracking and muon systems identify charged leptons; narrow reconstructed peaks can be distinguished from smooth backgrounds with control samples and a likelihood analysis. The ATLAS discovery paper reported a new resonance near $126.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$. An observed <mass> peak supports the existence of a new state; production channels, decay rates and angular distributions test whether it has the particular Higgs couplings and quantum numbers.<Observation of a new particle in the search for the Standard Model Higgs boson with the ATLAS detector at the LHC by ATLAS Collaboration>[abstract; Sections 2, 6–7 and 9]{ref}

Finally, the <hierarchy problem> asks why the electroweak scalar <mass> scale remains much smaller than a putative heavy physical scale without delicate cancellations. A cutoff estimate suggests corrections $\delta m_H^2\sim\Lambda^2/(16\pi^2)$ times couplings, but the quadratic cutoff term itself depends on the regularization convention. The physical concern persists when a heavy state of <mass> $M$ couples to the <Higgs field> and produces threshold corrections of order its coupling times $M^2/(16\pi^2)$. Matching a much smaller Higgs <mass> may then require cancellation between independent parameters. Supersymmetry, compositeness and other protective structures propose different resolutions; <renormalizability> of the <Standard Model> does not by itself resolve this sensitivity or establish that new particles must occur at one particular <mass>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Interactions produce <Feynman diagrams> with internal lines whose independent <loop momenta> are integrated without an upper bound. Their large-momentum contributions can have an <ultraviolet divergence>. Two explicit examples in a scalar quartic theory are a one-loop <tadpole diagram> contributing to the two-point function and a one-loop four-point <bubble diagram>. After a <Wick rotation>, a spherical cutoff $\Lambda$ gives the representative integrals
$$
I_1=\int_{|k_E|<\Lambda}\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{k_E^2+m^2}
=\frac1{16\pi^2}\left[\Lambda^2-m^2\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right)\right],
$$
$$
I_2(0)=\int_{|k_E|<\Lambda}\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k_E^2+m^2)^2}
=\frac1{16\pi^2}\left[\ln\left(1+\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2}\right)+\frac{m^2}{\Lambda^2+m^2}-1\right].
$$
Combinatorial factors and couplings multiply these integrals in a particular diagram. The first is quadratically divergent by cutoff <power counting>; the second is logarithmically divergent. Other schemes package the divergent pieces differently.

In <renormalization>, introduce a <regularization>, separate bare parameters into renormalized parameters and <counterterms>, and fix the latter by stated normalization conditions. Local divergent pieces of the scalar two- and four-point functions are absorbed into <mass>, field normalization and quartic-coupling counterterms, with all other operators allowed by the chosen symmetries included as needed. A perturbatively <renormalizable quantum field theory> requires this finite counterterm family to remain closed at all orders. It does not require every individual bare diagram to be finite. For <gauge theories>, identities expressing gauge symmetry and absence of a <gauge anomaly> are essential in addition to superficial counting.

The <action> is dimensionless in <natural units>, so in four spacetime dimensions $[\mathcal L]=4$ and $[\partial_\mu]=1$. The <engineering dimensions of relativistic fields> follow from matching the free kinetic terms:
$$
2[\phi]+2=4,\qquad2[\psi]+1=4,\qquad2[A_\mu]+2=4,
$$
and hence
$$
\boxed{[\phi]=1,\qquad[\psi]=\frac32,\qquad[A_\mu]=1.}
$$
For a local operator $\mathcal O$ of <mass dimension> $d_\mathcal O$, its coupling has dimension $4-d_\mathcal O$. Operators with dimension at most four are candidates for ordinary perturbative power-counting renormalizability. This test presupposes consistent kinetic terms, symmetries and the full set of induced counterterms; it is not a proof of physical consistency for an arbitrary vector interaction.

= Solution
{parent=a}

The operator $(\phi^\dagger\phi)^2$ has <mass dimension> four, so $[\lambda]=0$. It is a **power-counting renormalizable scalar quartic interaction**. It describes scalar self-interactions, including the quartic term in the <Higgs potential> for the appropriate scalar representation. Its sign in the potential must be chosen for a stable large-field theory; dimension counting alone does not ensure stability.

= Solution
{parent=b}

The operator $(\phi^\dagger+\phi)\bar\psi\psi$ has <mass dimension> $1+3=4$, so $[g]=0$. It is a **power-counting renormalizable Yukawa interaction** between a real scalar combination and a <fermion> bilinear. A scalar <vacuum expectation value> contributes to a <Dirac mass term>; its fluctuations couple to the <fermion> pair. This is a schematic scalar–<fermion> theory. In a chiral <gauge theory>, representations must be combined gauge-invariantly, as in the <Standard Model> Higgs–<fermion> interactions. Without additional protecting symmetries, all allowed scalar counterterms must be included as well.

= Solution

The operator $\phi^\dagger\phi\bar\psi\psi$ has <mass dimension> $2+3=5$, giving $[g]=-1$. It is **not power-counting renormalizable** as a fundamental interaction treated to arbitrary <energy>. It is a perfectly usable dimension-five <effective operator> below its suppression scale. Successive orders in that <effective field theory> require a derivative and operator expansion rather than one finite set of parameters valid at every order.

= Solution

The operator $A^\mu\bar\psi\gamma_\mu\psi$ has <mass dimension> $1+3=4$, so $[e]=0$. It is the **renormalizable electromagnetic current coupling** when completed as <quantum electrodynamics>: a massless Abelian <gauge field> couples minimally to the conserved Dirac current, with consistent <gauge fixing> and <Ward identities>. Indeed, replacing $\partial_\mu$ by a <gauge-covariant derivative> in the <Dirac equation> produces this interaction. The gauge structure, rather than dimension counting alone, ensures the necessary cancellations.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[§6.3.1, coupling the electromagnetic field to fermions]{ref}

= Solution
{parent=e}

The operator $A^\mu\bar\psi\partial_\mu\psi$ has <mass dimension> $1+3+1=5$, so $[e]=-1$. It is **not power-counting renormalizable**. Hermiticity may require a suitable conjugate or a symmetrized derivative, and electromagnetic gauge invariance would require a further consistent completion; neither repair changes its engineering dimension. Such derivative interactions can occur in an <effective field theory>, subject to its symmetries and operator redundancies.

= Solution
{parent=3}
{disambiguate=Fermi interaction}

Each Dirac current $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ has <mass dimension> three, so their product has dimension six. Its coefficient therefore has dimension $-2$:
$$
\boxed{[G_F]=-2.}
$$
The <Fermi interaction> is **not power-counting renormalizable**, but its success at low energies is explained by <effective field theory>. Exchange of a heavy <W boson> between the appropriate chiral weak currents has a propagator that expands as $1/(m_W^2-q^2)=m_W^{-2}[1+q^2/m_W^2+\cdots]$. The leading term is a local four-fermion interaction and further terms are suppressed by powers of $q^2/m_W^2$. The complete spontaneously broken electroweak <gauge theory> resolves the pointlike approximation at higher energies. The source's vector-current product illustrates the operator dimension; the actual weak interaction also has its specific flavors and chiral structure.
