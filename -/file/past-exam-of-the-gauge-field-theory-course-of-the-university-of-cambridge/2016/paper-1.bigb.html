= Solution
{parent=1}

A <variational symmetry of a Lagrangian density> maps admissible field configurations to configurations with the same <action>, up to an allowed boundary contribution. Thus (i) the <action> is invariant for suitable <boundary conditions>, while (ii) the <Lagrangian density> need only change by a <total divergence>, rather than be pointwise invariant. For an <internal symmetry of a classical field theory>, the coordinates are fixed. A spacetime transformation also changes the integration measure and must be treated accordingly.

A <conserved current> satisfies $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ <on shell>. The <Noether charge> $Q=\int j^0\,d^3x$ then obeys $\dot Q=-\int_{\partial V}\mathbf j\cdot d\mathbf S$, so conservation of the integrated charge additionally requires vanishing net boundary flux.

For several real field components $q_A$ and a constant infinitesimal parameter, write $\delta q_A=\varepsilon X_A$ and $\delta\mathcal L=\varepsilon\partial_\mu K^\mu$. The <product rule> gives
$$
\frac{\delta\mathcal L}{\varepsilon}=\sum_A\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial q_A}-\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu q_A)}\right)X_A+\partial_\mu\left(\sum_A\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu q_A)}X_A\right).
$$
Subtract the specified divergence. The <Euler-Lagrange field equations> make the first term vanish, proving the <Noether theorem> with
$$
\boxed{j^\mu=\sum_A\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu q_A)}X_A-K^\mu,\qquad \partial_\mu j^\mu=0.}
$$
For a <complex scalar field>, treat the field and its <complex conjugate> as independent variables in this calculation; this is equivalent to using its two real components. This is the <Noether current for a first-derivative scalar field>, summed over components.

Collect the two complex fields into $\Phi=(\phi_1,\phi_2)^T$. Their <Lagrangian density> has the radial form
$$
\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\Phi^\dagger\partial^\mu\Phi-m^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi-\lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2.
$$
For a constant <Hermitian matrix> $T$, the <unitary transformation> $\delta\Phi=i\varepsilon T\Phi$ preserves both the kinetic contraction and $\Phi^\dagger\Phi$. In particular the two specified transformations have generators $T_1=\sigma_1$ and $T_2=\sigma_2$, the first two <Pauli matrices>. Their <Noether currents> are instances of the <unitary scalar multiplet current>
$$
j_T^\mu=i\left[(\partial^\mu\Phi^\dagger)T\Phi-\Phi^\dagger T\partial^\mu\Phi\right].
$$
Explicitly,
$$
\begin{aligned}
j_1^\mu&=i\left(\phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_1^*+\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_2^*-\phi_2^*\partial^\mu\phi_1-\phi_1^*\partial^\mu\phi_2\right),\\
j_2^\mu&=\phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_1^*-\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_2^*+\phi_2^*\partial^\mu\phi_1-\phi_1^*\partial^\mu\phi_2.
\end{aligned}
$$
An overall change of current sign corresponds to reversing the associated symmetry parameter; the signs here follow the two transformations as written.

The <matrix commutator> of their infinitesimal matrices is $[i\sigma_1,i\sigma_2]=-2i\sigma_3$. The antisymmetric difference of successive transformations therefore supplies a third symmetry, after a nonzero rescaling of its parameter:
$$
\delta\phi_1=i\zeta\phi_1,\qquad\delta\phi_2=-i\zeta\phi_2.
$$
Its current is
$$
\boxed{j_3^\mu=i\left(\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_1^*-\phi_1^*\partial^\mu\phi_1-\phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_2^*+\phi_2^*\partial^\mu\phi_2\right).}
$$
The three <traceless matrices> $\sigma_a/2$ obey $[\sigma_a/2,\sigma_b/2]=i\epsilon_{abc}\sigma_c/2$. Their exponentials act as <SU(2)> on the complex doublet. This identifies both the <Lie algebra> and the group acting on these fields: the central element $-I$ changes $\Phi$ and is not the identity transformation, so the doublet action is not an action of $SO(3)$ obtained by identifying $I$ with $-I$.

There is also a common <global phase symmetry of a complex scalar field>, $\Phi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\Phi$, with current $j_I^\mu$. Together with <SU(2)> this gives $U(2)\simeq(SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_2$ among complex-linear transformations. Independent constant phases of $\phi_1$ and $\phi_2$, and field interchange, are already contained in this group.

In fact this particular <scalar potential> has a larger real internal symmetry. Write $\phi_1=(q_1+iq_2)/\sqrt2$ and $\phi_2=(q_3+iq_4)/\sqrt2$; then
$$
\mathcal L=\frac12\sum_{A=1}^4\partial_\mu q_A\partial^\mu q_A-\frac{m^2}{2}\sum_Aq_A^2-\frac\lambda4\left(\sum_Aq_A^2\right)^2.
$$
The <O(4) symmetry of a complex scalar doublet> includes transformations mixing $\Phi$ with $\Phi^*$, which need not be complex-linear. Its connected part has six rotation generators, so listing $U(2)$ alone would not exhaust the internal symmetries of the displayed isolated theory. Individual field conjugations are examples of its discrete transformations. Finally the spacetime-independent scalar coefficients and Lorentz contractions give <Poincaré symmetry>, and the real scalar action also permits spatial reflection and time reversal. These statements concern the action; a chosen vacuum or external <boundary conditions> can preserve fewer symmetries. Special parameter choices can introduce additional symmetries, but are not required here.

<Chirality> distinguishes the two inequivalent <Weyl spinor> representations of the proper <Lorentz group>. For a <Dirac spinor>, define $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ and the <chiral projectors>
$$
P_L=\frac{1-\gamma^5}{2},\qquad P_R=\frac{1+\gamma^5}{2},\qquad\psi=\psi_L+\psi_R.
$$
They satisfy $P_LP_R=0$ and $P_L+P_R=I$. Proper <Lorentz transformations> preserve each chiral representation, whereas <parity> exchanges them. <Helicity> instead measures the projection of <spin angular momentum> along <momentum>. For a massive particle it can change under a boost that overtakes the particle, so it must not be identified with <chirality>. For massless positive-energy particles the left-chiral field annihilates negative-helicity particles and creates positive-helicity antiparticles.

The <Standard Model> is a <chiral gauge theory>: left- and right-chiral fields have different <gauge group representations>. In the convention $Q=T_3+Y$, one generation contains
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{field}&SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\text{ representation and }Y\\\hline
Q_L=(u_L,d_L)^T&(\mathbf3,\mathbf2)_{1/6}\\
u_R&(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{2/3}\\
d_R&(\mathbf3,\mathbf1)_{-1/3}\\
L_L=(\nu_L,e_L)^T&(\mathbf1,\mathbf2)_{-1/2}\\
e_R&(\mathbf1,\mathbf1)_{-1}
\end{array}
$$
The minimal field content has no <right-handed neutrino>. A sterile right-handed field is a possible extension, not a required member of that minimal list. The table displays <weak isospin>, <hypercharge> and <color charge> assignments, not assignments of massive-particle helicity.

The <weak charged current> contains $\bar\nu\gamma^\mu P_Le\,W_\mu^+$ and its <Hermitian conjugate>, with coupling $g/\sqrt2$. Only the left-chiral lepton doublet participates, giving <parity violation>. Both chiralities can couple to the <Z boson>, but their coefficients differ:
$$
\mathcal L_Z=-\frac g{\cos\theta_W}Z_\mu\bar f\gamma^\mu\left[(T_3-Q\sin^2\theta_W)P_L-Q\sin^2\theta_WP_R\right]f.
$$
Here $T_3$ is that of the left-chiral component. For the electron, it is $-1/2$. The <photon> and <gluon> couplings of a charged massive <fermion> are vectorlike; this does not make the full <electroweak interaction> vectorlike.

A bare <Dirac mass term> $m\bar\psi\psi=m(\bar\psi_L\psi_R+\bar\psi_R\psi_L)$ joins the two chiral representations. Their different electroweak quantum numbers forbid that term before <electroweak symmetry breaking>. A <Yukawa interaction>, such as $-y_e\bar L_LHe_R+\mathrm{h.c.}$, is gauge invariant; the <Higgs doublet> expectation value then gives $m_e=y_ev/\sqrt2$. Up-type quarks use the conjugate doublet. Thus <chirality> explains the weak charge assignments, their <parity violation>, and why masses arise through the <Higgs mechanism>. The consistency of these chiral assignments also requires <Standard Model anomaly cancellation>.

<Gauge boson self-interactions> follow from noncommuting generators, rather than a separate assumption that the force carriers should attract one another. With $D_\mu=\partial_\mu+igA_\mu^aT^a$ and $[T^a,T^b]=if^{abc}T^c$, the <Yang-Mills field strength> is
$$
F_{\mu\nu}^a=\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a-gf^{abc}A_\mu^bA_\nu^c.
$$
The last term is required so that $F_{\mu\nu}$ transforms covariantly under the <gauge group>. Let $G_{\mu\nu}^a=\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a$. Expanding $\mathcal L=-F_{\mu\nu}^aF^{a\mu\nu}/4$ gives a quadratic kinetic term and the <cubic and quartic Yang-Mills self-interactions>
$$
\mathcal L_3=gf^{abc}(\partial_\mu A_\nu^a)A^{b\mu}A^{c\nu},\qquad
\mathcal L_4=-\frac{g^2}{4}f^{abc}f^{ade}A_\mu^bA_\nu^cA^{d\mu}A^{e\nu}.
$$
The <structure constants> and one <gauge coupling> fix both types of vertex. For an Abelian group the structure constants vanish: the renormalizable pure <electromagnetic field> action has no elementary three- or four-<photon> vertex. Photon scattering through charged-particle loops is a different statement, not a contradiction.

In <quantum chromodynamics>, the non-Abelian $SU(3)_c$ structure produces three- and four-<gluon> vertices; the gluons themselves carry <color charge>. These vertices influence multi-jet production and the <renormalization group> running of the strong coupling. In the electroweak sector the $SU(2)_L$ fields mix with the Abelian field after <electroweak symmetry breaking>. The resulting charged-vector vertices include $W^+W^-\gamma$ and $W^+W^-Z$, with couplings $e$ and $g\cos\theta_W$, together with the related four-boson vertices. Neutral-only triple vertices such as $ZZ\gamma$ are absent at tree level in the <Standard Model>.

The <electroweak triple gauge coupling> can be measured using $e^+e^-\to W^+W^-$. The neutrino exchange and neutral-vector exchange diagrams interfere: the gauge-related coefficients cancel the most rapidly growing longitudinal-polarization terms. Their relative strengths and angular dependence therefore test the theory more sharply than merely observing a pair of massive vector bosons. The electron-mass-suppressed Higgs contribution completes the corresponding mass-dependent cancellation. Adding arbitrary independent three- and four-vector couplings would generally destroy these relations.

At the <Large Electron–Positron Collider>, the <L3 experiment> fitted charged triple-gauge parameters using hadronic and semileptonic W-pair events, complemented by single-W and single-photon channels. These measurements agreed with the predicted self-couplings. Their interpretation uses reconstructed decay products and differential distributions, rather than direct pictures of a three-boson vertex.<Measurement of Triple-Gauge-Boson Couplings of the W Boson at LEP by L3 Collaboration>[2004, abstract: W-pair channels at 189–209 GeV, complementary channels and agreement with Standard Model self-couplings]{ref}

= Solution

The evidence tests several linked claims: a <weak neutral current>, massive charged and neutral mediators, their chiral couplings, and the non-Abelian interactions among those mediators. A theory must describe these with a common set of electroweak parameters.

The <Gargamelle neutral-current experiment> supplied a particularly direct early test. A neutrino beam crossed a heavy-liquid <bubble chamber>; charged reaction products made visible tracks, while incoming and outgoing neutrinos did not. Neutral-current candidates had a hadronic vertex without an accompanying charged lepton, or an isolated recoiling electron. The visible final state differs from a muon-neutrino charged-current event with an outgoing muon. Neutral hadrons entering from surrounding material could imitate a hadronic vertex, so background rejection was essential. The 1973 observations supported an interaction carried by a neutral weak mediator; they did not directly reconstruct an on-shell Z resonance.<Gargamelle by CERN>[Experimental description: neutrino beam, heavy-liquid chamber, hadronic and electron-scattering searches]{ref}

The <UA1 experiment> and <UA2 experiment> collider discoveries supplied complementary evidence in 1983. A proton–antiproton collider provided enough energy to produce the mediators. Charged W candidates used a high-transverse-momentum charged lepton and missing transverse momentum from an escaping neutrino; because its longitudinal momentum was not measured, a transverse-mass distribution was useful. Neutral Z candidates instead formed a charged-lepton pair with an invariant-mass peak. Observing both kinds of events and their masses tested the predicted mediator structure.<The Z boson by CERN>[History: 1983 W and Z discovery at the proton–antiproton collider]{ref}

The <Weinberg angle> connects independent measurements. At tree level, a <Higgs doublet> gives
$$
m_W=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad m_Z=\frac{v}{2}\sqrt{g^2+g'^2},\qquad e=g\sin\theta_W,
$$
so **$m_W=m_Z\cos\theta_W$**. Neutral-current scattering, the mediator masses and electromagnetic measurements must be consistent with one mixing angle after a common renormalization prescription and <radiative corrections> are included. Precise Z-resonance widths and asymmetries test the left- and right-chiral couplings; a mass peak alone would not establish those coupling assignments.

At higher collider energies, W-pair production tested the <electroweak triple gauge couplings>. Reconstructing hadronic jets or a charged lepton plus missing momentum identifies W decays; total rates and production/decay angles constrain the $WW\gamma$ and $WWZ$ vertices. Their non-Abelian relations are required for the characteristic interference with neutrino exchange. L3's 2004 analysis used these channels and found agreement with the Standard Model expectation.<Measurement of Triple-Gauge-Boson Couplings of the W Boson at LEP by L3 Collaboration>[Abstract: measured triple-gauge parameters and W-pair, single-W and single-photon samples]{ref}

Finally, the 2012 <Higgs boson discovery> and subsequent coupling tests address the symmetry-breaking sector, beyond the existence of gauge mediators. The initial observation was a new resonance in several decay channels; establishing all its properties requires further measurements. These are mutually supporting tests of the <electroweak interaction>, with distinct apparatus, observables and background controls, rather than a claim that any single event proves the entire theory.

= Solution

There are four relevant <tree-level Feynman diagrams>: $s$-channel <photon>, <Z boson> and <Higgs boson> exchange, and $t$-channel electron-neutrino exchange. The <electron> and <positron> are incoming, and $W^-$ and $W^+$ outgoing. The Higgs diagram is normally tiny because its electron coupling is proportional to $m_e$; it should nevertheless be included in the requested list.

\Image[paper-1-w-pair-diagrams.png]
{title=The four tree-level diagrams for electron–positron annihilation into a charged W pair}
{description=Original schematics. Fermion arrows show particle-number flow, which opposes the momentum of an incoming positron. W lines are labelled by charge.}

Let $v=2m_W/g$. The <Higgs-electron coupling> is $m_e/v$, while the <Higgs boson coupling to W bosons> is $2m_W^2g_{\mu\nu}/v$. Multiplying the vertices and scalar propagator gives, up to an irrelevant overall phase,
$$
\mathcal M_h=\frac{g^2m_e}{2(s-m_h^2)}\bar v(p_2)u(p_1)\,\epsilon_1^*\cdot\epsilon_2^*.
$$
A scalar propagator has no vector index, the <Yukawa interaction> supplies the scalar spinor bilinear, and the second vertex contracts the two polarization vectors. We retain the tree propagator and neglect widths, consistently with the stated leading-order calculation.

For unpolarized incoming beams the <spin average> is $1/4$, not a sum without normalization. The <fermion spin sums> and <gamma-matrix trace> identities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac14\sum_{r,t}|\bar v_t(p_2)u_r(p_1)|^2
&=\frac14\operatorname{tr}\left[(\not p_2-m_e)(\not p_1+m_e)\right]\\
&=p_1\cdot p_2-m_e^2
=\frac{s-4m_e^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The terms with one gamma matrix have zero trace; the two-gamma trace gives $4p_1\cdot p_2$. Both final polarization states are specified as longitudinal, so no final polarization average is appropriate.

In the <centre-of-momentum frame>, take $k_1=(E,k\widehat{\mathbf n})$ and $k_2=(E,-k\widehat{\mathbf n})$, where $E=\sqrt s/2$ and $k^2=E^2-m_W^2$. A pair of <longitudinal polarization vectors> is
$$
\epsilon_{1L}=\frac1{m_W}(k,E\widehat{\mathbf n}),\qquad
\epsilon_{2L}=\frac1{m_W}(k,-E\widehat{\mathbf n}).
$$
Each obeys $\epsilon_i\cdot k_i=0$ and $\epsilon_i^2=-1$. Their contraction is angle independent:
$$
\epsilon_{1L}\cdot\epsilon_{2L}=\frac{k^2+E^2}{m_W^2}=\frac{s}{2m_W^2}-1.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M_h|^2}_{LL}=\frac{g^4m_e^2(s-4m_e^2)}{8(s-m_h^2)^2}\left(\frac{s}{2m_W^2}-1\right)^2.}
$$

For the <two-body scattering cross section>, the outgoing-to-incoming momentum ratio is
$$
\frac{k}{p}=\frac{\beta_W}{\beta_e},\qquad
\beta_W=\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_W^2}{s}},\quad
\beta_e=\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_e^2}{s}}.
$$
The source's final sentence interchanges the particle names attached to these two momenta; physical final-state phase space and initial-state flux fix the ratio as written here. Since the Higgs-only spin-averaged expression is isotropic, integrating the <solid angle> gives $4\pi$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{h,LL}
&=\frac1{16\pi s}\frac{\beta_W}{\beta_e}\overline{|\mathcal M_h|^2}_{LL}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{g^4m_e^2\beta_e\beta_W}{128\pi(s-m_h^2)^2}\left(\frac{s}{2m_W^2}-1\right)^2},\qquad s\ge4m_W^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
There is no identical-particle factor: $W^+$ and $W^-$ are distinguishable. The threshold factor $\beta_W$ makes this contribution vanish when the outgoing momentum vanishes. This is the isolated squared Higgs amplitude for a specified longitudinal pair, not the complete physical cross section: the full result squares the coherent sum of all four amplitudes and includes their interference.
