= Solution

With the <valence quark> assignments $K^0=d\bar s$ and $\bar K^0=s\bar d$, <neutral kaon mixing> changes <strangeness> by two units. A representative leading <Standard Model> contribution is a <box Feynman diagram> with two <W bosons> and two internal up-type <quarks>:
$$
d\longrightarrow u_i\longrightarrow s,\qquad
\bar s\longrightarrow\bar u_j\longrightarrow\bar d,
\qquad u_i,u_j\in\{u,c,t\}.
$$
The two lines exchange the two <W bosons>. There are four <weak charged current> <interaction vertices>, so the <scattering amplitude> is fourth order in the weak <gauge coupling>, or second order in the <Fermi constant>. The full result sums the internal <quark flavors> with their <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix> factors; one box alone illustrates the mechanism.

= Solution
{parent=1/b}

A <kaon> is a <pseudoscalar meson>, with <intrinsic parity> $-1$. At rest, $P|\pm\rangle=-|\pm\rangle$. Combining this with the specified <CP> <eigenvalues> gives
$$
\boxed{C|+\rangle=-|+\rangle,\qquad C|-\rangle=+|-\rangle.}
$$
Equivalently, in the <exchange-positive CP convention for neutral kaon mixing> fixed by the question, $C|K^0\rangle=-|\bar K^0\rangle$ and $C|\bar K^0\rangle=-|K^0\rangle$. This sign is a convention for the flavor states; the specified <CP> <eigenvalues> and physical predictions must be kept consistent with it.

For two spin-zero <pions>, conservation of total <angular momentum> fixes the relative <orbital angular momentum> to zero. The resulting $\pi^+\pi^-$ or $\pi^0\pi^0$ state has <CP> $+1$. The lowest <partial waves> of $\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$ and $3\pi^0$ have <CP> $-1$. Thus the dominant nonleptonic assignments in the <CP symmetry> approximation are
$$
\boxed{|K_S\rangle\simeq|+\rangle\to2\pi,\qquad
|K_L\rangle\simeq|-\rangle\to3\pi.}
$$
The two-<pion> mode has substantially more available <kinetic energy> and larger <Lorentz-invariant phase space>; the three-<pion> mode has an additional final particle and lies closer to its <decay threshold>. This helps explain why the <CP>-odd state has the longer <mean lifetime>. The actual <mean lifetimes> also depend on the <decay amplitudes> and on semileptonic channels, so counting final particles alone is not a quantitative prediction.

The middle panel of the figure in part (a) shows $\bar s\to\bar uW^+$, $W^+\to u\bar d$, with the $d$ as a <spectator quark>. The outgoing $d\bar u$ and $u\bar d$ form $\pi^-$ and $\pi^+$. In the bottom panel, an additional <gluon> produces $d\bar d$, supplying the neutral <pion> component for $\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$. The charge-conjugate diagrams supply the other component of each of the <kaon CP eigenstates>. The drawn <gluon> splitting represents nonperturbative <hadronization>, rather than a quantitatively reliable perturbative prediction at the <kaon> scale.

These multiplicities describe the dominant low-<orbital angular momentum> modes, not an absolute rule for every three-<pion> configuration. In $\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0$, odd <orbital angular momentum> values of the charged pair and of its motion relative to $\pi^0$ can couple to total zero and give <CP> $+1$, but are strongly suppressed near <decay threshold>. Physical $K_L\to2\pi$ also occurs through <CP violation>.

Put $M_f=m_p+m_{\pi^+}+m_\Lambda+m_{K^0}$. In <natural units>, a stationary target <proton> and a beam <proton> of total <energy> $E_b$ have the <Mandelstam invariant>
$$
s=(p_b+p_t)^2=2m_p^2+2m_pE_b.
$$
At <fixed-target production threshold> the final particles have zero relative <momentum> in their <centre-of-mass frame>, so $s=M_f^2$. Restoring $c$, the total beam <energy> and its <kinetic energy> are
$$
\boxed{E_{b,\mathrm{thr}}=\frac{M_f^2-2m_p^2}{2m_p}c^2,\qquad
T_{b,\mathrm{thr}}=\frac{M_f^2-4m_p^2}{2m_p}c^2.}
$$
Using rounded <masses> in $\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$, $(938.27,139.57,1115.68,497.61)$, gives $E_{b,\mathrm{thr}}\simeq2.921\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $T_{b,\mathrm{thr}}\simeq1.983\,\mathrm{GeV}$.

One possible <strong interaction> <quark-line flavour rearrangement> is
$$
(uud)+(uud)\longrightarrow
\underbrace{uud}_{p}+\underbrace{uds}_{\Lambda^0}
+\underbrace{u\bar d}_{\pi^+}+\underbrace{d\bar s}_{K^0}.
$$
It needs newly created $d\bar d$ and $s\bar s$ pairs, with no net change of any <quark flavor>. For example, keep the first $uud$ as the outgoing <proton>; allocate the second incoming $u,d$ and the new $s$ to $\Lambda^0$, the remaining incoming $u$ and new $\bar d$ to $\pi^+$, and the new $d,\bar s$ to $K^0$. Two <gluon> splittings illustrate this flow. This is a possible <hadronization> topology, not a unique perturbative description of the <proton> collision.

\Image[2022-associated-kaon-production.png]
{title=One flavor-conserving quark-flow assignment for associated neutral-kaon production}

After several short <mean lifetimes>, the surviving coherent beam is approximately $|K_L\rangle=(|K^0\rangle-|\bar K^0\rangle)/\sqrt2$. In ordinary matter the <forward scattering amplitudes> of $K^0$ and $\bar K^0$ differ. For example, the $s$ in a <neutral antikaon> can enter a strange <baryon> through strong absorption; the opposite <strangeness> assignment has different available channels. If their forward <transmission amplitudes> through a thin block are $t_0$ and $\bar t_0$, respectively, then
$$
\boxed{|K_L\rangle\longmapsto
\frac{t_0+\bar t_0}{2}|K_L\rangle+
\frac{t_0-\bar t_0}{2}|K_S\rangle.}
$$
Thus the transmitted forward beam contains a regenerated short-lived component, with prompt two-<pion> decays after the block. Differential absorption changes the intensities as well as the relative phase. Scattered particles outside the forward beam can provide an incoherent component. This <neutral kaon regeneration> does not require <CP violation>: the material itself distinguishes matter and antimatter.

For a narrow unstable state, the effective <Hamiltonian> has the complex <eigenvalue> $m_j-i\Gamma_j/2$. With <natural units> and $\Gamma_j=1/\tau_j$, its <Schrödinger equation> gives
$$
A_j(t)=A_j(0)e^{-im_jt}e^{-t/(2\tau_j)},\qquad j=S,L.
$$
The first factor is the ordinary <energy> phase; squaring the second gives the <exponential decay> of the survival <probability>, $e^{-t/\tau_j}$. This assumes the approximately constant <decay rate> regime; exact very early and very late behavior need not be exponential.

Writing the surviving state as $A_S(t)|K_S\rangle+A_L(t)|K_L\rangle$, invert the two <kaon CP eigenstates> definitions:
$$
\boxed{A_{K^0}(t)=\frac{A_S(t)+A_L(t)}{\sqrt2},\qquad
A_{\bar K^0}(t)=\frac{A_S(t)-A_L(t)}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
Here $t$ is the <proper time>. For a relativistic beam of fixed <Lorentz factor> $\gamma$, substitute $t=t_{\mathrm{lab}}/\gamma$; in ordinary units the <mass> phase is $m_jc^2t/\hbar$.

= Solution

Normalize the incident intensity to one. Since $|K^0\rangle=(|K_S\rangle+|K_L\rangle)/\sqrt2$, the initial <probability amplitudes> are $A_S(0)=A_L(0)=1/\sqrt2$. Define $g_j=e^{-im_jt}e^{-t/(2\tau_j)}$. The flavor <probability amplitudes> are $(g_S+g_L)/2$ and $(g_S-g_L)/2$. Their absolute squares are
$$
\boxed{I_{K^0}(t)=\frac14\left[e^{-t/\tau_S}+e^{-t/\tau_L}
+2e^{-\frac t2(1/\tau_S+1/\tau_L)}\cos(\Delta m\,t)\right],}
$$
$$
\boxed{I_{\bar K^0}(t)=\frac14\left[e^{-t/\tau_S}+e^{-t/\tau_L}
-2e^{-\frac t2(1/\tau_S+1/\tau_L)}\cos(\Delta m\,t)\right],}
$$
where $\Delta m=m_L-m_S$ in <natural units>. Thus $A=B=1/4$ for both, $\phi=\Delta m\,t$, and
$$
\boxed{C_{K^0}(t)=\tfrac12e^{-\frac t2(1/\tau_S+1/\tau_L)},\qquad
C_{\bar K^0}(t)=-\tfrac12e^{-\frac t2(1/\tau_S+1/\tau_L)}.}
$$
Multiplying all three coefficients by the incident intensity gives any other normalization. Crucially, **$C$ is time dependent**. The printed template cannot hold with all of $A,B,C$ constant. For example, a nonzero constant $C$ would leave an undamped oscillation at arbitrarily late times, even after both unstable components have decayed.

In seconds, $\phi=\Delta m\,c^2t/\hbar$. With $x=t/\tau_S$, the supplied <mass> difference and <mean lifetimes> give
$$
r_\tau=\tau_S/\tau_L=0.00172934,\qquad
\phi=0.474975x,
$$
$$
I_{K^0,\bar K^0}(x)=\frac14\left[e^{-x}+e^{-r_\tau x}
\mathbin{\pm}2e^{-(1+r_\tau)x/2}\cos(0.474975x)\right].
$$
Initially the intensities are one and zero. Their sum is $\tfrac12(e^{-x}+e^{-r_\tau x})$, so these are unconditioned survival intensities, not <probabilities> conditioned on survival. The curves first meet at $x=\pi/(2\times0.474975)\simeq3.307$. Thereafter the <neutral antikaon> component briefly exceeds the $K^0$ component, before the <interference> dies away and both approach the surviving long-lived intensity $e^{-r_\tau x}/4$.

In <Fermi's golden rule>,
$$
\Gamma=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}|M|^2\rho,
$$
$\Gamma$ is a transition <decay rate>, measured in inverse time; $\hbar$ is the <reduced Planck constant>; $M=\langle f|H_{\mathrm{int}}|i\rangle$ is the transition <matrix element> of the interaction part of the <Hamiltonian>; and $\rho$ is the final <density of states> per unit <energy>, evaluated subject to <conservation of energy>. Final <spin> and other discrete labels must be summed, with initial labels averaged when the preparation requires it. If $M$ varies across the final <phase space>, $|M|^2\rho$ denotes the corresponding integral rather than a product of constants.

A convention warning: many <quantum field theory> texts use $\Gamma$ for an <energy> <decay width>. That convention is $\Gamma_{\mathrm{width}}=\hbar\Gamma_{\mathrm{rate}}$; the formula as printed uses the rate convention.

A representative <Feynman diagram> annihilates $u\bar d$ into $W^{+*}$, producing $\nu_\mu$ and an off-shell $\mu^+$; the charged <antilepton> emits a <photon> and becomes the outgoing $\mu^+$. The top panel below shows this contribution. A complete <gauge invariant> <decay amplitude> includes radiation from the charged hadronic side, together with structure-dependent terms. The physical separation into inner <bremsstrahlung>, structure-dependent radiation, and their <interference> is explicit in the standard <radiative pion decay> description.<Radiative pion and kaon decay form factors in the 2025 Review of Particle Physics>[Section 62.1, equations (62.1)–(62.4): decomposition into inner bremsstrahlung, structure-dependent radiation and interference]{ref}

\Image[2022-radiative-weak-decays.png]
{title=Representative radiative pion decay and internal-conversion muon decay diagrams}

Here is an explicit finite **model of <phase space> alone**, with its assumptions exposed. Neglect the <neutrino> <mass>, treat the <photon> as massless, and replace the spin-summed invariant <decay amplitudes> by constants over the available <Lorentz-invariant phase space>. Write $M=m_\pi$, $m=m_\mu$, $r=m^2/M^2$, and use <natural units>. The <three-body phase space with one massive daughter> follows by factoring through an intermediate invariant squared <mass> $s$:
$$
\Phi_3(M;m,0,0)=\frac{1}{128\pi^3M^2}
\int_{m^2}^{M^2}\frac{(M^2-s)(s-m^2)}{s}\,ds
=\frac{M^2}{256\pi^3}(1-r^2+2r\ln r).
$$
The two-body comparison has $\Phi_2(M;m,0)=(1-r)/(8\pi)$. Since $\Gamma_n=\overline{|\mathcal M_n|^2}\Phi_n/(2M)$, assuming $\overline{|\mathcal M_3|^2}/\overline{|\mathcal M_2|^2}=\kappa e^2/M^2$ gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{\Gamma_{\mu\nu\gamma}}{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}}
\simeq\frac{\kappa\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}}{8\pi}
\frac{1-r^2+2r\ln r}{1-r}.}
$$
Here $\kappa$ is a dimensionless constant encapsulating the assumed <matrix element> ratio, and $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ is the <fine-structure constant>. If the two-body mode dominates, this ratio approximates the radiative <branching fraction>. Near $m=M$, $1-r^2+2r\ln r=(1-r)^3/3+O((1-r)^4)$, displaying the extra <phase space> suppression.

**This model is not a prediction of a physical uncut radiative branching fraction.** Actual inner <bremsstrahlung> has a soft-<photon> singularity, so a count of events with a separately observed <photon> requires a positive <photon> <energy> cut. To see both the missing input and the <mass> dependence explicitly, the leading soft-<photon> result for $E_{\mathrm{cut}}<E_\gamma<E_{\mathrm{soft}}\ll E_{\max}$ is
$$
\boxed{\frac{\Gamma_{\gamma,\mathrm{soft}}}{\Gamma_{\mu\nu}}
\simeq\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}}{\pi}
\left[\frac{1+r}{1-r}\ln\frac1r-2\right]
\ln\frac{E_{\mathrm{soft}}}{E_{\mathrm{cut}}},\quad
E_{\max}=\frac{M^2-m^2}{2M}.}
$$
The coefficient follows from the radiation factor $e[p_\mu\cdot\epsilon/(p_\mu\cdot k)-p_\pi\cdot\epsilon/(p_\pi\cdot k)]$: angular integration gives $\beta^{-1}\ln[(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)]-2$, where $\beta=(1-r)/(1+r)$, and the soft <energy> integral is $\int dE_\gamma/E_\gamma$. Thus a finite physical number cannot be deduced from the two <masses> and <phase space> alone. The <infrared divergence> cancels only when the corresponding unresolved real radiation and virtual <radiative corrections> are combined in an inclusive observable.

For a three-body leptonic decay through the <Fermi interaction>, neglecting daughter <masses> and <radiative corrections>, <lepton universality> gives
$$
\Gamma(\ell^-\to\ell^{\prime-}\bar\nu_{\ell'}\nu_\ell)
=\frac{G_F^2m_\ell^5}{192\pi^3}
$$
in <natural units>. Therefore scaling the <muon decay> rate by $m_\tau^5/m_\mu^5$ predicts only one leptonic channel, not the total <tau lepton> <decay rate>.

The <tau lepton> has two allowed charged-lepton channels, $e^-\bar\nu_e\nu_\tau$ and $\mu^-\bar\nu_\mu\nu_\tau$, as well as hadronic channels from $W^{-*}\to d\bar u,s\bar u$. Each allowed <quark> channel has three <color charge> choices and its <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix> factor. Charm production is kinematically inaccessible in a physical hadronic final state at this <mass>. Treating the inclusive hadronic channels by a simple <quark-hadron duality> estimate,
$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq2+3(|V_{ud}|^2+|V_{us}|^2)\simeq5.
$$
Thus, using $m_\mu\simeq105.7\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$ and $m_\tau\simeq1777\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$,
$$
\boxed{\tau_\tau\simeq\frac{2.2\times10^{-6}\,\mathrm s}{5}
\left(\frac{105.7}{1777}\right)^5\simeq3.3\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm s.}
$$
Finite daughter <masses>, hadronic <decay thresholds> and resonances, <quantum chromodynamics> <radiative corrections>, and electroweak <radiative corrections> refine this estimate. The threefold <color multiplicity in a decay width> and extra decay channels are the main correction to naive fifth-power scaling.

= Solution

<Feynman diagrams> organize the perturbative calculation of the <matrix element> in <Fermi's golden rule>. Each external line labels an incoming or outgoing particle state; each internal line contributes a <propagator> for a generally off-shell intermediate field; and each <interaction vertex> contributes a coupling and the appropriate <spin>, <momentum> and internal-symmetry structure. All indistinguishable allowed diagrams must be summed as <probability amplitudes> before taking the absolute square, so their <interference> contributes to the rate.

In the <radiative pion decay> diagram in part (b), solid arrowed lines are <fermions>; the wavy line joining the hadronic and leptonic vertices is a <W boson> <propagator>; and the wavy outgoing line is an external <photon>. Arrows track fermion number, so an outgoing <antimuon> has an arrow towards its production vertex. Each vertex conserves <four-momentum>; an internal <propagator> need not satisfy the on-shell <energy-momentum relation>. A closed <loop Feynman diagram> additionally requires an integration over an internal <four-momentum>.

The final <Lorentz-invariant phase space>, conservation <Dirac delta functions>, and the initial-state normalization or incident <flux> turn the squared <scattering amplitude> into a <decay rate> or <cross section>. A diagram supplies the dynamics; it is not itself the final <density of states>.

The following are the leading <quantum electrodynamics> diagrams. Solid arrows indicate fermion-number flow, and wavy lines indicate <photons>. The heavy source line represents a charged <nucleus>; its recoil allows <four-momentum conservation>.

\Image[2022-qed-diagrams.png]
{title=Leading Compton, electron-line bremsstrahlung and nuclear-field pair-production diagrams}

For <Compton scattering>, $e^-\gamma\to e^-\gamma$, there are two diagrams: absorption of the incident <photon> followed along the <electron> line by emission of the outgoing <photon>, and the opposite ordering. These are the $s$- and $u$-channel <electron> <propagators>. Both <scattering amplitudes> are order $e^2$.

For <bremsstrahlung>, $e^-Z\to e^-Z\gamma$, a virtual <photon> transfers <momentum> between the <electron> and the source. The real <photon> is emitted before or after this interaction along the <electron> line. These two dominant external-field diagrams have <scattering amplitudes> of order $Ze^3$. A full finite-mass charged-target calculation also includes radiation from the target; it is suppressed in the heavy stationary-source approximation used here.

For nuclear-field <pair production>, $\gamma Z\to e^-e^+Z$, crossing the preceding diagrams gives two order-$Ze^3$ contributions, with the incoming real <photon> and the exchanged virtual <photon> in either order along the pair's fermion line. An isolated real <photon> cannot produce a massive pair in vacuum: its invariant squared <mass> is zero, whereas the pair has invariant squared <mass> at least $4m_e^2$. The source absorbs recoil <momentum>.

There is a $t$-channel <Z boson> contribution and a crossed, $u$-channel <W boson> contribution. The latter pairs the incoming <electron neutrino> with the outgoing <electron>, and the incoming <electron> with the outgoing <electron neutrino>. Both contribute coherently to the same elastic final state.

\Image[2022-neutrino-diagrams.png]
{title=Neutral- and charged-current diagrams for the four elastic neutrino scattering channels}

For a concrete comparison, take unpolarized targets, the same <Mandelstam invariant> $s$ in all four reactions, and $m_e^2\ll s\ll m_W^2$. In a stationary-<electron> experiment this includes $E_\nu\gg m_e$ with $s\simeq2m_eE_\nu$. Define $x=\sin^2\theta_W$, using the <weak mixing angle>, and $\sigma_*=G_F^2s/\pi$. The <weak neutral current> has chiral couplings $g_L=x-1/2$, $g_R=x$; the <weak charged current> shifts $g_L$ by one. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\mathrm{i}}=\sigma_*\left[(x+\tfrac12)^2+\frac{x^2}{3}\right].}
$$
The factor $1/3$ comes from integrating the opposite-chirality angular weight: in the massless limit $\int_0^1(1-y)^2dy=1/3$. Thus the relative rate depends on the chiral couplings and their <interference>, rather than simply on the number of diagrams.

Only $t$-channel <Z boson> exchange contributes at leading order. A <W boson> would connect $\nu_\mu$ to $\mu^-$, rather than to $e^-$, and therefore does not supply this elastic channel. With the assumptions and normalization of part (i),

\boxed{\sigma_{\mathrm{ii}}=\sigma_*\left[(x-\tfrac12)^2+\frac{x^2}{3}\right].}

For the illustrative value $x=0.231$, the dimensionless factors in parts (i) and (ii) are $0.5521$ and $0.09015$. The electron-flavor reaction is therefore about $6.1$ times as large in this limit. This uses the <weak flavour selection in neutrino-electron scattering>, not a hypothetical flavor-changing neutral vertex.

**The decay is allowed in the <Standard Model>.** Begin with ordinary <muon decay>, $\mu^-\to\nu_\mu W^{-*}$, $W^{-*}\to e^-\bar\nu_e$. = Solution
{parent=3/a}

Let $M_P$ and $M_D$ denote neutral atomic <rest masses> for the appropriate parent and daughter, and initially neglect electronic <binding energy> corrections and daughter excitation. The <Q values> are
$$
\boxed{Q_{\beta^-}=(M_P-M_D)c^2,\qquad
Q_{\beta^+}=(M_P-M_D-2m_e)c^2,\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{EC}}=(M_P-M_D)c^2.}
$$
For <beta-minus decay>, the daughter <nucleus> has one extra positive charge. Its neutral atom contains one more <electron> than the neutral parent, cancelling the emitted <electron>'s <rest mass> in the atomic accounting. For <beta-plus decay>, the neutral parent contains one more <electron> than the neutral daughter; together with the emitted <positron>, this gives the $2m_e$ subtraction. Thus <beta-plus decay> requires an atomic <mass> difference exceeding $2m_ec^2$, whereas <electron capture> need not.

For <electron capture>, the captured bound <electron> is already included in $M_P$. Capture from a shell leaving an atomic hole of excitation <energy> $E_{\mathrm{hole}}$, and a nuclear excited state of <energy> $E_x$, gives approximately
$$
E_\nu=Q_{\mathrm{EC}}-E_x-E_{\mathrm{hole}}-T_{\mathrm{recoil}}.
$$
The hole's <energy> later appears as atomic radiation or <Auger electrons>. Subtract $E_x$ for either <beta decay> as well. Exact endpoints also depend on daughter ionization and small atomic <binding energy> changes; the boxed expressions are the conventional leading atomic-<mass> formulas.

= Solution
{parent=3/b}

For a parent at rest, <momentum conservation> gives equal and opposite daughter and <alpha particle> <momenta>. In the nonrelativistic recoil approximation,
$$
Q=T_\alpha+T_D=\frac{p^2}{2m_\alpha}+\frac{p^2}{2M_D},\qquad
\boxed{T_\alpha=Q\frac{M_D}{M_D+m_\alpha}.}
$$
Approximating nuclear <masses> by their <mass numbers>, $M_D\simeq(A-4)m_u$ and $m_\alpha\simeq4m_u$, gives
$$
\boxed{T_\alpha\simeq\frac{A-4}{A}Q.}
$$
The printed phrase “atomic number $A$” must here mean **<mass number>**. <Atomic number> alone does not determine the recoil fraction. For example, ${}^{210}\mathrm{Po}$ and ${}^{212}\mathrm{Po}$ have the same <atomic number> $84$, but the approximate fractions are $206/210$ and $208/212$, respectively.

For completeness, exact <relativistic two-body decay> <kinematics> gives
$$
T_\alpha=\left(\frac{M_P^2+m_\alpha^2-M_D^2}{2M_P}-m_\alpha\right)c^2
=Q\frac{M_D+Q/(2c^2)}{M_P},
$$
where $M_P=M_D+m_\alpha+Q/c^2$. Expanding for $Q\ll M_Pc^2$ recovers the recoil-sharing expression.

= Solution
{parent=3/c}

Model <alpha decay> as a preformed <alpha particle> making repeated escape attempts from a short-range nuclear potential well, then undergoing <quantum tunnelling> through the external <Coulomb potential>. Write the daughter <atomic number> as $Z_D$, the nuclear radius as $R$, and the relative <reduced mass> as $\mu$. For zero <orbital angular momentum>, the <WKB approximation> gives
$$
P_{\mathrm{tun}}\simeq e^{-G},\qquad
G=\frac2\hbar\int_R^{r_2}\sqrt{2\mu(V(r)-Q)}\,dr,
\qquad V(r)=\frac{k}{r},\quad
k=\frac{2Z_De^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0},\quad r_2=\frac{k}{Q}.
$$
Set $v=\sqrt{2Q/\mu}$ and $r=r_2\sin^2\theta$. Then
$$
G=\frac{2\sqrt{2\mu Q}\,r_2}{\hbar}
\left[\arccos\sqrt{\frac R{r_2}}-
\sqrt{\frac R{r_2}\left(1-\frac R{r_2}\right)}\right].
$$
When $R/r_2\ll1$, the leading term is
$$
G\simeq\frac{2\pi k}{\hbar v}
=\frac{Z_De^2}{\epsilon_0\hbar v},\qquad
\boxed{\tau\simeq(P_\alpha\nu_{\mathrm{attempt}})^{-1}
\exp\left(\frac{Z_De^2}{\epsilon_0\hbar v}\right).}
$$
Here $P_\alpha$ is the <alpha preformation probability> and $\nu_{\mathrm{attempt}}$ the attempt frequency. The stated proportionality retains only the dominant barrier exponent. It assumes a thick classically forbidden barrier, nonrelativistic relative motion, a spherical unscreened external <Coulomb potential>, and slowly varying prefactors. Finite $R$, changes in <alpha preformation probability>, and a <centrifugal barrier> for nonzero <orbital angular momentum> produce significant corrections. The precise charge in the barrier is $Z_D=Z_P-2$; using the parent charge is an approximation for large $Z_P$. The measured <alpha particle> speed approximates $v$ only when daughter recoil is negligible.

= Solution
{parent=3/d/i}

For a closed unit-branching <radioactive decay chain>, the <rate equations> are
$$
\frac{dN_a}{dt}=-\lambda_aN_a,\qquad
\frac{dN_b}{dt}=\lambda_aN_a-\lambda_bN_b.
$$
The first gives $N_a=N_{a0}e^{-\lambda_at}$. Multiply the second by its <integrating factor> $e^{\lambda_bt}$:
$$
\frac{d}{dt}(N_be^{\lambda_bt})
=\lambda_aN_{a0}e^{(\lambda_b-\lambda_a)t}.
$$
Integrate from zero to $t$. For $\lambda_a\ne\lambda_b$,
$$
N_b=N_{b0}e^{-\lambda_bt}
+\frac{\lambda_aN_{a0}}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}
(e^{-\lambda_at}-e^{-\lambda_bt}),
$$
so
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{\lambda_aN_{a0}}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a},\qquad
\beta=N_{b0}-\frac{\lambda_aN_{a0}}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}.}
$$
The coefficients can individually be negative; the physical population is nonnegative because the production integral is nonnegative. For equal <nuclear decay constants> $\lambda_a=\lambda_b=\lambda$, the finite limit is
$$
\boxed{N_b=(N_{b0}+\lambda N_{a0}t)e^{-\lambda t}.}
$$
A sum of two constant-coefficient copies of the same <exponential function> cannot represent this limit; the $t$ factor is essential.

= ii
{parent=3/d}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=3/d/ii}

For $N_{b0}=0$, differentiate the distinct-<nuclear decay constant> result:
$$
\dot N_b=\frac{\lambda_aN_{a0}}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}
(-\lambda_ae^{-\lambda_at}+\lambda_be^{-\lambda_bt}).
$$
Equating the production and loss terms gives $e^{(\lambda_b-\lambda_a)t}=\lambda_b/\lambda_a$, hence
$$
\boxed{t_{\max}=\frac{\ln(\lambda_b/\lambda_a)}{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}.}
$$
For positive <nuclear decay constants> and $N_{a0}>0$, this is positive regardless of their ordering. Initially $\dot N_b>0$, while $N_b\to0$ at late times; the stationary point is the unique maximum. At that time $\lambda_aN_a=\lambda_bN_b$. If the <nuclear decay constants> are equal, differentiating $\lambda N_{a0}te^{-\lambda t}$ gives $\boxed{t_{\max}=1/\lambda}$, also the continuous limit of the expression above.

= Solution
{parent=3/d/iii}

A stable terminal product obeys $\dot N_c=\lambda_bN_b$. Conservation of the number of chain members gives $N_a+N_b+N_c=N_{a0}+N_{c0}$ when $N_{b0}=0$. Therefore, for distinct <nuclear decay constants>,
$$
\boxed{N_c(t)=N_{c0}+N_{a0}\left[1-
\frac{\lambda_be^{-\lambda_at}-\lambda_ae^{-\lambda_bt}}
{\lambda_b-\lambda_a}\right].}
$$
Set $N_{c0}=0$ for an initially absent terminal product. For equal <nuclear decay constants>,
$$
\boxed{N_c(t)=N_{c0}+N_{a0}[1-(1+\lambda t)e^{-\lambda t}].}
$$
Both expressions have $\dot N_c(0)=0$ because the intermediate population initially vanishes; their leading growth is $\tfrac12N_{a0}\lambda_a\lambda_bt^2$. For positive <nuclear decay constants>, $N_c$ increases monotonically to $N_{c0}+N_{a0}$.

= Solution
{parent=4/b}

The diagram refers to <ground state>, $L=0$, <color singlet> <baryons>. Their spatial <wavefunction> is symmetric and their color <wavefunction> is antisymmetric. The <Pauli exclusion principle> therefore requires a symmetric combined spin-and-flavor <wavefunction>. One cannot independently attach every three-spin coupling to each flavor assignment.

For three spin-$1/2$ particles, there are formally two ways to couple to total <spin> $1/2$. In the <ground state> <quark model> these are tied to the corresponding mixed-symmetry flavor functions. Their required symmetric combination yields one physical spin-$1/2$ flavor octet, not two independent species at each peripheral weight. In <spin-flavor symmetry> notation,
$$
\mathbf{56}_{SU(6)}=(\mathbf{10},\mathbf4)\oplus(\mathbf8,\mathbf2)
$$
under flavor $SU(3)$ and spin $SU(2)$. Thus there is one peripheral species in the <baryon octet>, each still possessing its two ordinary spin projections. The two abstract flavor-octet copies in $\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3\otimes\mathbf3$ do not double the physical <ground state> octet.

At the dotted positions, the flavor content would be $uuu$, $ddd$ or $sss$. These flavor <wavefunctions> are completely symmetric. The spin <wavefunction> must then also be completely symmetric, which means total <spin> $3/2$, not $1/2$. The corresponding <baryon decuplet> states are $\Delta^{++}$, $\Delta^-$ and $\Omega^-$. This exclusion is for the stated <ground state> spin-$1/2$ multiplet; it is not an exclusion of all excited <baryons> at those weights.

Both central <baryons> have <valence quarks> $uds$, <baryon number> $1$, <strangeness> $-1$ and <isospin> projection zero. Their total <isospins> differ:
$$
\boxed{\Sigma^0:\ I=1,\ I_z=0;\qquad
\Lambda^0:\ I=0,\ I_z=0.}

The $ud$ flavor part is symmetric for the <Sigma baryon> and antisymmetric for the <Lambda baryon>. = Solution
{parent=4/d}

Measured half-integer <spin> and fermionic behavior rule out a simple <quark>-<antiquark> pair: two spin-$1/2$ constituents have integer total <spin>, and adding integer <orbital angular momentum> does not change that. Their measured <baryon number> $B=1$, inferred through production and conservation bookkeeping, corresponds to $n_q-n_{\bar q}=3$. Their pattern of <electric charges>, <strangeness> and <isospin> then agrees with three light <valence quarks>.

<Baryon magnetic moments> provide a more detailed check. The additive <quark model> gives, for example,
$$
\mu_p=\frac43\mu_u-\frac13\mu_d,\qquad
\mu_n=\frac43\mu_d-\frac13\mu_u.
$$
Approximately equal up and down constituent <masses> give $\mu_u\simeq-2\mu_d$, hence $\mu_p/\mu_n\simeq-3/2$, close to the measured ratio of about $-1.46$.

These observations support three **valence** <quarks>; they do not prove the absence of extra <quark>-<antiquark> pairs. For instance, $qqqq\bar q$ also has $B=1$ and can have half-integer <spin>. Ordinary <quantum chromodynamics> <baryons> also contain sea pairs and <gluons>. The stronger interpretation that the measurements exclude every <antiquark> component is therefore false.

= Solution

Write the charge in units of $e$ as $Q=aI_z+b(B+S)$. The <proton> and <neutron> have $(I_z,B+S)=(1/2,1)$ and $(-1/2,1)$, so their measured charges imply $1=a/2+b$ and $0=-a/2+b$. Thus $a=1$, $b=1/2$:
$$
\boxed{Q=I_z+\frac{B+S}{2}.}
$$
This is the <Gell-Mann--Nishijima formula>, with the full <flavor hypercharge> $Y=B+S$. Applying the <quark> <baryon number> $1/3$ and the <isospin> and <strangeness> assignments from part (a),
$$
\boxed{Q_u=\frac12+\frac16=\frac23,\quad
Q_d=-\frac12+\frac16=-\frac13,\quad
Q_s=0+\frac{1/3-1}{2}=-\frac13.}
$$
Physical <electric charges> are these numbers multiplied by the positive elementary charge $e$.

= Solution
{parent=4/f}

The observed dominant two-body transition is $\boxed{\Sigma^0\to\Lambda^0\gamma}$. Both <baryons> have <valence quarks> $uds$ and the same <strangeness>; an <electromagnetic interaction> transition is therefore possible. Their <mass> difference is only about $77\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$, below the lightest <pion> <mass>, so no additional on-shell strongly interacting particle fits. A <photon> is neutral and massless, and can carry the required <angular momentum> through a <magnetic dipole transition> between the two positive-<parity> spin-$1/2$ states. A single <neutrino> would violate <lepton number> and would not give the required fermion parity.

At rest, <four-momentum conservation> gives $p_\Lambda=p_\Sigma-k$ and $k^2=0$. Thus
$$
M_\Lambda^2c^4=M_\Sigma^2c^4-2M_\Sigma c^2E_\gamma,
\qquad
\boxed{E_\gamma=\frac{M_\Sigma^2-M_\Lambda^2}{2M_\Sigma}c^2.}
$$
The result is slightly below $(M_\Sigma-M_\Lambda)c^2$ because the <Lambda baryon> recoils; rounded <masses> give about $74.5\,\mathrm{MeV}$.

The channels $n\pi^0$ and $p\pi^-$ are actually kinematically open: their total <rest energies> are about $1075$ and $1078\,\mathrm{MeV}$, below $M_\Sigma c^2\simeq1193\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Their suppression is therefore **not** a <decay threshold> prohibition. Each changes <strangeness> from $-1$ to zero and so requires a <weak interaction>. The much faster allowed electromagnetic transition makes such weak <branching fractions> extremely small. “Not observed” should be understood as overwhelming electromagnetic competition, not an exact ban in the <Standard Model>. The photon identification likewise uses the known low-<energy> spectrum and dominant transition mechanism, not neutrality alone.

The nine flavor combinations form $\mathbf3\otimes\bar{\mathbf3}=\mathbf8\oplus\mathbf1$. Here $B=0$, so the vertical coordinate $B+S$ equals <strangeness>. At the center, $u\bar u,d\bar d,s\bar s$ all have $I_z=S=0$. A useful orthonormal flavor basis is
$$
\boxed{|\pi^0\rangle=\frac{|u\bar u\rangle-|d\bar d\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|\eta_8\rangle=\frac{|u\bar u\rangle+|d\bar d\rangle-2|s\bar s\rangle}{\sqrt6},\qquad
|\eta_1\rangle=\frac{|u\bar u\rangle+|d\bar d\rangle+|s\bar s\rangle}{\sqrt3}.}
$$
The <neutral pion> has $I=1$; $\eta_8$ and $\eta_1$ have $I=0$. The <eta octet state> belongs to the octet, while $\eta_1$ is the flavor singlet. The physical <eta and eta prime mesons> are predominantly mixtures of these two isoscalar basis states,
$$
|\eta\rangle=\cos\theta\,|\eta_8\rangle-\sin\theta\,|\eta_1\rangle,\qquad
|\eta'\rangle=\sin\theta\,|\eta_8\rangle+\cos\theta\,|\eta_1\rangle.
$$
Unequal <quark masses> and the <chiral anomaly> prevent exact flavor symmetry and a degenerate nonet. The three central physical species are consequently $\pi^0,\eta,\eta'$, rather than three separately observable pure diagonal flavor pairs. Small isospin-breaking admixtures are neglected here.
