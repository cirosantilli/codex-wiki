= Solution
{parent=a}

**All three interactions respect <color charge>; <quark flavor> is conserved by the <strong interaction> and the <electromagnetic interaction>, but can change in the <weak interaction>.** These statements concern charges and net numbers, rather than an unchanging inventory of particles.

A <quark> transforms in the fundamental <representation> of the <color charge> <gauge group> $SU(3)_C$. The <strong interaction> can change an individual <quark>'s colour by exchanging a <gluon>, which itself carries <color charge>. For example, the colour flow $q_r\to q_b+g_{r\bar b}$ is allowed: demanding that the outgoing <quark> remain red would be incorrect. At each <interaction vertex> the full colour flow is conserved. The colourless <photon>, <W boson>, and <Z boson> instead couple to a <quark> with an identity factor $\delta_{ab}$ in colour space. A <weak charged current> can therefore change $d_r$ to $u_r$, but cannot turn $d_r$ into $u_b$ without another coloured participant. External isolated <hadrons> are <colour singlets>; <confinement> prevents direct observation of a free coloured state.

For each <quark flavor> $f$, define the <net quark flavor number> $F_f=N_f-N_{\bar f}$. The <strong interaction> and <electromagnetic interaction> preserve every $F_f$: pair creation $g\to s\bar s$, for example, changes neither net <strangeness> nor any other $F_f$. Their <quark> couplings are diagonal in <quark flavor>. The <weak charged current> contains $\bar u_i\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)V_{ij}d_j$, where $V$ is the <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix>, and changes these net numbers. <Weak neutral currents> are diagonal in <tree-level Feynman diagrams>; <flavor-changing neutral currents> can occur through higher-order <Feynman diagrams>.

The observed hierarchy of <mean lifetimes> and <decay channels> supplies evidence: associated strange-particle production through the <strong interaction> preserves net <strangeness>, whereas a <kaon>'s <weak interaction> decay to <pions> changes it. The <electromagnetic interaction> process $\pi^0\to\gamma\gamma$ has zero net <quark flavor> before and after annihilation. <Neutrino oscillations> show that individual <lepton> family numbers are not exact conserved quantities. Within the usual effectively massless-<neutrino> approximation, <charged current> decays such as $\mu^-\to e^-\bar\nu_e\nu_\mu$ preserve family numbers only when the emitted <neutrinos> are counted.

Evidence for three colours includes the <hadronic R ratio> $R=3\sum_fQ_f^2$, the colour multiplicities in <W boson> <decay widths>, and <three-jet events> revealing <gluon> radiation. These test <quantum chromodynamics> and its colour structure together; they are not direct measurements of a named red or blue charge on a free <quark>.

**Two mechanisms are a <tau lepton> pair with two leptonic decays, and a <W boson> pair with two leptonic decays.** With equal beam <energies>, the laboratory is the <centre-of-mass frame>, and <four-momentum conservation> gives $E_{\rm miss}=\sqrt{s}-E_{e^-}-E_{\mu^+}$ in <natural units>.

For the first mechanism,
$$
e^+e^-\to\gamma^*,Z^*\to\tau^+\tau^-,\qquad
\tau^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu\bar\nu_\tau,\qquad
\tau^-\to e^-\bar\nu_e\nu_\tau.
$$
There are four escaping <neutrinos>. Each <tau lepton> decay proceeds through a virtual <W boson>. The fixed-charge final state has <branching fraction> $B_\mu B_e$; the factor two for the inclusive $e^\pm\mu^\mp$ sample does not apply when the charges are specified.

For the second mechanism,
$$
e^+e^-\to W^+W^-,\qquad
W^+\to\mu^+\nu_\mu,\qquad W^-\to e^-\bar\nu_e.
$$
There are two escaping <neutrinos>. The leading <Feynman diagrams> comprise both $s$-channel <photon>/<Z boson> exchange and $t$-channel <electron neutrino> exchange. Their <scattering amplitudes> interfere; the complete set is needed for a consistent high-<energy> prediction. In particular, the neutrino-exchange diagram alone does not define an independently observable production rate.

\Image[2021-leptonic-production-diagrams.png]
{title=Tau-pair decay and the three leading W-pair production diagrams}

A <tau lepton> pair has enhanced production at the <Z boson> <particle resonance>, $\sqrt{s}\simeq91\,\mathrm{GeV}$. The four-<neutrino> <missing energy> has a broad continuous distribution. To derive a useful schematic, neglect daughter <masses> and parent <spin> correlations. In an unpolarized <tau lepton> rest frame, the <Michel spectrum> for $x=2E_\ell^*/m_\tau$ is
$$
f(x)=2x^2(3-2x),\qquad0\leq x\leq1,\qquad \langle x\rangle=\frac7{10}.
$$
A <Lorentz boost> gives $E_\ell/E_\tau=x(1+\beta_\tau u)/2$, with $u=\cos\theta^*$ uniform on $[-1,1]$ in this approximation. For $\beta_\tau\simeq1$, the charged-daughter <probability density function> in $z=E_\ell/E_\tau$ is
$$
g(z)=\int_z^1\frac{f(x)}{x}\,dx
=\frac53-3z^2+\frac43z^3,\qquad0\leq z\leq1.
$$
For two independent decays, $y=E_{\rm miss}/\sqrt{s}=1-(z_1+z_2)/2$ has the <convolution>
$$
p_\tau(y)=2\int_{\max(0,1-2y)}^{\min(1,2-2y)}
g(z)g(2-2y-z)\,dz,\qquad0\leq y\leq1,
$$
and $\langle y\rangle=0.65$. It vanishes at both endpoints and peaks on the larger-missing-<energy> side. This is a shape illustration, not the exact polarized <Z boson> prediction; <spin> correlations, acceptance, and radiative effects alter it.

The <W boson> process turns on near $\sqrt{s}=2m_W\simeq161\,\mathrm{GeV}$. At exact on-shell <production threshold>, each <neutrino> has <energy> $m_W/2$, so
$$
\boxed{E_{\rm miss}=m_W=\frac{\sqrt{s}}2\quad\text{at threshold}.}
$$
The <cross section> vanishes at the ideal two-body threshold, so this narrow limiting spectrum must not be called the maximum-rate spectrum. The large <W boson>-pair rate occurs above threshold, around $200\,\mathrm{GeV}$. There $\beta_W=\sqrt{1-4m_W^2/s}$ and each <neutrino> has
$$
E_\nu=\frac{\sqrt{s}}4(1+\beta_W\cos\theta^*),\qquad
\frac{\sqrt{s}}4(1-\beta_W)\leq E_\nu\leq\frac{\sqrt{s}}4(1+\beta_W).
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\frac{1-\beta_W}{2}\leq\frac{E_{\rm miss}}{\sqrt{s}}\leq\frac{1+\beta_W}{2}.}
$$
An isotropic independent-decay schematic is triangular, centered at $E_{\rm miss}/\sqrt{s}=1/2$, with endpoints approximately $0.20$ and $0.80$ at $200\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Actual <W boson> <particle polarizations> and their correlations change this shape; their <V-A interaction> is not isotropic for a fixed <helicity>. The finite <decay width>, beam spread, and radiation also smear the edges.

\Image[2021-missing-energy-spectra.png]
{title=Missing-energy shapes for explicitly stated spin-averaged decay approximations}

The phrase “maximum rate” also needs a production regime: a <Z boson> peak is a resonant maximum, whereas pure <photon>-mediated <tau lepton> production has its continuum maximum near $4.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$. Indeed, $\sigma_\gamma\propto\beta_\tau(3-\beta_\tau^2)/s$ has $s=(1+\sqrt{21})m_\tau^2$. The displayed high-boost curve describes the resonant regime; at the continuum maximum use $\beta_\tau\simeq0.532$ in the boost formula. An observed event-rate maximum additionally depends on <collider luminosity> and <detection efficiency>.

**The ideal two-shower event fraction is $B_e^2$, approximately $4\%$ in simple channel counting or $3.18\%$ using the measured <tau lepton> electronic <branching fraction>.** The selected <particle decay> chain is
$$
\tau^-\to e^-\bar\nu_e\nu_\tau,\qquad
\tau^+\to e^+\nu_e\bar\nu_\tau.
$$
The <electron> and <positron> each make a contained <electromagnetic shower>; <muons> penetrate, and generic <hadrons> do not deposit all their <energy> as a contained <electromagnetic shower>.

To estimate $B_e$, use <lepton universality> and neglect final <masses> and <quantum chromodynamics> corrections. The electronic and muonic <decay widths> each carry weight one. The accessible inclusive hadronic channels have weights $3|V_{ud}|^2$ and $3|V_{us}|^2$, where three is the <color multiplicity in a decay width>. The <charm quark> is inaccessible in physical <tau lepton> hadronic decays. With $|V_{ud}|^2+|V_{us}|^2\simeq1$, the total weight is $1+1+3=5$, giving
$$
B_e\simeq\frac15,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{N_{\rm two\ showers}}{N_{\tau^+\tau^-}}=B_e^2\simeq\frac1{25}.}
$$
The 2021 measured value $B_e=0.1782$ instead gives $B_e^2=0.0317552$.<Review of Particle Physics by Particle Data Group (2021)>[Tau listing, p. 6, mode 5: electronic branching fraction $(17.82\pm0.04)\%$]{ref} The difference reflects effects omitted by the simple free-channel estimate, especially the hadronic contribution to the total <decay width>.

There are two <tau leptons> per produced pair and two per selected pair, so the fraction of all produced <tau leptons> belonging to these two-shower events is also $B_e^2$. The unrestricted fraction of individual <tau leptons> decaying electronically is $B_e$; these are different selections. A real high-<energy> shower cut gives an additional acceptance factor: $N_{\rm selected}/N_{\rm pairs}=B_e^2\varepsilon_{ee}$, which cannot be computed without the cut and detector response. No factor of two is needed for the unique $e^+e^-$ assignment.

**For a purely <electromagnetic interaction> continuum with five active <quark flavors>, the usual estimate is $N_{e^+e^-}/N_{\rm had}=3/11$.** The unspecified <particle resonance> and the word “total” require qualifications before this can be treated as a unique physical prediction.

In <natural units>, neglecting final <masses>, single-<photon> annihilation gives
$$
\sigma(\mu^+\mu^-\to e^+e^-)=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s},\qquad
\sigma(\mu^+\mu^-\to q_f\bar q_f)=3Q_f^2\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}.
$$
Here $\alpha$ is the <fine-structure constant>, $Q_f$ the <quark> <electric charge> in elementary-charge units, and the factor three counts orthogonal colour final states. Consequently the inverse <hadronic R ratio> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{N_{e^+e^-}}{N_{\rm had}}=\frac1{3\sum_{f\ {\rm active}}Q_f^2}.}
$$
With $u,d,s,c,b$ accessible, the denominator is $3[2(2/3)^2+3(1/3)^2]=11/3$. The corresponding values for $u,d,s$ and $u,d,s,c$ are $1/2$ and $3/10$. Inclusive <quantum chromodynamics> corrections increase the hadronic denominator, approximately by $1+\alpha_s/\pi$ away from thresholds and <particle resonances>. These are annihilation ratios before selection and <detection efficiency> corrections, not ratios of uncorrected detector counts.

A future <muon-collider Higgs resonance> is a plausible reading of part (c), since its narrow <decay width> makes a detuning of a few $\mathrm{MeV}$ relevant. Even then, two further assumptions are necessary. First, being a few $\mathrm{MeV}$ below the <Higgs boson> peak does not eliminate it: for detuning $\Delta=\sqrt{s}-m_H$, the ideal narrow <Breit-Wigner resonance cross section> retains the relative factor
$$
\frac{\sigma_H(\Delta)}{\sigma_H(0)}\simeq
\frac{\Gamma_H^2}{4\Delta^2+\Gamma_H^2}.
$$
For $\Gamma_H=4\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Delta=-4\,\mathrm{MeV}$ this is $1/5$, not zero. Resonant hadronic production can still change the requested denominator substantially.

Second, at $\sqrt{s}\simeq125\,\mathrm{GeV}$ the <Z boson> continuum is not negligible. A more complete annihilation estimate including both <tree-level Feynman diagrams> uses
$$
\sigma_f=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}N_c^fF_f(s),\qquad
F_f=Q_f^2-2Q_fv_\mu v_f\operatorname{Re}\chi
+(v_\mu^2+a_\mu^2)(v_f^2+a_f^2)|\chi|^2,
$$
$$
v_f=T_3^f-2Q_fs_W^2,\quad a_f=T_3^f,\quad
\chi=\frac{s}{4s_W^2(1-s_W^2)(s-m_Z^2+i m_Z\Gamma_Z)}.
$$
Here $s_W^2$ is the squared sine of the <weak mixing angle>, $T_3^f$ is <weak isospin>, and $N_c^f$ is one for a <lepton> and three for a <quark>. Thus the continuum ratio is $F_e/[3(2F_u+3F_d)]$, rather than necessarily $3/11$. For example, $s_W^2=0.231$, $m_Z=91.1876\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $\Gamma_Z=2.4952\,\mathrm{GeV}$ give approximately $0.102$ at $125\,\mathrm{GeV}$ before <quantum chromodynamics> corrections.

If the unspecified <particle resonance> instead means the <Z boson>, detuning by a few $\mathrm{MeV}$ is tiny compared with its $2.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ <decay width>. The resonant ratio remains approximately $\Gamma(Z\to e^+e^-)/\Gamma(Z\to\mathrm{hadrons})=1/20.767\simeq0.0482$.<Review of Particle Physics by Particle Data Group (2021)>[Z listing, p. 9: hadronic-to-single-lepton partial-width ratio $20.767\pm0.025$]{ref} This is a concrete counterexample to treating any slight downward detuning as permission to discard the <particle resonance>. The source supplies neither its identity nor the detuning and selection needed for a unique all-process ratio. The boxed continuum formula is the charge-counting answer under its stated approximation; a literal total must also include remaining resonant and other accepted hadronic channels.

**In ordinary perturbative <Standard Model> reactions all three interactions conserve <electric charge>, <four-momentum>, total <angular momentum>, <baryon number>, and total <lepton number>.** The last two are accidental conservation laws of these perturbative processes, not exact nonperturbative statements: <electroweak sphalerons> violate $B+L$ while preserving <baryon-minus-lepton number>. Total <color charge> is respected throughout.

The <strong interaction> and <electromagnetic interaction> also conserve each <net quark flavor number>, including <strangeness>, <charm quantum number> and bottom flavour. The <weak charged current> can change them through the <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix>. <Isospin> is an approximate <strong interaction> symmetry in the small light-<quark> <mass> difference limit; the different <electric charges> of $u$ and $d$ break it in the <electromagnetic interaction>. Approximate <flavor symmetry> is distinct from exact net-flavour-number conservation by those interactions.

<Parity conservation> and <charge conjugation symmetry> hold for the <electromagnetic interaction> and, assuming a negligible strong CP parameter, the <strong interaction>. The <weak interaction> violates both, and also violates <CP symmetry> in suitable processes. <CPT symmetry> holds under the usual local <Lorentz invariant> <quantum field theory> assumptions. Individual <lepton> family numbers are conserved in the conventional massless-<neutrino> approximation but not in <neutrino oscillations>; an assertion of exact family conservation in all observed reactions would therefore be false.

**The <magnetic rigidity> gives the <momentum>, the bending direction gives the sign of <electric charge>, and the calorimetric <energy> then gives the <mass>.** For planar tracks perpendicular to a uniform <magnetic field>, the <Lorentz force> and circular motion give
$$
p=|q|Br,\qquad
p\,[\mathrm{GeV}/c]=0.2998\,|q/e|\,B\,[\mathrm T]r\,[\mathrm m].
$$
Trace each track away from its production or <decay vertex>. With the <magnetic field> into the page, a positive track bends counterclockwise and a negative track bends clockwise. For example, a positive particle initially travelling left accelerates downwards. The known incoming negative <pion> fixes the sign convention experimentally. A radius alone does not distinguish <electric charge> signs.

For a calibrated total <energy> $E$, the <relativistic energy-momentum relation> gives
$$
\boxed{m^2c^4=E^2-p^2c^2.}
$$
If the calorimeter instead supplies only <kinetic energy> $T$, solve $(T+mc^2)^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$ to obtain
$$
\boxed{mc^2=\frac{p^2c^2-T^2}{2T}.}
$$
Compare with candidate <pion>, <kaon>, and <proton> <masses>, together with the <electric charge> sign and shower behavior. An <electron> makes an <electromagnetic shower>; a <muon> is penetrating; a <hadron> generally requires a hadronic calorimeter and its own calibration. If either <energy> escapes or the response is uncalibrated, simply substituting a deposited signal for $E$ need not identify the particle correctly. The absent figure prevents attaching identities to individual drawn tracks, but the requested identification procedure does not require its geometry.

**The labels X and Y cannot be uniquely assigned from the surviving text: their <decay vertices> and daughter tracks were contained in the missing figure.** The available <conservation laws> nevertheless specify how the assignment would be obtained.

The initial $\pi^-p$ state has $(Q,B,S)=(0,1,0)$, with $S$ denoting <strangeness>. At a primary <strong interaction> vertex all three numbers must be reproduced. A final <Lambda baryon> or <Sigma baryon> has $B=1$, $S=-1$ and therefore needs accompanying $S=+1$, for example a neutral <kaon> $K^0=d\bar s$ or a positive <kaon> $K^+=u\bar s$, with the remaining <electric charge> balanced. At later <weak interaction> <decay vertices>, <strangeness> need not be conserved; it would be wrong to enforce it there.

A displaced $p\pi^-$ pair with the appropriate <invariant mass> identifies $\Lambda^0\to p\pi^-$. A displaced $\pi^+\pi^-$ pair can identify $K_S^0\to\pi^+\pi^-$ after an <invariant mass> and kinematic check. For a candidate <radiative Sigma-to-Lambda transition>, the neutral parent satisfies
$$
p_\Sigma=p_\Lambda+p_\gamma,\qquad
p_\Lambda=p_p+p_{\pi^-},\qquad
p_\Sigma^2=m_\Sigma^2,\quad p_\gamma^2=0
$$
in <natural units>. Its <photon> <energy> in the parent rest frame is $(m_\Sigma^2-m_\Lambda^2)/(2m_\Sigma)$. With known charged <four-momenta>, each unobserved neutral <momentum> is the difference between the incoming and visible outgoing <momenta> at its vertex; its inferred direction must agree with the dashed segment.

This information cannot establish which dashed segment was called X. For a concrete ambiguity, both
$$
\pi^-p\to K^0\Lambda^0,\qquad
\pi^-p\to K^0\Sigma^0\to K^0\Lambda^0\gamma
$$
obey the primary <electric charge>, <baryon number>, and <strangeness> constraints and can yield a neutral <kaon> decay plus a displaced <Lambda baryon> decay. Their distinct neutral segments and the extra <photon> would be resolved by the missing topology and kinematics. The supplied possible <Sigma baryon> decay alone is insufficient to label X and Y. A labelled answer is therefore a source gap, rather than a completed identification.

**The conversion from a measured beam peak to a <particle resonance> <mass> is fixed, but the missing graph supplies neither the peak position nor its width.** Let $T_R$ be the incoming <pion> <kinetic energy> at the peak and $\Delta T$ the <full width at half maximum> after allowing for instrumental resolution. With a stationary <proton> and <natural units>,
$$
s=m_p^2+m_\pi^2+2m_p(m_\pi+T)
=(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT.
$$
Thus the peak <invariant mass> is
$$
\boxed{M=\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_R}.}
$$
The width must also be transformed to the <centre-of-mass frame>. To first order about the peak,
$$
\frac{d\sqrt{s}}{dT}=\frac{m_p}{\sqrt{s}},\qquad
\boxed{\Gamma\simeq\frac{m_p}{M}\Delta T,\qquad
\tau=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma}.}
$$
For a broad feature, transform the two half-height positions separately: if they are $T_-$ and $T_+$, then the corresponding width in <invariant mass> is $\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_+}-\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT_-}$. An energy-dependent numerator or width can also displace the observed maximum from the <resonance pole> parameter.

The low-<energy> $\pi^+p$ channel is normally dominated by $\Delta^{++}(1232)$, a <Delta baryon> with $Q=2$, $B=1$ and $I=3/2$. As an **external reference example, not a reading of the absent plot**, $M=1232\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\Gamma=117\,\mathrm{MeV}$ give $T_R\simeq190\,\mathrm{MeV}$, $\Delta T\simeq154\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\tau\simeq5.63\times10^{-24}\,\mathrm s$.<Delta(1232) particle listing by Particle Data Group>[Breit-Wigner mass near 1232 MeV and width near 117 MeV; these are external comparison values]{ref} The actual graphical estimates remain unavailable. In particular, using the laboratory width directly in $\tau=\hbar/\Delta T$ misses the factor $m_p/M$.

= Solution
{parent=e}

**A constant-width <Breit-Wigner resonance cross section> is the simplest nonrelativistic curve; a <P-wave scattering> width and relativistic kinematics explain important expected distortions.** A comparison to specific observed deviations cannot be made without the missing plot.

Write $E$ for the <centre-of-mass frame> <kinetic energy>, $\mu=m_pm_\pi/(m_p+m_\pi)$ for the <reduced mass>, and $k$ for the relative wave number. The nonrelativistic relations are
$$
E\simeq\frac{m_p}{m_p+m_\pi}T,\qquad
k^2=\frac{2\mu E}{\hbar^2}.
$$
For an isolated <particle resonance> of <spin> $J$, averaging over the initial <proton> <spin> and using the zero <pion> <spin> gives
$$
\sigma_{\rm el}(E)=\frac{\pi}{k^2}\frac{2J+1}{2}
\frac{\Gamma_{\pi p}^2}{(E-E_R)^2+\Gamma^2/4}.
$$
For the nearly elastic $\Delta^{++}$ state, $J=3/2$ and $\Gamma_{\pi p}\simeq\Gamma$, so
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm el}(E)\simeq\frac{8\pi}{k^2}
\frac{\Gamma^2/4}{(E-E_R)^2+\Gamma^2/4}.}
$$
Freezing $k$ at $k_R$ gives a symmetric Lorentzian centered at $E_R$, with <full width at half maximum> $\Gamma$ and height $8\pi/k_R^2$. This is the local curve to sketch. If the displayed variable is the beam <kinetic energy> $T$, its width is $\Delta T\simeq(m_p+m_\pi)\Gamma/m_p$ within this nonrelativistic approximation. Keeping $k(E)$ changes the prefactor and hence the symmetry. Extending a constant-width approximation all the way to $E=0$ produces a spurious divergence, so it is not a valid threshold model.

The <pion> has <intrinsic parity> $-1$ and the <proton> has $+1$. The positive <parity> and $J=3/2$ of the <Delta baryon> require odd <orbital angular momentum>, with the lowest choice $l=1$. The <finite-range partial-wave threshold law> gives $\Gamma_{\pi p}(k)\propto k^{2l+1}=k^3$ near threshold. Inserting this in the elastic numerator makes $\sigma_{\rm el}\propto k^4$ away from the pole as $k\to0$, rather than diverging. Equivalently the elastic <scattering phase shift> has $\delta_1=O(k^3)$, and $\sin^2\delta_1/k^2=O(k^4)$. Away from threshold, form factors and other channels modify the simple cubic width.

At the illustrative peak of part (d), the incident <pion> <kinetic energy> is comparable to its rest <energy>, so a nonrelativistic <pion> treatment is already inaccurate. The exact relative <momentum> is
$$
p_*^2=\frac{[s-(m_p+m_\pi)^2][s-(m_p-m_\pi)^2]}{4s},\qquad k=\frac{p_*}{\hbar},
$$
with $\sqrt{s}=\sqrt{(m_p+m_\pi)^2+2m_pT}$. Relative to the symmetric local estimate, this changes the horizontal conversion, peak normalization and tails. The illustrative $1232\,\mathrm{MeV}$ peak has $p_*\simeq227\,\mathrm{MeV}/c$, giving $8\pi(\hbar c)^2/(p_*c)^2\simeq190\,\mathrm{mb}$ before small inelastic effects.

Finally, nonresonant <partial waves> can interfere with the resonant <scattering amplitude>, higher <particle resonances> and inelastic <scattering channels> can enter, and instrumental resolution can broaden a measured peak. These are physically distinct possible deviations; the missing data do not justify claiming any particular one was actually observed. Both the missing graphical overlay and its data-specific explanation remain source gaps.
