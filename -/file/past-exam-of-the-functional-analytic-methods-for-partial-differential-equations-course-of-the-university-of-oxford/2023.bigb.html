== 1

=== a
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Choose a nonnegative <mollifier> $\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ with $\int\rho=1$, and put $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-3}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$. <Sobolev approximation by mollifiers> in the <Sobolev space> under consideration states
$$
\boxed{u_\varepsilon:=\rho_\varepsilon*u\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3)\cap W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3),\qquad
\|u_\varepsilon-u\|_{W^{1,6}}\longrightarrow0\quad(\varepsilon\downarrow0).}
$$
In particular, it gives <density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space>. We prove convergence of both the function and all its first <weak derivatives>.

The usual smoothing properties of <convolution> give $u_\varepsilon\in C^\infty$. For a <test function> $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^3)$ and $i\in\{1,2,3\}$, <Fubini's theorem> and the definition of a <weak derivative> give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int u_\varepsilon(x)\partial_i\phi(x)\,dx
&=\int\rho_\varepsilon(z)\int u(y)\partial_i\phi(y+z)\,dy\,dz\\
&=-\int\rho_\varepsilon(z)\int\partial_i u(y)\phi(y+z)\,dy\,dz\\
&=-\int(\rho_\varepsilon*\partial_i u)(x)\phi(x)\,dx.
\end{aligned}
$$
The <integrals> are absolutely convergent: the <compact supports> of $\rho_\varepsilon$ and $\phi$ restrict $y$ to a <compact set>, on which $u$ and $\partial_i u$ are integrable by <Holder inequality>. Consequently
$$
\partial_i u_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*\partial_i u.
$$
The <approximate identity> theorem in the <Lp space> $L^6$ applies separately to $u$ and $\partial_i u$. Thus
$$
\|u_\varepsilon-u\|_6\to0,\qquad
\|\partial_i u_\varepsilon-\partial_i u\|_6\to0\quad(i=1,2,3).
$$
Each <convolution> belongs to $L^6$ by <Young's convolution inequality>. With the componentwise <Sobolev norm>, the sixth power of the required difference is the sum of these four sixth powers, proving the boxed claim. Any equivalent <Sobolev norm> gives the same convergence.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Here <Morrey's inequality> has <Hölder exponent> $1-3/6=1/2$. Every $u\in W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)$ has a unique continuous representative $u^*$ such that
$$
\boxed{\|u^*\|_{C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)},\qquad
[u^*]_{C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_6.}
$$
We use the <Hölder norm> $\|f\|_{C^{0,1/2}}=\|f\|_\infty+[f]_{C^{0,1/2}}$, where the <Hölder seminorm> is $\sup_{x\ne y}|f(x)-f(y)|/|x-y|^{1/2}$. The second bound is the homogeneous form of <Morrey's inequality>; the first includes control of the values themselves.

Let $u_j=\rho_{1/j}*u$ be the <mollifications> from (i). They converge to $u$ in the <Sobolev norm>. Apply the assumed smooth version of <Morrey's inequality> to the differences $u_j-u_\ell$:
$$
\|u_j-u_\ell\|_{C^{0,1/2}}\leq C\|u_j-u_\ell\|_{W^{1,6}}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus $(u_j)$ is a <Cauchy sequence> in the complete <Hölder space> $C^{0,1/2}(\mathbb R^3)$ and converges there to a function $w$. In particular the convergence is uniform. On every <bounded set> $K$ of finite volume,
$$
\|w-u\|_{L^6(K)}
\leq |K|^{1/6}\|w-u_j\|_\infty+\|u_j-u\|_{L^6(\mathbb R^3)}\longrightarrow0.
$$
Exhausting $\mathbb R^3$ by <balls> proves $w=u$ <almost everywhere>. Set $u^*=w$. Taking limits in the smooth <Hölder norm> bound gives the first boxed estimate. For any fixed $x\ne y$, taking limits in
$$
|u_j(x)-u_j(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{1/2}\|\nabla u_j\|_6
$$
gives the second boxed estimate after taking the supremum. Finally, two continuous representatives equal <almost everywhere> must coincide everywhere, since a nonzero continuous difference would remain nonzero on an <open ball> of positive volume.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

On the unbounded domain $\mathbb R^3$, translations already prevent <compactness> in every indicated <Hölder space>. Choose a <bump function> $\eta\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$ with $\eta(0)=1$, put $e_1=(1,0,0)$, and define
$$
\boxed{u_m(x)=\eta(x-3m e_1),\qquad m=1,2,\ldots.}
$$
A <change of variables> in the <Lebesgue integrals> defining the <Sobolev norm> gives $\|u_m\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)}=\|\eta\|_{W^{1,6}(\mathbb R^3)}$, so the sequence is bounded. Each $u_m$ is a <smooth function> of <compact support> and belongs to every <Hölder space> $C^{0,\gamma}(\mathbb R^3)$, $0<\gamma\leq1$: its bounded <gradient> gives the <Lipschitz> estimate for distances at most one, and its bounded values control larger distances.

For $m\ne\ell$, the <supports> are disjoint, and at $x=3m e_1$ we have $u_m(x)=1$ and $u_\ell(x)=0$. Therefore
$$
\|u_m-u_\ell\|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\geq\|u_m-u_\ell\|_\infty\geq1
\qquad(0<\gamma\leq1).
$$
No <subsequence> can be a <Cauchy sequence> in any of these <Hölder norms>, hence none converges. This uses the translation mechanism for <noncompactness of a Sobolev embedding by translation>; concentration at the critical <Hölder exponent> alone would not rule out convergence at every smaller <Hölder exponent>.

=== c
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Write $r=|x|$, $u(x)=\bar u(r)$, and let $g(r)$ be the <radial profile> of the <radial derivative> $\nabla u(x)\cdot x/|x|$, which may be assumed to be a <radial function> here. The <spherical coordinate system> gives
$$
\int_A|u|^6\,dx=4\pi\int_1^2|\bar u(r)|^6r^2\,dr,\qquad
4\pi\int_1^2|g(r)|^6r^2\,dr
\leq\int_A|\nabla u|^6\,dx.
$$
Since $1\leq r^2\leq4$, both $\bar u$ and $g$ belong to the <Lp space> $L^6(1,2)$. It remains to identify the <weak derivative> of the <radial profile> directly.

For $\phi\in C_c^\infty(1,2)$, each component of $\phi(r)x/r$ is a <test function> on $A$. Sum the three defining <weak derivative> identities, using the stated <divergence> formula:
$$
\begin{aligned}
4\pi\int_1^2g(r)\phi(r)r^2\,dr
&=\int_A\nabla u\cdot\frac{x}{r}\phi(r)\,dx\\
&=-\int_Au\left(\phi'(r)+\frac2r\phi(r)\right)dx\\
&=-4\pi\int_1^2\bar u(r)\bigl(r^2\phi(r)\bigr)'\,dr.
\end{aligned}
$$
Given an arbitrary <test function> $\zeta\in C_c^\infty(1,2)$, choose $\phi(r)=\zeta(r)/r^2$. Dividing by $4\pi$ gives $\int g\zeta=-\int\bar u\zeta'$, exactly the definition of the <weak derivative>. We have proved
$$
\boxed{\bar u\in W^{1,6}(1,2),\qquad\bar u'=g\ \text{almost everywhere},\qquad
\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(A)}.}
$$
This <radial Sobolev profile on an annulus> estimate follows from the two weighted <integrals> above and equivalence of the usual <Sobolev norms>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Use the continuous representative of $\bar u$ furnished by the one-dimensional <Sobolev embedding theorem>, and define $u^*(x)=\bar u(|x|)$ on $A$. The <one-dimensional Sobolev representative> is absolutely continuous; <Holder inequality> applied to its <fundamental theorem of calculus> gives
$$
|\bar u(r)-\bar u(s)|
=\left|\int_s^r\bar u'(t)\,dt\right|
\leq |r-s|^{5/6}\|\bar u'\|_{L^6(1,2)}.
$$
Also $\|\bar u\|_\infty\leq\|\bar u\|_6+\|\bar u'\|_6$: select a point where $|\bar u|$ is no larger than its $L^6$ <norm> on the unit-length interval and apply the same <fundamental theorem of calculus>. Thus the one-dimensional <Hölder norm> is controlled by $\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}$.

The two <Hölder seminorms> must be compared using the correct distances. By the <reverse triangle inequality>,
$$
\frac{|u^*(x)-u^*(y)|}{|x-y|^{5/6}}
\leq[\bar u]_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}
\frac{\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|^{5/6}}{|x-y|^{5/6}}
\leq[\bar u]_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}.
$$
Conversely, take $x=re$ and $y=se$ for any fixed unit vector $e$. Then $|x-y|=|r-s|$, so taking suprema yields equality of the two <Hölder seminorms>. Their <supremum norms> are equal as well. Part (i) now gives
$$
\boxed{\|u^*\|_{C^{0,5/6}(A)}
=\|\bar u\|_{C^{0,5/6}(1,2)}
\leq C\|\bar u\|_{W^{1,6}(1,2)}
\leq C'\|u\|_{W^{1,6}(A)}.}
$$
The representative agrees with $u$ <almost everywhere>, and the assignment is linear and injective on the radial <Sobolev space>. This proves the requested <continuous embedding>. The inner radius being positive makes the <radial profile> <Sobolev norm> comparable to its weighted radial version.

=== a

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

A <locally integrable function> $u$ has <weak derivative> $g_i$ in direction $i$ when $g_i$ is locally integrable and
$$
\boxed{\int_{B_1}u\,\partial_i\phi\,dx=-\int_{B_1}g_i\phi\,dx
\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in C_c^\infty(B_1).}
$$
The <test functions> have <compact support> inside the domain, so no boundary values enter this definition. Such $g_i$ is unique <almost everywhere> and is denoted $\partial_i u$.

For $1\leq p<\infty$, the first-order <Sobolev space> consists of the <Lp space> classes whose first <weak derivatives> are also in that <Lp space>:
$$
\boxed{W^{1,p}(B_1)=\{u\in L^p(B_1):\partial_i u\in L^p(B_1),\ i=1,\ldots,n\},\qquad
\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}=\left(\|u\|_p^p+\sum_{i=1}^n\|\partial_i u\|_p^p\right)^{1/p}.}
$$
This <Sobolev norm> is equivalent to $\|u\|_p+\|\nabla u\|_p$; either convention is acceptable. When $p=2$, this is the <Hilbert space> $H^1(B_1)$.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

For $\psi\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$, the functions $\psi$ and $\partial_i\psi$ are bounded. Hence $\psi u$ and $\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi$ belong to $L^p(B_1)$. For a <test function> $\phi$, the classical <product rule> and the <weak derivative> identity for $u$, tested with $\psi\phi$, give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{B_1}\psi u\,\partial_i\phi
&=\int_{B_1}u\,\partial_i(\psi\phi)-\int_{B_1}u(\partial_i\psi)\phi\\
&=-\int_{B_1}\bigl(\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi\bigr)\phi.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\psi u\in W^{1,p}(B_1),\qquad
\partial_i(\psi u)=\psi\partial_i u+u\partial_i\psi.}
$$
In fact $\psi u$ has <compact support> in $B_1$ and belongs to the corresponding zero-boundary <Sobolev space>, by <mollification> within that support.

For arbitrary $\psi\in C^\infty(B_1)$, the same identity still proves the local <product rule> for <weak derivatives>, but membership in the global <Sobolev space> can fail. To give a counterexample for every dimension and every $p$ in the question, take
$$
u\equiv1,\qquad\psi(x)=\frac1{1-|x|^2}.
$$
Here $u\in W^{1,p}(B_1)$ and $\psi$ is a <smooth function> on the open ball, while <polar coordinates> show
$$
\int_{B_1}|\psi|^p\,dx
\geq c_n\int_{1/2}^1(1-r^2)^{-p}\,dr=\infty\qquad(p\geq1).
$$
Thus $\psi u\notin L^p(B_1)$, and the global assertion is false. Boundedness of $\psi$ and its first <derivatives> would suffice without <compact support>.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Interpret the paper's unspecified $\Omega$ as containing $B_1$, the domain on which the coefficients are used. Write $A(x)=(a_{ij}(x))$, a real <symmetric matrix>. The <strict ellipticity> condition is pointwise positive definiteness:
$$
\boxed{\xi^TA(x)\xi>0\qquad(x\in B_1,\ \xi\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}).}
$$
The lower bound may depend on $x$. By continuity, <strict ellipticity> gives a positive lower bound on each <compact subset> of $B_1$, but does not require one bound throughout the whole open ball.

With the two-constant convention used in the next part, <uniform ellipticity> means
$$
\boxed{\lambda|\xi|^2\leq\xi^TA(x)\xi\leq\Lambda|\xi|^2
\qquad(x\in B_1,\ \xi\in\mathbb R^n),\quad0<\lambda\leq\Lambda<\infty.}
$$
Both constants are independent of $x$. Some terminology uses <uniform ellipticity> for just the lower bound and states coefficient boundedness separately; here the paper supplies both constants. The <spectral theorem> for the <symmetric matrix> gives $|A(x)\xi|\leq\Lambda|\xi|$. For example, $A(x)=(1-|x|^2)I$ satisfies <strict ellipticity> in $B_1$ but has no uniform positive lower bound as $|x|\uparrow1$.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

There is a small incompatibility in the printed <cutoff function> assumptions. No $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B_1)$ can equal one on $B_{1/2}$ and satisfy $|\nabla\varphi|\leq2$: for a unit vector $e$, choose $t<1$ beyond its <support>. Continuity gives $\varphi(e/2)=1$, while the <fundamental theorem of calculus> would give $1\leq2(t-1/2)<1$. Replace the number $2$ by a fixed sufficiently large absolute constant $C_0$; such a <cutoff function> exists, and the requested <Caccioppoli inequality> is unchanged apart from its constant. We keep $M=\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty$ in the calculation, which also yields the formal bound under the printed assumptions.

The <weak formulation> is $\int_{B_1}A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\zeta=0$ for <test functions> $\zeta$. Since $A$ is bounded, this extends by <density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space> to every $\zeta\in H_0^1(B_1)$. Part (a)(ii), followed by <mollification> inside the <compact support>, makes $\varphi^2(u-k)$ an admissible choice. The <product rule> gives
$$
0=\int\varphi^2 A\nabla u\cdot\nabla u
+2\int\varphi(u-k)A\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi.
$$
Put $X=\int\varphi^2|\nabla u|^2$ and $Y=\int(u-k)^2|\nabla\varphi|^2$. The lower and upper <uniform ellipticity> bounds, together with <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality>, imply
$$
\lambda X\leq2\Lambda X^{1/2}Y^{1/2}.
$$
If $X=0$ the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise divide by $X^{1/2}$ and square to obtain $X\leq4\Lambda^2\lambda^{-2}Y$. Since $\varphi=1$ on $B_{1/2}$,
$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}}|\nabla u|^2\,dx
\leq\frac{4\Lambda^2 M^2}{\lambda^2}\int_{B_1}(u-k)^2\,dx.}
$$
For a fixed valid <cutoff function>, $M\leq C_0$, this has the required constant independent of $k$. Formally inserting the printed value $M=2$ gives $16\Lambda^2/\lambda^2$. No derivative of $A$ was taken, so differentiability of the coefficients is unnecessary.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

First repeat the <Caccioppoli inequality> calculation on two concentric <balls>. If $h$ is a <harmonic function> on $B_R$, choose a <cutoff function> equal to one on $B_r$, supported in $B_R$, and with <gradient> bounded by $C/(R-r)$. The argument of (ii), with $A=I$ and $k=0$, gives
$$
\int_{B_r}|\nabla h|^2\,dx\leq\frac{C_*}{(R-r)^2}\int_{B_R}|h|^2\,dx
\qquad(0<r<R\leq1).
$$
Here $C_*$ depends at most on the dimension. When $h$ is only known to be smooth inside $B_R$ and in $L^2(B_R)$, the <weak formulation> and all <integrals> involving the <cutoff function> are still legitimate, since its <support> is strictly inside $B_R$.

Every classical <partial derivative> of the given smooth <harmonic function> $u$ is again a <harmonic function>, since constant-coefficient <partial derivatives> commute with the <Laplacian>. Choose indices $i_1,\ldots,i_m$ representing the <multi-index> $\alpha$, and define
$$
h_0=u,\qquad h_j=\partial_{i_j}\cdots\partial_{i_1}u,\qquad
R_j=1-\frac{j}{2m}\quad(0\leq j\leq m).
$$
For each $j\geq1$, the preceding <Caccioppoli inequality> yields
$$
\int_{B_{R_j}}|h_j|^2
\leq\int_{B_{R_j}}|\nabla h_{j-1}|^2
\leq C_*(2m)^2\int_{B_{R_{j-1}}}|h_{j-1}|^2.
$$
Starting with $u\in L^2(B_1)$, these estimates also establish the integrability needed at each subsequent step. Iterating $m$ times gives
$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}}|\partial^\alpha u|^2\,dx
\leq\bigl(4C_*m^2\bigr)^m\int_{B_1}|u|^2\,dx.}
$$
This is the desired <interior derivative estimate for a harmonic function>, with a constant depending only on $n$ and $m$, uniformly over every <multi-index> of size $m$.

==== iv
{scope}

===== Solution

Set $w=(u-k)^+$, the <positive part> of $u-k$. The allowed <Sobolev chain rule> gives
$$
\nabla w=\mathbf1_{\{u>k\}}\nabla u\quad\text{almost everywhere}.
$$
Use the admissible zero-boundary <Sobolev space> function $\varphi^2w$ in the <weak formulation>, with a valid <cutoff function> as explained in (ii). Its <gradient> is $\varphi^2\nabla w+2\varphi w\nabla\varphi$, so
$$
0=\int\varphi^2 A\nabla w\cdot\nabla w
+2\int\varphi w A\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi.
$$
Indeed the <integrands> vanish off $\{u>k\}$, and on that set $\nabla w=\nabla u$. The <uniform ellipticity> and <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> estimates from (ii) now apply to $w$ and give
$$
\boxed{\int_{B_{1/2}\cap\{u>k\}}|\nabla u|^2\,dx
\leq\frac{4\Lambda^2 M^2}{\lambda^2}
\int_{B_1\cap\{u>k\}}(u-k)^2\,dx,\qquad M=\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty.}
$$
Fixing the <cutoff function> gives the required constant independent of $k$. This is a <superlevel Caccioppoli inequality>; it uses the <positive part> as a test and does not require the truncated function itself to solve the original equation.

==== i

===== Solution

The zero <Dirichlet boundary condition> must be part of the definition. Thus $u\in H^1(B)$ is a <weak solution> precisely when $u\in H_0^1(B)$, equivalently its <Sobolev trace> vanishes on $\partial B$, and
$$
\boxed{\int_B\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx
=\int_B\chi_E\phi\,dx=\int_E\phi\,dx
\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in H_0^1(B).}
$$
Here $\chi_E$ is the <indicator function> of the <measurable set> $E$. One may initially require the identity only for <test functions> $\phi\in C_c^\infty(B)$: both sides are continuous in the <Sobolev norm>, and the definition of the <zero-trace Sobolev space> then extends it to all $H_0^1(B)$. This formulation uses first <weak derivatives> and encodes $-\Delta u=\chi_E$ without assuming classical second <derivatives>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

As printed, the answer is
$$
\boxed{\text{No }q\in[1,\infty]\text{ satisfies the stated estimate for all }u\in H^1(B).}
$$
The constant function $u\equiv1$ has zero <gradient> and positive <Lp norm> for every such $q$, including its <supremum norm> when $q=\infty$.

The missing assumption is a zero <Sobolev trace>, which is present for the <weak solutions> in the surrounding parts. With $H_0^1(B)$ in place of $H^1(B)$, the intended answer is
$$
\boxed{1\leq q\leq6,\qquad
\|u\|_{L^q(B)}\leq C_q\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}\quad(u\in H_0^1(B)).}
$$
This is the three-dimensional <Sobolev inequality> at $q=6$, followed by <Holder inequality> on the bounded ball for smaller $q$; it includes the zero-boundary <Poincaré inequality> at $q=2$. The subsequent parts use this zero-boundary version.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

By the definition in (i), $u$ itself is an admissible test in the <weak formulation>. Hence
$$
\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}^2=\int_Eu\,dx\leq\int_E|u|\,dx=\|u\|_{L^1(E)}.
$$
The absolute value makes a proof of positivity unnecessary. <Holder inequality> with exponents $6$ and $6/5$, followed by the zero-boundary <Sobolev inequality>, gives
$$
\|u\|_{L^1(E)}\leq |E|^{5/6}\|u\|_{L^6(B)}
\leq C_S|E|^{5/6}\|\nabla u\|_{L^2(B)}.
$$
Taking square roots of both estimates proves exactly the requested chain:
$$
\boxed{\|\nabla u\|_2\leq\|u\|_{L^1(E)}^{1/2}
\leq C_S^{1/2}|E|^{5/12}\|\nabla u\|_2^{1/2}.}
$$
If $\|\nabla u\|_2=0$, the zero-boundary <Poincaré inequality> gives $u=0$ and the conclusion follows. Otherwise divide the first-to-last estimate by $\|\nabla u\|_2^{1/2}$ and square, obtaining $\|\nabla u\|_2\leq C_S|E|^{5/6}$. Finally the same <Poincaré inequality> gives
$$
\boxed{\|u\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C|E|^{5/6}.}
$$
All constants depend only on the fixed unit ball and the dimension, and are independent of $E$ and $u$. If $|E|=0$, testing the homogeneous equation with $u$ gives the same zero solution directly.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Let $w_m=|E_m|^{5/6}u_m$. By linearity of the <weak formulation>, $w_m$ is the zero-boundary <weak solution> with forcing $\chi_{E_m}$. The estimate in (a)(iii) therefore gives
$$
\|w_m\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C|E_m|^{5/6},\qquad
\boxed{\|u_m\|_{H^1(B)}\leq C.}
$$
The <change of variables> $x=r_my$ gives $|E_m|=r_m^3|E|>0$, so the division is legitimate. Since $H^1(B)$ is a <Hilbert space>, <weak sequential compactness in a Hilbert space> now gives a <subsequence> and some $u\in H^1(B)$ such that
$$
\boxed{u_{m_j}\rightharpoonup u\quad\text{in }H^1(B).}
$$
In fact $u\in H_0^1(B)$: the <Sobolev trace operator> is continuous and linear, so its zero kernel is preserved by <weak convergence>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

The <Lp norm> of the forcing at the critical exponent is constant:
$$
\|f_m\|_{L^{6/5}(B)}^{6/5}
=|E_m|^{-1}\int_B\chi_{E_m}\,dx=1.
$$
To prove <weak convergence> in $L^{6/5}(B)$, the <duality of Lp spaces> says that we must test against an arbitrary $g\in L^6(B)$. <Holder inequality>, restricted to the shrinking <measurable set> $E_m$, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|\int_B f_mg\,dx\right|
&\leq |E_m|^{-5/6}\int_{E_m}|g|\,dx\\
&\leq |E_m|^{-5/6}|E_m|^{5/6}
\left(\int_{E_m}|g|^6\,dx\right)^{1/6}
=\left(\int_{E_m}|g|^6\,dx\right)^{1/6}\longrightarrow0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step is <absolute continuity of the Lebesgue integral>, since $|E_m|=r_m^3|E|\to0$ and $|g|^6$ is integrable. Thus
$$
\boxed{f_m\rightharpoonup0\text{ in }L^{6/5}(B),\qquad\|f_m\|_{6/5}=1.}
$$
Testing only bounded or smooth functions would not by itself establish this <weak convergence>; the restricted <Holder inequality> handles every member of the full dual space.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

Take the <weakly convergent> <subsequence> in (i), with limit $u\in H_0^1(B)$. For each $\phi\in H_0^1(B)$, the <weak formulation> gives
$$
\int_B\nabla u_{m_j}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx=\int_Bf_{m_j}\phi\,dx.
$$
The left side tends to $\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi$ by <weak convergence> in $H^1$. The zero-boundary <Sobolev inequality> gives $\phi\in L^6(B)$, so (ii) makes the right side tend to zero. Taking $\phi=u$ in the limiting identity yields $\int_B|\nabla u|^2=0$. The zero-boundary <Poincaré inequality> then gives $u=0$.

The same argument applies to a <weakly convergent> <subsequence> of any <subsequence> of $(u_m)$, because all remain bounded as in (i). To see why this proves convergence of the full sequence, suppose a continuous <linear functional> $F$ on $H^1(B)$ did not satisfy $F(u_m)\to0$. Some <subsequence> would have $|F(u_{m_j})|\geq\varepsilon>0$. Its boundedness gives a further <weakly convergent> <subsequence>, whose limit has just been proved to be zero; then $F$ tends to zero along it, a contradiction. Therefore
$$
\boxed{u_m\rightharpoonup0\quad\text{in }H^1(B)\text{ for the entire sequence}.}
$$
Uniqueness of a possible limit alone is not enough without the <weak sequential compactness in a Hilbert space> used to extract a further <subsequence>.

==== iv
{scope}

===== Solution

The <zero extension of H01> makes $v_m$ an element of $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$. The <chain rule> and <change of variables> give the exact scaling identities
$$
\boxed{\|\nabla v_m\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}^2=\|\nabla u_m\|_{L^2(B)}^2,\qquad
\|v_m\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^3)}^2=r_m^{-2}\|u_m\|_{L^2(B)}^2.}
$$
Indeed $\nabla v_m(x)=r_m^{3/2}\nabla u_m(r_mx)$ inside the expanded ball, and there is no boundary jump because the <Sobolev trace> is zero.

First take the printed convergence assertion as an assumption, as the question requests. It would imply $\|\nabla v_m\|_2\to\|\nabla v\|_2>0$. The strict inequality follows because an $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$ function with zero <weak gradient> is constant <almost everywhere>, and the only constant in $L^2(\mathbb R^3)$ is zero; this contradicts the asserted positivity of $v$. If $u_m$ converged strongly in $H^1(B)$, its limit would have to be its <weak limit> zero from (iii), forcing $\|\nabla u_m\|_2\to0$. The first scaling identity contradicts this. Thus the intended answer is
$$
\boxed{u_m\text{ does not converge strongly in }H^1(B).}
$$

There is nevertheless a substantive printing error: the asserted strong convergence of $v_m$ in the full $H^1(\mathbb R^3)$ is false for these positive sources. Here is a check that does not rely on any assertion about the limiting profile. Put $R_m=r_m^{-1}$ and $c=|E|^{-5/6}$. Rescaling the <weak formulation> gives
$$
-\Delta v_m=c\chi_E\quad\text{in }B_{R_m},\qquad v_m=0\quad\text{on }\partial B_{R_m}.
$$
The <spherical mean of a Poisson solution outside its source> provides an exact obstruction. The total source is $M=c|E|=|E|^{1/6}>0$. Let $a_m(s)=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{S^2}v_m(s\omega)\,d\omega$ be its <spherical mean>. On $1<s<R_m$ the function is a <harmonic function>. The radial flux identity, obtained by testing against radial <test functions> or by the <divergence theorem>, gives $-4\pi s^2a_m'(s)=M$, while the zero boundary value gives $a_m(R_m)=0$. Hence
$$
a_m(s)=\frac{M}{4\pi}\left(\frac1s-\frac1{R_m}\right)\qquad(1<s<R_m).
$$
<Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> on each sphere, followed by <spherical coordinates>, now yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\|v_m\|_2^2
&\geq4\pi\int_1^{R_m}s^2a_m(s)^2\,ds\\
&=\frac{M^2}{4\pi}\left(\frac{R_m}{3}-1+\frac1{R_m}-\frac1{3R_m^2}\right)
\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the printed sequence is not even bounded in the full $H^1$ <norm>. The result on <Poisson convergence on expanding balls in a homogeneous Sobolev space> gives the appropriate corrected convergence in the <homogeneous Sobolev space> $\dot H^1(\mathbb R^3)$, which controls the <gradient> in $L^2$ and the function in $L^6$, without requiring it to belong to $L^2$.

For completeness, the desired nonconvergence of $u_m$ has a direct proof that needs neither the incorrect claim nor its correction. Choose a nonnegative nonzero <test function> $\eta\in C_c^\infty(E)$, which exists since $E$ is a domain containing the origin. The rescaled <weak formulation> and <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> give
$$
0<c\int_E\eta\,dx
=\int_{\mathbb R^3}\nabla v_m\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx
\leq\|\nabla v_m\|_2\|\nabla\eta\|_2.
$$
Consequently, for every $m$,
$$
\boxed{\|\nabla u_m\|_2=\|\nabla v_m\|_2
\geq\frac{|E|^{-5/6}\int_E\eta\,dx}{\|\nabla\eta\|_2}>0.}
$$
Together with (iii), this rigorously proves failure of <strong convergence> for the actual sequence defined in the paper.
