= Solution
{parent=1/a}

Use <Gaussian units>, <plane waves> proportional to $e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r-i\omega t}$, and the **signed** <cyclotron frequency> $\Omega_s=q_sB_0/(m_sc)$. Write $\Omega_e=eB_0/(m_ec)>0$ for its <electron> magnitude, so the signed <electron> value is $-\Omega_e$. Define the species <plasma frequency> by $\omega_{ps}^2=4\pi n_{0s}q_s^2/m_s$. The <linearization> of the cold <Lorentz force> equations gives
$$
-i\omega u_{xs}-\Omega_su_{ys}=\frac{q_s}{m_s}E_x,\qquad
\Omega_su_{xs}-i\omega u_{ys}=\frac{q_s}{m_s}E_y,\qquad
-i\omega u_{zs}=\frac{q_s}{m_s}E_z.
$$
Solving this <linear system> and using the species <current density> $\mathbf j_s=n_{0s}q_s\mathbf u_s$ gives the <conductivity tensor>
$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol\sigma_s=\frac{n_{0s}q_s^2}{m_s}
\begin{pmatrix}
\dfrac{i\omega}{\omega^2-\Omega_s^2}&-\dfrac{\Omega_s}{\omega^2-\Omega_s^2}&0\\
\dfrac{\Omega_s}{\omega^2-\Omega_s^2}&\dfrac{i\omega}{\omega^2-\Omega_s^2}&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{i}{\omega}
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor> is $\boldsymbol\epsilon=\mathbf I+(4\pi i/\omega)\sum_s\boldsymbol\sigma_s$. Thus
$$
\boldsymbol\epsilon=\begin{pmatrix}S&ig&0\\-ig&S&0\\0&0&P\end{pmatrix},\quad
S=\epsilon_\perp=1-\sum_s\frac{\omega_{ps}^2}{\omega^2-\Omega_s^2},\quad
P=\epsilon_\parallel=1-\sum_s\frac{\omega_{ps}^2}{\omega^2},\quad
g=-\sum_s\frac{\omega_{ps}^2\Omega_s}{\omega(\omega^2-\Omega_s^2)}.
$$
At the stated <electron> <frequency> scale, the <ion> contribution is smaller by $O(Zm_e/m_i)$ for $q_i=Ze$ and <quasineutrality>. To this accuracy,
$$
\boxed{S=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2-\Omega_e^2},\qquad
P=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega^2},\qquad
g=\frac{\omega_{pe}^2\Omega_e}{\omega(\omega^2-\Omega_e^2)}.}
$$
These expressions require negligible <collisions> and the cold ordering $kv_{\mathrm{th},s}\ll|\omega|$; sufficiently close to a <cyclotron resonance>, thermal broadening or <collisions> must also be retained.

= Solution
{parent=1/b}

The <Faraday law> and <Ampere-Maxwell law> give $\delta\mathbf B=(c/\omega)\mathbf k\times\delta\mathbf E$ and
$$
\mathbf k\times(\mathbf k\times\delta\mathbf E)+\frac{\omega^2}{c^2}\boldsymbol\epsilon\cdot\delta\mathbf E=0.
$$
With <refractive index> $n=kc/\omega$, the <linear system> is therefore
$$
\begin{pmatrix}
S-n^2\cos^2\theta&ig&n^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\\
-ig&S-n^2&0\\
n^2\sin\theta\cos\theta&0&P-n^2\sin^2\theta
\end{pmatrix}\delta\mathbf E=0.
$$
Set
$$
\boxed{R=S-g,\qquad L=S+g,\qquad RL=S^2-g^2.}
$$
Here $R$ labels the <cold-plasma right-handed wave> and $L$ the <cold-plasma left-handed wave>, with their <polarizations> fixed explicitly in <1/d>. In the <electron>-only response,
$$
R=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega(\omega-\Omega_e)},\qquad
L=1-\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega(\omega+\Omega_e)}.
$$
Writing $N=n^2$, expansion of the <determinant> yields the <dispersion relation>
$$
A N^2-BN+C=0,\quad
A=S\sin^2\theta+P\cos^2\theta,\quad
B=RL\sin^2\theta+PS(1+\cos^2\theta),\quad C=PRL.
$$
Equivalently,
$$
P(N-R)(N-L)\cos^2\theta+(SN-RL)(N-P)\sin^2\theta=0.
$$
Division gives
$$
\boxed{\tan^2\theta=-\frac{P(n^2-R)(n^2-L)}{(Sn^2-RL)(n^2-P)}.}
$$
The undivided <determinant> equation remains valid when this rational form has simultaneous zero factors.

{parent=1}

= Solution
{parent=1/c}

A <plasma wave resonance> has $n^2\to\infty$. At finite coefficients of the <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor>, its generic condition is the vanishing of the coefficient of $n^4$:
$$
\boxed{S\sin^2\theta+P\cos^2\theta=0,\qquad \tan^2\theta=-P/S.}
$$
A <plasma wave cutoff> has $n^2=0$, so the constant term of the <dispersion relation> vanishes:
$$
\boxed{PRL=0,\quad\text{that is }P=0,\ R=0,\ \text{or }L=0.}
$$
Both statements are branch conditions: check the undivided <dispersion relation> at special angles and simultaneous zero factors. In particular, the parallel <plasma oscillation> at $P=0$ is a longitudinal branch with arbitrary $k$ in the cold limit, while the transverse parallel branches have <plasma wave cutoffs> at $R=0$ or $L=0$. Poles of the <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor> can give additional limiting <plasma wave resonances>, such as the parallel <cold-plasma right-handed wave> at $\omega=\Omega_e$; these are not obtained by assuming finite $S,P,g$.

= Solution
{parent=1/d}

For parallel propagation, use the undivided <dispersion relation>:
$$
P(n^2-R)(n^2-L)=0.
$$
The five requested cases are:

* **<Plasma oscillations>:** $P=0$ and $\delta\mathbf E\parallel\mathbf B_0\parallel\mathbf k$. With negligible <ion> response, $\omega^2=\omega_{pe}^2$, independently of $k$ in this cold approximation.
* **<Cold-plasma right-handed waves>:** $n^2=R$, $E_y=iE_x$, $E_z=0$, for propagation along $+\mathbf B_0$. At a fixed point, the real <electric field> rotates from $+\hat{\mathbf x}$ towards $+\hat{\mathbf y}$, in the sense of <electron> gyration.
* **<Cold-plasma left-handed waves>:** $n^2=L$, $E_y=-iE_x$, $E_z=0$. The real <electric field> rotates in the opposite sense.
* **<Ordinary plasma waves>:** for perpendicular propagation, $\theta=\pi/2$, $n^2=P$, and $\delta\mathbf E\parallel\mathbf B_0$. Their <dispersion relation> is $\omega^2=\omega_{pe}^2+c^2k^2$.
* **<Extraordinary plasma waves>:** for perpendicular propagation, $n^2=RL/S$, $E_z=0$, and $E_y=igE_x/(S-n^2)$. In general the <electric field> has both longitudinal and transverse components.

For the perpendicular cases the undivided <dispersion relation> factors as $(n^2-P)(Sn^2-RL)=0$, up to an overall nonzero factor. This also identifies the <extraordinary plasma wave> <plasma wave resonance> at $S=0$.

= Solution
{parent=1/e}

An <electrostatic plasma wave> has $\delta\mathbf E=-i\mathbf k\phi$. In the absence of externally imposed <charge density>, the <Gauss law> for the <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor> is $\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol\epsilon\cdot\delta\mathbf E=0$. Consequently,
$$
\boxed{k_\perp^2S+k_\parallel^2P=0.}
$$
The antisymmetric terms of the <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor> cancel in this scalar contraction. For oblique propagation this is the leading longitudinal condition in the large-<refractive index> approximation; a small transverse <electric field> is generally required by the full <Maxwell equations> at finite $n$. For parallel propagation an exactly longitudinal solution exists: $P=0$, hence $\omega^2\simeq\omega_{pe}^2$. It coincides with the $\theta\to0$ limit of the finite-coefficient <plasma wave resonance> condition in <1/c>. At exactly $\theta=0$, $P$ factors out of the transverse <dispersion relation>, leaving the independent <plasma oscillation> rather than an isolated divergence of the transverse branches.

= Solution
{parent=1/f}

For the <ordinary plasma wave>, choose $\mathbf k=k\hat{\mathbf x}$ and $\delta\mathbf E=E_z\hat{\mathbf z}$. The equations in <1/a> give $u_{xs}=u_{ys}=0$ and
$$
u_{zs}=\frac{iq_s}{m_s\omega}E_z.
$$
The driven particle motion is entirely parallel to $\mathbf B_0$: the parallel <Lorentz force> has no magnetic contribution. Nevertheless, **the <ordinary plasma wave> is electromagnetic**, since $\delta\mathbf E\perp\mathbf k$ and the <Faraday law> gives
$$
\boxed{\delta\mathbf B=-\frac{ck}{\omega}E_z\hat{\mathbf y}\ne0.}
$$
An <electrostatic plasma wave> instead has $\delta\mathbf E\parallel\mathbf k$ and zero perturbed <magnetic field>. Alignment with the equilibrium <magnetic field> does not make a wave electrostatic. At the exact <plasma wave cutoff> $k=0$, this propagating-wave distinction becomes degenerate.

Write the <ordinary plasma wave> as
$$
\delta\mathbf E(\mathbf r,t)=\operatorname{Re}\{[\mathbf a_0+\varepsilon\mathbf a_1+\cdots]e^{iS(\mathbf r,t)}\},\qquad
\mathbf k=\nabla S,\quad\omega=-\partial_tS.
$$
The <eikonal function> $S$ is dimensionless; it varies by order $\varepsilon^{-1}$ across a background scale $L_{\rm bg}$, where $\varepsilon\sim\lambda/L_{\rm bg}\ll1$. The amplitudes and equilibrium <plasma> parameters vary on $L_{\rm bg}$, so $|\nabla\mathbf a_j|\ll k|\mathbf a_j|$ and $|\nabla k|\ll k^2$. The equilibrium is stationary, so $\omega$ is constant along propagation. Assume infinitesimal fields, negligible <collisions>, $kv_{\mathrm{th},s}\ll\omega$, negligible <ion> response, and a fixed <ordinary plasma wave> <polarization> with $\mathbf B_0\parallel\hat{\mathbf z}$ and spatial variation along $x$. The <WKB approximation> is used away from <wave turning points> and <plasma wave resonances>.

Replacing derivatives on the fast factor by $i\mathbf k$ and $-i\omega$ in the <Maxwell equations> reproduces the local <magnetized cold-plasma dielectric tensor> equation of <1/b>. In this one-dimensional <ordinary plasma wave> geometry, the <Maxwell equations> and the local cold parallel <current density> in fact give the scalar equation directly:
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2\delta E_z}{dx^2}+k^2(x)\delta E_z=0,\qquad
k^2(x)=\frac{\omega^2-\omega_{pe}^2(x)}{c^2}.}
$$
For $\delta E_z=a(x)e^{iS(x)}$, substitution gives
$$
a''+2ik a'+ik'a+[k^2(x)-(S')^2]a=0.
$$
The leading <eikonal equation> is $(S')^2=k^2(x)$, and the next order gives $2ka'+k'a=0$, hence the <flux-normalized WKB amplitude> $a\propto k^{-1/2}$. In particular, require $|k'|/|k|^2\ll1$ and $|k''|/|k|^3\ll1$; these fail at the <wave turning points>.

= Solution

A <wave turning point> satisfies $k^2(x)=0$, equivalently $\omega_{pe}^2(x)=\omega^2$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{x=\pm x_T,\qquad x_T=L\sqrt{1-\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_{pe0}^2}}.}
$$
For $0<\omega<\omega_{pe0}$ the two <wave turning points> lie inside the column and are simple. Defining $a=-x_T$ and $b=x_T$, the local <dispersion relation> becomes
$$
k^2(x)=\frac{\omega_{pe0}^2}{c^2L^2}(x^2-x_T^2).
$$

= Solution

Use positive real local <wavenumbers>
$$
k_1(x)=\frac{\omega_{pe0}}{cL}\sqrt{x^2-x_T^2}\quad(x<a),\qquad
k_2(x)=\kappa(x)=\frac{\omega_{pe0}}{cL}\sqrt{x_T^2-x^2}\quad(a<x<b),\qquad
k_3(x)=\frac{\omega_{pe0}}{cL}\sqrt{x^2-x_T^2}\quad(x>b),
$$
within $|x|\le L$. If the exterior is <vacuum>, continue $k_1,k_3$ as $\omega/c$ outside the column. Define the positive <phase integrals> and barrier integral
$$
\Phi(x)=\int_x^a k_1(s)\,ds,\qquad
I(x)=\int_a^x\kappa(s)\,ds,\qquad
\Psi(x)=\int_b^x k_3(s)\,ds.
$$
The three <WKB approximations>, with the common $e^{-i\omega t}$ suppressed, are
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
E_{\rm I}&=\frac{A_{\rm in}e^{-i(\Phi-\pi/4)}+A_{\rm r}e^{i(\Phi-\pi/4)}}{\sqrt{k_1}},\\
E_{\rm II}&=\frac{D e^{-I}+G e^{I}}{\sqrt\kappa},\\
E_{\rm III}&=\frac{A_{\rm t}e^{i(\Psi-\pi/4)}+A_{\rm back}e^{-i(\Psi-\pi/4)}}{\sqrt{k_3}}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The <flux-normalized WKB amplitudes> make squared travelling-wave coefficients proportional to directed <Poynting flux>. Since $\Phi'=-k_1$ and $\Psi'=k_3$, $A_{\rm in}$ and $A_{\rm t}$ travel to the right, while $A_{\rm r}$ and $A_{\rm back}$ travel to the left. The constants $\pi/4$ are a convenient <Airy turning-point connection formula> convention.

= Solution
{parent=2/d}

At $a=-x_T$, define
$$
\alpha=-\left.\frac{dk^2}{dx}\right|_a=\frac{2\omega_{pe0}^2x_T}{c^2L^2}>0,\qquad
\ell=\alpha^{-1/3},\qquad z=\frac{x-a}{\ell}.
$$
The first-order <Taylor expansion> is $k^2(x)=-\alpha(x-a)+O((x-a)^2)$, so the local <wave equation> becomes
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2 E}{dz^2}-zE=0,\qquad E=C_A\operatorname{Ai}(z)+C_B\operatorname{Bi}(z).}
$$
This is the <Airy ordinary differential equation>. Its $z<0$ side is oscillatory and its $z>0$ side is evanescent. A matching overlap requires $\ell\ll|x-a|\ll x_T$, hence well-separated <wave turning points> with $\ell\ll x_T$. At $b=x_T$, the coordinate $z_R=(b-x)/\ell$ gives the same <Airy ordinary differential equation>, with its evanescent side again at positive argument.

= Solution

The real <Airy function> asymptotics used for the <Airy turning-point connection formula> are, for $z,s\to+\infty$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ai}(z)&\sim\frac{z^{-1/4}}{2\sqrt\pi}e^{-2z^{3/2}/3},&
\operatorname{Bi}(z)&\sim\frac{z^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}e^{2z^{3/2}/3},\\
\operatorname{Ai}(-s)&\sim\frac{s^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}\sin(2s^{3/2}/3+\pi/4),&
\operatorname{Bi}(-s)&\sim\frac{s^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}\cos(2s^{3/2}/3+\pi/4).
\end{aligned}
$$
These real-axis asymptotics fix both normalization and phase.<Airy function asymptotics in the Digital Library of Mathematical Functions>[Equations 9.7.5, 9.7.7, 9.7.9 and 9.7.11; leading terms for positive real argument]{ref} Near $a$, $I\sim2z^{3/2}/3$, $\Phi\sim2(-z)^{3/2}/3$, and $\kappa^{-1/2}\sim\ell^{1/2}z^{-1/4}$. Matching to the constants of <2/c> gives $C_A=2\sqrt\pi\ell^{1/2}D$ and $C_B=\sqrt\pi\ell^{1/2}G$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\frac{D e^{-I}+G e^I}{\sqrt\kappa}
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{2D\cos(\Phi-\pi/4)-G\sin(\Phi-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k_1}}.}
$$
In particular, the decaying component matches the cosine with the requested $-\pi/4$ shift, with a factor **two** in its oscillatory coefficient. The growing component matches a sine with the indicated minus sign. Expanding into travelling <waves> gives
$$
\boxed{A_{\rm in}=D-\frac{iG}{2},\qquad A_{\rm r}=D+\frac{iG}{2}.}
$$
The factor two is essential when converting the real oscillatory components to incident and reflected travelling <waves>.

Let $J(x)=\int_x^b\kappa(s)\,ds=\eta-I(x)$. The <WKB approximation> in the barrier can be rewritten as
$$
E_{\rm II}=\frac{D_R e^{-J}+G_R e^J}{\sqrt\kappa},\qquad
D_R=G e^\eta,\quad G_R=D e^{-\eta}.
$$
The right <Airy turning-point connection formula> gives
$$
E_{\rm III}=\frac{2D_R\cos(\Psi-\pi/4)-G_R\sin(\Psi-\pi/4)}{\sqrt{k_3}},
$$
so $A_{\rm t}=D_R+iG_R/2$ and $A_{\rm back}=D_R-iG_R/2$. Impose **no incident <wave> from the right**, $A_{\rm back}=0$. Then
$$
D_R=\frac{iG_R}{2},\qquad
G=\frac{iD}{2}e^{-2\eta},\qquad
A_{\rm t}=iD e^{-\eta},\qquad
A_{\rm in}=D\left(1+\frac14e^{-2\eta}\right).
$$
Consequently, within the leading <Airy turning-point connection formula> calculation,
$$
t=\frac{A_{\rm t}}{A_{\rm in}}=\frac{i e^{-\eta}}{1+e^{-2\eta}/4},\qquad
r=\frac{A_{\rm r}}{A_{\rm in}}=\frac{1-e^{-2\eta}/4}{1+e^{-2\eta}/4}.
$$
For an opaque barrier, $\eta\gg1$,
$$
\boxed{t\sim i e^{-\eta},\qquad T=|t|^2\simeq e^{-2\eta}.}
$$
The <transmission coefficient> is a ratio of <Poynting fluxes>; the $k^{-1/2}$ normalization already includes their local <wavenumber> factors. The displayed denominator is useful for consistent matching and <Wronskian flux> conservation, but does not claim accuracy for subleading terms beyond the <WKB approximation>, or for merging <wave turning points>.

= Solution
{parent=2/g}

For real $k^2(x)$, the <wave equation> has the conserved <Wronskian flux>
$$
\mathcal J=\frac{1}{2i}(E^*E'-EE'^*)=\operatorname{Im}(E^*E').
$$
For this <ordinary plasma wave>, the time-averaged <Poynting flux> is $\langle S_x\rangle=c^2\mathcal J/(8\pi\omega)$. With no incident <wave> from the right, evaluating the same <Wronskian flux> on both sides proves
$$
\boxed{R+T=1,\qquad R=|r|^2,\quad T=|t|^2.}
$$
The coefficients in <2/f> also satisfy this identity algebraically, before the opaque-barrier expansion.

A <plasma wave resonance> makes the ideal cold description singular and requires an appropriate causal regularization. A passive resonant layer can absorb <wave energy> into particle motion, or convert it into another branch. For the two measured outgoing channels one then has
$$
\boxed{R+T=1-A\le1,}
$$
with $A>0$ when resonant absorption or uncounted <mode conversion> occurs. A numerical value of $A$ requires the layer physics; a resonance by itself does not specify a universal loss fraction. Counting all converted and absorbed <energy> preserves <conservation of energy>.

= Solution
{parent=2/h}

The barrier <wavenumber> is $\kappa=\omega_{pe0}\sqrt{x_T^2-x^2}/(cL)$. The substitution $x=x_T\sin u$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\eta&=\frac{\omega_{pe0}}{cL}\int_{-x_T}^{x_T}\sqrt{x_T^2-x^2}\,dx
=\frac{\omega_{pe0}x_T^2}{cL}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2u\,du\\
&=\boxed{\frac{\pi\omega_{pe0}L}{2c}\left(1-\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_{pe0}^2}\right).}
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the leading <transmission coefficient> is
$$
\boxed{T\simeq\exp\!\left[-\frac{\pi\omega_{pe0}L}{c}\left(1-\frac{\omega^2}{\omega_{pe0}^2}\right)\right].}
$$
At fixed $\omega/\omega_{pe0}$, increasing the column width or peak <plasma frequency> suppresses transmission exponentially. Raising $\omega$ towards $\omega_{pe0}$ narrows the evanescent region and increases transmission. However, when $\eta$ becomes order one or smaller the two <wave turning points> interact; the opaque-barrier <WKB approximation> cannot be extrapolated to claim $T\to1$ at $\omega=\omega_{pe0}$. Smooth matching to an exterior is also implicit in the leading result; at $|x|=L$ the stated profile joins continuously to <vacuum>.

= Solution

Use <Gaussian units> and the <kinetic magnetohydrodynamics> equations
$$
\partial_t\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B),\qquad
\rho D_t\mathbf u=-\nabla\cdot\mathbf P+\frac{(\nabla\times\mathbf B)\times\mathbf B}{4\pi},\qquad
\mathbf P=p_\perp\mathbf I+(p_\parallel-p_\perp)\mathbf b\mathbf b,
$$
together with species <continuity equations>, <quasineutrality> and the <drift-kinetic equation>. Here $p_\perp,p_\parallel$ sum the species <pressures>. Interpret the <thermal speed> denominator in the source as the species <mass> $m_\alpha$, so $v_{\mathrm{th}\alpha}^2=2T_{0\alpha}/m_\alpha$ and the <first adiabatic invariant> is $\mu=v_\perp^2/(2B)$.

The specified <Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution> has zero mean motion and <isotropic> <pressure>, $p_{\perp0}=p_{\parallel0}=p_0$. All equilibrium scalars depend only on $x$, while $\mathbf b_0\cdot\nabla x=0$ and $\mathbf b_0\cdot\nabla B_0=0$. Therefore the equilibrium streaming and mirror terms of the <drift-kinetic equation> vanish. The remaining terms vanish because $\mathbf u_0=0$ and $E_{\parallel0}=0$; the parallel electric constraint is also satisfied because the equilibrium parallel <pressure gradient> is zero. Species <continuity equations> and the induction equation are stationary, and <quasineutrality> holds by assumption.

It remains to check the perpendicular <force balance>. The radial <magnetic pressure> and <magnetic tension> give
$$
0=-\frac{dp_0}{dx}-\frac{d}{dx}\frac{B_0^2}{8\pi}-\frac{B_0^2}{4\pi R}
=\frac{p_0}{L_p}+\frac{B_0^2}{4\pi L_B}-\frac{B_0^2}{4\pi R}.
$$
This is precisely
$$
\boxed{\frac1R-\frac1{L_B}=\frac{4\pi p_0}{B_0^2L_p}=\frac\beta{2L_p}.}
$$
Thus the <Z-pinch> is a steady solution of the stated <kinetic magnetohydrodynamics> system. Its equilibrium <electric current> is supplied by the small species drifts implicit in this approximation; zero leading common flow does not require zero <electric current>.

= Solution
{parent=3/b}

For a static equilibrium, <linearization> of magnetic induction gives
$$
\delta\mathbf B=\nabla\times(\boldsymbol\xi\times\mathbf B_0)
=(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla)\boldsymbol\xi-(\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B_0-\mathbf B_0\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\xi.
$$
The rotating local basis must be differentiated. In particular, $\partial_\parallel\hat{\mathbf x}=\mathbf b_0/R$ and $\partial_\parallel\mathbf b_0=-\hat{\mathbf x}/R$. Their contributions proportional to $\xi_\parallel$ cancel in the radial component, yielding
$$
\boxed{\delta b_x=ik_\parallel\xi_x,\qquad\delta b_y=ik_\parallel\xi_y.}
$$
Writing $h=\delta B/B_0$, the magnitude component is
$$
h=ik_\parallel\xi_\parallel+\frac{\xi_x}{R}+\frac{\xi_x}{L_B}-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\xi
=\frac{\xi_x}{L_B}-ik_y\xi_y,
$$
where $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\xi=\xi_x/R+ik_y\xi_y+ik_\parallel\xi_\parallel$ when $\partial_x\xi_x=0$.

Set $M=B_0^2/(4\pi)=\rho_0v_A^2$ and $\delta\Pi=\delta p_\perp+Mh$. The transverse $y$ component of the linear <kinetic magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation> is
$$
\rho_0(\omega^2-k_\parallel^2v_A^2)\xi_y=ik_y\delta\Pi.
$$
Since $k_y\gg k_\parallel\sim\omega/v_A$, perpendicular <magnetic pressure> enforces the leading <pressure-balanced plasma perturbation> condition $\delta\Pi=0$, giving
$$
\boxed{h=-\frac{4\pi\delta p_\perp}{B_0^2}=-\frac\beta2\frac{\delta p_\perp}{p_0}.}
$$
In the radial component, the curvature part of the perturbed magnetic force is $-2Mh/R$: one contribution comes from $(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla)\delta\mathbf B$ and one from $(\delta\mathbf B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B_0$. The perturbed <pressure anisotropy> contributes $-(\delta p_\perp-\delta p_\parallel)/R$. After the leading total-<pressure> constraint has removed $\partial_x\delta\Pi$, the radial equation is
$$
-\rho_0\omega^2\xi_x=-Mk_\parallel^2\xi_x-\frac{2Mh}{R}-\frac{\delta p_\perp-\delta p_\parallel}{R}.
$$
Substituting $Mh=-\delta p_\perp$ gives
$$
\boxed{(\omega^2-k_\parallel^2v_A^2)\xi_x=-\frac{\delta p_\perp+\delta p_\parallel}{\rho_0R}.}
$$
Although $h$ is small at low <plasma beta>, its contribution $Mh$ to the <force balance> is of the same order as $\delta p_\perp$ and must be kept.

= c
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=3/c}

Choose the <drift-kinetic equation> in $(\mathbf r,\mu,v_\parallel)$ with $v_\parallel$ the laboratory parallel <velocity> along the exact $\mathbf b$. To first order, before discarding terms small in the stated steep-gradient ordering, it is
$$
\begin{aligned}
(-i\omega+ik_\parallel v_\parallel)\delta f_\alpha
&+(u_x+v_\parallel\delta b_x)\partial_x f_{0\alpha}\\
&+\left[\frac{q_\alpha}{m_\alpha}\delta E_\parallel
-\mu(ik_\parallel\delta B+\delta b_xB_0')
+v_\parallel\mathbf u_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol\kappa_0\right]\partial_{v_\parallel}f_{0\alpha}=0,
\qquad\boldsymbol\kappa_0=-\frac{\hat{\mathbf x}}R.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last term is the linear acceleration associated with the moving curved field. Terms quadratic in the perturbations have already been removed. A formulation using parallel <velocity> relative to the common flow gives the same physical result after transforming back to these variables.

The two mirror terms and the curvature acceleration are subleading here. Their relative sizes are controlled by $h\ll\delta p/p_0$ and $L_p/L_B,L_p/R\ll1$. **The field-direction perturbation multiplying the steep equilibrium <gradient> must remain:**
$$
\delta(\mathbf b\cdot\nabla f_{0\alpha})=\delta b_x\partial_x f_{0\alpha}.
$$
Using $u_x=-i\omega\xi_x$ and $\delta b_x=ik_\parallel\xi_x$ therefore gives the leading equation
$$
\boxed{(-i\omega+ik_\parallel v_\parallel)(\delta f_\alpha+\xi_x\partial_xf_{0\alpha})
+\frac{q_\alpha}{m_\alpha}\delta E_\parallel\partial_{v_\parallel}f_{0\alpha}=0.}
$$
The displaced branch satisfying <quasineutrality> has $\delta E_\parallel=0$ to this order: its species <number densities> are $\delta n_\alpha=-\xi_xn_{0\alpha}'$, so $\sum_\alpha q_\alpha\delta n_\alpha=-\xi_x\partial_x\sum_\alpha q_\alpha n_{0\alpha}=0$. More explicitly, putting $\mathcal D=-i\omega+ik_\parallel v_\parallel$ gives
$$
\delta f_\alpha=-\xi_x f_{0\alpha}'-\frac{iq_\alpha\delta E_\parallel}{m_\alpha}\frac{\partial_{v_\parallel}f_{0\alpha}}{\omega-k_\parallel v_\parallel},\qquad
0=-i\delta E_\parallel\sum_\alpha\frac{q_\alpha^2}{m_\alpha}\int\frac{\partial_{v_\parallel}f_{0\alpha}}{\omega-k_\parallel v_\parallel}\,d^3v.
$$
Away from an independent longitudinal eigenfrequency, the summed susceptibility is nonzero, giving $\delta E_\parallel=0$. An independent longitudinal eigenmode is not forced by the interchange displacement. The required solution is thus
$$
\boxed{\delta f_\alpha=-\xi_x\partial_x f_{0\alpha}.}
$$
The apparent streaming denominator cancels identically, including at $\omega=k_\parallel v_\parallel$; this response has no leading <Landau damping> singularity.

To see the temperature and density contributions explicitly, differentiate at fixed $\mu,v_\parallel$ and neglect $B_0'$ at this order:
$$
\partial_xf_{0\alpha}=f_{0\alpha}\left[\frac{n_{0\alpha}'}{n_{0\alpha}}+
\left(\frac{m_\alpha(v_\parallel^2+2\mu B_0)}{2T_{0\alpha}}-\frac32\right)\frac{T_{0\alpha}'}{T_{0\alpha}}\right].
$$
The gyrotropic <velocity-space measure> is $d^3v=2\pi B_0\,d\mu\,dv_\parallel$. The species <perpendicular plasma pressure> and <parallel plasma pressure> are the <moments>
$$
p_{\perp\alpha}=m_\alpha\int\mu B f_\alpha\,d^3v,\qquad
p_{\parallel\alpha}=m_\alpha\int v_\parallel^2 f_\alpha\,d^3v.
$$
Explicit perturbations of the measure and of $\mu B$ produce terms proportional to $h$, which are subleading in these <pressure> moments; derivatives of the equilibrium measure produce the discarded $B_0'$ terms. The remaining <Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution> integrals give
$$
\delta p_{\perp\alpha}=\delta p_{\parallel\alpha}=-\xi_xp_{0\alpha}',\qquad
\boxed{\delta p_\perp=\delta p_\parallel=\frac{p_0}{L_p}\xi_x.}
$$
Insertion into <3/b> proves
$$
\boxed{\omega^2=k_\parallel^2v_A^2-\frac{2p_0}{\rho_0RL_p}.}
$$

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=3/d}

The result is a <fluid Eulerian perturbation> generated by translating the entire equilibrium <phase-space distribution function> through $\xi_x$:
$$
f_\alpha(x,\mu,v_\parallel,t)=f_{0\alpha}(x-\xi_x,\mu,v_\parallel)+O(\xi_x^2),\qquad
\boxed{\Delta f_\alpha=\delta f_\alpha+\xi_x\partial_xf_{0\alpha}=0.}
$$
Here $\Delta$ follows the displaced <flux tube>. <Magnetic flux freezing> carries the tube and its particles across the equilibrium <pressure gradient>. To leading order the tube changes neither its field strength nor its length by enough to alter the particle <phase-space distribution function>: these changes are smaller by <plasma beta> or $L_p/R$. Parallel streaming remains within the same displaced tube, on which the original equilibrium <phase-space distribution function> was uniform. It therefore cannot erase the displacement-generated contrast between adjacent tubes. This is why the term $v_\parallel\delta b_x\partial_xf_{0\alpha}$ in <3/c> exactly cancels the apparent parallel relaxation of $-\xi_x\partial_xf_{0\alpha}$. No collisional restoration of a <Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution> is needed.

= Solution
{parent=3/e}

Assume the stated outward-decreasing <pressure>, $L_p>0$, and set
$$
\omega_I^2=\frac{2p_0}{\rho_0RL_p}=\frac{\beta v_A^2}{RL_p},\qquad
\boxed{k_c=\frac{\omega_I}{v_A}=\sqrt{\frac\beta{RL_p}}.}
$$
For $|k_\parallel|<k_c$, the <interchange instability> has
$$
\omega=\pm i\gamma,\qquad\gamma=\sqrt{\omega_I^2-k_\parallel^2v_A^2},\qquad
\xi_x=C_+e^{\gamma t}+C_-e^{-\gamma t}.
$$
An outward-displaced <flux tube> carries higher <pressure> than its new surroundings. In the unfavorable <magnetic field-line curvature> of the <Z-pinch>, the resulting imbalance drives it farther outward. Parallel variation bends field lines, and <magnetic tension> supplies the stabilizing $k_\parallel^2v_A^2$ term. The fastest local interchange is at $k_\parallel=0$, with $\gamma=\omega_I$; this endpoint is understood as the limit of the stated Alfvénic ordering. There is no leading $k_y$ dependence once $k_y\gg k_\parallel$.

For $|k_\parallel|>k_c$, the two real <frequencies> are $\omega=\pm\sqrt{k_\parallel^2v_A^2-\omega_I^2}$: the displacement oscillates as a curvature-modified <Alfvén wave>. At $|k_\parallel|=k_c$, the leading equation is $\ddot\xi_x=0$, so the marginal solutions are a constant and a term linear in time; neglected corrections determine finer behavior. For a favorable reversed <pressure gradient>, $L_p<0$, this drive is stabilizing.

The leading <dispersion relation> is the same in <ideal magnetohydrodynamics>. Its adiabatic law gives $\delta p=-\xi_xp_0'-\Gamma p_0\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol\xi$; the compressive part is smaller than the steep-gradient advection by $O(L_p/R)$ or $O(\beta)$, giving the same leading $\delta p=-\xi_xp_0'$. Relaxing those orderings introduces compression, field-strength and field-length changes, and <pressure anisotropy>; the <drift-kinetic equation> can then introduce parallel <kinetic phase mixing> and <Landau damping>. At larger <plasma beta>, coupling to compressive branches matters; if <pressure anisotropy> develops, <mirror instability> and <firehose instability> may also become relevant. These are possibilities beyond the derived leading equation, not additional roots already established here.

= Solution
{parent=3/f}

Both mechanisms can extract <free energy> from an equilibrium <pressure gradient> in unfavorable <magnetic field-line curvature>, but the dynamics retained here is the macroscopic electromagnetic displacement of a <flux tube>. It depends on the total <pressure gradient>, including <electron> and <ion> contributions, and it does not require an <ion temperature gradient> specifically. Its stable branch has $\omega\sim k_\parallel v_A$; with low <plasma beta>, this is much faster than <ion> parallel streaming at the same $k_\parallel$.

The familiar <ion-temperature-gradient instability> is a slower drift-scale instability. In its usual electrostatic ordering the <Boltzmann electron response> is established by rapid <electron> parallel motion, while <ion> magnetic drifts, <ion> temperature evolution and <polarization drift> provide the instability dynamics. A representative ordering is
$$
k_\parallel v_{\mathrm{th},i}\lesssim|\omega|\ll k_\parallel v_{\mathrm{th},e},\qquad
|\omega|\ll k_\parallel v_A,\qquad
\omega_{*T_i}\sim\frac{k_yv_{\mathrm{th},i}^2}{\Omega_iL_{T_i}},\quad
\omega_{di}\sim\frac{k_yv_{\mathrm{th},i}^2}{\Omega_iR}.
$$
The last two expressions specify scales rather than exact geometric factors; $L_{T_i}^{-1}=-d\ln T_{0i}/dx$. The present leading <kinetic magnetohydrodynamics> system drops these relative species drifts and their finite-<gyroradius> corrections. It also retains the perturbed field direction, which enforces the advected-distribution cancellation in <3/c>; a rapid <electron> streaming rate alone cannot turn that cancellation into a <Boltzmann electron response> on an unperturbed field.

To recover <ion-temperature-gradient instability> physics, take the slow, nearly electrostatic limit $|\omega|/(k_\parallel v_A)\ll1$, retain <magnetic curvature drift>, <gradient-B drift> and <polarization drift> in low-flow <drift kinetics> or <gyrokinetics>, and allow a sufficiently strong destabilizing <ion temperature gradient>. At long perpendicular <wavelengths> one may still have $k_\perp\rho_i\ll1$, provided the leading nonzero <ion> polarization is kept; at $k_\perp\rho_i=O(1)$ full <finite Larmor radius effects> are needed. There is no contradiction between a drift-scale <ion-temperature-gradient instability> and the macroscopic <interchange instability> derived here: they use different asymptotic balances.
