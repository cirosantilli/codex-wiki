= Stress-energy tensor
{wiki}

= Energy-momentum tensor
{synonym}

The stress-energy tensor describes local energy density, momentum density, and stress. Compatibility with the <Einstein field equations> requires covariant conservation $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=0$.

= Hilbert stress-energy tensor
{c}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T_{ab}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_m}{\delta g^{ab}}\quad(+---)$}

The metric variational stress tensor is defined by the matter action's response to a metric change. With signature $(+---)$ and the corresponding usual matter-action signs, the displayed convention means $\delta S_m=\tfrac12\int\sqrt{-g}\,T_{ab}\delta g^{ab}=-\tfrac12\int\sqrt{-g}\,T^{ab}\delta g_{ab}$. Fix the independent matter variables while varying the metric. Sign conventions must be matched to the action; copying a mostly-plus stress formula into this convention can reverse its energy density.

= Stress-energy tensor of a canonical scalar field
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi-g_{\mu\nu}[(\nabla\phi)^2/2-V]$}

For signature $(+---)$ and $S_\phi=\int\sqrt{-g}[(\nabla\phi)^2/2-V(\phi)]$, the convention $T_{\mu\nu}=2(\delta S_\phi/\delta g^{\mu\nu})/\sqrt{-g}$ gives the displayed tensor. Varying the inverse metric yields the derivative product, while varying the volume element yields the term proportional to $g_{\mu\nu}$. A homogeneous field has energy density $\dot\phi^2/2+V$ and pressure $\dot\phi^2/2-V$.

= Stress-energy tensor improvement
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T\prime{}^{\mu\nu}=T^{\mu\nu}+\partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}$}

Adding $\partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}$ with $B^{\rho\mu\nu}=-B^{\mu\rho\nu}$ to a <stress-energy tensor> preserves its divergence identically, since commuting partial derivatives annihilates the antisymmetric pair. The total <four-momentum> changes only by a spatial boundary term. Improvements can make local densities symmetric or <gauge invariant>; equality of integrated charges requires the chosen <boundary conditions>. Equation-of-motion terms can also relate representatives <on shell>.

= Improved stress-energy tensor of a free massless scalar
{parent=Stress-energy tensor improvement}
{title2=$\Theta_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi-\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}(\partial\phi)^2+\frac{d-2}{4(d-1)}(\eta_{\mu\nu}\Box-\partial_\mu\partial_\nu)\phi^2$}

For $d>1$, the displayed <stress-energy tensor> is symmetric and conserved on the free massless <scalar field> equation $\Box\phi=0$. Its improvement is identically divergence-free. Since $\Box\phi^2=2(\partial\phi)^2$ on shell, its trace vanishes. Contracting it with a <conformal Killing vector field> therefore produces a conserved conformal current. In $d=2$ the improvement coefficient is zero: the canonical tensor already has zero trace.

= Stress-energy superpotential
{parent=Stress-energy tensor improvement}
{title2=$B^{\rho\mu\nu}=-B^{\mu\rho\nu}$}

A <stress-energy superpotential> is an antisymmetric tensor $B^{\rho\mu\nu}=-B^{\mu\rho\nu}$ whose divergence supplies a <stress-energy tensor improvement>. In the <Maxwell field>, the potential-dependent expression $B^{\rho\mu}{}_{\nu}=F^{\mu\rho}A_\nu$ produces the difference between canonical and gauge-invariant <stress-energy tensors> <on shell>. Antisymmetry ensures identically conserved improvement terms.

= Dust stress-energy tensor
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T^{\mu\nu}=mn_0u^\mu u^\nu$}

A pressureless fluid with proper number density $n_0$, rest mass $m$ and <four-velocity> $u$ has tensor $mn_0u^\mu u^\nu$. In its local rest frame there is energy density but no spatial stress. Particle-number conservation and the Lorentz force make its divergence the electromagnetic force density.

= Null dust
{parent=Dust stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T_{ab}=\varepsilon k_ak_b$}

Null dust is matter with a <stress-energy tensor> $T_{ab}=\varepsilon k_ak_b$ for a future-directed <null vector> $k$. For every future-directed <timelike vector> $t$, the energy current is $-T^a{}_bt^b=\varepsilon(-k\cdot t)k^a$. Therefore its <dominant energy condition> holds exactly when $\varepsilon\geq0$, allowing the zero current. Rescaling $k$ rescales $\varepsilon$ inversely by the square and leaves the <stress-energy tensor> unchanged. Ingoing <Vaidya metric> matter has $k=-\partial_r$, $k_a=-(dv)_a$, and $\varepsilon=\dot m/(4\pi r^2)$.

= Momentum density
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$q^{\hat i}=T^{\hat0\hat i}$}

<Momentum density> is <momentum> per unit physical volume. In a local <orthonormal tetrad> of the measuring observer it is $q^{\hat i}=T^{\hat0\hat i}$. A <perfect fluid in general relativity> moving at a small velocity relative to that observer has $q^{\hat i}=(\rho+P)v^{\hat i}$ to first order. For isotropic background <photons>, $P=\rho/3$, so the factor is $4\rho/3$ rather than $\rho$. Integrating the <kinetic stress-energy tensor> gives $q^{\hat i}=\int f p^{\hat i}\,d^3p$.

= Anisotropic stress
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$\pi^{ij}=T^{ij}-\tfrac13\delta^{ij}T^k{}_k$}

In a local <orthonormal tetrad>, <anisotropic stress> is the trace-free spatial part of the <stress-energy tensor>, conventionally denoted $\pi^{ij}$. Some <linear cosmological perturbation theory> conventions instead use $\Pi^{ij}=-\pi^{ij}$ and write $T^{ij}=P\delta^{ij}-\Pi^{ij}$. The sign must be specified before using a <photon Euler equation>. The <photon quadrupole> determines the linear <photon> <anisotropic stress>.

= Stress-energy tensor of a nonminimally coupled scalar field
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T_{ab}=\nabla_a\Phi\nabla_b\Phi-\frac12g_{ab}(\nabla\Phi)^2+2\xi[\Phi^2G_{ab}+g_{ab}\Box(\Phi^2)-\nabla_a\nabla_b(\Phi^2)]$}

For action $S=\int\sqrt{-g}[-\frac12(\nabla\Phi)^2-\xi R\Phi^2]d^4x$, <metric variation> defines $\delta S=\frac12\int\sqrt{-g}T^{ab}\delta g_{ab}d^4x$ and gives the displayed tensor. The <contracted Bianchi identity> and the scalar derivative commutator yield $\nabla^aT_{ab}=(\Box\Phi-2\xi R\Phi)\nabla_b\Phi$, so the <stress-energy tensor> is conserved on the field equation.

= Stress-energy conservation
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}

= Covariant conservation of stress-energy
{synonym}

Local energy-momentum conservation is the covariant equation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$. Its normal and spatial projections give energy and momentum evolution equations in a spacetime foliation.

= Noether identity
{c}
{parent=Stress-energy conservation}

A Noether identity is an off-shell differential relation among Euler-Lagrange expressions implied by a local gauge symmetry. Diffeomorphism invariance relates the divergence of the stress-energy tensor to the matter field equations.

= Diffeomorphism identity for a metric action
{parent=Noether identity}
{title2=$\nabla_aE^{ab}\equiv0$}

= Off-shell metric divergence identity
{synonym}

For a diffeomorphism-invariant pure metric action, write $\delta S=-\int\sqrt{-g}E^{ab}\delta g_{ab}$. Substitute $\delta g_{ab}=\nabla_a\xi_b+\nabla_b\xi_a$ for an arbitrary compactly supported <vector field> and integrate by parts. Symmetry of $E^{ab}$ gives $\nabla_aE^{ab}=0$ without using field equations. This <Noether identity> concerns the gravitational variational tensor alone.

= Diffeomorphism Noether identity for a scalar field
{parent=Noether identity}
{title2=$\nabla^aT_{ab}=\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta\Phi}\nabla_b\Phi$}

Simultaneously varying a <metric tensor> and a <scalar field> by the <Lie derivative> along a compactly supported <vector field> leaves a covariant matter action invariant. <Integration by parts> gives the displayed <Noether identity>. It implies <stress-energy conservation> when the scalar equation holds without needing to impose the metric field equation.

= Perfect fluid in general relativity
{title2=$T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b+pg_{ab}$}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}

A relativistic perfect fluid has energy density $\rho$, isotropic pressure $p$ and unit timelike four-velocity $u$. Projecting $\nabla_aT^{ab}=0$ along and orthogonally to $u$ gives its energy and Euler equations.

For a <perfect fluid>, the relativistic stress tensor is $T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b+pg_{ab}$ in signature $(-,+,+,+)$ with $u^au_a=-1$.

= Relativistic dust
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^\mu u^\nu$}

= Pressureless relativistic fluid
{synonym}

<Relativistic dust> is a continuum of noninteracting massive particles with one local <four-velocity> $u^\mu$ and negligible <pressure>. With signature $(+---)$, $u^\mu u_\mu=c^2$ and rest <mass density> $\rho$, its <stress-energy tensor> has the displayed form. Contracting <stress-energy conservation> with $u_\nu$ and using <metric compatibility> gives $c^2\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)=0$. Substituting back yields $u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0$ wherever $\rho>0$: each particle follows a <geodesic>. The cosmological <pressureless matter> model is a homogeneous application.

= Geodesic motion of uncharged dust
{parent=Relativistic dust}

For $T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^\mu u^\nu$ and constant normalized $u_\mu u^\mu=c^2$, contraction of $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ with $u_\nu$ gives $\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)=0$. At nonzero <density> the remaining projection gives $u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0$.

= Charged relativistic dust
{parent=Relativistic dust}
{title2=$u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=(q/m)F^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha$}

For identical particles of <rest mass> $m$ and <electric charge> $q$, the <four-current> is $j^\mu=(q/m)\rho u^\mu$. The matter <stress-energy tensor> obeys $\nabla_\mu T_{\rm m}^{\mu\nu}=F^{\nu\alpha}j_\alpha$, with the opposite force density in the electromagnetic <stress-energy tensor>. Antisymmetry of the <electromagnetic field tensor> makes its contraction with two <four-velocities> vanish, so mass continuity still holds. Removing that term leaves the displayed covariant <Lorentz force> law. The matter tensor alone is not divergence-free when an electromagnetic force acts.

= Canonical scalar stress as a perfect fluid
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$\rho=K+V,\quad p=K-V$}

For a canonical scalar with timelike gradient, its <stress-energy tensor> has perfect-fluid form. In a homogeneous background, $\rho=\dot\phi^2/2+V$ and $p=\dot\phi^2/2-V$. Potential domination gives negative pressure, while the scalar's physical rest-frame <sound speed> is one. A constant negative background $w$ therefore does not force its perturbations to obey the adiabatic barotropic closure $\delta p=w\delta\rho$.

= Conformal density of a canonical scalar field
{parent=Canonical scalar stress as a perfect fluid}
{title2=$T_{00}=a^2\rho$}

For $ds^2=a^2(d\tau^2-d\mathbf x^2)$ and a homogeneous canonical scalar, $\rho=\phi'^2/(2a^2)+V$ is the physical density, whereas $T_{00}=a^2\rho$. At $w=-1/3$, the kinetic and potential energies are $K=\rho/3$ and $V=2\rho/3$, so $\phi'^2=2a^2\rho/3=2T_{00}/3$. A single power of $\phi'$ cannot replace the square in this energy identity.

= Canonical vorticity of a relativistic fluid
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$\omega=d(\epsilon u^\flat)$}

The canonical vorticity two-form is $\omega_{ab}=\nabla_a(\epsilon u_b)-\nabla_b(\epsilon u_a)$, with <specific enthalpy per particle> $\epsilon$. Potential flow $\epsilon u_a=\nabla_a\chi$ has $\omega=0$ for a <torsion-free connection>. Contracting this identity with $u^a$ yields $\epsilon u^a\nabla_au_b=\nabla_b\epsilon-u_bu^a\nabla_a\epsilon$, the isentropic <relativistic Euler equation> in signature $(+---)$. This canonical condition does not mean the unweighted four-velocity one-form is closed.

= Variational action for relativistic potential flow
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}

In signature $(+---)$, the matter action $S=\int\sqrt{-g}[p(\epsilon)+n((\nabla\chi)^2-\epsilon^2)/(2\epsilon)]$ uses independent $n,\epsilon,\chi$. Variations of $n,\epsilon,\chi$ give $(\nabla\chi)^2=\epsilon^2$, $p'(\epsilon)=n$, and <particle number conservation in a relativistic fluid> for $u_a=\nabla_a\chi/\epsilon$. Its <Hilbert stress-energy tensor> is $T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b-pg_{ab}$. The action describes isentropic potential flow, with vanishing <canonical vorticity of a relativistic fluid>, rather than every vortical perfect-fluid solution.

= Particle number conservation in a relativistic fluid
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$\nabla_a(nu^a)=0$}

The number current is $J^a=nu^a$, where $n$ is the rest-frame <number density> and $u^a$ the unit <four-velocity>. Its covariant continuity equation is $\nabla_aJ^a=0$, equivalently $u^a\nabla_an+n\nabla_au^a=0$. Integration over a spacetime volume and the <divergence theorem> equate the particle numbers crossing its boundary. This conservation law is separate from <stress-energy conservation>.

= Specific enthalpy per particle
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$\epsilon=(\rho+p)/n$}

For <number density> $n$, rest-frame <energy density> $\rho$ and <pressure> $p$, the enthalpy per particle is $\epsilon=(\rho+p)/n$. In an isentropic <barotropic fluid>, $d\rho=\epsilon\,dn$ and $dp=n\,d\epsilon$. It differs from enthalpy per unit mass by the particle mass when that mass is fixed. Using $\epsilon$ as thermodynamic variable gives $n=p'(\epsilon)$ and $\rho=\epsilon p'(\epsilon)-p(\epsilon)$.

= Barotropic relativistic sound speed
{parent=Specific enthalpy per particle}
{title2=$c_s^2=dp/d\rho$}

In units $c=1$, an isentropic <perfect fluid in general relativity> has sound speed $c_s^2=dp/d\rho$. Writing $n=p'(\epsilon)$ gives $d\rho=\epsilon\,dn$ and $c_s^2=n/(\epsilon p''(\epsilon))$. Linearized <particle number conservation in a relativistic fluid> and the <relativistic Euler equation> then yield acoustic waves with this speed. Positive $c_s^2$ is needed for ordinary acoustic stability; a causal fluid model additionally requires $c_s^2\le1$.

= Perfect-fluid alignment in a static spacetime
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$u^a=k^a/\sqrt{k^bk_b}$}

For <metric signature> $(+---)$, a <perfect fluid> has $T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b-pg_{ab}$. The <Einstein field equations> and <static Killing field is a Ricci eigenvector> imply $(\rho+p)(k\cdot u)u_{[a}k_{b]}=0$. Two nonzero timelike vectors cannot be orthogonal, so $k\cdot u\ne0$. If $\rho+p\ne0$, the <four-velocity> is parallel to the static <Killing vector>. Choosing both future-directed gives the displayed normalization. For $\rho+p=0$, the <stress-energy tensor> is proportional to the <metric tensor> and does not determine a preferred fluid velocity; the strict enthalpy hypothesis is therefore material.

= Stiff fluid
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{title2=$P=\rho$}

A <perfect fluid> with <equation of state> $P=\rho$ is called a stiff fluid. For positive <energy density>, its constant equation-of-state parameter is $w=1$. The <cosmological continuity equation> gives $\rho\propto a^{-6}$. In a flat universe dominated by this fluid, the <Friedmann equation> then gives $a(t)\propto(t-t_B)^{1/3}$. This statement assumes a nonzero fluid density and negligible other components.

= Homogeneous free scalar as a stiff fluid
{parent=Stiff fluid}
{title2=$a^3\dot\phi=\mathrm{constant}$}

For a spatially homogeneous <minimally coupled scalar field> with zero <scalar potential>, the <stress-energy tensor> has $\rho=P=\dot\phi^2/2$. Its field equation is $\ddot\phi+3H\dot\phi=0$, so $a^3\dot\phi$ is constant and its <energy density> decreases as $a^{-6}$. Thus its homogeneous stress is precisely that of a <stiff fluid>. Spatial gradients would introduce directional stresses, so this equivalence requires homogeneity.

= Cosmological contraction with a free homogeneous scalar
{parent=Homogeneous free scalar as a stiff fluid}
{title2=$a(\tau)=(-\tau)^{1/2},\quad\phi_0=\sqrt{3/2}\log(-\tau)$}

In units $8\pi G=1$, a free homogeneous scalar has $\rho=P=\dot\phi_0^2/2$ and therefore a stiff equation of state. Its equation gives $a^2\phi_0'=\text{constant}$, while the <Friedmann equation> gives $3\mathcal H^2=\phi_0'^2/2$. The displayed contracting branch satisfies both equations for negative <conformal time>. Constant normalizations and an additive scalar shift can be restored.

= Newtonian-potential Hankel modes in stiff contraction
{parent=Cosmological contraction with a free homogeneous scalar}
{title2=$\Phi_k=N_k(-\tau)^{-1}H_1^{(1)}(-k\tau)$}

In <conformal Newtonian gauge>, a free scalar has no linear anisotropic stress. Eliminating its perturbation with the momentum constraint gives $\Phi_k''+3\Phi_k'/\tau+k^2\Phi_k=0$. Substitution of $\Phi_k=(-\tau)^{-1}y(-k\tau)$ gives the order-one <Bessel equation>. The displayed <Hankel function> has positive incoming frequency $e^{-ik\tau}$ at early times.

= Vacuum-normalized Newtonian spectrum in stiff contraction
{parent=Newtonian-potential Hankel modes in stiff contraction}
{title2=$|\Phi_k|^2\sim3\hbar/(8\pi k^4\tau^4)$}

The momentum constraint relates the incoming scalar mode $a^{-1}\sqrt{\hbar/(2k)}e^{-ik\tau}$ to the <Newtonian-potential Hankel modes in stiff contraction>. Large-argument Hankel asymptotics give $|N_k|^2=3\pi\hbar/(32k^2)$. The small-argument behavior $H_1^{(1)}(z)\sim-2i/(\pi z)$ then gives the displayed spectrum. Its dimensional power is $k^{-4}$ and its power per logarithmic interval is proportional to $k^{-1}$. These are Newtonian-potential spectra; a curvature-perturbation spectrum need not have the same exponent.

= Relativistic perfect-fluid energy equation
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}

The timelike projection of stress-energy conservation is $u^a\nabla_a\rho+(\rho+p)\nabla_au^a=0$. It is the local adiabatic first law for a perfect fluid.

= Relativistic Euler equation
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}

The spatial projection of stress-energy conservation is $(\rho+p)u^b\nabla_bu^a=-(g^{ab}+u^au^b)\nabla_bp$.

= Weak-field slow-motion equations for a relativistic fluid
{parent=Relativistic Euler equation}

For $ds^2=(1+2\phi)dt^2-d\mathbf x^2$ with static weak $\phi$, normalization gives $u^0=1-\phi+O(\phi^2,v^2)$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{00}=\partial_i\phi$. Around a homogeneous state, the first-order equations are $\partial_t\delta n+n_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0$, $\partial_t\delta\rho+(\rho_0+p_0)\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0$, and $(\rho_0+p_0)\partial_t\mathbf v=-\nabla\delta p-(\rho_0+p_0)\nabla\phi$. The inertial density is enthalpy density even though the flow is slow. Only a further nonrelativistic equation of state, $p\ll\rho\simeq mn$, replaces it by mass density.

= Hydrostatic equilibrium along a Killing vector
{parent=Relativistic Euler equation}
{title2=$dp=-(\rho+p)d\log N,\quad N=\sqrt{K^aK_a}$}

For <metric signature> $(+---)$, a stationary <perfect fluid> follows a timelike <Killing vector field> $K$ with <four-velocity> $U=K/N$. Its <Killing-flow four-acceleration> is $U^b\nabla_bU_a=-\nabla_a\log N$. The <relativistic Euler equation> and stationarity of the <pressure> give the displayed <hydrostatic equilibrium> equation. This uses stationarity along $K$ and does not require hypersurface orthogonality.

= Stationary radiation density
{parent=Hydrostatic equilibrium along a Killing vector}
{title2=$\rho N^4=C$}

The <equation of state> of isotropic <blackbody radiation> is $p=\rho/3$. The <barotropic hydrostatic first integral> gives $\rho N^4=C$. The <Stefan–Boltzmann law> $\rho\propto T^4$ then yields the <Tolman–Ehrenfest relation> $TN=\mathrm{constant}$. Here $N=\sqrt{|K^aK_a|}$ for the stationary timelike <Killing vector field>; its normalization only changes the constants.

= Radiation fluid has no regular free surface
{parent=Stationary radiation density}
{title2=$\rho=C/N^4>0$}

A connected nonvacuum <perfect fluid> with <equation of state> $p=\rho/3$ obeys the <stationary radiation density> law $\rho=C/N^4$ with $C>0$. On a compact region of a regular <spacetime>, a smooth <Killing vector field> has bounded norm. Consequently $\rho$ cannot tend to zero at a finite regular <free surface>. A vanishing timelike norm instead makes the density diverge. A radiation-filled cavity can have a material wall with nonzero boundary <pressure>; this is a different boundary condition.

= Barotropic hydrostatic first integral
{parent=Hydrostatic equilibrium along a Killing vector}
{title2=$\int^p\frac{d\widetilde p}{\rho(\widetilde p)+\widetilde p}+\log N=C$}

For a <barotropic fluid> in <hydrostatic equilibrium along a Killing vector>, assume $\rho+p>0$. Integrating $dp/(\rho+p)=-d\log N$ gives the displayed <first integral>, constant on each connected fluid region. For the <equation of state> $p=w\rho$ with constant $w>0$, it gives $\rho N^{(1+w)/w}=C_1$.

= Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation
{title2=TOV equation}
{c}
{parent=Perfect fluid in general relativity}
{wiki}

The Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation governs a static, spherically symmetric <perfect fluid in general relativity>. In geometrized units, if $m(r)$ is the mass inside areal radius $r$, then
$$
\frac{dm}{dr}=4\pi r^2\rho,
\qquad
\frac{dp}{dr}=-\frac{(\rho+p)(m+4\pi r^3p)}{r(r-2m)}.
$$

= Stellar compactness
{parent=Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation}
{title2=$C(r)=\frac{m(r)}r$}

The ratio of enclosed gravitational mass to areal radius in units $G=c=1$. In a spherical star the local value is $m(r)/r$, and the surface value is $M/R$. Bounds on this ratio constrain relativistic stellar models; it is unrelated to topological compactness.

= Regular central expansion of the TOV equations
{parent=Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation}
{title2=$m=\frac{4\pi}3\rho_cr^3+O(r^5)$}

For a smooth centre with density $\rho_c$ and pressure $p_c$, the <Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation> gives $m(r)=4\pi\rho_cr^3/3+O(r^5)$ and $p(r)=p_c-2\pi(\rho_c+p_c)(\rho_c/3+p_c)r^2+O(r^4)$. A fixed <barotropic stellar equation of state> leaves one physical central parameter; the additive lapse constant fixes time normalization.

= Interior Schwarzschild solution
{c}
{synonym}

The interior Schwarzschild metric is the exact static solution for a spherical fluid of constant density. If the star has mass $M$ and radius $R$, its pressure is
$$
p(r)=\rho
\frac{\sqrt{1-2Mr^2/R^3}-\sqrt{1-2M/R}}
{3\sqrt{1-2M/R}-\sqrt{1-2Mr^2/R^3}}.
$$

= Central pressure of a constant-density relativistic star
{parent=Interior Schwarzschild metric}

The <interior Schwarzschild metric> has central pressure
$$
p(0)=\rho
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-2M/R}}
{3\sqrt{1-2M/R}-1}.
$$
It diverges when $M/R=4/9$, the limiting compactness in <Buchdahl's theorem>.

= Dominant-energy compactness bound for an incompressible star
{parent=Central pressure of a constant-density relativistic star}
{title2=$\frac MR\leq\frac38$}

The <dominant energy condition> for the <interior Schwarzschild metric> reduces to its largest pressure satisfying $p_c\leq\rho$. Substitution of the exact central pressure gives $\sqrt{1-2M/R}\geq1/2$, hence $M/R\leq3/8$. Equality has finite central pressure and is allowed by the non-strict condition.

= Buchdahl's theorem
{c}
{parent=Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equation}
{wiki}

Buchdahl's theorem gives $2M/R\leq8/9$ for a static spherical perfect-fluid star whose density is nonincreasing outwards, subject to its regularity assumptions. Equality would require divergent central pressure, so every regular such star satisfies $M/R<4/9$.

= High-density core bound from TOV compactness
{parent=Buchdahl's theorem}
{title2=$a<\frac1{\sqrt{3\pi\rho_0}}$}

Let $\rho_0>0$ be a density threshold and let $a,\mu$ be the radius and enclosed mass where a monotonically decreasing stellar density reaches that threshold. Then $\mu\geq4\pi\rho_0a^3/3$. A local compactness bound $\mu/a<4/9$ implies $a<(3\pi\rho_0)^{-1/2}$ and $\mu<4/(9\sqrt{3\pi\rho_0})$, independently of the <equation of state> above the threshold. A total-mass bound also requires suitable control of the known low-density envelope.

= Null energy condition
{title2=NEC}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{wiki}

The null energy condition requires $T_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\geq0$ for every null vector $k$. Through the Einstein field equations this is equivalent to $R_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu\geq0$ when the cosmological term is absent.

= Perfect-fluid null energy condition
{parent=Null energy condition}
{title2=$\rho+p\geq0$}

For $T_{ab}=(\rho+p)u_au_b+pg_{ab}$ and nonzero null $k$, contraction gives $T_{ab}k^ak^b=(\rho+p)(u\cdot k)^2$. A timelike fluid velocity has nonzero scalar product with every nonzero null vector, so the <null energy condition> is exactly the displayed inequality. Individual nonnegativity of pressure or density is unnecessary for null focusing. A <cosmological constant> term has zero null contraction.

= Null energy condition for a canonical scalar field
{parent=Null energy condition}
{title2=$T_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=(\ell^a\partial_a\phi)^2\geq0$}

The classical <stress-energy tensor of a canonical scalar field> has a gradient-product term and a metric-proportional term. Contracting with a real <null vector> removes the latter, leaving the displayed square for a real field. This proves the <null energy condition> for a minimally coupled canonical scalar, including the massless case. It is not a statement about arbitrary curvature couplings, wrong-sign kinetic terms or a renormalized quantum <stress-energy tensor>.

= Null energy condition for a planar warped spacetime
{parent=Null energy condition}
{title2=$(\log A)''\le0$}

In a <planar warped spacetime>, contraction of the <Ricci tensor> with a <null vector> in an <orthonormal frame> gives $R_{ab}k^ak^b=-2(\log A)''(k^3)^2$. The <Einstein field equations> identify this with $8\pi T_{ab}k^ak^b$; metric terms, including a cosmological constant, vanish in a null contraction. Thus the <null energy condition> is equivalent to concavity of $\log A$ in the proper transverse coordinate $z$. Null directions confined to a planar slice saturate the condition.

= Null convergence condition
{parent=Null energy condition}
{title2=$R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0$}

The <null convergence condition> requires $R_{ab}k^ak^b\geq0$ for every <null vector>. Through the <Einstein field equations>, it follows from the <null energy condition>, since all terms proportional to the <metric tensor> vanish on a <null vector>.

= Cosmological constant preserves null convergence
{parent=Null convergence condition}
{title2=$R_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=8\pi G T_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b$}

Contract the <Einstein field equations> with a <null vector>. Both the trace term and the <cosmological constant> term multiply $g_{ab}\ell^a\ell^b=0$ and disappear. The <null energy condition> therefore still implies the <null convergence condition>, independently of the sign of the <cosmological constant>. A <black-hole area theorem> still needs the appropriate global horizon assumptions as well as this local focusing condition.

= Averaged null energy condition
{title2=ANEC}
{parent=Null energy condition}

The averaged null energy condition requires
$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}T_{ab}k^ak^b\,d\lambda\geq0
$$
along every complete affinely parametrized null geodesic. It is weaker than the pointwise null energy condition.

= Averaged null energy condition for a nonminimally coupled scalar
{parent=Averaged null energy condition}

For the curvature-coupled scalar equation considered here, contraction with an affine null tangent and integration by parts gives, for $\xi<0$,
$$
\int T_{ab}k^ak^b\,d\lambda
=\int\frac{1+\xi(4\xi-1)\Phi^2}{(1-\xi\Phi^2)^2}(\Phi')^2\,d\lambda\geq0
$$
when $\Phi\Phi'/(1-\xi\Phi^2)$ vanishes at the endpoints.

= Conserved Klein-Gordon scalar stress-energy ansatz
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{c}

For $\nabla_\alpha\nabla^\alpha\phi=m^2\phi$, consider
$$
T_{\mu\nu}=\frac12\left[
\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi
+g_{\mu\nu}\left(A\nabla_\rho\phi\nabla^\rho\phi+B\phi^2\right)
\right].
$$
Covariant conservation for all solutions holds exactly when
$$
A=-\frac12,
\qquad
B=-\frac{m^2}{2}.
$$

= Klein-Gordon scalar stress-energy tensor
{c}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}

For a real scalar satisfying $\nabla_a\nabla^a\psi-\mu^2\psi=0$,
$$
T_{ab}=\nabla_a\psi\nabla_b\psi-\frac12g_{ab}\left(\nabla_c\psi\nabla^c\psi+\mu^2\psi^2\right)
$$
is symmetric and covariantly conserved.

= Weak energy condition
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{wiki}

The weak energy condition requires
$$
T_{\mu\nu}X^\mu X^\nu\geq0
$$
for every timelike vector $X$, so every observer measures nonnegative energy density.

= Weak energy condition for a quadratic scalar stress-energy ansatz
{parent=Weak energy condition}

For the general scalar ansatz in <conserved Klein-Gordon scalar stress-energy ansatz>, work in a unit timelike frame. Then
$$
2T_{00}=(1+A)(\partial_0\phi)^2
-A|\nabla_{\rm s}\phi|^2-B\phi^2.
$$
The weak energy condition for arbitrary local field data is therefore equivalent to
$$
-1\leq A\leq0,
\qquad B\leq0.
$$

= Strong energy condition
{title2=SEC}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{wiki}

The strong energy condition requires $(T_{ab}-Tg_{ab}/2)u^au^b\geq0$ for every timelike vector $u$ in four spacetime dimensions. Through the <Einstein field equations>, it implies the timelike convergence condition $R_{ab}u^au^b\geq0$ when the cosmological constant vanishes.

= Dominant energy condition
{title2=DEC}
{parent=Stress-energy tensor}
{wiki}

The dominant energy condition requires the energy flux measured by every future-directed timelike observer to be future-directed and causal. Equivalently, $T_{ab}u^av^b\geq0$ for every pair of future-directed causal vectors $u$ and $v$ under the $(-,+,+,+)$ convention.

= Dominant energy condition for a kinetic scalar field
{parent=Dominant energy condition}

For a <scalar field> with $X=-q^2/2$, Lagrangian $F(X)$ and <stress-energy tensor> $T_{ab}=F\prime q_aq_b+Fg_{ab}$, the inequalities $A=F\prime\ge0$ and $Y=XF\prime-F\ge0$ imply the <dominant energy condition> for any gradient type. In a local <Lorentz frame>, $\rho=A(q_0^2+\sum_iq_i^2)/2+Y\ge0$ and $\rho^2-\sum_iT_{0i}^2=A^2X^2+A(q_0^2+\sum_iq_i^2)Y+Y^2\ge0$.
