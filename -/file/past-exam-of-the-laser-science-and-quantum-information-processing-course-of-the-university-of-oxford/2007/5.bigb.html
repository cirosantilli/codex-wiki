== 1
{scope}

=== Solution

Represent the <single-photon state> by its two path <probability amplitudes>. The first <Hadamard gate> sends $|0\rangle$ to $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. With $P(\phi)=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})$ for the lower-arm <phase shifter>, the complete <Mach-Zehnder interferometer> gives
$$
H P(\phi)H|0\rangle
=\frac{1+e^{i\phi}}2|0\rangle+\frac{1-e^{i\phi}}2|1\rangle
=e^{i\phi/2}\left(\cos\frac\phi2|0\rangle-i\sin\frac\phi2|1\rangle\right).
$$
The <global phase> has no effect on detection. By the <Born rule>,
$$
\boxed{P_0(\phi)=\cos^2\frac\phi2,\qquad P_1(\phi)=\sin^2\frac\phi2.}
$$
They sum to one. At $\phi=0$, port 1 is a <dark port>; at $\phi=\pi$, the roles reverse. The result uses coherent addition of the path amplitudes, rather than addition of separate path <probabilities>.

== 2
{scope}

=== Solution

Write $R_z(\theta)=e^{-i\theta Z/2}$ and $R_x(\theta)=e^{-i\theta X/2}$, using the <Pauli operators>. The <Hadamard gate> obeys $HZH=X$, so <unitary conjugation> of the <matrix exponential> gives $H R_z(\beta)H=R_x(\beta)$. Therefore the <symmetric z-x-z beam splitter factorization> is
$$
R_z(\alpha)H R_z(\beta)H R_z(\alpha)
=R_z(\alpha)R_x(\beta)R_z(\alpha)
=\begin{pmatrix}
e^{-i\alpha}\cos(\beta/2)&-i\sin(\beta/2)\\
-i\sin(\beta/2)&e^{i\alpha}\cos(\beta/2)
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The rightmost operator acts first. Each factor is <unitary>, which also proves the given matrix is unitary. Choosing
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac\pi2,\qquad\beta=\frac\pi2}
$$
gives $U_{\rm BS}=-iH$, so it is exactly a <Hadamard gate> up to <global phase>.

For the specified identical splitters with $\alpha=0$, put $c=\cos(\beta/2)$ and $s=\sin(\beta/2)$. Here $U_{\rm BS}=R_x(\beta)$, and direct multiplication gives
$$
U_{\rm BS}P(\phi)U_{\rm BS}|0\rangle
=(c^2-e^{i\phi}s^2)|0\rangle-ics(1+e^{i\phi})|1\rangle.
$$
Thus the upper-port probability is
$$
\boxed{P_0=c^4+s^4-2c^2s^2\cos\phi
=1-\sin^2\beta\cos^2\frac\phi2,}
$$
with $P_1=\sin^2\beta\cos^2(\phi/2)$. These <identical beam splitters in a two-path interferometer> give a nonconstant <interference fringe> whenever $\sin\beta\ne0$. With this matrix and port convention, two identical balanced splitters swap the bright and dark ports relative to the two-Hadamard model: at $\phi=0$, $R_x(\pi/2)^2=-iX$. The reflection phases must remain in the calculation.

The upper-port maximum and minimum are $1$ and $\cos^2\beta$. Hence the peak-to-peak modulation is $\sin^2\beta$ and its normalized <interferometric visibility> is
$$
\mathcal V=\frac{P_{\max}-P_{\min}}{P_{\max}+P_{\min}}
=\frac{\sin^2\beta}{1+\cos^2\beta}.
$$
Both measures of contrast are maximal precisely for
$$
\boxed{\beta=\frac\pi2+k\pi,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.}
$$
These are <balanced beam splitters>, with equal single-input transmission and reflection probabilities. When $\beta\in\pi\mathbb Z$, only one route contributes to the output and the fringes disappear.

== 3
{scope}

=== Solution

Calibrate the two-Hadamard <Mach-Zehnder interferometer> so that the passive box leaves no relative <wave phase> or path disturbance. Then $H^2|0\rangle=|0\rangle$, and port 1 is dark. A photon at port 1 is therefore impossible for the passive box under these assumptions.

For an active ideal detector in arm 1, the first <Hadamard gate> prepares $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Absorption, which triggers the detector, has probability $1/2$. On a no-absorption outcome, the surviving unnormalized state is $|0\rangle/\sqrt2$. Applying the second Hadamard gives
$$
H\frac{|0\rangle}{\sqrt2}=\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{2}.
$$
Its squared norm is $1/2$, the survival probability; normalizing it earlier and forgetting that probability would overcount successful trials. The unconditional outcomes for an active detector are
$$
\boxed{P(\text{absorption})=\frac12,\quad
P(\text{port }0)=\frac14,\quad P(\text{port }1)=\frac14.}
$$
Thus **a port-1 click identifies an active detector without triggering it**, with **single-pass success probability $1/4$**, conditional on testing an active device. A port-0 click is inconclusive. Among trials in which the photon survives, half are successful, but that conditional fraction is not the single-pass success probability.

This is the <balanced Hadamard interaction-free measurement> version of the <Elitzur-Vaidman bomb tester>. Conditioning on no absorption changes the <quantum coherence> available for recombination; a calibrated dark-port click reveals that change. An uncontrolled phase shift in the passive arm could also brighten the port, so the inference depends on the stated optical calibration. It does not assign an observed classical trajectory to the photon before detection. If the same active device is retested after inconclusive clicks, the <repeated weak-rotation bomb-test efficiency> gives eventual success $(1/4)/(1-1/4)=1/3$; the requested single pass remains $1/4$.
