== 1
{scope}

=== Solution

Use the ordered <computational basis> $(|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle)$, with $Z|0\rangle=|0\rangle$ and $Z|1\rangle=-|1\rangle$. Write $X,Y,Z$ for the <Pauli matrices>, $I$ for the <identity operator>, $J=\omega_{12}$, $\Sigma=\omega_1+\omega_2$, and $\Delta=\omega_1-\omega_2$. As in the question, the <Hamiltonian operators> have <angular frequency> units, so the <time-evolution operator> is $e^{-iHt}$. The <Heisenberg coupling matrix for two spins> is
$$
C=X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y+Z\otimes Z
=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0\\
0&-1&2&0\\
0&2&-1&0\\
0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Consequently the full <Heisenberg model> <Hamiltonian operator> and its proposed <Ising coupling> approximation are
$$
H_{\mathrm H}=\begin{pmatrix}
(\Sigma+J)/2&0&0&0\\
0&(\Delta-J)/2&J&0\\
0&J&(-\Delta-J)/2&0\\
0&0&0&(-\Sigma+J)/2
\end{pmatrix},\qquad
H_{\mathrm I}=\operatorname{diag}\!\left(
\frac{\Sigma+J}{2},\frac{\Delta-J}{2},
\frac{-\Delta-J}{2},\frac{-\Sigma+J}{2}\right).
$$

For <nondegenerate perturbation theory>, choose $H_{\mathrm I}$ as the unperturbed <Hamiltonian operator> and
$$
V=H_{\mathrm H}-H_{\mathrm I}
=J\bigl(|01\rangle\langle10|+|10\rangle\langle01|\bigr)
$$
as the perturbation. Only $|01\rangle$ and $|10\rangle$ are coupled, and their unperturbed <energy levels> differ by $\Delta$. All first-order <energy> corrections vanish. The first-order <eigenvectors> and second-order <energy> corrections are
$$
|\widetilde{01}\rangle=|01\rangle+\frac{J}{\Delta}|10\rangle
+O\!\left(\frac{J^2}{\Delta^2}\right),\qquad
|\widetilde{10}\rangle=|10\rangle-\frac{J}{\Delta}|01\rangle
+O\!\left(\frac{J^2}{\Delta^2}\right),
$$
$$
\delta E_{01}^{(2)}=\frac{J^2}{\Delta},\qquad
\delta E_{10}^{(2)}=-\frac{J^2}{\Delta}.
$$
The states $|00\rangle,|11\rangle$ are exact <eigenvectors> throughout. Hence the <weak coupling condition for two NMR spins> is
$$
\boxed{|\omega_1-\omega_2|\gg |\omega_{12}|.}
$$
A large common <Zeeman energy> alone does not ensure this condition. Crossings involving the uncoupled parallel-spin states do not invalidate the argument: the relevant denominator is the separation of the two states connected by $V$.

As an exact check, the middle $2\times2$ <matrix> has <eigenvalues>
$$
E_\pm=-\frac J2\pm\sqrt{\frac{\Delta^2}{4}+J^2}.
$$
For $\Delta\ne0$, labeling by the limiting <computational basis> states reproduces the shifts above. The <quantum transition probability> from $|01\rangle$ to $|10\rangle$ is
$$
P_{01\to10}(t)=\frac{4J^2}{\Delta^2+4J^2}
\sin^2\!\left(\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{\Delta^2+4J^2}\right).
$$
Thus unwanted exchange has a uniformly small amplitude when $|J/\Delta|\ll1$. If accumulated phases must also be approximated over a duration $t$, neglecting the second-order shifts additionally requires $J^2t/|\Delta|\ll1$, or those shifts must be retained in an improved effective <Hamiltonian operator>.

The same result is the <secular approximation for scalar-coupled spins>. In the <interaction picture> of the <Zeeman energy> terms,
$$
V_{\mathrm{int}}(t)=J\left(e^{i\Delta t}|01\rangle\langle10|
+e^{-i\Delta t}|10\rangle\langle01|\right).
$$
These terms average rapidly on the coupling timescale $1/|J|$ when $|\Delta|\gg|J|$, while $JZ\otimes Z/2$ survives.

For fixed nuclei and electronic environments, the individual <NMR> angular frequencies scale with the static <magnetic field> $B_0$: schematically $\omega_i=\kappa_i B_0$, where $\kappa_i$ includes the <gyromagnetic ratio> and magnetic shielding. The <NMR scalar coupling> $J$ in <angular frequency> units is approximately independent of $B_0$. Therefore a nonzero separation $|\Delta|=|\kappa_1-\kappa_2|B_0$ increases with <magnetic field>, improving the approximation. In particular, a fixed difference of <chemical shifts> gives a larger separation in hertz at higher <magnetic field>. If the two resonances remain exactly equal, increasing their common frequency does **not** suppress their exchange.

== 2
{scope}

=== Solution

Set $\omega_1=\omega_2=\omega$. Define the <quantum rotating frame> by
$$
H_Z=\frac{\omega}{2}(Z\otimes I+I\otimes Z),\qquad
R(t)=e^{-itH_Z},\qquad
|\psi_{\mathrm r}(t)\rangle=R(t)^\dagger|\psi(t)\rangle.
$$
The isotropic <Heisenberg model> coupling commutes with the total $z$ <spin angular momentum>, so $[H_Z,C]=0$. The transformed <Hamiltonian operator> is therefore
$$
H_{\mathrm r}=R^\dagger H_{\mathrm H}R-iR^\dagger\dot R
=R^\dagger H_{\mathrm H}R-H_Z=\frac J2C.
$$
Here removal of the common <Zeeman energy> is exact; the exchange is resonant and cannot be discarded using the <weak coupling condition for two NMR spins>.

Put $a=Jt$, $c=\cos a$, and $s=\sin a$. Exponentiating the parallel-spin entries and the middle $2\times2$ <matrix> gives the <time-evolution operator>
$$
\boxed{U(t)=e^{-itH_{\mathrm r}}=
\begin{pmatrix}
e^{-ia/2}&0&0&0\\
0&e^{ia/2}c&-ie^{ia/2}s&0\\
0&-ie^{ia/2}s&e^{ia/2}c&0\\
0&0&0&e^{-ia/2}
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
For example, at $a=\pi/2$ this equals the <SWAP gate> times $e^{-i\pi/4}$, which checks both the exchange angle and the overall phase.

The printed network reads from left to right as $U$, a <Pauli Z gate> on the **upper spin only**, $U$, and another upper-spin <Pauli Z gate>. Let
$$
P=Z\otimes I=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1).
$$
Its total <time-evolution operator>, with the first operation on the right, is $W=PUPU$. For this <Heisenberg exchange echo>,
$$
PH_{\mathrm r}P=\frac J2(-X\otimes X-Y\otimes Y+Z\otimes Z).
$$
The three paired <Pauli operators> $X\otimes X$, $Y\otimes Y$, $Z\otimes Z$ commute pairwise: for example both products $(X\otimes X)(Y\otimes Y)$ and $(Y\otimes Y)(X\otimes X)$ equal $-Z\otimes Z$. Thus $PH_{\mathrm r}P$ commutes with $H_{\mathrm r}$, and
$$
\boxed{W=e^{-itPH_{\mathrm r}P}e^{-itH_{\mathrm r}}
=e^{-iJtZ\otimes Z}
=\begin{pmatrix}
e^{-ia}&0&0&0\\
0&e^{ia}&0&0\\
0&0&e^{ia}&0\\
0&0&0&e^{-ia}
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
The <spin echo> cancels the $XX+YY$ exchange terms and retains the <Ising coupling>. Equivalently, over the total free-evolution time $2t$ it produces the effective <Hamiltonian operator> $JZ\otimes Z/2$ exactly for ideal instantaneous gates. It is generally **not the identity**. A physical $\pi$ rotation about $z$ is $e^{-i\pi Z/2}=-iZ$; using two such pulses instead of the ideal $Z$ gates changes $W$ only by a physically irrelevant <global phase> of minus one.

The implementation requires <frequency-selective spin control>. Under the usual common-<radio frequency> <NMR> assumptions, equal resonance frequencies give no way to apply a pulse to the first spin while leaving the second untouched. For equally coupled spins in a spatially uniform control field, the transverse control <Hamiltonian operator> has the form
$$
H_c(t)=\frac{u_x(t)}2(X\otimes I+I\otimes X)
+\frac{u_y(t)}2(Y\otimes I+I\otimes Y).
$$
Both this control and the drift <Hamiltonian operator> commute with the <SWAP gate> $S$. Every reachable <time-evolution operator> consequently commutes with $S$. But $SPS=I\otimes Z\ne P$: the individual pulse required by the diagram fails this symmetry test. This is the <exchange symmetry obstruction to individual spin control>. Applying simultaneous $Z$ gates gives $Q=Z\otimes Z$, which commutes with $H_{\mathrm r}$ and instead yields $QUQU=U^2$; it does not refocus the exchange. A common change of the RF phase reference also supplies only a collective rotation.

This obstruction assumes the usual common-RF control with no extra handle distinguishing the spins. Equal resonance frequencies do not universally forbid individual addressing: a spatially selective field, unequal control couplings, or another symmetry-breaking interaction could alter <controllability>. Such resources are absent from the specified equivalent-spin <NMR> model.

{scope}

=== Solution

<Quantum error correction> encodes a <logical qubit> redundantly, obtains an <error syndrome> without measuring its unknown logical amplitudes, and applies a recovery that reverses the specified errors. A <decoherence-free subspace> instead uses a symmetry of a specified noise model: each noise operator acts as a scalar on the entire encoded space, so no syndrome-dependent recovery is required. This is passive protection and can also be viewed as a special case of <quantum error correction>.

For the <two-spin decoherence-free encoding>, choose
$$
|0_L\rangle=|01\rangle,\qquad |1_L\rangle=|10\rangle,\qquad
|\psi_L\rangle=\alpha|01\rangle+\beta|10\rangle,
\qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1.
$$
Simultaneous <Pauli Z gates> give
$$
(Z\otimes Z)|01\rangle=-|01\rangle,\qquad
(Z\otimes Z)|10\rangle=-|10\rangle,
$$
so
$$
\boxed{(Z\otimes Z)|\psi_L\rangle=-|\psi_L\rangle.}
$$
Both logical components acquire the same <global phase>; their <relative phase> and amplitudes survive. The <density matrix> is exactly unchanged, so even an unknown random choice between applying $I$ and $Z\otimes Z$ causes no logical <dephasing>.

More strongly, the encoding protects against arbitrary <collective dephasing>:
$$
[R_z(\phi)\otimes R_z(\phi)]|\psi_L\rangle
=e^{-i\phi(Z\otimes I+I\otimes Z)/2}|\psi_L\rangle
=|\psi_L\rangle,
$$
because both basis states have total $Z$ <eigenvalue> zero. At $\phi=\pi$, $R_z(\pi)\otimes R_z(\pi)=-Z\otimes Z$, consistent with the previous overall phase. The protection relies on correlated noise: independent $Z$ rotations generally change the logical relative phase, and this two-spin code does not protect a whole <logical qubit> against arbitrary collective rotations about all three axes or against arbitrary single-spin errors.
