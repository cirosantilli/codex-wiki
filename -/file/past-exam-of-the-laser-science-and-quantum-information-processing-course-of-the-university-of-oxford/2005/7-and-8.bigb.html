== 7
{scope}

The printed opening block offers a choice of two protocols for 8 marks in total. All three choices are solved here. Sections 1 and 2 below identify the subsequent unlettered blocks, worth 10 and 7 marks respectively; these section numbers are navigation labels.

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

In <quantum dense coding>, a shared <Bell pair> lets Alice send two classical <bits> by transmitting one <qubit>. Use <photon polarization> as the <computational basis>, $|0\rangle=|H\rangle$, $|1\rangle=|V\rangle$, and define the <Bell states>
$$
|\phi^\pm\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|\psi^\pm\rangle=\frac{|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Alice and Bob initially share $|\phi^+\rangle_{AB}$. Alice encodes a two-<bit> message $(m,n)$ by applying the <Pauli operator> $X^nZ^m$ to her <qubit>:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(m,n)&\text{Alice's operation}&\text{shared state}\\\hline
(0,0)&I&|\phi^+\rangle\\
(1,0)&Z&|\phi^-\rangle\\
(0,1)&X&|\psi^+\rangle\\
(1,1)&XZ&-|\psi^-\rangle
\end{array}
$$
The last minus sign is a <global phase>. Alice transmits her <photon> to Bob, who now holds both <qubits> and uses a <Bell-basis measurement> to identify the message. The four <Bell states> are <orthogonal>, so an ideal <Bell state analyzer> decodes them without error. The resource cost is one previously shared <Bell pair> and one transmitted <qubit> per two classical <bits>; the shared <quantum entanglement> is consumed. Before transmission Bob's <reduced density matrix> is $I/2$ for every message, consistent with the <no-communication theorem>.

The <BBO> crystal supplies the <polarization-entangled photon pair>. An ultraviolet <laser> pumps this <nonlinear optical crystal>; <spontaneous parametric down-conversion> produces signal and idler <photons> with $\omega_p=\omega_s+\omega_i$ and <phase matching> $\mathbf k_p=\mathbf k_s+\mathbf k_i$. In a noncollinear <type-II phase matching> arrangement, collect the intersections of the two emission cones. The alternatives $|H\rangle_A|V\rangle_B$ and $|V\rangle_A|H\rangle_B$ must be indistinguishable in arrival time, spectrum and spatial mode. Compensation of <birefringence> and adjustment of the <relative quantum phase> then give $(|HV\rangle+|VH\rangle)/\sqrt2$; a local <Pauli X gate> converts it to $|\phi^+\rangle$. <Half-wave plates> and other <waveplates> implement Alice's <Pauli gates>. The <BBO> is the pair source; the message is encoded subsequently by the local optical elements.<New High-Intensity Source of Polarization-Entangled Photon Pairs by Paul G. Kwiat and others>[1995, Figures 1 and 2: intersecting type-II emission cones and compensation; Table I: four Bell states]{ref}

A basic passive <linear optics> <Bell state analyzer> with no extra resources identifies only two of the four polarization <Bell states> unambiguously. Thus the ideal two-<bit> capacity presupposes complete <Bell-basis measurement>; that basic optical implementation alone does not attain it deterministically.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

In <entanglement swapping>, start with independent <Bell pairs> $|\phi^+\rangle_{12}|\phi^+\rangle_{34}$. Send <qubits> 2 and 3 to a central <Bell state analyzer>, retaining 1 and 4 at separate endpoints. Reordering the <tensor product> factors to group 23 and 14 gives the identity
$$
|\phi^+\rangle_{12}|\phi^+\rangle_{34}
=\frac12\left(
|\phi^+\rangle_{23}|\phi^+\rangle_{14}
+|\phi^-\rangle_{23}|\phi^-\rangle_{14}
+|\psi^+\rangle_{23}|\psi^+\rangle_{14}
+|\psi^-\rangle_{23}|\psi^-\rangle_{14}\right).
$$
Consequently each ideal <Bell-basis measurement> outcome has <probability> $1/4$ and projects 14 onto the corresponding <Bell state>, even though those two <photons> have never interacted. Communicating the two-<bit> result permits an endpoint <Pauli gate> to convert every outcome into the same chosen <Bell pair>. Without that record, the endpoints have $\rho_{14}=I_4/4$, a <product state>, rather than a known <entangled state>.

The optical <Bell state analyzer> overlaps <photons> 2 and 3 at a <balanced beam splitter>, with matched spatial modes, spectra and arrival times, and resolves the output <photon polarizations> using <polarizing beam splitters> and <single-photon detectors>. <Two-photon interference> maps <Bell states> to different output patterns. The antisymmetric $|\psi^-\rangle$ gives one <photon> in each output with opposite <polarizations>. The symmetric $|\psi^+\rangle$ gives two opposite-polarization <photons> in the same output; the two polarization detectors there can resolve this pattern. The $|\phi^\pm\rangle$ states give same-polarization pairs and remain mutually ambiguous in this basic setup. The detected pattern therefore heralds a particular endpoint <Bell state>. With ideal detection this arrangement succeeds unambiguously on two of four equally likely inputs, with <probability> $1/2$; the successful events can be selected. A complete analyzer would accept all four outcomes. The endpoint <quantum entanglement> is conditional on the measurement record, so it enables no instantaneous signalling.<Experimental Entanglement Swapping: Entangling Photons That Never Interacted by Jian-Wei Pan and others>[1998, experimental Bell projection and conditional endpoint correlations]{ref}

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For <quantum key distribution> with <Bell pairs>, a source distributes one <photon> to Alice and its partner to Bob. One implementation is the <Ekert protocol>, using the <spin singlet state> $|\psi^-\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Each party independently chooses a <polarization> measurement setting after distribution. Equal settings give opposite outcomes; Bob reverses his outcome-to-<bit> assignment so these rounds give matching random <bits>. Other setting pairs test a <Bell inequality>, with ideal <spin singlet state> correlation $E(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)=-\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b$ for the corresponding <Bloch sphere> axes. The optical <polarizer> angles correspond to twice those angles on the appropriate <Bloch sphere> great circle.

Over an <authenticated public classical channel>, Alice and Bob announce settings and detection times, select coincident detection rounds, and perform <key sifting>. They reveal a test sample to estimate the <quantum bit error rate> and the chosen <Bell inequality> signal, discarding all revealed <bits>. Excess disturbance causes an abort. Otherwise <information reconciliation> makes the retained strings agree and <privacy amplification> shortens them to remove an adversary's residual information, including information disclosed during reconciliation. The tested <quantum entanglement> supplies correlations unavailable to an independently prepared classical mixture; a security claim also requires stated detector and sampling assumptions.<Quantum cryptography based on Bell's theorem by Artur K. Ekert>[1991, entanglement-based key distribution and Bell-correlation test]{ref}

Each <photon polarization> analyzer uses a setting-dependent <waveplate> followed by a <polarizing beam splitter>. A <single-photon detector> on each output converts the routed <photon> into an electrical click, recording the outcome and its time. For example, a <single-photon avalanche diode> is a reverse-biased <photodiode> operated above breakdown: absorption creates a charge carrier, <impact ionization> triggers an avalanche, and the resulting pulse is registered. A quenching circuit lowers the bias to stop the avalanche and then resets the device. The <detector dead time> during recovery limits the count rate; <dark counts> produce false clicks, and imperfect detection loses rounds. The <polarization> information comes from the preceding analyzer, while the detector registers which output fired. Coincidence timing associates Alice's and Bob's records with the same emitted pair. https://www.thorlabs.co.jp/images/Catalog/V21/V21_7_LightAnalysis.pdf[Thorlabs, Light Analysis catalogue, Single Photon Counter Modules: Geiger-mode operation, active quenching, blind time and dark counts]{ref}

=== 1
{scope}

==== Solution

Let the unknown input <qubit> be $|\psi\rangle_S=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle$, with $|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1$. Alice holds $S,A$, Bob holds $B$, and the shared <Bell pair> is $|\phi^+\rangle_{AB}$. All three-<qubit> strings below use the order $S,A,B$. Initially,
$$
|\Psi_0\rangle=|\psi\rangle_S|\phi^+\rangle_{AB}
=\frac{\alpha|000\rangle+\alpha|011\rangle+\beta|100\rangle+\beta|111\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
The <Bell-basis teleportation identity> gives
$$
|\Psi_0\rangle=\frac12\left[
|\phi^+\rangle_{SA}|\psi\rangle_B
+|\phi^-\rangle_{SA}Z|\psi\rangle_B
+|\psi^+\rangle_{SA}X|\psi\rangle_B
+|\psi^-\rangle_{SA}XZ|\psi\rangle_B\right].
$$
Thus a perfect <Bell state analyzer> on Alice's two <qubits> identifies the <Pauli operator> affecting Bob's <quantum state>.

To display the intermediate three-<qubit> states explicitly, realize that <Bell-basis measurement> by a <controlled-NOT gate> $S\to A$, a <Hadamard gate> on $S$, and two <computational-basis measurements>. After the <controlled-NOT gate>,
$$
|\Psi_1\rangle=\frac{\alpha|000\rangle+\alpha|011\rangle+\beta|110\rangle+\beta|101\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
After the <Hadamard gate>,
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\Psi_2\rangle=\frac12\big[&\alpha(|000\rangle+|100\rangle+|011\rangle+|111\rangle)\\
&+\beta(|010\rangle-|110\rangle+|001\rangle-|101\rangle)\big]\\
=\frac12\big[&|00\rangle(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle)
+|01\rangle(\beta|0\rangle+\alpha|1\rangle)\\
&+|10\rangle(\alpha|0\rangle-\beta|1\rangle)
+|11\rangle(-\beta|0\rangle+\alpha|1\rangle)\big]_{SA,B}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Measuring $S,A$ gives $(m,n)$ with <probability> $1/4$ for each outcome, independently of $\alpha,\beta$. Conditional on that result, the normalized three-<qubit> <post-measurement state> is
$$
|\Psi_{mn}\rangle=|mn\rangle_{SA}\otimes X^nZ^m|\psi\rangle_B.
$$
If the <Bell state analyzer> is instead regarded as an ideal direct <projective measurement> preserving the measured <qubits>, replace $|mn\rangle_{SA}$ here by the corresponding <Bell state>; Bob's factor is identical. A destructive optical analyzer records the same label but absorbs Alice's <photons>.

Alice sends the two classical <bits> $(m,n)$ to Bob. During transmission the above conditional three-<qubit> state is unchanged. Bob then applies the inverse <Pauli operator> $Z^mX^n$:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{Bell result}&(m,n)&\text{Bob's state before correction}&\text{correction}\\\hline
\phi^+&00&|\psi\rangle&I\\
\psi^+&01&X|\psi\rangle&X\\
\phi^-&10&Z|\psi\rangle&Z\\
\psi^-&11&XZ|\psi\rangle&ZX
\end{array}
$$
The final conditional three-<qubit> state is therefore $|mn\rangle_{SA}\otimes|\psi\rangle_B$. The protocol transmits **two classical bits per teleported qubit**, consuming one shared <Bell pair>. Without Alice's record Bob averages over four <Pauli operators> and has <density matrix> $I/2$, so <quantum teleportation> respects the <no-communication theorem>. Alice's input has been measured and is no longer an independent copy, consistent with the <no-cloning theorem>.

=== 2
{scope}

==== Solution

For <teleportation with an ambiguous Bell-state phase label>, write $\rho=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$. The <Bell-basis measurement> outcomes $\phi^+$ and $\phi^-$ each occur with <probability> $1/4$. In either case a correct guess gives Bob $\rho$, while the wrong guess differs by the <Pauli Z gate> and gives $Z\rho Z$. The other two <Bell states> are identified correctly. Since Alice guesses each ambiguous label with <probability> $1/2$, the overall wrong-correction <probability> is $(1/2)(1/2)=1/4$. Averaging over the actual <quantum measurement> outcome and her guess gives the <phase-flip channel>
$$
\boxed{\rho_{\rm out}=\mathcal E(\rho)=\frac34\rho+\frac14Z\rho Z
=\begin{pmatrix}|\alpha|^2&\alpha\beta^*/2\\\alpha^*\beta/2&|\beta|^2\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Conditional only on an ambiguous analyzer result, the output is $(\rho+Z\rho Z)/2$; that conditional channel has weight $1/2$ in the displayed unconditional result. There is no rejection of those rounds.

Use the <teleportation fidelity> for a <pure state> target, namely the <squared quantum fidelity>
$$
F_\psi=\langle\psi|\rho_{\rm out}|\psi\rangle.
$$
This compares the input with the resulting <mixed state>; averaging state vectors before forming a <density matrix> would give a different, incorrect answer. Direct substitution gives
$$
\boxed{F_\psi=\frac34+\frac14|\langle\psi|Z|\psi\rangle|^2
=\frac34+\frac14(|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2)^2
=1-|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2.}
$$
Thus **the maximum is $1$**, attained by $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ up to a <global phase>, because these are <eigenstates> of the <Pauli Z gate>. **The minimum is $3/4$**, attained by every equal-weight <quantum superposition>
$$
|\psi\rangle=\frac{|0\rangle+e^{i\theta}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad \theta\in\mathbb R,
$$
on the equator of the <Bloch sphere>. The <unsquared quantum fidelity> is $\sqrt{F_\psi}$ if that alternative convention is used. No average over different input <quantum states> is needed for the requested state-dependent answer.

== 8
{scope}

The three printed unlettered blocks are identified below by navigation labels 1, 2 and 3. Their mark allocations are 10, 10 and 5 respectively.

=== 1
{scope}

==== Solution

A <universal quantum gate set> can implement any <unitary operator> on any finite number of <qubits>, exactly when arbitrary continuous gate parameters are allowed, or to arbitrary accuracy with a suitable finite gate alphabet. More precisely, for every target $V$ and $\epsilon>0$ there is a finite <quantum circuit> $W$ with $\min_\gamma\|W-e^{i\gamma}V\|<\epsilon$ in <operator norm>. This ignores only an overall <global phase> of the complete operation. Universality lets a fixed collection of controllable elementary operations run arbitrary <quantum algorithms> rather than requiring a different physical interaction for each algorithm.

All single-<qubit> <unitary gates>, together with the <controlled-NOT gate> between selectable pairs, form an exact <universal quantum gate set>. One way to see sufficiency is to eliminate entries of a finite <unitary matrix> by two-level rotations between <computational basis> vectors; these rotations decompose into controlled single-<qubit> rotations, which in turn decompose into single-<qubit> gates and <controlled-NOT gates>. Single-<qubit> gates alone preserve <product states> and cannot supply general <quantum entanglement>. A finite approximate universal example is the <Hadamard gate>, the <T gate> $T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/4})$, and the <controlled-NOT gate>; finite exact words over a finite alphabet cannot represent every continuously parameterized <unitary operator> exactly.

Distinguish the <Hamiltonian operator> $H_{\rm int}$ from the <Hadamard gate> $H_{\rm Had}$. For the printed <collisional phase gate>,
$$
H_{\rm int}=\Omega|01\rangle\langle01|.
$$
As written, $\Omega$ has units of <energy>. The <Schrödinger equation> gives
$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{initial computational basis state}&\text{state after time }t\\\hline
|00\rangle&|00\rangle\\
|01\rangle&e^{-i\Omega t/\hbar}|01\rangle\\
|10\rangle&|10\rangle\\
|11\rangle&|11\rangle
\end{array}
$$
because only $|01\rangle$ has a nonzero <energy eigenvalue>. In the ordered <computational basis> $(00,01,10,11)$, define
$$
\boxed{U_\phi=e^{-iH_{\rm int}\tau/\hbar}
=I+(e^{-i\phi}-1)|01\rangle\langle01|
=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-i\phi},1,1),\qquad \phi=\frac{\Omega\tau}{\hbar}.}
$$
If an interaction strength is instead quoted as an <angular frequency> $\Omega_{\rm f}$, the <Hamiltonian operator> is $\hbar\Omega_{\rm f}|01\rangle\langle01|$ and $\phi=\Omega_{\rm f}\tau$. An alternative definition with $e^{+i\phi}$ would have $\phi=-\Omega\tau/\hbar$. The minus sign above follows the usual <time-evolution operator> convention. At $\phi=\pi$ these conventions give the same $U_\pi$.

For the <quantum circuit>, use $X$ on the first <qubit> to move the selective minus sign from $01$ to $11$:
$$
(X\otimes I)U_\pi(X\otimes I)=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)=\mathrm{CZ}.
$$
Conjugating the target of this <controlled-Z gate> by the <Hadamard gate> gives the required <controlled-NOT gate>:
$$
\boxed{\mathrm{CNOT}_{1\to2}
=(I\otimes H_{\rm Had})(X\otimes I)U_\pi(X\otimes I)(I\otimes H_{\rm Had})
=(X\otimes H_{\rm Had})U_\pi(X\otimes H_{\rm Had}).}
$$
The rightmost <matrix> acts first. Thus the network applies $X_1,H_2$, then $U_\pi$, then $X_1,H_2$, and maps $|a,b\rangle$ to $|a,b\oplus a\rangle$, where $\oplus$ is <exclusive or>.

=== 2
{scope}

==== Solution

Define the <Hadamard basis> $|\pm\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and write $s=e^{-i\phi}$. After $U_\phi$ the two-<qubit> <quantum state> is
$$
U_\phi|++\rangle=\frac{|00\rangle+s|01\rangle+|10\rangle+|11\rangle}{2}.
$$
Applying the <Hadamard gate> to the first <qubit> gives the normalized <quantum state>
$$
|\chi_2(\phi)\rangle
=\frac{2|00\rangle+(1+s)|01\rangle+(s-1)|11\rangle}{2\sqrt2}.
$$
For $\phi=n\pi$, $s=(-1)^n$, so
$$
\boxed{|\chi_2(n\pi)\rangle=
\begin{cases}
|0\rangle|+\rangle,&n\text{ even},\\
(|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt2=|\phi^-\rangle,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
$$
The first is a <product state>. The second is exactly the minus <Bell state> in the printed <phase gate> convention; its <reduced density matrices> are $I/2$.

For three <qubits>, let $U_{12}$ use the ordered pair $(1,2)$ and $U_{23}$ the ordered pair $(2,3)$. The two diagonal <quantum gates> commute. For $\phi=n\pi$ their action before the final <Hadamard gates> is
$$
U_{23}U_{12}|+++\rangle
=\frac1{\sqrt8}\sum_{a,b,c=0}^1(-1)^{n[(1-a)b+(1-b)c]}|abc\rangle.
$$
If $n$ is even this is $|+++\rangle$, and $H_1H_3$ gives $|0\rangle|+\rangle|0\rangle$. If $n$ is odd, group the <quantum superposition> by the middle <qubit>:
$$
U_{23}U_{12}|+++\rangle
=\frac{|+\rangle_1|0\rangle_2|-\rangle_3
-|-\rangle_1|1\rangle_2|+\rangle_3}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Since $H_{\rm Had}|+\rangle=|0\rangle$ and $H_{\rm Had}|-\rangle=|1\rangle$, the final states are
$$
\boxed{|\chi_3(n\pi)\rangle=
\begin{cases}
|0\rangle|+\rangle|0\rangle,&n\text{ even},\\
\displaystyle|G\rangle=\frac{|001\rangle-|110\rangle}{\sqrt2},&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
$$
The odd case is a <GHZ state>: each individual <qubit> has <reduced density matrix> $I/2$, and it is related to $(|000\rangle+|111\rangle)/\sqrt2$ by the local <Pauli gates> $Z_1X_3$. Conversely, applying those gates converts $|G\rangle$ to that standard form. They have not been applied in the printed network, so the actual output used in the next part is $|G\rangle=(|001\rangle-|110\rangle)/\sqrt2$.

=== 3
{scope}

==== Solution

Keep the actual odd-$n$ <GHZ state> from the preceding calculation. Resolving its first <qubit> in the <Hadamard basis> gives
$$
|G\rangle=\frac{|+\rangle_1|\psi^-\rangle_{23}
+|-\rangle_1|\psi^+\rangle_{23}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|\psi^\pm\rangle_{23}=\frac{|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Thus the $+$ outcome leaves $|\psi^-\rangle_{23}$ and the $-$ outcome leaves $|\psi^+\rangle_{23}$, each with <probability> $1/2$. Conditional on a known result the remaining <qubits> share a maximally <entangled state>.

When the result is not revealed, the appropriate <density matrix> is the <probability>-weighted mixture from a <nonselective quantum measurement>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_{23}
&=\frac12|\psi^-\rangle\langle\psi^-|+\frac12|\psi^+\rangle\langle\psi^+|\\
&=\frac12|01\rangle\langle01|+\frac12|10\rangle\langle10|\\
&=\frac12\bigl(|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|\bigr)
+\frac12\bigl(|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|\bigr)\\
&=\operatorname{diag}(0,1/2,1/2,0).
\end{aligned}
$$
The cross terms cancel. The displayed convex combination of <product states> proves directly that this is a <separable quantum state>. It still has perfect <computational-basis measurement> anticorrelation, so it is not itself a <product state>. It also equals the <partial trace> $\operatorname{Tr}_1|G\rangle\langle G|$, as required by the <no-communication theorem>: a local <quantum measurement> with an unobserved outcome cannot change the remote <reduced density matrix>.
