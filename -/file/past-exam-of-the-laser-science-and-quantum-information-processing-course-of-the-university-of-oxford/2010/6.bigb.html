== 1
{scope}
{title2=7 marks}

=== Solution

Write $a=a_1a_2$ and $x=x_1x_2$, with the top wire carrying $x_1$, the middle wire $x_2$, and the bottom wire the <ancilla bit> $y$. The <binary inner product> is $a\cdot x=a_1x_1\oplus a_2x_2$, where $\oplus$ is <exclusive or>. Let $C_{j\to y}$ denote a <CNOT gate> controlled by wire $j$ and targeting $y$. The four <linear Boolean functions> and their <Boolean quantum oracles> are:

|| $a$
|| $f_a(x)$
|| <Reversible circuit> for $U_a$
| $00$
| $0$
| $I$: three unchanged wires
| $01$
| $x_2$
| $C_{2\to y}$
| $10$
| $x_1$
| $C_{1\to y}$
| $11$
| $x_1\oplus x_2$
| $C_{2\to y}C_{1\to y}$

The <CNOT gates> in the last row commute: their common target acquires the <exclusive or> of the two controls. Each input wire is preserved, and applying any of these <reversible circuits> twice restores $y$.

\Image[6-circuits.png]
{title=The four two-bit linear oracles and their exact simplifications after Hadamard gates on all three wires; a filled dot is a control and a circled plus is a target}

For classical <reversible computation>, call the unknown <function oracle> on $|10\rangle|0\rangle$ and $|01\rangle|0\rangle$. The resulting <ancilla bits> are respectively $a_1$ and $a_2$:
$$
U_a|10\rangle|0\rangle=|10\rangle|a_1\rangle,
\qquad U_a|01\rangle|0\rangle=|01\rangle|a_2\rangle.
$$
Use a fresh zero <ancilla bit> for each call, or preserve the first result in workspace before restoring the target to zero. Thus **two classical calls determine the two-bit string**.

For $n$ input <bits>, query the <standard basis> strings $e_j$, since $a\cdot e_j=a_j$. This gives an upper bound of $n$ calls. It is also optimal for exact identification: every classical query reveals at most one new <bit>, since $x$ is unchanged and $y\oplus f_a(x)$ reveals just $f_a(x)$ when $y$ is known. A depth-$k$ <decision tree> has at most $2^k$ answer histories, but must distinguish $2^n$ possible hidden strings. Hence $k\ge n$, including adaptive strategies. The <classical query complexity of linear Boolean function identification> is therefore
$$
\boxed{D(n)=n\quad(n\ge1).}
$$

== 2
{scope}
{title2=10 marks}

=== Solution

Use the <Hadamard basis> states $|\pm\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The <Hadamard gate> satisfies $H|0\rangle=|+\rangle$, $H|1\rangle=|-\rangle$, and $H^2=I$. After the first three <Hadamard gates>, the full <quantum state> is
$$
|\psi_1\rangle=|+\rangle|+\rangle|-\rangle
=\frac12\bigl(|00\rangle+|01\rangle+|10\rangle+|11\rangle\bigr)|-\rangle.
$$
The <Pauli X gate> obeys $X|-\rangle=-|-\rangle$. Consequently the <Boolean quantum oracle> produces <phase kickback>:
$$
U_a|x\rangle|-\rangle=(-1)^{a\cdot x}|x\rangle|-\rangle.
$$
Factoring out the <ancilla qubit>, the four states immediately after the <function oracle> are
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\psi_{2,00}\rangle&=\tfrac12(|00\rangle+|01\rangle+|10\rangle+|11\rangle)|-\rangle=|++\rangle|-\rangle,\\
|\psi_{2,01}\rangle&=\tfrac12(|00\rangle-|01\rangle+|10\rangle-|11\rangle)|-\rangle=|+-\rangle|-\rangle,\\
|\psi_{2,10}\rangle&=\tfrac12(|00\rangle+|01\rangle-|10\rangle-|11\rangle)|-\rangle=|-+\rangle|-\rangle,\\
|\psi_{2,11}\rangle&=\tfrac12(|00\rangle-|01\rangle-|10\rangle+|11\rangle)|-\rangle=|--\rangle|-\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final <Hadamard gates> send $|+\rangle$ to $|0\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$ to $|1\rangle$. In particular, the printed <quantum circuit> includes a final <Hadamard gate> on the <ancilla qubit> too. Its full outputs, ordered as $x_1,x_2,y$, are
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
a&|\psi_{\mathrm{out}}\rangle\\\hline
00&|001\rangle\\
01&|011\rangle\\
10&|101\rangle\\
11&|111\rangle
\end{array}}
$$
Thus **one oracle call identifies $a$ with certainty**: a <computational-basis measurement> of the first two <qubits> returns $a_1a_2$. The <ancilla qubit> returns to $|1\rangle$ and carries no additional information about $a$. The <Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm> turns the pattern of relative <quantum phases> into a definite <computational-basis state>.

== 3
{scope}
{title2=8 marks}

=== Solution

The required identity is <CNOT control reversal in the Hadamard basis>. To derive it using standard <quantum circuit> identities, let $\mathrm{CZ}$ be the <controlled-Z gate>, which multiplies $|11\rangle$ by $-1$ and fixes the other <computational-basis states>. Since $HXH=Z$,
$$
C_{1\to2}=(I\otimes H)\,\mathrm{CZ}\,(I\otimes H).
$$
The <controlled-Z gate> is symmetric in its two wires. Combining this symmetry with $H^2=I$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
(H\otimes H)C_{1\to2}(H\otimes H)
&=(H\otimes I)\,\mathrm{CZ}\,(H\otimes I)\\
&=\boxed{C_{2\to1}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is an **exact operator identity**, including its <global phase>, and applies to arbitrary inputs. One can also check it directly from the <orthogonal projectors> $P_b=|b\rangle\langle b|$: conjugating $C_{1\to2}=P_0\otimes I+P_1\otimes X$ by $H\otimes H$ gives
$$
\frac{I+X}{2}\otimes I+\frac{I-X}{2}\otimes Z
=I\otimes P_0+X\otimes P_1=C_{2\to1}.
$$

For the three-wire <quantum circuit>, put $W=H^{\otimes3}$. Conjugation distributes over products because $W^2=I$: $WABW=(WAW)(WBW)$. Each <CNOT gate> in $U_a$ therefore reverses direction, while the <Hadamard gates> on untouched wires cancel. The <Hadamard-conjugated linear oracle> is
$$
\boxed{WU_aW=C_{y\to2}^{\,a_2}C_{y\to1}^{\,a_1}.}
$$
These are the simplified <quantum circuits> in the figure: for $a=00$ there are no gates; for $a=01$ there is $C_{y\to2}$; for $a=10$ there is $C_{y\to1}$; and for $a=11$ there are both. The <ancilla qubit> becomes their common control. On every <computational-basis state>, the entire simplified circuit acts as
$$
|x_1x_2\rangle|b\rangle
\longmapsto
|x_1\oplus ba_1,\ x_2\oplus ba_2\rangle|b\rangle.
$$
Starting the data <qubits> at $|00\rangle$, the prescribed <computational basis> preparation of the <ancilla qubit> must therefore be **$|1\rangle$**: it activates the required flips and gives $|a\rangle|1\rangle$. Starting it at $|0\rangle$ instead gives $|00\rangle|0\rangle$ for every $a$, so no information is obtained.

More generally, an initial <ancilla qubit> state $\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle$ in this same fixed <quantum circuit> gives
$$
\alpha|00\rangle|0\rangle+\beta|a\rangle|1\rangle.
$$
For nonzero $a$, the desired data output has <probability> $|\beta|^2$, and exact identification for every $a$ requires $\alpha=0$. This condition refers to the displayed <ancilla preparation for the Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm>: the state entering the <function oracle> must be $|-\rangle$, up to <global phase>. Other <quantum state preparation> procedures that deliver this same state are equivalent; $|1\rangle$ is the prescribed input before the first <Hadamard gate>.

For arbitrary $n$, <linearity> of the <linear Boolean function> supplies
$$
U_a=\prod_{j=1}^{n}C_{j\to y}^{\,a_j},
\qquad
H^{\otimes(n+1)}U_aH^{\otimes(n+1)}
=\prod_{j=1}^{n}C_{y\to j}^{\,a_j}.
$$
This decomposition is for analysis: the <Bernstein-Vazirani algorithm> calls the unknown <function oracle> as one supplied operation. The common control starts at $1$, so the simplified <quantum circuit> takes $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}|1\rangle$ to $|a\rangle|1\rangle$. Equivalently, <phase kickback> gives a product state
$$
\frac1{\sqrt{2^n}}\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^n}(-1)^{a\cdot x}|x\rangle
=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}\frac{|0\rangle+(-1)^{a_j}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},
$$
which the final <Hadamard transform> decodes to $|a\rangle$. Thus **one quantum oracle call suffices for every $n\ge1$**, compared with $n$ classical calls. This counts <function oracle> uses; the surrounding <Hadamard gates> and final <computational-basis measurement> still act on $n+1$ and $n$ <qubits>, respectively.
