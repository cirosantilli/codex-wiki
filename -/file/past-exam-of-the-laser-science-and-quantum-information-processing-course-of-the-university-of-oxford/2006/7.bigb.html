== 1
{scope}

=== Solution

In <superdense coding>, Alice applies a local <Pauli operator> to her half of the shared <Bell state>. Choose a phase bit $u$ and a parity bit $v$, and encode them using $X^vZ^u$ on Alice's qubit. With the standard definitions of the <Bell states>,
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
uv&\text{Alice's operator}&\text{Bell state, up to global phase}\\\hline
00&I&\Phi^+=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\\
01&X&\Psi^+=(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt2\\
10&Z&\Phi^-=(|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\\
11&XZ&\Psi^-=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2
\end{array}.
$$
Alice sends her <qubit> to Bob. He now holds both halves and performs a <Bell-basis measurement>, recovering $uv$ without error. Equivalently, a <CNOT gate> with Alice's qubit as control, followed by a <Hadamard gate> on that qubit, maps these states to their computational labels up to <global phases>. Four distinguishable alternatives give **two classical bits per transmitted qubit**, with one shared entangled pair consumed per use.

The initial entanglement distribution is independent of the later message and can be completed beforehand. Bob's local <reduced density matrix> before Alice's transmission is $I/2$ for every encoding, so <quantum no-signalling> prevents it from carrying her choice. This does not make the entangled pair free: if a qubit transmission is used to distribute it, counting that transmission as well gives two qubit transmissions for the two-bit message. The quoted dense-coding rate counts the subsequent message-bearing transmission with entanglement already shared.

== 2
{scope}

=== Solution

A realized message $x$ has <self-information> $I(x)=-\log_2p_x$. The average information per source message is its <Shannon entropy>,
$$
\boxed{H(M)=-p_A\log_2p_A-p_B\log_2p_B-p_C\log_2p_C.}
$$
The average reaches **$\log_2 3\simeq1.58496$ bits per message** at $p_A=p_B=p_C=1/3$. Individual rare realizations can have larger self-information; this bound concerns the average.

The given strings form a <prefix code> with lengths $\ell_A=1$ and $\ell_B=\ell_C=2$. To attain the entropy bound without redundancy, the resulting long bit stream must carry one bit of information per bit: unbiased bits with no predictable dependence. For an independent source, the <dyadic source matched to a complete prefix code> has
$$
\boxed{p_A=2^{-1}=\frac12,\qquad p_B=p_C=2^{-2}=\frac14.}
$$
These probabilities make the encoded stream equivalent to parsing independent fair bits using the code tree. Its <expected codeword length> and source entropy agree:
$$
\boxed{\overline\ell=H(M)=\frac12\cdot1+\frac14\cdot2+\frac14\cdot2=\frac32\ \text{bits per message}.}
$$
Here “optimal” means attaining the Shannon bound. If it means only an <optimal prefix code> among symbol-by-symbol binary prefix codes, the probabilities are not unique: for three positive-probability symbols, this tree is optimal whenever $p_A$ is a largest probability. <Huffman coding> need not attain entropy exactly for nondyadic probabilities.

Concatenating the specified source-message encodings gives
$$
AABCACBA\ \longmapsto\ 0\,|\,0\,|\,10\,|\,11\,|\,0\,|\,11\,|\,10\,|\,0
=001011011100.
$$
Pack consecutive bits in pairs, rather than allocating a fresh Bell pair to each variable-length source word:
$$
00\,|\,10\,|\,11\,|\,01\,|\,11\,|\,00.
$$
Under the mapping in part 1, transmit
$$
\boxed{\Phi^+,\ \Phi^-,\ \Psi^-,\ \Psi^+,\ \Psi^-,\ \Phi^+.}
$$
The block uses six message-bearing qubit transmissions and six preshared <Bell pairs>. Source-message boundaries are recovered by parsing the decoded prefix bit stream.

== 3
{scope}

=== Solution

The actual resource is a <two-component Bell resource in dense coding>. Applying the same local encoder $X^vZ^u$ to
$$
|\chi\rangle=\frac{2|\Phi^+\rangle+|\Phi^-\rangle}{\sqrt5}
$$
gives a superposition of the intended <Bell state> with amplitude magnitude $2/\sqrt5$ and its phase-label partner with amplitude magnitude $1/\sqrt5$. A <Bell-basis measurement> therefore reports **the wrong Bell state with probability $1/5$**, independently of $uv$. With our labeling, only the phase bit $u$ flips:
$$
(\widehat u,\widehat v)=(u\oplus N,v),\qquad
P(N=1)=\frac15.
$$
The first bit of each pair passes through a <binary symmetric channel> with error $1/5$; the second bit is correct. A uniformly chosen bit from the full stream consequently has
$$
\boxed{P_{\rm bit\ error}=\frac12\cdot\frac15=\frac1{10}.}
$$

This bit error probability **depends on the Bell labeling**. The <Bell-label Hamming distance and bit errors> rule multiplies the wrong-symbol probability by the number of bit positions changed. For example, relabel $\Phi^+,\Phi^-,\Psi^+,\Psi^-$ as $00,11,01,10$. Both wrong-state partner pairs then differ in two bits, giving bit error probability **$1/5$** instead of $1/10$. The physical wrong-Bell-state probability remains $1/5$.

For the entropy-matched independent source in part 2, the transmitted bit stream is fair, and consecutive two-bit labels are uniform. Write the <binary entropy> as $h_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)$. If a bit position is chosen uniformly and its phase/parity position is forgotten, the pooled corresponding-bit pair is a <binary symmetric channel> with error $1/10$. Its <mutual information> is
$$
\boxed{I(B;\widehat B)=1-h_2(1/10)\simeq0.531004\ \text{bits}.}
$$

The <bit-position information in a binary channel> distinguishes this pooled quantity from the information in the decoded blocks. Alice and Bob know which bit is first in each Bell label. Conditioned on that position $J$, the first bit gives $1-h_2(1/5)$ bits and the second gives one bit. Thus
$$
\boxed{I(B;\widehat B\mid J)=1-\frac12h_2(1/5)\simeq0.639036\ \text{bits per bit}.}
$$
Equivalently, the joint <mutual information> between the two-bit input and output labels is **$2-h_2(1/5)\simeq1.278072$ bits per transmitted Bell label** for the prescribed Bell measurement. Pooling without the position loses usable side information; it is not interchangeable with this block rate. The stated fair-input values use the optimal source distribution above; biased source bits require their actual joint distribution.
