== 1
{scope}

=== Solution

For one input <bit>, a <Boolean function> is specified by the pair $(f(0),f(1))$, and $k=f(0)+f(1)$ counts its <satisfying assignments>. In the ordered <computational basis> $(|0\rangle,|1\rangle)$, the supplied <phase oracle> convention gives
$$
U_f|x\rangle=(-1)^{f(x)\oplus1}|x\rangle,\qquad
U_f=\operatorname{diag}\bigl(2f(0)-1,2f(1)-1\bigr).
$$
Here $\oplus$ is <exclusive or>: **the oracle changes the sign of the inputs with $f(x)=0$**. With $I$ the <identity matrix> and $Z$ the <Pauli Z gate>, all four cases are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
f(x)&(f(0),f(1))&k&U_f\\\hline
0&(0,0)&0&\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}=-I\\[3pt]
x&(0,1)&1&\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=-Z\\[3pt]
1-x&(1,0)&1&\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}=Z\\[3pt]
1&(1,1)&2&\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=I
\end{array}
$$
The two middle cases are <balanced Boolean functions>; the others are <constant functions>. The specified zero-state sign change and the <Hadamard gate> are
$$
\boxed{U_0=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=-Z,\qquad
H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.}
$$

Use the top <qubit> as the first <tensor product> factor. Write the <controlled unitary gate> as $C(V)=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I+|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes V$. Reading the bracketed <quantum circuit> from left to right, its four operations are $C(U_f)$, $C(H)$, $C(U_0)$, $C(H)$. Thus the target operation in its control-one branch is
$$
G=H U_0 H U_f,
\qquad C(G)=C(H)C(U_0)C(H)C(U_f),
$$
where the rightmost <matrix> acts first. The control-zero branch is the <identity operator>. The <Hadamard conjugation of a controlled gate> identity gives
$$
\boxed{C(H)C(U_0)C(H)=(I\otimes H)C(U_0)(I\otimes H).}
$$
Indeed, on control zero the replacement applies $H I H=I$ to the target, while on control one both sides apply $H U_0 H$. By <linearity>, the identity holds on every <quantum superposition> and therefore in every repetition of the bracket. This replaces the two internal controlled <Hadamard gates> as a pair.

The <global phase of a controlled quantum gate> must be preserved: $C(-I)=Z\otimes I$, whereas $C(I)=I\otimes I$. Consequently the minus signs in $U_0=-Z$ and the special <phase oracles> $U_f=-I,-Z$ cannot be discarded as an unobservable <global phase> of the complete <quantum circuit>.

== 2
{scope}

=== Solution

Define the <Hadamard basis> states $|\pm\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The initial two <Hadamard gates> prepare $|+\rangle|+\rangle$. For one bracket, the two control branches give
$$
|+\rangle|+\rangle\ \longmapsto
\frac{|0\rangle|+\rangle+|1\rangle G|+\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Using the <Pauli X gate> $X=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, <unitary conjugation> by the <Hadamard gate> gives $H U_0 H=-X$, so $G=-XU_f$. Keeping every sign, the target states along the control-one branch, in the exact gate order, are
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c|c}
f&U_f|+\rangle&H U_f|+\rangle&U_0 H U_f|+\rangle&G|+\rangle&H G|+\rangle\\\hline
0&-|+\rangle&-|0\rangle&|0\rangle&|+\rangle&|0\rangle\\
x&-| -\rangle&-|1\rangle&-|1\rangle&-| -\rangle&-|1\rangle\\
1-x&| -\rangle&|1\rangle&|1\rangle&| -\rangle&|1\rangle\\
1&|+\rangle&|0\rangle&-|0\rangle&-|+\rangle&-|0\rangle
\end{array}
$$
The last column also includes the final target <Hadamard gate>, which lies outside the bracket. Writing $|\chi_f\rangle=HG|+\rangle$, applying the final top <Hadamard gate> gives the exact normalized two-<qubit> <quantum state>
$$
|\Psi_f\rangle=\frac12\left[|0\rangle\bigl(|0\rangle+|\chi_f\rangle\bigr)+|1\rangle\bigl(|0\rangle-|\chi_f\rangle\bigr)\right].
$$
Thus the complete outputs, in top-then-bottom <computational basis> order, are
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
f&|\Psi_f\rangle&\langle Z\otimes I\rangle\\\hline
0&|00\rangle&+1\\[2pt]
x&\tfrac12\bigl(|00\rangle-|01\rangle+|10\rangle+|11\rangle\bigr)&0\\[2pt]
1-x&\tfrac12\bigl(|00\rangle+|01\rangle+|10\rangle-|11\rangle\bigr)&0\\[2pt]
1&|10\rangle&-1
\end{array}}
$$
For the first <qubit>, the <Pauli matrix> $\sigma_z=Z$ has <expectation value> $P(0)-P(1)$ by the <Born rule>. The two $k=1$ cases each have $P(0)=P(1)=1/2$, while the $k=0$ and $k=2$ cases are deterministic. Equivalently, this <Hadamard test> measures
$$
\langle Z\otimes I\rangle=\operatorname{Re}\langle+|G|+\rangle=1-k
\qquad(r=1,\ n=1).
$$
This explains the <approximate quantum counting> interpretation: repeated preparations estimate the <expectation value>, and hence $k$. A single top-<qubit> <computational-basis measurement> need not identify $k$.

== 3
{scope}

=== Solution

For $r$ brackets the control-zero branch still does nothing, and the control-one branch applies $G^r$ to the target. Immediately before the final two <Hadamard gates>, the <quantum state> is
$$
\frac{|0\rangle|+\rangle+|1\rangle G^r|+\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
For the <constant function> with $k=0$, the <phase oracle> is $U_f=-I$, so $G=(-X)(-I)=X$. Since $|+\rangle$ is an <eigenstate> of the <Pauli X gate> with <eigenvalue> $+1$, $G^r|+\rangle=|+\rangle$ for every integer $r$. The final two <Hadamard gates> therefore give
$$
\boxed{|\Psi_{k=0}(r)\rangle=|00\rangle,\qquad \langle Z\otimes I\rangle=+1.}
$$
In particular, the first <qubit> always ends in $|0\rangle$.

For the <constant function> with $k=2$, $U_f=I$ and $G=-X$, so $G^r|+\rangle=(-1)^r|+\rangle$. Thus the target remains in $|+\rangle$, and the top <qubit> before its final <Hadamard gate> is $(|0\rangle+(-1)^r|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. The sign is a <relative quantum phase> between the control branches. The <Hadamard gate> converts it into the <integer parity> of $r$:
$$
\boxed{|\Psi_{k=2}(r)\rangle=|r\bmod2\rangle\otimes|0\rangle,\qquad
\langle Z\otimes I\rangle=(-1)^r.}
$$
The first <qubit> is therefore $|0\rangle$ for even $r$ and $|1\rangle$ for odd $r$, including all requested $r>1$.
