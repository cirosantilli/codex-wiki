== 1
{scope}

=== Solution

Normalize the supplied <Bell state> as $|\Psi^+\rangle=(|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt2$, with $|V\rangle=|0\rangle$ and $|H\rangle=|1\rangle$. For a <measurement of linear photon polarization>, the two orthogonal analyzer states can be taken as
$$
|\phi,+\rangle=\cos\phi|0\rangle+\sin\phi|1\rangle,\qquad
|\phi,-\rangle=-\sin\phi|0\rangle+\cos\phi|1\rangle.
$$
Assigning outcomes $+1,-1$ gives the <observable>
$$
M(\phi)=|\phi,+\rangle\langle\phi,+|-|\phi,-\rangle\langle\phi,-|
=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\phi&\sin2\phi\\\sin2\phi&-\cos2\phi\end{pmatrix}
=\cos2\phi\,Z+\sin2\phi\,X,
$$
where $X,Z$ are the <Pauli operators>. The angle of the <Bloch vector> is twice the physical analyzer angle. Hence
$$
\boxed{A_1=Z,\quad A_2=X,\quad A_3=\frac{Z+X}{\sqrt2};\qquad
B_1=Z,\quad B_2=\frac{Z-X}{\sqrt2},\quad B_3=\frac{Z+X}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
Each has <eigenvalues> $\pm1$ and square equal to the <identity operator>.

== 2
{scope}

=== Solution

Use an <Ekert protocol>. Alice and Bob choose their analyzer settings independently and randomly for each received pair, record the outcomes, and confirm reception before announcing settings over an <authenticated public classical channel>. The authentication prevents an adversary from impersonating either party; it does not conceal the setting announcements. Announcing settings after reception also avoids the <premature basis announcement in quantum key distribution> attack.

The <polarization correlations of the Psi-plus Bell state> satisfy
$$
E_{ij}=\langle\Psi^+|A_i\otimes B_j|\Psi^+\rangle,
\qquad E(\alpha,\beta)=-\cos 2(\alpha+\beta).
$$
Indeed, $\langle Z\otimes Z\rangle=-1$, $\langle X\otimes X\rangle=1$, and the cross terms vanish. In the specified setting order,
$$
(E_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2\\
0&-1/\sqrt2&1/\sqrt2\\
-1/\sqrt2&-1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For <key sifting>, retain the setting pairs **$(i,j)=(1,1)$ and $(3,2)$**. Their outcomes are perfectly anticorrelated. Alice assigns bit $0$ to outcome $+1$ and bit $1$ to outcome $-1$; Bob uses the opposite assignment. Their <raw keys> then agree. With uniform independent choices of the three settings, these pairs supply a raw-key fraction **$2/9$**, before sacrificing a test sample. They must keep the remaining raw-key outcomes secret. The four pairs $(1,2),(1,3),(2,2),(2,3)$ supply Bell-test data, and the other three setting pairs can be discarded.

Reveal the outcomes of those Bell-test rounds and estimate
$$
E_{ij}=\frac{N_{++}+N_{--}-N_{+-}-N_{-+}}{N_{++}+N_{--}+N_{+-}+N_{-+}},
\qquad
\boxed{S=-E_{12}-E_{13}-E_{22}+E_{23}.}
$$
The ideal entries give **$S=2\sqrt2$**. The <CHSH inequality> gives $|S|\le2$ for <local hidden-variable theories>: for predetermined outcomes $a_1,a_2,b_2,b_3\in\{\pm1\}$, the signed combination is $-a_1(b_2+b_3)-a_2(b_2-b_3)$. One bracket is zero and the other is $\pm2$, so averaging cannot exceed two in magnitude. The <Tsirelson bound> limits quantum correlations to $2\sqrt2$.

For the specified, trusted <Pauli operators>, the <maximal CHSH witness for a fixed Bell state> makes the ideal security statement precise. Its operator is
$$
\mathcal B=-A_1\otimes(B_2+B_3)-A_2\otimes(B_2-B_3)
=\sqrt2(X\otimes X-Z\otimes Z).
$$
Its largest <eigenvalue> $2\sqrt2$ has the unique <eigenvector> $|\Psi^+\rangle$. Exact attainment therefore fixes the shared <density matrix> to this <pure state>. Since <purity decouples a subsystem from its purification>, Eve cannot hold information correlated with that pair. This rules out information-gaining eavesdropping in the ideal model, rather than proving that no observer exists. More generally, <CHSH self-testing> identifies the entangled qubit pair up to local changes of representation under its own assumptions.

A <quantum measurement> that reads Eve's chosen polarization generally disturbs correlations in an incompatible basis. The <no-cloning theorem> also prevents copying arbitrary unknown photon polarizations and retaining a perfect copy while forwarding an unchanged original. The Bell test checks several incompatible settings, so observing perfect correlation in only one key basis is insufficient.

Alice and Bob also reveal a randomly chosen sample of their raw keys to estimate the <quantum bit error rate>, then discard that sample. <Information reconciliation> corrects discrepancies, and <privacy amplification> removes residual information, including leakage from the public transcript. With finite data they need statistical bounds and a security analysis appropriate to their devices and noise. A measured violation $S>2$ alone does not assert zero information for Eve; the exact ideal argument above uses the maximal value.<Quantum cryptography based on Bell's theorem by Artur K. == 3
{scope}

=== Solution

Eve's <computational-basis intercept-resend attack> applies a <projective measurement> of $Z$ to Bob's photon and discards her outcome from Alice and Bob's description. Their resulting <density matrix> is
$$
\rho_{AB}^{\rm Eve}=\frac12|01\rangle\langle01|+\frac12|10\rangle\langle10|.
$$
This <separable state> keeps $\langle Z\otimes Z\rangle=-1$ but removes $\langle X\otimes X\rangle$. Hence
$$
E_{\rm Eve}(\alpha,\beta)=-\cos2\alpha\cos2\beta,
\qquad
(E_{ij}^{\rm Eve})=\begin{pmatrix}
-1&-1/\sqrt2&-1/\sqrt2\\
0&0&0\\
-1/\sqrt2&-1/2&-1/2
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Using the same signed Bell-test statistic gives
$$
\boxed{S_{\rm Eve}=\sqrt2,\qquad S_{\rm ideal}=2\sqrt2.}
$$
The ideal value is halved, and the attack no longer violates the <CHSH inequality>.

The key data show why several settings matter. For $(1,1)$, Eve's measurement leaves perfect anticorrelation, so this subset has **zero bit errors** after Bob flips his assignment. For $(3,2)$, $E=-1/2$, so correct anticorrelation has probability $(1-E)/2=3/4$: its <quantum bit error rate> is **$1/4$**. If both key-setting pairs occur equally often, their combined raw-key error rate is **$1/8$**. The Bell-test change detects this attack even though the vertical/horizontal key subset alone would not.
