= Cranial kinesis
{parent=Skull}

Movement between parts of the <skull> beyond ordinary lower-jaw rotation. <joint> surfaces, soft tissues, <muscles> and constraints elsewhere in the <skull> jointly determine realized movement.

= Palate
{parent=Skull}

The roof of the mouth and its supporting <anatomy>. In avian comparisons, contacts among the <vomer>, <palatine bone>, <pterygoid bone> and braincase are important for <cranial kinesis>.

= Neognathous palate
{parent=Palate}

= Palaeognathous palate
{parent=Palate}

An avian palatal arrangement with extensive bracing among the <vomer>, <pterygoid bone>, <palatine bone> and <skull> base. It is associated with <Palaeognathae>; the name does not establish evolutionary primitiveness.

= Jaw

= Mastication
{parent=Anatomy}

Mechanical processing of food by repeated jaw and tooth movements. It changes food particle size and surface area before subsequent digestion.

= Orthogrady
{parent=Anatomy}

An upright orientation of the trunk. It is not synonymous with suspension, knuckle-walking or habitual terrestrial <bipedalism>.

= Bipedalism
{parent=Anatomy}

Locomotion using two limbs for support and propulsion. Occasional bipedal movement must be distinguished from habitual or obligate bipedal locomotion.

= Knuckle-walking
{parent=Anatomy}

Locomotion with weight supported on the dorsal surfaces of flexed fingers. Similar external support posture need not imply identical wrist mechanics or a unique evolutionary origin.

= Hair
{parent=Anatomy}

= Incisor

= Molar

= Tribospheny
{parent=Tooth}

= Tribosphenic molar
{synonym}

An occlusal construction combining shearing and crushing, with the lower molar talonid basin interacting with an upper cusp. Similar functional geometry does not alone settle whether all examples share one evolutionary origin.

= Dental mesowear
{parent=Tooth}

Assessment of macroscopic wear-related cusp shape and relief. It integrates a different temporal scale of food processing from <dental microwear>.

= Dental microwear
{parent=Tooth}

Small wear features on tooth surfaces used to infer food processing near the end of the wear record. Diet, external grit, preparation and post-mortem damage must be distinguished.

= Hypselodonty
{parent=Tooth}

Continued growth of a tooth, distinguished from a tall but finite reserve crown in <hypsodonty>.

= Hypsodonty
{parent=Tooth}

A high-crowned tooth construction supplying a reserve against wear. It differs from <hypselodonty>, and is not by itself proof of a grass diet.

= Selenodonty
{parent=Tooth}

A dental construction with crescentic ridges, characteristic of many <ruminants>.

= Lophodonty
{parent=Tooth}

A dental construction in which cusps are connected into ridges. Ridge arrangement and wear influence the cutting and grinding performance of the surface.

= Carnassial
{parent=Tooth}

A specialized shearing tooth. In <Carnivora> the principal opposing pair is the upper fourth premolar and lower first molar; other predatory lineages can specialize different teeth.

= Diphyodonty
{parent=Tooth}

A replacement pattern with two successive generations for the replaced tooth classes. It should not be taken to mean every tooth in the jaw is replaced exactly once.

= Dental occlusion
{parent=Tooth}

The relationship and contact of opposing <teeth> during jaw closure and chewing. Precise correspondence of opposing surfaces permits specialized cutting and grinding.

= Insect cuticle
{parent=Anatomy}

A protective extracellular surface layer. Embryonic cuticle patterns in insects can report developmental identities but require complementary molecular readouts.
