The <superficial degree of divergence> of this <scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex> is one. Its <ultraviolet pole> is consequently a local expression at most linear in the external <four-momenta>. The allowed <counterterm Lagrangian>, with the charge-conjugation and Hermiticity properties of <scalar electrodynamics>, contains a single one-derivative <scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex>:
$$
V_{\mathrm{ct}}^\mu(k,k')=-ie\delta_1(k+k')^\mu.
$$
Thus the coefficient $\delta_1$ is independent of the chosen external <four-momenta>. Setting $k'=0$ leaves $-ie\delta_1k^\mu$, so a nonzero generic $k$ still determines that coefficient. It does not determine the full finite <scattering amplitude> at other <four-momenta>.

Keep $m\ne0$ and generic <off shell> $k$ while extracting the <ultraviolet pole>. These choices avoid spurious <infrared divergences>; setting all scales to zero would instead produce <scaleless integrals> that can conceal the <ultraviolet pole>.

The integral printed in the text can be evaluated without reconstructing Figure 1. Put $A=l^2-m^2+i0$ and $B=(l+k)^2-m^2+i0$, and let $I^\mu$ denote that integral without its factor $\xi$. The numerator contraction responsible for its <covariant gauge> dependence is
$$
l_\alpha\left(g^{\alpha\beta}-(1-\xi)\frac{l^\alpha l^\beta}{l^2}\right)(l+2k)_\beta
=\xi(l^2+2l\cdot k).
$$
With the <scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex> $-ie(p+p')^\mu$, three such vertices, two <scalar propagators> and one <photon propagator> have overall factor $(-ie)^3i^2(-i)=-e^3$. Thus the supplied integrand gives $V_{\mathrm{supplied}}^\mu=-e^3\xi I^\mu$.

First isolate the numerator term $l^2$. Its contribution is
$$
I_0^\mu=\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{(2l+k)^\mu}{AB}.
$$
Under $l\mapsto-l-k$ the two denominators exchange and the numerator changes sign. A shift-invariant <dimensional regularization> therefore gives $\boxed{I_0^\mu=0}$, including the finite part.

For the remaining numerator $2(l\cdot k)(2l+k)^\mu$, only its term $4l^\mu(l\cdot k)$ contributes to the <ultraviolet pole>. Corrections from expanding the denominators, and the numerator $2k^\mu(l\cdot k)$, are <ultraviolet finite> by <power counting>. Use <auxiliary-mass extraction of ultraviolet poles> with any $M>0$ rather than literally integrating a massless <scaleless integral>. <Lorentz covariance> then gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
I^\mu\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}
&=\frac4d k_\nu g^{\mu\nu}\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac1{(l^2-M^2+i0)^2}\bigg|_{\mathrm{UV}}\\
&=iC_\epsilon k^\mu,\\
\boxed{V_{\mathrm{supplied}}^\mu\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}}&=\boxed{-ie^3\xi C_\epsilon k^\mu}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The complete <renormalization constant> can nevertheless be obtained independently. Here is a short <scalar QED wave-function pole> calculation, to avoid assuming either missing panel's value. For an external <four-momentum> $p$, the exchange contribution to the <scalar self-energy> has numerator
$$
N=(2p-l)^2-(1-\xi)\frac{[(2p-l)\cdot l]^2}{l^2}
=4p^2-4\xi p\cdot l+\xi l^2-4(1-\xi)\frac{(p\cdot l)^2}{l^2}.
$$
Its denominator is $l^2[(p-l)^2-m^2+i0]$. The necessary <ultraviolet poles>, with the measure $d^dl/(2\pi)^d$, are respectively
$$
\int\frac1{l^2D}\bigg|_{\mathrm{UV}}=iC_\epsilon,\quad
\int\frac{l^\mu}{l^2D}\bigg|_{\mathrm{UV}}=\frac{p^\mu}2iC_\epsilon,\quad
\int\frac1D\bigg|_{\mathrm{UV}}=m^2iC_\epsilon,\quad
\int\frac{l^\mu l^\nu}{(l^2)^2D}\bigg|_{\mathrm{UV}}=\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}4iC_\epsilon,
\qquad D=(p-l)^2-m^2+i0.
$$
The first two follow by a <Feynman parameter> and a shift of $l$; the third is a <tadpole integral>; the last follows by the same large-$l$ extraction used above. The <seagull vertex> contributes only a <photon> <tadpole integral>, which is a <scaleless integral>, and in any case cannot affect the coefficient of $p^2$. Multiplication by the exchange graph's factor $-e^2$ gives the <scalar self-energy> insertion
$$
-i e^2 C_\epsilon[(3-\xi)p^2+\xi m^2].
$$
The kinetic <counterterm> $i\delta_2p^2$ therefore requires $\delta_2=e^2(3-\xi)C_\epsilon$. The <scalar QED Ward identity> sets $\delta_1=\delta_2$, so
$$
\boxed{Z_1=1+\frac{e^2(3-\xi)}{16\pi^2\epsilon}+O(e^4).}
$$
Indeed the full unrenormalized <scalar electrodynamics three-point vertex> has <ultraviolet pole> $ie^3(3-\xi)C_\epsilon k^\mu$, which is cancelled by $-ie\delta_1k^\mu$. Subtracting the supplied contribution gives the gauge-independent sum stated in part (ii), without assigning either unreadable panel an integrand.

Let $\Sigma=m_e^2+m_t^2$, $Q_e=-1$, $Q_t=2/3$ and $N_c=3$. The single <tree-level Feynman diagram> is annihilation through a <virtual photon>. Up to an irrelevant common phase, its <scattering amplitude> is
$$
\mathcal M_\gamma=\frac{e^2Q_eQ_t}{s}\,[\bar v(p_2)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)]\,[\bar u(p_3)\gamma_\mu v(p_4)]\,\delta_{cd}.
$$
There are two initial <spin angular momentum> states for each particle, so the <spin average> is $1/4$. The final <color charge> sum gives $\sum_{c,d}|\delta_{cd}|^2=N_c$. There is no additional <photon polarization> average for a <virtual photon> and no separate polarization label beyond the <Dirac spinor> <spin sums>.

The <spin sums> and the <gamma-matrix trace> identities give
$$
\begin{aligned}
L^{\mu\nu}&=\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_2-m_e)\gamma^\mu(\not p_1+m_e)\gamma^\nu]\\
&=4[p_2^\mu p_1^\nu+p_2^\nu p_1^\mu-\tfrac{s}{2}g^{\mu\nu}],\\
H_{\mu\nu}&=\operatorname{tr}[(\not p_3+m_t)\gamma_\mu(\not p_4-m_t)\gamma_\nu]\\
&=4[p_{3\mu}p_{4\nu}+p_{3\nu}p_{4\mu}-\tfrac{s}{2}g_{\mu\nu}].
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $p_1\cdot p_2+m_e^2=p_3\cdot p_4+m_t^2=s/2$. Contracting gives
$$
\frac14L^{\mu\nu}H_{\mu\nu}
=8[(p_1\cdot p_3)(p_2\cdot p_4)+(p_1\cdot p_4)(p_2\cdot p_3)]+4s\Sigma.
$$
Since $2p_1\cdot p_3=2p_2\cdot p_4=\Sigma-t$ and $2p_1\cdot p_4=2p_2\cdot p_3=\Sigma-u$, this is the <massive vector-current annihilation kernel>
$$
2[(\Sigma-t)^2+(\Sigma-u)^2+2s\Sigma]
=2[t^2+u^2+4s\Sigma-2\Sigma^2].
$$
Thus the absolutely normalized result is
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M_\gamma|^2}
=\frac{2N_ce^4Q_e^2Q_t^2}{s^2}[t^2+u^2+4s\Sigma-2\Sigma^2]
=\frac{8e^4}{3s^2}[t^2+u^2+4s\Sigma-2\Sigma^2].}
$$
After taking out the displayed prefactor, $\boxed{A=1,\ B=4,\ C=-2}$. Because the source uses a proportionality sign, only their ratio is fixed until that prefactor is specified. Equivalently, after taking out just $e^4/s^2$, their values are $8/3,32/3,-16/3$. No mass has been neglected. At $m_e=0$ and $s=4m_t^2$, the bracket yields the finite isotropic threshold value of the <scattering amplitude>; the vanishing threshold <scattering cross-section> comes from <relativistic two-body phase space>.

The exchanged particle is now a <gluon>, and the incoming <up quark> mass replaces $m_e$. Define
$$
\Sigma_u=m_u^2+m_t^2,\qquad F_u=t^2+u^2+4s\Sigma_u-2\Sigma_u^2.
$$
The <Dirac trace> and <spin average> are unchanged. Normalize the fundamental <gauge generators> by $\operatorname{tr}(T^AT^B)=\delta^{AB}/2$. Averaging over the $N_c^2$ initial <color charge> choices and summing the final <color charge> choices gives
$$
C_g=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{A,B}\operatorname{tr}(T^AT^B)\operatorname{tr}(T^AT^B)
=\frac{N_c^2-1}{4N_c^2}=\frac29.
$$
Replace the factor $N_ce^4Q_e^2Q_t^2$ from part (ii) by $g_s^4C_g$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M_g|^2}=\frac{4g_s^4}{9s^2}F_u.}
$$
Keeping $m_u$ is allowed; if the usual negligible <up quark> mass approximation is wanted, simply set $m_u=0$. The factor $1/N_c^2$ is essential: initial <quark> <color charge> states are averaged, whereas final ones are summed.

There are two <tree-level Feynman diagrams>, with intermediate <photon> and <gluon> respectively. Their <color charge> tensors are $\delta_{ji}\delta_{kl}$ and $\sum_AT^A_{ji}T^A_{kl}$. The pure <photon> factor is
$$
C_\gamma=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{i,j,k,l}|\delta_{ji}\delta_{kl}|^2=1.
$$
Their interference vanishes after the <color charge> sum because of <photon-gluon colour orthogonality>:
$$
C_{\gamma g}=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_A\operatorname{tr}(T^A)\operatorname{tr}(T^A)=0.
$$
Thus there is no term proportional to $e^2g_s^2$, and with the $F_u$ of part (iii),
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M_\gamma+\mathcal M_g|^2}
=\frac{2F_u}{s^2}\left[e^4Q_u^2Q_t^2+\frac29g_s^4\right]
=\frac{F_u}{s^2}\left[\frac{32}{81}e^4+\frac49g_s^4\right].}
$$
Here $Q_u=Q_t=2/3$. The absence of interference concerns the specified averaged and summed result; a fixed choice of external <color charge> need not have zero interference.

= Solution

There are exactly four <tree-level Feynman diagrams>: one four-<photon> contact graph and one exchange graph for each of the three partitions $12|34$, $13|24$ and $14|23$ of the external legs. The exchange graphs contain two cubic <Feynman vertices>. A <fermion loop> or a closed <Faddeev-Popov ghost> line has positive <loop order>, so neither enters here.

\Image[2021-nonlinear-gauge-photon-scattering.png]
{title=The contact graph and three exchange graphs for physical photon scattering in the nonlinear gauge; all external momenta are incoming}

Write $p_1+p_2+p_3+p_4=0$ and take physical <photon polarization vectors> $\varepsilon_i$ with $p_i\cdot\varepsilon_i=0$ and $p_i^2=0$. At the cubic <Feynman vertex> attached to legs $i,j$, the two terms containing $p_i\cdot\varepsilon_i$ or $p_j\cdot\varepsilon_j$ vanish. If $q=p_i+p_j$ flows into the internal line, the remaining factor at that end is
$$
-2\lambda q^\mu(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j).
$$
At the other end the internal incoming <four-momentum> is $q$ rather than $-q$, giving $+2\lambda q^\nu(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l)$. Multiplication by the <photon propagator> gives
$$
i\mathcal M_{ij|kl}=(-2\lambda q^\mu)\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2}(2\lambda q^\nu)
(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j)(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l)
=4i\lambda^2(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j)(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l).
$$
The cancellation holds first at generic nonzero channel <Mandelstam invariants> and then by continuation at degenerate kinematics. Summing the three exchange graphs produces $4i\lambda^2P$, where
$$
P=(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_2)(\varepsilon_3\cdot\varepsilon_4)
+(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_3)(\varepsilon_2\cdot\varepsilon_4)
+(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_4)(\varepsilon_2\cdot\varepsilon_3).
$$
The quartic <Feynman vertex> contributes $-4i\lambda^2P$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma}^{\mathrm{tree}}=0.}
$$
This is a cancellation for physical <on shell> <photon polarization vectors>. The <off shell> four-<photon> correlation function need not vanish. Physical <light-by-light scattering> first occurs through charged-particle <loop Feynman diagrams>.

Put $r^2=\Phi^T\Phi$. The potential has its minimum at
$$
\frac{dV}{dr}=-m^2r+4\lambda r^3=0,\qquad \boxed{v^2=\frac{m^2}{4\lambda}}.
$$
Choose the <vacuum expectation value> $\Phi_0=(0,0,v)^T$. Since the <weak hypercharge> is zero, $Q=T^3$ on this <real scalar triplet>, and $T^3\Phi_0=0$. Neither $T^1$ nor $T^2$ annihilates $\Phi_0$. The <stabilizer subgroup> is consequently
$$
\boxed{SU(2)\times U(1)_Y\longrightarrow U(1)_{T^3}\times U(1)_Y.}
$$
In particular, <spontaneous symmetry breaking> has not reduced the two neutral <gauge generators> to a single one: $Y$ annihilates every component and remains unbroken independently of $T^3$.

The <scalar mass matrix> is
$$
\left.\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial\phi_i\partial\phi_j}\right|_{\Phi_0}
=(-m^2+4\lambda v^2)\delta_{ij}+8\lambda(\Phi_0)_i(\Phi_0)_j
=8\lambda v^2\delta_{i3}\delta_{j3}.
$$
With canonical kinetic normalization $\tfrac12(\partial h)^2$, the radial <Higgs boson> therefore has
$$
\boxed{m_h^2=8\lambda v^2=2m^2,\qquad m_h=\sqrt2\,m.}
$$
The two tangential zero <eigenvalues> are <Goldstone modes>, absorbed by the two massive <gauge bosons> through the <Higgs mechanism>.

Let $g$ and $g'$ be the two <gauge couplings>. In <unitary gauge>, $\Phi=(0,0,v+h)^T$ and the <weak hypercharge> term in the <gauge covariant derivative> vanishes. Using $(T^a)_{bc}=-i\epsilon_{abc}$, the kinetic term becomes
$$
\frac12(D_\mu\Phi)^T(D^\mu\Phi)
=\frac12(\partial h)^2+\frac12g^2(v+h)^2[W_\mu^1W^{1\mu}+W_\mu^2W^{2\mu}].
$$
Thus, in the basis $(W^1,W^2,W^3,B)$, the <gauge-boson mass matrix> and its <eigenvalues> are
$$
\boxed{M_V^2=g^2v^2\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0),\qquad
m_{W^\pm}=gv=\frac{gm}{2\sqrt\lambda},\quad m_{W^3}=m_B=0.}
$$
Here $W^\pm=(W^1\mp iW^2)/\sqrt2$. The two massless neutral <gauge bosons> may be rotated to any orthonormal basis; no neutral mass term selects a unique mixing angle.

Expanding the same kinetic term to first order in $h$ gives
$$
\mathcal L_{hVV}=g^2vh[(W^1)^2+(W^2)^2]=2g^2vhW^+_\mu W^{-\mu}.
$$
Functional differentiation gives the <Feynman rules>
$$
\boxed{hW^+_\mu W^-_\nu:\quad 2ig^2v\,g_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{2im_W^2}{v}g_{\mu\nu},}
$$
and, in the real basis, $hW^a_\mu W^b_\nu:2ig^2v\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}$ for $a,b\in\{1,2\}$. All such three-point <Feynman vertices> involving $W^3$ or $B$ vanish. The factor two in the real basis comes from the two identical <gauge boson> fields; in the charged basis it is already explicit in $\mathcal L_{hVV}$.

This cannot be the sole symmetry-breaking sector of the <Standard Model>. Its <gauge-boson mass matrix> has two zero <eigenvalues>, so besides the electromagnetic <photon> it predicts an extra massless neutral <gauge boson>. Every linear combination of $W^3$ and $B$, including the combination normally called $Z$, is massless. The observed massive <Z boson> is a concrete contradiction. Choosing $g,g',m,\lambda$ differently cannot change these two zero <eigenvalues>.

A <real scalar triplet> can occur as an additional sector, but a further <vacuum expectation value>, for example that of a <Higgs doublet>, is then necessary to obtain the observed neutral <gauge boson> mass pattern. This does not rescue the triplet-only model posed here.

Normalize the <generating functional> by its value with all sources zero, and take the connected part of the <time-ordered correlation function>. Source differentiation of the interaction exponential is equivalent to the <Dyson series> followed by <Wick's theorem>. Write $D(x-y)=\langle T\phi(x)\phi(y)\rangle_0$ and $F_{ij}(x-y)=\langle T\psi_i(x)\bar\psi_j(y)\rangle_0$ temporarily, so that every contraction has an unambiguous meaning.

The second-order <Dyson series> contributes
$$
\frac{(ig)^2}{2!}\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
\langle T\phi(x_a)\phi(y_a)\phi(x)\phi(y)
[\bar\psi_i(x)\psi_i(x)][\bar\psi_j(y)\psi_j(y)]\rangle_{0,c}.
$$
There are two connected external <scalar field> attachments:
$$
D(x_a-x)D(y_a-y)+D(x_a-y)D(y_a-x).
$$
They become equal after exchanging the integration variables $x,y$, cancelling $2!$. The connected <fermion> contractions have the <closed fermion loop sign>:
$$
\langle T[\bar\psi_i(x)\psi_i(x)][\bar\psi_j(y)\psi_j(y)]\rangle_{0,c}
=-F_{ij}(x-y)F_{ji}(y-x)
=-\operatorname{tr}[F(x-y)F(y-x)].
$$
The minus sign can also be checked directly: the pairing of the first with the fourth field and the second with the third has positive pairing parity, but $\langle T\bar\psi_i(x)\psi_j(y)\rangle=-F_{ji}(y-x)$. Multiplying it by $(ig)^2=-g^2$ leaves a positive result:
$$
G_c^{(2)}(x_a,y_a)=g^2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
D(x_a-x)D(y_a-y)\operatorname{tr}[F(x-y)F(y-x)].
$$
A contraction of the two external fields with each other times a <vacuum bubble> cancels against the normalization. Separate <fermion> <tadpole integrals> at $x$ and $y$ belong to a product of one-point functions, removed by taking the connected part.

To match the source's <generating functional> convention, distinguish its displayed kernels from contractions. The positive $iJ\Delta J/2$ exponent gives $D=-i\Delta$. With the source insertion order in equation (5), the positive $i\bar\eta S_F\eta$ exponent similarly gives $F=-iS_F$. The two <scalar propagators> and two <Dirac propagators> supply a total phase $(-i)^4=1$. Thus, writing $S=S_F$ as in the requested formula,
$$
\boxed{G_c^{(2)}(x_a,y_a)=g^2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
\Delta(x_a-x)\Delta(y_a-y)\operatorname{tr}[S(x-y)S(y-x)].}
$$
The <matrix trace> closes the <spinor> indices; no extra factor of two or additional <symmetry factor> remains for a <Dirac field> with this <Yukawa interaction>.

For commuting <real scalar fields>, remove the <closed fermion loop sign> and replace the <Dirac propagators> by <scalar propagators>. There are no <spinor> indices, hence no <matrix trace>. With two independent <real scalar fields> $\chi_1,\chi_2$ and interaction $g\phi\chi_1\chi_2$, the same normalization gives
$$
\boxed{G_{c,\chi_1\chi_2}^{(2)}=-g^2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,
D(x_a-x)D(y_a-y)G_1(x-y)G_2(y-x).}
$$
The overall sign has reversed relative to part (i) because $(ig)^2$ is now unaccompanied by a <closed fermion loop sign>. The same formula holds in the source's kernel convention, since there are again four <propagators>.

There is an additional convention to specify if the two symbols are intended to denote the *same* <real scalar field>. For the literal interaction $g\phi\chi^2$, there are two identical connected <Wick contractions> between $\chi^2(x)$ and $\chi^2(y)$, so the coefficient becomes $-2g^2$. If the conventional interaction is instead $(g/2)\phi\chi^2$, each vertex is $ig$ and the loop has <symmetry factor> $1/2$, giving coefficient $-g^2/2$. In both interpretations the <closed fermion loop sign> and <spinor> <matrix trace> disappear; the differing numerical factor comes from the interaction normalization and field identity.
