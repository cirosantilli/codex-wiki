= Solution

{c}

For a <Dirac field>, the <path integral> is a <Berezin integral> over independent <Grassmann fields> $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$:
$$
\boxed{Z=\mathcal N\int\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\,
\exp\left[i\int d^4x\,\bar\psi(i\not\partial-m)\psi\right].}
$$
The vacuum <boundary conditions> and the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> are understood; $\mathcal N$ fixes the vacuum normalization. This is the vacuum <generating functional> without sources, rather than a finite-temperature <partition function>.

The <Dirac adjoint> transforms as $\bar\psi\mapsto\bar\psi e^{-i\alpha}$. For constant $\alpha$, $\partial_\mu(e^{i\alpha}\psi)=e^{i\alpha}\partial_\mu\psi$. Both the kinetic term and the <Dirac mass term> therefore retain their values. This proves the <global phase symmetry>, whose <group> is the <unitary group> $U(1)$.

For the local variation $\delta\psi=i\alpha(x)\psi$, $\delta\bar\psi=-i\alpha(x)\bar\psi$, the terms proportional to $\alpha$ cancel and the differentiated phase gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\delta\mathcal L
&=i(-i\alpha\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi
+i\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu(i\alpha\psi)
-m\,\delta(\bar\psi\psi)\\
&=-\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\,\partial_\mu\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, for a compactly supported $\alpha$, <integration by parts> gives
$$
\boxed{j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi,\qquad
\delta S=-\int d^4x\,j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha
=\int d^4x\,\alpha\partial_\mu j^\mu.}
$$
This <Dirac current> is the <Noether current> in the convention $\delta S=-\int j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$. Using the convention with a plus sign reverses the <Noether current> and the <conserved charge> together. There is one independent <global symmetry in field theory>, with four spacetime components of its <Noether current>. The associated <conserved charge> is $Q=\int d^3x\,j^0$, with vacuum subtraction when required.

An <operator equation in a path integral> $\mathcal O(x)=0$ means that insertion of $\mathcal O(x)$ into every <time-ordered correlation function> vanishes when $x$ is separated from the other insertion points. At coincident points, <Ward identity contact terms> can remain; an <operator equation> does not assert that these distributions vanish.

Let $X$ be a product of local insertions. Make the local phase change of integration variables from part <1/b>. The <functional measure> has unit <functional Jacobian>: the opposite phases of $\psi$ and $\bar\psi$ cancel in a vector-symmetry-preserving <regularization in quantum field theory>. There is no vector <gauge anomaly>. Invariance under a change of integration variables gives
$$
0=\langle\delta_\alpha X\rangle
+i\int d^4x\,\alpha(x)\langle T\{\partial_\mu j^\mu(x)X\}\rangle.
$$
If $X=\prod_r\mathcal O_r(x_r)$ and $\delta_\alpha\mathcal O_r=iq_r\alpha(x_r)\mathcal O_r$, comparison of the coefficients of the arbitrary $\alpha(x)$ yields the <Ward-Takahashi identity>
$$
\boxed{\langle T\{\partial_\mu j^\mu(x)X\}\rangle
=-\sum_rq_r\delta^{(4)}(x-x_r)\langle TX\rangle.}
$$
Consequently $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ is an <operator equation> at separated points. This is stronger than just $\langle\partial_\mu j^\mu\rangle=0$. For example, $X=\psi(y)\bar\psi(z)$ has $q_y=1$, $q_z=-1$, so its two <Ward identity contact terms> are $[-\delta^{(4)}(x-y)+\delta^{(4)}(x-z)]\langle T\psi(y)\bar\psi(z)\rangle$; dropping them would give a false identity. Equivalently, the <Dirac equation> and the <adjoint Dirac equation> give $\partial_\mu(\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi)=0$, with the same coincident-point qualification in <time-ordered correlation functions>.

Couple a nondynamical <background gauge field> $B_\mu$ to the <Dirac current>, choosing a plus sign for the source term:
$$
\boxed{Z[B]=\mathcal N\int\mathcal D\bar\psi\,\mathcal D\psi\,
\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\,[\bar\psi(i\not\partial-m)\psi+B_\mu\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi]\right\}.}
$$
There is no integration over $B_\mu$. The <gauge covariant derivative> is $\partial_\mu-iB_\mu$, so the simultaneous transformations are
$$
\psi'=e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi,\qquad
\bar\psi'=\bar\psi e^{-i\alpha(x)},\qquad
B'_\mu=B_\mu+\partial_\mu\alpha.
$$
The kinetic term changes by $-j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$ and the source term changes by $+j^\mu\partial_\mu\alpha$. The <action> is therefore invariant, exactly for finite $\alpha$ as well as infinitesimally. Changing integration variables and using the invariant <functional measure> from part <1/c> proves the <background gauge invariance of a current generating functional>:
$$
\boxed{Z[B+\partial\alpha]=Z[B].}
$$
The allowed transformations preserve the vacuum <boundary conditions>. Its infinitesimal version is $\partial_\mu\delta Z/\delta B_\mu=0$. Because $B_\mu$ is only a source, this statement needs no <gauge fixing> or photon kinetic term. The absence of a vector <gauge anomaly> is essential to the change-of-variables argument.

Collect the two <Dirac fields> into $\Psi=(\psi_1,\psi_2)^T$, with a common nonzero <mass> $m$. Then
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=\sum_{i=1}^2\bar\psi_i(i\not\partial-m)\psi_i
=\bar\Psi(i\not\partial-m\mathbf1_2)\Psi.}
$$
Among complex-linear rotations of the <Dirac fields>, the <internal symmetry of a classical field theory> is $\Psi\mapsto U\Psi$, $U\in U(2)$, with $\bar\Psi\mapsto\bar\Psi U^\dagger$. Unitarity preserves the kinetic term, and the <mass matrix> commutes with every $U$. This <unitary flavour symmetry of degenerate Dirac fields> has <group> $(SU(2)\times U(1))/\mathbb Z_2$, where the quotient avoids counting the common central element twice.

Choose the four <Hermitian matrices> $T^0=\mathbf1_2/2$, $T^a=\sigma^a/2$ for $a=1,2,3$, with $\sigma^a$ the <Pauli matrices>. Promoting the four parameters to functions gives $\delta S=-\int d^4x\,(\partial_\mu\alpha^A)\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu T^A\Psi$. The four <Noether currents> are therefore
$$
\boxed{j_A^\mu=\bar\Psi\gamma^\mu T^A\Psi,\qquad A=0,1,2,3.}
$$
Their divergences vanish by the equal-mass <Dirac equations>. The singlet is half the total <Dirac current>; the other three currents rotate the two species.

**Free-theory qualification.** If “global internal symmetry” allows mixing the <Dirac fields> with <charge-conjugate fields>, the completely free theory written here has a larger <enhanced internal symmetry of free Dirac fields>. Write $\psi_i=(\lambda_{2i-1}+i\lambda_{2i})/\sqrt2$ using four <Majorana fermions>. Up to a <total derivative>,
$$
\mathcal L=\frac12\sum_{A=1}^4\bar\lambda_A(i\not\partial-m)\lambda_A.
$$
The full <orthogonal group> $O(4)$ acts on the four real species; its connected part is $SO(4)$. Its six <Noether currents> can be chosen as $J_{AB}^\mu=i\bar\lambda_A\gamma^\mu\lambda_B$ for $A<B$, up to the common sign convention. The antisymmetry of this <Majorana fermion> vector bilinear makes these the six independent rotations. A concrete rotation outside the stated complex-linear $U(2)$ is $\lambda_2\mapsto\cos\theta\lambda_2+\sin\theta\lambda_3$, $\lambda_3\mapsto-\sin\theta\lambda_2+\cos\theta\lambda_3$: it preserves the displayed <Lagrangian density> while mixing $\psi_i$ with their <charge-conjugate fields>. The usual $U(2)$ answer specifies the subgroup preserving the chosen common <fermion number>.

In the <Weyl representation of the gamma matrices>, write each <Dirac field> as $\psi_i=(\ell_{i\alpha},r_i^{\dot\alpha})^T$, where $\ell_i$ and $r_i$ have opposite <chirality>. With the <Weyl sigma matrices> $\sigma^\mu=(\mathbf1,\boldsymbol\sigma)$ and $\bar\sigma^\mu=(\mathbf1,-\boldsymbol\sigma)$,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=\sum_{i=1}^2\left[
i\ell_i^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu\ell_i
+ir_i^\dagger\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu r_i
-m(\ell_i^\dagger r_i+r_i^\dagger\ell_i)\right].}
$$
The <Dirac mass term> couples the two <Weyl fields>. Under $\ell\mapsto U_L\ell$, $r\mapsto U_Rr$, its first term acquires $U_L^\dagger U_R$, so a common nonzero <mass> requires $U_L=U_R$. At zero <mass> there is no such constraint:
$$
\boxed{G_{\rm number\ preserving}=U(2)_L\times U(2)_R.}
$$
This is the <chiral flavour symmetry of massless Dirac fields>. Its eight <Noether currents> are $j_{L,A}^\mu=\ell^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu T^A\ell$ and $j_{R,A}^\mu=r^\dagger\sigma^\mu T^Ar$. The sum $j_R+j_L$ is the <vector current>, and $j_R-j_L$ is the <axial current>, for each flavour generator. This statement concerns the free theory in flat spacetime; an interacting theory can have a <chiral anomaly>.

For completeness, allowing the extra transformations described in part <1/e> gives four left-handed <Weyl fields>, $q=(\ell_1,\ell_2,r_1^c,r_2^c)^T$, where $r_i^c$ is the left-handed <charge-conjugate field> of $r_i$. After <integration by parts> their kinetic term is $iq^\dagger\bar\sigma^\mu\partial_\mu q$, whose maximal linear <unitary group> is $U(4)$. The symmetric <mass matrix> for equal <Dirac masses> is
$$
M=m\begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf1_2\\\mathbf1_2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathcal L_m=-\frac12(q^TMq+\text{Hermitian conjugate}),
$$
with the antisymmetric spinor contraction implicit. A unitary change of basis makes $M=m\mathbf1_4$; preserving both the kinetic term and $M$ requires $V^\dagger V=V^TV=\mathbf1_4$, hence real $V\in O(4)$. Thus the unrestricted free-theory enhancement is $U(4)$ at zero <mass> and $O(4)$ at equal nonzero <mass>; the familiar $U(2)_L\times U(2)_R$ and diagonal $U(2)$ are their common-<fermion number>-preserving subgroups.

Use the source's <charge-conjugation matrix> convention $C\gamma^\mu C^{-1}=-(\gamma^\mu)^T$. It implies
$$
C^{-1}(\gamma^\mu)^TC=-\gamma^\mu,\qquad
C^{-1}K(p)^TC=K(-p).
$$
A <trace> equals the <trace> of its <matrix transpose>, which reverses the order of all factors. Inserting the displayed identities gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_A(\ell)&=\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu K(\ell+k_1)\gamma^\nu K(\ell-k_3)\gamma^\rho K(\ell)]\\
&=-\operatorname{tr}[K(-\ell)\gamma^\rho K(-\ell+k_3)\gamma^\nu K(-\ell-k_1)\gamma^\mu]\\
&=-\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu K(-\ell)\gamma^\rho K(-\ell+k_3)\gamma^\nu K(-\ell-k_1)].
\end{aligned}
$$
There is one minus sign from each of the three photon <interaction vertices>, and none from the transformed <Dirac propagators>. In the last line, change <loop momentum> to $\ell'=-\ell-k_1$. Since $k_1+k_3=-k_2$, its three <Dirac propagators> become $K(\ell'+k_1)$, $K(\ell'-k_2)$, $K(\ell')$, exactly those of the reversed orientation. Hence
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_A^{\mu\nu\rho}=-\mathcal M_B^{\mu\nu\rho},\qquad
\mathcal M_{\rm one\ loop}^{\mu\nu\rho}=0.}
$$
This is <Furry's theorem>. The <regularization in quantum field theory> matters: the individual <Feynman integrals> are superficially divergent, so an unregulated shift of a divergent integral is not a proof. A common <dimensional regularization> preserves <charge conjugation> and the vector <Ward identity>, making the change of variables legitimate. No <chirality matrix> occurs, so the axial triangle <anomaly (physics)> is not involved.

For an $n$-vertex <fermion loop>, reversing the <fermion flow> reverses the ordered product inside its <trace>. The same <charge-conjugation matrix> calculation transforms every <Dirac propagator> by $p\mapsto-p$ and contributes $-1$ for each vector photon <interaction vertex>. Relabel the <loop momentum> so that the external labels are fixed. The reversed <Feynman diagram> therefore obeys
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\rm reversed}=(-1)^n\mathcal M_{\rm original}.}
$$
For odd $n\geq3$, the $(n-1)!$ cyclic orders pair into opposite orientations and cancel. For $n=1$, the single photon tadpole maps into itself with a minus sign and is zero. The common <closed fermion loop sign> is present on both sides and does not supply the factor $(-1)^n$. This proves <Furry's theorem> for all the requested <one-loop Feynman diagrams>. The <charge conjugation> argument also gives vanishing odd-photon <correlation functions> to all orders in vacuum <QED>, provided no <charge-conjugation symmetry> breaking background or charged external state is introduced.

At large <loop momentum>, each <Dirac propagator> scales as $\ell^{-1}$. One four-dimensional loop and four internal <Dirac propagators> therefore give the <superficial degree of divergence>
$$
D=4-4=0.
$$
Thus a possible <ultraviolet divergence> in the sum is logarithmic. By locality its divergent coefficient is a degree-zero polynomial in the external <four-momenta>, namely a constant Lorentz tensor,
$$
\mathcal M_{\rm div}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
=\frac1\epsilon\left(a\,g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma}
+b\,g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}
+c\,g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}\right).
$$
The photon <Ward identities> apply to the complete sum for arbitrary external <four-momenta>, including <off-shell> ones: $k_{1\mu}\mathcal M^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0$, and likewise for the other legs. A constant tensor with $k_{1\mu}\mathcal M_{\rm div}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0$ for every $k_1$ must be zero. Hence $a=b=c=0$ and
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M_{\rm one\ loop}^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\ \text{is ultraviolet finite}.}
$$
Equivalently, a logarithmic divergence would require a local dimension-four $A^4$ <counterterm>, forbidden by Abelian <gauge invariance>. A gauge-invariant four-photon <local operator> starts with four <gauge field strengths>, for example $(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^2$, of <mass dimension> eight; it cannot be this <power-counting> divergence. Individual boxes need not be finite; their forbidden divergent parts cancel in the sum. There are no divergent proper loop subgraphs at this <loop order>. With the massive <Dirac field> in the source, there is also no soft or collinear <infrared divergence> at generic external kinematics. The argument establishes ultraviolet finiteness, and does not claim the absence of physical threshold singularities.

Choose $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-igA_\mu^at^a$ on the <Dirac field>, $t^a=\sigma^a/2$ in the <fundamental representation> of <SU(2)>, and $[t^a,t^b]=i\epsilon^{abc}t^c$. Define the <gauge field strength> and the <adjoint representation> <gauge covariant derivative> by
$$
F_{\mu\nu}^a=\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a
+g\epsilon^{abc}A_\mu^bA_\nu^c,\qquad
(D_\mu c)^a=\partial_\mu c^a+g\epsilon^{abc}A_\mu^bc^c.
$$
The required <gauge-fixed Yang-Mills Lagrangian in a covariant gauge>, including the matter term, is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}^aF^{a\mu\nu}
+\bar\psi(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\psi
-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial^\mu A_\mu^a)^2
-\bar c^a\partial^\mu(D_\mu c)^a.}
$$
Here $c^a$ and $\bar c^a$ are independent <Grassmann fields> in the <adjoint representation>: the <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields> and <antighost fields>. Under an infinitesimal <gauge transformation>, $\delta A_\mu^a=(D_\mu\omega)^a$; hence the <Lorenz gauge> function $G^a=\partial^\mu A_\mu^a$ varies by $\partial^\mu D_\mu^{ab}\omega^b$. Its <Faddeev-Popov determinant> is represented by the ghost term, with an irrelevant field-independent determinant sign absorbed into normalization. <Integration by parts> gives $\mathcal L_{\rm gh}=(\partial^\mu\bar c^a)(D_\mu c)^a$, explicitly showing the <ghost-gluon vertex>.

There is no scalar vacuum expectation value here, so <R-xi gauge> reduces to the linear <covariant gauge> displayed above, with no <Goldstone boson> term. At $\xi=1$ the <Feynman gauge> <gauge boson> <propagator> is $-i\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu}/(k^2+i0)$. With Fourier convention $e^{-ikx}$ and all three boson <four-momenta> incoming, this choice gives exactly the source's $+g\epsilon^{abc}$ three-boson <Feynman rule>, and the matter <interaction vertex> is $+ig\gamma^\mu t^a$. The sign of $D_\mu$ has been explicitly chosen here; the QED convention in question 2 instead has vertex $-ie\gamma^\mu$.

For the outgoing massless <gauge bosons>, take $k_1^2=k_2^2=0$. Work away from internal poles, with the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> understood. The <Dirac equations> and <four-momentum conservation> give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar v\not k_2&=\bar v(\not r_t-m),\\
\not k_2u&=-(\not r_u-m)u.
\end{aligned}
$$
Multiplying by the neighboring <Dirac propagator> numerator cancels its denominator. The two fermion-exchange diagrams therefore yield the <matrix commutator>
$$
\begin{aligned}
k_{2\nu}(\mathcal M_t+\mathcal M_u)^{ab\mu\nu}
&=g^2\bar v\gamma^\mu(t^at^b-t^bt^a)u\\
&=ig^2\epsilon^{abc}J_c^\mu,\qquad
J_c^\lambda=\bar v(p_2)\gamma^\lambda t^cu(p_1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The annihilation <vector current> obeys $q_\lambda J_c^\lambda=\bar v(\not p_1+\not p_2)t^cu=0$. Direct contraction of the three-boson tensor gives
$$
k_{2\nu}V^{\lambda\mu\nu}
=s g^{\lambda\mu}-q^\lambda k_2^\mu-k_2^\lambda k_1^\mu.
$$
Thus
$$
k_{2\nu}\mathcal M_s^{ab\mu\nu}
=-ig^2\epsilon^{abc}\left(J_c^\mu-\frac{J_c\cdot k_2}{s}k_1^\mu\right).
$$
The terms proportional to $J_c^\mu$ cancel, leaving the <non-Abelian Ward identity for fermion annihilation>
$$
\boxed{k_{2\nu}\mathcal M^{ab\mu\nu}
=ig^2\epsilon^{abc}\frac{J_c\cdot k_2}{s}\,k_1^\mu.}
$$
The open-index tensor is generally nonzero: neither $\epsilon^{abc}$ nor $J_c\cdot k_2$ is constrained to vanish. For a concrete nonzero configuration, take <centre-of-momentum frame> kinematics $p_1=(E,0,0,p)$, $p_2=(E,0,0,-p)$, $p=\sqrt{E^2-m^2}$, $k_1=(E,E,0,0)$, $k_2=(E,-E,0,0)$. Standard <Dirac spinors> with two-component spin labels $\chi=(1,0)^T$ for $u$ and $\eta=(0,1)^T$ for $v$ give the spinor current $(0,2E,2iE,0)$ and its contraction with $k_2$ is $2E^2$. Choosing both colour spinors $(1,0)^T$ and $(a,b,c)=(1,2,3)$ multiplies this by $1/2$, still nonzero. This explicitly rules out an unconditional tensor identity $k_{2\nu}\mathcal M^{ab\mu\nu}=0$.

For a physical <polarization vector> $\varepsilon_{1\mu}^*$, however, $k_1\cdot\varepsilon_1^*=0$, and therefore
$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_{1\mu}^*\,k_{2\nu}\mathcal M^{ab\mu\nu}=0.}
$$
This is the required decoupling of a pure-gauge <polarization vector> on leg 2 when the other leg is physical. Interchanging the legs gives the corresponding identity for leg 1. The role of the three-boson diagram is explicit: it cancels the noncommuting colour-current term left by the two fermion exchanges.

The non-Abelian counterpart of the photon <Ward identity> is a <Slavnov-Taylor identity>, derived from <BRST symmetry>. A <scattering amplitude> with all other external states physical vanishes when one external massless <gauge boson> <polarization vector> is replaced by its <four-momentum>. An amputated tensor with the remaining indices open need not be transverse: part <3/d> gives its explicit nonzero longitudinal remainder.

Consequently, amplitudes involving several unphysical covariant <polarization vectors> need not vanish. For example, contracting the result of part <3/d> with a timelike vector $n_\mu$ gives $ig^2\epsilon^{abc}(J_c\cdot k_2)(n\cdot k_1)/s$, nonzero in the explicit configuration there for $n=(1,0,0,0)$. Even with other states physical, the pure-gauge vector $k$ is the relevant null combination; separate timelike and spatial longitudinal basis vectors are not themselves pure gauge, and their separate amplitudes need not be zero.

These auxiliary components do **not** define separately observable positive probabilities. Physical states are classes in <BRST cohomology>, and the <S-matrix> preserves that physical state space in this anomaly-free <vectorlike gauge spectrum>. There is no physical transition from a physical state to a nonphysical state. The incorrect stronger statement would be that every covariant component called “unphysical” has a zero matrix element.

For <loop Feynman diagrams> or <unitarity cuts>, summing covariant gauge indices with $-g_{\mu\nu}$ propagates those auxiliary components. In a <non-Abelian gauge theory>, their cancellation requires the interacting <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields> as well as the <gauge boson> diagrams. Ghost contributions carry their <Grassmann field> loop signs and restore the <Slavnov-Taylor identities> and physical <unitarity>. One may instead use a formulation with only physical propagating <polarization vectors>, but one cannot simply discard the ghost diagrams while retaining the <Feynman gauge> rules from part <3/a>.
