Use <natural units>, the <Minkowski metric> $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription>. Write $\not p=\gamma^\mu p_\mu$. In question 1, $D=4-\epsilon$, $e_D=\mu^{\epsilon/2}e$, and $\mu$ is the <renormalization scale>; keeping the dimensionful coupling $e_D$ explicit prevents a factor-of-two ambiguity in the <ultraviolet pole>. Let the bare fields obey the <wave-function renormalization> definitions $\psi_0=Z_2^{1/2}\psi$, $A_{0\mu}=Z_3^{1/2}A_\mu$, and put $m_0=Z_m m$. Define the <vertex renormalization> factor by $e_0=\mu^{\epsilon/2}eZ_1Z_2^{-1}Z_3^{-1/2}$. Substitution into the bare <QED> <Lagrangian density> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=Z_2\bar\psi i\not\partial\psi-Z_2Z_m m\bar\psi\psi-\frac{Z_3}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-e_DZ_1\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu.}
$$
Equivalently, write $Z_i=1+\delta_i$ and $\delta_m=Z_2Z_m-1$. The <counterterm Lagrangian> is
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ct}}=\delta_2\bar\psi i\not\partial\psi-\delta_m m\bar\psi\psi-\frac{\delta_3}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-e_D\delta_1\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu.
$$
For the subsequent <Feynman gauge> calculation include $-(\partial\cdot A)^2/2$ in the <gauge-fixed action>. The <Ward identity for QED renormalization constants> implies $Z_1=Z_2$ when the <regularization> preserves <gauge invariance>; this is a consequence, rather than an extra independent definition of the <renormalization constants>.

The <Clifford algebra> of the <gamma matrices> gives $\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu=2g^{\nu\mu}-\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu$ and $\gamma^\nu\gamma_\nu=D\mathbf1$. Therefore
$$
\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\nu=2g^{\nu\mu}\gamma_\nu-\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma_\nu=2\gamma^\mu-D\gamma^\mu,
\qquad\boxed{\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma_\nu=(2-D)\gamma^\mu.}
$$
The contraction $g^\nu{}_{\nu}=D$ must be retained until the <dimensional regularization> calculation is complete; replacing it prematurely by four can change finite terms multiplying a <ultraviolet pole>.

With $q=p'-p$ and the routing just described, multiplication of the three <QED Feynman rules> for the <Feynman vertices>, the two <Dirac propagators> and the <photon propagator> gives $(-ie_D)^3i^2(-i)=-e_D^3$. Hence the unrenormalized amputated <Feynman vertex> through this order is

Keep $m>0$ and set $p=p'=0$, which is an <off shell> choice. The numerator is
$$
N^\mu=\gamma^\nu(-\not k+m)\gamma^\mu(-\not k+m)\gamma_\nu.
$$
Terms odd in $k$ integrate to zero. <Lorentz covariance> and <dimensional regularization> give, inside this integral,
$$
k_\rho k_\sigma\longmapsto\frac{k^2}{D}g_{\rho\sigma},\qquad
\not k\gamma^\mu\not k\longmapsto\frac{2-D}{D}k^2\gamma^\mu.
$$
Applying the <gamma matrices> identity from part (ii) once more yields
$$
N^\mu\longmapsto\left[\frac{(D-2)^2}{D}k^2+(2-D)m^2\right]\gamma^\mu.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{iV^\mu_{\mathrm{loop}}(0,0)=-e_D^3\gamma^\mu\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{\alpha k^2+\beta m^2}{(k^2+i0)(k^2-m^2+i0)^2},\quad\alpha=\frac{(2-\epsilon)^2}{4-\epsilon},\quad\beta=\epsilon-2.}
$$
This <massive zero-momentum QED vertex integral> uses an integrated tensor identity for the replacement of $N^\mu$, not an equality for each fixed $k$. The physical <photon> remains massless. The retained <fermion> <mass> supplies a scale and makes this zero-external-<four-momentum> calculation suitable for extracting the <ultraviolet pole> without a <scaleless integral>.

After a <Wick rotation>, the term in question has radial behavior
$$
I_m=m^2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\frac1{(k^2+i0)(k^2-m^2+i0)^2}=-i\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{m^2}{k_E^2(k_E^2+m^2)^2}.
$$
The radial integrand is proportional to $k_E$ at the origin and to $k_E^{-3}$ at infinity. Thus it has neither an <infrared divergence> nor an <ultraviolet divergence>. In fact the area of the unit three-sphere gives
$$
I_m=-\frac{i m^2}{8\pi^2}\int_0^\infty\frac{r\,dr}{(r^2+m^2)^2}=\boxed{-\frac{i}{16\pi^2}}.
$$
This also follows from the <Feynman parameter> identity
$$
\frac1{AB^2}=2\int_0^1dx\,\frac{1-x}{[xA+(1-x)B]^3}
$$
with $A=k^2+i0$, $B=k^2-m^2+i0$. Although the parameterized denominator becomes massless at $x=1$, its numerator $1-x$ cancels the apparent endpoint singularity. Since $\beta\to-2$, multiplication by $\beta$ preserves finiteness. No artificial <photon> <mass> is needed.

Only the term proportional to $\alpha$ has an <ultraviolet pole>. Its $k^2$ cancels the massless denominator, leaving the standard <Feynman integral>
$$
J_D=\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac1{(k^2-m^2+i0)^2}=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{D/2}}\Gamma(2-D/2)(m^2)^{D/2-2}.
$$
In the present <dimensional regularization> convention, $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)=2/\epsilon+O(1)$ and $\alpha=1+O(\epsilon)$. Therefore
$$
iV^\mu_{\mathrm{loop}}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=-ie_D\gamma^\mu\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
The <counterterm> contribution is $-ie_D\delta_1\gamma^\mu$. Cancellation of the <ultraviolet pole> fixes
$$
\boxed{Z_1=1+\delta_1=1-\frac{e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}+O(e^4)\quad(D=4-\epsilon),}
$$
where the displayed expression uses the <minimal subtraction scheme>; a different <renormalization scheme> may add finite $O(e^2)$ terms. In the frequently used convention $D=4-2\varepsilon$, the same result is $Z_1=1-e^2/(16\pi^2\varepsilon)+O(e^4)$. The sign agrees with $Z_1=Z_2$ from the <Ward identity for QED renormalization constants> in <Feynman gauge>.

The <proper quark-gluon vertex colour factors> come from two <one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams>, shown in the upper-middle and upper-right panels. One has a virtual <gluon> joining the open <quark> line on opposite sides of the external <gluon> insertion. The other has two virtual <gluons> meeting the external <gluon> at a three-gluon <Feynman vertex>, with their other ends joined by an internal <quark>. A <Faddeev-Popov ghost field> has no direct <quark> coupling, so it supplies no additional one-loop <Feynman vertex> graph here.

Let $T^a$ be the Hermitian generators of the <fundamental representation> of $SU(N)$, with $\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)=\delta^{ab}/2$, $[T^a,T^b]=if^{abc}T^c$, $\sum_bT^bT^b=C_F\mathbf1$, and $f^{abc}f^{dbc}=C_A\delta^{ad}$. Here $C_F=(N^2-1)/(2N)$ and $C_A=N$ are <quadratic Casimir operators> evaluated in the <fundamental representation> and the <adjoint representation>.

For the first graph, the ordered product is $\sum_bT^bT^aT^b$. Expand the double <commutator>:
$$
\sum_b[T^b,[T^b,T^a]]=2C_FT^a-2\sum_bT^bT^aT^b=C_AT^a.
$$
Thus its <colour factor> is
$$
\boxed{\sum_bT^bT^aT^b=\left(C_F-\frac{C_A}{2}\right)T^a=-\frac1{2N}T^a=-\frac16T^a\quad(N=3).}
$$
For the second graph choose the orientation of the three-gluon <Feynman vertex> so that its group tensor is $f^{abc}$ and the ordered product along the <quark> line is $T^bT^c$. Antisymmetry removes the <anticommutator> part:
$$
\boxed{f^{abc}T^bT^c=\frac12f^{abc}[T^b,T^c]=\frac{iC_A}{2}T^a=\frac{3i}{2}T^a\quad(N=3).}
$$
The corresponding real group coefficient is $C_A/2=3/2$ after the conventional factor of $i$ is incorporated into the remaining <Feynman rules>. If the vertex is written with $if^{abc}$, its raw matrix product is instead $-C_AT^a/2$. Reversing the ordered product reverses its sign together with the orientation-dependent three-gluon <Feynman rule>; these conventions describe the same <scattering amplitude>. External-leg corrections and local <counterterms> are separate from these two proper one-loop graphs.

Use the <path integral> with source term
$$
S_{\mathrm{src}}=\int d^4x\,[J^\mu A_\mu+\bar\eta_\alpha\psi_\alpha+\bar\psi_\alpha\eta_\alpha].
$$
The sources $\eta,\bar\eta$ are <Grassmann variables>; all derivatives below are left <functional derivatives>. Because $\eta$ stands to the right of $\bar\psi$, differentiating with respect to it introduces a minus sign. The insertion rules are therefore
$$
\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J^\mu}\longleftrightarrow A_\mu,\qquad
i\frac{\delta}{\delta\eta_\alpha}\longleftrightarrow\bar\psi_\alpha,\qquad
\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar\eta_\beta}\longleftrightarrow\psi_\beta.
$$
For example $\delta_L(\bar\psi\eta)/\delta\eta=-\bar\psi$. The order of the odd <functional derivatives> matters: the rightmost derivative acts first, and the displayed product inserts $\bar\psi_\alpha\psi_\beta$ in that order.

Expand $\exp[-ie\int\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi A_\mu]$ in its <power series>, replace each field by its source derivative, and pull the derivatives outside the free <path integral>. Re-exponentiating gives
$$
\boxed{Z[J,\eta,\bar\eta]=\exp\left[-ie\int d^4x\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta J^\mu(x)}\right)\left(i\frac{\delta}{\delta\eta_\alpha(x)}\right)\gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac1i\frac{\delta}{\delta\bar\eta_\beta(x)}\right)\right]Z_0[J,\eta,\bar\eta].}
$$
This identity concerns the unnormalized <generating functional>. Normalized <correlation functions> divide by its zero-source value so that vacuum contributions cancel.

First fix a convention that is implicit in the source. For the stated free <generating functional>
$$
Z_0/Z_0[0]=\exp\left(i\bar\eta S\eta+\frac i2J\Delta J\right),
$$
the time-ordered <Wick contractions> are $S_F=\langle T\psi\bar\psi\rangle_0=-iS$ and $\Delta_F=\langle TAA\rangle_0=-i\Delta$. These follow directly by the source differentiation rules in part (i). Thus the kernels called <propagators> in the question are distinguished here from the contractions, which already include their factors of $i$.

At first order in the <QED> interaction, write $a$ for its integration point. The normalized three-point <correlation function> is
$$
G_{\nu\alpha\beta}=-ie\int d^4a\,\Delta_{F\nu\mu}(x-a)\gamma^\mu_{rs}\langle T\{\psi_\alpha(y)\bar\psi_\beta(z)\bar\psi_r(a)\psi_s(a)\}\rangle_0+O(e^3).
$$
The zeroth-order term vanishes because the free <photon> one-point <correlation function> is zero. There are two nonzero <Wick contractions> of the four fields:
$$
\langle T\{\psi_\alpha(y)\bar\psi_\beta(z)\bar\psi_r(a)\psi_s(a)\}\rangle_0
=-S_{F\alpha\beta}(y-z)S_{Fsr}(0)+S_{F\alpha r}(y-a)S_{Fs\beta}(a-z).
$$
The first term pairs the two external <fermions> directly and closes the two fields at the interaction into a <fermion loop>; its minus sign is the <closed fermion loop sign>. The second term joins the external <fermions> through the interaction, giving the connected contribution. This is also obtained by differentiating the quartic term $(i\bar\eta S\eta)^2/2$ in the free <generating functional>; the two choices of bilinear cancel its factor $1/2$.

Insert $S_F=-iS$ and $\Delta_F=-i\Delta$. Since $(-ie)(-i)^3=e$, the result is exactly
$$
\boxed{G_{\nu\alpha\beta}=-e\int d^4a\,\Delta_{\mu\nu}(a-x)\left(\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu S(0)]S_{\alpha\beta}(y-z)-[S(y-a)\gamma^\mu S(a-z)]_{\alpha\beta}\right)+O(e^3).}
$$
The trace term is a disconnected product of a photon <tadpole diagram> with the free external <fermion> <propagator>; the other term is the connected three-point <correlation function>. In this <QED> calculation the disconnected term vanishes, as established next. With a convention using $S_F,\Delta_F$ themselves as the symbols for the <propagators>, the equivalent formula retains the prefactor $-ie$ and the connected-minus-trace ordering above; mixing these two conventions would give an erroneous overall phase.

The massive <Dirac propagator> is
$$
S_F(x)=\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{i(\not k+m)e^{-ik\cdot x}}{k^2-m^2+i0}.
$$
Use the <gamma matrix trace identities> $\operatorname{tr}\gamma^\mu=0$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho)=4g^{\mu\rho}$. They give
$$
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu S_F(0)]=4i\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{k^\mu}{k^2-m^2+i0}=\boxed{0}.
$$
The integrand is odd under $k\mapsto-k$, and <dimensional regularization> preserves that symmetry. The same vanishing holds for $S=iS_F$. One must not assert that $S(0)$ itself is zero: its term proportional to $m\mathbf1$ generally is nonzero and divergent, but its trace against a single <gamma matrix> is zero. This one-photon <fermion loop> is also the simplest instance of <Furry's theorem>.

The two <tree-level Feynman diagrams> are the $s$-channel and $u$-channel <quark> exchange graphs, drawn in the middle row of the figure. Along the directed <quark> line the incoming <photon> is absorbed before the outgoing <photon> is emitted in the first graph; those two insertions are reversed in the second. Their internal <four-momenta> are $q_s=p_1+p_2$ and $q_u=p_1-p_4$, respectively. There is no three-photon <Feynman vertex> in ordinary <QED>, and no two-photon-two-quark <contact interaction> in the <Dirac field> <Lagrangian density>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Put $Q_u=2/3$, $s=q_s^2$, $u=q_u^2$, and suppress the common <colour factor> $\delta_{ji}$. Define $i\mathcal M$ by the product of <Feynman rules>. With massless external <Dirac spinors> the two graphs give
$$
\mathcal M_{\mu\nu}=-e^2Q_u^2\bar u(p_3)\left[\gamma_\nu\frac{\not q_s}{s}\gamma_\mu+\gamma_\mu\frac{\not q_u}{u}\gamma_\nu\right]u(p_1).
$$
The massless <Dirac equation> gives $\not p_1u(p_1)=0$ and $\bar u(p_3)\not p_3=0$. Thus
$$
\frac{\not q_s\not p_2}{s}u(p_1)=\frac{\not q_s(\not q_s-\not p_1)}s u(p_1)=u(p_1).
$$
By <four-momentum conservation>, $p_2=p_3-q_u$, so
$$
\bar u(p_3)\frac{\not p_2\not q_u}{u}=\bar u(p_3)\frac{(\not p_3-\not q_u)\not q_u}{u}=-\bar u(p_3).
$$
The two terms cancel:
$$
\boxed{p_2^\mu\mathcal M_{\mu\nu}=-e^2Q_u^2\bar u(p_3)(\gamma_\nu-\gamma_\nu)u(p_1)=0.}
$$
This is the <Ward identity> for the incoming <photon>; replacing its <photon polarization vector> by its <four-momentum> produces zero. The outgoing <photon> obeys the analogous identity. Each exchange graph separately fails this test, so the cancellation requires their sum. Consequently the physical <scattering amplitude> is invariant under $\varepsilon^\mu(p_2)\mapsto\varepsilon^\mu(p_2)+c p_2^\mu$.

For <massless quark Compton scattering>, the <color average> contributes one, since $(1/N_c)\sum_{i,j}|\delta_{ji}|^2=1$. The <spin average> and incoming <photon> <polarization> average supply $1/(2\cdot2)=1/4$. The <Ward identities> permit use of $-g_{\mu\nu}$ for each <photon polarization completeness relation>, and the massless <fermion spin sum> is $\sum_ru_r(p)\bar u_r(p)=\not p$.

Let
$$
A_{\mu\nu}=\gamma_\nu\frac{\not q_s}s\gamma_\mu+\gamma_\mu\frac{\not q_u}u\gamma_\nu.
$$
Reversing the <gamma matrices> order gives its Dirac-conjugate matrix $\bar A^{\mu\nu}$. The squared <scattering amplitude> is
$$
\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=\frac{e^4Q_u^4}{4}\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3 A_{\mu\nu}\not p_1\bar A^{\mu\nu}).
$$
For clarity evaluate the four traces before dividing by their denominators. The <Mandelstam variables> obey $s+t+u=0$, with $t=(p_1-p_3)^2$, $p_1\cdot p_2=s/2$, $p_1\cdot p_4=u(-1/2)$ and $p_1\cdot p_3=-t/2$. For the squared $s$-channel contribution, the <gamma matrix trace identities> imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{ss}&=\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\nu\not q_s\gamma_\mu\not p_1\gamma^\mu\not q_s\gamma^\nu)\\
&=-2\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\nu\not q_s\not p_1\not q_s\gamma^\nu)\\
&=-2s\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\nu\not p_2\gamma^\nu)
=4s\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\not p_2)=-8su.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\not q_s\not p_1\not q_s=2(q_s\cdot p_1)\not q_s-q_s^2\not p_1=s\not p_2$ and $p_3\cdot p_2=-u/2$. For the squared $u$-channel contribution, $\not q_u\not p_1\not q_u=-u\not p_4$ gives
$$
T_{uu}=\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\mu\not q_u\gamma_\nu\not p_1\gamma^\nu\not q_u\gamma^\mu)=-16u(p_3\cdot p_4)=-8su.
$$
One interference trace reduces to
$$
\begin{aligned}
T_{su}&=\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\nu\not q_s\gamma_\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu\not q_u\gamma^\mu)\\
&=-2\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\nu\not q_s\not q_u\gamma^\nu\not p_1)
=-32(q_s\cdot q_u)(p_3\cdot p_1)=0,
\end{aligned}
$$
since $q_s\cdot q_u=(s+t+u)/2=0$. The other interference trace is its complex conjugate and also vanishes. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=-2e^4Q_u^4\left(\frac us+\frac su\right)=-\frac{32e^4}{81}\frac{s^2+u^2}{su},\qquad C=\frac{32e^4}{81}.}
$$
Equivalently $C=512\pi^2\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^2/81$ in terms of the <fine-structure constant>. In the physical noncollinear region $s>0$, $u<0$, so the result is positive. The singular massless endpoint $u=0$ reflects a <collinear divergence>; it is not a negative squared <scattering amplitude>.

<Goldstone's theorem> states that in a relativistic <quantum field theory>, spontaneous breaking of an exact continuous global internal <symmetry> produces a massless <Goldstone boson> for each independent broken generator. More precisely, a conserved global current whose charge acts nontrivially on the <vacuum expectation value> has a massless pole in an appropriate current-field <correlation function>. The usual counting assumes a <Lorentz-invariant> <vacuum state> and the standard locality and positivity hypotheses.

A local <gauge symmetry> is a <gauge redundancy>, so it does not by itself imply massless physical <Goldstone bosons>. In the <Higgs mechanism>, the corresponding scalar directions provide the longitudinal <polarizations> of massive <gauge bosons>.

= ii
{parent=a}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

For <fundamental-doublet breaking of SU(2)>, assume $\lambda>0$ and $v>0$. The minima of the <scalar potential> satisfy $\phi^\dagger\phi=v^2/2$, giving a three-sphere <vacuum manifold>. A chosen <vacuum expectation value> is $\phi_0=(0,v)^T/\sqrt2$. No nonzero generator $a_a\tau^a$ annihilates $\phi_0$: its squared norm is $v^2(a_1^2+a_2^2+a_3^2)/8$. At the group level, an $SU(2)$ matrix fixing the nonzero vector $\phi_0$ must be the identity. Thus all three generators of the <SU(2) gauge theory> are broken in the usual perturbative <Higgs mechanism> description.

Make the radial and angular fields explicit:
$$
\phi(x)=\exp\left(\frac{i\sigma^a\pi^a(x)}v\right)\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\v+h(x)\end{pmatrix}.
$$
A local <gauge transformation> removes the three angular fields $\pi^a$. In <unitary gauge>, the <Pauli matrices> identity $\{\tau^a,\tau^b\}=\delta^{ab}\mathbf1/2$ gives
$$
(D_\mu\phi)^\dagger D^\mu\phi=\frac12(\partial_\mu h)(\partial^\mu h)+\frac{g^2}{8}(v+h)^2A_\mu^aA^{a\mu}.
$$
The mixed derivative term cancels between a term and its complex conjugate. The <scalar potential> becomes
$$
V=\lambda\left(vh+\frac{h^2}{2}\right)^2=\lambda v^2h^2+\lambda vh^3+\frac\lambda4h^4.
$$
Consequently the complete physical <Lagrangian density> is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L={}&-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}^aF^{a\mu\nu}+\frac12(\partial h)^2
+\frac12m_A^2A_\mu^aA^{a\mu}-\frac12m_h^2h^2\\
&+\frac{g^2v}{4}hA_\mu^aA^{a\mu}+\frac{g^2}{8}h^2A_\mu^aA^{a\mu}-\lambda vh^3-\frac\lambda4h^4,\\
F_{\mu\nu}^a={}&\partial_\mu A_\nu^a-\partial_\nu A_\mu^a+g\epsilon^{abc}A_\mu^bA_\nu^c.
\end{aligned}
$$
Reading the quadratic terms with their canonical factors $1/2$ gives
$$
\boxed{m_{A^1}=m_{A^2}=m_{A^3}=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad m_h=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,v.}
$$
All physical scalar-vector and scalar self-interactions are displayed in the second line of $\mathcal L$. In particular the new cubic couplings proportional to $v$ are $hA^aA^a$ and $h^3$. Their <Feynman vertices>, together with the quartic ones, are
$$
\boxed{hA_\mu^aA_\nu^b:\ i\frac{g^2v}{2}\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu},\quad hhA_\mu^aA_\nu^b:\ i\frac{g^2}{2}\delta^{ab}g_{\mu\nu},\quad hhh:\ -6i\lambda v,\quad hhhh:\ -6i\lambda.}
$$
The three-vector and four-vector interactions are already contained in the non-Abelian <gauge field strength> term. There are no further physical fields or interactions in this specified model. Before fixing <unitary gauge>, the quadratic angular term is $\tfrac12(\partial_\mu\pi^a-m_AA_\mu^a)^2$. It exhibits three zero-curvature angular directions of the <scalar potential>, but also their mixing with the <gauge fields>. They supply the third <polarization> of each massive <gauge boson>, rather than massless physical <particles>.

The degrees of freedom count is $3\cdot2+4=10$ before breaking and $3\cdot3+1=10$ afterwards. If instead the symmetry were global, as in the decoupled $g=0$ scalar theory, the three angular fields would be physical massless <Goldstone bosons>. This is consistent with <Goldstone's theorem>. The language of a broken local symmetry refers to this gauge-fixed expansion; a gauge-dependent <vacuum expectation value> is not itself a gauge-invariant observable.

A <gauge-invariant> <path integral> counts all representatives of each <gauge orbit>. In a perturbative region with a regular <gauge fixing> slice, insert the <Faddeev-Popov gauge-orbit identity>
$$
1=\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]\int\mathcal D\omega\,\delta(F[A^\omega]-\sigma),\qquad
F[A]=\partial_\mu A^\mu+\lambda A_\mu A^\mu.
$$
The <Faddeev-Popov determinant> is the <functional Jacobian> converting integration over the <gauge transformation> parameter into integration over the condition. The redundant <gauge group> volume then factors out of normalized <correlation functions>.

Choose $A_\mu^\omega=A_\mu+\partial_\mu\omega$. Its infinitesimal variation gives
$$
\delta F=\partial^2\omega+2\lambda A^\mu\partial_\mu\omega,\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal M(x,y)=[\partial_x^2+2\lambda A^\mu(x)\partial_{x\mu}]\delta^{(4)}(x-y),\quad\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A]=\det\mathcal M.}
$$
There is no derivative of $A$ in this expression: only $A_\mu$ is varied in $A^2$. For $\lambda=0$, the determinant is field independent and can be absorbed into normalization. For $\lambda\ne0$, it is a functional of the <gauge field>, so dropping it changes the <path integral>, even though the <gauge group> is Abelian. Constant residual <gauge transformations> are removed by boundary conditions or by dividing out their volume, as usual in perturbative <gauge fixing>.

An ordinary bosonic <Gaussian functional integral> produces an inverse determinant. An integral over independent <Grassmann fields> $c,\bar c$ instead produces a determinant, allowing a local action to represent the <Faddeev-Popov determinant>. With one convenient antighost sign convention,
$$
\det\mathcal M\ \propto\ \int\mathcal D\bar c\,\mathcal Dc\,\exp\left(i\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}\right),\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}=-\bar c(\partial^2+2\lambda A^\mu\partial_\mu)c.}
$$
Multiplying the operator by the field-independent factor $-i$ changes only the normalization of its determinant. After <integration by parts>, an equivalent density is
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}=(\partial_\mu\bar c)(\partial^\mu c)-2\lambda\bar c A^\mu\partial_\mu c.
$$
Thus the <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields> have a massless kinetic term and a photon-ghost-antighost interaction proportional to $\lambda$. They are auxiliary anticommuting scalar fields, not physical external <particles>. Closed <ghost loops> carry the minus sign of <Grassmann variables>. Replacing $\bar c$ by $-\bar c$ reverses both displayed signs without changing any observable; both the kinetic and interaction conventions must be changed together.

For fixed $A$, the delta functional sets $\sigma(x)=F[A](x)$. Therefore its weighted <functional integral> is
$$
\int\mathcal D\sigma\,\delta(F[A]-\sigma)\exp\left[-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int d^4x\,\sigma^2\right]
=\exp\left[-\frac{i}{2\xi}\int d^4x\,F[A]^2\right].
$$
Since the action enters as $e^{iS}$, the <gauge fixing> contribution is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\frac1{2\xi}\left(\partial_\mu A^\mu+\lambda A_\mu A^\mu\right)^2.}
$$
The <Faddeev-Popov determinant> does not depend on $\sigma$ and remains in the <path integral>. The limit $\xi\to0$ enforces the original condition sharply; for nonzero $\xi$ this is a weighted average of the gauge slices.

Expand the <gauge fixing> square:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2-\frac\lambda\xi(\partial\cdot A)A_\mu A^\mu-\frac{\lambda^2}{2\xi}(A_\mu A^\mu)^2.}
$$
The second term is a derivative cubic <photon> interaction; the third is a quartic <photon> <contact interaction>. Both arise from <nonlinear Abelian gauge fixing>, rather than an additional physical electromagnetic force.

For $\xi=1$, differentiating the quartic term with respect to four identical <gauge fields> gives eight copies for each pairing, hence
$$
V_4^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=-4i\lambda^2(g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\gamma\delta}+g^{\alpha\gamma}g^{\beta\delta}+g^{\alpha\delta}g^{\beta\gamma}).
$$
With the Fourier convention $A(x)=\int d^4p\,(2\pi)^{-4}e^{ipx}A(p)$ and all <four-momenta> incoming, the differentiated cubic term gives
$$
V_3^{\alpha\beta\gamma}=2\lambda(g^{\beta\gamma}p_1^\alpha+g^{\alpha\gamma}p_2^\beta+g^{\alpha\beta}p_3^\gamma),
$$
matching the supplied <Feynman rules>. Reversing the Fourier convention reverses this cubic rule's sign; each exchange graph contains two such <Feynman vertices>, so its contribution is unchanged.

There are four <tree-level Feynman diagrams>: three <photon> exchange graphs with pairings $12|34$, $13|24$, $14|23$, and the four-photon <contact interaction>. They are drawn in the bottom row of the figure. With no external <fermions> or <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields>, an internal matter or ghost line would have to close into a loop; it cannot add a <tree-level Feynman diagram> here.

Use all-incoming null <four-momenta> $k_i$ with $\sum_i k_i=0$. Let $\varepsilon_i$ be the external <photon polarization vectors>, including the complex conjugation for physical outgoing <photons>, and impose $k_i\cdot\varepsilon_i=0$. For one pairing $ij|kl$, put $K=k_i+k_j=-(k_k+k_l)$. At the first cubic <Feynman vertex>, the terms proportional to $k_i\cdot\varepsilon_i$ and $k_j\cdot\varepsilon_j$ vanish, leaving
$$
\varepsilon_{i\alpha}\varepsilon_{j\beta}V_3^{\alpha\beta\rho}(k_i,k_j,-K)=-2\lambda(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j)K^\rho.
$$
At the other end, the incoming internal <four-momentum> is $+K$, giving $+2\lambda(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l)K^\sigma$. The <Feynman gauge> <photon propagator> then gives
$$
i\mathcal M_{ij|kl}=(-2\lambda)(2\lambda)(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j)(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l)K^\rho\frac{-ig_{\rho\sigma}}{K^2+i0}K^\sigma
=4i\lambda^2(\varepsilon_i\cdot\varepsilon_j)(\varepsilon_k\cdot\varepsilon_l).
$$
The last expression is evaluated at generic kinematics, with singular channel limits taken after cancellation. Summing the three pairings produces $+4i\lambda^2P$, where
$$
P=(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_2)(\varepsilon_3\cdot\varepsilon_4)+(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_3)(\varepsilon_2\cdot\varepsilon_4)+(\varepsilon_1\cdot\varepsilon_4)(\varepsilon_2\cdot\varepsilon_3).
$$
The quartic <Feynman vertex> contributes $i\mathcal M_{\mathrm{contact}}=-4i\lambda^2P$. Thus
$$
\boxed{i\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tree}}=4i\lambda^2P-4i\lambda^2P=0.}
$$
This is the <tree photon-scattering cancellation in a quadratic Abelian gauge>: the individual <gauge fixing> interactions cancel in the physical <scattering amplitude>. The argument uses transverse external <photon polarization vectors>; it does not assert that an arbitrary <off shell> four-point <correlation function> vanishes. Physical <light-by-light scattering> first occurs through charged-particle <loop Feynman diagrams>.
