The bundle filename says 2018, but the paper's cover establishes Trinity Term 2019, 24 April 2019, and formal code A15272W1. These are solutions to that 2019 paper. Use <natural units>, the <Minkowski metric> $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$, and the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription>. Write $\not p=\gamma^\mu p_\mu$. In question 1, $D=4-\epsilon$, $g_D=\mu^{\epsilon/2}g$, and the amputated <gluon self-energy> insertion is **$i\Pi^{\mu\nu,ab}$**. This fixes the overall sign and factor of $i$ in the results below; defining the insertion as $-i\Pi$ instead reverses all corresponding definitions of $\Pi$. The <quadratic Casimir operator> in the <adjoint representation> is $C_A=N_c$ for $SU(N_c)$, hence $C_A=3$ in <QCD>.

There are four topologies for the one-loop <gluon self-energy>, shown in the upper row of the figure:

* A closed <quark> loop with the two external <gluons> attached through quark-gluon <Feynman vertices>, summed over active <quark flavors>.
* A loop of two internal <gluons> joining two three-gluon <Feynman vertices>, each carrying one external <gluon>.
* A closed <Faddeev-Popov ghost field> loop with two ghost-gluon <Feynman vertices>.
* A single four-gluon <Feynman vertex> with two external <gluons> and its remaining two legs contracted into a <tadpole diagram>.

The two internal-<gluon> graphs have <symmetry factor> $1/2$. The directed <quark> and <ghost loops> have <symmetry factor> one and a minus sign for a closed anticommuting loop. The four-gluon <tadpole diagram> must be included in the list even though its massless <Feynman integral> vanishes in <dimensional regularization>. Local <counterterms> are excluded here.

Route a directed <ghost loop> so that its internal <four-momenta> are $l$ and $l+k$. At the external <gluon> with indices $(\mu,a)$, let the incoming ghost carry colour $c$ and the outgoing ghost carry colour $d$; the other <Feynman vertex> has the reverse order. The stated ghost-gluon <Feynman rules> supply $g_D^2f^{acd}f^{bdc}(l+k)^\mu l^\nu$. Their <colour factor> is
$$
f^{acd}f^{bdc}=-C_A\delta^{ab}.
$$
The two <ghost propagators> supply $i^2=-1$, while the closed <ghost loop> supplies another $-1$. Thus, with the insertion convention stated above,
$$
i\Pi_{\mathrm{gh}}^{\mu\nu,ab}=-g_D^2C_A\delta^{ab}I^{\mu\nu},\qquad
I^{\mu\nu}=\int\frac{d^Dl}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{(l+k)^\mu l^\nu}{(l^2+i0)((l+k)^2+i0)}.
$$
This establishes every prefactor before integration. To isolate its <ultraviolet pole>, take generic <off shell> $k$, initially $k^2<0$, so the massless integral has no overlapping <infrared divergence>. Introduce a <Feynman parameter>, shift $r=l+xk$, and set $\Delta=-x(1-x)k^2>0$. Odd powers of $r$ vanish, giving
$$
I^{\mu\nu}=\int_0^1dx\left[\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{D}\int\frac{d^Dr}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{r^2}{(r^2-\Delta+i0)^2}-x(1-x)k^\mu k^\nu\int\frac{d^Dr}{(2\pi)^D}\frac1{(r^2-\Delta+i0)^2}\right].
$$
The needed <dimensional regularization> integrals follow from the supplied <gamma function> formula. In $D=4-\epsilon$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
J_1(\Delta)&=\int\frac{d^Dr}{(2\pi)^D}\frac1{r^2-\Delta+i0}
=\frac{i\Delta}{16\pi^2}\frac2\epsilon+O(1),\\
J_2(\Delta)&=\int\frac{d^Dr}{(2\pi)^D}\frac1{(r^2-\Delta+i0)^2}
=\frac{i}{16\pi^2}\frac2\epsilon+O(1),\\
\int\frac{d^Dr}{(2\pi)^D}\frac{r^2}{(r^2-\Delta+i0)^2}&=J_1+\Delta J_2=\frac{i}{16\pi^2}\frac{4\Delta}{\epsilon}+O(1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)=2/\epsilon+O(1)$, rather than $1/\epsilon$. Using $1/D=1/4+O(\epsilon)$ and $\int_0^1x(1-x)\,dx=1/6$, obtain
$$
I^{\mu\nu}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=-\frac{i}{96\pi^2\epsilon}\left(k^2g^{\mu\nu}+2k^\mu k^\nu\right).
$$
Therefore the <ghost-loop pole of the gluon self-energy> is
$$
\boxed{\Pi_{\mathrm{gh}}^{\mu\nu,ab}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=\frac{g^2C_A\delta^{ab}}{96\pi^2\epsilon}\left(k^2g^{\mu\nu}+2k^\mu k^\nu\right).}
$$
The factor $\mu^\epsilon$ in $g_D^2$ affects only finite terms at this order. The isolated <ghost loop> is not transverse: contracting this result gives $g^2C_A\delta^{ab}k^2k^\nu/(32\pi^2\epsilon)$. Transversality is a condition on the complete <gluon self-energy>, including the other loop graphs.

There is one four-gluon <tadpole diagram>. Temporarily replace its internal <gluon propagator> by $-ig_{\rho\sigma}\delta^{cd}/(l^2-m_g^2+i0)$. Contract the supplied four-gluon <Feynman vertex> with $g_{\rho\sigma}\delta^{cd}$. Its first <colour factor> vanishes because $f^{cce}=0$. Each of the other two terms contributes $C_A\delta^{ab}(D-1)g^{\mu\nu}$, so
$$
\delta^{cd}g_{\rho\sigma}V_4^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma,abcd}=-2ig_D^2C_A\delta^{ab}(D-1)g^{\mu\nu}.
$$
Include the <symmetry factor> $1/2$ and the factor $-i$ from the internal <gluon propagator>:
$$
i\Pi_{4g}^{\mu\nu,ab}=-g_D^2C_A\delta^{ab}(D-1)g^{\mu\nu}J_1(m_g^2).
$$
The exact regulated <tadpole integral> is
$$
J_1(m_g^2)=-\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{D/2}}\Gamma(1-D/2)(m_g^2)^{D/2-1}.
$$
In particular,
$$
i\Pi_{4g}^{\mu\nu,ab}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=-\frac{i g^2C_A\delta^{ab}}{8\pi^2\epsilon}(D-1)m_g^2g^{\mu\nu}.
$$
The entire expression is proportional to $(m_g^2)^{1-\epsilon/2}$ and vanishes as the auxiliary <mass> tends to zero near four dimensions. Equivalently, its expanded terms are proportional to $m_g^2/\epsilon$, $m_g^2\log m_g^2$ and $m_g^2$, all of which disappear when the regulator is removed. Hence
$$
\boxed{\Pi_{4g}^{\mu\nu,ab}=0\qquad(m_g\to0).}
$$
This is the massless <scaleless integral> result obtained directly in <dimensional regularization>. The artificial <mass> is only an intermediate device for this graph; retaining it in the full theory would modify the <gauge symmetry> and cannot be used when applying the massless transversality identity in part (iv).

The <Slavnov-Taylor identity> implies transversality of the full perturbative <gluon self-energy>:
$$
k_\mu\left(\Pi_{3g}^{\mu\nu,ab}+\Pi_{4g}^{\mu\nu,ab}+\Pi_{\mathrm{gh}}^{\mu\nu,ab}+\Pi_q^{\mu\nu,ab}\right)=0.
$$
The <quark> loop is separately transverse. This follows without evaluating its <Feynman integral>: contract its vector <Feynman vertex> with $k_\mu$ and use $\not k=(\not l+\not k-m)-(\not l-m)$ between its two <Dirac propagators>. One obtains a difference of two loop integrals that cancel after a shift of $l$ in <dimensional regularization>. The <colour factor> is proportional to $\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)$ and does not obstruct this argument.

The four-gluon <tadpole diagram> vanishes after removal of $m_g$. Thus the nontransverse parts of the two-three-gluon-vertex loop and the <ghost loop> must cancel. Contracting the preceding <ghost-loop pole of the gluon self-energy> gives
$$
\boxed{k_\mu\Pi_{3g}^{\mu\nu,ab}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=-k_\mu\Pi_{\mathrm{gh}}^{\mu\nu,ab}\big|_{\mathrm{UV}}=-\frac{g^2C_A\delta^{ab}}{32\pi^2\epsilon}k^2k^\nu.}
$$
For <QCD>, set $C_A=3$. No evaluation of the remaining three-gluon <Feynman diagram> is required. Its uncontracted transverse part is not fixed by this identity.

For the one-loop <renormalization-group beta function>, write $t=\log\mu$. Separating variables gives
$$
\int_{\alpha_i}^{\alpha_f}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^2}=b_0\int_{\log\mu_i}^{\log\mu_f}dt,
\qquad
\boxed{\alpha(\mu_f)=\frac{\alpha(\mu_i)}{1-b_0\alpha(\mu_i)\log(\mu_f/\mu_i)}.}
$$
This expression resums the leading logarithms of the one-loop <running coupling>, while omitting higher-order terms in the <renormalization-group beta function>. Assume a positive initial coupling.

For $b_0<0$, increasing the <renormalization scale> decreases the <running coupling>; the zero-coupling <renormalization-group fixed point> is attractive in the ultraviolet. This is <asymptotic freedom>. Toward lower scales the one-loop expression has a formal pole at $\mu_i\exp[1/(b_0\alpha_i)]<\mu_i$. The strong-coupling neighborhood of that pole lies outside perturbative control. <QCD> provides the main example; the minimal <Standard Model> $SU(2)$ coupling is also <asymptotically free> at one loop.

For $b_0>0$, the <running coupling> grows with the <renormalization scale> and formally diverges at the ultraviolet <Landau pole> $\mu_i\exp[1/(b_0\alpha_i)]>\mu_i$. Toward the infrared it decreases to zero within this approximation. The <Standard Model> $U(1)$ <weak hypercharge> coupling has this sign, as does <QED> in a fixed charged-particle regime. A perturbative <Landau pole> marks failure of the approximation, rather than proving that the exact theory has that divergence.

For $b_0=0$, the <running coupling> is constant at this order; higher-loop terms decide whether it runs. Vanishing of the leading coefficient alone does not establish exact scale invariance.

For a concrete common convention, define $\alpha_i=g_i^2/(4\pi)$ with $g_Y$ in its usual, non-unified <weak hypercharge> normalization. In the minimal <Standard Model> with three generations and one Higgs doublet, above its particle thresholds,
$$
\frac{d\alpha_i}{d\log\mu}=\frac{b_i}{2\pi}\alpha_i^2,\qquad
(b_Y,b_2,b_3)=\left(\frac{41}{6},-\frac{19}{6},-7\right).
$$
Thus the question's $b_0$ is $b_i/(2\pi)$, with the sign regimes just described. Crossing a <mass> threshold changes the active field content and requires the appropriate piecewise <renormalization-group beta function>.

For a real local parameter $\alpha(x)$, the <Dirac adjoint> varies as $\delta\bar\psi=ie\alpha\bar\psi$. The matter kinetic term varies by
$$
\delta(\bar\psi i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi)=e(\partial_\mu\alpha)\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi.
$$
The <QED> interaction varies by $-e(\partial_\mu\alpha)\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$, since the phases in $\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$ cancel. These two variations cancel pointwise. The <mass> term is invariant for the same reason, and $\delta F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\alpha-\partial_\nu\partial_\mu\alpha=0$.

Only the <gauge fixing> term varies. Put $B=\partial_\mu A^\mu$; then $\delta B=\partial^2\alpha$, giving
$$
\boxed{\delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{QED}}=-\frac1\xi(\partial_\mu A^\mu)\partial^2\alpha.}
$$
For arbitrary local $\alpha$, the <gauge-fixed action> is therefore not invariant under this transformation of its dynamical fields. The restricted parameters satisfying $\partial^2\alpha=0$ give a residual invariance of the displayed density.

Let $\epsilon$ here be a constant infinitesimal transformation parameter, unrelated to the <dimensional regularization> parameter in question 1. The matter and Maxwell terms undergo an ordinary infinitesimal <gauge transformation> with $\alpha=\epsilon\phi$, and their variations cancel as before. The <gauge fixing> contribution is
$$
\delta\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gf}}=-\frac\epsilon\xi B\partial^2\phi.
$$
The extra real <scalar field> kinetic term varies by
$$
\delta\left(\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi\right)=\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\delta\phi=-\frac\epsilon\xi(\partial_\mu\phi)(\partial^\mu B).
$$
Adding the two gives a total derivative:
$$
\boxed{\delta\mathcal L=-\frac\epsilon\xi\partial_\mu(B\partial^\mu\phi),\qquad\delta S=0.}
$$
The last equality uses vanishing boundary contributions, for example sufficiently fast decay of the fields. No field equations were used. This <gauge-scalar global symmetry of covariantly fixed QED> leaves the action invariant even though the density need not be invariant pointwise; the scalar's variation supplies exactly the missing boundary-term cancellation.

First derive the contracted <gamma matrices> identities from the <Clifford algebra> in four dimensions. Moving the outer <gamma matrix> through two factors gives
$$
\gamma_\rho\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma^\rho=4g_{\alpha\beta}\mathbf1.
$$
Moving it through three factors, and using this two-factor result, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\gamma_\rho\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\beta\gamma_\gamma\gamma^\rho
&=2\gamma_\beta\gamma_\gamma\gamma_\alpha-4g_{\beta\gamma}\gamma_\alpha\\
&=-2\gamma_\gamma\gamma_\beta\gamma_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently $\gamma^\nu\not b\gamma_\mu\not c\gamma_\nu=-2\not c\gamma_\mu\not b$ and $\gamma_\mu\not b\not d\gamma^\mu=4(b\cdot d)\mathbf1$. Apply these in succession:
$$
\begin{aligned}
T&=\operatorname{tr}(\not a\gamma^\nu\not b\gamma_\mu\not c\gamma_\nu\not d\gamma^\mu)\\
&=-2\operatorname{tr}(\not a\not c\gamma_\mu\not b\not d\gamma^\mu)\\
&=-8(b\cdot d)\operatorname{tr}(\not a\not c)
=\boxed{-32(a\cdot c)(b\cdot d)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The final step uses $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\beta)=4g^{\alpha\beta}$. This is a genuinely four-dimensional instance of the <gamma matrix trace identities>; using it unchanged inside a $D$-dimensional loop calculation would require separate justification.

Label the <four-momenta> as $e^-(p_1)\mu^-(p_2)\to e^-(p_3)\mu^-(p_4)$. There is one <tree-level Feynman diagram>, the $t$-channel <photon> exchange shown in the lower-left panel. The two lepton species are distinct, so there is no exchanged-final-lepton diagram. With $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$, $t=(p_1-p_3)^2$ and $u=(p_1-p_4)^2$, the <Feynman rules> give
$$
i\mathcal M=\frac{ie^2}{t}[\bar u(p_3)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)][\bar u(p_4)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)].
$$
The massless <fermion spin sum> is $\sum_ru_r(p)\bar u_r(p)=\not p$. Averaging the two initial <spins> of each lepton supplies $1/4$, so
$$
\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=\frac{e^4}{4t^2}\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma^\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu)\operatorname{tr}(\not p_4\gamma_\mu\not p_2\gamma_\nu).
$$
The <gamma matrix trace identities> imply, for arbitrary null external <four-momenta>,
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not a\gamma^\mu\not b\gamma^\nu)\operatorname{tr}(\not c\gamma_\mu\not d\gamma_\nu)=32[(a\cdot c)(b\cdot d)+(a\cdot d)(b\cdot c)].
$$
To see the contraction, each trace is $4(a^\mu b^\nu+a^\nu b^\mu-g^{\mu\nu}a\cdot b)$; the terms proportional to $(a\cdot b)(c\cdot d)$ cancel in four dimensions, leaving the displayed two products. Here $p_1\cdot p_2=p_3\cdot p_4=s/2$ and $p_1\cdot p_4=p_2\cdot p_3=-u/2$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=2e^4\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}.}
$$
The requested proportionality constant is $2e^4=32\pi^2\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^2$, where $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ is the <fine-structure constant>. Both numerator terms are required by the <spin sum>; no final-state statistical factor enters a squared <scattering amplitude> for distinct leptons.

For <Møller scattering>, both the $t$-channel and $u$-channel <photon> exchange <tree-level Feynman diagrams> contribute, as shown in the two remaining lower panels. Exchanging the identical final <electrons> reverses the sign of the <scattering amplitude>, so the <fermionic sign> fixes
$$
\mathcal M=e^2\left(\frac{J_t}{t}-\frac{J_u}{u}\right),\qquad
J_t=[\bar u_3\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar u_4\gamma_\mu u_2],\quad
J_u=[\bar u_4\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar u_3\gamma_\mu u_2].
$$
The two direct squares follow from the preceding distinct-lepton calculation:
$$
\frac14\sum|J_t|^2=2(s^2+u^2),\qquad
\frac14\sum|J_u|^2=2(s^2+t^2).
$$
The interference cannot be discarded because the final <particles> are identical. Summing their <spins> joins all four external <Dirac spinors> into a single <trace>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum J_tJ_u^*&=\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma^\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu\not p_4\gamma_\mu\not p_2\gamma_\nu)\\
&=-32(p_3\cdot p_4)(p_1\cdot p_2)=-8s^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The result from part (i) was used with relabelled contracted indices. Its negative sign combines with the explicit subtraction of the two exchange <scattering amplitudes>, so the interference contribution to the averaged square is positive:
$$
-\frac{2e^4}{4tu}\operatorname{Re}\sum J_tJ_u^*=\frac{4e^4s^2}{tu}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\langle|\mathcal M|^2\rangle=2e^4\left[\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}+\frac{s^2+t^2}{u^2}+\frac{2s^2}{tu}\right].}
$$
This is symmetric under $t\leftrightarrow u$, as required by the identical final <electrons>. There is no additional $1/2!$ in the squared <scattering amplitude>. Such a factor is used when integrating the <cross section> over a final-state region that counts both orderings of the identical pair; alternatively one integrates over one representative half of that region.

After <integration by parts>, the quadratic <Proca action> with an external source is
$$
S[A,J]=\frac12\int d^4x\,A_\mu K^{\mu\nu}A_\nu+\int d^4x\,J^\mu A_\mu,\qquad
K^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}(\partial^2+M^2)-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu.
$$
Since $M>0$, this kernel is invertible with the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription>; the massless <gauge redundancy> is absent in this <unitary gauge> quadratic action. Complete the square by $A\mapsto A+K_F^{-1}J$ in the <Gaussian functional integral>. Its source-independent determinant cancels in the normalized <generating functional>:
$$
\boxed{\frac{Z[J]}{Z[0]}=\exp\left[-\frac i2\int d^4x\,d^4y\,J_\mu(x)(K_F^{-1})^{\mu\nu}(x-y)J_\nu(y)\right].}
$$
In <Fourier space>, the kernel is
$$
K^{\mu\nu}(k)=(M^2-k^2)g^{\mu\nu}+k^\mu k^\nu.
$$
For $k^2\ne0$, use the <transverse projector of a vector field> $P_T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-k^\mu k^\nu/k^2$ and the <longitudinal projector of a vector field> $P_L^{\mu\nu}=k^\mu k^\nu/k^2$. Their mixed-index products obey $P_TP_L=0$, so
$$
K=(M^2-k^2)P_T+M^2P_L,\qquad
K^{-1}=\frac{P_T}{M^2-k^2}+\frac{P_L}{M^2}=-\frac{g^{\mu\nu}-k^\mu k^\nu/M^2}{k^2-M^2}.
$$
The last expression also extends to $k^2=0$; that point only obstructs the intermediate projector decomposition. Two source <functional derivatives> yield the <gauge-boson propagator> $D_F=iK_F^{-1}$:
$$
\boxed{D_F^{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i}{k^2-M^2+i0}\left(g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{M^2}\right),\qquad
\langle T A^\mu(x)A^\nu(y)\rangle=\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}e^{-ik\cdot(x-y)}D_F^{\mu\nu}(k).}
$$
The pole residue is the physical massive-vector <massive vector polarization sum>. The longitudinal term is essential for inverting the quadratic kernel, and makes the naive $M\to0$ limit of this <unitary gauge> <Proca propagator> singular.

Expand the <matrix trace> explicitly:
$$
\mathcal L=\sum_{i,j=1}^N\left[\partial_\mu\Phi_{ij}^*\partial^\mu\Phi_{ij}-m^2\Phi_{ij}^*\Phi_{ij}\right].
$$
Write $\Phi_{ij}=(x_{ij}+iy_{ij})/\sqrt2$ and collect the $2N^2$ real fields into a vector $X$. Then
$$
\mathcal L=\frac12\sum_{A=1}^{2N^2}\left[\partial_\mu X_A\partial^\mu X_A-m^2X_A^2\right].
$$
Both quadratic forms are preserved by every constant <orthogonal matrix> $R$, acting as $X\mapsto RX$. Thus the full linear internal symmetry contains $O(2N^2)$, with connected <special orthogonal group> $SO(2N^2)$ as requested. The enlargement beyond unitary transformations is possible because the real and imaginary components have identical kinetic and <mass> terms and no interactions distinguishing them.

For a <Hermitian matrix> $\Phi$, there are $N$ real diagonal entries and $N(N-1)/2$ independent complex off-diagonal entries, hence $N^2$ real fields. Choose an <orthonormal basis> $T_A$ of <Hermitian matrices> for the <trace inner product>, $\operatorname{tr}(T_AT_B)=\delta_{AB}$, and put $\Phi=\sum_AX_AT_A/\sqrt2$. The same calculation gives
$$
\boxed{SO(2N^2)\ \text{for a general complex matrix},\qquad SO(N^2)\ \text{for a Hermitian matrix},}
$$
where these are the connected rotation groups; their full orthogonal extensions are $O(2N^2)$ and $O(N^2)$. A purely quadratic theory with $m^2<0$ would have an unstable <scalar potential>, but this algebraic symmetry statement still holds.

Let $X=\Phi^\dagger\Phi$. Under the global transformation of the <chiral matrix scalar model>,
$$
\Phi'=e^{i\theta}U_L\Phi U_R^\dagger,\qquad X'=U_RXU_R^\dagger.
$$
The phase and $U_L^\dagger U_L$ cancel, and $U_R^\dagger U_R=\mathbf1$. The <cyclic property of the trace> implies
$$
\operatorname{tr}X'=\operatorname{tr}X,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(X'^2)=\operatorname{tr}(U_RX^2U_R^\dagger)=\operatorname{tr}X^2.
$$
Therefore each of the three terms in the <scalar potential> is invariant separately. Since $\theta,U_L,U_R$ are constant, $\partial_\mu\Phi'=e^{i\theta}U_L(\partial_\mu\Phi)U_R^\dagger$, giving invariance of the kinetic <matrix trace> by the same argument. Hence
$$
\boxed{G=SU(N)_L\times SU(N)_R\times U(1)}
$$
is a global symmetry group of the <Lagrangian density>. Its action has a finite central kernel, which does not affect its <Lie algebra> or the broken-generator counting below. For special parameter choices the symmetry can be larger; the displayed group is a symmetry for the stated potential without such extra assumptions.

It is useful first to establish the global minimum, rather than only a stationary point. The <positive semidefinite matrix> $X=\Phi^\dagger\Phi$ has nonnegative <eigenvalues> $x_1,\ldots,x_N$. For fixed $r=\sum_i x_i$, the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> gives $\sum_i x_i^2\ge r^2/N$, with equality exactly when all $x_i$ are equal. Since $\alpha>0$,
$$
V\ge\frac12\left(\frac\alpha N+\beta\right)r^2+m^2r.
$$
For $m^2<0$ and $\beta>0$, this lower bound has its minimum at
$$
r=-\frac{Nm^2}{\alpha+N\beta},\qquad x_i=C^2=-\frac{m^2}{\alpha+N\beta}>0.
$$
The bound is attained, so every minimum has $\Phi^\dagger\Phi=C^2\mathbf1$, or $\Phi=CW$ with $W$ a <unitary matrix>. A global transformation removes $W$ by decomposing it into an overall phase and an $SU(N)$ matrix. Thus $\Phi_0=C\mathbf1$ is a valid representative of the <vacuum manifold>.

Preservation of this chosen <vacuum expectation value> requires
$$
e^{i\theta}U_LU_R^\dagger=\mathbf1.
$$
Infinitesimally, with traceless Hermitian generators $L,R$, this reads $\theta\mathbf1+L-R=0$. Taking the <trace> forces $\theta=0$, and then $L=R$. The surviving continuous transformations are consequently
$$
\boxed{\Phi\mapsto U\Phi U^\dagger,\qquad U\in SU(N),\qquad H_{\mathrm{connected}}\simeq SU(N)_{\mathrm{diagonal}}.}
$$
All other independent continuous directions change the chosen <vacuum expectation value>. Thus the number of broken generators is
$$
\boxed{[2(N^2-1)+1]-(N^2-1)=N^2.}
$$
Globally, taking the determinant of the stabilizer equation permits the discrete phases $e^{iN\theta}=1$, paired with $U_L=e^{-i\theta}U_R$. They induce the same conjugation action on $\Phi$. The faithful unbroken conjugation group is $SU(N)/\mathbb Z_N$; for example, at $N=2$, $U=-\mathbf1$ acts exactly like $U=\mathbf1$. Thus the question's description by $SU(N)$ is correct as a parametrization and at the <Lie algebra> level, with this central redundancy understood. It leaves the number of broken generators unchanged.

= Solution
{parent=iv}
{c}

Write $H=\phi_1$ and $P=\phi_2$, both <Hermitian matrices>. Through quadratic order around the chosen <vacuum expectation value>,
$$
X=\Phi^\dagger\Phi=C^2\mathbf1+\sqrt2 CH+\frac12(H^2+P^2+i[H,P]).
$$
The <trace of a commutator> vanishes. The needed <matrix traces> are therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}X&=NC^2+\sqrt2 C\operatorname{tr}H+\frac12\operatorname{tr}(H^2+P^2),\\
\operatorname{tr}X^2&=NC^4+2\sqrt2 C^3\operatorname{tr}H+C^2\operatorname{tr}(3H^2+P^2)+O((H,P)^3),\\
(\operatorname{tr}X)^2&=N^2C^4+2\sqrt2 NC^3\operatorname{tr}H+NC^2\operatorname{tr}(H^2+P^2)+2C^2(\operatorname{tr}H)^2+O((H,P)^3).
\end{aligned}
$$
Using $m^2=-(\alpha+N\beta)C^2$, all linear terms cancel. The quadratic <scalar potential> reduces to
$$
\boxed{V^{(2)}=\alpha C^2\operatorname{tr}H^2+\beta C^2(\operatorname{tr}H)^2,}
$$
with no $P^2$ term. The kinetic term is
$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{kin}}^{(2)}=\frac12\operatorname{tr}[(\partial H)^2+(\partial P)^2],
$$
since its mixed term is the <trace of a commutator>. A <Hermitian matrix> has $N^2$ independent real components, so $P$ describes exactly $N^2$ massless scalar modes.

To determine the other <masses>, take $T_0=\mathbf1/\sqrt N$ and traceless Hermitian $T_A$ with $\operatorname{tr}(T_AT_B)=\delta_{AB}$, and expand $H=h_0T_0+\sum_{A=1}^{N^2-1}h_AT_A$. These are canonically normalized real <scalar fields>. The <scalar mass matrix> is diagonal in this basis:
$$
\boxed{m_{h_0}^2=2C^2(\alpha+N\beta)=-2m^2,\qquad m_{h_A}^2=2\alpha C^2\quad(A=1,\ldots,N^2-1),\qquad m_{P_A}^2=0\quad(A=0,\ldots,N^2-1).}
$$
Thus $H$ contains one massive singlet and a degenerate multiplet of $N^2-1$ massive fields in the <adjoint representation> of the unbroken diagonal $SU(N)$. Both squared <masses> are strictly positive for the specified parameters. The $P$ fields are the $N^2$ <Goldstone bosons> associated with the broken generators: $N^2-1$ traceless relative left-right rotations and one overall phase rotation.

<Goldstone's theorem> guarantees those massless directions. It does not alone forbid additional accidental massless modes; the positive <scalar mass matrix> computed here supplies the further conclusion that every $H$ component is massive. The fields describe a global symmetry, so these <Goldstone bosons> are physical rather than being absorbed into massive <gauge bosons>.
