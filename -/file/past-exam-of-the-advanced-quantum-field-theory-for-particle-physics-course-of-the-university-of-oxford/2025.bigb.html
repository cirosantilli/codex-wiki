= Solution

The <scalar field path integral> integrates over real field histories with vacuum <boundary conditions>. With $S_0[\phi]=\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_0$, the <partition function> is
$$
\boxed{Z=\mathcal N\int\mathcal D\phi\,
\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\left[\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi-\frac12(m^2-i0)\phi^2-\frac\lambda{4!}\phi^4\right]\right\}.}
$$
The constant $\mathcal N$ fixes the desired vacuum normalization. A <generating functional> adds $\int d^4x\,J\phi$ to the <action>; the required <partition function> is its zero-source value. Equivalently, a <Wick rotation> gives $Z_E=\mathcal N_E\int\mathcal D\phi\,e^{-S_E[\phi]}$, with the positive-sign kinetic and potential terms in the <Euclidean action> when $m^2\geq0$ and $\lambda>0$.

Vary the <action>, taking the field variation to vanish at the boundary. <Integration by parts> gives
$$
\delta S_0=\int d^4x\left[\partial^\mu\phi\,\partial_\mu\delta\phi-m^2\phi\delta\phi-\frac\lambda6\phi^3\delta\phi\right]
=-\int d^4x\left(\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\frac\lambda6\phi^3\right)\delta\phi.
$$
Thus the <Euler-Lagrange field equation> is
$$
\boxed{\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\frac\lambda6\phi^3=0.}
$$
For the <field redefinition> in part <1/f>, define the <functional derivative> $E(\phi)=\delta S_0/\delta\phi=-(\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\lambda\phi^3/6)$. The sign does not affect the <equation of motion>, but it fixes the sign of an added <redundant operator>.

The <action> is dimensionless, so in four dimensions the <Lagrangian density> has <mass dimension> four. Its kinetic term fixes $[\phi]=1$ and $[\partial_\mu]=1$. An interaction $g_D\mathcal O_D$ with $[\mathcal O_D]=D$ has $[g_D]=4-D$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{D\leq4\ \text{is renormalizable by power counting};\qquad D>4\ \text{has a negative-dimensional coupling}.}
$$
In an <effective field theory> with heavy scale $\Lambda$, a higher-dimensional <local operator> enters as $c_D\mathcal O_D/\Lambda^{D-4}$. For characteristic scales $E,m\ll\Lambda$ and coefficients compatible with this expansion, its effects are suppressed by powers of the small ratios. Omitting these terms defines the leading approximation and its <truncation error in effective field theory>. It is not a fundamental prohibition: such terms must be retained if that accuracy is required. A continuum <renormalizable quantum field theory> can instead be defined by the closed set of <counterterms> of dimension at most four.

Up to <total derivatives>, all <Lorentz scalar> terms of dimension at most four built from one <real scalar field> are
$$
1,\quad\phi,\quad\phi^2,\quad\phi^3,\quad\phi^4,\quad
\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi.
$$
The term $\phi\Box\phi$ is equivalent to the kinetic term by <integration by parts>; $\Box\phi$ and $\Box(\phi^2)$ contribute only at the boundary. The displayed <Lagrangian density> omits the linear and cubic interactions and a field-independent constant. The exact <Z2 symmetry> $\phi\mapsto-\phi$ forbids both odd interactions, and <radiative corrections> preserving this <symmetry> cannot generate them. The constant shifts the vacuum <energy> and the normalization of $Z$ and can be dropped when gravity and absolute vacuum <energy> are not being studied. A constant vacuum <counterterm> may still be used when renormalizing unnormalized vacuum diagrams. The kinetic coefficient has already been fixed by field normalization.

For <dimension-six operators of a real scalar field>, let $n$ count fields and $r$ count <partial derivatives>. <Mass dimension> gives $n+r=6$. <Lorentz invariance> requires even $r$. Discard derivatives acting on the entire expression, but **do not yet identify different expressions by integration by parts**. A complete list of seven representatives is
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathcal O_1&=\phi^6,\\
\mathcal O_2&=\phi^2\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\phi,&
\mathcal O_3&=\phi^3\Box\phi,\\
\mathcal O_4&=\phi\Box^2\phi,&
\mathcal O_5&=\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\Box\phi,\\
\mathcal O_6&=(\Box\phi)^2,&
\mathcal O_7&=(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi)(\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\phi).
\end{aligned}}
$$
For $(n,r)=(6,0)$ there is one choice; for $(4,2)$ the derivative distribution is either $2$ or $1+1$; for $(2,4)$ it is $4$, $3+1$, or $2+2$, the last allowing two distinct metric contractions. Commuting <partial derivatives> identifies alternative orderings. Contractions with a <Levi-Civita symbol> vanish because an antisymmetric index pair necessarily contracts symmetric derivatives. The remaining case $(0,6)$ gives no field-dependent expression. No independent odd-$r$ <Lorentz scalar> exists.

The source's seven means this unreduced list. If “ignoring total derivative operators” were interpreted as already taking the full quotient by <total derivatives>, seven would be incorrect: the next part gives three representatives of that quotient. Dimensionful factors multiplying lower-dimensional terms do not count as new canonical-dimension-six field monomials.

Write $X\simeq Y$ when the two densities differ by a <total derivative>. Under the <boundary conditions> used in the <action>, they have the same integral. The following identities establish the <integration-by-parts reduction of scalar operators>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\partial_\mu(\phi^3\partial^\mu\phi)&=3\phi^2(\partial\phi)^2+\phi^3\Box\phi,
&\mathcal O_2&\simeq-\frac13\mathcal O_3,\\
\partial_\mu(\phi\partial^\mu\Box\phi)&=\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\Box\phi+\phi\Box^2\phi,
&\mathcal O_4&\simeq-\mathcal O_5,\\
\partial_\mu(\partial^\mu\phi\Box\phi)&=(\Box\phi)^2+\partial_\mu\phi\partial^\mu\Box\phi,
&\mathcal O_5&\simeq-\mathcal O_6,\\
\partial_\mu(\partial_\nu\phi\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\phi)&=
(\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\phi)^2+\partial_\nu\phi\partial^\nu\Box\phi,
&\mathcal O_7&\simeq-\mathcal O_5.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\{\phi^6,\ \phi^3\Box\phi,\ (\Box\phi)^2\}}
$$
spans the <operator basis in effective field theory> before <equation-of-motion reduction of scalar operators>. These four independent relations reduce the seven representatives to three; the three sectors have different field or derivative content, so <integration by parts> cannot identify them further.

Use the sign convention $E(\phi)=\delta S_0/\delta\phi$ from part <1/b>, and let
$$
S_\eta[\phi]=S_0[\phi]+\eta\int d^4x\,f(\phi)E(\phi).
$$
In the <path integral>, make the local <field redefinition> $\phi_{\rm old}=\chi-\eta f(\chi)$. The <functional Taylor expansion> of the <action> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
S_0[\chi-\eta f(\chi)]
&=S_0[\chi]-\eta\int d^4x\,f(\chi)\frac{\delta S_0}{\delta\chi(x)}+O(\eta^2),\\
\eta\int d^4x\,f(\phi_{\rm old})E(\phi_{\rm old})
&=\eta\int d^4x\,f(\chi)E(\chi)+O(\eta^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
The two first-order terms cancel. Neglecting the <functional Jacobian> as permitted,
$$
\boxed{Z_\eta=\mathcal N\int\mathcal D\chi\,\exp\{iS_0[\chi]+O(\eta^2)\}.}
$$
Thus $fE$ is a <redundant operator> at first order, by the <equivalence theorem for field redefinitions>. The same argument works for a local derivative-dependent $f$, which is needed to remove $(\Box\phi)^2$. Insertions and sources must be transformed too: arbitrary untransformed <off-shell> <correlation functions> need not be equal.

**Sign qualification.** If instead $E_+=\Box\phi+m^2\phi+\lambda\phi^3/6$ is used, then $E_+=-\delta S_0/\delta\phi$. An added $+\eta fE_+$ is removed by $\phi_{\rm old}=\chi+\eta f(\chi)$. The minus shift requested in the source therefore fixes which sign is meant by its otherwise unspecified $E$.

Let $a=\lambda/6$. Within terms already suppressed by $\Lambda^{-2}$, the leading <equation of motion> gives $\Box\phi=-m^2\phi-a\phi^3$. Equality here means equivalence under a perturbative <field redefinition>, together with <integration by parts>, rather than a pointwise identity inside arbitrary <off-shell> <correlation functions>. Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\phi^3\Box\phi&\doteq-m^2\phi^4-\frac\lambda6\phi^6,\\
(\Box\phi)^2&\doteq m^4\phi^2+\frac{m^2\lambda}{3}\phi^4+\frac{\lambda^2}{36}\phi^6.
\end{aligned}
$$
For the second relation, factor the difference as $(\Box\phi-m^2\phi-a\phi^3)(\Box\phi+m^2\phi+a\phi^3)$, which is explicitly a <redundant operator>. If
$$
\Delta\mathcal L=\frac1{\Lambda^2}\left[c_1\phi^6+c_2\phi^3\Box\phi+c_3(\Box\phi)^2\right],
$$
the reduced correction is
$$
\Delta\mathcal L\doteq\frac1{\Lambda^2}\left[
c_3m^4\phi^2+\left(-c_2m^2+\frac{c_3m^2\lambda}{3}\right)\phi^4
+\left(c_1-\frac\lambda6c_2+\frac{\lambda^2}{36}c_3\right)\phi^6\right].
$$
The first two coefficients shift the already present <mass> and quartic <coupling constant (physics)>. After these shifts,
$$
\boxed{\text{one genuinely new dimension-six interaction remains: }\quad
\frac{c_{6,\rm red}}{\Lambda^2}\phi^6,\qquad
c_{6,\rm red}=c_1-\frac\lambda6c_2+\frac{\lambda^2}{36}c_3.}
$$
This is the <equation-of-motion reduction of scalar operators> through $O(\Lambda^{-2})$; terms of $O(\Lambda^{-4})$ are outside that truncation. The lower-dimensional shifts should not be silently dropped when $m\ne0$.

i\Pi_{\mu\nu}(q)=(-1)\int_k\operatorname{tr}\left[

The <Ward identity> implies $q^\mu\Pi_{\mu\nu}=0$. <Lorentz invariance> then gives $\Pi_{\mu\nu}=(q^2g_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu)\Pi$, so $g^{\mu\nu}\Pi_{\mu\nu}=(d-1)q^2\Pi$. The <gamma matrices> satisfy $\gamma_\mu\not k\gamma^\mu=(2-d)\not k$. Odd <traces> vanish, and therefore
$$
g^{\mu\nu}\operatorname{tr}[\gamma_\mu(\not k+m)\gamma_\nu(\not k+\not q+m)]
=4[-(d-2)k\cdot(k+q)+dm^2].
$$
Substituting gives the requested <scalar> coefficient:
$$
\boxed{\Pi(q^2)=\frac{4ie^2}{(d-1)q^2}\int_k
\frac{-(d-2)k\cdot(k+q)+dm^2}{[(k+q)^2-m^2+i0][k^2-m^2+i0]}.}
$$
To extract the <ultraviolet divergence> without introducing a <Feynman parameter>, set $A=(k+q)^2-m^2$, $B=k^2-m^2$ and use
$$
2k\cdot(k+q)=A+B+2m^2-q^2.
$$
Translation invariance of <dimensional regularization> gives $\int_kA^{-1}=\int_kB^{-1}=\mathcal I_1$. Consequently
$$
\Pi(q^2)=\frac{4ie^2}{(d-1)q^2}\left[-(d-2)\mathcal I_1+
\left(2m^2+\frac{d-2}{2}q^2\right)\mathcal I_2(q^2)\right].
$$
In the <pole> coefficient one may set $d=4$: the bracket becomes
$$
\frac{i}{16\pi^2\epsilon}[-2m^2+2m^2+q^2]
=\frac{iq^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
Thus the <ultraviolet pole of QED vacuum polarization> is
$$
\boxed{\Pi(q^2)\big|_{\rm UV}=-\frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon},\qquad
\Pi_{\mu\nu}\big|_{\rm UV}=-\frac{e^2}{12\pi^2\epsilon}(q^2g_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu).}
$$
The cancellation of the apparent $m^2/q^2$ divergence is essential: <gauge invariance> forbids a <photon> <mass> <counterterm>. The scalar result at $q^2=0$ is understood by continuation after this cancellation.

The middle panel in part <2/a> has an internal <photon> of <momentum> $k$ joining two points on a <fermion> line; the internal <fermion> has <momentum> $p-k$. It is a <one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram> with no <closed fermion loop sign>. In <Feynman gauge>,
$$
-i\Sigma(p)=\int_k(-ie\gamma^\alpha)
\frac{i(\not p-\not k+m)}{(p-k)^2-m^2+i0}
(-ie\gamma^\beta)\frac{-ig_{\alpha\beta}}{k^2+i0},
$$
or
$$
\boxed{\Sigma(p)=-ie^2\int_k
\frac{(2-d)(\not p-\not k)+dm}{[k^2+i0][(p-k)^2-m^2+i0]}.}
$$
The decomposition in the source gives $\operatorname{tr}(\not p\Sigma)=4p^2S(p^2)$. In particular,
$$
S(p^2)=-\frac{ie^2(d-2)}{p^2}\int_k
\frac{p\cdot k-p^2}{[k^2+i0][(p-k)^2-m^2+i0]}.
$$
For a direct <ultraviolet divergence> extraction, use a <Feynman parameter> $x$ multiplying the massive denominator, shift $r=k-xp$, and define $\Delta=xm^2-x(1-x)p^2$. Then $\not p-\not k=(1-x)\not p-\not r$ and
$$
\int_0^1dx\int_r\frac1{(r^2-\Delta+i0)^2}
\bigg|_{\rm UV}=\frac{i}{16\pi^2\epsilon}\int_0^1dx.
$$
Odd $r$ terms integrate to zero. Taking the <trace> explicitly supplies the weight $(2-d)(1-x)$, whose integral tends to $-1$. Hence
$$
\operatorname{tr}(\not p\Sigma)\big|_{\rm UV}
=-\frac{4e^2p^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon},\qquad
\boxed{S(p^2)\big|_{\rm UV}=-\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.}
$$
As a check on the same <fermion self-energy> calculation,
$$
\Sigma(p)\big|_{\rm UV}=\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}(-\not p+4m).
$$
This is the <ultraviolet pole of QED fermion self-energy>. It is safe to extract it at generic external <momentum>; an <on-shell> or massless limit must distinguish any <infrared divergence> from this <ultraviolet divergence>.

In the right panel of part <2/a>, $p$ enters the <fermion> line, $p'=p+q$ leaves it, and a virtual <photon> of <momentum> $k$ bridges the external <photon> vertex. Write $\Gamma^\mu=\gamma^\mu+\Lambda^\mu$ and $F_1=1+F_1^{(1)}+\cdots$. The one-loop <amplitude> is
$$
\begin{aligned}
-ie\Lambda^\mu={}&\int_k(-ie\gamma^\alpha)
\frac{i(\not p'-\not k+m)}{(p'-k)^2-m^2+i0}
(-ie\gamma^\mu)\frac{i(\not p-\not k+m)}{(p-k)^2-m^2+i0}
(-ie\gamma^\beta)\frac{-ig_{\alpha\beta}}{k^2+i0},\\
&\boxed{\Lambda^\mu=-ie^2\int_k
\frac{\gamma^\alpha(\not p'-\not k+m)\gamma^\mu(\not p-\not k+m)\gamma_\alpha}
{[k^2+i0][(p'-k)^2-m^2+i0][(p-k)^2-m^2+i0]}.}
\end{aligned}
$$
This <Feynman integral> has a logarithmic <superficial degree of divergence>. Its <ultraviolet pole> is independent of the external <momenta> and $m$, because every such numerator insertion or denominator correction lowers the large-$k$ degree. Preserve an auxiliary positive <mass> $M$ while extracting the <ultraviolet pole>; setting everything to zero would create a <scaleless integral> and conceal the cancellation of <ultraviolet> and <infrared divergences>.

The leading numerator is $\gamma^\alpha\not k\gamma^\mu\not k\gamma_\alpha$. Symmetric integration replaces $k_\rho k_\sigma$ by $g_{\rho\sigma}k^2/d$. Two contractions of <gamma matrices> then give
$$
\gamma^\alpha\not k\gamma^\mu\not k\gamma_\alpha
\ \longmapsto\ \frac{(d-2)^2}{d}k^2\gamma^\mu,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}\left[\gamma^\alpha\not k\gamma^\mu\not k\gamma_\alpha\gamma_\mu\right]
=4(d-2)^2k^2.
$$
For the <auxiliary-mass extraction of ultraviolet poles>,
$$
J_M=\int_k\frac{k^2}{(k^2-M^2+i0)^3}
=\int_k\left[\frac1{(k^2-M^2+i0)^2}+\frac{M^2}{(k^2-M^2+i0)^3}\right],
\qquad J_M\big|_{\rm UV}=\frac{i}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.
$$
The second term is <ultraviolet> convergent. Thus
$$
\frac1{4d}\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda^\mu\gamma_\mu)\big|_{\rm UV}
=-ie^2\frac{(d-2)^2}{d}J_M\big|_{\rm UV},
\qquad
\boxed{F_1^{(1)}(q^2)\big|_{\rm UV}=\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2\epsilon}.}
$$
The <ultraviolet pole of the QED vertex> is therefore $\Lambda^\mu|_{\rm UV}=e^2\gamma^\mu/(16\pi^2\epsilon)$. The Pauli structure has zero <trace> against $\gamma_\mu$ and no <ultraviolet pole> at this order.

**Form-factor qualification.** The two <electron electromagnetic vertex form factors> in the source describe matrix elements between external <on-shell> <Dirac spinors>. A general <off-shell> vertex has more structures, so the unprojected <trace> is not a projector for the complete finite on-shell $F_1$. It does correctly isolate the divergent local $\gamma^\mu$ coefficient, which is all that is required here.

In $d=4-2\epsilon$, the <modified minimal subtraction scheme> subtracts $\Delta_{\overline{\rm MS}}=1/\epsilon-\gamma_E+\log(4\pi)$ for each one-loop pole, with no further finite, kinematics-dependent subtraction. Equivalently, use a rescaled <renormalization scale> $\overline\mu^2=4\pi e^{-\gamma_E}\mu_R^2$ and subtract only $1/\epsilon$ in that convention. This differs from the <minimal subtraction scheme> by a fixed scale convention.

Let $C=e^2\Delta_{\overline{\rm MS}}/(16\pi^2)$. With the source's <counterterm> insertion signs, cancellation proceeds as follows. The vertex has $-ie\gamma^\mu C$ from the loop and $-ie\gamma^\mu\delta_1$ from its <counterterm>, giving $\delta_1=-C$. The kinetic part of the <fermion self-energy> insertion is $+i\not p C$ and its <counterterm> is $+i\not p\delta_2$, giving $\delta_2=-C$. Finally the <photon vacuum polarization> insertion is $-i(q^2g_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu)(4C/3)$ and its <counterterm> is $-i(q^2g_{\mu\nu}-q_\mu q_\nu)\delta_3$, giving
$$
\boxed{\delta_1=\delta_2=-\frac{e^2}{16\pi^2}\Delta_{\overline{\rm MS}},\qquad
\delta_3=-\frac{e^2}{12\pi^2}\Delta_{\overline{\rm MS}}.}
$$
Writing $Z_i=1+\delta_i$ therefore verifies the <Ward identity for QED renormalization constants>, $Z_1=Z_2$, at one loop. The <proper-vertex Ward-Takahashi identity in QED> independently gives $\Lambda^\mu(p,p)=-\partial\Sigma(p)/\partial p_\mu$, whose divergent part reproduces the opposite signs of $F_1^{(1)}$ and $S$ found above. The equality holds in a common <gauge fixing> and subtraction prescription; the individual constants $Z_1,Z_2$ are gauge dependent.

Start with the formally gauge-redundant <path integral> $Z=\operatorname{vol}(\mathcal G)^{-1}\int\mathcal DA\,e^{iS[A]}$. Use $A_\mu^\theta=A_\mu+\partial_\mu\theta$, and the generalized <Lorenz gauge condition> $F_\omega[A]=\partial_\mu A^\mu-\omega$. In a perturbative neighborhood where residual zero modes are removed by <boundary conditions>, insert the <Faddeev-Popov gauge-orbit identity>
$$
1=\Delta_{\rm FP}[A]\int\mathcal D\theta\,
\delta[F_\omega(A^\theta)],\qquad
\frac{\delta F_\omega(A^\theta)(x)}{\delta\theta(y)}
=\Box_x\delta^{(4)}(x-y).
$$
Thus $\Delta_{\rm FP}=\det\Box$. <Gauge invariance> lets the $\theta$ integration factor out and cancel $\operatorname{vol}(\mathcal G)$. Average over $\omega$ with the <Gaussian integral> weight $\exp[-i\int\omega^2/(2\xi)]$; the delta functional fixes $\omega=\partial\cdot A$ and gives
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm gf}=-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2.
$$
Represent the <functional determinant> by the independent <Grassmann fields> $\bar c,c$. A field-independent determinant sign can be absorbed in normalization, so a convenient convention is
$$
\det\Box\ \propto\ \int\mathcal D\bar c\mathcal Dc\,
\exp\left[-i\int d^4x\,\bar c\Box c\right].
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{Z=\mathcal N\int\mathcal DA\mathcal D\bar c\mathcal Dc\,
\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\left[-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
-\frac1{2\xi}(\partial\cdot A)^2-\bar c\Box c\right]\right\}.}
$$
The <Faddeev-Popov ghosts> are free and decouple because $\Box$ is independent of $A$. For a <non-Abelian gauge theory>, an infinitesimal <gauge transformation> gives $\delta A_\mu^a=D_\mu^{ab}\theta^b$, so the <Faddeev-Popov operator> is $\partial^\mu D_\mu^{ab}[A]$ and
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm gh}=-\bar c^a\partial^\mu D_\mu^{ab}[A]c^b.
$$
It contains a <ghost-gluon vertex>: these <Faddeev-Popov ghosts> do not decouple in covariant <gauge fixing> of <Lorenz gauge> type.

Choose the <gauge covariant derivative> $D_\mu=\partial_\mu+ieA_\mu$. Write $\rho=f+h$, $r=1+h/f$ and $B_\mu=\partial_\mu\pi/f+eA_\mu$. The <polar parametrization of the Abelian Higgs field> gives
$$
D_\mu\phi=\frac{e^{i\pi/f}}{\sqrt2}\left[\partial_\mu h+i(f+h)B_\mu\right],
\qquad
\phi^\dagger\phi-\frac{f^2}{2}=fh+\frac{h^2}{2}.
$$
Hence the full <Lagrangian density> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L=-\frac14F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
+\frac12(\partial h)^2+\frac12\left(1+\frac hf\right)^2(\partial_\mu\pi+efA_\mu)^2
-\frac\lambda2f^2h^2-\frac\lambda2fh^3-\frac\lambda8h^4.}
$$
Assume $f>0$ and $\lambda>0$, so the minimum has $|\phi|=f/\sqrt2$. In this convention the local <gauge symmetry> acts as
$$
\boxed{\phi\mapsto e^{i\alpha}\phi,\qquad
A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu-\frac1e\partial_\mu\alpha,\qquad
h\mapsto h,\qquad\pi\mapsto\pi+f\alpha.}
$$
The combination $\partial_\mu\pi+efA_\mu$, the radial field $h$, and the <gauge field strength> are each invariant, proving invariance of the displayed <Lagrangian density>.

This parametrization follows the circle of classical minima: $h$ measures distance away from the <vacuum manifold>, whereas $\pi$ moves along a <gauge orbit>. It separates the massive radial <Higgs mode> from the would-be <Goldstone boson> and makes its removal by the <Higgs mechanism> transparent. At quadratic order,
$$
\mathcal L_2=-\frac14F^2+\frac12(\partial h)^2-\frac12\lambda f^2h^2
+\frac12(\partial\pi)^2+efA\cdot\partial\pi+\frac12e^2f^2A^2,
\qquad m_h^2=\lambda f^2,\quad m_A^2=e^2f^2.
$$
The <polar parametrization of the Abelian Higgs field> is a local description near the chosen nonzero <vacuum expectation value>; it is singular at $\phi=0$ and is not a global coordinate system for configurations with zeros or winding.

<Unitary gauge> fixes the scalar phase to zero. Locally choose $\alpha=-\pi/f$ in part <3/b>; then $\pi'=0$ and $A'_\mu=A_\mu+\partial_\mu\pi/(ef)$. The remaining fields are the radial <Higgs mode> and a massive vector. Its quadratic <Lagrangian density> is $-F^2/4+m_A^2A^2/2$ and its <gauge-boson propagator> is
$$
\boxed{D^U_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i}{k^2-m_A^2+i0}
\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{m_A^2}\right).}
$$
For $k^2\ne0$, introduce the transverse and longitudinal <orthogonal projectors> $P^T_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-k_\mu k_\nu/k^2$ and $P^L_{\mu\nu}=k_\mu k_\nu/k^2$. Omitting the infinitesimal in this algebraic decomposition,
$$
D^U=-\frac{iP^T}{k^2-m_A^2}+\frac{iP^L}{m_A^2}.
$$
At large <momentum> in <Euclidean space>, the transverse coefficient falls as $k^{-2}$ but the longitudinal coefficient approaches a constant of order $m_A^{-2}$. Thus <power counting> applied directly to <unitary gauge> <Feynman diagrams> suggests increasingly severe <ultraviolet divergences>. This hides the <renormalizability> of physical <scattering amplitudes>; it does not disprove it. A finite-$\xi$ <R-xi gauge> exposes the better <propagator> scaling while preserving the fields needed to track the cancellations.

Let $m_A=ef$, $r=1+h/f$, and $H=\partial\cdot A-\xi m_A r^2\pi$, so the given $G=H/\sqrt\xi$ contributes $\mathcal L_{\rm gf}=-H^2/(2\xi)$. Its quadratic cross term is $+m_A\pi\partial\cdot A$, which cancels $m_AA\cdot\partial\pi$ from the scalar kinetic term after <integration by parts>. The free vector and would-be <Goldstone boson> <propagators> are therefore diagonal; $\pi$ has gauge-dependent <mass> squared $\xi m_A^2$.

The field-dependent factor $r^2$ cancels more than just the constant-coefficient mixing, but a residual derivative interaction remains. Indeed,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_{\rm mix}+\mathcal L_{\rm gf,cross}
&=m_A r^2(A\cdot\partial\pi+\pi\partial\cdot A)\\
&=\partial_\mu(m_A r^2A^\mu\pi)-m_A\pi A^\mu\partial_\mu r^2\\
&\simeq-2e\left(1+\frac hf\right)\pi A^\mu\partial_\mu h.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the <nonlinear R-xi gauge in polar Higgs variables> removes quadratic mixing; it does not remove every interaction containing $A$ and $\pi$.

Use the <gauge transformation> in part <3/b>. Since $h$ is invariant,
$$
\delta_\alpha H=-\frac1e\Box\alpha-\xi m_A r^2f\alpha
=-\frac1e[\Box+\xi m_A^2r^2]\alpha.
$$
The factor $-1/(e\sqrt\xi)$ in $\delta G/\delta\alpha$ is field independent and is absorbed into the <partition function> normalization. Exponentiating the remaining <Faddeev-Popov determinant> with the sign convention of part <3/a> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\rm gh}=-\bar c\left[\Box+\xi e^2(f+h)^2\right]c
=-\bar c(\Box+\xi m_A^2)c
-2\xi e^2fh\bar cc-\xi e^2h^2\bar cc.}
$$
The <ghost coupling in the Abelian Higgs model> therefore survives even though the <gauge group> is Abelian: these <Faddeev-Popov ghosts> interact with $h$ and **do not decouple for generic $\xi$**. The distinction from part <3/a> is the scalar dependence of the <gauge fixing> function. In the $\xi\to0$ <Landau gauge> limit these particular ghost interactions vanish.

After the quadratic mixing cancellation, the vector inverse quadratic operator is
$$
K=-(k^2-m_A^2)P^T-\left(\frac{k^2}{\xi}-m_A^2\right)P^L.
$$
Inverting its two <orthogonal projectors> and multiplying by $i$ gives
$$
\boxed{D^{(\xi)}_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i}{k^2-m_A^2+i0}
\left[g_{\mu\nu}-(1-\xi)\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{k^2-\xi m_A^2+i0}\right].}
$$
Equivalently $D^{(\xi)}=-iP^T/(k^2-m_A^2+i0)-i\xi P^L/(k^2-\xi m_A^2+i0)$. At fixed finite $\xi$, both sectors fall as $k^{-2}$ at large <momentum> in <Euclidean space>; $\xi=1$ gives $-ig_{\mu\nu}/(k^2-m_A^2+i0)$, and taking $\xi\to\infty$ at fixed $k$ recovers <unitary gauge>. The latter limit is not uniform in <ultraviolet> <momentum>.

The expected <power counting> conclusion is that this $k^{-2}$ behaviour, together with polynomial interactions of <mass dimension> at most four in Cartesian scalar variables and the <gauge symmetry> identities, permits <renormalization of spontaneously broken gauge theories> using a finite set of physical parameters. **The propagator alone is not sufficient to establish that claim in the polar variables of this question.** In fact, part <3/b> contains $h(\partial\pi)^2/f$ and $h^2(\partial\pi)^2/(2f^2)$, of dimensions five and six, and the nonlinear <gauge fixing> term contains $-\xi e^2h^4\pi^2/(2f^2)$, also of dimension six. Ordinary <power counting> is consequently not manifest in these variables.

For a representation where it is manifest, write $\phi=(f+H+i\chi)/\sqrt2$ and choose the usual polynomial <R-xi gauge> function $\partial\cdot A-\xi ef\chi$. It yields the same free vector <propagator>, cancels the quadratic mixing, and leaves only dimension-at-most-four interactions, including its <Faddeev-Popov ghosts>. This supplies the missing vertex condition in the source's proposed inference; the <equivalence theorem for field redefinitions> relates physical predictions near the <vacuum>. Good <propagator> falloff is the necessary ingredient illustrated by the requested formula, rather than a complete proof for arbitrary parametrizations or nonlinear choices of <gauge fixing>.
