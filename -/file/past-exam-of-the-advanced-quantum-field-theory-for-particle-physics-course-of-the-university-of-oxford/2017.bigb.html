=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

<Gauge invariance> and <power counting> permit the same local operators as in the original <Renormalizability>[renormalizable action]. With renormalized fields, a convenient <counterterm> parametrization is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ct}}=-\delta Z_\phi(D_\mu\phi)^\dagger D^\mu\phi-\delta M^2\phi^\dagger\phi-\frac{\mu^\epsilon\delta\lambda}{4}(\phi^\dagger\phi)^2-\frac{\delta Z_A}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}.}
$$
Here $D_\mu=\partial_\mu-i\mu^{\epsilon/2}eA_\mu$. Expanding the first term supplies kinetic, scalar-scalar-photon, and <Seagull vertex>[seagull] <counterterms> with the relative coefficients required by the <Ward identity>. Equivalently introduce separate <vertex> constants first and then impose that identity. For $\phi_0=Z_\phi^{1/2}\phi$, $A_0=Z_A^{1/2}A$, and $e_0=\mu^{\epsilon/2}Z_e e$, it gives $Z_eZ_A^{1/2}=1$. Thus <charge renormalization> can be represented through the <photon> field factor, rather than adding an independent gauge-noninvariant <Interaction term>[interaction].

The <mass> coefficient defined here is $\delta M^2=Z_\phi m_0^2-m^2$; it must be distinguished from $m_0^2-m^2$. <Gauge fixing> may require the corresponding <Gauge-fixing parameter>[gauge-parameter] redefinition, and <vacuum diagrams> can require an additive <Vacuum energy>[vacuum-energy] <counterterm>. No <photon> <mass> <counterterm> is allowed by the <Gauge symmetry>[unbroken gauge symmetry]. The <Quartic interaction>[quartic] <Scalar field>[scalar] <counterterm> remains necessary for the full theory even at a scale where the renormalized $\lambda$ is zero, because gauge <Interaction term>[interactions] generate <Scalar field>[scalar] <Four-point function>[four-point] divergences.

==== Solution

The <One-particle-irreducible Feynman diagram>[one-particle-irreducible] <Scalar field>[scalar] <two-point function> has three <Loop diagram>[one-loop] topologies: a scalar-photon exchange loop built from two <Interaction term>[cubic vertices]; a <Tadpole diagram>[photon tadpole] at one <seagull vertex>; and a <Tadpole diagram>[scalar tadpole] at one <Quartic interaction>[quartic] <vertex>. The last is absent when $\lambda=0$. Reducible chains of <self-energy> insertions belong to the <Dyson series>[Dyson expansion] and are not additional <Loop diagram>[one-loop] <self-energy> graphs. The <scalar-QED photon-tadpole symmetry factor> is $1/2$ because its two identical <photon> legs are joined; the oriented complex-scalar <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] has no such identical-real-line factor.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Route the <photon> <momentum> as $l$ and the internal <Scalar field>[scalar] <momentum> as $l+k$. The <Transverse projector of a vector field>[transverse projector] obeys $P_{\alpha\beta}l^\beta=0$, so the two <Interaction term>[cubic vertices] give
$$
(2k+l)^\alpha P_{\alpha\beta}(l)(2k+l)^\beta=4k^\alpha P_{\alpha\beta}(l)k^\beta.
$$
Multiplying the two <vertices> and propagators therefore gives
$$
\boxed{i\Pi_{\mathrm{ex}}(k)=4e^2\mu^\epsilon\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{k^2-(k\cdot l)^2/l^2}{l^2[(l+k)^2+m^2]}.}
$$
For the <Seagull vertex>[seagull], $g^{\alpha\beta}P_{\alpha\beta}=d-1$. Including the photon-loop <symmetry factor> gives instead
$$
\boxed{i\Pi_{\mathrm{sg}}=\frac12(-2ie^2)\mu^\epsilon\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{-i(d-1)}{l^2+m_\gamma^2}
=-(d-1)e^2\mu^\epsilon\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac1{l^2+m_\gamma^2}.}
$$
**The printed <Seagull vertex>[seagull] coefficient is twice this result.** The <vertex>'s factor two differentiates the two <photon> fields; closing them into one line requires the compensating <symmetry factor>. Direct expansion of the <action> gives the same check: the <Lagrangian density>[interaction density] $-e^2A^2\phi^\dagger\phi$ has one <photon> <Wick contraction>[contraction], yielding coefficient $-e^2$, not $-2e^2$. The identical-photon origin of the <vertex> factor is also explicit in the standard scalar-electrodynamics rules.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[§6.5.1, charged scalars: the seagull vertex factor two comes from the two identical photon fields]{ref} This discrepancy does not affect the massless-photon limit in part d or the <Ultraviolet divergence>[ultraviolet pole] in part f.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Apply the <Dimensional regularization>[dimensionally regulated] <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] integral before removing the <Infrared regularization>[infrared regulator]:
$$
I_\gamma(m_\gamma)=\mu^\epsilon\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac1{l^2+m_\gamma^2}
=\frac{i\mu^\epsilon}{(4\pi)^{2-\epsilon/2}}\Gamma\left(-1+\frac\epsilon2\right)(m_\gamma^2)^{1-\epsilon/2}.
$$
At fixed regulator $0<\operatorname{Re}\epsilon<2$ away from <Gamma function>[gamma-function poles], the power of $m_\gamma^2$ has positive real part. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\lim_{m_\gamma\to0}I_\gamma(m_\gamma)=0.}
$$
Both the correctly weighted <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] and the source's doubled version vanish. This is the near-four-dimensional limit appropriate to the question; the order of limits is important. Equivalently the exactly massless <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] is a <scaleless integral> and is zero in <dimensional regularization>. The statement is not valid for every arbitrary fixed dimension: for example, $\epsilon=5/2$ gives a nonzero prefactor and growth as $m_\gamma^{-1/2}$. One must keep the regulator in its stated near-four-dimensional domain.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

First remove the <Projection operator>[projector] denominator explicitly:
$$
i\Pi_{\mathrm{ex}}=4e^2\mu^\epsilon\int\frac{d^dl}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{l^2k^2-(l\cdot k)^2}{(l^2)^2[(l+k)^2+m^2]}.
$$
Let $A=(l+k)^2+m^2$ and $B=l^2$. The <Feynman parameter for a simple and a squared propagator> follows by differentiating the elementary two-denominator <Feynman parameter>[Feynman formula] with respect to $B$ gives
$$
\boxed{\frac1{AB^2}=2\int_0^1dx\frac{1-x}{[xA+(1-x)B]^3}.}
$$
Shift $q=l+xk$. Then $xA+(1-x)B=q^2+D$, with $D=x(1-x)k^2+xm^2$. The numerator is unchanged by this parallel shift:
$$
l^2k^2-(l\cdot k)^2=q^2k^2-(q\cdot k)^2.
$$
Therefore the correctly combined integral is
$$
\boxed{i\Pi_{\mathrm{ex}}=8e^2\mu^\epsilon\int_0^1(1-x)dx\int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{q^2k^2-(q\cdot k)^2}{(q^2+D)^3}.}
$$
**The printed weight $x/2$ is not compatible with this denominator and prefactor.** Exchanging $x$ with $1-x$ changes the <Mass>[mass term] in $D$ as well and cannot supply the missing <normalization>. A concrete counterexample is $m=0$ with nonzero spacelike <Four-momentum>[external momentum] and $2<d<4$: after <Wick rotation>, the remaining integral is nonzero and symmetric under $x\leftrightarrow1-x$. The source's $4e^2\int(x/2)dx$ expression is then exactly one quarter of the correct expression. Thus the claimed equality cannot be imposed when computing the pole.

=== f
{scope}

==== Solution

<Lorentz covariance>[Lorentz-covariant] <Lorentz-covariant rank-two loop reduction>[tensor integration] replaces $q_\mu q_\nu$ by $g_{\mu\nu}q^2/d$. The numerator consequently contributes $(d-1)k^2q^2/d$. The supplied master integral with powers $a=1,b=3$ gives
$$
\int\frac{d^dq}{(2\pi)^d}\frac{q^2}{(q^2+D)^3}
=\frac{i}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\frac d4\Gamma(\epsilon/2)D^{-\epsilon/2}.
$$
Inserting the corrected parameter integral gives
$$
i\Pi_{\mathrm{ex}}=\frac{2i(d-1)e^2\mu^\epsilon k^2}{(4\pi)^{d/2}}\Gamma(\epsilon/2)\int_0^1(1-x)D^{-\epsilon/2}dx.
$$
The pole uses $\Gamma(\epsilon/2)=2/\epsilon+O(1)$, $D^{-\epsilon/2}=1+O(\epsilon)$ and $\int_0^1(1-x)dx=1/2$. Thus
$$
\boxed{i\Pi_{\mathrm{loop,div}}=i\frac{3e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon}k^2.}
$$
There is no momentum-independent pole at $\lambda=0$ in this transverse gauge after removing the <Tadpole diagram>[photon tadpole]. The <Two-point function>[two-point] <counterterm> insertion is $-i(\delta Z_\phi k^2+\delta M^2)$, so <Minimal subtraction scheme>[minimal subtraction] requires
$$
\boxed{\delta Z_\phi=\frac{3e^2}{8\pi^2\epsilon},\qquad \delta M^2=0\qquad(d=4-\epsilon).}
$$
These coefficients cancel the actual <Loop diagram>[one-loop] insertion derived from the <action>. Applying the misprinted weight in part e would give a wave-function pole four times too small. In a $d=4-2\varepsilon$ convention the same answer is $\delta Z_\phi=3e^2/(16\pi^2\varepsilon)$. Finally, $\delta M^2=0$ does not mean that the bare <Mass>[mass parameter] is unchanged: $Z_\phi m_0^2=m^2$ gives $m_0^2-m^2=-m^2\delta Z_\phi+O(e^4)$. <Photon>, charge and <Quartic interaction>[quartic] <counterterms> require their respective <Green functions> and cannot be determined from this <Scalar field>[scalar] <two-point function> alone.

===== Solution

Use the source term $\int d^4x[J\phi+\bar\varsigma\psi+\bar\psi\varsigma]$ and left <Grassmann derivative>[Grassmann functional derivatives]. Differentiating its exponential inserts fields according to
$$
\boxed{\phi\ \longleftrightarrow\ \frac1i\frac\delta{\delta J},\qquad
\bar\psi\ \longleftrightarrow\ i\frac\delta{\delta\varsigma},\qquad
\psi\ \longleftrightarrow\ \frac1i\frac\delta{\delta\bar\varsigma}.}
$$
The sign for $\bar\psi$ follows because a <Left Grassmann derivative>[left derivative] with respect to $\varsigma$ must cross $\bar\psi$. Keep the order $\bar\psi\gamma_5\psi\phi$ when replacing these fields. Expand $e^{iS_{\mathrm{int}}}$ in the original <path integral>; each field monomial can now be generated by these ordered derivatives acting on the <Generating functional>[free functional]. Resumming gives
$$
\boxed{Z[J,\bar\varsigma,\varsigma]=\mathcal N\exp\left\{i\int d^4x\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}\left(\frac1i\frac\delta{\delta J},\ i\frac\delta{\delta\varsigma},\ \frac1i\frac\delta{\delta\bar\varsigma}\right)\right\}Z_0[J,\bar\varsigma,\varsigma].}
$$
The arguments refer respectively to $\phi,\bar\psi,\psi$ at $x$. The <Generating functional>[free functional] arises by performing the free <Gaussian integral>[Gaussian] field integrals, and $\mathcal N$ may be chosen so that $Z[0]=1$.

**No additional field-integration measure remains in this differential-operator formula.** Such a measure is printed outside the operator in the source even though $Z_0$ has already integrated out those fields. At $\lambda=0$, that literal expression would contain $\int\mathcal D\phi\mathcal D\bar\psi\mathcal D\psi\,1$ rather than simply $Z_0$, which is not the <Generating functional>[free generating functional]. Also, when $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}$ denotes a density, the spacetime integral in the exponent is required.

{scope}

===== Solution

At first order the <Dyson series>[Dyson expansion] inserts $iS_{\mathrm{int}}=\lambda\int d^4x\,\bar\psi_\alpha(x)(\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}\psi_\beta(x)\phi(x)$. Contract the <scalar field> at the <vertex> with the external <Scalar field>[scalar]. The four remaining <fermion> fields have two admissible pairings: both external <fermions> attach to the <vertex>, or the two <vertex> <fermions> contract with each other while the external pair contracts freely. <Wick's theorem> gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\langle T\phi(y)\psi_\rho(z)\bar\psi_\sigma(w)\rangle
={}&\lambda(\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}\int d^4x\,i\Delta_F(y-x)\Bigl[
 iS_F(x-w)_{\beta\sigma}\,iS_F(z-x)_{\rho\alpha}\\
&\hspace{40pt}-iS_F(0)_{\beta\alpha}\,iS_F(z-w)_{\rho\sigma}\Bigr]+O(\lambda^3).
\end{aligned}}
$$
The minus sign in the second term is the <Fermionic sign>[fermion sign] of the closed <Wick contraction>[contraction]: $\langle T\bar\psi_\alpha(x)\psi_\beta(x)\rangle=-iS_F(0)_{\beta\alpha}$. The first term is connected; the second is a scalar-attached <fermion> <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] multiplied by a disconnected free external <Dirac propagator>[fermion propagator]. The full time-ordered expectation includes both topologies, whereas the connected <generating functional> retains only the first. There is no order-$\lambda^2$ contribution in this pure Yukawa expansion because the total number of <scalar fields> would be odd.

For a <Parity>[parity-preserving] free propagator, the <Tadpole diagram>[tadpole] coefficient is $\operatorname{tr}[\gamma_5S_F(0)]$, whose integrand contains only $\operatorname{tr}\gamma_5$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\gamma_5\gamma^\mu)$. Both vanish. Thus the second term is a legitimate formal <Wick contraction> but is zero with a compatible regulator; <normal ordering> the <Interaction term>[interaction] removes it from the outset.

===== Solution

The <different-flavour quark annihilation into a massive pair> has only an $s$-channel <gluon> graph at tree level when the two <Quark flavour>[quark flavours] differ. Its <Scattering amplitude>[amplitude] contains one incoming and one outgoing <Color charge>[colour current], with common magnitude factor $g_s^2/s$. <On shell>[On-shell] current conservation removes the gauge-parameter-dependent longitudinal part of the <gluon> propagator. To display the <Dirac field>[Dirac] signs transparently, evaluate <Gamma-matrix trace>[spin traces] with the auxiliary <Minkowski metric>[mostly-minus] <Minkowski metric>[metric] $\eta=-g$ and $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}$; the invariants $s,t$ are then the usual positive-energy <Mandelstam variables>[Mandelstam invariants], numerically identical to those defined in the paper.

The incoming <Color factor>[colour average] and outgoing <Color factor>[colour sum] give
$$
C_{\mathrm{col}}=\frac1{3^2}\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)
=\frac19\frac14\delta^{ab}\delta^{ab}=\frac29.
$$
Averaging over the two <spins> of each incident <particle> supplies an additional $1/4$. The physical <spin sums> are $\sum u\bar u=\not p+M$ and $\sum v\bar v=\not p-M$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\overline{|T|^2}=\frac{g_s^4}{18s^2}
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma_\mu(\not p_2-M)\gamma_\alpha(\not p_1+M)]\,
\operatorname{tr}[\gamma^\mu\not k_1\gamma^\alpha\not k_2].}
$$
The coefficient is equivalently $8\pi^2\alpha_s^2/(9s^2)$, with $\alpha_s=g_s^2/(4\pi)$.

**The two top projectors cannot both contain $-M$.** The printed expression has that error. It is not repaired by changing the <Minkowski metric>[metric] convention consistently: a <particle> and <antiparticle> still require the appropriate distinct projectors. In the auxiliary convention, its outgoing <tensor> would obey $q_\mu H_{\mathrm{printed}}^{\mu\alpha}=8M^2q^\alpha$, rather than zero for $q=p_1+p_2$. At <Particle-production threshold>[threshold] it gives a zero contracted <trace>, while the correctly averaged vector-current production <Scattering amplitude>[amplitude] squared has a nonzero limit $4g_s^4/9$. The <Lorentz-invariant phase space>[phase-space] <cross section> still vanishes at <Particle-production threshold>[threshold] because the final <velocity> tends to zero. The <Color charge>[colour] and <spin> prefactor above is consequently the prefactor for the corrected <trace>; no overall constant can make the misprinted massive <trace> the physical answer for all kinematics.

{scope}

===== Solution

Continue with $\eta=-g$ and dot products in that auxiliary <Minkowski metric>[metric]. Odd <Gamma-matrix trace>[gamma traces] vanish. The two current <tensors> are
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{\mu\alpha}&=4[p_{2\mu}p_{1\alpha}+p_{2\alpha}p_{1\mu}-\eta_{\mu\alpha}(p_1\cdot p_2+M^2)],\\
L^{\mu\alpha}&=4[k_1^\mu k_2^\alpha+k_1^\alpha k_2^\mu-\eta^{\mu\alpha}(k_1\cdot k_2)].
\end{aligned}
$$
Contracting gives
$$
H_{\mu\alpha}L^{\mu\alpha}=32[(p_1\cdot k_1)(p_2\cdot k_2)+(p_1\cdot k_2)(p_2\cdot k_1)+M^2(k_1\cdot k_2)].
$$
Let $u=2M^2-s-t$. The <Mass shell>[on-shell products] are $p_1\cdot k_1=p_2\cdot k_2=(M^2-t)/2$, $p_1\cdot k_2=p_2\cdot k_1=(M^2-u)/2$, and $k_1\cdot k_2=s/2$. Substitution yields
$$
\boxed{\overline{|T|^2}=\frac{4g_s^4}{9s^2}[(M^2-t)^2+(M^2-u)^2+2M^2s]
=\frac{64\pi^2\alpha_s^2}{9s^2}[(M^2-t)^2+(s+t-M^2)^2+2M^2s].}
$$
The result is already averaged over incoming <Color charge>[colours] and <spins> and summed over outgoing ones. For $M\to0$, it becomes $4g_s^4(t^2+u^2)/(9s^2)$.

An additional check uses the <Center-of-momentum frame>[centre-of-mass] angle and <velocity> $\beta_t=\sqrt{1-4M^2/s}$:
$$
\overline{|T|^2}=\frac{2g_s^4}{9}\left[1+\cos^2\theta+\frac{4M^2}{s}\sin^2\theta\right].
$$
It tends to $4g_s^4/9$ at <Particle-production threshold>[threshold] and is positive throughout the physical region. Keeping both printed minus signs would instead give $4g_s^4[(M^2-t)^2+(M^2-u)^2-2M^2s]/(9s^2)$, displaying the incorrect <mass> contribution explicitly.

The gauge-invariant <Path integral>[functional integral] counts every <gauge orbit> repeatedly. Insert a gauge-orbit identity
$$
1=\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}[A,\phi]\int\mathcal D\theta\,\delta(G[A^\theta,\phi^\theta]-f),\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{FP}}=\det\left(\frac{\delta G(x)}{\delta\theta(y)}\right).
$$
Dividing by the <Gauge group>[gauge-group] volume and averaging $f$ with a <Gaussian integral>[Gaussian] weight produces the term $-G^2/(2\xi)$. The <determinant> is represented by a pair of independent <Faddeev-Popov ghost fields>[anticommuting scalar fields] $c,\bar c$, giving the prescribed <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost] density $-\bar c\mathcal M c$, where $\mathcal M=\delta G/\delta\theta$. The <Grassmann integral> gives a <determinant> rather than the inverse <determinant> of a <Commutator>[commuting] <Gaussian integral>; field-independent constants and overall <determinant> signs are absorbed in <normalization>.

Although the <gauge group> is <Abelian group>[Abelian], the <determinant> here is field dependent because the <Gauge fixing>[gauge condition] contains the <Scalar field>[scalar] fluctuation $b$. Thus <Abelian group>[Abelian] <Faddeev-Popov ghosts>[ghosts] need not decouple in a broken-phase $R_\xi$ gauge. The fields implement gauge-orbit counting and are not physical external <particles>.

===== Solution

For the <ghost coupling in a linear Cartesian Abelian Higgs gauge>, the common factor in the <Scalar field>[scalar] parametrization cancels from the <gauge transformation>. Separating real and imaginary parts gives
$$
\delta h=g\theta b,\qquad \delta b=-g\theta(v+h),\qquad \delta(\partial\cdot A)=-\Box\theta.
$$
Consequently
$$
\mathcal M=-\Box+\xi g^2v(v+h),\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\mathrm{gh}}=\bar c\Box c-\xi g^2v^2\bar cc-\xi g^2v h\bar cc.}
$$
For the variables and <Gauge fixing>[gauge function] actually printed, the <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost mass] and <ghost coupling in a linear Cartesian Abelian Higgs gauge>[Higgs-ghost] <vertex> are therefore
$$
\boxed{m_c^2=\xi g^2v^2,\qquad V_{h\bar cc}=-i\xi g^2v.}
$$
For the usual nonnegative <Gauge-fixing parameter>[gauge parameter], $m_c=\sqrt\xi\,g|v|$. The momentum-space <ghost propagator> is $-i/(k^2+m_c^2-i0)$ in this kinetic convention. There is no direct <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost-photon] <vertex> or ghost-$b$ <vertex>. At $\xi=0$ the <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost] becomes massless and decouples.

**The <Scalar field>[scalar] prefactor $1/4$ is incompatible with the stated <Canonical field normalization>[canonical] broken-vacuum interpretation.** At $h=b=0$ it gives $|\phi|^2=v^2/16$, whereas the <Scalar potential>[potential] is minimized at $|\phi|^2=v^2/2$. In fact the printed parametrization gives
$$
\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial h}\right|_{h=b=0}=-\frac{7\lambda v^3}{256}\ne0.
$$
Its $h,b$ <kinetic terms> have coefficient $-1/16$, not $-1/2$, and its vector <Mass>[mass parameter] is $g^2v^2/8$. The given <Gauge fixing>[gauge function] consequently does not cancel the vector-scalar mixing in those literal variables. The <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost] operator computed above nevertheless follows algebraically from that <Gauge fixing>[gauge function] without changing the printed fields.

With a <canonical Abelian Higgs shift> $\phi=(v+h+ib)/\sqrt2$, the expansion is about a true <minimum>, the vector <mass> is $m_A=g|v|$, the <gauge fixing> cancels the mixing, and the same <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost] formulas acquire the usual interpretation $m_c^2=\xi m_A^2$. For the literal $1/4$ parametrization, a <Canonical field normalization>[canonical] fluctuation is $h_{\mathrm{can}}=h/\sqrt8$, so its <Faddeev-Popov ghost>[ghost] <vertex> would instead be $-i\sqrt8\,\xi g^2v$; the non-vacuum background problem still remains. These two interpretations must not be silently mixed.

===== Solution

<Gauge invariance> of the <scalar potential> implies the infinitesimal identity
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_i}(T^a)_{ij}\phi_j=0.
$$
The irrelevant common factor $i$ from the real representation's infinitesimal rotation has been divided out. Differentiate with respect to $\phi_k$ and evaluate at the <Stationary point>[stationary] <Vacuum state>[vacuum] $v$:
$$
\left.\frac{\partial^2V}{\partial\phi_k\partial\phi_i}\right|_v(T^a)_{ij}v_j
+\left.\frac{\partial V}{\partial\phi_i}\right|_v(T^a)_{ik}=0.
$$
The second term vanishes by <Stationary point>[stationarity], leaving
$$
\boxed{(M_\phi^2)_{ki}(T^av)_i=0.}
$$
Whenever $T^av\ne0$, this is a nonzero vector in the <kernel> of the <Scalar field>[scalar] <Mass matrix>[mass-squared matrix], so it is a zero-eigenvalue direction. It is tangent to the degenerate <Vacuum state>[vacuum] orbit. In a global theory it is a <Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix>[Goldstone direction]; in the gauged theory the corresponding would-be <Goldstone boson>[Goldstone field] supplies a <Longitudinal polarization of a massive vector boson>[longitudinal polarization] of a massive vector.

For the specific <Scalar potential>[potential], <Stationary point>[stationarity] at a nonzero <Vacuum state>[vacuum] gives $v^2=m^2/(4\lambda)$, assuming $m^2>0$ and $\lambda>0$. Its <Hessian matrix>[Hessian] is
$$
(M_\phi^2)_{ij}=(-m^2+4\lambda v^2)\delta_{ij}+8\lambda v_iv_j=8\lambda v_iv_j.
$$
The antisymmetry of the <Adjoint representation>[triplet generators] gives $v_i(T^av)_i=0$, verifying the claimed <kernel> directly.

===== Solution

Since the <Weak hypercharge>[hypercharge] is zero, <Electric charge>[electric neutrality] means $T^3v=0$. Choose the real-triplet <Vacuum state>[vacuum] $v_i=v\delta_{i3}$, with $v^2=m^2/(4\lambda)$. With <Canonical field normalization>[canonical] real-scalar kinetic density $-\tfrac12(D_\mu\Phi)^T D^\mu\Phi$, insert this <Vacuum state>[vacuum] into the <covariant derivative>. The <Weak hypercharge>[hypercharge] field $B_\mu$ has no coupling to this <Scalar field>[scalar], while only $T^1v,T^2v$ are nonzero. Thus
$$
\boxed{M_V^2=g^2v^2\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0,0)\quad\text{in }(W^1,W^2,W^3,B).}
$$
The <Electric charge>[charged] combinations $W^\pm=(W^1\mp iW^2)/\sqrt2$ have
$$
\boxed{m_{W^\pm}^2=g^2v^2=\frac{g^2m^2}{4\lambda},\qquad m_{W^3}=m_B=0.}
$$
Both $T^3$ and the independent <Weak hypercharge>[hypercharge] generator annihilate the <Vacuum state>[vacuum]. The connected <Stabilizer>[unbroken subgroup] is therefore
$$
\boxed{U(1)_{T^3}\times U(1)_Y.}
$$
The electromagnetic generator $Q=T^3+Y/2$ belongs to it, but it is only one linear combination of two surviving neutral generators. This model has two massless <Gauge field>[neutral gauge fields], rather than the single massless <photon> and massive neutral vector of the usual scalar-doublet electroweak model. A neutral-field rotation does not create a massive <eigenvalue> from this zero block.

The two transverse triplet components are eaten, leaving one real, electrically neutral radial <Scalar field>[scalar] $H=\phi_3-v$. The radial <eigenvalue> of the <Hessian matrix>[Hessian] found in part i is
$$
\boxed{m_H^2=8\lambda v^2=2m^2,\qquad m_H=\sqrt2\,m.}
$$
Indeed the radial <Scalar potential>[potential] is a constant plus $4\lambda v^2H^2+4\lambda vH^3+\lambda H^4$, fixing the factor of two between its quadratic coefficient and its <Mass>[mass squared]. The physical degree-of-freedom count is preserved: three real <Scalar fields>[scalars] plus four massless vectors give $3+4\times2=11$, while one <Scalar field>[scalar], two massive vectors and two massless vectors give $1+2\times3+2\times2=11$.
