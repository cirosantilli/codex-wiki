= Solution
{parent=a}

Read the arrows upwards through the central part of the diagram. The lower <anyon fusion tree> first splits $a$ into $b,c$, then $b$ into $d,j$ and $c$ into $h,e$. The upper <anyon fusion tree> combines $d,j$ into $f$, $h,e$ into $g$, and finally $f,g$ into $a$. The two interior crossings are not vertices. Thus admissibility requires the six <anyon fusion multiplicities>
$$
N^a_{bc}N^b_{dj}N^c_{he}N^f_{dj}N^g_{he}N^a_{fg}=1.
$$
If any factor vanishes, the diagram has zero value.

Make an <anyon vertex gauge transformation> by multiplying each splitting vertex $z\to x,y$ by an independent <wave phase> $u^{xy}_z$ and its adjoint fusion vertex by $(u^{xy}_z)^{-1}$. The diagram changes by
$$
G=\frac{u^{bc}_a u^{dj}_b u^{he}_c}{u^{fg}_a u^{dj}_f u^{he}_g}.
$$
Therefore the label-matching condition for cancellation of all <anyon vertex gauge transformation> factors is
$$
\boxed{b=f,\qquad c=g,}
$$
with $a,d,e,h,j$ otherwise unrestricted apart from the <anyon fusion rules>. This statement also allows coincident labels: the three ordered vertex triples in the numerator must equal those in the denominator as multisets; matching the triples in any of the six possible orders still forces $b=f,c=g$. Conversely, these equalities visibly give $G=1$.

Closure of the drawing alone does not guarantee invariance under an <anyon vertex gauge transformation>: unmatched fusion and splitting vertices retain their independently chosen basis phases. A zero value is, of course, invariant regardless of phases; the boxed condition describes cancellation of the vertex factors themselves.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Write $c_{u,v}$ for <braiding> and $M_{u,v}=c_{v,u}c_{u,v}$ for <anyon monodromy>. Transparency says $M_{a,x}=1$ for every $x$. The <hexagon identities for braiding> express <braiding> a composite $a\otimes b$ past $x$ as successive <braidings> of its two factors past $x$. If $a,b$ are both <transparent anyons>, move $x$ around $b$ and then around $a$; both full moves are identities, hence
$$
M_{a\otimes b,x}=1.
$$
This is the <fusion closure of transparent anyons> at the level of the composite object.

For completeness, let $i:c\to a\otimes b$ and $p:a\otimes b\to c$ be an inclusion and projection for an allowed <anyon fusion> summand, with $pi=1_c$. Naturality of <braiding> gives
$$
M_{c,x}=(p\otimes1_x)M_{a\otimes b,x}(i\otimes1_x)=1_{c\otimes x}.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{a,b\text{ transparent and }N^c_{ab}>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c\text{ transparent}.}
$$
The <vacuum anyon> belongs to this collection, and taking duals preserves transparency, so it is also closed under <antianyons>.

There is a further restriction on the <spin of a transparent anyon>. Apply the <anyon balancing identity> to the <vacuum anyon> channel of $a\times\bar a$:
$$
1=R^{a\bar a}_1R^{\bar a a}_1=\frac{\theta_1}{\theta_a\theta_{\bar a}}=\frac1{\theta_a^2}.
$$
Here ribbon duality gives $\theta_{\bar a}=\theta_a$ and $\theta_1=1$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\theta_a=+1\ \text{or}\ -1.}
$$
Their <topological spins> are those of <bosons> or <fermions>. This does not imply $d_a=1$: a <transparent anyon> can have several <anyon fusion> channels, with nontrivial exchange action within them, even though every full <anyon monodromy> is trivial. If the theory is additionally a <modular tensor category>, the only <transparent anyon> is the <vacuum anyon>; Question 1 need not impose that extra hypothesis.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

The two crossings of $j,h$ make their full <anyon monodromy>. For a <transparent anyon> $h$, this is the identity, so that pair of crossings can be removed together. No single-crossing exchange phase, and no <topological spin> from a twist, is introduced.

After this removal the upper and lower <anyon fusion trees> differ only in their intermediate labels. In the <physics normalization of anyon diagrams>, successive split-fuse bubbles give the <Kronecker deltas> $\delta_{bf}$ and $\delta_{cg}$. The remaining closed normalized state has norm one. Hence, for the admissible labelings of <1/a>,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal A_{bc;fg}=\delta_{bf}\delta_{cg}.}
$$
In particular, every admissible diagram satisfying the label matching in <1/a> evaluates to $1$. There is no factor $d_a$ here: the source explicitly permits the <physics normalization of anyon diagrams>, in which a closed normalized state has value $1$.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}

Use an <anyon fusion tree> in which the two crossing charges combine first. With external charges in the order $d,j,h,e$, define the normalized trees
$$
|T_{bc}\rangle=|((d j)_b(h e)_c)_a\rangle,\qquad
|X_{xy}\rangle=|(d((j h)_x e)_y)_a\rangle.
$$
The desired value is the matrix element of <anyon monodromy> between $|T_{bc}\rangle$ and $|T_{fg}\rangle$. Their closure is understood in the <physics normalization of anyon diagrams>.

First apply the <anyon F-matrix> to the three charges $d,j,c$, and then an inverse <anyon F-matrix> to $j,h,e$. With the source convention $|((uv)_r w)_t\rangle=\sum_s[F^{uvw}_t]_{rs}|(u(vw)_s)_t\rangle$, this gives
$$
|T_{bc}\rangle=\sum_{x,y}U_{bc;xy}|X_{xy}\rangle,\qquad
U_{bc;xy}=[F^{djc}_a]_{by}\,[F^{jhe}_y]^*_{xc}.
$$
Only allowed <anyon fusion> channels enter the sums: in the second tree these require $N^x_{jh}N^y_{xe}N^a_{dy}=1$. The complex conjugate in $U$ comes from the inverse change of parentheses, not from reversing a crossing.

\Image[2025-fusion-recoupling.png]
{title=Recoupling exposes the inner pair on which the full braid acts diagonally}

Reading the actual overcrossings from bottom to top gives two positive exchanges in the source's <anyon R-matrix> convention. Their product is
$$
m_x=R^{jh}_xR^{hj}_x.
$$
The order of $h,j$ is restored after the second exchange. In the $X$ basis this <anyon monodromy> is diagonal, so
$$
\boxed{\mathcal A_{bc;fg}=\sum_{x,y}U_{bc;xy}\,R^{jh}_xR^{hj}_x\,U^*_{fg;xy}.}
$$
Equivalently, entirely in terms of <anyon F-matrices> and <anyon R-matrices>,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal A_{bc;fg}=\sum_{x,y}[F^{djc}_a]_{by}[F^{jhe}_y]^*_{xc}\,R^{jh}_xR^{hj}_x\,[F^{djg}_a]^*_{fy}[F^{jhe}_y]_{xg}.}
$$
This expression is also meaningful for $b\ne f$ or $c\ne g$: it then carries the unmatched <anyon vertex gauge transformation> factors identified in <1/a>.

To check the transparent limit, put $m_x=1$ in every allowed channel. The <unitary matrix> identity for $F^{jhe}_y$ first gives
$$
\sum_x[F^{jhe}_y]^*_{xc}[F^{jhe}_y]_{xg}=\delta_{cg}.
$$
Once $c=g$, the <unitary matrix> identity for $F^{djc}_a$ gives
$$
\mathcal A_{bc;fg}=\delta_{cg}\sum_y[F^{djc}_a]_{by}[F^{djc}_a]^*_{fy}=\boxed{\delta_{cg}\delta_{bf}},
$$
as in <1/c>. The same cancellation proves that the recoupling expression is an identity operator whenever the full <anyon monodromy> is trivial, including when the individual exchanges have fermionic signs.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Cut one nonseparating circle on each handle of the closed <orientable surface> $\Sigma_g$ of <genus> $g$. The cut surface is a <sphere> with $2g$ boundary circles. Gluing a pair back together requires an <anyon> label $a_i$ on one boundary and the <antianyon> label $\bar a_i$ on the oppositely oriented boundary. Sum over these labels independently for the $g$ handles.

For a fixed labeling, the <dimension> of the resulting <anyon fusion space> on the <sphere>, with total <vacuum anyon> charge, is
$$
\left[N_{a_1}N_{\bar a_1}\cdots N_{a_g}N_{\bar a_g}\right]_{11}.
$$
The gluing rule of the <topological quantum field theory> therefore gives the <handle-cut formula for anyon ground-state degeneracy>:
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{GSD}(\Sigma_g)=\sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_g}\left[N_{a_1}N_{\bar a_1}\cdots N_{a_g}N_{\bar a_g}\right]_{11}=\left[\left(\sum_aN_aN_{\bar a}\right)^g\right]_{11}.}
$$
Here $1$ denotes the <vacuum anyon>, and the <anyon fusion matrices> have entries $(N_b)_{ac}=N^c_{ab}$, as in the source. At $g=0$, the empty product is the identity <matrix>, giving one <ground state>. At $g=1$, $(N_aN_{\bar a})_{11}=1$ for each simple charge, so the <ground-state degeneracy> of the <torus> is the number of <anyon> types.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Put $K=\sum_aN_aN_{\bar a}$. The <Verlinde formula> diagonalizes every <anyon fusion matrix> using the same <modular S-matrix>:
$$
N_a=S\,\operatorname{diag}_x\left(\frac{S_{ax}}{S_{1x}}\right)S^\dagger.
$$
Because $S_{\bar a x}=S_{ax}^*$, the <eigenvalue> of $K$ in column $x$ is
$$
\kappa_x=\sum_a\frac{S_{ax}S_{\bar a x}}{S_{1x}^2}=\frac{\sum_a|S_{ax}|^2}{S_{1x}^2}=\frac1{S_{1x}^2}.
$$
The last equality uses <unitary matrix> orthogonality. Therefore
$$
(K^g)_{11}=\sum_x|S_{1x}|^2\kappa_x^g=\sum_xS_{1x}^{2-2g}.
$$
Finally, insert $S_{1x}=d_x/\mathcal D$, where $d_x$ is the <quantum dimension> and $\mathcal D^2=\sum_xd_x^2$ is the squared <total quantum dimension>:
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{GSD}(\Sigma_g)=\mathcal D^{2g-2}\sum_xd_x^{2-2g}=\left(\sum_xd_x^2\right)^{g-1}\sum_xd_x^{2-2g}.}
$$
Although only <quantum dimensions> remain, the result is an integer because <2/a> counts an <anyon fusion space>.

The <sphere> value is $\mathcal D^{-2}\sum_xd_x^2=1$, and the <torus> value is $\sum_x1$. If all $n$ simple <anyons> have <quantum dimension> one, the answer is $n^g$, agreeing with the Abelian handle count. These checks also fix the exponent $2g-2$.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

Let $B^{ab}_c$ denote the split-fuse diagram with a charge-$c$ edge joining the lower fusion vertex to the upper splitting vertex, and outer strands $a,b$. Completeness in the <isotopy normalization of anyon diagrams> reads
$$
1_{a\otimes b}=\sum_c\sqrt{\frac{d_c}{d_ad_b}}\,B^{ab}_c.
$$
Resolve the two strands inside the encircling <normalized Kirby color> loop in this way. Move that loop onto the intermediate charge-$c$ edge. The <Kirby killing property> multiplies it by $\mathcal D\delta_{c1}$, leaving
$$
K_\Omega(a,b)=\mathcal D\sqrt{\frac{d_1}{d_ad_b}}\,B^{ab}_1.
$$
The <vacuum anyon> channel is allowed only for $b=\bar a$, and then $d_b=d_a$ and $d_1=1$. Erasing the vacuum edge turns $B^{a\bar a}_1$ into the cup-cap diagram $E_a$, which connects the incoming pair by a cap and the outgoing pair by a cup. Thus the requested <two-strand Kirby projector> is
$$
\boxed{K_\Omega(a,b)=\delta_{b,\bar a}\frac{\mathcal D}{d_a}\,E_a=\mathcal D P^{ab}_1.}
$$
Here $P^{a\bar a}_1=E_a/d_a$ is the <orthogonal projection> onto total <vacuum anyon> charge; for $b\ne\bar a$ it is zero. Both strands in the source point upwards, so the condition is $b=\bar a$, not $b=a$ unless $a$ is self-dual.

The coefficient can be checked without a convention guess: stacking two cup-cap diagrams creates one charge-$a$ loop, so $E_a^2=d_aE_a$. Consequently $(E_a/d_a)^2=E_a/d_a$, as required of an <orthogonal projection>. Omitting $1/d_a$ would fail this check.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Use the <Witten-Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant> with the source's <normalized Kirby color> on all $2g+1$ surgery components. Since the <signature> of the <linking matrix> is zero, the framing-anomaly factor is one, and the <TQFT partition function> is
$$
Z(\Sigma_g\times S^1)=\frac1{\mathcal D}\,\langle L(\Omega,\ldots,\Omega)\rangle.
$$
All evaluations in this part use the <isotopy normalization of anyon diagrams> and preserve the indicated <blackboard framing>.

Expand the long component as $\mathcal D^{-1}\sum_a d_a a$ and fix its charge $a$. Each pair of auxiliary components is in the <Borromean rings> arrangement with this component. Eliminate such a pair using the <two-strand Kirby projector> from <2/c>: bend the long component to expose its two oppositely oriented portions, viewed as equally oriented strands labeled $a,\bar a$. The first encircling <normalized Kirby color> loop replaces these by a vacuum cup-cap with coefficient $\mathcal D/d_a$. This vacuum connection opens the second encircling loop so that the same <two-strand Kirby projector> applies once more. Removing the resulting vacuum edges and straightening the bends restores the original charge-$a$ strand. Thus the <Borromean handle-pair evaluation> multiplies the bare strand by
$$
H_a=\left(\frac{\mathcal D}{d_a}\right)^2.
$$
There is no residual twist. This is a relative strand evaluation: the final closure of the long component is counted separately. As a normalization check, when $a=1$ the two auxiliary components become two unlinked <normalized Kirby color> loops, each of value $\mathcal D$, giving $H_1=\mathcal D^2$.

The $g$ pairs occupy disjoint portions of the long component, so their factors multiply. Closing that component gives its <quantum dimension> $d_a$. Combining this with its <normalized Kirby color> coefficient $d_a/\mathcal D$ gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle L(\Omega,\ldots,\Omega)\rangle
&=\sum_a\frac{d_a}{\mathcal D}\,d_a\left(\frac{\mathcal D^2}{d_a^2}\right)^g\\
&=\mathcal D^{2g-1}\sum_a d_a^{2-2g}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Applying the overall surgery prefactor yields
$$
\boxed{Z(\Sigma_g\times S^1)=\mathcal D^{2g-2}\sum_a d_a^{2-2g}.}
$$
Finally, the <topological quantum field theory> assigns the identity map to a cylinder over $\Sigma_g$. Closing its time direction takes the <trace> of that identity, so
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{GSD}(\Sigma_g)=\dim V(\Sigma_g)=Z(\Sigma_g\times S^1).}
$$
This independently reproduces <2/b>. For $g=0$ the surgery link is one zero-framed <normalized Kirby color> <unknot>: its value $\mathcal D$ cancels the surgery prefactor and gives $Z(S^2\times S^1)=1$. For $g=1$ the result is the number of simple <anyons>, as required for the <torus>.
