= Einstein field equations
{c}
{wiki}
{title2=$G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4$}

= Einstein equation
{c}
{synonym}

With signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and vanishing cosmological constant, the Einstein field equations are
$$
R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12 g_{\mu\nu}R=\kappa T_{\mu\nu}.
$$
In four spacetime dimensions, contraction with $g^{\mu\nu}$ gives the useful trace relation $R=-\kappa T$.

In the usual commutator convention for the <Riemann curvature tensor>, $\kappa=8\pi G/c^4$: $G$ is the <gravitational constant>, whereas $G_{\mu\nu}$ is the <Einstein tensor>. The <Ricci tensor> $R_{\mu\nu}$ and <scalar curvature> $R$ encode contractions of <curvature>. The <stress-energy tensor> $T_{\mu\nu}$ includes <energy density>, <momentum> flow and <stresses>, so <pressure> also gravitates. A <cosmological constant> adds $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ to the left-hand side. See https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S4.html[Tong's Einstein equations and matter coupling].

For a concrete consequence, set $T_{\mu\nu}=0$ and $\Lambda=0$. The trace gives $R=0$, and substitution gives $R_{\mu\nu}=0$. Yet the exterior <Schwarzschild spacetime> still has the <tidal forces> computed under <geodesic deviation>: vanishing <Ricci tensor> does not imply vanishing <Riemann curvature tensor>. Geometry carries gravitational effects through empty space. Einstein presented the final matter equations on 25 November 1915, including the essential trace correction, in https://d-nb.info/1379427029/34[Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, pages 844-847, especially equation (2a)]. His symbols and sign conventions differ from modern ones; the displayed formula uses modern notation.

= Trace coefficient fixed by stress-energy conservation
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{title2=$R_{\mu\nu}-\alpha g_{\mu\nu}R=-\kappa T_{\mu\nu}$}

In four dimensions, the <contracted Bianchi identity> gives $(1/2-\alpha)\partial_\mu R=-\kappa\nabla^\nu T_{\mu\nu}$ for the displayed equation. Its trace is $(1-4\alpha)R=-\kappa T$. For $\alpha\ne1/4$,
$$
\nabla^\nu T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1/2-\alpha}{1-4\alpha}\partial_\mu T.
$$
Compatibility with covariant <stress-energy conservation> for unrestricted conventional matter therefore requires $\alpha=1/2$, yielding the <Einstein tensor>. Other coefficients impose constant trace on conserved sources. The exceptional $\alpha=1/4$ equation instead requires $T=0$; conservation additionally makes $R$ constant. A trace-free equation sourced by $T_{\mu\nu}-g_{\mu\nu}T/4$ is a different system and does not impose vanishing matter trace.

= Einstein equation in the sequential-derivative curvature convention
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{c}

With signature $(+---)$, let $R^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{bc}-\partial_c\Gamma^a{}_{bd}-\Gamma^a{}_{pc}\Gamma^p{}_{bd}+\Gamma^a{}_{pd}\Gamma^p{}_{bc}$ and $R_{ab}=R^c{}_{acb}$. This curvature is the negative of the convention in which $[\nabla_c,\nabla_d]X^a=R^a{}_{bcd}X^b$. The physical <Einstein field equations> are $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=-8\pi G T_{ab}/c^4$. In a static weak field $R_{00}=-\nabla^2\Phi/c^2$; the trace-reversed equation then reproduces the <Poisson equation> $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho_{\rm mass}$. Metric variation of $\int\sqrt{-g}(R-2\Lambda)/(2\kappa)+S_m$ gives the same sign when $\delta S_m=\int\sqrt{-g}T_{ab}\delta g^{ab}/2$.

= Newtonian normalization of the Einstein field equations
{c}
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{title2=$G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=\kappa T_{ab}$}

Assume a metric theory in four dimensions, a <Levi-Civita connection>, covariant <stress-energy conservation> and field equations linear in second metric derivatives. The <contracted Bianchi identity> selects $G_{ab}=R_{ab}-g_{ab}R/2$. Its trace-reversed equation is $R_{ab}=\kappa(T_{ab}-g_{ab}T/2)+\Lambda g_{ab}$. In the convention $R_{00}\simeq-\nabla^2\varphi$ and signature $(+---)$, nonrelativistic matter gives $R_{00}\simeq\kappa\rho/2$. The <Poisson equation> $\nabla^2\varphi=4\pi G\rho$ fixes $\kappa=-8\pi G$. Reversing the curvature convention reverses this displayed coupling sign without changing the physical theory. The <strong equivalence principle> alone does not establish uniqueness without the stated additional assumptions.

= Einstein-equation coupling under reversed curvature
{c}
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{title2=$R^a{}_{bcd}\mapsto-R^a{}_{bcd}\implies\kappa\mapsto-\kappa$}

With the metric and <stress-energy tensor> fixed and zero <cosmological constant>, changing the sign convention for the <Riemann curvature tensor> changes the signs of the <Ricci tensor>, <scalar curvature> and <Einstein tensor>. The same physical field equation is therefore written with the opposite coupling sign. For signature $(+---)$, the convention $R^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_d\Gamma^a_{bc}-\partial_c\Gamma^a_{bd}-\Gamma^a_{ec}\Gamma^e_{bd}+\Gamma^a_{ed}\Gamma^e_{bc}$ and $R_{ab}=R^c{}_{acb}$ gives $R_{00}\simeq-\Delta\Phi$ in a static <Newtonian limit>. The trace-reversed equation and $\Delta\Phi=4\pi G\rho$ then require $G_{ab}=-8\pi GT_{ab}$.

= Planar Einstein metric with cubic radial function
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{title2=$ds^2=z^{-2}[-Fdt^2+dx^2+dy^2+F^{-1}dz^2],\quad F=1-\alpha z^3$}

This metric has $R_{ab}=-3g_{ab}$ and <cosmological constant> $\Lambda=-3$ in unit curvature-radius normalization. Its regular static coframe is real when $F>0$ and $z\ne0$. Setting $q=\alpha z^3$, its six independent <curvature 2-form> coefficients are $-(1-q)$ in the $03$ and $12$ planes and $-(1+q/2)$ in the other four planes. Their Ricci contractions cancel $q$. The <Weyl tensor> squared invariant is $12\alpha^2z^6$, so nonzero $\alpha$ does not give constant sectional curvature despite the Einstein equation.

= Lovelock's theorem
{c}
{parent=Einstein field equations}

In four dimensions, a local natural symmetric divergence-free metric tensor involving the metric and its derivatives of at most second order has the Lovelock form $aG_{ab}+bg_{ab}$ with constant coefficients. Generic nonlinear metric <f(R) gravity> instead has fourth-order terms and does not meet that derivative-order hypothesis. For affine $f$, the metric field equation is precisely of Lovelock form.

= Vacuum Einstein equations
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{c}

In a region with vanishing <stress-energy tensor>, the <Einstein field equations> reduce to $R_{\mu\nu}=0$ when the <cosmological constant> vanishes.

= Vacuum spacetime
{parent=Vacuum Einstein equations}
{title2=$T_{ab}=0$}

A <vacuum spacetime> has vanishing matter <stress-energy tensor>. In $D>2$ dimensions, tracing the <vacuum Einstein equations> gives $R=2D\Lambda/(D-2)$ and hence $R_{ab}=2\Lambda g_{ab}/(D-2)$; in four dimensions this reduces to $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$. With zero <cosmological constant> it is <Ricci flat>, although its full <Riemann curvature tensor> can be nonzero: a vacuum <Kerr black hole> and vacuum <gravitational waves> illustrate that distinction.

= Einstein-Hilbert action
{c}
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{wiki}

The Einstein-Hilbert action is the integral of the <Ricci scalar> against the metric volume form. Varying it with respect to the metric gives the vacuum <Einstein field equations>, up to a boundary term.

= Einstein-Cartan theory
{parent=Einstein-Hilbert action}
{c}
{wiki}
{title2=$(e^a,\omega^{ab})$}

An independent <vierbein> and metric-compatible <spin connection> allow <spin> density to source a <torsion tensor>. The connection equation from the <Einstein-Hilbert action> is algebraic in torsion. In the absence of connection-dependent matter it fixes the <Levi-Civita connection>. For a minimally coupled <Dirac field>, eliminating the connection generates a local four-fermion interaction. Thus its equivalent torsion-free second-order <action> includes this contact term; simply dropping it defines a different matter theory.

= 1.5-order formalism
{parent=Einstein-Cartan theory}
{title2=$\delta S/\delta\omega=0$}

Solve the algebraic connection equation for $\widehat\omega(e,\chi)$, but perform the remaining variations as though the connection were independent. The chain-rule contribution $(\delta S/\delta\omega)\delta\widehat\omega$ vanishes on that solution. The resulting equations are those of the connection-eliminated second-order <action>. This is especially useful for local <supersymmetry> calculations, where the gravitino-induced contorsion is quadratic in <fermions> and the omitted induced connection variation would otherwise be lengthy.

= Algebraic torsion of a Dirac field
{parent=Einstein-Cartan theory}
{title2=$K_{abc}=-\kappa^2\bar\chi\gamma_{abc}\chi/4$}

Use $K_{abc}=e_a{}^\mu(\omega-\omega(e))_{\mu bc}$ and $T_{abc}=K_{bac}-K_{cab}$. For the symmetrized Dirac <action> $-e\bar\chi\gamma^\mu\overleftrightarrow\nabla_\mu\chi/2-em\bar\chi\chi$, varying the <spin connection> gives $-e\bar\chi\gamma^{\mu ab}\chi\,\delta\omega_{\mu ab}/4$. Only totally antisymmetric contorsion is sourced. Its <action> is $-eK^2/(2\kappa^2)-eK_{abc}\bar\chi\gamma^{abc}\chi/4$, hence the stated solution. Substitution generates $e\kappa^2(\bar\chi\gamma^{abc}\chi)(\bar\chi\gamma_{abc}\chi)/32$. The torsion is an <auxiliary field>, not an independent propagating degree of freedom.

= First-order three-dimensional gravity
{parent=Einstein-Hilbert action}
{title2=$T^a=0,\quad R^{ab}=-3\lambda E^a\wedge E^b$}

For a nondegenerate <orthonormal coframe in spacetime> $E^a$ and independent antisymmetric Lorentz <connection 1-form>, the action $\int\epsilon_{abc}(R^{ab}\wedge E^c+\lambda E^a\wedge E^b\wedge E^c)$ has independent coframe and connection variations. Since $\delta R^{ab}=D\delta\omega^{ab}$, integration by parts yields a connection equation $\epsilon_{abc}T^c=0$. The three variations of the cubic coframe term give the coframe equation $\epsilon_{abc}(R^{ab}+3\lambda E^a\wedge E^b)=0$. In three dimensions these are exactly the equations displayed above. The solution has <constant sectional curvature> $K=-3\lambda$. Omitting the factor of three changes the cosmological normalization.

= Einstein-Proca theory
{c}
{parent=Einstein-Hilbert action}

Einstein-Proca theory couples <general relativity> to a massive vector field governed by the <Proca action>. Its matter stress-energy tensor is
$$
T_{ab}=F_{ac}F_b{}^c-\frac14g_{ab}F_{cd}F^{cd}
+m^2\left(A_aA_b-\frac12g_{ab}A_cA^c\right).
$$
The <contracted Bianchi identity> makes the Einstein equation compatible with the covariant <Proca equation>.

= Proca stress-energy tensor
{parent=Einstein-Proca theory}

The Proca stress-energy tensor is the metric variation of the matter part of the <Proca action>. It consists of the <electromagnetic stress-energy tensor> plus the quadratic mass contribution $m^2(A_aA_b-g_{ab}A^2/2)$.

= Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory
{c}
{parent=Einstein-Hilbert action}

Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory couples the metric to a scalar field and a Maxwell field with scalar-dependent gauge coupling, typically through $e^{\alpha\Phi}F_{ab}F^{ab}$. Its Maxwell equation is $\nabla_a(e^{\alpha\Phi}F^{ab})=0$.

= Magnetically charged dilaton black hole
{parent=Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory}
{title2=$V=1-r_+/r,\quad R^2=r(r-r_-)$}

The unit-coupling magnetic solution of <Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory> has $ds^2=-Vdt^2+V^{-1}dr^2+R^2d\Omega_2^2$, magnetic potential $A=P\cos\theta\,d\phi$, and dilaton $\phi=-\tfrac12\log(1-r_-/r)$, where $r_-=2P^2/r_+$ and $r_+>0$. Its physical asymptotic component has $r>r_-$. For $0\le r_-<r_+$ it has a regular <Killing horizon> at $r_+$, area $4\pi r_+(r_+-r_-)$ and <surface gravity> $1/(2r_+)$. Its asymptotic mass is $r_+/2$. For $r_->r_+$ the zero-area singularity is naked. The limiting case $r_-=r_+$ is singular and must not be treated as a regular nondegenerate horizon.

= Causal structure of the magnetic dilaton black hole
{parent=Magnetically charged dilaton black hole}

For $r_-<r_+$, an ingoing chart with $v=t+r_*$ has metric $-Vdv^2+2dv\,dr+R^2d\Omega_2^2$. In its future interior, $\nabla r=\partial_v+V\partial_r$ is future timelike, so every future causal curve decreases $r$ and cannot reach <future null infinity>. The singularity $r=r_-$ is spacelike. A <Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates> extension has the same qualitative <Penrose diagram> as <Schwarzschild spacetime>, with two exterior regions, a future black-hole interior and a past <white hole> interior. The past interior has the same radial interval but is not a <black hole> region; specifying the radial interval alone does not identify the future component. For $r_->r_+$, the physical exterior $r>r_-$ has $V>0$ everywhere and outgoing null rays escape from arbitrarily near a timelike <naked singularity>.

= Geodesic potential of the magnetic dilaton black hole
{parent=Magnetically charged dilaton black hole}
{title2=$\widetilde V=\tfrac12[V(\epsilon+L^2/R^2)-E^2]$}

Rotate any <geodesic> into an equatorial plane of the <magnetically charged dilaton black hole>. Conserved energy and angular momentum are $E=V\dot t$ and $L=R^2\dot\phi$. Normalizing its tangent to $g(\dot x,\dot x)=-\epsilon$, with $\epsilon=1,0,-1$ for timelike, null and spacelike geodesics, gives $\dot r^2=E^2-V(\epsilon+L^2/R^2)$. Thus its radial equation is $\dot r^2/2+\widetilde V=0$. Radial null rays have $\dot r=\pm E$ and use the same <Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate> with $2M$ replaced by $r_+$.

= Extremal dilaton metric photon capture
{parent=Geodesic potential of the magnetic dilaton black hole}
{title2=$B_c=2M,\quad \sigma_{\rm capture}=4\pi M^2$}

For $f=1-2M/r$ and squared areal radius $C=r(r-b)$, an equatorial <null geodesic> obeys $\dot r^2=E^2[1-B^2f/C]$, with <impact parameter> $B=L/E$. At $b=2M$, $f/C=1/r^2$ in the exterior. Its supremum is $1/(4M^2)$ at the null singular boundary, rather than at a regular exterior <photon sphere>. Rays with $B>2M$ turn at $r=B$; rays with $B<2M$ reach the boundary. Assuming those rays are absorbed gives the displayed geometric-optics cross-section despite zero limiting horizon area.

= Komar mass of the magnetic dilaton black hole
{c}
{parent=Magnetically charged dilaton black hole}
{title2=$M_K(r)=r_+/2-P^2/r$}

For the <magnetically charged dilaton black hole>, the metric-dual one-form of $k=\partial_t$ is $k^\flat=-Vdt$. With orientation $dt\wedge dr\wedge d\theta\wedge d\phi$, its <Hodge star operator> gives $\star dk^\flat=-V'R^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi$. Therefore the <Komar mass> is $R^2V'/2=r_+/2-P^2/r$. Its radial dependence is allowed because the <Ricci tensor> is nonzero; the divergence of $dk^\flat$ is sourced by $R_{ab}k^b$. At the horizon $M_K(r_+)=(r_+-r_-)/2=\kappa A_H/(4\pi)$.

= Trace-reversed Einstein field equations in D dimensions
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{c}

For $D>2$, tracing and substituting back gives
$$
R_{\mu\nu}
=\kappa\left(T_{\mu\nu}-\frac1{D-2}g_{\mu\nu}T\right).
$$

= Ricci curvature sourced by a Klein-Gordon scalar field
{parent=Trace-reversed Einstein field equations in D dimensions}
{c}

For the conserved scalar stress tensor above,
$$
R_{\mu\nu}
=\frac\kappa2\left[
\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi
+\frac{m^2}{D-2}g_{\mu\nu}\phi^2
\right].
$$

= Metric variation
{parent=Einstein field equations}

For $h_{ab}=\delta g_{ab}$, variation of the inverse metric and metric volume form gives
$$
\delta g^{ab}=-h^{ab},
\qquad
\delta(d\operatorname{vol}_g)=\frac12g^{ab}h_{ab}\,d\operatorname{vol}_g.
$$
The <Levi-Civita connection> varies by
$$
\delta\Gamma^a{}_{bc}=\frac12g^{ad}
(\nabla_bh_{cd}+\nabla_ch_{bd}-\nabla_dh_{bc}).
$$

= Palatini identity
{c}
{parent=Metric variation}
{wiki}
{title2=$\delta R_{ab}=\nabla_c\delta\Gamma^c{}_{ab}-\nabla_b\delta\Gamma^c{}_{ac}$}

For the <Levi-Civita connection>, the difference of two connections is a tensor. Varying the coordinate expression for the <Ricci tensor> then gives the displayed covariant identity. It is the starting point for the <metric variation of scalar curvature>.

= Metric variation of scalar curvature
{parent=Metric variation}

For $h_{ab}=\delta g_{ab}$, the <Ricci scalar> varies as
$$
\delta R=-R^{ab}h_{ab}+\nabla_a\nabla_bh^{ab}-\nabla^a\nabla_ah,
\qquad h=g^{ab}h_{ab}.
$$
The last two terms form a <covariant divergence> and hence contribute only a boundary term to a compactly supported variation of the <Einstein-Hilbert action>.

= Modified gravity
{parent=Einstein field equations}
{wiki}

Modified gravity changes the Einstein-Hilbert action or its field equations in order to produce gravitational dynamics different from general relativity.

= Tensor-vector-scalar gravity
{parent=Modified gravity}
{title2=TeVeS}

= TeVeS
{synonym}
{c}

A relativistic gravitational theory constructed by <Jacob Bekenstein> using tensor, vector and <scalar fields>, together with a specified physical <metric> for matter. It illustrates how the low-acceleration phenomenology of <MOND> can be embedded in a theory with calculable light deflection. Matching rotation curves is not sufficient to establish its lensing or cosmological predictions.<Relativistic gravitation theory for the MOND paradigm by Jacob Bekenstein>[Relativistic action and physical metric]{ref}

= Modified Newtonian dynamics
{parent=Modified gravity}
{title2=MOND}

= MOND
{synonym}
{c}

A low-acceleration modification associated with <Mordehai Milgrom>. For an isolated spherical system, $g\mu(g/a_0)=g_N$, with $\mu(x)\to1$ at high $x$ and $\mu(x)\to x$ at low $x$. An isolated point-like baryonic distribution then gives $v_c^4=GM_ba_0$ and a flat asymptotic <galaxy rotation curve>. The algebraic spherical relation is not a complete relativistic field theory and should not be assumed to hold vectorially for arbitrary <mass> distributions.<A modification of the Newtonian dynamics as a possible alternative to the hidden mass hypothesis by Mordehai Milgrom>[Pages 365–370]{ref}

= Scalar-tensor theory
{parent=Modified gravity}

A <scalar-tensor theory> uses a <metric tensor> and an additional <scalar field> to mediate gravity. A <scalar> multiplier of the <Ricci scalar> makes the <curvature> coupling depend on that field. The <Brans-Dicke theory> is a standard example; the choice of <scalar> potential and kinetic coupling distinguishes different members of the class.

= Metric variation of a scalar-curvature coupling
{parent=Scalar-tensor theory}
{title2=$E_{ab}=\Phi G_{ab}+\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi-g_{ab}\Box\Phi$}

With the <curvature sign convention> in which $\delta R_{bc}=\nabla_c\delta\Gamma^a{}_{ba}-\nabla_a\delta\Gamma^a{}_{bc}$, the inverse-metric <variation> of $\int\sqrt{-g}\,\Phi R$ is $\int\sqrt{-g}\,E_{ab}\delta g^{ab}$ plus a <boundary term>, for a <scalar> multiplier held fixed in that variation. The <Palatini identity> expresses the <curvature> variation as divergences of $\delta\Gamma$. Two <integrations by parts> move the <derivatives> onto $\Phi$, yielding the displayed terms. For constant $\Phi$ they vanish. The opposite <curvature> convention instead has $E_{ab}=\Phi G_{ab}+g_{ab}\Box\Phi-\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi$.

= Brans-Dicke theory
{c}
{parent=Scalar-tensor theory}
{wiki=Brans–Dicke_theory}
{title2=$\mathcal L_g=\Phi R+\omega\Phi^{-1}(\nabla\Phi)^2$}

The <Brans-Dicke theory> adds a dynamical <scalar field> $\Phi$ to a <metric tensor> theory of gravity, with constant dimensionless coupling $\omega$. In a $(+---)$ signature and the reversed-curvature convention used here, the displayed Lagrangian is supplemented by matter and integrated against the <metric tensor> volume density with prefactor $1/(16\pi G)$. Its <scalar> multiplier of the <Ricci scalar> lets the <curvature> coupling vary in spacetime. Both the <metric tensor> and <scalar> are varied; the <scalar equation of Brans-Dicke theory> follows by eliminating <curvature> between the <scalar> equation and the <trace> of the <metric tensor> equation. The original construction is described in https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.124.925[Brans and Dicke's 1961 article].

= Scalar equation of Brans-Dicke theory
{parent=Brans-Dicke theory}
{title2=$(2\omega+3)\Box\Phi=8\pi G T$}

Assume four spacetime dimensions, $\Phi\ne0$, constant $\omega$, no <scalar> potential and matter independent of $\Phi$. In the stated action convention, <scalar> <variation> gives $\Phi R+\omega\Phi^{-1}(\nabla\Phi)^2-2\omega\Box\Phi=0$. The <trace> of the <metric tensor> equation gives $\Phi R+\omega\Phi^{-1}(\nabla\Phi)^2+3\Box\Phi=8\pi G T$. Their subtraction proves the displayed <wave equation> with $T=g^{ab}T_{ab}$. The <degenerate coupling of Brans-Dicke theory> needs separate treatment before dividing by its coefficient.

= Degenerate coupling of Brans-Dicke theory
{parent=Scalar equation of Brans-Dicke theory}
{title2=$\omega=-3/2,\qquad T=0$}

At $\omega=-3/2$, the combined <scalar equation of Brans-Dicke theory> becomes the matter consistency condition $T=0$. It does not determine $\Box\Phi$ by division, since the wave coefficient has vanished. The original <metric tensor> and <scalar> <Euler-Lagrange equations> must be retained. Introducing scalar-dependent matter or a <scalar> potential changes the consistency condition.

= f(R) gravity
{c}
{parent=Modified gravity}

An f(R) theory replaces the Ricci scalar in the gravitational action by a smooth function $f(R)$. Its metric field equation is
$$
f'(R)R_{ab}-\frac12f(R)g_{ab}
+(g_{ab}\Box-\nabla_a\nabla_b)f'(R)=8\pi T_{ab}.
$$

= Einstein-frame representation of metric f(R) gravity
{c}
{parent=f(R) gravity}
{title2=$\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}=-f'g_{\mu\nu},\quad\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\log(-f')$}

In the mostly-minus convention with vacuum gravitational action proportional to $\int\sqrt{-g}f(R)$ and $f'<0$, the displayed <conformal transformation> turns its vacuum equation into $\widetilde G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu}(\phi)$, with potential $V=(Rf'-f)/(2f'^2)$ and the <stress-energy tensor of a canonical scalar field>. The conformal Ricci formula cancels the second derivatives of $f'$ against the original field equation. Its remaining derivative tensor is $\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi-\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde\nabla\phi)^2/2$. Expressing $V$ as a function of $\phi$ requires a locally invertible $R\mapsto f'(R)$. When $f''=0$, this scalar-tensor parametrization degenerates and introduces no new propagating scalar degree of freedom.

= Einstein metric condition in f(R) gravity
{c}
{parent=f(R) gravity}
{title2=$f(4\Lambda)=2\Lambda f'(4\Lambda)$}

Every four-dimensional Einstein metric with $R_{ab}=\Lambda g_{ab}$ has constant <Ricci scalar> $R=4\Lambda$. The vacuum metric <f(R) gravity> tensor reduces to $(\Lambda f'(4\Lambda)-f(4\Lambda)/2)g_{ab}$, giving the displayed necessary and sufficient condition for a fixed <cosmological constant>. Both function and derivative may vanish, so division by $f'$ is unnecessary. Requiring this for all real $\Lambda$ and a smooth $f$ instead yields $f(R)=CR^2$.

= Normalization of the metric f(R) field equation
{parent=f(R) gravity}
{title2=$A E_{ab}=\frac12T_{ab}$}

For a metric action $S_g=A\int\sqrt{-g}f(R)\,d^4x$ and the usual <stress-energy tensor> convention, covariant metric variation gives $A E_{ab}=T_{ab}/2$, where $E_{ab}=f'R_{ab}-fg_{ab}/2+(g_{ab}\Box-\nabla_a\nabla_b)f'$. With $A=1/(16\pi G)$ this becomes $E_{ab}=8\pi G T_{ab}$; with $A=1$ it becomes $E_{ab}=T_{ab}/2$. The chosen normalization does not alter the vacuum equation.

= Linearized f(R) gravity about Minkowski spacetime
{c}
{parent=f(R) gravity}

If $f(0)=0$, $f'(0)=1$, and $f''(0)=0$, then $f(R)=R+O(R^3)$ near zero. The field equations linearized about <Minkowski spacetime> are therefore the <linearized Einstein equations>; deviations first appear beyond linear order.
