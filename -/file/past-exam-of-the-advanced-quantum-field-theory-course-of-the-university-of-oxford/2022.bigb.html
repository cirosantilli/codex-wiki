There are **two** <one-loop Feynman diagrams>: the two orders of the labelled <photons> around a <top quark> <fermion loop>, with one <Yukawa interaction> insertion for the outgoing <Higgs boson>. They are added, rather than identified by a <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor>. Each has the same <color charge> trace $N_c=3$.

\Image[2022-top-triangle-routings.png]
{title=The two photon orders and consistent directed loop momenta}
{description=Original <Feynman diagrams>. Solid arrows show <fermion flow> and the labelled internal four-<momenta>. Wavy external lines are incoming <photons>; the dashed external line is the outgoing <Higgs boson>. The two panels use independent dummy <loop momenta>.}

In the first routing, traverse the <fermion loop> from the $p_2$ insertion to the $p_1$ insertion with <momentum> $l$, then to the <Higgs boson> insertion with <momentum> $l+p_1$, then back to the $p_2$ insertion with <momentum> $l-p_2$. Conservation at the <Higgs boson> insertion gives $p_h=p_1+p_2$. The second routing exchanges $(p_1,\mu)$ and $(p_2,\nu)$: its three internal <momenta> are $l$, $l+p_2$ and $l-p_1$. Reversing the orientation is another way to represent this second ordering. Two vector insertions give no <Furry theorem> cancellation.

Use <Minkowski metric> $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$ and the convention $S_{fi}=\delta_{fi}+i(2\pi)^4\delta^{(4)}(p_f-p_i)\mathcal M_{fi}$. Put $m=m_t$, $y=\lambda_t/\sqrt2$, $Q=e_t=2/3$ and
$$
D_0=l^2-m^2+i0,\qquad D_1=(l+p_1)^2-m^2+i0,\qquad D_2=(l-p_2)^2-m^2+i0.
$$
The <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> is understood in subsequent denominators. Contracting the <spinor> indices around the <fermion loop> gives the <trace>
$$
T_{\mu\nu}(l)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[\gamma_\mu(\not l+m)\gamma_\nu(\not l-\not p_2+m)(\not l+\not p_1+m)\right].
$$
Here $\not p=\gamma^\rho p_\rho$ is <Feynman slash notation>. The <closed fermion loop sign> is $-1$, the three <interaction vertices> contribute $(-iy)(-ieQ)^2$, and the three <Dirac propagators> contribute $i^3$. Their product, including the <color charge> trace, is $-N_cye^2Q^2$. Thus one ordering has
$$
i\mathcal M^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}=-N_cye^2Q^2\int\frac{d^4l}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(l)}{D_0D_1D_2},\qquad
\boxed{\mathcal M^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}=iN_cye^2Q^2\int\frac{d^4l}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{T_{\mu\nu}(l)}{D_0D_1D_2}.}
$$
Multiplying by the incoming <polarization vectors> and adding the exchanged <Feynman diagram> gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M=\epsilon_1^\mu\epsilon_2^\nu\left[\mathcal M^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}(p_1,p_2)+\mathcal M^{(1)}_{\nu\mu}(p_2,p_1)\right].}
$$
The displayed four-dimensional <Feynman integral> is formal until regularized; subsequent manipulations use <dimensional regularization>, which permits the required <loop momentum> shifts.

Set $s=(p_1+p_2)^2=m_h^2$, so $p_1^2=p_2^2=0$ and $p_1\cdot p_2=s/2$. The physical <polarization vectors> obey $\epsilon_1\cdot p_1=\epsilon_2\cdot p_2=0$. Write $X_{\mu\nu}\simeq Y_{\mu\nu}$ when they agree after contraction with these <polarization vectors>; terms proportional to $p_{1,\mu}$ or $p_{2,\nu}$ may then be discarded.

The <gamma matrix trace identities> kill the terms with an odd number of <gamma matrices>. The remaining three two-<momentum> terms and the term with three factors of $m$ are
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{T_{\mu\nu}}{4m}={}&m^2g_{\mu\nu}
+l_\mu(l-p_2)_\nu+(l-p_2)_\mu l_\nu-g_{\mu\nu}l\cdot(l-p_2)\\
&+l_\mu(l+p_1)_\nu+(l+p_1)_\mu l_\nu-g_{\mu\nu}l\cdot(l+p_1)\\
&+g_{\mu\nu}(l-p_2)\cdot(l+p_1)-(l-p_2)_\mu(l+p_1)_\nu+(l+p_1)_\mu(l-p_2)_\nu.
\end{aligned}
$$
In the $g_{\mu\nu}$ coefficient the terms linear in $l\cdot p_i$ cancel, leaving $m^2-l^2-s/2$. Among the other terms, the surviving mixed products of $l$ and $p_i$ also cancel. Consequently
$$
T_{\mu\nu}\simeq4m\left[4l_\mu l_\nu+p_{2,\mu}p_{1,\nu}+(m^2-s/2-l^2)g_{\mu\nu}\right].
$$
In the second <Feynman diagram>, exchange the external labels and change the dummy <loop momentum> $l\mapsto-l$. Its projected numerator and denominator become identical to those of the first <Feynman diagram>. Hence
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M'_{\mu\nu}=8iN_cye^2Q^2m\int\frac{d^4l}{(2\pi)^4}
\frac{4l_\mu l_\nu+p_{2,\mu}p_{1,\nu}+(m^2-s/2-l^2)g_{\mu\nu}}{D_0D_1D_2}.}
$$
With the common factor $8m$ extracted, the primitive integer coefficients are
$$
\boxed{A=4,\qquad B=1,\qquad C=1,\qquad \text{prefactor}=8iN_c\frac{\lambda_t}{\sqrt2}e^2e_t^2m_t.}
$$
Equivalently, extracting only $m$ gives $(A,B,C)=(32,8,8)$ and prefactor $iN_c\lambda_te^2e_t^2m_t/\sqrt2$. A common rescaling of all three coefficients changes the prefactor inversely. The physical <scattering amplitude> is $\epsilon_1^\mu\epsilon_2^\nu\mathcal M'_{\mu\nu}$; the projected integrand is not an identity for an arbitrary uncontracted off-shell <tensor>.

Continue the <loop momentum> to $D=4-\varepsilon$ dimensions, keeping $\operatorname{tr}1=4$ and the external physical <polarization vectors> four dimensional. Introduce <Feynman parameters> $x,y\geq0$, $x+y\leq1$, denoting this integration triangle by $\mathcal T$. Completing the square gives
$$
\frac1{D_0D_1D_2}=2\int_{\mathcal T}dx\,dy\,\frac1{(k^2-\Delta+i0)^3},\qquad
k=l+xp_1-yp_2,\qquad \Delta=m^2-sxy.
$$
Odd terms in $k$ integrate to zero. <Lorentz invariance> of <dimensional regularization> gives $k_\mu k_\nu\mapsto g_{\mu\nu}k^2/D$. The general projected numerator therefore becomes, under the <Feynman integral>,
$$
g_{\mu\nu}\left[\left(\frac AD-C\right)k^2+C(m^2-s/2+sxy)\right]
+(B-Axy)p_{2,\mu}p_{1,\nu}.
$$
Only the $k^2$ term can give an <ultraviolet divergence>. Define, with the conventional scale factor implicit,
$$
J_n(\Delta)=\mu_R^{\varepsilon}\int\frac{d^Dk}{(2\pi)^D}(k^2-\Delta+i0)^{-n},\qquad
I_2=J_2+\Delta J_3.
$$
The <gamma function> formula gives
$$
J_2=\frac{i}{16\pi^2}\frac2\varepsilon+O(1),\qquad
J_3=-\frac{i}{32\pi^2\Delta}+O(\varepsilon).
$$
Thus the pole of the numerator integral, including $2\int_{\mathcal T}dx\,dy=1$, is
$$
g_{\mu\nu}\left(\frac A4-C\right)\frac{i}{8\pi^2\varepsilon},
\qquad \boxed{A=4C.}
$$
The coefficients $(4,1,1)$ satisfy this condition. A nonzero local divergence would have to be removed by a <counterterm>. The <Ward identity> forbids a local $hA_\mu A^\mu$ term, and the first electromagnetic <gauge invariant> coupling with one <Higgs boson> and two <photons> has the form $hF_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$, of <engineering dimension> five. It is not an independent <counterterm> of the <Standard Model>, a <renormalizable quantum field theory>. The <top quark> contribution is separately electromagnetic <gauge invariant>; no additional <W boson> <loop Feynman diagram> is needed to cancel this pole.

**The finite remainder must still be retained.** For $A=4C$,
$$
\frac AD-C=\frac{C\varepsilon}{4-\varepsilon},\qquad
\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\left(\frac AD-C\right)I_2=\frac{iC}{32\pi^2}.
$$
Putting $D=4$ before evaluating the divergent <Feynman integral> would incorrectly set this finite contribution to zero.

= Solution
{parent=v}
{c}

Use the <Feynman parameters> and $J_3$ defined in <1/iv/solution>. With $A=4C$, the finite term just found is $-C\Delta J_3$. Combining it with the remaining $g_{\mu\nu}$ coefficient gives
$$
C[-\Delta+m^2-s/2+sxy]J_3
=-\frac{sC}{2}(1-4xy)J_3.
$$
The other coefficient is $(B-4Cxy)J_3$. Thus, writing $\mathscr I_{\mu\nu}$ for the <Feynman integral> without the overall prefactor,
$$
\mathscr I_{\mu\nu}=2\int_{\mathcal T}dx\,dy\,J_3(\Delta)
\left[-\frac{sC}{2}(1-4xy)g_{\mu\nu}+(B-4Cxy)p_{2,\mu}p_{1,\nu}\right].
$$
For $T_{\mu\nu}=\frac s2g_{\mu\nu}-p_{2,\mu}p_{1,\nu}$, the two coefficients must have the corresponding ratio. The mismatch is simply $(B-C)J_3$, independent of $xy$. Therefore the required relation is
$$
\boxed{B=C,}
$$
which is satisfied. In the primitive normalization the result is
$$
\mathscr I_{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{16\pi^2}\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\,
\frac{1-4xy}{m^2-sxy-i0}\,T_{\mu\nu},
$$
and the full <top quark> <scattering amplitude>, in our stated $S$ convention, is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal M=-\frac{N_cye^2Q^2m}{2\pi^2}\,
\epsilon_1^\mu\epsilon_2^\nu T_{\mu\nu}
\int_0^1dx\int_0^{1-x}dy\,\frac{1-4xy}{m^2-sxy-i0}.}
$$
Indeed $p_1^\mu T_{\mu\nu}=p_2^\nu T_{\mu\nu}=0$: replacing either <polarization vector> by its <photon> <momentum> makes the <scattering amplitude> vanish. This is the <Ward identity>, expressing electromagnetic <gauge invariance>. The result also is symmetric under exchange of the two <photons>. The finite term in <1/iv/solution> is essential for this <Ward identity>.

As an independent low-<energy> check, for $s/m^2\to0$ the <Feynman parameter> integral is
$$
\frac1{m^2}\int_{\mathcal T}(1-4xy)\,dx\,dy+O(s/m^4)
=\frac1{3m^2}+O(s/m^4).
$$
The coefficient is consequently $-N_cye^2Q^2/(6\pi^2m)$ at leading order. It has the inverse-<mass> dimension required for an effective $hF_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ coupling. If $y=m/v$, this coefficient stays finite in the large-$m$ limit at fixed $v$; that limit increases the <Yukawa coupling>, so it is not a fixed-coupling <heavy-field decoupling> limit.

At the stated order the <renormalization-group beta function> is separable:
$$
\int_{\alpha_i}^{\alpha_f}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha^2}
=b_0\int_{\mu_i}^{\mu_f}\frac{d\mu}{\mu}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\frac1{\alpha_f}=\frac1{\alpha_i}-b_0\ln\frac{\mu_f}{\mu_i},\qquad
\alpha_f=\frac{\alpha_i}{1-b_0\alpha_i\ln(\mu_f/\mu_i)}.}
$$
For a positive initial <running coupling>, this expression is used on the connected interval containing $\mu_i$ where its denominator is positive and <perturbation theory> remains valid.

If $b_0<0$, the <running coupling> decreases at high <renormalization scale> and tends to zero: this is <asymptotic freedom>. Decreasing the <renormalization scale> increases the <running coupling>, with a formal infrared pole at $\Lambda=\mu_i\exp[1/(b_0\alpha_i)]$. Near $\Lambda$, <perturbation theory> fails; the pole alone is not a proof of <confinement>. Both <QCD> and the <SU(2)> weak <gauge coupling> have this sign for the <Standard Model> particle content above the relevant thresholds.

If $b_0>0$, the <running coupling> decreases towards low <renormalization scale> and grows towards high <renormalization scale>. The formal ultraviolet <Landau pole> is $\mu_L=\mu_i\exp[1/(b_0\alpha_i)]$. The pole signals failure of this perturbative evolution, rather than a controlled physical infinity. The <hypercharge> <gauge coupling> and ordinary <QED> with charged matter have this sign.

If $b_0=0$, the <running coupling> is constant **at this order**; higher-order terms may still make it run. No <Standard Model> gauge factor has a vanishing one-<loop order> coefficient for the usual particle content.

For precise normalization, take $\alpha_i=g_i^2/(4\pi)$ and $Q=T_3+Y$. The <Standard Model one-loop gauge coefficients> then give
$$
(b_0^Y,b_0^2,b_0^3)=\frac1{2\pi}\left(\frac{41}{6},-\frac{19}{6},-7\right).
$$
These coefficients use $d/d\ln\mu$, not $d/d\ln\mu^2$. Threshold matching changes the active particle content, so the single constant-$b_0$ expression is applied piecewise across thresholds.

There is one <tree-level Feynman diagram>: annihilation of the <up quark> and its <antiquark> into an $s$-channel <gluon>, followed by production of the <down quark> and its <antiquark>. A <gluon> <interaction vertex> preserves <quark flavor>, so a $t$-channel line joining the incoming <up quark> to the outgoing <down quark> is impossible.

\Image[2022-annihilation-exchange-channels.png]
{title=Annihilation and exchange channels with labelled external momenta}
{description=Original <Feynman diagrams>, with arrows denoting <fermion flow>. The left panel with $V=g$ and $f'=d$ is the distinct-<quark flavor> process. Both panels with $V=\gamma$ describe <Bhabha scattering>; both with $V=g$ and $f'=f=u$ describe identical-<quark flavor> scattering. The wavy internal line stands for the indicated vector mediator.}

Let $i,j,k,l$ label the initial <quark>, initial <antiquark>, final <quark>, final <antiquark> <color charges>. Up to an irrelevant common sign, the <scattering amplitude> is
$$
\mathcal M_s=\frac{g^2}{s}(T^a)_{ji}(T^a)_{kl}
[\bar v(p_2)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)][\bar u(p_3)\gamma_\mu v(p_4)].
$$
The <Mandelstam variables> are $s=(p_1+p_2)^2$, $t=(p_1-p_3)^2$, $u=(p_1-p_4)^2$, with $s+t+u=0$ in the zero-<mass> limit. The <spin sum> uses $\sum u\bar u=\sum v\bar v=\not p$, and the initial <spin average> contributes $1/4$. The resulting two <gamma matrix> <traces> contract to
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac14\operatorname{tr}(\not p_2\gamma^\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu)
\operatorname{tr}(\not p_3\gamma_\mu\not p_4\gamma_\nu)
&=8[(p_2\cdot p_3)(p_1\cdot p_4)+(p_2\cdot p_4)(p_1\cdot p_3)]\\
&=2(t^2+u^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
The initial <color average> contributes $1/N_c^2$. Summing final <color charges> gives the <distinct-flavor quark scattering color factor>
$$
K_{ss}=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^b)
=\frac{N_c^2-1}{4N_c^2}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M_s|^2}=g^4\frac{N_c^2-1}{2N_c^2}\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2}
=\frac{4g^4}{9}\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2},\qquad C=\frac{4g^4}{9}.}
$$
Equivalently $C=64\pi^2\alpha_s^2/9$ for the <QCD coupling> $\alpha_s=g^2/(4\pi)$. There is no initial <gluon> <spin average>: the <gluon> is internal.

The two <Bhabha scattering> <tree-level Feynman diagrams> are the two panels of <2/b/i/solution> with a <photon> mediator: $s$-channel annihilation with internal <momentum> $p_1+p_2$, and $t$-channel exchange with internal <momentum> $p_1-p_3$. Each <interaction vertex> preserves the <electron> species. There is no independent $u$-channel <Feynman diagram>, since the outgoing <electron> and <positron> are distinguishable.

Removing the <color charge> factor from <2/b/i/solution> and replacing $g$ by $e$ gives
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M_s|^2}=2e^4\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2}.
$$
<Crossing symmetry> interchanges $s$ and $t$ for the exchange <Feynman diagram>, giving
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M_t|^2}=2e^4\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}.
$$
Squaring the sum of the two <scattering amplitudes> also produces the <interference> term $2\operatorname{Re}\overline{\mathcal M_s\mathcal M_t^*}$. The given third term must be this cross term: it has both <propagator> denominators $st$, whereas a squared individual <Feynman diagram> has $s^2$ or $t^2$. With the correct relative <fermionic sign>, its value is $4e^4u^2/(st)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=2e^4\left[\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2}+\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}+\frac{2u^2}{st}\right].}
$$
The coefficient of the last displayed fraction is positive, but $s>0$ and $t<0$ in the physical scattering region, so this <interference> is destructive. The relative sign originates in the two possible external <fermion> contractions; changing an external-state phase changes both <scattering amplitudes> together.<Quantum Field Theory by David Tong>[§6.6, Electron Positron Scattering: annihilation and exchange spinor contractions and their relative sign]{ref}

= Solution
{parent=iii}
{c}

Both $s$-channel annihilation and $t$-channel <gluon> exchange are now allowed. Their <spin sum> factors are the <Bhabha scattering> factors with $e\mapsto g$. Their <color charge> tensors, with the external indices of <2/b/i/solution>, are
$$
c_s=(T^a)_{ji}(T^a)_{kl},\qquad
c_t=(T^a)_{ki}(T^a)_{jl}.
$$
Each squared term has $K_{ss}=K_{tt}=(N_c^2-1)/(4N_c^2)$. The cross term instead has the <color average>
$$
K_{st}=\frac1{N_c^2}\sum_{a,b}\operatorname{tr}(T^aT^bT^aT^b).
$$
To evaluate it, use the <SU(N) fundamental completeness relation>
$$
\sum_a(T^a)_{ij}(T^a)_{kl}=\frac12\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}-\frac1{N_c}\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}\right).
$$
This follows by completing the orthogonal basis of traceless <Hermitian matrices> with the identity. Since $\operatorname{tr}T^b=0$, it gives $\sum_aT^aT^bT^a=-T^b/(2N_c)$, and hence
$$
\boxed{K_{st}=-\frac{N_c^2-1}{4N_c^3}=-\frac1{N_c}K_{ss}.}
$$
Multiplying the two squared <Bhabha scattering> terms by $K_{ss}$ and its cross term by $K_{st}$ gives
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=g^4\frac{N_c^2-1}{2N_c^2}
\left[\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2}+\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}-\frac2{N_c}\frac{u^2}{st}\right].}
$$
For the <SU(3)> <gauge group>, this is
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac{4g^4}{9}\left[\frac{t^2+u^2}{s^2}+\frac{s^2+u^2}{t^2}\right]
-\frac{8g^4}{27}\frac{u^2}{st}.}
$$
The negative <color charge> overlap reverses the sign of the <QED> <interference>. Since $st<0$, this cross contribution is positive in the physical region. No additional <spinor> calculation is needed; all the new information is in the <SU(N) fundamental completeness relation>.

The <Goldstone theorem> states that spontaneous breaking of a continuous internal <global symmetry in field theory> in a local relativistic <quantum field theory> produces a zero-<mass> <Goldstone boson> for each independent broken generator, under the usual positive-state-space and conserved-current hypotheses. For $G\to H$ there are $\dim G-\dim H$ symmetry-required <Goldstone bosons>. This count assumes a Lorentz-invariant <vacuum state>; nonrelativistic counting can differ.

At the classical level, invariance of the <scalar potential> gives $\partial_iV\,(T^a\phi)_i=0$. Differentiating at a stationary <vacuum expectation value> $\phi_0$ yields
$$
\boxed{(M^2)_{ij}(T^a\phi_0)_j=0,\qquad (M^2)_{ij}=\left.\partial_i\partial_jV\right|_{\phi_0}.}
$$
These are the <Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix>. Broken generators have $T^a\phi_0\ne0$, so their independent directions have zero quadratic <mass> with a canonical <kinetic term>. The theorem guarantees these modes; it does not exclude further accidental zero-<mass> modes. A local <gauge symmetry> is a redundancy and does not meet the physical global-symmetry hypothesis: the <Higgs mechanism> can instead supply longitudinal <particle polarizations> of massive <gauge bosons>.

= Solution

Put $r^2=\phi_i\phi_i$. Completing the square in the <scalar potential> gives
$$
V=\frac\lambda4\left(r^2-\frac{\mu^2}{\lambda}\right)^2-\frac{\mu^4}{4\lambda}.
$$
Since $\lambda>0$, its global minima form the <vacuum manifold>
$$
\boxed{r=v,\qquad v=\frac{\mu}{\sqrt\lambda}.}
$$
Here $\mu$ denotes the positive square root of the parameter $\mu^2$. The origin is a local maximum, not a <ground state>. Choose $\langle\boldsymbol\phi\rangle=(v,0,0)$. Its <stabilizer subgroup> is the <SO(2)> subgroup of rotations in the $(2,3)$ plane, so the stated <SO(3)> <global symmetry in field theory> is spontaneously broken to <SO(2)>.

Write $\phi_1=v+h$, $\phi_2=\pi_2$, $\phi_3=\pi_3$. The <kinetic terms> stay canonically normalized, while the <scalar mass matrix> is
$$
(M^2)_{ij}=(-\mu^2+\lambda\phi^2)\delta_{ij}+2\lambda\phi_i\phi_j,
\qquad
M^2\big|_{(v,0,0)}=\operatorname{diag}(2\mu^2,0,0).
$$
Equivalently, the quadratic <scalar potential> is $\mu^2h^2$. Therefore the particle <masses> are
$$
\boxed{m_h=\sqrt2\mu,\qquad m_{\pi_2}=m_{\pi_3}=0.}
$$
The two angular <Goldstone bosons> match $\dim SO(3)-\dim SO(2)=3-1=2$. The radial displacement changes $r$ and costs <potential energy>, giving the massive <Higgs mode>. These are tree-level <masses>; the <Goldstone theorem> protects the angular zero-<mass> modes in the exact global theory.

= Solution
{parent=iii}
{c}

For any fixed $r\geq0$, the source contribution obeys $c\phi_1\geq cr$, because $c<0$ and $\phi_1\leq r$. Equality occurs only at $(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3)=(r,0,0)$ when $r>0$. Thus the global <ground state> lies on the positive first axis. Minimize there:
$$
f(r)=-\frac12\mu^2r^2+\frac14\lambda r^4+cr,\qquad
f'(r)=\lambda r^3-\mu^2r+c.
$$
For $0\leq r\leq v=\mu/\sqrt\lambda$, $f'(r)<0$. For $r>v$, $f''(r)=3\lambda r^2-\mu^2>0$, so $f'$ increases strictly from $c$ to $+\infty$. There is exactly one root $\tilde v>v$, and it is the unique global minimum. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle\boldsymbol\phi\rangle=(\tilde v,0,0),\qquad
\lambda\tilde v^3-\mu^2\tilde v+c=0,\qquad \tilde v>\frac\mu{\sqrt\lambda}>0.}
$$
This argument selects the true <ground state> even if the full cubic has additional negative stationary roots.

Expand $\phi_1=\tilde v+h$, $\phi_2=\pi_2$, $\phi_3=\pi_3$. The source is linear, so it shifts the expansion point but has zero <Hessian matrix>. The <scalar mass matrix> is diagonal at the new <vacuum expectation value>:
$$
m_h^2=-\mu^2+3\lambda\tilde v^2,\qquad
m_{\pi_2}^2=m_{\pi_3}^2=-\mu^2+\lambda\tilde v^2.
$$
Eliminating $\lambda$ with the stationary equation gives
$$
\boxed{m_h^2=2\mu^2-\frac{3c}{\tilde v},\qquad
m_{\pi_2}^2=m_{\pi_3}^2=-\frac c{\tilde v}.}
$$
All three squared <masses> are positive. The <masses> themselves are the positive square roots of these expressions.

The source explicitly reduces the stated <SO(3)> <symmetry> to <SO(2)>; the selected <vacuum state> preserves that remaining <SO(2)>. There is consequently no spontaneously broken exact continuous <global symmetry in field theory> to which the <Goldstone theorem> applies. For small $|c|$, the two angular excitations are <pseudo-Goldstone bosons>. Their squared <masses> vanish as $c\to0^-$, while $\tilde v\to\mu/\sqrt\lambda$ and $m_h^2\to2\mu^2$.

In <unitary gauge> the quadratic <action> is that of a <Proca field>. Use <Minkowski metric> $(+---)$ and add a source $J_\mu A^\mu$. <Integration by parts>, dropping the boundary term, gives
$$
S[A]=\frac12\int d^4x\,A_\mu K^{\mu\nu}A_\nu,\qquad
K^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}(\Box+M^2)-\partial^\mu\partial^\nu.
$$
With <Fourier transform> convention $A(x)=\int d^4k\,e^{-ik\cdot x}A(k)/(2\pi)^4$, its kernel is
$$
K^{\mu\nu}(k)=(M^2-k^2)g^{\mu\nu}+k^\mu k^\nu.
$$
For $k^2\ne0$, introduce the <transverse and longitudinal momentum projectors>
$$
P_T^{\mu\nu}=g^{\mu\nu}-\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2},\qquad
P_L^{\mu\nu}=\frac{k^\mu k^\nu}{k^2}.
$$
They obey $P_TP_L=0$ and $P_T+P_L=1$ as mixed-index <linear operators>. Hence
$$
K=(M^2-k^2)P_T+M^2P_L,\qquad
K^{-1}=\frac{P_T}{M^2-k^2}+\frac{P_L}{M^2}
=-\frac{g-kk/M^2}{k^2-M^2}.
$$
The final expression is regular at $k^2=0$; the apparent singularities of the individual <projectors> have cancelled.

The normalized <Gaussian functional integral> is evaluated by <completing the square> with $A\mapsto A+K^{-1}J$:
$$
\frac{Z[J]}{Z[0]}=
\frac{\int\mathcal DA\,\exp\{i(\frac12AKA+JA)\}}{\int\mathcal DA\,\exp\{i\frac12AKA\}}
=\exp\left[-\frac i2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}J_\mu(-k)(K^{-1})^{\mu\nu}(k)J_\nu(k)\right].
$$
The source-independent <functional determinant> cancels in this ratio. Two source derivatives insert two fields, so the covariant <Feynman propagator> is $iK^{-1}$, with the <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> at the physical pole:
$$
\boxed{D_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{-i}{k^2-M^2+i0}\left(g_{\mu\nu}-\frac{k_\mu k_\nu}{M^2}\right),\qquad
D_{\mu\nu}(x-y)=\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}e^{-ik\cdot(x-y)}D_{\mu\nu}(k).}
$$
This is the <Proca propagator>. Its pole residue has the three physical <particle polarizations> of a massive spin-one <gauge boson>. No additional gauge-fixing term is needed to invert this already gauge-fixed massive quadratic <action>. In a full interacting <Higgs mechanism> calculation, any field-dependent <unitary gauge> measure belongs to the interacting theory; the free quadratic <gauge boson> <propagator> above is unaffected.
