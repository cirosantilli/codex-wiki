= Solution
{parent=a}

Use an <affine parameter> $\lambda$, with dots denoting its derivatives, and take $\alpha\ne0$ so the metric is nondegenerate for $r>0$. The <geodesic Lagrangian> is $\mathcal L=(\dot r^2+\alpha^2r^4\dot\phi^2)/2$. The <Euler-Lagrange equations> give
$$
\boxed{\ddot r-2\alpha^2r^3\dot\phi^2=0,\qquad \frac{d}{d\lambda}(\alpha^2r^4\dot\phi)=0,}
$$
or $\ddot\phi+4\dot r\dot\phi/r=0$. The conserved cyclic <angular momentum> is
$$
\boxed{J=\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot\phi}=\alpha^2r^4\dot\phi.}
$$
This is the <cyclic angular momentum in a rotational metric>, associated with the rotational <Killing vector> $\partial_\phi$. Explicitly, $\dot J=\alpha^2(4r^3\dot r\dot\phi+r^4\ddot\phi)=0$ by the angular equation.

Differentiating the <Lagrangian> and substituting both equations gives
$$
\frac{d\mathcal L}{d\lambda}=\dot r\ddot r+2\alpha^2r^3\dot r\dot\phi^2+\alpha^2r^4\dot\phi\ddot\phi=(2+2-4)\alpha^2r^3\dot r\dot\phi^2=0.
$$
Thus **the squared tangent norm $2\mathcal L$ is constant**. If $\lambda$ is <arc length>, it equals one; its numerical value otherwise depends on affine normalization. An arbitrary nonaffine parametrization would not preserve this constant-speed statement.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Denote the parent <rest mass> by $M$, the daughter <rest masses> by $m_i$, and their total energies in any <inertial frame> by $E_{\rm before}$ and $E_{\rm after}$. For an isolated disintegration with all products included, <energy> conservation gives $E_{\rm after}=E_{\rm before}$. The corresponding <kinetic energies> are $K_{\rm before}=E_{\rm before}-Mc^2$ and $K_{\rm after}=E_{\rm after}-\sum_i m_ic^2$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\Delta K=K_{\rm after}-K_{\rm before}=\left(M-\sum_i m_i\right)c^2.}
$$
Every <mass> on the right is a <rest mass>, hence invariant under a <Lorentz transformation>. This proves the <kinetic energy> released by relativistic decay is frame independent even though either total <kinetic energy> separately is not. In the parent <rest frame> the initial <kinetic energy> vanishes and the final <kinetic energy> equals the <energy> associated with this <mass defect>. External <energy> input or omission of radiation would invalidate the energy-cancellation step; if <photons> are products, include their energies with zero rest-mass subtraction.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Put $v=r\omega$, $\beta=v/c<1$ and $\gamma=(1-\beta^2)^{-1/2}$. For a <photon> emitted in laboratory direction $\mathbf n$ toward the stationary receiver, its <frequency> there is
$$
\nu=\frac{\nu_0}{\gamma(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_e/c)}.
$$
Since the receiver lies outside the circle, two emission points have a line of sight tangent to the circle. At those points the source <velocity> is directly toward or away from the receiver, so $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_e=\pm v$. Thus the <doppler extrema for a circularly moving source> are
$$
\boxed{\nu_{\max}=\nu_0\sqrt{\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}},\qquad \nu_{\min}=\nu_0\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}.}
$$
They do not depend on $d$, provided $d>r$; changing $d$ changes the emission and reception phases of the extrema. Closest and farthest source positions instead have transverse <velocity> relative to that ray, and are not the extrema.

\Image[paper-1-rotating-doppler.png]
{title=Original tangent-ray geometry for the largest approaching and receding shifts of a circular source}

For the corotating receiver, <velocities> must be evaluated at the actual emission and reception events. An unobstructed <vacuum> ray satisfies $\mathbf x_r-\mathbf x_e=c\Delta t\,\mathbf n$, while rigid steady rotation gives $\mathbf v_r-\mathbf v_e=\boldsymbol\omega\times(\mathbf x_r-\mathbf x_e)$. Therefore $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_r=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_e$. The emitter and receiver have equal <speed> magnitudes, hence equal <Lorentz factors>. The <frequency ratio of corotating clocks> is consequently
$$
\frac{\nu_r}{\nu_0}=\frac{\gamma_r(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_r/c)}{\gamma_e(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_e/c)}=\boxed{1}.
$$
Thus **the rim receiver measures $\nu_0$**, independently of its angular separation. Different transit times or a <Sagnac effect> do not by themselves imply a <frequency> change between these identical-radius clocks. The model treats $\nu_0$ as a fixed proper clock <frequency> and assumes a signal path is available; it does not specify the disc's electromagnetic opacity.

= Solution
{parent=a}

The <weak equivalence principle> states that freely falling, sufficiently small test bodies have the same motion in a given gravitational field irrespective of composition or <mass>, neglecting their own gravity and nongravitational <forces>. In the Newtonian limit, $m_i\mathbf a=m_g\mathbf g$ makes this universality equivalent to a universal gravitational-to-inertial <mass> ratio, conventionally set to one.

The <Einstein equivalence principle> adds that local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling, nonrotating laboratory have the laws of <special relativity>, independent of the laboratory's position and <velocity>. At an event one can choose a <local inertial frame> with $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\partial_\alpha g_{\mu\nu}=0$. A freely falling observer feels no supporting <force>, while a supported observer can model a locally uniform gravitational field by an accelerated laboratory. This explains why freely moving particles follow <timelike geodesics> and <light> follows <null geodesics> when other interactions are negligible.

The locality qualification is essential. The <Riemann curvature tensor> generally remains nonzero; separated freely falling bodies have tidal relative acceleration, measured by <geodesic deviation>. A finite-size experiment can therefore distinguish a curved gravitational field from a merely accelerated coordinate description of flat <spacetime>. Equivalence alone also does not select the complete <Einstein field equations>.

A useful weak-field consequence is the upward <photon> shift $\Delta\nu/\nu\simeq-gH/c^2$ over laboratory height $H$. In an upward-accelerating laboratory the receiver gains recession <velocity> during the <light> flight, giving the same leading redshift. The <Pound-Rebka experiment> tested this with resonant absorption of 14.4-keV <gamma rays> over about 22.5 m. A controlled source <velocity> calibrated the Doppler sensitivity; interchanging source and absorber reversed the gravitational contribution while retaining an intrinsic source–absorber offset. Monitoring their <temperature> difference was essential because lattice-motion shifts could mimic the small signal.<Apparent Weight of Photons by Robert Vivian Pound and Glen Rebka>[pp. 338–340, baseline, velocity calibration, reversed source/absorber positions and temperature corrections]{ref} This verifies a gravitational clock-shift prediction; by itself it is not a unique test selecting <general relativity> over every alternative metric theory.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Consider an ideal spherical collapse with a <vacuum> exterior of constant <mass> $M$. Once <pressure> support is insufficient, a surface can continue inward and cross the <Schwarzschild event horizon> $r_s=2GM/c^2$ in finite comoving <proper time>. An <event horizon> is a causal boundary: <light> emitted from inside cannot reach distant observers. It is not a material shell or, in the classical Schwarzschild solution, a local <curvature singularity> at $r=r_s$.

The <distant view of gravitational collapse> differs from the surface clock's history. Exterior <Schwarzschild time> has $dt/d\tau\propto(1-r_s/r)^{-1}$ for regular infall and diverges as the surface approaches the horizon. Outgoing radial <light> also takes an increasingly long coordinate time to escape: $dt/dr=[c(1-r_s/r)]^{-1}$ has a logarithmically divergent integral at the horizon. Signals received far away are progressively delayed and redshifted; both gravity and the inward emitter's <Doppler shift> contribute. <photons> carry less received <energy> and arrive at a lower rate, while the available escape directions also narrow.

Thus **a distant observer does not receive a <light> signal announcing the crossing; the image fades and reddens rather than remaining a permanently bright frozen surface**. A finite-band detector with finite sensitivity loses it in finite observation time. The divergence of one coordinate chart does not prevent the material crossing in finite <proper time>; horizon-regular coordinates describe that continuation. The pressureless <Oppenheimer-Snyder model> makes this distinction explicit, without representing all the <pressure>, rotation and radiation of a real stellar collapse.<On Continued Gravitational Contraction by J. Robert Oppenheimer and Hartland Snyder>[abstract and pp. 455–459, finite comoving collapse time and redshifted distant description]{ref}

\Image[paper-1-infall-signals.png]
{title=Original ideal radial-infall example separating finite proper-time crossing, delayed reception and fading frequency}

The plotted example is a test-particle <geodesic> falling from rest at infinity, used to isolate the coordinate and signal effects; it is not a fitted stellar-collapse <light> curve. No actual observer can hover on the horizon to supply a static local frame there.

= Solution
{parent=c}

A <light> ray follows a <null geodesic>. For a weak, approximately spherical field and <impact parameter> $b\gg GM/c^2$, the asymptotic <Schwarzschild light deflection> is
$$
\boxed{\Delta\theta=\frac{4GM}{bc^2}.}
$$
At the solar limb this is about $1.75$ arcseconds, whereas a simple Newtonian fast-particle calculation gives half that value. The difference tests relativistic <spacetime> geometry beyond the universality of slow-body free fall. The quoted <angle> compares incoming and outgoing directions far from the deflector, not a locally measured turn at a sharp vertex.

\Image[paper-1-light-bending.png]
{title=Original weak-deflection sketch with incoming direction, curved ray and apparent sky direction; the angle is exaggerated}

For an eclipse measurement, photograph background stars near the obscured <Sun> and compare their apparent positions with reference plates taken when that field is away from the <Sun>. Back-projecting the deflected incoming <light> makes a star appear displaced outward from the <Sun>'s apparent position. Fit the radial displacement against inverse angular separation from the <Sun>, while allowing plate translation, rotation and scale. The expected pattern must be separated from <atmospheric refraction>, <optical distortion> and changes of focus or scale, rather than inferred from one displaced star.

The 1919 expeditions obtained observations at Sobral and Príncipe, published by <Frank Watson Dyson>, <Arthur Eddington> and <Charles Davidson> in 1920. The original report compares eclipse and reference plates and discusses instrument scale and image-quality limitations; it should not be treated as an exact modern precision measurement.<A determination of the deflection of light by the Sun's gravitational field by Frank Watson Dyson and others>[pp. 291–333, observing arrangements, reference photographs, plate reductions and final deflection comparisons]{ref} Repeated observations with independent instruments and calibrated reference fields test whether the displacement follows the predicted geometry. <vacuum> gravitational bending is achromatic to this order, a useful distinction from dispersive propagation effects.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Use signature $(+---)$, coordinates $x^0=ct$ and <four-velocity> $u^\mu=dx^\mu/d\tau$ normalized by $u_\mu u^\mu=c^2$. The derivative is the metric-compatible <Levi-Civita connection>. Fix the curvature sign by $[\nabla_\alpha,\nabla_\beta]V^\mu=R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}V^\nu$. Then the required contractions are
$$
\boxed{R_{\nu\beta}=R^\mu{}_{\nu\mu\beta},\qquad R=g^{\nu\beta}R_{\nu\beta}.}
$$
With $G^{\mu\nu}=R^{\mu\nu}-g^{\mu\nu}R/2$, the <contracted Bianchi identity> gives $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu}=0$. The <Einstein field equations> $G^{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g^{\mu\nu}=(8\pi G/c^4)T^{\mu\nu}$, with constant $\Lambda$, therefore require <covariant conservation of stress-energy>. This is a consistency relation between geometry and the total matter/field source. It does not mean $\partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ in arbitrary coordinates, nor guarantee a global conserved <energy> in an arbitrary curved <spacetime>. The geometric identity alone, without the field equations or matter dynamics, is not an equation of motion for a freely specified material source.

For <relativistic dust>, $\rho$ is proper rest-mass <density>, measured in the fluid <rest frame>, and $u^\mu$ is its timelike <four-velocity> with the $c^2$ normalization above. In a local Cartesian <inertial frame> $u^\mu=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$, so
$$
T^{00}=\rho\gamma^2c^2,\qquad \boxed{T^{0i}=\rho\gamma^2cv^i.}
$$
Accordingly $T^{0i}/c$ is <momentum density> and $cT^{0i}$ is <energy flux> in direction $i$. The equality of momentum-density and energy-flux components after the appropriate factors of $c$ reflects the symmetry of the <stress-energy tensor>. In the comoving frame the spatial stress is zero, as required for pressureless matter.

Expand the dust conservation equation by the <product rule>:
$$
0=u^\nu\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)+\rho u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu.
$$
Contract with $u_\nu$. <metric compatibility> and constant normalization give $u_\nu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=\nabla_\mu(u_\nu u^\nu)/2=0$, leaving $c^2\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)=0$. Substitute this rest-mass continuity law back and divide by nonzero $\rho$:
$$
\boxed{u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0,\qquad \frac{d^2x^\nu}{d\tau^2}+\Gamma^\nu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=0.}
$$
This proves <geodesic motion of uncharged dust> along each fluid flow line. <pressure> <forces> or another exchange of momentum would change the argument; at zero <density> there is no dust <worldline> to constrain.

The <electromagnetic field tensor> is antisymmetric and has six independent components. In the stated local inertial coordinates choose $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$, giving
$$
F^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-E_x/c&-E_y/c&-E_z/c\\
E_x/c&0&-B_z&B_y\\
E_y/c&B_z&0&-B_x\\
E_z/c&-B_y&B_x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The fields are those measured by that local observer; changing observer mixes them through the <Lorentz transformation> of $F$. With $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$ and $\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}/2$, two independent quadratic contractions are
$$
\boxed{F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=2(B^2-E^2/c^2),\qquad F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=-\frac4c\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B.}
$$
The second is a <pseudoscalar>: it is invariant under proper <Lorentz transformations> and changes sign under an <orientation> reversal. Opposite choices for the <orientation> <tensor> change its written sign. A nonzero <null electromagnetic field> can have both contractions zero, so the two numbers do not determine whether the <tensor> itself vanishes.

For <charged relativistic dust>, insert $j^\alpha=(q/m)\rho u^\alpha$ into the force-density equation:
$$
u^\nu\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)+\rho u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=\frac qm\rho F^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha.
$$
Contraction with $u_\nu$ again gives the continuity law, because $u_\nu F^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha=0$ by antisymmetry. After removing the continuity term,
$$
\boxed{u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=\frac qm F^{\nu\alpha}u_\alpha.}
$$
In a <local inertial frame> the spatial components reduce to $d(\gamma m\mathbf v)/dt=q(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)$ and the temporal component gives $d(\gamma mc^2)/dt=q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v$. This is the relativistic <Lorentz force>, with gravity represented by the connection. The <force> is orthogonal to $u$ and preserves the rest-mass normalization. Matter alone now exchanges <energy> and momentum with the electromagnetic field; adding the field <tensor>'s stress-energy restores zero divergence of the combined source.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Take $\tau$ as particle <proper time>, dots as $d/d\tau$, and $f(r)=1-2\mu/r$ with $\mu=GM/c^2$. In the equatorial plane the <geodesic Lagrangian> is
$$
\mathcal L=\frac12\left(c^2f\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2\right).
$$
The <cyclic coordinates> give $d(c^2f\dot t)/d\tau=0$ and $d(-r^2\dot\phi)/d\tau=0$. Set the resulting constants to $c^2k$ and $-h$ respectively. The explicit second-order equations are
$$
\boxed{\ddot t+\frac{f'}f\dot r\dot t=0,\qquad \ddot\phi+\frac2r\dot r\dot\phi=0,}
$$
$$
\boxed{\ddot r-\frac{f'}{2f}\dot r^2+\frac{c^2ff'}2\dot t^2-rf\dot\phi^2=0,\qquad f'=\frac{2\mu}{r^2}.}
$$
The polar equation is satisfied by $\theta=\pi/2$, $\dot\theta=0$, consistently with <spherical symmetry>. The proper-time normalization $ds^2=c^2d\tau^2$ supplies the third first integral:
$$
\boxed{f\dot t=k,\qquad r^2\dot\phi=h,\qquad c^2f\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2=c^2.}
$$
The normalization is $2\mathcal L=c^2$; it is distinct from choosing an arbitrary affine tangent's norm.

Stationarity and axial symmetry make the covariant time and azimuthal momenta constant. For physical momentum and coordinate $t$ in seconds, $P_t=mc^2f\dot t=mc^2k$ and $P_\phi=-mr^2\dot\phi=-mh$. Thus **$mc^2k$ is the conserved <energy> normalized at infinity and $mh$ is the signed axial <angular momentum>**. This is <Killing energy with positive time signature>; a convention with negative timelike norm uses the opposite contraction sign. The supplied covariant-momentum form of the <geodesic equation> makes these cyclic constants immediate, but its parametrization must be understood: for $p^\mu=dx^\mu/d\lambda$ it is exactly as written. For physical $P^\mu=m\,dx^\mu/d\tau$, proper-time differentiation instead gives $dP_\mu/d\tau=(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\sigma})P^\nu P^\sigma/(2m)$; equivalently, the displayed mass-free form uses $\lambda=\tau/m$. This affine momentum normalization does not change either conservation law.

Multiply the normalization equation by $f$ and substitute the two cyclic integrals. Rearrangement gives the radial <energy> equation
$$
\boxed{\dot r^2+\frac{h^2}{r^2}\left(1-\frac{2\mu}r\right)-\frac{2c^2\mu}r=c^2(k^2-1).}
$$
For radial marginal escape $h=0$ and the future-directed constant is $k=1$, because the <speed> at infinity vanishes. Choose the outward root:
$$
\dot r=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}},\qquad \frac23(r^{3/2}-r_0^{3/2})=\sqrt{2GM}\,\tau.
$$
Hence the <marginal escape geodesic in Schwarzschild spacetime> has
$$
\boxed{r(\tau)=\left(r_0^{3/2}+a\tau\right)^{2/3},\qquad a=\frac32\sqrt{2GM}=\frac{3c}{2}\sqrt{2\mu}.}
$$
The constant $a$ has units of <length> to the power $3/2$ divided by time. This radius is an areal coordinate, not accumulated local proper <distance>.

A <Schwarzschild static observer> at exterior radius $r$ uses local radial rulers $d\ell=dr/\sqrt f$ and a local clock $dT=\sqrt f\,dt$. Therefore the <local radial speed of a Schwarzschild geodesic> is
$$
v_{\rm loc}=\frac{d\ell}{dT}=\frac1f\frac{dr}{dt}=\frac{\dot r}{k}.
$$
For the marginal trajectory $k=1$ this gives
$$
\boxed{v_{\rm loc}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}.}
$$
Its equality to $\dot r$ is particular to $k=1$; the local definition, rather than that accidental equality, is the required observer measurement. The Schwarzschild-coordinate <velocity> is instead $dr/dt=f\sqrt{2GM/r}$.

For nonzero asymptotic <speed> $0\le u<c$, the same <energy> constant is $k=\gamma_\infty=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. Combining the radial equation with the local-ruler relation gives <radial Schwarzschild escape with asymptotic speed>
$$
\boxed{v_{\rm loc}(r)=c\sqrt{1-\frac{f}{k^2}}=\sqrt{u^2+\frac{2GM}{r}\left(1-\frac{u^2}{c^2}\right)}.}
$$
The positive root describes the outward particle, at radii on its trajectory with $r\ge r_0$. Independently, a static observer has <four-velocity> $U=(1/\sqrt f,0,0,0)$ in these coordinates, giving relative <Lorentz factor> $g(U,u_{\rm particle})/c^2=\sqrt f\dot t=k/\sqrt f$ and the same <speed>. As $r\to\infty$ it tends to $u$; for $u=0$ it gives the preceding result. Its limiting value approaches $c$ as $r\downarrow2GM/c^2$, but that is a limit through exterior observers: **no static timelike observer exists on or inside the horizon**.
