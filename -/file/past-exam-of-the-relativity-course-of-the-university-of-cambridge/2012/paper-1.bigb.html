= Solution
{parent=a}

In the <rest frame>, <conservation of energy> would require $E_\gamma=mc^2$, whereas <conservation of momentum> would require the <photon> <momentum> to vanish. These contradict $|\mathbf p_\gamma|=E_\gamma/c$. More generally, <four-momentum> conservation would equate the initial massive <four-momentum> to a null <four-momentum>, although their <Minkowski norms> differ:
$$
P_\mu P^\mu=m^2c^2>0,\qquad p_{\gamma\mu}p_\gamma^\mu=0.
$$
**An isolated massive particle cannot decay into one <photon> in any <inertial frame>.** Directly, a laboratory <speed> $u<c$ gives $E=\gamma mc^2$ and $|\mathbf p|=\gamma mu$; matching a <photon> would require $E=c|\mathbf p|$, hence $u=c$. Recoil against another object could change the kinematics, but would introduce an additional participant.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Write the <four-velocity> $U^\mu=\gamma(c,\mathbf v)$ and <four-acceleration> $A^\mu=dU^\mu/d\tau$. Since $U_\mu A^\mu=0$, spatial <orthogonality> $\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf A=0$ implies $U^0A^0=0$. Thus $A^0=c\,d\gamma/d\tau=0$ and **the <speed> is constant**. If “<acceleration>” is read as the laboratory three-acceleration instead, $\gamma\mathbf v\cdot d\mathbf v/dt=0$ immediately gives the same conclusion.

For <four-acceleration in uniform circular motion>, the <speed> is $r|\omega|<c$, so $\gamma=(1-r^2\omega^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ is constant. Differentiating the <four-velocity> using $d/d\tau=\gamma\,d/dt$ gives
$$
\boxed{A^\mu=\left(0,-\gamma^2r\omega^2\cos\omega t,
-\gamma^2r\omega^2\sin\omega t,0\right).}
$$
The <proper acceleration> is $\sqrt{-A_\mu A^\mu}=\gamma^2r\omega^2$. The vanishing time component reflects constant <energy>; the spatial components describe the changing <momentum> direction.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

The <geodesics of the product-weighted diagonal plane metric> follow from the <geodesic Lagrangian> $L=(y^2\dot x^2+x^2\dot y^2)/2$. With dots denoting an <affine parameter> <derivative>, its <Euler-Lagrange equations> give
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
y^2\ddot x+2y\dot y\dot x-x\dot y^2&=0,\\
x^2\ddot y+2x\dot x\dot y-y\dot x^2&=0.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Substituting $y=mx$ with constant nonzero $m$ reduces both equations to $x\ddot x+\dot x^2=0$. Thus $x\dot x$ is constant, and a suitable affine parametrization exists; explicitly, $x^2=C_1\lambda+C_2$ on a segment of fixed sign.

**These straight rays are <geodesics> only in the regular domain $xy\ne0$.** The <metric tensor> <determinant> is $x^2y^2$. On the axes, including the origin, this is a <degenerate metric tensor>: the calculation does not establish a regular <geodesic> continuing through the origin. The slope-zero ray is outside the regular domain. Geometrically, on each quadrant the change $\rho=|xy|/\sqrt2$, $\vartheta=\log|y/x|$ gives $ds^2=d\rho^2+\rho^2d\vartheta^2$. A fixed slope has constant $\vartheta$ and is therefore locally a radial <geodesic> of a flat <metric tensor>.

= Solution
{parent=a}

In <general relativity>, <light> follows <null geodesics>. A weak <gravitational field> outside an isolated spherical <mass> gives the <Schwarzschild light deflection>
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac{4GM}{bc^2}}
$$
for <impact parameter> $b\gg GM/c^2$. At the solar limb this is approximately $1.75$ arcseconds. A Newtonian corpuscular calculation gives half this value; the full result depends on spatial <curvature> as well as the <gravitational time dilation>. The <parametrized post-Newtonian formalism> predicts $\alpha=2(1+\gamma_{\rm PPN})GM/(bc^2)$, with $\gamma_{\rm PPN}=1$ in <general relativity>.

\Image[paper-1-light-deflection.png]
{title=Weak-field light deflection}

The <Eddington experiment> compared photographic star positions near the eclipsed <Sun> with reference exposures taken without the <Sun> in that field. Rays bend toward the <Sun>, but their backward extrapolations place the apparent stellar images farther from it. Plate scale, focus changes, <atmospheric refraction> and comparison-field alignment must be separated from a radial displacement proportional to $1/b$. The original report discusses the instruments, plate reductions and competing predicted deflections; the test is a quantitative comparison with uncertainty, not a unique proof of a gravitational theory.<a determination of the deflection of light by the Sun's gravitational field by Frank Watson Dyson and others>[1920, pp. 291–333: eclipse observations, instruments and plate reductions]{ref}

<radio interferometry> permits an independent test without an eclipse. A 2005 <Very Long Baseline Array> experiment observed four compact sources at 43, 23 and 15 GHz. Differential <astrometry> suppressed common antenna errors, while a fit $\mathbf p(\nu)=\mathbf p_0+\mathbf I\nu^{-2}$ separated <refraction> in the <solar corona> from achromatic gravitational deflection. Tropospheric errors and radio-source structure also required calibration. The reported $\gamma_{\rm PPN}=0.9998\pm0.0003$ was consistent with <general relativity>.<Progress in Measurements of the Gravitational Bending of Radio Waves Using the VLBA by Edward Fomalont and others>[2009, §§II–III on observations, calibration and coronal subtraction; §V on the reported result]{ref}

= Solution
{parent=b}

<tidal forces> measure differences in <gravitational acceleration> across an extended region. Neighboring freely falling particles each have zero <four-acceleration>, but their separation need not accelerate uniformly. Let $u^\mu$ be the tangent to a reference <timelike geodesic> and $\xi^\mu$ its infinitesimal separation from a neighboring one, chosen from an affinely parametrized <geodesic congruence>. Using the <Riemann curvature tensor> convention specified in Solution 3,
$$
\frac{D^2\xi^\alpha}{D\tau^2}
=-R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
u^\beta u^\gamma\xi^\delta .
$$
Changing the overall <curvature> convention changes this displayed sign. The <covariant derivatives> compare <vectors> by <parallel transport>, avoiding a coordinate-dependent subtraction of <accelerations>.

In the <Newtonian limit>, expand the force field at two nearby points:
$$
\ddot\xi_i=-\partial_i\partial_j\Phi\,\xi_j.
$$
For $\Phi=-GM/r$, a local <orthonormal frame> aligned with the radius gives
$$
\boxed{\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}
=\frac{GM}{r^3}\operatorname{diag}(2,-1,-1)\boldsymbol\xi.}
$$
There is radial stretching and transverse compression; the trace vanishes outside the <mass>. Two freely falling test <masses> and a measurement of their changing separation implement <gravity gradiometry>. Their common <acceleration> drops out, while a relative <acceleration> proportional to separation remains. A <local inertial frame> removes <Christoffel symbols> at a point, but cannot remove nonzero <curvature> over a finite region.

= Solution
{parent=c}

With Cartesian <Minkowski spacetime> coordinates $x^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$, introduce the <four-current> $J^\mu=(c\rho,\mathbf J)$ and <electromagnetic four-potential> $\mathcal A^\mu=(\phi/c,\mathbf A)$. The antisymmetric <electromagnetic field tensor> $F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu\mathcal A^\nu-\partial^\nu\mathcal A^\mu$ has components $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=-\epsilon^{ijk}B_k$. <Maxwell equations> become
$$
\boxed{\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=\mu_0J^\nu,\qquad
\partial_\mu\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=0,}
\qquad
\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma},
\quad \epsilon^{0123}=+1.
$$
The time and space components reproduce <Gauss's law> and the <Ampère-Maxwell equation>; the dual equation gives absence of <magnetic monopoles> and <Faraday's law of induction>. Antisymmetry gives <charge conservation> $\partial_\nu J^\nu=0$. The <relativistic Lorentz force> is $dp^\mu/d\tau=qF^{\mu\nu}u_\nu$. Every displayed equation transforms tensorially under a <Lorentz transformation>; the <electric field> and <magnetic field> are different components of the same object.

Two independent algebraic <electromagnetic field invariants> are
$$
\boxed{F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=2(B^2-E^2/c^2),\qquad
F_{\mu\nu}\widetilde F^{\mu\nu}=-4\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B/c.}
$$
The second is a <pseudoscalar>: it is invariant under proper <Lorentz transformations> but changes sign under <spatial reflection>. For perpendicular fields, the sign of the first distinguishes a frame with purely <magnetic field> from one with purely <electric field>. Both invariants vanish for a <plane electromagnetic wave> with $\mathbf E\perp\mathbf B$ and $E=cB$, although the field itself is nonzero.

= Solution
{parent=3}

The <Einstein tensor> and its contractions are
$$
G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}R,\qquad
R_{\mu\nu}=R^\alpha{}_{\mu\alpha\nu},\qquad
R=g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}.
$$
To match the supplied <Ricci tensor> components, use the <Riemann curvature tensor> convention
$$
R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}
=\partial_\nu\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma}
-\partial_\mu\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma}
+\Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}
-\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma}.
$$
This convention also fixes the sign used for <geodesic deviation> above. In <vacuum>, tracing the <Einstein field equations> gives $-R+4\Lambda=0$. Substitution therefore yields **$R_{\mu\nu}=\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$**.

Differentiate the angular field equation to obtain
$$
-2A'-rA''=2\Lambda r,\qquad
\frac{A'}r+\frac{A''}2=-\Lambda.
$$
Consequently $R_{00}=\Lambda A=\Lambda g_{00}$ and $R_{11}=-\Lambda/A=\Lambda g_{11}$. The other angular component follows from $R_{33}=\sin^2\theta\,R_{22}$. Thus the single angular equation implies all the displayed <vacuum> equations wherever this coordinate chart is regular.

Integrating $(rA)'=1-\Lambda r^2$ gives $A=1+C/r-\Lambda r^2/3$. Match $g_{tt}\simeq1+2\Phi$ with the <Newtonian gravitational potential> $\Phi=-GM/r$ in the weak-field overlap region to identify $C=-2GM$. Hence the <Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime> has
$$
\boxed{A(r)=1-\frac{2GM}{r}-\frac{\Lambda r^2}{3}\quad(c=1).}
$$
Negative $\Lambda$ instead gives the <Schwarzschild--anti-de Sitter black hole> case. This matching fixes the normalization locally; a spacetime with nonzero $\Lambda$ is not asymptotically flat.

For <timelike geodesics> in the <orbital plane>, take <proper time> as parameter and use $L=(A\dot t^2-\dot r^2/A-r^2\dot\phi^2)/2$. The <cyclic coordinates> give $A\dot t=k$ and $r^2\dot\phi=h$; the angular <canonical momentum> is $-h$ in this signature. The <four-velocity> normalization $2L=1$ then gives
$$
\boxed{\dot r^2=k^2-A(r)\left(1+\frac{h^2}{r^2}\right).}
$$
Here $k$ is conserved <Killing energy> per unit rest <mass> in the chosen time normalization, and $h$ is conserved <angular momentum> per unit rest <mass>. There is no general interpretation of $k$ as <energy> measured at an asymptotically flat infinity when $\Lambda\ne0$.

For radial motion $h=0$, differentiation gives $2\dot r\ddot r=-A'\dot r$. The radial <Euler-Lagrange equation> extends the resulting expression through a <turning point> where division by $\dot r$ would fail. Thus
$$
\boxed{\ddot r=-\frac{A'}2=-\frac{GM}{r^2}+\frac{\Lambda r}{3}\quad(c=1).}
$$
This is the proper-time second <derivative> of the <areal radius>, not a nonzero <four-acceleration> of the freely falling particle. Positive $\Lambda$ contributes an outward term; negative $\Lambda$ reinforces inward attraction. Restoring units in the slow-motion, weak-field <Newtonian limit> gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2r}{dt^2}\simeq-\frac{GM}{r^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2r}{3},\qquad
\Phi=-\frac{GM}{r}-\frac{\Lambda c^2r^2}{6}.}
$$
The extra term describes the gravitational effect of a <cosmological constant>, not drag or a contact force. The approximation requires $GM/(rc^2)\ll1$, $|\Lambda|r^2\ll1$ and <speed> much less than $c$.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Put $f=1-2\mu/r$ and let dots now denote an <affine parameter> <derivative>. The <null geodesic> <lagrangian> in the <orbital plane> is $L=(fc^2\dot t^2-\dot r^2/f-r^2\dot\phi^2)/2$. The time and azimuthal <Euler-Lagrange equations> integrate once; the null normalization gives
$$
f\dot t=k,\qquad r^2\dot\phi=h,\qquad
fc^2\dot t^2-\dot r^2/f-r^2\dot\phi^2=0.
$$
For completeness, the differential radial <geodesic equation> is
$$
\ddot r+\frac{c^2ff'}2\dot t^2-\frac{f'}{2f}\dot r^2
-fr\dot\phi^2=0;
$$
the remaining differential equations are $\ddot t+(f'/f)\dot t\dot r=0$ and $\ddot\phi+2\dot r\dot\phi/r=0$. Spherical symmetry permits the equatorial choice.

Eliminating $\dot t,\dot\phi$ yields $\dot r^2+h^2f/r^2=c^2k^2$. For $h\ne0$, divide by $h^2$:
$$
\boxed{\frac{\dot r^2}{h^2}+V_{\rm eff}(r)=\frac1{b^2},\qquad
V_{\rm eff}=\frac1{r^2}-\frac{2\mu}{r^3},\qquad
b=\frac{|h|}{c|k|}.}
$$
The <impact parameter> is unchanged by a common rescaling of the <affine parameter>. At infinity it is $c$ times <angular momentum> divided by <energy>. Radial <photons> have $h=0$ and obey the undivided equation $\dot r^2=c^2k^2$.

The <effective potential> is zero at $r=2\mu$, positive outside, and tends to zero as $r\to\infty$. Its <derivative> is $V'_{\rm eff}=2(3\mu-r)/r^4$. Thus
$$
\boxed{r_{\rm circ}=3\mu,\quad
V_{\rm eff}(3\mu)=\frac1{27\mu^2},\quad
b_{\rm crit}=3\sqrt3\,\mu.}
$$
The <circular null geodesic> lies at a maximum, since $V''_{\rm eff}(3\mu)=-2/(81\mu^4)<0$. It is **unstable**: a small radial displacement away from the maximum permits motion away from the orbit. For incoming <photons> from infinity in the exterior of a <black hole>, an outer <turning point> exists when $b>b_{\rm crit}$; for $b<b_{\rm crit}$ the <photon> can cross the <event horizon>. For a material central body, the <vacuum> circular orbit is relevant only if $3\mu$ lies outside its surface.

\Image[paper-1-photon-potential.png]
{title=Photon potential and horizon-penetrating light cones}

For the new coordinates, set $q=2\mu/r$, so $f=1-q$. The <geodesic Lagrangian> is $L'=[fc^2\dot t'^2-2qc\dot t'\dot r-(1+q)\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2]/2$. Cyclic-coordinate conservation and the null condition give the required analogous equations:
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
f\dot t'-\frac q c\dot r&=k,& r^2\dot\phi&=h,\\
fc^2\dot t'^2-2qc\dot t'\dot r
-(1+q)\dot r^2-r^2\dot\phi^2&=0.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Here $k$ denotes the same conserved time-translation quantity as before. The transformation is $t'=t+(2\mu/c)\log|r/(2\mu)-1|$. In particular, the displayed time coordinate belongs to <Ingoing Kerr-Schild coordinates for Schwarzschild spacetime>; the usual advanced null coordinate of <ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates> is $v=t'+r/c$. Using a <metric tensor> with a vanishing $dr^2$ coefficient while retaining the name $t'$ would therefore solve a different coordinate representation.

An elimination that stays finite at the horizon is especially simple:
$$
\begin{aligned}
c^2k^2-\dot r^2
&=(fc\dot t'-q\dot r)^2-\dot r^2\\
&=f\left[fc^2\dot t'^2-2qc\dot t'\dot r
-(1+q)\dot r^2\right]\\
&=f r^2\dot\phi^2=\frac{fh^2}{r^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The middle equality uses $q^2+f(1+q)=1$. Hence **the radial <energy> equation and $V_{\rm eff}(r)$ are unchanged**, including the horizon limit. The radial <metric tensor> block has <determinant> $-c^2$ and is regular at $r=2\mu$. Its radial null directions are $dr/(c\,dt')=-1$ and $dr/(c\,dt')=f/(1+q)$, as shown in the original schematic.

This <coordinate invariance of the Schwarzschild photon radial potential> follows because the transformation changes the time slicing but preserves the <areal radius> and the stationary <Killing vector field> $\partial_{t'}=\partial_t$. Consequently the conserved <Killing energy>, <angular momentum> and <impact parameter> are the same. <photon> capture, the circular orbit and the radial <turning points> cannot depend on coordinate labels; coordinate velocities can.
