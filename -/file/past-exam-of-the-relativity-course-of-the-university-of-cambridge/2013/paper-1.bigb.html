= Solution
{parent=a}

Use the <angular-diameter ratio under a collinear boost>, with motion along the common sightline and small angular radius $\vartheta$. For a boost toward the source, <relativistic aberration> of the incoming light gives $\tan(\vartheta'/2)=e^{-\eta}\tan(\vartheta/2)$, where $\eta$ is the relative <rapidity>. Thus the observer seeing the smaller disk has <angular diameter> reduced by $e^{-\eta}$. The ratio two implies $e^\eta=2$, so
$$
\boxed{\frac{v_{\rm rel}}c=\tanh(\ln2)=\frac{2^2-1}{2^2+1}=\frac35}.
$$
The answer is **$0.60c$**. Both observations occur at one event, so this is a <relativistic aberration> comparison rather than a change of geometrical distance during observation. Arbitrary transverse relative motion would require additional directional information; the stated motion toward the same distant target selects the collinear interpretation.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For <geodesic motion of uncharged dust>, use the pressureless stress-energy form, $T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^\mu u^\nu$ and $u_\nu u^\nu=c^2$. <stress-energy conservation> expands to
$$
0=\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}
=u^\nu\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)+\rho u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu.
$$
Contracting with $u_\nu$ kills the second term, since $u_\nu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=\tfrac12\nabla_\mu(c^2)=0$. Hence $\nabla_\mu(\rho u^\mu)=0$, and wherever $\rho>0$,
$$
\boxed{u^\mu\nabla_\mu u^\nu=0}.
$$
On a flow line $u^\mu=dx^\mu/d\tau$, so this becomes $d^2x^\nu/d\tau^2+\Gamma^\nu{}_{\alpha\beta}(dx^\alpha/d\tau)(dx^\beta/d\tau)=0$. Each <relativistic dust> element therefore follows a <timelike geodesic> with <proper time> as an <affine parameter>. <pressure> gradients or external nongravitational <forces> would add an <acceleration> term.

The <Christoffel symbols of a spatially flat FLRW metric> depend on the time-coordinate convention. Here $x^0$ is $t$, not $ct$. For an <affine parameter> $\lambda$, take $\mathcal L=\tfrac12[c^2\dot t^2-a^2(t)\delta_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j]$. Its <Euler-Lagrange equations> are
$$
\ddot t+\frac{aa'}{c^2}\delta_{ij}\dot x^i\dot x^j=0,\qquad
\ddot x^i+2\frac{a'}a\dot t\dot x^i=0.
$$
Comparison with the <geodesic equation> gives
$$
\boxed{\Gamma^0{}_{ij}=\frac{aa'}{c^2}\delta_{ij},\qquad
\Gamma^i{}_{0j}=\Gamma^i{}_{j0}=\frac{a'}a\delta^i{}_j}.
$$
All others vanish because $g_{00}$ is constant and there are no spatial <metric tensor> <derivatives>. The lower-index interchange is essential: the printed list specifies one member of each symmetric mixed pair. The factor of two in the spatial <geodesic equation> comes from those two members.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Universality of <free fall> equates the passive gravitational response to <inertial mass> up to a universal choice of units. The <Einstein equivalence principle> further states that local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling laboratory obey <special relativity> independently of the laboratory's location and <velocity>. A supported laboratory instead has <proper acceleration>, just as an accelerating laboratory in flat spacetime does.

Around an event, freely falling coordinates can be chosen so that $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\mu\nu}=0$ there. The first <metric tensor> <derivatives> vanish at that event, and nearby corrections begin at order curvature times squared separation. The <metric tensor>'s second <derivatives> retain the <Riemann tensor>. Thus **a <local inertial frame> removes <Christoffel symbols> at an event, while tidal gravity remains measurable over a finite region**. Relative motion of separated freely falling test particles detects this curvature through <geodesic deviation>. Freely falling axes carried along an observer's <world line> extend the local construction, but do not make an extended curved region globally Minkowskian.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

<accretion-disk tests of strong-field gravity> put radiating matter in a strong gravitational field at relativistic orbital speeds. <general relativity> predicts an <innermost stable circular orbit>, $r_{\rm ISCO}=6GM/c^2$ for a nonrotating <black hole>, with a spin-dependent radius in the <Kerr metric>. If the radiating inner edge tracks that orbit, its fitted radius and binding-energy release probe the geometry. For a thin disk with negligible inner-edge torque, $L\simeq\eta\dot Mc^2$; the Schwarzschild efficiency is $\eta=1-\sqrt{8/9}\simeq0.057$.

In <relativistic iron-line spectroscopy>, a fluorescent iron line supplies a rest-energy reference. The <relativistic Doppler effect> from orbital motion, <relativistic beaming>, <gravitational redshift> and ray bending produce an asymmetric broadened profile, including a red wing from the deepest emitting radii. The ASCA observation of MCG-6-30-15 showed a broad asymmetric iron feature relative to the approximately $6.4\ \mathrm{keV}$ neutral-iron <energy>, interpreted through relativistic disk-line models.<ASCA: MCG-6-30-15 by NASA HEASARC>[Mission result description: broad iron line, low-energy asymmetry and relativistic interpretation]{ref} The observed line must be separated from the illuminating continuum, <absorption> and other <reflection> features; inclination, ionization and the radial illumination law affect the inferred radius.

<thermal-continuum fitting of an accretion disk> provides an independent method for a geometrically thin, optically thick disk. Its spectrum and normalization constrain a characteristic inner area, provided <mass>, distance, inclination and atmospheric spectral hardening are controlled. A pre-2013 application selected thermal-state observations of GRS 1915+105 at relatively low disk <luminosity> to make the thin-disk approximation appropriate.<The Spin of the Near-Extreme Kerr Black Hole GRS 1915+105 by Jeffrey E. McClintock and others>[Abstract: relativistic continuum fit, thermal-state selection, luminosity cut and inner-edge assumptions]{ref}

**Consistency among inner radius, line transfer and thermal emission tests the strong-field disk picture.** A <black-hole spin> estimate obtained by assuming a <Kerr metric> is not by itself a model-independent verification of <general relativity>. Stress across the nominal inner edge, finite disk thickness, coronal geometry, calibration and competing <absorption> models must be constrained; a gravity test compares alternative geometries while allowing for those astrophysical uncertainties. An emitting material surface or boundary layer can further complicate disks around <neutron stars>.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Write $r_s=2GM/c^2$, $f=1-r_s/r$ and $r_*=r+r_s\ln|r/r_s-1|$, so $dr_*/dr=f^{-1}$. The <ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates> use $v=t+r_*/c$ to label ingoing radial light fronts. Substituting $dt=dv-dr/(cf)$ into the <Schwarzschild metric> gives
$$
\boxed{ds^2=fc^2dv^2-2c\,dv\,dr-r^2d\Omega^2}.
$$
The radial block has <determinant> $-c^2$ even at $r=r_s$; its coefficients have no horizon divergence. The apparent singularity of Schwarzschild time at the future horizon is therefore a coordinate failure. The physical <curvature singularity> at $r=0$ remains.

The <radial null trajectories in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates> have
$$
\boxed{v=\text{constant}\quad\text{(ingoing)},\qquad
\frac{dr}{dv}=\frac c2\left(1-\frac{r_s}r\right)\quad\text{(outgoing)}}.
$$
The ingoing family crosses the future horizon smoothly. The outgoing family has zero outward radial coordinate speed at the horizon and decreasing radius inside it; both future null directions then lead toward smaller radius. These advanced coordinates cover the exterior and future black-hole horizon, rather than simultaneously regularizing the past white-hole horizon. Their null time coordinate is not a clock reading of a stationary observer.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Throughout this solution $c=1$ and $a>0$.

**Null <metric tensor> and boost.** Inverting the coordinate definitions gives $t=(p+q)/2$, $x=(q-p)/2$, and hence
$$
\boxed{ds^2=dp\,dq,\qquad
(g_{AB})_{p,q}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2\\1/2&0\end{pmatrix}}.
$$
For a frame moving at speed $v$ along positive $x$, use $t'=\gamma(t-vx)$, $x'=\gamma(x-vt)$. Their difference and sum give
$$
\boxed{p'=\alpha p,\qquad q'=q/\alpha,\qquad
\alpha=\gamma(1+v)=\sqrt{\frac{1+v}{1-v}}}.
$$
The reciprocal <scale factors> preserve $dp\,dq$. They are $e^{\pm\eta_v}$ in <rapidity> notation.

**<acceleration> invariants.** Proper-time normalization gives $\dot t^2-\dot x^2=1$, so $\dot p\dot q=1$. The <four-acceleration> has squared norm $\ddot t^2-\ddot x^2=-a^2$; therefore
$$
\boxed{\dot p\dot q=1,\qquad\ddot p\ddot q=-a^2}.
$$
For future motion write $\dot p=e^{-\eta}$ and $\dot q=e^\eta$. Then the second equation is $-(\dot\eta)^2=-a^2$. <acceleration> toward positive $x$ selects $\eta=\eta_0+a\tau$.

**<hyperbolic motion from an initial event and rapidity>.** The stated initial event fixes $p(0)=-1/a$, $q(0)=1/a$, but not the initial <rapidity> $\eta_0$. Integrating with positive signed <acceleration> gives the general family
$$
p=-\frac1a+\frac{e^{-\eta_0}}a(1-e^{-a\tau}),\qquad
q=\frac1a+\frac{e^{\eta_0}}a(e^{a\tau}-1).
$$
The initial event alone does not fix the initial <velocity> or signed <acceleration>. The trajectory explicitly requested in the next paragraph selects the additional conditions $\eta_0=0$ and positive-$x$ <acceleration>. With that convention,
$$
\boxed{p(\tau)=-\frac1ae^{-a\tau},\qquad
q(\tau)=\frac1ae^{a\tau}},
$$
and consequently
$$
\boxed{t(\tau)=\frac1a\sinh a\tau,\qquad
x(\tau)=\frac1a\cosh a\tau}.
$$
With arbitrary $\eta_0$ the equivalent expressions are $t=[\sinh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\sinh\eta_0]/a$ and $x=1/a+[\cosh(\eta_0+a\tau)-\cosh\eta_0]/a$. Thus position data alone do not select the displayed special <hyperbola>. The following calculations use that intended <hyperbola>, which is also explicitly given by the paper.

**Diagram and horizon.** The selected <world line> is the right-hand branch $x^2-t^2=a^{-2}$. Its future asymptote $x=t$ is a reception horizon: rightmoving signals have constant $p=t-x$, whereas the observer has $p<0$ and approaches zero only as $\tau\to\infty$. Events with $p\geq0$ cannot send a future signal to this observer if the <acceleration> continues indefinitely. This is an observer-dependent causal restriction in flat spacetime, not a <curvature singularity>.

**<photon reception by a uniformly accelerated observer>.** A rightmoving <photon> from the stationary source has <four-momentum> $P^\mu=h\nu_0(1,1)$. Contracting with the observer's <four-velocity> gives
$$
\boxed{\nu(\tau)=\nu_0(\dot t-\dot x)=\nu_0\dot p=\nu_0e^{-a\tau}}.
$$
The same result follows by differentiating its emission-time relation. A <photon> emitted at $x=L$ and time $t_e$ has $p=t_e-L$, so intersection requires
$$
\boxed{t_e=L-\frac1ae^{-a\tau}<L}.
$$
Thus **$t_e=L$ is the supremum of receivable emission times**, reached only in the limit of infinite receiver <proper time>. A <photon> emitted exactly then lies on the horizon and is never received at finite time; later <photons> cannot catch the ship. Restoring dimensions gives the limiting emission time $L/c$ for this chosen origin and trajectory.

**<Rindler coordinates>.** Substitution of the selected trajectory into the <coordinate transformation> gives
$$
\boxed{\xi(\tau)=\tau,\qquad\zeta(\tau)=0}.
$$
Differentiating the transformation also gives $ds^2=e^{2a\zeta}(d\xi^2-d\zeta^2)$. Curves of fixed $\zeta$ are hyperbolae, while curves of fixed $\xi$ are straight rays $t/x=\tanh(a\xi)$ within the right wedge. The coordinate $\xi$ is the selected ship's proper clock; at another fixed $\zeta$, <proper time> obeys $d\tau_\zeta=e^{a\zeta}d\xi$. The spatial coordinate is logarithmic in $\rho=\sqrt{x^2-t^2}=e^{a\zeta}/a$, rather than being proper distance itself. Fixed-$\zeta$ observers have <proper acceleration> $a e^{-a\zeta}$.

**<redshift>.** An incoming radial null ray satisfies $d\chi=-c\,dt/R(t)$. Consecutive wave crests follow the same comoving path, so the endpoint changes in the fixed path <integral> obey $dt_0/R_0=dt_e/R_e$. Comoving clocks read <cosmic time>; hence their measured periods and frequencies satisfy
$$
\boxed{1+z=\frac{\lambda_0}{\lambda_e}=\frac{dt_0}{dt_e}=\frac{R_0}{R_e}}.
$$
The physical <wavelength> stretches with the <scale factor> during propagation. It is not necessary to assign a unique global inertial-frame recession <velocity> to a distant source.

**Radial coordinate and <cosmological look-back time>.** Differentiating $1+z=R_0/R(t)$ gives $dz/dt=-(1+z)H(z)$. Combining this with the null-ray <integral> yields
$$
\boxed{\chi(z)=\frac c{R_0}\int_0^z\frac{d\bar z}{H(\bar z)},\qquad
t_0-t_e=\int_0^z\frac{d\bar z}{(1+\bar z)H(\bar z)}}.
$$
The extra factor $1+z$ in the second denominator records the conversion from scale-factor change to elapsed <cosmic time>.

**<proper-motion distance>.** A small transverse displacement at emission has proper length $d\ell=R_eS(\chi)d\theta$. Define its source-frame transverse speed as $v=d\ell/dt_e$; <cosmological time dilation> makes $dt_0=(1+z)dt_e$. Therefore
$$
\dot\theta_0=\frac{v}{(1+z)R_eS(\chi)},\qquad
\boxed{d_M=\frac v{\dot\theta_0}=R_0S(\chi(z))}.
$$
Equivalently, $d_M=(1+z)d_A$, where the <angular diameter distance> is $d_A=R_eS(\chi)$. The observer-time versus source-time distinction is essential. This calculation uses the stated transverse speed convention and the homogeneous background, without additional peculiar radial <relativistic Doppler effect> or <gravitational lensing>.

**<redshift shell volume at the present epoch>.** The induced spatial <metric tensor> on the present slice gives $dV_0=R_0^3S^2(\chi)d\chi\,d\Omega$. Since $d\chi/dz=c/(R_0H)$,
$$
\boxed{dV_0=\frac{cR_0^2S^2(\chi(z))}{H(z)}\,dz\,d\Omega
=\frac{cd_M^2(z)}{H(z)}\,dz\,d\Omega}.
$$
This is the volume at the present epoch of the comoving shell intersected by the past <light cone>. Its proper volume at emission is smaller by $(1+z)^3$; a conserved population's proper <number density> is larger by the same factor, preserving its count.

**<Einstein-de Sitter cumulative number counts>.** In flat geometry $S(\chi)=\chi$. Inserting the stated <Hubble parameter> into the radial <integral> gives
$$
d_M(z)=\frac{2c}{H_0}\left[1-(1+z)^{-1/2}\right].
$$
The constant present-volume <number density> makes $dN=n_0dV_0$. With no members of the population before its formation epoch,
$$
\boxed{N=\frac{4c^3n_0}{H_0^3}\int_0^{z_f}
\frac{[1-(1+z)^{-1/2}]^2}{(1+z)^{3/2}}\,dz}.
$$
As an independent check, substitution $u=(1+z)^{-1/2}$ evaluates this to
$$
\boxed{N=\frac{8c^3n_0}{3H_0^3}\left[1-(1+z_f)^{-1/2}\right]^3
=\frac{n_0d_M^3(z_f)}3}.
$$
This is exactly the Euclidean <comoving volume> per unit <solid angle> in the flat model. It assumes a complete count with the specified fixed population; <luminosity> selection, mergers or subsequent number-density evolution would require additional factors.
