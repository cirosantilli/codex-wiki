= Solution
{parent=a}

With $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$, <four-momentum conservation> gives total <energy> $E=(\gamma m_1+m_2)c^2$ and <momentum> $\mathbf p=\gamma m_1\mathbf v$. The compound system therefore has
$$
\boxed{M=\sqrt{m_1^2+m_2^2+2\gamma m_1m_2},\qquad
\mathbf V=\frac{\gamma m_1}{\gamma m_1+m_2}\mathbf v}.
$$
Here $M^2c^4=E^2-c^2p^2$. The excess over $m_1+m_2$ is retained <internal energy>, assuming <absorption> without escaping <radiation>.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

On a coordinate patch with $f\ne0$, $g_{xx}=1$, $g_{yy}=f^2$ and the only nonzero <metric tensor> <derivative> is $\partial_xg_{yy}=2ff'$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\Gamma^x{}_{yy}=-ff',\qquad
\Gamma^y{}_{xy}=\Gamma^y{}_{yx}=\frac{f'}f}.
$$
All remaining <Christoffel symbols> vanish. In the supplied <Riemann tensor> convention,
$$
R^x{}_{yxy}=\partial_x(-ff')-\Gamma^y{}_{yx}\Gamma^x{}_{yy}
=-(f'^2+ff'')+f'^2,
\qquad
\boxed{R^x{}_{yxy}=-ff''}.
$$
For example, $f=x$ is flat away from the polar-coordinate origin, while $f=\sin x$ gives positive <Gaussian curvature> $-f''/f=1$.

Use the largest Newtonian tidal-tensor <eigenvalue>, the radial <gravity> gradient $2GM/r^3$. Imposing the supplied gradient threshold gives
$$
\boxed{r_{\min}\simeq\left(\frac{2GM}{100\ \mathrm{s^{-2}}}\right)^{1/3}
=1.59\times10^6\ \mathrm m}.
$$
This is **about $1590\ \mathrm{km}$ from the centre**, or $1580\ \mathrm{km}$ above the stated surface. The tangential <eigenvalues> have half the radial magnitude. Since $GM/(r_{\min}c^2)\simeq1.4\times10^{-3}$, the Newtonian tidal estimate is adequate; this calculation uses the given threshold without inferring any additional material-response model.

= Solution
{parent=a}

<inertial mass> sets the response to <force>, $\mathbf F=m_i\mathbf a$. Passive <gravitational mass> sets the response to a given <gravitational potential>, $\mathbf F_g=-m_g\nabla\Phi$; active <gravitational mass> describes a body's gravitational sourcing. Universality of the passive-to-inertial mass ratio makes freely falling trajectories independent of composition. <torsion balance> comparisons and differential free-fall experiments test this <weak equivalence principle> by searching for composition-dependent acceleration, with environmental <forces> and instrumental asymmetries controlled.

The <Einstein equivalence principle> further says that local nongravitational experiments in a freely falling laboratory obey <special relativity>, independent of the laboratory's location and <velocity>. A local inertial coordinate system can remove <Christoffel symbols> at one event, but cannot remove a nonzero <Riemann tensor> throughout a finite region. Relative acceleration of separated freely falling particles reveals tidal <gravity>. Thus a uniformly accelerating laboratory in flat <spacetime> can mimic a local gravitational field, while extended tidal measurements distinguish genuine curvature. Equality of the measured mass responses does not make every gravitational field globally equivalent to acceleration.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Use coordinates $x^\mu=(ct,\mathbf x)$, signature $(+---)$, and the convention $F^{0i}=-E_i/c$, $F^{ij}=-\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$. The antisymmetric <electromagnetic field tensor> combines six field components:
$$
F^{\mu\nu}=
\begin{pmatrix}
0&-E_x/c&-E_y/c&-E_z/c\\
E_x/c&0&-B_z&B_y\\
E_y/c&B_z&0&-B_x\\
E_z/c&-B_y&B_x&0
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
It transforms as $F'^{\mu\nu}=\Lambda^\mu{}_\alpha\Lambda^\nu{}_\beta F^{\alpha\beta}$. Lowering indices changes the time–space signs: $F_{0i}=E_i/c$, while $F_{ij}=F^{ij}$. The <covariant tensor> instead uses the inverse coordinate Jacobian on each lower index.

For a frame moving with <velocity> $\mathbf v$ relative to the original one,
$$
\boxed{\mathbf E'_\parallel=\mathbf E_\parallel,\quad
\mathbf E'_\perp=\gamma(\mathbf E+\mathbf v\times\mathbf B)_\perp},
$$
$$
\boxed{\mathbf B'_\parallel=\mathbf B_\parallel,\quad
\mathbf B'_\perp=\gamma\left(\mathbf B-\frac{\mathbf v\times\mathbf E}{c^2}\right)_\perp}.
$$
Electric and <magnetic fields> therefore mix under a boost; they are frame-dependent parts of one tensor. For example, a purely transverse <electric field> acquires a <magnetic field> in a boosted frame. The <electromagnetic field invariants> $B^2-E^2/c^2$ and $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$ restrict which fields can be eliminated by a frame change. The covariant <force> law $dp^\mu/d\tau=qF^\mu{}_\nu U^\nu$ guarantees consistent particle dynamics in all <inertial frames>.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

The strongest pre-2014 evidence combines large gravitating mass with extreme compactness. In an <X-ray binary>, companion-star radial velocities determine a <binary mass function>; <orbital inclination>, donor properties and distance complete the mass inference. An unseen component above conventional neutron-star <masses> is a black-hole candidate. For example, a 2011 Cygnus X-1 analysis combined optical photometry, spectroscopy and distance information to infer a compact-object mass about $14.8M_\odot$, with model and observational uncertainties.<The Mass of the Black Hole in Cygnus X-1 by Jerome A. Orosz and others>[Abstract and dynamical-model analysis]{ref}

Near <Sagittarius A*>, infrared stellar <astrometry> and radial velocities trace nearly <Keplerian orbits> around a dark compact mass. The concentration of several million solar <masses> in a small volume strongly challenges an extended cluster of ordinary remnants; source confusion, reference-frame errors, distance and possible extended mass must be fitted.<Measuring Distance and Properties of the Milky Way's Central Supermassive Black Hole with Stellar Orbits by Andrea M. Ghez and others>[Abstract and orbital-fit/systematic-error discussion]{ref} Gas and <maser> dynamics provide related compact-mass tests in other nuclei.

<accretion> explains luminous X-rays, rapid variability and broad relativistic disk features. These constrain size and the strong-field environment, but emission alone is not unique to an <event horizon>. Lack of surface-like signatures is supporting, model-dependent evidence. **Mass, compactness and consistent <accretion> phenomenology together support the black-hole interpretation**; large-radius mass measurements alone do not directly demonstrate a horizon.

= Solution

**Boost and <velocity> transformation.** For the moving frame,
$$
t'=\gamma_v\left(t-\frac{vx}{c^2}\right),\quad
x'=\gamma_v(x-vt),\quad y'=y,\quad z'=z.
$$
The inverse has $v\mapsto-v$. Taking differentials of that inverse and dividing by $dt$ gives
$$
\boxed{u_x=\frac{u_x'+v}{1+vu_x'/c^2},\quad
u_y=\frac{u_y'}{\gamma_v(1+vu_x'/c^2)},\quad
u_z=\frac{u_z'}{\gamma_v(1+vu_x'/c^2)}}.
$$

**Constant <proper acceleration>.** For collinear motion, the instantaneous rest-frame acceleration is $a=\gamma^3dv/dt$. Since $dt/d\tau=\gamma$,
$$
\boxed{\frac{dv}{d\tau}=a\gamma^{-2}
=a\left(1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}\right)}.
$$
Write $v=c\tanh\eta$; then $d\eta/d\tau=a/c$. Starting from rest gives $\eta=a\tau/c$, $dt/d\tau=\cosh\eta$ and $dx/d\tau=c\sinh\eta$. <integration> with the stated initial origin yields
$$
\boxed{v=c\tanh(a\tau/c),\quad
t=\frac ca\sinh(a\tau/c),\quad
x=\frac{c^2}a[\cosh(a\tau/c)-1]}.
$$

**<photon rocket> mass ratio.** In the instantaneous <rest frame>, ejecting backward <photon> <energy> $dE_\gamma$ gives, to first order, $c^2dM=-dE_\gamma$ and $Mc\,d\eta=dE_\gamma/c$. Hence $dM/M=-d\eta$, and
$$
\boxed{\frac M{M_i}=e^{-\eta}
=\sqrt{\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}}}.
$$
Here $M$ includes all stored fuel and <energy>. This assumes complete conversion into a perfectly collimated <photon> exhaust without other losses.

**<photon-rocket fuel for acceleration and braking>.** With effectively instantaneous changes of speed, the cruise <proper time> is $D/(\gamma v)=10\ \mathrm{yr}$, giving
$$
\gamma\beta=10,\qquad
\gamma=\sqrt{101},\quad\beta=\frac{10}{\sqrt{101}},\quad
\eta=\operatorname{arsinh}10=2.99822.
$$
Braking to rest costs another factor $e^{-\eta}$, so $M_f/M_i=e^{-2\eta}=(\sqrt{101}-10)^2=0.00248758$. If equal rest <masses> of matter and <antimatter> annihilate, the consumed <antimatter> is half the total consumed fuel:
$$
\boxed{\frac{M_{\bar m}}{M_i}=\frac12(1-e^{-2\eta})=0.498756
\quad\text{(about $49.88\%$)}}.
$$
This is the fraction of initial total mass, not the <antimatter> fraction of the consumed matter–<antimatter> fuel, which is one half.

**<finite-acceleration correction to a relativistic cruise>.** Each constant-proper-acceleration leg has proper duration $\tau_a=c\eta/a$ and Earth-frame length $x_a=(c^2/a)(\gamma-1)$. Thus
$$
\tau_{\rm new}=2\frac{c\eta}{a}+
\frac{D-2x_a}{\gamma v}.
$$
Subtracting the original $D/(\gamma v)$ gives
$$
\boxed{\Delta\tau=\frac{2c}{a}
\left[\eta-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma\beta}\right]
\simeq3.98\ \mathrm{yr}}.
$$
For $a=10\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, each accelerating leg takes about $2.85$ proper years and covers $8.60$ light years in the Earth frame, leaving positive cruise distance. The new total is about **13.98 proper years**. Adding $2\tau_a$ to ten years would overcount the cruise segment replaced by acceleration and braking.

= Solution

**<geodesic> integrals and affine normalization.** Put $F(r)=1-2\mu/r$. Choose the <affine parameter> to be <proper time>, so $g_{ab}\dot x^a\dot x^b=c^2$. Cyclic $t$ and $\phi$ in the <geodesic Lagrangian> give $F\dot t=k$ and $r^2\dot\phi=h$. Substituting into the <four-velocity> normalization gives
$$
c^2=\frac{c^2k^2}{F}-\frac{\dot r^2}{F}-\frac{h^2}{r^2},
$$
hence
$$
\boxed{\dot r^2-\frac{2\mu h^2}{r^3}
+\frac{h^2}{r^2}-\frac{2\mu c^2}{r}
=(k^2-1)c^2}.
$$
An arbitrary constant rescaling of <affine parameter> changes its normalization; the displayed unit constant assumes the proper-time choice.

**<circular orbit>.** Define $V(r)=h^2/r^2-2\mu h^2/r^3-2\mu c^2/r$. The radial <geodesic equation> is $\ddot r=-V'/2$. A circular <geodesic> therefore needs $V'(R)=0$, not merely $\dot r=0$ at an instant. Solving and substituting into the radial first integral gives
$$
\boxed{h^2=\frac{\mu c^2R^2}{R-3\mu},\qquad
k^2=\frac{(R-2\mu)^2}{R(R-3\mu)}}.
$$
Existence requires $R>3\mu$. <circular orbits> below $6\mu$ are radially unstable, although they still define the instantaneous comparison here.

**<orthogonal local velocities in Schwarzschild spacetime>.** Use Alice's static <orthonormal frame> at the meeting event. Her proper clock is $d\tau_A=\sqrt F\,dt$ and tangential proper distance is $R\,d\phi$. Bob's local tangential speed is
$$
v_{\rm AB}=\frac{R\,d\phi}{\sqrt F\,dt}
=\frac{h\sqrt F}{Rk},
\qquad
\boxed{v_{\rm AB}=c\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{R-2\mu}}}.
$$
Carol has $h_C=0$ and $k_C=1$, because she starts from rest at infinity. The radial integral gives $\dot r_C=-c\sqrt{2\mu/R}$. Her static-frame radial speed is $(dr/\sqrt F)/(\sqrt F\,dt)=dr/(Fdt)$, and $F\dot t_C=1$, so
$$
\boxed{v_{\rm AC}=c\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{R}}}.
$$
This is inward radial motion, perpendicular to Bob's tangential motion in Alice's frame.

Their <Lorentz factors> relative to Alice are $\gamma_{\rm AB}=\sqrt{(R-2\mu)/(R-3\mu)}$ and $\gamma_{\rm AC}=\sqrt{R/(R-2\mu)}$. The <four-velocity> contraction therefore gives
$$
\gamma_{\rm BC}=\gamma_{\rm AB}\gamma_{\rm AC}
=\sqrt{\frac{R}{R-3\mu}},\qquad
\boxed{v_{\rm BC}=c\sqrt{\frac{3\mu}{R}}}.
$$

**Special-relativistic consistency.** Let Bob's tangential <velocity> in Alice's local frame be $u$ and Carol's inward radial speed be $w$. A boost to Bob's frame gives Carol components $v'_{\rm tang}=-u$ and $v'_{\rm rad}=-w/\gamma_u$. Consequently
$$
\frac{v_{\rm BC}^2}{c^2}
=\frac{u^2}{c^2}+\frac{w^2}{c^2}
-\frac{u^2w^2}{c^4}
=\frac{\mu}{R-2\mu}+\frac{2\mu}{R}
-\frac{2\mu^2}{R(R-2\mu)}
=\frac{3\mu}{R}.
$$
Thus the three measured relative speeds agree with local Lorentz <velocity> addition. Ordinary Euclidean subtraction of the two <velocity> vectors would omit the transverse-velocity transformation.
