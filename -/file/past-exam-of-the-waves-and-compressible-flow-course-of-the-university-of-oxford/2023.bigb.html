The large-time propagation region in 2(c) is an asymptotic concentration region, not a claim that all disturbance outside it vanishes.

= Solution
{parent=i}

The <velocity potential> gives horizontal and vertical <velocity> components $(\phi_x,\phi_z)$. The <kinematic boundary condition> at each fixed impermeable wall is zero normal fluid velocity, hence
$$
\boxed{\phi_x=0\text{ at }x=0,L,\qquad\phi_z=0\text{ at }z=-h}.
$$
Let $\eta(x,t)$ be the <surface elevation>. To first order in its <amplitude>, the <kinematic boundary condition for a free-surface graph> gives $\eta_t=\phi_z$ at $z=0$. The <dynamic boundary condition for an inviscid interface> fixes the surface <pressure> to atmospheric pressure. The unsteady <Bernoulli equation>, after choosing the time-dependent gauge of $\phi$, then gives $\phi_t+g\eta=0$ at $z=0$: the quadratic velocity term is second order in <amplitude>. Differentiate this last equation in time and eliminate $\eta_t$ to obtain the <linearized free-surface boundary conditions> in combined form,
$$
\boxed{\phi_{tt}+g\phi_z=0\qquad(z=0)}.
$$

= Solution

Apply <separation of variables> to <Laplace's equation>. The side-wall <Neumann boundary conditions> give horizontal factors $\cos(k_nx)$ with $k_n=n\pi/L$. The bottom condition fixes the vertical factor to $\cosh(k_n(z+h))$. Substitution into the surface condition in <1/a/i> gives the <normal modes of surface gravity waves in a rectangular tank>:
$$
\boxed{\phi_n=\cos(k_nx)\cosh(k_n(z+h))\,[A_n\cos(\omega_nt)+B_n\sin(\omega_nt)],\qquad\omega_n^2=gk_n\tanh(k_nh),\quad n\geq1}.
$$
The corresponding <surface elevation> follows from $\eta_n=-\partial_t\phi_n(x,0,t)/g$. The spatially uniform $n=0$ mode has zero frequency; its constant elevation changes total fluid volume, so it is absent when that volume is fixed. An additive constant in $\phi$ changes no fluid <velocity>.

The wall position is $x_w(t)=\operatorname{Re}(ce^{-i\omega t})$, so its <velocity> is $\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega ce^{-i\omega t})$. The <kinematic boundary condition> equates the normal fluid <velocity> to the normal wall <velocity>. Linearizing the evaluation point to the mean wall position gives
$$
\boxed{\phi_x(0,z,t)=\operatorname{Re}(-i\omega ce^{-i\omega t}),\qquad f_x(0,z)=-i\omega c}.
$$
The other wall and bottom retain $f_x(L,z)=0$ and $f_z(x,-h)=0$, while the free surface gives $f_z(x,0)=(\omega^2/g)f(x,0)$.

{parent=b}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Put $\lambda=\omega^2/g$ and
$$
Q=\frac{i\omega c}{2L}\big[(z+h)^2-(L-x)^2\big],\qquad G=f+Q.
$$
This polynomial is a <harmonic function>: $Q_{xx}+Q_{zz}=0$. Also $Q_x(0,z)=i\omega c$, $Q_x(L,z)=0$ and $Q_z(x,-h)=0$. Thus $G$ satisfies <Laplace's equation> with homogeneous <Neumann boundary conditions> on both side walls and the bottom. Its <Fourier cosine series> consequently has the form
$$
\boxed{G(x,z)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\cos(k_nx)\cosh(k_n(z+h)),\qquad k_n=\frac{n\pi}{L}}.
$$
The surface condition becomes $G_z-\lambda G=H(x)$, where
$$
H(x)=\frac{i\omega c}{L}\left[h-\frac\lambda2\big(h^2-(L-x)^2\big)\right].
$$
For a nonzero forcing frequency away from the natural frequencies, the coefficients of the <oscillating-wall forcing of a rectangular tank> are
$$
H_n=\frac2L\int_0^L H(x)\cos(k_nx)\,dx,\qquad a_0=-\frac{H_0}{\lambda},\qquad a_n=\frac{H_n}{k_n\sinh(k_nh)-\lambda\cosh(k_nh)}\quad(n\geq1).
$$
Here $k_0=0$ in the definition of $H_0$. In fact $H_n=2i\omega c\lambda/(Lk_n^2)$ for $n\geq1$, since $\int_0^L(L-x)^2\cos(k_nx)dx=2L/k_n^2$. The zero-mode coefficient ensures that the mean <surface elevation> is $hc/L$ in complex amplitude, exactly the first-order <volume conservation> adjustment to the moving wall.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iii}

For the $n$th <normal mode>, the denominator just obtained is
$$
k_n\sinh(k_nh)-\frac{\omega^2}{g}\cosh(k_nh)=\frac{\cosh(k_nh)}g(\omega_n^2-\omega^2).
$$
Its forcing coefficient is nonzero for $c\ne0$ and $\omega>0$. Therefore the periodic response grows like $(\omega_n^2-\omega^2)^{-1}$ as $\omega$ approaches $\omega_n$: this is <resonance>. At exact resonance there is no bounded periodic particular solution in the linear inviscid model; the resonant amplitude instead contains a secular term proportional to $t\sin(\omega_nt)$ or $t\cos(\omega_nt)$. Physical damping and nonlinear effects eventually matter. Away from resonance, undamped free oscillations can persist alongside the periodic particular solution.

Write the density and pressure as $\rho_0(z)+\rho'(x,z,t)$ and $p_0(z)+p'(x,z,t)$, respectively. The background satisfies <hydrostatic balance>, $p_0'=-g\rho_0$, and $\rho_0'/\rho_0=-\alpha$. Linearizing the <Euler equations for an inviscid fluid>, <incompressibility> and advection of density gives
$$
u_x+w_z=0,\qquad \rho_0u_t=-p'_x,\qquad \rho_0w_t=-p'_z-g\rho',\qquad \rho'_t=\alpha\rho_0w.
$$
Differentiate the horizontal equation in $x$ and use <incompressibility> to obtain $p'_{xx}=\rho_0w_{zt}$. Differentiate the vertical equation in time and twice in $x$:
$$
\rho_0w_{ttxx}=-\partial_z(\rho_0w_{ztt})-\alpha g\rho_0w_{xx}.
$$
Dividing by $\rho_0>0$ gives the equation for <internal waves in an exponentially stratified incompressible fluid>,
$$
\boxed{\partial_t^2(\nabla^2w-\alpha w_z)+\alpha g\,w_{xx}=0}.
$$
The first-derivative term retains the variation of inertial density with height; no <Boussinesq approximation> has been made.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

For $w=e^{\alpha z/2}W$, direct <differentiation> gives
$$
w_{zz}-\alpha w_z=e^{\alpha z/2}\left(W_{zz}-\frac{\alpha^2}{4}W\right),\qquad w_{xx}=e^{\alpha z/2}W_{xx}.
$$
Thus the transformed <partial differential equation> and its impermeable-wall <boundary conditions> are
$$
\boxed{\partial_t^2\left(\nabla^2W-\frac{\alpha^2}{4}W\right)+\alpha g W_{xx}=0,\qquad W(x,0,t)=W(x,h,t)=0}.
$$
The positive exponential factor does not change where the vertical <velocity> vanishes.

Substitute $W=f(z)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$ into the transformed equation. It yields
$$
f''+\left(\frac{\alpha g k^2}{\omega^2}-k^2-\frac{\alpha^2}{4}\right)f=0,\qquad f(0)=f(h)=0.
$$
The nonzero <eigenfunctions> of this <Dirichlet boundary value problem> are $f=A\sin(n\pi z/h)$, $n\geq1$. Hence, with $N=\sqrt{\alpha g}$ and $b_n^2=(n\pi/h)^2+\alpha^2/4$,
$$
\boxed{\omega^2=\frac{N^2k^2}{k^2+b_n^2}=\frac{4\alpha gh^2k^2}{4n^2\pi^2+h^2(\alpha^2+4k^2)}}.
$$
The physical vertical <velocity> includes the factor $e^{\alpha z/2}\sin(n\pi z/h)$. The zero horizontal wavenumber is the zero-frequency limiting case, not a propagating oscillation.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Choose the rightgoing signed <dispersion relation> $\omega(k)=Nk/\sqrt{k^2+b_n^2}$. The horizontal <phase velocity> and <group velocity> are
$$
\boxed{c_p=\frac\omega k=\frac{N}{\sqrt{k^2+b_n^2}},\qquad c_g=\frac{d\omega}{dk}=\frac{Nb_n^2}{(k^2+b_n^2)^{3/2}}}.
$$
The leftgoing branch has their negatives. For a convention using positive frequency at both signs of $k$, multiply each displayed speed by $\operatorname{sgn}k$. For every nonzero $k$, $0<c_g<c_p$ on the rightgoing branch; the <horizontal group-speed bound for a confined internal wave> is $c_g\leq N/b_n$, approached as $k\to0$.

Use the <Fourier transform> convention $\widehat W(k,z,t)=\int_{\mathbb R}W(x,z,t)e^{-ikx}dx$, with inverse factor $1/(2\pi)$. The initial profile occupies only the vertical mode $\sin(\pi z/h)$. Its transform is
$$
\widehat W(k,z,0)=\pi hC e^{-\epsilon h|k|}\sin(\pi z/h),\qquad \widehat W_t(k,z,0)=0.
$$
The transformed evolution is a <harmonic oscillator> for each $k$, with
$$
\omega(k)=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha g}\,k}{\sqrt{k^2+\pi^2/h^2+\alpha^2/4}}.
$$
Therefore inversion gives
$$
\boxed{W(x,z,t)=\frac{hC}{2}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi z}{h}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-\epsilon h|k|}\cos(\omega(k)t)e^{ikx}\,dk}.
$$
Using the nonnegative even frequency instead gives the same cosine. At $t=0$, $\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-a|k|}e^{ikx}dk=2a/(a^2+x^2)$ with $a=\epsilon h$, recovering the specified initial profile and its zero time derivative.

= Solution

Write the cosine in <2/c/i> as two complex exponentials. On a ray $x=vt$, the oscillatory phases are $kv\pm\omega(k)$. The <stationary phase method> requires $v=\mp\omega'(k)$. By <2/b/ii>, the largest absolute horizontal <group velocity> for the occupied $n=1$ mode is
$$
\boxed{\frac{|x|}{t}\leq\frac{N}{b_1}=\frac{2h\sqrt{\alpha g}}{\sqrt{4\pi^2+\alpha^2h^2}}}.
$$
Inside this region there are stationary wavenumbers supporting appreciable <wave packet> contributions. Outside it no stationary wavenumber exists; oscillatory cancellation suppresses the large-time response. This explains "mainly confined": the initial Lorentzian profile already has nonzero algebraic tails, so the displayed region is not the exact support of the solution. Near its boundary the stationary points meet $k=0$ and the usual nondegenerate stationary-phase formula needs a transition approximation.

Take a unit-width <control volume> spanning the stationary <hydraulic jump>. <Mass conservation> makes the discharge $q=hu$ constant. The horizontal <momentum flux> is $\rho h u^2$, and the integrated <hydrostatic pressure> force on a vertical cross-section is $\int_0^h\rho g(h-z)dz=\rho gh^2/2$. <Momentum conservation> therefore gives
$$
\boxed{[hu]_-^+=[hu^2+gh^2/2]_-^+=0}.
$$
These are the <Rankine-Hugoniot conditions> for the <shallow water equations> in the stationary <shock frame>. With depths $h_1\ne h_2$ on the two sides, eliminating the <velocity> gives
$$
q^2=\frac g2h_1h_2(h_1+h_2).
$$

{parent=a}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Orient the flow so $q>0$, and let subscripts 1 and 2 denote upstream and downstream. The mechanical <energy flux> is $\rho q B$, where $B=u^2/2+gh$. Inserting the relation for $q^2$ in <3/a/i> gives the <hydraulic jump head loss>,
$$
B_1-B_2=\frac{q^2}{2}\left(\frac1{h_1^2}-\frac1{h_2^2}\right)+g(h_1-h_2)=\frac{g(h_2-h_1)^3}{4h_1h_2}.
$$
An admissible jump dissipates mechanical <energy>, so $\rho q(B_1-B_2)>0$. The denominator is positive, hence
$$
\boxed{h_2>h_1}.
$$
The zero depth-change case is continuous flow. A depth-decreasing discontinuity would require an energy input at the jump; it is not the dissipative <hydraulic jump> selected by this model.

{parent=a}

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Put $R=h_2/h_1>1$. The squared <Froude numbers> follow directly from the discharge relation:
$$
\boxed{\mathrm{Fr}_1^2=\frac{u_1^2}{gh_1}=\frac{R(1+R)}2>1,\qquad \mathrm{Fr}_2^2=\frac{u_2^2}{gh_2}=\frac{1+R}{2R^2}<1}.
$$
Thus the jump connects <supercritical flow> to <subcritical flow>. Long <surface gravity waves> have speed $\sqrt{gh}$ relative to the fluid, so the <characteristic speeds> in the stationary frame are $u\pm\sqrt{gh}$. Upstream both point with the flow; downstream one points against it. The jump changes whether long-wave information can propagate upstream through the local flow.

Let $H$ be the depth behind the bore. The wall's <kinematic boundary condition> makes the <velocity> there $U$; ahead of the bore the depth and <velocity> are $h_0$ and zero. Across a rightward <hydraulic bore> moving at $V$, the <Rankine-Hugoniot conditions> are
$$
V(H-h_0)=HU,\qquad VHU=HU^2+\frac g2(H^2-h_0^2).
$$
The first gives $R=H/h_0=V/(V-U)=v/(v-1)>1$. Substituting into the second gives $F^2v=(R^2-1)/2$, where $v=V/U$ and $F=U/\sqrt{gh_0}$. Hence the <piston-driven shallow-water bore> obeys
$$
\boxed{F^2v^2=\frac{v(2v-1)}{2(v-1)^2}=1+\frac{3}{2(v-1)}+\frac1{2(v-1)^2},\qquad v>1}.
$$
The positive depth increase is consistent with the energy-loss condition in <3/a/ii>, now viewed in the bore's own <shock frame>.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

For fixed $F>0$, the left side $L(v)=F^2v^2$ is a <strictly increasing function> on $v>1$, with limit $F^2$ at $1$ and unbounded growth. The right side $R(v)=1+3/[2(v-1)]+1/[2(v-1)^2]$ is a <strictly decreasing function>, since
$$
R'(v)=-\frac{3}{2(v-1)^2}-\frac1{(v-1)^3}<0.
$$
It tends to infinity as $v\downarrow1$ and to one as $v\to\infty$. Their <continuity> and the <intermediate value theorem> give a crossing, and strict opposite <monotonicity> makes it unique. **There is exactly one admissible bore speed $V>U$ for every $F>0$.**
