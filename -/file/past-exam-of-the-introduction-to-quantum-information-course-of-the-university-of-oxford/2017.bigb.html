= a

= Solution
{parent=a}
{c}

The <Pauli matrices> are <Hermitian matrices> with zero <trace>, so a real <Bloch vector> makes $\varrho$ <Hermitian> and gives $\operatorname{tr}\varrho=1$. Their <Pauli multiplication identity> implies $(\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)^2=|\mathbf s|^2I$. Consequently the two <eigenvalues> of $\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma$ are $\pm|\mathbf s|$, including the zero-vector case, and
$$
\boxed{\lambda_\pm(\varrho)=\frac{1\pm|\mathbf s|}{2}.}
$$
A <density matrix> must also be <positive semidefinite>. The displayed <eigenvalues> are nonnegative exactly when $\boxed{|\mathbf s|\leq1}$. Thus real coordinates alone are insufficient: the allowed vectors fill the <Bloch ball>. Its boundary consists of <pure states>, with <eigenvalues> one and zero; its center is the <maximally mixed state> $I/2$.

= b
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

For any column vector $v$ and <positive semidefinite> input $\varrho$, the <Kraus representation> gives
$$
v^\dagger\mathcal E(\varrho)v=\sum_k(A_k^\dagger v)^\dagger\varrho(A_k^\dagger v)\geq0.
$$
Thus the output is <positive semidefinite>. The <cyclic property of the trace> and completeness of the <Kraus operators> give
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{tr}\mathcal E(\varrho)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(\varrho\sum_kA_k^\dagger A_k\right)=\operatorname{tr}\varrho.}
$$
More strongly, tensoring with an arbitrary <ancilla> replaces $A_k$ by $A_k\otimes I$, so the same argument proves <complete positivity> rather than only positivity on single-system <density matrices>.

If $\mathcal F$ has <Kraus operators> $B_j$, the <composition of quantum channels> has
$$
(\mathcal F\circ\mathcal E)(\varrho)=\sum_{j,k}B_jA_k\varrho A_k^\dagger B_j^\dagger,
\qquad
\sum_{j,k}(B_jA_k)^\dagger B_jA_k
=\sum_kA_k^\dagger\!\left(\sum_jB_j^\dagger B_j\right)A_k=I.
$$
This is again a trace-preserving <Kraus representation>. The composition of <completely positive maps> is <completely positive> even without the trace-preserving assumption; in the general case, the product <Kraus operators> still establish <complete positivity>.

= c
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

Conjugation by one <Pauli matrix> fixes that same matrix and reverses the other two. For example, $X X X=X$, $Y X Y=-X$, and $Z X Z=-X$. Applying this to each component of the <Bloch vector> gives
$$
\sum_{j=x,y,z}\sigma_j\varrho\sigma_j
=\frac12(3I-\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma).
$$
Hence the <quantum depolarizing channel> is
$$
\mathcal E_p(\varrho)
=\frac12\left[I+\left(1-\frac{4p}{3}\right)\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\right],
\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf s'=\frac{3-4p}{3}\mathbf s.}
$$
The <Kraus operators> $\sqrt{1-p}\,I$ and $\sqrt{p/3}\,\sigma_j$ establish <complete positivity> for $0\leq p\leq1$. At $p=3/4$ every state becomes <maximally mixed>. For $p>3/4$ the factor is negative: it reverses the vector as well as reducing its length. Its most negative value is $-1/3$, consistent with the complete-positivity range of the <isotropic qubit map>.

= d
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}
{c}

The <universal NOT gate> is the <isotropic qubit map> with factor $-1$. It sends
$$
\varrho=\frac12(I+\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)
\quad\longmapsto\quad
\mathcal N(\varrho)=\frac12(I-\mathbf s\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)=I-\varrho.
$$
For $|\mathbf s|\leq1$, the output has the same two nonnegative <eigenvalues> as the input, exchanged, and still has <trace> one. Thus **it maps every single-qubit density matrix to a density matrix**. Its linear extension to all operators is $\mathcal N(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A)I-A$, not $I-A$ for arbitrary unnormalized $A$. Positivity on one <qubit> does not establish <complete positivity> when an <ancilla> is present.

= e
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=e}
{c}

The input is the <Bell state> $\rho=|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|$, where $|\Phi^+\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Acting on the first <qubit> reverses the signs of all three terms containing its <Pauli matrices>, so in <computational basis> order $00,01,10,11$ the output is
$$
\boxed{(\mathcal N\otimes\operatorname{id})(\rho)
=\frac14(I\otimes I-X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y-Z\otimes Z)
=\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&-1/2\\
0&1/2&0&0\\
0&0&1/2&0\\
-1/2&0&0&0
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Its <eigenvalues> are $\boxed{-1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2}$: in particular, its <expectation value> in $|\Phi^+\rangle$ is $-1/2$. It therefore fails to be <positive semidefinite>, although its <trace> remains one. This single <entangled state> proves that the <universal NOT gate> is **positive but not completely positive**, so it cannot be a deterministic physical <quantum channel> on an arbitrary input.
