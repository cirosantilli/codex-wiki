= Solution
{parent=i}

Assume $Q\ge2$ and $L\ge2$; the trivial exceptions are described below. In this <periodic Potts chain>, every satisfied bond contributes $-J$ to the <energy>, and no bond can contribute less. All $L$ bonds can be satisfied simultaneously by choosing the same state at every site. Conversely, satisfying every bond forces this uniform configuration. Therefore the <ground state> <energy> and <degeneracy factor> are
$$
\boxed{E_0=-JL,\qquad d_0=Q.}
$$
The $Q$ choices are distinct configurations on the labelled lattice: a global relabelling of the states is a <symmetry>, not an identification of microstates.

{parent=a}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Let $w$ count the unequal neighbouring pairs, or <domain walls>. The <energy> is $E=-JL+Jw$. A single <domain wall> is impossible with <periodic boundary conditions>: following all the other, equal-state bonds around the ring would force its endpoints to be equal too. Two <domain walls> are possible, splitting the ring into two nonempty uniform arcs carrying different states. Thus the <first excited state> has
$$
\boxed{E_1=-JL+2J,\qquad E_1-E_0=2J.}
$$
Choose the two wall bonds in $\binom L2$ ways. For that choice, identify one of the resulting arcs by its smallest site label. Its state has $Q$ choices and the other arc has $Q-1$. This counts each configuration exactly once, giving the <two-domain-wall degeneracy of a Potts ring>
$$
\boxed{d_1=\binom L2Q(Q-1).}
$$
For example, $Q=2,L=4$ gives $d_0=2$ and $d_1=12$; the remaining two configurations are alternating and have four <domain walls>. The formula also covers $L=2$, where the specified ring <energy> counts the two directed neighbouring bonds, and an unequal pair breaks both. If $Q=1$ or $L=1$, every allowed configuration is a <ground state> and there is no <first excited state>. For $Q\ge3$, the number of <domain walls> need not be even: three distinct states on a three-site ring give three, although one remains impossible.

= Solution

The <Boltzmann factor> factorizes over bonds. Define the <spin-chain transfer matrix> by
$$
T_{ab}=e^{\beta J\delta_{ab}}
=1+(e^{\beta J}-1)\delta_{ab},\qquad a,b=1,\ldots,Q.
$$
Here $\delta_{ab}$ is the <Kronecker delta>. Summing the product of these <matrix elements> over all configurations, and closing the final bond by <periodic boundary conditions>, gives the <canonical partition function>
$$
Z=\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L}
T_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}\cdots T_{\sigma_L\sigma_1}
=\operatorname{Tr}T^L.
$$
Hence, writing $I$ for the <identity matrix> and $P$ for the <all-ones matrix>,
$$
\boxed{T=g(\beta J)I+P,\qquad g(\beta J)=e^{\beta J}-1.}
$$
Every element of $P^2$ is a sum of $Q$ ones, while every diagonal element of $P$ is one. Thus
$$
\boxed{P^2=QP,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}P=Q.}
$$
More explicitly, for $u=(1,\ldots,1)^T$, $Pu=Qu$. If $\sum_a v_a=0$, then $Pv=0$. These complementary <eigenspaces> have <dimensions> $1$ and $Q-1$. Equivalently, $P/Q$ is the <orthogonal projector> onto the <linear span> of $u$. The <eigenvalues> and their <eigenvalue multiplicities> are therefore
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
P:&\quad Q\ (1),\qquad 0\ (Q-1),\\
T:&\quad \lambda_+=e^{\beta J}+Q-1\ (1),\qquad
\lambda_-=e^{\beta J}-1\ (Q-1).
\end{aligned}}
$$
Since the two <matrices> share these <eigenvectors>, the exact finite-ring <canonical partition function> is
$$
\boxed{Z=(e^{\beta J}+Q-1)^L+(Q-1)(e^{\beta J}-1)^L.}
$$
Both terms matter in the finite-ring low-<temperature> limit: discarding the second would lose the correct <ground state> <degeneracy factor> and introduce a spurious single-wall contribution.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Put $x=e^{-\beta J}$. Factoring out the <ground state> <Boltzmann factor> from the exact <canonical partition function> gives
$$
Z=e^{\beta JL}\left\{[1+(Q-1)x]^L+(Q-1)(1-x)^L\right\}.
$$
At fixed finite $L$, the <binomial theorem> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[1+(Q-1)x]^L+(Q-1)(1-x)^L
&=1+(Q-1)\\
&\quad+[L(Q-1)-(Q-1)L]x\\
&\quad+\binom L2[(Q-1)^2+Q-1]x^2+O_{L,Q}(x^3)\\
&=Q\left[1+\binom L2(Q-1)x^2+O_{L,Q}(x^3)\right].
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking the <logarithm> yields the <finite-ring low-temperature expansion> of the <Helmholtz free energy>,
$$
\boxed{F=-JL-\frac{\log Q}{\beta}
-\frac{L(L-1)(Q-1)}{2\beta}e^{-2\beta J}
+O_{L,Q}\!\left(\frac{e^{-3\beta J}}{\beta}\right).}
$$
The constant term is the <ground state> <energy>. The term $-\beta^{-1}\log Q$ is the contribution of the <ground state> <degeneracy factor>. The first thermal correction follows directly by retaining the <first excited state> manifold:
$$
Z=e^{-\beta E_0}\left[d_0+d_1e^{-2\beta J}+\cdots\right],
\qquad \frac{d_1}{d_0}=\binom L2(Q-1).
$$
Each pair of <domain walls> costs $2J$, explaining the absence of an $e^{-\beta J}$ term. For $Q=2$, every configuration has an even number of <domain walls>, so the displayed remainder improves to $O_L(e^{-4\beta J}/\beta)$.

This is an expansion for fixed $L$, not a uniform <thermodynamic limit>. In particular, the small correction requires $\binom L2(Q-1)x^2\ll1$, with higher terms also controlled as $x\to0$ at fixed $L,Q$. Taking $L\to\infty$ first instead selects $\lambda_+$ and gives
$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac FL=-J-\frac1\beta\log[1+(Q-1)x].
$$
Its leading correction is of order $x$. The finite-ring pair constraint and the extensive population of <domain walls> in an arbitrarily long chain explain the different ordering of these two limits.

= Solution

Use $\hbar=1$ throughout. Let $N_a=a^\dagger a$, $N_b=b^\dagger b$ and $N=N_a+N_b$. Operators belonging to different <boson> species commute. The proposed <Schwinger boson representation> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^+S^-&=a^\dagger b b^\dagger a=N_a(N_b+1),\\
S^-S^+&=b^\dagger a a^\dagger b=N_b(N_a+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently the first of the <spin commutation relations> is
$$
\boxed{[S^+,S^-]=N_a-N_b=2S^z.}
$$
The <number operator> <commutators> $[N_a,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger$, $[N_a,a]=-a$ and their $b$ counterparts give
$$
\begin{aligned}
[S^z,S^+]&=\tfrac12([N_a,a^\dagger]b-a^\dagger[N_b,b])=a^\dagger b=S^+,\\
[S^z,S^-]&=-b^\dagger a=-S^-.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus all the <spin commutation relations> hold. Also $[N,S^\pm]=[N,S^z]=0$: each fixed-$N$ sector is invariant. To identify its <spin>, calculate the <Casimir operator>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
S^2&=(S^z)^2+\tfrac12(S^+S^-+S^-S^+)\\
&=\tfrac14(N_a-N_b)^2+N_aN_b+\tfrac12(N_a+N_b)\\
&=\frac N2\left(\frac N2+1\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The <irreducible spin representation> with prescribed $s$ therefore requires $N=2s$, and its normalized <Fock basis> correspondence is
$$
\boxed{|s,m\rangle\ \longleftrightarrow\ |s+m,s-m\rangle
=\frac{(a^\dagger)^{s+m}(b^\dagger)^{s-m}}
{\sqrt{(s+m)!(s-m)!}}|0,0\rangle.}
$$
Conversely, $s=(n_a+n_b)/2$ and $m=(n_a-n_b)/2$. The nonnegative integer <occupation numbers> give precisely $m=-s,-s+1,\ldots,s$, and there are $2s+1$ such states. As a check, the <spin raising operator> acts as
$$
a^\dagger b|s+m,s-m\rangle
=\sqrt{(s+m+1)(s-m)}\,|s+m+1,s-m-1\rangle,
$$
which is the usual <spin ladder operator> coefficient $\sqrt{s(s+1)-m(m+1)}$.

For a lattice of fixed <spins>, the <local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation> is essential:
$$
\boxed{(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)|\psi\rangle=2s|\psi\rangle
\quad\text{at every site }n.}
$$
The full two-species <bosonic Fock space> also contains other local <irreducible spin representations>; fixing only the total <boson> number does not remove them.

= Solution

For the specified positive-exponent <discrete Fourier transform>, the <inverse discrete Fourier transforms> have negative exponents:
$$
\boxed{a_n=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_qe^{-iqn}A_q,\qquad
b_n=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_qe^{-iqn}B_q.}
$$
Every sum runs over $q=2\pi j/L$, $j=0,\ldots,L-1$. Indeed, <orthogonality of roots of unity> gives
$$
\frac1L\sum_qe^{iq(r-n)}=\delta_{rn},
$$
so substituting the forward <discrete Fourier transform> into either proposed inverse recovers the original operator. Taking <Hermitian conjugates> supplies the inverse formulas for the <creation operators>.

Multiplying the inverse <discrete Fourier transforms> gives the local <number operator> combination
$$
\boxed{\frac12(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)
=\frac1{2L}\sum_{q,k}e^{i(q-k)n}
\left(A_q^\dagger A_k+B_q^\dagger B_k\right).}
$$
This local expression includes off-diagonal <momentum> terms. Only after summing over sites does <orthogonality of roots of unity> give
$$
\frac12\sum_n(a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n)
=\frac12\sum_q(A_q^\dagger A_q+B_q^\dagger B_q).
$$
In a physical fixed-$s$ state the first boxed operator acts as $s$ at every site, by the <local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation>.

= Solution

Take $L\ge2$, so neighbouring operators act at distinct sites. The identity
$$
S_n^xS_{n+1}^x+S_n^yS_{n+1}^y
=\tfrac12(S_n^+S_{n+1}^-+S_n^-S_{n+1}^+)
$$
expresses the <Hamiltonian operator> of this <quantum XY chain> in <spin ladder operators>. Substituting the <Schwinger boson representation> gives the requested quartic expression:
$$
\boxed{H=-\frac J2\sum_n
\left(a_n^\dagger b_n b_{n+1}^\dagger a_{n+1}
+b_n^\dagger a_n a_{n+1}^\dagger b_{n+1}\right).}
$$
Operators at different sites commute, allowing <normal ordering> without a correction here. For the first quartic term, the <inverse discrete Fourier transforms> give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_n a_n^\dagger b_{n+1}^\dagger b_n a_{n+1}
&=\frac1{L^2}\sum_{n,p,q,r,t}
e^{i(p+q-r-t)n}e^{i(q-t)}A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t\\
&=\frac1L\sum_{p,q,r,t}
\delta^{(L)}_{p+q,r+t}e^{i(q-t)}A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\delta^{(L)}$ is one when its two <momenta> agree modulo $2\pi$ and zero otherwise; it expresses lattice <momentum conservation>. The other quartic term has the same ordered operators with phase $e^{i(p-r)}$. On the support of this <Kronecker delta>, $p-r=t-q$ modulo $2\pi$, so the two phases sum to $2\cos(q-t)$. Thus the <Fourier vertex of a Schwinger boson chain> is
$$
\boxed{H=-\frac JL\sum_{p,q,r,t}
\delta^{(L)}_{p+q,r+t}\cos(q-t)\,
A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t.}
$$
An equivalent three-sum expression sets $t=p+q-r$ modulo $2\pi$, so its coefficient is $\cos(r-p)$. The factor $1/L$ comes from four factors $L^{-1/2}$ and one site sum, while the factor of two from the two exchange terms cancels the original $1/2$. The formula is <Hermitian>: conjugation exchanges $p$ with $t$ and $q$ with $r$, and the coefficient is unchanged after imposing lattice <momentum conservation>. It also preserves each local total <boson> number in its real-space form, hence preserves the physical constrained subspace.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Use $\langle O\rangle=\langle G|O|G\rangle$. The given state is a <two-species uniform boson number state>: only the $q=0$ mode of each species is occupied. Applying an <annihilation operator> to an unoccupied mode gives zero, while $A_0^\dagger A_0$ has <eigenvalue> $n_A$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\langle A_q^\dagger A_k\rangle=n_A\delta_{q0}\delta_{k0},\qquad
\langle B_q^\dagger B_k\rangle=n_B\delta_{q0}\delta_{k0}.}
$$
The two species have independent fixed <occupation numbers>, so mixed <expectation values> factorize. The inverse <discrete Fourier transforms> yield
$$
\langle a_i^\dagger a_j\rangle=\frac{n_A}{L},\qquad
\langle b_i^\dagger b_j\rangle=\frac{n_B}{L},\qquad
\boxed{\langle a_n^\dagger a_n+b_n^\dagger b_n\rangle
=\frac{n_A+n_B}{L}.}
$$
In the momentum-space <Hamiltonian operator>, only $p=q=r=t=0$ contributes: each <annihilation operator> must remove an occupied <boson>, and each <creation operator> must restore it to the same mode. Hence
$$
\langle A_p^\dagger B_q^\dagger B_r A_t\rangle
=n_An_B\delta_{p0}\delta_{q0}\delta_{r0}\delta_{t0},
\qquad
\boxed{\langle H\rangle=-\frac JLn_An_B.}
$$
There is no replacement of $n_B$ by $n_B+1$ in this <energy>: in the real-space calculation the relevant neighboring-site <commutator> is zero. Both expressions assume distinct neighbouring sites, as in <2023/2/b/ii>.

To match the prescribed <spin> length in the mean, impose $n_A+n_B=2sL=:N$. At fixed integer $N$, the <energy> is minimized by maximizing $n_An_B=[N^2-(n_A-n_B)^2]/4$. Consequently the best choices within this relaxed family are
$$
\boxed{\{n_A,n_B\}=\{\lfloor sL\rfloor,\lceil sL\rceil\}.}
$$
When $N$ is even, $n_A=n_B=sL$ and $\langle H\rangle=-Js^2L$. When $N$ is odd, either assignment of $(N-1)/2,(N+1)/2$ gives $\langle H\rangle=-J(N^2-1)/(4L)$. Without fixing $N$, this auxiliary <boson> <Hamiltonian operator> has no lower bound: taking $n_A=n_B\to\infty$ sends its <expectation value> to $-\infty$.

Next, $S_i^x=(a_i^\dagger b_i+b_i^\dagger a_i)/2$. Each term changes the two species' <occupation numbers>, producing a state <orthogonal> to $|G\rangle$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle S_i^x\rangle=0,\qquad \langle S_i^y\rangle=0,\qquad
\langle S_i^z\rangle=\frac{n_A-n_B}{2L}.}
$$
For distinct sites $i,j$ modulo $L$, expand the product of the two <spin operators>. The terms $S_i^+S_j^+$ and $S_i^-S_j^-$ again have zero <expectation value>. The remaining terms give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle S_i^+S_j^-\rangle
&=\langle a_i^\dagger a_j\rangle\langle b_i b_j^\dagger\rangle
=\frac{n_A}{L}\frac{n_B}{L},\\
\langle S_i^-S_j^+\rangle&=\frac{n_An_B}{L^2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\langle b_i b_j^\dagger\rangle=n_B/L+\delta_{ij}$ and the <Kronecker delta> vanishes at distinct sites. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\langle S_m^xS_{m+n}^x\rangle=\frac{n_An_B}{2L^2}
\quad(n\not\equiv0\pmod L).}
$$
The same holds for the $y$ components. At the balanced choice and as $L\to\infty$, each transverse <two-point correlation function> tends to $s^2/2$ independently of separation, whereas the one-point transverse <magnetization> is zero. This is <transverse spin order in a number eigenstate>: the state has a definite total $S^z=(n_A-n_B)/2$ and retains the global <U(1)> <symmetry> of rotations about $z$, yet has the transverse correlations of an ordered state with no selected common azimuth. The sum of the $x$ and $y$ correlations tends to $s^2$, describing ferromagnetic <long-range order> within this approximation. The <thermodynamic limit> is understood with both the separation and $L$ growing, without identifying the two sites periodically.

**Fixed-spin qualification.** The proposed state is not actually in the physical fixed-$s$ <Hilbert space> for $s>0,L>1$. It satisfies the <local spin constraint in the Schwinger boson representation> only in <expectation value>. For example, with $L=2,s=1/2,n_A=n_B=1$,
$$
|G\rangle=\tfrac12(a_0^\dagger+a_1^\dagger)(b_0^\dagger+b_1^\dagger)|\mathrm{vac}\rangle.
$$
The nonzero component $a_0^\dagger b_0^\dagger|\mathrm{vac}\rangle/2$ has local total <occupation numbers> $(2,0)$ and hence local <spins> $(1,0)$, instead of $(1/2,1/2)$. More generally the local total <number operator> $N_n$ has <variance>
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(N_n)
=\frac{n_A+n_B}{L}\left(1-\frac1L\right)
=2s\left(1-\frac1L\right)>0.}
$$
Each species contributes a <binomial distribution> at a given site, with success probability $1/L$, and the two contributions are independent. In the physical subspace that <variance> must be zero. The proposed state even has
$$
\langle S_n^2\rangle=s(s+1)+\tfrac14\operatorname{Var}(N_n),
$$
by the <Casimir operator> identity from <2023/2/a>. Thus the calculated <energy> is a relaxed <mean-field theory> estimate, not a <Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle> bound for the stated fixed-$s$ <quantum XY chain>. Projecting onto $N_n=2s$ at every site would give a physical trial state and generally change its <expectation values>; in the two-site example the projected state has <energy> $-J$, whereas the unprojected value is $-J/2$. The calculated <long-range order> characterizes the supplied approximation and does not establish <long-range order> in the exact one-dimensional <ground state>.
