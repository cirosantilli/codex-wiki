= Solution
{parent=i}

Each neighboring pair of <Ising spins> contributes at least $-J$ to the <energy>, with equality exactly when the two <Ising spins> are opposite. For even $N$, alternation closes consistently under the <periodic boundary conditions>. The <ground states> are therefore
$$
S_j=\sigma(-1)^{j-1},\qquad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\},
\qquad \boxed{E_0=-JN,\quad g_0=2.}
$$
Here $g_0$ is the <ground-state degeneracy>. Choosing $S_1$ fixes every other <Ising spin>, so these are all the <ground states>; translations do not give additional <spin configurations>. The two <ground states> are exchanged by <spin inversion symmetry>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Let $m$ count the parallel neighboring pairs, or <frustrated bonds in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring>. The <periodic boundary conditions> imply
$$
\prod_{j=1}^N(S_jS_{j+1})=\prod_{j=1}^NS_j^2=1.
$$
There are $N-m$ antiparallel pairs, so $(-1)^{N-m}=1$: the number of antiparallel pairs must be even. For odd $N$, $m$ must consequently be odd. Each <frustrated bond in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring> raises the <energy> by $2J$ relative to a satisfied pair, giving
$$
H=-JN+2Jm.
$$
The minimum is attained at $m=1$. Choose its position among the $N$ labeled bonds, choose either orientation of one <Ising spin>, then alternate across every other bond. This constructs exactly two <spin configurations> per position. Thus
$$
\boxed{E_0=-J(N-2),\qquad g_0=2N\quad(N\text{ odd}).}
$$
This is <geometric frustration>: every local interaction prefers opposite <Ising spins>, but the closed odd ring prevents all preferences being satisfied at once. No particular location is preferred for the single <frustrated bond in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring>.

Write $K=\beta J$, where $\beta=1/(k_BT)$ is the <inverse temperature>, $T$ is <temperature> and $k_B$ is the <Boltzmann constant>. In the <canonical ensemble>, each <spin configuration> has <probability> $e^{-\beta H}/Z_N$. The nearest-neighbor <Boltzmann factors> multiply, so the <spin-chain transfer matrix>, in the order $+1,-1$, is
$$
\mathsf T_{ss'}=e^{-Kss'},\qquad
\mathsf T=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-K}&e^K\\e^K&e^{-K}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Multiplying its entries around the ring and summing over every <Ising spin> yields the <partition function>:
$$
Z_N=\sum_{S_1,\ldots,S_N}\mathsf T_{S_1S_2}\cdots\mathsf T_{S_NS_1}
=\operatorname{tr}\mathsf T^N.
$$
The <trace> implements the <periodic boundary conditions>; replacing it by an endpoint sum would describe a different <partition function>. The normalized <eigenvectors> and corresponding <eigenvalues> are
$$
|+\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom11,\quad
|-\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom1{-1},\qquad
\lambda_+=2\cosh K,\quad \lambda_-=-2\sinh K.
$$
The negative <eigenvalue> is essential for the <antiferromagnetic Ising model>. Introduce their ratio
$$
t=\frac{\lambda_-}{\lambda_+}=-\tanh K.
$$
The exact finite-$N$ <partition function> and <Helmholtz free energy> are
$$
\boxed{Z_N=(2\cosh K)^N+( -2\sinh K)^N
=(2\cosh K)^N(1+t^N),}
$$
$$
\boxed{F_N=-\frac1\beta\log Z_N
=-\frac N\beta\log(2\cosh K)-\frac1\beta\log(1+t^N).}
$$
For finite $K>0$, $|t|<1$, so $Z_N>0$ for both even and odd $N$. The last term retains the finite-ring effect of <geometric frustration>.

To insert an <Ising spin> into this <trace>, use the <diagonal matrix> $\mathsf S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. In the <eigenbasis> of $\mathsf T$, it exchanges $|+\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$, and hence has vanishing diagonal entries. The <one-point correlation function> is therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle S_1\rangle=
\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf S\mathsf T^N)}{Z_N}=0.}
$$
The same conclusion follows directly from <spin inversion symmetry>: reversing every <Ising spin> preserves the <energy> and the <Boltzmann factor> while reversing $S_1$.

For the <two-point correlation function>, the two insertions divide the ring into paths of $n$ and $N-n$ bonds. Thus
$$
C_n\equiv\langle S_1S_{n+1}\rangle
=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(\mathsf S\mathsf T^n\mathsf S\mathsf T^{N-n})}{Z_N}
=\frac{\lambda_-^n\lambda_+^{N-n}+\lambda_+^n\lambda_-^{N-n}}
{\lambda_+^N+\lambda_-^N}.
$$
In particular, the exact <finite-ring antiferromagnetic Ising correlation> is
$$
\boxed{C_n=\frac{t^n+t^{N-n}}{1+t^N},\qquad t=-\tanh(\beta J),\quad0\le n\le N-1.}
$$
Because the <one-point correlation functions> vanish, this is also the <connected correlation function>. It gives $C_0=1$ and $C_n=C_{N-n}$, as required by $S_1^2=1$ and exchanging the two paths around the ring. At $K=0$, distinct <Ising spins> are independent and $C_n=0$ for $1\le n\le N-1$; $C_0=1$ is understood separately if a power $0^0$ occurs in the displayed expression.

= Solution

Take the <thermodynamic limit> $N\to\infty$ at fixed separation $n$ and fixed finite $K=\beta J>0$. Since $|t|=\tanh K<1$, the terms $t^{N-n}$ and $t^N$ in the <finite-ring antiferromagnetic Ising correlation> vanish, through either even or odd $N$. Therefore
$$
C_n\longrightarrow t^n=(-1)^n(\tanh K)^n
=(-1)^n\exp\bigl[n\log(\tanh K)\bigr].
$$
Matching the decaying <exponential function> gives the <correlation length>, in units of the site spacing:
$$
\boxed{\langle S_1S_{n+1}\rangle=(-1)^ne^{-n/\xi},\qquad
\xi=-\frac1{\log\tanh(\beta J)}
=\frac1{\log\coth(\beta J)}.}
$$
If the physical spacing is $a$, the physical <correlation length> is $a\xi$. The alternating sign records the preferred opposite orientation of neighboring <Ising spins>; the finite <correlation length> shows that this preference does not maintain long-range <Néel order> at positive <temperature>.

For large $K$, $\tanh K=1-2e^{-2K}+O(e^{-4K})$, so $\xi\sim e^{2K}/2$: the <correlation length> becomes large on cooling, but diverges only at zero <temperature>. At infinite <temperature>, $K\to0^+$ and $\xi\to0$; the separate equal-site <two-point correlation function> remains $C_0=1$.

= Solution

Keep $N$ fixed and put $q=\tanh K$, so $q\to1^-$ as <temperature> tends to zero. For even $N$, the <finite-ring antiferromagnetic Ising correlation> becomes
$$
C_n=(-1)^n\frac{q^n+q^{N-n}}{1+q^N}
\longrightarrow\boxed{(-1)^n\quad(N\text{ even}).}
$$
Both alternating <ground states> have $S_1S_{n+1}=(-1)^n$, so their equal-weight <canonical ensemble> has the same <two-point correlation function> despite its zero <one-point correlation function>.

For odd $N$, the signs instead give
$$
C_n=(-1)^n\frac{q^n-q^{N-n}}{1-q^N}.
$$
Direct substitution gives an indeterminate ratio. Expanding at fixed $N,n$ with $q=1-\delta$,
$$
q^n-q^{N-n}=(N-2n)\delta+O(\delta^2),\qquad
1-q^N=N\delta+O(\delta^2).
$$
Consequently the <zero-temperature correlation of an odd antiferromagnetic Ising ring> is
$$
\boxed{\lim_{\beta J\to\infty}\langle S_1S_{n+1}\rangle
=\begin{cases}
(-1)^n,&N\text{ even},\\
(-1)^n\left(1-\dfrac{2n}{N}\right),&N\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
$$
The odd-ring factor has a direct <probability> interpretation. At zero <temperature>, the <canonical ensemble> gives equal weight to its $2N$ <ground states>. The single <frustrated bond in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring> is uniformly located, with <probability> $n/N$ of lying on the chosen $n$-bond path. If it is absent from that path, $S_1S_{n+1}=(-1)^n$; if it is present, this product is $-(-1)^n$. Averaging gives $(-1)^n[(1-n/N)-n/N]$.

Thus the decay with separation here comes from averaging a movable, compulsory <frustrated bond in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring>, even after additional thermally excited <frustrated bonds in an antiferromagnetic Ising ring> disappear. In particular $C_1=-1+2/N$ for odd $N$, and $JN C_1=-J(N-2)$ reproduces the <ground-state energy>. The formula also respects $C_n=C_{N-n}$, although its factor $1-2n/N$ changes sign halfway around the ring.

The order and scale of limits matter. Taking the <thermodynamic limit> first at positive <temperature> gives the <exponential function> in part (c). Cooling an odd finite ring first leaves the linear factor $1-2n/N$. Both orders subsequently give $(-1)^n$ at fixed $n$, but this agreement is not uniform in separation: when $n/N$ stays nonzero, the odd-ring linear factor survives in the zero-<temperature> expression.

Use the standard <canonical anticommutation relations> for the two distinct <fermionic> orbitals. In particular $\{a,b^\dagger\}=\{a^\dagger,b\}=0$, in addition to the relations explicitly listed in the paper; these mixed relations are part of the usual two-orbital <fermionic Fock space> interpretation. Normalize the original <vacuum state> by $\langle0|0\rangle=1$ and fix the ordering
$$
|n_a n_b\rangle=(a^\dagger)^{n_a}(b^\dagger)^{n_b}|0\rangle.
$$
The <canonical anticommutation relations> give $a^2=(a^\dagger)^2=0$ and $aa^\dagger=1-a^\dagger a$. Consequently the <number operator> is an <orthogonal projector>:
$$
N_a^2=a^\dagger aa^\dagger a
=a^\dagger(1-a^\dagger a)a=N_a.
$$
The same holds for $N_b$. Hence each <eigenvalue> obeys $n^2=n$ and is either $0$ or $1$, expressing the <Pauli exclusion principle>. Also $[N_a,N_b]=0$, so the two <number operators> have a simultaneous <orthonormal basis> of <Fock states>:
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{state}&N_a\text{ eigenvalue}&N_b\text{ eigenvalue}\\\hline
|00\rangle=|0\rangle&0&0\\
|10\rangle=a^\dagger|0\rangle&1&0\\
|01\rangle=b^\dagger|0\rangle&0&1\\
|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle&1&1
\end{array}
$$
**Each <number operator> has <eigenvalues> $0,1$, each with <state degeneracy> two.** For $N_a$, the zero <eigenspace> is spanned by $|00\rangle,|01\rangle$ and the unit <eigenspace> by $|10\rangle,|11\rangle$; for $N_b$, these are respectively $|00\rangle,|10\rangle$ and $|01\rangle,|11\rangle$. Any normalized <linear combination> within one such <eigenspace> is also an <eigenstate> of that <number operator>.

The ordering convention will matter below: $b^\dagger a^\dagger|0\rangle=-|11\rangle$ and
$$
ab|11\rangle=aba^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle
=-aa^\dagger bb^\dagger|0\rangle=-|0\rangle.
$$

Writing the <Bogoliubov transformation> componentwise, together with its <Hermitian adjoint>, gives
$$
a=uc+vd^\dagger,\qquad b=-vc^\dagger+ud,\qquad
a^\dagger=uc^\dagger+vd,\qquad b^\dagger=-vc+ud^\dagger.
$$
The full <canonical anticommutation relations> for $c,d$ imply
$$
\{a,a^\dagger\}=u^2\{c,c^\dagger\}+v^2\{d^\dagger,d\}=u^2+v^2,
\qquad \{b,b^\dagger\}=u^2+v^2.
$$
Equal-type <anticommutators> vanish because $c^2=d^2=0$ and $\{c,d^\dagger\}=\{c^\dagger,d\}=0$:
$$
\{a,a\}=\{b,b\}=0.
$$
For the mixed <anticommutators>, the only potentially nonzero terms cancel:
$$
\{a,b\}=-uv\{c,c^\dagger\}+uv\{d^\dagger,d\}
=-uv+uv=0,
$$
while $\{a,b^\dagger\}=0$ follows from $\{c,c\}=\{d^\dagger,d^\dagger\}=\{c,d^\dagger\}=0$. Taking the <Hermitian adjoint> supplies the remaining relations. Thus the necessary and sufficient condition is
$$
\boxed{u^2+v^2=1.}
$$
Equivalently, the <matrix> $R=\begin{pmatrix}u&v\\-v&u\end{pmatrix}$ is an <orthogonal matrix>, $R^{\mathsf T}R=I$, giving a <canonical rotation of two fermionic modes>. Its inverse is therefore
$$
\boxed{c=ua-vb^\dagger,\qquad d=va^\dagger+ub.}
$$

= Solution

Let $N_c=c^\dagger c$, $N_d=d^\dagger d$ and $P=cd+d^\dagger c^\dagger$. Substitution of the <Bogoliubov transformation>, with <normal ordering> using $dd^\dagger=1-d^\dagger d$ and $cc^\dagger=1-c^\dagger c$, gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
a^\dagger a+b^\dagger b
&=(u^2-v^2)(N_c+N_d)+2v^2-2uvP,\\
ab+b^\dagger a^\dagger
&=2uv(N_c+N_d)-2uv+(u^2-v^2)P.
\end{aligned}
$$
For example, the $v^2dd^\dagger$ term in $a^\dagger a$ contributes $v^2-v^2N_d$; these constants must be retained to obtain the <ground-state energy>. The <two-orbital fermionic pairing Hamiltonian> is consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
H={}&[\varepsilon(u^2-v^2)+2\Delta uv](N_c+N_d)\\
&+[\Delta(u^2-v^2)-2\varepsilon uv]P
+2\varepsilon v^2-2\Delta uv.
\end{aligned}
$$
To eliminate the pairing term, choose a <canonical rotation of two fermionic modes> satisfying
$$
2\varepsilon uv=\Delta(u^2-v^2).
$$
With $u=\cos\theta$, $v=\sin\theta$, take $0<\theta<\pi/4$, so $\tan2\theta=\Delta/\varepsilon$. Setting $E=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}>0$ gives
$$
u^2-v^2=\frac\varepsilon E,\qquad 2uv=\frac\Delta E,\qquad
\boxed{u=\sqrt{\frac{E+\varepsilon}{2E}},\qquad
v=\sqrt{\frac{E-\varepsilon}{2E}}.}
$$
This positive choice fixes a convenient convention for the <vacuum state> in part (d). It yields
$$
\boxed{H=E(N_c+N_d)+\varepsilon-E,\qquad
\lambda=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2},\quad
\mu=\varepsilon-\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}.}
$$
Both <number operators> independently have <eigenvalues> $0,1$. Hence the <energy eigenvalues> and <state degeneracies> are
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
N_c+N_d&\text{energy}&\text{degeneracy}\\\hline
0&\varepsilon-E&1\\
1&\varepsilon&2\\
2&\varepsilon+E&1
\end{array}}
$$
There is one <fermionic Bogoliubov quasiparticle> of <energy> $E$ per occupied transformed orbital. The two <Nambu spinor> <matrix> <eigenvalues> $\pm E$ should not be mistaken for two independently occupiable levels of opposite <energy>; the <normal ordering> constant and the two positive <quasiparticle> excitation costs give the four physical <energy eigenvalues> above.

An independent <diagonalization of a matrix> checks both the pairing sign and the additive constant. In the ordered <orthonormal basis> $|0\rangle,|11\rangle$, the even-<fermion parity> block is
$$
H_{\mathrm{even}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\Delta\\-\Delta&2\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},
$$
because $ab|11\rangle=-|0\rangle$ and $b^\dagger a^\dagger|0\rangle=-|11\rangle$. Its <characteristic polynomial> is $x^2-2\varepsilon x-\Delta^2$, giving $x=\varepsilon\pm E$. The odd-<fermion parity> <Fock states> $a^\dagger|0\rangle,b^\dagger|0\rangle$ are unaffected by pairing and both have <energy> $\varepsilon$.

Equivalently, with the <Nambu spinor> $\Psi=(a,b^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}$,
$$
H=\Psi^\dagger h\Psi+\varepsilon,\qquad
h=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&-\Delta\\-\Delta&-\varepsilon\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R^{\mathsf T}hR=\operatorname{diag}(E,-E).
$$
Replacing $\Psi$ by $R(c,d^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}$ gives $E c^\dagger c-Edd^\dagger+\varepsilon=E(N_c+N_d)+\varepsilon-E$, reproducing the result by <normal ordering>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

The <canonical anticommutation relations> make $(a^\dagger b^\dagger)^2=0$, so the proposed <exponential function> terminates exactly:
$$
|\widetilde0\rangle=A\bigl(|0\rangle+B|11\rangle\bigr).
$$
Use the inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> $c=ua-vb^\dagger$, $d=va^\dagger+ub$, and the actions $a|11\rangle=b^\dagger|0\rangle$ and $b|11\rangle=-a^\dagger|0\rangle$. The two <vacuum state> conditions become
$$
c|\widetilde0\rangle=A(uB-v)b^\dagger|0\rangle=0,\qquad
d|\widetilde0\rangle=A(v-uB)a^\dagger|0\rangle=0.
$$
Both therefore require $B=v/u$. Normalizing this <quantum state> gives $1=A^2(1+B^2)$. Choosing positive $A$, which fixes an irrelevant overall <global phase>, yields $A=u$ because $u^2+v^2=1$. Thus the <two-mode fermionic paired vacuum> is
$$
\boxed{|\widetilde0\rangle=u|0\rangle+v a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle,\qquad
A=\sqrt{\frac{E+\varepsilon}{2E}},\qquad
B=\frac\Delta{E+\varepsilon}
=\sqrt{\frac{E-\varepsilon}{E+\varepsilon}},\quad
E=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2}.}
$$
The other sign for $A$ describes the same physical <quantum state>. The positive relative coefficient is fixed by the paper's pairing order and the convention $|11\rangle=a^\dagger b^\dagger|0\rangle$.

As a direct <eigenvector> check, $\Delta v=(E-\varepsilon)u$ and $\Delta u=(E+\varepsilon)v$ imply
$$
\begin{pmatrix}0&-\Delta\\-\Delta&2\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}
\binom uv=(\varepsilon-E)\binom uv.
$$
It is therefore the unique <ground state>, as also follows from positive <quasiparticle> excitation <energy> $E$. It has definite even <fermion parity> but is not an <eigenstate> of the original total <number operator>: the empty-pair and occupied-pair <probabilities> are $u^2$ and $v^2$. In particular $\langle N_a\rangle=\langle N_b\rangle=v^2$. The pair amplitude is $\langle ab\rangle=-uv$, consistently with $ab|11\rangle=-|0\rangle$.

For the limiting check $\Delta\to0^+$ at fixed positive $\varepsilon$, $A\to1$, $B\to0$, so $|\widetilde0\rangle\to|0\rangle$. For $\varepsilon\to0^+$ at fixed positive $\Delta$, $A\to1/\sqrt2$, $B\to1$, giving equal amplitudes for the empty and occupied pair. These checks also fix which branch of the <Bogoliubov transformation> produces the <ground state>.
