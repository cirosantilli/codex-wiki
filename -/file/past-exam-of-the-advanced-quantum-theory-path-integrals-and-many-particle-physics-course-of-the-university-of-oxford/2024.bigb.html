= Solution
{parent=a}

Put $\mathcal T=\beta\hbar>0$, the interval of <imaginary time>, and assume $m,\omega>0$. For a <variation> $x_0+\epsilon\eta$ with $\eta(0)=\eta(\mathcal T)=0$, the <first variation> of the <Euclidean action> is
$$
\delta S=m\int_0^{\mathcal T}(\dot x_0\dot\eta+\omega^2x_0\eta)\,d\tau
=m[\dot x_0\eta]_0^{\mathcal T}
+m\int_0^{\mathcal T}(-\ddot x_0+\omega^2x_0)\eta\,d\tau.
$$
The <boundary term> vanishes. The <fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations> therefore gives the <Euler-Lagrange equation>
$$
\boxed{\ddot x_0=\omega^2x_0.}
$$
Its sign differs from the real-<time> <harmonic oscillator equation> because the <Euclidean action> contains positive <kinetic energy> and positive <potential energy>. The general solution is $A\cosh(\omega\tau)+B\sinh(\omega\tau)$. Applying the two endpoint conditions gives $A=x$ and $B=-x\tanh(\omega\mathcal T/2)$. Equivalently, the <equal-endpoint Euclidean oscillator path> is
$$
\boxed{x_0(\tau)=x\frac{\cosh[\omega(\tau-\mathcal T/2)]}{\cosh(\omega\mathcal T/2)}.}
$$
This form displays reflection about the midpoint and satisfies both endpoints immediately. On this path,
$$
\frac d{d\tau}(x_0\dot x_0)=\dot x_0^2+x_0\ddot x_0
=\dot x_0^2+\omega^2x_0^2.
$$
Thus the <Euclidean action> reduces to a <boundary term>. Since $\dot x_0(\mathcal T)=\omega x\tanh(\omega\mathcal T/2)$ and $\dot x_0(0)=-\omega x\tanh(\omega\mathcal T/2)$,
$$
S[x_0]=\frac m2[x_0\dot x_0]_0^{\mathcal T}
=\boxed{m\omega x^2\tanh\!\left(\frac{\beta\hbar\omega}{2}\right).}
$$
The positive <quadratic form> $m\int_0^{\mathcal T}(\dot\eta^2+\omega^2\eta^2)\,d\tau$ is the <second variation>, so this stationary path is the unique minimum, rather than merely an arbitrary <stationary point>.

After the shift $x(\tau)=x_0(\tau)+y(\tau)$, the endpoint values of $y$ are both zero. Expanding the <Euclidean action> gives
$$
S[x_0+y]=S[x_0]+S[y]
+m\int_0^{\mathcal T}(\dot x_0\dot y+\omega^2x_0y)\,d\tau.
$$
By <integration by parts>, the cross term is
$$
m[\dot x_0y]_0^{\mathcal T}
+m\int_0^{\mathcal T}(-\ddot x_0+\omega^2x_0)y\,d\tau=0.
$$
The first term vanishes by the homogeneous <Dirichlet boundary conditions>, and the second by the <Euler-Lagrange equation>. The translation has unit <Jacobian determinant> in the <time-sliced configuration-space path integral>, so $\mathcal D x=\mathcal D y$. Consequently <classical-path factorization of a quadratic path integral> is exact here:
$$
\boxed{\langle x|e^{-\beta H}|x\rangle=e^{-S[x_0]/\hbar}Z,\qquad
Z=\int_{y(0)=y(\mathcal T)=0}\mathcal D y\,
\exp\!\left[-\frac m{2\hbar}\int_0^{\mathcal T}(\dot y^2+\omega^2y^2)\,d\tau\right].}
$$
All dependence on the common endpoint $x$ lies in
$$
\boxed{e^{-S[x_0]/\hbar}
=\exp\!\left[-\frac{m\omega x^2}{\hbar}\tanh\!\left(\frac{\beta\hbar\omega}{2}\right)\right],
\qquad Z\text{ is independent of }x.}
$$
The endpoint domain and the integrand defining $Z$ are the same for every $x$. This $Z$ is a zero-endpoint <Gaussian path integral>; the <canonical partition function> instead integrates the entire diagonal kernel over $x$. The final sentence of the printed part omits $/\hbar$ from its exponential: the physical <Boltzmann factor> has the dimensionless exponent $-S/\hbar$.

An optional normalization check uses the <Dirichlet oscillator fluctuation determinant>. Its <eigenvalues> are $m[(n\pi/\mathcal T)^2+\omega^2]$, $n\ge1$. Relative to the free-particle <Gaussian path integral>, the <hyperbolic-sine infinite product> gives
$$
\frac{Z}{\sqrt{m/(2\pi\hbar\mathcal T)}}
=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[1+\frac{\omega^2\mathcal T^2}{n^2\pi^2}\right]^{-1/2}
=\sqrt{\frac{\omega\mathcal T}{\sinh(\omega\mathcal T)}}.
$$
Thus $Z=\sqrt{m\omega/[2\pi\hbar\sinh(\beta\hbar\omega)]}$. Integrating the resulting <diagonal thermal kernel of a quantum harmonic oscillator> by a <Gaussian integral> yields $\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H}=[2\sinh(\beta\hbar\omega/2)]^{-1}$, including the <zero-point energy>.

Because $S[x_0]=0$ at $x=0$, the factor $Z$ cancels from the ratio. Write $z=\beta\hbar\omega$ and $q=\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x$. The exact answer is
$$
\boxed{R=\exp[-q^2\tanh(z/2)].}
$$
At high <temperature>, $z\ll1$. Expanding the <hyperbolic tangent> gives
$$
\log R=-q^2\left(\frac z2-\frac{z^3}{24}+O(z^5)\right),
\qquad
\boxed{R\simeq\exp\!\left(-\frac{\beta m\omega^2x^2}{2}\right).}
$$
The displayed leading expression is the ratio of classical <Boltzmann factors> for <potential energy> $V(x)=m\omega^2x^2/2$ and $V(0)=0$. Indeed the classical <canonical ensemble> gives
$$
p_{\rm cl}(x)\ \propto\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp\,
e^{-\beta[p^2/(2m)+V(x)]}
\ \propto\ e^{-\beta V(x)}.
$$
The <momentum> <Gaussian integral> is independent of $x$ and cancels in the ratio. This is the <classical phase-space limit of the thermal kernel>. Here $\beta=1/(k_BT)$, with $k_B$ the <Boltzmann constant>; the associated classical position <variance> is $1/(\beta m\omega^2)$. At fixed $x$, $R$ ultimately tends to one as <temperature> tends to infinity, while its width grows.

At low <temperature>, $z\gg1$. Now $\tanh(z/2)=1-2e^{-z}+O(e^{-2z})$, so at fixed $q$
$$
R=e^{-q^2}\left[1+2q^2e^{-z}+O(e^{-2z})\right],
\qquad
\boxed{R\simeq\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega x^2}{\hbar}\right).}
$$
Unlike the classical high-<temperature> expression, this limiting width remains nonzero at zero <temperature>, reflecting the <ground state> of the <quantum harmonic oscillator>.

Insert the complete <orthonormal basis> of <energy eigenstates> on either side of the <Boltzmann factor>. The <spectral theorem> gives
$$
e^{-\beta H}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\hbar\omega(n+1/2)}|n\rangle\langle n|,
\qquad
\boxed{\langle x|e^{-\beta H}|x\rangle
=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta\hbar\omega(n+1/2)}|\varphi_n(x)|^2.}
$$
At low <temperature>, the gap $\hbar\omega$ suppresses excited <energy eigenstates>. The <harmonic oscillator eigenfunction> of the <ground state> is
$$
\varphi_0(x)=\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}
e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}.
$$
Therefore
$$
\langle x|e^{-\beta H}|x\rangle
\sim e^{-\beta\hbar\omega/2}|\varphi_0(x)|^2,
\qquad
\boxed{R\longrightarrow\frac{|\varphi_0(x)|^2}{|\varphi_0(0)|^2}
=e^{-m\omega x^2/\hbar}.}
$$
The common <zero-point energy> factor cancels. Dividing the kernel by its <trace> would give the position <probability density> of the <canonical ensemble>; at zero <temperature> this tends to $|\varphi_0(x)|^2$, whose <variance> is $\hbar/(2m\omega)$. Thus zero <temperature> projects onto the <ground state> and retains its quantum spread, rather than concentrating at the minimum of the classical <potential energy>.

There is also a check on the first correction. With $q=\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\,x$, the first excited <harmonic oscillator eigenfunction> satisfies $|\varphi_1(x)|^2=2q^2|\varphi_0(x)|^2$ and $\varphi_1(0)=0$. Hence the <spectral theorem> gives $R=e^{-q^2}[1+2q^2e^{-z}+O(e^{-2z})]$, in agreement with part (c).

Let $V=L^3$ be the box <volume>. The <number operator> is the sum of the <occupation numbers> of all <momentum eigenstates>, including the zero mode:
$$
\boxed{\hat N=\int_V d^3r\,\psi^\dagger(\mathbf r)\psi(\mathbf r)
=\sum_{\mathbf k}\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf k}.}
$$
In the <Fourier series> for the <contact interaction>, the two <creation operators> supply phase $e^{-i(\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2)\cdot\mathbf r}$ and the two <annihilation operators> supply $e^{i(\mathbf k_3+\mathbf k_4)\cdot\mathbf r}$. <Fourier orthogonality> with <periodic boundary conditions> gives
$$
\int_V d^3r\,e^{i(\mathbf k_3+\mathbf k_4-\mathbf k_1-\mathbf k_2)\cdot\mathbf r}
=V\delta_{\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2,\,\mathbf k_3+\mathbf k_4}.
$$
The four normalization factors together contribute $V^{-2}$, leaving the <momentum-space contact-interaction Hamiltonian>
$$
\boxed{H_{\rm int}=\frac{u}{2V}
\sum_{\mathbf k_1,\mathbf k_2,\mathbf k_3,\mathbf k_4}
\delta_{\mathbf k_1+\mathbf k_2,\,\mathbf k_3+\mathbf k_4}
\psi_{\mathbf k_1}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf k_2}^\dagger
\psi_{\mathbf k_3}\psi_{\mathbf k_4}.}
$$
The <Kronecker delta> enforces <momentum conservation>. An equivalent expression that resolves the momentum transfer is
$$
H_{\rm int}=\frac{u}{2V}\sum_{\mathbf k,\mathbf p,\mathbf q}
\psi_{\mathbf k+\mathbf q}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf p-\mathbf q}^\dagger
\psi_{\mathbf p}\psi_{\mathbf k}.
$$
Both are already in <normal ordering>; no extra <commutator> constant is introduced.

Taking the <Hermitian adjoint> gives $\varphi_{\mathbf k}=c_k\psi_{\mathbf k}+s_k\psi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger$. For nonzero <wavevectors>, the <canonical commutation relations> imply
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\varphi_{\mathbf k},\varphi_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]
&=c_kc_q[\psi_{\mathbf k},\psi_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]
+s_ks_q[\psi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger,\psi_{-\mathbf q}]\\
&=(c_kc_q-s_ks_q)\delta_{\mathbf k,\mathbf q}.
\end{aligned}
$$
When the <Kronecker delta> is nonzero, $k=q$, so the <hyperbolic Pythagorean identity> $c_k^2-s_k^2=1$ gives
$$
\boxed{[\varphi_{\mathbf k},\varphi_{\mathbf q}^\dagger]=\delta_{\mathbf k,\mathbf q}.}
$$
For the two <annihilation operators>, the only potentially nonzero terms are
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\varphi_{\mathbf k},\varphi_{\mathbf q}]
&=c_ks_q[\psi_{\mathbf k},\psi_{-\mathbf q}^\dagger]
+s_kc_q[\psi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger,\psi_{\mathbf q}]\\
&=(c_ks_q-s_kc_q)\delta_{\mathbf k,-\mathbf q}=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here $\mathbf q=-\mathbf k$ implies equal magnitudes and hence equal $c,s$, because $\theta_k$ depends only on $|\mathbf k|$. Thus
$$
\boxed{[\varphi_{\mathbf k},\varphi_{\mathbf q}]=0.}
$$
The corresponding <commutators> of two <creation operators> vanish by the <Hermitian adjoint>. This verifies that the <Bogoliubov transformation> preserves the full <bosonic> <canonical commutation relations>.

= Solution

Normalize the specified state by $\langle0|0\rangle=1$ and write $\langle A\rangle=\langle0|A|0\rangle$. Its zero mode is a <coherent state>, so any normally ordered product of zero-mode <creation operators> and <annihilation operators> can be evaluated using $\psi_0\mapsto\sqrt{N_0}$ and $\psi_0^\dagger\mapsto\sqrt{N_0}$. In particular $\langle(\psi_0^\dagger)^2\psi_0^2\rangle=N_0^2$, rather than $N_0(N_0-1)$, which would apply to a zero-mode <number state>.

Sort the <momentum-space contact-interaction Hamiltonian> by its number of zero-mode factors. Four such factors give $uN_0^2/(2V)$. Three zero-mode factors and one nonzero <momentum> violate <momentum conservation>, so no such term survives. With two zero-mode factors there are three possibilities:

* Two zero-mode <creation operators> leave the opposite-<momentum> pair $\psi_{\mathbf k}\psi_{-\mathbf k}$.
* Two zero-mode <annihilation operators> leave $\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger$.
* One zero-mode factor on each side leaves $\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf k}$. Choosing which of the two <creation operators> and which of the two <annihilation operators> are zero gives four placements.

The sums over $\mathbf k\ne0$ include both members of each opposite-<momentum> pair, consistently with the overall factor $1/2$. Terms with one zero-mode factor contain three nonzero-mode factors; their <expected values> vanish in the centered <multimode squeezed vacuum>. Indeed each $\psi$ is linear in $\varphi,\varphi^\dagger$, and an odd product has zero <vacuum expectation value>. Terms with no zero-mode factors are independent of $N_0$ at fixed $\theta_k$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle H_{\rm int}\rangle=\frac{uN_0^2}{2V}
+\frac{uN_0}{2V}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}
\left[\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}\psi_{-\mathbf k}\rangle
+\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger\rangle
+4\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf k}\rangle\right]
+O(N_0^0).}
$$
To evaluate these <vacuum expectation values>, use the inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> and its <Hermitian adjoint>:
$$
\psi_{\mathbf k}=c_k\varphi_{\mathbf k}-s_k\varphi_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger,
\qquad
\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger=c_k\varphi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger-s_k\varphi_{-\mathbf k}.
$$
Since $\varphi_{\mathbf k}|0\rangle=0$, the only nonzero two-factor <vacuum expectation value> is $\langle\varphi_{\mathbf k}\varphi_{\mathbf q}^\dagger\rangle=\delta_{\mathbf k,\mathbf q}$. The <bosonic squeezed-vacuum contractions> are consequently
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf q}\rangle
&=s_k^2\delta_{\mathbf k,\mathbf q},\\
\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}\psi_{\mathbf q}\rangle
&=-c_ks_k\delta_{\mathbf k,-\mathbf q},\\
\langle\psi_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\psi_{\mathbf q}^\dagger\rangle
&=-c_ks_k\delta_{\mathbf k,-\mathbf q}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
In particular the three requested quantities are $-\sinh\theta_k\cosh\theta_k$, $-\sinh\theta_k\cosh\theta_k$ and $\sinh^2\theta_k$, respectively. The minus sign in the pair <vacuum expectation values> comes from the inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> and is essential to lowering the <interaction energy>. Substitution yields
$$
\boxed{\langle H_{\rm int}\rangle
=\frac{uN_0^2}{2V}+\frac{uN_0}{V}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}(2s_k^2-c_ks_k)+O(N_0^0).}
$$

= Solution

The <coherent state> in the zero mode has mean <occupation number> $N_0$. The other <occupation numbers> follow from part (c), giving exactly
$$
\boxed{\langle\hat N\rangle=N_0+D,\qquad
D\equiv\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\sinh^2\theta_k.}
$$
Impose the fixed mean <particle number> $\langle\hat N\rangle=N$ by choosing
$$
\boxed{N_0=N-D.}
$$
Here $D$ is the <quantum depletion of a Bose condensate>. The approximation requires $0\le D\ll N$, so $N_0$ remains positive and macroscopically large. The specified <coherent state> and <multimode squeezed vacuum> are not exact <eigenstates> of the total <number operator>; $N$ in this variational construction is their prescribed mean <particle number>.

Write $P=\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}s_kc_k$. Substituting into the retained part of the <interaction energy> before truncating gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle H_{\rm int}\rangle
&\simeq\frac{u}{2V}(N-D)^2+\frac{u}{V}(N-D)(2D-P)\\
&=\frac{uN^2}{2V}+\frac{uN}{V}(D-P)
+\frac{u}{V}\left(-\frac32D^2+DP\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The last two terms quadratic in $D,P$ have no explicit large-$N$ factor when the <Bogoliubov transformation> parameters are held fixed, and are discarded along with the nonzero-mode quartic contribution already omitted in part (c). This is the same truncation that underlies the <number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian>. Thus, defining the physical <number density> correctly as $\rho=N/V=N/L^3$,
$$
\boxed{\langle H_{\rm int}\rangle\simeq\frac{u\rho}{2}N
+u\rho\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}
(\sinh^2\theta_k-\sinh\theta_k\cosh\theta_k).}
$$
Replacing $N_0$ by $N$ at the outset would give the wrong coefficient $2\sinh^2\theta_k$: expanding the leading condensate <interaction energy> supplies the necessary subtraction.

**The printed definition $\rho=N/L$ is an error.** In three dimensions the <contact interaction> coupling $u$ has units of <energy> times <volume>, so $uN/L$ does not even have units of <energy>. There is a direct counterexample with no depletion: when $\theta_k=0$, the specified <coherent state> has exactly $\langle H_{\rm int}\rangle=uN^2/(2L^3)$. In fixed length and <energy> units, $L=2$, $u=1$ and $N=100$ give $625$, whereas the printed $u\rho N/2$ with $\rho=N/L$ gives $2500$. The requested formula and the minimization in part (e) hold with $\rho=N/L^3$.

= Solution
{parent=e}

Put $\epsilon_k=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)>0$ for $\mathbf k\ne0$ and $g=u\rho$, using $\rho=N/L^3$. Since the zero mode has zero <kinetic energy>, the exact <kinetic energy> in this trial state is
$$
\boxed{\langle H_{\rm KE}\rangle
=\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\epsilon_k\sinh^2\theta_k.}
$$
Together with part (d), the <fixed-mean-number Bogoliubov variational energy> is
$$
\mathcal E[\theta]\simeq\frac{gN}{2}
+\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}f_k(\theta_k),\qquad
f_k(\theta)=(\epsilon_k+g)\sinh^2\theta-g\sinh\theta\cosh\theta.
$$
For repulsive <contact interaction>, $u>0$, each $\epsilon_k+g>g>0$. Using the double-angle <hyperbolic-function identities>,
$$
f_k(\theta)=\frac{\epsilon_k+g}{2}(\cosh2\theta-1)
-\frac g2\sinh2\theta,
$$
so
$$
f_k'(\theta)=(\epsilon_k+g)\sinh2\theta-g\cosh2\theta.
$$
The <stationary point> therefore satisfies
$$
\boxed{\tanh2\theta_k=\frac{g}{\epsilon_k+g}
=\frac{u\rho}{u\rho+\hbar^2k^2/(2m)}.}
$$
This is a minimum, not just a vanishing <derivative>. Define the positive <Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion>
$$
E_k=\sqrt{(\epsilon_k+g)^2-g^2}
=\sqrt{\epsilon_k(\epsilon_k+2g)}.
$$
At the displayed solution $\theta_k^*$, $\cosh2\theta_k^*=(\epsilon_k+g)/E_k$ and $\sinh2\theta_k^*=g/E_k$. Hence
$$
f_k(\theta)=\frac{E_k}{2}\cosh[2(\theta-\theta_k^*)]
-\frac{\epsilon_k+g}{2},\qquad
f_k''(\theta_k^*)=2E_k>0.
$$
The <hyperbolic cosine> has its unique minimum at zero, proving global minimization of each retained term. Treating equal-magnitude <wavevectors> together simply multiplies the same function by their multiplicity. At a fixed <ultraviolet cutoff>, the resulting <energy> change from $\theta=0$ is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E_{\min}-\mathcal E[0]
=\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}(E_k-\epsilon_k-g)<0\quad(g>0).}
$$
Indeed $E_k<\epsilon_k+g$. More directly, for small positive $\theta$,
$$
f_k(\theta)=-g\theta+(\epsilon_k+g)\theta^2+O(\theta^3).
$$
The pair <vacuum expectation value> lowers the <interaction energy> at first order in $\theta$, whereas the nonzero <occupation number> and its <kinetic energy> cost start at second order. The <two-mode squeezed vacuum> admixes opposite-<momentum> pairs into the condensate, introducing spatial correlations that reduce the chance of two <bosons> coinciding. These correlations lower repulsive <contact interaction> enough to outweigh the added <kinetic energy>. A small <quantum depletion of a Bose condensate> can therefore lower the <energy> below the uncorrelated condensate value.

Two qualifications delimit this result. First, the paper does not state the sign of $u$. More generally a finite stable minimum exists only when $\epsilon_k+g>|g|$ for every retained nonzero mode; repulsion ensures this. For example, with $g=-\epsilon_k$ in some mode, $f_k(\theta)=\epsilon_k\sinh(2\theta)/2$ tends to $-\infty$ as $\theta\to-\infty$, so an unconditional minimum claim for arbitrary $u$ is false. Also the formula minimizes the truncated <Bogoliubov theory> <energy>, not the exact quartic <Hamiltonian> including its discarded terms, and it must remain in the regime of small <quantum depletion of a Bose condensate>.

Second, a three-dimensional unregulated <contact interaction> cannot give a finite absolute beyond-leading-order <ground-state energy> by this bare sum. For large $k$, $E_k-\epsilon_k-g=-g^2/(2\epsilon_k)+O(\epsilon_k^{-2})$, whose three-dimensional sum has an <ultraviolet divergence>. A finite <ultraviolet cutoff>, or <renormalization of a three-dimensional dilute-gas contact coupling> matched to a physical <scattering length>, is needed. This does not alter the modewise minimization or the finite-cutoff lowering mechanism just derived.
