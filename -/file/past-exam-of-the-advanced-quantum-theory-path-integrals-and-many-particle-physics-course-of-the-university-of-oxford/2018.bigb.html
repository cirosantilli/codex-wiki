We use $\hbar=1$, consistent with the source's momentum quantization, and take the pairing parameter $\Delta$ real so that the <Hamiltonian> is <Hermitian>.

== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

With no pairing, the <Hamiltonian> is diagonal in the <fermionic occupation numbers>. The <Pauli exclusion principle> allows each momentum mode to be either empty or occupied once. Without a fixed <fermion number> constraint, occupying a mode lowers the <energy> precisely when its single-particle <energy> is negative. Therefore, with $|0\rangle$ the <Fock vacuum>, a <ground state> is
$$
\boxed{|\mathrm{GS}\rangle=\prod_{p:\,\epsilon(p)<0}c_p^\dagger|0\rangle,\qquad E_{\mathrm{GS}}=\sum_{p:\,\epsilon(p)<0}\epsilon(p).}
$$
Choose a fixed ordering of the <fermionic creation operators>; changing it alters only the overall <phase>. Any zero-energy modes may be occupied or empty without changing the <ground-state energy>.

To identify the occupied modes, complete the square:
$$
\frac{\epsilon(p)}\alpha=\left(p^2-\frac\beta2\right)^2+\mu-\frac{\beta^2}{4}.
$$
Here $\beta$ is the dispersion parameter, not <inverse temperature>. If $\beta^2<4\mu$, every mode has positive <energy> and the unique <ground state> is the <Fock vacuum>, with $E_{\mathrm{GS}}=0$. If $\beta^2=4\mu$, there are still no negative-energy modes, but $p=\pm\sqrt{\beta/2}$ have zero <energy> if they lie on the allowed momentum grid. Each such mode doubles the <ground-state degeneracy>.

For $\beta^2>4\mu$, define
$$
p_-^2=\frac{\beta-\sqrt{\beta^2-4\mu}}2,\qquad
p_+^2=\frac{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^2-4\mu}}2.
$$
Both are positive, and the occupied <Fermi sea> consists of the two intervals $p_-<|p|<p_+$. With $p=2\pi n/L$, the requested finite-size <ground-state energy> is explicitly
$$
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{GS}}=2\alpha\!\sum_{\substack{n\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\\ Lp_-/(2\pi)<n<Lp_+/(2\pi)}}\left[\left(\frac{2\pi n}L\right)^4-\beta\left(\frac{2\pi n}L\right)^2+\mu\right].}
$$
The factor two counts opposite momenta. Endpoints are excluded from this <sum> because they have zero <energy>; when an endpoint is allowed, its optional occupation adds <ground-state degeneracy>. A narrow negative-energy interval can contain no allowed finite-size momenta, in which case the <sum> is empty. The zero-momentum mode is always empty since $\epsilon(0)=\alpha\mu>0$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For real angles write $u_p=\cos\theta_p$ and $v_p=\sin\theta_p$. For one independent pair $p>0$, the transformation and its conjugate read
$$
c_p=u_p\alpha_p+i v_p\alpha_{-p}^\dagger,\qquad
c_{-p}=u_p\alpha_{-p}-i v_p\alpha_p^\dagger.
$$
Using the <canonical anticommutation relations> for the $\alpha$ modes gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{c_p,c_p^\dagger\}&=u_p^2+v_p^2=1,\\
\{c_{-p},c_{-p}^\dagger\}&=u_p^2+v_p^2=1,\\
\{c_p,c_{-p}\}&=-i u_pv_p+i v_pu_p=0,\\
\{c_p,c_{-p}^\dagger\}&=0.
\end{aligned}
$$
The remaining <anticommutators> vanish for independent momentum pairs. The displayed two-by-two <matrix> is <unitary> and its inverse is obtained by changing $i$ to $-i$.

When the same formula is indexed over both signs of momentum, its first row at $-p$ must agree with the conjugate of its second row at $p$. Thus a consistent global convention requires
$$
\boxed{u_{-p}=u_p,\qquad v_{-p}=-v_p,\qquad \theta_{-p}=-\theta_p\pmod{2\pi}.}
$$
This is the <odd-momentum fermionic Bogoliubov angle> convention. If one checks only the first-row formulas, their cross <anticommutator> is $i\sin(\theta_p+\theta_{-p})$; its vanishing gives the condition modulo $\pi$, with possible signs absorbable in the $\alpha$ modes. Requiring both rows to describe the same operators fixes the convention above. Merely checking a separate <unitary matrix> at each momentum misses this redundancy: choosing $\theta_p=\theta_{-p}=\pi/4$ in both first rows would give $\{c_p,c_{-p}\}=i$.

At $p=0$, the <annihilation operator> and its conjugate belong to one mode. The full displayed formula is consistent only with $v_0=0$; choose $\theta_0=0$ and $\alpha_0=c_0$. This mode is not a two-mode pair.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Group the nonzero modes into independent pairs $p>0$. For one pair set $\varepsilon=\epsilon(p)=p^2/(2m)+\mu$, and introduce the <Nambu spinor> $\Psi_p=(c_p,c_{-p}^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}$. <Normal ordering> using $c_{-p}c_{-p}^\dagger=1-c_{-p}^\dagger c_{-p}$ gives
$$
H_p=\Psi_p^\dagger\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon&i\Delta\\-i\Delta&-\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}\Psi_p+\varepsilon.
$$
This exactly contains both kinetic terms and the pairing terms once; there is no additional factor of one half because only $p>0$ is summed.

Insert $\Psi_p=U_p\mathcal A_p$, where $\mathcal A_p=(\alpha_p,\alpha_{-p}^\dagger)^{\mathsf T}$ and $U_p$ is the <Bogoliubov transformation> <matrix>. Direct <matrix multiplication> yields
$$
U_p^\dagger h_pU_p=\begin{pmatrix}
\varepsilon\cos2\theta_p-\Delta\sin2\theta_p&i(\varepsilon\sin2\theta_p+\Delta\cos2\theta_p)\\
-i(\varepsilon\sin2\theta_p+\Delta\cos2\theta_p)&-\varepsilon\cos2\theta_p+\Delta\sin2\theta_p
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Choose the diagonalizing branch
$$
\boxed{\cos2\theta_p=\frac{\varepsilon}{E_p},\qquad \sin2\theta_p=-\frac\Delta{E_p},\qquad E_p=\sqrt{\varepsilon^2+\Delta^2},\quad p>0.}
$$
Then the off-diagonal terms vanish and the upper diagonal entry is $E_p>0$. Extend the angle to negative momenta as in part b; the sine relation just written applies to the positive representative of each pair. The <BCS coherence factors> can be chosen as
$$
u_p=\sqrt{\frac{E_p+\varepsilon}{2E_p}},\qquad
v_p=-\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{2E_p(E_p+\varepsilon)}}.
$$
Here $u_p$ denotes $\cos\theta_p$, and $v_p$ denotes $\sin\theta_p$; these expressions are nonsingular because $\mu>0$ and $m>0$. The minus sign of $v_p$ for positive $\Delta$ is fixed by the pairing <phase> in this <Hamiltonian>.

A second use of the <canonical anticommutation relations> gives
$$
H_p=E_p\left(\alpha_p^\dagger\alpha_p-\alpha_{-p}\alpha_{-p}^\dagger\right)+\varepsilon
=E_p\left(\alpha_p^\dagger\alpha_p+\alpha_{-p}^\dagger\alpha_{-p}\right)+\varepsilon-E_p.
$$
The zero-momentum term was never paired and is simply $\mu\alpha_0^\dagger\alpha_0$. Hence
$$
\boxed{H=\sum_pE(p)\alpha_p^\dagger\alpha_p+C,\qquad
C=\sum_{p>0}\bigl[\epsilon(p)-\sqrt{\epsilon(p)^2+\Delta^2}\bigr],}
$$
with
$$
\boxed{E(0)=\mu,\qquad E(p)=\sqrt{\epsilon(p)^2+\Delta^2}\quad(p\ne0).}
$$
The constant is the <ground-state energy>. At fixed $L$, its ultraviolet tail converges: $\epsilon(p)-E(p)=-\Delta^2/[\epsilon(p)+E(p)]\sim-m\Delta^2/p^2$ in one dimension. Replacing $E(0)$ by $\sqrt{\mu^2+\Delta^2}$ would incorrectly pair a spinless mode with itself.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Since every <quasiparticle> <energy> is positive, the <ground state> is the vacuum of all the $\alpha_p$ <annihilation operators>, including $\alpha_0=c_0$. Its zero-momentum mode is therefore empty. For each positive momentum, the inverse <Bogoliubov transformation> gives
$$
\alpha_p=u_pc_p-i v_pc_{-p}^\dagger,\qquad
\alpha_{-p}=u_pc_{-p}+i v_pc_p^\dagger.
$$
Both annihilate the normalized pair state $(u_p+i v_pc_p^\dagger c_{-p}^\dagger)|0\rangle$. For example, applying $\alpha_p$ produces the two cancelling terms $i u_pv_pc_{-p}^\dagger|0\rangle$. Thus
$$
\boxed{|\Omega\rangle=\prod_{p>0}\left(u_p+i v_pc_p^\dagger c_{-p}^\dagger\right)|0\rangle,\qquad c_0|\Omega\rangle=0.}
$$
The factors have even <fermion parity> and commute for disjoint momentum pairs. Each normalized factor is a <superposition> of zero and two <fermions>, whose pair-occupation <probability> is $v_p^2$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\langle\Omega|\widehat N|\Omega\rangle=2\sum_{p>0}v_p^2=\sum_{p>0}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon(p)}{E_p}\right).}
$$
There is no zero-mode contribution. For fixed $L$, the <sum> converges since $v_p^2=O(p^{-4})$.

A normalized simple excited state is $|k\rangle=\alpha_k^\dagger|\Omega\rangle$ for $k>0$. Its <excitation energy> is $E_k$, and its total <momentum> is $k$: both terms in $\alpha_k^\dagger=u_kc_k^\dagger+i v_kc_{-k}$ add momentum $k$. Acting on the $k,-k$ pair gives
$$
\alpha_k^\dagger\left(u_k+i v_kc_k^\dagger c_{-k}^\dagger\right)|0\rangle
=(u_k^2+v_k^2)c_k^\dagger|0\rangle=c_k^\dagger|0\rangle.
$$
The affected pair now contains exactly one <fermion> instead of mean number $2v_k^2$; all other pairs are unchanged. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\langle k|\widehat N|k\rangle=\langle\widehat N\rangle_\Omega+1-2v_k^2
=\langle\widehat N\rangle_\Omega+\frac{\epsilon(k)}{E_k}.}
$$
This is the <fermion number of a Bogoliubov excitation>. Its change in mean <fermion number> lies between zero and one for $\mu>0$, with the value one at $\Delta=0$. The <Bogoliubov quasiparticle> coherently mixes adding a <fermion> and removing the opposite-momentum <fermion>; it is not an eigen-excitation of the original <fermion number operator>. Indeed,
$$
[\widehat N,H]=2i\Delta\sum_{p>0}\left(c_p^\dagger c_{-p}^\dagger+c_{-p}c_p\right),
$$
so <fermion number> is not conserved when $\Delta\ne0$, while <fermion parity> is conserved. The generally noninteger change is an <expectation value> in a coherent paired background, rather than a fractional eigenvalue of the <fermion number operator>. In the weak-pairing limit at fixed $k$, it tends to one, as expected for an ordinary added <fermion>.

== 2
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $\beta=1/(k_BT)$ and use the two spin values $+1,-1$ as row and column labels. Define the <spin-chain transfer matrix>
$$
W_{st}=e^{-\beta E_0(s,t)},\qquad s,t\in\{+1,-1\}.
$$
It is a real <symmetric matrix> because $E_0(s,t)=E_0(t,s)$; every entry is strictly positive for finite bond <energies> at $T>0$. The <Boltzmann factor> of a spin configuration factorizes into the product $W_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}\cdots W_{\sigma_L\sigma_1}$. Summing the intermediate spin labels is exactly <matrix multiplication>, and the final closure imposes <periodic boundary conditions>. Thus
$$
\boxed{Z_L=\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L}\prod_{j=1}^LW_{\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}}=\operatorname{Tr}W^L.}
$$
Writing $W=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, its <eigenvalues> are
$$
\lambda_\pm=\frac{a+d\pm\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4b^2}}2,
\qquad \boxed{Z_L=\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L.}
$$
A positive-entry <matrix> need not have two positive <eigenvalues>; $\lambda_-$ can be negative. The <Perron–Frobenius theorem> ensures $\lambda_+>|\lambda_-|$ here. Therefore the <Helmholtz free energy> is $F_L=-k_BT\ln Z_L$, and its value per spin in the <thermodynamic limit> is $-k_BT\ln\lambda_+$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For an open chain there are $L-1$ bonds, with both endpoint <Ising spins> independently summed. Retain the same <spin-chain transfer matrix>, but replace the trace by endpoint vectors. With $\mathbf1=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$,
$$
\boxed{Z_L^{\mathrm{open}}=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}W^{L-1}\mathbf1.}
$$
This follows directly by expanding the product of $L-1$ <Boltzmann factors>. If $\mathbf v_\pm$ are orthonormal <eigenvectors> of the <symmetric matrix> $W$, the equivalent <spectral decomposition> is
$$
Z_L^{\mathrm{open}}=\sum_{\nu=\pm}(\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v_\nu)^2\lambda_\nu^{L-1}.
$$
Fixed endpoint <Ising spins> replace the corresponding $\mathbf1$ by a basis vector; extra endpoint <energies> replace its entries by the corresponding <Boltzmann factors>. The leading bulk <Helmholtz free energy> per spin agrees with the periodic result, because the boundary changes only subextensive terms. In particular, $L=1$ gives $Z_1^{\mathrm{open}}=\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=2$, as required for one free <Ising spin>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $M=L/2$, so that the total number of <Ising spins> is $3M$. Write $K=\beta J$ and group each triangle's two base spins and its apex into one cell. The <Ising chain with alternating triangular decorations> has a triangle bond followed by an undecorated connecting bond. Summing an apex <Ising spin> first gives a decorated-bond <Boltzmann weight>
$$
A_{st}=\sum_{\tau=\pm1}e^{-K[st+(s+t)\tau]}=2e^{-Kst}\cosh[K(s+t)].
$$
The undecorated bridge has <Boltzmann weight> $B_{ts'}=e^{-Kts'}$. In the basis $+1,-1$,
$$
A=\begin{pmatrix}e^K+e^{-3K}&2e^K\\2e^K&e^K+e^{-3K}\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}e^{-K}&e^K\\e^K&e^{-K}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Each cell contributes $T=AB$, so <periodic boundary conditions> give
$$
\boxed{Z_L=\operatorname{Tr}(AB)^M.}
$$
The two <matrices> have common normalized <eigenvectors> $(1,1)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2$ and $(1,-1)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2$, expressing <spin inversion symmetry>. Their respective <eigenvalues> are
$$
a_+=3e^K+e^{-3K},\quad a_-=e^{-3K}-e^K,\qquad
b_+=e^{-K}+e^K,\quad b_-=e^{-K}-e^K.
$$
Consequently the cell <transfer matrix> has <eigenvalues>
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_+&=(3e^K+e^{-3K})(e^{-K}+e^K)=3+e^{-4K}+e^{-2K}+3e^{2K},\\
\lambda_-&=(e^{-3K}-e^K)(e^{-K}-e^K)=e^{-4K}-e^{-2K}-1+e^{2K},
\end{aligned}
$$
and the finite-size <partition function> is
$$
\boxed{Z_L=\lambda_+^{L/2}+\lambda_-^{L/2}.}
$$
For $K>0$, $\lambda_-\geq0$ and $\lambda_+-\lambda_-=4+2e^{-2K}+2e^{2K}>0$, so only $\lambda_+$ survives in the <thermodynamic limit>. Divide the <Helmholtz free energy> by $3M$, the number of sites rather than the number of cells or base spins:
$$
\boxed{f(T)=-\frac{k_BT}{3}\ln\lambda_+
=-\frac{k_BT}{3}\ln\!\left(3+e^{-4\beta J}+e^{-2\beta J}+3e^{2\beta J}\right).}
$$
As a counting check, $K=0$ gives $\lambda_+=8$, $\lambda_-=0$, hence $Z_L=8^M=2^{3M}$, exactly the number of independent <Ising spin> configurations. This model has separated decorated bonds and should not be confused with the corner-sharing <sawtooth Ising chain>, which has a different site count.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For low <temperature>, factor out the largest <Boltzmann factor> from the dominant <eigenvalue>:
$$
\lambda_+=3e^{2K}\left(1+e^{-2K}+\frac13e^{-4K}+\frac13e^{-6K}\right).
$$
Thus the <Helmholtz free energy> per site has the expansion
$$
f=-\frac{2J}{3}-\frac{k_BT}{3}\ln3-\frac{k_BT}{3}e^{-2K}+O\!\left(k_BT e^{-4K}\right).
$$
Differentiate at fixed $J$, remembering that $K=J/(k_BT)$. The <entropy> per site is
$$
s=\frac{k_B}{3}\ln3+\frac{k_B}{3}(1+2K)e^{-2K}+O\!\left(k_B(1+K)e^{-4K}\right),
\qquad \boxed{\lim_{T\to0^+}s(T)=\frac{k_B}{3}\ln3.}
$$
The nonzero <residual entropy> comes from an exponentially large <ground-state degeneracy>, generated by <geometrical frustration>. A triangle with antiferromagnetic <energy> $J[st+(s+t)\tau]$ has minimum <energy> $-J$: if $s=t$, the apex must have the opposite sign; if $s=-t$, either apex sign is permitted. The two all-equal configurations instead have <energy> $3J$. Thus exactly one of a minimum-energy triangle's bonds is frustrated. The bridge can simultaneously minimize its <energy> at $-J$ by requiring $t=-s'$, where $s'$ is the next cell's first base spin.

For fixed $s$ and $s'$, the number of allowed apex choices is therefore two when $s=s'$ and one when $s=-s'$. The zero-temperature counting <transfer matrix> on successive first base spins is
$$
G=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\boxed{g_M=\operatorname{Tr}G^M=3^M+1,\qquad E_{\mathrm{GS}}=-2JM=-JL.}
$$
The <eigenvalues> of $G$ are $3$ and $1$. Dividing $k_B\ln g_M$ by the $3M$ sites and taking the <thermodynamic limit> gives $k_B\ln3/3$, independently confirming the derivative calculation. This does not assert a unique ordered <ground state>: the extensive choice of frustrated bonds is precisely what leaves <entropy> at zero <temperature>. At high <temperature>, all <Ising spins> become independent and $s\to k_B\ln2$ per site.
