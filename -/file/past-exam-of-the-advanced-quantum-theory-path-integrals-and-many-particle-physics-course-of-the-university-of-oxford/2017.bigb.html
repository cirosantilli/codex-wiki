== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Put $\beta=(k_BT)^{-1}$ and label the two <Spin>[spin values] in the order $+1,-1$. Assign each bond its <Boltzmann weight>,
$$
V_{st}=e^{-\beta E_0(s,t)},\qquad
V=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad s,t\in\{+1,-1\}.
$$
The <symmetry> of the <Energy>[bond energy] makes $V$ a <Symmetric matrix>[real symmetric matrix]. For a fixed configuration the product of successive <matrix> entries is its <Boltzmann weight>. Summing over all <spins> and identifying the last index with the first therefore gives
$$
\boxed{Z_L=\sum_{\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_L}V_{\sigma_1\sigma_2}\cdots V_{\sigma_L\sigma_1}=\operatorname{tr}V^L=\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L,}
$$
where
$$
\lambda_\pm=\frac{a+d\pm\sqrt{(a-d)^2+4b^2}}2.
$$
This replaces a sum over $2^L$ configurations by the powers of a two-by-two <matrix>. For finite bond <energies> at $T>0$, every <matrix> entry is positive, so the <Perron–Frobenius theorem> gives $\lambda_+>0$ and $|\lambda_-|<\lambda_+$. Hence the <Helmholtz free energy> per <spin> tends to $-k_BT\log\lambda_+$. A diagonal local <observable> $O$ can be inserted at one site: $\langle O_1\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(OV^L)/Z_L$. Its limiting average is the <expectation> in the <Normalization>[normalized] <eigenvector> of $\lambda_+$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

**The sum needs a stated interpretation.** The original equation leaves the second bond outside explicit parentheses, with its index then formally free. The natural <Translation invariance>[translation-invariant], equal-bond interpretation used here is the <energy> $-J\sum_j\tau_j(\sigma_j+\sigma_{j+1})$. The subsequently mentioned $h$ has no term in that <energy>, so no <magnetic field> or <single-ion anisotropy> is added. Such an extra term would define another model and change the answer.

Let $K=\beta J$. Sum over each decorating <spin> before summing over the remaining <spins>. The <decimation of an alternating mixed-spin chain> gives the effective <transfer matrix>:
$$
V_{st}=\sum_{t_0=-1}^{1}e^{Kt_0(s+t)}=1+2\cosh[K(s+t)]
=\begin{pmatrix}1+2\cosh2K&3\\3&1+2\cosh2K\end{pmatrix}_{st}.
$$
No on-site entropy has been omitted: the term $1$ is the weight of the zero decorating <spin>. The <Normalization>[normalized] <eigenvectors> are $v_\pm=(1,\pm1)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt2$, with <eigenvalues>
$$
\boxed{\lambda_+=4+2\cosh2K=2+4\cosh^2K,\qquad \lambda_-=2\cosh2K-2=4\sinh^2K.}
$$
There are $L$ transfer steps but $2L$ physical sites, so
$$
\boxed{Z_L=\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L,\qquad
f=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{-k_BT\log Z_L}{2L}=-\frac{k_BT}{2}\log(4+2\cosh2\beta J).}
$$
The <free energy> per two-site <unit cell> is twice this value. At $J=0$, the formula gives $Z_L=6^L$, as required by two states on one sublattice and three on the other. As $T\to0$, the <free energy> per physical site tends to $-J$, the <energy> of an aligned chain with two satisfied bonds per <unit cell>. These checks also fix the factor of two in the site count.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Insert $D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ at the first undecorated site. In the <eigenbasis> of the <transfer matrix>, $D$ exchanges $v_+$ and $v_-$, and both diagonal <matrix elements> vanish. Explicitly,
$$
\boxed{\langle\sigma_1\rangle_L=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(DV^L)}{\operatorname{tr}V^L}
=\frac{\lambda_+^L v_+^{\mathsf T}Dv_++\lambda_-^L v_-^{\mathsf T}Dv_-}{\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L}=0.}
$$
Thus its large-$L$ limit is zero at every fixed $T>0$. The result is exact even before taking that limit. Equivalently, simultaneous reversal of all <spins> pairs configurations of equal <energy> and opposite $\sigma_1$. Positivity of the <transfer matrix> at fixed <Temperature>[positive temperature] prevents a degenerate dominant <eigenvalue> and <spontaneous magnetization> in this one-dimensional zero-field chain. Selecting one aligned state at strictly <Absolute zero>[zero temperature] is a different order of limits.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Insert the decorating-spin <observable> into the one bond that contains it. The <decorated-spin occupation insertion> uses the <matrix>:
$$
Q_{st}=\sum_{t_0=-1}^{1}t_0^2e^{Kt_0(s+t)}=2\cosh[K(s+t)]
=\begin{pmatrix}2\cosh2K&2\\2&2\cosh2K\end{pmatrix}_{st}.
$$
The <partition function> still contains $L$ decorating-spin sums; one of them has been replaced by this weighted sum. Hence
$$
\langle\tau_1^2\rangle_L=\frac{\operatorname{tr}(QV^{L-1})}{Z_L}
=\frac{q_+\lambda_+^{L-1}+q_-\lambda_-^{L-1}}{\lambda_+^L+\lambda_-^L},
\qquad q_+=2\cosh2K+2,\quad q_-=2\cosh2K-2.
$$
Taking the dominant <eigenvector> gives
$$
\boxed{\lim_{L\to\infty}\langle\tau_1^2\rangle_L=\frac{q_+}{\lambda_+}
=\frac{\cosh2K+1}{\cosh2K+2}=\frac{2\cosh^2K}{1+2\cosh^2K}.}
$$
This is the <probability> that the decorating site is in either nonzero state. It tends to $2/3$ at high <temperature>, when its three states are equally likely, and to one at low <temperature>, when the nonzero aligned states gain <Energy>[bond energy]. Unlike the <magnetization>, this even <observable> is not forced to vanish by <spin> reversal.

== 2
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Interpret the two displayed exchange components as belonging to the common nearest-neighbour sum with coefficient $-J$. Write $H_f=|h|$ and, for $h\ne0$, $s=\operatorname{sgn}h$. The source explicitly fixes $J>0$ but does not separately constrain $h$ in this question; keeping $H_f$ covers either <Magnetic field>[field] direction.

For <Vector>[classical vectors] of length $S$, define $\mathbf q_j=(S_j^x,S_j^y)$. The <Cauchy–Schwarz inequality> gives $\mathbf q_j\cdot\mathbf q_{j+1}\le|\mathbf q_j||\mathbf q_{j+1}|\le S^2$, and $hS_j^x\le H_fS$. Each term is therefore bounded below, so
$$
E_{\mathrm{cl}}\ge-LJS^2-LH_fS^2.
$$
Uniform alignment along the <Magnetic field>[field] saturates every bound:
$$
\boxed{\mathbf S_j=(sS,0,0),\qquad E_{\mathrm{cl,min}}=-L(J+H_f)S^2\quad(h\ne0).}
$$
Equality requires all planar <vectors> to be parallel with length $S$, and the nonzero <Magnetic field>[field] then fixes their direction. At $h=0$, every uniformly oriented <vector> in the $xy$ plane is a <Ground state>[classical ground state]:
$$
\boxed{\mathbf S_j=S(\cos\phi,\sin\phi,0),\qquad E_{\mathrm{cl,min}}=-LJS^2.}
$$
A nonuniform configuration cannot improve these simultaneously saturated bond bounds, including on the periodic ring.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Holstein–Primakoff transformation> realizes a <Spin commutation relations>[spin algebra] using <bosonic creation operators> and <bosonic annihilation operators>. A <boson> counts a unit of <spin> deviation from the local positive quantization axis: $\widetilde S^z=S-n$ with $n=a^\dagger a$. The square root in $\widetilde S^+$ supplies the correct <Spin ladder operator>[spin-ladder] <matrix elements>. In particular,
$$
\widetilde S^+|n\rangle=\sqrt{n(2S-n+1)}|n-1\rangle,\qquad
\widetilde S^-|n\rangle=\sqrt{(n+1)(2S-n)}|n+1\rangle.
$$
Their <commutator> is $2(S-n)$ on every physical <Fock state>[number state], reproducing the <Spin commutation relations>. Although the <Bosonic Fock space>[oscillator Fock space] is infinite, the <Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint>[physical subspace] is only
$$
\boxed{n=0,1,\ldots,2S.}
$$
The order of the square root and oscillator <Linear operator>[operator] matters in obtaining these coefficients.

When the <Magnon>[spin deviations] are dilute, expand the square root in $n/(2S)$. The leading transverse <Linear operators>[operators] are linear in the <bosonic creation operators> and <bosonic annihilation operators> and turn <exchange interactions> into a <Quadratic bosonic Hamiltonian>[quadratic Hamiltonian], which can be diagonalized. Higher powers describe interactions between <spin waves>. The method therefore organizes corrections by inverse powers of $S$ about an ordered classical configuration. Large $S$ alone is insufficient: the average <One-dimensional planar spin-wave depletion>[depletion] must remain small compared with $S$. Possible problems include the <Bosonic occupation number>[occupation] constraint after truncation, noncollinear order requiring site-dependent axis <rotations>, <zero modes>, and <Infrared divergence>[infrared fluctuations] that destroy the assumed dilute expansion.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Choose the local positive $\widetilde z$ axis along the <classical spin>. For nonzero <Magnetic field>[field], a <Rotation>[proper rotation] is
$$
\boxed{S_j^x=s\widetilde S_j^z,\qquad S_j^y=\widetilde S_j^y,\qquad S_j^z=-s\widetilde S_j^x.}
$$
It preserves the <Spin commutation relations> and turns the <Magnetic field>[field] term into $-H_fS\sum_j\widetilde S_j^z$. At zero <Magnetic field>[field] choose the classical direction $+x$, so $s=1$. Expanding about the original $z$ axis without this <rotation> would put the reference state away from the classical minimum and would not give a controlled small-deviation expansion.

To the required order,
$$
\widetilde S_j^z=S-n_j,\qquad
\widetilde S_j^x=\sqrt{\frac S2}(a_j+a_j^\dagger)+O(S^{-1/2}),\qquad
\widetilde S_j^y=-i\sqrt{\frac S2}(a_j-a_j^\dagger)+O(S^{-1/2}).
$$
The longitudinal exchange gives $-JS^2+JS(n_j+n_{j+1})+O(S^0)$ on each bond. The transverse exchange gives
$$
-J\widetilde S_j^y\widetilde S_{j+1}^y
=\frac{JS}{2}\left[a_ja_{j+1}+a_j^\dagger a_{j+1}^\dagger-a_j^\dagger a_{j+1}-a_{j+1}^\dagger a_j\right]+O(S^0).
$$
Finally the <Magnetic field>[field] contributes its classical constant and $H_fSn_j$. Each site belongs to two bonds, so after removing the order-$S^2$ constant,
$$
\boxed{A=-\frac{JS}{2},\qquad B=\frac{JS}{2},\qquad C=S(2J+H_f).}
$$
These are the order-$S$ terms; the discarded products of <number operators> and the next square-root terms are order $S^0$ for <Bosonic occupation numbers>[occupations] held fixed as $S\to\infty$. A different transverse-axis phase can reverse the sign of $B$ without changing the <Energy eigenvalue>[spectrum], but the sign above follows from the stated <rotation> and oscillator convention.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the <Discrete Fourier transform>[unitary discrete Fourier transform]
$$
a_j=\frac1{\sqrt L}\sum_ke^{ikj}a_k,\qquad k=\frac{2\pi n}{L},\quad n=0,\ldots,L-1.
$$
The transformed <Linear operators>[operators] obey $[a_k,a_q^\dagger]=\delta_{kq}$. The hopping terms contribute $2A\cos k$ to the number coefficient. In the pairing sum, the odd sine part cancels between $k$ and $-k$. Thus
$$
H_{\mathrm{LSW}}=\sum_k\left[\alpha_k a_k^\dagger a_k+\frac{\Delta_k}{2}(a_ka_{-k}+a_k^\dagger a_{-k}^\dagger)\right],
$$
where
$$
\alpha_k=S(H_f+2J-J\cos k),\qquad \Delta_k=JS\cos k.
$$
The factor one half in the full <Sum>[momentum sum] avoids counting an <Bosonic Bogoliubov diagonalization>[anomalous pair] twice. For $H_f>0$, $\alpha_k>|\Delta_k|$ for every allowed <momentum>. Set
$$
a_k=\cosh\theta_k\,b_k-\sinh\theta_k\,b_{-k}^\dagger,\qquad
\tanh2\theta_k=\frac{\Delta_k}{\alpha_k}.
$$
Real even <angles> ensure consistency between opposite <momenta>, and $\cosh^2\theta_k-\sinh^2\theta_k=1$ preserves the <canonical commutation relations>. The anomalous coefficient becomes $\Delta_k\cosh2\theta_k-\alpha_k\sinh2\theta_k=0$. <Normal ordering> then gives
$$
\boxed{H_{\mathrm{LSW}}=\sum_k\epsilon(k)b_k^\dagger b_k+\frac12\sum_k[\epsilon(k)-\alpha_k],}
$$
with
$$
\boxed{\epsilon(k)=\sqrt{\alpha_k^2-\Delta_k^2}=S\sqrt{(H_f+2J)[H_f+2J(1-\cos k)]}.}
$$
The <field-polarized planar ferromagnetic spin wave> has <energy gap> $\epsilon(0)=S\sqrt{H_f(H_f+2J)}$. The <momenta> run over the whole <Brillouin zone> once, including the self-inverse <momenta> $0$ and, for even $L$, $\pi$; these use <Single-mode squeeze operator>[single-mode squeezing] rather than introducing an extra independent partner.

**The zero-field case has a <zero-mode obstruction to bosonic Bogoliubov diagonalization>.** Its nonzero-momentum modes have $\epsilon(k)=2\sqrt2JS|\sin(k/2)|$, but $\alpha_0=\Delta_0=JS$, so no finite <Bogoliubov transformation>[squeezing angle] exists for the uniform mode. With $Q_0=(a_0+a_0^\dagger)/\sqrt2$, its <Hamiltonian> is $JSQ_0^2-JS/2$, not an identically zero oscillator. For example, its <matrix element> between the <Fock vacuum>[vacuum] and the two-boson <Fock state>[number state] is $JS/\sqrt2$, whereas $\epsilon(0)b_0^\dagger b_0$ would vanish. Consequently the requested oscillator diagonalization is literal for nonzero <Magnetic field>[field]; at zero <Magnetic field>[field] treat the collective orientation separately or introduce a <Magnetic field>[field] regulator before taking a limit.

=== e
{scope}

==== Solution

For nonzero <Magnetic field>[field], the <Spin wave>[spin-wave] <ground state> is the <Fock vacuum>[vacuum] of all $b_k$. The <Bogoliubov transformation>[inverse Bogoliubov transformation] gives $\langle a_k^\dagger a_q\rangle=\delta_{kq}\sinh^2\theta_k$ and vanishing one-point oscillator averages. <Translation invariance>[Translational invariance] therefore gives the <One-dimensional planar spin-wave depletion>[depletion]
$$
\boxed{\delta S=\langle n_j\rangle=\frac1L\sum_k\sinh^2\theta_k
=\frac1{2L}\sum_k\left[\frac{H_f+2J-J\cos k}{\sqrt{(H_f+2J)[H_f+2J(1-\cos k)]}}-1\right].}
$$
Rotating back to the physical axes yields, to the retained <Spin wave>[spin-wave] order,
$$
\boxed{\langle S_j^x\rangle=s(S-\delta S),\qquad \langle S_j^y\rangle=0,\qquad\langle S_j^z\rangle=0.}
$$
The zero transverse components also follow from <rotation> by $\pi$ about the physical $x$ axis in a symmetry-preserving <ground state>. The nonzero <One-dimensional planar spin-wave depletion>[depletion] describes <Quantum fluctuation>[zero-point quantum fluctuations]: the original <boson> <Fock vacuum>[vacuum] is not the <ground state> because the <Bosonic Bogoliubov diagonalization>[anomalous terms] create pairs. It is nonnegative and must satisfy $\delta S\ll S$ for the expansion to be reliable. At strong <Magnetic field>[field] it tends to zero, with $\delta S\sim J^2/(8H_f^2)$ in the infinite-chain limit.

For fixed $H_f>0$, the <Sum>[momentum sum] has a finite <thermodynamic limit> obtained by $L^{-1}\sum_k\to(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dk$. At zero <Magnetic field>[field] the uniform mode already has no oscillator <Fock vacuum>[vacuum], as explained in part d. Even after removing that mode, the nonzero modes behave as
$$
\sinh^2\theta_k=\frac{1}{2\sqrt2|k|}-\frac12+O(|k|),\qquad
\delta S\sim\frac{\log L}{2\pi\sqrt2}+O(1).
$$
Thus assigning a finite ordered moment by simply setting $h=0$ in the infinite <Sum>[momentum sum] is invalid. The divergence indicates failure of this expansion about a fixed planar orientation; it is not a negative physical <magnetization>. At finite size an exact zero-field state chosen to have definite total $S^z$ has zero planar one-point <magnetization> by the <Global spin-rotation symmetry about one axis>[continuous rotation symmetry]. Establishing its full <correlations> requires a treatment beyond the divergent ordered-state approximation.
