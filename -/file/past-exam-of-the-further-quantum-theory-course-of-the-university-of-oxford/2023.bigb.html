Solutions to all three questions of the <Further quantum theory course of the University of Oxford> Part B examination, held on 29 May 2023. Source: https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/request_for_mathematics_examinat/response/3129786/attach/3/FOI%20202507%20740%20Data.pdf#page=268[original released PDF, bundle pages 268–271; paper pages 1–4]. In question 2 the paper defines $k=\sqrt{2mE}$, a <momentum>; the corresponding <wavenumber> is $q=k/\hbar$. Keeping these distinct retains the paper's convention without setting the <reduced Planck constant> to one.

= Solution
{parent=a}

An <irreducible spin representation> is a nonzero <Hilbert space> with no proper nonzero closed <invariant subspace> under all three <angular momentum operators>. Equivalently, the associated <unitary representation> of <SU(2)> cannot be decomposed into two nonzero orthogonal invariant pieces. The irreducible possibilities are labelled by a <spin> $j\in\{0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\ldots\}$ and have <dimension> $2j+1$.

Choose an <orthonormal basis> $|j,m_s\rangle$, with $m_s=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$. The <angular momentum commutation relations> imply $[\mathbf S^2,S_i]=0$, $[S_3,S_\pm]=\pm\hbar S_\pm$, $[S_+,S_-]=2\hbar S_3$, and $S_+^\dagger=S_-$. The complete action is
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\mathbf S^2|j,m_s\rangle&=\hbar^2j(j+1)|j,m_s\rangle,\\
S_3|j,m_s\rangle&=\hbar m_s|j,m_s\rangle,\\
S_\pm|j,m_s\rangle&=\hbar\sqrt{(j\mp m_s)(j\pm m_s+1)}\,|j,m_s\pm1\rangle.
\end{aligned}}
$$
In particular, the <spin raising operator> annihilates $|j,j\rangle$ and the <spin lowering operator> annihilates $|j,-j\rangle$. The coefficient follows by taking the squared <norm> and using $S_\mp S_\pm=\mathbf S^2-S_3^2\mp\hbar S_3$. Thus the <ladder operators> connect every member of the <basis>, while terminating at its two endpoints.

On the <tensor product> of spins $j_1$ and $j_2$, the combined <angular momentum operator> is $\mathbf J=\mathbf S^{(1)}\otimes I+I\otimes\mathbf S^{(2)}$. The <Clebsch-Gordan decomposition> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal H_{j_1}\otimes\mathcal H_{j_2}\cong\bigoplus_{J=|j_1-j_2|}^{j_1+j_2}\mathcal H_J,}
$$
where $J$ increases by one and each <irreducible spin representation> occurs once. The <dimensions> agree: $\sum_J(2J+1)=(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)$.

The uncoupled <orthonormal basis> diagonalizes $S_3^{(1)},S_3^{(2)}$; the coupled <orthonormal basis> diagonalizes $\mathbf J^2,J_3$. The <Clebsch-Gordan coefficients> are their change-of-<basis> entries, with convention
$$
|J,M\rangle=\sum_{m_1,m_2}C_{j_1,j_2}(J,M;m_1,m_2)\,|j_1,m_1\rangle\otimes|j_2,m_2\rangle.
$$
They vanish unless $m_1+m_2=M$. Since both <bases> are orthonormal, this change-of-<basis> <matrix> obeys <unitarity>. Overall phases of the coupled <basis> vectors are fixed by a convention and do not change the <energy eigenvalues>.

= Solution

Write $|\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\rangle$ for the two $S_3$ <eigenstates>, of <eigenvalues> $\pm\hbar/2$. The <spin-spin interaction> can be evaluated using
$$
\mathbf S^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf S^{(2)}=S_3^{(1)}S_3^{(2)}+\frac12\left(S_+^{(1)}S_-^{(2)}+S_-^{(1)}S_+^{(2)}\right).
$$
For $|\beta|\gg|\alpha|$, take $H_0=\beta(S_3^{(1)}-S_3^{(2)})/\hbar$ and $V=\alpha\mathbf S^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf S^{(2)}/\hbar^2$. The unperturbed <energy eigenvalues> are $\beta,-\beta,0,0$ on $|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle,|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle,|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$, respectively. For an isolated normalized <energy eigenstate> $|n\rangle$, <first-order nondegenerate perturbation theory> gives $\Delta E_n^{(1)}=\langle n|V|n\rangle$. The two antiparallel <spins> have expectation $-\alpha/4$, so
$$
\boxed{E_{\uparrow\downarrow}=\beta-\frac\alpha4+O(\alpha^2/|\beta|),\qquad E_{\downarrow\uparrow}=-\beta-\frac\alpha4+O(\alpha^2/|\beta|).}
$$
For the zero-<energy> <degenerate eigenspace>, <degenerate perturbation theory> requires diagonalizing the restriction of $V$. On the ordered <basis> $(|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle)$ it is $(\alpha/4)I$: the <ladder operators> cannot take one parallel state to the other. Indeed neither parallel state couples to anything else under $H$, so **the remaining two levels are exactly $\alpha/4$**, retaining their twofold <quantum degeneracy>.

For $|\alpha|\gg|\beta|$, take $H_0=\alpha\mathbf S^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf S^{(2)}/\hbar^2$ and $V=\beta D$, where $D=(S_3^{(1)}-S_3^{(2)})/\hbar$. The <spin-one-half triplet state> <basis> and <spin-one-half singlet state> are
$$
|t_+\rangle=|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,\quad |t_0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\quad |t_-\rangle=|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle,\quad |s\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$
The identity $\mathbf S^{(1)}\cdot\mathbf S^{(2)}=(\mathbf J^2-3\hbar^2/2)/2$ gives unperturbed <energy eigenvalues> $E_t^{(0)}=\alpha/4$ and $E_s^{(0)}=-3\alpha/4$. Direct action gives
$$
D|t_\pm\rangle=0,\qquad D|t_0\rangle=|s\rangle,\qquad D|s\rangle=|t_0\rangle.
$$
Consequently the restriction of $V$ to the entire triplet <degenerate eigenspace> is zero, so no first-order splitting is missed. The singlet expectation is also zero. In <second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory>, $\Delta E_n^{(2)}=\sum_{r\ne n}|\langle r|V|n\rangle|^2/(E_n^{(0)}-E_r^{(0)})$. Within the triplet, the corresponding second-order effective <matrix> is $\beta^2|t_0\rangle\langle t_0|/\alpha$, already diagonal in this <basis>. Thus, through second order,
$$
\boxed{E_{t_\pm}=\frac\alpha4,\qquad E_{t_0}=\frac\alpha4+\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha},\qquad E_s=-\frac{3\alpha}{4}-\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha}.}
$$
The last two expressions have higher corrections beginning at $O(\beta^4/|\alpha|^3)$, as the exact calculation in part (d) also verifies. For positive $\alpha$, coupling to the lower singlet raises the triplet's $M=0$ level and lowers the singlet; the signs are precisely the <energy> denominators above.

In the ordered product <basis> $(|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle,|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle,|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle,|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle)$, the <Hamiltonian operator> has <matrix>
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}
\alpha/4&0&0&0\\
0&-\alpha/4+\beta&\alpha/2&0\\
0&\alpha/2&-\alpha/4-\beta&0\\
0&0&0&\alpha/4
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The middle block has <characteristic polynomial> $(E+\alpha/4)^2-\beta^2-\alpha^2/4$. Hence the exact <energy eigenvalues> are
$$
\boxed{\frac\alpha4\quad\text{(twice)},\qquad E_\pm=-\frac\alpha4\pm\sqrt{\beta^2+\frac{\alpha^2}{4}}.}
$$
This is the <two-spin Heisenberg Hamiltonian in an opposing longitudinal field>, with its interaction and field parameters rescaled to the paper's units. For positive $\beta$ and $|\alpha|\ll\beta$, the <Taylor expansion> gives
$$
E_\pm=-\frac\alpha4\pm\left(\beta+\frac{\alpha^2}{8\beta}+O(\alpha^4/\beta^3)\right),
$$
which agrees with the first-order result of part (c). For positive $\alpha$ and $|\beta|\ll\alpha$,
$$
E_+=\frac\alpha4+\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha}+O(\beta^4/\alpha^3),\qquad E_-=-\frac{3\alpha}{4}-\frac{\beta^2}{\alpha}+O(\beta^4/\alpha^3).
$$
These agree with the second-order triplet and singlet results. If $\alpha<0$, their association with the signs $\pm$ reverses; if $\beta<0$, the two antiparallel product-state labels reverse. The unordered exact <spectrum> and the perturbative expressions with their signed denominators remain valid.

Let $X$ exchange the two equal-<spin> factors, so $X^\dagger=X=X^{-1}$. For the unperturbed <Hamiltonian operator>, <addition of angular momentum> gives
$$
E_J^{(0)}=\frac\alpha2\bigl[J(J+1)-2j(j+1)\bigr],\qquad J=0,1,\ldots,2j.
$$
For nonzero $\alpha$, different $J$ have distinct <energy eigenvalues>, while each <eigenspace> has the $2J+1$ values of $M$. The stated symmetry of the <Clebsch-Gordan coefficients> gives the <exchange parity of two coupled identical spins>:
$$
X|J,M\rangle=(-1)^{2j-J}|J,M\rangle.
$$
However, $D=(S_3^{(1)}-S_3^{(2)})/\hbar$ changes sign: $XDX=-D$. For any two <basis> vectors in the same $J$ <eigenspace>, insertion of $X^2=I$ therefore gives
$$
\langle J,M|D|J,M'\rangle=(-1)^{2(2j-J)}\langle J,M|XDX|J,M'\rangle=-\langle J,M|D|J,M'\rangle=0.
$$
Thus the entire projected perturbation $P_J\beta DP_J$ is zero, including its off-diagonal <matrix elements>. <Degenerate perturbation theory> now proves that every first-order-in-$\beta$ <energy> correction vanishes. This is <first-order cancellation for an exchange-odd perturbation>; checking only the individual diagonal expectations would not by itself settle a degenerate problem.

For $E>0$, put $k=\sqrt{2mE}$ and $q=k/\hbar>0$. Outside the support of the <potential energy>, a <generalized eigenfunction> of the <Hamiltonian operator> has the <plane wave> form
$$
\psi(x)=\begin{cases}A e^{iqx}+B e^{-iqx},&x<-R_0,\\C e^{iqx}+D e^{-iqx},&x>R_0.\end{cases}
$$
Here $R_0$ denotes the support radius, to distinguish it from the <quantum reflection probability> $R$. The <momenta> are $\pm k$ and the <velocities> are $\pm k/m$. Thus $A,D$ describe incoming <scattering channels> from the left and right, and $B,C$ describe outgoing <scattering channels> towards the left and right. These <energy eigenfunctions> are not <square-integrable functions>; normalizable incoming <wave packets> are formed by superposition over <energy>, and their passage through the interaction region gives the physical <quantum scattering> experiment.

For incidence from the left, normalize $A=1,D=0$ and write $B=r_L,C=t_L$. The <quantum reflection amplitude> is $r_L$ and the <transmission amplitude> is $t_L$. The ratios of outgoing to incident <probability currents> define
$$
\boxed{R=|r_L|^2,\qquad T=|t_L|^2.}
$$
No extra <velocity> ratio occurs because both exterior regions have the same <potential energy> and <mass>. The <scattering matrix>, with a fixed choice of channel order, is
$$
\boxed{\begin{pmatrix}B\\C\end{pmatrix}=S(q)\begin{pmatrix}A\\D\end{pmatrix},\qquad S(q)=\begin{pmatrix}r_L&t_R\\t_L&r_R\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
Its first and second columns are the responses to unit incidence from the left and right, respectively. For a reflection-symmetric <potential energy>, these conventions give $r_L=r_R=r$ and $t_L=t_R=t$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For a real <potential energy> and a <stationary state>, the <probability current> is
$$
j(x)=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\bigl(\psi^*\psi'-\psi\psi'^*\bigr)=\frac\hbar m\operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\psi').
$$
The time-independent <Schrödinger equation> gives $\psi''=2m(V-E)\psi/\hbar^2$, so $j'=\hbar\operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\psi'')/m=0$. In an exterior <plane wave> superposition, interference terms contribute no current and
$$
j_{\rm left}=\frac{k}{m}(|A|^2-|B|^2),\qquad j_{\rm right}=\frac{k}{m}(|C|^2-|D|^2).
$$
Consequently $|A|^2+|D|^2=|B|^2+|C|^2$ for every choice of incoming amplitudes. If $u=(A,D)^T$, this says $u^\dagger(I-S^\dagger S)u=0$ for every complex <vector> $u$. Since $I-S^\dagger S$ is <Hermitian>, vanishing of its <quadratic form> implies that the <matrix> itself vanishes. Therefore
$$
\boxed{S^\dagger S=I,\qquad R+T=1.}
$$
The second identity follows by choosing $u=(1,0)^T$. Since $S$ is a square <matrix>, the first also implies $SS^\dagger=I$, proving <unitarity>. A real <delta potential> preserves this proof: continuity of $\psi$ and the real derivative jump make the current continuous across the interaction point.

Assume $a>0$ and put $\mu=mg/\hbar^2$, $q=k/\hbar$, and $\gamma=\mu/q$. At a <delta potential> located at $x_0$, integration of the <Schrödinger equation> gives the matching conditions
$$
\psi(x_0^+)=\psi(x_0^-),\qquad \psi'(x_0^+)-\psi'(x_0^-)=2\mu\psi(x_0).
$$
Writing the adjacent <plane wave> coefficients as $(A_L,B_L)$ and $(A_R,B_R)$ gives
$$
(A_R-A_L)e^{iqx_0}+(B_R-B_L)e^{-iqx_0}=0,
$$
$$
iq\bigl[(A_R-A_L)e^{iqx_0}-(B_R-B_L)e^{-iqx_0}\bigr]=2\mu(A_Le^{iqx_0}+B_Le^{-iqx_0}).
$$
Solving these two linear equations yields the <transfer matrix of a delta potential>:
$$
\begin{pmatrix}A_R\\B_R\end{pmatrix}=D(x_0)\begin{pmatrix}A_L\\B_L\end{pmatrix},\qquad D(x_0)=\begin{pmatrix}1-i\gamma&-i\gamma e^{-2iqx_0}\\i\gamma e^{2iqx_0}&1+i\gamma\end{pmatrix},\qquad\det D(x_0)=1.
$$
For the <symmetric double-delta potential>, use separate <plane wave> pairs in $x<-a$, $-a<x<a$, and $x>a$. Successive matching gives $M=D(a)D(-a)$ between the two exterior pairs. For real $q$, multiplication gives
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}d^*&-iF\\iF&d\end{pmatrix},\qquad d=(1+i\gamma)^2+\gamma^2e^{4iqa},\qquad F=2\gamma\bigl[\cos(2qa)+\gamma\sin(2qa)\bigr].
$$
The <determinant> identity $\det M=1$ reads $|d|^2-F^2=1$. Left incidence means $(t,0)^T=M(1,r)^T$, whence
$$
\boxed{t=\frac1d=\frac{q^2}{(q+i\mu)^2+\mu^2e^{4iqa}},\qquad r=-\frac{iF}{d}.}
$$
Here the first relation also follows from $t=M_{11}-M_{12}M_{21}/M_{22}=\det M/M_{22}$. Therefore the requested <transmission probability> and <quantum reflection probability>, as functions of the printed <momentum> $k$, are
$$
\boxed{T(k)=\frac{1}{1+F(k)^2},\qquad R(k)=\frac{F(k)^2}{1+F(k)^2},\quad F(k)=\frac{2\mu\hbar}{k}\left[\cos\frac{2ka}{\hbar}+\frac{\mu\hbar}{k}\sin\frac{2ka}{\hbar}\right].}
$$
They obey $R+T=1$. As checks, $g=0$ gives $T=1$, and the coincident limit $a\to0$ gives $T=q^2/(q^2+4\mu^2)$, the answer for a single <delta potential> of strength $2g$.

Continue the <transmission amplitude> to complex <wavenumber> $q=i\kappa$ with $\kappa>0$, equivalently complex <momentum> $k=i\hbar\kappa$. Then $E=-\hbar^2\kappa^2/(2m)<0$. In the left exterior region the $A$ term grows at negative infinity and the $B$ term decays; in the right exterior region the $D$ term grows and the $C$ term decays. A <bound state> thus requires $A=D=0$, with nonzero $B,C$. The <transfer matrix> relation gives $M_{22}(i\kappa)=0$. Equivalently, a zero of the incoming coefficient is a <bound-state pole> of $t(q)$, whose numerator is nonzero at $q=i\kappa$.

The <denominator> found in part (c) gives
$$
0=(i\kappa+i\mu)^2+\mu^2e^{-4a\kappa}=-(\kappa+\mu)^2+\mu^2e^{-4a\kappa}.
$$
Factorization produces two branches. To identify their <parity>, use an even interior <wavefunction> $A\cosh(\kappa x)$ or an odd interior <wavefunction> $A\sinh(\kappa x)$ for $|x|<a$, joined to a decaying exterior <wavefunction> at $a$. Continuity and the derivative jump give, respectively,
$$
-\kappa\bigl[1+\tanh(\kappa a)\bigr]=2\mu,\qquad -\kappa\bigl[1+\coth(\kappa a)\bigr]=2\mu.
$$
Rearrangement gives the implicit <energy eigenvalue> equations
$$
\boxed{\kappa_{\rm even}=-\mu(1+e^{-2a\kappa_{\rm even}}),\qquad \kappa_{\rm odd}=-\mu(1-e^{-2a\kappa_{\rm odd}}),\qquad E_{\rm even,odd}=-\frac{\hbar^2\kappa_{\rm even,odd}^2}{2m}.}
$$
Only strictly positive <equation roots> $\kappa$ are retained. With $\nu=-\mu>0$, the even <equation root> lies above $\nu$ and the odd <equation root>, when it exists, lies below $\nu$, so the even <bound state> has lower <energy>. The formal zero <equation root> in the odd equation does not itself establish a <normalizable wavefunction>.

For $g\geq0$, the <Hamiltonian operator> is nonnegative: its <quadratic form> is $\hbar^2\int|\psi'|^2dx/(2m)+g(|\psi(a)|^2+|\psi(-a)|^2)\geq0$. Thus it has no negative-<energy> <bound states>. A nonnegative-<energy> exterior <wavefunction> cannot be square integrable unless it vanishes identically, so there are no other <bound states> either.

For $g<0$, set $\nu=-mg/\hbar^2>0$. The even equation is the zero of $f(\kappa)=\nu(1+e^{-2a\kappa})-\kappa$. Since $f(0)=2\nu>0$, $f(\kappa)\to-\infty$ and $f'(\kappa)=-2a\nu e^{-2a\kappa}-1<0$, the <intermediate value theorem> and strict <monotonicity> give exactly one positive <equation root>.

The odd equation is the zero of $h(\kappa)=\nu(1-e^{-2a\kappa})-\kappa$. Here $h(0)=0$, $h'(0)=2a\nu-1$, $h''(\kappa)=-4a^2\nu e^{-2a\kappa}<0$, and $h(\kappa)\to-\infty$. If $2a\nu\leq1$, strict <concavity> makes $h(\kappa)<0$ for every $\kappa>0$. If $2a\nu>1$, $h$ initially increases, has exactly one maximum, and then decreases through zero exactly once. This proves the <bound-state count of an attractive symmetric double-delta potential>:
$$
\boxed{N(g)=\begin{cases}
0,&g\geq0,\\
1,&-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2ma}\leq g<0,\\
2,&g<-\dfrac{\hbar^2}{2ma}.
\end{cases}}
$$
At the threshold $g=-\hbar^2/(2ma)$, the odd zero-<energy> solution is proportional to $x$ inside $(-a,a)$ and to $a\operatorname{sgn}x$ outside. It is bounded but not a <square-integrable function>, so it is a <zero-energy scattering resonance> rather than a second <bound state>. Each negative-<energy> <bound state> is either even or odd by <nondegeneracy of one-dimensional bound states>; the two branches above exhaust the count.

In the central <classically forbidden region>, define the positive decay <momentum> $\chi(x)=\sqrt{2m[V(x)-E]}$ and the <dimensionless> <action integral> $K(x)=\hbar^{-1}\int_x^{x_1}\chi(y)\,dy$. The general leading <WKB approximation> is
$$
\boxed{\psi_{\rm I}(x)\simeq\frac{A e^{K(x)}+B e^{-K(x)}}{\sqrt{\chi(x)}},\qquad 0<x<x_1.}
$$
Both coefficients are allowed: this interval has finite length, so the exponential that grows towards the barrier centre cannot be discarded by a condition at infinity. The <WKB validity condition> holds away from the <classical turning point> $x_1$; a local <Airy function> solution supplies its continuation there.

In the right-hand <classically allowed region>, put $p(x)=\sqrt{2m[E-V(x)]}$ and $s(x)=\hbar^{-1}\int_{x_1}^xp(y)\,dy$. A convenient form of the general <WKB approximation> is
$$
\boxed{\psi_{\rm II}(x)\simeq\frac{C\cos(s(x)+\pi/4)+D\sin(s(x)+\pi/4)}{\sqrt{p(x)}},\qquad x_1<x<x_2.}
$$
The phase origin $\pi/4$ is a choice of <basis> for the two oscillatory solutions, anticipating the <Airy turning-point connection formula>. Equivalently, one may use independent $e^{is}$ and $e^{-is}$ branches. The coefficients have not yet been related to those in either forbidden region.

Beyond the outer <classical turning point>, let $\chi(x)=\sqrt{2m[V(x)-E]}$ and $U(x)=\hbar^{-1}\int_{x_2}^x\chi(y)\,dy$. The <bound state> condition removes the growing branch at positive infinity, leaving

= Solution

The <WKB approximation> fails at a simple <classical turning point> $x_t$, because $p(x_t)=0$ makes its amplitude singular and violates the <WKB validity condition>. Locally replace $V(x)-E$ by $V'(x_t)(x-x_t)$. For a forbidden region on the right, $V'(x_t)>0$ and the rescaled coordinate $z=[2mV'(x_t)/\hbar^2]^{1/3}(x-x_t)$ changes the time-independent <Schrödinger equation> into the <Airy ordinary differential equation> $\psi_{zz}-z\psi=0$. Reversing the coordinate handles a forbidden region on the left.

The two independent <Airy functions> have asymptotic forms, for $z,w>0$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Ai}(z)&\sim\frac{z^{-1/4}}{2\sqrt\pi}e^{-2z^{3/2}/3},&\operatorname{Bi}(z)&\sim\frac{z^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}e^{2z^{3/2}/3},\\
\operatorname{Ai}(-w)&\sim\frac{w^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}\sin(2w^{3/2}/3+\pi/4),&\operatorname{Bi}(-w)&\sim\frac{w^{-1/4}}{\sqrt\pi}\cos(2w^{3/2}/3+\pi/4).
\end{aligned}
$$
Match these to the <WKB approximation> in an overlap region where the linearization of the <potential energy> and the large-argument <asymptotic expansions> both apply. If $K_t=\hbar^{-1}|\int_{x_t}^x\chi\,dx|$ on the forbidden side and $s_t=\hbar^{-1}|\int_{x_t}^xp\,dx|$ on the allowed side, the <Airy turning-point connection formula> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{B}{\sqrt\chi}e^{-K_t}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \frac{2B}{\sqrt p}\sin(s_t+\pi/4),\qquad\frac{A}{\sqrt\chi}e^{K_t}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \frac{A}{\sqrt p}\cos(s_t+\pi/4).}
$$
Thus a decaying exponential acquires a factor two in its allowed-side amplitude, whereas a growing exponential does not. The phase convention uses positive integrals measured away from the <classical turning point>, so it applies at both ends of the well without an extra orientation sign. These are leading semiclassical matching rules for simple, separated <classical turning points>.

Use the amplitudes and positive <action integrals> of part (a), and define the right-well phase
$$
\theta(E)=\frac1\hbar\int_{x_1(E)}^{x_2(E)}p(x)\,dx.
$$
Matching at the inner <classical turning point> gives $C=A$, $D=2B$. Matching the exterior decaying <wavefunction> at the outer <classical turning point> gives
$$
\psi_{\rm II}(x)\simeq\frac{2F}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\sin\left(\frac1\hbar\int_x^{x_2}p(y)\,dy+\frac\pi4\right)=\frac{2F}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\sin(\theta-s(x)+\pi/4).
$$
The <trigonometric identity>
$$
\sin(\theta-s+\pi/4)=\cos\theta\cos(s+\pi/4)+\sin\theta\sin(s+\pi/4)
$$
therefore yields
$$
\boxed{A=C=2F\cos\theta,\qquad B=F\sin\theta,\qquad D=2F\sin\theta.}
$$
All three pieces on $x>0$ are now fixed up to $E$ and $F$:
$$
\psi_R(x)\simeq\begin{cases}
\dfrac{F}{\sqrt{\chi(x)}}\bigl[2\cos\theta\,e^{K(x)}+\sin\theta\,e^{-K(x)}\bigr],&0<x<x_1,\\
\dfrac{2F}{\sqrt{p(x)}}\sin(\theta-s(x)+\pi/4),&x_1<x<x_2,\\
\dfrac{F}{\sqrt{\chi(x)}}e^{-U(x)},&x>x_2.
\end{cases}
$$
The <Airy function> descriptions replace the apparent singularities at the <classical turning points>. For the permitted <energy eigenvalues>, part (d) extends this right-hand <wavefunction> to the left by even or odd <parity>; $F$ is then determined by <wavefunction normalization>. At arbitrary $E$, the decaying right-hand solution instead has a unique continuation by the <Schrödinger equation>, which need not decay at negative infinity.

For $V(-x)=V(x)$, the <parity operator> $P\psi(x)=\psi(-x)$ commutes with the <Hamiltonian operator>. Also $P^2=I$, so its <eigenvalues> are $\pm1$ and the <energy eigenspaces> admit a <basis> of definite <parity>. More strongly, <nondegeneracy of one-dimensional bound states> says that each <bound state> <energy> here is nondegenerate: the <Wronskian> of two solutions at the same <energy> is constant by the <Schrödinger equation>, and decay at infinity makes it zero, forcing linear dependence. Thus each <bound state> itself is even or odd.

An <even function> obeys the <Neumann boundary condition> $\psi'(0)=0$; an <odd function> obeys the <Dirichlet boundary condition> $\psi(0)=0$. Write
$$
K_0=\frac1\hbar\int_0^{x_1}\chi(x)\,dx,\qquad \phi(E)=2K_0=\frac1\hbar\int_{-x_1(E)}^{x_1(E)}\sqrt{2m[V(x)-E]}\,dx.
$$
Smooth reflection symmetry gives $\chi'(0)=0$, while $K'(0)=-\chi(0)/\hbar$. Hence, for the central <WKB approximation> from part (c),
$$
\psi_R(0)\simeq\frac{F}{\sqrt{\chi(0)}}\bigl[2\cos\theta\,e^{K_0}+\sin\theta\,e^{-K_0}\bigr],
$$
$$
\psi_R'(0)\simeq\frac{F\sqrt{\chi(0)}}\hbar\bigl[-2\cos\theta\,e^{K_0}+\sin\theta\,e^{-K_0}\bigr].
$$
The two <boundary conditions> consequently give the <WKB quantization condition for a symmetric double well>:
$$
\boxed{\tan\theta(E)=C_\pm e^{\phi(E)},\qquad C_+=2\ \text{for even parity},\qquad C_-=-2\ \text{for odd parity}.}
$$
The full <wavefunction> is $\psi_+(x)=\psi_R(|x|)$ in the even case and $\psi_-(x)=\operatorname{sgn}(x)\psi_R(|x|)$ in the odd case, with the value at zero fixed continuously. The relevant <boundary condition> makes these extensions differentiable at zero. Both <action integrals> $\theta,\phi$ depend on $E$ through the integrands and the <classical turning points>.

A tall, broad central <potential barrier> has $\phi\gg1$. Let $\sigma=+1$ denote even <parity> and $\sigma=-1$ odd <parity>, and write $\theta=(n+\tfrac12)\pi+\delta_\sigma$, where $n=0,1,\ldots$. Since $\tan[(n+\tfrac12)\pi+\delta]=-\cot\delta$, the condition in part (d) gives
$$
\delta_\sigma=-\sigma\arctan\left(\frac12e^{-\phi}\right)=-\frac\sigma2e^{-\phi}+O(e^{-3\phi}).
$$
Thus the leading correction to the isolated-well <Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition> is
$$
\boxed{\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p(x)\,dx=\pi\hbar\left(n+\frac12\right)-\sigma\frac\hbar2\exp\left[-\frac1\hbar\int_{-x_1}^{x_1}\chi(x)\,dx\right]+O(\hbar e^{-3\phi}).}
$$
This is an implicit condition, with all <action integrals> evaluated at the corrected <energy>. To convert it into an explicit leading <energy> correction, let $E_n^{(0)}$ solve $\theta(E_n^{(0)})=(n+\tfrac12)\pi$ and define the full classical <oscillation period> in one well by $\mathcal T(E)=2m\int_{x_1}^{x_2}dx/p(x)$. Differentiation of the <action integral> has no endpoint terms because $p(x_1)=p(x_2)=0$, and gives
$$
\theta'(E)=\frac m\hbar\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\frac{dx}{p(x)}=\frac{\mathcal T(E)}{2\hbar}>0.
$$
Consequently, to leading exponentially small order within the <WKB approximation>,
$$
\boxed{E_{n,\sigma}\simeq E_n^{(0)}-\sigma\frac{\hbar}{\mathcal T(E_n^{(0)})}e^{-\phi(E_n^{(0)})},\qquad E_{n,-}-E_{n,+}\simeq\frac{2\hbar}{\mathcal T(E_n^{(0)})}e^{-\phi(E_n^{(0)})}.}
$$
The even member lies below its odd partner. For an approximately <harmonic oscillator> well, $\mathcal T\simeq2\pi/\omega$, giving the familiar <double-well tunneling splitting> $\Delta E\simeq(\hbar\omega/\pi)e^{-\phi}$. The explicit <energy> formula retains only its leading exponential correction; varying $\phi$ and $\theta'$ with the shifted <energy> also contributes at higher orders.

The <ground state> has an even, everywhere same-sign <wavefunction> with a peak in each well and a small nonzero value beneath the central <potential barrier>. Its derivative vanishes at zero. The first <excited state> is odd, has opposite-sign lobes in the two wells, and has exactly one interior <node of a wavefunction>, at zero. Both <wavefunctions> decay outside the wells. In terms of localized normalized <wavefunctions> $\varphi_L,\varphi_R$, with overlap $s=\langle\varphi_L|\varphi_R\rangle$, their qualitative shapes are the <lowest parity states of a symmetric double well>:
$$
\psi_{\rm ground}\simeq\frac{\varphi_L+\varphi_R}{\sqrt{2(1+s)}},\qquad \psi_{\rm first}\simeq\frac{\varphi_R-\varphi_L}{\sqrt{2(1-s)}}.
$$
The overall sign of the odd <wavefunction> is arbitrary. The sketch uses localized Gaussian profiles to show the <parity> and the central <node of a wavefunction>, rather than claiming exact <energy eigenfunctions> for an unspecified <potential energy>.
