Since $x=\cos y$ with real $y$, necessarily $-1\leq x\leq1$. Use <Euler's formula> and set $w=e^{iy}\ne0$. Then
$$
2x=w+w^{-1},\qquad w^2-2xw+1=0,

Centre <cylindrical coordinates> on the right-hand <sphere>, with the axial coordinate $s=z-\alpha a$ positive outwards. Using $R=\beta a$ and $d=(\alpha-\gamma)a$, the retained solid satisfies
$$

\begin{aligned}
M_e&=\rho\int_{-d}^{R}\int_0^{2\pi}\int_0^{\sqrt{R^2-s^2}}r\,dr\,d\phi\,ds\\

The scaling relation says that $z$ is a <homogeneous function> of degree $n$. Fix $(x,y)$ and differentiate the identity with respect to the positive parameter $\lambda$. The <chain rule> gives
$$
xz_x(\lambda x,\lambda y)+yz_y(\lambda x,\lambda y)=n\lambda^{n-1}z(x,y).
$$
Setting $\lambda=1$ proves the <Euler theorem for homogeneous functions> identity
$$
\boxed{xz_x+yz_y=nz.}
$$
Differentiate once more with respect to $\lambda$, using equality of the mixed <partial derivatives> for the assumed twice continuously differentiable <function>:
$$
x^2z_{xx}(\lambda x,\lambda y)+2xyz_{xy}(\lambda x,\lambda y)+y^2z_{yy}(\lambda x,\lambda y)=n(n-1)\lambda^{n-2}z(x,y).
$$
At $\lambda=1$ this is the <second-order Euler identity for homogeneous functions>:
$$
\boxed{x^2z_{xx}+2xyz_{xy}+y^2z_{yy}=n(n-1)z.}
$$
The repeated parameter <differentiation> accounts for the factor $n(n-1)$, rather than $n^2$.

<Taylor's theorem> gives a finite <Taylor polynomial> of degree $n$, consisting of the first $n+1$ terms, and a remainder:
$$
\boxed{f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{f^{(j)}(a)}{j!}(x-a)^j+R_{n+1}(x).}
$$
In the <Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder> form, assuming the required <derivatives> exist on an <interval> containing $a$ and $x$,
$$
\boxed{R_{n+1}(x)=\frac{f^{(n+1)}(\xi)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1},}
$$
for some $\xi$ strictly between $a$ and $x$ when $x\ne a$; at $x=a$ the remainder is zero. In particular, if $|f^{(n+1)}|\leq M$ on that segment, then $|R_{n+1}(x)|\leq M|x-a|^{n+1}/(n+1)!$. This is a finite expansion; it does not assume equality with an infinite <Taylor series>.
