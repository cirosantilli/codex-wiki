= Epigenesis
{synonym}

Progressive formation and differentiation of an organism during <biological development>, historically contrasted with <preformationism>. It must not be confused with the modern concept of <epigenetics>.

= Preformationism
{parent=Embryology}
{wiki}

The historical view that embryonic development enlarges an already formed organism rather than generating its organization progressively.

= Maternal-to-zygotic transition
{parent=Developmental biology}

The transition from predominant reliance on maternally supplied products to zygotic <gene expression>, accompanied by regulated turnover or redeployment of maternal molecules.

= Implantation
{parent=Developmental biology}

Attachment and embedding of a mammalian conceptus in maternal tissue. Embryonic and extraembryonic tissues undergo coordinated <morphogenesis> during this transition.

= Plant development
{parent=Developmental biology}

Formation and differentiation of plant tissues and organs, continuously influenced by internal state and the environment.

= Root nodule
{parent=Plant development}

A specialized plant organ hosting symbiotic nitrogen fixation in compatible associations. Nodule initiation, bacterial infection and effective nitrogen fixation are separable outcomes.

= Nodulation
{parent=Root nodule}

Formation of root nodules. More nodules do not necessarily imply more effective nitrogen fixation or greater host benefit.

= Autoregulation of nodulation
{parent=Nodulation}

Systemic feedback restricting additional nodulation after symbiotic signaling has begun. Root-to-shoot and shoot-to-root communication coordinates local initiation with whole-plant status.

= TML
{parent=Autoregulation of nodulation}
{c}

TOO MUCH LOVE, a root-localized negative regulator of symbiotic susceptibility targeted by <miR2111> in Lotus japonicus.

= HAR1
{parent=Autoregulation of nodulation}
{c}

HYPERNODULATION ABERRANT ROOT FORMATION1, a Lotus japonicus receptor kinase with a shoot role in systemic restriction of nodulation.

= Heart development
{parent=Developmental biology}

= Myocardium
{parent=Heart development}

The muscular tissue of the heart. Regional myocardial differentiation and growth are distinct from endocardial or supporting-cell development.

= Atrioventricular canal
{parent=Heart development}

The region connecting atrial and ventricular compartments during cardiac development. Its regulatory programme differs from that of rapidly expanding chamber <myocardium>.

= Chamber myocardium
{parent=Heart development}

<myocardium> acquiring chamber-associated growth, structural and electrophysiological properties, distinct from specialized non-chamber territories.

= Cardiac looping
{parent=Heart development}

Three-dimensional bending and torsion of the early <heart tube>. Geometry, left–right patterning, tissue growth and regional <cell differentiation> contribute distinct aspects.

= Heart tube
{parent=Heart development}

An early tubular cardiac structure whose construction includes tissue convergence, differentiation and continued addition of cells at its poles.

= Venous pole of the heart
{parent=Heart tube}

The inflow end of the developing heart, with progenitors contributing to inflow and atrial regions under species- and stage-specific conditions.

= Arterial pole of the heart
{parent=Heart tube}

The outflow end of the developing heart, receiving progenitor contributions on a stage-specific schedule.

= Cardiac crescent
{parent=Heart development}

An early arrangement of cardiogenic tissue before formation of the linear <heart tube> in the mammalian embryo.

= First heart field
{parent=Heart development}

An early cardiac progenitor domain that differentiates into much of the initial <myocardium>, with major contributions to the left ventricle in mouse. Its boundaries and contributions require stage- and species-specific <fate mapping>.

= Homeotic transformation
{parent=Developmental biology}

Replacement of one position-specific developmental identity by another. Demonstrating it requires positive evidence for the alternative identity as well as loss of the original features.

= Developmental timing
{parent=Developmental biology}

The timing and ordering of specification, differentiation, tissue movement and maturation. Equal chronological age need not imply equal developmental stage after perturbation.

= Lung development
{parent=Developmental biology}

= Pulmonary hypoplasia
{parent=Lung development}

= Fetal breathing movement
{parent=Lung development}

Movement before air breathing that contributes to fluid-related mechanical stimulation of the developing lung. Physical loading and mature respiratory function are distinct.

= Alveolar epithelium
{parent=Lung development}

The epithelial lining of pulmonary gas-exchange units, containing differently specialized cell types.

= Type II alveolar cell
{parent=Alveolar epithelium}

An alveolar <epithelial cell> with secretory functions including surfactant production and context-dependent progenitor activity.

= Type I alveolar cell
{parent=Alveolar epithelium}

A thin, extended <epithelial cell> contributing to the gas-exchange surface. Flattening and molecular differentiation should be measured separately.

= Skin development
{parent=Developmental biology}

= Feather placode
{parent=Skin development}

A localized epithelial primordium of a feather. Its molecular identity and underlying dermal aggregation can be coupled through mechanical and chemical signals.

= Developmental competence
{parent=Developmental biology}

The ability of a cell or tissue to respond to an instructive signal at a particular stage and location. The same signal can have different effects after competence changes.

= Neural development
{parent=Developmental biology}

Formation and organization of nervous-system cells and their connections.

= Neural progenitor cell
{parent=Neural development}

A precursor able to produce one or more neural <cell types>. <multipotency> is a property of an individual progenitor under tested conditions, not merely of a mixed population.

= Radial glial cell
{parent=Neural progenitor cell}

A neural progenitor with radial processes and context-dependent neuronal or glial outputs. <cell> <morphology>, molecular state and traced descendants provide complementary evidence for its role.

= Neuronal differentiation
{parent=Neural development}

Acquisition of neuronal identity and function. Commitment, subtype specification, maturation and survival should be measured separately rather than inferred from one neuronal marker.

= Axon guidance
{parent=Neural development}

Processes directing growing axons to appropriate regions or targets.

= Epicardium
{parent=Developmental biology}

The outer tissue layer covering the heart, with developmental contributions to supporting cells and signals affecting myocardial and coronary development.

= Endocardium
{parent=Developmental biology}

The inner endothelial lining of the heart, which also provides developmental signals to neighbouring myocardium.

= Second heart field
{parent=Developmental biology}

A population of cardiac progenitors that contributes cells to the developing heart after initial heart-tube formation. Retaining a proliferative reservoir allows progressive addition of differentiated myocardium.

= Regeneration
{parent=Developmental biology}

Replacement of lost biological structures through organized growth and differentiation. Recovery of mass alone need not demonstrate restoration of the original pattern or function.

= Blastema
{parent=Regeneration}

A regeneration-associated accumulation of progenitor cells. A blastema need not be a homogeneous pluripotent population; contributors can retain tissue and positional restrictions.

= Positional information
{parent=Developmental biology}

Information allowing a cell to respond according to its location within a developing or regenerating structure. Establishing a positional value and interpreting it as a differentiated outcome are distinct steps.

= Positional memory
{parent=Positional information}

Persistence of a position-associated cellular identity after the original spatial context or instructive signal changes. It is distinct from simply retaining a differentiated tissue identity.

= Intestinal development
{parent=Developmental biology}

Formation, regionalization and maturation of the <intestine>, including its epithelial surface, supporting tissues and renewing cell populations.

= Villus morphogenesis
{parent=Intestinal development}

Formation of epithelial villi through interactions among growth, tissue constraints and local signalling. The ordering of these events differs between species.

= Mesenchyme
{parent=Developmental biology}

A tissue organization with cells dispersed through <extracellular matrix>, rather than arranged as a continuous polarized <epithelium>. Mesenchymal populations differ in origin and developmental potential.

= Fate mapping
{parent=Developmental biology}

Assignment of later cellular or tissue contributions to identified precursor regions. Labels require positional and persistence controls; observed fate alone is not a complete test of evolutionary identity.

= Limb bud
{parent=Developmental biology}

An embryonic appendage precursor whose growth, signalling and cellular contributions can be compared between <forelimbs> and <hindlimbs>.

= Autopod
{parent=Limb bud}

The distal limb region including wrist or ankle elements and digits.

= Zeugopod
{parent=Limb bud}

The intermediate limb region containing the radius and ulna, or tibia and fibula, in familiar tetrapod limbs.

= Stylopod
{parent=Limb bud}

The proximal limb region containing the humerus or femur in familiar tetrapod limbs.

= Progress zone
{parent=Limb bud}

A classical model in which time spent in a distal region of the growing limb contributes to proximal-distal specification. Experimental support must be distinguished from alternative consequences of altered survival and growth.

= Apical ectodermal ridge
{parent=Limb bud}

A specialized distal ectodermal structure that supplies signals supporting limb outgrowth and patterning. Removing it simultaneously alters several processes, so truncation alone does not uniquely identify a timing mechanism.

= Zone of polarizing activity
{parent=Limb bud}

A posterior limb-bud signalling region involved in anterior–posterior patterning. The fate of its labelled cells and the identities of adjacent <anatomical digits> are related but distinct observations.

= ABC model of flower development
{parent=Developmental biology}
{c}

A model relating overlapping regulatory functions to floral organ identities. B- and C-function activity contributes to male organ identity, whereas C activity without B contributes to female identity, with additional partners required. Its simple domains are not universal across all angiosperms.

= Developmental modularity
{parent=Developmental biology}

Organization of <biological development> into relatively separable components. It can allow one structure or deployment to evolve with fewer effects on others, without implying complete independence.

= Developmental bias
{parent=Developmental biology}

Unequal accessibility of phenotypic variants caused by the organization of <biological development>. Repeated outcomes can reflect this structure as well as repeated <natural selection>; accessibility and selective advantage are different questions.

= Serial homology
{parent=Developmental biology}

Correspondence among repeated structures within an organism, such as repeated appendages or segments. Repeated structures can subsequently specialize through divergent developmental regulation.

= Heterotopy
{parent=Developmental biology}

An evolutionary change in the spatial deployment of a developmental process. Altering where a regulatory input is expressed can change the resulting pattern.

= Deep homology
{parent=Developmental biology}

Shared inherited developmental machinery underlying structures whose final organization can differ substantially. A shared regulator alone does not establish that two entire organs have the same historical origin.

= Gene co-option
{parent=Developmental biology}

Recruitment of an existing <gene> or <gene regulatory network> to a new developmental deployment or function. Changing the location of <gene expression> can generate a new structure using inherited cellular activities.

= Evolutionary developmental biology
{parent=Developmental biology}

Study of how changes in <biological development> contribute to <evolution>. Comparative <gene expression>, developmental perturbations and <phylogenetics> can connect inherited mechanisms to new forms.

= Heterometry
{parent=Evolutionary developmental biology}

Evolutionary change in the amount or level of a developmental process, structure or expression pattern. A quantitative difference still requires a causal molecular and developmental explanation.

= Digit identity frameshift hypothesis
{parent=Evolutionary developmental biology}
{title2=$C_{\mathrm{II}},C_{\mathrm{III}},C_{\mathrm{IV}}\mapsto D_{\mathrm{I}},D_{\mathrm{II}},D_{\mathrm{III}}$}

The classical avian hypothesis that <anatomical digit> identities I, II and III are expressed at positional condensations II, III and IV. It distinguishes position from developmental identity; evidence for a limited anterior shift does not require invariant identities for every posterior <anatomical digit>.<1,2,3 = 2,3,4: A solution to the problem of the homology of the digits in the avian hand by Günter P. Wagner and Jacques A. Gauthier>[Original hypothesis]{ref}

= Mostly male theory
{parent=Evolutionary developmental biology}

A specific historical hypothesis in which ectopic ovules arose on a predominantly male precursor during flower evolution. It combines gene-history and morphological arguments but remains distinct from a demonstrated ancestral developmental sequence.<The mostly male theory of flower evolutionary origins: from genes to fossils by Michael W. Frohlich and David S. Parker>[Historical proposal and evidence considered]{ref}

= Out-of-male hypothesis
{parent=Evolutionary developmental biology}

A proposed origin of a bisexual reproductive structure through regional modification of a formerly male structure. In one developmental scenario, restriction of B-class activity permits female identity in part of a C-active reproductive axis. Accessibility in a model system does not establish the historical precursor.

= New Head hypothesis
{parent=Developmental biology}
{c}

The hypothesis that integration of <neural crest>, <ectodermal placodes> and other cranial tissues helped enable vertebrate head innovation and more active feeding. Developmental necessity, morphological diversification and ancestral ecological reconstruction are different evidential claims.

= Germ layer
{parent=Developmental biology}

A principal embryonic cellular layer established through <gastrulation>; the classical three-layer organisation comprises <ectoderm>, <mesoderm> and <endoderm>.

= Mesendoderm
{parent=Germ layer}

A developmental designation for mesodermal and endodermal progenitors or a shared regulatory state. A shared marker profile does not prove every individual <cell> is bipotent.

= Prechordal plate
{parent=Mesendoderm}

An anterior axial mesendodermal population with morphogenetic and signalling roles. In <zebrafish> its coherent migration depends on contact-mediated directional information.

= Endoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

An embryonic <germ layer> contributing to the epithelial lining of much of the digestive and respiratory systems and associated organs.

= Definitive endoderm
{parent=Endoderm}

The embryonic <endoderm> population specified during <gastrulation>, distinguished from earlier extraembryonic <primitive endoderm>. In mouse, resulting gut <endoderm> can include intercalated <cells> from distinct histories.

= Endoderm specification in Caenorhabditis elegans
{parent=Endoderm}

The <E blastomere> produces the intestinal lineage. Maternal <SKN-1> and the P2-to-EMS inductive interaction converge on an <endoderm> <gene regulatory network> with parallel inputs and partly redundant <GATA transcription factors>. Loss of one late marker does not establish loss of the lineage.<MED GATA factors promote robust development of the C. elegans endoderm by Morris F. Maduro and others>[2015, pp. 66–79, MED-binding-site perturbations and quantitative gut phenotypes]{ref}

= Pharyngeal arch
{parent=Developmental biology}

A repeated embryonic pharyngeal structure assembled from several tissues, including <neural crest>, <mesoderm>, <ectoderm> and <endoderm>.

= Branchial arch
{parent=Pharyngeal arch}

A gill-bearing <pharyngeal arch> and its associated tissues.

= Branchial ray
{parent=Branchial arch}

A cartilaginous projection supporting the <gill> apparatus in living cartilaginous fishes. It is not the same structure as a bony-fish <gill> raker or a dermal <fin> ray.

= Ectodermal placode
{parent=Developmental biology}

A localised ectodermal thickening or precursor territory contributing to cranial sensory structures and associated cells. Placodes and <neural crest> make distinct interacting contributions.

= Mesoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

An embryonic <germ layer> supplying diverse tissues, including muscle and much connective tissue; particular connective tissues can also arise from ectoderm-derived <neural crest>.

= Lateral plate mesoderm
{parent=Mesoderm}

Lateral embryonic <mesoderm> contributing to body-wall, visceral and paired-appendage tissues under species- and stage-specific arrangements.

= Ectoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

An embryonic <germ layer> giving rise to epidermal and neural tissues, including the origin of vertebrate <neural crest>.

= Neural crest
{parent=Developmental biology}

A vertebrate embryonic population arising near the neural border, with migratory descendants contributing to cranial connective and skeletal tissues, peripheral neurons and glia, pigment cells and other derivatives. Individual cells and axial populations have restricted subsets of these potentials.

= Cellularization
{parent=Developmental biology}

Formation of individual cell boundaries around nuclei previously sharing a common cytoplasmic compartment.

= Syncytial specification
{parent=Developmental biology}

Positional specification of nuclei in a shared <cytoplasm> before complete <cellularization>, allowing spatial signals to influence nuclear transcription without conventional cell boundaries.

= Syncytial blastoderm
{parent=Syncytial specification}

An early <insect> embryonic stage with many nuclei sharing <cytoplasm> before <cellularization>. Transcriptional patterning can therefore precede formation of individual <cell> boundaries.

= Mosaic development
{parent=Developmental biology}

A pattern of development with relatively restricted adjustment after early cell loss or isolation. A missing structure after injury alone does not establish an irreversibly preassigned fate.

= Mosaic theory of development
{parent=Mosaic development}

A historical explanation assigning distinct developmental determinants to different early <blastomeres>. A normal cell fate is not automatically proof that the cell lacks other developmental capacities.<The Science and Philosophy of the Organism by Hans Driesch>[Part I, section B.1: discussion of Roux and experiments with separated blastomeres]{ref}

= Regulative development
{parent=Developmental biology}

Development in which cells can adjust their contributions after perturbation, for example replacing an ablated lineage. Tracing the source of replacement distinguishes fate conversion from expansion of residual cells.

= Heterochrony
{parent=Developmental biology}

An evolutionary change in the timing or relative rates of developmental processes. It can alter outcomes without requiring a wholly new developmental mechanism.

= Segment addition zone
{parent=Developmental biology}

A posterior embryonic region associated with sequential production of body segments; local timing and cell displacement convert changing programmes into spatial order.

= Clock and wavefront model
{parent=Developmental biology}

A model in which a temporal oscillator is converted into repeated spatial boundaries as tissue passes a maturation front. With steady relative front speed v and period T, predicted spacing is vT.

= Presomitic mesoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

Unsegmented posterior <paraxial mesoderm> from which <somites> are generated.

= Amnion
{parent=Developmental biology}

An extraembryonic membrane associated with the amniotic cavity; its early formation and geometry differ among mammalian developmental systems.

= Hypoblast
{parent=Developmental biology}

= Primitive endoderm
{synonym}

An early mammalian lineage contributing to extraembryonic endodermal tissues. It is distinct from the definitive <endoderm> produced during <gastrulation>.

= Visceral endoderm
{parent=Hypoblast}

An extraembryonic endodermal <epithelium> associated with early mammalian development. It supplies signals and matrix and must be distinguished from <definitive endoderm> formed through <gastrulation>.

= Anterior visceral endoderm
{parent=Visceral endoderm}

A signalling region that becomes positioned on the prospective anterior side of the mouse <embryo> and antagonizes posteriorizing signals, helping position the <primitive streak>.

= Inner cell mass
{parent=Developmental biology}

The internal cell population of a mammalian <blastocyst> that separates into <epiblast> and <hypoblast>.

= Trophectoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

The outer lineage of a mammalian <blastocyst>, contributing to extraembryonic trophoblast tissues rather than the embryo proper.

= Extraembryonic ectoderm
{parent=Trophectoderm}

A trophoblast-derived tissue adjacent to the early mouse <epiblast>. Its signalling interactions help organize <gastrulation>, while it is not itself an <epiblast>-derived <germ layer>.

= Somitogenesis
{parent=Developmental biology}

The formation of <somites> from <presomitic mesoderm>, coordinated with posterior tissue supply and the <segmentation clock>.

= Somite
{parent=Developmental biology}

One of the serial paired embryonic units produced from <presomitic mesoderm>; its derivatives contribute to axial skeletal, muscular and dermal tissues.

= Neural tube
{parent=Developmental biology}

An embryonic epithelial structure giving rise to the central nervous system, including the <brain> and <spinal cord> in vertebrates.

= Notochord
{parent=Developmental biology}

An axial tissue of <chordates> that provides structural and signalling functions during development.

= Convergent extension
{parent=Developmental biology}

A tissue narrows in one direction and lengthens in another through coordinated cell movements, often involving <cell intercalation>. Elongation need not imply increased tissue volume.

= Paraxial mesoderm
{parent=Developmental biology}

The <mesoderm> flanking the axial midline that produces <somites> during <somitogenesis>.

= Epiblast
{parent=Developmental biology}

The embryonic lineage that generates the embryo proper and, in mammals, also contributes to certain extraembryonic tissues. It is distinct from the <hypoblast>.

= Proamniotic cavity
{parent=Epiblast}

The early lumen associated with epithelializing mouse <epiblast>. Its formation involves polarity and apical membrane separation rather than requiring elimination of a central mass by <apoptosis>.

= Egg cylinder
{parent=Epiblast}

The characteristic early postimplantation mouse <embryo> arrangement, with cup-shaped <epiblast>, <extraembryonic ectoderm> and surrounding <visceral endoderm>. It differs geometrically from a flat embryonic disc.

= Tailbud
{parent=Developmental biology}

A posterior embryonic region that supplies cells to the extending body axis; its contribution to tissue growth and rearrangement varies with species and stage.

= Primitive streak
{parent=Developmental biology}

The transient region through which cells ingress during amniote <gastrulation>, supplying <mesoderm> and definitive <endoderm>.

= Gastrulation
{parent=Developmental biology}

The coordinated rearrangements that establish the principal <germ layers> and their relative positions in an early embryo.

= Epiboly
{parent=Gastrulation}

Spreading and thinning of a tissue to cover a larger area of the <embryo>, often requiring intercalation and forces generated by adjacent tissues.

= Enveloping layer
{parent=Epiboly}

A superficial <epithelial cell> layer of the <zebrafish> <embryo> that spreads over the yolk during <epiboly>.

= Yolk syncytial layer
{parent=Epiboly}

An extraembryonic syncytial domain in teleost <embryos> that generates forces and signalling inputs affecting <gastrulation>.

= Involution in gastrulation
{parent=Gastrulation}

Rolling of a tissue over an embryonic margin, followed by internal movement. It differs from individual-<cell> <ingression>.

= Invagination
{parent=Gastrulation}

Bending of a tissue sheet inward while retaining substantial epithelial continuity.

= Ingression
{parent=Gastrulation}

Internalization of individual <cells> from a surface tissue, often involving an <epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition>.

= Morphogenesis
{parent=Developmental biology}

The generation of biological shape through processes including tissue growth, cell movement and changes in cellular organisation.

= Lumenogenesis
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Formation of a tissue lumen through polarized membrane organization, transport and related <cell> behaviours. Hollowing and apoptotic cavitation are distinct possible mechanisms.

= Epithelial geometry
{parent=Morphogenesis}

The arrangement and dimensions of cells, junctions and lumina in an <epithelium>. Shared polarity orientation alone does not fix whether the tissue is a sheet, cyst or elongated tube.

= Differential growth
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Unequal growth of coupled tissue regions or layers. Mechanical incompatibility can generate stress and deformation even when no localized external force is imposed.

= Drosophila ventral furrow formation
{parent=Morphogenesis}
{c}

The epithelial invagination that internalizes prospective mesoderm during early fly development. Pulsed apical contractility and tissue coupling contribute to the movement.

= Apical constriction
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Reduction of a cell's apical surface area, often through localized <actomyosin contractility>. Coordinated constriction can bend an epithelial sheet when forces are transmitted through appropriate attachments.

= Drosophila germband extension
{parent=Morphogenesis}
{c}

Elongation of the early fly embryonic tissue through processes including polarized junction remodelling and <cell intercalation>. Neighbour changes and individual cell-shape changes make separable contributions to tissue deformation.

= Ventricular trabeculation
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Formation of projecting myocardial structures on the luminal side of a ventricular wall. Differentiation, adhesion, rearrangement and proliferation can contribute, so absent trabeculae do not uniquely diagnose a division defect.

= Lumen formation
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Creation and organization of an internal space in a tissue. Polarized surface delivery, division orientation, fluid transport and sometimes cell clearance can contribute; a cavity alone does not identify its mechanism.

= Epithelial cyst
{parent=Lumen formation}

An epithelial enclosure around a lumen, used experimentally to study coordinated polarity, division and surface traffic. Multiple lumina do not necessarily imply loss of polarity in every cell.

= Apical membrane initiation site
{parent=Lumen formation}

A transient region where apical determinants and membrane traffic organize a new luminal surface during epithelial polarization.

= Branching morphogenesis
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Development of a branched tissue network through coordinated <cell proliferation>, shape change, <cell migration> and rearrangement. A final tree does not uniquely identify which cellular process generated it.

= Cell shape change
{parent=Morphogenesis}

Alteration of cellular geometry, which can contribute to tissue deformation without a change in cell number. Its contribution can be distinguished from neighbour exchange by following cell outlines over time.

= Embryo
{parent=Developmental biology}

An early developing organism, whose cells and tissues are undergoing coordinated specification, growth and morphogenesis.

= Angiogenesis
{parent=Biological development}

Formation of new blood vessels from pre-existing vasculature. Increased vessel abundance need not imply normal perfusion or effective drug delivery.

= Placental development
{parent=Biological development}

The formation and growth of the organ mediating physiological exchange between maternal and embryonic tissues in placental mammals.

= Spermatogenesis
{parent=Biological development}

The production and maturation of male <gametes>, including <meiosis> in the relevant animals. Failure can cause male <hybrid> sterility without conspicuous changes in adult external form.

= Blastula
{parent=Biological development}
{wiki}

A <blastula> is an early multicellular stage of <animal> <biological development>. The <intestinal nucleus transfer experiment> distinguishes development beyond this stage from the earliest divisions.

= Blastocyst
{parent=Blastula}

A mammalian embryonic stage with an outer <trophectoderm>, a cavity and an <inner cell mass>.
