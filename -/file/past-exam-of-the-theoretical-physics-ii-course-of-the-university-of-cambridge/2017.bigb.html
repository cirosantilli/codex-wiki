= Solution
{parent=a}

In <second quantization>, expand the annihilating <quantized field> in a complete <orthonormal basis> of <one-particle states>, $\hat\psi(x)=\sum_\alpha\varphi_\alpha(x)a_\alpha$. At equal times, the <canonical commutation relation> for <bosons> and the <canonical anticommutation relations> for <fermions> are, respectively,
$$
[\hat\psi(x),\hat\psi^\dagger(x')]=\delta(x-x'),\qquad
[\hat\psi(x),\hat\psi(x')]=[\hat\psi^\dagger(x),\hat\psi^\dagger(x')]=0,
$$
and
$$
\{\hat\psi(x),\hat\psi^\dagger(x')\}=\delta(x-x'),\qquad
\{\hat\psi(x),\hat\psi(x')\}=\{\hat\psi^\dagger(x),\hat\psi^\dagger(x')\}=0.
$$
Here $\delta$ is a <Dirac delta distribution>. Multiplying by the basis <wave functions> and integrating gives $[a_\alpha,a_\beta^\dagger]=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ for <bosons>, or $\{a_\alpha,a_\beta^\dagger\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ for <fermions>; the two-annihilator and two-creator brackets vanish in the corresponding convention.

The mode <number operator> and total <particle number> operator are
$$
\boxed{n_\alpha=a_\alpha^\dagger a_\alpha,\qquad \hat N=\sum_\alpha n_\alpha.}
$$
The <bosonic occupation numbers> are $0,1,2,\ldots$. For <fermions>, $a_\alpha^2=0$ and the <canonical anticommutation relations> imply $n_\alpha^2=n_\alpha$, so the <fermionic occupation numbers> are only $0$ and $1$, as required by the <Pauli exclusion principle>.

Keeping only two modes later is a projection: the projected field bracket is $\sum_{j=1}^2\varphi_j(x)\varphi_j^*(x')$, rather than the full <Dirac delta distribution>.

Normalize the ring modes as $\varphi_j(x)=L^{-1/2}e^{ik_jx}$, with distinct $k_j=2\pi\ell_j/L$ and integers $\ell_j$. Inserting $\hat\psi(x)=\varphi_1(x)a_1+\varphi_2(x)a_2$ into the <contact interaction> gives
$$
H_{\rm int}=\frac g2\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^2 I_{ijkl}a_i^\dagger a_j^\dagger a_k a_l,
\qquad I_{ijkl}=\int_0^L\varphi_i^*\varphi_j^*\varphi_k\varphi_l\,dx.
$$
<Orthogonality> makes $I_{ijkl}=1/L$ when $k_i+k_j=k_k+k_l$, and zero otherwise: this is <momentum conservation> in the projected <Hamiltonian operator>. There is one all-mode-1 term, one all-mode-2 term, and four mixed terms with one particle in each mode. A pair conversion between distinct ring modes would require $2k_1=2k_2$ and therefore vanishes.

Using $a_j^{\dagger2}a_j^2=n_j(n_j-1)$ and $a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger a_1a_2=n_1n_2$ yields the <contact interaction between two ring modes>,
$$
\boxed{H_{\rm int}=\frac{g}{2L}\left[n_1(n_1-1)+n_2(n_2-1)+4n_1n_2\right],\qquad
\alpha=\beta=\frac g{2L},\quad\gamma=\frac{2g}{L}.}
$$
The factor four comes from two independent choices of ordering for the <creation operators> and <annihilation operators>. This is the two-mode projected model; a full ring <contact interaction> can also scatter particles into other modes while conserving total <momentum>.

= Solution

At fixed <particle number> $N$, write $n_1=n$ and $n_2=N-n$. The <contact interaction between two ring modes> has <energy eigenvalues>
$$
E(n)=\frac{g}{2L}\left[N(N-1)+2n(N-n)\right],\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,N.
$$
For $g>0$, the nonnegative product $n(N-n)$ is minimized at the endpoints. Consequently
$$
\boxed{E_{\min}=\frac{gN(N-1)}{2L},\qquad
|\psi_{\min}\rangle=c_1|N,0\rangle+c_2|0,N\rangle,\quad |c_1|^2+|c_2|^2=1.}
$$
For $N\geq1$ this is a two-dimensional degenerate <ground-state> subspace. $N=0$ instead has the unique <Fock vacuum>. In this projected model, putting all particles into one traveling mode avoids the extra exchange contribution paid when both distinct modes are occupied.

For the alternative coefficients, put $\alpha=\beta=A<0$ and $\gamma=0$. Then $E(n)=A[N(N-1)-2n(N-n)]$ is again minimized at $n=0,N$, with $E_{\min}=AN(N-1)$. Evenness of $N$ is unnecessary for this result; the balanced <Fock state> maximizes this attractive-interaction <energy> instead of minimizing it.

Add the one-particle <energy> term $H_0=\epsilon_1n_1+\epsilon_2n_2$. It is diagonal in the same <Fock state> basis as the <contact interaction between two ring modes>. For $N=2$, the ordered basis $(|2,0\rangle,|1,1\rangle,|0,2\rangle)$ gives
$$
\boxed{H=\begin{pmatrix}
2\epsilon_1+g/L&0&0\\
0&\epsilon_1+\epsilon_2+2g/L&0\\
0&0&2\epsilon_2+g/L
\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
For $g>0$, the middle <eigenvalue> exceeds the mean of the two endpoint <eigenvalues> by $g/L$, so it cannot be the smallest. The <ground state> is therefore **$|2,0\rangle$ if $\epsilon_1<\epsilon_2$, and $|0,2\rangle$ if $\epsilon_2<\epsilon_1$**, with
$$
\boxed{E_{\min}=2\min(\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2)+g/L.}
$$
If $\epsilon_1=\epsilon_2$, every normalized <linear combination> of the two endpoint <Fock states> is a <ground state>. The same endpoint argument works for general fixed $N$, since the interaction contribution is a concave quadratic function of the mode <occupation number>.

For distinct positive integers $p,q$, use normalized box modes $\varphi_1=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(p\pi x/L)$ and $\varphi_2=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(q\pi x/L)$. Their <contact interaction> overlaps obey
$$
I_{1111}=I_{2222}=\frac{3}{2L},\qquad I_{1122}=\frac1L.
$$
Unlike traveling ring modes, these standing modes also allow pair conversion. For the first two box modes, or any distinct pair without a third-harmonic resonance, the <contact interaction between two box modes> is
$$
\boxed{H_{\rm int}=\frac{3g}{4L}\left[n_1(n_1-1)+n_2(n_2-1)\right]
+\frac{2g}{L}n_1n_2+\frac{g}{2L}\left(a_1^{\dagger2}a_2^2+a_2^{\dagger2}a_1^2\right).}
$$
The last two terms change an individual mode <occupation number> by two while conserving total <particle number>. Thus the <Hamiltonian operator> is not diagonal in the <Fock state> basis, and minimizing diagonal entries alone does not find its <ground state>. For example, at $N=2$ with no dispersion, $\left(|2,0\rangle-|0,2\rangle\right)/\sqrt2$ has the lowest <energy> $g/(2L)$, below either unmixed endpoint's $3g/(2L)$.

The wording permits modes beyond the first two. For completeness, the identity $\sin^3u=(3\sin u-\sin3u)/4$ gives
$$
I_{1112}=-\frac{\delta_{q,3p}}{2L},\qquad I_{1222}=-\frac{\delta_{p,3q}}{2L}.
$$
When $q=3p$, add $-g(a_1^{\dagger2}a_1a_2+a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger a_1^2)/(2L)$; when $p=3q$, add $-g(a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger a_2^2+a_2^{\dagger2}a_1a_2)/(2L)$. These extra terms change an individual <occupation number> by one and require diagonalizing the corresponding fixed-$N$ matrix as well.

Complete the square in the <potential energy>. With $x_F=F/(M\omega^2)$,
$$
H=\frac{p^2}{2M}+\frac{M\omega^2}{2}(x-x_F)^2-\frac{F^2}{2M\omega^2}.
$$
The <quantum harmonic oscillator> is translated without changing its level spacing. If $\varphi_n(x)$ is its normalized unforced <energy eigenstate>, then
$$
\boxed{\psi_n^{(F)}(x)=\varphi_n(x-x_F),\qquad
E_n^{(F)}=\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right)-\frac{F^2}{2M\omega^2},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$
Equivalently, $|n;F\rangle=e^{-ix_Fp/\hbar}|n\rangle$. The <ground-state> <position> is centered at $x_F$, and its <position uncertainty> remains $\sqrt{\hbar/(2M\omega)}$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

A normalized oscillator <coherent state> is an <eigenstate> of the <annihilation operator>, $a|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle$. Expanding it in <Fock states> gives
$$
|\alpha\rangle=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle.
$$
Since $x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2M\omega)}(a+a^\dagger)$, $\langle a^2\rangle=\alpha^2$, and $\langle aa^\dagger\rangle=|\alpha|^2+1$, the <variance> is $\langle x^2\rangle-\langle x\rangle^2=\hbar/(2M\omega)$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\Delta x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2M\omega}},}
$$
independently of the complex amplitude $\alpha$.

For $A=\alpha a^\dagger$ and $B=-\alpha^*a$, the <commutator> is the scalar $[A,B]=|\alpha|^2I$. The <Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula> therefore terminates and gives the <displacement operator>
$$
D(\alpha)=e^{\alpha a^\dagger-\alpha^*a}
=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}e^{\alpha a^\dagger}e^{-\alpha^*a}.
$$
Because $a|0\rangle=0$, applying it to the <Fock vacuum> yields exactly the <Fock state> expansion above:
$$
\boxed{|\alpha\rangle=D(\alpha)|0\rangle.}
$$
For a general complex $\alpha$, its <complex conjugate> is required in the annihilation term. The expression with $-\alpha a$ printed in the paper is valid when $\alpha$ is real.

= Solution
{parent=c}

The <quantum state> is continuous through a sudden finite-force <quantum quench>, so it is still $|0\rangle$ at $t=0^+$. Define
$$
\lambda=\frac{F_0}{\sqrt{2M\hbar\omega^3}},\qquad b=a-\lambda,\qquad \theta=\omega t.
$$
The new <Hamiltonian operator> is $H=\hbar\omega(b^\dagger b+1/2)-\hbar\omega\lambda^2$. The old <Fock vacuum> obeys $b|0\rangle=-\lambda|0\rangle$, so the <coherent state after shifting an oscillator equilibrium> rotates with amplitude $-\lambda e^{-i\theta}$ relative to the new equilibrium. In the original oscillator basis, the full state is
$$
\boxed{|\psi(t)\rangle=e^{-i\theta/2+i\lambda^2(\theta-\sin\theta)}
\left|\lambda(1-e^{-i\theta})\right\rangle.}
$$
One way to obtain the phase is to write
$$
e^{-iHt/\hbar}|0\rangle
=e^{-i\theta/2+i\lambda^2\theta}D(\lambda)e^{-i\theta a^\dagger a}D(-\lambda)|0\rangle
$$
and apply the <displacement operator> composition rule. The <coherent-state oscillator expectation values> give
$$
\boxed{\langle x\rangle=\frac{F_0}{M\omega^2}(1-\cos\omega t),\qquad
\langle p\rangle=\frac{F_0}{\omega}\sin\omega t.}
$$
The <position uncertainty> stays $\sqrt{\hbar/(2M\omega)}$. These mean values obey the forced classical equation, while the nonzero <variance> retains the quantum width.

Specify the magnetic coupling before fixing its sign: let the <magnetic moment> be $\boldsymbol\mu=\gamma\mathbf S$, where $\gamma$ is a signed gyromagnetic ratio. The <Zeeman effect> gives $H_Z=-\gamma\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf S$. For <spin one-half>, $\mathbf S=\hbar\boldsymbol\sigma/2$, so with $B=|\mathbf B|$ and $u=\beta\gamma\hbar B/2$,
$$
\boxed{\rho=\frac{e^{-\beta H_Z}}{\operatorname{Tr}e^{-\beta H_Z}}
=\frac12\left[I+\tanh u\,\hat{\mathbf B}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\right],\qquad
Z=2\cosh u.}
$$
This is the <quantum Gibbs state>. Its <eigenvalues> are $(1\pm\tanh u)/2$, so its <purity> is $(1+\tanh^2u)/2$. For nonzero level splitting it approaches a <pure state> as $|u|\to\infty$: positive <inverse temperature> selects the lower <energy>, while negative <inverse temperature> selects the upper one. It is a <maximally mixed state> at $u=0$, including infinite <temperature> or zero magnetic splitting. At zero field the Gibbs state remains $I/2$; lowering <temperature> alone does not select a unique <pure state> from that degeneracy.

<Negative temperature> is meaningful for an internally equilibrated spin sector with a bounded <energy> spectrum and <population inversion>. It must be sufficiently isolated from unbounded motional degrees of freedom: the oscillator's unbounded spectrum, for instance, makes its partition sum diverge at negative <inverse temperature>. The two-level spin partition sum itself stays finite for either sign.

= Solution

For $\mathbf B=B\mathbf e_z$, the <quantum Gibbs state> is diagonal in the $S_z$ basis. Its two <probabilities> are proportional to $e^{\pm\beta\gamma\hbar B/2}$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\langle S_z\rangle=\frac\hbar2\tanh\left(\frac{\gamma\hbar B}{2k_BT}\right).}
$$
At small <inverse temperature>, $\langle S_z\rangle\simeq\beta\gamma\hbar^2B/4$. As a function of $u=\beta\gamma\hbar B/2$, the normalized <spin> component is the smooth odd curve $\tanh u$: it crosses zero at $u=0$ and saturates at $\pm1$. As a function of $\beta$ its direction is reversed if $\gamma B<0$. For $\gamma B>0$, positive <temperature> favors $S_z=+\hbar/2$, while negative <temperature> gives the inverted population.

For a time-independent <Hamiltonian operator>, the <time-evolution operator> is $U(T)=e^{-iHT/\hbar}$, with $T=t-t'$. Insert its resolution of identity in normalized <energy eigenstates>:
$$
\langle x|U(T)|x'\rangle
=\sum_\alpha\langle x|\alpha\rangle e^{-iE_\alpha T/\hbar}\langle\alpha|x'\rangle.
$$
Since $\langle x|\alpha\rangle=\varphi_\alpha(x)$, the retarded <quantum propagator> in the question is
$$
\boxed{K(x,t|x',t')=\Theta(T)\sum_\alpha\varphi_\alpha(x)\varphi_\alpha^*(x')e^{-iE_\alpha T/\hbar}.}
$$
The <Heaviside step function> imposes the chosen retarded convention. If the <Hamiltonian operator> has a <continuous spectrum>, the spectral sum includes its continuum integral; it must not be restricted to <bound states>. The <spectral resolution of a quantum propagator> also shows that $K$ tends to $\delta(x-x')$ as $T\downarrow0$. With the step factor it obeys $(i\hbar\partial_t-H)K=i\hbar\delta(T)\delta(x-x')$; the corresponding <Green function> with a unit delta source is $K/(i\hbar)$.

= Solution

Away from the <delta potential>, the decaying <bound state> obeys $E_0=-\hbar^2\kappa^2/(2m)$. Integrating the <Schrödinger equation> across the attractive interaction gives
$$
\varphi'(0^+)-\varphi'(0^-)=-\frac{2mg}{\hbar^2}\varphi(0).
$$
For an even exponential, the left side is $-2\kappa\varphi(0)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\kappa=\frac{mg}{\hbar^2},\qquad E_0=-\frac{mg^2}{2\hbar^2}.}
$$
The normalized <bound state in a delta potential> is $\varphi_0(x)=\sqrt\kappa e^{-\kappa|x|}$, since $2\kappa\int_0^\infty e^{-2\kappa x}dx=1$. The single positive solution for $\kappa$ gives one <bound state>; an odd decaying wave cannot be both continuous and nonzero with this interaction.

Choose the even-channel <scattering phase shift> convention $\varphi_{k,e}(x)=\pi^{-1/2}\cos(k|x|+\delta_e)$ for $k>0$. The <delta potential> derivative jump gives
$$
-2k\sin\delta_e=-2\kappa\cos\delta_e,
$$
so, choosing the branch that tends to zero as $k\to\infty$,
$$
\boxed{\delta_e(k)=\arctan(\kappa/k),\qquad \delta_o(k)=0.}
$$
An odd <scattering eigenfunction> is $\varphi_{k,o}(x)=\pi^{-1/2}\sin kx$: it vanishes at the interaction and its derivative is continuous, so it acquires no <scattering phase shift>. These parity channels, with the <bound state>, give the continuum part of the <spectral resolution of a quantum propagator>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

At the origin, the normalized <bound state in a delta potential> contributes $|\varphi_0(0)|^2=\kappa$, and the odd <scattering eigenfunctions> contribute zero. The even continuum functions from the preceding part have
$$
|\varphi_{k,e}(0)|^2=\frac1\pi\cos^2\delta_e=\frac1\pi\frac{k^2}{k^2+\kappa^2}.
$$
Their <energy> is $E_k=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$. Inserting these weights into the <spectral resolution of a quantum propagator>, and using evenness to extend the integral to the whole real axis, yields for $T>0$
$$
\boxed{K(0,T|0,0)=\kappa e^{-iE_0T/\hbar}
+\int_{-\infty}^\infty\frac{dk}{2\pi}\frac{k^2}{\kappa^2+k^2}
e^{-i\hbar k^2T/(2m)}.}
$$
This is the <quantum propagator of an attractive delta potential>. The printed continuum exponent has an extra factor of $\hbar$: its time phase must be $-iE_kT/\hbar=-i\hbar k^2T/(2m)$. This correction also makes the exponent <dimensionless>. Oscillatory integrals are understood with an infinitesimal positive convergence factor before taking its limit.

= Solution
{parent=e}

For fixed $g>0$, write $A=\hbar T/(2m)$. The bound contribution to the <quantum propagator of an attractive delta potential> remains $\kappa e^{i\hbar\kappa^2T/(2m)}$. For its continuum, the <stationary phase> is at $k=0$, where $k^2/(k^2+\kappa^2)=k^2/\kappa^2+O(k^4)$. The Gaussian oscillatory integral and its derivative give
$$
\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-iAk^2}dk=\sqrt\pi e^{-i\pi/4}A^{-1/2},\qquad
\int_{-\infty}^\infty k^2e^{-iAk^2}dk=\frac{\sqrt\pi}{2}e^{-3i\pi/4}A^{-3/2}.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{K(0,T|0,0)=\kappa e^{i\hbar\kappa^2T/(2m)}
+\frac{e^{-3i\pi/4}}{4\sqrt\pi\kappa^2}\left(\frac{2m}{\hbar T}\right)^{3/2}
+O(T^{-5/2}).}
$$
The continuum decays as $T^{-3/2}$, but the full kernel has a persistent oscillating <bound-state> contribution and no ordinary complex limit. This expansion requires $\hbar\kappa^2T/(2m)\gg1$; it is not uniform in the zero-coupling limit, where the bound state disappears and the free kernel instead decays as $T^{-1/2}$.

Outside a short-range scattering region, the stationary <Schrödinger equation> reduces to the two-dimensional <Helmholtz equation>. Separation in <polar coordinates> gives outgoing radial <Hankel functions>, whose large-$r$ dependence is $r^{-1/2}e^{ikr}$. The same power follows from conserved <probability current>: a circle has length proportional to $r$, so the squared outgoing amplitude must decrease as $1/r$.

For an incident <plane wave> in the $x$ direction, adopt the dimensionless amplitude convention
$$
\boxed{\psi(r,\theta)\sim e^{ikr\cos\theta}+\frac{e^{i\pi/4}}{\sqrt{kr}}f(\theta)e^{ikr}.}
$$
The phase $e^{i\pi/4}$ and factor $k^{-1/2}$ specify the definition of $f$; they can be absorbed into a differently normalized <scattering amplitude>. Here they are kept exactly so that the later two-dimensional <scattering cross-section> follows consistently.

= Solution
{parent=b}

The incident <plane wave> carries <probability current> $j_{\rm in}=\hbar k/m$. For the separated outgoing wave from the previous part,
$$
j_r^{\rm sc}=\frac\hbar m\operatorname{Im}(\psi_{\rm sc}^*\partial_r\psi_{\rm sc})
\sim\frac\hbar{mr}|f(\theta)|^2.
$$
The rate through a distant arc is $rj_r^{\rm sc}\,d\theta$. Dividing by incident flux defines the differential two-dimensional <scattering cross-section>,
$$
\boxed{\frac{d\lambda}{d\theta}=\frac{|f(\theta)|^2}{k},\qquad
\lambda=\frac1k\int_0^{2\pi}|f(\theta)|^2\,d\theta.}
$$
Because $f$ is <dimensionless>, $\lambda$ has units of length, as appropriate for scattering in a plane.

= Solution
{parent=c}

The incident <plane wave> is periodic in $\theta$, so expand it as a <Fourier series> $\sum_m R_m(r)e^{im\theta}$. Substitution into the <Helmholtz equation>, with $\rho=kr$, gives the radial <Bessel differential equation>
$$
\rho^2R_m''+\rho R_m'+(\rho^2-m^2)R_m=0.
$$
Regularity at the origin selects the <Bessel function of the first kind>, rather than the singular second solution. Thus
$$
\boxed{e^{ikr\cos\theta}=\sum_{m=-\infty}^\infty a_mJ_m(kr)e^{im\theta}.}
$$
The coefficients $a_m$ are independent of $r$; their values follow from matching near the origin in the next part. The completed identity is the <Jacobi-Anger expansion>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

For $m\geq0$, expand $e^{i\rho\cos\theta}=\exp[i\rho(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})/2]$. The lowest power producing the Fourier harmonic $e^{im\theta}$ is $(i\rho/2)^m/m!$. Meanwhile $J_m(\rho)\sim(\rho/2)^m/m!$. Matching these lowest powers gives $a_m=i^m$.

For negative order use $J_{-m}=(-1)^mJ_m$ and the equality of the positive and negative Fourier coefficients of this even function of $\theta$. This gives $a_{-m}=(-1)^mi^m=i^{-m}$. Therefore the <Jacobi-Anger expansion> is
$$
\boxed{e^{i\rho\cos\theta}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}i^mJ_m(\rho)e^{im\theta}
=J_0(\rho)+2\sum_{m=1}^\infty i^mJ_m(\rho)\cos(m\theta).}
$$
The cosine form has multiplicity one for $m=0$ and two for every positive $m$. Those multiplicities are essential for matching the incident <plane wave>.

= Solution
{parent=e}

Let $w_0=1$ and $w_m=2$ for $m\geq1$, as required by the cosine form of the <Jacobi-Anger expansion>. For a central real potential, the <partial-wave S-matrix> is $S_m=e^{2i\delta_m}$. Splitting each shifted radial cosine into incoming and outgoing exponentials leaves its incoming coefficient unchanged and multiplies its outgoing coefficient by $S_m$. Subtracting the incident <plane wave> gives
$$
\psi_{\rm sc}\sim\frac{e^{ikr-i\pi/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi kr}}
\sum_{m=0}^\infty w_m(S_m-1)\cos(m\theta).
$$
Comparing with the prescribed <scattering amplitude> convention yields the <two-dimensional partial-wave scattering formula>,
$$
\boxed{f(\theta)=\frac1{i\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{m=0}^\infty w_m(e^{2i\delta_m}-1)\cos(m\theta)
=\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\sum_{m=0}^\infty w_me^{i\delta_m}\sin\delta_m\cos(m\theta).}
$$
<Orthogonality> gives $\int_0^{2\pi}1\,d\theta=2\pi$ and $\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2(m\theta)d\theta=\pi$ for $m\geq1$, while all cross terms vanish. Hence
$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac4k\left[\sin^2\delta_0+2\sum_{m=1}^\infty\sin^2\delta_m\right].}
$$
The multiplicities printed in this part are reversed. With those printed values and every $\delta_m=0$, the purported free wave would be $\tfrac12e^{ikr\cos\theta}+\tfrac32J_0(kr)$, rather than the required incident <plane wave>. The extra $J_0$ contains an incoming cylindrical wave, which cannot be absorbed into $f$. The formulas above use the corrected multiplicities and therefore satisfy the specified scattering boundary condition.
