Arithmetic studies elementary operations on numbers.

= Number
{parent=Arithmetic}
{wiki}

Numbers are mathematical objects used to express quantities and carry out operations. Familiar families include the <natural numbers>, <integers>, <rational numbers>, <real numbers>, and <complex numbers>; specifying the family determines which operations and solutions are available.

= Sum of squares
{parent=Arithmetic}
{title2=$\sum_i a_i^2$}

A sum of squares adds the <squares of numbers> $a_i^2$ for specified <real numbers> $a_i$. It is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when every term is zero. For the coordinates of a <vector>, it is the squared <Euclidean norm>; for <regression residuals>, it gives the <residual sum of squares> minimized by <ordinary least squares>.

= Triangular number
{parent=Arithmetic}
{title2=$T_n=n(n+1)/2$}
{wiki}

A triangular number counts objects arranged in rows of lengths $1,2,\ldots,n$. Pairing that sum with its reverse gives $T_n=n(n+1)/2$. The identity $T_{n+1}^2-T_n^2=(n+1)^3$ then telescopes to $\sum_{r=1}^n r^3=T_n^2$.

= Geometric mean
{parent=Arithmetic}
{title2=$G=(x_1\cdots x_n)^{1/n}$}

The geometric mean of finitely many nonnegative real numbers is the nonnegative $n$th root of their product. For two positive numbers it is $\sqrt{ab}$. The <arithmetic-geometric mean inequality> compares it with their <arithmetic mean>. Unlike an arithmetic average, the geometric mean respects multiplicative scaling and is natural for growth factors.

= Arithmetic-geometric mean iteration
{parent=Geometric mean}
{title2=$a_{n+1}=\sqrt{a_nb_n},\quad b_{n+1}=(a_n+b_n)/2$}

Starting from $0<a_1\leq b_1$, replace the lower endpoint by the <geometric mean> and the upper endpoint by the <arithmetic mean>. The <arithmetic-geometric mean inequality> gives $a_n\leq a_{n+1}\leq b_{n+1}\leq b_n$. The <bounded monotone sequence theorem> gives limits, and the arithmetic recurrence forces them to coincide. The limiting value is the arithmetic-geometric mean of the initial pair.

= Archimedean property

The Archimedean property says that the <natural numbers> are unbounded in the <real numbers>. In particular, for every positive <real number> $a$ there is a positive <integer> $n$ with $1/n<a$. = Weighted mean
{synonym}

For positive weights $w_i$, the weighted mean is
$$
\bar x_w=\frac{\sum_iw_ix_i}{\sum_iw_i}.
$$
It lies between the smallest and largest $x_i$; the same result holds for integrals with a positive weight function.

= Arithmetic mean
{title2=$\bar x$}
{parent=Weighted arithmetic mean}
{wiki}

The arithmetic mean of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ is
$$
\bar x=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n x_i.
$$
It is the <weighted mean> with equal weights.

= Harmonic mean

The <harmonic mean> of positive numbers $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ is $H=n/(\sum_i1/x_i)$. The <Jensen inequality> for $1/x$ gives $H\le\overline x$, with equality exactly when all entries coincide.

= Product of numbers
{parent=Multiplication}
{title2=$\prod_{i=1}^n a_i$}

The product of numbers $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ is the result of their iterated <multiplication>. The empty product is $1$, the multiplicative identity. For positive factors, $\log(\prod_i a_i)=\sum_i\log a_i$, turning a multiplicative <conservation law> into an additive derivative identity.

= Exponentiation
{parent=Multiplication}
{title2=$a^b$}

For a nonnegative integer exponent, <exponentiation> iterates <multiplication>: $a^0=1$ and $a^{b+1}=a\,a^b$. This convention defines $0^0=1$ in <primitive recursive> arithmetic. Extensions to other exponents may use different domains and conventions.

= Squares of numbers
{synonym}

The square of a number $a$ is the product $a\cdot a$. For a <real number>, it is nonnegative. The <sum of squares> of real coordinates is the squared <Euclidean norm> of the corresponding <vector>.

= Hyperoperation
{parent=Exponentiation}
{title2=$a[n]b$}
{wiki}

The <hyperoperation> hierarchy successively iterates basic arithmetic operations. In the usual indexing, successor is followed by <addition>, <multiplication>, <exponentiation> and their higher iterates. A sequence beginning with addition shifts this index by one. Each fixed rank is a <primitive recursive function>; this does not imply that the evaluator taking the rank as an additional variable is <primitive recursive>.

= Dyadic block obstruction to infinite arithmetic progressions
{parent=Arithmetic progression}
{title2=$\chi(n)=\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor\bmod2$}

Colour each interval $[2^j,2^{j+1})\cap\mathbb N$ by the parity of $j$. Every infinite <arithmetic progression> of positive <common difference> d meets every sufficiently large such interval, since the interval has length greater than d. Both colours occur infinitely often. Thus neither colour class contains an infinite <arithmetic progression>, despite the finite-length conclusion of the <Van der Waerden theorem>.

= Lifting a short modular progression to an integer progression
{parent=Arithmetic progression}

If all residues of a modular <arithmetic progression> have integer representatives in an interval of diameter less than half the modulus, the representatives form an integer <arithmetic progression>. Consecutive differences lie in an interval shorter than the modulus, so the common modular difference has a unique possible integer representative.

= Continuous functions on the p-adic integers
{parent=Non-Archimedean analysis}
{title2=$\mathcal C(\mathbb Z_p,\mathbb Q_p)$}

The <continuous functions> from the <p-adic integers> to the <p-adic numbers> form a <Banach space> over $\mathbb Q_p$ with <supremum norm> $\|f\|_\infty=\sup_{x\in\mathbb Z_p}|f(x)|_p$. Compactness of the domain bounds the norm, and <uniform convergence> in the complete codomain proves completeness. Translation and the <forward difference operator> $\Delta f(x)=f(x+1)-f(x)$ are bounded operators, with $\|\Delta f\|_\infty\leq\|f\|_\infty$.

= Mahler's theorem
{parent=Continuous functions on the p-adic integers}
{c}
{title2=$f(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_n\binom xn$}
{wiki}

= Mahler theorem
{c}
{synonym}

= Mahler expansion
{synonym}
{c}

Every function in <continuous functions on the p-adic integers> has a unique uniformly convergent expansion in the <binomial polynomials>, with $a_n\to0$ and $a_n=(\Delta^nf)(0)$. Conversely, any coefficient sequence tending to zero defines such a <continuous function>. To prove the expansion once $a_n\to0$ is known, use $|\binom xn|_p\leq1$ for uniform convergence. Finite binomial inversion gives agreement with $f$ at each nonnegative integer, and density gives agreement everywhere. The coefficients recover successively from these integer values, proving uniqueness. Moreover $\|f\|_\infty=\sup_n|a_n|_p$, because finite differences bound each coefficient by the norm and the expansion gives the reverse inequality.

= Discrete antidifferentiation on the p-adic integers
{parent=Mahler's theorem}
{title2=$Jf(x)=\sum_{n\geq0}a_n(f)\binom{x}{n+1}$}

The <Mahler coefficients> tend to zero, so the displayed series defines a <continuous function> and has value zero at zero. The <Pascal's identity> and uniform convergence give $\Delta Jf=f$. Thus the <forward difference operator> is surjective on <continuous functions on the p-adic integers>. Its kernel consists of constants: period one implies agreement on the dense nonnegative integers, and <continuity> then implies constancy. This selects the unique <discrete antiderivative> vanishing at zero.

There is also a direct construction on <locally constant functions>. If $h$ has period $M=p^r$, put $a_j=h(j)$, $S=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}a_i$, and $P_j=\sum_{i<j}a_i$. For $x=j+My$ with $0\le j<M$ and $y\in\mathbb Z_p$, set
$$
Jh(x)=yS+P_j.
$$
This is a <continuous function> on each residue class. Increasing $j$ gives $Jh(x+1)-Jh(x)=a_j$; at $j=M-1$, the next point has residue zero and quotient $y+1$, giving the same identity. The <ultrametric inequality> gives $\|Jh\|_\infty\le\|h\|_\infty$, and $Jh(0)=0$. The construction is independent of the chosen period, since two normalized <discrete antiderivatives> agree on the nonnegative integers and then on the <p-adic integers> by <continuity>. It is linear on the <locally constant functions>, which form a <dense subset> in the <supremum norm>. Completeness therefore extends it to every <continuous function>, retaining $\Delta J=I$ and the norm bound. This proves surjectivity without first using the <Mahler theorem>.

= Translation-invariant linear forms on p-adic continuous functions vanish
{parent=Discrete antidifferentiation on the p-adic integers}
{title2=$T=0$}

Let $T:\mathcal C(\mathbb Z_p,\mathbb Q_p)\to\mathbb Q_p$ be linear and invariant under translation by one. For any $f$, choose its continuous <discrete antiderivative> $Jf$. Then $T(f)=T(\Delta Jf)=T(Jf(\cdot+1))-T(Jf)=0$. No <continuity> or boundedness of $T$ is assumed. In particular this applies to a form invariant under every translation by a <p-adic integer>.

= Mahler coefficient
{parent=Mahler's theorem}
{c}
{title2=$a_n=(\Delta^nf)(0)$}

For a <continuous function> $f:\mathbb Z_p\to\mathbb Q_p$, its nth Mahler coefficient is
$$
a_n=\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^{n-j}\binom nj f(j).
$$
This is the explicit iterate of the <forward difference operator>. These coefficients are exactly those of its <Mahler expansion>.

= Mahler coefficient exponential generating function
{c}
{parent=Mahler coefficient}
{title2=$\sum_{n\ge0}c_nT^n/n!=e^{-T}\sum_{n\ge0}f(n)T^n/n!$}

For the <Mahler coefficients> of a <continuous function> on the <p-adic integers>, finite <binomial inversion> gives $c_n=\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^{n-j}\binom njf(j)$. Equivalently their exponential generating function is the displayed identity in the formal power-series ring $\mathbb Q_p[[T]]$. Each coefficient involves only finitely many terms; no claim of convergence for every $p$-adic value of $T$ is needed.

= Mahler expansion of the parity function
{parent=Mahler coefficient}
{c}
{title2=$(-1)^x=\sum_{n\geq0}(-2)^n\binom xn$ on $\mathbb Z_2$}

The <locally constant function> equal to one on $2\mathbb Z_2$ and minus one on its other coset has <Mahler coefficients> $a_n=(-2)^n$: at nonnegative integers its values are $(-1)^j$, so the <forward difference operator> multiplies it by $-2$. Since $|a_n|_2=2^{-n}$ and the <binomial polynomials> have absolute value at most one on the <2-adic integers>, the series converges uniformly. The <binomial theorem> verifies its values on the nonnegative integers, and <continuity> extends the identity to the entire domain.

= Automatic decay of Mahler coefficients
{parent=Mahler coefficient}
{title2=$a_n(f)\longrightarrow0$}

Approximate a <continuous function> on $\mathbb Z_p$ uniformly by a function constant on residue classes modulo $p^r$. On this finite space, translation $S$ satisfies $S^{p^r}=I$, so $\Delta^{p^r}=(S-I)^{p^r}$ has every matrix coefficient divisible by $p$. Hence $\|\Delta^{jp^r}\|\leq p^{-j}$, and the <Mahler coefficients> of a locally constant function tend to zero. Since $|a_n(f)-a_n(g)|_p\leq\|f-g\|_\infty$, uniform approximation proves the assertion for every <continuous function>. This completes the coefficient-decay part of the <Mahler theorem>.

= Weak approximation for inequivalent absolute values
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}
{title2=$K\text{ is dense in }\prod_{i=1}^r K_{v_i}$}

= Weak approximation theorem
{synonym}

For finitely many pairwise inequivalent nontrivial <absolute values on a field>, an element of the field can approximate independently prescribed targets in the associated <completion of a valued field> at each place to arbitrary positive accuracies. Equivalently the diagonal map has dense image in the finite product. Requiring finitely many inequivalent places is essential; the conclusion controls finitely many inequalities, not infinitely many independently prescribed bounds at once, or incompatible targets at one place. <Weak approximation for number fields> is the class-field-theoretic instance.

= Bounded comparison forces equality of multiplicative absolute values
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}
{title2=$c|x|_1\le|x|_2\le C|x|_1\Longrightarrow |x|_1=|x|_2$}

If two multiplicative <absolute values on a field> satisfy a comparison with fixed positive constants $c,C$, apply it to $x^n$ and take $n$th roots. Letting $n\to\infty$ eliminates both constants. This stronger bounded comparison differs from equivalence merely as topologies: replacing an absolute value by its square generally preserves topology but not equality.

= Completion of a valued field
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}
{title2=$\widehat K$}

The metric completion of a <field> for an <absolute value on a field> is formed from Cauchy sequences modulo sequences tending to zero. Addition and multiplication extend continuously, and every nonzero completed element is invertible, so the result remains a <field>. The <absolute value on a field> extends uniquely. A place of a <number field> determines the corresponding completion $K_v$.

= Algebraic closure of the p-adic numbers is not complete
{parent=Completion of a valued field}

Choose compatible primitive <roots of unity> $\zeta_{p^{n+1}}$. The partial sums $A_N=\sum_{n=0}^Np^{2n}\zeta_{p^{n+1}}$ form a <Cauchy sequence>. Each $A_N$ generates $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^{N+1}})$: for a nonidentity cyclotomic automorphism with $r=v_p(a-1)$, the first changed term has valuation $2r+1/(p-1)$, strictly below every later changed term. Thus the conjugate distance is at least $p^{-2N-1/(p-1)}$. The tail bound is $p^{-2N-2}$, strictly smaller. If the limit were algebraic, <Krasner's lemma> would place every one of these unbounded-degree cyclotomic fields in one finite extension, a contradiction.

= Trivial absolute value
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}
{title2=$|x|=\mathbf1_{x\ne0}$}

The trivial <absolute value on a field> is zero at zero and one elsewhere. Its metric <topology> is discrete. Its <valuation ring> and <residue field> are both the field itself. This is why rational absolute-value classification and nontrivial discrete-value compactness criteria need a nontriviality hypothesis.

= Non-Archimedean absolute value
{title2=$|\cdot|$}
{c}
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}

A non-Archimedean absolute value satisfies the ultrametric inequality $|x+y|\leq\max(|x|,|y|)$.

= Integer criterion for a non-Archimedean absolute value
{parent=Non-Archimedean absolute value}

An <absolute value on a field> is non-Archimedean if and only if $|n\cdot1|\le1$ for every positive integer $n$. Necessity follows from the <ultrametric inequality>. Conversely the binomial theorem gives $|x+y|^m\le(m+1)\max(|x|,|y|)^m$, since every binomial coefficient has absolute value at most one. Take $m$th roots and let $m\to\infty$. In particular an <extension of an absolute value> that is non-Archimedean stays non-Archimedean.

= Bounded-integer criterion for a non-Archimedean absolute value
{parent=Non-Archimedean absolute value}

An absolute value on a field is non-Archimedean exactly when its values on the image of $\mathbb Z$ are bounded. The reverse implication follows by applying the ordinary triangle inequality to the binomial expansion of $(x+y)^n$ and taking $n$th roots as $n\to\infty$.

= Ultrametric inequality
{parent=Non-Archimedean absolute value}

The ultrametric inequality is $d(x,z)\leq\max(d(x,y),d(y,z))$. For unequal $|x|$ and $|y|$, it implies $|x+y|=\max(|x|,|y|)$.

= Extension of an absolute value
{parent=Absolute value (algebra)}

For a <field extension> $L/K$, an extension of an absolute value $|\cdot|$ on $K$ is an absolute value on $L$ whose restriction to $K$ is $|\cdot|$.

= Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension
{parent=Extension of an absolute value}

If $K$ is complete and $L/K$ is finite, an absolute value on $K$ extends uniquely to $L$. With the normalization that it restricts exactly to the given absolute value,
$$
|x|_L=|N_{L/K}(x)|^{1/[L:K]}.
$$
Every $K$-automorphism of $L$ preserves this extended absolute value.

= Finite-dimensional non-Archimedean norm equivalence over a complete field
{parent=Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension}
{title2=$c\max_i|a_i|\leq\|\sum_i a_i e_i\|\leq C\max_i|a_i|$}

Let $K$ be complete for a <non-Archimedean absolute value> and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional <vector space> with a norm satisfying $\|av\|=|a|\|v\|$ and the <ultrametric inequality>. All such norms are equivalent to the maximum of coordinates in any <basis>. The upper bound is immediate. For the lower bound, induct on dimension. The span $W$ of all but the last <basis> vector is complete by induction, hence closed for the given norm. Thus $\delta=\inf_{w\in W}\|e_n-w\|>0$. For $v=w+ae_n$, $\|v\|\geq |a|\delta$; also $\|w\|\leq\max(\|v\|,|a|\|e_n\|)$. The induction bound on coordinates of $w$ now bounds all coordinates by a constant times $\|v\|$. This proof needs no <local compactness>.

= Compact-sphere proof of finite-dimensional non-Archimedean norm equivalence
{parent=Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension}
{title2=$c\|x\|_0\leq\|x\|\leq C\|x\|_0$}

On a finite-dimensional vector space over $\mathbb Q_p$, the triangle inequality gives an upper bound by a coordinate maximum norm. The coordinate unit sphere is compact. The other norm is continuous and nonzero there, so has a positive minimum. Scaling by powers of $p$ gives the lower bound. The resulting <equivalence of norms in finite dimensions> proves completeness of finite extensions, and applying the comparison to powers of a field element proves equality of two extending multiplicative <absolute values on a field>.

= Pure-power reduction of a monic irreducible polynomial
{parent=Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension}
{title2=$\overline f=\varphi^m$}

Suppose a <non-Archimedean absolute value> extends uniquely to every finite extension. If $f\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ is <monic> and irreducible, every root has absolute value at most one: otherwise its leading power dominates all lower terms, contradicting $f(\alpha)=0$ by the <ultrametric inequality>. In a <splitting field>, let $\varphi$ be the <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element> of the residue of one root. Lift its coefficients to $\mathcal O_K$. The lift has absolute value less than one at that root and hence, by <equal absolute values of algebraic conjugates>, at every root. Consequently every root residue is a zero of $\varphi$. Since reduction preserves the product of the linear factors with multiplicities, every irreducible factor of $\overline f$ is $\varphi$, proving the displayed identity for some $m\geq1$.

= Equal absolute values of algebraic conjugates
{parent=Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension}
{title2=$|\alpha_i|=|\alpha_j|$}

Suppose a <non-Archimedean absolute value> on $K$ has a unique extension to every finite <field extension>. Any K-isomorphism $K(\alpha_i)\to K(\alpha_j)$ between conjugate roots pulls the absolute value back to an extension on the source field, which must be the given one. Thus $|h(\alpha_i)|=|h(\alpha_j)|$ for every $h\in K[X]$. This argument includes inseparable <irreducible polynomials>: no transitive Galois action or completeness assumption is needed.

= Integral closure in a finite extension of a complete discretely valued field
{parent=Unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension}

Let $K$ be complete for a discrete valuation and let $L/K$ be finite. The valuation ring $\mathcal O_L$ is exactly the integral closure of $\mathcal O_K$ in $L$. One direction follows from a monic integral equation; for the other, the coefficients of the minimal polynomial are elementary symmetric polynomials in conjugates of absolute value at most one.

= Conjugacy of extensions of a valuation to a normal extension
{parent=Extension of an absolute value}

If $L/K$ is a finite normal extension, the Galois group acts transitively on the absolute values of $L$ extending a fixed absolute value of $K$. For number fields, this is equivalent to transitivity on the prime ideals above a fixed prime.

= Valuation ring need not equal the integral closure over a noncomplete field
{parent=Extension of an absolute value}

Give $\mathbb Q$ its $5$-adic valuation, take $L=\mathbb Q(i)$, and choose the extension associated with the prime $(2+i)$. Then $(2+i)/(2-i)$ lies in the chosen valuation ring but has negative valuation at the conjugate prime. It is therefore not in the integral closure of $\mathbb Z_{(5)}$ in $L$.

= Local factorization and extended absolute values
{parent=Extension of an absolute value}

If $L=K(\alpha)$ is a finite separable extension and $v$ is an absolute value on $K$, the extensions of $v$ to $L$ correspond to the irreducible factors of the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over the completion of $K$ at $v$.

= Local prime decomposition of T5 plus 6T3 plus 252T2 plus 126
{title2=$T^5+6T^3+252T^2+126$}
{parent=Local factorization and extended absolute values}

This polynomial is <Eisenstein> at $2$. Over $\mathbb Q_3$, its <Newton polygon> has a single slope $-2/5$, so it is irreducible and a root generates a totally ramified extension of degree five. Over $\mathbb Q_7$, its factor degrees are $1,1,3$: the simple residue roots $1,-1$ lift, and the remaining roots have valuation $1/3$. Thus the degree-five <number field> generated by a root has one prime above $3$ and three above $7$.

= Prime ideals and local embedding orbits
{parent=Local factorization and extended absolute values}

For a finite extension of <number fields> $L/K$ and a nonzero <prime ideal> $\mathfrak p$ of $K$, the primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak p$ correspond to the orbits of
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K_{\mathfrak p}/K_{\mathfrak p})\quad\text{on}\quad\operatorname{Hom}_K(L,\overline K_{\mathfrak p}).
$$
An embedding determines a prime by pulling back the local <absolute value on a field>. Embeddings with the same prime extend to embeddings of the same completion and are conjugate. If $L=K(\alpha)$, these orbits are the irreducible factors of its <minimal polynomial> over $K_{\mathfrak p}$.

= Completion of a number field at a prime ideal
{title2=$K_{\mathfrak p}$}
{parent=Local factorization and extended absolute values}

For a <number field> $K$ and a nonzero <prime ideal> $\mathfrak p\subset\mathcal O_K$, the completion $K_{\mathfrak p}$ is the completion for the $\mathfrak p$-adic absolute value. It is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, where $p\mathbb Z=\mathfrak p\cap\mathbb Z$.

= p-adic tensor decomposition of a number field
{title2=$K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_p$}
{parent=Completion of a number field at a prime ideal}

For a <number field> $K$ and a rational prime $p$, the natural diagonal map gives an isomorphism of $\mathbb Q_p$-algebras
$$
K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q_p\cong\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid p}K_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

= Surjectivity of decomposition group reduction
{parent=Decomposition group}
{title2=$D_{\mathfrak q}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p})$}

For a finite <Galois extension> of <number fields>, choose a nonzero <residue field> generator at $\mathfrak q$ and lift it to an integral element $a$ whose residues at the other primes above $\mathfrak p$ are zero, using the <Chinese remainder theorem>. The orbit polynomial $\prod_{\sigma\in G}(T-\sigma a)$ has coefficients in the base <ring of integers>. Modulo $\mathfrak q$, its nonzero roots are precisely the orbit of $\overline a$ under the <decomposition group>. Its coefficients belong to $k_{\mathfrak p}$, so <finite-field Frobenius automorphism> permutes these roots. Consequently some decomposition-group element acts as Frobenius on the generator, and therefore on the entire <residue field>. Frobenius generates the finite-field <Galois group>, proving surjectivity.

H\backslash G/D\longrightarrow\{\mathfrak q\subset\mathcal O_L:\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p\},\qquad HgD\longmapsto g\mathfrak P\cap\mathcal O_L

Complete splitting means every right action of $D$ on $H\backslash G$ fixes each coset, so $D$ lies in the <subgroup core> of $H$. That core is trivial because the conjugates of $L$ generate $M$.

= Ostrowski's theorem
{c}
{parent=p-adic absolute value}
{wiki}

= Ostrowski theorem
{c}
{synonym}

Every nontrivial absolute value on $\mathbb Q$ is equivalent either to the usual absolute value or to one $p$-adic absolute value.

= Non-Archimedean part of Ostrowski theorem
{parent=Ostrowski's theorem}
{title2=$|x|=p^{-c v_p(x)},\quad c>0$}

For a nontrivial <non-Archimedean absolute value> on $\mathbb Q$, every integer has absolute value at most one. Some prime $p$ must have value less than one, or the value would be trivial on every rational number. Two distinct such primes are impossible by a <Bézout identity>. Prime factorization then gives the displayed formula. Distinct primes give inequivalent topologies, since powers of $p$ tend to zero only for the $p$-adic absolute value among these values.

= Absolute residue degree
{parent=Residue degree}
{title2=$f_{\mathfrak q}=[\mathcal O_F/\mathfrak q:\mathbb F_p]$}

The absolute <residue degree> is the degree of the <residue field> over its prime field. For a tower above $p$, degrees multiply: $[k_{\mathfrak q}:\mathbb F_p]=[k_{\mathfrak q}:k_{\mathfrak p}][k_{\mathfrak p}:\mathbb F_p]$. = Local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field
{parent=Local field}

A complete field with a nontrivial <non-Archimedean absolute value> is locally compact exactly when its <valuation> is discrete and its <residue field> is finite. In that case its <valuation ring> is the <inverse limit> of the finite rings $\mathcal O/\mathfrak m^n$ and is compact. The <nontrivial valuation in the local compactness criterion> is essential.

= Digit expansion with a nonuniformizer
{parent=Local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field}
{title2=$x=\sum_{n\geq r}a_n t^n$}

Let a nontrivially valued <local field> have compact <valuation ring> $\mathcal O$, and choose any $t$ with $0<|t|<1$. The quotient $\mathcal O/t\mathcal O$ is finite. A fixed set of representatives containing zero gives every field element a unique convergent digit expansion, with finitely many nonzero negative-index terms. Recursive reduction modulo $t\mathcal O$ gives existence; comparing the first differing digit modulo this ideal gives uniqueness. The element $t$ need not be a <uniformizer>.

= Completeness of locally compact nontrivially valued fields
{parent=Local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field}

In a locally compact nontrivially valued field, the <valuation ring> is compact. A Cauchy sequence has a tail inside a translate of this compact set, hence a convergent subsequence. The Cauchy property forces the full sequence to converge.

= Discrete valuation from nontrivial local compactness
{parent=Local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field}
{title2=$\mathfrak m=\pi\mathcal O_K,\quad|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m|<\infty$}

For a locally compact field with a nontrivial <non-Archimedean absolute value>, scaling a compact neighborhood makes its <valuation ring> compact. Its open <maximal ideal> gives a finite discrete <residue field>, so that ideal is also closed and compact. A nonzero element of maximal absolute value in the ideal generates it. Successive division by this <uniformizer> makes the value group discrete.

= Nontrivial valuation in the local compactness criterion
{parent=Local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field}

An infinite field with the trivial <absolute value on a field> is discrete, hence locally compact, but its <residue field> is the original infinite field. Thus local compactness forces a finite <residue field> and a discrete nontrivial value group only when the <valuation> is assumed nontrivial.

= Multiplicative group of a non-Archimedean local field
{title2=$K^\times$}
{parent=Local field}

If a non-Archimedean <local field> $K$ has <uniformizer> $\pi$, <valuation ring> $\mathcal O_K$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_K$ and residue field $\mathbb F_q$, then
$$
K^\times\cong\pi^{\mathbb Z}\times\mu_{q-1}\times(1+\mathfrak m_K).
$$
The first factor records the <discrete valuation>, the <Teichmuller representatives> split reduction $\mathcal O_K^\times\to\mathbb F_q^\times$, and $1+\mathfrak m_K$ is the group of <principal units>.

= Principal-unit filtration of a local field
{parent=Multiplicative group of a non-Archimedean local field}
{title2=$U_m=1+\mathfrak p^m\quad(m\ge1)$}

The groups $U_m$ form a compact-open neighbourhood basis at one inside the units of a non-Archimedean <local field>. A <smooth character> trivial on $U_{i+1}$ but nontrivial on $U_i$ cannot have a nonzero fixed vector under $U_m$ for $m\le i$. This elementary conductor comparison controls admissibility and smooth duals of direct sums.

= Principal-unit group of a mixed-characteristic local field
{title2=$1+\mathfrak m_K$}

If $K/\mathbb Q_p$ is finite of degree $d$, then as a topological <abelian group>

1+\mathfrak m_K\cong\mu_{p^a}(K)\times\mathbb Z_p^d,
$$
where $\mu_{p^a}(K)$ is the finite group of all <roots of unity> in $K$ whose orders are <prime powers> dividing a power of $p$.

= Hensel's lemma
{c}
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

= Hensel lemma
{c}
{synonym}

For a complete discrete valuation ring $R$, a simple root of a polynomial modulo the maximal ideal lifts uniquely to a root in $R$.

= Coprime-factor Hensel lifting
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

A monic <polynomial> over a complete <discrete valuation ring> factors with prescribed coprime monic residue factors. Given monic lifts $g_r,h_r$ correct modulo $\pi^r$, the degree-bounded map $(u,v)\mapsto u\bar h_r+v\bar g_r$ is a bijection over the <residue field>, by coprimality and equality of dimensions. Solve it for the error $(f-g_rh_r)/\pi^r$, and replace the lifts by $g_r+\pi^ru,h_r+\pi^rv$. Their coefficients converge by completeness and the limiting product is $f$. The completeness assumption cannot be replaced just by localization.

= Simple-root Hensel lifting by successive residues
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

For a <complete discrete valuation ring> $R$, let $f(a_1)\in(\pi)$ and let $f'(a_1)$ be a <unit>. If $f(a_n)\in(\pi^n)$, choose $c$ modulo $\pi$ solving $f(a_n)/\pi^n+c f'(a_n)\equiv0$ and put $a_{n+1}=a_n+c\pi^n$. Taylor expansion gives $f(a_{n+1})\in(\pi^{n+1})$. The sequence is <Cauchy> and its limit is a root. If two roots lift the same residue, factoring $f(x)-f(y)$ leaves $x-y$ times a unit, proving uniqueness.

= Coprime factor lifting from valuation-extension uniqueness
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

Suppose a <non-Archimedean absolute value> on $K$ has a unique <extension of an absolute value> to every finite extension. Factor a <monic> $g\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ into <monic> irreducible factors over $K$. All factors have integral coefficients because their roots have absolute value at most one. By <pure-power reduction of a monic irreducible polynomial>, each factor reduces to a power of a single irreducible <polynomial>. If $\overline g=\overline g_1\overline g_2$ is a <coprime> <monic> factorization, assign each whole irreducible factor of $g$, with its multiplicity, to the unique side containing its residue factor. The resulting <monic> $g_1,g_2\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ satisfy $g=g_1g_2$ and reduce to the prescribed factors. Completeness of $K$ is not required for this proof.

= Elements that are powers of infinitely many degrees in a local field
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

For a non-Archimedean local field $K$, an element of $K^\times$ is an $m$th power for infinitely many positive integers $m$ exactly when it belongs to $\mathcal O_K^\times$. Valuations prove necessity; for sufficiency, choose infinitely many $m$ coprime to the residue characteristic and to $|k^\times|$, for which exponentiation by $m$ is an automorphism of the unit group.

= Factorization of X4 plus 9X2 minus 2 over the 2-adic numbers
{title2=$X^4+9X^2-2$ over $\mathbb Q_2$}
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

The polynomial $X^4+9X^2-2$ has three irreducible factors over $\mathbb Q_2$, of degrees one, one, and two. Its quadratic in $X^2$ has one unit root congruent to one modulo eight, hence a square, and one root of valuation one, whose associated quadratic factor is Eisenstein.

= Strong form of Hensel lemma
{parent=Hensel's lemma}

Let $K$ be complete for a <discrete valuation> $v$. If $f\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ and
$$
v(f(a))>2v(f'(a)),
$$
then <Newton iteration over a valued field> converges to a root $\alpha\in\mathcal O_K$ with $v(\alpha-a)>v(f'(a))$.

= Newton iteration over a valued field
{parent=Strong form of Hensel lemma}

Starting from $a_0$, Newton iteration sets $a_{n+1}=a_n-f(a_n)/f'(a_n)$. Under the <strong form of Hensel lemma> inequality, the correction valuations grow at least geometrically, so $(a_n)$ is a <Cauchy sequence> converging to a root.

= Krasner's lemma
{c}
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

Let $K$ be complete, let $\alpha$ be separable over $K$, and let $\beta$ be algebraic over $K$. If $\beta$ is closer to $\alpha$ than any other $K$-conjugate of $\alpha$, then
$$
K(\alpha)\subseteq K(\beta).
$$

= Closest-root descent in a Galois extension
{parent=Krasner's lemma}

Let $L/K$ be a finite <Galois extension> of a field complete for a <non-Archimedean absolute value>. Suppose $\alpha$ is uniquely closest to $\beta$ among the roots of a polynomial over $K$ lying in $L$. Every element of $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K(\beta))$ preserves <absolute values on a field> and permutes these roots. It sends $\alpha$ to an equally close root, hence fixes $\alpha$. The <fundamental theorem of Galois theory> gives $K(\alpha)\subseteq K(\beta)$. The converse inclusion need not hold: for roots $0,\sqrt p,-\sqrt p$, the element $p\sqrt p$ is uniquely closest to zero but does not belong to $\mathbb Q_p$.

= Finiteness of fixed-degree extensions of a p-adic field
{parent=Krasner's lemma}

A <p-adic field> has only finitely many extensions of a fixed degree up to isomorphism. There are finitely many possible residue degrees and a unique <unramified extension> of each such degree. Over each maximal unramified subfield, a <totally ramified extension> is generated by a root of an <Eisenstein polynomial>. The coefficient space of these polynomials of fixed degree is compact. <Hensel lemma> and <Krasner's lemma> show that sufficiently close polynomials generate isomorphic extensions, giving a finite cover by neighborhoods of a constant extension type.

= Continuity of roots over a non-Archimedean field
{parent=Krasner's lemma}

The roots of a separable polynomial over a complete non-Archimedean field vary continuously with its coefficients after the roots are suitably matched. In particular, a sufficiently close coefficientwise approximation has one root in each sufficiently small disjoint ball around an original root.

= Local realization as a completion of a number field
{parent=Krasner's lemma}

Every finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ is isomorphic to the completion $F_{\mathfrak p}$ of some number field $F$ at a prime $\mathfrak p$ above $p$. Approximate a primitive element's minimal polynomial by a polynomial over $\mathbb Q$ and use <Krasner's lemma> to preserve the generated local field.

= Completion of an algebraic closure of a p-adic field is algebraically closed
{title2=$\mathbb C_p$ is algebraically closed}
{parent=Krasner's lemma}

The completion $\mathbb C_p$ of an <algebraic closure> of $\mathbb Q_p$ is an <algebraically closed field>. Approximate a polynomial over $\mathbb C_p$ by one over $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$, use <continuity of roots over a non-Archimedean field> to obtain a nearby algebraic root, and then use <Krasner's lemma> to show that the original root already belongs to $\mathbb C_p$.

= Weierstrass preparation theorem
{c}
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

Over a complete local ring, a power series whose first unit coefficient has index $d$ is a unit times a unique monic distinguished polynomial of degree $d$. Consequently its sufficiently small zeros, counted with multiplicity, are the zeros of that polynomial.

= Distinguished polynomial
{parent=Weierstrass preparation theorem}
{title2=$f(T)=T^d+a_{d-1}T^{d-1}+\cdots+a_0,\quad a_i\in p\mathbb Z_p$}

A distinguished polynomial over $\mathbb Z_p$ is monic and has every non-leading coefficient divisible by $p$. The <Weierstrass preparation theorem> expresses any nonzero element of $\mathbb Z_p[[T]]$ as a power of $p$ times a unit times a distinguished polynomial.

= Laurent series field
{title2=$k((t))$}
{parent=Local field}

= Formal Laurent series field
{synonym}

The Laurent series field $k((t))$ consists of <formal power series>[formal series] $\sum_{n\geq N}a_nt^n$ with finitely many negative-degree terms. Its $t$-adic valuation is the least exponent with nonzero coefficient.

= Prescribed ramification and residue degree for a Laurent series field
{parent=Laurent series field}
{title2=$\mathbb F_{p^f}((u))/\mathbb F_p((t)),\quad t=u^e$}

For arbitrary positive integers $e,f$, the displayed extension has degree $ef$, <ramification index> $e$, and <residue degree> $f$. If $|t|=p^{-1}$, its unique extended <absolute value on a field> is $|\sum a_nu^n|=p^{-\min\{n:a_n\ne0\}/e}$. A basis consists of $\gamma_i u^j$, where the $\gamma_i$ form an <basis> of $\mathbb F_{p^f}$ and $0\le j<e$. Regrouping a Laurent series by its exponent modulo $e$ proves the basis assertion. When $p$ divides $e$ the extension can be inseparable; the valuation calculation still applies.

= Finite extensions of the complex Laurent series field
{parent=Laurent series field}
{title2=$L\cong\mathbb C((t^{1/n})),\quad[L:\mathbb C((t))]=n$}

A finite extension of the complete discretely valued field $\mathbb C((t))$ has residue field $\mathbb C$, hence is totally ramified. If $\pi$ is its uniformizer, write $t=u\pi^n$. A root of the residue of $u$ lifts by <Hensel lemma> to an $n$th root $v$ of $u$, since the residue characteristic is zero. Then $s=v\pi$ satisfies $s^n=t$. Distinct valuations modulo $n$ make $1,s,\ldots,s^{n-1}$ linearly independent over the base, so $L=\mathbb C((t))(s)$. Expanding in $s$ with constant coefficients identifies it with $\mathbb C((s))$. Its automorphisms are $s\mapsto\zeta_ns$, giving a cyclic Galois group.

= Absolute Galois group of the complex Laurent series field
{parent=Finite extensions of the complex Laurent series field}
{title2=$\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb C((t))}/\mathbb C((t)))\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$}

Inside an algebraic closure, choose compatible roots $t^{1/n}$. The fields $\mathbb C((t^{1/n}))$ exhaust all finite extensions by <finite extensions of the complex Laurent series field>. Restriction from a degree-$m$ field to a degree-$n$ field for $n\mid m$ sends an exponent modulo $m$ to its reduction modulo $n$. The <absolute Galois group> is their <inverse limit>, namely the <profinite completion> of $\mathbb Z$. This is a procyclic group, rather than the direct-limit torsion group of all complex roots of unity.

= Completion at an irreducible polynomial over a finite field
{parent=Laurent series field}

For an irreducible polynomial $h\in\mathbb F_p[t]$ of degree $d$, the $h$-adic completion of $\mathbb F_p(t)$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb F_{p^d}((T))$. Its residue field is $\mathbb F_p[t]/(h)$ and $h$ is a uniformizer.

{synonym}
{c}

The Teichmuller representative of a nonzero residue class $\bar a\in\mathbb F_q^*$ is the unique lift $\omega$ satisfying $\omega^{q-1}=1$ and $\omega\equiv a$ modulo the maximal ideal.

= Finite prime-power Teichmuller representative
{parent=Teichmuller representative}
{title2=$y\equiv x^{p^{n-1}}\pmod{p^n}$}

For a prime $p$ and $n\geq1$, the unique residue $y$ modulo $p^n$ satisfying $y\equiv x\pmod p$ and $y^p\equiv y\pmod{p^n}$ is the displayed residue. Existence follows from <Fermat's little theorem> and <prime-power contraction under pth powers>. Uniqueness follows by raising any other such residue repeatedly to the $p$th power and applying the same contraction to its congruence with $x$. This is the finite-modulus version of a <Teichmuller representative>, including the zero class.

= Approximation of Teichmuller lifts by iterated pth powers
{title2=$v_K(y_n^{p^n}-[x])\geq n+1$}
{parent=Teichmuller representative}

For $K/\mathbb Q_p$ finite, let $x_n^{p^n}=x$ in its finite <residue field>, and choose arbitrary lifts $y_n\in\mathcal O_K$. With normalized <discrete valuation>,
$$
v_K(u^p-v^p)\geq\min\{v_K(p)+r,pr\}\geq r+1\quad\text{if }v_K(u-v)\geq r\geq1.
$$
Applying this binomial estimate repeatedly to $y_n$ and $[x_n]$ proves the displayed bound, and hence convergence to $[x]$.

= Teichmuller lift properties
{c}
{title2=$[a]\bmod\mathfrak m_K=a,\quad[a]^q=[a]$}
{parent=Teichmuller representative}

For residue field $\mathbb F_q$, these two properties characterize the <Teichmuller lift> on every residue class, including zero. The <Hensel lemma> applies to $T^q-T$, whose derivative reduces to $-1$. Uniqueness implies $[ab]=[a][b]$, $[0]=0$, $[1]=1$, and $[a^p]=[a]^p$. In mixed characteristic this multiplicative section need not be additive.

= Teichmuller expansion

If $K$ is a complete <discretely valued field> whose residue field $k$ is a <perfect field>, and $\pi$ is a <uniformizer>, the multiplicative Teichmuller lift $[\mathord\cdot]:k\to\mathcal O_K$ gives each $x\in\mathcal O_K$ a unique expansion
$$
x=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}[a_i]\pi^i,
\qquad a_i\in k.
$$

= Equal-characteristic complete discretely valued field

If a complete discretely valued field $K$ has positive characteristic and perfect residue field $k$, its <Teichmuller lift> is the unique ring section $k\to\mathcal O_K$. A choice of <uniformizer> $t$ then gives
$$
\mathcal O_K\cong k[[t]],
\qquad K\cong k((t)).

= Finite coefficient field in positive-characteristic local fields

{title2=$\mathbb F_q\hookrightarrow\mathcal O_K,\qquad a^q=a$}

For a characteristic-p <local field> with <residue field> $\mathbb F_q$, the simple roots of $X^q-X$ lift uniquely by <Hensel lemma>. = Unramified extension
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

= Unramified
{synonym}

A finite extension of local fields is unramified when its ramification index is one and its residue-field degree equals the field degree. A local field has a unique unramified extension of each positive degree inside a fixed algebraic closure.

= Unramified extensions remain unramified under base change
{parent=Unramified extension}
{title2=$L/K\text{ unramified}\ \Longrightarrow\ LE/E\text{ unramified}$}

For non-Archimedean <local fields>, let $L/K$ be an <unramified extension> of degree $f$, and let $E/K$ be finite. The degree-$f$ <unramified extension> of $E$ has a <residue field> containing the degree-$f$ residue extension of $K$. Lift an integral residue generator of $L$ into that field by <Hensel lemma>. This embeds $L$ into an <unramified extension> of $E$, so $LE/E$ is an <unramified> subextension. Applying this argument to each <completion of a number field at a prime ideal> proves the finite-prime assertion for <number fields>, even when $E/K$ is ramified.

= Lifting residue-field embeddings of unramified extensions
{parent=Unramified extension}

For an <unramified extension> $L/K$ of non-Archimedean <local fields> and any finite extension $M/K$, reduction is a bijection between $K$-<field homomorphisms> $L\to M$ and $k_K$-<field homomorphisms> $k_L\to k_M$. Choose an integral generator with separable irreducible reduction. Each specified residue image lifts uniquely to a root in $M$ by <Hensel lemma>. Conversely a $K$-embedding preserves the unique extended <valuation> and therefore induces the residue embedding. The same argument applies when $M$ is any non-Archimedean local extension with the given compatible embedding of $K$.

= Unramified generator criterion with separable reduction
{parent=Unramified extension}

A finite extension of complete discretely valued fields is <unramified> exactly when it has an integral generator whose reduced <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element> is separable. In one direction, lift a residue-field primitive element. In the other, the factorization form of <Hensel's lemma> makes the separable reduction irreducible, forcing residue degree to equal the entire extension degree.

= Unramified residue generator integral basis
{parent=Unramified generator criterion with separable reduction}
{title2=$\mathcal O_L=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{f-1}\mathcal O_K\alpha^j$}

For an <unramified extension> of <local fields> $L/K$, an integral element whose residue generates $k_L/k_K$ generates $L/K$: its degree is at least the <residue degree> $f=[L:K]$. The reduced powers $1,\bar\alpha,\ldots,\bar\alpha^{f-1}$ are a <basis> of the <residue field> extension. If $x=\sum c_j\alpha^j$, factor out a coefficient of least <valuation>. Reduction of the remaining sum is nonzero by that residue <basis>, so $v_L(x)=\min_j v_K(c_j)$. Consequently $x$ is integral exactly when all $c_j$ are integral.

= Residue-basis norm formula for an unramified extension
{parent=Unramified residue generator integral basis}
{title2=$|\sum_j a_jx^j|=\max_j|a_j|$}

Let $L/K$ be a finite <unramified extension> of complete fields with a <non-Archimedean absolute value>, with separable residue extension and $[L:K]=[k_L:k_K]=n$. If $x$ is integral and its residue generates $k_L/k_K$, then $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ form a $K$-basis. Divide any nonzero combination by a coefficient of largest absolute value. Its remaining coefficients are integral, at least one is a unit, and the residue-basis independence makes the reduction nonzero. The displayed norm formula follows. In particular, the combination is integral exactly when every coefficient is integral, proving $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_K[x]$ without requiring a discrete valuation.

= Existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions
{parent=Unramified extension}

For a non-Archimedean <local field> $K$, every finite extension $\ell/k_K$ of its <residue field> is realized by an <unramified extension> of $K$, unique up to $K$-isomorphism. Lift the irreducible polynomial of a residue generator to a monic integral polynomial and adjoin a root. Its residue degree equals its field degree, forcing ramification index one. In any other such extension the <Hensel lemma> lifts the same simple residue root, giving an isomorphism.

= Frobenius permutation and roots modulo a prime
{c}
{parent=Frobenius conjugacy class}
{title2=$f\bmod p\text{ has a root}\iff\operatorname{Frob}_p\text{ fixes a root}$}

Let a monic integer <polynomial> have distinct roots in its <splitting field>. Outside the finitely many primes dividing its <polynomial discriminant> or ramifying in that field, its roots reduce to distinct residue roots. The <Frobenius automorphism> permutes them by $a\mapsto a^p$; a reduced root belongs to $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when it is fixed. Distinctness of reduction identifies this with a fixed root of the Galois permutation itself. Combining this observation with a <derangement in a transitive group action> and the <Chebotarev density theorem> proves that an irreducible polynomial of degree at least two has rootless reduction at a positive <Dirichlet density> of primes. The degree bound is essential: every monic linear integer polynomial has a root modulo every prime.

= Positive ramification quotients are elementary abelian
{parent=Ramification group}
{title2=$G_i/G_{i+1}\hookrightarrow(k_L,+),\quad i\ge1$}

For $\sigma\in G_i$, the residue of $(\sigma\pi_L-\pi_L)/\pi_L^{i+1}$ gives a homomorphism to the additive residue field with kernel $G_{i+1}$. = Ramification of the splitting field of X3 plus pX plus p
{parent=Ramification group}
{title2=$X^3+pX+p$}

For an odd prime $p$, this <Eisenstein polynomial> generates a totally ramified cubic root field. Its <discriminant> is $-p^2(4p+27)$. For $p>3$ the unit part is a square exactly when $p\equiv1\pmod3$. The <splitting field> then has cyclic <Galois group> of order three; otherwise it has <symmetric group> $S_3$, with an <unramified extension> of degree two and <inertia group> $C_3$. Both cases are <tamely ramified>, so all positive <ramification groups> are trivial.

At $p=3$, adjoining $\beta=\sqrt{-39}$ gives a ramified quadratic subfield, and the <splitting field> has total <ramification index> six. A root $\alpha$ has normalized <valuation> two, while $\beta$ has <valuation> three, so $\pi=\beta/\alpha$ is a <uniformizer>. The derivative $3\alpha^2+3$ has <valuation> six, and all distinct root differences have <valuation> three. A three-cycle fixes $\beta$, giving $v_L(\sigma\pi-\pi)=2$. A transposition fixing $\alpha$ negates $\beta$, giving <valuation> one; conjugation handles the other transpositions. Hence $G_0=S_3$, $G_1=C_3$, and $G_i=1$ for $i\ge2$. The <different exponent> is seven.

= Lower ramification filtration of a prime-power cyclotomic extension
{parent=Ramification group}
{title2=$v_L(\sigma_a(\zeta-1)-(\zeta-1))=p^{v_p(a-1)}$}

For $L=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^n})$, the <uniformiser> $\zeta_{p^n}-1$ and the <uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups> give the displayed <valuation> for $a\ne1$. Thus $G_0$ is the full unit group modulo $p^n$, and $G_i$ consists of units congruent to one modulo $p^j$ when $p^{j-1}\leq i\leq p^j-1$, $1\leq j<n$. All groups are trivial for $i\geq p^{n-1}$. The formula includes $p=2$, where the first congruence subgroup is already the full group.

= Ramification break of an Artin–Schreier pole
{parent=Uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups}
{title2=$b_{\rm lower}=b_{\rm upper}=m$}

Let $K$ be an equal-characteristic-p <local field> and let $c\in K$ have $v_K(c)=-m<0$, with $p\nmid m$. If $\alpha^p-\alpha=c$, the <Artin–Schreier extension> $L=K(\alpha)$ has degree $p$ and is totally ramified: a root in $K$ would force the pole order to be divisible by $p$, and $p v_L(\alpha)=-m e(L/K)$ forces $e=p$. Choose $1\leq s<p$ and an integer $r$ with $rp-sm=1$. For a base <uniformizer> $t$, the element $\pi_L=t^r\alpha^s$ has <valuation> one. Under $\sigma_a(\alpha)=\alpha+a$, $a\in\mathbb F_p^\times$, the leading term in $\sigma_a(\pi_L)-\pi_L$ has <valuation> $rp-(s-1)m=m+1$. The <uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups> therefore gives the single lower break $m$. The <Herbrand function> is the identity up to that break, so the upper break is also $m$.

= Ramification groups of the splitting field of Xp minus p over the p-adic numbers
{title2=$\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p,\sqrt[p]{p})/\mathbb Q_p$}
{parent=Uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups}

For odd $p$, the splitting field $K=\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p,\sqrt[p]{p})$ is totally ramified of degree $p(p-1)$. With
$$
\varpi=\frac{\zeta_p-1}{\sqrt[p]{p}},
$$
the lower ramification groups are
$$
G_0=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q_p),\qquad
G_i\cong\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\ (1\leq i\leq p),\qquad
G_i=1\ (i\geq p+1).
$$

To verify the filtration, write $\alpha^p=p$ and $\lambda=\zeta_p-1$. The <Eisenstein polynomials> for $\alpha$ and $\lambda$ give coprime degrees $p$ and $p-1$, so their compositum has degree $p(p-1)$. Its element $\varpi=\lambda/\alpha$ has $p$-adic <valuation> $1/[p(p-1)]$, forcing total ramification. For an automorphism $\sigma_{a,b}$ with $\alpha\mapsto\zeta_p^a\alpha$ and $\zeta_p\mapsto\zeta_p^b$,
$$
\frac{\sigma_{a,b}(\varpi)}{\varpi}=\zeta_p^{-a}\frac{\zeta_p^b-1}{\zeta_p-1}.
$$
Its residue is $b$, so the normalized displacement <valuation> is one if $b\ne1$. If $b=1$ and $a\ne0$, that valuation is $1+v_K(\zeta_p^{-a}-1)=p+1$. The <uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups> now gives every group displayed above.

= Denominator criterion for Newton polygon irreducibility
{parent=Newton polygon}

If a monic degree-$n$ polynomial over a discretely valued field with normalized valuation has all its roots of valuation $a/n$, where $\gcd(a,n)=1$, then it is irreducible. Each irreducible factor of degree $d$ has constant term of valuation $da/n\in\mathbb Z$, forcing $n\mid d$. Equivalently, a single <Newton polygon> segment of relatively prime horizontal and vertical lengths gives irreducibility.

= Ramification index
{title2=$e(L/K)$}
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

For a finite extension $L/K$ of non-Archimedean local fields, the ramification index $e(L/K)$ is the index of the value group of $K$ in that of $L$. Normalized valuations satisfy $v_L|_K=e(L/K)v_K$.

= Ramification forced by a coprime root valuation
{parent=Ramification index}
{title2=$\alpha^m=a,\quad\gcd(m,v_p(a))=1\implies m\mid e_{\mathfrak p/p}$}

Suppose $\alpha^m=a\in\mathbb Q^\times$ in a <number field> and $\mathfrak p$ lies above $p$. Its normalized integral <valuation> satisfies $m v_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha)=e_{\mathfrak p/p}v_p(a)$. If $m$ and $v_p(a)$ are coprime, then $m\mid e_{\mathfrak p/p}$. In a degree-$m$ field the <fundamental identity for prime decomposition> forces a unique prime, <ramification index> $m$, and <residue degree> one. This detects total <ramification of a prime> even when the displayed defining polynomial is not <Eisenstein> at $p$.

The residue-field degree of a finite extension of local fields is $f(L/K)=[k_L:k_K]$, and $[L:K]=e(L/K)f(L/K)$.

= Totally ramified extension
{parent=Ramification index}
{wiki}

= Totally ramified
{synonym}

A finite extension of local fields is totally ramified when its <residue-field degree> is one, equivalently when its <ramification index> equals its field degree.

= Unramified base change preserves total ramification
{parent=Totally ramified extension}

If $L/K$ is an <unramified extension> and $M/K$ is a <totally ramified extension> of non-Archimedean <local fields> inside a fixed <algebraic closure>, then $LM/L$ is totally ramified of degree $[M:K]$. A <uniformizer> of $M$ has an <Eisenstein polynomial> over $K$. Since a <uniformizer> of $K$ remains a <uniformizer> in $L$, that polynomial remains Eisenstein over $L$ and generates $LM$.

= Cyclic quartic local extension without a totally ramified complement
{parent=Unramified base change preserves total ramification}
{title2=$N=\mathbb Q_3(\alpha),\quad \alpha^4-6\alpha^2+18=0$}

Let $K=\mathbb Q_3$, $L=K(i)$ with $i^2=-1$, and $\alpha^2=3(1+i)$. The extension $L/K$ is <unramified> quadratic, whereas $N=L(\alpha)$ is <totally ramified> quadratic over $L$, by the <Eisenstein polynomial> $T^2-3(1+i)$. Also $i=\alpha^2/3-1$, so $N=K(\alpha)$. The <Hensel lemma> gives $r\in K$ with $r^2=-2$. Put $\beta=(1+i)/r$; then $\beta^2=-i$ and $\beta\overline\beta=-1$. Consequently $i\mapsto-i$, $\alpha\mapsto\beta\alpha$ defines an automorphism whose square fixes $i$ and negates $\alpha$. It generates the cyclic <Galois group> of order four. Its unique quadratic subfield is $L$, which is unramified. A decomposition as an unramified field and a totally ramified field would require a totally ramified quadratic subfield, and therefore cannot exist.

= Eisenstein generator of a totally ramified extension
{c}
{parent=Totally ramified extension}

A finite extension $L/K$ of complete discretely valued fields is totally ramified exactly when $L=K(\alpha)$ for a root $\alpha$ of an <Eisenstein polynomial> over $\mathcal O_K$. In the reverse direction one may take $\alpha$ to be any <uniformizer> of $L$.

A p-adic unit is an element of $\mathbb Z_p^\times$, equivalently a p-adic integer of valuation zero.

= Unit square classes of the p-adic integers
{parent=p-adic unit}
{title2=$\mathbb Z_p^\times/(\mathbb Z_p^\times)^2$}

= p-adic unit square-class group
{synonym}

For odd $p$, a <p-adic unit> is a square exactly when its residue is a square, by the <Hensel lemma>. Its unit square-class group is $C_2$. For $p=2$, a unit is a square exactly when it is congruent to one modulo eight, by the <strong form of Hensel lemma>. The four <square classes> are represented by $1,3,5,7$, giving $C_2\times C_2$.

= 2-adic unit
{title2=$\mathbb Z_2^\times$}
{parent=p-adic unit}

A 2-adic unit is an odd element of $\mathbb Z_2$. It is a square in $\mathbb Z_2^\times$ exactly when it is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$.

= Digit lifting for square roots of 2-adic units
{parent=2-adic unit}
{title2=$x_{n+1}=x_n+\varepsilon_n2^{n-1}$}

An odd square is congruent to one modulo eight. Conversely, for $u\equiv1\pmod8$, begin with $x_3=1$ and $x_n^2\equiv u\pmod{2^n}$. For $n\ge3$, adding $\varepsilon_n2^{n-1}$ changes the square by $\varepsilon_n2^nx_n$ modulo $2^{n+1}$. Since $x_n$ is odd, one choice of $\varepsilon_n\in\{0,1\}$ gives the next congruence. The corrections tend to zero 2-adically, so completeness produces an odd square root. This directly proves the unit square criterion without needing a unit derivative in <Hensel lemma>.

= Square-class group of the 2-adic numbers
{title2=$\mathbb Q_2^\times/(\mathbb Q_2^\times)^2$}
{parent=2-adic unit}

An odd $2$-adic unit is a square exactly when it is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$. Valuation parity and the four odd residue classes modulo $8$ therefore give
$$
\mathbb Q_2^\times/(\mathbb Q_2^\times)^2
\cong(\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z)^3,
$$
with generators represented by $-1$, $2$, and $5$.

= Quadratic extensions of the 2-adic numbers
{parent=Square-class group of the 2-adic numbers}
{title2=$\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt d),\quad d=-1,5,-5,2,-2,10,-10$}

The three generators $-1,2,5$ of the <square-class group of the 2-adic numbers> give these seven distinct extensions. In characteristic different from two, completing the square shows every quadratic extension is of this form, and comparing coefficients in $\sqrt e=a+b\sqrt d$ proves that two such fields coincide precisely when $e/d$ is a square. The only unramified member is $\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt5)$: $(1+\sqrt5)/2$ is integral with irreducible reduction $X^2+X+1$. Conversely any unramified quadratic extension has that residue field, and <Hensel lemma> lifts a residue root of $X^2-X-1$, recovering the same field.

= Ramification in the quadratic extension generated by the square root of two
{parent=Quadratic extensions of the 2-adic numbers}
{title2=$G_1=G_2=C_2,\qquad G_i=1\ (i\ge3)$}

In $L=\mathbb Q_2(\sqrt2)$, set $\pi=\sqrt2$ and normalize $v_L(\pi)=1$, so $v_L(2)=2$. The two terms of $a+b\pi$ have valuations of opposite parity, giving $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z_2\oplus\mathbb Z_2\pi$. The nonidentity automorphism sends $\pi$ to $-\pi$, and $v_L(-2\pi)=3$. The <lower ramification numbering> therefore gives the displayed groups. The different exponent is three.

= pth-power criterion for p-adic units
{parent=p-adic unit}

For an odd prime $p$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb Z_p^\times$,
$$
\alpha\in(\mathbb Z_p^\times)^p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\alpha\bmod p^2\in((\mathbb Z/p^2\mathbb Z)^\times)^p.
$$
This follows from $\mathbb Z_p^\times\cong\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb Z_p)$ and $(1+p\mathbb Z_p)^p=1+p^2\mathbb Z_p$.

= Principal unit
{parent=p-adic unit}
{wiki}

For a non-Archimedean local field $K$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_K$, a principal unit is an element of $1+\mathfrak m_K$. The higher principal-unit groups are $1+\mathfrak m_K^n$.

= Prime-to-residue-characteristic power map on principal units
{parent=Principal unit}
{title2=$u\mapsto u^m$}

In a complete <discrete valuation ring> of residue characteristic $p$, the power map $u\mapsto u^m$ is a bijection on $1+\mathfrak m$ when $p\nmid m$. For a <principal unit> $a$, the <Hensel lemma> lifts the simple residue root one of $T^m-a$ uniquely. This can absorb principal-unit factors in tame radical descriptions of <local fields>.

= Higher principal-unit group
{title2=$1+\mathfrak m_K^n$}
{parent=Principal unit}

For a non-Archimedean local field $K$, the $n$th higher principal-unit group is $1+\mathfrak m_K^n$. These groups form a decreasing filtration of the <principal units>.

= Successive quotients of principal-unit groups
{parent=Higher principal-unit group}
{title2=$U_r/U_{r+1}\cong(k,+),\quad U_r=1+\pi^r\mathcal O_K$}

For $r\geq1$, the map $1+\pi^r a\mapsto\bar a$ is a surjective group homomorphism to the additive <residue field> with kernel $U_{r+1}$. The extra product term is divisible by $\pi^{r+1}$. Thus each quotient has cardinality $|k|$, a useful count when splitting the <unit group> by roots of unity.

= Cube map on 3-adic principal units
{parent=Higher principal-unit group}
{title2=$(1+3\mathbb Z_3)^3=1+9\mathbb Z_3$}

The cube of $1+3t$ is $1+9(t+3t^2+3t^3)$. Conversely the <polynomial> $T+3T^2+3T^3-a$ has a unique simple residue root modulo three for each $a\in\mathbb Z_3$, so <Hensel lemma> produces a cube root of every $1+9a$. Thus the <principal units> modulo their cubes have order three. The change of variable removes the derivative obstruction that prevents direct simple-root lifting for $X^3-u$.

= Principal-unit logarithm
{parent=Principal unit}

For a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_p$ and all sufficiently large $r$, the <p-adic logarithm> and <p-adic exponential> are inverse group isomorphisms
$$
1+\mathfrak m_K^r\cong(\mathfrak m_K^r,+).
$$

{parent=p-adic unit}

The valuation and first principal-unit digit give
$$
\mathbb Q_3^\times/(\mathbb Q_3^\times)^3
\cong\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z.

= Roots of unity in a p-adic field
{title2=$\mu(K)$}
{parent=Local field}

The roots of unity in a finite extension $K/\mathbb Q_p$ form a finite group. Each element of their prime-to-$p$ part is a <Teichmuller representative>, while any $p$-power part lies among the <principal units>.

= Valuation of a primitive p-power root of unity
{parent=Roots of unity in a p-adic field}
{title2=$v(\zeta_{p^a}-1)=v(p)/(p^{a-1}(p-1))$}

In a mixed-characteristic <discretely valued field>, a primitive $p^a$th <root of unity> reduces to one. For every $j$ prime to $p$, the ratio $(\zeta^j-1)/(\zeta-1)$ is a unit, so all factors of $\Phi_{p^a}(1)=p$ have the same valuation. Taking valuations gives the formula. Because the left side is a positive integer for a normalized valuation, the existence of such a root forces $p^{a-1}(p-1)\mid v(p)$, bounding the possible $p$-power torsion.

= Roots of unity at the tame ramification boundary
{parent=Valuation of a primitive p-power root of unity}

For odd $p$, both possibilities occur at $v(p)=p-1$: $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p)$ contains $p$-torsion, while $\mathbb Q_p(\sqrt[p-1]{p})$ does not. Both extensions are totally ramified of degree $p-1$ by the <Eisenstein criterion>. In the second field, if $\zeta_p=1+u\pi$ with $\pi^{p-1}=p$, the cyclotomic equation reduced after division by $p$ would give $1+\bar u^{p-1}=0$ in $\mathbb F_p$, contradicting $\bar u^{p-1}=1$. For $p=2$, $v(2)=1$ always permits the root $-1$, so the torsion exponent is exactly one and the zero-exponent alternative is impossible.

= Ramification bound for a primitive pth root of unity
{parent=Roots of unity in a p-adic field}
{title2=$e(L/\mathbb Q_p)\geq p-1$}

If a finite extension of the <p-adic numbers> contains a primitive $p$th <root of unity> $\zeta$, the identity $p=\prod_{j=1}^{p-1}(1-\zeta^j)$ gives $e=(p-1)v_L(1-\zeta)\geq p-1$. The ratios of these factors reduce to the nonzero integers $j$ in the <residue field>. Thus an extension with $e<p-1$ has only roots of unity of order prime to $p$.

= Roots of unity in the p-adic numbers
{parent=Roots of unity in a p-adic field}
{title2=$\mu(\mathbb Q_p)=\mu_{p-1}\ (p>2),\quad \mu(\mathbb Q_2)=\mu_2$}

For odd $p$, <Teichmuller representatives> provide the $p-1$ prime-to-$p$ roots, while the <logarithm isomorphism on deep principal units> makes $1+p\mathbb Z_p$ torsion-free. At two, the decomposition $\mathbb Z_2^\times=\{1,-1\}\times(1+4\mathbb Z_2)$ leaves exactly two roots.

= Unit decomposition of the 2-adic Gaussian field
{parent=Roots of unity in a p-adic field}
{title2=$\mathbb Z_2[i]^\times=\mu_4\times(1+(1-i)^3\mathbb Z_2[i])$}

The <Eisenstein polynomial> of $1-i$ is $T^2-2T+2$, so $\mathbb Q_2(i)$ has <residue field> $\mathbb F_2$ and <ramification index> two. The four roots $1,i,-1,-i$ have distinct cosets modulo $U_3$, which has index four by the <successive quotients of principal-unit groups>. The <logarithm isomorphism on deep principal units> gives $U_3\cong(\mathbb Z_2[i],+)$. Hence these four roots split the full unit group, and are all its torsion.

= Unit group of Q3 zeta3

For $K=\mathbb Q_3(\zeta_3)$, the element $\pi=\zeta_3-1$ is a <uniformizer> and

= Lubin–Tate formal group
{title2=$\mathcal F_f$}
{c}
{parent=Local field}
{wiki}

A <Lubin–Tate formal group> associated with a <Lubin–Tate series> $f$ is the unique one-dimensional commutative <formal group law> $\mathcal F_f$ over the <valuation ring> $\mathcal O_K$ for which $f$ is an <endomorphism> with linear coefficient the chosen <uniformizer> $\pi$. The <Lubin–Tate functional equation lemma> constructs the law and the <endomorphisms> $[a]_f$ for every $a\in\mathcal O_K$, with linear term $aX$. Their addition is given by the law, and their multiplication by composition. Thus the <valuation ring> acts on the group; $[\pi]_f=f$.

= Lubin–Tate series
{c}
{parent=Lubin–Tate formal group}
{title2=$f(T)$}

For a <uniformizer> $\pi$ of a non-Archimedean <local field> $K$ with <residue field> of cardinality $q$, a <Lubin–Tate series> is a series $f(T)\in\mathcal O_K[[T]]$ satisfying
$$
f(T)\equiv\pi T\pmod{T^2},\qquad f(T)\equiv T^q\pmod\pi.
$$
It determines the associated two-variable <Lubin–Tate formal group>. A useful example is $f(T)=\pi T+T^q$. For $K=\mathbb Q_p$ and $\pi=p$, the choice $(1+T)^p-1$ gives the <formal multiplicative group> and the <Lubin–Tate cyclotomic example>.

= Lubin–Tate change of series
{c}
{parent=Lubin–Tate formal group}

If $f$ and $g$ are Lubin–Tate series for the same <uniformizer>, there is a unique strict isomorphism $h:\mathcal F_f\to\mathcal F_g$ satisfying $h\circ f=g\circ h$ and $h(X)\equiv X\pmod{X^2}$.

= Lubin–Tate functional equation lemma
{c}
{parent=Lubin–Tate change of series}

Given Lubin–Tate series $f,g$ for $\pi$ and a linear form $L=\sum a_iX_i$, there is a unique power series $F\equiv L$ modulo terms of degree two satisfying
$$
f(F(X_1,\ldots,X_n))=F(g(X_1),\ldots,g(X_n)).
$$
Comparison degree by degree determines each homogeneous term; the Lubin–Tate congruences provide the required divisibility by $\pi$.

= Lubin–Tate torsion
{title2=$\mu_{f,n}$}
{c}
{parent=Lubin–Tate formal group}

The $\pi^n$-torsion of a <Lubin–Tate formal group> is
$$
\mu_{f,n}=\{x\in\overline{\mathfrak m}: [\pi^n]_f(x)=0\}.
$$
It is a free rank-one module over $\mathcal O_K/\pi^n\mathcal O_K$.

= Lubin–Tate extension
{parent=Lubin–Tate torsion}
{c}
{title2=$K_{\pi,n}=K(\mathcal F[\pi^n])$}

Fix a <uniformizer> $\pi$ of a <local field> $K$ and a <Lubin–Tate formal group> $\mathcal F$. Its nth torsion field is generated by all its $\pi^n$-torsion points. A primitive point $\lambda_n$ generates the <Lubin–Tate torsion> as a free rank-one $\mathcal O_K/\pi^n$-<module>; therefore $K_{\pi,n}=K(\lambda_n)$. If the residue field has size $q$, the <Eisenstein layers of Lubin–Tate torsion> give $[K_{\pi,n}:K]=(q-1)q^{n-1}$ and total ramification.

= Lubin–Tate cyclotomic example
{parent=Lubin–Tate extension}
{c}
{title2=$\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^n})$}

For $K=\mathbb Q_p$ and $\pi=p$, choose the <Lubin–Tate series> $f(T)=(1+T)^p-1$. The associated <formal group law> is the <formal multiplicative group>. Its torsion points are $\zeta-1$ for $\zeta^{p^n}=1$, so its <Lubin–Tate extension> is the <cyclotomic extension of a p-adic field> $\mathbb Q_p(\mu_{p^n})$. The Galois action $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^a$ gives $(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)^{\times}$, including the trivial first layer when $p=2$.

= Lubin–Tate tower
{parent=Lubin–Tate extension}
{c}
{title2=$K_\pi=\bigcup_{n\geq1}K_{\pi,n}$}

Choose compatible primitive <Lubin–Tate torsion> points with $[\pi]\lambda_{n+1}=\lambda_n$. Restriction of the <Lubin–Tate Galois action on primitive torsion> is reduction of <unit> classes modulo $\pi^n$. The <inverse limit> gives
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(K_\pi/K)\simeq\varprojlim_n(\mathcal O_K/\pi^n)^{\times}\simeq\mathcal O_K^{\times}
$$
as topological groups. This describes the <Galois group> without invoking local reciprocity.

= Lubin–Tate Galois action on primitive torsion
{parent=Lubin–Tate extension}
{c}
{title2=$\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\pi,n}/K)\simeq(\mathcal O_K/\pi^n)^{\times}$}

For a primitive <Lubin–Tate torsion> point $\lambda_n$, every Galois automorphism has $\sigma(\lambda_n)=[a_\sigma](\lambda_n)$ for a unique <unit> class $a_\sigma\pmod{\pi^n}$. All torsion points lie in $K(\lambda_n)$ because the integral endomorphism series converge there; hence the torsion field is Galois. The displayed map is an injective homomorphism. Its domain has order $(q-1)q^{n-1}$ by the primitive Eisenstein <polynomial>, equal to the number of <unit> classes, so it is an isomorphism.

= Eisenstein layers of Lubin–Tate torsion
{c}
{parent=Lubin–Tate torsion}

For $f(X)=\pi X+X^q$ and its iterates $f_n$, the quotient
$$
\Phi_n(X)=\frac{f_n(X)}{f_{n-1}(X)}
=\pi+f_{n-1}(X)^{q-1}
$$
is Eisenstein. A nonzero root of exact level $n$ therefore generates a totally ramified separable extension.

= First Lubin–Tate torsion fields for the p-adic numbers
{parent=Lubin–Tate torsion}

The two Lubin–Tate series $(1+X)^p-1$ and $X^p+pX$ for $\mathbb Q_p$ have respective nonzero $p$-torsion points $\zeta_p-1$ and the roots of $X^{p-1}=-p$. A <Lubin–Tate change of series> identifies their torsion fields, so
$$
\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_p)=\mathbb Q_p(\sqrt[p-1]{-p}).
$$

The maximal abelian extension $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$ of a field $K$ is the compositum in a fixed separable closure of all finite abelian extensions of $K$.

= Weil group
{title2=$W_K$}
{c}
{parent=Maximal abelian extension}
{wiki}

For a non-Archimedean local field $K$, the Weil group is the inverse image of the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by Frobenius under $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K/K)\to\operatorname{Gal}(\overline k/k)\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$. It is dense in the absolute Galois group with its profinite topology.

= Relative Weil group
{title2=$W(L/K)$}
{c}
{parent=Weil group}

For a Galois extension $L/K$ of non-Archimedean local fields, the relative Weil group consists of the automorphisms whose residue action is an integral power of <Frobenius>. It fits into
$$
1\longrightarrow I(L/K)\longrightarrow W(L/K)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1
$$
when the residue extension is the full algebraic extension.

= Weil-group topology
{parent=Relative Weil group}

The Weil-group topology makes the <inertia group> open with its profinite topology and makes the quotient by inertia discrete. It is generally finer than the topology induced from the ambient Galois group.

= Cyclotomic extension of a p-adic field
{parent=Maximal abelian extension}

= p-adic cyclotomic extension
{synonym}

The cyclotomic extension $\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_{p^n})/\mathbb Q_p$ is generated by a primitive $p^n$th root of unity. It is totally ramified and has Galois group $(\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z)^\times$.

= Total ramification criterion for local cyclotomic extensions
{parent=Cyclotomic extension of a p-adic field}
{title2=$\mathbb Q_p(\zeta_n)/\mathbb Q_p\text{ is totally ramified}\iff n=p^a m,\ m\mid p-1$}

Here $a\geq0$ and $p\nmid m$. The prime-to-$p$ <roots of unity> generate an <unramified extension> of degree equal to the multiplicative order of $p$ modulo $m$; for $m=1$ this degree is one. Indeed, their distinct residues lift uniquely by <Hensel lemma>, and the <Frobenius automorphism> acts by raising them to $p$. The $p$-power <roots of unity> generate a <totally ramified extension>, because the translated <cyclotomic polynomial> $\Phi_{p^a}(1+T)$ is Eisenstein. The two fields have trivial intersection and their compositum has the same residue degree as the prime-to-$p$ field, by <unramified base change preserves total ramification>. Thus total ramification is equivalent to $p\equiv1\pmod m$.

= Local Kronecker-Weber theorem
{c}

The local Kronecker-Weber theorem states that every finite abelian extension of $\mathbb Q_p$ is contained in an extension obtained by adjoining roots of unity.

= Local class field theory

Local class field theory describes the abelian extensions of a local field $K$ through its multiplicative group $K^\times$.

= Existence theorem of local class field theory
{parent=Local class field theory}

The finite-index open subgroups of $K^\times$ are exactly the norm subgroups $N_{L/K}(L^\times)$ for finite abelian extensions $L/K$. The subgroup determines $L$ uniquely inside $K^{\mathrm{ab}}$.

= Local Artin reciprocity
{c}

Local Artin reciprocity gives a continuous homomorphism $\operatorname{Art}_K:K^\times\to\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{ab}}/K)$ with dense image. For every finite abelian extension $L/K$, it induces an isomorphism
$$
K^\times/N_{L/K}(L^\times)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(L/K).
$$

= Hilbert norm residue symbol
{c}
{parent=Local Artin reciprocity}
{title2=$(a,b)_{F,m}$}

For a <local field> $F$ containing $\mu_m$, the symbol is $\operatorname{rec}_F(b)(a^{1/m})/a^{1/m}\in\mu_m$, after fixing a convention for <local Artin reciprocity>. It is independent of the chosen root and bilinear. Its kernel in the second variable is the norm subgroup of $F(a^{1/m})/F$. The <quadratic Hilbert symbol> is the sign-valued case relevant to quadratic forms.

= Quadratic Hilbert symbol
{parent=Hilbert norm residue symbol}
{title2=$(a,b)_v\in\{1,-1\}$}

The symbol is one exactly when $b$ is a norm from $K_v(\sqrt a)$, and is always one if $a$ is square. Equivalently the conic $z^2=ax^2+by^2$ is isotropic. This proves symmetry. The <cyclic local norm index> makes the norm subgroup have index two for nonsquare $a$, proving bilinearity and nondegeneracy on local square classes. Also $(a,-a)_v=1$ and $(a,a)_v=(a,-1)_v$.

= Explicit quadratic Hilbert symbols over the p-adic numbers
{parent=Quadratic Hilbert symbol}
{title2=$(p^\alpha u,p^\beta v)_p$}

For odd $p$ and <p-adic units> $u,v$, the <quadratic Hilbert symbol> is
$$
(p^\alpha u,p^\beta v)_p=(-1)^{\alpha\beta(p-1)/2}\left(\frac up\right)^\beta\left(\frac vp\right)^\alpha.
$$
The <Legendre symbols> record the two unit <square classes>. For $p=2$, put $\epsilon(u)=(u-1)/2\bmod2$ and $\omega(u)=(u^2-1)/8\bmod2$. Then
$$
(2^\alpha u,2^\beta v)_2=(-1)^{\epsilon(u)\epsilon(v)+\alpha\omega(v)+\beta\omega(u)}.
$$
The basis $-1,2,5$ of the <square-class group of the 2-adic numbers> gives this formula: its only negative basis pairings are $(-1,-1)_2$ and $(2,5)_2=(5,2)_2$. The first is obstructed by sums of squares modulo four, the second by $x^2-2y^2$ modulo eight; the remaining pairings have explicit <field norm> representatives.

= Hilbert-symbol annihilators of units and unramified square classes
{c}
{parent=Quadratic Hilbert symbol}
{title2=$\Delta_{\rm units}^{\perp}=\Delta_{\rm unr},\quad\Delta_{\rm unr}^{\perp}=\Delta_{\rm units}$}

In the <square-class group of a p-adic field>, the unit <subgroup> consists of classes of even <valuation>. The unramified <subgroup> consists of the trivial class and the unique nonsquare class defining an <unramified extension> of degree two. For odd $p$, the two <subgroups> coincide with $\{1,u\}$ for a nonsquare unit $u$. For $p=2$, they are $\{1,-1,5,-5\}$ and $\{1,5\}$. Norms from an unramified quadratic <local field> extension have even <valuation>, and every unit is a norm. The norm characterization of the <quadratic Hilbert symbol> therefore gives both displayed annihilator identities. Over $\mathbb Q_2$, the unramified class is $5$ because $(1+\sqrt5)/2$ satisfies $T^2-T-1$, whose reduction modulo two is irreducible and separable.

= Hilbert reciprocity law
{c}
{parent=Quadratic Hilbert symbol}
{title2=$\prod_v(a,b)_v=1$}

For nonzero $a,b$ in a <number field>, the product of their <quadratic Hilbert symbols> over all places is one. All but finitely many symbols are one. Over $\mathbb Q$, this is equivalent to quadratic reciprocity with its supplementary laws. It supplies the compatibility relation between the local invariants of a global quadratic form.

= Local Artin map

= Local reciprocity map

The local Artin map is the reciprocity homomorphism of <local Artin reciprocity>. Its normalization is fixed by choosing whether a <uniformizer> maps to arithmetic or geometric Frobenius.

= Cup-product construction of local reciprocity
{parent=Local Artin map}
{title2=$G^{\mathrm{ab}}\xrightarrow{\ \smile u_{L/K}\ }K^\times/N_{L/K}(L^\times)$}

For $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, <Tate cohomology of a finite group> identifies the source as $\widehat H^{-2}(G,\mathbb Z)$ and the target as $\widehat H^0(G,L^\times)$. Cup product with the <local fundamental class> is an isomorphism; its inverse is the finite <local Artin map>. The algebraic cup-product isomorphism applies because every subgroup has first cohomology zero by <Hilbert theorem 90>, and second cohomology cyclic of order equal to its size by the <local Brauer invariant>. Compatibility with restriction and corestriction yields the tower and norm compatibility of <local Artin reciprocity>.

= Lubin–Tate description of local reciprocity
{parent=Local Artin map}
{c}
{title2=$\operatorname{Art}_K(\pi^m u)|_{K_\pi}=[u^{-1}],\quad \operatorname{Art}_K(\pi^m u)|_{K^{\mathrm{nr}}}=\operatorname{Frob}_K^m$}

With the <arithmetic Frobenius> normalization, a chosen <uniformizer> $\pi$ and <Lubin–Tate formal group> give these actions on the <Lubin–Tate tower> and the <maximal unramified extension>. The two fields are linearly disjoint and their compositum is the <maximal abelian extension>. The inverse on units is essential: using geometric Frobenius would invert the entire <local Artin map>. The finite kernels on a compositum of residue degree $d$ and torsion level $r$ are $\pi^{d\mathbb Z}(1+\pi^r\mathcal O_K)$. They are a basis of finite-index open subgroups, yielding the <profinite completion> of $K^\times$ as the abelian <Galois group>.

= Norm subgroup of a local field extension
{title2=$N_{L/K}(L^\times)$}
{parent=Local Artin reciprocity}

For a finite extension of local fields $L/K$, its norm subgroup is the image of the <field norm> $N_{L/K}:L^\times\to K^\times$. For an abelian extension, it is the kernel of the induced local Artin map to $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$.

= Surjectivity of unit norms in unramified extensions
{parent=Norm subgroup of a local field extension}
{title2=$N_{L/K}(\mathcal O_L^\times)=\mathcal O_K^\times$}

For a finite <unramified extension> of non-Archimedean <local fields>, the <field norm> on residue units is surjective: on a cyclic group of order $q^n-1$ it is exponentiation by $(q^n-1)/(q-1)$ and has image of order $q-1$. For a common <uniformizer> $\pi$ and $r\geq1$, expansion of the conjugate product gives
$$
N_{L/K}(1+\pi^r a)\equiv1+\pi^r\operatorname{Tr}_{k_L/k_K}(\bar a)\pmod{\pi^{r+1}}.
$$
The <field trace> of a finite <finite field> extension is onto: its polynomial $X+X^q+\cdots+X^{q^{n-1}}$ is nonzero of degree below $q^n$, so defines a nonzero linear map to the base field. Lift a residue norm and correct successively with elements of $1+\pi^r\mathcal O_L$. The infinite product converges by <completeness>, and its norm is the prescribed unit. This argument also works in positive characteristic and when $p$ divides $n$.

= Cyclic local norm index
{parent=Norm subgroup of a local field extension}
{title2=$[K^\times:N_{L/K}L^\times]=[L:K]$}

For a cyclic extension of <p-adic fields>, the <Herbrand quotient of the local multiplicative group> together with <Hilbert theorem 90> gives this index. If the ramification index and residue degree are $e,f$, the norm valuation formula $v_K(Nx)=f v_L(x)$ then gives the unit norm index $e$. In particular all units are norms in an <unramified extension>.

= Norm-unit index in an abelian local extension
{parent=Norm subgroup of a local field extension}

If $L/K$ is a finite abelian extension of local fields, then
$$
[\mathcal O_K^\times:N_{L/K}(\mathcal O_L^\times)]=e(L/K).
$$
Indeed, <local Artin reciprocity> gives total norm index $[L:K]=ef$, while the valuation image of the norm subgroup is $f\mathbb Z$.

= Norm units in a tamely totally ramified extension
{parent=Norm subgroup of a local field extension}

For a tamely totally ramified extension of degree $q-1$ of a local field with residue field $\mathbb F_q$, the unit norms are exactly the principal units. Reduction sends a unit norm to its $(q-1)$st power, and the <norm-unit index in an abelian local extension> shows that this containment has the correct index.

= Norm subgroup of a 2-power cyclotomic extension of the 2-adic numbers
{title2=$N_{\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_{2^n})/\mathbb Q_2}(\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_{2^n})^\times)$}
{parent=Norm subgroup of a local field extension}

For every positive integer $n$,
$$
N_{\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_{2^n})/\mathbb Q_2}
\bigl(\mathbb Q_2(\zeta_{2^n})^\times\bigr)
=2^{\mathbb Z}(1+2^n\mathbb Z_2).
$$
Under <local Artin reciprocity>, the cyclotomic character identifies the quotient with $(\mathbb Z/2^n\mathbb Z)^\times$: a <uniformizer> acts trivially on the totally ramified extension, while a <2-adic unit> acts through its residue class modulo $2^n$.
