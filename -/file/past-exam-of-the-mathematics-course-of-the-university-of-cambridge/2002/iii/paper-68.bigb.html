= Solution
{parent=a}

Three independent motivations are **radiative stability, gauge unification and dark matter**. First, <supersymmetric cancellation of quadratic divergences> protects the Higgs <scalar mass> from the <hierarchy problem>; residual corrections are controlled by <soft supersymmetry breaking>, so avoiding large cancellations favours a breaking scale near the weak scale. Second, <supersymmetric gauge coupling unification> can reconcile the running of the three <Standard Model> couplings when <superpartner> thresholds lie near that scale. Third, with conserved <R-parity>, a stable <lightest supersymmetric particle> can provide <dark matter>, and weak-scale annihilation can produce an appropriate thermal abundance. These are conditional motivations, not proofs of a TeV threshold. Even without low-scale discovery, <supersymmetry> remains important **as a structure in supergravity and superstring theory**, potentially broken at a higher scale, and **as an exact calculational tool** through <superpotential non-renormalization>, <BPS states> and control of strongly coupled <quantum field theories>.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For physical finite-spin <one-particle states>, a positive-energy <Poincaré group> representation is labelled by mass and <spin> $(m,j)$ when $m>0$, or mass and <helicity> $(0,h)$ when massless. The individual states also carry their <four-momentum> and a spin projection. For <four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry>, the irreducible labels become $(m,j_{
m super})$, with $j_{
m super}$ the <superspin>, or $(0,h_{
m super})$, with a chosen <superhelicity> convention. The constituent representation labels are
$$
\boxed{\text{massive: }(m,j),(m,j),(m,j+\tfrac12),(m,j-\tfrac12),}
$$
with the last entry omitted for $j=0$, and
$$
\boxed{\text{massless: }(0,h),(0,h+\tfrac12).}
$$
A required <CPT completion of a supermultiplet> adds the conjugate labels $(0,-h),(0,-h-\tfrac12)$ and conjugates any internal charges.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

**Unbroken extended supersymmetry obstructs the observed chiral matter structure.** In four dimensions, the matter <hypermultiplets> of $\mathcal N\ge2$ theories contain conjugate <gauge group representations>, so their <fermions> are vectorlike rather than the chiral <Weyl fermions> of the <Standard Model>. <Extended supersymmetry> also relates interactions more strongly, restricting independent <Yukawa couplings> and <superpotentials>. It can occur at higher energies and break to $\mathcal N=1$ or to no <supersymmetry>; the objection is to its unbroken realization in an ordinary low-energy chiral particle theory.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}

In the **supersymmetric Higgs mechanism**, an internal <gauge symmetry> is broken by a charged <scalar field> expectation value while <supersymmetry> can remain unbroken. A massless <vector multiplet> absorbs a <chiral multiplet> to form a <massive N=1 vector multiplet>: the vector absorbs a scalar <Goldstone boson>, and the <gaugino> and matter <fermion> acquire the matching masses. In the **super-Higgs mechanism**, local <supersymmetry> itself is spontaneously broken: the <gravitino> absorbs the spin-$1/2$ <Goldstino> and becomes a massive spin-$3/2$ particle. In a flat vacuum this increases its physical states from two to four.

{parent=1}

= Solution
{parent=e}

**The supersymmetry algebra closes on spacetime translations.** Its <supercharges> are <Weyl spinors>, and
$$
\{Q_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta}\}=2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu.
$$
Thus the composition of two odd <supersymmetry transformations> moves the spacetime argument. The generators also change <spin> and connect <bosons> with <fermions>. They extend the <Poincaré group> to the <Super-Poincaré group>, rather than forming an internal symmetry that commutes with spacetime transformations.

{parent=1}

= Solution
{parent=f}

**Dependence on Grassmann variables alone does not specify a superfield transformation law.** A <superfield> is a function on <superspace>, with components transforming under the induced <supertranslation> and <Lorentz transformation> action. Any suitable superfunction can be an unconstrained <superfield> once equipped with that action; a fixed element of a <Grassmann algebra> with independently assigned components need not be covariant. A <chiral superfield>, for example,
$$
\Phi(y,\theta)=A(y)+\sqrt2\theta\psi(y)+\theta^2F(y),\qquad y^\mu=x^\mu+i\theta\sigma^\mu\bar\theta,
$$
also obeys $\bar D_{\dot\alpha}\Phi=0$. Keeping only a nonconstant $A(x)$ and forbidding the other components is not a closed <supermultiplet>: applying a <supercharge> generates Grassmann-dependent derivative terms. A real unconstrained <superfield> can instead describe a <vector superfield>.

= Solution
{parent=h}

At two derivatives, specify the <gauge group> and matter representations, a real <Kähler potential> $K(\Phi,\bar\Phi)$, a holomorphic <superpotential> $W(\Phi)$ and a symmetric holomorphic <gauge kinetic function> $f_{ab}(\Phi)$. Gauge couplings and topological angles are contained in $f$; global theories can also have allowed <Fayet–Iliopoulos terms> for Abelian factors. Local theories require the gravitational coupling and consistent gauging: a constant <Fayet–Iliopoulos term> is not an independent addition to arbitrary <supergravity> data. A constant in $W$ affects <supergravity> although it is irrelevant in the global theory. **$K$ is not holomorphic or protected; $W$ has no perturbative Wilsonian renormalization; holomorphic $f$ is perturbatively one-loop exact.** Nonperturbative corrections can modify $W$ and $f$, and <wave-function renormalization> makes physical couplings run despite <superpotential non-renormalization>. Higher-derivative actions require further data.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Choose the <extended supersymmetry> normalization
$$
\{Q^A_\alpha,\bar Q_{\dot\beta B}\}=2\sigma^\mu_{\alpha\dot\beta}P_\mu\delta^A{}_B,\qquad
\{Q^A_\alpha,Q^B_\beta\}=\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}Z^{AB},\qquad \epsilon_{12}=1.
$$
The <central charge in supersymmetry> is antisymmetric in $A,B$, because the full <anticommutator> is symmetric and $\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}$ is antisymmetric. In a massive <one-particle state> rest frame, $P_\mu=(M,0,0,0)$, and the first <anticommutator> becomes $2M\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^A{}_B$.

A <unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix> puts $Z$ into blocks $\begin{pmatrix}0&z_r\\-z_r&0\end{pmatrix}$, with a remaining zero direction if $\mathcal N$ is odd. For one block write $z=|z|e^{i\varphi}$, and label its two supersymmetries $1,2$. The combinations
$$
A_\pm=\frac{Q^1_1\pm e^{i\varphi}(Q^2_2)^\dagger}{\sqrt2},\qquad
B_\pm=\frac{Q^1_2\mp e^{i\varphi}(Q^2_1)^\dagger}{\sqrt2}
$$
have
$$
\{A_\pm,A_\pm^\dagger\}=\{B_\pm,B_\pm^\dagger\}=2M\pm|z|.
$$
For example the mixed contribution to the first expression is $\pm(e^{-i\varphi}z+e^{i\varphi}\bar z)/2=\pm|z|$. This is the phase-aligned version of taking a <supercharge> minus a unitary combination of its adjoints, as suggested by the hint.

For every operator $X$ and normalized state $|v\rangle$,
$$
\langle v|\{X,X^\dagger\}|v\rangle=\|Xv\|^2+\|X^\dagger v\|^2\ge0.
$$
Applying this to $A_-,B_-$ in every block proves **the BPS bound**
$$
\boxed{M\ge\frac12\max_r|z_r|.}
$$
If the algebra is instead written with $2Z$ on its right side, the same result is $M\ge\max_r|Z_r|$; the factor is purely a central-charge convention.

A <BPS state> saturates at least one of these inequalities. The corresponding zero-norm combinations of <supercharges> and their adjoints annihilate the representation, so part of <supersymmetry> is preserved. Its <massive supermultiplet> is shortened: each saturated block removes two complex fermionic oscillators, giving $(2j+1)2^{2\mathcal N-2k}$ states from a spin-$j$ <Clifford vacuum> when $k$ blocks saturate, before any necessary <CPT completion of a supermultiplet>. It preserves $4k$ of the $4\mathcal N$ real <supercharges>. Its mass is tied to the <central charges in supersymmetry> and remains protected while it stays on the same short branch. Stability is not unconditional: a <wall of marginal stability> can permit a charged <BPS state> to decay into other <BPS states> with the same total charge.

= Solution
{parent=a}

At fixed nonzero <four-momentum>, take the finite physical <supermultiplet> and write $P_F=(-1)^F$ for <fermion parity>. Each <supercharge> is odd, so $P_FQ_\alpha=-Q_\alpha P_F$ and the same holds for its adjoint. By the <cyclic property of the trace>,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(P_FQ_\alpha Q_\alpha^\dagger)
=\operatorname{Tr}(Q_\alpha^\dagger P_FQ_\alpha)
=-\operatorname{Tr}(P_FQ_\alpha^\dagger Q_\alpha).
$$
Consequently $\operatorname{Tr}[P_F\{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\}]=0$. Sum the two spinor components of the $\mathcal N=1$ <Super-Poincaré algebra>. Since $\operatorname{Tr}_{2\times2}\sigma^i=0$, their sum is $4H$. On a fixed-energy representation, $H=EI$, yielding
$$
0=4E\operatorname{Tr}P_F=4E(n_B-n_F).
$$
For a nonzero physical one-particle momentum, $E>0$, including the massless case. Hence **the bosonic and fermionic physical state counts agree**:
$$
\boxed{n_B=n_F.}
$$
Equivalently a nonzero normalized <supercharge> and its adjoint define an odd <involution> pairing the two subspaces. This is <supertrace pairing at positive energy>; it counts spin polarizations, not merely distinct names of particles.

The phrase “every supermultiplet” requires this positive-energy qualification. A zero-energy <supersymmetric vacuum> with all <supercharges> acting as zero is a one-dimensional bosonic representation with no fermionic partner. It is a counterexample to the unrestricted statement, because division by $E$ is then impossible. It does not contradict the requested equality for particle <supermultiplets>.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

Sum the diagonal spinor entries of the $\mathcal N=1$ <Super-Poincaré algebra>, obtaining
$$
\boxed{H=\frac14\sum_{\alpha=1}^2\{Q_\alpha,Q_\alpha^\dagger\}.}
$$
The expectation in any state is therefore
$$
\langle H\rangle=\frac14\sum_\alpha\bigl(\|Q_\alpha|v\rangle\|^2+\|Q_\alpha^\dagger|v\rangle\|^2\bigr)\ge0.
$$
This proves <energy positivity in global supersymmetry>, with the energy origin fixed by the algebra.

If a <ground state> is invariant under <supersymmetry>, all its <supercharge> variations vanish and its energy is zero. Conversely, zero energy makes every nonnegative squared norm above zero, so every <supercharge> annihilates that vacuum. Thus **strictly positive vacuum energy implies supersymmetry breaking**: some <supercharge> fails to annihilate the vacuum. A finite-volume regulator can be used before passing to vacuum-energy density. This is a statement about the global <supersymmetry algebra>; it is not a positivity theorem for the general <supergravity F-term potential>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Use Planck units and restrict to the physical <Kähler metric> domain
$$
x=S+\bar S>0,\qquad y=T+\bar T-|C|^2>0.
$$
Put $A=ae^{-\alpha S}$ and $w=A+b$, so that $W=C^3+w$. There are no gauged interactions specified, hence no additional <D-term scalar potential>. The <supergravity F-term potential> is $V=e^K(K^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW}-3|W|^2)$, with $e^K=1/(xy^3)$. Its <Kähler covariant derivatives of a superpotential> are
$$
D_SW=-\alpha A-\frac W x,\qquad D_TW=-\frac{3W}{y},\qquad D_CW=3C^2+\frac{3\bar C W}{y}.
$$
The nonzero metric entries are
$$
K_{S\bar S}=x^{-2},\quad
\begin{pmatrix}K_{T\bar T}&K_{T\bar C}\\K_{C\bar T}&K_{C\bar C}\end{pmatrix}
=\frac3{y^2}\begin{pmatrix}1&-C\\-\bar C&T+\bar T\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The determinant of this $T,C$ block is $9/y^3>0$, so it is positive definite. With the inverse-index convention $K_{i\bar j}K^{k\bar j}=\delta_i^k$, the raised components are
$$
K^{S\bar S}=x^2,\qquad
\begin{pmatrix}K^{T\bar T}&K^{T\bar C}\\K^{C\bar T}&K^{C\bar C}\end{pmatrix}
=\frac y3\begin{pmatrix}T+\bar T&\bar C\\C&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Keeping these complex-conjugate off-diagonal entries in their correct index positions is essential.

To exhibit the <no-scale supergravity> cancellation, write the $T,C$ norm as
$$
K^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW}\big|_{T,C}
=\frac y3\left[y|D_TW|^2+|D_CW+\bar C D_TW|^2\right]
=3|W|^2+3y|C|^4.
$$
Here $D_CW+\bar C D_TW=3C^2$. The $3|W|^2$ term cancels the negative term in the <supergravity F-term potential>. The result is **the nonnegative scalar potential**
$$
\boxed{V=\frac{|C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A|^2}{xy^3}+\frac{3|C|^4}{xy^2}.}
$$
This is the <matter-logarithm no-scale potential>, specialized to the exponential <superpotential>.

Choose the <supergravity auxiliary field> convention $F^i=-e^{K/2}K^{i\bar j}\overline{D_jW}$. Substitution into the full inverse <Kähler metric> gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
F^S&=e^{K/2}x\,\overline{C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A},\\
F^T&=e^{K/2}y\,\overline{A+b},\\
F^C&=-e^{K/2}y\,\bar C^2.
\end{aligned}}
$$
An overall auxiliary-field phase is conventional. The $C$ dependence of $F^T$ cancels between the two metric terms. If all three <auxiliary fields> vanished at a finite physical point, $F^C=0$ would force $C=0$, $F^T=0$ would force $A+b=0$, and $F^S=0$ would then force $\alpha x A=0$. Thus $a=b=0$ is necessary. **For any nontrivial choice of $a,b$, supersymmetry is broken at every finite physical point.** The allowed exception $a=b=0$, $C=0$ is supersymmetric; the statement cannot hold for all arbitrary parameters without this qualification.

The <no-scale volume runaway criterion> also determines whether the minimum is attained. For fixed $S,C$, differentiation with respect to $y$ gives
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial y}=-\frac{3|C^3+b+(1+\alpha x)A|^2}{xy^4}-\frac{6|C|^4}{xy^3}.
$$
It is negative unless both numerators vanish. Therefore a finite stationary vacuum must satisfy
$$
\boxed{C=0,\qquad b+(1+\alpha x)ae^{-\alpha S}=0.}
$$
Every such point is a global minimum with **$V_{\min}=0$**. For $a,b\ne0$, setting $r=|b/a|$ and $z=\alpha x>0$ reduces existence to
$$
r=(1+z)e^{-z/2},\qquad \arg a-\alpha\operatorname{Im}S=\arg(-b)\pmod{2\pi}.
$$
The derivative of the magnitude function is $(1-z)e^{-z/2}/2$. It rises from $1$ at the excluded boundary $z=0$ to $2e^{-1/2}$ at $z=1$, then falls to zero. Consequently **a finite zero-energy minimum exists exactly when**
$$
\boxed{a,b\ne0,\quad 0<|b/a|\le2e^{-1/2},}
$$
or in the separate trivial family $a=b=0$. There is one positive root for $0<r\le1$, two for $1<r<2e^{-1/2}$, and one double root at equality. If only one of $a,b$ vanishes, or $r$ exceeds this maximum, there is no finite minimum: the infimum is zero as $\operatorname{Re}T\to\infty$. Calling this runaway a finite zero-energy vacuum would be incorrect.

At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum, $W=A+b=-\alpha x A\ne0$, and the auxiliary expectation values reduce to
$$
F^S=F^C=0,\qquad F^T=e^{K/2}y\bar W\ne0.
$$
This explicitly verifies <supersymmetry breaking> with zero <vacuum energy> in <no-scale supergravity>. Both real components of $T$ are exact <flat directions of a scalar potential> on the minimum locus. Away from that locus, $\operatorname{Im}T$ is still absent from the potential, but $\operatorname{Re}T$ is not flat. At a minimum, $C$ is lifted at quartic order: at fixed $S,T$, $V=3|C|^4/[x(T+\bar T)^2]+O(|C|^6)$. A zero quadratic mass does not make $C$ an exact flat direction. The <quartic stabilization at a double no-scale root> likewise gives a vanishing quadratic mass, but no exact flat direction, for $\operatorname{Re}S$ at $z=1$. For $a=b=0$, the whole $C=0$ family has both $S$ and $T$ flat and <supersymmetry> unbroken.

Finally, the <gravitino mass from a superpotential> is
$$
\boxed{m_{3/2}=\frac{|C^3+A+b|}{\sqrt{x}\,y^{3/2}}.}
$$
At a nontrivial zero-energy minimum this becomes
$$
\boxed{m_{3/2}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{x}|A|}{y^{3/2}}=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{x}|b|}{(1+\alpha x)y^{3/2}}>0.}
$$
It depends on the unfixed volume modulus, so the model does not predict one numerical mass. For $a=b=0$, $C=0$, the <gravitino> is massless.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Use the printed <hypercharge> convention; reversing every hypercharge relative to another common convention does not change the gauge invariants. Write $U,D,E,N$ for the singlet <chiral superfields> labelled with a superscript $R$ in the paper, and define the weak-doublet contraction $A\cdot B=\epsilon_{ab}A^aB^b$. The most general homogeneous <cubic superpotential with right-handed-neutrino superfields> separates as $W_3=W_{B,L}+W_{\rm viol}$, where
$$
\boxed{W_{B,L}=(y_u)_{ij}(Q_i\cdot H_2)U_j+(y_d)_{ij}(Q_i\cdot H_1)D_j
+(y_e)_{ij}(L_i\cdot H_1)E_j+(y_\nu)_{ij}(L_i\cdot H_2)N_j,}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
W_{\rm viol}={}&\frac12\lambda_{ijk}(L_i\cdot L_j)E_k+\lambda'_{ijk}(L_i\cdot Q_j)D_k\\
&+\frac12\lambda''_{ijk}\epsilon^{abc}U_{ia}D_{jb}D_{kc}
+\eta_iN_i(H_1\cdot H_2)+\frac16\kappa_{ijk}N_iN_jN_k.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Repeated generation and color indices are summed. Because the <chiral superfields> commute while the gauge contractions are antisymmetric,
$$
\lambda_{ijk}=-\lambda_{jik},\qquad \lambda''_{ijk}=-\lambda''_{ikj},\qquad \kappa_{ijk}=\kappa_{(ijk)}.
$$
The factors $1/2$ and $1/6$ remove the corresponding overcounting.

Here is a completeness check, not merely a list of possible terms. A color-singlet cubic can have no colored fields, a fundamental–antifundamental pair, or three fundamentals or three antifundamentals. A colored pair must be $QU$ or $QD$: the remaining weak doublet and <hypercharge> select respectively $H_2$ or $H_1,L$. Three antifundamentals allow only $UDD$ at zero <hypercharge>; three $Q$ doublets have no weak singlet. With no colored fields, weak invariance requires zero or two doublets. Two positive-hypercharge doublets require $E$, giving $LH_1E$ and $LLE$, while $H_1\cdot H_1=0$. Opposite-hypercharge doublets require $N$, giving $LH_2N$ and $H_1H_2N$. With no doublets, only $NNN$ has zero <hypercharge>. These are exactly the nine structures above.

For <baryon number> and <lepton number>, assign
$$
B(Q)=\tfrac13,\quad B(U)=B(D)=-\tfrac13,\qquad L(L)=1,\quad L(E)=L(N)=-1,
$$
and zero charges to the Higgs fields. This is the conjugate-singlet convention, so the neutrino <Yukawa coupling> conserves <lepton number>. The first four terms conserve both charges. The $LLE$ and $LQD$ terms carry $L=1$, the $UDD$ term carries $B=-1$, the $NH_1H_2$ term carries $L=-1$, and $NNN$ carries $L=-3$. The gauge-singlet neutrino interactions must therefore be included in the violating sector.

If “cubic” instead means a polynomial of degree at most three, there are also the lower-degree gauge invariants
$$
W_{\le2}=W_0+t_iN_i+\frac12M_{ij}N_iN_j+\mu H_1\cdot H_2+\mu_iL_i\cdot H_2,\qquad M_{ij}=M_{ji}.
$$
The Higgs mass term conserves both charges; $t_iN_i$, $M_{ij}N_iN_j$ and $\mu_iL_i\cdot H_2$ violate <lepton number>. These additional terms distinguish the full renormalizable <superpotential> from its homogeneous cubic part.

To obtain <squark-mediated proton decay>, combine a <lepton-number>-violating $LQD$ vertex with a <baryon-number>-violating $UDD$ vertex. A nonzero example is $\lambda'_{112}$ with $\lambda''_{112}$, exchanging the singlet <strange-quark> <squark> of mass $M_{\widetilde s}$. The antisymmetry forbids $\lambda''_{111}$, so using three first-generation singlets would not be a valid example. The relevant component vertices contain $e_Lu_L\widetilde s^{\,c}$ and $u^cd^c\widetilde s^{\,c}$, with the required color contraction. Connecting one vertex to the complex conjugate of the other through the massive scalar propagator produces schematically
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm eff}\supset\frac{\lambda'_{112}\lambda''^{*}_{112}}{M_{\widetilde s}^2}
\epsilon_{abc}(u_R^a d_R^b)(e_Lu_L^c)+\mathrm{h.c.}
$$
Parentheses denote Lorentz scalar two-spinor contractions, dotted for the right-handed pair and undotted for the left-handed pair. This dimension-six <proton-decay operator> contains the three quarks $uud$ and an electron field that can create a <positron>. Its hadronic matrix element $\langle\pi^0|\epsilon_{abc}(u_R^ad_R^b)u_L^c|p\rangle$ gives the channel $p\to e^+\pi^0$. The internal strange flavor need not appear in the final state. The two interactions together violate both continuous charges, as required for this channel.

The coefficient of the dimension-six <effective operator> has mass dimension $-2$. Thus <dimensional analysis> gives
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(p\to e^+\pi^0)\sim c_h\,|\lambda'\lambda''|^2\frac{m_p^5}{M_{\widetilde s}^4},\qquad
\tau_p\sim\frac{M_{\widetilde s}^4}{c_h|\lambda'\lambda''|^2m_p^5},}
$$
where $c_h$ is a dimensionless hadronic and phase-space factor. Its detailed value cannot be fixed by dimensional reasoning. Applying the historical lower bound supplied in the paper gives the <proton-lifetime bound on a product of R-parity-violating couplings>
$$
\boxed{|\lambda'\lambda''|\lesssim\frac{M_{\widetilde s}^2}{\sqrt{c_h\,(2.4\times10^{64}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1})\,m_p^5}}
\simeq\frac{6.5\times10^{-27}}{\sqrt{c_h}}\left(\frac{M_{\widetilde s}}{1\,\mathrm{TeV}}\right)^2\left(\frac{1\,\mathrm{GeV}}{m_p}\right)^{5/2}.}
$$
The numerical coefficient is only the accuracy of the requested dimensional estimate; the squark mass was not fixed by the question.

Finally, <R-parity> is $R_p=(-1)^{3(B-L)+2s}$. At the superfield level use <matter parity> $P_M=(-1)^{3(B-L)}$: $Q,U,D,L,E,N$ are odd and $H_1,H_2$ are even. Each conserving <Yukawa coupling> contains two matter <chiral superfields> and one Higgs field, so it is even. Every violating homogeneous cubic above is odd: $LLE,LQD,UDD,NNN$ contain three matter fields, while $NH_1H_2$ contains one. **R-parity forbids all the listed cubic violations and preserves the Yukawa terms that generate fermion masses.** The Higgs bilinear is also even, whereas the lepton bilinear $LH_2$ and the singlet linear term are odd.

The final assertion must not be read as forbidding every possible violation of continuous <baryon number> or <lepton number>. In particular, <matter parity allows Majorana neutrino masses>: $N_iN_j$ is even but carries $L=-2$, a concrete counterexample once quadratic terms are included. Higher-dimensional operators such as $QQQL$ and $(LH_2)^2$ are likewise even and violate the continuous charges. The verified statement here concerns the homogeneous cubic sector, including the neutrino singlet terms.
