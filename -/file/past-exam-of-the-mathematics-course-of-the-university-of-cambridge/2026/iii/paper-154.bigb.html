= Solution
{parent=1}

For a radial function $u=u(r)$ on $\mathbb R^d$, the <radial Laplacian> is
$$
\Delta u=u''+\frac{d-1}{r}u'.
$$
The problem therefore becomes
$$
u''(r)+\frac{d-1}{r}u'(r)+u(r)^p=0,
\qquad u(0)>0,\quad u'(0)=0,
\qquad u(r)\to0.
$$
The equation has the <scaling symmetry of a partial differential equation>[scaling symmetry]
$$
u(r)\longmapsto u_\lambda(r)=\lambda^{2/(p-1)}u(\lambda r).
$$
Choosing $\lambda=u(0)^{-(p-1)/2}$ normalizes any positive solution to $u_\lambda(0)=1$.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=2}

Put
$$
a=\frac2{p-1},\qquad s=\log r,
\qquad v(s)=r^au(r).
$$
Then $u=r^{-a}v$ and direct differentiation gives
$$
\Delta u=r^{-a-2}
\left(\ddot v+(d-2-2a)\dot v-a(d-2-a)v\right).
$$
Since $ap=a+2$ and $d-2-2a=2(s_c-1)$, the <Emden-Fowler transformation> turns the equation into
$$
\ddot v+2(s_c-1)\dot v-c_\infty^{p-1}v+v^p=0,
$$
where
$$
c_\infty^{p-1}=a(d-2-a)
=\frac2{p-1}\left(d-2-\frac2{p-1}\right)>0.
$$

{parent=1}

= Solution
{parent=3}

Seek a homogeneous solution $u(r)=cr^{-\beta}$. Matching the powers in $\Delta u+u^p=0$ gives $\beta p=\beta+2$, hence
$$
\beta=\frac2{p-1}=a.
$$
Since
$$
\Delta r^{-\beta}=-\beta(d-2-\beta)r^{-\beta-2},
$$
matching coefficients gives
$$
c^{p-1}=\beta(d-2-\beta).
$$
Thus the positive <Singular homogeneous solution of the Lane-Emden equation> is
$$
\boxed{u_\infty(r)=c_\infty r^{-2/(p-1)}.}
$$

= 4
{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=4}

When $s_c=1$, one has $p=(d+2)/(d-2)$. The normalized <Aubin-Talenti bubble>
$$
u(r)=\left(1+\frac{r^2}{d(d-2)}\right)^{-(d-2)/2}
$$
solves the radial equation, has $u(0)=1$, and tends to zero. It also follows from the stated one-dimensional soliton after the <Emden-Fowler transformation>, because the damping term vanishes.

As $r\to\infty$,
$$
r^{d-2}u(r)\longrightarrow[d(d-2)]^{(d-2)/2}>0,
$$
so $\alpha=d-2$. Moreover $u'(r)=O(r^{-(d-1)})$ at infinity and $u'(r)=O(r)$ near zero. Therefore
$$
\int_{\mathbb R^d}|\nabla u|^2,dx
\asymp\int_0^\infty|u'(r)|^2r^{d-1},dr<\infty,
$$
and $\nabla u\in L^2(\mathbb R^d)$.

= 5
{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=5}

Let $A=c_\infty^{p-1}$ and
$$
J=\dot v^2+f(v),
\qquad
f(v)=-Av^2+\frac2{p+1}v^{p+1}.
$$
Using the transformed equation,
$$
\dot J
=2\dot v(\ddot v-Av+v^p)
=-4(s_c-1)\dot v^2\leq0.
$$
Thus $J$ is a strict <Lyapunov function> away from equilibria.

On $\mathbb R_+$, $f(0)=0$, it decreases to its unique minimum at $v=c_\infty$, where
$$
f(c_\infty)=-\frac{p-1}{p+1}c_\infty^{p+1}<0,
$$
then increases, crosses zero at $v=((p+1)/2)^{1/(p-1)}c_\infty$, and tends to $+\infty$.

= 6
{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=6}

Take the regular radial solution with $u(0)=1$. In the transformed variables it satisfies
$$
v(s)\sim e^{as},\qquad \dot v(s)\sim ae^{as}
\quad(s\to-\infty),
$$
so $J(s)\to0$. Since $s_c>1$, the <Lyapunov function> immediately becomes negative. The trajectory cannot reach $v=0$, where $J=\dot v^2\geq0$, and $J<0$ confines it below the positive zero of $f$. Hence it remains positive and bounded for all $s$.

The identity $\dot J=-4(s_c-1)\dot v^2$ and the <LaSalle invariance principle> force the omega-limit set to consist of equilibria. The negative limiting energy excludes $v=0$, leaving
$$
v(s)\longrightarrow c_\infty.
$$
Consequently
$$
r^{2/(p-1)}u(r)\longrightarrow c_\infty>0,
$$
so $\alpha=2/(p-1)$. At infinity, $|u'(r)|\sim ac_\infty r^{-a-1}$, and
$$
\int^infty |u'(r)|^2r^{d-1},dr
\asymp\int^infty r^{d-3-2a},dr
=\int^infty r^{2s_c-3},dr=\infty
$$
because $s_c>1$. Thus $\nabla u\notin L^2(\mathbb R^d)$.

= Solution
{parent=1}

The homogeneous <Sobolev embedding theorem> gives
$$
\dot H^\sigma(\mathbb R^d)\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\mathbb R^d),
\qquad
\frac1{p+1}=\frac12-\frac\sigma d,
$$
so
$$
\sigma=\frac d2-\frac d{p+1}
=\frac{d(p-1)}{2(p+1)}.
$$
Now $s_c>0$ is equivalent to $p-1>4/d$, which gives $\sigma>2/(p+1)$. Similarly $s_c<1$ is equivalent to $(d-2)(p-1)<4$, which gives $\sigma<1$.

= 2
{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=2}

Interpolate between $L^2$ and $\dot H^1$ and then use the preceding <Sobolev embedding theorem>:
$$
\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}
\leq C\|u\|_2^{(1-\sigma)(p+1)}
\|\nabla u\|_2^{\sigma(p+1)}.
$$
<Mass conservation for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation> fixes $\|u(t)\|_2=\|u_0\|_2$, while <Energy conservation for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation> and $E(u_0)\leq0$ imply
$$
\frac12\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2
\leq\frac1{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{p+1}^{p+1}.
$$
Since $\sigma(p+1)>2$, rearrangement gives
$$
\|\nabla u(t)\|_2\geq c(u_0)>0
$$
uniformly on the maximal lifespan.

{parent=2}

= Solution
{parent=3}

Set
$$
I_\chi(t)=\int\chi|u|^2,
\qquad
V_\chi(t)=\operatorname{Im}\int\nabla\chi\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla u\,\overline u.
$$
Substituting $u_t=i(\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u)$ and integrating by parts gives the <localized virial identity>
$$
\frac12I_\chi'(t)=V_\chi(t).
$$
Differentiating once more, integrating the Laplacian terms twice, and observing that the gauge-invariant nonlinearity contributes only through $\Delta\chi$, gives
$$
\frac12V_\chi'(t)
=\int \operatorname{Hess}\chi(\nabla u,\nabla\overline u)
-\frac14\int\Delta^2\chi|u|^2
-\left(\frac12-\frac1{p+1}\right)
\int\Delta\chi|u|^{p+1}.
$$
These are the required formulas, with $\chi''|\nabla u|^2$ interpreted as the Hessian quadratic form for a radial weight.

= 4
{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=4}

For a smooth radial function that decays at infinity,
$$
r^{d-1}|u(r)|^2
=-\int_r^\infty\frac d{ds}\bigl(s^{d-1}|u(s)|^2\bigr),ds
\leq2\int_r^\infty s^{d-1}|u(s)||u'(s)|,ds.
$$
The <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> therefore yields the <Radial Sobolev inequality>
$$
|u(r)|^2\leq\frac2{r^{d-1}}\|u\|_2\|\nabla u\|_2,
$$
up to the harmless common normalization of surface measure. Taking the supremum over $r\geq R$ gives the claimed estimate; density extends it from smooth radial functions to every $u\in H^1_r$.

= Solution
{parent=5}

For $\psi_R(x)=R^2\psi(x/R)$, the assumptions imply $\operatorname{Hess}\psi_R\leq I$, while on $|x|\leq2R$ one has
$$
\operatorname{Hess}\psi_R=I,
\qquad \Delta\psi_R=d,
\qquad \Delta^2\psi_R=0.
$$
Apply the <localized virial identity> and compare its interior terms with
$$
E(u)=\frac12\|\nabla u\|_2^2-
\frac1{p+1}\|u\|_{p+1}^{p+1}\leq0.
$$
The coefficient $d(p-1)/4$ is strictly greater than one because $s_c>0$. The resulting negative multiple of $\|\nabla u\|_2^2$ can be moved to the left. All errors in the nonlinear term are supported on $|x|\geq2R$, and $\Delta^2\psi_R=O(R^{-2})$ is supported on $2R\leq|x|\leq10R$. Thus
$$
\boxed{c(d,p)\int|\nabla u|^2
+\frac12\frac d{dt}V_{\psi_R}(t)
\leq C(d,p)\left[
\int_{|x|\geq2R}|u|^{p+1}
+\frac1{R^2}\int_{2R\leq|x|\leq10R}|u|^2
\right].}
$$

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=6}

Suppose that $T=\infty$. Since $s_c<1$ and $d\geq3$,
$$
p<1+\frac4{d-2}\leq5.
$$
The <Radial Sobolev inequality> and <Mass conservation for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation> give
$$
\int_{|x|\geq2R}|u|^{p+1}
\leq C(u_0)R^{-(d-1)(p-1)/2}
\|\nabla u\|_2^{(p-1)/2}.
$$
Because $(p-1)/2<2$, <Young inequality> and a sufficiently large fixed $R$ absorb this term into the left side of the estimate from part 5. The annular term is at most $C(u_0)R^{-2}$. Using the uniform lower bound $\|\nabla u(t)\|_2\geq c(u_0)$ from part 2 and enlarging $R$ once more yields
$$
V_{\psi_R}'(t)\leq-\delta(u_0)<0.
$$

Since $I_{\psi_R}'=2V_{\psi_R}$, two integrations give
$$
I_{\psi_R}(t)
\leq I_{\psi_R}(0)+2V_{\psi_R}(0)t-\delta t^2.
$$
The right-hand side is negative for large $t$, contradicting $I_{\psi_R}(t)=\int\psi_R|u|^2\geq0$. Hence the maximal forward lifespan is finite: $T<\infty$.

= Solution
{parent=1}

The <NLS ground state> is the positive radial solution of
$$
-\Delta Q+Q-Q^3=0.
$$
It is, up to phase, translation, and scaling, the optimizer of the <Sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality>
$$
\|w\|_4^4
\leq\frac2{\|Q\|_2^2}\|w\|_2^2\|\nabla w\|_2^2.
$$
Consequently
$$
E(w)
\geq\frac12\left(1-\frac{\|w\|_2^2}{\|Q\|_2^2}\right)
\|\nabla w\|_2^2.
$$
Equality in the sharp inequality occurs precisely for the symmetry orbit of $Q$.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=2}

For every real $a$, the <gauge transform> $w_a=e^{ia\psi}w$ has the same $L^2$ norm as $w$. Since $\|w\|_2\leq\|Q\|_2$, the variational bound from part 1 gives $E(w_a)\geq0$. Expanding its gradient gives
$$
E(w_a)=E(w)+aI+\frac{a^2}{2}A,
$$
where
$$
I=\operatorname{Im}\int\overline w\,\nabla w\mathbin{\cdot}\nabla\psi,
\qquad
A=\int|\nabla\psi|^2|w|^2.
$$
This quadratic polynomial is nonnegative for every $a\in\mathbb R$, so its discriminant is nonpositive. Therefore
$$
I^2\leq2E(w)A,
$$
which is the desired estimate.

= Solution

If $\|u_0\|_2<\|Q\|_2$, the sharp variational estimate and the conservation laws give
$$
E(u_0)=E(u(t))
\geq\frac12\left(1-\frac{\|u_0\|_2^2}{\|Q\|_2^2}\right)
\|\nabla u(t)\|_2^2.
$$
**Thus the $H^1$ norm remains bounded on every finite time interval. The <Blowup alternative for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation> then prevents a finite endpoint of the maximal lifespan, so $T=\infty$.**

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=4}

Let $(u_n)$ be bounded in $H^1_r(\mathbb R^2)$. After passing to a subsequence it converges weakly in $H^1$, while the <Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem> gives strong $L^4$ convergence on every bounded ball. The <Radial Sobolev inequality> gives uniformly
$$
\int_{|x|\geq R}|u_n|^4
\leq\|u_n\|_{L^\infty(|x|\geq R)}^2\|u_n\|_2^2
\leq\frac C R.
$$
The same estimate applies to the weak limit. Choosing $R$ large and then using local compactness proves strong convergence in $L^4(\mathbb R^2)$. Hence
$$
H^1_r(\mathbb R^2)\hookrightarrow L^4(\mathbb R^2)
$$
is compact.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=5}

By the <Blowup alternative for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation>, choose $t_n\uparrow T$ with $\|\nabla u(t_n)\|_2\to\infty$, and set
$$
\lambda_n=\frac{\|\nabla Q\|_2}{\|\nabla u(t_n)\|_2}\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
v_n(x)=\lambda_nu(t_n,\lambda_nx).
$$
The mass-critical scaling gives
$$
\|v_n\|_2=\|Q\|_2,
\qquad
\|\nabla v_n\|_2=\|\nabla Q\|_2,
\qquad
E(v_n)=\lambda_n^2E(u_0)\to0.
$$

The sequence is bounded and radial in $H^1$, so part 4 yields, after extraction, weak $H^1$ and strong $L^4$ convergence to some $v$. The energy identity gives $\|v_n\|_4^4\to2\|\nabla Q\|_2^2$. The <Sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality> and weak lower semicontinuity force equality in every limiting norm inequality. Hence $v_n\to v$ strongly in $H^1$, and the variational characterization identifies
$$
v=Qe^{i\gamma}
$$
for some $\gamma\in\mathbb R$; radiality removes translations and the gradient normalization fixes the scale.

= Solution
{parent=6}

Choose a smooth radial cutoff $\theta_R$ that vanishes on $|x|\leq R$, equals one on $|x|\geq2R$, and satisfies $|\nabla\theta_R|\leq C/R$. The first <localized virial identity> and the estimate from part 2, applied to $w=u(t)$ and $\psi=\theta_R$, give
$$
\left|\frac d{dt}\int\theta_R|u(t)|^2\right|
\leq\frac{C(u_0)}R,
$$
because the mass and energy are conserved.

For fixed $R$, the strong profile convergence from part 5 and $\lambda_n\to0$ imply
$$
\int\theta_R(x)|u(t_n,x)|^2,dx
=\int\theta_R(\lambda_n y)|v_n(y)|^2,dy\longrightarrow0.
$$
Integrating the flux estimate from $t$ to $t_n$ and then letting $n\to\infty$ yields
$$
\int_{|x|\geq2R}|u(t,x)|^2,dx
\leq\frac{C(u_0)T}{R}
$$
uniformly for $0\leq t<T$. Taking $R$ sufficiently large proves the claim.

= Solution
{parent=7}

Change variables $x=\lambda_ny$ and use the profiles from part 5:
$$
\int_{\mathbb R^2}|u(t_n,x)|^2\phi(x),dx
=\int_{\mathbb R^2}|v_n(y)|^2\phi(\lambda_ny),dy.
$$
Since $v_n\to Qe^{i\gamma}$ in $L^2$, the difference made by replacing $|v_n|^2$ with $|Q|^2$ tends to zero against the bounded function $\phi(\lambda_n\cdot)$. For each fixed $y$, continuity gives $\phi(\lambda_ny)\to\phi(0)$, and <dominated convergence theorem>[dominated convergence] then gives
$$
\int|Q(y)|^2\phi(\lambda_ny),dy
\longrightarrow\phi(0)\|Q\|_2^2.
$$
**Thus the mass measures $|u(t_n,x)|^2dx$ converge weakly to the <Dirac delta function> $\|Q\|_2^2\delta_0$.**
