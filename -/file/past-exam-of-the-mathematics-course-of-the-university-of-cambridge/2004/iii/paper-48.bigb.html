= Solution
{parent=1}

Work with canonically normalized real components of the <scalar field>; a complex <representation> can first be written in terms of its real and imaginary components. At a <vacuum expectation value> $\varphi_0$, expansion of the <scalar potential> gives
$$
V(\varphi_0+\eta)=V(\varphi_0)+\frac12\eta^T M^2\eta+\cdots,\qquad M^2_{rs}=\partial_r\partial_s V(\varphi_0).
$$
The linear term vanishes by stationarity. Thus the <scalar mass matrix> is the <Hessian matrix> of the <scalar potential>. Invariance under each <Lie algebra generator> gives the identity $\partial_r V(\varphi)(iT_a\varphi)_r=0$. Differentiating this identity with respect to $\varphi_s$ and evaluating at the stationary point removes the term containing $\partial_rV$, leaving
$$
M^2(iT_a\varphi_0)=0.
$$
The kernel of the map $T\mapsto iT\varphi_0$ is precisely the <Lie algebra> of the <stabilizer subgroup> H. Consequently its restriction to a complementary space of broken <Lie algebra generators> is injective: a linear combination that annihilates $\varphi_0$ would belong both to the stabilizer algebra and its complement, and hence would vanish. There are therefore $\dim G-\dim H$ independent <Goldstone directions in the scalar mass matrix>, tangent to the <vacuum manifold>. **There are $\dim G-\dim H$ symmetry-protected massless scalars.**

This <Goldstone theorem> guarantees at least that many zero quadratic masses. The claim that these are all the massless scalars additionally requires nonzero curvature transverse to the vacuum orbit. For example, $V=\kappa(\varphi^T\varphi-v^2)^4$, with $\kappa>0$, has an ordinary spherical <vacuum manifold> but its entire <Hessian matrix> vanishes there. These <accidental massless radial modes beyond Goldstone modes> are not excluded by the hypotheses. For the indicated <orthogonal group> breaking, the protected number is
$$
\boxed{\dim O(n)-\dim O(n-1)=\frac{n(n-1)-(n-1)(n-2)}2=n-1.}
$$

For the <Higgs doublet>, put $r=\sqrt{2\phi^\dagger\phi}=v+H$ and $z=\sqrt2\phi/r$ wherever $r\ne0$. If $z=(z_1,z_2)^T$, then
$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}z_2^*&z_1\\-z_1^*&z_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad U^\dagger U=1,\qquad\det U=1,\qquad U\binom01=z.
$$
This explicitly exhibits the three angular coordinates as an <SU(2)> transformation of a real radial field. In particular, the <polar coordinates for the electroweak Higgs doublet> can locally be chosen in the form
$$
\phi=\exp(-i\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma+in_3 I)\frac1{\sqrt2}\binom0{v+H}.
$$
At the vacuum the three variations generated by $-i\sigma_1$, $-i\sigma_2$, and $i(I-\sigma_3)$ acting on $(0,1)^T$ are independent over the reals. They span the three broken directions; the remaining generator $Q=(\sigma_3+I)/2$ annihilates the vacuum. This proves that the exponential supplies local angular coordinates. An inverse local <gauge transformation>, applied also to the <gauge fields>, removes these three fields in <unitary gauge>. They supply the longitudinal polarizations of the massive <W bosons> and <Z boson> through the <Higgs mechanism>. The parameterization is local about a nonzero vacuum, rather than a coordinate system through every possible zero of the <Higgs doublet>.

Use the metric $(+---)$ and define the charged <W bosons> and neutral <gauge fields> by
$$
W_\mu^\pm=\frac{A_\mu^1\mp iA_\mu^2}{\sqrt2},\qquad Z_\mu=c_W A_\mu^3-s_W B_\mu,\qquad A_\mu^\gamma=s_W A_\mu^3+c_W B_\mu,
$$
where $s_W=\sin\theta_W$, $c_W=\cos\theta_W$, and $\tan\theta_W=g'/g$. Direct substitution into the <gauge-covariant kinetic term> gives
$$
D_\mu\phi=\binom{\displaystyle \frac{ig}{2}(v+H)W_\mu^+}{\displaystyle \frac{\partial_\mu H}{\sqrt2}+\frac{i(v+H)}{2\sqrt2}(-gA_\mu^3+g'B_\mu)},
$$
and hence
$$
(D_\mu\phi)^\dagger D^\mu\phi=\frac12(\partial H)^2+\frac{g^2(v+H)^2}{4}W_\mu^-W^{+\mu}+\frac{(v+H)^2}{8}(gA_\mu^3-g'B_\mu)^2.
$$
The <electroweak doublet gauge-boson mass matrix> in the $(A^3,B)$ basis is
$$
M^2_{\rm neutral}=\frac{v^2}{4}\begin{pmatrix}g^2&-gg'\\-gg'&g'^2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its normalized zero direction is $(s_W,c_W)$ and its massive direction is $(c_W,-s_W)$. Thus the scalar-vector terms in physical fields are
$$
\mathcal L_{H,\rm vectors}=m_W^2\left(1+\frac H v\right)^2W_\mu^-W^{+\mu}+\frac12m_Z^2\left(1+\frac H v\right)^2Z_\mu Z^\mu,
$$
with the <tree-level electroweak gauge-boson masses>
$$
\boxed{m_W=\frac{gv}{2},\qquad m_Z=\frac{v\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}}2,\qquad m_\gamma=0,\qquad m_W=m_Z\cos\theta_W.}
$$
These are also charge eigenstates. The unbroken <U(1) gauge symmetry> has generator $Q=T_3+Y$; on the <Higgs doublet>, $Y=I/2$. Since $[Q,T_\pm]=\pm T_\pm$, the combinations $W^\pm$ have charges $\pm1$, while Z, the <photon>, and H are neutral. The <photon> disappears from the displayed scalar-vector terms because $gA^3-g'B=\sqrt{g^2+g'^2}Z$. **The photon is massless and has no direct tree-level coupling to the remaining radial scalar in this sector.** This does not remove its interactions with charged <W bosons>, or exclude a scalar-to-photon interaction generated by loops.

Finally, retain the printed normalization of the <Higgs field potential>:
$$
V(H)=\frac\lambda2\left(vH+\frac{H^2}{2}\right)^2=\frac{\lambda v^2}{2}H^2+\frac{\lambda v}{2}H^3+\frac\lambda8H^4.
$$
The kinetic term is $\tfrac12(\partial H)^2$, so the <Higgs radial mass for a half-normalized quartic potential> is **$\boxed{m_H^2=\lambda v^2}$**. The extra factor of two familiar from a potential with prefactor $\lambda$, rather than $\lambda/2$, does not apply here.

= Solution
{parent=2}

The derivative convention uses <hypercharge> Y with $Q=T_3+Y$. For each <lepton> family $l=e,\mu,\tau$, the left-handed fields form an <SU(2)> doublet $L_l=(\nu_l,l)_L^T$, with $Y=-1/2$. Their <weak isospin> values are $T_3=+1/2,-1/2$ and their electric charges are $0,-1$. The right-handed charged <lepton> $l_R$ is an <SU(2)> singlet, with $T_3=0$, $Y=-1$ and charge $-1$. The minimal model used here has no right-handed <neutrino>. A sterile right-handed <neutrino>, if added as a singlet with $Y=0$, would have no <charged weak current> interaction. Antiparticles carry the conjugate representations and opposite electric charges.

Expanding $\bar L_l i\gamma^\mu D_\mu L_l$ supplies $-g\bar L_l\gamma^\mu A_\mu^a\sigma_aL_l/2$. The off-diagonal entries of $A^a\sigma_a$ are $\sqrt2W^+$ and $\sqrt2W^-$. With the <chiral projector> $P_L=(1-\gamma^5)/2$, one has $\bar\nu_L\gamma^\mu l_L=\bar\nu\gamma^\mu P_L l$. Thus the two <W boson> interactions are
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{W^+,l}=-\frac{g}{2\sqrt2}W_\mu^+\bar\nu_l\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)l,\qquad\mathcal L_{W^-,l}=-\frac{g}{2\sqrt2}W_\mu^-\bar l\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)\nu_l.}
$$
For <quarks>, separate rotations diagonalize the up-type and down-type mass terms. If their left-handed rotations are $U_{uL}$ and $U_{dL}$, the charged interaction contains the <CKM matrix> $V=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}$:
$$
J_{\rm had}^\mu=\sum_{i,j}\bar u_i\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)V_{ij}d_j.
$$
There is an implicit sum over <color charge>. This <quark mixing> permits transitions between different families, rather than just the paired fields in a single doublet.

Let $J^\mu=\sum_l\bar\nu_l\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)l+J_{\rm had}^\mu$ and $\kappa=g/(2\sqrt2)$. At momenta much smaller than $m_W$, neglect derivatives in the heavy <W boson> equation. Its mass and source terms can be completed to a square:
$$
m_W^2W_\mu^+W^{-\mu}-\kappa(W_\mu^+J^\mu+W_\mu^-J^{\dagger\mu})=m_W^2\left(W_\mu^+-\frac\kappa{m_W^2}J_\mu^\dagger\right)\left(W^{-\mu}-\frac\kappa{m_W^2}J^\mu\right)-\frac{\kappa^2}{m_W^2}J_\mu^\dagger J^\mu.
$$
This explicitly performs <integrating out a charged vector boson>. The resulting <Fermi interaction> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_{\rm eff}=-\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}J_\mu^\dagger J^\mu,\qquad\frac{G_F}{\sqrt2}=\frac{g^2}{8m_W^2}.}
$$
The omitted terms contain derivatives and are relatively suppressed by momentum squared divided by $m_W^2$.

For the <leptonic pion decay>, <Lorentz covariance> restricts a vacuum-to-spin-zero current matrix element to a constant times $p^\alpha$. In the rest frame, rotations eliminate the spatial components. Under <parity>, the temporal vector current is even, whereas the vacuum is even and the <pion> is odd, so its temporal matrix element equals its negative and vanishes. The temporal axial current is odd and is allowed. This proves the <vacuum-to-pseudoscalar current selection rule>, rather than merely assuming an axial current. Define the <pion decay constant> by
$$
\langle0|\bar u\gamma^\alpha\gamma^5d|\pi^-(p)\rangle=if_\pi p^\alpha.
$$
Then the vector-minus-axial <charged weak current> has matrix element $-if_\pi V_{ud}p^\alpha$, up to an irrelevant overall phase convention. Consequently the <scattering amplitude> for the decay can be written
$$
\mathcal M=\frac{iG_Ff_\pi V_{ud}}{\sqrt2}\,p_\alpha\bar u_e(k)\gamma^\alpha(1-\gamma^5)v_\nu(q).
$$
Use $p=k+q$, the momentum-space <Dirac equation> $\bar u_e\not k=m_e\bar u_e$, and $\not qv_\nu=0$ for a massless <neutrino>. Since $\not q(1-\gamma^5)=(1+\gamma^5)\not q$, the neutrino contribution vanishes, leaving
$$
\mathcal M=\frac{iG_Ff_\pi V_{ud}m_e}{\sqrt2}\bar u_e(1-\gamma^5)v_\nu.
$$
The <fermion spin sum> is, using the supplied <gamma matrix trace identities>,
$$
\sum_{\rm spins}|\bar u_e(1-\gamma^5)v_\nu|^2=\operatorname{tr}\bigl[(\not k+m_e)(1-\gamma^5)\not q(1+\gamma^5)\bigr]=8k\cdot q=4(m_\pi^2-m_e^2).
$$
Here $(k+q)^2=m_\pi^2$, $k^2=m_e^2$, and $q^2=0$. The mass term in the trace vanishes; anticommuting $\gamma^5$ reduces the remaining trace to twice $\operatorname{tr}(\not k\not q)$. It follows that
$$
\boxed{\sum_{\rm spins}|\mathcal M|^2=2G_F^2f_\pi^2|V_{ud}|^2m_e^2(m_\pi^2-m_e^2).}
$$
Using <two-body decay phase space>, with final momentum magnitude $(m_\pi^2-m_e^2)/(2m_\pi)$, gives the <leptonic pseudoscalar decay width>
$$
\Gamma(\pi^-\to e^-\bar\nu_e)=\frac{G_F^2f_\pi^2|V_{ud}|^2m_\pi m_e^2}{8\pi}\left(1-\frac{m_e^2}{m_\pi^2}\right)^2.
$$
Thus the requested $m_e^2(m_\pi^2-m_e^2)$ factor is already present in the squared amplitude, and the phase space supplies one more threshold factor.

The physical explanation is <helicity suppression>. In the <pion> rest frame the two momenta are opposite. The massless antineutrino has positive <helicity>; zero total angular momentum would require the electron to have positive <helicity> too. The <charged weak current> instead creates a left-chiral electron, whose <helicity> is negative when its mass vanishes. A nonzero electron mass admits the required opposite <helicity> with an amplitude proportional to $m_e$. **The decay amplitude vanishes for $m_e=0$, and the rate is suppressed by $m_e^2$.**

= Solution
{parent=3}

Start with the <Dirac equation> $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-m)\psi=0$. The equation for the <Dirac adjoint> is $i(\partial_\mu\bar\psi)\gamma^\mu+m\bar\psi=0$. Transpose it and multiply by the <charge-conjugation matrix> C to obtain
$$
iC(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}\partial_\mu\psi^C+m\psi^C=0,\qquad\psi^C=C\bar\psi^T.
$$
For this to be the original <Dirac equation> multiplied by minus one, the necessary matrix identity is
$$
\boxed{C(\gamma^\mu)^TC^{-1}=-\gamma^\mu.}
$$
Using $C^\dagger=C^{-1}$, the <charge conjugation of the Dirac adjoint> follows directly:
$$
\overline{\psi^C}=(C\bar\psi^T)^\dagger\gamma^0=\psi^T(\gamma^0)^TC^{-1}\gamma^0=-\psi^TC^{-1}.
$$
The final step uses $(\gamma^0)^TC^{-1}=-C^{-1}\gamma^0$ and $(\gamma^0)^2=1$.

Let $j_V^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$, $j_A^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\psi$, and $j_L=j_V-j_A$. For anticommuting fields, or normal-ordered operator bilinears, the minus sign in the transformed <Dirac adjoint> cancels the minus sign from interchanging the two fields. The <charge conjugation of fermion bilinears> is therefore
$$
\bar\psi\Gamma\psi\longmapsto\bar\psi(C^{-1}\Gamma C)^T\psi.
$$
The matrix identity just proved gives $j_V\mapsto-j_V$. Also $C(\gamma^5)^TC^{-1}=\gamma^5$, obtained by transposing its four <gamma matrices> and reversing their order. Hence
$$
(C^{-1}\gamma^\mu\gamma^5 C)^T=\gamma^5(-\gamma^\mu)=\gamma^\mu\gamma^5,
$$
so $j_A\mapsto j_A$ and $j_L\mapsto-j_R$, where $j_R=j_V+j_A$. Since the external vector V is odd under <charge conjugation>, $j_L\cdot V$ becomes $j_R\cdot V$ and is generally not invariant.

For <parity symmetry in quantum field theory>, write $P^\mu{}_{\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)$. The given spinor transformation gives $j_V\mapsto Pj_V$, $j_A\mapsto-Pj_A$, and $V\mapsto PV$, with arguments evaluated at the reflected point. The contracted interaction again becomes $j_R\cdot V$. Each of <parity> and <charge conjugation> interchanges the two chiral contractions; applying both restores $j_L\cdot V$. **This particular neutral current interaction is CP invariant, although neither P nor C is separately a symmetry.** This conclusion uses the stipulated C-odd vector transformation; it is not a claim that all interactions of the <Standard Model> preserve <CP symmetry>.

For <time reversal of a Dirac field>, the <gamma matrix adjoint and transpose identities> give
$$
(\gamma^\mu)^*=(\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0)^T,\qquad C(\gamma^\mu)^*C^{-1}=-\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0.
$$
With $B=\gamma^5C$, anticommutation of $\gamma^5$ with every <gamma matrix> yields
$$
B(\gamma^\mu)^*B^{-1}=\gamma^0\gamma^\mu\gamma^0=t_\mu\gamma^\mu,\qquad t_0=1,\quad t_i=-1.
$$
Equivalently, **$\boxed{B(\gamma^{0*},-\boldsymbol\gamma^*)B^{-1}=(\gamma^0,\boldsymbol\gamma)}$**. Since <time reversal> is an <antiunitary operator>, the factor i in $\gamma^5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$ must also be conjugated. Thus
$$
B\gamma^{5*}B^{-1}=-i(t_0t_1t_2t_3)\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3=\gamma^5.
$$
Using the stated transformations of the field and its <Dirac adjoint>, the transformed bilinear consequently is
$$
\bar\psi(x_T)B[\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma^5)]^*B^{-1}\psi(x_T)=t_\mu j_L^\mu(x_T).
$$
The external vector has the same component signs. Their contraction is unchanged, proving <time reversal of a real neutral chiral vector interaction>. A real coupling is understood; an <antiunitary operator> also conjugates coefficients, and relative complex weak phases need not satisfy this invariance.

A <charged weak box contribution to kaon mixing> is shown below. The incoming $d\bar s$ and outgoing $s\bar d$ connect through two charged <W bosons> and two internal up-type <quarks>. The black arrows are fermion flow, so the lower line's arrows oppose the momentum of the corresponding antiquark. The blue arrows specify the direction of positive charge carried by the charged propagators. Each vertex is a <charged weak current> vertex, with the appropriate <CKM matrix> element; the internal flavors are summed over $u,c,t$.

\Image[paper-48-kaon-box.png]
{title=Charged weak box diagram converting a neutral kaon into its antiparticle}

Use precisely the printed <exchange-positive CP convention for neutral kaon mixing>. In the flavor basis, <CP symmetry> acts by $S=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Write the restricted effective matrix as
$$
M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad b=M_{12},\quad c=M_{21}.
$$
Then $SMS=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&c\\b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, so CP invariance of this mixing operator is exactly $b=c$. Therefore **CP noninvariance in this neutral-kaon mixing block means $M_{12}\ne M_{21}$**. Violation elsewhere in a larger Hamiltonian would not, by itself, prove an inequality for this particular block.

For <neutral-kaon mass matrix diagonalization>, choose correlated roots $r^2=b$, $s^2=c$, and eigenvalues $\lambda_\pm=a\pm rs$. The vectors $(r,s)^T$ and $(r,-s)^T$ are the corresponding eigenvectors, as direct multiplication verifies. Taking orthonormal flavor states and writing the printed <CP eigenstates> as $K_1=(K^0+\bar K^0)/\sqrt2$ and $K_2=(K^0-\bar K^0)/\sqrt2$, one finds
$$
rK^0+s\bar K^0=\frac{r+s}{\sqrt2}(K_1+\epsilon K_2),\qquad rK^0-s\bar K^0=\frac{r+s}{\sqrt2}(K_2+\epsilon K_1),
$$
where the <kaon CP mixing parameter> is
$$
\boxed{\epsilon=\frac{\sqrt{M_{12}}-\sqrt{M_{21}}}{\sqrt{M_{12}}+\sqrt{M_{21}}},\qquad K_+=\frac{K_1+\epsilon K_2}{\sqrt{1+|\epsilon|^2}},\qquad K_-=\frac{K_2+\epsilon K_1}{\sqrt{1+|\epsilon|^2}}.}
$$
Overall phases have been absorbed. The <kaon mixing square-root branch convention> must be chosen continuously so that $r=s$ in the CP-conserving limit for the eigenstate labeled plus. These formulas are exact in the nondegenerate case with $r+s\ne0$; the requested small admixtures follow if $|\epsilon|\ll1$. Smallness is an additional weak-CP-violation assumption, not a consequence of $b\ne c$ alone. A Hermitian mass operator has $c=b^*$; the same algebra also applies to the effective non-Hermitian operator that includes decays, where the two eigenstates need not be orthogonal. At a fully degenerate $b=c=0$, a CP eigenbasis can simply be chosen.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Put $s=q^2>0$ and neglect electron and final-quark masses compared with $\sqrt s$. First fix a final <color charge>; its multiplicity will be restored for the inclusive rate. The photon-exchange <scattering amplitude> is, up to an overall phase,
$$
\mathcal M=\frac{e^2Q_q}{s}[\bar v(p_2)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)][\bar u(k_1)\gamma_\mu v(k_2)].
$$
Averaging over the two spins of each incident particle and summing final spins gives
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac{e^4Q_q^2}{4s^2}\operatorname{tr}(\not p_2\gamma^\mu\not p_1\gamma^\nu)\operatorname{tr}(\not k_1\gamma_\mu\not k_2\gamma_\nu).
$$
By the <gamma matrix trace identities>, the first trace is $4(p_2^\mu p_1^\nu+p_2^\nu p_1^\mu-g^{\mu\nu}p_1\cdot p_2)$, and likewise for the second. Contracting the two tensors cancels the terms proportional to $(p_1\cdot p_2)(k_1\cdot k_2)$, giving
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=\frac{8e^4Q_q^2}{s^2}\bigl[(p_1\cdot k_1)(p_2\cdot k_2)+(p_1\cdot k_2)(p_2\cdot k_1)\bigr].
$$
In the centre-of-mass frame all four energies are $\sqrt s/2$. The first pair of scalar products equals $s(1-\cos\theta)/4$ each, and the second pair equals $s(1+\cos\theta)/4$ each. Hence $\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=e^4Q_q^2(1+\cos^2\theta)$.

For clarity, the printed flux hint has a factor-of-four inconsistency: when $\mathcal F=4\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}=2s$, the prefactor multiplying the spin-averaged amplitude is $1/\mathcal F$, not $1/(4\mathcal F)$. Alternatively, $1/(4F_0)$ is correct if $F_0=\sqrt{(p_1\cdot p_2)^2-m_1^2m_2^2}=s/2$. Use this <massless two-body scattering flux normalization>, keeping the spin average separate.

To obtain the <two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space> directly, integrate the momentum-conserving spatial delta function, leaving $\boldsymbol k_2=-\boldsymbol k_1$. The remaining radial integral is $\int dk\,\delta(\sqrt s-2k)=1/2$, so $d\Phi_2/d\Omega=1/(32\pi^2)$. The <relativistic scattering cross-section> is consequently
$$
\boxed{\frac{d\sigma_{\rm one\ color}}{d\Omega}=\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}{64\pi^2s}=\frac{\alpha^2Q_q^2}{4s}(1+\cos^2\theta).}
$$
Since $\int d\Omega(1+\cos^2\theta)=2\pi\int_{-1}^1(1+x^2)\,dx=16\pi/3$, the integrated <electron-positron annihilation into a quark pair> result, for this fixed color, is
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm one\ color}=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2Q_q^2}{3s}.}
$$
The first two printed formulas are thus color-stripped. Distinct final colors are orthogonal states and must be summed, producing $N_c=3$. At leading order each accessible <quark> flavor contributes independently. Away from thresholds and narrow resonances, <quark-hadron duality> relates the inclusive parton rate to the observed <inclusive electron-positron annihilation into hadrons>, giving
$$
\boxed{\sigma_{\rm had}^{(\gamma)}\simeq\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}\,3\sum_{f\ {
m accessible}}Q_f^2.}
$$
This formula concerns the photon channel, assumes $\sqrt s$ is large compared with the relevant quark masses and the strong-interaction scale, and does not include <Z boson> exchange.

Beyond leading order, an exactly exclusive pair of colored <quarks> is not an observable asymptotic state because of <confinement> and <hadronization>. Moreover its virtual corrections have <infrared divergences>; real soft or collinear gluon emission cannot be excluded if a finite rate is wanted. The inclusive sum includes both the corrected two-parton channel and channels such as $q\bar qg$, with their degenerate soft and collinear limits. Real and virtual <infrared divergences> then cancel for an <infrared-safe observable>. A specified jet observable can also be meaningful if it defines an <infrared-safe observable>; a bare two-quark final state is not.

A convenient inclusive calculation uses the <optical theorem>. For the electromagnetic hadronic current $j_\mu=\sum_fQ_f\bar q_f\gamma_\mu q_f$, define
$$
i\int d^4x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\langle0|Tj_\mu(x)j_\nu(0)|0\rangle=(q_\mu q_\nu-q^2g_{\mu\nu})\Pi(q^2).
$$
The <hadronic electromagnetic-current spectral density> is obtained from its discontinuity. In this convention the <hadronic R ratio> is $R(s)=\sigma_{\rm had}/\sigma_{\mu\mu}=12\pi\operatorname{Im}\Pi(s+i0)$, where $\sigma_{\mu\mu}=4\pi\alpha^2/(3s)$ at this order. Cutting the forward amplitude sums all allowed hadronic intermediate states, so perturbative <quantum chromodynamics> applied to this inclusive quantity implements the required sum of real and virtual contributions.

Finally, write $a=\alpha_s(\mu^2)$, $C=1.99-0.11n_f$, $L=\ln(s/\mu^2)$, and $\beta_0=11-2n_f/3$. The given truncated correction is
$$
K=1+\frac a\pi+\frac{a^2}{\pi^2}\left(C-\frac{\beta_0}{4}L\right).
$$
Differentiate with respect to $t=\ln\mu^2$, using the stated one-loop <beta function (physics)> $da/dt=-\beta_0a^2/(4\pi)$ and $dL/dt=-1$. The derivative of $a/\pi$ cancels the explicit logarithmic derivative at order $a^2$. Keeping the residual derivative of the truncated expression gives
$$
\frac{dK}{dt}=-\frac{\beta_0a^3}{2\pi^3}\left(C-\frac{\beta_0}{4}L\right).
$$
Thus the <principle of minimal sensitivity>, applied to this expression with nonzero coupling and $\beta_0\ne0$, selects the <one-loop stationary scale for the hadronic annihilation correction>
$$
\boxed{\mu_*^2=s\exp\left[-\frac{4(1.99-0.11n_f)}{11-2n_f/3}\right].}
$$
This is a prescription based on the residual order-$a^3$ scale dependence of an order-$a^2$ approximation. At strictly order $a^2$, the derivative already vanishes for every <renormalization scale>; the prescription does not assert exact scale dependence of the observable. It is useful only while the selected <running coupling> remains perturbative. If $a=0$ or $\beta_0=0$, the stated derivative imposes no unique scale.
