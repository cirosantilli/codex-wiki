Use rational coefficients and finite <singular chains>. For an $N$-dimensional <stratified pseudomanifold> with filtration $X_j$, the <lower middle perversity> is
$$
\boxed{\bar m(k)=\left\lfloor\frac{k-2}{2}\right\rfloor\quad(k\ge2).}
$$
A <singular simplex> $\sigma:\Delta^i\to X$ is an <allowable singular simplex> when, for every $k\ge2$, $\sigma^{-1}(X_{N-k})$ lies in the $(i-k+\bar m(k))$-skeleton of $\Delta^i$; negative-dimensional skeletons are empty. Let $A_i$ be the span of these simplices in $S_i(X;\mathbb Q)$. The <intersection chains> are
$$
IS_i(X)=\{c\in A_i:\partial c\in A_{i-1}\}.
$$
The condition on $\partial c$ is imposed after combining equal simplices and cancelling coefficients: the faces of an allowable simplex need not themselves be allowable. If $c\in IS_i$, then $\partial c\in A_{i-1}$ and $\partial(\partial c)=0$, so $\partial c\in IS_{i-1}$. Thus $IS_*$ is a <chain subcomplex> of $S_*$, and $IH_i(X)=H_i(IS_*)$ defines <intersection homology>.

Write $v$ for the vertex of $cY$. Its codimension is $2n$, so the vertex condition for an allowable $i$-simplex is
$$
\sigma^{-1}(v)\subseteq (\Delta^i)^{(i-n-1)}.
$$
In particular all simplices of degree at most $n$ avoid $v$. For $i<n$, both the degree-$i$ chains and the degree-$(i+1)$ chains that can bound them lie in $cY\setminus\{v\}=Y\times(0,1)$. The two <chain complexes> therefore have the same cycles and boundaries in degree $i$, proving the low-degree isomorphism.

For $i\ge n$, let $z$ be an allowable $i$-cycle. Cone every simplex of $z$ radially to $v$, using the cone on $\Delta^i$ as $\Delta^{i+1}$, and write the resulting chain as $cz$. At the vertex, coning an old inverse image in the $(i-n-1)$-skeleton gives a subset of the $(i-n)$-skeleton, and the new cone vertex also lies in this skeleton because $i-n\ge0$. For every other singular stratum, coning the inverse image increases its permitted skeleton dimension by one, exactly as increasing the simplex dimension does. Thus $cz$ is allowable. The usual cone identity is $\partial(cz)=z-c(\partial z)=z$; since $z$ is allowable, the boundary condition for <intersection chains> holds as well. Every cycle is consequently a boundary. This proves the <intersection homology cone formula> here:
$$
\boxed{IH_i(cY)=\begin{cases}IH_i(Y\times(0,1)),&i<n,\\0,&i\ge n.\end{cases}}
$$
The assumption $\dim Y=2n-1$ entails $n\ge1$, so no augmented degree-zero exception arises in this coning argument.

For the <suspension of a topological space>, the two endpoints are collapsed separately, giving two distinct vertices. Use the following standard computational facts for <intersection homology>: a product with an interval has the same <intersection homology> as the original <stratified space>, via the projection and its stratum-preserving homotopy inverse; and an open cover has a <Mayer–Vietoris sequence> whose overlap map is the signed pair of inclusion maps. Cover the suspension by its two open cones. Their overlap is $Y\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$. Below degree $n$, the overlap map is, under the interval identifications, $a\mapsto(a,-a)$ in $IH_i(Y)\oplus IH_i(Y)$, hence is injective with cokernel $IH_i(Y)$. In degree $n$ both cone groups vanish and the next overlap map, in degree $n-1$, is still injective. Above degree $n$ both adjacent cone groups vanish. Exactness therefore gives
$$
\boxed{IH_i(\operatorname{Susp}Y)=\begin{cases}IH_i(Y),&i<n,\\0,&i=n,\\IH_{i-1}(Y),&i>n.\end{cases}}
$$
This differs from ordinary reduced suspension homology: below the cutoff the cycles cannot use either vertex, and in the middle degree coning kills them. If $Y$ is a compact oriented <Witt space>, its <Poincare duality> pairs $IH_i(Y)$ with $IH_{2n-1-i}(Y)$. The displayed groups of the suspension are correspondingly paired in complementary degrees $i$ and $2n-i$, while its middle group is zero. The new vertices have even codimension $2n$ and thus impose no additional <Witt space> condition; the suspension is again Witt. Its middle <intersection form> has zero rank and, when its dimension is divisible by four, zero <signature>.

Put $B=\partial M$ and let $v$ be the cone vertex. Specify the convention away from the Witt case: first take <intersection cohomology> to mean the <hypercohomology> of the <intersection complex> $IC_{\bar m}$, normalized in degree zero on the regular stratum. The <lower middle perversity> at the vertex is $\bar m(2m+1)=m-1$. The standard local cone calculation for this convention says
$$
IH_{\bar m}^i(\widehat U)=\begin{cases}H^i(B;\mathbb Q),&i<m,\\0,&i\ge m,\end{cases}
$$
and restriction to $U\simeq B$ is the identity below the cutoff. Here this can also be read directly from the construction $IC_{\bar m}=\tau_{\le m-1}Rj_*\mathbb Q$, with $j:\widehat M\setminus\{v\}\hookrightarrow\widehat M$: punctured conical neighbourhoods retract onto $B$, and the truncation discards the degrees above $m-1$.

Define relative <intersection cohomology> as the <hypercohomology> of the homotopy fibre of restriction, namely $R\Gamma(\widehat M,\widehat U;IC)=\operatorname{Cone}(R\Gamma(\widehat M;IC)\to R\Gamma(\widehat U;IC))[-1]$. Restricting to the pair $(M,U)$ gives a map to the corresponding ordinary relative <cohomology>, since $IC|_M=\mathbb Q_M$. The induced map of <exact triangles> gives the requested <commutative diagram>:
$$
\begin{array}{ccccccccc}
\cdots\to&IH^i(\widehat M,\widehat U)&\to&IH^i(\widehat M)&\to&IH^i(\widehat U)&\to&IH^{i+1}(\widehat M,\widehat U)&\to\cdots\\
&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&&\downarrow&\\
\cdots\to&H^i(M,U)&\to&H^i(M)&\to&H^i(U)&\to&H^{i+1}(M,U)&\to\cdots .
\end{array}
$$
Both rows are <long exact cohomology sequences of a pair>. Commutativity includes the <connecting homomorphisms>, because they arise from the same map of restriction cones, with a common sign convention. The relative vertical maps are isomorphisms: <excision> removes the common conical neighbourhood, leaving precisely the <manifold> pair outside it, where $IC$ is constant. Also the collar retracts onto $B$, so $H^*(M,U)\cong H^*(M,B)$.

The only possible nonmanifold stratum is $v$, with link $B$ of dimension $2m$. Since <intersection homology> of a <manifold> is ordinary homology, the exact necessary and sufficient condition is
$$
\boxed{\widehat M\text{ is Witt}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H^m(B;\mathbb Q)=0.}
$$
The equivalence with the defining $H_m(B;\mathbb Q)=0$ follows from the <universal coefficient theorem for cohomology> over $\mathbb Q$. In the Witt case the cone restriction is an isomorphism through degree $m$, since both degree-$m$ groups are zero. Comparing the two exact rows, or applying the <five lemma> to each five-term segment, gives $IH^i(\widehat M)\cong H^i(M)$ for $i\le m$. For $i>m$, both $IH^{i-1}(\widehat U)$ and $IH^i(\widehat U)$ vanish, so the top row identifies the absolute group with the relative one. Hence
$$
\boxed{IH^i(\widehat M)\cong\begin{cases}H^i(M;\mathbb Q),&i\le m,\\H^i(M,B;\mathbb Q),&i>m.\end{cases}}
$$
Without the Witt hypothesis, comparison still gives $IH_{\bar m}^i(\widehat M)\cong H^i(M)$ for $i<m$. At degree $m$ the upper row reads
$$
H^{m-1}(B)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^m(M,B)\longrightarrow IH_{\bar m}^m(\widehat M)\longrightarrow0.
$$
The lower row identifies the quotient by $\operatorname{im}\delta$ with the image of the relative-to-absolute map. In higher degrees both cone terms vanish. Thus the full lower-middle Deligne answer is
$$
\boxed{IH_{\bar m}^i(\widehat M)\cong\begin{cases}H^i(M),&i<m,\\\operatorname{im}(H^m(M,B)\to H^m(M)),&i=m,\\H^i(M,B),&i>m.\end{cases}}
$$
For clarity, if $IH^i$ is instead defined as the linear dual of the lower-middle <intersection chains> of question 1, it corresponds to the complementary <upper middle perversity> Deligne <sheaf>. Its cone group retains $H^m(B)$ as well. The same diagram then gives
$$
\boxed{IH_{\bar n}^i(\widehat M)\cong\begin{cases}H^i(M),&i\le m,\\\operatorname{im}(H^{m+1}(M,B)\to H^{m+1}(M)),&i=m+1,\\H^i(M,B),&i>m+1.\end{cases}}
$$
Equivalently the exceptional group is $H^{m+1}(M,B)/\operatorname{im}(H^m(B)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{m+1}(M,B))$. These conventions agree under the stated Witt condition, explaining why that condition removes the ambiguity.

Take the solid <torus> $M=S^1\times D^2$, whose boundary is the two-dimensional <torus> $B=T^2$. Its ordinary <cohomology> is $H^0(M)=H^1(M)=\mathbb Q$, zero otherwise; <Poincare duality> for a <manifold> with boundary gives $H^2(M,B)=H^3(M,B)=\mathbb Q$ and all other relative groups zero. The lower-middle Deligne formula gives
$$
\boxed{\dim IH_{\bar m}^i(\widehat M)=(1,0,1,1)\quad\text{for }i=0,1,2,3.}
$$
Thus degrees one and two have different dimensions, so there can be no three-dimensional <Poincare duality>. In the dual-lower-chain convention the dimensions are $(1,1,0,1)$, which likewise fail duality. Both computations exhibit the obstruction $H^1(T^2)=\mathbb Q^2\ne0$.

All <cohomology> below has rational coefficients. First describe the incidence space geometrically. The image of $g$ is $\mathbb P(W)\cong\mathbb {CP}^1$, inside $\operatorname{Gr}(1,4)\cong\mathbb {CP}^3$. For a line $U\subset W$, choosing a plane $V$ containing $U$ is equivalent to choosing the line $V/U$ in $\mathbb C^4/U$. If $\mathcal L$ is the tautological line bundle on $\mathbb P(W)$, then
$$
F_W\cong\mathbb P\big((\mathbb P(W)\times\mathbb C^4)/\mathcal L\big).
$$
This is the <projective bundle> of a rank-three quotient bundle, with fibre $\mathbb {CP}^2$. Local frames of the quotient bundle give local product charts. In particular $F_W$ is an oriented real six-dimensional <manifold>.

For $V\in S_W\setminus\{W\}$, the intersection $V\cap W$ is exactly a line, so $f^{-1}(V)$ is a single point and the inverse map is $V\mapsto(V\cap W,V)$. The intersection line varies continuously, indeed holomorphically, wherever its dimension is constant: in local <Grassmannian> charts it is the kernel of a matrix of constant rank, whose kernel bases can be chosen locally. Thus $f$ is an isomorphism over the open stratum. At the remaining point,
$$
\boxed{f^{-1}(W)=\mathbb P(W)\cong\mathbb {CP}^1.}
$$
Set $K=Rf_*\mathbb Q_{F_W}$, a <derived direct image of sheaves>. The <proper base change for sheaves> theorem identifies its derived stalks with the <cohomology> complexes of the fibres. Consequently $K$ is the constant rational <sheaf> in degree zero on $S_W\setminus\{W\}$, and
$$
H^a(i_W^*K)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Q,&a=0,2,\\0,&a\ne0,2.\end{cases}
$$
The <cohomology sheaves> are therefore finite-rank <local systems> on the two strata, with no other nonzero degrees. This proves that $K$ is a <constructible complex of sheaves> and verifies normalization. The open stratum has real dimension six, so the isolated point has codimension six. Its middle <perversity> is $\bar m(6)=2$, and the <intersection-complex support and cosupport axioms> require stalk vanishing in degrees above two. The fibre calculation verifies the support axiom, as well as vanishing in negative degrees.

For an oriented real six-dimensional <manifold>, <Verdier duality> gives $D_{F_W}\mathbb Q_{F_W}\cong\mathbb Q_{F_W}[6]$. For a <proper map>, <Verdier duality> commutes with derived direct image. Applying these statements here yields
$$
D_{S_W}K\cong Rf_*D_{F_W}\mathbb Q_{F_W}\cong Rf_*\mathbb Q_{F_W}[6]=K[6],\qquad
\boxed{\Sigma^{-6}D_{S_W}K\cong K.}
$$
The duality identity $i_W^!D_{S_W}K\cong D_{\{W\}}i_W^*K$ converts this into
$$
H^a(i_W^!K)\cong H^{6-a}(i_W^*K)^*.
$$
Here $i_W^!K$ is the <costalk>, and dualization of rational complexes reverses degrees. Thus its only nonzero groups are $\mathbb Q$ in degrees four and six. In particular they vanish for $a\le3$, which is precisely the cosupport axiom at this isolated six-dimensional singularity. The uniqueness characterization by constructibility, normalization, support and cosupport now identifies $K$ with the unshifted <intersection complex> of $S_W$. Taking <hypercohomology>, and using the composition of derived global sections with $Rf_*$, gives
$$
\boxed{IH^*(S_W)\cong\mathbb H^*(S_W,K)\cong H^*(F_W).}
$$
Finally apply the fibration result allowed in the question to $g:F_W\to\mathbb P(W)$. Its base is the simply connected <complex projective line>, its fibre is the <complex projective plane>, and the projective-bundle charts above verify the fibration hypothesis. Hence, as graded rational <vector spaces>,
$$
\boxed{IH^*(S_W)\cong H^*(\mathbb {CP}^1)\otimes H^*(\mathbb {CP}^2).}
$$
The resulting <Betti numbers> and <Poincare polynomial> are
$$
\boxed{\dim IH^i(S_W)=\begin{cases}1,&i=0,6,\\2,&i=2,4,\\0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad P_{IH}(t)=1+2t^2+2t^4+t^6.}
$$
This uses $H^*(\mathbb {CP}^r)=\mathbb Q[h]/(h^{r+1})$ with $\deg h=2$. No assertion that an arbitrary projective fibration has the product <cohomology> ring is needed.

An $N$-dimensional <stratified pseudomanifold> has a closed filtration $\varnothing=X_{-1}\subseteq X_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_N=X$ with each nonempty $X_s\setminus X_{s-1}$ an $s$-dimensional <manifold>, dense top stratum, and $X_{N-1}=X_{N-2}$. Each point of an $s$-dimensional stratum has a stratum-preserving conical neighbourhood $\mathbb R^s\times cL$, where $L$ is a compact $(N-s-1)$-dimensional <stratified pseudomanifold>. The definition is inductive, starting with zero-dimensional spaces. There are no singular codimension-one strata; connectedness of links is not required. One may work in the usual triangulable category used for <Witt bordism>.

A rational <Witt space> is such a <stratified pseudomanifold> for which every stratum of odd codimension $2r+1$ has link $L$ satisfying $IH_r^{\bar m}(L;\mathbb Q)=0$. No extra condition is imposed at even codimension. A <Witt space with collared boundary> has Witt interior and Witt boundary, and a stratum-preserving collar $\partial X\times[0,\varepsilon)$ with the product stratification. An <orientation> means an <orientation> of the regular stratum, inducing the boundary <orientation> on its collar.

A three-pronged graph, with its central vertex and open edges as strata, is a <stratified space> but not a one-dimensional <pseudomanifold>: the central vertex is a singular codimension-one stratum, and deleting it leaves three local branches instead of the two branches of a one-manifold. For a non-Witt <pseudomanifold>, take the <suspension> of $T^2$. Its two vertices have codimension three and link $T^2$, whose middle group is $IH_1(T^2;\mathbb Q)=H_1(T^2;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q^2$. It violates the <Witt space> condition.

The rational <Witt bordism classification> in dimensions divisible by four states that, for every $k\ge1$, the middle <intersection form> induces an isomorphism
$$
\boxed{\Omega_{4k}^{\mathrm{Witt}}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }W(\mathbb Q),\qquad [X]\longmapsto[IH^{2k}(X;\mathbb Q),\lambda_X].}
$$
Here $W(\mathbb Q)$ is the <Witt group of a field>, so a nonsingular symmetric form represents zero precisely when it is metabolic, that is, when it has a half-dimensional totally isotropic subspace. The <complex projective plane> gives a nonzero example: its middle group is generated by the degree-two class $h$, with $\langle h^2,[\mathbb {CP}^2]\rangle=1$. Its form is $\langle1\rangle$, of <signature> one; a metabolic form has <signature> zero. Thus $[\mathbb {CP}^2]\ne0$ in $\Omega_4^{\mathrm{Witt}}$.

For a closed oriented <Witt space> $X$ of dimension $2k+1$, take the closed cone
$$
Y=(X\times[0,1])/(X\times\{0\}),\qquad\partial Y=X\times\{1\}.
$$
Away from its vertex it is a stratified product with an interval, so all old <Witt space> conditions persist. At the new vertex the codimension is $2k+2$, which is even; hence its odd-dimensional link $X$ creates no new Witt condition. The collar $X\times(1-\varepsilon,1]$ is explicit, and the product <orientation> may be chosen to induce the given <orientation> on $X$. This is a compact oriented <Witt space with collared boundary>, proving
$$
\boxed{\Omega_{2k+1}^{\mathrm{Witt}}=0.}
$$

Now let $\dim X=2$. Its singular strata are zero-dimensional. Their links are compact one-dimensional <pseudomanifolds>, which have no singular strata and are therefore **nonempty finite disjoint unions of circles**. A single circle is allowed, since a chosen stratum need not be intrinsically singular. The Witt condition is automatic in dimension two, because singular strata have even codimension.

Construct the <normalization of a two-dimensional pseudomanifold> concretely. Remove disjoint small conical neighbourhoods of the finitely many zero-stratum points. The remainder is a compact oriented <surface with boundary>, with one boundary circle for each local branch. Cap every such circle with a separate disk. The result $\widetilde X$ is a closed compact oriented topological <surface>. Map each new disk homeomorphically onto its branch of the original cone, sending all the disk centres belonging to one old singular point to that point. Along the disk boundaries this agrees with the identification with the remainder, so it defines a continuous surjection $\pi:\widetilde X\to X$. It is a <homeomorphism> off these finitely many centres, in particular over the prescribed open stratum. Every fibre is a point except over a zero-stratum point, where it is the finite set of its branch centres. Thus every fibre is zero-dimensional, as required.

Each component of $\widetilde X$ bounds an oriented <handlebody>: a <surface> of genus $g$ is the boundary of a three-ball with $g$ one-handles attached. Let $H$ be the disjoint union of these handlebodies, oriented so that $\partial H=\widetilde X$. Form
$$
Z=H\cup_{\pi}(X\times[0,1]),
$$
gluing $x\in\partial H$ to $(\pi(x),0)$. The quotient is compact and Hausdorff, and its only remaining boundary is $X\times\{1\}$, with an explicit product collar. Near regular points of the gluing locus, two half-ball neighbourhoods glue to a three-ball; the induced <orientations> match after choosing the collar <orientation> in the usual boundary-gluing convention.

It remains to check the singular links, rather than merely assert that this quotient is a <Witt space>. If an old point $p$ has $r$ circle branches, the new one-dimensional stratum $\{p\}\times(0,1)$ has link the disjoint union of those $r$ circles. This has codimension two and imposes no Witt condition. At $(p,0)$, each branch supplies a half-three-ball from $H$ and another from the collar. The halves glue to a full three-ball; the different branches share only the positive collar axis. Intersecting with a small link therefore gives $r$ copies of $S^2$ identified at one common point. This is a two-dimensional <pseudomanifold> with zero middle <intersection homology>: an allowable one-cycle avoids the common point, so decomposes into cycles in the $r$ punctured spheres, each homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^2$; each such cycle bounds there. Consequently $IH_1(\bigvee_{a=1}^r S^2;\mathbb Q)=0$. This is exactly the Witt condition at the new codimension-three stratum. The boundary has the required product stratification. Hence $Z$ is a three-dimensional <Witt space with collared boundary> with $\partial Z\cong X$, proving
$$
\boxed{\Omega_2^{\mathrm{Witt}}=0.}
$$

For the final product assertion, “product” means a nontrivial product with at least two positive-dimensional factors; a single four-dimensional factor would allow the preceding $\mathbb {CP}^2$ counterexample. State the standard rational intersection-homology product fact used here: products of <Witt spaces> are Witt, their <intersection cohomology> satisfies the <Künneth theorem>, and the complementary-degree intersection pairing on elementary tensors is the product of the factor pairings, with the sign $(-1)^{\deg\beta\deg\alpha'}$ for $\alpha\otimes\beta$ and $\alpha'\otimes\beta'$. Terms whose degrees in a factor do not add to that factor's dimension pair to zero. Regrouping factors reduces total dimension four to cases $1+3$ or $2+2$.

In the $1+3$ case, write $X=A\times B$. The middle <intersection cohomology> is
$$
IH^2(X)=(IH^0(A)\otimes IH^2(B))\oplus(IH^1(A)\otimes IH^1(B)).
$$
Each summand is totally isotropic, because the degrees in $A$ are respectively $0+0$ and $1+1$, neither equal to one. They pair perfectly with one another by factor <Poincare duality>. Thus either summand has half the total dimension and is a Lagrangian for the middle symmetric form.

For dimensions $2+2$, the middle group is the direct sum
$$
(IH^0(A)\otimes IH^2(B))\oplus(IH^1(A)\otimes IH^1(B))\oplus(IH^2(A)\otimes IH^0(B)).
$$
The outside summands are isotropic and pair perfectly with one another. Each factor's degree-one pairing is a nonsingular skew-symmetric form, so choose a <Lagrangian subspace> $L_A\subset IH^1(A)$. Inside the central summand, $L_A\otimes IH^1(B)$ is isotropic and has half its dimension. It is orthogonal to both outside summands by their degrees. Therefore
$$
(IH^0(A)\otimes IH^2(B))\oplus(L_A\otimes IH^1(B))
$$
is an isotropic subspace of half the full middle dimension. In both cases the form is metabolic. The <Witt bordism classification> now proves the desired **$[X]=0$ in $\Omega_4^{\mathrm{Witt}}$**. This also proves the assertion for more factors, using the regrouping just described.

For a <cochain complex> $E$, define the <shift of a cochain complex> by
$$
\boxed{(\Sigma E)^i=E^{i+1},\qquad d_{\Sigma E}^i=-d_E^{i+1}.}
$$
For a <cochain map> $a:E\to F$, put $(\Sigma a)^i=a^{i+1}$. This is again a <cochain map>, so the construction is a functor. The sign ensures the standard cone identities; the shifted differential squares to zero. Iteration gives $E[r]^i=E^{i+r}$ and differential $(-1)^rd_E$, and $H^i(E[r])=H^{i+r}(E)$.

For $\phi:E\to F$, its algebraic <mapping cone> is
$$
\boxed{C(\phi)^i=F^i\oplus E^{i+1},\qquad d_C(f,e)=(d_Ff+\phi e,-d_Ee).}
$$
Applying this differential twice gives first component $d_F^2f+d_F\phi e-\phi d_Ee=0$, since $\phi$ is a <cochain map>, and second component $d_E^2e=0$. The maps
$$
u:F\to C(\phi),\quad u(f)=(f,0),\qquad v:C(\phi)\to\Sigma E,\quad v(f,e)=e
$$
are <cochain maps>: $d_Cu(f)=(d_Ff,0)=u(d_Ff)$ and $v(d_C(f,e))=-d_Ee=d_{\Sigma E}v(f,e)$. They are maps of complexes of <sheaves>, because every displayed operation is a <sheaf> morphism in each degree.

The <bounded below derived category of sheaves> $D(X)=D^+(X)$ can be constructed by first quotienting the category of bounded below <cochain complexes> by <cochain homotopy> and then formally inverting all <quasi-isomorphisms>. A morphism may be represented by a roof $E\xleftarrow{\sim}E'\to F$. Alternatively, take <injective resolutions of sheaves> and use homotopy classes of maps between the resulting injective complexes. The <cohomology sheaf> functors descend to the localization, because they already identify homotopic maps and turn <quasi-isomorphisms> into isomorphisms. If $E\cong0$ in $D(X)$, applying these functors gives $\mathcal H^i(E)=0$ for every $i$. Conversely, if all these <sheaves> vanish, the unique map $E\to0$ is a <quasi-isomorphism>, which becomes invertible in $D(X)$. Hence
$$
\boxed{E\cong0\text{ in }D(X)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathcal H^i(E)=0\text{ for every }i.}
$$
An <exact triangle in a derived category> is a triangle isomorphic to a cone triangle
$$
E\xrightarrow{\phi}F\xrightarrow{u}C(\phi)\xrightarrow{v}\Sigma E.
$$
In particular the last arrow is part of the structure; a mere sequence of three objects with successive composite zero does not suffice.

Suppose $0\to E\xrightarrow{\phi}F\xrightarrow{\psi}G\to0$ is degreewise exact. Define
$$
r:C(\phi)\to G,\qquad r(f,e)=\psi(f).
$$
Then $r(d_C(f,e))=\psi(d_Ff)+\psi\phi(e)=d_G\psi(f)=d_Gr(f,e)$, so $r$ is a <cochain map>. It is natural under maps of short exact sequences. It is degreewise surjective as a <sheaf> map, and its kernel can be identified with $E^i\oplus E^{i+1}$ using $(a,e)\mapsto(\phi(a),e)$. In these coordinates the kernel differential is
$$
d(a,e)=(d_Ea+e,-d_Ee).
$$
There is a <contracting homotopy> $h^i(a,e)=(0,a)$ in degree $i-1$:
$$
dh(a,e)=(a,-d_Ea),\qquad hd(a,e)=(0,d_Ea+e),\qquad dh+hd=\operatorname{id}.
$$
Thus the kernel has zero <cohomology sheaves>. The <cohomology-sheaf exact sequence> is the sequence of the short exact sequence of complexes $0\to\ker r\to C(\phi)\to G\to0$, or equivalently its ordinary complex-cohomology sequence at every stalk. It follows that $r$ induces isomorphisms of all <cohomology sheaves>, proving
$$
\boxed{C(\phi)\xrightarrow{\sim}G\text{ is a natural quasi-isomorphism}.}
$$
Under this isomorphism the cone triangle becomes the <exact triangle>
$$
E\xrightarrow{\phi}F\xrightarrow{\psi}G\xrightarrow{\delta}\Sigma E,
$$
where $\delta=v\circ r^{-1}$ in the derived category.

For the final construction write $U=S^2\setminus S^1$ and set $A=i_!\mathbb Q_U$, $B=\mathbb Q_{S^2}$, $C=j_*\mathbb Q_{S^1}$, each initially placed in degree zero. The stated sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ is exact on stalks: on $U$ its first map is an isomorphism and its last <sheaf> is zero; on the equator its first <sheaf> is zero and the second map is an isomorphism. Take its connecting morphism
$$
\boxed{\mu=\delta:C\longrightarrow A[1]\quad\text{in }D(S^2).}
$$
The source has only $\mathcal H^0(C)=C$, whereas the target has only $\mathcal H^{-1}(A[1])=A$. Therefore every induced map $\mathcal H^i(\mu)$ is zero: in any degree at least one of its source and target is zero. This is a <ghost morphism in a derived category>.

To prove the derived morphism itself is nonzero, apply derived global sections, giving the <hypercohomology> sequence. The <extension by zero> identifies $H^*(S^2;A)$ with the <compactly supported cohomology> of $U$, and closed direct image identifies $H^*(S^2;C)$ with $H^*(S^1;\mathbb Q)$. The complement $U$ is two open disks, each homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^2$, so
$$
H_c^q(U;\mathbb Q)=\begin{cases}\mathbb Q^2,&q=2,\\0,&q\ne2.\end{cases}
$$
The degree-one part of the resulting exact sequence is
$$
0=H^1(S^2;\mathbb Q)\longrightarrow H^1(S^1;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\xrightarrow{\mu_*}H_c^2(U;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q^2\longrightarrow H^2(S^2;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\longrightarrow0.
$$
Thus $\mu_*$ is injective, in particular nonzero. With the hemispheres oriented from $S^2$, the last map is $(a,b)\mapsto a+b$, so an appropriately oriented equator generator maps to $(1,-1)$. A zero morphism in the <derived category of sheaves> would induce a zero map after derived global sections and taking <cohomology>. Consequently **$\mu\ne0$, although $\mathcal H^i(\mu)=0$ for every $i$**. This establishes explicitly that the collection of cohomology-sheaf functors detects zero objects but is not faithful on morphisms.
