= Solution
{parent=1}

For <pressureless matter>, the <cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation> gives $\rho\propto a^{-3}$. The flat <Friedmann equation> then gives $a\propto t^{2/3}$, and <conformal time> $d\eta=dt/a$ gives $a\propto\eta^2$. Choose the initial conformal time to be zero. A galaxy seen at emission time $\eta_e$ has comoving radius $\chi=\eta_0-\eta_e$ and <cosmological redshift> $1+z=(\eta_0/\eta_e)^2$.

Two opposite comoving galaxies are separated by $2\chi$. Direct one-way communication between their worldlines before emission requires the <particle horizon> $\eta_e$ to cover this separation: $2(\eta_0-\eta_e)\le\eta_e$. Thus the threshold is $\eta_e=2\eta_0/3$, and
$$
\boxed{z_c=\frac54\quad\text{for direct communication}.}
$$
The distinction in <direct cosmological communication and a shared causal past> matters here. If causal contact means that their past light cones overlap, the condition instead is $2\chi\le2\eta_e$, giving $\eta_e=\eta_0/2$ and $\boxed{z_c=3\text{ for a shared causal past}}$. These are different operational meanings of the horizon criterion; the boundary corresponds to the limiting initial-time contact.

For <radiation in cosmology>, $\rho\propto a^{-4}$ by the <cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation>. The flat <Friedmann equation> gives $a\propto t^{1/2}$, so $a\propto\eta$ in <conformal time>. Hence $1+z=\eta_0/\eta_e$.

The opposite galaxies again have separation $2(\eta_0-\eta_e)$. Direct communication requires this to be at most $\eta_e$, so
$$
\boxed{z_c=\frac12\quad\text{for direct communication}.}
$$
For overlap of their past light cones, the criterion is instead $\eta_e\ge\eta_0/2$, giving $\boxed{z_c=1\text{ for a shared causal past}}$. The two notions are precisely those distinguished in <direct cosmological communication and a shared causal past>.

A fixed comoving volume has physical volume proportional to $a^3$. The stretched <domain walls> within it have area proportional to $a^2$, and their energy per physical area is constant. The <cosmological domain wall network> therefore has $\rho\propto a^{-1}$, equivalently a <barotropic equation of state> with $w=-2/3$.

The flat <Friedmann equation> becomes $\dot a^2=Ca$ for a positive constant $C$. On the expanding branch, integration gives $a=(C/4)(t-t_B)^2$. Measuring age from the initial time $t_B=0$, $H=\dot a/a=2/t$. Thus
$$
\boxed{H_0t_0=2.}
$$

Set $a(t)=a_0(t/t_0)^2$. The remaining future <conformal time> is
$$
\eta_\infty-\eta_0=\int_{t_0}^\infty\frac{dt}{a(t)}=\frac{t_0}{a_0}.
$$
A light ray sent to a comoving reflector at separation $\chi$ and immediately returned consumes $2\chi$ of conformal time. The <cosmological round-trip communication horizon> therefore has supremum physical distance
$$
\boxed{d_{\max}=a_0\chi_{\max}=\frac{t_0}{2}=\frac1{H_0}.}
$$
Finite-time reception requires $d_0<d_{\max}$. Indeed the total travel equation is $2d_0/a_0=(t_0/a_0)(1-t_0/t_r)$, giving $t_r=t_0/(1-2d_0/t_0)$. At the limiting distance the return time tends to infinity. This round-trip radius is half the current physical <cosmological event horizon> radius.

= Solution

Write $F=f'(R)<0$, so $\Omega^2=-F$, and let $(\nabla F)^2=g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu F\partial_\nu F$. Contracting the supplied transformed <Ricci tensor> with $\widetilde g^{\mu\nu}=\Omega^{-2}g^{\mu\nu}$ gives
$$
\boxed{\widetilde R=\Omega^{-2}\left[R+\frac{3}{2F^2}(\nabla F)^2-\frac3F\Box F\right].}
$$
The last term contains one trace contribution from $\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F$ and four from the term proportional to $g_{\mu\nu}$.

In the trace subtraction, $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}\Omega^{-2}=g_{\mu\nu}$. Consequently the transformed <Einstein tensor> is
$$
\boxed{\widetilde G_{\mu\nu}=G_{\mu\nu}
+\frac{3}{2F^2}\left[\partial_\mu F\partial_\nu F-\frac12g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla F)^2\right]
-\frac1F\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F+\frac1F g_{\mu\nu}\Box F.}
$$
These derivatives refer to the original metric. This is the tensor identity used in the <Einstein-frame representation of metric f(R) gravity>.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For the canonical scalar action and the stress-tensor convention in the paper, varying the inverse metric and volume element gives the <stress-energy tensor of a canonical scalar field>
$$
T_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi-\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}
\left[\frac12\widetilde g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\phi\partial_\beta\phi-V(\phi)\right].
$$
The vacuum <f(R) gravity> equation, divided by $F$, is
$$
G_{\mu\nu}-\frac1F\nabla_\mu\nabla_\nu F+\frac1F g_{\mu\nu}\Box F
=-\frac{RF-f}{2F}g_{\mu\nu}.
$$
Insert this in the transformed <Einstein tensor> from part (a). Since $g_{\mu\nu}=-\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}/F$, its algebraic term becomes $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(RF-f)/(2F^2)$. Also $\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\log(-F)$ gives $\partial_\mu\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\,\partial_\mu F/F$. The remaining derivative tensor is exactly the kinetic part of $T_{\mu\nu}$: the combination $g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla F)^2$ equals $\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}(\widetilde\nabla F)^2$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\widetilde G_{\mu\nu}=T_{\mu\nu},\qquad V(\phi)=\frac{Rf'(R)-f(R)}{2f'(R)^2}.}
$$
This establishes the <Einstein-frame representation of metric f(R) gravity> in the specified normalization. To express the potential as a function of $\phi$, choose a branch where $R\mapsto f'(R)$ is locally invertible; the conformal metric already requires $f'<0$.

For the linear function, $F=-1/2$ and $RF-f=\Lambda$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{V=2\Lambda,\qquad\phi=\sqrt{3/2}\log(1/2).}
$$
The factor two follows from the paper's <conformal transformation> $\widetilde g=g/2$. Its vacuum equation is consequently $\widetilde G_{\mu\nu}=2\Lambda\widetilde g_{\mu\nu}$. Here $f''=0$ and the scalar is fixed: the <Einstein-frame representation of metric f(R) gravity> degenerates to ordinary <general relativity> with a rescaled <cosmological constant>, and supplies no additional propagating scalar field.

The new <neutrino> species undergoes <thermal decoupling in cosmology> when its interaction rate becomes comparable to the <Hubble parameter>. Equating the given rates gives
$$
G_A^2T_D^5=\frac{1.66\sqrt{g_*}\,T_D^2}{m_{\mathrm{Pl}}},\qquad
T_D=\left(\frac{1.66\sqrt{g_*}}{G_A^2m_{\mathrm{Pl}}}\right)^{1/3}.
$$
With $g_*\simeq100$, $G_A=10^{-12}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$ and $m_{\mathrm{Pl}}\simeq10^{19}\,\mathrm{GeV}$,
$$
\boxed{T_D\simeq(1.66\times10^6)^{1/3}\,\mathrm{GeV}\simeq1.2\times10^2\,\mathrm{GeV}.}
$$
This is an order-of-magnitude electroweak-scale estimate under the given approximation. The mass in this formula is the unreduced <Planck mass>, not the <reduced Planck mass>.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Count one neutrino species as the usual two thermally populated particle-antiparticle helicity states, with negligible chemical potential. Its entropy weight is $g_{*s,X}=(7/8)2=7/4$. In the ideal high-temperature <Standard Model>, the bosonic count is $16+8+4=28$ from <gluons>, electroweak <gauge bosons> and the <Higgs field>. The fermionic count is $72+12+6=90$ from <quarks>, charged <leptons> and the three ordinary <neutrino> species. Hence the <relativistic entropy content of the Standard Model> is $g_{*s,D}=28+(7/8)90=106.75$ for the remaining bath. Before the new species leaves, the total count is $108.5$; its separately cooling states are excluded from the subsequent bath count.

Near ordinary <neutrino decoupling>, the still-coupled bath has
$$
g_{*s}=2+\frac78(4+6)=10.75
$$
from photons, electron-positron pairs and the three ordinary neutrinos. After those neutrinos decouple, the electromagnetic bath falls from $2+(7/8)4=11/2$ to two when the pairs annihilate. <Cosmological entropy conservation> in that bath gives $T_\nu/T_\gamma=(4/11)^{1/3}$.

The new species has cooled as $T_X\propto a^{-1}$ since its earlier decoupling, whereas annihilation of Standard Model species heated the remaining bath. The <temperature ratio of an early-decoupled neutrino> is thus
$$
\frac{T_X}{T_\nu}=\left(\frac{10.75}{106.75}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
\boxed{\frac{T_X}{T_\gamma}=\left(\frac{43}{11\times106.75}\right)^{1/3}\simeq0.332.}
$$
These are ideal-gas estimates with the high-temperature count implicit in the question; precise electroweak thresholds change them slightly.

Entropy degrees of freedom weight temperature ratios with their cube, while energy degrees of freedom use their fourth power. The remaining Standard Model entropy today, in photon-temperature units, is $g_{*s,0}^{\mathrm{SM}}=2+(7/8)6(4/11)=43/11$. The new contribution is $(7/4)(T_X/T_\gamma)^3$. Therefore the <fractional entropy of a relativistically decoupled species> is
$$
\boxed{\frac{s_X}{s_{\mathrm{total}}}
=\frac{(7/4)(T_X/T_\gamma)^3}{43/11+(7/4)(T_X/T_\gamma)^3}
=\frac{7/4}{106.75+7/4}\simeq0.0161.}
$$
About **1.6% of the total entropy** is in the new species. Separate comoving entropy conservation also derives this fraction directly at decoupling. A model populating four helicity states instead would require the corresponding doubled species count.

Assume the new <neutrino> is stable and retains the abundance established by relativistic <thermal decoupling in cosmology>. While it remains relativistic, its contribution to the <effective number of neutrino species> is
$$
\Delta N_{\mathrm{eff}}=(T_X/T_\nu)^4
=\left(\frac{10.75}{106.75}\right)^{4/3}\simeq0.047.
$$
Thus its effect on the radiation density and expansion during <Big Bang nucleosynthesis> is reduced relative to a fully heated extra neutrino species.

After it becomes nonrelativistic, its energy density is approximately $m_Xn_X$. A two-state relativistic fermion relic has $n_X/n_\gamma=(3/4)(T_X/T_\gamma)^3\simeq0.0275$. The <density bound on a relativistically decoupled massive relic> then gives
$$
\frac{\Omega_X}{\Omega_\gamma}
=\frac{m_X}{\langle E_\gamma\rangle}\frac{n_X}{n_\gamma},\qquad
\boxed{m_X\le\frac{\Omega_{X,\max}}{\Omega_\gamma}
\frac{\pi^4T_{\gamma,0}}{30\zeta(3)}\frac1{0.0275}.}
$$
Here $\Omega_{X,\max}$ is the allowed present matter-density fraction and $\langle E_\gamma\rangle=\pi^4T_{\gamma,0}/(30\zeta(3))$. Choosing the total available matter fraction yields the basic overclosure bound. Its mass cannot be raised freely at a fixed relic number density.

There is also a perturbation constraint. <Neutrino free streaming> suppresses clustering below the distance traveled before the species becomes nonrelativistic. The ratio $T_X/m_X$ controls this distance, and the relic matter fraction controls the strength of the suppression. A significant hot or warm relic component must therefore satisfy the observed structure-formation constraints in addition to the abundance bound. Its allowed dark-matter contribution is limited jointly by these two effects. Numerical observational bounds depend on the chosen matter budget and perturbation data; the temperature and abundance dilution derived here are the factors needed to apply them.

A <scalar cosmological perturbation> has two scalar gauge freedoms. In <Newtonian gauge in cosmology>, these remove the scalar shift and spatial shear, leaving the lapse potential $\Phi$ and spatial-curvature potential $\Psi$:
$$
ds^2=a^2(\eta)[(1+2\Phi)d\eta^2-(1-2\Psi)\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j].
$$
A <perfect fluid> has no scalar <anisotropic stress>. The traceless spatial <Einstein field equations> therefore set $\Phi=\Psi=\phi$ for nonzero spatial Fourier modes, producing the line element in the question. Homogeneous perturbations include background and gauge changes and require separate treatment. The single potential describes the physical scalar perturbation in this gauge.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For constant <barotropic equation of state> $P=w\rho$, the <cosmological perfect-fluid continuity equation> gives $\bar\rho\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$. In conformal time the flat <Friedmann equation> becomes $\mathcal H^2=(8\pi G/3)a^2\bar\rho$, where $\mathcal H=a'/a$. Integration, for $w\ne-1/3$, gives
$$
\boxed{a\propto|\eta-\eta_B|^{2/(1+3w)},\qquad a^2\bar\rho\propto(\eta-\eta_B)^{-2}.}
$$
Choose the conformal-time origin so that $\eta_B=0$ and work on the appropriate expansion branch. Write $\beta=2/(1+3w)$, so $\mathcal H=\beta/\eta$ and $2\mathcal H'+(1+3w)\mathcal H^2=0$.

Combining the supplied energy and momentum constraints gives the <comoving-gauge density contrast> Poisson relation
$$
\nabla^2\phi=4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho[\delta-3\mathcal H(1+w)v]
=4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho\Delta.
$$
For this barotropic fluid $\delta P=w\bar\rho\delta$. Substitute the energy constraint into the pressure equation to obtain
$$
\phi''+3(1+w)\mathcal H\phi'
+[2\mathcal H'+(1+3w)\mathcal H^2]\phi-w\nabla^2\phi=0,
$$
so the bracket vanishes and the coefficient of $\phi'/\eta$ is $A=6(1+w)/(1+3w)$.

Because $a^2\bar\rho=C\eta^{-2}$, the Poisson relation gives $\Delta=(\eta^2/(4\pi GC))\nabla^2\phi$. Apply the same potential equation to $F=\eta^2\nabla^2\phi$: differentiating $\eta^{-2}F$ gives
$$
F''+\frac{A-4}{\eta}F'+\frac{6-2A}{\eta^2}F-w\nabla^2F=0.
$$
Since $F$ is a constant multiple of $\Delta$, the <constant-equation-of-state comoving density equation> is
$$
\boxed{\Delta''+\frac{2(1-3w)}{(1+3w)\eta}\Delta'
-\frac{6(1-w)}{(1+3w)\eta^2}\Delta-w\nabla^2\Delta=0.}
$$
The primes here denote conformal-time derivatives, corresponding to the overdots in the question. A pure vacuum fluid $w=-1$ has zero enthalpy and no independent fluid velocity or nonzero-wave-number density perturbation; its formal equation should be interpreted with that degeneracy in mind.

For <radiation in cosmology>, $w=1/3$, and the Fourier mode of the <comoving-gauge density contrast> satisfies
$$
\Delta_k''+\left(\frac{k^2}{3}-\frac2{\eta^2}\right)\Delta_k=0.
$$
Set $x=k\eta/\sqrt3$, the ratio to the radiation <sound horizon> scale. The <radiation-era comoving density mode> has the exact basis
$$
\boxed{\Delta_k=A\left(\frac{\sin x}{x}-\cos x\right)
+B\left(\frac{\cos x}{x}+\sin x\right).}
$$
Direct differentiation verifies the equation. For $x\ll1$, the first function is $x^2/3+O(x^4)$ and the second is $x^{-1}+O(x)$, so the outside-horizon solutions grow as $\eta^2$ or decay as $\eta^{-1}$. Regularity at early time removes the second one: $B=0$. For $x\gg1$, the regular solution is $-A\cos x+O(x^{-1})$, a constant-amplitude acoustic oscillation. A regular mode thus grows while outside the sound horizon and oscillates after entering it.

The amplitude is arbitrary; the plotted mode sets $A=1$. The <radiation-era comoving density mode> approaches the early-time quadratic growth and the late-time cosine shown alongside it.

For <pressureless matter>, $w=0$, the <constant-equation-of-state comoving density equation> reduces to $\Delta''+2\Delta'/\eta-6\Delta/\eta^2=0$. Substitution of $\Delta\propto\eta^s$ gives $s^2+s-6=(s-2)(s+3)=0$. Since $a\propto\eta^2$,
$$
\boxed{\Delta=C_+a+C_-a^{-3/2}.}
$$
In the growing mode, $a^2\bar\rho\Delta$ is constant, so the Poisson constraint makes $\phi$ constant for a nonzero Fourier mode. The momentum constraint and the flat <Friedmann equation> give
$$
\mathcal H\phi=-4\pi Ga^2\bar\rho v=-\frac32\mathcal H^2v,
\qquad\boxed{v=-\frac{2\phi}{3\mathcal H}=-\frac{\phi\eta}{3}\propto a^{1/2}.}
$$
This is the <growing-mode velocity potential in matter domination>, in the question's convention where the peculiar velocity is $\nabla v$.

Differentiate the scalar-field <Friedmann equation> on the expanding branch $H>0$:
$$
2H\dot H=\frac{\dot\Phi}{3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2}(V'+\ddot\Phi).
$$
The scalar equation of motion gives $V'+\ddot\Phi=-3H\dot\Phi$. Canceling $H$ yields
$$
\boxed{2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\dot H=-\dot\Phi^2.}
$$
Thus the <Hubble parameter> cannot increase for a canonical homogeneous scalar field. The mass scale here is the <reduced Planck mass>.

The <inflaton> has quantum fluctuations while their wavelengths are shorter than the <Hubble radius>. Choosing the <Bunch-Davies vacuum> fixes the short-wavelength positive-frequency modes. During near-de Sitter <slow-roll inflation>, expansion carries each mode through $k=aH$, leaving a field fluctuation of amplitude approximately $H/(2\pi)$ on spatially flat slices.

The <inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations> converts this field displacement into a local time shift of magnitude $|\delta\Phi/\dot\Phi|$, and hence into a <comoving curvature perturbation> of magnitude $H|\delta\Phi/\dot\Phi|$. For the single-field adiabatic growing mode on an attractor, the <superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature> makes this perturbation approximately constant at $k\ll aH$. These vacuum-generated perturbations seed the later density fluctuations.

The <slow-roll equations> give $H^2\simeq V/(3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2)$ and $\dot\Phi\simeq-V'/(3H)$. Substituting in the stated <slow-roll curvature power spectrum> yields
$$
\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}=\frac{H^4}{4\pi^2\dot\Phi^2}
\simeq\frac{9H^6}{4\pi^2V'^2}
=\frac{V^3}{12\pi^2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^6V'^2}.
$$
With the <potential slow-roll parameter> $\epsilon_V=M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2V'^2/(2V^2)$,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal P_{\mathcal R}\simeq\frac{V}{24\pi^2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^4\epsilon_V}
=\frac8{3\epsilon_V}\left(\frac{V^{1/4}}{\sqrt{8\pi}\,M_{\mathrm{Pl}}}\right)^4.}
$$
All quantities are evaluated at horizon exit. The two forms have the same normalization because $(\sqrt{8\pi})^4=64\pi^2$.

At horizon exit $k=aH$, $d\log k/dt=H(1-\epsilon_H)$, where $\epsilon_H=-\dot H/H^2$ is the <Hubble slow-roll parameter>. To leading slow-roll order,
$$
\frac{d}{d\log k}\simeq\frac{\dot\Phi}{H}\frac{d}{d\Phi}
\simeq-M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\frac{V'}V\frac{d}{d\Phi}.
$$
The <slow-roll curvature power spectrum> is proportional to $V^3/V'^2$. Therefore the <scalar spectral index in potential slow-roll parameters> is
$$
n_s-1\simeq-M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\frac{V'}V\left(3\frac{V'}V-2\frac{V''}{V'}\right)
=-3M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\left(\frac{V'}V\right)^2+2M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2\frac{V''}V.
$$
Using the <potential slow-roll parameter> and the <second potential slow-roll parameter> gives
$$
\boxed{n_s\simeq1-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V.}
$$
The neglected horizon-exit derivative correction contributes at higher slow-roll order.

= Solution

Apply the horizon-exit derivative from part (d) to the <potential slow-roll parameter> and the <second potential slow-roll parameter>. The <potential slow-roll flow equations> are
$$
\frac{d\epsilon_V}{d\log k}\simeq4\epsilon_V^2-2\epsilon_V\eta_V,
\qquad\frac{d\eta_V}{d\log k}\simeq2\epsilon_V\eta_V-\xi_V^2,
$$
where the <third potential slow-roll parameter> is $\xi_V^2=M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^4V'V'''/V^2$. For example, differentiating $\eta_V$ gives $-M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^4(V'/V)(V'''/V-V''V'/V^2)$, which is the second expression. Differentiating $n_s-1\simeq-6\epsilon_V+2\eta_V$ now gives the <running of the scalar spectral index>
$$
\boxed{\frac{dn_s}{d\log k}\simeq-6(4\epsilon_V^2-2\epsilon_V\eta_V)
+2(2\epsilon_V\eta_V-\xi_V^2)
=16\epsilon_V\eta_V-24\epsilon_V^2-2\xi_V^2.}
$$
This is the leading second-order slow-roll running. The notation $\xi_V^2$ denotes a signed derivative combination and does not force its value to be nonnegative.
