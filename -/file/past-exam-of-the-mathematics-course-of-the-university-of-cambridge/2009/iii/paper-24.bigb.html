Use the elementary, or weak, <topos> axioms: <finite limits> and a <power object> $PA$ for every object $A$, naturally representing $\operatorname{Sub}(A\times X)$. No arbitrary products or coproducts are assumed. In particular $\Omega=P1$ is the <subobject classifier>. Inverse image of the membership relation defines $Pf:PB\to PA$ for $f:A\to B$.

Interchanging the factors in a relation gives natural bijections
$$
\mathcal E(A,PB)\cong\operatorname{Sub}(A\times B)\cong\mathcal E(B,PA).
$$
Thus the <contravariant power-object functor> $P:\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal E$ has left adjoint $P^{\mathrm{op}}$. Its <adjunction unit> $\eta_A:A\to PPA$ sends a generalized element $a$ to the predicate on $PA$ given by membership of $a$.

This unit is monic. If $x,y:T\to A$ have the same image under $\eta_A$, membership of $x$ and $y$ agrees in every $T$-parameterized <subobject> of $A$. Apply this to the graph of $x$ in $A\times T$: membership of $x$ is true, so membership of $y$ is true, which means $y=x$. This argument uses parameterized relations, not an assumption that global elements detect morphisms.

The <functor> $P$ reflects isomorphisms. Suppose $Pf$ is invertible. Then $PPf$ is invertible, and naturality $PPf\,\eta_A=\eta_Bf$ together with monicity of $\eta_A$ shows that $f$ is monic. Regard $f$ as a <subobject> of $B$. Its inverse image along itself is the whole of $A$, just as for the whole <subobject> of $B$. Those two <subobjects> correspond to maps $1\to PB$; since $Pf$ is monic, they coincide. Therefore the <subobject> $f$ is all of $B$, and $f$ is an isomorphism.

We now prove the preservation condition for the <crude monadicity theorem>. A <reflexive pair> in $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}$ is a <coreflexive pair> $f,g:B\rightrightarrows A$ in $\mathcal E$, with $rf=rg=1_B$ for some $r:A\to B$. Let $e:E\hookrightarrow B$ be its <equalizer>. For any <monomorphism> $m$, define $\exists_m$ on <power objects> by composing a parameterized <subobject> with $m$; no general image construction is needed because $m$ is monic. Inverse image followed by this extension obeys $Pm\,\exists_m=1$.

Here both $f,g$ are monic. For a parameterized <subobject> $S$ of $B$, an element $b$ belongs to $g^{-1}(f(S))$ exactly when $gb=fb'$ for some $b'\in S$. Applying $r$ gives $b=b'$, so this condition is precisely $b\in S$ and $fb=gb$. Consequently, as morphisms of <power objects>,
$$
Pf\,\exists_f=1_{PB},\qquad Pg\,\exists_f=\exists_e Pe,\qquad Pe\,\exists_e=1_{PE}.
$$
If $h:PB\to Z$ equalizes $Pf,Pg$, these identities give
$$
h=hPf\,\exists_f=hPg\,\exists_f=h\exists_e Pe.
$$
It therefore factors uniquely through $Pe$, which is a split <epimorphism> with section $\exists_e$. Since $fe=ge$, $Pe$ also equalizes $Pf,Pg$. Hence $Pe$ is their <coequalizer>. This proves explicitly that <power objects turn coreflexive equalizers into coequalizers>.

Finite <equalizers> in $\mathcal E$ give all <coequalizers> of reflexive pairs in $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}$, and we have shown that $P$ preserves them and reflects isomorphisms. The <crude monadicity theorem> now yields
$$
\boxed{P\text{ is monadic},\qquad\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathcal E^{PP}.}
$$

Fix a set $I$ of cardinality $\kappa$. If $\mathcal E$ has $I$-indexed <products in a category>, its <Eilenberg-Moore category> for the double-power <monad> has them, since the forgetful <functor> creates existing limits. The displayed equivalence then gives $I$-products in $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}$, hence $I$-<coproducts in a category> in $\mathcal E$.

For the converse, suppose those coproducts exist. The diagonal $\Delta:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E^I$ is a <logical functor>, because all the elementary topos structure in $\mathcal E^I$ is pointwise, and it has left adjoint $\coprod_I$. Apply <power-object monadicity> to both toposes. Under their monadic identifications, $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$ is the lift of $\Delta$ to the double-power <Eilenberg-Moore categories>. The base <functor> $\Delta$ is a right adjoint to $\coprod_I$, so the <adjoint lifting theorem for monad algebra functors> gives a left adjoint to its algebra lift. Its required reflexive <coequalizers> exist in $\mathcal E^{\mathrm{op}}$, since they are opposites of finite <equalizers> in $\mathcal E$. Taking opposites gives a right adjoint to $\Delta$, which is exactly the $I$-product <functor>. Thus the <products and coproducts of fixed cardinality in a topos> satisfy
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E\text{ has }\kappa\text{-products}\iff\mathcal E\text{ has }\kappa\text{-coproducts}.}
$$

Let $(G,\varepsilon,\delta)$ be a <Cartesian comonad>, so its endofunctor preserves <finite limits>. A <coalgebra for a comonad> is $(A,a)$ with $a:A\to GA$, $\varepsilon_Aa=1_A$ and $\delta_Aa=Ga\,a$. Its morphisms satisfy $bf=Gf\,a$. Write $U:\mathcal E^G\to\mathcal E$ for the <forgetful functor> and $R(X)=(GX,\delta_X)$ for the <cofree coalgebra>. Transposition is
$$
\mathcal E^G((A,a),R(X))\cong\mathcal E(A,X),\qquad f\longmapsto\varepsilon_X f,\quad h\longmapsto Gh\,a.
$$
The <forgetful functor> creates <finite limits>, since $G$ preserves them. We construct both <exponential objects> and the <subobject classifier>.

For coalgebras $(A,a),(B,b)$, set $D=B^A$ and $W=(GB)^A$ in the ambient <topos>. Define $p,q:GD\to W$ by transposing
$$
p^\flat=b\,\operatorname{ev}(\varepsilon_D\times1_A),\qquad q^\flat=G\operatorname{ev}(1_{GD}\times a),
$$
where $GD\times GA\cong G(D\times A)$. Their cofree transposes are coalgebra morphisms
$$
\widehat p=Gp\,\delta_D,\quad\widehat q=Gq\,\delta_D:R(D)\rightrightarrows R(W).
$$
Let $H$ be their coalgebra <equalizer>. For a coalgebra $(C,c)$, an arrow $C\to R(D)$ corresponds to a map $h:C\times A\to B$. Its evaluation respects coalgebra structure exactly when
$$
bh=Gh\,(c\times a).
$$
Under the displayed transpositions this is precisely the condition that it equalize $\widehat p,\widehat q$. Thus $\mathcal E^G(C,H)\cong\mathcal E^G(C\times A,B)$ naturally, and $H$ is the required <exponential in a coalgebra topos>.

Let $\kappa:G\Omega\to\Omega$ classify $G\top:G1\cong1\hookrightarrow G\Omega$. In $R(\Omega)$ take the <equalizer>
$$
\Omega_G\hookrightarrow R(\Omega)\mathrel{\substack{\xrightarrow{1}\\[-2pt]\xrightarrow[G\kappa\,\delta_\Omega]{}}}R(\Omega).
$$
The truth map into $R(\Omega)$ is $G\top$ and factors through this <equalizer>. To verify its universal property, take an underlying <subobject> $m:S\hookrightarrow A$ of a coalgebra. It carries a compatible coalgebra structure exactly when $a$ maps $S$ into $GS$. Such a structure, if it exists, is unique because $Gm$ is monic; its laws follow by cancellation of $m,Gm,G^2m$. Moreover $a^{-1}(GS)\subseteq S$ always: applying $\varepsilon_A$ to a point of $GS$ lands in $S$. Thus stability is equivalent to $S=a^{-1}(GS)$.

If $\chi:A\to\Omega$ classifies $S$, this last equality is exactly
$$
\chi=\kappa G\chi\,a.
$$
The cofree transpose $\widehat\chi=G\chi\,a:A\to R(\Omega)$ satisfies this equation precisely when it factors through $\Omega_G$. Indeed transposing the equality $\widehat\chi=G\kappa\,\delta_\Omega\widehat\chi$ gives the equation above. Pulling back its truth map recovers $S$, because $\kappa$ classifies $G\top$. Hence $\Omega_G$ is the <subobject classifier of a coalgebra topos>. We have obtained <finite limits>, exponentials and a classifier, proving **$\mathcal E^G$ is a topos**.

Now let $f:\mathcal H\to\mathcal E$ be a <geometric morphism>, and put $L=f^*$, $V=f_*$, with unit $\eta$. Both <functors> preserve <finite limits>, so $G=LV$ is a <Cartesian comonad> on $\mathcal H$. Put $\mathcal D=\mathcal H^G$. Its comparison <functor>
$$
K:\mathcal E\longrightarrow\mathcal D,\qquad K(X)=(LX,L\eta_X)
$$
is left exact and has right adjoint
$$
J(A,a)=\operatorname{Eq}\bigl(Va,\eta_{VA}:VA\rightrightarrows V L V A\bigr).
$$
To check the adjunction, transpose an underlying map $LX\to A$ to $X\to VA$. The coalgebra-morphism equation becomes exactly the equality of the two composites into $V L V A$, so the transposed arrow factors uniquely through the displayed <equalizer>.

The counit $KJ(A,a)\to(A,a)$ is invertible. Applying $L$ to the defining <equalizer> gives the <equalizer> of $Ga,\delta_A:GA\rightrightarrows G^2A$. The structure map $a:A\to GA$ is exactly that <equalizer>: it equalizes by coassociativity; if $t:T\to GA$ equalizes, apply $\varepsilon_{GA}$ to obtain $t=a\varepsilon_A t$, giving its unique factorization through $a$. Thus the underlying comparison counit is an isomorphism, and the <forgetful functor> reflects isomorphisms. Therefore $J$ is <full and faithful>.

The adjunctions $U\dashv R$ and $K\dashv J$ define
$$
\boxed{\mathcal H\xrightarrow{p}\mathcal D\xrightarrow{i}\mathcal E,\qquad f=i\circ p.}
$$
Here $p^*=U$ is faithful, so $p$ is a <surjective geometric morphism>; $i_*=J$ is full and faithful, so $i$ is a <geometric embedding>. Their inverse-image composite is $UK=L$, establishing the <surjection-embedding factorization of a geometric morphism>.

For uniqueness, let $S$ be the class of arrows in $\mathcal E$ inverted by $f^*$. In any surjection-embedding factorization $f=i'p'$, conservativity of $p'^*$ shows that $S$ is also the class inverted by $i'^*$. The essential image of the fully faithful $i'_*$ consists exactly of the $S$-local objects $Y$, namely those for which $\mathcal E(B,Y)\to\mathcal E(A,Y)$ is bijective for every $A\to B$ in $S$. One direction follows from the adjunction. For the converse, the reflector unit $Y\to i'_*i'^*Y$ belongs to $S$; locality gives a retraction, and locality of the reflected object makes that retraction also a right inverse. Thus the unit is invertible. The image subcategory is consequently determined by $f$, independently of the factorization. The two intermediate toposes are equivalent over $\mathcal E$, and their inverse-image composites with the embeddings determine the surjection legs. This proves **uniqueness up to equivalence**.

A <local operator> is an inflationary, idempotent, finite-meet-preserving map $j:\Omega\to\Omega$ with $j(\top)=\top$, expressed in the <internal logic of a topos>. For a <subterminal object> $U$ with truth value $u$, the <closed local operator> and <quasi-closed local operator> are respectively
$$
c(u)(p)=u\vee p,\qquad q(u)(p)=((p\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u).
$$
The first is a <local operator> by distributivity. For the second put $n(p)=p\Rightarrow u$. Then $p\le n^2p$, $n$ is order-reversing, and $n^3p=np$: the first inequality gives $n^3p\le np$, while its application to $np$ gives the reverse inequality. Thus $q=n^2$ is inflationary and idempotent, and $q(\top)=\top$.

For meet preservation, monotonicity gives $q(p\wedge r)\le qp\wedge qr$. Conversely put $t=(p\wedge r)\Rightarrow u$. From $t\wedge p\le r\Rightarrow u$ and $qr\wedge(r\Rightarrow u)\le u$, we obtain $t\wedge qr\le p\Rightarrow u$. Intersecting with $qp$ gives $qp\wedge qr\wedge t\le u$, hence $qp\wedge qr\le q(p\wedge r)$. This proves all local-operator axioms. Also $q(u)(0)=u$ and $q(u)(u)=u$. Equivalently, it is the largest <local operator> leaving $u$ fixed: if $k(u)=u$, then
$$
kp\wedge k(p\Rightarrow u)\le u\quad\Longrightarrow\quad kp\le k(p\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u\le(p\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u.
$$
This explains the quasi-closed terminology as relative double negation above the closed bottom $u$.

The <subobject classifier> of $\mathbf{sh}_j(\mathcal E)$ is the <closed-subobject classifier> $\Omega_j=\{p:jp=p\}$. Its bottom is $j0$, its meet is inherited, its join is $j(p\vee r)$, and implication between closed truth values is the ambient implication. To verify the last claim, if $jv=v$, then
$$
j(p\Rightarrow v)\wedge jp=j((p\Rightarrow v)\wedge p)\le v.
$$
It follows that $j(p\Rightarrow v)\le jp\Rightarrow v\le p\Rightarrow v$; inflationarity gives equality and also $jp\Rightarrow v=p\Rightarrow v$.

Suppose $j=q(u)$. Every closed $p$ lies above $u$, and its complement in $\Omega_j$ is $np=p\Rightarrow u$, also closed because $n^3=n$. We have $p\wedge np=u$. Moreover $n(p\vee np)=np\wedge n^2p=np\wedge p=u$, so $q(p\vee np)=\top$. Thus the fixed truth values form a <Boolean algebra>, and the sheaf topos is a <Boolean topos>.

Conversely suppose the sheaf topos is Boolean and put $u=j0$. Complementation in its classifier is $p\mapsto p\Rightarrow u$. Applying the double-complement identity to $jp$, and using the implication calculation above, gives
$$
jp=((jp\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u)=((p\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u).
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathbf{sh}_j(\mathcal E)\text{ is Boolean}\iff j=q(j0).}
$$

For the cover, work in the <slice category> $\mathcal E/\Omega$. Its generic <subterminal object> is $T=(\top:1\hookrightarrow\Omega)$. Form its quasi-closed Boolean subtopos $\mathcal B=\mathbf{sh}_{q(T)}(\mathcal E/\Omega)$, and compose its embedding with the slice projection to obtain $b:\mathcal B\to\mathcal E$. The slice projection has inverse image $X\mapsto X\times\Omega$, and sheafification is left exact, so this is a <geometric morphism>.

To prove surjectivity, take a <monomorphism> $S\hookrightarrow X$ with characteristic predicate $p(x)$, and suppose $b^*$ makes it invertible. Pullback to the slice followed by sheafification makes this exactly the assertion that it is $q(T)$-dense. Internally on $X\times\Omega$, therefore,
$$
((p(x)\Rightarrow u)\Rightarrow u)=\top
$$
for the generic truth value $u$. Substitute $u=p(x)$ by pulling back along the graph $X\to X\times\Omega$. The left side becomes $(\top\Rightarrow p(x))=p(x)$, so $p(x)=\top$ and the original mono is invertible. Thus $b^*$ reflects invertible monos. If it identifies two parallel arrows, their <equalizer> becomes invertible; reflection then shows the arrows were already equal. Hence $b^*$ is faithful, and
$$
\boxed{\mathbf{sh}_{q(T)}(\mathcal E/\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathcal E\text{ is a surjection from a Boolean topos}.}
$$
The generic truth value is essential: substituting only a fixed global value such as $0$ would give a Boolean subtopos, but need not give a surjection onto the original topos.

A signature $\Sigma$ specifies sorts, finite-arity function symbols and finite-arity relation symbols. A <coherent formula> is built from atomic relations and equality using $\top,\bot$, finite conjunction, finite disjunction and existential quantification. A <coherent theory> consists of <coherent sequents> $\phi\vdash_{\vec x}\psi$ in finite typed contexts. The context expresses universal quantification of the free variables; unrestricted universal quantification or negation is not a formula constructor here.

Construct the <coherent syntactic category> $\mathcal C_{\mathbb T}$ as follows. Objects are formulas in context $\{\vec x.\phi\}$, modulo renaming and provable equivalence. An arrow to $\{\vec y.\psi\}$ is a formula $\rho(\vec x,\vec y)$, modulo provable equivalence, for which the theory proves
$$
\rho\vdash\phi\wedge\psi,\qquad\phi\vdash_{\vec x}\exists\vec y\,\rho,\qquad\rho(\vec x,\vec y)\wedge\rho(\vec x,\vec y')\vdash\vec y=\vec y'.
$$
These say that the relation is supported on the specified objects, total and single-valued. The identity is equality on the context, and composition is
$$
(\sigma\circ\rho)(\vec x,\vec z)=\exists\vec y\,(\rho(\vec x,\vec y)\wedge\sigma(\vec y,\vec z)).
$$
The logical rules give associativity and the identity laws. The empty-context true formula is terminal; conjunction in disjoint contexts gives products; conjunction with equality of the two functional relations gives <equalizers>. Existential quantification gives images, and finite disjunction gives unions of <subobjects>. Substitution preserves these constructions, so images and unions are stable under pullback. This makes $\mathcal C_{\mathbb T}$ a <coherent category>.

Let $M$ be a $\mathbb T$-model in a <topos> $\mathcal E$. Interpret each context as a finite product of its sort objects and each formula as its corresponding <subobject>. Define
$$
F_M(\{\vec x.\phi\})=\llbracket\vec x.\phi\rrbracket_M.
$$
The interpretation of an arrow relation $\rho$ is a total single-valued relation between these objects. Its projection to the domain is both an <epimorphism>, by totality, and a <monomorphism>, by single-valuedness, hence invertible in a topos. Its other projection therefore determines a unique morphism. Composition agrees with relational composition, so $F_M$ is a <functor>. Finite limits interpret equality and conjunction; images interpret existential quantification; finite unions interpret disjunction, including the empty union for $\bot$. Consequently $F_M$ is a <coherent functor>.

Conversely there is a canonical syntactic model $U$ in $\mathcal C_{\mathbb T}$. Interpret a sort $s$ by $\{x:s.\top\}$, a function symbol by its term graph, and a relation symbol by its atomic formula-in-context <subobject>. Induction on formulas identifies the interpretation of every $\phi$ in $U$ with its formula-in-context object. Every axiom is then an inclusion of the associated <subobjects>, because it is provable in $\mathbb T$.

For a <coherent functor> $F:\mathcal C_{\mathbb T}\to\mathcal E$, set $M_F=F(U)$. Preservation of <finite limits>, images and unions proves inductively that $F$ preserves all coherent interpretations, so every axiom holds in $M_F$. The same induction supplies natural isomorphisms
$$
F(\{\vec x.\phi\})\cong\llbracket\vec x.\phi\rrbracket_{M_F},
$$
compatible with the functional relations defining arrows. Thus $F\cong F_{M_F}$; directly on the sorts, operations and relations, $M_{F_M}\cong M$.

Finally these constructions agree on morphisms. A model homomorphism $h:M\to N$ induces maps on finite contexts. Atomic relations are preserved by definition; finite conjunction and disjunction and existential witnesses then show that each formula object maps to the corresponding formula object. Compatibility with functional relations makes these maps a <natural transformation> $F_M\to F_N$. Conversely a <natural transformation> between <coherent functors> restricts to its components on sort objects; naturality for term graphs preserves operations, and naturality for relation inclusions preserves relations. It is therefore a model homomorphism. The two correspondences are inverse. We have proved the categorical, not merely objectwise, equivalence
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathbb T}(\mathcal E)\simeq\operatorname{Coh}(\mathcal C_{\mathbb T},\mathcal E).}
$$

A <coverage on a category> specifies covering families stable under refinement along arrows: if $(f_i:C_i\to C)$ covers and $h:B\to C$ is any arrow, there is a covering family $(g_j:B_j\to B)$ such that every $hg_j$ factors through some $f_i$. Equivalently one works with the associated saturated covering <sieves on a category>, satisfying maximality, pullback stability and local transitivity. These determine the same <sheaves on a site>. We use this <sieve on a category> formulation of $J$.

An object is a <J-irreducible object of a site> if its only covering <sieve on a category> is the maximal <sieve on a category>. The <coverage on a category> is a <rigid coverage> if every object is covered by the <sieve on a category> generated by arrows whose domains are J-irreducible.

Assume $\mathcal C$ has finitely many arrows and every <idempotent morphism> splits. There are finitely many <sieves on a category> on each object $C$, so the intersection of all covering <sieves on a category> is a covering <sieve on a category> $R_C$: finite intersections of covers are covers by the topology axioms. It is the least cover. Let $I$ be the collection of all arrows $f:B\to C$ lying in $R_C$. This is an <idempotent ideal in a finite category>.

Here are the details. It is closed under precomposition because $R_C$ is a <sieve on a category>. For $h:C\to D$, the <sieve on a category> $h^*R_D$ covers $C$; minimality gives $R_C\subseteq h^*R_D$, so $hf\in R_D$. Thus it is also closed under postcomposition. Local transitivity says that the <sieve on a category>
$$
S_C=\bigcup_{f\in R_C}fR_{\operatorname{dom}f}
$$
covers $C$. It is contained in $R_C$, while minimality gives the opposite inclusion. Hence $S_C=R_C$, or $I^2=I$.

We prove that such an ideal is generated by the identities it contains. Take $f\in I$. Since $I^2=I$, repeatedly factor its remaining right factor to obtain
$$
f=a_1b_1=a_1a_2b_2=\cdots=a_1\cdots a_nb_n,
$$
with every factor in $I$. Write $p_n=a_1\cdots a_n$. Finiteness gives $p_i=p_j$ for some $i<j$. Their domains coincide, and $t=a_{i+1}\cdots a_j$ is an endomorphism in $I$ satisfying $p_it=p_i$. Some positive power $e=t^m$ is idempotent: the powers are eventually periodic, so choose $m$ beyond the eventual threshold and divisible by its period. Then $p_ie=p_i$. Split $e=ir$ with $i:B\to\operatorname{dom}(p_i)$, $r:\operatorname{dom}(p_i)\to B$ and $ri=1_B$. Since $I$ is a two-sided ideal,
$$
1_B=rei\in I,\qquad f=p_i e b_i=(p_i i)(r b_i).
$$
Thus $f$ factors through an object $B$ whose identity lies in $I$. The latter means $1_B\in R_B$, hence $R_B=yB$; every covering <sieve on a category> on $B$ must therefore be maximal. So $B$ is J-irreducible.

Every arrow of $R_C$ consequently factors through a J-irreducible object. The <sieve on a category> generated by arrows from all such objects contains $R_C$ and is a cover. This proves **every coverage on the finite idempotent-complete category is rigid**. If $R_C$ is empty, the same conclusion holds because the empty <sieve on a category> already covers $C$.

Let $\mathcal D$ be the full subcategory of J-irreducible objects. For a covering <sieve on a category> $S$ on $C$ and any $f:D\to C$ with $D\in\mathcal D$, the pulled-back <sieve on a category> $f^*S$ covers $D$ and is maximal, so $f\in S$. Therefore $S|_{\mathcal D}=yC|_{\mathcal D}$. For a <categorical presheaf> $F$ on $\mathcal D$, define its extension by
$$
H_F(C)=\operatorname{Nat}(yC|_{\mathcal D},F).
$$
This is the right Kan extension along the inclusion. Its adjunction gives
$$
\operatorname{Nat}(S,H_F)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(S|_{\mathcal D},F)=H_F(C),
$$
which is precisely unique amalgamation on $S$. Hence $H_F$ is a sheaf. The <Yoneda lemma> gives $H_F(D)\cong F(D)$ for $D\in\mathcal D$.

Conversely, if $G$ is a sheaf, its sections on all arrows from $\mathcal D$ into $C$ determine a section on their covering <sieve on a category>. A compatible family extends to an arrow factoring through such a $D$ by restriction from its given $D$-section. If an arrow has two factorizations, restrict to the covering <sieve on a category> generated by arrows from $\mathcal D$ into its domain; compatibility makes the two restrictions equal, and the sheaf's uniqueness of amalgamation makes the sections equal. The resulting matching family glues uniquely over $C$. Thus $G(C)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(yC|_{\mathcal D},G|_{\mathcal D})$. Restriction and $F\mapsto H_F$ are inverse up to <natural isomorphism>, proving
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal C,J)\simeq[\mathcal D^{\mathrm{op}},\mathbf{Set}].}
$$

Work internally with the <Heyting algebra> $\Omega$ and the truth value $u$ of $U\hookrightarrow1$. For $o(u)(p)=u\Rightarrow p$, the identities
$$
u\Rightarrow\top=\top,\quad p\le u\Rightarrow p,\quad u\Rightarrow(p\wedge q)=(u\Rightarrow p)\wedge(u\Rightarrow q),\quad u\Rightarrow(u\Rightarrow p)=(u\wedge u)\Rightarrow p=u\Rightarrow p
$$
prove that it is an <open local operator>.

For a <monomorphism> $m:S\hookrightarrow X$ with characteristic map $\chi$, density means $u\Rightarrow\chi=\top$, equivalently $u\le\chi$. This says exactly that $U\times X$ is contained in $S$, or that the pullback mono $U\times S\to U\times X$ is invertible. Consequently
$$
\boxed{m\text{ is }o(U)\text{-dense}\iff U^*(m)\text{ is an isomorphism}.}
$$

An object $X$ is an $o(U)$-sheaf exactly when the canonical map $\eta_X:X\to X^U$, taking a value to its constant function on $U$, is invertible. Indeed $T\times U\hookrightarrow T$ is dense for every $T$. The sheaf condition makes $\mathcal E(T,X)\to\mathcal E(T\times U,X)\cong\mathcal E(T,X^U)$ bijective for every $T$, which proves $\eta_X$ invertible by the <Yoneda lemma>. Conversely, if $\eta_X$ is invertible and $m:S\hookrightarrow T$ is dense, the isomorphism $S\times U\cong T\times U$ gives
$$
\mathcal E(T,X)\cong\mathcal E(T\times U,X)\cong\mathcal E(S\times U,X)\cong\mathcal E(S,X),
$$
so $X$ is a sheaf.

The pullback <functor> $L:\mathcal E\to\mathcal E/U$ has right adjoint $\Pi_U$, sending $A\to U$ to $A^U$. Every map $X\times U\to A$ is automatically over $U$, since $U$ is <subterminal object>, proving this adjunction directly. Its counit $U\times A^U\to A$ is evaluation and is invertible. The inverse sends $a\in A$ to its image in $U$ together with the constant function $U\to A$ of value $a$; all elements of $U$ agree, so the composites are identities. The right adjoint is therefore full and faithful, and its image consists of objects fixed by $X\to X^U$. The sheaf characterization above proves
$$
\boxed{\mathbf{sh}_{o(U)}(\mathcal E)\simeq\mathcal E/U.}
$$

For a general <local operator> $j$, its <dense monomorphism classifier> is the ordered <subobject>
$$
J=\{p\in\Omega:jp=\top\}\hookrightarrow\Omega.
$$
A mono is <j-dense> exactly when its characteristic map factors through $J$. If $j=o(u)$, then $J=\{p:u\le p\}$, which has least element $u$. This is least internally over every parameter object, not just among global points.

Conversely suppose a global $u:1\to J$ is internally least. Upward closure of $J$ gives $jp=\top$ exactly when $u\le p$, in every context. For $S\hookrightarrow X$ let $\overline S$ be its $j$-closure, with characteristic predicate $jp$. The mono $S\hookrightarrow\overline S$ is dense: restricting $jp$ to $\overline S$ gives truth. Its characteristic map therefore lands in $J$, so leastness of $u$ gives $U\cap\overline S\subseteq S$. Internally this is
$$
u\wedge jp\le p,\qquad\text{hence }jp\le u\Rightarrow p.
$$
On the other hand $ju=\top$, and applying $j$ to $u\wedge(u\Rightarrow p)\le p$ gives $j(u\Rightarrow p)\le jp$. Inflationarity then gives $u\Rightarrow p\le jp$. Both inequalities yield
$$
\boxed{J\text{ has a least element }u\iff j=o(U),\qquad j(p)=u\Rightarrow p.}
$$
This proves the <least dense truth characterizes an open local operator> criterion without replacing its internal order condition by the weaker condition on global truth values.

= Solution
{parent=7}

Use one ring sort with $0,1,+,-,\cdot$ and the usual unital <commutative ring> equations. Write $\operatorname{Inv}(x)$ for the <coherent formula> $\exists y\,(xy=1)$. Add the <coherent sequents>
$$
0=1\vdash\bot,\qquad\top\vdash_x\operatorname{Inv}(x)\vee\operatorname{Inv}(1-x).
$$
This presents the <coherent theory of local rings>. For an ordinary nonzero ring satisfying these axioms, the nonunits form an ideal: a multiple of a nonunit cannot be a unit; and if nonunits $a,b$ had unit sum, multiplying by $(a+b)^{-1}$ would make two nonunits sum to $1$, contradicting the second axiom. They are also closed under additive inverses. This proper ideal contains every proper ideal, since a proper ideal contains no unit, and its quotient is a field; it is the unique <maximal ideal>. Conversely in a <local ring>, $x$ and $1-x$ cannot both belong to the unique <maximal ideal>, so one is a unit. The first sequent excludes the zero ring. In a <topos>, the disjunction asserts that these unit alternatives hold locally.

Let $\mathcal C$ be a small skeleton of the opposite of the category of finitely presented unital <commutative rings>; write $C_A$ for the object corresponding to $A$. The <Zariski coverage on finitely presented rings> is generated by
$$
\{C_{A[f_i^{-1}]}\longrightarrow C_A\}_{i=1}^n,\qquad(f_1,\ldots,f_n)=A,
$$
and the empty cover of $C_0$. Localization is finitely presented, and its compatibility with base change proves the covering axioms.

Here is the classifying argument. For an internal commutative ring $M$ and a presentation $A=\mathbb Z[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(p_1,\ldots,p_r)$, define $F_M(C_A)$ as the <subobject> of $M^n$ satisfying the polynomial equations $p_i=0$. Ring maps induce maps of these solution objects in the site direction. This is independent of presentation, and it preserves <finite limits>: finite colimits of finitely presented rings correspond to joining finite generators and equations, whose solution objects are <finite limits>. Conversely a left-exact <functor> $F:\mathcal C\to\mathcal E$ reconstructs a commutative ring $F(C_{\mathbb Z[t]})$ using the polynomial maps for addition, multiplication, constants and additive inverse. Presentations by generators and equations then show $F\cong F_M$. Thus flat <functors> on $\mathcal C$ correspond to internal <commutative rings>.

Under $F_M$, the localization arrow above identifies its domain with the locus where $f_i$ evaluates to a unit; the inverse witness is unique. The family is jointly epimorphic exactly when these unit loci cover the solution object for $A$. The locality axiom implies this for every unit-ideal family: from a finite sum $\sum f_i g_i=1$, repeatedly apply the two-summand unit alternative, rescaling a unit partial sum when necessary; some $f_i g_i$, hence some $f_i$, is a unit locally. Conversely the cover of $\mathbb Z[t]$ by its localizations at $t$ and $1-t$ gives precisely the locality axiom, while the empty cover of the zero ring gives $0=1\vdash\bot$. Therefore continuous flat <functors> are exactly models of the displayed <coherent theory>. The <Diaconescu equivalence for geometric morphisms> now identifies
$$
\operatorname{Geom}(\mathcal E,\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal C,Z))\simeq\operatorname{Mod}_{\mathrm{local\ rings}}(\mathcal E),
$$
naturally in $\mathcal E$. This proves the asserted <classifying topos> identification.

Since the site is <subcanonical>, the <generic local ring> is the representable sheaf
$$
R=yC_{\mathbb Z[t]},\qquad R(C_A)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(\mathbb Z[t],A)=A.
$$
Its ring operations are induced by polynomial substitution. Its infinitesimal <subobject> is likewise representable:
$$
D=yC_{\mathbb Z[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)},\qquad D(C_A)=\{a\in A:a^2=0\}.
$$
Products of representables correspond to tensor products of their rings. For every finitely presented $A$, exponential adjunction and the <Yoneda lemma> give
$$
\begin{aligned}
(R^D)(C_A)&\cong\operatorname{Hom}(yC_A\times D,R)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}(yC_{A[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)},yC_{\mathbb Z[t]})\\
&\cong A[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2).
\end{aligned}
$$
Every element of this ring has a unique expression $a+b\varepsilon$, because $1,\varepsilon$ form an $A$-basis. This holds even when $A$ has nilpotents or zero divisors. The expression is natural under every <ring homomorphism> $A\to B$, which sends its two coefficients to their images. The canonical morphism $R\times R\to R^D$, transposed from $(a,b,d)\mapsto a+bd$, is consequently a bijection at every stage $C_A$, with a natural inverse extracting the coefficients. Therefore
$$
\boxed{R\times R\xrightarrow{\sim}R^D,\qquad(a,b)\longmapsto(d\mapsto a+bd).}
$$
This proves the <Kock-Lawvere axiom for the generic local ring>. The uniqueness is an internal sheaf assertion tested at all finitely presented rings, including the ring with its generic square-zero element; restricting the test to fields would lose that element and would not prove uniqueness.
