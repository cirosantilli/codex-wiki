= Solution
{parent=a}

Choose local coordinates $y^1,\ldots,y^{D-1}$ on the <hypersurface> $\Sigma$, and let $\Phi_s$ be the <local flow> of the <vector field> $X$. The map $F(s,y)=\Phi_s(p(y))$ follows the <integral curves of a vector field> starting at $p(y)\in\Sigma$. At $s=0$, its differential maps the $s$ direction to $X$ and the remaining directions to a basis of $T_p\Sigma$. Transversality makes these vectors linearly independent, so the <inverse function theorem> makes $F$ a local <manifold chart>.

Set $x^0=s$ and transport the $y^i$ unchanged along each <integral curve of a vector field>. Then **the adapted coordinates satisfy**
$$
\boxed{X=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^0},\qquad X(x^i)=0.}
$$
The <flow-box theorem> gives this construction locally. The parameter is the flow parameter, and need not be <arc length>; a global <manifold chart> would require additional hypotheses.

= Solution
{parent=i}

In a <stationary spacetime> chart, take $V=\partial_0$. Its squared length is $g_{00}<0$, so $V$ is a <timelike vector>. Since its components are constant, the <Lie derivative of a tensor field> obeys $(\mathcal L_Vg)_{\mu\nu}=\partial_0g_{\mu\nu}=0$. For the <Levi-Civita connection>,
$$
(\mathcal L_Vg)_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu V_\nu+\nabla_\nu V_\mu,
$$
so $V$ satisfies the <Killing equation>.

Conversely, a timelike <Killing vector field> is nowhere zero. Apply the <flow-box theorem> to choose coordinates with $V=\partial_0$. The <Killing equation> then gives $\partial_0g_{\mu\nu}=0$, and $g_{00}=g(V,V)<0$. Thus **local stationarity is equivalent to the existence of a timelike Killing vector field**.

= Solution

Use the standard meaning of <static spacetime>: a <stationary spacetime> chart whose time-space components also vanish. The printed static definition needs to inherit the timelike requirement $g_{00}<0$; without it, the requested timelike conclusion does not follow.

The preceding argument shows that $V=\partial_0$ is a timelike <Killing vector field>. Its metric-dual <one-form> is $v=V_\mu dx^\mu=g_{00}dx^0$. Its <exterior derivative> satisfies
$$
v\wedge dv=g_{00}dx^0\wedge (dg_{00}\wedge dx^0)=0.
$$
For a <torsion-free connection>, antisymmetrizing the <covariant derivative> of a <one-form> gives its <exterior derivative>. Consequently **the Killing vector has hypersurface orthogonality**:
$$
\boxed{V_{[\alpha}\nabla_\mu V_{\nu]}=0.}
$$
Geometrically, the hypersurfaces $x^0=\mathrm{constant}$ are orthogonal to $V$.

= Solution

Write $F=V_\rho V^\rho<0$ and $A_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu V_\nu$. The <Killing equation> makes $A_{\mu\nu}$ antisymmetric. Expanding <hypersurface orthogonality> and contracting with $V^\alpha$ gives
$$
V_\alpha A_{\mu\nu}+V_\mu A_{\nu\alpha}+V_\nu A_{\alpha\mu}=0,
\qquad
F A_{\mu\nu}+\frac12V_\mu\partial_\nu F-\frac12V_\nu\partial_\mu F=0.
$$
Here <metric compatibility> gives $\partial_\mu F=2V^\rho A_{\mu\rho}$. Therefore
$$
2F A_{\mu\nu}=V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F,
$$
and the <normalized Killing one-form> satisfies
$$
\nabla_\mu(F^{-1}V_\nu)-\nabla_\nu(F^{-1}V_\mu)
=\frac{2A_{\mu\nu}}F-\frac{V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F}{F^2}=0.
$$
Thus **the required exponent is** $\boxed{n=-2}$. The notation $|V|^2$ in this paper denotes the negative scalar $F$, rather than a positive squared norm; the answer uses only its real reciprocal. More generally, for a multiplier $h(F)$ the antisymmetric derivative is $(h/F+h')(V_\nu\partial_\mu F-V_\mu\partial_\nu F)$, so $h=C/F$ is the universally valid choice. If $F$ is constant, other exponents can work in that particular metric.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

The <normalized Killing one-form> $\eta=F^{-1}V_\mu dx^\mu$ is a <closed differential form>. The <Poincare lemma> gives a local scalar potential $\phi$ with $\eta=d\phi$, hence $V_\mu=F\partial_\mu\phi$. Choose the adapted chart of part (i), so $V=\partial_0$. Then
$$
\partial_0\phi=\eta(V)=1,\qquad
\phi=x^0+f(x^1,x^2,x^3),\qquad
\partial_i f=\frac{g_{0i}}{g_{00}}.
$$
The <closed differential form> condition supplies the integrability of these spatial derivatives. For a fixed $g$ and $V$, $f$ is determined locally up to an additive constant; its derivatives cannot be chosen freely despite the wording in the paper.

Use $\bar x^0=\phi$, $\bar x^i=x^i$. The <Jacobian determinant> is one, so this is a local coordinate change. Holding $\bar x^i$ fixed gives $V=\partial_{\bar0}$, while its metric-dual <one-form> is $v=F\,d\bar x^0$. Thus $\bar g_{00}=F<0$ and $\bar g_{0i}=0$. The <Killing equation> still holds, so $\partial_{\bar0}\bar g_{\mu\nu}=0$. **The metric is locally static**:
$$
\boxed{\bar g_{0i}=0,\qquad\partial_{\bar0}\bar g_{\mu\nu}=0.}
$$
A <closed differential form> need not be globally exact; neither a global potential nor a global static chart follows without additional topological assumptions.

= Solution
{parent=a}

The <contracted Bianchi identity> gives $\nabla^aR_{ab}=\tfrac12\nabla_bR$. Combining the proposed equation with <stress-energy conservation> therefore imposes $\nabla_bR=0$. Taking its trace gives $R=\kappa T$, with $T=g^{ab}T_{ab}$, so for nonzero $\kappa$ the matter trace must also be constant. Ordinary matter need not satisfy this extra condition. For example, if asymptotic flatness forces the constant <scalar curvature> to vanish, the proposal would require $T=0$ everywhere.

**The problem is an unjustified restriction on the matter trace**, rather than the absence of every possible solution. The divergence-free <Einstein tensor> avoids it:
$$
\boxed{G_{ab}=R_{ab}-\frac12Rg_{ab},\qquad\nabla^aG_{ab}=0.}
$$
The <Einstein field equations> $G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=\kappa T_{ab}$, with constant <cosmological constant> $\Lambda$, are consequently compatible with <stress-energy conservation>.

= Solution
{parent=i}

Contract the <Christoffel symbol> formula for the <Levi-Civita connection>. The first and third derivative terms cancel after relabelling their summed indices:
$$
\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}=\frac12g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma}.
$$
The <Jacobi determinant derivative formula> gives $\partial_\mu g=g\,g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma}$. In a Lorentzian chart $g<0$, and hence $\partial_\mu\sqrt{-g}=\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\rho\sigma}\partial_\mu g_{\rho\sigma}$. Thus **the trace is the logarithmic derivative of the volume density**:
$$
\boxed{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\rho\mu}=\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu\sqrt{-g}.}
$$
In particular, the <covariant divergence formula> for a <vector field> is $\nabla_\mu A^\mu=(-g)^{-1/2}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}A^\mu)$.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Vary the inverse <metric tensor> while holding the matter fields fixed. Differentiating $g_{a\mu}g^{\mu b}=\delta_a{}^b$ gives $\delta g_{ab}=-g_{a\mu}g_{b\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$. The <Jacobi determinant derivative formula> consequently implies the <inverse metric volume variation>
$$
\delta\sqrt{-g}=-\frac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\delta g^{\alpha\beta}.
$$
Since the matter <Lagrangian> in this question contains no derivatives of the <metric tensor>, its metric variation is algebraic. The product rule gives
$$
\frac{\partial(\sqrt{-g}L)}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}
=\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}-\frac12g_{\alpha\beta}L\right).
$$
Multiplying by $-2/\sqrt{-g}$ proves **the equivalence of the stress-energy definitions**:
$$
\boxed{T_{\alpha\beta}=-2\frac{\partial L}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}+Lg_{\alpha\beta}.}
$$
The variations of the symmetric <metric tensor> are understood symmetrically. If the matter <action> contained metric derivatives, the definition would instead require a functional derivative and the corresponding integration by parts.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

For the <minimally coupled scalar field>, keep the <metric tensor> fixed and vary $\phi$. A scalar's first <covariant derivative> equals its ordinary derivative, so
$$
\delta S=\int_M\sqrt{-g}\left[-\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\delta\phi-V'(\phi)\delta\phi\right]d^4x.
$$
Apply <integration by parts for tensor fields>. The boundary contribution is proportional to $\delta\phi$ and vanishes under the given boundary condition, leaving
$$
\delta S=\int_M\sqrt{-g}\,[\Box\phi-V'(\phi)]\delta\phi\,d^4x.
$$
The interior variation is arbitrary. Thus **the scalar equation of motion is**
$$
\boxed{\Box\phi-V'(\phi)=0,\qquad
\Box\phi=\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi).}
$$
Here the <d'Alembert operator> contains the <Levi-Civita connection> in the second <covariant derivative>, even though the first derivative of the <scalar field> is ordinary.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

The metric dependence of the <minimally coupled scalar field> <Lagrangian> is $\partial L/\partial g^{\alpha\beta}=-\tfrac12\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi$. Part (ii) therefore gives **the stress-energy tensor**
$$
\boxed{T_{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_\alpha\phi\nabla_\beta\phi-g_{\alpha\beta}\left[\frac12\nabla_\rho\phi\nabla^\rho\phi+V(\phi)\right].}
$$
Use <metric compatibility> and the <symmetric Hessian of a scalar field> to compute
$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_{\alpha}
&=(\Box\phi)\nabla_\alpha\phi+\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\mu\nabla_\alpha\phi
-\nabla^\mu\phi\,\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\mu\phi-V'(\phi)\nabla_\alpha\phi\\
&=(\Box\phi-V'(\phi))\nabla_\alpha\phi.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is the <scalar stress-energy divergence identity>, an instance of the <diffeomorphism Noether identity for a scalar field>. On the scalar equation of motion it gives **stress-energy conservation**, $\boxed{\nabla_\mu T^\mu{}_{\alpha}=0}$.

= Solution
{parent=i}

For each fixed coordinate function $x^\alpha$, the expression $\Box x^\alpha$ is a scalar: $\alpha$ labels four chosen functions and is not a free <tensor> index. The equation is therefore coordinate invariant when those functions are held fixed. However, the demand that the currently chosen coordinates themselves be <harmonic coordinates> is a chart condition, not a four-vector equation invariant under arbitrary changes of coordinate functions.

Indeed, for new functions $\bar x^\alpha=f^\alpha(x)$ the <d'Alembert operator> gives
$$
\Box\bar x^\alpha=f^\alpha{}_{,\beta}\Box x^\beta+g^{\beta\gamma}f^\alpha{}_{,\beta\gamma}.
$$
The second term prevents a vector transformation law. An affine redefinition preserves <harmonic coordinates>; an arbitrary nonlinear one does not. For example, a Cartesian spatial coordinate $x$ in <Minkowski spacetime> has $\Box x=0$, whereas $\Box(x^2)=2$, and $x^2$ is a valid new coordinate on $x>0$. **The equations are scalar equations for specified functions, while harmonic gauge is chart dependent.**

= ii
{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Treat each $x^\alpha$ as a <scalar field>. Its second <covariant derivative> is $\nabla_\beta\nabla_\gamma x^\alpha=-\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\gamma\beta}$, so $\Box x^\alpha=-H^\alpha$, where $H^\alpha=g^{\beta\gamma}\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}$.

On the other hand, <metric compatibility> gives
$$
0=\nabla_\nu g^{\mu\nu}=\partial_\nu g^{\mu\nu}+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\lambda\nu}g^{\lambda\nu}+\Gamma^\nu{}_{\lambda\nu}g^{\mu\lambda}.
$$
Using the <Christoffel trace identity> in the last term yields
$$
\partial_\nu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu})=-\sqrt{-g}\,H^\mu.
$$
Since the volume density never vanishes in a valid chart, **the harmonic coordinate conditions are equivalent**:
$$
\boxed{\Box x^\alpha=0\iff g^{\beta\gamma}\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}=0
\iff\partial_\nu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu})=0.}
$$

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Contract the printed <Riemann curvature tensor> using $R_{ab}=R^\mu{}_{a\mu b}$. Only the derivative terms can contain second derivatives of the <metric tensor>:
$$
R_{ab}=\partial_\mu\Gamma^\mu{}_{ab}-\partial_b\Gamma^\mu{}_{a\mu}
+\Gamma^\tau{}_{ab}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\tau\mu}-\Gamma^\tau{}_{a\mu}\Gamma^\mu{}_{\tau b}.
$$
Substituting the <Christoffel symbol> formula, its second derivative part is
$$
\frac12g^{\mu\nu}\left(\partial_\mu\partial_a g_{\nu b}+\partial_\mu\partial_b g_{\nu a}
-\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{ab}-\partial_a\partial_b g_{\mu\nu}\right).
$$
Set $H_b=g_{b\lambda}H^\lambda=g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu g_{\nu b}-\tfrac12g^{\mu\nu}\partial_b g_{\mu\nu}$. The first, second and fourth terms are the second derivative terms of $\tfrac12(\partial_aH_b+\partial_bH_a)$. Derivatives of the inverse <metric tensor> produce only products of first derivatives. Thus
$$
R_{ab}=-\frac12g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{ab}
+\frac12(\partial_aH_b+\partial_bH_a)+Q_{ab}(g,\partial g),
$$
where $Q$ contains at most first metric derivatives. In <harmonic coordinates>, $H^\lambda$ vanishes throughout the chart, so $H_b$ and its derivatives vanish. **The vacuum equations have the wave principal part**
$$
\boxed{R_{ab}=-\frac12g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu g_{ab}+Q_{ab}(g,\partial g)=0.}
$$
These are <quasilinear partial differential equations> with the principal part of a <wave equation>, because $g^{\mu\nu}$ has Lorentzian signature. This is the <harmonic reduction of the vacuum Einstein equations>.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

First, the inverse <metric tensor> printed in the PDF has a missing factor: its lower-left entry must be $g^{i0}=\alpha^{-2}\beta^i$, symmetric with $g^{0i}$. This correction follows either from symmetry or direct block multiplication and does not change the evolution equation used here.

Let $D_t=\partial_t-\beta^k\partial_k$. Contract the spatial <metric tensor> evolution equation with $\gamma^{ij}$. Both shift-gradient terms contribute $\partial_k\beta^k$, giving
$$
\gamma^{ij}D_t\gamma_{ij}-2\partial_k\beta^k=-2\alpha\gamma^{ij}K_{ij}=-2\alpha K.
$$
The <Jacobi determinant derivative formula> applies to every derivative of $\gamma=\det(\gamma_{ij})$ and gives $D_t\gamma=\gamma\gamma^{ij}D_t\gamma_{ij}$. Therefore **the spatial determinant evolves as**
$$
\boxed{\partial_t\gamma-\beta^k\partial_k\gamma-2\gamma\partial_k\beta^k=-2\alpha\gamma K.}
$$
Equivalently, the <spatial volume evolution identity> is $D_t\sqrt\gamma=\sqrt\gamma(\partial_k\beta^k-\alpha K)$, with $K$ the trace of the <extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface>.

Use the equivalent density form of <harmonic slicing> from part (ii). With $\sqrt{-g}=\alpha\sqrt\gamma$, positive <lapse function> $\alpha$, and the corrected inverse <metric tensor>, its time component becomes
$$
0=-\partial_t\left(\frac{\sqrt\gamma}{\alpha}\right)
+\partial_i\left(\frac{\sqrt\gamma}{\alpha}\beta^i\right).
$$
Thus $D_t(\sqrt\gamma/\alpha)=(\sqrt\gamma/\alpha)\partial_i\beta^i$, or
$$
D_t\log\sqrt\gamma-D_t\log\alpha=\partial_i\beta^i.
$$
The <spatial volume evolution identity> gives $D_t\log\sqrt\gamma=\partial_i\beta^i-\alpha K$. Subtraction yields $D_t\log\alpha=-\alpha K$, hence **the <harmonic lapse evolution> is**
$$
\boxed{\partial_t\alpha-\beta^i\partial_i\alpha=-\alpha^2K,\qquad f(\alpha)=-\alpha^2.}
$$
The <shift vector> need not vanish; its transport term is essential in a general <3+1 decomposition of spacetime>.

= Solution
{parent=a}

A <spacetime> is a smooth four-dimensional <manifold> equipped with a smooth <Lorentzian metric>, conventionally of <metric signature> $(-,+,+,+)$, and a choice of <time orientation>. The usual manifold assumptions include the <Hausdorff space> and <second-countable space> conditions. A physical model also specifies matter fields and requires the <Einstein field equations> and matter equations.

<Diffeomorphism invariance of general relativity> means that relabelling events by a smooth invertible map, while transforming the <metric tensor> and every matter field together, preserves the form of the equations. Passively, a coordinate change gives new components for the same geometric fields. Actively, pulling all fields back by a <diffeomorphism> gives another representative of the same physical geometry, subject to any prescribed boundary conditions or boundary symmetries.

**The geometry and matter together carry the physical content; coordinate labels do not.** A <diffeomorphism> need not be an <isometry> of a fixed metric, and unrelated metrics are not automatically physically equivalent.

= Solution
{parent=i}

Use the connection convention $\nabla_\alpha Y^\rho=\partial_\alpha Y^\rho+\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\alpha}Y^\mu$, with the derivative index last. This is consistent with the printed curvature formula and the final formula in part (iv). The paper's $T_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^\rho{}_{\nu\mu}$ is then the negative of the geometric <torsion tensor> defined by $\mathcal T(X,Y)=\nabla_XY-\nabla_YX-[X,Y]$. Keep the paper's component convention throughout this question.

Set $A_{\mu\nu\rho}=g_{\rho\lambda}(\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}-S^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu})$. The <difference of affine connections is a tensor>. Since the <Levi-Civita connection> $S$ is symmetric and metric compatible, expanding the <nonmetricity tensor> gives
$$
T_{\mu\nu\rho}=A_{\mu\nu\rho}-A_{\nu\mu\rho},\qquad
N_{\mu\nu\rho}=-A_{\mu\rho\nu}-A_{\nu\rho\mu}.
$$
Here $T_{\mu\nu\rho}=g_{\rho\lambda}T_{\mu\nu}{}^\lambda$ is antisymmetric in its first two slots, while $N_{\mu\nu\rho}$ is symmetric in its first two slots. Cyclically permuting these identities and eliminating the other components gives
$$
A_{\mu\nu\rho}=\frac12\left(T_{\mu\nu\rho}+T_{\nu\rho\mu}-T_{\rho\mu\nu}
+N_{\mu\nu\rho}-N_{\nu\rho\mu}-N_{\rho\mu\nu}\right).
$$
Raise the last slot to obtain **the connection decomposition**
$$
\boxed{\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}=S^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}-K_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho+W_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho,}
$$
where
$$
K_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho=-\frac12\left(T_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho+T_\nu{}^\rho{}_\mu-T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\right),\qquad
W_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho=\frac12\left(N_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho-N_\nu{}^\rho{}_\mu-N^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\right).
$$
**The PDF is missing the factor $1/2$ in its definition of $K$.** With the printed $K$, the decomposition would instead contain $-K/2$. In these formulas $T^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}$ raises the first slot of the fully lowered <tensor>; it must not be confused with $T_{\mu\nu}{}^\rho$, which raises the last slot.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

The <Levi-Civita connection> is the unique <affine connection> that has both vanishing <torsion tensor> and <metric compatibility>. The <affine connection decomposition> shows exactly how an arbitrary <affine connection> departs from it: the <contorsion tensor> contributes the metric-compatible torsion correction, and the <disformation tensor> contributes the nonmetricity correction. The torsion and <nonmetricity tensor>, together with the <metric tensor>, determine the difference uniquely.

In the paper's convention the actual contorsion correction is $-K$, with the corrected half factor from part (i), and the disformation correction is $W$. Both corrections are <tensors>, although the separate connection coefficients $\Gamma$ and $S$ are not <tensors>. **If both torsion and nonmetricity vanish, then** $\boxed{\Gamma=S}$. Vanishing nonmetricity alone permits torsion, and vanishing torsion alone permits nonmetricity.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Let $\gamma_X(s)$ be the affinely parametrized <geodesic> of the <affine connection> with $\gamma_X(0)=p$ and $\dot\gamma_X(0)=X$. For fixed $t$, the curve $\eta(u)=\gamma_X(tu)$ has initial velocity $tX$. The <geodesic> equation is homogeneous of degree two in the velocity, so its left-hand side for $\eta$ is $t^2$ times the left-hand side for $\gamma_X$, and is zero. Uniqueness of the <geodesic> <initial value problem> gives $\eta=\gamma_{tX}$.

By the definition of the <affine exponential map>, **rescaling the initial velocity rescales affine parameter**:
$$
\boxed{\exp_p(tX)=\gamma_{tX}(1)=\gamma_X(t).}
$$
At $t=0$ both sides equal $p$. The statement is made where the geodesics exist; without <geodesic completeness>, the <affine exponential map> is defined only on a suitable neighbourhood of zero in $T_pM$, not necessarily all of $T_pM$. An affine parameter distance need not be a metric length.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

Choose a basis of $T_pM$ and use the <affine exponential map> to define <affine normal coordinates>. Its differential at zero is the identity, so the <inverse function theorem> gives a chart near $p$. By part (iii), radial <geodesics> have coordinates $x^\mu(s)=sX^\mu$. Their geodesic equations at $p$ imply $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(p)X^\mu X^\nu=0$ for every $X$. The <polarization identity> therefore gives $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{(\mu\nu)}(p)=0$.

The <disformation tensor> $W_{\mu\nu}{}^\lambda$ is symmetric in $\mu,\nu$, whereas $T_{\mu\nu}{}^\lambda$ is antisymmetric. Also $T_\nu{}^\lambda{}_\mu=-T^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}$. Taking the symmetric part of the corrected <affine connection decomposition> consequently gives **the normal-coordinate identity**
$$
\boxed{S^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}+W_{\mu\nu}{}^\lambda-\frac12T^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}-\frac12T^\lambda{}_{\nu\mu}=0\quad\text{at }p.}
$$
Thus the final identity in the PDF is correct after the half-factor repair in part (i). In general $\Gamma^\lambda{}_{\mu\nu}(p)=\tfrac12T_{\mu\nu}{}^\lambda(p)$ in the paper's convention, rather than zero. Neither $S$ nor the first derivatives of the <metric tensor> need vanish in these <affine normal coordinates>. With the <Levi-Civita connection>, both torsion and nonmetricity vanish and the usual vanishing of the <Christoffel symbols> is recovered.
