= Solution
{parent=a}

Include all fixed resource states and all measurement records in an environment, so the entire physical protocol has an <linear isometry> $U$ from the unknown input to a larger pure output. If every recipient receives a perfect copy, its <reduced density matrix> is the pure projector $|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$. A pure marginal forces factorization: write a joint <vector> in a <basis> beginning with $|\psi\rangle$; its squared components along every <orthogonal> <basis> <vector> vanish because their marginal <probabilities> are zero. Applying this successively to all recipients gives
$$
U(|\psi\rangle|R\rangle)=|\psi\rangle^{\otimes N}|E_\psi\rangle,
$$
where $|R\rangle$ is the fixed initial resource and $|E_\psi\rangle$ may depend on the input. This includes Alice's discarded systems and every possible outcome record.

Choose two distinct nonorthogonal <pure states>, for example $|0\rangle$ and $|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$, and write their overlap as $s$. Preservation of the <inner product> by the <linear isometry> requires
$$
s=s^N\langle E_\psi|E_\varphi\rangle.
$$
Normalized environment <vectors> have overlap of absolute value at most one. Thus $0<|s|<1$ would imply $|s|\leq|s|^N$, impossible for $N>1$. Hence \b[perfect transmission of a copy to every recipient is impossible for $N>1$]. This proves the <no-cloning theorem> obstruction even though Alice keeps no copy herself.

Write $N=2k+1$ and abbreviate the <Dicke states> by $|D_j^n\rangle=|\Psi_j^n\rangle$. Alice's shared resource has decomposition
$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}\left(|0\rangle|D_{k+1}^N\rangle+|1\rangle|D_k^N\rangle\right).
$$
Follow the actual circuit order: a <controlled-NOT gate> from the input to $q_0$, a <Hadamard gate> on the input and a <Pauli X gate> on $q_0$, followed by <computational basis> measurements. Just before measurement, the joint <vector> is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac12\big[&|00\rangle(\alpha|D_k^N\rangle+\beta|D_{k+1}^N\rangle)\\
+&|01\rangle(\alpha|D_{k+1}^N\rangle+\beta|D_k^N\rangle)\\
+&|10\rangle(\alpha|D_k^N\rangle-\beta|D_{k+1}^N\rangle)\\
+&|11\rangle(\alpha|D_{k+1}^N\rangle-\beta|D_k^N\rangle)\big].
\end{aligned}
$$
The <Dicke states> in each branch are <orthogonal>, so each branch has squared <norm> $1/4$. Conditioning on outcome $00$ and normalizing therefore gives the state of the other $N$ parties:
$$
\boxed{|\Omega\rangle=\alpha|D_{(N-1)/2}^N\rangle+\beta|D_{(N+1)/2}^N\rangle.}
$$
This is the encoding used in <Dicke-resource telecloning>. The extra <Pauli X gate> on Alice's resource <qubit> is essential to the displayed ordering of the amplitudes.

The state in part (b) is symmetric in the receiving <qubits>. Single out Bob using the <Dicke state> recursion. For $k\geq1$, the two <vectors> are
$$
\begin{aligned}
|D_k^N\rangle&=\sqrt{\frac{k+1}{N}}|0\rangle|D_k^{N-1}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{k}{N}}|1\rangle|D_{k-1}^{N-1}\rangle,\\
|D_{k+1}^N\rangle&=\sqrt{\frac{k}{N}}|0\rangle|D_{k+1}^{N-1}\rangle+\sqrt{\frac{k+1}{N}}|1\rangle|D_k^{N-1}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
Distinct excitation counts give <orthogonal> states of the other recipients. Taking their <partial trace>, the only surviving off-diagonal term comes from the common rest <vector> $|D_k^{N-1}\rangle$. The <one-qubit reduction of Dicke-state superpositions> is consequently
$$
\boxed{\rho=\frac1N\begin{pmatrix}k+|\alpha|^2&(k+1)\alpha\beta^*\\(k+1)\alpha^*\beta&k+|\beta|^2\end{pmatrix},\qquad k=\frac{N-1}{2}.}
$$
Put $p=|\alpha|^2$ and $q=|\beta|^2$, so $p+q=1$. The overlap with the input is
$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\psi|\rho|\psi\rangle&=\frac{p(k+p)+q(k+q)+2(k+1)pq}{N}\\
&=\frac{k+p^2+q^2+2(k+1)pq}{N}=\frac{k+1+2kpq}{N}\\
&=\boxed{\frac{N+1+2|\alpha|^2|\beta|^2(N-1)}{2N}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
For $N=1$ there is no system to trace out, and the same <matrix> is $|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$, with overlap one. For every $N>1$ the overlap is below one, even at its maximum $p=q=1/2$, so these are imperfect copies.

Define $E|0\rangle=|D_k^N\rangle$ and $E|1\rangle=|D_{k+1}^N\rangle$. From the branch calculation in part (b), outcome $R_0=0,R_1=1$ leaves
$$
\alpha|D_{k+1}^N\rangle+\beta|D_k^N\rangle=E(\beta|0\rangle+\alpha|1\rangle)=EX|\psi\rangle.
$$
Thus \b[the effective input teleported was $X|\psi\rangle=\beta|0\rangle+\alpha|1\rangle$]. A <Pauli X gate> flips every bit and hence maps a <Dicke state> with $j$ excitations to one with $N-j$ excitations. Since $N-k=k+1$, applying $X$ at every receiving party interchanges the two logical <basis> states and restores $E|\psi\rangle$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\text{Bob and each other recipient should apply }X.}
$$
In particular Bob's uncorrected <reduced density matrix> is $X\rho X$, and his correction returns it to the <matrix> in part (c). The remaining branches can likewise be corrected: $Z^{\otimes N}$ implements logical $Z$ up to the common <global phase> $(-1)^k$, while $X^{\otimes N}$ implements logical $X$. Thus the outcome dependence can be removed by local <Pauli gates> even though the resulting copies remain imperfect.

Put $L=2^N$ and define $\theta=\arcsin\sqrt{M/L}$. The normalized good and bad <vectors> have disjoint <computational basis> supports, so they are <orthogonal>. Counting the terms in the uniform <quantum superposition> gives
$$
\boxed{|\phi\rangle=\sin\theta\,|\Psi_g\rangle+\cos\theta\,|\Psi_b\rangle,\qquad\sin\theta=\sqrt{M/2^N}.}
$$
Since $V_0=I-2|0^N\rangle\langle0^N|$, the <Hadamard transform> gives $-H^{\otimes N}V_0H^{\otimes N}=2|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|-I$, the <Grover diffusion operator>. On the ordered <orthonormal basis> $(|\Psi_g\rangle,|\Psi_b\rangle)$, the two <reflection operators> have <matrices>
$$
U_f=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad 2|\phi\rangle\langle\phi|-I=\begin{pmatrix}-\cos2\theta&\sin2\theta\\\sin2\theta&\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Their product is
$$
\boxed{G=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&\sin2\theta\\-\sin2\theta&\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}.}
$$
This is a plane <rotation> through angle $-2\theta$ in the displayed coordinate convention, with the invariant good-bad plane preserved by both operators. In particular its action sends $(\sin a,\cos a)$ to $(\sin(a+2\theta),\cos(a+2\theta))$. Induction therefore gives the <Grover rotation angle> formula
$$
G^n|\phi\rangle=\sin((2n+1)\theta)|\Psi_g\rangle+\cos((2n+1)\theta)|\Psi_b\rangle.
$$

= Solution
{parent=b}

Measuring the state from part (a) in the <computational basis> succeeds with <probability> $p_n=\sin^2((2n+1)\theta)$. In the sparse-solution regime $0<M\leq2^{N-1}$, one has $0<\theta\leq\pi/4$. The first success interval with $p_n\geq1/2$ is $\pi/4\leq(2n+1)\theta\leq3\pi/4$. Entering it requires $n\geq\pi/(8\theta)-1/2$, so the <first half-probability Grover iterate> is
$$
\boxed{n_{\min}=\left\lceil\frac{\pi}{8\arcsin\sqrt{M/2^N}}-\frac12\right\rceil.}
$$
For this integer, the angle is at least $\pi/4$ and less than $\pi/4+2\theta\leq3\pi/4$, so the rounding cannot skip the required interval. Every smaller nonnegative integer has angle below $\pi/4$ and hence success below $1/2$, proving minimality. For $M\ll2^N$, the leading size is $n_{\min}\sim(\pi/8)\sqrt{2^N/M}$. If $M\geq2^{N-1}$, the initial measurement already succeeds with <probability> at least one half and the smallest count is zero; if $M=0$ no search can succeed.

For the known bit string $y$, define the <Pauli gate> product $T_y=\bigotimes_{j=0}^{N-1}Z_j^{y_j}$. Its diagonal action is $T_y|x\rangle=(-1)^{x\cdot y}|x\rangle$, where the <binary inner product> is taken modulo two. The <Hadamard transform> produces $|\phi_y\rangle=H^{\otimes N}|y\rangle=T_y|\phi\rangle$. Thus the required <vectors> for <Grover search with a nonzero reflection label> are
$$
\boxed{|\widetilde\Psi_g\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt M}\sum_{f(x)=1}(-1)^{x\cdot y}|x\rangle,\qquad |\widetilde\Psi_b\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt{2^N-M}}\sum_{f(x)=0}(-1)^{x\cdot y}|x\rangle.}
$$
They remain <orthonormal> and $|\phi_y\rangle=\sin\theta|\widetilde\Psi_g\rangle+\cos\theta|\widetilde\Psi_b\rangle$. The new iterate is $G_y=(2|\phi_y\rangle\langle\phi_y|-I)U_f$. Since $T_y$ commutes with the diagonal <marked-state phase oracle> and $T_y^2=I$,
$$
G_y=T_yGT_y.
$$
Consequently its <matrix> on the signed <basis> is exactly the <rotation matrix> in part (a), proving the claimed equivalence of the two evolutions.

Initialize the register in
$$
\boxed{|\phi_y\rangle=H^{\otimes N}|y\rangle=T_y|\phi\rangle.}
$$
This can be prepared by <Pauli X gates> on the one-bits of $y$, starting from $|0^N\rangle$, followed by <Hadamard gates> on all <qubits>. Alternatively, prepare the original uniform state and apply <Pauli Z gates> at those same bit positions. The conjugation calculation gives
$$
G_y^n|\phi_y\rangle=T_yG^n|\phi\rangle.
$$
The final diagonal sign operator changes no <computational basis> outcome <probabilities>. Thus the success <probability> and the iteration count in part (b) are unchanged. No final correction is needed when the objective is to measure a marked string.

The <Pauli matrices> obey $X|0\rangle=|1\rangle$, $X|1\rangle=|0\rangle$, $Y|0\rangle=i|1\rangle$, and $Y|1\rangle=-i|0\rangle$. Thus $X\otimes X$ and $Y\otimes Y$ contribute opposite amplitudes on $|00\rangle,|11\rangle$ and equal amplitudes on $|01\rangle,|10\rangle$. For the <XY exchange interaction> $E=(X\otimes X+Y\otimes Y)/2$,
$$
\boxed{E|00\rangle=0,\quad E|01\rangle=|10\rangle,\quad E|10\rangle=|01\rangle,\quad E|11\rangle=0.}
$$
Equivalently $E=|01\rangle\langle10|+|10\rangle\langle01|$. It transfers an excitation across a link without creating or destroying excitations, so a sum of such links preserves the <single-excitation subspace>.

= Solution
{parent=b}

In the <single-excitation subspace>, part (a) turns each coupling into a hopping coefficient between adjacent sites. With $|\widetilde n\rangle$ denoting the excitation at site $N+n$, the two central links give
$$
\boxed{H_e|\widetilde0\rangle=\frac{J_0}{\sqrt2}(|\widetilde{-1}\rangle+|\widetilde1\rangle).}
$$
For $N\geq2$ the links from these neighbors to the next sites have strength $J_1$. Applying the <Hamiltonian operator> a second time yields
$$
\boxed{H_e^2|\widetilde0\rangle=J_0^2|\widetilde0\rangle+\frac{J_0J_1}{\sqrt2}(|\widetilde{-2}\rangle+|\widetilde2\rangle).}
$$
The central coefficient is $2(J_0/\sqrt2)^2=J_0^2$. On the comparison path, direct multiplication gives
$$
H_T|0\rangle=J_0|1\rangle,\qquad H_T^2|0\rangle=J_0^2|0\rangle+J_0J_1|2\rangle.
$$
Thus the two calculations match under $|0\rangle\mapsto|\widetilde0\rangle$ and $|j\rangle\mapsto(|\widetilde{-j}\rangle+|\widetilde j\rangle)/\sqrt2$ for $j=1,2$. If $N=1$, the terms involving $J_1$ and distance two are absent on both sides.

Define <orthonormal> symmetric <vectors>
$$
|s_0\rangle=|\widetilde0\rangle,\qquad |s_j\rangle=\frac{|\widetilde{-j}\rangle+|\widetilde j\rangle}{\sqrt2}\quad(1\leq j\leq N).
$$
The <symmetric-arm reduction of an exchange Hamiltonian> here has two arms. To verify it directly, the link between radial distances $j$ and $j+1$ has strength $J_j$ on either side, while the two central links have strength $J_0/\sqrt2$. The calculation of part (a) therefore gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
H_e|s_0\rangle&=J_0|s_1\rangle,\\
H_e|s_j\rangle&=J_{j-1}|s_{j-1}\rangle+J_j|s_{j+1}\rangle\quad(1\leq j<N),\\
H_e|s_N\rangle&=J_{N-1}|s_{N-1}\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
In particular this subspace is invariant and contains the initial <vector>. Define the <linear isometry> $F|j\rangle=|s_j\rangle$. The displayed <matrix> entries prove $H_eF=FH_T$, or $F^\dagger H_eF=H_T$. Induction gives $H_e^mF=FH_T^m$ for every nonnegative integer $m$; hence the convergent exponential series proves $e^{-iH_et}F=Fe^{-iH_Tt}$. This establishes the mapping for the entire evolution, rather than just its first two powers. The supplied endpoint transfer also forces every $J_j$ to be nonzero, since a broken link would disconnect the two endpoints. Successive powers applied to $|s_0\rangle$ then generate all $|s_j\rangle$, so this is exactly the <cyclic subspace> governing the initial state.

Using the stated <perfect quantum state transfer> identity at time $\pi/2$,
$$
\boxed{e^{-iH_e\pi/2}|\widetilde0\rangle=F|N\rangle=\frac{|\widetilde{-N}\rangle+|\widetilde N\rangle}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
The two terms have the excitation at sites $0$ and $2N$, respectively, and all intermediate <qubits> are zero in both terms. The full state thus factors into a <Bell state> at the endpoints and the vacuum on the interior. Its <partial trace> is
$$
\boxed{\rho_{0,2N}=|\Psi^+\rangle\langle\Psi^+|,\qquad |\Psi^+\rangle=\frac{|10\rangle+|01\rangle}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
The coherent cross terms survive the trace because the interior <vectors> coincide. We have used the transfer phase convention supplied in the question; a common transfer phase would leave this <density operator> unchanged.

Replace the two arms by three identical arms of length $N$ attached to one central <qubit>. Label the hub $c$ and the sites on arm $a$ by $(a,j)$, $a=1,2,3$, $j=1,\ldots,N$. Use <XY exchange interactions> with coupling $J_0/\sqrt3$ on each hub-to-first-site link, and coupling $J_j$ on each arm link $(a,j)$ to $(a,j+1)$. Initially excite only the hub: this is the <product state> $|1\rangle_c$ with every arm <qubit> in $|0\rangle$.

In the symmetric <single-excitation subspace>, define $|s_0\rangle=|c\rangle$ and $|s_j\rangle=3^{-1/2}\sum_{a=1}^3|a,j\rangle$. The hub coupling is
$$
H|s_0\rangle=\frac{J_0}{\sqrt3}\sum_{a=1}^3|a,1\rangle=J_0|s_1\rangle,
$$
and the reverse coupling has the same coefficient. All other radial links have coefficients $J_j$, just as in part (c). Thus the <symmetric-arm reduction of an exchange Hamiltonian> is again exactly $H_T$. At time $\pi/2$ its evolution is
$$
|s_0\rangle\longmapsto|s_N\rangle=\frac{|1,N\rangle+|2,N\rangle+|3,N\rangle}{\sqrt3}.
$$
Every nontip <qubit> is zero, so tracing them out leaves the three tips in the pure <W state>
$$
\boxed{|W_3\rangle=\frac{|100\rangle+|010\rangle+|001\rangle}{\sqrt3}.}
$$
The essential change is the additional identical arm and the normalization of each hub coupling by $\sqrt3$ instead of $\sqrt2$.

Label the control <qubits> by binary weight: the bit $x_n$ contributes $2^n$ to $x=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}2^nx_n$. For each $n$, use that <qubit> as the control of the supplied controlled-$V^{2^n}$, with the same target <eigenstate> $|v_j\rangle$. The <controlled unitary gate> acts by
$$
\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}|v_j\rangle\longmapsto\frac{|0\rangle+e^{i2^n\phi_j}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}|v_j\rangle,
$$
since $V^{2^n}|v_j\rangle=e^{i2^n\phi_j}|v_j\rangle$. The target remains unchanged after every application. Multiplying these factors gives
$$
\left[\bigotimes_{n=0}^{N-1}\frac{|0\rangle+e^{i2^n\phi_j}|1\rangle}{\sqrt2}\right]|v_j\rangle=\boxed{\frac1{\sqrt{2^N}}\sum_{x=0}^{2^N-1}e^{i\phi_jx}|x\rangle}\,|v_j\rangle.
$$
The ordering of the tensor factors is chosen to match the binary place values. Discarding the unchanged target leaves the required phase register. This uses <quantum phase kickback> in the phase accumulation step of <quantum phase estimation>.

Set $L=2^N$. Applying the inverse <quantum Fourier transform> to the register in part (a) gives <probability amplitude> at label $m$ equal to
$$
a_m=\frac1L\sum_{x=0}^{L-1}e^{ix\delta},\qquad\delta=\phi_j-\frac{2\pi m}{L}.
$$
Choose $m$ nearest to $\eta=L\phi_j/(2\pi)$, with $m$ reduced modulo $L$ at the phase boundary. Then the difference can be represented with $|\delta|\leq\pi/L$. For $\delta\ne0$, sum the <finite geometric series> to obtain
$$
a_m=e^{i(L-1)\delta/2}\frac{\sin(L\delta/2)}{L\sin(\delta/2)}.
$$
Let $z=L|\delta|/2\leq\pi/2$. On $[0,\pi/2]$, <concavity> of sine gives $\sin z\geq2z/\pi$, the line through its endpoint values. Also $|\sin(\delta/2)|\leq|\delta|/2$. Therefore
$$
|a_m|\geq\frac{(2/\pi)(L|\delta|/2)}{L|\delta|/2}=\frac2\pi,
$$
and the <Born rule> proves the <nearest-integer success bound for quantum phase estimation>:
$$
\boxed{\Pr(m)=|a_m|^2\geq\frac4{\pi^2}.}
$$
If $\delta=0$, every summand is one and the <probability> is exactly one. If the scaled phase is halfway between two integers, either nearest label separately satisfies the displayed bound. Interpreting the measured integer as $m/L$ gives the closest available $N$-bit phase estimate modulo one.

Choose an <orthonormal> eigenbasis of $V$, with <eigenphases> $\phi_0,\phi_1$, counting multiplicity. The <maximally mixed state> is <basis> independent, so
$$
\rho=\frac12|v_0\rangle\langle v_0|+\frac12|v_1\rangle\langle v_1|.
$$
<Linearity> means that the experiment is the equal mixture of the two <eigenstate> experiments. After the inverse <quantum Fourier transform>, the joint state is $\frac12\sum_{j=0}^1|\chi_j\rangle\langle\chi_j|\otimes|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|$, where $|\chi_j\rangle=\sum_m a_m(\phi_j)|m\rangle$ and
$$
a_m(\phi)=\frac1L\sum_{x=0}^{L-1}e^{ix(\phi-2\pi m/L)}.
$$
Thus the exact output distribution for <quantum phase estimation with a maximally mixed target> is
$$
\boxed{\Pr(m)=\frac12|a_m(\phi_0)|^2+\frac12|a_m(\phi_1)|^2,\qquad m=0,\ldots,L-1.}
$$
For a nonzero phase mismatch, the squared <probability amplitude> is $\sin^2[L(\phi-2\pi m/L)/2]/\{L^2\sin^2[(\phi-2\pi m/L)/2]\}$, with value one at an exact match. If both <eigenphases> are exactly representable on the $L$-point grid, their two labels occur with <probability> $1/2$ each; coincident labels have their <probabilities> added. For general <eigenphases> there can be nonzero <probabilities> at other labels, so it would not be exact to list only the two best approximations with <probability> $1/2$ each. Each <eigenphase> is sampled with spectral weight $1/2$, and its nearest-label event has <conditional probability> at least $4/\pi^2$. In particular a nearest label for one <eigenphase> has unconditional <probability> at least $2/\pi^2$, with additional contributions possible from the other phase.

= Solution
{parent=d}

With access to the uncontrolled physical gate, use <single-qubit process tomography>. Prepare the positive <eigenstate> of each of $X,Y,Z$, apply $V$, and on independent copies measure each of $X,Y,Z$. Arbitrary single-qubit <rotation gates> let one prepare these inputs and rotate each measurement into the <computational basis>. The nine measured means are
$$
R_{ij}=\operatorname{Tr}\left[\sigma_iV\frac{I+\sigma_j}{2}V^\dagger\right]=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(\sigma_iV\sigma_jV^\dagger),\qquad i,j\in\{x,y,z\}.
$$
They determine the real <rotation matrix> $R$ of the <Bloch vector>, and hence the action of $V$ on every <qubit> state. To see precisely what this identifies, suppose another unitary $U$ has the same action. Then $V^\dagger U$ commutes with every <Pauli matrix>. Commuting with $Z$ makes it diagonal; commuting with $X$ makes its diagonal entries equal. Therefore $U=e^{i\gamma}V$.

Consequently \b[tomography determines $V$, and its <eigenvalues>, up to a common phase]. More explicitly, write a representative as $V=e^{i\gamma}(\cos a\,I-i\sin a\,\boldsymbol n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)$. Its <eigenvalues> are $e^{i(\gamma-a)}$ and $e^{i(\gamma+a)}$, and its <Bloch vector> action is <rotation> through $2a$ about $\boldsymbol n$. The measured <rotation> identifies the unordered relative <eigenphase> information; for example $\operatorname{Tr}R=1+2\cos(2a)$. Once a representative with a specified overall phase is supplied, diagonalizing that representative gives the corresponding two <eigenvalues>.

The common phase itself cannot be recovered with the stated uncontrolled access. Indeed, $(e^{i\gamma}V)\rho(e^{i\gamma}V)^\dagger=V\rho V^\dagger$ for every input, including inputs entangled with ancillas. Replacing $V$ by $e^{i\gamma}V$ therefore leaves every such preparation-and-measurement experiment unchanged, whereas it multiplies both <eigenvalues> by $e^{i\gamma}$. For example, $I$ and $e^{i\gamma}I$ are indistinguishable physical gates under these operations. This proves that the request for absolute <eigenvalue> phases needs an additional phase reference or controlled-gate assumption: the <global phase of an uncontrolled quantum gate is unobservable>. The controlled powers in the earlier parts supply that extra resource, since $\operatorname{diag}(I,e^{i\gamma}V)$ changes a relative phase between the two control branches.

The practical advantages are that no unknown <eigenstate> preparation, controlled powers, entangling gates or inverse <quantum Fourier transform> are needed: a fixed set of single-qubit preparations and measurements suffices. The disadvantages are <statistical estimation> and the phase ambiguity just proved. Estimating the bounded <Pauli measurement> means to additive accuracy $\epsilon$ at fixed confidence requires order $\epsilon^{-2}$ independent repetitions, giving the usual sampling cost for this direct procedure. The earlier <quantum phase estimation> circuit uses $N$ phase <qubits> and high controlled powers to resolve scale $2^{-N}$ in one coherent experiment with a constant success bound; constructing those powers from controlled uses of $V$ costs $2^N-1$ gate calls, so its exponential-looking precision is not obtained with only $N$ uses of $V$. Repetitions can raise confidence. Finally, full process tomography grows exponentially with the number of system <qubits>, whereas the eigenstate-based phase procedure can estimate a selected <eigenphase> without reconstructing the entire operator.
