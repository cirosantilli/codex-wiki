Use dimensionless <spin angular momentum> operators, so their commutators have no additional $\hbar$. A fully polarized state $|F\rangle=\bigotimes_n|S,S\rangle_n$ is an exact <ground state> of the <Heisenberg ferromagnet>, with **$E_0=-NJS^2$**. On each bond the largest eigenvalue of $\mathbf S_n\cdot\mathbf S_{n+1}$ is $S^2$, obtained in the two-spin total-spin-$2S$ sector. Hence the bondwise lower bound on the Hamiltonian is attained by this state.

The <ferromagnetic ground-state multiplet> has total spin $NS$. Applying the global lowering operator $(S^-_{\rm tot})^r$, $r=0,\ldots,2NS$, generates its $2NS+1$ magnetic states; their linear combinations are also <ground states>. Global spin rotations generate polarized <ground states> pointing in arbitrary directions and span the same multiplet. Uniform reorientation therefore costs no energy. Slowly varying orientations give gapless <ferromagnetic magnons>, with quadratic rather than linear long-wavelength dispersion. In the ordered thermodynamic <ground state>, the two broken spin generators are canonically paired because $\langle[S^x_{\rm tot},S^y_{\rm tot}]\rangle=i\langle S^z_{\rm tot}\rangle\ne0$, producing one <type-B Goldstone boson>. For a finite chain the zero-wavevector motion remains inside the exact ground multiplet; nonzero wavevectors have a finite-size spacing that vanishes as $N^{-2}$. This is a ground-state statement, not a claim of finite-temperature long-range order in one dimension.

= Solution
{parent=a}

The <Holstein–Primakoff transformation> acts on the physical number-state subspace $0\leq n\leq2S$. Its operator ordering matters: $S^+=\sqrt{2S-\hat n}\,a$ lowers the number first. Therefore
$$
S^+|n\rangle=\sqrt{n(2S-n+1)}|n-1\rangle,\qquad
S^-|n\rangle=\sqrt{(n+1)(2S-n)}|n+1\rangle,
$$
while $S^z|n\rangle=(S-n)|n\rangle$. The square roots vanish at the appropriate ends, so the spin operators preserve the <Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint>. The <bosonic Fock space> outside this subspace is not part of the spin-$S$ representation.

These matrix elements give $[S^z,S^\pm]=\pm S^\pm$. The two products on $|n\rangle$ are $(n+1)(2S-n)$ and $n(2S-n+1)$, respectively, so their difference is $2(S-n)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{[S^+,S^-]=2S^z,\qquad[S^z,S^\pm]=\pm S^\pm.}
$$
Also $(S^z)^2+\frac12(S^+S^-+S^-S^+)=S(S+1)I$. The transformation therefore has both the correct algebra and the correct spin <Casimir operator>, not just its small-occupation approximation.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For occupation much smaller than $2S$, expand the square root in the <Holstein–Primakoff transformation>. To leading order $S_n^+=\sqrt{2S}\,a_n+O(S^{-1/2})$, $S_n^-=\sqrt{2S}\,a_n^\dagger+O(S^{-1/2})$ and $S_n^z=S-a_n^\dagger a_n$. Consequently
$$
\mathbf S_n\cdot\mathbf S_{n+1}
=S^2-S(\hat n_n+\hat n_{n+1})
+S(a_n^\dagger a_{n+1}+a_{n+1}^\dagger a_n)+O(S^0).
$$
Periodic boundaries count each occupation in two bonds. The <linear spin-wave approximation> is therefore
$$
\boxed{H=-NJS^2+JS\sum_n[2a_n^\dagger a_n-a_n^\dagger a_{n+1}-a_{n+1}^\dagger a_n]+O(S^0).}
$$
The displayed fluctuation Hamiltonian is bilinear and conserves <boson> number. The omitted terms are magnon interactions, quartic at the first omitted order. With occupation held of order one, they are smaller by $1/S$ than the order-$S$ quadratic terms. The polarized vacuum has no zero-point depletion in this ferromagnetic expansion.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Use $a_n=N^{-1/2}\sum_ke^{ikn}a_k$ with $k=2\pi j/N$, choosing $N$ distinct values modulo $2\pi$ in the <Brillouin zone>. The lattice spacing is one; for spacing $a$ the dimensionless variable here would be $ka$. Orthogonality of the discrete Fourier modes diagonalizes the bilinear Hamiltonian:
$$
H=E_0+\sum_k\varepsilon_k a_k^\dagger a_k,\qquad
\boxed{\varepsilon_k=2JS(1-\cos k)=4JS\sin^2(k/2).}
$$
This is the <nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion>. The printed expression is missing a factor of four for the stated exchange Hamiltonian. If $\omega_k$ denotes a physical frequency, $\hbar\omega_k=\varepsilon_k$; setting $\hbar=1$ does not remove that factor. The printed coefficient would require an exchange constant $J/4$ in the original Hamiltonian.

An independent check uses one spin lowering at site $n$. The exact one-magnon Hamiltonian relative to $E_0$ has diagonal element $2JS$ and off-diagonal elements $-JS$ at $n\pm1$, giving the same Fourier eigenvalue. At small $k$, **$\varepsilon_k\simeq JS k^2$**, and the $k=0$ state belongs to the degenerate ground multiplet. The quadratic low-energy branch is the ferromagnetic <type-B Goldstone boson>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Repeat the <linear spin-wave approximation> for bonds of lengths one and two. The polarized energy is $E_0=-NS^2(J_1+J_2)$ and each bond length contributes its own lattice Laplacian. Thus
$$
\boxed{\varepsilon_k=2S[J_1(1-\cos k)+J_2(1-\cos2k)]
=4S[J_1\sin^2(k/2)+J_2\sin^2k].}
$$
The <next-nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion> remains nonnegative for the stated positive exchanges and has its only zero at $k=0$ modulo $2\pi$ when $J_1>0$. At small $k$, $\varepsilon_k\simeq S(J_1+4J_2)k^2$: longer-range ferromagnetic exchange increases the stiffness.

At this harmonic order, a bosonic occupation configuration has energy $E_0+\sum_k\varepsilon_kn_k$. The one-magnon dispersion is also exact. General multi-magnon energies acquire the interactions omitted in the large-$S$ expansion; the formula does not claim an exact diagonalization of the full interacting many-spin Hamiltonian.

= Solution

Write $\mathcal V=L^3$ and $N_0=N-N_{\rm ex}$, where $N_{\rm ex}=\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}$. Macroscopic occupation permits $b_0,b_0^\dagger\simeq\sqrt{N_0}$, with relative commutator corrections of order $1/N_0$. Keep the fixed-total-number correction before replacing $n_0$ by $n$.

The four-condensate term is $V_0N_0(N_0-1)/(2\mathcal V)$. Its leading expansion gives $V_0N^2/(2\mathcal V)-nV_0N_{\rm ex}$. Terms with two condensate operators give normal scattering $n(V_0+V_{\mathbf k})b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}$ and conversion of two condensate particles into opposite-momentum excitations, with coefficient $nV_{\mathbf k}/2$. The $nV_0$ normal contribution cancels against the change of condensate energy at fixed $N$. Hence the <number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian> is
$$
\boxed{H\simeq\frac{\mathcal V}{2}V_0n^2+
\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left[(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}+nV_{\mathbf k})b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}
+\frac{nV_{\mathbf k}}2(b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger+b_{-\mathbf k}b_{\mathbf k})\right].}
$$
Use a real even interaction transform, $V_{\mathbf k}=V_{-\mathbf k}$, as for a real inversion-symmetric pair potential. The constant is the condensate interaction energy; the diagonal part combines particle kinetic energy with condensate scattering; the anomalous pair terms create and annihilate correlated opposite momenta while transferring particles to or from the condensate.

At fixed volume, counting replaced condensate operators gives quadratic vertices proportional to $N_0$, omitted cubic vertices proportional to $\sqrt{N_0}$ and quartic vertices with no condensate factor. There are also the finite-$N_0$ correction in $N_0(N_0-1)$ and corrections from the condensate-number expansion. Their physical control parameter is small depletion, $N_{\rm ex}/N\ll1$, together with weak interaction; simply dropping them is not justified by low temperature in a strongly interacting gas. This is the <weakly interacting Bose gas> approximation.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Put $A_k=\epsilon_k+nV_k$ and $B_k=nV_k$, and assume the homogeneous condensate is stable, so $A_k>|B_k|$ for nonzero modes. A real <Bogoliubov transformation> is
$$
b_{\mathbf k}=u_k\alpha_{\mathbf k}-v_k\alpha_{-\mathbf k}^\dagger,\qquad u_k^2-v_k^2=1.
$$
The canonical condition preserves the bosonic <canonical commutation relations>. Cancellation of pair terms requires $2A_ku_kv_k=B_k(u_k^2+v_k^2)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{E_k=\sqrt{A_k^2-B_k^2}=\sqrt{\epsilon_k(\epsilon_k+2nV_k)},}
$$
$$
u_k^2=\frac12\left(\frac{A_k}{E_k}+1\right),\qquad
v_k^2=\frac12\left(\frac{A_k}{E_k}-1\right),\qquad u_kv_k=\frac{B_k}{2E_k}.
$$
The sign of $v_k$ follows that of $B_k$. <Normal ordering> the transformed operators gives the <bosonic Bogoliubov zero-point shift>:
$$
\boxed{H=\frac{\mathcal V}{2}V_0n^2+\frac12\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}(E_k-A_k)+\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}E_k\alpha_{\mathbf k}^\dagger\alpha_{\mathbf k}.}
$$
Rearranging the last two terms is exactly the requested $-\frac12\sum A_k+\sum E_k(\alpha_k^\dagger\alpha_k+1/2)$ form. Each momentum appears in the sums, so opposite-momentum pairs are not counted again separately.

The <Bogoliubov quasiparticle dispersion> is linear at low momentum when $V_k\to V_0>0$:
$$
\boxed{E_k\simeq\hbar c_s|k|,\qquad c_s=\sqrt{nV_0/m}.}
$$
It is a gapless <phonon> of the condensate, unlike the free-particle quadratic spectrum. For a contact repulsion $V_k=g>0$, the <healing length> $\xi=\hbar/\sqrt{2mng}$ controls crossover to the particle regime $E_k\simeq\epsilon_k+ng$. A momentum-dependent potential can modify the higher-energy curve; the sketch uses the contact case and does not assume every interaction has that entire shape.

= Solution
{parent=c}

In the <ground state> of the diagonal Hamiltonian, $\alpha_{\mathbf k}|0_B\rangle=0$. The <Bogoliubov transformation> therefore gives $\langle b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}\rangle=v_k^2$. Summing all nonzero momenta proves the <quantum depletion of a Bose condensate>:
$$
\boxed{n-n_0=\frac1{2L^3}\sum_{\mathbf k\ne0}\left(\frac{\epsilon_{\mathbf k}+nV_{\mathbf k}}{E_{\mathbf k}}-1\right).}
$$
The nonzero depletion arises from correlated pairs in the interacting <quasiparticle> vacuum, not thermally populated <quasiparticles>.

The printed expression is the zero-temperature result. At a nonzero temperature the complete leading-order expression instead has
$$
\langle b_{\mathbf k}^\dagger b_{\mathbf k}\rangle=v_k^2+(u_k^2+v_k^2)n_B(E_k),\qquad
n_B(E)=\frac1{e^{\beta E}-1}.
$$
The additional term is thermal depletion and cannot be discarded merely because the gas is condensed.

= Solution

Single-valued rotor wavefunctions are periodic in $\phi$. The normalized eigenfunctions are $\langle\phi|n\rangle=e^{in\phi}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ with integer $n$, angular momentum $\hbar n$ and energy $E_n=\hbar^2n^2/(2I)$. The <quantum rotor> consequently has
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\beta\hbar^2n^2/(2I)}.}
$$
The nonzero levels have the $n\leftrightarrow-n$ degeneracy; the <ground state> is $n=0$. No twisted boundary condition or applied flux has been introduced.

= Solution
{parent=b}

The propagator is the quantum amplitude for evolving from one angular position to another. It evolves a wavefunction by $\Psi(\phi_F,t)=\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi_I\,K(\phi_F,\phi_I;t)\Psi(\phi_I,0)$. The <path integral> weights each trajectory by $e^{iS/\hbar}$, with $S=\int dt\,I(\partial_t\phi)^2/2$. Since the configuration space is a circle, paths must include all possible lifts of the final angle to the real line.

Take $t=-i\hbar\beta$ by <Wick rotation>. Let $\tau=t_E/\hbar$, which runs from zero to $\beta$, and let the dot denote $d/d\tau$. The dimensionless Euclidean exponent is then $I\int_0^\beta\dot\phi^2d\tau/(2\hbar^2)$. Tracing the kernel identifies final and initial angles modulo $2\pi$, while the lift can change by $2\pi m$. Thus the <rotor winding-sector path integral> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z=\int_0^{2\pi}d\phi_0\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}
\int_{\phi(0)=\phi_0}^{\phi(\beta)=\phi_0+2\pi m}\mathcal D\phi\,
\exp\left[-\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}\int_0^\beta d\tau\,\dot\phi^2\right].}
$$
The integration measure is fixed by the short-time free-rotor kernel. The dot on $\phi$ is essential; it is present in the PDF but lost in the TeX aid.

= Solution
{parent=c}

In sector $m$, separate the stationary winding path from its endpoint-fixed fluctuations:
$$
\phi(\tau)=\phi_0+\frac{2\pi m}{\beta}\tau+\eta(\tau),\qquad\eta(0)=\eta(\beta)=0.
$$
The cross term integrates to $2(2\pi m/\beta)[\eta(\beta)-\eta(0)]=0$. Hence
$$
\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}\int_0^\beta\dot\phi^2d\tau
=\frac{I(2\pi m)^2}{2\hbar^2\beta}+\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}\int_0^\beta\dot\eta^2d\tau.
$$
The Gaussian fluctuation integral is independent of $m$ and $\phi_0$. Denoting it by $\mathcal Z_0$, integration over the starting angle gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z=2\pi\mathcal Z_0\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}e^{-I(2\pi m)^2/(2\hbar^2\beta)}.}
$$
The <rotor return-kernel fluctuation prefactor> $\mathcal Z_0$ is the coincident-point free-particle kernel on the lifted line, or the return amplitude density from zero-net-winding paths. It is not the $n=0$ angular-momentum contribution: winding number and momentum quantum number label different descriptions.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Analytically continue the free-particle return kernel using $M=I$ and $t=-i\hbar\beta$. This fixes
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{I}{2\pi\hbar^2\beta}}.}
$$
Set $a=2\pi^2I/(\hbar^2\beta)$. The Gaussian Fourier integral in <Poisson resummation> is
$$
\int_{-\infty}^\infty dx\,e^{-ax^2+2\pi inx}=\sqrt{\frac\pi a}\,e^{-\pi^2n^2/a}.
$$
Applying it to the winding sum gives $\sum_me^{-am^2}=\sqrt{\pi/a}\sum_ne^{-\pi^2n^2/a}$. The prefactors cancel exactly, $2\pi\mathcal Z_0\sqrt{\pi/a}=1$, and $\pi^2/a=\beta\hbar^2/(2I)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\beta\hbar^2n^2/(2I)},}
$$
in agreement with the spectral trace. This is <momentum-winding duality of the quantum rotor>. At low temperature the momentum <ground state> dominates; at high temperature the winding-zero sector gives $\mathcal Z\simeq\sqrt{2\pi I/(\beta\hbar^2)}$.

= Solution
{parent=e}

For smooth phase differences, $-J\cos(\phi_{n+1}-\phi_n)=-J+J(\phi_{n+1}-\phi_n)^2/2+\cdots$. After integrating the angular momenta in the same inverse-energy time coordinate $\tau$, the <coupled quantum rotor chain> has the fluctuation action
$$
\boxed{\mathcal S=\int_0^\beta d\tau\int dx\left[\frac{I}{2\hbar^2}(\partial_\tau\phi)^2+\frac J2(\partial_x\phi)^2\right].}
$$
Unit spacing replaces the lattice sum and difference by their continuum limits. The constant energy $-J$ per bond contributes an overall factor $e^{\beta JN}$, suppressed when describing fluctuations. The phase is compact; the Gaussian action describes its smooth sector.

There is an <imaginary-time normalization for a coupled rotor chain> issue in the printed formula. Factoring out $1/\hbar^2$ from the action above places $\hbar^2J$, not $J$, in its spatial bracket. The printed bracket is therefore correct in $\hbar=1$ units, or with its spatial coefficient redefined as $J_{\rm bracket}=\hbar^2J_{\rm Hamiltonian}$. With the same dimensional $J$ as in the Hamiltonian, it lacks that factor.

In physical real time the wave equation is $I\partial_t^2\phi=J\partial_x^2\phi$. The <harmonic phase mode of a quantum rotor chain> consequently has
$$
\boxed{\omega(q)=\sqrt{J/I}\,|q|,\qquad E(q)=\hbar\sqrt{J/I}\,|q|.}
$$
The lattice expression is $\omega(q)=2\sqrt{J/I}|\sin(q/2)|$. The mode is a gapless phase wave analogous to sound in a <superfluid>, with rotor angular momentum conjugate to its phase. In one dimension this harmonic regime has strong long-distance phase fluctuations rather than true continuous-symmetry long-range order; compact <phase slips> can change the infrared phase outside the smooth-phase approximation.

= Solution

Let $D_n=\hat n_{n\uparrow}\hat n_{n\downarrow}$. On a site, empty and doubly occupied states have spin zero, while either singly occupied state has spin $1/2$. Thus
$$
\hat{\mathbf S}_n^2=\frac34(\hat n_n-2D_n),\qquad
\boxed{\hat{\mathbf S}_n^2-\frac34\hat n_n=-\frac32D_n=:\hat{\mathbf S}_n^2:.}
$$
The <on-site spin-square normal-ordering identity> rewrites the interaction as $3U\sum_nD_n$. Its contact coefficient is $3U$ in this parameterization, rather than the $U$ convention often used for the <Hubbard model>.

Using a <fermionic coherent-state trace> and time slicing, replace each normal-ordered monomial by its <Grassmann field> symbol. With $\xi_{\mathbf k}=\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu$,
$$
\boxed{\mathcal Z=\int\mathcal D(\bar\psi,\psi)e^{-\mathcal S},\qquad
\mathcal S=\int_0^\beta d\tau\left[\sum_{\mathbf k\sigma}\bar\psi_{\mathbf k\sigma}(\partial_\tau+\xi_{\mathbf k})\psi_{\mathbf k\sigma}
+3U\sum_n\bar\psi_{n\uparrow}\bar\psi_{n\downarrow}\psi_{n\downarrow}\psi_{n\uparrow}\right].}
$$
The independent fields have antiperiodic thermal boundary conditions. <Normal ordering> is crucial: the explicit $3\hat n/4$ subtraction removes the spin-square contraction, so there is no extra chemical-potential shift from it.

= Solution

At $U=0$, use fermionic <Matsubara frequencies> $\omega_r=(2r+1)\pi/\beta$. The <fermionic Gaussian integral> has inverse propagator $-i\omega_r+\xi_{\mathbf k}$. With its thermal normalization, the determinant for one single-particle level is $1+e^{-\beta\xi}$, as also follows from its empty and occupied states. Two spin species therefore give
$$
\mathcal Z_0=\prod_{\mathbf k}[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu)}]^2,
\qquad
\boxed{\Omega_0=-\frac1\beta\ln\mathcal Z_0=-2k_BT\sum_{\mathbf k}\ln[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu)}].}
$$
The expression labeled <Helmholtz free energy> in the PDF is the <grand potential>, since the trace contains $H-\mu\hat N$. The thermodynamic <Helmholtz free energy> at a prescribed electron number $N_e$ is **$F=\Omega_0+\mu N_e$**, with $\mu$ fixed by $N_e=2\sum_{\mathbf k}n_F(\epsilon_{\mathbf k})$. The Matsubara determinant requires a normalization prescription; a raw infinite sum of logarithms is not an ordinary convergent sum.

= Solution

Define $\mathbf j_n=\bar\psi_n\boldsymbol\sigma\psi_n$ and $D=\bar\psi_\uparrow\bar\psi_\downarrow\psi_\downarrow\psi_\uparrow$. The <Pauli matrix completeness identity>, or direct evaluation of its three components, gives $\mathbf j^2=-6D$. Consequently the quartic action is $3UD=-U\mathbf j^2/2$.

The <Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling> follows by completing a three-component square at each site and time slice:
$$
\int d^3M\,\exp\left[-\Delta\tau\left(\frac{\mathbf M^2}{2U}-\mathbf M\cdot\mathbf j\right)\right]
=C\exp\left[\frac{U\Delta\tau}{2}\mathbf j^2\right]=C e^{-3U\Delta\tau D}.
$$
The constant $C$ is absorbed into the normalized auxiliary-field measure, and $U>0$ ensures convergence of the real <Gaussian integral>. Thus the <spin-vector Hubbard–Stratonovich decoupling> yields
$$
\boxed{\mathcal S=\int_0^\beta d\tau\sum_{\mathbf k\sigma}\bar\psi_{\mathbf k\sigma}(\partial_\tau+\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu)\psi_{\mathbf k\sigma}
+\int_0^\beta d\tau\sum_n\left[\frac{\mathbf M_n^2}{2U}-\bar\psi_n\mathbf M_n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\psi_n\right].}
$$
Integrating out $\mathbf M$ reconstructs the normal-ordered interaction exactly. The auxiliary field is periodic in imaginary time, while the fermionic fields remain antiperiodic.

= Solution

In the homogeneous spin-field sector, choose $\mathbf M=M\hat{\mathbf e}_z$. The fermionic levels become $\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu-\sigma M$, where $\sigma=\pm1$ is the Pauli eigenvalue. The <fermionic one-level thermal determinant> can be applied independently to every momentum and spin, while the field cost is $\beta NM^2/(2U)$. Hence the <homogeneous spin-field effective action> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal S_{\rm eff}[M]=-\sum_{\mathbf k,\sigma}\ln[1+e^{-\beta(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\mu-\sigma M)}]+\frac{\beta NM^2}{2U}.}
$$
The remaining homogeneous-field integral is $\mathcal Z\simeq\int\mathcal DM\,e^{-\mathcal S_{\rm eff}}$ in this mean-field restriction. Orientation and fluctuation-independent measure factors do not alter the displayed extensive action or its stationary equation. The two bands are exchange split by $2M$; $M$ has energy units and is proportional to the spin polarization, rather than being an electron number itself.

= Solution

Differentiate the <homogeneous spin-field effective action>:
$$
\frac{d\mathcal S_{\rm eff}}{dM}=\beta\left[\frac{NM}{U}-\sum_{\mathbf k,\sigma}\sigma n_F(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\sigma M)\right].
$$
At a stationary field, $M=(U/N)\sum_{\mathbf k,\sigma}\sigma n_F(\epsilon_{\mathbf k}-\sigma M)$. Define the <spin-summed density of states> per lattice site by
$$
\nu(\epsilon)=\frac2N\sum_{\mathbf k}\delta(\epsilon-\epsilon_{\mathbf k}).
$$
Then the requested form is
$$
\boxed{M=\frac U2\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}\sigma\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)n_F(\epsilon-\sigma M).}
$$
The factor one half fixes the spin convention. With a <single-spin density of states> $\nu_s=\nu/2$, the prefactor would instead be $U$. In terms of site occupations, the same equation is $M=U(n_\uparrow-n_\downarrow)=2U\langle S^z\rangle$. The <Fermi distribution> includes the <chemical potential> in its argument definition; it must not be subtracted a second time.

= Solution
{parent=f}

The spin-independent term cancels in the signed sum. The linearized stationary equation becomes
$$
M=U\chi_0(T)M+O(M^3),\qquad
\chi_0(T)=\int d\epsilon\,\nu(\epsilon)[-\partial_\epsilon n_F(\epsilon)]>0.
$$
The even powers vanish because the equation is odd under $M\mapsto-M$. Equivalently, the mean-field action has curvature
$$
\mathcal S_{\rm eff}(M)=\mathcal S_{\rm eff}(0)+\frac{\beta N}{2}[U^{-1}-\chi_0(T)]M^2+O(M^4).
$$
Thus the unpolarized stationary point becomes unstable when
$$
\boxed{U\chi_0(T)>1,\qquad U_c\chi_0(T)=1.}
$$
At low temperature, the derivative of the <Fermi distribution> samples a narrow window around $\mu$, so $\chi_0\simeq\nu(\mu)$ for a smooth <density of states>. Measuring energy from the Fermi level gives **$U_c\nu(0)\simeq1$**, the <Stoner criterion> in the spin-summed convention fixed above. This identifies local instability of the paramagnetic state; the detailed transition order also depends on higher terms in the action.

In the isotropic ordered phase of <itinerant ferromagnetism>, continuous spin-rotation symmetry leaves a degenerate direction of magnetization. Its transverse fluctuations produce one gapless, quadratically dispersing <mean-field itinerant ferromagnetic Goldstone mode>, $E(\mathbf q)\propto q^2$. The two broken spin generators form a canonical pair, as for the <type-B Goldstone boson> of the localized ferromagnet. The system also remains metallic, with exchange-split <Fermi surfaces> and low-energy spin-conserving particle-hole excitations. Longitudinal-amplitude fluctuations and the spin-flip continuum are distinct from the long-wavelength magnon; their energy and damping scales depend on the band structure. Spin anisotropy, if added, could gap the transverse mode, but it is absent from the stated Hamiltonian.
