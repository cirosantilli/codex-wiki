A <modulus of a number field> is a formal product
$$
\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\mathfrak m_\infty,
$$
where $\mathfrak m_0=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}}$ is a nonzero integral ideal and $\mathfrak m_\infty$ is a product of distinct real places. Only finitely many exponents $n_{\mathfrak p}$ are nonzero.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=2}

Let $L/K$ be a finite abelian extension and let $\mathfrak m$ be an admissible modulus, so every ramified prime divides $\mathfrak m_0$ and the <Artin reciprocity map>
$$
\theta_{L/K}:I_K(\mathfrak m)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
$$
is defined. If $\mathfrak p\nmid\mathfrak m_0$, then the <ideal-theoretic decomposition law> says that its residue degree is
$$
f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)
=\operatorname{ord}\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p),
$$
and, since $\mathfrak p$ is unramified, it has
$$
g(\mathfrak p)=\frac{[L:K]}{\operatorname{ord}\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p)}
$$
prime factors in $L$.

Indeed, $\theta_{L/K}(\mathfrak p)$ is the <Artin symbol>, whose restriction to the residue field is $x\mapsto x^{N\mathfrak p}$. The Galois group of the finite residue-field extension is generated by this Frobenius automorphism and has order $f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)$. The fundamental identity $efg=[L:K]$, with $e=1$, gives the formula for $g$.

The <Hilbert class field> $H_K$ is the maximal unramified abelian extension of $K$, including the condition that real places do not become complex when infinite ramification is included. The <Artin reciprocity law> induces a canonical isomorphism
$$
\operatorname{Cl}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Gal}(H_K/K),
\qquad
[\mathfrak a]\longmapsto\left(\frac{H_K/K}{\mathfrak a}\right).
$$
**Consequently $[H_K:K]=h_K$.**

= Solution

For an unramified prime $\mathfrak p$, the <ideal-theoretic decomposition law> gives
$$
\mathfrak p\text{ splits completely in }H_K
\iff
\left(\frac{H_K/K}{\mathfrak p}\right)=1.
$$
Under the <Hilbert class field> isomorphism, this Artin symbol is the image of $[\mathfrak p]\in\operatorname{Cl}(K)$. It is trivial exactly when $[\mathfrak p]=1$, which means exactly that $\mathfrak p$ is a <principal ideal>.

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6})$, whose discriminant is $-24$, and put
$$
H=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6},\sqrt2)
=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{-3}).
$$
This is a biquadratic extension of $\mathbb Q$ whose three quadratic subfields have discriminants $-24$, $8$, and $-3$. The <discriminant of a biquadratic field> is therefore
$$
d_H=(-24)\cdot8\cdot(-3)=576=d_K^2.
$$
The relative discriminant formula gives
$$
N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{H/K})
=\frac{|d_H|}{|d_K|^{[H:K]}}=1,
$$
so $H/K$ is unramified at every finite prime. Both fields are totally imaginary, so no infinite place ramifies. Since $[H:K]=2=h_K$, the extension has the full degree of the <Hilbert class field>, and hence
$$
\boxed{H_K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-6},\sqrt2).}
$$

For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, the <Dedekind zeta function> of $K$ is
$$
\zeta_K(s)=\sum_{0\ne\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathcal O_K}(N\mathfrak a)^{-s}.
$$
Unique factorization of ideals gives its <Euler product>
$$
\zeta_K(s)=\prod_{\mathfrak p}\left(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}\right)^{-1},
$$
over the nonzero prime ideals of $\mathcal O_K$.

= Solution
{parent=2}

The <analytic class number formula> states that $\zeta_K(s)$ has a simple pole at $s=1$ with
$$
\operatorname*{res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)
=\frac{2^{r_1}(2\pi)^{r_2}h_KR_K}
{w_K\sqrt{|d_K|}},
$$
where $(r_1,r_2)$ is the signature, $h_K$ the class number, $R_K$ the regulator, $w_K$ the number of roots of unity in $K$, and $d_K$ the field discriminant.

A <Dirichlet character> modulo $m$ is a group homomorphism
$$
\chi:(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb C^\times,
$$
extended periodically to $\mathbb Z$ by setting $\chi(n)=0$ when $(n,m)>1$. Its <Dirichlet L-function> is
$$
\boxed{L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}
=\prod_p(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})^{-1}
\qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>1).}
$$

= Solution

Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be quadratic with discriminant $d_K$, and let
$$
\chi_{d_K}(n)=\left(\frac{d_K}{n}\right)
$$
be its primitive quadratic <Kronecker symbol>[Dirichlet character]. For an unramified prime $p$, the Euler factor of $\zeta_K$ is
$$
\begin{cases}
(1-p^{-s})^{-2},&p\text{ splits},\\
(1-p^{-2s})^{-1},&p\text{ is inert}.
\end{cases}
$$
Since $\chi_{d_K}(p)$ is respectively $1$ or $-1$, this equals the product of the Euler factors of $\zeta(s)$ and $L(s,\chi_{d_K})$. At a ramified prime $\chi_{d_K}(p)=0$, and the same equality holds. Thus
$$
\boxed{\zeta_K(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{d_K}).}
$$

Suppose $d_K<-4$. Then $K$ has signature $(0,1)$, regulator $1$, and exactly $w_K=2$ roots of unity. The <analytic class number formula> becomes
$$
\operatorname*{res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)
=\frac{\pi h_K}{\sqrt{|d_K|}}.
$$
Because $\zeta_K(s)=\zeta(s)L(s,\chi_{d_K})$ and $\operatorname*{res}_{s=1}\zeta(s)=1$,
$$
h_K=\frac{\sqrt{|d_K|}}{\pi}|L(1,\chi_{d_K})|.
$$
Putting $m=|d_K|$ into the supplied odd-character identity yields the <quadratic class number formula>
$$
h_K
=\frac1{|2-\chi_{d_K}(2)|}
\left|
\sum_{\substack{a\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times\\a<m/2}}
\chi_{d_K}(a)
\right|.
$$

For $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-11})$, the fundamental discriminant is $-11$ and $\chi_{-11}(2)=-1$ because $-11\equiv5\pmod8$. The quadratic residues modulo $11$ are
$$
1,3,4,5,9.
$$
Therefore
$$
\sum_{1\leq a<11/2}\chi_{-11}(a)
=\chi(1)+\cdots+\chi(5)
=1-1+1+1+1=3.
$$
The <quadratic class number formula> gives
$$
\boxed{h_K=\frac{|3|}{|2-(-1)|}=1.}
$$

For a set $S$ of nonzero prime ideals of $K$, its <Dirichlet density> is
$$
\delta(S)=\lim_{s\to1^+}
\frac{\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}}
{\log(1/(s-1))},
$$
provided the limit exists.

The <Chebotarev density theorem> says that if $L/K$ is a finite Galois extension with group $G$ and $C\subseteq G$ is a conjugacy class, then the unramified primes $\mathfrak p$ whose Frobenius conjugacy class equals $C$ have <Dirichlet density>
$$
\boxed{\frac{|C|}{|G|}.}
$$

= Solution
{parent=3}

Let $\mathfrak f_{L/K}$ be the <conductor of an abelian extension> and let $\mathfrak m$ be divisible by it. For any $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, the <Chebotarev density theorem> supplies infinitely many unramified prime ideals $\mathfrak p$ with
$$
\left(\frac{L/K}{\mathfrak p}\right)=\sigma.
$$
Only finitely many primes divide $\mathfrak m_0$, so $\mathfrak p$ may be chosen prime to $\mathfrak m_0$. It then belongs to $I_K(\mathfrak m)$ and its image under the <Artin reciprocity map> is $\sigma$. Hence the Artin map is surjective.

A prime splits completely in $L$ exactly when its Frobenius conjugacy class is $\{1\}$. Applying the <Chebotarev density theorem> to the identity class gives
$$
\boxed{\delta(\mathcal S_{L/K})
=\frac1{|\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)|}
=\frac1{[L:K]}.}
$$

The <adele ring> is the restricted product
$$
\mathbb A_K=\prod_v'K_v
=\left\{(x_v)_v:x_v\in\mathcal O_v\text{ for all but finitely many finite }v\right\}.
$$
Addition and multiplication are componentwise. If $x_v,y_v\in\mathcal O_v$ outside finite sets, then $x_v+y_v,x_vy_v\in\mathcal O_v$ outside their union, so these operations are closed on $\mathbb A_K$. The componentwise ring axioms make it a ring. Its <restricted product topology> has basic open sets $\prod_vU_v$ with each $U_v\subseteq K_v$ open and $U_v=\mathcal O_v$ at all but finitely many finite places.

The <idele group> is
$$
\mathbb I_K=\prod_v'K_v^\times
=\left\{(x_v)_v:x_v\in\mathcal O_v^\times
\text{ for all but finitely many finite }v\right\},
$$
the restricted product with respect to the local unit groups.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Under
$$
\phi:\mathbb A_K^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb A_K\times\mathbb A_K,
\qquad
x\longmapsto(x,x^{-1}),
$$
the preimage of a basic product neighbourhood $U\times V$ is
$$
U\cap V^{-1}.
$$
At almost all finite places, $U_v=V_v=\mathcal O_v$, and the condition $x_v,x_v^{-1}\in\mathcal O_v$ is exactly $x_v\in\mathcal O_v^\times$. Thus these preimages form the usual restricted-product basis for $\mathbb I_K$. Conversely, every basic idele neighbourhood is obtained by choosing suitable $U_v$ and $V_v$ locally. Hence the <restricted product topology on the idele group> equals the subspace topology induced by $\phi$.

The diagonal map
$$
K^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb I_K,
\qquad
\alpha\longmapsto(\alpha)_v,
$$
is an injective homomorphism because every completion map $K\to K_v$ is injective. The diagonal copy of $K$ is discrete in the <adele ring>. Choose an adelic neighbourhood $W$ of $1$ with $W\cap K=\{1\}$. By the subspace description of the idele topology, $W\cap\mathbb I_K$ is an idele neighbourhood of $1$ meeting diagonal $K^\times$ only at $1$. Translation proves that $K^\times$ is a discrete subgroup of $\mathbb I_K$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

For each place $v$ of $K$ and each $w\mid v$ of $L$, the inclusion $K_v\hookrightarrow L_w$ defines
$$
i_{L/K}:\mathbb I_K\longrightarrow\mathbb I_L,
\qquad
(x_v)_v\longmapsto(x_v)_{w\mid v}.
$$
At all but finitely many finite $v$, the component $x_v$ is a unit, and its image is a unit at every $w\mid v$, so the image is an idele. The map is a homomorphism and is injective because every local inclusion is injective.

The <idele norm> has $v$-component
$$
\bigl(N_{L/K}x\bigr)_v
=\prod_{w\mid v}N_{L_w/K_v}(x_w).
$$
For the principal idele associated with $\alpha\in L^\times$, compatibility of local and global field norms gives
$$
N_{L/K}((\alpha)_w)
=\bigl(N_{L/K}(\alpha)\bigr)_v.
$$
The right side is the principal idele of the element $N_{L/K}(\alpha)\in K^\times$.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ and let
$$
M=L^{[G,G]}
$$
be the fixed field of the commutator subgroup. Then $M/K$ is the maximal abelian subextension of $L/K$, with
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(M/K)\cong G^{\mathrm{ab}}.
$$
The <nonabelian norm-residue kernel theorem> identifies the kernel of the global reciprocity map
$$
C_K\longrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ab}}
$$
with $N_{L/K}C_L$. Applied to the abelian extension $M/K$, the <Artin reciprocity law> identifies the kernel of the same map with $N_{M/K}C_M$. Therefore
$$
N_{L/K}C_L=N_{M/K}C_M,
$$
so the norm group of the Galois extension $L/K$ is the norm group of the abelian extension $M/K$.
