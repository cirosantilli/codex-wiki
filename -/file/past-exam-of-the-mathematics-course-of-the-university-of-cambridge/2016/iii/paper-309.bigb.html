= Solution
{parent=i}

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$, so the future-directed <unit normal> obeys $n^\mu n_\mu=-1$. The <spatial projection tensor> and the <induced metric> are
$$
P^\mu{}_\alpha=\delta^\mu{}_\alpha+n^\mu n_\alpha,
\qquad \gamma_{\alpha\beta}=g_{\alpha\beta}+n_\alpha n_\beta.
$$
Indeed $P^\mu{}_\alpha n^\alpha=0$ and $P^\mu{}_\rho P^\rho{}_\alpha=P^\mu{}_\alpha$: this is an <idempotent linear map> onto the <tangent space> of the spatial <hypersurface>. For a covariant <tensor> both slots must be projected:
$$
\boxed{(\perp T)_{\alpha\beta}=P^\mu{}_\alpha P^\nu{}_\beta T_{\mu\nu}.}
$$
Contracting either slot with $n$ supplies the vanishing factor $P^\mu{}_\alpha n^\alpha$. Thus **both normal contractions vanish**, without requiring symmetry of $T$.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Take the orientation induced by the future-directed <unit normal> and positive <lapse function> $\alpha$. In coordinates adapted to the <3+1 decomposition of spacetime>, any component $\epsilon_{\ell ijk}$ with all four indices spatial vanishes: only three distinct spatial indices are available. The <shift vector> therefore contributes nothing, and
$$
\epsilon_{\mu ijk}n^\mu=\alpha^{-1}\epsilon_{0ijk}
=\alpha^{-1}\sqrt{|g|}\,[ijk]
=\sqrt\gamma\,[ijk].
$$
Here $[ijk]$ is the <Levi-Civita symbol>, and the last equality uses the <metric determinant in a 3+1 decomposition>, $|g|=\alpha^2\gamma$. The right side is precisely the spatial <metric volume tensor>, with component $\sqrt\gamma$ at $ijk=123$, the appropriate permutation sign, and zero for repeated indices. Hence
$$
\boxed{\tilde\epsilon_{ijk}=n^\mu\epsilon_{\mu ijk}.}
$$
More invariantly, the spatial <volume form> is the restriction to the <hypersurface> of the contraction of the spacetime <volume form> with $n$.

= Solution
{parent=1}

For the <vacuum Einstein equations> with zero <cosmological constant>, $R_{\alpha\beta}=0$, and its <tensor contraction> gives $R=0$. Every <Ricci tensor> and <scalar curvature> term in the <Weyl tensor> consequently disappears:
$$
\boxed{C_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}.}
$$
Thus **the <Weyl tensor> contains all vacuum curvature**. Here vacuum means Ricci-flat; a nonzero <cosmological constant> would retain a constant-curvature contribution to the <Riemann curvature tensor>.

= Solution
{parent=2}

The <electric part of the Weyl tensor> has one normal in each antisymmetric pair of the <Weyl tensor>. Therefore
$$
n^\alpha E_{\alpha\beta}=n^\alpha n^\rho C_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}n^\sigma=0,
\qquad
n^\beta E_{\alpha\beta}=n^\beta n^\sigma C_{\alpha\rho\beta\sigma}n^\rho=0.
$$
For the <magnetic part of the Weyl tensor>, contracting the first slot gives $n^\alpha n^\lambda\epsilon_{\alpha\lambda\mu\nu}=0$ by antisymmetry of the <metric volume tensor>; contracting the second gives $n^\beta n^\rho C^{\mu\nu}{}_{\beta\rho}=0$ by antisymmetry of the last curvature pair. Consequently **both tensors are entirely spatial**:
$$
\boxed{E_{\alpha\beta}=(\perp E)_{\alpha\beta},\qquad B_{\alpha\beta}=(\perp B)_{\alpha\beta}.}
$$
These <spatial projection tensor> identities actually hold for any <Weyl tensor>; the vacuum assumption is needed subsequently to replace it by the <Riemann curvature tensor>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

All free indices below are spatial, and $K=\gamma^{\mu\nu}K_{\mu\nu}$. Contract the vacuum <Ricci tensor> using $g^{\mu\nu}=\gamma^{\mu\nu}-n^\mu n^\nu$:
$$
0=(\perp R_{\alpha\beta})
=\gamma^{\mu\nu}(\perp R)_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}
-n^\mu n^\nu R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}.
$$
Reversing both antisymmetric curvature pairs identifies the last term as $-E_{\alpha\beta}$. The spatial <Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface> then give
$$
E_{\alpha\beta}
=\gamma^{\mu\nu}\bigl(\mathcal R_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}
+K_{\mu\nu}K_{\beta\alpha}-K_{\mu\beta}K_{\nu\alpha}\bigr)
=\mathcal R_{\alpha\beta}+KK_{\alpha\beta}-K^\nu{}_\beta K_{\nu\alpha}.
$$
For the <magnetic part of the Weyl tensor>, moving the normal to the first slot of the <metric volume tensor> introduces a minus sign:
$$
\epsilon_{\alpha\lambda\mu\nu}n^\lambda=-\tilde\epsilon_{\alpha\mu\nu}.
$$
The remaining curvature indices are spatial, so the normal projection in the <Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
B_{\alpha\beta}
&=-\frac12\tilde\epsilon_\alpha{}^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu\beta\rho}n^\rho\\
&=-\frac12\tilde\epsilon_\alpha{}^{\mu\nu}(-D_\mu K_{\nu\beta}+D_\nu K_{\mu\beta})\\
&=\tilde\epsilon_\alpha{}^{\mu\nu}D_\mu K_{\nu\beta}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The interchange $\mu\leftrightarrow\nu$ makes the two <spatial covariant derivative> terms equal. Thus **the constants, with the paper's orientation and extrinsic-curvature convention, are**
$$
\boxed{(k_1,k_2,k_3,k_4)=(1,1,-1,1).}
$$
In particular, the sign of the magnetic expression must include the minus from moving $n$ past the first volume-tensor index. These formulas reconstruct the spatial <electric part of the Weyl tensor> and <magnetic part of the Weyl tensor> from the <induced metric> and the <extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface>.

Let $\lambda$ be an <affine parameter> and $X=dx^\mu/d\lambda\,\partial_\mu$ the tangent to the <geodesic>. Since $\nabla_{\partial_\mu}\partial_\nu=\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\partial_\rho$, the product rule for the <covariant derivative> gives
$$
\nabla_XX=\left(\frac{d^2x^\rho}{d\lambda^2}
+\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}\right)\partial_\rho.
$$
Independence of the coordinate basis vectors yields the **coordinate geodesic equation**
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2x^\rho}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^\rho{}_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda}=0.}
$$
The <affine parameter> condition matters: an arbitrary reparametrization instead allows a multiple of the tangent on the right, as in the <unparametrized geodesic equation>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Set $f=1-2M(v)/r$, so $f_r=2M/r^2$ and $f_v=-2M'/r$, where $M'=dM/dv$. Dots in this solution mean derivatives with respect to <proper time> $\tau$. The <geodesic Lagrangian> becomes
$$
L=f\dot v^2-2\dot v\dot r-r^2\dot\theta^2-r^2\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2.
$$
The <Euler-Lagrange equation> for $r$ gives $\ddot v+(f_r/2)\dot v^2-r(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2)=0$. The <Euler-Lagrange equation> for $v$ gives
$$
2f\ddot v+f_v\dot v^2+2f_r\dot r\dot v-2\ddot r=0.
$$
Eliminate $\ddot v$ between these equations. The two angular <Euler-Lagrange equations> follow by differentiating $-2r^2\dot\theta$ and $-2r^2\sin^2\theta\dot\phi$. Together the four <geodesic equations> are
$$
\begin{aligned}
0&=\ddot v+\frac{M}{r^2}\dot v^2-r\dot\theta^2-r\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2,\\
0&=\ddot r+\left(\frac{fM}{r^2}+\frac{M'}r\right)\dot v^2-\frac{2M}{r^2}\dot v\dot r-fr\dot\theta^2-fr\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2,\\
0&=\ddot\theta+\frac2r\dot r\dot\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta\dot\phi^2,\\
0&=\ddot\phi+\frac2r\dot r\dot\phi+2\cot\theta\dot\theta\dot\phi.
\end{aligned}
$$
Reading off the symmetric quadratic coefficients gives **all nonzero <Christoffel symbols of the Vaidya metric>**:
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma^v{}_{vv}&=\frac Mr^2,&
\Gamma^v{}_{\theta\theta}&=-r,&
\Gamma^v{}_{\phi\phi}&=-r\sin^2\theta,\\
\Gamma^r{}_{vv}&=\frac{fM}{r^2}+\frac{M'}r,&
\Gamma^r{}_{vr}&=\Gamma^r{}_{rv}=-\frac Mr^2,&
\Gamma^r{}_{\theta\theta}&=-fr,\\
\Gamma^r{}_{\phi\phi}&=-fr\sin^2\theta,&
\Gamma^\theta{}_{r\theta}&=\Gamma^\theta{}_{\theta r}=\frac1r,&
\Gamma^\theta{}_{\phi\phi}&=-\sin\theta\cos\theta,\\
\Gamma^\phi{}_{r\phi}&=\Gamma^\phi{}_{\phi r}=\frac1r,&
\Gamma^\phi{}_{\theta\phi}&=\Gamma^\phi{}_{\phi\theta}=\cot\theta.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Every unlisted <Christoffel symbol> vanishes. The lower-index symmetry is that of the <Levi-Civita connection>. In particular, the time-dependent mass contributes $+M'/r$ to $\Gamma^r{}_{vv}$; it is absent from $\Gamma^v{}_{vv}$. There are eleven independent nonzero entries, or fifteen when both lower-index orders are counted.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

For radial motion, take an <affine parameter> $\lambda$ and use dots for $d/d\lambda$. The <radial null geodesics of the Vaidya metric> satisfy the two radial <geodesic equations>
$$
\ddot v+\frac Mr^2\dot v^2=0,
\qquad
\ddot r+\left(\frac{fM}{r^2}+\frac{M'}r\right)\dot v^2-\frac{2M}{r^2}\dot v\dot r=0.
$$
The radial squared norm is $-f\dot v^2+2\dot v\dot r$. If $v$ is constant it vanishes, and the <geodesic equation> reduces to $\ddot r=0$. Thus **constant-$v$ radial curves are null geodesics**, with $r=a\lambda+b$ an affine parametrization. Future ingoing motion has $a<0$.

For the other family let $dr/dv=f/2$. Its tangent $k=\partial_v+(f/2)\partial_r$ is a <null vector>. Using the <Christoffel symbols of the Vaidya metric>, direct differentiation gives
$$
\nabla_kk=\frac{f_r}{2}k=\frac Mr^2k.
$$
Hence **these are also null geodesics, but $v$ is generally not affine**. For completeness, put $p=dv/d\lambda$ and choose
$$
\frac{d\log p}{dv}=-\frac{M(v)}{r(v)^2},
\qquad \frac{d\lambda}{dv}=C\exp\left(\int^v\frac{M(u)}{r(u)^2}\,du\right),\quad C\ne0.
$$
This makes $\ddot v=-(M/r^2)\dot v^2$. Differentiating $\dot r=(f/2)\dot v$ then gives $\ddot r=(f_v/2)\dot v^2=-M'\dot v^2/r$; substitution verifies the radial $r$ equation as well. Thus the <null condition> and both radial <geodesic equations> hold, not just the null condition alone.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

Rearranging the outgoing <radial null geodesics of the Vaidya metric> equation along $r=r_0(v)$ gives
$$
\boxed{M(v)=\frac{r_0(v)}2\left(1-2\frac{dr_0}{dv}\right).}
$$
For the prescribed parametrization by $w$, the <chain rule> yields
$$
\frac{dv}{dw}=1+2\beta n w^{n-1}>0,
\qquad
\frac{dr_0}{dv}=\frac{\beta n w^{n-1}}{1+2\beta n w^{n-1}},
\qquad
M(v(w))=\frac{\beta w^n}{2(1+2\beta n w^{n-1})}.
$$
Since $n>1$, $v/w=1+2\beta w^{n-1}\to1$ as $w\to0^+$. The <asymptotic equivalence> $w\sim v$ therefore implies
$$
\boxed{M(v)\sim\frac\beta2 v^n,\qquad c_1=\frac\beta2.}
$$
More precisely $w=v+O(v^n)$ and $M(v)=(\beta/2)v^n[1+O(v^{n-1})]$, using <Big O notation>. The positivity of $dv/dw$ justifies treating $w$ as a single-valued function of $v$ locally.

= Solution

Write $V=T_p\mathcal M$ and $V^*$ for its <dual space>. A type-$(1,1)$ <tensor> is equivalently a <linear map> $A:V\to V$: the corresponding scalar-valued <multilinear map> is $(X,\eta)\mapsto\eta(A(X))$. Thus the composition defines
$$
C(X,\eta)=\eta\bigl(B(A(X))\bigr).
$$
It is linear in $X$ because $A$ and $B$ are <linear maps>, and linear in the <covector> $\eta$ by evaluation. This proves directly that **the composition is a tensor of type $(1,1)$**.

Choose a basis $e_\alpha$ of the <tangent space> and its dual basis $e^\beta$. With the paper's component order $A(e_\alpha)=A_\alpha{}^\gamma e_\gamma$,
$$
B(A(e_\alpha))=A_\alpha{}^\gamma B_\gamma{}^\beta e_\beta,
\qquad
\boxed{C_\alpha{}^\beta=A_\alpha{}^\gamma B_\gamma{}^\beta.}
$$
Equivalently, in the usual output-first matrix notation, $C^\beta{}_\alpha=B^\beta{}_\gamma A^\gamma{}_\alpha$: **the matrix product is $BA$, not $AB$**. This <composition of mixed tensors> contracts one output index with one input index. Under the <tensor component transformation law>, the two Jacobian factors at that contracted index cancel, leaving exactly one factor for each free index.

= Solution

The <Lie derivative of a function> is differentiation along the flow, while the <Lie derivative of a vector field> compares a vector with its pullback by that flow. Their index-free expressions are
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L_X f=X(f)=df(X),\qquad \mathcal L_XY=[X,Y].}
$$
Here $X(f)$ is the <directional derivative> of the <smooth function>, and $[X,Y]$ is the <Lie bracket of vector fields>, characterized by $[X,Y](f)=X(Y(f))-Y(X(f))$. To see the second expression directly, if $\phi_t$ is the <local flow> of $X$, differentiating $(d\phi_t)^{-1}Y\circ\phi_t$ at $t=0$ gives $dY(X)-dX(Y)=[X,Y]$. This uses the <flow definition of the Lie derivative of a tensor field>.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

On <smooth functions>, the <Lie derivative of a function> satisfies
$$
[\mathcal L_X,\mathcal L_Y]f=X(Y(f))-Y(X(f))=[X,Y](f)=\mathcal L_{[X,Y]}f.
$$
On an arbitrary <vector field> $W$, the <Lie derivative of a vector field> gives
$$
[\mathcal L_X,\mathcal L_Y]W=[X,[Y,W]]-[Y,[X,W]]=[[X,Y],W]=\mathcal L_{[X,Y]}W.
$$
The middle equality follows from the <Jacobi identity> for the <Lie bracket of vector fields>. One can verify that identity without assuming the result being proved: view <vector fields> as derivations acting on <smooth functions>, expand their <commutators>, and cancel the six compositions. Equality as derivations implies equality as <vector fields>.

For the second identity, let $A,B,C$ be any operators on either <smooth functions> or <vector fields>. Associativity of composition gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
[[A,B],C]&=ABC-BAC-CAB+CBA,\\
[[B,C],A]&=BCA-CBA-ABC+ACB,\\
[[C,A],B]&=CAB-ACB-BCA+BAC.
\end{aligned}
$$
Each term cancels in the sum. Applying this to $A=\mathcal L_X$, $B=\mathcal L_Y$, $C=\mathcal L_Z$ proves **both requested identities on both kinds of arguments**:
$$
\boxed{[\mathcal L_X,\mathcal L_Y]=\mathcal L_{[X,Y]},\qquad
[[\mathcal L_X,\mathcal L_Y],\mathcal L_Z]+[[\mathcal L_Y,\mathcal L_Z],\mathcal L_X]+[[\mathcal L_Z,\mathcal L_X],\mathcal L_Y]=0.}
$$
The <commutator identity for Lie derivatives> expresses that the <Lie derivative of a tensor field> represents the <Lie bracket of vector fields>.

= Solution

Use $t$ as an <affine parameter> on each member of the <geodesic variation>, so $\nabla_TT=0$. The parameter directions commute, giving $[T,S]=0$ along the variation. The <torsion-free connection> then gives
$$
\nabla_TS-\nabla_ST=[T,S]=0.
$$
Consequently the <covariant derivative> and curvature definition give
$$
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_T\nabla_TS
&=\nabla_T\nabla_ST\\
&=R(T,S)T+\nabla_S\nabla_TT+\nabla_{[T,S]}T\\
&=R(T,S)T.
\end{aligned}
$$
This is **the geodesic-deviation equation with the stated curvature sign**:
$$
\boxed{\nabla_T\nabla_TS=R(T,S)T=R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}T^\beta T^\gamma S^\delta e_\alpha.}
$$
The component expression defines the slot order: $\beta$ is the vector acted on, and $\gamma,\delta$ are the two derivative directions of the <Riemann curvature tensor>. Both the commuting parameter directions and affine parametrization are essential; a general connecting <vector field> or a nonaffine parameter would add terms.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Let $\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu V_\nu+\partial_\nu V_\mu$. Substitution into the <linearized Levi-Civita connection> cancels four terms and leaves
$$
\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\nu\rho}=\partial_\nu\partial_\rho V^\mu.
$$
To first order in the <metric perturbation>, the products of <Christoffel symbols> are quadratic and may be discarded. Therefore the change in the <Riemann curvature tensor> is
$$
\delta R^\mu{}_{\nu\rho\sigma}
=\partial_\rho\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\sigma\nu}-\partial_\sigma\delta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\rho\nu}
=\partial_\rho\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu V^\mu-\partial_\sigma\partial_\rho\partial_\nu V^\mu=0.
$$
This is the <gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor>. Lowering the first index with the <Minkowski metric> does not change the conclusion; the change from using the full <metric tensor> instead is of second order because the background curvature is zero. Hence
$$
\boxed{\delta R^{(1)}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=0.}
$$
The result is **invariance at linear order about flat spacetime**, not componentwise invariance of arbitrary nonlinear curvature under finite coordinate changes. Directly in the four-term formula, the eight third derivatives of $V$ cancel in pairs by commuting partial derivatives.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Put $A,B\in\{1,2\}$ and write $h_{AB}=H_{AB}e^{ik\cdot x}$, where $H_{11}=H_+$, $H_{22}=-H_+$ and $H_{12}=H_{21}=H_\times$. Physical fields are the real parts. Use $c=1$ here, and take the initial separation at equal time, so $L^0=0$. For propagation in the positive $x^3$ direction one may set $k_\mu=(-\omega,0,0,\omega)$; only $k_0^2=\omega^2$ enters the answer.

The <linearized Levi-Civita connection> in this <transverse-traceless gauge> has the potentially nonzero components
$$
\Gamma^0{}_{AB}=\frac12\partial_0h_{AB},\qquad
\Gamma^3{}_{AB}=-\frac12\partial_3h_{AB},\qquad
\Gamma^A{}_{0B}=\Gamma^A{}_{B0}=\frac12\partial_0h_{AB},\qquad
\Gamma^A{}_{3B}=\Gamma^A{}_{B3}=\frac12\partial_3h_{AB},
$$
with all others zero to this order. In particular $\Gamma^\mu{}_{00}=0$, so a particle initially at rest has fixed spatial coordinates in <transverse-traceless gauge>, and $t$ equals its <proper time>. Expanding the <Riemann curvature tensor> gives
$$
R^{(1)}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac12\bigl(\partial_\rho\partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma}+\partial_\sigma\partial_\mu h_{\nu\rho}-\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h_{\nu\sigma}-\partial_\sigma\partial_\nu h_{\mu\rho}\bigr),
\qquad R_{0A0B}^{(1)}=-\frac12\ddot h_{AB},
\qquad R^A{}_{00B}^{(1)}=\frac12\ddot h_{AB}.
$$
The plus sign in the last expression agrees with $R(T,S)T$ in the <geodesic deviation> convention above.

For the separation measured in a <parallel-propagated orthonormal frame>, write $S^{\hat\mu}=L^{\hat\mu}+\xi^{\hat\mu}$. The frame removes the connection terms from the time derivative, so the <geodesic deviation> equation reduces to $\ddot\xi^{\hat A}=R^{\hat A}{}_{\hat0\hat0\hat B}L^{\hat B}=\ddot h_{AB}L^{\hat B}/2$ at first order. **The observable transverse relative acceleration is**
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\ddot\xi^{\hat0}&=0,\qquad \ddot\xi^{\hat3}=0,\\
\ddot\xi^{\hat1}&=-\frac{k_0^2}{2}(H_+L^{\hat1}+H_\times L^{\hat2})e^{ik\cdot x},\\
\ddot\xi^{\hat2}&=-\frac{k_0^2}{2}(H_\times L^{\hat1}-H_+L^{\hat2})e^{ik\cdot x}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
These are the <plus polarization> and <cross polarization> tidal patterns; there is no longitudinal acceleration. The curvature is evaluated along the reference worldline, and replacing $S$ by $L$ on the right changes only second-order terms. This is the infinitesimal-separation approximation intrinsic to <geodesic deviation>.

There is an important component distinction if the symbols $L^\mu+\xi^\mu$ are instead interpreted literally in the <transverse-traceless gauge> coordinate basis. Expanding the left side, rather than replacing a <covariant derivative> by an ordinary derivative, gives
$$
(\nabla_T\nabla_TS)^\mu=\ddot\xi_{\rm coord}^{\mu}+\partial_0\Gamma^\mu{}_{0j}L^j+O(\epsilon^2),
\qquad
R^\mu{}_{00j}L^j=\partial_0\Gamma^\mu{}_{0j}L^j+O(\epsilon^2).
$$
Thus **in TT coordinates**
$$
\boxed{\ddot\xi_{\rm coord}^{\mu}=0.}
$$
With zero initial coordinate displacement and velocity, $\xi_{\rm coord}=0$ throughout. This does not remove the physical <gravitational wave> strain. To first order an <orthonormal coframe in spacetime> along the reference worldline is $\vartheta^{\hat A}=(\delta^A{}_B+h_{AB}/2)dx^B$; its dual frame is parallel propagated because $\partial_0(\delta^A{}_B-h_{AB}/2)+\Gamma^A{}_{0B}=0$. Hence
$$
S^{\hat A}=S^A+\frac12h_{AB}L^B,
\qquad \xi^{\hat A}=\xi_{\rm coord}^A+\frac12h_{AB}L^B,
$$
after choosing the initial flat-frame reference. The <TT coordinate separation and measured separation> distinction accounts exactly for the apparently different accelerations. In a region initially free of the wave, the measured displacement is $\xi^{\hat A}=h_{AB}L^{\hat B}/2$ with the corresponding initial conditions.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

Take the oscillation to be $z(t)=L\cos\omega t$; a different initial phase does not affect a <period average>. In the mass-density normalization of the <quadrupole formula>, the <second mass moment tensor> of the <point mass> has only $I_{33}=mz^2$ nonzero. Its trace-free <mass quadrupole moment> is therefore
$$
Q_{ij}=mL^2\cos^2\omega t\;\operatorname{diag}\left(-\frac13,-\frac13,\frac23\right)_{ij}.
$$
The constant part does not radiate. Since $d^3(\cos^2\omega t)/dt^3=4\omega^3\sin2\omega t$, the <tensor contraction> is
$$
\dddot Q_{ij}\dddot Q_{ij}
=16m^2L^4\omega^6\sin^2(2\omega t)\left(\frac19+\frac19+\frac49\right)
=\frac{32}{3}m^2L^4\omega^6\sin^2(2\omega t).
$$
Using $\langle\sin^2(2\omega t)\rangle=1/2$ in the <quadrupole formula> gives **the averaged positive radiated power**
$$
\boxed{\left\langle\frac{dE_{\rm rad}}{dt}\right\rangle=\frac{16Gm^2L^4\omega^6}{15c^5}.}
$$
The <mass quadrupole moment> oscillates at twice the source's <angular frequency>, and the source energy loss has the opposite sign. The expression uses the leading slow-motion <weak-field approximation>, $\omega L\ll c$. With SI <stress-energy tensor> components, the mass-density integrand is $T_{00}/c^2$; the displayed $c^{-5}$ <quadrupole formula> uses that mass normalization. This is the point-mass contribution requested by the model: an apparatus maintaining an accelerating mass would also contribute its own <mass quadrupole moment>.
