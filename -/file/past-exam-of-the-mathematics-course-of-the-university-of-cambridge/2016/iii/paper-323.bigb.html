= Solution
{parent=i}

The useful object is the <Choi state> of the <entanglement-breaking channel>. Put $d_A=\dim\mathcal H_A$, introduce a reference $R$ with the same <computational basis> as $A$, and use the normalized <maximally entangled state>
$$
|\Phi\rangle_{RA}=d_A^{-1/2}\sum_j|j\rangle_R|j\rangle_A,\qquad
C_{RB}=(\operatorname{id}_R\otimes\mathcal E)(|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|).
$$
The <entanglement-breaking channel> makes $C_{RB}$ a <separable quantum state>. Its <partial trace> is $\operatorname{Tr}_B C_{RB}=I_R/d_A$, because $\mathcal E$ is a <quantum channel>. Write a <separable positive operator> decomposition
$$
C_{RB}=\sum_y\alpha_y\otimes\beta_y,\qquad \alpha_y\geq0,\quad\beta_y\geq0.
$$
Discard any term with $\operatorname{Tr}\beta_y=0$: a <positive operator> with zero <trace> is zero. Define
$$
\tau_y=\frac{\beta_y}{\operatorname{Tr}\beta_y},\qquad
E_y=d_A(\operatorname{Tr}\beta_y)\alpha_y^T.
$$
Each $\tau_y$ is a <density operator>. Each $E_y$ is a <positive operator>, and taking the <matrix transpose> of the <partial trace> identity gives
$$
\sum_y E_y=d_A\left(\sum_y(\operatorname{Tr}\beta_y)\alpha_y\right)^T=I_A.
$$
Thus the $E_y$ form a <POVM>. In this reference-first convention, the <Choi reconstruction formula> is
$$
\mathcal E(L)=d_A\operatorname{Tr}_R[(L^T\otimes I_B)C_{RB}]
=\sum_y\tau_y\operatorname{Tr}(E_yL).
$$
The factor $d_A$ is essential because we used a normalized <Choi state>. A <measurement channel> storing the outcome $y$, followed by the prescribed <quantum state preparation> of $\tau_y$, has precisely this action on every <linear operator> $L$. **The desired factorization is therefore**
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E^{B\leftarrow A}=\mathcal P^{B\leftarrow\widetilde Y}\mathcal M^{\widetilde Y\leftarrow A}.}
$$

= Solution

Write $X$ and $Y$ for the classical <quantum registers> $\widetilde X$ and $\widetilde Y$. By the <quantum mutual information> formula $I(U:V)=S(U)+S(V)-S(UV)$, expanding the right-hand side of the <quantum mutual information balance identity> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
&I(X:B)+I(XB:D)-I(B:D)\\
&=S(X)+S(B)-S(XB)+S(XB)+S(D)-S(XBD)\\
&\qquad-S(B)-S(D)+S(BD)\\
&=S(X)+S(BD)-S(XBD)=I(X:BD).
\end{aligned}
$$
All <Von Neumann entropies> here are evaluated in $\omega$.

We use three facts: <quantum mutual information> is nonnegative by <nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy>; a local <quantum channel> cannot increase it by <data processing for quantum mutual information>; and the <quantum mutual information> of a <classical-quantum state> is its ensemble's <Holevo quantity>. Write $\chi(\mathcal T)$ for the <Holevo capacity>, the supremum of the output <Holevo quantity> over finite input ensembles.

The $XB$ marginal is an output ensemble for $\mathcal E$, with inputs $\rho(x)_A=\operatorname{Tr}_C\rho(x)_{AC}$. Consequently $I(X:B)_\omega\leq\chi(\mathcal E)$. Also $\omega_{XBD}$ is obtained from $\sigma_{XYD}$ by a <quantum channel> on $XY$ which retains $X$ and prepares $B$ from $Y$. Hence
$$
I(XB:D)_\omega\leq I(XY:D)_\sigma.
$$
To bound the latter even for <entangled states> $\rho(x)_{AC}$, exhibit the <conditional input ensemble after a local measurement>. Set
$$
q(y|x)=\operatorname{Tr}[(E_y\otimes I_C)\rho(x)_{AC}],\qquad
\rho_C^{x,y}=\frac{\operatorname{Tr}_A[(\sqrt{E_y}\otimes I_C)\rho(x)_{AC}(\sqrt{E_y}\otimes I_C)]}{q(y|x)}
$$
when $q(y|x)>0$; zero-weight outcomes can be omitted. The numerator is a <positive operator>, its <trace> is $q(y|x)$, and the $q(y|x)$ sum to one. Thus
$$
\sigma_{XYD}=\sum_{x,y}P_X(x)q(y|x)|x,y\rangle\langle x,y|\otimes\mathcal N(\rho_C^{x,y}).
$$
This is a <classical-quantum state> with an output ensemble for $\mathcal N$, so $I(XY:D)_\sigma\leq\chi(\mathcal N)$. Combining these bounds with $I(B:D)_\omega\geq0$ yields
$$
\boxed{I(X:BD)_\omega\leq\chi(\mathcal E)+\chi(\mathcal N),\qquad
\chi(\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal N)\leq\chi(\mathcal E)+\chi(\mathcal N).}
$$
The last step takes the supremum over all input ensembles on $AC$. **The left-hand channel direction is $B\leftarrow A$**, as established in part (i); the printed $A\leftarrow B$ in the last inequality is a typographical reversal. Independent product ensembles also give the reverse inequality, so this proves <Holevo-capacity additivity for entanglement-breaking channels>.

Use an <orthonormal eigenbasis> of the <Hermitian operator> $A$ and set $b_j=\langle\alpha_j|B|\alpha_j\rangle$. Since $B$ is a <positive contraction>,
$$
0\leq b_j\leq1,\qquad \sum_jb_j=r,\qquad \operatorname{Tr}(AB)=\sum_j\lambda_jb_j.
$$
For $0<r<d$, the missing weight among the first $r$ entries equals the weight in the remaining entries:
$$
t:=\sum_{j<r}(1-b_j)=\sum_{j\geq r}b_j.
$$
The ordered <eigenvalues> satisfy $\lambda_j\geq\lambda_{r-1}$ for $j<r$ and $\lambda_j\leq\lambda_{r-1}$ for $j\geq r$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{j<r}\lambda_j-\operatorname{Tr}(AB)
=\sum_{j<r}\lambda_j(1-b_j)-\sum_{j\geq r}\lambda_jb_j
\geq\lambda_{r-1}t-\lambda_{r-1}t=0.
$$
This argument does not require the <eigenvalues> of $A$ to be positive. For $r=0$, positivity and zero <trace> give $B=0$; for $r=d$, the same argument applied to $I-B$ gives $B=I$. Thus **in every case**
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Tr}(AB)\leq\sum_{j<r}\lambda_j.}
$$
The <orthogonal projection> onto the leading $r$ <eigenvectors> attains equality. This is the <Hermitian effect variational principle>, extending the <Ky Fan maximum principle> to all <positive contractions> with the prescribed <trace>.

Choose <Kraus representations>
$$
\mathcal C(L)=\sum_a C_aLC_a^\dagger,\qquad
\mathcal D(M)=\sum_b D_bMD_b^\dagger,\qquad
\sum_aC_a^\dagger C_a=I_Q,\quad\sum_bD_b^\dagger D_b=I_K.
$$
The composite <quantum channel> has <Kraus operators> $T_{ba}=D_bC_a$. Each has <matrix rank> at most $k$, because it factors through the $k$-dimensional space $K$, and
$$
\sum_{a,b}T_{ba}^\dagger T_{ba}=I_Q.
$$
The <operation fidelity> uses the unsquared <quantum fidelity> on a <purification of a density operator>, so its square is <entanglement fidelity>. The <Kraus formula for entanglement fidelity> gives
$$
F_{\rm op}(\mathcal D\mathcal C,\rho_Q)^2
=\sum_{a,b}|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_Q T_{ba})|^2.
$$
Indeed each overlap $\langle\Psi_\rho|(I_R\otimes T_{ba})|\Psi_\rho\rangle$ equals $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_QT_{ba})$.

For any one of these <Kraus operators> $T$, let $\Pi_T$ be the <orthogonal projection> onto its image, of <matrix rank> $r_T\leq k$. Since $\Pi_TT=T$, the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> for the <Hilbert-Schmidt inner product> gives the <rank bound for a weighted operator trace>:
$$
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_QT)|^2
&=|\operatorname{Tr}[(\Pi_T\sqrt{\rho_Q})^\dagger(T\sqrt{\rho_Q})]|^2\\
&\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_Q\Pi_T)\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_QT^\dagger T)\\
&\leq\left(\sum_{j<k}\lambda_j\right)\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_QT^\dagger T).
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step uses part (i) at $r_T$, followed by nonnegativity of the <eigenvalues> of the <density operator> $\rho_Q$. Summing over the <Kraus operators> and using their completeness relation proves **the <finite-dimensional quantum compression converse>**:
$$
\boxed{1-\epsilon\leq F_{\rm op}(\mathcal D\mathcal C,\rho_Q)^2\leq\sum_{j<k}\lambda_j.}
$$
No invertibility of $\rho_Q$, or restriction to an isometric decoder, was used.

The <absolute value of an operator>, <trace norm>, and <operator norm> are respectively
$$
|L|=\sqrt{L^\dagger L},\qquad
\|L\|_1=\operatorname{Tr}|L|,\qquad
\|L\|_{\rm op}=\sup_{\|v\|=1}\|Lv\|.
$$
The <positive square root of an operator> makes $|L|$ well defined. Its <eigenvalues> $s_j\geq0$ are the <singular values> of $L$, so $\|L\|_1=\sum_js_j$ and $\|L\|_{\rm op}=\max_js_j$.

Use the <polar decomposition of a bounded operator> $L=U|L|$, with the given <unitary operator> $U$. In an <orthonormal eigenbasis> $e_j$ for $|L|$,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}(ZL)=\sum_js_j\langle e_j|ZU|e_j\rangle.
$$
The <operator norm> assumption and unitarity imply $|\langle e_j|ZU|e_j\rangle|\leq\|Z\|_{\rm op}\leq1$. The <triangle inequality> therefore yields **the required trace estimate**:
$$
\boxed{|\operatorname{Tr}(ZL)|\leq\sum_js_j=\|L\|_1.}
$$
Choosing $Z=U^\dagger$ attains equality, which also gives the finite-dimensional <trace duality> formula $\|L\|_1=\max_{\|Z\|_{\rm op}\leq1}|\operatorname{Tr}(ZL)|$. A singular $L$ causes no difficulty: in a finite-dimensional square space the polar factor can be extended to a <unitary operator> on the complementary subspaces.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Let $\Gamma=T_B$ denote the <partial transpose>. The <partial transpose of a maximally entangled projector> is
$$
(\phi^+)^\Gamma=\frac1d\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes|j\rangle\langle i|=\frac Sd,
$$
where $S$ is the <swap operator>, satisfying $S^\dagger S=I$ and $\|S\|_{\rm op}=1$. If $\sigma$ has <positive partial transpose>, then $\sigma^\Gamma$ is a <positive operator> of <trace> one, hence $\|\sigma^\Gamma\|_1=1$.

For a <pure state> $\phi^+=|\Phi\rangle\langle\Phi|$, the unsquared <quantum fidelity> obeys $F(\phi^+,\sigma)^2=\langle\Phi|\sigma|\Phi\rangle$. Using the <trace-adjoint identity for partial transpose> and part (i),
$$
F(\phi^+,\sigma)^2
=\operatorname{Tr}(\phi^+\sigma)
=\frac1d\operatorname{Tr}(S\sigma^\Gamma)
\leq\frac1d\|\sigma^\Gamma\|_1=\frac1d.
$$
The overlap is nonnegative, so taking its square root proves **the <PPT maximally entangled overlap bound>**:
$$
\boxed{F(\phi^+,\sigma)\leq\frac1{\sqrt d}.}
$$
The <product state> $|00\rangle\langle00|$ attains the bound, so it is sharp.

In the <computational basis> ordered as $00,01,10,11$, the <Hermitian matrix> is
$$
\rho_{AB}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&\alpha\\0&0&0&0\\0&0&0&0\\\alpha&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Its <trace> is one, and its <eigenvalues> are $(1+\alpha)/2$, $(1-\alpha)/2$, $0$, and $0$. Thus the remaining <density operator> condition, positivity, holds exactly when **both nonzero candidates are nonnegative**:
$$
\boxed{-1\leq\alpha\leq1.}
$$
This is the admissible parameter interval for the <dephased Bell-state mixture>.

A <density operator> is a <pure state> exactly when its <matrix rank> is one. Within the admissible interval from part (a), one of $(1+\alpha)/2$ and $(1-\alpha)/2$ must vanish. Therefore **the pure-state parameters and vectors are**
$$
\boxed{\alpha=1:\quad\rho_{AB}=|\Phi^+\rangle\langle\Phi^+|,\qquad
\alpha=-1:\quad\rho_{AB}=|\Phi^-\rangle\langle\Phi^-|,}
$$
where the <Bell states> are $|\Phi^\pm\rangle=(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Equivalently, the <purity of a density operator> is $\operatorname{Tr}\rho_{AB}^2=(1+\alpha^2)/2$, reaching one exactly at these two endpoints.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Every <separable quantum state> has <positive partial transpose>. Here the <partial transpose> is
$$
\rho_{AB}^{T_B}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\0&0&\alpha&0\\0&\alpha&0&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$
with <eigenvalues> $1/2,1/2,\alpha/2,-\alpha/2$. Any nonzero $\alpha$ therefore gives a negative <eigenvalue>, excluding a <separable quantum state>. At $\alpha=0$, the explicit decomposition
$$
\rho_{AB}=\frac12|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle0|+
\frac12|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes|1\rangle\langle1|
$$
is a mixture of <product states>. **Consequently**
$$
\boxed{\rho_{AB}\text{ is separable}\iff\alpha=0.}
$$
Only necessity of the <positive partial transpose criterion> is needed here; sufficiency at zero follows directly from the decomposition.

For any normalized <pure state> on finite-dimensional <Hilbert spaces> $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$, the <Schmidt decomposition> gives
$$
\boxed{|\psi\rangle_{AB}=\sum_{j=1}^r\sqrt{p_j}\,|u_j\rangle_A|v_j\rangle_B,\qquad
p_j>0,\quad\sum_jp_j=1,\quad r\leq\min(d_A,d_B).}
$$
The $u_j$ and $v_j$ form <orthonormal sets> in their respective spaces. The positive numbers $\sqrt{p_j}$ are the <Schmidt coefficients>, and $r$ is the <Schmidt rank>. Taking a <partial trace> gives the <reduced density matrices>
$$
\rho_A=\sum_jp_j|u_j\rangle\langle u_j|,\qquad
\rho_B=\sum_jp_j|v_j\rangle\langle v_j|.
$$
Thus the nonzero <eigenvalues> of the two <reduced density matrices> agree.

One construction is a <singular value decomposition> of the coefficient <matrix> $M$ in $|\psi\rangle=\sum_{a,b}M_{ab}|a\rangle|b\rangle$. If $M=U\Sigma V^\dagger$, the columns of $U$ give $u_j$, the complex conjugates of the columns of $V$ give $v_j$, and the <singular values> give $\sqrt{p_j}$. This also explains why the <Schmidt coefficients> are real and nonnegative.

For a bipartite <pure state>, the <partial traces> give $\psi_A=MM^\dagger$ and $\psi_B=M^T\overline M$. Therefore **the requested reduced density matrices are**

\boxed{\psi_A=\frac1{15}\begin{pmatrix}9&2\\2&6\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\psi_B=\frac1{15}\begin{pmatrix}10&0\\0&5\end{pmatrix}.}

The columns of $M$ are orthogonal. Their norms are $\sqrt{2/3}$ and $\sqrt{1/3}$, so normalizing them gives the <orthonormal set>
$$
|u_0\rangle=\frac{2|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt5},\qquad
|u_1\rangle=\frac{-|0\rangle+2|1\rangle}{\sqrt5}.

With $|v_0\rangle=|0\rangle$ and $|v_1\rangle=|1\rangle$, a <Schmidt decomposition> is

Expanding this expression reproduces the minus sign in the $|01\rangle$ coefficient. The two <reduced density matrices> both have <eigenvalues> $2/3$ and $1/3$, consistent with these <Schmidt coefficients>.

Write $|\zeta\rangle=\sum_{i,j}M_{ij}|i\rangle_A|j\rangle_B$. Normalization says $\|M\|_{\rm HS}=1$, and the <Schmidt rank> equals the <matrix rank> of $M$. Its nonzero <singular values> are therefore $s_1,\ldots,s_r$ with $\sum_js_j^2=1$.

The overlap with the <maximally entangled state> is $\langle\phi^+|\zeta\rangle=\operatorname{Tr}(M)/\sqrt d$. The <trace norm> estimate of Q3(i), with test <linear operator> $I$, followed by the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality>, gives
$$
|\operatorname{Tr}M|\leq\|M\|_1=\sum_{j=1}^rs_j\leq\sqrt{r\sum_js_j^2}=\sqrt r.
$$
For two <pure states>, the unsquared <quantum fidelity> is the absolute value of their overlap. **Hence the <maximally entangled overlap bound from Schmidt rank> is**
$$
\boxed{F(|\phi^+\rangle\langle\phi^+|,|\zeta\rangle\langle\zeta|)\leq\sqrt{\frac rd}.}
$$
The bound is attained by $|\zeta\rangle=r^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}|jj\rangle$. Thus uniform <Schmidt coefficients> on $r$ aligned basis pairs give the greatest possible overlap for that <Schmidt rank>.

= Solution
{parent=1}

Use logarithms to base two, so <Von Neumann entropy> and <quantum relative entropy> are measured in bits. For <density operators> $\rho,\sigma$,
$$
\boxed{S(\rho)=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\log_2\rho),\qquad
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(\log_2\rho-\log_2\sigma)].}
$$
The <quantum relative entropy> formula applies when the <support of a positive operator> $\rho$ is contained in that of $\sigma$; otherwise $D(\rho\|\sigma)=+\infty$. Use $0\log_2 0=0$ in the <Von Neumann entropy> formula.

The binary <density operator> $\omega[p]$ is diagonal with <eigenvalues> $1-p,p$. Its <Von Neumann entropy> is the <binary entropy>:
$$
\boxed{S(\omega[p])=h_2(p)=-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)-p\log_2p.}
$$
The two diagonal <density operators> commute, so their <quantum relative entropy> is
$$
\boxed{D(\omega[p]\|\omega[q])=(1-p)\log_2\frac{1-p}{1-q}+p\log_2\frac pq.}
$$
A term with zero numerator has value zero; a positive numerator and zero denominator give $+\infty$. In particular, $D(\omega[0]\|\omega[0])=D(\omega[1]\|\omega[1])=0$, whereas $q=0,p>0$ or $q=1,p<1$ gives $+\infty$.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Choose $P_X$ uniform on $\mathcal A_M$ and zero outside it, so the marginal <density operator> of $Q$ is $\bar\rho_Q=k^{-1}\sum_m\rho(m)_Q$. Define the <binary test for quantum decoding success> by
$$
\boxed{F(0)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal A_M}|m\rangle\langle m|_{\widetilde X}\otimes E(m)_Q,\qquad
F(1)=I_{\widetilde XQ}-F(0).}
$$
Each $E(m)$ is a <positive contraction>. The <computational basis> blocks of $F(0)$ are these effects on $\mathcal A_M$ and zero elsewhere. Thus $0\leq F(0)\leq I$, making $F$ a <POVM> on the whole space, including labels outside $\mathcal A_M$.

On the correlated <classical-quantum state>, its acceptance probability is
$$
\operatorname{Tr}[F(0)\rho_{\widetilde XQ}]
=\frac1k\sum_m\operatorname{Tr}[E(m)\rho(m)]=1-\epsilon.
$$
On the product of the marginal <density operators>,
$$
\operatorname{Tr}[F(0)(\rho_{\widetilde X}\otimes\bar\rho_Q)]
=\frac1k\sum_m\operatorname{Tr}[E(m)\bar\rho_Q]
=\frac1k\operatorname{Tr}\bar\rho_Q=\frac1k,
$$
since $\sum_mE(m)=I_Q$. **The two requested probabilities are therefore $1-\epsilon$ and $1/k$.** In the notation for binary output <density operators>, the <measurement channel> sends the joint input to $\omega[\epsilon]$ and the product input to $\omega[1-1/k]$; their parameters describe outcome one, not outcome zero.

= Solution
{parent=3}

For the distribution and <POVM> from part 2, apply the <data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy> to the <measurement channel>. The <quantum mutual information> is the <quantum relative entropy> from the joint <density operator> to the product of its marginals, so for $k\geq2$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
I(\widetilde X:Q)_\rho
&=D(\rho_{\widetilde XQ}\|\rho_{\widetilde X}\otimes\bar\rho_Q)\\
&\geq D(\omega[\epsilon]\|\omega[1-1/k])\\
&=(1-\epsilon)\log_2 k-h_2(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log_2(1-1/k)\\
&\geq(1-\epsilon)\log_2 k-1.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step uses $h_2(\epsilon)\leq1$ for the <binary entropy> and $\log_2(1-1/k)\leq0$. If $0\leq\epsilon<1$, rearrangement and then the supremum over $P_X$ give **the <one-shot classical-quantum coding converse>**:
$$
\boxed{\log_2 k\leq\frac{I(\widetilde X:Q)_\rho+1}{1-\epsilon}
\leq\frac{\sup_{P_X}I(\widetilde X:Q)_{\rho_{\widetilde XQ}}+1}{1-\epsilon}.}
$$
For $k=1$, the sole <POVM> effect is $I_Q$, forcing $\epsilon=0$, and the bound is trivial. For $\epsilon=1$, division by $1-\epsilon$ is undefined: the undivided inequality remains valid and gives no size restriction. The displayed coding bound is therefore understood for $\epsilon<1$, or with a vacuous $+\infty$ right-hand side at $\epsilon=1$.

An alternative uses the original decoder directly. Let $M$ be the uniform message and $\widehat M$ its measured estimate. For $k\geq2$, <Fano's inequality> bounds the <conditional entropy> by $H(M|\widehat M)\leq h_2(\epsilon)+\epsilon\log_2(k-1)$. The <Holevo bound> gives
$$
I(\widetilde X:Q)_\rho\geq I(M:\widehat M)
=\log_2 k-H(M|\widehat M)
\geq\log_2 k-h_2(\epsilon)-\epsilon\log_2(k-1)
\geq(1-\epsilon)\log_2 k-1.
$$
Thus the same coding bound follows by bounding the classical <mutual information> obtainable from the <POVM>.
