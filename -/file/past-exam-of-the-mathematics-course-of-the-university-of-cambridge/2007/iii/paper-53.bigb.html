= Solution
{parent=1}

Adopt the phase convention $CP|K^0\rangle=-|\bar K^0\rangle$ and $CP|\bar K^0\rangle=-|K^0\rangle$. Changing both phases changes the convention for the mixing parameter, not physical predictions. The flavor contents are $K^0=d\bar s$ and $\bar K^0=s\bar d$. A pair of <charged weak currents> changes <strangeness> by two units through the following box contractions, with $u_i,u_j=u,c,t$ summed over.

\Image[paper-53-kaon-boxes.png]
{title=Charged-current box contractions for neutral-kaon mixing; solid arrows carry quark number and wavy lines denote W bosons}

The drawing uses <unitary gauge>: the <W boson> <gauge-boson propagator> contains its longitudinal part. In a gauge of a <renormalizable quantum field theory>, charged <Goldstone boson> boxes must also be included to obtain the same gauge-independent result. The external lines represent the valence <quarks>; the <meson> <matrix element> also contains <strong interaction> binding effects.

Put $\lambda_i=V_{is}^*V_{id}$, $x_i=m_i^2/m_W^2$ and $P_L=(1-\gamma^5)/2$. The <charged weak box contribution to kaon mixing> has the effective <four-fermion interaction> structure
$$
H_{\Delta S=2}=C(\bar s\gamma_\mu P_Ld)(\bar s\gamma^\mu P_Ld)+\text{h.c.},\qquad
C\sim\frac{G_F^2m_W^2}{16\pi^2}\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i\lambda_j S(x_i,x_j).
$$
Here $G_F$ is the <Fermi constant>, $V$ is the <CKM matrix>, and $S$ is a dimensionless loop function from a <Feynman integral>; the estimate suppresses convention-dependent numerical factors. Four <weak interaction> <Feynman vertices> and a one-loop <Feynman integral> give the coupling and <loop order> suppression. <Unitarity> of the <CKM matrix> gives $\sum_i\lambda_i=0$, so the <GIM mechanism> cancels the <mass>-independent part. With a negligible <up quark> <mass>, the leading <charm quark> term has $S(x_c,x_c)\sim x_c$. Define $f_K$ by $\langle0|\bar s\gamma_\mu\gamma^5d|K^0(p)\rangle=if_Kp_\mu$. Estimating the hadronic <matrix element> by $f_K^2m_K B_K$, with the dimensionless <kaon bag parameter> $B_K$ of order unity, gives
$$
\boxed{|H'_{21}|_{\rm charm}\sim
\frac{G_F^2m_c^2}{16\pi^2}|V_{cs}^*V_{cd}|^2 f_K^2m_K B_K}.
$$
The hadronic normalization includes division by $2m_K$ when relativistically normalized states are converted to the <effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing>. Since $|V_{cs}^*V_{cd}|\simeq\sin\theta_C\cos\theta_C$, the <Cabibbo suppression> is explicit. Illustrative scales $G_F\sim10^{-5}\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-2}$, $m_c\sim1\,\mathrm{GeV}$, $f_K\sim0.1\,\mathrm{GeV}$ and $m_K\sim0.5\,\mathrm{GeV}$ give an order $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-15}\,\mathrm{GeV}$ estimate. The full sum includes <charm quark>-<top quark> and <top quark>-<top quark> terms, important for <CP violation>; long-distance <strong interaction> contributions prevent this dimensional estimate from being a precision prediction. A <meson> <matrix element> necessarily needs hadronic input in addition to electroweak parameters.

For the decaying <kaon> system write $H'=M-i\Gamma/2$, where $M$ and $\Gamma$ are <Hermitian matrices>. The entries denoted $M_{ij}$ in the question are entries of this <effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing>; they need not themselves form a <Hermitian matrix>. <CPT> invariance equates the diagonal <masses> and diagonal <decay widths>, giving
$$
\boxed{H'_{11}=H'_{22}}.
$$
One can see why there is no <complex conjugation> on the right as follows. In the flavor subspace let $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and represent the <antiunitary> <CPT> operation by $J$ followed by <complex conjugation>. <CPT> changes a projected forward propagator into its adjoint. Indeed, if $P$ projects onto the flavor subspace and $H_{\rm full}$ is the Hermitian microscopic generator, its invariance and the <antiunitary> reversal of $i$ give $\Theta P e^{-iH_{\rm full}t}P\Theta^{-1}=P e^{+iH_{\rm full}t}P=(P e^{-iH_{\rm full}t}P)^\dagger$. In the time-independent two-state approximation, the effective generator this means $J{H'}^*J={H'}^\dagger$, whose diagonal entries give $(H'_{22})^*=(H'_{11})^*$; the off-diagonal entries impose no further condition. Equivalently, apply <CPT> separately to the dispersive and absorptive <Hermitian matrices>. Treating the decaying <effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing> as a <Hermitian matrix> would incorrectly discard the absorptive part.

In the chosen flavor phase convention <CP> is represented by $-J$. Its invariance requires $JH'J=H'$. Thus it gives diagonal equality and additionally
$$
\boxed{H'_{12}=H'_{21}}.
$$
The normalized <CP eigenstates> are
$$
\boxed{|K_1^0\rangle=\frac{|K^0\rangle-|\bar K^0\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|K_2^0\rangle=\frac{|K^0\rangle+|\bar K^0\rangle}{\sqrt2}}.
$$
Applying <CP> directly gives <eigenvalues> $+1$ and $-1$ respectively.

Now use <CPT> to put $H'=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&a\end{pmatrix}$ and take correlated <square roots> $\alpha=\sqrt b$, $\beta=\sqrt c$. Its <characteristic polynomial> equation is $(a-\mu)^2-bc=0$, and direct multiplication shows
$$
H'\binom{\alpha}{-\beta}=(a-\alpha\beta)\binom{\alpha}{-\beta},\qquad
H'\binom{\alpha}{\beta}=(a+\alpha\beta)\binom{\alpha}{\beta}.
$$
Expressing these two <eigenvectors> in the <CP eigenstate> basis gives, respectively, coefficients proportional to $(\alpha+\beta,\alpha-\beta)$ and $(\alpha-\beta,\alpha+\beta)$. Therefore the <kaon CP mixing parameter> is
$$
\epsilon=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\alpha+\beta}
=\frac{\sqrt{M_{12}}-\sqrt{M_{21}}}{\sqrt{M_{12}}+\sqrt{M_{21}}},\qquad
\frac qp=\frac\beta\alpha=\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon},
$$
and, because the <CP eigenstate> basis is <orthonormal>, the normalized states at production are
$$
\boxed{|K_S^0\rangle=\frac{|K_1^0\rangle+\epsilon|K_2^0\rangle}{\sqrt{1+|\epsilon|^2}},\qquad
|K_L^0\rangle=\frac{|K_2^0\rangle+\epsilon|K_1^0\rangle}{\sqrt{1+|\epsilon|^2}}}.
$$
The <kaon mixing square-root branch convention> correlates the roots continuously with the limit of conserved <CP> $b=c$, where $\alpha=\beta$ and $\epsilon=0$. Reversing one root interchanges the two <eigenstates>. The labels short and long are fixed by their <decay widths>: their complex <eigenvalues> are $\mu_{S,L}=m_{S,L}-i\Gamma_{S,L}/2$, and subsequent evolution supplies $e^{-i\mu_{S,L}t}$. The displayed formula assumes nonzero mixing and $\alpha+\beta\ne0$, as in the physical <kaon> system; degenerate or defective matrices require a separate limiting treatment.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Use <metric signature> $(+---)$ and average over the four initial <spin> states, summing over final <spins> and the $N_c=3$ <quark> <color charge> states. The <electrons> carry no <color charge>, so their initial <color charge> average is trivial. A final <color charge> average would not give the inclusive <relativistic cross-section> requested.

<Photon> exchange gives the tree amplitude, up to an irrelevant overall phase,
$$
\mathcal M=\frac{e^2Q_q}{s}\delta_{ab}
[\bar v(p_1)\gamma^\mu u(p_2)]
[\bar u(q_1)\gamma_\mu v(q_2)],\qquad e^2=4\pi\alpha.
$$
Massless <spin sums> give $\sum u\bar u=\not p$ and $\sum v\bar v=\not p$. Therefore
$$
\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
=\frac{N_c e^4Q_q^2}{4s^2}
\operatorname{tr}(\not p_1\gamma^\mu\not p_2\gamma^\nu)
\operatorname{tr}(\not q_1\gamma_\mu\not q_2\gamma_\nu).
$$
The <color charge> multiplicity is $\sum_{a,b}|\delta_{ab}|^2=N_c$, not $N_c^2$. Each <trace> equals $4(a^\mu b^\nu+a^\nu b^\mu-g^{\mu\nu}a\cdot b)$. Contracting them, the terms proportional to $(p_1\cdot p_2)(q_1\cdot q_2)$ cancel, leaving $32[(p_1\cdot q_1)(p_2\cdot q_2)+(p_1\cdot q_2)(p_2\cdot q_1)]$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}
=\frac{384\pi^2\alpha^2Q_q^2}{s^2}
[(p_1\cdot q_2)(p_2\cdot q_1)+(p_2\cdot q_2)(p_1\cdot q_1)]}.
$$

In the <centre-of-momentum frame> every energy is $\sqrt s/2$. Let $\theta$ be the angle between $p_1$ and $q_2$. Then
$$
p_1\cdot q_2=p_2\cdot q_1=\frac s4(1-\cos\theta),\qquad
p_1\cdot q_1=p_2\cdot q_2=\frac s4(1+\cos\theta).
$$
Consequently $\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}=N_c e^4Q_q^2(1+\cos^2\theta)$.

The supplied <two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space> formula has a factor-of-two error. Deriving the normalization explicitly,
$$
d\Phi_2=\frac{d\Omega}{32\pi^2},\qquad
\text{incident flux}=4p_1\cdot p_2=2s,
$$
so the <massless two-body scattering flux normalization> is
$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}
=\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}{64\pi^2s}
=\frac{\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}}{128\pi^2p_1\cdot p_2}.
$$
For example, integrating the spatial <Dirac delta> in $d\Phi_2$ leaves $[4(2\pi)^2]^{-1}dk\,d\Omega\,\delta(\sqrt s-2k)$; its radial integral supplies the factor $1/2$. The printed denominator $64\pi^2p_1\cdot p_2$ would double the <relativistic cross-section> and cannot reproduce the requested result with the standard <scattering amplitude>.

The correctly normalized differential and total results for <electron-positron annihilation into a quark pair> are
$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{3\alpha^2Q_q^2}{4s}(1+\cos^2\theta),\qquad
\int d\Omega(1+\cos^2\theta)=\frac{16\pi}{3},
$$
and hence
$$
\boxed{\sigma_q=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{s}Q_q^2}.
$$

At energies where <photon> exchange and the <parton> description apply, sum over kinematically accessible <quark> flavors. The produced <quarks> undergo <hadronization>, so the leading inclusive hadronic rate is
$$
\sigma_{\rm had}=\frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{s}\sum_qQ_q^2,\qquad
R(s)=\frac{\sigma_{\rm had}}{\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)}
=3\sum_qQ_q^2.
$$
This is the <hadronic R ratio>. Perturbative <QCD> adds a leading relative correction $\alpha_s(s)/\pi$; thresholds, narrow resonances and, near the <Z boson> scale, electroweak exchange require additional treatment. The calculation is a <photon>-exchange, massless result from a <tree-level Feynman diagram>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

<Quark mixing> arises because the up-type and down-type <quark> <Yukawa matrices> need not be diagonal in the same <weak interaction> basis. In the <Standard Model> the three left-handed <quark> doublets $Q'_L$ transform as $(3,2,1/6)$ under $SU(3)_c\times SU(2)_L\times U(1)_Y$, whereas $u'_R$ and $d'_R$ transform as $(3,1,2/3)$ and $(3,1,-1/3)$. With a <Higgs doublet> $H$ and its conjugate $\widetilde H=i\sigma^2H^*$, the <gauge-invariant> <Yukawa interaction> is
$$
\mathcal L_Y=-\bar Q'_L Y_dH d'_R-\bar Q'_L Y_u\widetilde H u'_R+\text{h.c.}
$$
The generation indices are matrix-multiplied. <Electroweak symmetry breaking>, $\langle H\rangle=(0,v_H/\sqrt2)^T$, produces $M_u=v_HY_u/\sqrt2$ and $M_d=v_HY_d/\sqrt2$.

Each complex <fermion mass matrix> admits a <biunitary diagonalization>,
$$
U_{uL}^\dagger M_uU_{uR}=D_u,\qquad U_{dL}^\dagger M_dU_{dR}=D_d,
$$
where the diagonal entries are real nonnegative <quark> <masses>. Define $u'_L=U_{uL}u_L$ and $d'_L=U_{dL}d_L$, and similarly for right-handed fields. The <kinetic terms> retain their canonical form because the transformations are <unitary matrices>. However, the <weak charged current> becomes
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm cc}=-\frac{g_2}{\sqrt2}\bar u_L\gamma^\mu Vd_LW^+_\mu+\text{h.c.},\qquad
\boxed{V=U_{uL}^\dagger U_{dL}}.
$$
This <unitary matrix> is the <Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix>. Its entries multiply transitions between up-type and down-type <mass eigenstates>. The minimal <Standard Model> has no analogous right-handed <charged weak current>.

<Quark mixing> does not introduce tree-level <flavor-changing neutral currents>. <Photon>, <gluon> and <weak neutral current> couplings are proportional to the identity within each fixed-charge <quark> sector, and $U^\dagger I U=I$. The single <Higgs doublet> also has diagonal neutral <scalar field> couplings after the same transformations that diagonalize the <masses>. At higher <loop orders>, flavor changes are possible. For example, an $s\to d$ amplitude contains $\sum_iV_{is}^*V_{id}f(m_i^2)$. <Unitarity> of the <CKM matrix> sets the sum of coefficients to zero, cancelling any <mass>-independent part. This is the <GIM mechanism>; <mass> splittings leave a suppressed, generally nonzero amplitude.

For $N$ generations a <unitary matrix> has $N^2$ real parameters: $N(N-1)/2$ mixing angles and $N(N+1)/2$ phases. Rephasing the $N$ up-type and $N$ down-type <quark> fields removes $2N-1$ phases; a common <baryon number> phase leaves every matrix entry unchanged. Hence the physical numbers are
$$
N_{\rm angles}=\frac{N(N-1)}2,\qquad
N_{\rm weak\ CP\ phases}=\frac{(N-1)(N-2)}2.
$$
Two generations therefore give a real Cabibbo rotation,
$$
V=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_C&\sin\theta_C\\-\sin\theta_C&\cos\theta_C\end{pmatrix},
$$
with <strangeness>-changing charged-current transitions suppressed by $\sin\theta_C$. Three generations have three mixing angles and one irreducible weak <CP> phase. This counting assumes nondegenerate <masses>; degeneracies allow additional basis rotations.

Writing $s_{ij}=\sin\theta_{ij}$ and $c_{ij}=\cos\theta_{ij}$, a standard exact parametrization is
$$
V=\begin{pmatrix}
c_{12}c_{13}&s_{12}c_{13}&s_{13}e^{-i\delta}\\
-s_{12}c_{23}-c_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{12}c_{23}-s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&s_{23}c_{13}\\
s_{12}s_{23}-c_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&-c_{12}s_{23}-s_{12}c_{23}s_{13}e^{i\delta}&c_{23}c_{13}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Complex entries alone do not establish <CP violation>, because <quark> <quantum field rephasings> can change their phases. For nondegenerate <quark> <masses> the weak mixing sector conserves <CP> precisely when a basis makes $V$ real. A useful invariant test is the <Jarlskog invariant>,
$$
J=\operatorname{Im}(V_{ud}V_{cs}V_{us}^*V_{cd}^*)
=c_{12}c_{23}c_{13}^2s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}\sin\delta.
$$
A nonzero $J$ requires all three mixing angles and a nontrivial phase. If two same-charge <quark> <masses> coincide, the additional basis freedom removes the corresponding physical <CP> effect. The possible <QCD theta angle> is a separate <CP> parameter, not part of this weak-mixing count.

The <Wolfenstein parametrization> displays the hierarchy of <quark mixing> economically. Put $\lambda_W=s_{12}$, $s_{23}=A\lambda_W^2$, and $s_{13}e^{i\delta}=A\lambda_W^3(\rho+i\eta)$. Expanding the exact matrix gives
$$
V=\begin{pmatrix}
1-\lambda_W^2/2&\lambda_W&A\lambda_W^3(\rho-i\eta)\\
-\lambda_W&1-\lambda_W^2/2&A\lambda_W^2\\
A\lambda_W^3(1-\rho-i\eta)&-A\lambda_W^2&1
\end{pmatrix}+O(\lambda_W^4).
$$
The observed pattern is predominantly diagonal, with $1$-$2$ mixing largest and $2$-$3$, $1$-$3$ mixing successively suppressed. The expansion implies $J\simeq A^2\lambda_W^6\eta$. The truncated matrix must not be treated as an exactly <unitary matrix>.

Column <orthogonality> gives
$$
V_{ud}V_{ub}^*+V_{cd}V_{cb}^*+V_{td}V_{tb}^*=0.
$$
The three complex numbers form a <CKM unitarity triangle>, with area $|J|/2$. Dividing by $-V_{cd}V_{cb}^*$ puts its vertices at $0$, $1$ and $\bar\rho+i\bar\eta$, where $\bar\rho+i\bar\eta=-V_{ud}V_{ub}^*/(V_{cd}V_{cb}^*)$. The barred parameters include higher-order corrections to the Wolfenstein parameters. Comparing independent measurements of its sides and angles tests both <quark mixing> and the absence of additional flavor dynamics.

Semileptonic <decay rates> determine magnitudes of <CKM> entries through factors $|V_{ij}|^2$ once hadronic <matrix elements> are controlled. Neutral <kaon> and neutral B-<meson> mixing constrain products of entries through weak box <scattering amplitudes>. <CP> asymmetries probe their invariant relative phases, with <kaon> mixing providing indirect <CP violation> and <decay amplitudes> providing direct <CP violation>. These processes complement tests of the <unitarity> constraints on rows and columns. **The minimal three-generation <Standard Model> describes weak <quark mixing> by a <unitary matrix> with three angles and one physical <CP> phase; it does not predict the <Yukawa couplings> or their hierarchy.**

= Solution
{parent=4}

There is a sign inconsistency in the printed model. For the ordinary <cross product> let $J(a)z=a\times z$. The vector triple-product identity gives $[J(a),J(b)]=J(a\times b)$. Consequently the printed $D_\mu=\partial_\mu+eJ(A_\mu)$ has <gauge curvature>
$$
[D_\mu,D_\nu]\Phi=eF^+_{\mu\nu}\times\Phi,\qquad
F^+_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+eA_\mu\times A_\nu.
$$
Its <gauge curvature> cannot instead contain the printed negative quadratic term. For a noncommuting <pure gauge potential> for this <gauge covariant derivative>, $F^+=0$ whereas $F^-=-2eA_\mu\times A_\nu\ne0$. Thus the printed gauge <kinetic term> assigns a nonzero value to a connection obtained by applying the <gauge-field transformation law> to the zero connection. Literally the printed action has global rotations but not the requested local triplet <gauge symmetry>. The <cross-product convention for an adjoint covariant derivative> makes the minimal repair explicit: change the <gauge field strength> quadratic term to a plus sign, retaining the printed <gauge covariant derivative>. Changing the sign in the <gauge covariant derivative> instead would be another consistent convention. The remaining calculation uses the first repair.

The corrected theory has local <SO(3)> <gauge symmetry>, equivalently the adjoint-field realization of the <SU(2)> <Lie algebra>; its <adjoint scalar fields> and <gauge fields> are insensitive to the <center of a group> of <SU(2)>. Explicitly, $\Phi\mapsto R(x)\Phi$ and
$$
eJ(A_\mu)\mapsto R\,eJ(A_\mu)R^{-1}-(\partial_\mu R)R^{-1}.
$$
Both $D_\mu\Phi$ and $F_{\mu\nu}$ transform as triplets, so their <dot products> and the <scalar potential> are invariant. Assume $\lambda>0$, $e\ne0$ and take $v\ge0$ without loss of generality. For $v>0$ the minima have $\Phi^2=v^2$. Choose the <scalar-field vacuum> $\Phi_0=v(0,0,1)$, whose <stabilizer> consists of rotations around the third axis: **the residual <gauge symmetry> is SO(2), locally <U(1)>.**

In <unitary gauge> write $\Phi=(0,0,v+h)$, $A_\mu=A^3_\mu$ and $W^\pm_\mu=(A^1_\mu\mp iA^2_\mu)/\sqrt2$. These are the neutral massless <gauge field>, two charged massive <gauge fields> and a real radial <scalar field>. Define
$$
f_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu,\qquad
C_{\mu\nu}=W^+_\mu W^-_\nu-W^+_\nu W^-_\mu,
$$
$$
G^\pm_{\mu\nu}=(\partial_\mu\mp ieA_\mu)W^\pm_\nu-(\partial_\nu\mp ieA_\nu)W^\pm_\mu.
$$
Substitution gives $F^3_{\mu\nu}=f_{\mu\nu}-ieC_{\mu\nu}$ and $(F^1_{\mu\nu}\mp iF^2_{\mu\nu})/\sqrt2=G^\pm_{\mu\nu}$. The entire physical-field <Lagrangian density> is therefore
$$
\mathcal L=-\frac14(f_{\mu\nu}-ieC_{\mu\nu})(f^{\mu\nu}-ieC^{\mu\nu})
-\frac12G^+_{\mu\nu}G^{-\mu\nu}
+\frac12\partial_\mu h\partial^\mu h+e^2(v+h)^2W^+_\mu W^{-\mu}
-\frac\lambda8[(v+h)^2-v^2]^2.
$$
In particular $D_\mu\Phi=(e(v+h)A^2_\mu,-e(v+h)A^1_\mu,\partial_\mu h)$; this directly verifies the <scalar field> kinetic and vector <mass terms>. Expanding the <scalar potential> gives $\lambda v^2h^2/2+\lambda vh^3/2+\lambda h^4/8$. Comparing the quadratic terms with standard <real scalar field> and charged-vector <mass terms> yields the <adjoint triplet Higgs spectrum>,
$$
\boxed{m_{W^\pm}^2=e^2v^2,\qquad m_A=0,\qquad m_h^2=\lambda v^2}.
$$
This also rewrites the purely vector interactions, which are already contained in the displayed <gauge field strengths> rather than omitted from the theory.

The original symmetry remains unbroken **at $v=0$**. The unique minimum is then $\Phi=0$, with three massless <gauge fields> and three massless <real scalar field> components; the quartic <scalar field> interaction remains. The physical <degrees of freedom> number $3\times2+3=9$. At $v>0$ the two broken generators supply two <Goldstone bosons>, eaten to give <longitudinal gauge-boson polarizations> of the charged vectors. The physical count is $2\times3+2+1=9$: two massive vectors, one massless vector and one radial <scalar field>. The scalar components eaten in <unitary gauge> are not additional physical particles.

For the broken phase all interactions containing the physical <scalar field> are
$$
\mathcal L_{\rm scalar,int}=-\frac{\lambda v}{2}h^3-\frac\lambda8h^4
+2e^2vhW^+_\mu W^{-\mu}+e^2h^2W^+_\mu W^{-\mu}.
$$
Multiplying interaction coefficients by $i$ and differentiating with respect to the external fields includes the identical-field <factorials>. The <scalar vertices of an adjoint triplet Higgs model> are
$$
\boxed{hhh:\ -3i\lambda v,\quad hhhh:\ -3i\lambda,\quad
hW^+_\mu W^-_\nu:\ 2ie^2v g_{\mu\nu},\quad
hhW^+_\mu W^-_\nu:\ 2ie^2g_{\mu\nu}}.
$$
For example, the $h^3$ rule contains $3!$, whereas the $hhW^+W^-$ rule contains only $2!$ from its two identical <scalar fields>. There are no $hAA$ or $hhAA$ vertices: the radial <scalar field> is neutral under the residual <U(1)>. There is no derivative scalar-vector vertex in this physical spectrum in <unitary gauge>.

\Image[paper-53-scalar-vertices.png]
{title=All physical-scalar interaction vertices in the broken triplet Higgs model, with the unbroken-phase scalar vertices shown below; dashed lines are scalars and wavy lines are vectors}

For completeness, if the unbroken phase is chosen, its physical <scalar fields> are the three components $\phi_a$, not a single radial $h$. Expanding its <gauge-covariant kinetic term> gives
$$
\mathcal L_{A\phi\phi}=e\epsilon^{iaj}(\partial_\mu\phi_i)A^{a\mu}\phi_j,\qquad
\mathcal L_{AA\phi\phi}=\frac{e^2}{2}[A_\mu^aA^{a\mu}\phi_b\phi_b-A_\mu^a\phi_aA^{b\mu}\phi_b].
$$
With all momenta incoming and <Fourier transform> convention $e^{-ipx}$, the three unbroken-phase rules are
$$
A^a_\mu\phi_b(p)\phi_c(q):\quad e\epsilon^{abc}(q-p)_\mu,
$$
$$
A^a_\mu A^b_\nu\phi_c\phi_d:\quad
ie^2(2\delta^{ab}\delta^{cd}-\delta^{ac}\delta^{bd}-\delta^{ad}\delta^{bc})g_{\mu\nu},
$$
$$
\phi_a\phi_b\phi_c\phi_d:\quad
-i\lambda(\delta^{ab}\delta^{cd}+\delta^{ac}\delta^{bd}+\delta^{ad}\delta^{bc}).
$$
The first rule follows by differentiating once on each possible scalar: for $a=3,b=1,c=2$, the interaction is $eA^3_\mu(\phi_1\partial^\mu\phi_2-\phi_2\partial^\mu\phi_1)$, which fixes the momentum sign. The remaining rules follow from the quadratic gauge interaction and $-\lambda(\phi_a\phi_a)^2/8$. There are no unbroken-phase cubic <scalar field> <Feynman vertices>.
