= Solution
{parent=a}

Let $H=\dot a/a$ be the <Hubble parameter>, and normalize the <scale factor> by $a(t_0)=1$. The <cosmological continuity equation> gives $\rho_M=\rho_{M0}a^{-3}$ for <pressureless matter>, whereas the <cosmological constant> has constant energy density $\rho_\Lambda$. The spatially flat <Friedmann equation> therefore becomes
$$
H^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3}(\rho_{M0}a^{-3}+\rho_\Lambda).
$$
The present <Hubble constant> is $H_0=H(t_0)$. In terms of the present <critical density> $\rho_{\mathrm{crit},0}=3H_0^2/(8\pi G)$, the matter <cosmological density parameter> is $\Omega_M=\rho_{M0}/\rho_{\mathrm{crit},0}$. Spatial flatness gives $\rho_\Lambda/\rho_{\mathrm{crit},0}=1-\Omega_M$, so
$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=H_0^2\left[\frac{\Omega_M}{a^3}+1-\Omega_M\right].}
$$

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

For $0<\Omega_M<1$, set $x=a^{3/2}$ and choose the expanding branch of the <Friedmann equation>. Then
$$
\dot x=\frac32H_0\sqrt{\Omega_M+(1-\Omega_M)x^2}.
$$
Separating variables and choosing <cosmic time> $t=0$ at the <Big Bang> gives
$$
\frac{\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(x\sqrt{(1-\Omega_M)/\Omega_M}\right)}{\sqrt{1-\Omega_M}}=\frac32H_0t.
$$
Thus the <flat dust-and-vacuum expansion> is
$$
\boxed{a(t)=\left(\frac{\Omega_M}{1-\Omega_M}\right)^{1/3}\sinh^{2/3}\!\left(\frac32H_0\sqrt{1-\Omega_M}\,t\right).}
$$
Setting $a(t_0)=1$ gives the <age of a flat matter-Lambda universe>,
$$
t_0=\frac{2}{3H_0\sqrt{1-\Omega_M}}\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{\frac{1-\Omega_M}{\Omega_M}}.
$$
The two <matter-vacuum age endpoint limits> follow below.

As $\Omega_M\downarrow0$, use $\operatorname{arsinh}z=\log(z+\sqrt{1+z^2})\sim\log(2z)$ to obtain
$$
\boxed{H_0t_0\sim\frac13\log\frac4{\Omega_M}\ \longrightarrow\ \infty.}
$$
At fixed <Hubble constant>, the <age of a flat matter-Lambda universe> diverges logarithmically. The limiting purely vacuum-dominated <de Sitter spacetime> has an exponential <scale factor> and no <Big Bang> at a finite value of <cosmic time> in this flat slicing.

As $\Omega_M\uparrow1$, use $\operatorname{arsinh}z\sim z$ near zero. Consequently

\boxed{t_0\longrightarrow\frac2{3H_0}.}

The <flat dust-and-vacuum expansion> tends to $a(t)=(3H_0t/2)^{2/3}$, the <Einstein-de Sitter universe>.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

A radial light ray in the <FLRW metric> travels <comoving radial distance> $d\chi=dt/a$ in units $c=1$. The <total comoving visibility radius> is therefore the full available <conformal time> interval,
$$
d_c=\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{a(t)}=\int_0^\infty\frac{da}{a^2H(a)}.
$$
Insert the <Friedmann equation> from part (a), then put $y=1/a$ to get
$$
\boxed{d_c=\frac1{H_0}\int_0^\infty\frac{dy}{\sqrt{1-\Omega_M+\Omega_My^3}}.}
$$
For $0<\Omega_M<1$ the integrand is bounded at zero and decays as $y^{-3/2}$ at infinity, so the <total conformal lifetime of a flat matter-Lambda universe> is finite. Equivalently, this distance is the present <comoving particle horizon> plus the remaining comoving distance to the <cosmological event horizon>. This describes causal visibility under the assumed ability to see back to the initial singularity.

The substitution $u=y[\Omega_M/(1-\Omega_M)]^{1/3}$ also evaluates the integral using the <beta function>:
$$
d_c=\frac{\Omega_M^{-1/3}(1-\Omega_M)^{-1/6}}{3H_0}\,\mathrm B\!\left(\frac13,\frac16\right).
$$

= Solution

The <degeneracy factor> $g_A$ counts internal states with the same energy, including the allowed <spin> states. The <chemical potential> $\mu_A$ measures the free-energy cost of changing the number of particles of species $A$; in this convention the rest energy remains in the exponential of the <nonrelativistic Maxwell--Boltzmann number density>.

The <chemical potential> is fixed by the densities of <conserved charges> and by <chemical equilibrium> between reactions. For a reaction $\sum_A\nu_A A=0$, <chemical equilibrium> requires $\sum_A\nu_A\mu_A=0$. Equivalently, when these are the only constraints, $\mu_A$ is a linear combination of the <chemical potentials> of the <conserved charges> carried by $A$. An unconstrained species has zero <chemical potential>. In particular, the <photon chemical potential> vanishes in thermal equilibrium when photon-number-changing reactions are effective.

= b
{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

For $p+e\rightleftharpoons H+\gamma$, <chemical equilibrium> and the vanishing <photon chemical potential> imply $\mu_H=\mu_p+\mu_e$. Taking the ratio of the three <nonrelativistic Maxwell--Boltzmann number densities> cancels these <chemical potentials>:
$$
\frac{n_H}{n_pn_e}=\frac{g_H}{g_pg_e}\left(\frac{2\pi M_H}{M_pM_eT}\right)^{3/2}\exp\!\left(\frac{M_p+M_e-M_H}{T}\right).
$$
The ground-state <hydrogen atom> has four combined <electron> and <proton> <spin> states if the small hyperfine splitting is ignored, whereas $g_p=g_e=2$. Hence $g_H/(g_pg_e)=1$. With <Hydrogen binding energy> $B=M_p+M_e-M_H$ and $M_H\simeq M_p$, the <Saha ionization equation> gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{n_H}{n_pn_e}\simeq\left(\frac{2\pi}{M_eT}\right)^{3/2}e^{B/T}.}
$$
The approximation in the prefactor neglects relative corrections of order $(M_e+B)/M_p$; the <Hydrogen binding energy> must be retained in the exponential.

= c
{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

Write $n_b=n_p+n_H$ for the <cosmological baryon density> in this hydrogen-only model, and $x_e=n_p/n_b$ for the <hydrogen ionization fraction>. By <charge neutrality>, $n_e=n_p$, so the <Saha ionization equation> becomes
$$
\frac{1-x_e}{x_e^2}=n_b\left(\frac{2\pi}{M_eT}\right)^{3/2}e^{B/T}.
$$
The <baryon-to-photon ratio> is $\eta=n_b/n_\gamma$, and the equilibrium <photon number density> is $n_\gamma=2\zeta(3)T^3/\pi^2$. At an order-one <hydrogen ionization fraction>, neglecting factors of order one therefore gives
$$
1\simeq\eta\left(\frac T{M_e}\right)^{3/2}e^{B/T},\qquad T\simeq\frac{B}{\log[\eta^{-1}(M_e/T)^{3/2}]}.
$$
Replacing $T$ inside the slowly varying logarithm by $B$ gives
$$
\boxed{T\simeq\frac{B}{\log[\eta^{-1}(M_e/B)^{3/2}]}\ll B.}
$$
With $B=13.6\,\mathrm{eV}$, $M_e=5\times10^5\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\eta=10^{-10}$, this is $T\simeq0.351\,\mathrm{eV}\simeq4.1\times10^3\,\mathrm K$. Solving the preceding implicit approximation instead gives $T\simeq0.306\,\mathrm{eV}\simeq3.5\times10^3\,\mathrm K$. Both are reasonable estimates of the <recombination temperature> at the accuracy requested. The <small baryon abundance delays hydrogen recombination>: even when $T\ll B$, the high-energy tail of the abundant <photons> can still ionize the much rarer <hydrogen atoms>.

With an approximately constant <Hubble parameter> $H$, the <scale factor> is $a(t)=e^{Ht}$ after a choice of normalization. Integrate $d\tau=dt/a(t)$ and choose the integration constant so that the future endpoint is $\tau=0$:
$$
\tau=-\frac{e^{-Ht}}H,\qquad\boxed{a(\tau)=-\frac1{H\tau},\quad-\infty<\tau<0.}
$$
Substituting $dt=a\,d\tau$ in the <FLRW metric> gives $ds^2=a^2(\tau)(-d\tau^2+d\mathbf x^2)$. This is <Conformal time during de Sitter expansion>.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

For the <FLRW metric> in <conformal time>, $\sqrt{-g}=a^4$, $g^{00}=-a^{-2}$ and $g^{ij}=a^{-2}\delta^{ij}$. The equation for a <minimally coupled massless scalar field> is consequently
$$
-(a^2\delta\phi')'+a^2\nabla^2\delta\phi=0.
$$
For a <Fourier mode> of comoving wavevector $\mathbf k$, the <Laplacian> contributes $-k^2$, where $k=|\mathbf k|$. Thus
$$
\delta\phi_k''+2\frac{a'}a\delta\phi_k'+k^2\delta\phi_k=0.
$$
Since $a'/a=-1/\tau$, this yields
$$
\boxed{\delta\phi_k''-\frac2\tau\delta\phi_k'=-k^2\delta\phi_k.}
$$
The absence of a curvature-coupling term is material: a <conformally coupled scalar field> obeys a different mode equation.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Denote the comoving box volume by $\mathcal V$, keeping it distinct from the <inflaton> potential $V(\phi)$. For the <Canonically rescaled de Sitter scalar mode> $u_k=a\delta\phi_k$, the equation in part (b) becomes
$$
u_k''+\left(k^2-\frac2{\tau^2}\right)u_k=0.
$$
Direct differentiation verifies the positive-frequency solution
$$
u_k=\frac{1-i/(k\tau)}{\sqrt{2k\mathcal V}}e^{-ik\tau},\qquad\boxed{\delta\phi_k^{(+)}=\frac{H(-\tau+i/k)}{\sqrt{2k\mathcal V}}e^{-ik\tau}.}
$$
For $|k\tau|\gg1$, $u_k$ tends to $e^{-ik\tau}/\sqrt{2k\mathcal V}$, the positive-frequency flat-space mode. It therefore selects the incoming <Bunch-Davies vacuum>. The <Bunch-Davies mode normalization in a finite comoving volume> is fixed by the <Wronskian> condition
$$
a^2\mathcal V\left(\delta\phi_k^{(+)}\delta\phi_k^{(+)*\prime}-\delta\phi_k^{(+)*}\delta\phi_k^{(+)\prime}\right)=i.
$$
After <cosmological horizon exit>, $|k\tau|\ll1$, the mode tends to the time-independent value $iH/\sqrt{2k^3\mathcal V}$. The complex mode and its <Hermitian conjugate> together make the real quantum field.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}

In the incoming <Bunch-Davies vacuum>, only $\langle a_{\mathbf k}a_{\mathbf q}^{\dagger}\rangle=\delta_{\mathbf k\mathbf q}$ contributes to the coincident <vacuum expectation value>. Thus
$$
\langle\delta\hat\phi^2\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf k}|\delta\phi_k^{(+)}|^2=\sum_{\mathbf k}\frac1{2k\mathcal V}\left(\frac1{a^2}+\frac{H^2}{k^2}\right).
$$
Replacing the box sum by $\mathcal V\int d^3k/(2\pi)^3$ gives
$$
\boxed{\langle\delta\hat\phi^2\rangle=\int\frac{d^3k}{(2\pi)^3}\frac1{2k}\left(\frac1{a^2}+\frac{H^2}{k^2}\right)=\frac1{4\pi^2}\int_0^\infty dk\left(\frac{k}{a^2}+\frac{H^2}{k}\right).}
$$
The <subhorizon vacuum contribution> is the flat-space zero-point term; at fixed comoving $k$ it redshifts as $a^{-2}$. The other term gives <frozen scalar power> on <superhorizon scales>. Specifically, the <dimensionless cosmological power spectrum> per logarithmic interval is
$$
\Delta_{\delta\phi}^2(k,\tau)=\frac{k^2/a^2+H^2}{4\pi^2}\ \longrightarrow\ \boxed{\left(\frac H{2\pi}\right)^2}.
$$
Its late-time value is independent of $k$, producing a <scale-invariant inflationary power spectrum>. Each fixed <Fourier mode> freezes separately as $\tau\to0$.

\Image[paper-62-scalar-freezing.png]
{title=Vacuum redshifting and frozen power of massless scalar modes during de Sitter expansion}

The coincident integral is formal: the vacuum part diverges at large $k$, and an unlimited scale-invariant part diverges at small $k$. The conclusion concerns the power in each finite logarithmic band. A finite inflationary history supplies a largest generated wavelength, as described by the <infrared qualification of a scale-invariant covariance>; taking the late-time limit inside an unregulated integral would not be justified.

= Solution

A perturbation of the <inflaton> shifts its local clock: $\delta\phi\simeq\dot\phi\,\delta t$. Regions with different clock shifts reach the end of inflation at different times and hence undergo different amounts of expansion. The <inflaton clock-shift origin of curvature perturbations> gives a local expansion difference of magnitude $H\delta t=H\delta\phi/\dot\phi$. The sign depends on whether $\delta t$ denotes a clock advance or a delay to a fixed end value.

The <comoving curvature perturbation> has amplitude $\mathcal R\simeq H\delta\phi/\dot\phi$. For adiabatic evolution, <superhorizon conservation of single-field comoving curvature> carries this amplitude into the later growing <density contrast>, with the numerical conversion depending on the epoch and the density slice. Thus the approximate density-amplitude statement in the question follows; it does not identify the <density contrast> and $\mathcal R$ in every gauge.

The <frozen scalar power> per $d\log k$ is $(H/2\pi)^2$, so the <slow-roll curvature power spectrum> is
$$
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2(k)=\left(\frac{H^2}{2\pi\dot\phi}\right)^2_{k\text{ at horizon exit}}.}
$$
Consequently $\langle\mathcal R^2\rangle=\int (dk/k)\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2(k)$, giving the requested approximate growing-density spectrum. Both $H$ and $\dot\phi$ are evaluated separately when each <Fourier mode> undergoes <cosmological horizon exit>.

{parent=4}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

At <cosmological horizon exit>, $k$ is proportional to $aH$; the convention-dependent factor $2\pi$ has zero logarithmic derivative. Define the <first Hubble slow-roll parameter> $\epsilon_H=-\dot H/H^2$. Then
$$
\frac{d\log k}{d\log a}=1-\epsilon_H,\qquad\frac d{d\log k}=\frac1{1-\epsilon_H}\frac d{d\log a}\simeq\frac d{d\log a}.
$$
The <chain rule> gives $d/d\log a=(\dot\phi/H)d/d\phi$. In <reduced Planck units>, the <slow-roll equations> are $3H\dot\phi\simeq-V_{,\phi}$ and $3H^2\simeq V$. Therefore the <horizon-exit logarithmic derivative in slow roll> is
$$
\boxed{k\frac d{dk}\simeq a\frac d{da}=\frac{\dot\phi}H\frac d{d\phi}\simeq-\frac{V_{,\phi}}V\frac d{d\phi}.}
$$

= Solution
{parent=c}

The <slow-roll equations> turn the <slow-roll curvature power spectrum> into
$$
\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2\simeq\frac{V^3}{12\pi^2V_{,\phi}^2}=\frac{V}{24\pi^2\epsilon},\qquad\epsilon=\frac12\left(\frac{V_{,\phi}}V\right)^2.
$$
The <spectral tilt> is the logarithmic derivative of this dimensionless spectrum. Apply the <horizon-exit logarithmic derivative in slow roll>:
$$
\Delta n=\frac{d\log\Delta_{\mathcal R}^2}{d\log k}\simeq-\frac{V_{,\phi}}V\left(3\frac{V_{,\phi}}V-2\frac{V_{,\phi\phi}}{V_{,\phi}}\right)=-3\left(\frac{V_{,\phi}}V\right)^2+2\frac{V_{,\phi\phi}}V.
$$
With the <potential slow-roll parameter> $\epsilon$ above and the <second potential slow-roll parameter> $\eta=V_{,\phi\phi}/V$,
$$
\boxed{\Delta n=-6\epsilon+2\eta.}
$$
This is the first-order departure of the <scalar spectral index> from unity, in <reduced Planck units>.

During <radiation domination>, the <cosmological continuity equation> gives $\rho_R\propto a^{-4}$. The flat <Friedmann equation> in <conformal time> is $(a'/a)^2=8\pi Ga^2\rho_R/3$. Hence $a'^2$ is constant, and choosing the <Big Bang> at $\tau=0$ yields
$$
\boxed{a\propto\tau,\qquad8\pi G\rho_Ra^2=\frac3{\tau^2}.}
$$
For the <CDM density equation in a matter-radiation universe>, neglect $\rho_C\delta_C$ while retaining $\delta_C$ itself. The <adiabatic initial conditions> give $\delta_R=4\delta_C/3$, so
$$
\delta_C''+\frac1\tau\delta_C'=\frac4{\tau^2}\delta_C.
$$
This <Euler-Cauchy equation> has powers $\tau^p$ with $p(p-1)+p-4=0$, namely $p=2,-2$. Selecting the growing <superhorizon adiabatic CDM mode in radiation domination> gives
$$
\boxed{\delta_C(\tau,k)=A_R(k)\tau^2.}
$$

{parent=5}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

After <cosmological horizon entry> in <radiation domination>, the oscillating radiation source averages away. Neglecting both that source and CDM self-gravity in the <CDM density equation in a matter-radiation universe> gives
$$
\delta_C''+\frac1\tau\delta_C'=0,\qquad(\tau\delta_C')'=0,
$$
so $\delta_C=C_1(k)+C_2(k)\log\tau$. The <superhorizon adiabatic CDM mode in radiation domination> has amplitude $A_R(k)\tau_h^2\sim A_R(k)k^{-2}$ at entry $\tau_h\sim k^{-1}$, fixing this scale for the subsequent <logarithmic CDM growth after radiation-era horizon entry>.

More explicitly, in a sharp-transition approximation with $\tau_h=1/k$, continuity of $\delta_C$ and $\delta_C'$ gives
$$
\delta_C=\frac{A_R(k)}{k^2}\,[1+2\log(k\tau)].
$$
The <matching the CDM growing mode at horizon entry> therefore yields the late subhorizon scaling
$$
\boxed{\delta_C(\tau,k)\simeq A_R(k)k^{-2}\log(k\tau).}
$$
Here $\simeq$ suppresses order-one coefficients and the nonlogarithmic term, as in the question. A smooth treatment of the radiation forcing through entry changes those coefficients; the factor two is exact only for the sharp matching model just used. This logarithmic growth, rather than growth proportional to $a$, is the <Mészáros effect>.

= Solution

During <matter domination>, $\rho_C\propto a^{-3}$. The flat <Friedmann equation> in <conformal time> implies $a'^2\propto a$, and hence
$$
\boxed{a\propto\tau^2,\qquad8\pi G\rho_Ca^2=\frac{12}{\tau^2}.}
$$
With radiation neglected, the <CDM density equation in a matter-radiation universe> reduces to
$$
\delta_C''+\frac2\tau\delta_C'-\frac6{\tau^2}\delta_C=0.
$$
For this <Euler-Cauchy equation>, $\delta_C\propto\tau^p$ requires $p(p-1)+2p-6=(p-2)(p+3)=0$. Thus the <matter-era growing and decaying density modes> are $\tau^2$ and $\tau^{-3}$, respectively, and
$$
\boxed{\delta_C(\tau,k)\propto\tau^2\propto a.}
$$
There is no explicit $k$ in this pressureless equation, so the result holds on all scales within the stated CDM-comoving <synchronous gauge> description. The amplitude can still depend on $k$ through its earlier evolution; the result does not equate <density contrasts> on different time slicings.
