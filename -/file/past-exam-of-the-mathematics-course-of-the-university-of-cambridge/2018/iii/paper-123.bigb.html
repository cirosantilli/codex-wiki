Use the simple-root form of the <Hensel lemma>: for $F\in\mathcal O_K[T]$ and $\bar a\in k_K$ satisfying $\overline F(\bar a)=0$ and $\overline{F'}(\bar a)\ne0$, there is a unique $a\in\mathcal O_K$ with $\bar a$ as its reduction and $F(a)=0$.

Let $d=[\ell:k_K]$. The <residue field> $k_K$ is finite, so $\ell/k_K$ is separable, and the <primitive element theorem> gives $\ell=k_K(\bar\alpha)$. Let $\bar F$ be the monic irreducible polynomial of $\bar\alpha$, of degree $d$, and choose a monic coefficient lift $F\in\mathcal O_K[T]$. Irreducible reduction implies that $F$ is irreducible over $K$: any monic factorization over $K$ has integral coefficients, since all its roots are integral over the <valuation ring>, and would reduce to a factorization of $\bar F$.

Adjoin a root $\alpha$ and put $L=K(\alpha)$. Then $[L:K]=d$ and $\alpha\in\mathcal O_L$. Its reduction satisfies $\bar F$, so $k_L$ contains a copy of $\ell$ over $k_K$. For finite extensions of non-Archimedean <local fields>, the fundamental degree relation is
$$
[L:K]=e(L/K)[k_L:k_K].
$$
The residue degree is at least $d$, while the left side equals $d$, hence
$$
\boxed{e(L/K)=1,\qquad k_L\cong\ell,\qquad [L:K]=[\ell:k_K].}
$$
This constructs the required <unramified extension>.

For uniqueness, let $L'/K$ be another unramified extension with a chosen identification $k_{L'}\cong\ell$. The element corresponding to $\bar\alpha$ is a simple residue root of $F$, because finite fields are perfect. The <Hensel lemma> in $L'$ gives a root $\alpha'\in\mathcal O_{L'}$ with this reduction. Since $F$ is irreducible of degree $d$, $[K(\alpha'):K]=d=[L':K]$, so $L'=K(\alpha')$. Sending $\alpha$ to $\alpha'$ yields a $K$-isomorphism $L\to L'$, compatible with the prescribed residue identification. Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{the unramified lift of }\ell/k_K\text{ is unique up to }K\text{-isomorphism}.}
$$
This proves the <existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions> using the stated version of Hensel's lemma, without assuming the classification in advance.

Write $q=\#k_K=p^f$. For every $a\in k_K$, the polynomial $F(T)=T^q-T$ has $a$ as a residue root and $\overline{F'}=-1$. By the <Hensel lemma> there is a unique element $[a]\in\mathcal O_K$ such that
$$
\boxed{\overline{[a]}=a,\qquad [a]^q=[a].}
$$
This defines the <Teichmuller lift>, including $[0]=0$. The two displayed properties determine each value uniquely by Hensel's uniqueness statement.

They also imply the standard <Teichmuller lift properties>. The elements $0,1$ already satisfy their defining equations, so $[0]=0$ and $[1]=1$. The product $[a][b]$ reduces to $ab$ and satisfies $( [a][b])^q=[a][b]$, so uniqueness gives $[ab]=[a][b]$. Likewise $[a]^p$ reduces to $a^p$ and is fixed by its $q$th power, giving
$$
\boxed{[ab]=[a][b],\qquad [a^p]=[a]^p.}
$$
For $a\ne0$, division of $[a]^q=[a]$ by $[a]$ gives $[a]^{q-1}=1$. Thus the nonzero <Teichmuller representatives> are precisely the prime-to-$p$ roots of unity lifting the nonzero residue classes. This is a multiplicative section of reduction; additivity is not asserted in mixed characteristic.

For the convergence, use the normalized <discrete valuation> $v_K$ with $v_K(\pi)=1$, and put $e=v_K(p)\geq1$. A useful binomial estimate is
$$
v_K(u^p-v^p)\geq\min\{e+r,pr\}\geq r+1
\quad\text{if }u,v\in\mathcal O_K,\ v_K(u-v)\geq r\geq1.
$$
Indeed, writing $u=v+\delta$, every intermediate coefficient $\binom pj$ is divisible by $p$, so all terms of $(v+\delta)^p-v^p$ have valuation at least $\min\{e+r,pr\}$. The <ultrametric inequality> gives the bound for their sum.

Finite fields are perfect, so the successive $p$th roots $x_n$ exist uniquely and satisfy $x_n^{p^n}=x$. Let $a_n=[x_n]$. Since $y_n-a_n$ lies in the maximal ideal, iterating the estimate $n$ times gives
$$
v_K(y_n^{p^n}-a_n^{p^n})\geq n+1.
$$
Multiplicativity, or the Frobenius identity above, gives $a_n^{p^n}=[x_n^{p^n}]=[x]$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{v_K(y_n^{p^n}-[x])\geq n+1,\qquad y_n^{p^n}\longrightarrow[x].}
$$
This proves the <approximation of Teichmuller lifts by iterated pth powers> for completely arbitrary choices of the lifts $y_n$, including $x=0$. The original PDF confirms $x\in k_K$, correcting the capital $K_K$ in the TeX transcription.

Give $\overline K_{\mathfrak p}$ the unique extension of the absolute value of $K_{\mathfrak p}$. We use two standard facts: a complete non-Archimedean absolute value extends uniquely to every finite algebraic extension, and any two embeddings of a finite separable extension into an algebraic closure are conjugate by an automorphism of that algebraic closure.

A $K$-embedding $\tau:L\to\overline K_{\mathfrak p}$ pulls back this absolute value to a finite <place of a number field> on $L$ extending the given place of $K$. By the permitted equivalence of places and primes, it determines a <prime ideal> $\mathfrak q_\tau$ above $\mathfrak p$. Every element of $\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K_{\mathfrak p}/K_{\mathfrak p})$ preserves the absolute value by uniqueness, so $\mathfrak q_\tau$ is constant on each orbit.

Every prime $\mathfrak q$ above $\mathfrak p$ occurs: its <completion of a number field at a prime ideal> $L_{\mathfrak q}$ is a finite extension of $K_{\mathfrak p}$ and admits a $K_{\mathfrak p}$-embedding into $\overline K_{\mathfrak p}$. Composing with $L\hookrightarrow L_{\mathfrak q}$ gives an embedding inducing $\mathfrak q$.

Conversely, suppose two embeddings induce the same prime. Their pulled-back absolute values have the same normalization on $K$, so they extend continuously to embeddings of the same field $L_{\mathfrak q}$. To see that their images are still inside the algebraic closure rather than only its completion, the closure of $\tau(L)$ is exactly the finite complete field $K_{\mathfrak p}\tau(L)$. The two resulting embeddings of $L_{\mathfrak q}$ are conjugate by the stated separable-extension fact. Restricting the conjugacy to $L$ proves that the original embeddings are in the same orbit. Thus the <prime ideals and local embedding orbits> are naturally identified:
$$
\boxed{\{\mathfrak q\subset\mathcal O_L:\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p\}
\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\overline K_{\mathfrak p}/K_{\mathfrak p})\backslash
\operatorname{Hom}_K(L,\overline K_{\mathfrak p}).}
$$

If $L=K(\alpha)$, an embedding is determined by the image of $\alpha$, which may be any root of its <minimal polynomial> $f$. The orbits on these roots are exactly the root sets of the monic irreducible factors of $f$ over $K_{\mathfrak p}$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\{\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p\}\cong
\{\text{monic irreducible factors of }f\text{ in }K_{\mathfrak p}[T]\}.}
$$
If $f_j$ corresponds to $\mathfrak q_j$, then $L_{\mathfrak q_j}\cong K_{\mathfrak p}[T]/(f_j)$ and $[L_{\mathfrak q_j}:K_{\mathfrak p}]=\deg f_j$. This is the <local factorization and extended absolute values> correspondence and does not require an integral power basis.

The polynomial is <Eisenstein> at $2$: every nonleading coefficient is even, and its constant term $126$ is not divisible by $4$. Therefore it is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ and
$$
\boxed{[M:\mathbb Q]=5.}
$$
By part (a), the number of primes above a rational prime is the number of irreducible factors over the corresponding $p$-adic field.

We use the <Newton polygon> theorem in this form: a segment of slope $-r$ and horizontal length $d$ accounts for $d$ roots of valuation $r$, counted with multiplicity, in an algebraic closure with $v_p(p)=1$. We also use the <denominator criterion for Newton polygon irreducibility>: if all roots have valuation $a/n$ with $\gcd(a,n)=1$, any monic factor of degree $d$ has constant-term valuation $da/n\in\mathbb Z$, hence $n\mid d$.

At $3$, the nonzero coefficient points are
$$
(0,2),\ (2,2),\ (3,1),\ (5,0).
$$
The lower hull is the single segment from $(0,2)$ to $(5,0)$, with slope $-2/5$. All five roots therefore have valuation $2/5$. A proper factor of degree $d$ would require $5\mid d$, which is impossible for $1\leq d<5$. Thus $g$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q_3$, giving
$$
\boxed{\#\{\mathfrak q\subset\mathcal O_M:\mathfrak q\mid3\}=1.}
$$
The completion has ramification index $5$ and residue degree $1$: the value $2/5$ forces its ramification index to be divisible by $5$, and its degree is $5$.

At $7$, the nonzero coefficient points are
$$
(0,1),\ (2,1),\ (3,0),\ (5,0).
$$
The lower hull consists of a length-three segment of slope $-1/3$ and a length-two horizontal segment. Thus three roots have valuation $1/3$, and two have valuation zero. Reduction gives
$$
\bar g(T)=T^3(T^2-1)=T^3(T-1)(T+1)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_7[T].
$$
Both $1$ and $-1$ are simple roots, so the <Hensel lemma> gives two distinct linear factors over $\mathbb Q_7$. The remaining cubic accounts for all the valuation-$1/3$ roots and is irreducible by the same denominator argument. Hence the local factor degrees are $1,1,3$, and
$$
\boxed{\#\{\mathfrak q\subset\mathcal O_M:\mathfrak q\mid7\}=3.}
$$
The two linear factors give $(e,f)=(1,1)$, and the cubic gives $(e,f)=(3,1)$. These data sum to degree five, as required. This computes the <local prime decomposition of T5 plus 6T3 plus 252T2 plus 126>. Counting distinct factors merely modulo $3$ would not justify the result, since that reduction is $T^5$.

The only possible rational roots of the monic cubic are $1,-1$, and neither is a root. Thus $f$ is irreducible. The <discriminant of a depressed cubic> gives
$$
\Delta=-4\cdot2^3-27\cdot1^2=-59.
$$
It is not a rational square. By the permitted <cubic discriminant criterion>, the splitting field has <Galois group> $S_3$, degree six, and contains its quadratic subfield generated by $\sqrt\Delta$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-59})\subseteq L,\qquad
[L:K]=3,\qquad\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)=A_3\cong C_3.}
$$

For a rational prime $r\ne59$, the reduction of $f$ is separable because its discriminant is a unit at $r$. Choose a finite extension of $\mathbb F_r$ in which that reduction splits. Part 1(a) lifts it to an <unramified extension> of $\mathbb Q_r$, and the <Hensel lemma> lifts all the simple roots into that extension. The local splitting field is therefore unramified over $\mathbb Q_r$. In particular all primes of $L/K$ above such $r$ are unramified.

At $59$, the supplied simple residue root $-28$ lifts to a root in $\mathbb Q_{59}$. The remaining factor is quadratic, so the local splitting field has degree at most two over $\mathbb Q_{59}$. It contains $\sqrt{-59}$, which is not a square in $\mathbb Q_{59}$ because its valuation is odd. It is therefore exactly $\mathbb Q_{59}(\sqrt{-59})$. This is also the completion of $K$ at its unique prime above $59$: the polynomial $T^2+59$ is <Eisenstein> there. Thus
$$
L_{\mathfrak P}=K_{\mathfrak p}=\mathbb Q_{59}(\sqrt{-59})
$$
for a prime $\mathfrak P$ above $\mathfrak p$. All the other primes above it give the same local conclusion by Galois conjugacy. The relative extension is therefore unramified at $59$ as well; indeed that prime splits completely in $L/K$.

The field $K$ has only complex places, so there is no infinite-place ramification. Hence $\boxed{L/K\text{ is unramified everywhere}}$.

We use the <Hilbert class field> theorem: for a number field $E$, its maximal everywhere unramified abelian extension $H_E$ is finite, contains every such extension, and satisfies $\operatorname{Gal}(H_E/E)\cong\operatorname{Cl}_E$, so $[H_E:E]=h_E$. The cyclic degree-three extension just constructed is contained in $H_K$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{3\mid h_K,\qquad h_K>1.}
$$
This is the <unramified cubic extension of Q of square root minus fifty-nine>; only divisibility of the class number, rather than its exact value, is needed here.

Use the <Hilbert class field> theorem stated in part (a), and let $H_L$ be the Hilbert class field of $L$. Put $F=M\cap H_L$ inside a common algebraic closure.

Any intermediate extension of $M/L$ is totally ramified at the given prime. To verify this even when $M/L$ is not Galois, there is a unique prime of $F$ above $\mathfrak p$, its residue field embeds into the residue field at the unique prime of $M$ and therefore equals $k_{\mathfrak p}$, and the <fundamental identity for prime decomposition> gives ramification index $[F:L]$. On the other hand $F/L$ is a subextension of $H_L/L$, so it is unramified there. Its ramification index is consequently both $[F:L]$ and $1$, forcing
$$
\boxed{M\cap H_L=L.}
$$
Because $H_L/L$ is Galois, this intersection identity gives
$$
[MH_L:M]=[H_L:L]=h_L,\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(MH_L/M)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(H_L/L).
$$
Thus $MH_L/M$ is abelian. We use the standard local fact that unramified extensions remain unramified under finite base extension: after base change each factor is obtained by lifting an extension of the residue field. Since $H_L/L$ is unramified at every finite prime, $MH_L/M$ is also unramified at every finite prime. There are no real places of $M$, since every embedding of $M$ restricts to an embedding of the totally imaginary field $L$. Hence there is no infinite-place obstruction.

The maximality of $H_M$ now implies $MH_L\subseteq H_M$. Taking degrees over $M$ proves the <class number divisibility under total ramification>:
$$
\boxed{h_L=[MH_L:M]\mid[H_M:M]=h_M.}
$$
The argument uses the degree of an unramified class-field extension, and does not assume that extension of ideals induces an injection of <ideal class groups>.

We use the following standard facts about <cyclotomic fields>. For an odd prime $\ell$, the unique quadratic subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$ is
$$
\mathbb Q\left(\sqrt{(-1)^{(\ell-1)/2}\ell}\right).
$$
Also $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\ell^r})$ has degree $\varphi(\ell^r)=\ell^{r-1}(\ell-1)$, and $\ell$ is totally ramified, with a unique prime and residue field $\mathbb F_\ell$. These are the <quadratic subfield of a prime cyclotomic field> and <total ramification in a prime-power cyclotomic field> results.

Take
$$
E_r=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{59^r})\quad(r\geq1).
$$
Since $59\equiv3\pmod4$, every $E_r$ contains $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-59})$, through its subfield $E_1$. The unique prime above $59$ is totally ramified in $E_r/K$: the absolute ramification index is $58\cdot59^{r-1}$, the index in $K/\mathbb Q$ is $2$, and the relative index is therefore
$$
e(E_r/K)=29\cdot59^{r-1}=[E_r:K].
$$
The field $K$ has no real places. By part (b), $h_K\mid h_{E_r}$, and part (a) showed $3\mid h_K$. Thus
$$
\boxed{3\mid h_{E_r}\quad\text{for every }r\geq1.}
$$
The <ring of integers of a number field> is a <Dedekind domain>, and is a <principal ideal domain> exactly when its <ideal class group> is trivial. Hence none of these rings of integers is principal. Finally the degrees $58\cdot59^{r-1}$ strictly increase, so the fields are pairwise distinct. The explicit infinite family of <nonprincipal cyclotomic fields of conductor a prime power> is
$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q(\zeta_{59}),\ \mathbb Q(\zeta_{59^2}),\ \mathbb Q(\zeta_{59^3}),\ldots.}
$$

Normalize the <discrete valuation> by $v_L(\pi_L)=1$, and put $v_L(0)=+\infty$. For $\sigma\ne1$, define its ramification number
$$
i_G(\sigma)=\min_{a\in\mathcal O_L}v_L(\sigma(a)-a),
$$
and set $i_G(1)=+\infty$. The minimum exists: all finite values are nonnegative integers, and a nonidentity automorphism moves some integral element. The <lower ramification numbering> is
$$
\boxed{G_s=\{\sigma\in G:i_G(\sigma)\geq s+1\}\quad(s\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}).}
$$
Thus $G_s=G_{\lceil s\rceil}$, $G_0$ is the <inertia group>, and $G_1$ is the <wild inertia group>. This states the convention at integer endpoints as well as between them. Automorphisms preserve $v_L$, so these are normal subgroups of $G$.

Define the <Herbrand function> and its inverse by
$$
\varphi_{L/K}(s)=\int_0^s\frac{dt}{[G_0:G_t]},\qquad
\psi_{L/K}=\varphi_{L/K}^{-1}.
$$
The integrand is positive and piecewise constant. Since $G_t=1$ for sufficiently large $t$, the function is continuous, strictly increasing and unbounded, and its inverse is defined on all nonnegative reals. The <upper ramification numbering> is
$$
\boxed{G^u=G_{\psi_{L/K}(u)}\quad(u\geq0).}
$$
In particular, the denominator in the Herbrand integral involves $G_0$, even when $L/K$ is not totally ramified. These conventions are also given in https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf[Milne's Class Field Theory, Chapter I, §4].

Now use the monogenic hypothesis $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_K[\alpha]$. Since $L/K$ is Galois, its minimal polynomial has the distinct conjugates $\sigma(\alpha)$ as roots, so
$$
f'(\alpha)=\prod_{\sigma\ne1}(\alpha-\sigma(\alpha)).
$$
Taking valuations proves the first equality.

For every polynomial $P\in\mathcal O_K[T]$, the difference $P(\sigma(\alpha))-P(\alpha)$ is divisible in $\mathcal O_L$ by $\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha$. Every element of $\mathcal O_L$ is such a polynomial value, and taking the element $\alpha$ attains the resulting lower bound. Hence
$$
i_G(\sigma)=v_L(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)\quad(\sigma\ne1).
$$
For an integer $i_G(\sigma)=m\geq0$, the element $\sigma$ lies in exactly the groups $G_0,\ldots,G_{m-1}$; if $m=0$, it lies in none. Summing this count over the nonidentity elements and interchanging the two finite sums gives
$$
\boxed{v_L(f'(\alpha))
=\sum_{\sigma\ne1}v_L(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)
=\sum_{s\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}}(|G_s|-1).}
$$
This proves the <ramification-group sum for a monogenic integer ring>. Equivalently it is the <different exponent from ramification groups>, since the <different ideal> is generated by $f'(\alpha)$ under the same monogenic hypothesis.

The polynomial is <Eisenstein> at $3$, so a root $\alpha$ generates a totally ramified cubic field $F=\mathbb Q_3(\alpha)$ and is a <uniformizer> of $F$. Its cubic discriminant is
$$
\Delta=-4(-3)^3-27\cdot3^2=-135=-5\cdot3^3.
$$
Its $3$-adic valuation is $3$, so it is not a square. The <cubic discriminant criterion> therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q_3)\cong S_3,\qquad[M:\mathbb Q_3]=6.}
$$
The unique quadratic subfield is generated by $\sqrt\Delta$. Since the permitted square root $\sqrt{-5}$ is in $\mathbb Q_3$, this subfield is
$$
E=\mathbb Q_3(\sqrt3).
$$
It is totally ramified of degree two, by the <Eisenstein polynomial> $T^2-3$. The ramification index of $M/\mathbb Q_3$ is divisible by both the index $3$ of $F$ and the index $2$ of $E$. Since the whole degree is $6$, it follows that
$$
\boxed{e(M/\mathbb Q_3)=6,\qquad k_M=\mathbb F_3.}
$$
In particular $G_0=G$.

We use the following standard facts about totally ramified extensions: every uniformizer generates the valuation ring over the base valuation ring, and for a totally ramified Galois extension
$$
G_i=\{\sigma:v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)\geq i+1\}\quad(i\geq0).
$$
Thus we can use the <uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups>. Put $s=\sqrt3$. Since $v_M(\alpha)=2$ and $v_M(s)=3$,
$$
\pi=\frac{\alpha^2}{s},\qquad v_M(\pi)=4-3=1
$$
is a uniformizer of $M$.

Let $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ be the three roots. The <Galois group> acts transitively on their unordered pairs and preserves the valuation, so all differences between distinct roots have the same valuation $d$. The discriminant identity gives
$$
18=v_M(\Delta)=2\sum_{i<j}v_M(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)=6d,
$$
hence $d=3$. Thus, for any $\sigma$, either $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha$ or $\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha$ has valuation $3$.

A nontrivial three-cycle fixes $s$, since it fixes the quadratic subfield, and sends $\alpha$ to a different root. Also $v_M(\sigma(\alpha)+\alpha)=2$: write the sum as $2\alpha+(\sigma(\alpha)-\alpha)$, whose two terms have unequal valuations $2,3$. Therefore
$$
v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)=3+2-3=2
\quad\text{for either nontrivial three-cycle}.
$$
A transposition changes the sign of $s$. Consequently
$$
\sigma(\pi)-\pi=-\frac{\sigma(\alpha)^2+\alpha^2}{s}.
$$
The numerator has valuation $4$: it is $2\alpha^2$ plus terms of strictly higher valuation if the root changes, and exactly $2\alpha^2$ if the root is fixed. Thus every transposition has $v_M(\sigma(\pi)-\pi)=1$. We conclude that the <ramification groups of the splitting field of T3 minus 3T plus 3 over Q3> are
$$
\boxed{G_0=S_3,\qquad G_1=A_3\cong C_3,\qquad G_i=\{1\}\quad(i\geq2).}
$$

As an independent check, the cubic field has $\mathcal O_F=\mathbb Z_3[\alpha]$ and different exponent $v_F(g'(\alpha))=v_F(3(\alpha^2-1))=3$. The extension $M/F$ is tamely totally ramified of degree two, so its different exponent is $1$. The <different in a tower> gives $d(M/\mathbb Q_3)=1+2\cdot3=7$, agreeing with the ramification sum $(6-1)+(3-1)=7$.

The <decomposition group> of the chosen prime is its stabilizer under the <Galois group>:
$$
\boxed{D=D_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma(\mathfrak P)=\mathfrak P\}.}
$$
The <transitivity of the Galois action on primes> identifies the primes of $M$ above $\mathfrak p$ with $G/D$ through $gD\mapsto g\mathfrak P$.

Put $H=\operatorname{Gal}(M/L)$. Contraction of prime ideals to $\mathcal O_L$ is constant on every $H$-orbit, because every element of $H$ fixes $L$ pointwise. It is surjective onto the primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak p$, since prime ideals extend in the integral extension $\mathcal O_M/\mathcal O_L$. Moreover, two primes of $M$ contracting to the same prime of $L$ are conjugate by $H$: the extension $M/L$ is Galois and the same transitivity theorem applies to it.

Thus the fibers of contraction are exactly the $H$-orbits on $G/D$. These orbits are the <double cosets> $H\backslash G/D$, proving the <primes in an intermediate field as double cosets> correspondence
$$
\boxed{H\backslash G/D\xrightarrow{\sim}\{\mathfrak q\subset\mathcal O_L:\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p\},\qquad
HgD\longmapsto g\mathfrak P\cap\mathcal O_L.}
$$
Explicitly, replacing $g$ by $hgd$ does not change the contracted prime. Conversely, if $g\mathfrak P$ and $g'\mathfrak P$ contract to the same prime, some $h\in H$ sends the former to the latter, so $g'^{-1}hg\in D$ and $Hg'D=HgD$. This also verifies the stated order of the two sides of the double coset.

A prime is completely split in an extension of degree $n$ exactly when there are $n$ primes above it. Indeed, the <fundamental identity for prime decomposition> is $\sum_{\mathfrak q\mid\mathfrak p}e_{\mathfrak q}f_{\mathfrak q}=n$, and every summand is a positive integer. Thus $n$ primes means that every ramification index and residue degree equals one, and conversely.

By part (a), the number of primes of $L$ above $\mathfrak p$ is the number of right $D$-orbits on $H\backslash G$. This set has $[G:H]=[L:K]$ elements. It has that many orbits exactly when every orbit is a singleton, equivalently when
$$
HgD=Hg\quad\text{for all }g\in G.
$$
This condition says $gDg^{-1}\subseteq H$ for every $g$, or
$$
D\subseteq\bigcap_{g\in G}g^{-1}Hg=\operatorname{Core}_G(H),
$$
the <subgroup core>.

Because $M$ is the <Galois closure> of $L/K$, it is generated by all the fields $g(L)$. The fixed field of $gHg^{-1}$ is $g(L)$, so the fixed field of the core is their compositum, namely $M$. The <Galois correspondence> therefore gives $\operatorname{Core}_G(H)=1$. Hence
$$
\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D=1.
$$
In the Galois extension $M/K$, the number of primes above $\mathfrak p$ is $[G:D]$, so $D=1$ is equivalent to complete splitting in $M$. This proves that <complete splitting is preserved by Galois closure>:
$$
\boxed{\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }L\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathfrak p\text{ completely split in }M.}
$$
No prior unramifiedness assumption is needed: complete splitting already forces all ramification indices to be one, and the decomposition-group argument includes the ramified case.
