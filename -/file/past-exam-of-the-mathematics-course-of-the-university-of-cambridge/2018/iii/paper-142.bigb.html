= Solution
{parent=1}

For the <first Chern class> identity, use finite-dimensional classifying maps. A <complex line bundle> on a <compact Hausdorff space> embeds in a finite-dimensional <trivial vector bundle>, so it is a <pullback vector bundle> of the <tautological bundle> on some <complex projective space>. For the two <complex line bundles>, the universal <tensor product of vector bundles> is classified by the map
$$
m:\mathbb{CP}^N\times\mathbb{CP}^M\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^{(N+1)(M+1)-1},\qquad ([v],[w])\longmapsto[v\otimes w].
$$
Its restriction to either factor pulls the <tautological bundle> back to the <tautological bundle> on that factor. The degree-two <cohomology> of the product is the <direct sum> of the degree-two <cohomology> of its factors, so these two restrictions determine its <first Chern class>. Pulling back to $X$ proves
$$
\boxed{c_1(L_1\otimes L_2)=c_1(L_1)+c_1(L_2).}
$$
This proves the integral identity, including possible <torsion elements> in the <cohomology> of $X$; an argument using only curvature forms would not detect those <torsion elements>.

The <splitting principle for complex vector bundles> says that for a <vector bundle> $E\to X$ there is an iterated <projective bundle> $p:F(E)\to X$ for which $p^*$ is injective on integral <cohomology>, and $p^*E$ is a <direct sum> of <complex line bundles>. The same assertion holds with rational coefficients, and a common such space can split any finite collection of <vector bundles>. The injectivity follows at each stage from the <projective bundle formula for complex vector bundles>; a <Hermitian metric> splits the resulting filtration into a <direct sum>.

Write $x_i=c_1(L_i)$ for the <Chern roots> of the pulled-back <vector bundle>. Define the degree-$2j$ component of its <Chern character> by
$$
\operatorname{ch}_0(E)=\operatorname{rank}E,\qquad
\operatorname{ch}_j(E)=\frac{1}{j!}\sum_i x_i^j,\qquad
\operatorname{ch}(E)=\sum_{j\geq0}\operatorname{ch}_j(E).
$$
The <fundamental theorem of symmetric polynomials> expresses each <power-sum symmetric polynomial> $\sum_i x_i^j$ uniquely as an integral polynomial in the <elementary symmetric polynomials>, which are the <Chern classes> of $E$. Thus the definition makes sense on $X$ itself, without choosing actual <Chern roots> there. On a <compact Hausdorff space>, each <vector bundle> is classified by a finite-dimensional <Grassmannian>; its high-degree <Chern character> components therefore vanish. Hence this defines a class in the direct sum $H^{2*}(X;\mathbb Q)$ even if $X$ has no finite dimension. On more general spaces an infinite <Chern character> is instead interpreted in the product of the even-degree <cohomology> groups.

On a common splitting space, the <Chern roots> of a <direct sum> are the combined root lists, whereas the <first Chern class> identity makes the <Chern roots> of a <tensor product of vector bundles> the pairwise sums $x_i+y_j$. Consequently
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{ch}(E\oplus F)&=\sum_i e^{x_i}+\sum_j e^{y_j},\\
\operatorname{ch}(E\otimes F)&=\sum_{i,j}e^{x_i+y_j}
=\left(\sum_i e^{x_i}\right)\left(\sum_j e^{y_j}\right).
\end{aligned}
$$
The injectivity in the <splitting principle for complex vector bundles> descends both identities to $X$. Additivity extends the <Chern character> to the <Grothendieck group> by $\operatorname{ch}([E]-[F])=\operatorname{ch}(E)-\operatorname{ch}(F)$. Multiplicativity and $\operatorname{ch}(1)=1$ then show that \b[the <Chern character> is a unital <ring homomorphism>].

For <complex K-theory of complex projective space>, let $\gamma$ be the <tautological bundle>, put $t=1-[\gamma]$, and set $h=-c_1(\gamma)$. The <cohomology ring of complex projective space> is $\mathbb Z[h]/(h^{n+1})$. The <CW filtration> gives a <cofibration>
$$
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^n\longrightarrow S^{2n}.
$$
The <K-theory six-term exact sequence> and <complex K-theory of a sphere> give, inductively,
$$
K^{-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0,\qquad
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\{b_n\}\longrightarrow K^0(\mathbb{CP}^n)
\longrightarrow K^0(\mathbb{CP}^{n-1})\longrightarrow0.
$$
Here $b_n$ is the image of a <Bott element> from $S^{2n}$, normalized so that $\operatorname{ch}(b_n)=h^n$. This normalization follows from the multiplicative form of <Bott periodicity>: the <Chern character> of the degree-two <Bott element> is an integral generator, and multiplication yields the top-degree generator on $S^{2n}$. In particular, the <abelian group> $K^0(\mathbb{CP}^n)$ is a <free abelian group> of rank $n+1$.

Assume inductively that $1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1}$ form an integral basis on $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$. Their lifts, together with $b_n$, form an integral basis on $\mathbb{CP}^n$. Since
$$
\operatorname{ch}(t)=1-e^{-h}=h-\frac{h^2}{2!}+\frac{h^3}{3!}-\cdots,
$$
the <Chern characters> of this basis are <linearly independent> over $\mathbb Q$: their first nonzero degrees are $0,2,\ldots,2n$. Thus the <Chern character> is injective on $K^0(\mathbb{CP}^n)$. By induction $t^n$ restricts to zero on $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$, so $t^n=a b_n$ for an integer $a$. Comparing <Chern characters> gives $a=1$. Also $\operatorname{ch}(t^{n+1})=0$, so $t^{n+1}=0$. This proves the integral answer, rather than merely its rationalization:
$$
\boxed{K^0(\mathbb{CP}^n)\cong\mathbb Z[t]/(t^{n+1}),\qquad t=1-[\gamma].}
$$
The induction begins with $\mathbb{CP}^0$ a point and $t=0$.

Let $E$ have rank $r\geq1$, let $p:\mathbb P(E)\to X$ be its <projectivization of a real vector bundle>, and let $\lambda\subset p^*E$ be its <tautological bundle>. Put $u=w_1(\lambda)\in H^1(\mathbb P(E);\mathbb F_2)$. The <projective bundle definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes> starts from the isomorphism
$$
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r-1}H^{*-j}(X;\mathbb F_2)
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }H^*(\mathbb P(E);\mathbb F_2),\qquad
(a_0,\ldots,a_{r-1})\longmapsto\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}p^*a_j\smile u^j.
$$
In particular, $p^*$ is injective. Expand $u^r$ uniquely in this basis. The coefficients define the <Stiefel–Whitney classes> through the relation
$$
\boxed{u^r+p^*w_1(E)u^{r-1}+\cdots+p^*w_r(E)=0.}
$$
Set $w_0(E)=1$ and $w_i(E)=0$ for $i>r$; for a rank-zero <vector bundle> set $w(E)=1$. Uniqueness also proves naturality of the <Stiefel–Whitney classes> under a <pullback vector bundle>.

To prove the <Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes>, apply the <splitting principle for real vector bundles> simultaneously to $E$ and $E'$. Its injective pullback on mod-two <cohomology> follows by iterating the <projective bundle definition of Stiefel–Whitney classes>. It suffices to compute for a split <vector bundle> $E=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r L_i$. Put $x_i=w_1(L_i)$.

On $\mathbb P(E)$, the coordinate projections of the tautological inclusion $\lambda\hookrightarrow p^*E$ give sections of the <real line bundles> $\lambda^*\otimes p^*L_i$ with no common zero. The <mod-two Euler class of a real line bundle> is its first <Stiefel–Whitney class>, and the <first Stiefel–Whitney class of a tensor product of real line bundles> gives $u+p^*x_i$. Multiplication of <Euler classes> under a <direct sum>, and vanishing of the <Euler class of a vector bundle> with a nowhere-zero section, therefore give
$$
\prod_{i=1}^r(u+p^*x_i)=0.
$$
Comparing with the unique monic relation defining the <Stiefel–Whitney classes> shows that $w_j(E)$ is the $j$th <elementary symmetric polynomial> in the $x_i$. Thus $w(E)=\prod_i(1+x_i)$. A <direct sum> joins the two lists, so injectivity of the splitting pullback gives
$$
\boxed{w(E\oplus E')=w(E)w(E'),\qquad
w_k(E\oplus E')=\sum_{i+j=k}w_i(E)\smile w_j(E').}
$$

For the <immersion> obstruction, use the notation $g=(f,f)$: the printed $f\times f$ here means the map from one copy of <real projective space> into the product. Write $x=w_1(\gamma_{\mathbb R})$ and let $z$ be the mod-two reduction of $c_1(\gamma_{\mathbb C}^*)$. The <mod-two cohomology ring of real projective space> and the <cohomology ring of complex projective space> give
$$
H^*(\mathbb{RP}^{2k+1};\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[x]/(x^{2k+2}),\qquad
H^*(\mathbb{CP}^k;\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^{k+1}),\qquad
|x|=1,\quad |z|=2.
$$
Nontriviality in degree two forces $f^*z=x^2$. Necessarily $k\geq1$, since the degree-two <cohomology> of $\mathbb{CP}^0$ vanishes.

The standard <tangent bundle> identities for <real projective space> and <complex projective space> are
$$
T\mathbb{RP}^{2k+1}\oplus\mathbb R\cong(2k+2)\gamma_{\mathbb R},\qquad
T\mathbb{CP}^k\oplus\mathbb C\cong(k+1)\gamma_{\mathbb C}^*.
$$
For the underlying real <vector bundle> of a <complex line bundle>, its total <Stiefel–Whitney class> is $1+c_1\bmod2$. Hence
$$
w(T\mathbb{RP}^{2k+1})=(1+x)^{2k+2},\qquad
w(T\mathbb{CP}^k)=(1+z)^{k+1}.
$$
If $g$ is <homotopic> to an <immersion> $j$, its <normal bundle> $\nu$ has rank $4k-(2k+1)=2k-1$, and
$$
T\mathbb{RP}^{2k+1}\oplus\nu\cong j^*T(\mathbb{CP}^k\times\mathbb{CP}^k).
$$
The <Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes> and <homotopy invariance of cohomology> yield
$$
w(\nu)=\frac{(1+x^2)^{2k+2}}{(1+x)^{2k+2}}
=(1+x)^{2k+2}.
$$
The division is legitimate in the truncated <cohomology ring>, since $1+x$ is a unit. The degree-$2k$ component is
$$
w_{2k}(\nu)=\binom{2k+2}{2k}x^{2k}
=(k+1)(2k+1)x^{2k}=(k+1)x^{2k}.
$$
It must vanish because $2k>\operatorname{rank}\nu$, while $x^{2k}\ne0$. Therefore \b[$k$ must be odd].

All <topological K-theory> groups below are complex and are indexed modulo two using <Bott periodicity>. For $n\geq1$, <complex K-theory of a sphere> gives
$$
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|cc}
&K^0(S^n)&K^{-1}(S^n)\\ \hline
n\text{ even}&\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z&0\\
n\text{ odd}&\mathbb Z&\mathbb Z
\end{array}}
$$
For an even-dimensional <sphere>, choose a generator $u$ of <reduced topological K-theory>. Its square vanishes: the <Chern character> sends $u$ to a top-degree <cohomology> class, whose square is zero, and the <Chern character> is injective on this torsion-free <abelian group>. Thus the <ring> answers are
$$
\boxed{K^0(S^{2m})\cong\mathbb Z[u]/(u^2)\quad(m\geq1),\qquad K^0(S^{2m+1})\cong\mathbb Z.}
$$
The exceptional zero-dimensional <sphere> consists of two points: $K^0(S^0)=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ with coordinatewise multiplication and $K^{-1}(S^0)=0$. In particular its reduced generator is idempotent rather than square-zero.

For the <Euler characteristic> identity, induct over the cells of a <finite CW complex>. The starting <CW complex> consisting of $v$ vertices has $K^0\cong\mathbb Z^v$, $K^{-1}=0$, and $\chi=v$. If $X$ is obtained from $A$ by attaching one $d$-dimensional cell, the quotient is $S^d$, and the <K-theory six-term exact sequence> is
$$
\widetilde K^0(S^d)\longrightarrow K^0(X)\longrightarrow K^0(A)
\longrightarrow\widetilde K^{-1}(S^d)\longrightarrow K^{-1}(X)
\longrightarrow K^{-1}(A)\longrightarrow\widetilde K^0(S^d).
$$
These <abelian groups> are finitely generated by induction. Tensoring the <exact sequence> with $\mathbb Q$ preserves exactness. The alternating sum of dimensions in a cyclic six-term <exact sequence> is zero, so
$$
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{rank}K^0(X)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(X)
&=\operatorname{rank}K^0(A)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(A)\\
&\quad+\operatorname{rank}\widetilde K^0(S^d)
-\operatorname{rank}\widetilde K^{-1}(S^d)\\
&=\chi(A)+(-1)^d=\chi(X).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore
$$
\boxed{\chi(X)=\operatorname{rank}K^0(X)-\operatorname{rank}K^{-1}(X).}
$$

There is a genuine convention issue in the nilpotence assertion. With the usual definition of <reduced topological K-theory> as the kernel of restriction to one basepoint, \b[the assertion needs $X$ to be connected]. For $X=S^0$ based at its first point, $(0,1)$ lies in $\widetilde K^0(X)$ and satisfies $(0,1)^m=(0,1)$ for every $m\geq1$. Thus it is not a <nilpotent element>. For an arbitrary <finite CW complex>, the correct assertion uses the <rank map in topological K-theory> on every <connected component>:
$$
I(X)=\ker\bigl(K^0(X)\longrightarrow H^0(X;\mathbb Z)\bigr).
$$
For a <connected space>, $I(X)=\widetilde K^0(X)$.

Here is an induction proving the corrected assertion. On the vertices, $I(X)=0$. For a positive-dimensional cell attachment $A\subset X$, the ideal $J=\ker(K^0(X)\to K^0(A))$ is square-zero. Indeed, lift two elements of $J$ to <relative topological K-theory> $K^0(X,A)$. Their product is induced by the reduced diagonal
$$
X/A=S^d\longrightarrow S^d\wedge S^d=S^{2d}.
$$
This map is <null-homotopic>, since $d<2d$ and $S^{2d}$ is $(2d-1)$-connected. The <relative product in topological K-theory> is therefore zero, giving $J^2=0$. If $a\in I(X)$, its restriction belongs to $I(A)$, so induction gives $(a|_A)^m=0$ for some $m$. Then $a^m\in J$ and $a^{2m}=0$. We have proved \b[every <topological K-theory> class of rank zero on every <connected component> is a <nilpotent element>], and hence the requested result for <connected> $X$.

For the <K-theory transfer of a finite covering>, let $V\to Y$ be a <vector bundle> and define
$$
(p_!V)_x=\bigoplus_{y\in p^{-1}(x)}V_y.
$$
On an evenly covered neighborhood the $n$ sheets identify this with a <direct sum> of $n$ local <vector bundles>. On overlaps the identifications permute the sheets and apply the transition maps of $V$, so these local descriptions glue to a <vector bundle> on $X$. This operation respects <direct sums>, hence extends to a homomorphism of <Grothendieck groups>
$$
\boxed{p_!:K^0(Y)\longrightarrow K^0(X),\qquad
p_!([V]-[W])=[p_!V]-[p_!W].}
$$
It obeys the <projection formula for the K-theory transfer>:
$$
p_!(p^*a\cdot b)=a\cdot p_!b.
$$
For actual <vector bundles>, this follows fiberwise by distributing the <tensor product of vector bundles> over the <direct sum>; it then extends to their <Grothendieck groups>.

Let $P=p_!(1_Y)$ be the permutation <vector bundle> of the covering. It has rank $n$ on every <connected component>, but need not be a <trivial vector bundle>; in particular one cannot replace $p_!p^*$ by multiplication by $n$ integrally. Instead write
$$
[P]=n+\eta,\qquad \eta\in I(X).
$$
The componentwise nilpotence result from the preceding solution gives $\eta^N=0$ for some $N$. In the <localization of a ring> $K^0(X)\otimes\mathbb Z[1/n]$, the finite <geometric series>
$$
(n+\eta)^{-1}=\frac1n\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\left(-\frac{\eta}{n}\right)^j
$$
is an inverse. The <projection formula for the K-theory transfer> gives
$$
p_!p^*(a)=[P]a=(n+\eta)a.
$$
Thus $p^*(a)=0$ implies $a=0$ after inverting $n$. More explicitly, $(n+\eta)^{-1}p_!$ is a left inverse, proving
$$
\boxed{p^*:K^0(X)\otimes\mathbb Z[1/n]\hookrightarrow K^0(Y)\otimes\mathbb Z[1/n].}
$$

= Solution

Use the standard complex <K-theory Thom class> convention. For a rank-$r$ complex <vector bundle>, its <K-theory Euler class> is the pullback of the <K-theory Thom class> along the zero section, namely
$$
\boxed{e_K(E)=\lambda_{-1}(E^*)=\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^j[\Lambda^jE^*]\in K^0(X).}
$$
Here $\Lambda^j$ denotes an <exterior power>, and a <Hermitian metric> identifies the <dual bundle> with the conjugate <vector bundle>. Choosing $\lambda_{-1}(E)$ instead changes the <K-theory Euler class> by the unit $(-1)^r[\det E]$, so its kernel and cokernel computations are unchanged.

For the <disk bundle> $D(E)$ and <sphere bundle> $S(E)$, the <K-theory Thom class> identifies $K^i(D(E),S(E))$ with $K^i(X)$. The <exact sequence> of this pair becomes the <K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle>:
$$
\boxed{\cdots\longrightarrow K^i(X)\xrightarrow{\ e_K(E)\cdot\ }K^i(X)
\xrightarrow{\pi^*}K^i(S(E))\xrightarrow{\partial}K^{i+1}(X)
\xrightarrow{\ e_K(E)\cdot\ }K^{i+1}(X)\longrightarrow\cdots.}
$$
The groups are two-periodic by <Bott periodicity>.

View <real projective space> as the <circle bundle> of $\gamma^{\otimes2}$ over <complex projective space>, where $\gamma\to\mathbb{CP}^n$ is the <tautological bundle>. Indeed, $S^{2n+1}/\{\pm1\}=\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}$ maps to $S(\gamma^{\otimes2})$ by $[v]\mapsto v\otimes v$; in each fiber squaring identifies exactly $v$ and $-v$.

Use <complex K-theory of complex projective space> in the form
$$
R=K^0(\mathbb{CP}^n)=\mathbb Z[t]/(t^{n+1}),\qquad
 t=1-[\gamma],\qquad K^{-1}(\mathbb{CP}^n)=0.
$$
The <K-theory Euler class> of $\gamma^{\otimes2}$ is $1-[\gamma]^{-2}$. Multiplying it by the unit $-[\gamma]^2$ gives $1-[\gamma]^2=2t-t^2$. The <K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle> therefore gives
$$
K^0(\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1})\cong R/(2t-t^2),\qquad
K^{-1}(\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1})\cong\ker((2t-t^2)\cdot:R\to R).
$$
Under the projection $q:\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}\to\mathbb{CP}^n$, the <pullback vector bundle> $q^*\gamma$ is the <complexification of a real vector bundle> $L$ of the <tautological bundle> of <real projective space>: the fiber map sends $[v,z]$ to $zv$. Thus $t$ pulls back to $1-[L]$. Put $\mu=[L]-1$. The relation $L^{\otimes2}\cong1$ gives $\mu^2=-2\mu$, and $t^{n+1}=0$ gives $2^n\mu=0$. Hence
$$
\boxed{K^0(\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1})\cong
\mathbb Z[\mu]/(\mu^2+2\mu,\,2^n\mu),\qquad \mu=[L]-1.}
$$
For completeness, these relations give precisely the <abelian group> $\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2^n$. In the basis $t,\ldots,t^n$, the image of multiplication by $2t-t^2$ is generated by $2t-t^2,2t^2-t^3,\ldots,2t^n$; its relation matrix is triangular with determinant $2^n$, and the relations express every $t^j$ as $2^{j-1}t$. Thus there is exactly one cyclic <torsion subgroup> of order $2^n$.

If $n\geq1$ and $a=a_0+a_1t+\cdots+a_nt^n$ is in the kernel, successive coefficients of $(2t-t^2)a$ give $2a_0=0$, $2a_1-a_0=0$, and so on, up to $2a_{n-1}-a_{n-2}=0$. Hence $a_0=\cdots=a_{n-1}=0$, with $a_n$ arbitrary. For $n=0$ the multiplier is zero on $R=\mathbb Z$. In all cases,
$$
\boxed{K^{-1}(\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1})\cong\mathbb Z\qquad(n\geq0).}
$$

Finally let $E=L^{\oplus k}$ with $k\geq1$, over $B=\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}$. Since $L^*\cong L$, multiplicativity of the <K-theory Euler class> gives
$$
e_K(E)=(1-[L])^k=(-\mu)^k=-2^{k-1}\mu.
$$
On $K^0(B)=\mathbb Z\oplus(\mathbb Z/2^n)\mu$, the image of multiplication by this <K-theory Euler class> is the subgroup generated by $2^{k-1}\mu$: multiplication by an integer produces every such multiple, while multiplication by $\mu$ only adds multiples of $2^k\mu$. Therefore
$$
\operatorname{coker}(e_K(E):K^0(B)\to K^0(B))
\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2^{\min(n,k-1)}.
$$
Multiplication by $\mu$ is zero on $K^{-1}(B)\cong\mathbb Z$, since $2^n\mu=0$ and this <abelian group> has no nonzero <torsion elements>. Thus the <K-theory Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle> gives a <short exact sequence>
$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/2^{\min(n,k-1)}
\longrightarrow K^0(S(E))\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$
It splits as an <exact sequence> of <abelian groups> because its quotient is a <free abelian group>. Consequently
$$
\boxed{K^0(S(L^{\oplus k}))\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2^{\min(n,k-1)}\qquad(n\geq0,\ k\geq1).}
$$
The convention $\mathbb Z/1=0$ covers $n=0$ and $k=1$. For example, when $k=1$ the <sphere bundle> is $(S^{2n+1}\times S^1)/\{(v,z)\sim(-v,-z)\}$, a <mapping torus> of the antipodal map; its <topological K-theory> has no nonzero <torsion elements>, agreeing with the formula. If $k=0$ is allowed, the rank-zero <vector bundle> has an empty unit <sphere bundle>, so $K^0(S(E))=0$ instead.
