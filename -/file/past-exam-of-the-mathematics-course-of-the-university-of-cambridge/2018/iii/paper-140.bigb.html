<Moser theorem> says that if $M$ is a <compact> <smooth manifold> without boundary and $\omega_t$, $0\leq t\leq1$, is a smooth family of <symplectic forms> with constant <de Rham cohomology> class, then there is a <smooth isotopy> $\psi_t$, with $\psi_0=\mathrm{id}$, such that
$$
\boxed{\psi_t^*\omega_t=\omega_0.}
$$
Thus the endpoints are <symplectomorphic>. The hypothesis is a whole path of <symplectic forms>, not merely equality of the endpoint <de Rham cohomology> classes.

= Solution
{parent=b}

A <symplectomorphism> is a <diffeomorphism>, so it preserves the <genus of a surface>. It also preserves the <orientation> determined by the <symplectic form> and, by integration of a <pullback of a differential form>, preserves the <symplectic area>.

Conversely, equal <genus of a surface> gives a <diffeomorphism> $f:\Sigma_0\to\Sigma_1$. We can choose $f$ to preserve the <orientations> determined by $\omega_0,\omega_1$: if necessary, compose with an <orientation-reversing diffeomorphism> of the standard <closed orientable surface> of that <genus of a surface>, obtained by reflection of a symmetric handle model. On $\Sigma_0$ put $\eta=f^*\omega_1$. Both $\eta$ and $\omega_0$ are positive <volume forms> for the same <orientation>, and their integrals agree. By <top-degree de Rham cohomology>, $[\eta]=[\omega_0]$.

The family $\omega_t=(1-t)\omega_0+t\eta$ consists of positive <volume forms>, hence <symplectic forms> in dimension two, with constant <de Rham cohomology> class. <Moser theorem> supplies $\psi$ with $\psi^*\eta=\omega_0$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{(f\circ\psi)^*\omega_1=\omega_0.}
$$
Therefore \b[the <genus of a surface> and <symplectic area> classify <compact> <connected> <symplectic surfaces>].

= Solution
{parent=c}

There is an <orientation> convention in this statement: the two <volume forms> must be positive for one fixed <orientation>, as is usual in <Moser theorem for volume forms>. Without that convention, the stated necessity is false. For example, on an oriented <two-sphere>, take a positive <volume form> $\beta_0$ and an <orientation-reversing diffeomorphism> $r$, and set $\beta_1=r^*\beta_0$. Then $(r^{-1})^*\beta_1=\beta_0$ but $\int\beta_1=-\int\beta_0$. We prove the intended result with a common <orientation>.

If $\phi^*\beta_1=\beta_0$, positivity forces $\phi$ to preserve the <orientation>, and integration of the <pullback of a differential form> gives equality of the integrals.

For sufficiency, <top-degree de Rham cohomology> gives a <differential form> $\alpha$ of degree $n-1$ with $\beta_1-\beta_0=d\alpha$. Put $\beta_t=(1-t)\beta_0+t\beta_1$. Positivity makes every $\beta_t$ a <volume form>. The <interior product of a differential form> gives an <linear isomorphism>
$$
T_pM\longrightarrow\Lambda^{n-1}T_p^*M,\qquad v\longmapsto\iota_v\beta_t.
$$
Thus there is a unique smooth time-dependent <vector field> $X_t$ satisfying $\iota_{X_t}\beta_t=-\alpha$. Since $M$ is <compact>, its <local flow> exists throughout $0\leq t\leq1$ and defines a <smooth isotopy> $\phi_t$ starting at the identity. By <Cartan's magic formula>, and since a top-degree <differential form> has zero <exterior derivative>,
$$
\frac{d}{dt}\phi_t^*\beta_t
=\phi_t^*\bigl(d\alpha+\mathcal L_{X_t}\beta_t\bigr)
=\phi_t^*\bigl(d\alpha+d\iota_{X_t}\beta_t\bigr)=0.
$$
Hence
$$
\boxed{\phi_1^*\beta_1=\beta_0.}
$$
The zero-dimensional case, a single point, is immediate from equality of the integrals.

Let $\dim V=2n$ and let $V_+=\ker(S-I)$ and $V_-=\ker(S+I)$ be the two <eigenspaces>. The <involution> identity gives a <direct sum>, since
$$
v=\frac{v+Sv}{2}+\frac{v-Sv}{2},\qquad V=V_+\oplus V_-.
$$
For $u,v$ in either one of these <eigenspaces>, the <anti-symplectic involution> identity implies
$$
\Omega(u,v)=\Omega(Su,Sv)=-\Omega(u,v),
$$
so both are <isotropic subspaces of a symplectic vector space>. Such a subspace has dimension at most $n$: $W\subset W^\Omega$ and the <nondegenerate bilinear form> $\Omega$ gives $\dim W^\Omega=2n-\dim W$, where $W^\Omega$ is the <symplectic orthogonal complement>. As their dimensions add to $2n$, both have dimension $n$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Fix}(S)=V_+\text{ is a Lagrangian subspace}.}
$$

Use the decomposition into <Lagrangian subspaces> $V=V_+\oplus V_-$ from part (a). The <bilinear form> $\Omega$ pairs $V_+$ and $V_-$ nondegenerately: a <vector> in either summand annihilating the other already annihilates its own summand and hence all of $V$, so is zero.

Choose a <basis> $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $V_+$. The <linear map>
$$
V_-\longrightarrow V_+^*,\qquad f\longmapsto\bigl(e\longmapsto\Omega(e,f)\bigr)
$$
is an <linear isomorphism>. Let $f_j$ correspond to the <dual basis> of the $e_i$. Then $\Omega(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}$, and the pairings within each summand vanish. Thus
$$
\boxed{(e_1,\ldots,e_n,f_1,\ldots,f_n)\text{ is a symplectic basis},\quad Se_i=e_i,\quad Sf_i=-f_i.}
$$

For $p\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)$, differentiation gives $(d\sigma_p)^2=I$ and $(d\sigma_p)^*\omega_p=-\omega_p$. Thus $d\sigma_p$ is an <anti-symplectic involution> of the <symplectic vector space> $T_pM$. By part (a), its $+1$ <eigenspace> is a <Lagrangian subspace>.

In the local <manifold chart> supplied in the question, $\sigma$ is linear, so its <fixed-point set> is the intersection of the chart with this $+1$ <eigenspace>. Therefore the <fixed-point set> is an <embedded submanifold> and
$$
T_p\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma)=\ker(d\sigma_p-I).
$$
Every such <tangent space> has half the ambient dimension and the <symplectic form> vanishes on it. Hence \b[every nonempty <fixed-point set> is a <Lagrangian submanifold>]. The <fixed-point set> itself need not be <connected>.

Let $\sigma(z)=\overline z$, componentwise <complex conjugation>. Interpret the printed $|z^2|$ in the potential as $|z|^2=\sum_j|z_j|^2$. With $D=1+|z|^2$, the local <Fubini-Study form> is
$$
\widetilde\omega_{FS}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j,k}
\left(\frac{\delta_{jk}}D-\frac{\overline z_jz_k}{D^2}\right)dz_j\wedge d\overline z_k.
$$
Its <pullback of a differential form> under $\sigma$ is
$$
\sigma^*\widetilde\omega_{FS}
=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j,k}
\left(\frac{\delta_{jk}}D-\frac{z_j\overline z_k}{D^2}\right)d\overline z_j\wedge dz_k
=-\widetilde\omega_{FS},
$$
where the last equality swaps $j,k$ and uses antisymmetry of the <wedge product of differential forms>. Since $\sigma^2=I$, it is an <anti-symplectic involution>, with <fixed-point set> $\mathbb R^n$. Part (c) gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbb R^n\subset(\mathbb C^n,\widetilde\omega_{FS})\text{ is Lagrangian}.}
$$

As usual for <complex projective space> and <real projective space>, <homogeneous coordinates> are represented by nonzero vectors. Componentwise <complex conjugation> descends to the <diffeomorphism>
$$
\tau:\mathbb{CP}^n\longrightarrow\mathbb{CP}^n,\qquad [z]\longmapsto[\overline z].
$$
It is well defined because $\overline{\lambda z}=\overline\lambda\,\overline z$, and $\tau^2=I$. Every standard <manifold chart> is invariant under $\tau$, with $\varphi_i\circ\tau=\sigma\circ\varphi_i$. Part (d) and the gluing of the <Fubini-Study form> therefore imply $\tau^*\omega_{FS}=-\omega_{FS}$.

A point $[z]$ in the <fixed-point set> satisfies $\overline z=\lambda z$. Applying <complex conjugation> again gives $|\lambda|=1$. Choose $a\ne0$ with $a/\overline a=\lambda$; then $\overline{az}=az$, so $[z]$ has a real representative. Conversely every real representative determines a point in the <fixed-point set>. Hence $\operatorname{Fix}(\tau)=\mathbb{RP}^n$, and part (c) gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbb{RP}^n\subset(\mathbb{CP}^n,\omega_{FS})\text{ is a Lagrangian submanifold}.}
$$

Take the usual convention that the <smooth isotopy> starts at $\rho_0=\mathrm{id}$. The <exterior derivative> commutes with a <pullback of a differential form>, so
$$
d(\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda)
=\rho_t^*d\lambda-d\lambda
=\omega-\rho_t^*\omega.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda\text{ is closed for every }t
\iff \rho_t^*\omega=\omega\text{ for every }t.}
$$
The right-hand condition says precisely that $\rho_t$ is a <symplectic isotopy>.

Here an <exact symplectic manifold> is necessarily noncompact in positive dimension: if $\dim M=2n$ and $\omega=-d\lambda$, then $\omega^n=-d(\lambda\wedge\omega^{n-1})$, contradicting the positive <symplectic volume> and the <generalized Stokes theorem> on a <compact> <smooth manifold> without boundary. Thus the paper's introductory word “closed” must here be read as “without boundary”, as its parenthesis suggests, rather than as including <compactness>.

Use the <Hamiltonian vector field> convention $\iota_{X_t}\omega=dH_t$, which is forced by the sign of the formula in this part. This is the opposite of the default sign in the linked general article. Since $d\lambda=-\omega$, <Cartan's magic formula> gives
$$
\mathcal L_{X_t}\lambda
=d(\iota_{X_t}\lambda)+\iota_{X_t}d\lambda
=d(\iota_{X_t}\lambda-H_t).
$$
Differentiate the <pullback of a differential form> along the <smooth isotopy> and integrate from $0$ to $t$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda
&=\int_0^t\rho_s^*\mathcal L_{X_s}\lambda\,ds\\
&=d\int_0^t(\iota_{X_s}\lambda-H_s)\circ\rho_s\,ds.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus the required <exact differential form> has the explicit primitive
$$
\boxed{F_t=\int_0^t(\iota_{X_s}\lambda-H_s)\circ\rho_s\,ds,\qquad\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda=dF_t.}
$$

Differentiating the assumed primitive identity and using <Cartan's magic formula> gives
$$
d\dot F_t=\rho_t^*\mathcal L_{X_t}\lambda
=\rho_t^*\bigl(d(\iota_{X_t}\lambda)-\iota_{X_t}\omega\bigr).
$$
Apply the <pullback of a differential form> by $\rho_t^{-1}$ and rearrange:
$$
\boxed{\iota_{X_t}\omega=dH_t,\qquad
H_t=\iota_{X_t}\lambda-\dot F_t\circ\rho_t^{-1}.}
$$
This smooth family of <Hamiltonian functions> proves that $\rho_t$ is a <Hamiltonian isotopy> in the convention of part (b).

To connect this with the preceding assertion that every $\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda$ is an <exact differential form>, the smooth choice of primitives causes no additional obstruction. Fix $p_0\in M$ and normalize $F_t(p_0)=0$. Define $F_t(p)$ by the <line integral> of $\rho_t^*\lambda-\lambda$ from $p_0$ to $p$. The <fundamental theorem for line integrals> makes this independent of the path. Near any $p$, use one fixed path to a nearby chart center followed by coordinate line segments; this expression is smooth jointly in $t,p$. Thus these normalized primitives form the required smooth family.

Let $\pi:T^*X\to X$ be the <cotangent bundle> projection and $\lambda$ its <canonical one-form on a cotangent bundle>, defined by $\lambda_{(x,\xi)}(v)=\xi(d\pi(v))$. Use the <symplectic form> $\omega=-d\lambda$, so in local coordinates $\omega=\sum_jdx_j\wedge d\xi_j$.

The graph map $s_\beta(x)=(x,\beta_x)$ is a <smooth embedding>, with $s_\beta^*\lambda=\beta$, and hence
$$
s_\beta^*\omega=-d\beta.
$$
Its image has dimension $\dim X$, half the dimension of $T^*X$. By the definition of a <Lagrangian submanifold>,
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{graph}(\beta)\text{ is Lagrangian}\iff d\beta=0.}
$$
This is the <graph of a closed one-form is Lagrangian> criterion. The opposite conventional sign for the <symplectic form> gives the same criterion.

The <cotangent fiber translation> $f$ is a <diffeomorphism>, with inverse $(x,\xi)\mapsto(x,\xi-\beta_x)$. Because $\pi\circ f=\pi$, the definition of the <canonical one-form on a cotangent bundle> gives

A <compatible almost complex structure> is a smooth bundle map $J:TM\to TM$ such that, for all <tangent vectors> $u,v$,
$$
\boxed{J^2=-I,\qquad\omega(Ju,Jv)=\omega(u,v),\qquad\omega(u,Ju)>0\quad(u\ne0).}
$$
It determines the <Riemannian metric>
$$
\boxed{g(u,v)=\omega(u,Jv).}
$$
Indeed, the invariance of the <symplectic form> under $J$ and its antisymmetry imply $\omega(v,Ju)=\omega(u,Jv)$, so $g$ is symmetric; the positivity condition makes it positive definite. A <compatible triple> consists of this <symplectic form>, this <compatible almost complex structure>, and this <Riemannian metric>. Useful equivalent identities are $\omega(u,v)=g(Ju,v)$ and $g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v)$.

At a point $p$, write $W=T_pL$ and take the <orthogonal complement> with respect to the <Riemannian metric> $g$. The <compatible triple> identity gives
$$
g(Ju,v)=\omega(u,v).
$$
If $L$ is a <Lagrangian submanifold>, then $\omega(u,v)=0$ for $u,v\in W$, so $JW\subset W^\perp$. Both have dimension $\frac12\dim M$, hence equality.

Conversely, if $JW=W^\perp$, equality of dimensions gives $\dim W=\frac12\dim M$, and $g(Ju,v)=0$ for $u,v\in W$ gives $\omega|_W=0$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{L\text{ is Lagrangian}\iff J(T_pL)=(T_pL)^\perp\text{ for every }p.}
$$
A positive-definite <inner product> has $W\cap W^\perp=\{0\}$. Hence $W\cap JW=\{0\}$, proving that \b[every <Lagrangian submanifold> is a <totally real submanifold> for a <compatible almost complex structure>].

For $X\in\mathfrak h$, the <fundamental vector field> of the restricted <Lie group action> is $(iX)_M$. If $\phi$ is the original <moment map>, then
$$
d\langle\pi\circ\phi,X\rangle_H
=d\langle\phi,iX\rangle_G
=\iota_{(iX)_M}\omega
=\iota_{X_M}\omega.
$$
This is the defining <Hamiltonian action> identity, in the convention $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$.

If equivariance is included in the definition of a <moment map>, it is also preserved: the <dual map> $\pi=i^*$ intertwines the <coadjoint actions> of $H$, since $i(\operatorname{Ad}_hX)=\operatorname{Ad}_h(iX)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\phi_H=i^*\phi=\pi\circ\phi}
$$
is a <moment map> for the restricted <Hamiltonian action> of the <Lie subgroup> $H$.

Use the paper's <Fubini-Study form> normalization $\widetilde\omega_{FS}=\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline\partial\log(1+|z|^2)$, the <Hamiltonian vector field> convention $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, and the <Lie algebra> generator whose <circle> action is $e^{i\theta}$. $$
\boxed{\phi([z_0,z_1,z_2])=-\frac1{2D}\bigl(|z_1|^2,|z_2|^2\bigr).}
$$
The <moment map> image is the filled triangle
$$
\boxed{\phi(\mathbb{CP}^2)=\{(u,v):u\leq0,\ v\leq0,\ u+v\geq-\tfrac12\}.}
$$
Indeed, the three quantities $|z_j|^2/D$ are nonnegative and add to one, and any such triple is realized by choosing their square roots as <homogeneous coordinates>.

For the diagonal <circle> action, a point in the <fixed-point set> satisfies $[z_0,tz_1,tz_2]=[z_0,z_1,z_2]$ for every $t\in S^1$. If $z_0\ne0$, the projective scaling must be one, forcing $z_1=z_2=0$. If $z_0=0$, all coordinates are scaled together. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Fix}(S^1)=\{[1,0,0]\}\ \sqcup\ \{[0,z_1,z_2]\}\cong\{\mathrm{point}\}\sqcup\mathbb{CP}^1.}
$$
By part (b), the diagonal <moment map> is
$$
\boxed{\mu([z])=-\frac{|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2}{2D},\qquad\mu(\mathbb{CP}^2)=[-\tfrac12,0].}
$$
The factor $1/2$ follows from the specified <Fubini-Study form>, not the integral normalization in the linked general article. For example, in one affine complex coordinate $w=re^{i\theta}$ the <symplectic form> is $r(1+r^2)^{-2}dr\wedge d\theta$, whose <interior product of a differential form> with $\partial_\theta$ is $-r(1+r^2)^{-2}dr=d[-r^2/(2(1+r^2))]$. Choosing the generator $e^{2\pi it}$ rescales all <moment maps> by $2\pi$; reversing the defining sign reverses their signs. The negative level in parts (d) and (e) uses the convention displayed here.

\boxed{(T_pN)^\omega=\mathbb RX_p\subset T_pN.}

Thus \b[the level set is a <coisotropic submanifold>, neither an <isotropic submanifold> nor a <Lagrangian submanifold>]. The level equation is $|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2=|z_0|^2$. In particular $z_0\ne0$, so the global affine coordinates on this level are $w_j=z_j/z_0$. They identify it with
$$
N=\{(w_1,w_2)\in\mathbb C^2:|w_1|^2+|w_2|^2=1\}=S^3.
$$
The diagonal <circle> action becomes $(w_1,w_2)\mapsto(tw_1,tw_2)$ and is free: at least one coordinate is nonzero, so fixing a point forces $t=1$. Since the <circle> is <compact>, the <free proper Lie-group action theorem> makes the quotient a smooth two-dimensional <smooth manifold>.

The map $(w_1,w_2)\mapsto[w_1,w_2]$ is the <Hopf fibration>. It is onto the <complex projective line>, and two unit representatives determine the same complex line precisely when they differ by a scalar of modulus one, hence by a <circle> orbit. Local sections, for example $[1,u]\mapsto(1,u)/\sqrt{1+|u|^2}$, show that the induced identification is a <diffeomorphism>. Therefore
$$
\boxed{M_{\mathrm{red}}=\mu^{-1}(-1/4)/S^1\cong\mathbb{CP}^1\cong S^2.}
$$
This identifies the <symplectic reduction> as a <smooth manifold>; no computation of its reduced <symplectic form> is needed.
