= Solution

A <compact H-hull> is a bounded relatively closed set $K\subset\mathbb H$ whose complement $D=\mathbb H\setminus K$ is a <simply connected domain>. Equivalently one can retain its compact closure in $\overline{\mathbb H}$; real <boundary> points do not affect the <mapping-out function>. The <Riemann mapping theorem> and <Schwarz reflection principle> near infinity give a unique <conformal map> $g_K:D\to\mathbb H$ with <hydrodynamic normalization at infinity>. Its <Laurent series> is
$$
g_K(z)=z+\frac{a_K}{z}+O(|z|^{-2}),\qquad a_K\in\mathbb R.
$$
The <half-plane capacity> is **$\operatorname{hcap}(K)=a_K$**. The missing constant term is part of the normalization, not a choice to be made again when comparing different hulls.

Here are the <conformal map> facts we use to justify positivity and comparison. The inverse $f_K:\mathbb H\to D\subset\mathbb H$ has expansion $f_K(w)=w-a_K/w+O(w^{-2})$. The upper-half-plane <Schwarz-Pick theorem>, applied to $w$ and $iY$ and then letting $Y\to\infty$, implies $\operatorname{Im}f_K(w)\geq\operatorname{Im}w$. Indeed the inequality between pseudohyperbolic distances gives
$$
\frac{4\operatorname{Im}f_K(w)\operatorname{Im}f_K(iY)}{|f_K(w)-\overline{f_K(iY)}|^2}
\geq\frac{4Y\operatorname{Im}w}{|w+iY|^2},
$$
and multiplying by $Y$ gives the height comparison in the limit. At $w=iY$, the inverse expansion reads $\operatorname{Im}f_K(iY)=Y+a_K/Y+O(Y^{-2})$, so $a_K\geq0$.

For $r>0$, uniqueness of the <mapping-out function> gives
$$
g_{rK}(z)=r g_K(z/r)
=z+\frac{r^2a_K}{z}+O(z^{-2}).
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{hcap}(rK)=r^2\operatorname{hcap}(K).}
$$
Translation by a real number $b$ similarly gives $g_{K+b}(z)=b+g_K(z-b)$ and leaves <half-plane capacity> unchanged.

If $K\subset A$ are <compact H-hulls>, their <mapping-out functions> compose as $g_A=g_L\circ g_K$, where $L$ is the filled image of $A\setminus K$. Comparing <Laurent series> yields
$$
\operatorname{hcap}(A)=\operatorname{hcap}(K)+\operatorname{hcap}(L)\geq\operatorname{hcap}(K).
$$
This proves <monotonicity of half-plane capacity>, rather than assuming it from inclusion alone. The filled half-disc $A=\{z\in\mathbb H:|z-b|\leq R\}$ has <mapping-out function>
$$
g_A(z)=z+\frac{R^2}{z-b},
$$
which takes its semicircular <boundary> to the real line and is a <conformal bijection> onto $\mathbb H$. Thus its <half-plane capacity> is $R^2$. Enclose $K$ in such a half-disc, compare capacities, and minimize over real centres and radii. This proves the sharp bound
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{hcap}(K)\leq\operatorname{rad}(K)^2.}
$$
If one defines the radius by an infimum, take $R$ strictly larger than that infimum and then let $R$ decrease; no attainment assumption is needed.

We use the following standard <planar Brownian motion> facts: its exit <probability distribution> is <harmonic measure>; <conformal maps> preserve that <probability distribution> after an increasing time change; and the <Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane> gives its real-boundary exit density. For rough hulls the <boundary> is interpreted through <prime ends>, with <boundary> correspondence at harmonic-measure-almost every exit point. This avoids imposing a smoothness or local-connectedness assumption on $K$.

Under $g_K$, the exit event through $K$ corresponds to a measurable set $E_K\subset\mathbb R$ of <boundary> images. It is bounded: the <mapping-out function> extends by <Schwarz reflection> along the real line outside a sufficiently large interval enclosing $K$, and its <boundary> near infinity corresponds to the original real <boundary> near infinity. All <prime ends> representing $K$ therefore have images in a bounded interval. The <boundary> correspondence assertion can equivalently be formulated using the almost-everywhere limits of $g_K^{-1}$; changing null sets does not change $E_K$'s measure.

Write $g_K(iy)=u_y+iv_y$. The <hydrodynamic normalization at infinity> gives $u_y=O(y^{-2})$ and $v_y=y-a_K/y+O(y^{-2})$. By <conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion> and the <Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane>,
$$
\mathbb P_{iy}(B_T\in K)=\frac1\pi\int_{E_K}\frac{v_y}{(s-u_y)^2+v_y^2}\,ds.
$$
On the bounded set $E_K$, the integrand multiplied by $y$ tends uniformly to one. Therefore the limit exists and is finite:
$$
\boxed{c(K)=\frac{|E_K|}{\pi}.}
$$
This is the <harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane> with the normalization $c(K)=\operatorname{cap}(K)/\pi$; it is different from <half-plane capacity>. If real <boundary> points are retained in the definition of $K$, include their <boundary> images in $E_K$ as well; the argument is unchanged.

For the scaling statement, <Brownian scaling> says that $r^{-1}B_{r^2t}$ started from $iy/r$ is again standard <planar Brownian motion>. The exit event scales with the hull, so
$$
\mathbb P_{iy}(B_T\in rK)
=\mathbb P_{i(y/r)}(B_T\in K).
$$
Set $v=y/r$, multiply by $y=rv$ and take the limit. It follows that
$$
\boxed{c(rK)=r\,c(K).}
$$
The same conclusion follows geometrically from $E_{rK}=rE_K$ and the formula above.

First derive the <Brownian representation of half-plane capacity> with the chosen normalization. Set
$$
h(z)=\operatorname{Im}(z-g_K(z)),\qquad z\in\mathbb H\setminus K.
$$
The height comparison from part (a), applied to $f_K=g_K^{-1}$, gives $h\geq0$. On the <boundary>, $\operatorname{Im}g_K$ tends to zero; on $K$ the limiting value of $h$ is therefore $\operatorname{Im}z$, and on the real <boundary> it is zero. To see the asserted boundary-height limit without assuming a smooth hull, suppose a bounded sequence approaching the <boundary> had mapped images with imaginary part bounded away from zero. The inverse's expansion near infinity prevents these images from being unbounded, and an interior subsequential limit would map back to an interior point of $D$, a contradiction.

At infinity $h$ tends to zero by the <Laurent series>. The <maximum principle for harmonic functions> now bounds $h$ between zero and $R=\operatorname{rad}(K)$, since every point of $K$ has imaginary part at most $R$. Apply the <optional sampling theorem> to the bounded <harmonic function> $h(B_{t\wedge T})$. The exit time $T$ is almost surely finite because it is no larger than the first time the imaginary coordinate of <planar Brownian motion> reaches zero. Taking $t\to\infty$ gives
$$
h(z)=\mathbb E_z[\operatorname{Im}B_T].
$$
This uses the bounded harmonic difference, not optional sampling of the unbounded imaginary coordinate at an unbounded <stopping time>.

At $z=iy$, the expansion gives $yh(iy)\to a_K$. Since
$$
0\leq\operatorname{Im}B_T\leq R\,\mathbf1_{\{B_T\in K\}},
$$
we obtain
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{hcap}(K)=\lim_{y\to\infty}y\mathbb E_{iy}[\operatorname{Im}B_T]
\leq\operatorname{rad}(K)c(K).}
$$
Thus the quadratic <half-plane capacity> is controlled by a length times the linearly scaling <harmonic capacity from infinity in the upper half-plane>. The factor $1/\pi$ is essential if the alternative normalization $\operatorname{cap}(K)=\pi c(K)$ is used.

Write $g_t=g_{K_t}$ and, for $s<t$, let $K_{s,t}$ be the filled image hull $g_s(K_t\setminus K_s)$. The <half-plane-capacity composition rule> gives
$$
g_t=g_{K_{s,t}}\circ g_s,\qquad\operatorname{hcap}(K_{s,t})=2(t-s).
$$
The <Loewner local growth property> says that these mapped increments shrink uniformly on bounded time intervals: for every $A<\infty$,
$$
\omega_A(h):=\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq A,\ t-s\leq h}\operatorname{rad}(K_{s,t})\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{as }h\downarrow0.
$$
Using <diameter> instead of radius is equivalent, since a nonempty <compact H-hull> has its closure meeting the real axis. This is a condition on the increments after mapping out the past, not merely on the Euclidean size of $K_t\setminus K_s$ in the original domain.

For fixed $t$, the nonempty compact sets $\overline{K_{t,t+h}}$, $h>0$, are nested as $h$ decreases. Their diameters tend to zero by <Loewner local growth property>. The nested-compact-set property gives a single intersection point, and its imaginary part is zero because $K_{t,t+h}$ lies in a real-centred disc of radius tending to zero. Define the <Loewner transform> by
$$
\boxed{\{\xi_t\}=\bigcap_{h>0}\overline{K_{t,t+h}},\qquad\xi_t\in\mathbb R.}
$$
One may restrict the intersection to sufficiently small $h$. Every point of $K_{t,t+h}$ is within $2\omega_A(h)$ of $\xi_t$ when $t+h\leq A$.

Here is a direct proof of <continuity>, using the permitted displacement estimate. Fix $s<t$ and put $\delta=t-s$. Take $0<h\leq\delta$ and a point $v\in K_{t,t+h}$. Set $z=g_{K_{s,t}}^{-1}(v)$. The hull composition rule puts $z\in K_{s,t+h}$, while the displacement estimate gives
$$
|v-z|\leq C\operatorname{rad}(K_{s,t})\leq C\omega_A(\delta).
$$
Since $z$ is within $2\omega_A(\delta+h)$ of $\xi_s$, let $h\downarrow0$, taking $v\to\xi_t$, to obtain
$$
\boxed{|\xi_t-\xi_s|\leq C\omega_A(\delta)+2\omega_A(2\delta).}
$$
Use a slightly larger fixed time interval if $t$ is its endpoint. Both terms tend to zero with $\delta$. This proves <continuity> from both sides, and indeed <uniform continuity> on every compact time interval. The real <boundary> value is obtained as a limit; evaluating the displacement estimate directly on a possibly rough <boundary> is unnecessary.

The <Loewner correspondence theorem> states that a capacity-$2t$ increasing hull family with <Loewner local growth property> has the unique continuous real <Loewner driving function> just defined, and its <mapping-out functions> solve the <chordal Loewner equation>
$$
\boxed{\partial_tg_t(z)=\frac2{g_t(z)-\xi_t},\qquad g_0(z)=z.}
$$
For each $z\in\mathbb H$, this solution is taken up to its maximal lifetime; $\mathbb H\setminus K_t$ is precisely the set of points whose lifetimes exceed $t$. Conversely a continuous real driver gives such a hull family through this <ordinary differential equation>. A continuous driver alone need not have a continuous <Loewner trace>; that additional assertion is part of the trace theory of <SLE>, not of the deterministic correspondence theorem.

A continuous process $\gamma:[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb H}$ is chordal <Schramm–Loewner evolution> with parameter $\kappa$ from $0$ to infinity when $\gamma_0=0$, its filled past generates these capacity-$2t$ hulls, and their <Loewner driving function> is
$$
\boxed{\xi_t=\sqrt\kappa\,B_t,}
$$
with $B$ standard real <Brownian motion>. The filling includes bounded components cut off from infinity. Equivalently its <Loewner trace> is the continuous extension of $\gamma_t=\lim_{y\downarrow0}g_t^{-1}(\xi_t+iy)$. This last <boundary> limit is the trace-existence input for <SLE>.

Fix $r>0$ and scale space and time together:
$$
\widehat K_t=r^{-1}K_{r^2t},\qquad
\widehat g_t(z)=r^{-1}g_{r^2t}(rz),\qquad
\widehat\xi_t=r^{-1}\xi_{r^2t}.
$$
The <half-plane capacity> of $\widehat K_t$ is $2t$ by part (a). Differentiation gives
$$
\partial_t\widehat g_t(z)=\frac2{\widehat g_t(z)-\widehat\xi_t}.
$$
By <Brownian scaling>, $r^{-1}B_{r^2t}$ is again standard <Brownian motion>, so the new driver has the same law as the old one. Uniqueness of the <chordal Loewner equation> transfers this equality to the hulls and their continuous <Loewner traces>. Thus
$$
\boxed{(r^{-1}\gamma_{r^2t})_{t\geq0}\ \overset{d}=\ (\gamma_t)_{t\geq0}.}
$$
This is <capacity-parametrized scale invariance of a Loewner chain>. It includes $\kappa=0$, when the driver is zero and the trace is the deterministic vertical slit $\gamma_t=2i\sqrt t$.

= Solution

The common <Brownian motion> cancels from the difference $D_t=Y_t-X_t$. Before either lifetime,
$$
dD_t=a\left(\frac1{Y_t}-\frac1{X_t}\right)dt
=-\frac{aD_t}{X_tY_t}\,dt,
\qquad
D_t=(y-x)\exp\left(-a\int_0^t\frac{ds}{X_sY_s}\right)>0.
$$
Hence $0<X_t<Y_t$ and $\sigma\leq\tau$: if $Y$ reached zero first, the positive smaller solution $X$ could not persist beyond that time. Write
$$
\Theta_t=\frac{D_t}{Y_t}=1-\frac{X_t}{Y_t}\in(0,1),\qquad t<\sigma.
$$
Using the <Itô formula> for $Y^{-1}$ gives
$$
d(Y_t^{-1})=-Y_t^{-2}dB_t+(1-a)Y_t^{-3}dt.
$$
The difference has <finite variation>, so there is no cross-variation term in the <Itô product rule> for $D/Y$. Since $X=Y(1-\Theta)$,
$$
\boxed{d\Theta_t=-\frac{\Theta_t}{Y_t}dB_t
+\frac{\Theta_t}{Y_t^2}\left(1-a-\frac a{1-\Theta_t}\right)dt.}
$$
For $N_t=\chi(\Theta_t)$, the <Itô formula> therefore makes its drift vanish exactly when
$$
\frac12\theta^2\chi''(\theta)
+\theta\left(1-a-\frac a{1-\theta}\right)\chi'(\theta)=0.
$$
A nonconstant increasing <scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion> satisfies
$$
\frac{\chi''(\theta)}{\chi'(\theta)}
=-\frac{2-4a}{\theta}+\frac{2a}{1-\theta},
\qquad
\chi'(\theta)=C\theta^{4a-2}(1-\theta)^{-2a}.
$$
The assumptions $a>1/4$ and $a<1/2$ are precisely the integrability conditions at zero and one. Normalize the scale function by its endpoints:
$$
\boxed{\chi(\theta)=\phi(\theta)=
\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\theta u^{4a-2}(1-u)^{-2a}\,du}
{\displaystyle\int_0^1 u^{4a-2}(1-u)^{-2a}\,du}
=I_\theta(4a-1,1-2a).}
$$
Here $I$ is the regularized <incomplete beta function>. The normalized function is continuous and strictly increasing on $[0,1]$, with $\phi(0)=0$ and $\phi(1)=1$. We have obtained the <local martingale>
$$
\boxed{dN_t=-\frac{\Theta_t\phi'(\Theta_t)}{Y_t}\,dB_t,\qquad0\leq N_t\leq1.}
$$

The terminal identification needs care; mere boundedness does not by itself say what happens when both lifetimes coincide. Stop first on compact subsets of $0<X<Y$, and then exhaust the interval $[0,\sigma)$. The <bounded local martingale criterion> makes $N$ a bounded <martingale> with a limit $N_{\sigma-}$, and <optional sampling theorem> gives $\mathbb E[N_{\sigma-}]=N_0$. For completeness, applying the <Itô formula> to each stopped $N^2$ bounds the expected <quadratic variation> by one. <Monotone convergence> over the localization sequence implies
$$
[N]_{\sigma-}=\int_0^\sigma\frac{\Theta_t^2\phi'(\Theta_t)^2}{Y_t^2}\,dt<\infty
\quad\text{almost surely}.
$$
Since $\phi^{-1}$ is continuous on $[0,1]$, $\Theta_t$ itself has a limit $\Theta_{\sigma-}$.

On $\{\sigma<\tau\}$, $Y_\sigma>0$ while $X_t\to0$, so $\Theta_{\sigma-}=1$ and $N_{\sigma-}=1$. On $\{\sigma=\tau\}$, the supplied divergent clock is
$$
\int_0^\sigma\frac{dt}{Y_t^2}=\infty.
$$
If $\Theta_{\sigma-}>0$, then eventually $\Theta_t\phi'(\Theta_t)$ is bounded below by a positive constant. This remains true if its limit is one: $\phi'(\theta)$ diverges there, rather than tending to zero. The divergent clock would then force $[N]_{\sigma-}=\infty$, contradicting the finite <quadratic variation>. Thus $\Theta_{\sigma-}=0$ and $N_{\sigma-}=0$ on the simultaneous-lifetime event. We have proved
$$
N_{\sigma-}=\mathbf1_{\{\sigma<\tau\}}.
$$
Taking expectations yields the complete <probability> formula
$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(\sigma<\tau)=\phi\!\left(\frac{y-x}{y}\right).}
$$
This is the <beta integral for strict ordering of coupled Bessel lifetimes>; the complementary <probability> is the <probability> of simultaneous swallowing, not of the larger initial point dying first.

To connect the calculation to <SLE>, let its driver be $W_t=\sqrt\kappa\,\beta_t$. For a positive <boundary> point $r$ before its <boundary swallowing time>, put $V_t^r=(g_t(r)-W_t)/\sqrt\kappa$. The <chordal Loewner equation> gives
$$
dV_t^r=-d\beta_t+\frac{2/\kappa}{V_t^r}\,dt.
$$
Thus two positive <boundary> points give exactly the coupled <Bessel processes> above, with $B=-\beta$ and $a=2/\kappa$. The interval $1/4<a<1/2$ corresponds to $4<\kappa<8$. Their <boundary swallowing times> obey
$$
\boxed{\mathbb P(T_x<T_y)=I_{1-x/y}\left(\frac8\kappa-1,1-\frac4\kappa\right),\qquad0<x<y.}
$$
The formula quantifies whether the growing hull separates one <boundary> point from infinity before the other, or swallows them together inside the same <boundary> interval. It does not identify swallowing with visiting each point. At $\kappa=6$, the two beta parameters are $1/3$ and this becomes the <Cardy boundary crossing formula> in the corresponding cross-ratio coordinate, explaining its role in the <conformal invariance of planar percolation>.

The <conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane> with the indicated three <boundary> values is
$$
\boxed{\Phi(z)=\frac z{z+1}.}
$$
Its <Möbius transformation> matrix has determinant one, so it preserves $\mathbb H$. The inverse is $\Phi^{-1}(w)=w/(1-w)$. In particular
$$
\Phi\bigl(( -\infty,-1]\bigr)=(1,\infty]
$$
on the extended real <boundary>. Therefore the image trace's first contact with the latter ray corresponds to the original time $S$. The map sends the original target at infinity to the new target $1$; it does not preserve the target of the chordal curve.

We use the image <half-plane capacity> as its time parameter:
$$
u(t)=\frac12\operatorname{hcap}(K_t^*)=\int_0^t\Phi_s'(\xi_s)^2\,ds,\qquad t<S.
$$
Since $\Phi_t$ is a <conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane> analytic at $\xi_t$, its real <derivative> there is strictly positive. Hence $u$ is continuous and strictly increasing before $S$. The original time $t$ and image time $u$ must be kept distinct throughout the comparison.

By the tip correspondence in the conjugacy,
$$
\xi_t^*=\Phi_t(\xi_t),\qquad \xi_t=\sqrt6\,B_t.
$$
The time-dependent <Itô formula> is valid after localization away from the pole and the terminal time $S$. The map's time variation is of <finite variation>, so there is no extra stochastic cross-variation term. Using the supplied conjugacy <derivative>,
$$
\begin{aligned}
d\xi_t^*&=\Phi_t'(\xi_t)\sqrt6\,dB_t
+\left(\dot\Phi_t(\xi_t)+3\Phi_t''(\xi_t)\right)dt\\
&=\sqrt6\,\Phi_t'(\xi_t)dB_t.
\end{aligned}
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\xi_t^*\text{ is a continuous local martingale on }[0,S),\qquad
[\xi^*]_t=6u(t).}
$$
The cancellation is special to $\kappa=6$: for a general <SLE> parameter the drift would be $(\kappa/2-3)\Phi_t''(\xi_t)dt$. This is the <transformed SLE driving function>, and explains the special <locality property of SLE> at six.

Let $t(u)$ be the inverse image-capacity clock. Part (a) and the <quadratic variation of a stochastic integral> give
$$
\widetilde\xi_u=\xi^*_{t(u)},\qquad [\widetilde\xi]_u=6u.
$$
The <Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem>, or the <Lévy characterization of Brownian motion>, now shows that $\widetilde\xi_u/\sqrt6$ is standard <Brownian motion> up to the terminal image time. If that terminal clock is finite, it can be extended by a fresh Brownian continuation; this does not assert anything about the subsequent image trace. The initial value is zero because $\Phi(0)=0$.

Differentiating the image <Loewner chain> with respect to its own <half-plane capacity> time gives
$$
\partial_u\widetilde g_u(z)=\frac2{\widetilde g_u(z)-\widetilde\xi_u},\qquad
\widetilde g_u=g^*_{t(u)}.
$$
Thus, until its first hit of $[1,\infty)$, the reparametrized image $\Phi(\gamma)$ has the ordinary chordal <SLE> law from $0$ to infinity. This follows from the uniqueness of the <chordal Loewner equation> for a given continuous driver; the continuous <Loewner trace> is recovered from the inverse-map limit. Localization can be removed by exhausting times strictly before this <boundary> contact. The terminal event is defined from the trace itself, so equality of the stopped trace laws follows as well.

On the other hand, the definition of <conformal invariance of SLE> says that the full unparameterized image $\Phi(\gamma)$ has the law of chordal $\operatorname{SLE}_6$ from $\Phi(0)=0$ to $\Phi(\infty)=1$, namely the law of $\gamma'$. Its stopping event at $[1,\infty)$ is exactly the image of the original stopping event at $(-\infty,-1]$. Therefore the same stopped image has both laws: ordinary $\operatorname{SLE}_6$ from $0$ to infinity stopped at $T$, and $\operatorname{SLE}_6$ from $0$ to $1$ stopped at $T'$. We conclude
$$
\boxed{(\gamma_t:0\leq t<T)\ \overset d=\ (\gamma'_t:0\leq t<T')
\quad\text{as curves up to increasing reparametrization}.}
$$
The statement does not identify $T$ with $T'$ in their original clocks. It is <target-change locality of SLE6>, derived from the zero drift and capacity time change rather than assumed as a theorem.

= Solution
{parent=c}

The two curves have different destinations, so the equality of their initial segments is not an equality of their complete laws. At the first contact with $[1,\infty)$, the initial hull separates the target $1$ from infinity. Except for the null event of hitting the fixed point $1$ itself, this happens by a contact at a real point strictly larger than $1$. The fact that a fixed nonzero real point is almost surely not visited is the <SLE does not hit a fixed nonzero boundary point> property; it is consistent with the strictly positive <probability> of swallowing an entire interval at once in Question 3.

The original curve, whose target is infinity, continues in the closure of the unbounded surviving component. The curve targeted at $1$ continues in the surviving component whose <boundary> contains its target, which is now separated from infinity. Its domain normalization must follow that component. Ultimately one curve tends to infinity and the other to $1$. This is <target-dependent continuation of SLE6>.

The precise failure of the argument is the loss of the conformal conjugacy between the same surviving components. In the transformed construction, at time $S$ the pole $-1$ is swallowed; its image is infinity. The composition $g_t^*\circ\Phi\circ g_t^{-1}$ was a <conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane> only while both maps described corresponding surviving domains. After the <boundary> contact, the image of the original target-containing domain is the component containing $1$, whereas $g_t^*$ in the ordinary half-plane-capacity convention maps out the hull relative to the component containing infinity. These are no longer the same component. Equivalently, the swallowed pole collides with the growing hull, and the analytic-neighbourhood assumption at the <Loewner driving function> cannot be continued by the preceding argument.

Hence neither the given analytic identities nor the <Brownian motion> description obtained from their capacity clock licenses a continuation of the comparison beyond the <stopping time>. The zero drift before contact does not remove this geometric obstruction. The restriction to pre-contact segments is essential, even though the curves can continue in their respective target components.
