= Solution

Use signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and the <Riemann curvature tensor> convention $[\nabla_a,\nabla_b]X^c=R^c{}_{dab}X^d$. Lower the index of the <Killing vector field> and set $T_{abc}=\nabla_a\nabla_bV_c$. Differentiating the <Killing equation> gives $T_{abc}=-T_{acb}$, while the <Ricci identity> gives
$$
T_{abc}-T_{bac}=-R^d{}_{cab}V_d.
$$
Define $S_{abc}=R_{cbad}V^d$. The first-pair antisymmetry of the <Riemann curvature tensor> gives $S_{abc}=-S_{acb}$. The pair symmetry and the <first Bianchi identity> give
$$
S_{abc}-S_{bac}=(R_{cbad}-R_{cabd})V^d=-R_{dcab}V^d.
$$
Thus $D=T-S$ is symmetric in its first two indices and antisymmetric in its last two. These two symmetries force it to vanish:
$$
D_{abc}=D_{bac}=-D_{bca}=-D_{cba}=D_{cab}=D_{acb}=-D_{abc}.
$$
Consequently $T_{abc}=R_{cbad}V^d$. Raising $c$ using <metric compatibility> proves the required <second covariant derivative of a Killing vector>:
$$
\boxed{\nabla_a\nabla_bV^c=R^c{}_{bad}V^d.}
$$

The <Levi-Civita connection> has symmetric <Christoffel symbols>, so antisymmetrization removes its connection terms. Hence the <Papapetrou electromagnetic field> is
$$
F_{ab}=\nabla_aV_b-\nabla_bV_a=2\nabla_aV_b,
$$
where the last equality uses the <Killing equation>. As a <differential two-form>, $F=dV^\flat$, so the identity $d^2=0$ for the <exterior derivative> proves $\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0$. Contracting the preceding <second covariant derivative of a Killing vector> gives
$$
\nabla^a\nabla_aV_b=-R_{bd}V^d,
\qquad \nabla^aF_{ab}=-2R_{bd}V^d.
$$
A <vacuum spacetime> here has $R_{ab}=0$, with no <cosmological constant>. Therefore **both source-free Maxwell equations hold**:
$$
\boxed{\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0,\qquad \nabla^aF_{ab}=0.}
$$
This is a test <electromagnetic field> on the given <vacuum spacetime>; its own <stress-energy tensor> is not included in that background's <Einstein field equations>.

In Cartesian coordinates $x=r\sin\theta\cos\phi$, $y=r\sin\theta\sin\phi$, $z=r\cos\theta$, the rotational <Killing vector field> and its dual <differential one-form> are
$$
V=-y\partial_x+x\partial_y,\qquad V^\flat=-y\,dx+x\,dy.
$$
Therefore the <Papapetrou electromagnetic field> is
$$
F=dV^\flat=2\,dx\wedge dy.
$$
Choose the <electromagnetic field> convention $F_{it}=E_i$ and $F_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B^k$. There are no time components, and $F_{xy}=2$, so **this is a uniform magnetic field along the rotation axis, with no electric field**:
$$
\boxed{\mathbf E=0,\qquad \mathbf B=2\,\mathbf e_z.}
$$
Multiplying the <Killing vector field> by $B_0/2$ produces any desired uniform <magnetic field> strength $B_0$.

Write $f=1-2M/r$ in the <Schwarzschild metric>. For the stationary <Killing vector field> $V=\partial_t$, its dual is $V^\flat=-f\,dt$ and the <Papapetrou electromagnetic field> is
$$
F=-f'\,dr\wedge dt=\frac{2M}{r^2}\,dt\wedge dr.
$$
Outside the <Schwarzschild event horizon>, use the static <orthonormal coframe> $e^{\hat0}=\sqrt f\,dt$, $e^{\hat r}=dr/\sqrt f$, $e^{\hat\theta}=r\,d\theta$, $e^{\hat\phi}=r\sin\theta\,d\phi$. Then $F=(2M/r^2)e^{\hat0}\wedge e^{\hat r}$. With the <electric field> convention $E_{\hat i}=F_{\hat i\hat0}$ from the preceding part,
$$
\boxed{E_{\hat r}=-\frac{2M}{r^2},\qquad \mathbf B=0.}
$$
Thus the stationary <Killing vector field> gives a **Coulomb electric field**, directed inward in this convention. If the charge is normalized by $E_{\hat r}=Q/r^2$, its charge is $Q=-2M$. A convention with $F\mapsto-F$ reverses that charge; the invariant content is a radial monopole <electric field> of magnitude $2M/r^2$.

For the axial <Killing vector field> $V=\partial_\phi$, $V^\flat=r^2\sin^2\theta\,d\phi$, giving
$$
F=2r\sin^2\theta\,dr\wedge d\phi+2r^2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta\wedge d\phi.
$$
In the same static <orthonormal coframe>, this is a purely <magnetic field> with
$$
\boxed{B_{\hat r}=2\cos\theta,\qquad B_{\hat\theta}=-2\sqrt f\sin\theta,\qquad B_{\hat\phi}=0.}
$$
At large $r$ these are the spherical components of $2\mathbf e_z$. This is therefore the **regular magnetic test field asymptotic to a uniform axial field**, distorted by the <Schwarzschild black hole>. Its net magnetic monopole flux is zero because the integral of $\cos\theta$ over a sphere vanishes. Both <electromagnetic fields> are regular at the future <event horizon>: in <ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates>, the electric field is $(2M/r^2)dv\wedge dr$, and the magnetic two-form already contains only regular spatial coordinates. Static observers themselves cease to exist on the <event horizon>.

Put $r_+=\ell\sqrt M$ and $f=(r^2-r_+^2)/\ell^2$. The apparent failure of the nonrotating <BTZ black hole> coordinates at $r=r_+$ is a <coordinate singularity>. Define the <tortoise coordinate> and ingoing time by
$$
r_* =\frac{\ell^2}{2r_+}\log\left|\frac{r-r_+}{r+r_+}\right|,
\qquad v=t+r_*.
$$
The <ingoing BTZ coordinates> give
$$
ds^2=-f\,dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2d\phi^2.
$$
All coefficients are analytic at $r_+>0$, and the determinant is $-r^2$, so the <metric tensor> extends nondegenerately through that radius. The <Killing vector field> $K=\partial_v$ agrees with $\partial_t$ in the exterior. On $r=r_+$, $K^2=-f=0$ and $K_a=(dr)_a$. It is therefore both normal and tangent to this <null hypersurface>, proving that it is a <Killing horizon>.

One may include both horizon branches and their <bifurcation surface> explicitly. Set $\kappa=r_+/\ell^2$, $u=t-r_*$ and, initially in the exterior, $U=-e^{-\kappa u}$, $V=e^{\kappa v}$. Then
$$
UV=-\frac{r-r_+}{r+r_+},\qquad
r=r_+\frac{1-UV}{1+UV},
\qquad
ds^2=-\frac{4\ell^2\,dU\,dV}{(1+UV)^2}+r_+^2\left(\frac{1-UV}{1+UV}\right)^2d\phi^2.
$$
This <analytic extension of a spacetime> is regular near $UV=0$, and $K=\kappa(V\partial_V-U\partial_U)$ extends analytically. The surfaces $U=0$ and $V=0$ form the **extended Killing horizon at $r=r_+$**; $K$ vanishes only at their regular bifurcation circle.

Use the exterior-normalized <Killing vector field> $K=\partial_t=\partial_v$ from the previous part. In <ingoing BTZ coordinates>, its only nonzero acceleration component on $r=r_+$ is

The defining equation for <surface gravity>, $\nabla_KK=\kappa K$, therefore gives the positive future-branch value

$$
This value uses exactly the prescribed <Killing vector field> normalization. A constant rescaling of the <Killing vector field> rescales its <surface gravity>.

= Solution

Write $M=-a^2$ with $a>0$. On a constant-time slice, the proper distance from the axis to radius $r$ and the circumference at that radius are
$$
s(r)=\int_0^r\frac{dR}{\sqrt{a^2+R^2/\ell^2}}=\frac r a+O(r^3),
\qquad C(r)=2\pi r.
$$
Hence $C/s\to2\pi a$. A smooth axis in a <Riemannian metric> requires the Euclidean limit $C/s\to2\pi$, so regularity forces $a=1$, or $M=-1$. For $a\ne1$ the <conical singularity of negative-mass BTZ geometry> has deficit angle $2\pi(1-a)$; for $a>1$ this is an angular excess. Even integer $a>1$ is singular with the stipulated angular period $2\pi$: a multiple covering does not make the tip a smooth point.

At $M=-1$, set $r=\ell\sinh\chi$. The <metric tensor> becomes
$$
ds^2=-\cosh^2\chi\,dt^2+\ell^2(d\chi^2+\sinh^2\chi\,d\phi^2),
$$
which is regular at $\chi=0$ and describes <anti-de Sitter spacetime> with nonperiodic time. For every $M<0$, $f=a^2+r^2/\ell^2$ stays positive. An outward <radial null geodesic> obeys $dt/dr=1/f$, and reaches the timelike conformal boundary in finite coordinate time, since $\int_0^\infty dr/f=\pi\ell/(2a)$. Thus no <event horizon> hides the conical axis. **The geometry is nakedly singular except at $M=-1$.** Its local curvature away from the axis is that of <anti-de Sitter spacetime>; the argument is a global angular-identification test, not curvature blowup.

For a curve with fixed $v,\theta,\phi$, its tangent is proportional to $\partial_r$. The <Vaidya metric> has $g_{rr}=0$, so the curve is a <null curve>. Moreover, the <Christoffel symbols> with two lower $r$ indices vanish:
$$
\Gamma^a{}_{rr}=\frac12g^{ab}(2\partial_rg_{br}-\partial_bg_{rr})=0,
$$
because $g_{vr}=1$ is constant and every other $g_{br}$ is zero. Thus the <covariant derivative> along this tangent gives $\nabla_{\partial_r}\partial_r=0$. The <geodesic equation> is satisfied with $r$ as an <affine parameter>; choosing future orientation gives the tangent $k=-\partial_r$ and an affine parameter increasing as $-r$. **These are the ingoing radial null geodesics.**

Use the future-directed <null vector> $k=-\partial_r$, whose dual is $k_a=-(dv)_a$. The <stress-energy tensor> has the <null dust> form
$$
T_{ab}=\varepsilon k_ak_b,\qquad \varepsilon=\frac{\dot m(v)}{4\pi r^2}.
$$
For every future-directed <timelike vector> $t$, $k\cdot t<0$. The energy current appearing in the <dominant energy condition> is
$$
J^a=-T^a{}_bt^b=\varepsilon(-k\cdot t)k^a.
$$
If $\varepsilon\geq0$, this is a future-directed <null vector>, or zero; also $T_{ab}t^at^b=\varepsilon(k\cdot t)^2\geq0$. If $\varepsilon<0$, the current is past-directed and that energy density is negative. Therefore, given the already stipulated positivity and smoothness of $m$, the necessary and sufficient additional condition is
$$
\boxed{\dot m(v)\geq0\quad\text{for every }v.}
$$

An <event horizon> is a <null hypersurface>. For its spherical defining function $H(v,r)=r-r_H(v)$, the inverse radial <metric tensor> has $g^{vv}=0$, $g^{vr}=1$ and $g^{rr}=f=1-2m(v)/r$. The normal's <null condition> is
$$
0=g^{ab}\partial_aH\partial_bH=f(v,r_H)-2\dot r_H.
$$
It follows that the <event horizon> follows the outgoing <radial null geodesics of the Vaidya metric>:
$$
\boxed{2r_H\dot r_H=r_H-2m(v).}
$$
This <ordinary differential equation> also describes other outgoing <null hypersurfaces>; the global <event horizon> is selected by its future boundary condition, not by this local equation alone.

In the final <Schwarzschild spacetime>, an outgoing ray with $r>2M_1$ escapes to <future null infinity>, whereas one with $r<2M_1$ cannot do so and ends at the <Schwarzschild singularity>. Their boundary is the outgoing <null geodesic> $r=2M_1$. Since the final region continues indefinitely, the boundary of the <causal past> of <future null infinity> there is exactly that <Schwarzschild event horizon>. Equivalently, integrating the outgoing equation backward from this final boundary selects the complete <event horizon>. Thus
$$
\boxed{r_H(v)=2M_1\qquad(v>v_0).}
$$

= Solution
{parent=e}

Let $\Delta M=M_1-M_0$ and retain only terms first order in $\mu/M_0$ and $\rho/M_0$. The outgoing <event horizon> equation expands to
$$
4M_0\dot\rho=\rho-2\mu,
\qquad \dot\rho-\kappa_0\rho=-\frac{\mu}{2M_0},
\qquad \kappa_0=\frac1{4M_0}.
$$
Here $\kappa_0$ is the initial <Schwarzschild surface gravity>. The <integrating factor> $e^{-\kappa_0v}$ gives $d(e^{-\kappa_0v}\rho)/dv=-e^{-\kappa_0v}\mu/(2M_0)$. Future stationarity imposes $\rho=2\Delta M$ for $v>v_0$ and removes the growing homogeneous solution. Integrating from $v$ to infinity yields the <teleological response of a Vaidya event horizon>:
$$
\boxed{\rho(v)=\frac1{2M_0}\int_v^\infty e^{-\kappa_0(s-v)}\mu(s)\,ds.}
$$
The integral is finite because $\mu$ is bounded. Integrating by parts also gives
$$
\rho(v)=2\mu(v)+2\int_v^\infty e^{-\kappa_0(s-v)}\dot\mu(s)\,ds.
$$
For $v>v_0$, this reduces to $2\Delta M$. For $v<0$, the first form becomes
$$
\rho(v)=C e^{\kappa_0v},\qquad C=\frac1{2M_0}\int_0^\infty e^{-\kappa_0s}\mu(s)\,ds.
$$
Consequently **$r_H\to2M_0$ as $v\to-\infty$**. The positive constant $C$ shows that this <event horizon> is already expanding in the initially vacuum region. For monotone accretion, $\rho-2\mu\geq0$, so it lies outside the instantaneous <apparent horizon> to first order. All displayed radius corrections are understood up to $O((\Delta M)^2/M_0)$.

The <stress-energy tensor> is already first order, so its energy flux can be evaluated on the unperturbed <Schwarzschild event horizon> $r=2M_0$, with the infinity-normalized <Killing vector field> $\xi=\partial_v$. On this background $g^{rv}=1$, and hence
$$
T^r{}_b\xi^b=T^r{}_v=T_{vv}=\frac{\dot\mu}{4\pi(2M_0)^2}.
$$
The conserved <stress-energy current from a Killing vector> is $J^a=-T^a{}_b\xi^b$. Its radial component is negative, so the positive inward energy flux is $-J^r$. Using the directed null-surface measure $r_H^2\,dv\,d\Omega$ gives
$$
\Delta E=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{S^2}T^r{}_v(2M_0)^2\,d\Omega\,dv
=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\dot\mu(v)\,dv
=\boxed{\Delta M}.
$$
The initial and final <event horizon> areas are $A_0=16\pi M_0^2$ and $A_1=16\pi(M_0+\Delta M)^2$. Thus $\Delta A=32\pi M_0\Delta M+O((\Delta M)^2)$. With <Schwarzschild surface gravity> $\kappa_0=1/(4M_0)$, the <physical-process first law of black-hole mechanics> follows:
$$
\boxed{\Delta E=\Delta M=\frac{\kappa_0}{8\pi}\Delta A+O((\Delta M)^2/M_0).}
$$
The flux is <Killing energy>, including gravitational redshift, rather than the energy measured by a sequence of static observers arbitrarily close to the <event horizon>.

Locally write the <null hypersurface> as $S=0$ with $dS\ne0$, and extend its normal as $n_a=\nabla_aS$. On the hypersurface, $n^an_a=0$, so $n^a\nabla_aS=n^2=0$: the raised normal is itself tangent. The <Levi-Civita connection> and symmetry of the scalar Hessian give
$$
n^b\nabla_bn_a=n^b\nabla_an_b=\frac12\nabla_a(n^2).
$$
Although $n^2$ vanishes on the hypersurface, it need not vanish off it. Its tangential derivatives vanish, so $\nabla_a(n^2)$ must be proportional to the normal $n_a$. Hence $\nabla_nn=\kappa n$ on the hypersurface for some smooth local coefficient $\kappa$. The normal's <integral curves of a vector field> remain in the hypersurface and satisfy the nonaffine <geodesic equation>. Rescale to $k=hn$, where $n(\log h)=-\kappa$ along each generator. Direct substitution gives $\nabla_kk=0$. Thus **the normal generates null geodesics within the hypersurface**, with an <affine parameter> after this rescaling. This proves the <null hypersurface normal generates null geodesics> property without incorrectly setting a transverse derivative of $n^2$ to zero.

Choose the affine tangent $k^a$ to a <null geodesic congruence>, and an auxiliary <null vector> $\ell^a$ with $k\cdot\ell=-1$. The <screen-space projector>
$$
q_{ab}=g_{ab}+k_a\ell_b+\ell_ak_b
$$
annihilates $k$ and $\ell$ and restricts to a positive-definite two-dimensional metric in four spacetime dimensions. Neighboring transverse rays have relative displacement governed by the <optical tensor>
$$
B_{ab}=q_a{}^cq_b{}^d\nabla_dk_c.
$$
Its irreducible decomposition defines the three requested optical quantities:
$$
\boxed{\theta=q^{ab}B_{ab},\qquad
\sigma_{ab}=B_{(ab)}-\frac12\theta q_{ab},\qquad
\omega_{ab}=B_{[ab]}.}
$$
The <null expansion> $\theta$ is the fractional rate of change of transverse area, $d\log A/d\lambda$. The <null shear> $\sigma$ changes a beam's shape at fixed area to first order; it is symmetric and trace-free. The <null twist> $\omega$ is antisymmetric and measures local rotation of the transverse rays; it vanishes for a hypersurface-orthogonal <null geodesic congruence>. In $D$ dimensions replace $1/2$ by $1/(D-2)$. These definitions use the trace convention for <null expansion>, rather than the alternative screen-averaged convention.

= Solution

Take an affinely parametrized <null geodesic congruence>, $\nabla_kk=0$. Choose the auxiliary <null vector> and a transverse orthonormal screen basis to undergo <parallel transport> along each ray. For the unprojected tensor $C_{ab}=\nabla_bk_a$, the product rule and the <Ricci identity> yield
$$
\begin{aligned}
k^c\nabla_cC_{ab}
&=\nabla_b(k^c\nabla_ck_a)-(\nabla_bk^c)(\nabla_ck_a)
+k^c[\nabla_c,\nabla_b]k_a\\
&=-(\nabla_bk^c)(\nabla_ck_a)-R_{acbd}k^ck^d.
\end{aligned}
$$
Here the <Riemann curvature tensor> convention is the one in Question 1; its last-index antisymmetry converts the covector commutator to the displayed curvature term. Because $C_{ab}k^a=C_{ab}k^b=0$, inserting the <screen-space projector> in the product term adds only terms killed by these contractions. Parallel propagation makes differentiation commute with the screen projection. Therefore, in screen indices $A,B$,
$$
\frac{dB_{AB}}{d\lambda}=-B_{AC}B^C{}_B-R_{AcBd}k^ck^d.
$$
This is the optical matrix evolution equation. Its trace has curvature contraction $q^{ab}R_{acbd}k^ck^d=R_{cd}k^ck^d$: the additional terms in $q^{ab}-g^{ab}$ vanish by curvature antisymmetry. Write $B=(\theta/2)q+\sigma+\omega$ using the <null expansion>, <null shear>, and <null twist>. Symmetric--antisymmetric cross terms have zero trace, $\sigma$ is trace-free, and
$$
\operatorname{tr}(B^2)=\frac12\theta^2+\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}-\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab}.
$$
The minus sign in the last trace comes from $\omega_{ab}=-\omega_{ba}$ and the positive screen metric. Taking the trace therefore derives the <null Raychaudhuri equation>:
$$
\boxed{\frac{d\theta}{d\lambda}=-\frac12\theta^2-\sigma_{ab}\sigma^{ab}+\omega_{ab}\omega^{ab}-R_{ab}k^ak^b.}
$$
For a nonaffine tangent obeying $\nabla_kk=\kappa k$, the same product-rule derivation adds $+\kappa\theta$ on the right; the projected derivative of $\kappa$ multiplies $k_a$ and vanishes. In $D$ dimensions the quadratic <null expansion> term is $-\theta^2/(D-2)$.

= Solution

Away from a bifurcation set, the normal to a <Killing horizon> is its nonzero <Killing vector field> $K$. Any affine tangent to the same generators has the form $k=hK$. In the <optical tensor>, derivatives of $h$ contribute a factor of $K_a$ and disappear under the <screen-space projector>. Consequently
$$
B_{(ab)}=h\,q_a{}^cq_b{}^d\nabla_{(d}K_{c)}=0
$$
by the <Killing equation>. The trace and trace-free symmetric part are therefore zero: $\theta=0$ and $\sigma_{ab}=0$.

For the antisymmetric part, locally the horizon normal is proportional to a gradient. The restriction of its dual one-form to every tangent direction on the horizon is zero. Taking its <exterior derivative> and evaluating on two screen tangents therefore gives zero as well; equivalently this is the hypersurface-orthogonality consequence of the <Frobenius theorem>. Since both screen indices are tangential, $B_{[ab]}=0$. Thus the <optical scalars on a Killing horizon> satisfy
$$
\boxed{\theta=0,\qquad\sigma_{ab}=0,\qquad\omega_{ab}=0.}
$$
No affine normalization of the <Killing vector field> itself was assumed. At a regular <bifurcation surface>, $K$ vanishes but the smooth null generators do not; the conclusions for their optical quantities follow by continuity along each horizon branch.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Work initially in units $G=c=\hbar=k_B=1$ and consider gravitational collapse settling to a nonextremal <Schwarzschild black hole>. Classically, outgoing rays sufficiently close to the forming <event horizon> suffer an arbitrarily large redshift. Quantum mechanically, the meaning of <positive frequency> at <past null infinity> differs from that at <future null infinity>; a <Bogoliubov transformation> between these two mode descriptions can therefore mix <creation operators> and <annihilation operators>. Hawking's argument computes that mixing and obtains a thermal occupation, even when the incoming state has no particles.

To see why the frequency mixing is universal, use final <Schwarzschild time> $t$, the <Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate> $r_*$, and retarded time $u=t-r_*$. Near the final <event horizon>, $r_*\sim(2\kappa)^{-1}\log(r-2M)$, with <Schwarzschild surface gravity> $\kappa=1/(4M)$. On a fixed advanced-time section, $u=v-2r_*$, so $r-2M$ is proportional to $e^{-\kappa u}$. Tracing a ray back through the regular collapsing region maps this small separation smoothly to its separation from the last escaping incoming ray. If $U$ is an affine incoming <null coordinate> and $U_H$ labels that limiting ray, the <Hawking exponential ray map> is
$$
U_H-U=Ae^{-\kappa u}(1+o(1)),\qquad A>0.
$$
The logarithm at a simple horizon root fixes the exponent. Smooth propagation through the earlier collapse changes $A$ and phases but not the leading exponential redshift.

For a massless bosonic test <scalar field>, an outgoing mode of frequency $\omega>0$ at <future null infinity> behaves as $e^{-i\omega u}$. Trace it backward in the high-frequency <geometric optics> approximation. Its relevant incoming dependence is, up to normalization and a phase,
$$
p_\omega(U)\sim\Theta(U_H-U)\left(\frac{U_H-U}{A}\right)^{ia},\qquad a=\frac\omega\kappa.
$$
Here $\Theta$ is the <Heaviside step function>. Incoming rays with $U>U_H$ do not escape. This truncated logarithmic phase is not purely <positive frequency> with respect to $U$. Decomposing it into incoming <positive-frequency solutions> $e^{-i\omega' U}$ and negative modes $e^{+i\omega' U}$ gives the <Bogoliubov transformation>. With $x=U_H-U$, the two relevant <Fourier transform> integrals are
$$
I_-(\omega')=\int_0^\infty x^{ia}e^{-(\epsilon+i\omega')x}\,dx,
\qquad
I_+(\omega')=\int_0^\infty x^{ia}e^{-(\epsilon-i\omega')x}\,dx,
\qquad\epsilon>0.
$$
The former is the positive-frequency coefficient and the latter is the negative-frequency coefficient; the <Klein-Gordon inner product> introduces the same frequency normalization into their modulus ratio. By the defining integral of the <gamma function> and analytic continuation in its Laplace parameter,
$$
I_\mp=\Gamma(1+ia)(\epsilon\pm i\omega')^{-1-ia}.
$$
For a complex number $z$ in the right half-plane, $|z^{-1-ia}|=|z|^{-1}e^{a\arg z}$. As $\epsilon\downarrow0$, the two arguments tend to $+\pi/2$ and $-\pi/2$. This gives the <thermal ratio of Hawking Bogoliubov coefficients>:
$$
\boxed{\frac{|\beta_{\omega\omega'}|^2}{|\alpha_{\omega\omega'}|^2}=e^{-2\pi\omega/\kappa}.}
$$
The regulator fixes the branches and is essential to the sign of the thermal exponent.

Expand the outgoing annihilator as $b=\int d\omega'(\alpha_{\omega\omega'}^*a_{\omega'}-\beta_{\omega\omega'}^*a_{\omega'}^\dagger)$, including a complete set of incoming channels. The <canonical identities for a bosonic Bogoliubov transformation> express $[b,b^\dagger]=1$. For normalized outgoing <wave packets>, they give $\int(|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2)\,d\omega'=1$. In the incoming <Fock vacuum>, the outgoing mean of the <particle number operator> is $N_\omega=\int|\beta|^2\,d\omega'$. Combining the two identities with the thermal ratio, in the late-time narrow-frequency packet limit, yields
$$
N_\omega=\frac1{e^{2\pi\omega/\kappa}-1},\qquad
\boxed{T_H=\frac\kappa{2\pi}=\frac1{8\pi M}.}
$$
<Wave packets> avoid interpreting the divergent normalization of infinitely long continuum modes as a finite particle count. The temperature is the <Hawking temperature>, and the occupation is the zero-chemical-potential <Bose-Einstein distribution>. Restoring constants, the <Schwarzschild black hole> temperature is $T_H=\hbar c^3/(8\pi G M k_B)$ for physical mass $M$.

The preceding thermal occupation describes the horizon-originating channel before exterior scattering. The exterior angular and curvature potential partly reflects it. For a <scalar field>, the number flux at <future null infinity> is consequently
$$
\frac{dN}{dt\,d\omega}=\frac1{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^\infty(2\ell+1)\frac{\Gamma_\ell(\omega)}{e^{\omega/T_H}-1},
$$
where the <greybody factor> $\Gamma_\ell$ is the transmission probability of a partial wave. Multiplication by $\omega$ gives its energy flux. Thus the asymptotic spectrum has the thermal denominator but is not an exact blackbody spectrum. For fermionic fields the <canonical anticommutation relations> replace the minus sign in the occupation denominator by a plus sign, at the same <Hawking temperature>.

The relevant state is the <collapse vacuum for Hawking radiation>: no incoming thermal bath at <past null infinity>, and regular short-distance behavior in freely falling coordinates at the forming <event horizon>. It predicts an outgoing flux, unlike equilibrium with a thermal bath on an eternal <black hole>. The derivation uses <quantum field theory in curved spacetime> on a prescribed classical geometry; it does not require outgoing particles to follow forbidden classical paths out of the interior. Positive energy carried to infinity is accompanied, in the semiclassical description, by negative Killing-energy flux into the <black hole>. Including that flux in slow backreaction decreases its <mass>, giving <black-hole evaporation>.

The approximation applies to the late radiation of a large nonextremal <black hole> while its <surface gravity> changes slowly on a time scale $\kappa^{-1}$. Backward propagation involves very high locally measured frequencies, so the calculation assumes the usual regular short-distance quantum-field state. It determines the leading radiation and temperature, not the Planck-scale endpoint of <black-hole evaporation>. **The exponential horizon redshift forces positive/negative frequency mixing with a thermal ratio, giving outgoing Hawking radiation at $T_H=\kappa/(2\pi)$.**
