Let $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ with $\alpha$ an <algebraic integer>, let $m\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be its monic <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element>, and write $A=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. The <Kummer-Dedekind theorem> applies to a rational prime $p$ not dividing the finite index $[\mathcal O_K:A]$. If
$$
\overline m=\prod_{j=1}^r g_j^{e_j}\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p[X]
$$
with distinct monic <irreducible polynomials> $g_j$, choose monic integer lifts $\widetilde g_j$. Then the distinct <prime ideals> above $p$ are
$$
\mathfrak p_j=(p,\widetilde g_j(\alpha)),\qquad
\boxed{p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathfrak p_j^{e_j},\quad
[\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p_j:\mathbb F_p]=\deg g_j}.
$$
In particular, $p$ is unramified precisely when $\overline m$ has no repeated irreducible factor. The exclusion of primes dividing the index is essential.

Here is the <quotient-ring proof of the Kummer-Dedekind theorem>. The finite group $\mathcal O_K/A$ has order coprime to $p$. Localizing at the integers coprime to $p$ therefore gives $A\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}=\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_{(p)}$, and reduction yields
$$
\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\simeq A/pA\simeq\mathbb F_p[X]/(\overline m).
$$
The second isomorphism follows from $A\simeq\mathbb Z[X]/(m)$. The <Chinese remainder theorem> gives
$$
\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\simeq\prod_j\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j^{e_j}).
$$
Each factor is a <local ring> with unique <maximal ideal> generated by $g_j$ and <residue field> $\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j)$. Pulling its <maximal ideal> back to $\mathcal O_K$ gives exactly $\mathfrak p_j$; these exhaust the primes above $p$. This proves the asserted <residue degrees> and their independence of the chosen lifts.

It remains to prove the exponents rather than simply identify the primes. Unique <prime ideal factorization> in the <Dedekind domain> $\mathcal O_K$ writes $(p)=\prod_j\mathfrak p_j^{b_j}$. At $\mathfrak p_j$, its <discrete valuation ring> has $p=u\pi^{b_j}$, with $u$ a unit. Thus the <maximal ideal> of $\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p_j}/p\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p_j}$ has <nilpotence index of an ideal> $b_j$: its $b_j$th power is zero and its $(b_j-1)$st is nonzero. In $\mathbb F_p[X]/(g_j^{e_j})$ this index is $e_j$, since $g_j^{e_j}=0$ but $g_j^{e_j-1}\ne0$. The displayed product isomorphism identifies these local factors, hence $b_j=e_j$. This completes the theorem's proof. The same local argument works over a base <number field> whenever $\mathcal O_K[\alpha]$ equals the full relative <ring of integers> after localizing at the chosen base prime.

For the required <cyclotomic field>, the allowed <ring of integers> formula makes the index one. Its <cyclotomic polynomial> is
$$
\Phi_{80}(X)=\frac{X^{40}+1}{X^8+1}=X^{32}-X^{24}+X^{16}-X^8+1.
$$
For $p\notin\{2,5\}$, the polynomial $X^{80}-1$ has derivative $80X^{79}$ coprime to it modulo $p$. Its divisor $\overline\Phi_{80}$ is therefore squarefree, so the <Kummer-Dedekind theorem> gives no ramification.

The <cyclotomic reduction at a conductor prime> is explicit at the remaining two primes:
$$
\overline\Phi_{80}=(X^4+X^3+X^2+X+1)^8\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[X],
$$
$$
\overline\Phi_{80}=(X^8+1)^4=(X^4+2)^4(X^4+3)^4\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5[X].
$$
The quartic over $\mathbb F_2$ has no linear factor. Its only possible irreducible quadratic divisor would be $X^2+X+1$, whose roots have order three, whereas its own roots have order five. Hence it is irreducible. The two quartics over $\mathbb F_5$ are also irreducible: their roots have multiplicative order sixteen because their fourth powers are $2$ or $3$, both of order four; the <finite-field Frobenius automorphism> has orbit length four on an element of order sixteen, since $5^4\equiv1\pmod{16}$ and neither $5$ nor $5^2$ is $1$ modulo sixteen.

Consequently the <prime ideal factorization> above $2$ has one prime with ramification index eight and <residue degree> four. Above $5$ it has two primes, each with ramification index four and <residue degree> four. In particular,
$$
\boxed{\text{The ramified rational primes are exactly }2\text{ and }5.}
$$

Write $G=\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)$ and
$$
D_{\mathfrak q}=\{\sigma\in G:\sigma\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q\}.
$$
Every member of this <decomposition group> preserves $\mathcal O_F$ and $\mathfrak q$, so reduction defines a homomorphism
$$
D_{\mathfrak q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}).
$$
Its kernel is the <inertia group>. We prove <surjectivity of decomposition group reduction> without presupposing a lift of Frobenius.

If the two <residue fields> coincide, surjectivity is immediate. Otherwise choose a nonzero $\overline a\in k_{\mathfrak q}$ generating it over $k_{\mathfrak p}$; a generator exists by the <primitive element theorem> for <finite fields>. Use the <Chinese remainder theorem> in $\mathcal O_F$ to choose $a$ whose residue is $\overline a$ at $\mathfrak q$ and zero at every other prime above $\mathfrak p$. Consider
$$
P(T)=\prod_{\sigma\in G}(T-\sigma a).
$$
Its coefficients are integral and fixed by $G$, hence lie in $\mathcal O_K$: an element of $K$ integral over $\mathcal O_K$ belongs to this integrally closed <ring of integers>.

Reduce at $\mathfrak q$. When $\sigma\notin D_{\mathfrak q}$, the chosen residue of $a$ at $\sigma^{-1}\mathfrak q$ is zero, so $\sigma a$ reduces to zero at $\mathfrak q$. When $\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}$, its residue is $\overline\sigma(\overline a)$, which is nonzero. Therefore
$$
\overline P(T)=T^{|G|-|D_{\mathfrak q}|}
\prod_{\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}}(T-\overline\sigma(\overline a)).
$$
Although this product is written in $k_{\mathfrak q}[T]$, its coefficients belong to $k_{\mathfrak p}$. Put $q=|k_{\mathfrak p}|$. The <finite-field Frobenius automorphism> $b\mapsto b^q$ therefore permutes its nonzero roots. In particular, $\overline a^q$ is $\overline\sigma(\overline a)$ for some $\sigma\in D_{\mathfrak q}$. Since $\overline a$ generates $k_{\mathfrak q}$, that $\overline\sigma$ agrees with Frobenius on the whole field. The <Galois group of a finite field extension> is cyclic and generated by Frobenius, so its generator lies in the image. Thus
$$
\boxed{D_{\mathfrak q}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}),\qquad
D_{\mathfrak q}/I_{\mathfrak q}\simeq\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p})}.
$$

Put $a=\sqrt[3]{2}$ and $\zeta=\zeta_3$. The polynomial $X^3-2$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by the <Eisenstein criterion> at $2$, so $[\mathbb Q(a):\mathbb Q]=3$. This field is real and cannot contain the nonreal $\zeta$. Adjoining $\zeta$ gives a quadratic extension and hence $[F:\mathbb Q]=6$. Since $F$ is the <splitting field> of $X^3-2$, its <Galois group> acts faithfully on $a,\zeta a,\zeta^2a$ and has order six. Consequently
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\simeq S_3.
$$

For a prime $\mathfrak q$ over a rational prime $p$, its <absolute residue degree> is the order of the cyclic <quotient group> $D_{\mathfrak q}/I_{\mathfrak q}$ from part (i). If that degree were six, then $D_{\mathfrak q}$, a subgroup of $S_3$, would have order at least six, so $D_{\mathfrak q}=S_3$ and $I_{\mathfrak q}=1$. This would make $S_3$ cyclic, a contradiction. The argument applies also at ramified primes. Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{No prime of }F\text{ has absolute residue degree }6.}
$$

For the second request printed with the same subpart label, work over $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta)$. The <Galois group> of $F/E$ is cyclic of order three, with generator $\sigma(a)=\zeta a$ and $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta$. The <ideal norm> of $(\zeta+3)$ is seven, since
$$
(\zeta+3)(\zeta^2+3)=7.
$$
In its <residue field> we have $\zeta\equiv-3\equiv4\pmod{\mathfrak p_1}$. The polynomial $X^3-2$ has no root in $\mathbb F_7$, because its cube residues are $0,1,6$. It is therefore irreducible there. Its discriminant $-108$ is a unit at $\mathfrak p_1$. To check the local index condition, the <trace pairing> matrix of $1,a,a^2$ has this unit determinant. For any integral element $b$ of $F$, its traces against these three basis elements are integral; solving with this invertible matrix shows that its basis coefficients are integral locally. Thus the relative <ring of integers> equals $\mathcal O_E[a]$ at $\mathfrak p_1$. The relative <Kummer-Dedekind theorem> gives one unramified prime above $\mathfrak p_1$, of <residue degree> three.

Use <arithmetic Frobenius>, characterized by $b\mapsto b^7$ on this <residue field>. As $a^3=2$,
$$
a^7=4a\equiv\zeta a\pmod{\mathfrak q}.
$$
The three possible images $a,\zeta a,\zeta^2a$ remain distinct modulo $\mathfrak q$, since $a$ is nonzero and $1,4,2$ are distinct modulo seven. This congruence therefore identifies the actual automorphism uniquely:
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p_1}(a)=\zeta_3a,\qquad
\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p_1}(\zeta_3)=\zeta_3.}
$$
The geometric Frobenius convention would give its inverse. Since $F/E$ is abelian, the arithmetic element is independent of the choice of prime above $\mathfrak p_1$.

The three quadratic subfields are $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2})$, $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})$ and $\mathbb Q(\sqrt6)$, with respective <fundamental discriminants> $-8,-3,24$. Their associated quadratic <Dirichlet characters> are the <Kronecker symbols>
$$
\chi_{-8}(n)=\left(\frac{-8}{n}\right),\qquad
\chi_{-3}(n)=\left(\frac{-3}{n}\right),\qquad
\chi_{24}(n)=\left(\frac{24}{n}\right).
$$
The <regular representation> of $C_2\times C_2$ is the direct sum of the trivial character and these three nontrivial characters. At an unramified prime the character is the sign by which <Frobenius automorphism> acts on the square root defining its quadratic subfield; at a ramified prime the <linear character> has no <inertia group> invariants. The <local factor of an Artin L-function> consequently gives
$$
L(\rho,s)=\zeta(s)L(\chi_{-8},s)L(\chi_{-3},s)L(\chi_{24},s).
$$
The zero values of a quadratic <Dirichlet character> at conductor primes mean that its local factor is one, not that the whole <Artin L-function> vanishes there.

Only the rational primes $2,3,5,7$ can contribute to coefficients with index at most ten. Their character values are
$$
\begin{array}{c|rrr|l}
p&\chi_{-8}(p)&\chi_{-3}(p)&\chi_{24}(p)&L_p(\rho,s)\ \text{with }T=p^{-s}\\ \hline
2&0&-1&0&(1-T^2)^{-1}\\
3&1&0&0&(1-T)^{-2}\\
5&-1&-1&1&(1-T^2)^{-2}\\
7&-1&1&-1&(1-T^2)^{-2}
\end{array}
$$
For the odd unramified primes these entries follow by checking squares: modulo five, both $-8$ and $-3$ are the nonsquare $2$, while $24$ is the square $4$; modulo seven they are respectively $6,4,3$. At $3$, $-8\equiv1$ is a square. At $2$, the $-3$ character is $-1$ because the integral quadratic generator has polynomial $X^2+X+1$, irreducible modulo two. The other entries at $2$ and $3$ are zero exactly where the corresponding <fundamental discriminant> is divisible by that prime. This verifies every local factor, including the ramified ones.

The <Euler product> makes $a_n$ a <multiplicative arithmetic function>. Expanding the first two factors gives
$$
(1-T^2)^{-1}=1+T^2+T^4+\cdots,\qquad
(1-T)^{-2}=1+2T+3T^2+\cdots.
$$
Therefore $a_2=0$, $a_4=1$, $a_8=0$, $a_3=2$ and $a_9=3$. Both factors for $5$ and $7$ have zero coefficient of $T$, so $a_5=a_7=0$. Multiplicativity gives $a_6=a_2a_3=0$ and $a_{10}=a_2a_5=0$, while $a_1=1$. The answer is
$$
\boxed{(a_1,\ldots,a_{10})=(1,0,2,1,0,0,0,0,3,0)}.
$$
As an arithmetic check, the <regular representation> <Artin L-function> is the <Dedekind zeta function> of $F$. The local factors say there is one prime of norm four above two and two primes of norm three above three. Their powers give respectively one ideal of norm four and three ideals of norm nine, matching the nonzero coefficients above.

= Solution

We use the <Dirichlet density> form of the <Chebotarev density theorem>, the version directly proved by the permitted information at $s=1$. If $L/K$ is a finite <Galois extension> of <number fields> with group $G$ and $C$ is a <conjugacy class>, let $S_C$ be the unramified <prime ideals> of $K$ whose <Frobenius conjugacy class> is $C$. Then
$$
\boxed{\delta(S_C):=\lim_{s\downarrow1}
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S_C}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}}
{\log(1/(s-1))}=\frac{|C|}{|G|}}.
$$
The same conclusion holds for a union of <conjugacy classes>, replacing $|C|$ by the size of that union. The denominator may equivalently be the sum over all primes of $K$. Here and below $s>1$ is real. This specifies the density being proved; an unweighted prime-counting asymptotic is a stronger formulation and is not being inferred merely from a limit at one.

First establish the necessary pole for a general base field. The <Dedekind zeta function as a permutation Artin L-function> gives it using exactly the allowed assumptions. Take a finite normal closure $E$ of $K/\mathbb Q$, and let $W$ be the <permutation representation> on $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(K,E)$. Its trivial constituent has multiplicity one: an invariant function on the transitive set of embeddings is constant. At a rational prime, a basis of $W^I$ consists of the sums over inertia orbits of embeddings. <Frobenius automorphism> permutes these orbit sums. Within the decomposition-group orbit belonging to a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$, the resulting cycle has length $f_{\mathfrak p}$, because the residue embeddings are permuted cyclically by finite-field Frobenius. A cycle of length $f$ has determinant $\det(1-T\rho(\operatorname{Frob}))=1-T^f$. Consequently the <local factor of an Artin L-function> gives
$$
L_{\mathbb Q}(W,s)=\prod_p\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid p}(1-p^{-f_{\mathfrak p}s})^{-1}
=\zeta_K(s),
$$
including at ramified primes. Decomposing $W=\mathbf1\oplus\bigoplus_{\psi\ne\mathbf1}m_\psi\psi$ gives
$$
\zeta_K(s)=\zeta(s)\prod_{\psi\ne\mathbf1}L_{\mathbb Q}(\psi,s)^{m_\psi}.
$$
All factors after $\zeta$ are holomorphic and nonzero at one by the allowed hypothesis. Thus $\zeta_K$ has a simple pole there and
$$
\log\zeta_K(s)=\log\frac1{s-1}+O(1).
$$
This argument also explains why the trivial relative <Artin L-function> is $\zeta_K$, rather than always the Riemann zeta function.

Now let $\chi$ be an <irreducible character> of $G$, of degree $d_\chi$. At an unramified prime the <Euler product> has local factor $\det(1-\rho_\chi(g_{\mathfrak p})(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}$, where $g_{\mathfrak p}$ is a Frobenius representative. The <eigenvalues> of $\rho_\chi(g)$ are <roots of unity>, since $g$ has finite order. Expanding the logarithm for $s>1$ therefore gives
$$
\log L_K(\chi,s)
=\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}\sum_{m\ge1}
\frac{\chi(g_{\mathfrak p}^m)}{m(N\mathfrak p)^{ms}}+R_\chi(s),
$$
where the finitely many ramified local factors contribute a function bounded as $s\downarrow1$. They are nonzero and finite near one because their <eigenvalues> have modulus one while $(N\mathfrak p)^{-1}<1$.

The terms $m\ge2$ are uniformly bounded. Indeed $|\chi(g)|\le d_\chi$, and for $N\mathfrak p\ge2$ and $s\ge1$,
$$
\sum_{m\ge2}\frac{(N\mathfrak p)^{-ms}}m
\le\sum_{m\ge2}(N\mathfrak p)^{-m}\le2(N\mathfrak p)^{-2}.
$$
There are at most $[K:\mathbb Q]$ primes of $K$ above each rational prime and each has norm at least that prime. Hence $\sum_{\mathfrak p}(N\mathfrak p)^{-2}<\infty$. Isolating $m=1$ proves the <character prime sums in the Chebotarev proof> identity
$$
\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}
\chi(g_{\mathfrak p})(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}
=\log L_K(\chi,s)+O(1).
$$
For nontrivial $\chi$, the allowed holomorphy and nonvanishing at one give a local <holomorphic logarithm> and a bounded right side. The logarithm specified by the Euler series differs from that local branch by a constant multiple of $2\pi i$ on the real interval near one, so it too is bounded. For the trivial character, the right side is $\log(1/(s-1))+O(1)$ by the pole of $\zeta_K$. Thus
$$
\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}
\chi(g_{\mathfrak p})(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}
=\begin{cases}\log(1/(s-1))+O(1),&\chi=\mathbf1,\\ O(1),&\chi\ne\mathbf1.\end{cases}
$$

Expand the conjugacy-class indicator by <character orthogonality>. For $c\in C$,
$$
\mathbf1_C(g)=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}
\overline{\chi(c)}\,\chi(g).
$$
To see the coefficient, take its inner product with $\chi$: $|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in C}\overline{\chi(g)}=|C|\overline{\chi(c)}/|G|$. The <irreducible characters> form an orthonormal basis of <class functions>, so this is the full expansion. Substituting the prime-sum estimates leaves only the trivial character's logarithmic term:
$$
\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S_C}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}
=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log\frac1{s-1}+O(1).
$$
Division by the diverging logarithm proves the theorem. The same trivial-character calculation shows that all primes of $K$ have density one; adding or removing finitely many primes does not change a <Dirichlet density>.

There is a necessary qualification for the final polynomial application: \b[the printed claim is false for degree one]. For example, $X$ is monic and irreducible in $\mathbb Z[X]$, yet has the root zero modulo every prime. We prove the intended assertion for degree $n\ge2$.

Let $L$ be the <splitting field> of the monic <irreducible polynomial> over $\mathbb Q$, and let $\Omega$ be its $n$ roots. The <Galois group> $G$ acts transitively on $\Omega$. There is a <derangement in a transitive group action>: count the pairs $(g,\alpha)$ satisfying $g\alpha=\alpha$. The <orbit-stabilizer theorem> gives
$$
\sum_{g\in G}|\operatorname{Fix}_\Omega(g)|
=\sum_{\alpha\in\Omega}|G_\alpha|=n\frac{|G|}{n}=|G|.
$$
If every $g$ fixed at least one root, the identity, which fixes $n\ge2$, would make this sum at least $|G|+n-1$, a contradiction. Therefore some $g$ fixes no root, and neither does any conjugate of $g$.

For completeness, relate this group action to reduction without assuming the desired splitting result. All roots are <algebraic integers>, since the polynomial is monic. Exclude the finitely many primes ramifying in $L$ or dividing the nonzero <polynomial discriminant>. At a prime $\mathfrak q$ over any remaining $p$, the roots reduce to distinct elements of $k_{\mathfrak q}$ and give all roots of the reduced polynomial. <Arithmetic Frobenius> permutes them by $b\mapsto b^p$. A reduced root belongs to $\mathbb F_p$ exactly when it is fixed by this map. Because reduction is injective on the original root set, this happens exactly when the Frobenius permutation fixes an original root. This proves the <Frobenius permutation and roots modulo a prime> criterion.

The <Chebotarev density theorem> assigns the <conjugacy class> of our fixed-point-free $g$ the positive <Dirichlet density> $|C_g|/|G|$. All but finitely many of these primes have rootless reduction, so there are infinitely many. More precisely, applying the same argument to the union of all fixed-point-free classes gives
$$
\boxed{\delta\{p:f\bmod p\text{ has no root in }\mathbb F_p\}
=\frac{|\{g\in G:\operatorname{Fix}_\Omega(g)=\varnothing\}|}{|G|}>0
\quad(n\ge2).}
$$
