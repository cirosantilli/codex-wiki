= Solution
{parent=1}

Put $C=\bigcup_{r\geq1}\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^r})$, where $\mu_{p^r}$ is the group of $p^r$th <roots of unity>. The compatible <Galois groups> of the <cyclotomic fields> give
$$
\operatorname{Gal}(C/\mathbb Q)
\cong\varprojlim_r(\mathbb Z/p^r\mathbb Z)^\times
=\mathbb Z_p^\times.
$$
For odd $p$, the <Teichmüller character> gives
$$
\mathbb Z_p^\times=\mu_{p-1}\times(1+p\mathbb Z_p).
$$
The <p-adic logarithm> identifies $1+p\mathbb Z_p$ with $p\mathbb Z_p$, which is topologically isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. More explicitly, $a\mapsto(1+p)^a$ is an <isomorphism> from the additive <p-adic integers> onto $1+p\mathbb Z_p$: the logarithm of $1+p$ has <valuation> one and generates $p\mathbb Z_p$. Take the fixed field of $\mu_{p-1}$ in $C$.

For $p=2$, use instead
$$
\mathbb Z_2^\times=\{\pm1\}\times(1+4\mathbb Z_2).
$$
The <p-adic logarithm> identifies the second factor with $4\mathbb Z_2$, and $a\mapsto5^a$ identifies it with $\mathbb Z_2$. The fixed field of $\{\pm1\}$ in $C$ is the union of the maximal real subfields of the <cyclotomic fields> of $2$-power conductor. Thus in both cases the **cyclotomic Zp-extension** exists:
$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q[p^\infty]=C^{\Delta_p},\qquad
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q[p^\infty]/\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Z_p,}
$$
where $\Delta_p=\mu_{p-1}$ for odd $p$ and $\Delta_2=\{\pm1\}$.

The closed subgroup $p^n\mathbb Z_p$ is the unique subgroup of index $p^n$, so the <Galois correspondence> supplies the unique degree-$p^n$ field $\mathbb Q[p^n]$. In the odd case it is the $\Delta_p$-fixed field in $\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^{n+1}})$; in the even case it is the real subfield of $\mathbb Q(\mu_{2^{n+2}})$. These finite layers are all <totally real number fields>.

Now set $A=\mathbb Q[p^n]$, $B=\mathbb Q[p^m]$, and let $H_A$ be the ordinary <Hilbert class field> of $A$. Thus $H_A/A$ is abelian, unramified at finite primes, and split at every real place, with degree $h(A)$. If $m=n$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise the unique prime over $p$ is totally ramified in $B/A$, by the permitted total-ramification fact and multiplicativity of the <ramification index>. Any nontrivial intermediate field of $B/A$ is ramified at that prime. Hence
$$
H_A\cap B=A.
$$
It follows that the two extensions are <linearly disjoint field extensions>, and
$$
[BH_A:B]=[H_A:A]=h(A).
$$
Unramified extensions remain unramified after base change. The real places also remain split: $H_A$ and $B$ are totally real, so their compositum is totally real. Therefore $BH_A/B$ lies inside the ordinary <Hilbert class field> $H_B/B$. Its degree divides $[H_B:B]=h(B)$, proving
$$
\boxed{h(p^n)\mid h(p^m).}
$$
This is <class number divisibility with split real places>; using a class-field statement restricted to imaginary fields would unnecessarily omit the present real layers.

Write $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K)\cong\mathbb Z_p$. Its only finite subgroup is trivial. In particular, a real place cannot acquire complex inertia in this <Zp-extension>: the possible nontrivial inertia at a real place has order two. Thus every infinite place splits in the tower.

Suppose that no finite prime ramified. Every finite layer would then be an abelian <everywhere unramified extension of number fields>, including splitting at real places. Every layer would lie in the ordinary <Hilbert class field> of $K$, a finite extension. Their degrees are unbounded, a contradiction. Therefore **some finite prime must ramify**.

Let $v$ have residue characteristic $\ell\ne p$. Apply <local class field theory> to the corresponding <decomposition group>, a closed subgroup of $\Gamma$. The image of the unit group $\mathcal O_{K_v}^\times$ is the <inertia group>. Its first principal-unit subgroup is a <pro-l group>, so its continuous image in the <pro-p group> $\Gamma$ is trivial. The residue-unit quotient is the finite group $\mathbb F_{q_v}^\times$. Its image is finite, and is therefore also trivial in the torsion-free group $\mathbb Z_p$. The entire <inertia group> is trivial. Hence
$$
\boxed{v\text{ ramifies in }K_\infty/K\ \Longrightarrow\ v\mid p.}
$$
Equivalently, the <tame ramification> relation with a Frobenius element would force a tame inertia generator to satisfy $\tau^{q_v-1}=1$, which is impossible nontrivially in $\mathbb Z_p$.

For the final assertion, the <cyclotomic Zp-extension> of $K$ is $K\mathbb Q[p^\infty]$. Fix $v\mid p$ and put $E=K_v$, a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$. Let $C_n$ be the completion of $\mathbb Q[p^n]$ at its unique prime over $p$. This is a <totally ramified extension> of $\mathbb Q_p$ of degree $p^n$. In a compatible local algebraic closure,
$$
e(EC_n/E)
=\frac{e(EC_n/\mathbb Q_p)}{e(E/\mathbb Q_p)}
\geq\frac{p^n}{e(E/\mathbb Q_p)}.
$$
Here the inequality follows from the tower through $C_n$. The right side is unbounded. These composita occur among the completions of the cyclotomic tower over $K$, so $v$ has unbounded <ramification index>, in particular nontrivial <inertia group>. Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{Every prime }v\mid p\text{ ramifies in the cyclotomic Zp-extension.}}
$$
This argument allows an arbitrary finite intersection between $K$ and the rational cyclotomic tower; it does not assume disjointness.

Let $\mathcal G=\operatorname{Gal}(M_\infty/F)$. The <Galois group> sequence is
$$
1\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow\mathcal G\longrightarrow\Gamma\longrightarrow1.
$$
For $g\in\Gamma$ and $x\in X$, choose a lift $\widetilde g\in\mathcal G$ and define
$$
g\cdot x=\widetilde g\,x\,\widetilde g^{-1}.
$$
This is independent of the lift because $X$ is abelian. Write $X$ additively. As an abelian <pro-p group>, it is a compact $\mathbb Z_p$-<module>, with $a x$ defined by the limit of integer powers, and the conjugation action is continuous and $\mathbb Z_p$-linear. It extends to the <Iwasawa algebra>
$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]
=\varprojlim_{r,n}(\mathbb Z/p^r\mathbb Z)[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^n}].
$$
Indeed, use the finite quotients of $X$ obtained by intersecting it with open normal subgroups of $\mathcal G$; on each quotient the action factors through one of these finite group rings. This defines the natural <compact Galois module> structure, even when $X$ is not finitely generated. Choosing $\gamma$ identifies $\Lambda$ with $\mathbb Z_p[[T]]$ through $T=\gamma-1$.

The subgroup $(\gamma-1)X$ is closed: it is the image of the compact group $X$ under a continuous map. Every element $(\gamma-1)x$ is a <commutator> of a lift of $\gamma$ with $x$. Consequently
$$
(\gamma-1)X\subseteq\overline{[\mathcal G,\mathcal G]}.
$$
Conversely, in the quotient by $(\gamma-1)X$, the lift of $\gamma$ centralizes the image of $X$. Its powers are dense in the procyclic subgroup they generate. Since these powers map densely onto $\Gamma$, compactness shows that $\mathcal G$ is generated by $X$ and that procyclic subgroup. The quotient is therefore abelian. Hence
$$
\boxed{\overline{[\mathcal G,\mathcal G]}=(\gamma-1)X.}
$$
One can also see this by choosing a lift of $\gamma$. Since $\mathcal G$ is a <pro-p group>, the closed subgroup generated by this lift is $\mathbb Z_p$, and its map to $\Gamma$ is an isomorphism. This gives a <semidirect product> splitting.

The maximal abelian subextension $M/F$ is the fixed field of the closed <commutator subgroup>. Because $F_\infty/F$ is abelian, $F_\infty\subseteq M$. Restriction of the <Galois correspondence> to $X$ now gives the **coinvariant formula**
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Gal}(M/F_\infty)\cong X/(\gamma-1)X=X_\Gamma.}
$$
The closed image above is important: the formula is a quotient of compact groups, not a quotient by an unclosed abstract subgroup. This is <abelianization over a Zp-extension>.

= Solution
{parent=i}

Let $F_n=\mathbb Q(\mu_{p^{n+1}})$ and $F_0=\mathbb Q(\mu_p)$. The <cyclotomic character> identifies
$$
G=\operatorname{Gal}(F_\infty/\mathbb Q)
=\Delta\times\Gamma,\qquad
\Delta\cong(\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z)^\times,\quad
\Gamma\cong1+p\mathbb Z_p.
$$
Choose $\gamma$ with cyclotomic value $u=1+p$, and put
$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]=\mathbb Z_p[[T]],\qquad T=\gamma-1.
$$
The <Iwasawa algebra> of $G$ is $\Lambda[\Delta]$. Conjugation, independent of lift as in the preceding solution, makes $X$ a <compact Galois module> over this ring. It is the <p-ramified Iwasawa module>, not the unramified class-group module.

Because $p-1$ is a unit in $\mathbb Z_p$, the <Teichmüller character> $\omega$ gives orthogonal <character idempotents>
$$
e_\chi=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\chi(\delta)^{-1}\delta,
\qquad \chi=\omega^i,\quad0\leq i\leq p-2.
$$
They sum to one, so $X=\bigoplus_\chi X_\chi$, where $X_\chi=e_\chi X$. Each is a <module> over the one-variable ring $\Lambda$. Complex conjugation is the element $-1$ of $\Delta$; characters with $\chi(-1)=1$ are even, and those with $\chi(-1)=-1$ are odd.

The basic **Iwasawa-module rank theorem** in this tower gives
$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X_\chi=
\begin{cases}
0,&\chi\text{ even},\\
1,&\chi\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}}
$$
In particular,
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X=\frac{p-1}{2},\qquad
X^+\text{ is torsion},\qquad
\operatorname{rank}_\Lambda X^-=\frac{p-1}{2}.}
$$
Thus it would be incorrect to describe all of $X$ as a <torsion module>.

Here is the <class-field unit sequence> explaining these ranks. Let $U_\infty$ be the inverse limit, under local norms, of the pro-$p$ completions of the unit groups at the unique prime over $p$ in $F_n$. Let $\overline E_\infty$ be the inverse limit of the closures of the global units in these local unit groups, and let
$$
Y=\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Cl}(F_n)(p)
$$
under ideal norms. The <Artin reciprocity> maps yield
$$
0\longrightarrow\overline E_\infty
\longrightarrow U_\infty
\longrightarrow X
\longrightarrow Y
\longrightarrow0.
$$
The final module is the <unramified Iwasawa module> and is finitely generated torsion, as proved in the other essay. Local-unit theory gives $U_\infty$ <Iwasawa-module rank> $p-1$, with rank one in each $\Delta$-character. The <Leopoldt theorem for abelian number fields> gives $\overline E_\infty$ rank $(p-1)/2$, with rank one in each even character and zero in each odd character. In odd characters the possible norm-compatible roots of unity contribute a rank-zero term, not a free summand. Subtracting ranks in the exact sequence gives the displayed result and also finite generation.

For any character, the <Iwasawa module structure theorem> describes $X_\chi$ up to <pseudo-isomorphism>:
$$
X_\chi\sim
\Lambda^{r_\chi}
\oplus\bigoplus_j\Lambda/(p^{a_j})
\oplus\bigoplus_k\Lambda/(f_k(T)^{b_k}),
$$
where the $f_k$ are irreducible <distinguished polynomials> and the kernel and cokernel of a <pseudo-isomorphism> are finite. For a torsion component, its <characteristic ideal> is generated by
$$
p^{\sum_j a_j}\prod_k f_k(T)^{b_k}.
$$
The <Iwasawa invariants> are $\mu=\sum a_j$ and $\lambda=\sum b_k\deg f_k$. A characteristic ideal describes the elementary divisors only in aggregate; it is not an assertion that the module is cyclic or that an odd component is actually free.

The trivial character component $X_1$ is zero. Indeed, the maximal abelian pro-$p$ extension of $\mathbb Q$ unramified outside $p$ is precisely the rational <cyclotomic Zp-extension>, by the <Kronecker–Weber theorem>. Coprime descent using the <character idempotent> $e_1$, followed by the <abelianization over a Zp-extension> formula, identifies $(X_1)_\Gamma$ with the additional abelian pro-$p$ quotient over $\mathbb Q$. There is no such extension beyond the cyclotomic one, so $(X_1)_\Gamma=0$, and the <compact Nakayama lemma> gives $X_1=0$.

The central arithmetic description concerns the nontrivial even characters. Let $g_\chi(T)$ be the integral <Kubota-Leopoldt p-adic L-function> power series with convention
$$
g_\chi(u^{\,1-s}-1)=L_p(\chi,s).
$$
For positive integers $k$ of the matching parity, the interpolation is
$$
L_p(\chi,1-k)
=-\left(1-(\chi\omega^{-k})(p)p^{k-1}\right)
\frac{B_{k,\chi\omega^{-k}}}{k},
$$
where the Dirichlet character in the Euler factor is its primitive associate, and $B_{k,\eta}$ is a <generalized Bernoulli number>. The **Iwasawa main conjecture**, a theorem here, says
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{char}_\Lambda X_\chi=(g_\chi(T))
\quad(\chi\ne1,\ \chi\text{ even}).}
$$
The trivial character is excluded from this displayed analytic normalization: its $p$-adic zeta function has a pole and must be treated separately.

The proof mechanism starts with norm-compatible <cyclotomic units>, for instance ratios
$$
c_n(a)=\frac{1-\zeta_{p^{n+1}}^a}{1-\zeta_{p^{n+1}}}
\quad(p\nmid a),
$$
and their character projections. A <Coleman power series> encodes a norm-compatible local unit by a single power series. Applying its logarithmic derivative to these cyclotomic units produces the <p-adic L-function> above. If $C_\infty$ denotes their closed norm-limit module, this calculation determines
$$
\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(U_{\infty,\chi}/C_{\infty,\chi})=(g_\chi).
$$
The remaining global input is
$$
\operatorname{char}_\Lambda Y_\chi
=\operatorname{char}_\Lambda(\overline E_{\infty,\chi}/C_{\infty,\chi})
$$
for nontrivial even $\chi$. In the <class-field unit sequence> modulo $C_{\infty,\chi}$, multiplicativity of <characteristic ideals> cancels these two terms and gives the boxed formula for $X_\chi$.

There are two established routes to this global input. The <Mazur-Wiles theorem> constructs suitable abelian extensions from Galois representations associated with <modular forms> and the <Eisenstein ideal>; their sizes supply the missing divisibility. The <cyclotomic Euler system> route uses norm relations for cyclotomic units at auxiliary primes and descent to bound the class-group module. The <analytic class number formula> and the cyclotomic-unit index formula then supply the equality of characteristic ideals. These are substantial arithmetic theorems, rather than consequences of the abstract structure theorem alone.

A useful further result is the **Ferrero-Washington theorem**: $\mu(Y)=0$ for this cyclotomic tower, more generally for cyclotomic towers of abelian <number fields>. Its proof rules out an identically zero reduction modulo $p$ of the relevant $p$-adic $L$-series, using distribution of $p$-adic digits. Together with the main conjecture, it gives $\mu(X_\chi)=0$ for each nontrivial even $\chi$. Therefore these torsion components are finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_p$ up to finite error.

Finally, <Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory> relates the even $p$-ramified module to an odd unramified component:
$$
X_\chi\sim Y_{\omega\chi^{-1}}^{\,\iota}(1),
\qquad \iota(\gamma)=\gamma^{-1},
$$
where $(1)$ is the <Tate twist> by the cyclotomic character. The corresponding power-series substitution is
$$
T\longmapsto u(1+T)^{-1}-1.
$$
This explains why the $p$-ramified interpolation involves $u^{1-s}$ while the reflected class-group interpolation involves $u^s$. Both the involution and the twist matter. The rank theorem, the class-field sequence, the structure theorem and the explicit even-character characteristic ideals together give the known <compact Galois module> structure; none justifies replacing every odd component by a free module without further argument.

Let $F_n$ be the degree-$p^n$ layer, $\Gamma_n=\Gamma^{p^n}$, and
$$
\Lambda=\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]\cong\mathbb Z_p[[T]],
\qquad \omega_n(T)=(1+T)^{p^n}-1.
$$
Conjugation makes $Y$ a compact $\Lambda$-<module>. The goal is to show that it is finitely generated and has <Iwasawa-module rank> zero. This proof applies to every <Zp-extension>, not only a cyclotomic one, and makes no <Leopoldt conjecture> assumption.

By the ramification argument above, only primes over $p$ ramify in $F_\infty/F$, and at least one does. Every nonzero closed <inertia group> in $\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p$ is open. After replacing $F$ by a finite layer, every prime which ramifies is totally ramified in the remaining tower. The number $s\geq1$ of such primes then stays constant. This replacement does not affect whether $Y$ is a <torsion module>: $\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]$ is finite free over the <Iwasawa algebra> of an open subgroup, and the two module ranks vanish together.

Let $\mathcal G_n=\operatorname{Gal}(L_\infty/F_n)$, and let $D_n$ be its maximal abelian quotient. The <abelianization over a Zp-extension> formula gives an <exact sequence>
$$
0\longrightarrow Y_{\Gamma_n}
\longrightarrow D_n
\longrightarrow\Gamma_n
\longrightarrow0,
\qquad
Y_{\Gamma_n}=Y/\omega_nY.
$$
Choose a lift of a generator of $\Gamma_n$ if a splitting is desired. We will bound the $\mathbb Z_p$-rank of the middle term independently of $n$.

At each of the $s$ ramified primes, inertia in $L_\infty/F_n$ maps isomorphically onto $\Gamma_n$: its kernel is inertia in the unramified extension $L_\infty/F_\infty$, hence trivial, and the map onto the totally ramified base inertia is surjective. Thus each image in $D_n$ is procyclic and has $\mathbb Z_p$-rank at most one. No other finite prime contributes inertia. Quotienting $D_n$ by the closed subgroup generated by these $s$ images gives an abelian extension of $F_n$ unramified at all finite primes. Its <Galois group> is finite by <class field theory>, using the ordinary or narrow <ideal class group> according to the treatment of real places. Any infinite-place inertia is finite and does not affect the rank bound. Therefore $D_n$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_p$ and
$$
\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_p}D_n\leq s.
$$
The preceding <exact sequence> now yields the uniform bound
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z_p}(Y/\omega_nY)\leq s-1.}
$$
It does not assert that these <coinvariant modules> are always finite when several primes ramify.

At $n=0$, $Y/TY$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_p$, so $Y/(p,T)Y$ is finite. Lift a finite basis of this quotient to $Y$. The <compact Nakayama lemma> shows that these lifts generate $Y$ over $\Lambda$. Briefly, the quotient by their compact generated image satisfies $Q=(p,T)Q$. Every finite continuous $\Lambda$-quotient of $Q$ has nilpotent action by $(p,T)$: $p$ is nilpotent, and modulo $p$ a finite pro-$p$ action makes $\gamma-1$ nilpotent. Such a quotient must be zero. Finite quotients separate points of a compact <pro-p group>, so $Q=0$.

Suppose now that $Y$ had positive $\Lambda$-rank $r$. The <Iwasawa module structure theorem> provides a <pseudo-isomorphism> to
$$
\Lambda^r\oplus E
$$
with $E$ a finitely generated <torsion module>. Passing to $\Gamma_n$-<coinvariant modules> leaves a finite cokernel. But
$$
\Lambda/\omega_n\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z_p[\Gamma/\Gamma_n]
$$
is free of rank $p^n$ over $\mathbb Z_p$. Thus the <coinvariant modules> would have rank at least $r p^n$, contradicting the uniform bound $s-1$. Equivalently their ranks have asymptotic form $r p^n+O(1)$, with the torsion elementary divisors contributing only a bounded rank.

It follows that $r=0$. Since $\Lambda$ is an <integral domain>, rank zero means every element is killed by a nonzero scalar. Therefore
$$
\boxed{Y\text{ is a finitely generated torsion }\mathbb Z_p[[\Gamma]]\text{-module}.}
$$
This is the <unramified Iwasawa torsion theorem>. The crucial arithmetic inputs are finite class fields and the bounded number of ramified primes; neither vanishing of a $\mu$-invariant nor a cyclotomic main conjecture is required.
