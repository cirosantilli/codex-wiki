= Solution
{parent=i}

The first relation is vertical <hydrostatic equilibrium>. In a <thin disc>, the central object's gravity near the midplane is $g_z=-\Omega^2z$; the <pressure> <gradient> balances it. The second is an <equation of state> combining <ideal gas> <pressure> with isotropic <radiation pressure>. Write $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_p)$, so $p_g=\mathcal R\rho T$ and $p_r=4\sigma T^4/(3c)$; $\sigma$ is the <Stefan-Boltzmann constant> and $\mu_m$ the <mean molecular weight> in proton-mass units.

The third is local energy balance: the increase of upward <radiative flux> with height equals viscous dissipation. Here $\mu$ is <dynamic viscosity>, not mean molecular weight. A <Keplerian disc> has shear $r\,d\Omega/dr=-3\Omega/2$, so its heating rate is $\mu(r\Omega')^2=9\mu\Omega^2/4$. The fourth is optically thick <radiative diffusion>, with <opacity> per unit mass $\kappa$ and diffusion directed down the <temperature> <gradient>. Midplane symmetry gives $F(0)=0$ and oppositely directed fluxes from the two faces. \b[The four relations express vertical force balance, gas–radiation thermodynamics, local heating, and diffusive cooling.]

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Take the positive constant ratio $\lambda=p_r/p_g$. The <equation of state> implies
$$
\rho=\frac{4\sigma}{3c\lambda\mathcal R}T^3,\qquad p=(1+\lambda)\mathcal R\rho T\propto T^4.
$$
This is a <polytropic equation of state> of index three. Differentiating the <pressure> and using vertical <hydrostatic equilibrium> gives $4(1+\lambda)\mathcal R\rho\,T_z=-\rho\Omega^2z$. Hence
$$
T=T_0-\frac{\Omega^2z^2}{8(1+\lambda)\mathcal R}.
$$
Define the semi-thickness $H$ by $T(H)=0$ and put $s=1-z^2/H^2$. Then the <constant gas-radiation ratio vertical disc structure> is
$$
\boxed{T=T_0s,\qquad\rho=\rho_0s^3,\qquad p=p_0s^4,\qquad |z|<H,}
$$
with
$$
\boxed{T_0=\frac{\Omega^2H^2}{8(1+\lambda)\mathcal R},\quad\rho_0=\frac{\sigma\Omega^6H^6}{384c\lambda(1+\lambda)^3\mathcal R^4},\quad p_0=\frac{\rho_0\Omega^2H^2}{8}=\frac{\sigma\Omega^8H^8}{3072c\lambda(1+\lambda)^3\mathcal R^4}.}
$$
The <surface density> is the integral across both faces. The supplied polynomial integral gives $\int_0^1(1-x^2)^3dx=16/35$, so $\Sigma=(32/35)\rho_0H$. Substitution yields
$$
\boxed{420\lambda(1+\lambda)^3\Sigma=\left(\frac{\mu_m m_p}{k}\right)^4\frac\sigma cH^7\Omega^6.}
$$
All coefficients here are local in radial position; the derivation fixes the vertical profile rather than a global accretion rate. The zero-temperature surface is a formal boundary of this bulk model; optically thick diffusion ultimately fails in a thin photospheric layer.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iii}

For constant <opacity>, the ratio $T^3/\rho=3c\lambda\mathcal R/(4\sigma)$ cancels the height dependence in the <radiative diffusion> coefficient. Since $T_z=-\Omega^2z/[4(1+\lambda)\mathcal R]$, the <radiative flux> is
$$
\boxed{F(z)=\frac c\kappa\frac\lambda{1+\lambda}\Omega^2z.}
$$
Consequently $F_z=c\lambda\Omega^2/[\kappa(1+\lambda)]$. Equating this to the viscous heating rate gives
$$
\boxed{\mu=\frac{4c}{9\kappa}\frac\lambda{1+\lambda},\qquad |z|<H.}
$$
Thus the <dynamic viscosity> is uniform in height even though <pressure> and <mass density> decrease. Its units are mass per length per time, since $c/\kappa$ has those units. A pointwise pressure-proportional alpha prescription would be incompatible with this exact profile, which is why the subsequent alpha relation is vertically averaged.

{parent=1}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iv}

The required <pressure> integral uses $\int_0^1(1-x^2)^4dx=128/315$. With $\Sigma=(32/35)\rho_0H$ and $p_0/\rho_0=\Omega^2H^2/8$,
$$
\int_{-H}^Hp\,dz=\frac{256}{315}p_0H=\frac{\Sigma\Omega^2H^2}{9}.
$$
The <vertically averaged alpha closure for a mixed-pressure layer> therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\bar\nu=\frac{\alpha H^2\Omega}{9}.}
$$
The height-integrated <dynamic viscosity> is $2H\mu=8cH\lambda/[9\kappa(1+\lambda)]$. Equate it to $\bar\nu\Sigma$ to obtain
$$
\boxed{\frac\lambda{1+\lambda}=\frac{\alpha H\Omega\kappa\Sigma}{8c}.}
$$
These are integrated equalities, not an imposed pointwise relation $\mu(z)\propto p(z)$.

Now keep $\alpha$, the <opacity> and molecular constants independent of radius, and use <Keplerian rotation> $\Omega\propto r^{-3/2}$. In the gas-dominated limit, $\lambda\ll1$ but positive, the last equality gives $\lambda\propto H\Omega\Sigma$. The column-density relation gives $\lambda\Sigma\propto H^7\Omega^6$. Eliminating $\lambda$ yields $H^6\propto\Sigma^2\Omega^{-5}$, whence
$$
\boxed{\bar\nu\propto\Sigma^{2/3}\Omega^{-2/3}\propto r\Sigma^{2/3}\qquad(\lambda\ll1).}
$$
This is the <gas-pressure branch of a mixed-pressure alpha disk>. In the radiation-dominated limit, $\lambda/(1+\lambda)\to1$, so $H\propto(\Omega\Sigma)^{-1}$ and
$$
\boxed{\bar\nu\propto\Omega^{-1}\Sigma^{-2}\propto r^{3/2}\Sigma^{-2}\qquad(\lambda\gg1).}
$$
For <viscous stability of an accretion disk>, linearize the <Keplerian viscous diffusion equation> at fixed radius: a local <mass density> perturbation has diffusion coefficient $3\partial_\Sigma(\bar\nu\Sigma)$. The gas branch has $\bar\nu\Sigma\propto\Sigma^{5/3}$ and a positive derivative, so it smooths perturbations. The radiation branch has $\bar\nu\Sigma\propto\Sigma^{-1}$ and a negative derivative, giving <radiation-pressure viscous instability>. \b[The gas-pressure branch is viscously stable; the radiation-pressure branch is viscously unstable in this closure.] This concerns radial mass-transport stability on wavelengths where the vertically averaged thin-disc model applies.

= Solution

The boundary of a steady <Kida vortex> must be material. Put $E=x^2/b^2+y^2/a^2$. Requiring $\mathbf u\cdot\nabla E=0$ on the ellipse gives
$$
2xy\left(\frac\alpha{b^2}-\frac\beta{a^2}\right)=0,\qquad\beta=r^2\alpha.
$$
The relative <vorticity> is $\partial_xu_y-\partial_yu_x=-\beta-\alpha=-S+\zeta_0$, so the supplied <vorticity> relation implies
$$
\alpha+\beta=S-\zeta_0=\frac{S(r^2+1)}{r(r-1)}.
$$
Combining these independent conditions yields
$$
\boxed{\alpha=\frac S{r(r-1)},\qquad\beta=\frac{Sr}{r-1}.}
$$
For the usual positive orbital shear both are positive, the flow circulates clockwise and its streamlines have the same aspect ratio as the patch. The velocity coefficients in this question are unrelated to the alpha viscosity parameter in Question 1.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

The steady incompressible <shearing sheet> equation is
$$
\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\mathbf u+2\Omega\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf u=-\rho^{-1}\nabla p+2\Omega Sx\mathbf e_x.
$$
The final term follows from the local tidal potential $-\Omega Sx^2$. For the given linear velocity, the advective acceleration is $-\alpha\beta(x\mathbf e_x+y\mathbf e_y)$, while the <Coriolis acceleration> is $2\Omega\beta x\mathbf e_x+2\Omega\alpha y\mathbf e_y$. Equating components gives
$$
\frac{p_x}\rho=[\alpha\beta+2\Omega(S-\beta)]x,\qquad\frac{p_y}\rho=\alpha(\beta-2\Omega)y.
$$
The cross derivatives vanish, so the <pressure Hessian of an elliptical shearing-sheet vortex> integrates to
$$
\boxed{\frac p\rho=\frac12Ax^2+\frac12By^2+\text{constant},\quad A=\frac{S^2}{(r-1)^2}-\frac{2\Omega S}{r-1},\quad B=\frac{S^2}{(r-1)^2}-\frac{2\Omega S}{r(r-1)}.}
$$
The additive constant is fixed by exterior matching or the <pressure> reference. No zero-pressure free-boundary condition is imposed on an incompressible vortex patch: it is embedded in a surrounding fluid.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iii}

A pressureless test particle feels tidal gravity, <Coriolis acceleration> and <linear drag> toward the prescribed gas velocity. Its planar equations are
$$
\boxed{\ddot x-2\Omega\dot y=2\Omega Sx-\gamma(\dot x-\alpha y),\qquad\ddot y+2\Omega\dot x=-\gamma(\dot y+\beta x).}
$$
There is no gas-pressure force on this test particle. For $(x,y)=(X,Y)e^{\lambda t}$, the coefficient matrix is
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\lambda^2+\gamma\lambda-2\Omega S&-2\Omega\lambda-\gamma\alpha\\2\Omega\lambda+\gamma\beta&\lambda^2+\gamma\lambda\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Nontrivial solutions require a zero determinant. Expansion gives
$$
\boxed{\lambda^4+2\gamma\lambda^3+[2\Omega(2\Omega-S)+\gamma^2]\lambda^2+2\Omega(\alpha+\beta-S)\gamma\lambda+\alpha\beta\gamma^2=0.}
$$
Each root has a nonzero modal vector in the matrix kernel; real motions are obtained from conjugate pairs. Repeated roots may also supply polynomially weighted exponential solutions. Negative real parts make the centre linearly asymptotically attracting for trajectories governed by this interior linear flow.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iv}

Take every finite positive <drag coefficient> $\gamma>0$ and define
$$
K=2\Omega(2\Omega-S),\qquad L=\Omega(\alpha+\beta-S),\qquad P=\alpha\beta.
$$
The coefficients of the <characteristic polynomial> are $c_3=2\gamma$, $c_2=K+\gamma^2$, $c_1=2L\gamma$ and $c_0=P\gamma^2$. The nontrivial <Routh-Hurwitz criterion> reduces exactly to
$$
c_3c_2c_1-c_3^2c_0-c_1^2=4\gamma^2\left[L(K-L)+\gamma^2(L-P)\right]>0.
$$
The printed strict chain $K>L>P>0$ makes every coefficient positive and both bracket terms positive, so it proves attraction for every positive <drag coefficient>.

There is, however, a boundary error in the asserted necessity. To have the bracket positive for every $\gamma>0$, its constant and slope as a function of $\gamma^2$ must be nonnegative, and cannot both vanish. Together with coefficient positivity this gives the exact <drag-polynomial stability for all positive stopping rates>:
$$
\boxed{K\geq L\geq P>0,\qquad K>P.}
$$
Necessity follows by taking arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large positive $\gamma$; if one bracket coefficient is negative, the inequality fails in the corresponding limit. Sufficiency follows because at least one coefficient is strictly positive. Thus $K=L>P$ and $K>L=P$ still give strict decay at each finite positive $\gamma$. They need not give a uniform stability margin in the zero- or infinite-drag limit.

In a <Keplerian shearing sheet>, $S=3\Omega/2$ and
$$
\frac K{\Omega^2}=1,\quad\frac L{\Omega^2}=\frac{3(r+1)}{2r(r-1)},\quad\frac P{\Omega^2}=\frac9{4(r-1)^2}.
$$
The differences are
$$
\frac{K-L}{\Omega^2}=\frac{(r-3)(2r+1)}{2r(r-1)},\qquad\frac{L-P}{\Omega^2}=\frac{3(2r+1)(r-2)}{4r(r-1)^2}.
$$
Hence $r>3$ establishes the requested strict chain and trapping conclusion. At $r=3$, however, $K=L=\Omega^2$ and $P=9\Omega^2/16$, so
$$
c_3c_2c_1-c_3^2c_0-c_1^2=\frac74\Omega^2\gamma^4>0.
$$
All coefficients remain positive. This is an explicit counterexample within the stated vortex model: \b[$r=3$ also attracts particles for every finite positive <drag coefficient>, so the literal strict “if and only if” is false.] The exact Keplerian all-positive-drag condition is $r\geq3$. If $\gamma=0$ is included, the polynomial has zero and purely imaginary roots and there is no asymptotic attraction; “all drag coefficients” must mean positive drag. The local attraction claim presumes the particle remains in the patch, as is assured for sufficiently small perturbations of its centre.

= Solution
{parent=i}

Let $\Sigma_0$ be the uniform background <surface density>, $\mathbf u_0=-Sx\mathbf e_y$, and write $\Sigma=\Sigma_0+\Sigma'$, $\mathbf u=\mathbf u_0+\mathbf v$, $\Phi_d=\Phi_{d0}+\Phi_d'$. The background sheet has no horizontal gravitational <gradient>; its vertical field is irrelevant to the stated planar equations. Put $D_0=\partial_t-Sx\partial_y$. Keeping first-order terms gives
$$
\boxed{D_0\Sigma'+\Sigma_0(\partial_xv_x+\partial_yv_y)=0,}
$$
$$
\boxed{D_0v_x-2\Omega v_y=-\partial_x\left(c_s^2\frac{\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0}+\Phi_{d,m}'\right),\qquad D_0v_y+(2\Omega-S)v_x=-\partial_y\left(c_s^2\frac{\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0}+\Phi_{d,m}'\right),}
$$
$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\Phi_d'=4\pi G\Sigma'\delta(z),\qquad\Phi_{d,m}'=\Phi_d'(x,y,0,t).}
$$
The $-Sv_x$ contribution comes from $\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\mathbf u_0$, while the <Coriolis acceleration> supplies the remaining coupling. The prescribed tidal potential has no perturbation, and its background force already balances the background Coriolis term. The isothermal <pressure> linearizes to $c_s^2\nabla\Sigma'/\Sigma_0$.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Apply the <shearing-wave ansatz> to each disturbance. Acting on its phase gives
$$
D_0e^{i[k_x(t)x+k_y(t)y]}=i[(\dot k_x-Sk_y)x+\dot k_y y]e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}.
$$
Thus a spatially uniform amplitude system exists when
$$
\boxed{\dot{\mathbf k}=Sk_y\mathbf e_x,\qquad k_y=\text{constant},\qquad k_x(t)=k_x(0)+Sk_yt.}
$$
For a nonzero horizontal <wavenumber> $k=|\mathbf k|$, the <razor-thin disk Poisson kernel> follows from $(\partial_z^2-k^2)\widetilde\Phi_d'=4\pi G\widetilde\Sigma'\delta(z)$. The potential decays above and below the sheet as $\widetilde\Phi_{d,m}'e^{-k|z|}$. Integrating across the midplane gives the jump $-2k\widetilde\Phi_{d,m}'=4\pi G\widetilde\Sigma'$, hence
$$
\widetilde\Phi_{d,m}'=-\frac{2\pi G}{k}\widetilde\Sigma'.
$$
Substitute this potential and the phase evolution into the linearized equations. The <barotropic shearing-wave gravitational amplitude system> is
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}\dot{\widetilde\Sigma}'&=-i\Sigma_0(k_x\widetilde v_x+k_y\widetilde v_y),\\\dot{\widetilde v}_x-2\Omega\widetilde v_y&=-ik_x\left(c_s^2-\frac{2\pi G\Sigma_0}{k}\right)\frac{\widetilde\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0},\\\dot{\widetilde v}_y+(2\Omega-S)\widetilde v_x&=-ik_y\left(c_s^2-\frac{2\pi G\Sigma_0}{k}\right)\frac{\widetilde\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0}.\end{aligned}}
$$
The self-gravity sign is attractive and opposes the <pressure> response. The uniform $k=0$ <mass density> shift requires its own gravitational reference and is not obtained by dividing by $k$.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Write $B=2\Omega-S$, $A(t)=c_s^2-2\pi G\Sigma_0/k(t)$, and $w=i k_x\widetilde v_y-i k_y\widetilde v_x$. Differentiation, including $\dot k_x=Sk_y$, gives
$$
\dot w=iSk_y\widetilde v_y+i k_x(-B\widetilde v_x-ik_yA\widetilde\Sigma'/\Sigma_0)-ik_y(2\Omega\widetilde v_y-ik_xA\widetilde\Sigma'/\Sigma_0)=-iB(k_x\widetilde v_x+k_y\widetilde v_y).
$$
The two <pressure>/self-gravity cross terms cancel. Continuity gives $\dot{\widetilde\Sigma}'=-i\Sigma_0(k_x\widetilde v_x+k_y\widetilde v_y)$, so $\dot w=B\dot{\widetilde\Sigma}'/\Sigma_0$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\frac d{dt}\left[\frac{ik_x\widetilde v_y-ik_y\widetilde v_x}{\Sigma_0}-\frac{(2\Omega-S)\widetilde\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0^2}\right]=0.}
$$
The conserved amplitude is <linearized vortensity>: the perturbation of the absolute vertical <vorticity> divided by <surface density>,
$$
q=\frac{2\Omega+\partial_xu_y-\partial_yu_x}{\Sigma},\qquad q_0=\frac{2\Omega-S}{\Sigma_0}.
$$
A two-dimensional inviscid <barotropic fluid> materially conserves this <potential vorticity>. Both <pressure> enthalpy and gravitational forces are gradients, so they create no baroclinic <vorticity> source. Since $q_0$ is uniform, its linear perturbation obeys $D_0q'=0$, explaining why a <shearing wave> has constant <vortensity> amplitude.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iv}

For $k_y=0$ and nonzero $k_x$, the <wavenumber> is constant. Continuity gives $\dot{\widetilde\Sigma}'=-ik_x\Sigma_0\widetilde v_x$, and the conserved <vortensity> relation gives
$$
ik_x\widetilde v_y=\Sigma_0\widetilde q'+\frac{(2\Omega-S)\widetilde\Sigma'}{\Sigma_0}.
$$
Differentiate continuity and substitute radial momentum and this identity. The <forced axisymmetric density mode with vortensity> obeys
$$
\boxed{\ddot{\widetilde\Sigma}'+\omega_k^2\widetilde\Sigma'=-2\Omega\Sigma_0^2\widetilde q',\qquad\omega_k^2=c_s^2k_x^2-2\pi G\Sigma_0|k_x|+\kappa^2,\quad\kappa^2=2\Omega(2\Omega-S).}
$$
The constant forcing represents the stationary vortical or balanced component, which is lost if every mode is assumed to have nonzero frequency. Let $k_*=\pi G\Sigma_0/c_s^2=\kappa/(Qc_s)$. Completing the square gives
$$
\omega_k^2=c_s^2(|k_x|-k_*)^2+\kappa^2(1-Q^{-2}).
$$
For $Q>1$, every nonzero radial <wavenumber> has positive $\omega_k^2$. The solution is a constant balanced offset $-2\Omega\Sigma_0^2\widetilde q'/\omega_k^2$ plus bounded sine and cosine oscillations. <Pressure> stabilizes short wavelengths, rotation stabilizes long wavelengths, and self-gravity is strongest at intermediate wavelengths.

For $Q<1$, the <Toomre stability criterion> fails in the band
$$
\boxed{k_*\left(1-\sqrt{1-Q^2}\right)<|k_x|<k_*\left(1+\sqrt{1-Q^2}\right).}
$$
There $\omega_k^2<0$ and the homogeneous solutions grow or decay exponentially. The fastest rate is $s_{\max}=\kappa\sqrt{Q^{-2}-1}$ at $|k_x|=k_*$. Outside the band the modes oscillate; at its edges the frequency vanishes.

At $Q=1$, $\omega_k^2\geq0$ and it vanishes only at $|k_x|=\kappa/c_s$. At that critical <wavenumber>,
$$
\boxed{\widetilde\Sigma'(t)=C_0+C_1t-\Omega\Sigma_0^2\widetilde q' t^2.}
$$
This <Toomre marginal algebraic growth> is spectrally marginal, not bounded for all data: a nonzero conserved <vortensity> gives quadratic secular growth, and even zero <vortensity> can allow the linear term. Other wavenumbers still oscillate about a balanced offset. \b[$Q>1$ gives bounded axisymmetric modes, $Q<1$ allows exponential instability, and $Q=1$ has a zero-frequency marginal mode with possible algebraic growth.] These statements are axisymmetric; nonaxisymmetric shearing disturbances can have transient swing behavior. For $k_x=k_y=0$, continuity instead fixes the uniform <mass density> shift, while the velocities execute epicyclic motion; no $1/k$ gravitational formula is used.
