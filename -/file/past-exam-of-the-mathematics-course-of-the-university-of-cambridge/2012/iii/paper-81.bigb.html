Let $L/K$ be an extension of <number fields>, let $\alpha\in\mathcal O_L$ be a <primitive element of a field extension>, and let $f\in\mathcal O_K[X]$ be its monic <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element>. Put $R=\mathcal O_K[\alpha]$ and $S=\mathcal O_L$. The <Kummer-Dedekind theorem> applies at a nonzero <prime ideal> $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathcal O_K$ when $R_{\mathfrak p}=S_{\mathfrak p}$: equivalently, $\mathfrak p$ is outside the support of the finite-index quotient $S/R$. Write the factorization over the <residue field> $k=\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p$ as
$$
\overline f=\prod_{j=1}^r\overline g_j^{\,m_j},
$$
with distinct monic <irreducible polynomials> $\overline g_j$, and choose monic lifts $g_j\in\mathcal O_K[X]$. Then
$$
\boxed{\mathfrak pS=\prod_{j=1}^r\mathfrak P_j^{\,m_j},\qquad
\mathfrak P_j=(\mathfrak p,g_j(\alpha)),\qquad
f(\mathfrak P_j/\mathfrak p)=\deg\overline g_j.}
$$
Here $f(\mathfrak P_j/\mathfrak p)$ denotes the <residue-field degree>, and the <ramification index of a prime ideal> is $m_j$.

To prove this, the local index condition gives
$$
S/\mathfrak pS\simeq R/\mathfrak pR
\simeq k[X]/(\overline f)
\simeq\prod_{j=1}^r k[X]/(\overline g_j^{\,m_j}).
$$
The final isomorphism is the <Chinese remainder theorem>. Each factor is a local ring with unique maximal ideal generated by $\overline g_j$, so its inverse image in $S$ is exactly $\mathfrak P_j$, and its <residue field> is $k[X]/(\overline g_j)$. This proves the list of primes and the <residue-field degrees>. For the exponents, localize at $\mathfrak P_j$. The <ring of integers of a number field> is a <Dedekind domain>, so $S_{\mathfrak P_j}$ is a <discrete valuation ring>. If $\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak P_j}=\mathfrak P_j^{e_j}S_{\mathfrak P_j}$, the quotient has $e_j$ successive layers isomorphic to $S/\mathfrak P_j$, and hence $k$-dimension $e_j\deg\overline g_j$. The corresponding polynomial factor has $k$-dimension $m_j\deg\overline g_j$. Thus $e_j=m_j$, completing the proof rather than merely identifying the radicals.

For the calculation, set $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and $t=(\sqrt5-1)/2$. The <ring of integers of a quadratic field> gives $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[t]$, with $t^2+t-1=0$. The <algebraic norm> of $4+\sqrt5$ is $11$, so its <principal ideal> has <ideal norm> $11$. Its quotient ring has prime order and is therefore the <finite field> $\mathbb F_{11}$; consequently **$(4+\sqrt5)$ is a prime ideal**. In that <residue field>, $\sqrt5=-4=7$ and $t=3$.

Let $L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$. Since $t=\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}$ lies in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$, the permitted identity $\mathcal O_L=\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]$ gives $\mathcal O_L=\mathcal O_K[\zeta_5]$. Thus the local index condition holds at every prime. The relative <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element> is $X^2-tX+1$, whose reduction is
$$
X^2-3X+1=(X-5)(X-9)\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_{11}[X].
$$
The roots are distinct. Applying the <Kummer-Dedekind theorem> gives
$$
\boxed{(4+\sqrt5)\mathcal O_L
=(4+\sqrt5,\zeta_5-5)(4+\sqrt5,\zeta_5-9)
=(11,\zeta_5-5)(11,\zeta_5-9).}
$$
For the last equality, evaluation at $5$ or $9$ in $\mathbb F_{11}$ gives $\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1}=3$ and therefore $4+\sqrt5=5+2t=0$. Both descriptions are the same kernels of these evaluation maps. Each prime has <ideal norm> $11$, <residue-field degree> one and <ramification index of a prime ideal> one.

Let $F/K$ be a finite <Galois extension>, choose a <prime ideal> $\mathfrak q$ above $\mathfrak p$, and write $k_{\mathfrak q}$ and $k_{\mathfrak p}$ for the <residue fields>. The <decomposition group> and <inertia group> are
$$
\boxed{D_{\mathfrak q}=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(F/K):\sigma\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q\},\qquad
I_{\mathfrak q}=\ker\bigl(D_{\mathfrak q}\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Gal}(k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p})\bigr).}
$$
The <Frobenius automorphism> on the <finite field> $k_{\mathfrak q}$ is $x\mapsto x^{N\mathfrak p}$. Its inverse image defines a distinguished coset in $D_{\mathfrak q}/I_{\mathfrak q}$; a Frobenius element is a lift of that coset. At an <unramified> prime, $I_{\mathfrak q}=1$ and the lift is unique. Changing the prime above $\mathfrak p$ conjugates it, so a <Frobenius conjugacy class> is intrinsically attached to $\mathfrak p$. These are arithmetic Frobenius conventions.

Write $e=e(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak p)$, $f=f(\mathfrak q/\mathfrak p)$ and $g$ for the number of primes above $\mathfrak p$. The <Galois group> acts transitively on those primes, with stabilizer $D_{\mathfrak q}$. The <fundamental identity for prime decomposition> and the <orbit-stabilizer theorem> therefore give
$$
[F:K]=efg,\qquad |D_{\mathfrak q}|=[F:K]/g=ef.
$$
It remains to establish that the residue action has image of order $f$, rather than assume this in the desired equality.

Use the <decomposition group of a prime and local Galois group> identification $D_{\mathfrak q}=\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\mathfrak q}/K_{\mathfrak p})$. A generator of the <finite field extension> $k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}$ has a separable monic <minimal polynomial of an algebraic element>. Lift its coefficients to $\mathcal O_{K_{\mathfrak p}}$. The <Hensel lemma> lifts each of its distinct residue roots to $F_{\mathfrak q}$. One such lift has field degree at most $f$, while its residue generates the degree-$f$ <finite field extension>, so its field degree is exactly $f$ and its <ramification index of a prime ideal> is one. It therefore generates an <unramified extension> of degree $f$, and sending it to each of the other lifts gives its $f$ embeddings. Because $F_{\mathfrak q}/K_{\mathfrak p}$ is a <Galois extension>, these embeddings extend to its automorphisms. Their reductions realize every automorphism of $k_{\mathfrak q}/k_{\mathfrak p}$. Thus the residue action is surjective, and
$$
\boxed{|I_{\mathfrak q}|=|D_{\mathfrak q}|/f=e.}
$$

Now let $G$ be cyclic of order $\ell^2$, and let $H=\operatorname{Gal}(F/L)$ be its unique subgroup of order $\ell$. In a tower of <Galois extensions>, inertia maps onto inertia in the intermediate extension. This also follows from the just-proved order formula: its image has order $e(F/K)/e(F/L)=e(L/K)$, using multiplicativity of the <ramification index of a prime ideal> in a tower. If $\mathfrak p$ ramifies in $L/K$, this image in $G/H$ is nontrivial. Every proper subgroup of $G$ is contained in $H$, so inertia upstairs must be $G$. Hence
$$
\boxed{e(F/K)=\ell^2,\qquad \mathfrak p\text{ is totally ramified in }F/K.}
$$
The <fundamental identity for prime decomposition> then also forces a single prime above $\mathfrak p$ and <residue-field degree> one.

For a direct check of <unramified> behavior, the <cyclotomic polynomial> $\Phi_{25}$ divides $X^{25}-1$. Modulo $7$, the latter has derivative $25X^{24}=4X^{24}$ and has no common root with that derivative. Thus $\overline\Phi_{25}$ is separable, so $7$ does not divide its <polynomial discriminant>. The <field discriminant> divides this polynomial discriminant, and therefore $7$ is <unramified> in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{25})$. The <Frobenius automorphism> satisfies
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Frob}_7(\zeta_{25})=\zeta_{25}^{\,7}.}
$$
Indeed its residue action is seventh powering. The reduced $25$th roots of unity are distinct because $7\nmid25$, so the residue congruence identifies the exponent uniquely modulo $25$.

The <Galois group> is $(\mathbb Z/25\mathbb Z)^\times$, of order $\varphi(25)=20$. Since $7^2=49\equiv-1\pmod{25}$, $7^4\equiv1$ but $7^2\not\equiv1$. Consequently the <Frobenius automorphism> has order four. An <unramified> <decomposition group> is generated by that element, and the <orbit-stabilizer theorem> gives
$$
\boxed{g=20/4=5,\qquad f=4,\qquad e=1.}
$$
There are therefore **five primes above $7$**, each with <residue field> $\mathbb F_{7^4}$.

For a <number field> $K$ and a <modulus of a number field> $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\mathfrak m_\infty$, let $I_K(\mathfrak m)$ be the group of <fractional ideals> prime to $\mathfrak m_0$, and let $P_{K,1}(\mathfrak m)$ consist of <principal fractional ideals> $(a)$ with $a\equiv1$ at the finite modulus and $a>0$ at the real places in $\mathfrak m_\infty$. The <ray class group> is $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)=I_K(\mathfrak m)/P_{K,1}(\mathfrak m)$.

The <main theorem of class field theory> states that there is a unique finite abelian <ray class field> $K_{\mathfrak m}/K$ for which the <Artin reciprocity map> induces
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\simeq
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak m}/K).}
$$
It is <unramified> outside the finite modulus and has no ramification at real places omitted from $\mathfrak m_\infty$. More generally, subgroups $H$ with $P_{K,1}(\mathfrak m)\subseteq H\subseteq I_K(\mathfrak m)$ correspond, with inclusions reversed, to intermediate abelian fields, with <Galois group> $I_K(\mathfrak m)/H$. Every finite abelian extension occurs for some modulus. Equivalently, $K_{\mathfrak m}$ is the maximal abelian extension with <conductor of an abelian extension> dividing $\mathfrak m$. This states both reciprocity and the <existence theorem of global class field theory>.

There is no infinite component in the modulus $(5)$. The ordinary <ideal class group> of $\mathbb Q$ is trivial, and its <unit group> is $\{\pm1\}$. The <ray class exact sequence> therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Cl}_{(5)}(\mathbb Q)
\simeq(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}\simeq C_2.}
$$
Concretely, a <fractional ideal> prime to $5$ has a rational generator $r=a/b$ with $5\nmid ab$. Reducing $r$ modulo $5$ is well-defined up to multiplication by the units $\pm1$. Its ray class is trivial precisely when one of the two generators $r,-r$ is congruent to one. Thus the quotient has the two classes $\{1,4\}$ and $\{2,3\}$. No positivity restriction is imposed on a generator.

To identify the corresponding field, put $E=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$. The <maximal real subfield of the fifth cyclotomic field> is fixed by complex conjugation, the element $-1$ of $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times$. For a prime $\ell\ne5$, the <Frobenius automorphism> of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)/\mathbb Q$ is $\zeta_5\mapsto\zeta_5^\ell$. Its restriction to $E$ thus corresponds exactly to the residue class of $\ell$ modulo $\{\pm1\}$. Multiplicativity extends this to the <Artin reciprocity map> on all <fractional ideals> prime to $5$, whose kernel is exactly $P_{\mathbb Q,1}((5))$. Its <field discriminant> $5$ also confirms that $E$ is <unramified> outside $5$; moreover $E$ is a <totally real number field>. The uniqueness in the <main theorem of class field theory> now gives
$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q_{(5)}=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5).}
$$

Including the real place changes the <ray class group>: a principal generator must then be positive as well as congruent to one. The <residue and signature group of a modulus> gives
$$
\operatorname{Cl}_{(5)\infty}(\mathbb Q)
\simeq\bigl((\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times\times\{\pm1\}\bigr)
/\langle(-1,-1)\rangle
\simeq(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times.
$$
Here the isomorphism sends $(u,\varepsilon)$ to $\varepsilon u$. Consequently $\mathbb Q_{(5)\infty}=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$, of degree four. **The finite modulus gives the real quadratic field; the modulus with the real place gives the full cyclotomic field.** The printed finite-modulus assertion needs no correction.

Set $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{pq})$ and $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt p,\sqrt q)$. The distinct primes give independent square classes, so $[E:\mathbb Q]=4$ and $[E:K]=2$. In particular $K(\sqrt p)=K(\sqrt q)=E$: these are the same quadratic extension of $K$. We prove it is <unramified> by an explicit <field discriminant> calculation.

Since $p,q\equiv1\pmod4$, put $a=(1+\sqrt p)/2$, $b=(1+\sqrt q)/2$. Both are <algebraic integers>. The ring $R=\mathbb Z[a,b]\subseteq\mathcal O_E$ has basis $1,a,b,ab$. Up to the ordering of the basis, its <trace pairing> matrix is the tensor product of the quadratic trace matrices, because the four embeddings of $E$ choose the two radical signs independently. The two quadratic matrices are
$$
T_p=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&(p+1)/2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
T_q=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&(q+1)/2\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det T_p=p,\quad\det T_q=q.
$$
Thus
$$
\operatorname{disc}(R)=\det(T_p\otimes T_q)=p^2q^2.
$$
On the other hand, $pq$ is squarefree and congruent to one modulo four, so $d_K=pq$. Let $j=[\mathcal O_E:R]$. The <discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice> and the <relative discriminant> tower formula give
$$
\frac{p^2q^2}{j^2}=d_E
=d_K^{[E:K]}N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{E/K})
=p^2q^2N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak d_{E/K}).
$$
The <ideal norm> on the right is a positive integer, while $j$ is a positive integer. Hence $N(\mathfrak d_{E/K})=1/j^2$ forces $j=1$ and $\mathfrak d_{E/K}=\mathcal O_K$. Its prime divisors are exactly the ramified finite primes, so **$E/K$ is unramified at every finite prime**, including primes above $2,p,q$. This also proves $d_E=p^2q^2$, agreeing with the <discriminant of a biquadratic field> formula.

Every embedding of $E$ is real, so the real places of $K$ split as well. The extension is quadratic, hence abelian. The ordinary <Hilbert class field characterization> therefore implies
$$
\boxed{\mathbb Q(\sqrt p,\sqrt q)\subseteq H_K.}
$$
Finally, the <Artin reciprocity map> identifies $\operatorname{Cl}(K)$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(H_K/K)$. Restriction onto $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)\simeq C_2$ is surjective, so the <ideal class group> has a quotient of order two. Therefore **$\boxed{|\operatorname{Cl}(K)|\text{ is even}}$.**

= Solution

We prove the <Dirichlet density> form of the <Chebotarev density theorem>. Let $L/K$ be a finite <Galois extension> with <Galois group> $G$, let $C$ be a <conjugacy class> in $G$, and let $S_C$ consist of the <unramified> prime ideals of $K$ whose <Frobenius conjugacy class> is $C$. The assertion is
$$
\boxed{\delta(S_C)=\lim_{s\downarrow1}
\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S_C}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}}
{\log(1/(s-1))}=\frac{|C|}{|G|}.}
$$
The same result holds for any union of <conjugacy classes>, by addition. The following proof uses exactly the allowed one-dimensional <Artin L-function> behavior at $1$ and the permitted induction and Euler-product properties.

First determine the <order of vanishing> at $1$ for an arbitrary <character of a representation> $\chi$ of $G$. The <Brauer induction> theorem supplies subgroups $H_j\leq G$, <linear characters> $\psi_j$ of $H_j$, and integers $n_j$, possibly negative, such that
$$
\chi=\sum_jn_j\operatorname{Ind}_{H_j}^G\psi_j.
$$
Put $K_j=L^{H_j}$. Compatibility of <Artin L-functions> with <induced representations> and direct sums gives, initially for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$,
$$
L_K(\chi,s)=\prod_jL_{K_j}(\psi_j,s)^{n_j}.
$$
Each $\psi_j$ is a one-dimensional <Galois representation> for $L/K_j$, so the stated analytic assumptions apply to every factor. A trivial $\psi_j$ contributes a simple pole; a nontrivial $\psi_j$ is holomorphic and nonzero at one. Thus the product is meromorphic near one and
$$
\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_K(\chi,s)
=-\sum_jn_j\langle\psi_j,1_{H_j}\rangle_{H_j}.
$$
The <character inner product> is $\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_G=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\alpha(g)\overline{\beta(g)}$. Applying <Frobenius reciprocity> to each induced term gives
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{ord}_{s=1}L_K(\chi,s)
=-\sum_jn_j\langle\operatorname{Ind}_{H_j}^G\psi_j,1_G\rangle_G
=-\langle\chi,1_G\rangle_G.}
$$
In particular, a nontrivial <irreducible character> has order zero, so its <Artin L-function> is holomorphic and nonzero near one. For the trivial character, $L_K(1_G,s)=\zeta_K(s)$, the <Dedekind zeta function> of the base field $K$, and its pole is simple. This argument establishes the behavior needed at one, not holomorphy everywhere for arbitrary <Artin L-functions>.

Next convert that order calculation into a prime sum. For an <irreducible character> $\chi$, choose a unitary representation of dimension $d_\chi$ affording it, and choose a <Frobenius automorphism> $g_{\mathfrak p}$ at each <unramified> prime. The logarithm specified by the absolutely convergent <Euler product> gives, for real $s>1$,
$$
\log L_K(\chi,s)
=\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}\sum_{m\geq1}
\frac{\chi(g_{\mathfrak p}^{\,m})}{m(N\mathfrak p)^{ms}}
+R_\chi(s).
$$
Only finitely many ramified primes occur. Their Euler-factor logarithms $R_\chi(s)$ stay bounded near one, since the relevant eigenvalues have modulus one and $(N\mathfrak p)^{-1}<1$. Also $|\chi(g)|\leq d_\chi$, so the contribution with $m\geq2$ is uniformly bounded for $s\geq1$ by
$$
\sum_{\mathfrak p}\sum_{m\geq2}
\frac{d_\chi}{m(N\mathfrak p)^{ms}}
\leq2d_\chi\sum_{\mathfrak p}(N\mathfrak p)^{-2}<\infty.
$$
Convergence follows, for example, from the convergent ideal series defining $\zeta_K(2)$. Therefore
$$
\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}
\frac{\chi(g_{\mathfrak p})}{(N\mathfrak p)^s}
=\log L_K(\chi,s)+O(1).
$$
Writing $a_\chi=\langle\chi,1_G\rangle_G$, the order computation means $L_K(\chi,s)=(s-1)^{-a_\chi}U_\chi(s)$, where $U_\chi$ is holomorphic and nonzero near one. Choose its local logarithm. Along the real interval approaching one, this and the Euler-product logarithm differ only by a fixed multiple of $2\pi i$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\sum_{\mathfrak p\ \mathrm{unramified}}
\frac{\chi(g_{\mathfrak p})}{(N\mathfrak p)^s}
=a_\chi\log\frac1{s-1}+O(1).}
$$
Only the trivial <irreducible character> has $a_\chi=1$; all the others have $a_\chi=0$.

Finally, <character orthogonality> makes the <irreducible characters> an orthonormal basis of class functions. For $c\in C$, the coefficient of $\chi$ in the class indicator is $\langle1_C,\chi\rangle_G=(|C|/|G|)\overline{\chi(c)}$. Hence
$$
1_C(g)=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\chi\in\operatorname{Irr}(G)}
\overline{\chi(c)}\chi(g).
$$
Sum this identity over the <unramified> primes with weight $(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}$. The finite character sum and the boxed estimates give
$$
\boxed{\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S_C}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}
=\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log\frac1{s-1}+O(1).}
$$
Dividing by $\log(1/(s-1))$ proves the asserted <Dirichlet density>. The same Euler-product calculation with $\zeta_K$ gives $\sum_{\mathfrak p}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}=\log(1/(s-1))+O(1)$, so using all primes as the denominator is equivalent. Finitely many ramified primes do not affect either limit. No prime-counting asymptotic or natural-density Tauberian step is required by this proof.
