= Solution
{parent=i}

Take $0<r_1<r_2$ and positive mean <kinematic viscosity> $\bar\nu$. The <steady state> of the supplied <viscous evolution of an accretion disk> equation satisfies $\mathcal F'=-2\pi rS(r)$. The outer no-flux condition therefore gives
$$
\mathcal F(r)=2\pi\int_r^{r_2}vS(v)\,dv.
$$
Positive $S$ produces positive inward <accretion rate> in this sign convention. Set $Y=r^{1/2}\bar\nu\Sigma$. In a <Keplerian disk>, the <viscous torque in an accretion disk> is proportional to $Y$, so the <zero-torque inner boundary condition> gives $Y(r_1)=0$. Since $Y'=\mathcal F/(6\pi r^{1/2})$, a second integration yields
$$
\boxed{\Sigma(r)=\frac{1}{3\bar\nu(r)r^{1/2}}\int_{r_1}^{r}u^{-1/2}\left[\int_u^{r_2}vS(v)\,dv\right]du.}
$$
This is the <steady viscous disk supplied by a radial source>. It does not require $\bar\nu$ to be independent of radius: the equation directly determines $\bar\nu\Sigma$. If viscosity depends on the local thermodynamic state, the displayed formula is an implicit relation completed by a separate closure. For nonnegative integrable $S$ it gives nonnegative <surface density of a disk>, and the inner <accretion rate> equals the total supplied mass per unit time, $2\pi\int_{r_1}^{r_2}rS(r)dr$. Differentiating both nested integrals recovers the flux and the differential equation.

= Solution

Interpret the outer <Dirac delta function> as a boundary source carrying its full mass, or as the limit of a narrow source lying just inside $r_2$. Then $\int_u^{r_2}vS(v)dv=r_2S_0$ for $r_1<u<r_2$, and
$$
\boxed{\Sigma(r)=\frac{2r_2S_0}{3\bar\nu(r)}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r}}\right)=\frac{\dot M}{3\pi\bar\nu(r)}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{r_1}{r}}\right),\qquad \dot M=2\pi r_2S_0.}
$$
Thus the interior is the usual steady <Keplerian accretion disk> with a constant inward <accretion rate>. The product $\bar\nu\Sigma$ vanishes at the stress-free inner edge, and the <surface density of a disk> does too if the viscosity has a finite nonzero boundary value; far from that edge $\bar\nu\Sigma$ approaches $\dot M/(3\pi)$. Its density need not itself be constant because the viscosity may vary.

The <outer-edge source of a viscous disk> requires care about the boundary trace. The flux just inside the injecting edge is $\mathcal F(r_2^-)=\dot M$, while the no-flux value outside the source is $\mathcal F(r_2^+)=0$; the source produces the intervening jump. A simultaneously continuous interior condition $\mathcal F(r_2^-)=0$ would contradict nonzero steady injection. A symmetrically regularized delta integrated only up to its center can instead give half its full weight; that would mean a different supplied mass, not a different interior solution for the same $\dot M$. The physically normalized one-sided limiting source removes this convention ambiguity.

In the vertical equations, $\mu$ denotes <dynamic viscosity>, while $\mu_m$ is the <mean molecular weight> in units of the proton mass $m_p$. The symbol $k$ denotes the <Boltzmann constant>. For the intended <hydrostatic equilibrium> calculation, put $P=K\rho^\gamma$ with $K>0$, $\gamma>1$, and set $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_p)$. Dividing the hydrostatic equation by $\rho$ and integrating from the symmetric midplane gives
$$
\frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}=\frac{\gamma K}{\gamma-1}\rho_m^{\gamma-1}-\frac{\Omega^2z^2}{2}.
$$
The <specific enthalpy> vanishes at the free surface. Hence the <polytropic vertical structure in stellar gravity> is
$$
\boxed{\rho(z)=\rho_m\left(1-\frac{z^2}{H^2}\right)^{1/(\gamma-1)},\qquad H^2=\frac{2\gamma K\rho_m^{\gamma-1}}{(\gamma-1)\Omega^2}=\frac{2\gamma\mathcal R T_m}{(\gamma-1)\Omega^2}.}
$$
On $|z|<H$, the <ideal gas> relation gives $T=T_m(1-z^2/H^2)$ and $P=P_m(1-z^2/H^2)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$; the vacuum extension sets $\rho=P=0$ outside. The formula needs $\gamma>1$ for a finite positive semi-thickness and assumes the harmonic vertical gravity remains valid through this height.

There is a genuine inconsistency if all four printed structure equations are imposed literally with $F=0$ and finite positive <opacity>: <radiative diffusion> then requires $dT/dz=0$, whereas the polytropic <temperature> above has a nonzero <gradient> away from the midplane. The intended result is valid for the hydrostatic <polytropic equation of state> with the radiative closure replaced or neglected, for example an adiabatic opaque limit. It is not an exact simultaneous solution of the stated finite-opacity radiative system. With that radiative system and a nonvacuum <ideal gas>, $\gamma=1$ instead gives an isothermal solution
$$
\rho=\rho_m\exp\left[-\frac{\Omega^2z^2}{2\mathcal R T_m}\right],
$$
which has no finite free surface. For $\gamma\ne1$, constant $T$ and constant $K$ would force constant density, contradicting hydrostatic balance on a nontrivial vertical interval. This is the <zero-radiative-flux obstruction for a polytropic ideal gas>.

= Solution

Use a gas-pressure-supported, <optically thick> <alpha disk>, with $\mathcal R=k/(\mu_m m_p)$ and positive parameters. Here $\sigma$ is the Stefan–Boltzmann constant in the <Stefan–Boltzmann law>, and $F_+$ and $F_-$ below are heating and cooling flux estimates per disk face. <Hydrostatic equilibrium> and the <ideal gas> relation give $\mathcal R T\sim\Omega^2H^2$, while $\rho\sim\Sigma/H$. Integrating viscous heating over one semi-thickness and estimating <radiative diffusion> give
$$
F_+\sim\mu\Omega^2H\sim\alpha\Sigma\mathcal R T\Omega\sim\alpha\Sigma\Omega^3H^2,\qquad F_-\sim\frac{\sigma T^4}{\kappa\rho H}\sim\frac{\sigma}{\kappa_0}T^{-6}\Sigma^{-4/3}H^{1/3}.
$$
Here the stipulated <negative hydrogen ion opacity> exponent is $\kappa=\kappa_0\rho^{1/3}T^{10}$, which is a local approximation distinct from other empirical <opacity> fits. Substituting $T\sim\Omega^2H^2/\mathcal R$ and equating heating and cooling gives
$$
\boxed{H^{41/3}\sim\frac{\sigma}{\kappa_0}\mathcal R^6\Omega^{-15}\alpha^{-1}\Sigma^{-7/3}.}
$$
Dropping numerical constants of order one is essential here. This is the <negative-hydrogen-opacity alpha-disk thickness scaling>. Its angular thickness estimate is
$$
\boxed{\frac Hr\sim\left(\frac{\sigma}{\alpha\kappa_0}\right)^{3/41}\mathcal R^{18/41}\Sigma^{-7/41}\frac{\Omega^{-45/41}}r.}
$$
For a central mass $M$, <Keplerian rotation> gives $\Omega=(GM/r^3)^{1/2}$, so $H/r\propto r^{53/82}\Sigma(r)^{-7/41}$. The isothermal <sound speed> $c_s^2=\mathcal R T$ is
$$
\boxed{c_s\sim\Omega H\propto\Omega^{-4/41}\Sigma^{-7/41}\propto r^{6/41}\Sigma(r)^{-7/41}.}
$$
An adiabatic <sound speed> differs by the constant factor $\sqrt\gamma$ if the adiabatic index is fixed. For $\Sigma\propto r^{-p}$, constant $\alpha$, and fixed composition, the <disk aspect ratio> grows as $r^{(53+14p)/82}$, so there is <disk flaring> when $p>-53/14$; $c_s\propto r^{(6+7p)/41}$. The problem supplies no numerical parameters or specified $\Sigma(r)$, so no unique numerical $H/r$ or unconditional radial power can be inferred.

If the additional standard closure of a constant inward <accretion rate> well outside a zero-torque edge is intended, $\bar\nu\sim\alpha\Omega H^2$ and $\bar\nu\Sigma\simeq\dot M/(3\pi)$ imply $\Sigma\propto(\alpha\Omega H^2)^{-1}$. Eliminating it gives $H^9\propto\Omega^{-38/3}$ at fixed $\dot M,\alpha$, hence $H/r\propto r^{10/9}$ and $c_s\propto r^{11/18}$. These stronger powers use that extra steady-flow assumption. All these local scalings cease to apply if the <thin disk> or the stated <opacity> regime fails.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

Assume the <vertical dynamical timescale of a disk> is shorter than the <thermal timescale of an accretion disk>, which is itself shorter than the <viscous timescale>. This ordering is controlled in a <thin disk> with $\alpha\ll1$: $t_{\rm dyn}\sim\Omega^{-1}$, $t_{\rm th}\sim(\alpha\Omega)^{-1}$ and $t_\nu\sim(r/H)^2t_{\rm th}$. Therefore compare heating and cooling at fixed <surface density of a disk>, radius, $\alpha$ and composition. Vertical <hydrostatic equilibrium> adjusts on the <vertical dynamical timescale of a disk>, giving $H\propto T^{1/2}$ and $\rho\propto T^{-1/2}$. Consequently the stipulated <negative hydrogen ion opacity> varies as $\kappa\propto T^{10-1/6}=T^{59/6}$. The rates per disk face then scale as
$$
\boxed{Q^+\sim\alpha\Sigma\mathcal R\Omega T\propto T,\qquad Q^-\sim\frac{\sigma T^4}{\kappa\Sigma}\propto T^{-35/6}.}
$$
Thus heating increases while cooling decreases after a positive <temperature> perturbation. At equilibrium $Q^+=Q^-=Q_0$, a small change gives $\delta(Q^+-Q^-)=(41/6)(Q_0/T)\delta T$, which drives the perturbation further from equilibrium for positive effective <heat capacity>. A negative <temperature> perturbation likewise increases the net cooling. This proves <thermal instability from negative hydrogen ion opacity> within the local closure; it is the temperature-slope test of <thermal stability of an accretion disk>. Holding volume density rather than <surface density of a disk> fixed would give a different cooling exponent, but still the same qualitative instability; the fixed-column comparison is the relevant thin-disk thermal test.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Let $K=\rho_0|\mathbf u|^2/2$ be the <kinetic energy> per unit volume. Dot the momentum equation with $\rho_0\mathbf u$. The <Coriolis acceleration> does no work because $\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf u)=0$. <Incompressibility> turns the <advection> and <pressure> terms into divergences, yielding
$$
\boxed{\partial_tK+\nabla\cdot\bigl[(K+P)\mathbf u\bigr]=\rho_0\Omega^2(3x-2qz)u_x.}
$$
Thus $\mathbf F=(K+P)\mathbf u$ is the <energy flux> and $S=\rho_0\Omega^2(3x-2qz)u_x$ is the source in the requested kinetic-energy form.

To decide about <conservation of energy>, absorb the ordinary radial <shearing-sheet tidal potential> $\Phi_t=-3\Omega^2x^2/2$ into the density. Its material derivative cancels the $3x$ work term:
$$
\boxed{\partial_t(K+\rho_0\Phi_t)+\nabla\cdot\bigl[(K+P+\rho_0\Phi_t)\mathbf u\bigr]=-2\rho_0\Omega^2qz\,u_x.}
$$
For $q=0$ this is the usual conservative <Jacobi energy in a shearing sheet>, subject to vanishing boundary energy flux. For $q\ne0$, the extra body force has nonzero <curl>, $\nabla\times[-2\Omega^2qz\mathbf e_x]=-2\Omega^2q\mathbf e_y$, so no scalar potential can absorb it. A potential $2\Omega^2qxz$ would necessarily introduce a vertical force $-2\Omega^2qx\mathbf e_z$, absent from the model. Therefore **this vertical-shear model does not generically conserve the standard total mechanical energy**: its maintained shear/irradiation background can supply or remove energy. This is the <energy balance of the incompressible vertical-shear model>.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Write the proposed background as $\mathbf U=U(x,z)\mathbf e_y$, $U=-3\Omega x/2+q\Omega z$. It satisfies <incompressibility>, and $\mathbf U\cdot\nabla\mathbf U=U\partial_y\mathbf U=0$. A constant <pressure> has zero <gradient>. The <Coriolis acceleration> is $-2\Omega\mathbf e_z\times\mathbf U=2\Omega U\mathbf e_x=-(3x-2qz)\Omega^2\mathbf e_x$, which exactly cancels the prescribed body force. Thus it is a <steady state>.

**The background is ordinary Keplerian radial shear together with vertical shear:** $\partial_xU=-3\Omega/2$ and $\partial_zU=q\Omega$. At a given radius, different heights have different azimuthal velocities. The small $q$ in this question measures vertical shear; it is distinct from the <orbital shear parameter>, whose radial value here remains $3/2$. The <vertical shear instability> draws on this height-dependent differential rotation.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Set $\mathbf k=(k_x,0,k_z)\ne0$. <Incompressibility> gives $(\mathbf k\cdot\tilde{\mathbf u})f'(\xi)e^{st}=0$. Since $f$ is nonconstant and differentiable, it follows that $\mathbf k\cdot\tilde{\mathbf u}=0$; therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathbf u'\cdot\nabla\mathbf u'=e^{2st}ff'(\tilde{\mathbf u}\cdot\mathbf k)\tilde{\mathbf u}=0.}
$$
The background has no <advection> of a $y$-independent perturbation. Its only remaining cross-advection term is $(\mathbf u'\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U=(-3\Omega u'_x/2+q\Omega u'_z)\mathbf e_y$. The velocity/time terms have spatial factor $f$, while $\nabla P'=\tilde P\mathbf k g'(\xi)e^{st}$. For nonzero <pressure> amplitude, $g'$ must therefore be a constant multiple of $f$; absorbing that multiple into $\tilde P$ gives $g'=f$. An additive spatial constant in $g$ is a <pressure> gauge. If $\tilde P=0$, there is no such restriction on the unused <pressure> profile.

Matching coefficients gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
s\tilde u_x&=-k_x\tilde P/\rho_0+2\Omega\tilde u_y,\\
s\tilde u_y&=-\Omega\tilde u_x/2-q\Omega\tilde u_z,\\
s\tilde u_z&=-k_z\tilde P/\rho_0,\\
k_x\tilde u_x+k_z\tilde u_z&=0.
\end{aligned}}
$$
Because the quadratic perturbation term vanishes identically, these amplitudes give an <exact single-phase incompressible perturbation of a shear flow>, not merely a first-order truncation. Any sufficiently differentiable profile with $g'=f$ works locally; boundary conditions may restrict the profiles in a particular domain.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Eliminate <pressure> by multiplying the radial equation by $k_z$ and subtracting $k_x$ times the vertical equation. For $k_z\ne0$, incompressibility gives $\tilde u_z=-(k_x/k_z)\tilde u_x$, and the pressure-free relation is $s(k_x^2+k_z^2)\tilde u_x/k_z=2\Omega k_z\tilde u_y$. Substitution in the azimuthal equation yields
$$
\boxed{s^2=\Omega^2\frac{2qk_xk_z-k_z^2}{k_x^2+k_z^2}=-\Omega^2\frac{k_z^2}{k^2}\left(1-2q\frac{k_x}{k_z}\right).}
$$
Thus the <vertical shear instability> occurs when $2qk_xk_z>k_z^2$, or $2q(k_x/k_z)>1$ for $k_z\ne0$. Otherwise the nonzero modes oscillate; equality is marginal in the exponential-growth sense. With $q>0$, growing disturbances have $k_x/k_z>1/(2q)$ and are strongly inclined in <wavevector> space.

Put $R=k_x/k_z$. Maximizing $(2qR-1)/(1+R^2)$ gives $qR^2-R-q=0$. For $q>0$, the unstable maximizing root is
$$
R_{\max}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4q^2}}{2q},\qquad \boxed{s_{\max}=\Omega\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{1+4q^2}-1}{2}}=\Omega q\,[1+O(q^2)].}
$$
More precisely its bracket is $1-q^2/2+O(q^4)$, and $R_{\max}\sim q^{-1}$. This is the <maximum growth rate of the vertical shear instability>. For negative $q$, the maximum uses the corresponding negative root and is asymptotically $\Omega|q|$. At $q=0$ there is no exponentially growing mode. The undivided formula handles $k_z=0$: $s^2=0$, not an exponential instability; an allowed vertical velocity can instead force a secular azimuthal change. The excluded $\mathbf k=0$ is a spatially uniform disturbance and does not belong to the stipulated nonconstant one-phase family.

= Solution
{parent=i}

The <razor-thin disk approximation> uses vertically integrated <pressure> and <surface density of a disk>. To close the printed equations, interpret the <barotropic equation of state> as $P=P(\Sigma)$, or supply an equivalent fixed vertical conversion from the printed $\rho$ to $\Sigma$. Without that conversion, the literal $P=P(\rho)$ does not close this two-dimensional system. Set $c_s^2=(dP/d\Sigma)_0>0$, and write $\Sigma=\Sigma_0+\sigma$, $\mathbf u=\mathbf u_0+\mathbf u'$, $h'=P'/\Sigma_0=c_s^2\sigma/\Sigma_0$. <Axisymmetric flow> here means $\partial_y=0$, so background <advection> vanishes. The background shear enters through $\mathbf u'\cdot\nabla\mathbf u_0=-3\Omega u'_x\mathbf e_y/2$. The complete <linearized equations> are
$$
\boxed{\partial_tu'_x=2\Omega u'_y-\partial_xh',\qquad \partial_tu'_y=-\frac\Omega2u'_x,\qquad \partial_t\sigma=-\Sigma_0\partial_xu'_x,\qquad h'=\frac{c_s^2}{\Sigma_0}\sigma.}
$$
These are the compressible <Keplerian shearing sheet> equations for an <inertial-acoustic wave>. No perturbed self-gravity is present in the given force law, so no gravitational potential perturbation is to be added. This closure is the <barotropic closure of a razor-thin disk>.

For a <normal mode> proportional to $e^{ikx-i\omega t}$, the linear equations give
$$
-i\omega U_x=2\Omega U_y-ik\frac{c_s^2}{\Sigma_0}\hat\sigma,\qquad -i\omega U_y=-\frac\Omega2U_x,\qquad -i\omega\hat\sigma=-ik\Sigma_0U_x.
$$
For $\omega\ne0$, eliminate $U_y=-i\Omega U_x/(2\omega)$ and $\hat\sigma=k\Sigma_0U_x/\omega$. The <density-wave dispersion relation in a non-self-gravitating disk> is
$$
\boxed{\omega^2=\Omega^2+c_s^2k^2.}
$$
The $\Omega^2$ term is the <radial epicyclic frequency> squared for <Keplerian rotation>: <Coriolis force> and background shear restore an orbital displacement. The $c_s^2k^2$ term is acoustic restoration by a <pressure> <gradient>. Long wavelengths are mainly <epicyclic motion>; short wavelengths are mainly <acoustic waves>. The given model contains no disk self-gravity term.

For completeness, the full three-variable determinant is proportional to $\omega(\omega^2-\Omega^2-c_s^2k^2)$. Its additional $\omega=0$ branch has $U_x=0$ and $2\Omega U_y=ikc_s^2\hat\sigma/\Sigma_0$. This is a stationary <pressure>/Coriolis balance, the <zero-frequency balanced mode of an axisymmetric disk>, rather than a propagating density wave. At $k=0$ it reduces to a uniform surface-density change. Dividing by $\omega$ is legitimate only for the wave branches just derived.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Choose the positive-frequency branch and real radial amplitude $u$. The azimuthal amplitude of the <inertial-acoustic wave> is $U_y=-i\Omega u/(2\omega)$, so taking real parts gives
$$
u'_x=u\cos(kx-\omega t),\qquad u'_y=\frac{\Omega u}{2\omega}\sin(kx-\omega t).
$$
For $|k|c_s/\Omega\ll1$, $\omega=\Omega[1+O((kc_s/\Omega)^2)]$. Thus, to leading long-wavelength order,
$$
\boxed{u'_x\simeq u\cos(kx-\Omega t),\qquad u'_y\simeq\frac u2\sin(kx-\Omega t).}
$$
The quarter-cycle phase shift and the factor $1/2$ are the <epicyclic motion> relation in a <Keplerian shearing sheet>. This approximation drops the small frequency detuning; over times $|\omega-\Omega|t\gtrsim1$ that phase approximation is not uniform. The associated fractional <surface density of a disk> perturbation is $\sigma/\Sigma_0=(ku/\omega)\cos(kx-\omega t)$, so <linearization> also requires $|ku/\omega|\ll1$. The source's arbitrary real $u$ sets the wave phase/amplitude within that small-perturbation regime.

= Solution
{parent=i}

The <pressureless dust fluid in a shearing sheet> has no <pressure> restoration and is driven by <drag force> toward the prescribed gas velocity; gas backreaction is omitted in the given model. After linearizing about the common background shear, axisymmetry gives
$$
(\partial_t+\epsilon\Omega)v'_x-2\Omega v'_y=\epsilon\Omega u'_x,\qquad (\partial_t+\epsilon\Omega)v'_y+\frac\Omega2v'_x=\epsilon\Omega u'_y.
$$
Apply $D_\epsilon=\partial_t+\epsilon\Omega$ to the first equation and use the second to eliminate $v'_y$. The exact linear scalar equation is
$$
\bigl[\partial_t^2+2\epsilon\Omega\partial_t+(1+\epsilon^2)\Omega^2\bigr]v'_x=\epsilon\Omega\partial_tu'_x+\epsilon^2\Omega^2u'_x+2\epsilon\Omega^2u'_y.
$$
For the supplied leading-order epicycle, set $\theta=kx-\Omega t$. Since $\partial_tu'_x=\Omega u\sin\theta$ and $u'_y=u\sin\theta/2$, this becomes
$$
\boxed{\partial_t^2v'_x+2\epsilon\Omega\partial_tv'_x+(1+\epsilon^2)\Omega^2v'_x=2\epsilon\Omega^2u\sin\theta+\epsilon^2\Omega^2u\cos\theta.}
$$
The final cosine term is missing from the printed equation. Dropping it is justified at leading order in $0<\epsilon\ll1$, recovering the intended forcing, but the printed equation retains an order-$\epsilon^2$ frequency term on the left and is not an exact derivation at that order. A direct countercheck is $\mathbf v'=\mathbf u'$: the drag vanishes and the pair solves both dust equations exactly, while $v'_x=u\cos\theta$ leaves residual $\epsilon^2\Omega^2u\cos\theta$ in the printed scalar equation. This is the <forced dust epicycle with aerodynamic drag>; its damping time is $(\epsilon\Omega)^{-1}$.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

For either scalar equation, the <complementary solution> is
$$
v'_{x,h}=e^{-\epsilon\Omega t}\bigl[A(x)\cos(\Omega t)+B(x)\sin(\Omega t)\bigr],
$$
so initial velocity transients decay when $t\gg(\epsilon\Omega)^{-1}$. In the exact corrected <forced dust epicycle with aerodynamic drag>, $v'_{x,p}=u\cos\theta$ is a <particular solution>, and the original radial equation then gives $v'_{y,p}=u\sin\theta/2$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\mathbf v'\longrightarrow\mathbf u'\quad\text{at late times}.}
$$
This is <resonant dust entrainment by an epicyclic gas wave>: in the adopted long-wavelength gas approximation, the forcing is a free epicycle of the dust too, so matching the gas makes the drag zero. The convergence holds for every $\epsilon>0$ in this linear model; at $\epsilon=0$ transients never decay, so the zero-drag and late-time limits do not commute.

If instead the printed scalar equation is treated literally for finite $\epsilon$, its particular solution is
$$
\boxed{v'_{x,p}=u\left[\frac4{4+\epsilon^2}\cos\theta+\frac{2\epsilon}{4+\epsilon^2}\sin\theta\right].}
$$
It has amplitude $|u|/\sqrt{1+\epsilon^2/4}$ and phase offset $\arctan(\epsilon/2)$, not exact phase/amplitude agreement. Agreement in that truncated equation means only leading order as $\epsilon\to0$. This distinguishes the intended approximation from a false exact reading of the printed assertion.

There is a further order-of-limits condition: a real finite-wavelength gas wave has $\omega-\Omega\ne0$. Leading resonant entrainment over the damping time requires $|\omega-\Omega|\ll\epsilon\Omega$, or $(kc_s/\Omega)^2\ll\epsilon$. Otherwise solve the dust transfer equations at the actual $\omega$; detuning can reduce the dust response even though $|k|c_s/\Omega\ll1$.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Let $\Sigma_{d0}$ be the uniform dust background and put $\delta_d=\Sigma_d'/\Sigma_{d0}$. The linearized <continuity equation> is $\partial_t\delta_d=-\partial_xv'_x$ because background shear has no <divergence> or axisymmetric <advection>. Using the late entrained velocity $v'_x=u\cos\theta$ gives
$$
\partial_t\delta_d=ku\sin\theta,\qquad \boxed{\delta_d=\frac{ku}{\Omega}\cos\theta+C(x).}
$$
Thus the coherent travelling wave has **maximum fractional excess $|ku|/\Omega$**, and its crest density is $\Sigma_{d0}[1+|ku|/\Omega]$ when the stationary part is absent. This is the <density contrast of an entrained dust wave>, and requires $|ku|/\Omega\ll1$ in the present linear approximation. Its fractional oscillating density amplitude is the same as the gas's long-wavelength amplitude, so the coherent wave produces no additional dust-to-gas concentration ratio at this order.

Velocity damping alone does not erase the time-independent $C(x)$; initial density and the history of the forcing are needed to fix it. The printed data therefore determine the travelling-wave contrast, rather than a unique total late-time concentration for arbitrary initial histories. As an explicit example, switch on the prescribed gas epicycle at $t=0$ with initially uniform dust and $\mathbf v'=0$. The corrected velocity pair gives $v'_x=u(1-e^{-\epsilon\Omega t})\cos\theta$. Integrating continuity then leaves
$$
C(x)=-\frac{ku}{\Omega}\frac{\epsilon^2\cos(kx)+\epsilon\sin(kx)}{1+\epsilon^2}
$$
at late times. It is smaller by order $\epsilon$ in the stipulated weak-coupling regime, and confirms the stated leading wave-crest estimate while showing why arbitrary stationary density structure is not removed by the velocity transient.
