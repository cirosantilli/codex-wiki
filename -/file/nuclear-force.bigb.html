= Nuclear force
{wiki}

The residual <strong interaction> between <nucleons>. Its long-distance part involves <pion> exchange and depends on <spin> and <isospin>. It is distinct from directly exchanging gluons between color-singlet nucleons.

Why do <protons> and <neutrons> bind into an <atomic nucleus>, even though <protons> repel electrically? An attractive <nuclear force> can lower the total <energy> below that of separated <nucleons>, producing <nuclear binding energy>. The <deuteron>, containing one <proton> and one <neutron>, is the simplest such <bound state>.

In <quantum chromodynamics>, <nucleons> are composite <colour singlets> built from <quarks> and <gluons>. Their residual <nuclear force> has a longest-range strong contribution from <one-pion exchange>, with additional interactions at shorter distances.<Modern Theory of Nuclear Forces by Evgeny Epelbaum and others>[Modern Theory of Nuclear Forces, inspected preprint sections I and I.B]{ref}

= Nuclear matter saturation
{parent=Nuclear force}

The approximately bounded <binding energy per nucleon> and approximately constant interior density of well-bound medium and heavy nuclei. The short effective range and many-body character of the <nuclear force> prevent each nucleon from binding independently to every other nucleon with an unsuppressed long-range attraction.

= Neutron-proton radiative capture
{parent=Nuclear force}
{title2=$np\to d\gamma$}

Formation of a <deuteron> from a <neutron> and a <proton> with <photon> emission. For an initial pair at rest, two-body <energy> and <momentum> conservation gives $E_\gamma=B-B^2/[2(m_p+m_n)]$ in <natural units>, where $B=m_p+m_n-m_d$. The missing <binding energy> becomes <nuclear recoil>.

= Diproton
{parent=Nuclear force}

A two-<proton> system. Its low-energy spin-singlet strong channel does not give a vacuum <bound state>, and electrostatic repulsion further opposes binding. This is distinct from <proton> pairing correlations within an <atomic nucleus>.

= Dineutron
{parent=Nuclear force}

A two-<neutron> system. At low <orbital angular momentum> its identical-fermion exchange symmetry forces the spin-singlet, isospin-one channel; there is no bound <dineutron> in vacuum at physical parameters. This does not forbid correlated <neutron> pairs inside larger nuclei.

= Charge independence of nuclear forces
{parent=Nuclear force}

The approximate invariance of the strong <nuclear force> under <isospin> rotations. It compares channels with the same <spin>, orbital and total-isospin quantum numbers; it does not identify the spin-triplet isospin-zero <deuteron> channel with the spin-singlet isospin-one neutron-neutron channel. Electromagnetic effects and <quark> <mass> differences break the approximation.

= Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces
{parent=Nuclear force}
{c}

In 1935, <Hideki Yukawa> proposed a new massive exchange quantum to explain <nucleon> binding. His paper began with the inadequacy of an <electron>–<neutrino> exchange description of the binding strength. It then required a <potential energy> falling faster with distance than a <Coulomb potential>, introduced an exponentially decaying field, and related its inverse range to the exchange particle's <rest mass>. This was a theoretical proposal, not a detection with an apparatus of his own.<On the Interaction of Elementary Particles. I by Hideki Yukawa>[On the Interaction of Elementary Particles. I, opening motivation, equations (1)–(3) and (12); text inspected in a retypeset mirror]{ref}

The crucial idea is that a finite <rest mass> supplies a length scale even in empty space. In a scalar model, the static limit of a <Klein-Gordon equation> produces the <modified Helmholtz equation>. Its decaying <Green's function> gives the <Yukawa potential>. The derivation below isolates this range mechanism; <one-pion exchange> also carries <spin> and <isospin> structure.

The <nuclear emulsion track experiment> used developed <photographic emulsions> exposed to <cosmic rays> and inspected microscopically. Stopping <pions> gave daughter <muon> tracks with closely grouped ranges. Comparing penetrating <muons> with <pion>-induced nuclear breakup distinguished their interactions; this was not a direct static <force> measurement. The <muon> is a <lepton>, not Yukawa's strongly interacting <meson>.<The Cosmic Radiation by Cecil Frank Powell>[The Cosmic Radiation, printed pp. 144–145 and 151–152, especially Fig. 5; inspected in the Columbia-hosted Nobel volume]{ref}

= Static Yukawa Green function
{parent=Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces}
{title2=$G_\kappa(r)=e^{-\kappa r}/(4\pi r)$}

Let $\kappa>0$ be an inverse length. In three spatial dimensions, seek the <Green's function> that decays at infinity and satisfies
$$
(-\nabla^2+\kappa^2)G_\kappa(\mathbf r)=\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf r),\qquad r=|\mathbf r|,
$$
where $\nabla^2$ is the <Laplacian> and $\delta^{(3)}$ the three-dimensional <Dirac delta function>. The <point source> and the <differential operator> have <spherical symmetry>. For $r>0$, the <radial Laplacian> gives
$$
G_\kappa''+\frac2rG_\kappa'-\kappa^2G_\kappa=0.
$$
Writing $u(r)=rG_\kappa(r)$ reduces this to $u''-\kappa^2u=0$. Decay at infinity removes the growing <exponential function>, leaving $G_\kappa(r)=C e^{-\kappa r}/r$.

The <Dirac delta function> fixes the coefficient. Integrating over a ball $B_\epsilon$ and using the <divergence theorem> gives
$$
-4\pi\epsilon^2G_\kappa'(\epsilon)+\kappa^2\int_{B_\epsilon}G_\kappa\,d^3r=1.
$$
The first term tends to $4\pi C$, while the second is $O(\epsilon^2)$. Hence $C=1/(4\pi)$ and
$$
G_\kappa(r)=\frac{e^{-\kappa r}}{4\pi r}.
$$
This solves the source equation in the sense of <distributions>, including at the origin. Merely verifying the equation for $r>0$ would leave the normalization undetermined. The limit $\kappa\to0$ at fixed $r>0$ is $1/(4\pi r)$, the <Green function of the Laplacian> for the convention $-\nabla^2$.

For an attractive scalar toy interaction, absorb constants into $A>0$ and set
$$
V(r)=-A\frac{e^{-\kappa r}}r,\qquad F_r=-\frac{dV}{dr}=-A\frac{e^{-\kappa r}}{r^2}(1+\kappa r).
$$
Here $A$ has units of <energy> times length, and negative $F_r$ points inward. At equal $A$, comparison with the unscreened model $V_0=-A/r$ gives
$$
\frac{|V|}{|V_0|}=e^{-x},\qquad \frac{|F_r|}{|F_{0r}|}=(1+x)e^{-x},\qquad x=\kappa r.
$$
Thus the <potential energy> and <force> have the same exponential scale but different suppression factors. At $x=1$, these ratios are approximately $0.368$ and $0.736$; at $x=3$, they are $0.0498$ and $0.199$. Finite range means rapid decay, not an abrupt cutoff.

= Mass and range of a nuclear exchange field
{parent=Yukawa meson theory of nuclear forces}
{title2=$\lambda=\hbar/(mc)$}

A free scalar <Klein-Gordon equation> with <rest mass> $m$ is
$$
\left(\frac1{c^2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2}-\nabla^2+\frac{m^2c^2}{\hbar^2}\right)\phi=0.
$$
Here $c$ is the <speed of light> and $\hbar$ the reduced <Planck constant>. In the static limit, comparison with the <static Yukawa Green function> identifies
$$
\kappa=\frac{mc}{\hbar},\qquad \lambda=\kappa^{-1}=\frac{\hbar}{mc}=\frac{\hbar c}{mc^2}.
$$
This $\lambda$ is the reduced <Compton wavelength>, rather than $h/(mc)$; the two differ by $2\pi$. It is an exponential decay length of this exchange field, not the geometric size of a <nucleon> or an exact radius of a <bound state>. This article uses $\lambda$ for a length; the 1935 paper used that letter for its inverse.

For a deliberately rounded worked example, stipulate $\hbar c=197.3\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}$ and $mc^2=140\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Then
$$
\lambda=\frac{197.3}{140}\,\mathrm{fm}=1.4092857\ldots\,\mathrm{fm}\approx1.41\,\mathrm{fm},\qquad \kappa\approx0.70958\,\mathrm{fm}^{-1}.
$$
A <femtometre> is $10^{-15}\,\mathrm m$. The chosen <rest energy> is a convenient <pion>-scale input; neither it nor the resulting <Yukawa potential> is asserted to be an exact measured nuclear interaction. Holding $\hbar c$ fixed, chosen <rest energies> $70$, $140$ and $280\,\mathrm{MeV}$ give lengths $2.819$, $1.409$ and $0.7046\,\mathrm{fm}$. Doubling the exchange <rest mass> halves the decay length.

Range alone does not guarantee binding: the <potential energy> must overcome the <kinetic energy> cost of localizing a <wavefunction>. The existing <exponential variational bound for the Yukawa potential> makes this precise. For the same toy $V$, with <reduced mass> $M$, choosing a normalized exponential <wavefunction> proportional to $e^{-\alpha r}$ with $\alpha=\kappa/2$ yields
$$
\langle H\rangle=\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{8M}-\frac{A\kappa}{8}.
$$
For additional stipulated inputs $Mc^2=470\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $A=100\,\mathrm{MeV\,fm}$, this gives $\langle H\rangle\approx-3.66\,\mathrm{MeV}$. A negative value proves a <bound state> in this scalar model by the <quantum variational principle>; it is an upper bound on its <ground-state energy>, not a prediction for the <deuteron>. A nonnegative trial value would not prove the absence of binding.

= One-pion exchange
{parent=Nuclear force}

Although the <pion> has <spin> zero, <one-pion exchange> depends on <nucleon> <spin> and <isospin> and includes a <nuclear tensor force>. Shorter-range and multiple-<nucleon> interactions also matter. The scalar <Yukawa potential> isolates a range mechanism rather than specifying the complete <nuclear force>.<Modern Theory of Nuclear Forces by Evgeny Epelbaum and others>[Modern Theory of Nuclear Forces, inspected preprint sections I.B and II.B, especially equation (2.11) and Fig. 12]{ref}

= Nuclear tensor force
{parent=One-pion exchange}
{title2=$S_{12}=3(\boldsymbol\sigma_1\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})(\boldsymbol\sigma_2\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})-\boldsymbol\sigma_1\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma_2$}

The <linear operator> $V_T(r)S_{12}$ couples <nucleon> <spin> orientations to their separation direction $\hat{\mathbf r}$. Here $\boldsymbol\sigma_i$ are <Pauli matrices>. A scalar radial <potential energy> alone omits this directional dependence.

= Deuteron quadrupole evidence for a tensor force
{parent=Nuclear tensor force}

An isotropic pure $L=0$ spatial state has zero <electric quadrupole moment>. The nonzero <deuteron> quadrupole moment consequently requires anisotropic structure. With ground-state $J^P=1^+$, a <nuclear tensor force> can mix $\,{}^3S_1$ and $\,{}^3D_1$ waves while preserving total <angular momentum> and <parity>. A purely central spin-independent potential cannot produce this mixing. The inference of an orientation-dependent force was already explicit in 1939.<The Electric Quadrupole Moment of the Deuteron and the Field Theory of Nuclear Forces by Christian Møller and Léon Rosenfeld>[Abstract and opening discussion, Nature 144, 476–477]{ref}
