= Compact operator
{wiki}

A bounded operator is compact when it maps the unit ball to a relatively compact set.

= Compact factorial triangular operator on l1
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$(T\alpha)_n=\sum_{m>n}(n!/m!)\alpha_m$}

The operator in the title is bounded and compact on the <absolutely summable sequence space>. The <norm> of a <matrix> operator on $\ell^1$ is the supremum of its absolute column sums: the <triangle inequality> gives one bound and coordinate unit vectors give the other. Here the column sum is $c_m=\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}n!/m!\leq2/m$, using $\sum_{n=1}^kn!\leq2k!$. The latter inequality follows by induction. Retaining the first $N$ columns gives a <finite-rank operator> and a remainder of <norm> at most $2/(N+1)$. A <norm> limit of finite-rank operators is a <compact operator>, proving the assertion. This column-decay method can prove compactness even when each row has infinitely many nonzero entries.

= Quotient-distance criterion for compactness
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$T\text{ compact}\iff\inf_{\dim Y<\infty}\|\pi_YT\|=0$}

For a <bounded linear operator> into a <Banach space>, compactness is equivalent to the displayed condition, where $\pi_Y$ is the <quotient map> by a finite-dimensional subspace of the target. A finite net for the image of the unit ball gives such a subspace by taking its span. Conversely, if the image lies within $\varepsilon$ of a finite-dimensional subspace, choose approximants there; they remain bounded because the original image is bounded. A finite net for these approximants gives a slightly larger finite net for the image. This proves <total boundedness> and hence compactness. This condition does not require choosing the approximants by a <linear map>, and therefore does not assume an approximation property for the <Banach space>.

= Compact closed-ball image forces finite rank on c0
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$S(B_{c_0})\text{ compact}\Longrightarrow\operatorname{rank}S<\infty$}

Suppose a linear map $S:c_0\to Y$ has compact <image> of the closed unit ball. It is bounded, and each $g\in Y^*$ attains its maximum on this compact <image>. Thus $S^*g$ attains its <norm>, so <norm attainment on the vanishing sequence space> implies $S^*(Y^*)\subseteq c_{00}$. The <Baire category theorem> applied to the inverse images of the first $n$ coordinate spans makes this adjoint finite rank: these are closed <linear subspaces> covering the complete <continuous dual space> $Y^*$, so one has interior and is all of $Y^*$. Finally the <Hahn-Banach theorem> identifies $\ker S$ as the common kernel of $S^*(Y^*)$, proving finite rank of $S$. Ordinary <compact operators> require only relative compactness of the ball <image>, so they need not have finite rank.

= Block averaging compactness counterexample
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$(Tx)_k=m_k^{-1/q}\sum_{j\in B_k}x_j$}

For $1<p<\infty$, let $q=p/(p-1)$ and partition the coordinates into consecutive finite blocks $B_k$ of lengths $m_k\to\infty$. The displayed map defines a norm-one operator on the <l-p sequence space>. By <Hölder's inequality>, $|(Tx)_k|^p\leq\sum_{j\in B_k}|x_j|^p$, giving the upper <norm> bound. For the unit <vector> $e_j$ with $j\in B_k$, $Te_j=m_k^{-1/q}e_k$, so its <norm> tends to zero as $j\to\infty$.

Nevertheless, $x^{(k)}=m_k^{-1/p}\mathbf1_{B_k}$ has <norm> one and maps to $e_k$. Those images are pairwise distance $2^{1/p}$ apart. Thus $T$ is not a <compact operator>, and has <norm> exactly one. Vanishing images of a chosen basis do not alone test <compactness> on $\ell^p$ for $p>1$. The map is the adjoint of the isometric block embedding $(Uy)_j=m_k^{-1/q}y_k$, $j\in B_k$, on $\ell^q$.

= Nonlinear compact operator
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$T:X\to Y$}

A <nonlinear compact operator> between <Banach spaces> is a <continuous map> that sends every <bounded set> to a <relatively compact subset>. Thus every bounded <sequence> of inputs has a <subsequence> whose images converge in the target <norm>. No <linearity> is assumed. On a bounded domain of definition it suffices that the entire image be <relatively compact>. For <linear operators>, this recovers the usual <compact operator> notion.

= Compact L2 potential perturbation of the Dirichlet Laplacian
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$K=M_w\Delta_D^{-1},\quad w\in L^p,\ p>n\geq3$}

On a bounded open set, let $Tv\in W_0^{1,2}$ be the <weak solution> of $\Delta Tv=v$. The energy estimate bounds $T:L^2\to W_0^{1,2}$. For $p>n$, put $q=2p/(p-2)<2n/(n-2)$. The zero-boundary <Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem> gives compact embedding into $L^q$, and <Hölder's inequality> gives $\|wz\|_2\leq\|w\|_p\|z\|_q$. Hence $Kv=wTv$ is compact on $L^2$, without a regularity assumption on the boundary. Writing $v=\Delta u$ makes $\Delta u-wu=f$ equivalent to $(I-K)v=f$. The <Fredholm alternative for a compact operator> then identifies unique solvability with triviality of the homogeneous kernel.

= Nondegenerate representations by compact operators
{parent=Compact operator}

A nondegenerate star-algebra of <compact operators> decomposes its <Hilbert space> into irreducible closed reducing subspaces, each equivalence class occurring finitely many times. Pass to its norm closure. A nonzero compact positive element supplies a finite-rank spectral projection; a minimal projection in its finite-dimensional corner has scalar corner algebra. A cyclic vector in its range generates an irreducible reducing subspace. A maximal orthogonal family exhausts the space by nondegeneracy. Infinitely many equivalent copies would give orthogonal unit vectors whose images under some compact algebra element have equal nonzero norms, contradicting compactness.

= Compact operators send weak convergence to norm convergence
{parent=Compact operator}
{title2=$f_n\rightharpoonup f\Longrightarrow Kf_n\to Kf$}

For a bounded operator $K$ between <Hilbert spaces>, compactness is equivalent to $f_n\rightharpoonup f$ implying $Kf_n\to Kf$ in norm. The <uniform boundedness principle> bounds a weakly convergent sequence. Relative compactness of its images and the unique possible weak limit then give norm convergence. Conversely, every bounded domain sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence, so the image of the <unit ball> is relatively compact.

= Norm-closedness of compact operators
{parent=Compact operator}

An <operator-norm> limit of compact operators between <Banach spaces> is compact. Approximate the image of the unit ball by the image under one compact operator and transfer a finite epsilon-net across the small operator-norm error.

= Schauder theorem for compact operators
{c}
{parent=Compact operator}

A bounded operator $T:X\to Y$ between <Banach spaces> is compact if and only if its <dual map of bounded linear operator> $T^*:Y^*\to X^*$ is compact.

= Orthogonal eigenvectors of a compact operator
{parent=Compact operator}

If $Tx_j=\lambda_jx_j$ for an infinite orthonormal sequence $(x_j)$ and a compact operator $T$ on a <Hilbert space>, then $\lambda_j\to0$. Otherwise a subsequence of the vectors $Tx_j$ stays pairwise separated, contradicting compactness.

= Finite-rank approximation theorem for compact operators on a Hilbert space
{parent=Compact operator}

Every compact operator on a Hilbert space is an operator-norm limit of finite-rank operators. Cover the compact closure of the image of the unit ball by finitely many small balls, span their centres by a finite-dimensional space $E$, and approximate $T$ by $P_ET$.

= Fredholm alternative
{parent=Compact operator}
{wiki}
{c}

= Fredholm alternative for a compact operator
{synonym}

If $T$ is compact and $\lambda\ne0$, then $T-\lambda I$ is injective exactly when it is surjective. Equivalently, every nonzero point of the spectrum of $T$ is an eigenvalue. Compactness turns an approximate eigenvector sequence into a convergent eigenvector sequence.

= Neumann compatibility from a shifted compact resolvent
{c}
{parent=Fredholm alternative}
{title2=$(I-K)u=Kf,\quad K=(L+I)^{-1}$}

For a symmetric uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator with zero conormal flux and zero reaction, <Lax-Milgram theorem> constructs the shifted inverse $K:L^2\to H^1$. The compact <Rellich-Kondrachov theorem> embedding makes it compact on $L^2$, and symmetry makes it self-adjoint. If the homogeneous kernel consists of constants, then $\ker(I-K)$ is the constant line and $K1=1$. The <Fredholm alternative> makes solvability equivalent to $Kf$ being orthogonal to constants, or $\langle Kf,1\rangle=\langle f,K1\rangle=\int f=0$. The <weak solution> is unique up to an additive constant. For a disconnected domain the identical argument gives one compatibility condition per connected component.

= Hilbert-Schmidt
{c}
{synonym}

A bounded <linear operator> on a <Hilbert space> is Hilbert-Schmidt when $\sum_n\|Te_n\|^2<\infty$ for one, equivalently every, <orthonormal basis>. Every Hilbert-Schmidt operator is a <compact operator>.

= Hilbert-Schmidt operators form a Hilbert space
{c}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}
{title2=$\mathcal S_2(X)$}

= Completeness of the Hilbert-Schmidt norm
{c}
{synonym}

On a <Hilbert space> $X$ with a countable <Hilbertian basis> $(e_k)$, the <Hilbert-Schmidt operators> form a <Hilbert space> under $\langle S,T\rangle=\sum_k\langle Se_k,Te_k\rangle$, using the same linearity convention as the <inner product> on $X$. The <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> proves convergence of this sum. The finite-sum <triangle inequality> and <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> give $\|T\|\leq\|T\|_2$. Thus a <Cauchy sequence> $(T_n)$ in <Hilbert-Schmidt norm> converges in <operator norm> to a <bounded linear operator> $T$. For $n,m\geq N$ with $\|T_n-T_m\|_2\leq\varepsilon$, taking $m\to\infty$ in each finite column sum gives $\sum_{k=1}^K\|(T_n-T)e_k\|^2\leq\varepsilon^2$ for every $K$. Taking the supremum over $K$ proves $T_n-T\in\mathcal S_2(X)$ and $\|T_n-T\|_2\leq\varepsilon$, so $T\in\mathcal S_2(X)$ and completeness follows.

= Finite column sum does not imply boundedness
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}

An everywhere-defined <linear operator> $T$ on an infinite-dimensional <separable Hilbert space> need not be bounded merely because $\sum_k\|Te_k\|^2<\infty$ for a <Hilbertian basis> $(e_k)$. Let $v=\sum_{k\geq1}2^{-k}e_k$, extend $\{e_k:k\geq1\}\cup\{v\}$ to a <Hamel basis>, and let the <linear functional> $\phi$ equal one on $v$ and zero on every other basis vector. Then $Tx=\phi(x)e_1$ has $Te_k=0$ for every $k$ but $Tv=e_1$. For $v_N=\sum_{k=1}^N2^{-k}e_k$, $v-v_N\to0$ while $T(v-v_N)=e_1$, proving discontinuity. Thus the definition of a <Hilbert-Schmidt operator> must require boundedness, or first define the operator on finite <linear combinations> and take its unique continuous extension.

= Compact averaging of a rank-one operator
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}
{title2=$K_\xi=\int_G U_gR_{\xi,\xi}U_g^*\,dg$}

For a strongly continuous <unitary representation> of a <compact group>, this <Bochner integral> exists in the <Hilbert-Schmidt norm> and is a positive <compact operator> commuting with the representation. It is nonzero if $\xi\ne0$, because $\langle K_\xi\xi,\xi\rangle=\int_G|\langle U_g\xi,\xi\rangle|^2\,dg>0$. A nonzero <eigenspace> is finite-dimensional and invariant. Applying this on <orthogonal complements> yields an <orthogonal direct sum> of finite-dimensional <irreducible representations>, with no separability assumption on the represented <Hilbert space>.

= Hilbert-Schmidt kernel bound
{c}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}
{title2=$\|T\|\leq\|k\|_{L^2}$}

For $(Tg)(v)=\int k(v,w)g(w)\,dw$, the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> gives $\|Tg\|_2\leq\|k\|_{L^2(v,w)}\|g\|_2$. The kernel norm is the <Hilbert-Schmidt norm>, which bounds the <operator norm>. Uniform kernel bounds apply also to spatially parametrized operators after integrating over position.

= Hilbert-Schmidt norm
{c}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}
{title2=$\|T\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=(\sum_n\|Te_n\|^2)^{1/2}$}

The Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a <Hilbert-Schmidt operator> is the square root of the displayed sum. The value is independent of the <orthonormal basis>, by the <Parseval identity>. For a finite <matrix>, it is the <Frobenius norm>.

= Frobenius norm
{c}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt norm}
{title2=$\|A\|_F=(\sum_{i,j}|a_{ij}|^2)^{1/2}$}

The Frobenius norm is the finite-matrix <Hilbert-Schmidt norm>. It equals $\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}(A^*A)}$, is unchanged by unitary multiplication on either side, and is the square root of the sum of squared singular values. For unit vectors $u,v$, $\|uu^*-vv^*\|_F^2=2(1-|u^*v|^2)$, a useful sign-invariant measure of distance between rank-one <orthogonal projection matrices>.

= Hilbert-Schmidt inner product
{title2=$\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}$}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}

For <Hilbert-Schmidt operators> $A$ and $B$, define the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product by $\langle A,B\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sum_k\langle Ae_k,Be_k\rangle$ for a <Hilbertian basis> $(e_k)$. With an <inner product> linear in its first argument this equals $\operatorname{tr}(B^*A)$; with an <inner product> conjugate-linear in its first argument it equals $\operatorname{tr}(A^*B)$. Its induced <norm> is the <Hilbert-Schmidt norm>, independently of that convention.

= Rank bound for a weighted operator trace
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt inner product}
{title2=$|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho T)|^2\leq(\sum_{j<k}\lambda_j)\operatorname{Tr}(\rho T^\dagger T)$}

If $\rho$ is a <density operator> with decreasing <eigenvalues> $\lambda_j$ and $T$ has <matrix rank> at most $k$, let $\Pi$ project onto the image of $T$. The <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> for the <Hilbert-Schmidt inner product> gives
$$
|\operatorname{Tr}(\rho T)|^2\leq\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Pi)\operatorname{Tr}(\rho T^\dagger T).
$$
The <Hermitian effect variational principle> bounds $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Pi)$ by $\sum_{j<k}\lambda_j$, proving the claimed estimate without assuming $\rho$ is invertible.

= Hilbert-Schmidt distance
{title2=$d_{\mathrm{HS}}(A,B)$}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt inner product}

The Hilbert-Schmidt distance is $d_{\mathrm{HS}}(A,B)=\lVert A-B\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}$.

= Trace-class operator
{title2=$\mathcal S_1$}
{parent=Hilbert-Schmidt operator}
{wiki}

= Trace-class
{synonym}

A bounded operator $T$ on a separable Hilbert space is trace class when $|T|^{1/2}$ is Hilbert--Schmidt. Its trace norm is
$$
\|T\|_1=\bigl\||T|^{1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\sum_n\langle|T|e_n,e_n\rangle.
$$

= Trace-class duality
{parent=Trace-class operator}
{title2=$\mathcal S_1(H)^*\cong B(H)$}

The dual of the <trace-class operators>, and also of the normed space of <finite-rank operators> with the <trace norm>, is isometrically the space of <bounded operators>. The pairing is $F_B(T)=\operatorname{Tr}(BT)$. For $R_{x,y}z=\langle z,y\rangle x$, one has $\|R_{x,y}\|_1=\|x\|\|y\|$ and $F_B(R_{x,y})=\langle Bx,y\rangle$, using an <inner product> linear in its first argument. A bounded functional on these <rank-one operators> gives a bounded <sesquilinear form> and hence a unique $B$ by the <Riesz representation theorem>. The <singular value decomposition> proves $\|F_B\|=\|B\|$ and the <triangle inequality> for the <trace norm>.

= Fredholm determinant
{c}
{parent=Trace-class operator}
{title2=$\det(I+K)=\prod_j(1+\lambda_j)$}

For a trace-class operator $K$ on a <Hilbert space>, the Fredholm determinant is the convergent product over its eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity. For self-adjoint $K$, <spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators> reduces the product to a diagonal calculation. For $\|K\|<1$, $\log\det(I+K)=\sum_{n\geq1}(-1)^{n+1}\operatorname{tr}(K^n)/n$. This identity exposes the successive <cumulants> in a <Gaussian quadratic exponential moment>.

= Fredholm determinant of an exponential commutator
{parent=Fredholm determinant}
{title2=$\det(e^Ae^Be^{-A}e^{-B})=e^{\operatorname{Tr}[A,B]}$}

For bounded operators with trace-class commutator, the multiplicative commutator differs from identity by a <trace-class operator>. Along $F(t)=e^{tA}e^Be^{-tA}e^{-B}$, the trace of its logarithmic derivative is the constant $\operatorname{Tr}[A,B]$. This follows by writing $e^BAe^{-B}-A$ as the integral of conjugates of $[B,A]$ and using trace invariance under bounded similarities. Integrating the <Fredholm determinant> derivative from $F(0)=I$ proves the formula. Individual traces of $A$ and $B$ need not exist.

= Hilbert-Schmidt factorization of a trace-class operator
{parent=Trace-class operator}

An operator is trace class exactly when it factors as $T=AB$ with $A$ and $B$ Hilbert--Schmidt. The factors can be chosen so that $\|T\|_1=\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$ by using the <polar decomposition of a bounded operator>.

= Schatten norm Hölder inequality
{c}
{parent=Trace-class operator}

The Schatten norm Hölder inequality gives $\lVert AB\rVert_r\leq\lVert A\rVert_p\lVert B\rVert_q$ when $1/r=1/p+1/q$. In particular, the product of two <Hilbert-Schmidt operators> is trace class and
$$
\lVert AB\rVert_1\leq\lVert A\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}\lVert B\rVert_{\mathrm{HS}}.
$$

= Operator trace
{title2=$\operatorname{tr}T$}
{parent=Trace-class operator}

For a trace-class operator, the series $\operatorname{tr}T=\sum_n\langle Te_n,e_n\rangle$ converges absolutely and is independent of the orthonormal basis. It satisfies $|\operatorname{tr}T|\leq\|T\|_1$.

The <trace-class operator> condition makes this <operator trace> well-defined; the class of eligible operators and the trace functional are distinct concepts.

= Trace duality
{title2=$\mathcal S_1^*=\mathcal B(H)$}
{parent=Operator trace}

Every bounded operator $S$ defines a functional $T\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(ST)$ on the trace-class operators, and
$$
\|S\|=\sup_{\|T\|_1\leq1}|\operatorname{tr}(ST)|.
$$
Finite-rank density in $\mathcal S_1$ makes every continuous functional arise uniquely this way.

= Rank-one operator
{title2=$x\otimes y$}
{parent=Trace-class operator}
{wiki}

For vectors $x,y$ in a Hilbert space, the rank-one operator $(x\otimes y)z=\langle z,y\rangle x$ has $\|x\otimes y\|_1=\|x\|\|y\|$ and $\operatorname{tr}(x\otimes y)=\langle x,y\rangle$.

= Norms of a rank-one operator
{parent=Rank-one operator}
{title2=$\|u\otimes v\|=\|u\otimes v\|_2=\|u\|\|v\|$}

For the <rank-one operator> $Tf=\langle f,v\rangle u$ on a <Hilbert space> with a countable <Hilbertian basis> $(e_k)$, the <operator norm> and <Hilbert-Schmidt norm> both equal $\|u\|\|v\|$. The <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> gives $\|Tf\|\leq\|u\|\|v\|\|f\|$, with equality at $f=v/\|v\|$ when $v\ne0$. The <Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis> gives $\sum_k\|Te_k\|^2=\|u\|^2\sum_k|\langle e_k,v\rangle|^2=\|u\|^2\|v\|^2$. The zero-vector cases are immediate.

= Powers of a rank-one operator
{parent=Rank-one operator}
{title2=$K^m=\varphi(a)^{m-1}K\quad(m\geq1)$}

Let $Kx=a\varphi(x)$ on a <vector space>, where $\varphi$ is a <linear functional>. Its <image of a linear map> lies in the span of $a$. Applying $K$ twice gives $K^2x=a\varphi(a)\varphi(x)=\varphi(a)Kx$. <Mathematical induction> then proves the displayed power formula. If $\varphi(a)=0$, $K^2=0$, even when $K\ne0$; this is a <nilpotent operator>. If $\varphi(a)\ne0$, then $K/\varphi(a)$ is an <idempotent linear map>. In a real <inner product space>, the special case $\varphi(x)=b\cdot x$ gives $\varphi(a)=a\cdot b$.

= Exponential of a rank-one operator
{parent=Powers of a rank-one operator}
{title2=$e^{tK}=I+\frac{e^{t\varphi(a)}-1}{\varphi(a)}K$}

For the bounded <rank-one operator> $Kx=a\varphi(x)$, insert the <powers of a rank-one operator> into the convergent <operator exponential> <power series>. Writing $c=\varphi(a)$ gives the displayed formula for $c\ne0$. At $c=0$ the quotient has <limit> $t$, so $e^{tK}=I+tK$. This includes the zero operator and the nonzero <nilpotent operator> cases without dividing by zero.

= Finite-rank operator
{parent=Compact operator}
{wiki}

A finite-rank operator has finite-dimensional image and is compact.

= Finite-rank Fredholm alternative
{parent=Finite-rank operator}

For a finite-rank operator $S$ and $\lambda\ne0$, if $\lambda$ is not an eigenvalue then $S-\lambda I$ is surjective. Decompose the Hilbert space into $\operatorname{im}S$ and its orthogonal complement, then use injectivity and surjectivity equivalence on the finite-dimensional image.

= Spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators
{parent=Compact operator}

= Spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators
{synonym}

A compact Hermitian operator has real nonzero eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, with zero as their only possible accumulation point, and an orthonormal eigenbasis after a basis of its kernel is included.

This is the discrete-eigenbasis case of the <spectral theorem> for a <compact operator> that is self-adjoint.

= Courant–Fischer min-max principle
{c}
{parent=Spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators}
{wiki}

For a compact positive self-adjoint operator $T$ with decreasing eigenvalues $\lambda_1\geq\lambda_2\geq\cdots$, the Courant–Fischer principle gives
$$
\lambda_k
=\min_{\dim V=k-1}\max_{\substack{x\perp V\\\lVert x\rVert=1}}
\langle Tx,x\rangle.
$$
In particular, $S\geq T$ in quadratic-form order implies $\lambda_k(S)\geq\lambda_k(T)$ for every $k$.

= Finite-rank truncation of a compact Hermitian operator
{parent=Spectral theorem for compact Hermitian operators}

If
$$
Tx=\sum_n\lambda_n\langle x,e_n\rangle e_n,
\qquad \lambda_n\to0,
$$
then truncating the sum after $N$ terms gives finite-rank Hermitian $T_N$ with
$$
\lVert T-T_N\rVert=\sup_{n>N}|\lambda_n|\to0.
$$

= Coordinate-projection approximation of a compact operator
{parent=Compact operator}

Let $P_N$ project $\ell^2$ onto its first $N$ coordinates. Although $P_N\to I$ only strongly on the whole unit ball, convergence is uniform on every compact subset. Hence compact $T$ satisfies
$$
\lVert P_NT-T\rVert\to0,
$$
and each $P_NT$ has finite rank.

= Riesz–Schauder theorem
{c}
{parent=Compact operator}
{wiki}

Every nonzero spectral point of a compact operator is an isolated eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity, and zero is the only possible accumulation point of the spectrum.

= Finite-section approximation of a compact operator
{parent=Compact operator}

If finite-rank orthogonal projections $P_n$ converge strongly to the identity and $A$ is compact, then $\lVert A-P_nAP_n\rVert\to0$. The finite-section spectra converge to $\operatorname{Sp}(A)$ in Hausdorff distance, including for nonnormal $A$.

= Compact resolvent
{parent=Compact operator}
{wiki}

A closed densely defined operator has compact resolvent when $(A-zI)^{-1}$ is compact at one, equivalently every, resolvent point. Its spectrum consists only of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, with possible accumulation only at infinity.

= Compact elliptic spectral theorem
{parent=Compact resolvent}

On a <closed manifold> with a <Riemannian metric> the <positive Laplace-Beltrami operator> is <self-adjoint> with <compact resolvent>. It has a complete smooth <orthonormal eigenbasis>, finite-dimensional <eigenspaces>, nonnegative <eigenvalues> tending to infinity, and polynomial eigenvalue-counting bounds. <Elliptic regularity> bounds derivatives of its <eigenfunctions> polynomially in their <eigenvalues>.

= Eigenvalue counting function
{parent=Compact elliptic spectral theorem}
{title2=$N(\Lambda)=\#\{j:\lambda_j\leq\Lambda\}$}

For a nonnegative <self-adjoint operator> with <compact resolvent>, this function counts <eigenvalues> up to a given level with their multiplicities. For the <positive Laplace-Beltrami operator> on a closed $n$-dimensional <Riemannian manifold>, $N(\Lambda)\leq C(1+\Lambda)^{n/2}$. To prove this bound, multiply each vector in the low-eigenvalue subspace by a finite set of chart cutoffs and record its local <Fourier coefficients> of frequency at most $R$. The total number of retained coefficients is $O(R^n)$, while the sum of discarded squared norms is at most $CR^{-2}(1+\Lambda)\|u\|_2^2$ by <Parseval identity> and the energy bound. For $R$ a sufficiently large multiple of $\sqrt{1+\Lambda}$, vanishing of all recorded coefficients forces $u=0$. The recording map is injective and proves the dimension bound. Inverting this estimate gives $\lambda_j\geq c(j+1)^{2/n}-1$.

= Imaginary Airy operator
{c}
{parent=Compact resolvent}

The imaginary Airy operator $-d^2/dx^2+ix$ on $L^2(\mathbb R)$ has compact resolvent but empty spectrum. Real translations shift it by a purely imaginary scalar, forcing vertical translation invariance of its spectrum.
