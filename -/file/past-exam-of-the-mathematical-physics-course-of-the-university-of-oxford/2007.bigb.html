= Solution

For identical <bosons> the full <wave function> is symmetric under the <particle exchange operator>:
$$
\boxed{\psi(x_2,x_1)=\psi(x_1,x_2).}
$$
In the given position-only model this is the spatial parity under the <particle exchange operator>. If additional <spin> variables are present, the symmetry applies to the complete state including them.

For identical <fermions> the full <wave function> is antisymmetric:
$$
\boxed{\psi(x_2,x_1)=-\psi(x_1,x_2).}
$$
In particular it vanishes when the two complete one-particle arguments coincide. Applying this to the position-only model assumes that no independent <spin> symmetry changes the required spatial <parity>.

= Solution
{parent=1}

The <orthogonal> coordinate change preserves the <kinetic energy> and takes the <particle exchange operator> to $(x_+,x_-)\mapsto(x_+,-x_-)$. Thus the spatial <wave function> is even in $x_-$ for <bosons> and odd for <fermions>.

The total <potential energy> and <Hamiltonian> separate as
$$
V=\frac{m\omega^2}{2}x_+^2+\frac{m\omega^2(1+2\kappa)}2x_-^2,\qquad
H=H_+(\omega)+H_-(\Omega),\quad\Omega=\omega\sqrt{1+2\kappa}.
$$
Assume $1+2\kappa>0$ so the relative <oscillator> is confining. Its spectrum is $E_{n_+,n_-}=\hbar\omega(n_++1/2)+\hbar\Omega(n_-+1/2)$, and its <parity> in the <two-particle relative coordinate> is $(-1)^{n_-}$.

The lowest <boson> state uses $n_+=n_-=0$, whereas the lowest <fermion> state uses $n_+=0$, $n_-=1$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{E_B=\tfrac12\hbar\omega[1+\sqrt{1+2\kappa}],\qquad
E_F=\tfrac12\hbar\omega[1+3\sqrt{1+2\kappa}].}
$$
For an <oscillator> of frequency $\Omega$, $\langle x_-^2\rangle_n=(n+1/2)\hbar/(m\Omega)$. Since $(x_1-x_2)^2=2x_-^2$,
$$
\boxed{\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_B=\frac\hbar{m\Omega},\qquad
\langle(x_1-x_2)^2\rangle_F=\frac{3\hbar}{m\Omega}.}
$$
The factor three is the extra relative-coordinate excitation required by antisymmetry. The supplied <oscillator> <wave functions> correspond to <kinetic energy> $P^2/(2m)$; the extra factor $1/2$ printed before that term in the hint is inconsistent with those functions and the stated energies. The calculation uses the conventional <kinetic energy> dictated by particle <mass> $m$.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Use the normalized <Rayleigh quotient> $R(\psi)=\langle\psi,H\psi\rangle/\langle\psi,\psi\rangle$. Its real directional <derivative> in an arbitrary complex direction $\eta$ is
$$
\delta R=\frac{2\operatorname{Re}\langle\eta,(H-R)\psi\rangle}{\langle\psi,\psi\rangle}.
$$
It vanishes for every direction precisely when $(H-R)\psi=0$, since testing $\eta=(H-R)\psi$ gives its squared norm. Conversely an <eigenvector> makes every <derivative> zero. Stationarity over all states is stronger than stationarity in a restricted trial family.

Normalize the <Gaussian> as $\psi_\alpha=(\alpha/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\alpha x^2/2}$, with $\alpha>0$. Its moments are $\langle X^2\rangle=1/(2\alpha)$, $\langle X^4\rangle=3/(4\alpha^2)$ and $\langle P^2\rangle=\hbar^2\alpha/2$. The printed <potential energy> consequently gives
$$
\boxed{\mathcal E(\alpha)=\frac{\hbar^2\alpha}{4m}+\frac{m\omega^2}{4\alpha}+
\frac{15m^2\omega^3}{256\hbar\alpha^2}.}
$$
Put $a=\alpha\hbar/(m\omega)$. Then $\mathcal E/(\hbar\omega)=a/4+1/(4a)+15/(256a^2)$, whose second <derivative> is $1/(2a^3)+45/(128a^4)>0$. It diverges at both positive endpoints, so its sole <stationary point> is the unique <global minimum>. Its equation is
$$
\boxed{a^3-a-\frac{15}{32}=0,\qquad a_*\simeq1.18179610.}
$$
The <variational principle> therefore yields the optimized upper bound
$$
\boxed{E_0\leq\mathcal E(\alpha_*)\simeq0.548944745\,\hbar\omega,\qquad
\alpha_*\simeq1.18179610\,\frac{m\omega}\hbar.}
$$
**The printed minimizer is inconsistent with the printed quartic coefficient.** At $a=3/2$ the <derivative> is $5/48>0$, so this is not the stationary <Gaussian> for that <potential energy>. It is nevertheless a legitimate trial vector and gives the simple, weaker bound $\boxed{E_0\leq109\hbar\omega/192}$. If the quartic coefficient were instead $5m^2\omega^3/(16\hbar)$, four times the printed value, then $a=3/2$ would indeed minimize the family and its <energy> would be $31\hbar\omega/48$. This possible coefficient correction is identified explicitly rather than silently used. The reusable calculation is <Gaussian trial for a quadratic-plus-quartic oscillator>.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Let $\Psi\ne0$ and take the reduced <positive operator> $\rho_1=\operatorname{Tr}_2|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|$. Choose orthonormal <eigenvectors> $\xi_j$ for its positive <eigenvalues> $\lambda_j$. Define
$$
v_j=(\langle\xi_j|\otimes I)\Psi,\qquad c_j=\sqrt{\lambda_j},\qquad\eta_j=v_j/c_j.
$$
The defining identity of the <partial trace> gives $\langle v_i,v_j\rangle=\lambda_j\delta_{ij}$, so the $\eta_j$ are orthonormal. Expansion in a full eigenbasis of $\rho_1$ yields $\Psi=\sum_j\xi_j\otimes v_j$; zero-<eigenvalue> terms have zero squared norm and contribute nothing. Thus
$$
\boxed{\Psi=\sum_jc_j|\xi_j\rangle|\eta_j\rangle,\qquad c_j>0.}
$$
The zero vector has the empty decomposition. Unit <normalization> would give $\sum_jc_j^2=1$, but no <normalization> is needed for the theorem itself.

For the given vector, the <coefficient matrix> is $M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Its second reduced operator is $M^*M=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, whose <eigenvalues> are $3,1$ and <eigenvectors> $\eta_+=(E_1+E_2)/\sqrt2$, $\eta_-=(E_1-E_2)/\sqrt2$. The corresponding normalized first-factor vectors are
$$
\xi_+=\frac{e_1+e_2+2e_3}{\sqrt6},\qquad\xi_-=\frac{e_1-e_2}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Their norms are one and their <inner product> is zero. Hence
$$
\boxed{\psi=\sqrt3\,|\xi_+\rangle|\eta_+\rangle+|\xi_-\rangle|\eta_-\rangle.}
$$
Expanding the two terms returns the original three contributions. The vector has squared norm $4$; if a normalized state is wanted, divide by two and the <Schmidt coefficients> become $\sqrt3/2$ and $1/2$.

= Solution
{parent=4}

The <no-cloning theorem> states that no fixed deterministic <quantum operation> can perfectly duplicate every unknown <pure state>. For a <unitary> copier with a fixed blank, suppose it cloned two normalized vectors $|a\rangle,|b\rangle$. Preservation of <inner products> would give $\langle a|b\rangle=\langle a|b\rangle^2$, impossible for a nonorthogonal distinct pair with overlap strictly between zero and one in modulus. Including an environment does not help: its final overlap has modulus at most one, so one would require $|\langle a|b\rangle|\leq|\langle a|b\rangle|^2$. Thus a universal perfect copier is impossible. <Orthogonal> known alternatives do not contradict this restriction.

Label the three <qubits> as input $S$, Alice's entangled <qubit> $A$ and Bob's <qubit> $B$. Initially their joint state is
$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}[\alpha_0(|000\rangle+|011\rangle)+\alpha_1(|100\rangle+|111\rangle)].
$$
The <controlled-NOT gate> from $S$ to $A$ changes this to
$$
\frac1{\sqrt2}[\alpha_0(|000\rangle+|011\rangle)+\alpha_1(|110\rangle+|101\rangle)].
$$
Apply $H|0\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $H|1\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ to the first <qubit> and group by Alice's two computational-basis outcomes. The result is
$$
\boxed{\frac12\bigl[|00\rangle|\psi\rangle+|01\rangle X|\psi\rangle+
|10\rangle Z|\psi\rangle+|11\rangle XZ|\psi\rangle\bigr].}
$$
In expanded form the last two Bob factors are $\alpha_0|0\rangle-\alpha_1|1\rangle$ and $\alpha_0|1\rangle-\alpha_1|0\rangle$. **Their Alice labels must be $10$ and $11$; the repeated $00$ labels in the source are printing errors.**

Alice measures her two <qubits> and sends the two classical bits $(a,b)$ to Bob. Each outcome has probability $1/4$ and Bob's conditional state is $X^bZ^a|\psi\rangle$. He applies $\boxed{Z^aX^b}$ to recover the input, up to physically irrelevant <global phase>. Explicitly the corrections for $00,01,10,11$ are $I,X,Z,ZX$. This is <quantum teleportation>: Alice's input is consumed by <measurement>, so no second surviving copy is created. Before her classical message, Bob's <reduced density operator> is $I/2$, independent of the input, so the protocol also supplies no faster-than-light communication.

= Solution
{parent=5}

Use <metric signature> $(+---)$ and the <curvature> sign fixed by the stated <Newtonian limit>. In flat spacetime the source-free <Maxwell equations> are $\partial_aF^{ab}=0$ and $\partial_{[a}F_{bc]}=0$. The <stress-energy tensor> in mixed form is $T^a{}_b=F^{ac}F_{cb}+\delta^a_bF_{cd}F^{cd}/4$. Its divergence is
$$
\partial_aT^a{}_b=(\partial_aF^{ac})F_{cb}+F^{ac}\partial_aF_{cb}+\frac14\partial_b(F_{cd}F^{cd}).
$$
Contracting the cyclic Maxwell equation with $F^{ac}$ gives $F^{ac}\partial_aF_{cb}=-\partial_b(F_{cd}F^{cd})/4$. The first term also vanishes, proving $\boxed{\partial_aT^{ab}=0}$. The component $b=0$ expresses local electromagnetic <energy conservation>, and the spatial components express <momentum conservation>. Integrating converts them into the usual energy and momentum flux balances.

In curved spacetime the <tensor> equations are
$$
\boxed{\nabla_aF^{ab}=0,\qquad\nabla_{[a}F_{bc]}=0.}
$$
They agree with the flat equations in coordinates of a <local inertial frame> at a point, where the connection vanishes, and covariance then extends them to arbitrary coordinates. <Antisymmetry> makes the homogeneous equation equivalent to $dF=0$. <Metric compatibility> permits the same contraction proof, now giving $\boxed{\nabla_aT^{ab}=0}$. In general,
$$
\partial_aT^{ab}=-\Gamma^a{}_{ac}T^{cb}-\Gamma^b{}_{ac}T^{ac},
$$
which need not vanish. Covariant local balance remains valid; ordinary coordinate components do not provide a universal conserved global energy-momentum vector in a curved geometry. Additional spacetime symmetries can furnish conserved currents. A locally inertial frame removes the connection at a point, not the <curvature> or the global obstruction.

The alternating array with fixed component $\epsilon_{0123}=1$ is appropriate in oriented inertial special-relativistic coordinates, whose proper <Lorentz transformations> have determinant one. It is not itself a covariant <tensor> under general coordinate changes. For an oriented <Lorentzian metric>, replace it by the metric <metric volume tensor> $\varepsilon_{abcd}=\sqrt{-\det g}\,[abcd]$. The genuine dual is
$$
\boxed{(*F)_{ab}=\tfrac12\varepsilon_{abcd}F^{cd}.}
$$
This is the <Hodge star>; the determinant factor, <orientation> and raising conventions are essential. Even in flat spacetime arbitrary curvilinear coordinates require the <metric volume tensor>, so the distinction is about coordinate covariance rather than <curvature> alone.

The isolated equation for $R_{00}$ singles out a coordinate component and cannot be a generally covariant law. Replacing it by a proportionality $R_{ab}\propto T_{ab}$ still fails: the <contracted Bianchi identity> gives $\nabla^aR_{ab}=\nabla_bR/2$, whereas stress-<energy conservation> requires $\nabla^aT_{ab}=0$. Such a law would force $R$ constant and hence impose an unphysical restriction on a varying <relativistic dust> density. Missing units or a coupling constant in the proposed proportionality would not repair this structural problem.

The <Einstein tensor> $G_{ab}=R_{ab}-Rg_{ab}/2$ is divergence-free. Therefore
$$
\boxed{G_{ab}=-8\pi G T_{ab}}
$$
is consistent with covariant stress-<energy conservation> in the source's <curvature> convention. Its trace gives $R=8\pi GT$ and hence $R_{ab}=-8\pi G(T_{ab}-Tg_{ab}/2)$. For slowly moving <relativistic dust> with $V^aV_a=1$, $T_{00}\simeq\rho$, $T\simeq\rho$, $g_{00}\simeq1$, so $R_{00}\simeq-4\pi G\rho$, exactly matching $-\Delta\phi$ and Poisson's equation.

Finally, electromagnetic stress-energy is trace-free: $T^a{}_a=F^{ac}F_{ca}+F_{cd}F^{cd}=0$ by <antisymmetry>. The traced field equation then gives $\boxed{R=0}$ for a purely electromagnetic source. This does not say $R_{ab}=0$ or that spacetime is flat; its trace-free <Ricci curvature> can still be nonzero.

= Solution
{parent=6}

Under a smooth coordinate change $x\mapsto x'$, a <contravariant vector> obeys
$$
\boxed{T'^a=\frac{\partial x'^a}{\partial x^b}T^b.}
$$
The <scalar>-contraction hypothesis gives $U'_a(\partial x'^a/\partial x^b)T^b=U_bT^b$ for every choice of $T^b$. Equality of all coefficients and multiplication by the inverse <Jacobian matrix> give $\boxed{U'_a=(\partial x^b/\partial x'^a)U_b}$, the <covector> law.

Let $V^a=dx^a/ds$ be a <timelike geodesic> tangent. <Metric compatibility> and the <geodesic equation> give
$$
\frac d{ds}(T_aV^a)=V^aV^b\nabla_bT_a+T_a\frac{DV^a}{Ds}
=V^aV^b\nabla_{(a}T_{b)}.
$$
A <Killing vector> therefore gives a conserved contraction. Conversely, if that contraction is conserved along every <timelike geodesic>, then at any point its symmetric <tensor> $S_{ab}=\nabla_{(a}T_{b)}$ satisfies $S_{ab}V^aV^b=0$ for every unit timelike initial tangent. Homogeneity extends this to the entire <timelike cone>, an open set of tangent vectors. A <quadratic polynomial> vanishing on an open set has every coefficient zero, so $\boxed{\nabla_{(a}T_{b)}=0}$. Considering a single selected <geodesic> would not suffice. This is the <converse geodesic characterization of a Killing vector>.

For the given exterior transformation, differentiating at fixed $r$ gives
$$
\boxed{T=\partial_t=-\frac{U}{4m}\partial_U+\frac{V}{4m}\partial_V.}
$$
The transformation also gives $UV=(2m-r)e^{r/(2m)}$. This determines $r$ smoothly for $r>0$ throughout $UV<2m$. The flow of the displayed vector field rescales $U\mapsto e^{-\lambda/(4m)}U$, $V\mapsto e^{\lambda/(4m)}V$. It leaves $UV$, $r$, $dU\,dV$ and the angular metric unchanged. Thus it preserves the full metric everywhere in that region and extends as a global <Killing field>, not merely by a formal exterior coordinate substitution.

Its squared norm is
$$
\boxed{g(T,T)=-\frac{UV}{r}e^{-r/(2m)}=1-\frac{2m}{r}.}
$$
It is **timelike** for $UV<0$ ($r>2m$), **spacelike** for $0<UV<2m$ ($0<r<2m$), and a nonzero **<null vector>** on either horizon $UV=0$ away from $U=V=0$. At $U=V=0$ it is the **<zero vector>**, on the whole <bifurcation surface> of angular points. Zero must be distinguished from a nonzero <null vector>. This is the <Schwarzschild time-translation Killing field in Kruskal coordinates>.

= Solution
{parent=7}

The coordinate <derivatives> of the smooth two-parameter map commute. With a torsion-free <Levi-Civita connection>,
$$
\frac{DY^a}{Ds}=\frac{\partial^2x^a}{\partial s\partial\tau}+\Gamma^a{}_{bc}V^bY^c
=\frac{DV^a}{D\tau},
$$
because $\Gamma^a{}_{bc}$ is symmetric in its lower indices. Each member of the family is a <geodesic>, so $DV/Ds=0$. Consequently
$$
\frac{D^2Y^d}{Ds^2}=\frac D{Ds}\frac D{D\tau}V^d
=\left(\frac D{Ds}\frac D{D\tau}-\frac D{D\tau}\frac D{Ds}\right)V^d.
$$
The <commutator> of <covariant derivatives> is the action of the <Riemann curvature tensor>. With the source's index and sign convention this gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{D^2Y^d}{Ds^2}=R_{abc}{}^dV^aY^bV^c.}
$$
This is <geodesic deviation>: <free-fall> relative acceleration measures <curvature>, even where an individual observer can eliminate the connection at one point.

For Earth's exterior Newtonian field, $\phi=-GM/r$ and acceleration is $a_i=-\partial_i\phi$. Nearby freely falling particles have relative acceleration $\delta a_i=-\phi_{,ij}Y^j$. At the observation point align $z$ radially upward and $x,y$ horizontally. Since $GM/R_E^2=g$,
$$
-\phi_{,ij}=\frac g{R_E}\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,2).
$$
The weak-field <geodesic>-deviation equation therefore identifies
$$
\boxed{R_{010}{}^1\simeq-\frac g{R_E},\quad R_{020}{}^2\simeq-\frac g{R_E},\quad
R_{030}{}^3\simeq\frac{2g}{R_E}.}
$$
In these units time is measured in seconds as in the stated Newtonian comparison; if the zeroth coordinate is $ct$ instead, the <curvature> components acquire the corresponding $c^{-2}$ factor. A small freely falling cloud is squeezed in the two horizontal directions and stretched radially. These are tidal effects, not the uniform acceleration removed by a freely falling frame.

Their characteristic inverse-square time is $g/R_E$, so $\boxed{t_{\rm tide}=\sqrt{R_E/g}\simeq8.1\times10^2\ {\rm s}\simeq13.4\ {\rm min}}$. One concrete thought experiment is an ideal satellite moving in a <circular orbit> just above a spherical Earth, ignoring atmosphere and terrain. Its orbital <angular frequency> is $\sqrt{GM/R_E^3}=\sqrt{g/R_E}$ and its period is $2\pi t_{\rm tide}\simeq84$ minutes. Equivalently, initially nearby <free-fall> trajectories develop order-one tidal changes on this characteristic scale. This is the <surface tidal time scale of a spherical gravitating body>.

Write $f(r)=1-2m/r$ and restore $c$ for the numerical estimate. A stationary <clock> at radius $r$ has $d\tau=\sqrt{f(r)}\,dt$. Let radii $r_1$ and $r_2=r_1-h$ denote the upper and lower computers. The metric is stationary and the same two fixed spatial points exchange both signals, so each signal has the same coordinate propagation time. Therefore the interval between reception events equals the interval between emission events in <coordinate time>: the common delay cancels.

For upper proper running time $\tau_1$, the lower computer has run for
$$
\boxed{\tau_2=\sqrt{\frac{f(r_2)}{f(r_1)}}\,\tau_1.}
$$
In the <weak gravitational field> near Earth's surface, $m=GM/c^2$ and
$$
\sqrt{f(r_2)/f(r_1)}\simeq1-\frac{gh}{c^2}.
$$
The lower <clock> runs more slowly, so its remaining work is
$$
\boxed{N_{\rm left}\simeq(10^{12}\ {\rm s}^{-1})(604800\ {\rm s})
\frac{(9.81\ {\rm m\,s}^{-2})(10\ {\rm m})}{(2.99792458\times10^8\ {\rm m\,s}^{-1})^2}
\simeq6.6\times10^2.}
$$
Thus **about 660 micro-operations remain**; the proper-time shortfall is only about $6.6\times10^{-10}$ seconds.

Successful comparison tests the gravitational dependence of stationary proper-<clock> rates, specifically the radial variation of $g_{tt}$ and its weak-field <gravitational redshift> prediction, assuming the computers execute equally per unit <proper time>. It also checks that this <clock> effect is independent of the chosen physical <clock> mechanism. It does not separately verify the Schwarzschild radial metric coefficient: although light propagation uses the null metric, the identical start and finish propagation delays cancel from this particular observable. This is <gravitational redshift measured by a computation counter>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Put $f=1-2m/r$, use <proper time> $s$, and denote its <derivatives> by dots. The <timelike geodesic> equations, away from the Schwarzschild coordinate singularity, are
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\ddot t+\frac{f'}f\dot r\dot t&=0,\\
\ddot r+\frac{ff'}2\dot t^2-\frac{f'}{2f}\dot r^2-rf(\dot\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2)&=0,\\
\ddot\theta+\frac{2\dot r}r\dot\theta-\sin\theta\cos\theta\dot\phi^2&=0,\\
\ddot\phi+\frac{2\dot r}r\dot\phi+2\cot\theta\dot\theta\dot\phi&=0,
\end{aligned}}
$$
with <normalization> $f\dot t^2-f^{-1}\dot r^2-r^2\dot\theta^2-r^2\sin^2\theta\dot\phi^2=1$. <Spherical symmetry> permits an <equatorial plane>, $\theta=\pi/2$, and the cyclic coordinates give $\boxed{E=f\dot t,\quad J=r^2\dot\phi}$. <Normalization> then gives $\dot r^2=E^2-f(1+J^2/r^2)$.

For $J\ne0$ and $u=m/r$, $du/d\phi=-m\dot r/J$. Thus
$$
\boxed{(u')^2=\frac{(E^2-1)m^2}{J^2}+\frac{2m^2}{J^2}u-u^2+2u^3.}
$$
At $E=1$, $J=4m$, the right side factors as $2u(u-1/4)^2$. Set $q=2\sqrt u$, so $u=q^2/4$ and $(q')^2=(q^2-1)^2/8$. Integrating the regions $0<q<1$ and $q>1$ gives, up to angular shifts and time reversal,
$$
\boxed{u=\tfrac14\tanh^2\!\left(\frac{\phi-\phi_0}{2\sqrt2}\right),\qquad
u=\tfrac14\coth^2\!\left(\frac{\phi-\phi_0}{2\sqrt2}\right).}
$$
The constant $u=1/4$, or $r=4m$, is the corresponding circular timelike orbit. The hyperbolic-tangent branch lies at $r>4m$ and approaches that orbit from outside as $|\phi|\to\infty$; the hyperbolic-cotangent branch lies at $0<r<4m$ and approaches it from inside. Near the limit $u-1/4$ decays exponentially as $e^{-|\phi|/\sqrt2}$. Since $\dot\phi$ tends to $J/(4m)^2=1/(4m)$, the limiting approach also takes infinite <proper time>. Neither nonconstant branch reaches and joins the <circular orbit> at a finite angular parameter.

Differentiating the orbit equation gives $u''=m^2/J^2-u+3u^2$. A perturbation about $u=1/4$ obeys $\delta u''=\delta u/2$, showing that the <circular orbit> is unstable. The two explicit curves are its <separatrix> branches, not ordinary small stable oscillations. For $E=1$ the energy is marginally bound, so the outer critical branch is the limiting orbit between <scattering> and <Schwarzschild marginally bound capture> from infinity. Time-reversed inner and outer branches describe the corresponding approach or departure from that unstable orbit. These are the <explicit separatrices of the marginally bound Schwarzschild orbit>.
