= Symplectic geometry
{wiki}

Symplectic geometry studies smooth manifolds equipped with a closed nondegenerate differential two-form.

= Presymplectic form
{parent=Symplectic geometry}
{title2=$d\omega=0,\quad\operatorname{rank}\omega=\mathrm{constant}$}

A <presymplectic form> is a closed <two-form>, commonly assumed to have constant rank, that need not be nondegenerate. A <symplectic form> is its nondegenerate special case. Its kernel distribution is integrable: for kernel fields $X,Y$, the identity $d\omega=0$ gives $\iota_{[X,Y]}\omega=0$. If the characteristic leaf quotient is smooth and the form descends, that quotient has a <symplectic form>. A regular <Hamiltonian> energy hypersurface carries such a form with kernel spanned by its <Hamiltonian vector field>.

= Symplectic capacity
{parent=Symplectic geometry}
{title2=$c(X,\omega)$}

A symplectic capacity is monotone under <symplectic embeddings>, scales by $c(X,a\omega)=a\,c(X,\omega)$ for $a>0$, and has positive unit-ball capacity and finite unit-cylinder capacity. It is invariant under <symplectomorphisms>. A <normalized symplectic capacity> additionally assigns $\pi$ to both unit domains.

= Normalized symplectic capacity
{parent=Symplectic capacity}
{title2=$c(B^{2n}(1))=c(Z^{2n}(1))=\pi$}

A normalized <symplectic capacity> has the displayed unit-ball and unit-cylinder values. Dilation gives $\pi R^2$ for both radius-$R$ domains. Monotonicity then implies the sharp <Gromov non-squeezing theorem>.

= Eliashberg–Gromov rigidity theorem
{parent=Symplectic capacity}
{c}
{title2=$\operatorname{Symp}(M,\omega)\text{ is }C^0\text{-closed in }\operatorname{Diff}(M)$}

A locally uniform limit of <symplectomorphisms> that is a smooth diffeomorphism is symplectic. By <capacity preservation under uniform limits> and <linear capacity rigidity>, its derivative is symplectic or <anti-symplectic>. Repeat this argument after taking the product of every map with the identity on a symplectic plane. An anti-symplectic derivative on the original factor and a symplectic derivative on the added factor would give neither sign on the product, a contradiction. The argument uses <symplectic capacities> in the original and stabilized dimensions.

= Linear capacity rigidity
{parent=Symplectic capacity}
{title2=$c(TE)=c(E)\text{ for all ellipsoids}\implies T^*\omega=\pm\omega$}

An invertible real linear map preserving <symplectic capacities> of all <solid ellipsoids> is symplectic or <anti-symplectic>. For a pair with $\omega(u,v)=1$, if $0<|\omega(T^Tu,T^Tv)|<1$, complete this pair and its normalized transformed pair to <symplectic bases>. The resulting transpose matrix sends a unit ball into a <symplectic cylinder> of radius less than one, contradicting the capacity axioms after iterating the resulting linear map: its first coordinate pair contracts geometrically, while the image ellipsoid retains positive capacity. Perturbation handles a zero pairing; using $T^{-1}$ handles absolute value greater than one. Thus the absolute bilinear pairings agree. Their squares agree as polynomials, and the polynomial ring being an <integral domain> forces one global sign.

= Capacity preservation under uniform limits
{parent=Symplectic capacity}

Suppose diffeomorphisms preserving capacities of <solid ellipsoids> converge uniformly on compact sets to a diffeomorphism $\phi$. For $0<a<1$, uniform closeness and <degree of a continuous mapping> give $\phi_j(aE)\subseteq\phi(E)\subseteq\phi_j(a^{-1}E)$. The capacity axioms squeeze $c(\phi(E))$ between $a^2c(E)$ and $a^{-2}c(E)$. Letting $a$ tend to one proves equality. Rescaling a smooth $\phi$ near a point and repeating the argument also proves capacity preservation by its derivative.

= Geometric quantization
{parent=Symplectic geometry}

Geometric quantization associates quantum states and observables with a <symplectic manifold>. A first step uses a complex <line bundle> with a <connection on a vector bundle> whose curvature is proportional to the <symplectic form> divided by $\hbar$; its existence imposes an integrality condition. A real polarization is an integrable half-dimensional distribution with <Lagrangian submanifolds> as leaves. Requiring sections to be covariantly constant along the leaves reduces the variables to a configuration-space description. Restricting the curvature to a Lagrangian leaf gives zero, permitting local flat sections; global sections can require trivial holonomy. Exact Lagrangian graphs also describe local semiclassical phases through their generating functions.

= Polarization in geometric quantization
{parent=Geometric quantization}
{title2=$\mathcal F\subset T_{\mathbb C}P$}

= Geometric quantization polarization
{synonym}

A polarization is an involutive complex rank-half distribution on which the <symplectic form> vanishes. Polarized prequantum sections satisfy $\nabla_Xs=0$ for $X\in\mathcal F$. A real polarization is tangent to a <Lagrangian foliation>; a complex <Kähler> polarization uses the antiholomorphic tangent spaces. Only observables whose <Hamiltonian flows> preserve the chosen distribution act directly on the polarized space. Singular leaves, <holonomy> and changes of polarization are genuine quantization difficulties.

= Blattner-Kostant-Sternberg pairing
{c}
{parent=Polarization in geometric quantization}

= BKS pairing
{c}
{synonym}

The pairing compares state spaces belonging to two polarizations using their prequantum sections and half-forms. When it defines a suitable identification or projection, a classical <Hamiltonian flow> can transport a state to its transported polarization and the pairing can return it to the original state space. Differentiating this construction supplies operators for some observables that do not directly preserve the original polarization. Existence, domains and unitarity require additional hypotheses; it is not a universal canonical equivalence of all polarizations.

= Metaplectic correction
{parent=Polarization in geometric quantization}

= Half-form correction
{synonym}

Tensor the prequantum bundle with a square root of the canonical <determinant> bundle of a polarization, when such a square root exists. The half-form contributes to the state pairing and to the observable action. It improves coordinate invariance and accounts for familiar Maslov and oscillator zero-point shifts. Its existence is additional global data, not automatic from prequantum integrality.

= Prequantization
{parent=Geometric quantization}
{title2=$[\omega/(2\pi\hbar)]\in H^2(P,\mathbb Z)$}

= Pre-quantization
{synonym}

<Prequantization> realizes a <symplectic manifold> using a Hermitian <line bundle> with connection whose curvature is $i\omega/\hbar$ in the convention $\iota_{X_f}\omega=df$. Its existence requires integral symplectic periods. On sections, the <Kostant-Souriau prequantum operator> represents every classical observable and its <Poisson bracket>, but the resulting states depend on the full phase space. A <polarization in geometric quantization> removes this excess of variables.

= Kostant-Souriau prequantum operator
{c}
{parent=Prequantization}
{title2=$\widehat f=-i\hbar\nabla_{X_f}+f$}

= Prequantum operator
{synonym}

For $\iota_{X_f}\omega=df$ and $F_\nabla=i\omega/\hbar$, the curvature <commutator> and $[X_f,X_g]=-X_{\{f,g\}}$ give $[\widehat f,\widehat g]=i\hbar\widehat{\{f,g\}}$. In cotangent coordinates, $\theta=p\,dq$ gives $\widehat f=-i\hbar X_f+f-\theta(X_f)$. Restriction to a vertical polarization yields $\widehat q=q$ and $\widehat p=-i\hbar\partial_q$.

= Prequantum line bundle
{parent=Prequantization}
{title2=$F_\nabla=i\omega/\hbar$}

A <prequantum line bundle> is a Hermitian <line bundle> over a <symplectic manifold> with unitary <connection on a vector bundle> of the displayed curvature. With $\omega=-d\theta$, a local connection is $\nabla=d-i\theta/\hbar$. Reversing the Hamiltonian-vector-field convention or passing to the dual <line bundle> reverses the corresponding curvature convention. Integrality of $\omega/(2\pi\hbar)$ on closed two-cycles is the topological existence condition.

= Hamiltonian group action
{c}
{parent=Symplectic geometry}

= Hamiltonian action
{c}
{synonym}

A Hamiltonian group action is a <Lie group action> on a <symplectic manifold> with an equivariant <moment map> $\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*$. In the sign convention $\iota_{X_H}\omega=dH$, each component satisfies $d\langle\mu,X\rangle=\iota_{X_M}\omega$, where $X_M$ is the <fundamental vector field>. The opposite <Hamiltonian vector field> convention reverses the sign.

= Weakly Hamiltonian action
{parent=Hamiltonian group action}

A symplectic <Lie group action> is weakly Hamiltonian when every <fundamental vector field> is a <Hamiltonian vector field>. A <Hamiltonian group action> additionally supplies Hamiltonians as components of an equivariant <moment map>. Constants in the component Hamiltonians do not affect their <vector fields>, but the ability to choose them with the required equivariance is an additional global condition.

= Hamiltonian existence for a semisimple symplectic action
{parent=Weakly Hamiltonian action}
{c}
{title2=$[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]=\mathfrak g$}

For a symplectic left <Lie group action>, use actual fundamental fields $\xi_P$ and $\iota_{X_f}\omega=df$. They satisfy $[\xi_P,\eta_P]=-[\xi,\eta]_P$. <Cartan's magic formula> then gives $\iota_{[\xi,\eta]_P}\omega=d[\omega(\xi_P,\eta_P)]$. Thus every commutator generator has a globally exact contraction. A <semisimple Lie algebra> is perfect, so every generator has a global Hamiltonian. The separate <semisimple moment-map equivariance> result removes the constant two-cocycle obstruction and gives an equivariant <moment map> for a connected group.

= Symplectic reduction
{parent=Hamiltonian group action}
{title2=$\mu^{-1}(c)/G$}
{wiki}

For a <Hamiltonian group action>, a <regular value> level preserved by a free proper <group action> has a smooth quotient. The restricted <symplectic form> descends to a <symplectic form> on that quotient when the kernel is exactly the orbit directions; this is symplectic reduction.

= Lagrangian lift through circle reduction
{parent=Symplectic reduction}
{title2=$\widetilde L=\pi^{-1}(L)$}

At a regular free <circle group> level of a <moment map>, the inverse image of a <Lagrangian submanifold> of the reduced space is a <Lagrangian submanifold> of the original space. Its restricted <symplectic form> is the pullback of the reduced form and hence vanishes. Its dimension is one more than that of the reduced Lagrangian, exactly half the ambient dimension. It is a <circle bundle> over the original Lagrangian.

= Planar rotational symplectic reduction
{parent=Symplectic reduction}
{title2=$\omega_{\mathrm{red}}=dr\wedge dp_r$}

For a planar <cotangent bundle>, rotation has <moment map> $\ell=xp_y-yp_x$. At $\ell\ne0$ its level is regular and the circle action is free. Polar canonical coordinates give $\omega=dr\wedge dp_r+d\theta\wedge d\ell$. Fixing $\ell$ and quotienting $\theta$ leaves $T^*\mathbb R_{>0}$ with the displayed form. The unit-mass free <Hamiltonian> becomes $p_r^2/2+\ell^2/(2r^2)$.

= Symplectic reduction of an isotropic oscillator
{parent=Symplectic reduction}
{title2=$H^{-1}(E)/S^1\cong\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$}

For $H=\sum_j(q_j^2+p_j^2)/2$ and $E>0$, the oscillator flow is a free circle action on the sphere of radius $\sqrt{2E}$. Its quotient is <complex projective space>. For two modes, the reduced form in $w=z_2/z_1$ is $iE\,dw\wedge d\bar w/(1+|w|^2)^2$, of total area $2\pi E$. Reduction at zero energy is singular instead of a smooth quotient of the asserted dimension.

= Marsden-Weinstein theorem
{c}
{parent=Symplectic reduction}
{title2=$\mu^{-1}(c)/G$}

For a <Hamiltonian group action>, let $c$ be a <regular value> of the <moment map> fixed by the <coadjoint action>, and assume the action on $\mu^{-1}(c)$ is free and proper. The quotient is a <symplectic manifold> of dimension $\dim M-2\dim G$. Its unique reduced <symplectic form> is characterized by $\pi^*\omega_c=\iota^*\omega$, where $\iota$ includes the level set and $\pi$ is the quotient projection. The kernel of the restricted two-form consists precisely of the orbit directions. More general noncentral levels use their stabilizer group; the stated central-level version suffices for <circle group> reduction.

= Restriction of a Hamiltonian group action
{parent=Hamiltonian group action}

Restriction to a <Lie subgroup> with <Lie algebra> inclusion $i:\mathfrak h\to\mathfrak g$ has <moment map> $i^*\mu$. The <dual map> $i^*$ composes with the original <moment map>.

= Moment map
{parent=Hamiltonian group action}
{title2=$\mu:M\to\mathfrak g^*$}
{wiki}

= Momentum map
{synonym}

A moment map assigns to each element of a <Lie algebra> a <Hamiltonian function> for its <fundamental vector field>, and is equivariant for the <coadjoint action>. For an abelian <Lie group>, constant shifts are allowed; the numerical scale also depends on the chosen <Lie algebra> generators and the normalization of the <symplectic form>.

= Unitary moment map of an isotropic oscillator
{parent=Moment map}
{title2=$\mu_A=z^\dagger A z,\quad z=(x+ip)/\sqrt2$}

On the canonical <cotangent bundle> $T^*\mathbb R^n$, $\omega=i\sum dz_j\wedge d\bar z_j$ and $H=z^\dagger z$. The <unitary group> acts by $z\mapsto Uz$, preserving both. For a Hermitian $A$, the generator $-iA$ has Hamiltonian $\mu_A=z^\dagger Az$. The <Poisson bracket> satisfies $\{\mu_A,\mu_B\}=\mu_{-i[A,B]}$, with no central term. The central generator has moment map $H$; imaginary antisymmetric matrices give ordinary <angular momentum>, while real symmetric matrices give quadratic observables mixing position and momentum.

= Moment-map equivariance obstruction
{parent=Moment map}
{title2=$\kappa(X,Y)=\{\mu_X,\mu_Y\}-\mu_{[X,Y]}$}

For a weakly <Hamiltonian> left <Lie group action> on a connected <symplectic manifold>, choose $\iota_{X_f}\omega=df$. <Fundamental vector fields> and <Hamiltonian vector fields> are both antihomomorphisms, so the displayed difference is constant. The <Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket> makes it a two-cocycle. Changing $\mu_X$ by $b(X)$ changes the difference by $-b([X,Y])$. Thus its class in <Lie algebra cohomology> obstructs choosing an equivariant <moment map>. For a connected group, vanishing of this class gives infinitesimal and group equivariance.

= Fixed-point normalization removes the moment-map cocycle
{parent=Moment-map equivariance obstruction}
{title2=$\{\mu_\xi,\mu_\eta\}=\mu_{[\xi,\eta]}$}

Suppose a symplectic <Lie group action> on a connected <symplectic manifold> has global component Hamiltonians and a common fixed point $p_0$. Choose their constants linearly so $\mu_\xi(p_0)=0$. The difference $c(\xi,\eta)=\{\mu_\xi,\mu_\eta\}-\mu_{[\xi,\eta]}$ has zero <Hamiltonian vector field> because fundamental fields and Hamiltonian fields have compatible Lie-bracket conventions; it is therefore constant. At $p_0$, all fundamental fields vanish, so both terms in $c$ vanish. Thus $c=0$ everywhere and the <Poisson brackets> of the components reproduce the group <Lie algebra>. For a connected group this also yields coadjoint equivariance. A linear unitary action on a vector space has the origin as such a fixed point.

= Semisimple moment-map equivariance
{parent=Moment-map equivariance obstruction}
{title2=$H^2(\mathfrak g,\mathbb R)=0$}

For a semisimple <Lie algebra>, its nondegenerate <Killing form> turns a two-cocycle into an operator $D$ through $B(DX,Y)=\kappa(X,Y)$. The cocycle identity makes $D$ a derivation. Choose $Z$ by $B(Z,X)=\operatorname{tr}(D\operatorname{ad}X)$. Using $[D,\operatorname{ad}X]=\operatorname{ad}(DX)$ and cyclicity of trace gives $B([Z,X],Y)=B(DX,Y)$, so $D=\operatorname{ad}Z$. Consequently $\kappa(X,Y)=B(Z,[X,Y])$ is a coboundary. This proves that component Hamiltonians can be normalized to a <Lie algebra> homomorphism.

= Moment map for rotation of the complex projective line
{parent=Moment map}
{title2=$H(z)=|z|^2/(1+|z|^2)$}

For the <symplectic form> $\omega=i\,dz\wedge d\bar z/(1+|z|^2)^2$, the <circle group> rotates $z$ by $e^{i\theta}$. Its real generator is $K=iz\partial_z-i\bar z\partial_{\bar z}$. The contraction is $\iota_K\omega=-dH$, so $H$ is a <Hamiltonian function> in this sign convention. In the other chart $w=1/z$, $H=1/(1+|w|^2)$, proving smoothness at infinity. Its image is $[0,1]$ with the endpoints at the fixed poles. The <symplectic area> is $2\pi$; rescaling to the round area $4\pi$ would double this moment function.

= Symplectic group
{title2=$Sp(2n,\mathbb R)$}
{parent=Symplectic geometry}
{wiki}

The real symplectic group consists of matrices $A$ satisfying $A^TJA=J$. It is an embedded matrix Lie group of dimension $2n^2+n$ in size $2n$.

= Symplectic group over a field
{parent=Symplectic group}
{title2=$Sp(V,B)$}

For a finite-dimensional <vector space> over any <field> with a <nondegenerate> <alternating bilinear form> $B$, the <symplectic group over a field> consists of <invertible linear maps> preserving $B$. In a <symplectic basis> its matrices satisfy $g^{\mathsf T}Jg=J$. Matching two <symplectic bases> gives a unique such <linear map>, and a <symplectic basis> exists by successively splitting off paired vectors. This includes arbitrary-characteristic and infinite-field versions, beyond the real <Lie group> and the <symplectic group over a finite field>.

= Symplectic matrix
{parent=Symplectic group}
{title2=$A^TJA=J$}

An invertible real matrix preserving the <standard symplectic form>. With $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{pmatrix}$, it satisfies $A^TJA=J$, equivalently $AJA^T=J$. The first identity describes preservation of the form; the second describes preservation of coordinate <Poisson brackets>.

= Symplectic parabolic subgroup
{parent=Symplectic group}

The stabilizer of a totally isotropic $k$-space in a finite symplectic space also stabilizes its orthogonal complement. Its block decomposition has a unipotent normal subgroup $Q$ and a block-diagonal complement $L\cong\operatorname{GL}_k(q)\times\operatorname{Sp}_{2m-2k}(q)$. The paired $k$-space carries the contragredient action. A row-vector convention gives upper-triangular blocks; a column-vector convention transposes the arrangement. The <unipotent radical count for a symplectic parabolic subgroup> yields its order.

= Symplectic Lagrangian stabilizer
{parent=Symplectic parabolic subgroup}
{title2=$P_m=\operatorname{Sym}_m(q)\rtimes GL_m(q)$}

With a <Lagrangian subspace> first in a paired <symplectic basis>, its stabilizer consists of $\begin{pmatrix}A&AS\\0&A^{-\mathsf T}\end{pmatrix}$ with $S$ symmetric. The additive symmetric-matrix <unipotent radical> has order $q^{m(m+1)/2}$; the <Levi subgroup> is $GL_m(q)$, acting by $S\mapsto ASA^{\mathsf T}$. Its index in $Sp_{2m}(q)$ is $\prod_{i=1}^m(q^i+1)$, the number of Lagrangian subspaces.

= Symplectic point stabilizer
{parent=Symplectic parabolic subgroup}
{title2=$P_1=U\rtimes(\mathbb F_q^\times\times Sp_{2m-2}(q))$}

For a line $\langle e\rangle$, complete $(e,f)$ to a <symplectic basis> with complementary symplectic space $W$. The <unipotent radical> has elements $u(b,t)$ sending $e$ to $e$, $w$ to $w-B(w,b)e$ and $f$ to $f+b+te$. Its multiplication is $(b,t)(b',t')=(b+b',t+t'+B(b,b'))$, giving order $q^{2m-1}$. Its commutator is central with parameter $2B(b,b')$. It is Heisenberg-type in odd characteristic and abelian in characteristic two. The full stabilizer also scales $e,f$ inversely and acts symplectically on $W$.

= Unipotent radical count for a symplectic parabolic subgroup
{parent=Symplectic parabolic subgroup}
{title2=$|Q|=q^{2k(m-k)+k(k+1)/2}$}

In row-block order $(W',U,W)$ with dual bases, a unit-diagonal symplectic flag stabilizer satisfies $E=J_UD^T$ and $F-F^T=DJ_UD^T$. The matrix $D$ is arbitrary. The latter right side is alternating, so each off-diagonal pair of entries of $F$ gives one free choice and each diagonal entry is free. Consequently
$$
|Q|=q^{2k(m-k)+k(k+1)/2}.
$$
Reversed dual-basis order inserts a reversal matrix but does not change the count. The argument holds in characteristic two without division by two.

= Finite symplectic group order
{parent=Symplectic group}
{title2=$q^{m^2}\prod_{i=1}^m(q^{2i}-1)$}

Counting successive symplectic pairs gives
$$
|\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}(q)|=q^{m^2}\prod_{i=1}^m(q^{2i}-1).
$$
For each nonzero first vector there are $q^{2m-1}$ partners with pairing one, after which its nondegenerate plane splits off. The exact defining-characteristic part is $q^{m^2}$, realized by an upper symplectic unitriangular subgroup.

= Symplectic group as a regular level set
{parent=Symplectic group}

The map $F:M_{2n}(\mathbb R)\to\operatorname{Skew}_{2n}(\mathbb R)$ given by $F(A)=A^TJA$ has $Sp(2n,\mathbb R)=F^{-1}(J)$. At a symplectic $A$,
$$
DF_A(H)=H^TJA+A^TJH
$$
is surjective, so the <regular level set theorem> makes the symplectic group an embedded submanifold.

= Tangent space of the symplectic group
{parent=Symplectic group}

At the identity, the symplectic Lie algebra consists of $X$ satisfying $X^TJ+JX=0$; tangent spaces elsewhere are its left translates.

= Poisson manifold
{parent=Symplectic geometry}
{wiki}

A Poisson manifold is a smooth manifold with a bilinear bracket on smooth functions that is antisymmetric, obeys the <Jacobi identity>, and is a derivation in each argument. Its Poisson bivector may have nonconstant rank, unlike the inverse of a symplectic form.

= Momentum-space Poisson structure for a uniform magnetic field
{parent=Poisson manifold}
{title2=$\pi^{ij}=e\epsilon_{ijk}B_k$}

A uniform <magnetic field> defines a constant <Poisson bivector> on momentum space by the displayed components. For the Hamiltonian $E(\mathbf p)$ and convention $\dot f=\{f,E\}$, it gives $\dot{\mathbf p}=e\nabla E\times\mathbf B$. Constancy makes every term in the coordinate <Jacobi identity> vanish. If $e\mathbf B\ne0$, the <symplectic leaves> are the planes $\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf p=c$, and energy is conserved along the flow. The whole three-dimensional momentum space is not symplectic, and an energy surface need not itself be a Poisson submanifold.

= Symplectic foliation
{parent=Poisson manifold}

The <symplectic foliation> of a <Poisson manifold> consists of maximal connected integral manifolds of its Hamiltonian distribution. Each leaf has a nondegenerate closed two-form inverse to the restricted Poisson structure, with compatible sign conventions. Ranks can differ between leaves, making this a singular foliation. Hamiltonian flows remain within a leaf, and <Casimir functions of a Poisson manifold> are constant along every leaf; a Casimir level set need not be a single leaf.

= Poisson map
{c}
{parent=Poisson manifold}

A smooth map $r:M\to N$ between <Poisson manifolds> is a <Poisson map> when $\{f\circ r,g\circ r\}_M=\{f,g\}_N\circ r$ for all smooth functions on $N$. When a Lie group acts by Poisson automorphisms and the quotient is smooth, brackets of invariant functions descend and make the quotient projection Poisson. The quotient can be Poisson without being symplectic.

= Poisson tensor
{c}
{parent=Poisson manifold}
{title2=$\pi$}

A <Poisson tensor> is a smooth bivector field defining $\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)$ whose bracket obeys the Jacobi identity. In coordinates, $\pi^{i\ell}\partial_\ell\pi^{jk}+\pi^{j\ell}\partial_\ell\pi^{ki}+\pi^{k\ell}\partial_\ell\pi^{ij}=0$. Its Hamiltonian distribution integrates into <symplectic leaves>, even though its rank can vary across the manifold. The tensor specifies a bracket without requiring a globally nondegenerate symplectic form.

= Poisson structure
{parent=Poisson manifold}
{c}
{title2=$\{f,g\}=\omega^{ab}f_ag_b$}

A <Poisson structure> is an antisymmetric smooth bivector field whose induced function bracket satisfies the <Jacobi identity>. In coordinates its condition is the vanishing cyclic sum of $\omega^{dc}\partial_d\omega^{ab}$. Applying Jacobi to coordinate functions proves necessity; expanding arbitrary brackets cancels the second derivatives and proves sufficiency. A function bracket-commuting with every function is a <Casimir function of a Poisson manifold>.

= Poisson operator
{parent=Poisson structure}
{c}
{title2=$\{F,G\}=\int\frac{\delta F}{\delta u}\,J\!\left(\frac{\delta G}{\delta u}\right)dx$}

For field <functionals>, a Poisson operator $J$ is an operator whose displayed pairing of <variational derivatives> defines a <Poisson bracket>. It must be skew under the integral pairing and satisfy the <functional> <Jacobi identity>. A constant skew <differential operator>, such as $J=\partial_x$ with periodic or vanishing <boundary conditions>, gives the simplest examples. The <Hamiltonian> evolution is $u_t=J(\delta H/\delta u)$.

= Poisson pencil
{parent=Poisson structure}
{c}
{title2=$\Pi_\lambda=\Pi_1+\lambda\Pi_2$}

Two <Poisson structures> are compatible when every linear combination $\Pi_1+\lambda\Pi_2$ is a <Poisson structure>. This family is a Poisson pencil. In terms of the <Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket>, compatibility means $[\Pi_1,\Pi_2]=0$, in addition to the two individual <Jacobi identities>. Constant <Poisson bivectors> are compatible. Over a real <manifold>, complex parameter values refer to the complexification of its <function> algebra.

= Lenard-Magri recursion
{parent=Poisson pencil}
{c}
{title2=$\Pi_1dH_n=-\Pi_2dH_{n-1}$}

Start from a <Casimir function> $H_0$ of the first <Poisson structure>. Whenever possible, choose $H_n$ whose first <Hamiltonian vector field> is minus the second <Hamiltonian vector field> of $H_{n-1}$. A sequence extending indefinitely is the <coefficient> sequence of a formal <Casimir function> for the <Poisson pencil>. Its <functions> pairwise commute for both <Poisson brackets>. Existence at each stage is an additional solvability requirement; compatibility alone does not guarantee it.

= Obstruction to a Lenard-Magri recursion
{parent=Lenard-Magri recursion}

The next step of a <Lenard-Magri recursion> requires $-\Pi_2dH_{n-1}$ to be a <Hamiltonian vector field> for $\Pi_1$. It must be tangent to the <symplectic leaves>, and its contraction with the leaf <symplectic form> must have a global potential, with a compatible smooth extension between leaves. Even the first step may fail: on $\mathbb R^2$, take $\Pi_1=0$, $\Pi_2=\partial_x\wedge\partial_y$ and $H_0=x$. Both <Poisson structures> are compatible, but $\Pi_1dH_1=-\Pi_2dx$ cannot hold, because its left side is zero and its right side is nonzero.

= Commuting coefficients of a Poisson pencil Casimir
{parent=Lenard-Magri recursion}
{title2=$\{H_i,H_j\}_1=\{H_i,H_j\}_2=0$}

For a <Lenard-Magri recursion>, antisymmetry shifts an index: $\{H_i,H_j\}_1=\{H_{i-1},H_{j+1}\}_1$ when $i\geq1$. Repeating reaches $\{H_0,H_{i+j}\}_1=0$, because $H_0$ is a <Casimir function>. The recursion then gives $\{H_i,H_j\}_2=-\{H_{i+1},H_j\}_1=0$. This proof applies to every finite initial portion for the indices that can be shifted inside it, and to all pairs in an infinite sequence.

{c}
{synonym}

A <J-holomorphic curve> is regular when the linearized <Cauchy–Riemann operator> is <surjective>. Regularity only for simple curves is weaker than regularity for all maps: multiple covers of an exceptional sphere can fail regularity even when that simple sphere is regular.

= Generic transversality for simple holomorphic spheres
{parent=Regularity of a J-holomorphic curve}

For a residual set of smooth <compatible almost complex structures>, all <simple J-holomorphic spheres> in a specified countable set of spherical classes are regular. The corresponding evaluation maps at marked points are transverse to generic incidence conditions; generic paths give parameterized <transversality>. The universal linearization can vary the target structure near an <injective> point of the curve, which removes its <cokernel>. This assertion concerns simple curves and does not ensure regularity of arbitrary multiple covers.

= Negative-square exclusion under full sphere regularity
{parent=Regularity of a J-holomorphic curve}

If every nonconstant <J-holomorphic sphere> under consideration is regular, no such sphere in a four-dimensional target has class $A$ with $A^2\leq-2$. For a <simple J-holomorphic sphere>, the <adjunction inequality for a simple J-holomorphic sphere> makes the predicted moduli dimension negative. A degree-$m$ multiple cover of class $B$ has $B^2\leq-1$ and $c_1(B)\leq1$, so its predicted real dimension is at most $2m-2$. The <rational covering maps of the projective line> already form a family of dimension $4m-4>2m-2$, contradicting regularity. The full regularity hypothesis matters: multiple covers of exceptional minus-one spheres violate the conclusion under a simple-only regularity assumption.

= Lagrangian boundary condition
{c}
{parent=Pseudoholomorphic curve}
{title2=$u(s,i)\in L_i$}

A Lagrangian boundary condition for a <J-holomorphic curve> requires specified boundary components of the source to map into specified <Lagrangian submanifolds>. For the strip, this is $u(s,0)\in L_0$ and $u(s,1)\in L_1$. For a <compatible almost complex structure> these target submanifolds are totally real. The condition accompanies the interior equation $u_s+Ju_t=0$.

= Energy identity for a J-holomorphic curve
{c}
{parent=Pseudoholomorphic curve}

For compatible metrics on a Riemann surface and symplectic target,
$$
E(u)=\frac12\int_\Sigma|du|^2
=\int_\Sigma u^*\omega+\int_\Sigma|\bar\partial_Ju|^2.
$$
Thus a J-holomorphic curve minimizes energy among maps in its homology class.

= Monotonicity theorem for a J-holomorphic curve
{c}
{parent=Pseudoholomorphic curve}

On a compact region with controlled compatible almost complex structure, there are $c,r_0>0$ such that a nonconstant J-holomorphic curve through the center of a ball of radius $r<r_0$, with boundary outside the ball, has symplectic area at least $cr^2$. For the standard complex structure the sharp local constant is $\pi$.

= Arbitrarily large symplectic balls in a cotangent cylinder
{parent=Non-squeezing theorem}

The symplectic manifold $T^2\times\mathbb R^2$ contains symplectic balls of arbitrarily large radius. A steep linear Lagrangian graph in $\mathbb R^4=T^*\mathbb R^2$ has a thin neighborhood that injects under the quotient $\mathbb R^2\to T^2$, while a symplectic base-fiber dilation fits a large round ball into that long thin neighborhood.

= Symplectic fiber sum along a square-zero surface
{parent=Symplectic manifold}

If symplectic four-manifolds contain symplectic copies of the same closed surface with self-intersection zero, their trivial symplectic normal bundles may be identified with reversed circle fibers. Removing the two tubular neighborhoods and gluing their boundaries gives a <symplectic sum> carrying a natural symplectic form.

= Gompf realization theorem
{c}
{parent=Symplectic manifold}

Every finitely presented group is the <fundamental group> of a closed symplectic four-manifold. The construction starts from products of surfaces, realizes relator words by embedded loops after stabilization, and takes <symplectic sums> with copies of the <rational elliptic surface> along square-zero tori. Since the complement of a regular elliptic fiber in the rational elliptic surface is simply connected, van Kampen's theorem imposes the desired generators and relators.

The Gromov width of a symplectic manifold is the supremum of $\pi r^2$ over radii of symplectically embedded balls $B^{2n}(r)$.

= Gromov width of the monotone product of projective lines

{c}

When each projective line has area $\pi$, the product $\mathbb{CP}^1\times\mathbb{CP}^1$ has Gromov width $\pi$. The lower bound comes from the product of two area-$\pi$ discs; the upper bound comes from a J-holomorphic sphere of area $\pi$ through the center of any proposed ball.

= Liouville class of a Lagrangian submanifold
{title2=$[\lambda|_L]$}
{c}
{parent=Symplectic manifold}

For a Lagrangian submanifold $L\subset(\mathbb R^{2n},d\lambda)$, the restriction $\lambda|_L$ is closed. Its cohomology class is preserved by ambient symplectomorphisms because every closed one-form on $\mathbb R^{2n}$ is exact. On a product torus of circles, its periods are the enclosed symplectic areas of the factors.

= Holomorphic disk detects a nonzero Liouville class
{parent=Liouville class of a Lagrangian submanifold}

For a smooth nonconstant holomorphic disk $h$ in $\mathbb C^n$ with boundary on a <Lagrangian submanifold> $L$, its symplectic area is $\int_{D^2}|\partial_sh|^2\,ds\,dt>0$. The <Cauchy-Riemann equations> give $\partial_th=J\partial_sh$, so this is the pullback of the standard <symplectic form>. The <Stokes theorem> identifies the area with the period of a primitive along the boundary loop. Thus the <Liouville class of a Lagrangian submanifold> evaluates nontrivially on this loop and cannot vanish.

= Rational Lagrangian submanifold
{parent=Liouville class of a Lagrangian submanifold}

For a <Lagrangian submanifold> $L$ in an exact symplectic manifold with primitive $\lambda$, its period subgroup is $\{\int_c\lambda:c\in H_1(L;\mathbb Z)\}\subset\mathbb R$. It is rational when this subgroup is discrete. If it is nonzero, it is $\gamma\mathbb Z$ for a unique $\gamma>0$. Some conventions reserve the word rational for this nonzero case. The zero subgroup corresponds to an <exact differential form> $\lambda|_L$; its least positive period is interpreted as $+\infty$ if that endpoint convention is admitted.

= Least positive Liouville period
{parent=Rational Lagrangian submanifold}
{title2=$\gamma(L)=\inf\{a>0:a\in\operatorname{Per}(L)\}$}

For a nonzero discrete Liouville period group, this is its positive generator. In Euclidean symplectic space, the same group is obtained from signed symplectic areas of disks with boundary on $L$, by the <Stokes theorem> and contractibility of the ambient space. A product of circles of common radius $r$ is a <rational Lagrangian submanifold> with $\gamma(L)=\pi r^2$. Translation changes a primitive only by an exact form and hence leaves the period group unchanged.
