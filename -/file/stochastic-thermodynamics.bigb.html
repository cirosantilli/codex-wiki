= Stochastic thermodynamics
{wiki}

Stochastic thermodynamics assigns work, heat, and entropy production to individual fluctuating trajectories while recovering ordinary thermodynamics after ensemble averaging.

= Local detailed balance
{parent=Stochastic thermodynamics}

Local detailed balance states that the logarithm of the probability ratio between a trajectory and its time reverse equals the entropy delivered to the environment in units of $k_B$. For one heat bath, $\log(P_F/P_B)=\Delta Q/(k_BT)$ when positive $\Delta Q$ flows into the bath.

= Microscopic reversibility
{parent=Local detailed balance}
{wiki}

For an equilibrium process, a path and its physically time-reversed path have equal probability after including their equilibrium starting weights. Conditional on their endpoints, this means
$$
\frac{P_F}{P_B}=\exp[-(F_2-F_1)/(k_BT)].
$$
This is the path version of <detailed balance>. A time-even <order parameter> is read backward without changing sign, while a transport flux changes sign. The <Onsager--Machlup path probability> implements this reversal in a coarse-grained stochastic description.

= Entropy production
{title2=$\Delta S_{\rm tot}$}
{parent=Stochastic thermodynamics}
{wiki}

Entropy production is the sum of the entropy changes of a system and its environment. Under local detailed balance it is represented by a forward-to-reversed path-probability logarithm and has nonnegative expectation.

= Entropy gain from impact and thermal equilibration
{parent=Entropy production}

A body of mass $m$, <heat capacity> $c$, <temperature> $T_i$ and speed $v$ that stops in a large reservoir at $T_L$ produces <entropy> $mc[\log(T_L/T_i)+T_i/T_L-1]+mv^2/(2T_L)$, assuming constant <heat capacity> and no phase changes. The first term follows by adding the body's logarithmic <entropy> change to the reservoir's <heat> gain; the second is kinetic-energy dissipation. A numerical answer needs the reservoir <temperature>.

= Thermodynamic entropy flux
{parent=Entropy production}
{title2=$j_\eta=q/\theta$}

Local equilibrium associates the outgoing <heat flux> $q$ with outgoing <entropy> flux $q/\theta$ at positive <temperature> $\theta$. Its <divergence> appears in the <Lagrangian entropy inequality>, with the appropriate nominal flux in reference coordinates.

= Fluctuation theorem
{parent=Entropy production}
{wiki}

A fluctuation theorem compares the <probability> of a fluctuating <sample trajectory> with that of its reverse. Positive mean <entropy production> permits negative individual realizations: what is constrained is their relative <probability>, not a prohibition on every reversal. The finite statement below compares specified forward and reverse <probability laws>; replacing the reverse law by the forward law requires an additional symmetry. The <biased-step entropy production model> exhibits that symmetry exactly, while the <Crooks fluctuation theorem> compares two different driving protocols.

= Finite trajectory entropy production
{parent=Fluctuation theorem}
{title2=$\Sigma=\Delta S_{\rm tot}/k_B$}

Consider a finite-duration <Markov process> with discrete, time-even states coupled to one <thermal reservoir> at positive <temperature> $T$. Let $P_F[\gamma]$ include the initial <probability> $\rho_0(x_0)$ of the <sample trajectory> $\gamma$. Define $P_R$ by reversing the control protocol and initializing it with the actual forward final <probability distribution> $\rho_\tau$. Write $\Theta\gamma$ for the same states visited in reverse time order. Require normalized path laws with matching <probability support>: a path has positive forward weight exactly when its reverse has positive reverse weight. This excludes irreversible forbidden transitions and missing initial states.

Under <local detailed balance>, with positive <heat> $Q_{\rm bath}$ delivered to the <thermal reservoir>, the full path-weight <logarithm> is
$$
\Sigma_F[\gamma]=\ln\frac{P_F[\gamma]}{P_R[\Theta\gamma]}
=\frac{Q_{\rm bath}[\gamma]}{k_BT}+\ln\frac{\rho_0(x_0)}{\rho_\tau(x_\tau)}
=\frac{\Delta S_{\rm tot}[\gamma]}{k_B}.
$$
The endpoint term is the change in stochastic system <entropy>; the conditional path ratio alone accounts for the <thermal reservoir>. Here $k_B$ is the <Boltzmann constant>. Both terms matter even for a short observation. <Entropy production along a stochastic trajectory and an integral fluctuation theorem by Udo Seifert>[Seifert, 2005, Eqs. (14)–(18) and (30): conditional and full path weights, endpoint distributions, total entropy]{ref}

Define the conjugate reverse functional by $\Sigma_R[\eta]=\ln(P_R[\eta]/P_F[\Theta\eta])$, so $\Sigma_R[\Theta\gamma]=-\Sigma_F[\gamma]$. Summing the path identity over paths with a given value gives the exact finite <fluctuation theorem>
$$
\frac{p_F(s)}{p_R(-s)}=e^s,
$$
for nonzero denominator, using <probability masses> or corresponding <probability density functions>. This $\Sigma_R$ is defined by the paired laws: it need not be the total <entropy production> obtained by independently restarting the thermodynamic construction for the reverse experiment. A generic driven experiment therefore does not imply $p_F(s)/p_F(-s)=e^s$.

= Integral fluctuation theorem
{parent=Finite trajectory entropy production}
{title2=$\langle e^{-\Sigma_F}\rangle_F=1$}

Normalization of the reverse law gives an exact finite identity:
$$
\left\langle e^{-\Sigma_F}\right\rangle_F
=\sum_\gamma P_F[\gamma]\frac{P_R[\Theta\gamma]}{P_F[\gamma]}=1.
$$
The sum notation describes discrete paths; the corresponding path <integral> has the same change-of-variable argument. <Jensen's inequality> for the <exponential function> then gives $e^{-\langle\Sigma_F\rangle_F}\le1$, hence $\langle\Sigma_F\rangle_F\ge0$. Equivalently this mean is the <Kullback-Leibler divergence> between the forward path law and the reverse path law pulled back by $\Theta$. Equality requires those two laws to agree. <Entropy production along a stochastic trajectory and an integral fluctuation theorem by Udo Seifert>[Seifert, 2005, Eqs. (17)–(18): integral relation and its mean inequality]{ref}

Rare negative <entropy production> contributes disproportionately to this <expectation> through $e^{-\Sigma_F}$. Missing such events in a finite sample can spoil an estimate of the identity. Matching <probability support> is also essential: reverse paths absent from the forward law cannot be recovered by cancelling forward weights. This <second law of thermodynamics> statement concerns an <ensemble average>; an individual negative value is compatible with it.

= Biased-step entropy production model
{parent=Fluctuation theorem}
{title2=$S_n=a(2K-n),\ a=\ln(p/q)$}

Take $n$ <independent> steps, each $+1$ with <probability> $p>1/2$ and $-1$ with <probability> $q=1-p>0$. Reverse a step sequence by reading it backward and changing each sign; use the same biased law for both experiments. With $K$ positive steps, the forward sequence weight is $p^Kq^{n-K}$ and its reversed weight is $p^{n-K}q^K$. Their <logarithm> ratio is
$$
S_n=(2K-n)a,\qquad a=\ln(p/q)>0,\qquad
K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p).
$$
Here $S_n$ is dimensionless <entropy production>, not an <entropy> in joules per kelvin. A <Markov chain> on a three-state ring gives a state-space realization: start uniformly, take a clockwise step with <probability> $p$ and a counterclockwise step with <probability> $q$. The uniform <stationary distribution> makes endpoint weights cancel. A physical interpretation as exchanged <heat> requires <local detailed balance> with bath <entropy> $\pm k_Ba$ per directed step; the abstract model alone specifies path <probabilities>.

For an allowed value $s=a(2k-n)$, the <binomial distribution> gives
$$
p_n(s)=\binom nkp^kq^{n-k},\qquad
\frac{p_n(s)}{p_n(-s)}
=\frac{\binom nkp^kq^{n-k}}{\binom n{n-k}p^{n-k}q^k}
=\left(\frac pq\right)^{2k-n}=e^s.
$$
This is an exact same-law finite <fluctuation theorem>, with no long-time approximation. For $p=3/4$, $n=9$ and $k=6$, $s=3\ln3$ and $p_n(s)/p_n(-s)=27$. The positive mean is $\langle S_n\rangle=na(p-q)$, although the all-negative path has nonzero <probability> $q^n$. Directly, $\langle e^{-S_n}\rangle=(pe^{-a}+qe^a)^n=(q+p)^n=1$.

\Image[biased-step-entropy-probabilities.png]
{title=Exact biased-step model: discrete entropy-production probabilities and their log ratio at nine steps}

The left panel shows exact <probability masses> for $p=3/4$, with negative values highlighted; the right panel tests the <logarithm> ratio against $s$. These are model values, without experimental measurements or simulated measurement noise.

= Negative entropy production over finite durations
{parent=Biased-step entropy production model}
{title2=$\Pr(S_n<0)$}

A single step has negative <entropy production> with <probability> $q$, independent of how long an observation lasts. A whole window is negative only if negative steps outnumber positive ones:
$$
\Pr(S_n<0)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\binom nkp^kq^{n-k}.
$$
For even $n$, the tie $K=n/2$ has zero <entropy production> and is excluded. At $p=3/4$, the negative-window <probabilities> for $n=1,9,21$ are approximately $0.25$, $0.04893$, and $0.00642$. Longer windows still contain negative increments: their expected number is $nq$. Their total is usually positive because the expected number of positive increments is $np$.

\Image[negative-entropy-window-duration.png]
{title=Exact biased-step model: negative totals become rare with duration while negative increments remain frequent}

Only odd window lengths are plotted to avoid alternating tie effects. The left panel gives the exact negative-window <probability> and the upper bound $(2\sqrt{pq})^n$; the right gives expected counts $np$ and $nq$. For steps of fixed duration $\delta t$, physical duration is $n\delta t$. These are counts and <probabilities> predicted by the model, not bead traces. The bound follows from $\Pr(S_n<0)\le\langle e^{-S_n/2}\rangle=(2\sqrt{pq})^n$. Since $2\sqrt{pq}<1$ for $p\ne q$, negative totals are exponentially suppressed even though the negative-step fraction stays $q$.

= Steady-state asymptotic fluctuation theorem
{parent=Fluctuation theorem}
{title2=$I(-s)-I(s)=s$}

A <steady state> does not by itself give every fluctuating observable an <entropy production> symmetry. If the dimensionless total <entropy production> $\Sigma_t$ has the same-law finite relation and a <large deviation principle> with <rate function> $I$ for $\Sigma_t/t$, then, on its common domain,
$$
p_t(\Sigma_t/t\approx s)\asymp e^{-tI(s)},\qquad
I(-s)-I(s)=s.
$$
The first expression denotes leading exponential scaling; the second follows by taking the scaled <logarithm> of the probability ratio. A finite-state, autonomous <Markov process> with time-even states, positive bidirectional rates and a positive <stationary distribution> supplies a standard setting for a finite total-<entropy production> relation. The stochastic system <entropy> is then bounded, so its endpoint contribution does not change the long-time <rate function> of bath <entropy production>. <Stochastic thermodynamics, fluctuation theorems, and molecular machines by Udo Seifert>[Seifert, 2012, §3.3.2, p. 22, Eq. (65); §4.4.3, p. 28, Eqs. (93)–(95): finite stationary total entropy and endpoint-conditioned medium entropy]{ref}

Unbounded state spaces can have endpoint fluctuations on the scale relevant to a <large deviation principle>. Dropping system <entropy>, changing the measured observable to <heat>, or changing the reverse preparation therefore requires a fresh argument. The exact finite <integral fluctuation theorem>, a same-law finite relation, and an asymptotic <rate function> symmetry are distinct claims.

= Crooks fluctuation theorem
{c}
{parent=Fluctuation theorem}
{title2=$P_F(W)/P_R(-W)=e^{\beta(W-\Delta F)}$}

For a system coupled to a <thermal reservoir> at fixed <temperature> $T$, prepare the forward process in the initial <canonical ensemble> and the reverse process in the final control parameter's <canonical ensemble>. Reverse the protocol, assume <microscopic reversibility> and matching path <probability support>, and count $W$ as <work> done on the system. With <Helmholtz free energy> difference $\Delta F=F_{\rm final}-F_{\rm initial}$ and $\beta=1/(k_BT)$,
$$
\frac{P_F(W)}{P_R(-W)}=e^{\beta(W-\Delta F)}.
$$
The final forward distribution need not already be in <thermal equilibrium>. Its reverse preparation is prescribed equilibrium preparation, rather than the actual forward final distribution used in <finite trajectory entropy production>. Thus the paired path <logarithm> $\beta(W-\Delta F)$ is dissipated <work> in thermal units, not automatically the instantaneous total stochastic <entropy production> at the end of an arbitrary driven protocol. <The Entropy Production Fluctuation Theorem and the Nonequilibrium Work Relation for Free Energy Differences by Gavin E. Crooks>[Crooks, 1999, Eqs. (5)–(7), (9)–(11): reversal, equilibrium weights and work relation]{ref} <Entropy production along a stochastic trajectory and an integral fluctuation theorem by Udo Seifert>[Seifert, 2005, Eq. (19) and following paragraph: actual final distribution versus final equilibrium distribution]{ref}

= Jarzynski equality
{c}
{parent=Crooks fluctuation theorem}
{title2=$\langle e^{-\beta W}\rangle_F=e^{-\beta\Delta F}$}

Integrating the <Crooks fluctuation theorem> over <work> and using normalization of the reverse <probability distribution> gives
$$
\langle e^{-\beta W}\rangle_F=e^{-\beta\Delta F}.
$$
Consequently <Jensen's inequality> gives $\langle W\rangle_F\ge\Delta F$. Some realizations may nevertheless have $W<\Delta F$. This is a <work> relation with equilibrium endpoint preparations, while the <integral fluctuation theorem> for total <entropy production> uses the actual final forward distribution. <The Entropy Production Fluctuation Theorem and the Nonequilibrium Work Relation for Free Energy Differences by Gavin E. Crooks>[Crooks, 1999, Eq. (3) and derivation from Eqs. (10)–(11)]{ref}

= Dragged-bead fluctuation theorem experiment
{parent=Fluctuation theorem}

The 2002 experiment trapped a $6.3\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ bead in <water> with a $980\,\mathrm{nm}$ <laser>, a $100\times$, <numerical aperture> $1.3$ objective and a piezo-driven microscope stage. A quadrant <photodiode> tracked bead position at $1\,\mathrm{kHz}$ with approximately $500\,\mathrm{Hz}$ bandwidth and reported $15\,\mathrm{nm}$ resolution. Stage translation drove water past the fixed <optical trap> at $1.25\,\mathrm{\mu m/s}$. Weak trapping prolonged the transient; stationary recording before translation established the position reference. <Stokes drag law> calibration was checked against the bead's measured <diffusion coefficient>. <Experimental Demonstration of Violations of the Second Law of Thermodynamics for Small Systems and Short Time Scales by G. M. Wang and others>[Wang and colleagues, 2002, p. 050601-2: apparatus, calibration and trajectory protocol]{ref}

From calibrated <displacement> and calibrated <harmonic optical trap> <force>, the experimenters evaluated the dimensionless driven-bead dissipation functional
$$
\Sigma_t^{\rm drive}=\frac1{k_BT}\int_0^t\mathbf v_{\rm opt}\cdot\mathbf F_{\rm opt}(u)\,du.
$$
Among 540 trajectories, negative values became less frequent with duration. Figure 2 compared the negative/positive count ratio with $\langle e^{-\Sigma_t^{\rm drive}}\rangle_{\Sigma_t^{\rm drive}>0}$; its agreement tested the paper's integrated transient relation. The detailed log-ratio plot in Figure 4 used simulated particle <sample trajectories>, not bead measurements. Near-zero sign discrimination and stage-start transients limited the earliest points. <Experimental Demonstration of Violations of the Second Law of Thermodynamics for Small Systems and Short Time Scales by G. M. Wang and others>[Wang and colleagues, 2002, Eq. (2), Eq. (3), pp. 050601-2–4 and Figs. 1, 2 and 4]{ref}

For any continuous <probability distribution> satisfying the same-law relation, integration over $s>0$ yields
$$
\frac{\Pr(\Sigma<0)}{\Pr(\Sigma>0)}
=\left\langle e^{-\Sigma}\right\rangle_{\Sigma>0}.
$$
This conditional relation explains the comparison without equating it to the unconditional <integral fluctuation theorem>. The experiment supports a specified driven-system relation; it does not establish a universal same-law symmetry for any definition of <heat> or <entropy production>. In particular, a transient work-like functional should not silently acquire the actual-distribution endpoint term in <finite trajectory entropy production>. Negative realizations are compatible with positive mean dissipation and the <second law of thermodynamics>; they do not provide a cyclic machine with reliable <work> output from a single <thermal reservoir>.

= Fluctuation-dissipation relation for a Langevin particle
{title2=$\sigma^2=2\zeta k_BT$}
{parent=Stochastic thermodynamics}

For a Langevin particle with drag coefficient $\zeta$ and white-force covariance amplitude $\sigma^2$, detailed balance at temperature $T$ requires $\sigma^2=2\zeta k_BT$.

= Vector noise normalization in inertial Langevin dynamics
{parent=Fluctuation-dissipation relation for a Langevin particle}
{title2=$\langle\mathbf A(t)\cdot\mathbf A(s)\rangle=(6\gamma k_BT/m^2)\delta(t-s)$}

Divide the inertial <Langevin equation> by mass and let $\zeta=\gamma/m$. An isotropic three-dimensional equilibrium velocity has $\langle|\mathbf u|^2\rangle=3k_BT/m$ by the <equipartition theorem>. Its white acceleration dot-covariance amplitude is therefore $6\zeta k_BT/m$, while the amplitude per Cartesian component is $2\zeta k_BT/m$. A scalar moment formula must use the component variance rather than the trace.
