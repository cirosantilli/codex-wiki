= Schwarzschild spacetime
{c}

= Schwarzschild metric
{synonym}
{c}

= Schwarzschild black hole
{synonym}
{c}

In units $G=c=1$, the Schwarzschild exterior metric is
$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2
+\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2.
$$

= Coordinate invariance of the Schwarzschild photon radial potential
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$V_{\rm eff}(r)=(1-2\mu/r)/r^2$}

A stationary time-coordinate change $t'=t+F(r)$ leaves both the <areal radius> and the time-translation <Killing vector field> unchanged. The conserved <Killing energy> and <angular momentum> of a <null geodesic> therefore yield the same radial equation in either chart, $\dot r^2+h^2(1-2\mu/r)/r^2=c^2k^2$. The <effective potential> and radial <turning points> agree, even when the coordinate time velocities and horizon regularity differ.

= Schwarzschild static observer
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}

An observer at fixed exterior Schwarzschild spatial coordinates. Its <proper time> is $dT=\sqrt f\,dt$, with $f=1-2GM/(rc^2)>0$. It is accelerated, not freely falling; no such static timelike <worldline> exists on or inside the <event horizon>.

= Local radial speed of a Schwarzschild geodesic
{parent=Schwarzschild static observer}
{title2=$v_{\rm loc}$}

For a radial <timelike geodesic> of specific <Killing energy> $k=f\,dt/d\tau$, the static observer measures $v_{\rm loc}=(dr/dt)/f=\dot r/k$. Its relative <Lorentz factor> is $k/\sqrt f$, giving $v_{\rm loc}^2=c^2(1-f/k^2)$.

= Radial Schwarzschild escape with asymptotic speed
{parent=Local radial speed of a Schwarzschild geodesic}

For asymptotic <speed> $0\le u<c$, $k=(1-u^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$ and the local outward <speed> obeys $v_{\rm loc}^2=u^2+(2GM/r)(1-u^2/c^2)$. It tends to $c$ as the horizon is approached from outside, though a static observer cannot actually remain on the horizon.

= Marginal escape geodesic in Schwarzschild spacetime
{parent=Local radial speed of a Schwarzschild geodesic}

The outgoing radial <timelike geodesic> with zero <speed> at infinity has $k=1$ and $dr/d\tau=\sqrt{2GM/r}$. Thus $r(\tau)=[r_0^{3/2}+(3/2)\sqrt{2GM}\tau]^{2/3}$ and the local static-observer <speed> is $\sqrt{2GM/r}$.

= Timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$h^2=\mu c^2R^2/(R-3\mu),\quad k^2=(R-2\mu)^2/[R(R-3\mu)]$}

For the <Schwarzschild metric> with $\mu=GM/c^2$, let $k=(1-2\mu/r)\dot t$ and $h=r^2\dot\phi$ on an equatorial <timelike geodesic>. The normalization of the <four-velocity> gives $\dot r^2+V(r)=c^2(k^2-1)$, where $V(r)=(1-2\mu/r)h^2/r^2-2\mu c^2/r$. A circular <geodesic> obeys $V'(R)=0$, giving the displayed constants and requiring $R>3\mu$. Its coordinate <angular velocity> is $\Omega^2=GM/R^3$, and its proper clock rate is $d\tau/dt=\sqrt{1-3\mu/R}$. Differentiating $V$ twice at the circular solution gives stability for $R>6\mu$.

= Marginally bound circular Schwarzschild orbit
{parent=Timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic}
{title2=$r_{\mathrm{mb}}=4GM/c^2,\quad E=mc^2$}

The circular timelike orbit with conserved <energy> equal to <rest energy> at infinity. Substituting $E/(mc^2)=(1-2\mu/r)/\sqrt{1-3\mu/r}$ and solving $E=mc^2$ gives $r=4\mu$, with $\mu=GM/c^2$. It is unstable. Strictly bound circular orbits have $r>4\mu$ and $E<mc^2$; radial stability requires $r>6\mu$. This limiting orbit is not the smallest radius ever reached by a bound-energy plunging trajectory.

= Schwarzschild circular-orbit local speed
{parent=Timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic}
{c}
{title2=$v_{\mathrm{loc}}^2/c^2=\mu/(r-2\mu),\quad\mu=GM/c^2$}

A <Schwarzschild static observer> at a circular orbit measures speed $v_{\rm loc}=r(d\phi/dt)/\sqrt{1-2\mu/r}$. Combining the circular <geodesic> <frequency> with this clock rate gives the displayed formula. The observer-relative <Lorentz factor> is $\gamma=\sqrt{(r-2\mu)/(r-3\mu)}$, and $r\,d\phi/d\tau=\gamma v_{\rm loc}$. Thus the local speed is $c/2$ at the Schwarzschild <innermost stable circular orbit>. Coordinate and locally measured speeds are distinct.

= Face-on frequency shift of a circular Schwarzschild source
{parent=Timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic}
{title2=$\nu_\infty/\nu_{\rm em}=\sqrt{1-3GM/(c^2r)}$}

A source on a <timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic> at radius $r>3GM/c^2$ emits light to a static observer at infinity on the orbital symmetry axis. Such a ray has zero conserved axial <angular momentum>. The locally measured photon <energy> is the contraction of its <four-momentum> with the observer's <four-velocity>. Comparing the emitter with the observer at infinity therefore gives $\nu_{\rm em}=U^t\nu_\infty$, where $U^t=[1-3GM/(c^2r)]^{-1/2}$, proving the formula.

Equivalently the <gravitational redshift> factor $\sqrt{1-2GM/(c^2r)}$ multiplies the transverse <Doppler effect> factor $1/\gamma$ measured by a local static observer. For $R_S=2GM/c^2\ll r$,
$$
\nu_{\rm em}=\nu_\infty\left[1+\frac{3R_S}{4r}+O\!\left(\frac{R_S^2}{r^2}\right)\right].
$$
The coefficient contains both gravitational and orbital time dilation. It assumes an observer at infinity on the symmetry axis; other viewing directions generally introduce a first-order longitudinal <Doppler effect>.

= Comparison of circular-orbit and static Schwarzschild clocks
{parent=Timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic}
{title2=$\tau_s/\tau_E=\sqrt{(1-3\mu/R_s)/(1-2\mu/R_E)}$}

A clock following a <timelike circular Schwarzschild geodesic> of <radius> $R_s$ advances at $\sqrt{1-3\mu/R_s}$ per unit Schwarzschild coordinate time. A static clock at <radius> $R_E>2\mu$ advances at $\sqrt{1-2\mu/R_E}$. Their ratio over the same coordinate-time interval gives the displayed result. For periodic signal comparisons, successive corresponding signals must also have the same propagation delay, as happens for repeated overhead passages in a fixed circular orbit observed from a static pole. To first order in $\mu/R$, the ratio is $1+\mu/R_E-3\mu/(2R_s)$.

= Schwarzschild circular-ring energy shift
{parent=Comparison of circular-orbit and static Schwarzschild clocks}
{c}
{title2=$1+z=(1-3r_g/r)^{-1/2}(1+\beta\sin i)$}

For the receding edge of a circular emitter ring, the direct-projection model gives $1+z=(1-3r_g/r)^{-1/2}[1+\sqrt{r_g/(r-2r_g)}\sin i]$, with $r_g=GM/c^2$. Gravitational and transverse shifts combine with the line-of-sight <Doppler factor>. Mapping the distant inclination directly to a local <photon> direction neglects gravitational ray bending.

= Edge-on Schwarzschild ISCO redshift envelope
{parent=Schwarzschild circular-ring energy shift}
{c}
{title2=$1+z_{\max}=3/\sqrt2$}

At $r=6r_g$, the local circular speed is $c/2$, so the receding direct-projection shift is $\sqrt2(1+\sin i/2)$. The upper envelope over inclinations is $3/\sqrt2$. It is not the shift for a face-on or arbitrary azimuthal emitter, and it excludes plunging-region emission and ray-tracing corrections.

= Schwarzschild radial fall from rest at a finite radius
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$r=\tfrac{r_0}2(1+\cos\eta),\quad s=\sqrt{r_0^3/(8M)}(\eta+\sin\eta)$}

For radial timelike <geodesic> fall released from rest at $r_0>2M$, the <Killing energy> is $E=\sqrt{1-2M/r_0}$ and the <proper time> radial equation is $(dr/ds)^2=2M/r-2M/r_0$. The displayed parameterization integrates its inward branch, with $s=0$ at release. The <Schwarzschild event horizon> is reached at $\eta_H=\arccos(4M/r_0-1)$ in finite proper time, even though <Schwarzschild time> diverges.

= Finite pulse count for a falling Schwarzschild beacon
{parent=Schwarzschild radial fall from rest at a finite radius}
{title2=$N=\left\lceil(s_H-\delta)/P\right\rceil$}

A freely falling source emits <optical pulses> at proper times $\delta+nP$, for integers $n\ge0$. If its horizon-crossing <proper time> is finite, only pulses beginning before $s_H$ can escape. Their number is the displayed ceiling, for $0\le\delta<P<s_H$. Infinite <Schwarzschild time> at crossing does not give infinitely many emitted pulses. The pulse phase $\delta$ fixes both the count and the redshift of the last pulse. If a finite pulse straddles the horizon, its exterior portion has an arbitrarily late, increasingly redshifted received tail.

= Proper-time bound inside a Schwarzschild black hole
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$\Delta s\leq2M\arcsin\sqrt{r_0/(2M)}-\sqrt{r_0(2M-r_0)}<\pi M$}

Inside the future black-hole region, the radius decreases along every future-directed timelike path. Its unit tangent obeys $|dr/ds|\geq\sqrt{2M/r-1}$ from the metric normalization, independently of radial acceleration or angular motion. Integrating from $r_0<2M$ to zero gives the displayed finite upper bound. The statement concerns future continuation within the black-hole region; reversing time in the white-hole region reverses the radial direction.

= Horizon redshift of a Schwarzschild infaller
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

For smooth radial infall through a future <Schwarzschild event horizon>, outgoing retarded time obeys $u\sim-4M\log(\tau_H-\tau)$. A distant observer sees the <redshift> grow as $e^{t_{\rm rec}/(4M)}$. Its exponent in emission <Schwarzschild time> is instead $1/(2M)$, because $t_{\rm rec}\sim2t_{\rm em}$. At finite fixed observer radius the wavelength ratio includes $\sqrt{1-2M/r_B}$.

= Schwarzschild time
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}
{title2=$t$}

<Schwarzschild time> $t$ is the exterior time coordinate normalized to the <proper time> of static observers at infinity. At fixed exterior radius, $d\tau=\sqrt{1-2M/r}\,dt$. A received signal and its emission occur at different values of this coordinate.

= Kruskal spacetime
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}

The <Kruskal spacetime> is the maximal analytic extension of the positive-mass vacuum <Schwarzschild spacetime>. It contains two exterior regions, a <black hole>, and a <white hole>. <Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates> remove both horizon coordinate singularities, while $r=0$ remains a <curvature singularity>.

= Non-traversability of the Schwarzschild bridge
{parent=Kruskal spacetime}

In <Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates>, every future-directed radial causal curve has nondecreasing $U$ and $V$. The right exterior has $U<0,V>0$, while the left has $U>0,V<0$. No future-directed causal curve can travel between the exteriors because it would have to decrease one null coordinate. The spatial bridge on a time-symmetric slice is therefore not a traversable passage.

= Kruskal diagram
{parent=Kruskal spacetime}
{c}
{title2=$T_K=(V_K+U_K)/2,\quad X_K=(V_K-U_K)/2$}

This radial spacetime diagram uses <Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates> to display both exteriors, the black hole and the white hole of maximally extended <Schwarzschild spacetime>. Each point represents a symmetry two-sphere. Radial <null geodesics> have slopes $\pm1$ in the displayed coordinates; the horizons are $T_K=\pm X_K$ and the singularities satisfy $T_K^2-X_K^2=1$ in the usual normalization. A further compactification gives the corresponding <Penrose diagram>.

= Radial timelike incompleteness in Kruskal spacetime
{parent=Kruskal spacetime}

Every maximal radial <timelike geodesic> in positive-mass <Kruskal spacetime> is incomplete in at least one direction. Its equations are $\dot r^2=E^2-1+2M/r$ and $\ddot r=-M/r^2$. A finite turning point is a maximum, and each trajectory reaches $r=0$ at one or both ends. The remaining <proper time> is finite because $d\tau\sim\sqrt{r/(2M)}\,dr$. Complete circular timelike geodesics exist, but they have nonzero <angular momentum>.

= Negative heat capacity of a Schwarzschild black hole
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$C=-8\pi M^2$}

In units $G=\hbar=c=k_B=1$, a <Schwarzschild black hole> has <Hawking temperature> $T=1/(8\pi M)$, so its <heat capacity> is $dM/dT=-8\pi M^2$. It heats up as it loses <mass>, preventing stable canonical equilibrium with an unlimited thermal reservoir.

= Ultrarelativistic limit of the boosted Schwarzschild metric away from the null plane
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

Boost a Schwarzschild mass with speed $v$ and hold $p=m\gamma$ fixed as $v\to1$. Away from the null plane $x=t$, the isotropic-coordinate expression tends to
$$
ds^2=ds_{\rm Minkowski}^2+\frac{4p}{|x-t|}(dt-dx)^2.
$$
The complete distributional limit on the null plane requires the additional coordinate treatment that produces the Aichelburg-Sexl shock wave.

= Kottler spacetime
{c}
{synonym}

The Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime is the spherically symmetric vacuum solution with positive <cosmological constant>. In static coordinates its metric function is $f(r)=1-2m/r-H^2r^2$, with a black-hole horizon and a cosmological horizon when $mH<1/\sqrt{27}$.

= Radial geodesic acceleration in Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime
{parent=Schwarzschild-de Sitter spacetime}
{title2=$d^2r/d\tau^2=-GM/r^2+\Lambda r/3$}

For $ds^2=A\,dt^2-dr^2/A-r^2d\Omega^2$, $A=1-2GM/r-\Lambda r^2/3$, and $c=1$, a radial timelike <geodesic> has $\dot r^2=k^2-A$. The radial <geodesic equation> gives $\ddot r=-A'/2=-GM/r^2+\Lambda r/3$, including at a <turning point>. This is a second <derivative> of the <areal radius> with respect to <proper time>; the <geodesic>'s <four-acceleration> still vanishes. In the slow-motion weak-field limit, restoring units gives $d^2r/dt^2=-GM/r^2+\Lambda c^2r/3$.

= Geodetic effect

= Geodetic precession
{synonym}

Geodetic precession is the rotation of a parallel-transported gyroscope relative to the orbital radial direction in curved spacetime. For a circular Schwarzschild orbit its exact precession per revolution is $2\pi[1-\sqrt{1-3M/R}]$.

= Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{wiki}

Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates write the Schwarzschild metric as
$$
ds^2=-dt^2+\left(dr+\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,dt\right)^2+r^2d\Omega^2.
$$
The constant-$t$ spatial slices are flat, and the coordinates remain regular at the <Schwarzschild radius>.

= Domain of the ingoing Painleve-Gullstrand chart
{parent=Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates}
{title2=$V>0,\quad UV<1$}

Choose <Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates> with the right exterior at $U<0,V>0$ and $UV=(1-r/(2M))e^{r/(2M)}$. The ingoing <Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates> cover that exterior and the future black-hole region $U>0,V>0$, together with their common future horizon. They exclude the other exterior, the white hole, the bifurcation sphere and the singularity. Indeed $\tau=v+F(r)-r_*(r)$, with $v=4M\log V$ and $F'=\sqrt{2M/r}/f$; the difference $F-r_*$ is finite at $r=2M$, whereas $V=0$ requires infinite coordinate time.

= Free-fall proper time in Schwarzschild spacetime
{parent=Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates}
{title2=$d\tau=dt+\frac{\sqrt{2M/r}}{1-2M/r}dr$}

The inward radial <geodesic congruence> with unit <Killing energy> has $U=f^{-1}\partial_t-\sqrt{2M/r}\,\partial_r$, where $f=1-2M/r$. Its metric dual is $U^\flat=-d\tau$, so $\tau$ is a synchronized <proper time> for the congruence. Locally $\tau=t+2\sqrt{2Mr}+2M\log| (\sqrt r-\sqrt{2M})/(\sqrt r+\sqrt{2M}) |$. This yields <Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates> with flat spatial slices and a regular future horizon.

= Schwarzschild horizon-to-singularity proper time for fall from infinity
{parent=Free-fall proper time in Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}
{title2=$\Delta\tau=4M/3$}

A radial observer released from rest at infinity has $dr/d\tau=-\sqrt{2M/r}$. Integration from $r=2M$ to $r=0$ gives $\Delta\tau=\int_0^{2M}\sqrt{r/(2M)}dr=4M/3$ in geometric units. In ordinary units it is $4GM/(3c^3)$. The endpoint is the curvature singularity, not a regular spacetime centre; this value belongs to that specified energy, not every possible infalling worldline.

= Orthonormal coframe in Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates
{parent=Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates}

An orthonormal coframe is
$$
e^0=dt,
\qquad e^1=dr+\sqrt{\frac{2M}{r}}\,dt,
\qquad e^2=r\,d\theta,
\qquad e^3=r\sin\theta\,d\phi.
$$

= Radial Weyl curvature of Schwarzschild spacetime
{title2=$C_{trtr}=-2M/r^3$}
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

With the curvature convention
$$
R^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
=\partial_\gamma\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\delta}
-\partial_\delta\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}+\cdots,
$$
the vacuum Schwarzschild metric satisfies
$$
C_{trtr}=R_{trtr}=-\frac{2M}{r^3}.
$$
The opposite Riemann-sign convention reverses this sign.

= Schwarzschild tidal force
{parent=Radial Weyl curvature of Schwarzschild spacetime}

The orthonormal-frame components of Schwarzschild curvature scale as $M/r^3$. The <geodesic deviation> equation therefore gives radial stretching and transverse compression whose magnitude diverges as $r\to0$.

= Tidal gravity and the equivalence principle
{parent=Radial Weyl curvature of Schwarzschild spacetime}

Weyl curvature produces relative acceleration between neighbouring freely falling trajectories and therefore tidal deformation. A freely falling frame removes gravity at one event, as required by the equivalence principle, but cannot remove curvature over an extended region.

= Equatorial null orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

For inverse radius $y=1/r$ and azimuth $\phi$, a Schwarzschild null geodesic obeys
$$
(y')^2+y^2-2My^3=\frac1{b^2},
\qquad
y''+y=3My^2,
$$
where $b=L/E$ is the <impact parameter>.

= Finite-azimuth Schwarzschild photon plunge
{parent=Equatorial null orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}
{title2=$r\sim\tfrac\mu2(\phi_*-\phi)^2$}

For a captured nonradial <null geodesic> with $u=1/r$, the large-$u$ orbit equation is $(du/d\phi)^2\sim2\mu u^3$. Integration on the inward branch gives
$$
\phi_*-\phi\sim\sqrt{2/(\mu u)},\qquad
r\sim\frac\mu2(\phi_*-\phi)^2.
$$
The <curvature singularity> is reached at finite azimuth. This does not prevent a near-critical ray from winding repeatedly near the <photon sphere> before its final plunge. A radial ray has constant azimuth, so this asymptotic coordinate parametrization is inapplicable to it.

= Affine-time scaling of a nonradial Schwarzschild photon plunge
{parent=Finite-azimuth Schwarzschild photon plunge}
{c}
{title2=$r\propto(\lambda_*-\lambda)^{2/5}$}

For nonzero angular constant $h$, the interior null radial equation gives $\dot r^2\sim2\mu h^2/r^3$. Its inward branch integrates to $r^{5/2}\sim\tfrac52\sqrt{2\mu}|h|(\lambda_*-\lambda)$. Thus the singularity is at finite <affine parameter> and $r\propto(\lambda_*-\lambda)^{2/5}$. The zero-angular-momentum case has a different affine scaling.

= Null effective potential in Schwarzschild spacetime
{parent=Equatorial null orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$V_{\rm eff}=\frac{h^2}{2r^2}(1-2\mu/r),\quad\mu=GM/c^2$}

For an equatorial <null geodesic> in <Schwarzschild spacetime>, let $k=(1-2\mu/r)\,dt/d\lambda$ and $h=r^2d\phi/d\lambda$ be the conserved quantities for an <affine parameter> $\lambda$. The null <metric> condition gives $\dot r^2/2+V_{\rm eff}=c^2k^2/2$. For $h\ne0$, the <effective potential> is zero at the <Schwarzschild event horizon>, positive outside, and tends to zero at infinity. Its unique exterior maximum is at $r=3\mu$, with value $h^2/(54\mu^2)$ and second <derivative> $-h^2/(81\mu^4)$. This maximum gives the unstable <photon sphere>.

The <impact parameter> of an incoming ray is $b=|h|/(ck)$ for $k>0$. Capture requires its radial energy to exceed the maximum, namely $b<3\sqrt3\mu$. At equality the incoming ray approaches the circular orbit asymptotically; it does not cross the horizon. For $h=0$, the potential is identically zero and the ingoing radial ray is captured.

= Critical photon orbits of Schwarzschild spacetime
{parent=Equatorial null orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime}
{title2=$u-\tfrac13=-4Ae^\phi/(1+2Ae^\phi)^2$}

At the critical <impact parameter> $b=3\sqrt3M$, the null orbit equation factors as $(u')^2=(u-1/3)^2(2u+1/3)$ for $u=M/r$. One oriented family has the displayed solution. As $\phi\to-\infty$, positive $A$ approaches the <photon sphere> from larger radii and negative $A$ from smaller radii; $A=0$ is the circular orbit. The physical domain must retain $r>0$ and the positive square root in its implicit parametrization.

= Circular null geodesic
{parent=Equatorial null orbit in Schwarzschild spacetime}

A circular null geodesic remains at constant areal radius. It lies at a stationary point of the null radial effective potential and may be stable or unstable according to the sign of the second derivative there.

= Photon sphere
{parent=Circular null geodesic}
{wiki}

A photon sphere is a spherical set of circular <null geodesics>. In <Schwarzschild spacetime> it occurs at $r=3M$ and is unstable.

= Schwarzschild photon escape cone
{c}
{parent=Photon sphere}
{title2=$\alpha_{\rm crit}=\arcsin\left[\frac{3\sqrt3\mu}{r}\sqrt{1-\frac{2\mu}{r}}\right]$}

At $2\mu<r\le3\mu$, measure the emission <angle> $\alpha$ from the outward radial direction in a static observer's <orthonormal tetrad>. Local <photon> energy is $E_{\rm loc}=E_\infty/\sqrt{1-2\mu/r}$, and its tangential <momentum> is $E_{\rm loc}\sin\alpha/c$. Thus its <impact parameter> is $b=r\sin\alpha/\sqrt{1-2\mu/r}$. Combining this with the <null effective potential in Schwarzschild spacetime> gives escape precisely for outward directions $0\le\alpha<\alpha_{\rm crit}$; the limiting ray approaches the <photon sphere> and does not escape.

As $r\downarrow2\mu$ the cone shrinks to zero opening. A static observer does not exist on the horizon itself, and no future-directed ray starting on the <Schwarzschild event horizon> reaches exterior infinity. At $r=3\mu$, the critical half-angle is $\pi/2$; the exactly tangential critical ray stays on the unstable circular orbit. Escape cones for a moving observer are changed by <relativistic aberration>.

= Photon capture cross-section of a Reissner-Nordstrom black hole
{parent=Photon sphere}
{title2=$\sigma=2\pi r_{\mathrm{ph}}^3/(r_{\mathrm{ph}}-M)$}

For a <Reissner-Nordstrom black hole> with $|Q|\le M$, the outer unstable <circular null geodesic> has radius $r_{\mathrm{ph}}=(3M+\sqrt{9M^2-8Q^2})/2$. Maximizing $f/r^2$ for $f=1-2M/r+Q^2/r^2$ gives the critical <impact parameter> $b_c^2=r_{\mathrm{ph}}^2/f(r_{\mathrm{ph}})=2r_{\mathrm{ph}}^3/(r_{\mathrm{ph}}-M)$. The <geometrical optics> capture cross-section is $\pi b_c^2$, reducing to $27\pi M^2$ in <Schwarzschild spacetime> and $16\pi M^2$ in the extremal charged case. Finite-wavelength absorption need not equal this geometric limit.

= Schwarzschild radius
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}
{wiki}

The Schwarzschild radius is $r_s=2GM/c^2$, or $2M$ in geometrized units.

= Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}

The tortoise coordinate satisfies
$$
\frac{dr_*}{dr}=\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1},
\qquad
r_*=r+2M\log\left|\frac{r}{2M}-1\right|.
$$

= Outgoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
{parent=Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate}
{title2=$u=t-r_*$}

The retarded coordinate $u=t-r_*$ puts the radial <Schwarzschild metric> into $ds^2=-f\,du^2-2\,du\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, with $f=1-2M/r$. Outgoing radial <null geodesics> have constant $u$. These coordinates are regular at the past horizon; an infaller crossing the future horizon has $u\to+\infty$.

= Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
{parent=Schwarzschild tortoise coordinate}

The advanced coordinate $v=t+r_*$ puts the radial Schwarzschild metric into the form
$$
ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right)dv^2+2\,dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2,
$$
which is regular at the future horizon.

These are the advanced, ingoing member of the <Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates>.

= Finkelstein diagram
{parent=Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates}
{c}

A <Finkelstein diagram> plots <areal radius> $r$ against $T=v-r$ in <ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates>. Its radial <null directions> show explicitly that both future light-ray families decrease $r$ inside a <Schwarzschild black hole>. It is not a compactified <Penrose diagram>.

= Radial null trajectories in ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates
{parent=Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates}

Radial null curves comprise the ingoing family $v=\text{constant}$ and the outgoing family
$$
\frac{dr}{dv}=\frac12\left(1-\frac{2M}{r}\right).
$$
Outside the horizon the outgoing family moves to larger $r$; inside it both future-directed families move to smaller $r$.

= Schwarzschild event horizon
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{c}

The surface $r=2M$ is the future event horizon of Schwarzschild spacetime. Its singularity in Schwarzschild coordinates is removable, whereas $r=0$ is a curvature singularity.

= Schwarzschild horizon collision energy
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild event horizon}
{title2=$E_{\rm com}^2=m^2[4+(h_1-h_2)^2/(4M^2)]$}

For two equal-mass inward <timelike geodesics> of unit specific <Killing energy>, their invariant <center-of-mass energy> has this finite horizon limit. If both are captured from infinity, <Schwarzschild marginally bound capture> gives $E_{\rm com}<m\sqrt{20}$, with $m\sqrt{20}$ a supremum. No such universal bound follows for particles prepared inside the angular-momentum barrier.

= Schwarzschild singularity
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

The Schwarzschild singularity is the spacelike curvature singularity at $r=0$. Curvature invariants and orthonormal tidal components diverge there.

= Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}
{wiki}

Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates extend the Schwarzschild solution smoothly through its future and past horizons. Constant Schwarzschild radius curves are hyperbolae, while the horizons are null lines through the bifurcation sphere.

= Kruskal extension across a simple static horizon
{c}
{parent=Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates}
{title2=$r_*=(2\kappa)^{-1}\log|r-r_H|+h(r)$}

For $ds^2=-fdt^2+dr^2/f+R^2d\Omega^2$, suppose $f$ and $R$ are analytic at $r_H$, $f(r_H)=0$, $f'(r_H)=2\kappa>0$ and $R(r_H)>0$. The <tortoise coordinate> obeys the displayed expansion with analytic $h$. For $U=-e^{-\kappa(t-r_*)}$ and $V=e^{\kappa(t+r_*)}$ in the right exterior, $-UV=(r-r_H)e^{2\kappa h(r)}$ after fixing an additive constant. The inverse function theorem makes $r$ analytic in $UV$ through zero, while $ds^2=f\,dU\,dV/(\kappa^2UV)+R^2d\Omega^2$ has a finite nonzero radial coefficient there. This supplies the local analytic extension and its <bifurcate Killing horizon>.

= Schwarzschild geodesic from the bifurcation sphere to the singularity
{parent=Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates}

A radial geodesic orthogonal to the time-symmetric Kruskal slice starts at $r=2M$ with zero Schwarzschild energy and reaches $r=0$ after proper time $\pi M$.

= Gravitational redshift between static Schwarzschild observers
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

For static observers at radii $r_A$ and $r_B$, equal Schwarzschild-coordinate time intervals correspond to proper times $d\tau=\sqrt{1-2M/r}\,dt$. Hence
$$
\frac{\Delta\tau_B}{\Delta\tau_A}
=\sqrt{\frac{1-2M/r_B}{1-2M/r_A}}.
$$

This static-observer relation is a special case of <gravitational redshift>, not a definition of every gravitational frequency shift.

= Proper acceleration of a static Schwarzschild observer
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

A static observer at Schwarzschild radius $r>2M$ must maintain the outward <proper acceleration>
$$
a=\frac{M}{r^2\sqrt{1-2M/r}}.
$$
It approaches the Newtonian value $M/r^2$ at large radius and diverges at the horizon.

= Birkhoff theorem
{c}
{synonym}

Every spherically symmetric vacuum solution of the Einstein field equation is locally a portion of Schwarzschild spacetime. In particular, a spherically symmetric body's exterior field depends only on its total mass.

= Redshifted gravitational mass of a static thin shell
{parent=Birkhoff's theorem}

A thin spherical shell of proper surface mass density $\rho$ held at Schwarzschild radius $r_{\rm st}$ around mass $M$ contributes, to first order in its mass,
$$
\Delta M_\infty
=4\pi r_{\rm st}^2\rho\sqrt{1-\frac{2M}{r_{\rm st}}}
$$
to the exterior Schwarzschild mass parameter.

= Schwarzschild light deflection
{c}
{parent=Schwarzschild spacetime}

A null Schwarzschild geodesic with $b\gg r_s$ is deflected through $2r_s/b=4GM/(bc^2)$ to first order.

This is the leading spherical-mass deflection underlying a <gravitational lens>.

= Eddington experiment
{parent=Schwarzschild light deflection}
{c}
{wiki}

The 1919 <solar-eclipse> expeditions organized by <Frank Watson Dyson> and <Arthur Eddington> compared background-star positions near the obscured <Sun> with reference photographs. The optical train used a slowly rotating flat mirror to send the light into a horizontal photographic telescope. A smaller telescope at Sobral provided a separate set of plates. Focus and plate-scale changes had to be distinguished from the radial pattern predicted by <Schwarzschild light deflection>. The experiment therefore tested a spatial pattern and its amplitude, not merely whether a star image had moved.<A determination of the deflection of light by the Sun's gravitational field by Frank Watson Dyson and others>[1920 report of the 1919 observations]{ref} https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1283[Equipment taken and the Sobral expedition: horizontal telescopes, tracking mirrors and reference plates]{ref}

= Perturbative derivation of Schwarzschild light deflection
{parent=Schwarzschild light deflection}

Writing $y=b^{-1}\sin\phi+\Delta y$ in $y''+y=3My^2$ and retaining first order in $M$ gives
$$
\Delta y''+\Delta y=\frac{3M}{2b^2}(1-\cos2\phi).
$$
The displacement of the outgoing zero is $4M/b$.
