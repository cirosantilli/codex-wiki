Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}R$ be an <affine scheme>, and let $(U_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ be an <open cover>. Write each complement as a <Zariski closed> subset $X\setminus U_\lambda=V(I_\lambda)$ for an <ideal> $I_\lambda\subseteq R$. The intersection rule for <Zariski closed> subsets gives
$$
\varnothing=\bigcap_{\lambda\in\Lambda}V(I_\lambda)
=V\!\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}I_\lambda\right).
$$
A proper <ideal> lies in a <maximal ideal>, which is a <prime ideal>; consequently $V(J)=\varnothing$ forces $J=R$. Thus $1\in\sum_\lambda I_\lambda$. By the definition of a sum of <ideals>, this membership is witnessed by finitely many elements:
$$
1=a_1+\cdots+a_r,\qquad a_j\in I_{\lambda_j}.
$$
Hence $\sum_{j=1}^r I_{\lambda_j}=R$, so $\bigcap_{j=1}^r V(I_{\lambda_j})=\varnothing$, and therefore $X=\bigcup_{j=1}^r U_{\lambda_j}$. If $R$ is the <zero ring>, $X$ is empty and the empty subcover suffices. This proves
$$
\boxed{\text{Every affine scheme is quasi-compact}.}
$$
The proof uses only complements, the intersection rule for <Zariski closed> subsets, and the finite expression of an element in a sum of <ideals>.

**The quotient.** Evaluation $\mathbb Z[t]\to\mathbb Z[i]$, $t\mapsto i$, is a surjective <ring homomorphism> whose <kernel of a ring homomorphism> is $(t^2+1)$. Indeed, division by this <monic polynomial> leaves a remainder $a+bt$, and $a+bi=0$ for $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ forces $a=b=0$. Taking the further <quotient ring> modulo $p$ gives the explicit <isomorphism>
$$
\boxed{\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^2+1)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathbb Z[i]/(p),\qquad \overline t\longmapsto\overline i.}
$$

**All fibers, including the generic fiber.** For a point $s\in\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ with <residue field> $\kappa(s)$, its <scheme-theoretic fiber> is
$$
X_s=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(\mathbb Z[i]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\kappa(s)\bigr).
$$
At the <generic point> $s=(0)$, the <residue field> is $\mathbb Q$, and $t^2+1$ is an <irreducible polynomial> over $\mathbb Q$, so $X_{(0)}=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q(i)$. For a <closed point> $(p)$ the <residue field> is $\mathbb F_p$. The permitted criterion for a square root of $-1$ gives three cases. If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, choose $a\in\mathbb F_p$ with $a^2=-1$; the two roots $a,-a$ are distinct, so the <Chinese remainder theorem> gives $\mathbb F_p[t]/(t^2+1)\cong\mathbb F_p\times\mathbb F_p$. If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, the quadratic has no root and is an <irreducible polynomial>, giving the <finite field> $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. Finally, in <characteristic> two, $t^2+1=(t+1)^2$; setting $\epsilon=t+1$ gives the <dual numbers>. Thus
$$
\boxed{X_s\cong\begin{cases}
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q(i),&s=(0),\\
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p\ \amalg\ \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_p,&s=(p),\ p\equiv1\pmod4,\\
\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb F_{p^2},&s=(p),\ p\equiv3\pmod4,\\
\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(\mathbb F_2[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)\bigr),&s=(2).
\end{cases}}
$$
The split <scheme-theoretic fiber> has two <closed points>, represented by $(p,i-a)$ and $(p,i+a)$. The inert <scheme-theoretic fiber> has one <closed point>, represented by $(p)$. The <scheme-theoretic fiber> over $(2)$ has one <closed point>, represented by $(1+i)$, but is a <nonreduced scheme>: its nonzero element $\epsilon$ is <nilpotent>. These descriptions retain the <residue fields> and the <nilpotent> structure, which a count of points alone would lose.

**The topology.** The printed PDF says that the nonempty proper <Zariski closed> subsets are finite subsets of $\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{0\}$. Its points are <prime ideals>, so the intended set is $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[i]\setminus\{(0)\}$.

Put $R=\mathbb Z[i]$. Since $R$ is a <principal ideal domain>, every nonzero <prime ideal> is a <maximal ideal>. Each such <prime ideal> contains a nonzero <Gaussian integer> $z$, and therefore contains the positive integer $z\overline z$. Its contraction to $\mathbb Z$ is consequently a nonzero <prime ideal> $(p)$, so the <scheme-theoretic fibers> just computed list all the <closed points> of $X$. The sole remaining point is the <generic point> $(0)$.

Every proper <Zariski closed> subset is $V((a))$ with $a\ne0$: if its defining <ideal> were zero, it would be all of $X$. A nonzero nonunit $a$ has a finite <unique factorization>
$$
a=u\pi_1^{e_1}\cdots\pi_r^{e_r},\qquad
V((a))=\{(\pi_1),\ldots,(\pi_r)\}.
$$
Here the $\pi_j$ are pairwise nonassociate <prime elements>. Conversely, any finite set of nonzero <prime ideals> $\{(\pi_1),\ldots,(\pi_r)\}$ equals $V((\pi_1\cdots\pi_r))$; the empty set is $V((1))$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\begin{gathered}
X=\{(0)\}\ \cup\ \{\text{nonzero prime ideals of }\mathbb Z[i]\},\\
\text{proper closed subsets of }X=\text{finite subsets of its closed points}.
\end{gathered}}
$$
In particular, the closure of the <generic point> is $X$, and every nonempty <open subset> contains that <generic point> and all but finitely many <closed points>.

**A nonidentity automorphism.** <Complex conjugation>
$$
c:R\longrightarrow R,\qquad a+bi\longmapsto a-bi,
$$
is a <ring automorphism> fixing $\mathbb Z$. It induces the <automorphism> $\sigma=\operatorname{Spec}(c)$ over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$. Since $c(i)=-i\ne i$, this is not the identity <morphism of schemes>. On the <generic fiber> it is the nontrivial <field automorphism> of $\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q$. On a split <scheme-theoretic fiber> it swaps the two <closed points>, since $i-a$ maps to $-(i+a)$. On an inert <scheme-theoretic fiber> it fixes the only point but induces the nonidentity <finite-field Frobenius automorphism> $z\mapsto z^p$ on its <residue field>, because $i^p=-i$. On the <scheme-theoretic fiber> over $(2)$ it is the identity on the entire <scheme>: $-i=i$ in $R/(2)$, so $\epsilon=i+1$ is fixed. Thus
$$
\boxed{\sigma:\quad\text{generic conjugation; split points exchanged; inert Frobenius; identity over }2.}
$$

**The immersions and gluing.** For $p>0$, the <prime ideals> of the <localization> $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ are $(0)$ and $(p)\mathbb Z_{(p)}$. Inverting $p$ gives $\mathbb Z_{(p)}[1/p]=\mathbb Q$, so
$$
\boxed{\psi_p:\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }D(p)\hookrightarrow U_p}
$$
is an <open immersion> sending the <generic point> to the <generic point>. For $p=0$, $\mathbb Z_{(0)}=\mathbb Q$, and take $\psi_0$ to be the identity. Identify each pair of these <open subschemes> using the identity of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Q$. The identities compose to the identity on every triple overlap, so <gluing of schemes along open subschemes> applies. No finiteness of the family is required.

The resulting <scheme> $Y$ has one common <generic point> $\eta$ and a distinct <closed point> $y_p$ for every positive <prime number> $p$. Its <affine open subschemes> $U_p=\{\eta,y_p\}$, for $p>0$, intersect pairwise in $U_0=\{\eta\}$. A subset $W\subseteq Y$ is open precisely when its intersection with every $U_p$ is open. In the two-point <topological space> $U_p$, the open subsets are $\varnothing$, $\{\eta\}$ and $U_p$. Consequently
$$
\boxed{\text{The nonempty opens of }Y\text{ are exactly }W_S=\{\eta\}\cup\{y_p:p\in S\},}
$$
where $S$ is any subset of the positive <prime numbers>, including the empty subset.

**The canonical morphism.** The <ring homomorphisms> $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ give <morphisms of schemes> $U_p\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ agreeing on the overlaps, hence a <morphism of schemes> $f:Y\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$. By uniqueness of the <structure morphism>, this is the required map. Contraction of <prime ideals> gives
$$
\boxed{f(\eta)=(0),\qquad f(y_p)=(p).}
$$
These are all points of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, each exactly once, so $f$ is a <bijection>. It is continuous because its restrictions to the <open cover> $(U_p)$ are continuous; explicitly, every nonempty <open subset> of $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ is the complement of a finite collection of <closed points>, whose inverse image is one of the $W_S$.

**Sections on every open.** For any $S$, the <sheaf gluing axiom> on the cover of $W_S$ by $U_0$ and the $U_p$ with $p\in S$ identifies
$$
\boxed{\Gamma(W_S,\mathcal O_Y)=\bigcap_{p\in S}\mathbb Z_{(p)}\ \subseteq\mathbb Q,}
$$
where the intersection over the empty set is $\mathbb Q$. Indeed, a section on $U_p$ is an element of $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$; restrictions to every overlap are its inclusion into $\mathbb Q$. Compatibility means that all these elements are the same rational number. In lowest terms $a/b$, this rational number lies in the intersection exactly when no <prime number> in $S$ divides $b$. Equivalently, this is the <integer localization at a set of primes> obtained by inverting all positive <prime numbers> outside $S$.

Now let $V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ be nonempty and open. Its finite complement consists of $(p_1),\ldots,(p_r)$; put $N=p_1\cdots p_r$, with $N=1$ for an empty complement. Then $V=D(N)$ and
$$
\Gamma(V,\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z})=\mathbb Z[1/N]
=\bigcap_{p\nmid N}\mathbb Z_{(p)}
=\Gamma(f^{-1}V,\mathcal O_Y).
$$
The equality follows by the denominator criterion above. The map $f^\#$ is this same equality of subrings of $\mathbb Q$, so it is a <ring isomorphism>. Over the empty <open subset> both rings of sections are the <zero ring>. These identifications respect every restriction map. Thus the precise comparison of <structure sheaves> is
$$
\boxed{\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }f_*\mathcal O_Y.}
$$
Here “over every open set” refers to an <open subset> $V$ of the target and its inverse image. The preceding intersection formula additionally describes the sections on every <open subset> of $Y$, including ones whose images are not open in the target.

**Topology and finiteness.** The singleton $\{\eta\}=U_0$ is open in $Y$, but $\{(0)\}$ is not open in $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$: a nonempty <open subset> there contains all but finitely many of the infinitely many <closed points>. Therefore $f$ is not a <homeomorphism>, despite being a continuous <bijection> and giving the indicated <isomorphism> of <structure sheaves> after <direct image>.

The <open cover> $(U_p)_{p>0}$ of $Y$ has no finite subcover, because $y_q$ belongs only to $U_q$. Thus $Y$ is not a <quasi-compact scheme>, and part (a) rules out an <affine scheme>. Each $\mathbb Z_{(p)}$ is a <Noetherian ring>, being a <localization> of the <Noetherian ring> $\mathbb Z$. Hence the $U_p$ form an <affine open cover> by spectra of <Noetherian rings>, proving that $Y$ is a <locally Noetherian scheme>. A <Noetherian scheme> is a <quasi-compact scheme>, so
$$
\boxed{Y\text{ is locally Noetherian, not quasi-compact, not affine, and not Noetherian}.}
$$

**Constructing the subsheaf.** On an <integral scheme>, every nonempty <open subset> is an <irreducible topological space> and hence a <connected space>. A <locally constant function> from such an <open subset> to $F$ is constant. Thus the <constant sheaf> $\underline F$ has sections $F$ on every nonempty <open subset>, and the <structure sheaf> embeds in it by restriction to the <generic point>. Empty members of the given <open cover> may be discarded.

For every <open subset> $W\subseteq X$, define
$$
\mathcal L(W)=\{s\in\underline F(W):\ f_i s|_{W\cap U_i}\in\mathcal O_X(W\cap U_i)\text{ for every }i\}.
$$
This is a subsheaf of the <sheaf of modules> $\underline F$: its condition is local, and compatible sections glue in $\underline F$, with the regularity conditions gluing in $\mathcal O_X$. On $U_i\cap U_j$, the hypothesis says that $f_j/f_i$ is a section of the <sheaf of units of the structure sheaf>. Therefore multiplication by it preserves $\mathcal O_X$, and
$$
f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_X|_{U_i\cap U_j}=f_j^{-1}\mathcal O_X|_{U_i\cap U_j}.
$$
It follows that $\mathcal L|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}$: the condition with index $i$ implies every other condition on an overlap, and the converse is part of the definition. Multiplication by $1/f_i$ is an <isomorphism> $\mathcal O_{U_i}\to\mathcal L|_{U_i}$, with inverse multiplication by $f_i$. Hence $\mathcal L$ is an <invertible sheaf>, or equivalently a <line bundle>, and
$$
\boxed{\mathcal L(U_i)=\mathcal O_X(U_i)\cdot\frac1{f_i}\subseteq F.}
$$
These local descriptions also give uniqueness as a subsheaf of $\underline F$. This construction is the <divisor line bundle> for the local rational equations $f_i$.

**The tensor product.** Refine the two <open covers> to $W_{ij}=U_i\cap V_j$. The ratios
$$
\frac{f_i g_j}{f_{i'}g_{j'}}=\frac{f_i}{f_{i'}}\frac{g_j}{g_{j'}}
$$
are sections of the <sheaf of units of the structure sheaf> on the corresponding overlaps, so the products give a <line bundle> $\mathcal M\subseteq\underline F$ by the construction just proved. Multiplication in $F$ defines a <sheaf morphism>
$$
\mathcal L\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal L'\longrightarrow\mathcal M.
$$
On each $W_{ij}$ it sends the local generator $(1/f_i)\otimes(1/g_j)$ to the local generator $1/(f_i g_j)$. It is thus an <isomorphism> on an <open cover>, and hence an <isomorphism> globally. In particular,
$$
\boxed{(U_i\cap V_j,f_i g_j)\text{ represents }\mathcal L\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal L'.}
$$

**The sign for projective twists.** Write <homogeneous coordinates> $[x_0:\cdots:x_n]$ on <projective space> $\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb Z}$, and let $U_i=D_+(x_i)$ be its standard <affine open cover>. For any <integer> $m$, take the nonzero elements of the <function field>
$$
\boxed{f_i=\left(\frac{x_0}{x_i}\right)^m\in\mathbb Q(x_1/x_0,\ldots,x_n/x_0)^*,\qquad 0\leq i\leq n.}
$$
Their ratios are $f_i/f_j=(x_j/x_i)^m$, which are sections of the <sheaf of units of the structure sheaf> on $U_i\cap U_j$, including when $m<0$. The <twisting sheaf on projective space> $\mathcal O(m)$ has local generator $x_i^m$ on $U_i$. Divide a rational section of $\mathcal O(m)$ by $x_0^m$ to embed it in the <constant sheaf> of the <function field>; the local generator becomes $(x_i/x_0)^m=1/f_i$. This identifies the constructed <line bundle> with $\mathcal O(m)$ and fixes the sign explicitly.

For $n\geq1$, $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb Z})\cong\mathbb Z$, with generator $[\mathcal O(1)]$; the displayed data gives all its powers, with $\mathcal O(m)\otimes\mathcal O(r)\cong\mathcal O(m+r)$ by the product rule above. If $n=0$, <projective space> is $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, its <Picard group> is zero, and the same formula gives only the trivial <line bundle>.

Discard empty members of the <open cover>: every <Čech cochain group> factor supported on an empty intersection is the zero <abelian group>, so this changes no <Čech cohomology>. Every remaining member is the one-point space $X$; the index set is nonempty even if the same <open subset> is listed repeatedly. Choose a <well-order> with least index $\alpha$. Use the <ordered Čech cochain complex> permitted in the question:
$$
C^q=\prod_{i_0<\cdots<i_q} A,\qquad
(\delta s)_{i_0\cdots i_{q+1}}=\sum_{r=0}^{q+1}(-1)^r s_{i_0\cdots\widehat{i_r}\cdots i_{q+1}}.
$$
All restriction maps are identities. For $q\geq1$ define a <cochain homotopy> $h_q:C^q\to C^{q-1}$ by
$$
(h_qs)_{i_0\cdots i_{q-1}}=
\begin{cases}
0,&i_0=\alpha,\\
s_{\alpha i_0\cdots i_{q-1}},&i_0>\alpha.
\end{cases}
$$
We verify $\delta h_q+h_{q+1}\delta=\operatorname{id}_{C^q}$ for every $q\geq1$. Fix $I=(i_0<\cdots<i_q)$. If $i_0=\alpha$, then $(h_{q+1}\delta s)_I=0$. In $(\delta h_qs)_I$, the term deleting $i_0$ is $s_I$ and every other term is zero because its retained first index is $\alpha$. Thus the identity holds on $I$.

If $i_0>\alpha$, then
$$
(\delta h_qs)_I=\sum_{r=0}^q(-1)^r s_{\alpha,I\setminus i_r},\qquad
(h_{q+1}\delta s)_I=(\delta s)_{\alpha,I}
=s_I-\sum_{r=0}^q(-1)^r s_{\alpha,I\setminus i_r}.
$$
Adding proves the identity in this case also. Each formula uses only finitely many terms on each component, so it remains valid for an infinite <open cover> and arbitrary <abelian group> $A$.

If $s\in C^q$ is a <Čech cocycle>, then $\delta s=0$, and the identity gives $s=\delta(h_qs)$: every <Čech cocycle> in positive degree is a <Čech coboundary>. Therefore, using the permitted comparison with ordered <Čech cohomology>,
$$
\boxed{\check H^q_{\mathcal U}(X,\underline A)=0\qquad(q\geq1).}
$$
The identity is asserted in positive degrees; degree zero has $H^0\cong A$, so an unaugmented contraction to zero in every degree would be incorrect.

In an <irreducible topological space>, any two nonempty <open subsets> intersect. Let $W\subseteq X$ be a nonempty <open subset>. Two nonempty subsets open in $W$ are also open in $X$, and hence intersect; thus $W$ is itself an <irreducible topological space>. If $W$ were disconnected, a separation into two disjoint nonempty <open subsets> would contradict this intersection property. Hence $W$ is a <connected space>. The empty <open subset> is connected as well under the usual convention.

Discard empty members of $\mathcal U$ as in (b). Every finite intersection $U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_q}$ is nonempty by repeated use of the intersection property and is an <open subset>, hence a <connected space>. A <locally constant function> on this intersection is constant. It follows that the <constant sheaf> has
$$
\underline A(U_{i_0}\cap\cdots\cap U_{i_q})\cong A,
$$
and every restriction map between these nonempty intersections is the identity on $A$. The whole <Čech cochain complex> is therefore the same as for the indexed <open cover> of a point having the same index set. The <cochain homotopy> constructed in (b) applies component by component and proves
$$
\boxed{\text{Every open subset of }X\text{ is connected},\qquad
\check H^q_{\mathcal U}(X,\underline A)=0\quad(q\geq1).}
$$
The argument concerns the <constant sheaf> of <locally constant functions>; arbitrary coefficient sheaves on an <irreducible topological space> need not have the same <Čech cohomology>.

Choose an <affine open subscheme> $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq Y$ containing $y$. Since $f$ is an <affine morphism>, $f^{-1}V=\operatorname{Spec}B$; by hypothesis $B$ is a <finitely generated module> over $A$. If $\mathfrak m\subset A$ corresponds to the <closed point> $y$, then $\kappa(y)=A/\mathfrak m$. The <scheme-theoretic fiber> is
$$
X_y=X\times_Y\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y)
\cong\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(B\otimes_A\kappa(y)\bigr)
=\operatorname{Spec}(B/\mathfrak mB).
$$
If $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ generate the $A$-<module> $B$, their images span the <algebra> $B/\mathfrak mB$ as a <vector space> over $\kappa(y)$. It is therefore finite-dimensional. By the allowed fact about the <spectrum of a finite-dimensional commutative algebra>, it has only finitely many <prime ideals>. These are exactly the points of $X_y$, so
$$
\boxed{|f^{-1}(y)|=|\operatorname{Spec}(B/\mathfrak mB)|<\infty.}
$$
The points of the <scheme-theoretic fiber> correspond to the <prime ideals> of $B$ contracting to $\mathfrak m$, which explains the equality with the underlying set-theoretic <fiber>. An empty <fiber> is allowed. In fact, replacing $A/\mathfrak m$ by $\kappa(y)$ gives the same finite-dimensional argument for every point, without requiring it to be a <closed point>.

**Failure in general.** Let $f:\mathbb A^1_k=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ be the <structure morphism> and take the trivial <line bundle> $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}$. The <polynomial ring> $k[t]$ is a <Noetherian ring>, so this is a <coherent sheaf>. Its <direct image> on the one-point target has sections
$$
\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}k,f_*\mathcal L)=k[t].
$$
A <coherent sheaf> on $\operatorname{Spec}k$ corresponds to a finite-dimensional <vector space> over $k$, whereas $1,t,t^2,\ldots$ are <linearly independent>. Thus
$$
\boxed{f_*\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}\text{ is not coherent}.}
$$

**Quasi-coherence for the stated morphism.** Now let $f$ satisfy (a), and let $\mathcal F$ be a <quasi-coherent sheaf> on $X$. Over any <affine open subscheme> $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $Y$, write $f^{-1}V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ and $\mathcal F|_{f^{-1}V}=\widetilde M$ for a $B$-<module> $M$. Regard $M$ as an $A$-<module> through the <ring homomorphism> $A\to B$. For $a\in A$,
$$
\Gamma(D(a),f_*\mathcal F)
=\Gamma\bigl(D(\varphi(a)),\widetilde M\bigr)
=M_{\varphi(a)}=M_a.
$$
The last two <localizations> are identical: both invert the action of the same element on $M$. These identifications commute with restrictions, so on the basis of <principal open subsets> they identify $(f_*\mathcal F)|_V$ with the <affine module sheaf> $\widetilde{{}_AM}$. Hence this <direct image> is a <quasi-coherent sheaf> on each $V$, and therefore on $Y$. This part uses only that $f$ is an <affine morphism>.

**Coherence under the Noetherian hypotheses.** If $X,Y$ are <Noetherian schemes> and $\mathcal F$ is a <coherent sheaf>, then $M$ is a <finitely generated module> over $B$. Choose generators $m_1,\ldots,m_s$ of $M$ over $B$, and generators $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ of $B$ over $A$, supplied by (a). Then the finite family
$$
\{b_jm_\ell:1\leq j\leq r,\ 1\leq\ell\leq s\}
$$
generates $M$ as an $A$-<module>: expand each coefficient of an expression $m=\sum_\ell c_\ell m_\ell$ in the $b_j$. Since $A$ is a <Noetherian ring>, the <affine module sheaf> $\widetilde{{}_AM}$ is a <coherent sheaf>. This holds on an <affine open cover> of $Y$, proving
$$
\boxed{f_*:\operatorname{QCoh}(X)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(Y),\qquad
f_*:\operatorname{Coh}(X)\to\operatorname{Coh}(Y)\text{ if }X,Y\text{ are Noetherian}.}
$$

**The exact sequence and associated modules.** Put $A=k[x]$ and $\mathfrak m=(x)$. Evaluation at zero gives the <short exact sequence> of $A$-<modules>
$$
0\longrightarrow(x)\xrightarrow{\ j\ }A\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{ev}_0\ }A/(x)=k\longrightarrow0.
$$
The <affine module sheaf> associated to $(x)$ is the <ideal sheaf> $\mathcal J$ of the <closed point> $p$, and the one associated to $A$ is the <structure sheaf> $\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}$. To identify the third term, on a <principal open subset> $D(g)$,
$$
\Gamma(D(g),\widetilde k)=k_g
=\begin{cases}k,&g(0)\ne0,\\0,&g(0)=0,\end{cases}
$$
which equals $\Gamma(i^{-1}D(g),\mathcal O_p)$. The restrictions also agree, so $\widetilde k\cong i_*\mathcal O_p$. <Exactness of localization> proves that applying the <affine module sheaf> construction to the module sequence remains exact: on every <stalk> the sequence is its <localization>. Thus
$$
\boxed{0\longrightarrow\mathcal J\longrightarrow\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}\longrightarrow i_*\mathcal O_p\longrightarrow0.}
$$
Each term is an <affine module sheaf>, hence a <quasi-coherent sheaf>. In this example each is also a <coherent sheaf>, since its defining <module> is finitely generated over the <Noetherian ring> $k[x]$.

**The failure of left exactness.** For an <affine module sheaf> $\widetilde M$, the <pullback of a sheaf of modules> along $i$ corresponds to the <tensor product of modules> $k\otimes_A M$. In particular,
$$
i^*\mathcal J\ \leftrightarrow\ (x)\otimes_A A/(x)
\cong(x)/(x)^2\cong k,
\qquad
i^*\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}\ \leftrightarrow\ k.
$$
The first <vector space> is nonzero, with <basis> the class of $x$. But $i^*j$ sends this class to the class of $x$ in $A/(x)$, which is zero. Thus $i^*j$ is the zero map $k\to k$, and is not injective. More explicitly, the pulled-back sequence is
$$
0\longrightarrow k\xrightarrow{\ 0\ }k\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{id}\ }k\longrightarrow0,
$$
where exactness fails at its first copy of $k$. Thus $i^*$ is not a <left-exact functor>:
$$
\boxed{i^*\text{ is not a left-exact functor on sheaves of }\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^1_k}\text{-modules}.}
$$
Although $(x)$ is a <free module> over $A$ with one generator, its inclusion into $A$ becomes multiplication by $x$ after choosing its generator; the <tensor product of modules> with $A/(x)$ kills that map. This makes explicit why local freeness of $\mathcal J$ does not preserve this injection under the given <pullback>.
