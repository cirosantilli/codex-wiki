= Classical field theory
{wiki}

= Global symmetry in field theory
{parent=Classical field theory}

A global symmetry has transformation parameters constant across spacetime and preserves the action. It can relate physically distinct states. Continuous global symmetries give <Noether currents> and conserved charges when boundary fluxes vanish; discrete symmetries do not receive currents from the same infinitesimal theorem. A <gauge redundancy> instead uses local parameters to relate descriptions of the same physical state, except that nontrivial boundary transformations may carry physical charges. The <global phase symmetry of a complex scalar field> is an important internal example.

= Scattering charge selection rule from global gauge symmetry
{parent=Global symmetry in field theory}
{title2=$[Q,S]=0\ \Longrightarrow\ (Q_f-Q_i)\langle f|S|i\rangle=0$}

For a conserved charge $Q$ and invariant vacuum, its global symmetry commutes with the <S-matrix>. Matrix elements between charge eigenstates give the displayed selection rule. Constant electromagnetic phases cancel at every QED vertex, and nonzero physical amplitudes conserve total charge. Local <gauge invariance> strengthens this global rule to the <Ward-Takahashi identity>.

= Massive two-form field
{parent=Classical field theory}
{title2=$\partial^mF_{mnp}=\mu^2A_{np}$}

= Massive antisymmetric tensor field
{synonym}

An antisymmetric tensor $A_{mn}$ with $F=dA$ obeys $\partial^mF_{mnp}=\mu^2A_{np}$ for $\mu\ne0$. Taking a divergence yields $\partial^mA_{mn}=0$, and the remaining equation is the <Klein-Gordon equation> for each component. The physical <particle polarizations> form the <exterior square> of the massive <little group> vector representation, of dimension $(D-1)(D-2)/2$. Unlike a <two-form gauge field>, it has physical longitudinal polarizations.

= Light-cone decomposition of a massive two-form
{parent=Massive two-form field}
{title2=$A_{-i},\ A_{ij}$}

If $\partial_-$ is invertible, the divergence constraint of a <massive two-form field> makes $A_{-i}$ and $A_{ij}$ independent. It gives $A_{+-}=-\partial_-^{-1}\partial_iA_{-i}$ and $A_{+i}=\partial_-^{-1}(-\partial_+A_{-i}+\partial_jA_{ji})$. All independent components obey the <Klein-Gordon equation>. Their count is $(D-2)+\binom{D-2}{2}=\binom{D-1}{2}$.

= Hamiltonian density
{parent=Classical field theory}
{c}
{title2=$\mathcal H=\pi_a\dot\phi_a-\mathcal L$}

A <Hamiltonian density> is a local integrand for the field-theory <Hamiltonian>. For canonical fields with invertible velocity-momentum relation it is the <Legendre transform in mechanics> of the <Lagrangian density>, summing over all conjugate field components. <Gauge theories> require constraints and multiplier terms as well.

= Canonical Hamiltonian density of a real scalar field
{parent=Hamiltonian density}
{title2=$\mathcal H=\tfrac12\pi^2+\tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2+V(\phi)$}

For a <real scalar field> with <Lagrangian density> $\mathcal L=\dot\phi^2/2-|\nabla\phi|^2/2-V(\phi)$, the <canonical momentum> is $\pi=\dot\phi$. Its <Hamiltonian density> is the sum of momentum energy, gradient energy and potential energy. Hamilton's equations recover the <Euler-Lagrange equation>.

= Gauge theory
{parent=Classical field theory}
{wiki}

A <classical field theory> is a gauge theory when its description has a local redundancy represented by <gauge transformations>. A <gauge field> defines the <covariant derivative> that compares matter fields at different points, and its <gauge field strength> measures the curvature of that comparison. <Electromagnetism> is the familiar Abelian example; <Yang-Mills theory> generalizes it to a non-Abelian internal group. Both classical equations and their <quantum field theory> can have this structure. Physically equivalent gauge descriptions must give the same observables, even though the <gauge potential> changes.

= Grand unified theory
{parent=Gauge theory}

= GUT
{synonym}
{c}

A theory embedding the <Standard Model> gauge factors in a larger gauge group. Particular embeddings can relate couplings and matter multiplets or predict <proton decay>. The unification hypothesis alone does not specify a unique <mass> spectrum or an experimentally established extension.

= Pati-Salam model
{parent=Grand unified theory}
{c}
{title2=$SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$}

A quark-lepton unification framework with conventional minimal <gauge group> $SU(4)_C\times SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R$. <leptons> supply a fourth <color charge>, and a <right-handed neutrino> completes each family. <fermion> <masses> additionally depend on symmetry breaking and <Yukawa couplings>.

= Pati-Salam charge embedding
{parent=Pati-Salam model}
{c}
{title2=$Q=T_{3L}+T_{3R}+(B-L)/2$}

The charge relation combines left and right weak <isospin> with the generator distinguishing <quarks> from <leptons> inside the extended <color charge> group.

= Pati-Salam neutrino hypercharge cancellation
{parent=Pati-Salam charge embedding}
{c}

For the right-handed neutrino, $T_{3R}=1/2$ and $B-L=-1$, so $Y=T_{3R}+(B-L)/2=0$. Its colour-singlet and left-weak-singlet assignments make it sterile under the Standard Model after symmetry breaking.

= Chiral gauge theory
{parent=Gauge theory}

A <gauge theory> whose left- and right-chiral <fermions> transform differently under its <gauge group>. The <weak charged current> gives a physical example. A chiral representation assignment must satisfy the relevant <gauge anomaly> cancellation conditions to define a consistent quantum theory.

= Lattice gauge theory
{parent=Gauge theory}

A formulation of a <gauge theory> with discretized <spacetime>. Matter fields are assigned to sites and gauge-group elements to links between nearby sites. Products of link variables around closed paths provide gauge-invariant quantities. This structure preserves the local <gauge symmetry> while making nonperturbative numerical calculations possible. Physical predictions require control of finite spacing, finite volume and statistical errors rather than treating one finite grid as the continuum theory.

= Noether gauging procedure
{c}
{parent=Gauge theory}
{title2=$\delta S_0=\int j^\mu\partial_\mu\epsilon$}

= Noether gauging principle
{c}
{synonym}

Localizing a parameter of a global <symmetry> produces its <Noether current> times a derivative of that parameter. Introduce a <gauge field> with an inhomogeneous transformation and couple it to the <Noether current> to cancel this variation. Recompute the remaining variation and add the required interaction and transformation terms. Closure of the local <symmetry> algebra constrains the completion. For <supersymmetry>, its <supercurrent> couples to the <gravitino>, and the translation in the <supersymmetry> algebra requires a <vierbein>.

= Internal symmetry of a classical field theory
{parent=Classical field theory}

An internal symmetry changes field values while leaving spacetime coordinates fixed, preserving the <action>. A constant phase rotation of a <complex scalar field> is an example. This distinguishes internal charges from the spacetime translation charges in the <canonical stress-energy tensor>. Localizing the parameter generally requires gauge fields; a global internal symmetry is not automatically a gauge symmetry.

= O(4) symmetry of a complex scalar doublet
{parent=Internal symmetry of a classical field theory}
{c}

Two canonically normalized <complex scalar fields> with potential $V=m^2\Phi^\dagger\Phi+\lambda(\Phi^\dagger\Phi)^2$ are four real fields with a radial potential. Consequently the isolated action is invariant under the <orthogonal group> $O(4)$, not only under complex-linear $U(2)$ transformations. Writing $\phi_1=(q_1+iq_2)/\sqrt2$ and $\phi_2=(q_3+iq_4)/\sqrt2$ makes the kinetic term $\sum_A(\partial q_A)^2/2$ and the potential a function of $\sum_Aq_A^2$. The six real-plane rotations give currents $j_{AB}^\mu=q_B\partial^\mu q_A-q_A\partial^\mu q_B$. Gauge or Yukawa couplings can break this larger symmetry, so it should not be asserted for an arbitrary interacting doublet.

= Reciprocal rescaling symmetry of two cross-coupled scalar fields
{parent=Internal symmetry of a classical field theory}
{title2=$\phi_1\mapsto e^s\phi_1,\quad\phi_2\mapsto e^{-s}\phi_2$}

For $\mathcal L=\phi_{1,t}\phi_{2,t}-c^2\phi_{1,x}\phi_{2,x}$, the real linear internal transformations preserving the density are $\operatorname{diag}(r,r^{-1})$ and $\begin{pmatrix}0&r\\r^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}$ with $r\ne0$. There are four <connected components>; the <identity component> is the displayed reciprocal rescaling with $r=e^s>0$. In the sum-and-difference basis it preserves a <bilinear form> of signature $(1,1)$, with the identity component isomorphic to that of the <one-dimensional Lorentz group>. Its <Noether current> in coordinates $(t,x)$ is $j^0=\phi_1\phi_{2,t}-\phi_2\phi_{1,t}$ and $j^x=c^2(\phi_2\phi_{1,x}-\phi_1\phi_{2,x})$. The two wave equations give $\partial_tj^0+\partial_xj^x=0$. The energy quadratic form is indefinite: the fields $\phi_\pm=(\phi_1\pm\phi_2)/\sqrt2$ have equal and opposite kinetic signs.

= Variational symmetry of a Lagrangian density
{parent=Classical field theory}

A differentiable infinitesimal transformation is a variational symmetry when its change of the <Lagrangian density> is a divergence, $\delta\mathcal L=\varepsilon\partial_\mu K^\mu$. Under suitable <boundary conditions> the <action> is invariant, so exact pointwise invariance is unnecessary. The off-shell variation identity gives the <Noether current for a first-derivative scalar field>. This definition is for a constant transformation parameter; a spacetime-dependent parameter is a different, gauge-symmetry problem.

= Scalar field
{parent=Classical field theory}
{wiki}

A <scalar field> assigns one scalar to each point of a specified space, such as <Euclidean space> or <spacetime>. For example, $F(x,y)=x^2+2y^2$ is a <real-valued function> on a plane, and its change along a moving observation is given by the <derivative along a curve>. Under a change of coordinates, a scalar field's value at the same physical point is unchanged.

= Minimally coupled scalar field
{parent=Scalar field}
{title2=$L=-\tfrac12g^{ab}\nabla_a\phi\nabla_b\phi-V(\phi)$}

A real <scalar field> is minimally coupled to a <metric tensor> when its kinetic term uses the metric contraction of first derivatives and its <scalar potential> depends only on the field, without an explicit <scalar curvature> coupling. For <metric signature> $(-,+,+,+)$ the displayed <Lagrangian> and volume density $\sqrt{-g}$ give $\Box\phi-V'(\phi)=0$. Its <stress-energy tensor> is $T_{ab}=\nabla_a\phi\nabla_b\phi-g_{ab}[\tfrac12(\nabla\phi)^2+V]$. The <scalar stress-energy divergence identity> explains its conservation on the field equation.

= Scalar stress-energy divergence identity
{parent=Minimally coupled scalar field}
{title2=$\nabla^aT_{ab}=(\Box\phi-V^{\prime}(\phi))\nabla_b\phi$}

For a <minimally coupled scalar field> with a <Levi-Civita connection>, <metric compatibility> and the <symmetric Hessian of a scalar field> give the displayed off-shell identity. It is a special case of the <diffeomorphism Noether identity for a scalar field>, and gives <stress-energy conservation> when the scalar equation is satisfied. No gravitational field equation is needed.

= Scalar mass
{parent=Scalar field}
{title2=$m$}

The mass of a canonically normalized <scalar field> is determined by its quadratic fluctuation potential at a stationary vacuum. With several real fields, physical squared masses are the <eigenvalues> of the <scalar mass matrix>. A negative squared mass signals an unstable direction rather than a stable massive particle.

= Adjoint scalar field
{parent=Scalar field}

A <scalar field> transforming in the <adjoint representation of a Lie algebra> transforms by a <Lie bracket> with the infinitesimal gauge parameter.

{title2=$\phi^6$}

= Zero-dimensional scalar field theory
{parent=Scalar field}

A <scalar field> with no spacetime coordinates reduces its <path integral> to an ordinary integral. It preserves <Wick contractions> and <Feynman-diagram symmetry factors>, making it a useful model of <perturbative quantum field theory>.

= Zero-dimensional quartic perturbation series
{parent=Zero-dimensional scalar field theory}
{title2=$I(\lambda)/I(0)=1-\lambda/(8m^4)+35\lambda^2/(384m^8)+\cdots$}

For $m^2>0$, the <zero-dimensional scalar field theory> with <Euclidean action> $m^2x^2/2+\lambda x^4/4!$ has a <Gaussian integral> normalization $I(0)=\sqrt{2\pi/m^2}$. Its <Feynman rules> assign $1/m^2$ to each <propagator>, $-\lambda$ to each quartic vertex, and the reciprocal <Feynman-diagram symmetry factor> to each <vacuum diagram>. The integral itself includes disconnected <vacuum diagrams>; its logarithm selects connected ones. At second order the three possible numbers of edges joining the two vertices are zero, two and four, with <Feynman-diagram symmetry factors> $128,16,48$ respectively. Their weights add to $35\lambda^2/(384m^8)$.

The <Wick contractions> give the general coefficient $I(0)(-1)^v(4v-1)!!/[v!(4!)^v m^{4v}]$. Successive absolute coefficients have ratio $(4v+3)(4v+1)/[24(v+1)m^4]$, which tends to infinity. Thus this is an <asymptotic expansion> as $\lambda\downarrow0$, rather than a convergent <power series>. For every fixed truncation the <Taylor theorem> remainder estimate for $e^{-u}$, $u\geq0$, can be integrated against the <Gaussian distribution> to justify the corresponding remainder bound.

= Charged scalar field
{parent=Scalar field}

A charged scalar field is a complex <scalar field> transforming nontrivially under a gauge symmetry. For an electromagnetic potential $A_a$, its gauge-covariant derivative is $\mathcal D_a=\nabla_a-iqA_a$.

= Linear-in-potential charged-scalar Lagrangian
{parent=Charged scalar field}

The density
$$
\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-m^2|\phi|^2
+ieA_\mu(\phi\partial^\mu\phi^*-\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi)
$$
contains the term linear in the potential from minimal coupling, but omits $e^2A_\mu A^\mu|\phi|^2$. It has exact global phase symmetry, not exact arbitrary local phase symmetry. For a prescribed background its field equation is $(\Box+m^2)\phi+ie(\partial\cdot A)\phi+2ieA\cdot\partial\phi=0$, together with its <complex conjugate>. The omitted quadratic interaction must not be added silently when solving this specified theory.

= Global phase current of a linearly coupled complex scalar
{parent=Linear-in-potential charged-scalar Lagrangian}
{title2=$J^\mu=i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)-2eA^\mu|\phi|^2$}

For $\delta\phi=-i\epsilon\phi$, the conserved current is $J^\mu=i(\phi^*\partial^\mu\phi-\phi\partial^\mu\phi^*)-2eA^\mu|\phi|^2$. The conjugate field equations imply
$$
\phi^*\Box\phi-\phi\Box\phi^*
=-2ie\,\partial_\mu(A^\mu|\phi|^2),
$$
which directly cancels the <divergence> of the explicit background term. Conservation requires no <Lorenz gauge condition>; constancy of total charge additionally requires vanishing boundary flux.

= Canonical Hamiltonian of a linearly coupled complex scalar
{parent=Linear-in-potential charged-scalar Lagrangian}

For the <linear-in-potential charged-scalar Lagrangian>, $\pi=\dot\phi^*-ieA_0\phi^*$ and $\pi^*=\dot\phi+ieA_0\phi$. With $A_\mu=(A_0,-\mathbf A)$, the <Hamiltonian> density is
$$
\mathcal H=(\pi+ieA_0\phi^*)(\pi^*-ieA_0\phi)
+|\boldsymbol\nabla\phi|^2+m^2|\phi|^2
-ie\mathbf A\cdot(\phi\boldsymbol\nabla\phi^*-\phi^*\boldsymbol\nabla\phi).
$$
The first product includes $+e^2A_0^2|\phi|^2$ after the <Legendre transform>; that term can arise in <canonical variables> even though the original <Lagrangian> has no interaction quadratic in $A$.

= Gauge-covariant derivative of a charged scalar field
{title2=$\mathcal D_a$}
{parent=Charged scalar field}

For electric charge $q$ and <electromagnetic four-potential> $A_a$, the derivative $\mathcal D_a=\nabla_a-iqA_a$ transforms covariantly under an electromagnetic <gauge transformation>.

= Gauge-covariant wave equation for a charged scalar field
{parent=Charged scalar field}

The massless gauge-covariant wave equation is $\mathcal D_a\mathcal D^a\Phi=0$. In a stationary charged background, the electrostatic potential shifts the local frequency from $\omega$ to $\omega+qA_t$.

= Scalar potential
{title2=$V(\phi)$}
{parent=Scalar field}

A scalar potential is the derivative-free energy density $V(\phi)$ of a <scalar field>. Its derivative supplies the force term in the field equation, and its local minima describe candidate vacuum field values.

= Flat direction of a scalar potential
{parent=Scalar potential}

= Flat directions of a scalar potential
{synonym}

In vacuum analysis, an exact <flat direction of a scalar potential> is a nonconstant continuous family of degenerate stationary vacua. The <scalar potential> stays constant along this family and its <gradient> vanishes there. An ordinary level set does not suffice: $V(x,y)=x^2+y^2$ is constant on every circle, but its vacuum at the origin has no flat direction. A vanishing <eigenvalue> of the quadratic <Hessian matrix> also does not suffice: $V(x)=x^4$ has zero quadratic mass at zero but no family of nearby vacua. Exact flat directions can be lifted by corrections absent from the classical <scalar potential>.

= Scalar-field shift symmetry
{parent=Scalar field}

A scalar-field shift symmetry acts by $\phi(x)\mapsto\phi(x)+c$ for constant $c$. Its <Noether charge> is the spatial integral of the canonical momentum, up to the normalization of $c$, and interactions invariant under the symmetry contain derivatives of the scalar field.

= Lagrangian
{c}
{disambiguate=field theory}
{parent=Classical field theory}
{wiki}
{title2=$S[\phi]=\int d^dx\,\mathcal L(\phi,\partial\phi,x)$}

Lagrangian field theory specifies an <action> by integrating a Lagrangian density $\mathcal L(\phi,\partial\phi,x)$ over spacetime. Requiring its <first variation> to vanish for compactly supported field variations yields the field <Euler-Lagrange equations>. The variational <Lagrangian> is the integrand, whereas this field-theoretic formalism specifies the continuum fields and their dynamics.

= Lagrangian density
{title2=$\mathcal L$}
{parent=Lagrangian (field theory)}

A <Lagrangian density> is integrated over spacetime to give the <action> $S=\int d^dx\,\mathcal L$ in <Lagrangian field theory>.

= Kinetic term
{parent=Lagrangian density}
{wiki}

A kinetic term contains derivatives of a field and determines its free propagation. Its quadratic differential operator becomes the inverse <quantum field theory propagator>.

= Gauge-covariant kinetic term
{parent=Kinetic term}

A gauge-covariant kinetic term replaces ordinary derivatives by covariant derivatives. Expanding it generates interactions between the matter field and one or more gauge fields, including contact vertices with two gauge bosons.

= Mass term
{parent=Lagrangian density}
{wiki}

A mass term is a quadratic term without derivatives whose coefficient sets the mass scale of a free field.

= Field interaction term
{parent=Lagrangian density}

A field interaction term is nonlinear in the fields and produces the interaction vertices of perturbation theory.

= Euler-Lagrange field equation
{parent=Lagrangian density}

For a field $\Phi$,
$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\Phi}
-\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\Phi)}=0.
$$

= Damped and antidamped fields from an antisymmetric velocity coupling
{parent=Euler-Lagrange field equation}
{title2=$\omega_{1,2}=\mp i/(2\tau)\pm\sqrt{c^2k^2-1/(4\tau^2)}$}

Adding $(\phi_1\phi_{2,t}-\phi_2\phi_{1,t})/(2\tau)$ to the cross-coupled scalar <Lagrangian density> produces independent equations $\phi_{1,tt}-c^2\phi_{1,xx}+\phi_{1,t}/\tau=0$ and $\phi_{2,tt}-c^2\phi_{2,xx}-\phi_{2,t}/\tau=0$. For the convention $e^{i(kx-\omega t)}$, their frequencies are respectively $-i/(2\tau)\pm\sqrt{c^2k^2-1/(4\tau^2)}$ and $+i/(2\tau)\pm\sqrt{c^2k^2-1/(4\tau^2)}$. Above the threshold they oscillate with decaying or growing envelopes; below it they have nonoscillatory real temporal exponents. At equality the repeated temporal root supplies $(A+Bt)e^{\mp t/(2\tau)}$. At $k=0$ each equation also has a constant solution. The full system is generically unstable because of the amplified second field, even though it arises from an autonomous variational system with conserved, indefinite total energy.

= Euler-Lagrange field equation with second derivatives
{parent=Euler-Lagrange field equation}
{c}
{title2=$\mathcal L_\phi-\partial_\mu\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu}}+\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu\nu}}=0$}

For an <action> $S=\int\mathcal L(\phi,\partial\phi,\partial^2\phi)d^{d+1}x$, vary the field with compact support, or fix both $\delta\phi$ and its first derivatives on the boundary. One <integration by parts> on the first-derivative term and two on the second-derivative term give the displayed <Euler-Lagrange field equation>. With ordered repeated indices, define the second-derivative coefficient symmetrically so that $\delta\mathcal L=\mathcal L_\phi\delta\phi+\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu}}\partial_\mu\delta\phi+\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu\nu}}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu\delta\phi$. This convention avoids an unintended factor of two in mixed derivative terms. Higher spatial derivatives alone do not require higher time derivatives.

= On shell
{parent=Euler-Lagrange field equation}
{wiki}

A field configuration or identity is on shell when the <Euler-Lagrange field equations>[equations of motion] hold.

= Noether current
{title2=$j^\mu$}
{parent=Classical field theory}
{c}

A continuous variational symmetry gives a current satisfying $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$ <on shell>. Integrating $j^0$ over space gives a conserved charge when boundary flux vanishes.

= Axial angular-momentum current of an elastic string
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$L_x=\int\rho(\psi_y\dot\psi_z-\psi_z\dot\psi_y)\,dx$}

Rotating the two transverse displacement components around the string axis gives density $\ell_x=\rho(\psi_y\dot\psi_z-\psi_z\dot\psi_y)$ and flux $\mathcal J_x=-F(\psi_y\psi_z'-\psi_z\psi_y')$. The pair of <wave equations> yields $\partial_t\ell_x+\partial_x\mathcal J_x=0$. The total charge is physical <angular momentum> about the axis; it is conserved only when the net endpoint <torque> flux vanishes.

= Transverse momentum current of an elastic string
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$j_y^\mu=(\rho\dot\psi_y,-F\psi_y\prime)$}

For $\mathcal L=\rho(\dot\psi_y^2+\dot\psi_z^2)/2-F((\psi_y')^2+(\psi_z')^2)/2$, constant displacement symmetry in $y$ gives $j^0=\rho\dot\psi_y$ and $j^x=-F\psi_y'$. The <wave equation> implies $\partial_tj^0+\partial_xj^x=0$. Its integrated charge is physical transverse momentum; endpoint forces can change that charge on a finite segment.

= Unitary scalar multiplet current
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$j_T^\mu=i[(\partial^\mu\Phi^\dagger)T\Phi-\Phi^\dagger T\partial^\mu\Phi]$}

For a <complex scalar field> multiplet with canonical kinetic term and <scalar potential> invariant under $\Phi\mapsto e^{i\varepsilon T}\Phi$, a constant <Hermitian matrix> $T$ gives the displayed <Noether current>. Vary the field and its conjugate independently in the <Noether current for a first-derivative scalar field>. A common alternative convention reverses both the parameter and current sign. Its divergence vanishes <on shell>.

= Shift current of an elastic rod
{parent=Noether current}

For a longitudinal displacement field $\psi(z,t)$ and <Lagrangian density> $\mathcal L=A[\rho\dot\psi^2-K\psi'^2]/2$, uniform field shifts give <Noether current> $j^0=A\rho\dot\psi$, $j^z=-AK\psi'$. Its spatial integral is the rod's longitudinal mechanical <momentum>. A prescribed position-dependent <mass density> changes $j^0$ to $A\rho(z)\dot\psi$ but does not break this internal shift symmetry. Boundary traction can carry momentum into or out of a finite rod, so action symmetry alone does not guarantee conservation of the integrated charge.

= Noether current for a constant shift with second derivatives
{parent=Noether current}
{c}
{title2=$j^\mu=\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu}}-\partial_\nu\mathcal L_{\phi_{,\mu\nu}}$}

If a <Lagrangian density> depending on first and second derivatives has no undifferentiated field, the <scalar-field shift symmetry> $\delta\phi=\epsilon$ gives the displayed <Noether current>. Its <divergence> is minus the <Euler-Lagrange field equation with second derivatives>, so it is conserved <on shell>. The spatial integral of $j^0$ is constant only if the boundary current vanishes. For $\mathcal L=\rho\phi_t^2/2+\alpha\phi_x^2/2-\beta\phi_{xx}^2/2$, it gives $j^0=\rho\phi_t$, $j^x=\alpha\phi_x+\beta\phi_{xxx}$. Fixed transverse endpoint positions break the global shift symmetry of the boundary problem; support forces can then change the total transverse momentum even though the bulk continuity equation still holds.

= Conformal current
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$j^\mu=\Theta^{\mu\nu}\omega_\nu$}

For a symmetric conserved <stress-energy tensor> $\Theta$ and a <conformal Killing vector field> $\omega$ in dimension $d$, the displayed <conformal current> has divergence $(\partial\cdot\omega)\Theta^\mu{}_{\mu}/d$. This follows by contracting the <conformal Killing equation> with $\Theta$. A traceless tensor therefore gives a conserved current for every conformal generator.

= Virial current
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$T^\mu{}_{\mu}=\partial_\mu V^\mu$}

A <virial current> $V^\mu$ represents the <trace> of a conserved <stress-energy tensor> as a divergence. Then the dilation <Noether current> $D^\mu=x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}-V^\mu$ is conserved, since its divergence is $T^\mu{}_{\mu}-\partial_\mu V^\mu$. This establishes <scale invariance>; a traceless <stress-energy tensor improvement> is the extra condition needed for <conformal currents>.

= Noether current for a spacetime symmetry
{c}
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$j^\mu=\sum_a\Pi_a^\mu Q_a+\mathcal L\xi^\mu-K^\mu$}

For $x'^\mu=x^\mu+\varepsilon\xi^\mu$ and $\phi'_a(x')=\phi_a(x)+\varepsilon\Delta_a$, the fixed-coordinate field variation is $Q_a=\Delta_a-\xi^\nu\partial_\nu\phi_a$. Suppose action invariance is expressed as $\delta_0\mathcal L/\varepsilon+\partial_\mu(\mathcal L\xi^\mu)=\partial_\mu K^\mu$. Writing $\Pi_a^\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi_a)$ and $E_a=\mathcal L_{\phi_a}-\partial_\mu\Pi_a^\mu$, the product rule gives $\partial_\mu j^\mu=-\sum_a E_aQ_a$. Thus $j^\mu$ is a <conserved current> <on shell>. A vanishing spatial boundary flux is additionally required for its <Noether charge> to be conserved. This formula includes internal transformations, translations and <Lorentz transformations>.

= Noether current for a first-derivative scalar field
{c}
{parent=Noether current}
{title2=$j^\mu=\mathcal L_{\partial_\mu\phi}X-K^\mu$}

For $\delta\phi=\varepsilon X$ and a <variational symmetry of a Lagrangian density>, write $\Pi^\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)$ and $\mathcal E=\mathcal L_\phi-\partial_\mu\Pi^\mu$. The product rule gives $\delta\mathcal L/\varepsilon=\mathcal E X+\partial_\mu(\Pi^\mu X)$. Therefore $j^\mu=\Pi^\mu X-K^\mu$ obeys $\partial_\mu j^\mu=-\mathcal E X$, so it is a <conserved current> <on shell>. Spatial integration gives a conserved <Noether charge> only when the boundary flux vanishes. The formula extends to several components by summation.

= Conserved current
{parent=Noether current}

= Current conservation
{synonym}

A conserved current is a vector field $j^\mu$ satisfying the continuity equation $\partial_\mu j^\mu=0$. Its spatial integral is constant in time when no current crosses the boundary.

= Conserved charge
{parent=Conserved current}
{title2=$Q=\int j^0\,dx$}

An integral of the time component of a <conserved current> is a <conserved charge> when the spatial boundary flux vanishes. Charges need not come from an immediately apparent elementary symmetry; a field theory can have an infinite higher-derivative current hierarchy. A <Noether charge> is obtained from the symmetry current supplied by the <Noether theorem>.

= Conserved-current two-point function
{parent=Conserved current}
{title2=$G(k^2)$}

In a massless four-dimensional theory, current conservation gives a transverse tensor structure, $\int d^4x\,e^{-ikx}\langle J_\mu(x)J_\nu(0)\rangle=(k_\mu k_\nu-\delta_{\mu\nu}k^2)G(k^2)$. With current <mass dimension> three, the scalar coefficient has dimension zero.

= Noether charge
{title2=$Q$}
{c}
{parent=Noether current}

A Noether charge is the spatial integral $Q=\int j^0d^3x$ of a <Noether current>. It is time-independent when the current has vanishing flux through the spatial boundary, and in a Hamiltonian formulation it generates the associated continuous symmetry.

= Dilatation current
{title2=$D^\mu$}
{parent=Noether current}

The dilatation current associated with scale transformations is $D^\mu=x_\nu T^{\mu\nu}$ up to improvement terms. Its divergence is the trace of the stress-energy tensor, so classical scale invariance requires a traceless improved tensor on shell.

= On-shell divergence of a quartic scalar dilation current
{parent=Dilatation current}
{title2=$\partial_\mu J^\mu=(4-n)\mathcal L$}

For $\mathcal L=(\partial\phi)^2/2-\lambda\phi^4$ and the displayed-weight dilation variation $\delta\phi=\phi+x\cdot\partial\phi$, the full divergence identity is $(4-n)\mathcal L+\delta\phi(\Box\phi+4\lambda\phi^3)$. The field equation removes the second term. In four <spacetime> dimensions the <density> changes by a total divergence, so the <Noether current> includes the subtraction $-x^\mu\mathcal L$. This is a classical statement about generic solutions and this particular current.

= Scale-breaking sources in derivative field theories
{parent=Dilatation current}

For a first-derivative real <scalar field> in one space and one time dimension, put $j_D^\mu=\mathcal L_{\partial_\mu\psi}(z\psi'+t\dot\psi)-x^\mu\mathcal L$. Its on-shell divergence is
$$
\partial_\mu j_D^\mu=\mathcal L_{\partial_\mu\psi}\partial_\mu\psi-2\mathcal L-z\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial z}\right|_{\psi,\partial\psi}-t\left.\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial t}\right|_{\psi,\partial\psi}.
$$
A quadratic derivative density with arbitrary field-dependent coefficients has zero derivative-homogeneity term. An extra term $-\alpha\psi'^4$ instead gives $-2\alpha\psi'^4$. A position-dependent kinetic coefficient $A\rho(z)/2$ gives $-Az\rho'(z)\dot\psi^2/2$. These source terms distinguish <scale invariance> from internal field-shift symmetry.

= Dilatation charge of a one-dimensional elastic wave
{parent=Dilatation current}

For constant $A,\rho,K$ on all space and time, the active waveform transformation $\psi_b(z,t)=\psi(bz,bt)$ leaves the <action> of $\mathcal L=A(\rho\dot\psi^2-K\psi'^2)/2$ invariant: the density gains $b^2$ and the integration measure gains $b^{-2}$. With <canonical momentum> $\Pi=A\rho\dot\psi$, its <Noether current> can be chosen as
$$
j_D^0=t\mathcal H+z\Pi\psi',\qquad
j_D^z=-tAK\dot\psi\psi'-z\mathcal H.
$$
The integrated charge $D=\int j_D^0dz$ is conserved if its boundary flux vanishes and the weighted integral exists. For a rightgoing <wave packet> $\psi=f(z-ct)$, $c=\sqrt{K/\rho}$, it becomes $D=-A\rho c\int\xi[f'(\xi)]^2d\xi$. A fixed finite interval generally breaks this scale symmetry unless its boundaries and boundary data are transformed too.

= Canonical stress-energy tensor
{title2=$T^{\mu\nu}_{\mathrm{can}}$}
{parent=Noether current}

= Canonical energy-momentum tensor
{synonym}

Spacetime translations give the canonical stress-energy tensor
$$
T^\mu{}_{\nu}=\sum_a
\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\mu\Phi_a)}
\partial_\nu\Phi_a-\delta^\mu_\nu\mathcal L.
$$

= Scalar-field momentum flux
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T^{01}=-\phi_t\phi_x,\quad T^{11}=\tfrac12\phi_t^2+\tfrac12\phi_x^2-V$}

For a real <scalar field> in one spatial dimension with metric signature $(+,-)$ and potential $V$, the <stress-energy conservation> law is $\partial_tT^{01}+\partial_xT^{11}=0$. The force on the field to the left of a fixed cut is $-T^{11}$ at the cut when the far-left boundary flux vanishes. <Finite-energy field configurations> also permit this conclusion by spatial cutoffs, without assuming pointwise decay of all derivatives.

= Four-momentum of a free real scalar field
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$P^\mu=\int T^{0\mu}\,d^3x$}

For the free <real scalar field> with signature $(+,-,-,-)$,
$$
E=\frac12\int(\dot\phi^2+|\nabla\phi|^2+m^2\phi^2)d^3x,
\qquad \mathbf P=-\int\dot\phi\,\nabla\phi\,d^3x.
$$
The <canonical stress-energy tensor> is $T^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu\phi\partial^\nu\phi-\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal L$. Its divergence vanishes by the <Klein-Gordon equation>, giving constant integrated charges when integrals and boundary flux are well behaved. The minus sign in contravariant spatial <momentum> is from $\partial^i=-\partial_i$; lower-index spatial charges have the opposite sign. A <plane wave> $\cos(Et-\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x)$ has <momentum> density pointing along $\mathbf k$.

= Normal-ordered free scalar four-momentum
{parent=Four-momentum of a free real scalar field}
{title2=$P^\mu=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}p^\mu a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}$}

For a canonically quantized <real scalar field>, the quadratic Hamiltonian contains $\frac12\sum_{\mathbf p}E_{\mathbf p}$ in a regulated box. <Normal ordering> subtracts this <zero-point energy>, leaving
$$
P^\mu=\int\frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3}p^\mu a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger a_{\mathbf p}.
$$
The vacuum momentum is zero with an inversion-symmetric regulator. The <canonical commutation relation> then gives $[P^\mu,a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger]=p^\mu a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger$, identifying the momentum and energy added by one particle. The un-subtracted energy differs by a constant and is not literally the same operator.

= Translation of a scalar creation operator
{parent=Normal-ordered free scalar four-momentum}
{title2=$e^{-i\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf y}a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger e^{i\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf y}=e^{-i\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf y}a_{\mathbf p}^\dagger$}

The <canonical commutation relation> gives $[P^\mu,a_p^\dagger]=p^\mu a_p^\dagger$ for a free <real scalar field>. Differentiate the exponential conjugation: its derivative is a scalar multiple of the same operator, yielding the displayed phase. The <annihilation operator> receives the opposite phase, so at equal time $e^{-i\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf y}\phi(\mathbf x)e^{i\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf y}=\phi(\mathbf x+\mathbf y)$. The <spatial translation operator> translates a state's position wavefunction in the opposite argument direction.

= Canonical stress-energy tensor of the Maxwell field
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\mu\rho}\partial^\nu A_\rho+\frac14\eta^{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}$}

For signature $(+,-,-,-)$, translation invariance of the <Maxwell Lagrangian> gives this tensor. It is generally neither symmetric nor gauge invariant. The <antisymmetric superpotential improvement> $-\partial_\rho(F^{\rho\mu}A^\nu)$ produces, on the source-free equations of motion, the symmetric <electromagnetic stress-energy tensor> $\Theta^{\mu\nu}=-F^{\mu\rho}F^\nu{}_{\rho}+\eta^{\mu\nu}F^2/4$, which is traceless in four spacetime dimensions.

= Gauge-invariant Maxwell stress-energy tensor
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor of the Maxwell field}
{title2=$\Theta^{\mu\nu}$}

In signature $+---$, the <Maxwell Lagrangian> yields
$$
\Theta^\mu{}_{\nu}=-F^{\mu\rho}F_{\nu\rho}+\tfrac14\delta^\mu{}_{\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}.
$$
It is symmetric after raising its second index, <gauge invariant>, and conserved on the source-free <Maxwell equations>. Its energy density is $\frac12(\mathbf E^2+\mathbf B^2)$ and its energy flux is the <Poynting vector>. Its difference from the <canonical stress-energy tensor> is an antisymmetric superpotential divergence plus an equation-of-motion term, so integrated charges coincide when the constraints and vanishing boundary terms are imposed.

= Antisymmetric superpotential improvement
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}
{title2=$T^{\mu\nu}\mapsto T^{\mu\nu}+\partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}$}

If $B^{\rho\mu\nu}=-B^{\mu\rho\nu}$, commuting partial derivatives gives $\partial_\mu\partial_\rho B^{\rho\mu\nu}=0$. Such an improvement preserves conservation of a <stress-energy tensor> and changes integrated charges only by a spatial boundary term. The <Belinfante-Rosenfeld stress-energy tensor> is obtained through this type of improvement.

= Belinfante-Rosenfeld stress-energy tensor
{c}
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}
{wiki=Belinfante–Rosenfeld_stress–energy_tensor}

The Belinfante-Rosenfeld construction adds the divergence of a spin-current superpotential to the <canonical stress-energy tensor>, producing a symmetric conserved tensor with the same integrated four-momentum under suitable boundary conditions.

= Lorentz current
{title2=$J^{\mu\rho\sigma}$}
{c}
{parent=Canonical stress-energy tensor}

For a symmetric conserved stress-energy tensor, the Lorentz current is $J^{\mu\rho\sigma}=x^\rho T^{\mu\sigma}-x^\sigma T^{\mu\rho}$. Its spatial integrals generate rotations and boosts.

= Boost charge of a one-dimensional scalar field
{parent=Lorentz current}
{title2=$J^{01}=tP-\int x\mathcal E\,dx$}

For a free massless real <scalar field> with <wave speed> set to one and metric $(+,-)$, define $\mathcal E=(\phi_t^2+\phi_x^2)/2$ and $p=-\phi_t\phi_x$. Its symmetric <stress-energy tensor> is $T^{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal E&p\\p&\mathcal E\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. The <Lorentz current> has density $t p-x\mathcal E$ and flux $t\mathcal E-xp$. Its conserved charge is the displayed expression when boundary flux vanishes. In one spatial dimension it generates boosts, not spatial rotations; there is no independent purely spatial angular-momentum component. Its conservation says that the center of energy moves uniformly. This is a symmetry of the wave-field model and need not survive boundaries or additional material effects.

= Dirac current
{c}
{title2=$j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$}
{parent=Noether current}
{wiki}

The global phase symmetry of a <Dirac field> gives the conserved Dirac current $j^\mu=\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi$.

= Causal Dirac spinor current
{parent=Dirac current}
{title2=$V^0=\epsilon^\dagger\epsilon,\quad V^2\le0$}

For a commuting spinor in mostly-plus signature, orient its <Dirac current> to the future and write $V^i=\epsilon^\dagger\alpha^i\epsilon$, with Hermitian $\alpha^i=-\gamma^0\gamma^i$. For every spatial <unit vector> $u$, $(u\cdot\alpha)^2=1$ implies $|u\cdot V|\le\epsilon^\dagger\epsilon=V^0$. Choosing $u$ along the spatial current proves $V^2\le0$. Thus spinor bilinears supply causal <vectors> suitable for energy-condition estimates. A Grassmann-valued spinor does not have this ordinary positivity interpretation.

= Null current of a commuting Majorana spinor
{parent=Causal Dirac spinor current}
{title2=$K^\mu K_\mu=0$}

In four dimensions a single commuting <Majorana spinor> is specified by a two-component complex spinor $\xi$. Its future <Dirac current> is proportional to $(\xi^\dagger\xi,\xi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\xi)$, and the <Pauli matrix multiplication law> implies $|\xi^\dagger\boldsymbol\sigma\xi|^2=(\xi^\dagger\xi)^2$. Thus it is null. A Dirac spinor or a sum of independent Majorana currents can be timelike. This distinction matters when applying a timelike-current classification to minimal real supersymmetry.

= Gordon identity
{c}
{parent=Dirac current}
{title2=$2m\bar u\prime\gamma^a u$}

= Gordon decomposition
{c}
{synonym}

The <Gordon identity> rewrites a vector-current matrix element between equal-mass on-shell <Dirac spinors> using a momentum term and an antisymmetric spin term. In the mostly-minus convention $\{\Gamma^a,\Gamma^b\}=2\eta^{ab}$, $(\not p-m)u=0$, and $\Gamma^{ab}=[\Gamma^a,\Gamma^b]/2$, it reads $2m\bar u'\Gamma^au=\bar u'[(p'+p)^a-\Gamma^{ab}(p'_b-p_b)]u$. Derive it by adding the two external <Dirac equation> identities. The equivalent formula in the <mostly-plus Dirac convention> is $\bar u'[\gamma^{ab}(p'_b-p_b)+2m\gamma^a+p^a+p'^a]u=0$. The identity before division by $m$ remains valid for massless equal-mass states.

= Dirac electromagnetic current
{c}
{parent=Dirac current}

The Dirac electromagnetic current is the electric charge times the <Dirac current>. Its coupling $-ej^\mu A_\mu$ to the <electromagnetic four-potential> is the matter interaction of <quantum electrodynamics>.

= Derivative coupling to a conserved current
{parent=Dirac current}

An interaction $-\lambda j^\mu\partial_\mu\phi$ with an exactly <conserved current> is redundant: integration by parts gives $\lambda\phi\,\partial_\mu j^\mu$ up to a boundary term. For the <Dirac current>, the local phase redefinition $\psi=e^{-i\lambda\phi}\chi$ removes the coupling, so its on-shell scattering amplitudes vanish.
