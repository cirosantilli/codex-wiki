= Quantum information
{scope}
{title2=A16608W1}

Source paper: https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/ba_computer_science_past_papers/response/2577745/attach/2/202402%20183%20CS%20Papers.zip?cookie_passthrough=1

Solutions to all three questions.<Quantum information examination paper A16608W1 (2022)>[Cover dated 3 June 2022; printed pp. 2–7, Questions 1–3]{ref} System labels specify the order of each <tensor product of quantum systems>. In an operator product the rightmost operator acts first. In Question 3(d), the stated fidelity is the <squared quantum fidelity> of the full two-<qubit> output with the desired two-copy <pure state>.

== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

A <basic measurement> of a finite-dimensional <quantum system> is specified by an <orthonormal basis> $\{|b_j\rangle\}_j$ of its <Hilbert space>. For a normalized input <pure state> $|\chi\rangle$, the <Born rule> gives
$$
\boxed{p_j=|\langle b_j|\chi\rangle|^2,\qquad
|\chi_j\rangle=\frac{|b_j\rangle\langle b_j|\chi\rangle}{\sqrt{p_j}}
\quad(p_j>0).}
$$
The <post-measurement state> is therefore $|b_j\rangle$ up to a <global phase>. An outcome of zero <probability> has no conditional <post-measurement state>. For a general input <density matrix> $\rho$, the same <basic measurement> has $p_j=\langle b_j|\rho|b_j\rangle$ and conditional <density matrix> $|b_j\rangle\langle b_j|$ whenever $p_j>0$.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the <Pauli X eigenstates> $|\pm\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $\cos\theta+i\sin\theta=e^{i\theta}$. Their <probability amplitudes> are
$$
\langle\pm|\phi\rangle=\frac{\sqrt2\pm e^{i\theta}}{\sqrt6}.
$$
The <Born rule> gives
$$
\boxed{\Pr(+)=\frac12+\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\cos\theta,\qquad
\Pr(-)=\frac12-\frac{\sqrt2}{3}\cos\theta.}
$$
The two <probabilities> sum to one. Their dependence on the <relative quantum phase> is the <quantum interference> between the two components in the <computational basis>.

=== c
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

If Alice's <basic measurement> returns a normalized vector $|u\rangle_A$, form Bob's unnormalized conditional vector
$$
|\widetilde\chi_u\rangle_B=(\langle u|_A\otimes I_B)|\chi\rangle_{AB}.
$$
The <Born rule> and normalization give
$$
\boxed{p_u=\|\widetilde\chi_u\|^2,\qquad
|\chi_u\rangle_B=\frac{|\widetilde\chi_u\rangle_B}{\sqrt{p_u}}\quad(p_u>0).}
$$
The joint <post-measurement state> is $|u\rangle_A\otimes|\chi_u\rangle_B$. For $|u\rangle_A=\alpha|0\rangle_A+\beta|1\rangle_A$ with real coefficients and $\alpha^2+\beta^2=1$, contraction of the <Bell state> gives
$$
(\langle u|\otimes I)|\phi_+\rangle
=\frac{\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle}{\sqrt2},
\qquad p_u=\frac12.
$$
Thus **Bob's normalized conditional state is $\alpha|0\rangle_B+\beta|1\rangle_B$**. For complex coefficients the contraction instead contains their <complex conjugates>; this is the same conjugation underlying <conjugate-basis steering of a Bell pair>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Let $p_{ab}$ denote the conditional winning <probability> in the <CHSH game>. With both settings zero, <computational-basis measurements> of the <Bell state> always agree, so $p_{00}=1$. When just one setting is one, Bob's conditional <pure state> from part (i), or the corresponding calculation with Alice and Bob exchanged, gives agreement <probability> $\cos^2\theta$. Hence $p_{01}=p_{10}=\cos^2\theta$.

For $a=b=1$, winning requires different output <bits>. Alice obtains each of her two outcomes with <probability> $1/2$. Given her first outcome, Bob's desired overlap is
$$
\langle f_1(\theta)|e_0(\theta)\rangle=2\sin\theta\cos\theta=\sin2\theta;
$$
given her second outcome, the other desired overlap is $\langle f_0(\theta)|e_1(\theta)\rangle=-\sin2\theta$. Thus $p_{11}=\sin^2(2\theta)$. Averaging over the four equally likely inputs gives
$$
\boxed{p_{\mathrm{win}}(\theta)=\frac{1+2\cos^2\theta+\sin^2(2\theta)}4
=\frac{3+\cos2\theta-\cos^2(2\theta)}4.}
$$
The maximum winning <probability> for noncommunicating classical players is $3/4$, the game form of the <CHSH inequality>. Setting $t=\cos2\theta$, strict improvement requires $t-t^2>0$, or $0<t<1$. On the stipulated interval this gives
$$
\boxed{0<\theta<\frac\pi4.}
$$
The endpoints give equality with the classical bound. Within this <reflected real-basis CHSH strategy>, the maximum is $13/16$ at $t=1/2$, namely $\theta=\pi/6$; this follows by completing the square in $t$.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Use <entanglement-assisted discrimination of a Pauli basis>. Define the known single-<qubit> <unitary operator> $V$ by $V|0\rangle=|\psi\rangle$ and $V|1\rangle=|\psi^\perp\rangle$. Prepare the device input $A$ and a reference <ancilla qubit> $R$ in
$$
|\Omega\rangle_{AR}=(V_A\otimes I_R)|\phi_+\rangle
=\frac{|\psi\rangle_A|0\rangle_R+|\psi^\perp\rangle_A|1\rangle_R}{\sqrt2}.
$$
Apply the unknown device once to $A$, then apply the known inverse $V^\dagger$ to $A$. Since
$$
V^\dagger U_0V=I,\quad V^\dagger U_1V=Z,\quad
V^\dagger U_2V=X,\quad V^\dagger U_3V=iY,
$$
the possible two-<qubit> outputs are
$$
\begin{aligned}
U_0:&\quad (|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2,\\
U_1:&\quad (|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt2,\\
U_2:&\quad (|01\rangle+|10\rangle)/\sqrt2,\\
U_3:&\quad (|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2.
\end{aligned}
$$
These are the four mutually <orthogonal> <Bell states>. A <Bell-basis measurement> therefore identifies the device with certainty. Explicitly, apply a <controlled-NOT gate> $A\to R$, then a <Hadamard gate> on $A$, followed by <computational-basis measurements>. The readouts $00,10,01,11$ identify $U_0,U_1,U_2,U_3$, respectively. Thus **one use of the device and one reference qubit suffice**.

{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

For normalized vector representatives in the same <Hilbert space>, two <pure states> are $\varepsilon$-close in <pure-state norm distance> when
$$
\boxed{\bigl\||\psi\rangle-|\phi\rangle\bigr\|_2\leq\varepsilon.}
$$
Here $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the <Euclidean norm> of the coefficient vector in any <orthonormal basis>. Equivalently, $2-2\operatorname{Re}\langle\psi|\phi\rangle\leq\varepsilon^2$. If the convention identifies vector representatives differing by a <global phase>, first minimize over that phase, giving $\min_\gamma\||\psi\rangle-e^{i\gamma}|\phi\rangle\|_2=\sqrt{2-2|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|}$; the vector-norm convention above specifies the representatives.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Two <unitary transformations> $U,V$ on the same <Hilbert space> are $\varepsilon$-close in <operator-norm distance between unitary gates> when
$$
\boxed{\|U-V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
=\sup_{\|\psi\|_2=1}\bigl\|U|\psi\rangle-V|\psi\rangle\bigr\|_2\leq\varepsilon.}
$$
Thus their outputs are $\varepsilon$-close in <pure-state norm distance> for every normalized input. This is an <operator norm> condition, stronger than checking only one chosen input. If <global phase> is disregarded for the whole operation, use $\min_\gamma\|U-e^{i\gamma}V\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ instead, with one phase chosen for the operator rather than separately for each input.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

An <entangling gate> is a <unitary operator> on two <quantum systems> for which **some pure product input is mapped to an entangled state**:
$$
\boxed{\exists\,|u\rangle_A,|v\rangle_B:\quad
U(|u\rangle_A\otimes|v\rangle_B)\ \text{is entangled}.}
$$
It need not create <quantum entanglement> from every <product state>. For example, a <controlled-NOT gate> entangles $|+\rangle|0\rangle$ but leaves $|00\rangle$ a <product state>.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

**The gate set is universal.** Since the <Hadamard gate> satisfies $H^\dagger=H$ and $H^2=I$, the supplied $U$ is a <unitary operator>, and
$$
U^2=\frac{(I-iH)^2}{2}=-iH.
$$
Two applications of $U$ therefore realize a <Hadamard gate> up to a <global phase>. Together with the supplied <controlled-NOT gate> and <T gate>, this simulates the given <universal quantum gate set>.

An exact matrix representative of $H$ can also be synthesized if desired. Put $A=U^2=-iH$ and $S=T^2$. Direct multiplication gives $(AS)^6=-iI$, hence
$$
\boxed{(AS)^{18}A=H.}
$$
This finite product uses only $U$ and $T$, so the conclusion does not rely on discarding a <global phase>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

**The gate set is not universal.** The displayed two-<qubit> <matrix> is the <SWAP gate>:
$$
W(|u\rangle\otimes|v\rangle)=|v\rangle\otimes|u\rangle.
$$
Both $V$ and the <Hadamard gate> act on one <qubit>. Thus every gate preserves a fully factored <product state>, and induction gives the same property for every <quantum circuit> built from them. This is the <product-state obstruction to SWAP universality>.

For a quantitative obstruction to even approximate universality, every two-<qubit> <product state> has squared overlap at most $1/2$ with a <Bell state>. Indeed, for normalized coefficient vectors $u,v$,
$$
|\langle\phi_+|u\otimes v\rangle|^2
=\frac{|u_0v_0+u_1v_1|^2}{2}\leq\frac12
$$
by the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality>. A <controlled-NOT gate> acting on $|+\rangle|0\rangle$ would instead produce that <Bell state>, so these circuits cannot approximate all <unitary transformations>. The fact that $V^2=e^{-i\pi/8}T$ supplies a <T gate> up to <global phase> does not supply an <entangling gate>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the <entanglement-assisted promised equality protocol>. Alice applies the <phase oracle> $O_x|i\rangle=(-1)^{x_i}|i\rangle$ on $A$, and Bob applies $O_y|i\rangle=(-1)^{y_i}|i\rangle$ on $B$. These are <unitary operators> because their diagonal entries have <modulus> one. After Alice sends $A$ to Bob, the joint <pure state> is
$$
|\Omega_{xy}\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^{x_i+y_i}|ii\rangle.
$$
Let $|\Omega\rangle=n^{-1/2}\sum_i|ii\rangle$ be the original <maximally entangled state>. If $d=d_H(x,y)$ is the <Hamming distance>, then
$$
\langle\Omega|\Omega_{xy}\rangle
=\frac1n\sum_i(-1)^{x_i+y_i}
=\frac{n-d-d}{n}=1-\frac{2d}{n}.
$$
Bob performs the <projective measurement> with projectors $P=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ and $I-P$. Under the promise,
$$
\boxed{d=0\implies\Pr(P)=1,\qquad
d=n/2\implies\Pr(P)=0.}
$$
He declares equal strings for the first outcome and distance $n/2$ for the other. This is exact because the promised outputs occupy <orthogonal> subspaces. A <basic measurement> also suffices: extend $|\Omega\rangle$ to an <orthonormal basis> and group all its other outcomes together. Alice transmits one $n$-dimensional system, realizable using $\lceil\log_2n\rceil$ <qubits>; $n$ need not be a power of two.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

Use <Hadamard conjugation of a controlled gate> to obtain the <controlled-Z gate>:
$$
CZ=(I\otimes H)\operatorname{CNOT}_{1\to2}(I\otimes H)
=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1).
$$
Conjugating by <Pauli X gates> on both <qubits> moves its negative entry from $|11\rangle$ to $|00\rangle$:
$$
(X\otimes X)CZ(X\otimes X)=I-2|00\rangle\langle00|.
$$
Finally $(H\otimes H)|00\rangle=|+\rangle|+\rangle=|e_0\rangle$, so a <two-qubit diffusion circuit> is
$$
\boxed{C=(H\otimes H)(X\otimes X)(I\otimes H)
\operatorname{CNOT}_{1\to2}(I\otimes H)(X\otimes X)(H\otimes H)=-W.}
$$
In temporal order, apply <Hadamard gates> on both <qubits>, <Pauli X gates> on both, a <Hadamard gate> on the second, a <controlled-NOT gate> $1\to2$, a <Hadamard gate> on the second, <Pauli X gates> on both, then <Hadamard gates> on both. Every gate is permitted. The overall minus sign is the allowed <global phase>, so this implements the required <inversion about the mean> for <Grover search algorithm>.

== 3
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

The two conditions on a <density matrix> are **positive semidefiniteness and unit trace**:
$$
\boxed{\rho\geq0,\qquad\operatorname{Tr}\rho=1.}
$$
Here <positive semidefinite> includes being a <Hermitian matrix>, with $v^\dagger\rho v\geq0$ for every vector $v$. Equivalently, a <density matrix> is <Hermitian> with nonnegative <eigenvalues> summing to one.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

The <Pauli Z gate> gives a **rotation through $\pi$ about the $z$ axis** of the <Bloch sphere>. In <Bloch vector> coordinates,
$$
\boxed{(r_x,r_y,r_z)\longmapsto(-r_x,-r_y,r_z).}
$$
For example, this follows from $ZXZ=-X$, $ZYZ=-Y$ and $ZZZ=Z$ in the <Pauli matrix> expansion $\rho=(I+r_xX+r_yY+r_zZ)/2$.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Since $H=(X+Z)/\sqrt2$, the <Hadamard gate> is, up to a <global phase>, the <rotation gate>
$$
e^{-i\pi(X+Z)/(2\sqrt2)}=-iH.
$$
Thus its <Bloch sphere> action is a **rotation through $\pi$ about the axis $(1,0,1)/\sqrt2$**, halfway between the positive $x$ and $z$ axes:
$$
\boxed{(r_x,r_y,r_z)\longmapsto(r_z,-r_y,r_x).}
$$
The identities $HXH=Z$, $HYH=-Y$ and $HZH=X$ verify this <Bloch vector> transformation directly.

=== c
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

**The state is separable.** The <identity matrix> resolves into the four projectors of the <computational basis>, giving the explicit <quantum state ensemble>
$$
\boxed{\frac{I_4}{4}=\frac14\sum_{i,j=0}^1
\bigl(|i\rangle\langle i|\bigr)_A\otimes\bigl(|j\rangle\langle j|\bigr)_B
=\frac{I_2}{2}\otimes\frac{I_2}{2}.}
$$
Each member is a <pure state> that is a <product state>, so this is a <separable quantum state> by its defining decomposition.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

**The state is separable.** The off-diagonal terms of the two <Bell state> projectors have opposite signs and cancel:
$$
\boxed{\frac12|\phi_+\rangle\langle\phi_+|+\frac12|\phi_-\rangle\langle\phi_-|
=\frac12|00\rangle\langle00|+\frac12|11\rangle\langle11|.}
$$
This is a <quantum state ensemble> of two <pure states> that are <product states>, with <probability> $1/2$ each, establishing a <separable quantum state>. Its correlations are classical; an ensemble decomposition into <entangled states> does not itself establish <quantum entanglement> of the <density matrix>.

=== d
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

For input $|0\rangle_S$, the <controlled-NOT gate> fixes $|00\rangle_{SC}$, which is the ideal two-copy <pure state>. Thus its <global cloning fidelity> is $F_0=1$. For input $|+\rangle_S$, the actual output is the <Bell state> $|\phi_+\rangle$, whereas the desired output is $|++\rangle$. Their overlap is
$$
\langle++|\phi_+\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt2},\qquad F_+=\frac12.
$$
With the given equal input <probabilities>,
$$
\boxed{F_0=1,\qquad F_+=\frac12,\qquad
\overline F=\frac12\left(1+\frac12\right)=\frac34.}
$$
These values use the specified <squared quantum fidelity> for the full output, rather than a one-copy marginal fidelity.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Assume perfect cloning of both specified <pure states>. Allowing irrelevant output <global phases>, this would require
$$
U|00\rangle=e^{i\alpha}|00\rangle,
\qquad U|+0\rangle=e^{i\beta}|++\rangle.
$$
A <unitary operator> preserves <inner products>, and therefore their magnitudes. But the input overlap and required output overlap have different magnitudes:
$$
\boxed{|\langle00|+0\rangle|=\frac1{\sqrt2}
\ne\frac12=|\langle00|++\rangle|.}
$$
This contradiction proves that **no such two-qubit unitary exists**, without assuming the <no-cloning theorem>.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

Construct an <economical two-state quantum cloner> by moving both actual outputs slightly toward one another relative to the two desired copies. Set $|a\rangle=|00\rangle$ and $|b\rangle=|++\rangle$. Their overlap is $1/2$, so their sum and difference yield the <orthonormal> vectors
$$
|u\rangle=\frac{|a\rangle+|b\rangle}{\sqrt3},\qquad
|v\rangle=|a\rangle-|b\rangle.
$$
The desired outputs have the symmetric expressions
$$
|a\rangle=\cos\frac\pi6|u\rangle+\sin\frac\pi6|v\rangle,
\qquad
|b\rangle=\cos\frac\pi6|u\rangle-\sin\frac\pi6|v\rangle.
$$
Choose the actual outputs to be
$$
|c_0\rangle=\cos\frac\pi8|u\rangle+\sin\frac\pi8|v\rangle,
\qquad
|c_+\rangle=\cos\frac\pi8|u\rangle-\sin\frac\pi8|v\rangle.
$$
They are normalized and satisfy
$$
\langle c_0|c_+\rangle=\cos\frac\pi4=\frac1{\sqrt2}
=\langle00|+0\rangle.
$$
Consequently the prescription $|00\rangle\mapsto|c_0\rangle$, $|+0\rangle\mapsto|c_+\rangle$ preserves the <Gram matrix> of the two inputs and defines a <linear isometry> on their span. The <unitary extension of a finite-dimensional isometry> gives a <unitary operator> on the same four-dimensional, two-<qubit> <Hilbert space>; no extra workspace is required.

For an explicit full-space construction, define $c=\cos(\pi/8)$, $s=\sin(\pi/8)$ and the <orthonormal basis>
$$
|p\rangle=c|00\rangle+s|10\rangle,\qquad
|m\rangle=s|00\rangle-c|10\rangle,\qquad |01\rangle,\ |11\rangle.
$$
Choose two further <orthonormal> vectors perpendicular to $u,v$,
$$
|w\rangle=\frac{|01\rangle-|10\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad
|t\rangle=\frac{|01\rangle+|10\rangle-2|11\rangle}{\sqrt6}.
$$
Then
$$
U=|u\rangle\langle p|+|v\rangle\langle m|
+|w\rangle\langle01|+|t\rangle\langle11|
$$
maps one <orthonormal basis> to another and is therefore <unitary>. Since $|00\rangle=c|p\rangle+s|m\rangle$ and $|+0\rangle=c|p\rangle-s|m\rangle$, it has exactly the required two outputs.

Finally,
$$
\langle a|c_0\rangle=\langle b|c_+\rangle
=\cos\frac\pi6\cos\frac\pi8+\sin\frac\pi6\sin\frac\pi8
=\cos\frac\pi{24}.
$$
Both inputs therefore have the same <global cloning fidelity>, and so does their average:
$$
\boxed{F_0=F_+=\overline F=\cos^2\frac\pi{24}\approx0.9829629131.}
$$
This proves the requested existence with a two-<qubit> <unitary gate>.
