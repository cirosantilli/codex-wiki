Source: https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/request_for_mathematics_examinat/response/3129786/attach/3/FOI%20202507%20740%20Data.pdf#page=317[released examination PDF, bundle pages 317–320; paper pages 1–4]. Numbering follows the original paper. In Question 1(c), the printed count needs a strict-cutoff qualification: at $ak=(n-1/2)\pi$ the newly counted state has no exterior <exponential decay> and must be excluded. The solution includes that correction and the exceptional small-parameter balances. In Question 3(d), purely <elastic unloading> is valid only until <reverse yielding> begins.

= Solution
{parent=a}

For <antiplane shear>, the <displacement field> is $\mathbf u=(0,0,w(x,y,t))$, independent of $z$. Its <divergence> is zero. Consequently the only possibly nonzero components of the <stress tensor> are the <shear stresses>
$$
\tau_{xz}=\tau_{zx}=\mu w_x,\qquad
\tau_{yz}=\tau_{zy}=\mu w_y.
$$
The $x$ and $y$ components of the <Navier-Cauchy equation> vanish identically. Its $z$ component gives $\rho w_{tt}=\partial_x(\mu w_x)+\partial_y(\mu w_y)$. Each material has constant <shear modulus> and <mass density>, so the required <wave equation> and <wave speeds> are
$$
\boxed{w_{j,tt}=c_j^2(w_{j,xx}+w_{j,yy}),\qquad
c_j=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_j}{\rho_j}},\quad j=1,2.}
$$

At a <perfectly bonded elastic interface>, both the <displacement field> and the <traction> are continuous. Using the same normal $\mathbf e_y$ on both sides, the only nonzero <traction> component is $\tau_{zy}$. Thus the interface <boundary conditions> are
$$
\boxed{w_1(x,h,t)=w_2(x,h,t),\qquad
\mu_1w_{1,y}(x,h,t)=\mu_2w_{2,y}(x,h,t).}
$$
<Continuity> of “stress” here means <continuity> of <traction>; it does not require equality of the full <stress tensors>. The rigid plate supplies the remaining <boundary condition> $w_1(x,0,t)=0$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Substitution of the proposed <normal mode> into the <wave equation> gives the <ordinary differential equations>
$$
f_j''+k^2\left(\frac{c^2}{c_j^2}-1\right)f_j=0.
$$
A nonzero exterior <displacement field> has <exponential decay> at infinity only when $c<c_2$. With
$$
\ell=k\sqrt{1-c^2/c_2^2}>0,
$$
its <wave amplitude> is $f_2(y)=D e^{-\ell(y-h)}$. At $c=c_2$ the exterior <wave amplitude> is affine and cannot decay; at $c>c_2$ it is oscillatory and cannot decay. Setting the exterior <wave amplitude> identically zero would impose both $f_1(h)=0$ and $f_1'(h)=0$, forcing the entire <normal mode> to vanish.

We also need $c>c_1$. Indeed, if $c<c_1$, the rigid <boundary condition> gives $f_1(y)=A\sinh(qy)$ with $q=k\sqrt{1-c^2/c_1^2}>0$. At the interface,
$$
\frac{f_1'(h)}{f_1(h)}=q\coth(qh)>0,
\qquad
\frac{f_2'(h)}{f_2(h)}=-\ell<0,
$$
which contradicts <continuity> of <traction> because both values of the <shear modulus> are positive. At $c=c_1$, the layer <wave amplitude> is $Ay$ and the same contradiction follows from $f_1'(h)/f_1(h)=1/h>0$.

Therefore $c_1<c<c_2$. Define the positive transverse <wavenumber>
$$
m=k\sqrt{c^2/c_1^2-1}.
$$
The rigid <boundary condition> now gives $f_1(y)=A\sin(my)$. The interface <boundary conditions> give
$$
D=A\sin(mh),\qquad
\mu_1mA\cos(mh)=-\mu_2\ell A\sin(mh).
$$
Neither $\sin(mh)$ nor $\cos(mh)$ can vanish for a nontrivial <normal mode> with $m,\ell>0$. Dividing therefore gives the <rigid-backed shear-wave dispersion> relation:
$$
\boxed{\frac{c^2}{c_1^2}=1+\frac{m^2}{k^2},\qquad
\frac{c^2}{c_2^2}=1-\frac{\ell^2}{k^2},\qquad
\tan(mh)=-\frac{\mu_1m}{\mu_2\ell}.}
$$
Conversely, positive $m,\ell$ satisfying these relations generate a nonzero <normal mode> through the <wave amplitudes> above. Its physical <displacement field> is the real part of the complex expression.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Eliminating the <phase velocity> from the first two relations yields
$$
\ell^2=k^2\left(1-\frac{c_1^2}{c_2^2}\right)-\frac{c_1^2}{c_2^2}m^2.
$$
With $\theta=mh$, put
$$
\boxed{a=h\sqrt{\frac{c_2^2}{c_1^2}-1}
=h\sqrt{\frac{\mu_2\rho_1}{\mu_1\rho_2}-1},\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{\mu_1c_2}{\mu_2c_1}
=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_1\rho_1}{\mu_2\rho_2}}.}
$$
Then $\ell=(c_1/(c_2h))\sqrt{a^2k^2-\theta^2}$. The <dispersion relation> becomes
$$
\boxed{\tan\theta=-\frac{\epsilon\theta}{\sqrt{A^2-\theta^2}},\qquad
0<\theta<A,\qquad A=ak.}
$$
Here $a>0$ follows from $c_1<c_2$. The parameter $a$ has dimensions of length; $A$ and $\epsilon$ are dimensionless.

For the <strict mode count for a rigid-backed elastic layer>, consider
$$
g(\theta)=-\frac{\epsilon\theta}{\sqrt{A^2-\theta^2}},\qquad
g'(\theta)=-\frac{\epsilon A^2}{(A^2-\theta^2)^{3/2}}<0.
$$
The right side is negative, so intersections occur only on the negative branches of the <tangent>,
$$
p_n<\theta<n\pi,\qquad p_n=(n-\tfrac12)\pi,\quad n=1,2,\ldots.
$$
On each such branch $\tan\theta-g(\theta)$ is strictly increasing. If $p_n<A$, its limit at $p_n^+$ is negative infinity. At the other end of the available interval, it is positive: either $\theta\to n\pi^-$ with $n\pi<A$, or $\theta\to A^-$ with $A\leq n\pi$. The <intermediate value theorem> and strict <monotonicity> give exactly one intersection. No such interval exists when $p_n\geq A$.

\Image[2023-root-count.png]
{title=Intersections of the negative tangent branches with the interface loading curve. A branch beginning exactly at the cutoff contributes no trapped mode.}

Thus the exact answer, including all cutoff values, is
$$
\boxed{N=\#\{n\geq1:(n-\tfrac12)\pi<A\}
=\left\lceil\frac{A}{\pi}-\frac12\right\rceil.}
$$
Away from $A=(n-1/2)\pi$, this equals the printed <floor function> expression $\lfloor A/\pi+1/2\rfloor$. At $A=(n-1/2)\pi$, the printed expression is one too large: the $n$th interval is empty and the limiting state has $\ell=0$, contradicting <exponential decay>. This exception is present in the native paper, rather than introduced by transcription.

Now hold $A$ fixed and let $\epsilon\to0^+$. Neglecting the right side gives $\theta_n\simeq n\pi$ and hence the candidate <phase velocities>
$$
\boxed{c_n\simeq c_1\sqrt{1+\frac{n^2\pi^2}{k^2h^2}}.}
$$
The <zero> $\theta=0$ is excluded: it would require $c=c_1$, already ruled out. For every $n\pi<A$, the candidate is a valid leading <asymptotic expansion>. A first correction, obtained by expanding the <tangent> about $n\pi$, is
$$
\theta_n=n\pi-\frac{\epsilon n\pi}{\sqrt{A^2-n^2\pi^2}}+O(\epsilon^2).
$$
The <big O notation> here assumes a fixed positive gap between $n\pi$ and $A$. In particular, when $A>N\pi$, all $N$ leading approximations are valid. The <phase velocity> increases strictly with $\theta$, so only the last branch can cause an endpoint difficulty.

If $(N-1/2)\pi<A<N\pi$, the formal last <equation root> $N\pi$ lies outside the allowed interval. The actual last <equation root> approaches $A$, where the small factor $\epsilon$ is balanced by the vanishing denominator. This is a <singular perturbation>. For fixed $A$ strictly inside that interval, write $\theta_N=A-\delta$. Since $\tan A<0$,
$$
\tan A\sim-\frac{\epsilon A}{\sqrt{2A\delta}},\qquad
\boxed{\delta\sim\frac{\epsilon^2A}{2\tan^2A},\quad
\theta_N=A-\frac{\epsilon^2A}{2\tan^2A}+O(\epsilon^4).}
$$
The valid <endpoint approximation for a rigid-backed shear mode> consequently gives
$$
\boxed{c_N=c_2\left[1-\frac{\epsilon^2}{2\tan^2A}
\left(1-\frac{c_1^2}{c_2^2}\right)+O(\epsilon^4)\right].}
$$
Here we used $c^2=c_1^2(1+\theta^2/(kh)^2)$ and $A^2/(kh)^2=c_2^2/c_1^2-1$. Thus this <normal mode> approaches the upper <wave speed> from below; it does not approach the inadmissible value obtained from $\theta=N\pi>A$.

There is a further exceptional fixed value $A=N\pi$. Put $\theta_N=A-\delta$ again. Now $\tan(A-\delta)\sim-\delta$, so the balance changes to
$$
\delta\sqrt{2A\delta}\sim\epsilon A,\qquad
\boxed{\delta\sim\left(\frac{\epsilon^2A}{2}\right)^{1/3},\qquad
c_N\sim c_2\left[1-\left(1-\frac{c_1^2}{c_2^2}\right)\frac{\delta}{A}\right].}
$$
In the last formula the bracket specifies the first nonzero correction. The zeroth-order candidate $c_N=c_2$ is correct as a limit but misses this correction and the positive exterior <exponential decay> rate. More generally, close to $A=N\pi$, let $\Delta=A-N\pi$ and $\theta_N=N\pi-\delta$. For $|\Delta|,\delta\ll1$, a transition approximation valid on both sides is
$$
\boxed{\delta^2(\Delta+\delta)\simeq\frac{\epsilon^2N\pi}{2},\qquad
\delta>0,\quad\Delta+\delta>0,\qquad
c_N\simeq c_1\sqrt{1+\frac{(N\pi-\delta)^2}{(kh)^2}}.}
$$
It recovers the $\epsilon^{2/3}$ balance at $\Delta=0$ and the ordinary expansion above the transition. The fixed-endpoint formula involving $\tan A$ should be used when $A$ is below, and bounded away from, $N\pi$.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Work to first order in the <slope> $w'$ and take $B$ constant. The <elastic beam> is slender, its cross-sections remain plane and normal to its centreline, transverse shear deformation is neglected, and the loading is quasi-static. There are no distributed couples. In a non-contact interval the distributed <force> is $-p\mathbf e_z$ and there is no axial distributed <force>. These are the assumptions of the small-deformation <Euler-Bernoulli beam equation>, with an optional prescribed <tension> retained in transverse <force balance>.

Choose the internal section resultant on the right face of a segment as
$$
\mathbf F=T\mathbf e_x+(Tw'+N)\mathbf e_z,
$$
where $N$ is the <transverse shear force>, and take the section <bending moment> as $M\mathbf e_y$. Longitudinal and transverse <force balance> on a segment of length $dx$ give $T'=0$ and $(Tw'+N)'=p$. Balancing the <torques> about its left end gives $M'=N$: the <torque> from the transverse section resultant is $-N\,dx\,\mathbf e_y$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{T'=0,\qquad Tw''+N'=p,\qquad M'=N.}
$$
With this positive-$y$ <bending moment> convention, the linear relation between <bending moment> and <curvature> is $M=-Bw''$. Thus $N=-Bw'''$, and substitution gives
$$
\boxed{Bw''''-Tw''+p=0.}
$$
The <tension> need not be small compared with the transverse restoring terms, but products arising solely from finite deformation, such as the change of axial extension proportional to $(w')^2$, are outside this linear model. A separate axial constitutive constraint would be required to determine an unknown <tension> from fixed end positions.

In an open contact interval, the upward distributed <normal contact force> is $R$. At this order the smooth obstacle supplies no axial <force>; its vertical resultant changes the downward load to $p-R$. Hence
$$
\boxed{Bw''''-Tw''+p=R.}
$$
For nonadhesive <unilateral beam contact>, the obstacle can push but cannot pull, so $R\geq0$. The accompanying constraints are $w\geq f$ and $(w-f)R=0$. A negative $R$ would require adhesion and would invalidate the proposed contact region. If $f$ is four times differentiable, the distributed <normal contact force> inside an open contact interval is $R=Bf''''-Tf''+p$.

= Solution
{parent=b}

The <jump conditions> express a connected <elastic beam> with no concentrated horizontal <force> or applied couple. <Continuity> of <tension>, $[T]=0$, follows from horizontal <force balance>. <Continuity> of the <displacement field> and <slope>, $[w]=[w']=0$, excludes a break or a kink; a kink would also have unbounded bending <elastic energy> in this model. <Continuity> of the <bending moment> in the absence of a point couple gives $[Bw'']=0$, or $[w'']=0$ for constant $B$.

The only allowed discontinuous section resultant is the <transverse shear force>. Integrating vertical <force balance> through an upward <point force> $R_i$ gives $[N]=-R_i$. Since $N=-Bw'''$, this is precisely
$$
\boxed{[Bw''']=R_i.}
$$
The same sign follows by integrating $Bw''''-Tw''+p=R_i\delta(x-x_i)$, where $\delta$ is the <Dirac delta function> and the regular terms have vanishing integrals over a shrinking interval. For nonadhesive <unilateral beam contact>, the required constraint is
$$
\boxed{R_i\geq0.}
$$
A negative <point force> would pull the <elastic beam> towards the obstacle. At an endpoint where $R_i=0$, the third <derivative> is continuous as well.

= Solution
{parent=i}

With zero <tension> and zero distributed load, a free interval satisfies $w''''=0$. Before contact the entire <elastic beam> is free, so $w$ is a cubic <polynomial>. The four end <boundary conditions> determine it uniquely. Reflection $x\mapsto-x$ preserves those <boundary conditions>, so the unique solution is an <even function> and its odd terms vanish. Direct substitution gives
$$
\boxed{w(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2L}(x^2-L^2).}
$$
For $\alpha\geq0$, its minimum occurs at $x=0$ and is $-\alpha L/2$. The constraint $w\geq-H$ is therefore satisfied exactly when $\alpha\leq2H/L$. The first contact is at the midpoint:
$$
\boxed{\alpha_{\mathrm{first}}=\frac{2H}{L}.}
$$
At equality the <normal contact force> is still zero. The uniqueness used here can also be checked by integrating $v''''v$ for the difference of two solutions with homogeneous <clamped boundary conditions>, which gives $\int_{-L}^L(v'')^2\,dx=0$ and hence $v=0$.

{parent=c}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

Seek an <even function> with isolated contact at $x=0$. On $0\leq x\leq L$, the contact and reflection conditions impose $w(0)=-H$ and $w'(0)=0$, while the free interval still has $w''''=0$. Write
$$
w(x)=-H+A_2x^2+A_3x^3.
$$
The two end <boundary conditions> give
$$
A_2L^2+A_3L^3=H,\qquad
2A_2L+3A_3L^2=\alpha,
$$
and therefore the full <displacement field> is
$$
\boxed{w(x)=-H+\frac{3H-\alpha L}{L^2}x^2
+\frac{\alpha L-2H}{L^3}|x|^3.}
$$
The <jump condition> at zero gives the upward <point force>
$$
\boxed{R_0=B[w''']_0=\frac{12B}{L^3}(\alpha L-2H).}
$$
It is nonnegative precisely when $\alpha\geq2H/L$. In that range $A_3\geq0$. Nonpenetration near zero requires $A_2\geq0$, equivalently $\alpha\leq3H/L$. Conversely, when both inequalities hold,
$$
w(x)+H=x^2(A_2+A_3|x|)\geq0,
$$
with strict inequality away from zero. Thus this is an admissible isolated-contact solution throughout
$$
\boxed{\frac{2H}{L}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{3H}{L}.}
$$
At the upper endpoint the contact <curvature> $w''(0)=2A_2$ vanishes, and the profile remains above the obstacle for $x\ne0$ because $A_3>0$. Increasing $\alpha$ further would make $A_2<0$, forcing penetration near zero. An interval of contact must then develop.

The admissible solution is unique within this linear model: the bending <elastic energy> $B\int(w'')^2/2$ is strictly convex on the <convex set> of admissible profiles with the prescribed end data. Integration by parts shows that a solution with nonnegative contact <point forces> satisfies the corresponding minimum condition, since every admissible variation lifts a point already on the obstacle. Reflection therefore justifies the assumed <even function> also after contact.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Let the contact interval be $[-s,s]$, where $w=-H$. Its <slope> and <curvature> vanish. <Continuity> of the <displacement field>, <slope> and <bending moment> at $x=s$ gives
$$
w(s)=-H,\qquad w'(s)=0,\qquad w''(s)=0.
$$
On the free interval $s<x<L$, the cubic <polynomial> satisfying these three conditions is $w=-H+C(x-s)^3$. Put $d=L-s$. The end <boundary conditions> become
$$
Cd^3=H,\qquad3Cd^2=\alpha.
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{d=\frac{3H}{\alpha},\qquad
s=L-\frac{3H}{\alpha},\qquad
C=\frac{H}{d^3}.}
$$
This requires $\alpha\geq3H/L$, with $s=0$ at the transition. The full <displacement field> is
$$
\boxed{w(x)=
\begin{cases}
-H,&|x|\leq s,\\
-H+H\left(\dfrac{|x|-s}{L-s}\right)^3,&s\leq|x|\leq L.
\end{cases}}
$$
It satisfies nonpenetration. In the open contact interval the distributed <normal contact force> is $R=Bw''''=0$, since the obstacle is flat and $p=T=0$. The <normal contact force> is concentrated at the two endpoints. At $x=s$, $w'''$ jumps from zero to $6H/d^3$; at $x=-s$, it jumps from $-6H/d^3$ to zero. Both upward <point forces> are therefore
$$
\boxed{R_{-s}=R_s=\frac{6BH}{d^3}>0.}
$$
At $s=0$ they merge into the single midpoint <point force> of the preceding regime. These formulae belong to the small-<slope> model; interpreting arbitrarily large $\alpha$ as a finite physical rotation would exceed its assumptions.

= Solution
{parent=iv}

The net upward <force> is the sum of the contact <point forces>, since the distributed <normal contact force> vanishes in every regime here. Before contact it is zero. During isolated contact it is $R_0=12B(\alpha L-2H)/L^3$. For an interval of contact it is $R_{-s}+R_s=12BH/d^3$, with $d=3H/\alpha$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{F_{\mathrm{up}}(\alpha)=
\begin{cases}
0,&0\leq\alpha\leq2H/L,\\
\dfrac{12B}{L^2}\left(\alpha-\dfrac{2H}{L}\right),
&2H/L\leq\alpha\leq3H/L,\\
\dfrac{4B\alpha^3}{9H^2},&\alpha\geq3H/L.
\end{cases}}
$$
The formulae agree at both transitions. At $\alpha=3H/L$ they give $12BH/L^3$, and their first <derivatives> also agree there. As a check on the sign and normalization, integrating the <Euler-Bernoulli beam equation> over the whole <elastic beam> gives $F_{\mathrm{up}}=B(w'''(L^-)-w'''(-L^+))$, which yields the same values.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Assume homogeneous <Lamé parameters>, with $\mu>0$ and positive <bulk modulus> $K=\lambda+2\mu/3$. For <spherical symmetry>, write $q=\tau_{rr}$ and $t=\tau_{\theta\theta}=\tau_{\phi\phi}$. Radial <force balance> is $q'+2(q-t)/r=0$. The given <linear elasticity> relations imply
$$
q-t=2\mu\left(u'-\frac ur\right),\qquad
q'=(\lambda+2\mu)u''+2\lambda\left(\frac{u'}r-\frac{u}{r^2}\right).
$$
Substitution into radial <force balance> gives
$$
0=(\lambda+2\mu)\left(u''+\frac{2u'}r-\frac{2u}{r^2}\right)
=\frac{d}{dr}\left[(\lambda+2\mu)\left(u'+\frac{2u}r\right)\right].
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dr}\left[(\lambda+2\mu)\left(u'+\frac{2u}r\right)\right]=0.}
$$
The <volumetric strain> is $e=u'+2u/r$, and the <trace> of the <stress tensor> is
$$
q+2t=(3\lambda+2\mu)e.
$$
Since $\lambda+2\mu=K+4\mu/3>0$ is constant, $e'=0$. The <radial elastic compatibility> condition follows:
$$
\boxed{\frac{d}{dr}(\tau_{rr}+\tau_{\theta\theta}+\tau_{\phi\phi})=0.}
$$
This condition is local to a region governed by homogeneous <linear elasticity>; it is not a constitutive condition for a plastically deforming region.

{parent=3}

= Solution
{parent=b}

In the completely elastic exterior, <radial elastic compatibility> makes $q+2t$ constant. The far-field <stress> vanishes, so $q+2t=0$ and $t=-q/2$. Radial <force balance> then gives $q'+3q/r=0$, whence $q=C/r^3$. The <pressure> <boundary condition> is $q(a)=-P$: positive applied <pressure> is compressive. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\tau_{rr}=-P\frac{a^3}{r^3},\qquad
\tau_{\theta\theta}=\tau_{\phi\phi}=\frac P2\frac{a^3}{r^3}.}
$$
For completeness, the corresponding radial <displacement field> is $u=Pa^3/(4\mu r^2)$, which gives these components through <Hooke's law> and decays at infinity.

The <Tresca yield criterion> becomes
$$
f(r)=\frac12|q-t|=\frac{3P}{4}\frac{a^3}{r^3}.
$$
For $P\geq0$ this is largest at the cavity wall and decreases strictly with radius when $P>0$. The first <yield point> is therefore at $r=a$, where $3P/4=\tau_Y$. Hence
$$
\boxed{P_c=\frac43\tau_Y,\qquad r_{\mathrm{first}}=a.}
$$
The normalization matters: $\tau_Y$ in this paper is a shear <yield stress>, rather than the uniaxial <yield strength>.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

During increasing <pressure>, the elastic solution reaches the <Tresca yield criterion> with $t-q>0$. In the plastic shell of a material with <perfect plasticity>, this sign is retained and
$$
t-q=2\tau_Y.
$$
Radial <force balance> becomes $q'-4\tau_Y/r=0$. Integrating from the cavity wall, where $q(a)=-P$, gives
$$
q=-P+4\tau_Y\log(r/a),\qquad t=q+2\tau_Y,
\qquad a<r<s.
$$
Outside the plastic shell, <radial elastic compatibility> and vanishing far-field <stress> again give $q=-C/r^3$ and $t=C/(2r^3)$. At the advancing plastic front $r=s$, the <Tresca yield criterion> gives $3C/(4s^3)=\tau_Y$, so $C=P_cs^3$. <Continuity> of radial <traction> now gives
$$
-P+4\tau_Y\log(s/a)=-P_c.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{s=a\exp\left(\frac{P-P_c}{4\tau_Y}\right).}
$$
The complete <stress> field of this <spherical elastoplastic cavity> is
$$
\boxed{\tau_{rr}=
\begin{cases}
-P+4\tau_Y\log(r/a),&a\leq r\leq s,\\
-P_c(s/r)^3,&r\geq s,
\end{cases}
\qquad
\tau_{\theta\theta}=\tau_{\phi\phi}=
\begin{cases}
-P+4\tau_Y\log(r/a)+2\tau_Y,&a\leq r\leq s,\\
\tfrac12P_c(s/r)^3,&r\geq s.
\end{cases}}
$$
At $s$, the tangential <stress> also matches because $-P_c+2\tau_Y=P_c/2$. Outside $s$, the function in the <Tresca yield criterion> is $\tau_Y(s/r)^3<\tau_Y$, which verifies that the remaining material is elastic. As $P\downarrow P_c$, $s\downarrow a$ and the solution joins the entirely elastic regime. The radius $a$ is the fixed reference radius of the small-<strain> model used by the paper.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Let
$$
s_m=a\exp\left(\frac{P_m-P_c}{4\tau_Y}\right),\qquad
\Delta=P_m-P,\qquad0\leq\Delta\leq P_m.
$$
At maximum <pressure>, the <stress> is the field from part (c) with $P=P_m$ and $s=s_m$. Under the assumed <elastic unloading>, previously accumulated plastic <strain> is frozen. Therefore it is the <stress> increment, rather than the total <stress>, that satisfies homogeneous <linear elasticity> and <radial elastic compatibility>. Applying the <superposition principle>, a <pressure> change $-\Delta$ produces the elastic increments
$$
\delta q=\Delta\frac{a^3}{r^3},\qquad
\delta t=-\frac{\Delta}{2}\frac{a^3}{r^3}.
$$
They have zero far-field <stress> and change the wall <traction> from $-P_m$ to $-P$. These increments are the same in the formerly plastic and formerly elastic regions because their elastic <Lamé parameters> agree. Adding them to the maximum-load field gives
$$
\boxed{q(r;P)=
\begin{cases}
-P_m+4\tau_Y\log(r/a)+\Delta(a/r)^3,&a\leq r\leq s_m,\\
-P_c(s_m/r)^3+\Delta(a/r)^3,&r\geq s_m,
\end{cases}}
$$
and
$$
\boxed{t(r;P)=\tau_{\phi\phi}(r;P)=
\begin{cases}
-P_m+4\tau_Y\log(r/a)+2\tau_Y-\tfrac12\Delta(a/r)^3,&a\leq r\leq s_m,\\
\tfrac12P_c(s_m/r)^3-\tfrac12\Delta(a/r)^3,&r\geq s_m.
\end{cases}}
$$
These formulae retain $s_m$: it records the plastic loading history and is not the instantaneous loading-front formula with $P$ substituted for $P_m$. If the entire return is elastic, setting $\Delta=P_m$ gives the <residual stress> after removal of the applied <pressure>.

To check admissibility, examine the signed difference of the <principal stresses> in the formerly plastic shell:
$$
D(r)=q-t=-2\tau_Y+\frac{3\Delta}{2}\left(\frac ar\right)^3.
$$
It immediately moves into the interior of the <Tresca yield criterion> when unloading starts. It can next reach the opposite limit $D=+2\tau_Y$. For fixed $\Delta>0$, $D$ is largest at $r=a$ and stays above $-2\tau_Y$. At the wall,
$$
-2\tau_Y+\frac32\Delta=2\tau_Y
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Delta=\frac83\tau_Y=2P_c.
$$
There is no earlier violation in the outer region. Indeed,
$$
D(r)=D(s_m)\left(\frac{s_m}{r}\right)^3,\qquad r\geq s_m,
$$
so its magnitude is no larger than $|D(s_m)|$, which has already been controlled by the shell calculation. Hence the first <reverse yielding> occurs at the cavity wall, with
$$
\boxed{\Delta_{\mathrm{reverse}}=2P_c=\frac83\tau_Y,\qquad
P_{\mathrm{reverse}}=P_m-2P_c.}
$$
If $P_c<P_m<2P_c$, the <stress> stays within the <yield surface> throughout unloading to zero. If $P_m=2P_c$, it reaches reverse yield exactly at $P=0$; further unloading would produce reverse plastic deformation. If $P_m>2P_c$, reverse plastic deformation starts while the applied <pressure> is still positive. Thus
$$
\boxed{\text{reverse yielding before zero pressure occurs iff }P_m>\frac83\tau_Y.}
$$
If “yield again while unloaded” includes first touching the <yield surface> at the final endpoint, the corresponding condition is $P_m\geq8\tau_Y/3$. The displayed <elastic unloading> fields are physically valid only for $0\leq\Delta\leq\min(P_m,2P_c)$; beyond that interval a new reverse-plastic region must be solved for. Continuing the elastic formula to $P=0$ when $P_m>2P_c$ would violate the given <Tresca yield criterion>.
