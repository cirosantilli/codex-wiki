= Solution
{parent=a}

The <infinitesimal strain tensor>[infinitesimal strain] is $e_{ij}=(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i})/2$. A constant <translation> has zero <gradient>. For the remaining term, $u_i=\epsilon_{ikl}\omega_kx_l$ has $u_{i,j}=\epsilon_{ikj}\omega_k$, a <skew-symmetric matrix>. Therefore
$$
\boxed{e_{ij}=0.}
$$
Physically $\mathbf a$ is a <translation> and $\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf x$ is an infinitesimal <rigid motion>[rigid rotation], with axis along $\boldsymbol\omega$ and <rotation> angle $|\boldsymbol\omega|$. It is the linearized <rotation> <displacement>, not the exact <displacement> of a general finite <rotation>. This distinction matters because the <infinitesimal strain tensor>[infinitesimal strain] omits quadratic <displacement>-<gradient> terms.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Choose zero energy in the unstrained state. The required <strain energy density> is
$$
\boxed{W(e)=\frac\lambda2(\operatorname{tr}e)^2+\mu e:e.}
$$
Its <variation> is $\delta W=(\lambda\operatorname{tr}(e)I+2\mu e):\delta e$, giving the prescribed <stress> as the <tensor> <gradient>. This variational notation handles the symmetric off-diagonal components without a spurious factor of two.

Decompose $e=e^{\rm dev}+(\operatorname{tr}e)I/3$ into <orthogonal> deviatoric and spherical parts. Then
$$
\boxed{W=\mu\,e^{\rm dev}:e^{\rm dev}+\frac{3\lambda+2\mu}{6}(\operatorname{tr}e)^2.}
$$
The two squares vanish together only for $e=0$. Thus $\mu>0$ and $3\lambda+2\mu>0$ are sufficient for <positive definiteness>. They are necessary by testing a nonzero <trace>-free <strain> and a pure <dilation> separately. Equivalently both <shear modulus> $\mu$ and <bulk modulus> $K=\lambda+2\mu/3$ must be positive.

= Solution
{parent=c}

Multiply <equilibrium> by the <displacement> and integrate. <Integration by parts> and the <traction> condition give
$$
0=\int_B\mathbf u\cdot(\nabla\cdot T+\rho\mathbf g)\,dV
=\int_{\partial B}\mathbf u\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\,dS-\int_BT:\nabla\mathbf u\,dV+\int_B\rho\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf g\,dV.
$$
The <stress> is symmetric, so it contracts only with the symmetric part of $\nabla\mathbf u$, namely $e$. <Hooke's law> then gives $T:e=\lambda(\operatorname{tr}e)^2+2\mu e:e=2W$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\int_B\rho\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf g\,dV=\int_B2W\,dV-\int_{\partial B}\mathbf u\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\,dS.}
$$
The identity is the linear-elastic work balance: twice the stored quadratic energy equals the body-force and boundary-<traction> work paired with the final <displacement>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Apply <energy uniqueness for a linear elastic traction problem>. Subtract two solutions with the same loading, writing their <displacement> difference as $\mathbf v$. Linearity makes its <body force> and <traction>[boundary traction] zero. The preceding energy identity therefore yields $\int_B2W(e(\mathbf v))\,dV=0$. <Positive definiteness> and continuity imply $e(\mathbf v)=0$ everywhere. By the permitted zero-strain characterization,
$$
\boxed{\mathbf v=\mathbf a+\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf x.}
$$
Thus the solution is unique modulo infinitesimal rigid <translations> and <rotations>, on each connected body. Conversely adding such a <displacement> changes neither <strain> nor <stress>, so these are exactly the unconstrained modes. This is conditional <uniqueness>; existence of a pure-<traction> solution additionally requires balanced resultant force and <torque>.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Use the <torsion stress function for a simply connected bar>. The only nonzero <strain> components are $e_{xz}=(w_x-\Omega y)/2$ and $e_{yz}=(w_y+\Omega x)/2$. Thus
$$
\tau_{xz}=\mu(w_x-\Omega y),\qquad\tau_{yz}=\mu(w_y+\Omega x).
$$
The axial <equilibrium> equation is $\partial_x\tau_{xz}+\partial_y\tau_{yz}=0$. Since the section is <simply connected>, the planar <divergence-free> <stress> field has a <stream function>:
$$
\boxed{\tau_{xz}=\phi_y,\quad\tau_{yz}=-\phi_x,\quad w_x=\Omega y+\phi_y/\mu,\quad w_y=-\Omega x-\phi_x/\mu.}
$$
Equality of the mixed <torsional warping>[warping] derivatives gives $\boxed{\Delta\phi=-2\mu\Omega}$. On the lateral boundary the axial <traction> is $\phi_y n_x-\phi_xn_y$, the tangential derivative of $\phi$. <Traction>-free walls therefore make $\phi$ constant on the connected boundary, and its arbitrary additive constant can be chosen so $\boxed{\phi=0\text{ on }\partial D}$.

The axial <torque> is
$$
M=\int_D(x\tau_{yz}-y\tau_{xz})\,dA=-\int_D(x\phi_x+y\phi_y)\,dA=\boxed{2\int_D\phi\,dA}.
$$
The <divergence theorem> removes the boundary term because $\phi=0$.

Tresca's yield criterion requires the maximum <shear stress>, half the largest principal-<stress> difference, not to exceed the material's shear <yield stress> $\tau_Y$. The <stress> <eigenvalues> here are $0,\pm\sqrt{\tau_{xz}^2+\tau_{yz}^2}$, so the maximum shear is $|\nabla\phi|$ and
$$
\boxed{|\nabla\phi|\leq\tau_Y.}
$$
If yield is instead quoted as a uniaxial tensile <stress>, that parameter is $2\tau_Y$; the present convention is the source's shear parameter.

For the <ellipse>, the proposed quadratic vanishes on its boundary. Its Laplacian fixes
$$
\boxed{C=\frac{\mu\Omega a^2b^2}{a^2+b^2},\qquad\phi=C(1-x^2/a^2-y^2/b^2).}
$$
The corresponding <torsional warping>[warping] is $w=\Omega(b^2-a^2)xy/(a^2+b^2)+\text{constant}$. Elliptic polar coordinates $x=ar\cos\vartheta$, $y=br\sin\vartheta$ give $\int_D\phi=C\pi ab/2$, hence
$$
\boxed{M=\frac{\pi\mu\Omega a^3b^3}{a^2+b^2},\qquad R=M/\Omega=\frac{\pi\mu a^3b^3}{a^2+b^2}.}
$$
To locate first yield, maximize $|\nabla\phi|=2|C|\sqrt{x^2/a^4+y^2/b^4}$. It increases radially to the boundary. For $a>b$, the maximum there occurs at $(0,\pm b)$ and equals $2|C|/b$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{|\Omega|_Y=\frac{(a^2+b^2)\tau_Y}{2a^2b\mu}.}
$$
The positive-twist value is the requested expression. The ends of the minor axis yield first, not the ends of the longer axis. This is <elliptical-bar torsion and first yield>.

= Solution
{parent=a}

For an admissible <variation> $\eta$ with $\eta(0)=\eta(L)=0$,
$$
\delta U=\int_0^L(EI\theta'\eta'-P\sin\theta\,\eta)\,ds=-\int_0^L(EI\theta''+P\sin\theta)\eta\,ds.
$$
Stationarity for every <variation> therefore gives the <Euler-Lagrange equation>
$$
\boxed{EI\theta''+P\sin\theta=0.}
$$
The first energy term is <bending energy>: the <curvature> of an inextensible planar beam is $\theta_s$. The shortening relative to the straight reference is $\int(1-\cos\theta)\,ds$. A prescribed compressive <dead load> does positive work on that shortening, so its potential-energy contribution is minus $P$ times that integral. A <minimizer> must satisfy the stationary equation, but a stationary solution need not be a <minimizer>.

= Solution

Set $\boxed{\xi=s/L,\quad\lambda=PL^2/(\pi^2EI)}$. The endpoint interval becomes $[0,1]$, and multiplication of the dimensional equation by $L^2/EI$ gives
$$
\boxed{\theta_{\xi\xi}+\pi^2\lambda\sin\theta=0,\qquad\theta(0)=\theta(1)=0.}
$$
The energy in these variables is $U=(EI/L)\int_0^1[\theta_\xi^2/2-\pi^2\lambda(1-\cos\theta)]\,d\xi$. Thus $\lambda$ measures load relative to the first linearized <eigenvalue> for the angular endpoint conditions actually imposed.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=c}

<Linearization> gives $\theta''+\pi^2\lambda\theta=0$. The two homogeneous Dirichlet conditions select $\boxed{\lambda=n^2,\quad\theta=A\sin(n\pi\xi),\quad n=1,2,\ldots}$. For any small trial <amplitude> in this mode, $\int_0^1\sin^2(n\pi\xi)=\int_0^1\cos^2(n\pi\xi)=1/2$, so
$$
\boxed{U[A\sin(n\pi\xi)]=\frac{\pi^2EIA^2}{4L}(n^2-\lambda)+O(A^4).}
$$
For $\lambda<1$ every nonzero quadratic <variation> is positive by the sharp <Poincare inequality>. For $\lambda>1$, the first mode gives negative energy at sufficiently small <amplitude>, so zero cannot be a <local minimum>.

**The printed strict “only if” conclusion omits the critical case.** At $\lambda=1$ the quadratic test is degenerate, not negative. In fact zero is still the unique minimum of the full displayed <functional>: use $1-\cos\theta\leq\theta^2/2$ and $\int\theta'^2\geq\pi^2\int\theta^2$ to get $U\geq0$, with strict inequality for a nonzero continuous function because $1-\cos t<t^2/2$ when $t\ne0$. In particular on the critical first mode,
$$
\boxed{U[A\sin\pi\xi]=\frac{\pi^2EI}{64L}A^4+O(A^6)>0\quad\text{for small }A\ne0.}
$$
Thus strict positivity of the <second variation> holds for $\lambda<1$, while local minimality of the full energy holds also at $\lambda=1$. This is the distinction captured by <critical-load minimality of the angular Euler strut>.

= Solution
{parent=d}

Near the first <eigenvalue>, load detuning supplies a term $\epsilon\lambda_1\theta$, while the first nonlinearity in $\sin\theta=\theta-\theta^3/6+\cdots$ is cubic. Balancing them gives $\theta^2=O(\epsilon)$, so $\boxed{\theta=O(\sqrt\epsilon)}$.

Put $\theta=\sqrt\epsilon[A_0\sin\pi\xi+\epsilon\Theta_1+\cdots]$. At order $\epsilon^{3/2}$,
$$
(\partial_\xi^2+\pi^2)\Theta_1=-\pi^2\lambda_1A_0\sin\pi\xi+\frac{\pi^2A_0^3}{6}\sin^3\pi\xi.
$$
The operator has <kernel> $\sin\pi\xi$, so Fredholm solvability requires its forcing to be <orthogonal> to that function. Since $\int\sin^2\pi\xi=1/2$ and $\int\sin^4\pi\xi=3/8$,
$$
-\lambda_1A_0/2+A_0^3/16=0,\qquad\boxed{A_0(A_0^2-8\lambda_1)=0.}
$$
When this holds the nonresonant forcing is $-\pi^2A_0^3\sin3\pi\xi/24$; for instance $\Theta_1=A_0^3\sin3\pi\xi/192$ is a solution after absorbing its arbitrary first-mode component into the <amplitude> convention.

The branches are $\boxed{A_0=0\text{ for all }\lambda_1,\qquad A_0=\pm\sqrt{8\lambda_1}\text{ for }\lambda_1\geq0}$. This is a <supercritical pitchfork bifurcation>. The leading reduced energy is proportional to $A_0^4-16\lambda_1A_0^2$: the nonzero branches are minima for $\lambda_1>0$, while zero loses minimality on that side. At the origin the quartic term still stabilizes the critical state.

= Solution
{parent=a}

Take $z$ positive upwards, so the source's sign convention makes $p$ a downward applied load. The small-<slope> <tension> contribution on a short interval is $Tw''$. Let $r\geq0$ be the upward contact reaction. Force balance gives $Tw''-p+r=0$. <Nonpenetration constraint>[Nonpenetration] is $w\geq f$, and reaction is absent where $w>f$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{w-f\geq0,\qquad Tw''-p=-r\leq0,\qquad(w-f)(Tw''-p)=0.}
$$
The contact boundary must have continuous $w$ to avoid a broken string, and continuous <slope> in this smooth-contact model with no concentrated point force. Integrating force balance over a vanishing interval around a contact endpoint gives $T[w']=0$ when the load and reaction are locally bounded. This is the one-dimensional <obstacle problem>, with its reaction acting as a nonnegative <constraint> force.

= b
{parent=4}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}

For any admissible $v$, put $q=v-w$. At endpoints $q=0$. On the <contact set of an obstacle problem>[contact set], $w=f$ and $v\geq f$ imply $q\geq0$, while away from contact the reaction $r=p-Tw''$ is zero. Hence $rq\geq0$ everywhere. <Integration by parts> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
T\int_{-1}^1w'(v'-w')\,dx+\int_{-1}^1p(v-w)\,dx
&=\int_{-1}^1(p-Tw'')(v-w)\,dx\\
&=\int_{-1}^1r(v-w)\,dx\geq0.
\end{aligned}
$$
Rearranging proves the requested <variational inequality>. Piecewise integration is legitimate because the <slope> is continuous at the contact boundary, so there are no omitted point-force terms.

= Solution
{parent=c}

The small-<slope> excess length is $\int(w')^2/2$, so the first term is the work needed against uniform <tension>. The second is the <potential energy> of the downward distributed load. Their sum is total <potential energy> subject to the obstacle <constraint>.

Expand the difference exactly:
$$
U[v]-U[w]=T\int w'(v'-w')\,dx+\int p(v-w)\,dx+\frac T2\int(v'-w')^2\,dx.
$$
The first two integrals together are nonnegative by part (b), and the square is nonnegative. Thus
$$
\boxed{U[w]\leq U[v]\quad\text{for every admissible }v.}
$$
For $T>0$ this also proves <uniqueness>: equality for two <minimizers> forces identical derivatives, and the fixed endpoints remove their additive constant. The <convex> admissible set and strictly <convex> energy make the <variational inequality> a global minimization condition.

= Solution
{parent=d}

With no applied load the free string segments are straight, while in contact $w=f=h-x^2$. By symmetry use contact $[-s,s]$ and a right-hand line through $(1,0)$ tangent at $s$. The <tangency> conditions are $w(s)=h-s^2$ and $w'(s)=-2s$, giving $w=2s(1-x)$ on $[s,1]$ and $h-s^2=2s(1-s)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{s=1-\sqrt{1-h},\qquad w(x)=\begin{cases}h-x^2,&|x|\leq s,\\2s(1-|x|),&s\leq|x|\leq1.\end{cases}}
$$
The other algebraic root $1+\sqrt{1-h}$ is outside the admissible contact interval except at the coincident endpoint $h=1$.

Verification makes the construction complete: the values and <slopes> match at $\pm s$, the endpoints are zero, and outside contact $w-f=(|x|-s)^2\geq0$. Inside contact $Tw''=-2T\leq0$; outside it $w''=0$, so <unilateral contact complementarity>[complementarity] holds. The minimization argument proves this verified solution is the unique <equilibrium>. At $h=0$ the <contact set of an obstacle problem>[contact set] is the single point $\{0\}$ and $w=0$; at $h=1$ contact fills the whole interval. This is the <parabolic obstacle under a taut string>.
