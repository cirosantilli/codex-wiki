= Linearized gravity
{wiki}

= Metric perturbation
{synonym}

Linearized gravity writes $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}$ with $|h_{\mu\nu}|\ll1$ and discards terms quadratic in $h$ and its derivatives.

= Gauge-invariant scalar-vector-tensor perturbations of Minkowski spacetime
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$\Phi=A-\dot B+\ddot E,\quad\Psi=C-\Delta E/3,\quad V_i=B_i-\dot E_i$}

= Gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbation
{synonym}

For signature $(+---)$, decompose a <metric perturbation> into $h_{00}=2A$, $h_{0i}=\partial_iB+B_i$ and $h_{ij}=2C\delta_{ij}+2(\partial_i\partial_j-\delta_{ij}\Delta/3)E+\partial_iE_j+\partial_jE_i+h_{ij}^{\rm TT}$, where the spatial vectors are divergence-free and $h^{\rm TT}$ is a <transverse-traceless tensor>. Under a passive coordinate shift $\xi^0=\tau$, $\xi^i=\partial_iL+L_i$, with $\partial_iL_i=0$, the changes are $\delta A=-\dot\tau$, $\delta B=\dot L-\tau$, $\delta C=\Delta L/3$, $\delta E=L$, $\delta B_i=\dot L_i$, and $\delta E_i=L_i$. The displayed combinations and $h^{\rm TT}$ are therefore gauge invariant. The decomposition is unique after appropriate spatial decay or mode restrictions; homogeneous and boundary modes require separate conventions.

= Vacuum constraints for gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbations
{parent=Gauge-invariant scalar-vector-tensor perturbations of Minkowski spacetime}
{title2=$\Delta\Psi=0,\quad\Delta V_i=0,\quad\Delta\Phi=0,\quad\Box h_{ij}^{\rm TT}=0$}

With the reversed convention in <curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities>, choose the representative $h_{00}=2\Phi$, $h_{0i}=V_i$, $h_{ij}=2\Psi\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}^{\rm TT}$. The linearized Einstein components are $G_{00}^{(1)}=-2\Delta\Psi$, $G_{0i}^{(1)}=-2\partial_i\dot\Psi-\Delta V_i/2$ and $G_{ij}^{(1)}=(\partial_i\partial_j-\delta_{ij}\Delta)(\Phi-\Psi)-2\delta_{ij}\ddot\Psi-(\partial_i\dot V_j+\partial_j\dot V_i)/2+\Box h_{ij}^{\rm TT}/2$. On all space with regular decaying fields, or for nonzero spatial Fourier modes, the vacuum constraints remove the scalar and vector parts, leaving two radiative <gravitational wave polarizations>. Singular Coulomb fields, spatial boundaries and homogeneous modes are not covered by that removal argument.

= Static scalar perturbation of Minkowski spacetime
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$ds^2=(1+2A)dt^2-(1+2C)\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j$}

For static scalar perturbations with no mixed time-space or spatial shear terms, the displayed <metric tensor> uses signature $(+---)$. With the reversed convention in <curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities>, its first-order <Einstein tensor> is $\delta G_{00}=2\Delta C$, $\delta G_{0i}=0$, and $\delta G_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j(A+C)-\delta_{ij}\Delta(A+C)$. These follow by substituting the metric into the first-order <Christoffel symbols> and <Ricci tensor>, discarding products of perturbations. A static source with the <dust stress-energy tensor> obeys $\Delta C=-4\pi G\rho$ and the decaying spatial equation makes $A=-C$. For a point mass the <Newtonian potential of a point mass> gives $A=-GM/r$, $C=GM/r$. The spatial potential is necessary to determine light propagation; a time potential alone is insufficient.

= Linearized Schwarzschild metric
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$ds^2=(1-r_S/r)dt^2-(1+r_S/r)dr^2-r^2d\Omega^2$}

For a static spherical point source, the weak-field solution in isotropic radius $\rho$ has $g_{tt}=1-2GM/\rho$ and spatial metric $-(1+2GM/\rho)(d\rho^2+\rho^2d\Omega^2)$. The <isotropic-to-areal weak-field radius change> gives the displayed metric with $r_S=2GM$. Its validity requires $r_S/r\ll1$. Treating it as an exact metric near $r\sim r_S$ can turn omitted terms into large corrections; it does not establish horizons or strong-field photon dynamics.

= Weak-field photon-orbit validity
{parent=Linearized Schwarzschild metric}
{title2=$u=r_S/r\ll1$}

The first-order null-orbit equation $(u')^2=r_S^2E^2/h^2-u^2(1-u)$ formally has a circular solution at $u=2/3$, with $|h/E|=3\sqrt3r_S/2$. Its second-order equation gives a perturbation $\delta u''=\delta u$, hence instability. However, $u=2/3$ is outside the weak-field expansion. The exact <Schwarzschild spacetime> has a corresponding unstable <photon sphere>, but that physical result needs the exact geometry; extrapolating the linearized metric is not a controlled proof.

= Isotropic-to-areal weak-field radius change
{parent=Linearized Schwarzschild metric}
{title2=$r=\rho+GM+O(G^2M^2/\rho)$}

The area of a symmetry sphere in the weak-field isotropic metric is $4\pi\rho^2(1+2GM/\rho)$. Equating it to $4\pi r^2$ defines the <areal radius> and gives the displayed change. To first order $dr=d\rho$, and replacing $1/\rho$ by $1/r$ changes the perturbation only at second order. This explains why the isotropic angular correction disappears in the <linearized Schwarzschild metric> without discarding spatial curvature.

= Quadratic gravitational effective stress-energy tensor
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$t_{\mu\nu}=-G^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}[h,h]/(8\pi)$}

= Quadratic gravitational stress-energy
{synonym}

Expand the <Einstein tensor> about the <Minkowski metric>. The quadratic term in the <metric perturbation> defines $t_{\mu\nu}=-G^{(2)}_{\mu\nu}[h,h]/(8\pi)$, a symmetric tensor relative to the background. For a first-order vacuum solution, the expanded <contracted Bianchi identity> gives $\partial^\mu t_{\mu\nu}=0$. Equivalently, a second-order correction $j$ obeys $G^{(1)}[j]=8\pi t$, whose flat divergence vanishes identically. This conservation is an on-shell statement. The local $t$ depends on perturbative gauge and the background split; conservation alone does not make it a gauge-independent local gravitational <stress-energy tensor>.

= Massless Fierz-Pauli action
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$\delta S=-\int d^4x\,G^{(1)}_{\alpha\beta}\delta h^{\alpha\beta}$}

A <massless Fierz-Pauli action> is the quadratic flat-background action for a symmetric metric perturbation that yields the vacuum <linearized Einstein equations>. One normalization, up to a total derivative, is
$$
\mathcal L=-\frac14\partial_\mu h^{\rho\sigma}\partial^\mu h_{\rho\sigma}
+\frac12A_\mu A^\mu-\frac12A^\mu\partial_\mu h
+\frac14\partial_\mu h\partial^\mu h,\qquad A_\mu=\partial_\nu h^\nu{}_\mu.
$$
Varying the symmetric field with compact support gives the displayed linearized <Einstein tensor> coefficient. Its linearized coordinate-gauge symmetry is consistent with the contracted <Bianchi identity>. It is the massless gravitational action, distinct from imposing the massive <Fierz-Pauli equations> with five polarizations.

= Linearized coordinate gauge transformation
{parent=Linearized gravity}
{title2=$h_{\mu\nu}\mapsto h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_\mu\xi_\nu-\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$}

An infinitesimal coordinate change generated by $\xi$ changes a metric perturbation about <Minkowski spacetime> by $h_{\mu\nu}\mapsto h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_\mu\xi_\nu-\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, in the passive sign convention. For a passive coordinate change $x^\mu\mapsto x^\mu+\xi^\mu$, the first-order change is minus the <Lie derivative of a tensor field> of the background metric, equivalently the pullback by the inverse flow. The <gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor> follows because the added terms have commuting third derivatives, which cancel in the curvature antisymmetrizations. Gauge choices alter the metric components while preserving linearized tidal effects.

= Affine transverse trace in a diagonal linearized plane wave is pure gauge
{parent=Linearized coordinate gauge transformation}

For a perturbation $h_{xx}=2a(t-z)$, $h_{yy}=2b(t-z)$ with all other components zero, the linearized <vacuum Einstein equations> give $(a+b)''=0$. Put $s=a+b$ and choose $\xi_x=sx/2$, $\xi_y=sy/2$, $\xi_t=-s'(x^2+y^2)/4$, $\xi_z=s'(x^2+y^2)/4$. Under the <linearized coordinate gauge transformation>, the mixed time/transverse and longitudinal/transverse components cancel, while the other time/longitudinal changes are proportional to $s''=0$. The remaining components are $h_{xx}=a-b$, $h_{yy}=-(a-b)$, $h_{xy}=0$, a <transverse-traceless gauge> perturbation of <plus polarization>. This explicitly removes a nonzero affine trace without assuming it vanishes by boundary conditions. If $(a-b)''=0$ too, all linearized curvature vanishes.

= Weak-field approximation
{parent=Linearized gravity}

The weak-field approximation requires every dimensionless component of the <metric perturbation> to have magnitude much smaller than one, so terms quadratic in the perturbation may be neglected.

= Linearized Einstein equations
{c}
{parent=Linearized gravity}

In terms of the <trace-reversed metric perturbation> and in the <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity>, the linearized Einstein equations on Minkowski spacetime are
$$
\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}=-16\pi T_{\mu\nu}.
$$

= Linearized vacuum Einstein equations
{parent=Linearized Einstein equations}
{title2=$G^{(1)}_{\mu\nu}[h]=0$}

The source-free <linearized Einstein equations>. For a <metric perturbation> about the <Minkowski metric>, imposing the <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity> reduces them to $\Box h_{\mu\nu}=0$ together with $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$, where $\bar h$ is the <trace-reversed metric perturbation>. A <linearized coordinate gauge transformation> preserves $G^{(1)}[h]=0$, while preserving the imposed gauge additionally requires its generator to satisfy the <wave equation>.

= Parallel photon beams in linearized gravity
{parent=Linearized Einstein equations}
{title2=$h_{ik}=H(u,y,z)\ell_i\ell_k,\quad\Delta_\perp H=16\pi G\rho(u)$}

For signature $(+---)$ and the curvature convention giving $\Box\bar h_{ik}=-16\pi G\epsilon^{-1}T_{ik}$, take $u=t-x$, $\ell_i=(1,-1,0,0)$ and $T_{ik}=\epsilon\rho(u)\ell_i\ell_k$. A trace-free Lorenz-gauge particular solution has the displayed form. The vector $\ell^i=(1,1,0,0)$ is null, and $\Gamma^i{}_{jk}\ell^j\ell^k=0$, so co-propagating test photons are undeflected by this beam field. For a transversely uniform source, $H=4\pi G\rho(u)(y^2+z^2)$ is one particular solution. A function of $u$ alone has vanishing wave operator and cannot source nonzero $\rho$; homogeneous gravitational perturbations and boundary conditions remain separate data.

= Radiation boundary condition for linearized gravity
{parent=Linearized Einstein equations}

The retarded source solution of the <linearized Einstein equations> is selected by excluding added incoming or freely specified homogeneous <gravitational waves>. A homogeneous wave can satisfy <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity> while having no source <mass quadrupole moment>. Thus a formula for the retarded source field is not an identity for every solution of the sourced wave equation.

= Retarded quadrupole field
{parent=Linearized Einstein equations}
{title2=$\bar h_{ij}(t,\mathbf x)\simeq 2\ddot I_{ij}(t-r)/r$}

For a compact slowly moving source in the <weak-field approximation>, with no incoming homogeneous wave and $G=c=1$, the retarded <linearized Einstein equations> give this leading far-zone <trace-reversed metric perturbation>. The key consequence of <stress-energy conservation> is $\ddot I_{ij}=2\int T_{ij}\,d^3x$. The physical <gravitational wave> is obtained by the <transverse-traceless projector>.

= Long-wavelength source approximation
{parent=Retarded quadrupole field}
{title2=$d/\tau\ll1$}

Dropping the position-dependent source delay in the leading far-zone retarded field requires source size $d$ to be small compared with its variation time $\tau$, in units $c=1$. The <retarded quadrupole field> also requires $d/r\ll1$. For a typical nonrelativistic bound source, $\tau\sim d/v$ implies this long-wavelength condition; an arbitrarily rapidly oscillating small displacement is not controlled merely by its instantaneous speed.

= Linearized inverse metric
{parent=Linearized gravity}

If $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}$, then $g^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}+O(h^2)$, with indices on $h$ raised using the background metric.

= Linearized Levi-Civita connection
{parent=Linearized gravity}

About a Cartesian flat background,
$$
\Gamma^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}
=\frac12\eta^{\alpha\rho}
(\partial_\beta h_{\gamma\rho}+\partial_\gamma h_{\beta\rho}-\partial_\rho h_{\beta\gamma})+O(h^2).
$$

= Linearized Riemann curvature operator
{parent=Linearized gravity}

The linearized Riemann tensor defines an operator on two-forms by
$$
(\mathcal R\omega)_{ab}=\frac12R^{(1)}_{ab}{}^{cd}\omega_{cd}.
$$
Unlike the metric perturbation itself, this operator is invariant under linearized gauge transformations.

= Gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor
{parent=Linearized Riemann curvature operator}

Under $h_{\mu\nu}\mapsto h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, every contribution to the linearized Riemann tensor contains three commuting derivatives of $\xi$ and cancels pairwise. Linearized curvature is therefore gauge invariant about flat spacetime.

= Flat linearized metric perturbations are locally pure gauge
{parent=Gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor}
{title2=$R^{(1)}[h]=0\ \Longrightarrow\ h_{ab}=2\partial_{(a}\xi_{b)}$}

On a <contractible> Cartesian patch of a flat background, zero <linearized Riemann curvature operator> says that the <connection 1-forms> $\Gamma^{(1)a}{}_{bc}\,dx^c$ are closed. The <Poincaré lemma> writes them as <derivatives> of a matrix-valued potential. Symmetry of the two lower <affine connection> indices then supplies a second potential, giving $\Gamma^{(1)a}{}_{bc}=\partial_b\partial_c\xi^a$. Linearized <metric compatibility> implies that $h_{ab}-2\partial_{(a}\xi_{b)}$ is constant. Its symmetric constant part is absorbed by an affine <vector field>. Thus the <metric perturbation> is locally a coordinate change. Global <topology> and imposed <boundary conditions> can obstruct the corresponding global gauge choice.

= Trace-reversed metric perturbation
{title2=$\bar h_{\mu\nu}$}
{parent=Linearized gravity}

In four dimensions, the trace-reversed perturbation is
$$
\bar h_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac12\eta_{\mu\nu}h,
\qquad h=\eta^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}.
$$
Trace reversal simplifies the linearized Einstein equations.

= Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity
{c}
{parent=Trace-reversed metric perturbation}

The Lorenz condition $\partial^\mu\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$ reduces the vacuum field equation to $\Box\bar h_{\mu\nu}=0$. A plane-wave amplitude therefore satisfies $k^\mu H_{\mu\nu}=0$ and $k^\mu k_\mu=0$.

= Residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity
{parent=Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity}

An infinitesimal coordinate change generated by $\xi_\mu$ changes
$$
\bar h_{\mu\nu}\mapsto
\bar h_{\mu\nu}-\partial_\mu\xi_\nu-\partial_\nu\xi_\mu
+\eta_{\mu\nu}\partial_\rho\xi^\rho.
$$
It preserves Lorenz gauge exactly when $\Box\xi_\mu=0$.

= Pure-gauge plane wave in linearized gravity
{parent=Residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity}

Under the convention $\delta h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$, choose $\xi^\mu=b^\mu\sin(k\cdot x)$ with constant $b^\mu$ and null $k^\mu$. The <trace-reversed metric perturbation> has amplitude
$$
\epsilon_{\rm gauge}^{\mu\nu}=k^\mu b^\nu+k^\nu b^\mu-\eta^{\mu\nu}(k\cdot b).
$$
It satisfies $k_\mu\epsilon_{\rm gauge}^{\mu\nu}=0$ and $\Box\xi^\mu=0$, so it is an allowed <residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity>. Its <linearized Riemann curvature operator> is zero: the four wavevector factors in each second-derivative term cancel in the antisymmetric curvature combinations. Nonzero transverse-traceless curvature distinguishes the two physical <gravitational wave polarizations> from these coordinate modes.https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/tong/gr/grhtml/S5.html[Section 5.3.1, equations 5.232–5.234: residual plane-wave gauge transformation and transverse-traceless reduction]{ref}

= Plane gravitational wave in linearized gravity
{parent=Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity}

= Plane gravitational waves in linearized gravity
{synonym}

A vacuum mode $\bar h_{\mu\nu}=H_{\mu\nu}e^{ik\cdot x}$ has a null wavevector and a transverse symmetric amplitude. Residual gauge freedom can further reduce it to two physical transverse-traceless polarizations.

= Transverse-traceless gauge
{title2=TT gauge}
{parent=Plane gravitational wave in linearized gravity}
{wiki}

Transverse-traceless gauge sets the time and longitudinal components and the spatial trace of a vacuum gravitational-wave perturbation to zero.

= Freely falling ring in transverse-traceless gauge
{parent=Transverse-traceless gauge}
{title2=$\delta\ell/a=\tfrac12h_+\cos(2\varphi)$}

In a weak plus-polarized <gravitational wave>, initially stationary test particles have constant spatial coordinates in <transverse-traceless gauge> because $\Gamma^i{}_{00}=0$. Their physical separations nevertheless change. For a small transverse ring, $X=(1+h_+/2)x$ and $Y=(1-h_+/2)y$, so its principal semiaxes oscillate as $a(1\pm h_+/2)$. The radial response has the displayed angular dependence. These are local distance measurements in the long-wavelength limit; finite-arm light-travel times require the full detector response.

= Explicit plane-wave reduction to transverse-traceless gauge
{parent=Transverse-traceless gauge}
{title2=$H_+=(H_{11}-H_{22})/2,\quad H_\times=H_{12}$}

For a <plane gravitational wave in linearized gravity> with $k_\mu=(-\omega,0,0,\omega)$, <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity> gives $H_{3\nu}=-H_{0\nu}$. Under $\delta H_{\mu\nu}=i(k_\mu X_\nu+k_\nu X_\mu-\eta_{\mu\nu}k^\rho X_\rho)$, choose $X_i=H_{0i}/(i\omega)$ for $i=1,2$, $X_3-X_0=iH_{00}/\omega$, and $X_0+X_3=(H_{11}+H_{22})/(2i\omega)$. This <residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity> removes all time and longitudinal entries and the transverse trace. The remaining amplitudes $H_+,H_\times$ are the two <gravitational wave polarizations>. The formula uses the plus-sign convention for $\delta h=2\partial_{(\mu}\xi_{\nu)}$; reversing the definition of $\xi$ reverses the gauge change.

= TT coordinate separation and measured separation
{c}
{parent=Transverse-traceless gauge}
{title2=$\xi^{\hat A}=\xi_{\rm coord}^A+\tfrac12h_{AB}L^B$}

In <transverse-traceless gauge>, particles initially at rest have fixed spatial coordinates because $\Gamma^\mu{}_{00}=0$. Their separation measured in a <parallel-propagated orthonormal frame> changes: to first order $S^{\hat A}=S^A+h_{AB}S^B/2$. Hence the physical <geodesic deviation> is $\ddot\xi^{\hat A}=\ddot h_{AB}L^B/2$, whereas the coordinate displacement obeys $\ddot\xi^A_{\rm coord}=0$. The extra $\partial_0\Gamma^A{}_{0B}L^B$ term in the coordinate expansion of the second <covariant derivative> reconciles the two results. This applies to infinitesimal separations and linear <metric perturbations>.

= Initial conditions for an eternal monochromatic gravitational wave
{parent=TT coordinate separation and measured separation}
{title2=$\xi=\tfrac12(h-h_0)X+(V-\tfrac12\dot h_0X)t$}

At first order, <geodesic deviation> gives $\ddot\xi_i=\ddot h_{ij}X^j/2$ for a small detector in a <plane gravitational wave in linearized gravity>. For physical initial displacement zero and initial <velocity> $V_i$, integration gives $\xi_i=[h_{ij}(t)-h_{ij}(0)]X^j/2+[V_i-\dot h_{ij}(0)X^j/2]t$. A bounded periodic response selects $V_i=\dot h_{ij}(0)X^j/2$. Prescribed positions alone do not select that response when the wave has no wave-free past.

= Angular response of a gravitational-wave separation
{parent=TT coordinate separation and measured separation}

For a short pair of stationary test particles in <transverse-traceless gauge>, spatial coordinates stay fixed while the measured <proper length> changes. In background signature $+---$, let $h_{xx}=-h_{yy}=H_+$ and $h_{xy}=H_\times$. A coordinate separation of length $\Delta$ at angle $\theta$ has
$$
\ell^2=\Delta^2[1-H_+\cos(2\theta)-H_\times\sin(2\theta)],\qquad
\frac{\delta\ell}{\Delta}=-\frac12[H_+\cos(2\theta)+H_\times\sin(2\theta)]+O(H^2).
$$
The <plus polarization> and <cross polarization> differ by a $45$-degree rotation of their principal axes. With background signature $-+++$ and the same numerical spatial perturbation, the sign reverses because the positive spatial metric is then $\delta_{ij}+h_{ij}$ rather than $\delta_{ij}-h_{ij}$. Consistently redefining the signed strain leaves the physical effect unchanged.

= Sliding-bead gravitational wave detector
{parent=TT coordinate separation and measured separation}
{title2=$\ddot L=\tfrac12\epsilon\ddot h_{nn}L_0+O(\epsilon^2)$}

A short rigid nonrotating guide constrains two freely sliding beads to one axis $n$. In its <parallel-propagated orthonormal frame>, the axial <geodesic deviation> is $\ddot L=-R_{n0n0}L$. For a transverse <gravitational wave>, $R_{n0n0}=-\epsilon\ddot h_{nn}/2$, so a wave incident after the beads are at rest changes the measured separation by $\epsilon h_{nn}L_0/2$ to first order. Transverse reaction forces do not supply an axial restoring force. Alignment with the propagation direction gives no first-order length change.

= Gravitational wave polarization
{parent=Transverse-traceless gauge}
{wiki}

A gravitational wave in four-dimensional general relativity has two independent linear polarizations, conventionally called plus and cross. A rotation by angle $\vartheta$ in the transverse plane rotates their pair by $2\vartheta$.

= Circular gravitational-wave polarization
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}
{title2=$h_+=A\cos\Phi,\quad h_\times=\pm A\sin\Phi$}

Equal-amplitude <plus polarization> and <cross polarization> with a quarter-cycle phase difference form circularly polarized <gravitational radiation>. The principal transverse strain axes rotate by half the phase angle. An observer on the rotation axis of a circular binary sees this polarization; the sign distinguishes the handedness relative to a stated propagation direction.

= Plus-polarized gravitational wave
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}

A <gravitational wave> carrying the plus component relative to a chosen pair of transverse axes.

= Transverse-traceless tensor
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}
{title2=$k_ih_{ij}=0,\quad h_{ii}=0$}

A transverse-traceless spatial tensor is orthogonal to its <Fourier mode> wavevector in each index and has zero trace. The <transverse-traceless projector> extracts this part. Trace terms and symmetrized coordinate-displacement gradients have no such part, explaining the linear gauge invariance of <tensor cosmological perturbations>.

= Plus polarization
{title2=$+$ polarization}
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}

A <gravitational wave> of plus polarization has opposite transverse strain or <geodesic deviation> <eigenvalues> along two fixed perpendicular axes. For a sinusoidal <periodic function> profile, the stretching and compressing roles alternate every half-period.

= Cross polarization
{title2=$\times$ polarization}
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}

The cross polarization is the plus pattern rotated through $45$ degrees in the transverse plane.

= Transverse-traceless projector
{title2=$\Pi_{ij,mn}^{\rm TT}$}
{parent=Gravitational wave polarization}

The transverse-traceless projector maps a symmetric tensor to the part orthogonal to a chosen wavevector and with zero trace. It is the identity on physical gravitational-wave polarizations.

= Polarization completeness relation
{parent=Transverse-traceless projector}

A polarization completeness relation expresses a projector as a sum of outer products of an orthonormal polarization basis. For transverse-traceless tensors, $\sum_s\epsilon_{ij}^s\epsilon_{mn}^{s*}$ is the transverse-traceless projector up to the chosen normalization.

= Linearized Ricci tensor and scalar
{parent=Linearized gravity}

To first order around flat spacetime,
$$
R_{\mu\nu}
=\frac12\left(
\partial_\rho\partial_\mu h^\rho{}_{\nu}
+\partial_\rho\partial_\nu h^\rho{}_{\mu}
-\mathop{\Box}h_{\mu\nu}
-\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h
\right),
$$
and
$$
R=\partial_\mu\partial_\nu h^{\mu\nu}-\mathop{\Box}h,
\qquad h=\eta^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}.
$$

= Linearized curvature of a two-potential weak-field metric
{parent=Linearized Ricci tensor and scalar}
{title2=$ds^2=-(1+2\Phi/c^2)(dx^0)^2+(1-2\Psi/c^2)d\mathbf x^2$}

For <metric signature> $(-,+,+,+)$, $x^0=ct$ and small dimensionless potentials, the <metric perturbation> is $h_{00}=-2\Phi/c^2$, $h_{ij}=-2\Psi\delta_{ij}/c^2$. Substitution into the <linearized Ricci tensor and scalar> formula gives
$$
R_{00}=\frac{\nabla^2\Phi+3\partial_0^2\Psi}{c^2},\quad
R_{0i}=\frac{2\partial_0\partial_i\Psi}{c^2},\quad
R_{ij}=\frac{\partial_i\partial_j(\Psi-\Phi)+\delta_{ij}(\nabla^2\Psi-\partial_0^2\Psi)}{c^2}.
$$
The contraction is $R=(4\nabla^2\Psi-2\nabla^2\Phi-6\partial_0^2\Psi)/c^2$, so $G^0{}_0=-2\nabla^2\Psi/c^2$. These signs use $R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}V^\nu=[\nabla_\alpha,\nabla_\beta]V^\mu$. Static zero-anisotropic-stress boundary conditions give $\Phi=\Psi$ and the <Newtonian limit>; without those assumptions the two potentials and their time derivatives must be retained.

= Newtonian limit of general relativity
{c}
{synonym}

For a weak, static field produced by nonrelativistic matter, $T_{00}=\rho$ is the dominant stress-energy component. The field equations and the slow-particle <geodesic equation> imply
$$
h_{00}=-2\Phi,
\qquad
\kappa=8\pi G,
$$
where the <Poisson equation> is $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho$.

= Newtonian connection from an exponential lapse
{c}
{parent=Newtonian limit}
{title2=$\Gamma^i{}_{tt}=\partial_i\phi$}

For smooth spatial $\phi$ and $g(c)=-c^2e^{2\phi/c^2}dt^2+\delta_{ij}dx^idx^j$, the only nonzero <Christoffel symbols> are $\Gamma^t{}_{ti}=\Gamma^t{}_{it}=c^{-2}\partial_i\phi$ and $\Gamma^i{}_{tt}=e^{2\phi/c^2}\partial_i\phi$. Consequently the <Levi-Civita connection> converges smoothly on compact sets as $c\to\infty$ although the metric itself diverges in these coordinates. The limiting <torsion-free connection> has only $\Gamma^i{}_{tt}=\partial_i\phi$ and gives $d^2x^i/dt^2=-\partial_i\phi$ when $t$ is an <affine parameter>. With $R^a{}_{bcd}=\partial_c\Gamma^a{}_{db}-\partial_d\Gamma^a{}_{cb}+\cdots$, its <Ricci tensor> is $(\Delta\phi)dt\otimes dt$: quadratic connection terms vanish and $R^i{}_{tjt}=\partial_j\partial_i\phi$. Thus zero Ricci tensor is the <Laplace equation>, while full flatness would require the entire Hessian to vanish.

= Static radius
{title2=$r_{\rm s}=(3GM/\Lambda)^{1/3}$}
{parent=Newtonian limit}

For a point mass in a positive <cosmological constant>, the inward Newtonian acceleration $GM/r^2$ balances the outward cosmological acceleration $\Lambda r/3$ at the static radius $r_{\rm s}=(3GM/\Lambda)^{1/3}$.

= Linearized point-mass metric in radial Cartesian form
{parent=Newtonian limit}

For a point mass and $r>0$, write
$$
h_{00}=\frac{2GM}{r},
\qquad
h_{ij}=f(r)x_i x_j.
$$
The vacuum scalar-curvature equation gives $rf'+3f=0$, so $f=C/r^3$. The remaining vacuum equations fix $C=2GM$ in the corresponding Schwarzschild gauge.

= Slowly rotating weak-field metric
{parent=Linearized gravity}

Far from a slowly rotating isolated body of mass $m$ and angular momentum $\mathbf J$, the scalar part of the metric is the linearized point-mass field while $g_{0i}$ is the dipole field of $\mathbf J$. For rotation about the z-axis, the cross term is $4Jr^{-3}(y\,dx-x\,dy)dt$ in a common convention.
