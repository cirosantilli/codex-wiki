== 1
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Use <metric signature> $(-,+,+,+)$, $x^0=ct$, and retain terms linear in $\Phi/c^2$ or $\Psi/c^2$. The <inverse metric> is $g^{00}=-(1-2\Phi/c^2)$ and $g^{ij}=(1+2\Psi/c^2)\delta^{ij}$. In the <Christoffel symbol> formula
$$
\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}=\frac12g^{\alpha\delta}(\partial_\beta g_{\delta\gamma}+\partial_\gamma g_{\delta\beta}-\partial_\delta g_{\beta\gamma}),
$$
the differentiated <metric perturbation> is already first order, so the prefactor can be replaced by the flat <inverse metric>. For example $\Gamma^0{}_{ij}=\tfrac12\partial_0g_{ij}=-\delta_{ij}\partial_0\Psi/c^2$ and $\Gamma^k{}_{00}=-\tfrac12\partial_kg_{00}=\partial_k\Phi/c^2$. Evaluating the remaining components gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Gamma^0{}_{00}&=\frac{\partial_0\Phi}{c^2},&
\Gamma^0{}_{0i}&=\frac{\partial_i\Phi}{c^2},&
\Gamma^0{}_{ij}&=-\frac{\partial_0\Psi}{c^2}\delta_{ij},\\
\Gamma^k{}_{00}&=\frac{\partial_k\Phi}{c^2},&
\Gamma^k{}_{0i}&=-\frac{\partial_0\Psi}{c^2}\delta^k_i,&
\Gamma^k{}_{ij}&=-\frac{\partial_i\Psi\delta^k_j+\partial_j\Psi\delta^k_i-\partial^k\Psi\delta_{ij}}{c^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The lower-index symmetry follows from the <torsion-free connection>. Products of these first-order <Christoffel symbols> are beyond the retained <weak-field approximation>.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

A <pressureless relativistic fluid> has <stress-energy tensor> $T^{\mu\nu}=\rho u^\mu u^\nu$, with <four-velocity> normalized by $u^\mu u_\mu=-c^2$. In its <rest frame>, $u^i=0$, so $\boxed{T^0{}_0=-\rho c^2,\ T^i{}_j=0}$. Here $\rho$ is rest-frame <mass density>, or <energy density> divided by $c^2$.

For the <linearized Ricci tensor and scalar>, discard the quadratic connection terms. The contracted connection is $\Gamma^\mu{}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\nu(\Phi-3\Psi)/c^2$. Differentiation then gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
R_{00}&=\frac{\nabla^2\Phi+3\partial_0^2\Psi}{c^2},\\
R_{0i}&=\frac{2\partial_0\partial_i\Psi}{c^2},\\
R_{ij}&=\frac{\partial_i\partial_j(\Psi-\Phi)+\delta_{ij}(\nabla^2\Psi-\partial_0^2\Psi)}{c^2},\\
R&=\frac{4\nabla^2\Psi-2\nabla^2\Phi-6\partial_0^2\Psi}{c^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The <Ricci scalar> uses the zeroth-order <inverse metric>, $R=-R_{00}+\delta^{ij}R_{ij}$, because multiplying first-order <curvature> by another <metric perturbation> would again be second order. These are the <linearized curvature of a two-potential weak-field metric> in the paper's <Riemann curvature tensor> sign convention.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The <Einstein tensor> is $G^\mu{}_\nu=R^\mu{}_\nu-\tfrac12\delta^\mu_\nu R$. Thus $G^0{}_0=-R_{00}-R/2$, where the time raising introduces the minus sign. Substitution cancels the $\Phi$ and time-derivative terms in that component and gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
G^0{}_0&=-\frac{2\nabla^2\Psi}{c^2}=-\frac{8\pi G\rho}{c^2},\\
G^i{}_j&=\frac{2\partial_0^2\Psi\delta^i_j+\nabla^2(\Phi-\Psi)\delta^i_j+\partial^i\partial_j(\Psi-\Phi)}{c^2}=0.
\end{aligned}}
$$
The right sides follow from the <Einstein field equations> $G^\mu{}_\nu=(8\pi G/c^4)T^\mu{}_\nu$ with zero <cosmological constant>. The full equations also contain the momentum constraint $G^0{}_i=-2\partial_0\partial_i\Psi/c^2=0$ for dust at rest to this order. Specifying arbitrary time-dependent potentials without that constraint would not give a solution of all the <Einstein field equations>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a massive particle parametrized by <proper time>, the <geodesic equation> is
$$
\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\tau^2}+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=0,
\qquad g_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau}=-c^2.
$$
The <weak-field approximation> alone does not imply Newtonian motion. Also take a <nonrelativistic limit>, $|v|/c\ll1$, in the usual quasistatic regime so the potentials' time variation does not compensate for the small velocity factors. Reparametrizing by coordinate time and retaining the leading velocity-independent spatial term gives
$$
\boxed{\frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2}=-c^2\Gamma^i{}_{00}=-\partial_i\Phi.}
$$
In empty space the first field equation gives $\nabla^2\Psi=0$. For static potentials, the spatial equations imply $\nabla^2(\Phi-\Psi)=0$ and then $\partial_i\partial_j(\Phi-\Psi)=0$. The difference is at most affine; isolated-system boundary conditions fixing both potentials to zero at infinity remove it. Thus $\Phi=\Psi$ and
$$
\boxed{\nabla^2\Phi=0\quad\text{in vacuum},}
$$
the empty-space <Newton-Poisson equation>. With the same quasistatic assumptions and dust present it becomes $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho$. Keeping rapid time dependence instead leaves $\nabla^2\Phi=-3\partial_0^2\Psi$ in vacuum, so the static qualification matters.

== 2
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Although $\nabla_\beta f=\partial_\beta f$ for a <scalar field>, its first derivative is a <covector field>. Therefore
$$
\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta f=\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta f-\Gamma^\gamma{}_{\alpha\beta}\partial_\gamma f.
$$
Commuting <partial derivatives> and using the <torsion-free connection> gives $\boxed{[\nabla_\alpha,\nabla_\beta]f=0}$. For a <vector field>, differentiate the $(1,1)$ <tensor> $\nabla_\beta V^\mu$ with both its indices included:
$$
\nabla_\alpha\nabla_\beta V^\mu
=\partial_\alpha(\nabla_\beta V^\mu)+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\epsilon}\nabla_\beta V^\epsilon
-\Gamma^\epsilon{}_{\alpha\beta}\nabla_\epsilon V^\mu.
$$
Subtract the expression with $\alpha,\beta$ exchanged. Second derivatives of $V$ cancel; the terms multiplying first derivatives cancel pairwise, and the final lower-index connection terms cancel by symmetry. The remaining coefficient of $V^\nu$ is
$$
\boxed{[\nabla_\alpha,\nabla_\beta]V^\mu=
\left(\partial_\alpha\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\nu}-\partial_\beta\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\nu}
+\Gamma^\mu{}_{\alpha\epsilon}\Gamma^\epsilon{}_{\beta\nu}-\Gamma^\mu{}_{\beta\epsilon}\Gamma^\epsilon{}_{\alpha\nu}\right)V^\nu
=R^\mu{}_{\nu\alpha\beta}V^\nu.}
$$
This is the <curvature commutator>. On a covariant index the sign reverses, giving the <curvature commutator on a covariant tensor> used in the next part.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

For a <torsion-free connection>, the connection terms in the antisymmetrized <covariant derivative> cancel: $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$. Thus the <electromagnetic field tensor> is the <exterior derivative> of the <four-potential>. It is a <tensor>, is invariant under $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\chi$, and obeys the homogeneous <Maxwell equations> $\nabla_{[\alpha}F_{\beta\gamma]}=0$. The covariant divergence equation is the generally covariant form of the local source equation; use its minus sign as specified in the paper.

Expand that equation before imposing the <Lorenz gauge condition>:
$$
\nabla^\mu F_{\mu\nu}=\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu A_\nu-\nabla^\mu\nabla_\nu A_\mu.
$$
The <curvature commutator on a covariant tensor> gives
$$
[\nabla_\rho,\nabla_\nu]A_\mu=-R^\alpha{}_{\mu\rho\nu}A_\alpha,
\qquad
\nabla^\mu\nabla_\nu A_\mu=\nabla_\nu(\nabla^\mu A_\mu)+R^\alpha{}_\nu A_\alpha.
$$
Here the contraction $g^{\mu\rho}R^\alpha{}_{\mu\rho\nu}=-R^\alpha{}_\nu$ accounts for the second sign reversal. Hence in <Lorenz gauge>
$$
\boxed{\nabla^\mu\nabla_\mu A_\nu-R^\alpha{}_\nu A_\alpha=-\mu_0J_\nu.}
$$
This <curved-spacetime electromagnetic potential wave equation> contains a <Ricci tensor> term even in a locally inertial coordinate system: a connection can vanish at one point while its derivatives and <curvature> remain nonzero.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $\mathcal H=a'/a$, with the prime denoting $\partial_0=c^{-1}\partial_\eta$. The <metric> and <inverse metric> are $g_{\mu\nu}=a^2\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $g^{\mu\nu}=a^{-2}\eta^{\mu\nu}$. Since $a$ depends only on $x^0$, the <Christoffel symbols> reduce to
$$
\Gamma^\alpha{}_{\beta\gamma}
=\mathcal H\left(\delta^\alpha_\beta\delta^0_\gamma+\delta^\alpha_\gamma\delta^0_\beta-\eta_{\beta\gamma}\eta^{\alpha0}\right).
$$
Consequently
$$
\boxed{\Gamma^0{}_{00}=\mathcal H,\quad\Gamma^0{}_{0i}=0,\quad\Gamma^0{}_{ij}=\mathcal H\delta_{ij},\quad
\Gamma^k{}_{00}=0,\quad\Gamma^k{}_{0i}=\mathcal H\delta^k_i,\quad\Gamma^k{}_{ij}=0.}
$$
For the stated <de Sitter spacetime> scale factor, $\mathcal H=-1/(c\eta)$. The factor $c^{-1}$ is required because the prime is not differentiation with respect to $\eta$ itself.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a <minimally coupled massless scalar field>, the <covariant wave operator> is
$$
\Box\phi=\frac1{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu\phi)
=\frac1{a^2}\left[-\partial_0^2\phi-2\mathcal H\partial_0\phi+\nabla^2\phi\right].
$$
The <Fourier mode> $\phi=u(\eta)e^{i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}$ therefore satisfies
$$
u_{\eta\eta}-\frac2\eta u_\eta+\omega^2u=0,\qquad\omega=c|k|.
$$
Substituting $u=(A+B\eta)e^{-i\omega\eta}$ leaves $2(i\omega A-B)e^{-i\omega\eta}/\eta$. Thus
$$
\boxed{B=i\omega A=ic|k|A,\qquad
\phi=A(1+ic|k|\eta)e^{-ic|k|\eta+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}.}
$$
This is an <unrescaled de Sitter massless scalar mode>. For $k\ne0$, the opposite-frequency time solution supplies the second independent mode; a real <scalar field> combines appropriate conjugate spatial modes. For $k=0$ the particular ansatz gives only the constant solution, while the full homogeneous solution is $u=C+D\eta^3$. The requested ansatz was not a general zero-mode parametrization.

== 3
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Use <mass density> units for the $\rho$ appearing in the <Friedmann equation>; an actual <energy density> is $\rho c^2$. Conservation, $\dot\rho+3H(\rho+P/c^2)=0$, gives $\boxed{\rho_M=\rho_{M,0}a^{-3},\quad\rho_R=\rho_{R,0}a^{-4}}$ for <pressureless matter> and <radiation in cosmology>, respectively, with $a_0=1$. The first scaling counts dilution of particle number, while the second includes the <cosmological redshift> of each photon's <energy>.

Define the present <cosmological density parameters> by $\Omega_{X,0}=8\pi G\rho_{X,0}/(3H_0^2)$. Then
$$
\frac{H^2}{H_0^2}=\Omega_{M,0}a^{-3}+\Omega_{R,0}a^{-4}+\Omega_{k,0}a^{-2},\qquad
\Omega_{k,0}=-\frac{kc^2}{H_0^2}=1-\Omega_{M,0}-\Omega_{R,0}.
$$
For $k=0$, <conformal time> gives $da/d\eta=a^2H$, so the expanding branch obeys $(da/d\eta)^2=H_0^2(\Omega_{M,0}a+\Omega_{R,0})$. Set $\eta=0$ at the <Big Bang>. Integrating gives
$$
\sqrt{\Omega_{M,0}a+\Omega_{R,0}}=\sqrt{\Omega_{R,0}}+\frac12\Omega_{M,0}H_0\eta,
$$
and hence the <flat matter-radiation conformal solution>
$$
\boxed{a(\eta)=\sqrt{\Omega_{R,0}}H_0\eta+\frac14\Omega_{M,0}(H_0\eta)^2.}
$$
The limiting pure-radiation and pure-matter cases follow continuously.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

At <matter-radiation equality>, $\rho_{M,0}a^{-3}=\rho_{R,0}a^{-4}$, so $a_{\rm eq}=\Omega_{R,0}/\Omega_{M,0}$. Evaluating the integrated square-root relation gives
$$
\boxed{\eta_{\rm eq}=\frac{2(\sqrt2-1)\sqrt{\Omega_{R,0}}}{\Omega_{M,0}H_0}.}
$$
This assumes both present densities are positive and uses the same <Big Bang> origin for <conformal time>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For dust alone, write $\dot a^2=c^2(A/a-k)$, where
$$
\boxed{A=\frac{8\pi G\rho_{M,0}}{3c^2}=\frac{\Omega_{M,0}H_0^2}{c^2}.}
$$
Set $\theta=\sqrt{k}c\eta$ and $a=(A/k)\sin^2(\theta/2)$. Differentiation gives $\dot a=c\sqrt{k}\cot(\theta/2)$ once $dt=a\,d\eta$ is used, verifying the <Friedmann equation>. Integrating the time relation from the <Big Bang> yields the <closed matter-dominated Friedmann solution>
$$
a=\frac A{k}\sin^2\frac\theta2,\qquad
t=\frac{A}{2ck^{3/2}}(\theta-\sin\theta),\qquad0<\theta<2\pi.
$$
The <Hubble parameter> and the <cosmological density parameter> are
$$
\boxed{H(\theta)=\frac{ck^{3/2}}A\frac{\cos(\theta/2)}{\sin^3(\theta/2)},\qquad
\Omega_M(\theta)=\sec^2\frac\theta2.}
$$
The first decreases from positive infinity through zero at maximum expansion to negative infinity at the <Big Crunch>. The second approaches one near either singular endpoint and diverges at maximum expansion. This is a <density-parameter divergence at a cosmological turnaround>: $H=0$ makes the <critical density> vanish even though the physical dust density there is finite.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

At maximum expansion, $a_{\max}=A/k$. With $a_0=1$, the present <Friedmann equation> gives $kc^2=H_0^2(\Omega_{M,0}-1)$, so positive curvature requires $\Omega_{M,0}>1$. Taking $H_0>0$ for the presently expanding branch gives
$$
\boxed{a_{\max}=\frac{\Omega_{M,0}}{\Omega_{M,0}-1},\qquad
t_{\rm life}=\frac{\pi\Omega_{M,0}}{H_0(\Omega_{M,0}-1)^{3/2}},\qquad
q_0=\frac{\Omega_{M,0}}2.}
$$
The <lifetime of a closed dust universe> follows by setting $\theta=2\pi$. The <deceleration parameter> follows from the <Friedmann acceleration equation>, $\ddot a/a=-4\pi G\rho_M/3$. Thus this model always decelerates while expanding. Its total lifetime includes the elapsed age; it is not the remaining lifetime.

The <supernova discovery of accelerating expansion> does not support this dust-only recollapse model as a description of the observed expansion history. Already the 1998 and 1999 <Type Ia supernova> analyses favoured <accelerating expansion>, requiring ingredients beyond this zero-<cosmological constant>, positive-curvature dust model within the usual <FLRW metric> interpretation.<Observational Evidence from Supernovae for an Accelerating Universe and a Cosmological Constant by Adam Riess and others>[Abstract: evidence for present acceleration]{ref}<Measurements of Omega and Lambda from 42 High-Redshift Supernovae by Saul Perlmutter and others>[Abstract: positive cosmological constant preferred]{ref}

== 4
{scope}

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Throughout this question use <natural units> with $c=1$. For zero potential and nonzero <kinetic energy>, the homogeneous canonical <scalar field> has $P=\rho=\dot\varphi^2/2$, so $\boxed{w=1}$. Its <equation of motion> becomes $d(a^3\dot\varphi)/dt=0$, giving $\dot\varphi\propto a^{-3}$ and therefore
$$
\boxed{\rho\propto a^{-6}.}
$$
The same scaling follows from <cosmological energy conservation> with constant <equation-of-state parameter> $w=1$. If the field is exactly constant, both $P$ and $\rho$ vanish and their ratio is undefined; that is the separate empty solution.

=== b
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $M=M_{\rm Pl}$ for the <reduced Planck mass>. For a nontrivial power law with $\lambda\ne0$, $H=p/t$, $\dot\varphi=BM/t$, and $V=V_0(t/t_0)^{-\lambda B}$. Matching its time dependence to the kinetic and <Friedmann equation> terms requires $\lambda B=2$. The <scalar field equation of motion> and the <Friedmann equation> then reduce to
$$
B(3p-1)M^2=\lambda V_0t_0^2,\qquad
3M^2p^2=\frac{B^2M^2}{2}+V_0t_0^2.
$$
Solving gives the <exponential-potential scalar-dominated power-law solution>
$$
\boxed{p=\frac2{\lambda^2},\qquad B=\frac2\lambda,\qquad
t_0^2=\frac{2M^2(6-\lambda^2)}{\lambda^4V_0}.}
$$
Choose $t>0$ and the positive square root for $t_0$. The <existence conditions for exponential-potential scaling> matter: if $V_0>0$, a finite real $t_0$ requires $0<\lambda^2<6$, rather than an inclusive bound on signed $\lambda$. At $\lambda^2=6$, nonzero $V_0$ is incompatible with this logarithmic ansatz. If $V_0<0$, the corresponding scaling solution instead requires $\lambda^2>6$. The sign of $\lambda$ changes the field's rolling direction, not $p$. For $\lambda=0$ the potential is constant and this finite-$p$ ansatz does not apply; with positive $V_0$, a constant field gives <de Sitter spacetime> expansion.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

The scaling solution has $\rho=3M^2p^2/t^2$ and $\rho+P=\dot\varphi^2=4M^2/(\lambda^2t^2)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{w=-1+\frac{\lambda^2}{3},\qquad q_0=\frac1p-1=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}-1.}
$$
More generally any real canonical <scalar field> with positive total <energy density> obeys $\rho+P=\dot\varphi^2\ge0$, so **it cannot have $w<-1$**. A negative potential does not reverse this conclusion.

The proper <particle horizon> is $a(t)\int_0^t dt'/a(t')$. For $a\propto t^p$ the lower-limit integral diverges exactly when $p\ge1$. Thus, assuming the same power law extends to the initial boundary, there is no finite <particle horizon> for $\boxed{0<\lambda^2\le2}$. For the strict inequality, $p>1$, the proper <cosmological event horizon> and present age are
$$
\boxed{R_{\rm EH,0}=\frac{t_{\rm now}}{p-1}=\frac{2}{(2-\lambda^2)H_0},\qquad
t_{\rm now}=\frac{p}{H_0}=\frac{2}{\lambda^2H_0}.}
$$
At $\lambda^2=2$, $a\propto t$ has an infinite particle-horizon integral but also an infinite future event-horizon integral, so no finite event horizon exists. The age remains $H_0^{-1}$. The $\lambda=0$ constant-potential case is separate: its flat <de Sitter> patch has event-horizon radius $H_0^{-1}$ and no finite-age power-law <Big Bang>.

=== d
{scope}

==== Solution

For a single constant-$w$ <perfect fluid> in a flat expanding <FLRW metric> model, <cosmological energy conservation> gives $\rho=\rho_0a^{-3(1+w)}$ and $H(z)=H_0(1+z)^{3(1+w)/2}$. With $a_0=1$, the <luminosity distance> follows by integrating the radial <null geodesic>:
$$
D_L(z)=(1+z)\int_0^z\frac{dz'}{H(z')}.
$$
The <constant-equation-of-state luminosity distance> is consequently
$$
\boxed{D_L(z)=\begin{cases}
\displaystyle\frac{2(1+z)}{H_0(1+3w)}\left[1-(1+z)^{-(1+3w)/2}\right],&w\ne-1/3,\\[5pt]
\displaystyle\frac{1+z}{H_0}\log(1+z),&w=-1/3.
\end{cases}}
$$
This includes $w=-1$ regularly, giving $D_L=z(1+z)/H_0$. Restoring conventional units multiplies the distance formulas by $c$. Expanding about $z=0$ gives
$$
\boxed{D_L(z)=\frac1{H_0}\left[z+\frac{1-3w}{4}z^2+O(z^3)\right],\qquad
q_0=\frac{1+3w}{2}.}
$$
The second result also follows directly from the <Friedmann acceleration equation>. The expansion agrees with the general coefficient $(1-q_0)/2$ of $z^2$.

For <phantom energy>, $w<-1$, put $n=3(1+w)/2<0$. Integration of $\dot a=H_0a^{1-n}$ with $a(t_{\rm now})=1$ gives
$$
a(t)=\left[1+nH_0(t-t_{\rm now})\right]^{1/n}.
$$
Because $1/n<0$, the <scale factor> diverges at the <Big Rip> time
$$
\boxed{t_{\rm rip}-t_{\rm now}=\frac{2}{3|1+w|H_0}.}
$$
This is a property of the stipulated constant-$w$ fluid model; it is not realizable by the positive-density canonical scalar of the preceding parts.
