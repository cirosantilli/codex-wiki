= Hagedorn temperature
{c}
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}
{title2=$T_H=\beta_H^{-1}$}

If a density of states has high-energy growth proportional to a power of energy times $e^{\beta_HE}$, its canonical Boltzmann sum fails for $\beta<\beta_H$. The Hagedorn temperature is the corresponding threshold. The <open bosonic string mass spectrum> and <large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string> give $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$ in units with Boltzmann's constant, the speed of light and Planck's constant set to one. Polynomial prefactors affect behavior at the threshold itself; the growth alone does not distinguish a limiting temperature from a phase transition.

= Thermal winding instability of a closed bosonic string
{parent=Hagedorn temperature}
{title2=$m_{\rm th}^2=\beta^2/(4\pi^2\alpha'^2)-4/\alpha'$}

Euclidean finite <temperature> compactifies time to a circle of radius $R_\beta=\beta/(2\pi)$. The <string oscillator> ground states with zero <momentum> number and winding $w=\pm1$ have the displayed mass from the <bosonic string mass spectrum>. They become massless at $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$ and tachyonic at higher <temperature>. This thermal winding mode diagnoses breakdown of the perturbative low-temperature string gas at the <Hagedorn temperature>. The endpoint and order of a transition require interactions and a suitable stable vacuum; the ordinary zero-winding bosonic <tachyon> is already unstable independently of this thermal threshold.

= Finite canonical partition function at the bosonic Hagedorn threshold
{parent=Hagedorn temperature}
{title2=$Z_1(\beta_H)<\infty\quad\text{but}\quad Z_1\text{ is nonanalytic at }\beta_H$}

For a critical <closed bosonic string> in 25 noncompact spatial dimensions, the smoothed <string oscillator> rest-mass density behaves as $\rho(M)\sim C M^{-26}e^{\beta_H M}$, with $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$. At large $M$, the centre-of-mass <momentum> integral of $e^{-\beta\sqrt{M^2+p^2}}$ is asymptotic to $(2\pi M/\beta)^{25/2}e^{-\beta M}$, up to the convention for phase-space measure. Thus the high-mass tail of the one-string <canonical partition function> is proportional to $\int_{M_0}^\infty M^{-27/2}e^{-(\beta-\beta_H)M}\,dM$. This converges at $\beta=\beta_H$ but fails for $\beta<\beta_H$. Expanding about the endpoint gives twelve finite derivatives and a nonanalytic term proportional to $(\beta-\beta_H)^{25/2}$; the thirteenth derivative diverges. Consequently exponential state growth does not imply divergence of the <partition function> at the threshold itself. The discussion assumes the separate infrared instability of the bosonic <tachyon> has been regulated and does not claim a stable bosonic thermal vacuum.

= Brane
{parent=String theory}
{title2=$p\text{-brane}$}

A brane is an extended object with $p$ spatial dimensions. Its history is a $(p+1)$-dimensional <worldvolume> embedded in target <spacetime>. A point particle has $p=0$, a string has $p=1$, and a membrane has $p=2$. Geometric brane actions depend on the <induced worldvolume metric>; <D-branes> also carry open-string endpoint and gauge-field data.

= Four-dimensional supermembrane
{parent=Brane}

The four-dimensional minimal <supersymmetric> membrane has embedding coordinates $(X^m,\theta^\alpha)$ with four real odd components. Its area term uses the <supertranslation-invariant superspace coframe>, and a <Wess-Zumino brane coupling> integrates a superspace three-form potential with closed four-form curvature. Equal charge magnitude and tension permit <kappa symmetry>. After <worldvolume> reparametrization and kappa fixing, one transverse scalar and one three-dimensional Majorana <fermion> remain. This gives the long-wavelength description of the translation and <fermion> zero modes of a <supersymmetric domain wall>; it is distinct from restricting the eleven-dimensional M2-brane to a four-dimensional target.

= Supermembrane closed four-form
{parent=Four-dimensional supermembrane}
{title2=$G_4\ \propto\ i\Pi^m\wedge\Pi^n\wedge d\bar\theta\gamma_{mn}d\theta$}

The four-dimensional membrane <Fierz rearrangement> identity $(C\gamma_{mn})_{(\alpha\beta}(C\gamma^n)_{\gamma\delta)}=0$ makes the displayed supertranslation-invariant four-form closed. It is locally the <exterior derivative> of a three-form potential, which can change by an exact form under target <supersymmetry>. Pulling the potential back to a closed <worldvolume> gives an invariant <Wess-Zumino brane coupling>. The charge magnitude and normalization of this form are fixed together by cancellation of the area variation under <kappa symmetry>.

= Dirac membrane action
{c}
{parent=Brane}
{title2=$S=-T\int d^3\xi\sqrt{-\det h}$}

A relativistic membrane sweeps out a three-dimensional <worldvolume> with <induced worldvolume metric> $h_{ij}=\partial_iX^m\partial_jX^n\eta_{mn}$. Its leading geometric <action> is tension times its invariant volume. In static gauge in four-dimensional spacetime, one transverse field $Y$ gives $h_{ij}=\eta_{ij}+\partial_iY\partial_jY$, and the <matrix determinant lemma> gives $S=-T\int\sqrt{1+\partial_iY\partial^iY}$. Expanding supplies the translation-zero-mode kinetic term. A <domain wall> of finite thickness also has higher-curvature corrections outside the long-wavelength limit.

= Wess-Zumino brane coupling
{parent=Brane}
{c}
{title2=$qT_p\int_\Sigma X^*A_{p+1}$}

A charged <brane> couples to an appropriate differential-form <gauge potential> through its <pullback>. Under $A\mapsto A+d\Lambda$, a closed worldvolume changes the action by a boundary term, by <Stokes theorem>. Reversing the brane charge reverses this coupling, which matters in probe-force and supersymmetry calculations.

= Electric and magnetic dimensions of a form-coupled brane
{parent=Wess-Zumino brane coupling}
{title2=$p_{\rm magnetic}=d-p_{\rm electric}-4$}

A $(p+1)$-form <gauge potential> couples electrically through its pullback to a $(p+1)$-dimensional <worldvolume>, whose object has $p$ spatial dimensions. Its field strength has degree $p+2$ and its magnetic dual potential has degree $d-p-3$, hence magnetic spatial dimension $d-p-4$. Electric and magnetic fluxes are integrated over linking spheres of dimensions $d-p-2$ and $p+2$, respectively. This dimensional dictionary applies when the indicated objects and dual descriptions exist; top forms need separate treatment and carry no ordinary propagating polarization.

= Gauge invariance of an open-brane coupling
{parent=Wess-Zumino brane coupling}
{title2=$S=q\int_WX^*C+q\int_{\partial W}X^*B$}

For an oriented <brane> worldvolume $W$, a <gauge transformation> $C\mapsto C+d\Lambda$ changes the <Wess-Zumino brane coupling> by $q\int_{\partial W}X^*\Lambda$, by the <generalized Stokes theorem>. If the brane boundary lies on a manifold carrying a form $B$ of one lower degree, adding the displayed boundary term and transforming $B\mapsto B-\Lambda$ cancels this variation. The sign uses the induced boundary orientation. A closed worldvolume needs no boundary term; noncompact cases require appropriate support or decay conditions.

= Field-strength force on a charged brane
{parent=Wess-Zumino brane coupling}
{title2=$\delta\int_W X^*A=\int_W X^*(\iota_VF)+\int_{\partial W}X^*(\iota_VA)$}

For $F=dA$ and an embedding variation $V$, the pullback <Lie derivative> and <Cartan's magic formula> give the displayed variation. Consequently the interior force from the <Wess-Zumino brane coupling> depends only on the gauge-invariant field strength. A change $A\mapsto A+d\Lambda$ changes the action only on the boundary, by the <generalized Stokes theorem>; boundaries require the appropriate fixed boundary data or additional boundary coupling.

= Brane tension
{parent=Brane}
{title2=$T_p$}

The brane tension is the energy per unit spatial <brane> volume and multiplies its geometric action. In units $\hbar=c=1$, a $p$-brane tension has mass dimension $p+1$. The $p=1$ case is the <string tension>.

= Auxiliary worldvolume metric

= Worldvolume metric
{synonym}

An auxiliary worldvolume metric is an independent metric introduced in a <brane> action, distinct from the <induced worldvolume metric>. Its algebraic equation can eliminate it. For the <Polyakov action> of a string, it is determined only up to a <Weyl transformation>.

= Auxiliary-metric brane action
{parent=Auxiliary worldvolume metric}
{title2=$\int\sqrt{-\gamma}(\gamma^{\mu\nu}h_{\mu\nu}-(p-1))$}

= Polyakov-type brane action
{synonym}
{c}

The auxiliary-metric brane action is a quadratic embedding action with an independent <worldvolume metric>. Its embedding equation is $\partial_\mu(\sqrt{-\gamma}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu X^a)=0$. Its metric equation is $h_{\mu\nu}-\gamma_{\mu\nu}(\gamma^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}-(p-1))/2=0$. For $p\ne1$, tracing determines $\gamma^{\rho\sigma}h_{\rho\sigma}=p+1$ and hence $\gamma=h$; eliminating the auxiliary field gives twice the invariant <worldvolume> volume, before including the overall <brane tension>. The <Weyl-invariance exception for the string among branes> prevents unique elimination at $p=1$.

= Weyl-invariance exception for the string among branes
{parent=Auxiliary-metric brane action}
{c}
{title2=$p=1$}

Under $\gamma_{\mu\nu}\mapsto e^{2\omega}\gamma_{\mu\nu}$, the quadratic kinetic density of a $p$-brane scales as $e^{(p-1)\omega}$ and its constant density scales as $e^{(p+1)\omega}$. At $p=1$ the first factor is one and the constant term in the <auxiliary-metric brane action> vanishes. The traced metric equation is then an identity, leaving a <Weyl transformation> freedom. For $p\ne1$, the metric equation instead identifies the auxiliary and <induced worldvolume metrics>.

= Induced worldvolume metric
{parent=Worldvolume}
{title2=$h_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu X^a\partial_\nu X^b g_{ab}$}

The induced worldvolume metric is the pullback of the target <metric tensor> by a <brane> embedding. For a nondegenerate timelike embedding, $\sqrt{-\det h}$ is its invariant <worldvolume> volume density. The $p=1$ specialization is the <induced worldsheet metric>.

= M-theory
{parent=String theory}
{c}
{wiki}
{title2=$R_{11}=g_s\sqrt{\alpha'}$}

An eleven-dimensional nonperturbative framework suggested by superstring dualities, with eleven-dimensional supergravity as its low-energy limit. Type IIA at coupling $g_s$ has an interpretation on a circle of radius $R_{11}=g_s\sqrt{\alpha'}$, so an additional dimension becomes visible at strong coupling. Perturbative string descriptions represent particular limits of this framework.

= M-theory K3 duality
{parent=M-theory}
{c}

<M-theory> on a <K3 surface> and the <heterotic string> on a three-torus have matching seven-dimensional moduli and charge lattices. The <K3 intersection lattice> supplies twenty-two vector charges; an <M5-brane> wrapped on K3 becomes the heterotic <fundamental string>. The strong-coupling interpretation and low-energy matching are derived in section 4.7 of https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9503124[String Theory Dynamics in Various Dimensions].

When appropriate negative-square two-cycles of a <K3 surface> collapse, wrapped <M2-branes> become massless. Their intersection pattern is an ADE root diagram, and the massless charged states enlarge the abelian gauge algebra to the corresponding simply laced <Lie algebra>.

= M-theory torus duality
{parent=M-theory}
{c}
{title2=$\tau_{T^2}=C_0+i/g_B$}

<M-theory> on a two-torus and <type IIB superstring theory> on a circle describe the same nine-dimensional physics. Torus shape supplies the <axion-dilaton>; torus area supplies the circle-radius modulus after matching metrics. An <M2-brane> on a one-cycle gives a <(p,q) string>; an <M5-brane> on the whole torus gives a <D3-brane>. = M-theory circle duality
{parent=M-theory}
{c}
{title2=$R_{11}=g_s\ell_s,\quad\ell_p^3=g_s\ell_s^3$}

Compactifying <M-theory> on a circle gives <type IIA superstring theory>. The circle radius sets its coupling. Multiplying <M2-brane> and <M5-brane> tensions by the circle circumference gives the <fundamental string> and <D4-brane> tensions, while unwrapped objects give <D2-branes> and <NS5-branes>. Momentum and a <Kaluza-Klein monopole> supply <D0-brane> and <D6-brane> charges. The circle dictionary and worldvolume reductions are developed in https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9612121[Four Lectures on M-theory].

= M-theory origin of type IIA D-branes
{c}
{parent=M-theory circle duality}

<D0-branes> are circle momentum states, <D2-branes> are unwrapped <M2-branes>, <D4-branes> are circle-wrapped <M5-branes>, and <D6-branes> arise from <Kaluza-Klein monopoles> with the M-circle as fibre. Wrapped M2-branes give <fundamental strings>, and unwrapped M5-branes give <NS5-branes>. The <D8-brane> is a source for <Romans mass> and is outside this ordinary massless-circle dictionary. Tension matching uses $R_{11}=g_s\ell_s$ and $\ell_p^3=g_s\ell_s^3$.

This is the regular positive-charge membrane throat, rather than the limit of an arbitrary singular harmonic multipole.

= M2-brane probe no-force identity
{parent=M2-brane supergravity solution}
{c}
{title2=$\mathcal L_{\rm static}=-T_2$}

For $F=\operatorname{vol}_{012}\wedge dH^{-1}$ and $F=dA$, choose $A=(1-H^{-1})\operatorname{vol}_{012}$. = Supermembrane action
{parent=Supermembranes}

The first term uses the <induced worldvolume metric> from the <supervielbein>; the second is the <Wess-Zumino brane coupling> to the super-three-form. = Orientifold
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

A string construction combining worldsheet orientation reversal with a suitable spacetime involution. It projects the spectrum and introduces fixed-locus sources whose charges must be treated together with branes and fluxes. In compactifications, such projections can reduce <supersymmetry> and modify the gauge and matter spectrum. Charge cancellation is part of consistency.

= Type I string theory
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

= Type I superstring theory
{synonym}

An unoriented theory containing open and closed superstrings. Consistent gauge and gravitational anomaly cancellation yields the familiar $\mathfrak{so}(32)$ gauge algebra. Open-string <Chan-Paton factors> supply the gauge degrees of freedom, while closed strings contain gravity. It is related by strong/weak duality to the corresponding heterotic theory.

= Heterotic string
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

A <string theory> combining a bosonic left-moving system with a supersymmetric right-moving system. Ten target coordinates leave sixteen left-moving internal degrees of freedom for gauge currents. Modular consistency allows the familiar gauge algebras $\mathfrak e_8\oplus\mathfrak e_8$ and $\mathfrak{so}(32)$. Suitable compactification can produce spacetime <supersymmetry> and chiral gauge matter.

= Heterotic Calabi-Yau compactification
{parent=Heterotic string}

Compactification of a heterotic string on a <Calabi-Yau threefold> with a consistent <holomorphic vector bundle>. Suitable bundle and geometry choices preserve four-dimensional N=1 <supersymmetry> and generate chiral matter from internal Dirac zero modes. The index controls net chirality. In the standard embedding, the net generation index is half the internal <Euler characteristic>, up to chirality orientation conventions.

= Superstring theory
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

String theories with worldsheet fermionic degrees of freedom and the corresponding supersymmetric consistency conditions. In the <RNS string> description, consistent supersymmetric <GSO projections> produce spacetime <supersymmetry> and remove the NS tachyon. The flat critical dimension is ten. <Worldsheet supersymmetry> alone does not guarantee spacetime <supersymmetry> without a suitable projection.

= Compactification in string theory
{parent=String theory}

Compactification makes some target spatial dimensions compact and sufficiently small that lower-energy observations see an effective lower-dimensional spacetime. A simple geometry is a product of noncompact spacetime with a compact internal manifold; its <momentum> and winding sectors influence the effective spectrum. Reaching a four-dimensional model from a critical ten-dimensional superstring requires such additional geometric and field-theoretic data. <Worldsheet supersymmetry> alone does not choose the compactification or establish a realistic particle spectrum.

= Modulus of a string compactification
{parent=Compactification in string theory}

= Moduli of a string compactification
{synonym}

A parameter of an internal geometry, gauge background or <dilaton> configuration that becomes a scalar field in the lower-dimensional theory. A continuously undetermined parameter gives a flat direction and often a massless field. Fluxes and other effects can generate a potential, leading to <moduli stabilization>. Shape, size and coupling moduli affect low-energy observables.

= Narain moduli space
{parent=Modulus of a string compactification}
{c}
{title2=$O(d,d+16;\mathbb Z)\backslash O(d,d+16)/(O(d)\times O(d+16))$}

For a toroidally compactified <heterotic string>, the torus metric, antisymmetric tensor and gauge <Wilson lines> determine a positive $d$-plane in a charge space of signature $(d,d+16)$, modulo lattice identifications. The continuous dimension is $d(d+16)$; the coupling is an additional modulus. The three-torus case has fifty-seven shape and Wilson-line parameters.

= Moduli stabilization
{parent=Modulus of a string compactification}

Generating a potential with an acceptable stable minimum for previously undetermined compactification scalar fields. It can involve fluxes, branes and nonperturbative effects, with <supersymmetry> and consistency restrictions. A plausible phenomenological model must determine or suitably control these fields rather than leave arbitrary massless scalars and varying couplings.

= Flux compactification
{parent=Compactification in string theory}

A compactification with quantized background form-field strengths on internal cycles. Fluxes can generate potentials for <moduli of a string compactification> and affect <supersymmetry> and vacuum geometry. They must satisfy field equations, Bianchi identities and global charge constraints; choosing flux integers alone is not a complete consistency proof.

= Unprojected low levels of the open RNS string
{parent=Spinning string}
{title2=$\alpha'M^2\leq1$}

Before the <GSO projection>, the <Neveu–Schwarz sector> has <string oscillator> levels $0,1/2,1,3/2$, with physical multiplicities $1,8,36,128$ in ten-dimensional <light-cone gauge in string theory>. The eight transverse <worldsheet Majorana fermions> supply eight single $\psi_{-1/2}$ states. Level one has eight $\alpha_{-1}$ states and $\binom82=28$ pairs of distinct $\psi_{-1/2}$ modes. Level $3/2$ has $8$ single $\psi_{-3/2}$ modes, $8\cdot8$ mixed <boson>–<fermion> modes and $\binom83=56$ triples. Their squared masses are respectively $-1/(2\alpha'),0,1/(2\alpha'),1/\alpha'$. In the <Ramond sector>, the <Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra> gives a sixteen-dimensional ground space. At level one, $\alpha_{-1}^i$ and $\psi_{-1}^i$ each provide $8\cdot16$ states, hence multiplicities $16,256$ at squared masses $0,1/\alpha'$. No half-integer Ramond levels occur. The <GSO projection> keeps odd NS <fermion> number and one total Ramond <chirality>, giving $8$ <bosons> and $8$ <fermions> at zero mass, then $128$ of each at the first massive level.

= First massive level of the open RNS string
{parent=Spinning string}
{title2=$\alpha'M^2=1,\quad128_B+128_F$}

After the <GSO projection>, the first massive open-string level has NS oscillator number $3/2$ and Ramond oscillator number $1$. Eight transverse directions give $8+64+\binom83=128$ NS states and $8\cdot8+8\cdot8=128$ R states. They form the massive Spin(9) representations $44+84$ and a gamma-traceless vector-spinor of dimension $9\cdot16-16=128$. The identical chiral oscillator count in a <closed string> has the different mass normalization $\alpha'M^2=4$.

= GSO projection
{c}
{parent=Spinning string}
{title2=$P_{\mathrm{GSO}}$}
{wiki}

The Gliozzi–Scherk–Olive projection selects consistent worldsheet fermion-number and chirality sectors in an RNS string theory. In suitable supersymmetric choices it removes the tachyonic <Neveu–Schwarz sector> ground state and keeps a chosen <Ramond sector> chirality. Together with the consistent sector combinations it produces spacetime <fermions> and, for theories such as <Type II superstring theory>, a tachyon-free spectrum. The mere presence of classical <worldsheet supersymmetry> does not specify this projection or guarantee tachyon removal.

= Half-level Neveu–Schwarz vector state
{parent=Neveu–Schwarz sector}
{title2=$|A;p\rangle=A\cdot b_{-1/2}|0;p\rangle$}

The lowest fermionic excitation has level $1/2$. Its old-covariant physical conditions are $\alpha'p^2+1/2-a=0$ and $p\cdot A=0$. For nonzero real momentum, timelike $p$ gives a positive polarization space, null $p$ gives a nonnegative space with a longitudinal null direction, and spacelike $p$ permits a negative-norm time polarization. The limiting $a=1/2$ state is a massless gauge vector after quotienting null polarizations. This level-specific test does not prove positivity of every higher NS level.

= Nonzero-momentum condition in the massless vector norm test
{parent=Half-level Neveu–Schwarz vector state}
{title2=$p=0,\ a=1/2,\ A=(1,0,\ldots)\ \Rightarrow\ A^*\cdot A=-1$}

The usual half-level NS claim that all physical vector polarizations have nonnegative norm for $a\le1/2$ assumes nonzero particle momentum. At zero momentum and limiting intercept, all positive-mode <constraints> and transversality allow a timelike polarization with negative norm, and $A\sim A+\kappa p$ removes nothing. Excluding this exceptional momentum restores the ordinary null-vector argument; retaining it makes the unrestricted elementary criterion strict $a<1/2$.

= Worldsheet supersymmetry
{parent=Spinning string}

Local <supersymmetry> on a <string worldsheet> relates embedding coordinates to their <worldsheet Majorana fermion> partners. Fixing this fermionic gauge symmetry introduces commuting <superconformal ghosts>.

= On-shell closure of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry
{parent=Worldsheet supersymmetry}
{title2=$[\delta_1,\delta_2]=2\bar\epsilon_1\rho^\alpha\epsilon_2\,\partial_\alpha$}

For $\delta X=-\bar\epsilon\psi$ and $\delta\psi=\rho^\alpha\partial_\alpha X\epsilon$, with constant Grassmann-odd Majorana parameters, the commutator on $X$ is the displayed translation. Set $M=\epsilon_1\bar\epsilon_2-\epsilon_2\bar\epsilon_1$. In two dimensions $\{\rho^\alpha,M\}=2\bar\epsilon_1\rho^\alpha\epsilon_2$ times the identity, so the fermion commutator is the translation minus $M\rho^\alpha\partial_\alpha\psi$. The remainder vanishes on the fermion field equation. This distinguishes on-shell closure from the off-shell total-derivative invariance of the action.

= Superconformal gauge
{parent=Worldsheet supersymmetry}

The local gauge choice $h_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\omega}\eta_{\mu\nu}$ and $\chi_\mu=0$ for an <RNS string>. Diffeomorphisms and Weyl symmetry fix the metric, while local <supersymmetry> and <super-Weyl symmetry> fix the gravitino. Stress-tensor and supercurrent constraints survive the gauge choice and eliminate longitudinal degrees of freedom. Higher-genus surfaces retain metric and spin-structure moduli.

= Super-Weyl symmetry

A local fermionic gauge symmetry shifting the gamma-trace part of the <worldsheet gravitino>, schematically $\delta\chi_\mu=\gamma_\mu\rho$. Together with local <worldsheet supersymmetry> it permits the gravitino to be set to zero locally in <superconformal gauge>. Global spin structures and moduli still remain.

= Worldsheet gravitino
{parent=Worldsheet supersymmetry}
{title2=$\chi_\mu$}

The worldsheet gravitino is the fermionic vector-spinor gauge field for local <worldsheet supersymmetry>, paired with the worldsheet metric in two-dimensional <supergravity>. In a locally supersymmetric string action it couples to the worldsheet supercurrent. Fixing its gauge and the bosonic <conformal gauge> leaves the usual flat rigid transformations on embeddings and <worldsheet Majorana fermions>; it should not be silently omitted when claiming an arbitrary curved-worldsheet locally supersymmetric action.

= Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term
{parent=Worldsheet supersymmetry}
{title2=$\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu J^\mu$}

In flat <conformal gauge>, take $\mathcal L=\partial X\cdot\partial X+i\alpha'\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu\psi$ and rigid transformations $\delta X=iA\bar\epsilon\psi$, $\delta\psi=B\gamma^\nu\partial_\nu X\epsilon$, with $A=\alpha'B$. The <Majorana Grassmann bilinear interchange> and <Clifford algebra> combine the variations into
$$
\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu\bigl(iA\partial_\nu X\cdot\bar\epsilon\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\psi\bigr).
$$
The identity is off shell. The integrated action is invariant when its boundary flux vanishes, for example on a closed worldsheet or with compatible supersymmetric endpoint conditions. Constant spinors and ordinary derivatives here refer to flat gauge; arbitrary curved worldsheets require covariant spinor derivatives and the locally supersymmetric completion.

= Chiral proof of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry
{parent=Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term}
{title2=$\delta\mathcal L=i\epsilon_2\partial_-(\psi_1\partial_+X)-i\epsilon_1\partial_+(\psi_2\partial_-X)$}

Let $\partial_\pm=\partial_\tau\pm\partial_\sigma$ and $\mathcal L=\partial_+X\cdot\partial_-X+i\psi_1\cdot\partial_+\psi_1+i\psi_2\cdot\partial_-\psi_2$. For constant Grassmann-odd parameters, set $\delta X=i(\epsilon_2\psi_1-\epsilon_1\psi_2)$, $\delta\psi_1=-\epsilon_2\partial_-X$, $\delta\psi_2=\epsilon_1\partial_+X$. In the first chiral sector the variation of the fermionic term cancels $i\epsilon_2\partial_+\psi_1\cdot\partial_-X$ from the bosonic term, leaving $i\epsilon_2\partial_-(\psi_1\cdot\partial_+X)$. The second sector gives the other <total derivative>. This proves off-shell invariance when the boundary flux vanishes. Closure on <fermions> uses their free <equations of motion>. A constant parameter must also preserve the chosen boundary conditions and <spin structure>.

= Spin-structure obstruction to constant worldsheet supersymmetry
{parent=Rigid worldsheet supersymmetry boundary term}

The local rigid <worldsheet supersymmetry> identity $\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu J^\mu$ does not by itself prove preservation of every <spin structure>. If $X$ is periodic on a spatial circle and $\psi$ is antiperiodic, a constant $\epsilon$ gives antiperiodic $\delta X=i\bar\epsilon\psi$. It does not preserve the bosonic boundary condition. Compatible periodic fields admit the constant transformation; other sectors must be handled with the local superconformal formulation and their global spin data.

= Worldsheet Majorana fermion

Each real chiral fermion has <conformal weight> $1/2$ and contributes $1/2$ to the <central charge>. The <Ramond sector> and <Neveu–Schwarz sector> differ by periodic versus antiperiodic boundary conditions.

= Worldsheet chirality and propagation direction
{parent=Worldsheet Majorana fermion}
{title2=$\psi_+=F(\tau-\sigma),\ \psi_-=G(\tau+\sigma)$}

In signature $(-,+)$, choose real <gamma matrices> $\gamma^0=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, $\gamma^1=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and $\gamma_*=-\gamma^0\gamma^1$. Positive <chirality> is right-moving and negative <chirality> is left-moving in this orientation. Reversing the definition of $\gamma_*$ exchanges the labels without changing the physics.

The string coupling controls topology change and string splitting and joining. A constant background <dilaton> $\Phi_0$ sets $g_s=e^{\Phi_0}$.

= String genus expansion

For a connected oriented closed-string worldsheet of genus $g$ with $n$ external closed-string vertices, the perturbative amplitude carries $g_s^{2g-2+n}$. The exponent combines the dilaton factor $g_s^{-\chi}$ for Euler characteristic $\chi=2-2g$ with one $g_s$ from each normalized vertex.

= String-loop effective action expansion

Connected oriented <closed string> worldsheets of genus $g$ give the spacetime effective-action term $g_s^{2g-2}S_g$. Each $S_g$ has a separate low-energy derivative expansion in $\alpha'$. The <string coupling> orders quantum string loops, while $\alpha'$ orders effects of finite string size.

= Dilaton Euler-characteristic weighting

A constant <dilaton> contributes $\Phi_0\chi(\Sigma)$ to the Euclidean <worldsheet> action by the <Gauss-Bonnet theorem>. With <string coupling> $g_s=e^{\Phi_0}$ and <Euler characteristic> $\chi=2-2g$, the path-integral weight is $g_s^{2g-2}$. Canonically normalized external closed-string vertices add one power of $g_s$ each.

String perturbation theory expands amplitudes in powers of the <String coupling> by summing over worldsheet topologies. It is reliable where the local effective coupling $e^{\Phi(X)}$ is small.

= Open-string scattering amplitude

Tree-level scattering of four ordered <open string> tachyons gives the <Veneziano amplitude>. Its poles factorize through intermediate open-string states.

= Veneziano amplitude

{c}
{title2=$A(s,t)=\frac{\Gamma(-1-\alpha's)\Gamma(-1-\alpha't)}{\Gamma(-2-\alpha'(s+t))}$}
{wiki}

The ordered tree-level four-tachyon <open string> amplitude is proportional to $\Gamma(-1-\alpha's)\Gamma(-1-\alpha't)/\Gamma(-2-\alpha'(s+t))$. Its <Mandelstam variables> locate the mass levels, and pole residues reveal spin content.

= Fixed-angle softness of an open-string amplitude
{parent=Veneziano amplitude}
{title2=$\log|A|\sim-\alpha's[-x\log x-(1-x)\log(1-x)]$}

At high energy with $x=-t/s\in(0,1)$ fixed, reflection and Stirling formulas for the <gamma function> give the displayed exponential envelope, away from real-axis resonance poles or after suitable analytic continuation. It is softer than the power-law fixed-angle behavior of typical point-particle interactions and exposes the finite string length. This limit differs from the fixed-$t$ Regge limit and does not by itself prove finiteness of every loop amplitude.

= Veneziano amplitude pole residue
{parent=Veneziano amplitude}
{c}
{title2=$\operatorname*{Res}_{s=n-1}A=-\frac1{n!}\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}(t+j)$}

In units $\alpha'=1$, generic fixed $t$ gives <simple poles> at $s=n-1$, $n\geq0$, with <residue> $-\prod_{j=2}^{n+1}(t+j)/n!$. The empty product is one. A <residue> zero removes that pole, and fixed $t$ must be away from its own channel singularities.

= Odd-level pole cancellation in the symmetric Veneziano amplitude
{parent=Veneziano amplitude pole residue}
{title2=$\operatorname*{Res}A_{\rm sym}\propto[1+(-1)^n]\prod_{j=1}^n(\alpha't+j+1)$}

For identical open-string tachyons, $s+t+u=-4/\alpha'$. At $\alpha's=n-1$, the ordered <Veneziano amplitude> residues in the $s,t$ and $s,u$ terms differ by $(-1)^n$, so their symmetric sum cancels odd-level $s$-channel poles at generic $t$. In particular the massless vector does not couple to this identical-scalar singlet amplitude. It remains a state of the open-string spectrum and is visible in suitable ordered or color-dependent amplitudes. Pole absence due to an external-state selection rule must not be interpreted as absence of the exchanged state from the theory.

= Massless vector pole of the Veneziano amplitude
{parent=Veneziano amplitude pole residue}
{title2=$\operatorname*{Res}_{s=0}A=-(t+2)$}

The pole at $s=0$ has <residue> $-(t+2)=-(t-u)/2$ for unit-slope tachyons. It is proportional to conserved scalar-pair vector currents and linear in the scattering-angle cosine, exhibiting a massless spin-one intermediate string state.

= Dilaton

{parent=String coupling}
{wiki}

The dilaton is a scalar closed-string background field coupled to the worldsheet Ricci scalar. Its constant mode determines the <string coupling>, while spacetime variation modifies the background-field equations.

= Weyl variation of a nonconstant dilaton coupling
{parent=Dilaton}
{c}
{title2=$\delta_\omega S_\Phi=-(2\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt g\,\omega\Delta_g\Phi(X)$}

In two worldsheet dimensions, $\delta_\omega(\sqrt gR)=-2\sqrt g\Delta_g\omega$. Thus the standard <dilaton> curvature coupling does not have classical <Weyl invariance> for a general spacetime-varying dilaton. A constant dilaton gives the topological <Euler characteristic> instead. In the quantum sigma model, this classical variation combines with the loop anomaly; vanishing improved <sigma-model beta functions> is the full background conformal condition.

= Dilaton normalization and Euler-characteristic weighting
{parent=Dilaton}
{title2=$g_s=e^{\varphi_0},\quad e^{-\varphi_0\chi}=g_s^{2g-2}$}

With Euclidean curvature coupling $(4\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt h\,\varphi R$, a constant <dilaton> contributes $\varphi_0\chi$ by the <Gauss-Bonnet theorem>. The genus-$g$ closed-string weight is $e^{-\varphi_0\chi}=g_s^{2g-2}$ with $g_s=e^{\varphi_0}$. If the coefficient is instead written as $\int\sqrt h\,\Phi R$, then in the same convention $\varphi=4\pi\Phi$. The field normalization must be fixed before reading off the coupling.

= Lorentzian dilaton coupling sign convention
{c}
{parent=Dilaton}

With worldsheet signature $(-,+)$, $e^{iS_L}=e^{-S_E}$, and the curvature convention $R[e^{2\omega}h]=e^{-2\omega}(R[h]-2\Box_h\omega)$, the usual negative Lorentzian kinetic action becomes positive in Euclidean signature. A positive Lorentzian term $(4\pi)^{-1}\int\sqrt{-h}\,R\Phi$ becomes a negative Euclidean curvature coupling. Therefore the conventional Euclidean <dilaton> with positive curvature coupling is $\varphi=-\Phi$. The <leading metric-dilaton Weyl condition> is $R_{ab}+2\nabla_a\nabla_b\varphi=0$, or $R_{ab}-2\nabla_a\nabla_b\Phi=0$ with that literal positive Lorentzian coupling. Keeping the same named field and reversing the action's curvature-coupling sign reverses the Hessian term. The continuation follows directly from $d\tau=-i\,d\tau_E$ and $S_E=-iS_L$; the geometric scalar curvature continues without an extra arbitrary sign.

= Bosonic string theory
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

Bosonic string theory quantizes a relativistic string using only bosonic worldsheet fields. Lorentz-invariant quantization requires 26-dimensional target spacetime and contains a tachyonic ground state.

= Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{title2=$4+N_f/2-26=0$}

Keeping the ordinary reparameterization <bc ghost system>, four embedding bosons require 44 additional real chiral fermions in each chirality to cancel the <worldsheet Weyl anomaly>. These are internal matter fields, not an assumed locally supersymmetric RNS construction. Their bilinear currents generate the chiral $\mathfrak{so}(44)$ current algebra, with $\binom{44}{2}=946$ currents. A full closed fermionic model also requires consistent spin-structure and modular projections; the central-charge count alone does not determine these choices.

= Zero- and two-fermion vertices of an internally fermionized bosonic string
{parent=Internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string}
{title2=$M^2=-4/\alpha',\ -2/\alpha',\ 0$}

In the unprojected polynomial Neveu–Schwarz vertex sector, zero fermion insertions give the usual closed-string tachyon and the massless graviton, two-form and dilaton. Two insertions split into a left-right pair $\psi^i\bar\psi^j e^{ipX}$ of internal weights $(1/2,1/2)$ and a same-chirality current pair of weight one. The first has $M^2=-2/\alpha'$ and 44-squared scalar components. The second requires a bosonic derivative on the opposite side for level matching and gives the two sets of 946 massless gauge vectors. Four-fermion scalar-current products belong to a different insertion count. A chosen global projection may retain a subset, so these operators do not by themselves specify a projected string spectrum.

= Open string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{wiki}

An open string has a worldsheet with boundary. Its endpoint conditions may be <Neumann boundary conditions>, <Dirichlet boundary conditions>, or mixtures selected independently in target-space directions.

= Open-string oscillator degeneracy
{parent=Open string}
{title2=$F(w)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1-w^n)^{-24}$}

For an ordinary critical <open string> with all <Neumann boundary conditions>, there are 24 independent transverse creation oscillators at each positive mode number $n$. Summing their nonnegative occupation numbers gives one factor $(1-w^n)^{-24}$. The coefficient $d_p=[w^p]F(w)$ counts oscillator polarizations at level $p$, excluding continuous center-of-mass momentum and additional endpoint multiplicities. <Cauchy coefficient formula> gives $d_p=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint F(w)w^{-p-1}dw$.

= Large-level degeneracy of an open bosonic string
{parent=Open-string oscillator degeneracy}
{title2=$d_p\sim2^{-1/2}p^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt p}$}

The <Dedekind eta function> modular transformation gives $F(e^{-t})\sim(t/2\pi)^{12}\exp(4\pi^2/t-t)$. The <saddle-point approximation> for coefficient extraction has $t_0=2\pi/\sqrt{p-1}$, phase $4\pi\sqrt{p-1}$ and curvature $(p-1)^{3/2}/\pi$. Its prefactor $t_0^{12}$ supplies $(p-1)^{-6}$ and the Gaussian width supplies $(p-1)^{-3/4}/\sqrt2$. This explains both the exponential growth and the power $-27/4$.

= Open-string endpoint momentum flux
{parent=Open string}
{title2=$J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$}

For the <Nambu-Goto phase-space action>, $\dot P_m=\partial_\sigma J_m$ with $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Thus $\dot{\mathcal P}_m=J_m(\pi)-J_m(0)$. Free endpoint variations force $J_m=0$ individually. A <Dirichlet boundary condition> allows an external support or <D-brane> to absorb momentum in the fixed direction.

= Variational open-string boundary conditions
{parent=Open-string endpoint momentum flux}
{title2=$J_m\delta X^m|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

The spatial integration-by-parts term in the <Nambu-Goto phase-space action> pairs endpoint displacement with momentum flux $J_m=eT^2X'_m+uP_m$. Free endpoint displacements require zero flux. Fixed normal coordinates instead set those variations to zero, while tangential variations retain a <Neumann boundary condition>. Boundary conditions must also be preserved by the equations of motion. In a boundary-preserving gauge with $u=0$, mixed <Neumann boundary conditions> and <Dirichlet boundary conditions> implement a planar <D-brane>.

= Free-end string boundary condition
{parent=Open-string endpoint momentum flux}
{title2=$X'^m|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

Free endpoint variations set the <open string> flux $eT^2X'_m+uP_m$ to zero. In a boundary-adapted temporal gauge, freedom to vary time forces $u=0$ at the end. Nonzero lapse then gives $X'^m=0$, a <Neumann boundary condition>.

= Null motion of a free string endpoint
{parent=Free-end string boundary condition}
{title2=$\dot X^2|_{\partial\Sigma}=0$}

In a boundary-adapted <temporal gauge>, a free <open string> endpoint has $X'^m=0$. The <Nambu–Goto phase-space constraints> then imply $P^2=0$, while $\dot X=eP$ implies $\dot X^2=0$. For $X^0=t$, its spatial speed is one. This null boundary is compatible with a timelike interior <worldsheet>.

= Open-string mode expansion
{parent=Open string}

In <conformal gauge>, a free open-string coordinate with <Neumann boundary conditions> has a cosine expansion. Canonical quantization turns its nonzero Fourier coefficients into <string oscillators>.

= Integer-mode expansion with parallel D-brane endpoints
{parent=Open-string mode expansion}
{title2=$\cos n\sigma\ \text{for NN},\quad\sin n\sigma\ \text{for DD}$}

On $0\le\sigma\le\pi$, a coordinate with <Neumann boundary conditions> at both ends has a constant position, linear time <zero mode> and cosine oscillators. A coordinate with <Dirichlet boundary conditions> at both ends has a linear spatial interpolation between endpoint positions and sine oscillators, with no <momentum> <zero mode>. Both have <integer> frequencies. This differs from an <ND boundary condition>, which applies different conditions at the two ends of the same coordinate and gives half-integer frequencies.

= Oscillator symplectic form of an open string
{parent=Open-string mode expansion}
{title2=$\{\alpha_j^m,\alpha_k^n\}=-ij\eta^{mn}\delta_{j+k,0}$}

The Fourier kinetic term $\sum_{k>0}i\alpha_{-k}\cdot\dot\alpha_k/k$ supplies conjugate pairs, with $\alpha_{-k}=\alpha_k^*$. Inverting its <symplectic form> yields the displayed <Poisson brackets>. Nonzero modes are independent of the center-of-mass pair, while $\alpha_0=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p$ is the momentum zero mode. The imaginary factors are compatible with a real action up to a total derivative.

A transverse <open string> coordinate has an ND boundary condition when one endpoint obeys a <Neumann boundary condition> and the other a <Dirichlet boundary condition>. On a strip of width $\pi$, its nonconstant spatial <eigenfunctions> are $\cos((n+1/2)\sigma)$ when the free endpoint is at zero. = Bosonic ND boundary-changing conformal weight

{title2=$h_{\mathrm{ND}}=1/16$}

For one free boson, the mixed ND open-string sector has strip vacuum energy $E_0=1/48$. The strip-to-plane transformation relates this energy to the plane <Virasoro algebra> zero mode by $E_0=h-c/24$, with <central charge> $c=1$. Hence its ground <conformal weight> is $h=1/48+1/24=1/16$. For $d$ independent ND coordinates the weights add to $d/16$. = Closed string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}
{wiki}

= Closed bosonic string
{synonym}

A closed string has a cylindrical worldsheet without boundary. Periodicity permits independent left- and right-moving oscillator sectors and, in a compact target direction, winding modes.

= Large-level degeneracy of a closed bosonic string
{parent=Closed string}
{title2=$g_N\sim\tfrac12(N-1)^{-27/2}e^{8\pi\sqrt{N-1}}$}

The left and right transverse oscillator sectors each have <generating function> $\prod_{m\ge1}(1-q^m)^{-24}$. Its coefficient satisfies $d_N\sim2^{-1/2}(N-1)^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt{N-1}}$. <Closed-string level matching> requires equal levels but leaves their polarizations independent, so $g_N=d_N^2$. With $\alpha'M^2=4(N-1)$, the exponential is $e^{\beta_H M}$ with $\beta_H=4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$. The smooth rest-mass density is proportional to $M^{-26}e^{\beta_H M}$, since $dN/dM=\alpha'M/2$. This distinguishes degeneracy per level from density per unit mass. The <polynomial> prefactor matters at the boundary of canonical convergence.

= Closed-string mode expansion
{parent=Closed string}

A free closed-string embedding coordinate is the sum of center-of-mass zero modes and independent periodic left- and right-moving oscillator expansions.

= Covariant quantization of the bosonic string
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

Covariant quantization keeps target-spacetime Lorentz covariance manifest, imposes canonical commutation relations on every embedding coordinate and removes unphysical states with the <Virasoro constraints>.

= No-ghost theorem below the critical bosonic dimension
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}
{title2=$2\le d\le26,\quad a=1$}

For integer target dimension $2\le d\le26$, intercept $a=1$ and ordinary nonzero momentum, the <old covariant string quantization> conditions leave a positive-semidefinite physical inner product. Quotienting its physical <null string states> removes gauge degeneracy. A <DDF operator> basis displays $d-2$ positive transverse oscillators; below dimension 26 the remaining physical longitudinal sector can be organized as a unitary <Virasoro algebra> sector with central charge $26-d$ and highest weight zero. At $d=26$ this extra sector becomes null and the quotient reduces to the 24 transverse oscillators. The result concerns negative-norm states, not the removal of the bosonic <tachyon>, and it does not itself cancel the <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> of a subcritical matter theory.

= DDF operator
{c}
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}
{title2=$[A_m^i,A_n^j]=m\delta^{ij}\delta_{m+n,0}$}

= DDF oscillator
{c}
{synonym}

DDF operators are transverse physical <string oscillators> constructed as contour integrals of weight-one <string vertex operators>. Choose a null reference momentum $q$ and transverse polarizations $\epsilon_i$ orthogonal to it and to a reference on-shell momentum. The integrand is proportional to $\epsilon_i\cdot\partial X\,e^{inq\cdot X}$; its normalization and $q\cdot p$ are fixed so the operators obey the oscillator algebra in the title and commute with the positive-mode <Virasoro constraints>. For an open-string tachyon seed, one can use $2\alpha' q\cdot p=1$. Acting with negative modes shifts momentum and raises oscillator level while preserving the physical mass-shell condition. Their transverse <Fock space> has positive norm and makes the physical content of the <no-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string> explicit.

= No-ghost theorem for the critical bosonic string
{parent=Covariant quantization of the bosonic string}

In flat 26-dimensional <bosonic string theory>, at ordinary nonzero momentum, take <physical string states> satisfying $L_{n>0}|\psi\rangle=0$ and $(L_0-1)|\psi\rangle=0$. The <inner product> restricted to these states is positive semidefinite. Quotienting by physical <null string states> removes the degeneracy; the resulting <Hilbert space> is represented by the 24 transverse <string oscillators>. This is a statement about negative-norm matter states, not about removing the auxiliary <Faddeev-Popov ghosts>. It does not remove the bosonic <tachyon>.

= Level-two open-string polarization decomposition

= Polyakov action
{c}

The Polyakov action introduces an independent worldsheet metric $g_{\alpha\beta}$:

S_{\rm P}=-\frac T2\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-g}\,
g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X\mathbin\cdot\partial_\beta X.
$$
Its metric equation makes $g_{\alpha\beta}$ conformal to the <induced worldsheet metric>, reducing it classically to the <Nambu–Goto action>.

= String sigma model in a spacetime metric
{parent=Polyakov action}

Replacing the flat target metric in the <Polyakov action> by a <spacetime metric> $g_{\mu\nu}(X)$ gives the displayed string sigma model. The embedding fields $X^\mu$ map the <worldsheet> into target spacetime, while $h_{ab}$ is an independent <worldsheet metric>. Classical <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl transformations> permit <conformal gauge>; quantum consistency additionally requires vanishing <sigma-model beta functions>.

= String sigma model in an isotropic plane wave
{parent=String sigma model in a spacetime metric}
{title2=$ds^2=-2,dX^+dX^--m^2|X|^2(dX^+)^2+dX^I dX^I$}

In <conformal gauge> and <light-cone gauge in string theory> $X^+=\kappa\tau$, the transverse part of the <string sigma model in a spacetime metric> reduces to
$$
S_\perp=\frac T2\int d\tau d\sigma\,\left(\dot X^I\dot X^I-X'^IX'^I-m^2\kappa^2X^IX^I\right).
$$
The omitted longitudinal term is a total time derivative. Each transverse coordinate obeys a massive <wave equation>. If the spatial period is $L$, the longitudinal momentum is $p^+=T\kappa L$ in this normalization. Keeping the gauge slope explicit prevents missing a factor of $p^+$ when changing conventions.

= Plane-wave string mode Hamiltonian

Expand every transverse <closed string> coordinate in a real orthonormal <Fourier basis> of the spatial circle. Canonical quantization gives $\omega_a(a_{Ia}^\dagger a_{Ia}+1/2)$ for each nonzero-frequency mode; a zero-frequency mode retains the free particle term $p_{Ia}^2/(2T)$. The formal <zero-point energy> requires a regulator; it is not automatically the massless flat-background intercept. Opposite travelling-wave oscillator combinations diagonalize the translation generator and satisfy <closed-string level matching>.

= Plane-wave string mode frequency

For a <closed string> in a <string sigma model in an isotropic plane wave>, insert $e^{-i\omega_n\tau+2\pi in\sigma/L}$ into the transverse <wave equation>. The <Polyakov path integral> integrates over <string worldsheet> metrics as well as embedding fields, modulo <worldsheet diffeomorphisms> and <Weyl transformations>. = Worldsheet conformal Killing group

The worldsheet conformal Killing group is the connected residual group of conformal automorphisms of the chosen compact <Riemann surface>. Its complex dimensions are three on a sphere, one on a torus, and zero for <genus> at least two. The sphere group consists of <Möbius transformations>, while the torus's connected group is translation. = Closed-string physical-state Virasoro conditions

= Background field expansion of a string sigma model
{parent=String nonlinear sigma model}

Expand the <string embedding map> along target geodesics as $X=\exp_{\bar X}Y$. This is why the target gravitational equation arises from quantum <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> cancellation, rather than from the classical worldsheet metric variation. = Dilaton equation from contracted Bianchi identity

The additional dilaton anomaly coefficient fixes $C=(D-26)/(3\alpha')$ at leading order for the bosonic string without an antisymmetric background. In critical dimension it is zero; noncritical backgrounds have a central-charge-deficit term.

= Ghost oscillator vacuum
{parent=Worldsheet ghost field}

For a <closed string>, the currents $J_\pm=P\pm TX'$ have vanishing mixed <Poisson brackets>. The densities $\mathcal H_\pm=J_\pm^2/(4T)$ therefore generate commuting constraint algebras. = Rotating Nambu–Goto string

= Free-ended straight-string Regge relation

= Global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators
{parent=Two-dimensional conformal field theory}

For <primary operators> of holomorphic weights $h_i$ and a <Möbius transformation> invariant vacuum, infinitesimal translations, dilations and special conformal transformations give $\sum_i\partial_iG=0$, $\sum_i(z_i\partial_i+h_i)G=0$, and $\sum_i(z_i^2\partial_i+2h_i z_i)G=0$. = Liouville field theory

<Liouville field theory> is a two-dimensional scalar theory with a curvature background charge and an exponential interaction. = Radial quantization

<Radial quantization> treats circles around the origin as equal-time slices and logarithmic radius as Euclidean time. A local insertion at the origin prepares a state on a surrounding circle. Contours of the stress tensor act on that insertion, turning its singular <operator product expansion> into the <Virasoro algebra> action on the corresponding state.

= Quasi-primary operator

A <quasi-primary operator> transforms with weights $(h,\widetilde h)$ under global conformal, or Möbius, transformations. In <radial quantization>, quasi-primary states are annihilated by $L_1,\widetilde L_1$, whereas <primary states> are annihilated by every positive Virasoro mode. = Free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory

A real chiral <Majorana fermion> with contraction $\psi(z)\psi(w)\sim1/(z-w)$ has stress tensor $T=-:\psi\partial\psi:/2$. The even and odd fermion-number parts of its <Neveu–Schwarz sector> give unitary modules with lowest weights $0$ and $1/2$. Their cyclic highest-weight submodules are irreducible: a positive-energy invariant proper submodule would have a lowest vector orthogonal to every descendant of the original highest vector, which is impossible in a positive inner-product cyclic module.

A beta-gamma system is a pair of commuting chiral fields with first-order action $\int\beta\bar\partial\gamma$. If $\gamma$ has weight $h$ and $\beta$ has weight $1-h$, its central charge is $2(6h^2-6h+1)$.

= Stress tensor of a commuting beta-gamma system
{parent=Beta-gamma system}
{title2=$T=(1-\lambda):\partial\beta\,\gamma:-\lambda:\beta\,\partial\gamma:,\quad c=12\lambda^2-12\lambda+2$}

Using the <radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions>, single contractions give $T(z)\beta(w)\sim\lambda\beta(w)/(z-w)^2+\partial\beta(w)/(z-w)$ and the corresponding weight $1-\lambda$ for $\gamma$. = Radial-ordering sign in commuting beta-gamma contractions

= Exceptional massless ground states of a bosonic circle
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{title2=$R=|n|\sqrt{\alpha'}/2\ \text{or}\ R=2\sqrt{\alpha'}/|w|$}

In the critical <closed bosonic string>, compactification of one coordinate gives $M^2=n^2/R^2+w^2R^2/\alpha'^2+2(N+\widetilde N-2)/\alpha'$ and $\widetilde N-N+nw=0$. At oscillator level zero, level matching forces $nw=0$. These are threshold states descended from the bosonic <tachyon>; they are additional to the neutral <graviton>, two-form, vectors, <dilaton> and radius modulus. = Compact-circle closed-string level matching
{parent=Momentum and winding modes}
{title2=$\widetilde N-N+nw=0$}

For a compact string coordinate of radius $R$, choose $p_L=n/R+wR/\alpha'$ and $p_R=n/R-wR/\alpha'$, where the left-moving oscillators are denoted by tildes. The two classical zero-mode <Virasoro constraints> give $M^2=p_L^2+4\widetilde N/\alpha'=p_R^2+4N/\alpha'$. Their difference yields the displayed shifted <closed-string level matching> condition. Classically the center-of-mass momentum can be continuous; the integer momentum number belongs to compact-coordinate quantization. Quantum intercepts shift the mass formula but cancel out of level matching.

= First-order action for Abelian worldsheet duality

Eliminating $A$ from the full action, including the multiplier term, gives $\tfrac12V^{-1}\partial_\mu\widetilde z\partial^\mu\widetilde z$. Omitting the multiplier term during substitution would give the wrong sign. This derives the block-diagonal metric inversion in the <Buscher rules>.

= Oscillator vacua
{synonym}

An oscillator vacuum is annihilated by all positive-frequency oscillator annihilation operators. For a <Ramond sector> string this leaves a degenerate space on which the fermionic zero modes act through the <Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra>. Vacuum annihilation therefore does not select a unique spinor state.

= Half-integer open-string oscillator
{parent=String oscillator}
{title2=$\alpha_r^i,\quad r\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12$}

For a <Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition>, the transverse modes have frequencies $r\in\mathbb Z+1/2$. The <canonical commutation relations> give $[\alpha_r^i,\alpha_s^j]=r\delta^{ij}\delta_{r+s,0}$ and $\alpha_r^\dagger=\alpha_{-r}$. One derivation expands $X-y=\sum_{r>0}q_r\cos(r\sigma)$: spatial <orthogonality> reduces the <Polyakov action> to $(8\alpha')^{-1}\int\sum_r(\dot q_r^2-r^2q_r^2)d\tau$. These are <harmonic oscillators> of <mass> $1/(4\alpha')$, whose normalized <creation operators> satisfy $\alpha_{-r}=i\sqrt r\,a_r^\dagger$ in the conventional phased expansion.

= String level operator
{title2=$N$}
{parent=String oscillator}

The string level operator is the weighted oscillator number $N=\sum_{n>0}\alpha_{-n}\mathbin\cdot\alpha_n$. A closed string has independent operators $N$ and $\widetilde N$ for its two chiral sectors.

= Integer string oscillator level
{parent=String level operator}
{title2=$N=\sum_{k,i}k r_{ki}$}

The <canonical commutation relations> imply $[N,\alpha_{-k}^i]=k\alpha_{-k}^i$ for $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. With zero level assigned to the <oscillator vacuum>, a <Fock space> basis state containing $r_{ki}$ <creation operators> of each mode has level $\sum_{k,i}kr_{ki}$. The level is therefore a nonnegative integer; it counts oscillator excitation weighted by frequency, not simply the number of creation operators.

= Closed-string level matching
{parent=String oscillator}

Closed-string periodicity and the residual spatial constraint require equal left- and right-moving excitation levels, $N=\widetilde N$.

= Normal-ordering constant of a string
{title2=$a$}
{parent=String oscillator}

= String intercept
{synonym}

The normal-ordering constant is the zero-point shift in the quantum Virasoro generator. Closure of the target-space Lorentz algebra fixes $a=1$ and the spacetime dimension $D=26$ for the bosonic string.

= Critical-vector assumption in the string intercept argument
{parent=Normal-ordering constant of a string}
{title2=$M_1^2=(1-a)/\alpha'$}

In the usual critical Lorentz-invariant bosonic string, level-one transverse vector polarizations fit a massless representation, whereas a massive vector would require an extra polarization. This fixes the intercept to one. Polarization counting alone does not establish that conclusion in every dimension: in three dimensions the one transverse polarization transforms trivially under the transverse rotation group and can be interpreted as a scalar. The quantum consistency assumptions must accompany an argument that the first level must be massless.

= Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy
{c}
{parent=Normal-ordering constant of a string}
{title2=$E_0=d/48$}

Each of $d$ physical transverse <bosons> with a <Neumann-Dirichlet open-string boundary condition> contributes $\tfrac12\sum_{r>0}r=1/48$ by the <half-integer zeta-regularized mode sum>. Thus $L_0^\perp=N+d/48$ and the convention $L_0=\alpha'p^2+N-a$ has $a=-d/48$. For $d=24$, the vacuum shift is $+1/2$. The ordinary integer-moded open-string shift is instead $-d/24$; these endpoint sectors have different normal-ordering constants.

= Lowest levels of a fully transverse ND bosonic string
{parent=Neumann-Dirichlet string zero-point energy}

With 24 physical <half-integer open-string oscillators>, the first three <string level operator> <eigenvalues> are $0,1/2,1$. Their states are the vacuum, $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}|0\rangle$, and $a_{1/2}^{i\dagger}a_{1/2}^{j\dagger}|0\rangle$. The <creation operators> commute, so the third level is the <symmetric square> of the 24-dimensional transverse vector, of dimension $24\cdot25/2=300$. Its trace is a scalar and its trace-free part has dimension 299. Including the vacuum shift gives $\alpha'M^2=1/2,1,3/2$ when the two longitudinal directions are common directions with <Neumann boundary conditions>. The representation labels refer to the surviving ND rotation group, not full unbroken target Lorentz symmetry.

= Bosonic string mass spectrum
{parent=String oscillator}

For a closed bosonic string,
$$
M^2=\frac4{\alpha'}(N-a)=\frac4{\alpha'}(\widetilde N-a),
\qquad N=\widetilde N.
$$

= Closed-bosonic-string mass shell from primary weights
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$M^2=4(N-1)/\alpha',\quad N=\widetilde N$}

A matter vertex with left and right oscillator levels $N,\widetilde N$ and momentum $k$ has weights $N+\alpha'k^2/4,\widetilde N+\alpha'k^2/4$. An <integrated string vertex operator> must have weights $(1,1)$, giving the displayed mass shell and level matching with $M^2=-k^2$. Physical states are further identified modulo <null string states>, equivalently through <BRST cohomology>; merely counting all weight-one primaries would include gauge redundancies.

= Massive two-form at closed-string level two
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$M^2=8\pi T$}

Each level-two chiral sector of the critical <closed string> is the <symmetric traceless square> $S^2_0V$ of the massive <little group> vector space. The <tensor product> of its two sectors contains the <exterior square> $\Lambda^2V$: the equivariant map $(S,T)\mapsto ST-TS$ is onto the antisymmetric matrices. Compact-group complete reducibility realizes this quotient as a subrepresentation. It describes a <massive two-form field> with $M^2=8\pi T=4/\alpha'$.

= Massless first closed-string level
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$N=\widetilde N=1,\quad M^2=0$}

In the critical <bosonic string theory>, the states $\alpha_{-1}^i\widetilde\alpha_{-1}^j|0;p\rangle$ have $M^2=0$. Their coefficient matrix decomposes into a symmetric trace-free tensor, an antisymmetric tensor, and a scalar trace, representing the <graviton>, <Kalb–Ramond field>, and <dilaton>. Their transverse <particle polarization> counts add to $(D-2)^2$. The <normal-ordering constant of a string> is one.

= Open bosonic string mass spectrum
{parent=Bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$\alpha'M^2=N-a$}

The <string level operator> for a free-ended <open string> is $N=\sum_{k>0}\alpha_{-k}\cdot\alpha_k$. The quantum condition is $\alpha'M^2=N-a$, where $a$ is the <normal-ordering constant of a string>. Lorentz-consistent <bosonic string theory> has $a=1$.

= Lowest light-cone levels of an open bosonic string
{parent=Open bosonic string mass spectrum}
{title2=$N=0,1,2$}

The first levels are the <oscillator vacuum>, $\alpha_{-1}^I|0\rangle$, and $\alpha_{-2}^I|0\rangle,\alpha_{-1}^I\alpha_{-1}^J|0\rangle$. At level two the transverse <vector representation> and <symmetric square> assemble into the massive <symmetric traceless square> of one extra rotation dimension.

= Critical dimension of string theory
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

= Critical dimension of the bosonic string
{synonym}

The critical dimension is the target-spacetime dimension in which quantum anomalies obstructing Lorentz or Weyl symmetry cancel. It is 26 for the bosonic string.

Here critical means cancellation of the string’s quantum anomalies; it does not mean the upper or lower critical dimension of a phase transition.

= Bosonic-string critical dimension from ghost descendants
{parent=Critical dimension of string theory}
{title2=$L_2L_{-2}|\Omega\rangle=(2\alpha_0^2+D/2-17)|\Omega\rangle$}

For the <FP ghost> vacuum annihilated by $b_{n\ge0}$ and $c_{n>0}$, the <FP ghost> level-one and level-two descendant coefficients are $-2$ and $-17$. Matter contributes $\alpha_0^2$ and $2\alpha_0^2+D/2$. <Virasoro closure from BRST nilpotence> at level one fixes the total highest weight to $\alpha_0^2/2-1$; level two then requires $D=26$. Imposing ground-state BRST closure gives $\alpha_0^2=2$ and the open bosonic <tachyon>. Interpret the coefficients with the stated <ghost zero-mode pairing>.

= Critical dimension of the RNS superstring
{parent=Critical dimension of string theory}
{title2=$D=10$}

Each of $D$ embedding <bosons> contributes one to the chiral <central charge>, and each real <worldsheet Majorana fermion> contributes one half. The reparameterization <bc system> contributes $-26$, and the <superconformal ghosts> contribute $+11$. Vanishing total <worldsheet Weyl anomaly> therefore requires $3D/2-26+11=0$, or $D=10$. This differs from the critical dimension 26 of the bosonic string, which has no worldsheet <fermions> or superconformal ghosts.

= Massless chiral RNS spectrum after GSO projection
{parent=Critical dimension of the RNS superstring}
{title2=$8_v\oplus8_{s\text{ or }c}$}

In one critical chiral <RNS string> sector, <light-cone gauge in string theory> leaves eight transverse directions. The <Neveu–Schwarz sector> massless states $b_{-1/2}^i|0;k\rangle$ give eight spacetime bosonic polarizations. The <Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra> gives sixteen ground-state polarizations, split into two eight-dimensional spinor chiralities. The supersymmetric <GSO projection> keeps one chirality, giving eight spacetime fermionic polarizations. Without that projection the massless counts do not agree. Counting a chiral sector is distinct from tensoring left and right sectors into closed-string states.

= Koba-Nielsen factor
{c}
{parent=Bosonic string theory}

The Koba-Nielsen factor is the product of powers of pairwise worldsheet insertion-point distances produced by contractions of string vertex operators.

= Möbius invariance of on-shell closed-string vertex integrals
{c}
{parent=Koba-Nielsen factor}
{title2=$\alpha'p_i^2=4$}

Under $z\mapsto(az+b)/(cz+d)$ with $ad-bc=1$, the coordinate measure contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-4}$ at insertion $i$. The <Koba-Nielsen factor> contributes $|cz_i+d|^{-\alpha'\sum_{j\ne i}p_i\cdot p_j}$. <Four-momentum conservation> makes this exponent $\alpha'p_i^2$, so the factors cancel precisely on the displayed <tachyon> mass shell. The correlator alone is covariant; its product with all the coordinate measures is invariant.

= Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude
{c}
{parent=Koba-Nielsen factor}
{wiki}

The Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude is the crossing-symmetric tree-level four-point amplitude of closed-string tachyons. Its worldsheet integral is a complex beta integral, and its poles display the closed-string spectrum.

= Fixed-angle softness of the closed-string amplitude
{parent=Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude}
{title2=$\log|A|\sim-2s f(a)$}

For $t\simeq-as$, $u\simeq-(1-a)s$ with $0<a<1$, the smooth high-energy envelope satisfies $\log|A|\simeq-2s[-a\log a-(1-a)\log(1-a)]$. The <Gamma function> asymptotics cancel the leading $s\log s$ terms, leaving exponential suppression. Exact real-axis resonance poles must be excluded or treated by analytic continuation.

= Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude pole residue
{c}
{parent=Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude}
{title2=$s_n=4(n-1)/\alpha'$}

For $a=-1-\alpha's/4$, $b=-1-\alpha't/4$ and $a+b+c=1$, the amplitude has generic <simple poles> at $a=-n$. The <gamma function> <residue> and recurrence give
$$
\operatorname*{Res}_{s=s_n}A^{(4)}=-\frac{8\pi g_s^2}{\alpha'(n!)^2}\prod_{j=1}^n(b-j)^2.
$$
The <residue> polynomial has degree $2n$, consistent with closed-string exchange up to spin $2n$. When $a=-n+\delta_a$ and $b=-m+\delta_b$, the reciprocal factor $1/\Gamma(a+b)$ removes a putative double pole: the leading ratio is $\binom{n+m}{n}^2(1/\delta_a+1/\delta_b)$. Thus channel singularities must be counted using the kinematic constraint, not by multiplying independent numerator poles.

= Scattering equation
{parent=CHY formalism}

For null or hard external momenta, the scattering equations are
$$
\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{p_i\cdot p_j}{z_i-z_j}=0.

They arise as stationary-point equations for the Koba-Nielsen exponent and are covariant under projective transformations of the insertion points.

= Type II gamma-factor mass poles
{c}

A coincident stack has a non-Abelian low-energy Yang-Mills action; this does not imply that a naive traced determinant is exact at every order.

= Dimensionless electric displacement in DBI theory
{parent=Dirac-Born-Infeld action}
{title2=$\mathbf d=T_3^{-1}\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf E$}

With $\mathbf E_i=2\pi\alpha'F_{0i}$, this normalization separates the nonlinear constitutive relation from the <D3-brane> tension. It is the normalized canonical electric momentum. The <Gauss law constraint in gauge theory> requires its divergence to vanish away from string endpoints. On the electric <BIon> locus it equals the scaled electric field, but this equality is not true for arbitrary configurations.

= Born–Infeld soliton
{parent=Dirac-Born-Infeld action}
{c}

= BIon
{synonym}
{c}

A supersymmetric spike of a <D-brane> combines a worldvolume gauge field with a transverse scalar profile. Its geometry and charge represent strings ending on the brane. Electric spikes represent <fundamental strings>; magnetic spikes on a <D3-brane> represent <D1-branes>. The original construction is https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9708147[Brane Dynamics From the Born-Infeld Action].

= Validity of the abelian BIon approximation
{parent=Born–Infeld soliton}

The <DBI action> resums field strengths but truncates their <derivative expansion>. A slowly varying large-charge throat may therefore be controlled even when its field strengths are not small. Large derivatives, large embedding curvature, string loops and gravitational backreaction restrict this description, and its ideal singular core is not uniformly controlled. The electric throat window $|N|g_s\gg1$, $g_s^2|N|\ll1$ and magnetic window $|N|\gg1$, $g_s|N|\ll1$ separate derivative and backreaction requirements.

= String-tension interpretation of BIon energy
{parent=Born–Infeld soliton}
{title2=$\Delta E=4\pi T_3c\,(X_{\rm cut}-X_\infty)$}

For $X-X_\infty=c/r$, subtracting the planar <D3-brane> energy leaves $T_3\int|\nabla X|^2d^3x=4\pi T_3c^2/r_0$ outside a cutoff. Since $X_{\rm cut}-X_\infty=c/r_0$, its coefficient is the carried string tension. Electric charge quantization gives $c=\pi g_s|N|\alpha'$ and coefficient $|N|T_{F1}$; magnetic charge gives $c=\pi|N|\alpha'$ and coefficient $|N|T_{D1}$.

= BPS equations for a one-scalar D3-brane
{parent=Born–Infeld soliton}
{c}
{title2=$\mathbf E=\cos\theta\nabla X,\quad\mathbf B=\sin\theta\nabla X,\quad\Delta X=0$}

For one constant charge angle, the <DBI Hamiltonian bound for an electric spike> and its dyonic extension give the displayed equations away from sources. Each aligned branch preserves eight of the sixteen linearly realized worldvolume supersymmetries. The corresponding spacetime string–D3 configuration preserves eight of thirty-two. The bound and charge interpretation are derived in https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9711205[BPS Bounds for Worldvolume Branes].

= DBI Hamiltonian bound for an electric spike
{parent=Born–Infeld soliton}
{c}
{title2=$\mathcal H^2/T_3^2=(1+\mathbf d\cdot\nabla X)^2+|\mathbf d-\nabla X|^2$}

For a static <D3-brane> with one scalar and no magnetic field, taking the Legendre transform of the <DBI action> gives the displayed identity. It bounds energy by the electric scalar charge and is saturated at $\mathbf d=\nabla X$. The opposite orientation uses the analogous identity with reversed signs. On saturation, the constitutive relation gives $\mathbf E=\mathbf d$ and the <Gauss law constraint in gauge theory> reduces to the <Laplace equation> for $X$.

= Hermitian commutator potential in D-brane Yang-Mills theory
{c}
{parent=Dirac-Born-Infeld action}
{title2=$V=-\frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2}\sum_{I,J}\operatorname{Tr}[\phi^I,\phi^J]^2\geq0$}

For Hermitian scalar matrices, their commutator is anti-Hermitian, so $-\operatorname{Tr}[\phi^I,\phi^J]^2=\operatorname{Tr}[\phi^I,\phi^J]^\dagger[\phi^I,\phi^J]\geq0$. Dimensional reduction gives $F_{IJ}=i[\phi^I,\phi^J]$, fixing the minus sign before the literal commutator square inside the conventional overall-negative Yang-Mills action. Commuting scalar expectation values minimize this potential and describe ordinary separated branes. A plus sign instead requires interpreting the squared symbol as the positive Hermitian norm, not literal matrix multiplication.

= D-brane worldvolume pullbacks
{c}
{parent=Dirac-Born-Infeld action}
{title2=$\gamma_{ab}=G_{\mu\nu}(X)\partial_aX^\mu\partial_bX^\nu,\quad B_{ab}=B_{\mu\nu}(X)\partial_aX^\mu\partial_bX^\nu$}

The brane embedding pulls the spacetime metric and two-form back to the <worldvolume>. The <dilaton> is evaluated at that embedding. If the quoted tension includes the constant-background factor $e^{-\Phi_0}$, use $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi(X)-\Phi_0$; otherwise use $\widetilde\Phi=\Phi(X)$ and a tension coefficient independent of that factor. These are normalization conventions for the same <Dirac-Born-Infeld action>.

= Worldvolume fields from open-string massless states
{parent=D-brane}

A massless open-string vector polarization tangential to a planar <D-brane> becomes a worldvolume gauge field. Transverse polarizations become <scalar fields> describing fluctuations of its position. This supports the interpretation of a Dirichlet plane as a dynamical extended object. The unprojected bosonic open-string <tachyon> additionally signals that its brane ground state is unstable; the massless field interpretation does not remove that instability.

= Chan-Paton factor
{parent=D-brane}
{c}

An endpoint label attached to an open string. With several coincident branes, two endpoint labels organize the massless open-string fields as matrices and give nonabelian gauge symmetry. Strings between different stacks carry representations associated with the two endpoint groups. These labels are physical internal degrees of freedom, not extra spacetime coordinates.

= Open string stretched between D-branes
{c}
{parent=D-brane}

An open string stretched a distance $L$ between parallel D-branes has squared mass $M^2=T^2L^2+(N-1)/\alpha'$. The stretching energy makes strings between separated branes massive.

= Bosonic stretched-string tachyon threshold
{parent=Open string stretched between D-branes}
{title2=$d_0=2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$}

For two static parallel <D-branes> separated transversely by $d$, the <open bosonic string mass spectrum> becomes $M^2=d^2/(4\pi^2\alpha'^2)+(N-1)/\alpha'$. The classical stretching contribution is the square of the <string tension> times the separation, while the oscillator vacuum contributes the intercept term. Thus the ground state is a <tachyon> for $d<2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}$, massless at equality, and massive above it. Its real rest energy, when stable, is $\sqrt{d^2/(4\pi^2\alpha'^2)-1/\alpha'}$; the vacuum correction belongs to energy squared and is not an additive fixed subtraction from the stretching energy. Angled branes change oscillator moding and require a different formula.

= D-brane scalar-position normalization
{c}
{parent=Open string stretched between D-branes}
{title2=$X_r^I=2\pi\alpha'\langle\phi_r^I\rangle$}

A commuting diagonal expectation value has one transverse eigenvalue vector for each brane. In the covariant derivative normalization $D_a\phi=\partial_a\phi+i[A_a,\phi]$, the off-diagonal gauge field has mass $m^2=\sum_I(\phi_1^I-\phi_2^I)^2$. The corresponding stretched string has mass $|X_1-X_2|/(2\pi\alpha')$ in its massless oscillator sector. Equality fixes the displayed scalar-position normalization, up to a common coordinate origin. The trace describes center-of-mass motion and the eigenvalue difference describes separation.

= Coincident-D-brane gauge enhancement
{c}
{parent=Open string stretched between D-branes}

When $n$ parallel D-branes coincide, oriented strings joining distinct branes become massless alongside strings whose ends lie on one brane. Their Chan-Paton labels fill the adjoint representation of $U(n)$, enhancing $U(1)^n$ to $U(n)$.

= Bosonic D3-brane low-energy field content
{parent=Coincident-D-brane gauge enhancement}
{title2=$A_a,\quad\Phi^I\ (I=1,\ldots,22)$}

For coincident <D3-branes> in the critical 26-dimensional <bosonic string theory>, the massless open-string oscillator has a four-dimensional gauge vector and 22 transverse <scalar fields>. <Chan-Paton factors> put all these fields in the adjoint of $U(k)$ for $k$ coincident branes. Ignoring the <tachyon>, their leading interactions are the <dimensional reduction> of 26-dimensional <Yang-Mills theory>. This contains a Yang-Mills sector but is not pure four-dimensional <Yang-Mills theory> and is not the supersymmetric six-scalar D3-brane theory of the ten-dimensional superstring.

= Open-string boundary condition in a B-field
{parent=D-brane}

For a D-brane in metric $G$, pulled-back B-field $B$ and worldvolume field strength $F$, tangential open-string coordinates obey the mixed condition $G_{ab}\partial_\sigma X^b+(B+2\pi\alpha'F)_{ab}\partial_\tau X^b=0$. Only the gauge-invariant combination $B+2\pi\alpha'F$ is physical.

= Gauge-gravity duality
{parent=String theory}
{wiki}

Gauge/gravity duality relates a quantum field theory without dynamical gravity to a gravitational or string theory in one additional emergent dimension.

= Conformal field theory
{title2=CFT}
{parent=Gauge-gravity duality}
{wiki}

A conformal field theory is a quantum field theory invariant under conformal transformations. Local operators are organized into conformal families labelled by scaling dimension and spin.

= Conformal cross-ratio
{parent=Conformal field theory}

A conformal cross-ratio is a dimensionless combination of point separations invariant under conformal transformations. Four generic points in dimension greater than two have two independent cross-ratios,
$$
u=\frac{x_{12}^2x_{34}^2}{x_{13}^2x_{24}^2},
\qquad
v=\frac{x_{14}^2x_{23}^2}{x_{13}^2x_{24}^2}.
$$

= Scaling dimension
{title2=$\Delta$}
{parent=Conformal field theory}
{wiki}

The scaling dimension of a local operator is its eigenvalue under dilations. Under $x\mapsto\lambda x$, a scalar operator of dimension $\Delta$ transforms by $\mathcal O(x)\mapsto\lambda^{-\Delta}\mathcal O(\lambda x)$.

= Exactly marginal operator
{parent=Scaling dimension}

An exactly marginal operator has scaling dimension equal to the spacetime dimension and a deformation coupling whose beta function vanishes to all orders. Varying that coupling moves along a family of conformal field theories without introducing a scale.

= Conformal algebra
{parent=Conformal field theory}
{wiki}

The conformal algebra in $d$ Euclidean dimensions is generated by translations $P_a$, rotations $M_{ab}$, dilations $D$, and special conformal transformations $K_a$.

= Conformal primary operator
{parent=Conformal field theory}
{wiki}

A conformal primary operator is annihilated at the origin by every special conformal generator. Acting with translation generators produces its conformal descendants.

= Scalar primary operator
{parent=Conformal primary operator}

A scalar primary operator transforms without a spin rotation and is characterized by its <scaling dimension>. Conformal symmetry fixes its two- and three-point functions up to constants, while a four-point function can depend on conformal cross-ratios.

= Conformal descendant
{parent=Conformal primary operator}
{wiki}

A conformal descendant is obtained by applying one or more translation generators to a <conformal primary operator>. Its scaling dimension exceeds that of the primary by a nonnegative integer.

= Conformal unitarity bound
{parent=Conformal primary operator}
{wiki}

Positivity of descendant norms constrains the scaling dimension of a primary operator. For a nonidentity scalar primary in $d\geq2$, unitarity requires $\Delta\geq(d-2)/2$.

= State–operator correspondence
{parent=Conformal field theory}
{wiki}

The state–operator correspondence maps a local operator of scaling dimension $\Delta$ to a state on $S^{d-1}\times\mathbb R$ whose cylinder energy is $\Delta/r$ for sphere radius $r$.

= State-operator correspondence for Virasoro primaries
{parent=State–operator correspondence}
{title2=$|\mathcal O\rangle=\mathcal O(0)|0\rangle$}

For a <primary operator>, $T(z)\mathcal O(0)\sim h\mathcal O(0)/z^2+\partial\mathcal O(0)/z$. Taking the residue against $z^{n+1}$ gives the weight for $n=0$, the derivative descendant for $n=-1$, and zero for every $n>0$. Conversely the absence of positive-mode actions excludes higher poles in this operator product and gives the primary transformation law. The antiholomorphic argument is identical. Thus <primary states> and <primary operators> correspond under <radial quantization>.

= Low-energy pure-gravity spectrum in AdS4
{title2=Low-energy pure-gravity spectrum in $\mathrm{AdS}_4$}
{c}
{parent=State–operator correspondence}

If the only light field in AdS4 is the graviton, the dual CFT3 spectrum begins with the vacuum at dimension zero, the spin-two stress tensor at dimension three, and its non-null level-one descendants of spins two and three at dimension four.

= AdS/CFT correspondence
{c}
{parent=Gauge-gravity duality}
{wiki}

The AdS/CFT correspondence identifies a conformal field theory with quantum gravity on an asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetime. Boundary sources couple to CFT operators and equal boundary values of bulk fields.

= Euclidean conformal transformation
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}

A Euclidean conformal transformation pulls the flat metric back to a position-dependent positive multiple of itself. Its infinitesimal generator obeys the conformal Killing equation.

= Scalar-primary two-point function
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}

Conformal invariance fixes the two-point function of identical scalar primaries of dimension $\Delta$ to $C_2/|x_{12}|^{2\Delta}$.

= Scalar-primary three-point function
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}

Three scalar primaries have a conformally fixed position dependence, leaving one theory-dependent coefficient $C_{123}$.

= Witten diagram
{c}
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}
{wiki}

A Witten diagram is a position-space perturbative diagram in anti-de Sitter spacetime whose external bulk-to-boundary propagators end at boundary operator insertions.

= Single-trace operator
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}
{wiki}

In a large-rank gauge theory, a single-trace operator is dual at leading order to a single bulk field, while multi-trace operators correspond to multiparticle bulk states.

= Holographic dictionary
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}
{wiki}

The holographic dictionary identifies leading near-boundary bulk-field coefficients with CFT sources and subleading normalizable coefficients with operator expectation values.

= Fefferman--Graham coordinates
{c}
{parent=Holographic dictionary}
{wiki}

Fefferman--Graham coordinates put an asymptotically anti-de Sitter metric in the form $ds^2=L^2[dZ^2/Z^2+g_{ij}(Z,x)dx^idx^j/Z^2]$.

= AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics
{parent=Holographic dictionary}

A scalar field of mass $m$ in $\operatorname{AdS}_{d+1}$ behaves near the conformal boundary as $\phi=z^{d-\Delta}J+z^\Delta A+\cdots$, where $m^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$. The leading coefficient is the source and the normalizable coefficient determines the operator expectation value.

= AdS scalar mass--dimension relation
{title2=$m^2L^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$}
{c}
{parent=AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics}

For a scalar field of mass $m$ on $\operatorname{AdS}_{d+1}$ of radius $L$, the scaling dimension of the dual scalar primary obeys $m^2L^2=\Delta(\Delta-d)$. The two roots correspond to the two independent near-boundary falloffs.

= Alternative quantization of an AdS scalar field
{title2=Alternative quantization of an AdS scalar field}
{c}
{parent=AdS scalar-field boundary asymptotics}

When both scalar falloffs in anti-de Sitter spacetime are normalizable, alternative quantization exchanges which near-boundary coefficient is treated as the source and which as the expectation value. In AdS4, a field with $m^2=-2$ may be dual to a scalar operator of dimension one.

= Holographic renormalization
{parent=Holographic dictionary}
{wiki}

Holographic renormalization subtracts local boundary counterterms from the regulated on-shell bulk action. It gives finite correlation functions and identifies the renormalized normalizable coefficient with an operator expectation value.

= Holographic entanglement entropy
{parent=Holographic dictionary}
{wiki}

At leading semiclassical order, holographic entanglement entropy is the area of a bulk extremal surface anchored on the boundary of the chosen region, divided by $4G_N$.

= Ryu–Takayanagi formula
{c}
{parent=Holographic entanglement entropy}
{wiki}

For a static holographic state and boundary region $A$,
$$
S(A)=\frac{\operatorname{Area}(\gamma_A)}{4G_N},
$$
where $\gamma_A$ is the least-area bulk surface homologous to $A$ and anchored on $\partial A$.

= Ryu–Takayanagi surface for a disk
{c}
{parent=Ryu–Takayanagi formula}

For a round disk of radius $R$ in the vacuum of a holographic conformal field theory on flat space, the static Poincare-AdS Ryu--Takayanagi surface is the hemisphere $r^2+z^2=R^2$.

= Brown--Henneaux central charge
{title2=$c=3R_{\rm AdS}/(2G)$}
{c}
{parent=Holographic entanglement entropy}

The Brown--Henneaux central charge is the central charge of the two-dimensional conformal field theory dual to Einstein gravity on $\operatorname{AdS}_3$:
$$
c=\frac{3R_{\rm AdS}}{2G_3}.
$$

= Casimir energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory
{title2=$E_0=-c/12$}
{parent=Brown--Henneaux central charge}

For a unit spatial circle, the vacuum energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory is $E_0=-c/12$. It arises from the anomalous Schwarzian shift when the plane vacuum is mapped to the cylinder and is dual to the renormalized energy of global $\operatorname{AdS}_3$.

= Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface
{title2=HRT surface}
{c}
{parent=Holographic entanglement entropy}

A Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface is the covariant extremal-area surface homologous to a boundary region. Its area divided by $4G_N$ gives leading semiclassical entanglement entropy in a time-dependent holographic spacetime.

= One-sided unitary excitation of a thermofield double state
{parent=Hubeny–Rangamani–Takayanagi surface}

A unitary acting on only one factor of a thermofield double state preserves the spectrum of either reduced density matrix and hence preserves left--right entanglement. A bulk shell created on one boundary can enlarge the late event horizon while the HRT surface remains at the earlier bifurcation surface.

= Thermofield double state
{title2=TFD state}
{parent=Holographic entanglement entropy}
{wiki}

For two copies of a quantum system,
$$
|\Psi_\beta\rangle=Z(\beta)^{-1/2}
\sum_ne^{-\beta E_n/2}|n\rangle_L^*|n\rangle_R.
$$
Tracing out either copy produces the thermal density operator $e^{-\beta H}/Z$.

= Modular Hamiltonian
{title2=$K=-\log\rho$}
{parent=Thermofield double state}
{wiki}

The modular Hamiltonian of a density operator is $K=-\log\rho$. For a Gibbs state, $K=\beta H+\log Z$.

= First law of entanglement entropy
{parent=Modular Hamiltonian}

Linearizing the von Neumann entropy around a reference state gives $\delta S=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\rho K)$. Around a thermal state this becomes $\delta S=\beta\,\delta\langle H\rangle$.

= Holographic mutual information
{parent=Holographic entanglement entropy}

Holographic mutual information is nonnegative because the disconnected union $\gamma_A\cup\gamma_B$ is an admissible surface for $A\cup B$, whose minimizing surface can have no larger area.

= Entanglement-wedge phase transition
{parent=Holographic mutual information}

As two boundary regions separate, the minimizing surface for their union can switch from connected to disconnected. At leading order this produces a continuous entropy with a kink and makes holographic mutual information vanish beyond a critical separation.

= Holographic chemical potential
{parent=Holographic dictionary}

For a bulk gauge field, the leading boundary value of its time component is the dual chemical potential, while the normalizable radial coefficient determines the charge density.

= Holographic D.C. conductivity
{parent=Holographic dictionary}

At nonzero charge density, a translationally invariant holographic state has infinite D.C. conductivity because an electric field continuously injects conserved momentum. Momentum relaxation broadens the zero-frequency delta function and makes the D.C. value finite.

= ABJM theory
{c}
{parent=AdS-CFT correspondence}
{wiki}

ABJM theory is a three-dimensional supersymmetric Chern–Simons matter theory with a large-$N$ holographic dual. In its M-theory regime the bulk Newton constant scales as $G_N\sim N^{-3/2}$ in AdS units.
