= Quantum harmonic oscillator
{wiki}
{title2=$H=p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2x^2/2$}

The <quantum harmonic oscillator> describes a particle of <mass> $m>0$ in a quadratic <potential energy> with <angular frequency> $\omega>0$. In the position representation its <quantum Hamiltonian> is
$$
H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+\frac{m\omega^2x^2}2.
$$
The model is useful far beyond a literal spring: a <Taylor series> about a stable equilibrium with positive potential curvature has this quadratic term as its leading nonconstant approximation. The pure <quartic oscillator> has zero curvature at its minimum, so that particular approximation does not apply. https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2013/808334d09369e3a726f6f20d82315c20_MIT8_04S13_Lec08.pdf[Allan Adams, MIT 8.04 Lecture 8, Quantum Harmonic Oscillator, pp. 3–4]{ref}.

In units with $\hbar=1$, <creation and annihilation operators> give
$$
H=\omega\left(N+\frac12\right),
\qquad N=A^\dagger A.
$$
Here $N$ is the <number operator>. Restoring the reduced <Planck constant>, the <energy eigenvalues> are $E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$ for $n=0,1,2,\ldots$: neighboring levels are separated by $\hbar\omega$, and the <ground-state energy> is the <zero-point energy> $\hbar\omega/2$.

With length scale $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$ and dimensionless coordinate $u=x/\ell$, the <ground state> is the normalized <Gaussian wave packet>
$$
\phi_0(x)=\frac1{\pi^{1/4}\sqrt\ell}e^{-u^2/2},\qquad \langle x^2\rangle_0=\frac{\ell^2}2.
$$
For $\hbar=m=\omega=1$, this is precisely the $a=1$ trial <wavefunction> in the <quantum variational principle>. The balance of <kinetic energy> and <potential energy> therefore recovers the exact <ground-state energy> using only <Gaussian integrals>.

= Oscillator ground-state density in a comparison wavelength
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$dP=(2\sqrt\pi/\lambda)e^{-4\pi^2x^2/\lambda^2}\,dx$}

A free particle whose <kinetic energy> equals the ground-state total <energy> $\hbar\omega/2$ has <momentum> $\hbar/a$ and <de Broglie wavelength> $\lambda=2\pi a$, where $a=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$. Substitution into the normalized bound-state <Gaussian> yields the title density. The comparison <wavelength> is a useful <length> scale; it is not a claim that the <bound state> has a single free <momentum> or spatially uniform <probability density>.

= Harmonic-oscillator wavefunction inflections
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\psi_n''=b^{-2}(\xi^2-2n-1)\psi_n$}

For a one-dimensional <quantum harmonic oscillator>, let $b=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$ and $\xi=x/b$. The stationary <Schrödinger equation> gives the displayed curvature relation. Inflections occur at <classical turning points> $\xi=\pm\sqrt{2n+1}$ and at simple <wavefunction> <wavefunction nodes>, provided the curvature changes sign. Thus the first excited state has an inflection at its central <wavefunction node> as well as at both turning points. Restricting attention to where $V=E$ misses <wavefunction node> inflections.

= Oscillator uncertainty and allocation of classical energy
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\Delta x_n^2=(n+1/2)\hbar/(m\omega)$}

For a one-dimensional oscillator eigenstate, <parity> gives $\langle x\rangle=0$ and its coordinate <variance> is the displayed expression. A classical oscillator with that coordinate <energy> has turning amplitude $A=\sqrt{2E_x/(m\omega^2)}$, so $\Delta x=A/\sqrt2$. In several dimensions the total <energy> does not specify a given coordinate's classical amplitude until its <energy> allocation is fixed. <zero-point energies> in the other quantum coordinates must not silently be assigned to that one classical mode.

= Anisotropic quantum harmonic oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$E_{n_xn_yn_z}=\hbar\sum_i\omega_i(n_i+1/2)$}

For a positive diagonal quadratic potential $V=m\sum_i\omega_i^2x_i^2/2$, coordinate <Hamiltonians> commute and <wavefunctions> separate into one-dimensional oscillator factors. Equality of two squared <frequencies> gives <rotation> <symmetry> in their plane; equality of all three gives full <rotation> <symmetry>. Equalities create <energy> degeneracies and extra conserved <angular momentum>. Merely finding an accidental equality between particular level energies does not establish a global <rotational symmetry>.

= Capacity of an isotropic oscillator shell
{parent=Anisotropic quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$g_N^{\rm spatial}=(N+1)(N+2)/2$}

The number of nonnegative triples with $n_x+n_y+n_z=N$ is the stars-and-bars count $\binom{N+2}{2}$. Including a two-state <electron> <spin> gives capacity $(N+1)(N+2)$. Filling non-interacting shells therefore uses capacities $2,6,12,\ldots$, with shell <energy> $(N+3/2)\hbar\omega$ per <electron>. A partly filled shell has degenerate many-body <ground states>; rearranging its occupants is not a positive excitation <energy>.

= Quantum occupation scale of a classical oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\bar n\simeq m\omega A^2/(2\hbar)$}

A classical oscillator of amplitude $A$ has <energy> $m\omega^2A^2/2$. Comparing with the oscillator spacing $\hbar\omega$ gives the large-occupation scale displayed. A <coherent state> with this classical amplitude has exactly this <mean> excitation occupation and a Poisson number distribution; its total <mean> <energy> also includes the zero-point term. An observed macroscopic amplitude does not, by itself, establish a pure <coherent state>.

= Euclidean oscillator diagonal kernel
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{c}

At imaginary duration $\beta\hbar$, $K_E(q,q)=\sqrt{m\omega/[2\pi\hbar\sinh x]}\exp[-m\omega q^2\tanh(x/2)/\hbar]$, $x=\beta\hbar\omega$. Integrating the common endpoint yields $[2\sinh(x/2)]^{-1}$.

= Canonical oscillator coordinate variance
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\langle x^2\rangle_T=\hbar\coth(\beta\hbar\omega/2)/(2m\omega)$}

A canonical <quantum harmonic oscillator> has geometric number probabilities and mean occupation $(e^{\beta\hbar\omega}-1)^{-1}$. The position operator then gives the displayed variance. It interpolates between zero-point fluctuations $\hbar/(2m\omega)$ and the classical variance $k_BT/(m\omega^2)$. The kinetic and potential <energies> are equal at every <temperature>, although classical equipartition describes their values only at high <temperature>.

= Ground-state oscillator probability beyond the turning points
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}

The ground-state turning points are $\pm\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$. In dimensionless position $y=x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$, the <probability> outside them is $(2/\sqrt\pi)\int_1^\infty e^{-y^2}\,dy=\operatorname{erfc}(1)\simeq0.15730$. This tail demonstrates that a bound quantum state need not be confined to its classically allowed region.

= Exact ground state of two coordinate-coupled oscillators
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\omega_\pm=\sqrt{\omega^2\pm k/m}$}

For identical <masses> and bare <frequencies> with coupling $kxy$, coordinates $(x\pm y)/\sqrt2$ diagonalize the <Hamiltonian>. A stable normalizable <ground state> requires $|k|<m\omega^2$. Its wave function is the product of Gaussians of <frequencies> $\omega_\pm$ in these <normal coordinates>; a two-mode squeeze of the original bare oscillators alone is generally insufficient.

= Electric-field displacement of a quantum harmonic oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$x_0=qE/k$}

For $H=p^2/(2m)+kx^2/2-qEx$, completing the square translates the well by $x_0=qE/k$ and shifts every <energy> by $-q^2E^2/(2k)$. The <wavefunction> of the <ground state> is translated without changing its width. Consequently the induced <electric dipole moment> is exactly linear in $E$, even when a low-order perturbative approximation to the state is inaccurate.

= Normalized two-state truncation of a displaced oscillator
{parent=Electric-field displacement of a quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\langle x\rangle_{01}=x_0/(1+\eta^2)$}

Let $x_{\rm zp}=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}$ and $\eta=x_0/(2x_{\rm zp})$. The normalized first-order truncation is $(|0\rangle+\eta|1\rangle)/\sqrt{1+\eta^2}$. Its mean displacement is $2\eta x_{\rm zp}/(1+\eta^2)$, agreeing with $x_0$ only at small $|\eta|$. The exact translated <ground state> is a <coherent state> whose expansion contains all <number states> and has mean occupation $\eta^2$. Correct linear response does not justify extrapolating a truncated state to large fields.

= Uncertainty product of oscillator number states
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}

An <energy eigenstate> has $\langle x\rangle=\langle p\rangle=0$, $\langle x^2\rangle=\hbar(n+1/2)/(m\omega)$ and $\langle p^2\rangle=m\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$. Thus $\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar(n+1/2)$; only the ground state in this sequence minimizes the <Heisenberg uncertainty principle>.

= Harmonic oscillator eigenfunction
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\psi_n$}

With oscillator length $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$, the eigenfunctions are proportional to $H_n(x/\ell)e^{-x^2/(2\ell^2)}$. They have $n$ real nodes, <parity> $(-1)^n$, and energy $\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$.

= Sudden oscillator frequency quench
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\alpha^2-\beta^2=1$}

A jump from <positive frequency> $\omega$ to $\Omega$ keeps an oscillator mode and its first derivative continuous. A normalized positive-frequency incident solution has outgoing coefficients $\alpha=(\Omega+\omega)/(2\sqrt{\Omega\omega})$ and $\beta=(\Omega-\omega)/(2\sqrt{\Omega\omega})$, satisfying $\alpha^2-\beta^2=1$. This temporal matching is not a sum-one spatial-scattering <probability> rule.

= Harmonic-oscillator forbidden-region probability
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$P_0=\operatorname{erfc}(1)$}

For the normalized ground state of a reduced <quantum harmonic oscillator>, $\phi_0(q)=\pi^{-1/4}e^{-q^2/2}$ and $E_0=1/2$. Its <classical turning points> are $q=\pm1$. The <Born rule> assigns <probability> $P=2\pi^{-1/2}\int_1^\infty e^{-q^2}\,dq=\operatorname{erfc}(1)\simeq0.157299$ to the <classically forbidden region>. This is a stationary <probability> of detection outside the classical interval. It is not a transmitted flux or an escape rate: the real stationary <wavefunction> has zero <probability current>.

= Gaussian variational bound for a radial harmonic oscillator
{c}
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\lambda_0=n,\quad y_0=e^{-x^2/2}$}

Consider $-y''-(n-1)y'/x+x^2y=\lambda y$ for a positive <integer> $n$, with regular radial data at zero and square-integrable decay at infinity in the measure $x^{n-1}dx$. Its <Sturm-Liouville form> has $p=w=x^{n-1}$ and $q=x^{n+1}$. For the Gaussian trial $y=e^{ax^2}$, $a<0$, the <Rayleigh quotient> is
$$
\Lambda(a)=(4a^2+1)\frac{\int_0^\infty x^{n+1}e^{2ax^2}dx}{\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}e^{2ax^2}dx}=-n\left(a+\frac1{4a}\right).
$$
The <Gamma function recurrence> evaluates the ratio, and the minimum is $n$ at $a=-1/2$. It is the exact lowest <eigenvalue>: after the endpoint term $[x^ny^2]_0^\infty$ vanishes, <integration by parts> gives
$$
\int_0^\infty\left[x^{n-1}(y')^2+x^{n+1}y^2\right]dx-n\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}y^2dx=\int_0^\infty x^{n-1}(y'+xy)^2dx\geq0.
$$
Equality gives the displayed positive Gaussian <eigenfunction>. For $n=1$, the radial regularity condition is the <Neumann boundary condition> at zero, not a vanishing value there. Changing the boundary condition changes the lowest <eigenvalue> and whether this trial is admissible.

= Half-harmonic oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$E_r=\hbar\omega(2r+3/2)$}

A <half-harmonic oscillator> has $V=m\omega^2x^2/2$ on $x>0$ and an infinite wall at $x=0$. Its <wavefunction> vanishes at the wall. Only the odd full-oscillator <energy eigenstates> satisfy this condition; restricting them to the positive half-line and multiplying by $\sqrt2$ restores normalization. Thus $E_r=\hbar\omega(2r+3/2)$ for $r=0,1,\ldots$. With $\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$, its normalized <ground state> is
$$
\psi_h(x)=\frac{2x}{\pi^{1/4}\ell^{3/2}}e^{-x^2/(2\ell^2)}\quad(x>0),\qquad \psi_h(x)=0\quad(x<0).
$$
It has no interior node on its allowed half-line, and energy $3\hbar\omega/2$.

= Exponential trial state for a half-harmonic oscillator
{parent=Half-harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\psi_\alpha=xe^{-\alpha x},\quad\Lambda=\alpha^2+3/\alpha^2\geq2\sqrt3$}

For the dimensionless <half-harmonic oscillator> $-\psi''+x^2\psi=\lambda\psi$ on $x>0$ with $\psi(0)=0$, the trial family $xe^{-\alpha x}$, $\alpha>0$, is admissible for the <Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle>. The moments $\int_0^\infty x^ne^{-2\alpha x}dx=n!/(2\alpha)^{n+1}$ give norm squared $1/(4\alpha^3)$, derivative energy $1/(4\alpha)$, and potential energy $3/(4\alpha^5)$. Its <Rayleigh quotient> is $\alpha^2+3/\alpha^2$, minimized at $\alpha=3^{1/4}$. The resulting upper bound $2\sqrt3$ exceeds the exact lowest eigenvalue three; the exact ground-state shape is $xe^{-x^2/2}$.

= Removal of a half-oscillator wall
{parent=Half-harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$P_{\mathrm{new\ ground}}=1/\pi$}

Suddenly remove the wall of a <half-harmonic oscillator> in its ground state, leaving the same harmonic potential on the whole line. The <sudden approximation> keeps its wavefunction unchanged during removal. The new normalized ground state is $\psi_0=\pi^{-1/4}\ell^{-1/2}e^{-x^2/(2\ell^2)}$, so
$$
\langle0|\psi_h\rangle=\frac{2}{\sqrt\pi\ell^2}\int_0^\infty xe^{-x^2/\ell^2}\,dx=\frac1{\sqrt\pi},\qquad \boxed{P_0=\frac1\pi.}
$$
The integration is over the initially occupied half-line. The final first excited state instead has probability $1/2$, since the initial half-line state equals $\sqrt2$ times its positive-half restriction. This also checks that removing the wall does not simply create the full oscillator's odd state.

= Cartesian and spherical bases of the first oscillator shell
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\phi_{+1}=-(\phi_x+i\phi_y)/\sqrt2,\quad\phi_0=\phi_z,\quad\phi_{-1}=(\phi_x-i\phi_y)/\sqrt2$}

In the isotropic three-dimensional <quantum harmonic oscillator>, the first excited Cartesian <energy eigenstates> are proportional to $x,y,z$ times the common Gaussian $e^{-r^2/(2a_0^2)}$. If $\phi_x,\phi_y,\phi_z$ are normalized with the same real phase convention, their combinations in the title are normalized <angular momentum eigenstates> with $l=1$ and $m=1,0,-1$.

Indeed, $z=r\cos\theta$ and $x\pm iy=r\sin\theta e^{\pm i\varphi}$ identify a common radial factor times the <spherical harmonics> $Y_{10},Y_{1,\pm1}$. The transformation is unitary, so it changes the basis rather than the threefold orbital degeneracy.

= Harmonic-oscillator number-state second moments
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\langle x^2\rangle_n=\hbar(n+1/2)/(m\omega),\quad\langle p^2\rangle_n=m\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$}

For a <number operator> <eigenstate> $|n\rangle$, the <annihilation operator> and <creation operator> satisfy $\langle a\rangle=\langle a^\dagger\rangle=\langle a^2\rangle=\langle(a^\dagger)^2\rangle=0$, $\langle a^\dagger a\rangle=n$ and $\langle aa^\dagger\rangle=n+1$. Inserting $x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}(a+a^\dagger)$ and $p=i\sqrt{m\hbar\omega/2}(a^\dagger-a)$ gives the displayed second moments and zero means.

Thus the <kinetic energy> and <potential energy> each have mean $\hbar\omega(n+1/2)/2$, and the uncertainty product is $\Delta x\Delta p=\hbar(n+1/2)$. Among number states only the ground state saturates the <Heisenberg uncertainty relation>. A <quantum superposition> can have nonzero position and momentum means despite each constituent number state's zero means.

= Oscillator length
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\ell=\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}$}

The oscillator length fixes the spatial scale of a <quantum harmonic oscillator> of mass $m$ and angular frequency $\omega$. Its normalized ground <wavefunction> is $\varphi_0(x)=\pi^{-1/4}\ell^{-1/2}\exp[-x^2/(2\ell^2)]$, so $|\varphi_0(0)|^2=1/(\sqrt\pi\ell)$ and $\langle x^2\rangle=\ell^2/2$. The definition of $\ell$ is distinct from the root-mean-square displacement $\ell/\sqrt2$.

= Stability of monomial ladder-operator perturbations
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\Delta E_0^{(2)}=-\lambda^2\hbar\omega(r-1)!$}

The perturbation $\lambda\hbar\omega(a^r+(a^\dagger)^r)$ couples the oscillator vacuum only to $|r\rangle$ at first order. Its squared coupling is $(\hbar\omega)^2r!$ and the <energy> denominator is $-r\hbar\omega$, giving the displayed formal second-order coefficient. For $r>2$ and nonzero real $\lambda$, coherent-state expectations contain $R^2+2\lambda R^r\cos r\theta$; choosing its phase negative proves the <Hamiltonian> is unbounded below. The formal vacuum branch must therefore not be called an exact ground state. For $r=2$ boundedness requires $|\lambda|<1/2$, while the displaced $r=1$ oscillator is stable for every real strength.

= Harmonic oscillator transition kernel
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$K_\omega(q_f,q_i;T)$}

For <action> $\frac m2\int(\dot q^2-\omega^2q^2)dt$, the kernel is $\sqrt{m\omega/(2\pi i\sin\omega T)}\exp\{im\omega[(q_f^2+q_i^2)\cos\omega T-2q_fq_i]/(2\sin\omega T)\}$. The <Dirichlet oscillator determinant ratio> supplies its prefactor, while the classical boundary <action> supplies its exponent. The <Feynman i-epsilon prescription> specifies its continuation through caustics.

= Minimum-energy normalized oscillator mode
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$q(t)=e^{-i\omega t+i\alpha}/\sqrt{2\omega}$}

For a <quantum harmonic oscillator> with $\omega>0$, a complex mode satisfying the <Wronskian normalization> $q\dot q^*-\dot q q^*=i$ can be written $q=re^{is}$ with $\dot s=-1/(2r^2)$. Its <vacuum energy> is $\dot r^2/2+1/(8r^2)+\omega^2r^2/2$. This is at least $\omega/2$, with equality exactly when $\dot r=0$ and $r^2=1/(2\omega)$. The same argument fixes the subhorizon positive-frequency normalization in the <Bunch-Davies vacuum> of a canonical cosmological mode.

= One-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$X=H^1(\mathbb R)\cap L^2(\mathbb R,x^2\,dx)$}

This <Hilbert space> has norm $\|u\|_X^2=\|u\|_2^2+\|u'\|_2^2+\|xu\|_2^2$. Completeness follows from completeness of <H1 space> and closedness of the <multiplication operator> $u\mapsto xu$. Smooth cutoffs followed by convolution with a <mollifier> show that smooth compactly supported functions are dense. The space is the natural energy domain of the dimensionless <quantum harmonic oscillator>, whereas the operator domain additionally requires $u''$ and $x^2u$ to lie in <L2 space>.

= Graph estimate for the harmonic oscillator
{parent=One-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain}
{title2=$\|u''\|_2^2+\|(c+x^2)u\|_2^2\leq\|-u''+(c+x^2)u\|_2^2+2\|u\|_2^2$}

For $c>0$ and smooth compactly supported $u$, <integration by parts> gives
$$
\|-u''+(c+x^2)u\|_2^2
=\|u''\|_2^2+\|(c+x^2)u\|_2^2+2\int(c+x^2)|u'|^2\,dx-2\|u\|_2^2.
$$
Dropping the nonnegative weighted derivative term proves the displayed bound. For a weak resolvent solution in the <one-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain>, apply the bound to $\chi_Ru$, where $\chi_R$ is a smooth cutoff. The cutoff error $-2\chi_R'u'-\chi_R''u$ is bounded in <L2 space> and tends to zero. The <Fatou lemma> then shows that $u''$ and $x^2u$ belong to <L2 space>, identifying the strong operator domain.

= Resolvent of the shifted harmonic oscillator
{parent=Graph estimate for the harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$\|(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|_{2\to2}\leq(\lambda+1)^{-1}$}

For $Au=u''-(1+x^2)u$ on $D(A)=\{u\in H^2(\mathbb R):x^2u\in L^2\}$, the form $a_\lambda(u,v)=\int u'\overline{v'}+(1+\lambda+x^2)u\bar v$ is coercive on the <one-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain> when $\lambda>0$. The <Lax-Milgram theorem> gives a unique weak solution of $(\lambda-A)u=g$. The <graph estimate for the harmonic oscillator> puts it in $D(A)$, and $a_\lambda(u,u)\geq(1+\lambda)\|u\|_2^2$ gives the displayed <resolvent> bound. The domain is dense and the bounded resolvent proves closedness, so the <Hille-Yosida theorem> gives a <contraction semigroup>. An <exponentially shifted semigroup> with generator $A+I$ solves the heat equation with oscillator potential.

= Thermal partition function of a quantum harmonic oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$Z(\beta,\omega)=[2\sinh(\beta\omega/2)]^{-1}$}

For a <quantum harmonic oscillator> with $\hbar=1$, $\omega>0$ and <inverse temperature> $\beta>0$, the <canonical partition function> is $\sum_{r\ge0}e^{-\beta\omega(r+1/2)}$. The <geometric series> gives the displayed formula. Keeping the <zero-point energy> is essential to its prefactor.

= Quantum-oscillator energy and heat capacity
{parent=Thermal partition function of a quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$C_V=k_Bx^2e^x/(e^x-1)^2$}

For one <quantum harmonic oscillator> of <frequency> $\Omega$, $Z=e^{-x/2}/(1-e^{-x})$ with $x=\hbar\Omega/(k_BT)$. Differentiating $\ln Z$ gives $U=\hbar\Omega[1/2+1/(e^x-1)]$ and $C_V=k_Bx^2e^x/(e^x-1)^2$. <Zero-point energy> leaves $U\to\hbar\Omega/2$ as $T\to0$ but contributes no <heat capacity>. Excitation freeze-out makes $C_V$ exponentially small, while high <temperature> gives $U\sim k_BT$ and $C_V\to k_B$.

= Displaced quantum harmonic oscillator
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$E_n=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)-F^2/(2m\omega^2)$}

Adding $-Fx$ to the <quantum harmonic oscillator> potential gives $\frac12m\omega^2(x-d)^2-F^2/(2m\omega^2)$, where $d=F/(m\omega^2)$. Translation preserves the oscillator spectrum, apart from the displayed constant shift. Eigenfunctions are translated by $d$ and the energy spacing is unchanged.

= Shaken harmonic trap
{parent=Displaced quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$H'(t)=-m\omega^2x_0(t)x+\tfrac12m\omega^2x_0(t)^2I$}

Displacing a one-dimensional <quantum harmonic oscillator> produces a linear force term and a scalar shift relative to the unshifted <Hamiltonian>. The scalar adds only a common phase. The linear term couples <number states> separated by one excitation. Arbitrary linear driving of the <ground state> creates a <coherent state>; a first-order expansion of that state contains only the vacuum and first excited state.<Topics in Quantum Mechanics by David Tong>[§4.4.2, printed pp.132–133: a forcing term creates coherent states]{ref}

= Weak resonant driving
{parent=Shaken harmonic trap}
{title2=$\varepsilon\omega T/a_0\ll1$}

A sinusoidal trap displacement with amplitude $\varepsilon$ and duration $T$ produces a resonant first-order excitation amplitude proportional to $\varepsilon\omega T/a_0$, where $a_0$ is the <oscillator length>. Keeping its square much smaller than one is the long-time condition for weak excitation. The small-displacement condition $\varepsilon/a_0\ll1$ alone does not ensure uniform validity as $T$ grows. Exact linear driving remains solvable as a <coherent state> after this first-order approximation fails.

= First-order selection rule for a linearly driven oscillator
{parent=Shaken harmonic trap}
{title2=$\langle n|x|0\rangle=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}\,\delta_{n1}$}

The ladder identity $x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}(a+a^\dagger)$ proves the matrix element in the title. In <time-dependent perturbation theory>, a linear force excites only $n=1$ directly from $n=0$. <Parity> alone rules out even excited states; the ladder identity also rules out odd $n>1$. Higher-order evolution can reach other levels, so the driven oscillator is not an exact <two-level system>.

= Exponential switch-on of a driven quantum oscillator
{parent=Displaced quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$P_1^{(0)}\simeq\eta^2(\omega\tau)^2/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$}

For $H(t)=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+1/2)-Fx e^{t/\tau}$ on $t\leq0$, start in the unforced <ground state> at $t=-\infty$. Define $x_{01}=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}$ and $\eta=Fx_{01}/(\hbar\omega)$. <Time-dependent perturbation theory> gives
$$
c_1(0)=\frac{iFx_{01}}\hbar\int_{-\infty}^0 e^{(1/\tau+i\omega)t}\,dt=\frac{i\eta\omega\tau}{1+i\omega\tau}.
$$
Thus the first excited-state probability in the original oscillator basis tends to zero for a rapid turn-on and to $\eta^2$ for slow turn-on, within the weak-force approximation $|\eta|\ll1$.

This finite adiabatic limit is not a failure of the <adiabatic theorem>: the instantaneous <ground state> is displaced and has a nonzero projection onto the original excited states. In fact a linear force preserves a <coherent state>, whose exact original-basis displacement at $t=0$ is $\alpha=i\eta\omega\tau/(1+i\omega\tau)$. It follows either from $\dot\alpha=-i\omega\alpha+iFx_{01}e^{t/\tau}/\hbar$ or from the forced oscillator equations. Consequently the exact original-basis probability is $P_1^{(0)}=|\alpha|^2e^{-|\alpha|^2}$. The instantaneous oscillator basis has displacement $\eta$, so its mean excitation is instead $|\alpha-\eta|^2=\eta^2/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$, which vanishes adiabatically.

= Ground-state overlap after sudden oscillator displacement
{parent=Displaced quantum harmonic oscillator}
{title2=$P_0=\exp[-m\omega d^2/(2\hbar)]$}

In the <sudden approximation>, an initial oscillator <ground state> overlaps the displaced ground state by $\exp[-m\omega d^2/(4\hbar)]$. Completing the square in the product of the two normalized Gaussian wavefunctions and using the <Gaussian integral> proves this amplitude. Squaring it gives the displayed probability. Only an irrelevant overall phase evolves before the sudden switch.

= Zero-point energy
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{wiki}

Zero-point energy is the nonzero ground-state energy left by quantum fluctuations. One harmonic oscillator contributes $\omega/2$ before any regularization or normal-ordering subtraction.

= Zero-point motion
{parent=Zero-point energy}

<Zero-point motion> is the nonzero quantum fluctuation of position and momentum in a system's <ground state>. For a <quantum harmonic oscillator>, the <ground state> has <energy> $\hbar\omega/2$ and nonzero position and momentum variances even at zero <temperature>. It does not represent a thermal mixture and need not carry <entropy>. The large <zero-point motion> of light <helium> atoms helps explain why <helium> can remain a <liquid> at low <pressure> even as <temperature> approaches <absolute zero>.

A <squeezed coherent state> is obtained from the vacuum by a squeeze operator and a displacement operator. Its quadrature variances can differ while saturating the uncertainty product. With zero displacement it is a <squeezed vacuum state>.

= Creation and annihilation operators
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}
{wiki}

The bosonic ladder operators satisfy
$$
[A,A^\dagger]=1,
\qquad
A|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle,
\qquad
A^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle.
$$

An annihilation operator lowers the occupation <number operator> eigenvalue by one and annihilates the <Fock vacuum>.

A creation operator raises the occupation <number operator> eigenvalue of a <quantum harmonic oscillator> by one. Repeated application builds <Fock states>.

= Single-mode squeeze operator
{title2=$S(\gamma)$}
{parent=Creation and annihilation operators}

For a real squeezing parameter $\gamma$, the single-mode squeeze operator is
$$
S(\gamma)=\exp\left[-\frac\gamma2\left(A^{\dagger2}-A^2\right)\right].
$$
Its exponent is <skew-adjoint>, so $S(\gamma)$ is a <unitary operator>, and it implements the <Bogoliubov transformation>
$$
S^\dagger AS=A\cosh\gamma-A^\dagger\sinh\gamma.
$$

= Squeezed vacuum state
{parent=Single-mode squeeze operator}

The squeezed vacuum is the zero-displacement <squeezed coherent state> $|\gamma\rangle=S(\gamma)|0\rangle$. Its position and momentum variances are rescaled in opposite directions,
$$
(\Delta X)^2=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}e^{-2\gamma},
\qquad
(\Delta P)^2=\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}e^{2\gamma},
$$
so it remains an <equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation>[minimum-uncertainty state] with $\Delta X\Delta P=\hbar/2$.

= Breathing Gaussian state in a harmonic oscillator
{parent=Squeezed vacuum state}
{title2=$\Sigma(t)=M(t)\Sigma(0)M(t)^{\mathsf T}$}

A real centered <Gaussian wave packet> with initial <position> <variance> $1/(2\alpha)$ and <momentum> <variance> $\hbar^2\alpha/2$ evolves under a <quantum harmonic oscillator> by
$$
M(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\omega t&\sin\omega t/(m\omega)\\
-m\omega\sin\omega t&\cos\omega t\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The <covariance matrix> obeys $\Sigma(t)=M\Sigma(0)M^{\mathsf T}$ and $\det\Sigma=\hbar^2/4$. Thus the <Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation> stays saturated, although the position-momentum <variance> product can exceed $\hbar^2/4$ because of nonzero <quantum covariance>. The widths are stationary only for $\alpha=m\omega/\hbar$; otherwise they breathe with <frequency> $2\omega$.

= Squeezed one-photon state
{parent=Squeezed vacuum state}
{title2=$S(r)|1\rangle$}

<photon addition> to an <optical squeezed vacuum> gives $a^\dagger S|0\rangle=\cosh r\,S|1\rangle$; subtraction gives $aS|0\rangle=-\sinh r\,S|1\rangle$. After successful-event normalization these are the same state up to phase, with mean occupation $1+3\sinh^2r$ and odd <photon> parity. At zero squeezing subtraction never succeeds; the normalized limiting state is not an output at that exact parameter value.

= Optical squeezed vacuum
{parent=Squeezed vacuum state}

A single optical mode in a <squeezed vacuum state>. In principal <optical quadratures>, its variances are $e^{-2r}/4$ and $e^{2r}/4$, while its mean <photon> number is $\sinh^2r$. Its mean field vanishes. A degenerate <optical parametric amplifier> generates the state by pair creation with phase fixed by the pump. Loss admixes unsqueezed vacuum and limits the measured variance reduction.

= Photon statistics of a squeezed vacuum
{parent=Optical squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$p_{2m}=\dfrac{(2m)!\tanh^{2m}r}{2^{2m}(m!)^2\cosh r},\quad p_{2m+1}=0$}

The <single-mode squeeze operator> changes occupation in pairs. Consequently an <optical squeezed vacuum> has only even <photon numbers>, with mean $s=\sinh^2r$ and <variance> $2s(s+1)$. Its large number fluctuations are compatible with <quadrature squeezing>: they concern different observables.

= Squeezed-vacuum photon-number moments
{parent=Photon statistics of a squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$\langle N^2\rangle=3s^2+2s,\quad\operatorname{Var}N=2s(s+1)$}

Here $s=\sinh^2r$. The pair moment is $\langle N(N-1)\rangle=3s^2+s$. These expressions follow from the single-mode <Bogoliubov transformation> or by differentiating the even-occupation <probability> generating function $G(t)=[\cosh r\sqrt{1-t^2\tanh^2r}]^{-1}$.

= Multimode squeezed vacuum
{parent=Squeezed vacuum state}
{title2=$|0\rangle=C\exp(\tfrac12a'{}^\dagger M a'{}^\dagger)|0'\rangle$}

For an invertible finite-mode <Bogoliubov transformation> $a=Aa'+Ba'{}^\dagger$, the symmetric squeezing matrix is $M=-A^{-1}B$. The <canonical commutation relations> make its singular values strictly less than one. The normalized state has $|C|=\det(I-MM^\dagger)^{1/4}$. Infinitely many modes additionally require <bosonic mode mixing implementability>.

= Two-mode squeezed vacuum
{parent=Multimode squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$|\Psi\rangle=\operatorname{sech}r\sum_{n\geq0}(e^{i\theta}\tanh r)^n|n,n\rangle$}

Pair creation in two bosonic modes gives the normalized state displayed. The two occupations coincide in each term, while either mode alone has a geometric number distribution. At small $r$, the leading nonvacuum term is a single pair. This optical construction does not require particle-antiparticle modes.

= Thermal reduction of a two-mode squeezed vacuum
{parent=Two-mode squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$\rho_1=(1-q)\sum_{n\ge0}q^n|n\rangle\langle n|$}

Tracing out one mode leaves geometric occupation <probabilities> with $q=|\lambda|^2$. In an oscillator basis of <frequency> $\omega$, they are <Boltzmann weights> with $k_BT=\hbar\omega/\ln(1/q)$. The joint state can be pure while each reduced state is mixed.

= Particle-antiparticle two-mode squeezed vacuum
{parent=Multimode squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{1-|q|^2}\sum_n(-q)^n|n,n\rangle$}

For a pair of distinct bosonic <particle> and <antiparticle> modes, the normalized state obeying $(a+qb^\dagger)|\psi\rangle=(b+qa^\dagger)|\psi\rangle=0$ is $\sqrt{1-|q|^2}\sum_{n\geq0}(-q)^n|n,n\rangle$, for $|q|<1$. It contains equal occupations and is generated by a pair-creation exponential. Infinite products require a separate implementability or regulator check.

= Geometric pair-number distribution
{parent=Particle-antiparticle two-mode squeezed vacuum}
{title2=$P(n)=(1-r)r^n$}

The pair count of a two-mode squeezed <vacuum> has $P(n)=(1-r)r^n$, where $r=|q|^2<1$. Its mean is $r/(1-r)$ and its <variance> is $r/(1-r)^2$. Equal mode occupations follow from pair creation, not from independent <particle> and <antiparticle> draws.

= Number operator
{parent=Creation and annihilation operators}
{wiki}

= Particle number operator
{synonym}

The number operator $N=A^\dagger A$ satisfies
$$
[N,A]=-A,
\qquad
[N,A^\dagger]=A^\dagger.
$$
It is self-adjoint and positive semidefinite.

= Number-difference shift identity
{parent=Number operator}
{title2=$Xf(\Delta)=f(\Delta-2)X$}

For a hopping operator $X$ with $[\Delta,X]=2X$, spectral functions obey $Xf(\Delta)=f(\Delta-2)X$. The coefficient on the left of the hop is evaluated on the final state. This ordering determines the shifted energy denominator in virtual-hopping calculations.

= Photon number
{parent=Number operator}
{title2=$n$}

For a single <electromagnetic field> mode, the <eigenvalues> of the <number operator> count its <photons>.

= Number state
{title2=$|n\rangle$}
{parent=Number operator}

A number state is a normalized <eigenstate> of the <number operator>:
$$
N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}.
$$
For a single <quantum harmonic oscillator>, it is also an <energy eigenstate> with energy $\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$.

= Integer spectrum of the number operator
{parent=Number operator}

If $N|\nu\rangle=\nu|\nu\rangle$, then $A$ lowers the eigenvalue by one and $\|A|\nu\rangle\|^2=\nu\|\nu\rangle\|^2$. Positivity forces the lowering ladder to terminate at eigenvalue zero, so every eigenvalue is a nonnegative integer.

= Two commensurate quantum harmonic oscillators
{parent=Quantum harmonic oscillator}

For independent frequencies $1$ and $2$,
$$
H_0=N_A+2N_B+\frac32,
$$
so the eigenspace at energy $k+3/2$ has one basis state $|n,m\rangle$ for each nonnegative solution of $n+2m=k$.
