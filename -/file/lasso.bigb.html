= Lasso regression

The Lasso minimizes regression error plus an $\ell^1$ penalty.

= Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso}
{title2=$X^{\mathsf T}(Y-Xb)/n\in\lambda\partial\|b\|_1$}

For the <Lasso> criterion $\|Y-Xb\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\|b\|_1$, the displayed <subgradient optimality condition> is necessary and sufficient. A nonzero coefficient has residual score $\lambda\operatorname{sign}(b_k)$, and a zero coefficient has residual score in $[-\lambda,\lambda]$. The <subdifferential of the L1 norm> supplies both cases.

= Lasso regularization path
{parent=Lasso}
{c}
{title2=$\widehat\beta(t)$}

The <Lasso> regularization path follows its coefficients as the penalty or constraint radius changes. Under the usual least-angle construction, the coefficient path is piecewise linear between knots, and variables can enter or leave the active set. Reading support at a selected <cross-validation> fraction requires checking which coefficients are actually zero at that fraction, rather than retaining only visibly large traces.

= KKT-consistent resolution of tied Lasso knots
{c}
{parent=Lasso regularization path}
{title2=$v=\operatorname*{argmin}\{\tfrac12v^TX^TXv-s_E^Tv_E:\ v_{E^c}=0,\ s_jv_j\geq0\ (j\in E\setminus N)\}$}

At a positive penalty level, let $E$ be the residual-score equality set, $s_j$ its signs and $N$ the nonzero <regression coefficient> support. With full column rank, the displayed <strictly convex> <quadratic program> selects the direction for a small decrease of the penalty. Existing nonzero <regression coefficients> have unrestricted tangent components; tied zero <regression coefficients> may move only with their residual signs. Stationarity gives $X_j^TXv=s_j$ for $j\in N$ and for entering zero coordinates, and $s_jX_j^TXv\geq1$ for tied coordinates that remain zero. These are exactly the first-order <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso> along the next segment. Normalize $Xv$ to obtain the corresponding least-angle direction. This resolves simultaneous entries or dropouts without adding a generic-position assumption.

= Lasso dropout knot
{c}
{parent=Lasso regularization path}
{title2=$\gamma_{\rm drop}=\min_{j\in A:\,-\beta_j/d_j>0}(-\beta_j/d_j)$}

On a least-angle segment $\beta(\gamma)=\beta+\gamma d$, a nonzero active <regression coefficient> reaches zero at the displayed first positive root. Continue only as far as the first dropout, inactive-entry event or zero-correlation endpoint. At a dropout remove the vanishing <regression coefficient> from the active direction and recompute. This preserves agreement between nonzero <regression coefficient> signs and residual-score signs, which the <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions for the Lasso> require. A removed <regression coefficient> may re-enter later; the active set need not grow monotonically.

= Gaussian score event for the Lasso
{parent=Lasso}
{c}
{title2=$\Omega=\{\|X^T\varepsilon\|_\infty/n\leq c\lambda\}$}

Let $0<c<1$, $A>0$, $\sigma>0$ and $p\geq2$. For <design matrix> columns of norm $\sqrt n$ and $\varepsilon\sim N_n(0,\sigma^2I)$, the event $\|X^T\varepsilon\|_\infty/n\leq c\lambda$ has probability at least $1-p^{1-A^2c^2/2}$ when $\lambda=A\sigma\sqrt{\log(p)/n}$. Apply the <sharp two-sided Gaussian tail bound> to each standardized score and then the <union bound>; independence of the scores is not required.

= Sparse maximum eigenvalue
{parent=Lasso}
{title2=$\kappa_m^2$}

For a <design matrix> $X$ with $p$ columns, define $\kappa_m^2=\max_{|M|=m}\lambda_{\max}(X_M^TX_M/n)$ for $1\le m\le p$. Equivalently this bounds $\|Xv\|_2^2/(n\|v\|_2^2)$ for <vectors> supported on at most $m$ coordinates. These quantities are nondecreasing: extend the support of a maximizing <vector> by zero coordinates and use the variational characterization of the largest <eigenvalue>. Values at $m>p$, including infinity, require a separate convention.

= Lasso support bound from sparse eigenvalues
{c}
{parent=Sparse maximum eigenvalue}
{title2=$|\widehat S|\le64\kappa_p^2s/\phi^2$}

For $\lambda>0$ and $\phi>0$, use the <Lasso> objective $\|Y-X\beta\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\|\beta\|_1$. Suppose the noise score has maximum <norm> at most $\lambda/2$, the true support has size $s$, and $\|X(\widehat\beta-\beta^0)\|_2^2/n\le16\lambda^2s/\phi^2$. For any nonempty $B\subseteq\widehat S$, the <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> imply
$$
\lambda|B|/2\le n^{-1}\operatorname{sgn}(\widehat\beta_B)^TX_B^TX(\beta^0-\widehat\beta)\le4\lambda\kappa_{|B|}\sqrt{|B|s}/\phi.
$$
The upper bound is the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality>. Hence $|B|\le64\kappa_{|B|}^2s/\phi^2$. A finite first index $m_*$ violating this inequality must exceed $|\widehat S|$. Its minimality also gives $|\widehat S|\le64\kappa_{m_*}^2s/\phi^2$. If no such index exists within $1,\ldots,p$, use the always defined bound $|\widehat S|\le64\kappa_p^2s/\phi^2$.

= Lava estimator
{c}
{title2=$\widehat\delta+\widehat\beta$}
{parent=Lasso}

= Regression with sparse and dense components
{synonym}

The Lava estimator minimizes $\|Y-X(\delta+\beta)\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda_1\|\delta\|_1+\lambda_2\|\beta\|_2^2$. It combines a component selected by <Lasso> with a component shrunk by <ridge regression>. Profiling the latter yields a transformed <Lasso> problem; this is not the same coefficient penalty as <elastic net regularization>.

= Lasso reduction for the Lava estimator
{parent=Lava estimator}

For $Q=(X^TX+2n\lambda_2I)^{-1}$ and $A=(I-XQX^T)^{1/2}$, profiling the dense coefficient gives $\widehat\beta=QX^T(Y-X\widehat\delta)$. The sparse coefficient minimizes $\|AY-AX\delta\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda_1\|\delta\|_1$. The transformation uses the <principal square root of a positive semidefinite matrix> and remains valid for rank-deficient designs.

= Fused Lasso
{parent=Lasso}

The fused Lasso adds an <L1 norm> penalty on adjacent coefficient differences to a squared-error objective, optionally together with an <L1 norm> penalty on the coefficients themselves. The differences penalty encourages neighbouring coefficients to coincide.

= One-dimensional total variation denoising
{title2=$\widehat\mu_\lambda=\arg\min_\mu\{\|Y-\mu\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\sum_i|\mu_{i+1}-\mu_i|\}$}
{parent=Fused Lasso}

= One-dimensional fused Lasso
{synonym}

This version of the <fused Lasso> penalizes only adjacent differences. It estimates a piecewise-constant signal while preserving an unpenalized constant level. Larger penalties generally favour fewer fitted changes.

= Dantzig selector
{c}
{parent=Lasso}
{wiki}

The Dantzig selector minimizes the coefficient $\ell^1$ norm subject to an $\ell^\infty$ bound on residual correlations.

= Square-root Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso}

The square-root Lasso uses the unsquared residual norm plus an $\ell^1$ penalty.

= Residual-score decomposition from square-root Lasso
{parent=Square-root Lasso}

For a fixed residual direction $R=Z-X\widetilde\beta_\gamma\ne0$ obtained by <square-root Lasso>, its optimality condition gives $\|X^TR\|_\infty\leq\sqrt n\,\gamma\|R\|_2$. In a fixed-design <normal linear model>, the score $R^T(Y-X\widehat\beta)/\|R\|_2$ equals a $N(0,\sigma^2)$ component plus a remainder bounded by $\sqrt n\,\gamma\|\beta^0-\widehat\beta\|_1$. The direction must be fixed or independent of the response noise for that normality claim.

= Tuning relation between Lasso and square-root Lasso
{title2=$\gamma=\lambda/\widehat\sigma_\lambda$}
{parent=Square-root Lasso}

A <Lasso> minimizer with positive residual scale $\widehat\sigma_\lambda=\|Y-X\widehat\beta_\lambda\|_2/\sqrt n$ also minimizes the <square-root Lasso> objective at the displayed tuning parameter. Divide its <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> by the residual scale and use the <subgradient optimality condition>.

= Group Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso}
{wiki}

The group Lasso partitions a coefficient vector into groups $G_1,\ldots,G_q$ and replaces the coordinatewise $\ell^1$ penalty with a weighted sum of group norms,
$$
P(\beta)=\sum_{j=1}^q m_j\lVert\beta_{G_j}\rVert_2.
$$
It tends to retain or discard an entire group at once. Its dual norm is
$$
P^*(v)=\max_j m_j^{-1}\lVert v_{G_j}\rVert_2,
$$
so the corresponding <Hölder inequality> is $|u^Tv|\leq P(u)P^*(v)$.

= Paired group Lasso for Gaussian graph estimation
{parent=Group Lasso}
{title2=$\sum_{j<k}\sqrt{\Theta_{jk}^2+\Theta_{kj}^2}$}

Joint squared-error <nodewise regression> with this penalty groups the two coefficients of each unordered edge. Its norm is nonsmooth only when the whole pair is zero, encouraging edge-wise sparsity. A fitted edge is present when its pair norm is positive. The penalty does not require equal directional coefficients, two nonzero entries in every selected group, or a symmetric positive definite <precision matrix>.

= Paired group Lasso optimality conditions
{parent=Paired group Lasso for Gaussian graph estimation}
{title2=$g=0:\ \|c\|_2\leq\lambda;\qquad g\ne0:\ c=\lambda g/\|g\|_2$}

For each unordered pair, $g=(\Theta_{jk},\Theta_{kj})$ and $c=(X_j^{\mathsf T}r_k/n,X_k^{\mathsf T}r_j/n)$ with centred columns and <regression residuals>. The <subdifferential> of the <Euclidean norm> is the unit ball at zero and $g/\|g\|_2$ otherwise. These <KKT conditions> are necessary and sufficient for the convex paired objective and explain joint edge deletion.

= Basic inequality for the Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso}

Comparing the <Lasso> objective at an estimate $\widehat\beta$ and a target $\beta^0$, then expanding the squared loss, bounds the prediction error by a noise-score term and the decrease in the $\ell^1$ penalty.

= Prediction inequality from Lasso stationarity
{parent=Basic inequality for the Lasso}
{title2=$\|X\delta\|_2^2/n\leq\eta^TX\delta/n+\lambda(\|\beta^0\|_1-\|\widehat\beta\|_1)$}

For <Lasso> loss $\|Y-X\beta\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\|\beta\|_1$ and $Y=X\beta^0+\eta$, stationarity with a penalty <subgradient> gives $\|X\delta\|_2^2/n\leq\eta^TX\delta/n+\lambda(\|\beta^0\|_1-\|\widehat\beta\|_1)$, $\delta=\widehat\beta-\beta^0$. This follows by multiplying the <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> by $\delta$ and using $z^T\widehat\beta=\|\widehat\beta\|_1$ and $z^T\beta^0\leq\|\beta^0\|_1$.

= Slow-rate prediction bound for the Lasso
{title2=$\|X(\widehat\beta-\beta^0)\|_2^2/n\leq4\lambda\|\beta^0\|_1$}
{parent=Basic inequality for the Lasso}

For $Y=X\beta^0+\eta$, the <Lasso> objective $\|Y-X\beta\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\|\beta\|_1$ has this prediction bound whenever $\|X^T\eta\|_\infty/n\leq\lambda$. Compare its value at $\widehat\beta$ and $\beta^0$, apply <Holder inequality> to the score, and use the triangle inequality. No <restricted eigenvalue condition> is required.

= Lasso cone condition
{c}
{parent=Basic inequality for the Lasso}

On an event where the noise-score infinity norm is at most half the penalty, the Lasso error $\delta$ obeys $\lVert\delta_{S^c}\rVert_1\leq3\lVert\delta_S\rVert_1$.

= Restricted eigenvalue condition
{parent=Lasso cone condition}

A design matrix satisfies a restricted eigenvalue condition with constant $\gamma>0$ on a cone $C$ when $\lVert X\delta\rVert_2^2/n\geq\gamma\lVert\delta\rVert_2^2$ for every nonzero $\delta\in C$.

= Compatibility condition for the Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso cone condition}

A compatibility condition lower-bounds prediction norm by a scaled $\ell^1$ norm on a <Lasso cone condition>. One convention requires $|S|\lVert X\delta\rVert_2^2/n\geq\phi^2\lVert\delta_S\rVert_1^2$ throughout the cone.

= Fast-rate Lasso prediction bound under compatibility
{parent=Compatibility condition for the Lasso}
{title2=$Q+(1-c)\lambda\|\delta_{S^c}\|_1\leq(1+c)^2\lambda^2s/\phi^2$}

Let $\delta=\widehat\beta-\beta^0$, $s=|S|>0$ and $\phi>0$. On the score event $\|X^T\eta\|_\infty/n\leq c\lambda$ with $0<c<1$, the <prediction inequality from Lasso stationarity> gives $Q+(1-c)\lambda\|\delta_{S^c}\|_1\leq(1+c)\lambda\|\delta_S\|_1$. If the <compatibility condition for the Lasso> with constant $\phi$ holds on this cone, then $Q+(1-c)\lambda\|\delta_{S^c}\|_1\leq(1+c)^2\lambda^2s/\phi^2$. Here $Q=\|X\delta\|_2^2/n$ and $S$ is the true <support of a vector>.

= Prediction and coefficient error bound for compatible Lasso
{parent=Fast-rate Lasso prediction bound under compatibility}
{title2=$Q+\lambda\|\delta\|_1\leq16\lambda^2s/\phi_0^2$}

With objective $\|Y-Xb\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda\|b\|_1$, the <Gaussian score event for the Lasso> $\|X^T\epsilon/n\|_\infty\leq\lambda/2$ gives $Q+\lambda\|\delta_{S^c}\|_1\leq3\lambda\|\delta_S\|_1$, where $Q=\|X\delta\|_2^2/n$. The <Lasso cone condition> activates the <compatibility condition for the Lasso>. Then $Q+\lambda\|\delta\|_1\leq4\lambda\sqrt{sQ}/\phi_0\leq Q/2+8\lambda^2s/\phi_0^2$. Thus even $Q+2\lambda\|\delta\|_1\leq16\lambda^2s/\phi_0^2$. This controls prediction and coefficient error together, including a design with correlated columns.

= Lasso sign recovery under compatibility
{parent=Fast-rate Lasso prediction bound under compatibility}
{c}
{title2=$|\beta^0_k|>(1+c)\lambda s/\phi^2$}

The <fast-rate Lasso prediction bound under compatibility> implies $\|\widehat\beta_S-\beta^0_S\|_1\leq(1+c)\lambda s/\phi^2$. Each true coefficient larger in absolute value than this bound has the correct estimated sign. This conclusion alone does not ensure absence of false positives.

= Compatibility constant
{title2=$\phi_\Sigma$}
{parent=Compatibility condition for the Lasso}

For a positive-semidefinite matrix $\Sigma$, an active set $S$ of size $s$, and cone factor three, one compatibility constant is defined by
$$
\phi_\Sigma^2
=s\inf_{\|\delta_S\|_1=1,\,\|\delta_{S^c}\|_1\leq3}
\delta^T\Sigma\delta.
$$
It measures how strongly the quadratic form controls active-coordinate $\ell^1$ error on the <Lasso cone condition>.

= Stability of a compatibility constant under entrywise perturbation
{parent=Compatibility constant}

If $\|\Theta-\Sigma\|_{\max}\leq\phi_\Sigma^2/(32s)$, then $\phi_\Theta^2\geq\phi_\Sigma^2/2$. Indeed, every normalized cone vector has $\|\delta\|_1\leq4$, so its quadratic form changes by at most $16\|\Theta-\Sigma\|_{\max}\leq\phi_\Sigma^2/(2s)$.

= Lasso sign recovery from cone invertibility
{c}
{parent=Compatibility condition for the Lasso}

Suppose every $\delta$ in the <Lasso cone condition> satisfies $\psi\|\delta_S\|_\infty\leq\|\widehat\Sigma\delta\|_\infty$. On the usual score event, the <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> give $\|\widehat\Sigma(\widehat\beta-\beta^0)\|_\infty\leq3\lambda/2$. Hence every active-coordinate error is at most $3\lambda/(2\psi)$, and a larger minimum nonzero signal guarantees coordinatewise sign recovery.

= Debiased Lasso
{c}
{parent=Lasso}

The debiased Lasso adds an approximate inverse-Gram correction to a Lasso estimate so that each corrected coordinate has an asymptotically linear expansion.

= Debiased-Lasso remainder bound
{parent=Debiased Lasso}
{title2=$\|\Delta\|_\infty\le\sqrt n\|\beta^0-\widehat\beta\|_1\max_j\lambda_j/\widehat\tau_j^2$}

Let $\widehat\Sigma=X^TX/n$ and use <nodewise Lasso> with $\lambda_j>0$ and nonzero design columns. Form $c_j$ with entry $j$ equal to one and other entries $-\widehat\gamma^{(j)}$, and put row $j$ of $\widehat\Theta$ equal to $c_j^T/\widehat\tau_j^2$. The <nodewise-Lasso residual identity> and the <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> show that the diagonal of $\widehat\Theta\widehat\Sigma$ equals one and its row-$j$ off-diagonal entries have magnitude at most $\lambda_j/\widehat\tau_j^2$. For the <debiased Lasso>,
$$
\sqrt n(\widehat b-\beta^0)=\widehat\Theta X^T\varepsilon/\sqrt n+\Delta,\qquad \Delta=\sqrt n(\widehat\Theta\widehat\Sigma-I)(\beta^0-\widehat\beta).
$$
The displayed bound follows row by row from <Hölder inequality>. If the noise is conditionally <multivariate normal> with <covariance> $\sigma^2I$, and the nodewise fits and tuning parameters depend only on $X$, the first term is conditionally normal with <covariance> $\sigma^2\widehat\Theta\widehat\Sigma\widehat\Theta^T$.

= Approximate inverse of a Gram matrix
{parent=Debiased Lasso}

A matrix $\Theta$ is an $\eta$-approximate inverse of a Gram matrix $\widehat\Sigma$ in entrywise maximum norm when
$$
\lVert\widehat\Sigma\Theta-I\rVert_{\max}\leq\eta.
$$
This turns the bias remainder of a <debiased Lasso> into a product of $\eta$ and the coefficient $\ell^1$ error.

= Nodewise Lasso
{c}
{parent=Debiased Lasso}

The nodewise Lasso regresses each design column on the remaining columns and uses its residual to estimate a column of the inverse covariance or inverse Gram matrix.

= Nodewise-Lasso residual identity
{parent=Nodewise Lasso}
{title2=$\widehat\tau_j^2=X_j^Tr_j/n$}

For <nodewise Lasso> with objective $\|X_j-X_{-j}\gamma\|_2^2/(2n)+\lambda_j\|\gamma\|_1$, put $r_j=X_j-X_{-j}\widehat\gamma^{(j)}$. Its <Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions> give $X_{-j}^Tr_j/n=\lambda_jz_j$ with $z_j\in\partial\|\widehat\gamma^{(j)}\|_1$. Hence
$$
X_j^Tr_j/n=\|r_j\|_2^2/n+\lambda_j\|\widehat\gamma^{(j)}\|_1=\widehat\tau_j^2.
$$
For $\lambda_j>0$ this is positive whenever $X_j\ne0$; if it were zero both nonnegative terms would vanish, forcing $\widehat\gamma^{(j)}=0$ and $X_j=r_j=0$. Nonzero columns are therefore an essential hypothesis for division by this quantity.

= Debiased-Lasso asymptotic normality
{c}
{parent=Debiased Lasso}

Under sparsity, compatibility, and inverse-Gram estimation conditions, the standardized coordinatewise error of the <debiased Lasso> converges in distribution to a <standard normal distribution>.

= Gaussian identity design for debiased Lasso
{c}
{parent=Debiased-Lasso asymptotic normality}

In the <normal linear model>, assume $\varepsilon\sim N_n(0,\sigma^2I)$ is independent of $X$. Independent rows of $X$ with <multivariate normal distribution> $N_p(0,I_p)$ give a simple random design for <debiased Lasso>. Let $p\to\infty$, $\log p=o(n)$ and $s\log p=o(\sqrt n)$, with fixed positive noise scale. Choose the <Lasso> penalty and all <nodewise Lasso> penalties as sufficiently large constant multiples of $\sqrt{\log p/n}$, including the noise scale in the former. Uniform sample cross-correlations are bounded by this scale, so zero is the nodewise solution with <probability> tending to one. Column squared <norms> divided by $n$ converge uniformly to one. To check the <compatibility condition for the Lasso> directly, put $\widehat\Sigma=X^TX/n$ and $\eta=\max_{j,k}|\widehat\Sigma_{jk}-\mathbf1_{\{j=k\}}|$. On the <Lasso cone condition>, $\|\delta\|_1\le4\|\delta_S\|_1$, and the <Cauchy-Schwarz inequality> gives
$$
\delta^T\widehat\Sigma\delta\ge\|\delta\|_2^2-\eta\|\delta\|_1^2\ge(1/s-16\eta)\|\delta_S\|_1^2.
$$
For $s\ge1$, <entrywise concentration of a Gaussian Gram matrix> gives $\eta\le D_0\sqrt{\log p/n}$ with <probability> tending to one for sufficiently large fixed $D_0$. The stated sparsity condition implies $s\eta\to0$ on this event, so one can take $\phi^2\ge1/2$ eventually. The conditional <Gaussian tail bound> for the noise score and the <basic inequality for the Lasso> then give $\|\widehat\beta-\beta^0\|_1\le C s\sqrt{\log p/n}$ with <probability> tending to one. Consequently the <debiased-Lasso remainder bound> is at most $A s\log p/\sqrt n$ with <probability> tending to one, for a fixed constant $A$. These assumptions allow both $p$ and $s$ to grow, for example $p=n^2$, $s=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor$.

= Soft-thresholding operator
{synonym}

Soft thresholding maps $u$ to $\operatorname{sgn}(u)(|u|-\lambda)_+$ and is the proximal operator of the absolute-value penalty.

= Berhu penalty
{c}
{parent=Lasso}

= Reverse-Huber penalty
{c}
{synonym}

The Berhu, or reverse-Huber, penalty with threshold $\delta>0$ is
$$
\rho_\delta(t)=
\begin{cases}
|t|,&|t|\leq\delta,\\
(t^2+\delta^2)/(2\delta),&|t|>\delta.
\end{cases}
$$
It behaves like the <absolute value> near zero and quadratically for large coefficients.

= Elastic net regularization
{parent=Lasso}
{wiki}

= Elastic net
{synonym}

Elastic-net regression minimizes squared error plus both an $\ell^1$ coefficient penalty and a squared $\ell^2$ penalty. It combines exact sparsity with strict convexity when the quadratic penalty is positive.

= Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space
{parent=Elastic net regularization}
{title2=$J(u)=\|u\|_1+\beta\|u\|_2^2,\quad\beta>0$}

Extend $J$ by infinity outside $\ell^1$. For a <bounded linear operator> $K:\ell^2\to\ell^2$, the objective $\|Ku-f\|_2^2/2+\alpha J(u)$ is coercive, weakly lower semicontinuous and <strictly convex> for $\alpha>0$, so it has a unique minimizer. The quadratic penalty ensures existence and uniqueness even if $K$ has a <null space>. Its limiting exact solution minimizes $J$ in the data fiber, which need not be the <minimum-norm least-squares solution>.

= Nonlinearity of elastic net reconstruction
{parent=Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space}

For fixed $\alpha>0$ and nonzero bounded $K$, the reconstruction vanishes when $\|K^*f\|_\infty\leq\alpha$, which includes a neighborhood of zero. If it were linear it would vanish everywhere. Choose a coordinate $e_j$ with $Ke_j\ne0$ and data $f=tKe_j$ with $t\|Ke_j\|^2>\alpha$; the optimality inequality at zero fails, so its minimizer is nonzero. Thus the map is nonlinear. The qualification $K\ne0$ is essential: $K=0$ gives the zero linear map.

= Norm convergence of elastic net regularization
{parent=Elastic net regularization in Hilbert sequence space}

Assume an exact solution of finite penalty exists and select the unique $J$-minimizing exact solution $u^\dagger$. Minimality gives $\|Ku_\alpha^\delta-f^\delta\|^2/2+\alpha J(u_\alpha^\delta)\leq\delta^2/2+\alpha J(u^\dagger)$. If $\alpha\to0$ and $\delta^2/\alpha\to0$, boundedness, <weak sequential compactness of bounded sequences in a reflexive Banach space> and lower semicontinuity identify every weak limit with $u^\dagger$. The separate lower semicontinuity of $\|u\|_1$ and $\|u\|_2^2$, together with the upper bound on $J$, forces the Hilbert norms to converge. The <Radon-Riesz theorem> then gives <norm convergence>.

= Grouping effect of the elastic net
{parent=Elastic net regularization}

The quadratic penalty encourages strongly correlated predictors to receive similar coefficients, so an elastic net tends to retain or discard correlated predictors as a group more readily than the Lasso.
