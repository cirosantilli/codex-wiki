= Solution
{parent=1}

In the rotating terrestrial frame, horizontal <pressure gradient force> and <Coriolis force> balance in <geostrophic balance>. Gravity establishes the approximately vertical <hydrostatic equilibrium>; it is not an additional horizontal force in this balance. With eastward $x$, northward $y$, upward unit vector $\hat{\mathbf z}$ and <Coriolis parameter> $f=2\Omega\sin\phi$,
$$
0=-\frac{\nabla_hp}{\rho}-f\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u_g,\qquad
\boxed{\mathbf u_g=\frac{\hat{\mathbf z}\times\nabla_hp}{\rho f},\quad
u_g=-\frac{p_y}{\rho f},\quad v_g=\frac{p_x}{\rho f}.}
$$
The <geostrophic wind> runs along <atmospheric isobars>, with lower pressure on its left in the Northern Hemisphere.

Interpreting the stated south-to-north gradient as $p_y>0$, $p_y=200\,\mathrm{Pa}/(2.00\times10^5\,\mathrm m)=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,m^{-1}}$, while $p_x=0$. Using the external nominal <Earth's rotation> rate $\Omega=7.292115\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ gives the <meridional-pressure-gradient geostrophic wind>
$$
\boxed{u_g(30^\circ\mathrm N)=-11.4\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad
u_g(60^\circ\mathrm N)=-6.60\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\qquad v_g=0.}
$$
Both zonal velocities are westward; their signs reverse if the supplied gradient is interpreted as a northward decrease. <IERS Conventions (2010) by International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service>[Table1.2, physical PDF p.19: nominal mean angular velocity]{ref}

The <validity criteria for geostrophic balance> require horizontal acceleration and friction to be small compared with the balancing forces: <Rossby number> $U/(|f|L)\ll1$ and slow evolution compared with $1/|f|$. The approximation fails near the equator, on sufficiently small or rapidly changing scales, in strongly curved flow needing appreciable centripetal acceleration, and in the frictional surface layer. Neglecting friction near the ground is the stated idealization.

For an illustrative <geostrophic wind with linear drag>, use acceleration $-r\mathbf u$ with $r>0$. The steady horizontal momentum equation gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}r&-f\\ f&r\end{pmatrix}\binom uv=-\frac1\rho\binom{p_x}{p_y},\qquad
\boxed{u=-\frac{fp_y}{\rho(f^2+r^2)},\quad v=-\frac{rp_y}{\rho(f^2+r^2)}\quad(p_x=0).}
$$
Drag reduces the wind and hence its Coriolis acceleration; the wind crosses isobars toward lower pressure. Here it points southwest. The pressure-gradient acceleration points south, Coriolis northwest and drag northeast; the three forces sum to zero. The cross-isobar angle satisfies $\tan\alpha=r/f$ for $f>0$. A turbulent boundary layer has a vertically varying stress rather than necessarily this constant linear drag.

Use spectral <radiant exitance> per unit wavelength. The <Planck law> is
$$
M_\lambda(T)=\frac{2\pi hc^2}{\lambda^5}\frac1{e^{hc/(\lambda k_{\rm B}T)}-1}.
$$
A hotter <blackbody> radiates more at every wavelength, its wavelength peak moves to shorter wavelengths according to <Wien's displacement law>, and its integrated flux rises as $\sigma T^4$ by the <Stefan–Boltzmann law>. The wavelength convention matters: a spectrum plotted per unit frequency has a different peak transformation.

\Image[paper-1-ap2013-blackbody-spectra.png]
{title=Illustrative wavelength spectra: a hotter blackbody has a larger total flux and a shorter peak wavelength}
{source=paper-1-ap2013-blackbody-spectra.py}

For <planetary equilibrium temperature>, assume an isotropic blackbody star, a circular orbit, negligible internal heating, unit surface thermal emissivity and efficient global heat redistribution so the surface has one temperature. The received stellar irradiance is $F_*=\sigma T_*^4(R_*/a)^2$. A planet of radius $R_p$ intercepts $\pi R_p^2F_*$ but radiates over $4\pi R_p^2$, giving the <projected-area factor in planetary energy balance>
$$
\boxed{T_0=T_*\sqrt{\frac{R_*}{2a}}=303.4\,\mathrm K.}
$$
Without the uniform-temperature assumption, this calculation gives $\langle T^4\rangle^{1/4}$, not a unique arithmetic mean of surface temperature.

With <Bond albedo> $A=0.05$, the absorbed fraction is $1-A$. Hence
$$
\boxed{T_A=T_0(1-A)^{1/4}=299.5\,\mathrm K,}
$$
a decrease of approximately $3.87\,\mathrm K$. This albedo weights incident stellar light and does not imply thermal emissivity $0.95$.

Define the <planetary effective emissivity> by outgoing top-of-atmosphere flux $F_{\rm out}=\epsilon_{\rm eff}\sigma T_s^4$. Maintaining the same absorbed stellar power with $T_s=312\,\mathrm K$ gives
$$
\boxed{\epsilon_{\rm eff}=\left(\frac{T_A}{T_s}\right)^4=0.849.}
$$
The <greenhouse effect> allows thermal radiation to escape from colder layers, so the outgoing flux is less than a black surface at $T_s$ would emit. Downward atmospheric emission contributes to the surface energy balance; this does not violate the top-of-atmosphere energy balance.<Climate and Earth's Energy Budget by NASA Earth Observatory>[Thermal radiation, atmospheric absorption and effective radiating versus surface temperature]{ref}

A <single-layer greenhouse model> makes the distinction quantitative. Take a shortwave-transparent, isothermal layer with infrared absorptivity and emissivity $\epsilon_a$, above a surface black in the thermal infrared. Writing $Q=(1-A)F_*/4$, its surface and atmospheric balances are
$$
Q+\epsilon_a\sigma T_a^4=\sigma T_s^4,\qquad
\epsilon_a\sigma T_s^4=2\epsilon_a\sigma T_a^4.
$$
For $\epsilon_a>0$, $T_a^4=T_s^4/2$. Radiation escaping to space is $(1-\epsilon_a)\sigma T_s^4+\epsilon_a\sigma T_a^4$, so
$$
\boxed{\epsilon_{\rm eff}=1-\frac{\epsilon_a}{2},\qquad
\epsilon_a=0.301,\qquad T_a=262.4\,\mathrm K.}
$$
Thus **$0.849$ is the planet's effective emissivity; $0.301$ is the layer's infrared emissivity within this particular model.** They are not interchangeable. Real atmospheres require wavelength-dependent absorption, a vertical temperature profile and nonradiative heat transport; the measured temperatures alone do not uniquely retrieve an atmospheric optical profile.

A <teleconnection> is correlated atmospheric variability between separated regions. A principal dynamical pathway is a <Rossby-wave teleconnection>: a displaced air column conserves <potential vorticity>, so changing planetary vorticity must be balanced by changing relative <vorticity>. The background potential-vorticity gradient supplies the restoring mechanism.

In a uniform zonal current $U$ on a <beta plane>, a simple <barotropic Rossby wave> has
$$
\boxed{\omega=Uk-\frac{\beta k}{k^2+l^2},\qquad\beta=\frac{df}{dy}.}
$$
The intrinsic phase propagates westward for $\beta>0$; advection can leave a nearly stationary pattern in the terrestrial frame. Energy and information travel with the <group velocity>, not necessarily with individual crests or the same air parcels. A <jet stream> can guide wave packets and link distant ridges and troughs.

For example, persistent anomalous tropical heating can excite wave trains that alter remote pressure, temperature and storm tracks. Tropical Pacific ocean-temperature anomalies can modify that heating, yielding reproducible extratropical patterns. Their phase and strength depend on the background winds, potential-vorticity gradients, damping, season and eddy feedbacks. **A correlation pattern is not by itself proof of a unique causal pathway**, and not every teleconnection is a single stationary linear wave.<Teleconnections and stationary Rossby waves by Isaac Held>[9March2015: rotating-sphere response to localized equatorial heating and extratropical ENSO patterns]{ref}

A <foehn wind> is relatively warm, dry downslope air on a mountain's lee side. In the classical condensation pathway, ascending windward air expands and cools at the <dry adiabatic lapse rate> until the <lifting condensation level>. Condensation then releases <latent heat>, reducing the cooling rate to the <moist adiabatic lapse rate>. <Atmospheric precipitation> removes condensate, and the descending parcel warms by compression at approximately the dry rate. Its <relative humidity> falls.

For equal starting and finishing heights, complete condensate removal and a constant moist lapse rate above condensation height $z_c$, the <precipitation-driven foehn warming> is
$$
\boxed{T_{\rm lee}-T_{\rm windward}\simeq(\Gamma_d-\Gamma_m)(H-z_c)>0.}
$$
Here $H>z_c$ is the parcel's maximum height and $\Gamma_d>\Gamma_m$. The warming is adiabatic compression during descent, not heating by friction. Without condensation or a difference in source air, a reversible dry parcel returning to its original height would regain its original temperature.

Precipitation is not essential to every foehn event. <Isentropic foehn drawdown> can bring air of higher <potential temperature> from aloft down the lee slope; turbulent mixing and extra solar heating beneath clear skies can also contribute. The sketch shows the classical rainout pathway rather than asserting a unique mechanism.<Foehn effect by Met Office>[How does the foehn effect work? Condensation/rainout, drawdown, mixing and radiative warming]{ref}

For <dry atmospheric Ertel potential vorticity>, use <absolute vorticity> $\boldsymbol\omega_a=\nabla\times\mathbf u+2\boldsymbol\Omega$ and <potential temperature> $\theta=T(p_0/p)^{R_d/c_p}$:
$$
\boxed{q_E=\frac{\boldsymbol\omega_a\cdot\nabla\theta}{\rho},\qquad
\frac{Dq_E}{Dt}=0.}
$$
The conservation statement is <Ertel's theorem> for smooth, inviscid, adiabatic flow with conservative body forces and a thermodynamic equation of state $p=p(\rho,\theta)$. Stretching of $\boldsymbol\omega_a/\rho$ cancels deformation of $\nabla\theta$ in their material derivative; the remaining baroclinic triple product vanishes because $\nabla p$ lies in the span of $\nabla\rho,\nabla\theta$. The atmospheric form includes $1/\rho$; the commonly used <Boussinesq approximation> version has a different normalization.

In a predominantly vertical stratification, $q_E\simeq(f+\zeta)\,\partial_z\theta/\rho$. Thus rotation and stratification jointly label air masses. Material conservation explains how parcels deform and stretch while preserving a useful scalar, supports <Rossby waves>, and helps diagnose sharp fronts, jets and stratospheric intrusions.

Under an appropriate balance model, <potential-vorticity inversion> recovers associated wind and temperature fields from a PV distribution together with stratification and boundary data. **PV alone does not specify every component of an arbitrary atmospheric flow.** <Potential-vorticity generation by heating and friction> limits conservative parcel reasoning near strong heating, surface stress or mixing.<Fundamentals of Atmosphere-Ocean Dynamics by Michael Edgeworth McIntyre>[PartII§12, equationII.17, physical PDF pp.150–152: dry atmospheric PV and qualified invertibility]{ref}
