= Solution
{parent=1}

Put $g=\gamma P_0$. For a temporally uniform envelope, $A_z=igA$ and $A_{TT}=0$, so the propagation equation reduces to $-gA+\gamma P_0A=0$. Thus **the <power> stays $P_0$ and the accumulated nonlinear <wave phase> is $g z$**. The <optical Kerr effect> gives an intensity-dependent <refractive index>, and hence <self-phase modulation>. For the usual <power> normalization $\gamma\simeq n_2\omega_0/(cA_{\rm eff})$, with effective guided-mode area $A_{\rm eff}$. Losslessness preserves the background <power>; it does not remove the <self-phase modulation>. The carrier <wave phase> has already been factored out.

Write $A=(\sqrt{P_0}+\epsilon)e^{igz}$. To first order,
$$
|\sqrt{P_0}+\epsilon|^2(\sqrt{P_0}+\epsilon)
=P_0^{3/2}+P_0(2\epsilon+\epsilon^*)+O(|\epsilon|^2).
$$
Cancelling the background and its phase derivative leaves
$$
i\epsilon_z-\frac{\beta_2}{2}\epsilon_{TT}+g(\epsilon+\epsilon^*)=0.
$$
The last term couples a <perturbation> to its <complex conjugate>: the pump can transfer <energy> into a pair of sidebands. It is not an independent <wave phase> of each Fourier component. This <sideband coupling> is the linear origin of <fibre modulational instability>.

For real $K,\Omega,c_1,c_2$, put $d=\beta_2\Omega^2/2$. Matching the two exponential coefficients gives
$$
\begin{pmatrix}d+g-K&g\\g&d+g+K\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}c_1\\c_2\end{pmatrix}=0.
$$
A nonzero pair requires a zero <determinant>, hence
$$
\boxed{K^2=d(d+2g)=\frac{\beta_2^2\Omega^2}{4}
\left(\Omega^2+\frac{4g}{\beta_2}\right).}
$$
For unstable complex $K$, the real-coefficient assumption is too restrictive. The consistent pair is $\epsilon=u e^{iKz-i\Omega T}+v^*e^{-iK^*z+i\Omega T}$, with complex $u,v$. Its equations give the same <determinant> and allow the growing and decaying branches. Merely inserting imaginary $K$ while treating the two original coefficients as real would not respect <complex conjugation>.

For $P_0>0$ and nonzero $\beta_2,\gamma$, exponential growth requires **$\beta_2\gamma<0$** and
$$
0<\Omega^2<-\frac{4\gamma P_0}{\beta_2}.
$$
For ordinary positive Kerr nonlinearity this means anomalous <group velocity dispersion>, $\beta_2<0$. Define $\Omega_c=\sqrt{4g/|\beta_2|}$. The growing branch is $K=-iG$, with <amplitude> growth rate
$$
G(\Omega)=\frac{|\beta_2||\Omega|}{2}\sqrt{\Omega_c^2-\Omega^2},\qquad
\boxed{|\Omega|_{\max}=\Omega_c/\sqrt2,\quad G_{\max}=g.}
$$
Sideband <power> grows at rate $2G$, rather than $G$. The cutoff and zero-frequency modes have no exponential growth; in a degenerate zero-frequency sector a secular phase response can remain. Positive dispersion with positive $\gamma$ gives real $K$ and oscillatory <perturbations>.

Initially small ripples grow into pronounced <intensity> peaks and deplete the uniform background. Dispersion and <self-phase modulation> can then support localized bright pulses. A <fundamental temporal bright soliton> obeys $\gamma P_sT_0^2=|\beta_2|$, so its width is comparable to $1/\Omega_c$ when its peak <power> is comparable to the original <power>. The dominant ripple spacing is $2\pi/|\Omega|_{\max}$, not simply $1/\Omega_c$. Propagation distance $z$ is the evolution variable; $T$ measures time within the envelope. An ideal conservative fibre need not settle permanently into a unique pulse train: nonlinear compression, interactions and recurrence depend on the seed and boundary conditions.

= Solution
{parent=2}

A degenerate <optical parametric amplifier> sends a strong pump at $2\omega$ through a phase-matched second-order nonlinear medium, creating pairs in a selected mode near $\omega$. With the pump treated classically and undepleted, the effective interaction is quadratic in the mode's <creation and annihilation operators>. Vacuum input evolves into an <optical squeezed vacuum>; the pump phase fixes the quiet <optical quadrature>. A cavity enhances the interaction below oscillation threshold. <balanced homodyne detection> compares this <optical quadrature>'s noise with the blocked-signal vacuum reference; phase locking, detector balance, electronic-noise measurement and loss calibration distinguish squeezing from an instrumental noise reduction. A primary cavity experiment observed below-vacuum noise by degenerate down conversion and balanced homodyne readout.<Generation of Squeezed States by Parametric Down Conversion by Ling-An Wu and others>[1986, abstract: cavity down conversion and homodyne noise reduction]{ref}

Use $S(r)=\exp[\tfrac r2(a^2-a^{\dagger2})]$, so $S^\dagger aS=a\cosh r-a^\dagger\sinh r$. Write $s=\sinh^2r$, $c=\cosh r$ and $N=a^\dagger a$. Vacuum expectations then give
$$
\boxed{\langle N\rangle=s},\qquad
\langle a^2\rangle=-\sinh r\cosh r.
$$
Pair creation makes the mean field zero while the mean <photon number> is positive. Expanding the transformed ladder operators in the vacuum gives the <squeezed-vacuum photon-number moments>: $\langle N(N-1)\rangle=3s^2+s$ and $\langle N^2\rangle=3s^2+2s$; equivalently $\operatorname{Var}N=2s(s+1)$.

The vectors produced by <photon addition> and <photon subtraction> in the paper are not normalized. Their squared norms are $s+1$ and $s$, respectively. A particularly direct evaluation is
$$
a^\dagger S(r)|0\rangle=cS(r)|1\rangle,\qquad
aS(r)|0\rangle=-\sinh r\,S(r)|1\rangle.
$$
Both successful operations therefore prepare the same <squeezed one-photon state>, up to an overall phase, whenever subtraction has nonzero <probability>. Since $\langle1|S^\dagger NS|1\rangle=c^2+2s=1+3s$, the unnormalized moments are
$$
M_+=\langle\Psi_+|N|\Psi_+\rangle=(c^2+2s)c^2=(3s+1)(s+1),
\qquad M_-=\langle\Psi_-|N|\Psi_-\rangle=s(3s+1).
$$
**The printed photon-added formula equals $M_+$, not a normalized mean.** With its literal unnormalized convention, the corresponding subtraction result is $M_-$ and
$$
\boxed{M_+-M_-=1+3\sinh^2r>0.}
$$
This difference tends to one as $r\to0$ and grows as $\tfrac34e^{2r}$ for large positive $r$. Keeping this convention recovers the paper's requested expression without misidentifying it as a physical conditional mean.

For the <normalization of photon-added and photon-subtracted states>, divide by the squared norms to obtain actual conditional means:
$$
\boxed{\bar n_+=\frac{M_+}{s+1}=1+3s,
\qquad \bar n_-=\frac{M_-}{s}=1+3s,
\qquad \bar n_+-\bar n_-=0.}
$$
These equalities for subtraction require $s>0$. As $r\to0$ the normalized successful subtraction tends to $|1\rangle$, but its success <probability> vanishes; exactly at $r=0$, $a|0\rangle=0$ gives no conditional state. At large $r$, both normalized means grow as $3e^{2r}/4$, while their difference remains zero.

For the <photon statistics of a squeezed vacuum>, the even-number <probabilities> are
$$
p_{2m}=\frac{(2m)!}{2^{2m}(m!)^2}\frac{\tanh^{2m}r}{\cosh r},\qquad p_{2m+1}=0.
$$
After subtraction the normalized distribution is $p^-_n=(n+1)p_{n+1}/s$. It has only odd occupation and no vacuum component. <photon addition> gives $p^+_n=np_{n-1}/(s+1)$, identical here to $p^-_n$. The diagram uses normalized distributions with an explicitly chosen $r$, rather than treating the unnormalized moments as means.

\Image[paper-1-conditioned-photon-statistics.png]
{title=Original normalized even squeezed-vacuum and odd conditional photon-number distributions, with raw-moment and mean conventions distinguished}

A subtraction event samples initial occupation with weight $n$, preferentially selecting the larger components before removing one <photon>. In general $\bar n_-=\langle N(N-1)\rangle/\langle N\rangle=\langle N\rangle-1+\operatorname{Var}N/\langle N\rangle$. Here the large <variance> gives **$\bar n_--\langle N\rangle=1+2s>0$**. This <conditional photon-number bias> does not create <energy> by removing a <photon>: the unconditional ensemble also includes the runs without a heralded event.

= Solution
{parent=a}

An <optical soliton> maintains a localized profile because nonlinear refraction compensates linear spreading. A <temporal optical soliton> balances <group velocity dispersion> against <self-phase modulation>; a <spatial optical soliton> balances <diffraction> against <self-focusing>. The relevant coordinate and sign of nonlinearity must be specified.

For anomalous <group velocity dispersion> and positive $\gamma$, a <fundamental temporal bright soliton> is
$$
\boxed{A(z,T)=\sqrt{P_s}\operatorname{sech}(T/T_0)
 e^{i\gamma P_s z/2},\qquad \gamma P_sT_0^2=|\beta_2|.}
$$
The identity $(\operatorname{sech}u)''=\operatorname{sech}u-2\operatorname{sech}^3u$ verifies both the width relation and propagation phase. Its <dispersion length> $L_D=T_0^2/|\beta_2|$ equals its <nonlinear length> $L_{\rm NL}=1/(\gamma P_s)$. Bright pulses decay to zero background. <dark solitons> are <intensity> depressions on a nonzero background with a phase change across the dip; black dips reach zero <intensity>, while grey dips do not. For positive $\gamma$ the corresponding temporal defocusing regime has normal <group velocity dispersion>.

A <higher-order temporal soliton> has <soliton order> $N_s=\sqrt{\gamma P_sT_0^2/|\beta_2|}$ equal to an integer greater than one for the special initial $N_s\operatorname{sech}$ profile in normalized units. It breathes through compression and splitting/recombination, rather than keeping one fixed shape. Separated fundamental <solitons> can interact and acquire collision shifts. An arbitrary pulse with noninteger $N_s$ is not automatically a pure higher-order <soliton>; dispersive radiation can carry part of its <energy>.

In ideal one-dimensional cubic propagation, dispersion and nonlinearity support stable fundamental bright pulses. Loss, higher-order dispersion, delayed nonlinear response and finite bandwidth change real-fibre propagation. A one-dimensional spatial bright solution also does not guarantee <stability> against two-dimensional collapse. Profile preservation, finite-energy background conditions and robustness should be checked rather than inferred from any solitary-looking peak.

= Solution
{parent=b}

A detector too slow to resolve a femtosecond field can measure its integrated nonlinear response instead. Split the pulse, delay one replica, overlap both in a thin phase-matched crystal, and select the sum-frequency signal. For negligible depletion and sufficiently broad <phase matching>, the cross-generated field is proportional to $A(t)A(t-\tau)$; integrated signal gives the <second-harmonic intensity autocorrelation>
$$
C(\tau)\propto\int|A(t)A(t-\tau)|^2\,dt=\int I(t)I(t-\tau)\,dt.
$$
A noncollinear geometry can reject each replica's individual second harmonic. The optical response supplies the fast gate while the detector integrates slowly. Crystal thickness, <group-velocity mismatch>, spectral acceptance, beam overlap and delay calibration limit resolution.

For a <Gaussian pulse> specified by its <intensity> profile, the autocorrelation's <full width at half maximum> is $\sqrt2$ times the <intensity> <full width at half maximum>. For a sech-squared <intensity> profile the ratio is about $1.543$. These are model-dependent conversion factors. An <intensity autocorrelation> is symmetric in delay and loses phase information; different pulses can share it. An <interferometric autocorrelation> retains rapid carrier fringes but still does not generally reconstruct an arbitrary complex field uniquely.

<frequency-resolved optical gating> also resolves the gated signal's spectrum, giving a two-dimensional delay-frequency record. For <second-harmonic frequency-resolved optical gating>,
$$
I_{\rm FROG}(\omega,\tau)\propto
\left|\int A(t)A(t-\tau)e^{-i\omega t}\,dt\right|^2,
$$
where $\omega$ is measured relative to the signal's carrier. An iterative retrieval seeks an envelope whose predicted record matches the measurement. Alternatively, <polarization-gating frequency-resolved optical gating> uses the <optical Kerr effect> to gate the probe by a delayed replica's <intensity>, with signal envelope proportional to $A(t)|A(t-\tau)|^2$. The original <FROG (optics)> work introduced nonlinear optical gating plus spectral resolution to characterize arbitrary femtosecond pulses.<Characterization of arbitrary femtosecond pulses using frequency-resolved optical gating by Daniel J. Kane and Rick Trebino>[1993, abstract: nonlinear gating and spectrogram construction]{ref}

Absolute <wave phase> and absolute time origin remain undetermined by these <intensity> records. The SHG record also cannot distinguish $A(t)$ from $A^*(-t)$, so its time-direction ambiguity remains. Retrieval agreement, an independently measured spectrum, adequate signal dynamic range and gate-response calibration are practical consistency checks. A pulse train that changes between shots can yield an averaged record that no single pulse faithfully represents. Thus nonlinear gating solves the detector-speed problem but does not remove <inverse problem> ambiguities or experimental limitations.

= Solution
{parent=c}

For $X=(a+a^\dagger)/2$ and $Y=(a-a^\dagger)/(2i)$, $[X,Y]=i/2$, so $\Delta X\Delta Y\geq1/4$. Vacuum and <coherent states> have <variance> $1/4$ in either <optical quadrature>. <quadrature squeezing> means that some rotated <optical quadrature> has <variance> below this reference, with compensating noise elsewhere. A reduced second moment about zero is not the correct test when the field's mean is displaced.

For $S(\zeta)=\exp[(\zeta^*a^2-\zeta a^{\dagger2})/2]$ and $\zeta=re^{i\theta}$, the quiet principal axis of the <optical squeezed vacuum> is at angle $\theta/2$ under the squeeze-operator convention used above, and
$$
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}X_{\theta/2}=\tfrac14e^{-2r},\qquad
\operatorname{Var}X_{\theta/2+\pi/2}=\tfrac14e^{2r}.}
$$
The pure <Gaussian quantum state> saturates the uncertainty product. Displacement moves its centre without changing its <covariance ellipse>; <squeezed thermal states> instead have a larger uncertainty product. The <optical squeezed vacuum> has zero mean field, even <photon-number parity> and mean occupation $\sinh^2r$; it need not have sub-Poissonian <photon statistics>. <number squeezing> and <quadrature squeezing> are different conditions.

Degenerate parametric amplification generates single-mode squeezing; a <two-mode squeezed vacuum> instead has correlated occupations and joint <quadrature squeezing>. Either individual mode of an ideal two-mode state is thermal after the other is ignored, not individually squeezed. <balanced homodyne detection> measures the noise versus <local oscillator> phase. Reference phase drift admixes the anti-squeezed <optical quadrature>, while electronic noise and detector imbalance need separate checks.

For an optical loss channel with efficiency $\eta$ and vacuum entering the unused port, <optical squeezing degradation by loss> gives
$$
\boxed{V_{\rm out}=\eta V_{\rm in}+(1-\eta)/4.}
$$
The linear-loss limit follows directly by writing the output field as $\sqrt\eta\,a+\sqrt{1-\eta}\,v$ with independent vacuum mode $v$. Loss therefore places a floor under measured squeezing. Squeezed input can improve phase-sensitive measurements when aligned with the measured <optical quadrature>; it does not reduce every noise source or both conjugate uncertainties at once.
