== 1

=== a
{scope}

==== Solution

Use the <continuous> convention $0\log_2(0/q)=0$. Let $S=\{r:p_r>0\}$. Applying the allowed <logarithm> <inequality> to $q_r/p_r$ for $r\in S$ gives
$$
-d(p,q)=\sum_{r\in S}p_r\log_2\frac{q_r}{p_r}
\leq c\sum_{r\in S}(q_r-p_r)=c\left(\sum_{r\in S}q_r-1\right)\leq0.
$$
Hence $d(p,q)\geq0$. <Equality> forces $\sum_{r\in S}q_r=1$. Since every $q_r$ is strictly positive, this makes $S$ the entire <alphabet>. <Equality> in every weighted <logarithm> <inequality> then forces $q_r/p_r=1$ for every $r$. Conversely $p=q$ makes every summand zero. Thus
$$
\boxed{d(p,q)\geq0,\qquad d(p,q)=0\iff p=q.}
$$
This is <Gibbs inequality> for <relative entropy>, with zero <probabilities> in the first argument handled explicitly.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

For the <uniform distribution> $u_r=1/n$, direct expansion gives
$$
d(p_X,u)=\sum_rp_X(r)\log_2p_X(r)+\log_2n=\log_2n-H(X).
$$
Part (a) therefore proves
$$
\boxed{H(X)\leq\log_2n,}
$$
with <equality> exactly for $p_X(r)=1/n$ for every $r$. Thus the <uniform distribution> is the unique <Shannon entropy>[entropy] maximizer.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

Normalize the candidate distribution by setting
$$
q_r=c2^{-r},\qquad c=\frac1{1-2^{-n}}=\frac{2^n}{2^n-1}.
$$
The supplied <finite sum> gives its <mean>
$$
\sum_{r=1}^nrq_r=c\left(2-(n+2)2^{-n}\right)=\frac{2^{n+1}-(n+2)}{2^n-1}=m.
$$
Thus it meets the <constraint>. Since $\log_2q_r=\log_2c-r$, every other distribution $p$ with <mean> $m$ satisfies
$$
d(p,q)=-H(p)-\log_2c+m=H(q)-H(p).
$$
Nonnegativity and the <equality> case in part (a) prove
$$
\boxed{p_Y(r)=\frac{2^n}{2^n-1}\,2^{-r},\qquad H(Y)_{\max}=m-\log_2\frac{2^n}{2^n-1}.}
$$
The <maximum> is unique; the <Shannon entropy>[entropy] comparison uses the fixed <mean> rather than an unconstrained optimization.

==== iii
{scope}

===== Solution

The given logarithmic moment is
$$
\ell=\frac2{n(n+1)}\sum_{r=1}^nr\log_2r,
$$
where the $r=1$ term is zero. It is attained by the positive distribution
$$
q_r=\frac{2r}{n(n+1)},
$$
whose <probabilities> <sum> to one. Write $A=2/[n(n+1)]$, so $\log_2q_r=\log_2A+\log_2r$. For any distribution $p$ with the required logarithmic moment,
$$
d(p,q)=-H(p)-\log_2A-\ell=H(q)-H(p).
$$
Consequently the unique maximizer and its <Shannon entropy>[entropy] are
$$
\boxed{p_Z(r)=\frac{2r}{n(n+1)},\qquad H(Z)_{\max}=\log_2\frac{n(n+1)}2-\ell.}
$$
The increasing weights are consistent with the prescribed moment; they need not be a decreasing <power law>.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Write $w_i=(Aq)_i$. For each row put $S_i=\{j:a_{ij}>0\}$; the positive row entries $\alpha_i=(a_{ij})_{j\in S_i}$ form a <probability distribution> because the row <sum> is one. When $w_i>0$, define the <probability distribution>
$$
r_i(j)=\frac{a_{ij}q_j}{w_i}\qquad(j\in S_i).
$$
Part (a), applied on this row <support>, gives $d(r_i,\alpha_i)\geq0$. Its weighted <sum> expands as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{i:w_i>0}w_i d(r_i,\alpha_i)
&=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}q_j\log_2\frac{q_j}{w_i}\\
&=\sum_jq_j\log_2q_j\sum_i a_{ij}-\sum_iw_i\log_2w_i\\
&=H(w)-H(q).
\end{aligned}
$$
Rows with $w_i=0$ contribute zero, since all $a_{ij}q_j$ in such a row vanish. Using the convention that zero-weight logarithmic terms are zero, the result is
$$
\boxed{H(W)-H(V)=\sum_{i:w_i>0}w_i d(r_i,\alpha_i)\geq0.}
$$
Both stochasticity conditions have a role: row sums normalize the comparison distributions, while column sums preserve the total contribution from $q$.

This is <entropy increase under doubly stochastic mixing>.

=== a
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

A binary code assigns a finite binary word $c_k$ to each source symbol $x_k$. In the ordinary unframed convention these <codewords> are nonempty. It is <uniquely decodable code>[uniquely decodable] when <concatenation> gives an <injective> map from finite symbol strings to <bit> strings:
$$
\boxed{c_{i_1}\cdots c_{i_r}=c_{j_1}\cdots c_{j_s}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=s\text{ and }i_t=j_t\text{ for all }t.}
$$
The decoder can recover the entire symbol sequence without being told where <codewords> end. This is stronger than merely assigning distinct words to individual symbols.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

An <prefix code>[instantaneous code] can decode each symbol as soon as its final <bit> arrives, without waiting for later <bits>. Equivalently it is <prefix code>[prefix-free]: no <codeword> is a proper prefix of another, and different symbols receive different <codewords>.

For nonempty <codewords>, decoding greedily from the start of the <bit> string identifies the first <codeword> unambiguously; repeating identifies the whole sequence. Thus **an instantaneous code is uniquely decodable** under the ordinary nonempty-word convention.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

The <codeword> lengths are <integers>. For necessity, take $L=\max_k l_k$ and view <codewords> as <nodes> of the <binary tree>. A <codeword> of length $l_k$ has $2^{L-l_k}$ descendant words at depth $L$. <Prefix code>[Prefix-freeness] makes these descendant <sets> disjoint, so
$$
\sum_k2^{L-l_k}\leq2^L,\qquad\text{hence}\quad\sum_k2^{-l_k}\leq1.
$$
For sufficiency, reorder the lengths nondecreasingly. Assign words successively. At depth $l_k$, words extending previously assigned <codewords> number
$$
\sum_{i<k}2^{l_k-l_i}.
$$
The asserted <inequality>, even after dropping the later nonnegative terms, gives
$$
\sum_{i<k}2^{l_k-l_i}+1\leq2^{l_k}.
$$
Thus at least one depth-$l_k$ word does not extend an earlier word. Choose it. Since lengths are nondecreasing, it cannot be a proper prefix of a shorter earlier word, and equal-length duplicates have been excluded. <Induction> constructs the required code. Therefore <Kraft inequality> is necessary and sufficient:
$$
\boxed{\text{a binary prefix code with these lengths exists}\iff\sum_k2^{-l_k}\leq1.}
$$

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

First consider any instantaneous code. Put $K_0=\sum_k2^{-l_k}\leq1$ and $q_k=2^{-l_k}/K_0$. Part 1(a) gives
$$
0\leq d(p,q)=-H(X)+\mathbb EL+\log_2K_0,
$$
so $\mathbb EL\geq H(X)-\log_2K_0\geq H(X)$.

For the construction, take the Shannon lengths $l_k=\lceil-\log_2p_k\rceil$. Then $2^{-l_k}\leq p_k$, so Kraft's condition holds. Also $l_k<-\log_2p_k+1$ for every $k$. Averaging gives
$$
\boxed{H(X)\leq\mathbb EL<H(X)+1.}
$$
For a nontrivial <alphabet> all $p_k<1$, so these lengths are positive and the ordinary code convention applies. For a deterministic one-symbol source, the usual zero-bit convention uses $l_1=0$ when the number of source symbols is externally known. If strictly nonempty <codewords> and arbitrary unframed message lengths are required instead, that degenerate case has $\mathbb EL\geq1$ and cannot satisfy the printed strict upper bound. This convention issue does not affect sources with at least two symbols.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

Let $F_k=\sum_{r<k}p_r$ and let $J_k=[F_k,F_k+p_k)$ be the <probability> cell of symbol $k$. Its <midpoint> is $G_k=F_k+p_k/2$. Use the <binary expansion> terminating in zeros at a <dyadic rational>, so truncation to $l_k$ <bits> gives
$$
a_k=2^{-l_k}\lfloor2^{l_k}G_k\rfloor.
$$
The chosen <codeword> corresponds to the <dyadic interval> $D_k=[a_k,a_k+2^{-l_k})$. It contains $G_k$, and its width satisfies $2^{-l_k}\leq p_k/2$. Consequently
$$
a_k>G_k-2^{-l_k}\geq F_k,\qquad a_k+2^{-l_k}\leq G_k+2^{-l_k}\leq F_k+p_k.
$$
Thus $D_k\subseteq J_k$. The <probability> cells are disjoint, so the code <intervals> are disjoint. If one binary <codeword> were a prefix of another, its <dyadic interval> would contain the other's <interval>, contradicting this disjointness. Hence the code is instantaneous.

Finally, $l_k=\lceil-\log_2p_k\rceil+1<-\log_2p_k+2$. Multiplying by $p_k$ and summing yields
$$
\boxed{\mathbb EL<H(X)+2.}
$$
This is the <Shannon-Fano-Elias code>: the extra <bit> makes the <dyadic interval> narrow enough to remain inside the cell around its <midpoint>.

=== a

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

For finite-valued <random variables>, their <mutual information> is
$$
\boxed{I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)=H(Y)-H(Y\mid X)=H(X)-H(X\mid Y).}
$$
Equivalently,
$$
I(X;Y)=\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log_2\frac{p(x,y)}{p_X(x)p_Y(y)},
$$
with zero joint-probability terms omitted. It is the <relative entropy> of the joint law from the product of its marginals and is nonnegative, with <equality> exactly when the two variables are <independent>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

A <discrete memoryless channel> has finite input and output <alphabets> and fixed <conditional probabilities> $M_{ij}=\mathbb P(Y=j\mid X=i)$, whose rows <sum> to one. Memorylessness means that for successive uses, conditional on the input sequence, the output <probabilities> factor as the product of these one-use <conditional probabilities>.

Its information <Channel capacity>[capacity] in <bits> per use is
$$
\boxed{C=\max_{p_X}I(X;Y)=\max_{p_X}\left[H(Y)-\sum_i p_X(i)H(M_{i\cdot})\right].}
$$
The maximization varies the input distribution while keeping the <Communication channel>[channel] <matrix> fixed. The output distribution is $p_XM$ in the row-vector convention.

=== b
{scope}

==== i
{scope}

===== Solution

Let $B$ be the selected block, and write $\alpha=\mathbb P(B=1)$. Because both the input and output <alphabets> are disjoint across blocks, $B$ is determined by either the input or the output. The mutual-information chain rule therefore gives
$$
I(X;Y)=H(B)+I(X;Y\mid B)\leq h_2(\alpha)+\alpha C_1+(1-\alpha)C_2.
$$
For any fixed $\alpha$, <equality> is achievable by using a capacity-achieving distribution inside each block. It remains to maximize this binary expression.

Put $S=2^{C_1}+2^{C_2}$ and $r=2^{C_1}/S$. A direct expansion of <binary relative entropy> yields
$$
h_2(\alpha)+\alpha C_1+(1-\alpha)C_2
=\log_2S-d\bigl((\alpha,1-\alpha),(r,1-r)\bigr).
$$
Part 1(a) makes the <maximum> $\log_2S$, uniquely at $\alpha=r$. Hence
$$
\boxed{C=\log_2(2^{C_1}+2^{C_2}),\qquad\mathbb P(B=i)=\frac{2^{C_i}}{2^{C_1}+2^{C_2}}.}
$$
The block choice itself communicates information, in addition to any information sent within the selected block.

This is <capacity of a disjoint sum of channels>.

==== ii
{scope}

===== Solution

There are three distinguishable output groups: columns $\{1,2\}$ can arise only from input one; column $\{3\}$ can arise from inputs two or three, whose rows are identical; and columns $\{4,5\}$ can arise only from input four.

Each group has zero internal <channel capacity>. A block with one input cannot carry a variable input choice, and the middle block has identical output laws for both of its inputs. Combining the three disjoint zero-capacity blocks therefore gives
$$
\boxed{C_N=\log_2(1+1+1)=\log_2 3\text{ bits per use}.}
$$
Equivalently, the output identifies exactly which of these three groups was used, so <mutual information> is the <Shannon entropy>[entropy] of that group label. It is maximized when all group <probabilities> are one third. The capacity-achieving input distributions are precisely
$$
p_1=p_4=\frac13,\qquad p_2+p_3=\frac13.
$$
Splitting the middle <probability> arbitrarily cannot communicate an extra distinction because its two <Communication channel>[channel] rows agree.

=== c
{scope}

==== Solution

For any chosen input distribution, the chain rule and nonnegativity of <conditional mutual information> give
$$
I(U;V,W)=I(U;V)+I(U;W\mid V)\geq I(U;V),
$$
and likewise $I(U;V,W)\geq I(U;W)$. Choosing an input distribution attaining either marginal <Channel capacity>[capacity] proves $K\geq\max\{K_1,K_2\}$.

For the upper bound, <conditional independence> implies $H(V,W\mid U)=H(V\mid U)+H(W\mid U)$, while <Shannon entropy>[entropy] subadditivity gives $H(V,W)\leq H(V)+H(W)$. Therefore
$$
\begin{aligned}
I(U;V,W)&=H(V,W)-H(V,W\mid U)\\
&\leq H(V)+H(W)-H(V\mid U)-H(W\mid U)\\
&=I(U;V)+I(U;W)\leq K_1+K_2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Maximizing over the input law gives
$$
\boxed{\max\{K_1,K_2\}\leq K\leq K_1+K_2.}
$$
More precisely, under the conditional-independence assumption,
$$
I(U;V,W)=I(U;V)+I(U;W)-I(V;W).
$$
The outputs may be dependent through the common input, and this final term measures the information they share rather than add independently.

This is <information from two conditionally independent observations>.
