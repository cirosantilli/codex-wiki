= Solution
{parent=2010/1/a}

A binary code assigns a finite bit string to each source symbol. Use nonempty codewords for an unframed variable-length stream. The code is <uniquely decodable code>[uniquely decodable] if no two distinct finite sequences of source symbols produce the same concatenated bit string. It is a <prefix code>[prefix-free code] if no <codeword> is an initial segment of a different <codeword>.

To prove unique decoding of a <prefix code>, suppose two concatenations agree. Their first codewords both begin the same bit string, so the shorter is a prefix of the longer. The prefix condition forces the first codewords to be equal. Cancel them and repeat. Since the codewords are nonempty, this process ends with both symbol sequences exhausted at the same time; otherwise a nonempty remainder could not equal the empty string. Hence the source sequences agree.

The code $\{0,01\}$ is not a <prefix code>, since $0$ is a prefix of $01$. Nevertheless it is <uniquely decodable code>[uniquely decodable]: decode from the right. A final $1$ forces the last <codeword> to be $01$, whereas a final $0$ forces it to be $0$. Removing that final word recursively gives a unique parsing. Thus **the converse fails**.

= Solution
{parent=2010/1/b}

Put $S=\sum_{k=1}^a2^{-l_k}$ and let $l_{\max}$ be the largest length. Expanding $S^n$ sums $2^{-L}$ over all strings of $n$ source symbols, where $L$ is the total code length. By unique decodability, the concatenations of length $r$ in this <sum> are all distinct, so there are at most $2^r$ of them. Since $n\leq r\leq nl_{\max}$,
$$
S^n\leq\sum_{r=n}^{nl_{\max}}2^r2^{-r}\leq nl_{\max}.
$$
Taking $n$th roots and sending $n$ to infinity proves the <Kraft–McMillan inequality>
$$
\boxed{\sum_{k=1}^a2^{-l_k}\leq1.}
$$
Now $q_k=2^{-l_k}/S$ is a <probability distribution>. With base-two logarithms, <Gibbs inequality> gives
$$
0\leq D(p\Vert q)=\sum_kp_k\log_2\frac{p_k}{q_k}
=-H(X)+\mathbb EL+\log_2 S.
$$
Terms with $p_k=0$ contribute zero. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\mathbb EL-H(X)=D(p\Vert q)-\log_2S\geq0.}
$$
The normalization by $S$ is important: the raw numbers $2^{-l_k}$ need not <sum> to one.

= Solution
{parent=2010/1/c}

For the nontrivial case $a\geq2$, every $p_r<1$ and
$$
l_r=\lceil-\log_2p_r\rceil\geq1.
$$
The <probability> ordering makes $l_1\leq\cdots\leq l_a$. At a dyadic number, choose the terminating <binary expansion> followed by zeros. Let $c_r$ be the first $l_r$ bits of $t_r$, and let
$$
J_r=\left[\frac{\lfloor2^{l_r}t_r\rfloor}{2^{l_r}},
\frac{\lfloor2^{l_r}t_r\rfloor+1}{2^{l_r}}\right)
$$
be its binary cylinder interval.

Suppose $r<s$ and $c_r$ were a prefix of $c_s$. Since $l_r\leq l_s$, the numbers $t_r$ and $t_s$ would have the same first $l_r$ bits, so both would lie in $J_r$. In particular $t_s-t_r<2^{-l_r}$. But
$$
t_s-t_r=\sum_{i=r}^{s-1}p_i\geq p_r\geq2^{-l_r},
$$
a contradiction. A later word cannot be a shorter prefix of an earlier word because the lengths are nondecreasing; equal-length coincidences were also excluded. Thus the <cumulative Shannon code> is a <prefix code>.

The ceiling inequalities give
$$
-\log_2p_r\leq l_r<-\log_2p_r+1.
$$
Multiply by $p_r$ and <sum> to obtain
$$
\boxed{H(X)\leq\mathbb EL<H(X)+1.}
$$
For a one-symbol deterministic source the displayed construction assigns the empty word and gives $H=\mathbb EL=0$. This zero-bit convention is valid when the number of source emissions is supplied separately. It is not an unframed <uniquely decodable code>: concatenating different numbers of empty words gives the same bit string. If positive-length unframed words are required, the shortest deterministic code has length $1$, so the strict upper bound $\mathbb EL<H+1$ requires the nontrivial-source assumption.

= Solution
{parent=2010/2/a/i}

For finite-valued random vectors, write $p(x,y)=P(X=x,Y=y)$ and $p_Y(y)=P(Y=y)$. The <conditional entropy> is the average <Shannon entropy>[entropy] of the posterior distributions:
$$
H(X\mid Y)=\sum_{y:p_Y(y)>0}p_Y(y)
\left[-\sum_xp(x\mid y)\log_2p(x\mid y)\right].
$$
Equivalently, $H(X\mid Y)=-\sum_{x,y:p(x,y)>0}p(x,y)\log_2p(x\mid y)$. Since $p(x,y)=p_Y(y)p(x\mid y)$, splitting the <logarithm> gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
H(X,Y)&=-\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log_2p_Y(y)
-\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log_2p(x\mid y)\\
&=\boxed{H(Y)+H(X\mid Y)}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Use the usual convention $0\log_20=0$ and omit conditionals at zero-<probability> outputs. The same identities hold for countable alphabets whenever the <Shannon entropy>[entropy] sums are well-defined, without subtracting two infinite quantities.

= Solution
{parent=2010/2/a/ii}

Set $Z=g(X)$. Given $X$, the value of $Z$ is certain, so $H(Z\mid X)=0$. Applying the <Shannon entropy>[entropy] identity in the two orders gives
$$
H(X)=H(X,Z)=H(Z)+H(X\mid Z)\geq H(Z),
$$
because <conditional entropy> is nonnegative. Consequently
$$
\boxed{H(g(X))\leq H(X).}
$$
A deterministic coarsening can merge distinguishable source outcomes, but cannot create additional <Shannon entropy>.

Choose a most probable symbol $x_*$, and let $B=\mathbf1_{\{X=x_*\}}$. Its <binary entropy> is
$$
H(B)=h_2(\Pi)=-\Pi\log_2\Pi-(1-\Pi)\log_2(1-\Pi).
$$
Since $B$ is a function of $X$, part (a)(ii) gives $H(X)\geq h_2(\Pi)$. On $[1/2,1]$, the concave function $h_2$ lies above the chord joining $(1/2,1)$ and $(1,0)$. That chord has height $2(1-\Pi)$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{H(X)\geq h_2(\Pi)\geq2(1-\Pi).}
$$
The endpoint $\Pi=1$ is included by <continuity> and corresponds to a deterministic source.

Every positive atom has <probability> at most $\Pi$, so $-\log_2p(x)\geq-\log_2\Pi$. Averaging yields
$$
H(X)=\sum_xp(x)(-\log_2p(x))\geq-\log_2\Pi.
$$
For $0<\Pi\leq1/2$, the supplied inequality $4\Pi\leq4^\Pi$ gives $2+\log_2\Pi\leq2\Pi$. Rearranging proves
$$
\boxed{H(X)\geq-\log_2\Pi\geq2(1-\Pi).}
$$
The two regimes combine into the <entropy lower bound from the largest atom>, valid for every finite <probability distribution>.

= Solution
{parent=2010/2/c}

Given an output $y$ of positive <probability>, the ideal observer chooses a symbol maximizing the posterior:
$$
\boxed{g(y)\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{x\in A}P(X=x\mid Y=y).}
$$
Ties may be broken arbitrarily, and the choice at an impossible output does not matter. This is <maximum a posteriori decoding>. It minimizes conditional error at each $y$, because a deterministic decision $x$ is correct with <probability> $P(X=x\mid Y=y)$; a randomized decision is a weighted average of these success probabilities and cannot exceed their maximum.

Write $\Pi_y=\max_xP(X=x\mid Y=y)$. Then $w(y)=1-\Pi_y$. Applying the two cases from part (b) to the posterior distribution gives
$$
H(X\mid Y=y)\geq2(1-\Pi_y)=2w(y).
$$
Average with respect to the output law to obtain the <conditional entropy upper bound on minimum decoding error>:
$$
\boxed{\mathbb Ew(Y)\leq\frac12H(X\mid Y).}
$$
This is an upper bound on the optimum error <probability>; it does not hold for arbitrary deliberately poor decoding rules.

= Solution
{parent=2010/3/a}

For a finite joint distribution, the <mutual information> is
$$
\boxed{I(X;Y)=\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log_2\frac{p(x,y)}{p_X(x)p_Y(y)}
=H(Y)-H(Y\mid X)=H(X)-H(X\mid Y).}
$$
Zero-<probability> terms contribute zero. It measures the reduction in uncertainty about one variable upon observing the other.

A <discrete memoryless channel> has discrete input/output alphabets and a fixed <transition matrix> $W(y\mid x)$. Over successive uses it obeys
$$
P(Y_1=y_1,\ldots,Y_n=y_n\mid X_1=x_1,\ldots,X_n=x_n)
=\prod_{j=1}^nW(y_j\mid x_j).
$$
Thus the output at a use depends on that input and no other channel history. Its <channel capacity> is
$$
\boxed{C=\max_{p_X}I(X;Y),}
$$
in bits per use for base-two logarithms. On finite alphabets the maximum exists by <continuity> on the <probability simplex>; for more general discrete alphabets one uses a supremum.

= Solution
{parent=2010/3/b}

Fix the channel matrix $W$. The mixed input induces the mixed output $q_3=\lambda q_1+(1-\lambda)q_2$. The <concavity of information entropy> implies
$$
H(q_3)\geq\lambda H(q_1)+(1-\lambda)H(q_2).
$$
For completeness, the difference is $\lambda D(q_1\Vert q_3)+(1-\lambda)D(q_2\Vert q_3)\geq0$ by <Gibbs inequality>, including zero coordinates with the usual conventions.

On the other hand, conditional output <Shannon entropy>[entropy] is linear in the input law:
$$
H(Y_3\mid X_3)=\sum_xp_3(x)H(W(\cdot\mid x))
=\lambda H(Y_1\mid X_1)+(1-\lambda)H(Y_2\mid X_2).
$$
Subtracting gives the <concavity of mutual information in the input distribution>:
$$
\boxed{I(X_3;Y_3)\geq\lambda I(X_1;Y_1)+(1-\lambda)I(X_2;Y_2).}
$$
This makes maximizing <mutual information> over the input <probability simplex> a concave optimization problem: a local maximum is global. It also justifies averaging input distributions over channel symmetries when searching for an optimum, since symmetry averaging cannot lower the achieved <mutual information>. The channel itself is held fixed throughout.

= Solution
{parent=2010/3/c/i}

Let the input probabilities be $(s,t,u)$ and put $v=s+u=1-t$. The output distribution and <conditional entropy> are
$$
q=\left(\frac s2,1-\frac v2,\frac u2\right),\qquad H(Y\mid X)=s+u=v.
$$
For fixed $v$, the first and third output masses <sum> to $v/2$. Their <Shannon entropy>[entropy] is maximized when they are equal, so $s=u=v/2$. Equivalently, if $v>0$,
$$
H(Y)=h_2(v/2)+(v/2)h_2(s/v)\leq h_2(v/2)+v/2.
$$
The same bound holds at $v=0$ by <continuity>. Therefore $I(X;Y)\leq h_2(v/2)-v/2$, with equality for symmetric outer input probabilities. Writing $z=v/2\in[0,1/2]$, differentiate:
$$
\frac d{dz}(h_2(z)-z)=\log_2\frac{1-z}z-1.
$$
The unique zero is $z=1/3$; strict <concavity> makes it the global maximum. Thus $v=2/3$ and
$$
\boxed{p_X=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad C=h_2(1/3)-1/3=\log_2(3/2).}
$$
The optimum output is $(1/6,2/3,1/6)$.

= Solution
{parent=2010/3/c/ii}

Every row has <Shannon entropy> $1$, so $H(Y\mid X)=1$ for every input law. Since the output alphabet has three symbols, <Gibbs inequality> gives $H(Y)\leq\log_23$. The uniform input gives
$$
q=\left(\frac{p_1+p_2}2,\frac{p_1+p_3}2,\frac{p_2+p_3}2\right)=(1/3,1/3,1/3),
$$
so it attains the upper bound. Therefore
$$
\boxed{p_X=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad C=\log_23-1=\log_2(3/2).}
$$
Uniform output is necessary for equality in the <Shannon entropy>[entropy] bound, and the three displayed equations then force the uniform input, so this optimum input law is unique.

= Solution
{parent=2010/3/c/iii}

Let the input law again be $(s,t,u)$. The second output symbol has <probability> $1/2$ regardless of the input, and the two remaining output probabilities <sum> to $1/2$. Hence
$$
H(Y)\leq h_2(1/2)+\frac12\cdot1=\frac32,
$$
with equality when the first and third output symbols each have <probability> $1/4$. The row entropies are $1,3/2,1$, so
$$
H(Y\mid X)=1+\frac t2,
\qquad I(X;Y)\leq\frac12-\frac t2\leq\frac12.
$$
Take $t=0$ and $s=u=1/2$. The output is $(1/4,1/2,1/4)$ and both bounds are attained. Therefore
$$
\boxed{p_X=(1/2,0,1/2),\qquad C=1/2\text{ bit per use}.}
$$
The equality conditions also show uniqueness of the optimum input law. The middle row is the average of the outer rows and carries extra <conditional entropy> without introducing a new output law; <removing a redundant input symbol from a channel> explains why it need not be used.
