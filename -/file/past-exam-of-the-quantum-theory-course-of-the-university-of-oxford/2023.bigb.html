= Solution

The <free particle> has <Hamiltonian operator> $H=P^2/(2m)$, with <momentum operator> $P=-i\hbar\partial_x$. Its time-dependent <Schrödinger equation> is
$$
\boxed{i\hbar\frac{\partial\Psi}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\Psi}{\partial x^2}},\qquad
\Psi(t,x+2\pi R)=\Psi(t,x).
$$
Here $x$ measures arc length for the <particle on a circle>, so the <wavefunction normalization> uses $dx$, rather than $d(x/R)$.

= ii
{parent=a}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}

A <stationary state> has $\Psi(t,x)=e^{-iEt/\hbar}\psi(x)$. Substitution into the <Schrödinger equation> gives the <energy eigenvalue> equation
$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi''=E\psi.
$$
The trial <wavefunction> $Ce^{ikx}$ satisfies it with $E=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$. The <periodic boundary condition> requires $e^{2\pi ikR}=1$, hence $k=n/R$, $n\in\mathbb Z$. The <wavefunction normalization> is $1=2\pi R|C|^2$, so a convenient common <global phase> gives the <orthonormal basis>
$$
\boxed{\phi_n(x)=\frac{e^{inx/R}}{\sqrt{2\pi R}},\qquad E_n=\frac{\hbar^2n^2}{2mR^2},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.}
$$
Indeed, $\int_0^{2\pi R}\phi_n^*\phi_q\,dx=\delta_{nq}$. This is the <free particle spectrum on a circle>. The <ground state> $\phi_0$ has <energy> zero and is nondegenerate. Each positive <energy level> $\hbar^2n^2/(2mR^2)$, $n=1,2,\ldots$, has <energy eigenspace> $\operatorname{span}\{\phi_n,\phi_{-n}\}$ and <quantum degeneracy> two. Real sine and cosine combinations give another basis of the same <energy eigenspace>.

Using $\sin^2u=\tfrac12-\tfrac14e^{2iu}-\tfrac14e^{-2iu}$, the <energy eigenstate> decomposition is
$$
\boxed{\Psi(0,x)=\mathcal N\sqrt{2\pi R}\left(\frac12\phi_0(x)-\frac14\phi_2(x)-\frac14\phi_{-2}(x)\right).}
$$
Only the <energy eigenvalues> zero and $E_\star=2\hbar^2/(mR^2)$ occur. The last two terms are distinct <momentum eigenstates> in the same <energy eigenspace>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

The <orthonormal basis> makes the squared <norm> equal to the sum of squared expansion coefficients:
$$
1=|\mathcal N|^2(2\pi R)\left(\frac14+\frac1{16}+\frac1{16}\right)
=|\mathcal N|^2\frac{3\pi R}{4}.
$$
Choose a positive real <wavefunction normalization> constant, fixing an irrelevant <global phase>:
$$
\boxed{\mathcal N=\frac2{\sqrt{3\pi R}},\qquad
\Psi(0)=\sqrt{\frac23}\,\phi_0-\frac1{\sqrt6}(\phi_2+\phi_{-2}).}
$$
As a direct check, $\int_0^{2\pi R}\sin^4(x/R)\,dx=3\pi R/4$.

{parent=b}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=iii}

Under <unitary time evolution>, each <energy eigenstate> acquires its own <dynamical phase>. Since $E_2=E_{-2}=E_\star$, the <wavefunction> is
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Psi(t,x)&=\sqrt{\frac23}\,\phi_0(x)-\frac{e^{-iE_\star t/\hbar}}{\sqrt6}\bigl(\phi_2(x)+\phi_{-2}(x)\bigr)\\
&=\frac{1-e^{-i\Omega t}\cos(2x/R)}{\sqrt{3\pi R}},\qquad
\Omega=\frac{E_\star}{\hbar}=\frac{2\hbar}{mR^2}.
\end{aligned}}
$$
At $t=0$, the identity $1-\cos(2x/R)=2\sin^2(x/R)$ recovers the normalized initial <wavefunction>.

= Solution

By the <Born rule>, the <probability> for a measured <energy eigenvalue> is the squared <norm> of the <orthogonal projection> onto its whole <energy eigenspace>. Thus
$$
\boxed{\Pr(E=0)=\frac23,\qquad
\Pr(E=E_\star)=\frac16+\frac16=\frac13.}
$$
Every other <energy eigenvalue> has <probability> zero. These <probabilities> are independent of $T$: <unitary time evolution> under the unchanged <Hamiltonian operator> changes the <dynamical phases> but not the squared expansion coefficients. The two equal-energy terms describe one measurement outcome.

= Solution

Let $\Pi_\star=|\phi_2\rangle\langle\phi_2|+|\phi_{-2}\rangle\langle\phi_{-2}|$ be the <orthogonal projection> for $E_\star$. The <Lüders rule> gives the normalized <post-measurement state>
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Psi(T^+,x)&=\frac{\Pi_\star\Psi(T^-,x)}{\sqrt{1/3}}\\
&=-\frac{e^{-iE_\star T/\hbar}}{\sqrt2}\bigl(\phi_2(x)+\phi_{-2}(x)\bigr)
=-\frac{e^{-iE_\star T/\hbar}}{\sqrt{\pi R}}\cos\!\left(\frac{2x}{R}\right).
\end{aligned}}
$$
The factor $-e^{-iE_\star T/\hbar}$ is a <global phase>, so $\cos(2x/R)/\sqrt{\pi R}$ is an equivalent representative. The <projective measurement> preserves the <quantum coherence> between the two <momentum eigenstates>; it does not select one of them. This matters for the subsequent position <probabilities>.

= Solution

Put $\tau=t-T$ and retain the <global phase> from the preceding <Lüders rule> update. Since $P\phi_n=(\hbar n/R)\phi_n$, the same <orthonormal basis> diagonalizes the new <Hamiltonian operator>, with
$$
H_{\mathrm{new}}=\frac{(P-ms)^2}{2m},\qquad
\widetilde E_n=\frac1{2m}\left(\frac{\hbar n}{R}-ms\right)^2.
$$
In particular, $\widetilde E_{\pm2}=E_\star+ms^2/2\mp2\hbar s/R$. Applying <unitary time evolution> from $T$ therefore gives
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Psi(t,x)&=-\frac{e^{-iE_\star T/\hbar}}{\sqrt2}
\left(e^{-i\widetilde E_2\tau/\hbar}\phi_2(x)+e^{-i\widetilde E_{-2}\tau/\hbar}\phi_{-2}(x)\right)\\
&=-\frac{e^{-iE_\star T/\hbar}e^{-i(E_\star+ms^2/2)\tau/\hbar}}{\sqrt{\pi R}}
\cos\!\left(\frac{2(x+s\tau)}R\right).
\end{aligned}}
$$
This is <rigid translation from a momentum term in the Hamiltonian>: the position <probability density> moves with velocity $-s$. The plus sign in $x+s\tau$ follows directly from the negative sign of $-sP$. At $\tau=0$ the expression agrees exactly with the preceding <post-measurement state>.

= Solution
{parent=ii}

For an arc represented by $0\leq x\leq a$, take $0\leq a\leq2\pi R$. The <Born rule> uses
$$
|\Psi(t,x)|^2=\frac1{\pi R}\cos^2\!\left(\frac{2(x+s\tau)}R\right),\qquad \tau=t-T.
$$
Integrate using $\cos^2u=(1+\cos2u)/2$:
$$
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
\Pr(0\leq x\leq a)&=\frac1{\pi R}\int_0^a\cos^2\!\left(\frac{2(x+s\tau)}R\right)dx\\
&=\frac{a}{2\pi R}+\frac1{8\pi}
\left[\sin\!\left(\frac{4(a+s\tau)}R\right)-\sin\!\left(\frac{4s\tau}R\right)\right].
\end{aligned}}
$$
The result is zero at $a=0$ and one at $a=2\pi R$, checking the <wavefunction normalization>. At $s=0$ it reduces to $a/(2\pi R)+\sin(4a/R)/(8\pi)$, the stationary position <probability>. Longer intervals on the periodically identified coordinate require specifying the corresponding subset of the circle; integrating multiple turns would count the same positions repeatedly.

The <Hamiltonian operator> separates into two commuting one-dimensional <quantum harmonic oscillators>. Write
$$
\xi_j=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_j}{\hbar}}x_j,\qquad
G=e^{-(\xi_1^2+\xi_2^2)/2},\qquad E_{00}=\frac{\hbar}{2}(\omega_1+\omega_2).
$$
Products of <harmonic oscillator eigenfunctions> give
$$
\psi_{n_1,n_2}\propto H_{n_1}(\xi_1)H_{n_2}(\xi_2)G,\qquad
E_{n_1,n_2}=E_{00}+\hbar(n_1\omega_1+n_2\omega_2).
$$
Here $H_n$ is the physicists' <Hermite polynomial>, so $H_0=1$, $H_1(u)=2u$ and $H_2(u)=4u^2-2$. The first six <energy eigenstates>, ordered with multiplicity, are
$$
\boxed{\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(n_1,n_2)&E_{n_1,n_2}&\psi_{n_1,n_2}\ \text{up to a nonzero constant}\\\hline
(0,0)&E_{00}&G\\
(1,0)&E_{00}+\hbar\omega_1&\xi_1G\\
(0,1)&E_{00}+\hbar\omega_2&\xi_2G\\
(2,0)&E_{00}+2\hbar\omega_1&(4\xi_1^2-2)G\\
(1,1)&E_{00}+\hbar(\omega_1+\omega_2)&\xi_1\xi_2G\\
(0,2)&E_{00}+2\hbar\omega_2&(4\xi_2^2-2)G
\end{array}}
$$
For this <two-dimensional oscillator level ordering>, the excitation <energies> obey
$$
0<\omega_1\leq\omega_2<2\omega_1\leq\omega_1+\omega_2\leq2\omega_2<3\omega_1.
$$
Every state with $n_1+n_2\geq3$ costs at least $3\hbar\omega_1$, so none has been missed. Equalities within the list occur when $\omega_2=\omega_1=\omega$. Then the <two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator> has
$$
\boxed{E_N=\hbar\omega(N+1),\qquad g_N=N+1,\qquad N=0,1,2,\ldots.}
$$
The <quantum degeneracy> counts $n_1=0,\ldots,N$ with $n_2=N-n_1$. The six listed states occupy three distinct <energy levels>, of <quantum degeneracies> one, two and three.

= Solution
{parent=b}

Since $X$ and $P$ are <self-adjoint operators>, the <adjoint operator> relation requires $c_+=c_-=c$ and $d_+=-d_-= -d$. Thus $a_-=cX+idP$ and $a_+=cX-idP$. The <canonical commutation relation> $[X,P]=i\hbar I$ gives
$$
[a_-,a_+]=2\hbar cd\,I,\qquad
a_+a_-=c^2X^2+d^2P^2-\hbar cd\,I.
$$
Matching the $X^2$, $P^2$ and <identity operator> coefficients in $H=\hbar\omega(a_+a_-+\lambda I)$ yields
$$
c^2=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar},\qquad
d^2=\frac1{2m\hbar\omega},\qquad cd=\frac1{2\hbar},\qquad\lambda=\frac12.
$$
Consequently all real solutions are
$$
\boxed{c_+=c_-=\varepsilon\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}},\qquad
d_-=-d_+=\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}},\qquad
\lambda=\frac12,\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.}
$$
The conventional <creation and annihilation operators> use $\varepsilon=1$. The other choice multiplies both by minus one and satisfies all the given equations. This harmless freedom is the <oscillator ladder-operator sign convention>; the subsequent formulas use the positive choice in both coordinates.

= Solution

Use the <canonical commutation relations>
$$
[X_j,P_k]=i\hbar\delta_{jk}I,\qquad [X_j,X_k]=[P_j,P_k]=0.
$$
Writing $c_j=\sqrt{m\omega_j/(2\hbar)}$, $d_j=1/\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega_j}$, direct expansion of the <commutator> gives
$$
[a_{j,-},a_{k,+}]=\hbar(c_jd_k+d_jc_k)\delta_{jk}I
=\boxed{\delta_{jk}I}.
$$
The <Fock vacuum> $\psi_{0,0}$ is annihilated by both $a_{j,-}$. Successive <creation operators> generate
$$
\psi_{n_1,n_2}\propto(a_{1,+})^{n_1}(a_{2,+})^{n_2}\psi_{0,0}.
$$
Hence, in the order of part (a), the six <energy eigenstates> are proportional to
$$
\boxed{\psi_{0,0},\quad a_{1,+}\psi_{0,0},\quad a_{2,+}\psi_{0,0},\quad
(a_{1,+})^2\psi_{0,0},\quad a_{1,+}a_{2,+}\psi_{0,0},\quad(a_{2,+})^2\psi_{0,0}.}
$$
If the <Fock vacuum> has unit <norm>, dividing the general expression by $\sqrt{n_1!n_2!}$ gives normalized <number states>. Normalization is not required here, but using this convention makes the later change of basis unambiguous.

= Solution

The <orbital angular momentum> $L_3=X_1P_2-X_2P_1$ generates rotations in the plane. The <canonical commutation relations> give
$$
[L_3,X_1]=i\hbar X_2,\qquad [L_3,X_2]=-i\hbar X_1,\qquad
[L_3,P_1]=i\hbar P_2,\qquad [L_3,P_2]=-i\hbar P_1.
$$
Use the <commutator product rule> $[A,B^2]=[A,B]B+B[A,B]$. Since different coordinates commute,
$$
\begin{aligned}
[L_3,P_1^2+P_2^2]&=2i\hbar P_1P_2-2i\hbar P_2P_1=0,\\
[L_3,\omega_1^2X_1^2+\omega_2^2X_2^2]
&=2i\hbar(\omega_1^2-\omega_2^2)X_1X_2.
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus, for arbitrary positive <angular frequencies>,
$$
[L_3,H]=i\hbar m(\omega_1^2-\omega_2^2)X_1X_2.
$$
At $\omega_1=\omega_2$, this becomes $\boxed{[L_3,H]=0}$. The <rotational symmetry> of the <two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator> allows a basis of simultaneous <energy eigenstates> and <orbital angular momentum> <eigenstates>.

Set $\omega_1=\omega_2=\omega$. Inverting the <creation and annihilation operators> gives
$$
X_j=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(a_{j,+}+a_{j,-}),\qquad
P_j=i\sqrt{\frac{m\hbar\omega}{2}}(a_{j,+}-a_{j,-}).
$$
Substitute these expressions in $L_3=X_1P_2-X_2P_1$. Terms with two <creation operators> or two <annihilation operators> cancel because different modes commute. The remaining <orbital angular momentum in oscillator ladder operators> is
$$
\boxed{L_3=i\hbar\left(a_{2,+}a_{1,-}-a_{1,+}a_{2,-}\right).}
$$
Equivalently it is $-i\hbar(a_{1,+}a_{2,-}-a_{2,+}a_{1,-})$, consistent with the <circular ladder operators of a two-dimensional oscillator>.

Choose the first three <energy eigenstates> consistently as $\psi_{0,0}$, $\psi_{1,0}=a_{1,+}\psi_{0,0}$ and $\psi_{0,1}=a_{2,+}\psi_{0,0}$. The <Fock vacuum> is annihilated by both lowering operators, so
$$
\boxed{L_3\psi_{0,0}=0,\qquad
L_3\psi_{1,0}=i\hbar\psi_{0,1},\qquad
L_3\psi_{0,1}=-i\hbar\psi_{1,0}.}
$$
For example $a_{1,-}a_{1,+}\psi_{0,0}=\psi_{0,0}$, while $a_{2,-}\psi_{1,0}=0$, giving the middle formula. Thus the simultaneous <eigenstates> can be chosen as
$$
\boxed{\chi_0=\psi_{0,0},\qquad
\chi_+=\frac{\psi_{1,0}+i\psi_{0,1}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\chi_-=\frac{\psi_{1,0}-i\psi_{0,1}}{\sqrt2}.}
$$
Their pairs of <eigenvalues> $(H,L_3)$ are respectively $(\hbar\omega,0)$, $(2\hbar\omega,+\hbar)$ and $(2\hbar\omega,-\hbar)$. The factor $1/\sqrt2$ normalizes the combinations when the original three states are normalized. Mixing only inside the degenerate first-excited <energy eigenspace> preserves the <energy eigenvalue> while diagonalizing $L_3$.

Write $\alpha=m\omega/\hbar$. The first <Hermite polynomials> give $\psi_{0,0}\propto e^{-\alpha(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2}$ and $\psi_{1,0},\psi_{0,1}$ proportional, with the same constant, to $x_1e^{-\alpha(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2}$ and $x_2e^{-\alpha(x_1^2+x_2^2)/2}$. The <angular momentum commutation relations> are
$$
\boxed{[J_i,J_j]=i\hbar\sum_{k=1}^3\varepsilon_{ijk}J_k},\qquad
J^2=J_1^2+J_2^2+J_3^2,\qquad [J^2,J_i]=0,
$$
where $\varepsilon_{ijk}$ is the <Levi-Civita symbol>. The <angular momentum eigenstates> have
$$
\boxed{J^2|j,\mu\rangle=\hbar^2j(j+1)|j,\mu\rangle,\qquad
J_3|j,\mu\rangle=\hbar\mu|j,\mu\rangle,}
$$
with <angular momentum quantum number> $j=0,\tfrac12,1,\tfrac32,\ldots$ and <magnetic quantum number> $\mu=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$. There are $2j+1$ such states in one <irreducible spin representation>, so the $J^2$ <eigenspace> has <dimension> $2j+1$ within that multiplet. The use of $\mu$ avoids confusing the <magnetic quantum number> with the particle <mass> in the earlier questions.

For completeness, the <ladder operators> $J_\pm=J_1\pm iJ_2$ satisfy $[J_3,J_\pm]=\pm\hbar J_\pm$ and
$$
\|J_\pm|j,\mu\rangle\|^2=\hbar^2\bigl[j(j+1)-\mu(\mu\pm1)\bigr].
$$
The upper and lower ends are $\mu=\pm j$; integer steps between them require $2j$ to be a nonnegative integer. This explains both the spectrum and the multiplet <dimension>. Pure <orbital angular momentum> permits only integer $j$, whereas general <spin> allows half-integers. If a larger <Hilbert space> contains several copies of the same <irreducible spin representation>, the full $J^2$ <eigenspace> includes all those copies: its <dimension> is $(2j+1)$ times their multiplicity, and can be infinite. The paper's dimension question is understood per irreducible multiplet.

$$
\boxed{E_\mu=\hbar\omega\mu,\qquad

$$
|\Psi(t)\rangle=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\theta},1,e^{i\theta})v_+
=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}e^{-i\theta}\\\sqrt2\\e^{i\theta}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The <probability amplitudes> for the second <projective measurement> are
$$
\langle v_+|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2},\qquad
\langle v_0|\Psi(t)\rangle=-\frac{i\sin\theta}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\langle v_-|\Psi(t)\rangle=\frac{\cos\theta-1}{2}.
$$
The <Born rule> therefore gives the general <spin-one transverse measurement after axial precession>:
$$
\Pr(+\hbar)=\cos^4\frac\theta2,\qquad
\Pr(0)=\frac12\sin^2\theta,\qquad
\Pr(-\hbar)=\sin^4\frac\theta2.
$$
At the specified times $t_k=\pi k/\omega$, $\theta=\pi k$, so
$$
\boxed{\Pr(+\hbar)=\frac{1+(-1)^k}{2},\qquad
\Pr(0)=0,\qquad
\Pr(-\hbar)=\frac{1-(-1)^k}{2}.}
$$
For even $k$, the result is certainly $+\hbar$; for odd $k$, it is certainly $-\hbar$. Although zero belongs to the spectrum of the <observable>, it never occurs at these times. Indeed, for even $k$ the evolved vector is $v_+$, while for odd $k$ it is $-v_-$, where the minus sign is a <global phase>.
