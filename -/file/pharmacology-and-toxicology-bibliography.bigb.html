= Case of Criminal Poisoning with Oxalic Acid, in Which Perforation of the Stomach Took Place by Henry Duncan Littlejohn
{parent=Pharmacology and toxicology bibliography}
{c}
{tag=Work by Henry Duncan Littlejohn}
{title2=1861}

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5279526/

Edinburgh Medical Journal 7(1), July 1861, pp.13–20. Original pre-examination report of severe gastric injury and perforation; a particular case, not an invariant post-mortem pattern.

= Acute Renal Failure Following Oxalic Acid Poisoning: A Case Report
{parent=Pharmacology and toxicology bibliography}
{c}
{title2=2012}

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3527234/

Original case report; renal biopsy showed tubular injury with refractile <calcium oxalate> crystals. The case does not establish that every exposure produces <hypocalcaemia>. Indexed full text was available; direct retrieval intermittently displayed a browser-verification page.

= Plasma Oxalic Acid and Calcium Levels in Oxalate Poisoning

https://jcp.bmj.com/content/20/3/283

Journal of Clinical Pathology 20(3), pp.283–285. Original investigation of five patients following <oxalic acid> or ethylene-glycol poisoning; primary indexed abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5602563/. Used for later biochemical evidence, not attributed to 1864.
