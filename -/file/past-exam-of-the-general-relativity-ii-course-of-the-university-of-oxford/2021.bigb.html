Complete solutions to the three questions of the Part C <General relativity II course of the University of Oxford> paper, sat on 1 June 2021. https://www.whatdotheyknow.com/request/oxford_physics_past_papers_2/response/2340696/attach/9/Physics%20Papers%202020.pdf?cookie_passthrough=1#page=63[Original released PDF, bundle pages 63–65; paper pages 1–3]. All questions and both Roman-numbered parts of question 1(d) are covered.

= Solution
{parent=a}
{c}

Write $D=u^a\nabla_a$, $\theta=\nabla_a u^a$ for the <expansion scalar>, $a_b=u^a\nabla_a u_b$ for the <four-acceleration>, and $P_b{}^c=\delta_b{}^c+u_bu^c$ for the <spatial projector of a timelike congruence>. The normalization of the <four-velocity> gives $u^ba_b=0$ and $u^b\nabla_a u_b=0$. The <product rule> and <metric compatibility> give the <covariant derivative> of the <perfect fluid in general relativity> <stress-energy tensor>:
$$
F_b:=\nabla^aT_{ab}
=u_b\bigl[D(\rho+p)+(\rho+p)\theta\bigr]+(\rho+p)a_b+\nabla_b p.
$$
Its <tensor contraction> with the <four-velocity> is
$$
u^bF_b=-D\rho-(\rho+p)\theta,
$$
and its projection with the <spatial projector of a timelike congruence> is
$$
P_b{}^cF_c=(\rho+p)a_b+P_b{}^c\nabla_c p.
$$
Thus <stress-energy conservation> implies the <relativistic perfect-fluid energy equation> and the <relativistic Euler equation>:
$$
\boxed{u^a\nabla_a\rho+(\rho+p)\nabla_a u^a=0,\qquad
(\rho+p)u^a\nabla_a u_b+(g_{ab}+u_au_b)\nabla^a p=0.}
$$
Conversely, the two displayed equations set both projections of $F_b$ to zero. Since $F_b=P_b{}^cF_c-u_bu^cF_c$, they imply $F_b=0$. This establishes equivalence, including points where $\rho+p=0$, without dividing by that quantity.

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

Use <stress-energy conservation> and symmetry of the <stress-energy tensor>. The <Killing equation> for the <Killing vector field> $V$ then gives the <stress-energy current from a Killing vector>:
$$
\nabla^aJ_a=(\nabla^aT_{ab})V^b+T_{ab}\nabla^aV^b
=\frac12T^{ab}(\nabla_aV_b+\nabla_bV_a)=0.
$$
Hence $\boxed{\nabla^aJ_a=0}$. The assumption of <stress-energy conservation>, supplied by the equations of motion in part (a), is essential; the <Killing equation> alone only eliminates the second term.

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

The sign of $T_{ab}X^aX^b$ is unchanged by a nonzero rescaling of the <timelike vector> $X$. Normalize it to a <four-velocity> $W$ and, after reversing its direction if necessary, write $W=\gamma(u+w)$, where $u\cdot w=0$, $w\cdot w=s\in[0,1)$ and $\gamma=(1-s)^{-1/2}$ is the <Lorentz factor>. For the <perfect fluid in general relativity>,
$$
T_{ab}W^aW^b=(\rho+p)\gamma^2-p
=\rho+(\rho+p)(\gamma^2-1).
$$
Taking $W=u$ requires $\rho\ge0$. Allowing the <Lorentz factor> to become arbitrarily large requires $\rho+p\ge0$; otherwise sufficiently fast observers measure a negative value. Conversely, these two inequalities make both terms in the last expression nonnegative. Therefore the <weak energy condition for a perfect fluid> is exactly
$$
\boxed{\rho\ge0,\qquad \rho+p\ge0.}
$$
Property (1) is the <weak energy condition>: **every physical observer measures nonnegative local <energy density>**, namely $T_{ab}W^aW^b$ for their normalized <four-velocity> $W$. It allows negative <pressure> down to $p=-\rho$.

= Solution
{parent=i}
{c}

For <relativistic dust>, $p=0$, the <relativistic Euler equation> gives $\rho a_b=0$. The nowhere-vanishing <density> therefore makes the <four-acceleration> zero: $u$ is a <timelike geodesic vector field>. Let $\alpha=u^\flat$ be the <differential one-form> obtained by the <musical isomorphism>. Its <exterior derivative> satisfies
$$
(\iota_u d\alpha)_b=u^a(\nabla_a u_b-\nabla_bu_a)
=a_b-\frac12\nabla_b(u^au_a)=0.
$$
Here $\iota_u$ is the <interior product>. By <hypersurface orthogonality> and the <Frobenius theorem>, $\alpha\wedge d\alpha=0$. Taking the <interior product> and using $\alpha(u)=-1$ gives
$$
0=\iota_u(\alpha\wedge d\alpha)
=-d\alpha-\alpha\wedge\iota_u d\alpha=-d\alpha.
$$
Thus $\boxed{du^\flat=0}$. By the <Poincaré lemma>, locally $\alpha=-dt$, and then $u(t)=-\alpha(u)=1$. Choose spatial <coordinate functions> $x^i$ on one level set of $t$ and extend them constantly along the <integral curves of a vector field> $u$, using its <smooth flow>. This constructs $u=\partial_t$. Since $dt(\partial_i)=0$,
$$
g_{tt}=g(u,u)=-1,\qquad g_{ti}=\alpha(\partial_i)=-dt(\partial_i)=0.
$$
The metric on the orthogonal complement of a <timelike vector> is <positive definite>, so the <synchronous coordinates for irrotational dust> have
$$
\boxed{u=\partial_t,\qquad g=-dt^2+g_{ij}(t,\mathbf x)\,dx^i\otimes dx^j.}
$$
This construction is local; crossing <geodesics> can prevent these <synchronous coordinates> from covering a larger region.

= ii
{parent=d}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=ii}
{c}

In the prescribed <synchronous coordinates>, the <Christoffel symbols> satisfy
$$
\Gamma^a{}_{tt}=\frac12g^{ab}(2\partial_tg_{bt}-\partial_b g_{tt})=0.
$$
Consequently the <four-acceleration> of $u=\partial_t$ vanishes. For a spatial index $i$, $u_i=0$, so the spatial component of the <relativistic Euler equation> becomes
$$
0=(\rho+p)a_i+(\delta_i{}^a+u_i u^a)\partial_a p=\partial_i p.
$$
On a connected coordinate neighbourhood,
$$
\boxed{p=p(t).}
$$
A nonzero <pressure> is allowed, but its spatial <gradient> must vanish if the fluid's <four-velocity> follows these freely falling coordinate lines. No division by $p$ or $\rho+p$ is needed.

= Solution

For the <plane gravitational wave in linearized gravity>, the <chain rule> gives $\partial_\kappa\tilde h_{\mu\nu}=n_\kappa\tilde h'_{\mu\nu}$. The <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity>, called wave gauge in the paper, imposes zero <divergence> of the <trace-reversed metric perturbation>. Hence
$$
0=\partial^\mu\overline{\tilde h}_{\mu\nu}
=n^\mu\tilde h'_{\mu\nu}-\frac12n_\nu\tilde h',
\qquad
\boxed{n^\mu\tilde h'_{\mu\nu}=\frac12\tilde h'n_\nu.}
$$
The <wave equation> itself holds for any smooth profile of $u$, because $\Box\tilde h_{\mu\nu}=(n^\kappa n_\kappa)\tilde h''_{\mu\nu}=0$ for the constant <null vector> $n$.

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

Use the active sign convention for the <linearized coordinate gauge transformation>, $h_{\mu\nu}\mapsto h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu$. The paper's prescribed generator has precisely this sign; using the passive convention would require its negative. Direct <partial differentiation> gives
$$
\partial_\nu\xi_\mu=\frac14\left[
\tilde h''_{\kappa\sigma}x^\kappa x^\sigma n_\mu n_\nu
+2\tilde h'_{\nu\kappa}x^\kappa n_\mu
-2\tilde h'_{\mu\kappa}x^\kappa n_\nu-2\tilde h_{\mu\nu}\right].
$$
The middle terms cancel when the indices are symmetrized. Therefore
$$
\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu
=\frac12\tilde h''_{\kappa\sigma}x^\kappa x^\sigma n_\mu n_\nu-\tilde h_{\mu\nu},
\qquad
\boxed{\hat h_{\mu\nu}=\phi n_\mu n_\nu,\quad
\phi=\frac12\tilde h''_{\kappa\sigma}x^\kappa x^\sigma.}
$$
To test both equations (3), put $H(u)=\tilde h(u)=\eta^{\kappa\sigma}\tilde h_{\kappa\sigma}(u)$, the <trace> of the original <metric perturbation>. Since $n$ is a <null vector>, $\hat h=0$ and its <trace-reversed metric perturbation> equals $\hat h$. Differentiating the result of part (a) gives
$$
n\cdot\partial\phi=n^\kappa\tilde h''_{\kappa\sigma}x^\sigma
=\frac12uH'',
\qquad
\Box\phi=H''+2n^\kappa\tilde h'''_{\kappa\sigma}x^\sigma
=H''+uH'''.
$$
In the second identity, the term with $n^2$ is zero. Thus
$$
\boxed{\partial^\mu\overline{\hat h}_{\mu\nu}=\frac12uH''n_\nu,\qquad
\Box\hat h_{\mu\nu}=(H''+uH''')n_\mu n_\nu.}
$$
**In general $\hat h$ does not satisfy (3).** On an open interval of $u$, both equations hold exactly when $H''=0$ there: the first equation forces this for $u\ne0$, and smoothness gives it at $u=0$ as well. A <linearized coordinate gauge transformation> preserves the <linearized vacuum Einstein equations>, but only a <residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity> preserves the imposed <Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity>.

For an explicit failure, use the <null coordinates> of part (c) and take only $\tilde h_{uv}=\tilde h_{vu}=u^2/4$ nonzero. Then $H=u^2$, the original field obeys (3), but $\phi=uv/2$ gives $\partial^\mu\overline{\hat h}_{\mu u}=u$ and $\Box\hat h_{uu}=2$.

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

With $u=-t+z$ and $v=t+z$, the <Minkowski metric> and the specified <null vector> become
$$
\eta=du\,dv+dx^2+dy^2,\qquad
\eta_{uv}=\eta_{vu}=\frac12,\qquad
n=2\partial_v,\qquad n^\flat=du.
$$
The <Minkowski wave operator in null coordinates> is $\Box=4\partial_u\partial_v+\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2$. All components of the original <metric perturbation> still depend only on $u$, since this coordinate change is linear. The result of part (a), differentiated once, reads
$$
2\tilde h''_{v\nu}=\frac12H''\delta_\nu{}^u.
$$
It follows that $\tilde h''_{vv}=\tilde h''_{vx}=\tilde h''_{vy}=0$ and $\tilde h''_{uv}=H''/4$. Taking the <trace> gives
$$
H''=4\tilde h''_{uv}+\tilde h''_{xx}+\tilde h''_{yy},
\qquad \tilde h''_{xx}+\tilde h''_{yy}=0.
$$
Expanding the scalar $\phi=\tilde h''_{AB}x^Ax^B/2$ in these <null coordinates> therefore yields
$$
\boxed{\phi=a(u)(x^2-y^2)+b(u)xy+c(u)v+d(u)x+e(u)y+f(u),}
$$
where the coefficients are explicitly
$$
\boxed{a=\frac12\tilde h''_{xx},\quad b=\tilde h''_{xy},\quad
c=\frac14uH'',\quad d=u\tilde h''_{ux},\quad
e=u\tilde h''_{uy},\quad f=\frac12u^2\tilde h''_{uu}.}
$$
The quadratic transverse term is a <harmonic quadratic plane-wave profile>. In particular $\partial_v\phi=c$, $\partial^\mu\overline{\hat h}_{\mu\nu}=2c\,n_\nu$ and $\Box\phi=4c'$, consistently reproducing the failure of wave gauge in part (b).

{parent=2}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=d}
{c}

Continue with the active sign convention for the <linearized coordinate gauge transformation>. Choose local <functions> $A,B,C,F$ of $u$ by <integration> so that
$$
A''=\frac c2,\qquad B''=\frac d2,\qquad C''=\frac e2,\qquad F'=-\frac f2.
$$
For example, for any fixed $u_0$ one may take
$$
A(u)=\frac12\int_{u_0}^u(u-s)c(s)\,ds,\quad
B(u)=\frac12\int_{u_0}^u(u-s)d(s)\,ds,\quad
C(u)=\frac12\int_{u_0}^u(u-s)e(s)\,ds,\quad
F(u)=-\frac12\int_{u_0}^u f(s)\,ds.
$$
A generator, specified as a <covector> with components in the <null coordinates> $(u,v,x,y)$, is
$$
\boxed{\chi=A\,dv+B\,dx+C\,dy+
\bigl(-A'v-B'x-C'y+F\bigr)\,du.}
$$
Indeed, with $\delta h_{AB}=\partial_A\chi_B+\partial_B\chi_A$, the mixed components cancel:
$$
\delta h_{uv}=A'-A'=0,\qquad
\delta h_{ux}=B'-B'=0,\qquad
\delta h_{uy}=C'-C'=0.
$$
All components without a $u$ index vanish as well, and
$$
\delta h_{uu}=-2A''v-2B''x-2C''y+2F'
=-cv-dx-ey-f.
$$
Since $n^\flat=du$, only the $uu$ component needs changing. Thus
$$
\boxed{\mathring h=\psi\,du^2,\qquad
\psi=a(u)(x^2-y^2)+b(u)xy.}
$$
**Yes, $\mathring h$ satisfies both equations (3).** Its <trace> is zero, $\partial_v\psi=0$ gives zero <divergence>, and the <harmonic quadratic plane-wave profile> gives
$$
\partial^A\overline{\mathring h}_{AB}=2\partial_v\psi\,\delta_B{}^u=0,
\qquad
\Box\psi=4\partial_u\partial_v\psi+\partial_x^2\psi+\partial_y^2\psi=2a-2a=0.
$$
The two surviving <functions> encode the two <gravitational wave polarizations>; constant and linear transverse terms have been removed by the explicit <linearized coordinate gauge transformation>.

= Solution

Put $d\Omega_2^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$, the standard <metric tensor> on the <unit sphere>. From $t=v+m/\lambda$ one has $dt=dv-m\lambda^{-2}d\lambda$. The apparently divergent terms cancel:
$$
\boxed{g=-\lambda^2dv^2+2m\,dv\,d\lambda+m^2d\Omega_2^2.}
$$
The $(v,\lambda)$ <metric tensor> block has <determinant> $-m^2\ne0$, independently of $\lambda$. Its coefficients are <real analytic>, so this formula defines a regular <Lorentzian metric> on
$$
\boxed{M_1=\mathbb R_v\times\mathbb R_\lambda\times S^2.}
$$
The polar-coordinate degeneracies at the poles are handled by ordinary <two-sphere> charts. Thus $\lambda=0$ is a <coordinate singularity> of the original chart and is crossed by this <spacetime extension>.

{parent=3}
{scope}

= Solution
{parent=b}
{c}

The inverse of the $(v,\lambda)$ <metric tensor> block is
$$
(g^{AB})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/m\\1/m&\lambda^2/m^2\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The normal <covector> $d\lambda$ has squared norm $\lambda^2/m^2$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{\{\lambda=\lambda_0\}\text{ is timelike for }\lambda_0\ne0,
\quad\{\lambda=0\}\text{ is null}.}
$$
More explicitly, the induced <metric tensor> is $-\lambda_0^2dv^2+m^2d\Omega_2^2$; at zero its sole zero direction is $\partial_v$. The coefficients are independent of $v$, so $K=\partial_v$ is a <Killing vector field>. On $\lambda=0$,
$$
K^2=-\lambda^2=0,\qquad
K^\flat=-\lambda^2dv+m\,d\lambda=m\,d\lambda.
$$
Hence $K$ is both tangent and normal to this <null hypersurface>, proving it is a <Killing horizon>. The definition of <surface gravity> is $\nabla_KK=\kappa K$ on the <Killing horizon>, or equivalently $d(K^2)=-2\kappa K^\flat$. Since $d(K^2)=-2\lambda\,d\lambda$ vanishes there while $K^\flat\ne0$,
$$
\boxed{\kappa=0.}
$$
Thus this is a <degenerate Killing horizon>, and $v$ is an <affine parameter> on each of its <null geodesics>.

For a concrete <horizon frequency comparison>, fix the same angular position for a narrow high-<frequency> <light> beam and two freely falling radial observers. Work in <geometric optics>, with the beam's affinely transported <wavevector> tangent to the <Killing horizon>, $k=qK$. Here $q>0$ is constant because $\nabla_KK=0$. Choose the observers as translates of one <timelike geodesic> by the <isometry> generated by $K$, crossing at $v_1$ and $v_2$. Give them the same positive <Killing energy> per unit <rest mass>, $E=-K\cdot W$. At their crossings their normalized <four-velocities> have
$$
W^\lambda=-\frac Em,\qquad W^v=\frac1{2E},\qquad
W^\theta=W^\varphi=0,
$$
which satisfy $g(W,W)=2mW^vW^\lambda=-1$. They can be realized by local <geodesics> with precisely these initial data. The measured <angular frequency> is
$$
\omega=-k\cdot W=qE,\qquad
\boxed{\frac{\omega_2}{\omega_1}=1.}
$$
To connect this measurement to <surface gravity>, if $\nabla_KK=\kappa K$ with constant $\kappa$, affine transport of $k=q(v)K$ would give $dq/dv=-\kappa q$. For observers related by the same <Killing vector field> translation, $-K\cdot W$ agrees at the crossings, giving $\omega_2/\omega_1=e^{-\kappa(v_2-v_1)}$. Here there is **no accumulated <redshift> along the <Killing horizon>** for these equally prepared observers. Arbitrary observers with different <four-velocities> can still measure different <frequencies> through their different <Killing energies>.

= Solution
{parent=c}
{c}

First use the original <null coordinates> $u=t+m/\lambda$, $v=t-m/\lambda$. They obey $u-v=2m/\lambda$, and the radial <metric tensor> becomes
$$
g_{(2)}=-\frac{4m^2}{(u-v)^2}\,du\,dv.
$$
Apply the given substitutions $u=\tan(U/2)$ and $v=-\cot(V/2)$. The identities
$$
u-v=\frac{\cos((U-V)/2)}{\cos(U/2)\sin(V/2)},\qquad
du=\frac{dU}{2\cos^2(U/2)},\qquad
dv=\frac{dV}{2\sin^2(V/2)}
$$
give the <analytic extension of a spacetime>
$$
g=-\frac{m^2}{\cos^2((U-V)/2)}\,dU\,dV+m^2d\Omega_2^2.
$$
The poles of the individual tangent and cotangent substitutions are now ordinary coordinate lines wherever $\cos((U-V)/2)\ne0$. Set $T=(U+V)/2$ and $X=(U-V)/2$. Allowing these <coordinate functions> to continue across every such line gives
$$
\boxed{\widetilde M=\mathbb R_T\times(-\pi/2,\pi/2)_X\times S^2,\qquad
\widetilde g=m^2\sec^2X(-dT^2+dX^2)+m^2d\Omega_2^2.}
$$
This is the <universal cover> of <two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime> of <radius> $m$, times a <two-sphere> of <radius> $m$: the simply connected <Bertotti-Robinson spacetime>. In defining this <maximal analytic extension>, **$T$ ranges over the whole real line without periodic identification**. A periodic identification would instead create <closed timelike curves> and would be an additional global choice.

For clarity, the original patch is embedded by the branches $-\pi<U<\pi$ and $0<V<2\pi$. The inequality $u>v$ is equivalent on these branches to $|U-V|<\pi$, so the original patch occupies
$$
-\frac\pi2<X<\frac\pi2,\qquad X<T<\pi-X.
$$
The first <spacetime extension> in part (a) occupies the larger band $0<T-X<2\pi$, with $|X|<\pi/2$. Its finite-$v$ <Killing horizon> is the interior line $T+X=\pi$. The edges $T-X=0,2\pi$ of that band are further regular <Poincaré horizons>; continuing through them produces the full <conformal strip of two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime>.

The original coordinate $\lambda$ has a smooth continuation throughout the strip:
$$
\boxed{\lambda=\frac{2m\cos(U/2)\sin(V/2)}{\cos X}
=m\frac{\sin T-\sin X}{\cos X}.}
$$
Consequently the complete set of zero-level <Killing horizons> is
$$
\boxed{T=X+2\pi j\quad\text{or}\quad T=\pi-X+2\pi j,\qquad j\in\mathbb Z.}
$$
These are two alternating families of parallel <null lines>; different families meet only at the excluded <conformal boundaries>, never inside the <spacetime>. The continuation of the original <Killing vector field> is
$$
K=(1-\sin T\sin X)\partial_T+\cos T\cos X\,\partial_X,
\qquad K^2=-\lambda^2.
$$
The field never vanishes in the interior, including on the <Killing horizons>.

To obtain the <Penrose diagram>, suppress the <two-sphere> and multiply by the <conformal factor> $\cos^2X/m^2$. The resulting radial <metric tensor> is $-dT^2+dX^2$, so the diagram is an infinitely tall strip of width $\pi$, with <null lines> at 45 degrees and timelike edges $X=\pm\pi/2$. Generically,
$$
\boxed{X\to-\pi/2:\ \lambda\to+\infty,\qquad
X\to+\pi/2:\ \lambda\to-\infty.}
$$
At the special edge points where the two families of <Killing horizons> end, the displayed formula is a $0/0$ limit and $\lambda$ has no unique limiting value. Thus the infinity labels on the diagram refer to the open edge segments, not those vertices. Each interior point of the diagram represents a <two-sphere> of fixed <radius> $m$; neither edge is a centre where that <two-sphere> shrinks.

Finally, these timelike edges are <conformal boundaries>, not omitted regular points of a further <spacetime extension>. One way to establish maximality is <geodesic completeness>. The <two-dimensional anti-de Sitter spacetime> factor embeds locally as $Z\cdot Z=-m^2$ in a flat space with <metric signature> $(-,-,+)$. Along an affinely parametrized <geodesic> with constant $\epsilon=\dot Z\cdot\dot Z$, the ambient equation is
$$
\ddot Z=\frac{\epsilon}{m^2}Z.
$$
Its oscillatory, linear or hyperbolic solutions for negative, zero or positive $\epsilon$ exist for all real <affine parameters> and lift to the <universal cover>. The <two-sphere> is also <geodesically complete>, and <geodesics> of the product split into <geodesics> of its factors. Hence $\widetilde g$ is <geodesically complete>. A proper regular <spacetime extension> would furnish a <geodesic> reaching its boundary in finite <affine parameter>, contradicting that completeness. This proves that the displayed simply connected analytic <spacetime> is maximal.
