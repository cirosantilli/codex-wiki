**The product is 1,4-dimethoxy-2,5-bis(2-methylbutan-2-yl)<benzene>.** Each new substituent is $\mathrm{-C(CH_3)_2CH_2CH_3}$, a <tert-amyl group>, rather than the unrearranged secondary alkyl group of the <alcohol>. The two substituents are related by the <symmetry> of the ring.

The <proton> assignments are:

|| <chemical shift> / ppm
|| Assignment
|| Reason

| $6.75$
| **Two aromatic H, at positions 3 and 6**
| <symmetry> makes them equivalent; neither has an adjacent aromatic H to give a resolved ordinary ortho splitting.

| $3.79$
| **Two <methoxy groups>, six H**
| Equivalent $\mathrm{OCH_3}$ groups give a <NMR singlet>.

| $1.81$
| **Two ethyl $\mathrm{CH_2}$ groups, four H**
| Each couples to a terminal three-proton <methyl group>, giving a <NMR quartet>.

| $1.33$
| **Four <methyl groups> attached to the two quaternary benzylic <carbons>, twelve H**
| These <methyl groups> have no neighboring <methine group> <proton> and give a <NMR singlet>.

| $0.65$
| **Two terminal ethyl <methyl groups>, six H**
| Each couples to two <methylene group> <protons>, giving a <NMR triplet> with the same coupling constant as the <NMR quartet>.

The integrals add to 30 H. The unusually shielded terminal ethyl methyl signal is compatible with an aryl-containing environment; its <NMR quartet>–<NMR triplet> partner and integral are the decisive assignment evidence. The <molecular formula> and <degree of unsaturation>, $\mathrm{DBE}=4$, fit a <benzene> ring with saturated side chains.

<protonation> of the <alcohol> converts its OH group into a <water> <leaving group>. Departure of <water> initially gives the secondary <carbocation> $\mathrm{CH_3-CH^+-CH(CH_3)_2}$. A **<1,2-hydride shift>** from the neighboring <methine group> transfers its C–H bonding pair to the electron-deficient <carbon>:
$$
\mathrm{CH_3-CH^+-CH(CH_3)_2\longrightarrow CH_3CH_2-C^+(CH_3)_2}.
$$
The resulting tertiary <carbocation> is the tert-amyl <electrophile>. An aromatic $\pi$ bond of the dimethoxybenzene attacks that <carbon>, producing a <Wheland intermediate>. Removal of the <proton> from the attacked ring <carbon> restores <aromaticity> and regenerates acid. Repeating this <Friedel-Crafts alkylation> at the symmetry-related second site gives the disubstituted product.

The <hydride> migration explains the new ethyl group and the absence of a benzylic <methine group> <proton>. Simply attaching the original <alcohol> skeleton at its OH-bearing <carbon> would not explain the twelve-proton <NMR singlet>.

The <methoxy groups> activate the <aromatic ring> by electron donation through <chemical resonance> and direct electrophilic substitution ortho and para. Their para positions are already occupied by one another, so the first alkylation occurs at an available ortho position; the four initially vacant positions are equivalent by <symmetry>.

After placing the first <tert-amyl group> at position 2, **position 5 is favored for the second substitution**. It is para to that bulky group and ortho to the other <methoxy group>. Position 3 is crowded between methoxy and tert-amyl substituents, while position 6 is near the first <tert-amyl group> across the intervening methoxy-bearing <carbon>. The 2,5 arrangement distributes the bulky groups on opposite parts of the ring and preserves the equivalence seen in the spectrum.

Alkyl groups do not electronically deactivate the ring, so the stopping point needs a steric explanation. After two such substitutions the remaining positions are much more congested, each adjacent to a bulky <tert-amyl group> and a <methoxy group>. **Two large substituents are accommodated much more readily than a third**, despite excess <alcohol>. The formula and <NMR> identify this sterically favored disubstitution; they do not imply that a <Friedel-Crafts reaction> always stops after two alkylations.

**The new ring is formed by intramolecular Friedel-Crafts addition to the <ketone>.** <titanium tetrachloride> coordinates the <ketone> <oxygen> of the cyclic <beta-ketoester>, increasing electrophilicity at that <carbonyl> <carbon>. The nearby electron-rich aryl ring attacks this activated <ketone> at the available position ortho to its ethyl tether.

The electron-flow sequence is: an aromatic $\pi$ bond forms the new aryl–carbonyl-carbon bond, the <ketone> $\pi$ bond moves onto <oxygen>, and loss of the aromatic <proton> restores <aromaticity>. The two-carbon tether and the shared quaternary <carbon> make the newly closed carbocycle six-membered. Hydrolytic work-up converts the titanium-bound <alkoxide> into the tertiary <alcohol>.

The <lactone> <carbonyl> survives. This is an addition to the <ketone> with retention of its <oxygen> as OH, rather than an acyl substitution that removes that <oxygen>. The pendant terminal <alkene> is not involved. These bond changes account for the fused <aromatic ring> system and the <alcohol> in C without needing to assign the new <stereochemistry>.

For an **amine-first pathway**, use <triethylamine> as a <nucleophile> in a reversible <conjugate addition>. Its <lone pair> attacks C3 of the <enone>; <electron> shifts through C2 and the <carbonyl> give a zwitterionic <enolate> with a C3–$\mathrm{NEt_3^+}$ bond.

**There are two, two and four <stereoisomers>, respectively; all eight individual <stereoisomers> are <chiral>.** The drawings below give the mirror pairs. Solid and hashed wedges distinguish opposite faces; ordinary changes between conformers are not counted again.

* The benzyl-substituted alkylidenecyclohexane has an axial stereogenic element. At one end the exocyclic <alkene> presents Ph and H; at the other end the substituted ring <carbon> presents benzyl and H. The ring constrains the intervening paths, so exchanging one end pair gives the nonsuperposable mirror image. There is one enantiomeric pair, rather than an independent ordinary $E/Z$ pair multiplied by a carbon-center count.
* The diphenylspiro compound also has one <axial chirality>. The two four-membered rings occupy different, approximately perpendicular planes. Their remote Ph/H substitutions distinguish the two handed arrangements. It gives one enantiomeric pair and no meso form. <axial chirality> of this spiro framework is experimentally established, for example by resolution of its 2,6-dicarboxylic-acid derivatives.<Enantiopure spiro[3.3]heptane-2,6-dicarboxylic acid by Hong-Zhi Tang and others>[Abstract, isolation of enantiopure spiro derivatives]{ref}
* The phospholane has a stereogenic <carbon> at the COOH-bearing position and a pyramidal <phosphorus stereocenter>. The <phosphorus> has three different <carbon> ligands and a <lone pair>: its two ring paths differ because only one encounters the substituted <carbon> immediately. The four configurations are $(R_P,R_C)$, $(R_P,S_C)$, $(S_P,R_C)$ and $(S_P,S_C)$. The first and last form a mirror pair, as do the middle two. <carbon> and <phosphorus> cannot be interchanged by a <symmetry operation>, so there is no meso reduction in the count.

Unlike the usual rapidly inverting trialkylamine, a pyramidal <phosphine> can retain a <phosphorus> configuration on an ordinary observational timescale; its two <phosphorus> configurations must therefore be included.

**Both reactions are <E2 eliminations> requiring an axial <chloride> and an axial beta <hydrogen> in an <antiperiplanar conformation> relative to the C–Cl bond.** The reacting C–H bond supplies the <alkene> $\pi$ bond as the C–Cl bond breaks; <hydroxide> accepts the <proton>.

For the trans-1,2 substrate, the low-energy chair has methyl and <chlorine> both equatorial. <ring inversion> is needed to place <chlorine> axial, but this also places methyl axial. The population of the reactive chair is consequently small, explaining the slow reaction. At the methyl-bearing neighboring <carbon> the axial position is occupied by methyl, not <hydrogen>. Only the other beta <carbon> has the needed anti axial H, so elimination gives **3-methylcyclohexene** rather than the more substituted <alkene>.

For the cis substrate, a favorable chair has methyl equatorial and <chlorine> axial. It can react without paying the axial-methyl penalty, explaining the faster rate. Both adjacent <carbons> now supply an anti axial <hydrogen>. Removing the H on the methyl-bearing <carbon> gives **1-methylcyclohexene, the major product**; removing the other gives **3-methylcyclohexene, the minor product**. The former has the more substituted <double bond>.

Thus <alkene> stability selects between accessible pathways in the cis case, but it cannot create the missing <antiperiplanar conformation> H in the trans case.

Number the <nitrogen> N1, the phenyl-bearing <carbon> C2, and then continue through C3–C6 around the piperidine. Call the solid-wedge <epoxide> face up; the <phenyl group> is down in both starting structures. The bulky <phenyl group> favors a pseudoequatorial orientation.

<epoxide> opening follows the **Fürst-Plattner preference for a chair-like, trans-diaxial opening trajectory**. Attack at the two <epoxide> <carbons> is not equivalent: the favored route evolves toward an ordinary chair, whereas the alternative demands a less favorable twist/boat-like distortion. Such conformational control of epoxy-2-arylpiperidine opening is supported by synthetic studies.<1,3-Allylic Strain as a Strategic Diversification Element for Constructing Libraries of Substituted 2-Arylpiperidines by Thomas C. Coombs and others>[Scheme 1, chair-like trans-diaxial epoxide opening; applied here with the free-NH conformer]{ref}

For the first <epoxide>, **azide attacks C5 from down**, breaking the C5–O bond. The residual C4–O bond is retained, so work-up gives **C5–N3 down and C4–OH up**, with the original C2–Ph down. For the second, down-face <epoxide>, **azide attacks C4 from up**, leaving **C4–N3 up and C5–OH down**. These assignments are shown in the middle row of the original stereochemical map.

In each mechanism an azide terminal <nitrogen> attacks the <epoxide> <carbon>, the C–O bonding pair goes to <oxygen>, and <protonation> gives the <alcohol>. Each attacked center inverts, and the azide and OH are trans. The phenyl-bearing center is unchanged. Reversing the <epoxide> face changes which <carbon> has the favored axial attack trajectory; a steric rule based only on distance from phenyl misses that conformational effect.

<sulfide> first opens one <epoxide> by backside attack, giving a carbon-bound <thiolate> and an <alkoxide>. Using the same numbering, take this first attack at C2: the C2–S bond is down and C3–O remains up.

The attached <sulfur> still bears a nucleophilic <lone pair> and negative charge. It attacks C5 of the second <epoxide> **intramolecularly**, breaking C5–O and closing a bridge between C2 and C5. Because the original <epoxides> are cis, both new C–S bonds point to the same opposite face and one <sulfur> <chemical atom> can connect them. The <sulfur> therefore replaces two separate external <nucleophiles>: **two <epoxides> are opened but only one <sulfur> <chemical atom> is incorporated**. Adding that <sulfur> and two work-up <protons> gives $\mathrm{C_6H_{10}O_2S}$, consistent with B.

The first step is a **vinylogous <aldol condensation> at C5 of the unsaturated <lactam>**. Number <nitrogen> N1, the <lactam> <carbonyl> C2, and then the methoxy-substituted <alkene> C3=C4 and the <methylene group> C5.

The hydroxyl group first binds <aluminium>, normally by <deprotonation> to an <aluminium> <alkoxide> with release of <hydrogen>. Coordination also brings <aluminium> close to the <alkyne>. **<directed trans-hydroalumination>** then puts <hydride> on the <alkyne> <carbon> proximal to CH2O and <aluminium> on the distal <carbon>.

One useful mechanistic description has external <hydride> attacking the coordinated <alkyne> opposite the aluminium-containing face. The resulting <vinylaluminate> is stabilized by a five-membered O–Al–C–C–CH2 chelate. This is not the ordinary undirected syn addition of a neutral dialkylaluminium <hydride>. Propargylic-alcohol-directed trans-hydroalumination with <Red-Al> gives <vinylaluminate> intermediates that can be intercepted by <electrophiles>.<trans-Hydroalumination and alkylation by Neil F. Langille and Timothy F. Jamison>[Abstract, stereoselective vinylalanate formation from propargylic alcohols]{ref}

<iodine> replaces the distal C–Al bond with C–I without scrambling the <alkene> geometry; aqueous work-up releases the <alcohol>. <hydride> and <iodine> are consequently **trans** across the <alkene>, while <iodine> and the CH2OH group are on the same side.

By <Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules> priority the product is therefore **$Z$**: CH2OH outranks H at one <alkene> <carbon> and I outranks CH2R' at the other. Describing the two <carbon> chains as trans would not make this an $E$ <alkene>, because the <iodine> changes which substituent has higher priority.

The two contacts close an eight-membered hydrogen-bonded ring, shown explicitly below.

Both <molecules> therefore act once as hydrogen-bond donor and once as hydrogen-bond acceptor, giving two O–H···O=C contacts. This is the conventional self-complementary <dimer> motif; the schematic is not a prediction of every weaker contact in an unprovided <crystal structure>.

**Two <molecules> of the <aminopyridine> form a self-complementary <dimer> through two N–H···N contacts.** In each direction, one exocyclic amino <hydrogen> is the donor and the <pyridine> ring <nitrogen> of the other <molecule> is the acceptor.

Orient the two amino/pyridine-N edges toward one another, as in the second panel of the original homodimer drawing. Each <molecule> donates one NH and accepts at one ring N; the second amino H remains available for additional packing contacts. The <chlorine> substituent is retained but is not one of the two principal donor–acceptor sites.

= Solution
{parent=iii}

The strongest donor belongs to the acid, $\alpha_1=3.5$, and the strongest acceptor to the <aminopyridine>, $\beta_2=8.5$. **The <cocrystal> pairs the strongest donor with the strongest acceptor**, while the reciprocal amino-NH···<carbonyl> contact closes a complementary two-contact motif.

Using the simple favorable-contact score $\alpha\beta$, one heterodimer gives
$$
S_{\rm hetero}=\alpha_1\beta_2+\alpha_2\beta_1
=(3.5)(8.5)+(3.0)(5.5)=46.25.
$$
The self-associated comparison, normalized to the same two <molecules> and two <hydrogen bonds>, is half an acid <dimer> plus half an <aminopyridine> <dimer>:
$$
S_{\rm self}=\alpha_1\beta_1+\alpha_2\beta_2
=(3.5)(5.5)+(3.0)(8.5)=44.75.
$$
Thus
$$
\boxed{\Delta S=1.50=(\alpha_1-\alpha_2)(\beta_2-\beta_1)>0}.
$$
In a model where bond <free energy> contains $-\alpha\beta$, this is a favorable energy change. No bonds have been artificially added to one side of the comparison: both alternatives contain two contacts per acid/<aminopyridine> pair.

The calculation rationalizes the hydrogen-bond preference. It is not a complete <crystal> <free energy>: packing, additional contacts, <entropy> and possible <solvent> effects also matter. Without a specified calibration, the numerical score should not be promoted to a measured cocrystallization <free energy>.
