= Solution
{parent=1}

Let $t(n)$ denote <traction>: the force per unit area exerted across an oriented internal surface with unit normal $n$. Define $\sigma_{ij}$ as component $i$ of the <traction> on a coordinate plane whose normal is $e_j$. <Balance of linear momentum> on a shrinking tetrahedron makes the surface-force terms, of order area, dominate its inertia and <body forces>, of order volume. Resolving its oblique face into the three coordinate projected areas gives Cauchy's <traction> formula
$$
\boxed{t(n)=\sigma n,\qquad t_i(n)=\sigma_{ij}n_j.}
$$
Opposite faces have opposite <tractions>, as follows independently from a shrinking pillbox. This fixes both the index and normal conventions.

For a <material volume> $V(t)$, the <Reynolds transport theorem> says $d\int_{V(t)}f\,dV/dt=\int_{V(t)}[f_t+\nabla\cdot(fu)]dV$. Apply it componentwise to $f=\rho u_i$ and use <mass conservation>. With no <body force> in this calculation, <balance of linear momentum> and the <divergence theorem> give
$$
\int_{V(t)}\rho\frac{Du_i}{Dt}\,dV
=\int_{\partial V(t)}\sigma_{ij}n_j\,dS
=\int_{V(t)}\partial_j\sigma_{ij}\,dV.
$$
Arbitrariness of the <material volume> yields $\boxed{\rho Du_i/Dt=\partial_j\sigma_{ij}}$, with $D/Dt=\partial_t+u_j\partial_j$.

For constant <dynamic viscosity> and incompressible <velocity>, $\partial_ju_j=0$, so
$$
\partial_j\sigma_{ij}=-\partial_ip+\mu\partial_j\partial_ju_i+
\mu\partial_i(\partial_ju_j)=-\partial_ip+\mu\nabla^2u_i.
$$
Thus $\boxed{\rho(u_t+u\cdot\nabla u)=-\nabla p+\mu\nabla^2u}$.

In fully developed steady <pipe flow> $u=w(x,y)e_z$, the convective acceleration vanishes. The transverse equations give $p_x=p_y=0$ and the axial equation gives $p_z=\mu(w_{xx}+w_{yy})$. One side depends only on $z$ and the other only on $(x,y)$, so both equal a constant. Hence
$$
\boxed{w_{xx}+w_{yy}=p_z/\mu,\qquad p_z=\text{constant}.}
$$
If $n$ is outward from the fluid cross-section, the wall-on-fluid axial shear per length is $\mu\oint\partial_nw\,ds=\mu\int_A\Delta w\,dA=Ap_z$. The fluid-on-pipe force is its negative:
$$
\boxed{\text{drag per unit length}=-Ap_z.}
$$
For forward <pressure>-driven flow $p_z<0$, this force points along the flow. Its value depends on <cross-sectional area> and <pressure> drop, even though the detailed <velocity> profile depends on the shape.

= Solution
{parent=2}

Let the thin transverse length be $Re^{-1/2}$ times the longitudinal scale and write $Y=Re^{1/2}y$, $V=Re^{1/2}v$. Continuity then remains $u_x+V_Y=0$. Transverse momentum gives $p_Y=0$ to leading order; the quiescent exterior has constant <pressure>, hence $p_x=0$. Axial viscous diffusion in $x$ is smaller by $Re^{-1}$, leaving
$$
\boxed{uu_x+Vu_Y=u_{YY},\qquad u_x+V_Y=0.}
$$
<Symmetry> about the jet centreline requires $V=0$ and $u_Y=0$ at $Y=0$. At large positive $Y$, require $u\to0$ and $u_Y\to0$. The transverse <velocity> can approach a finite nonzero scaled <entrainment> <velocity>; imposing $V\to0$ as well would rule out the jet. The physical transverse <velocity> remains smaller by $Re^{-1/2}$ and matches an entraining outer flow.

Using continuity, the momentum equation is $\partial_x(u^2)+\partial_Y(uV-u_Y)=0$. Integrating across the jet, the boundary terms vanish because $u,u_Y\to0$ while $V$ is finite. Thus
$$
\boxed{M=\int_{-\infty}^\infty u^2\,dY=\text{constant}.}
$$
Apart from the dimensional <density> and <velocity>/length factors, this is the conserved axial <momentum flux> supplied by the nozzle. <Entrainment> increases <volume flux> without adding axial momentum from the resting exterior.

For $u=x^\alpha f'(\eta)$ with $\eta=Y/x^\beta$, use the <stream function> $\psi=x^{\alpha+\beta}f(\eta)$ so that $u=\psi_Y$ and $V=-\psi_x$. Momentum conservation requires $2\alpha+\beta=0$. Inertia and diffusion scale as $x^{2\alpha-1}$ and $x^{\alpha-2\beta}$, so $\alpha+2\beta=1$. Therefore $\boxed{\alpha=-1/3,\quad\beta=2/3}$. In particular
$$
V=x^{-2/3}(2\eta f'-f)/3.
$$
Substitution reduces momentum to $3f^{(3)}+ff''+(f')^2=0$, with $f(0)=f''(0)=0$ and $f'(\infty)=0$. Integrating first gives $3f''+ff'=0$, the integration constant vanishing at the centreline. A second integration gives $3f'+f^2/2=C$. Parameterize its positive constant as $C=18A^2$ and solve the resulting <Riccati equation> with $f(0)=0$:
$$
\boxed{f=6A\tanh(A\eta),\qquad u=6A^2x^{-1/3}\operatorname{sech}^2(AYx^{-2/3}).}
$$
The momentum <integral> fixes its scale, since $\int_{-\infty}^\infty\operatorname{sech}^4z\,dz=4/3$:
$$
\boxed{M=48A^3,\qquad A=(M/48)^{1/3}.}
$$
The upper-half <entrainment> is $V\to-2Ax^{-2/3}$; the negative side has the opposite sign, drawing ambient fluid inward. This is the <Bickley jet>, valid in the slender far-downstream laminar regime, rather than in the immediate nozzle region.

= Solution
{parent=3}

Scale lateral distance with $L$, transverse distance with $h$, lateral <velocity> with $U$, and the normal <velocity> with $Uh/L$. Balancing lateral <pressure> force with transverse viscous stress gives <pressure> scale $\mu UL/h^2$. The ratio of inertia to that stress is $Uh^2/(\nu L)$, while lateral viscous <derivatives> are smaller by $(h/L)^2$. The two stated smallness conditions therefore reduce the lateral equations to $\mu u_{zz}=p_x$, $\mu v_{zz}=p_y$. The normal component of <balance of linear momentum> gives $p_z=0$ at leading order; continuity remains $u_x+v_y+w_z=0$. The no-slip, impermeable rigid plates require $u=v=w=0$ at both plate surfaces.

Integrating with those boundary values gives
$$
(u,v)=-\frac{z(h-z)}{2\mu}\nabla p,\qquad
\boxed{\bar u=-K\nabla p,\quad K=\frac{h^2}{12\mu}.}
$$
Depth-integrated continuity has no normal boundary flux, so $\nabla\cdot\bar u=0$ and $\boxed{\nabla^2p=0}$ away from the injection point.

Neglecting <surface tension>, the <fluid free boundary> has $p=p_{\rm ext}$, and its normal <velocity> is the fluid <velocity>: $\dot R=\bar u_r(R)$. Any constant <capillary pressure> for a circular interface can instead be included in that spatially uniform boundary <pressure>. Conservation of injected volume gives $2\pi rh\bar u_r=Q$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\bar u_r=\frac{Q}{2\pi hr},\quad
R^2=R_0^2+\frac{Qt}{\pi h},\quad
p(r,t)=p_{\rm ext}+\frac{6\mu Q}{\pi h^3}\log\frac{R(t)}r.}
$$
The logarithmic <pressure> singularity represents the ideal <point source>, which in a physical cell is replaced by a finite inlet. The formula is an exact radial solution of the depth-averaged model for either sign of $Q$, while $R^2>0$.

Its robustness differs sharply between the signs. With viscous fluid inside a much less viscous exterior, injection displaces the less viscous phase and is stabilizing; suction draws the exterior into the viscous region and is prone to fingering. One can see this directly without relying on a slogan. Write the boundary as $R+a_n\cos n\theta$ and its regular <pressure> <perturbation> as $b_nr^n\cos n\theta$. Linearizing the constant-<pressure> condition gives $\delta p(R)=-a_np_0'(R)=a_nQ/(2\pi hKR)$. The <perturbation> radial <velocity> is then $-na_nQ/(2\pi hR^2)$, and evaluation of the base <velocity> at the displaced boundary adds $-a_nQ/(2\pi hR^2)$. Thus
$$
\boxed{\dot a_n=-\frac{(n+1)Q}{2\pi hR^2}a_n.}
$$
Modes decay for injection and grow for suction. Accordingly the suction circle is a mathematically possible symmetric solution but generally an unstable prediction, failing before its formal collapse in a perturbed experiment. This is <stability of an interior Hele-Shaw blob under injection and suction>.

= Solution
{parent=4}

Two-dimensional <incompressibility> permits $u=\psi_y$, $v=-\psi_x$. Taking the <curl> of the Stokes momentum equation eliminates <pressure> and gives $\nabla^2(v_x-u_y)=0$. Since $v_x-u_y=-\nabla^2\psi$, the <stream function> obeys $\boxed{\nabla^4\psi=0}$.

Scale radius by $a$, <velocity> by $U$ and <stream function> by $Ua$. In <polar coordinates> $u_r=r^{-1}\psi_\theta$, $u_\theta=-\psi_r$. No penetration makes $\psi$ constant on the cylinder; choose that constant zero. No slip also requires $\psi_r=0$ at $r=1$. The uniform stream has $\psi=r\sin\theta$, specifying the far-field condition.

The boundary forcing has angular mode $\sin\theta$, so its corresponding mode must have $\psi=f(r)\sin\theta$. Define $L_1=d^2/dr^2+r^{-1}d/dr-r^{-2}$. Then $\nabla^4\psi=(L_1^2f)\sin\theta$, while $L_1r^s=(s^2-1)r^{s-2}$. Thus the <indicial equation> of $L_1^2$ is $(s^2-1)[(s-2)^2-1]=(s-3)(s-1)^2(s+1)=0$, giving
$$
\boxed{f=Ar^3+Br\log r+Cr+Dr^{-1}.}
$$
The repeated exponent one supplies the logarithmic term. The condition $f'\to1$ first forces $A=B=0$ and $C=1$. Then $f(1)=0$ forces $D=-1$, but $f'(1)=C-D=2\ne0$. No additional <Fourier mode> can repair the missing <boundary condition> in this angular mode. Therefore the unbounded-domain Stokes problem has no solution with both no slip and a nonzero uniform far stream.

This is <Stokes' paradox>. It identifies a nonuniform approximation, rather than the absence of physical flow. For any small but nonzero <Reynolds number>, convective inertia becomes important sufficiently far away. An inner Stokes region must be matched to an outer Oseen or Navier-Stokes region that retains that inertia. Alternatively, finite external boundaries introduce a different well-posed physical problem; they cannot be sent to infinity while keeping the naive Stokes solution unchanged.

= Solution
{parent=5}

For a <material volume>, <mass conservation> is $d\int_{V(t)}\rho\,dV/dt=0$. The transport theorem and arbitrariness of that volume give $\boxed{\rho_t+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0}$. <Balance of linear momentum> includes the <pressure> <traction> and <body force>:
$$
\frac d{dt}\int_{V(t)}\rho u\,dV=-\int_{\partial V(t)}pn\,dS+\int_{V(t)}\rho g\,dV.
$$
Apply transport, the <divergence theorem> and the mass equation to obtain
$$
\boxed{u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\frac1\rho\nabla p+g.}
$$
For small <perturbations> of the uniform resting barotropic state, write $\rho=\rho_0+\rho'$, $p=p_0+c_0^2\rho'+\cdots$. Linearized mass and momentum are
$$
\rho'_t+\rho_0\nabla\cdot u=0,\qquad
u_t=-\frac{c_0^2}{\rho_0}\nabla\rho',\qquad\boxed{c_0^2=P'(\rho_0)>0.}
$$
Taking the <curl> shows that initially vanishing <vorticity> remains zero, so the acoustic <velocity> has a potential $u=\nabla\phi$. Integrating momentum, using the <additive freedom of a velocity potential> to remove the spatially constant integration term, gives $\rho'=-(\rho_0/c_0^2)\phi_t$. Continuity now yields $\boxed{\phi_{tt}=c_0^2\nabla^2\phi}$.

For <spherical symmetry>, set $\phi=e^{-i\omega t}F(r)$ and $k=\omega/c_0$. The radial equation is $(r^2F')'+k^2r^2F=0$, or $(rF)''+k^2(rF)=0$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\phi=\frac{e^{-i\omega t}}r[A\cos kr+B\sin kr].}
$$
Rigid boundaries require radial <velocity> $F'=0$ at both radii. A form automatically satisfying the condition at $a$ is
$$
F(r)=\frac C r\left[\cos k(r-a)+\frac1{ka}\sin k(r-a)\right].
$$
At $b$, the condition $b(rF)'-(rF)=0$ becomes
$$
k(b-a)\cos k(b-a)-(1+k^2ab)\sin k(b-a)=0.
$$
Therefore the nonzero oscillatory eigenfrequencies obey
$$
\boxed{(1+k^2ab)\tan k(b-a)=k(b-a),\qquad\omega=kc_0.}
$$
The undivided <sine>-<cosine> form avoids artificial issues from dividing by a <cosine>. The formal $k=0$ root is the spatially constant zero-frequency potential <additive freedom of a velocity potential>, not a propagating acoustic oscillation. This is the <radial acoustic spectrum between rigid concentric spheres>.

= Solution
{parent=6}

For a vector $a$ described in axes rotating at constant <angular velocity> $\Omega$, the <rotating-axes theorem> is $(da/dt)_{\rm inertial}=(da/dt)_{\rm rotating}+\Omega\times a$. Inertial particle <velocity> is $u+\Omega\times x$. Applying the theorem once more gives inertial acceleration $Du/Dt+2\Omega\times u+\Omega\times(\Omega\times x)$. Thus the rotating momentum equation is
$$
\boxed{\frac{Du}{Dt}+2\Omega\times u+\Omega\times(\Omega\times x)=-\frac1\rho\nabla p+g.}
$$
If $g=-\nabla\Phi$ and <density> is constant, the centrifugal term can be absorbed into the <reduced pressure in a rotating fluid>:
$$
\boxed{P=p+\rho\Phi-\frac\rho2|\Omega\times x|^2,\qquad
u_t+(u\cdot\nabla)u+2\Omega\times u=-\nabla P/\rho.}
$$
For small disturbances with $\Omega=\Omega e_z$, linearize to $u_t-2\Omega v=-P_x/\rho$, $v_t+2\Omega u=-P_y/\rho$, $w_t=-P_z/\rho$, together with <incompressibility>. Put $\zeta=v_x-u_y$. Taking the vertical <curl> gives $\zeta_t=2\Omega w_z$. Taking divergence gives $\nabla^2P=2\rho\Omega\zeta$. Applying the Laplacian to the vertical momentum equation therefore gives $\partial_t\nabla^2w=-2\Omega\zeta_z$. Another time <derivative> yields
$$
\boxed{\partial_t^2\nabla^2w+4\Omega^2w_{zz}=0.}
$$
All three <velocity> components are retained, even for fields independent of $y$; rotation produces the transverse component.

Set $m=\pi/h$ and seek $w=ae^{-i\omega t}W(x)\sin mz$. The rigid-wall conditions are then automatic and the scalar equation gives
$$
W''+m^2\left(\frac{4\Omega^2}{\omega^2}-1\right)W=0,\qquad W(0)=1.
$$
For $0<|\omega|<2|\Omega|$, define $q=m\sqrt{4\Omega^2/\omega^2-1}$. The two possible oscillatory factors are distinguished by energy propagation. With horizontal <wavenumber> $k$, the <dispersion relation> is $\omega^2=4\Omega^2m^2/(k^2+m^2)$ and $c_{g,x}=-\omega k/(k^2+m^2)$. An outgoing wave in $x>0$ must have $\omega k<0$, giving
$$
\boxed{w=ae^{-i\omega t-i\operatorname{sgn}(\omega)qx}\sin(\pi z/h).}
$$
For $|\omega|>2|\Omega|$, define $\kappa=m\sqrt{1-4\Omega^2/\omega^2}$. Reject the exponentially growing branch, leaving the evanescent response
$$
\boxed{w=ae^{-i\omega t-\kappa x}\sin(\pi z/h).}
$$
The real part is the physical <velocity>. The radiation or bounded-decay condition is needed in addition to the wavemaker boundary value to pick a unique half-channel response. Steady forcing ($\omega=0$) is a singular limit: with nonzero rotation a steady <geostrophic balance> and impermeable top/bottom require $w=0$, incompatible with a nonzero imposed <sine> profile. At the cutoff equality, the bounded outgoing polarization also degenerates. These exceptional cases cannot be obtained by blindly substituting into the two strict-inequality formulas. The propagating sign is the <radiation sign for an inertial wave>.

= Solution
{parent=7}

Long-wave geometry makes vertical acceleration small, so <pressure> is approximately hydrostatic, $p=p_{\rm atm}+\rho g(h-z)$. The leading horizontal <velocity> is nearly depth-independent. Integrating <incompressibility> through the depth and using the impermeable bed and kinematic free-surface condition gives $\boxed{h_t+(hu)_x=0}$. The horizontal <Euler equation> then gives $\boxed{u_t+uu_x+gh_x=0}$.

Linearizing about a uniform resting layer gives wave speed $\boxed{c=\sqrt{gh}}$. Since $h=c^2/g$, the nonlinear equations can be written $c_t+uc_x+(c/2)u_x=0$ and $u_t+uu_x+2cc_x=0$. Combining them verifies
$$
\boxed{[\partial_t+(u\pm c)\partial_x](u\pm2c)=0.}
$$
Let $c_0=\sqrt{gh_0}$ and $V\geq0$ be the receding wall's speed, so its position is $x=-Vt$ and its fluid <velocity> condition is $u=-V$. The outward-moving rarefaction has the other invariant fixed at its resting value, $u-2c=-2c_0$. In its varying part, <self-similarity> gives $x/t=u+c$. Therefore
$$
\boxed{c=\frac{x/t+2c_0}{3},\qquad u=\frac23(x/t-c_0).}
$$
The wall-adjacent state has $u=-V$ and $c=c_w=c_0-V/2$. Its right-going <characteristic curve> speed is $c_0-3V/2$, whereas the undisturbed head travels at $c_0$. The complete solution in the fluid $x>-Vt$ is
$$
\boxed{(u,c)=\begin{cases}
(-V,c_0-V/2),&-V<x/t<c_0-3V/2,\\
(\tfrac23(x/t-c_0),\tfrac13(x/t+2c_0)),&c_0-3V/2\leq x/t\leq c_0,\\
(0,c_0),&x/t>c_0.
\end{cases}}
$$
Both edges match continuously and the wall <velocity> condition holds. The assumption $V^2<4gh_0$ means $V<2c_0$, giving positive wall depth and placing the fan tail to the right of the wall by speed $c_w>0$. The depth everywhere is $h=c^2/g$. This is a <shallow-water rarefaction from a receding wall>.

= Solution
{parent=8}

Work in the rest frame of the planar <shock>, with nonzero <mass flux> $j=\rho u$. <Conservation of mass> gives $[j]=0$; the tangential and normal components of <balance of linear momentum> give $[jv]=0$ and $[p+ju]=0$. Thus $[v]=0$. For an <ideal gas>, <specific internal energy> is $e=c_vT=p/[(\gamma-1)\rho]$, and <specific enthalpy> is $e+p/\rho=\gamma p/[(\gamma-1)\rho]$. Total energy flux is $j[(u^2+v^2)/2+e+p/\rho]$. Its continuity, with $v$ already continuous, gives
$$
\boxed{[\rho u]=[v]=[p+\rho u^2]=\left[\frac{u^2}2+\frac{\gamma p}{(\gamma-1)\rho}\right]=0.}
$$
Put $c^2=\gamma p/\rho$ and $M=u/c$. Since $p=ju/(\gamma M^2)$, the normal component of <balance of linear momentum> says $u[1+1/(\gamma M^2)]$ is constant. Hence
$$
\boxed{\frac{u_+}{u_-}=\frac{M_+^2(1+\gamma M_-^2)}{M_-^2(1+\gamma M_+^2)}.}
$$
The energy equation says $u^2[1/2+1/((\gamma-1)M^2)]$ is constant, giving
$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{u_+}{u_-}\right)^2=\frac{M_+^2[2+(\gamma-1)M_-^2]}{M_-^2[2+(\gamma-1)M_+^2]}.}
$$
Set $a=M_-^2$, $b=M_+^2$, square the first relation and equate to the second. After clearing positive denominators the equation factors as
$$
\boxed{(b-a)\{b[2\gamma a-(\gamma-1)]-[2+(\gamma-1)a]\}=0.}
$$
The first factor is the unchanged state. For a genuine <shock> the second factor vanishes, yielding
$$
\boxed{M_+^2=\frac{2+(\gamma-1)M_-^2}{2\gamma M_-^2-(\gamma-1)}.}
$$
The physical selection principle is <entropy> increase in the direction of flow. For $u_\pm>0$, the upstream side is $x<0$ and the downstream side is $x>0$. A compressive <entropy>-admissible <shock> has $\boxed{M_->1,\quad M_+<1}$; interpreting the printed “ahead” and “behind” spatial labels must respect these explicit side assignments. The expansion-<shock> branch would violate the second law.

Substitution gives the <velocity> ratio
$$
\boxed{\frac{u_+}{u_-}=\frac{2+(\gamma-1)M_-^2}{(\gamma+1)M_-^2}<1.}
$$
Since $v$ is unchanged while the normal component decreases, $|v|/u$ increases. Thus an oblique flow bends **towards the <shock> plane**, equivalently away from its normal. If $v=0$ there is no angular bending; the flow stays normal and simply slows. The tangential continuity, not a change in the tangential speed, causes the deflection.
