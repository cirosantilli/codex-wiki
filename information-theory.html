Bit (b∈{0,1})

Words: 48
Information theory OurBigBook.com
A bit is a binary classical symbol with two distinguishable values, zero and one. A uniform random bit has one bit of information entropy, while a biased binary symbol has less. Sending one bit means transmitting one symbol from this binary alphabet; the mutual information it carries about a particular input need not be one.

Memoryless classical information source (p(un)=∏j=1n​p(uj​))

Words: 49
Information theory OurBigBook.com
A memoryless classical source emits independent and identically distributed random variables from a fixed finite alphabet. Its block probability mass function factors as shown, and its information per symbol is the Shannon entropy H(U)=−∑u​p(u)log2​p(u). Zero-probability symbols never occur and contribute zero to the entropy. The typical sequence theorem describes the size and probability of the high-probability block set used for reliable compression.

Chain rule for conditional entropy (H(X,Z∣Y)=H(X∣Y)+H(Z∣X,Y))

Words: 43
Conditional entropy OurBigBook.com
For classical finite random variables, H(X,Z∣Y)=H(X∣Y)+H(Z∣X,Y)=H(Z∣Y)+H(X∣Z,Y). Each identity follows by inserting the corresponding joint entropy into H(X,Z,Y)−H(Y). The two orders are particularly useful when one variable is a deterministic function of the others, as in Fano's inequality via an error indicator.

Conditional entropy under a deterministic change of variables

Words: 39
Conditional entropy OurBigBook.com
If f(x,z) is a bijection of x for every fixed z, then
H(f(X,Z)∣Z)=H(X∣Z).
(3)
In particular, translating a random variable in a finite group by a value determined by the conditioning variable does not change its conditional entropy.

Fano's inequality

Words: 181 Articles: 2
Conditional entropy OurBigBook.com
Let X take values in an alphabet of size m, let X be an estimate determined by Y, and put pe​=P(X=X). Then
H(X∣Y)≤h2​(pe​)+pe​log2​(m−1),
(4)
where h2​ is the binary entropy function. The error indicator costs at most h2​(pe​) bits, and after an error there are at most m−1 possible values of X.

Fano's inequality via an error indicator (H(X∣Y)≤h(pe​)+pe​log2​(∣JX​∣−1))

Words: 62
Fano's inequality OurBigBook.com
For a guess f(Y) of a finite-alphabet variable X, let Z=1X=f(Y)​ and pe​=P(Z=1). The chain rule for conditional entropy gives H(X∣Y)=H(Z∣Y)+H(X∣Z,Y). The first term is at most the binary entropy h(pe​). The second is zero when the guess is right and at most log2​(∣JX​∣−1) when it is wrong, proving H(X∣Y)≤h(pe​)+pe​log2​(∣JX​∣−1). This needs no optimality assumption on the guess.

List-decoding Fano inequality (pe​≥1−{I(J;Y)+log2}/log(N/B))

Words: 60
Fano's inequality OurBigBook.com
If a uniform index J has N possibilities and every proposed success neighbourhood contains at most B<N possibilities, then any estimator has failure probability at least the displayed expression. Condition on its success indicator: conditional entropy is at most log2+(1−pe​)logB+pe​logN. Subtract this from logN to bound the mutual information and rearrange. A metric Hamming ball gives approximate recovery rather than exact index decoding.

Compression of an entropy sum

Words: 162 Articles: 2
Entropy submodularity OurBigBook.com
A union-intersection compression can only decrease the sum of information entropies ∑A∈A​H(XA​) for a finite-valued random vector X. Each elementary step follows from entropy submodularity; iteration proves the assertion with multiset multiplicities. Uniform coordinate multiplicity gives Shearer inequality by compressing to repeated full sets and empty sets.
Balister–Bollobás entropy inequality
Words: 123 Articles: 1

Let A be a finite multiset of subsets and let ri​ count its occurrences of coordinate i. Its nested compression consists of Lj​={i:ri​≥j}. Then ∑j​H(XLj​​)≤∑A∈A​H(XA​), by repeated entropy submodularity. This is the compression formulation in Balister and Bollobás, "Projections, Entropy and Sumsets".
Madiman–Tetali entropy inequality
Words: 75
Balister–Bollobás entropy inequality OurBigBook.com
For a nonempty ordered coordinate set A, write A−=[minA−1] and A+=[maxA−1]∖A. An exact k-cover satisfies ∑A​H(XA​∣XA+​)≤kH(X)≤∑A​H(XA​∣XA−​). A short proof uses the chain rule for information entropy contributions hi​=H(Xi​∣X[i−1]​): each conditional subset entropy bounds ∑i∈A​hi​ on the appropriate side, by conditioning reduces entropy. Weighting gives the lower inequality for a fractional packing and the upper inequality for a fractional cover. See Madiman and Tetali, "Information Inequalities for Joint Distributions".

Shearer's inequality

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Entropy submodularity OurBigBook.com
Let X=(X1​,…,Xn​) be a discrete random vector, and let S be a collection of subsets of [n] in which every index occurs at least r times. Then
rH(X)≤∑S∈S​H(XS​).
(8)
Apply the chain rule for information entropy to each projection XS​ and use conditioning reduces entropy to compare every term with the corresponding conditional entropy in the full chain rule.
Entropy bound for graphs with isolated-vertex-free intersections (∣F∣≤2n(n−2)/2)
Words: 52
Shearer's inequality OurBigBook.com
If every pair of graphs in a family on [n] has a graph intersection without an isolated vertex, the family has at most 2n(n−2)/2 members. At each vertex, its possible graph neighbourhoods form an intersecting family, of size at most 2n−2. Every edge occurs in two such neighbourhood projections. Shearer inequality bounds twice the full information entropy by the sum of their information entropies.

Conditioned entropic Ruzsa distance of a summand

Words: 54

(17)
Indeed, conditioning reduces entropy and independence give
dR​(U∣U+V;X)≤H(U+X)−21​H(U)−21​H(V)+21​H(U+V)−21​H(X).
(18)

Entropic Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem

Words: 66
Entropic Ruzsa distance OurBigBook.com
For finitely supported random variables A,B in an abelian group,
dR​(A;B∥A+B)≤3I(A;B)+2H(A+B)−H(A)−H(B).
(23)
To prove it, take two conditionally independent copies (A1​,B1​) and (A2​,B2​) of (A,B) given A+B. Since A1​+B1​=A2​+B2​, entropy submodularity gives
H(A1​−B2​)≤H(A1​−B2​,A1​)+H(A1​−B2​,B1​)−H(A1​−B2​,A1​,B1​).
(24)
The first two terms are at most H(A)+H(B). The last joint entropy is
2H(A,B)−H(A+B).
(25)
Consequently H(A1​−B2​)≤H(A+B)+2I(A;B). Subtracting
H(A∣A+B)=H(B∣A+B)=H(A)+H(B)−I(A;B)−H(A+B)
(26)
from this bound proves the theorem.

Cyclic ternary channel capacity (C=log2​3−h2​(q))

Words: 75
Weakly symmetric channel capacity OurBigBook.com
A discrete memoryless channel on the additive group Z/3Z maps x to x with probability 1−q and to x+1 with probability q. Every input gives the same conditional entropy h2​(q), whereas the output Shannon entropy is at most log2​3. A uniform input produces a uniform output since every column of the transition matrix sums to one. Hence the channel capacity is log2​3−h2​(q) bits per use. This cyclic-error channel differs from a channel distributing its error probability equally over both other output symbols.

Entropy monotonicity under independent addition

Words: 46

The weak law of large numbers applied to −log2​p(Uj​) proves its probability tends to one. Every typical word has probability between 2−n(H+ε) and 2−n(H−ε). Summing the lower bound gives the displayed cardinality upper bound; if the set has probability at least 1−δ, summing the upper bound gives cardinality at least (1−δ)2n(H−ε).
To compress at any rate R>H(U), choose 0<ε<R−H(U), assign one fixed-length binary index to each typical word, and reserve one additional index for all atypical words. At most ⌈nR⌉ bits suffice for all large n. Decoding inverts the typical-word indices and makes an arbitrary output for the reserved index. The reconstruction error is at most the probability of the atypical set and tends to zero.
