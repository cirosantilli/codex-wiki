Rutherford impact-parameter relation (b=2Eκ​cot2θ​)

 Home Physics Atomic physics Rutherford scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 133
Let θ be the scattering angle between incoming and outgoing velocities, so forward scattering is θ=0 and exact reversal is θ=π. Define the length a=κ/(2E). Then
b=acot2θ​​,θ=2arctanba​.
(65)
For b>0, this gives 0<θ<π; b=0 is the head-on limiting case θ=π. Large deflections select small impact parameters, rather than requiring every incident alpha particle to reverse.
A short derivation uses conservation of angular momentum and conservation of energy. Choose the polar angle ϕ=0 at closest approach, and write u=1/r. The Binet equation for the repulsive Coulomb potential gives
dϕ2d2u​+u=−L2mκ​,u=L2mκ​(ϵcosϕ−1),ϵ2=1+mκ22EL2​.
(66)
The last equality follows by substituting this orbit into E=mr˙2/2+L2/(2mr2)+κ/r. The hyperbola reaches infinity at ϕ=±ϕ∞​, with cosϕ∞​=1/ϵ. Its scattering angle is θ=π−2ϕ∞​, hence
cot2θ​=ϵ2−1​=κ2E/m​L​=κ2Eb​.
(67)
This proves the relation without assuming that the deflection is small. The symbol ϵ here is a dimensionless orbital parameter, distinct from ε0​.
