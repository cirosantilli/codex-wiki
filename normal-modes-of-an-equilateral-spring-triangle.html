Normal modes of an equilateral spring triangle (ω2=0,0,0,3k/(2m),3k/(2m),3k/m)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Lagrangian mechanics Small oscillation Generalized eigenvalue problem for small oscillations
OurBigBook.com Words: 189
Three equal point masses m>0 move in a plane and are connected pairwise by ideal springs of equal positive stiffness k. At an unstretched equilateral triangle equilibrium, let ri​ be the vertex positions relative to the centre, and let ui​ be small displacements. If eij​=(ri​−rj​)/∣ri​−rj​∣, the quadratic potential energy is (k/2)∑i<j​[eij​⋅(ui​−uj​)]2, and the kinetic energy is (m/2)∑i​∣u˙i​∣2.
There are three independent zero-frequency motions: two common translations and the infinitesimal rigid rotation ui​=Jri​, where J(x,y)=(−y,x). The uniform radial motion ui​=ri​ is the breathing normal mode, with ω2=3k/m. The two shear modes are ui​=(rix​,−riy​) and ui​=(riy​,rix​), each with ω2=3k/(2m).
For a compact proof of the spectrum, form the 3×6 matrix B taking the six displacement components to the three first-order spring extensions, orienting the three edges cyclically. Its row norms squared are two, and every pair of rows has inner product 1/2. Thus BBT=(3/2)I+(1/2)11T, with eigenvalues 3,3/2,3/2. The nonzero eigenvalues of BTB are the same, so the stiffness matrix kBTB has the three positive values in the title and a three-dimensional kernel of a linear map. The three rigid motions span that kernel. A zero mode has time factor A+Bt rather than a nonconstant periodic oscillation.
