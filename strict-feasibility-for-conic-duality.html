Strict feasibility for conic duality

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Convex optimization Conic optimization Conic dual problem
OurBigBook.com Words: 197
For a proper cone K, suppose a point satisfies Ax−b∈intK and p∗​ is finite. This generalized Slater condition gives strong duality and attainment of the conic dual problem's optimum. It does not by itself assert primal attainment.[ref]
The two worked examples have strict primal points: (u,v)=(2,2) has slack (2,2,1) in the interior of the nonnegative orthant, and (t,z)=(2,0) has slack (2,1,0) in the interior of the second-order cone. Both optima were already certified by explicit feasible pairs. Slater's condition explains why a matching optimal dual variable can be expected under these hypotheses; checking a supplied zero-gap pair needs only weak duality.
Changing cone geometry preserves the bound mechanism. For a positive semidefinite cone, the dual cone pairing is the Frobenius inner product of two matrices. This connects the same certificate idea to semidefinite programming, while the central path uses strictly interior primal and dual points to approach vanishing gap.
In 1995, Lieven Vandenberghe and Stephen Boyd presented a primal-dual interior-point method for convex optimization with matrix inequalities. Their algorithmic study included exploiting problem structure and allowing incomplete Conjugate gradient method solves within an iteration. This illustrates how the primal-dual viewpoint guides computation as well as checking a final answer.[ref]
