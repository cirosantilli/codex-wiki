Gravity deflection of an atomic beam (y=gL2/(2v2))

 Home Physics Experimental apparatus Molecular beam
OurBigBook.com Words: 358
Immanuel Estermann, O. C. Simpson and Otto Stern used gravitational deflection to measure atomic speeds. Stern describes scanning a hot-wire surface ionization detector through a cesium beam and recording ion electric current.[ref] Their 1947 paper reports potassium and cesium beams; a deficit of slow arrivals increased with oven pressure, supporting a source-slit collision explanation.[ref]
First consider an original point-launch model, with horizontal speed v>0, zero initial vertical velocity, flight distance L, and uniform gravitational acceleration g. Downward displacement y follows from flight time t=L/v:
y=2v2gL2​=v2C​,C=2gL2​.
(30)
Slow particles fall farther. However, a detector scan counts arrivals per interval of height, not per interval of speed. Change of variables for probability densities supplies the Jacobian determinant:
py​(y)=g(C/y​)2y3/2C​​=y3b2​e−b/y,b=aC=4kB​TmgL2​,y>0.
(31)
Here g(v) denotes the effusive beam speed distribution; the unadorned constant g in C denotes gravitational acceleration. The integral of py​ is one: substituting u=b/y reduces it to ∫0∞​ue−udu=1. Its mode is y=b/3. Simply mapping the most probable arrival speed into a fall distance instead gives 2b/3. The peaks differ because equal height bins cover unequal speed intervals.
For those chosen parameters, b≃176.54μm, so the detector-height probability density peaks near 58.85μm. A detector covering only 0<y<Y accepts the fraction
F(Y)=∫0Y​py​(y)dy=e−b/Y(1+Yb​).
(32)
At Y=0.80mm this is about 97.9%, even before allowing for imperfect detection. Discarding the remainder changes the observed normalization.
Real slits transmit a range of launch heights and vertical velocities. Their geometrical response must be combined with the gravitational deflection and detector width; the point-launch formula is not an exact reduction of the historical apparatus. In the point-launch model, an initial vertical velocity w would add wL/v to y. Source collisions, detector efficiency and alignment can also distort a profile. Varying oven pressure helps distinguish such source losses from an incorrect thermal distribution; inferring a source temperature from the peak alone ignores both selection and the detector's coordinate transformation.
