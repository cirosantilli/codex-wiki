Suppose disjoint nonempty groups have sizes nj​ and arithmetic means xˉj​. The arithmetic mean of all observations is
xˉ=∑j​nj​∑j​nj​xˉj​​.​
(23)
Each group contributes the sum nj​xˉj​; add those totals before dividing by the overall count. This is exactly a weighted mean of group means with weights equal to group sizes. [ref]
Take a synthetic example with two observations equal to 90 and eight equal to 60. The two group means are 90 and 60, and the combined mean is 66. Giving the two groups equal weights instead produces (90+60)/2=75, which summarizes the two group means with equal influence. To summarize all ten individuals with equal influence, use their group counts.
The group means and counts determine the combined arithmetic mean, but do not generally determine the combined variance. Variation within the groups is additional information.
