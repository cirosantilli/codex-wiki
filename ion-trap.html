Ion trap

 Home Physics Atomic physics Atomic measurement methods
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 23
An ion trap confines an ion using electromagnetic fields, allowing repeated interrogation of the same particle. The name also covers related traps for an electron. A Paul trap uses an oscillating electric quadrupole field; a Penning trap combines a static electric quadrupole field with a static magnetic field. Confinement creates time to measure an energy level, frequency or magnetic dipole moment; it does not itself cool the particle or identify its internal quantum state.
The obstacle is Earnshaw's theorem. In a charge-free neighbourhood the static electric potential Φ obeys Laplace's equation, ∇2Φ=0. For a particle of fixed nonzero electric charge Q, the potential energy QΦ is also a harmonic function. The maximum principle for harmonic functions excludes a strict interior minimum. For a quadratic example, the Hessian matrix has zero trace; it cannot have three positive eigenvalues. A static purely electric field therefore cannot supply three-dimensional stable confinement of a free point electric charge. Time variation in a Paul trap, and the velocity-dependent Lorentz force in a Penning trap, change the assumptions of this argument.
