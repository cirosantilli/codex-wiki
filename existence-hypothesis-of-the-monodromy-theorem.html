Existence hypothesis of the monodromy theorem

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Holomorphic function Analytic continuation Monodromy theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 175
The monodromy theorem gives equality of continuations that exist under its hypothesis. It does not assert that every holomorphic function admits analytic continuation everywhere, even within a simply connected domain.
For an explicit obstruction, take f(z)=1/(1−z) near a=0 and attempt to apply the theorem on D=C. The path η(t)=t ends at 1, where no terminal germ of a holomorphic function exists. If a holomorphic representative G near 1 continued f, then the identity theorem would extend (1−z)G(z)=1 to that neighbourhood. Substituting z=1 would give 0=1. Thus the pole prevents the existence hypothesis, although C is a simply connected domain. The analytic continuation article explains why the same rational function does continue throughout C∖{1}.
Existence at an endpoint by some route is also weaker than existence along every path from the base point. Along the real segment from 1 towards 0, a holomorphic square root of z tends to 0, yet cannot continue holomorphically through 0: a local representative G would satisfy G2=z by the identity theorem, and differentiating at 0 would give 2G(0)G′(0)=1 with G(0)=0. A finite limiting value therefore does not remove a branch point. More severe obstructions appear at a natural boundary of a holomorphic function.
