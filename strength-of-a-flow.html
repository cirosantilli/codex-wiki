Strength of a flow (∣f∣)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Flow network Flow
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
The strength of a flow, also called its flow value, is net outflow from s, equivalently net inflow to t:
∣f∣=∑v:(s,v)∈E​fsv​−∑u:(u,s)∈E​fus​=∑u:(u,t)∈E​fut​−∑v:(t,v)∈E​ftv​.
(14)
The notation ∣f∣ denotes this signed value, rather than an absolute value.[ref]
The equality follows by summing the divergence of a flow over all vertices: the internal vertices contribute zero by flow conservation, while every edge cancels between its two endpoints. Hence divf(s)=−divf(t). For the running flow, ∣f∣=3+2=2+3=5.
Net outflow matters even when the source has incoming edges. As a separate example, give s→a, a→s and s→t flow network edge capacities 2,2,3 and flow amounts 2,2,3. At a, flow conservation reads 2=2. The source sends 5 units along outgoing edges but receives 2 back, so the flow value is 5−2=3, exactly the amount arriving at t. The two-edge network circulation through a raises total edge usage without increasing net delivery.
