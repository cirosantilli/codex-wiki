An element a is a prime element when it is nonzero and not a unit, and a∣bc implies a∣b or a∣c. If a=bc and a is a prime element, assume for example b=ad. Cancellation in a=adc gives dc=1, so c is a unit. This proves every prime element is an irreducible element.
In a Euclidean domain, each nonzero ideal has a generator of least Euclidean value: division of another member by that generator leaves a remainder in the ideal of smaller value, so the remainder must vanish. Let a be an irreducible element and suppose a∣bc but a∤b. Write (a,b)=(d). Since d∣a, irreducibility says d is a unit or is associate to a. The second case would give a∣b, so d is a unit. Hence 1=ra+sb for some r,s. Multiplying by c shows a∣c. Thus irreducibles in a Euclidean domain are prime.
For R=Z[i7​], use the multiplicative integer field norm N(m+ni7​)=m2+7n2. Units have field norm one and are exactly ±1. If 2=uv were a product of nonunits, their norms would be integers greater than one multiplying to four, hence both would have field norm two. But m2+7n2=2 has no integer solution: n=0 makes the left side at least seven, and n=0 would give m2=2. Therefore 2 is an irreducible element.
