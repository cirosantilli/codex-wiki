With parameter t along a characteristic curve, the equations are
dtdx​=y,dtdy​=1,dtdu​=u,(x,y,u)∣t=0​=(s,0,s).
(23)
Their solutions are y=t, x=s+t2/2 and u=set. Thus the projected curve satisfies y2=2(x−s), as required. Eliminating the parameters gives
u(x,y)=(x−2y2​)ey.​
(24)
Indeed ux​=ey and uy​=ey(x−y2/2−y), so yux​+uy​=u, and the initial values hold.
The parameter map (s,t)↦(s+t2/2,t) has Jacobian one and is globally invertible. Every point whose characteristic curve meets the initial segment therefore lies in the strip
1≤x−2y2​≤2,−∞<y<∞.​
(25)
The uniquely determined open region is its interior, with the boundary values determined on the two endpoint characteristic curves. Both signs of y are included. Outside the strip, a general solution is u=eyF(x−y2/2) with F(s)=s only required for 1≤s≤2. Smooth perturbations of F supported outside that interval produce different solutions with the same prescribed data, proving the stated region is exact.
