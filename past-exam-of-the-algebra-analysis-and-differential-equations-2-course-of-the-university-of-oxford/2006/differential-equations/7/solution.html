Assume the vector field and multiplier are continuously differentiable, as required for Green's theorem and uniqueness of trajectories. A nonconstant periodic trajectory of a planar autonomous system is a simple closed orbit: uniqueness prevents crossings, and one traversal can be taken over its least period. Let Ω be its bounded interior. Along the orbit dx=fdt, dy=gdt, so
∮∂Ω​ϕfdy−ϕgdx=∫ϕ(fg−gf)dt=0.
(19)
Green's theorem identifies this with ∬Ω​[∂x​(ϕf)+∂y​(ϕg)]dxdy, which is strictly positive by hypothesis, a contradiction. Thus there is no nonconstant periodic orbit. This standard periodic-orbit criterion does not by itself exclude stationary solutions.
For the first concrete system choose ϕ=−1. Then
∂x​(ϕf)+∂y​(ϕg)=e−x>0.
(20)
There are also no equilibria: f=0 requires y=1, but then g=−e−x−x2<0. Consequently it has no periodic solution, including constant ones.
For the second system the origin is outside the domain of the printed vector field. For r>0,
r˙=rxx˙+yy˙​​=1−r,θ˙=r2xy˙​−yx˙​=1.
(21)
Thus for an admissible initial point with ρ=a2+b2​>0,
r(t)=1+(ρ−1)e−t,θ(t)=α+t,​
(22)
where a=ρcosα, b=ρsinα. These formulas are valid while r>0, in particular for every t≥0. Trajectories rotate counterclockwise, spiral outward when 0<r<1, and inward when r>1, approaching the unit circle. On r=1 they give x(t)=cos(α+t), y(t)=sin(α+t), a periodic orbit with least period 2π. It is an attracting limit cycle. There is no contradiction with the preceding criterion: this vector field is singular at the origin and has the exhibited closed orbit.
