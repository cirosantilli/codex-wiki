Use ordinary matrix addition and multiplication on R=K[A]. Sums and products of polynomials in A remain such polynomials, and they commute because powers of the same matrix commute. The identity is A0=I. Evaluation ϕ(∑ci​Xi)=∑ci​Ai is a surjective unital algebra homomorphism over K. It is unique among maps fixing the scalar matrices and sending X to A.
The scalar condition is needed for the word “unique”: as an unrestricted ring-homomorphism assertion over an arbitrary field, prescribing only the image of X does not suffice. For example, over C with a real scalar matrix A, conjugating every coefficient gives another unital ring homomorphism with the same image of X. The intended polynomial-evaluation map is the K-algebra map just defined.
The kernel of a ring homomorphism is an ideal of the principal ideal domain K[X]. It is nonzero because finitely many powers of A must become linearly dependent in the finite-dimensional matrix space. Its unique monic generator is precisely the minimal polynomial m: division by m shows that a polynomial killing A has zero remainder, by minimality. Hence kerϕ=(m) and R≅K[X]/(m).
If gcd(f,m)=1, Bézout's identity gives uf+vm=1; evaluation gives u(A)f(A)=I. Conversely an inverse in R has form g(A), so fg−1∈(m), which forces gcd(f,m)=1. Thus invertibility in this ring is exactly coprimality with the minimal polynomial.
