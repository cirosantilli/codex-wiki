For t>0, integrate eitz/(1+z2) over the upper semicircle of radius R. The arc tends to zero, since ∣eitz∣≤1 there and the denominator is of order R2. The sole pole is i, of residue e−t/(2i), so the real part gives I(t)=πe−t. The integrand defining I is even in t, and I(0)=π by the arctangent integral. Hence
I(t)=πe−∣t∣(t∈R).​
(11)
For J use eitz/[z(1+z2)] with a small clockwise semicircular indentation above zero. Its residue at zero is one, so the small arc tends to −iπ. The pole i has residue −e−t/2, and the large arc again vanishes. Thus
PV∫−∞∞​x(1+x2)eitx​dx−iπ=−iπe−t.
(12)
The imaginary part is the ordinary convergent integral J(t): its apparent singularity at zero is removable, with limit t, and its tails are absolutely integrable. Therefore J(t)=π(1−e−t) for t>0. Oddness in t and the value zero at t=0 give
J(t)=⎩⎨⎧​π(1−e−t),0,−π(1−et),​t>0,t=0,t<0.​​
(13)
Differentiation is valid at every real t, including zero. Indeed the mean value theorem bounds the absolute difference quotient of sin(tx)/[x(1+x2)] with respect to t by 1/(1+x2), an integrable majorant independent of t. Dominated convergence therefore gives J′(t)=I(t) for all t∈R​. In particular both one-sided derivatives at zero equal π.
