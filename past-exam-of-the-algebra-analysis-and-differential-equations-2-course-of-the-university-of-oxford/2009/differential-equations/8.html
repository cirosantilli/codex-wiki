For the particular equation, write Dx​=x∂x​ and Dy​=y∂y​. Its left side is (Dx2​−Dy2​)u. Choose ϕ=xy and ψ=x/y; these are smooth invertible coordinates in the positive quadrant, with inverse x=ϕψ​, y=ϕ/ψ​. In them Dx​=ϕ∂ϕ​+ψ∂ψ​ and Dy​=ϕ∂ϕ​−ψ∂ψ​. Subtraction of their squares leaves 4ϕψuϕψ​, so the canonical equation is uϕψ​=0. Integrating it gives u=F(ϕ)+G(ψ).
Parameterize the data curve by (x,y)=(s,s2), s≥1. Its coordinate values are (ϕ,ψ)=(s3,s−1), so
F(s3)+G(s−1)=s6+s−1,s2F′(s3)+s−2G′(s−1)=2s5+s−2.
(25)
Differentiate the first identity along the curve to get 3s2F′(s3)−s−2G′(s−1)=6s5−s−2. Adding gives F′(s3)=2s3, and then G′(s−1)=1. Thus F(ϕ)=ϕ2+C for ϕ≥1 and G(ψ)=ψ−C for 0<ψ≤1. Therefore
u(x,y)=x2y2+x/ywhere xy≥1, x/y≤1.​
(26)
The coordinate rectangle gives exactly the region
x>0,y≥max(x,1/x).​
(27)
Its boundary consists of xy=1 up to (1,1) and the ray y=x from that point onward. It is unbounded upward. The original data curve lies inside this region. The sketch displays both characteristic boundaries and that data arc.
To establish the exact extent of uniqueness, outside this region either ϕ<1 or ψ>1. A smooth function supported in that unprescribed coordinate range can be added to F or G without changing either datum on the arc. Hence the displayed global formula is one extension, but data alone do not uniquely determine values outside the shaded region. Boundary values are fixed by continuity.
