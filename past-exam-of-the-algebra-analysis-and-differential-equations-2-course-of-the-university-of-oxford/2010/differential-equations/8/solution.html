Use a parameter t. The characteristic equations are
x˙=x+cy−bz,y˙​=y+az−cx,z˙=z+bx−ay.​
(33)
For a graph of a function z=f(x,y) satisfying the equation, solve the first two equations after substituting z=f(x,y). The chain rule and the partial differential equation give df(x(t),y(t))/dt=f+bx−ay, exactly the third equation. Uniqueness for the resulting linear ordinary differential equation system shows that its characteristic through the initial point agrees with this curve on the graph of a function. This proves invariance as long as the graph of a function remains defined along the curve. A claim about the entire curve additionally requires a surface continued along that flow; a restricted graph of a function patch alone cannot imply global continuation.
Let k=(a,b,c), ℓ=(l,m,n) and r=(x,y,z). Then r˙=r−k×r. Since ∣k∣=1,
dtd​(k⋅r)=k⋅r,dtd​∣r∣2=2∣r∣2.
(34)
Along the characteristic starting from sℓ these give k⋅r=ets(k⋅ℓ) and ∣r∣=et∣s∣. Consequently the characteristic surface generated by the given line satisfies
∣ax+by+cz∣=∣al+bm+cn∣x2+y2+z2​.​
(35)
To see that the whole displayed cone is generated, not just contained in it, decompose ℓ=κk+v with v⊥k, κ=k⋅ℓ. The full solution is
r(s,t)=set(κk+costv−sint(k×v)).
(36)
When 0<∣κ∣<1, variation of t gives all azimuths about the axis, while the freely chosen s supplies every positive radius on both halves. This is precisely the two-sided circular cone, with
vertex (0,0,0),axis R(a,b,c),semi-angle arccos∣al+bm+cn∣.​
(37)
The edge cases matter: κ=0 gives the plane ax+by+cz=0 (semi-angle π/2), and ∣κ∣=1 generates only the axis (semi-angle zero), not a two-dimensional surface. In the latter case the initial line is itself characteristic, so it does not determine a unique integral surface. The nondegenerate cone is smooth away from its vertex and can be used as a graph of a function only on patches whose tangent plane is not vertical. Thus the printed geometric conclusion has its ordinary characteristic-surface meaning in the nondegenerate case, with these domain and degeneracy qualifications; it cannot be asserted for every global smooth graph of a function without them.
