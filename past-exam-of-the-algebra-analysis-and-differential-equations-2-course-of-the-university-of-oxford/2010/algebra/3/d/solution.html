The zero ideal is a prime ideal because R is an integral domain. Each of (2),(3),(5) is a prime ideal: for p among these three, reduction a/b↦ab−1 is a surjective ring homomorphism R→Fp​ with kernel of a ring homomorphism (p), so its quotient ring is a field.
Any other nonzero proper ideal in (c) has generator d=2α3β5γ with total exponent at least two. Choose one prime factor p and put e=d/p. Then pe=d∈(d) but neither p nor e belongs to (d), as seen from the required prime exponents. Such an ideal is not prime. Thus with the standard proper-ideal convention the answer is
(0),(2),(3),(5).​
