For a nonzero principal part the discriminant Δ=b2−ac classifies the equation: it is hyperbolic for Δ>0, parabolic for Δ=0, and elliptic for Δ<0. These are the three definitions requested by the inline roman labels.
In the hyperbolic case the characteristic equation a(dy)2−2bdxdy+c(dx)2=0 gives two distinct real tangent directions. Locally, with suitably smooth coefficients, solve the corresponding characteristic ordinary differential equations to obtain two independent families of curves. Label their level sets ϕ=constant and ψ=constant. Their gradients satisfy
aϕx2​+2bϕx​ϕy​+cϕy2​=0,aψx2​+2bψx​ψy​+cψy2​=0,
(30)
and the coordinate Jacobian is nonzero. The chain rule therefore eliminates uϕϕ​ and uψψ​. The remaining mixed coefficient 2[aϕx​ψx​+b(ϕx​ψy​+ϕy​ψx​)+cϕy​ψy​] is nonzero: otherwise the invertible coordinate change would send the nondegenerate principal quadratic form to zero. Divide by this coefficient and absorb all first-order terms into g, giving uϕψ​=g(ϕ,ψ,u,uϕ​,uψ​). This canonical reduction is local; a global chart is not automatic.
