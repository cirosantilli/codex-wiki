An ideal I of a commutative ring is an additive subgroup such that rx∈I for every r∈R and x∈I. A prime ideal is a proper ideal A=R such that xy∈A implies x∈A or y∈A.
If (x+A)(y+A)=0+A in the quotient ring, then xy∈A. Primality implies x+A=0+A or y+A=0+A. Therefore there are no two nonzero cosets whose product is zero:
R/A has no nonzero zero-divisors.​
(21)
Properness also ensures its identity coset is not zero, so this quotient is an integral domain.
