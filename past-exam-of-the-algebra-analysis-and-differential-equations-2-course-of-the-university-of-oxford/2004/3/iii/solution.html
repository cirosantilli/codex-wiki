The inverse image I=θ−1(J) is an ideal, by preservation of differences and multiplication. It is proper since θ(1)=1∈/J. If ab∈I, then θ(a)θ(b)∈J; primality of J gives θ(a)∈J or θ(b)∈J, hence a∈I or b∈I. Therefore the inverse image is prime.
For maximality, take the inclusion Z↪Q and J=(0). This is a maximal ideal of the field Q, but its inverse image is (0) in Z, strictly contained in the proper ideal 2Z, so it is not maximal. Preservation of identity matters for proper inverse images: without it the zero homomorphism could pull a proper ideal back to the whole ring.
