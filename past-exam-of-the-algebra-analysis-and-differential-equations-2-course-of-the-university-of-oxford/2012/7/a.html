A critical point of a dynamical system or equilibrium is a point (a,b) with f(a,b)=g(a,b)=0, so the constant function (x,y)=(a,b) is a solution. Put u=x−a, v=y−b. For a continuously differentiable vector field, its linearization is
dtd​(uv​)=M(uv​),M=D(f,g)(a,b),
(45)
with remainder o(u2+v2​) in the original nonlinear system. For distinct eigenvalues, M is diagonalizable over C, and the linear solutions are combinations of eλj​twj​, where wj​ are corresponding eigenvectors. Real parts determine growth or decay; nonzero imaginary parts give rotation. For hyperbolic equilibria, meaning no eigenvalue has zero real part, the Hartman-Grobman theorem transfers the local stability and qualitative orbit pattern to the nonlinear system. A purely imaginary pair is not hyperbolic: it determines a linear centre, while higher-order terms decide whether the nonlinear system actually has a centre.
