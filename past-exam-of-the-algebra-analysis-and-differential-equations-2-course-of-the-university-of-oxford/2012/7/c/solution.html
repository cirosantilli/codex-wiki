The Bendixson-Dulac theorem states that if (P,Q) is a continuously differentiable planar vector field on a simply connected open region D, and a continuously differentiable Dulac function B makes
∂x∂(BP)​+∂y∂(BQ)​
(56)
have one weak sign throughout D, without vanishing identically on any nonempty open subset, then there is no nonconstant periodic orbit contained in D. To see why, a hypothetical simple periodic orbit bounds a region in D. The divergence theorem gives the integral of this expression as the outward flux of B(P,Q) across the orbit. The flux is zero because the vector field is tangent to its orbit, whereas the one-sign area integral is nonzero, a contradiction. The usual stricter version assumes a strictly positive or strictly negative expression everywhere.
For the printed polynomial system, the origin is an equilibrium and
M(0,0)=(0−1​10​),λ=±i.
(57)
Thus its linearization is a centre equilibrium. For the full system take B=1. The divergence is
Px​+Qy​=x4−3x2+x2y2=x2(x2+y2−3).
(58)
On the open disk x2+y2<3 this is nonpositive, and is negative wherever x=0. Its zero set inside the disk is a line, so it cannot vanish on any open subset. The Bendixson-Dulac theorem therefore proves
There are no nonconstant periodic orbits contained in x2+y2<3.​
(59)
This also exposes a terminology problem in the source: a nonlinear centre equilibrium has surrounding closed orbits, so the printed claim of a centre cannot hold in that sense. The imaginary eigenvalues establish only a centre of the linearized system. In fact the full equilibrium is an asymptotically stable weak focus, as the following direct check shows.
A quartic Lyapunov correction for a weak focus makes the nonlinear damping visible. Put r2=x2+y2 and consider the Lyapunov function
V(x,y)=2x2+y2​−85x3y+3xy3​=2r2​+O(r4).
(60)
It is positive definite sufficiently close to zero. Differentiating along the polynomial vector field gives
V˙=−83​(x2+y2)2+O(r6)=−83​r4+O(r6).
(61)
For clarity, the quartic terms come from −x4 in the derivative of r2/2, and from applying y∂x​−x∂y​ to the quartic correction. Their sum is −3x4/8−3x2y2/4−3y4/8; all other terms have degree at least six. Therefore V˙<0 in a sufficiently small punctured neighbourhood, proving asymptotic stability by Second Lyapunov theorem. Finally
θ˙=r2xQ−yP​=−1+O(r2),
(62)
so nonconstant nearby orbits turn clockwise as they converge. The equilibrium is a weak focus despite the centre in its linearization. This interpretation resolves both the local dynamics and the absence of closed orbits without changing the printed coefficients.
