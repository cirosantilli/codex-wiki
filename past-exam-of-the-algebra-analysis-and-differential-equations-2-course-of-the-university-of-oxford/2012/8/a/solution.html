For the linear differential operator L=D2+4D+5, the characteristic equation is m2+4m+5=0, with roots −2±i. Take the fundamental system of solutions
u(x)=e−2xcosx,v(x)=e−2xsinx,W(x)=uv′−u′v=e−4x.
(63)
The Wronskian is never zero, so variation of parameters is valid on the whole interval. Write a particular solution as a(x)u(x)+b(x)v(x), and impose the auxiliary condition a′u+b′v=0. Substitution then gives a′u′+b′v′=f. These two equations give
a′=−Wvf​,b′=Wuf​.
(64)
Choosing both integrals to start at zero makes the resulting particular solution and its first derivative vanish there:
yp​(x)=−u(x)∫0x​W(t)v(t)f(t)​dt+v(x)∫0x​W(t)u(t)f(t)​dt=e−2x∫0x​e2tsin(x−t)f(t)dt.
(65)
A general solution is Au+Cv+yp​. The boundary condition at zero sets A=0. The condition at ℓ=π/2 gives
C=−∫0π/2​e2tcostf(t)dt,
(66)
because sinℓ=1 and sin(ℓ−t)=cost. Combining the two integrals yields the Green's function representation
y(x)=∫0π/2​G(x,t)f(t)dt,G(x,t)=−e2(t−x)sin(min(x,t))cos(max(x,t)).​
(67)
Indeed, for t<x the kernel is −e2(t−x)sintcosx, since sin(x−t)−sinxcost=−cosxsint; for x<t it is −e2(t−x)sinxcost.
For fixed interior t, the Green's function vanishes at both endpoints, is continuous at x=t, solves the homogeneous equation away from t, and satisfies
Gx​(t+,t)−Gx​(t−,t)=sin2t+cos2t=1.
(68)
This derivative jump is the normalization required by the leading coefficient one in L. Finally, a homogeneous solution satisfying the first boundary condition is Ce−2xsinx, and the second boundary condition at π/2 forces C=0. Thus the boundary value problem has exactly one solution for every continuous forcing, justifying the unique inverse represented by this kernel.
