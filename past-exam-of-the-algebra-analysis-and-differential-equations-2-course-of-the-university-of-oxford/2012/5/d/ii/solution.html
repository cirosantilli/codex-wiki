Suppose, towards a contradiction, that for some r,w,ε the punctured-disk image misses the open disk of radius ε about w. Then
h(z)=f(z)−w1​
(34)
is holomorphic and bounded by 1/ε on 0<∣z∣<r. Part (i) extends it to a holomorphic function H on the full disk. If H(0)=0, then w+1/H extends f holomorphically at zero, making zero a removable singularity.
If H(0)=0, the function H is not identically zero, since it is nonzero on the punctured disk. The Taylor series and identity theorem give a finite zero order k≥1, so H(z)=zkq(z) with q holomorphic and q(0)=0. Hence
f(z)=w+z−k/q(z)
(35)
has a pole of order k. Both alternatives contradict an essential singularity. Consequently
f({z:0<∣z∣<r})​=Cfor every 0<r<s.​
(36)
This is the Casorati-Weierstrass theorem: every complex value can be approximated arbitrarily closely in every punctured neighbourhood of an essential singularity.
