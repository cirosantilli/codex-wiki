Coriolis acceleration (−2Ω×v)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Reference frame Rotating reference frame Coriolis force
OurBigBook.com Words: 742 Articles: 4
The Coriolis acceleration is the Coriolis force divided by the particle's mass:
acor​=−2Ω×vrot​.
(65)
It is therefore independent of mass for a specified relative velocity and angular velocity. This is one contribution to the acceleration measured in a rotating reference frame. For a fixed common origin with an inertial frame and constant Ω, the full equation of motion in a rotating frame is
full rotating accelerationr¨rot​​​=mFreal​​Coriolis−2Ω×r˙rot​​​centrifugal−Ω×(Ω×r)​​.
(66)
All vectors here are expressed in the rotating axes; Freal​ is the sum of actual forces. An accelerating origin adds a translational apparent force, and time-dependent angular velocity also adds the Euler acceleration.
The factor two follows directly from the rotating-frame derivative formula. First vlab​=r˙rot​+Ω×r. Differentiate again in the inertial frame: differentiating r˙rot​ contributes one Ω×r˙rot​, and differentiating Ω×r contributes another. Move both terms to the rotating side of Newton's second law to obtain the minus sign and the factor two. The centrifugal acceleration comes from the remaining double cross product.
