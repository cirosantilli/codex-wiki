Invariant subspace

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory Group representation
OurBigBook.com Words: 277 Articles: 3
An invariant subspace of a linear map A:V→V is a vector subspace W⊆V satisfying A(W)⊆W. For a group representation, invariance means ρ(g)W⊆W for every represented group element g.
Invariance of a vector subspace does not mean that each individual vector stays fixed. The zero-sum plane in the three-coordinate example is invariant even though the cyclic permutation rotates its nonzero vectors. By contrast, the constant-coordinate line is pointwise fixed. Distinguishing these statements prevents a symmetry-preserved mode space from being mistaken for a static mode.
