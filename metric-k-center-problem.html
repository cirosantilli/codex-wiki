Metric k-center problem (minC⊆V,∣C∣≤k​maxv∈V​minc∈C​d(v,c))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 263 Articles: 1
The metric k-center problem chooses at most k centers from a finite metric space to minimize the largest distance from a point to its nearest center. For 1≤k≤∣V∣, exactly k centers gives the same optimum: extra centers never increase the radius. The restriction that centers belong to V matters; allowing centers anywhere in an ambient metric space is a different problem.
The basic packing argument is short. If a radius-r solution uses k centers, its k closed balls cover V. Two points assigned to the same center have distance at most 2r by the triangle inequality. Thus k+1 points with pairwise distances greater than 2r certify that no radius-r solution exists. Greedily selecting a new center only outside all existing radius-2r closed balls therefore uses at most k centers whenever a radius-r solution exists, and its final covering radius is at most 2r. The farthest-first traversal achieves the same approximation ratio without knowing r.
