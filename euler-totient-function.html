Euler totient function (φ(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 983 Articles: 18
Euler's totient function is the arithmetic function
φ(n)=#{j∈{1,…,n}:gcd(j,n)=1},n>0.
(93)
It counts the congruence classes modulo n represented by integers coprime to n. In particular, φ(1)=1, since gcd(1,1)=1. For 12, the coprime integers among 1,…,12 are 1,5,7,11, so φ(12)=4; they form a reduced residue system. [ref]
Counting these congruence classes is useful because they are exactly the classes with modular multiplicative inverses. Their number supplies the exponent in Euler theorem. Möbius inversion gives the product formula
φ(n)=n∏p∣n​(1−p1​),
(94)
where p runs over distinct prime factors of n. Thus φ(pa)=pa−1(p−1) for prime powers with a≥1, and φ(12)=12(1−1/2)(1−1/3)=4. The product for n=1 is an empty product, so it gives the same convention φ(1)=1.
