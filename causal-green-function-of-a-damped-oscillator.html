Causal Green function of a damped oscillator (G(t)=H(t)e−ktsin(ωt)/ω)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Damped harmonic oscillator
OurBigBook.com Words: 407 Articles: 5
For L=Dt2​+2kDt​+k2+ω2, with k>0 and ω>0, the causal Green function is G(t)=H(t)e−ktsin(ωt)/ω, where H is the Heaviside step function. It satisfies LG=δ(t): it is continuous at zero, with a unit jump in its first derivative. Its smooth positive-time kernel g obeys g(0)=0,g′(0)=1. The zero-frequency limit is H(t)te−kt. Shifting G gives the zero-initial-data response to a delayed Dirac delta function.
With the Fourier transform convention f​(ξ)=∫f(t)e−iξtdt, the transformed equation gives G(ξ)=[k2+ω2−ξ2+2ikξ]−1. Its poles ξ=ik±ω lie in the upper half of the complex plane. Inverting by contour integration therefore gives zero for negative time and an exponentially damped oscillation for positive time.
