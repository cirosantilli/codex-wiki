Orthogonal null-space completion of a square matrix (M=A+LR⊤)

 ... Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Linear map Matrix Matrix rank
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
Let A be a real n×n matrix of matrix rank n−m, and let the columns of L,R be bases of kerA⊤ and kerA, respectively. Then B=LR⊤ has matrix rank m, A⊤B=AB⊤=0, and M=A+B is an invertible matrix.
Proof. Since R has independent columns, its Gram matrix R⊤R is invertible, and R⊤ maps onto Rm. Thus imB=imL=kerA⊤ and rankB=m. The basis conditions give the two zero products. In particular,
MM⊤=AA⊤+BB⊤,AA⊤BB⊤=0.
(48)
If MM⊤x=0, taking its dot product with x gives ∥A⊤x∥2+∥B⊤x∥2=0. Hence x∈kerA⊤=imB and simultaneously x∈kerB⊤=(imB)⊥. Therefore x=0, and M is invertible. The case m=0 is immediate.
For a common eigenvector of AA⊤ and BB⊤, the two eigenvalues have zero product and positive sum: exactly one is zero. Thus the added term fills the missing orthogonal output directions without interfering with the original ones.
