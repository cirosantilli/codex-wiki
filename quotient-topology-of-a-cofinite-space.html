Quotient topology of a cofinite space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Quotient topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 82
A quotient space of a space carrying the cofinite topology has a topology contained in the cofinite topology on its underlying quotient set. If q:X→Y is the quotient map and U is a nonempty open subset of Y, its preimage has finite complement. Since q is surjective, the complement of U is the image of that finite complement, hence is finite. Equality need not hold: partition Z into its even and odd integers. Both classes are infinite, so their two-point quotient space has the indiscrete topology, whereas the cofinite topology on a finite set is the discrete topology.
