First row of RSK as a minimum-tail table

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Increasing subsequence Longest increasing subsequence Minimum-tail table for increasing subsequences
OurBigBook.com Words: 140
For a permutation input, the first row of its RSK insertion tableau is the minimum-tail table for increasing subsequences: its jth entry is the least attainable final value of a strictly increasing subsequence of length j in the processed prefix. Inserting z replaces the first entry larger than z, or appends if none exists. In replacement position r, the previous entry in position r−1 supplies a subsequence that can be extended by z. Shorter minimum tails were already smaller, and a longer subsequence ending at z would require an old tail in position at least r smaller than z, contrary to the replacement rule. This proves the invariant inductively. Consequently the first-row length is the longest increasing subsequence length. The row entries need not be a subsequence together: input 2,4,3,1 gives first row 1,3, although one occurs after three. They are minima for different lengths, obtained from potentially different subsequences.
