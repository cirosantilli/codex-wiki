X-ray tube cutoff (λmin​=hc/(eV))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation X-ray X-ray tube
OurBigBook.com Words: 133
For an electron starting with negligible kinetic energy and accelerated through a constant positive electric potential difference V, electrical work supplies K=eV. In the ideal single-electron bremsstrahlung model, conservation of energy bounds any one emitted photon:
Eγ​=hν=λhc​≤eV,νmax​=heV​,λmin​=eVhc​.
(10)
The endpoint corresponds to essentially all available kinetic energy going into one photon. It is a limiting bound, not a claim that every electron produces an endpoint photon. Neglect target recoil, initial kinetic energy, and losses before the emitting interaction. If the accelerating voltage varies, its largest value sets the corresponding ideal upper endpoint; a mean voltage does not substitute for that value.
For a calculated constant-V example, 50kV gives Emax​=50keV and λmin​=0.0248nm. Doubling V halves λmin​. Producing a 0.0200nm photon requires at least V=hc/(eλ)≃62.0kV in this model. A continuous frequency spectrum spans lower photon energies; the cutoff calculation predicts neither its shape nor the efficiency of photon emission.
