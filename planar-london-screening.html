Planar London screening (B(z)=B0​e−z/λL​)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetic field London equations Meissner effect Meissner state
OurBigBook.com Words: 374 Articles: 1
Take a homogeneous material exhibiting superconductivity in the half-space z≥0, bounded by vacuum at z=0. Fix the temperature below the superconducting transition temperature, take a constant positive London penetration depth λL​, and assume a local, linear London equation description in a Meissner state. The applied magnetic field is weak enough that it neither nucleates a quantum vortex nor appreciably suppresses the material's superconductivity. Assume translation invariance in x,y, no imposed transport electric current, and a tangential boundary magnetic field B(0)=B0​x. Here B0​ is the local surface magnetic flux density, equal to the imposed vacuum magnetic field in this infinite planar geometry; it is not automatically the far-away applied value for a finite object.
For a bounded, decaying interior solution, the London equations, magnetostatic Ampère law and Gauss's law for magnetism give
∇2B=λL2​B​,dz2d2Bx​​=λL2​Bx​​,Bx​(0)=B0​,Bx​(∞)=0.
(136)
The solution is
B(z)=B0​e−z/λL​x.
(137)
At one London penetration depth the magnetic field is e−1≃0.368 of its surface value; at five it is e−5≃0.00674. There is no finite depth where this exponential is exactly zero. The differential equation excludes the growing exponential through the deep-interior boundary condition. A constant normal component cannot decay in this geometry: Gauss's law for magnetism would require ∂z​Bz​=0. A perpendicular applied magnetic field on a finite plate therefore requires a different spatial problem.[ref]
