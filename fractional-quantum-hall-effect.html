Fractional quantum Hall effect (∣σxy​∣=νe2/h)

The fractional quantum Hall effect is quantized transverse sheet conductivity at fractional filling factors, produced by interacting electrons. The Laughlin wavefunction describes the sequence ν=1/m, with odd m, including ν=1/3.[ref]
For an original ideal readout example, take a Hall bar on the ν=1/3 plateau with negligible longitudinal sheet conductivity. The quantum Hall conductivity tensor inversion gives
RH​=I∣VH​∣​=νe2h​=3RK​,I=10nA⟹∣VH​∣≃0.774384mV.
(38)
Here RK​ is the von Klitzing constant; RH​ denotes transverse electrical resistance, rather than the bulk Hall coefficient. Thus a fractional sheet conductivity corresponds to a larger transverse electrical resistance. The fraction labels a collective quantum state: it does not mean that each electron has acquired one third of its usual electric charge.
