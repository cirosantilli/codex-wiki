Subsonic isothermal breeze (u∝r−2,ρ→ρ∞​>0)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Astrophysical fluid dynamics Parker wind
OurBigBook.com Words: 265 Articles: 2
The steady isothermal Parker wind differential equation also has positive solutions describing subsonic flow at every radius. Write their first integral as w−lnw=4lnx+4/x+C, where w=u2/c2 and x=r/rs​. On the small-w branch at large radius, w∼e−Cx−4, so u∼ce−C/2x−2. Constant spherical mass flow rate then gives nonzero limiting mass density and hence finite external pressure. This distinguishes a pressure-confined breeze from an escaping wind into vanishing pressure. Specifying outward motion alone does not select the supersonic asymptotic branch.
