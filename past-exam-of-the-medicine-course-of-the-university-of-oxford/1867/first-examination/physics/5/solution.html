Boyle's law states that a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature obeys
pV=constant,​

where p is absolute pressure, not gauge pressure. It is an ideal-gas approximation, not an assertion that every rapid compression is isothermal.
The respiratory diaphragm contracts and lowers its dome, increasing thoracic volume. Mechanical coupling through the pleural space lets the lungs expand; alveolar pressure falls below atmospheric pressure, drawing air in. Relaxation of the respiratory diaphragm and elastic recoil drive quiet expiration. These respiratory mechanics describe ventilation, distinct from chemical cellular respiration.[ref]
