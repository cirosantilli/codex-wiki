Let an upward force P act along x=0 and a downward parallel force Q along x=d>0. An equivalent resultant force must preserve both total force and torque. Its signed vertical component is R=P−Q, and taking torques about the first line gives RxR​=−Qd. Thus
R=P−Q,xR​=−P−QQd​(P=Q).​
(1)
Its magnitude is ∣P−Q∣ and its direction agrees with the larger force. Its line lies outside the two original lines on the side of that larger force; for example, P>Q gives xR​<0. The products of the original forces and their perpendicular distances from this line are equal.
If P=Q=0 and d=0, the total force vanishes but the torque is −Pd. No single force is equivalent to this system: it is a force couple of magnitude Pd. Its torque is independent of the reference point because translating that point changes the torque by the cross product of the shift and the zero resultant force. Equal opposite forces on the same line instead cancel completely.
The eye is rotated by six extraocular muscles. The rectus muscles approach it from behind; the oblique muscles have oblique lines of pull, with the superior oblique muscle tendon redirected by an orbital pulley. Attachments on the sclera give lever arms about the rotation centre. In the straight-ahead position their actions are as follows; vertical and torsional contributions vary with gaze direction.[ref]
Extraocular muscleActionsInnervation
Medial rectus muscleTurns the eye inwardOculomotor nerve, III
Lateral rectus muscleTurns the eye outwardAbducens nerve, VI
Superior rectus muscleElevates; also turns inward and rotates the upper pole inwardOculomotor nerve, III
Inferior rectus muscleDepresses; also turns inward and rotates the upper pole outwardOculomotor nerve, III
Superior oblique muscleRotates the upper pole inward; also depresses and turns outwardTrochlear nerve, IV
Inferior oblique muscleRotates the upper pole outward; also elevates and turns outwardOculomotor nerve, III
Coordinated contraction selects the desired rotation and balances unwanted components. This illustrates torque control; the full ocular geometry is not simply the parallel-force pair of the first calculation.
