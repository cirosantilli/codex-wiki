The strongest period examples are cholera, typhoid fever, dysentery and other diarrhoeal illness associated with contaminated supplies. Other diseases were attributed to water on weaker grounds; the particular contaminant and route of exposure must be specified. A chemical impurity, an infectious agent and an organism that uses water during its life cycle are different kinds of cause.
For an answer situated in 1865, John Snow's work already provided evidence connecting cholera with swallowed material from an affected person's intestinal discharges, including contaminated drinking water. His 1855 second edition compared populations supplied with different waters, as well as investigating local outbreaks. The persuasive feature is an exposure comparison, not simply proximity to a bad smell. Neither a single chemical analysis nor the later course of an epidemic can by itself settle every link in transmission.[ref]
For typhoid fever, the association with sewage-contaminated wells or supplies should be explained through infectious excreta reaching food or drink. William Budd's 1861 discussion placed the infectious material in the intestinal discharges and opposed spontaneous generation from ordinary decay. That contemporary reasoning gives a much more precise answer than saying that any foul water creates a fever. Modern knowledge identifies a specific bacterium, Salmonella Typhi, rather than an unspecified product of putrefaction.[ref]
Diarrhoea and dysentery name clinical patterns with several possible causes. Sewage contamination can convey organisms producing them, but not every episode is a waterborne disease. Current authoritative guidance recognizes transmission through unsafe drinking water of cholera, typhoid fever, dysenteric and other diarrhoeal infections, hepatitis A and polio. The last two are present-day additions to the explanation, not an assertion that their viral agents were identified in 1865.[ref]
Parasites need the same care about route. Guinea-worm disease can follow swallowing infected tiny crustaceans in unsafe drinking water; it is not caused by a generic chemical property of stagnant water. That life-cycle account is modern corroboration. Other water-associated infections can be acquired through contact rather than drinking, and diseases carried by insects breeding near water form another category.[ref]
An important example of a nineteenth-century attribution is goitre and the developmental disorder then called “cretinism”. An 1861 review linked their distribution to lime-rich drinking water and treated this as a strong causal demonstration. This is evidence of what was supposed, not a conclusion to repeat as established medicine. Endemic goitre is closely associated with iodine deficiency, and severe deficiency during development can have profound neurological consequences; water hardness alone does not identify the relevant cause in a population.[ref][ref]
Similarly, malaria should not be explained as poisoning from a mouthful of stagnant water. Its ordinary transmission is through infected mosquitoes; aquatic breeding conditions can affect vector populations without making ingestion the route of infection. Modern public-health accounts distinguish such water-related vector exposure from enteric transmission.[ref]
There are also genuine noninfectious hazards. Lead can enter drinking water from plumbing and cause lead poisoning; other sources can introduce toxic arsenic. These are poisonings by particular substances, not evidence that every mineralized supply is harmful. Current guidance explicitly distinguishes microbial contamination from naturally occurring or infrastructure-derived chemicals.[ref]
Thus the sound list is accompanied by a causal distinction: diseases transmitted by contaminated water, diseases merely associated with living near water, specific chemical poisonings, and historical claims requiring correction. Clear, pleasant-tasting water may transmit infection, while a conspicuous harmless mineral constituent may have nothing to do with an outbreak. Epidemiological comparison and investigation of the source are essential to deciding which relation is present.
