OurBigBook.com Words: 435
Urine microscopy can identify the kind and possible site of urinary disease, directing further investigation and cause-specific treatment; a sediment finding alone is seldom a prescription. This is a general methods question. No particular specimen, microscopic field or patient result is supplied.
Examine a fresh, properly collected sample, noting collection and storage conditions. After gentle mixing, examine a standardised sediment with a microscope: a low-power objective helps find urinary casts, while higher power characterises cells, bacteria and crystals. A centrifuge concentrates sediment when that method is used, but excessive delay or unsuitable processing can destroy cells and casts or generate crystals after collection. Interpret findings with symptoms, urine chemistry and kidney function; a dirty container or vaginal contamination can otherwise mimic urinary disease.[ref]
Red blood cells indicate haematuria, whose source may be anywhere along the tract. Red blood cell casts or convincing dysmorphic red blood cells support a glomerular origin and justify assessment for glomerulonephritis, including renal function, proteinuria and appropriate referral. They do not establish a particular immunological cause. Isomorphic red blood cells with pain may suggest a stone; persistent unexplained haematuria also requires assessment for other urinary pathology. A historically tempting order to give an astringent would not address these different mechanisms.[ref]
Many white blood cells, with compatible symptoms and bacteria, support urinary tract infection; obtain a suitable bacterial culture when indicated and select an antibiotic according to the clinical syndrome and susceptibility. Contamination or asymptomatic bacteriuria is not equivalent to symptomatic infection. White blood cell casts favour an intrarenal process such as pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis, so the treatment cannot be inferred from pus alone. Numerous squamous epithelial cells instead raise concern about collection quality.[ref]
Renal tubular epithelial cells and granular casts can support tubular injury; fatty elements together with marked proteinuria suggest a nephrotic process. These findings direct assessment of kidney disease, fluid balance and potentially nephrotoxic exposures. A few hyaline casts may occur without a specific kidney disease. Casts are moulds formed within renal tubules; that origin is why they can localise a finding more effectively than free cells.[ref]
Urinary crystals must be interpreted with urine acidity, concentration and the time after collection. Uric acid or calcium oxalate crystals can suggest a stone-forming environment but do not prove an obstructing stone or justify an unrestricted alkali prescription. Hexagonal cystine crystals raise a different metabolic question. Infection-associated phosphate stones require attention to the associated infection as well as stone management. Modern decisions combine stone analysis, imaging and metabolic assessment; hydration, urinary alkalinisation or other measures depend on the confirmed disorder. Microscopy does not by itself measure glucose, albumin loss or renal filtration, so these require separate tests.[ref]
