A mercury barometer balances atmospheric pressure against the hydrostatic head between the open cistern surface and the mercury surface in a closed tube. Writing pt​ for the pressure above the column, the ideal relation is
patm​=pt​+ρHg​(T)glocal​h.​
(7)
The relevant h is the vertical difference between the two mercury levels. It is not simply the length of filled glass, and the top space need not be an absolutely perfect vacuum.
A cistern barometer needs the following corrections or controlled adjustments:
  • Cistern level and scale zero. When mercury rises in a fixed tube, the reservoir level falls. A fixed-zero scale must allow for both motions. For uniform tube area At​ and effective cistern free area Ac​, volume conservation gives Ac​Δzc​=−At​Δzt​, so Δh=(1+At​/Ac​)Δzt​. In a Fortin barometer, an adjustable cistern brings its surface to a fixed index before the column is read.
  • Temperature. Both mercury density and scale length change. To express a corrected physical height h as an equivalent height at reference density ρ0​ and standard gravity gs​, use hs​=ρ(T)glocal​h/(ρ0​gs​). If a scale marked at the reference temperature expands with coefficient αs​, while mercury has volume-expansion coefficient β, a reading R at temperature difference ΔT has the first-order thermal factor 1+(αs​−β)ΔT. For an ordinary metal scale warmer than its reference, the mercury-density effect dominates and the thermal correction reduces the reading.
  • Gravity. The same physical mercury height represents a different pressure where g differs. Reduction to a conventional mercury pressure unit includes glocal​/gs​; this does not assert that local gravity has physically changed.
  • Capillarity, scale and sighting. Correct the capillary depression and calibrated scale/index errors, read the proper part of the meniscus, avoid parallax, and set the instrument vertical. Mercury contamination and changes in the meniscus can compromise a previously determined capillary correction.
  • Top-space pressure. Trapped gas and mercury vapour add pt​. A significant trapped-air error cannot be cured by pretending the top space is empty.
The observational precautions are also described in the Guide to Meteorological Instruments and Methods of Observation by the World Meteorological Organization.[ref] The quantitative corrections are separated in Mercury Barometers and Manometers by W. G. Brombacher and others, particularly the cistern, temperature and gravity discussions. Reduction of a weather-station pressure to sea level is a separate atmospheric comparison, not an intrinsic correction to the instrument.[ref]
