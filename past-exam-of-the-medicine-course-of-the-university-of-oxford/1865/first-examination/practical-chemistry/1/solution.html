A useful examination would give each specimen its own labelled record and preserve an untouched portion. Record physical appearance and behaviour in suitable solvents before choosing tests. A soluble inorganic salt calls for separate investigation of its cation and anion; an insoluble material requires a justified preparation step before solution tests can be interpreted. A colour change, precipitate or evolved gas must be recorded as an observation before it is used as evidence for a composition. Fresh portions prevent one reagent from introducing the ion sought by the next test.
In qualitative chemical analysis, a discriminating result is followed by an independent confirmation and comparison with a reagent blank and a known reference. For example, the behaviour of a metal hydroxide depends on both the cation and the chemical conditions; “a precipitate formed” is not a complete identification.[ref] The record supplied here contains none of those observations, so no particular reaction or identity can be assigned to any of the four letters.
