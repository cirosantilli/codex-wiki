In modern notation the formulas are HCN, HCOOH, H2​C2​O4​, Hg2​Cl2​, HgCl2​, As2​O3​, a hydrated potassium antimony tartrate, and KClO3​, respectively. Several historical names need qualification.
  • Hydrocyanic acid is hydrogen cyanide, HCN​. The pharmaceutical name normally concerns an aqueous chemical solution; its concentration is not encoded by the molecular chemical formula.
  • Formic acid is HCOOH​, equivalently CH2​O2​.
  • Anhydrous oxalic acid is H2​C2​O4​​, or (COOH)2​. Its common crystalline dihydrate is H2​C2​O4​⋅2H2​O; the water of crystallization must be included when calculating a weighed quantity.
  • Calomel is mercury(I) chloride, Hg2​Cl2​​. The formula HgCl gives only the simplest composition ratio and conceals the paired mercury(I) ion.
  • Corrosive sublimate is mercury(II) chloride, HgCl2​​. It is chemically distinct from calomel.
  • The solid historically sold as arsenious acid is normally arsenic trioxide, As2​O3​​, often written AsO3​ in older equivalent notation. The genuinely hydrated molecular arsenous acid is H3​AsO3​, also written As(OH)3​. One should not quietly assign the hydrated formula to the dry commercial oxide.
  • Tartar emetic, or potassium antimony tartrate, is conventionally represented as K(SbO)C4​H4​O6​⋅21​H2​O​. A structurally more informative doubled formula is K2​Sb2​(C4​H2​O6​)2​⋅3H2​O: the complete elemental totals agree. The older “antimonyl tartrate” formula is a compositional convention and does not imply isolated SbO+ units in the crystal.[ref]
  • Chlorate of potash is potassium chlorate, KClO3​​.
The chemical formulas above use modern atomic weights and distinguish molecular formulas, formula units and hydrates. Historical equivalent notation sometimes halves the subscripts attached to divalent chemical elements or writes water as HO. Such formulas must be read together with their contemporary atomic weights, rather than treated as inconsistent modern molecular formulas.
