 ... Medicine course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the medicine course of the University of Oxford 1864 First examination Chemistry 8
OurBigBook.com Words: 342
At the same temperature and pressure, one volume of HCl corresponds to 21​ volume of hydrogen and 21​ volume of chlorine; one of steam to one of hydrogen and 21​ of oxygen; one of N2​O to one of nitrogen and 21​ of oxygen; one of NO to 21​ of each element; and one of ammonia to 21​ of nitrogen and 23​ of hydrogen. All elemental volumes here refer to their ordinary diatomic gases.
The simplest way to avoid an erroneous factor of two is to balance each chemical reaction using H2​, Cl2​, N2​ and O2​. Under Avogadro's law, their stoichiometric coefficients are also gas volume ratios, provided all gases are compared at the same temperature and pressure and ideal gas behaviour is a good approximation.
  • For gaseous hydrogen chloride, H2​+Cl2​→2HCl. Thus VHCl​:VH2​​:VCl2​​=2:1:1​. There is no change in total gas volume in this idealized combination.
  • For water vapor, 2H2​+O2​→2H2​O(g). Thus VH2​O(g)​:VH2​​:VO2​​=2:2:1​: three reactant volumes give two product volumes.
  • “Protoxide of nitrogen” is nitrous oxide, N2​O. The composition equation 2N2​+O2​→2N2​O gives VN2​O​:VN2​​:VO2​​=2:2:1​. This equation expresses composition; it does not assert that direct mixing gives an easy preparative reaction.
  • “Binoxide of nitrogen” in the historical equivalent nomenclature is nitric oxide, modern NO, rather than modern nitrogen dioxide. The equation N2​+O2​→2NO gives VNO​:VN2​​:VO2​​=2:1:1​. Older NO2​ notation with oxygen equivalent 8 describes the same oxygen-to-nitrogen mass ratio as modern NO with oxygen atomic weight 16; reading its subscript by modern rules changes the substance.[ref]
  • For Ammonia, N2​+3H2​→2NH3​ gives VNH3​​:VN2​​:VH2​​=2:1:3​: four reactant volumes correspond to two product volumes.
If the common chosen volume of each compound is V, the corresponding ordered elemental volumes are therefore (V/2,V/2), (V,V/2), (V,V/2), (V/2,V/2) and (V/2,3V/2). The ratios concern molecular counts and hypothetical free elemental gases, not separate compartments of elemental gas still present inside a compound.
“Hydrochloric acid” must here mean gaseous hydrogen chloride, rather than its aqueous chemical solution; water must remain as steam. If water condenses or Ammonia dissolves in the collecting liquid, measured volumes no longer show these simple ratios. For an experimental comparison, dry-gas and hydrostatic pressure corrections are those discussed in 3.
