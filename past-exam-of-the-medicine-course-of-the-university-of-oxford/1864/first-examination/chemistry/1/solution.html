OurBigBook.com Words: 240
For complete neutralization, the required masses of anhydrous potassium carbonate, anhydrous sodium carbonate and magnesium oxide are in the ratio 138:106:40=69:53:20. Here “carbonate of potash” means potassium carbonate, “carbonate of soda” means sodium carbonate, and “caustic magnesia” means magnesium oxide, rather than magnesium hydroxide or a magnesium carbonate preparation. The hydration state of the sodium carbonate matters considerably.
Each formula unit of either carbonate consumes two protons; each formula unit of magnesium oxide also consumes two protons. Thus one mole of each base neutralizes one mole of sulfuric acid when the final salts are the normal sulfates:
K2​CO3​+H2​SO4​Na2​CO3​+H2​SO4​MgO+H2​SO4​​⟶K2​SO4​+CO2​+H2​O,⟶Na2​SO4​+CO2​+H2​O,⟶MgSO4​+H2​O.​
(1)
The carbon dioxide evolved in the first two chemical reactions escapes, whereas magnesium oxide produces no gas. The stoichiometric coefficients, rather than the visibly different behaviour of the bases, determine the required amounts.
Let m be the mass of pure H2​SO4​ in the given chemical solution. Using the rounded relative atomic masses H=1, C=12, O=16, Na=23, Mg=24 and K=39 gives
mK2​CO3​​=98138​m,mNa2​CO3​​=98106​m,mMgO​=9840​m.​
(2)
Thus 98 mass units of pure sulfuric acid require respectively 138, 106 and 40 mass units. Present atomic weights slightly change these rounded values, without changing the one-to-one mole ratios. If ms​ denotes the mass of the supplied acid and w its mass fraction of H2​SO4​, substitute m=wms​. A volume alone is insufficient without its concentration or an equivalent density and composition.
If “carbonate of soda” is instead the crystalline decahydrate, sodium carbonate decahydrate, its rounded molar mass is 286 gmol−1, so the corresponding factor is 286/98, rather than 106/98. Likewise an impure base requires correction for its assayed content. The unspecified given quantity can therefore be handled exactly by proportional reasoning; no missing numerical measurement is needed.
