General anasarca is extensive interstitial fluid accumulation, produced when vascular filtration exceeds the capacity of lymphatic drainage and the body's regulation of salt and water. Several mechanisms commonly act together.
Raised venous and capillary hydrostatic pressure promotes filtration. Heart failure, especially when systemic venous pressure is elevated, is a major example; renal retention of sodium and water reinforces the swelling. Severe kidney failure can itself cause general fluid overload when salt and water cannot be excreted adequately. Acute glomerulonephritis may therefore cause widespread oedema without requiring the extremely low plasma protein concentration of every nephrotic case.[ref]
Reduced plasma oncotic pressure contributes when circulating albumin is markedly depleted. Causes include heavy urinary protein loss in nephrotic syndrome, reduced synthesis in advanced liver disease, protein loss through the intestine, and severe malnutrition. In cirrhosis, altered circulation and renal salt retention add to reduced protein synthesis; the mechanism cannot be reduced to a single blocked vein. Likewise, nephrotic syndrome can include primary renal salt retention as well as a fall in oncotic pressure.[ref]
Increased vascular permeability during severe systemic inflammation, sepsis or extensive tissue injury can allow fluid and protein into the interstitium. Impaired lymphatic drainage can amplify systemic oedema, although a single obstructed lymphatic or vein ordinarily causes regional rather than general swelling. Excess administered fluid and some drugs can contribute by salt retention or altered vascular tone. Multiple mechanisms are particularly likely in a severely ill patient.
A nineteenth-century description would group much of this as cardiac, renal or hepatic “dropsy,” and might invoke an impoverished state of the blood. Modern physiology specifies the abnormalities that those broad descriptions leave unresolved. A general examination should distinguish pitting fluid accumulation from a predominantly non-pitting disorder such as myxoedema, and from isolated ascites. The distribution, jugular venous filling, heart and lung findings, urinary albumin, renal function and plasma albumin help distinguish causes. Anasarca is a sign with several possible causes, not a diagnosis of renal disease by itself.
