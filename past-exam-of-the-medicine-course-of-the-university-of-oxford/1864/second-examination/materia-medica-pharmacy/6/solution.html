 ... Medicine course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the medicine course of the University of Oxford 1864 Second examination Materia medica, pharmacy 6

The 1864 iron list includes metallic, ferrous, ferric and compound preparations; its wet Ferri Peroxidum Hydratum was used historically as an antidote to arsenic poisoning. It must be distinguished from dried Ferri Peroxidum and from later editions' reassignment of similar Latin names.
The officinal articles and preparations are:
  • metallic forms: iron wire, Ferrum, and Ferrum Redactum;
  • simple salts and oxides: Ferri Arsenias, Ferri Carbonas Saccharata, Ferri Iodidum, Ferri Oxidum Magneticum, Ferri Peroxidum, Ferri Peroxidum Hydratum, Ferri Phosphas, Ferri Sulphas, Ferri Sulphas Exsiccata and Ferri Sulphas Granulata;
  • complex salts: Ferri et Ammoniae Citras, Ferri et Quiniae Citras and Ferrum Tartaratum;
  • solutions: Liquor Ferri Perchloridi, Liquor Ferri Pernitratis and the preparative Liquor Ferri Persulphatis;
  • compounded forms: Mistura Ferri Composita, Pilula Ferri Carbonatis, Pilula Ferri Iodidi, Syrupus Ferri Iodidi, Syrupus Ferri Phosphatis, Tinctura Ferri Perchloridi and Vinum Ferri.
[ref] [ref]
Prepare the hydrated peroxide by inducing precipitation from the ferric solution with sodium hydroxide, collecting and washing the wet precipitate, and preserving it without drying in a covered vessel. The starting solution is Liquor Ferri Persulphatis, whose “persulphate” here means ferric sulfate, not a modern peroxydisulfate salt. Add the appropriately diluted ferric solution to the alkaline liquor with stirring, allow the precipitate to settle, collect it on a calico filter, and wash with distilled water until the washings no longer give a precipitate with barium chloride. This tests removal of soluble sulfate. The resulting fresh, hydrated material is kept wet; drying gives the separate dried preparation. [ref]
In modern chemical notation the precipitation can be idealised as
Fe3++3OH−⟶Fe(OH)3​(s).
(1)
The fresh solid is a hydrated ferric oxide material with variable water content; the equation is not a claim that every pharmaceutical batch is one pure, crystalline iron hydroxide. Its historical use depended on binding dissolved arsenic into less available material in the digestive tract, especially the freshly precipitated, wet state. It was not a universal antidote to every metal or every stage of absorbed arsenic poisoning. Contemporary discussion explicitly identified the new hydrated peroxide as an arsenic antidote. [ref] [ref]
Modern treatment of significant arsenic poisoning concerns systemic toxicity and may involve specialist-directed chelation therapy; a nineteenth-century ferric paste is not the modern treatment standard. [ref]
