 ... Medicine course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the medicine course of the University of Oxford 1866 Second examination Therapeutics 7
OurBigBook.com Words: 386
For an 1866 discussion, distinguish conditions demanding special caution from a universal prohibition. Modern correction goes further: chloroform is not an acceptable routine surgical anaesthetic. Its toxicity cannot be removed by choosing an apparently healthy person.
The historical inquiry would examine the heart, lungs, general strength and the nature of the proposed procedure. A failing circulation, suspected serious myocardial degeneration, great exhaustion or blood loss, and severe respiratory impairment gave strong reasons to avoid or postpone a nonessential exposure. A full stomach, obstruction of the airway, difficulty maintaining breathing, or an operation threatening to contaminate the airway created additional hazards. Acute intoxication or other depressant drugs could complicate administration. The balance depended partly on whether the operation was essential and on what alternative method of relieving pain was available.
It would nevertheless be historically inaccurate to declare that the contemporary profession unanimously regarded all heart disease as an absolute contraindication. The 1864 committee's report said that heart disease in general did not automatically preclude surgical use, while specifically calling for care over fatty degeneration. It also discussed the difficulty of recognizing that condition during life. Its treatment of mouth and throat operations was likewise more qualified than a universal ban.[ref] This is a record of the committee's judgement, not endorsement of its safety conclusion.
The delivery conditions also matter. An uncontrolled vapour concentration, failure to ensure air entry, lack of a dedicated competent observer, or inability to respond to loss of breathing and circulation makes an already hazardous intervention less defensible. Such circumstances concern how the drug is used rather than a disease-specific contraindication. The period distinction between direct circulatory failure and respiratory failure explains why watching only one sign was inadequate.[ref]
Modern correction: chloroform can depress the central nervous system, disrupt cardiac function and cause delayed hepatic and renal injury. Its former use as an anaesthetic does not make it a current option. Modern anaesthetic assessment still considers cardiovascular and respiratory reserve, aspiration risk, other drugs and the proposed procedure, but uses contemporary agents, monitoring and trained personnel.[ref][ref] In particular, absence of obvious heart disease is not evidence that a chloroform exposure is safe.
