Open rectangles in a finite product

OurBigBook.com Words: 152
On two copies of the real line with their usual topologies, let U=(1,2) and V=(2,4). The coordinate projection maps give
π1−1​(U)=U×R,π2−1​(V)=R×V,π1−1​(U)∩π2−1​(V)=U×V.
(45)
The first two open sets are unbounded strips; their set intersection is an open rectangle. The point (1.4,3) passes both coordinate tests, whereas (2,3) fails the first because the endpoint 2 is excluded.
Every point of an open set in the Euclidean plane has such an open rectangle around it inside that open set. Conversely, every open rectangle contains a small Euclidean ball around each of its points. Thus the product topology here is the usual metric topology of the Euclidean plane. With finitely many factors all coordinates may be restricted; on an infinite Cartesian product a basic cylinder set must leave all but finitely many factors free.
