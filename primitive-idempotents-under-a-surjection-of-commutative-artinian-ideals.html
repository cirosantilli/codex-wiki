Primitive idempotents under a surjection of commutative Artinian ideals

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Ring Idempotent Primitive idempotent
OurBigBook.com Words: 148
Let A,B be finite-dimensional commutative algebras, let I⊂A and J⊂B be ideals, and let f:I→J be a surjective algebra homomorphism. The ideals need not have identities. Then f gives a bijection between the primitive idempotents of A lying in I with nonzero image and the primitive idempotents of J.
Decompose A into its commutative Artinian local rings Ai​, with identities ei​. Each I∩Ai​ is either all of Ai​, in which case ei​∈I, or a proper ideal and therefore a nilpotent ideal. The sum of the latter ideals is nilpotent, so its image contributes no idempotents. Each nonzero f(ei​) has local corner algebra f(ei​)J=f(Ai​), a quotient of Ai​, so is primitive. These idempotents are orthogonal, and the remaining part of J is nilpotent; hence they are exactly its primitive idempotents. This statement does not require the entire kernel of f to be nilpotent.
