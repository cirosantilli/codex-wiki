Nuclear elastic electron scattering cross-section (dσ/dΩ≃(dσ/dΩ)point​∣F(q)∣2)

For an ultrarelativistic electron scattered elastically by a static, spherical, spin-zero atomic nucleus, a plane-wave Born approximation retains a single electromagnetic interaction. The Fourier transform of its Coulomb potential is proportional to ZeF(q)/q2. Relative to the same point charge, the scattering amplitude is therefore multiplied by F(q) and the differential scattering cross-section by ∣F(q)∣2:
dΩdσ​≃(2EZαℏc​)2sin4(θ/2)cos2(θ/2)​∣F(q)∣2,q≃ℏc2E​sin2θ​.
(14)
Here E≫me​c2 is the incident total electron energy, θ the scattering angle, dΩ a solid angle element, and α the fine-structure constant. The cosine factor already accounts for the projectile's spin; a spinless projectile would give a different point-charge factor. The formula neglects nuclear recoil, electron rest-mass corrections, radiative corrections and Coulomb distortion in electron scattering. The latter can be substantial at large Zα: electron waves then require treatment in the target's Coulomb potential, rather than undistorted plane waves.
Nonzero target spin can introduce magnetic and additional electric contributions, so division by this point-charge expression does not generally isolate one charge form factor for a proton or deuteron. Even for spin zero, finite energy resolution, recoil and Coulomb distortion in electron scattering can change a measured minimum. A zero of the uniform-sphere model is a clear illustration of amplitude cancellation, not a universal prediction of a zero experimental counting rate.
