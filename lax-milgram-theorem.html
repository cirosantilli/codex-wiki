Lax-Milgram theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 453 Articles: 7
The theorem supplies solvability for a coercive weak formulation.
A bounded bilinear form a on a real Hilbert space H that is a coercive bilinear form represents every bounded linear functional uniquely: for each ℓ∈H′, there is a unique u∈H with a(u,v)=ℓ(v) for every v∈H.
For a proof, the Riesz representation theorem gives a bounded linear operator T and a vector g with a(u,v)=⟨Tu,v⟩ and ℓ(v)=⟨g,v⟩. Coercivity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give ∥Tu∥≥α∥u∥, so T is injective and its image is closed: convergence of Tun​ makes un​ a Cauchy sequence, whose limit supplies the image limit. If v belongs to the orthogonal complement of the image, then a(u,v)=0 for every u; setting u=v and using coercivity forces v=0. Thus the image is also dense and equals H. There is consequently a unique u with Tu=g, and ∥u∥≤∥ℓ∥/α. The complex version uses a bounded sesquilinear form with Rea(u,u)≥α∥u∥2.
