For a coherently prepared state proportional to ρ∣KS​⟩+∣KL​⟩, evolve each component with λj​=mj​−iΓj​/2. The proper time is t, and ℏ=1. Since both components reach the same final state, their decay amplitudes add before squaring. Factoring out ∣AS​∣2 and a common beam normalization gives the two-pion intensity
Iππ​(t)=∣ρ∣2e−ΓS​t+∣ηππ​∣2e−ΓL​t+2e−(ΓS​+ΓL​)t/2Re(ρ∗ηππ​e−iΔmt),Δm=mL​−mS​.
(16)
The last term is quantum interference. It vanishes upon averaging over an independently random preparation phase, but cannot be discarded from a coherent preparation. Summing the two positive contributions alone would lose the phase-sensitive information.
At late proper time, the short-lived contribution and the quantum interference term die away, leaving the long-lived tail ∣ηππ​∣2e−ΓL​t. Such a tail is easier to identify after the initially abundant short-lived component has disappeared. The useful experimental balance is between eliminating that component and retaining enough long-lived decays to observe a rare decay channel.
