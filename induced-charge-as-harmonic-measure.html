Induced charge as harmonic measure (Qj​=−qwj​(r0​))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electric field Electric potential Electrostatic reciprocity identity
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
In a grounded conducting enclosure, let the harmonic function wj​ equal one on boundary component j and zero on the others. A point charge q at r0​ induces total electric charge Qj​=−qwj​(r0​) on that component. The weighting harmonic function is its harmonic measure.
To prove the identity, let ϕ be the zero-boundary electric potential, so ∇2ϕ=−qδ(3)(r−r0​)/ϵ0​. Green's second identity gives −qwj​(r0​)/ϵ0​=∫∂D​wj​∂n​ϕdS, where n points out of the vacuum domain into the metal. The electrostatic boundary conditions at a conductor give σ=ϵ0​∂n​ϕ, proving the result. In an unbounded domain require the additional boundary term at infinity to vanish. Between concentric spheres, wa​(r)=a(b−r)/(r(b−a)); between parallel planes z=0,d, w0​=1−z/d and wd​=z/d.
