Two-current resistor model (RP​=(2a+r)∥(2b+r),RAP​=(a+b+r)/2)

Consider two identical magnetic layers separated by a nonmagnetic layer, between reservoirs that impose the same terminal electric potential difference on both spin channels. Neglect spin-flip scattering and assume each layer obeys Ohm's law. In a magnetic layer, define positive channel electrical resistances a,b>0. Let the spacer contribute r≥0 to each channel. These are circuit parameters, not measured iron or chromium resistivities.
For parallel layer magnetizations, one channel meets a,r,a and the other b,r,b. For opposite layer magnetizations, each meets one a, one b, and the spacer. The two channels share the same terminal electric potential difference and run in parallel. Writing x∥y=xy/(x+y) gives
RP​=2(a+b+r)(2a+r)(2b+r)​,RAP​=2a+b+r​,RAP​−RP​=2(a+b+r)(a−b)2​≥0.
(27)
The inequality is algebra, given the specified network. It shows how a difference between spin-channel responses can yield a difference between magnetic configurations even though all the constituent resistors are ohmic.
For chosen a=1Ω, b=4Ω, r=1Ω, the parallel channels have total electrical resistances 3 and 9Ω, while the opposite-alignment channels each have 6Ω. Thus RP​=2.25Ω and RAP​=3.00Ω. At a common terminal electric potential difference 1V, the total electric currents are 4/9A and 1/3A, respectively.
