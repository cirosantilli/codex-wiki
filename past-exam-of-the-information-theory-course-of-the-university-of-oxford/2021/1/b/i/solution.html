D(λq+(1−λ)r∥p)≤λD(q∥p)+(1−λ)D(r∥p).

If p has zero coordinates, the finite-valued domain consists of probability mass functions supported on {i:pi​>0}; it is convex and the same proof applies there. Outside that domain the right side is infinite whenever an offending probability mass function has a positive mixture weight, so the extended-valued convexity assertion still holds. At mixture weights zero or one the inequality is an identity, without needing the expression 0⋅∞.
