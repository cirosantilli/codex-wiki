Use the standard deterministic channel encoder and channel decoder, with m message labels and code rate R=(logm)/n. The Fano's inequality hint printed here replaces log(m−1) by logm−1, which is false in general: take X uniform on {0,1} and Y=2 in the common alphabet {0,1,2}. The error probability is one and its binary entropy is zero, so the proposed bound asserts 1≤log3−1<1. The correct Fano's inequality alone gives (1−Pe​)logm≤n(1−q)+h2​(Pe​), which does not immediately give the requested additive 1/n. The following erasure-pattern counting bound for maximal decoding error proves the displayed claim directly.
The case m≤2 is immediate because R≤1/n. Suppose m>2, put L=logm−1>0, and let w0​ be the message that the channel decoder returns on the all-erased output. Choose W uniformly among the other m−1 messages. Every one of these messages is decoded correctly with probability at least 1−δ, by the assumed maximal decoding error probability bound.
Let S be the random set of unerased coordinates and K=∣S∣. For each fixed S with K=k≥1, at most 2k output words are possible. A deterministic channel decoder can correctly identify at most one message per output word, so its conditional success probability, averaged over these m−1 messages, is at most min{1,2k/(m−1)}. When K=0, success is zero because w0​ was excluded.
