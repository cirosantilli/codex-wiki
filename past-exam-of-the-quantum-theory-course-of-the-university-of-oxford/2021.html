For a free particle, the Hamiltonian operator is H=P2/(2m), where P=−iℏd/dx is the momentum operator. The time-independent Schrödinger equation is therefore
−2mℏ2​dx2d2ψ​=Eψ,ψ(x+2π)=ψ(x).​
(1)
The full stationary state is Ψ(x,t)=e−iEt/ℏψ(x). The periodic boundary condition identifies x and x+2π, giving a particle on a circle with wavefunction normalization over one period.

For a momentum eigenstate with eigenvalue p, the equation −iℏψ′=pψ has solution ψ(x)=Ceipx/ℏ. The periodic boundary condition imposes e2πip/ℏ=1, so p/ℏ∈Z. The wavefunction normalization gives 1=2π∣C∣2. Choosing a convenient global phase yields

⟨ψn​,ψk​⟩=2π1​∫02π​ei(k−n)xdx=δnk​.
(3)
Here δnk​ is the Kronecker delta.

The position operators and momentum operators are self-adjoint operators. Using conjugate linearity of the adjoint operator, (cA+dB)∗=cA∗+dB∗, gives
aj∗​=2mωℏ​mωXj​−iPj​​.​
(18)
Write D=2mωℏ​. Bilinearity and antisymmetry of the commutator, together with the canonical commutation relations, give [Pj​,Xk​]=−iℏδjk​I. Hence
D2[aj​,ak​]D2[aj​,ak∗​]​=imω[Xj​,Pk​]+imω[Pj​,Xk​]=−mωℏδjk​I+mωℏδjk​I=0,=−imω[Xj​,Pk​]+imω[Pj​,Xk​]=2mωℏδjk​I.​
(19)
The terms involving two position operators or two momentum operators vanish by the canonical commutation relations. Finally, reversing products under the adjoint operator, (AB)∗=B∗A∗, gives [aj​,ak​]∗=−[aj∗​,ak∗​]. Therefore
[aj​,ak​]=[aj∗​,ak∗​]=0,[aj​,ak∗​]=δjk​I.​
(20)
Here I is the identity operator. These are the relations for two independent pairs of creation and annihilation operators. The operator algebra in this question is understood on a common invariant operator domain, such as finite linear combinations of the number states constructed in part (c).

[N,aj∗​]=∑k=12​(ak∗​[ak​,aj∗​]+[ak∗​,aj∗​]ak​)=aj∗​.

Inverting the definitions of the creation and annihilation operators gives
Xj​=2mωℏ​​(aj​+aj∗​),Pj​=−i2mωℏ​​(aj​−aj∗​).

a1​u=ψ,a2​u=0,a1​v=0,a2​v=ψ.

The angular momentum eigenstates have J2 eigenvalue ℏ2j(j+1), where the angular momentum quantum number is j=0,21​,1,23​,…. For each j, the magnetic quantum number runs in unit steps from −j to j. Here that magnetic quantum number is +1/2, so only half-integer j are compatible:
λ=j(j+1),j=21​,23​,25​,…,λ∈{43​,415​,435​,…}.​
(37)
Integer j cannot contain a J3​ eigenvalue ℏ/2. Applying the angular momentum lowering operator and using [J3​,J−​]=−ℏJ−​ gives
J3​φ=J3​J−​ψ=(J−​J3​−ℏJ−​)ψ=−2ℏ​φ​.
(38)
The lowered vector is nonzero, since the adjoint operator identity J−∗​=J+​ gives
∥φ∥2=⟨ψ,J+​J−​ψ⟩=ℏ2(λ−41​+21​)=ℏ2(λ+41​)>0.
(39)
Finally, J3​ is a self-adjoint operator, so its eigenstates for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal. Explicitly,
−2ℏ​⟨φ∣ψ⟩=⟨J3​φ∣ψ⟩=⟨φ∣J3​ψ⟩=2ℏ​⟨φ∣ψ⟩,
(40)
and hence ⟨φ∣ψ⟩=0​.

In the ordered orthonormal basis (ψ1​,ψ2​), the Hamiltonian operator has matrix
H=ℏω(01​10​)=ℏωσ1​,
(45)
where σ1​ is a Pauli matrix. Its symmetric and antisymmetric eigenvectors give
E+​=+ℏω,χ+​=2​ψ1​+ψ2​​;E−​=−ℏω,χ−​=2​ψ1​−ψ2​​.​

The energy eigenstates χ±​ are normalized and orthogonal; each energy eigenvalue is nondegenerate. Their full stationary states are e−iωtχ+​ and eiωtχ−​. Since they form an orthonormal basis, the general solution to the Time-dependent Schrödinger equation is
Ψ(t)=c+​e−iωtχ+​+c−​eiωtχ−​,c+​,c−​∈C.​
(47)
For a normalized quantum state, ∣c+​∣2+∣c−​∣2=1; the constants are determined by its initial inner products with χ±​.

Continue with the initial quantum state ψ1​ from part (ii), and let Δt=T/N. Conditional on every preceding outcome being +ℏ/2, the post-measurement state after each ideal projective measurement is again ψ1​, up to global phase. Part (ii) therefore gives the same conditional probability cos2(ωΔt) for each successive outcome. Multiplying these conditional probabilities yields the repeated projective-measurement survival probability
PN​(T)=[cos2(NωT​)]N.​
(50)
This multiplication uses the quantum state reset at each successful projective measurement; the outcomes need not be assumed statistically independent.
For fixed T, put z=ωT. The Taylor expansion cos2u=1−u2+O(u4) gives the quadratic short-time quantum survival probability. For sufficiently large N, taking logarithms gives
logPN​(T)=Nlog(1−N2z2​+O(N−4))=−Nz2​+O(N−3)⟶0.
(51)
Here the remainder uses Big O notation. Exponentiating proves
N→∞lim​PN​(T)=1,PN​(T)=1−N(ωT)2​+O(N−2).​
(52)
This is the quantum Zeno effect: increasingly frequent ideal projective measurements inhibit the transition away from the initial quantum state. The departure probability in a short interval is quadratic in its duration, so N intervals of duration T/N produce a total leading departure probability proportional to T2/N. Without intervening measurements, the return probability at time T would be cos2(ωT). The result concerns obtaining +ℏ/2 at every measurement in the ideal limit of instantaneous measurements with unitary time evolution between them.
