iℏ∂t∂Ψ​=−2mℏ2​∂x2∂2Ψ​​,Ψ(t,x+2πR)=Ψ(t,x).

A stationary state has Ψ(t,x)=e−iEt/ℏψ(x). Substitution into the Schrödinger equation gives the energy eigenvalue equation
−2mℏ2​ψ′′=Eψ.
(2)
The trial wavefunction Ceikx satisfies it with E=ℏ2k2/(2m). The periodic boundary condition requires e2πikR=1, hence k=n/R, n∈Z. The wavefunction normalization is 1=2πR∣C∣2, so a convenient common global phase gives the orthonormal basis
ϕn​(x)=2πR​einx/R​,En​=2mR2ℏ2n2​,n∈Z.​
(3)
Indeed, ∫02πR​ϕn∗​ϕq​dx=δnq​. This is the free particle spectrum on a circle. The ground state ϕ0​ has energy zero and is nondegenerate. Each positive energy level ℏ2n2/(2mR2), n=1,2,…, has energy eigenspace span{ϕn​,ϕ−n​} and quantum degeneracy two. Real sine and cosine combinations give another basis of the same energy eigenspace.

By the Born rule, the probability for a measured energy eigenvalue is the squared norm of the orthogonal projection onto its whole energy eigenspace. Thus
Pr(E=0)=32​,Pr(E=E⋆​)=61​+61​=31​.​
(8)
Every other energy eigenvalue has probability zero. These probabilities are independent of T: unitary time evolution under the unchanged Hamiltonian operator changes the dynamical phases but not the squared expansion coefficients. The two equal-energy terms describe one measurement outcome.

Put τ=t−T and retain the global phase from the preceding Lüders rule update. Since Pϕn​=(ℏn/R)ϕn​, the same orthonormal basis diagonalizes the new Hamiltonian operator, with
Hnew​=2m(P−ms)2​,En​=2m1​(Rℏn​−ms)2.
(10)

Ψ(t,x)​=−2​e−iE⋆​T/ℏ​(e−iE2​τ/ℏϕ2​(x)+e−iE−2​τ/ℏϕ−2​(x))=−πR​e−iE⋆​T/ℏe−i(E⋆​+ms2/2)τ/ℏ​cos(R2(x+sτ)​).​​
(11)
This is rigid translation from a momentum term in the Hamiltonian: the position probability density moves with velocity −s. The plus sign in x+sτ follows directly from the negative sign of −sP. At τ=0 the expression agrees exactly with the preceding post-measurement state.

∣Ψ(t,x)∣2=πR1​cos2(R2(x+sτ)​),τ=t−T.

The Hamiltonian operator separates into two commuting one-dimensional quantum harmonic oscillators. Write
ξj​=ℏmωj​​​xj​,G=e−(ξ12​+ξ22​)/2,E00​=2ℏ​(ω1​+ω2​).
(14)
Products of harmonic oscillator eigenfunctions give
ψn1​,n2​​∝Hn1​​(ξ1​)Hn2​​(ξ2​)G,En1​,n2​​=E00​+ℏ(n1​ω1​+n2​ω2​).
(15)
Here Hn​ is the physicists' Hermite polynomial, so H0​=1, H1​(u)=2u and H2​(u)=4u2−2. The first six energy eigenstates, ordered with multiplicity, are
(n1​,n2​)(0,0)(1,0)(0,1)(2,0)(1,1)(0,2)​En1​,n2​​E00​E00​+ℏω1​E00​+ℏω2​E00​+2ℏω1​E00​+ℏ(ω1​+ω2​)E00​+2ℏω2​​ψn1​,n2​​ up to a nonzero constantGξ1​Gξ2​G(4ξ12​−2)Gξ1​ξ2​G(4ξ22​−2)G​​​
(16)
For this two-dimensional oscillator level ordering, the excitation energies obey
0<ω1​≤ω2​<2ω1​≤ω1​+ω2​≤2ω2​<3ω1​.
(17)
Every state with n1​+n2​≥3 costs at least 3ℏω1​, so none has been missed. Equalities within the list occur when ω2​=ω1​=ω. Then the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator has
EN​=ℏω(N+1),gN​=N+1,N=0,1,2,….​
(18)
The quantum degeneracy counts n1​=0,…,N with n2​=N−n1​. The six listed states occupy three distinct energy levels, of quantum degeneracies one, two and three.

Since X and P are self-adjoint operators, the adjoint operator relation requires c+​=c−​=c and d+​=−d−​=−d. Thus a−​=cX+idP and a+​=cX−idP. The canonical commutation relation [X,P]=iℏI gives
[a−​,a+​]=2ℏcdI,a+​a−​=c2X2+d2P2−ℏcdI.
(19)
Matching the X2, P2 and identity operator coefficients in H=ℏω(a+​a−​+λI) yields
c2=2ℏmω​,d2=2mℏω1​,cd=2ℏ1​,λ=21​.
(20)
Consequently all real solutions are
c+​=c−​=ε2ℏmω​​,d−​=−d+​=2mℏω​ε​,λ=21​,ε∈{1,−1}.​
(21)
The conventional creation and annihilation operators use ε=1. The other choice multiplies both by minus one and satisfies all the given equations. This harmless freedom is the oscillator ladder-operator sign convention; the subsequent formulas use the positive choice in both coordinates.

[Xj​,Pk​]=iℏδjk​I,[Xj​,Xk​]=[Pj​,Pk​]=0.

Writing cj​=mωj​/(2ℏ)​, dj​=1/2mℏωj​​, direct expansion of the commutator gives
[aj,−​,ak,+​]=ℏ(cj​dk​+dj​ck​)δjk​I=δjk​I​.

The orbital angular momentum L3​=X1​P2​−X2​P1​ generates rotations in the plane. The canonical commutation relations give
[L3​,X1​]=iℏX2​,[L3​,X2​]=−iℏX1​,[L3​,P1​]=iℏP2​,[L3​,P2​]=−iℏP1​.
(26)
Use the commutator product rule [A,B2]=[A,B]B+B[A,B]. Since different coordinates commute,
[L3​,P12​+P22​][L3​,ω12​X12​+ω22​X22​]​=2iℏP1​P2​−2iℏP2​P1​=0,=2iℏ(ω12​−ω22​)X1​X2​.​
(27)
Thus, for arbitrary positive angular frequencies,
[L3​,H]=iℏm(ω12​−ω22​)X1​X2​.
(28)
At ω1​=ω2​, this becomes [L3​,H]=0​. The rotational symmetry of the two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator allows a basis of simultaneous energy eigenstates and orbital angular momentum eigenstates.

Choose the first three energy eigenstates consistently as ψ0,0​, ψ1,0​=a1,+​ψ0,0​ and ψ0,1​=a2,+​ψ0,0​. The Fock vacuum is annihilated by both lowering operators, so
L3​ψ0,0​=0,L3​ψ1,0​=iℏψ0,1​,L3​ψ0,1​=−iℏψ1,0​.​

i OurBigBook.com
The angular momentum commutation relations are
[Ji​,Jj​]=iℏk=1∑3​εijk​Jk​​,J2=J12​+J22​+J32​,[J2,Ji​]=0,
(35)
where εijk​ is the Levi-Civita symbol. The angular momentum eigenstates have
J2∣j,μ⟩=ℏ2j(j+1)∣j,μ⟩,J3​∣j,μ⟩=ℏμ∣j,μ⟩,​
(36)
with angular momentum quantum number j=0,21​,1,23​,… and magnetic quantum number μ=−j,−j+1,…,j. There are 2j+1 such states in one irreducible spin representation, so the J2 eigenspace has dimension 2j+1 within that multiplet. The use of μ avoids confusing the magnetic quantum number with the particle mass in the earlier questions.
For completeness, the ladder operators J±​=J1​±iJ2​ satisfy [J3​,J±​]=±ℏJ±​ and
∥J±​∣j,μ⟩∥2=ℏ2[j(j+1)−μ(μ±1)].
(37)
The upper and lower ends are μ=±j; integer steps between them require 2j to be a nonnegative integer. This explains both the spectrum and the multiplet dimension. Pure orbital angular momentum permits only integer j, whereas general spin allows half-integers. If a larger Hilbert space contains several copies of the same irreducible spin representation, the full J2 eigenspace includes all those copies: its dimension is (2j+1) times their multiplicity, and can be infinite. The paper's dimension question is understood per irreducible multiplet.

The Hamiltonian operator H=ωJ3​ is already diagonal in the angular momentum eigenstate basis of part (b). Its three distinct energy eigenvalues and stationary states are
Eμ​=ℏωμ,∣Ψμ​(t)⟩=e−iωμt∣1,μ⟩,μ=1,0,−1.​
(46)
Thus the three column eigenvectors are (1,0,0)T, (0,1,0)T and (0,0,1)T, with energies +ℏω, zero and −ℏω, respectively. Each is nondegenerate since ω>0. Any nonzero scalar multiple gives the same stationary state after normalization; a quantum superposition of different energies is generally not stationary.

Diagonalizing the J1​ matrix gives the normalized eigenvectors
v+​=21​​12​1​​,v0​=2​1​​10−1​​,v−​=21​​1−2​1​​,
(47)
with eigenvalues +ℏ,0,−ℏ, respectively. The first projective measurement prepares v+​ up to global phase, regardless of the earlier state, conditional on obtaining +ℏ.
The unitary time evolution under H=ωJ3​ gives, with θ=ωt,
∣Ψ(t)⟩=diag(e−iθ,1,eiθ)v+​=21​​e−iθ2​eiθ​​.
(48)
The probability amplitudes for the second projective measurement are
⟨v+​∣Ψ(t)⟩=21+cosθ​,⟨v0​∣Ψ(t)⟩=−2​isinθ​,⟨v−​∣Ψ(t)⟩=2cosθ−1​.
(49)
The Born rule therefore gives the general spin-one transverse measurement after axial precession:
Pr(+ℏ)=cos42θ​,Pr(0)=21​sin2θ,Pr(−ℏ)=sin42θ​.
(50)
At the specified times tk​=πk/ω, θ=πk, so
Pr(+ℏ)=21+(−1)k​,Pr(0)=0,Pr(−ℏ)=21−(−1)k​.​
(51)
For even k, the result is certainly +ℏ; for odd k, it is certainly −ℏ. Although zero belongs to the spectrum of the observable, it never occurs at these times. Indeed, for even k the evolved vector is v+​, while for odd k it is −v−​, where the minus sign is a global phase.
