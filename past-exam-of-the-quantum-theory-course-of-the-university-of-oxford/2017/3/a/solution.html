The given angular momentum commutation relations imply [L3​,L1​]=iℏL2​ and [L3​,L2​]=−iℏL1​. By linearity,
[L3​,L±​]=iℏL2​±i(−iℏL1​)=±ℏL±​​.
(35)
Similarly,
[L+​,L−​]=−2i[L1​,L2​]=2ℏL3​​.
(36)
For the remaining commutators, the commutator product rule gives, for each j,
[L2,Lj​]=∑i​(Li​[Li​,Lj​]+[Li​,Lj​]Li​)=iℏ∑i,k​ϵijk​(Li​Lk​+Lk​Li​)=0.
(37)
The last sum vanishes because the Levi-Civita symbol is antisymmetric under i↔k, while Li​Lk​+Lk​Li​ is symmetric. Taking j=3 and then linear combinations with j=1,2 proves
[L2,L3​]=0=[L2,L±​].​
(38)
If L2ψ=λℏ2ψ and L3​ψ=mℏψ, these identities yield
L2L+​ψ=L+​L2ψ=λℏ2L+​ψ,L3​L+​ψ=(L+​L3​+ℏL+​)ψ=(m+1)ℏL+​ψ.
(39)
One must also prove that this candidate eigenstate is nonzero. Since the angular momentum operators are self-adjoint operators, L+∗​=L−​, and expanding the product gives
L−​L+​=L12​+L22​+i[L1​,L2​]=L2−L32​−ℏL3​.
(40)
Therefore
∥L+​ψ∥2=ℏ2[λ−m(m+1)]∥ψ∥2.
(41)
This is nonnegative and, by the assumed inequality, nonzero. Hence it is positive. The angular momentum raising operator really does produce a nonzero simultaneous eigenstate with the claimed eigenvalues.
To prove that raising terminates, let v be any nonzero state reached after r steps, with projection q=m+r. Positivity gives
λℏ2∥v∥2=∑i=13​∥Li​v∥2≥∥L3​v∥2=q2ℏ2∥v∥2,
(42)
so q2≤λ. Thus the sequence cannot remain nonzero for every integer r≥0. At its last nonzero state v=(L+​)kψ, one has L+​v=0, and the squared norm identity gives
λ=(m+k)(m+k+1)for some integer k≥0.​
(43)
Under the paper's initial inequality the first step is nonzero, so in fact k≥1. An angular momentum ladder endpoint must therefore be reached: bounded projection follows from positivity, and the final raising norm fixes the total angular momentum eigenvalue. Writing j=m+k, the complementary product L+​L−​=L2−L32​+ℏL3​ gives ∥L−​v∥2=2jℏ2∥v∥2, also showing j≥0.
