First apply the commutator product rule in the form [AB,C]=A[B,C]+[A,C]B:
[N,aj∗​]=∑k=12​(ak∗​[ak​,aj∗​]+[ak∗​,aj∗​]ak​)=aj∗​.
(21)
Repeated use of the commutator product rule gives, for n≥1,
[N,(aj∗​)n]=∑r=0n−1​(aj∗​)r[N,aj∗​](aj∗​)n−1−r=n(aj∗​)n.
(22)
This is the commutator with powers of an eigenoperator. For n=0, the commutator with the identity operator is zero, agreeing with the formula.
Each ak∗​ak​ is unchanged by taking the adjoint operator, so N∗=N in this algebra. Taking the adjoint of a commutator yields [N,(aj∗​)n]∗=[ajn​,N]=najn​. By antisymmetry,
[N,(aj∗​)n]=n(aj∗​)n,[N,ajn​]=−najn​,n=0,1,2,….​
(23)
In particular, each creation operator raises the number operator eigenvalue by one, and each annihilation operator lowers it by one.
