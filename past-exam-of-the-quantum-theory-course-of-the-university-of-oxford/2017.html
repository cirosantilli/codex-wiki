Inside the infinite square well, the Schrödinger equation and Dirichlet boundary conditions are
iℏ∂t∂Ψ​=−2mℏ2​∂x2∂2Ψ​,0<x<a,Ψ(0,t)=Ψ(a,t)=0.
(1)
The wavefunction vanishes outside the box. These walls constrain its values, not its first derivatives: requiring both to vanish at an endpoint would eliminate all the nonzero stationary solutions.
For a nonzero separated solution, separation of variables gives
iℏTT′​=−2mℏ2​ψψ′′​=E,T(t)=T(0)e−iEt/ℏ,−2mℏ2​ψ′′=Eψ.
(2)
The last equation is the time-independent Schrödinger equation. Its energy eigenvalue is real. Multiplication by ψ∗ and integration by parts, with the endpoint values zero, gives
E∫0a​∣ψ∣2dx=2mℏ2​∫0a​∣ψ′∣2dx≥0.
(3)
Equality would force ψ to be constant and hence zero, so a nonzero energy eigenstate has E>0. Writing k=2mE​/ℏ, the general solution is Asinkx+Bcoskx. The left wall gives B=0; the right gives sinka=0. Negative integers reproduce the same functions up to a constant, and n=0 gives the zero function, leaving k=nπ/a for n=1,2,….
Finally ∫0a​sin2(nπx/a)dx=a/2. Thus wavefunction normalization with positive coefficient gives
ψn​(x)=a2​​sinanπx​,En​=2ma2n2π2ℏ2​,n≥1.​
(4)
An arbitrary constant global phase has no effect on the quantum state.

The operator R is the midpoint parity operator about a/2. The angle-subtraction identity gives
Rψn​(x)=a2​​sin(nπ−anπx​)=(−1)n+1ψn​(x).​
(5)
Consequently HRψn​=RHψn​=En​(−1)n+1ψn​. The sine functions form a complete orthonormal basis of L2(0,a), so the simultaneous diagonal action implies commutation on the Hamiltonian operator's operator domain. More directly, R preserves the Dirichlet boundary conditions and (Rψ)′′(x)=ψ′′(a−x); hence HRψ=RHψ whenever Hψ is defined. This checks the domain as well as the differential expressions.
For arbitrary f,g∈L2(0,a), use y=a−x in the inner product:
⟨f,Rg⟩=∫0a​f(x)∗g(a−x)dx=∫0a​f(a−y)∗g(y)dy=⟨Rf,g⟩.
(6)
Also ∥Rf∥=∥f∥, so R is bounded and defined everywhere. The identity therefore proves R∗=R​, with no extra domain restriction. In particular R is a self-adjoint operator; R2=I also makes it a unitary operator.

The requested energy eigenvalue is E2​. By the Born rule, its probability amplitude is the inner product c2​=⟨ψ2​,Ψ(0)⟩. Put u=πx/a and use eiusinu=21​sin2u+isin2u:
c2​=π2​∫0π​sin2ueiusinudu=π1​∫0π​sin22udu+π2i​∫0π​sin2usin2udu=21​.
(8)
The first integral is π/2; the second is zero under u↦π−u. Therefore
Pr(E=E2​)=∣c2​∣2=41​.​
(9)
For the time-independent Hamiltonian operator, expand in its orthonormal basis of energy eigenstates:
Ψ(x,t)=∑n≥1​cn​e−iEn​t/ℏψn​(x).
(10)
Unitary time evolution changes c2​ to c2​e−iE2​t/ℏ, whose modulus is unchanged. Thus the same energy measurement probability holds at every time, even though relative quantum phases and other measurements can change.

Write k=π/a, A=2/a​ and Ψ0​=Aeikxsinkx. Its derivative is
Ψ0′​=Akeikx(coskx+isinkx),∣Ψ0′​∣2=A2k2.
(11)
Integration by parts with the Dirichlet boundary conditions gives the energy expectation
⟨H⟩0​=2mℏ2​∫0a​∣Ψ0′​∣2dx=ma2π2ℏ2​=2E1​.​
(12)
It is constant in time, since the time-independent Hamiltonian operator has fixed energy measurement probabilities: ⟨H⟩t​=∑n​En​∣cn​∣2.
For the midpoint parity operator, RΨ0​=−Ae−ikxsinkx. Hence, again putting u=kx,
⟨R⟩0​=−π2​∫0π​e−2iusin2udu=21​.​
(13)
Indeed the imaginary part integrates to zero by reflection and ∫0π​cos2usin2udu=−π/4. This expectation value is also constant in time, because the bounded self-adjoint operator R commutes with H and hence with its time-evolution operator. Equivalently, ⟨R⟩t​=∑n​(−1)n+1∣cn​∣2.
For the momentum operator, direct evaluation gives
⟨P⟩0​​=−iℏA2k∫0a​sinkx(coskx+isinkx)dx=ℏA2k2a​=aπℏ​.​​
(14)
The real sine-cosine integral vanishes. This expectation value is not constant in time: the infinite square well walls reverse motion. One can prove the change exactly without approximating the wavefunction. At
t∗​=E1​πℏ​=πℏ2ma2​,e−iEn​t∗​/ℏ=e−iπn2=(−1)n,
(15)
the energy eigenstate expansion and Rψn​=(−1)n+1ψn​ give Ψ(t∗​)=−RΨ0​. Differentiation shows PR=−RP, so
⟨P⟩t∗​​=−⟨P⟩0​=−aπℏ​.​
(16)
This half revival in an infinite square well is a concrete counterexample to momentum conservation. The sign from −R is only a global phase.
The apparent interior identity [H,P]=0 cannot justify conservation here: the momentum operator does not preserve the Dirichlet boundary conditions of the Hamiltonian operator's operator domain. For sufficiently regular states, retaining the boundary terms in Ehrenfest theorem instead gives the boundary force in an infinite square well,
dtd⟨P⟩​=2mℏ2​(∣∂x​Ψ(0,t)∣2−∣∂x​Ψ(a,t)∣2).
(17)
In the strictly confined interval, −iℏ∂x​ with both endpoint values zero is symmetric but not itself a self-adjoint operator. The requested integral is the conventional mean momentum; it also equals the full-line momentum mean of the zero-extended state, as measured upon releasing the walls. This domain distinction explains why the formal interior commutator misses the wall force.

On a smooth wavefunction f, the position operator and momentum operator give
[X,P]f=−iℏxf′+iℏ(xf)′=iℏf.
(18)
Thus the canonical commutation relation is [X,P]=iℏI​. Since X and P are self-adjoint operators and m,ω are real, the adjoint operator of a−​ is
a+​=P+imωX.​
(19)
Expanding the products in the stated order is essential:
a+​a−​a−​a+​​=P2+m2ω2X2+imω[X,P]=2mH−mℏω,=P2+m2ω2X2−imω[X,P]=2mH+mℏω.​
(20)
Subtracting gives [a−​,a+​]=2mℏωI​. These identities initially hold on smooth rapidly decaying functions, which contain the quantum harmonic oscillator energy eigenstates used below.
The factorization also proves a lower bound: for a normalized energy eigenstate Hψ=Eψ,
0≤∥a−​ψ∥2=⟨ψ,a+​a−​ψ⟩=2m(E−21​ℏω).
(21)
Therefore E≥E0​:=ℏω/2. The commutator product rule, applied to H=a+​a−​/(2m)+E0​, gives
[H,a+​]=ℏωa+​,[H,a−​]=−ℏωa−​.
(22)
So a nonzero application of the creation operator a+​ raises the energy eigenvalue by ℏω, while the annihilation operator a−​ lowers it by the same amount.
Begin with any energy eigenstate, whose existence is assumed. Repeated lowering must terminate: otherwise it would eventually produce a nonzero energy eigenstate below E0​, contradicting the lower bound. More explicitly, if (E−E0​)/(ℏω)=N+r with integer N≥0 and 0≤r<1, the first N lowering steps are nonzero. If r>0, one further step is also nonzero by its positive squared norm and has energy E0​+(r−1)ℏω<E0​, an impossibility. Thus r=0 and the last state satisfies a−​ψ0​=0. This proves
En​=(n+21​)ℏω,n=0,1,2,….​
(23)
Every level exists as well: ∥a+​ψn​∥2=2m(En​+ℏω/2)=2mℏω(n+1)>0, so repeated raising from ψ0​ never vanishes. The same factorization bounds the energy expectation of any normalized state in the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain below by E0​, proving that the terminated state really is a ground state.
To find its probability density, solve the first-order equation
(P−imωX)ψ0​=−i(ℏψ0′​+mωxψ0​)=0.
(24)
Its solutions are ψ0​=C0​exp[−mωx2/(2ℏ)]. With α=mω/ℏ>0, the supplied Gaussian integral gives
1=∣C0​∣2∫−∞∞​e−αx2dx=∣C0​∣2απ​​.
(25)
Taking C0​=(α/π)1/4 fixes an irrelevant global phase. Consequently
∣ψ0​(x)∣2=πℏmω​​exp(−ℏmωx2​).​
(26)
The ground-state energy is positive even though the potential minimum is zero: it is the oscillator's zero-point energy.

Put γ=2mℏω. If ψn​ is a normalized energy eigenstate at En​, the factorization from part (a) gives
∥a+​ψn​∥2=⟨ψn​,a−​a+​ψn​⟩=γ(n+1).
(27)
It follows inductively that vn​=(a+​)nψ0​ has squared norm γnn!. The commutator with H establishes its energy eigenvalue at each step. Hence
ψn​=(2mℏω)n/2n!​(a+​)nψ0​​​
(28)
is normalized and has En​=(n+21​)ℏω. Distinct levels are orthogonal because H is a self-adjoint operator. In particular,
a+​ψn​=γ(n+1)​ψn+1​,a−​ψn​=γn​ψn−1​.
(29)
To decompose the specified initial wavefunction, retain this exact phase convention for the creation and annihilation operators. Their difference gives
X=2imωa+​−a−​​.
(30)
Since a−​ψ0​=0 and a−​a+​ψ0​=γψ0​,
X2ψ0​​=−4m2ω21​(a+2​−a+​a−​−a−​a+​+a−2​)ψ0​=2mωℏ​(ψ0​−2​ψ2​).​
(31)
This quadratic excitation of an oscillator ground state, Ψ(x,0)=C0−1​X2ψ0​, contains only the n=0 and n=2 energy eigenstates. The printed initial function has an unspecified overall normalization, so the Born rule requires division by its squared norm. After wavefunction normalization the state is
3​1​ψ0​−32​​ψ2​.
(32)
The minus sign is consistent with the paper's convention: its a+​ is i times the usual real-coordinate raising operator multiplied by γ​, so its ψ2​ has the opposite sign to the usual real second harmonic oscillator eigenfunction. The energy measurement probabilities are independent of this choice of phase.
Therefore the complete answer is
Pr(E=21​ℏω)=31​,Pr(E=25​ℏω)=32​.​
(33)
Every other energy has probability zero. At any later time the two coefficients only acquire their separate dynamical phases, so these probabilities remain the same. A direct check of the normalization is
∫−∞∞​x4e−αx2dx=dα2d2​απ​​=4α5/23π​​,α=ℏmω​.
(34)
This also confirms the factor of three in the squared norm of the two-component expansion.

The given angular momentum commutation relations imply [L3​,L1​]=iℏL2​ and [L3​,L2​]=−iℏL1​. By linearity,
[L3​,L±​]=iℏL2​±i(−iℏL1​)=±ℏL±​​.
(35)
Similarly,
[L+​,L−​]=−2i[L1​,L2​]=2ℏL3​​.
(36)
For the remaining commutators, the commutator product rule gives, for each j,
[L2,Lj​]=∑i​(Li​[Li​,Lj​]+[Li​,Lj​]Li​)=iℏ∑i,k​ϵijk​(Li​Lk​+Lk​Li​)=0.
(37)
The last sum vanishes because the Levi-Civita symbol is antisymmetric under i↔k, while Li​Lk​+Lk​Li​ is symmetric. Taking j=3 and then linear combinations with j=1,2 proves
[L2,L3​]=0=[L2,L±​].​
(38)
If L2ψ=λℏ2ψ and L3​ψ=mℏψ, these identities yield
L2L+​ψ=L+​L2ψ=λℏ2L+​ψ,L3​L+​ψ=(L+​L3​+ℏL+​)ψ=(m+1)ℏL+​ψ.
(39)
One must also prove that this candidate eigenstate is nonzero. Since the angular momentum operators are self-adjoint operators, L+∗​=L−​, and expanding the product gives
L−​L+​=L12​+L22​+i[L1​,L2​]=L2−L32​−ℏL3​.
(40)
Therefore
∥L+​ψ∥2=ℏ2[λ−m(m+1)]∥ψ∥2.
(41)
This is nonnegative and, by the assumed inequality, nonzero. Hence it is positive. The angular momentum raising operator really does produce a nonzero simultaneous eigenstate with the claimed eigenvalues.
To prove that raising terminates, let v be any nonzero state reached after r steps, with projection q=m+r. Positivity gives
λℏ2∥v∥2=∑i=13​∥Li​v∥2≥∥L3​v∥2=q2ℏ2∥v∥2,
(42)
so q2≤λ. Thus the sequence cannot remain nonzero for every integer r≥0. At its last nonzero state v=(L+​)kψ, one has L+​v=0, and the squared norm identity gives
λ=(m+k)(m+k+1)for some integer k≥0.​
(43)
Under the paper's initial inequality the first step is nonzero, so in fact k≥1. An angular momentum ladder endpoint must therefore be reached: bounded projection follows from positivity, and the final raising norm fixes the total angular momentum eigenvalue. Writing j=m+k, the complementary product L+​L−​=L2−L32​+ℏL3​ gives ∥L−​v∥2=2jℏ2∥v∥2, also showing j≥0.

Put fl​(θ,ϕ)=sinlθe−ilϕ. For the regular, single-valued orbital angular momentum states, l is a nonnegative integer. Differentiation immediately gives
L3​fl​=−iℏ∂ϕ​fl​=(−iℏ)(−il)fl​=−lℏfl​​.
(44)
For 0<θ<π, the derivatives needed for L2 are
∂θ​fl​=lcotθfl​,∂θ2​fl​=(l2cot2θ−lcsc2θ)fl​,∂ϕ2​fl​=−l2fl​.
(45)
Consequently the angular differential expression inside the parentheses of −L2/ℏ2 equals
[(l2+l)cot2θ−(l+l2)csc2θ]fl​=−l(l+1)fl​,
(46)
using csc2θ−cot2θ=1. Therefore
L2fl​=l(l+1)ℏ2fl​.​
(47)
The expressions extend regularly through the polar coordinate singularities: fl​ is the restriction of the polynomial (x−iy)l to the unit sphere. It is proportional to the lowest-projection spherical harmonic of degree l; for l=0 it is the constant state.

Starting from f−1​=sinθe−iϕ, part (i) gives L2f−1​=2ℏ2f−1​ and L3​f−1​=−ℏf−1​. Apply the angular momentum raising operator explicitly:
L+​f−1​=ℏeiϕ(cosθe−iϕ+icotθ(−isinθe−iϕ))=2ℏcosθ.
(48)
A second application gives
L+​cosθ=−ℏsinθeiϕ.
(49)
Since L+​ commutes with L2, both raised functions retain the eigenvalue 2ℏ2. Their L3​ eigenvalues follow either by differentiating their ϕ dependence or from [L3​,L+​]=ℏL+​. Thus three valid unnormalized functions, in the requested order, are
f−1​=sinθe−iϕ,f0​=cosθ,f+1​=sinθeiϕ.​
(50)
For later Born rule calculations, normalize with the solid angle measure dΩ=sinθdθdϕ. Their squared norms are 8π/3, 4π/3, 8π/3, respectively. For example ∫0π​sin3θdθ=4/3 and ∫0π​cos2θsinθdθ=2/3. A convenient orthonormal basis is therefore
χ−1​=8π3​​sinθe−iϕ,χ0​=4π3​​cosθ,χ+1​=8π3​​sinθeiϕ.
(51)
Orthogonality follows from the distinct Fourier factors in ϕ. An overall phase of each basis function is arbitrary; these choices need not impose the usual phase convention for the spherical harmonics.

Recover L1​ from the given angular momentum raising operator and angular momentum lowering operator:
L1​=21​(L+​+L−​)=iℏ(sinϕ∂θ​+cotθcosϕ∂ϕ​).
(52)
This is the generator of rotations about the first coordinate axis. A natural candidate is the angular function pointing along that axis, fx​=sinθcosϕ. Direct substitution gives
L1​fx​=iℏ(sinϕcosθcosϕ−cotθcosϕsinθsinϕ)=0.
(53)
Also fx​=(f+1​+f−1​)/2, so linearity and part (ii) give L2fx​=2ℏ2fx​. Thus an answer is sinθcosϕ. Its squared norm on the sphere is
∫02π​cos2ϕdϕ∫0π​sin3θdθ=π34​=34π​,
(54)
so the normalized quantum state is
ψx​(θ,ϕ)=4π3​​sinθcosϕ,L2ψx​=2ℏ2ψx​,L1​ψx​=0.​
(55)
The supplied starting state gives an alternative derivation with a changed quantization axis. Put g=cosθ+isinθsinϕ and K+​=L2​+iL3​. The angular momentum commutation relations give [L1​,K+​]=ℏK+​ and [L2,K+​]=0, so K+​ raises the L1​ projection from −ℏ to zero. Direct differentiation gives
L2​g=iℏsinθcosϕ,L3​g=ℏsinθcosϕ,K+​g=2iℏsinθcosϕ,
(56)
which is nonzero and proportional to the same ψx​. This also connects the construction directly to the given state.
