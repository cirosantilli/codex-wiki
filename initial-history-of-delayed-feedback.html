Initial history of delayed feedback (x(t)=ϕ(t),−τ≤t≤0)

OurBigBook.com Words: 148
For positive feedback delay τ, an initial condition at a single instant is insufficient. Prescribe a continuous function ϕ throughout [−τ,0]: for 0≤t≤τ, the derivative depends on x(t−τ)=ϕ(t−τ). A right derivative at t=0 is understood; the earlier history need not itself obey the subsequent control law. Once this first interval is computed, it supplies the delayed values for the next interval. This successive integration is the method of steps for a delay differential equation. [ref]
For the constant initial history of delayed feedback ϕ(t)=1, direct integration gives
x(t)=1−kt(0≤t≤τ),x(t)=1−kt+2k2​(t−τ)2(τ≤t≤2τ).

For k=1, τ=1.2, the plant output crosses zero at t=1, while its measured output remains positive until t=2.2. At t=1.4, the delayed command therefore increases the magnitude of the actual deviation.
