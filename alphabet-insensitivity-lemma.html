Alphabet insensitivity lemma

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Ramsey theory Combinatorial line Hales-Jewett theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 207 Articles: 1
For a finite coloring of [m]N, a combinatorial subspace is (a,b)-insensitive if replacing a by b, or conversely, in any of its variable coordinates leaves the color unchanged. For every number of colors, alphabet size m≥2, and dimension d, sufficiently large N guarantees such a d-dimensional combinatorial subspace for any specified distinct letters a,b.
A proof uses the pigeonhole principle on chains ajbL−j in consecutive coordinate blocks. Choose block lengths from left to right, making each long enough to pigeonhole the vector of colors for every earlier word and every later variable assignment. Process the blocks from right to left. Two equal color vectors give a nonempty variable interval insensitive to a,b. Insensitivity already obtained in later blocks survives all restrictions of earlier coordinates. This lemma proves the Hales-Jewett theorem by induction on alphabet size.
