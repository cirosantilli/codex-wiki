Stoichiometry (νi​)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 561 Articles: 3
Stoichiometry relates the quantities consumed and formed in a chemical reaction. Its coefficients are ratios of entity counts, or equivalently of amount of substance, rather than ratios of masses. For the water chemical equation, consuming two molecular hydrogen molecules per molecular oxygen molecule gives the same 2:1 ratio as consuming two moles of molecular hydrogen per mole of molecular oxygen. Multiplying every entity count by the same factor preserves the ratio. See Flowers et al., Chemistry 2e, reaction stoichiometry.
Coefficients and subscripts do different jobs. The coefficient in 2H2​O counts two water molecules; the subscript says that each contains two hydrogen chemical atoms. Changing coefficients scales quantities. Changing a subscript changes the specified chemical substance.
This distinction catches a common counting error: one mole of water molecules contains two moles of hydrogen chemical atoms and one mole of oxygen chemical atoms. Always specify what is counted.
Stoichiometry records reactant and product multiplicities in a chemical reaction or a chemical reaction network. The change caused by one reaction is its stoichiometric vector; multiplying by the reaction rate gives its contribution to the concentration equations. For two identical reactants the rate-constant convention absorbs any combinatorial factor.
