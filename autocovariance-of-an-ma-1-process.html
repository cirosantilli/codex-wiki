For the MA(1) Xt​=μ+εt​+θεt−1​, centered white noise gives
γ(h)=⎩⎨⎧​σ2(1+θ2),σ2θ,0,​h=0,∣h∣=1,∣h∣≥2,​ρ(h)=⎩⎨⎧​1,θ/(1+θ2),0,​h=0,∣h∣=1,∣h∣≥2.​
(24)
Only products of noise values with matching time indices have nonzero expectation. At lag zero there are two squared weights, 1 and θ2; at lag one there is one shared noise term with weight product θ; at higher lags there are none. The calculation needs zero covariance between distinct noise values, not independence. This tridiagonal finite covariance structure makes the finite-sample innovations of an MA(1) process particularly simple.
The lag-one autocorrelation can never exceed 1/2 in magnitude, since 1+θ2≥2∣θ∣. For θ=0, replacing θ by 1/θ leaves the entire ACF unchanged. For example θ=2 and θ=1/2 both give ρ(1)=2/5; rescaling the latter noise variance by 4 also matches every autocovariance. Moving-average root reflection explains this ambiguity and why a stable invertibility convention helps identify a representation. Equality of second-order laws need not imply equality of all joint probability distributions unless the processes are Gaussian processes. See Bartlett, lecture 6: equivalent representations and covariance of ARMA processes.
