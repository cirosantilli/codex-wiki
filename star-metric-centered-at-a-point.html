Star metric centered at a point

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Topological analysis Metric space
OurBigBook.com Words: 136
Given a metric space (X,d) and a∈X, define a new metric by da​(x,x)=0 and da​(x,y)=d(x,a)+d(a,y) for distinct points. It is the distance obtained by joining every point to a central vertex with its original distance to a as edge length. Symmetry and positive definiteness are immediate. With three distinct points the triangle inequality follows from the extra nonnegative term 2d(z,a) in the route through an intermediate point; repeated-point cases are immediate.
Every b=a is isolated, since Bda​​(b,d(b,a))={b}. At the centre, however, Bda​​(a,r)=Bd​(a,r). Thus a map out of this space is continuous exactly when it is continuous at a in the original metric. The closure of B is B together with a if a∈/B and d(a,B)=0; otherwise it is simply B. This construction isolates every point except the centre while preserving exactly its original neighbourhood filter.
