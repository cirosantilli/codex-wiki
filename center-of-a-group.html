Center of a group (Z(G))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 344 Articles: 1
The center of a group consists of the elements that commute with every element of that group:
Z(G)={z∈G:zg=gz for every g∈G}=⋂g∈G​CG​(g),
(23)
where CG​(g) is the centralizer of g. These are operations whose position in any product can be changed freely. The center is an abelian group and a characteristic subgroup, hence a normal subgroup. A group is an abelian group precisely when Z(G)=G.
For the symmetric group S3​, the conjugacy classes are
{e},{(12),(13),(23)},{(123),(132)}.
(24)
An element belongs to the center precisely when its conjugacy class has one element, so Z(S3​)={e}. Yet commutator subgroup of the symmetric group on three letters gives S3′​=A3​, with three elements. Thus belonging to the commutator subgroup does not mean commuting with everything. The alternating group A3​ is an abelian group as a subgroup, but its nonidentity elements do not commute with the transpositions in the surrounding S3​.
For the dihedral group D8​ of group order eight in abelianization of the square symmetry group, the center is {e,r2}. A rotation ri commutes with s precisely when ri=r−i, which requires i even. No reflection ris commutes with r, since (ris)r=ri−1s whereas r(ris)=ri+1s. Consequently
Z(D8​)={e,r2}=D8′​.
(25)
The half-turn can be performed before or after any square symmetry with the same result. Its appearance both here and in the commutator subgroup is a feature of this example, rather than a general equality. See Conrad, Dihedral groups I, theorem 3.4 and example 3.5.
The center and the commutator subgroup answer different useful questions: which operations already commute with everything, and which operations must disappear for the entire group to commute. In an abelian group these answers are Z(G)=G and G′={e}. Another way to use the center is through conjugation: the group homomorphism g↦(h↦ghg−1) has group kernel Z(G), so the first isomorphism theorem gives G/Z(G)≅Inn(G), the inner automorphism group. The quotient group by the center thus records the distinct conjugation operations; it need not be an abelian quotient.
A nontrivial finite p-group always has a nontrivial center. In the class equation, each noncentral conjugacy class has size divisible by p, so ∣Z(G)∣≡∣G∣≡0(modp). The identity element ensures Z(G) is nonempty, hence it has at least p elements. See McKernan, MIT 18.703, lemmas 23.6–23.7 and definition-lemma 23.10.
