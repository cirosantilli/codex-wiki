Gargamelle

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Weak interaction Neutral current
OurBigBook.com Words: 390
Gargamelle was a heavy-liquid bubble chamber at CERN. Its 1973 collaboration report, Observation of neutrino-like interactions without muon or electron in the Gargamelle neutrino experiment, by F. J. Hasert and colleagues, described hadronic events with no identified muon or electron and evaluated rates relative to charged current events. This is evidence for a neutral current, rather than direct production or photography of a Z boson.[ref]
CERN's Gargamelle account identifies the target liquid as CF3​Br and distinguishes hadronic events from the separate isolated-electron signal. The latter is an antineutrino transferring momentum to an electron, whose electromagnetic particle shower supplies visible tracks. These are different final states, not two names for one event.[ref]
A neutrino beam uses protons striking a target to produce charged pions and kaons; focusing selects parents that undergo particle decay in flight. Their neutrinos pass through shielding into the target. Scanning photographs locates vertices and classifies outgoing tracks.[ref]
Expansion prepares a bubble chamber's superheated liquid; ionization by charged secondaries seeds bubbles for photography.[ref]
The recorded light reveals bubbles, not a directly visible incoming or outgoing neutrino. Reconstructing a vertex and classifying bubble-chamber tracks connects that optical record to a hypothesis about a particle interaction.
The collaboration compared samples with matched hadronic cuts. A flat longitudinal vertex distribution resembled neutrino interactions, but sideways neutrons and multi-step cascades could also flatten it. The background estimate combined visible parent-interaction/secondary-neutron pairs with a cascade calculation including surrounding material. Later proton exposures tested the predicted cascade lengths. Escaping charged hadrons could imitate muons; there was no independent perfect muon tag.[ref]
The inference combines positive evidence for an interaction with controlled alternatives for its visible final state. Shielding suppresses unwanted beam components, yet interactions in surrounding material can generate secondary neutrons; shielding alone cannot certify a neutral current. Nor does a successful neutral current search by itself establish every coupling or the Higgs mechanism. Its importance for the electroweak interaction is that the theory predicts an additional weak channel, with coupling relations that further cross section measurements can test.
