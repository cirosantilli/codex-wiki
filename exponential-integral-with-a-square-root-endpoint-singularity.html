Exponential integral with a square-root endpoint singularity (F(t)=∫01​x1−x​etx−1​dx)

For every real t, the mean value theorem bounds ∣etx−1∣≤∣t∣xe∣t∣ on 0<x<1. Hence the apparent singularity at zero cancels, and the remaining bound ∣t∣e∣t∣/1−x​ is Lebesgue integrable. On a bounded parameter interval ∣t∣≤M, every positive-order parameter derivative of the integrand is bounded in absolute value by eM/1−x​. Repeated dominated differentiation under the integral sign therefore proves that F is a smooth function, with
F(k)(t)=∫01​1−x​xk−1etx​dx(k≥1).
(84)
In particular, F(0)=0, F′(0)=2, and F′′(t)>0. Thus F is strictly convex, lies above its tangent 2t at zero, and satisfies F′(t)>2 for t>0 and F′(t)<2 for t<0. The inequalities follow directly as well by comparing etx to one. The substitution x=1−s2 removes the square-root endpoint singularity for numerical integration:
F(t)=2∫01​1−s2et(1−s2)−1​ds,F′(t)=2∫01​et(1−s2)ds.
(85)
