Contraction iteration error bound (d(xn​,x∗​)≤Lnd(x1​,x0​)/(1−L))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fixed-point theorem Contraction mapping theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 80
Successive steps satisfy d(xn+1​,xn​)≤Lnd(x1​,x0​). Summing the remaining steps with the triangle inequality gives a bound available before the iteration starts:
d(xn​,x∗​)≤1−LLn​d(x1​,x0​)(n≥0).
(150)
After computing a new step, the contraction mapping gives the often sharper bound
d(xn​,x∗​)≤1−LL​d(xn​,xn−1​)(n≥1).
(151)
Equivalently, the residual at any z∈X satisfies d(z,x∗​)≤d(z,Tz)/(1−L). These estimates convert visible step sizes into guaranteed distances to an unknown fixed point. When L is close to one, the factor 1/(1−L) can be large: a small step alone needs interpretation through the contraction constant.
