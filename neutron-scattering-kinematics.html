Neutron scattering kinematics (Q=ki​−kf​,ℏω=Ei​−Ef​)

 ... Subatomic particle Hadron Baryon Nucleon Neutron Neutron scattering
OurBigBook.com Words: 297 Articles: 1
Treat the incident and outgoing neutrons as free nonrelativistic particles outside the specimen. Their wave vectors, de Broglie wavelengths, momenta and kinetic energies obey
ki​=λi​2π​,kf​=λf​2π​,pi​=ℏki​,pf​=ℏkf​,Ei,f​=2mn​ℏ2ki,f2​​.
(1)
Here ki​=∣ki​∣, kf​=∣kf​∣, mn​ is the neutron mass, and ℏ=h/(2π) uses the Planck constant. Subtracting the outgoing projectile quantities from the incoming ones gives the momentum transfer and energy delivered to the specimen:
Δp=ℏQ,Q=ki​−kf​,ΔE=Ei​−Ef​=ℏω.
(2)
Thus Q has units of inverse length, rather than momentum. A positive ω means the specimen gains energy; negative ω means it supplies energy to the neutron. If the angle between incident and outgoing propagation directions is 2θ, the law of cosines gives
Q2=ki2​+kf2​−2ki​kf​cos(2θ).
(3)
Knowing the detector angle alone therefore leaves Q undetermined unless the outgoing energy is known or an elastic scattering assumption is justified.
For a reproducible unit conversion choose mn​=1.67492750056×10−27kg, h=6.62607015×10−34Js and 1eV=1.602176634×10−19J. The first is a recommended central value; the latter two conversions are exact.[ref] With 1 ångström equal to 10−10m and 1meV=10−3 electronvolts, substitution into E=h2/(2mn​λ2) gives
E[meV]=(λ[A˚])281.8042​=2.07212(k[A˚−1])2.
(4)
Square brackets here mean the numerical value in the indicated unit. For an original chosen example, λi​=2 ångströms and λf​=2.5 ångströms give Ei​≃20.451meV, Ef​≃13.089meV and ℏω≃7.362meV. At 2θ=60∘, Q≃2.879A˚−1. These values stipulate an allowed projectile geometry; they do not assert that any particular specimen has an excitation there.
