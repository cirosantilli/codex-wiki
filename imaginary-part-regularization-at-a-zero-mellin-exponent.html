Imaginary-part regularization at a zero Mellin exponent (Im[−a1​ia+logx1​]=a2+log2x1​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Integral transform Mellin transform
OurBigBook.com Words: 79
At exponent zero the complex integral ∫0​x−1e−tx/(ia+logx)dx fails absolute and ordinary convergence through its real part, proportional to dx/(xlogx). The imaginary-part kernel displayed above instead decays as dx/(xlog2x) and is integrable. Thus a positive-exponent family can have a well-defined imaginary-part boundary limit even when its full complex limit is invalid. Endpoint expansion coefficients need not be uniform in that limiting exponent: a sine zero can cancel a gamma-function pole and introduce a new nonzero constant term.
