Abelianization of the square symmetry group (D8ab​≅C2​×C2​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Commutator subgroup Abelianization
OurBigBook.com Words: 276
Let D8​ denote the dihedral group of group order eight acting on a square. Here the subscript specifies group order; the same group is called D4​ in the convention that counts vertices of a polygon, as in the group action example. Let r be a counterclockwise quarter-turn rotation and s a reflection in a diagonal. Then
D8​={e,r,r2,r3,s,rs,r2s,r3s},r4=s2=e,srs=r−1.
(18)
Products act rightmost first. The group commutator of the generators of a group is
[r,s]=r−1s−1rs=r−1(srs)=r−2=r2.
(19)
It is a half-turn rotation, so forcing r and s to commute must identify a half-turn with e.
More concretely, write each element uniquely as risj, with 0≤i<4 and j∈{0,1}. The function
ϕ:D8​⟶C2​×C2​,ϕ(risj)=(imod2,j)
(20)
is a surjective group homomorphism. Indeed, srs=r−1 gives
(risj)(rksℓ)=ri+(−1)jksj+ℓ,
(21)
where the exponents are reduced modulo four and two. Modulo two, −k and k agree, so ϕ adds the two coordinates separately. Its group kernel is N={e,r2}. Since the target is an abelian group, D8′​⊆N; since [r,s]=r2, also N⊆D8′​. Thus
D8′​={e,r2},D8ab​≅C2​×C2​.
(22)
The four cosets are {e,r2}, {r,r3}, {s,r2s}, and {rs,r3s}. Each coset forgets the difference of a half-turn. The quotient group still distinguishes rotations from reflections, and even from odd numbers of quarter-turns; the two surviving choices are independent. This is the Klein four-group, rather than the cyclic group C4​, because each nonidentity element squares to the identity element. For the general dihedral group calculation, see Conrad, Dihedral groups I, theorem A.1; that source uses the vertex-count convention and xyx−1y−1 for individual group commutators.
