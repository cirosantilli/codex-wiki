Haar sampling by Gaussian QR decomposition (Q(G)∼Haar(O(n)))

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Measure Haar measure
OurBigBook.com Words: 128
Generate a real n-by-n matrix G with independent entries having the standard normal distribution. Take its QR decomposition with positive diagonal in the upper triangular matrix factor, G=QR. The orthogonal matrix Q has normalized Haar measure on the full orthogonal group O(n). If a numerical factorization returns Q0​R0​, set S=diag(sgnR0,ii​) and return Q0​S. The signs are nonzero almost surely: successively drawn Gaussian columns have probability zero of lying in the proper linear subspace spanned by earlier columns.
For correctness, every fixed orthogonal matrix U preserves the standard Gaussian column density and column independence, so UG and G have the same probability distribution. The positive-diagonal QR decomposition is unique: two decompositions would give an orthogonal matrix that is also an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal, hence the identity. Therefore Q(UG)=UQ(G) and Q(G) has a left-invariant probability law. Uniqueness of normalized Haar measure on the compact group O(n) identifies that law. Each determinant sign has probability 1/2 by reflection invariance.
