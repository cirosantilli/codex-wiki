Burette

 Home Chemistry Acid-base chemistry Acid (chemistry) Weak acid Weak acid dissociation measurement
OurBigBook.com Words: 269
A burette delivers an adjustable measured volume of a chemical solution; the change in its reading gives the delivered volume in an acid-base titration.
Its downward-increasing scale measures a change in reading, not the initial liquid inventory. For initial reading Ri​ and final reading Rf​ without refilling, the nominal delivered volume is v=Rf​−Ri​. An illustrative 2.35mL to 12.35mL reading therefore represents 10.00mL. There is no requirement to start at zero. An air bubble disappearing from the tip uses part of the apparent delivery to fill the tip, and a hanging drop has not yet entered the sample; both break the intended volume account.
For transparent aqueous samples, read the bottom of the liquid meniscus at eye level; viewing from above or below shifts its apparent alignment. The mark is a comparison between a curved liquid surface and a scale, not a direct measure of amount of substance. Eye-level reading and the distinction between contained and delivered volumes are part of solution preparation and volumetric calibration.[ref]
Both readings contribute measurement uncertainty. If each has independent standard deviation σR​, their difference has standard deviation 2​σR​; a common additive reading offset cancels, while a scale-factor error remains. For the inventory n=cB​v, uncertainty in titrant concentration remains even with perfect readings. This is why repeat delivery, independent volume calibration, and titrant standardization answer different questions.
