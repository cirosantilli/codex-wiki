Poisson distribution (Pois(λ))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 33
For λ>0, a Poisson random variable N has probability mass function
P(N=k)=e−λk!λk​,k=0,1,2,…,EN=Var(N)=λ.
(38)
The exponential function series verifies normalization. At λ=0, define N=0 with certainty. The equality of mean and variance is a distinguishing model property, though it does not by itself characterize the law.
When counting events in a homogeneous Poisson process, distinguish its rate r from the distribution parameter: an interval of duration t has λ=rt. A rate measured per minute times a duration measured in minutes gives a dimensionless expected count. This process assumes independent increments, a constant rate, and a negligible chance of multiple events relative to interval length in a sufficiently short interval. A rate alone does not establish a Poisson distribution. See Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis, chapter 6 summary, pages 46-47.
For a constructed arrival model with r=2 events per minute over t=1.5 minutes, λ=3. The mean and variance are both three, and
P(N=0)=e−3≈0.0497871,P(N=3)=e−33!33​=4.5e−3≈0.2240418.
(39)
The probability of at least one event is 1−e−3≈0.9502129. These results describe the specified process, not every arrival mechanism with the same average rate.
The Poisson limit theorem, often called the law of rare events, connects this law to the binomial distribution. Keep λ>0 fixed and let Kn​∼Bin(n,λ/n) for integers n≥λ. For each fixed nonnegative integer k and sufficiently large n≥k,
P(Kn​=k)=k!λk​(∏j=0k−1​(1−nj​))(1−nλ​)n−k⟶e−λk!λk​.
(40)
There are more opportunities, each less likely to succeed, while the mean stays λ and the variance λ(1−λ/n) approaches λ. For λ=3, the probability of three successes is 0.2668279 at n=10, 0.2310569 at n=50, and 0.2257398 at n=200, approaching the Poisson distribution value 0.2240418.
The panels show the same count scale and the same Poisson distribution reference. At n=10, p=0.3 is not especially small and the difference is visible. No universal trial-count cutoff guarantees an adequate approximation for every event.
The independence assumption matters even when every success is rare. For n≥3, if all n indicator random variables equal one shared Bernoulli random variable of parameter 3/n, their sum is zero with probability 1−3/n and n with probability 3/n. Its mean is three, yet its zero-count probability tends to one, not e−3. Some dependent counts still admit approximation through the Stein-Chen method, but this requires additional control of dependence.
For historical orientation, the BnF records Poisson's original probability treatise, Recherches sur la probabilité des jugements, as published in Paris in 1837. This bibliographic fact alone does not establish when the modern distribution terminology arose. See BnF record of Poisson's 1837 treatise.
