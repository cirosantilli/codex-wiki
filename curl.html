For a continuously differentiable vector field in three-dimensional Euclidean space, the component of its curl along a unit normal vector is the limiting circulation per unit area around a small loop in the perpendicular plane. The loop direction and the normal obey the right-hand rule. Stokes theorem gives the corresponding relation for a finite surface.
For the rigidly rotating velocity field G=(−Ωy,Ωx,0), direct differentiation gives ∇×G=(0,0,2Ω). A circle of radius R has tangential speed ΩR and circumference 2πR, so its circulation is 2πΩR2, twice the angular velocity times the enclosed area. The factor of two distinguishes vorticity from rigid-body angular velocity.
Curved integral curves alone do not demonstrate nonzero curl. The field H=(x,−y,0) has zero curl, yet its trajectories satisfy x=x0​et, y=y0​e−t and hence xy=x0​y0​: away from the axes they are hyperbolas. The curl measures local circulation of the field, not the curvature of an individual trajectory.
