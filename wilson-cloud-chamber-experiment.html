Wilson cloud-chamber experiment

 Home Physics Particle detection Particle detector Cloud chamber Expansion cloud chamber
OurBigBook.com Words: 216
Charles Thomson Rees Wilson's 1927 lecture recalls electron cloud trails produced with X-rays in 1911, then alpha particle trails from a radium-tipped source. The improved 1912 chamber had a wide, shallow space with a suddenly falling floor to limit stirring. A roof–floor clearing electric field removed unwanted ions. Sharp photography required expansion, then the ionizing particle, then illumination.[ref]
The piston mechanism connects the space beneath the moving floor to an evacuated vessel: opening the valve lets the floor drop. Water in a trough supplies the vapor. The Smithsonian's surviving demonstration object is explicitly a 1961 replica of the 1912 apparatus.[ref]
After dust removal, X-rays increased droplet formation above a lower expansion threshold; larger expansions also produced dense background cloud.[ref]
The control logic separates a localized ionization trail from fog. Delays permit ion displacement and drop growth; stirring deforms the trail. A photograph alone supplies neither an absolute event time nor a calibrated particle energy.
