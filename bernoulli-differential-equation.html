Bernoulli differential equation (y′+P(x)y=Q(x)yn)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
A Bernoulli differential equation is a first-order ordinary differential equation of the displayed form, with P and Q continuous functions on an interval and a fixed real exponent n. A nonlinear power can hide a first-order linear differential equation: on a suitable branch, the Bernoulli power substitution z=y1−n removes it exactly. The transformed solution of a differential equation can be completely regular even when recovering y produces finite-time blowup.
For example, the reciprocal Bernoulli equation with finite-time blowup y′+y=exy2 becomes z′−z=−ex under z=1/y. The initial conditions y(0)=1/2 and y(0)=−1 select a positive trajectory blowing up at x=2 and a negative trajectory tending to zero as x→∞, respectively. The integrating factor exposes both behaviors with the same short calculation.
The cases n=0 and n=1 are already first-order linear differential equations: respectively y′+Py=Q, with the zeroth-power term interpreted as the constant 1, and y′+(P−Q)y=0. There is no need for a nonlinear change of variable; for n=1, y1−n=1 would discard the unknown entirely. For other exponents, the substitution must respect the domain of exponentiation and may lose the zero solution of a differential equation.[ref]
