Principal ideal ring

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Ring
OurBigBook.com Words: 86
A principal ideal ring is a ring whose left ideals and right ideals each have one generator. For a commutative ring, this says that every ideal is a principal ideal. The examples here use commutative rings. Unlike a principal ideal domain, it may have zero divisors: Z/4Z has this property, with ideals (0), (2), and (1). A finite direct product of rings that are principal ideal rings is again a principal ideal ring. Indeed, the coordinate idempotent elements show that every ideal is a product of coordinate ideals; a tuple of their generators generates the product.
