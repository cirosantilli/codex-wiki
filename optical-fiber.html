Optical fiber

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 13
An optical fiber is a thin, flexible electromagnetic waveguide that carries light along its length. In a conventional step-index optical fiber, an optical fiber core with refractive index n1​ is surrounded by an optical fiber cladding with lower refractive index n2​. Suitable light rays remain confined by total internal reflection at this interface. The cladding is an optical part of the guide, distinct from its protective coating.[ref]
The useful surprise is that a transparent strand can deliver light around a route that a free beam would leave. This makes optical fibers useful for communications and for carrying collected light to a fiber-fed spectrograph. Guidance, successful launch, and surviving optical power are separate questions: the optical fiber acceptance angle constrains launch geometry, while optical fiber attenuation describes loss along the route.
