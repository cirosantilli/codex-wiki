Electron accelerating-voltage wavelength (λ=h/2me​eU​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Wave function Matter wave de Broglie wavelength
OurBigBook.com Words: 287
Let an electron start with negligible kinetic energy and move through an increase U>0 in electric potential. Its electric charge is q=−e, where e>0 is the elementary charge, so the decrease in potential energy is eU. In a field-free region after acceleration,
K=eU,∣p∣=2me​eU​,λ=2me​eU​h​≃U/(1V)​1.2264nm​.
(11)
This is an electron formula, not a universal conversion from voltage to wavelength. It assumes negligible initial kinetic energy, negligible losses, and eU≪me​c2, where c is the speed of light. It describes the de Broglie wavelength outside the crystal; the potential energy inside a material can change the local kinetic momentum.
For U=54V, take h=6.62607015×10−34Js, e=1.602176634×10−19C, and me​≃9.1093837×10−31kg. The rounded electron mass is consistent with the 2022 recommended constants.[ref]
K=8.65175×10−18J=54eV,∣p∣≃3.97019×10−24kgms−1,λ≃1.66895×10−10m=0.166895nm.
(12)
The electronvolt is an energy unit: 54eV must be converted to joules before combining it with me​ in SI units. Dimensional analysis checks both steps:
[eU]=CV=J,[2me​eU​]=kgJ​=kgms−1,[h/p]=kgms−1Js​=m.
(13)
The resulting velocity is about 4.3584×106ms−1, or 0.01454c, so the nonrelativistic assumption is consistent. For a larger accelerating voltage, the relativistic energy-momentum relation gives pc=K(K+2me​c2)​, with K=eU, while λ=h/p still holds. At 54V the relativistic wavelength is shorter by about 0.00264%.
Explicitly, the exact special relativity formula and its nonrelativistic quantum mechanics limit are
λrel​(U)=eU(eU+2me​c2)​hc​=2me​eU​h​(1+2me​c2eU​)−1/2eU≪me​c2≃​2me​eU​h​.
(14)
Here K=eU is the gained kinetic energy; the total energy is E=me​c2+K. At 80kV, the nonrelativistic quantum mechanics expression gives 4.336pm, about 3.84% larger than the exact 4.176pm. This difference matters when using electron diffraction ring positions to infer lattice plane spacings.
