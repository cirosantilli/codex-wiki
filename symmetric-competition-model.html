Symmetric competition model (x˙=λx−μxy,y˙​=λy−μxy)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Population dynamics
OurBigBook.com Words: 87
Two populations grow at the same per-capita rate and each loses members at a rate proportional to their product. On the invariant diagonal x=y=z, the logistic differential equation gives z(t)=λ/[μ+(λ−μ)e−λt] for z(0)=1 and positive rates. The positive symmetric equilibrium (λ/μ,λ/μ) is stable along that diagonal but unstable transversely: (x−y)′=λ(x−y), while the Jacobian matrix has eigenvalues −λ,+λ. Thus an exactly symmetric deterministic solution can relax toward coexistence while a small imbalance grows. A corresponding density-dependent Markov jump process has a controlled fluid limit on each fixed finite interval, rather than uniform control for all time.
