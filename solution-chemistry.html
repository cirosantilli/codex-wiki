Solution (chemistry)

 Home Chemistry Mixture
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 49
A chemical solution is a homogeneous mixture in which designated solutes are distributed in a solvent. Representative equal-volume samples have the same composition after mixing. Individual molecules and ions remain distinguishable. The solute/solvent labels describe their roles in this mixture.[ref]
A dissolved solute is dispersed as molecules, chemical atoms, or ions, rather than as the larger solid particles of a particle suspension. For example, sucrose retains its molecular formula during dissolution in water; a chemical reaction is not required. Dissolved sodium chloride supplies sodium cations and chloride anions, surrounded by water molecules through solvation. Dissolved does not mean chemically destroyed or converted into water.[ref]
This makes a useful connection between microscopic identity and laboratory sampling. If a chemical solution is well mixed, the amount of substance in a measured portion follows from its concentration and volume. A scoop from a poorly mixed particle suspension need not represent the whole vessel. Visual clarity alone cannot establish composition or exclude a fine dispersion; the quantitative claim needs representative sampling.
