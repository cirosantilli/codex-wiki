Triangle-removal proof of Roth theorem

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Additive combinatorics Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions
OurBigBook.com Words: 116
Embed A⊆[N] in the odd cyclic group G=Z/(2N+1)Z, so additive three-term relations in A cannot wrap around. Form three copies of G, putting edges x∼y when y−x∈A, y∼z when z−y∈A, and x∼z when (z−x)/2∈A. A triangle in a graph encodes a+b=2c in A. If A has no nonconstant arithmetic progression, every triangle in a graph has a=b=c; there are exactly ∣G∣∣A∣ triangles in a graph, and they are edge-disjoint. Thus at least ∣G∣∣A∣ edges must be removed to eliminate them. For fixed positive subset density of A, this is a positive fraction of the squared vertex count, whereas the triangle in a graph count is only quadratic. The triangle removal lemma permits deletion of an arbitrarily small quadratic fraction when the cubic triangle in a graph subset density is sufficiently small. This contradiction establishes Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions.
