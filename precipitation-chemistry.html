Precipitation (chemistry)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 14
Precipitation is formation of a solid from a chemical solution; the formed solid is a precipitate. Dissolved chemical species enter a separate thermodynamic phase. A newly formed solid can create a surface for heterogeneous catalysis; observing it does not establish the composition or catalytic role of that surface.
For example, mixing suitable chemical solutions of calcium chloride and sodium carbonate can form calcium carbonate:
Ca2+(aq)+CO32−​(aq)⟶CaCO3​(s).
(2)
Here aq means dissolved in water, and s marks the solid. Both sides contain one calcium chemical atom, one carbon chemical atom, and three oxygen chemical atoms; net electric charge is +2−2=0 on the left and zero on the right. The arrow describes the net change when precipitation occurs, rather than promising complete removal of either ion. The calcium carbonate precipitation experiment makes this phase separation visible.[ref]
The attraction is that an invisible dissolved inventory becomes a recoverable solid. Net ionic equations track that transfer; the saturation criterion for precipitation determines whether it is favored under specified conditions. This combination supports separations and chemical analysis.[ref]
