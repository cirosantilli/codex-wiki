Finite mean matters in Wald's equation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Wald's equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 251
For a simple symmetric random walk starting at zero, let T1​ be its first hitting time of 1. The infinite mean first passage of a simple symmetric random walk shows that T1​<∞ almost surely but E[T1​]=∞. Since ST1​​=1, assuming a finite expected value for T1​ would let Wald's equation assert 1=0E[T1​]=0, a contradiction. The formal expression 0⋅∞ is not an allowed use of the theorem.[ref]
Compare T±2​=inf{n≥0:∣Sn​∣=2}. From every surviving position in {−1,0,1}, three consecutive positive increments force absorption within three steps. Each fresh block has probability 1/8 of this event, so P(T±2​>3k)≤(7/8)k and E[T±2​]≤24. This establishes finiteness before invoking the theorem. Even for T1​, each bounded stopping time T1​∧n satisfies E[ST1​∧n​]=0. The stopped process tends to 1 almost surely, but its expected value does not tend to 1. Rare long negative excursions retain the balancing contribution. The existing stopped-walk martingale with almost sure but not L1 convergence explains the related failure of uniform integrability; almost sure convergence alone does not permit exchanging a limit and an expected value.
