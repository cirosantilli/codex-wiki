Diatomic bond stretch (q=r−re​,ω=k/μ​)

Let r be the instantaneous bond length, re​>0 its equilibrium value, and q=r−re​ the stretching displacement. Assume one fixed electronic quantum state, a smooth potential energy V(r) with a nondegenerate local minimum at re​, and omit molecular rotation initially. The Taylor series has no linear term because V′(re​)=0:
V(re​+q)=V0​+21​kq2+O(q3),V0​=V(re​),k=V′′(re​)>0.
(25)
Keeping only the quadratic term is the harmonic approximation near a potential minimum. The bond force constant k has units Nm−1. It measures local stiffness; it is not the bond-dissociation energy, which concerns the depth and distant shape of the entire well.
The two-particle relative coordinate explains the reduced mass. For stretching along a fixed axis in a stationary centre of mass frame, let u1​,u2​ be the nuclear displacements from equilibrium. Then u2​−u1​=q and m1​u1​+m2​u2​=0, so
u1​=−m1​+m2​m2​​q,u2​=m1​+m2​m1​​q,T=21​m1​u˙12​+21​m2​u˙22​=21​μq˙​2,μ=m1​+m2​m1​m2​​.
(26)
Here T is the internal kinetic energy, and m1​,m2​>0 are the two nuclear masses. The lighter partner moves farther; the reduced mass is smaller than either individual mass. The restoring force −dV/dq≃−kq gives simple harmonic motion:
μq¨​=−kq,q(t)=Acos(ωt+ϕ),ω=μk​​,ν=2πω​,νharm​=2πcω​.
(27)
The angular frequency is ω, the ordinary frequency is ν, and νharm​ is the corresponding spectroscopic wavenumber; c is the speed of light. Use c in cms−1 to obtain νharm​ in cm−1. The symbol k here is a bond force constant, not an angular wavenumber.
For an original dimensionless example, choose m1​=m2​=2 and k=4 in consistent model units. Then μ=1 and ω=2. Releasing at q(0)=0.1 with q˙​(0)=0 gives q(t)=0.1cos(2t) and u1​=−q/2, u2​=q/2. Each atomic nucleus moves only half the stretching amplitude, while the conserved internal mechanical energy above V0​ is 21​k(0.1)2=0.02. Fixing one partner instead would give ω=2​, demonstrating why a one-body calculation changes the model.
