Radiometry

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 6k Articles: 40
Radiometry measures the energy and power carried by electromagnetic radiation, including light. It asks how much radiation a source supplies, where it travels, and how much reaches a receiving surface. The measurements are physical rather than weighted by the sensitivity of the human eye: equal radiant fluxes at different wavelengths need not look equally bright. [ref].
The five quantities below answer different questions about the same radiation. The International System of Units makes the distinctions visible: a watt is a joule per second, while a steradian measures solid angle. [ref].
QuantityWhat is resolved?Units
Radiant flux ΦTotal radiation power in the specified beam or collectionW
Radiant intensity IPower per outgoing solid angleWsr−1
Radiance LPower per projected area and per solid angleWm−2sr−1
Irradiance EIncident power per receiving areaWm−2
Radiant exitance MOutward emitted power per emitting areaWm−2
Resolving wavelength or frequency adds a further “per unit interval”, as in spectral radiance. The notation is consistent with NIST's table of spectral quantities. In particular, radiant flux is power, whereas the existing astronomical radiative flux is power per area. [ref].
This bookkeeping makes an optical camera's collected signal calculable from a source's radiance and the geometry of the receiving aperture. It also lets radiation measurements from different instruments be compared. For example, NIST describes separate calibrations of spectral radiance and spectral irradiance for instruments used to observe Earth. [ref].
