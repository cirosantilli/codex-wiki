Electric dipole

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrostatics Electric dipole moment
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 5
A finite electric dipole consists of equal and opposite electric charges separated by a nonzero displacement. It has charge neutrality but a nonzero electric dipole moment p=qd. An ideal point electric dipole is the mathematical limit d=∣d∣→0 with p fixed, so q must increase as 1/d. Shrinking the separation while holding q fixed instead makes the electric dipole moment vanish. The ideal electric dipole describes an exterior electric field; it does not reproduce the two near-charge singularities or the internal structure of a molecule. [ref].
Place the pair's midpoint at the origin and write ke​=1/(4πϵ0​), where ϵ0​ is vacuum permittivity. By superposition principle and Coulomb's law, its exact electric potential away from the two point charges is
Φpair​(r)=ke​q(∣r−d/2∣1​−∣r+d/2∣1​).
(33)
For r=∣r∣≫d, retain the first nonzero term of a Taylor series. The electric potential and its negative gradient become
Φdip​(r)=ke​r2p⋅r​,Edip​(r)=r3ke​​[3(p⋅r)r−p],r>0.
(34)
Here r=r/r and the products in parentheses are dot products. These are exact exterior formulas for the ideal electric dipole and distant approximations for a finite pair. The electric field on the outer axis points along p with magnitude 2ke​p/r3; on the perpendicular bisector it points opposite p with magnitude ke​p/r3. This directional structure is what a scalar total electric charge misses. [ref].
In a prescribed uniform external electric field Eext​, a rigid pair has opposite forces F±​=±qEext​. Its net force is zero, but summing their moments about the midpoint gives the torque
τ=2d​×qEext​+(−2d​)×(−qEext​)=p×Eext​.
(35)
The cross product makes the torque perpendicular to both the electric dipole moment and the external electric field. If θ is the angle from Eext​ to p in their plane, the signed turning torque is τθ​=−pEext​sinθ. It tends to reduce a small positive θ. [ref].
The external interaction potential energy, with zero chosen at θ=π/2, is
U=−p⋅Eext​=−pEext​cosθ,τθ​=−dθdU​.
(36)
Parallel alignment minimizes this potential energy; antiparallel alignment maximizes it even though the torque is zero in both positions. This omits the pair's constant internal binding potential energy and assumes a fixed permanent electric dipole moment. [ref].
A nonuniform external electric field can exert a net force on a neutral electric dipole. Before taking the small-pair approximation, the exact external force is F=q[Eext​(R+d/2)−Eext​(R−d/2)], where R is the midpoint. A first-order Taylor series gives, for a fixed electric dipole moment and an external electric field varying slowly across d,
F≃(p⋅∇)Eext​=∇(p⋅Eext​)
(37)
in electrostatics. The last equality uses the vanishing curl of the external electric field, with p held fixed while differentiating. Thus uniform external electric fields turn a misaligned electric dipole, whereas gradients can also move its midpoint. For example, the local source-free electric field Eext​=Gxx+(E0​−Gy)y​ gives F=(Gpx​,−Gpy​,0), so reversing the electric dipole moment reverses the net force. This stipulated local model has zero divergence and zero curl.
A motivating experiment is Filsinger and colleagues' study of electrostatic deflection of polar molecules. Benzonitrile (C7​H5​N) in helium expanded from a pulsed valve into vacuum; two 1-mm skimmers narrowed the beam before a 15-cm deflector. A grounded trough and a high-voltage rod produced a vertical electric field gradient. Scanning a focused laser vertically downstream and recording benzonitrile ions from photoionization measured the beam's relative density. With voltage off, the profile spanned about 2 mm; applying voltage shifted and broadened it upwards. At 10 kV, many molecules lay outside the original profile. [ref].
Interpretation uses a quantum state's Stark effect energy W(E): μeff​=−dW/dE gives Fy​=μeff​∂y​E. States whose energy falls with increasing electric field seek stronger fields. The voltage-off profile controls the mechanical aperture. Inferring state populations needs calculated trajectories, speed distributions and a rotational temperature model; profile displacement alone does not measure a permanent electric dipole moment uniquely. This is an electric Stark effect experiment, with photoionization occurring after deflection. [ref].
For the symmetric finite pair, the first omitted electric multipole expansion term after the electric dipole is third order, so typical relative errors scale as (d/r)2. This stronger error estimate need not hold for an arbitrary neutral molecule with a nonzero electric quadrupole moment. A concrete check for a pair on the z axis compares the exact and ideal electric fields at r>d/2 on the positive axis and at r>0 on the perpendicular bisector:
Ez,dip​(0,0,r)Ez,pair​(0,0,r)​=(1−4r2d2​)−2,Ez,dip​(r,0,0)Ez,pair​(r,0,0)​=(1+4r2d2​)−3/2.
(38)
Both perpendicular-bisector components are negative. At r=5d, the ratios are approximately 1.0203 and 0.9852: errors of +2.03% and −1.48% relative to the ideal electric field. The condition r≫d concerns observing the pair's own electric field; the separate condition for its external force approximation concerns variation of the applied electric field across the pair.
