The Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation keeps the real part discarded in the proof of the Robertson uncertainty principle. Write A′=A−⟨A⟩I and B′=B−⟨B⟩I. Their quantum covariance is CAB​=Re⟨A′ψ∣B′ψ⟩. On the same finite-moment and product operator domains used above, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
(ΔA)2(ΔB)2≥∣⟨A′ψ∣B′ψ⟩∣2=CAB2​+41​∣⟨[A,B]⟩∣2.

A larger coordinatewise product can therefore encode a tilted covariance ellipse, rather than a larger determinant. This explains why the equality case of the Heisenberg uncertainty relation is more restrictive than equality in the Robertson-Schrödinger uncertainty relation.
