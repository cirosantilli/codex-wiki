Oxidation state sum rule (∑i​OSi​=z)

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Redox Oxidation Oxidation state
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
Sum oxidation states over all chemical atoms of the specified molecule, ion or formula unit, including multiplicities:
∑i​OSi​=z,q=ze.
(24)
Here q is the net electric charge and e the positive elementary charge. For a neutral entity the sum is zero; for an ion it equals its signed electric charge number z. The reason is simple: the allocation assigns every valence electron exactly once, so the sum is the neutral electron budget minus the actual electron count.
For water, 2(+1)+(−2)=0; for hydrogen peroxide, 2(+1)+2(−1)=0. In permanganate, MnO4−​, assigning each oxygen −2 gives x+4(−2)=−1, so manganese has oxidation state +7. This is not a free Mn7+ ion: the entire permanganate anion has net electric charge −e. The sum rule supplies a constraint; without additional assignments it cannot determine every individual oxidation state.
