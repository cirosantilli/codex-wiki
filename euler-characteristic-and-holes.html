Euler characteristic and holes (χ=b0​−b1​+b2​−⋯)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Homology (mathematics) Simplicial homology Euler characteristic
OurBigBook.com Words: 111
For a finite CW complex, b0​ counts connected components: its connected components are its path components, as recorded by the zeroth homology group. For a finite graph regarded as a one-dimensional CW complex, no higher homology groups occur, so b1​=E−V+b0​ counts independent graph cycles, and χ=b0​−b1​. For a connected planar surface obtained by removing h disjoint open disks from the interior of a closed disk, (b0​,b1​,b2​)=(1,h,0) and χ=1−h; the annulus is h=1.
The word "hole" needs its dimension. A two-sphere encloses a cavity but has b1​=0 and b2​=1. A closed orientable surface of genus g instead has (b0​,b1​,b2​)=(1,2g,1) and χ=2−2g: increasing the genus by one adds two independent one-dimensional homology classes. In higher dimensions, further homology groups enter with alternating signs; there is no universal "components minus handles" rule.
