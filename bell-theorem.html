Bell theorem

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Foundations of quantum mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 32
Bell's theorem shows that no theory assigning outcomes through local hidden variables can reproduce every quantum correlation.
The force of the Bell theorem is that a disagreement about a local hidden-variable theory becomes a constraint on frequencies in a Bell test. Quantum entanglement alone does not guarantee a violation for every measurement in quantum mechanics: the quantum state, analyzer settings and treatment of missed events all matter. A violation of the CHSH inequality tests the conjunction of Bell local causality, measurement independence and the stated outcome and sampling assumptions; it remains compatible with quantum no-signalling.
