With the quotient realization defined in part (i), the answers are no and no. Write W=∣K∣ as countably many closed arms [0,1]n​ with all their zero endpoints identified to v.
First, the outer vertices D={un​:n≥1} form a closed discrete subset. Its intersection with every closed arm is the closed singleton at coordinate one, so D is closed by the weak topology. The terminal half-arm (1/2,1]n​ is an open neighbourhood of un​ meeting D only there. Thus D is infinite, closed and discrete. Since closed discrete subsets of compact spaces are finite, W is not compact. A homeomorphism to a compact subset of the plane would make W compact, which is impossible.
For the stronger obstruction, suppose (Un​) were a countable neighbourhood basis at v. Each Un​ contains an initial interval in arm n, so choose xn​∈Un​ on that arm with coordinate tn​∈(0,1). Define V to meet arm n in [0,tn​/2). It is an open neighbourhood of v, because each of these intersections is open in the closed interval [0,1]. But xn​∈Un​∖V for every n, so no Un​ is contained in V. This contradicts the basis condition.
Hence W is not first countable. Every metric space is first-countable, with local basis of balls of radius 1/n, and this property passes to subsets with the subspace topology. Thus W is not homeomorphic to any subset of R2. This is the weak realization of a countable star.
The realization convention matters. For the metric realization of a simplicial complex using Euclidean distance on barycentric coordinates, the second answer would be yes: map the point on arm n at parameter t to tei/n. Each ray is isolated away from zero, and at zero both spaces have neighbourhood bases given by a single radius on all arms. This gives the metric realization of a countable star as a planar subset, still noncompact. It does not give the quotient topology used in part (i).
