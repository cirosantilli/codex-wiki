An abstract simplicial complex is a pair K=(V,Σ), where V is a set of vertices and Σ is a family of finite nonempty subsets of V satisfying two conditions: every singleton {v} is in Σ, and every nonempty subset of a member of Σ is again in Σ. Its members are the simplexes; a face with n+1 vertices has dimension n.
To form its topological realization, take a disjoint copy of the standard geometric simplex Δ∣σ∣−1 for every σ∈Σ, label its vertices by σ, and identify shared labelled faces using affine face inclusions. Give the resulting space ∣K∣ the quotient topology of this disjoint union. Equivalently, its points have finite-support barycentric coordinates λv​≥0 with ∑v​λv​=1 and positive support belonging to Σ.
The topology is part of the definition: U⊆∣K∣ is open exactly when U∩∣σ∣ is open in each closed geometric simplex. This is the weak topology of a simplicial realization, used below for the infinite complex. For finite complexes it coincides with the usual Euclidean realization topology.[ref]
