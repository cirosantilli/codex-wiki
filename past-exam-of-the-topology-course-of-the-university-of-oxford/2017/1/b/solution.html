For every k∈K, the Hausdorff condition gives disjoint open neighbourhoods Uk​ of k and Vk​ of x. The Uk​ form an open cover of K, so choose a finite subcover Uk1​​,…,Ukm​​. Put
U=⋃j=1m​Ukj​​,V=⋂j=1m​Vkj​​.
(5)
Then U,V are open sets, K⊆U and x∈V. If a point belonged to U∩V, it would belong to some Ukj​​ and to Vkj​​, contrary to their choice. Thus
K⊆U,x∈V,U∩V=∅.​
(6)
The finiteness matters: a finite intersection of open sets is open. This is separation of a point and a compact set. If K=∅, use U=∅, V=X.
Every x∈X∖K consequently has an open neighbourhood V contained in X∖K. The complement is open, so a compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed.
The conclusion can fail without the Hausdorff condition. Take the two-point set X={0,1} with the indiscrete topology {∅,X}. The singleton K={0} is compact, since one member of any open cover suffices. It is not closed, because its complement {1} is not open. The two points cannot have disjoint open neighbourhoods, so this example is indeed not Hausdorff.
