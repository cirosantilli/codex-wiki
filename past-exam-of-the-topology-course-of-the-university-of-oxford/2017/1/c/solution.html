A subset A is connected when it is a connected space in its subspace topology: there is no separation of a topological space
A=P⊔Q
(7)
into two nonempty disjoint open subsets of A. Equivalently, the only clopen sets of A are ∅ and A.
Since the given A is not connected, choose such P,Q. Each is the complement of the other in A, so both are also closed subsets of A. They are compact by the closed subset of a compact space result. We now separate these two compact subsets by ambient open sets.
For each p∈P, part (b), applied to Q and p, gives disjoint ambient open sets Gp​,Hp​ with p∈Gp​ and Q⊆Hp​. Choose p1​,…,pm​ such that the Gpj​​ cover P, using its compactness. Define
U=⋃j=1m​Gpj​​,V=⋂j=1m​Hpj​​.
(8)
Then P⊆U, Q⊆V, and U,V are disjoint open sets. Indeed a point of U lies in some Gpj​​, whereas every point of V lies in the corresponding Hpj​​. Consequently
A⊆U∪V,A∩U=P=∅,A∩V=Q=∅.​
(9)
This proves the requested ambient separation, using the more general separation of disjoint compact subsets. The initially given relatively open sets P,Q need not themselves be open in X; the finite construction supplies ambient open neighbourhoods.
