Fix x0​,y0​∈Z. For a continuous function f:Z×Z→R, every coordinate slice is continuous, because the maps x↦(x,y) and y↦(x0​,y) into the product topology are continuous. Their target R is Hausdorff. Part (a)(i) gives
f(x,y)=f(x0​,y)=f(x0​,y0​)for every (x,y).
(19)
Therefore every such f is a constant function. This is the two-factor case of continuous maps from products of cofinite spaces to Hausdorff spaces are constant.
The second coordinate projection π2​:Z×R→R is continuous and nonconstant. If a homeomorphism H:Z×Z→Z×R existed, π2​∘H would be a nonconstant continuous function, contradicting the preceding conclusion. Hence
Z×Z≅Z×R.​
(20)
For the half-plane, put H+​=[0,∞) as a set, so K=R×H+​. Its two ambient topologies give different answers:
ambient spaceZ×ZZ×R​K closed?noyes​K compact?yesno.​​
(21)
In Z×Z, if K were closed, its inverse image under the continuous map y↦(x0​,y) would be closed in Z. That inverse image is H+​, which is neither a finite set nor all of Z, so it is not closed in the cofinite topology. Thus K is not closed.
To establish compactness, note that the subspace topology on any subset A of a topological space with the cofinite topology is again cofinite. An open cover of a nonempty such A has a nonempty member omitting at most finitely many points; finitely many more members cover those points. Hence every subset of a cofinite space is compact. In particular both Z and H+​ with its cofinite subspace topology are compact. The subspace topology on K⊆Z×Z is their product topology, as one checks by tracing basis rectangles. The product of two compact spaces is compact, so K is compact here.
For clarity, the finite-product compactness result needs no Hausdorff condition. Given an open cover of A×B, cover each point by a basis rectangle lying in one covering member. For fixed a∈A, use compactness of B to select finitely many rectangles whose second factors cover B, and intersect their first factors to get an open neighbourhood Ua​ of a. Those finitely many original members cover Ua​×B. Finally finitely many Ua​ cover A, producing a finite subcover of A×B.
In Z×R, the second coordinate projection has
K=π2−1​([0,∞)).
(22)
Since the ray is closed in the usual real line, K is closed. It is not compact: its image under a continuous map under π2​ is [0,∞), which is not compact. For an explicit obstruction, the open intervals (−1,n), n≥1, cover this ray but have no finite subcover.
Finally consider the diagonal W in Z×R. Define
φ:R⟶W,φ(t)=(t,t).
(23)
This is a bijection. The identity function from the usual real line to Z is continuous: each nonempty cofinite open set is the complement of a finite set, and finite sets are closed in the usual real line. Both coordinates of φ are therefore continuous, so φ is continuous for the product topology and the subspace topology of W. Its inverse is the restricted second coordinate projection, which is continuous. Therefore
W≅R,W≅Z.​
(24)
For the second conclusion, Z is compact whereas W≅R is not compact; homeomorphisms preserve compactness. The diagonal in a product with cofinite and Euclidean topologies thus inherits the usual topology, despite one cofinite factor.
