OurBigBook.com Words: 142
The set X/R consists of the equivalence classes [x] for R. The canonical surjection q:X→X/R, q(x)=[x], defines its quotient topology:
O⊆X/R is open⟺q−1(O) is open in X.
(15)
The quotient space includes this topology, not just the collection of equivalence classes.
Regard the circle as S1={z∈C:∣z∣=1} and use the continuous surjection
h:S1⟶[0,1],h(z)=21+Rez​.
(16)
Define zRw precisely when h(z)=h(w). This is an equivalence relation, being equality of function values. Equivalently w=z or w=z, so the construction is the circle modulo reflection.
Since h is constant on each equivalence class, it induces a bijection
h:S1/R⟶[0,1],[z]⟼h(z).
(17)
It is continuous by the universal property of the quotient topology, because h∘q=h is continuous. The circle is compact by the Heine-Borel theorem, and its quotient space is a continuous image of a compact space, hence compact. The closed interval is Hausdorff. Part (a)(ii) therefore proves
S1/R≅[0,1].​
(18)
Geometrically, the equivalence relation folds the upper and lower semicircles together, retaining the two real endpoints separately.
