Use the same 4×4 square grid with four centre-based triangles per small square, but identify boundary points by
(x,y)∼(1−x,1−y)for (x,y)∈∂Q.
(25)
This gives the real projective plane. To see the model explicitly, a radial homeomorphism from the square, centred at (1/2,1/2), to the closed disc carries the boundary involution to the antipodal map. The map from that disc to the projective plane
(u,v)⟼[u:v:1−u2−v2​]
(26)
is continuous and surjective: every one-dimensional vector subspace of R3 has a unit vector representative in the closed upper hemisphere. Its only identifications are antipodal boundary points. The compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem therefore identifies this disc quotient space with RP2.
The square triangulation descends to an abstract simplicial complex K2​. Small-square centres remain distinct interior vertices; each triangle has one centre and two adjacent grid vertices. Boundary pairings identify whole grid edges, while preserving distinct vertices in each triangle, so this is a finite triangulation of the real projective plane. Its counts are 33 vertices, 96 edges and 64 triangles, giving Euler characteristic one.
Choose σ to be the interior triangle with vertices (1/4,1/4), (1/2,1/4) and (3/8,3/8). Removing its relative interior and cutting from (0,0) to (1/4,1/4) gives the same nine-sided cut disc as before. Parametrize it by a convex polygon, transporting the boundary identifications. Its boundary word is now
ababcdefc−1​.
(27)
The boundary involution pairs the two a sides and the two b sides in matching traversal directions; the c pair restores the slit. Leave d,e,f unpaired. The quotient space is
Y=∣K2​∣∖inside(σ),
(28)
the real projective plane with an open disc removed. In particular it is a compact connected surface with boundary consisting of one three-edge circle.
