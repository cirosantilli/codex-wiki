False, with “disjoint union” understood as a topological disjoint union. Take the rational numbers Q with the usual subspace topology from the real line.
Every connected subset of Q has at most one point. Indeed, if it contained r<s, choose an irrational number t∈(r,s). The two relatively open sets
Q∩(−∞,t),Q∩(t,∞)
(19)
partition Q and separate any subset containing both r and s. Hence the connected components of Q are singletons.
If a topological disjoint union of nonempty connected spaces were homeomorphic to Q, the image of each summand would be connected, hence a singleton. Every summand would therefore be a one-point topological space. Their topological disjoint union is a discrete space, since each summand is open. But Q is not a discrete space: for each r∈Q, the distinct rational numbers r+1/n approach r, so r is not an isolated point. This is a contradiction. The set decomposition into connected components always exists; giving those pieces the topological disjoint union topology need not recover the original topology.
