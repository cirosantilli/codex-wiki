Let W=(N×S1)/A, and choose in each circle the point opposite o:
zn​=(0,2/n)∈Cn​,B={zn​:n≥1}⊆X.
(24)
In the subspace topology from the Euclidean plane, the sequence zn​ converges to o, which does not belong to B. Thus B is not closed in X.
On the other hand, let D=fˉ​−1(B)⊆W. With the quotient map q from part (i),
q−1(D)=N×{(0,1)}.
(25)
This is closed in N×S1: its complement is the open set N×(S1∖{(0,1)}). The definition of the quotient topology, applied to complements, says that D is closed in W. A homeomorphism would carry this closed set to the closed set B, a contradiction. Therefore fˉ​ is not a homeomorphism.
The obstruction occurs at the common point. The open set W∖D contains the collapsed point, but its image X∖B contains no neighbourhood of o, since zn​→o. The wedge sum topology allows a separate small neighbourhood on each circle; a neighbourhood of o in the Hawaiian earring must contain all sufficiently small whole circles.
