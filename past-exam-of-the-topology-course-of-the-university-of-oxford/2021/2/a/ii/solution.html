Let qS​:S2→S2/∼S​ and qD​:D2→D2/∼D​ be the quotient maps. The continuous map
h:D2⟶S2,h(x,y)=(x,y,1−x2−y2​)
(14)
is a homeomorphism onto the upper hemisphere; its inverse function is the restriction of the projection map (x,y,z)↦(x,y). Every antipodal pair on S2 meets that hemisphere. Moreover, two points of this hemisphere are antipodal only if both lie on the equator. Thus
qS​(h(u))=qS​(h(v))⟺u∼D​v.
(15)
By the universal property of the quotient topology, qS​∘h induces a continuous bijection
H:D2/∼D​⟶S2/∼S​,H(qD​(u))=qS​(h(u)).
(16)
To justify continuity of the inverse function, the source is compact, being a continuous image of a compact space D2; the Heine-Borel theorem gives compactness of D2. The target is Hausdorff, as follows. For distinct equivalence classes [u]=[v], put
δ=min(∥u−v∥,∥u+v∥)>0.
(17)
In S2, take the union U of the two relative open balls of radius δ/3 about u,−u, and similarly take V about v,−v. Every distance between their respective centres is at least δ, so the triangle inequality gives U∩V=∅. These open sets contain entire antipodal pairs, hence qS−1​(qS​(U))=U and similarly for V. The quotient topology therefore makes qS​(U) and qS​(V) disjoint open neighbourhoods of the two classes.
The compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem now gives the required homeomorphism H.
