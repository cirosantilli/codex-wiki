The two conditions for a basis of a topology both hold. First, every real number x belongs to [x,x+1), so the proposed basic intervals cover the real line. Second, if
x∈[a,b)∩[c,d),
(1)
then max(a,c)≤x<min(b,d), and
[a,b)∩[c,d)=[max(a,c),min(b,d))
(2)
is itself one of the proposed basic intervals. Thus every point in an intersection of two basic sets has a basic neighbourhood contained in that intersection.
Consequently the arbitrary unions of members of B, including the empty union, form a topology T. The basis of a topology conditions give closure under finite intersections; arbitrary unions are built into the definition. This is the lower limit topology, also called the Sorgenfrey line.
