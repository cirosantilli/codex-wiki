Korselt criterion (N squarefree, p−1∣N−1 (p∣N))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Carmichael number
OurBigBook.com Words: 296 Articles: 1
A composite integer N>1 is a Carmichael number if and only if it is a squarefree integer and
p−1∣N−1for every prime p∣N.
(79)
The criterion is named for Alwin Korselt. The two requirements are different: the first excludes repeated prime factors, while the second makes the exponent N−1 a common multiple of the prime-field return exponents.[ref]
For 1105=5⋅13⋅17, the numbers 4,12,16 divide 1104 with quotients 276,92,69. Thus 1105 is another Carmichael number. By contrast 341=11⋅31 is squarefree, but 30∤340; a single failed divisor condition excludes it. This illustrates the appeal of the criterion: a statement about every unit becomes a small calculation using a known prime factorization.
The composite condition must be retained. A prime number is squarefree and satisfies the divisibility condition, but is excluded from the definition of a Carmichael number. The resulting certificate also depends on the supplied factors really being prime numbers; merely finding a product decomposition is insufficient.
