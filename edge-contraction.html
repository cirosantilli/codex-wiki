Edge contraction (G/e)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Graph minor
OurBigBook.com Words: 278
An edge contraction merges the two endpoints of an existing edge. For a finite simple graph G and e=uv, replace u,v by a new vertex w. Retain every edge with neither endpoint in {u,v}, and join w to every member of
NG/e​(w)=(NG​(u)∪NG​(v))∖{u,v}.
(22)
Here NG​(u) and NG​(v) denote the original open graph neighbourhoods, and NG/e​(w) is the new graph neighbourhood. Remove the self-loop arising from uv and merge duplicate edges, so the result G/e is again a simple graph. This is not deletion of uv, which keeps both original vertices.[ref]
The left example contracts uv in the cycle graph C4​ to give {wa,ab,bw}. Its endpoints have no common neighbours, so four edges become three. The right example contracts uv in the triangle in a graph with edges uv,ua,va. Its endpoints have the single common neighbour a, so the two edges ua,va become one edge wa: three edges become one.
For any such edge contraction, the exact counts are
∣V(G/e)∣=∣V(G)∣−1,∣E(G/e)∣=∣E(G)∣−1−∣NG​(u)∩NG​(v)∣.
(23)
The contracted edge accounts for one loss. Each common neighbour z contributes exactly one further loss, since uz,vz become a single edge wz. All other edges correspond one-to-one to surviving edges. In particular the new degree of a vertex is
degG/e​(w)=degG​(u)+degG​(v)−2−∣NG​(u)∩NG​(v)∣.
(24)
These formulas depend on simplifying to a simple graph; a multigraph convention that retains duplicate edges has different counts. In the six-vertex branch set of a graph minor diagram, contracting 12 loses one edge, contracting the resulting 34 loses two because of its common neighbour A, and contracting 56 loses one. Hence its seven edges become the three edges of K3​: 7−1−2−1=3.
An edge contraction can increase a degree of a vertex even while decreasing both total counts. For example, contracting uv in the tree with edges uv,ua,ub,vc,vd merges two vertices of degree of a vertex three into one of degree of a vertex four. This is the mechanism behind the distinction between a graph minor and a topological minor.
