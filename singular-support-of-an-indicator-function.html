Singular support of an indicator function (singsupp1A​=essclΩ​A∩essclΩ​(Ω∖A))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Distribution theory Singular support
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
For any Lebesgue measurable set A⊆Ω, the singular support of its indicator function, viewed as a regular distribution, is the intersection of the two essential closures in the displayed formula. Outside this intersection the indicator function is constant almost everywhere on a ball and hence represents a smooth function. At a point in the intersection, each sufficiently small ball has positive Lebesgue measure in both sets. Any continuous representative on that ball must take only the values zero and one everywhere: every other value would persist in a smaller ball and violate equality almost everywhere. By connectedness of the ball it would then be constant, a contradiction.
If A is open, its essential closure is its relative closure. Its singular support can still be smaller than its topological boundary: A=Rn∖{0} represents the constant one and has empty singular support.
