Percolation theory

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 111
Percolation theory asks how many local connections must be available before a large connected structure appears. Start with a graph, randomly retain some edges or vertices, and study the resulting percolation clusters. In independent bond percolation, every edge is open with probability p, independently of the others. In independent site percolation, the random decisions concern vertices instead. A percolation cluster is a connected component of a graph formed by the available connections; an open path joins two vertices exactly when they belong to the same percolation cluster.
The attraction is that a local rule can produce a global change. One open edge says little about travel across a large graph. Many alternative graph paths can nevertheless make distant connections likely. The model separates the geometry of possible connections from the probability distribution of which connections are usable. This was the central perspective of Broadbent and Hammersley's 1957 paper, which discussed porous solids and disease transmission as interpretations of the same mathematical structure: [ref]. Independent bond percolation supplies a simplified model of those systems; the physical interpretation still requires justification of its independence assumptions.
For a completely calculable example, consider the four edges around a square and ask whether opposite corners A and C are connected. There are two graph paths, each using two edges. Each open path has probability p2. Their events are independent events because the paths share no edges, even though they share endpoints. The inclusion-exclusion principle therefore gives
Pp​(A↔C)=2p2−p4.​
(1)
At p=1/2, the connection probability is 7/16, larger than the 1/4 for either chosen graph path. Counting all sixteen equally likely edge configurations gives the same answer: two configurations with exactly two open edges, four with three, and one with four connect A to C. Connectivity benefits from alternative routes; adding their probabilities without subtracting their overlap would count the all-open configuration twice.
This finite calculation is already useful for studying redundant connections. The deeper question is what happens as the graph becomes arbitrarily large: does connectivity remain confined to finite percolation clusters, or can an infinite percolation cluster appear? The percolation probability and percolation critical probability make that question precise. A finite picture can illustrate the geometry, but cannot establish the existence of an infinite percolation cluster.

Directed percolation

Words: 398 Articles: 5
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
In bond percolation on a directed graph, independently open each directed edge with probability p. The out-cluster of x consists of vertices reachable by open directed paths. In site percolation, independently open vertices and require every vertex of the directed path, including x, to be open. The same definitions allow a multigraph. The orientation matters: an infinite underlying connected graph need not contain an infinite out-cluster from a specified root.

Oriented site percolation (pcsite​)

Words: 55
Directed percolation OurBigBook.com
Independently open vertices of a directed graph with common probability p, and require every vertex of an infinite directed path to be open, including its root. Conditioning the root to be open changes the survival probability by a factor p and does not change the critical probability. This model gives an independent comparison process in the independent oriented-percolation comparison for the contact process.

Oriented bond percolation (pcbond​)

Words: 57
Directed percolation OurBigBook.com
Independently open the bonds of a directed graph with a common probability p, and ask whether an infinite open directed path starts at a specified root. On the oriented square lattice, edges run from (x,n) to (x±1,n+1) with x+n even. The critical probability is the infimum of parameters with positive survival probability. The incoming-edge coupling of site and bond percolation compares this threshold with that of oriented site percolation.

Parallel-bond ray with different site and bond thresholds

Words: 63
Directed percolation OurBigBook.com
Take a directed graph with vertices v0​,v1​,… and n+1 parallel bonds from vn​ to vn+1​. This is a locally finite graph, although the incoming degrees are unbounded. For bond percolation, θb​(p)=∏n≥0​(1−(1−p)n+1)>0 whenever p>0: the failure probabilities are summable, so the infinite product is positive. For site percolation, survival requires every vertex of the ray to be open, which has probability zero for p<1. Thus the survival and susceptibility thresholds for directed percolation satisfy pHb​=0 and pHs​=1.

Incoming-edge coupling of site and bond percolation

Words: 84
Directed percolation OurBigBook.com
Suppose every vertex of a directed graph has at most Δ incoming bonds. In bond percolation with parameter q, open a site whenever at least one incoming bond is open. Incoming bond sets have different heads and hence are disjoint, so these site indicators are independent random variables. Supplement their probabilities to r=1−(1−q)Δ. Every vertex reached by a nontrivial open directed path is now open. Conditioning the root to be open gives θs​(r)≥rθb​(q), since a simple directed path from the root never uses a bond entering the root. Thus pHs​≤1−(1−pHb​)Δ.

Survival and susceptibility thresholds for directed percolation (pH​,pT​)

Words: 78
Directed percolation OurBigBook.com
For the out-cluster Cx​ in directed percolation, define pH​=inf{p:Pp​(∣Cx​∣=∞)>0} and pT​=inf{p:Ep​∣Cx​∣=∞}, with value 1 when the relevant set is empty below 1. The first is a survival threshold; the second is a percolation susceptibility threshold. Both depend on the root in a general directed graph. Exploring a site percolation cluster and using each fresh vertex coin for its first tested incoming bond gives a coupling of probability distributions with bond percolation such that θs​(p)≤pθb​(p) and χs​(p)≤pχb​(p). Consequently pHb​≤pHs​ and pTb​≤pTs​.

Conformal invariance of planar percolation

Words: 43
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
For critical site percolation on the triangular lattice, scaling-limit crossing probabilities transform naturally under conformal maps between planar domains. This rigorously established property supports descriptions of interfaces by SLE and calculations of planar percolation critical exponents. Transferring it to a different lattice or to bond percolation requires a separate universality argument.

Lace expansion

Words: 45
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
An iterative expansion of long-range connectivity functions that separates an approximately random-walk contribution from corrections caused by loops and intersections. In suitable high-dimensional percolation models it controls these corrections and proves mean-field percolation exponents. It also applies to other models, including self-avoiding walks; its use always requires model-specific estimates.

Percolation probability (θ(p))

Words: 1k Articles: 12
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
For independent bond percolation on an infinite graph with a specified root o, let Co​ be its percolation cluster. The percolation probability is
θ(p)=Pp​(∣Co​∣=∞).
(2)
It concerns the root, rather than the existence of an infinite percolation cluster somewhere else. Even above the percolation critical probability of the square lattice, an isolated root has probability (1−p)4, because its four incident edges can all be closed. Thus θ(p)≤1−(1−p)4<1 when p<1. Positive percolation probability does not mean every vertex is connected to infinity.
On the square lattice, the events that o is joined by an open path to the boundary of [−n,n]2 decrease as n increases. Their probabilities converge to θ(p) by continuity from above of a measure. This relates an infinite question to finite observations, without equating any one finite boundary connection with survival forever. The monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation shows that θ is a monotone function of p.

Boundary-side amplification for planar percolation (P(ET​∩EB​)≥1−2(1−P(DN​))1/4)

Words: 72
Percolation probability OurBigBook.com
If an infinite percolation cluster exists almost surely, the probability that a large square box intersects one tends to one. The four rotationally symmetric exterior-side arm events are increasing. By the Harris-FKG inequality, their common probability is at least 1−(1−P(DN​))1/4, where DN​ is their union. Thus a pair of specified opposite sides both has an infinite exterior arm with probability at least 1−2(1−P(DN​))1/4, tending to one. The rays start at a boundary graph vertex and immediately leave the graph vertex box.

Right continuity of percolation probability

Words: 124 Articles: 1
Percolation probability OurBigBook.com
For independent site percolation or bond percolation on a rooted locally finite graph, θ(p) is right continuous on [0,1). Finite-radius connection events have polynomial probabilities in p and decrease to infinite connection. Thus θ is their infimum by continuity from above of a measure. This infimum of continuous functions is upper semicontinuous; parameter monotonicity from the monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation then gives right continuity.

Supercritical continuity of percolation probability

Words: 77
Right continuity of percolation probability OurBigBook.com
Independent site percolation and bond percolation on the cubic lattice have continuous percolation probability on (pc​,1]. Fix p>pc​ and an intermediate q∈(pc​,p). By uniqueness of the infinite percolation cluster, every origin in the infinite p-cluster has a finite p-open graph path to the infinite q-cluster. In the uniform-label monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation, the finitely many labels on this graph path are strictly below fixed p almost surely, so it persists at some parameter below p. The coupled onset parameter for origin percolation has no attained atom there, giving left continuity, including at 1. Combine this with right continuity of percolation probability.

Percolation critical probability (pc​)

Words: 824 Articles: 7
Percolation probability OurBigBook.com
For the rooted infinite model defining percolation probability, the percolation critical probability is
pc​=inf{p:θ(p)>0}.
(3)
Below pc​, the root has probability zero of belonging to an infinite percolation cluster. Above pc​, that probability is positive, by the definition and the monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation. The definition alone does not determine behavior at pc​, or the numerical value of pc​: these require results specific to the graph and the model.
A finite graph with M edges has 2M open/closed configurations. The probability of any connectivity event is a finite sum of terms pk(1−p)M−k, hence a polynomial in p. The square's 2p2−p4 is an example. These finite probabilities vary smoothly, although an infinite-system limit can distinguish a whole interval with θ(p)=0 from one with θ(p)>0. A steep finite connection curve and a mathematically proved percolation critical probability are different kinds of information.

Sharpness of the percolation transition (p<pc​ ⟹ Pp​(0↔∂Λn​)≤Ce−cn)

Words: 72
Percolation critical probability OurBigBook.com
Independent bond percolation on the cubic lattice has exponential connection decay at every p<pc​ and positive percolation probability at every p>pc​. The finite-set criterion for percolation sharpness gives decay below its auxiliary threshold. The Margulis–Russo formula and exploration from a finite box boundary give gn′​(p)≥(1−gn​(p))infS∋0​φp​(S)/(p(1−p)), with the infimum over interior finite sets. Above the auxiliary threshold the infimum is at least one; integrating gives positive percolation probability and identifies the two thresholds. This theorem does not identify the value of pc​ for a particular lattice.

Connective-constant lower bound for percolation (pc​≥μ−1)

Words: 44
Percolation critical probability OurBigBook.com
For independent bond percolation on a transitive locally finite lattice, let cn​ count rooted self-avoiding walks and let μ=limcn1/n​ be the connective constant. An infinite open percolation cluster supplies an open walk of every length. The union bound gives θ(p)≤cn​pn, which tends to zero when pμ<1. Thus pc​≥1/μ.

Harris-Kesten theorem (pc​(Z2)=1/2)

Words: 580 Articles: 4
Percolation critical probability OurBigBook.com
The percolation critical probability of independent bond percolation on the infinite square lattice is exactly
pc​(Z2)=21​.​
(4)
In this model, θ(p)=0 for p≤1/2 and θ(p)>0 for p>1/2. The particular graph, independent edge decisions, and distinction between bond percolation and site percolation matter: this value is not a universal threshold for arbitrary random networks. The equality of open and closed edge probabilities at 1/2 suggests a relation to dual bond percolation, but that symmetry alone is not a proof of long-distance connectivity.
Harris established the lower bound, including absence of an infinite percolation cluster at p=1/2, in 1960. Kesten completed the equality in 1980. His original paper states the threshold result as Theorem 1 and the positive/zero percolation probability alternatives as Theorem 2: [ref]. These are claims about the infinite square lattice, rather than deductions from a simulated finite sample.

Alternating arms argument at the self-dual percolation parameter
Words: 409 Articles: 2
Harris-Kesten theorem OurBigBook.com
At parameter 1/2, primal and dual bond percolation on the square lattice have the same probability distribution. Suppose an infinite percolation cluster exists. Large boxes meet it with probability tending to one. The square-root trick for positively associated events and quarter-turn symmetry imply that each side has an infinite exterior open arm with probability tending to one. The same holds for dual arms. A union bound then gives positive probability of primal top and bottom arms together with dual left and right arms.
Use a primal box [−r,r]2 and a dual box with sides at ±(r+1/2). Open only primal edges whose endpoints both lie in the primal box, preserving all outward and exterior arm edges. The two primal arms are joined inside. Planarity prevents the two alternating dual arms from joining: a proposed dual connection together with the inner boundary arc separates one primal infinite arm from infinity. Thus the modified configuration has two infinite dual clusters. Its positive probability by finite modification of Bernoulli percolation contradicts uniqueness of the infinite percolation cluster. Consequently the percolation probability at 1/2 is zero. This is often called Zhang's argument.
Three-fold symmetric primal-dual noncoexistence
Words: 156
Alternating arms argument at the self-dual percolation parameter OurBigBook.com
For independent bond percolation on a periodic plane lattice with three-fold rotation symmetry, the primal model at p and the dual bond percolation model at 1−p cannot both percolate. To adapt Zhang's argument, divide a large boundary circle into six arcs, repeating the division in each 120-degree sector. Vary the division point until the failure probabilities a,b of the two alternating primal arc types are comparable within a factor (1−p)−2. Moving past a single crossing edge changes a failure probability by at most a factor (1−p)−1, by the FKG inequality. If the probability of no primal exterior arm is at most ε, then a3b3≤ε and both failures are at most (1−p)−1ε1/6. For the dual model, at least one arc type has failure at most ε1/6; its three rotations have the same bound. Four alternating primal/dual arms now occur with positive probability, contradicting the alternating exterior-arm separation lemma and uniqueness after a finite modification. This extends the four-fold argument to the martini lattice.
Alternating exterior-arm separation lemma
Words: 98
Alternating arms argument at the self-dual percolation parameter OurBigBook.com
Four infinite exterior arms entering a square in alternating primal-open and dual-open order force the two primal arms to be disjoint outside the square. If they met, their finite connecting crosscut together with a boundary arc would enclose one dual arm, preventing its escape to infinity. Connecting the two primal arms through the square creates a proper doubly infinite open path, separating the two remaining dual infinite tails. finite-energy property of Bernoulli percolation can then turn this configuration into two infinite dual clusters, contradicting uniqueness. Care is needed to preserve the exterior dual tails even if the finite modification destroys their original boundary attachments.

Monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation

Words: 337 Articles: 3
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
Assign each edge e an independent uniform distribution label Ue​ on [0,1]. At parameter p, declare e open exactly when Ue​≤p. Each open/closed decision then has the required Bernoulli distribution, and different edge decisions are independent. The same construction applies to site percolation with labels on vertices.
Keep those labels fixed when increasing p. For p≤q, every p-open edge is also q-open, so every p-open graph path survives and each percolation cluster can only gain vertices. This coupling of probability distributions compares complete configurations on one probability space, rather than comparing two unrelated pictures. Any increasing event, such as an open path between specified vertices, consequently has a nondecreasing probability.
The three panels use one seeded collection of labels on a finite 17×17 square lattice box. Colored edges and dots show the central root's percolation cluster; gray edges are open but disconnected from it. A percolation cluster can grow by many vertices when one newly opened edge joins it to another large percolation cluster. The panels illustrate a nested construction, not an estimate of θ(p) or a proof of the Harris-Kesten theorem.

Sprinkling coupling for Bernoulli percolation

Connective-constant Peierls bound (pc​≤1−μ−1)

Words: 69
Peierls argument OurBigBook.com
Independent bond percolation on the square lattice satisfies pc​≤1−μ−1, where μ is its connective constant. If (1−p)μ<1, closed dual graph cycles have a summable large-length tail: their counts are bounded by a polynomial factor times the count of self-avoiding walks. Conditioning a sufficiently large finite box to be open excludes short enclosing graph cycles without changing the law of the remaining edges. With positive conditional probability no enclosing closed dual graph cycle remains, so the origin belongs to an infinite percolation cluster.

Finite-energy property (percolation)

Words: 127 Articles: 2
Percolation theory OurBigBook.com
A percolation measure has the finite-energy property if, conditional on all exterior states, every prescribed state of any finite collection of sites or edges has positive probability almost surely. This permits finite local modifications within events of positive probability. It is a hypothesis on the measure and does not follow merely from invariance under graph symmetries.

Finite modification of Bernoulli percolation

Finite-energy property of Bernoulli percolation OurBigBook.com
For 0<p<1, every prescribed state pattern on a finite edge set has positive conditional probability given all exterior states: independence makes it pa(1−p)b for a prescribed open and b prescribed closed edges. Thus a positive-probability exterior event remains possible after any finite local surgery. This is the finite-energy property of Bernoulli percolation, and applies equally to dual bond percolation.

OSSS inequality OurBigBook.com
Choosing an intermediate radius uniformly and exploring the open cluster meeting that sphere gives a decision tree for a one-arm event. Translation and a union bound control each revealment by a constant times the average of the one-arm probabilities over the possible radii.
