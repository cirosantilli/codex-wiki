Laue method

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering X-ray diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 506 Articles: 1
In the Laue method, a fixed single crystal structure receives a collimated beam containing many wavelengths. Each reciprocal lattice vector can select its own wavelength; waves with different wavelengths need not interfere with one another. For G=0, the elastic scattering constraint gives the particularly useful test
λG​=−∣G∣24πsin​⋅G​.
(33)
A candidate reflection requires λG​>0 within the source spectrum and an outgoing ray that reaches the detector. Varying wavelength changes the radius and center of the Ewald sphere while leaving the reciprocal lattice fixed. A monochromatic wave instead fixes the radius: rotating the crystal structure or varying the incident wavelength in a scan finds reflections sequentially. A white-beam photograph integrates many spectral selections in one exposure; it is not an angular scan at one known wavelength.
The Laue method is useful for orienting a single crystal structure: tilting it changes which reciprocal lattice vectors meet the spectral and detector conditions. Absolute scale has an ambiguity if the wavelengths are unknown. Scaling all direct lattice translations by c>0 and all selected wavelengths by c leaves every outgoing direction unchanged. Independently known spectral information or length calibration is needed to remove this ambiguity.
