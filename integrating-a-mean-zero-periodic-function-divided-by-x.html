Integrating a mean-zero periodic function divided by x (∫L∞​h(x)/xdx)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Riemann integration Riemann integral Improper integral
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
Let h be measurable, integrable over a positive period L, and have mean zero there. Its positive and negative parts have equal integrals H. On the k-th period the signed integral of h(x)/x has absolute value at most
LH​(k1​−k+11​).
(90)
This follows by bounding the positive and negative weighted integrals between H/((k+1)L) and H/(kL). The bounds are summable, so the integrals at period endpoints converge. A leftover partial period has absolute integral at most ∥h∥L1(0,L)​/(kL), tending to zero, which proves convergence for arbitrary upper endpoints too.
In contrast, each full-period integral of ∣h(x)∣/x is at least 2H/((k+1)L). It diverges in total unless H=0, equivalently h=0 almost everywhere. Thus a nonzero mean-zero periodic function gives a convergent improper integral here but no Lebesgue integrability.
