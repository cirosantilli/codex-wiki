Transition state theory (k=(kB​T/h)exp[−ΔG‡∘/(RT)])

 Home Chemistry Chemical reaction Chemical kinetics Catalysis Catalyst
OurBigBook.com Words: 153 Articles: 3
Transition state theory relates an elementary reaction's rate constant to a Gibbs energy of activation. The Eyring expression used in the catalyst example assumes every passage counted at the transition state succeeds. More generally, a factor accounting for unsuccessful passage multiplies it. Comparing barriers alone gives the quoted speedup only when the remaining prefactors agree. Free-energy profiles can depend on temperature, so the example's values at 300K do not determine a temperature curve. See the IUPAC Gold Book: transition state theory.
