Basis intensities and phase ambiguity

Keep the cubic translations above, but attach an atomic basis with A at fractional crystal coordinates (0,0,0) and B at (u,0,0). Stipulate real, angle-independent relative scattering amplitudes fA​=1 and fB​=0.6. Neglect wave absorption, repeated crystal scattering and thermal motion. This is a point-scatterer model under the Born approximation, not a claim about the scattering strengths of named elements. Its crystal basis structure factor is
Fhkl​(u)=1+0.6e2πihu,Jhkl​(u)=∣Fhkl​(u)∣2=1.36+1.2cos(2πhu).
(5)
Here J is a dimensionless squared motif amplitude. Comparing it with measured integrated wave intensities requires the experimental geometry and detector response as well. The algebra follows directly by multiplying F by its complex conjugate; the cross term records wave interference between the two sites.
With u=1/2, reflections with even h have J=2.56, whereas odd h gives J=0.16, a ratio of 16:1. With u=1/4, the first four (h00) values instead are 1.36,0.16,1.36,2.56. Their geometrically possible positions are unchanged, although their strengths change. For equal stipulated amplitudes fA​=fB​=1 and u=1/2, the odd-h amplitude vanishes exactly: an example of a systematic absence. Different motif weights can thus expose a repeat that equal weights conceal.
The same formula also proves an ambiguity: u=1/4 and u=3/4 give identical Jhkl​ for every integer h,k,l, because cos(2πh(1−u))=cos(2πhu). Yet F100​ changes from 1+0.6i to 1−0.6i, reversing its wave phase. This particular ambiguity is inversion of the motif; it does not prove that these two simple motifs are distinct chemical substances. The conclusion is conditional on the stipulated real scattering amplitudes.
