plants establish epigenetic states through sequence- and context-dependent recruitment, maintain selected states through replication and regulatory feedback, and erase others when development requires a reset. These processes protect repetitive DNA, stabilize cell identity and coordinate developmental or environmental responses. An epigenetic state is therefore regulated information, not simply a collection of chemical marks that always switch genes off.
plant DNA methylation occurs in CG, CHG and CHH contexts, where H denotes A, C or T. The contexts have overlapping but distinct control mechanisms. At a methylated CG dyad, DNA replication initially produces hemimethylated daughter DNA; MET1 helps restore methylation on the new strand. This provides a direct route for maintaining an existing pattern. By contrast, asymmetric CHH methylation cannot be retained merely by copying an equivalent methylated cytosine across a symmetric dyad: continued targeting by small-RNA or chromatin pathways is required. CHG methylation is strongly coupled to histone recognition and reinforcing chromatin feedback rather than being explained by CG maintenance alone.
For establishment at many repeats, canonical RNA-directed DNA methylation provides sequence specificity. Pol IV produces precursors that, with RDR2, generate double-stranded RNA; DCL3 processes these into 24-nucleotide small interfering RNAs. An Argonaute such as AGO4 uses guide information in association with nascent scaffold transcripts made by Pol V, helping recruit DRM2 to the matching locus. DRM2 can establish methylation in multiple cytosine contexts. Thus transcription can paradoxically be required to establish transcriptional silencing: the scaffold supplies a locus-specific interaction surface rather than representing productive expression of the silenced gene.[ref]
Maintenance is also reinforced by interactions between DNA and histone states. CMT3 recognizes H3K9me2-associated chromatin and contributes to CHG methylation. Histone methyltransferases including SUVH4, SUVH5 and SUVH6 couple methylated DNA to H3K9 methylation, forming a reinforcing relationship. CMT2 contributes strongly to CHH methylation in heterochromatic regions, while RdDM is particularly important in other targeted contexts. Mutant methylomes show both specialization and redundancy, as well as dependencies linking non-CG methylation, small RNAs and H3K9 states.[ref] These are interacting pathways, not three enzymes independently copying three unrelated codes.
Accessibility is an essential part of the mechanism. Dense nucleosomes and linker histones can restrict a methyltransferase even when an appropriate DNA sequence is present. DDM1 remodels H1-containing heterochromatin to allow access, complementing RNA-directed targeting in different chromatin environments.[ref] This explains why maintenance can depend on both an enzyme that writes a modification and machinery that permits the enzyme to reach its substrate.
Histone-based developmental memory is a distinct layer. Polycomb repressive complex 2 deposits H3K27me3 at selected developmental loci. Sequence-specific regulators, transcriptional context and environmental signals determine where recruitment occurs; recycling of parental histones and restoration of modifications help preserve selected states after replication. Yet the presence of a histone mark does not prove that the mark autonomously copies itself or is sufficient for inheritance. Recruitment, feedback, nucleosome turnover and the transcriptional state all need to be considered. H3K27-based silencing of developmental genes should also be distinguished from H3K9-associated transposon heterochromatin.
vernalization illustrates how a state is established for a purpose and maintained for a limited interval. In Arabidopsis thaliana, prolonged cold promotes stable repression of the floral repressor FLC, allowing later flowering in appropriate conditions. Natural accessions differ in the cold exposure needed for stable silencing, correlated with accumulation of H3K27me3.[ref] Maintaining the state after warming allows the plant to retain information about winter rather than respond only to the instantaneous temperature.
Erasure is equally important. Active DNA demethylation can involve DNA glycosylase/lyase activity followed by repair that replaces methylated bases. The identification of ROS1 connected promoter demethylation to protection against inappropriate gene silencing.[ref] Reproductive reprogramming is cell-type specific: DEMETER has a maternal role in imprinting and seed viability, rather than representing indiscriminate erasure in every cell.[ref]
An environmentally acquired state may also need resetting between generations. The vernalized FLC state is normally reset during reproduction and seed development, so the offspring can require its own winter. Perturbing the histone demethylase ELF6 impaired resetting and allowed inheritance of a partially vernalized state.[ref] This contrasts with loci whose methylation states remain stable through reproduction. plants cannot be described either as preserving every acquired mark or as globally erasing all epigenetic information.
Why retain these systems? Silencing mobile and repetitive DNA limits genome damage, chromatin states help cells maintain appropriate expression despite a shared genome, and developmental memory coordinates events separated in time. Selective reprogramming supports gametes and seeds, while boundaries and active demethylation prevent necessary silencing from spreading into genes that must function. Methylation within an expressed gene is not equivalent to repression at a promoter, and a transient transcriptional response is not automatically an inherited state. plant epigenetic control is therefore a balance of targeting, faithful persistence and timely erasure, with different mechanisms serving different biological intervals and genomic contexts.
