A logical formula separates A from B in S if it is provable at the standard numeral tuple of every member of A and refutable at that of every member of B:
n∈A⟹S⊢F(nˉ),n∈B⟹S⊢¬F(nˉ).
(9)
There is no obligation on tuples outside the two sets. In a first-order consistent first-order theory these two obligations make A and B disjoint.
Let h be the Gödel number of H(v1​) and take the literal diagonal first-order sentence R=H(hˉ). If S⊢R, then h belongs to the second source set, so separation gives S⊢¬H(hˉ), a contradiction. If S⊢¬R, then h belongs to the first source set, so separation gives S⊢H(hˉ), again a contradiction. Hence
S consistent⟹S⊬R and S⊬¬R.​
(10)
The direction of separation matters: here H is provable on refutable diagonal codes and refutable on provable diagonal codes.
