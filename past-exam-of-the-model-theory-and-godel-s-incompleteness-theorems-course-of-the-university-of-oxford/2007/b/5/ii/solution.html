A sufficient condition is that T be first-order consistent, complete, and full in its own first-order language. Work in the allowed function-free setting. Since T proves the logically valid first-order sentence ∃x(x=x), fullness guarantees at least one constant and a nonempty collection of names.
Form the carrier from equivalence classes [c] of constant symbols under
c∼d⟺T⊢c=d.
(38)
Logical equality logic makes this an equivalence relation. Interpret a constant by its class and interpret each relation symbol by
R([c1​],…,[cn​])⟺T⊢R(c1​,…,cn​).
(39)
Substitution of equals makes this independent of representatives. This defines a canonical first-order structure because every carrier element is named by a constant.
The truth lemma, proved by induction on logical formulas with constants substituted for their free variables, says
M⊨ϕ([c])⟺T⊢ϕ(c).
(40)
Atomic formulas hold by construction; completeness and first-order consistency give the Boolean steps. For the existential step, a witness in the first-order structure is a named class, so its truth gives a proved instance and hence a proved existential sentence. Conversely a proved existential sentence has a constant witness by fullness, and the induction hypothesis realizes that instance in the first-order structure. The universal case follows by negation. Thus M⊨T, proving
Consistent complete full theories have canonical first-order structures.​
(41)
The witness condition is needed in the original first-order language; adding new constants produces a canonical first-order structure in an expanded first-order language, which is a different assertion.
