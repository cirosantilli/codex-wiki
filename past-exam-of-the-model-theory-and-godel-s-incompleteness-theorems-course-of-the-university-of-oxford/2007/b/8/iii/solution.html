Adjoin to T the negations of all closed-term instances of θ. If the resulting set had a first-order model A, its ground-term substructure B from (ii) would still first-order model T. The assumed existential consequence therefore supplies a tuple b in B satisfying θ. Each bj​ is the value of a closed term tj​.
Quantifier-free agreement between a substructure and its ambient first-order structure gives
A⊨θ(t1​,…,tk​),
(57)
contradicting the adjoined negation of this very ground instance. Thus
T∪{¬θ(t1​,…,tk​):t1​,…,tk​ closed terms}
(58)
is unsatisfiable. Compactness theorem gives a finite unsatisfiable subset. Its finitely many ground negations imply that, for some finite list of first-order term tuples,
T⊨i=1⋁m​θ(ti1​,…,tik​).​
(59)
If the finite subset already shows T itself inconsistent and supplies no ground negations, choose any one closed-term tuple using the available constant; then any first-order sentence is a consequence of T, so m=1 suffices. Otherwise the selected list is nonempty. This also explains why the conclusion contains a finite disjunction rather than necessarily one universal choice of a witness first-order term.
