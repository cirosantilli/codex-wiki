The set of closed-term values is nonempty because the first-order language has a constant. It contains the value of every constant. If f is an r-ary function symbol and t1​,…,tr​ are closed terms, then
fA(t1A​,…,trA​)=(f(t1​,…,tr​))A,
(55)
which is another closed-term value. Hence the set is closed under every operation. Restrict the operations and relations of A to it; this defines a substructure B whose carrier is exactly the specified set.
Part (i) now applies to the universal first-order theory, giving
B⊆A,B⊨T.​
(56)
It is the substructure generated by the first-order language's constants, often called the ground-term substructure. The source's constant-symbol assumption ensures its nonemptiness, even when no other elements can be generated.
