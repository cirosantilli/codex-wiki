Use two expansions of the rational order. In the first interpret cn​=n, so the named sequence is unbounded above. In the second interpret cn​=−1/n, so the named sequence is strictly increasing but has upper bounds in the rational order. Both expansions are countably infinite first-order models of the specified first-order theory.
An isomorphism preserving each cn​ would preserve the existence of an element greater than all the named constants, since any such element in one first-order structure would map to such an element in the other. The first first-order structure has none and the second has, for instance, 0. They cannot be isomorphic. Thus
DLOω​ is not ℵ0​-categorical.​
(44)
The property of lying above all cn​ is an infinite partial type rather than a single first-order sentence. This is why its realization can distinguish the two first-order models without contradicting the completeness established in (iv).
