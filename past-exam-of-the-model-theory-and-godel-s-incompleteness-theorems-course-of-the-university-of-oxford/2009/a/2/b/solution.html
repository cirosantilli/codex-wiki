For Rosser separation by a bounded stage comparison, use the numeralwise enumeration in arithmetic properties meant by enumeration in S: membership supplies some standard stage with a provable positive instance, while nonmembership makes every standard-stage instance refutable. These are stronger than merely attaching a Σ1​ syntactic label to an arbitrary formula. The finite-initial-segment axiom lets us combine finitely many numeralwise refutations into a bounded universal assertion.
If n is provable, choose a standard stage p with S⊢P(n,p​). Consistency means n is not refutable. Therefore S⊢¬R(n,j​) for each j≤p. Using the displayed finite-segment axiom and equality substitution, combine these proofs to get S⊢∀z≤p​¬R(n,z). Existential introduction with stage p proves the separator at n.
If n is refutable, choose a standard stage q with S⊢R(n,q​). Consistency implies that n is not provable, so all standard instances of P(n,⋅) are refutable. In particular their finitely many instances through q give S⊢∀z≤q​¬P(n,z). For an arbitrary proposed witness z, the total-comparison axiom gives z≤q​ or q​≤z. In the first case its P conjunct fails; in the second case the known R instance at q contradicts its bounded no-refutation conjunct. Generalize on z to refute the existential separator. Hence the proposed first-proof-before-refutation formula separates the two sets.
