No. In the standard successor structure, every element other than the named zero has a predecessor:
∀x(x=0 ∨ ∃ys(y)=x).
(22)
The proposed structure fails this first-order sentence at the integer −1. Its nonnegative elements map to positive elements, while every negative element maps to an integer at most −2; hence no element maps to −1. This gives a direct first-order distinction, without treating the negative branch as a two-sided successor chain.
