Every integer is definable without parameters in the ring first-order language. For a≥0, use x=aˉ; for a=−m<0, use x+mˉ=0. For a finite tuple a, the conjunction δa​(x) of these coordinate logical formulas uniquely defines that tuple in Z.
If ϕ(x) belongs to its complete type, the first-order sentence
∀x (δa​(x)→ϕ(x))
(57)
is true in Z, since there is only the specified tuple to check. It therefore belongs to T=Th(Z). The logical formula δa​ is first-order consistent with T, since it is realized in Z, and implies every member of the partial type modulo T. Hence every complete type realized in Z is a principal complete type, isolated by its tuple-defining logical formula.
