Laue X-ray diffraction experiment of 1912

 ... Branches of physics Quantum mechanics Quantum scattering Scattering amplitude Crystal scattering X-ray diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
Max von Laue proposed crystal X-ray diffraction and wrote the theoretical section; Walther Friedrich and Paul Knipping performed the experiments.[ref]
An X-ray tube, lead apertures and shielding supplied a narrow beam through a crystal on a goniometer. Photographic plates recorded the direct beam and separate spots. Copper(II) sulfate trials preceded oriented zinc blende. Optical alignment was checked with X-rays. Removing the crystal removed the extra spots; grinding destroyed their ordered arrangement; translation preserved it, while tilt changed it. Rotation about the beam rotated the pattern.[ref]
Spots and orientation dependence supported wave interference and three-dimensional crystal periodicity. They determined neither all atomic positions nor an absolute wavelength scale. The authors left open whether the selected radiation originated as crystal fluorescence or was already in the incident beam. This differs from the modern Laue method.[ref]
The 1914 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Max von Laue; the discovery's experimental account remains jointly credited to Walther Friedrich and Paul Knipping.[ref] The subsequent inference of particular crystal structures is developed under X-ray crystallography.
