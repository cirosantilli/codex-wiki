Parametrically forced counterpropagating waves (AT​=μA+νB−βA∣A∣2−γA∣B∣2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Bifurcation theory Amplitude equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 112 Articles: 1
Two counterpropagating modes transform as (A,B)↦(eikδA,e−ikδB) under spatial translation. Forcing at twice their natural frequency breaks continuous temporal phase symmetry but preserves simultaneous sign reversal. Hence B transforms like A and A like B, allowing linear parametric coupling. An equal-amplitude phase-locked standing wave has ∣A∣2=∣B∣2=z>0 with ∣β+γ∣2z2−2μRe(β+γ)z+μ2−ν2=0. A nonnegative discriminant is only an algebraic compatibility condition: a physical nonzero wave also requires a positive root.
