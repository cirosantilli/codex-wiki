Minimal polynomial (mA​(t))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 22
For a square matrix over a field, the minimal polynomial mA​ is the unique monic polynomial of least degree satisfying mA​(A)=0. The Cayley-Hamilton theorem guarantees at least one such polynomial: the characteristic polynomial. The minimal polynomial divides every polynomial that annihilates A, so reducing matrix powers modulo mA​ can need fewer coefficients than reducing modulo the characteristic polynomial.
For example, 2I2​ has minimal polynomial t−2, whereas the Jordan block (20​12​) has minimal polynomial (t−2)2. Both have the same characteristic polynomial. Their different matrix powers are illustrated in repeated eigenvalues in the Cayley-Hamilton theorem.
