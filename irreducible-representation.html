Irreducible representation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Representation theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 448 Articles: 9
An irreducible group representation is nonzero and has no nonzero proper invariant subspace. These are the pieces that cannot be decomposed further by taking a smaller symmetry-preserved vector space.
The real two-dimensional rotation through 120 degrees illustrates the role of the coefficient field. A one-dimensional real invariant subspace would supply a real eigenvector, but the characteristic polynomial λ2+λ+1 has no real root. The representation is therefore irreducible over the real numbers. Over the complex numbers, that polynomial has distinct roots of unity e2πi/3 and e−2πi/3, giving two invariant eigenlines. Irreducibility over the real numbers does not imply absolute irreducibility.
