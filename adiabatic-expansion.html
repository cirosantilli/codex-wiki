Adiabatic expansion

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics First law of thermodynamics Adiabatic process
OurBigBook.com Words: 294
Adiabatic expansion increases the accessible volume without heat transfer. In a loaded piston expansion of the fixed-composition ideal gas considered in adiabatic process, positive outward work comes from internal energy, so the final temperature decreases. This provides a cooling mechanism without removing heat during the stroke. In the Carnot cycle, reversible adiabatic expansion connects two different temperatures between its isothermal processes.[ref]
For the same constant-γ reversible ideal-gas adiabat, put b=V2​/V1​>1. The scaling and first law of thermodynamics give
T1​T2​​=b1−γ,p1​p2​​=b−γ,Wout​=CV​(T1​−T2​)=γ−1p1​V1​​(1−b1−γ)>0.
(10)
With the figure's stipulated γ=1.4, p1​V1​=1kJ and b=2, the final ratios are T2​/T1​≃0.75786 and p2​/p1​≃0.37893. Outward work is 0.60535kJ and ΔU=−0.60535kJ. Reversing this particular path from its cooled endpoint would require the same work input; the figure's separate compression starts at the original state, so its input is different. Endpoint entropy supplies another independent check:
ΔS=CV​lnT1​T2​​+nRlnV1​V2​​=0.
(11)
The two logarithm terms cancel using CV​(γ−1)=nR, confirming the reversible path is an isentropic process. [ref]
PASCO's expansion demonstration first compresses trapped air and waits for thermal equilibrium before the rapid expansion. Its manual warns that friction-warmed cylinder regions contact the gas during expansion, compromising quantitative comparison. [ref] The hold separates cooling caused by expansion from simply undoing the earlier hot compression. As proposed controls, observe the temperature dip and subsequent warming, compare rapid and slow strokes from matched initial states, and assess wall heat transfer and probe lag before fitting a reversible model.
Cooling is not a universal consequence of adiabatic expansion: free expansion of an ideal gas has no outward work and returns to the same equilibrium temperature. Its entropy increases, so the reversible power laws do not describe it. Reuse free expansion, Joule's free-expansion experiment and temperature sensitivity of a free-expansion experiment for that apparatus, its energy budget and the real-gas qualifications. The useful distinction is the load: expansion cools this ideal gas when it must spend internal energy doing work.
