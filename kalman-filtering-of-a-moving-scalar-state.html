Kalman filtering of a moving scalar state (Pt−​=Pt−1​+Q,Kt​=Pt−​/(Pt−​+R))

Consider a local-level state-space model xt​=xt−1​+wt​, yt​=xt​+vt​, with mutually independent centered Gaussian noises of variances Q=1/4 and R=1. Start with x0​∼N(10,4), independently of these noises. Before the first observation, the Kalman prediction step gives m1−​=10, P1−​=17/4. If y1​=14, the Kalman gain is 17/21, and the posterior mean and posterior variance become
m1​=10+2117​(14−10)=21278​,P1​=2117​.
(47)
The next Kalman prediction step keeps the mean m2−​=278/21 but adds fresh process noise variance, giving P2−​=89/84. If y2​=12, then
K2​=17389​,m2​=21278​+17389​(12−21278​)=1732180​,P2​=17389​.
(48)
Thus new uncertainty is introduced at each transition and reduced by each measurement. It does not shrink to zero merely because many measurements have arrived: the state continues to change.
The steady-state local-level Kalman gain here is
K∞​=817​−1​≈0.390388.
(49)
With R=1, the limiting posterior variance has the same numerical value. By contrast, the static-state model Q=0 with P0​=4 gives Pt​=1/(t+1/4) and Kt​=1/(t+1/4). That decreasing gain is appropriate for a state that truly remains constant. Applied to the changing random walk, it builds confidence in the wrong model. The choice of process noise is therefore a statement about state evolution, rather than just an instruction to make a plot look smoother.
