Speed (v=∣v∣)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Position Velocity
OurBigBook.com Words: 129
Speed is the nonnegative magnitude of velocity, a scalar rather than a vector. In orthonormal Cartesian coordinates,
v(t)=∣v(t)∣=vx​(t)2+vy​(t)2+vz​(t)2​.
(57)
Its SI unit is m/s. In the reversal example under velocity, the endpoint velocities −2m/s and +2m/s both have speed 2m/s, but the speed falls to zero between them. A speed reading alone therefore cannot tell which way the body is moving.
For a smooth path, the total distance traveled is its arc length, L=∫t1​t2​​v(t)dt. Unlike the integral of a signed velocity component, this integral cannot cancel outward and return motion. The reversal example travels 2m in 2s, giving an average speed of 1m/s, while its average velocity is zero. This makes speed useful for estimating travel duration or fuel use, while velocity also tracks progress toward a destination. [ref]
