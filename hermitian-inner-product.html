Hermitian inner product (H(v,v)>0(v=0))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Sesquilinear form Hermitian form
OurBigBook.com Words: 522 Articles: 2
A Hermitian inner product is a Hermitian form H on a complex vector space which has positive definiteness: H(v,v)>0 for every nonzero vector v. It is precisely a complex inner product. Only with this definiteness condition does
∥v∥=H(v,v)​
(13)
define a norm rather than a seminorm or an expression that may not be real. The standard Hermitian inner product on Cn is H(z,w)=∑j​zj​wj​​; positive weights cj​ give ∑j​cj​zj​wj​​, with squared norm ∑j​cj​∣zj​∣2.[ref]
A Hermitian inner product need not have a real matrix. For instance, with the linear-first coordinate convention,
A=(2−i​i2​),H(z,w)=zTAw,H(z,z)=∣z1​−iz2​∣2+∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2.
(14)
The last expression proves positive definiteness without a lengthy calculation of eigenvalues. For z=(1,i) it gives 6, while for z=(1,−i) it gives 2. These unequal values expose why a silent switch to z∗Az would change this particular Hermitian inner product: with these same entries it gives 2 at (1,i). All entries must keep their roles from the matrix of a Hermitian form.
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for a Hermitian inner product states
∣H(v,u)∣2≤H(v,v)H(u,u).
(15)
Equality holds exactly when u,v are linearly dependent, including when either is zero. For a nonzero u, outline the proof by setting a=H(v,u)/H(u,u) and r=v−au. Then H(r,u)=0, and the Pythagorean theorem gives
H(v,v)=H(u,u)∣H(v,u)∣2​+H(r,r).
(16)
Nonnegativity proves the inequality, while positive definiteness makes equality equivalent to r=0. The scalar a has no extra conjugate because the first argument is linear.[ref]
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality also holds for a positive semidefinite Hermitian form, but equality need not imply linear dependence: for H0​(z,w)=z1​w1​​, the independent vectors e1​ and e1​+e2​ give 1=1. Equality then describes dependence after passing to the quotient vector space by the radical of a Hermitian form. The indefinite Hermitian form in Hermitian form shows that conjugate symmetry alone does not even guarantee the inequality.
For a worked orthogonal projection, take H+​(z,w)=z1​w1​​+2z2​w2​​, u=(1,1) and v=(2,0). Then a=2/3, au=(2/3,2/3) and r=(4/3,−2/3), with H+​(r,u)=0 and 4=4/3+8/3 for the squared norms. The residual is perpendicular in this Hermitian inner product even though its ordinary real dot product with u is 2/3. For complex v=(1,i), the same orthogonal projection uses a=(1+2i)/3; rotating v multiplies a by eiθ, while rotating u multiplies a by e−iθ. This combines geometric decomposition with sensitivity to the relative complex arguments of the multipliers.
A Hermitian inner product therefore supplies more than a positive diagonal: orthogonality, orthogonal projection onto a span, and the triangle inequality for its norm make approximation and distance meaningful. For the function space of complex continuous functions on [0,1], H(f,g)=∫01​f(t)g(t)​dt has positive definiteness, because a nonzero continuous function has positive modulus on a small interval. The same construction turns overlap under integration into lengths and best approximation in finite-dimensional vector subspaces.
