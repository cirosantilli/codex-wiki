Local isostatic response to an added surface layer (w=(ρb​−ρf​)H/(ρm​−ρf​))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Geophysics Isostasy
OurBigBook.com Words: 145
Let a deposited layer have thickness H and density ρb​, replacing a fluid of density ρf​, while the original crust translates downward by w above mantle of density ρm​. Assume all deposited material stays in the same fluid environment, unchanged pre-existing layer thicknesses, an external material source, and complete local isostatic adjustment without bending resistance.
At a fixed compensation depth, added column mass is (ρb​−ρf​)H per unit area, whereas depression replaces a column contrast (ρm​−ρf​)w. Equal hydrostatic pressure therefore requires
(ρb​−ρf​)gH=(ρm​−ρf​)gw.
(2)
It follows that
w=ρm​−ρf​ρb​−ρf​​H,Δz=H−w=ρm​−ρf​ρm​−ρb​​H.
(3)
The pre-existing ground subsides by w, while the top of the new layer rises by Δz if ρb​<ρm​. For a subaerial deposit, air density is negligible. For a fully submerged deposit, use seawater density. A deposit that crosses the fluid surface requires separate treatment of its submerged and emerged portions. Specifying only volume V leaves the answer dependent on loaded area through H=V/A.
