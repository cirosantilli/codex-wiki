An explicit answer, initially written where yz=0, is the meromorphic differential on a Riemann surface
η=y/zd(x/z)​=yzzdx−xdz​.​
(39)
The ratios x/z and y/z are functions on C, so the expression does not depend on the representative of its homogeneous coordinates. It is a scalar multiple of an invariant differential on an elliptic curve.
On the complex torus C/Λ, the differential dw descends from C because translation by any element of the period lattice leaves dw unchanged. It is holomorphic and nowhere zero. The given biholomorphism satisfies, wherever the displayed quotient is initially defined,
Φ∗η=℘′(w)d℘(w)​=dw.
(40)
Consequently, η=(Φ−1)∗(dw) extends across every apparent denominator zero and across [0:1:0] as a nonzero holomorphic differential form on all of C.
One can also check the apparent exceptional points directly. On z=1, put P(X)=4∏i​(X−ei​), so Y2=P(X). At Y=0 the roots are distinct, and differentiating gives
YdX​=P′(X)2dY​,
(41)
which is regular and nonvanishing there. At [0:1:0], use u=x/y, v=z/y. The equation is v=4∏i​(u−ei​v), so u is a local parameter on a smooth algebraic curve and v=4u3+O(u5). Hence
η=vd(u/v)=du−vu​dv=(−2+O(u2))du,
(42)
also regular and nonvanishing.
Only distinctness of the ei​ is needed. The paper additionally writes ei​=0, which is not true for every period lattice: for Λ=Z+iZ, the symmetry ℘(iw)=−℘(w) makes the half-period value at w=(1+i)/2 zero, since iw≡w(modΛ). This unnecessary restriction has no effect on the answer.
