Choose a nonzero meromorphic differential on a Riemann surface η and write K=(η). Multiplication defines a linear map
L(K)⟶HD(C),f⟼fη.
(34)
It takes values in the holomorphic differential forms because (fη)=(f)+K≥0. It is injective since η=0. It is surjective because, for any holomorphic differential form ω, the ratio ω/η is a meromorphic function and has (ω/η)+K=(ω)≥0. Thus this map identifies HD(C) with the canonical Riemann-Roch space.
Furthermore, L(0)=C: a meromorphic function with no poles is holomorphic on the compact Riemann surface C, and hence constant by the maximum modulus principle. Applying the Riemann-Roch theorem with D=0 gives
1−ℓ(K)=1−g.
(35)
Therefore
dimC​HD(C)=ℓ(K)=g.​
(36)
