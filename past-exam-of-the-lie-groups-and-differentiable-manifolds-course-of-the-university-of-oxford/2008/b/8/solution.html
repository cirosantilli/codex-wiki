A Riemannian metric is a smooth positive definite symmetric bilinear form gq​ on each tangent space. The inverse form on the cotangent space defines the kinetic Hamiltonian
H(q,p)=21​gq−1​(p,p)=21​gij(q)pi​pj​.
(34)
It is smooth and satisfies H(q,ap)=a2H(q,p). With the canonical symplectic form Ω=∑i​dqi∧dpi​, define ιXH​​Ω=dH. This gives Hamilton's equations
q˙​i=∂pi​​H=gijpj​,p˙​i​=−∂qi​H.
(35)
Thus pi​=gij​q˙​j. Differentiating the inverse metric yields p˙​i​=21​∂i​gjk​q˙​jq˙​k. Differentiating pi​=gij​q˙​j and substituting gives
q¨​i+Γjki​q˙​jq˙​k=0.
(36)
Therefore the projections of the Hamiltonian integral curves are affinely parametrized geodesics, and lifting a geodesic by its metric covector gives a Hamiltonian integral curve.
For the Killing vector field define the fibrewise linear function f(q,p)=p(Yq​)=pi​Yi(q). Along the lifted geodesic it equals g(q˙​,Y). Metric compatibility and the geodesic equation give
dtd​g(q˙​,Y)=g(q˙​,∇q˙​​Y)=21​(LY​g)(q˙​,q˙​)=0.
(37)
Thus f is conserved. Equivalently the cotangent lift of the isometry flow preserves H, and its generator has Hamiltonian f, so their Poisson bracket vanishes.
For the final two metrics write s=ℓ(x)+m(y), restricting to an open set where the printed tensors are smooth nondegenerate metrics. Their inverse forms give
H1​=2spx2​+py2​​,H2​=2sm(y)px2​−ℓ(x)py2​​=m(y)H1​−21​py2​.
(38)
Along the H1​ flow, H1​ is constant, while
y˙​=py​/s,p˙​y​=2s2m′(y)​(px2​+py2​)=sm′(y)H1​​.
(39)
Hence
dtdH2​​=m′(y)spy​​H1​−py​sm′(y)H1​​=0​.
(40)
This is the Liouville metric geodesic integral, a quadratic conserved quantity. It does not require a Killing vector field; it arises from the separated metric coefficients. When both printed metrics are Riemannian, their positivity in particular requires s>0, m>0 and ℓ<0; the calculation also applies wherever the corresponding inverse tensors are defined.
