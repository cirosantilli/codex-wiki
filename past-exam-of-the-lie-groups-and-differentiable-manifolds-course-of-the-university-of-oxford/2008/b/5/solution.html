One definition of Tx​M is the space of derivations at x: linear maps v:C∞(M)→R satisfying v(fh)=f(x)v(h)+h(x)v(f). In local coordinates u1,…,un, each has the unique form v=∑i​vi∂/∂ui∣x​, identifying Tx​M with Rn. Equivalently it is the space of velocities of curves through x, identifying curves with the same coordinate derivative.
For a chart ϕ:U→Rn, give TM∣U​ coordinates (x,v)↦(ϕ(x),Dϕx​v). If F=ψ∘ϕ−1 is a base transition, the induced transition is
(u,v)⟼(F(u),DFu​v).
(21)
This is a smooth bijection with smooth inverse coming from the reverse chart. The resulting bundle atlas makes TM a smooth manifold of dimension 2n. These local product charts also give the usual Hausdorff and second-countable topology, inherited from a countable base atlas and its fibre coordinates.
For n≥1, identify nonzero vectors in each fibre up to nonzero real scalar multiplication. Locally this gives P(TM)∣U​≅U×RPn−1. In a projective chart where vj=0, use the ratios vi/vj for i=j. Under a base change, the fibre line maps by [v]↦[DFu​v]; its projective coordinates are ratios of smooth linear expressions with nonzero denominator on the overlap. This defines a smooth manifold of dimension n+(n−1)=2n−1.
For the sphere, a point is a pair (x,L) with ∣x∣=1 and an unoriented line L⊂x⊥. Choose a unit v∈L. The triple (x,v,x×v) is a positively oriented orthonormal frame. Given a second pair, the unique orientation-preserving linear map between chosen frames lies in SO3​ and carries the first pair to the second. Thus the action is transitive.
A rotation stabilizing (x,L) fixes x, so rotates its tangent plane. To preserve the unoriented line it must act by v↦v or v↦−v, giving exactly the identity and a half-turn about x. Hence the stabilizer has order two, and P(TS2)≅SO3​/C2​.
This is an example of the projectivized tangent bundle.
