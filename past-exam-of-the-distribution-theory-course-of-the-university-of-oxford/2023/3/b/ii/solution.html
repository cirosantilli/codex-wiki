If F is any other fundamental solution, then H=F−E satisfies ∂zˉ​H=0 as a distribution. Applying 4∂z​ gives ΔH=0. Elliptic regularity for the Laplacian implies that the initially arbitrary distribution H is a smooth function. Its equation is then the ordinary Cauchy-Riemann equation, so H is an entire function.
For a direct justification of the needed regularity, choose a radial mollifier ηr​. Locally, H∗ηε​ is a smooth harmonic function. On a ball with sufficient room for both radii, the mean value property gives (H∗ηε​)∗ηr​=H∗ηε​. Passing to the weak convergence of distributions limit ε→0 yields H∗ηr​=H on a smaller ball. The left side is smooth, proving the claim locally. This establishes the distributional Cauchy-Riemann lemma without assuming in advance that H is a locally integrable function.
Conversely every entire function h satisfies ∂zˉ​h=0 and can be added to E. Hence all fundamental solutions are
F(z)=πz1​+h(z),h any entire function.​
(56)
No growth condition at infinity was imposed, so h need not be a polynomial.
