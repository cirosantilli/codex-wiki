The bound 0≤dn​≤2−n−1 proves uniform convergence of the series by the Weierstrass M-test. Each summand is continuous and satisfies dn​(x+1)=dn​(x), so its sum t, the Takagi function, is continuous and has period one.
For n≥m, both 2num​ and 2nvm​ are integers, giving dn​(um​)=dn​(vm​)=0. Therefore

The corners of dn​ occur on the grid 2−n−1Z. When n<m, that grid is contained in 2−mZ, so the interior of [um​,vm​] contains no corner of dn​. Its slope on this interval is consequently +1 or −1.
The interval [um+1​,vm+1​] is a half of [um​,vm​]. All the old slopes persist on this half, and the new summand contributes one more slope in {±1}. Thus
Sm+1​−Sm​∈{±1}.​
(9)
If t had a finite derivative L at x0​, write t(y)=t(x0​)+L(y−x0​)+r(y) with r(y)=o(∣y−x0​∣). Since um​≤x0​<vm​ and both endpoints tend to x0​,
∣Sm​−L∣≤vm​−um​∣r(vm​)∣+∣r(um​)∣​⟶0.
(10)
Indeed ∣vm​−x0​∣+∣um​−x0​∣=vm​−um​; when um​=x0​, its remainder is zero. This is the secants straddling a differentiability point argument, including dyadic x0​. But the displayed increments prevent Sm​ from converging. Hence t is nowhere differentiable​, using periodicity to cover all real x0​.
For the distributional derivative, set rn​(x)=s(2nx), using s from part (iii). The change of variable in the distributional derivative pairing gives dn′​=rn​; these are the Rademacher functions. Uniform convergence implies weak convergence of distributions, and the continuity of distributional differentiation allows termwise differentiation in that topology. Explicitly,
t′=n=0∑∞​rn​in D′(R),⟨t′,ϕ⟩=−∫R​t(x)ϕ′(x)dx.​

This series assertion is a distributional convergence statement. Its remainder satisfies the concrete estimate
​⟨t′−∑n=0N​rn​,ϕ⟩​≤2−N−1∥ϕ′∥L1​⟶0.
