Electromagnetism

 Home Physics Branches of physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 115k Articles: 1k
Electromagnetism describes how electric charge and electric current interact through the electric field E and magnetic field B. The Maxwell equations describe the fields and their sources; the Lorentz force describes the response of a particle of electric charge q and velocity v:
F=q(E+v×B),dtdEparticle​​=qE⋅v.
(1)
The electric field can change the particle's energy. The magnetic part of the Lorentz force is perpendicular to velocity, so magnetic forces do no work; they can bend a trajectory. These are local interactions: a particle responds to the electromagnetic field at its position. [ref].
The remarkable unification is that the same Maxwell equations connect Coulomb's law, Faraday's law of induction, and freely propagating electromagnetic waves. A changing electric field contributes to magnetic circulation through displacement current; together with Faraday's law, this permits waves in vacuum without conducting matter. The Poynting theorem then explains how the fields store and transport energy. This connects the force on a charge to the energy carried by light.
In his 1865 paper, James Clerk Maxwell derived transverse disturbances and compared the propagation speed inferred from electrical measurements with independently measured light speeds. Part VI, especially §§95–97, argues that their agreement supports identifying light as an electromagnetic wave. This was a comparison with finite experimental accuracy, rather than a numerical identity between the historical measurements. [ref]; [ref].
