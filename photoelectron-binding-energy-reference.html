Photoelectron binding energy reference (EB​=hν−Ekin​−ϕanalyzer​)

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Photoemission spectroscopy X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 409
Let a conducting sample and conducting analyzer be in good electrical contact, unbiased, at equilibrium, and without appreciable charging during illumination. Their Fermi levels then coincide. Choose that common Fermi level as zero. Define EB​>0 as the binding energy relative to this zero: removing an electron into the electron reservoir at the Fermi level costs EB​. It is not the cost of removing the electron all the way to the sample's vacuum level.
Write the sample and analyzer work functions as ϕs​ and ϕa​. Their local vacuum levels lie at ϕs​ and ϕa​ above the common Fermi level. Conservation of energy for an electron emitted without additional losses gives total outgoing energy hν−EB​, where h is the Planck constant and ν the photon frequency. Immediately outside the sample its kinetic energy is Ks​=hν−EB​−ϕs​. The contact-related electric potential changes this to
Ekin​=Ks​+ϕs​−ϕa​=hν−EB​−ϕa​,EB​=hν−Ekin​−ϕa​.
(24)
Thus the sample work function cancels when the measured kinetic energy is referenced to the analyzer. Substituting ϕs​ for ϕa​ in the final conversion would mix two different energy references. An instrument's calibrated binding energy scale can incorporate its effective analyzer work function, so the correction need not appear as a separate operator-entered number.
As an original numerical example, stipulate hν=1486.6eV, ϕa​=4.5eV and a line at EB​=284.0eV. The predicted analyzer kinetic energy is 1198.1eV. If ϕs​=5.0eV, the surface value is instead Ks​=1197.6eV; the contact-related increase is 0.5eV. A second line at 286.0eV gives 1196.1eV at the analyzer. A +2.0eV chemical shift in photoelectron spectroscopy therefore appears as a −2.0eV shift in measured kinetic energy. These are chosen values, not a measured carbon spectrum.
Charging breaks the assumed reference. If a uniformly charging sample rises by electric potential V>0 relative to the analyzer, an escaping electron loses an additional energy eV, with e the positive elementary charge. Applying the unbiased conversion consequently returns
EB,apparent​=EB,true​+eV.
(25)
Two features from the same stable potential retain their separation. Features from regions at different potentials acquire different offsets, which can mimic chemical differences or broaden a line. A conducting holder alone does not establish electrical contact through an insulating surface layer. Compare repeat scans, electrical contact and charge-compensation settings before interpreting an offset as a change in chemical bonds.
Measurement calibration is a separate issue: a known reference line tests the scale, while charge compensation controls the specimen's electric potential. In an original offset model EB,read​=EB,true​+b, one known line determines b. An unknown scale factor in EB,read​=aEB,true​+b requires two distinct known lines to determine both a and b. Re-reading a reference after a sample checks drift. Treating an unidentified contamination peak as a universally fixed reference would assume the chemical identity and electrical behavior that the measurement should test.
