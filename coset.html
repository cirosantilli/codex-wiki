Coset (gH, Hg)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
For a subgroup H≤G of a group G, a left coset is gH={gh:h∈H}; a right coset is Hg={hg:h∈H}. The name refers to the side on which the representative g multiplies H. Think of a coset as a translated copy of a subgroup: it remembers the relative differences allowed by H, while moving the whole collection to another location in the group.
Two left cosets of the same subgroup are either equal or disjoint. Thus distinct left cosets form a partition of a set covering G; the same statement holds separately for right cosets. The useful membership test is
aH=bH⟺b−1a∈H.
(29)
This also defines an equivalence relation: changing a representative by right multiplication with a member of H leaves its left coset unchanged. These facts hold even for infinite groups. See MIT's coset notes, definitions 3.6 and 3.9.
For an explicit additive example, take the cyclic group G=Z/6Z, with modular arithmetic, and the subgroup H={0,3}. Adding each representative to both members gives exactly
0+H={0,3},1+H={1,4},2+H={2,5}.
(30)
Representatives 3,4,5 repeat these three cosets, respectively. For example, 4+H={4,1}=1+H, since 4−1=3∈H. Every residue appears once in the displayed partition of a set. Each coset has two members, so the group order of H is 2 and its index of a subgroup is 3. Left and right cosets coincide here because G is an abelian group.
A coset need not be a subgroup. In this example, {1,4} lacks the identity element 0, and 1+1=2 leaves it. In fact, the only left or right coset of H that is a subgroup is H itself: a subgroup must contain the identity element, and the coset containing that element is H. Equal sizes come instead from the bijection h↦gh, whose inverse is x↦g−1x; for right cosets, use h↦hg and x↦xg−1.
The nonabelian group S3​, the symmetric group on three letters, separates the two sides. Use function composition with the rightmost permutation applied first, and let H={e,(12)}. Then (13)(12)=(123) but (12)(13)=(132), giving the differing pair
(13)H={(13),(123)},H(13)={(13),(132)}.
(31)
The complete partition of a set into left cosets consists of H, {(13),(123)} and {(23),(132)}; the complete partition of a set into right cosets consists of H, {(13),(132)} and {(23),(123)}. Each lists all six permutations exactly once. Both have three parts of size two, although their parts differ.
Left cosets support a group action by a⋅(gH)=(ag)H for every subgroup, as in the coset action. Multiplication of representatives defines a quotient group operation (aH)(bH)=(ab)H precisely when H is a normal subgroup. Otherwise the answer can depend on the representatives: in this S3​ example, e and (12) represent the same coset H, yet e(13)H=(13)H and (12)(13)H=(23)H differ. In the additive example, H is a normal subgroup, making (1+H)+(2+H)=3+H=H valid. See MIT's quotient-group notes, theorem 9.1.
