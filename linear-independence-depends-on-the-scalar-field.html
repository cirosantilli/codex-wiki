Linear independence depends on the scalar field (dimR​C=2,dimC​C=1)

 ... Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Linear independence Linear dependence
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
Consider the vectors 1,i in the complex numbers, where i is the imaginary unit. With scalar field R, a linear relation a⋅1+b⋅i=0 has a,b∈R. Taking its real part and imaginary part gives a=b=0, so the pair is linearly independent. Every complex number x+iy also has coefficients (x,y) in the real numbers, so 1,i form a basis and the dimension of a vector space is two.
With scalar field C, the same pair is linearly dependent, since
i⋅1+(−1)⋅i=0
(11)
uses the nonzero coefficients (i,−1) from the complex numbers. The single vector 1 is now a basis, because z=z⋅1 for every complex number z, giving dimension of a vector space one. For example 3+i=3⋅1+1⋅i=(3+i)⋅1+0⋅i: the second representation uses a coefficient unavailable over the real numbers. Specifying the field is part of the definition of linear independence.
