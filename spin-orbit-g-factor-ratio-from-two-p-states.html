Spin-orbit g-factor ratio from two P states (gs​/gL​=(4R−2)/(R+1))

An original reduced model makes the cancellation explicit. Assume LS coupling, neglect nuclear spin, and write the electronic magnetic dipole moment as −μB​(gL​L+gs​S)/ℏ. For L=1, S=1/2, the projection used to derive the Landé g-factor gives
g3/2​=32gL​+gs​​,g1/2​=34gL​−gs​​.
(94)
These coefficients follow by substituting J=3/2 or 1/2 into the angular-momentum projection formula; neither level measures pure spin alone. In a weak uniform magnetic field, adjacent electronic projections have spacing hfJ​=gJ​μB​B when gJ​>0. Therefore
R=f1/2​f3/2​​=g1/2​g3/2​​=4−x2+x​,x=gL​gs​​,x=R+14R−2​.​
(95)
The common magnetic field and Bohr magneton cancel. The ratio determines gs​/gL​, not gs​ without further knowledge of gL​. For gL​=1, it gives ae​=(R−2)/(R+1). Small departures from R=2 are magnified differently from small departures of gs​ from 2; the two fractional changes should not be identified.
With the stipulated inputs gL​=1, gs​=2.0024 and μB​B/h=100MHz, the two electronic frequencies are 66.5867 and 133.4133MHz. Their ratio is approximately 2.0036043, and inversion recovers ae​=0.0012. These are ideal electronic lines, not historical gallium data. Nuclear spin, mixing between levels and other deviations from LS coupling require a more complete model in an actual atom.
