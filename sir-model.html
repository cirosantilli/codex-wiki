SIR model (s+i+r=1)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Mathematical biology Compartmental models (epidemiology)
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 17
The SIR model is a mathematical model of an epidemic that follows three groups: a susceptible compartment whose members can acquire infection, an infectious compartment whose members can pass it on, and a removed compartment whose members neither acquire nor pass on infection. The letters stand for susceptible, infectious and recovered or removed. Here we study the simplest closed, constant-parameter version, with homogeneous mixing and no births, deaths, migration or waning immunity. A person who leaves the infectious compartment never returns to the susceptible compartment in this version.[ref]
The appeal of the SIR model is that one can explain a rise and fall in the infectious compartment without changing anyone's behavior: fewer opportunities for new infection eventually reverse the growth. The SIR first integral even determines the SIR infection peak without computing its time. These are useful deductions from explicit assumptions. The stipulated numbers below illustrate the mathematical model; they are neither estimates for an actual disease nor a forecast or medical advice.
Kermack and McKendrick's 1927 paper, "A contribution to the mathematical theory of epidemics", in Proceedings of the Royal Society A, is an early foundation for the SIR model. Its DOI is 10.1098/rspa.1927.0118, also recorded in the Royal Society's archive of contemporary referee reports.[ref][ref]
