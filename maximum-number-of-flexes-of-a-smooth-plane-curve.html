Maximum number of flexes of a smooth plane curve (3d(d−2))

 ... Algebraic geometry Degree of an algebraic variety Degree of a projective curve Bézout's theorem Intersection multiplicity Inflection point of an algebraic plane curve
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
A nonsingular complex projective plane curve of degree d≥2, with no common irreducible component with its Hessian curve, has at most 3d(d−2) distinct flexes. The Hessian criterion for a flex identifies the flexes with the intersection, and Bézout's theorem counts its total intersection multiplicity. Higher contact can make this bound on distinct points strict.
For a general nonsingular curve of degree d≥3, all flexes have tangent contact exactly three, making the bound an equality. For a fixed point and line through it, contact at least four imposes four independent linear conditions on the coefficients; the space of such point-line pairs has dimension three. Thus degree-d equations with contact at least four form a proper closed exceptional locus. In local graph coordinates y=h(x), the Hessian determinant along the curve is a nonvanishing factor times h′′(x), so contact exactly three makes the intersection simple. A nonsingular plane conic has no flexes; a line is excluded because its tangent is a component and its Hessian determinant is identically zero.
