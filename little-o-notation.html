Little o notation (f=o(g))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Big O notation
OurBigBook.com Words: 107
When g is nonzero sufficiently near the specified limit, little o notation means
f=o(g)⟺f/g⟶0.​
(53)
Thus the numerator becomes negligible relative to the denominator's scale. As n→∞, n=o(n2) because n/n2=1/n→0; as h→0, h2=o(h) because h2/h=h→0. The direction of the limit changes the comparison. [ref]
Equivalently, for every ε>0, the bound ∣f∣≤ε∣g∣ holds sufficiently near that limit, with a threshold allowed to depend on ε. Big O notation needs only one fixed multiplier, making it weaker. For example, 2n=O(n) but 2n is not o(n). The useful relation f∼g⟺f−g=o(g) turns asymptotic equivalence into a statement about a negligible remainder.
