Electrical insulator

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Permittivity Dielectric
OurBigBook.com Words: 220 Articles: 1
An electrical insulator has sufficiently small transport of electric charge for its operating conditions. Insulation is a practical requirement involving electric potential difference, temperature, geometry and duration, rather than a universal claim of zero electrical conductivity. Electric polarization can be substantial in an electrical insulator; dielectric breakdown can end the insulating regime.
For an idealized uniform slab with small constant electrical conductivity σ, the Ohmic conductor model predicts leakage electrical resistance R=d/(σA) in parallel with capacitance C=ϵA/d. An isolated capacitor then has
dtdQ​=−RCQ​,Q(t)=Q(0)e−t/(RC),RC=σϵ​.
(11)
This is a derived model of slow discharge, not an assertion that every leakage mechanism obeys Ohm's law. Its time scale matches charge relaxation. The fixed-charge comparison in dielectric capacitor response needs an observation time short compared with RC, but long enough for electric polarization to settle. During a maintained voltage, leakage consumes power V2/R as Joule heating; delayed electric polarization can add dielectric loss under changing electric fields.[ref]
Large relative permittivity alone does not rule out either mechanism.[ref]
