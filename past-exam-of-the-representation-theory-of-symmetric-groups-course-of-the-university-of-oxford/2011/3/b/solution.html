Write λ=(a,b) with a≥b>0. If the removed corner is in the first row, it must be its last cell, with a>b. Remove that entry and output it as v; all remaining row and column inequalities persist, and the shape is (a−1,b).
If the corner is the last cell of the second row, remove its value c. In the first row find the rightmost entry d<c, replace it by c, and output v=d. Such an entry exists: the first-row cell above the removed corner has smaller value than c. The chosen position is therefore at least column b.
The new first-row entry exceeds all entries to its left, while every entry to its right is larger than c by the rightmost-choice rule. After deleting the lower corner, no second-row cell remains below the changed position, since that row now has length b−1. Thus all column inequalities remain valid too. The resulting shape is (a,b−1), omitting a zero part if b=1.

The entries remain distinct and increasing along rows and columns, but the missing label is the ejected v, not necessarily the original corner value. The resulting tableau is therefore standard on its remaining ordered entry set, the generalized-standard convention in the question.
