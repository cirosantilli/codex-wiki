Track the first row after any prefix of the input permutation. Its jth entry is the smallest possible final value of an increasing subsequence of length j in that prefix. This minimum-tail invariant follows inductively from the row-insertion update.
Indeed, when a new value z replaces the first first-row entry larger than it, at position r, the previous entry in position r−1 is smaller than z when r>1. An increasing subsequence ending there can be extended by z, so z supplies a new attainable tail of length r. It improves the old length-r minimum. The minima for shorter lengths were already smaller than z and remain unchanged; a longer subsequence ending at z is impossible because its previous tail would have to be smaller than z, contradicting the choice of r. If no first-row entry exceeds z, it appends and creates a new longest subsequence. This proves the invariant at every step.
In particular, the number of first-row cells is the maximum increasing-subsequence length. At the final tableau it is λ1​, so
No increasing subsequence of σ has length λ1​+1.​
(20)
This proves the required nonextension assertion, even without requiring the first-row tuple itself to occur as a subsequence.
That occurrence is not automatic for a general permutation. For example the word 2,4,3,1 gives P with rows (1,3),(2),(4). Its first-row tuple (1,3) is not a subsequence of the word, because one occurs after three. The first row collects minimum tails for different subsequence lengths; those tails need not come from one common subsequence. If the source's premise is read as an additional hypothesis that the tuple does occur, the preceding maximum-length proof establishes its claimed maximality directly.
The invariant is the first row of RSK as a minimum-tail table.
