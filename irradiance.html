Irradiance (E=dΦincident​/dA)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry
OurBigBook.com Words: 125 Articles: 1
Irradiance measures incident radiant flux per actual receiving area. Its units Wm−2 match radiant exitance, but it counts the incoming side of the radiation exchange. Measure incoming angles from the unit normal pointing toward the incident hemisphere. Then
E=∫Ωincident​​Lincoming​(θ,φ)cosθdΩ.
(29)
For 2W distributed uniformly over 0.010m2, E=200Wm−2. If the receiving plane is tilted 60∘ in the same broad uniform parallel beam, the incident power per actual area is halved to 100Wm−2. This receiving-surface cosine factor is geometric and does not assume Lambertian reflectance. Together, irradiance and radiant exitance describe how radiation supplies and removes energy at a surface, while radiance preserves the directional information.
