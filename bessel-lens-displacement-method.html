Bessel lens displacement method (f=(L2−d2)/(4L))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Geometrical optics Lens (optics) Thin lens
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
The Bessel lens displacement method measures a converging thin lens's focal length by moving only the optical lens between a fixed object and screen. If their separation is L>4f, two settings give sharp real images: one enlarged, the other reduced. Read their separation d on an optical bench, then calculate
d=L2−4Lf​,f=4LL2−d2​.
(20)
The two settings exchange object and image distances, so their signed transverse magnifications are reciprocals. This makes the experiment a visible test of conjugate optical planes as well as a focal length measurement.[ref]
For an original stipulated example, put the object at x=0, the screen at x=L=45cm, and take an ideal thin lens with f=10cm. Its two settings are x−​=15cm and x+​=30cm, with d=15cm. The transverse magnifications are m−​=−2 and m+​=−1/2: both real images are inverted. An object 0.50cm tall would give signed image heights −1.00cm and −0.25cm. Conversely, stipulated readings L=45cm and d=15cm give f=(452−152)/(4×45)=10cm. These are model calculations, not measured data or a manufacturer's calibration.
A fixed offset between a carrier's reference mark and an ideal thin lens plane cancels when subtracting the two carrier readings. That useful cancellation does not make a thick lens equivalent to one unknown optical centre: its two principal planes can be separated. The Bessel lens displacement experiment below makes this limitation explicit.
