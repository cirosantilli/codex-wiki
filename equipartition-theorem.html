Equipartition theorem

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Canonical ensemble
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
The equipartition theorem says that each independent quadratic term in a Hamiltonian described by classical mechanics has mean energy kB​T/2 in a canonical ensemble. Here kB​ is the Boltzmann constant and T>0 the absolute temperature. Count terms in the energy, rather than vaguely counting everything that can move: one harmonic oscillator has one position degree of freedom but TWO quadratic terms, one in momentum and one in displacement.
A precise sufficient setting is H(y,ξ)=ay2/2+H0​(ξ) with a>0, where y is one of the canonical variables, ξ denotes all remaining variables, and the phase space integration measure factors as dydξ. The range of y is the whole real line, independently of ξ; there is no constraint coupling its allowed values to other variables. The partition function must be finite, and the relevant expected values must exist. The Boltzmann factor then gives
β=kB​T1​,ρy​(y)=2πβa​​e−βay2/2,⟨2ay2​⟩=2kB​T​.
(25)
The probability density function ρy​ is a normal distribution with variance kB​T/a. A larger coefficient narrows the fluctuations enough to cancel its greater energy cost. This is why the mean contribution is independent of mass or spring constant.[ref]
For a proof outline, the one-variable Gaussian integral is Zy​=∫−∞∞​e−βay2/2dy=2π/(βa)​, so −∂β​lnZy​=1/(2β). More generally, integration by parts in a canonical variable gives ⟨y∂y​H⟩=kB​T when its boundary term [ye−βH] vanishes and the integrals converge. For ay2/2 this is twice its mean energy. At a finite hard boundary the boundary term need not vanish; hard-wall correction to harmonic equipartition illustrates the resulting departure. A constraint, a nonquadratic potential energy, or a noncanonical integration measure therefore requires its own calculation. The theorem is an ensemble average, not equality of instantaneous energies, and applying it to a recorded trajectory requires equilibration and sufficient sampling.
For a classical one-dimensional harmonic oscillator, with positive mass m and spring constant k,
H=2mp2​+2kx2​,2m⟨p2⟩​=2k⟨x2⟩​=2kB​T​,⟨H⟩=kB​T.
(26)
The origin is the equilibrium position. Its heat capacity is kB​ at fixed m,k, not kB​/2. Although the two mean energies agree, their measured amplitudes do not: ⟨p2⟩=mkB​T and ⟨x2⟩=kB​T/k. In a harmonic optical trap, the position measurement observes the potential energy contribution; it does not measure both contributions by observing x alone.[ref]
For N particles in a three-dimensional classical monatomic gas, neglect interactions and thermally accessible internal excitations. Its three translational momentum terms give internal energy U=3NkB​T/2, heat capacity at constant volume CV​=3NkB​/2, and heat capacity at constant pressure Cp​=CV​+NkB​=5NkB​/2. Per mole, replace NkB​ by the molar gas constant R. For example, using stipulated R=8.314Jmol−1K−1, the molar heat capacity at fixed volume is 12.471Jmol−1K−1. These results assume a dilute, nonrelativistic ideal gas with classical translational statistics, rather than a quantum degeneracy regime.
For an ideal gas of diatomic molecules, modeled as two chemical atoms with a bond, there are three translational quadratic terms, two accessible rotational kinetic energy terms for changes of bond orientation, and one bond-stretching harmonic oscillator with two quadratic terms. Rotation about the bond axis is not an additional active mode in this linear-molecule model. The three familiar limits of heat capacity at constant volume are
NkB​CV​​≃⎩⎨⎧​3/2,5/2,7/2,​rotations and vibrations frozen,rotations active, vibrations frozen,rotations and vibrations classical.​
(27)
Corresponding molar heat capacities are 3R/2, 5R/2 and 7R/2. Counting the stretching harmonic oscillator only once as kB​T/2 would give the wrong last value.[ref]
The frozen regimes require quantum mechanics. In the simple molecular rotation model, Ej​=kB​θr​j(j+1) with state degeneracy 2j+1, θr​=ℏ2/(2IkB​), and moment of inertia I. For the bond-stretching quantum harmonic oscillator, θv​=ℏω/kB​ with angular frequency ω and ℏ=h/(2π) from the Planck constant. When θr​≪θv​, the three limits above correspond to T≪θr​, θr​≪T≪θv​, and T≫θv​, provided translational motion remains classical. They are smooth crossovers, not abrupt switches. Real molecules can have different allowed rotational populations; at high temperature, thermal dissociation, internal excitations and nonharmonic bond motion can invalidate the fixed-molecule model before its last limit is reached.[ref]
For a quantum harmonic oscillator with fixed ω, put z=θv​/T. Its exact mean energy and heat capacity are
⟨H⟩=2ℏω​+ez−1ℏω​,kB​Cosc​​=(1−e−z)2z2e−z​.
(28)
At T≫θv​, the mean energy approaches kB​T and Cosc​ approaches kB​. At T≪θv​, thermal excitation is exponentially suppressed and Cosc​ tends to zero, although the zero-point energy ℏω/2 survives. Frozen thus means little thermal response, not zero motion or zero energy.[ref]
A motivating measurement is thermal calibration of an optical trap. A focused laser through a microscope objective confines a bead in water; imaging or a quadrant photodetector records a position time series. Gieseler and colleagues describe a polystyrene bead of radius 1.03μm at 293.15K, a 1064nm trapping laser, and photodetector acquisition at 105Hz. Their recorded bead continues undergoing Brownian motion; higher trapping power produces a narrower equilibrium position distribution. Infer the centered variance, not a squared distance from an arbitrary camera origin:
σx2​=⟨(x−⟨x⟩)2⟩,k=σx2​kB​T​.
(29)
The normal distribution position histogram and agreement with dynamical calibration methods support the local harmonic optical trap interpretation. For a stipulated example, T=300K and σx​=20nm imply k≃1.04×10−5N/m=0.0104pN/nm. This calculated scale is not an experimental reading.[ref]
Position calibration is essential: if detector output v has conversion x=s(v−v0​), its physical variance is s2Var(v). An unknown scale s cannot be determined together with k from that one variance. A complementary calibration demonstrated by Tolić-Nørrelykke and colleagues combines the power spectrum of thermal motion with the bead response to known sinusoidal motion of a translation stage. The driven response supplies an additional length scale, and their method does not require bead size, liquid dynamic viscosity or wall distance as inputs. It still uses a thermal dynamical model; agreement between methods is a consistency check, not a theory-free proof of equipartition theorem.[ref]
The readout itself can hide fluctuations. Wong and Halvorsen used a 1064nm optical trap and adjustable-exposure CCD imaging of a bead in water. They varied exposure and trapping power, held the bead away from the nearest wall, adjusted illumination to maintain comparable images, and measured tracking noise using a stuck bead. The measured variance fell as exposure increased; corrected results agreed with their time-averaging model. For a stationary overdamped harmonic optical trap, with optical trap relaxation time τ, an exposure of length W measures xˉW​=W−1∫0W​x(t)dt. Its ideal noise-free variance is
Var(xˉW​)=kkB​T​S(α),α=τW​,S(α)=α22(α−1+e−α)​.
(30)
For α>0, S(α)<1: using the blurred variance in the uncorrected formula overestimates k by 1/S(α). At W=τ, the calculated factor is e/2≃1.36. Additive tracking noise instead raises the measured variance and biases the inferred k downward if left uncorrected.[ref]
Useful controls are to record over many optical trap relaxation times, compare blocks for drift, calibrate the position scale, measure fixed-bead noise, vary exposure, and check the position histogram for departures from a normal distribution. A sparse sequence of accurate instantaneous positions can estimate an equilibrium variance; a long exposure averages away motion even if many frames are collected. Flow, appreciable laser heating, escape from the harmonic region or nonconservative forces can break the assumed thermal equilibrium. Inferring k by imposing equipartition theorem is a calibration, whereas testing the relation requires an independently established stiffness and position scale. The beautiful practical connection is that microscopic randomness can set a force scale: once calibrated, a small constant applied force displaces the mean by f/k. The same quadratic-term counting predicts macroscopic heat capacity and makes microscopic fluctuations useful measurements.
