Gravitational acceleration (g)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Newton's law of universal gravitation Newtonian gravitational field
OurBigBook.com Words: 187 Articles: 2
Gravitational acceleration is the acceleration of a test particle in free fall in a Newtonian gravitational field. It is a vector pointing along that field; g commonly denotes its positive magnitude. Near Earth's surface, the magnitude is approximately constant over shallow vertical scales. For a fixed spherical source it instead varies as g(r)=μ/r2, where μ is the standard gravitational parameter.
This is distinct from gravitational weight, the force mg, and from apparent weight, the support force a scale measures. An orbiting body can have substantial gravitational acceleration while its apparent weight is zero.
