Total derivative (dtdF​=∂t∂F​+∑i​∂xi​∂F​dtdxi​​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Derivative

A total derivative accounts for every dependence of a quantity on the variable of differentiation. For a differentiable function F(t,x1​,…,xn​) evaluated along differentiable coordinate functions xi​(t), the multivariable chain rule gives
dtd​F(t,x1​(t),…,xn​(t))=∂t∂F​+∑i=1n​∂xi​∂F​dtdxi​​.
(96)
All the partial derivatives on the right are evaluated at (t,x1​(t),…,xn​(t)). The first term captures explicit dependence on t; the remaining terms capture changes transmitted through the coordinates.
For example, take F(t,x)=tx2 and x(t)=t. The partial derivative with respect to t, holding x fixed, is x2, whose value on the path is t2. The total derivative is instead
dtd​F(t,t)=dtd​t3=3t2=∂t​Ft2​​+∂x​Fx′2t2​​.
(97)
At t=2, the partial derivative is 4 and the total derivative is 12. The difference is the contribution from changing x.
If the composite function is continuously differentiable on [a,b], the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
∫ab​dtd​F(t,x(t))dt=F(b,x(b))−F(a,x(a)).
(98)
This is the sense in which an integrated total derivative contributes endpoint terms. The endpoint statement concerns the whole composite function along the path. [ref].
