Allele

 Home Biology Genetics
OurBigBook.com Words: 215 Articles: 3
An allele is one of the alternative inherited states at a genetic locus, often distinguished by a difference in DNA sequence. A population can contain many alleles, although a two-copy diploid individual carries at most two at that genetic locus, one inherited from each parent. See NHGRI: allele.
Count copies, not just individuals, when computing allele frequency: in 100 individuals with genotype counts 49 AA, 42 Aa, 9 aa, there are 2(49)+42=140 copies of A among 200, so pA​=0.70. The heterozygotes contribute to both allele counts. This converts observed genotypes into the population quantity needed by the Hardy-Weinberg principle.
