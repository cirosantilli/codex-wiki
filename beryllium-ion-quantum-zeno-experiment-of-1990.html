Beryllium-ion quantum Zeno experiment of 1990

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum measurement Quantum Zeno effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 127
In 1990, Wayne M. Itano, Daniel J. Heinzen, J. J. Bollinger and David J. Wineland reported inhibited transitions in about 5,000 9Be+ ions held in a cylindrical Penning trap. Trapped magnesium ions, illuminated at 280 nm, kept the beryllium ions cold. A 320.7 MHz radio frequency field coupled two ground-state hyperfine structure sublevels during a 256 ms pulse adjusted for complete transfer. Up to 64 intervening 313 nm pulses addressed an optical transition from level 1: scattering distinguished it from level 2. Afterward, calibrated fluorescence counts over 100 ms measured the final quantum state population. This ensemble readout did not select ions that remained in the initial quantum state at every check; departure followed by return also contributes. The apparatus made inhibition by repeated quantum measurements experimentally accessible.[ref]
