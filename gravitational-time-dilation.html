Gravitational time dilation (dτ=Ndt,Δτ/τ≃ΔΦ/c2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Gravitational redshift
OurBigBook.com Words: 357 Articles: 1
In a static spacetime, a clock held at a fixed position records proper time dτ=Ndt for metric time component −N2c2dt2. Two clocks compared over the same coordinate interval have rate ratio Nupper​/Nlower​. Each clock ticks normally in its own laboratory; the difference appears when their readings or transmitted frequencies are compared. For a weak Newtonian gravitational potential, N≃1+Φ/c2, so the fractional excess of the upper rate is δ≃(Φupper​−Φlower​)/c2.
Take two clocks maintained at rest above Earth, separated vertically by h=1000m. Approximate the local gravitational acceleration as constant, g=9.81ms−2, neglect rotation, and use the exact speed of light c=299792458ms−1 from NIST's constants database. Then ΔΦ≃gh=9810m2s−2 and
δ≃c2gh​=1.09151×10−13,Δτ≃δ(86400s)=9.43064×10−9s.
(5)
The upper clock gains about 9.4 nanoseconds during one 86400-second interval on the lower clock. This is a leading weak-field estimate with h≪R⊕​, not a precision prediction for a particular mountain: Earth's rotation and actual gravitational potential matter in accurate comparisons. The effect is experimentally accessible. Chou, Hume, Rosenband and Wineland raised an optical clock by 0.33m and measured a fractional rate change (4.1±1.6)×10−17; the same formula with g=9.80ms−2 predicts 3.60×10−17. See their [ref]. Such comparisons make clocks probes of gravitational potential as well as instruments for timekeeping.
For the ideal spherical, nonrotating Schwarzschild spacetime, the exact static-clock factor relative to time at infinity is N(r)=1−rs​/r​, where rs​=2GM/c2 is the Schwarzschild radius. Its weak-field expansion is 1−rs​/(2r). The figure compares these factors only in a vacuum exterior with r>rs​; for a material body, the radius must also exceed its surface radius. It does not extend the exterior formula through the body's interior. See Tong's lecture notes, section 6.1 on Schwarzschild exterior geometry.
