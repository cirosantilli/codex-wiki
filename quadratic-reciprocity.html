Quadratic reciprocity

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 652 Articles: 4
Quadratic reciprocity compares two questions: is the prime number p a quadratic residue modulo q, and is q a quadratic residue modulo p? Let p,q be distinct odd primes. In terms of the Legendre symbol,
(qp​)(pq​)=(−1)2p−1​2q−1​.
(68)
The two Legendre symbols have opposite signs exactly when both primes are 3 modulo 4; otherwise they are equal. Their values are nonzero because the primes are distinct. Equal signs can mean two quadratic residues or two quadratic nonresidues: reciprocity compares the answers rather than declaring either answer positive.[ref]
For a direct computation, the nonzero quadratic residues modulo 3,5,7 are respectively {1}, {1,4}, and {1,2,4}. Squaring just 1,…,(p−1)/2 suffices because x and p−x have the same square modulo an odd prime p.
(73​)(75​)​=−1,=−1,​(37​)(57​)​=(31​)=1,=(52​)=−1.​
(69)
The pair 3,7 has a sign flip, whereas 5,7 has two negative signs. These small quadratic residue tables make the distinction tangible.
The first supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity handles numerator −1, and the second supplementary law for quadratic reciprocity handles numerator 2:
(p−1​)=(−1)(p−1)/2,(p2​)=(−1)(p2−1)/8.
(70)
For an odd prime p, −1 is a quadratic residue exactly when p≡1(mod4), while 2 is a quadratic residue exactly when p≡1,7(mod8). The remaining odd congruence classes modulo 8, namely 3,5, make 2 a quadratic nonresidue. These laws fill the cases excluded from swapping distinct odd primes.[ref]
