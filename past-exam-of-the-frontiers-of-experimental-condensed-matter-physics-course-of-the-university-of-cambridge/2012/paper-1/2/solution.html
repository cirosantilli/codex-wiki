The large rises mark the opening of Ga and As K-shell photoionization channels at their respective thresholds. Above each edge, the outgoing photoelectron interferes with waves backscattered by neighboring atoms; that interference modulates the absorption transition probability. Near-edge resonances and multiple scattering require a different level of treatment from the simple high-energy single-scattering formula. Ga has four As nearest neighbors, while As has four Ga nearest neighbors, so the two edges sample different central atoms and scattering factors even though they probe the same nearest-neighbor bond lengths.
Write k=2me​(E−E0​)​/ℏ and χ=(μ−μ0​)/μ0​ relative to a smooth atomic background. The phase 2kRj​+ϕj​(k) includes the outward-and-return propagation distance and atomic phase shifts. The factor Rj−2​ describes geometrical spreading; the backscattering amplitude depends on the neighbor species. The exponential e−2Rj​/λ(k) accounts for loss of coherent photoelectron propagation. Averaging over Gaussian bond-length fluctuations gives ⟨e2ikδR⟩=e−2k2σj2​.
The printed polarization factor needs a notation correction: for K-shell dipole absorption it is proportional to (Rj​⋅ϵ)2=cos2θj​, not literally the cosine of a dimensional vector dot product. Four equivalent tetrahedral bonds have an isotropic sum of these projection weights. If the sum is over shells rather than individual neighbors, it also needs the coordination number. A common conventional amplitude contains S02​fj​(k)/(kRj2​); omitted normalization and 1/k factors must be absorbed in the printed fB​ convention rather than silently changing its dimensions.
These are the ingredients of extended X-ray absorption fine structure. A shell fit uses calculated or reference scattering amplitudes and phases, and refines bond distances, coordination and mean-square relative disorder together with an edge-energy correction. Fourier transformation separates approximate distance contributions, but the uncorrected peak position is shifted by the scattering phase and is not an exact bond length.[ref]
Sample A has the larger Ga–As bond length. Its oscillations advance slightly faster with k: at higher k its corresponding maxima lie to the left of B's. Reading several full cycles gives approximate periods ΔkA​≃1.47A˚−1 and ΔkB​≃1.50A˚−1. In the constant/common-phase-slope estimate, R∝1/Δk, so
RB​RA​−RB​​≈ΔkA​ΔkB​​−1∼0.02.​
(1)
This is a rough percent-level difference, not a precise fitted strain. Nonconstant atomic phase shifts affect the absolute distance inferred from a period and also limit this fractional estimate. The graph does not establish which alloy has which indium concentration or how much its bulk lattice parameter changed. Local Ga–As relaxation need not follow a uniform bulk strain exactly.
Low temperature reduces thermal relative motion, preserving high-k oscillations and separating small phase changes more clearly. It does not remove zero-point motion or static alloy disorder. To obtain an illustrative disorder estimate, take the high-k envelope of the plotted kχ(k) to fall from about 0.19 at k=7.5A˚−1 to 0.12 at k=10.4A˚−1. If other envelope factors vary slowly over this interval,
σ2≈2(10.42−7.52)log(0.19/0.12)​A˚2≃4×10−3A˚2​,σ∼0.06A˚.

σ2=⟨[(uAs​−uGa​)⋅R]2⟩,
