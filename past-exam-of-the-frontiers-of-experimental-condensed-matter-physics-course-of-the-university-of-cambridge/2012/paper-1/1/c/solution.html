In X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, the electron binding energy relative to the chosen reference follows EB​=hν−EK​−ϕanalyzer​. A core-level chemical shift is a change in a core peak's binding energy between environments. Initial-state contributions include charge redistribution, bonding, the local electrostatic potential and chemical-potential changes. Final-state contributions include relaxation and screening of the newly created core hole. The measured shift contains both; it is not a direct meter of an integer oxidation state.
For a related chemical series, electron withdrawal often raises a core binding energy, but screening and referencing can change that trend. A surface core-level shift can separate surface atoms from bulk atoms because coordination and screening differ. Adsorption can introduce another component for chemically bound surface atoms. Spin-orbit doublets, multiplet or satellite features must be distinguished from separate chemical environments.
Reliable comparison needs a consistent Fermi-level or other justified energy reference, compensation or diagnosis of sample charging, adequate energy resolution and a stated line-shape/background model. Angle or photon-energy variation changes surface weighting through electron escape depth, helping distinguish surface and bulk assignments. A shifted, broader or overlapping peak alone does not uniquely identify a chemical species or its concentration.
