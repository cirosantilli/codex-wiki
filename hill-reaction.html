Hill reaction

 Home Biology Biological cell Chloroplast
OurBigBook.com Words: 508 Articles: 1
The Hill reaction couples light-driven electron transfer from water to an added electron acceptor with molecular oxygen release in an isolated photosynthetic preparation. The chloroplast dye-reduction experiment uses DCPIP.[ref]
In 1937, Robin Hill's Oxygen Evolved by Isolated Chloroplasts described a spectroscopic molecular oxygen measurement in illuminated isolated chloroplasts: oxygenation of muscle hemoglobin provided the indicator. This was an oxygen-binding readout. The publicly accessible first paragraph establishes that method; it does not specify enough preparation or control details to reconstruct the entire historical experiment here.[ref]
The experimental idea is powerful because isolation asks how much of photosynthesis remains functional outside an intact biological cell. Breaking a chloroplast envelope does not logically imply destroying every internal thylakoid membrane. Conversely, finding an active preparation cannot establish that its chloroplast envelope remains intact. Activity and structural integrity require different evidence.
