Gaussian spectral doublet resolution (Δθ>2σθ​ for two maxima in the equal-Gaussian model)

OurBigBook.com Words: 305
A spectral doublet comprises two neighbouring spectral lines. Resolving it requires information about the line response, relative strengths and noise, not only precision in reading an angle. Choose two equal angular responses shaped as Gaussian functions with common standard deviation σ and separation Δ. If x is angular displacement from their midpoint, their summed signal is
f(x)=exp(−2σ2(x−Δ/2)2​)+exp(−2σ2(x+Δ/2)2​).
(16)
These are assumed response shapes, not a measured Siegbahn frequency spectrum. By symmetry f′(0)=0, and
f′′(0)=σ22e−Δ2/(8σ2)​(4σ2Δ2​−1).
(17)
The midpoint changes from a maximum to a minimum at Δ=2σ. For this particular equal-width, equal-height model, two distinct maxima occur when Δ>2σ. Each isolated Gaussian function has full width at half maximum 22ln2​σ≃2.355σ; the two-maxima threshold is therefore about 0.849 times that width.
In this chosen model, the two Bragg angles are 30.000000∘ and 30.033085∘. Their separation is 0.827 times σ for σ=0.040∘, giving one maximum, and 4.136 times σ for σ=0.008∘, giving two. Sharpening the response can reveal the doublet without changing either emitted wavelength.
The Rayleigh criterion uses a first-zero convention for specified diffraction patterns. A Gaussian function has no finite zero, so its two-maxima threshold is not the Rayleigh criterion. Neither convention is a universal inference limit: known response shapes and adequate signal-to-noise ratio can support estimating a separation before two visible maxima appear. Unequal strengths, background, finite sampling and uncertainty in the response can make that inference much harder. Measurement resolution, the precision of an isolated line's centre and absolute wavelength accuracy should therefore be reported separately.
