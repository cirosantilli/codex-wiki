Boltzmann distribution and state degeneracy (P(E)=g(E)e−βE/Z)

Group microstates by their energy E. The state degeneracy g(E) counts how many microstates have that energy; their aggregate probability is
P(E)=∑i:Ei​=E​pi​=Zg(E)e−βE​,Z=∑distinct E​g(E)e−βE.
(69)
Here P(E) is the probability of the macrostate retaining only energy, while pi​ is the probability of one individual microstate. For two such macrostates,
P(Eb​)P(Ea​)​=g(Eb​)g(Ea​)​e−β(Ea​−Eb​).
(70)
Counting more microstates can compensate for a smaller Boltzmann factor. Omitting the state degeneracy confuses a sum over microstates with a sum over distinct energies. [ref]
