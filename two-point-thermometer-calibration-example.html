Two-point thermometer calibration example

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Laws of thermodynamics Zeroth law of thermodynamics Zeroth-law thermometer comparison

Consider an original worked thermometer calibration, using the height X of a liquid column responding through thermal expansion. Stipulate two equilibrated reference baths whose corrected thermometer readings are 0∘C and 40∘C, in degree Celsius units. Suppose the corresponding column heights are 12mm and 52mm. These are constructed inputs, not observed measurements. Assume the response is linear over this interval, with the same immersion and reading procedure throughout. Applying thermometer calibration gives
∘CtC​(X)​=4052mm−12mmX−12mm​.
(34)
For X=37mm, first subtract the zero-reference height, then divide by the full reference span: (37−12)/(52−12)=25/40. Multiplying by the 40∘C span gives tC​=25∘C, or T=298.15K in kelvin. Thus equal 37mm readings in separately equilibrated samples test equal temperatures, regardless of how much internal energy those samples contain. The Celsius offset and modern absolute-scale definition are described in degree Celsius and kelvin.
The two endpoints determine the assumed line, but do not establish linearity. An independent middle reference and a return to the initial bath can test curvature and drift. If the height has an error of 0.2mm while reference values and endpoints are held exact in this calculation, the inferred temperature error is 0.2∘C; that is only the height contribution, not a full measurement error budget. A boiling-water reference needs a measured atmospheric pressure correction, rather than an automatic 100∘C assignment. The modern kelvin is not defined by either bath.
