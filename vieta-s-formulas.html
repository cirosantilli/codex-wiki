Vieta's formulas (ek​=(−1)kan−k​/an​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 637 Articles: 4
Vieta's formulas connect two descriptions of a polynomial: its coefficients and its roots of a polynomial. They let us read the sum and product of the roots of a polynomial without finding the individual roots of a polynomial, and turn a proposed factorization of polynomials into checkable coefficient identities. The key is that coefficients encode elementary symmetric polynomials, which are unchanged by a permutation of the roots of a polynomial.
For a polynomial of polynomial degree n≥1, with nonzero leading coefficient an​, write its factorization of polynomials in a splitting field as
p(x)=an​xn+an−1​xn−1+⋯+a0​=an​∏i=1n​(x−ri​).
(14)
List every root of a polynomial according to its multiplicity of a root. Then
ek​(r1​,…,rn​)=(−1)kan​an−k​​,0≤k≤n.​
(15)
In particular, ∑i​ri​=−an−1​/an​ and ∏i​ri​=(−1)na0​/an​. With coefficients in the real numbers or complex numbers, the fundamental theorem of algebra ensures that a list of n roots of a polynomial in the complex numbers exists when multiplicity of a root is counted.[ref]
For a monic polynomial, this is the familiar convention
Xn+c1​Xn−1+⋯+cn​=∏i=1n​(X−ri​),
(16)
where (−1)kck​ is the kth elementary symmetric polynomial in the roots of a polynomial; in particular, ∏i​ri​=(−1)ncn​. The ratio by an​ in the general statement matters: multiplying every coefficient by the same nonzero scalar preserves both the roots of a polynomial and these ratios.
A short derivation explains every alternating sign. In the product ∏i​(x−ri​), a term of polynomial degree n−k arises by choosing −ri​ from k distinct factor positions and x from the others. Summing the resulting products gives (−1)kek​. Values at different positions may coincide: the selection is of positions, not distinct root values. Matching coefficients gives the formula. This is the distributive property organized by the number of chosen root factors.
