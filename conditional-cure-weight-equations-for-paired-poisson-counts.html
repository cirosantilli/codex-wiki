Conditional cure-weight equations for paired Poisson counts (θ=n−1∑i​qi​,p​=∑i​(1−qi​)ti​∑i​yi​​)

Consider an approximate conditional likelihood in which a paired total ti​ leads to a structural zero with probability θ, and otherwise a binomial distribution with success probability p. For an observed zero, the posterior cure weight is qi​=θ/[θ+(1−θ)(1−p)ti​]; for a positive count it is zero. Differentiating the log-likelihood gives the displayed equations at an interior maximum-likelihood estimator, with the weights evaluated at that estimator. Recomputing the weights and then updating the parameters is an expectation-maximization algorithm. Boundary fits must also be considered. These equations describe a conditional mixture: if cure is instead specified before two Poisson distributions are conditioned on their total, its conditional probability generally depends on the total and on the baseline nuisance rate. A zero observed in a short interval is not by itself proof of permanent biological cure.
