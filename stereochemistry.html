Words: 31
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Chirality associated with the arrangement of substituents relative to a plane, as in an unsymmetrically substituted coordinated cyclopentadienyl ring. Two planar-chiral coordination ligands can give a meso arrangement and a pair of enantiomeric arrangements.

Suprafaciality

Words: 40
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Bond-making or bond-breaking at the same face of the interacting orbital array in a pericyclic reaction. It is a local stereochemical relation, not a statement that all substituents end on one molecular face or a specification of an absolute clockwise rotation.

Dynamic kinetic resolution

Words: 29
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Selective conversion of interconverting substrate stereoisomers to an enriched product. Unlike a simple kinetic resolution, rapid substrate interconversion can in principle allow more than 50% yield of one stereoisomer.

Catalytic asymmetric synthesis

Words: 31
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
A chiral catalyst preferentially forms one stereoisomer from an achiral or stereochemically labile substrate. Product stereochemistry depends on competing transition-state free energies, rather than a catalyst simply transferring its own R or S label.

Pyramidal inversion

Interconversion of the two spatial orientations of a three-coordinate pyramidal centre through a flatter geometry. Quaternary ammonium nitrogen has no lone pair and cannot follow the ordinary three-coordinate amine pathway.

Enantiotopicity

Topicity OurBigBook.com
Two sites are enantiotopic if separately replacing one or the other by the same distinguishing group gives enantiomers. Achiral conditions do not distinguish them by chirality alone.

Diastereotopic

Words: 47
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Two sites are diastereotopic if replacing one or the other with the same test group gives diastereomers. In an achiral solvent they can have different chemical shifts, unlike a simple pair of symmetry-related sites. A rigid chiral metal chelate can make the two hydrogens of a linker methylene diastereotopic.

A 1,3-Diaxial interaction is the close nonbonded contact between axial groups separated by one ring atom in a chair. Replacing a carbon-bearing axial hydrogen by an oxygen changes this contact, so cyclohexane substituent preferences cannot be transferred unchanged to dioxanes.

Anomeric effect

Cram chelation model

Words: 34
Diastereoselectivity OurBigBook.com
An oxygen adjacent to a carbonyl can coordinate the same metal as the carbonyl oxygen, constraining conformation. Nucleophilic attack on the less hindered face of the chelate can differ from an open Felkin–Anh prediction.

Fürst-Plattner rule

Words: 36
Diastereoselectivity OurBigBook.com
The Fürst-Plattner rule describes a preference for trans-diaxial opening of suitable bridged intermediates on six-membered rings. A chair-like pathway generally avoids the unfavorable geometry of a competing twist/boat pathway; substrate fusion and substitution determine which route is accessible.

Felkin-Anh model

Words: 45
Diastereoselectivity OurBigBook.com
The Felkin-Anh model predicts facial preference in nucleophilic addition beside a stereogenic centre under suitable open, non-chelating conditions. Orient the large alpha substituent approximately perpendicular to C=O, then favour approach opposite it and past the small substituent. It is a predictive model rather than a universal guarantee.

Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules

Words: 34
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
The Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules rank substituent paths to assign stereochemical descriptors. Compare the next shell only after a tie; multiple bonds and aromatic paths require the prescribed duplicate-atom treatment. Spatial orientation and priority ranking are separate steps.

Newman projection

Words: 36
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
A Newman projection views a molecule along a chosen bond, showing substituents on the front atom and rear atom. It makes relative torsions visible without assuming that the two centres lie in the plane of a skeletal drawing.

Sugar puckering

Words: 23
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Sugar puckering describes the nonplanar conformation of a sugar ring. In nucleic acids, C2′-endo and C3′-endo conformations alter backbone geometry and help distinguish B-DNA from A-DNA.

Trans configuration

Words: 26
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
A trans configuration places the specified substituents on opposite sides of a restricted structural element. For comparable fatty-acyl chains, trans unsaturation usually permits closer packing than cis unsaturation.

Cis configuration

Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
A cis configuration places the specified substituents on the same side of a restricted structural element. A cis double bond in a fatty-acyl chain commonly disrupts close chain packing relative to the comparable trans arrangement.

Chirality (chemistry)

Words: 2k Articles: 19
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
Chirality means that a molecule cannot be superposed on its mirror image. A carbon attached to four different groups is a common source of chirality; the definition concerns the whole molecule, rather than requiring this particular structural feature.
The test is spatial: apply a translation and a rotation to the entire molecule until corresponding chemical atoms coincide, keeping their identities and chemical bonds. A mirror operation is a reflection; physically turning a model is a rotation. They are different operations. If no such superposition exists, the two forms are enantiomers; if one succeeds, the molecule is achiral. A different-looking flat drawing alone establishes neither conclusion.
For a simple molecule with exactly one tetrahedral stereocenter and four constitutionally different substituents, the mirror forms cannot superpose. Compare entire attached groups, including paths around a ring, rather than just the first chemical atom. Several tetrahedral stereocenters require a whole-molecule check: a meso compound can be achiral. Conversely, axial chirality needs no tetrahedral stereocenter.[ref]
Why care about this distinction? Two enantiomers can fit differently into an already chiral molecule, just as two opposite hands fit differently into one glove. Thus a protein's spatial arrangement can distinguish a mirror pair even when an achiral measurement cannot. Polarimetry provides a different kind of test: it measures a sample's net optical rotation, which depends on the populations of its enantiomers.

Enantiomer

Words: 108 Articles: 1
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
Enantiomers are a pair of nonsuperposable mirror-image molecules. The D and L configurations of alanine are an example; glycine has no such pair because its amino acid alpha carbon carries two identical hydrogen chemical atoms. [ref]
The word names a relation between two stereoisomers. Merely finding two different spatial arrangements does not make them enantiomers: arrangements that are different but not mirror partners are diastereomers.

Racemate

Words: 43
Enantiomer OurBigBook.com
A racemate contains the same amount of substance of each member of an enantiomer pair. Their equal and opposite contributions cancel in ordinary solution polarimetry under identical conditions. Zero net optical rotation therefore does not establish achirality of the individual molecules.[ref]

Chiral molecule

Words: 8
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
A chiral molecule fails the whole-molecule mirror-superposition test defining chirality.

Achirality

Words: 43
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
Achirality means that a molecule is superposable on its mirror image. A reflection plane that leaves the complete labelled spatial structure unchanged establishes achirality. Absence of such a plane alone is not a complete test: other orientation-reversing spatial symmetries can also make a rigid structure achiral.

Tetrahedral mirror-image test

Words: 286
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
Take a central chemical atom at the origin and four substituents at the vertices of an ideal regular tetrahedron:
a=(1,1,1),b=(1,−1,−1),c=(−1,1,−1),d=(−1,−1,1).
(1)
These dimensionless coordinates describe shape, not measured bond lengths. For distinct labels A, B, C and D, define a signed determinant
Δ=det​∣a−d∣​∣b−d∣​∣c−d∣​​=+16.
(2)
It is six times the oriented volume of the labelled tetrahedron. A translation cancels from the differences. A rotation Q has detQ=+1 and preserves Δ, whereas the mirror matrix S=diag(−1,1,1) has detS=−1 and changes it to −16. Consequently no rotation can place every distinct label on its mirror partner. This argument uses three-dimensional coordinates, not clockwise order on a page.
Now let C and D both be the same substituent H. Swapping these identical groups is allowed. For this model the half-turn
Q=​−100​001​010​​,QTQ=I,detQ=+1,
(3)
about the axis (0,1,1) maps the reflected A and B to their original positions and exchanges the two H positions. The models superpose. With distinct C and D, the same half-turn fails precisely at those two labels; the determinant argument rules out every other rotation as well. This is the local reason two identical hydrogen chemical atoms prevent glycine's amino acid alpha carbon from being a tetrahedral stereocenter.
For a flexible molecule, also distinguish conformational isomerism from a persistent enantiomer pair. A particular frozen shape may be chiral while ordinary internal motions interconvert mirror shapes. Turning the entire model is always allowed in a spatial comparison; an internal motion must be considered with its actual accessibility and timescale.

Meso compound

Words: 100
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
A meso compound is an achiral molecule despite having stereocenters. Tartaric acid, HOOC−CH(OH)−CH(OH)−COOH, has two tetrahedral carbon stereocenters. Its (2R,3R) and (2S,3S) forms are enantiomers, but (2R,3S) and (2S,3R) name the same meso form after equivalent ends are identified. A symmetric spatial arrangement has a mirror plane exchanging its two halves. Thus there are three stereoisomers, not four.[ref]
Opposite R and S configurations alone do not guarantee a meso compound: the rest of the molecule must permit mirror superposition. Likewise, a meso compound is one achiral chemical compound, whereas a racemate is a mixture of two chiral molecule populations. Both can give zero optical rotation for different reasons.

Axial chirality

Words: 113 Articles: 3
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
An allene has a C=C=C unit whose terminal substituents lie in perpendicular planes. In abC=C=Ccd, with a=b and c=d, the two terminal pairs can define enantiomers without any tetrahedral carbon stereocenter. The groups need be different within each end pair, not all four mutually different. This is axial chirality.[ref]

Optical activity

Words: 1k Articles: 8
Chirality (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
Optical activity is the ability to rotate the direction of linear polarization of transmitted light. In an achiral solvent, the two members of an enantiomer pair have equal and opposite optical rotations at the same wavelength, temperature and concentration. The measured angle concerns a bulk sample; it is not a geometric rotation of the molecules.[ref]
The rotating object in this description is the direction along which the electric field oscillates. At each position the transmitted light can still have linear polarization: its electric field moves back and forth along a line, while the orientation of that line changes along the sample. Nothing requires the molecules to turn bodily through the observed angle. This makes polarimetry a bridge from molecular chirality to a visible bulk measurement, and, with a suitable calibration, a way to follow concentration or the balance of enantiomers.
In a transparent, isotropic chemical solution, natural optical rotation can be understood as circular birefringence: the two opposite circular polarizations accumulate different wave phases, and their equal-amplitude sum remains linearly polarized along a rotated direction. Circular dichroism instead means unequal absorption of those components and can produce elliptical polarization. These are related responses, not interchangeable measurements. General birefringence need not produce this simple rotation of linear polarization.[ref]

Optical rotation (α)

Words: 439 Articles: 2
Optical activity OurBigBook.com
Optical rotation is the signed angle measured in polarimetry. Positive rotation is clockwise for an observer facing the incoming light; negative rotation is counterclockwise. State the viewing convention, wavelength, temperature, solvent, concentration and path length when comparing measurements. R and S configuration specifies spatial arrangement, and does not predict the sign of optical rotation.[ref]
Here the observer stands beyond the sample, on the detector side, looking back toward the source. Measure both the polarimeter analyzer angle θ and the optical rotation α clockwise from the blank's polarization direction. Looking from the source end reverses the apparent sense. A line has no distinguished arrowhead, so an intensity scan alone determines its orientation only modulo 180∘; continuity or additional information must choose the appropriate rotation branch.
Specific rotation ([α]λT​)
Words: 312 Articles: 1
Optical rotation OurBigBook.com
For a chemical solution in a validated linear regime,
α=[α]λT​lc,
(4)
where l is path length in dm and c is solute mass per final solution volume in gmL−1. These conventions give specific rotation units degmLg−1dm−1. Specify solvent, temperature T and wavelength λ: specific rotation is conditional on the sample environment.[ref]
For an original model, stipulate an unnamed enantiomer pair in the same achiral solvent at T=20∘C and wavelength λ=589nm. Assign the positive member specific rotation +40degmLg−1dm−1, assumed constant over the modeled concentrations. With l=1dm and c=0.200gmL−1,
α=(+40)(1)(0.200)∘=+8∘.
(5)
These are chosen model parameters, not measured properties of a named material. Doubling only l or only c doubles α within this stipulated linear model; doubling both multiplies it by four. A real chemical solution must be checked for concentration dependence rather than assuming that 0.200gmL−1 is dilute enough.
For an additive mixture containing only this enantiomer pair as solutes, put k=[α]+​ and write c+​,c−​ for their mass concentrations. Then
α=kl(c+​−c−​),c=c+​+c−​.
(6)
Because enantiomers have the same molar mass, their relative mole fractions within the pair also equal their relative mass fractions: c+​=x+​c, c−​=x−​c. Thus α=klc(x+​−x−​), which connects optical rotation to enantiomeric excess.
Enantiomeric excess (ee)

The enantiomeric excess of an enantiomer pair is the magnitude of the difference between their relative mole fractions, ee=∣x+​−x−​∣, often reported as a percentage. Here x+​+x−​=1 counts the pair only, excluding solvent; the signs label optical rotation under the specified conditions, not R and S configuration.
In the model above, x+​=0.75 and x−​=0.25 give ee=0.50=50%. Keep the same total pair concentration c=0.200gmL−1, path length, solvent, temperature and wavelength. The two contributions are
c+​=0.150,c−​=0.050(gmL−1),α=40(1)(0.150−0.050)∘=+4∘.
(7)
The +8∘ pure-member reference therefore gives α/αpure,+​=+0.50. This ratio measures signed population imbalance only for a pure pair with additive linear responses under matching conditions. Adding unrelated solutes, changing total concentration, or changing chemical equilibrium can invalidate that inference. For x+​=x−​=0.50, the racemate gives zero net optical rotation by cancellation; each molecule remains chiral. A zero reading alone proves neither a racemate nor molecular achirality.

Polarimetry

Words: 604 Articles: 3
Optical activity OurBigBook.com
Polarimetry measures optical rotation with a polarimeter. The vertical Vernier Go Direct instrument uses a 589nm light-emitting diode source, fixed polarizer, sample cell, motor-driven polarimeter analyzer, photodetector and optical rotary encoder. Calibrate using the same solvent at 10cm fill height; rinse with sample, fill to 10.0cm, and read the meniscus to 0.1cm. The readout plots illumination against analyzer angle; a maximum near zero or a cosine-squared fit locates α.[ref]
An actual teaching experiment in the Truman State University-hosted Optical Activity Laboratory handout, revised July 2013, uses sucrose chemical solutions. Weigh the sucrose, dissolve completely without heating, and measure final solution volume for concentration. Use one polarimeter throughout; measure the water blank's extinction reading, then each sample's extinction reading and actual liquid path length near 10cm. Subtract the blank and plot corrected optical rotation against concentration. The attached apparatus description specifies a vertical light path, an incandescent or tungsten source with yellow filter, sheet polarizers and a rotating eyepiece polarimeter analyzer. Its coarse demonstration permits temperature control to the nearest 5∘C, path length to the nearest millimeter and approximately 1∘ angular precision. These are design tolerances, not measured results.[ref]
For a quantitative comparison, equilibrate blank, reference and sample at the same recorded temperature; use a thermostatted cell when needed. This is a recommended control, not a claim that the teaching instruments provide a thermostat. A yellow filter also passes a band of wavelengths, so its result need not match a narrow-source reference exactly. The laboratory's concentration series tests the linear model rather than establishing it in advance; a second series varying only path length tests the other factor.
For our original ideal model, Malus's law gives
I(θ)=I0​cos2(θ−α),
(8)
where I0​ is light intensity immediately before the ideal polarimeter analyzer, after sample transmission. It is not the intensity of the original unpolarized source. The angle difference is evaluated in consistent units; the plotted degree values are converted to radians before computing the cosine. Assume transmitted light retains linear polarization, with negligible scattering and no polarization-dependent absorption. The pure positive model, 75:25 mixture and racemate shift the maxima to 8∘, 4∘ and 0∘, respectively. Minima shift by the same amount, to 98∘, 94∘ and 90∘. Thus an extinction measurement and a maximum measurement recover the same shift after their respective blanks are subtracted.
Repeat blanks and use a reference to check drift and scale. Bubbles, particles, stressed windows and sample absorption can spoil the ideal curve. Vernier specifies ±1∘ accuracy; the manual's ±0.1∘ fit reproducibility does not establish that accuracy. It warns against interpreting its illumination readout quantitatively and requires a transparent homogeneous sample.[ref] Neither a zero reading nor a matching specific rotation uniquely identifies a molecule.

Absolute configuration

Absolute configuration records a spatial arrangement without leaving its handedness relative to its mirror unspecified. R and S configuration is one naming convention; optical rotation is a measured response.

R and S configuration (R, S)

Words: 72
Absolute configuration OurBigBook.com
For a tetrahedral stereocenter, rank its four substituents using stereochemical sequence rules. With lowest-priority group pointing away, a clockwise 1→2→3 sequence is R and a counterclockwise sequence is S. Looking from the other side reverses the apparent sequence, so the viewing condition is essential. The geometric letters A/B/C/D in the tetrahedral mirror-image test carry no assigned priority. Neither R nor S determines positive or negative optical rotation.[ref]

Stereochemical sequence rule

Words: 38
Absolute configuration OurBigBook.com
Stereochemical sequence rules rank substituents for R and S configuration. Compare the directly attached chemical atoms by atomic number first, then resolve ties by comparing successive shells at the first difference. Isotopes and multiple chemical bonds require additional rules. This ranking convention does not involve optical rotation.[ref]

Stereocenter

Words: 90 Articles: 2
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
At a stereocenter, exchanging two attached groups produces a different spatial configuration. The open-chain ribose and glucose examples have carbon stereocenters bearing four different groups; C2 of deoxyribose has two identical hydrogen substituents and is not a stereocenter. See Moss, Basic Terminology of Stereochemistry, IUPAC Recommendations 1996: stereogenic units.

Phosphorus stereocenter

Words: 26
Stereocenter OurBigBook.com
A phosphorus centre with an appropriate set of four distinguishable substituent directions can be stereogenic. A phosphate triester with three different alkoxy groups illustrates phosphorus chirality.

Quaternary ammonium stereocenter

Words: 20
Stereocenter OurBigBook.com
A nitrogen bonded to four distinct substituents is stereogenic. It cannot undergo ordinary three-coordinate amine inversion without a bond-breaking process.

Fischer projection

Words: 100
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
In a Fischer projection, each cross represents a carbon position: horizontal chemical bonds point toward the viewer and vertical chemical bonds point away. For the open-chain aldehyde sugars illustrated here, place CHO at the top and CH2​OH at the bottom. This is a convention for stereochemistry, not a measured molecular shape. See Moss, IUPAC Recommendations 1996: Fischer projection.
In these Fischer projections, D specifies that the hydroxy group at the lowest stereocenter lies on the right: C4 for ribose and deoxyribose, C5 for glucose. D does not specify every other stereocenter, and it does not specify the direction of rotation of polarized light. See McMurry: the meaning of D and L.

Haworth projection

Words: 21
Stereochemistry OurBigBook.com
A Haworth projection depicts cyclic monosaccharide connectivity and substituents above or below a reference ring plane; it does not assert a physically flat ring.
