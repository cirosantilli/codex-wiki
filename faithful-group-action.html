Faithful group action (kerρ={e})

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action
OurBigBook.com Words: 604 Articles: 3
A group action is faithful when only the identity acts as the identity on every point.
For a group action of G on a set X, form the group homomorphism
ρ:G⟶Sym(X),ρ(g)(x)=g⋅x.
(41)
A faithful group action means that ρ is injective: different group elements induce different permutations of X. Equivalently, its group kernel is trivial:
K=kerρ={g∈G:g⋅x=x for every x∈X}=⋂x∈X​Gx​={e}.
(42)
Here Gx​ is the stabilizer subgroup of x, and e is the identity element.[ref]
The test is global. A faithful group action requires each nonidentity group element to move at least one point; a free group action requires it to move every point. In the faithful coset action of the symmetric group on three letters, (12) fixes one coset and exchanges the other two. Thus a nontrivial stabilizer subgroup does not rule out faithfulness. The distinction follows directly from the order of the quantifiers:
faithful: ∀g=e ∃x: g⋅x=x,free: ∀g=e ∀x: g⋅x=x.
(43)
On a nonempty set, a free group action is therefore a faithful group action. A transitive group action instead asks whether every point can reach every other; it allows several group elements to perform exactly the same rearrangement. The nonfaithful coset action of the symmetric group on three letters is a counterexample to the claim that every transitive group action is a faithful group action.
For a short proof of the group kernel criterion, ρ(g)=ρ(h) exactly when ρ(h−1g) is the identity permutation, or h−1g∈K. If K={e}, this forces g=h; if K contains a nonidentity element, that element and e act identically. Thus a faithful group action turns G into an actual subgroup of a symmetric group, retaining its group operation as function composition. Choosing a suitable coset action can give a much smaller set of labels than the regular group action used in Cayley's theorem.
