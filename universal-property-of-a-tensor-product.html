Universal property of a tensor product (B=L∘τ)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Multilinear algebra Tensor product
OurBigBook.com Words: 196
For a bilinear map B:V×W→U, there is exactly one linear map L:V⊗F​W→U satisfying L(v⊗w)=B(v,w). Equivalently, B=L∘τ, where τ(v,w)=v⊗w. The universal property of a tensor product concerns the vector space together with this distinguished bilinear map.[ref]
Here is a complete calculation over the real numbers. Define the bilinear map
B(v,w)=(v1​w1​+2v1​w2​+v2​w2​, v2​w1​−v1​w2​).
(4)
Its values on pairs of basis vectors determine L on the four tensor-product basis elements:
L(e1​⊗f1​)L(e2​⊗f1​)​=(1,0),=(0,1),​L(e1​⊗f2​)L(e2​⊗f2​)​=(2,−1),=(1,0).​
(5)
Consequently, for t=∑i,j=12​cij​ei​⊗fj​,
L(t)=(c11​+2c12​+c22​, c21​−c12​).
(6)
For v=(2,1) and w=(−1,3), the direct calculation gives B(v,w)=(−2+12+3,−1−6)=(13,−7). Using the expansion of v⊗w instead gives L(v⊗w)=−2(1,0)+6(2,−1)−(0,1)+3(1,0)=(13,−7). The linear map also handles a nonseparable tensor such as t=e1​⊗f1​+e2​⊗f2​: L(t)=(2,0), even though no single pair has v⊗w=t.
A short proof uses a tensor-product basis: assign L(ei​⊗fj​)=B(ei​,fj​) and extend by linearity. Expanding both inputs verifies L(v⊗w)=B(v,w) by bilinearity; the assigned values on a basis force uniqueness. More generally, every multilinear map F:V1​×⋯×Vr​→U factors uniquely through a linear map from V1​⊗⋯⊗Vr​. This lets formulas involving several inputs define linear maps without choosing coordinates; tensor contraction uses the evaluation pairing between a vector and a covector.
