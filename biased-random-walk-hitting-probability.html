Biased random walk hitting probability (hi​=(q/p)i,p>q=1−p,i≥0)

For a biased random walk on the nonnegative integers with upward probability p>q=1−p>0, the hitting probability of zero from i is (q/p)i. Indeed, first-step analysis gives hi​=phi+1​+qhi−1​ for i≥1, with h0​=1. Its characteristic roots are 1 and β=q/p, so any solution is A+(1−A)βi. Nonnegativity as i→∞ forces A≥0 and hence a value at least βi. This candidate itself satisfies the recurrence. The theorem on the minimal nonnegative solution for hitting probabilities therefore identifies it with hi​. A walk on all of Z has the same probability until it first reaches zero.
