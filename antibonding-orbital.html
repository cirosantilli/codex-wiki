Antibonding orbital (σu∗​)

 Home Chemistry Quantum chemistry Molecular orbital theory Molecular orbital
OurBigBook.com Words: 117
Occupying an antibonding molecular orbital opposes a chemical bond. For equivalent 1s atomic orbitals of the hydrogen atom, the difference σu∗​=σ−​ vanishes on the plane halfway between the protons: every point there is equally far from both centres, so the amplitudes cancel. This nodal plane removes the contribution of this molecular orbital to electron density on that plane; it does not make an entire finite region empty. The 1s-derived antibonding molecular orbital lies above its bonding molecular orbital partner in energy. See Field, MIT 5.61, lecture 25, pp. 1–2.
In the He2​ minimal model, (σu∗​)2 adds Na​=2 and cancels the bond order contribution of (σg​)2. Opposite signs on the two lobes describe probability amplitudes, while both lobes have nonnegative probability density after squaring. The star is an orbital label, not an extra electron or an electric charge.
