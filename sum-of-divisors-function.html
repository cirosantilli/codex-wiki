Sum-of-divisors function (σ1​(n))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Multiplicative function Divisor sum
OurBigBook.com Words: 384 Articles: 1
The sum-of-divisors function adds all positive integer divisors of a positive integer, including the number itself:
σ(n)=σ1​(n)=∑d∣n​d,σ(1)=1.
(15)
It is the divisor sum of the identity arithmetic function id(n)=n, so σ=id∗1 under Dirichlet convolution. Both factors are multiplicative functions, hence so is the sum-of-divisors function. The notation σ1​ distinguishes it from other weighted divisor sums. [ref]
For a prime number p and a nonnegative exponent a, the positive integer divisors of the prime power pa are precisely 1,p,…,pa. Their finite geometric series gives
σ(pa)=1+p+⋯+pa=p−1pa+1−1​.
(16)
For example, σ(8)=1+2+4+8=15=(24−1)/(2−1). For a prime factorization n=∏k=1r​pkak​​ with distinct prime numbers pk​ and positive exponents ak​, multiplicativity gives
σ(n)=∏k=1r​(1+pk​+⋯+pkak​​)=∏k=1r​pk​−1pkak​+1​−1​.
(17)
For n=1, this is an empty product, equal to 1. [ref]
The distributive property makes the formula especially beautiful: choosing one term from each factor produces each positive integer divisor exactly once, by the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. For 12=4⋅3=22⋅3, the choices form a three-by-two grid with entries 2i3j, where 0≤i≤2 and 0≤j≤1. Adding its rows gives
σ(12)=(1+2+4)+(3+6+12)=(1+2+4)(1+3)=7⋅4=28.
(18)
The divisor function counts six cells; the sum-of-divisors function adds their six values. Once the prime factorization is known, the product formula avoids testing every candidate integer divisor. Shared prime factors require care: σ(4)=7=9=σ(2)2, so the sum-of-divisors function is not a completely multiplicative function.
Subtracting the number itself gives the proper-divisor sum s(n)=σ(n)−n. Comparing s(n) with n distinguishes perfect numbers, deficient numbers, and abundant numbers. For 6, s(6)=1+2+3=6; for 28, s(28)=1+2+4+7+14=28. Both are perfect numbers. The calculation s(8)=1+2+4=7<8 makes 8 a deficient number, while s(12)=1+2+3+4+6=16>12 makes 12 an abundant number. These comparisons turn the sizes of integer divisors into a classification of positive integers. [ref]
This connection is ancient: Euclid's Elements, Book IX, Proposition 36, constructs a perfect number by multiplying the last term of 1,2,…,2k−1 by their sum, when that sum is a prime number. In modern notation, put q=2k−1 and N=2k−1q, with k≥2 and q a prime number. The sum-of-divisors function proves the construction immediately:
σ(N)=(2k−1)(1+q)=q2k=2N.
(19)
Consequently the proper-divisor sum is s(N)=N. Taking k=2,3 produces the perfect numbers 6,28. [ref]
