Infinitely many heads as a tail event (A={Xj​=1 infinitely often})

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Independent sigma-algebras Tail sigma-algebra Tail event
OurBigBook.com Words: 244
On {0,1}N, the event of infinitely many heads is
A=⋂m=1∞​⋃j≥m​{Xj​=1}.
(31)
It says that after every index there is another head. For each n, the same intersection can start at m=n, so A∈σ(Xn​,Xn+1​,…); therefore A is a tail event. This verifies measurability directly using countable unions and intersections.
Assume now that the coordinates are fair independent Bernoulli trials. For fixed m, let Bm,r​ be the event of at least one head among Xm​,…,Xm+r−1​. Its complement requires r tails, so
P(Bm,r​)=1−2−r,Bm​=⋃r≥1​Bm,r​,P(Bm​)=1.
(32)
The last equality follows from continuity of probability. Now A=⋂m≥1​Bm​, a countable intersection of almost sure events, and hence P(A)=1. This determines which of the two possibilities in Kolmogorov's zero–one law occurs. Equivalently, the Borel-Cantelli second lemma applies to the independent events {Xj​=1}.
Nevertheless, no finite prefix certifies membership in A: every finite record can be extended by all tails or by alternating heads and tails. Under the fair independent model, conditioning on any exact finite prefix leaves P(A)=1, since A is independent of the initial sigma-algebra. The excluded all-tails continuation has probability zero, but remains a possible member of the sample space. Logical determination by a finite record and a conclusion holding almost surely are different statements.
