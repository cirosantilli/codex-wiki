Kolmogorov-Smirnov test

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
The one-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test checks goodness of fit by comparing an empirical distribution function Fn​ with a specified cumulative distribution function F0​. Its Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic Dn​ is the largest vertical gap anywhere between the two curves. Every threshold asks the same concrete question: what proportion of the observations are at or below it, and what probability does the proposed probability distribution assign there? A large discrepancy supplies evidence against the null hypothesis.
For example, four stipulated observations (1/10,2/5,4/5,9/10) compared with the uniform distribution on [0,1] give D4​=3/10. Just before 4/5, only two observations have been counted, so the empirical distribution function approaches 1/2, while F0​ approaches 4/5. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic section calculates every jump-side gap. These are exact illustrative numbers, not experimental measurements or a claim of statistical significance.
The ordinary one-sample calibration assumes independent and identically distributed real observations and a simple hypothesis H0​:F=F0​, with F0​ a continuous function fixed before inspecting these observations. All statistical parameters of F0​ must be specified.[ref] Under that null hypothesis, the probability integral transform gives Ui​=F0​(Xi​) with the uniform distribution on (0,1), retaining independence. Consequently the sampling distribution of Dn​ depends on the sample size n, but not on the particular continuous F0​. This is the useful sense in which the calibration is distribution-free.
For the exponential distribution of rate one, F0​(x)=1−e−x for x≥0. Take xi​=−log(1−ui​) for the four stipulated ui​ above. The probability integral transform changes the horizontal coordinates but preserves both the order statistics and their cumulative heights, so the same Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic 3/10 appears on both scales. More generally, for continuous F0​, the jump-side formulas depend only on F0​(X(i)​). Strict increase everywhere is unnecessary: intervals on which F0​ is constant have probability zero under the null hypothesis. This explains the invariance without assuming a strictly positive probability density function.
The observed Kolmogorov-Smirnov statistic is a discrepancy, whereas its p-value is a tail probability under the null hypothesis:
p=PrH0​​(Dnnew​≥dobs​).
(7)
Here Dnnew​ is computed from a fresh sample of the same sample size. Choose a significance level α before the test and reject when the calibrated p-value is at most α. The value D4​=3/10 alone supplies neither a p-value nor a rejection decision. Failure to reject does not establish that the null hypothesis is true. A finite-sample calibration and the asymptotic Kolmogorov-Smirnov theorem are distinct; a cutoff for Dn​ must be rescaled before use with n​Dn​.[ref]
Fitting statistical parameters on the tested observations replaces F0​ by the random Fθ​. The resulting transformed observations need not be independent and identically distributed uniforms, so the ordinary reference law fails. The Lilliefors test addresses a particular fitted normal distribution. More general parametric bootstrap calibration must simulate from the fitted statistical model and repeat the fitting in every replicate; holding the fitted statistical parameters fixed during those comparisons calibrates a different procedure. Such a parametric bootstrap requires its own validity justification rather than inheriting exactness from the simple-null test.
For a discrete probability distribution, F0​(X) is generally not uniform. Equal observations also arise from rounding, so tied data require a calibration that accounts for the discrete probability distribution or the rounding rule. The discrepancy remains meaningful, but continuous-null p-values are not automatically valid. Statistical dependence likewise changes the reference law. The two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test compares two empirical distribution functions and has a different calibration.[ref]
