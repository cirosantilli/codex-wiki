Information projection and rare sample means

Take independent and identically distributed draws from the uniform three-outcome reference above. An observed sample mean at least 1/2 is unusual because the source expected value is zero. For two draws, the only successful ordered pairs are (0,1),(1,0),(1,1), giving probability 3/9=1/3.
For long samples, the relevant empirical distributions have mean at least 1/2. The method of types assigns a profile q a leading probability cost e−nD(q∥p). The least costly admissible profile is the information projection on three outcomes: in this example the minimizing mean is on the boundary 1/2, and the leading exponent is approximately 0.197378 nats per draw. The exponential scale is not an exact finite-sample probability formula.[ref]
The attraction of this connection is that a constrained optimization problem predicts the most probable way an unusual aggregate observation can occur. It links relative entropy, expected value constraints and rare empirical distributions without identifying a rare event with just one exact attainable type class.
