Kelvin thermoelectric relation (Π=TS)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Thermoelectric effect Peltier effect Peltier coefficient
OurBigBook.com Words: 218
With the Seebeck coefficient convention dV=−SdT at zero electric current, and the transported-heat convention of Peltier coefficient, the Kelvin thermoelectric relation is
Π(T)=TS(T),ΠA→Babs​(T)=T[SB​(T)−SA​(T)].
(19)
T is absolute temperature in kelvins; use consistent material references. This scalar linear response relation assumes local thermal equilibrium and reciprocal transport without time-reversal-breaking fields or order. Onsager reciprocal relations supply the symmetry; conservation of energy alone is insufficient. A magnetic field requires a reversed-field reciprocity comparison.[ref]
In particular, the A-minus-B thermocouple coefficient in Seebeck effect is SA​−SB​, whereas absorbed junction heat for conventional current A to B uses SB​−SA​. There is no contradiction: terminal voltage order and crossing direction are separate choices. Multiplying a measured voltage by an absolute temperature without specifying these choices does not determine junction heating.
A short reciprocity calculation makes the factor T visible. At a common reference temperature, write the local linear response with electrical driving field E=−∇V and heat flux q as
j=L11​TE​+L12​T2−∇T​,q=L21​TE​+L22​T2−∇T​.
(20)
At j=0, the Seebeck coefficient convention gives S=L12​/(TL11​). At ∇T=0, the Peltier coefficient is q/j=L21​/L11​. Thus L21​=L12​ gives Π=TS. The chosen forces are conjugate to the fluxes in the local entropy production, jE/T+q(−∇T)/T2; arbitrary rescaling of just one force would spoil this argument.
The relation is local. Varying S(T) permits distributed Thomson effect heating, with τ=TdS/dT; the constant-coefficient model below omits it. Endpoint substitutions into a constant-property formula can therefore break its energy accounting.
