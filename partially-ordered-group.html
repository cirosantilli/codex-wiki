Partially ordered group

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 40
A group with a partial order preserved by multiplication on both sides: a≤b implies xay≤xby for all x,y. In additive notation this says a≤b⇒x+a+y≤x+b+y. Preserving only one side is a different orderability condition.

Positive cone of an ordered group (G≥1​={g:1≤g})

Words: 53
Partially ordered group OurBigBook.com
The nonnegative cone determines the partial order by a≤b⟺a−1b∈G≥1​. It contains the identity element, is closed under products and conjugation, and intersects its inverse only in the identity. Conversely these conditions define a two-sided invariant partial order. Its strictly positive part omits the identity; conventions for G+ differ, so specify which is intended.

Convex subgroup of an ordered group

Words: 43
Partially ordered group OurBigBook.com
A subgroup C is convex if c1​≤g≤c2​ with c1​,c2​∈C implies g∈C. In a linearly ordered group, convex subgroups form a chain under inclusion: positive elements witnessing incomparability would be comparable and force the smaller one into the other subgroup by convexity.

Lattice-ordered group (ℓ-group)

Words: 2k Articles: 37
Partially ordered group OurBigBook.com
An ordered group whose partial order is a lattice. Each pair has a join f∨g and a meet f∧g. Translations preserve these operations, for example a(f∨g)b=afb∨agb. Pointwise maximum and minimum make the order automorphisms of a chain into a lattice-ordered group.

Riesz decomposition in a lattice-ordered group (1≤x≤ab⟹x=a′b′,1≤a′≤a,1≤b′≤b)

Words: 50
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
For a,b≥1, take a′=x∧a and b′=(a′)−1x. Then a′≥1 and b′=1∨a−1x is between one and b. Thus x=a′b′. Repetition splits a positive element bounded by a finite product into factors bounded by the corresponding factors. Every resulting positive factor is also bounded by x.

Lattice-ordered group homomorphism

Words: 20
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A group homomorphism preserving both join and meet. A bijective such map is an isomorphism of lattice-ordered groups; an injective one is an embedding.

Absolute value in a lattice-ordered group (∣g∣=g∨g−1)

Words: 64 Articles: 1
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
The displayed join is nonnegative in a lattice-ordered group. In an additive Abelian group it becomes ∣g∣=g∨(−g). This order-theoretic absolute value need not be a real scalar.

Positive and negative parts in a lattice-ordered group (g+=g∨1,g−=g−1∨1)

Words: 40
Absolute value in a lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
The two positive parts satisfy g=g+(g−)−1, commute with one another, and have meet one. They isolate positive and negative displacement in the pointwise model of a lattice-ordered permutation group. These are group elements, unlike the scalar positive part of a real number.

Convex lattice subgroup

Words: 653 Articles: 9
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A convex subgroup of an ordered group that is also closed under join and meet. A normal convex lattice subgroup is the kernel of the quotient lattice-ordered group homomorphism.

One-sided bounds for adjoining a positive element (⟨H,a⟩c​={g:∣g∣≤han}={g:∣g∣≤anh})

Words: 123
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
Assume uv≤v2u2 for all u,v≥1 in a lattice-ordered group. For a convex lattice subgroup H and a≥1, the displayed bounds allow h∈H with h≥1 and integers n≥0. To prove the first set is a subgroup, use (han)(kam)≤hk2a2n+m. Products and inverse products are both bounded because xy≤∣x∣∣y∣ and (xy)−1≤∣y∣∣x∣. Taking the larger exponent and a join of the two bounds in H bounds their absolute value in a lattice-ordered group. Inversion preserves that absolute value; common bounds using h∨k prove lattice closure and convexity. The set contains H and a, and any convex lattice subgroup containing them contains the entire set. The second equality follows by applying the proof to the opposite group, where the required inequality is the original one with the variables interchanged.

Ordered right cosets of a convex lattice subgroup (Pg≤Ph⟺g≤ph for some p∈P)

Words: 49
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
The displayed relation makes the right cosets into a lattice, with Pg∨Ph=P(g∨h) and Pg∧Ph=P(g∧h). Antisymmetry uses convexity: g≤ph and h≤qg give 1≤phg−1≤pq∈P, hence hg−1∈P. A prime convex lattice subgroup makes this order total even if the subgroup is not normal.

Lattice of convex lattice subgroups

Words: 71
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
The convex lattice subgroups of a lattice-ordered group form a distributive lattice, with intersection as meet and the convex lattice subgroup generated by a union as join. A positive element of a join is bounded by a finite product of positive elements from the generating subgroups. Riesz decomposition in a lattice-ordered group splits such an element into positive factors from those subgroups. If the original element belongs to another convex subgroup E, every factor does too. Consequently E∩(C∨D)=(E∩C)∨(E∩D).

Principal convex lattice subgroup (⟨u⟩c​={g:u−n≤g≤un for some n})

Words: 42
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
For u≥1 in a lattice-ordered group, the displayed set is the smallest convex lattice subgroup containing u. Products of two such bounds are bounded by a larger power of u; inversion and the lattice operations preserve the bounds. This gives an explicit test for membership.

Prime convex lattice subgroup

Words: 41
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
A proper convex lattice subgroup P is prime when a,b≥1 and a∧b=1 imply a∈P or b∈P. Equivalently the ordered right cosets of P form a totally ordered set. Prime refers to this order property, and does not require a normal subgroup or prime index.

Value in a lattice-ordered group

Words: 307 Articles: 3
Convex lattice subgroup OurBigBook.com
A value of g=1 is a convex lattice subgroup maximal among those omitting g. Zorn's lemma supplies a value: the union of a chain of convex lattice subgroups omitting g still omits g. Such subgroups are prime. Their unique cover is the smallest convex lattice subgroup properly containing them.
Squared bound from comparison at values (Vpq≥Vx for all values of x⟹p2q2≥x)
Words: 110
Value in a lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
Let p,q,x≥1 in a lattice-ordered group. Under the displayed hypothesis, suppose d=(xq−2p−2)∨1>1 and choose a value P of d. If x∈P, then 1≤d≤x contradicts its omission. Otherwise extend P to a value V of x. The right-coset test is Vu≤Vv⟺(uv−1)∨1∈V. If p∈/V, put c=(xq−2p−1)∨1∈V. Then (pc−1)∨1∈/V, while its inverse positive part is (cp−1)∨1=d. These parts have meet one, so primeness of P forces d∈P. If p∈V, put c=(xq−1)∨1∈V and t=p2q∈/V; the disjoint pair (tc−1)∨1 and (ct−1)∨1=d gives the same contradiction. No normality assumption is used in this separation argument.
Cover of a value in a lattice-ordered group (V∗=⟨V,t⟩c​)
Words: 151 Articles: 1
Value in a lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
If V is a value in a lattice-ordered group of t>1, every convex lattice subgroup strictly containing V contains t. Thus the convex lattice subgroup generated by V and t is the unique least subgroup properly containing V. It is called the cover of V. There is no intermediate convex lattice subgroup. A normal-valued lattice-ordered group requires V to be a normal subgroup of this cover, not necessarily of the whole group. Values are prime convex lattice subgroups: maximality omitting t makes them meet-irreducible in the lattice of convex lattice subgroups, so their ordered right cosets form a chain.
Power cofinality in the cover of a value (Vb≤Van,bV≤amV)
Words: 70
Cover of a value in a lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
Under the inequality in one-sided bounds for adjoining a positive element, if a≥1 belongs to the cover of a value in a lattice-ordered group V∗ but not to V, then ⟨V,a⟩c​=V∗. Thus every b≥1 in the cover is bounded above by both van and amw for suitable nonnegative v,w∈V and integers m,n≥0. The corresponding right and left coset bounds give the displayed cofinality, without first assuming that V is normal. Left-coset order is obtained by inversion of right-coset order.

Normal-valued lattice-ordered group

Words: 143 Articles: 1
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A lattice-ordered group is normal-valued when every value in a lattice-ordered group is a normal subgroup of its cover. Normality in the cover is weaker than normality in the entire group. Every linearly ordered group is normal-valued.

Wolfenstein inequality for normal-valued lattice-ordered groups (∣f∣∣g∣≤∣g∣2∣f∣2)

Words: 118
Normal-valued lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A lattice-ordered group is normal-valued precisely when the displayed inequality holds for all its elements. The theorem is proved without using it as a definition. In the forward direction a normal value has a linearly ordered quotient of its cover with no proper nontrivial convex subgroup. That quotient is Archimedean, hence Abelian by Archimedean linearly ordered groups are Abelian. Apply squared bound from comparison at values to p=b,q=a,x=ab. For the reverse direction, power cofinality in the cover of a value excludes a conjugate u=a−1va outside the value: its right cosets Vun are all bounded by Va, contradicting cofinality above Va2. Left-coset cofinality treats ava−1. Every element is a quotient of nonnegative lattice parts, proving normality in the cover. In a linearly ordered group, comparison of a,b≥1 immediately gives ab≤max(a,b)2≤b2a2, so every such group is normal-valued.

Linearly ordered group

Words: 348 Articles: 5
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A group with a two-sided invariant total order. Thus it is a lattice-ordered group whose join and meet choose the larger and smaller element. An order on only one side is not sufficient here. Every linearly ordered group is torsion-free: a positive element has strictly increasing positive powers, and a negative element has a positive inverse.

Archimedean ordered group

Words: 137 Articles: 1
Linearly ordered group OurBigBook.com
An ordered group is Archimedean when a≥1 and an≤b for all positive integers force a=1. In an additive Abelian group this excludes a positive element whose every integer multiple stays below one fixed bound. This generalizes the Archimedean property of the real numbers.
Archimedean linearly ordered groups are Abelian
Words: 100
Archimedean ordered group OurBigBook.com
An Archimedean ordered group with a two-sided invariant total order is commutative. If there is a least positive element, its cofinal powers exhaust the group. Otherwise each c>1 has d>1 with d2<c. To see this, choose 1<s<r<c and use rs−1 if its square is below r; if not, the inequality (rs−1)2≥r gives s2≤r<c. If positive g,h satisfied gh<hg, put c=(gh)−1hg and choose such a d. Cofinality gives dm≤g<dm+1 and dn≤h<dn+1. Two-sided order invariance then gives both hg<dm+n+2 and hg=ghc>dm+n+2, a contradiction. Replacing negative elements by their inverses reduces arbitrary commutativity to the positive case.

Lexicographically ordered group

Words: 41
Linearly ordered group OurBigBook.com
For ordered Abelian groups, compare tuples at their first differing coordinate. This gives a two-sided invariant total order on their direct product. The additive group Z2 with its first coordinate dominant is not an Archimedean ordered group, since n(0,1)<(1,0) for every positive integer n.

Convex subgroups of a finite-rank ordered Abelian group (#{convex subgroups}≤r+1)

Words: 73
Linearly ordered group OurBigBook.com
For a torsion-free abelian group of finite rational rank r, each convex subgroup of an ordered group is pure: if ng∈C for a positive integer n, then g lies between zero and ng or their negatives and hence belongs to C. A proper inclusion of convex subgroups therefore increases rational rank. They form a chain, so there are at most r+1, including zero and the whole group. A lexicographically ordered copy of Zr attains the bound.

Ohnishi orderability criterion

Words: 47
Linearly ordered group OurBigBook.com
A group admits a two-sided invariant total order exactly when every finite list of nonidentity elements can be signed so that the conjugation-invariant semigroup generated by the signed elements excludes the identity. A compactness theorem argument supplies a compatible global sign assignment. Omitting the conjugates changes the criterion to a different orderability problem.

Abelian lattice-ordered group

Words: 421 Articles: 5
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A commutative lattice-ordered group, usually written additively. Examples include real-valued function groups with pointwise addition, maximum and minimum. Such a group need not have a total order.

Hahn group (V(Γ,R))

Words: 185 Articles: 1
Abelian lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
For a totally ordered set Γ, take real coefficient families whose support satisfies the maximum condition: every nonempty subset of the support has a largest index. Add coefficients pointwise and decide the sign from the largest nonzero coordinate. This defines an ordered Abelian group. For Γ=Z>0​ with its usual order, the maximum condition forces finite support. Some presentations reverse the index order and use a minimum instead; specify the convention when applying an embedding theorem.
Finite-support Hahn embedding along a countable convex chain (G≅⨁n≥1​R)
Words: 112
Hahn group OurBigBook.com
Let a divisible ordered Abelian group be the union of a strictly increasing sequence of convex subgroups C1​=0,C2​,…, with each ordered quotient Cn+1​/Cn​ isomorphic to the additive real line. Convexity makes every Cn​ divisible, so these are rational vector subspaces. Choose a rational-linear section for each quotient. Every element then has a unique finite sum of section values. Its sign is the sign of the coefficient of the largest active index, because convexity makes the corresponding ordered quotient decide its sign. The coefficients give an embedding, indeed an ordered-group isomorphism, into the Hahn group with positive integer indices in the maximum-support convention. No compatibility of a separate real scalar multiplication is needed for this group embedding.

Free Abelian lattice-ordered group

Words: 212 Articles: 2
Abelian lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
The free object on generators x1​,…,xn​ has the universal property that an arbitrary assignment of these generators to an Abelian lattice-ordered group extends uniquely to a lattice-ordered group homomorphism. It can be constructed from terms using addition, negation, zero, join and meet, modulo identities valid in every Abelian lattice-ordered group. Its underlying group need not be finitely generated even when there are only two lattice generators.
Piecewise integer-linear representation of a free Abelian lattice-ordered group (Fn​=⟨x1​,…,xn​⟩ℓ​⊆RRn)
Words: 154 Articles: 1
Free Abelian lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
Evaluate the generators as coordinate projections on Rn. The lattice-ordered group they generate consists of continuous positively homogeneous piecewise linear functions with finitely many integer-linear pieces. Equivalently its elements are finite joins of finite meets of integer linear forms. The universal property follows because the real line detects every identity of Abelian lattice-ordered groups: if an identity fails, pass to a prime ordered quotient and select the finitely many active linear pieces. The resulting rational linear inequalities have a real solution by the alternative underlying Farkas' lemma, giving a real witness to failure. This is a statement about integer coefficients and positive homogeneity, not arbitrary real coefficients or affine constant terms.
Integer-linear hinge functions have infinite independent rank (hn​(x,y)=max(0,x−ny))
Words: 55
Piecewise integer-linear representation of a free Abelian lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
The piecewise linear functions hn​, for distinct integer indices, are linearly independent over the integers. Restrict a finite integer relation to y=1. At x=n, only hn​ has a jump of one in its slope; hence the coefficient of that function must be zero. This proves that the underlying group of the two-generated free Abelian lattice-ordered group has infinite rank of an abelian group.

Lattice-ordered permutation group

Words: 524 Articles: 8
Lattice-ordered group OurBigBook.com
A group of order automorphisms of a totally ordered set that is closed under pointwise maximum and minimum. Products act on the right when writing αf; then α(fg)=(αf)g. The pointwise lattice operations make it a lattice-ordered group.

Order-primitive lattice permutation group

Words: 134 Articles: 1
Lattice-ordered permutation group OurBigBook.com
A transitive lattice-ordered permutation group is order-primitive when its only invariant equivalence relations with convex classes are equality and the universal relation. This concerns convex blocks in a chain, rather than arbitrary blocks of a primitive group action.
McCleary trichotomy theorem
Words: 101
Order-primitive lattice permutation group OurBigBook.com
A faithful transitive order-primitive lattice permutation group is either a regular Archimedean translation action, an order two-transitive action, or a periodic action. In the periodic case a fixed-point-free order automorphism of the Dedekind completion commutes with the action and has cofinal and coinitial iterates; the point stabilizers act order two-transitively within period intervals. Only the regular case satisfies ∣f∣∣g∣≤∣g∣2∣f∣2 for every pair. In either other case, order two-transitivity on the whole chain or on a period interval supplies positive a,b and x<y<z<t with xa=y, ya=z, xb=x, yb=t, giving xab=t>z=xb2a2. The classification itself is a theorem, not a consequence of that witness calculation.

Holland representation theorem

Words: 50
Lattice-ordered permutation group OurBigBook.com
Every lattice-ordered group embeds in a lattice-ordered permutation group on one totally ordered set. Choose a value in a lattice-ordered group for each nonidentity element, act on its ordered right cosets, and combine these chains in an order sum. The original element moves its own value's identity coset, making the action faithful. The coset lattice formulas make the embedding preserve both join and meet.

Order n-transitivity

Words: 125 Articles: 1
Lattice-ordered permutation group OurBigBook.com
An ordered permutation group is order n-transitive if it can send any increasing n-tuple to any other increasing n-tuple. This compares tuples with the same relative order, unlike unrestricted n-transitive group actions. In the usual transitive chain setting, order two-transitivity implies every finite order-transitivity for a lattice-ordered permutation group.
Finite interpolation by lattice operations (h=⋁i​⋀j​hij​)
Words: 80
Order n-transitivity OurBigBook.com
Suppose hij​ sends the ith and jth nodes of an increasing input tuple to the corresponding output nodes. At the ith input, every hij​ agrees, so their pointwise minimum takes the required value. At every other input it is bounded above by the required value, using the corresponding j term. The pointwise maximum over i therefore interpolates the entire tuple. The construction uses closure under finite join and meet, without assuming arbitrary restrictions of group elements belong to the group.

Positive bump of an order automorphism

Words: 182 Articles: 2
Lattice-ordered permutation group OurBigBook.com
A positive bump of the real line is an order automorphism whose nonempty support of a permutation is one open interval and which moves every point of that interval to the right. It fixes the complement. Iterating any interior point tends to the right endpoint in positive time and to the left endpoint in negative time.
Conjugacy of positive real-line bumps
Words: 132 Articles: 1
Positive bump of an order automorphism OurBigBook.com
Choose fundamental intervals [x,xf) and [y,yg) for two positive bumps of an order automorphism. Any increasing bijection matching their endpoints extends by b(xfn)=(xb)gn to their support intervals. Extend it monotonically over the two complementary rays. Then fb=bg, so b−1fb=g. Bounded supports impose no further invariant for positive bumps in the full real-line order-automorphism group.
Conjugating positive bumps while preserving a fundamental interval
Words: 81
Conjugacy of positive real-line bumps OurBigBook.com
If two positive bumps of an order automorphism move a point c beyond an interval [c,d], their conjugacy can be chosen to fix that interval: begin with the identity there, complete the increasing map between fundamental intervals, and extend equivariantly along bump iterates. If both bump supports lie in one fundamental interval of another positive motion h, fix that cell's endpoints and repeat the conjugator on all its h-translates. The result commutes with h, and with any automorphism supported in the interval fixed pointwise.
