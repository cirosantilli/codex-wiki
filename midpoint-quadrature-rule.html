Midpoint quadrature rule (M(f)=(b−a)f((a+b)/2))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 412 Articles: 1
The midpoint quadrature rule replaces the integrand on [a,b], a<b, by its value halfway across the interval:
I(f)=∫ab​f(x)dx≈M(f)=(b−a)f(2a+b​).
(43)
Geometrically, M(f) is the signed area of a rectangle with width b−a and height f((a+b)/2). Its single quadrature node is (a+b)/2 and its quadrature weight is b−a. Sampling in the interior is useful when endpoint evaluation is inconvenient, although an endpoint singularity can invalidate the quadrature error estimates below.
The midpoint quadrature rule is exact for every affine function f(x)=αx+β: the average height over an interval equals its midpoint height. For example, on [0,2], f(x)=2x+1 gives I(f)=6 and M(f)=2f(1)=6. Positive and negative area discrepancies cancel for a sloping straight line. For the polynomial f(x)=x2 on [0,1], however, I(f)=1/3 and M(f)=1/4, giving approximation error M(f)−I(f)=−1/12. Thus its degree of precision of a quadrature rule is exactly one.[ref]
For the polynomial f(x)=x2 on [0,1], four panels give composite midpoint quadrature rule value 21/64 and composite trapezoidal rule value 11/32, bracketing the exact integral 1/3. Individual midpoint rectangles can cross the curve; it is their total signed area that underestimates the integral.
For f∈C2[a,b], meaning that its first two derivatives exist and are continuous functions on the closed interval, the signed approximation error has the form
M(f)−I(f)=−24(b−a)3​f′′(ξM​),a<ξM​<b.
(44)
Hence a convex function with f′′≥0 is underestimated, and a concave function with f′′≤0 is overestimated. A short justification uses the Taylor formula with integral remainder: writing m=(a+b)/2, it gives I(f)−M(f)=∫ab​k(t)f′′(t)dt, where k(t)=(t−a)2/2 for a≤t≤m and k(t)=(b−t)2/2 for m≤t≤b. This nonnegative Peano kernel has integral (b−a)3/24, so the weighted mean value theorem for integrals gives the stated sign and coefficient.
