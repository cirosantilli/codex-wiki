Strict stability implies self-decomposability (Rc​=d(1−cα)1/αX′)

 ... Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Cauchy distribution Stable distribution Strictly stable distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 103
Let X have a strictly stable distribution of index α, and let X′ be an independent copy. For integers 1≤k<n, split a sum of n independent copies into blocks of sizes k and n−k. Strict stability of each block and of the full sum gives
(k/n)1/αX+(1−k/n)1/αX′=dX.
(37)
For any 0<c<1, choose rationals km​/nm​→cα. On the fixed probability space carrying X,X′, the left side converges almost surely to cX+(1−cα)1/αX′. Thus it converges in distribution; since its probability distribution is constant, the limit also has the law of X. This proves a self-decomposable distribution with the displayed remainder. The proof does not require any finite moments.
