Prime-power contraction in a formal group ([pn](x)⟶0(x∈m))

 ... Algebraic geometry Normalization of an algebraic curve Geometric genus Genus one curve Elliptic curve Formal group law
OurBigBook.com Words: 110
Let R be the valuation ring of a complete non-Archimedean valued field whose residue field has characteristic p, and let m={x:∣x∣<1}. For a one-dimensional formal group law over R, repeated addition gives [p](X)=pX+∑j≥2​cj​Xj with cj​∈R. Hence ∣[p](x)∣≤max(∣p∣∣x∣,∣x∣2). The series converges for x∈m because its coefficients are bounded and ∣x∣<1.
For x0​=x and xn+1​=[p](xn​), choose ρ=max(∣p∣,∣x∣)<1. The values remain in the ball ∣xn​∣≤∣x∣, so ∣xn​∣≤ρn∣x∣→0. Associativity identifies xn​=[pn](x). The argument also works in equal characteristic p, where the linear coefficient vanishes, and does not require a discrete valuation. It proves contraction of prime-power multiplication near the formal identity without assuming a characteristic-zero formal logarithm.
