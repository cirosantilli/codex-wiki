Scaling property of the Mellin transform (M{f(a⋅)}(s)=a−sMf(s))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Integral transform Mellin transform
OurBigBook.com Words: 146
For a>0, let g(x)=f(ax). Whenever the Mellin transform of f is absolutely convergent,
Mg(s)=a−sMf(s),a−s=exp(−sloga).
(10)
The Mellin fundamental strip is unchanged: the corresponding integrals of absolute values differ by the finite positive factor a−σ. The change of variables y=ax gives xs−1dx=a−sys−1dy, proving the rule. For s=σ+iτ, the transform's absolute value changes by a−σ, while the factor e−iτloga changes its phase. On the line σ=0, wherever absolute convergence holds, the transform's absolute value is preserved.
For the Mellin transform of an interval indicator, replacing 1[1,4]​(x) by 1[1,4]​(2x)=1[1/2,2]​(x) gives
s2s−(1/2)s​=2−ss4s−1​.
(11)
At the removable singularity s=0, both sides have value log4. Scaling leaves the width measured by the natural logarithm unchanged. Likewise, the exponential function e−ax has Mellin transform a−sΓ(s) on Res>0.
