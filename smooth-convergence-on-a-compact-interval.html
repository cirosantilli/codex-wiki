Smooth convergence on a compact interval (pk​(fn​−f)→0 for every k)

 ... Analysis Functional analysis Topological vector space Locally convex space Fréchet space Derivative seminorms on a compact interval
OurBigBook.com Words: 168
Smooth convergence on a compact interval requires uniform convergence of the functions and of their derivatives of every order separately, as measured by the derivative seminorms. The need for several scales is visible in
fn​(t)=nsin(nt)​,p0​(fn​)≤n1​⟶0,fn′​(t)=cos(nt),p1​(fn​)=1.
(12)
For n≥2, p0​(fn​)=1/n because t=π/(2n) lies in [0,1]. The derivative has supremum norm one for every n, already at t=0. Thus fn​ converges uniformly to zero, but fails the test for smooth convergence to zero. Andreas Buttenschoen, Applied Functional Analysis, spaces of continuous and differentiable functions discusses this example and the family of smoothness measurements.
More generally, for any fixed K≥0, hn​(t)=sin(nt)/nK+1 has pK​(hn​)≤1/n but pK+1​(hn​)=1 for n≥2. If a norm N induced the smooth topology, its continuity would give N(f)≤CpK​(f) for some finite K, while continuity of pK+1​ for N would give pK+1​(f)≤C′N(f). Substituting hn​ contradicts these two bounds. Smooth convergence therefore cannot be reduced to one norm. Its beauty is that every finite order can be controlled without fixing a final order in advance.
