Protein tertiary structure

 Home Biology Protein Protein structure
OurBigBook.com Words: 164 Articles: 2
The protein tertiary structure is the overall spatial arrangement of one polypeptide chain. It includes how its protein secondary structures and intervening regions fit together. For example, the schematic under protein places positions 4 and 9 near each other despite their separation in protein primary structure. Such cooperation can help construct an active site; sequence distance and spatial separation answer different questions. Hydrophobicity, backbone hydrogen bonds and other interactions help stabilize folds, and some proteins additionally contain protein disulfide bonds. See Cooper: tertiary structure and interactions between chain regions.
