OurBigBook.com Words: 216
Let u be the boost velocity, and decompose the fields into components parallel and perpendicular to it. The parallel components are unchanged. The perpendicular components obey
E⊥′​=γ(E⊥​+u×B⊥​),B⊥′​=γ(B⊥​−u×E⊥​/c2),γ=(1−u2/c2)−1/2.
(32)
All vectors inside these expressions are perpendicular to u. Thus ∣u×B⊥​∣2=u2B⊥2​, and similarly for E⊥​. In the difference of squares, the mixed terms cancel because
E⊥​⋅(u×B⊥​)=−B⊥​⋅(u×E⊥​).
(33)
It follows that
E⊥′2​−c2B⊥′2​​=γ2[(1−u2/c2)E⊥2​−(c2−u2)B⊥2​]=E⊥2​−c2B⊥2​.​
(34)
Adding the unchanged parallel components proves
E′2−c2B′2=E2−c2B2.​
(35)
For the mixed dot product, expand directly. The terms E⊥​⋅(u×E⊥​) and (u×B⊥​)⋅B⊥​ vanish. The remaining cross-product identity is
(u×B⊥​)⋅(u×E⊥​)=u2E⊥​⋅B⊥​.
(36)
Hence
E⊥′​⋅B⊥′​=γ2(1−u2/c2)E⊥​⋅B⊥​=E⊥​⋅B⊥​.
(37)
Parallel-perpendicular mixed dot products are zero in both frames, so
E′⋅B′=E⋅B.​
(38)
Rotations also preserve these two expressions, completing the proof for proper Lorentz transformations. The second expression is a pseudoscalar under spatial parity, but is invariant under the boosts at issue here.
These results also follow from tensor contractions. In the convention used below, Fμν​Fμν=2(B2−E2/c2) and Fμν​Fμν=−4E⋅B/c. The numerical sign of the latter depends on the chosen orientation of the dual electromagnetic field tensor, while its invariance under proper Lorentz transformations does not.
