The consistent Liénard–Wiechert potential obeys A=uφ/c2. To first order in β=u/c, its large-distance form is
φ≃4πϵ0​rq​[1+n⋅β(tr​)],A≃4πrμ0​q​u(tr​),tr​≃t−cr​.
(10)
Terms varying as 1/r2 give near fields and are negligible for the radiated power at large r. Keep the first-order velocity term in φ: although small, its retarded-time derivative contributes a leading acceleration field. To the required order, ∂tr​/∂t≃1 and ∇tr​≃−n/c. Holding the observation direction fixed at leading order gives
−∇φ​1/r​=4πϵ0​crq​n(n⋅β˙​),−∂t∂A​​1/r​=−4πϵ0​crq​β˙​.
(11)
Thus the outgoing electric field is transverse:
Erad​(r,t)=4πϵ0​crq​[n×(n×β˙​)]tr​​=4πϵ0​c2rq​[n×(n×a)]tr​​.​
(12)
Here a=du/dtr​. The magnetic field follows from ∇×A, or from the outgoing plane electromagnetic wave relation:
Brad​=−4πrμ0​q​[n×β˙​]tr​​=c1​n×Erad​.​
(13)
The minus sign is essential. Both fields fall as 1/r, are mutually orthogonal and obey ∣E∣=c∣B∣. The corresponding Liénard-Wiechert field also contains nonradiative terms, omitted in this leading far-field calculation.
