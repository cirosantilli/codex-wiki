Take u>0 to mean that the mirror recedes along the incident plane electromagnetic wave. Put β=u/c, with ∣β∣<1. The moving-mirror Doppler shift can be obtained directly by matching wave phase on the moving surface. Write the incident wave phase as ki​z−ωi​t and the reflected wave phase as −kr​z−ωr​t, where ki​=ωi​/c and kr​=ωr​/c are positive wavenumbers. At the mirror, z=z0​+ut, the two phases must have the same time dependence; their constant offset incorporates the reflection phase shift. Thus
ki​u−ωi​=−kr​u−ωr​,ωi​(1−β)=ωr​(1+β).
(1)
Consequently the observed reflected angular frequency and ordinary frequency are
ωr​=ωi​1+u/c1−u/c​,fr​=fi​1+u/c1−u/c​.​
(2)
This is a redshift for a receding mirror and a blueshift for an approaching mirror. At small velocity, ωr​≃ωi​(1−2u/c).
Equivalently, the relativistic Doppler effect gives ωi′​=γ(1−β)ωi​ in the mirror rest inertial frame. Reflection there reverses propagation and preserves frequency. Transforming the backward-going reflected wave to the laboratory gives another factor γ(1−β), so ωr​=γ2(1−β)2ωi​=ωi​(1−β)/(1+β). A single Doppler factor would miss the effect of the return path.
