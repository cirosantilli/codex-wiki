Let uθ​=(cosθ,sinθ)T be the transmission direction. An ideal linear polarizer projects the incident electric field onto this direction, so its Jones matrix is
P(θ)=uθ​uθT​=(cos2θcosθsinθ​cosθsinθsin2θ​)​.
(1)
Since uβT​uα​=cos(α−β), the amplitude transmitted by a sequence of polarizers is the product of the projections between successive axes. Here the four directions are 0,ωt,−2ωt,0. After the first polarizer the unpolarized light input has intensity I/2 and is in linear polarization along x. The transmission through a sequence of linear polarizers therefore follows from successive applications of Malus's law
Iout​(t)=2I​cos2(ωt)cos2(3ωt)cos2(2ωt)​.
(2)
The relative angle between the rotating polarizers is 3ωt, not ωt. The first factor 1/2 is an ensemble average over incident polarization: unpolarized light cannot be represented by a single deterministic Jones vector. Equivalently, its coherency matrix is (I/2)I2​; the total matrix P(0)P(−2ωt)P(ωt)P(0) gives the same output intensity by the trace rule. The quasistatic optical-element approximation assumes the mechanical rotation is slow compared with the optical oscillation.
