General pure state. Use time dependence ei(kz−ωt). The most general nonzero Jones vector can be written
J=Aeiχ(cosαeiδsinα​),A>0,0≤α≤2π​,0≤δ<2π.
(17)
There are four real parameters in the full complex field. Removing overall intensity A2 and the unobservable common phase χ leaves two real degrees of freedom for pure polarization, α and δ. Their endpoint degeneracies simply reflect the fact that the phase of a vanishing component is irrelevant. A single Jones vector cannot represent partially polarized light.
Three linear polarizers. Define the transmission axis aθ​=(cosθ,sinθ)T. An ideal linear polarizer has Jones matrix Pθ​=aθ​aθT​. For definiteness label (1,−i)T/2​ as right-circular in the chosen convention; reversing the handedness convention changes no intensity below. The first polarizer produces (1,0)T/2​. The intermediate polarizer and final y analyzer then give
Jout​=Pπ/2​Pθ​P0​JR​=2​sinθcosθ​(01​),
(18)
so Malus's law gives
Iin​Iout​​=81​sin2(2θ).​
(19)
The maximum is 1/8 at θ=π/4+mπ/2, and the transmission vanishes at θ=mπ/2. The first polarizer accounts for an initial factor 1/2; retaining that factor is essential when quoting transmission relative to the incident circularly polarized beam.
Waveplate and quarter-wave matrix. A lossless waveplate has linear birefringence: its orthogonal fast and slow axes have indices nf​<ns​. A thickness L produces slow-minus-fast phase retardation
δ=λ0​2πL​(ns​−nf​).
(20)
Resolving the incident Jones vector into the plate axes, advancing the two phases and transforming back gives, up to a common phase,
Jδ​(θ)=R(−θ)(10​0eiδ​)R(θ).
(21)
A quarter-wave plate has δ=π/2 modulo 2π. With cθ​=cosθ and sθ​=sinθ, its Jones matrix is therefore
Q(θ)=(cθ2​+isθ2​(1−i)cθ​sθ​​(1−i)cθ​sθ​sθ2​+icθ2​​).​
(22)
In particular Q(0)=diag(1,i). Equal incident components along the two axes acquire a relative quarter-cycle phase, converting 45∘ linear polarization to circular polarization; an incident component along either principal axis stays linearly polarized.
Half-wave replacement. A half-wave plate has δ=π and hence
H(θ)=(cos2θsin2θ​sin2θ−cos2θ​).
(23)
It converts the x-polarized output of the first polarizer to a linear state at angle 2θ. The final y analyzer gives
Iin​Iout​​=21​sin2(2θ).​
(24)
The maximum is 1/2, because the ideal half-wave plate loses no light after the first polarizer.
Quarter-wave replacement. The transmitted y amplitude is (1−i)cθ​sθ​/2​, giving
Iin​Iout​​=41​sin2(2θ).​
(25)

Preparing an arbitrary ellipse. Yes: two rotatable quarter-wave plates already suffice. Let the input left-circular state be JL​=(1,i)T/2​. Up to an overall phase, any target pure polarization can be parameterized by its polarization ellipse:
Jtarget​=R(−β)(cosηisinη​),−4π​≤η≤4π​.
(26)
Here β is the major-axis orientation, and η sets the signed minor-to-major semiaxis ratio tanη. First note directly from Q(θ) that
Q(θ)JL​ ∝ (cos(θ−π/4)sin(θ−π/4)​).
(27)
Choose the two fast-axis angles as
θ1​=β+η+4π​,θ2​=β.​
(28)
The first quarter-wave plate produces linear light at β+η. In the axes of the second, its components are (cosη,sinη); the second gives precisely Jtarget​. This proves pure-polarization preparation with two quarter-wave plates, including the linear and circular limiting cases. A half-wave plate can also rotate the axes, but is not required. The phrase about left-handed input is understood as left-circular light in this construction; the explicit vector fixes the convention.
Switchable retardation. The electro-optic Kerr effect induces linear birefringence quadratic in a static transverse electric field. Defining the coefficient K by Δn=λ0​KEdc2​, its phase retardation is
δK​=λ0​2πLΔn​=2πKLEdc2​.
(29)
The two eigenpolarizations are linear, parallel and perpendicular to the applied field. Thus an appropriate field produces a switchable quarter-wave plate:
Edc2​=4∣K∣L1​for the smallest ∣δK​∣=π/2.​
(30)
The sign of K determines which axis is fast. Switching the field off removes the induced retardation, assuming no residual birefringence.
The Faraday effect instead gives circular birefringence for a longitudinal magnetic field. The two circular eigenmodes acquire different phases, rotating a linear polarization through
θF​=VBL=λ0​πL​(n+​−n−​),
(31)
with sign set by the handedness convention and the Verdet constant V. Its Jones matrix in the linear basis is a real rotation, up to a common phase. It preserves the ellipticity of a pure state, so a linear input remains linear. A π/2 phase difference between circular eigenmodes rotates a linear state by π/4; it does not make it circular. The Kerr device supplies the switchable quarter-wave plate; a Faraday rotator alone does not.
