Gauge freedom. The magnetic vector potential satisfies B=∇×A. The transformation
A↦A+∇χ​
(32)
leaves B unchanged because the curl of a gradient is zero. For time-dependent fields, transform the electric scalar potential as ϕ↦ϕ−∂t​χ as well, so that E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A is unchanged. The scalar χ must have the regularity and single-valuedness appropriate to the physical region and its boundary conditions. Local values of A therefore depend on the gauge transformation.
Gauge and nonzero component. In the standard axisymmetric Coulomb gauge representative for the long-solenoid approximation,
∇⋅A=0,Ar​=Az​=0,A=Aϕ​(r)ϕ^​.​
(33)
The divergence vanishes since Aϕ​ is independent of the azimuth. Inside, this agrees with the usual symmetric gauge representative A=21​B×r. The Coulomb gauge condition alone does not force the other two components to vanish; the symmetry, regularity and customary boundary choice select this representative from the remaining gauge freedom.
Radial dependence. Write the interior magnetic field as B0​z^, where B0​=μ0​nI for a vacuum solenoid of solenoid turn density n. Its exterior magnetic field vanishes in the infinitely long idealization. Applying Stokes theorem to a coaxial circle gives 2πrAϕ​(r)=Φ(r). Therefore the Coulomb-gauge vector potential of a long solenoid is
Aϕ​(r)={B0​r/2,B0​R2/(2r),​r<R,r>R.​​
(34)
It grows linearly inside, is continuous at R, and decays as r−1 outside. Although its exterior curl is zero, its circulation is the enclosed magnetic flux Φ=B0​πR2. No regular single-valued gauge function removes it throughout the exterior while preserving this circulation.
Electric field of the rotating charged surface. For clarity retain absolute permittivity ϵ and permeability μ in the cylinder formulae; setting them to unity gives the rationalized-unit convention in the question. If the stated unity means relative material constants in SI, use ϵ0​,μ0​ instead. Assume prescribed charge densities in the laboratory frame and the nonrelativistic, steady-rotation model.
The axisymmetric surface charge is stationary as a distribution even while the material rotates. A Gaussian cylinder of length L has enclosed charge zero for r<R and 2πRLσ for r>R. Gauss's law therefore gives
E(r)=⎩⎨⎧​0,ϵrσR​r^,​r<R,r>R.​​
(35)
The radial electric field jumps from zero to σ/ϵ at the surface and then decays as r−1. Its direction reverses for negative σ. The angular velocity does not enter this electric-field result for a specified surface charge.
Magnetic field of the volume-charged cylinder. A shell of radius a and thickness da has surface charge ρda, azimuthal surface current dKϕ​=ρωada, and the field of an infinite solenoid: dBz​=μρωada inside that shell and zero outside it. At radius r<R, only shells with a>r contribute. Thus the magnetic field of a rotating charged solid cylinder is
B(r)=μρω∫rR​adaz^=2μρω​(R2−r2)z^,r<R.​
(36)
With no applied background magnetic field, B=0 outside. The profile is parabolic, largest on the axis and zero at the boundary. The sign follows ρω. As a check, the current density is Jϕ​=ρωr, and Ampère's circuital law gives −dBz​/dr=μJϕ​, as required.
Electron impulse when the current is switched off. Choose positive I so the initial magnetic flux is Φi​=μ0​nIπR2 along +z. Faraday's law of induction gives, outside the solenoid,
Eϕ​(d,t)=−2πd1​dtdΦ​.
(37)
For an electron of electric charge q=−e, treat its position as fixed while the short pulse acts. The inductive impulse outside a switched solenoid is
Δp=−2πdq​(Φf​−Φi​)ϕ^​=−2πdeΦi​​ϕ^​.
(38)
Consequently the nonrelativistic final velocity is
vf​=−2me​deμ0​nIR2​ϕ^​.​
(39)
The induced electric field circulates in the positive azimuthal direction while a positive flux decreases, so the electron accelerates in the negative direction. Once the pulse has passed, there is no exterior field and it travels along the tangent at the impulse point, rather than continuing in a circular orbit. If the resulting momentum is relativistic, use vf​=cpf​/me2​c2+∣pf​∣2​ instead. The sudden-switch result assumes negligible displacement during the pulse and the usual quasistatic electromagnetic induction approximation; “instantaneous” switching does not mean instantaneous propagation of fields to arbitrary distance.
One can check the sign using conserved canonical angular momentum: axial symmetry conserves pϕcan​=Lz​+qrAϕ​. Initially Lz​=0, and finally Aϕ​=0, so Lz​=qΦi​/(2π)=−eΦi​/(2π).
Interferometric measurement at constant current. Use a coherent electron beam split into two paths passing on opposite sides of a shielded long solenoid, then recombine them to form interference fringes. Keep the current constant during the passage and suppress leakage fields on the paths. In the Aharonov-Bohm effect, the phase difference due to the magnetic vector potential is the closed-loop integral
Δφ=ℏq​∮A⋅dl=ℏqΦ​=−ℏeμ0​nπR2​I(mod2π).​
(40)
The overall sign reverses if the path orientation is reversed. The displacement in fringe periods is Δφ/(2π), so the measured fringe shift is linear in I and repeats when
ΔI=eμ0​nπR2h​.​
(41)
A gauge transformation adds a gradient whose closed-loop integral vanishes for a single-valued χ. Endpoint changes in electron wavefunction phase cancel in the interference measurement. The measured Aharonov-Bohm phase is therefore gauge invariant: it determines the flux, or equivalently the circulation of A. In the chosen Coulomb gauge this implies Aϕ​(d)=μ0​nIR2/(2d), but the experiment does not select or measure a unique local vector potential independently of gauge.
Far field of the rotating sphere. Interpret uniform charge as uniform volume charge of total Q, and take ω=ωz^. The magnetic dipole moment of a rigidly rotating charge distribution is
m=21​∫r×Jd3r,J=ρω×r.
(42)
Its axial component is mz​=(ρω/2)∫r⊥2​d3r. For a solid sphere, ∫r⊥2​d3r=8πR5/15 and ρ=3Q/(4πR3), so
m=5QR2​ω.​
(43)
The magnetic field of a rotating charged spherical body is a static magnetic dipole field at large distances:
B(r)=4πr3μ0​​[3(m⋅r^)r^−m],r≫R.​
(44)
In particular, ∣B∣=μ0​∣m∣1+3cos2ϑ​/(4πr3). Both the angular mean of the magnitude and its root mean square therefore scale as r−3​; the latter is Brms​=2​μ0​∣m∣/(4πr3). The angular average of the vector is zero, which is different from average strength. Steady rotation leaves the charge and current distributions stationary, so this is magnetostatics, not a radiative r−1 field. If uniform charge instead means a thin spherical surface, replace the dipole moment by QR2ω/3; the angular pattern and r−3 scaling are unchanged.
