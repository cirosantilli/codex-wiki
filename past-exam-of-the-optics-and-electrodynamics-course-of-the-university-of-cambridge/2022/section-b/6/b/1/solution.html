Write ay​=EA,y0​, az​=EA,z0​, by​=EB,y0​ and bz​=EB,z0​, evaluated at the same transverse coordinates (y,z). A superscript 0 labels each filament's own rest frame, rather than a single common frame in all cases. The filaments are infinite and uniform along x, so the change of longitudinal coordinate under a Lorentz boost creates no extra dependence in these functions. After the acceleration transients have passed, the magnetic field of each filament vanishes in its own rest frame.
For a filament moving at +vx, use the inverse of part (a): its transverse electric field in the laboratory is γE0 and its magnetic field is v×E/c2. Superposition for the two co-moving filaments therefore gives, in O,
Ex​Ey​Ez​​=0,=γ(ay​+by​),=γ(az​+bz​),​Bx​By​Bz​​=0,=−c2γv​(az​+bz​),=c2γv​(ay​+by​).​​
(45)
In the common co-moving frame O′ both filaments are at rest, so
Ex′​Ey′​Ez′​​=0,=ay​+by​,=az​+bz​,​Bx′​By′​Bz′​​=0,=0,=0.​​
(46)
For example, By′​=γ[−γv(az​+bz​)/c2+vγ(az​+bz​)/c2]=0, a useful sign check. The supplied η is the proper linear charge density. Thus each laboratory density is γη and each laboratory electric current is γηv, consistently with length contraction and the four-current transformation. In O′ each density is η and both currents vanish.
