The information in the original question is insufficient to determine a unique spin-induced change of charge density. It specifies ideal dielectric filaments with a prescribed excess linear charge density, but not the charge carrier species, their spin state, charge carrier mobility, binding potential or relaxation mechanism. A classical line charge has no spin variable at all. The following conditional conclusions are supported by the fields already calculated.
For a carrier moving with its filament, the local magnetic field in its instantaneous rest frame is, for the transverse fields here,
Brest,⊥​=γu​(B−c2u×E​),
(56)
where u is that carrier's laboratory velocity. Exclude its filament's singular self-field and first retain only the prescribed straight convective motion.
In Case 1 there is a common rest frame in which both filaments and the excess charges are stationary and the interfilament magnetic field vanishes. Equivalently, in the laboratory BA​=v×EA​/c2, so B's co-moving charges have BA,rest​=0. Thus uniform common translation alone creates no extra mutual rest-field spin splitting. This does not exclude intrinsic spin-orbit coupling of electrons moving within their atomic binding potentials; that material physics was never specified.
In Case 2 there is relative motion and no frame in which both source distributions are stationary. Put E∗​=η/(2πϵ0​d) as in part (c). At the respective filament axes, the external fields experienced in each carrier distribution's own rest frame are
BA→B(Brest)​=c2γvE∗​​y​,BB→A(Arest)​=c2γvE∗​​y​.​
(57)
For the first expression B is at rest in O; for the second A is at rest in O′ and the field comes from B moving at −vx. For a carrier with magnetic dipole moment μs​, the interaction Hs​=−μs​⋅Brest​ distinguishes opposite transverse spin orientations. A field gradient could exert a spin-dependent force ∇(μs​⋅Brest​) on mobile carriers; spin relaxation could instead produce a spin polarization. These are possible responses, not consequences of charge density alone. In B's frame the displayed interaction is directly a magnetic-moment coupling to the moving source's field; treating the moving carrier's electrostatic orbital term in another frame also requires consistent binding and acceleration dynamics, including any applicable Thomas precession.
For example, if η>0 and mobile electron spins can relax, the lower magnetic-energy orientation has the electron's magnetic moment toward +y​ and its spin toward −y​. Oppositely oriented moments would experience opposite gradient forces. The sign changes if the source charge changes sign. None of these statements fixes a net charge redistribution without a confinement and response model, and the stipulated zero transverse size supplies no resolved “sides” on which charge could accumulate.
Therefore Case 1 has no new mutual spin effect from common bulk motion alone; Case 2 permits motion-induced spin-dependent interactions, but neither case determines an additional charge-density profile from the supplied data. In particular, the factor γ relating proper and laboratory linear charge densities in part (b) follows from length contraction and is not an effect of spin-orbit coupling. If the charges remain pinned in an ideal dielectric, their imposed density remains the prescribed density in either case.
