An accelerated charge radiates. For ultrarelativistic circular motion, the emitted radiation is concentrated into a cone of angular width of order γ−1 about the instantaneous velocity. As the cone sweeps past a distant observer, propagation-time compression produces a short pulse of width of order ρ/(γ3c), where ρ is orbit curvature radius. Its Fourier spectrum extends to high harmonics, with conventional critical angular frequency
ωc​=2ρ3γ3c​.​
(9)
This is a spectral scale, not a sharp upper cutoff. For transverse acceleration and v≃c, the classical radiated power is
P=6πϵ0​c3q2γ4a2​≃6πϵ0​ρ2q2cγ4​.
(10)
The radiation is linearly polarized for an observer in the orbit plane, with elliptical polarization away from it. The two sides of the orbit plane have opposite handedness. The strong forward preference and high-frequency spectrum follow from the relativistic radiation calculation.[ref]
Magnetic bending fields steer the charge but do no work, so maintaining a storage-ring beam requires energy replacement by accelerating fields. Bright electron beams provide useful radiation from infrared to X-rays; stronger curvature or higher electron energy shifts the spectral scale. The classical formulas assume negligible quantum recoil.
