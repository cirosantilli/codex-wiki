Let R run from the charge's present position to the observation point, and let β=v/c be constant. Lorentz-transforming the Coulomb field gives

At fixed R, the radial magnitude is proportional to (1−β2)/(1−β2sin2θ)3/2. It is reduced by γ−2 along the motion and enhanced by γ perpendicular to it. The field is therefore concentrated around the transverse plane at high speed. For negative charge all electric-field arrows reverse.
Although the fields depend causally on retarded motion, for constant velocity the electric-field direction points along the separation from the present position. This does not imply instantaneous action: the velocity-dependent part of the retarded field removes a naive directional lag. Both fields fall as R−2; uniform straight-line motion produces no acceleration-radiation term proportional to R−1.
