A Hertzian dipole is an ideal localized oscillating source much smaller than its radiation wavelength. Its electric version is an infinitesimal current element; its magnetic version is a small current loop with moment m=IAn. Such elements model the far-field radiation and radiation resistance of electrically small antennas. The approximation requires source dimensions much smaller than c/ω.
The supplied magnetic field has a quasistatic [m]/r3 term, an induction [m˙]/(cr2) term and a transverse radiation [m¨]/(c2r) term. The electric field has induction and radiation terms. Brackets mean evaluation at the retarded time tr​=t−r/c, not a time average. Near terms mainly exchange stored energy; only the r−1 fields give a nonvanishing outgoing power integral as r→∞.
For a fixed dipole axis the radiation fields satisfy Eϕ​=−cBθ​. The radial Poynting flux is therefore
Sr​=μ0​cBθ2​​=16π2c3r2μ0​[m¨]2​sin2θ.
(16)
Using ∫sin2θdΩ=8π/3 yields
P(t)=6πc3μ0​[m¨]2​=6πϵ0​c5[m¨]2​.​
(17)
For a general changing dipole direction, replace the squared scalar acceleration by ∣m¨∣2.
Radiation resistance is the equivalent input resistance whose mean power equals radiated power, not ohmic heating. For a fixed small loop carrying I(t)=I0​cosωt, m=AI, so
P=12πϵ0​c5A2I02​ω4​=21​I02​Rrad​,Rrad​(ω)=6πϵ0​c5A2ω4​.​
(18)
This uses peak current I0​; with rms current the power is Irms2​Rrad​.
The satellite loop needs its changing orientation as well as its induced current. Put the spin axis along z, the external field along x, and let n(t)=(cosωt,sinωt,0). With A=πb2 and resistance R=10−4Ω, the resistance-only approximation gives
Φ=BAcosωt,E=BAωsinωt,I(t)=RBAω​sinωt.
(19)
Write m0​=BA2ω/R. The full magnetic moment is
m(t)=m0​sinωtn(t)=2m0​​[sin(2ωt)x+(1−cos(2ωt))y​].
(20)
It has a static component and a rotating component at 2ω. Consequently ∣m¨∣2=4m02​ω4, and
Ploop​=3πϵ0​c5R22B2A4ω6​=3ϵ0​c5R22π3B2b8ω6​.​
(21)
Holding the dipole axis fixed while inserting the sinusoidal current would instead give B2A4ω6/(12πϵ0​c5R2), eight times smaller. That is the fixed-axis-loop result; it does not describe the stated rotating loop. This is frequency doubling of an induction-driven rotating loop; the changing vector direction is essential.
The resistance-only model is conditional: loop inductance is not supplied. If self-inductance L matters, solve LI˙+RI=BAωsinωt. In the steady state I0​=BAω/R2+ω2L2​. Its phase changes the static moment direction but not the twice-frequency component's magnitude, so the power becomes
Ploop​(L)=3πϵ0​c5(R2+ω2L2)2B2A4ω6​.​
(22)
The first expression is the requested resistive idealization and an upper estimate within this passive RL correction. Radiation reaction is negligible compared with the stipulated ohmic resistance. The radiated energy comes from rotational kinetic energy: the induced magnetic torque brakes the loop. Most lost rotational energy is Joule heating; a small part escapes as radiation. Maintaining a constant spin would require external mechanical power.
For scale only, take B=50μT, a representative near-Earth field rather than a supplied orbital measurement. The terrestrial surface range reported by NOAA is roughly 25–65μT; the actual orbital value depends on position.[ref] With b=1m and ω=2π(500s−1), the resistance-only model gives
P≃2.3×10−10W(50μTB​)2,frad​=1000Hz,λrad​≃300km.​
(23)
Inductance would reduce this power. The kilohertz signal is exceptionally weak and a metre-scale receiving antenna is electrically tiny. Ionospheric absorption/reflection and strong natural or instrumental low-frequency backgrounds make ordinary ground reception impractical. This is not a theorem that the ionosphere blocks every low-frequency mode: field-guided propagation and condition-dependent leakage are real, as the VIPER mission's measurements were designed to test.[ref]
At a separation r=100km, krad​r≃2.1, so the observer is not securely in the far field. The radiation term alone has at most the characteristic magnetic amplitude
Brad​∼4πc2rμ0​(2m0​ω2)​≃3.4×10−18T(50μTB​)
(24)
in this optimistic resistance-only vacuum model. Near and induction fields must be retained for a full prediction; a simple far-field inverse-square power estimate is not an exact received signal here. Another satellite avoids the whole ground ionospheric path, but plasma propagation, receiving sensitivity, bandwidth, orientation, integration time and spacecraft noise remain unspecified. Detection is not forbidden in principle, but routine practical detection is implausible at this scale; a definitive instrument-level yes/no does not follow from the supplied data.
