Use the inverse coordinate transformation,
r⊥​=r⊥′​,r∥​=γ(r∥′​+vt′),t=γ(t′+c2v⋅r′​).
(51)
Substitution into the wave phase k⋅r−ωt and comparison of the coefficients of r′ and t′ gives
ω′=γ(ω−v⋅k),k∥′​=γ(k∥​−c2ω​v),k⊥′​=k⊥​.​
(52)
The first is the relativistic Doppler effect; the changed direction of the wavevector describes relativistic aberration. Equivalently,
k′=k+[(γ−1)v2v⋅k​−γc2ω​]v.
(53)
The electric-field wave amplitude follows from part (b), after rotating the boost axis to v:
E0′​=E0,∥​+γ(E0,⊥​+v×B0​).​
(54)
For a vacuum plane wave, Maxwell equations give B0​=k×E0​/ω and k⋅E0​=0. Thus an expression using only the given electric wave amplitude and wavevector is
E0′​=E0,∥​+γ[E0,⊥​+ωv×(k×E0​)​].​
(55)
Equivalently, E0′​=γ(E0​+v×B0​)−[γ2/(γ+1)]v(v⋅E0​)/c2. The wave amplitude is not a scalar carried unchanged by the wave phase transformation; its direction must remain transverse to the transformed wavevector.
