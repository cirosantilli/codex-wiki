The magnetic vector potential is defined by B=∇×A. The gauge transformation
A↦A+∇χ,ϕ↦ϕ−∂t​χ​
(13)
leaves both B and E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A unchanged. In magnetostatics choose the Coulomb gauge ∇⋅A=0. Then Ampère's law and the curl-of-curl identity give
μ0​J=∇×(∇×A)=∇(∇⋅A)−∇2A,∇2A=−μ0​J.​
(14)
The free-space Green function identity ∇2∣r−r′∣−1=−4πδ(3)(r−r′) therefore gives
A(r)=4πμ0​​∫∣r−r′∣J(r′)​d3r′.​
(15)
The boundary condition selects the homogeneous solution; the usual decay condition assumes suitably localized sources. An infinitely long static wire needs a reference for its logarithmically divergent potential, although its magnetic field is finite away from the wire.
For time-dependent sources use the Lorenz gauge, ∇⋅A+c−2∂t​ϕ=0, and the outgoing retarded potential
A(r,t)=4πμ0​​∫∣r−r′∣J(r′,t−∣r−r′∣/c)​d3r′.​
(16)
The scalar potential has the analogous retarded charge-density integral. The two gauges should not be conflated for a general time-dependent source.[ref]
For the prescribed uncharged wire, ρ=0 and ϕ=0 is consistent. Translation invariance makes A=Az​(r,t)z^ with ∇⋅A=0, so this particular solution satisfies both gauge conditions. With s=z′−z,
Az​=4πμ0​I​∫−∞∞​r2+s2​Θ(t−r2+s2​/c)​ds.
(17)
It is zero for t<r/c. For t>r/c, set L=c2t2−r2​. The causal interval is −L<s<L, giving the switched infinite-wire retarded potential
Az​=2πμ0​I​arsinhrL​=2πμ0​I​arcoshrct​=2πμ0​I​ln(rct+c2t2−r2​​).​
(18)
Differentiation gives the fields and Poynting vector, for r>0, t>r/c:
EHN=E×H​=−2πc2t2−r2​μ0​Ic​z^,=2πrc2t2−r2​Ict​ϕ^​,=4π2r(c2t2−r2)μ0​I2c2t​r^.​​
(19)
Here B=μ0​H and Bϕ​=−∂r​Az​. All three fields vanish before arrival. For positive I, E opposes the imposed current and the radial energy flow after switching a line current points radially outward: (−z^)×ϕ^​=r^.
For the graph, let u=ct/r. Normalized to E∗​=μ0​Ic/(2πr), H∗​=I/(2πr) and N∗​=μ0​I2c/(4π2r2), the curves are
Ez​/E∗​=−u2−1​1​,Hϕ​/H∗​=u2−1​u​,Nr​/N∗​=u2−1u​(u>1).
(20)
At the wavefront, ∣Ez​∣ and Hϕ​ diverge as (u−1)−1/2 and Nr​ as (u−1)−1. This abrupt line-current wavefront singularity reflects the ideal instantaneous turn-on of a line current; a finite rise time smooths the front. At late times,
Ez​∼−2πtμ0​I​→0,Hϕ​→2πrI​,Nr​∼4π2rtμ0​I2​→0.
(21)
The vector potential grows logarithmically with time in this infinite-wire idealization even as the local magnetic field approaches its static limit. The divergent front should not be interpreted as a finite-energy pulse from a real instantaneous switching experiment.
