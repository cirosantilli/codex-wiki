cosθ=nvc​=nβ1​,γ=(1−n2cos2θ1​)−1/2.

The transverse spatial coherence width is much larger than the longitudinal coherence length. The longitudinal scale is controlled by spectral bandwidth:
ℓc​∼Δνc​∼Δλλ2​.
(3)
For broad, unfiltered visible sunlight it is of order a wavelength to a few wavelengths, roughly 10−6m. This is an order-of-magnitude scale, not a uniquely defined solar constant; the precise value depends on spectral response and the coherence-width convention.
The Sun subtends angular diameter δ≃1919′′≃9.3×10−3rad at one astronomical unit.[ref] Consequently the sunlight coherence comparison gives a transverse scale
wc​∼δλ​∼6×10−5m≫ℓc​,​
(4)
using λ=550nm only as an illustrative visible wavelength. The uniform-disk spatial coherence model has first coherence zero at 1.22λ/δ, about 72μm at that wavelength. The small angular size permits coherence across tens of micrometres, while the broad spectrum destroys coherence over much shorter path differences. Spectral filtering increases ℓc​; spatial filtering changes the transverse scale.[ref]

Birefringence is polarization-dependent propagation in an optically anisotropic medium. Its permittivity tensor relates D to E. In a lossless uniaxial dielectric, take the optic axis along z and the principal relative permittivities as (no2​,no2​,ne2​). For a wave vector making angle ϑ with that axis, the ordinary mode has index no​, while the extraordinary phase-index angular dependence is
nex​(ϑ)21​=no2​cos2ϑ​+ne2​sin2ϑ​.​
(9)
The ordinary electric field is perpendicular to the plane containing the wave vector and optic axis. The extraordinary electric displacement field lies in that plane and is perpendicular to the wave vector, but its electric field need not be. Its energy-flow direction can therefore differ from the wave-normal direction: birefringent walk-off. Along the optic axis the two indices coincide. Refraction generally splits an incident beam into two differently polarized rays, producing double images, as in calcite.
A waveplate instead uses the relative phase of two eigenpolarizations. With the axes chosen for the propagation direction, the retardance is Δ=2πL(n2​−n1​)/λ0​. A quarter-wave plate gives ∣Δ∣=π/2 modulo 2π and converts equal-amplitude orthogonal components between linear and circular polarization. A half-wave plate gives ∣Δ∣=π and rotates a linear polarization. This is a phase effect; unequal absorption is a separate property.[ref]

Synchrotron radiation is radiation from relativistic charges undergoing transverse acceleration, for example electrons bent by a magnetic field. Relativistic aberration concentrates the radiation within an angle of order 1/γ about the instantaneous velocity. An observer sees short pulses as this cone sweeps past; synchrotron pulse compression produces a broad spectrum extending far above the orbital frequency.
For an ultrarelativistic circular trajectory of curvature radius ρ, the characteristic scales are
ωc​≃2ρ3γ3c​,Δtpulse​∼γ3cρ​.​
(10)
[ref] The synchrotron critical frequency sets the spectral turnover; it is not a sharp upper cutoff. The transverse-acceleration Lienard formula gives
P=6πϵ0​c3ρ2q2γ4v4​≃6πϵ0​ρ2q2cγ4​.
(11)
The strong energy dependence is a major loss mechanism in electron storage rings. Radiation observed in the orbital plane is linearly polarized in that plane; away from it the two components generally produce elliptical polarization, with opposite handedness on opposite sides. Accelerator light sources exploit its brightness, broad tunable spectrum and pulsed timing. At low speeds the relativistic beaming and high-frequency extension disappear, approaching ordinary cyclotron radiation.[ref]

A photonic crystal has a spatially periodic dielectric response on the scale of the optical wavelength. For a nonmagnetic, lossless, nondispersive dielectric, the source-free Maxwell equations can be written as the eigenproblem
∇×[ϵr​(r)−1∇×H]=c2ω2​H.
(12)
The Bloch theorem gives Hnk​=eik⋅runk​(r) with periodic u. The allowed frequencies form photonic band structures. Coupling of counterpropagating waves near a Brillouin zone boundary opens a stop band. A one-dimensional dielectric multilayer is a simple example; two-dimensional hole arrays and three-dimensional dielectric networks allow more extensive control.
A complete photonic band gap excludes bulk propagating modes for every direction and polarization in a frequency interval. Periodicity alone does not guarantee such a gap: dimensionality, symmetry and refractive-index contrast matter. A missing or altered element can support a localized defect state in the gap, making a cavity; a line defect can guide light. Finite crystals have leakage and wavelength-dependent transmission rather than an infinitely sharp exclusion. Applications include selective mirrors, optical filters, small resonators and integrated waveguides.[ref]

The magnetic vector potential is defined by B=∇×A. The gauge transformation
A↦A+∇χ,ϕ↦ϕ−∂t​χ​
(13)
leaves both B and E=−∇ϕ−∂t​A unchanged. In magnetostatics choose the Coulomb gauge ∇⋅A=0. Then Ampère's law and the curl-of-curl identity give
μ0​J=∇×(∇×A)=∇(∇⋅A)−∇2A,∇2A=−μ0​J.​
(14)
The free-space Green function identity ∇2∣r−r′∣−1=−4πδ(3)(r−r′) therefore gives
A(r)=4πμ0​​∫∣r−r′∣J(r′)​d3r′.​
(15)
The boundary condition selects the homogeneous solution; the usual decay condition assumes suitably localized sources. An infinitely long static wire needs a reference for its logarithmically divergent potential, although its magnetic field is finite away from the wire.
For time-dependent sources use the Lorenz gauge, ∇⋅A+c−2∂t​ϕ=0, and the outgoing retarded potential
A(r,t)=4πμ0​​∫∣r−r′∣J(r′,t−∣r−r′∣/c)​d3r′.​
(16)
The scalar potential has the analogous retarded charge-density integral. The two gauges should not be conflated for a general time-dependent source.[ref]
For the prescribed uncharged wire, ρ=0 and ϕ=0 is consistent. Translation invariance makes A=Az​(r,t)z^ with ∇⋅A=0, so this particular solution satisfies both gauge conditions. With s=z′−z,
Az​=4πμ0​I​∫−∞∞​r2+s2​Θ(t−r2+s2​/c)​ds.
(17)
It is zero for t<r/c. For t>r/c, set L=c2t2−r2​. The causal interval is −L<s<L, giving the switched infinite-wire retarded potential
Az​=2πμ0​I​arsinhrL​=2πμ0​I​arcoshrct​=2πμ0​I​ln(rct+c2t2−r2​​).​
(18)
Differentiation gives the fields and Poynting vector, for r>0, t>r/c:
EHN=E×H​=−2πc2t2−r2​μ0​Ic​z^,=2πrc2t2−r2​Ict​ϕ^​,=4π2r(c2t2−r2)μ0​I2c2t​r^.​​
(19)
Here B=μ0​H and Bϕ​=−∂r​Az​. All three fields vanish before arrival. For positive I, E opposes the imposed current and the radial energy flow after switching a line current points radially outward: (−z^)×ϕ^​=r^.
For the graph, let u=ct/r. Normalized to E∗​=μ0​Ic/(2πr), H∗​=I/(2πr) and N∗​=μ0​I2c/(4π2r2), the curves are
Ez​/E∗​=−u2−1​1​,Hϕ​/H∗​=u2−1​u​,Nr​/N∗​=u2−1u​(u>1).
(20)
At the wavefront, ∣Ez​∣ and Hϕ​ diverge as (u−1)−1/2 and Nr​ as (u−1)−1. This abrupt line-current wavefront singularity reflects the ideal instantaneous turn-on of a line current; a finite rise time smooths the front. At late times,
Ez​∼−2πtμ0​I​→0,Hϕ​→2πrI​,Nr​∼4π2rtμ0​I2​→0.
(21)
The vector potential grows logarithmically with time in this infinite-wire idealization even as the local magnetic field approaches its static limit. The divergent front should not be interpreted as a finite-energy pulse from a real instantaneous switching experiment.

Spherical-particle frequency scaling. For a dielectric sphere of radius a≪λ, the quasistatic electric polarizability is
αs​=4πϵ0​a3ϵr​+2ϵr​−1​,p(t)=Re(αs​E0​e−iωt).
(22)
The electric dipole radiation formula and I0​=ϵ0​c∣E0​∣2/2 give
⟨P⟩=12πϵ0​c3ω4∣αs​∣2∣E0​∣2​,σ=I0​⟨P⟩​=38π​k4a6​ϵr​+2ϵr​−1​​2.​
(23)
Thus Rayleigh scattering scales as ω4, or λ−4, when the polarizability varies slowly with frequency. Material resonances and absorption can change this simple scaling. For the incident z polarization, the dipole is along z: intensity is proportional to sin2χ, where χ is the angle from z. There is no radiation on the dipole axis and a maximum in the xy plane. In a direction n^ the scattered electric field is along z^−(z^⋅n^)n^, hence linearly polarized wherever nonzero.[ref]
Needle model used in parts (a)–(d). Let its axis be u=(ux​,uy​,uz​)=(sinθcosϕ,sinθsinϕ,cosθ). A very slender, high-permittivity needle has dominant longitudinal response, so use the rank-one needle polarizability α≃α∥​uuT. Its transverse response is neglected at leading order. For observation along y, define v=ux​x^+uz​z^, s=∣v∣2=1−uy2​ and
K=16π2ϵ02​R2k4∣α∥​∣2​.
(24)
For a unit incident Jones vector e, the common far-field relation is Es​∝v(u⋅e), and Is​=KI0​s∣u⋅e∣2. Here Is​ is irradiance at distance R; multiplying by R2 gives power per solid angle. No absolute value of α∥​ is given or needed for the requested relative intensities.
Diffuse random cloud, with the incident polarization of (a). The single-particle amplitudes through x and z analysers are proportional to ux​uz​ and uz2​. The isotropic orientation fourth moment gives the needle-scattering analyzer moments
⟨ux2​uz2​⟩=151​,⟨uz4​⟩=51​.
(25)
For example, ⟨uz4​⟩=21​∫−11​μ4dμ=1/5; symmetry and ux2​+uy2​+uz2​=1 give 2⟨ux2​uz2​⟩=1/3−1/5. Since the particles scatter mutually incoherently, intensities add. For N illuminated particles,
Ix​=15NKI0​​,Iz​=5NKI0​​,Ix​:Iz​=1:3.​
(26)
The cross correlation vanishes by reflection symmetry. The degree of linear polarization is (Iz​−Ix​)/(Iz​+Ix​)=1/2​: random anisotropic scatterers produce partially depolarized light, even though every individual needle produces a single linear polarization.
Continuous homogeneous dielectric. One must sum amplitudes coherently and include their spatial phases, rather than retain the preceding sum of intensities. Random orientations give ⟨uuT⟩=I/3, so the averaged response is isotropic and its induced polarization is along z. Its coherent radiation toward y, when allowed by the sample geometry, is therefore purely z polarized, with Ix​=0. The independent-needle prefactor is no longer a quantitative dense-medium model; local-field and collective effects determine the effective permittivity.
There is also coherent continuum phase matching: a uniform bulk driven by a plane wave has no arbitrary outgoing 90∘ plane-wave component. In an ideal infinite homogeneous medium there is no diffuse side scattering at all; propagation, refraction and reflection are determined by the medium and its interfaces. A finite sample's coherent side signal depends on its shape and boundaries. Thus the old 1:3 diffuse ratio is replaced by a coherent isotropic-medium response, not by a universal nonzero N2 side-scattering intensity.

Unpolarized incident light has equal, mutually incoherent y- and z-polarized intensities. Both components scatter into the same direction v, so their coherency matrices add to
Js​=2KI0​​(uy2​+uz2​)(ux2​ux​uz​​ux​uz​uz2​​).
(31)
This matrix has rank one when the signal is nonzero. The light is therefore fully linearly polarized along v, with

(32)
A fixed highly anisotropic needle can act as a polarizing scatterer even for unpolarized input. The equality of total intensities with (c) does not mean the incident states were identical.
