A1 OurBigBook.com
Take u>0 to mean that the mirror recedes along the incident plane electromagnetic wave. Put β=u/c, with ∣β∣<1. The moving-mirror Doppler shift can be obtained directly by matching wave phase on the moving surface. Write the incident wave phase as ki​z−ωi​t and the reflected wave phase as −kr​z−ωr​t, where ki​=ωi​/c and kr​=ωr​/c are positive wavenumbers. At the mirror, z=z0​+ut, the two phases must have the same time dependence; their constant offset incorporates the reflection phase shift. Thus
ki​u−ωi​=−kr​u−ωr​,ωi​(1−β)=ωr​(1+β).
(1)
Consequently the observed reflected angular frequency and ordinary frequency are
ωr​=ωi​1+u/c1−u/c​,fr​=fi​1+u/c1−u/c​.​
(2)
This is a redshift for a receding mirror and a blueshift for an approaching mirror. At small velocity, ωr​≃ωi​(1−2u/c).
Equivalently, the relativistic Doppler effect gives ωi′​=γ(1−β)ωi​ in the mirror rest inertial frame. Reflection there reverses propagation and preserves frequency. Transforming the backward-going reflected wave to the laboratory gives another factor γ(1−β), so ωr​=γ2(1−β)2ωi​=ωi​(1−β)/(1+β). A single Doppler factor would miss the effect of the return path.

A2 OurBigBook.com
Use a transparent reciprocal wave plate at its design wavelength. For the return calculation, assume a mirror at normal incidence with the same reflection coefficient for both transverse components; this is the usual ideal double-pass quarter-wave plate arrangement.
Retardation mechanism
Words: 86
Solution OurBigBook.com
Birefringence gives two orthogonal linear eigenpolarizations with different refractive indices, nf​ and ns​. They accumulate different wave phases through the same physical thickness d. Their relative phase retardation is
δ=cωd​(ns​−nf​)=λ0​2πd​(ns​−nf​),δ=±2π​(mod2π)​.
(3)
Here λ0​ is the vacuum wavelength. For the thinnest positive-retardance plate, d=λ0​/[4(ns​−nf​)]. The plate uses a difference in phase velocity, rather than differential wave absorption. Equal wave amplitudes along the two axes, obtained from input linear polarization at 45∘ to them, acquire phase quadrature and become circular polarization. Reversing that conversion gives linear polarization from the corresponding circular input. A different input angle generally gives elliptical polarization on one pass.
Returned polarization
Words: 193
Solution OurBigBook.com
Use the same fixed laboratory x,y axes for the incident and returning Jones vectors, with x along the fast axis. Remove a common phase factor so that the one-pass Jones matrix is J=diag(1,i). The reverse pass through this reciprocal plate accumulates the same relative phase retardation, not its inverse. The mirror has scalar reflection coefficient rm​ in these fixed axes. Therefore
ereturn​=J(rm​I)Jein​=rm​(10​0−1​)ein​.​
(4)
The effective retardance is π: the complete return acts as a half-wave plate. For initial linear polarization at angle α to the fast axis,
(cosαsinα​)⟼(cosα−sinα​),αreturn​=−α(modπ).​
(5)
In particular, the 45∘ input used for linear-to-circular conversion returns in linear polarization perpendicular to its original line: (1,1)/2​↦(1,−1)/2​. An input along either plate axis returns along that same axis.
The wording does not specify the initial polarization of waves. Thus “orthogonal linear return” requires the usual 45∘ linearly polarized input; it is not a universal answer for arbitrary polarized input. The Jones matrix above supplies the general answer. A non-normal metallic reflection may introduce different complex reflection coefficients for the two components and would require additional geometry and optical data. Descriptions of the intermediate circular polarization must also account for the reversed propagation direction when assigning handedness.

A3 OurBigBook.com
The transverse and longitudinal quantities characterize different correlations: spatial optical coherence across a wavefront and temporal optical coherence between delayed fields. Their numerical values require a convention for how much correlation counts as coherent.
Transverse coherence estimate
Words: 126
Solution OurBigBook.com
Convert the distances to SI units: r=1.5×1011m and D=1.4×109m. The angular solar diameter is
Θ≃rD​=9.33×10−3rad.
(6)
Two receivers separated by a transverse baseline b see a spread of relative wave phases of order 2πbΘ/λ. Averaging over incoherent source directions therefore reduces interferometric visibility when b reaches the transverse optical coherence length scale λ/Θ. At λ=500nm,
ℓ⊥​∼Dλr​=1.4×109(5.00×10−7)(1.5×1011)​m=5.36×10−5m≃54μm.​
(7)
If “coherence length” specifically means the first zero of uniform-disk stellar visibility, a uniformly bright circular solar disk gives g(1)(b)=2J1​(x)/x, with x=πbΘ/λ. The first zero then lies at b0​=1.21967λ/Θ≃65μm​. This more specific number assumes a uniform disk and a first-zero criterion; the characteristic estimate is approximately 50–60μm. Solar brightness variation across the disk changes the exact contrast curve.
Longitudinal coherence estimate
Words: 143
Solution OurBigBook.com
The coherence length from a finite spectral band is most safely estimated by converting its endpoints to frequency, because the quoted band is broad:
Δν=c(275nm1​−875nm1​)≃7.48×1014Hz.
(8)
The reciprocal-bandwidth optical coherence time and corresponding longitudinal optical coherence length are
τc​∼Δν1​≃1.34×10−15s,ℓ∥​∼cτc​=875−275(275)(875)​nm≃401nm=0.40μm.​
(9)
The given temperature is consistent with the spectral peak: Wien's displacement law gives λpeak​=2.898×10−3mK/5778K≃502nm. Using the common narrow-band estimate λpeak2​/Δλ, with Δλ=600nm, gives about 0.42μm. Its closeness here does not justify the narrow-band approximation for arbitrary broad spectra.
The 0.40μm result is a bandwidth-scale estimate. A flat spectral density per unit frequency would make it the first-zero optical coherence length; a truncated Planck law spectrum is not flat and would have a different correlation function. Neither the stated endpoints nor the word “coherence” specifies an exact visibility threshold. Propagation to Earth sets the angular size and transverse scale, but does not by itself narrow the solar optical spectrum or lengthen this longitudinal scale.

B4 OurBigBook.com
Use retarded time tr​ and reserve r for the large observation distance. The source velocity is nonrelativistic, ∣u∣/c≪1. There are two errors in the original PDF that must be corrected before the radiation zone calculation: the vector-potential prefactor contains an extraneous 1/r, and the displayed magnetic radiation field has the wrong sign for the stated outward unit vector n. The extra distance factor is dimensionally wrong; the magnetic sign would make the Poynting vector point inward. The following derivation uses dimensionally consistent retarded potentials and outgoing radiation.
Radiation fields
Words: 164
Solution OurBigBook.com
The consistent Liénard–Wiechert potential obeys A=uφ/c2. To first order in β=u/c, its large-distance form is
φ≃4πϵ0​rq​[1+n⋅β(tr​)],A≃4πrμ0​q​u(tr​),tr​≃t−cr​.
(10)
Terms varying as 1/r2 give near fields and are negligible for the radiated power at large r. Keep the first-order velocity term in φ: although small, its retarded-time derivative contributes a leading acceleration field. To the required order, ∂tr​/∂t≃1 and ∇tr​≃−n/c. Holding the observation direction fixed at leading order gives
−∇φ​1/r​=4πϵ0​crq​n(n⋅β˙​),−∂t∂A​​1/r​=−4πϵ0​crq​β˙​.
(11)
Thus the outgoing electric field is transverse:
Erad​(r,t)=4πϵ0​crq​[n×(n×β˙​)]tr​​=4πϵ0​c2rq​[n×(n×a)]tr​​.​
(12)
Here a=du/dtr​. The magnetic field follows from ∇×A, or from the outgoing plane electromagnetic wave relation:
Brad​=−4πrμ0​q​[n×β˙​]tr​​=c1​n×Erad​.​
(13)
The minus sign is essential. Both fields fall as 1/r, are mutually orthogonal and obey ∣E∣=c∣B∣. The corresponding Liénard-Wiechert field also contains nonradiative terms, omitted in this leading far-field calculation.
Mean angular power
Words: 109
Solution OurBigBook.com
Since E⋅n=0, the Poynting vector is
S=μ0​1​E×B=ϵ0​c∣E∣2n.
(14)
The area on a distant sphere corresponding to solid angle dΩ is r2dΩ. Consequently
dΩd⟨P⟩​=16π2ϵ0​c3q2​⟨∣a∣2−(n⋅a)2⟩=16π2ϵ0​cq2​⟨∣n×β˙​∣2⟩.​
(15)
The average is over the source motion at retarded time. At leading nonrelativistic order, differences between source-time and reception-time averaging are beyond the approximation. The units are power per solid angle, Wsr−1.
The acceleration covariance in nonrelativistic radiation is another way to organize this result: with Cij​=⟨ai​aj​⟩, the angular factor is trC−nTCn. Integrating over all observation directions uses ∫[∣a∣2−(n⋅a)2]dΩ=(8π/3)∣a∣2 and gives the averaged Larmor formula, ⟨P⟩=q2⟨a2⟩/(6πϵ0​c3).
Linear oscillation
Words: 85
Solution OurBigBook.com
For the prescribed displacement along z, the source acceleration is a(tr​)=−aω2cos(ωtr​)z. If θ is the angle from the z axis, ∣a∣2−(n⋅a)2=a2ω4cos2(ωtr​)sin2θ. Averaging cos2 over one wave period gives
dΩd⟨P⟩linear​​=32π2ϵ0​c3q2a2ω4​sin2θ.​
(16)
The angular distribution of electric dipole radiation has zero intensity on the oscillation axis and maximum intensity in the equatorial plane, with no azimuthal dependence. Its total power is
⟨P⟩linear​=12πϵ0​c3q2a2ω4​.​
(17)
The condition aω≪c is the required nonrelativistic condition. The plotted radial coordinate below is angular power, rather than field wave amplitude.
Circular orbit
Words: 154
Solution OurBigBook.com
Denote the orbit radius by b here to distinguish it from observation distance; it is the quantity called R in this part of the paper. Write
x(tr​)=b(cosωtr​,sinωtr​,0),a(tr​)=−bω2(cosωtr​,sinωtr​,0).
(18)
For observation at spherical coordinates (θ,ϕ), measured from the orbit normal,
n⋅a=−bω2sinθcos(ωtr​−ϕ),⟨(n⋅a)2⟩=21​b2ω4sin2θ.
(19)
The acceleration magnitude is constant, ∣a∣=bω2. Therefore the radiation pattern of a circularly moving nonrelativistic charge is
dΩd⟨P⟩circular​​=32π2ϵ0​c3q2b2ω4​(1+cos2θ),⟨P⟩circular​=6πϵ0​c3q2b2ω4​.​
(20)
Time averaging removes the azimuthal dependence. The axial intensity is twice the equatorial intensity, and no direction has zero mean intensity: the instantaneous dipole axis continually rotates. This is also the sum of the two orthogonal linear-oscillation patterns with their relative phase quadrature. At equal radius and linear-oscillation amplitude, the total mean power is twice the linear result. The calculation requires bω≪c; relativistic synchrotron radiation is outside this approximation.

B5 OurBigBook.com
Treat a single weakly driven electron or an independent bound charge, so that the incident electric field is approximately uniform across its displacement. The classical calculation uses electric dipole radiation and excludes both a resonant divergence and substantial quantum recoil.
Rayleigh limit
Words: 85
Solution OurBigBook.com
In Rayleigh scattering, a small bound system develops an induced electric dipole moment p(t)=Re[α(ω)E0​e−iωt]. Well below its binding resonances, the electric polarizability is approximately its static value α(0), independent of frequency. The mean radiated power and incident light intensity are
⟨P⟩=12πϵ0​c3ω4∣αE0​∣2​,⟨S⟩=21​ϵ0​c∣E0​∣2.
(21)
Dividing gives the total scattering cross section
σR​=6πϵ02​c4ω4∣α(0)∣2​∝ω4∝λ−4.​
(22)
This Rayleigh scattering wavelength dependence comes from the two time derivatives of an oscillating electric dipole moment, whose radiated field is proportional to ω2p0​. It requires the same scatterer with approximately constant low-frequency electric polarizability; resonances and strong optical dispersion invalidate the simple fourth-power law.
Thomson limit
Words: 87
Solution OurBigBook.com
Thomson scattering describes the low-recoil scattering of electromagnetic radiation by a free charge. For charge q and mass m, the weak incident field gives peak acceleration ∣q∣E0​/m. The mean Larmor formula power is therefore
⟨P⟩=12πϵ0​m2c3q4∣E0​∣2​.
(23)
There is no binding force to introduce a characteristic frequency. Dividing by the same incident light intensity gives the Thomson cross-section:
σT​=6πϵ02​m2c4q4​=38π​(4πϵ0​mc2q2​)2.​
(24)
For an electron, the length inside parentheses is the classical electron radius. The cross section has units m2 and is independent of wavelength in this classical limit. The driven velocity must remain small compared with c, and ℏω≪mc2 must make Compton recoil kinematics negligible.
Bound-charge response
Words: 159
Solution OurBigBook.com
Put ω02​=k/m, where k is the binding spring constant rather than an optical wavenumber. The steady complex amplitude solution of the forced Lorentz oscillator model is
x0​=k−mω2qE0​​=m(ω02​−ω2)qE0​​,α(ω)=m(ω02​−ω2)q2​.
(25)
The acceleration complex amplitude is −ω2x0​. Hence
⟨P⟩=12πϵ0​c3q2ω4∣x0​∣2​,σ(ω)=6πϵ02​c4(k−mω2)2q4ω4​=σT​(ω02​−ω2)2ω4​.​
(26)
This is the scattering cross-section of a harmonically bound charge. The displacement and induced electric dipole moment are parallel to the incident linear polarization, and integrating their dipole radiation over all directions gives the total cross section above.
For ω≪ω0​, binding dominates and
σ≃σT​(ω/ω0​)4,​
(27)
recovering Rayleigh scattering with static electric polarizability q2/k. For ω≫ω0​, inertia dominates: x0​≃−qE0​/(mω2), so its second time derivative becomes frequency independent and
σ≃σT​.​
(28)
This is a classical Thomson scattering regime only while ℏω≪mc2. Exactly at ω=ω0​, an undamped driven oscillator has no finite steady-state amplitude; the divergent formula must not be used. Finite damping and radiation reaction regulate the physical resonant response. Coherent contributions from many charges in an extended medium can also require a structure calculation rather than multiplying this single-charge result by a particle count.
Binding scale and recoil
Words: 138
Solution OurBigBook.com
A distinct classical Thomson scattering window requires two separated scales:
ω0​≪ω≪ωC​,ωC​=ℏmc2​.
(29)
To remove that window, the binding frequency must be comparable with or greater than the Compton scattering recoil scale. Thus the formal condition for bound-electron scattering without a Thomson frequency window is
mk​​≳ℏmc2​,k≳ℏ2m3c4​.​
(30)
Using the stipulated approximate electron mass m=10−30kg, c=2.998×108ms−1 and ℏ=1.055×10−34Js gives
kscale​≃7.3×1011Nm−1,ωC​≃8.5×1020s−1.​
(31)
The dimensions are [m3c4/ℏ2]=kgs−2=Nm−1. The inequality denotes a scale comparison, not a uniquely specified spring constant or a sharp transition.
There is also a physical limitation to the question's premise. Compton scattering does not begin discontinuously at ℏω=mc2; recoil corrections grow as photon energy approaches the rest energy. If ℏω0​≳mc2, the assumed nonrelativistic binding model is itself no longer reliable. The calculation therefore locates where no well-separated classical Thomson scattering range exists; it cannot establish a literal direct transition of a real strongly bound system using this oscillator equation alone.

First prove the two electromagnetic field invariants from the supplied three-dimensional transformations. Then distinguish the correct covariant transversality of a vacuum plane wave from the index and equivalence issues in the original tensor statement.
Field invariants
Words: 216
Solution OurBigBook.com
Let u be the boost velocity, and decompose the fields into components parallel and perpendicular to it. The parallel components are unchanged. The perpendicular components obey
E⊥′​=γ(E⊥​+u×B⊥​),B⊥′​=γ(B⊥​−u×E⊥​/c2),γ=(1−u2/c2)−1/2.
(32)
All vectors inside these expressions are perpendicular to u. Thus ∣u×B⊥​∣2=u2B⊥2​, and similarly for E⊥​. In the difference of squares, the mixed terms cancel because
E⊥​⋅(u×B⊥​)=−B⊥​⋅(u×E⊥​).
(33)
It follows that
E⊥′2​−c2B⊥′2​​=γ2[(1−u2/c2)E⊥2​−(c2−u2)B⊥2​]=E⊥2​−c2B⊥2​.​
(34)
Adding the unchanged parallel components proves
E′2−c2B′2=E2−c2B2.​
(35)
For the mixed dot product, expand directly. The terms E⊥​⋅(u×E⊥​) and (u×B⊥​)⋅B⊥​ vanish. The remaining cross-product identity is
(u×B⊥​)⋅(u×E⊥​)=u2E⊥​⋅B⊥​.
(36)
Hence
E⊥′​⋅B⊥′​=γ2(1−u2/c2)E⊥​⋅B⊥​=E⊥​⋅B⊥​.
(37)
Parallel-perpendicular mixed dot products are zero in both frames, so
E′⋅B′=E⋅B.​
(38)
Rotations also preserve these two expressions, completing the proof for proper Lorentz transformations. The second expression is a pseudoscalar under spatial parity, but is invariant under the boosts at issue here.
These results also follow from tensor contractions. In the convention used below, Fμν​Fμν=2(B2−E2/c2) and Fμν​Fμν=−4E⋅B/c. The numerical sign of the latter depends on the chosen orientation of the dual electromagnetic field tensor, while its invariance under proper Lorentz transformations does not.
Transversality and tensor conventions
Words: 424
Solution OurBigBook.com
Write a nonzero vacuum plane electromagnetic wave with wavevector k=(ω/c)s, where s is a unit propagation direction. Its propagation Lorentz four-vector is Kμ=(ω/c,k), or, up to a scalar normalization in any one frame, nμ=(1,s). It is null because Kμ​Kμ=0.
Choose coordinates xμ=(ct,x) and Minkowski metric ημν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). With the supplied three-dimensional boost formulas, a consistent convention assigns the printed array to upper-index components,
F0i=−Ei​/c,Fij=−ϵijk​Bk​.
(39)
The index conventions for the electromagnetic field tensor then require F0i​=+Ei​/c and Fij​=Fij when both indices are lowered. The printed lower-index label therefore cannot be kept together with its electric signs and a forward propagation vector in this convention.
This is not merely an arbitrary overall sign. For a valid forward wave with s=z, E=E0​x and B=(E0​/c)y​, multiplying the printed array, unchanged, by nμ=(1,0,0,1) gives an x component −2E0​/c, rather than zero. One consistent repair is to retain the printed array as Fμν and contract with nμ​=(1,−s). Equivalently, lower both tensor indices before forming the contraction asked for in the question.
Using that corrected lower-index tensor gives
nμFμν​=(−cs⋅E​,cE​+s×B).
(40)
To establish both electric and magnetic transversality, include the dual electromagnetic field tensor. Choose F0i=−Bi​ and Fij=ϵijk​Ek​/c; lowering both indices then gives
nμFμν​=(−s⋅B,B−cs×E​).
(41)
The source-free plane-wave Maxwell equations are precisely the pair of covariant conditions
nμFμν​=0,nμFμν​=0.​
(42)
Their temporal components give k⋅E=0 and k⋅B=0. Their spatial components give the associated amplitude and orientation relations, E/c=−s×B and B=s×E/c.
To see the Lorentz covariance explicitly, let Vν​=KμFμν​. It is a covector, so if Vν​=0 in one inertial frame, its Lorentz transformation is also zero in every other inertial frame. The same is true of KμFμν​. Since the transformed wave still has K′0>0 and ∣k′∣=ω′/c, their temporal components imply
k′⋅E′=k′⋅B′=0.​
(43)
Here Kμ, rather than a vector renormalized to have temporal component one in every frame, is the actual Lorentz four-vector; multiplying a zero contraction by a nonzero normalization does not affect the argument.
Finally, the two scalar perpendicularity statements alone are not equivalent to one tensor contraction. They do not enforce the amplitude relation between E and B. Conversely nμFμν​=0 alone cannot exclude a longitudinal magnetic field: adding B∥​s leaves its cross product unchanged. For a genuine source-free plane electromagnetic wave, the remaining Maxwell equations supply that missing constraint, and both tensor contractions vanish. This qualification and the corrected index placement are necessary for the intended invariance argument.
