During the time light crosses a telescope of length L, Earth moves sideways by approximately vL/c. The telescope must tilt towards Earth's motion so that both ends intercept the ray. Thus the annual stellar aberration amplitude at a transverse sightline is δ≃v/c.
Taking one orbital year as 365.25 days and treating the orbit as circular,
v=365.25(86400s)2π(1.5×1011m)​=2.99×104ms−1,δ≃9.96×10−5rad=20.5 arcseconds.​
(5)
This is the instantaneous shift relative to a stationary heliocentric observer, not the doubled peak-to-peak annual excursion. The exact Lorentz transformation for a ray transverse in that frame gives sinδ=v/c, with the same first-order result.
