A transverse static electric field can induce linear birefringence through the electro-optic Kerr effect. Define its coefficient by Δn=λ0​KEdc2​, with the principal axes parallel and perpendicular to the applied field. A length dK​ then gives δK​=2πKdK​Edc2​.
For input polarized at 45∘ to those axes, switching from zero retardation to ∣δK​∣=π/2 switches linear to circular polarization. Other input angles generally become elliptical, and axis-aligned input stays linear. The required field magnitude for the lowest-order setting is
∣Edc​∣=4∣K∣dK​​1​.​
(23)
A Kerr cell is suitable as a switchable quarter-wave plate. The field can be changed electronically without mechanically rotating the axes; actual switching time depends on the material response and driving circuit. This is induced linear birefringence, not polarization-selective absorption.[ref]
