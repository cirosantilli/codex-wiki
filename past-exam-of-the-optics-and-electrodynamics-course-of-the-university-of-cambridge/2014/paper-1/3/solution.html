Use metric η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and common spacetime origins. A four-vector transforms as A′=ΛA under a Lorentz transformation. A Lorentz invariant is a scalar unchanged by that transformation. Since ΛTηΛ=η,
A′⋅B′=ATΛTηΛB=ATηB=A⋅B.​

The wave phase labels the same stage of a physical oscillation at a spacetime event, independently of the observer's coordinates. Equal-phase wavefronts and interference phase differences are physical; fixing a common phase origin makes the continuous phase a Lorentz scalar. For a plane wave,
ϕ=ωt−k⋅r=Kμ​Xμ,Kμ​=∂μ​ϕ.
(7)
The gradient of a scalar is a covector; raising its index gives the four-wavevector Kμ=(ω/c,k). Under an x boost,
ω′=γ(ω−vkx​),kx′​=γ(kx​−vω/c2),ky′​=ky​,kz′​=kz​.​
(8)
For vacuum light, kx​=(ω/c)cosθ, giving
ω′=γω(1−βcosθ).​
(9)
A photon has four-momentum pμ=ℏKμ, with K2=0. For two photons separated by angle α,
(p1​+p2​)2=2ℏ2K1​⋅K2​=c22ℏ2ω1​ω2​​(1−cosα).
(10)
In the centre-of-momentum frame the least possible pair energy is 2me​c2, when both final particles are at rest. Thus the threshold has (p1​+p2​)2=4me2​c2; above it, excess energy supplies their kinetic energy. Therefore
ω2,th​=ℏ2ω1​(1−cosα)2me2​c4​.​
(11)
The kinematic equality is a limiting threshold; nonzero production phase space requires energies above it. For parallel photons no finite threshold is available, whereas head-on incidence minimizes the required energy:
E2,min​=E1​(me​c2)2​(α=π).​
(12)
The blackbody-peak photon pair-production estimate, using the frequency-spectrum blackbody peak, E1​=2.82144kB​T=7.29×10−4eV at 3K, gives E2,min​≃3.58×1014eV, or 358TeV. If “peak” means the wavelength-spectrum peak, E1​=4.96511kB​T instead gives 2.03×1014eV. The spectral convention changes the prefactor, not the conclusion that photons of order a few 1014eV are needed for this idealized peak-photon threshold.
