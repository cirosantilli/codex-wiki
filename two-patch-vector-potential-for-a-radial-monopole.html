Two-patch vector potential for a radial monopole (A±​=rsinθ±1−cosθ​eϕ​,∇×A±​=er​/r2)

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Magnetic monopole
OurBigBook.com Words: 157
The radial vector field B=er​/r2 on punctured three-dimensional Euclidean space has flux 4π through every centered sphere. It has no vector potential smooth on an entire enclosing sphere: the Stokes theorem on a closed surface would force zero flux. Two overlapping patches suffice. In spherical coordinates take A+​=(1−cosθ)eϕ​/(rsinθ), regular away from the negative z axis, and A−​=−(1+cosθ)eϕ​/(rsinθ), regular away from the positive z axis. Their curls both equal B.
The Cartesian forms A+​=(−y,x,0)/[r(r+z)] and A−​=(y,−x,0)/[r(r−z)] show that the first is smooth at the north pole and the second at the south pole. On the overlap their difference is A+​−A−​=2∇ϕ, where the azimuth ϕ is defined locally. Its multivaluedness around the axis explains the circulation difference 4π. Splitting the sphere along a latitude, applying the Stokes theorem with A+​ on the north and A−​ on the south, and using their opposite boundary orientations recovers the total flux. A coordinate singularity in the tangential spherical basis does not by itself make a vanishing smooth vector field singular at a pole.
