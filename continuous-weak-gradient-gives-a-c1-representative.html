Continuous weak gradient gives a C1 representative (Dj​u∈C ⟹ u=U a.e. with U∈C1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Distribution theory Distributional derivative Weak derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
If a locally integrable function has weak derivatives represented by a continuous vector field g, it has a representative U∈C1 with ∇U=g. The assertion is local, so work on a ball compactly contained in the domain and regularize using a mollifier. The regularizations satisfy the segment identity uε​(x)−uε​(y)=∫01​∇uε​(y+t(x−y))⋅(x−y)dt. Averaging over a smaller ball in y fixes the additive constant through local L1 convergence. The gradients converge uniformly on compact sets, so the averaged identity gives local uniform convergence to U. Passing to the limit on each segment then gives U(x+h)−U(x)−g(x)⋅h=o(∣h∣). Continuity of g proves that U is continuously differentiable.
