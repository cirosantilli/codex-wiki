Length contraction (L=L0​/γ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz factor
OurBigBook.com Words: 184
A rod of rest length L0​ moving parallel to its length has simultaneous laboratory length L=L0​/γ, where γ is the Lorentz factor. Here L0​ is the rod's proper length measured in its rest frame. Transverse lengths are unchanged by the corresponding Lorentz transformation.
For a rod at rest in S′ with endpoints x′=0 and x′=10m, let S′ move at v=0.6c relative to the laboratory S. Record both endpoint positions at laboratory time t=0. The Lorentz transformation then gives x′=1.25x, so their recorded positions are 0 and 8m: the laboratory length is 10/1.25=8m. This is an operational definition: synchronized laboratory detectors register the endpoints at the same laboratory time. A photograph collects light emitted at different times and need not show this length. [ref].
For those same two endpoint spacetime events, Δt′=−1.25(0.6c)(8m)/c2=−6m/c. Thus the front-end measurement occurs earlier in S′ by about 20ns. Length contraction compares simultaneous endpoint measurements in each frame, which select different pairs of spacetime events; it does not mean that the rod has been compressed in its own rest frame.
