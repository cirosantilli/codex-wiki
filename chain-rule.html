Chain rule ((g∘f)′(a)=g′(f(a))f′(a))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Derivative
OurBigBook.com Words: 859 Articles: 7
The chain rule calculates the derivative of a function composition by combining local rates. If the inner function f is differentiable at a and the outer function g is differentiable at f(a), then
(g∘f)′(a)=g′(f(a))f′(a).
(100)
The evaluation point matters: the outer derivative is evaluated at the inner output f(a), not at a. A small input change first scales by f′(a); the resulting intermediate change then scales by g′(f(a)). In terms of linear approximations, composing those two local descriptions multiplies their scale factors. [ref].
For example, h(x)=sin(x2) applies a quadratic function and then the sine. With f(x)=x2 and g(u)=sinu, the chain rule gives
h′(x)=cos(x2)2x,h′(1)=2cos1≈1.08060461.
(101)
Here all angles are in radians. Near x=1, increasing the input by Δx changes x2 by about 2Δx, and the sine changes by about cos1 times that intermediate change. For Δx=0.01, the linear approximation predicts an output increase of 0.0108060; the exact increase is sin(1.0201)−sin1≈0.0106894.
The same rule handles several nested stages. For h=g∘f∘k, the derivative is h′(x)=g′(f(k(x)))f′(k(x))k′(x) under the corresponding differentiability assumptions. Writing each intermediate output prevents evaluating a derivative at the wrong stage. It also makes the chain rule useful for propagating sensitivities through a mathematical model, where one measured quantity influences another through several transformations.
For a quick proof idea, replace f near a by its linear approximation, then replace g near f(a) by its linear approximation. The composite leading term is their product; differentiability ensures the remaining error is smaller than the original input change. This argument still applies when f′(a)=0, so the informal cancellation dh/dx=(dh/du)(du/dx) should not be interpreted as division by a possibly zero finite change. The hypotheses are sufficient, but are not necessary for every composite to be differentiable, as the nondifferentiable inner function with a differentiable nondegenerate composite example shows.
