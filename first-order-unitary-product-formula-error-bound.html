First-order unitary product-formula error bound

For Hermitian matrices H1​,…,Hm​ and t≥0, let S(t)=∏j=1m​e−itHj​. Then
​S(t)−e−it∑j​Hj​​≤2t2​j<l∑​∥[Hj​,Hl​]∥.​
(17)
Here every norm is the spectral norm. A short proof starts with two summands. Differentiate F(s)=e−i(t−s)(A+B)e−isAe−isB; its derivative has norm at most ∥[e−isA,B]∥. Differentiating e−iuABeiuA and integrating yields ∥[e−isA,B]∥≤s∥[A,B]∥, because unitary operators preserve the norm. Integrating s from zero to t gives t2∥[A,B]∥/2. Inductively separate H1​ from the remaining sum, use the triangle inequality on their commutator, and apply the telescoping bound for products of operators to obtain the displayed many-term bound.
Repeating steps of size t/k and telescoping across k steps gives
​S(t/k)k−e−it∑j​Hj​​≤2kt2​∑j<l​∥[Hj​,Hl​]∥.
(18)
For a chain of O(n) bounded nearest-neighbor terms, only O(n) pairs fail to commute. Consequently first-order product-formula Hamiltonian simulation has error O(nt2/k) and uses O(nk) constant-size gates. The general bound is also given in Proposition 9 of Childs and collaborators' analysis of Trotter error.
