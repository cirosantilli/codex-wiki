The function is not Lebesgue integrable. Fix 0<η<π/2 and restrict to x≥1, 0<y<η. There cosy≥cosη>0. Tonelli's theorem for the nonnegative absolute value gives the lower bound
∬∣g(x,y)∣dxdy​≥cosη∫1∞​∣cos2x∣∫0η​e−xydydx=cosη∫1∞​∣cos2x∣x1−e−ηx​dx≥cosη(1−e−η)∫1∞​x∣cos2x∣​dx.​
(14)
The last integral diverges. Indeed, on [kπ,kπ+π/6], ∣cos2x∣≥1/2; each such interval contributes at least a positive constant times 1/(k+1). The harmonic series diverges. Hence the integral of ∣g∣ is infinite, regardless of possible signed cancellations. One cannot infer Lebesgue integrability by formally applying Fubini's theorem to the oscillatory integrand.
