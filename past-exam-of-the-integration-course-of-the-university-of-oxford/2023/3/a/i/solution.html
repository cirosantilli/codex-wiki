OurBigBook.com Words: 96
Minkowski inequality states that on a measure space (X,Σ,μ), for 1≤p≤∞ and measurable real or complex f,g of finite Lp norm,
∥f+g∥p​≤∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​.​
(25)
For p=1, integrate the scalar triangle inequality ∣f+g∣≤∣f∣+∣g∣. For p=∞, the almost everywhere bounds ∣f∣≤∥f∥∞​ and ∣g∣≤∥g∥∞​ give the same result by taking the essential supremum.
For 1<p<∞, first ensure that the left side is finite. The convex function s↦sp on [0,∞) gives
∣f+g∣p≤(∣f∣+∣g∣)p≤2p−1(∣f∣p+∣g∣p),
(26)
so f+g has finite Lp norm. Set q=p/(p−1) and H=∥f+g∥p​. Since (p−1)q=p, Holder inequality gives
Hp≤∫X​(∣f∣+∣g∣)∣f+g∣p−1dμ≤(∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​)(∫X​∣f+g∣pdμ)1/q=(∥f∥p​+∥g∥p​)Hp−1.
(27)
If H=0 the result is immediate; otherwise divide by Hp−1. This proves the triangle inequality for every claimed exponent.
