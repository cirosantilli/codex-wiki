Solutions to all three questions of the examination sat on 16 June 2021, including all optional parts. Integrability means Lebesgue integrability; a conditionally convergent improper integral does not suffice. The Roman items in Question 3(a) are the three required properties of one construction; the construction and its limiting properties are explained first, and each Roman item is checked separately.

On (0,1), the integrand is bounded by one. For x→∞, αxβ−2logx→∞, since every positive power grows faster than the natural logarithm. Thus e−αxβ≤x−2 eventually. The comparison test for integrals proves Lebesgue integrability.
To prove joint continuity, fix (α0​,β0​) with both coordinates positive. In a sufficiently small parameter rectangle, α≥a:=α0​/2>0 and β≥b:=β0​/2>0. For every parameter pair in this rectangle,
0≤e−αxβ≤H(x):={1,e−axb,​0<x<1,x≥1.​
(4)
The first argument shows that H is integrable. Along any parameter sequence tending to (α0​,β0​), the integrands converge pointwise, and the same H dominates their eventual tail. The dominated convergence theorem gives g(α,β)→g(α0​,β0​). The sequential criterion for continuity proves the required joint continuity throughout the positive quadrant.
For comparison, the change of variables u=αxβ identifies the value with a gamma function:
g(α,β)=βα1/βΓ(1/β)​.​
(5)
The domination argument establishes continuity directly, without needing a separate theorem about parameter dependence of the gamma function.

Near zero, sinx/x→1, so ∣sinx∣/xα is comparable to x1−α. The power integral test shows that the integral near zero is finite exactly when α<2.
For the tail, ∣sinx∣≤1 proves integrability when α>1. To prove necessity, take the disjoint intervals
Ik​=[2kπ+π/6, 2kπ+5π/6],k≥1.
(9)

(10)

∬∣g(x,y)∣dxdy​≥cosη∫1∞​∣cos2x∣∫0η​e−xydydx=cosη∫1∞​∣cos2x∣x1−e−ηx​dx≥cosη(1−e−η)∫1∞​x∣cos2x∣​dx.​

a OurBigBook.com
Set C0​=[0,1]. Inductively, replace each component interval of Cn​ by its two closed end intervals, each of length ℓn+1​:=qn+1​/2n+1. The current component length is ℓn​=qn​/2n. Because qn+1​<qn​, the removed central gap has positive length
ℓn​−2ℓn+1​=2nqn​−qn+1​​.
(18)
This is a generalized Cantor set construction. The three required properties are checked in parts i, ii and iii.
The sets are closed, measurable, decreasing, and contained in [0,1], which has finite Lebesgue measure. By continuity from above of a measure,
m(C)=m(n=0⋂∞​Cn​)=n→∞lim​qn​.​
(19)
This limit exists because (qn​) is a bounded decreasing monotone sequence. To exclude a nondegenerate interval J⊆C, note that its connectedness forces it into a single component interval of every Cn​: crossing two components would cross a removed gap. Its length would therefore be at most 2−n for every n, which is impossible for a positive-length interval. Thus C contains no nondegenerate interval, even when its Lebesgue measure is positive.

The points belonging to infinitely many En​ form their limit superior of sets:
G=⋂N≥1​⋃n≥N​En​.
(22)
For every N, countable subadditivity gives
0≤m(G)≤∑n≥N​m(En​)≤∑n≥N​2−n=21−N⟶0.
(23)
Thus m(G)=0​. Outside this null set, each point belongs to only finitely many En​, so its indicator functions are eventually zero. Therefore χEn​​(x)→0 almost everywhere​. This is the summable-measure argument behind the first Borel-Cantelli lemma; no independence assumption is needed.

limsupn​∫A​fn​=∫R​f−liminfn​∫Ac​fn​≤∫R​f−∫Ac​f=∫A​f.

The lower and upper bounds prove ∫A​fn​→∫A​f​.
For the asserted L1 convergence, choose an integer k so large that ∫∣x∣>k​f<ε, possible by Lebesgue integrability. The first result applied to this fixed tail set makes ∫∣x∣>k​fn​<2ε eventually. The additional local hypothesis makes ∫−kk​∣fn​−f∣<ε eventually. Therefore
∫R​∣fn​−f∣≤∫−kk​∣fn​−f∣+∫∣x∣>k​fn​+∫∣x∣>k​f<4ε.
