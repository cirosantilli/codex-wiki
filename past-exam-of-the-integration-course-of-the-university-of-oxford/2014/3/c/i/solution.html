Fix 0<x<1. On the open interval (0,x), the function y↦(x−y)β−1 is finite, positive and continuous, and f is nonnegative and Lebesgue measurable. Their product is therefore a nonnegative measurable function. Its Lebesgue integral is always defined as an element of [0,∞], with no possibility of cancellation of two infinities. Thus g(x) is well defined as a nonnegative extended integral.
The endpoint y=x need not receive its formal power value: for β<1 that value would be singular. The integral is over (0,x), and any chosen value at the endpoints has no effect because endpoints form a null set.
Finiteness is not implied by the present assumptions. For example, f(y)=1/y is an admissible nonnegative measurable function. For any fixed x, the factor (x−y)β−1 is bounded below by a positive constant on 0<y<x/2, so g(x)≥Cx​∫0x/2​dy/y=∞. The later L1 norm bound will establish finiteness almost everywhere when f is integrable. The construction is a Volterra power-kernel integral.
