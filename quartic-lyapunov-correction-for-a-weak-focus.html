Quartic Lyapunov correction for a weak focus (V=21​r2+V4​,V˙=κr4+O(r6))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Nonhyperbolic equilibrium Weak focus
OurBigBook.com Words: 159
Consider a smooth planar system x˙=y+P3​(x,y)+O(r5), y˙​=−x+Q3​(x,y)+O(r5), where P3​,Q3​ are homogeneous cubic polynomials and r2=x2+y2. The leading derivative of r2/2 is the quartic polynomial H4​=xP3​+yQ3​. Let κ be the angular average of H4​ on the unit circle. A homogeneous quartic correction V4​ solving
(y∂x​−x∂y​)V4​=κr4−H4​
(51)
gives V˙=κr4+O(r6) for V=r2/2+V4​. The right side has zero angular average. Since y∂x​−x∂y​=−∂θ​, its nonconstant angular Fourier modes can be integrated individually to obtain a periodic quartic correction; only modes of orders two and four can occur after removing the constant average.
The correction is O(r4), so V remains positive definite close to zero. If κ<0, its orbital derivative is strictly negative there, and the Second Lyapunov theorem proves asymptotic stability. If κ>0, apply the same argument in reversed time to obtain repulsion. If κ=0, this quartic calculation alone does not decide stability. This method converts the averaged damping of a weak focus into a pointwise sign for a Lyapunov function.
