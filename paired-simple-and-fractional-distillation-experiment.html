Paired simple and fractional distillation experiment

 Home Chemistry Distillation
OurBigBook.com Words: 172
McMaster University's 1997/98 laboratory manual compares simple distillation and fractional distillation using charges of 25mL each of cyclohexane and p-xylene. Part B uses a 100mL round-bottom flask and a fractionating column. The protocol specifies a boiling chip, a heating mantle on a ring support, and the top of the thermometer bulb midway in the opening toward the laboratory condenser. Heating is adjusted for roughly one drop per second. Students record head temperature every 2mL collected, up to 45mL, and plot both runs against collected volume on the same axes.[ref]
Charge, atmospheric operation, and collection rate provide comparison conditions; the fractionating column is changed. A sharper separation of temperature regions would support improved fractionation. It would not directly measure product mole fractions or prove purity. The manual distinguishes collected volume from composition and states that this pair forms no azeotrope. No student dataset is supplied here, and the constant-relative volatility example is not assigned to these liquids.[ref]
