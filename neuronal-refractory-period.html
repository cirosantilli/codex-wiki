Neuronal refractory period

 Home Biology Neuroscience Neuron Action potential
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 5
A neuronal refractory period is the change in excitability following an action potential: recent firing changes whether, and how readily, a neuron can fire again. The absolute neuronal refractory period prevents a second regenerative action potential; during the relative neuronal refractory period, some stimuli that work after full recovery still fail. These are useful distinctions, but their durations are not universal constants. The preparation, temperature, starting membrane potential, stimulation protocol and criterion for detecting an action potential must accompany a measured interval.
The mechanism continues the opposing sodium and potassium electric currents described under action potential and the Hodgkin-Huxley model. After a spike, ion-channel inactivation leaves fewer sodium-selective voltage-gated ion channels available for regenerative depolarization, while persisting potassium electrical conductance opposes that depolarization. In the Hodgkin-Huxley model, recovery of h restores sodium availability and relaxation of n reduces potassium electrical conductance. Closed but available channels differ from inactivated channels: opening and recovery from ion-channel inactivation are different processes.[ref]
This short memory matters for spike trains: a stimulus-only Poisson process can predict very short interspike intervals that a refractory neuron cannot produce. Neuronal paired-pulse recovery makes that memory visible.
