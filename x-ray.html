X-ray

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electromagnetic radiation
OurBigBook.com Words: 5k Articles: 25
X-rays are high-frequency electromagnetic radiation, conventionally with wavelengths of roughly 0.01 to 10nm. The precise wavelength convention varies with context. Hot accreting gas and energetic electronic processes can emit X-rays, whose short wavelengths make them useful probes of compact objects and electron shells.
For a photon, E=hν=hc/λ, where h is the Planck constant and c is the speed of light. As a calculated example, λ=0.10nm gives E≃12.4keV, using hc≃1.23984keVnm. The same wavelength can describe different production mechanisms: bremsstrahlung produces a continuous frequency spectrum, whereas Characteristic X-rays arise from discrete electronic changes in chemical atoms. Measuring transmitted X-rays gives radiography; measuring their frequency spectrum gives spectroscopy.
