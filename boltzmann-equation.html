Relaxation-time approximation (Icoll​[f]≃−δf/τ)

Words: 38
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
Approximation in which a small departure from a specified equilibrium distribution relaxes exponentially with time constant τ. Different observables can have different effective relaxation times; momentum relaxation is not automatically the same as every microscopic scattering lifetime.

Binary collision approximation

Words: 53
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
Treat each interaction as an isolated two-particle scattering event, neglecting simultaneous interactions involving three or more particles. For a dilute short-range gas, the interaction range is much smaller than the mean free path, making this approximation compatible with a local Boltzmann equation. It is distinct from molecular chaos for the Boltzmann equation, which is the statistical factorization of incoming pair distributions.

BGK model (C[f]=−(f−M[f])/τ)

Words: 100 Articles: 1
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
Replace the nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator by relaxation to the local Maxwellian M[f] with the same mass density, mean velocity and random kinetic energy as f. Matching those velocity moments ensures vanishing collision contributions to mass, momentum and kinetic energy balance. The collision relaxation time τ is often treated as a constant, although microscopic collision rates generally depend on the state and particle velocity.

Collision relaxation time (τ)

Words: 53
BGK model OurBigBook.com
The timescale on which a kinetic relaxation model damps departures from a matching local Maxwellian. In the spatially homogeneous BGK model with constant τ, conserved velocity moments fix M, and f(t)=M+[f(0)−M]e−t/τ. The parameter is of the order of an effective mean time between collisions rather than an exact universal microscopic collision time.

Renormalized Boltzmann solution

Words: 65
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
A weak solution framework in which a suitable nonlinear transform β(f) satisfies a transport equation with transformed collision source β′(f)Q(f,f), interpreted with the required integrability and possible framework-dependent defect terms. Renormalization addresses distributions whose raw collision term is not integrable enough to support the ordinary weak formulation. This is different from a classical smooth solution and does not by itself imply smooth global well-posedness.

Hydrodynamic limit of the Boltzmann equation

Words: 51
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
In a scaling ∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f=ε−1Q(f,f) with small Knudsen number, the leading collision balance formally forces a local Maxwellian. The conserved moments then give the Euler equations with a monatomic ideal-gas closure. The next nonequilibrium correction produces viscous and heat-conduction terms. These substitutions are formal: rigorous limits need uniform estimates, initial and boundary conditions, and sufficient convergence.

Spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation (∂t​f=Q(f,f))

Words: 59
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
When the velocity distribution is independent of position and there are no forces, the free transport equation contributes zero and only collisions evolve f. For a bounded cutoff kernel, ∥Q(f,g)∥1​≤C∥f∥1​∥g∥1​ gives local existence and uniqueness by the Banach fixed-point theorem. Positivity and mass conservation bound the norm and permit global continuation in this particular model. Stronger kernels require moment or weighted estimates.

Nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator (Q(f,f)(v)=∫B(g,n)(f′f∗′​−ff∗​)dv∗​dn)

Words: 257 Articles: 4
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
For equal-mass elastic collisions, one reflection parametrization is v′=v−((v−v∗​)⋅n)n and v∗′​=v∗​+((v−v∗​)⋅n)n. The fixed-n transformation preserves pairwise momentum, kinetic energy and velocity-pair measure. A nonnegative kernel invariant under particle interchange and collision reversal yields a gain-loss operator. Its weak Boltzmann collision identity proves conservation and its entropy identity proves the Boltzmann H theorem. Unlike the Maxwell molecule collision operator, this general kernel may depend on relative speed.

Boltzmann scattering kernel (B(g,θ))

Words: 55
Nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator OurBigBook.com
The nonnegative weight describing the rate and angular dependence of binary elastic collisions in the Boltzmann equation. In a scattering-angle representation with solid-angle measure dΩ, it contains relative speed g times differential scattering cross-section. A parametrization by collision normals has a different Jacobian. Any symmetry factor from integrating over a hemisphere instead of a sphere must be included consistently.

Dissipation form of the linearized Boltzmann operator (−⟨h,Lh⟩M​=41​∫BMM∗​(Δh)2dvdv∗​dn)

Words: 55
Nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator OurBigBook.com
Write f=M(1+h) around a fixed Maxwellian and expand the nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator to first order. The weak Boltzmann collision identity gives the nonnegative dissipation form in L2(Mdv), and polarization proves symmetry on an appropriate common domain. Its zero modes are the collision invariants when the scattering kernel is nondegenerate. A spectral gap, and self-adjointness of an unbounded realization, require additional domain and kernel hypotheses.

Grad angular cutoff (∫S2​B(g,n)dn<∞)

Words: 49
Nonlinear Boltzmann collision operator OurBigBook.com
The angular collision rate is integrable at each nonzero relative velocity, with any needed speed-growth bounds stated separately. This makes the separate gain and loss integrals available under suitable velocity integrability. Non-cutoff kernels can have infinite angular collision rate and require cancellation rather than the same naive gain-loss split.

Here Δψ=ψ(v′)+ψ(v∗′​)−ψ(v)−ψ(v∗​) is the pairwise collision change, not a Laplace operator. Average over the two particle labels and use collision reversal to obtain the formula. Every collision invariant has Δψ=0, giving mass, momentum and kinetic energy conservation. A second symmetrization replaces ff∗​Δψ/2 by (f′f∗′​−ff∗​)(−Δψ)/4.

Local Maxwellian (M(t,x,v)=exp(a(t,x)+b(t,x)⋅v+c(t,x)∣v∣2),c<0)

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
A Gaussian velocity profile at each position, with coefficients encoding local density, mean velocity and temperature. Its logarithm is a linear combination of collision invariants, so its collision term vanishes pointwise in position. Transport need not vanish: the coefficients must also satisfy their transport equations for a local Maxwellian to solve the full Boltzmann equation.

Expanding Gaussian solution of the Boltzmann equation (f(t,x,v)=Aexp(−α∣x−tv∣2−β∣v∣2),A,α,β>0)

Words: 55
Local Maxwellian OurBigBook.com
This spatially nonconstant local Maxwellian is constant along free characteristic curves. Its collision term vanishes by the collision invariants, so it solves the full Boltzmann equation. It is positive, rapidly decaying and integrable in phase space. Completing its velocity square gives local mean αtx/(β+αt2) and local variance proportional to (β+αt2)−1, while its spatial profile broadens with time.

Boltzmann entropy dissipation (D(f)=41​∫B(A−B0​)log(A/B0​)dvdv∗​dn)

Words: 59
Boltzmann H functional OurBigBook.com
Put A=f′f∗′​ and B0​=ff∗​. The weak Boltzmann collision identity applied to logf gives H′(t)=−∫D(f)dx when transport boundary fluxes vanish. Monotonicity of the logarithm makes every integrand nonnegative. For positive sufficiently regular distributions and nondegenerate angular scattering, zero dissipation forces logf to be a collision invariant, hence f to be a local Maxwellian. This equality statement alone does not establish convergence rates.

H-theorem (H′(t)=−16π1​∫(a−b)log(a/b)≤0)

Words: 52
Boltzmann H functional OurBigBook.com
For the normalized isotropic Maxwell molecule collision operator, put a=f′f∗′​ and b=ff∗​. The integral is over position, both velocities and collision direction. The symmetrized weak identity yields the displayed derivative of the Boltzmann H functional. It is nonpositive because (a−b)log(a/b)≥0 for positive a,b. Spatial transport contributes only a boundary flux, which must vanish for the total-space inequality.

Maxwell molecule collision operator (Q(f,f)=4π1​∫(f′f∗′​−ff∗​)dv∗​dσ)

Words: 317 Articles: 5
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
For the normalized isotropic cutoff model, collisions redistribute the direction of relative velocity uniformly while keeping its length. With center C=(v+v∗​)/2 and relative speed r=∣v−v∗​∣, outgoing velocities are C±rσ/2. Momentum and kinetic energy are preserved, and the collision rate has no relative-speed factor. The displayed operator uses unit total angular rate. General Maxwell molecule kernels can have nonconstant angular dependence.

Collision invariant (ϕ(v′)+ϕ(v∗′​)=ϕ(v)+ϕ(v∗​))

Words: 53
Maxwell molecule collision operator OurBigBook.com
A collision invariant has an unchanged pairwise sum in every allowed elastic collision. The functions 1, each velocity coordinate, and ∣v∣2 encode mass, momentum and energy. Symmetrized weak collision identities then give zero moments of the collision operator against these functions. Under standard regularity assumptions, the invariants form their five-dimensional linear span in three velocity dimensions.

Fourier distance of order two (d2​(f,g)=supξ=0​∣f​(ξ)−g​(ξ)∣/∣ξ∣2)

Words: 103 Articles: 1
Bobylev identity OurBigBook.com
For finite nonnegative measures with equal mass and first moment and finite second moments, the constant and linear terms of their Fourier transforms cancel. Taylor's remainder bounds their difference by a constant times ∣ξ∣2, making this distance finite. Fourier uniqueness gives definiteness. The origin is excluded from the supremum; the quotient need not have a direction-independent limit there.

Unit-mass solutions of the isotropic Maxwell molecule collision operator with matching first moments obey this comparison. Angular exchange for elastic collisions ((C,r,ω,σ)↔(C,r,σ,ω))

Words: 56
Maxwell molecule collision operator OurBigBook.com
Write incoming relative velocity as rω and outgoing relative velocity as rσ. For isotropic scattering, the product measure dCr2drdωdσ is invariant under exchanging the two angular variables. This gives the pre/post-collision weak identities. Keeping σ fixed and sending only the velocities to the outgoing pair is not invertible: that map discards the incoming angular direction.

Linear Boltzmann equation (∂t​f+v⋅∇x​f=Kt​f−af)

Words: 536 Articles: 11
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
A linear kinetic transport equation with velocity-integral gain Kt​ and nonnegative loss coefficient a. Its characteristic integral formulation combines the damped free-transport evolution with the Boltzmann Volterra operator. A uniform square-integrable velocity kernel yields bounded gain on Lx,v2​ and a globally convergent Volterra series for the linear Boltzmann equation on every finite time interval.

Order preservation for the linear Boltzmann equation (f1,0​≥f2,0​⟹f1,t​≥f2,t​)

Words: 52
Linear Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
Nonnegative gain kernels and positive damping preserve pointwise order. The Volterra series for the linear Boltzmann equation has nonnegative terms when its initial datum is nonnegative. Applying this to the difference of two solutions proves comparison, with uniqueness identifying that difference with the positive series. This order property is distinct from positivity of a quadratic form on a Hilbert space.

Relativistic kinetic theory

Words: 218 Articles: 3
Boltzmann equation OurBigBook.com
Relativistic kinetic theory describes particles by a scalar phase-space distribution function on the future mass shell. Its Boltzmann equation uses geodesic streaming and a collision operator. Integrating its momentum moments supplies a kinetic stress-energy tensor and particle-number currents. An orthonormal tetrad makes the local momentum integrals identical in form to those of special relativity.

One-particle distribution function (f(x,p))

Words: 113 Articles: 1
Relativistic kinetic theory OurBigBook.com
A scalar function on the future mass shell describing the expected particle count in a phase-space distribution cell. In a local orthonormal tetrad, the number crossing a spacelike surface element is fpμdΣμ​d3p/E. On a constant-time slice it becomes fd3xd3p. Including degeneracy and phase-space normalization in f gives the stress-energy tensor Tμν=∫(d3p/E)fpμpν. Other conventions may put the degeneracy or (2π)−3 explicitly in the measure.
