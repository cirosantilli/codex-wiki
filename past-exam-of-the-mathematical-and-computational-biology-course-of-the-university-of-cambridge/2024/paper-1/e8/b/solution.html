The sample covariance matrix is S=XTX/(3−1); using divisor three instead changes eigenvalues by a common factor and leaves principal component loadings unchanged. For XTX, the characteristic polynomial is
det(XTX−λI)=(114−λ)(86−λ)−482=λ2−200λ+7500=(λ−150)(λ−50).

For the larger eigenvalue 150, the eigenvector equation gives −36α−48β=0, so 3α+4β=0. Normalize to unit Euclidean norm and choose v1​=(4/5,−3/5)T. An orthogonal unit eigenvector for 50 is v2​=(3/5,4/5)T. Therefore
PC1​=54​x−53​y,PC2​=53​x+54​y​.
(68)
The sample variances of these principal components are 75 and 25, respectively. Their principal component scores for observations A,B,C are
ABC​PC1​5−105​PC2​50−5​​.
(69)
Either eigenvector may be multiplied by −1 without changing its principal component direction or its variance. No coordinate standardization is applied here: that would instead be principal component analysis on a correlation matrix.
