A diagonal substitution matrix score measures enrichment over a background match, rather than simply rewarding an identical letter. tryptophan residues are comparatively rare, so a W–W match has a small chance background frequency pW2​. Its observed conservation produces a much larger ratio qWW​/pW2​ than the corresponding ratio for the more common leucine. The BLOSUM62 diagonal therefore awards W–W more than L–L. Both the background frequency and the observed pair frequency matter; rarity alone does not determine a diagonal score.[ref]
