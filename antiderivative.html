Antiderivative (F′=g)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Integral
OurBigBook.com Words: 641 Articles: 3
An antiderivative recovers a function from its local rate of change. For a real-valued function g on an open subset U of the real line, a differentiable function F is an antiderivative when
F′(x)=g(x)(x∈U).​
(24)
For example, g(x)=x2 has the antiderivatives FC​(x)=x3/3+C for every real additive constant C. Their derivatives agree, while their values can differ. On an interval, the constant of integration describes the entire freedom in the answer. [ref]
The indefinite-integral notation ∫g(x)dx=F(x)+C records this family on an interval. For the illustrated family, the extra condition F(1)=2 selects C=5/3. Thus reversing differentiation requires both a rate and a reference value if a unique function is wanted.
The fundamental theorem of calculus supplies a construction when g is continuous on an interval I. Choose x0​∈I and set F(x)=∫x0​x​g(t)dt; then F′=g. Integrability alone does not guarantee a classical antiderivative. The Heaviside step function is Riemann integrable on bounded intervals, but cannot be a derivative throughout an interval containing its jump: Darboux theorem for derivatives requires every intermediate value.
