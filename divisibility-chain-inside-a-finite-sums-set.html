Divisibility chain inside a finite-sums set (xi​∣xi+1​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Ramsey theory Hindman theorem Finite-sums set
OurBigBook.com Words: 160
Every finite-sums set generated by an infinite sequence of positive integers contains another finite-sums set generated by a strictly increasing sequence xi​ satisfying xi​∣xi+1​. Consequently the divisibility requirement can be added to the conclusion of Hindman theorem.
Choose each xi​ as a sum over a finite block of indices of the original sequence, with these index blocks disjoint and successive. Given xi​=m, take two fresh groups of m terms. In either group the m+1 partial sums, including 0, give two equal congruence classes modulo m by the pigeonhole principle. Their difference is a nonempty consecutive sum divisible by m, and hence at least m. Add the two such sums to obtain xi+1​≥2m divisible by m. Its index block is the union of the two selected subblocks. Discard the rest of these groups and continue after them. Because the blocks are disjoint, every finite sum of the xi​ is a finite sum of distinct original terms. This proves both the finite-sums set containment and the strictly increasing divisibility chain.
