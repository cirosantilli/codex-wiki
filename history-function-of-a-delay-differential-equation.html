History function of a delay differential equation (y(t)=ϕ(t),−τ≤t≤0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Delay differential equation Retarded delay differential equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 261
A history function of a delay differential equation supplies the past interval needed to start a retarded delay differential equation. A single initial condition y(0)=ϕ(0) generally leaves earlier delayed values unspecified.[ref]
For a continuous function history, the natural state at time t is the entire segment
yt​(θ)=y(t+θ),−τ≤θ≤0.
(4)
It belongs to the function space C([−τ,0],R), an infinite-dimensional vector space: the functions 1,θ,θ2,… are linearly independent. Shifting yt​ forward retains the part of the past that the equation can still read. Thus even one scalar unknown can require a state in an infinite-dimensional vector space.
The insufficiency of one value is visible without any approximation. Consider y′(t)=y(t−1) with the two history functions of a delay differential equation
ϕA​(s)=1,ϕB​(s)=1+s,−1≤s≤0.
(5)
Both give y(0)=1, yet on the first step the respective derivatives are 1 and t. Direct integration gives
yA​(t)=1+t,yB​(t)=1+2t2​,0≤t≤1.
(6)
In particular yA​(1)=2 while yB​(1)=3/2. On the next step,
yA​(t)=1+t+2(t−1)2​,yB​(t)=23​+(t−1)+6(t−1)3​,1≤t≤2.
(7)
At t=2 they are 7/2 and 8/3, a gap of 5/6. These different futures reflect different prescribed histories; each fully specified problem has a unique solution of a differential equation. More strongly, for y′(t)=ay(t−1) with a=0, observing the entire first-step derivative recovers every history value through ϕ(s)=y′(s+1)/a for −1≤s≤0, using the right derivative at 0. The future can depend on the whole function, rather than a finite list of its samples.
