The four dimensional equations express mass conservation, momentum balance with pressure, gravity and viscous stress, heat advection and conduction, and a linear thermal-expansion equation of state. For heating from below, suitable plate conditions are u=0 and T=T0​+ΔT at z=0, and u=0, T=T0​ at z=d. No slip and thermal insulation on the sidewall provide one suitable lateral completion; prescribed lateral temperatures would be another specified thermal setup.
Let κ=k/(ρ0​cp​) be thermal diffusivity and ν=μ/ρ0​ kinematic viscosity. Use d, d2/κ, κ/d and ΔT as length, time, velocity and temperature scales. Subtract the static reference pressure pH​ with ∇pH​=−ρ0​gk and scale its remainder by μκ/d2. Then
B=αΔT,Pr=ν/κ,Ra=νκgαΔTd3​.​
(1)
Density becomes ρ0​(1−BT). Substitution and division by the corresponding diffusion/viscous scales give
−BTt​+∇⋅[(1−BT)u]=0,Pr−1(1−BT)(ut​+u⋅∇u)=−∇p+RaTk+Δu,
(2)
(1−BT)(Tt​+u⋅∇T)=ΔT.
(3)
In the Boussinesq small-density-contrast limit retain buoyancy while neglecting the other B factors. At infinite Prandtl number, after any fast initial adjustment, the equations are
∇⋅u=0,0=−∇p+RaTk+Δu,Tt​+u⋅∇T=ΔT.​
(4)
The velocity is instantaneously constrained by a viscous elliptic balance; the temperature still evolves.
Take the divergence of the vector Poisson equation. Derivatives commute, and divergence of a curl is zero, so Δ(∇⋅A)=0. Its boundary value is zero, hence the maximum principle gives ∇⋅A=0 throughout D. The vector identity now gives curlcurlA=curlu, or
curl(curlA−u)=0.
(5)
The layer domain is simply connected because its horizontal section is simply connected. For a curl-free field on such a domain, define χ(x) as its line integral from a fixed base point to x. Contractibility of loops and Stokes theorem make the integral path independent, and differentiation gives its gradient equal to the field. Thus curlA−u=∇χ.
Incompressibility then gives Δχ=0, while the prescribed normal curl gives ∂n​χ=0. Green's identity yields
∫D​∣∇χ∣2dV=∫∂D​χ∂n​χdS−∫D​χΔχdV=0.
(6)
Hence χ is constant on the connected domain and curlA=u​. The extra condition A⋅n=0 belongs to the stated potential boundary problem; the reconstruction argument above uses the divergence and normal-curl conditions. On a multiply connected domain a curl-free field need not be a globally single-valued gradient.
Finally curl(Tk)=(Ty​,−Tx​,0). Taking the curl of the infinite-Prandtl momentum balance and using curlu=−ΔA gives
0=Ra(Ty​i−Tx​j)−Δ2A,∇4A+Ra(Tx​j−Ty​i)=0.​
(7)
The derivation is an instance of velocity reconstruction from a mixed-boundary vector Poisson problem.
