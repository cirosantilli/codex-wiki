Hydrostatic pressure varies with height as pl​−patm​=−ρgz when the far flat bath is the zero-height reference. Surface tension balances that pressure jump by curvature; with the curvature sign chosen in the equation this becomes ρgz=σκ. Here ρ is liquid mass density, g gravitational acceleration, σ surface tension and κ the signed planar curvature. The capillary length ℓc​=σ/(ρg)​ balances these effects. Scale both physical z and h by ℓc​ to obtain
z=hzz​(1+hz2​)−3/2.​
(1)
For the wall-rising meniscus h decreases as z rises. At the distant bath, z↓0, h→∞ and hz​→−∞, because the free surface becomes horizontal when expressed as z(x). In the first integral this gives A=−1​.
At zero wall contact angle the interface is tangent to the vertical wall. In the representation x=h(z) this means h(z0​)=0 and hz​(z0​)=0, so z02​/2−1=0. The rising contact point is z0​=2​​. Put q=z2/2−1. Then hz​=q/1−q2​=2​(z−z0​)+O((z−z0​)2). Integration from the contact point gives
h(z)∼2​(z−z0​)2​.​
(2)
The sign of hz​ is negative on the approach from below, while h is positive, as required by the geometry. This local quadratic shape is the zero-contact-angle wall meniscus matching law.

Let χ1​(x,t) equal one in phase one and zero in phase two; its interface value is immaterial for area integrals. In the absence of phase change or interfacial mass transfer, the distributional phase mass conservation is ∂t​(χ1​ρ)+∇⋅(χ1​ρu)=0. For a cross-section S of area A, define
α=A1​∫S​χ1​dA,ρ1​=Aα1​∫S​χ1​ρdA,u1​=Aαρ1​∫S​χ1​ρux​dA​.​
(10)
Here the symbol ux​ in the integral denotes the axial velocity component, not a derivative. Integrating through an arbitrary axial slice and using zero normal flux at the rigid pipe wall gives
∂t​(ρ1​α)+∂x​(ρ1​αu1​)=0.​
(11)
The velocity is mass weighted, so the averaged mass flux factors exactly; an unweighted phase mean would not in general have this property. This is the cross-section averaged phase mass balance.
For phase two use χ2​=1−χ1​, volume fraction 1−α, and
ρ2​=A(1−α)∫S​χ2​ρdA​,u2​=A(1−α)ρ2​∫S​χ2​ρux​dA​.
(12)
Then ∂t​[ρ2​(1−α)]+∂x​[ρ2​(1−α)u2​]=0​. Mean quantities in a phase that is absent are unnecessary; the products giving conserved masses and fluxes remain the meaningful variables.

A spherical bubble displaces liquid volume 4πa3/3. Neglect its own weight compared with the liquid buoyancy, as required by the stated gas/liquid approximation. Equating 4πa3ρl​g/3 with the quadratic drag gives
(u1​−u2​)∣u1​−u2​∣=3CD​4ag​.​
(13)
Retaining a nonnegligible gas density would add the factor 1−ρg​/ρl​. Let Vs​=4ag/(3CD​)​>0, so the upward slip is u1​−u2​=Vs​.
With constant phase densities, adding the two volume balances gives ∂x​[αu1​+(1−α)u2​]=0. The closed lower end fixes the total volume flux to zero. Solving it together with the slip relation yields
u1​=Vs​(1−α),u2​=−Vs​α,αt​+Vs​[α(1−α)]x​=0.​
(14)
Individual bubbles move upward for 0≤α<1, while small phase-fraction disturbances move at the characteristic speed q′(α)=Vs​(1−2α). The latter is negative when α>1/2: wave speed and bubble speed are different quantities.
A cleared region starts at the closed bottom, with αL​=0 and αR​=3/4. Its Rankine-Hugoniot condition speed is [q]/[α]=Vs​/4. The intended clearing solution is therefore
α(x,t)={0,3/4,​0<x<Vs​t/4,Vs​t/4<x<1,​0<t<4/Vs​.​
(15)
It is an entropy shock for the concave conservation law flux, since q′(0)=Vs​>Vs​/4>q′(3/4)=−Vs​/2. Its speed also equals the bubble speed on its uncleared side, so it follows the last layer of bubbles. Under this continuation the last bubble reaches the outlet when
tclear​=Vs​4​=ag12CD​​​,​
(16)
after which α=0 throughout the pipe.
There is a boundary qualification for this dense initial mixture. The characteristics in the uncleared state point into the pipe from the top. To continue the displayed solution all the way to the stated clearance time, its incoming top trace must remain α(1−,t)=3/4 until clearing. Initial characteristics alone cover the shock only until t=4/(3Vs​), when its backward right-state characteristic first meets the top boundary. Atmospheric pressure by itself is not a boundary condition for this scalar phase-fraction equation. Thus the displayed formula and printed clearance time give the intended maintained-trace branch; a different top phase-fraction closure could change the later evolution. This is the incoming boundary condition in dense bubble clearing, and is separate from the local derivation of the conservation law.
