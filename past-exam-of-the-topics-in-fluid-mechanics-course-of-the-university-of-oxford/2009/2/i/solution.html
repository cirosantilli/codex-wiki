Let χ1​(x,t) equal one in phase one and zero in phase two; its interface value is immaterial for area integrals. In the absence of phase change or interfacial mass transfer, the distributional phase mass conservation is ∂t​(χ1​ρ)+∇⋅(χ1​ρu)=0. For a cross-section S of area A, define
α=A1​∫S​χ1​dA,ρ1​=Aα1​∫S​χ1​ρdA,u1​=Aαρ1​∫S​χ1​ρux​dA​.​
(10)
Here the symbol ux​ in the integral denotes the axial velocity component, not a derivative. Integrating through an arbitrary axial slice and using zero normal flux at the rigid pipe wall gives
∂t​(ρ1​α)+∂x​(ρ1​αu1​)=0.​
(11)
The velocity is mass weighted, so the averaged mass flux factors exactly; an unweighted phase mean would not in general have this property. This is the cross-section averaged phase mass balance.
For phase two use χ2​=1−χ1​, volume fraction 1−α, and
ρ2​=A(1−α)∫S​χ2​ρdA​,u2​=A(1−α)ρ2​∫S​χ2​ρux​dA​.
(12)
Then ∂t​[ρ2​(1−α)]+∂x​[ρ2​(1−α)u2​]=0​. Mean quantities in a phase that is absent are unnecessary; the products giving conserved masses and fluxes remain the meaningful variables.
