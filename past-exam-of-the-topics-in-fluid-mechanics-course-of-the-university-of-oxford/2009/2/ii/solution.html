With constant phase densities, adding the two volume balances gives ∂x​[αu1​+(1−α)u2​]=0. The closed lower end fixes the total volume flux to zero. Solving it together with the slip relation yields

after which α=0 throughout the pipe.
There is a boundary qualification for this dense initial mixture. The characteristics in the uncleared state point into the pipe from the top. To continue the displayed solution all the way to the stated clearance time, its incoming top trace must remain α(1−,t)=3/4 until clearing. Initial characteristics alone cover the shock only until t=4/(3Vs​), when its backward right-state characteristic first meets the top boundary. Atmospheric pressure by itself is not a boundary condition for this scalar phase-fraction equation. Thus the displayed formula and printed clearance time give the intended maintained-trace branch; a different top phase-fraction closure could change the later evolution. This is the incoming boundary condition in dense bubble clearing, and is separate from the local derivation of the conservation law.
