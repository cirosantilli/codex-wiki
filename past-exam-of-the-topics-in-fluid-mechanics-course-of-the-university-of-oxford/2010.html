Choose x vertically upward. Let G(x,t) be the gas-occupied part of a pipe cross-section, of area αA. Define the phase mean mass density, mass-weighted velocity, second moment of velocity, and phase mean pressure by
ρˉ​g​=αA1​∫G​ρg​dA,vˉ=αAρˉ​g​∫G​ρg​vx​dA​,v2=αAρˉ​g​∫G​ρg​vx2​dA​,pˉ​g​=αA1​∫G​pg​dA.
(1)
The void fraction α is a volume fraction, not a mass fraction. The gas momentum in a slice of length dx is Aαρˉ​g​vˉdx; its advected momentum flux is Aαρˉ​g​v2. The two end pressures give the net force −A∂x​(αpˉ​g​)dx. Include every other vertical force on the gas, including interfacial forces, in Mg​dx. Newton's second law for this fixed slice, followed by division by Adx, therefore gives
∂t​(αρˉ​g​vˉ)+∂x​(αρˉ​g​v2)=Mg​/A−∂x​(αpˉ​g​).​
(2)
This is a cross-section averaged phase momentum balance. In particular, mass weighting makes the momentum and its flux exact averages; one must not replace v2 by vˉ2 without an additional closure. With liquid mean velocity uˉ, constant liquid mass density ρℓ​, and liquid second moment u2, the same calculation gives
∂t​[(1−α)ρℓ​uˉ]+∂x​[(1−α)ρℓ​u2]=Mℓ​/A−∂x​[(1−α)pˉ​ℓ​].​
(3)
With the common pressure p=pˉ​g​=pˉ​ℓ​, the specified pressure contributions to Mg​,Mℓ​ cancel the phase-fraction derivatives from the product rule. The remaining pressure forces per unit mixture volume are −αpx​ and −(1−α)px​.
Assume that the bubbles at a given height have the stated radius a. Their number per unit mixture volume is N=3α/(4πa3). The drag force on a rising bubble opposes its relative velocity, so multiplication of the single-bubble law by N gives the signed magnitude
Dv​=4a3CD​ρℓ​α​vˉ∣vˉ∣.
(4)
Gravity acts downward; the drag force on the liquid is the opposite of that on the gas. With no wall force,
Mg′​/A=−αρˉ​g​g−Dv​,Mℓ′​/A=−(1−α)ρℓ​g+Dv​.​
(5)
A bubble retains its mass in the absence of transfer between phases. Consequently a3ρˉ​g​=a03​ρ0​ and
a=a0​(ρ0​/ρˉ​g​)1/3.​
(6)
For clarity suppress bars on p,ρg​,v in the steady calculation. On a macroscopic variation length ℓ, gas inertia per unit volume scales like αρg​v2/ℓ, whereas the quadratic drag force scales like αρℓ​v2/a. Their ratio is O[(ρg​/ρℓ​)(a/ℓ)]. Thus ρg​=O(ρ0​)≪ρℓ​, together with the scale separation a≪ℓ inherent in this averaging model, justifies the leading-order neglect of gas inertia. Small reference density alone does not justify it in an arbitrarily short or strongly compressed region. Neglect gas weight at the same order, with the liquid fraction not vanishing. For a stationary liquid, summing the two momentum equations gives
px​=−g[αρg​+(1−α)ρℓ​]∼−ρℓ​g(1−α).
(7)
The leading gas balance is 0=−αpx​−Dv​. Substitution of the bubble-radius law yields
px​=−ρℓ​g(1−α)=−4a0​3CD​ρℓ​​(ρg​/ρ0​)1/3v∣v∣.​
(8)
The gas continuity equation, with constant pipe area and no mass exchange, is ∂t​(αρg​)+∂x​(αρg​v)=0. Hence the steady gas mass flux J=αρg​v is constant. For 0<α<1 the pressure equation selects v>0, and
v2=3CD​4a0​g​(1−α)(ρ0​/ρg​)1/3,J2=3CD​4a0​g​ρ01/3​α2(1−α)ρg5/3​.
(9)
Evaluate the latter constant at a reference point ρg​=ρ0​, α=α0​ and divide. The terminal-drift density law for compressible bubbles is
ρ0​ρg​​=[α2(1−α)α02​(1−α0​)​]3/5.​
(10)
The exponent 3/5 comes from combining mass conservation with the density dependence of bubble radius; no gas equation of state was needed.

Write ΔT=T0​−TB​, ΔC=C0​−CB​ and assume these scales are nonzero. The two reference liquidus relations imply ΔT=−mΔC. For the similarity variable η=z/(2κt​) and front h=2λκt​,
ηt​=−2tη​,ηz​=2κt​1​,h′=λκ/t​.
(11)
Thus a temperature profile T=T∗​+ΔTθ(η) has Tt​=−ΔTηθ′/(2t) and κTzz​=ΔTθ′′/(4t). Each heat equation becomes θ′′+2ηθ′=0. The solute diffusion equation becomes Cl′′+2LeηCl′=0, with
Le=κ/D.​
(12)
This Lewis number compares thermal and solutal diffusivities. Inside the mushy layer, Ts−TB​=−m(Cs−CB​) gives Cs=θs​. The wall and far-field conditions become θs(0)=0 and θl,Cl→0 as η→∞.
At the moving phase boundary, equality of temperatures gives θs=θl+(T∞​−TB​)/ΔT. Equality of concentrations gives Cs=Cl+1. Therefore the dimensionless parameters with the convention used here are
Rθ​=T0​−TB​T0​−T∞​​,RC​=C0​−CB​C0​​,S=k(T0​−TB​)ρLκ​.​
(13)
Neither concentration nor temperature offsets should be dropped when enforcing continuity. In particular Rθ​ can be negative. If k=ρcp​κ, then S=L/[cp​ΔT]: this is the inverse Stefan number, although the paper calls S its Stefan number.
To check every factor of two in the flux conditions, divide the thermal Stefan condition by kΔT/(2κt​), and the solute balance by DΔC/(2κt​). This gives
2ϕλS=θs′−θl′,2ϕLeλ(Cl+RC​)=−Cl′.​
(14)
Finally Tzl​=−mCzl​ and −mΔC=ΔT imply θl′=Cl′​. This is marginal equilibrium at a mush-liquid boundary. Together these calculations produce all the rescaled differential equations and all three interface balances.

Integrating Y′′+2aηY′=0 once gives Y′=Ke−aη2. A second integration expresses the solution using the error function when the value at zero is prescribed, and the complementary error function when decay at infinity is prescribed. Write θλ​=θs(λ) and take λ>0, Le>0. The wall condition and the interface value give
θs(η)=Cs(η)=θλ​erfλerfη​,0≤η≤λ.​
(15)
For the exterior liquid, continuity at the interface fixes θl(λ)=θλ​−1+Rθ​ and Cl(λ)=θλ​−1. Thus the two decaying similarity solutions are
θl(η)=(θλ​−1+Rθ​)erfcλerfcη​,Cl(η)=(θλ​−1)erfc(Le​λ)erfc(Le​η)​,η≥λ.​
(16)
The factor Le​ is required because solute and heat diffuse on different length scales. Both denominators are positive for a finite positive λ. These formulas use the differential equations, wall/far-field data and continuity; the remaining three flux conditions determine their unknown constants.

Introduce three positive functions of the unknown front parameter:
P(λ)=π​erfλe−λ2​,Q(λ)=π​erfcλe−λ2​,R(λ)=π​erfc(Le​λ)Le​e−Leλ2​.
(17)
Differentiating the error function profiles gives θs′=2Pθλ​, θl′=−2Q(θλ​−1+Rθ​) and Cl′=−2R(θλ​−1) at the interface. The three flux conditions are therefore exactly
ϕλSϕLeλ(θλ​−1+RC​)Q(θλ​−1+Rθ​)​=Pθλ​+Q(θλ​−1+Rθ​),=R(θλ​−1),=R(θλ​−1).​​
(18)
These undivided equations are the safest description of the constant-fraction mushy-layer similarity solution, including degenerate parameter choices. In the nondegenerate case the third equation first gives
θλ​=1+R(λ)−Q(λ)Q(λ)Rθ​​.​
(19)
The second then gives the solid fraction
ϕ=Leλ(θλ​−1+RC​)R(λ)(θλ​−1)​.​
(20)
Use the third equation to replace the Q term in the first, and eliminate ϕ. The front parameter is a positive root of the single transcendental equation
Le(θλ​−1+RC​)SR(λ)(θλ​−1)​−P(λ)θλ​−R(λ)(θλ​−1)=0,θλ​=1+R(λ)−Q(λ)Q(λ)Rθ​​.​
(21)
Then substitute back for θλ​,ϕ and h(t)=2λκt​. Since the physical parameters have not been assigned numbers, an implicit scalar equation is the appropriate determination of λ.
The divided formulas require R=Q and θλ​−1+RC​=0. If either vanishes, return to the boxed three-equation system. For example Le=1 gives R=Q, so the last equation demands Rθ​=0; otherwise this proposed similarity solution cannot exist. A physical branch must also have λ>0 and 0≤ϕ≤1. The algebra alone does not establish existence or uniqueness of such a branch for arbitrary parameters.
