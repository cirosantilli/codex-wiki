Take z positive away from the wall into the liquid, and write pa​ for atmospheric pressure. For a constant-mass density Newtonian fluid, the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equation and continuity equation are
ux​+wz​ρ(ut​+uux​+wuz​)ρ(wt​+uwx​+wwz​)​=0,=−px​+μ(uxx​+uzz​)+ρgsinα,=−pz​+μ(wxx​+wzz​)−ρgcosα.​
(1)
The stationary wall imposes the no-slip boundary condition and no-penetration boundary condition, u=w=0 at z=0. For a flat free surface with negligible air shear stress, the stress boundary condition is uz​=0, p=pa​ at z=h0​; the kinematic boundary condition is w=0 there.
For steady flow independent of x, these equations give w=0, μuzz​=−ρgsinα and pz​=−ρgcosα. Two integrations and the boundary conditions give the uniform gravity-driven Newtonian film:
uˉ=2μρgsinα​(2h0​z−z2),wˉ=0,pˉ​=pa​+ρgcosα(h0​−z).​
(2)
Its surface velocity is U0​=ρgh02​sinα/(2μ), while its mean velocity is 2U0​/3.

For A=2cotα/3+8Re/15>0, maximize the growth rate σ(k)=ε(Ak2−Sk4) over real wavenumbers. Its derivative is 2εk(A−2Sk2), so
∣k∗∣=2SA​​,σ∗=4SεA2​.​

Ω∗=LU0​​σ∗=8γ3ρgh0​sinαU0​A2​.
(24)
The corresponding dimensional wavenumber is k∗/L=3ρgsinαA/(4γ)​, independent of the arbitrary reference length L. Let d be the physical slope length. A disturbance convected at the leading kinematic wave speed 2U0​ has residence time d/(2U0​) and accumulates the growth exponent
N=2U0​Ω∗d​=16γ3ρgh0​sinαA2d​=23​ℓd​,ℓ=ρgh0​sinαA28γ​.​
(25)
Thus d≪ℓ implies N≪1, and the amplitude multiplier eN remains close to one while the disturbance passes down the slope. This is the requested convective amplification length criterion. With the exact coefficients used here, one e-fold of convected growth occurs at d=2ℓ/3; the stated much-less-than criterion is unaffected by this order-one factor. It is a transport-versus-growth estimate within the long-wave approximation, rather than a boundary-value stability theorem for every possible inlet and outlet condition.

Take z vertically upward and the porous skeleton stationary. Darcy's law and incompressibility are
u=−μk​(∇p+ρgez​),∇⋅u=0.​
(26)
Here u is the Darcy velocity, a volume flux per unit total cross-sectional area, including solid and void. If v is the liquid pore velocity and all the pore space is saturated and connected, then
u=ϕv.​
(27)
The porosity ϕ therefore enters storage and the conversion to pore velocity; it does not multiply the Darcy velocity again when calculating discharge.

i OurBigBook.com
For a constant-mass density incompressible flow with no imposed body force, the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equation is
ux​+vy​ρ(ut​+uux​+vuy​)ρ(vt​+uvx​+vvy​)​=0,=−px​+μ(uxx​+uyy​),=−py​+μ(vxx​+vyy​).​​
(50)
A conservative body force, if retained, may be incorporated into a modified pressure. Using scales L,U0​,L/U0​ and ρU02​ gives
Re=μρU0​L​=νU0​L​,ut​+u⋅∇u=−∇p+Re−1∇2u.​
(51)
Here ν=μ/ρ is the molecular kinematic viscosity. At high Reynolds number, inertia and the pressure gradient dominate on the outer length scale, while molecular viscous diffusion is small there. Thin boundary layers, narrow shear flows and fine turbulent scales can still have sufficiently large velocity gradients for viscous effects to matter. High Reynolds number permits instability and turbulence, but does not alone prove that every flow is turbulent. Molecular viscous dissipation remains essential at the small end of the turbulent cascade.

(55)

The product rule and fluctuation incompressibility give the pointwise identity
(ui′​uj′​),j​=uj′​ui,j′​+ui′​uj,j′​=uj′​ui,j′​.
(56)
Averaging therefore gives
uj′​ui,j′​​=(ui′​uj′​),j​​=∂j​ui′​uj′​​.​
(57)
The fluctuation term is consequently the divergence of a Reynolds stress. The eddy viscosity model represents this momentum transport through a coefficient multiplying the mean strain-rate tensor:
−ρui′​uj′​​≃μT​(Ui,j​+Uj,i​)
(58)
for its shear or deviatoric part. For example, −ρu′v′≃μT​Uy​ in a thin parallel shear flow. The dynamic viscosity μT​ is an effective flow-dependent transport coefficient, rather than a molecular material property. Large eddies carry linear momentum between regions of different mean velocity.
There is a necessary qualification to the printed tensor equation (4). Taking its trace literally gives 0=−ρui′​ui′​​, which would exclude nonzero turbulence. A consistent deviatoric eddy-viscosity closure in d fluctuating dimensions is
−ρui′​uj′​​=μT​(Ui,j​+Uj,i​)−d2ρet​​δij​,et​=21​uk′​uk′​​.
(59)
The isotropic term is absorbed into the effective pressure Peff​=P+2ρet​/d. Here d=2 for a strictly two-dimensional fluctuation model. The subsequent equations use P for this effective pressure, or equivalently use (4) as a shear-stress closure. This preserves the intended thin-layer model without asserting an impossible full covariance identity.
For turbulent motions of scale l and velocity scale ul​, the estimate μT​∼ρul​l gives μT​/μ∼ul​l/ν. It is much greater than one on energetic large scales and in the inertial range well above the Kolmogorov length scale. At the molecular dissipation scales it is no longer large, and molecular transport also dominates very near a smooth solid wall.

Let δ≪L be a representative mixing-layer thickness, ϵm​=δ/L, and let Us​ be a representative streamwise velocity. Use the scales
x∼L,y∼δ,U∼Us​,V∼ϵm​Us​,P∼ρUs2​.
(60)
The transverse velocity scale follows from the mean continuity equation. For turbulent transverse transport to balance streamwise inertia,
LUs2​​∼δ2νT​Us​​,νT​∼LUs​δ2​.
(61)
With this ordering, the streamwise eddy-stress divergence is
∂x​(2νT​Ux​)+∂y​[νT​(Uy​+Vx​)]=∂y​(νT​Uy​)+O(ϵm2​Us2​/L).
(62)
Thus transverse diffusion of streamwise linear momentum is retained, while streamwise diffusion and the Vx​ contribution are smaller by ϵm2​. Molecular kinematic viscosity is neglected where νT​≫ν.
In the transverse momentum equation, inertia and the retained eddy stresses have scale ϵm​Us2​/L, whereas an order-one transverse pressure variation would give Py​/ρ∼Us2​/(ϵm​L). Hence the leading dimensionless transverse balance is Py​=0. The resulting boundary layer equations for the planar turbulent mixing layer are
Ux​+Vy​UUx​+VUy​0​=0,=−ρ−1Px​+∂y​(νT​Uy​),=−ρ−1Py​.​​
(63)
Here νT​=μT​/ρ. The effective pressure convention explained in part (a)(iii) is understood. Since the outer streams have uniform horizontal velocities, their inviscid momentum equations give Px​=0; choosing the far-field gauge pressure as zero gives P=0 throughout this approximation.

For the approximate similarity profile, F′=Aerf(ξ/2​), and therefore
V=−ΔUCS​[Be−ξ2/2+D],V(±∞)=−ΔUCS​D.​
(75)
For the prescribed undisturbed horizontal streams, a suitable choice is D=0, which gives zero transverse velocity at both far-field limits. Then V=−ΔUCS​Be−ξ2/2 inside the layer. The nonzero internal crossflow is consistent with the streamwise widening of the velocity transition.
Another possible convention is to make the line y=0 a streamline, imposing V(0)=0. This requires D=−B, equivalently F(0)=0, and gives V(±∞)=ΔUCS​B. It therefore requires a uniform outer crossflow relative to the coordinates used for the centred error function profile. A single constant cannot impose both zero far-field crossflow and V(0)=0 unless ΔU=0. The physical lateral boundary conditions, or the chosen material centreline, determine which convention is appropriate.
