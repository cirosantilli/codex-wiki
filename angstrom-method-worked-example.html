Ångström method worked example

 ... Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Thermal conduction Thermal diffusivity Ångström periodic heating method
OurBigBook.com Words: 254
Consider an original stipulated model, not measured material data: thermal diffusivity α=10−4m2s−1, period P=100s, driven temperature amplitude Θ0​=4K, and thermometers at x1​=0.020m and x2​=0.050m. Without side losses, a=b=17.7245m−1 and thermal penetration depth δ=0.05642m. Thus
A1​=2.80612K,A2​=1.64883K,D=Δϕ=0.531736,Δt=8.46284s.
(22)
The two no-loss estimates and the combined estimate all return 10−4m2s−1.
Now retain the same thermal diffusivity and drive but introduce λ=0.010s−1. The exact complex wavenumber gives a=19.1863m−1 and b=16.3742m−1, hence
A1​DΔt​=2.72527K,=0.575588,=7.81809s,​A2​Δϕαcombined​​=1.53262K,=0.491225rad,=10−4m2s−1.​
(23)
Using attenuation alone with the no-loss formula would give 8.5343×10−5m2s−1; using lag alone would give 1.17174×10−4m2s−1. Their disagreement is informative. If an independent volumetric heat capacity measurement gives ρcp​=2.0×106Jm−3K−1, the combined estimate implies thermal conductivity k=200Wm−1K−1 and H=2.0×104Wm−3K−1.
The exact complex amplitudes can be checked independently by discretizing αθ′′−(λ+iω)θ=0 and solving the resulting linear system with a tridiagonal matrix. Halving the spatial step quarters the discrepancy, as expected for centered finite differences. Separately fitting the real time traces to a constant plus sine and cosine terms recovers both amplitudes and their lag; this also checks the sign convention used in the combined estimate.
