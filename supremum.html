Supremum (sup)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Infimum and supremum
OurBigBook.com Words: 918 Articles: 4
For a nonempty set A⊆R, a real number s is its supremum precisely when it is an upper bound and no smaller real number is an upper bound. It is unique: two least upper bounds must each be no greater than the other.
For A=(0,1), every number at least one is an upper bound, and none smaller than one bounds the whole set. Thus supA=1, although 1∈/A. For A=[0,1], that same supremum is also the maximum. The approximation property of a supremum supplies witnesses arbitrarily close to an unattained upper edge.
