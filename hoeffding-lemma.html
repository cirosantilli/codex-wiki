Hoeffding lemma (logEeλ(X−EX)≤λ2(b−a)2/8)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability inequality Concentration inequality Hoeffding inequality
OurBigBook.com Words: 330
The Hoeffding lemma controls the entire centered moment-generating function using only an interval width. For an essentially bounded real random variable X with finite deterministic bounds a≤X≤b almost surely, every real λ satisfies
ψ(λ):=logEeλ(X−EX)≤8λ2(b−a)2​.​
(28)
Equivalently, Eeλ(X−EX)≤eλ2(b−a)2/8. This single-random variable statement needs no independence assumption; independence enters when applying it to the factors for a sum in the Hoeffding inequality. Both signs of λ are allowed, and equality holds at λ=0 or when a=b.[ref]
In the convention logEeλY≤λ2σ2/2 for a centered sub-Gaussian random variable Y, the Hoeffding lemma supplies σ2=(b−a)2/4. This upper parameter need not equal the actual variance. For a Bernoulli random variable B of success probability p,
logEeλ(B−p)=log(1−p+peλ)−pλ≤λ2/8.
(29)
The actual variance is p(1−p), whereas the supplied parameter is 1/4 for every p. This explains part of the conservatism visible in Hoeffding sample size for Bernoulli frequencies.
A short proof exposes why the constant is 1/8. Put Y=X−EX and define exponential tilting by
dPλ​=eλY−ψ(λ)dP.
(30)
The tilted probability measure has the same interval of possible values, and differentiating the cumulant-generating function gives ψ′′(λ)=VarPλ​​(Y). Boundedness justifies these derivatives for every finite λ. Under any such tilted law, Popoviciu's inequality on variances gives ψ′′(λ)≤(b−a)2/4: the variance is no larger than the expected value of the squared distance from the interval midpoint, itself at most (b−a)2/4. Since ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0, the integral form of Taylor's theorem yields
ψ(λ)=λ2∫01​(1−s)ψ′′(sλ)ds≤λ2(b−a)2/8.
(31)
This expression handles either sign of λ without changing the argument.
The constant 1/8 cannot be reduced uniformly over all essentially bounded real random variables. Equal endpoint probabilities at a and b give Y=±(b−a)/2, a scaled Rademacher random variable, and
limλ→0​λ2logEeλY​=2Var(Y)​=8(b−a)2​.
(32)
Thus the sharpness is already forced near zero by the variance; the bound need not be equality at nonzero parameters for a particular probability distribution.
