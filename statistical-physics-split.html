Statistical physics

 Home Physics Branches of physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 169k Articles: 2k
Statistical physics explains collective behavior using probability distributions over microscopic possibilities. Its central surprise is that incomplete microscopic knowledge can still produce precise predictions for internal energy, entropy and heat capacity. The bridge is statistical mechanics: specify the available microstates, assign their probabilities, then compute the expected values of measured quantities.
A two-level system already displays the idea. Warming it makes its higher energy more likely; its heat capacity first rises, then falls as the two probabilities approach equality. With two distinguishable units, merely counting microstates explains why the middle energy has twice the state degeneracy. These small examples expose the machinery before the much larger sums needed for a photon gas or an Ising model.
The appeal is economical: the same microscopic energies and probabilities explain both an average reading and its variance. A measurement's spread therefore becomes useful physical information rather than an inconvenience to discard.
