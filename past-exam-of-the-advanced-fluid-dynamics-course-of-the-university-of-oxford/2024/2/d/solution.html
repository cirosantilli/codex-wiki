The cross term vanishes because the conditional mean of v is zero. In particular, set K=0 and take the equilibrium configuration probability density ψeq​∝e−HR2/(2kB​T). The left side is 3nζkB​T/m>0, while nHC:K=0. This is a counterexample even at thermal equilibrium, so no rearrangement of the earlier conformation tensor equation can prove the printed statement.
The discrepancy is thermal bead drag and heat-bath power: gross viscous loss of microscopic thermal motion is replenished by the thermal bath. It is not identical to mechanical polymer stress power. Moreover, R˙ in the earlier configuration continuity equation is a probability current velocity, including a distribution-dependent osmotic drift; it cannot simultaneously be identified with an independently sampled centered Maxwellian velocity.

The polymer stress tensor σp​=n(HC−kB​TI) has σp​:K=nHC:K in an incompressible flow. It stores or releases free energy as well as dissipating it, so the total stress-work term in part a need not itself be a purely dissipative quantity for this viscoelastic material. Stress power equals D only for stationary free energy, and D uses the probability-current slip. Neither condition turns the printed independent-Maxwellian average into that expression.
