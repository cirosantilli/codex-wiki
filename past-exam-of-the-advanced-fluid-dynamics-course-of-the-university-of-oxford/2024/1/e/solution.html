Conversely, a+D>0 also makes their sum positive and therefore gives two positive squared frequencies. A fixed nonzero magnetic mode is exponentially unstable for k2vA2​+D<0, oscillatory for k2vA2​+D>0, and marginal at equality. Stability against every allowed nonzero wavenumber in the unbounded ideal model requires D≥0; finite-domain restrictions can remove the unstable band.
For Φ=−GM/R, the equilibrium relation gives Ω2=GM/R3, so D=−3Ω2 and κ2=Ω2. Hence the Keplerian disk has the magnetorotational instability band
0<k2vA2​<3Ω2.​
(14)
Without the magnetic field, the polynomial becomes ω2(ω2−κ2)=0. The Rayleigh centrifugal stability criterion instead requires κ2>0, equivalently d(R4Ω2)/dR>0. A Keplerian disk satisfies that hydrodynamic criterion. Magnetic coupling therefore destabilizes a disk that has a restoring hydrodynamic epicyclic oscillation; the field couples angular-momentum exchange between elements, rather than acting only as an isolated restoring spring.
