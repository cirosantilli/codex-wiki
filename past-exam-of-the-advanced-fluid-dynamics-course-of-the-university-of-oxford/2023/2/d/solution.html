Take z positive upward, let sphere A's centre be Z(t)ez​, and write its downward velocity as −Uez​, with Z˙=−U. At B's centre the displacement from A is r=ℓex​−Zez​. The vorticity of translating-sphere Stokes flow therefore gives
ω∞​=−2(ℓ2+Z2)3/23aUℓ​ey​.
(23)
B is held in translation but has zero applied torque. By Faxén's rotational law, it rotates with half the incident vorticity:
ΩB​=−4(ℓ2+Z2)3/23aUℓ​ey​.
(24)
Integrating the rotation induced by a passing Stokes sphere along the complete path cancels U:
Δθ=43aℓ​ey​∫+∞−∞​(ℓ2+Z2)3/2dZ​=−2ℓ3a​ey​.​
(25)
Its magnitude is 3a/(2ℓ) radians, with direction about the negative y axis by the right-hand rule. All instantaneous rotations have this same axis, so the integrated angular velocity gives the net angle without a noncommuting-rotation correction. The result is independent of B's radius in the prescribed incident-flow calculation.
The inequality ℓ>a+b ensures no overlap. It does not by itself make mutual hydrodynamic interactions negligible: the stated flow is the isolated-A solution, and a held finite B also disturbs A's flow. Thus the displayed angle is the result for that supplied incident flow, or the leading one-way approximation to separated bodies. A fully coupled two-sphere calculation uses the method of reflections for Stokes flow or an exact pair resistance solution.
