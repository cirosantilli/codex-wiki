In this question, the coefficient multiplying ∇2B is the magnetic diffusivity. Denote it ηm​; it is the paper's η, with dimensions of length squared per time. If ηOhm​ denotes electrical resistivity, then ηm​=ηOhm​/μ0​. The two conventions must not be interchanged.
For an imposed real positive ω, retain the momentum equation and use
−iωρ0​v=μ0​ikB0​​b,(−iω+ηm​k2)b=ikB0​v.
(10)
Elimination gives the exact Resistive damping of an Alfvén wave relation
ω2+iηm​ωk2−vA2​k2=0,k2=vA2​−iηm​ωω2​.​
(11)
Here k is complex because the driving frequency is real. Introduce the dimensionless diffusion parameter ϵ=ηm​ω/vA2​≪1. For propagation and attenuation along +z, choose the square-root branch with Rek,Imk>0. The spatial damping of a driven Alfvén wave is
k=vA​ω​(1−iϵ)−1/2=vA​ω​(1+2iϵ​+O(ϵ2)).​
(12)
With the convention ei(kz−ωt), the amplitude decays as e−Imkz. Define its e-folding length LA​=1/Imk and the wavelength λ=2π/Rek. Then
LA​∼ηm​ω22vA3​​,λLA​​∼πϵ1​.​
(13)
If damping length instead refers to wave energy, the squared amplitude decays twice as fast: LE​=LA​/2 and LE​/λ∼1/(2πϵ). Stating the convention avoids a factor-of-two ambiguity. The temporal damping rate ηm​k2/2 at fixed real k is a different specification from this imposed-frequency problem.
