Let Sa​ be the sphere surface, ns​ point from the sphere into the fluid, and write
w=uS​−u∞​,τ=σS​−σ∞​.
(36)
Because the prescribed exterior body forces are unchanged, they cancel on subtraction: the disturbance is a homogeneous Stokes flow in the exterior domain. On the sphere the no-slip boundary condition is
w=V+ωs​×x−u∞​,
(37)
allowing an arbitrary sphere rotation ωs​.
Use as auxiliary flow the translating-sphere solution of part b, with arbitrary test velocity U. Its surface velocity is U and its surface traction is −3μU/(2a). Applying the Lorentz reciprocal theorem on the exterior domain gives, after cancelling the common inner-normal minus sign,
∫Sa​​w⋅σ∗ns​dS=∫Sa​​U⋅τns​dS.
(38)
The outer surface terms vanish for decaying disturbances: velocities are at most O(r−1), stresses O(r−2), and the integrated cross-work is O(r−1).
There are no body forces inside the sphere in the original ambient problem. Thus the smooth ambient stress tensor has zero divergence there, and
∫Sa​​σ∞​ns​dS=∫r<a​∇⋅σ∞​dV=0.
(39)
Also ∫Sa​​xdS=0, so the rotation term drops out of the left side. Consequently the reciprocal identity reduces to
−2a3μ​U⋅[4πa2V−∫Sa​​u∞​dS]=U⋅FH​.
(40)
Since U is arbitrary, the result before imposing mechanical balance is
FH​=−6πμa[V−4πa21​∫Sa​​u∞​dS].​
(41)
For a freely suspended sphere with negligible inertia and no applied external force, FH​=0. Its sphere velocity as a surface average of ambient Stokes flow is therefore
V=4πa21​∫∣x∣=a​u∞​(x)dS.​
(42)
The implicit force-free condition is essential. More generally, an external force Fext​ gives V=⟨u∞​⟩Sa​​+Fext​/(6πμa). For example, an externally pulled sphere in the zero ambient flow has nonzero velocity, contrary to an unrestricted surface-average-only claim. No torque-free assumption is needed for this translational identity, because the auxiliary surface traction is constant.
