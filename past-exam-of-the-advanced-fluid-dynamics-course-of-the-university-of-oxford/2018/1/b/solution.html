Thus for ϵ=∣k∥​∣/k⊥​≪1, energy travels almost along the rotation axis, with a small horizontal component. By contrast, the normal-to-phase-front phase velocity vph​=ωk/k2 is almost horizontal. Indeed k⋅vg​=0, so these phase and group directions are perpendicular. Specifying which meaning of propagation is intended matters for an inertial wave.
For the wave polarization, incompressibility and the y equation in part a give
ux​=−k⊥​k∥​​uz​,uy​=k⊥​σik​uz​.​
(7)
The horizontal component parallel to k⊥​ is small, but ∣uy​∣≃∣uz​∣: the other horizontal and vertical components are in quadrature. More exactly, introduce the orthonormal transverse basis
e1​=y​,e2​=k×y​=k1​(−k∥​,0,k⊥​).
(8)
Then u=A(e1​−σie2​). The physical velocity at a fixed point traces a circle in the plane perpendicular to k; its handedness reverses between branches. In the anisotropic limit this plane is nearly vertical. The group velocity and polarization of an inertial wave therefore have quite different geometric roles.
