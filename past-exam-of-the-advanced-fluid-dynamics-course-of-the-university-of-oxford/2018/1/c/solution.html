Let L⊥​=k⊥−1​ and let U be the common size of horizontal and vertical velocity perturbations, as found in part b. The linear inertial wave time and nonlinear advection time are
τw−1​∼2Ωk⊥​∣k∥​∣​,τnl−1​∼k⊥​U.
(9)
The critical balance in a rapidly rotating fluid is therefore
ϵ=k⊥​∣k∥​∣​∼2Ωk⊥​U​≪1,ω∼k⊥​U∼2Ωϵ.​
(10)
The Rossby number is of order ϵ. Horizontal geostrophic flow balances the leading pressure gradient against Coriolis acceleration, so
u⊥​∼u∥​∼U,δp∼2ΩL⊥​U∼U2/ϵ,k∥​∼ϵk⊥​.​
(11)
These are dimensionally comparable statements, rather than comparisons of a dimensional frequency with a dimensional velocity. Equivalently, use L⊥​ and (2Ω)−1 as units. Then
k⊥​L⊥​=O(1),k∥​L⊥​=O(ϵ),2Ωω​=O(ϵ),2ΩL⊥​u⊥​​,2ΩL⊥​u∥​​=O(ϵ),(2ΩL⊥​)2δp​=O(ϵ).
(12)
Thus rotation stays O(1) in these units, while the motions evolve on the slower O(ϵ−1) time. Vertical advection u∥​∂z​ is smaller than horizontal advection by ϵ.
Write u⊥​=u⊥(0)​+u⊥(1)​+⋯, with u⊥(1)​=O(ϵU), and p=p0​+p1​+⋯. At leading horizontal order,
0=−∇⊥​p0​+2Ωu⊥(0)​×z.
(13)
Define the stream function Φ=p0​/(2Ω), including its horizontally uniform part in this convention. Then
u⊥(0)​=z×∇⊥​Φ=(−∂y​Φ,∂x​Φ),∇⊥​⋅u⊥(0)​=0.​
(14)
The next-order incompressibility condition is not zero horizontal divergence:
∇⊥​⋅u⊥(1)​=−∂z​u∥​.​
(15)
This term has size ϵk⊥​U and is essential: its product with the large rotation rate is a leading contribution to the vertical vorticity equation.
To see that contribution explicitly, write D⊥​=∂t​+u⊥(0)​⋅∇⊥​. At the first horizontal inertial order,
D⊥​u⊥(0)​=−∇⊥​p1​+2Ωu⊥(1)​×z.
(16)
Taking its vertical curl eliminates p1​. The two-dimensional advection identity and the two divergence orders give
D⊥​ζ=−2Ω∇⊥​⋅u⊥(1)​=2Ω∂z​u∥​,ζ=(∇⊥​×u⊥(0)​)z​=∇⊥2​Φ.
(17)
Equivalently the ageostrophic flow satisfies
u⊥(1)​=2Ωz×[D⊥​u⊥(0)​+∇⊥​p1​]​,
(18)
whose divergence is −D⊥​ζ/(2Ω). This explicitly verifies that the next-order divergence has the required sign.
The vertical momentum equation, at the same slow order, is
D⊥​u∥​=−∂z​p0​=−2Ω∂z​Φ.
(19)
Finally u⊥(0)​⋅∇⊥​f={Φ,f}, where the canonical two-dimensional Poisson bracket is {f,g}=fx​gy​−fy​gx​. The reduced equations for an anisotropic rotating fluid are
∂t​∇⊥2​Φ+{Φ,∇⊥2​Φ}∂t​u∥​+{Φ,u∥​}​=2Ω∂z​u∥​,=−2Ω∂z​Φ.​​
(20)
The omitted vertical-advection, ageostrophic-advection and relative-vorticity stretching terms are smaller by O(ϵ) in the equations retained here. Linearizing these equations recovers ω2=4Ω2k∥2​/k⊥2​, the anisotropic limit of part a.
