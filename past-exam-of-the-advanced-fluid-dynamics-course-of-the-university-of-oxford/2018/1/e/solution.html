For a uniform axial magnetic field, the anisotropic Shear Alfvén wave is predominantly horizontally polarized, whereas the pseudo-Alfvén wave is predominantly vertically polarized. Without rotation both have characteristic frequency ωA​∼∣k∥​∣vA​. Pressure projection makes the effective Coriolis acceleration couple these two transverse wave polarization states at the inertial-wave rate
ωI​∼2Ωk⊥​∣k∥​∣​.
(23)
Thus the useful comparison is not Ω alone, nor anisotropy alone, but
ωA​ωI​​∼k⊥​vA​2Ω​≪1.​
(24)
In this limit rotational coupling of shear and pseudo-Alfvén waves is negligible on an Alfvén propagation time, so the two familiar linear polarizations are decoupled to leading order. The factor k∥​ cancels: simply making both disturbances more anisotropic does not by itself remove their relative rotational coupling.
For a nonlinear reduction one must also make the rotational mixing slow compared with the nonlinear evolution being retained. Under Alfvénic critical balance, ∣k∥​∣vA​∼k⊥​U, the same inequality gives ωI​≪k⊥​U. Rapid rotation relative to the horizontal nonlinear rate can therefore coexist with leading Alfvénic decoupling in the window
k⊥​U≪2Ω≪k⊥​vA​.​
(25)
High electrical conductivity makes magnetic diffusion slow, and the corresponding inviscid approximation is also assumed. If 2Ω≳k⊥​vA​, rotational and magnetic restoring effects are comparable and the modes are mixed magneto-Coriolis disturbances; in the rotation-dominated limit they separate into fast inertial-like and slow magnetostrophic branches, rather than into independently evolving shear and pseudo-Alfvén polarizations.
At finite amplitude, independence is generally one-way in the usual anisotropic ideal magnetohydrodynamics reduction: the Alfvénic subsystem has no leading feedback from pseudo-Alfvénic perturbations, but it can still advect them. The small rotational-coupling condition removes an additional two-way coupling, rather than all nonlinear advection.
This is an asymptotic finite-timescale statement. The two nonrotating Alfvén polarizations are degenerate, so even a weak nonzero rotational coupling can rotate their superposition over the much longer time ωI−1​. Exact decoupling for arbitrarily long times does not follow from a small ratio alone. The strictly k∥​=0 nonpropagating limit is separate.
