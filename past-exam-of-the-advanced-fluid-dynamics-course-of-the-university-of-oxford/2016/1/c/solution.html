Equality is marginal, rather than exponential growth. The pressure anisotropy must have negative sign; its absolute magnitude alone is insufficient.
For the intergalactic plasma, a negative relative pressure anisotropy of −10−2 reaches this fluid threshold only for β>200. In particular, the literal pair β=100 and relative pressure anisotropy −0.01 is stable because the threshold is −0.02. Since the quoted numbers are approximate and allow larger plasma beta, this weakly magnetized plasma is susceptible, rather than necessarily unstable at every quoted value. For example, β=300 and relative pressure anisotropy −0.01 are unstable. Positive pressure anisotropy does not excite this branch.
For the fusion-device plasma, β∼10−2 requires a negative relative pressure anisotropy below roughly −200, whereas its magnitude is only about 0.1. The stabilizing magnetic tension dominates, so it is not susceptible to this fluid firehose instability under the stated conditions. Indeed, nonnegative perpendicular pressure requires the relative pressure anisotropy to be no smaller than −1; at such a small plasma beta even this extreme cannot satisfy the threshold.
