Average the equations in part b and make exactly the prescribed scale-separation approximation: the retained perturbations vary on lengths much greater than ℓ, and perturbations at the tangle scale are omitted. Products of two retained perturbations can then be neglected. Writing v=δu, m=δM and π=δp, the linearized equations are
∂t​vi​=−∂i​π+∂j​mij​,∂t​mij​=vA2​(∂j​vi​+∂i​vj​),∂i​vi​=0.
(9)
No unmodelled small-scale response has been inserted into this closure.
Introduce the fluid displacement ξ by v=∂t​ξ and choose ∇⋅ξ=0. Integrating the magnetic second-moment tensor equation gives
mij​=vA2​(∂i​ξj​+∂j​ξi​)+sij​(r),​
(10)
where s is a time-independent integration tensor set by the initial stress. For a pure oscillatory normal mode, s=0. Its restoring force is therefore vA2​∇2ξ: the isotropic magnetic tangle has the same transverse response as an incompressible elastic medium with shear modulus ρ0​vA2​.
For a plane wave proportional to ei(k⋅r−ωt), with ω=0, incompressibility gives k⋅v=0. The tensor equation and magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation become
mij​=−ωvA2​​(ki​vj​+kj​vi​),−iωvi​=−iki​π+ikj​mij​.
(11)
Dotting the second equation with k yields π=0 for k=0, since ki​kj​mij​=0. Substitution then gives
ω2=vA2​k2,mij​=ivA2​(ki​ξ​j​+kj​ξ​i​),k⋅ξ​=0.​
(12)
There are two independent transverse polarizations. The phase speed is vA​ in every direction, and the two signs of ω give group velocities ±vA​k. These magnetoelastic waves in an isotropic magnetic tangle exist because a shear deformation distorts the second moment of the tangled magnetic field, producing a restoring magnetic tension even though its mean is zero.
For comparison, a uniform magnetic field with Alfvén velocity B0​ gives the incompressible Alfvén wave relation ω2=(k⋅B0​)2. Its frequency depends on the direction of k, and energy propagates along B0​. Here the averaged restoring response is isotropic: there is no preferred mean-field direction. Both systems resist transverse deformation through magnetic tension, rather than through compressive sound.
The propagating branch is not the entire perturbation space. For example, v=0, mij​=f(r)δij​ and π=f(r) solve the linearized equations without propagation, for any small, slowly varying f. Such a static stress perturbation in magnetoelastic wave equations balances a pressure gradient. Thus the requested wave conclusion applies to the oscillatory branch; it must not exclude stationary initial stress perturbations.
