Physical cosmology

 Home Physics Branches of physics Cosmology
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
Physical cosmology connects the universe's history to the measurable behaviour of its contents. The Friedmann equations describe cosmic expansion; atomic physics, radiation pressure and gravity determine what that expanding universe looks like. A particularly revealing example is the route from cosmological recombination through the photon-baryon fluid to the Cosmic microwave background acoustic peaks. The universe supplies the initial conditions and the laboratory; the prediction must still pass through a model of the telescope before it can be compared with observations.
The practical reward is that one sky contains several different tests. The Cosmic microwave background power spectrum carries information about the sound horizon, the cosmological baryon density and cosmic geometry. These are related quantities with different observational signatures. Angular sound-horizon degeneracy explains why measuring a characteristic angle alone cannot count the universe's baryons.
