Archimedean property (∀x∈R ∃n∈N: n>x)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Real number
OurBigBook.com Words: 40
The Archimedean property says that the natural numbers are unbounded in the real numbers. In particular, for every positive real number a there is a positive integer n with 1/n<a. Otherwise a purported upper bound for all natural numbers contradicts the least-upper-bound property by translating that bound by one.
