Multiplicative function (f(1)=1,f(mn)=f(m)f(n) if gcd(m,n)=1)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 13
An arithmetic function f is a multiplicative function when f(1)=1 and f(mn)=f(m)f(n) for all coprime integers m,n>0, meaning their greatest common divisor is 1. The condition f(1)=1 excludes the identically zero function. This definition makes no assertion about a pair whose greatest common divisor exceeds 1. [ref]
For the divisor function, the integer divisors of 2 are 1,2, those of 3 are 1,3, and those of 6 are 1,2,3,6. Hence
τ(6)=4=τ(2)τ(3)=2⋅2.
(11)
Here 2 and 3 are coprime integers. In contrast, τ(4)=3=4=τ(2)2, because the integer divisors of 4 are 1,2,4. Thus the divisor function is a multiplicative function but not a completely multiplicative function.
The practical gain is that prime factorization reduces all values to values at prime powers. For distinct prime numbers p1​,…,pr​ and positive exponents a1​,…,ar​,
n=∏k=1r​pkak​​⟹f(n)=∏k=1r​f(pkak​​).
(12)
For instance, f(12)=f(4)f(3) since 4 and 3 are coprime integers; replacing f(4) by f(2)2 additionally requires a completely multiplicative function. The short proof repeatedly applies the defining rule to prime powers belonging to different prime numbers, which are coprime integers.
