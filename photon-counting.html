Photon counting

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Quantum optics
OurBigBook.com Words: 594 Articles: 4
Photon counting registers discrete detector events and their times. A beam splitter sends the selected light to two photodetectors; a coincidence circuit or time-recording system compares the outputs. With a vacuum input at the unused optical port, splitter fractions R,T and independent quantum efficiencies η1​,η2​, a selected input photon reaches the two recorded channels with probabilities p1​=Rη1​ and p2​=Tη2​. Conditional on input occupation N, each pair of distinct photons contributes p1​p2​ to the cross-channel pair moment. Therefore
⟨K1​⟩=p1​μ,⟨K2​⟩=p2​μ,⟨K1​K2​⟩=p1​p2​⟨N(N−1)⟩.
(10)
Their normalized ratio recovers g(2)(0). This assumes number-resolving counts, or sufficiently dilute gates for threshold detectors to approximate them. One photon cannot create both channel counts in this ideal model.
