Stabilizer subgroup (Gx​=StabG​(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Group theory Group action
OurBigBook.com Words: 358 Articles: 3
For a group action of a group G on a set X, the stabilizer subgroup of x∈X consists of the transformations that leave x fixed:
Gx​=StabG​(x)={g∈G:g⋅x=x}≤G.
(50)
It is a subgroup: the identity element fixes x, products of transformations fixing x fix x, and the inverse element of a transformation fixing x also fixes x. A stabilizer subgroup is a subset of G, whereas the orbit of a group action is a subset of X. See Ackerman's algebra notes, definition 0.4.1.2.
For the square group actions defined under group action, direct calculation gives
(D4​)A​={e,s},(D4​)AB​={e,rs},(D4​)AC​={e,r2,s,r2s},(D4​)O​=D4​.
(51)
The reflection s fixes A and C individually. The reflection rs preserves the unoriented side of a polygon AB by exchanging its two vertices of a polygon. The half-turn rotation r2 exchanges the two ends of the unoriented diagonal of a polygon AC while preserving that diagonal of a polygon. Thus fixing a side of a polygon or diagonal of a polygon as an object does not mean fixing every point on it; the latter condition uses a pointwise stabilizer. The four stabilizer subgroups have group orders 2,2,4,8, matching group orbit sizes 4,4,2,1 through the orbit-stabilizer theorem.
A stabilizer subgroup records the transformations still available after imposing a constraint. Fixing a vertex of a polygon of the square leaves two choices; fixing its centroid leaves all eight. In a faithful group action, a stabilizer subgroup can still contain a nonidentity transformation, as s∈(D4​)A​ shows: a faithful group action requires that only the identity element fix every point of X simultaneously, not that every individual stabilizer subgroup be trivial. The two-point cyclic group example under group action has both stabilizer subgroups equal to the group kernel {0,2} of its associated group homomorphism.
