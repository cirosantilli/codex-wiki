CKM unitarity triangle (Vud​Vub∗​+Vcd​Vcb∗​+Vtd​Vtb∗​=0)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Quantum field theory Standard Model Standard Model fermion Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix
OurBigBook.com Words: 212
Column orthogonality of the CKM matrix gives three complex numbers
a=Vud​Vub∗​,b=Vcd​Vcb∗​,c=Vtd​Vtb∗​,a+b+c=0.
(52)
Drawing these as successive vectors in the complex plane produces a closed triangle. Its area is 21​∣Im(ab∗)∣=∣J∣/2, because unitarity identifies this imaginary quartet with a signed Jarlskog invariant. A nonzero area therefore detects physical CP violation for nondegenerate quark masses.
For a worked example, choose the exact parametrization of the CKM matrix with s12​=1/4, s23​=1/10, s13​=1/100 and δ=π/3. These are illustrative choices, not fitted quark mixing parameters. They give J≃2.085597×10−4. The unscaled triangle has area approximately 1.042799×10−4.
Divide the closure relation by −b, assuming b=0, and define the complex number
z=ρˉ​+iηˉ​=−ba​.
(53)
The normalized triangle has vertices 0,1,z, with z≃0.195376+0.335788i for these choices. Dividing each side by ∣b∣ divides area by ∣b∣2: its area is ∣J∣/(2∣Vcd​Vcb∗​∣2)=∣ηˉ​∣/2, approximately 0.167894. The familiar normalized picture therefore does not itself have area ∣J∣/2.
Under quantum field rephasings, a,b,c all acquire the same phase, so the original triangle merely rotates and z stays fixed. Ratios and angles can thus compare quark mixing measurements without dependence on field conventions. If the Jarlskog invariant vanishes, the triangle collapses; additional quark mass degeneracies require extra care in interpreting the converse.
