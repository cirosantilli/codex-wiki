Helstrom measurement for the zero and plus states

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Helstrom-Holevo bound Helstrom measurement for two pure states
OurBigBook.com Words: 325
Suppose one qubit is prepared in either of the known pure states ∣0⟩ and ∣+⟩=(∣0⟩+∣1⟩)/2​, with equal prior probabilities. Measuring in the computational basis seems natural, but a tilted projective measurement gives better quantum state discrimination. The two density operators and their difference are
ρ0​=(10​00​),ρ+​=21​(11​11​),Δ=ρ0​−ρ+​=21​(1−1​−1−1​)=21​(σz​−σx​).
(22)
The Pauli matrices give Δ2=I/2, and the eigenvalues of Δ are λ±​=±1/2​. Their absolute sum gives trace distance D=1/2​, agreeing with trace distance between qubit states.
The positive spectral projector points along the unit Bloch vector n=(−1,0,1)/2​:
P+​=2I+n⋅σ​=21​(I+2​σz​−σx​​),P−​=I−P+​.
(23)
The outcome labels + and − here refer to the sign of the eigenvalue of Δ; they do not denote a Hadamard basis measurement. Guess ∣0⟩ after P+​ and ∣+⟩ after P−​. This sign choice is the equal-prior Helstrom measurement for two pure states, a case of the Holevo–Helstrom theorem. [ref]
An explicit orthonormal basis for this projective measurement is
∣h+​⟩=cos(π/8)∣0⟩−sin(π/8)∣1⟩,∣h−​⟩=sin(π/8)∣0⟩+cos(π/8)∣1⟩,
(24)
with P±​=∣h±​⟩⟨h±​∣. Thus the real coefficient rotation angle is β=−π/8 relative to the computational basis, while the Bloch vector of P+​ is tilted by π/4 from the positive z axis toward negative x. State-vector and Bloch sphere angles differ by a factor of two.
Write a=(1+1/2​)/2 and b=(1−1/2​)/2, so a+b=1. The Born rule gives the conditional outcome probabilities
inputρ0​ρ+​​P+​ab​P−​ba​​a≃0.853553,b≃0.146447.
(25)
The event P+​ has probability gap a−b=1/2​=D, attaining the variational characterization of trace distance. Both preparations are identified correctly with conditional probability a, so
Psuccessopt​=2a+a​=21​(1+2​1​)≃0.853553.
(26)
For comparison, a Z projective measurement in the computational basis has conditional outcome probabilities (1,0) on ρ0​ and (1/2,1/2) on ρ+​. Guess zero on outcome zero and plus on outcome one. The average success is
PsuccessZ​=21​(1+21​)=43​,
(27)
and the measured total variation distance is only 1/2. The optimal tilted projective measurement gains about 0.103553 in absolute success probability by balancing the two conditional error rates instead of identifying ∣0⟩ perfectly at the expense of ∣+⟩.
These are statistics for alternative measurements on independently prepared copies. Applying Z first and then the tilted projective measurement is a different experiment, because the first quantum measurement changes the density operator.
