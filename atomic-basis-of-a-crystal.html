Atomic basis of a crystal ({(sj​,rj​)}j=1N​)

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Crystal structure
OurBigBook.com Words: 492 Articles: 2
An atomic basis of a crystal is the motif of chemical species sj​ and relative positions rj​ attached identically to every lattice point R of a Bravais lattice Λ. The ideal periodic structure is the labeled set
{(sj​,R+rj​):R∈Λ,j=1,…,N}.
(9)
The primitive lattice translation vectors generate R by integer combinations; the atomic basis lists what is repeated at R. These are different meanings of the word basis. In particular, the offsets rj​ need not be linearly independent vectors, and one of them may be zero. A primitive unit cell represents N motif sites, even though it represents only one lattice point.
Changing the origin, choosing another primitive unit cell, or moving a motif site by a Bravais lattice translation changes the coordinates used to describe the same infinite crystal structure. The grouping into a motif does not, by itself, identify bonded molecules. In solid sodium chloride, grouping one sodium cation and one chlorine anion into an atomic basis does not create a collection of discrete NaCl molecules; the ionic crystal has an extended network of equivalent neighboring ions.
