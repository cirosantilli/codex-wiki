Zorn's lemma in a finite poset

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Set theory Zorn's lemma
OurBigBook.com Words: 202
Consider the following finite set of subsets, ordered by subset containment:
P={∅,{a},{b},{c},{a,b},{b,c}}.
(22)
Every nonempty chain in a partial order has a greatest element within that chain in a partial order, hence an upper bound in P. For the empty chain in a partial order, choose ∅∈P. Thus the hypothesis of Zorn's lemma holds. The two maximal elements are {a,b} and {b,c}: neither is a proper subset of another member of P. There is no greatest element, because it would have to contain both, and no member of P contains {a,b,c}.
For the chain in a partial order C={∅,{b}}, the member {b} is already an upper bound, although it is not a maximal element of P. Both orange members are also upper bounds for C. A chain in a partial order need not have a unique upper bound, and the upper bound used to check the hypothesis need not be the eventual maximal element.
The restriction to chains in a partial order matters. The subset {{a},{c}}⊆P has no upper bound in P, but its two members are incomparable, so it is not a chain in a partial order. Requiring every subset of P to have an upper bound would be much stronger: taking that subset to be P itself would force a greatest element.
