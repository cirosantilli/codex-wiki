Differentiation along refining metric partitions (avgMk​(x)​∣f(y)−f(x)∣⟶0almost everywhere)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Conditional expectation First-crossing maximal inequality for refining partitions
OurBigBook.com Words: 113
Assume in addition that the probability space is a metric space, continuous integrable functions are dense in L1, and the diameters of its partition cells tend to zero at each point. For a continuous g, the oscillation average tends to zero because every point in Mk​(x) is within its diameter of x. For h=f−g, the upper limit of the oscillation of f is bounded by the partition maximal function of h plus ∣h(x)∣. The first-crossing maximal inequality for refining partitions and Chebyshev's inequality bound the measure where this exceeds 2ε by 2∥h∥1​/ε. Approximation proves the displayed almost everywhere assertion. Refinement supplies the maximal estimate; shrinking diameter supplies the approximation argument. Without shrinking diameter, a constant partition generally converges only to its cell averages.
