Proportional-integral control (u=kp​e+ki​z,z˙=e)

Proportional-integral control is the proportional and integral part of a PID controller, with the derivative term omitted. Proportional-integral control combines proportional control with integral action. For the ideal controlled plant, a constant reference signal, exact measurement and a constant control disturbance, the ordinary differential equations give
e˙=−kp​e−ki​z−w,z˙=e,e¨+kp​e˙+ki​e=0.
(26)
The characteristic polynomial is λ2+kp​λ+ki​. Positive kp​ and ki​ imply asymptotic stability of the equilibrium point: real roots of a polynomial have negative sum and positive product, hence are both negative; a nonreal complex conjugate pair has real part −kp​/2<0. This is a short stability check for this ideal controlled plant, rather than a rule for every dynamical system.
At the equilibrium point, e∗​=0, x∗​=r, and z∗​=−w/ki​. For w=0.2 and ki​=1, the stored value z∗​=−0.2 supplies u∗​=−0.2 even though the tracking error is zero. This separates the eventual accuracy from the transient response: integral action cancels the lasting offset but can make the plant output pass beyond the goal. The authors discuss practical limits on integral action in Åström and Murray, Feedback Systems, Chapter 11.
For kp​=2, ki​=1, x(0)=z(0)=0 and w=0, the characteristic polynomial is (λ+1)2. The initial conditions are e(0)=1 and e˙(0)=−2, giving
e(t)=(1−t)e−t,x(t)=1−(1−t)e−t,z(t)=te−t.
(27)
The tracking error changes sign at t=1. Since x˙=(2−t)e−t, the largest plant output occurs at t=2 and is 1+e−2≃1.135335, about 13.53% above the reference signal.
For a separate comparison, start the control disturbance w=0.2 at t=4. For t≥4, write s=t−4. The exact responses are
xP​(t)=1−e−2t+0.1(1−e−2s),xPI​(t)=1−(1−t)e−t+0.2se−s.
(28)
The proportional control response tends to 1.1, while the proportional-integral control response returns to 1. At t=8, the values are approximately 1.099966 and 1.017001. The added term in the proportional-integral control response eventually vanishes, exposing how memory removes a constant control disturbance's lasting effect.
