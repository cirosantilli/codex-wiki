For entropy gain from impact and thermal equilibration, let the large lake be a reservoir at an initially unspecified temperature TL​, in kelvin, and take the final meteorite temperature to be TL​. Its initial temperature is Ti​=1273.15K. The meteorite loses thermal energy mc(Ti​−TL​), and its kinetic energy mv2/2 is also dissipated into the lake. Thus
ΔSmet​=mclog(TL​/Ti​),ΔSlake​=TL​mc(Ti​−TL​)+mv2/2​,
(1)
and

(2)

Reflected and transmitted waves arise because the fields must satisfy the electromagnetic boundary conditions. With no free surface charge or current, tangential E,H and normal D,B are continuous. At normal incidence between lossless media, define the real wave impedance Zj​=μj​/ϵj​​. Writing r=Er​/Ei​ and t=Et​/Ei​ in a common transverse electric-field direction gives
1+r=t,Z1​1−r​=Z2​t​,r=Z2​+Z1​Z2​−Z1​​,t=Z1​+Z2​2Z2​​.​
(18)
For the power normalization of Fresnel amplitudes, power fractions are R=∣r∣2 and T=(Z1​/Z2​)∣t∣2, so R+T=1. An electric-field amplitude coefficient alone is not a transmitted power fraction.
At oblique incidence, phase matching along the interface gives Snell's law, n1​sinθi​=n2​sinθt​. Displacement current makes Ampère's law compatible with charge conservation. The macroscopic Maxwell equations are
∇⋅D=ρf​,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×H=Jf​+∂t​D.​
(19)
Taking the divergence of the last equation and using Gauss's law gives ∇⋅Jf​+∂t​ρf​=0. Omitting ∂t​D would wrongly require the conduction current to be divergence free during charging. The displacement-current density ∂t​D has current-density units, but need not represent free charges crossing a region.
The surface independence of Ampère circulation during capacitor charging illustrates the need directly. A surface spanning the circuit can cut the wire or pass between the plates. For ideal parallel plates, Q=ϵ0​AE and
I=Q˙​=ϵ0​AE˙=∫gap​∂t​D⋅dA.
(20)
The corrected circulation of H is independent of that surface choice. Free conduction current stops at a plate; displacement current supplies the gap contribution.
In vacuum the two time-dependent curl equations support waves. Taking a curl of Faraday's law in a source-free region yields
∇2E−μ0​ϵ0​∂t2​E=0,c=(μ0​ϵ0​)−1/2.
(21)
The corresponding magnetic equation is identical. For a plane wave, k⋅E=k⋅B=0 and B=k×E/ω. The fields carry energy through the Poynting flux. Dropping displacement current everywhere would remove these freely propagating solutions.[ref]
In a metal, conduction and displacement compete. For a uniform ohmic material with Jf​=σE, constant ϵ,μ, and transverse waves,
∇2E=μϵ∂t2​E+μσ∂t​E.
(22)
Use eik⋅r−iωt. The dispersion relation and conduction-to-displacement current ratio are
k2=μϵω2+iμσω,∣Jdisp​∣∣Jcond​∣​=ωϵσ​.​
(23)
When σ≫ωϵ, k≃(1+i)/δ with δ=2/(μσω)​: fields are screened within a skin depth and energy is dissipated by Joule heating. This quasistatic limit neglects displacement current locally compared with conduction; it does not invalidate the full Maxwell law. At sufficiently high frequency, conductivity and permittivity are frequency dependent, so a constant-σ approximation must be replaced by a material model.
In an unmagnetized cold plasma, electron inertia gives a cutoff. With immobile ions and negligible collisions, me​v˙=−eE and J=−ne​ev give σ(ω)=ine​e2/(me​ω). For the transverse wave cutoff in a cold unmagnetized plasma, combining this carrier current with vacuum displacement current gives
ϵeff​=ϵ0​(1−ω2ωp2​​),ωp2​=me​ϵ0​ne​e2​,ω2=ωp2​+c2k2.​
(24)
Transverse waves propagate above ωp​ and are evanescent below it. The group velocity is c1−ωp2​/ω2​<c even though the phase velocity exceeds c. The cold model also has longitudinal charge-density oscillations at ωp​, distinct from transverse light waves. Collisions add damping, and pressure or magnetic fields modify these simple dispersions. To avoid carrier-current double counting in effective permittivity, once the carrier response is included in ϵeff​ it must not be counted a second time as a separate conductivity. Displacement current therefore connects circuit continuity, vacuum radiation, conductor screening and plasma propagation within one consistent field theory.[ref]

A normal mode is a motion in which all coordinates have a fixed relative pattern and oscillate at one frequency. Near a stable equilibrium, let the quadratic kinetic and potential energies be T=q˙​TMq˙​/2 and V=qTKq/2. With positive-definite mass matrix M, the equations and harmonic ansatz give
Mq¨​+Kq=0,Kas​=ωs2​Mas​.​
(36)
The stiffness and mass matrices are symmetric. For distinct frequencies, their symmetry gives (ωs2​−ωr2​)arT​Mas​=0, so the mode vectors are orthogonal in the mass inner product. A basis can also be chosen in a degenerate eigenspace. Expanding q=∑s​Qs​as​ decouples the equations into Q¨​s​+ωs2​Qs​=0; initial displacements and velocities determine the coefficients. Positive stiffness gives real frequencies, a zero eigenvalue can describe a free symmetry motion, and a negative one signals instability rather than an oscillatory mode.[ref]
For two equal masses between fixed walls with three equal springs, the stiffness matrix is k(2−1​−12​) and the mass matrix is m1. The patterns (1,1) and (1,−1) have frequencies k/m​ and 3k/m​. An initial displacement of one mass excites both modes and can produce beats.
For a uniform taut string fixed at both ends, the wave equation ∂t2​y=cs2​∂x2​y, with cs​=T/μℓ​​, has patterns and angular frequencies
yn​(x)∝sin(nπx/l),ωn​=nπcs​/l,n=1,2,….​
(37)
The boundary conditions select the spectrum. Fourier expansion of arbitrary admissible initial data provides the mode amplitudes, illustrating the extension from a finite set of coordinates to a field.
For the normal modes of inductively coupled LC circuits, take two identical circuits with self-inductance L, capacitance C and mutual inductance M, where ∣M∣<L. The capacitor charges obey Lq¨​1​+Mq¨​2​+q1​/C=0 and the exchanged equation. In-phase and opposite-phase charge patterns have
ω+​=C(L+M)​1​,ω−​=C(L−M)​1​.​
(38)
The positive inductance matrix for two identical coils is the physical counterpart of positive mass. Here inductance plays the role of the mass matrix. Mechanical, wave and electrical examples therefore share the same eigenvalue structure. Weak forcing excites modes according to its spatial overlap with each pattern; damping broadens the resonances, while strong nonlinearity can invalidate a fixed linear-mode decomposition.

A liquid-gas thermodynamic critical point is the endpoint of the coexistence curve where distinct liquid and gas densities merge. In the van der Waals illustration, write the original constants as av​,bv​ to distinguish them from the later polynomial coefficients. The critical isotherm has a horizontal inflection in p(V); setting its first two volume derivatives to zero gives
Vc​=3Nbv​,kB​Tc​=27bv​8av​​,pc​=27bv2​av​​.​
(39)
Below this temperature the homogeneous van der Waals loop contains an unstable region; physical equilibrium instead separates into liquid and gas.[ref]
For the critical point of a cubic density equation of state, assume b,c>0. Its density derivatives are
pn​=kB​T−bn+2c​n2,pnn​=−b+cn.
(40)
Because n=N/V is a smooth monotone function of V at finite positive density, a stationary inflection obeys pn​=pnn​=0 as well. Solving these equations and substituting back gives
nc​=cb​,kB​Tc​=2cb2​,pc​=6c2b3​.​
(41)
The symbols b,c here have different units from the van der Waals excluded-volume parameter. With x=n/nc​, t=T/Tc​, the isotherms become
pc​p​=x3−3x2+3tx,dxd(p/pc​)​=3[(x−1)2+t−1].
(42)
The spinodal densities of a cubic gas delimit the two-extrema regime. The curves are monotone for t>1, have a stationary inflection at (x,p/pc​)=(1,1) for t=1, and have two extrema x=1±1−t​ for 0<t<1.
For the critical-density compressibility of a cubic gas, since κT​=(1/n)(∂n/∂p)T​, evaluation at the critical density gives
κT​(nc​,T)=nc​kB​(T−Tc​)1​.​
(43)
It diverges as T→Tc+​ in this mean-field model. For T<Tc​ it is negative on the homogeneous branch at nc​, violating mechanical stability: a pressure increase would expand rather than compress that branch.
The actual equilibrium below criticality is coexistence of two phases, with equal pressure and chemical potential and different densities. A sample's mean density can change by changing phase proportions. The unstable homogeneous branch is not a realizable equilibrium isotherm. The Maxwell construction in density coordinates requires care: the equal-area construction is performed in p versus volume, not by using unweighted area in p versus density. The truncated polynomial is an approximate mean-field equation, so its predictions need not give quantitative critical behaviour for a real gas.

(vx​,vy​,vz​)⟼(−vx​−2u,vy​,vz​).​
(50)
The exact kinetic-energy change in this ideal massive-piston model is ΔK=2muvx​+2mu2. Thus, to first order in the small piston speed,
Kafter​=Kbefore​+2muvx​+O(u2).​
(51)

(52)

The Second law of thermodynamics restricts which energy-conserving processes can occur. In the Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law, no cyclic engine can have as its sole effect the extraction of heat from one reservoir and its complete conversion into work. In the Clausius statement of the second law, no process can have as its sole effect transferring heat from a colder reservoir to a hotter one. An engine or refrigerator combined with the reverse of a reversible device shows that a violation of either statement would violate the other.
A heat engine takes heat Qh​>0 from a reservoir at Th​, rejects Qc​>0 to one at Tc​<Th​, and returns its working substance to the initial state. Energy conservation gives W=Qh​−Qc​ and efficiency η=W/Qh​=1−Qc​/Qh​. Carnot's theorem states that no engine between these reservoirs is more efficient than a reversible one, and that all reversible engines between them have the same efficiency. Otherwise, drive a reversible engine backwards with the work of a more efficient engine; the composite cycle transfers heat from cold to hot without external work, contradicting the Clausius statement.
A reversible Carnot cycle consists of two isotherms joined by two reversible adiabats. For an ideal gas, Qh​=NkB​Th​log(V2​/V1​) and Qc​=NkB​Tc​log(V3​/V4​). The adiabats give V2​/V1​=V3​/V4​, so
Qh​Qc​​=Th​Tc​​,ηreversible​=1−Th​Tc​​.​
(53)
Carnot's theorem makes this independent of the working substance and defines the thermodynamic absolute-temperature scale consistently with ideal-gas temperature.[ref]
For any reversible cycle, ∮δQrev​/T=0. This makes the integral between two equilibrium states independent of the reversible path, defining the state function entropy:
SB​−SA​=∫AB​TδQrev​​.​
(54)
Heat itself is path dependent; entropy is not. An irreversible change can be evaluated using any imagined reversible path between its equilibrium endpoints. For example, a fixed amount of ideal gas with constant CV​ has ΔS=CV​log(TB​/TA​)+NkB​log(VB​/VA​).
For a general cycle the Clausius inequality is ∮δQ/Tboundary​≤0. The entropy balance for a process is ΔS≥∫δQ/Tboundary​, with equality for reversible heat exchange and no entropy production. The surroundings' entropy must be included, giving ΔSuniverse​≥0. A system's own entropy can decrease by exporting entropy; an isolated system's cannot.
The engine's reservoirs have net entropy change Qc​/Tc​−Qh​/Th​≥0, so
η≤1−Th​Tc​​,​
(55)
with equality for a reversible engine. Friction, heat flow through a finite temperature difference and unrestrained expansion produce entropy and reduce usable work. Thus energy conservation fixes the balance of heat and work, while the second law fixes their direction and the attainable efficiency.[ref]
