For generalized coordinates qi​ with Lagrangian L(q,q˙​,t), the conjugate momentum is
pi​=∂q˙​i​∂L​,p˙​i​=∂qi​∂L​.​
(28)
A coordinate absent from L is cyclic, and its conjugate momentum is conserved. More generally, Noether's theorem connects continuous symmetries of the action to conserved quantities: spatial translations give linear momentum and rotations give angular momentum. If L has no explicit time dependence, the conserved energy function is
E=∑i​pi​q˙​i​−L,dtdE​=−∂t∂L​=0.
(29)
The momentum conjugate to an angle is an angular momentum, not a linear velocity. Canonical momentum can also differ from mechanical momentum: for L=21​mv2+qA⋅v−qϕ, p=mv+qA. Thus a conserved conjugate momentum must be identified from the actual Lagrangian and its symmetry, rather than assumed to equal mv. Gauge-dependent canonical momenta require the corresponding gauge choice or gauge-covariant symmetry interpretation.[ref]
