At an interface with unit normal n^ pointing from medium 1 to medium 2, the magnetic boundary conditions are
n^⋅(B2​−B1​)=0,n^×(H2​−H1​)=Kf​.​
(12)
The first follows from a pillbox applied to ∇⋅B=0. The tangential magnetic field jump from a free surface current follows from a shrinking rectangular Ampère loop; Kf​ is the free surface current. With no free sheet current, normal B and tangential H are continuous. In linear isotropic media B=μH, so neither all components of B nor all components of H generally remain continuous.
For magnetic field-line refraction at a permeability interface, with angles measured from the normal, these conditions give tanθ2​/tanθ1​=μ2​/μ1​. They determine the bending of magnetic flux at a material interface. In a slender core electromagnet, flux continuity and Ampère's law lead to the magnetic-circuit estimate
Φ≃∑j​lj​/(μj​Aj​)NI​.
(13)
An air gap can dominate the reluctance even when the iron core has high magnetic permeability. Boundary conditions also determine demagnetizing fields; fringing, saturation and hysteresis limit the simple linear circuit model. A high-magnetic permeability material redirects flux, whereas a perfect-conductor boundary is a different physical assumption.[ref]
