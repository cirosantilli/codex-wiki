An inductor stores magnetic energy and opposes changes of current through electromagnetic induction. For a fixed linear device, the flux linkage is Ψ=NΦ=LI, the self-induced electromotive force is −LdI/dt, and the passive terminal voltage is V=LdI/dt. Hence

The voltage leads a sinusoidal current by a quarter cycle. A long solenoid has B≃μNI/l and L≃μN2A/l; its energy agrees with the volume integral of B2/(2μ).
For two coupled coils, Ψ1​=L1​I1​+MI2​, Ψ2​=MI1​+L2​I2​, with signs set by coil orientations. Reciprocity gives the same mutual inductance in both equations. For the energy stored in a nonlinear inductor, U=∫IdΨ replaces the simple quadratic formula, and hysteresis can dissipate energy. Real coils also have resistance, core loss and parasitic capacitance, so an ideal inductance model has a finite useful frequency range.[ref]
