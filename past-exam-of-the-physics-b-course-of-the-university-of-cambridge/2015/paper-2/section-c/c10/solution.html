A liquid-gas thermodynamic critical point is the endpoint of the coexistence curve where distinct liquid and gas densities merge. In the van der Waals illustration, write the original constants as av​,bv​ to distinguish them from the later polynomial coefficients. The critical isotherm has a horizontal inflection in p(V); setting its first two volume derivatives to zero gives
Vc​=3Nbv​,kB​Tc​=27bv​8av​​,pc​=27bv2​av​​.​
(39)
Below this temperature the homogeneous van der Waals loop contains an unstable region; physical equilibrium instead separates into liquid and gas.[ref]
For the critical point of a cubic density equation of state, assume b,c>0. Its density derivatives are
pn​=kB​T−bn+2c​n2,pnn​=−b+cn.
(40)
Because n=N/V is a smooth monotone function of V at finite positive density, a stationary inflection obeys pn​=pnn​=0 as well. Solving these equations and substituting back gives
nc​=cb​,kB​Tc​=2cb2​,pc​=6c2b3​.​
(41)
The symbols b,c here have different units from the van der Waals excluded-volume parameter. With x=n/nc​, t=T/Tc​, the isotherms become
pc​p​=x3−3x2+3tx,dxd(p/pc​)​=3[(x−1)2+t−1].
(42)
The spinodal densities of a cubic gas delimit the two-extrema regime. The curves are monotone for t>1, have a stationary inflection at (x,p/pc​)=(1,1) for t=1, and have two extrema x=1±1−t​ for 0<t<1.
For the critical-density compressibility of a cubic gas, since κT​=(1/n)(∂n/∂p)T​, evaluation at the critical density gives
κT​(nc​,T)=nc​kB​(T−Tc​)1​.​
(43)
It diverges as T→Tc+​ in this mean-field model. For T<Tc​ it is negative on the homogeneous branch at nc​, violating mechanical stability: a pressure increase would expand rather than compress that branch.
The actual equilibrium below criticality is coexistence of two phases, with equal pressure and chemical potential and different densities. A sample's mean density can change by changing phase proportions. The unstable homogeneous branch is not a realizable equilibrium isotherm. The Maxwell construction in density coordinates requires care: the equal-area construction is performed in p versus volume, not by using unweighted area in p versus density. The truncated polynomial is an approximate mean-field equation, so its predictions need not give quantitative critical behaviour for a real gas.
