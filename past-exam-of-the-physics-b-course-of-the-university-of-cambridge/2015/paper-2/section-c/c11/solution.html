The Boltzmann distribution assigns probability Pi​=e−Ei​/(kB​T)/Z to each microstate. For the equilibrium height distribution in a uniform gravitational field, take a dilute gas above a floor in a constant gravitational field. Uniform cross-section and no relevant upper boundary give the normalized height density
P(z)=kB​Tmg​e−mgz/(kB​T),z≥0.
(44)
Thus
⟨Ug​⟩=∫0∞​mgzP(z)dz=kB​T.​
(45)
The gravitational potential is linear in height, so its mean is not a quadratic-mode contribution of kB​T/2.
The equipartition states that each independent quadratic term in a classical equilibrium Hamiltonian contributes mean energy kB​T/2, provided the relevant degrees of freedom are thermally accessible. Equivalently, ⟨xi​∂H/∂xi​⟩=kB​T when the phase-space integration by parts has vanishing boundary terms. Three translational velocity components therefore contribute 3kB​T/2.
For rotational equipartition in a rough-ball gas, collisions exchange both translational and rotational energy. In the intended three-dimensional classical equilibrium model there are three translational and three rotational quadratic terms, giving 3kB​T in total kinetic energy per ball. Adding ⟨Ug​⟩=kB​T, and conserving the initial energy mgh per ball, gives
mgh=(23​+23​+1)kB​T=4kB​T,T=4kB​mgh​.​
(46)
Roughness is relevant because it allows rotation to thermalize. The effective mechanical temperature of released rough balls describes the balls' mechanical degrees of freedom, not their internal material temperature; finite ball size and excluded-volume effects are neglected.[ref]
The height tail is
P(z>h)=∫h∞​P(z)dz=e−mgh/(kB​T)=e−4=0.0183≃1.83%.​
(47)
This can be nonzero because collisions redistribute energy between balls; an individual ball is not restricted to its original energy. The exponential distribution is the large-number statistical approximation under the stated effective-equilibrium assumption.
