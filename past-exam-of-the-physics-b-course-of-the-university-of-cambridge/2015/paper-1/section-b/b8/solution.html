The characteristic impedance of a lossless transmission line is the ratio of the voltage to the current amplitude of a single forward-travelling wave. If L and C denote inductance and capacitance per unit length, the lossless telegrapher equations give
∂z​V=−L∂t​I,∂z​I=−C∂t​V,Z=L/C​​,vp​=(LC)−1/2.
(22)
A backward wave has I−​=−V−​/Z when current is positive along +z. Thus characteristic impedance is not a resistor's electrical resistance: it describes travelling-wave amplitudes and determines impedance matching, power transfer and reflections. A semi-infinite line, or a line terminated by a matching load, presents that impedance at its input.
At a junction with no lumped branch, voltage and electric current are continuous:
Vi​+Vr​=Vt​,Z1​Vi​−Vr​​=Z2​Vt​​.
(23)
Eliminating Vt​ gives the voltage reflection coefficient
r=Vi​Vr​​=Z2​+Z1​Z2​−Z1​​.​
(24)
For real positive Z1​, incident and reflected waves occupy the same line, so their power ratio is ∣r∣2.
For the slightly mismatched coaxial cables, the change in characteristic impedance is
ΔZ=2πnZvac​​ln(1+bΔb​)≃2πnZvac​​bΔb​.
(25)
Consequently
r≃2ZΔZ​=2ln(b/a)1​bΔb​,R≃4ln2(b/a)1​(bΔb​)2.​
(26)
The requested power limit 0.01%=10−4 implies ∣Δb∣/b<0.02ln(b/a). Since ln(b/a)=2πnZ/Zvac​=1.918 for the stated line, the adjacent cables' outer radii must match within approximately 3.84%. If a tolerance is quoted separately as a symmetric manufacturing range ±δb and arbitrary worst-case pairs can differ by 2δb, the corresponding individual tolerance is approximately ±1.92%. The leading calculation is for small mismatches; the exact logarithmic expression gives slightly asymmetric positive and negative limits. The vacuum wave impedance used here is approximately 376.73 Ω.[ref]
A needle is a shunt electrical resistance Rn​, so the load seen at its location is the continuation of the line in parallel with Rn​: ZL​=ZRn​/(Z+Rn​). The local voltage reflection coefficient and transmission amplitude are
r=−2Rn​+ZZ​≃−2Rn​Z​​,t=1+r.
(27)
For periodic resistive loading of a transmission line, the reflection from the next needle travels an additional round trip 2L and crosses each previous needle twice. Taking the first needle as phase reference, the single-return approximation for periodic line reflections therefore sums to
Rfirst​=r∑j=0∞​[t2eiϕ]j=1−(1+r)2eiϕr​,ϕ=2kL=vp​4πνL​.
(28)
Here vp​ is the propagation phase velocity in the cable, c/n for the stated nondispersive nonmagnetic filling. Squaring the amplitude gives the requested expression
Rfirst​(ν)=​1−(1+r)2eiϕ(ν)r​​2.​
(29)
For a finite number N of needles the amplitude has the additional numerator 1−[t2eiϕ]N. The displayed semi-infinite result assumes sufficiently many needles, no terminal reflection, and neglects intrinsic propagation loss.
For real r<0, write q=t2. The denominator of the power coefficient is 1+q2−2qcosϕ. It has constructive peaks at ϕ=2πj and minima at ϕ=(2j+1)π, repeating with frequency spacing Δν=vp​/(2L). Within this approximation,
Rmax​=(2+r)21​≃41​,Rmin​=[1+(1+r)2]2r2​≃4r2​.
(30)
Weak individual shunts thus give narrow interference peaks, with phase width of order ∣r∣.
The requested sum excludes paths that reflect repeatedly between different needles. It should not be read as an exact multiple-scattering result, particularly at the coherent peaks. For example the exact semi-infinite amplitude satisfies
R=r+1−reiϕRt2eiϕR​.
(31)
At ϕ=2πj this equation reduces to (R+1)2=0: the exact ideal infinite array has unit power reflection there, rather than the first-return approximation's value near 1/4. This does not change the requested geometric-series derivation, but identifies its physical limitation.
