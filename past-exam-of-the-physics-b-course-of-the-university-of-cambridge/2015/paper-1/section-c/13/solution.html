For a Newtonian fluid with velocity u(z)x, τ=η∂z​u is the x component of traction on a plane whose normal points along +z. The opposite face has the opposite traction. The coefficient η is dynamic viscosity, with units Pas. Faster layers transfer positive x momentum toward slower neighbours; the internal shear stress resists relative sliding.
Take a thin fluid element with horizontal area A and thickness dz. The difference between its upper and lower viscous tractions is Aηuzz​dz. The pressure gradient supplies −Apx​dz. Incompressibility is satisfied because u has no x dependence, and the convective acceleration vanishes: (u⋅∇)u=u∂x​u=0.
