A1 OurBigBook.com
An infinite sheet of surface charge density σ gives an electric field of magnitude Es​=σ/(2ε0​) on either side, normal to the sheet and directed away from positive charge. Use the superposition principle for the two sheets. Choose normals y​ for y=0 and n=(−x+y​)/2​ for y=x. Away from the charged planes,
E=Es​[sgn(y)y​−sgn(y−x)2​−x+y​​].​
(1)
The minus sign on the second contribution makes its field point toward the negatively charged sheet. Each sector has a uniform electric field; its direction changes on crossing a sheet.
For example, above both sheets the field points right and slightly upward, whereas in the sector y>0 but y<x it points left and steeply upward. The other two sectors follow by inversion through the origin. The normal field jumps by the appropriate surface charge divided by ε0​; a single field value on an ideal zero-thickness charged sheet is not required.

Let the signed axial velocity on the axis be w(z). Smooth axisymmetric flow has uz​(r,z)=w(z)+O(r2) and regular radial velocity at r=0. The incompressible flow condition in cylindrical coordinates is
r1​∂r∂(rur​)​+∂z∂uz​​=0.
(2)

ur​=−2r​w′(z)+O(r3).
(3)

ur​(r,z)≃2z43Ar​,r≪∣z∣.​
(4)

The source specifies a magnitude rather than an unambiguous signed component. On z>0, reversing the axial flow while keeping its magnitude would reverse the radial answer. A large gas volume can reduce the effective mass density of the water surrounding the hull. In the simple quasihydrostatic model for the buoyancy of an aerated liquid, gas void fraction α gives
ρmix​=(1−α)ρw​+αρg​≃(1−α)ρw​.
(5)
By Archimedes' principle, a boat displacing volume Vdisp​ then has buoyant force ρmix​gVdisp​. To support the same boat mass m, it needs a larger displaced volume m/ρmix​, so its draft increases. If that exceeds the available watertight displacement, it can lose flotation or become swamped:
ρmix​Vmax​<m⟹insufficient hydrostatic buoyancy.​
(6)
Uneven aeration and strong plume motion can also disturb stability. This explains a possible hazard, not a claim that any bubble plume must sink a boat: upward water flow, turbulence and the distribution of bubbles can exert substantial additional forces.

The boundary-value method. In a charge-free region, the electric potential obeys the Laplace equation, ∇2Φ=0, and E=−∇Φ. Conductors impose equipotential boundaries, with specified voltages or charges, and a far-field condition fixes the remaining behaviour. The Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem guarantees that a solution meeting the prescribed conductor potentials is the physical one. To see why, the difference w of two candidate solutions is harmonic and vanishes on the boundaries; integration by parts gives ∫∣∇w∣2dV=0, hence no difference in their fields. An exterior problem also needs the appropriate condition at infinity.
Two axisymmetric harmonic functions. For Φ=f(r)cosθ in spherical coordinates, the Laplacian reduces to
∇2Φ=r2cosθ​[drd​(r2f′)−2f].
(13)
For f=r, the bracket is 2r−2r=0. For f=r−2, it is 2r−2−2r−2=0. Therefore
∇2(rcosθ)=0,∇2(cosθ/r2)=0(r>0).​
(14)
The second function is singular at the origin, which is allowed here because that point lies inside the excluded conductor. The printed spherical Laplacian has a typo in its azimuthal term: its denominator should be r2sin2θ. Both functions are axisymmetric, so that term vanishes and the typo does not affect these calculations.
Potential and field geometry. Set the lower conductor to zero potential and the distant upper plate to V. The small boss sees approximately uniform background field −E0​z, where E0​=V/d. Combining the two harmonic functions to make Φ=0 on r=a gives the conducting hemispherical protrusion in a uniform field,
Φ=E0​(r−r2a3​)cosθ=E0​z(1−r3a3​).​
(15)
It also vanishes on the surrounding plane z=0. The electric field components are
Er​=−E0​(1+r32a3​)cosθ,Eθ​=E0​(1−r3a3​)sinθ.
(16)
Equipotentials crowd near the apex, and field lines meet the conductor normally.
The formula has the correct uniform far field but does not exactly impose a flat upper equipotential at finite d. It is the leading approximation justified by a≪d; an exact finite-gap solution would include further corrections.

How the fields differ from those in vacuum. The two metal fields have the same exponential envelope and spatial period 2πδ, but their phase arguments differ by π/4.
An individual travelling wave in the lossless exterior has no attenuation, real wave impedance and in-phase electric and magnetic fields. The exterior total field also includes the reflected wave and may show interference, so it need not share that simple phase relation everywhere. (43)
The decrease becomes Joule heating in each layer. The Poynting theorem includes this transfer from field energy to the metal; averaged electromagnetic energy storage has zero time derivative in the periodic state, so no energy is lost from the complete balance.

Inertia and principal directions. About the centre of mass, the inertia tensor is
Iij​=∫(r2δij​−ri​rj​)dm.
(55)
It relates angular momentum and angular velocity by J=Iω and gives rotational kinetic energy T=ω⋅Iω/2. The principal axes are orthogonal eigenvector directions of this real symmetric tensor; in that basis it is diagonal, with entries the principal moments of inertia I1​,I2​,I3​.
Derivatives in a rotating frame. Write J=∑i​Ji​ei​ in a body-fixed orthonormal basis. Each basis vector rotates according to (dei​/dt)0​=ω×ei​. Differentiating the components and basis separately proves the rotating-frame derivative formula,
dtdJ​​0​=dtdJ​​body​+ω×J.​
(56)
The body derivative differentiates the scalar components in that basis. A fixed pivot can replace the centre of mass when the corresponding angular-momentum balance is valid.
Euler equations. The inertial angular-momentum equation is dJ/dt=G. Since the principal moments are constant in the body frame, substitute Ji​=Ii​ωi​:
G1​G2​G3​​=I1​ω˙1​+(I3​−I2​)ω2​ω3​,=I2​ω˙2​+(I1​−I3​)ω3​ω1​,=I3​ω˙3​+(I2​−I1​)ω1​ω2​.​​

These are Euler's equations in the principal body frame.

I1​ω˙1​=(I2​−I3​)Ωω2​,I2​ω˙2​=(I3​−I1​)Ωω1​.

Physical interpretation OurBigBook.com
Both the magnitude J of angular momentum and rotational kinetic energy are conserved after the impulse. At fixed J, eliminate J32​=J2−J12​−J22​ to write
T=2I3​J2​+2J12​​(I1​1​−I3​1​)+2J22​​(I2​1​−I3​1​).
(64)
The fixed-angular-momentum energy test for principal-axis rotation makes the stability geometric. The largest moment gives a constrained energy minimum, the smallest a maximum, and the intermediate moment a saddle. Conservation of energy keeps a nearby motion near either isolated extremum, but a saddle permits departure in one direction while compensating in another. Thus both extremes are stable without dissipation; it would be incorrect to demand an energy minimum alone in this conservative problem.

The differential gravitational acceleration responsible for a tidal force is the difference between accelerations at separated points of a body. Relative to a freely falling reference point R, a small offset ξ experiences
Δg=g(R+ξ)−g(R)≃Tξ,Tij​=∂Rj​∂gi​​.
(65)
The tidal tensor has units s−2; the vector called T(ξ) in the source is the resulting differential acceleration, in ms−2. An actual differential force is this vector multiplied by the test mass. Measurable effects include relative motion of separated freely falling masses, radial stretching, transverse squeezing, deformation and, when self-gravity or strength is insufficient, disruption. A spatially uniform acceleration alone produces no tidal deformation.

The extra radial term. A circular Kepler orbit has Ωorb2​=GM/R3. In the frame rotating with the synchronously spinning body, centrifugal acceleration increases by Ωorb2​a across a radial offset a. This adds the outward differential acceleration
Δacent​=R3GM​a.​
(70)
Equivalently, in the inertial frame the farther corotating point needs an extra inward centripetal acceleration Ωorb2​a, which gravity alone does not supply. The total radial stretching relevant to the static corotating body is therefore 3GMa/R3. This is the corotating point-mass tidal field. The source's “force” expression is per unit mass.
Self-gravitational survival. Denote the asteroid radius by s, distinct from the orbital radius R. Its mass is ma​=4πρs3/3 and its surface self-gravitational acceleration is Gma​/s2=4πGρs/3. Compare this restoring acceleration with radial tidal stretching 3GMs/R3:
34πGρs​≳R33GMs​.
(71)

Rmin​≃(4πρ9M​)1/3.​
(72)
This is the requested spherical local force-balance estimate, not an exact fluid Roche figure. It neglects deformation and material cohesion.

Their ratio is therefore independent of s, provided s≪R so that the tidal expansion is valid. Density, rather than asteroid size, controls this estimate.

Compression and stretch viewed on rotated faces. To isolate shear, take equal small principal strains ϵxx​=−e, ϵyy​=+e. A square with sides at 45∘ to these directions becomes a rhombus: adjacent side lengths are unchanged to first order, but their right angle changes by 2e. In axes rotated through 45∘, the same strain tensor has zero diagonal entries and off-diagonal entries of magnitude e.
The pure shear from opposite principal strains therefore has engineering shear strain γ=2e, not e. Equal opposite principal stresses (−S,+S) give purely tangential traction of magnitude S on those rotated faces. Hence
γ=2e,τ=S=Gγ=2Ge.​
(74)
If the compression and stretch are not equal and opposite, an additional dilatational strain remains; their deviatoric part still supplies shear.

Meaning and microscopic origin. For a Newtonian fluid, the dynamic viscosity η measures tangential momentum transfer between fluid layers moving at different speeds. For the Newtonian shear considered here, τxz​=ηdvz​/dx, with units Pas for η. Molecular motion and collisions transport momentum across layers in a gas; intermolecular interactions contribute strongly in liquids. The resulting viscous forces oppose relative shear motion and convert organized kinetic energy to heat. The constitutive law is Newtonian and isotropic; incompressibility alone does not establish it.
Vertical force balance. Choose z downward, as in the original diagram, and x outward normal to the plate. In a uniform steady film, v=vz​(x)z has zero advective acceleration and no along-plate pressure gradient. On a slice of thickness dx, the net shear force per unit face area is τ(x+dx)−τ(x), while its weight per unit area is ρgdx. Therefore
dτ=−ρgdx.​
(87)
Here τ=ηdvz​/dx denotes the downward traction on a face with positive x normal. The opposite face has the opposite traction orientation, accounting for the sign in the force difference.
Boundary conditions. At the fixed solid wall, the no-slip boundary condition is vz​(0)=0, with no normal penetration. At the free surface x=d, neglect air shear, so τ(d)=0 and hence vz′​(d)=0. The flat free surface has normal pressure patm​, neglecting surface-curvature effects. These are a velocity condition at the wall and a stress condition at the free surface, not zero velocity at both boundaries.
The parabolic speed profile. Integrating τ′=−ρg from the shear-free surface gives τ(x)=ρg(d−x). Thus
ηdxdvz​​=ρg(d−x),
(88)
and the wall condition yields
vz​(x)=ηρg​(dx−2x2​)=ax+bx2,a=ηρgd​,b=−2ηρg​.​
(89)
It is zero at the wall and largest at the free surface, where its gradient vanishes. The wall shear ρgd supports the downslope weight of the film per unit plate area.

0=−∂x∂p​−ρgcosθ,0=−∂s∂p​+ρgsinθ+ηdx2d2v​.
