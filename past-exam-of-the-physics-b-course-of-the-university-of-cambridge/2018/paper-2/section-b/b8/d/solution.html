For a slender elastic beam undergoing small bending, assume plane cross-sections remain plane and normal to the neutral axis. Fibres a signed distance z from that axis have longitudinal strain εxx​=−z/R, where R is the local radius of curvature. Hooke's law gives stress σxx​=−Ez/R, where E is Young's modulus. Zero resultant axial force places the neutral axis through the section's centroid for a homogeneous beam.
The bending moment is the moment of this stress distribution. Its magnitude is
M=REIA​​,IA​=∫section​z2dA.​
(43)
The second moment of area IA​ has units m4, and the bending stiffness EIA​ has units Nm2. A rectangular section of width b and depth h has IA​=bh3/12, so placing more material far from the neutral axis strongly resists bending. This is the purpose of an I-section.
For small deflection y(x), the signed curvature is approximately y′′. A consistent variational sign convention follows from the elastic energy and a transverse load q(x):
Π[y]=21​∫EIA​(y′′)2dx−∫qydx,(EIA​y′′)′′=q.​
(44)
For constant bending stiffness this is the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation EIA​y′′′′=q. A clamped end fixes y=y′=0; a simply supported end has y=0 and zero bending moment; an unloaded free end has zero moment and shear, y′′=y′′′=0 for uniform EIA​. Endpoint loads replace the corresponding zero-force boundary conditions.
For a cantilever of length L carrying a tip force F, choose positive deflection along F. The internal bending gives EIA​y′′=F(L−x), with y(0)=y′(0)=0. Two integrations give
y(x)=6EIA​Fx2(3L−x)​,y(L)=3EIA​FL3​.​
(45)
This approximation requires small slopes, elastic strains and a length large compared with section dimensions. Shear deformation, rotary inertia, plastic yielding and large rotations require extensions. Adding inertia gives flexural waves with ω2=(EIA​/ρm​As​)k4 for a uniform beam, where ρm​As​ is mass per unit length.
