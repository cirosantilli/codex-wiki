A fictitious force is a term required when writing Newton's second law in an accelerating frame; it comes from the coordinate choice rather than a new interaction. For axes rotating at constant Ω about a fixed inertial origin,
(dtdA​)I​=(dtdA​)R​+Ω×A.
(26)
Apply this identity twice to position to get aI​=aR​+2Ω×vR​+Ω×(Ω×r). Moving the last two terms to the right of maI​=F gives the stated Coriolis and centrifugal forces. An accelerating origin or changing rotation rate would add further terms.
A slowly rotating fluid planet follows an equipotential of gravitational plus centrifugal potential. The centrifugal potential is −Ω2r2cos2λ/2, most negative at the equator. An outward equatorial shift raises gravitational potential and restores the balance, producing an oblate figure. Self-gravity also changes with the shape; no flattening coefficient follows from rotation alone without a density model.
Using local north and radial-up unit vectors, the centrifugal acceleration on a stationary mass is
acf​=Ω2R(cos2λeU​−sinλcosλeN​).
(27)
It points away from the rotation axis: upward and equatorward, at angle λ to radial up in the northern hemisphere. Its vertical fraction of gravitational force is
mgFcf,U​​=gΩ2R​cos2λ≃3.45×10−3cos2λ,​
(28)
using a 24h day for this approximate number. The resultant effective weight tilts equatorward relative to radial down. A plumb-defined local vertical already follows effective gravity; the component calculation uses the spherical radial vertical. A supported stationary mass of course has zero net acceleration.
For the Coriolis deflection of vertical launch and drop, retain only first order in Ω, use effective constant g, and neglect horizontal velocities in the Coriolis term as instructed. With east positive, vertical velocity gives x¨=−2Ωcosλz˙ and no northward acceleration. For launch, z=ut−gt2/2 and the flight time is 2u/g. Integrating with zero initial horizontal velocity gives
x˙=−2Ωcosλz,xX​=−3g24Ωu3cosλ​,yX​=0.​
(29)
The landing is westward. The westward velocity accumulated on ascent is only recovered on descent, so the two displacement contributions do not cancel.
For release from rest at h0​=u2/(2g), z=h0​−gt2/2, tfall​=u/g, and x¨=2Ωgtcosλ. Thus
xX′​=3g2Ωu3cosλ​=−41​xX​,yX′​=0.​
(30)
The drop lands eastward with one quarter the launch-return displacement magnitude. North–south corrections from horizontal Coriolis coupling are beyond the retained approximation.
