For a moving loop, Faraday's law of induction is E=∮(E+u×B)⋅dl=−dΦB​/dt, where u is the local wire velocity, ΦB​=∫B⋅dS, and the oriented boundary and surface follow the right-hand rule. For a fixed loop the motional term vanishes. Lenz's law is the minus sign: the induced response opposes the change of linked flux, not necessarily the applied field itself.
Let s increase downhill and choose positive current I to produce an uphill magnetic force. The normal component of the imposed field is Bcosθ, so the changing external flux is Blcosθs. With initially zero current and no resistance, conservation of flux linkage gives LI=Blcosθs. The rod feels gravity vertically downward, a rail reaction normal to the slope, and magnetic force of magnitude IlB horizontally uphill. Its downhill component is −IlBcosθ; its normal component presses the rod into the rails, giving N=mgcosθ+IlBsinθ.
