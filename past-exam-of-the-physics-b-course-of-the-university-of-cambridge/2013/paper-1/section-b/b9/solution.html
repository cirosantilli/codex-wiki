For cold, collisionless, unmagnetized electrons with stationary heavy ions, −me​ω2x=−eE. Therefore P=−nex=−ne2E/(me​ω2) and
ϵr​(ω)=1−ω2ωp2​​,ωp​=me​ϵ0​ne2​​.​
(21)
This is the cold collisionless plasma dielectric response. For a propagating transverse wave, B=k×E/ω, so E/B=c in vacuum and E/B=c/np​ in plasma, where np​=ϵr​​. Equivalently E/H=Z0​ and Z0​/np​ for μ=μ0​.
Converting the density gives n=1018m−3, so ωp​=5.64×1010s−1 and fp​=8.98GHz. At 10GHz,
ϵr​≃0.19384,np​≃0.4403.​
(22)
At an interface without independently imposed surface charge or sheet current, tangential E,H and normal B,D are continuous, with the electron response included in the effective permittivity. Normal E need not be continuous; the induced electron charge is represented by that response. In a convention treating it explicitly as a surface charge, the normal vacuum-displacement jump equals that charge instead.
For the downward transmitted wave, transversality gives kx​Ex​+kz​Ez​=0. The measured field therefore has kx​/(−kz​)=3, so tanθt​=3 and sinθt​=3/10​. Tangential phase matching gives
θi​=sin−1(np​10​3​)≃24.69∘.​
(23)
For inferring incident power from a transmitted plasma field, choose Ei​=Ei​(cosθi​,0,sinθi​) and Er​=Er​(cosθi​,0,−sinθi​). Their magnetic fields have opposite y directions. The two tangential conditions are
(Ei​+Er​)cosθi​=1Vm−1,Ei​−Er​=np​10​Vm−1.
(24)
Hence Ei​≃1.2464Vm−1 and Er​≃−0.1458Vm−1. Since these are peak phasor amplitudes,
⟨Si​⟩=2Z0​Ei2​​≃2.06×10−3Wm−2.​
(25)
This is incident power per area perpendicular to its propagation. Per area of the interface the incoming normal flux is ⟨Si​⟩cosθi​≃1.87×10−3Wm−2. The reflected sign is a phase reversal, not negative reflected energy.
At 1GHz<fp​, the relative permittivity is negative and there is no propagating transverse bulk wave. The field decays evanescently into the plasma and the ideal collisionless half-space reflects all incoming normal power. Collisions would allow absorption; a complex refractive index here does not by itself imply dissipative heating.
