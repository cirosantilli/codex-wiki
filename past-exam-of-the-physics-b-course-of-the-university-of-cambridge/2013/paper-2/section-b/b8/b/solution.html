Viscosity transfers momentum between adjacent fluid layers. A Newtonian fluid has shear stress proportional to strain rate: for simple shear, τxy​=η∂ux​/∂y​. Thus parallel plates of area A, separation d and relative speed U require force magnitude ηAU/d in a steady linear profile.

The Reynolds number ρUL/η compares inertial and viscous forces. Small Reynolds number supports a creeping-flow approximation, while large values permit thin viscous boundary layers and possible turbulence. It does not mean molecular viscosity has disappeared. Gas viscosity results from molecular momentum transport; a liquid's microscopic transport depends strongly on intermolecular interactions. Non-Newtonian materials require a more general constitutive law.
