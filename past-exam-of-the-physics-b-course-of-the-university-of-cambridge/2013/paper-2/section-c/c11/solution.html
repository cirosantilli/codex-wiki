A thermodynamic potential packages the equilibrium state in variables suited to the imposed constraints. For a simple fluid, U(S,V,N) has differential dU=TdS−pdV+μdN. Legendre transforms give H=U+pV, F=U−TS and G=U+pV−TS, with natural variables of a thermodynamic potential (S,p,N), (T,V,N) and (T,p,N) respectively. At fixed particle number, Helmholtz free energy is minimized at fixed T,V; Gibbs free energy at fixed T,p.
A flow vessel is an open control volume, even if its walls are enclosed. For each inlet, pressure forces carry flow work pdV in addition to internal energy. The steady first-law balance is
∑in​m˙(h+v2/2+gz)+Q˙​−W˙shaft​=∑out​m˙(h+v2/2+gz).
(32)
With no heat exchange, shaft work, or significant kinetic and gravitational energy differences, the enthalpy fluxes are equal. Mass flow must also balance. This equality concerns mass-flow-weighted specific enthalpies, not an unweighted sum over pipes.
Use the liquefaction yield with separate warm-gas and liquid outlets. The wording specifies that the outgoing gas is at 14 K; it does not require the outgoing liquid to have that temperature. The two outlet streams need not be in mutual thermal equilibrium. Taking H as the printed specific enthalpy, the steady-flow energy balance gives

This uses a low-pressure approximation: p0​=1 atm is not literally the zero-pressure table entry. Linear interpolation to 1 atm would instead give H(p0​)≃8.651×104Jkg−1 and α≃0.193. The sparse table does not determine an exact continuous optimum between entries.
Atmospheric-pressure liquid helium must be much colder than the 14 K gas outlet: its normal boiling point is about 4.224 K.[ref] A counterflow heat exchanger can return cold gas through the incoming stream before that gas leaves the overall control volume, while the collected liquid leaves separately at the cold end. This is a possible interpretation of the whole apparatus, rather than an extra diagram specified by the question. The enthalpy calculation needs only the stated inlet and outlet properties; it does not assume one equilibrium vessel containing both phases at 14 K. In particular, H(p0​)−Hℓ​ is an outlet-stream enthalpy difference, not the latent heat of vaporization at one common temperature.
