A Newtonian fluid exerts viscous shear stress proportional to the rate of strain. For an incompressible fluid of constant dynamic viscosity η,
σ=−pI+η[∇u+(∇u)T].
(34)
The local momentum balance is the Navier-Stokes equation:
ρ(∂t∂u​+u⋅∇u)=−∇p+η∇2u+ρg.​
(35)
The left side is density times material acceleration. A steady velocity field can still have convective acceleration when a moving parcel changes speed or direction; setting ∂u/∂t=0 does not eliminate it.
The Reynolds number Re=ρUL/η compares inertia with viscous effects. At low Reynolds number, the fluid's own inertia can often be neglected, giving Stokes flow. This does not say that every immersed body has zero acceleration: its mass and the net pressure, viscous and body forces still enter its own equation of motion.
For a small rigid sphere moving through a slowly varying viscous background, Stokes drag is −6πηa(v−u). In still fluid, a simple settling model includes weight, buoyancy and drag:
mv˙=(m−ρf​Vs​)g−6πηav.
(36)
Ignoring fluid added mass and unsteady history forces, this gives relaxation time m/(6πηa) and terminal velocity
vt​=9η2a2(ρs​−ρf​)g​.​
(37)
At the terminal velocity the net force and acceleration vanish, while viscous drag and gravity remain individually nonzero. The dissipated power 6πηavt2​ is supplied by the loss of gravitational potential energy relative to buoyant support. The creeping-flow and quasisteady-drag assumptions must be checked before applying this expression at large speed or immediately after a rapid start.
