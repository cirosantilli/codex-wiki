Mutual inductance expresses how a changing electric current in one circuit induces an electromotive force in another. Choose winding orientations and define their linked magnetic fluxes by
Λ1​=L1​I1​+MI2​,Λ2​=MI1​+L2​I2​.
(15)
For a reciprocal linear magnetic system, the same M appears in both expressions. Faraday's law of induction gives E2​=−MdI1​/dt when the secondary electric current does not itself change. The sign depends on winding orientation and is consistent with Lenz's law.
The stored magnetic energy is
U=21​L1​I12​+MI1​I2​+21​L2​I22​.
(16)
Its nonnegativity for arbitrary electric currents requires M2≤L1​L2​. Strong coupling makes ∣M∣ approach L1​L2​​; flux leakage makes it smaller.
An ideal electrical transformer has two windings linked by the same time-varying core magnetic flux. The induced winding voltages are proportional to their turn counts. For appropriately chosen terminal polarities,
V1​V2​​=N1​N2​​,V1​I1​=V2​I2​,I1​I2​​=N2​N1​​.​
(17)
The electric current convention in the power relation takes primary power into the device and secondary power out to the load. Consequently a load electrical impedance ZL​ appears at the primary as Zin​=(N1​/N2​)2ZL​. A larger secondary voltage therefore comes with a smaller available secondary electric current, rather than extra energy.
A steady direct electric current produces no sustained transformer action after its switching transient. Practical departures from the ideal include winding electrical resistance, leakage magnetic flux, finite magnetizing electric current, core saturation, eddy currents and magnetic hysteresis losses.
