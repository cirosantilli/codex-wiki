For the two unlabelled follow-up requests, distinguish re-equilibrating effusion from collisionless effusion. The calculations below show that escaping atoms carry mean kinetic energy 2kB​T, whereas a bulk atom has mean kinetic energy 3kB​T/2. If the aperture is small and interatomic collisions maintain near-equilibrium conditions, faster atoms escape preferentially, and the remaining gas relaxes toward a lower-temperature Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. No heat supplied by the walls is available to replace the lost energy.
For a monatomic gas at fixed container volume, conservation of energy gives a quantitative check on this effusive cooling picture:
d(23​NkB​T)=2kB​TdN,TdT​=31​NdN​.​
(62)
Since dN<0, the temperature falls. Under these idealizations T∝N1/3; the gas is not left with a permanently truncated velocity tail when collisions rapidly restore equilibrium.
If interatomic collisions are absent, the individual surviving atoms do not rethermalize to a new common temperature. Their speed distribution becomes selectively depleted, generally favouring slower survivors, and their angular and spatial distribution can also develop structure determined by which trajectories reach the hole. A single lower temperature does not describe the full nonequilibrium distribution. With a direction-randomizing but energy-preserving wall model, an illustrative survival law is f(v,t)∝f(v,0)e−κvt; specular walls can give more detailed directional selection and even persistent nonescaping trajectories. The question supplies no wall-scattering or container-geometry model from which a unique collisionless loss law could be inferred.
