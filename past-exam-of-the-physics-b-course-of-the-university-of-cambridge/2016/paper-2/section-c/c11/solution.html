The classical equipartition theorem assigns mean energy kB​T/2 to each independent quadratic term in the Hamiltonian, provided the canonical distribution and its relevant boundary integrals exist. For a quadratic term aq2, the Boltzmann distribution is proportional to e−βaq2, with β=1/(kB​T). Integrating a total derivative gives
0=∫−∞∞​dqd​(qe−βaq2)dq=∫e−βaq2dq−2βa∫q2e−βaq2dq.

After dividing by the normalization,
⟨aq2⟩=2β1​=21​kB​T.​
(56)
The same argument applies to quadratic momentum terms. Other independent canonical coordinates factor out of the ratio. It is a classical statement: widely spaced quantum energy levels can freeze out a contribution at low temperature.
The confining quartic potential energy U(x)=Qx4, with Q>0, is not quadratic, so the kB​T/2 rule cannot be assigned to it. This does not invalidate the canonical Boltzmann distribution or its generalized integration-by-parts identity. Indeed,

(57)

⟨U⟩=Q⟨x4⟩=41​kB​T,Cpotential​=dTd⟨U⟩​=41​kB​.​

Equivalently, Zx​=Γ(1/4)(βQ)−1/4/2 and −∂lnZx​/∂β=1/(4β). This is the potential contribution only; an additional standard kinetic term p2/(2m) would contribute a further kB​T/2 to energy and kB​/2 to heat capacity.
For an intact diatomic molecule, the classical degrees of freedom comprise three translational quadratic terms, two rotational quadratic terms, and two terms for its one bond-stretching vibration: one kinetic and one potential. Thus complete classical equipartition predicts
CV,molar​=27​R.​
(59)
The heat capacity of a diatomic gas has lower-temperature regimes because rotational and vibrational levels are quantized. In a dilute molecular gas, the expected progression is
RCV​​≃⎩⎨⎧​3/2,5/2,7/2,​rotations and vibrations frozen,rotations active, vibrations frozen,rotations and vibrations active.​
(60)
The rotational energy scale is normally much smaller than the vibrational scale, so these steps are separated. The high-temperature value treats the vibration as harmonic and excludes dissociation and electronic excitation; the lowest-temperature gas regime is meaningful only while the sample remains dilute and gaseous.[ref]
The plotted example uses a quantum rigid rotor and harmonic vibration, with an illustrative ratio of characteristic temperatures. It is a model calculation, not measured data for a particular molecular species. For the vibrational part,
RCV,vib​​=(TΘv​​)2(eΘv​/T−1)2eΘv​/T​,
(61)
which tends to zero on freezing out and to one when many vibrational levels are accessible. The classical equipartition theorem is recovered when the relevant level spacings are small compared with kB​T.
