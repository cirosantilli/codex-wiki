Take a long strip along y, occupying −w/2<x<w/2 in z=0, with uniform surface current K=I/w. Above the strip's centre, the contributions odd in x cancel, while the Biot-Savart law gives the magnetic field of a uniformly conducting strip
Bx​(0,z)=2πwμ0​I​∫−w/2w/2​x′2+z2zdx′​=πwμ0​I​tan−12zw​,z>0.
(1)
The field reverses below the strip. Its field lines wrap around the longitudinal current: nearly parallel to the faces near the centre, then curving round the edges and approaching circles at large distances. The sketch is an original field integration; the conductor thickness is idealized as negligible.
