Take ζ as signed distance from the neutral axis within the beam cross-section. Bending into a large radius R changes the length of a fibre proportionally to ζ/R, so linear elasticity gives σ=Eζ/R, up to the curvature sign convention. The moment magnitude is
B=∫A​σζdA=RE​∫A​ζ2dA=REI​​,I=∫A​ζ2dA.
(38)
Here I is the second moment of area about the neutral axis, with units of length to the fourth power, not the mass moment of inertia. A homogeneous cross-section has its neutral axis through the centroid under pure bending.
For the straight pin-ended beam, the compressive dead load produces bending moment −Fy at transverse offset y. In the small-slope approximation, curvature is y′′ with the corresponding sign convention, giving
EIy′′+Fy=0,y(0)=y(L)=0.​
(39)
The stated endpoints fix positions rather than slopes: this interpretation is consistent with the illustrated end forces and the later sinusoidal unstressed shape. Truly clamped endpoints would have a different threshold. A nonzero solution y=An​sin(nπx/L) requires Fn​=n2π2EI/L2. Thus the first Euler load is Fc​=π2EI/L2. Below it the perfect straight state is stable, at it the first mode is marginal in linear theory, and above it the straight state is unstable. The linear equation selects thresholds but cannot set the amplitude or describe a finite postbuckled equilibrium.
For the initially curved compressed beam, elastic stress responds to the change from its stress-free curvature. With y=y0​+η, the moment is EI(y′′−y0′′​)=EIη′′, so force balance gives
EIη′′=−F(y0​+η),η(0)=η(L)=0.​
(40)
Put q=π/L and seek the branch continuously connected to the unloaded shape. Since y0​=Ysinqx, substitute η=Csinqx to obtain −EIq2C=−F(Y+C), or
η(x)=Fc​−FF​YsinLπx​,y(x)=Fc​−FFc​​YsinLπx​.​
(41)
Away from eigenvalues, the homogeneous solution Asin(F/(EI)​x)+Bcos(F/(EI)​x) vanishes under the two end conditions, so this particular solution is the unique linear equilibrium. For 0≤F<Fc​ and Y>0, the maximum additional displacement is ηmax​=YF/(Fc​−F) and maximum total displacement is ymax​=Y/(1−F/Fc​), both at midspan.
This sinusoidal imperfection amplification of a column produces deflection at every positive load and strong amplification near Fc​. The apparent divergence signals loss of the small-slope model before infinite displacement; nonlinear geometry is needed there. The formal continuation above the first threshold is not a stable physical loading branch. Both total and additional displacement are distinguished in the graph.
