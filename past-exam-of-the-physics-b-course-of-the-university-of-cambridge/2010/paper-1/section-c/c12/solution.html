For excitation energy Δ=ℏω, the geometric series gives the thermal partition function of a quantum harmonic oscillator with its zero-point energy subtracted:

To count electromagnetic modes, put a large cavity in a periodic box of volume V. One transverse polarization has wavevector-state density V/(2π)3; there are two independent polarizations. In the shell k→k+dk,
dN=2(2π)3V​4πk2dk=π2V​k2dk.
(39)
With ω=ck, the electromagnetic mode density in a box becomes
g(ω)=dωdN​=π2c3Vω2​.​
(40)
The continuum expression is the large-cavity leading term; using standing-wave boundary conditions gives the same bulk count. Both photon polarizations must be included. This is density per angular frequency, not per ordinary frequency.
Each mode is an oscillator with zero photon chemical potential in thermal equilibrium. Multiplying its mean thermal energy by the mode density per volume gives the angular-frequency Planck energy density
u(ω,T)=π2c3ℏω3​eℏω/(kB​T)−11​.​
(41)

u(T)=∫0∞​u(ω,T)dω=π2ℏ3c3kB4​T4​∫0∞​ex−1x3​dx=15ℏ3c3π2kB4​​T4.​

The integral is the stipulated π4/15. This is a volume energy density, aT4; for isotropic cavity radiation the outward black-surface flux is cu/4=σSB​T4, with σSB​=π2kB4​/(60ℏ3c2). Distinguishing flux, spectral density and total volume energy avoids extra factors of c, 4 or 2π.
