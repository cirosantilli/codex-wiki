Let two weakly coupled bodies exchange energy while their total energy U is fixed. If body1 has U1​, the number of compatible states is Ω1​(U1​)Ω2​(U−U1​). Its most probable equilibrium division maximizes the logarithm, giving
∂U1​∂lnΩ1​​=∂U2​∂lnΩ2​​.
(32)
This equal intensive quantity labels thermal equilibrium. With S=kB​lnΩ and thermodynamic 1/T=(∂S/∂U)V,N​,
(∂U∂lnΩ​)V,N​=kB​T1​=β.​
(33)
Ordinary positive heat capacities make the entropy maximum locally stable. Particle numbers and volumes are held fixed during the exchange.
For a subsystem coupled to a large heat bath, the exact level weight is proportional to gj​Ωb​(U−ϵj​). Expanding the bath entropy gives lnΩb​(U−ϵj​)=lnΩb​(U)−βϵj​+⋯. Normalization therefore gives
P(ϵj​)=Zgj​e−βϵj​​,Z=i∑​gi​e−βϵi​.​
(34)
The Boltzmann form uses the reservoir limit; an arbitrary small isolated pair instead retains its exact bath state count. The degeneracy counts distinct subsystem microstates at the same energy, rather than adding another Boltzmann factor for the whole level.
In the two-level donor occupancy model, set the single bound-state energy to zero and the conduction representative to Δ=0.04eV with degeneracy g. Ignoring spin, its bound probability is
Pb​(T)=1+ge−Δ/(kB​T)1​.
(35)
The measured model occupancy gives ge−Δ/(kB​150K)=9, so
g=9e0.04/(150kB​)≃1.99×102≃200,​
(36)
using kB​=8.61733×10−5eVK−1. The supplied occupancy and energy are rounded, so there is no reason to demand an exact nonrounded integer from that inference.
For Pb​=0.9, the excited-to-bound weight must be 1/9. Eliminate g using the first point:
T1​=150K1​+Δ2kB​ln9​,T≃62.0K.​
(37)
The probability decreases monotonically from1 as T→0 toward 1/(1+g)≃0.005 as T→∞. The nonzero high-temperature limit follows from the stipulated finite effective degeneracy, not from a full continuum conduction-band calculation. A realistic band with energy-dependent density of states need not retain this two-level limit.
