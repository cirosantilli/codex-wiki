At fixed positive pressure, the extrapolated term Cmp​ln(T/T0​) tends to −∞ as T→0+. That prediction is not physically realistic for an equilibrium sample. The classical ideal-gas description with constant heat capacity cannot extend unchanged to absolute zero. A real substance generally undergoes phase changes, and quantum statistics and thermal freeze-out change its heat capacity and entropy. For a nondegenerate equilibrium ground state, the third law gives entropy approaching zero; residual ground-state degeneracy can give a finite residual value rather than this negative divergence. This is a breakdown of the extrapolation, not a violation of the third law by a real gas.
