An Euler strut is a slender initially straight elastic column under axial compression. For a uniform bending stiffness EI and a small transverse displacement y(x), the second-order potential energy under a conservative compressive load P is
Π(2)=21​∫0L​[EI(y′′)2−P(y′)2]dx.
(23)
The bending term is positive; shortening releases load potential. Stationarity yields EIy(4)+Py′′=0. Pinned ends impose y(0)=y(L)=0 and zero end bending moments, y′′(0)=y′′(L)=0. Substitution of y=Asin(nπx/L) gives
Pn​=L2n2π2EI​,Pcr​=L2π2EI​.​
(24)
The straight configuration loses stability first in the longest admissible bending mode. The second moment of area I=∫z2dA is geometric, not a mass moment of inertia. Boundary conditions matter: a fixed-free column has effective length 2L, while fixed-fixed ends have effective length L/2, changing the threshold by factors 1/4 and 4. Slenderness, elastic material response, alignment and the weak bending direction must be checked. Imperfections produce bending before the ideal threshold, and a short column can yield or crush first. Linear buckling theory does not predict an unlimited deflection or a complete postbuckling curve.
