stress describes internal force per area. A cut with unit normal n^ has traction t=σn^; its normal component is n^⋅σn^ and its remainder is shear traction. force balance constrains the stress divergence, while ordinary local angular-momentum balance makes the Cauchy stress tensor symmetric in a medium without body couples.

Crystals can be anisotropic and need a fourth-rank elastic stiffness tensor instead of the isotropic two-constant law.
elastic deformation is recoverable on unloading; it need not remain linear at large strain. Beyond yield, irreversible rearrangements such as dislocation motion cause plastic strain, often followed by hardening. Ductile samples can neck before fracture; brittle failure has much less plastic deformation. Engineering stress uses initial area, while true stress uses the changing instantaneous area. A decreasing engineering stress during necking therefore need not mean that local true stress decreases. stress concentrations, flaws and loading geometry can determine failure before a uniform-material strength is reached. Viscoelastic materials also show rate dependence, creep and stress relaxation; their losses cannot be described by a static Hooke law alone.
A tensile test should measure force and gauge-length extension independently, correct machine compliance, align the grips, and control strain rate and temperature. Unloading distinguishes recoverable strain from a residual plastic extension. Separate shear or hydrostatic tests determine moduli that one axial measurement does not fix. Compression specimens need friction and buckling checks. Work per volume is ∫σij​dϵij​; the recoverable part is elastic energy, while a dissipative cycle produces a loss rather than creating energy.
