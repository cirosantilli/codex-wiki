Below cutoff the ideal wave is evanescent, with penetration length c/ωp2​−ω2​; it does not propagate as an ordinary real-k bulk wave. Evanescence in the lossless limit is not Joule dissipation. Nonzero collision rate gives a dissipative conductivity and smooths this ideal cutoff picture.

A field amplitude falls as e−z/δ, while intensity falls as e−2z/δ. The phase velocity is ωδ in this limit. Magnetic and electric amplitudes are not related by the vacuum ratio: Zs​=E/H=(1−i)μω/(2σ)​ with the chosen time convention. Its positive real part is the surface resistance. The local mean heating is 21​Reσ∣E∣2, consistent with electromagnetic energy flowing into the metal. skin depth is an amplitude length; interpreting it as an intensity length gives a factor-of-two error.
A transmission-line mode propagates chiefly through the dielectric between conductors, even though finite metal conductivity causes loss at its boundaries. With per-length parameters R′,L′,G′,C′, the telegrapher's equations give, for the same e−iωt convention,
dzdV​=−(R′−iωL′)I,dzdI​=−(G′−iωC′)V.
(17)
Writing a forward wave as e−Γz, Γ=α−iβ, gives Γ2=(R′−iωL′)(G′−iωC′). In the low-loss regime R′≪ωL′ and G′≪ωC′, set Z0​≃L′/C′​ to obtain
α≃2Z0​R′​+2G′Z0​​,β≃ωL′C′​.​
(18)
This low-loss transmission-line attenuation separates series conductor loss from dielectric leakage loss. Average forward power falls as e−2αz. In the normal skin-effect regime, reduced conducting area makes the AC conductor resistance increase roughly as ω​; dielectric loss can supply a separate frequency dependence. Reflection from a mismatched termination changes delivered power without being, by itself, dissipation in the line.[ref]
To test loss, compare several lengths of otherwise identical line with matched terminations, calibrate connectors and incident/reflected power, and monitor temperature. Separate amplitude attenuation from phase delay and from standing-wave minima. Bulk penetration, line attenuation and dielectric propagation speed are related electromagnetic quantities but describe different experimental geometries.
