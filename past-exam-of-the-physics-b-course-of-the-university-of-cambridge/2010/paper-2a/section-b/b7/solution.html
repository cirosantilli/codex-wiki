Faraday's law for a moving circuit is E=∮(E+v×B)⋅dℓ=−dΦB​/dt. The magnetic flux ΦB​ is through a surface bounded by the instantaneous loop, with consistent orientation. For the stationary external field here, the motion supplies the motional electromotive force.
Let x be downward penetration of the bottom segment, v=x˙, and K=B0​a. While 0<x<b, flux grows at rate Kv. Define positive current as the direction whose magnetic force is upward. With negligible self-inductance, I=Kv/R and
mv˙=mg−KI=mg−RK2​v,τ=K2mR​.
(11)
The resistive magnetic entry of a falling loop gives
v=gτ(1−e−t/τ),I=RKgτ​(1−e−t/τ),x=gτ[t−τ(1−e−t/τ)].​
(12)
The initial acceleration is g and the speed approaches gτ if the loop remains partly immersed long enough. Joule heating I2R removes gravitational energy. At the first x=b, both horizontal segments are inside the uniform field: the net flux becomes constant, the net magnetic force vanishes, and this zero-inductance model has I=0 thereafter. The subsequent speed increases with acceleration g.
For negligible electrical resistance, conservation of total linked flux instead gives LI=Kx in the braking-current convention. Therefore
mx¨=mg−LK2​x,ω=mL​K​,
(13)
and the lossless limit of magnetic loop braking yields
x=ω2g​(1−cosωt),v=ωg​sinωt,I=Kmg​(1−cosωt).​
(14)
Here energy is stored reversibly in LI2/2, not dissipated. If b>2g/ω2, the loop reverses before complete entry and executes the ideal oscillatory motion while straddling the boundary. If b<2g/ω2, it reaches complete entry on its first descent; thereafter the current is constant, the forces on its two horizontal segments cancel, and it falls with acceleration g. The exact grazing case needs care at the ideal discontinuous field boundary. An entry-only sinusoid must not be extrapolated through full immersion.
