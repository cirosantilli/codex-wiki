A normal mode has all displacement coordinates proportional to a common time dependence, with fixed relative amplitudes. For a nonzero frequency the time dependence is sinusoidal. A zero-frequency mode is a free displacement or uniform drift rather than an oscillation.

This is the generalized eigenvalue problem for normal modes of three masses connected by two equal springs. Substitute x=ueiωt to obtain Ku=ω2Mu. These are all three eigenmodes. The last two keep the centre of mass fixed and are orthogonal in the mass-weighted inner product. The general zero-mode contribution is (A+Bt)(1,1,1), accounting for both a centre-of-mass shift and a conserved total momentum. There are no fixed end springs in this system.
The original amplitude profiles illustrate M=2m; the symmetric internal shape is rescaled for display. The formula above gives every positive mass ratio.
Two further physical examples are standing-wave modes of a string fixed at both ends, with spatial shapes sin(nπx/L) and frequencies nπc/L, and electrical normal modes of two coupled LC circuits, in which the capacitor charges oscillate at fixed relative amplitudes. Independent normal mode coordinates simplify both systems in the same way as the spring-chain eigenvectors.
