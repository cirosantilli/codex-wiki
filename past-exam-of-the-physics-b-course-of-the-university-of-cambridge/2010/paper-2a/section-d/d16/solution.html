Linearity means that the differential operator L=∂x2​−c−2∂t2​ obeys L(ay1​+by2​)=aLy1​+bLy2​. Therefore any linear combination of homogeneous solutions is again a solution; associated initial or boundary data must be combined by the same rule.
With characteristic coordinates ξ=x−ct and η=x+ct, the equation becomes 4yξη​=0. Integrating in these coordinates gives
y=F(x−ct)+G(x+ct).​
(47)
The two profiles propagate without changing shape at speeds +c and −c. Equivalently write y=P(t−x/c)+Q(t+x/c). At x=0, the data imply P+Q=f and −P′+Q′=0. Hence P−Q is constant; its two contributions cancel in y, giving
y(x,t)=21​[f(t−x/c)+f(t+x/c)],yx​(x,t)=2c−f′(t−x/c)+f′(t+x/c)​.​
(48)
The slope formula holds classically wherever the two derivatives exist, and as a weak derivative otherwise. Since the string tension is ρc2, ρ here must be mass per unit length. These are two-sided string data with zero slope. The stipulated boundary data specify incoming as well as outgoing waves; this is not a pulse generated on an everywhere initially resting infinite string. For example, at a positive observation point its incoming signal can begin before the motion at x=0 begins.
For explicit profiles put u=t/a, s=x/(ca) and
h(v)={sin(πv),0,​∣v∣≤1,∣v∣>1.​
(49)
Then ys​(u)=[h(u−s)+h(u+s)]/2 and cayx​=[−h′(u−s)+h′(u+s)]/2. At ordinary points, h′(v)=πcos(πv) for ∣v∣<1 and zero for ∣v∣>1. Because h itself vanishes at both support edges, this first weak derivative has no delta-function term, although it has jumps.
At x=ca/2, the displacement is
y1/2​(u)=⎩⎨⎧​21​cos(πu),−21​cos(πu),0,​−3/2<u<−1/2,1/2<u<3/2,otherwise.​​
(50)
For non-front times its slope is
cayx​=⎩⎨⎧​−2π​sin(πu),−πsin(πu),−2π​sin(πu),0,​−3/2<u<−1/2,−1/2<u<1/2,1/2<u<3/2,∣u∣>3/2.​​
(51)
This is displacement cancellation without slope cancellation in crossing pulses: displacement cancels throughout the overlap −1/2<u<1/2, but slope generally does not. Cancellation at one spatial position for an interval of time does not imply cancellation of the spatial derivative there. At u=±1/2,±3/2, the classical slope is not defined; the adjacent pieces specify its one-sided limits.
At x=ca the two windows meet without an open overlap. Their displacements combine into
y1​(u)={−21​sin(πu),0,​−2≤u≤2,∣u∣>2,​​
(52)
with slope
cayx​=⎩⎨⎧​−2π​cos(πu),2π​cos(πu),0,​−2<u<0,0<u<2,∣u∣>2 or u=0.​​
(53)
At u=±2 the classical slope is undefined. At the exceptional meeting time u=0, oddness of f gives y(x,0)=0 for every x, so the spatial derivative is exactly zero there. Its one-sided time limits at x=ca are −π/(2ca) and +π/(2ca); these do not determine its value at the meeting point.
The original graphs distinguish continuous displacements, open-ended one-sided slope traces, and the zero slope at the isolated meeting point. The sharp temporal window is not differentiable at its endpoints, so the overall solution is piecewise classical and globally weak. In particular the literal boundary slope at (x,t)=(0,±a) is not a classical derivative: the even spatial profile has a cusp there. The zero-slope boundary prescription is satisfied at regular times and in its almost-everywhere trace sense; it cannot be read as a globally smooth classical condition for this nonsmooth f.
