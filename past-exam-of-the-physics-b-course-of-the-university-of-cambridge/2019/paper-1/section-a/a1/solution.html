OurBigBook.com Words: 125
For two circular Kepler orbits about the same body, Kepler's third law gives T2/r3=constant. The reference radius is measured from Earth's centre:
r1​=(6400+120) km=6520 km.
(1)
A geostationary orbit is prograde and equatorial, with one sidereal day as its orbital period. Using Tg​≃1436 min and the supplied T1​=109 min,
rg​=r1​(T1​Tg​​)2/3≃3.64×104 km=3.64×107 m.​
(2)
Using 24 h instead changes this estimate by less than one per cent. This is the orbital radius, not the altitude; the corresponding altitude is about 3.00×104 km.
There is a numerical inconsistency in the source data. The quoted reference orbit implies GME​=4π2r13​/T12​≃2.56×1014 m3s−2, appreciably below the measured terrestrial value, about 3.99×1014 m3s−2. Using the latter instead gives the familiar geostationary orbit radius 4.22×104 km. The boxed estimate consistently uses the period actually printed in the question.
