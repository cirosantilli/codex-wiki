Closest approach. In the heavy-target approximation, a head-on alpha particle of charge 2e converts its initial kinetic energy to repulsive Coulomb potential energy at its turning point. The nuclear radius bound from a Coulomb turning point is therefore
Eα​=4πε0​rmin​2Ze2​,rmin​=5.51 MeV2(82)(1.440 MeVfm)​.
(9)
Thus the point-projectile interpretation of the radius bound is
RPb​≲4.3×10−14 m.​
(10)
It is an upper limit from Coulomb scattering, not a measurement of the actual nuclear radius. If “size” denotes diameter, the associated bound is twice this value. Including nuclear recoil, Ecm​≃[207/(207+4)]Eα​, increases the closest separation by about two per cent to 4.4×10−14 m.
Count-rate estimate and normalization. The graph has approximate slope ΔlnS/ΔlnA≃15/10=3/2. It therefore supplies the empirical log-log plot relation S∝A3/2. Calibrating its normalization with the lead observation gives
SAg​≃60(207108​)3/2≃23 particles per minute.​
(11)
The Rutherford differential cross-section at fixed beam energy and scattering angle instead has a Z2 dependence per target nucleus. More generally, the target normalization in Rutherford scattering rates is
SPb​SAg​​=NPb​NAg​​(8247​)2,
(12)
where N is the target nuclei per unit area. If the foils have equal N, the theoretical estimate is 19.7, or about 20 particles per minute, close to the graphical estimate at its rough level. Equal foil mass per area would instead give 37.8 per minute because N∝1/A.
The source does not specify that target normalization. Thus 23 per minute is the estimate from its supplied graph; an exact rate prediction from Rutherford theory alone is underdetermined without comparable foil areal densities. The empirical A3/2 slope and an exact equal-N Z2 law should not be presented as identical assumptions.
