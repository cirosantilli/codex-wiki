Two interface effects. For n2​<n1​, Snell's law gives sinθt​=(n1​/n2​)sinθi​. If sinθi​>n2​/n1​, no propagating transmitted ray exists: there is total internal reflection, with an evanescent wave on the far side. An optical fibre uses this to guide light.
For ordinary isotropic nonmagnetic media, tanθi​=n2​/n1​ is the Brewster angle. The reflected p-polarized component, whose electric field lies in the incidence plane, vanishes; the reflected s-polarized component generally remains. Reflection polarizers and polarized glare reduction exploit this selectivity.
The plasma response. Neglect collisions, thermal-pressure corrections and background magnetic field. Let the wave vary as e−iωt. An electron displacement obeys
−me​ω2xe​=−eE,xe​=me​ω2eE​.
(37)
Its dipole moment is −exe​, so the electron electric polarization is Pe​=−ne2E/(me​ω2). Ions of charge Ze give a further contribution Pi​=−nZe2E/(mi​ω2), smaller by Zme​/mi​. Thus heavy ions move negligibly at these electron-wave frequencies. Using D=ε0​E+P gives the cold collisionless plasma dielectric response,
εr​=1−ω2ωp2​​,ωp2​=me​ε0​ne2​.​
(38)
For this model μr​≃1, so nrefr2​=εr​ and c2k2=ω2−ωp2​ for the transverse electromagnetic wave.
The vertical cutoff and density. Below the plasma frequency, the squared refractive index is negative: the wave is an evanescent wave instead of propagating through a layer hundreds of kilometres thick. The observed 5 MHz cutoff therefore identifies fp​=5.0×106 Hz, with ωp​=2πfp​. Consequently
n=e2ε0​me​(2πfp​)2​≃3.10×1011 m−3.​
(39)
This uses ε0​=8.854×10−12 Fm−1, me​=9.109×10−31 kg and e=1.602×10−19 C. The density is also 3.10×105 cm−3.
The transmission elevation. At f=8 MHz, the ionosphere has refractive index
ni​=1−(85​)2​=839​​≃0.7806.
(40)
If α is elevation above horizontal, incidence at the layer is θi​=π/2−α. Avoiding total internal reflection requires sinθi​=cosα<ni​. The limiting elevation is therefore
αmin​=arccosni​=arcsin(5/8)≃38.7∘.​
(41)
Strictly, it must point above this limiting angle to carry upward power through the finite layer: at equality the transmitted wave is grazing. With a suitable satellite position, the stated orbital altitude imposes no additional angular constraint in this flat-layer approximation.
Polarization-selective ground reflection. The ray's incidence plane is vertical. Horizontal electric polarization is s polarization; the other transverse polarization lies in the vertical incidence plane and is p polarization. Reflection of s but not p identifies the Brewster angle, not total internal reflection, which would reflect both in this lossless isotropic model. Thus
tanθB​=ni​,θB​≃38.0∘.
(42)
For a reflecting height h=200 km, the horizontal range consists of two equal legs. The Brewster reflection from an ionospheric interface gives
D=2htanθB​=400ni​ km≃312 km.​
(43)
At this angle θB​ is below the critical incidence angle, so a transmitted ray also exists; the detected horizontal component is an ordinary partial reflection. The s-polarized reflected power fraction is approximately [(1−ni2​)/(1+ni2​)]2≃0.059, whereas the ideal p-polarized reflected fraction is zero. These results use the question's sharp planar interface model and exclude upper-interface reflections.
