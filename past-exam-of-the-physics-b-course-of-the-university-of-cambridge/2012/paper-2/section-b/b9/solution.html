A velocity field u(x,t) assigns a velocity to each point. Following a fluid parcel rather than holding position fixed gives the material or convective derivative
DtD​=∂t​+u⋅∇.​
(21)
The convective term can accelerate fluid even in a steady flow, for example as a stream narrows. Conservation of mass is ∂t​ρ+∇⋅(ρu)=0; constant-density incompressible flow has ∇⋅u=0.
For an incompressible Newtonian fluid of constant density and viscosity, with conservative body-force potential per mass Φ, momentum balance is
DtDu​=−ρ∇p​−∇Φ+ν∇2u.
(22)
Neglect viscosity and assume steady flow. Dotting with an element along a streamline, or using (u⋅∇)u=∇(u2/2)−u×(∇×u), gives
ρp​+2u2​+Φ=Balong a streamline.​
(23)
This is Bernoulli's equation: pressure work, kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy interconvert. In a connected irrotational region under the same assumptions, B is the same for all streamlines. In a rotational flow it can vary between them. Viscous dissipation, pumps or unsteady motion require the corresponding modified balance; the steady inviscid expression cannot be applied indiscriminately.
Vorticity ζ=∇×u measures local fluid rotation. For constant-density incompressible flow it evolves by
DtDζ​=(ζ⋅∇)u+ν∇2ζ,
(24)
showing vortex stretching and viscous diffusion. A potential flow has u=∇ϕ locally, so irrotational incompressible motion satisfies ∇2ϕ=0. Boundary conditions then determine flows around simple shapes. A multiply connected region can have circulation even when its local vorticity vanishes; ideal potential flow also misses no-slip boundary layers and viscous drag.
For a Newtonian fluid, shear stress is proportional to strain rate. No-slip at solid walls produces velocity gradients and dissipation. Fully developed laminar pipe flow, for example, has
uz​(s)=4μℓΔp​(R2−s2),Q=8μℓπR4Δp​.
(25)
The Reynolds number Re=UL/ν compares inertial and viscous terms. Small values favour creeping-flow approximations; large values can permit thin boundary layers and turbulence, without guaranteeing turbulence in every geometry. The appropriate flow model follows from conservation laws, constitutive properties, boundaries and scale comparisons.
