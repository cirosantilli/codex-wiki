For a central force, conserved angular momentum reduces radial motion to an effective potential Ueff​=U(r)+L2/(2mr2). Its extrema determine circular orbits and their radial stability. In several coordinates, small oscillations follow from the kinetic-energy mass matrix M and potential Hessian K through det(K−ω2M)=0.
Energy is also central to the Lagrangian L=T−U and the Euler-Lagrange equations. A time-independent natural mechanical system has conserved energy; cyclic coordinates yield additional momenta. Energy alone does not normally determine every coordinate of a multi-degree-of-freedom motion. Its value is to expose allowed motion, stability and reductions before solving the full differential equations.
