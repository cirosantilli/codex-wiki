At coexistence, the two phases have equal chemical potential. For a single component, dμ=−sdT+vdp, with entropy and volume measured on the same molar or mass basis. Equating the phase derivatives along coexistence gives Δvdp=ΔsdT. Reversible latent heat satisfies L=TΔs, hence
dTdp​=TΔvL​.​
(26)
Here p and T are coexistence pressure and absolute temperature, L is latent heat for transforming phase 1 to phase 2, and Δv=v2​−v1​ uses the same amount of material as L.
For vaporization, neglect liquid molar volume compared with ideal-vapour volume RT/p. The ideal-vapour Clausius-Clapeyron approximation becomes
dTdp​=RT2pLe​​,lnpb​p​=−RLe​​(T1​−Tb​1​).
(27)
Using the normal boiling reference Tb​=373.15K, pb​=1.01325×105Pa and the supplied constant Le​ gives
p(273K)≃8.2×102Pa.​
(28)
This deliberately constant-latent-heat extrapolation does not reproduce the supplied triple-point pressure exactly. It predicts the liquid-vapour branch, metastable against ice below the triple-point temperature; it is not a precision sublimation-pressure prediction.
The phase diagram has solid, liquid and vapour regions meeting at the triple point. The sublimation and vaporization boundaries have positive slopes, whereas the ordinary ice–water boundary slopes negatively because ice has larger specific volume. The liquid–vapour line terminates at a critical point; the original schematic below emphasizes topology and marks the supplied triple point.
For the melting-pressure estimate, Archimedes' principle gives ρi​=0.90ρw​=900kgm−3, so
Δv=vw​−vi​=10001​−9001​=−1.111×10−4m3kg−1.
(29)
Convert the molar evaporation heat using water molar mass M≃0.0180kgmol−1, rounded from the tabulated value 18.0153gmol−1.[ref] An enthalpy cycle through vapour then gives
Lf​=Ls​−MLe​​≃2.8×106−0.018040700​≃5.39×105Jkg−1.
(30)
This uses the question's temperature-independent evaporation heat all the way to the triple point; no replacement experimental fusion heat is inserted. The melting pressure inferred from flotation and latent heats, on holding Lf​ and Δv constant, is
p−pt​=ΔvLf​​lnTt​T​≃5.37×107Pa,p≃54MPa,​
(31)
using Tt​=273.16K and T=270.15K. Linearizing the logarithm about the triple point gives 53.4MPa instead of 53.7MPa. Both are estimates within the stated constant-property approximation; the negative melting slope makes a positive pressure necessary to lower the melting temperature.
