A heat engine's thermal efficiency is net work out divided by heat absorbed,
η=Qin​Wout​​=1−Qin​Qout​​,​
(37)
where both heat quantities are positive magnitudes. The last equality follows from the first law over a complete cycle.
Use n moles of an ideal gas with constant molar CV​,Cp​ and γ=Cp​/CV​>1. For the Otto cycle, reversible adiabats give T2​=T1​rγ−1 and T3​/T4​=rγ−1. Isochoric heat input and rejection are Qin​=nCV​(T3​−T2​) and Qout​=nCV​(T4​−T1​). Therefore
ηO​=1−T3​−T2​T4​−T1​​=1−r1−γ.​
(38)
The denominator's temperature difference is rγ−1 times the numerator's difference.
For the ideal Atkinson cycle, heating at fixed volume gives T3​/T2​=rp​. The longer expansion ends when p4′​=p1​. Combining the adiabatic pressure–temperature relation with p2​/p1​=rγ yields
T2​=T1​rγ−1,T3​=T1​rp​rγ−1,T4′​=T3​(p3​p1​​)(γ−1)/γ=T1​rp1/γ​.​
(39)
Also V4′​=V1​rp1/γ​>V1​ for rp​>1. The PDF asks explicitly for T3​,T4′​; the transcription additionally names T2​, which is supplied above too.
Heat input remains nCV​T1​rγ−1(rp​−1), while the isobaric return rejects nCp​T1​(rp1/γ​−1). Hence the Atkinson efficiency advantage from extended expansion is quantified by
ηA​=1−γr1−γrp​−1rp1/γ​−1​.​
(40)
Compare cycles with the same initial state, compression ratio and heat input. The extra expansion and constant-pressure return enclose positive extra work,
ΔW=∫V1​V4′​​[pexpansion​(V)−p1​]dV>0.
(41)
It uses heat that the Otto cycle would reject, while Qin​ is unchanged. Equivalently, concavity gives γ(rp1/γ​−1)<rp​−1, so ηA​>ηO​ for rp​>1 under this matched comparison. This is an ideal-cycle result; the question's named real-engine application is not needed to establish it.
