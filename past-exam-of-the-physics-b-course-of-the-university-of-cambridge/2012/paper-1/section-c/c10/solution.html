A normal mode is a motion in which all displacement coordinates oscillate at one angular frequency with fixed amplitude ratios. For small conservative oscillations, the symmetric stiffness and mass matrices give the generalized eigenvalue problem for small oscillations, Ku=ω2Mu. For positive mass and a stable equilibrium, its mass-orthogonal eigenvectors span the motion. A superposition of these modes, with phases and amplitudes fixed by initial displacements and velocities, gives the general solution; different modes need not share a phase.
Use upward end displacements. The spring compressions are x0​−x1​ and x0​−x2​, while the beam's centre rises by q=(x1​+x2​)/2. The potential energy is
V=2k​[(x0​−x1​)2+(x0​−x2​)2]+2mg​(x1​+x2​).
(30)
Static force balance gives 2kx0​=mg, cancelling the linear terms and leaving
V=21​k(x12​+x22​)+kx02​.​
(31)
For a uniform beam, its central moment of inertia in the vertical plane is ml2/12. To first order in the tilt, θ=(x2​−x1​)/l. Its translational and rotational kinetic energies are
T=8m​(x˙1​+x˙2​)2+24m​(x˙2​−x˙1​)2=6m​(x˙12​+x˙1​x˙2​+x˙22​).
(32)
Thus M=(m/6)(21​12​) and K=k1. The normal modes of a rigid beam on two end springs have eigenvectors
us​=(1,1),ωs​=2k/m​;ua​=(1,−1),ωa​=6k/m​.​

The first translates without tilt; the second rocks about a stationary centre. Ordinary frequencies are ωs​/(2π) and ωa​/(2π).
Assume the system had settled before the end load leaves, and put that load at end 1. Its extra static spring load is Mg at that end and zero at the other. Therefore x1​(0)=−Mg/k, x2​(0)=0, and both initial velocities vanish because no impulse is imparted. Decomposing into the two normal modes gives
x1​(t)=−2kMg​(cosωs​t+cosωa​t),x2​(t)=−2kMg​(cosωs​t−cosωa​t).
(34)
Only the symmetric mode contributes to the centre position:
zCM​(t)−zeq​=−2kMg​cos(m2k​​t).​
(35)
The datum zeq​ is the unloaded beam's equilibrium height; no absolute height is supplied. The rocking mode is present even though it cancels out of this coordinate. These results use small tilt, Mg/(kl)≪1, ideal light vertical springs and negligible damping.
