In a charge-free region of homogeneous permittivity, Gauss's law and E=−∇V give Laplace's equation, ∇2V=0. Prescribed surface electric potentials specify a Dirichlet problem. Its Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem permits replacing conductors by fictitious charges outside the physical solution region: a harmonic candidate with the same boundary conditions is the physical solution. For an exterior problem its behaviour at infinity must also be prescribed. For a proof, subtract two candidate potentials to obtain u with zero boundary values. Green's first identity gives ∫∣∇u∣2d3x=0, so u is constant and its boundary value fixes it to zero. Conditions on a normal electric field instead determine a potential only up to an additive constant when the usual compatibility condition holds.

Thus the circle, and its cylindrical extension, is an equipotential. This is an Apollonius circle: a constant ratio of distances gives a circle rather than a constant distance from either charge.
For the cylinder above the plane, call its centre height H>a and set s=H2−a2​. The cylinder-plane image charges are +λ at height s and −λ at height −s. Their distances are equal on the grounded plane, while their distance ratio is (H+s)/a on the circular surface. The positive fictitious line lies inside the metal, so the exterior is charge-free and Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem applies. A Gaussian surface enclosing the physical cylinder confirms that its charge per unit length is indeed λ. Its voltage is
