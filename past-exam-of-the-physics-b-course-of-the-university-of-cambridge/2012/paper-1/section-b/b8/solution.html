For a steady thin-wire electric current, the Biot-Savart law is
dB(r)=4πμ0​I​∣r−r′∣3dℓ′×(r−r′)​,
(19)
where the source element follows positive current. For a circular loop, source points are (Rcosϕ,Rsinϕ,0) and the observer is (0,0,z). Their cross product with dℓ′ has axial component R2dϕ; transverse components integrate to zero. Thus the on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop, multiplied by the number of coincident turns, is
B0​(z)=Bz​(0,z)=2(R2+z2)3/2μ0​N1​I1​R2​.​
(20)
Take positive current counterclockwise as seen from +z. magnetic field lines pass upward through the loop and return around its exterior, closing rather than terminating. They fan outward above its centre and converge below it. The original numerical Biot-Savart law integration below illustrates these directions; the wire singularities are masked.
The axisymmetric magnetic field near a regular axis follows from Gauss's law for magnetism. In cylindrical coordinates, regularity gives Bρ​(0,z)=0, while symmetry gives Bz​(ρ,z)=B0​(z)+O(ρ2). Integrate ∂ρ​(ρBρ​)=−ρB0′​(z) from the axis:
Bρ​(ρ,z)=−2ρ​B0′​(z)+O(ρ3),Bρ​(ρ,z)≃4(R2+z2)5/23μ0​N1​I1​R2ρz​.​
(21)
A formal C(z)/ρ term is excluded by regularity. For z>0 the axial field decreases upward, so the radial component is outward, consistent with the sketch.
On the small receiving coil, dℓ=adϕϕ​. The Lorentz force I2​dℓ×B has axial component −I2​aBρ​(a,d)dϕ. Contributions from the axial field cancel radially around the loop. Therefore
Fz​≃−2πN2​I2​aBρ​(a,d)=−2(R2+d2)5/23πμ0​N1​N2​I1​I2​R2a2d​.​
(22)
With both currents giving magnetic dipole moments along +z and d>0, this is attraction. Reversing one current reverses the force. The small coil has m2​=N2​I2​πa2, so the result is exactly the leading magnetic dipole force Fz​=m2​B0′​(d). Equivalently U=−m2​⋅B0​. The approximation requires a≪R and negligible variation over the receiving coil; corrections begin at relative order a2/(R2+d2).
