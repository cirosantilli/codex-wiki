For a slender elastic beam, the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation assumes small strain, small deflection slopes and cross-sections that remain plane and normal to the deflected centreline. A fibre at signed distance y from the neutral axis has longitudinal strain ϵxx​=−yκ, where κ is the centreline curvature. Hooke's law gives σxx​=−Eyκ, with Young's modulus E.
Choose the internal bending-moment sign so that M=−∫yσxx​dA. Then
M=EIA​κ,IA​=∫A​y2dA.​
(23)
Here IA​ is the second moment of area, with units m4, not the mass moment of inertia of a rotating body. For a uniform material under pure bending, zero axial resultant places the neutral axis through the section's centroid.
For small slopes, κ≃w′′(x). Combining the moment-curvature relation with transverse force and moment balance gives, with a consistent load sign convention,
dx2d2​(EIA​dx2d2w​)=q(x).​
(24)
For constant stiffness this becomes EIA​w(4)=q. The bending stiffness EIA​ has units Nm2. Boundary conditions specify displacement and slope at a clamped end, or bending moment and shear force at a free end.
For a cantilever of length ℓ carrying a transverse end load F, take deflection positive towards the load. The moment magnitude is M(x)=F(ℓ−x), and integration with w(0)=w′(0)=0 gives
w(x)=6EIA​Fx2(3ℓ−x)​,w(ℓ)=3EIA​Fℓ3​.​
(25)
The free-end moment vanishes and the tip shear supplies the applied load. For a rectangular section of width b and bending thickness h, IA​=bh3/12, so increasing thickness strongly increases bending resistance. The model ceases to be reliable for large deflections, plastic strains or short thick beams where shear deformation matters.
