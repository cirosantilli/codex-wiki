The printed formula has a physical problem for the specified geometry. Let χ=μ−1 be the linear magnetic susceptibility, where μ is the source's relative magnetic permeability. The constitutive equation is M=χHin​, not in general χH0​/μ.
For a small uniformly magnetized ellipsoid with its principal axis along the applied field, include its demagnetizing field through the SI demagnetizing factor Nd​:
Hin​=H0​−Nd​M,M=χHin​.
(49)
Solving gives
M=1+Nd​(μ−1)μ−1​H0​=2[1+Nd​(μ−1)](a2+z2)3/2(μ−1)a2I​.​
(50)
A slender rod aligned along the field has Nd​→0 as its aspect ratio grows. Its ideal slender-rod limit at fixed μ is therefore
Mrod​≃(μ−1)H0​=2(a2+z2)3/2(μ−1)a2I​,​
(51)
provided Nd​∣μ−1∣≪1. A finite cylinder generally has nonuniform magnetization, so an ellipsoidal shape factor is an approximation, and its aspect ratio is needed for a more precise correction. Small sample length compared with the field-variation scale makes H0​ approximately uniform across it; that condition does not set the demagnetizing factor to one.
The source's displayed M=(μ−1)H0​/μ instead follows from Nd​=1, the thin-plate limit with field normal to the plate. It can also be obtained by imposing Bin​=B0​, but that identification is not justified for a thin rod parallel to the field. Consequently the requested extra 1/μ cannot honestly be derived from the stated rod geometry. The next part gives both the shape-consistent result and the force conditional on using the printed magnetisation.
