For the intended ideal Venturi effect, take steady flow, incompressible flow, negligible viscous losses, and the same height at the two stations shown. The Bernoulli equation along a streamline gives
p1​+21​ρv12​=p2​+21​ρv22​.
(1)
Using water mass density ρ≃1000 kgm−3,
p2​−p1​=21​(1000)(22−62)=−1.60×104 Pa.​
(2)
Thus the outlet pressure is lower by 16 kPa. The increased kinetic energy per unit volume comes from the drop in pressure.
The source's assumption of laminar flow alone does not imply negligible viscosity or losses. The numerical result uses the usual lossless Bernoulli equation approximation, with the quoted speeds interpreted as representative streamline speeds. A real tube with significant viscous losses requires additional data; in a uniform-velocity energy balance its pressure drop would include an extra positive loss term.

The principal moments of inertia are the eigenvalues of the symmetric inertia tensor. In units of kgm2, its characteristic determinant is
det(I−λ1)=(1−λ)[(2−λ)2−1]=(1−λ)2(3−λ).
(3)
Therefore the requested algebraic answer is
I1​=3 kgm2,I2​=I3​=1 kgm2.​
(4)
An orthonormal basis of principal axes is (1,1,0)/2​, (1,−1,0)/2​, and (0,0,1); the last two span the degenerate eigenspace and may be replaced by any orthonormal basis of it.
There is a physical inconsistency in calling the supplied matrix a rigid body's inertia tensor. The moment-of-inertia triangle inequalities require each principal moment to be no greater than the sum of the other two. Here 3>1+1. In principal coordinates, I2​+I3​−I1​=2∫x12​dm cannot be negative for a nonnegative mass distribution. Thus the eigenvalue calculation is well defined, but no ordinary rigid mass distribution realizes this tensor. The positive second-moment matrix test for an inertia tensor makes the same obstruction explicit.

For the two-body approximation with a dominant central mass, Kepler's laws of planetary motion are:
  • Kepler's first law: a bound planetary orbit is an ellipse with the central attracting body at one focus.
  • Kepler's second law: the line from that focus to the planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, equivalent to constant areal velocity.
  • Kepler's third law: the squared orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis, T2=4π2a3/(GM) when the orbiting mass is negligible.
The first law includes a circular Kepler orbit as a special case. The third-law constant is the same for bodies orbiting the same dominant mass.

Use a Hohmann transfer orbit. At the initial radius r0​, make a short prograde burn parallel to the existing velocity. The increased speed changes the circular Kepler orbit into an elliptic orbit whose periapsis is the burn point and whose apoapsis lies on the desired larger circle. Coast for half the transfer orbital period to apoapsis.
At that point the transfer velocity is again tangent to the target circle, but its magnitude is smaller than the circular speed there. A second short prograde burn raises it to the required circular speed. Thus both burns increase the instantaneous speed and preserve its direction at the burn, even though the final circular speed is lower than the original one. During the coast the spacecraft loses kinetic energy while gaining gravitational potential energy.
The figure illustrates 3r0​ for the subsequent parts, but the same construction works for any larger target radius. The impulsive approximation neglects the distance travelled during each burn and keeps both circles and the transfer ellipse coplanar.

A normal mode of a linearized mechanical system is a motion in which every generalized displacement oscillates at a single common angular frequency, with fixed relative amplitudes and phases. In coordinates qj​, an undamped oscillatory mode may be written
qj​(t)=Aj​cos(ωt+δ),​

The electric field is approximately radial in the long interior, but near an electrode end its lines spread into a fringing field of a capacitor. The electric field magnitude falls on moving outward through that end region. The dielectric's induced electric dipoles experience a net force toward the stronger field, pulling the material into the capacitor. For a weakly perturbing polarizable element this is expressed by F=21​α∇E2, with positive electric polarizability α.
In the sketch the field lines run from the positive electrode to the negative one, curving outward near their ends. The material is attracted toward the interior where the field is stronger. A perfectly uniform interior field by itself would not give a longitudinal dipole-gradient force; the end region is essential to the local explanation. Neglecting its small contribution to the total capacitance does not justify omitting the finite insertion force that it transmits. For a linear induced response, reversing I reverses H0​ and the sample magnetization, while also reversing the field gradient. Their product in Fz​=μ0​Vχeff​H0​H0′​ therefore keeps the same sign and magnitude:
F(−I)=F(I).​
(56)
This is also explicit in the I2 dependence in part (d), for both the shape-consistent and printed alternatives. The conclusion assumes no independent permanent moment, remanence or hysteresis; those would require a different response model.

Changing shape changes the demagnetizing field, and hence the magnetization induced by the same applied magnetic field intensity. For a small linear sample in the ellipsoid approximation, the force magnitude scales with
χeff​=1+Nd​(μ−1)μ−1​.
(57)
For μ>1, a more elongated body aligned with the field has smaller Nd​ and a stronger response. A sphere has Nd​=1/3, giving χeff​=3(μ−1)/(μ+2); a very thin plate with field normal to it has Nd​→1, giving (μ−1)/μ. Thus an axially slender rod generally experiences a stronger attraction than a sphere or a transverse thin plate of the same material and volume, within the linear uniform-applied-field approximation.
The shape response is precisely why the 1/μ factor in part (c) cannot apply universally to every small magnetic body. Orientation also matters through the appropriate principal demagnetizing factor; for general nonellipsoidal bodies the internal field is not uniform and a single scalar correction need not be exact.
