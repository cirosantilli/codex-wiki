For the equilibrium estimate, use the Clapeyron equation for melting. The specific volume change on melting is Δv=1/ρw​−1/ρi​, and the latent heat of fusion L gives Δs=L/Tm​. Thus the common-pressure melting-point slope is
dpdTm​​=LTm​​(ρw​1​−ρi​1​).
(10)
Take Tm​≃273.15 K and the usual rounded water density ρw​≃1000 kgm−3, in addition to the supplied ρi​=920 kgm−3 and L=3.3×105 Jkg−1. Then
dpdTm​​≃−7.20×10−8 KPa−1,ΔTm​≃−0.353 K,
(11)
so
Tm​≃272.80 K≃−0.35∘C.​
(12)
The sign is negative because ice contracts on melting. This is a linear equilibrium estimate treating the load as a common local pressure on coexisting ice and water. The actual contact stress is nonuniform, and frictional heating, deformation and drainage also affect skating; the estimate is not a complete account of the lubricating water film.
