Cold air is denser than room air at approximately the same pressure. The ideal gas law gives a fractional mass density contrast of order Δρ/ρ∼ΔT/T. The initial outflow is therefore a gravity current driven by reduced gravity
g′=gρΔρ​∼gTΔT​.
(13)
For an illustrative refrigerator height H∼1 m, room temperature T∼295 K and temperature difference ΔT∼20 K, this gives g′∼0.66 ms−2. Balancing the released potential energy with kinetic energy gives U∼g′H​∼0.8 ms−1. The buoyancy exchange time of a cold-air enclosure is consequently
t∼g′H​H​=g′H​​∼1–2 s,of order seconds.​
(14)
Using constant acceleration over height H gives 2H/g′​∼1.7 s, consistent at this level. Warm air enters as cold air leaves, so unrestricted vacuum free fall is not the right acceleration model. No dimensions or temperatures are specified in the source, so an order-of-magnitude estimate with explicit representative values is the appropriate result; shelves and mixing can delay complete replacement.
