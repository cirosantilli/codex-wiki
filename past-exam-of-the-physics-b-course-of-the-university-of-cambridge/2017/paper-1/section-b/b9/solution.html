Field equations. In a homogeneous source-free nonmagnetic medium, D=ϵε0​E and B=μ0​H. Maxwell's equations become
∇⋅E=0,∇⋅H=0,∇×E=−μ0​∂t​H,∇×H=ϵε0​∂t​E.​
(36)
The first equation follows from ∇⋅D=0 for spatially constant nonzero ϵ. When the effective relative permittivity is negative below, its charge response is already included in D; it must not also be counted as an extra free source.
Amplitude relations and bulk dispersion. Work with complex phasors proportional to eikx−Kz−iωt. For H=(0,Hy​,0), the Ampere-Maxwell law gives
(KHy​,0,ikHy​)=−iωϵε0​(Ex​,0,Ez​),
(37)
so the amplitudes must obey
Ex​=ωϵε0​iK​Hy​,Ez​=−ωϵε0​k​Hy​.​
(38)
They are not arbitrary independent constants: the dispersion relation alone would not make arbitrary amplitudes satisfy the equations. These relations give ikEx​−KEz​=0, so the electric divergence vanishes; the magnetic divergence vanishes because there is no y dependence. The only nonzero component of Faraday's law of induction is
−KEx​−ikEz​=iωμ0​Hy​.
(39)
Substitution then gives
k2−K2=ω2ϵε0​μ0​=c2ω2ϵ​.​
(40)
This proves that all four field equations hold for the specified transverse magnetic polarization when both the amplitude relations and the dispersion relation are imposed.
Interface matching. Let region 1 be vacuum above the interface, with ϵ1​=1, K=β1​>0; region 2 has ϵ2​=ϵ, K=β2​<0. These signs make the evanescent fields decay as z→±∞. In the absence of free surface current, tangential Hy​ is continuous, giving a common interface amplitude. Tangential Ex​ is also continuous, hence
ωε0​iβ1​Hy​​=ωϵε0​iβ2​Hy​​,ϵβ1​=β2​.​
(41)
Normal Dz​ is continuous as well: both sides have Dz​=−kHy​/ω. This does not require Ez​ itself to be continuous; the material can have polarization surface charge. The normal magnetic field is zero on both sides.
Existence and surface dispersion. The opposite signs of β1​ and β2​ require ϵ<0. The two bulk equations are
k2−β12​=c2ω2​,k2−β22​=c2ω2ϵ​.
(42)
Use β22​=ϵ2β12​ to eliminate the decay constants. For the nontrivial interface branch,
k2=c2ω2​ϵ+1ϵ​,β12​=−ϵ+1ω2/c2​.​
(43)
A positive β12​ together with real propagating k gives ϵ<−1. A negative permittivity between −1 and zero is insufficient. This lossless planar surface-plasmon dispersion describes a surface plasmon polariton; ϵ=1 would instead be identical media and supplies no decaying bound mode.
Frequency range and long wavelengths. With ϵ=1−ωp2​/ω2, the existence condition becomes
0<ω<2​ωp​​.​
(44)
The upper value is a limiting frequency approached as k→∞, not an attained maximum at finite k. Substitution into the dispersion relation gives
k2=c2ω2​ωp2​−2ω2ωp2​−ω2​.
(45)
For ω≪ωp​, choosing propagation in the positive x direction,
k=cω​[1+2ωp2​ω2​+O(ωp4​ω4​)]≃cω​.​
(46)
It approaches the vacuum light line from larger k. The vacuum decay constant is then β1​≃ω2/(cωp​), so confinement becomes weak at low frequency. This calculation assumes the stated real, lossless effective permittivity; dissipation would require complex propagation and decay constants.
