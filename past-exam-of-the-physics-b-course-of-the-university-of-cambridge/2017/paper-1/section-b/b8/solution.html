Field law and centre value. The Biot-Savart law for a steady thin wire is
dB=4πμ0​I​∣r−r′∣3dℓ′×(r−r′)​.
(29)
Here dℓ′ follows the conventional electric current and the displacement points from source to observation point. For a circular loop of radius a, every element at the centre has distance a and contributes in the same axial direction. Choose +z by the right-hand rule. Then
B(0)=4πa2μ0​I​(2πa)z=2aμ0​I​z.​
(30)
On-axis integration. At axial distance z, every wire element has distance a2+z2​. Azimuthal symmetry cancels transverse components. Parameterizing r′=(acosφ,asinφ,0) gives axial cross-product element a2dφ, so the on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop is
B(0,z)=B0​(z)z,B0​(z)=2(a2+z2)3/2μ0​Ia2​.​
(31)
It points the same way on the two halves of the axis; its strength is even in z.
Directions near the axial point. At a point on the positive axis the field is axial, but nearby lines spread outward because B0​ decreases as z increases. On the negative-axis side they converge toward the axis while still progressing in the positive axial direction. There is no azimuthal field. The near-axis sketch shows this local spreading; the complete magnetic field lines return around the outside of the loop and form closed curves.
Radial component from zero divergence. Smooth axial symmetry gives Bz​(ρ,z)=B0​(z)+O(ρ2) and Bρ​=O(ρ). By Gauss's law for magnetism,
ρ1​∂ρ​(ρBρ​)+∂z​Bz​=0.
(32)
Integrate radially from zero, excluding a singular 1/ρ term, to obtain the near-axis radial magnetic field, Bρ​=−ρB0′​(z)/2+O(ρ3). Differentiating the axial field therefore gives
Bρ​≃4(a2+z2)5/23μ0​Ia2zρ​.​
(33)
For z>0 it is outward; for z<0 it is inward. This supplies the direction reasoning used in the sketch.
Force on the second loop. Put the second loop at z=d and radius ρ=a. For d≫a, the radial field due to the first loop is Bρ​≃3μ0​Ia3/(4d4). The element dℓ=adφφ​ experiences Lorentz force
dF=Iadφ(Bz​ρ​−Bρ​z).
(34)
The radial contributions cancel as vectors around the loop. The axial contributions add, giving the force between distant coaxial current loops,
Fz​=−2πaIBρ​≃−2d43πμ0​I2a4​.​
(35)
The sign follows φ​×ρ​=−z. Both the small radial coordinate relative to d and the dipole approximation are justified by a/d≪1.
Force direction. The second loop is pulled back toward the first: the force is attractive for the same current sense. Reversing one current reverses the force. The much larger axial field chiefly produces radial element forces; retaining the smaller radial field is essential for the net axial attraction.
