Inductance and interface conditions. For a linear coil, self-inductance is the ratio L=Λ/I of its own magnetic flux linkage Λ=NΦ to current. Its induced electromotive force is −LdI/dt for fixed L. Across an interface with unit normal n from medium 1 to 2,
n⋅(B2​−B1​)=0,n×(H2​−H1​)=Kf​.​
(21)
These electromagnetic boundary conditions follow from Gauss's law for magnetism and Ampère's law. Here Kf​ is free surface current. At the iron–air faces there is no free surface current, so normal magnetic flux density and tangential magnetic field intensity are continuous. In each isotropic linear medium, B=μ0​μr​H.
Complete core. A slender toroid has approximately uniform H along mean path length ℓ. Ampère's law gives Hℓ=NI, so B=μμ0​NI/ℓ. The flux per turn is Φ=BA, yielding the inductance of a toroidal winding,
L0​=INΦ​=ℓμμ0​N2A​.​
(22)
This uses constant relative magnetic permeability μ and neglects leakage, fringing and saturation.
Why the gap lowers inductance. The gap replaces an easy magnetic path in iron with air. Maintaining the same flux now requires a much larger magnetic field intensity in part of the path and therefore more ampere-turns. Equivalently, magnetic reluctance increases, so the flux linkage for a fixed current and hence self-inductance decrease. A narrow gap can matter greatly because its permeability is smaller by the factor μ.
Oblique-gap calculation. In the original diagram t is the perpendicular separation of the cut faces. Let s point along the core, and let n,q be normal and tangential to the gap faces. With the face at angle θ to the core direction, s=sinθn+cosθq. Write the core flux density as Bs. The boundary conditions give
Bg,n​=Bsinθ,Hg,q​=μμ0​Bcosθ​,
(23)
so
Hg​=μ0​B​(sinθn+μcosθ​q).
(24)
The gap area normal to its nearly normal field is A/sinθ, not A. Its leading reluctance is consequently tsinθ/(μ0​A). Adding the core reluctance gives the inductance of a toroid with an oblique narrow air gap,
NI≃μ0​B​(μℓ​+tsinθ),L≃ℓ/μ+tsinθμ0​N2A​.
(25)
Thus at θ=45∘,
L≃ℓ+μt/2​μμ0​N2A​.​
(26)
The approximation retains the possibly order-one ratio μt/ℓ while neglecting t/ℓ, fringing and the small tangential gap contribution. More precisely, a local uniform-field path along the core, with missing iron length t/sinθ subtracted, gives denominator ℓ/μ+tsinθ(1−1/μ). Its correction is beyond the stipulated large-μ, narrow-gap accuracy; simply neglecting μt would not be justified.
Field in the slot. The component expression above gives
Hg​=μ0​B​sin2θ+μ2cos2θ​​.
(27)
For the 45∘ gap and μ≫1,
Hg​≃2​μ0​B​≃t+2​ℓ/μNI​.​
(28)
Its units are Am−1. The field is almost perpendicular to the cut faces, rather than following the iron direction straight across the cut. Its small angle away from the face normal satisfies tanα=1/μ at 45∘; the sense reverses if the current reverses.
