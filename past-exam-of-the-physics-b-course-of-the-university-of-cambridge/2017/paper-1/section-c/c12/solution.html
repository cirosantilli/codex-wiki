Normal-mode idea. Near a stable equilibrium, expand the potential energy to quadratic order and the kinetic energy as a quadratic form in generalized velocities. The Euler-Lagrange equations then take the linear form Mx¨+Kx=0. A normal mode has fixed displacement ratios, x=qcos(ωt+φ), with Kq=ω2Mq. Real systems admit this description for sufficiently small motion, before higher-order nonlinearities or strong damping matter. Superposition permits a general motion to be assembled from the modes. A free symmetry direction may instead be a zero mode, with constant or linearly changing coordinate.
Lagrangian and bead equations. Take each xi​ as an arc-length displacement positive in the same sense around the fixed circular wire. Spring length changes are x2​−x1​, x3​−x2​ and x1​−x3​. Equal equilibrium spring tensions give no net tangential force; their linear energy terms sum to zero. Dropping an irrelevant constant, the Lagrangian is
L=2m​(x˙12​+x˙22​+x˙32​)−2k​[(x2​−x1​)2+(x3​−x2​)2+(x1​−x3​)2].​
(68)
For example, differentiating the potential with respect to x1​ gives k(2x1​−x2​−x3​). The three Euler-Lagrange equations therefore are
mx¨1​mx¨2​mx¨3​​=−k(2x1​−x2​−x3​),=−k(2x2​−x3​−x1​),=−k(2x3​−x1​−x2​).​​
(69)
The wire supplies normal constraint forces but does no tangential work. These are the equal-mass case of the normal modes of three beads connected by springs on a circular hoop.
Frequencies. The stiffness matrix divided by k is
K/k=​2−1−1​−12−1​−1−12​​=3I−11T,1=(1,1,1)T.

It annihilates 1. On the two-dimensional subspace q1​+q2​+q3​=0, its action is multiplication by three. Thus the angular frequencies of the normal modes are
ω0​=0,ω1​=ω2​=m3k​​.​
(71)
The repeated frequency is a degeneracy of normal modes, while the zero frequency expresses free rigid rotation of all beads along the wire.
General solution. Two independent vectors spanning the vibrating subspace are qA​=(1,−1,0)T and qB​=(1,0,−1)T. Each amplitude satisfies Q¨​+ω2Q=0, with ω=3k/m​. The uniform coordinate satisfies Q¨​0​=0. Hence
x(t)=qA​Acos(ωt+ϕ)+qB​Bcos(ωt+ψ)+1(Ct+D).​
(72)
The six freely specifiable real constants A,ϕ,B,ψ,C,D give the six initial data; ω is fixed by k,m and is not an additional independent integration constant. The linear zero-mode term must be included to allow nonzero total tangential momentum.
Shapes and uniqueness. In the first term beads 1 and 2 have opposite equal displacements and bead 3 remains at its equilibrium position. In the second term beads 1 and 3 do this while bead 2 stays fixed. The last term moves all three identically, leaving spring extensions unchanged: D is a rigid phase shift and C a uniform drift around the wire. The sketches show positive modal amplitudes; all arrows reverse half a vibration period later.
The two nonzero-frequency eigenvectors are not unique, because any independent linear combinations in their degenerate two-dimensional eigenspace form an equally valid basis. In particular the displayed pair is independent but not orthogonal; one could use (1,−1,0) and (1,1,−2) instead. The zero eigenspace is one-dimensional, so its vector is fixed up to scale and sign.
Specified release. Zero initial velocities permit ϕ=ψ=0 and require C=0. At t=0, the three displacement conditions become A+B+D=a, −A+D=0, −B+D=0. Therefore one convenient set of constants is
A=B=D=3a​,C=0,ϕ=ψ=0,ω=3k/m​.​
(73)
Equivalently,
x1​(t)=3a​[1+2cos(ωt)],x2​(t)=x3​(t)=3a​[1−cos(ωt)].​
(74)
Their sum remains a, as required by zero initial total tangential momentum. Alternative amplitude signs with compensating phase shifts represent the same motion.
