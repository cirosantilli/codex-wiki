An Euler strut is a slender elastic column under axial compression. Let its bending stiffness about the weaker section axis be EIA​ and its length be L. With small lateral deflection y(x) and a dead compressive load P, the quadratic total potential energy is
Π[y]=21​∫0L​EIA​(y′′)2dx−2P​∫0L​(y′)2dx.
(34)
The first term is bending energy; the negative term is work released by the load as bending shortens the end-to-end distance. Stationarity gives EIA​y′′′′+Py′′=0. For pinned ends, y=y′′=0 at 0,L, and y∝sin(nπx/L) requires Pn​=n2π2EIA​/L2. The first instability is Euler's critical load,
Pcr​=L2π2EIA​​.​
(35)
Below this load, the straight state has positive quadratic energy against these perturbations. At the threshold, the lowest bending mode has zero quadratic restoring energy; above it the straight state is unstable. For a rectangular section, IA​=bh3/12 about the appropriate bending axis, so the smaller second moment of area controls the first buckling direction.
Endpoint constraints matter. Write Pcr​=π2EIA​/(KL)2: K=1 for pinned-pinned, 2 for fixed-free and 1/2 for fixed-fixed conditions. This elastic instability differs from compressive material failure. Imperfections cause bending before the ideal threshold, and a short column may yield before Euler buckling occurs.
