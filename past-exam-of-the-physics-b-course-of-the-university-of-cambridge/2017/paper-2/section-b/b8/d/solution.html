Archimedes' principle states that the resultant hydrostatic pressure force on a body equals the weight of the fluid displaced, directed opposite to gravity. Let Vd​ be the displaced fluid region, with outward normal n from the body into the fluid. Since the fluid pressure force is inward,
Fb​=−∮∂Vd​​pndA=−∫Vd​​∇pdV.

Hydrostatic balance is ∇p=ρf​g, with downward gravitational acceleration g. Therefore
Fb​=−∫Vd​​ρf​gdV=−ρf​Vd​g​
(44)
for uniform fluid density and gravity. Uniform atmospheric pressure gives no net force on a closed surface. For a floating body, the same displaced-volume construction includes the free surface; any significant air buoyancy can be included as a second displaced fluid.
A freely floating body of mass m satisfies mg=ρf​gVd​, so its displaced volume is m/ρf​. A homogeneous body of density ρb​<ρf​ floats with submerged fraction ρb​/ρf​; if ρb​>ρf​, hydrostatic buoyancy alone cannot support it. The buoyant force acts through the centre of buoyancy, the centroid of the displaced volume for constant density, while weight acts through the centre of mass. Their relative positions determine rotational equilibrium and stability. For a fully submerged body, a centre of mass below the centre of buoyancy gives a restoring torque; a floating body's changing displaced shape additionally requires the metacentric height criterion. Surface tension, externally driven flow and contact forces can alter the simple force balance.
