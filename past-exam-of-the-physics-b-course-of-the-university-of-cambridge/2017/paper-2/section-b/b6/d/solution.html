The Poynting vector is the electromagnetic energy flux density,
S=E×H,[S]=Wm−2.​
(23)
In vacuum it equals E×B/μ0​. For a linear nondispersive medium with time-independent constitutive coefficients, Maxwell's equations and the identity ∇⋅(E×H)=H⋅∇×E−E⋅∇×H give Poynting's theorem,
∂t∂uEM​​+∇⋅S=−Jf​⋅E,uEM​=21​E⋅D+21​B⋅H.
(24)
The source term is work done by the fields on free charges; for an Ohmic conductor it is the positive local Joule heating rate σE2. Integrating over a volume states that loss of stored field energy plus outward energy flow equals energy transferred to matter. In dispersive media, such as a plasma, material motion contributes to stored energy and the simple nondispersive expression cannot be used indiscriminately.
For a vacuum plane wave, B=E/c and E, H and the propagation direction form a right-handed triad. With real peak field E0​, the time-averaged wave intensity is
⟨S⟩=2μ0​cE02​​=21​ϵ0​cE02​.
(25)
More generally, complex peak amplitudes give ⟨S⟩=21​Re(E0​×H0∗​). Along a transmission line, the cross-section integral of the longitudinal Poynting vector is the transmitted electrical power VI. Energy flows through the electromagnetic fields in the surrounding dielectric, even though the conductors guide the currents.
