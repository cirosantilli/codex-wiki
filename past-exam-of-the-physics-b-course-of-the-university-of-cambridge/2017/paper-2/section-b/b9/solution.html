Following the moving fluid. The Eulerian velocity field u(x,t) assigns a velocity to each position. A fluid particle satisfies x˙=u, so the chain rule gives the material derivative, also called the convective derivative,
DtD​=∂t∂​+u⋅∇,a=DtDu​.​
(45)
Even in steady flow a particle can accelerate because it moves between regions with different velocity. For example, flow around a curved obstacle changes direction despite ∂t​u=0. Mass conservation is
∂t​ρ+∇⋅(ρu)=0,DtDρ​+ρ∇⋅u=0.
(46)
For a constant-density incompressible fluid, ∇⋅u=0.
Euler equations for an inviscid fluid and Bernoulli's equation. Neglect viscosity and take the body force per unit mass to be −∇Φ. Newton's second law for a fluid element gives
ρDtDu​=−∇p−ρ∇Φ.
(47)
For steady flow, dot with displacement along a streamline. The tangential component of u⋅∇u is the derivative of u2/2 along that line. For constant density, integration gives
ρp​+2u2​+Φ=B,B constant along a streamline.​
(48)
In uniform gravity, Φ=gz. Thus pressure, kinetic energy per unit mass and gravitational potential energy per unit mass exchange along a streamline. For a barotropic compressible fluid, replace p/ρ by ∫dp/ρ(p). Different streamlines need not have the same Bernoulli constant.
Vorticity and potential flow. Vorticity is ω=∇×u. A small fluid element rotates locally with angular velocity ω/2 after its deformational part is separated. The vector identity
(u⋅∇)u=∇(u2/2)−u×ω
(49)
gives ∇B=u×ω for steady constant-density inviscid flow. Thus an irrotational region has one common Bernoulli constant on each connected region. In a simply connected irrotational domain, introduce a velocity potential u=∇ϕ. Incompressibility then gives Laplace's equation, ∇2ϕ=0. For unsteady irrotational flow,
∂t​ϕ+21​∣∇ϕ∣2+ρp​+Φ=C(t).
(50)
An impermeable stationary wall fixes ∂n​ϕ=0, but ideal potential flow does not enforce no slip. In a multiply connected domain, irrotational flow may still have nonzero circulation around an excluded vortex core, so a globally single-valued potential requires care.
For example, uniform two-dimensional flow past a circular cylinder has ϕ=U(r+a2/r)cosθ outside r=a. Its normal velocity vanishes at the cylinder, and its surface tangential velocity is −2Usinθ. Bernoulli's equation predicts a symmetric pressure distribution and zero drag. This is D'Alembert's paradox: the assumptions omit the viscous boundary layers, separation and wake responsible for real drag.
For an incompressible fluid of constant kinematic viscosity ν, the curl of the motion equation gives
DtDω​=(ω⋅∇)u+ν∇2ω.
(51)
The first term stretches and tilts vortex lines; the second diffuses vorticity. In two-dimensional incompressible inviscid flow the stretching term is absent and vorticity is carried with the fluid. In a three-dimensional inviscid flow it can be amplified by stretching. Density gradients not parallel to pressure gradients provide an additional source in a general compressible fluid.
Viscous flow. A Newtonian fluid's shear stress is proportional to rate of strain. For constant viscosity and incompressibility the Navier-Stokes equation is
ρDtDu​=−∇p−ρ∇Φ+η∇2u,​
(52)
with dynamic viscosity η and kinematic viscosity ν=η/ρ. At an ordinary solid wall, the no-slip boundary condition sets fluid velocity equal to the wall velocity. Viscosity transfers momentum between neighbouring layers and converts mechanical energy into heat; unlike the pressure force, its shear contribution depends on velocity gradients.
For plane Couette flow between plates y=0,h, with the upper plate moving at U, the steady solution is ux​=Uy/h, with shear stress ηU/h. For pressure-driven flow through a pipe of radius a, a constant pressure gradient gives the parabolic Poiseuille profile
uz​(r)=4η−dp/dz​(a2−r2),Q=8ηπa4​(−dzdp​).
(53)
The Reynolds number Re=ρUL/η compares convective inertia with viscous force. Small Reynolds number often permits the Stokes-flow approximation; large Reynolds number can produce thin boundary layers and, under suitable conditions, turbulence. It does not justify neglecting the wall conditions. Bernoulli's inviscid constant is generally not conserved along a viscous streamline, and pipe flow instead shows a pressure loss corresponding to dissipation.
