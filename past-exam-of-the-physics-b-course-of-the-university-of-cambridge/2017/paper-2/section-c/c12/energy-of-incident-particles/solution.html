Wall arrivals are not an unbiased sample of the gas: faster atoms arrive more frequently. The normalized speed distribution among incident atoms is
fhit​(v)=vvf(v)​.
(81)
The angular factor in the arrival rate cancels when calculating the mean kinetic energy. Thus
⟨K⟩hit​=2m​∫0∞​vf(v)dv∫0∞​v3f(v)dv​=2m​I3​I5​​.
(82)
Using I5​=2I3​/α gives
⟨K⟩hit​=αm​=2kB​T.​
(83)
By contrast, an atom chosen randomly from the bulk has mean kinetic energy 3kB​T/2. The arrival weighting raises the normal-direction contribution to kB​T while each tangential direction still contributes kB​T/2. This is also the mean energy carried by atoms effusing through a thin aperture from an equilibrated reservoir.
