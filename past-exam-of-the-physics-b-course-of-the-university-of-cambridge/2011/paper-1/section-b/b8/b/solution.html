Kepler's laws of planetary motion state that, in the relative two-body description, bound trajectories are ellipses with the attracting body at a focus, the radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal times, and the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semimajor axis for a fixed total mass. Their Newtonian derivation is especially informative. With relative coordinate r and μg​=G(M+m), the inverse-square acceleration is −μg​r/r3. Its zero torque conserves h=r×r˙, fixing a plane and the areal velocity h/2.
For u=1/r, the orbit equation is u′′+u=μg​/h2 as a function of polar angle. Therefore
r=1+ecos(ϕ−ϕ0​)h2/μg​​.

Bound negative-energy motion has 0≤e<1 and semilatus rectum h2/μg​=a(1−e2). Dividing ellipse area by areal velocity gives

For a planet much lighter than its star, replace M+m by M. Zero-energy and positive-energy encounters are parabolic and hyperbolic rather than periodic ellipses. Perturbations from other bodies and departures from point-mass Newtonian gravity can precess or otherwise modify the orbit; Kepler's laws are a two-body approximation, not an exact rule for every observed planetary path.
