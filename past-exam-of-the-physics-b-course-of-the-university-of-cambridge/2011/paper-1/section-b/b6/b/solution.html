An electric dipole has equal and opposite charges separated by a small vector from negative to positive, with electric dipole moment p=qd. More generally, a localized neutral distribution has p=∫r′ρ(r′)d3r′, independent of origin. At distances large compared with its size, the leading nonzero term in the multipole expansion is

The field points along p on either axial side and opposite p in the equatorial plane; its axial magnitude is twice the equatorial magnitude at equal radius. Near the charges the finite-charge expression must replace the far-field approximation.
A permanent dipole in an externally imposed slowly varying electrostatic field has interaction energy −p⋅E, torque τ=p×E and force ∇(p⋅E) for fixed p. A uniform field aligns it but gives no net force. For a linearly induced dipole p=αE, the formation-inclusive energy is −αE2/2, not −αE2: the moment itself grows while the field is applied. Dipole density gives electric polarization, whose bound charges are ρb​=−∇⋅P and σb​=P⋅n^.
