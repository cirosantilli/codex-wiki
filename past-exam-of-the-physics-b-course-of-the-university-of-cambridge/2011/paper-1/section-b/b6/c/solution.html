For a fixed circuit, Faraday's law of induction reads
∮C​E⋅dl=−dtd​∫S​B⋅dA,∇×E=−∂t∂B​.
(13)
Surface orientation and contour direction obey the right-hand rule. The negative sign is Lenz's law: a passive induced current acts against the flux change, consistently with energy conservation. The induced electric field generally has nonzero curl and cannot be represented by one single-valued electrostatic potential.
For a material circuit moving with local velocity u, the measured electromotive force includes the magnetic Lorentz force on its carriers:
E=∮C(t)​(E+u×B)⋅dl=−dtdΦB​[C(t)]​.
(14)
This distinguishes transformer induction in a stationary coil from motional induction in a changing circuit. A straight sliding rod of length l moving perpendicular to itself and to uniform B has ∣E∣=Blv. With circuit resistance R, the electrical power (Blv)2/R equals the external mechanical power needed to oppose magnetic drag. An ideal transformer gives V2​/V1​=N2​/N1​ in magnitude from common flux linkage; real leakage, resistance and core losses limit that simple result. pickup coils measure flux change rather than an absolute static magnetic field, so integration and a reference state are needed to recover a field history.
