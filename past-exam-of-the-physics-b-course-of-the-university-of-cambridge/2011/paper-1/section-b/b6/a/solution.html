Gauss's law connects electric flux through a closed surface to its enclosed charge:
∮∂V​E⋅dA=ϵ0​Qenc​​,∇⋅E=ϵ0​ρ​.
(11)
The differential form follows from the divergence theorem. The law is exact; extracting a field magnitude by simple division additionally needs symmetry. For spherical symmetry, Er​(r)=Q(r)/(4πϵ0​r2); a uniformly charged ball therefore has a field linear in r inside and an inverse-square field outside. An infinite line of linear charge density Λ gives Er​=Λ/(2πϵ0​r) by a cylindrical surface. A uniformly charged infinite sheet gives E=σ/(2ϵ0​) on either side by a pillbox.
An electrostatic conductor has zero interior field and constant potential; excess charge occupies its surface. A pillbox gives En,out​=σ/ϵ0​ when the inside is conducting. More generally, n^⋅(E2​−E1​)=σ/ϵ0​. For dielectric matter, ∇⋅D=ρfree​ separates bound polarization charge from free charge. Gauss's law alone does not establish a uniform field for an arbitrary finite plate or recover a detailed nonsymmetric charge distribution from one flux measurement.
