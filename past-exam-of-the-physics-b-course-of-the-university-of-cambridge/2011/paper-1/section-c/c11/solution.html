Keep the problem's one-mole convention throughout. The excluded-volume parameter b represents the loss of available translational volume due to short-range repulsion; it is an effective covolume, not simply the literal molecular material volume. The attraction parameter a reduces the pressure below its purely repulsive value. In a mean-field picture, interaction energy is proportional to pair density, so the attractive free-energy contribution scales as −a/V for one mole and the pressure correction as −a/V2. Equivalently, molecular cohesive forces and the number striking a wall each supply a density factor. These parameters approximate microscopic correlations rather than measuring every pair potential exactly.
At fixed temperature, dF=−pdV. Substituting p=RT/(V−b)−a/V2 and integrating gives the isothermal free-energy change of a Van der Waals gas
ΔF=−RTlnV1​−bV2​−b​+a(V1​1​−V2​1​).​
(34)
The arbitrary temperature-only term cancels. The internal-energy derivative is instead
(∂V∂U​)T​=T(∂T∂p​)V​−p=V2a​,ΔU=a(V1​1​−V2​1​).​
(35)
Expansion raises the attractive internal energy toward zero. As a check, the entropy change is Rln[(V2​−b)/(V1​−b)], so ΔF=ΔU−TΔS. Constant a,b and a homogeneous branch with V>b are assumed. If a subcritical equilibrium path crosses coexistence, a Maxwell construction replaces the unstable homogeneous continuation; the displayed integral is the direct equation-of-state result, not a claim that such a continuation is physically stable.
At the Van der Waals critical point, the critical isotherm has a stationary inflection:
0=−(Vc​−b)2RTc​​+Vc3​2a​,0=(Vc​−b)32RTc​​−Vc4​6a​.
(36)
Equating the resulting expressions for RTc​ gives 2Vc​=3(Vc​−b), hence
Vc​=3b,RTc​=27b8a​,pc​=27b2a​,RTc​pc​Vc​​=83​.​
(37)
In reduced variables x=V/Vc​, the critical curve is p/pc​=8/(3x−1)−3/x2: it decreases through (1,1) with zero first and second derivative, rather than having separate liquid/gas turning points at the critical temperature.
