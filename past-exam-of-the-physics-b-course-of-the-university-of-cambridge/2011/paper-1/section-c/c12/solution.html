In the Carnot cycle, let 1→2 be reversible isothermal expansion at Th​, 2→3 reversible adiabatic expansion, 3→4 isothermal compression at Tc​, and 4→1 adiabatic compression. The pressure–volume curve is clockwise. The entropy–temperature figure below uses temperature on the horizontal axis and entropy on the vertical axis, literally an S–T plot: isotherms are vertical and adiabats horizontal. The hot isotherm increases entropy by ΔS and absorbs Qh​=Th​ΔS; the cold one rejects Qc​=Tc​ΔS. Thus W=(Th​−Tc​)ΔS and ηC​=1−Tc​/Th​.
For the second engine, choose the working direction with heat addition during A→B at p1​ and rejection during C→D at p2​. A reversible ideal-gas adiabat obeys T∝p(γ−1)/γ. Define
r=(p2​p1​​)(γ−1)/γ>1,TC​TB​​=TD​TA​​=r.
(38)
The two constant-pressure heat transfers are Qin​=cp​(TB​−TA​) and Qout​=cp​(TC​−TD​)=Qin​/r, where rejected heat is quoted positive. Therefore the Brayton cycle efficiency is
η=1−r1​,ηmono​=1−(p1​p2​​)2/5​
(39)
for γ=5/3. All heat-capacity amount conventions cancel in the ratio.
Since TB​>TA​ and TC​=TB​/r, TD​=TA​/r, the maximum is at B and the minimum at D. The Brayton-Carnot efficiency comparison is
ηC​−η=(1−TB​TD​​)−(1−r1​)=r1​(1−TB​TA​​)>0.
(40)
The inequality is strict for a nondegenerate cycle with positive heat input. Although each process can be reversible, the isobaric heat transfer spans a range of temperatures; making the whole cycle reversible requires a succession of matched-temperature reservoirs, rather than just the hottest and coldest reservoirs.
For an ideal diatomic gas with active rotation and frozen vibration, γ=7/5 and
ηdiatomic​=1−(p1​p2​​)2/7<1−(p1​p2​​)2/5.
(41)
Here 0<p2​/p1​<1. This comparison holds at the same pressure ratio and assumes temperature-independent heat capacities in the stated mode regime. Frozen rotation or excited vibration would require their actual heat capacities.
