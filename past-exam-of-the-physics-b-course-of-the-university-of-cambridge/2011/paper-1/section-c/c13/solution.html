A microstate specifies the microscopic state: classically the positions and momenta of all particles, or quantum mechanically an allowed many-body state. A macrostate specifies a few coarse variables such as energy, volume and particle number. Many microstates can give the same macrostate. A statistical ensemble is a probability assignment over hypothetical copies prepared with the same macroscopic constraints; it does not require a sample to be literally divided into many physical copies. An observable's ensemble mean is ⟨A⟩=∑i​pi​Ai​, with an integral replacing the sum in classical phase space.
For an isolated system at fixed U,V,N, the microcanonical ensemble assigns equal probability to accessible microstates in a narrow energy shell. If their number is Ω(U,V,N), the Boltzmann entropy is S=kB​lnΩ. Thermal contact between two initially isolated systems allows energy to be shared while their sum stays fixed. The most probable division maximizes S1​(U1​)+S2​(U−U1​), so equilibrium requires equal ∂S/∂U. This gives the statistical temperature definition
T1​=(∂U∂S​)V,N​,β=kB​T1​=(∂U∂lnΩ​)V,N​.​
(42)
For positive temperature, energy tends to flow from lower entropy slope to higher entropy slope until those slopes agree. The narrow energy-shell width represents macroscopic resolution; it must not exclude the physically accessible states.
For a small subsystem exchanging energy with a much larger bath, the bath has energy Utot​−Ei​ when subsystem microstate i has energy Ei​. Expanding its entropy gives Sb​(Utot​−Ei​)=Sb​(Utot​)−Ei​/T+⋯. Counting compatible bath states therefore yields the Boltzmann distribution and canonical ensemble:
pi​=Ze−βEi​​,Z=∑i​e−βEi​,U=−∂β∂lnZ​,F=−kB​TlnZ.
(43)
The exponential weights individual microstates. An energy level of degeneracy gj​ has total probability gj​e−βEj​/Z; a more degenerate excited level can outweigh one ground-state level even though each of its microstates is less probable. The Gibbs entropy −kB​∑i​pi​lnpi​ gives S=kB​lnZ+U/T. The equilibrium energy variance is ⟨(ΔE)2⟩=kB​T2cV​, linking measurable fluctuations to a macroscopic response.
If particle exchange is also allowed, a reservoir fixes T and chemical potential μ. The grand canonical ensemble weights a state by e−β(Ei​−μNi​)/Ξ, with Ξ=∑N,i​e−β(Ei​−μN). Mean particle number is kB​T∂lnΞ/∂μ, while volume remains a constraint in this version. Thus the ensemble follows the physical exchange conditions: fixed energy, thermal contact, or thermal and particle contact.
In ordinary large short-range systems, relative fluctuations often decrease as N−1/2 and the ensembles give the same bulk thermodynamics away from singular limits. Finite systems, critical fluctuations and long-range interactions require care. Interpreting a time average as an ensemble average additionally requires appropriate sampling or mixing dynamics; it is not guaranteed solely by naming an ensemble. thermal equilibrium probability assignments do not describe every driven or relaxing state.
