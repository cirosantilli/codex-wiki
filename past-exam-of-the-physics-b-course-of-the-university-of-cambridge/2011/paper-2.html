Neglect leakage and fringing in the narrow gap, and take the cross section as uniform. Continuity of magnetic flux gives Hg​=Hr​+M. With no free current, Ampère's law gives lHr​+aHg​=0. Hence

Put the positive plate at z=d/2 and the negative plate at z=−d/2. Reflection through z=0, combined with reversal of the two plate charges, makes the tangential fields cancel at every point of the mid-plane. Thus Ex​=Ey​=0 at the corner as well as inside the capacitor.
For the uniform-charge estimate at a capacitor corner, assemble four identical square pairs around their common corner. Their normal fields there are equal by rotation symmetry and add to the central field σ/ϵ0​ of the larger plate pair. With the bulk parallel-plate relation σ≃ϵ0​V/d, the conventional corner estimate is
Ecorner​≃(0,0,−4dV​)(uniform charge sheets).​
(3)
This quarter-field result uses prescribed uniform surface charge, not symmetry alone for finite equipotential conductors. Real conducting plates redistribute charge near their edges; d≪a justifies the bulk relation away from the edges but does not remove the local corner fringe. For that exact conductor problem only the zero tangential components follow from the stated reflection symmetry; its normal coefficient requires a fringing-field boundary-value solution. The standard parallel-plate formula explicitly neglects edge effects.[ref]

ϕ−​(x,y,z)=4πϵ0​(1+ϵr​)r+​2Q​(x<0).​

LI˙+RI=CωI,I(t)=I(0)exp[LCω−R​t]
(15)
for prescribed constant ω. Thus
ωc​=CR​=μ0​Na22Rl​.​

Insert ∇×E=−∂t​B and ∇×H=J+∂t​D into the given identity. For a linear, time-independent, nondispersive medium, D=ϵE and B=μH, giving
∇⋅(E×H)=−J⋅E−∂t​u,u=21​E⋅D+21​H⋅B.
(19)
The divergence theorem then yields
∮S​(E×H)⋅dS=−∫V​[J⋅E+∂t​u]dV.​
(20)
The left side is outward electromagnetic power flow. The current term is power delivered to matter, becoming Joule heating for an Ohmic conductor, and ∂t​u is the change in stored electric plus magnetic energy. The minus sign says outward flux reduces stored energy unless supplied locally.
Follow the drawing's coordinates: lower strip at z=0, upper strip at z=d, current I along +x in the upper strip and back along the lower. The wording “both strips lie in the xy-plane” must mean parallel to that plane, since the drawing separates them in z. Neglect the side and end fringes using l≫a≫d. Then I=V0​/(R+RL​) and the top-strip potential is V(x)=V0​−IRx/l, while the lower strip is equipotential at zero.
The harmonic function matching those two surfaces is
ϕ=dz​(V0​−lIRx​).​
(21)
Indeed ∇2ϕ=0, and it has the required potentials at z=0,d. Thus
E=(ldIRz​,0,−dV(x)​),H=(0,aI​,0).
(22)
The two current sheets reinforce their magnetic field between the strips; its direction follows the right-hand rule. The Poynting flow in resistive parallel strips follows from their cross product:
S=(adIV(x)​, 0, aldI2Rz​).​
(23)
Power flows along the gap towards the load and upwards into the resistive strip. Across a transverse area ad, Px​(x)=IV(x) falls from IV0​ to I2RL​. The upper surface receives ∫0l​aSz​(z=d)dx=I2R, exactly the strip's heating; the ideal lower strip receives none. In the charge-free gap, ∂x​Sx​+∂z​Sz​=0, consistent with a static field and no local dissipation. The small longitudinal electric field cannot be discarded if this upward energy transfer is to be represented.

In a homogeneous, source-free, nonmagnetic dielectric the electric wave equation is
∇2E−c2ϵr​​∂t2​E=0.​
(24)
Set θ=ωt−kx and Ey​=f(z)cosθ. Substitution gives f′′+(ϵr​ω2/c2−k2)f=0. For the labelled field form (a), f=E0​cosKz, so K2=ϵr​ω2/c2−k2>0. For form (b), f=E0′​esK′z with s=±1, so K′2=k2−ϵr​ω2/c2>0. These give the two stated inequalities, with equality a zero-transverse-wavenumber limiting case.
Faraday's law gives the associated wave magnetic field, without an added static field,
H=μ0​ω1​(f′(z)sinθ,0,kf(z)cosθ).​
(25)
Thus in case (a), Hx​=−KE0​sin(Kz)sinθ/(μ0​ω) and Hz​=kE0​cos(Kz)cosθ/(μ0​ω). In case (b), Hx​=sK′E0′​esK′zsinθ/(μ0​ω) and Hz​=kE0′​esK′zcosθ/(μ0​ω). In both cases Hy​=0.
For the planar dielectric waveguide the core is dielectric but the exterior is vacuum. Consequently the exterior decay constant has K′2=k2−ω2/c2, while the interior oscillation has K2=ϵr​ω2/c2−k2. Adding gives the relation labelled (c):
K2+K′2=c2(ϵr​−1)ω2​.​
(26)
For an even bound mode the complete profile can be written
f(z)={E0​cos(Kz),E0​cos(Kd)e−K′(∣z∣−d),​∣z∣≤d,∣z∣≥d.​
(27)
Continuity of tangential Ey​ fixes the exterior amplitude; continuity of tangential Hx​ makes f′ continuous because both media have magnetic permeability μ0​. At z=d,
−KE0​sin(Kd)=−K′E0​cos(Kd),Ktan(Kd)=K′,​
(28)
which is the relation labelled (d). The same even profile also matches at z=−d; continuity of Hz​ follows from that of Ey​.
In the requested K,K′ plot, (c) is a first-quadrant circle and (d) rises from the origin to its first pole at Kd=π/2. Equivalently put u=Kd, w=K′d, and V=(ωd/c)ϵr​−1​. At u=0 the circle has w=V>0 whereas utanu=0. At the other end of 0<u<min(V,π/2), the rising tangent curve exceeds the falling circle. Monotonicity therefore gives one intersection on this first branch for every V>0.
The fundamental even mode of a symmetric dielectric slab has no positive cutoff in index contrast or thickness. Its electric profile is a nodeless cosine maximum at the mid-plane, smoothly joined to decaying exterior tails. The qualifier ϵr​>1 is essential: for the printed limiting value ϵr​=1, the circle collapses to K=K′=0 and there is no transversely confined bound mode. A uniform unconstrained wave is not a guided mode. This even-mode argument does not assert that the specified cosine describes every possible odd guide mode.

Relative to an origin, the inertia tensor is Iij​=∫(r2δij​−ri​rj​)dm, with J=Iω. Its orthogonal eigenvectors are principal axes, with eigenvalues the principal moments of inertia. Different components of ω are multiplied by different eigenvalues, so angular momentum need not point along angular velocity.
For a uniform thin disc, radial integration gives Iz​=∫r2dm=ma2/2. The perpendicular axis theorem and symmetry then give Ix​=Iy​=Iz​/2, hence
Id​=ma2/4about a diameter.​
(29)
For the elastic eccentric impact of a thin disc, neglect gravity's impulse and take the pole's impulse as vertical. Let z point up and let the contact position relative to the centre be rx^. An upward impulse P gives
vC​=−u+P/m,ω=−Id​rP​y^​.
(30)
For an elastic impact with a fixed pole, the contact point reverses its normal velocity, so −u+P/m+r2P/Id​=+u. Therefore
P=1+4r2/a22mu​,vC​=ua2+4r2a2−4r2​,ω=−a2+4r28ur​y^​.​
(31)
Here vC​>0 denotes upward motion; the centre can still move down if r>a/2. Substitution gives
vcontact​=vC​+(ω×rx^)z​=u.​
(32)
This also follows by combining conserved angular momentum about the fixed contact with conserved kinetic energy and selecting the separating, nonzero-impulse solution. At r=0 the disc simply rebounds without rotation; at r=a/2 its centre is instantaneously stationary. The rebound result concerns the contacting material point immediately after this ideal impact, not its subsequent contact history.

A normal mode is a small-oscillation motion with every coordinate proportional to one common sinusoid, at a fixed amplitude ratio and relative phase. Expanding an arbitrary small motion in normal mode coordinates decouples its linear equations into independent oscillators. With mechanical mass matrix M and mechanical stiffness matrix K, the generalized eigenvalue equation is Ku=ω2Mu.
Horizontal gravitational restoring force on each bob is −mi​(g/l)xi​. The two springs add k(x2​−x1​) on the first bob, k(x1​−2x2​+x3​) on the middle bob, and k(x2​−x3​) on the last. Dividing by the bob masses gives the stated equations with
α=g/l,β=k.​
(43)
Reflection symmetry separates an antisymmetric end motion from two symmetric motions. The three coupled pendulums with equal end masses have independent modes
amplitude vector(1,1,1)(1,0,−1)(1,−2m/M,1)​ω2g/lg/l+k/mg/l+k/m+2k/M​motionall bobs together; springs unstretchedend bobs opposite; middle at restends together; middle opposite.​​
(44)
Call their positive frequencies ω0​,ωa​,ωs​ respectively. The middle amplitude in the last mode ensures mx1​+Mx2​+mx3​=0. The modes are orthogonal in the mass-weighted inner product, rather than generally in the unweighted coordinate dot product.
The initially stationary displacement decomposes as
(0,0,d)=M+2mmd​(1,1,1)−2d​(1,0,−1)+2(M+2m)Md​(1,−2m/M,1).
(45)
There are no sine terms because every initial velocity is zero. Therefore
x1​(t)x2​(t)x3​(t)​=M+2mmd​cosω0​t−2d​cosωa​t+2(M+2m)Md​cosωs​t,=M+2mmd​(cosω0​t−cosωs​t),=M+2mmd​cosω0​t+2d​cosωa​t+2(M+2m)Md​cosωs​t.​​
(46)
These give (0,0,d) at t=0 and solve all three coupled equations. The spring constant is assumed positive; for k=0 the frequencies become degenerate and the same superposition reduces to uncoupled pendulum motion.
The original example uses M=2m and k/m=g/l to display the mode vectors and resulting superposition. Those illustrative parameter ratios are not additional supplied data.
