Insert ∇×E=−∂t​B and ∇×H=J+∂t​D into the given identity. For a linear, time-independent, nondispersive medium, D=ϵE and B=μH, giving
∇⋅(E×H)=−J⋅E−∂t​u,u=21​E⋅D+21​H⋅B.
(19)
The divergence theorem then yields
∮S​(E×H)⋅dS=−∫V​[J⋅E+∂t​u]dV.​
(20)
The left side is outward electromagnetic power flow. The current term is power delivered to matter, becoming Joule heating for an Ohmic conductor, and ∂t​u is the change in stored electric plus magnetic energy. The minus sign says outward flux reduces stored energy unless supplied locally.
Follow the drawing's coordinates: lower strip at z=0, upper strip at z=d, current I along +x in the upper strip and back along the lower. The wording “both strips lie in the xy-plane” must mean parallel to that plane, since the drawing separates them in z. Neglect the side and end fringes using l≫a≫d. Then I=V0​/(R+RL​) and the top-strip potential is V(x)=V0​−IRx/l, while the lower strip is equipotential at zero.
The harmonic function matching those two surfaces is
ϕ=dz​(V0​−lIRx​).​
(21)
Indeed ∇2ϕ=0, and it has the required potentials at z=0,d. Thus
E=(ldIRz​,0,−dV(x)​),H=(0,aI​,0).
(22)
The two current sheets reinforce their magnetic field between the strips; its direction follows the right-hand rule. The Poynting flow in resistive parallel strips follows from their cross product:
S=(adIV(x)​, 0, aldI2Rz​).​
(23)
Power flows along the gap towards the load and upwards into the resistive strip. Across a transverse area ad, Px​(x)=IV(x) falls from IV0​ to I2RL​. The upper surface receives ∫0l​aSz​(z=d)dx=I2R, exactly the strip's heating; the ideal lower strip receives none. In the charge-free gap, ∂x​Sx​+∂z​Sz​=0, consistent with a static field and no local dissipation. The small longitudinal electric field cannot be discarded if this upward energy transfer is to be represented.
