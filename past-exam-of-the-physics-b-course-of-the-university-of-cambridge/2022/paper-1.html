The bending moment B(x) is the internal torque transmitted across a cut through an elastic beam, about the transverse bending axis. It balances the moments of the external forces on the isolated portion on one side of the cut. Its sign depends on the chosen convention, and its units are Nm.
The relevant “moment of area” is the second moment of area of the cross-section about its neutral axis:
I=∫A​z2dA,​
(29)
where z is perpendicular distance from that axis within the section. It has units m4 and is a geometrical quantity, unlike a mass moment of inertia. For a homogeneous beam under pure bending, the neutral axis passes through the section's centroid, and its longitudinal strain is zero.

Assume a slender homogeneous elastic beam obeying linear elasticity, with plane sections remaining plane and no resultant longitudinal force. A length element of its neutral axis subtending a small angle dϕ has length Rdϕ. A neighbouring fibre at distance z from that axis has a length change of magnitude zdϕ, giving longitudinal strain ε=z/R with signs chosen consistently for tensile and compressed fibres. By Hooke's law, its longitudinal stress is σ=Eε=Ez/R.
The torque of these longitudinal forces about the neutral axis is therefore
B=∫A​zσdA=RE​∫A​z2dA=REI​,R=BEI​.​
(30)
Here E is Young's modulus and I the second moment of area. The displayed relation uses a signed radius and bending moment; an unsigned geometrical radius is ∣R∣=EI/∣B∣. For consistency with the negative bending moments in the later parts, take the deflection y positive downward and the signed curvature as 1/R≃y′′. Then the small-slope Euler-Bernoulli beam equation reads EIy′′=B. Reversing the vertical coordinate reverses the corresponding sign relation but leaves the physical deflection unchanged.

The taper concentrates magnetic flux into a smaller pole-face area, increasing the gap magnetic field intensity and magnetic flux density for the same winding electric current. If μh≪Lα2, the enhancement approaches 1/α2=(R/r)2. If instead μh≫L, the gap dominates the magnetic reluctance and the enhancement approaches unity: both fields approach NI/h.
The benefit is therefore strongest when the untapered core's contribution is significant, while the taper lengths contribute little. The same-core-length convention is the comparison defined by parts (d) and (e); if one instead fixes the full iron path length, the untapered comparator has length L+2s, a difference negligible in the stated limit. A smaller gap area can increase the field even though the total magnetic flux need not increase. Magnetic saturation and magnetic fringing, excluded from this model, limit the ideal concentration.
