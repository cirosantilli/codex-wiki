This is the Brayton cycle. Assume fixed gas amount, constant heat capacities, and internally reversible adiabatic processes, as intended by the ideal engine model. Put rp​=ph​/pl​=p2​/p1​>1 and
a=γγ−1​,k=rpa​.
(56)
Combining pVγ=constant with the ideal gas law gives T∝p(γ−1)/γ along either reversible ideal-gas adiabat. Therefore
T1​T2​​=k,T4​T3​​=k,T3​−T2​=k(T4​−T1​).
(57)
During a constant-pressure process the heat supplied equals the enthalpy increase. If Cp​ is the molar heat capacity at constant pressure, the positive heat input and output are
Qin​=nCp​(T3​−T2​),Qout​=nCp​(T4​−T1​).
(58)
The insulated branches exchange no heat, so the first law of thermodynamics gives W=Qin​−Qout​. Hence
η=1−Qin​Qout​​=1−k1​=1−(p1​p2​​)(1−γ)/γ.​
(59)
An adiabatic process alone need not be an isentropic process; the relations used here also require internal reversibility. Dissipative compression or expansion would require extra information and would not, in general, obey this efficiency formula.
