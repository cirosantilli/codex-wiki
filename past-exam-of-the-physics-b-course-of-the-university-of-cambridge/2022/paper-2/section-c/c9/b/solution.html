Each independent cavity mode is a quantum harmonic oscillator of angular frequency ω. Its occupation numbers n=0,1,… represent the numbers of photons in that mode. At thermal equilibrium, photons can be absorbed and emitted by the walls, so their number is not conserved and the photon chemical potential is zero. The Boltzmann distribution of the oscillator excitations then gives
⟨n⟩=eℏω/(kB​T)−11​,Uˉ=ℏω⟨n⟩.
(66)
The expression supplied in the paper is the thermal excitation energy. The oscillator's temperature-independent zero-point energy is excluded from the emitted thermal radiation.
Putting ϵ=ℏω and dϵ=ℏdω into the mode count from (a) gives the number of modes per unit cavity volume:
L3dN​=π2c3ω2​dω.
(67)
Multiplying by the mean thermal energy in each mode yields Planck's law in the angular-frequency convention:
u(T,ω)dω=π2c3ω2dω​eℏω/(kB​T)−1ℏω​.​
(68)
The two photon polarizations are already included in the density of states; no further factor of two is needed. u(T,ω)dω has units Jm−3, whereas u(T,ω) has units Jsm−3.
