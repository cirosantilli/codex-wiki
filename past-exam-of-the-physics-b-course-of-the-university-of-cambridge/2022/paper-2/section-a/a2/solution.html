Use the pipe-diameter convention for the Reynolds number, with mean velocity vˉ, density ρ and dynamic viscosity μ:
Re=μρvˉD​,Dcrit​=ρvˉRecrit​μ​.
(4)
The Reynolds number compares inertial and viscous effects in the fluid flow. A conventional estimate for the onset of turbulence in a pipe is Recrit​≃2000, giving
Dcrit​=(789kgm−3)(0.5ms−1)2000(3.16×10−4Pas)​=1.60×10−3m.
(5)
Hence the usual onset estimate is D≳1.6mm​. Using the also common transition criterion Recrit​=2300 gives 1.84mm, whereas Re≳4000, used for established turbulence, gives D≳3.2mm. There is no universal sharp minimum: inlet disturbances and pipe conditions affect the transition from laminar flow, so the threshold convention must accompany the answer.
