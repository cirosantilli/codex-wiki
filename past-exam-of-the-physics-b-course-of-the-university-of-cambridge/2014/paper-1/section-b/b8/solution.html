The macroscopic Maxwell equations, with free charge and current, are
∇⋅D=ρf​,∇⋅B=0,∇×E=−∂t​B,∇×H=Jf​+∂t​D.
(12)
Inside this body D=ϵ0​E and B=μ0​H. The wave magnetic field lies along z; its sign follows the local wave component. Faraday's and Ampère's laws give
∂x​Ey​=−μ0​∂t​Hz​,J=(0,−∂x​Hz​−ϵ0​∂t​Ey​,0).​
(13)
Static background fields are excluded from this wave solution. The magnetic force density is along x, with fx​=μ0​Jy​Hz​. For a steady periodic state, Hz​∂t​Ey​​=−Ey​∂t​Hz​​. Therefore
f​x​=−∂x​(21​μ0​Hz2​​+21​ϵ0​Ey2​​).​
(14)
The result gives radiation-force density without Ohm's law. Integrating through the body gives its pressure as the boundary value of the averaged electromagnetic energy density minus its value deep inside. In both limiting cases below the latter is zero. Take A as the incident electric field's peak amplitude and write Zv​=μ0​/ϵ0​​.
