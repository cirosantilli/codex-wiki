Guided electromagnetic waves propagate along a structure whose boundaries select transverse field patterns. At a perfect conductor the tangential electric field vanishes. In a hollow rectangular guide, TE modes have Ez​=0 and TM modes have Hz​=0. Their longitudinal wavenumber obeys
kz2​=v2ω2​−k⊥2​,k⊥2​=(amπ​)2+(bnπ​)2.
(12)
Below the waveguide cutoff frequency ωc​=vk⊥​, that mode is evanescent rather than freely propagating. TM modes require both indices positive; TE modes may have one zero but not both. For a>b, the lowest cutoff is the TE10​ mode, with fc​=v/(2a). A hollow single-conductor guide has no TEM mode, whereas a two-conductor transmission line can support one without this geometrical cutoff.[ref]
For a propagating TE or TM mode, vp​=v/1−ωc2​/ω2​ and vg​=v1−ωc2​/ω2​. The phase velocity can exceed v without superluminal information transfer. Frequency-dependent group delay can broaden pulses; excitation of several modes adds intermodal dispersion.
