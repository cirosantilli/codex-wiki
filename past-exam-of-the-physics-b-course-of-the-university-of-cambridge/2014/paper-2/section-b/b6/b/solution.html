In magnetostatics, Ampère's circuital law in vacuum is
∮C​B⋅dℓ=μ0​Ienc​,∇×B=μ0​J.
(9)
It is especially useful when symmetry makes the tangential field constant on an Amperian loop. It gives B=μ0​I/(2πr) for an infinite straight wire, B≃μ0​nI inside a long solenoid, and B≃μNI/(2πr) in an ideal toroid. The long-solenoid and toroidal expressions neglect fringing.
For time-dependent fields, displacement current is essential:
∮C​B⋅dℓ=μ0​Ienc​+μ0​ϵ0​dtdΦE​​.
(10)
In matter, the corresponding macroscopic form is ∮H⋅dℓ=Ifree​+d∫D⋅dA/dt. The enclosed electric current alone is sufficient only in the magnetostatic limit.
