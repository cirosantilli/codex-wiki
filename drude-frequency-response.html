Jx​(t)=1+ω2τ2​σ0​E0​​cos(ωt−ϕ),ϕ=arctan(ωτ).

Thus a positive argument of complex conductivity in the e−iωt convention means that current density lags the electric field. At ωτ=1, its real part and imaginary part are both σ0​/2, its modulus is σ0​/2​, and the lag is π/4.
For ωτ≪1, complex conductivity tends to the positive constant σ0​: current density nearly follows the electric field. For ωτ≫1 within this model,
σ(ω)∼mωine2​,Reσ∼mω2τne2​,ϕ⟶2π​.
(39)
The current density amplitude decreases and its response becomes predominantly inertial. Average power absorbed per volume is p​=21​Reσ∣E∣2≥0. These limits describe the material response to a specified local electric field, not the complete electromagnetic response of a finite specimen with contacts. The Drude response has its pole at ω=−i/τ, consistent with the decaying causal response of a first-order relaxation equation.
The simple Drude model does not predict the microscopic momentum relaxation time. It neglects electronic band structure and Fermi-Dirac statistics; a useful semiclassical extension replaces m by an appropriate band effective mass, but neither a single scalar mass nor one constant momentum relaxation time describes every material. Several charge carriers, energy-dependent scattering, and transitions between electronic bands can spoil the simple Drude frequency response. A successful low-angular frequency fit does not justify extrapolation to arbitrarily large angular frequencies.
