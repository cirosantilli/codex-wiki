Synaptic vesicle

 Home Biology Neuroscience Neuron Synapse Chemical synapse
OurBigBook.com Words: 323 Articles: 2
A synaptic vesicle is a small membrane-bounded compartment in a presynaptic terminal that stores neurotransmitter for release. Fusion with the cell membrane exposes its contents to the synaptic cleft; the packet supplied by one synaptic vesicle is a quantum of release. Quantal neurotransmitter release refers to these discrete packets, rather than the release of one molecule at a time. The postsynaptic response to a packet can vary with its contents, receptors and recording conditions. See Purves and colleagues: vesicles as the source of transmitter quanta.
The useful cycle is filling, preparation for fusion, calcium-triggered exocytosis, and recovery of membrane and proteins through endocytosis for reuse. Different recovery routes occur; recycling need not preserve a particular synaptic vesicle as an identifiable container throughout. Limited availability of prepared synaptic vesicles can reduce release during repeated stimulation, while changes in synaptic release probability can also change successive responses. See Basic Neurochemistry: secretion machinery and vesicle reuse and 3.1 Synapses.
For an illustrative calculation, assume four release sites, each with one prepared synaptic vesicle, each releasing with synaptic release probability p=0.25 during one presynaptic action potential. Assume independent Bernoulli trials and at most one release per site. The released packet count K has a binomial distribution:
K∼Bin(4,0.25),E[K]=4(0.25)=1,Var(K)=4(0.25)(0.75)=0.75.
(67)
The probability of no release is P(K=0)=0.754=0.31640625; at least one release has probability 0.68359375. An expected value of one packet does not mean that every action potential releases one packet. If each packet contributes a stipulated 0.5nS conductance increment and increments add, the expected value of that increment is 0.5nS, with zero increment on about 31.6% of trials. These assumptions are a teaching model: shared calcium fluctuations, unequal sites, depletion and nonlinear receptor responses can invalidate them.
