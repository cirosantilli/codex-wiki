Likelihood-ratio test for an exponential support endpoint (R=(1−X(1)​/X)n, nn−1​(−2logR)∼χ22​ under H0​)

 ... Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical hypothesis test Likelihood-ratio test Generalized likelihood-ratio test
OurBigBook.com Words: 207
Let Xi​=θ+Ei​ for n≥2 independent and identically distributed observations, with Ei​ having an exponential distribution of unknown scale β>0. Test the null hypothesis θ=0 within θ≥0. For nondegenerate data, the likelihood function is
L(θ,β)=β−nexp[−n(X−θ)/β]1{0≤θ≤X(1)​}​.
(28)
Maximizing over the scale gives β​(θ)=X−θ, and the resulting profile likelihood increases with θ. Thus the unrestricted maximum-likelihood estimates are θ=X(1)​ and β​=X−X(1)​, while the null estimate is β​=X. The likelihood ratio is consequently
R=(1−XX(1)​​)n.​
(29)
Under the null, put A=nX(1)​ and D=∑i​(Xi​−X(1)​). The memoryless property gives independent gamma distributions of shapes 1 and n−1 and common scale β. For a direct verification, fix which observation is the minimum and write its value as z and the other observations as z+ej​. Their joint density is proportional to e−nz/β∏j​e−ej​/β, which factors into an exponential density for the minimum and independent exponential densities for the excesses. The minimum's index does not affect the resulting laws of A,D.
By beta sampling by gamma ratios, D/(A+D)∼Beta(n−1,1). Therefore
Pr0​(R≤k)=k(n−1)/n,0≤k≤1.
(30)
An exact test size α is obtained by rejecting for R≤αn/(n−1). Equivalently,
nn−1​(−2logR)∼χ22​.​
(31)
Although the alternative adds one endpoint parameter, a regular one-parameter Wilks theorem calibration is incorrect: the support depends on the endpoint, and the null lies on its boundary. In particular, −2logR tends to a chi-squared distribution with two statistical degrees of freedom, not one.
