X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy

 Home Chemistry Physical chemistry Photoemission spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy uses X-ray excitation to investigate electron removal energies, including those of core electrons. The common name electron spectroscopy for chemical analysis emphasizes its use in distinguishing chemical surroundings. The whole recorded spectrum can also contain electrons produced by processes other than direct photoionization; a peak assignment must identify the emission process as well as the chemical element.
In a modern implementation, a quartz crystal selects aluminum characteristic X-rays according to Bragg's law and focuses them onto the sample. Vacuum reduces gas collisions and contamination; a separately pumped entry chamber limits exposure to air. Lenses collect photoelectrons; an electrostatic electron energy analyzer transmits a narrow kinetic energy band. Stepping that band and counting detector pulses produces the spectrum. An electron flood source can compensate positive electric charge on insulating samples. Ion cleaning can alter vulnerable samples.[ref]
For a scan with count Nj​ and dwell time tj​ at setting j, compare rates Nj​/tj​, rather than raw counts when dwell times differ. Under an independent Poisson distribution model, the count standard deviation is approximately Nj​​ for a sufficiently populated bin. Longer acquisition reduces this relative counting measurement uncertainty; it does not automatically improve separation of overlapping peaks. Photoelectron peak overlap and resolution gives an example in which the same chemical separation produces either two maxima or one.
