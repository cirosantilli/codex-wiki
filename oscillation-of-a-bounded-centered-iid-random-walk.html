Oscillation of a bounded centered iid random walk (liminfSn​=−∞,limsupSn​=+∞)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Stochastic process Bounded increments Bounded-increment martingale convergence-or-oscillation dichotomy
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
Let Sn​=∑j=1n​Xj​, where the increments are nonconstant independent and identically distributed random variables with zero expected value and ∣Xj​∣≤c. Then the random walk oscillates between arbitrarily large positive and negative levels almost surely. For Ta​=inf{n:Sn​≥a}, the stopped martingale a+c−Sn∧Ta​​ is nonnegative. The martingale convergence theorem implies that Sn​ converges finitely on {Ta​=∞}. However, some δ>0 has P(∣X1​∣>δ)>0, and the Borel-Cantelli lemmas imply ∣Xn​∣>δ infinitely often. Hence finite convergence is impossible. Apply this to every positive integer a and to −Sn​.
The bounded increments also imply infinitely many visits to [−c/2,c/2]: a path cannot cross from above c/2 to below −c/2 without entering this interval. Identical distribution cannot simply be dropped. For independent symmetric signs εj​, the increments Xj​=c4−(j−1)εj​ satisfy ∣Sn​∣≥2c/3 for every n, even though they are nonzero, independent, symmetric and uniformly bounded.
