Minimum roughness property of a clamped cubic spline (J(T)=J(S)+J(T−S),J(u)=∫x0​xn​​(u′′)2dx)

Let S be a clamped cubic spline interpolant and let T have the same values at all spline knots and the same endpoint slopes. For T∈C2[x0​,xn​], or more generally T′ an absolutely continuous function with square-integrable second derivative, the exact displayed identity holds. In particular S uniquely minimizes the second derivative roughness penalty J among these competitors.
To prove the identity, put E=T−S. On each knot interval, two applications of integration by parts give ∫S′′E′′=[S′′E′−S′′′E], since the cubic piece has zero fourth derivative. Terms containing E vanish at the spline knots; terms containing E′ cancel at interior spline knots because S′′ and E′ are continuous. The endpoint terms vanish because E′ is zero there. Thus ∫S′′E′′=0, and expanding (S′′+E′′)2 proves the identity. Equality makes E′′=0, so E is an affine function; a prescribed endpoint value and slope force E=0.
If T also matches the first derivative of S at every spline knot, both E and E′ vanish at the ends of each knot interval. For a sufficiently differentiable T, integration by parts separately on that interval gives the stronger local identity ∫ET(4)=∫(E′′)2≥0.
