Spatial sampling of a wave (∣k∣<π/p)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Partial differential equation Wave equation Wavenumber
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
In spatial sampling of a wave, a detector records a wave at positions xn​=np separated by pitch p>0. Angular wavenumbers differing by 2πm/p, with integers m, give identical samples of a complex Fourier mode:
ei(k+2πm/p)np=eiknp.
(69)
This is spatial aliasing. To distinguish arbitrary sinusoids within a prescribed band, require ∣k∣<π/p, equivalently ∣νx​∣<1/(2p), the Nyquist spatial frequency. Thus p<λmin​/2 for the shortest allowed wavelength. At equality a sine can vanish at every sample, so phase information can be lost. The Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem states the precise reconstruction conditions for a bandlimited function; a finite scan introduces further limitations.
The synthetic two-tone snapshot is
Au(x)​=cos(2πν1​x)+21​cos(2πν2​x),ν1​=2m−1,ν2​=8m−1,
(70)
where both νj​ count cycles per metre. The component wavelengths are 0.50m and 0.125m. With p=0.05m, both lie below the Nyquist spatial frequency of 10 cycles per metre. With p=0.10m, the 8-cycle component aliases to the 2-cycle component: at every xn​=n/10, cos(2π⋅8xn​)=cos(2π⋅2xn​). The sampled sum is therefore indistinguishable from 1.5cos(4πx), although the continuous curves differ between samples. Drawing lines between sample points cannot resolve this ambiguity.
