LIGO differential strain measurement (hD​=(δLx​−δLy​)/L)

OurBigBook.com Words: 671 Articles: 1
Each LIGO instrument is a Michelson interferometer with approximately 4km arms containing Fabry–Pérot interferometers. A stabilized 1064nm laser illuminates suspended 40kg fused-silica mirrors in vacuum. Four-stage suspensions and active isolation suppress ground motion; low-loss materials reduce thermal noise. Power recycling increases circulating power; signal recycling shapes the response to the gravitational wave.[ref]
Why measure two perpendicular arms? In the long-wavelength limit, take a gravitational wave with plus polarization normally incident on ideal perpendicular arms. Its transverse-traceless tensor has hxx​=h+​ and hyy​=−h+​. Expanding each proper length to first order gives
LδLx​​=2h+​​,LδLy​​=−2h+​​,ΔL=δLx​−δLy​=Lh+​.
(1)
Here h+​ is dimensionless, whereas ΔL is a length. A stipulated h+​=10−21 and L=4000m imply ΔL=4×10−18m, with individual arm changes of opposite sign and magnitude 2×10−18m. These are equivalent differential arm-length changes, not a photograph of mirrors moving against a rigid external ruler. The observable compares returning light phases; coordinate systems can distribute the description between mirror coordinates and light propagation.
For unit arm directions u,v, define the detector tensor Dij=(uiuj−vivj)/2. If eij+​,eij×​ are the transverse polarization tensors, then
hD​=DijhijTT​=F+​h+​+F×​h×​,FA​=DijeijA​.
(2)
The dimensionless factors FA​ encode sky direction and the polarization basis. Thus even identical sources can have different observed amplitudes and wave phases. Motion changing both arms equally cancels in this ideal differential observable, although real asymmetries can couple common disturbances into it.
Suspension supplies an inertial reference above its resonances. For example, a single ideal harmonic oscillator with support motion y obeys x¨+ω02​(x−y)=0. Its response at angular frequency ω is x/y=ω02​/(ω02​−ω2), which decreases in magnitude as (ω0​/ω)2 well above resonance. The mirror follows slow support drift but approximately free motion in the high-frequency limit. Real multistage suspensions also require damping and alignment control; resonant frequencies are not regions of good isolation.
Rainer Weiss's 1972 report proposed suspended masses, multiple-pass optical delay lines, and a servo holding an interference fringe. Its noise analysis separately treats photon-counting fluctuations, laser frequency instability, mechanical thermal noise, radiation pressure and ground motion. Those delay lines should be distinguished from the resonant arm cavities of Advanced LIGO.[ref]
