Comparing surface emission at equal temperature

 ... Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Stefan–Boltzmann law Emissivity
OurBigBook.com Words: 211
Oberlin College's documented demonstration heats a cube internally with a bulb and compares polished aluminium, dull aluminium, black-painted and white-painted faces at the same temperature. The apparatus includes a thermopile and a digital meter. Its preparation specifies 20 minutes of preheating and heater setting 6; these are instructions, not measured outcomes. The thermopile supplies the electrical readout for the relative thermal radiation comparison. Cover it between readings: otherwise detector warming changes its reference temperature. The account reports little difference between painted black and white faces and a larger contrast between shiny and painted faces. The comparison motivates a surface-dependent emissivity and shows why visible color alone is insufficient.[ref]
Equal source temperature is the principal control: it holds the Stefan–Boltzmann law factor σT4 fixed. For a sharper comparison, also hold detector distance, viewing angle, viewed area and surrounding temperatures fixed, and record a covered-detector baseline. These are additional diagnostic controls. Under those assumptions, a changed readout can reveal changed directional emissivity averaged over the detector's wavelength response. The demonstration supplies neither an absolute calibration nor a scan over all directions and wavelengths; it therefore does not directly measure total hemispherical emissivity or establish the fourth-power law. Comparing cooling rates would additionally require separating convection, thermal conduction and heat capacity effects, as in net radiative heat exchange.
