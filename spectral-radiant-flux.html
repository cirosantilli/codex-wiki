Spectral radiant flux (Φe,λ​, Φe,ν​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Radiometry Radiant flux
OurBigBook.com Words: 256
Spectral radiant flux resolves radiant flux by wavelength or frequency:
Φe​=∫0∞​Φe,λ​(λ)dλ=∫0∞​Φe,ν​(ν)dν.
(5)
Its units are Wm−1 per metre of wavelength or WHz−1 per hertz of frequency. A density quoted per nanometre must be integrated with wavelength intervals in nanometres; its numerical value is 10−9 times the per-metre density. The frequency and wavelength spectral radiance conversion applies equally to spectral radiant flux, through the change of variables formula:
Φe,ν​(ν)=Φe,λ​(c/ν)ν2c​,
(6)
with c the speed of light in vacuum and Φe,λ​ expressed per metre. The Jacobian determinant preserves integrated power rather than the height or location of an optical spectrum's peak.
For two explicitly synthetic optical spectra, let x=λ/(1nm) and quote the following densities in mWnm−1:
fA​(x)={0.020(1−∣x−500∣/100),0,​400≤x≤600,otherwise,​fB​(x)={0.010,0,​600≤x≤800,otherwise.​
(7)
The triangle under fA​ and the rectangle under fB​ both have area 2mW: 200×0.020/2=200×0.010=2. Their band radiant fluxes differ: 450–550nm contains 1.5mW of A, while 650–750nm contains 1.0mW of B. These are specified functions, not measured source spectra.
The dark-subtracted electric current of a linear photodiode is
i=∫R(λ)Φe,λ​(λ)dλ,[R]=AW−1,
(8)
using its spectral responsivity. The figure's stipulated R gives iA​=0.60mA and iB​=1.00mA despite equal total radiant flux. Symmetry places A's mean wavelength at 500nm and B's at 700nm; a linear R averages to its value at that mean. A single electric current therefore cannot determine an unknown broadband radiant flux without additional spectral information or a sufficiently flat calibrated response.
