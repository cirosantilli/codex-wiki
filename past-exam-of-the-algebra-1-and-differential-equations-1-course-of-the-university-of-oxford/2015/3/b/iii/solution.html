Proceed by induction on the dimension. For positive dimension, the fundamental theorem of algebra gives a root of the characteristic polynomial, hence a nonzero eigenvector v. Normalize it to have norm one. Its span is an invariant subspace, and part (ii) makes its orthogonal complement invariant too:
V=span{v}⊕span{v}⊥.
(36)
The restriction of T to the complement remains skew-adjoint, since the same inner product identity holds there. The induction hypothesis supplies an orthonormal eigenbasis for that smaller space. Adjoining v gives an orthonormal eigenbasis for V.
The zero-dimensional base case is immediate. Thus T is diagonalizable, indeed unitarily diagonalizable. This proves the finite-dimensional case of the spectral theorem for a skew-adjoint operator without assuming that distinct eigenvalues have multiplicity one.
