For every a=0 and every real b, there is exactly one solution on the maximal connected interval containing a. The formula in part (i) proves existence on the entire appropriate half-line, and the initial condition fixes its sole constant uniquely. This agrees with the local Picard-Lindelöf theorem.
For a=0, there is no classical solution on an interval containing the initial point, whatever b is: the printed equation is not defined there. Even if one only imposes the equation for x=0 and asks for a finite continuous extension across zero, the negative side rules it out. For x→0−:
  • If C=0, then C+arctanx→C, while x−2e−1/x diverges in magnitude.
  • If C=0, then arctanx∼x, and y(x)∼e−1/x/x, which also diverges in magnitude.
Thus no choice of the negative-side integration constant admits a finite left limit.
There is a useful distinction with a separately extended one-sided problem. On x>0, every member of the solution family tends to zero with all its derivatives as x↓0, since the exponential dominates every inverse power of x. If the forcing is separately defined to be zero at the right endpoint, that modified one-sided problem has infinitely many smooth solutions with y(0)=0, and none with y(0)=0. Those one-sided extensions do not give solutions on the two-sided intervals in the question.
