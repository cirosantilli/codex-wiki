Let P=b(T)q(T) and Q=a(T)p(T). By part (ii), these are complementary projection operators with images U=ker(T−λI)α and W=ker(T−μI)β. Thus D=λP+μQ acts as λI on U and μI on W.
The linear operator T preserves both summands because it commutes with its own polynomials. Therefore N=T−D preserves them too, and its restrictions satisfy
N∣U​=(T−λI)∣U​,(N∣U​)α=0,N∣W​=(T−μI)∣W​,(N∣W​)β=0.
(9)
Put k=max(α,β). Both restrictions of Nk vanish, so the direct sum gives
(T−D)k=0.​
(10)
Hence T−D is a nilpotent linear map. The operator D is diagonalizable, since its two summands are its eigenspaces, and it commutes with T. This illustrates the Jordan–Chevalley decomposition in the case of two distinct eigenvalues.
