The identity T2=T gives the decomposition
v=(v−Tv)+Tv,T(v−Tv)=0.
(1)
The first summand lies in the kernel of a linear map kerT, and the second in the image of a linear map imT. If w belongs to their intersection, write w=Tu; then Tw=T2u=Tu=w, while Tw=0. Thus w=0, proving the direct sum
V=kerT⊕imT.​
(2)
On these two invariant subspaces, T is respectively zero and the identity map. Joining a basis of each produces a basis of eigenvectors, with matrix
diag(0,…,0,1,…,1).​
(3)
Hence every linear projection is diagonalizable, over the original field, including in characteristic two.

Yes, provided “commute” means pairwise commute. Necessity follows because three diagonal matrices commute pairwise.
For sufficiency, first choose the common eigenbasis for the first two linear maps, say S,T, obtained in part (b). Their joint eigenspaces
Wλ,μ​=ker(S−λI)∩ker(T−μI)

An autonomous differential equation has no explicit dependence on the independent time variable: its vector field depends only on the current state. The two families of nullclines are
X(x,y)=0andY(x,y)=0.​

On the first family the horizontal velocity is zero; on the second the vertical velocity is zero. A critical point of a dynamical system is a state where both components vanish. It gives a constant solution, also called an equilibrium.

Translate the equilibrium to the origin by z=(x−a,y−b)T. If the vector field is continuously differentiable, its first-order Taylor expansion is
z˙=Mz+r(z),∥z∥∥r(z)∥​⟶0.
(40)
The Jacobian matrix M gives the linearization z˙=Mz, whose general solution is z(t)=etMz(0) using the matrix exponential. Its eigenvalues determine the exponential factors, while its eigenvectors and, when necessary, generalized eigenvectors determine their directions.
For distinct eigenvalues λ1​,λ2​ and corresponding eigenvectors v1​,v2​,
z(t)=c1​eλ1​tv1​+c2​eλ2​tv2​.​
(41)
For a real matrix with nonreal conjugate eigenvalues, choose conjugate coefficients to obtain real solutions. Equivalently, if λ=a+ib and v=p+iq, a real pair of independent solutions is

When no eigenvalue has zero real part, the hyperbolic equilibrium theory justifies the corresponding local qualitative classification. Zero real parts require additional nonlinear analysis.
