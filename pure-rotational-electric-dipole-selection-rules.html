Pure rotational electric-dipole selection rules (ΔJ=±1)

 Home Chemistry Molecule Molecular rotation Rigid rotor Linear rigid rotor
OurBigBook.com Words: 126
For the field-free, spin-free linear rigid rotor in one fixed internal quantum state, the electric-dipole interaction is −d⋅E(t). A nonzero permanent electric dipole moment along the molecular axis allows pure rotational photon absorption with ΔJ=+1 and photon emission with ΔJ=−1. The matrix element between initial and final quantum states must be nonzero: the existence of an energy gap alone is insufficient. Relative to a chosen z axis, polarization of an electromagnetic wave along z selects ΔM=0; transverse polarization of an electromagnetic wave permits ΔM=±1.[ref]
Hydrogen chloride and carbon monoxide are examples with a permanent electric dipole moment. By symmetry, molecular hydrogen and carbon dioxide have no permanent electric dipole moment in the ideal symmetric internal state. They still rotate and have rotational energy eigenvalues, but the simple permanent-electric dipole spectrum described here is absent. This restriction concerns this interaction and these assumptions, not every possible way of detecting molecular rotation.
