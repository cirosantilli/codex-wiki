For Hopf thresholds in a two-pool calcium model, with the unique equilibrium parametrized by u=μ, locate changes of stability by solving the two equations
f(μ,v)=0,fv​(μ,v)−γfu​(μ,v)=ϵ.​
(39)
If there are two simple roots μ−​<μ+​ with positive trace between them, the equilibrium is a repeller throughout that interval, since its determinant stays positive. At a nondegenerate crossing a Hopf bifurcation is possible, with linear frequency [−fv​/ϵ]1/2. A complete classification of criticality requires a nonlinear coefficient, not merely the trace calculation. At leading singular order the thresholds approach the folds determined by f=0 and fv​−γfu​=0, equivalently V′=−1/γ.
Periodic solutions are also supported globally in the positive quadrant. The axes point inward for positive input, v′≤(β−δv)/ϵ bounds v, and the u equation then bounds u by a linear restoring term plus a bounded source. A large rectangle can thus be chosen forward invariant. For an initial point distinct from the repelling unique equilibrium, its omega-limit set cannot contain that equilibrium or a boundary equilibrium. The Poincaré-Bendixson theorem supplies a periodic orbit. In the strong separation regime it follows the two attracting branches and jumps between them, a relaxation oscillation.
The interval requires the maximum of fv​−γfu​ along the critical manifold to exceed ϵ; the listed scale hierarchy alone is not a substitute for that test for arbitrary remaining constants. As an explicit example, β=0.1, δ=10−5, ϵ=5×10−4 and γ=0.1 give approximately μ−​=0.22618 and μ+​=0.37350. At μ=0.3 the trace is positive, whereas at 0.03 and 1 it is negative. The figures use this example and label their singular-limit portions accordingly.
