In a relaxation cycle, cytoplasmic calcium rises slowly on the left attracting branch while total calcium is accumulated, then rises rapidly during store discharge along a constant-w fast path to the right branch. It subsequently decreases on the right slow branch as extrusion dominates input, and finally drops rapidly during store uptake back to the left branch. The result is a repeated pulse with slow sections joined by sharp transitions, rather than a sinusoid except near a small-amplitude Hopf bifurcation.
