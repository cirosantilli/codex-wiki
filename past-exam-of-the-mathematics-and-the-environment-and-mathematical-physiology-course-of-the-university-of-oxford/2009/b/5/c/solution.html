At every equilibrium the store balance gives f=0, and adding γ times that balance to the cytoplasmic one gives u=μ. The unique-root result therefore gives a single positive equilibrium (μ,V(μ)). Its Jacobian is
A=(−1−γfu​/ϵfu​/ϵ​−γfv​/ϵfv​/ϵ​),detA=−fv​/ϵ>0,trA=−1+(fv​−γfu​)/ϵ,​

with derivatives evaluated at that equilibrium. At small μ, fu​>0 and the trace is negative; sufficiently large μ also has fu​>0, since J′ is of order u−3 and K′ of order u−5. Thus both extreme regimes have a linearly stable unique equilibrium.
The fast variable is not v alone. Using total-calcium fast fibres, introduce total scaled calcium w=u+γv. Then w˙=μ−u, while on fast time σ=τ/ϵ the leading equations give du/dσ=−γf, dv/dσ=f and w constant. Fast paths are therefore straight lines of slope −1/γ in the (u,v) plane. Attracting portions of the critical manifold satisfy fv​−γfu​<0, rather than merely fv​<0. Along v=V(u), slow motion obeys
dτdu​=1+γV′(u)μ−u​,V′=−fu​/fv​.​
(38)
Starting with large u and v=0, f>0 drives a rapid move upward and left, loading the store until the trajectory approaches the right attracting branch. For large μ in the right stable regime, slow motion on that branch carries it toward u=μ; the direction reverses appropriately if the starting value lies below that equilibrium. For sufficiently small μ, slow motion travels left along the right branch until its fold, then a fast approximately constant-w jump carries the trajectory to the left attracting branch. Subsequent slow motion reaches the small-u equilibrium. At a fold the slow approximation is singular, so the full equations resolve the transition. At very small u an additional loss of time-scale separation is possible because the rates themselves vanish; the fast/slow drawing describes the separated portions, not a uniform estimate arbitrarily near the origin.
