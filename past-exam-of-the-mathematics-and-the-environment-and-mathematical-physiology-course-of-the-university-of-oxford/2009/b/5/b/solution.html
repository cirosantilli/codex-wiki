For the three-region calcium nullcline, define
J(u)=1+u2βu2​,K(u)=1+u4u4​,f=J(u)−K(u)1+v2v2​−δv.
(34)
For u>0, f(u,0)>0, f→−∞ as v→∞, and fv​=−2K(u)v/(1+v2)2−δ<0. Thus there is exactly one positive root v=V(u) for every positive u. The curve starts at the origin and initially satisfies v∼βu2/δ.
For 0<β<1, let uc​>0 solve J(uc​)=K(uc​) and put Kc​=K(uc​), Jc′​=J′(uc​), Kc′​=K′(uc​). Explicitly, with zc​=uc2​,
zc​=2(1−β)−1+1+4β(1−β)​​,J′(u)=(1+u2)22βu​,K′(u)=(1+u4)24u3​.
(35)
In particular uc​∼β​ and Kc′​−Jc′​>0 for small β.
On the large-v branch away from the crossing, replace v2/(1+v2) by one. This gives v∼[J(u)−K(u)]/δ where this is large and positive. Near uc​ retain the next term 1−v−2 and linearize J−K:
0≃−(Kc′​−Jc′​)(u−uc​)+Kc​/v2−δv,u−uc​≃Kc′​−Jc′​Kc​/v2−δv​.​
(36)
Finally where the leak is negligible compared with the other terms, v2/(1+v2)≃J(u)/K(u), so v≃[J/(K−J)]1/2 on the side J<K. These are overlapping asymptotic descriptions with different validity ranges, not three exact disconnected curves. The curve rises to a large peak, falls sharply near uc​, then approaches a small finite level as u→∞. At leading small-δ order that last level is [β/(1−β)]1/2.
