Here h is water depth, u the depth-averaged downstream speed, s bed elevation relative to the reference slope, and η=s+h free-surface elevation relative to that reference. The constants ρw​,ρs​ are water and grain densities, S is the reference bed slope, τ bed shear stress, ϕ bed porosity, c suspended sediment mass concentration, qb​ bedload solid volume flux per unit width, and vE​,vD​ erosion and deposition velocities. The settling speed is denoted by vs​ in the constitutive laws.
Depth integration of incompressible water conservation gives the depth-storage plus discharge-divergence balance. Depth-averaged momentum has local and advective acceleration on the left, and gravity driven by the actual surface gradient together with bed drag on the right. Hydrostatic pressure and a shallow-water approximation underlie this Saint-Venant description.
Suspended sediment conservation first has the conservative form (hc)t​+(huc)x​=ρs​(vE​−vD​); the water balance reduces it to the material-derivative form printed in the source. Solid bed volume per unit horizontal area is (1−ϕ)s. Its storage plus bedload divergence equals deposition minus erosion, giving the Exner bed balance. Adding the suspended and bed balances after converting bed volume to grain mass cancels exchange terms: erosion and deposition transfer sediment between the two reservoirs rather than create it.
