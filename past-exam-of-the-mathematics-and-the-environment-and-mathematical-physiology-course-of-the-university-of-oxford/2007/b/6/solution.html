For the travelling wave coordinate ξ=x+st, the chain rule gives
s(U′+γV′)=μ−U+εU′′,εsV′=f(U,V).​
(21)
Fast-slow matching for a calcium pulse joins two fast transitions, B→C and D→A, joined by slow portions C→D and A→B. Write the nullcline as V=g(U). The resting point has UB​=μ, VB​=g(μ) in the leading matched description.
Put ξ=εX. Multiplying the first equation by ε gives UXX​=s(UX​+γVX​)+ε(U−μ). At leading order, integrate and use UX​→0, (U,V)→(UB​,VB​) at the start of the front. Together with the second equation this gives
UX​=s[U−UB​+γ(V−VB​)],sVX​=f(U,V).​
(22)
The fast equilibria are intersections of V=g(U) and V=VB​−(U−UB​)/γ. As the slope −1/γ changes, there may be one or three simple intersections, with two distinct intersections at a tangency. At a simple point of the nullcline, fU​=−fV​g′, and the sign convention above/below it gives fV​<0. The Jacobian matrix of the fast system has
detJ=fV​−γfU​=fV​(1+γg′),trJ=s+fV​/s.
(23)
For the usual three-intersection configuration, the two outer points have 1+γg′>0, hence negative determinant and one positive and one negative eigenvalue: they are saddle points. The middle point has positive determinant; it is linearly stable if s2<−fV​ and unstable if s2>−fV​. Zero trace is a possible oscillatory stability threshold, with a nonlinear classification requiring further information about f.
To select the front speed, follow the appropriate branch of the unstable manifold of B and vary s until it meets the stable manifold of C. This is a shooting method for a heteroclinic orbit. Follow the high-U slow branch from C to D; the falling front obeys the same fast equations with the integration constant anchored at D. Choose D so that its descending saddle connection reaches a low-U point A at that same speed. The remaining slow branch returns toward the resting point, completing the matched spike. The construction requires the indicated connections; a nullcline sketch alone does not specify their numerical speed or prove their existence for every possible kinetic function f.
On the slow portions, f(U,V)=0 at leading order, so V=g(U) and V′=g′(U)U′. Neglecting the small diffusion term yields
s(1+γg′(U))U′=μ−U.​
(24)
On the outer rising branches the coefficient on the left is positive. Recovery from C toward D requires decreasing U, hence μ<UD​; the low branch from A back toward rest requires increasing U, hence UA​<μ. Therefore UA​<μ<UD​​. These inequalities distinguish the recovery branches from a trajectory that would move away from the required matching points.
