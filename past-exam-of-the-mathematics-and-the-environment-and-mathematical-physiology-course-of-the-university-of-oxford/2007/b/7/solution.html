Systole is ventricular contraction and ejection; diastole includes ventricular relaxation and filling. Let Va​,Vv​,VLV​ denote arterial, venous and left-ventricular blood volumes. Let Q+ flow through the aortic valve into the arteries, Qc​ through the peripheral circulation, and Q− through the mitral valve into the ventricle. Conservation of volume gives
V˙a​=Q+−Qc​,V˙v​=Qc​−Q−,V˙LV​=Q−−Q+.​
(25)
The same Qc​ leaves arteries and enters veins because the capillary compartment has no changing stored volume in this approximation. Adding the equations conserves total modeled blood volume.
Use constant vascular compliances Ca​,Cv​, with V˙a​=Ca​p˙​a​ and V˙v​=Cv​p˙​v​, and ventricular elastance pLV​=ELV​(t)(VLV​−V0​). The hydraulic resistances and ideal one-way valves give
Qc​=Rc​pa​−pv​​,Q+=Ra​[pLV​−pa​]+​,Q−=Rv​[pv​−pLV​]+​,
(26)
where [z]+=max(z,0). Substitution into the conservation equations gives the two stated arterial/venous pressure equations. Since VLV​−V0​=pLV​/ELV​, differentiating this identity gives the third equation. The positive parts encode valve closure against a reverse pressure difference.
During isovolumetric contraction both valves are closed. Let V+ be the end-diastolic ventricular volume, V0​ its unstressed reference volume, and pa+​ arterial pressure before ejection. The rapidly raised elastance generates Es​(V+−V0​), so the aortic valve opens if Es​(V+−V0​)>pa+​​.
During ejection, Ra​/Rc​≪1 makes the open-valve pressure drop small on the peripheral drainage time scale, so pLV​≈pa​. The mitral valve is closed, and adding arterial and ventricular volume balances eliminates Q+. With pv​≪pa​ this gives
Rc​Ca​p˙​a​+Rc​dtd(pa​/ELV​)​≈−pa​.
(27)
For approximately constant systolic elastance, set Cs​=1/Es​ and reset time to the start of the slow ejection phase. Then
pa​(t)≈p^​a​exp[−Rc​(Ca​+Cs​)t​].​
(28)
Here p^​a​ is the matched pressure at that start. If peripheral outflow is negligible during the preceding rapid artery-ventricle pressure equilibration, conservation gives the more explicit p^​a​=[Ca​pa+​+V+−V0​]/(Ca​+Cs​).
After aortic closure the ventricular volume is fixed at the end-systolic value V−. Relaxation decreases ELV​ until the mitral valve opens at pLV​=pv​, hence ELV​∼pv​/(V−−V0​)​, with pv​ the venous filling pressure at opening. During filling the aortic valve is closed, so Rc​Ca​p˙​a​=−(pa​−pv​)≈−pa​. With pa−​ the arterial pressure at the start of filling and time reset there,
pa​(t)≈pa−​e−t/(Rc​Ca​).​
(29)
The change from Ca​+Cs​ to Ca​ is physical: the ejecting ventricle shares the arterial pressure during systole, whereas a closed aortic valve disconnects it during filling.
