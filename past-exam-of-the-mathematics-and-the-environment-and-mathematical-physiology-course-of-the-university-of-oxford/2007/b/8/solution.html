In this delayed carbon-dioxide ventilation feedback model, p is a representative carbon-dioxide partial pressure, K an effective storage capacitance, M>0 metabolic production, and pV removal by pulmonary ventilation. The delay represents transport and sensing between the gas-exchange compartment and the ventilatory response. The Hill equation response rises sigmoidally with pressure, reaches half its maximum α at p=θ, and saturates at α; n>1 permits a steep threshold-like response.
For positive parameters, pV(p) increases strictly from zero to infinity because its derivative is V+pV′>0. Its graph therefore intersects the horizontal line M exactly once: M=p∗V(p∗),p∗>0​. The figure shows this argument and the first imaginary-root crossing.
For a small perturbation z=p−p∗, linearization gives
z˙=−Az(t)−Bz(t−τ),A=V(p∗)/K,B=p∗V′(p∗)/K.
(30)
The sigmoidal delayed ventilation threshold follows from the characteristic equation of a delay differential equation, λ+A+Be−λτ=0. At an imaginary root λ=iω, separate real and imaginary parts:
A+Bcos(ωτ)=0,ω−Bsin(ωτ)=0.
(31)
For B>A>0, the first positive crossing has
ωc​=B2−A2​,τc​=B2−A2​arccos(−A/B)​.​
(32)
At zero delay the scalar equation is stable. No root can cross the imaginary axis before this first crossing, and zero itself is not a root. To check the crossing direction, implicit differentiation gives
λ′=−1+τ(λ+A)λ(λ+A)​,Reλ′(τc​)=(1+Aτc​)2+ωc2​τc2​ωc2​​>0.​
(33)
All subsequent imaginary crossings have the same direction. Thus the equilibrium is linearly stable below τc​ and unstable above it. This establishes the oscillatory linear threshold; the full nonlinear Hopf bifurcation additionally requires the usual nondegeneracy conditions. If B≤A, no finite imaginary-root crossing occurs and the equilibrium is stable for every finite delay. For the stated response, B/A=nθn/(θn+(p∗)n), showing explicitly when the gain can exceed instantaneous damping.
If A≪B, then ωc​≈B and ωc​τc​≈π/2. Consequently the small-amplitude period near onset is 2π/ωc​≈4τc​​. The comparison is to the critical delay near the first instability, rather than an arbitrary delay far beyond onset. A longer circulatory transit time can move the physiological system across this delayed-feedback threshold, providing a mechanism for periodic breathing associated with impaired cardiac output.
