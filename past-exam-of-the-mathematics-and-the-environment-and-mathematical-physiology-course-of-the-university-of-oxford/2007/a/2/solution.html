The first Saint-Venant equation conserves water volume. The second balances material acceleration against downslope gravity, bed friction and the free-surface pressure gradient. With length scale L, depth scale H, slope S and velocity scale U, the conventional gravity-friction scaling has ε=H/(SL) and Froude number F=U/gH​. The pressure-gradient/downslope-gravity ratio is ε, and the inertia/downslope-gravity ratio is εF2.
The uniform steady solution obeys u2=h and hu=Q, hence u0​=Q1/3, h0​=Q2/3​. In the constant-flux river momentum relaxation reduction, substitute h=Q/u and X=1−s. Since us​=−uX​ and hs​=QuX​/u2, the resulting quasilinear partial differential equation is
ut​+c(u)uX​=εF21−u3/Q​,c(u)=F2u2Q​−u.​
(4)
A downstream boundary at X=0 supplies data into X>0 precisely when c(u1​)>0, equivalent to u1​<Q1/3/F2/3​. Although water flows out there, the relevant characteristic curves carry information upstream in this subcritical regime.
Along a characteristic curve, dX/dt=c(u) and du/dt=(1−u3/Q)/(εF2). Positive u relaxes toward u0​; near u0​, its displacement decays at rate 3/(εF2u0​). Boundary-generated deviations therefore become small after a time of order ε and, while the characteristic speed stays bounded away from zero, within a distance of order ε. The outer state is the initial uniform solution. A jump between initial and boundary values at their corner can produce a weak solution or a thin initial adjustment; a globally smooth corner solution requires compatible data.
For the moving boundary put Y=X−vt. The characteristic speed relative to it is c(u)−v, while the outer speed is
vb​=c(u0​)=Q1/3(F−2−1).​
(5)
For the intended tidal slowing 0<u1​≤u0​, c is decreasing in u and hence c(u)≥vb​ throughout the relaxation. Thus v<vb​ leaves an incoming family and an attached thin boundary layer. The diagram shows numerical characteristics of this scalar reduction, with a slower boundary velocity. When v>vb​, the tidal boundary overtakes the unperturbed characteristic family; an attached smooth relaxation layer is no longer appropriate, and a tidal bore or shock wave can arise in a fuller river model.
There are two qualifications to the printed reduction. First, without the physical slowing assumption, v<vb​ alone need not make the boundary incoming: for u1​>u0​, also require v<c(u1​). Second, imposing hu=Q exactly everywhere in the original continuity equation gives ht​=0, and consequently ut​=0. The time-dependent characteristics above solve the requested momentum-only approximation, rather than the complete Saint-Venant equation system with an exactly constant flux.
