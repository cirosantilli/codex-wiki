In the non-scattering grey atmosphere approximation, I is the frequency-integrated specific intensity along a ray, s is distance along that ray, ρ is the mass density, and κ is the mass absorption coefficient. The local thermodynamic equilibrium source is the frequency-integrated Planck function B=σSB​T4/π. Across a thin element ds, absorption removes κρIds and thermal emission adds κρBds. Their balance gives dI/ds=−κρ(I−B).
Take the upward vertical as the polar axis, put μ=cosθ, and define downward optical depth by
τ(z)=∫z∞​κ(z′)ρ(z′)dz′.​
(1)
Along a ray, dz/ds=μ and dτ/ds=−κρμ. The chain rule therefore gives μIτ​=I−B​. In a plane-parallel, azimuthally symmetric field, the solid angle element is dω=dφdμ, so
J(τ)=21​∫−11​I(τ,μ)dμ.​
(2)
Local radiative equilibrium requires zero net radiative heating of the material: absorption 4πκρJ balances emission 4πκρB. Where the absorption coefficient is nonzero this gives B=J, and substitution gives the required angular radiative transfer equation. It does not require the specific intensity itself to be isotropic.
At the top of an atmosphere with no external incoming long-wave radiation, impose I(0,μ)=0(−1≤μ<0)​. These are inward directions; prescribing the same zero value for outward directions would remove the emergent radiation as well. The upward radiative flux and its derivative are
Φ=2π∫−11​μIdμ,dτdΦ​=2π∫−11​(I−B)dμ=4π(J−B)=0.
(3)
Thus Φ is constant with height​: local radiative equilibrium expresses energy conservation through successive atmospheric layers.
