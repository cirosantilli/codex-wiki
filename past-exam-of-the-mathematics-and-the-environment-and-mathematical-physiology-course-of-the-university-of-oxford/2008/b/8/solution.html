Resting cells enter proliferation at rate β(N)N. A proliferating cell takes a fixed cycle time τ to divide and survives that cycle with probability e−γτ under a constant death hazard γ. Thus exit from the proliferating compartment at time t is e−γτβ(N(t−τ))N(t−τ), and it returns twice as many daughter cells to the resting population. Resting-cell loss at rate δN is separate.

Negative feedback in β(N) suppresses recruitment when the resting population is high; its Hill exponent gives a steep response around the concentration scale θ.
