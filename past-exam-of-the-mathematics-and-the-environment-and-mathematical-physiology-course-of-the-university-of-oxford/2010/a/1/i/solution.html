The heat capacity per unit horizontal area is C=ρcp​d: mass density times specific heat capacity times atmospheric depth. Thus CT˙ is the rate of stored thermal energy per unit area. The solar constant Q is the incident radiative flux on a surface normal to sunlight; the ratio of the Earth's intercepting disk area to its spherical area is 1/4. The absorbed fraction is 1−a, where albedo a is the reflected fraction. The outgoing radiative flux is σγT4, a Stefan–Boltzmann law with effective radiation factor γ incorporating the simplified greenhouse effect. Every term therefore has units of power per area.
The albedo curve decreases smoothly, passes through (T0​,1/2), and approaches 1/4 at high temperature. At T=0 it is (2+tanhμ)/4, close to 3/4 when μ is large. Its derivative is

An ice-covered planet reflects more sunlight than exposed land or ocean. Warming removes reflective ice, lowers albedo, and increases absorbed radiative flux: this is ice-albedo feedback. The parameter μ controls the sharpness of this transition; the formula is an idealized global response, rather than a local melting law.
