The preceding figure separates these two time scales; a single time axis would compress the initial fall into a nearly vertical segment.
In the sharp-transition regime, after selection of the warm branch a∗(T)≃1/4. Solving the linear ordinary differential equation with the leading matched value a(0+)=1/2 gives
a(t)≃41​+41​e−t,T(t)≃(23−e−t​)1/4.​
(7)
These are outer solutions: their initial temperature is 1, so the fast cooling layer is required to satisfy the actual initial temperature.
There is a useful qualification to the two-limit argument. For fixed finite μ>2, put x=1−2a on the slow manifold. Then xt​=−x+21​tanh(μx), and x=0 is an unstable equilibrium with growth rate μ/2−1. The small positive O(ε) seed from the fast layer can therefore produce a delay of order log(1/ε)/(μ/2−1). The simple exponential approximation assumes that the sharp albedo transition is sufficiently fast to make this delay negligible on the displayed slow scale. It is not a uniform approximation for every fixed μ>2 as ε→0.
