On a characteristic emitted by the boundary x=0, the invariant is zero and H=H0​(x). The travel time of the one emitted at time zero is
f(x)=(n+2)−p∫0x​[z(1−z)]−pdz=(n+2)−pBx​(q,q).​

The incomplete beta function is finite at both endpoints because 0<p<1 for the physical exponents n>0. Before characteristic crossings, the initial-data branch and boundary-data branch have the displayed forms in the source, with the appropriate emission time t−f(x) for the latter.
Glacier characteristic admissibility gives a substantive domain restriction. Factoring the invariant gives A(x)−A(ξ)=(x−ξ)(1−x−ξ). A positive forward branch requires
0≤ξ<x<1−ξ,0≤ξ<1/2.​
(30)
Equivalently it is the image of this parameter domain under (ξ,x)↦(x,t(ξ,x)), restricted further to regions where that map is single-valued. Characteristics cannot start with positive thickness growth at ξ>1/2, where the prescribed initial rate Ht​=1−2ξ is negative. This is why the printed piecewise expression is a formal characteristic construction, not a global nonnegative solution from a bare bed. For example for x>1/2, putting Lξ​=1−2ξ gives
t(ξ,x)=(n+2)−pLξ−n/(n+2)​B(x−ξ)/Lξ​​(q,q)≥f(x).
(31)
Both factors increase with ξ there, so no positive initial-data branch exists at that x for 0<t<f(x), contrary to a literal global reading of the first case. Compression can also make characteristics intersect; entropy solutions then require shock waves.
One can nevertheless give the exact zero-thickness endpoint locus of these formal characteristics. Let K=f(1)=(n+2)−pB(q,q). At x=1−ξ the change of variable z=ξ+(1−2ξ)y gives
tend​(ξ)=K(1−2ξ)−n/(n+2),xend​(t)=21​[1+(tK​)(n+2)/n],t≥K.​
(32)
This is the formal snout locus inferred from the given characteristic family; it retreats from 1 towards 1/2. It is not justified as the physical terminus of an accumulating glacier formed from zero initial ice. In particular it says nothing about a positive advancing snout at t<K. A physical free boundary problem must specify no ablation on a bare bed, maintain H≥0, and select an entropy solution or retain the small slope regularization. Those additional conditions are absent from the printed first-order construction. Thus a unique global physical snout trajectory cannot be claimed from that construction alone. The steady endpoint remains x=1. The degenerate n=0 limit would instead have the same endpoint time K for every initial characteristic, so the inverted formula presumes n>0.
