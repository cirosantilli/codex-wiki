For z=ct−x the chain rule gives ∂t​=cd/dz, ∂x​=−d/dz, ∂xx​=d2/dz2. Thus
ϵcvz​=f(v)−w+ϵ2vzz​,cwz​=γv−w.​
(49)
As z increases from the material ahead of the wave towards that behind it, the singular pulse construction is A→B→C→D→A. The front AB raises v at approximately fixed w=0 from the low root to the high root. On the plateau BC, f(v)≃w on the right attracting branch while w rises slowly. The back CD decreases v at approximately fixed w=w0​ to the left attracting branch. The recovery tail then lowers w back to zero on that branch, returning to A=(0,0). Both sharp transitions have thickness O(ϵ) in z; the other segments have the slower recovery scale.
The figure labels all four points and the front/back orientations. This frozen-recovery construction needs ϵγ≪1 as well as ϵ≪1 if γ is large, so that the recovery variable remains nearly fixed in an inner transition. It also needs an admissible positive front speed for the chosen orientation, addressed below.
