Put X=z/ϵ. The travelling equations become cvX​=f(v)−w+vXX​ and cwX​=ϵ(γv−w). Hence, for nonzero O(1) speed and ϵγ≪1, w is constant to leading order across the front.
Writing p=vX​ gives the planar system vX​=p, pX​=cp−f(v)+w. At the outer cubic roots A=(V−​,0) and B=(V+​,0), f′(V±​)<0. The Jacobian determinant is f′(V±​)<0, so both are saddle points. The increasing front is a heteroclinic orbit from the unstable manifold of A to the stable manifold of B. Varying c supplies the shooting parameter required to match these manifolds. For this cubic one can also construct it explicitly, as in the next solution.
Multiply the second-order equation by vX​ and integrate across the front. Because vX​→0 at both ends, the term ∫vXX​vX​dX=[vX2​/2]−∞∞​ vanishes. The remaining identity is
c(w)=∫−∞∞​vX2​dX∫V−​(w)V+​(w)​[f(v)−w]dv​.​
(50)
The denominator is positive for a nonconstant front, so its direction is determined by the signed area between the cubic and the horizontal recovery line: Maxwell balance for a scalar reaction-diffusion front gives zero speed when that area vanishes.
The derivative in the denominator must use the inner coordinate X. If one instead uses the source's original z and denotes Jz​=∫vz2​dz, then Jz​=ϵ−1∫vX2​dX and the identity is c=∫(f−w)dv/(ϵJz​). Thus the printed v′(z) is valid only if z has silently been reused for the rescaled inner coordinate; with the original coordinate it misses a factor ϵ.
