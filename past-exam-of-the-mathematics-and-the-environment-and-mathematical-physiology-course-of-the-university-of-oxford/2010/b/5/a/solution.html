The nullclines are the cubic w=f(v) and the straight line w=γv. At an equilibrium either v=0, or γ=(a−v)(v−1). The latter quadratic has maximum (1−a)2/4, so γ>(1−a)2/4 leaves only the resting state (0,0). Its Jacobian matrix is
J=(−a/ϵγ​−1/ϵ−1​),trJ=−a/ϵ−1<0,detJ=(a+γ)/ϵ>0.
(46)
Both eigenvalues have negative real parts; the rest state is linearly stable and asymptotically stable.
On the fast time t/ϵ, the recovery variable w is approximately fixed. At w≃0, the scalar fast equation has stable roots v=0,1 and an unstable root v=a. A small kick below a decays towards rest; a kick above it moves rapidly towards the right attracting branch. For finite ϵ, the threshold is displaced from the frozen root by slow recovery, but the leading threshold mechanism is this unstable middle branch.
After the fast excitation, wt​=γv−w>0 raises w while v follows the right attracting branch of the cubic. At its upper fold the branch ends and v makes a fast downward jump to the left branch. Recovery then lowers w slowly, with a negative undershoot in v, before return towards (0,0). Thus the action potential time trace has rapid depolarization, a slower high-voltage excursion, rapid repolarization, and slow recovery. On the fast segments w scarcely changes; on slow segments w≃f(v) and f′(v)<0.
