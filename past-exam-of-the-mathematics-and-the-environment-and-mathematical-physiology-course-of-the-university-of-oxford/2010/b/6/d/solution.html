At a locked state choose the rotating frame with θi′​=0. For small phase differences, Taylor expansion gives h(θj​−θi​)−h(0)=h′(0)(θj​−θi​)+O(∣θj​−θi​∣2). Let the graph Laplacian have Lii​=∑j​dij​ and Lij​=−dij​ for i=j. The leading locked equations are
0=ξ−Ω1​1−h′(0)Lθ.
(60)
If coupling is balanced, ∑j​dij​=∑j​dji​ for every i, so 1TL=0. Summing over cells cancels all linear coupling terms and gives
Ω1​=N1​i=1∑N​ξi​+O(ijmax​∣θj​−θi​∣2).​
(61)
In particular symmetric reciprocal gap-junction conductances satisfy this condition. To leading order the collective physical frequency is 1+ϵN−1∑i​ξi​.
The arithmetic-mean conclusion requires this extra balance assumption. The printed condition dii​=0 alone is insufficient. For a connected directed coupling network let πTL=0, normalized by πT1=1. The valid weighted collective oscillator frequency result is
Ω1​=i∑​πi​ξi​+O(max∣θj​−θi​∣2).​
(62)
For two cells with d12​=p>0, d21​=q>0, one has π=(q,p)/(p+q) and Ω1​=(qξ1​+pξ2​)/(p+q) at linear order. For example p=1,q=2,ξ1​=0,ξ2​=δ gives Ω1​=δ/3, rather than δ/2, while the locked phase difference is O(δ) when h′(0)=0. This supplies a counterexample with arbitrarily small phase differences. It also identifies exactly why reciprocal coupling recovers the requested mean: the left null space is then spanned by the uniform vector.
