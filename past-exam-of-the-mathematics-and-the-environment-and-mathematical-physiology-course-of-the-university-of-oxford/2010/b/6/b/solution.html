Differentiating the autonomous cycle equation U′=F(U) gives U′′=DF(U)U′, so U′ lies in the periodic null space of L. For a hyperbolic attracting limit cycle, its tangent direction has Floquet multiplier one and every transverse multiplier has modulus below one. Hence it is the only periodic direction, proving the required one-dimensional null space.
Strictly, mere nonlinear stability does not ensure this spectral assertion. In polar coordinates r˙=−(r−1)3, φ˙​=2π has an attracting unit-period cycle but neutral radial linearization at r=1, supplying a second periodic solution of the variational equation. Thus the intended phase reduction assumes the usual simple unit Floquet multiplier, or equivalently hyperbolic transverse attraction; this assumption is made explicit here.
