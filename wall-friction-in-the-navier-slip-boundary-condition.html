Wall friction in the Navier slip boundary condition (Dw​=μurel2​/b)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Navier slip boundary condition
OurBigBook.com Words: 200
For a Newtonian fluid and a flat wall with 0<b<∞, the Navier slip boundary condition is equivalent to a tangential wall-on-fluid traction
tw,t​=−bμ​urel​.
(23)
The sign expresses resisting friction: ∂n​u uses the inward unit normal, whereas the wall-on-fluid traction uses the outward unit normal of the fluid domain. The irreversible loss of mechanical energy per unit wall area and time is
Dw​=−tw,t​urel​=bμ​urel2​≥0,[Dw​]=Wm−2.
(24)
For two tangential directions replace urel2​ by the squared Euclidean norm of relative tangential velocity. At a moving wall this relative-velocity loss must be distinguished from the power supplied by the wall's motion. Bulk viscous dissipation also remains present. A finite slip length models reduced interfacial resistance, without declaring every boundary ideal or removing dynamic viscosity.
The limiting loss depends on how the flow is driven. In Couette flow with a slipping lower wall,
Dw​=(H+b)2μU2b​.
(25)
It tends to zero for both b→0 and b→∞ at fixed H and U. At b=0 impose the no-slip boundary condition directly, rather than dividing by zero. In planar Poiseuille flow with equal wall slip at fixed G, each wall instead dissipates G2bH2/(4μ), which grows with b because relative velocity grows. The bulk loss per unit width is G2H3/(12μ); adding both wall losses gives Gq, the pressure work per unit channel length and width. This energy balance shows why small friction coefficients do not guarantee small losses under every driving constraint.
