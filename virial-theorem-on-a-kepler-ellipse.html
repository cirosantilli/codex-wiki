Virial theorem on a Kepler ellipse (⟨K⟩=κ/(2a),⟨U⟩=−κ/a)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Virial theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 280
Consider the exact two-body problem with masses m1​,m2​. In the inertial centre of mass frame, its internal kinetic energy is K=m∣r˙∣2/2, where r is their relative position, m=m1​m2​/(m1​+m2​) is the reduced mass, and U=−κ/r with κ=GN​m1​m2​. Take a noncollision elliptic Kepler orbit with semi-major axis a and orbital eccentricity 0<e<1. Write η for eccentric anomaly, to avoid confusing it with total energy E. With mean motion n=κ/(ma3)​ and orbital period P=2π/n, Kepler's equation and the orbit geometry give
nt=η−esinη,r=a(1−ecosη),K=rκ​−2aκ​,U=−rκ​,E=−2aκ​.
(33)
The time origin is periapsis. Since dt=(1−ecosη)dη/n, the time average of 1/r over one orbital period is exactly 1/a. Therefore ⟨K⟩P​=κ/(2a) and ⟨U⟩P​=−κ/a, independently of orbital eccentricity. Equal steps in eccentric anomaly are not equal time steps; unweighted averaging of a drawing of the ellipse gives the wrong answer.
The original dimensionless example in both diagrams has m=a=κ=1 and e=0.6. At periapsis, r=0.4, K=2, U=−2.5; at apoapsis, r=1.6, K=0.125, U=−0.625. Both have E=−0.5, but 2K+U is respectively 1.5 and −0.375. Only the orbital period averages are ⟨K⟩P​=0.5 and ⟨U⟩P​=−1. The instantaneous zero of 2K+U occurs at r=a, not at every point of the orbit.
The endpoint term can also be evaluated exactly: G=mr⋅r˙=mna2esinη. Starting at periapsis, a window ending at eccentric anomaly η has
2⟨K⟩T​+⟨U⟩T​=aκ​η−esinηesinη​.
(34)
At T=P/4, Kepler's equation gives η≃2.09133 and a positive residual ≃0.3314κ/a. At T=P/2 or P, G returns to zero and the residual vanishes exactly.
