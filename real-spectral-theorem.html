Real spectral theorem (A=QΛQT,QTQ=I)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 91
The real case of the finite-dimensional spectral theorem says that a self-adjoint operator on a finite-dimensional real inner product space has a real orthonormal eigenbasis. In orthonormal coordinates this is the spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices, A=QΛQT. The worked ellipse under finite-dimensional spectral theorem shows why both orthogonal diagonalization and orthogonality matter: the new coordinates separate the quadratic form into squares without distorting lengths or angles.
A real normal matrix need not have real eigenvalues. For example, the quarter-turn (01​−10​) is an orthogonal matrix, but its characteristic polynomial is z2+1 and its eigenvalues are i,−i. Over the complex numbers it has an orthonormal eigenbasis; over the real numbers it has no eigenvector. Self-adjointness supplies the reality conclusion in the real spectral theorem.
