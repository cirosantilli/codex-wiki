Haar projection (ΠVJ​​f=∑k​⟨f,ϕJ,k​⟩ϕJ,k​)

The Haar projection at level J is the orthogonal projection onto the functions constant on level-J dyadic intervals. On each cell it replaces f by its cell average. This is the closest cellwise-constant approximation in the L2 norm, because choosing a constant c on a cell minimizes ∫I​∣f−c∣2 at its cell average.
On [0,1], take the step function with values 2,4,6,8 on successive quarter-intervals. At level zero, its Haar projection is the constant 5. At level one, it has values 3 and 7 on the two halves. At level two, it recovers the four original values exactly. With normalized Haar wavelets, these approximations are
P0​f=5ϕ,P1​f=5ϕ−2ψ,P2​f=5ϕ−2ψ−2​1​ψ1,0​−2​1​ψ1,1​.
(48)
The coefficients are L2 inner products, so they differ from the discrete vector coefficients by the factor 1/4​=1/2. Their squared sum also has a direct meaning:
∥f∥22​=422+42+62+82​=30=52+(−2)2+(−2​1​)2+(−2​1​)2.
(49)
The approximation errors in squared L2 norm of P0​f,P1​f,P2​f are respectively 5,1,0: each added detail removes exactly its own squared coefficient from the error.
The Haar projection can be expressed using level-J Haar scaling functions, or through a coarse scaling family and the Haar wavelets at intermediate levels. On [0,1] with lowest level zero, that coarse family is the single constant function ϕ. On the whole real line, use the integer translates of a coarse Haar scaling function instead; one global constant is not square-integrable. Refinement improves the representation while retaining the earlier coefficients.
