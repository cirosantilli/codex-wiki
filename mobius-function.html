Möbius function (μ(n))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function
OurBigBook.com Words: 896 Articles: 7
The Möbius function assigns a cancellation weight to each positive integer:
μ(n)=⎩⎨⎧​1(−1)r0​n=1,n=p1​⋯pr​ with distinct primes p1​,…,pr​,p2∣n for some prime p.​
(64)
Thus a squarefree integer contributes a sign determined by its number of distinct prime factors; a repeated prime factor makes the weight zero. This packages the alternating signs of inclusion-exclusion into an arithmetic function. [ref]
For example, μ(2)=μ(3)=−1, μ(6)=1, and μ(30)=(−1)3=−1, since 30=2⋅3⋅5. In contrast, μ(4)=μ(12)=μ(60)=0: all three have the square of the prime number 2 as a divisor. On prime powers, μ(p)=−1 and μ(pa)=0 for a≥2.
The Möbius function is a multiplicative function: for coprime integers, their prime factors are disjoint, so either a squared prime factor forces both sides of μ(mn)=μ(m)μ(n) to vanish, or the counts of distinct prime factors add and their signs multiply. It is not a completely multiplicative function, since μ(4)=0=1=μ(2)2. Its Möbius divisor-sum identity makes it the inverse of the constant-one arithmetic function under Dirichlet convolution: μ∗1=ε, the Identity for Dirichlet convolution. This is why the small set of weights −1,0,1 can undo an entire divisor sum.
