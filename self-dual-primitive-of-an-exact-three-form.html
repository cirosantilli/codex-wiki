Self-dual primitive of an exact three-form (σ=δψ+∗δψ,dσ=β)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential form Hodge star operator Harmonic differential form Hodge decomposition theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 66
On a compact oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension four without boundary, every exact three-form has a self-dual two-form primitive. Write β=dω and use the Hodge decomposition theorem to split ω=h+dξ+δψ. Then β=dδψ. For a=δψ, the codifferential identity δ=−∗d∗ and the fact ∗2=1 on two-forms give ∗a=−d∗ψ, so d∗a=0. Thus σ=a+∗a satisfies ∗σ=σ and dσ=β. It is twice the self-dual projection of a, rather than the projection itself.
