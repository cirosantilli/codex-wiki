Unsteady Stokes equation (ρ∂t​u=−∇p+μ∇2u+f)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Viscous fluid flow Stokes flow Stokes equation
OurBigBook.com Words: 110
For variations on a time scale T, the ratio of local inertia to viscous resistance is L2/(νT). Steady Stokes flow at each instant requires both UL/ν≪1 and L2/(νT)≪1. If only the first is small, retain local acceleration:
ρ∂t∂u​=−∇p+μ∇2u+f,∇⋅u=0.
(10)
For an oscillating sphere with angular frequency ω, the second condition becomes ωa2/ν≪1 on the radius scale a. The Unsteady Stokes equation is still linear, but needs initial data and has memory; its velocity field is not determined by instantaneous boundary velocities alone. See E. J. Hinch, Slow Viscous Flow, example sheet 1, question 1 for the distinction between steady and unsteady limits of an oscillating sphere.
