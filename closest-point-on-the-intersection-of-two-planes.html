Closest point on the intersection of two planes (a=αb+βc)

 ... Analysis Topological analysis Metric space Euclidean distance Distance from a point to a line Distance from a point to a line in three dimensions
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
For two planes r⋅b=λ and r⋅c=μ with linearly independent normal vectors, put B=b⋅b, C=c⋅c, D=b⋅c and Δ=BC−D2>0. Their intersection is a line parallel to b×c. Its closest point to the origin is orthogonal to that direction, hence has the form a=αb+βc. The constraints give Bα+Dβ=λ, Dα+Cβ=μ, so
α=ΔCλ−Dμ​,β=ΔBμ−Dλ​,∣a∣2=ΔCλ2−2Dλμ+Bμ2​.
(14)
For every other point a+t(b×c), orthogonality gives squared distance ∣a∣2+t2∣b×c∣2, proving the global minimum and its uniqueness. The positive determinant Δ=∣b×c∣2 also proves that an intersection exists for every real λ,μ. Parallel normals require a separate consistency check and do not define a unique intersection line.
