Unit vector (∥v∥=1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Vector space Vector
OurBigBook.com Words: 345 Articles: 2
A unit vector is a vector of norm one in a normed vector space. For a nonzero vector v, vector normalization produces one by dividing by ∥v∥. In a real Euclidean space, this keeps the direction and removes the magnitude. The zero vector cannot be normalized because its norm is zero.
For v=(3,4) with Euclidean norm 5, the corresponding unit vector is
v=(53​,54​),∥v∥22​=259​+2516​=1.
(15)
The Euclidean vector can therefore be reconstructed as v=5v: size and direction are distinct pieces of information. The standard basis vectors are familiar unit vectors, but a unit vector need not point along a coordinate axis.
