Inverse photoemission spectroscopy

 Home Physics Spectroscopy
OurBigBook.com Words: 38
Inverse photoemission spectroscopy injects an electron of known kinetic energy into a sample and detects the photon emitted as it enters an unoccupied electronic state. Conservation of energy, together with incident-angle information and an appropriate surface model, probes the unoccupied electronic band structure. Ordinary angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy instead removes electrons from occupied states.
