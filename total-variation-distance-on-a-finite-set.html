Total variation distance on a finite set (dTV​(P,Q)=21​∑x​∣P(x)−Q(x)∣)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Total variation distance
OurBigBook.com Words: 191
Consider the probability mass functions P=(0.4,0.1,0.2,0.3) and Q=(0.1,0.3,0.3,0.3) on the finite set S={A,B,C,D}. Their difference is (0.3,−0.2,−0.1,0). Thus
dTV​(P,Q)=21​(0.3+0.2+0.1+0)=0.3.​
(38)
The event E={A} attains this difference: P(E)=0.4 and Q(E)=0.1. Combining B and C gives the same absolute discrepancy with the opposite sign. Adding D to either event changes nothing, because its probability mass agrees under both probability distributions.
The factor 1/2 is necessary because ∑x​(P(x)−Q(x))=0: every unit of excess probability mass must match a unit of deficit. For any event, including a negative term cannot increase its signed discrepancy, while omitting a positive term cannot increase it either. Therefore {x:P(x)>Q(x)} attains the positive maximum. This is a short way to compute an apparent optimization over 2∣S∣ subsets using one pass through S.
For probability density functions p,q relative to the same measure λ, the corresponding identity is dTV​(P,Q)=21​∫∣p−q∣dλ. Such densities can always be taken as Radon-Nikodym derivatives relative to λ=P+Q. Beware the notation: the total variation norm of a measure applied to the signed measure P−Q equals 2dTV​(P,Q). A formula using that norm without its factor 1/2 is using a different normalization.
