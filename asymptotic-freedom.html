Asymptotic freedom

 ... Quantum field theory Perturbative quantum field theory Renormalization Renormalization scale Running coupling Beta function (physics)
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 8
An asymptotically free theory has a running coupling that tends to zero at arbitrarily high energy. Its negative weak-coupling beta function makes short-distance perturbation theory increasingly accurate.
In Quantum chromodynamics, this means that a short-distance probe can resolve quarks with a small QCD coupling, even though isolated quarks are absent from the observed hadron spectrum. This made asymptotic freedom a bridge between the approximately free constituents of the parton model and an interacting quantum field theory. Asymptotic freedom supplies control at large momentum transfer; it does not itself prove confinement.[ref]
Use the four-dimensional QCD coupling convention αs​=gs2​/(4π). For the SU(3) gauge group and nf​ active quark flavors in the fundamental representation, the one-loop order beta function is
μdμdgs​​=−16π2β0​gs3​​+O(gs5​),β0​=11−32nf​​,dlnμ2dαs​​=−4πβ0​​αs2​+O(αs3​).
(27)
Here nf​ counts quark flavors, not the three values of color charge. The chain rule converts the first equation to the last; changing from lnμ to lnμ2 supplies a factor of two. For β0​>0, the negative sign makes gs​=0 an attractive high-energy renormalization-group fixed point.[ref]
For β0​>0, set the renormalization scale μ=Q, where Q is a positive energy scale. The one-loop running of the strong coupling then gives
αs​(Q)=β0​ln(Q2/Λ2)4π​.
(28)
The strong-coupling scale Λ has the same units of measurement as Q, so the logarithm has a dimensionless argument. This is a fixed-nf​ approximation on the positive branch Q>Λ; controlled perturbative quantum field theory requires a small QCD coupling and suitably small omitted corrections, not merely a positive denominator. Heavy quark masses require matching the QCD scale across a quark threshold, changing nf​ and the associated Λ. Both the renormalization scheme and the loop order matter when quoting a numerical Λ.
