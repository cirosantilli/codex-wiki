Solid angle (Ω)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Sphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 922 Articles: 4
A solid angle measures how much of the surrounding directions a region occupies, as seen from a specified point O. Draw a sphere of radius R centered at O and mark where those directions meet its surface. If the marked patch has surface area AR​, then
Ω=R2AR​​.​
(17)
A Euclidean similarity scales both AR​ and R2 by the same factor, so the chosen radius does not affect the answer. On the unit two-sphere, the numerical value is simply the patch's surface area. Unlike an angle drawn in one plane, a solid angle measures coverage in two angular directions.
For example, the full sphere has surface area 4πR2 and hence solid angle 4π steradians. A hemisphere occupies 2π steradians. Three mutually perpendicular coordinate planes divide the directions into eight congruent pieces, so each octant occupies 4π/8=π/2 steradians. A solid angle of one steradian therefore covers 1/(4π)≈7.96% of all directions, rather than one full turn.
This makes a geometric question answer a collection question. For an ideal point emitter with isotropy, equal solid angles carry equal radiant flux. A detector accepting directions of total solid angle Ω intercepts the fraction Ω/(4π) of that emitted radiant flux, before losses. With direction-dependent radiant intensity I, the accepted radiant flux is instead ∫IdΩ.
