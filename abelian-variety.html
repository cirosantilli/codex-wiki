Prime-to-characteristic torsion of an abelian variety (A[N]≅(Z/NZ)2dimA)

Words: 83
Abelian variety OurBigBook.com
If N is invertible in the base field, multiplication by N on an abelian variety is finite étale: its differential is N times the identity, and properness turns its zero-dimensional fibres into finite fibres. The Theorem of the Cube gives [N]∗L≅LN2 for a symmetric ample line bundle. Top intersection products give degree N2dimA. For each prime power ℓr∣N, the kernels killed by ℓs have orders ℓ2sdimA; the classification of finite abelian groups forces 2dimA cyclic factors of length r. Combining the prime powers gives the displayed group.

Jacobian variety (J(C)=Pic0(C))

Words: 1k Articles: 20
Abelian variety OurBigBook.com
The Jacobian variety of a smooth projective algebraic curve parametrizes degree-zero line bundles. Its dimension is the genus of the algebraic curve. An effective degree-d Cartier divisor defines a point in a degree-d torsor through the Abel map of an algebraic curve. The theta divisor induces an isomorphism with the dual abelian variety.

Torelli theorem

Words: 66
Jacobian variety OurBigBook.com
A smooth projective curve over the complex numbers is determined up to isomorphism by its Jacobian variety together with its canonical principal polarization of a complex torus. For genus at least three, the branch locus of the theta Gauss map recovers the canonical curve by projective biduality theorem, or recovers the canonical rational normal curve and the branch points of a hyperelliptic curve. In genus two the theta divisor itself is the curve; in genus one the Jacobian variety is the curve after choosing an origin. The polarization is essential data in this statement.

Universal property of the Jacobian variety

Words: 53
Jacobian variety OurBigBook.com
For a smooth connected projective curve C with chosen basepoint p, the Abel map of an algebraic curve ip​:C→J(C) sends q to [q−p]. Every morphism g:C→A to an abelian variety with g(p)=0 factors uniquely through a homomorphism J(C)→A. This universal property converts a curve embedded in an abelian variety into a homomorphism from its Jacobian.

Theta divisor (Θ⊂Picg−1(C))

Words: 1k Articles: 14
Jacobian variety OurBigBook.com
The theta divisor consists of degree-g−1 line bundles with a nonzero global section. It is the irreducible image of the symmetric product of a curve C(g−1), with its reduced Cartier divisor structure. The determinant description of the theta divisor supplies equations, multiplicities and pullbacks in families.

Riemann vanishing theorem ({z:θ(z,B)=0}=Wg−1​−κ)

Words: 215 Articles: 2
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
For the period matrix of a genus-g compact Riemann surface, there is a vector κ depending on the marking and base point such that the Riemann theta function vanishes exactly on this translate of the image of the Abelian sum map. Pairing sides of the cut surface proves that a nonzero pullback θ(u(p)−z) has a degree-g zero divisor whose Abel sum is z+κ. At a theta zero that pullback includes the base point. Irreducibility and dimension of the effective-divisor locus give equality of supports; the primitive First Chern class of the theta line bundle makes the divisor reduced.
Theta pullback zero-divisor identity (degDz​=g,AJ(Dz​)=z+κ)
Words: 75
Riemann vanishing theorem OurBigBook.com
If p↦θ(u(p)−z) is not identically zero, its zero divisor Dz​ has these properties. The logarithmic derivative changes by −2πiωj​ across the paired bj​ translation, giving zero count g. For a variation of z, the logarithm changes there by 2πiδzj​; integrating by parts in the first-moment contour integral gives δAJ(Dz​)=δz. Thus the difference is constant. Weighted zero divisors, rather than a distinct-zero assumption, make the statement valid at collisions.
Vector of Riemann constants (κ=AJ(Dz​)−z)
Words: 53
Riemann vanishing theorem OurBigBook.com
The vector of Riemann constants is the parameter-independent class obtained from the zeros of p↦θ(u(p)−z) using the displayed formula. Under this sign convention the theta divisor is Wg−1​−κ. It depends on the base point and normalized symplectic marking; some accounts use its negative. Changing the marking or sign convention must also change the translation formula consistently.

Gauss map of a theta divisor (Γ:Θsm​→P(T0​J∗))

Words: 142 Articles: 1
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
Translate the tangent hyperplane at a smooth point of the theta divisor to the identity of the Jacobian variety. Its annihilator defines the Gauss map. At L=OX​(D) with h0(L)=1, the derivative of the Abelian sum map identifies this annihilator with the unique line H0(KX​(−D)). Thus Γ(L)=[ω] implies D≤div(ω). This connects the intrinsic geometry of the theta divisor to the canonical map of the curve.
Branch locus of the theta Gauss map (B⊂P(T0​J∗))
Words: 87
Gauss map of a theta divisor OurBigBook.com
Normalize the target in the function field of the generically finite Gauss map of a theta divisor; its finite map has an intrinsic branch locus. For a nonhyperelliptic smooth projective curve, this locus is the projective dual variety of the canonical curve. For a hyperelliptic curve of genus at least three, it is the union of the dual hypersurface of its canonical rational normal curve and the 2g+2 hyperplanes corresponding to branch points. Locally, ramification exchanges two roots of a hyperplane section, or exchanges the two lifts of a root passing through a branch point. Biduality then recovers the curve or its double-cover data.

Riemann singularity theorem (multL​Θ=h0(X,L))

Words: 174 Articles: 2
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
For a compact Riemann surface of genus g≥1, the theta divisor in Picg−1(X) has the displayed multiplicity. A local determinant description of the theta divisor reduces to an r by r matrix vanishing at L, where r=h0(L). Its linear term is cup product with a tangent class in H1(OX​). The invertible cup-product direction for a line bundle on a curve shows that its determinant has order exactly r. Consequently the singular locus consists precisely of classes with at least two independent global sections.

Holomorphic row and column operations leave a square matrix M of size r=h0(L) with zero constant term. Its linear part in a tangent direction ξ∈H1(OX​) is ξ⌣−:H0(L)→H1(L). The invertible cup-product direction for a line bundle on a curve makes the leading degree-r determinant nonzero, establishing order exactly r rather than just a lower bound.

Theta autoduality of a Jacobian (λΘ​:J(C)∼​J(C)​)

Words: 60
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
For Tq​(x)=x+q, the homomorphism associated to a line bundle on an abelian variety gives λΘ​(q)=Tq∗​O(Θ)⊗O(Θ)−1. Restrict to the negative Abel map of an algebraic curve. The restriction of the theta divisor to an Abel curve identifies this restriction with the degree-zero class q. Pullback of algebraically trivial line bundles does not change when the embedding is translated. Thus pullback ρ:J(C)​→J(C) satisfies ρλΘ​=1. Equal dimensions and properness make λΘ​ an isomorphism, including on schemes, rather than only on closed points.

Restriction of the theta divisor to an Abel curve (ϕD∗​Θ=D0​)

Words: 83
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
For a degree-g divisor class D with h0(OC​(D))=1, let D0​ be its unique effective representative. Under ϕD​(P)=[D−P], evaluation of the unique global section gives ϕD∗​Θ=D0​, including multiplicities. If h0(OC​(D))≥2, every evaluation kernel is nonzero and the entire curve lies in the theta divisor. These restrictions also show that h0(O(Θ))=1: after pulling back to a dominant product of curves, the ratio of any section to the defining section of the theta divisor is constant on each general coordinate curve, hence constant everywhere.

Determinant description of the theta divisor

Words: 335 Articles: 3
Theta divisor OurBigBook.com
Choose an effective Cartier divisor E of degree at least g on a smooth projective genus-g algebraic curve. For a family M of degree-g−1 line bundles, the evaluation map from π∗​M(E) to π∗​(M(E)∣E​) is between vector bundles of the same rank. Its determinant vanishes exactly when H0(C,M) is nonzero. Changing E changes the presentation by cancelling an invertible determinant factor; the zero Cartier divisor is the theta divisor. At a point with h0(M)=h1(M)=1, the derivative is the cup-product map H1(OC​)→Hom(H0(M),H1(M)). By Serre duality it is nonzero, since the product of nonzero sections of M and KC​⊗M−1 is nonzero. Hence this Cartier divisor is generically reduced.
Invertible cup-product direction for a line bundle on a curve (ξ⌣−:H0(L)∼​H1(L))
Words: 91
Determinant description of the theta divisor OurBigBook.com
Suppose a line bundle L of degree g−1 on a smooth projective curve has h0(L)=h1(L)=r. Choose r distinct points where evaluation is invertible for both H0(L) and H0(KX​⊗L−1). Such choices exist simultaneously: each determinant defines a nonempty open subset of Xr, since independent sections cannot vanish at every point. Let ξ∈H1(OX​)=H0(KX​)∗ be a sum of nonzero weighted evaluation functionals at these points. The Serre duality matrix of ξ⌣− factors as the product of the two invertible evaluation matrices and an invertible diagonal matrix. Its determinant is nonzero. This gives the nonzero leading determinant needed for the Riemann singularity theorem.

Abel map of an algebraic curve (ad​:C(d)→Picd(C))

Words: 197 Articles: 2
Jacobian variety OurBigBook.com
The Abel map of an algebraic curve sends an effective Cartier divisor D to its line bundle OC​(D). Its scheme fibre over a line bundle L is the projective space of one-dimensional subspaces of H0(C,L). This follows in families: a relative effective Cartier divisor in the fixed class determines a nowhere fibre-zero section of L up to a line bundle from the parameter space, equivalently a line subbundle of H0(C,L)⊗OT​. Thus the scheme fibre is smooth, not merely reduced on its points. The dimensions can nevertheless jump, so the Abel map of an algebraic curve need not be a smooth morphism.

Derivative of the Abelian sum map (duD​:H0(OD​(D))→H1(OX​))

Words: 115 Articles: 1
Abel map of an algebraic curve OurBigBook.com
For an effective divisor on an algebraic curve D on a smooth projective curve, the Zariski tangent space to its symmetric product of a curve is H0(OD​(D)). The derivative of the Abelian sum map is the connecting homomorphism of 0→OX​→OX​(D)→OD​(D)→0. Its dual under Serre duality is restriction of holomorphic differential forms to D, including all jets at repeated points. Hence its rank is degD+1−h0(OX​(D)), and its image annihilates H0(KX​(−D)).

Curve generation criterion for an abelian variety

Words: 99
Abelian variety OurBigBook.com
A smooth projective curve C⊂A generates A by its point differences if and only if it meets every prime Weil divisor. If a prime Weil divisor avoids C, its restriction to C has degree zero; constancy of line bundle degree in a family shows that every translate either contains C or misses it. This forces invariance under C−C and puts the generated subgroup inside the line bundle translation stabilizer. If this subgroup were all of A, the divisor would be algebraically trivial, contradicting its positive intersection with an ample line bundle. Conversely a proper closed generated subgroup admits a disjoint divisor by the pole divisor avoiding a fiber construction, after translating the fiber to contain C.

Dual abelian variety (A=Pic0(A))

Words: 175 Articles: 2
Abelian variety OurBigBook.com
The dual of an abelian variety represents algebraically trivial line bundles rigidified at the origin. Its group law is tensor product. A group homomorphism f:A→B induces f​:B→A by pullback of these line bundles.

Weil pairing for an abelian variety (eN​:A[N]×A[N]→μN​)

Words: 99
Dual abelian variety OurBigBook.com
For an abelian variety and an integer N invertible in the base field, a torsion line bundle M∈A[N] has trivial pullback by [N]. Choose a trivialization. The descent action of the kernel A[N] on that trivial line bundle acts through a character χM​:A[N]→μN​. Define eN​(P,M)=χM​(P). This is independent of rescaling the trivialization and bilinear under translation and tensor product. A trivial character descends the trivial bundle, so M is trivial. Since both groups have order N2dimA, the character map is an isomorphism and the Weil pairing for an abelian variety is perfect. The theta autoduality of a Jacobian turns it into a pairing on two copies of the Jacobian variety's torsion group.

Theorem of the Cube

Words: 363 Articles: 8
Abelian variety OurBigBook.com
The Theorem of the Cube says that the third finite difference of a line bundle on an abelian variety is trivial. On X3, it is the alternating tensor product of the pullbacks along the seven nonempty partial-sum maps.

Arithmetic cube identity for Weil heights (h(P+Q+R)−h(P+Q)−h(P+R)−h(Q+R)+h(P)+h(Q)+h(R)−h(O)=O(1))

Words: 63
Theorem of the Cube OurBigBook.com
For a line bundle on an elliptic curve over a number field, the geometric cube identity remains exact as a line-bundle identity. The Weil height machine translates it into the displayed bounded-error identity for a chosen logarithmic height, uniformly on the rational triples. This distinction is essential: arbitrary naive heights do not obey an exact quadratic identity. Dividing the identity for 2nP,2nQ,2nR by 4n produces an exact identity for the canonical height of an elliptic curve.

Cohomological proof of the cube theorem for elliptic curves

Words: 83
Theorem of the Cube OurBigBook.com
If a line bundle on E3 is trivial on each zero-coordinate face, its First Chern class vanishes: the integral second cohomology consists of single-factor second cohomology and cross-products of first cohomology from pairs, and each summand is detected on a coordinate face. The holomorphic exponential sequence then puts the bundle in Pic0(E3). The decomposition of first cohomology, including Dolbeault cohomology, identifies this group with Pic0(E)3. Triviality on the three coordinate axes makes all three factors trivial. Applying this cube principle to the alternating partial-sum pullbacks proves the usual addition-map Theorem of the Cube.

For group homomorphisms f,g:A→B, this Picard group class is symmetric and additive in both arguments. The Theorem of the Cube says precisely that the third additive difference of h↦[h∗L] vanishes, proving additivity of the cross-effect. The Poincaré line bundle expresses it as (f,ϕL​g)∗PB​, so it depends only on the Néron-Severi group class of L. Its associated homomorphism is f​ϕL​g+g​ϕL​f.
