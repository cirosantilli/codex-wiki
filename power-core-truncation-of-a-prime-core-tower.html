Power-core truncation of a prime-core tower

 ... Representation theory Representation theory of the symmetric group Partition of an integer Beta set of a partition Abacus of a partition Core tower of a partition
OurBigBook.com Words: 134
For a prime number p, integer k≥1, and γ=Cpk​(λ), taking the pk-core of a partition preserves the entries of the core tower of a partition at levels 0,…,k−1 and makes every level at least k empty. Also the weight of a partition wpk​(λ) equals the total size at level k of the quotient tower of a partition.
For the first claim, a removable rim hook of length pk is a bead move by pk on the abacus of a partition. It preserves the numbers of beads on the p runners and hence the p-core. Under the abacus divisible-hook correspondence, it becomes removal of a pk−1-hook in one quotient component; iteration preserves the cores at the next k−1 levels. After all pk-hooks are removed, every component at quotient level k is empty, so every higher core level is empty as well. The weight assertion follows from iterated quotient equals a power quotient up to permutation and the size formula for the quotient of a partition.
