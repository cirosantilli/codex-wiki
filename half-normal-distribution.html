Half-normal distribution (H=∣Z∣)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Normal distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 208 Articles: 1
The half-normal distribution describes the absolute value of a centered Gaussian random variable. Its unit-scale version is H=∣Z∣ for a standard normal random variable Z. It folds the negative half of the standard normal distribution onto the positive half, retaining the size of a deviation while discarding its sign. If a centered Gaussian random variable models measurement error, its magnitude therefore has a half-normal distribution.

Here ϕ is the standard normal density and Φ is the standard normal distribution function. For x≥0, the event H≤x is −x≤Z≤x, so the symmetric probability distribution of Z gives FH​(x)=Φ(x)−Φ(−x)=2Φ(x)−1. The upper-tail probability is therefore P(H>x)=2(1−Φ(x)). For example, P(H≤1)≈0.6826894921. The doubled probability density function integrates to one on [0,∞), and P(H=0)=0.
The mean and variance of the unit-scale half-normal distribution are

More generally, σ∣Z∣ for σ>0 has a half-normal distribution with probability density function 2ϕ(x/σ)/σ for x≥0. Its mean is σ2/π​ and its variance is σ2(1−2/π); here σ is the scale of the underlying Gaussian random variable, not the standard deviation of its absolute value.
