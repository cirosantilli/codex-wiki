COBE DMR sky anisotropy experiment

 ... Physics Branches of physics Cosmology Cosmic microwave background Cosmic microwave background anisotropy Differential Microwave Radiometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 459 Articles: 1
In 1992 the COBE DMR team, including George F. Smoot and Charles L. Bennett, used radiometer antennas to compare antenna temperatures between sky directions 60∘ apart at 31.5, 53 and 90GHz, with two nearly independent channels per frequency. Repeated sky coverage supported least-squares solution map reconstruction.[ref]
The analysis fitted magnetic response and removed the assumed kinematic angular dipole and its second-order Doppler effect contribution. Channel sums retained sky structure; channel differences estimated noise. Cross-channel correlations, different frequency maps and Milky Way latitude cuts tested the interpretation. Residual large-angle structure supported Cosmic microwave background anisotropy; Galactic contamination particularly limited the cosmic microwave background quadrupole. Brightness fluctuations alone were not sufficient evidence of primordial structure.[ref]
The two differences serve different purposes. Comparing two sky directions measures angular variation. Comparing two independently calibrated receiver maps observing the same sky tests noise and consistency. A common sky signal cancels in the latter comparison if their antenna beam patterns and response scales match; a systematic error shared by both channels need not cancel. Separating observer motion, local emission and intrinsic Cosmic microwave background anisotropy requires these additional tests.
