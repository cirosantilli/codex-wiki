Nuisances Removal Act for England (Amendment) Act 1863 (26 & 27 Vict. c.117)

Words: 29
Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com
www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/26-27/117/pdfs/ukpga_18630117_en.pdf
Original printed Act, enacted 28 July 1863. Section 2 provides inspection, seizure and a justice's disposal order for unfit food; it does not provide an exhaustive disease-by-disease whole-carcass list.

Current United States meat-inspection regulations, 9 CFR Part 311

Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com

Present United States comparative criteria, specifically §311.2, tuberculosis and §311.16, carcasses presenting a food-poisoning risk. These are not English law in 1864.

Health of the Navy: Home Station report (1864)

Words: 30
Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com
www.pdavis.nl/H1864_Home.htm
Transcription of the official 1864 naval medical report, venereal-disease discussion. It distinguishes infecting sores, soft sores, buboes, relapses, mercurial practice and disagreement. The transcription is not a newly collated facsimile.

Health Tract No.154: Disinfectants (1865) (1865)

Words: 22
Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com
pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9199822/
1865, volume 12, p.154. Primary tract on cleanliness, deodorization and proposed disinfectants. Its recipes, miasma explanations and oxygen-refreshing claims are not endorsed.

Water, Sanitation and Health in Humanitarian Emergencies by the World Health Organization

Words: 24
Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com
www.who.int/teams/environment-climate-change-and-health/water-sanitation-and-health/environmental-health-in-emergencies/humanitarian-emergencies
Institutional explanation of epidemic risks, enteric disease, contact exposures and mosquito breeding. Cited to distinguish exposure routes, not to prescribe a historical water allowance.

The British Soldier in India in The British and Foreign Medico-Chirurgical Review (1861) (1861)

Words: 34
Public health and forensic medicine bibliography OurBigBook.com
pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5182393/
January 1861, 27(53), pp.38–48; especially pp.43–46, an asserted lime-water explanation for goitre and cretinism. This primary period review documents the historical belief, not a correct modern causal conclusion. PMC metadata misspells “Soldier” as “Solider”.

Current institutional fact sheet: enteric infections, chemically contaminated water and lead from water-system components. “Water and health” separates several mechanisms.
