Finite supremum of positive measures (⋁i=1N​μi​)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Measure theory Measure Positive measure
OurBigBook.com Words: 257 Articles: 1
For finite positive measures on the same sigma-algebra, their least common upper bound in the order on measures is
(⋁i​μi​)(A)=supA=⨆i​Ai​​∑i​μi​(Ai​).
(15)
Empty cells are allowed. This is a measure, not merely a set function: for a disjoint countable union, intersect any candidate partition with its components for the upper bound, and combine partitions whose errors sum to less than a prescribed ε for the lower bound. Countable additivity of each component gives both inequalities. Any measure dominating every μi​ dominates each partition sum and hence this supremum. The result is bounded above by ∑i​μi​ and so remains finite.
If all components have densities hi​ with respect to a common measure κ, then the supremum has density maxi​hi​. For the upper bound integrate the pointwise maximum. For equality, partition by the smallest index attaining that maximum; these are measurable sets. For example two measures supported on disjoint sets have their sum as supremum, rather than the numerical maximum of their masses on the whole space.
