Barkhausen pulse area (∫edt=−Δλ)

 ... Branches of physics Electromagnetism Bound charge and bound current Magnetization Ferromagnetism Barkhausen effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 243
Write the magnetic flux linkage of the stationary pickup coil as λ=∑n=1N​Φn​, in SI units of weber. If every turn links the same magnetic flux Φ, then λ=NΦ; the turn count is dimensionless. With a fixed winding and terminal convention, Faraday's law of induction gives the induced electromotive force
e(t)=−dtdλ​,∫ta​tb​​e(t)dt=−[λ(tb​)−λ(ta​)].
(42)
This is the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to magnetic flux linkage. Its units are Vs=Wb, not volts alone. Reversing the leads reverses the sign. In a high-input-electrical resistance voltage measurement the input approximately follows this electromotive force; coil loading and the frequency response of the electronics require separate calibration.
Separate direct drive linkage from material linkage: λ=λdrive​+λmaterial​. Even an empty pickup coil has −dλdrive​/dt. In an ideal long, uniformly coupled specimen with negligible demagnetizing field correction and eddy current shielding, a useful material approximation is
λmaterial​=μ0​NAs​M,∫ematerial​dt=−μ0​NAs​ΔM.
(43)
Here As​ is specimen cross-sectional area, not the larger coil aperture; μ0​ is vacuum permeability and M the axial magnetization sampled by the coil. The units check directly: (Vs/(Am))(m2)(A/m)=Vs. Finite geometry and nonuniform magnetization generally require a calibrated coupling in place of μ0​NAs​. The exact linkage identity survives that failure of the uniform approximation.
As an original illustrative calculation, stipulate N=100, As​=10−6m2, ΔM=103A/m and rounded μ0​=4π×10−7Vs/(Am). Then
Δλ=4π×10−8Wb≈0.125664μWb,∫ematerial​dt≈−0.125664mVms.
(44)
A uniform change over 0.10ms would give a rectangular pulse of −1.25664mV; over 0.20ms it gives −0.628319mV with the same area. Thus pulse height mixes change size with change speed. These numbers describe an ideal model, not observed pulses or material constants.
