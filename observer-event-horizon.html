Observer event horizon (Hγ+​=∂I−(γ),Hγ−​=∂I+(γ))

 Home Physics Branches of physics General relativity Causal hierarchy
OurBigBook.com Words: 160 Articles: 2
An observer's future event horizon bounds the events that can ever signal to its future-inextendible worldline. Its past event horizon bounds the events reachable by signals from its past-inextendible worldline. A complete inertial observer in Minkowski spacetime has neither. The uniformly accelerated worldline t=a−1sinh(aτ), x=a−1cosh(aτ) has I−(γ)={t−x<0} and I+(γ)={t+x>0}. Their boundaries are its two Rindler horizons. This observer dependence is distinct from a black-hole horizon defined using future null infinity.
