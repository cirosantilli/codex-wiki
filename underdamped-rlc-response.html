Underdamped RLC response (ωd​=ω02​−α2​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit RLC circuit Series RLC circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
An underdamped RLC response is a free series RLC circuit response with 0<R<2L/C​, equivalently 0<α<ω0​. Its characteristic roots are −α±iωd​, and its general electric charge is
q(t)=e−αt(Acos(ωd​t)+Bsin(ωd​t)).
(34)
For initial conditions q(0)=q0​, i(0)=0,
q(t)=q0​e−αt(cos(ωd​t)+ωd​α​sin(ωd​t)),i(t)=−ωd​q0​ω02​​e−αtsin(ωd​t).
(35)
In the dimensionless example ω0​=1, α=0.5, the ringing angular frequency is ωd​=3​/2≈0.866025, below the driven electric current resonance at ω0​=1. The electric charge first crosses zero at t=4π/(33​)≈2.41840 and reaches its first negative extremum at t=2π/3​≈3.62760, where q/q0​=−e−π/3​≈−0.163034.
The sinusoidal factor changes sign while its envelope decays as e−αt. The total energy follows the exact balance E′=−Ri2, rather than a pure exponential decay at every instant; in particular, its rate of loss is zero whenever i=0. As α→0, the displayed solution tends to the lossless harmonic oscillator q=q0​cos(ω0​t). As α→ω0​ from below, sin(ωd​t)/ωd​→t, recovering the critically damped RLC response continuously. The overdamped RLC response reaches the same limit through sinh(βt)/β→t.
