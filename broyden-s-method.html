Broyden's method (Bk+1​=Bk​+(yk​−Bk​sk​)skT​/(skT​sk​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 127
A secant-updating method for a vector equation F(x)=0, named for Charles George Broyden. Solve Bk​dk​=−F(xk​), choose xk+1​=xk​+αk​dk​, and form sk​=xk+1​−xk​, yk​=F(xk+1​)−F(xk​). For a nonzero step the displayed “good Broyden” update satisfies Bk+1​sk​=yk​, learning the Jacobian matrix action along the observed step rather than evaluating a new full Jacobian matrix.
The update also has a least-change characterization in the Frobenius norm. Write u=yk​−Bk​sk​. Any correction E satisfying Esk​=u has squared row norms bounded below by ui2​/∥sk​∥2, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Summing these bounds gives ∥E∥F2​≥∥u∥2/∥sk​∥2, with equality for E=uskT​/∥sk​∥2. This proves the rank-one correction is the least-change update satisfying the secant constraint. Superlinear convergence needs local regularity and suitable initialization; it is not a global consequence of this algebraic identity.[ref]
