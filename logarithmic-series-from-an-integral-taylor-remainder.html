The integral remainder in Taylor theorem for log(1−t) gives the exact finite identity
−log(1−t)=∑k=1N​ktk​+∫0t​(1−x)N+1(t−x)N​dx.
(18)
For 0≤x≤t<1, (t−x)/(1−x)≤t, so the nonnegative remainder is at most tN+1/(1−t) and tends to zero. At t=1/2 it is at most 2−N, proving both convergence to log2 and an explicit error bound without assuming the logarithmic power series beforehand.
