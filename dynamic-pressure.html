Dynamic pressure (q=ρU2/2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Lift (force) Lift coefficient
OurBigBook.com Words: 594
Dynamic pressure is the bulk kinetic energy per unit volume associated with fluid motion at speed U in a specified reference frame:
q=21​ρU2,V21​mU2​=21​ρU2,Jm−3=Pa.
(7)
Here ρ=m/V is mass density. The word "pressure" identifies its units and its use as a pressure scale; q is not an extra isotropic stress to add to the local static pressure in every surface traction. A stationary fluid can have large static pressure with q=0. Conversely, knowing U and ρ fixes q without fixing static pressure. The definition remains useful in viscous fluid flow and compressible flow, even when the simplest Bernoulli equation is inapplicable. [ref]
For stipulated mass density ρ∞​=1.20kgm−3 and reference speed U∞​=20ms−1, q∞​=240Pa. At 40ms−1 with the same mass density, q∞​=960Pa. This quadratic scaling makes dynamic pressure a useful normalization for the lift coefficient, drag coefficient and pressure coefficient; it predicts four times a particular force only if its corresponding coefficient remains unchanged.
The connection with stagnation pressure requires hypotheses. For steady flow, constant mass density, inviscid flow, and gravity as the only body force, the Bernoulli equation gives, along one streamline,
p+21​ρU2+ρgz=B.
(8)
Here p is static pressure, g is gravitational acceleration, and z is height. Bring that flow to rest without losses at the same height and the limiting static pressure is the stagnation pressure p0​:
p0​=p+q,p0​−p=21​ρU2.
(9)
There must be no intervening pump supplying work or other energy input. Constancy along one streamline does not assert equal B on all streamlines; irrotational flow in a connected region supplies that additional conclusion. Changing height requires retaining ρgz. In compressible flow the stopping pressure difference generally differs from ρU2/2, and viscous dissipation can reduce recovered stagnation pressure. [ref]
A Pitot-static tube turns this relation into a measurement: its upstream-facing opening senses stagnation pressure, while separate side openings sample static pressure. Connect these to the positive and negative ports of a differential pressure transducer or manometer. Under the hypotheses above, the measured difference Δp gives
U=ρ2Δp​​.
(10)
Thus the stipulated 240Pa and 1.20kgm−3 imply 20ms−1, not an independently measured speed. Align the probe with the incoming flow and keep its passages clear: blockage and pinched tubing invalidate the comparison. At very low speed, the difference can be smaller than instrument error; at high Mach number, compressibility and possibly a shock wave require a different reduction. [ref]
A useful control is differential-pressure calibration. For a digital manometer, zeroing establishes the reading at equal port pressures; it does not establish the gain away from zero. The manufacturer's Series 475 Handheld Digital Manometer: Specifications - Installation and Operating Instructions by Dwyer Instruments, Inc. directs the user to vent both ports to the atmosphere before zeroing. Its stated accuracy is a fraction of full scale, not of the reading, and depends on instrument temperature. Select a suitable range and record the instrument variant and units; the manual covers several ranges.[ref]
An independent physical check can use a liquid-column manometer: apply a stable pressure difference to both instruments and compare the digital reading with the hydrostatic pressure inferred from the vertical level difference. For equal-height connections carrying a gas of mass density ρg​ above a manometric liquid of mass density ρm​,
∣Δp∣=(ρm​−ρg​)g∣h∣.
(11)
This follows by balancing hydrostatic pressure down and up the two connected columns. Use vertical height, even if a tube is inclined. Known mass densities, measured height and gravitational acceleration set the comparison scale. Check several differences across the working range and reverse the port connections to check the sign. This is a proposed independent check, not a claim that the cited teaching experiment performed a full instrument calibration. Recheck zero after reconnecting the sealed tubing; a zero adjustment alone cannot detect a blocked passage or an incorrect scale.
