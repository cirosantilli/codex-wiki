Phase stiffness (ρs​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Critical phenomenon Goldstone boson Goldstone-mode effective free energy
OurBigBook.com Words: 109
The phase stiffness is the coefficient of 21​∫ddx(∇θ)2 in the long-distance free energy. Its renormalized value determines the thermal phase fluctuations and algebraic correlation function of a two-dimensional O(N) model with N=2.
Operationally impose a small phase difference ϕ between opposite sides of a square sample of side L. The smooth minimum has θ=ϕx/L, so the two-dimensional excess free energy is
F(ϕ)−F(0)=21​ρs​ϕ2+o(ϕ2),ρs​=∂ϕ2∂2F​​ϕ=0​.
(60)
The derivative concerns a prescribed twist at the stated observation scale; in d dimensions its conventional size factor is Ld−2. This response definition includes thermal fluctuations and vortices after equilibration, whereas a microscopic gradient coefficient need not. A finite phase stiffness requires no nonzero spatially uniform order parameter; the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition is the instructive example.
