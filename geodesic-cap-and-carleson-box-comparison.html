Geodesic-cap and Carleson-box comparison

OurBigBook.com Words: 202
For a short arc I of normalized length ℓ, every point of its hyperbolic geodesic cap lies within Euclidean distance Cℓ of its center boundary point. Conversely, for any fixed b>0, the region with angular projection in I and depth 1−∣z∣≤bℓ lies in Q(κI) for a fixed enlargement κ depending only on b, when I is sufficiently short. To verify this, rotate the midpoint to one and write the endpoints as e±iα. The cap inequality is 2Rez>(1+∣z∣2)cosα. Its radial depth at the midpoint is 1−secα+tanα, comparable to α for small α; its angular projection lies in I. Enlarging α by a sufficiently large fixed factor makes the inequality hold throughout the indicated rectangle. Large arcs use a finite cover of the disc. Thus Carleson boxes and hyperbolic geodesic caps define equivalent Carleson measure conditions.
The rectangle containment can also be checked directly. Write z=reiθ with ∣θ∣≤πℓ and 1−r≤bℓ. Its membership in the enlarged cap is equivalent to
cosθ−cos(κπℓ)>2r(1−r)2​cos(κπℓ).
(31)
For small enough ℓ, r≥1/2. The right side is at most b2ℓ2, while the left side is at least (κ2−1)π2ℓ2/8, using the sine product formula and sinx≥x/2 for small positive x. Thus κ=4+4b/π suffices.
