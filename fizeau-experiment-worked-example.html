Fizeau experiment worked example

Choose ℓ=1.50m, n=4/3, λ0​=550nm and u=6.00ms−1, with c=299792458ms−1. These are illustrative choices, not measured values or a reconstruction of Hippolyte Fizeau's data. Each beam traverses L=3.00m of water. The nondispersive prediction is
N=(299792458)(550×10−9)4(1.50)(6.00)(7/9)​≃0.16981,2N≃0.33963,Δt≃3.1154×10−16s.
(41)
The first number is the offset from rest to one flow setting; the second compares equal opposite settings. The very short equivalent delay explains why interference is useful: a measurable fraction of a wavelength replaces an unattainable direct timing measurement.
The Galilean transformation would give phase velocity magnitudes c/n±u, hence Nfull​≃4ℓun2/(cλ0​). At the chosen values this is 0.38815 fringe from rest, larger than the special relativity prediction by n2/(n2−1)=16/7. A model with no flow-induced change of phase velocity instead gives N=0. The three alternatives are experimentally distinguishable even when u/c is tiny.
Hippolyte Fizeau's positive displacement favoured Augustin-Jean Fresnel's partial-drag law over zero or full drag. He explicitly distinguished support for that law from establishing its underlying luminiferous aether hypothesis.[ref] The Fizeau experiment therefore supplies a nonzero relative-motion measurement, not a null test proving an luminiferous aether's absence. The derivation above exhibits the same first-order law within special relativity, showing why agreement with the coefficient alone cannot select an physical interpretation.
Two limitations matter for precision. The velocity profile sampled by the beam need not equal the cross-sectional average inferred from volumetric flow rate. Also, the Doppler effect gives opposite rest-frame frequencies for the counterpropagating beams. With optical dispersion, the coefficient n2−1 in the interference fringe formula becomes
n2−1+nωdωdn​=n2−1−nλ0​dλ0​dn​,
(42)
where the refractive index and its derivative are evaluated for stationary water at the laboratory angular frequency ω=2πc/λ0​. A wavelength-independent n therefore defines an approximation, not a precision fit to real water.[ref] Flow reversal removes a fixed phase difference, but a mechanical or temperature change that reverses with the flow can still imitate the signal and must be controlled.
