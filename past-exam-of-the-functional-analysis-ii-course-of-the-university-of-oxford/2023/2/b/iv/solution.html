Let J:C([0,1])→L1(0,1) send a continuous function to its almost everywhere equivalence class. The domain with the supremum norm and the target with the L1 norm are Banach spaces, and
∥Jf∥L1​=∫01​∣f(t)∣dt≤∥f∥∞​.
(23)
Thus J is a bounded linear operator, and the requested vector subspace is J(C([0,1])).
To show this image of a linear map is not closed, consider the continuous functions
fn​(t)=min{1,max{0,n(t−1/2)}}(n≥2)
(24)
and g=1(1/2,1)​. Their L1 norm difference is the area of one triangle:
∥Jfn​−g∥L1​=∫1/21/2+1/n​(1−n(t−1/2))dt=2n1​⟶0.
(25)
But g has no continuous function representative. Indeed, a continuous function equal to zero almost everywhere on (0,1/2) must be identically zero there: any nonzero value would persist on an open interval of positive Lebesgue measure. Likewise any continuous representative would be identically one on (1/2,1). Continuity at 1/2 makes these requirements incompatible. Therefore g∈/J(C([0,1])), although it lies in its closure.
The preceding operator range category dichotomy proves continuous functions are meagre in L1:
J(C([0,1])) is of the first category in L1(0,1).​
(26)
