Uniformly magnetized cylinder (M=Mz,Kb​=Mϕ​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Bound charge and bound current Magnetization
OurBigBook.com Words: 349
Prescribe constant magnetization M=Mz, with M>0, inside a circular cylinder of radius R and length L, surrounded by zero magnetization. In cylindrical coordinates, the outward unit normal on the curved surface is r, so
Jb​=0in the interior,Kb​=Mz×r=Mϕ​on the side.
(36)
The endcaps have outward unit normals +z and −z. Their cross products with magnetization vanish, so Kb​=0 on each endcap. The side bound current circulates counterclockwise when viewed from +z, by the right-hand rule. Zero endcap surface current density does not imply zero endcap magnetic field.
For an original example, let M=800A/m, R=0.020m, and L=0.10m. The volume and total magnetic dipole moment are
V=πR2L=4π×10−5m3,m=MVz=0.032πAm2z≈0.100531Am2z.
(37)
Check the result independently using the equivalent bound current: a side strip of axial length dz forms a current loop with dI=Mdz and magnetic dipole moment dm=πR2Mdzz. Its integral over L gives the same MπR2Lz. In particular, ML=80A is the sum of the equivalent loop electric currents across a full axial cut; it is not a surface current density.
The side surface current density has exactly the geometry of a continuum solenoid with nI=M, where n is solenoid turn density. In the infinite-cylinder idealization with no applied magnetic field, the long-solenoid approximation therefore becomes
Bin​=μ0​Mz,Bout​=0,H=0.
(38)
The limit is useful well away from the ends of a sufficiently long circular cylinder. A finite circular cylinder instead has fringing magnetic field and a demagnetizing field. For this prescribed uniform magnetization, integrate the on-axis magnetic field of a circular current loop, or substitute nI=M in the on-axis magnetic field of a finite solenoid, to obtain the central magnetic field:
Bz​(0)=μ0​ML2+4R2​L​.
(39)
Our example has L/(2R)=2.5 and Bz​(0)/(μ0​M)=5/29​≈0.92848, rather than unity. With the rounded vacuum permeability μ0​≈4π×10−7Tm/A, this is approximately 0.9334mT, compared with the infinite-cylinder value 1.0053mT. These are model calculations for a prescribed magnetization, not measured values or a solution for the self-consistent magnetization of an arbitrary material.
