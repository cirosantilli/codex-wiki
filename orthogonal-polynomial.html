Orthogonal polynomial (⟨pn​,pk​⟩w​=hn​δnk​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 18
An orthogonal polynomial family chooses polynomials that do not overlap under a specified inner product. For real polynomials on an interval (a,b), use the weighted inner product
⟨p,q⟩w​=∫ab​p(x)q(x)w(x)dx,∥p∥w2​=⟨p,p⟩w​.
(1)
Assume a<b, the weight w is measurable and positive almost everywhere, and its absolute moments ∫ab​∣x∣kw(x)dx are finite for every integer k≥0. These conditions make the integrals finite and give every nonzero polynomial positive squared norm. A sequence p0​,p1​,… is an orthogonal polynomial family when pn​ has real coefficients and polynomial degree exactly n, and ⟨pn​,pk​⟩w​=0 for n=k.[ref]
Here hn​=∥pn​∥w2​>0 and δnk​ is the Kronecker delta. Orthogonality does not require hn​=1: an orthonormal set is obtained only after replacing pn​ by pn​/hn​​. Multiplying each member by any nonzero real constant preserves orthogonality, but changes its norm and the corresponding expansion coefficient. The monic orthogonal polynomial convention instead fixes each leading coefficient to one.
The first four Legendre polynomials, named for Adrien-Marie Legendre, use w=1 on [−1,1] and the normalization Pn​(1)=1:
P0​(x)=1,P1​(x)=x,P2​(x)=23x2−1​,P3​(x)=25x3−3x​.
(2)
Their orthogonality and squared norms are
∫−11​Pn​(x)Pk​(x)dx=2n+12​δnk​.
(3)
Thus their Gram matrix has a positive diagonal and zero entries elsewhere, although their function graphs cross and overlap on the page. Orthogonality measures an integral of products, rather than separation of function graphs.[ref]
For these four polynomials, a short verification expands each product and uses ∫−11​x2j+1dx=0 and ∫−11​x2jdx=2/(2j+1). For example, ⟨P0​,P2​⟩=(3(2/3)−2)/2=0 and ⟨P1​,P3​⟩=(5(2/5)−3(2/3))/2=0. The same calculation gives the diagonal entries 2,2/3,2/5,2/7. More generally, apply the Gram-Schmidt process to the monomial basis 1,x,x2,…: at step n, subtract projections onto the earlier polynomials. The result retains its leading term, has degree n, and has positive norm, proving existence without a differential equation. Different degrees also give linear independence, so p0​,…,pm​ form a basis of the polynomials of degree at most m.
This basis turns least-squares solutions for functions into independent scalar calculations, as the decoupled coefficients of orthogonal polynomial projections show. In a spectral method, increasing the degree enlarges the approximation space while keeping earlier projection coefficients fixed. Weighted integrals also connect the same orthogonal polynomials to Gaussian quadrature: its nodes are polynomial roots of a member of the family. The projection problem minimizes an integrated squared error; Gaussian quadrature uses the family to choose evaluation points for an integral.

Decoupled coefficients of orthogonal polynomial projections (cn​=⟨f,pn​⟩w​/hn​)

Words: 484 Articles: 1
Orthogonal polynomial OurBigBook.com
Let f be a real function with ∥f∥w2​=∫ab​f(x)2w(x)dx<∞, and let Vm​ be the vector space of real polynomials of degree at most m. Its orthogonal projection is the least-squares polynomial in an orthogonal-polynomial basis
qm​=∑n=0m​cn​pn​,cn​=hn​⟨f,pn​⟩w​​,hn​=⟨pn​,pn​⟩w​.
(5)
Each coefficient depends on just one weighted inner product with f and one squared norm. In particular, the coefficients c0​,…,cm​ stay the same when the allowed polynomial degree increases. This statement assumes a fixed weight, interval and normalization of the orthogonal polynomials.
To see why the least-squares normal equations decouple, first write a trial polynomial as q=∑j=0m​aj​ϕj​ in any basis of Vm​. The residual must be orthogonal to each permitted direction, giving
∑j=0m​Gij​aj​=bi​,Gij​=⟨ϕj​,ϕi​⟩w​,bi​=⟨f,ϕi​⟩w​.
(6)
The Gram matrix usually couples the unknown coefficients. With ϕj​=pj​, it becomes Gij​=hi​δij​, leaving the separate equations hi​ai​=bi​. For the monomial basis on [−1,1] with w=1, by contrast, Gij​ is zero when i+j is odd and 2/(i+j+1) when i+j is even. Distinct even powers, or distinct odd powers, remain coupled.
There is also a proof of the minimum that uses no differentiation. The defining coefficients give ⟨f−qm​,pn​⟩w​=0 for 0≤n≤m. Thus f−qm​ is orthogonal to all of Vm​. For every v∈Vm​, the Pythagorean theorem in an inner-product space gives
∥f−v∥w2​=∥f−qm​∥w2​+∥qm​−v∥w2​.
(7)
The second term is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when v=qm​, proving that the least-squares solution is unique. This is an integrated squared-error optimum; it does not assert a smallest error at every point or a smallest uniform norm.
For N>m, the nested orthogonal projections satisfy the exact residual budget
∥f−qm​∥w2​=∥f−qN​∥w2​+∑n=m+1N​cn2​hn​.
(8)
Indeed, qN​−qm​ is a linear combination of mutually orthogonal polynomials, and f−qN​ is orthogonal to it. Every newly retained component removes exactly cn2​hn​ from the squared error. No infinite expansion or completeness assumption is needed for this finite identity. The Legendre least-squares projections of a cubic make every term explicit.

Legendre least-squares projections of a cubic (f(x)=1+x+x2+x3)

Take the polynomial f(x)=1+x+x2+x3 on [−1,1] with unit weight. Using the four Legendre polynomials above, direct integration gives
n0123​hn​22/32/52/7​⟨f,Pn​⟩8/316/154/154/35​cn​4/38/52/32/5​cn2​hn​32/9128/758/458/175​​
(9)
For example, ⟨f,P2​⟩=21​∫−11​(3x4+2x2−1)dx=4/15: the omitted odd terms have zero integral. Dividing by h2​=2/5 gives c2​=2/3. Substituting all four coefficients proves the exact identity
f=34​P0​+58​P1​+32​P2​+52​P3​.
(10)
The successive orthogonal projections onto degrees at most m are therefore
m0123​qm​(x)4/34/3+8x/51+8x/5+x21+x+x2+x3​Em​=∥f−qm​∥22​608/315352/15758/1750​​
(11)
Here ∥g∥22​=∫−11​g(x)2dx is the squared L2 norm, without division by the interval length. Each row retains the previous Legendre polynomial coefficients and appends one new term. The ordinary power coefficients can change: adding 32​P2​ changes the constant from 4/3 to 1, and adding 52​P3​ changes the linear coefficient from 8/5 to 1. Independence belongs to the chosen orthogonal polynomial coordinates.
The full squared L2 norm is ∥f∥22​=192/35, equal to 32/9+128/75+8/45+8/175. The residual budgets are the omitted terms:
E0​=75128​+458​+1758​=315608​,E1​=458​+1758​=1575352​,E2​=1758​,E3​=0.
(12)
For a direct check independent of summing the budgets, f−q2​=x3−3x/5, and integrating its square gives 2/7−12/25+6/25=8/175. This residual is orthogonal to 1,x,x2, exactly the conditions for the best quadratic least-squares solution. An orthogonal projection need not be a polynomial interpolant: for example, q2​(1)=18/5 while f(1)=4. The approximation allocates error across the interval according to the chosen weighted inner product.

Kravchuk polynomials (Ks​(t;n,q)=∑j=0s​(−1)j(q−1)s−j(jt​)(s−jn−t​))

Words: 75
Orthogonal polynomial OurBigBook.com
These polynomials are orthogonal for a binomial distribution weight and encode the coefficient transformation in the MacWilliams identity. Their generating function follows by expanding the two factors:
∑s=0n​Ks​(t;n,q)zs=(1−z)t(1+(q−1)z)n−t.
(13)
For binary linear codes, q=2. Expanding the factors gives Ks​(t;n,2)=∑j​(−1)j(jt​)(s−jn−t​), where binomial coefficients outside their usual integer range are zero. Orthogonality follows by multiplying generating functions and summing over t with weight (tn​)(q−1)t: the result is qn(1+(q−1)zw)n, whose coefficient of zrws is zero for r=s and qn(q−1)r(rn​) for r=s.

Orthogonality forces many interior zeros

Words: 94
Orthogonal polynomial OurBigBook.com
If a nonzero continuous real function on an interval is orthogonal to every polynomial of degree below n, it has at least n distinct interior zeros. If it had only finitely many zeros and fewer than n sign changes, multiply it by the product of linear factors at its sign-changing zeros. The product has constant sign and is nonzero on an open interval, so its integral cannot vanish. Orthogonality to this degree-below-n polynomial contradicts that conclusion. Infinitely many interior zeros already satisfy the assertion. Orthogonal polynomials of respective degrees 0,…,n−1 form a basis of the needed polynomial space.

Zernike polynomials (Znm​(ρ,ϕ))

Words: 88 Articles: 1
Orthogonal polynomial OurBigBook.com
The real Zernike polynomials form an orthogonal polynomial basis on the unit Euclidean disk, with radial factor Rnm​(ρ) and angular factor cosmϕ or sinmϕ. Here 0≤m≤n and n−m is even. They decompose wavefront errors into modes useful for optical correction. Normalizations differ, so coefficients should specify the chosen convention.

Hermite polynomial derivative recurrence (Hn′​=2nHn−1​)

Words: 54
Hermite polynomial OurBigBook.com
For the physicists' Hermite polynomials Hn​=(−1)nex2Dne−x2, the displayed recurrence holds for n≥1, while H0′​=0 separately. Put u=e−x2. Differentiating u′+2xu=0 exactly n times using the Leibniz rule yields
(D+2x)Dnu=−2nDn−1u.
(16)
Differentiating the defining product for Hn​ then gives
Hn′​=(−1)nex2(D+2x)Dnu=2n(−1)n−1ex2Dn−1u=2nHn−1​.
(17)
The nonnegative integer convention is essential for the derivative-based definition; no negative-order derivative or H−1​ is needed.

Hermite function (Hn​(x)=π1/42nn!​e−x2/2​(−1)nex2dxndn​e−x2)

Words: 208 Articles: 2
Hermite polynomial OurBigBook.com
The normalized Hermite functions multiply the physicists' Hermite polynomials by a Gaussian. They form an orthonormal basis of L2(R) and satisfy (−d2/dx2+x2)Hn​=(2n+1)Hn​. With a=(x+d/dx)/2​ and a∗=(x−d/dx)/2​, they are (a∗)nH0​/n!​ and the quantum harmonic oscillator is 2a∗a+1. For completeness, if u is orthogonal to all of them, all moments of u(x)e−x2/2 vanish. Its transform ∫u(x)e−x2/2ezxdx is entire by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, has every derivative zero at zero, and hence vanishes identically. Uniqueness of the Fourier transform gives u=0.

Hermite functions are Fourier eigenfunctions (F(Hn​(x)e−x2/2)=(−i)nHn​(x)e−x2/2)

Words: 74
Hermite function OurBigBook.com
Use the unitary Fourier transform with phase e−iλx. It commutes with −d2/dx2+x2, because differentiation transforms to multiplication by iλ and multiplication by x transforms to id/dλ. The Hermite differential equation has a unique polynomial solution of each degree up to scale, and multiplying by the Gaussian gives a Hermite function. Transforming a polynomial times this Gaussian again gives a polynomial times the Gaussian of the same degree; comparison of leading coefficients gives the eigenvalue (−i)n.

Mehler formula for Hermite functions (∑n≥0​tnHn​(x)Hn​(y)=π(1−t2)​exp(2(1−t2)4xyt−(1+t2)(x2+y2)​)​)

Words: 62
Hermite function OurBigBook.com
For ∣t∣<1, this identity sums the Hermite function expansion into a Gaussian. One proof takes the Bargmann-Fock space inner product of the generating functions π−1/4exp(−x2/2+2​xz−z2/2) at t​z. Writing z=u+iv, the two resulting real Gaussian integrals have quadratic coefficients 1+t and 1−t and linear coefficients 2t​(x+y) and i2t​(x−y). Evaluation gives the formula. Setting t=e−2s and multiplying by e−s gives the harmonic oscillator transition kernel of e−s(−d2/dx2+x2).
