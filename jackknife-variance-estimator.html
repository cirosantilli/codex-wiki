Jackknife variance estimator (VJ​)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical inference Jackknife resampling
OurBigBook.com Words: 325 Articles: 1
The jackknife variance estimator estimates the sampling variance of the full-data estimator, using the deletion estimates and their mean from jackknife resampling:
VJ​=nn−1​∑i=1n​(θ(−i)​−θJ​)2,SEJ​=VJ​​.
(3)
The square root is the estimated standard error. The factor n−1 multiplies the divisor-n variance of the deletion estimates: those estimates vary less than estimates from new independent experiments because almost all their observations are shared. Ryan Tibshirani's [ref] give the same scaling in terms of the divisor-(n−1) sample variance of the deletion estimates.
For the sample mean, this formula has an exact algebraic simplification. With sn2​ denoting the usual sample variance,
θ(−i)​=n−1nx−xi​​,θJ​=x,θ(−i)​−θJ​=−n−1xi​−x​,
(4)
and therefore
VJ​=n(n−1)1​∑i=1n​(xi​−x)2=nsn2​​.
(5)
Under the independent and identically distributed assumption and a finite second moment, sn2​ is an unbiased estimator of the population variance σ2. Consequently VJ​ is an unbiased estimator of the sample mean's sampling variance σ2/n. The equality VJ​=sn2​/n itself holds for any observed data with n>1; its sampling interpretation uses the stated assumptions.
For (1,2,3,4,10), the sum of squared deviations from the sample mean is 50, giving sample variance s52​=50/4=12.5. The deletion sample means have squared deviations totaling 3.125, so
VJ​=54​(3.125)=2.5=512.5​,SEJ​=2.5​≈1.58114.
(6)
Here 12.5 estimates the variance of individual observations, while 2.5 estimates the sampling variance of their sample mean. These answer different questions.
For more general statistics, validity requires regularity beyond the deletion formula, such as a sufficiently smooth function of sample means with suitable moments. Ordinary delete-one jackknife resampling can fail for nonsmooth quantiles, including the median; see Larry Wasserman's [ref]. With statistical dependence, deleting individual observations need not reproduce the relevant sampling variance; the deletion scheme must reflect that statistical dependence. Shalizi's [ref] distinguish the basic independent and identically distributed case from extensions.
