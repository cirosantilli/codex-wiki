Quadratic profiles for nonlinear diffusion with constant loss (ht​=(hhx​)x​−1)

For the displayed nonlinear diffusion equation, a quadratic ansatz h=A(t)+B(t)x2 reduces the partial differential equation exactly to
B′=6B2,A′=2AB−1.
(57)
Indeed (hhx​)x​=2AB+6B2x2, and equality of the constant and quadratic coefficients proves the reduction. For nonzero curvature, integration gives B=−1/[6(t−t0​)]. On τ=t−t0​>0, the remaining linear ordinary differential equation is A′+A/(3τ)=−1, whose integrating factor is τ1/3. Thus, including spatial translation,
h(x,t)=Cτ−1/3−6τ(x−x0​)2​−43​τ.
(58)
All three constants C,x0​,t0​ are arbitrary, on a domain with τ>0. When the quadratic coefficient vanishes, the more general affine ansatz gives h=px+(p2−1)t+q.
A narrower ansatz h=at−1/3(1−x2/t2/3)−bt fixes two coefficients together and permits exactly (a,b)=(1/6,3/4) or (0,1). The zero value of a is a valid spatially constant branch, not an algebraic exception to discard. These finite-parameter families are exact solutions; they are not the complete general solution of the nonlinear partial differential equation, and their sums need not be solutions.
