Blackbody peak and detector response

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Radiative transfer Planck function Wien's displacement law
OurBigBook.com Words: 299
Consider a hypothetical star whose emitted specific intensity is exactly that of a uniform 5000K blackbody. Assume transparent propagation with negligible Doppler shift and interstellar extinction, so geometric dilution multiplies every spectral interval by the same factor. The stipulated temperature gives
λmax​=0.579554391μm,νmax​=293.946288THz,c/νmax​=1.01988857μm.
(35)
Using the converted frequency peak as though it maximized Bλ​ would return b/(c/νmax​)≃2841.26K, a substantial error caused solely by mixing spectral conventions.
Now stipulate a photodetector with constant quantum efficiency η over the relevant wavelengths, collecting equal narrow wavelength bins. For fixed accepted projected area times solid angle G, with dilution included in G, its expected photon count rate density is
dλdN˙​=ηGhcλBλ​​.
(36)
This follows by dividing the received power by the photon energy hc/λ. It peaks where x=4(1−e−x), at x=3.9206903949…, giving 0.73394057μm for the same model star. Constant quantum efficiency is not constant energy spectral responsivity: the latter includes the wavelength-dependent photon energy conversion. For example, G=10−12m2sr, η=0.5 and a 1nm bin centered at 0.600μm give approximately 1.93×1010 detected photons per second in the narrow-bin approximation. These are calculated model values, not observations of a named star.
A more general linear detector channel has output
Si​−Si,0​=G∫0∞​Ri​(λ)Bλ​(T)dλ,
(37)
where Si,0​ is the background reading and Ri​ includes spectral selection, optical transmission and detector spectral responsivity. A raw channel maximum need not locate either Planck function maximum. Recovering a temperature requires an appropriate calibrated response and an emission model. For a real stellar atmosphere, opacity and radiative transfer can reshape the spectrum; interstellar extinction can reshape it again. A single peak does not establish a unique physical temperature without those assumptions.
