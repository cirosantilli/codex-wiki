Supersaturation

Words: 104 Articles: 1
Extremal graph theory OurBigBook.com
Supersaturation strengthens an extremal existence theorem by forcing many copies of a forbidden graph when the edge count exceeds its extremal threshold by a fixed density. For a fixed clique, the Erdős-Stone theorem and averaging over bounded-size random vertex subsets give a positive multiple of n∣V(F)∣ copies.

Clique supersaturation by sampling (e(G)≥(1−1/r+ϵ)(2n​)⟹kr+1​(G)≥ηnr+1)

Words: 59
Supersaturation OurBigBook.com
Choose a fixed sample size m for which the normalized Turan theorem bound lies below the given density by a positive margin. The expected edge count in a random m-vertex subset then forces a positive proportion of subsets to contain a forbidden-size clique. Each clique belongs to a known number of subsets, so double counting gives a positive constant times nr+1 cliques.

Extremal graph for disjoint cliques

For fixed positive integers r,s and sufficiently large n, the unique extremal graph forbidding s vertex-disjoint copies of the complete graph Kr+1​ is the join of graphs Ks−1​+Tr​(n−s+1), up to isomorphic graphs. Each forbidden clique would require a distinct vertex of Ks−1​.

Mantel theorem (ex(n,K3​)=⌊n2/4⌋)

Words: 442 Articles: 1
Extremal number OurBigBook.com
The complete bipartite graph K3,3​ has vertex sets A={a1​,a2​,a3​} and B={b1​,b2​,b3​}, with every edge between A and B and none inside either part. Its six vertices support 3⋅3=9 edges and no triangles in a graph: among any three vertices, two belong to the same independent set and have no joining edge.
Add the single edge a1​a2​. Each bj​ is a common neighbour of its endpoints, producing the triangle in a graph a1​a2​bj​. These are exactly the three new triangles in a graph, because every triangle in a graph must use the only edge inside a part. The new count is ten edges; nine was the last possible count without a triangle in a graph on six vertices.
In general, every simple graph with a finite set of n≥0 vertices that is triangle-free has at most
m≤⌊4n2​⌋
(3)
edges. This is the r=2 case of Turán's theorem, with forbidden clique K3​. Equality holds exactly for the balanced complete bipartite graph K⌊n/2⌋,⌈n/2⌉​, up to graph isomorphism; empty parts are allowed for n<2.[ref]
The floor function gives the exact integer bound. Splitting n vertices into two independent sets of sizes a and n−a, and putting in every cross edge, gives
a(n−a)=4n2​−(a−2n​)2.
(4)
The count is largest when a is an integer nearest n/2. For n=2k it is k2; for n=2k+1 it is k(k+1). Both equal ⌊n2/4⌋. This proves attainability for every n, including the empty graph.
A short proof of the upper bound uses degrees of vertices. In a triangle-free graph, the endpoints u,v of an edge have disjoint vertex neighbourhoods: a common neighbour would complete a triangle in a graph. Thus d(u)+d(v)≤n. Summing over edges counts d(v) once for each of the d(v) edges incident to v, giving
∑v∈V​d(v)2=∑uv∈E​(d(u)+d(v))≤nm.
(5)
For n>0, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and handshaking lemma yield
4m2=(∑v∈V​d(v))2≤n∑v∈V​d(v)2≤n2m.
(6)
If m>0, division gives m≤n2/4; m=0 is immediate. Since m is an integer, apply the floor function. The case n=0 is immediate too. This proves the bound; the full equality characterization follows from Turán's theorem.
The useful reversal is that more than ⌊n2/4⌋ edges force a triangle in a graph, whatever their placement. A global count certifies a local pattern. The extremizer is bipartite, but a triangle-free graph need not be bipartite: a five-vertex graph cycle has five edges, below the bound of six. Historically, Willem Mantel posed the triangle-exclusion question in 1907.[ref]

Triangle lower bound one edge above the Mantel threshold

Words: 38
Mantel theorem OurBigBook.com
Every graph on 2n vertices with at least n2+1 edges contains at least n triangles. An induction deletes the endpoints of an edge lying in no triangle; if every edge lies in a triangle, the edge-triangle incidence bound is already sufficient.

Turán's theorem (ex(n,Kr+1​)=tr​(n))

Words: 958 Articles: 9
Extremal graph theory OurBigBook.com
How many edges can we retain while excluding a prescribed clique? For integers r≥1 and n≥0, every simple graph on n vertices containing no complete subgraph Kr+1​ satisfies
e(G)≤tr​(n):=e(Tr​(n)).
(7)
Equality holds exactly for the Turán graph Tr​(n), up to graph isomorphism. Thus the extremal number is ex(n,Kr+1​)=tr​(n).[ref]
For example, T3​(8) has independent sets of sizes 3,3,2 and 21 edges, as counted in Turán graph. It contains triangles in a graph, but no four-vertex clique. Consequently any simple graph on eight vertices with 22 edges contains K4​, while 21 edges alone do not force one. Excluding larger cliques permits denser graphs; Mantel theorem is precisely the first nontrivial case.
A short proof outline uses Zykov symmetrization. If an extremizer is not a complete multipartite graph, it has vertices x,y,z with xy,xz absent and yz present. If either y or z has larger vertex degree than x, clone that vertex in place of x. Otherwise replace both y,z by nonadjacent clones of x, gaining 2d(x)−d(y)−d(z)+1>0 edges. Both contradict extremality; balancing the resulting at most r parts gives Tr​(n).[ref]
The vertex cloning in a graph step preserves clique exclusion because any clique using a clone can substitute its original vertex; nonadjacent clones cannot occur together in a clique. At most r parts can be nonempty, since selecting one vertex from each gives a clique. If fewer than r parts are nonempty and one has at least two vertices, splitting it creates more edges, so it cannot be extremal. For r=1 the bound is zero. For r≥n, the Turán graph is Kn​, and the bound is the total (2n​) possible edges.

Edit-distance stability for clique-free graphs (e(G)≥tr​(n)−k⟹∣E(G)△E(H)∣≤3k)

Words: 115 Articles: 1
Turán's theorem OurBigBook.com
A clique-free graph near the Turan theorem edge bound is close to a complete multipartite graph. Choose a maximum-degree vertex, split its neighbourhood from the remaining vertices, and apply induction inside the neighbourhood. The degree bound implies that missing cross edges are at least twice the number of edges inside the remaining class. This inequality controls all three types of edits and gives the constant three.

Bipartite stability from few odd cycles (∣E(G)△E(H)∣≤11ϵ(2n​))

Words: 55
Edit-distance stability for clique-free graphs OurBigBook.com
Fix an odd cycle length. If the graph has edge density at least 1/2−ϵ and sufficiently few copies of that cycle, odd-cycle copies from positive triangle density forces few triangles in a graph. The triangle removal lemma deletes at most ϵ(2n​) edges, after which the clique-free edit bound applies with two parts. Absorbing its linear rounding error into the density margin gives the displayed bound for large order.

Turán graph (Tr​(n))

Words: 355 Articles: 1
Turán's theorem OurBigBook.com
The Turán graph Tr​(n) is a complete multipartite graph with r independent sets of sizes differing by at most one, with every cross edge present. Empty parts are allowed when r>n.[ref]
In this example, the independent sets are A={a1​,a2​,a3​}, B={b1​,b2​,b3​} and C={c1​,c2​}. Count the edges between each pair of parts:
t3​(8)=3⋅3+3⋅2+3⋅2=9+6+6=21.
(8)
Any four vertices put at least two in the same independent set, by the pigeonhole principle, so they cannot form K4​. Conversely, every choice of one vertex from each part is a triangle in a graph, giving 3⋅3⋅2=18 triangles in a graph. Clique exclusion here does not mean triangle-free.
For the exact count, Euclidean division writes n=rq+s, where q=⌊n/r⌋ uses the floor function and s=nmodr is the remainder, with 0≤s<r. There are s parts of size q+1 and r−s of size q. For any part sizes a1​,…,ar​ summing to n, count cross edges by expanding the square:
e(Ka1​,…,ar​​)=∑i<j​ai​aj​=21​(n2−∑i=1r​ai2​).
(9)
Therefore
tr​(n)=21​[n2−s(q+1)2−(r−s)q2]=(1−r1​)2n2​−2rs(r−s)​.
(10)
For n=8,r=3, we have q=2,s=2, giving (64−2⋅9−1⋅4)/2=21. The Quadratic Turan edge bound gives 64/3 instead: the exact correction subtracts 1/3.
Balancing is an integer optimization with a simple reason. If two part sizes satisfy a≥b+2, moving one vertex from the larger part to the smaller decreases their contribution to the sum of squares by
a2+b2−(a−1)2−(b+1)2=2(a−b−1)>0,
(11)
so it increases the edge count by a−b−1. For eight vertices, parts 4,2,2 give 20 edges; moving one vertex produces 3,3,2 and 21 edges. The most symmetric permitted sizes pack the most cross edges, which explains the sharp construction in Turán's theorem.

Triangle support line between bipartite and tripartite Turan graphs

Words: 92
Turán graph OurBigBook.com
For n≥3, put ei​=e(Ti​(n)), t3​=k3​(T3​(n)), and cn​=(e3​−e2​)/t3​. Every graph on n vertices satisfies
e(G)−cn​k3​(G)≤e2​.
(12)
Thus an edge count e2​+θ(e3​−e2​) forces at least θt3​ triangles in a graph. This is an exact finite bound, with the rounding in the Turán graph retained. To prove it, use edge-triangle symmetrization. The inequality cn​≥2/n allows merging the two smallest classes when there are at least four classes. With three classes, fixing the middle class makes the objective affine in the product of the other two sizes; either merge them or balance them. Only T2​(n) and T3​(n) need remain.

Zykov symmetrization

Words: 116 Articles: 1
Turán's theorem OurBigBook.com
Zykov symmetrization replaces one of two nonadjacent vertices by a clone of the other. Cloning the vertex of larger degree does not decrease the edge count and does not create a larger clique. Iteration reduces the extremal problem for clique-free graphs to complete multipartite graphs.

Edge-triangle symmetrization

Words: 71
Zykov symmetrization OurBigBook.com
For each real c, some maximizer of e(G)−ck3​(G) among graphs on n vertices is complete multipartite graph. Among maximizers maximize ∑v​d(v)2. Cloning either of two nonadjacent vertices preserves the objective, since maximality forces their local contributions d(v)−ce(G[N(v)]) to agree. If their vertex neighbourhoods differ, the sum of the two changes in the squared-degree of a vertex objective is twice the size of their symmetric difference, a contradiction. Nonadjacency therefore partitions the vertices into classes with identical vertex neighbourhoods.

Rhombus-free edge bound

Words: 92 Articles: 1
Turán's theorem OurBigBook.com
A graph-theoretic rhombus is two triangles sharing an edge. Every rhombus-free graph on n≥4 vertices has at most
e(T2​(n))=⌊4n2​⌋
(14)
edges. If the graph is triangle-free this is Turán's theorem. Otherwise remove a triangle: every remaining vertex has at most one neighbor in it, and induction bounds the edge count by
⌊(n−3)2/4⌋+n≤⌊n2/4⌋.

Logarithmic Erdős-Stone theorem (t≥⌊d(r,ϵ)logn⌋)

Words: 165 Articles: 2
Erdős-Stone theorem OurBigBook.com
An edge density exceeding the Turan theorem threshold for Kr+1​ by a fixed positive amount forces a balanced Kr+1​(t) subgraph with logarithmic part size. Combine clique supersaturation by sampling with the dense clique family blow-up lemma. Dense binomial random graphs show that the logarithmic order is optimal for a uniform density guarantee.

Logarithmic clique blow-up from minimum degree (δ(G)≥(1−1/r+ε)n⟹Kr+1​(⌊dlogn⌋)⊆G)

Words: 74
Logarithmic Erdős-Stone theorem OurBigBook.com
A fixed positive excess over the Turan theorem threshold forces logarithmic balanced complete multipartite graphs. There is an elementary induction using common neighbourhood from bipartite density: first obtain a logarithmic r-partite subgraph from the induction hypothesis, and select disjoint transversal r-cliques in it. Each such clique has at least rεn common neighbours, by the union bound on non-neighbour sets. Apply the bipartite common-neighbour count to these cliques as left-side objects. Their selected union and a large common neighbourhood form the extra part.

Random obstruction to larger clique blow-ups (E#Ks​(t)≤nstp(2s​)t2)

In a binomial random graph with fixed edge probability below one, the displayed first-moment bound tends to zero for t=Clogn and sufficiently large fixed C. At the same time the edge density concentrates near p. Thus a positive fixed density cannot force balanced complete multipartite subgraphs whose part size grows faster than logarithmically.

High-minimum-degree multipartite stability subgraph

Words: 179 Articles: 1
Erdős-Stone theorem OurBigBook.com
For fixed positive integers r,t, a graph G on n vertices with e(G)=(1−1/r+o(1))n2/2 and no balanced complete multipartite blow-up Kr+1​(t) contains an r-partite subgraph H with n−o(n) vertices, balanced classes of size n/r+o(n), and minimum degree of a graph δ(H)=(1−1/r+o(1))n.
One proof first removes a vanishing fraction of vertices of low degree of a vertex, finds a slowly growing Kr​(L) by the Erdős-Stone theorem, and assigns almost every remaining vertex to a root class in which it has fewer than t neighbours of a vertex. After discarding a further vanishing fraction, a Kt,t​ inside an assigned class would extend to Kr+1​(t) using the root classes. Thus internal edges and missing cross-class edges both number o(n2). Removing the vertices with unusually many missing cross-class edges gives the stated minimum degree of a graph.

Minimum degree of an extremal forbidden-subgraph graph

Words: 71
High-minimum-degree multipartite stability subgraph OurBigBook.com
If a fixed graph F has chromatic number r+1, every extremal F-free graph G on n vertices has minimum degree of a graph (1−1/r+o(1))n. Use a high-minimum-degree multipartite stability subgraph H: replacing any vertex by one adjacent to all but one class of H preserves F-freeness. Any supposed new copy of F can replace the new vertex by an unused common neighbour in the omitted class. Extremality therefore gives the lower bound on every degree of a vertex; the Erdős-Stone theorem gives the upper bound on the average degree of a vertex.
