ELODIE spectrograph

OurBigBook.com Words: 268
Built by André Baranne, Michel Mayor, Didier Queloz and colleagues, the ELODIE fiber-fed spectrograph carried light from Haute-Provence's 1.93-metre telescope through optical fibers. Its echelle grating dispersed light in high diffraction orders; an optical prism and grism cross-disperser separated them perpendicularly on a charge-coupled device. This cross-dispersed echelle spectrograph achieved spectral resolving power R=42000. A simultaneous thorium reference tracked instrumental drift. Wavelength calibration mapped detector positions to wavelengths; spectral cross-correlation combined many spectral lines into a fitted profile. A barycentric velocity correction removed the observer's motion.[ref]
Why can a spectrograph measure a Doppler shift much smaller than one spectral resolution element? Resolution concerns separating neighbouring features, whereas locating the centre of a known profile uses information across that profile and across many spectral lines. For a chosen example, at λ=500nm a recession velocity of 60ms−1 gives
Δλ≃λcvr​​=1.00069×10−4nm.
(22)
At R=42000, one spectral resolution element corresponds to c/R≃7.14kms−1, about 119 times larger. A well measured centre can move by a small fraction of that width. More photons and more useful spectral lines improve the estimate, but do not remove wavelength calibration errors or stellar activity.
A simple mathematical picture of spectral cross-correlation makes the readout concrete. Define x=clog(λ/λ0​), with fixed reference wavelength λ0​, and let d(x) be continuum-normalized line absorption. For small velocities, a common Doppler shift approximately translates d(x). A positive absorption mask m(x) then gives
C(u)=∫d(x)m(x−u)dx.
(23)
If d(x)=m(x−vr​), the maximum occurs at u=vr​; a convention correlating the received observed radiative flux instead produces a dip. A fitted correlation centre is an effective collective line position, not a claim that each individual spectral line has been resolved into a narrower feature. The lamp tracks instrumental drift, while the barycentric velocity correction removes the observer's changing line-of-sight velocity; neither removes changes in the star itself.
