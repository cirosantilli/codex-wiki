Cayley transform between the half-plane and disk (C(z)=(z−i)/(z+i))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 301
The Cayley transform gives a conformal bijection between an unbounded half-plane and the unit disc. Two conventions are useful. For the right half-plane, use
R(z)=z+1z−1​,R−1(w)=1−w1+w​,1−∣R(z)∣2=∣z+1∣24Rez​.
(9)
For the upper half-plane, use
C(z)=z+iz−i​,C−1(w)=i1−w1+w​,1−∣C(z)∣2=∣z+i∣24Imz​.
(10)
Rotating the upper half-plane onto the right half-plane by z↦−iz explains the relationship C(z)=R(−iz). The factors of i distinguish which half-plane is mapped onto the unit disc. [ref]
In particular, C(i)=0, C(2i)=1/3 and C(1+i)=(1−2i)/5. The whole positive imaginary axis becomes the real diameter: C(iy)=(y−1)/(y+1) for y>0. Horizontal grid lines become circles tangent to the unit circle at 1 when completed by their endpoint at infinity; they are not hyperbolic geodesics.
On the Riemann sphere, C maps the extended real line onto the unit circle:
C(−1)=i,C(0)=−1,C(1)=−i,C(∞)=1.
(11)
Every finite real x has ∣C(x)∣=1; the remaining point w=1 corresponds to infinity. The pole z=−i is outside the upper half-plane, and the pole of C−1 at w=1 is on the boundary of the unit disc. Neither pole obstructs the interior conformal bijection.
For a short verification, subtract the squared moduli:
∣z+i∣2−∣z−i∣2=4Imz,ImC−1(w)=∣1−w∣21−∣w∣2​.
(12)
These identities put each interior image in the required domain; solving w(z+i)=z−i gives the stated inverse function. Both maps are holomorphic, with C′(z)=2i/(z+i)2=0, so the conformal bijection preserves Euclidean angles between tangent directions.
Euclidean distance is not preserved: i and 2i are one unit apart, whereas 0 and 1/3 are only one third of a unit apart. The striking feature is that C does preserve length for the hyperbolic metrics of the Poincaré half-plane model and Poincaré disk model:
dsH​=Imz∣dz∣​,dsD​=1−∣w∣22∣dw∣​,1−∣C(z)∣22∣C′(z)∣∣dz∣​=Imz∣dz∣​.
(13)
Consequently it is an isometry for these hyperbolic metrics, including their induced hyperbolic distances. For the same example, the corresponding hyperbolic geodesic segments have equal length:
∫12​ydy​=log2=∫01/3​1−r22dr​.
(14)
Thus the Cayley transform lets us choose the most convenient picture: a straight boundary for calculations, or a bounded unit disc for visualization. It also transports automorphisms of the unit disk into upper half-plane automorphisms by function composition C−1∘g∘C.
