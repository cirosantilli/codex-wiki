Navier slip boundary condition (urel​=b∂n​u)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
The Navier slip boundary condition allows a fluid to slide tangentially past a solid wall while resisting that motion through wall friction in the Navier slip boundary condition. For parallel flow past a flat, impermeable wall translating uniformly with tangential velocity Uw​, write the fluid's tangential velocity as u and its relative tangential velocity as urel​=u−Uw​. Then
urel​=b∂n​u,b≥0,
(23)
where b is the slip length, also written ℓs​. The unit normal n points from the wall into the fluid, and ∂n​u=n⋅∇u is the normal derivative. The separate no-penetration boundary condition requires (u−Uw​)⋅n=0: tangential sliding does not allow flow through the wall.
The sign convention matters. In a channel 0<z<H, the lower wall has inward unit normal +ez​ and the upper wall has inward unit normal −ez​. If both walls obey the Navier slip boundary condition, their tangential velocities satisfy
u(0)−U0​=b0​u′(0),u(H)−UH​=−bH​u′(H).
(24)
Here the prime means the derivative with respect to z. Using +bH​u′(H) at the upper wall would reverse the friction law. The examples Couette flow with a slipping lower wall and planar Poiseuille flow with equal wall slip use different upper-wall boundary conditions.
This flat-wall formula is a local linear model. On a curved wall the Newtonian fluid stress tensor must supply the tangential traction; the normal derivative alone generally misses curvature terms. The author-written review by Lauga, Brenner and Stone states the full strain-based condition with the unit normal directed into the liquid. [ref]
The same review credits Navier's 1823 treatise with the linear slip law. Its history is a reminder that the no-slip boundary condition is an experimentally successful model, whose validity depends on the interface and scale. The history here follows that review rather than a new reading of Navier's original memoir. [ref]
