Avogadro constant from sedimentation and diffusion (NAsed​=RTb/(mb​g),NAdiff​=RT/(6πηaD))

For two observed layers z2​>z1​, define the positive logarithmic count slope
b=z2​−z1​ln[C(z1​)/C(z2​)]​=H1​.
(11)
Since kB​=R/NA​, where R is the molar gas constant, the sedimentation scale height gives
NAsed​=mb​gRTb​=mb​gHRT​.
(12)
Several heights check whether one slope fits the whole profile. A mixture of grain radii generally gives a sum of exponential functions, with the larger grains depleted faster aloft; one apparent radius need not describe every height.
An independent readout uses horizontal displacements at lag τ, avoiding the vertical gravitational drift. Define the one-coordinate mean squared displacement Mx​(τ)=⟨[x(t+τ)−x(t)]2⟩. In a stationary liquid without horizontal advection, translational diffusion predicts
Mx​(τ)=2Dτ,⟨(Δx)2+(Δy)2⟩=4Dτ.
(13)
A camera sees two horizontal coordinates, so its squared radial displacement has twice the one-coordinate mean. Neither observable is path length or instantaneous velocity. The bulk Stokes–Einstein relation, with dynamic viscosity η, then gives
D=6πηakB​T​,NAdiff​=6πηaDRT​.
(14)
The 1926 lecture compares displacement-based and equilibrium determinations of the Avogadro constant.[ref]
Rotational diffusion supplies a further observable when an identifiable grain orientation can be followed; centre positions alone cannot measure it.
For the chosen model above and η=1.00×10−3Pas, the bulk prediction is D≃0.8731μm2s−1. At τ=10s, Mx​≃17.46μm2 and the planar value is 34.92μm2. Reconstructing NA​ from these stipulated model outputs returns 6.02214076×1023mol−1 by construction; this arithmetic consistency check is not experimental confirmation. Historical force comes from comparing separately measured profiles and displacements using independently checked material parameters.
The diffusive flux and gravitational particle flux provide another useful connection. With constant drag coefficient ζ, zero vertical net flux reads
jz​=−Ddzdn​−ζmb​g​n=0,H=mb​gDζ​=vs​D​,vs​=ζmb​g​,
(15)
where vs​ is the downward settling velocity magnitude. With ζ=6πηa, the chosen model gives vs​≃0.02725μms−1. The equilibrium profile forgets how rapidly it was established, while displacement measurements retain that rate information.
