Rapidity (θ=artanhv)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity Lorentz transformation Lorentz boost
OurBigBook.com Words: 529 Articles: 1
Rapidity is a dimensionless parameter for a signed collinear velocity u with ∣u∣<c, defined using the inverse hyperbolic functions by
θ=artanh(u/c)=21​ln1−u/c1+u/c​,u=ctanhθ.
(16)
Here c is the speed of light, and tanh is the hyperbolic tangent. Zero velocity has zero rapidity; negative velocity has negative rapidity. Every finite real rapidity gives ∣u∣<c, while u→±c requires θ→±∞. Thus a light ray has no finite rapidity in this parametrization. The header's formula uses natural units c=1: its v is the numerical velocity u/c.
For the inertial frames in the velocity-addition formula, S′ moves at signed velocity V along the x-axis of S. Set ϕ=artanh(V/c). Re-expressing the same particle in the moving inertial frame gives
θ′=θ−ϕ,θ=θ′+ϕ.
(17)
The subtraction is a passive change of reference frame; the inverse adds the particle's rapidity in S′ to the frame's rapidity relative to S. For example, V=0.6c and u′=0.6c give ϕ=θ′=ln2, so θ=ln4 and u=ctanh(ln4)=15c/17, exactly as in the velocity-addition formula.
For a massive particle of rest mass m>0 moving in one spatial dimension, its energy and signed momentum are
E=mc2coshθ,p=mcsinhθ.
(18)
The hyperbolic cosine is the particle's Lorentz factor, and the hyperbolic sine is γu/c. The hyperbolic Pythagorean identity immediately gives the relativistic energy-momentum relation E2−c2p2=m2c4. In the older c=1 notation, writing the rest mass as M recovers v=tanhθ, E=Mcoshθ, and p=Msinhθ. These expressions use natural units in which the factors of c are numerically one; the preceding formulas restore those factors explicitly. [ref].
The useful simplification follows from the hyperbolic-function identity tanh(a+b)=(tanha+tanhb)/(1+tanhatanhb): the nonlinear velocity-addition formula becomes ordinary addition of rapidities. See iterated collinear boosts with equal rapidity for a plotted example. A common collinear Lorentz boost shifts every rapidity by the same constant, so a rapidity difference is invariant. This makes rapidity useful for relativistic factorized scattering in one spatial dimension, where two-body S-matrices depend on the difference of the incoming rapidities, with particle species fixed.
