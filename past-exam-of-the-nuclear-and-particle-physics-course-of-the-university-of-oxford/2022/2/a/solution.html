In Fermi's golden rule,
Γ=ℏ2π​∣M∣2ρ,
(15)
Γ is a transition decay rate, measured in inverse time; ℏ is the reduced Planck constant; M=⟨f∣Hint​∣i⟩ is the transition matrix element of the interaction part of the Hamiltonian; and ρ is the final density of states per unit energy, evaluated subject to conservation of energy. Final spin and other discrete labels must be summed, with initial labels averaged when the preparation requires it. If M varies across the final phase space, ∣M∣2ρ denotes the corresponding integral rather than a product of constants.
A convention warning: many quantum field theory texts use Γ for an energy decay width. That convention is Γwidth​=ℏΓrate​; the formula as printed uses the rate convention.
