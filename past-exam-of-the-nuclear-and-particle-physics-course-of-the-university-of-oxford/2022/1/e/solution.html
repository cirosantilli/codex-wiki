Normalize the incident intensity to one. Since ∣K0⟩=(∣KS​⟩+∣KL​⟩)/2​, the initial probability amplitudes are AS​(0)=AL​(0)=1/2​. Define gj​=e−imj​te−t/(2τj​). The flavor probability amplitudes are (gS​+gL​)/2 and (gS​−gL​)/2. Their absolute squares are
IK0​(t)=41​[e−t/τS​+e−t/τL​+2e−2t​(1/τS​+1/τL​)cos(Δmt)],​
(10)
IKˉ0​(t)=41​[e−t/τS​+e−t/τL​−2e−2t​(1/τS​+1/τL​)cos(Δmt)],​
(11)
where Δm=mL​−mS​ in natural units. Thus A=B=1/4 for both, ϕ=Δmt, and
CK0​(t)=21​e−2t​(1/τS​+1/τL​),CKˉ0​(t)=−21​e−2t​(1/τS​+1/τL​).​
(12)
Multiplying all three coefficients by the incident intensity gives any other normalization. Crucially, C is time dependent. The printed template cannot hold with all of A,B,C constant. For example, a nonzero constant C would leave an undamped oscillation at arbitrarily late times, even after both unstable components have decayed.
In seconds, ϕ=Δmc2t/ℏ. With x=t/τS​, the supplied mass difference and mean lifetimes give
rτ​=τS​/τL​=0.00172934,ϕ=0.474975x,
(13)
IK0,Kˉ0​(x)=41​[e−x+e−rτ​x±2e−(1+rτ​)x/2cos(0.474975x)].
(14)
Initially the intensities are one and zero. Their sum is 21​(e−x+e−rτ​x), so these are unconditioned survival intensities, not probabilities conditioned on survival. The curves first meet at x=π/(2×0.474975)≃3.307. Thereafter the neutral antikaon component briefly exceeds the K0 component, before the interference dies away and both approach the surviving long-lived intensity e−rτ​x/4.
