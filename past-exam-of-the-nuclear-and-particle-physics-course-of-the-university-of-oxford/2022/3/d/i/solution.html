For a closed unit-branching radioactive decay chain, the rate equations are
dtdNa​​=−λa​Na​,dtdNb​​=λa​Na​−λb​Nb​.
(35)
The first gives Na​=Na0​e−λa​t. Multiply the second by its integrating factor eλb​t:
dtd​(Nb​eλb​t)=λa​Na0​e(λb​−λa​)t.
(36)
Integrate from zero to t. For λa​=λb​,
Nb​=Nb0​e−λb​t+λb​−λa​λa​Na0​​(e−λa​t−e−λb​t),
(37)
so
α=λb​−λa​λa​Na0​​,β=Nb0​−λb​−λa​λa​Na0​​.​
(38)
The coefficients can individually be negative; the physical population is nonnegative because the production integral is nonnegative. For equal nuclear decay constants λa​=λb​=λ, the finite limit is
Nb​=(Nb0​+λNa0​t)e−λt.​
(39)
A sum of two constant-coefficient copies of the same exponential function cannot represent this limit; the t factor is essential.
