All three interactions respect color charge; quark flavor is conserved by the strong interaction and the electromagnetic interaction, but can change in the weak interaction. These statements concern charges and net numbers, rather than an unchanging inventory of particles.
A quark transforms in the fundamental representation of the color charge gauge group SU(3)C​. The strong interaction can change an individual quark's colour by exchanging a gluon, which itself carries color charge. For example, the colour flow qr​→qb​+grbˉ​ is allowed: demanding that the outgoing quark remain red would be incorrect. At each interaction vertex the full colour flow is conserved. The colourless photon, W boson, and Z boson instead couple to a quark with an identity factor δab​ in colour space. A weak charged current can therefore change dr​ to ur​, but cannot turn dr​ into ub​ without another coloured participant. External isolated hadrons are colour singlets; confinement prevents direct observation of a free coloured state.
For each quark flavor f, define the net quark flavor number Ff​=Nf​−Nfˉ​​. The strong interaction and electromagnetic interaction preserve every Ff​: pair creation g→ssˉ, for example, changes neither net strangeness nor any other Ff​. Their quark couplings are diagonal in quark flavor. The weak charged current contains uˉi​γμ(1−γ5)Vij​dj​, where V is the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix, and changes these net numbers. Weak neutral currents are diagonal in tree-level Feynman diagrams; flavor-changing neutral currents can occur through higher-order Feynman diagrams.
The observed hierarchy of mean lifetimes and decay channels supplies evidence: associated strange-particle production through the strong interaction preserves net strangeness, whereas a kaon's weak interaction decay to pions changes it. The electromagnetic interaction process π0→γγ has zero net quark flavor before and after annihilation. Neutrino oscillations show that individual lepton family numbers are not exact conserved quantities. Within the usual effectively massless-neutrino approximation, charged current decays such as μ−→e−νˉe​νμ​ preserve family numbers only when the emitted neutrinos are counted.
Evidence for three colours includes the hadronic R ratio R=3∑f​Qf2​, the colour multiplicities in W boson decay widths, and three-jet events revealing gluon radiation. These test Quantum chromodynamics and its colour structure together; they are not direct measurements of a named red or blue charge on a free quark.
