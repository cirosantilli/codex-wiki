The conversion from a measured beam peak to a particle resonance mass is fixed, but the missing graph supplies neither the peak position nor its width. Let TR​ be the incoming pion kinetic energy at the peak and ΔT the full width at half maximum after allowing for instrumental resolution. With a stationary proton and natural units,
s=mp2​+mπ2​+2mp​(mπ​+T)=(mp​+mπ​)2+2mp​T.
(21)
Thus the peak invariant mass is
M=(mp​+mπ​)2+2mp​TR​​.​
(22)
The width must also be transformed to the centre-of-mass frame. To first order about the peak,
dTds​​=s​mp​​,Γ≃Mmp​​ΔT,τ=Γℏ​.​
(23)
For a broad feature, transform the two half-height positions separately: if they are T−​ and T+​, then the corresponding width in invariant mass is (mp​+mπ​)2+2mp​T+​​−(mp​+mπ​)2+2mp​T−​​. An energy-dependent numerator or width can also displace the observed maximum from the resonance pole parameter.
The low-energy π+p channel is normally dominated by Δ++(1232), a Delta baryon with Q=2, B=1 and I=3/2. As an external reference example, not a reading of the absent plot, M=1232MeV and Γ=117MeV give TR​≃190MeV, ΔT≃154MeV and τ≃5.63×10−24s.[ref] The actual graphical estimates remain unavailable. In particular, using the laboratory width directly in τ=ℏ/ΔT misses the factor mp​/M.
