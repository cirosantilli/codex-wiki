With the valence quark assignments K0=dsˉ and Kˉ0=sdˉ, neutral kaon mixing changes strangeness by two units. A representative leading Standard Model contribution is a box Feynman diagram with two W bosons and two internal up-type quarks:
d⟶ui​⟶s,sˉ⟶uˉj​⟶dˉ,ui​,uj​∈{u,c,t}.
(1)
The two lines exchange the two W bosons. There are four weak charged current interaction vertices, so the scattering amplitude is fourth order in the weak gauge coupling, or second order in the Fermi constant. The full result sums the internal quark flavors with their Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factors; one box alone illustrates the mechanism.

A kaon is a pseudoscalar meson, with intrinsic parity −1. At rest, P∣±⟩=−∣±⟩. Combining this with the specified CP eigenvalues gives
C∣+⟩=−∣+⟩,C∣−⟩=+∣−⟩.​
(2)
Equivalently, in the exchange-positive CP convention for neutral kaon mixing fixed by the question, C∣K0⟩=−∣Kˉ0⟩ and C∣Kˉ0⟩=−∣K0⟩. This sign is a convention for the flavor states; the specified CP eigenvalues and physical predictions must be kept consistent with it.
For two spin-zero pions, conservation of total angular momentum fixes the relative orbital angular momentum to zero. The resulting π+π− or π0π0 state has CP +1. The lowest partial waves of π+π−π0 and 3π0 have CP −1. Thus the dominant nonleptonic assignments in the CP symmetry approximation are
∣KS​⟩≃∣+⟩→2π,∣KL​⟩≃∣−⟩→3π.​
(3)
The two-pion mode has substantially more available kinetic energy and larger Lorentz-invariant phase space; the three-pion mode has an additional final particle and lies closer to its decay threshold. This helps explain why the CP-odd state has the longer mean lifetime. The actual mean lifetimes also depend on the decay amplitudes and on semileptonic channels, so counting final particles alone is not a quantitative prediction.
The middle panel of the figure in part (a) shows sˉ→uˉW+, W+→udˉ, with the d as a spectator quark. The outgoing duˉ and udˉ form π− and π+. In the bottom panel, an additional gluon produces ddˉ, supplying the neutral pion component for π+π−π0. The charge-conjugate diagrams supply the other component of each of the kaon CP eigenstates. The drawn gluon splitting represents nonperturbative hadronization, rather than a quantitatively reliable perturbative prediction at the kaon scale.
These multiplicities describe the dominant low-orbital angular momentum modes, not an absolute rule for every three-pion configuration. In π+π−π0, odd orbital angular momentum values of the charged pair and of its motion relative to π0 can couple to total zero and give CP +1, but are strongly suppressed near decay threshold. Physical KL​→2π also occurs through CP violation.

In Fermi's golden rule,
Γ=ℏ2π​∣M∣2ρ,
(15)
Γ is a transition decay rate, measured in inverse time; ℏ is the reduced Planck constant; M=⟨f∣Hint​∣i⟩ is the transition matrix element of the interaction part of the Hamiltonian; and ρ is the final density of states per unit energy, evaluated subject to conservation of energy. A convention warning: many quantum field theory texts use Γ for an energy decay width. That convention is Γwidth​=ℏΓrate​; the formula as printed uses the rate convention.

A representative Feynman diagram annihilates udˉ into W+∗, producing νμ​ and an off-shell μ+; the charged antilepton emits a photon and becomes the outgoing μ+. The top panel below shows this contribution. A complete gauge-invariant decay amplitude includes radiation from the charged hadronic side, together with structure-dependent terms. The physical separation into inner bremsstrahlung, structure-dependent radiation, and their interference is explicit in the standard radiative pion decay description.[ref]
Here is an explicit finite model of phase space alone, with its assumptions exposed. Neglect the neutrino mass, treat the photon as massless, and replace the spin-summed invariant decay amplitudes by constants over the available Lorentz-invariant phase space. Write M=mπ​, m=mμ​, r=m2/M2, and use natural units. The three-body phase space with one massive daughter follows by factoring through an intermediate invariant squared mass s:
Φ3​(M;m,0,0)=128π3M21​∫m2M2​s(M2−s)(s−m2)​ds=256π3M2​(1−r2+2rlnr).
(16)
The two-body comparison has Φ2​(M;m,0)=(1−r)/(8π). Since Γn​=∣Mn​∣2​Φn​/(2M), assuming ∣M3​∣2​/∣M2​∣2​=κe2/M2 gives
Γμν​Γμνγ​​≃8πκαem​​1−r1−r2+2rlnr​.​
(17)
Here κ is a dimensionless constant encapsulating the assumed matrix element ratio, and αem​ is the fine-structure constant. If the two-body mode dominates, this ratio approximates the radiative branching fraction. Near m=M, 1−r2+2rlnr=(1−r)3/3+O((1−r)4), displaying the extra phase space suppression.
This model is not a prediction of a physical uncut radiative branching fraction. Actual inner bremsstrahlung has a soft-photon singularity, so a count of events with a separately observed photon requires a positive photon energy cut. To see both the missing input and the mass dependence explicitly, the leading soft-photon result for Ecut​<Eγ​<Esoft​≪Emax​ is
Γμν​Γγ,soft​​≃παem​​[1−r1+r​lnr1​−2]lnEcut​Esoft​​,Emax​=2MM2−m2​.​
(18)
The coefficient follows from the radiation factor e[pμ​⋅ϵ/(pμ​⋅k)−pπ​⋅ϵ/(pπ​⋅k)]: angular integration gives β−1ln[(1+β)/(1−β)]−2, where β=(1−r)/(1+r), and the soft energy integral is ∫dEγ​/Eγ​. Thus a finite physical number cannot be deduced from the two masses and phase space alone. The infrared divergence cancels only when the corresponding unresolved real radiation and virtual radiative corrections are combined in an inclusive observable.

For a three-body leptonic decay through the Fermi interaction, neglecting daughter masses and radiative corrections, lepton universality gives
Γ(ℓ−→ℓ′−νˉℓ′​νℓ​)=192π3GF2​mℓ5​​

Each allowed quark channel has three color charge choices and its Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix factor. Neff​≃2+3(∣Vud​∣2+∣Vus​∣2)≃5.

Feynman diagrams organize the perturbative calculation of the matrix element in Fermi's golden rule. Each external line labels an incoming or outgoing particle state; each internal line contributes a propagator for a generally off-shell intermediate field; and each interaction vertex contributes a coupling and the appropriate spin, momentum and internal-symmetry structure. All indistinguishable allowed diagrams must be summed as probability amplitudes before taking the absolute square, so their interference contributes to the rate.
In the radiative pion decay diagram in part (b), solid arrowed lines are fermions; the wavy line joining the hadronic and leptonic vertices is a W boson propagator; and the wavy outgoing line is an external photon. Arrows track fermion number, so an outgoing antimuon has an arrow towards its production vertex. Each vertex conserves four-momentum; an internal propagator need not satisfy the on-shell energy–momentum relation. A closed loop Feynman diagram additionally requires an integration over an internal four-momentum.
The final Lorentz-invariant phase space, conservation Dirac delta functions, and the initial-state normalization or incident flux turn the squared scattering amplitude into a decay rate or cross section. A diagram supplies the dynamics; it is not itself the final density of states.

The following are the leading quantum electrodynamics diagrams. Solid arrows indicate fermion-number flow, and wavy lines indicate photons. The heavy source line represents a charged nucleus; its recoil allows four-momentum conservation.
For Compton scattering, e−γ→e−γ, there are two diagrams: absorption of the incident photon followed along the electron line by emission of the outgoing photon, and the opposite ordering. These are the s- and u-channel electron propagators. Both scattering amplitudes are order e2.
For bremsstrahlung, e−Z→e−Zγ, a virtual photon transfers momentum between the electron and the source. The real photon is emitted before or after this interaction along the electron line. These two dominant external-field diagrams have scattering amplitudes of order Ze3. A full finite-mass charged-target calculation also includes radiation from the target; it is suppressed in the heavy stationary-source approximation used here.
For nuclear-field pair production, γZ→e−e+Z, crossing the preceding diagrams gives two order-Ze3 contributions, with the incoming real photon and the exchanged virtual photon in either order along the pair's fermion line. An isolated real photon cannot produce a massive pair in vacuum: its invariant squared mass is zero, whereas the pair has invariant squared mass at least 4me2​. The source absorbs recoil momentum.

Let MP​ and MD​ denote neutral atomic rest masses for the appropriate parent and daughter, and initially neglect electronic binding energy corrections and daughter excitation. The Q values are
Qβ−​=(MP​−MD​)c2,Qβ+​=(MP​−MD​−2me​)c2,QEC​=(MP​−MD​)c2.​
(27)
For beta-minus decay, the daughter nucleus has one extra positive charge. Its neutral atom contains one more electron than the neutral parent, cancelling the emitted electron's rest mass in the atomic accounting. For beta-plus decay, the neutral parent contains one more electron than the neutral daughter; together with the emitted Positron, this gives the 2me​ subtraction. Thus beta-plus decay requires an atomic mass difference exceeding 2me​c2, whereas electron capture need not.
For electron capture, the captured bound electron is already included in MP​. Capture from a shell leaving an atomic hole of excitation energy Ehole​, and a nuclear excited state of energy Ex​, gives approximately
Eν​=QEC​−Ex​−Ehole​−Trecoil​.
(28)
The hole's energy later appears as atomic radiation or Auger electrons. Subtract Ex​ for either beta decay as well. Exact endpoints also depend on daughter ionization and small atomic binding energy changes; the boxed expressions are the conventional leading atomic-mass formulas.

Write the daughter atomic number as ZD​, the nuclear radius as R, and the relative reduced mass as μ. For zero orbital angular momentum, the WKB approximation gives
Ptun​≃e−G,G=ℏ2​∫Rr2​​2μ(V(r)−Q)​dr,V(r)=rk​,k=4πϵ0​2ZD​e2​,r2​=Qk​.

The diagram refers to ground state, L=0, color singlet baryons. Their spatial wavefunction is symmetric and their color wavefunction is antisymmetric. The Pauli exclusion principle therefore requires a symmetric combined spin-and-flavor wavefunction. One cannot independently attach every three-spin coupling to each flavor assignment.
For three spin-1/2 particles, there are formally two ways to couple to total spin 1/2. In the ground state quark model these are tied to the corresponding mixed-symmetry flavor functions. Measured half-integer spin and fermionic behavior rule out a simple quark-antiquark pair: two spin-1/2 constituents have integer total spin, and adding integer orbital angular momentum does not change that. Their measured baryon number B=1, inferred through production and conservation bookkeeping, corresponds to nq​−nqˉ​​=3. Their pattern of electric charges, strangeness and isospin then agrees with three light valence quarks.
Baryon magnetic moments provide a more detailed check. The additive quark model gives, for example,
μp​=34​μu​−31​μd​,μn​=34​μd​−31​μu​.
(47)
Approximately equal up and down constituent masses give μu​≃−2μd​, hence μp​/μn​≃−3/2, close to the measured ratio of about −1.46.
These observations support three valence quarks; they do not prove the absence of extra quark-antiquark pairs. For instance, qqqqqˉ​ also has B=1 and can have half-integer spin. Ordinary Quantum chromodynamics baryons also contain sea pairs and gluons. The stronger interpretation that the measurements exclude every antiquark component is therefore false.

f OurBigBook.com
The observed dominant two-body transition is Σ0→Λ0γ​. Both baryons have valence quarks uds and the same strangeness; an electromagnetic interaction transition is therefore possible. Their mass difference is only about 77MeV/c2, below the lightest pion mass, so no additional on-shell strongly interacting particle fits. A photon is neutral and massless, and can carry the required angular momentum through a magnetic dipole transition between the two positive-parity spin-1/2 states. A single neutrino would violate lepton number and would not give the required fermion parity.
At rest, four-momentum conservation gives pΛ​=pΣ​−k and k2=0. Thus
MΛ2​c4=MΣ2​c4−2MΣ​c2Eγ​,Eγ​=2MΣ​MΣ2​−MΛ2​​c2.​
(50)
The result is slightly below (MΣ​−MΛ​)c2 because the Lambda baryon recoils; rounded masses give about 74.5MeV.
The channels nπ0 and pπ− are actually kinematically open: their total rest energies are about 1075 and 1078MeV, below MΣ​c2≃1193MeV. Their suppression is therefore not a decay threshold prohibition. Each changes strangeness from −1 to zero and so requires a weak interaction. The much faster allowed electromagnetic transition makes such weak branching fractions extremely small. “Not observed” should be understood as overwhelming electromagnetic competition, not an exact ban in the Standard Model. The photon identification likewise uses the known low-energy spectrum and dominant transition mechanism, not neutrality alone.
