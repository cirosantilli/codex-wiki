Leaky integrate-and-fire model (CV˙=−gL​(V−EL​)+I(t))

 Home Biology Neuroscience Computational neuroscience
OurBigBook.com Words: 488 Articles: 1
The leaky integrate-and-fire model combines a passive equation for membrane potential with a firing rule. Between events, inward-positive applied electric current I(t) obeys
CdtdV​=−gL​(V−EL​)+I(t),R=gL​1​,τ=gL​C​=RC,
(36)
where C>0 is membrane capacitance, gL​>0 is the leak conductance, R is the corresponding electrical resistance, and EL​ is the leak reversal potential. Here the leak conductance is constant. The time constant τ describes passive relaxation. When V first reaches a threshold Vθ​ from below, record an action potential event and reset V to Vr​<Vθ​. In the version used here, hold V=Vr​ for a fixed neuronal refractory period tref​, then resume integration. See 1.3 Integrate-And-Fire Models.
For constant I, with t=0 at the start of an integration interval and V(0)=Vinitial​, the exact subthreshold solution is
V(t)=EL​+RI+(Vinitial​−EL​−RI)e−t/τ.
(37)
The equilibrium point of this passive equation is V∞​=EL​+RI. Subtracting V∞​ leaves d(V−V∞​)/dt=−(V−V∞​)/τ, explaining the exponential function. This solution applies only until a threshold event or a change in I. Starting below threshold, constant input produces repeated events if V∞​>Vθ​; if V∞​=Vθ​, the threshold is approached asymptotically and never reached at finite time.
For a worked example, choose
EL​=Vr​=Vinitial​=−65mV,Vθ​=−50mV,τ=20ms,R=100MΩ,tref​=2ms.
(38)
These imply gL​=10nS and C=200pF. All these parameters are illustrative model choices, not measurements from a particular neuron. With I=200pA, Ohm's law gives RI=20mV and hence V∞​=−45mV. Thus
V(t)=−45mV−20mVe−t/(20ms).
(39)
At threshold, e−t/τ=1/4, so the first event occurs after T=20ln4ms≈27.7259ms. Each subsequent cycle includes the 2ms neuronal refractory period, giving
f=T+tref​1​≈33.6407Hz.
(40)
This is the ideal sustained firing rate; the first-event latency is T, not T+tref​.
With I=100pA instead, RI=10mV and V∞​=−55mV. From the same initial state, V(t)=−55mV−10mVe−t/τ stays below −50mV: no events occur, however long this current is held. The critical sustained current is (Vθ​−EL​)/R=150pA; equality gives asymptotic approach rather than finite-time firing.
The leak gives old inputs progressively less influence, while capacitance prevents an ordinary finite electric current step from changing V instantly. This combination makes the leaky integrate-and-fire model useful for exploring input summation, event timing and network activity at low computational cost. Its fixed parameters and reset omit the evolving voltage-gated ion channels of the Hodgkin-Huxley model, spatial integration in dendrites and mechanisms of neural adaptation. Treating a synapse as a prescribed I(t) also omits its voltage-dependent driving force unless a conductance term is added. See 1.4 Limitations of the Leaky Integrate-and-Fire Model and constant-input firing rate of a leaky integrate-and-fire neuron.
