Magnification

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Image formation
OurBigBook.com Words: 666 Articles: 2
Magnification compares an image's scale with a specified reference. Transverse magnification compares signed lengths in object and image planes; angular magnification compares viewing angles at an observer. Both are dimensionless quantities, but they answer different questions. A projected real image twice as tall has ∣m∣=2; an optical telescope's advertised viewing enlargement normally concerns angular magnification. [ref].
Always specify the sign and the reference arrangement. For signed transverse magnification, m<0 means inverted and m>0 means upright; ∣m∣>1 means enlarged and 0<∣m∣<1 means reduced. A larger virtual image can subtend a modest viewing angle if it is farther away. At u=f, an ideal converging thin lens has no finite image height and hence no finite transverse magnification, while angular magnification can remain finite. Enlargement also does not guarantee extra detail: diffraction-limited resolution and optical aberrations limit the information available to enlarge. [ref].
