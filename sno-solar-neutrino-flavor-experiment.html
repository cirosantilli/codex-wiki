SNO solar-neutrino flavor experiment (2001–2002)

 ... Physics Particle detection Particle detector Cherenkov detector Kajita-McDonald neutrino flavor-conversion experiments Sudbury Neutrino Observatory
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
In 2001, 240.95 live days, reconstructed radius below 5.50m and effective electron kinetic energy above 6.75MeV gave a CC neutrino flux of 1.75×106cm−2s−1 for the assumed boron-8 spectrum. Source calibrations measured 1.4% instrumental-cut signal loss. SNO's own CC–ES difference was 1.6σ; comparison with Super-Kamiokande's precise ES flux gave 3.3σ evidence for non-electron active neutrinos. This was not an NC measurement.[ref]
In 2002 the pure-heavy water analysis used 306.4 live days and a 5MeV reconstructed kinetic-energy threshold. NC neutrons captured on deuterons emitted 6.25MeV gamma rays, producing secondary electron light. The NC reaction threshold was 2.2MeV, a different quantity. Californium-252 calibrated 29.9% capture efficiency, but only 14.4% detection efficiency after fiducial and energy cuts. Assays constrained backgrounds from radioactive decay and photodisintegration.[ref]
Energy, radius and direction relative to the Sun statistically separated channels. Assuming the standard boron-8 spectral shape, the NC total active neutrino flux was 5.09−0.43+0.44​(stat.)−0.43+0.46​(syst.)×106cm−2s−1, consistent with stellar structure predictions. The non-electron active component differed from zero by 5.3σ. This established active flavour conversion, without individually tagging muon neutrinos and tau neutrinos. It did not exclude every sterile admixture.[ref]
