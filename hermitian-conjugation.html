Hermitian conjugation (A†=AT)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Hilbert space Riesz representation theorem Adjoint operator
OurBigBook.com Words: 179
Hermitian conjugation is the operation of taking the adjoint operator, written T†=T∗. For a matrix in orthonormal bases, it takes the complex conjugate of each entry and exchanges rows with columns:
(A†)jk​=Akj​​,A†=AT.
(25)
For a real matrix it is the ordinary transpose. For the complex matrix T in the adjoint operator example, its second row (2,1−i) becomes the second column (2,1+i)T of T†. This is the same conjugate transpose used in MIT's treatment of complex inner products and Hermitian matrices.[ref]
For bounded operators with matching source and target Hilbert spaces, Hermitian conjugation satisfies
(A+B)†=A†+B†,(αA)†=αA†,(BA)†=A†B†,(A†)†=A.
(26)
The reversal of function composition is forced by moving both linear operators across an inner product:
⟨BAx,y⟩=⟨Ax,B†y⟩=⟨x,A†B†y⟩.
(27)
The calculation identifies the adjoint operator by the uniqueness theorem in Melrose's MIT notes.[ref]
For a computed order check, let S=(01​10​) and reuse T=(12​i1−i​). Then
(ST)†=T†S=(21+i​1−i​),ST†=(−i1​1+i2​).
(28)
Keeping the original order gives a different matrix. Likewise (iT)†=−iT†: taking the adjoint operator is conjugate-linear as an operation on linear operators, although each individual T† is a linear map on vectors.
