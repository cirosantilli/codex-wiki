Hopf bifurcation

 Home Physics Branches of physics Dynamical systems Bifurcation theory
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 12
A Hopf bifurcation creates or destroys a small periodic orbit near an equilibrium point as a pair of complex conjugate eigenvalues crosses the imaginary axis. It explains how a resting state can give way to sustained oscillations: the linearization of a dynamical system supplies rotation and growth, while a nonlinear term can saturate that growth at a nonzero amplitude.
The simplest supercritical Hopf bifurcation is the exact Hopf normal form
z˙=(μ+iω)z−∣z∣2z,z=x+iy,ω>0.
(6)
Here z is a complex number, μ is the bifurcation parameter, and ω is the angular frequency. In polar coordinates z=reiθ, the equations become r˙=μr−r3 and θ˙=ω for r>0. At μ<0, each turn shrinks towards the equilibrium point. At μ>0, small radii grow and large radii shrink towards r=μ​, producing a limit cycle. For example, μ=0.04 and ω=2 give radius 0.2, period π, and frequency 1/π cycles per unit time. The limiting angular frequency stays nonzero while the amplitude tends to zero: a tiny newborn oscillation can still repeat at a finite rate.
For a precise generic statement, consider a smooth function f defining an autonomous differential equation x˙=f(x,α) on Rn, with a smooth branch of equilibrium points x∗​(α) and critical parameter α=0. Require:
  • the Jacobian matrix Dx​f(x∗​(α),α) to have a simple pair of eigenvalues β(α)±iω(α), with β(0)=0 and ω(0)>0;
  • every other eigenvalue to have nonzero real part at α=0;
  • a transverse crossing, β′(0)=0;
  • a nonzero cubic radial coefficient a=Reℓ(0)=0 in the reduced Hopf normal form.
Then a locally unique branch of small periodic orbits, up to phase, emerges on the side βa<0. On the two-dimensional centre manifold, the branch attracts for a<0 and repels for a>0. Attraction in the full phase space additionally requires all remaining eigenvalues to have negative real part; any transverse unstable direction makes the periodic orbit unstable there.[ref]
An eigenvalue-crossing bifurcation test alone cannot establish these nonlinear conclusions. For example, the purely linear equation z˙=(μ+iω)z has the same crossing but no isolated periodic orbit: at μ=0 every nonzero radius is constant, and at μ=0 none is. If the cubic coefficient vanishes, higher-order terms must be examined; a generalized Hopf bifurcation is one possible degeneracy. The usefulness of the generic theorem is that a large system can have its small oscillatory onset understood through one Hopf normal form, subject to these hypotheses and local limits.
