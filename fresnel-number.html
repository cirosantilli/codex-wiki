Fresnel number (ND​=D2/(λz),Na​=a2/(λz))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Fresnel diffraction
OurBigBook.com Words: 185
A Fresnel number compares squared aperture size with the product of wavelength and propagation distance. For an optical slit of full width D=2a, explicitly distinguish the full-width and half-width conventions:
ND​=λzD2​,Na​=λza2​=4ND​​,Δϕedge−centre​=λzπa2​=πNa​=4πND​​.
(8)
The full-width convention is used throughout the Fresnel slit propagation example below. The definition D2/(λz) also appears in the Delft treatment.[ref]
For uniform incident wave phase, ND​≪1 makes the omitted aperture wave phase small and gives the Fraunhofer diffraction regime. When ND​ is of order one or larger, retain it and use Fresnel diffraction, provided the paraxial approximation still holds. These are continuous changes in accuracy, rather than a physical boundary at one selected number. For a rectangular aperture, check both transverse dimensions. Incident wavefront curvature contributes its own wave phase through U0​; the aperture-to-screen Fresnel number alone does not describe a spherical source's illumination.
The scaling is revealing: doubling the optical slit width at fixed wavelength and distance multiplies the Fresnel number by four. Maintaining the same Fresnel number then requires four times the distance. Two systems can consequently have the same dimensionless pattern despite very different physical lengths.
