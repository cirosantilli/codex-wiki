Modular inversion converges to a p-adic inverse (rn​≡a−1(modpn) ⟹ rn​→a−1)

OurBigBook.com Words: 204
Let a be an integer not divisible by p. Its modular inverse modulo pn has a unique least nonnegative representative rn​. Reducing the inverse equation modulo pn shows that the representatives are compatible. Since a is a p-adic unit,
∣rn​−a−1∣p​=∣arn​−1∣p​≤p−n,
(46)
so the sequence converges to the inverse a−1 in Zp​. This computes a fraction through finite integer operations.
For a=3 and p=2, the first eight representatives are 1,3,3,11,11,43,43,171. Each satisfies 3rn​≡1(mod2n). The resulting expansion is
31​=1+2+8+32+⋯=1+k≥0∑​22k+1in Z2​.​
(47)
The geometric series has ratio 4, whose 2-adic absolute value is 1/4, and its sum is 1+2/(1−4)=1/3. In the ordinary real number absolute value, the representatives grow. Different metrics impose different meanings of a good approximation. The periodic digits here are a specific instance of the rational expansions studied in Conrad's authored note.[ref]
