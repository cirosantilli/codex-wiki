Serial dilution repeatedly transfers a measured portion of one well-mixed chemical solution and dilutes that portion to a new final volume. With transferred volume vj​ and final volume Vj​ at step j, conserved solute gives

This product follows by repeated substitution. Each step conserves the solute in the portion transferred into its receiving flask, not the entire inventory in the preceding flask. Uniform mixing makes the selected fraction representative.
Continue the original solution mass and volume bookkeeping example. A 10.00mL portion of the 0.2000molL−1 stock contains 0.002000mol of analytical sodium chloride, corresponding to 0.11688g before rounding. After dilution to 100.0mL, its analytical concentration is 0.02000molL−1. A new 5.000mL portion of that mixed chemical solution contains 0.0001000mol; dilution to 50.00mL gives 0.002000molL−1. The two factors 10.00/100.0 and 5.000/50.00 multiply to 1/100.
The remaining stock holds 0.05000−0.002000=0.04800mol; the remaining first dilution holds 0.002000−0.0001000=0.001900mol. Including the final flask restores the starting inventory:
0.04800+0.001900+0.0001000=0.05000mol.
(7)
This is a direct check that making a dilute final sample does not destroy the solute. Most of it remains in earlier vessels.
Serial dilution makes a small final concentration accessible using measurable portions instead of a single extremely small transfer. Its limitation is that systematic errors propagate: if each transferred volume is 1% below its intended value while final volumes are correct, two steps give 0.992=0.9801 times the intended concentration, a 1.99% deficit. Repeated readings alone do not remove that bias. The gain in manageable volume therefore depends on calibrated transfer and representative mixing.
