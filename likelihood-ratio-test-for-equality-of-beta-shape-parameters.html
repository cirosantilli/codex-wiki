Likelihood-ratio test for equality of beta shape parameters (λ=nnmm(n+m)n+m​Tn(1−T)m)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling Statistical hypothesis test Likelihood-ratio test
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
Take two independent statistical samples from beta distributions with parameters (θ,1) and (τ,1), of positive sizes n,m. Put A=−∑i​logXi​, B=−∑j​logYj​, and T=A/(A+B). To test the null hypothesis θ=τ, divide the maximized common-rate likelihood function by the unrestricted maximum to obtain the displayed likelihood ratio. Indeed the unrestricted fitted rates are n/A,m/B, and the common fitted rate is (n+m)/(A+B); substitution cancels the exponential factors.
Under the null hypothesis, A and B have independent gamma distributions with a common rate. Beta-gamma independence gives T∼Beta(n,m). The derivative of log(Tn(1−T)m) changes sign at n/(n+m), so the exact critical region of size α is T<k1​ or T>k2​, with k1​<n/(n+m)<k2​. Its cutoffs satisfy
k1n​(1−k1​)m=k2n​(1−k2​)m,Fn,m​(k1​)+1−Fn,m​(k2​)=α.
(23)
Here Fn,m​ is the beta distribution cumulative distribution function. Equal likelihood ratios at the cutoffs do not generally mean equal tail probabilities. Increasing the ratio threshold continuously adds positive null distribution mass on both sides, proving uniqueness for 0<α<1.
