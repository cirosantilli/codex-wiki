By the first isomorphism theorem for rings, the given surjection identifies S with R/kerϕ. Since R is a principal ideal domain, write kerϕ=(a).
If a=0, the quotient ring is R, an integral domain, so part (a)(ii) applies. If a is a unit, the quotient ring is the zero ring; its unique element is associate to itself by multiplication by its identity, so it too is an associator ring.
In the remaining case, unique factorization gives
a=εp1k1​​⋯prkr​​,
(13)
where ε is a unit, the pi​ are pairwise nonassociate prime elements, and ki​≥1. The principal ideals (piki​​) are pairwise comaximal: different prime factors have greatest common divisor 1, and Bézout's identity gives a linear combination equal to 1. The Chinese remainder theorem therefore gives a ring isomorphism
R/(a) ≅ ∏i=1r​R/(piki​​).
(14)
Every factor is an associator ring by part (c), and their finite direct product of rings is an associator ring by part (b). A ring isomorphism preserves units and principal ideals, so the property transfers to S. Hence
S is an associator ring.​
(15)
