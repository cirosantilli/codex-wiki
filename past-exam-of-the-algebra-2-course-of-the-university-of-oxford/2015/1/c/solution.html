Put A=R/(pk). A principal ideal domain is a unique factorization domain, so every nonzero element of this quotient ring can be written
a=p​es,0≤e<k,p∤s.
(9)
Indeed, remove the largest power of the prime element p from a representative that is not divisible by pk. Since s and pk have greatest common divisor 1 up to a unit, Bézout's identity in the principal ideal domain gives xs+ypk=1. Thus s is a unit in A.
Consequently every nonzero principal ideal in A is generated by one of 1,p​,…,p​k−1. Include the zero ideal by assigning it exponent e=k. These ideals are distinct: their inverse images in R are (1),(p),…,(pk), and equality of two different powers would, by cancellation in the integral domain, make the nonunit p a unit.
Now suppose (a)=(b). If this is the zero ideal, both generators are zero and are associate elements. Otherwise their exponents agree, so write
a=p​es,b=p​et
(10)
with s,t both units. Then
a=(st−1)b,
(11)
and the multiplier is a unit. Thus
R/(pk) is an associator ring for every k≥1.​
(12)
The exponent detects the principal ideal, while its remaining factor is invertible; this is why zero divisors cause no obstruction here.
