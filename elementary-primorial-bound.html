Elementary primorial bound

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Primorial
OurBigBook.com Words: 75
For every real X≥1,
P(X)≤4X.
(14)
For integer n, strong induction proves this by splitting according to parity. If n=2k, every prime in (k,2k] divides (k2k​), so
P(2k)≤P(k)(k2k​)≤4k4k.
(15)
If n=2k+1, the valuation of a near-central binomial coefficient gives
P(2k+1)≤P(k+1)(k+12k+1​)≤4k+14k.
(16)
The binomial estimates follow from the binomial theorem; in the odd case the two equal central coefficients together are at most 22k+1. The result for real X follows by replacing X with ⌊X⌋.
