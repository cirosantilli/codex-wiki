Radius change at constant angular momentum (Iω=Lz​,Krot​=Lz2​/(2I))

Place two equal point masses of 1kg each on opposite sides of a fixed origin, at equal radius r, and let them share a signed angular velocity ω=θ˙ in a plane. Their total mass is M=2kg and their moment of inertia about the perpendicular axis is I=Mr2. Radial pulling forces have zero torque about that origin, so
Lz​=Iω=Mr2ω=constant,Krot​=21​Iω2=2ILz2​​.
(26)
Take an initial radius ri​=1m and angular velocity ωi​=1rad/s, and a final radius rf​=0.5m. Then
Ii​=2kgm2,If​=0.5kgm2,Lz​=2kgm2/s,ωf​=4rad/s,
(27)
and
Ki​=1J,Kf​=4J,W=Kf​−Ki​=+3J.
(28)
These are endpoint states with r˙i​=r˙f​=0. During the pull, the full kinetic energy also contains radial motion: K=Mr˙2/2+Krot​. Assume the pulling apparatus supplies all net work to the two point masses; the work-energy theorem then fixes its net work at +3J, including any radial acceleration and braking. Each mass spirals inward during the pull, allowing its inward force to do positive work. Conservation of angular momentum therefore predicts the final angular velocity without requiring kinetic energy conservation. For an apparatus included in a larger system, its stored energy supplies the increase consistently with conservation of energy.
