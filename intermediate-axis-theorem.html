Intermediate axis theorem

 ... Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics Rigid body dynamics Euler's equations (rigid body dynamics) Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
For a rigid body with three distinct positive principal moments of inertia 0<I1​<I2​<I3​ and zero external torque about its centre of mass, steady rotation about the first or third principal axis is Lyapunov stable in angular velocity, while rotation about the intermediate principal axis is unstable. Here steady rotation means ω=Ωe^i​ in the principal body frame, with constant Ω=0. A small sideways perturbation remains small about the extreme principal moments of inertia, but generically grows about the intermediate one. Stable does not mean that the wobble decays: without damping, there is no asymptotic stability. Coincident principal moments of inertia and a stationary body are excluded.[ref]
No external torque is needed for a body to tumble. Conservation of angular momentum fixes L in the fixed space frame, but the body can turn relative to L, so its angular velocity components in the principal body frame change. The torque-free rigid-body invariants constrain this motion. A supported gyroscope with gravitational torque has different governing assumptions.
