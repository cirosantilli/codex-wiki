Write g=(g1​,…,gn​) for the continuous representatives of the weak derivatives. Integrating the preceding identity over B=B(0,1) gives
∣B∣uε​(x)=∫B​uε​(y)dy+∫B​∫01​(ρε​∗g)(y+t(x−y))⋅(x−y)dtdy.
(13)
The first term converges to ∫B​u because uε​→u in L1. For fixed x, all segments in the second term lie in a compact set. Continuity of g implies ρε​∗g→g uniformly on that set. Hence every x has the finite limit
U(x)=∣B∣1​(∫B​u(y)dy+∫B​∫01​g(y+t(x−y))⋅(x−y)dtdy).
(14)
The same argument is uniform for x in any fixed compact set: all the segments then lie in a common compact set. Thus uε​→U locally uniformly, so U is continuous; the local L1 convergence also gives U=u almost everywhere.
Passing to the limit in the segment identity, and taking its endpoints to be x and x+h, gives
U(x+h)−U(x)−g(x)⋅h=∫01​[g(x+th)−g(x)]⋅hdt=o(∣h∣).
(15)
Therefore U is differentiable with continuous gradient g: U∈C1,U=u almost everywhere,∇U=g​. This is the continuous weak gradient gives a C1 representative criterion.
