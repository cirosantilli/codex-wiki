A weak solution is u∈H01​(Ω) such that
∫Ω​∇u⋅∇φ=∫Ω​f(u)φ(φ∈H01​(Ω)).
(30)
Let Mf​=∥f∥∞​ and define the Nemytskii operator N(v)=f∘v on L2(Ω). It satisfies ∥N(v)∥2​≤Mf​∣Ω∣1/2. It is continuous in L2: from any sequence vj​→v in L2, every subsequence has a further subsequence converging almost everywhere; continuity and boundedness of f, followed by the dominated convergence theorem, give convergence of the corresponding N(vj​) in L2. This subsequence criterion proves continuity of the whole sequence.
For h∈L2, the Lax-Milgram theorem defines the inverse Dirichlet solution map Sh∈H01​ by ∫∇(Sh)⋅∇φ=∫hφ. Testing with Sh and using the Poincaré inequality yields
∥∇Sh∥2​≤CP​∥h∥2​,∥Sh∥2​≤CP2​∥h∥2​.
(31)
The Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem makes S:L2→L2 compact. Therefore T=S∘N is a nonlinear compact operator and its image lies in the closed L2 ball of radius R=1+CP2​Mf​∣Ω∣1/2. This ball is nonempty, closed, bounded and convex. The Schauder fixed-point theorem gives u=Tu. Since Tu∈H01​, its defining identity is exactly the required weak formulation. Thus a weak solution exists. This is the bounded semilinear Dirichlet existence theorem.
