For a uniform contraction from a comparison function on a bounded set, the closed set M is complete and has diameter at most 2R. Suppose first R>0. The limit condition supplies r0​>0 and q0​<1 such that ψ(r)/r≤q0​ for 0<r≤r0​. On the remaining compact interval [r0​,2R], if nonempty, the continuous ratio ψ(r)/r attains a maximum q1​<1, since it is strictly below one at every point. Thus there is one q<1 with ψ(r)≤qr for every distance 0≤r≤2R arising in M.
Consequently d(Tx,Ty)≤qd(x,y) throughout M. Banach's theorem applies and gives a unique fixed point in M. No linear structure or compactness of the ball is used; the compact object in the argument is only the scalar distance interval. If the closed ball is the singleton at radius zero, its self-map fixes that point. The estimate on the ratio near zero is what prevents the compact-interval supremum argument from approaching one at its missing endpoint.
