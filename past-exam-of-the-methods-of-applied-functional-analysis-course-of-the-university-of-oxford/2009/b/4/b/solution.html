A weak solution belongs to V=H01​(Ω) and satisfies
∫Ω​∇u⋅∇hdx=∫Ω​f(u)hdx(h∈V),​
(19)
with the nonlinear right-hand side defining a bounded functional on V. Under the stated global Lipschitz hypothesis this requirement is automatic: ∣f(s)∣≤∣f(0)∣+ϵ∣s∣ and boundedness of Ω give f(u)∈L2 whenever u∈V. Continuity of f alone, without growth control, would not guarantee this for an arbitrary Sobolev function.
For the semilinear Poisson contraction threshold, use the gradient norm on V, and let CP​ satisfy the Poincaré inequality ∥v∥2​≤CP​∥∇v∥2​. For g∈L2, Lax-Milgram gives the zero-Dirichlet Poisson solution Pg∈V. Testing with Pg yields ∥∇Pg∥2​≤CP​∥g∥2​. Define T(v)=P(f(v)), a self-map of the complete space V. For v,w∈V,
∥T(v)−T(w)∥V​≤CP​∥f(v)−f(w)∥2​≤ϵCP2​∥v−w∥V​.
(20)
Therefore ϵCP2​<1 is a sufficient condition for Banach's theorem to give a unique fixed point and hence a unique weak solution. There is no need to assume f(0)=0 or to restrict to a small ball: the Lipschitz bound makes the map defined on all of V. The same estimates give
∥∇u∥2​≤1−ϵCP2​CP​∣Ω∣1/2∣f(0)∣​.
(21)
With the optimal Poincaré constant, the threshold is ϵ<λ1​ for the First Dirichlet eigenvalue; the stated sufficient condition does not depend on knowing that optimal constant.
