Banach's fixed-point theorem states that a map T:Y→Y on a nonempty complete metric space with d(Tx,Ty)≤qd(x,y) for a constant 0≤q<1 has a unique fixed point, approached from every starting point by iteration.
Choose x0​ and let xn+1​=Txn​. Induction gives d(xn+1​,xn​)≤qnd(x1​,x0​). For m>n, the triangle inequality and geometric series give
d(xm​,xn​)≤1−qqn​d(x1​,x0​).
(17)
Thus the sequence is Cauchy and converges by completeness to some x∗​. The contraction estimate implies continuity, so Tx∗​=limTxn​=limxn+1​=x∗​. If y∗​ were another fixed point, d(x∗​,y∗​)≤qd(x∗​,y∗​) would force equality of the points. Passing m→∞ also yields the quantitative error estimate
d(xn​,x∗​)≤1−qqn​d(x1​,x0​).​
(18)
