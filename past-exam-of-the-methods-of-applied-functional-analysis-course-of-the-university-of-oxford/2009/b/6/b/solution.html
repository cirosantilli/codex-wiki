For the log-cosh energy on a Sobolev space, the elementary inequalities 0≤logcosht≤t2/2 follow by integrating ∣tanht∣≤∣t∣ from zero. Thus
0≤I(v)≤21​(∥∇v∥22​+∥v∥22​)<∞,
(28)
so the energy is well-defined on H1. For a fixed v, its proposed derivative is linear in the direction h and bounded, because ∣tanhv∣≤∣v∣ gives
∣∂h​I(v)∣≤∥∇v∥2​∥∇h∥2​+∥v∥2​∥h∥2​≤∥v∥H1​∥h∥H1​.
(29)
To justify differentiation, use (logcosht)′′=sech2t∈(0,1]. Taylor's integral remainder pointwise, combined with the exact quadratic gradient expansion, gives
0≤I(v+h)−I(v)−∫Ω​(∇v⋅∇h+tanh(v)h)dx≤21​∥h∥H12​.
(30)
Every term is integrable by the displayed bounds. The remainder is quadratic in the norm, so the functional is even Fréchet differentiable, and in particular Gâteaux differentiable with the required derivative. This also proves that A(v) belongs to the stated dual space.
For v,w∈H1,
⟨A(v)−A(w),v−w⟩=∥∇(v−w)∥22​+∫Ω​[tanhv−tanhw](v−w)dx.
(31)
Both terms are nonnegative, and the integrand in the second is strictly positive wherever v=w, since tanh is strictly increasing. Distinct Sobolev elements differ on a set of positive measure, so the expression is positive. Hence A is strictly monotone.
This gives strict monotonicity without coercivity on H1: it is not coercive on H1. For constant functions vt​=t with t→+∞, the norm is t∣Ω∣1/2 and
∥vt​∥H1​⟨A(vt​),vt​⟩​=∣Ω∣1/2tanht⟶∣Ω∣1/2,
(32)
which is finite. In contrast it is coercive on H01​: if ∥v∥2​≤CP​∥∇v∥2​, then
⟨A(v),v⟩=∥∇v∥22​+∫Ω​vtanhvdx≥1+CP2​∥v∥H12​​.
(33)
Dividing by the norm proves growth to infinity. On this smaller space the operator is understood by restricting its dual action to zero-trace test functions. Removing the constant directions by the boundary condition is what restores coercivity.
