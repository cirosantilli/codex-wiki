The weak derivative condition is
∫Ω​u∂i​ϕdx=−∫Ω​vϕdx(ϕ∈Cc∞​(Ω)).​
(1)
Both integrals exist since u,v are locally integrable and the test function has compact support. Derivatives are defined up to almost-everywhere equality. The space W1,p(Ω) consists of u∈Lp(Ω) whose first weak derivatives Di​u all lie in Lp(Ω). One standard norm is
∥u∥1,p​=(∥u∥pp​+∑i=1n​∥Di​u∥pp​)1/p(p<∞),∥u∥1,∞​=max(∥u∥∞​,∥D1​u∥∞​,…,∥Dn​u∥∞​).
(2)
A sum in the last formula gives an equivalent norm. For restriction to E, extend any ϕ∈Cc∞​(E) by zero to Ω. Its support is compactly contained in E, so the extension is smooth. The identity over Ω then reduces exactly to the identity over E, proving locality of the weak derivative.
For the jump contribution to a piecewise weak derivative, integration by parts on the two sides of the joining point gives the distributional derivative
Du=2x1(0,1)​+1(1,2)​+(a−1)δ1​.
(3)
The coefficient is the right trace 1+a minus the left trace 2. If a locally integrable weak derivative existed, locality on each open side would identify it almost everywhere with the displayed regular part. Substituting that into the global test identity would force (a−1)ϕ(1)=0 for every test function, hence a=1. Equivalently a nonzero Dirac point mass cannot be represented by a locally integrable derivative.
For a=1 the point-mass term vanishes, and both the function and its piecewise derivative are bounded on the finite interval. Thus u∈W1,∞ and hence in every listed W1,p. Therefore membership holds exactly for a=1. Changing a single point value is irrelevant to Sobolev membership; it cannot remove mismatched one-sided traces when a=1.
