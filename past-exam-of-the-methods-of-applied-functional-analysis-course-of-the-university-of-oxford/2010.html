We prove existence directly through quadratic energy minimization by the parallelogram law. Let m=infv∈Hg1​​A[v]. The set contains g, and uniform ellipticity gives 0≤m≤A[g]<∞. Choose a minimizing sequence vj​ with A[vj​]→m. Since (vj​+vk​)/2∈Hg1​, the parallelogram law gives

(6)

∥vj​−vk​∥H12​≤λ1+CP2​​A[vj​−vk​]⟶0.
(7)

For n=2, take w=(log(1/r))1/3 on a sufficiently small ball. With s=log(1/r), the gradient integral is proportional to ∫∞s−4/3ds<∞, while the weighted integral is proportional to ∫∞s−2/3ds=∞. Its L2 integral is finite because the area factor contributes exponential decay in s. These singular functions have the indicated weak derivatives; deleting a small central ball and passing to the limit in integration by parts leaves no boundary term.
Thus w2∇u⋅∇v need not be integrable in dimensions at least two. This is the squared Sobolev gradient counterexample. With the additional restriction w∈L∞, the form is well-defined and monotone in any dimension.
