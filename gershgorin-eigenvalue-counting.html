Gershgorin eigenvalue counting (#{λ in C}=kwith algebraic multiplicity)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis Gershgorin circle theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 218
Let C be a connected component of the union of the row Gershgorin discs of an n×n matrix. If C comprises k of the indexed Gershgorin discs, then it contains exactly k eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity. Coincident disks still count separately, once for each row. In particular, a Gershgorin disc disjoint from all the others contains one simple eigenvalue. More generally, any union of k indexed Gershgorin discs disjoint from the remaining n−k contains exactly k eigenvalues, whether or not that union is connected.[ref]
The separation is essential: overlapping Gershgorin discs do not individually promise one eigenvalue each. Discs that touch at a boundary point belong to the same connected component. For example, the matrix (200​120​008​) has Gershgorin discs of radii 1,0,0 with centres 2,2,8. The component around 2 comprises two indexed disks and contains the eigenvalue 2 with algebraic multiplicity two; the isolated point at 8 contains the remaining eigenvalue.
For intuition, continuously turn on the off-diagonal entries using the diagonal matrix E=diag(a11​,…,ann​) and A(t)=E+t(A−E), 0≤t≤1. At t=0 the eigenvalues are the disk centres; at every later time the Gershgorin discs stay inside their final union. The continuity of the eigenvalues as a collection means that no eigenvalue can cross a gap between final components. The count gives a useful certificate that an eigenvalue cluster is complete.[ref]
