Set ϕ​(x)=ϕ(−x)​. This is a Schwartz function and its Fourier transform is ϕ​​(ξ)=ϕ​(ξ)​. The convolution theorem gives
ϕ∗ϕ​​(ξ)=∣ϕ​(ξ)∣2.
(25)
The convolution of two Schwartz functions is again a Schwartz function: differentiating under the integral and expanding xm=(y+(x−y))m bounds every weighted derivative by products of integrable and bounded weighted derivatives. Evaluate Fourier inversion for this convolution at zero:
∫R​∣ϕ(x)∣2dx=(ϕ∗ϕ​)(0)=2π1​∫R​∣ϕ​(ξ)∣2dξ.​
(26)
The Schwartz space is dense in the L2 space. For f∈L2(R) choose Schwartz functions ϕj​→f in the L2 norm. The identity makes ϕ​j​ a Cauchy sequence in the L2 space, and its limit defines Ff, independently of the approximation. It agrees with the distributional Fourier transform, because Cauchy-Schwarz inequality makes convergence in the L2 norm imply convergence when paired with every Schwartz function. For f∈L1∩L2 it therefore agrees almost everywhere with the ordinary Fourier transform.
The extension is onto: its image is closed by the norm identity and contains the Schwartz space, since Fourier inversion maps that space to itself. This proves Plancherel theorem:
∥Ff∥22​=2π∥f∥22​,(2π)−1/2F:L2(R)⟶L2(R) is unitary.​
(27)
Its inverse is the extension of the inverse Fourier transform, with limits understood in the L2 norm.
