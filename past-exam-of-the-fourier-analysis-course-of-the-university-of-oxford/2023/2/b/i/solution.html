For real s, the Sobolev space Hs(R) consists of those tempered distributions u whose Fourier transform is represented by a locally square-integrable function and for which
∥u∥Hs2​:=∫R​(1+ξ2)s∣u(ξ)∣2dξ<∞.​
(28)
This is the Sobolev norm used here; inserting the fixed factor (2π)−1 gives an equivalent convention.
For the constant function c, Fourier inversion at zero gives
⟨c,ϕ⟩=c∫ϕ​(x)dx=2πcϕ(0),c=2πcδ0​.
(29)
A nonzero Dirac delta distribution cannot be represented by a locally square-integrable function. Indeed, if G represented 2πcδ0​, choose a test function ρ with ρ(0)=1, and put ρε​(ξ)=ρ(ξ/ε). On a fixed compact interval containing their supports, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives
∣2πc∣=​∫Gρε​​≤∥G∥L2(K)​ε​∥ρ∥2​⟶0.
(30)
Thus c=0. Every Sobolev space requires local square integrability of the Fourier transform, regardless of how negative s is. Therefore
c∈Hs(R) for some s∈R⟺c=0.​
(31)
The zero constant function belongs to every Sobolev space.
