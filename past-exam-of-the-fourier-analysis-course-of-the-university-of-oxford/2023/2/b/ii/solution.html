The distributional derivative is defined by ⟨u′,ϕ⟩=−⟨u,ϕ′⟩. Since differentiation under the integral sign gives (ϕ​)′=−iξϕ​, we obtain
⟨u′,ϕ⟩​=⟨u′,ϕ​⟩=−⟨u,(ϕ​)′⟩=⟨u,iξϕ​⟩=⟨u,iξϕ⟩=⟨iξu,ϕ⟩.​
(32)
This proves the Fourier transform of a derivative identity u′=iξu​ for all tempered distributions.
If u,u′∈Hs(R), their Fourier transforms are represented by locally square-integrable functions, and the identity identifies u′ with iξu almost everywhere. Hence the Sobolev norms satisfy
∥u∥Hs+12​​=∫(1+ξ2)s(1+ξ2)∣u∣2dξ=∫(1+ξ2)s∣u∣2dξ+∫(1+ξ2)s∣u′∣2dξ,​
(33)
