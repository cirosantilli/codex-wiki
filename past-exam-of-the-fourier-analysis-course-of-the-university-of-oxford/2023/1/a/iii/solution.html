A tempered distribution is a continuous linear functional u:S(R)→C. Equivalently, for some integer N and constant C,
∣⟨u,ϕ⟩∣≤C∑0≤m,n≤N​pm,n​(ϕ)(ϕ∈S(R)).

The Fourier transform of a tempered distribution is defined by
⟨u,ϕ⟩:=⟨u,ϕ​⟩.​
(11)
The Fourier bounds for Schwartz functions make the right side a continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space, so u is again a tempered distribution.
For an integrable function f, the regular distribution uf​ acts by ⟨uf​,ϕ⟩=∫fϕ. The estimate ∣⟨uf​,ϕ⟩∣≤∥f∥1​p0,0​(ϕ) proves it is a tempered distribution. Since ∬∣f(x)ϕ(ξ)∣dxdξ<∞, Fubini's theorem gives
⟨uf​​,ϕ⟩=∫f(x)∫ϕ(ξ)e−ixξdξdx=∫f​(ξ)ϕ(ξ)dξ=⟨uf​​,ϕ⟩.
(12)
Thus the distributional Fourier transform agrees with the ordinary Fourier transform.
