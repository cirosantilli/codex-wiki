If α>1, then fα​ is an integrable function, so the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma supplies a continuous representative of its Fourier transform. Equivalently, in the mixture for hα​ the majorant t(α−3)/2e−t is integrable at zero precisely when α>1. In this case dominated convergence also gives

The divergence comes from zero, where the exponent is at most −1. A continuous representative H would agree with hα​ almost everywhere. Since both are continuous on each open half-line, they must agree everywhere on those half-lines: a nonzero continuous difference would be nonzero on an interval of positive Lebesgue measure. The displayed blow-up then contradicts continuity of H at zero. Changing a value at one point cannot remove this singularity. Consequently
fα​​ has a continuous representative on R⟺α>1.​
