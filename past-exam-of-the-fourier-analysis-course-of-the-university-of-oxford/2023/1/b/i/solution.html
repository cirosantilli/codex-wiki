Since 0<fα​≤1, its regular distribution satisfies
∣⟨fα​,ϕ⟩∣≤∥ϕ∥1​≤π(p0,0​(ϕ)+p2,0​(ϕ)),
(13)
so fα​ is a tempered distribution for every α>0.
Put a=α/2. The defining integral of the gamma function, with r=(1+x2)t, gives
fα​(x)=Γ(a)1​∫0∞​ta−1e−te−tx2dt.
(14)
The Fourier transform of a Gaussian suggests the following representative for the Fourier transform of an inverse power of one plus x squared:
hα​(ξ)=Γ(a)π​​∫0∞​ta−3/2e−t−ξ2/(4t)dt(ξ=0).​
(15)
All factors are positive. Tonelli's theorem evaluates its total integral, allowing an infinite value at the single point zero:
∫R​hα​(ξ)dξ​=Γ(a)π​​∫0∞​ta−3/2e−t(∫R​e−ξ2/(4t)dξ)dt=Γ(a)2π​∫0∞​ta−1e−tdt=2π.​
(16)
Assign any finite value at zero if necessary. This gives an integrable function with ∥hα​∥1​=2π.
To verify equality as tempered distributions, take any Schwartz function ϕ. Substituting the mixture into ⟨fα​,ϕ​⟩ is justified by
Γ(a)1​∫0∞​ta−1e−t∫e−tx2∣ϕ​(x)∣dxdt≤∥ϕ​∥1​.
(17)
For each t>0, Fubini's theorem and the Fourier transform of a Gaussian identify the inner pairing with ∫π/t​e−ξ2/(4t)ϕ(ξ)dξ. Interchanging the resulting integrals is valid because their absolute value is bounded by 2π∥ϕ∥∞​ after integrating the positive kernel. Therefore
⟨fα​​,ϕ⟩=∫hα​(ξ)ϕ(ξ)dξ.
(18)
Finally, on a compact set with ∣ξ∣≥δ>0, the mixture integrand is bounded by ta−3/2e−t−δ2/(4t), which is integrable both at zero and at infinity. Dominated convergence proves continuity there. Hence fα​​=hα​∈L1(R), hα​(ξ)>0 and continuous for ξ=0​.
