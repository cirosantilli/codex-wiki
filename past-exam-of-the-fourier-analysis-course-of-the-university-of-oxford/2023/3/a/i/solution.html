The 2π periodization of a Schwartz function is
Pϕ(x)=k∈Z∑​ϕ(x+2πk).​
(38)
For every nonnegative integer j, the Schwartz seminorms give ∣ϕ(j)(x+2πk)∣≤Cj​(1+∣k∣)−2 uniformly for x∈[0,2π]. The Weierstrass M-test gives absolute and uniform convergence there of the defining series and all its differentiated series. Termwise differentiation proves that Pϕ is a smooth function, with (Pϕ)(j)=P(ϕ(j)). Reindexing the absolutely convergent series proves Pϕ(x+2π)=Pϕ(x), so it is a periodic function.
For an explicit smooth periodic partition of unity, choose the bump function
η(x)={exp(−1/(1−(x/(2π))2)),0,​∣x∣<2π,∣x∣≥2π.​
(39)
It is a test function, positive on [−π,π]. The smooth function S(x)=Pη(x) is 2π periodic and strictly positive, since every x has a translate in [−π,π]. Set
Ψ(x)=S(x)η(x)​∈Cc∞​(R),PΨ(x)=S(x)∑k​η(x+2πk)​=1.​
(40)
Division preserves smoothness because S>0, and the support remains contained in [−2π,2π].
