Put h(x)=(α2+x2)−1 and form its period-one periodization of an integrable function
H(x)=∑k∈Z​h(x+k).
(54)
The function h is not a Schwartz function, so justify the needed Poisson summation formula directly. For every j≥0, its derivative satisfies h(j)(x)=O(∣x∣−2−j) as ∣x∣→∞. Thus the Weierstrass M-test gives uniform convergence on [0,1] of this series and each differentiated series. It follows that H is a smooth period-one periodic function.
Absolute integrability and Fubini's theorem allow its Fourier coefficients to be computed by splitting the real line into unit intervals:
bn​​=∫01​H(x)e−2πinxdx=k∑​∫kk+1​h(y)e−2πinydy=h(2πn)=απ​e−2πα∣n∣.​
(55)
These Fourier coefficients are absolutely summable. Their Fourier series defines a continuous periodic function with the same Fourier coefficients as H; uniqueness of Fourier coefficients in L1 identifies the two almost everywhere, and continuity then identifies them everywhere. Evaluating at zero gives the rigorously justified Poisson summation formula
α21​+2∑n=1∞​α2+n21​=H(0)=απ​(1+2∑n=1∞​e−2παn).
(56)
Rearranging proves
n=1∑∞​α2+n21​=2απ​−2α21​+απ​n=1∑∞​e−2παn(α>0).​
(57)
The last series is a geometric series with sum (e2πα−1)−1. Equivalently, the left side is πcoth(πα)/(2α)−1/(2α2), where coth is the hyperbolic cotangent.
