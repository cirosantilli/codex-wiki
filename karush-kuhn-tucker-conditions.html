Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions (∇x​L=0,λi​gi​=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions combine membership of the feasible set with a balance of gradients and complementary slackness. For a minimization problem with differentiable functions on an open subset of Rn, write
minx​f(x),gi​(x)≤0 (1≤i≤m),hj​(x)=0 (1≤j≤p),
(69)
and use the optimization Lagrangian
L(x,λ,μ)=f(x)+∑i=1m​λi​gi​(x)+∑j=1p​μj​hj​(x).
(70)
A candidate (x∗​,λ,μ) satisfies the KKT conditions exactly when all four requirements hold:
  • Primal feasibility: gi​(x∗​)≤0 and hj​(x∗​)=0, so x∗​ is a feasible point.
  • Dual sign feasibility: λi​≥0. The equality Lagrange multipliers μj​ are unrestricted real numbers.
  • Stationarity: the gradient of the optimization Lagrangian in the decision variables vanishes,
    ∇x​L(x∗​,λ,μ)=∇f(x∗​)+∑i​λi​∇gi​(x∗​)+∑j​μj​∇hj​(x∗​)=0.
    (71)
  • Complementary slackness: λi​gi​(x∗​)=0 for every inequality optimization constraint.[ref]
The sign of each inequality Lagrange multiplier follows the chosen convention gi​≤0: on the feasible set, λi​gi​≤0 makes the optimization Lagrangian a lower bound on the objective function. An equality contributes zero on the feasible set for either sign of its Lagrange multiplier. Complementary slackness says an inequality with gi​(x∗​)<0 must have λi​=0, while λi​>0 implies an active constraint. An active constraint can still have zero Lagrange multiplier; the quadratic minimization with a moving upper bound below gives an explicit example.
The name records William Karush's 1939 master's thesis and the 1951 work of Harold W. Kuhn and Albert W. Tucker.[ref]
