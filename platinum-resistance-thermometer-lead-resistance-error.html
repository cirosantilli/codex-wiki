With two shared leads of electrical resistances r1​,r2​, Ohm's law gives
Vtwo​=I[R(t)+r1​+r2​],Rindicated​=R(t)+r1​+r2​.
(21)
Using the Pt100 local linear model, positive lead electrical resistance appears as a warmer temperature. At true 25∘C, stipulated leads of 0.50Ω each change 109.625Ω into 110.625Ω, producing 27.597…∘C: a calculated bias of about 2.60∘C. Subtracting a measured 1.00Ω loop correction works only while that loop electrical resistance remains appropriate; cable temperature changes can invalidate it.
Four-terminal sensing separates the electric current and electric potential difference leads. Negligible sensing electric current suppresses sensing-lead drops; force-lead drops lie outside the interval. Real voltmeter loading, connections, and leakage still need checking.[ref]
For a stable offset E from the thermoelectric effect, two signed electric potential differences obey V+​=IR+E and V−​=−IR+E. The current-reversal calculation in four-terminal sensing yields
R=2IV+​−V−​​.
(22)
At stipulated I=1mA, R=109.625Ω and E=3μV, V+​=109.628mV and V−​=−109.622mV recover R exactly in this model. If the offset or sample temperature changes between readings, cancellation is incomplete. Reversal also leaves Joule heating unchanged because it depends on I2.
