Vertex of a linear polyhedron (rankAI(p)​=n)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Mathematical optimization Linear programming Linear polyhedron
OurBigBook.com Words: 139
For a linear polyhedron P={x∈Rn:Ax≤b}, a vertex of a linear polyhedron is a feasible point p at which the normals of the active constraints span Rn. Equivalently, n linearly independent active constraints uniquely determine p. A linear polyhedron may be unbounded or lower-dimensional; the definition applies in those cases as well as to a convex polytope.
Such a point is an extreme point. Indeed, if p=λu+(1−λ)v with u,v∈P and 0<λ<1, every active constraint at p must also be an equality at u and v. Selecting n independent active rows forces u=v=p. Conversely, if the active normals do not span Rn, take a nonzero vector h annihilated by all active rows. The finitely many inactive inequalities have positive slack, so both p+εh and p−εh remain feasible for sufficiently small ε>0. Their midpoint is p, so p is not an extreme point. Thus these notions coincide for a linear polyhedron.
