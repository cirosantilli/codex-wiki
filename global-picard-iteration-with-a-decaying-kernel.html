Global Picard iteration with a decaying kernel (4∣b∣<c)

 ... Area of mathematics Analysis Differential equation Ordinary differential equation Picard-Lindelöf theorem Picard iteration
OurBigBook.com Words: 161
Consider the Volterra integral equation
y(t)=bh(t)+∫0t​K(t,s)y(s)2ds,t≥0,
(57)
where h is continuous, ∣h∣≤1, h(0)=1, and the continuous kernel satisfies 0≤K(t,s)≤e−c(t−s) for 0≤s≤t, with c>0. If 0<∣b∣<c/4, there is exactly one continuous solution on the whole half-line. It is bounded, and Picard iteration beginning at y0​=bh converges in supremum norm with contraction ratio at most γ=4∣b∣/c<1.
For a proof, the integral of the kernel is at most 1/c. Thus T maps the closed ball of radius 2∣b∣ in the Banach space of bounded continuous functions into itself, since ∥Ty∥∞​≤∣b∣+4b2/c<2∣b∣. Factoring y2−v2 gives ∥Ty−Tv∥∞​≤γ∥y−v∥∞​ on this ball. The contraction mapping theorem gives its unique fixed point. A first-exit argument shows that every continuous solution, even one not initially assumed bounded, stays strictly inside the same ball: a first time with ∣y∣=2∣b∣ would contradict ∣y∣≤∣b∣+4b2/c. Reapplying the estimate gives the strict global bound ∥y∥∞​≤∣b∣(1+γ)<2∣b∣, so uniqueness is unrestricted within the continuous solution class. If b=0, zero is the only solution by the Gronwall inequality on each finite interval.
