Boolean valuation

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Mathematical logic Propositional logic
OurBigBook.com Words: 277 Articles: 1
A Boolean valuation v assigns each primitive proposition a value in {F,T}, equivalently {0,1}, and extends recursively to every propositional formula using the classical logical connectives. The same primitive proposition always receives the same value within one Boolean valuation. For compound propositional formulas A and B,
v(¬A)=Tv(A∧B)=Tv(A∨B)=Tv(A→B)=F​⟺v(A)=F,⟺v(A)=T and v(B)=T,⟺v(A)=T or v(B)=T,⟺v(A)=T and v(B)=F.​
(11)
Every other case receives the opposite value. Thus v(p)=T,v(q)=F gives v((p→q)∧p)=F. A truth table varies the assignment; recursive evaluation computes one row. The truth-functional restriction means that changing what the primitive propositions describe, while retaining their values, cannot change the value of the compound propositional formula. See forall x: Calgary, chapter 9 and section 10.1.
