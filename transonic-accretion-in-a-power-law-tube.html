Transonic accretion in a power-law tube

 ... Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Compressible flow Sonic point Transonic branch
OurBigBook.com Words: 142
Consider steady isentropic flow toward a Newtonian gravitational potential −GM/r through a tube of cross-sectional area A(r)=Crn. Write v>0 for the inward speed and use a polytropic equation of state with specific-heat ratio γ>1. Mass conservation and the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid give
ρvA=M˙,(v−vcs2​​)v′=rncs2​​−r2GM​,
(5)
where cs​ is the adiabatic sound speed. A regular sonic point therefore has vs​=cs​ and rs​=GM/(ncs2​). The Bernoulli equation, matched to a nearly stationary reservoir with sound speed c0​, gives
2v2​+γ−1c2​−rGM​=γ−1c02​​,cs2​=(2n+1)−(2n−1)γ2c02​​.
(6)
For n>1/2, a finite positive sonic point requires 1<γ<(2n+1)/(2n−1). Differentiating the flow equation at that point, with x=rs​vs′​/cs​, gives
(γ+1)x2+2n(γ−1)x+n2(γ−1)−n=0.
(7)
Its discriminant is 4n[(2n+1)−(2n−1)γ], so the same bound permits real regular slopes. The branch on which the Mach number rises inward selects the negative sign in
x=γ+1−n(γ−1)±n[(2n+1)−(2n−1)γ]​​.
(8)
For a spherical tube n=2, this recovers the 5/3 threshold of Bondi accretion; for a dipolar flux-tube area, n=3 gives 7/5. The tube approximation must remain valid between the reservoir matching region and the accretor.
