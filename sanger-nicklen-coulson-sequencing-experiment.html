Sanger-Nicklen-Coulson sequencing experiment

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics DNA sequencing Sanger sequencing
OurBigBook.com Words: 183
In 1977, Frederick Sanger, S. Nicklen and Alan Coulson tested chain termination on Phi X 174 DNA. They isolated restriction enzyme fragments as primers, heated them with template to 100∘C for three minutes and annealed at 67∘C for thirty minutes. Four extension mixtures used the Klenow fragment of DNA polymerase and radioactive dATP. Their Figure 1 used ddGTP, ddATP, ddTTP and araCTP, an alternative terminating substrate; Figure 2 used all four dideoxy types. This distinction matters when relating the original experiments to an all-ddNTP teaching model.[ref]
Figure 1 used a 12% acrylamide gel at 40mA for fourteen hours, read by autoradiography. Omitting the unlabeled dATP chase caused unwanted A stops across samples: a chemistry scientific control. Ordered bands supported local nucleotide sequences; comparisons could revise provisional assignments. Weak T bands, unresolved long runs and structure-dependent migration limited interpretation. The paper's observations are evidence from particular preparations, not a guarantee that every endpoint is resolved.[ref]
