For a positive-definite weighted inner product on a compact interval, a least-squares polynomial is the orthogonal projection of a function onto the finite-dimensional space Πn​ of polynomials of degree at most n. In a basis ϕ0​,…,ϕn​, its coefficients solve the normal equations Gc=d, where Gij​=(ϕi​,ϕj​) and di​=(u,ϕi​). The Gram matrix is positive definite: zTGz=∥∑zi​ϕi​∥2>0 for z=0. Thus the coefficients exist and are unique. The residual is orthogonal to every candidate polynomial, giving ∥u−q∥2=∥u−pn​∥2+∥pn​−q∥2 and proving optimality. An orthogonal basis makes these equations diagonal, as in a least-squares polynomial in an orthogonal-polynomial basis.
