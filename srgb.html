sRGB

sRGB specifies a red-green-blue colour encoding. Restricting attention to component values in [0,1], its encoded component s corresponds to the linear-light component
D(s)={s/12.92,((s+0.055)/1.055)2.4,​0≤s≤0.04045,0.04045<s≤1.​
(2)
Thus the conversion is piecewise, not a single power law. The reverse conversion used here is
E(l)={12.92l,1.055l1/2.4−0.055,​0≤l≤0.0031308,0.0031308<l≤1.​
(3)
These conventional rounded thresholds are used by the W3C conversion routines; they give a tiny mismatch at the join rather than a mathematically exact pair of inverses everywhere.[ref][ref]
For an eight-bit neutral grayscale code q, use s=q/255 in all three components. Its relative luminance is then D(s). For example, D(0.5)≈0.21404, whereas E(0.5)≈0.73536. This is why averaging encoded values and averaging linear light give different answers. sRGB is a specified interpretation; merely calling a raster image grayscale does not imply it uses sRGB.
