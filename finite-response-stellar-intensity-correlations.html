This rectangular-gate calculation specifies a counting model. An analog instrument's amplifier bandwidth and impulse response instead weight the fluctuations it actually passes; the Sirius amplifiers were bandpass devices. Both require measuring the response and calibrating the zero-baseline excess. When p, ρj​, and the temporal response are unchanged, the ratio of excesses recovers V2:

Narrower optical bandwidth lengthens optical coherence time, whereas wider usable electronic bandwidth can resolve faster fluctuations; these are distinct bandwidths. Background changes, unequal spectral responses, drift, or averaging over changing interferometric baselines can invalidate a common calibration.
