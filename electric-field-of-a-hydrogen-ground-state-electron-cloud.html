For spherical charge density ρe​=−ee−2r/a0​/(πa03​), integrate 4πs2ρe​(s) from zero to r to obtain the displayed enclosed charge. Gauss's law then gives Ee​=Qe​(r)/(4πϵ0​r2), directed inward. Near the origin Ee​≃−er/(3πϵ0​a03​); at large radius it approaches the field of charge −e.
Adding a point nucleus of charge +e leaves the unperturbed total field
Eatom​(r)=4πϵ0​ee−2r/a0​​(r21​+a0​r2​+a02​2​).

It is exponentially small rather than identically zero, because the electron cloud has no compact boundary. This frozen-density field alone does not describe the dominant long-range response to another charge: charge-induced dipole attraction requires allowing the atom to polarize.
