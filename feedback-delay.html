Feedback delay (τ)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Control theory Closed-loop control
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 3
A feedback delay means a controller reacts to an earlier plant output. An accurate measurement can still be stale: when the plant output has already crossed the reference signal, a controller using the earlier value may continue pushing it away from the target. In a pure constant-delay model, ym​(t)=y(t−τ) with τ≥0. This isolates timing from measurement noise; it does not describe every sampling or communication mechanism. [ref]
For the ideal scalar controlled plant x˙=u, a zero reference signal, no control disturbance, and proportional control with constant feedback gain k>0, the equations are
y(t)=x(t),ym​(t)=x(t−τ),u(t)=−kym​(t),x′(t)=−kx(t−τ).
(29)
With τ=0, this is an ordinary differential equation with exponential decay x(t)=x(0)e−kt. With τ>0, it is a delay differential equation, requiring an initial history of delayed feedback. The delay stability of proportional integrator control depends on the product kτ: even this simple controlled plant loses asymptotic stability at kτ=π/2.
