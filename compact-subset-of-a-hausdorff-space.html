Compact subset of a Hausdorff space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Hausdorff space
OurBigBook.com Words: 450 Articles: 2
Let K⊆X, where the ambient topological space X is Hausdorff and K is compact in its subspace topology. Then K is a closed set in X: the complement X∖K is an open set in X. The ambient space need not be compact. For example, [0,1] is a compact closed interval in the non-compact Hausdorff real line.[ref]
The ambient Hausdorff hypothesis matters even when K itself is Hausdorff. In the Sierpiński space S={0,1}, the open sets are ∅,{1},S, so the closed sets are ∅,{0},S. Thus K={1} is compact and Hausdorff in its one-point subspace topology, but is not closed in S: its complement {0} is not an open set. Every open neighborhood of 0 contains 1, so separation from K fails. In contrast, the discrete topology on the same underlying set is Hausdorff and makes both singletons closed.
The diagram lists every open set and closed set, so the distinction can be checked directly. All subsets in both panels are compact: for any open cover of a finite set, choose one covering open set per point, obtaining a finite subcover. Compactness alone therefore cannot distinguish the two panels.
Here is the finite-selection mechanism in the general proof. For x∈X∖K and each k∈K, the Hausdorff property gives disjoint open neighborhoods Uk​ of k and Vk​ of x. The Uk​ give an ambient open cover of K. Select a finite subcover Uk1​​,…,Ukm​​ and set
U=⋃j=1m​Ukj​​,V=⋂j=1m​Vkj​​.
(31)
Then K⊆U, x∈V, and U∩V=∅. The finite intersection makes V an open set; an infinite intersection would not guarantee this. Thus every point of X∖K has an open neighborhood contained in that complement, proving that K is closed. The empty set is already closed.
This theorem combines with the continuous image of a compact space theorem to make every continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space a closed map: a closed subset of its domain is compact, and its image is compact and hence closed in the target. This is the reason the inverse in the compact-to-Hausdorff continuous bijection theorem is continuous. The converse implication, from closed to compact, needs additional hypotheses: the real line is closed in itself but not compact; in a compact space, every closed subset is compact.
