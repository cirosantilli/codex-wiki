Wet-ice bias in fusion calorimetry (Lapp​=(1−r)L−Qenv​/ma​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Latent heat of fusion Ice-melting calorimetry
OurBigBook.com Words: 276
Surface water counted as ice needs warming but has already undergone melting. Its presence therefore biases a dry-ice estimate of latent heat of fusion downward. This is why drying the added ice is a quantitative control in ice-melting calorimetry, rather than cosmetic preparation. Let ma​ be the total added mass at Tm​, of which a fraction r is already liquid and (1−r) remains solid. The correct constant-property balance is
(mw​cw​+Ccal​)(Ti​−Tf​)+Qenv​=(1−r)ma​L+ma​cw​(Tf​−Tm​).
(14)
If an analysis incorrectly treats all ma​ as solid and sets Qenv​=0, it obtains
Lapp​=ma​(mw​cw​+Ccal​)(Ti​−Tf​)​−cw​(Tf​−Tm​)=(1−r)L−ma​Qenv​​.
(15)
Thus r=0.10 gives a 10% low estimate with no heat leak. Unaccounted incoming heat also lowers the estimate; outgoing heat raises it. These signs follow directly from the displayed energy balance. Conversely, dry ice initially below Tm​ absorbs additional sensible heat; neglecting that term overestimates L. These are different biases and may partially cancel, so agreement with a reference value alone does not validate the controls.
For this model with r=0 and Qenv​=0, the balance predicts Tf​−Tm​≃8.829K, with 16.700kJ consumed by fusion and 1.845kJ by warming the melted water. At r=0.10 with the same zero leak, it predicts Tf​−Tm​≃10.369K and Lapp​=300.6kJkg−1. A net incoming 1kJ shifts Lapp​ downward by a further 20kJkg−1 because ma​=0.050kg. The warmer final water in the wet case is evidence of a smaller fusion budget, not a larger true L.
