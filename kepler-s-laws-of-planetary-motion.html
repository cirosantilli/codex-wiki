Kepler's laws of planetary motion

 Home Physics Mathematical physics
OurBigBook.com Words: 954 Articles: 4
Kepler's laws describe three connected features of an elliptic orbit: its shape, how fast it is traversed, and how its size determines its orbital period. Kepler's first law gives an ellipse with the attracting centre at one focus of a conic; Kepler's second law gives equal swept areas in equal times; Kepler's third law relates the squared orbital period to the cubed semi-major axis. Together they turn a drawing of a Kepler orbit into a prediction of where the bodies will be and when they will return. [ref]
Here the mathematical model is an isolated two-body problem in Newtonian gravity, with point masses M,m>0 and a bound, noncolliding elliptic orbit. Let r=rm​−rM​ be the relative displacement, r=∣r∣ the separation, and μ=G(M+m) the gravitational parameter, where G is the gravitational constant. Newton's law of universal gravitation then gives
r¨=−r3μ​r.
(50)
The relative Kepler orbit has semi-major axis a and orbital eccentricity 0≤e<1. Both bodies move around their shared astronomical barycenter, the system's centre of mass. In its rest frame their displacements are rm​=Mr/(M+m) and rM​=−mr/(M+m). Their individual semi-major axes therefore add to a; the relative ellipse's attracting focus of a conic represents zero separation, rather than a body fixed in space.
One example will connect all three laws. After nondimensionalization, choose a=1, e=0.6 and μ=1. The semi-minor axis is b=a1−e2​=0.8, while the periapsis distance and apoapsis distance are
rp​=a(1−e)=0.4,ra​=a(1+e)=1.6.
(51)
Thus a=(0.4+1.6)/2=1 measures the ellipse's size even though the separation changes fourfold. The specific angular momentum will be 0.8, the two endpoint speeds 2 and 0.5, and the orbital period 2π. These are values in the chosen scaled units, not measurements of a particular planet.
Johannes Kepler published Kepler's first law and Kepler's second law in Astronomia Nova in 1609 and Kepler's third law in Harmonices Mundi in 1619. These publication dates are documented by [ref]. Their appeal is that three short statements organize both geometry and timing; Newtonian gravity supplies a common dynamical explanation.
