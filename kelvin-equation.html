Kelvin equation (ln(p/psat​)≃2Γvl​/(RkB​T))

OurBigBook.com Words: 132 Articles: 1
In the ideal-vapor, incompressible-liquid capillarity approximation, a spherical droplet of radius R has the displayed equilibrium vapor-pressure relation. Here vl​ is liquid volume per molecule, Γ is surface tension, and psat​ is the flat-interface saturation pressure at the same temperature. The Young–Laplace equation raises liquid pressure by 2Γ/R, increasing its chemical potential by approximately 2Γvl​/R. Matching this with the ideal-vapor chemical-potential increase kB​Tln(p/psat​) proves the formula. The liquid contribution from the vapor-pressure change itself is neglected relative to its curvature pressure, and bulk properties are taken independent of radius. A convex small drop therefore requires a higher ambient vapor pressure than a flat interface.
