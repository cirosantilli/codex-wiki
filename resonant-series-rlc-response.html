Resonant series RLC response (Iamp​(ω0​)=V0​/R)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit RLC circuit Series RLC circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 360
Drive a series RLC circuit with electric potential difference V(t)=V0​cos(ωt), where V0​>0 and ω>0. After the free transient decays, the electric current has the same angular frequency as the source:
iss​(t)=Iamp​cos(ωt−ϕ),Iamp​(ω)=R2+(ωL−1/(ωC))2​V0​​,tanϕ=RωL−1/(ωC)​,−2π​<ϕ<2π​.
(25)
For fixed positive R,L,C,V0​, the electric current amplitude is largest exactly at ω=ω0​=1/LC​. The two electrical reactance contributions cancel there, leaving only the drop from electrical resistance. In particular,
iss​(t)=RV0​​cos(ω0​t),qss​(t)=Rω0​V0​​sin(ω0​t).
(26)
Every solution approaches this periodic response because its difference from the displayed solution is a decaying free response. The peak remains at ω0​ even for an overdamped RLC response; free ringing is not required for a maximum of driven electric current. [ref].
For example, stipulate L=10mH, C=1μF, R=20Ω, and V0​=1V. Then ω0​=104s−1, f0​=ω0​/(2π)≈1591.55Hz, and the peak electric current amplitude is 50mA. Holding the source amplitude fixed is essential when comparing responses at different frequencies.
Three frequencies must be distinguished. The free underdamped RLC response rings at ωd​=ω02​−α2​. Driven electric current peaks at ω0​. Driven electric charge has amplitude
qamp​(ω)=ωIamp​(ω)​=(ω02​−ω2)2+4α2ω2​V0​/L​,
(27)
whose peak occurs at ω02​−2α2​ only if α<ω0​/2​. Otherwise it decreases from its zero-frequency limit CV0​, with no positive-frequency peak. The different peak locations follow because electric current is the time derivative of electric charge, multiplying a sinusoidal amplitude by ω. The limiting responses are Iamp​∼V0​Cω as ω→0+ and Iamp​∼V0​/(Lω) as ω→∞.
The Q factor measures selectivity:
Q=RLω0​​=2αω0​​.
(28)
The average Joule heating power is RIamp2​/2. Its half-maximum angular frequencies and their separation are exactly
ω±​=ω02​+α2​±α,Δω=ω+​−ω−​=2α=LR​=Qω0​​.
(29)
These follow by setting ∣ωL−1/(ωC)∣=R. In the numerical example, Q=5 and Δω=2000s−1. Reducing R makes a taller, narrower electric current peak; varying C tunes the selected frequency. Measuring the drop vR​=Ri gives a band-pass filter that suppresses very low and very high frequencies while passing a range near resonance. This is useful for selecting a radio signal, though the range must be broad enough to pass its modulation. [ref].
