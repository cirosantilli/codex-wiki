Curvature of a plane curve (κ)

OurBigBook.com Words: 421 Articles: 4
For a twice continuously differentiable regular curve r(t)=(x(t),y(t)) in the plane, unsigned curvature is the absolute value of its signed curvature:
κ(t)=(x′(t)2+y′(t)2)3/2∣x′(t)y′′(t)−y′(t)x′′(t)∣​.
(65)
The nonvanishing velocity vector makes the denominator positive. For a graph of a function (t,f(t)), this reduces to ∣f′′(t)∣/(1+f′(t)2)3/2; omitting the absolute value gives the signed curvature of a plane graph for increasing t.
For the parabola r(t)=(t,t2), the derivatives are r′=(1,2t) and r′′=(0,2). Therefore
v=1+4t2​,k(t)=κ(t)=(1+4t2)3/22​,ρ(t)=2(1+4t2)3/2​.
(66)
The curvature is greatest at the vertex t=0, where κ=2 and the radius of curvature is 1/2. At t=±1/2 the curvature is 1/2​, so the parabola bends less sharply despite its larger speed 2​.
Its unit tangent vector and left unit normal are
T=1+4t2​(1,2t)​,NL​=1+4t2​(−2t,1)​.
(67)
Since k>0, NL​ is also its principal normal vector. At t=0 the osculating circle consequently has center (0,0)+(1/2)(0,1)=(0,1/2) and equation
x2+(y−1/2)2=1/4.
(68)
The lower branch y=1/2−1/4−x2​ has value 0, derivative 0, and second derivative 2 at x=0, matching the parabola y=x2 through second order. Away from the vertex the two curves separate: an osculating circle describes a local bend, not the whole curve.
Vanishing curvature at one point does not imply a straight segment. The graph of a function y=x3 has signed curvature 6x/(1+9x4)3/2, which vanishes at its inflection point x=0 and changes sign across it. By contrast, if curvature vanishes throughout a connected regular interval, dT/ds=0 throughout that interval: the constant unit tangent vector forces the image to lie on a straight line.
