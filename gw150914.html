GW150914

GW150914 is the first directly detected gravitational wave, interpreted as a binary black hole inspiral, merger and ringdown. Its discovery paper reports a peak projected gravitational wave strain of approximately 10−21 and a combined matched filter signal-to-noise ratio of 24. The inferred source-frame component masses were 36−4+5​ and 29−4+4​ solar masses, with 90 percent credible intervals. These are model-dependent estimates from a detector network.[ref]
The interesting measurement is a chain: curvature changes relative light travel times, wave interference turns those changes into a photodetector signal, calibrated records from separate sites constrain a common source, and statistical inference tests competing explanations.
