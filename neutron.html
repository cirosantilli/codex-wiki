Neutron (n)

 Home Physics Subatomic particle Hadron Baryon Nucleon
OurBigBook.com Words: 4k Articles: 33
The neutron is the electrically neutral spin-one-half baryon found with protons in most atomic nuclei.
A neutron is a nucleon: it contributes to an atomic nucleus without adding electric charge. Its spin one-half makes it a fermion, and it belongs to the baryons. Electrical neutrality does not mean an absence of interactions: a neutron can transfer momentum and kinetic energy to an atomic nucleus, producing a charged nuclear recoil that a particle detector can register. This indirect route is central to neutron detection by proton recoil.
The beautiful feature of the Chadwick neutron discovery experiment is that an unseen projectile acquired a measurable mass through the motion of things it struck. Penetration alone left competing explanations open; combining nuclear recoils from different target masses supplied a much stronger test.

Prompt neutron

A neutron released on the short timescale associated with the fission event, rather than after a precursor radioactive transformation. Prompt describes emission timing, rather than the neutron speed after subsequent scattering.

Delayed neutron

Words: 103 Articles: 2
Neutron OurBigBook.com
A neutron emitted following a radioactive transformation of a precursor, rather than promptly in the original nuclear fission. The waiting time reflects the precursor lifetime. It is distinct from the later detection of a prompt neutron after scattering or moderation.

The fraction of fission-related neutron production assigned to delayed neutrons under the chosen averaging convention. Effective kinetics parameters can include spatial and spectral weighting; a small fraction can still have a large influence on population timescales.

Neutron scattering

Words: 1k Articles: 12
Neutron OurBigBook.com
Neutron scattering measures how a specimen changes the directions and kinetic energies of incident neutrons. Neutron diffraction uses the elastic scattering pattern to investigate crystal structure and magnetic order; inelastic neutron scattering uses energy transfer to investigate excitations such as phonons and magnons. The two measurements ask complementary questions: what arrangement repeats in space, and what disturbances can propagate through it?
The 1994 Nobel Prize in Physics recognized Clifford Glenwood Shull's diffraction work and Bertram Neville Brockhouse's spectroscopy.[ref]
Nuclear scattering couples to atomic nuclei; magnetic scattering couples the neutron's magnetic moment to electronic magnetic moments. The latter underlies both magnetic neutron diffraction and magnon spectroscopy. An atomic number is not a nuclear neutron scattering weight: the relevant coefficient in the elementary coherent model is the nuclear neutron scattering length.

Perpendicular neutron recoil and phonon absorption (ℏω=ℏ2Q2/(2mn​))

Define transfer gained by the neutron as Q=kf​−ki​. If it is perpendicular to ki​, expansion of the free-neutron kinetic energy gives Ef​−Ei​=ℏ2Q2/(2mn​). Absorbing one phonon requires this positive energy to equal ℏω. A periodic phonon branch may therefore intersect a nonperiodic recoil parabola in an extended-zone representation. The sign of Q is opposite to the common sample-transfer convention and must be declared.

Neutron diffraction

Words: 366 Articles: 2
Neutron scattering OurBigBook.com
In neutron diffraction, coherent elastic scattering adds scattering amplitudes from regularly positioned scatterers. Since Ef​=Ei​, the neutron scattering kinematics gives kf​=ki​=2π/λ and
Q=λ4π​sinθ.
(5)
For lattice planes of interplanar spacing dp​, constructive addition requires Q=2πn/dp​ along the plane normal. Equating these expressions proves Bragg's law:
2dp​sinθ=nλ.
(6)
For example, stipulate dp​=2 ångströms and λ=2 ångströms. The first diffraction order has θ=30∘ and detector deflection 60∘. A peak position constrains a period; its intensity also depends on the crystal basis structure factor.
Clifford Glenwood Shull describes powdered sodium chloride, a crystal monochromator, and a shielded counter recording angular counts. Inserting cadmium measured background; substantial diffuse scattering complicated peak interpretation.[ref]
Powdering supplies many crystal orientations. Comparing the angular peaks with Bragg's law constrains crystal structure; a background estimate helps distinguish specimen peaks from unwanted counts. A two-axis diffraction instrument has no separate outgoing energy analysis, so an angular pattern alone does not separate every inelastic contribution. The richer magnetic example, including a warm/cold comparison and its limits, is the manganese monoxide neutron diffraction experiment. Its extra peaks reveal magnetic order beyond the chemical periodicity; the associated magnetic neutron diffraction model is developed there.

Nuclear neutron scattering length (b)

The nuclear neutron scattering length is the effective length coefficient for low-energy nuclear scattering amplitude. This coefficient describes the atomic nucleus, including its isotope and nuclear spin dependence; it is not proportional to atomic number. Inelastic neutron scattering

Words: 646 Articles: 2
Neutron scattering OurBigBook.com
Inelastic neutron scattering resolves Ei​−Ef​=0. Through conservation of energy, a peak at positive ℏω identifies an allowed excitation energy; through momentum conservation, its location in Q constrains the spatial variation of that excitation. A phonon is a quantum of lattice vibration; a magnon is a quantum of a spin wave. Their different couplings make energy transfer alone insufficient to identify which was created.
Bertram Neville Brockhouse's aluminium experiment rotated a large single crystal, scanning analyzer angle for neutron groups. His vanadium comparison exposed the elastic resolution. Later magnetite measurements used a magnetic field intensity test to identify magnons.[ref]
Successive orientations sample different wave vectors; analyzer settings distinguish outgoing energies. This supplies points on a dispersion relation, rather than only an angular diffraction pattern. Finite resolution can merge neighboring excitations; background, multiple scattering and processes involving several phonons can complicate interpretation. A lattice model is a testable explanation of selected features, not a guarantee that every spectral peak has the same origin.

Triple-axis spectrometer

Words: 173
Inelastic neutron scattering OurBigBook.com
A triple-axis spectrometer uses Bragg's law at an initial crystal monochromator to select λi​ and at a second analyzing crystal to select λf​. The sample sits between these selectors. The three axes refer to the monochromator, sample and analyzer rotations. The final counter measures accepted counts; the analyzer's wavelength selection makes those counts sensitive to outgoing energy even when the counter does not measure each neutron's energy directly.
For a constant-Q scan, change incident or outgoing energy while coordinating sample and analyzer settings so that ki​−kf​ stays fixed. Counts versus Ei​−Ef​ then probe excitations at one momentum transfer. Changing only the analyzer angle generally changes both transferred quantities. Incident-beam monitoring, analyzer response and the instrumental resolution must enter quantitative interpretation; the diagram's arrows specify information flow, not an absolute intensity calibration.

(12)
This chosen broadening illustrates readout width; it is not a measured resolution or a full spectrometer resolution function. Integrating over all ΔE gives w(x). The small negative-energy tail is a broadening artifact, not a zero-temperature phonon-annihilation signal.
These panels separate three kinds of information: allowed reciprocal lattice positions, cancellation within a crystal basis structure factor, and an excitation's energy as a function of wave vector. The chain has no magnetic degrees of freedom, optical branches, damping or multi-phonon processes. It illustrates how one excitation could be read; it proves nothing about the completeness of a real material's excitation spectrum.

Chadwick neutron discovery experiment

Words: 393
Neutron OurBigBook.com
Walther Bothe and Herbert Becker observed penetrating radiation from beryllium struck by alpha particles before James Chadwick's interpretation.[ref]
Irène Joliot-Curie and Frédéric Joliot found that adding material containing hydrogen increased ionization, interpreting the ejected protons as Compton scattering recoils from extremely energetic gamma rays. James Chadwick tested this interpretation against nuclear recoils in other elements.[ref]
The counter coupled a small ionization chamber to an amplifier and oscillograph: charged particles produced ions, and the electrical signal gave a deflection. With Norman Feather, James Chadwick also observed nitrogen recoil tracks in a cloud chamber.[ref]
The apparatus used polonium on silver facing beryllium inside an evacuable vessel. Paraffin wax before the aluminium counter window supplied recoil protons; intervening aluminium screens measured their particle range. Deflections were photographed on bromide paper. The first counter contained air; later fillings included hydrogen, helium, nitrogen, oxygen and argon. Counts with wax absent included gas recoils; wax insertion added entering protons. Thick lead between source and counter hardly changed the initial counts, testing penetration against the low background.[ref]
The diagram separates two routes to a signal: a proton entering from the wax and a nuclear recoil created inside the chamber gas. Its lower row is a logical summary, rather than a reconstructed experimental trace. In either route the observed ionization belongs to a charged secondary particle; identifying the incident radiation requires an additional collision model.
In the February letter, a photon interpretation requiring about 52MeV for the protons predicted only about 0.4MeV for nitrogen recoils, below the observed ionization. Here 1MeV=106 electronvolts. James Chadwick proposed a neutral particle near the proton mass and the nuclear reaction
49​Be+24​He⟶612​C+01​n.
(13)
These are the letter's approximate historical values, not a modern mass determination.[ref]
Why compare two materials? A gamma ray is a photon, so its incident momentum is Eγ​/c, where c is the speed of light. A slow massive projectile instead has momentum mv and kinetic energy mv2/2. The relationship between incoming momentum and energy therefore changes. An elastic collision model must explain the recoil endpoints for both target masses with the same incident projectile population. Calling the radiation neutral supplies no such agreement by itself.
The original measurements depended on particle range calibration, material in the path, detector response and whether a recoil was fully stopped in the sensitive gas. Large deflections alone need not specify a complete recoil energy. The calculations below expose the ideal collision assumptions behind the inference; applying them to a measured spectrum requires checking those assumptions.

Neutron detection by proton recoil

Words: 728 Articles: 2
Neutron OurBigBook.com
In neutron detection by proton recoil, a neutron strikes a hydrogen atomic nucleus, namely a proton, and the moving proton produces detectable ionization. Paraffin wax provides many hydrogen targets. There are two distinct questions: how much kinetic energy can a collision transfer, and how often does it occur? Elastic collision kinematics answers the first. It does not determine a collision cross-section or a detector's efficiency.
For the following derivations the target is initially at rest in the laboratory but is free to recoil. Its mass is finite. In particular a proton must not be treated as an immovable target. All massive particles are nonrelativistic particles, their translational kinetic energies are mv2/2, and the collision changes no internal state. Target binding and thermal motion are neglected relative to the incident kinetic energy. These assumptions suit an introductory description of fast-neutron elastic collisions; they do not cover every neutron capture or other nuclear reaction.

Maximum elastic recoil energy fraction (fmax​=4mM/(m+M)2)

Words: 261

Let a projectile have mass m, incident speed v, and incident kinetic energy E=mv2/2. The stationary target has mass M. In a head-on elastic collision, the signed outgoing projectile velocity v′ and target velocity u obey momentum conservation and conservation of energy:
mv=mv′+Mu,21​mv2=21​mv′2+21​Mu2.
(14)

2mp2​=2mp2+q2−2pqcosθ​+2Mq2​,q=m+M2Mpcosθ​.
(16)
Thus T=Tmax​cos2θ for a nonzero recoil, and the endpoint occurs at θ=0.
For an original numerical example, stipulate E=4MeV and m=mp​, with mp​ the proton mass. Approximate hydrogen, helium and nitrogen target masses by M=mp​, 4mp​ and 14mp​ respectively. Their maximum fractions and recoil energies are

These stipulated values illustrate the approximation mn​=mp​; they are not measurements from 1932. With mp​c2≃938MeV, the incident ratio E/(mp​c2)≃0.0043 gives v/c≃0.092, consistent with the nonrelativistic limit used here. A million electronvolts is an energy unit, whereas a mass expressed in MeV/c2 requires the factor c2 when inserted into an energy equation.
The hydrogen endpoint permits essentially complete transfer because the projectile and target masses match. For a very heavy target, M≫m, the fraction approaches 4m/M, which is small: the target can take substantial momentum with little kinetic energy. The exact inequality follows from (m−M)2≥0, which implies (m+M)2≥4mM.

(20)

For comparison, Compton scattering of a photon of energy Eγ​ from a stationary free target of mass M has the exact relativistic endpoint
Tγ,max​=Mc2+2Eγ​2Eγ2​​,Eγ​=2T+T2+2TMc2​​.
(21)
This follows by combining momentum conservation with the target's relativistic energy, maximizing transfer when the photon reverses direction. Unlike the massive-projectile calculation, this equation does not approximate the incident photon as a nonrelativistic particle.
As a separate, explicitly hypothetical test, take mp​c2=938MeV and target rest energies 938MeV and 14×938MeV. To reproduce the stipulated hydrogen recoil endpoint T=4MeV, the photon would need Eγ​≃45.36MeV. The same photon would give the heavier target at most 0.311MeV, whereas the stipulated massive projectile gave 0.996MeV. This is the structure of a two-target discrimination, not a transcription of a 1932 measurement. Actual radiation can contain more than one energy group or both photons and neutrons; a useful model must account for the observed endpoints and their origin together.

Slow neutron

Words: 484 Articles: 5
Neutron OurBigBook.com
A slow neutron has low kinetic energy relative to the fast neutrons commonly emitted in nuclear reactions. The term covers an energy range, rather than a single speed. A neutron moderator transfers kinetic energy to surrounding matter through collisions. Slowing a neutron can increase its chance of causing particular neutron capture reactions, even though each collision has reduced its kinetic energy. The Fermi slow-neutron activation experiment made this surprising distinction experimentally visible.

Thermal neutron

Words: 58
Slow neutron OurBigBook.com
A thermal neutron belongs to a population approximately in thermal equilibrium with its surroundings. Such a population has a distribution of kinetic energies, not one fixed energy. The scale kB​T uses the Boltzmann constant and thermodynamic temperature; it is about 0.0259eV at 300K. The mean translational kinetic energy for a classical three-dimensional equilibrium population is 3kB​T/2. Not every slow neutron has reached this equilibrium.

Neutron moderation

Words: 372 Articles: 3
Slow neutron OurBigBook.com
Neutron moderation reduces the kinetic energy of fast neutrons through repeated collisions in a neutron moderator. In a simple elastic collision model, hydrogen is especially effective because a proton and a neutron have nearly equal masses: the maximum elastic recoil energy fraction can approach one. The mean logarithmic energy decrement describes typical accumulated slowing, whereas the maximum transfer describes a single endpoint.
Slowing, absorption and escape compete. A material can remove neutrons by neutron capture before they become thermal neutrons, and some neutrons escape its boundaries. Describing a neutron moderator therefore requires more than collision kinematics. Near thermal equilibrium, target thermal motion can also give energy back to a neutron; the initially stationary target approximation must then be replaced.

Neutron activation

Words: 1k Articles: 5
Neutron OurBigBook.com
Neutron activation is the production of radioactivity by irradiating a material with neutrons. One route is neutron capture producing an unstable isotope; other nuclear reactions can also produce unstable atomic nuclei. A specimen's later radioactive decay provides a way to detect that a transformation occurred during irradiation. It does not by itself identify the product or reconstruct the incoming neutron energy distribution.
This makes neutron activation a useful bridge between an invisible radiation field and a delayed measurement. The neutron flux and collision cross-section determine production; radioactive decay and detection efficiency determine what remains observable later. Neutron activation buildup and decay keeps these stages separate.

Fermi slow-neutron activation experiment

Words: 416
Neutron activation OurBigBook.com
Enrico Fermi's May 1934 report describes cylindrical specimens irradiated by a radon-beryllium neutron source, then placed around a thin-aluminium-wall Geiger-Müller counter to record pulses per minute. The irradiation and counting were successive operations.[ref]
The October paper by Enrico Fermi, Edoardo Amaldi, Bruno Pontecorvo, Franco Rasetti and Emilio Segrè reports that interposed paraffin wax increased silver activation; surrounding blocks enhanced it further, depending on geometry. Water gave a comparable effect. A preparation without beryllium gave no effect, arguing against gamma rays alone. Oxygen-containing material without hydrogen did not give a comparable enhancement, and not all activated elements responded alike. Repeated collisions with hydrogen were proposed to slow the neutrons.[ref]
The University of Pisa's account of Bruno Pontecorvo's work with Edoardo Amaldi describes stronger silver activation on wood than on marble. This is a retrospective account; the October paper above directly documents the deliberate paraffin wax and water comparisons.[ref]
To interpret this readout, compare an irradiated specimen with an unirradiated specimen or empty-counter background, using the same counting geometry. Compare irradiation conditions at matched irradiation durations, delays and counting windows. These are requirements of the measurement model, rather than a claim that every such control is documented in the short October letter. Otherwise ordinary radioactive decay during transfer can imitate a change in activation. A persistent, decaying excess after irradiation supports an unstable product; neither a larger count nor a fitted half-life uniquely identifies an isotope.
In his 1938 Nobel lecture, Enrico Fermi distinguished single and superposed exponential decay curves and described chemical separation to identify their carriers. His slowing interpretation connected elastic collisions with enhanced capture. The lecture also described neutron capture resonances, so its account cannot support a universal rule that slower always means more reactive.[ref]
The 1934 assignments of some uranium activities to elements beyond uranium were mistaken interpretations, not a discovery of nuclear fission. The published lecture's later footnote explicitly calls for reexamining the transuranic assignments after evidence of splitting. Distinguish that correction from the lecture as delivered.[ref]

Neutron flux (ϕ(E)=v(E)n(E))

Words: 283 Articles: 1
Neutron activation OurBigBook.com
Let n(E)dE be the number of neutrons per unit volume in an energy interval. The scalar neutron flux per unit energy is ϕ(E)=v(E)n(E), with v(E) the speed. Its integral has units of inverse area per unit time. It counts path length traversed per volume per time, including all directions; it is not the signed current through an oriented surface.
For a small uniform specimen containing NT​ target atomic nuclei, a reaction-specific collision cross-section σa​(E) gives the expected production rate
R=NT​∫0∞​σa​(E)ϕ(E)dE.
(26)
Assume the incident field is little changed within the specimen, target depletion is negligible and each counted reaction creates the product of interest. If that product has a production branching fraction below one, include it in σa​ or as an additional factor. A single neutron activation measurement supplies one weighted integral of an energy distribution, not a unique reconstruction of that distribution.
For an original two-group example, stipulate response collision cross-sections σs​=10σf​. With the same total scalar neutron flux Φ, changing from all fast neutrons to half slow and half fast changes R/(NT​σf​Φ) from 1 to 5.5. No additional neutrons need be emitted by the source. The chosen factor ten is not a historical silver measurement.
For some capture reactions over a limited low-energy interval, σa​∝1/v is a useful approximation. At fixed neutron flux, it gives increasing reaction probability per path length as speed decreases. At fixed neutron number density, σa​v is constant in this approximation, so the rate per target is unchanged. A real neutron moderator changes the local density, energy distribution, escape and absorption together. Neither all collision cross-sections nor all neutron activation rates vary monotonically as 1/v.

Neutron fluence (Φ(t)=∫0t​ϕ(t′)dt′)

Neutron flux OurBigBook.com
The time integral of scalar neutron flux, with inverse-area units. Multiplication by a constant microscopic reaction collision cross-section gives a dimensionless exposure parameter. A single integrated exposure does not specify how the neutron energy distribution varied during irradiation.

Neutron capture resonance

Words: 57
Neutron activation OurBigBook.com
A neutron capture resonance is an energy interval where a matching excited state of the combined atomic nucleus enhances capture. Its collision cross-section can have a peak rather than a monotone dependence on energy. The resonance pole description connects this to quantum scattering. Slowing a neutron through such a region can first increase and then decrease a particular reaction response. The capture probability also competes with other reaction channels.

Neutron activation buildup and decay (N˙=R−λN)

Neutron activation OurBigBook.com
Let N be the expected value of the number of radioactive product atomic nuclei, initially zero, produced at constant rate R. Let their nuclear decay constant be λ>0. Assume one product species, negligible target depletion and negligible capture by the product. During irradiation, production adds Rdt while radioactive decay removes λNdt in expectation. Multiplying the resulting ordinary differential equation by eλt gives
dtd​(eλtN)=Reλt,N(t)=λR​(1−e−λt),A(t)=R(1−e−λt).
(27)
The expected radioactive activity A=λN tends to the production rate R. This saturation is a balance of continuing production and radioactive decay, not exhaustion of target atomic nuclei.
If irradiation ends at ti​, let s denote elapsed time afterward. Setting the production term to zero gives
Nafter​(s)=λR​(1−e−λti​)e−λs,Aafter​(s)=R(1−e−λti​)e−λs.
(28)
For detection efficiency ε, constant background count rate b, delay d and counting-window duration Δ, integrating the expected count rate gives
E[C]=bΔ+ελR​(1−e−λti​)e−λd(1−e−λΔ).
(29)
This assumes at most one recorded pulse per product transformation and negligible counter losses. Detector response, branching fractions and background must be calibrated independently. Subtracting an estimated background does not remove its counting uncertainty. Several products give a sum of these terms with distinct nuclear decay constants; a single exponential decay fit can then mislead.
For a wholly stipulated example, choose R=120min−1, half-life 2min and ti​=4min. Then λ=log2/(2min), A(ti​)=90min−1 and N(ti​)≃259.69. Choose ε=0.25, b=2min−1 and d=Δ=2min. The result is
E[C]−bΔ=log2/20.25×120​×43​×21​×21​≃16.23,E[C]≃20.23.
(30)
The background contributes four expected counts. Multiplying the initial net count rate in that window by its duration would give 22.5 net counts, overestimating the integrated 16.23 because the product decays appreciably during counting.
