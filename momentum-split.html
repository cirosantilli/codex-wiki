Momentum (p)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Classical mechanics
OurBigBook.com Words: 8k Articles: 72
For a nonrelativistic point mass of fixed mass m>0, measured in an inertial frame, its momentum is
p=mv.
(1)
Momentum is a vector pointing along the velocity, with International System of Units unit kgm/s. Doubling either mass or velocity doubles momentum. In one dimension, a chosen positive direction makes p=mv a signed quantity: a 2kg body moving right at 3m/s has p=+6kgm/s; moving left at the same speed gives p=−6kgm/s. [ref]
For a collection, total momentum is the vector sum P=∑i​mi​vi​. Two equal masses moving with opposite velocities can have zero total momentum while both have positive kinetic energy. Direction is therefore essential when using momentum conservation; adding speeds instead of signed velocities gives the wrong balance.
Newton's second law connects the net force on one fixed-mass point mass to its momentum through Fnet​=dp/dt. Integrating that change gives impulse. Here p denotes mechanical momentum; when canonical momentum differs from mass times velocity, the distinction in mechanical momentum below applies. The nonrelativistic formula also has a limited regime: special relativity is needed at speeds comparable with the speed of light.
