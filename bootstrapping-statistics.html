Bootstrapping (statistics)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Statistical model Statistical modelling
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 16
The bootstrap approximates a sampling distribution by repeatedly sampling with replacement from the empirical measure of the observed data. In the ordinary nonparametric version, observations are assumed independent and identically distributed; a bootstrap sample has the same size as the original statistical sample. A statistic, such as a sample mean or sample median, is recomputed on each resample. Its variation across resamples estimates how the original statistic would vary across repeated sampling from the unknown probability distribution.[ref]
The key distinction is between the observed statistical sample and its still-unknown source probability distribution. Condition on the observed values x1​,…,xn​ and form the empirical measure
Pn​=n1​∑i=1n​δxi​​.
(55)
Here δxi​​ is a Dirac measure. Draw independent indices I1​,…,In​ from the uniform distribution on a finite set {1,…,n} and set Xj∗​=xIj​​. Each observed slot has probability 1/n on every draw. If equal values occupy several slots, their total probability is the sum of those slot probabilities; distinct observed values need not be equally likely. A star denotes this conditional resampling experiment. There are nn equally likely ordered index sequences, although bootstrap count vectors and statistics can combine many such sequences.
A complicated sampling distribution need not stop statistical inference when recomputing the statistic is easy. The bootstrap reuses that calculation on simulated datasets. The plug-in estimator principle replaces the unknown probability measure by Pn​ in the whole repeated-sampling experiment. Whether that substitution approximates the target sampling distribution needs an appropriate bootstrap consistency theorem for the sample mean or a result for the particular statistic. A larger number of resamples reduces Monte Carlo error; it cannot repair the bootstrap failure for a uniform endpoint, an unrepresentative statistical sample or ignored statistical dependence.[ref]
