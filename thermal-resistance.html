Thermal resistance (Rth​=ΔT/Q˙​)

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Heat Thermal conduction
OurBigBook.com Words: 195 Articles: 1
Thermal resistance measures the temperature drop required to drive a given rate of heat transfer. For the steady one-dimensional bar model in thermal conduction,
Rth​=Q˙​Th​−Tc​​=kAL​[KW−1],r=ARth​=kL​[m2KW−1],q=rTh​−Tc​​.
(9)
Here r relates the temperature drop to heat flux, while Rth​ relates it to the rate of heat transfer. The single bar above has r=0.050m2KW−1 and Rth​=5KW−1, so its 20K drop drives 4W.
To see why series thermal resistances add, apply Fourier's law in each layer to obtain ΔTi​=Q˙​Ri​. With no accumulation or side losses the rate of heat transfer is the same through both, so adding the drops gives Th​−Tc​=Q˙​(R1​+R2​). This lets us size layered walls without solving a new differential equation for every layer. The structure matches Ohm's law: temperature difference plays the role of electric potential difference, the rate of heat transfer plays the role of electric current, and thermal resistance plays the role of electrical resistance.
