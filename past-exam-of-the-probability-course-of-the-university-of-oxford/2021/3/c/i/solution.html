Past exam of the probability course of the University of Oxford / 2021 / 3 / c / i / Solution

 ... Probability course of the University of Oxford Past exam of the probability course of the University of Oxford 2021 3 c i
OurBigBook.com Words: 243
Define
L={−c−b,…,−c−1},U={c+1,…,c+a},C=L∪I∪U={−c−b,…,c+a}.
(50)
An interior jump cannot go below −c−b or above c+a, and every exterior reset lands in I. Thus C is closed.
To prove that it is one communicating class, start anywhere in C. From L or U, a reset can reach I with positive probability. From any state of I, repeated +a steps reach some state of U, after staying in I up to the last step. A reset from this state can reach every point of the consecutive block
B={c−b+1,…,c}.
(51)
Every integer j∈{−c−b,…,c} has the form t−kb for some t∈B and an integer k≥0. Repeated −b steps from t reach j, with all departure states in I until a possible last step into L. This gives access to all of I∪L. Finally, each j∈U can be reached from j−a∈I. Every path described has strictly positive probability. Therefore every state in C reaches every other state in C.
Each state outside C can only stay put or reset into I, and no transition from C returns to it. Different states outside C cannot reach one another. Hence the complete list of communicating classes is
C={−c−b,…,c+a}, and {i} for each i∈/C.​
(52)
The finite class C is the unique closed communicating class. The exterior singleton classes are transient communicating classes, since their self-transition probabilities are strictly less than one. Notice that this reachability proof does not require a,b to be coprime.
