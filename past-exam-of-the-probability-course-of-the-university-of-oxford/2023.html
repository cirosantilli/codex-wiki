The uniqueness theorem for moment-generating functions states that if MX​ and MY​ are finite and equal on an open real interval containing zero, then
X=dY.​
(17)
For the convergence theorem for moment-generating functions, suppose that the moment-generating functions MXn​​ are finite on a common open real interval (−a,a), a>0, and MXn​​(t)→MX​(t) for every ∣t∣<a, where MX​ is finite there. Then
Xn​⟶d​X.​
(18)
One may equivalently assume convergence to a finite function M on that interval: the theorem then asserts existence of a unique limiting probability distribution with moment-generating function M as well as convergence in distribution.

Fix 0<c<1 and choose Rc​, independently of X, with the normal distribution
Rc​∼N((1−c)μ,(1−c2)σ2).​

A probability distribution on the countable state space S is precisely a row vector satisfying
πi​≥0(i∈S),i∈S∑​πi​=1.​
(37)
A transition matrix is a stochastic matrix, meaning

(38)
Every row therefore gives the conditional probability distribution of the next state given the present state. These conditions do not require π to be an invariant distribution of P.

Under the specified evolution rule, conditional on the entire history, the next event's probability distribution depends only on the current population size. This is the time-homogeneous Markov property. Birth and immigration both increase the size by one, while death decreases it by one. Writing q=1−p, the transition probabilities on Z≥0​ are
P(k,k+1)=uk​=k+θkp+θ​(k≥0),P(k,k−1)=dk​=k+θkq​(k≥1),​
(43)
and all other entries are zero. In particular P(0,1)=1 and d0​=0. The row sum is [kp+θ+kq]/(k+θ)=1. Every neighboring pair communicates, so this is an irreducible Markov chain, specifically a birth-death-immigration jump chain.

Every step changes the population by one, so Xn​ has the parity of n when X0​=0. Hence P(X2m+1​=0)=0. A return in two steps has positive probability from every state, and all return times are even, so the period of a state in a Markov chain is exactly two.
Each transition exchanges the even and odd classes. Since π is an invariant distribution, their masses are equal: π(even)=π(odd)=1/2. The chain sampled at times 2m, restricted to the even states, is irreducible and positive recurrent, and it is aperiodic because its diagonal entries are positive. P(X2m​=0)⟶2π0​=(1−r)a+1,P(X2m+1​=0)=0.​
