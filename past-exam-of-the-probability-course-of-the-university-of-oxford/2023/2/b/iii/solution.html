Under independence of random variables, the law of a random variable Z is the mixed probability distribution (1−p)δ0​+pUnif(−1,1). For negative z, only the uniform component can contribute to P(Z≤z); at zero the probability mass 1−p is included. Thus its cumulative distribution function is
FZ​(z)=⎩⎨⎧​0,p(z+1)/2,1−p+p(z+1)/2,1,​z<−1,−1≤z<0,0≤z<1,z≥1.​​
(23)
The sketch consists of a horizontal segment at height zero before −1, a line of slope p/2 ending at the open point (0,p/2), a jump of size 1−p to the closed point (0,1−p/2), another line of slope p/2 ending at (1,1), and a horizontal segment thereafter. The cumulative distribution function is right-continuous at the jump.
Under the literal assumptions, write H(z)=P(U≤z∣B=1). The general answer is FZ​(z)=(1−p)1{z≥0}​+pH(z), so the continuous part of the sketch is undetermined. The jump at zero is still exactly 1−p, because P(U=0)=0. In the dependent counterexample from (ii), FZ​(z)=0 for z<0, FZ​(z)=(1+z)/2 for 0≤z<1, and FZ​(z)=1 for z≥1.
