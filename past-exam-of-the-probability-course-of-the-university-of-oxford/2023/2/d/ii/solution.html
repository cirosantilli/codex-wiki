First take integers 1≤k<n. For independent copies X1​,…,Xn​ of X, define
A=k−1/α∑j=1k​Xj​,B=(n−k)−1/α∑j=k+1n​Xj​.
(32)
The two blocks involve disjoint families of independent random variables, so A and B are independent. The assumed strictly stable distribution gives A=dX, B=dX, and
(k/n)1/αA+(1−k/n)1/αB=n−1/α∑j=1n​Xj​=dX.
(33)
Therefore, on any fixed probability space carrying X and an independent copy X′, their joint probability distribution gives
(k/n)1/αX+(1−k/n)1/αX′=dX.
(34)
Now fix an arbitrary 0<c<1 and choose rationals rm​=km​/nm​∈(0,1) tending to cα. Continuity of the real powers gives, on that fixed probability space,
rm1/α​X+(1−rm​)1/αX′⟶cX+(1−cα)1/αX′almost surely.
(35)
The left sides all have the probability distribution of X. Since almost sure convergence implies convergence in distribution, their limit has that same probability distribution. Hence
Rc​=(1−cα)1/αX′,Rc​ independent of X,cX+Rc​=dX.​
(36)
This proves strict stability implies self-decomposability. The limit argument uses no moment-generating function or finite moments, which is essential for general stable distributions.
