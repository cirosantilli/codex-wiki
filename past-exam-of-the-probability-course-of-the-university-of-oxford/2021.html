The definition of convergence in distribution gives Fn​(t)→F(t) at each continuity point t of F. We must handle a moving argument rather than assume this convergence is uniform.
A cumulative distribution function has at most countably many discontinuities: each is a jump of positive size, and there are at most r jumps of size at least 1/r, since their sizes sum to at most one. Thus we can choose continuity points uj​<c<vj​ with uj​↑c and vj​↓c. For fixed j, eventually uj​<cn​<vj​. The monotonicity of each cumulative distribution function implies
Fn​(uj​)≤Fn​(cn​)≤Fn​(vj​).

Independently, the downward predecessor j+b contributes a units when j≤c−b; when j≥c−b+1, resets from the a states of U contribute those a units instead. Thus every column total is a+b units.

Let d=gcd(a,b)>1 and put a′=a/d, b′=b/d. Then a′,b′ are coprime integers, and p=b′/(a′+b′). The six requested revisions are as follows.
(b)(i): communicating classes. Every increment is divisible by d, so the residue class modulo d is preserved. Conversely, if j−i is divisible by d, Bézout's identity applied to a′,b′ gives a path just as in (b)(i). Thus the original Markov chain is reducible on Z, with exactly the d closed communicating classes
r+dZ,r=0,1,…,d−1.​
(55)
The specified walk starting at zero stays in dZ.
(b)(ii): period of a state in a Markov chain. The return equation au=bv reduces to a′u=b′v. Hence u=b′k, v=a′k. For every state, the period of a state in a Markov chain is therefore
a′+b′=da+b​.​
(56)
This is still at least two, so no state is aperiodic.
(b)(iii): stationary distribution. The conclusion is unchanged: there is no stationary distribution on Z. The maximum-mass proof in (b)(iii) applies verbatim, without using coprimality. Equivalently, each closed communicating class, after translating and dividing its state labels by d, is the same centered random walk with step sizes a′,−b′ and has no stationary distribution.
(b)(iv): occupation of zero. On dZ, the rescaled walk Sn​/d has exactly the transition probabilities of the coprime walk with parameters a′,b′. The return-probability argument in (b)(iv) therefore makes this class null recurrent. The renewal process proof of the occupation limit applies unchanged:
