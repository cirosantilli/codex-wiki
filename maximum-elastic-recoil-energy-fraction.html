Let a projectile have mass m, incident speed v, and incident kinetic energy E=mv2/2. The stationary target has mass M. In a head-on elastic collision, the signed outgoing projectile velocity v′ and target velocity u obey momentum conservation and conservation of energy:
mv=mv′+Mu,21​mv2=21​mv′2+21​Mu2.
(14)

This is the maximum over scattering angles, not the energy of every recoil. To see the bound without assuming a head-on event, set the recoil momentum magnitude to q and its laboratory angle to the incident direction to θ. Conservation of energy with outgoing projectile momentum p−q gives
2mp2​=2mp2+q2−2pqcosθ​+2Mq2​,q=m+M2Mpcosθ​.
(16)
Thus T=Tmax​cos2θ for a nonzero recoil, and the endpoint occurs at θ=0.

These stipulated values illustrate the approximation mn​=mp​; they are not measurements from 1932. With mp​c2≃938MeV, the incident ratio E/(mp​c2)≃0.0043 gives v/c≃0.092, consistent with the nonrelativistic limit used here. A million electronvolts is an energy unit, whereas a mass expressed in MeV/c2 requires the factor c2 when inserted into an energy equation.
The hydrogen endpoint permits essentially complete transfer because the projectile and target masses match. For a very heavy target, M≫m, the fraction approaches 4m/M, which is small: the target can take substantial momentum with little kinetic energy. The exact inequality follows from (m−M)2≥0, which implies (m+M)2≥4mM.
