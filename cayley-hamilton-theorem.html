Cayley-Hamilton theorem (χA​(A)=0)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Minimal polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 728 Articles: 6
Every square matrix satisfies its own characteristic polynomial. More explicitly, for an n-by-n matrix A over a commutative ring with identity, form
χA​(t)=det(tI−A)=tn+cn−1​tn−1+⋯+c1​t+c0​.
(1)
Then
An+cn−1​An−1+⋯+c1​A+c0​I=0.​
(2)
Here 0 is the zero matrix, and the constant term multiplies the identity matrix. This is a polynomial evaluated at a matrix; it is stronger than saying that each eigenvalue is a root of a polynomial. Neither diagonalizability nor distinct eigenvalues is required.[ref]
The practical payoff is that arbitrarily high matrix powers can be reduced to a combination of I,A,…,An−1. These powers arise when repeatedly applying a linear map, solving a linear recurrence relation, or evolving a finite state space model. The characteristic polynomial turns that repeated action into a fixed-order scalar recurrence. The minimal polynomial may reduce the number of required powers further.

Polynomial evaluated at a matrix (p(A)=∑j=0d​aj​Aj)

Words: 44
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
For a polynomial p(t)=∑j=0d​aj​tj, substitute a fixed square matrix A for t, taking A0=I. In particular, p(t)=t2−3t+1 becomes p(A)=A2−3A+I, not A2−3A with the scalar 1 added separately to each entry. Polynomials in the same matrix commute with each other, even though arbitrary matrix multiplication is noncommutative.

Two-coefficient reduction of matrix powers (An=an​A+bn​I)

Words: 142
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
Consider the real matrix
A=(21​11​),χA​(t)=(t−2)(t−1)−1=t2−3t+1.
(3)
The Cayley-Hamilton theorem gives A2=3A−I. Consequently
A3=3A2−A=8A−3I,A4=21A−8I,A5=55A−21I.
(4)
Thus
A5=(8955​5534​).​
(5)
All four entries come from just two scalar coefficients. If An=an​A+bn​I, multiplying once by A and replacing A2 gives the linear recurrence relation
an+1​=3an​+bn​,bn+1​=−an​,(a0​,b0​)=(0,1).
(6)
Equivalently an+2​=3an+1​−an​, with a0​=0,a1​=1. The appearance of Fibonacci numbers is explained by A=Q2, where Q=(11​10​) advances the Fibonacci recurrence. No eigenvectors need to be computed.
The same identity computes an inverse. Rearrange it as A(3I−A)=I, giving
A−1=3I−A=(1−1​−12​).​
(7)
This works because the constant term of this characteristic polynomial is nonzero and the coefficients lie in a field. Generally, when c0​=0 over a field, rearranging the Cayley-Hamilton theorem expresses A−1 as a polynomial evaluated at a matrix. Over a general commutative ring, a nonzero constant need not be a unit; invertibility requires that extra condition.

Polynomial reduction of matrix powers (f=qχA​+r⟹f(A)=r(A))

Words: 85
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
By polynomial division, write f(t)=q(t)χA​(t)+r(t) with degr<n. Because χA​ is a monic polynomial, this division works even over a commutative ring. Evaluating at A makes the multiple of χA​(A) vanish, leaving f(A)=r(A). This explains the reduction in two-coefficient reduction of matrix powers without multiplying out every intermediate matrix.
Over a field, the minimal polynomial mA​ divides χA​ and gives the strongest such universal reduction: use the polynomial remainder modulo mA​ instead. For the scalar matrix 2I2​, the characteristic polynomial is (t−2)2, while the minimal polynomial is only t−2. Every matrix power is then a scalar multiple of I2​.

Repeated eigenvalues in the Cayley-Hamilton theorem (B=2I+N,N2=0)

Words: 172
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
Take a different real matrix,
B=(20​12​)=2I+N,N=(00​10​).
(8)
Its only eigenvalue is 2, with algebraic multiplicity two. However, the polynomial q(t)=t−2, which vanishes at that eigenvalue, gives q(B)=N=0. Merely checking values at eigenvalues cannot prove a matrix identity. The full characteristic polynomial does work:
χB​(t)=(t−2)2,χB​(B)=N2=0.
(9)
The matrix is a Jordan block, and its minimal polynomial is also (t−2)2.
The binomial theorem gives, for positive integers n,
Bn=2nI+n2n−1N.​
(10)
Terms containing N2 or a higher matrix power vanish. After removing the common growth factor 2n, the remaining linear map is the shear mapping I+(n/2)N. It moves (x,y) to (x+ny/2,y). The single eigenvalue controls the exponential scale, while the nilpotent matrix adds a factor growing linearly in n.
In terms of polynomial interpolation, reduction modulo (t−2)2 retains both a value and a derivative:
f(B)=f(2)I+f′(2)N.
(11)
Indeed, polynomial division gives f(t)=f(2)+f′(2)(t−2)+(t−2)2h(t). This is a simple Hermite interpolation condition. For f(t)=tn, it gives exactly the displayed matrix power. Repeated eigenvalues retain information about how a polynomial changes, not just where it vanishes.

Cayley's matrix memoir

Words: 102
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
Arthur Cayley's 1858 paper A Memoir on the Theory of Matrices motivates matrices as an abbreviated way to write systems of linear equations. Its opening already connects the characteristic equation with reducing polynomials in a matrix to lower degree. In §21 he works out the two-by-two identity; in §23 he says he has verified the three-by-three case but does not give a formal proof for every size. Thus this source distinguishes the general statement from a general proof.[ref] The theorem's name also commemorates William Rowan Hamilton.

Cayley-Hamilton theorem from cyclic subspaces (χT​(T)=0)

Words: 65
Cayley-Hamilton theorem OurBigBook.com
Over a field, a cyclic vector gives a companion matrix, whose characteristic relation annihilates its generator and hence every iterate. If there is no cyclic vector spanning the whole space, a nonzero proper cyclic subspace is invariant. Induction annihilates the restriction and the quotient; the quotient polynomial first sends the whole space into the invariant subspace, and the restriction polynomial then kills it. The block-triangular characteristic polynomial is the product of these two polynomials.
