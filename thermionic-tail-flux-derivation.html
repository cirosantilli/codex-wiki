Thermionic tail-flux derivation (T3/2×T1/2=T2)

Consider a planar surface with outward normal along z. For an auxiliary equilibrium electron gas just outside it, let nv​ be the total number density, counting both signs of vz​. Its Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution has normal-component density
fz​(vz​)=2πkB​Tme​​​e−me​vz2​/(2kB​T).
(8)
An electron with vz​>0 crosses more area per unit time when it moves faster. The outgoing number flux therefore uses vz​fz​, rather than just the probability of vz​>0:
Γ=nv​∫0∞​vz​fz​(vz​)dvz​=nv​2πme​kB​T​​.
(9)
This integral contributes only T1/2. Holding a classical bulk number density fixed and imposing a normal kinetic energy threshold W would instead give Γ=nkB​T/(2πme​)​e−W/(kB​T); that argument alone does not produce T2.
For the ideal surface model, measure external electron energies from the metal's chemical potential μ. A freely propagating external state has E−μ=W+p2/(2me​). When W≫kB​T, its Fermi-Dirac distribution reduces to the Maxwell–Boltzmann statistics tail. Counting two spin states per phase space cell of volume h3 gives
nv​=h32​∫R3​e−[W+p2/(2me​)]/(kB​T)d3p=2(h22πme​kB​T​)3/2e−W/(kB​T).
(10)
Each of the three Gaussian integrals supplies T1/2. Combining this T3/2 state count with the T1/2 crossing factor gives
J=eΓ=h34πme​ekB2​​T2e−W/(kB​T).
(11)
The auxiliary atmosphere supplies an equilibrium state-counting argument; an operating collector removes the outgoing electrons. It need not maintain a real equilibrium gas throughout the gap. Unit transmission and the ideal free electron state count are substantive assumptions, so this calculation does not determine an arbitrary real surface's measured A.
In particular, electrons inside a metal generally form a degenerate Fermi gas. A classical distribution for the escaping tail does not imply a classical distribution for the whole interior. Richardson himself emphasized this distinction in his lecture.[ref]
