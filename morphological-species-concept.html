Morphological species concept

 Home Biology Taxonomy (biology) Species Species concept
OurBigBook.com Words: 121
A morphological species concept distinguishes species using morphology: measurable differences in form or structure. It can be applied to preserved specimens and fossils when mating cannot be observed. Similar appearance need not imply the same species, and different appearance need not imply different species. [ref]
An original operational example is to compare a chosen structure in specimens of comparable age, sex and rearing conditions. Suppose two apparent groups differ in size, but one group contains only juveniles. The observed difference does not yet diagnose two species. Specify the measured phenotype, variation within each sample, and whether the distinction persists across comparable specimens. A single striking specimen is a poor substitute for that comparison.
