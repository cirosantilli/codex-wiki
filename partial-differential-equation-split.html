Partial differential equation (F(x,t,u,ut​,ux​,uxx​,…)=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 56k Articles: 947
A partial differential equation relates an unknown function of several independent variables to its partial derivatives. For u(x,t), the notation ut​=∂u/∂t means changing time while holding position fixed; uxx​=∂2u/∂x2 measures how its spatial slope changes. Unlike an ordinary differential equation, a partial differential equation can describe an entire evolving profile rather than a single trajectory.
The heat equation ut​=Duxx​ smooths spatial variations, the transport equation ut​+cux​=0 moves them, and Laplace's equation Δu=0 describes a spatial equilibrium. Here D>0 is a diffusivity, c is a velocity, and Δ is the Laplacian. The periodic heat and transport comparison makes the difference visible using identical initial conditions.
A partial differential equation usually supplies a local rule rather than a complete answer. For example, every u(x,t)=A+Bx satisfies the heat equation with zero PDE source term; data select which profile is relevant. A complete problem specifies its domain of a function, coefficients, initial conditions, boundary conditions, and meaning of solution of a partial differential equation. A well-posed problem has existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence on initial data in stated spaces; the equation alone guarantees none of these.[ref]
