Connectedness of an intersection from connected finite intersections (⋂j∈J​Kj​ nonempty and connected for finite J ⟹ ⋂i​Ki​ compact and connected)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Connected space
OurBigBook.com Words: 176 Articles: 1
Let X be a compact Hausdorff space and let Ki​ be compact subsets such that all finite intersections are nonempty and connected. Their total intersection K is nonempty by the finite intersection characterization of compactness, and compact because it is closed in X.
If K were not connected, its separation of a topological space would divide it into two nonempty compact subsets. Separation of disjoint compact subsets gives disjoint ambient open sets U,V containing the two pieces. The family consisting of X∖(U∪V) and all Ki​ has empty total intersection, so some finite subfamily has empty intersection. Hence a finite intersection KJ​ of the Ki​ lies in U∪V. Since K⊆KJ​ meets both open sets, they disconnect KJ​, a contradiction. The hypothesis on all finite intersections is stronger than requiring each individual Ki​ to be connected.
