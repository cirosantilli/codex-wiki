Pea seed respirometry

 Home Biology Metabolism Cellular respiration Respirometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 274
The University of Oklahoma K20 Center's handout describes three sealed vial-and-pipette respirometers in each temperature bath: soaked, germinating peas; nongerminating peas plus inert plastic beads; and beads alone. Displaced water establishes equal occupied volumes. Absorbent cotton holds potassium hydroxide, which removes carbon dioxide; nonabsorbent cotton separates it from the peas. The source equilibrates the vials before submersion and again before the initial reading. Colored water makes the boundary visible in each calibrated pipette. Readers then record positions every five minutes for twenty minutes.[ref]
Bath temperature, vial position and seal integrity matter. The beads-only respirometer detects common temperature or atmospheric pressure drift; its change is subtracted from each pea reading. Nongerminating peas provide a biological comparison, not an assumption of zero cellular respiration. A larger corrected uptake slope for germinating peas would support greater molecular oxygen consumption by that sample under the tested conditions.[ref]
Equal displaced volumes help match the gas spaces; they do not establish equal living mass or equal developmental state. Comparing uptake per seed and uptake per dry mass asks different questions. Leaks, incomplete carbon dioxide absorption, gas dissolution and changing water-vapour contributions can distort the respirometer inference. A blank correction cannot repair a sample-specific leak. These are consequences of the respirometer gas balance, rather than measurements reported in the handout.
