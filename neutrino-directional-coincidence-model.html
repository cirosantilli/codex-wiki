Neutrino directional-coincidence model (q(ψ)=(1−cosψ)/2)

How much does a small directional uncertainty help? Consider an original idealized model: a source direction fixed in advance, uniform background directions over the full sphere, and circular acceptance radius ψ. The accepted solid angle and single-event background probability are
Ω(ψ)=∫02π​dϕ∫0ψ​sinθdθ=2π(1−cosψ),q(ψ)=4πΩ​=21−cosψ​.
(18)
The derivation integrates the area element on a unit sphere. For small ψ in radians, q≃ψ2/4: halving the radius quarters the accidental acceptance, to leading order. At psi=0 acceptance vanishes; at ψ=π it covers the full sphere. The solid angle has units of steradians; q is dimensionless.
For 0<ψ≤π, take a deliberately crude point spread function: stipulate that every signal direction lies uniformly inside that cap. The signal and background probability density functions per steradian are S=1/Ω inside the cap and zero outside, and B=1/(4π) everywhere. Both integrate to one. For an event inside the cap their likelihood ratio is
BS​=q1​.
(19)
This ratio compares two specified directional models; it is not the probability that the event came from the source. Such a conditional probability would additionally require prior source odds and the other selection information. For fixed source fraction f among selected events, a normalized mixture distribution has density (1−f)B+fS. For independent events the likelihood is the product of those densities; its ratio to background alone is
Λ(f)=∏i​[1+f(Bi​Si​​−1)].
(20)
Outside-cap events contribute factors 1−f rather than being silently discarded. At f=0, Λ=1; for a full-sky cap, S=B and every f gives Λ=1. A directional model that cannot distinguish source from background carries no directional evidence.
As an original numerical example, radii 1∘ and 10∘ give q≃7.615×10−5 and 7.596×10−3, respectively: nearly a hundredfold difference. For a fixed number N of independent background events, the binomial distribution gives
P(at least one inside)=1−(1−q)N.
(21)
With chosen N=100, those radii give approximately 0.00759 and 0.5335. A small single-event acceptance can become a common coincidence after many opportunities. This probability concerns a fixed cap, rather than a scan for the most favorable cap anywhere. For m independent searches with common local tail probability p, the corresponding global tail probability is 1−(1−p)m; overlapping sky searches generally violate that independence. Actual source tests must include directional response, nonuniform exposure, energy information and the look-elsewhere effect in their null calibration.
