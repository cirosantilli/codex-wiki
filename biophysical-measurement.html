DNA fluorescence in situ hybridization

Words: 23
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Detection of selected genomic regions with complementary fluorescent probes, supporting spatial-distance and nuclear-position measurements. Fixation, denaturation and probe design can affect the measurement.

DamID

Words: 23
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Mapping DNA near a protein by tethering a DNA adenine methyltransferase and detecting its deposited mark. Expression level and exposure duration affect spatial and temporal interpretation.

CUT&RUN

Words: 20
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Antibody-targeted nuclease cleavage and release of DNA near a chosen chromatin-associated protein or mark. Background, antibody specificity and cleavage conditions still need controls.

SunTag

Words: 22
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
A repeated peptide-epitope system detectable by fluorescent binders. Placing tags on nascent chains can make a biological translation site visible separately from completed proteins.

Isotope labelling

Words: 63 Articles: 2
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Introducing selected isotopes into molecules to support detection or assignment. In nuclear magnetic resonance, labelling can add observable spins or suppress unwanted relaxation pathways.

Selective methyl labelling

Words: 16
Isotope labelling OurBigBook.com
Isotopic labelling of selected methyl groups to provide sensitive NMR probes in a relatively sparse spin network.

Deuteration

Words: 26
Isotope labelling OurBigBook.com
Replacement of hydrogen with deuterium. In protein NMR, suitable deuteration reduces proton dipolar relaxation but also removes directly observable proton signals unless selected sites are retained or reintroduced.

Traction force microscopy

Words: 27
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
traction force microscopy estimates forces that cells exert on a deformable substrate from measured substrate displacement and a mechanical model. Calibration, boundary conditions and material properties affect the inferred forces.

Electron cryotomography

Words: 29
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Electron cryotomography reconstructs three-dimensional electron-scattering density from a tilt series of a vitrified specimen. It can reveal native cellular assemblies such as receptor arrays, subject to dose, sampling and resolution limits.

Selection assay

Words: 33
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
A selection assay preferentially retains, amplifies or permits survival of candidates satisfying a criterion. It differs from directly measuring every candidate and can fail if the selectable phenotype becomes detached from the intended biochemical function.

Screening assay

Words: 25
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
A screening assay measures candidate variants individually or in distinguishable compartments. In protein engineering, expression, stability and intrinsic activity must be separated when interpreting an increased signal.

Surface plasmon resonance

Words: 51 Articles: 1
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Surface plasmon resonance can monitor binding near an optical sensor surface in real time through refractive-index changes. Concentration-dependent association and dissociation can constrain kinetics, subject to controls for nonspecific binding, transport limitation and multivalency.

Localized surface plasmon resonance

Words: 20
Surface plasmon resonance OurBigBook.com
A resonant collective electronic response confined to a finite conducting nanostructure. Its near field depends on geometry, environment and loss.

Isothermal titration calorimetry

Words: 35
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Isothermal titration calorimetry measures heat accompanying additions of one binding partner to another at controlled temperature. With suitable concentration ranges, stoichiometry and a binding model, it can estimate affinity and binding enthalpy; dilution and buffer effects need controls.

Dynamic light scattering

Words: 34
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Dynamic light scattering analyses fluctuations in scattered light to infer diffusion and hydrodynamic size under a suitable model. Large particles can dominate the intensity, making it sensitive to aggregation but limiting simple interpretation as a number distribution.

Toeprinting

Words: 37
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Toeprinting maps a reverse-transcription stop caused by an assembled ribosome or initiation complex on RNA. It can report occupancy near a translation start site, but controls are needed for stops caused by RNA structure or other bound proteins.

Radiation damage

Words: 108 Articles: 3
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
radiation damage is a radiation-induced change in a specimen. In biological electron microscopy, accumulated exposure can alter chemical groups and remove high-resolution information, so recording more electrons has competing signal and damage effects.

Knock-on displacement

Words: 21
Radiation damage OurBigBook.com
Displacement of an atom when a collision transfers enough momentum to exceed its displacement threshold. Its energy dependence differs from radiolysis.

Radiolysis

Words: 57 Articles: 1
Radiation damage OurBigBook.com
Chemical damage initiated by ionization and electronic excitation from radiation. It is distinct from direct momentum-transfer knock-on displacement; the useful image information per damaging event depends on beam energy and specimen.

Water radiolysis

Words: 27
Radiolysis OurBigBook.com
Chemical transformation of water initiated by ionizing radiation. Both reducing and oxidizing products occur; observed net redox chemistry depends on the solution and reactions of primary products.

Double electron-electron resonance

Words: 24
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
double electron-electron resonance is a pulsed electron paramagnetic resonance method that uses spin–spin coupling to constrain distances between labels. Label conformation, distance distributions and background correction affect the structural interpretation.

Colocalization

Words: 25
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
colocalization is spatial coincidence of measured distributions within the resolution of an imaging method. Shared pixels can suggest a common compartment without demonstrating direct molecular contact.

Single-particle tracking

Words: 26
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
single-particle tracking estimates successive positions of individual labeled objects. Displacements can constrain diffusion, confinement or directed movement, provided localization error, missed detections and label effects are accounted for.

TurboID

Words: 37
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
TurboID is an engineered promiscuous biotin ligase used for proximity labeling. A fusion can label nearby proteins during a short pulse, while localization-matched and no-added-biotin controls help distinguish the intended neighbourhood from background.[ref]

Proximity labeling

Words: 29
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
proximity labeling uses a localized reactive system to mark nearby molecules before extraction. The recovered label integrates access, distance, labeling rate and residence time; proximity alone does not demonstrate a direct protein-protein interaction.

Co-immunoprecipitation

Words: 28
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
co-immunoprecipitation captures a protein using an antibody and tests which other components remain associated. Reciprocal capture, input abundance and negative controls help interpret the result; coprecipitation need not imply direct contact.

Affinity purification-mass spectrometry

Words: 34
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
In affinity purification-mass spectrometry, a bait protein is isolated with associated material, and mass spectrometry identifies candidate partners. Enrichment relative to matched controls supports association; it does not alone establish a direct protein-protein interaction or the number of copies per assembly.

Förster resonance energy transfer

Words: 2k Articles: 15
Biophysical measurement OurBigBook.com
Förster resonance energy transfer is a nonradiative transfer of energy from an excited state of a FRET donor to a nearby FRET acceptor, through coupling of their electric dipoles. The transfer step does not emit a photon that the FRET acceptor subsequently absorbs. It competes with the FRET donor's own fluorescence and other routes out of its excited state. A fluorescent FRET acceptor can then emit fluorescence; an absorbing but nonfluorescent FRET acceptor can still receive the transferred energy.[ref]
The striking use of FRET is to turn a change in molecular proximity into an optical readout. Proteins carrying a FRET donor and a FRET acceptor may change the readout when they associate, as in the chemotaxis FRET assay; two labels on one protein may report a change in its shape. Interpretation depends on the FRET spectral overlap, FRET dipole orientation factor and labeling geometry, as well as separation. A change in FRET is therefore evidence about proximity under specified conditions, rather than a direct measurement of an arbitrary distance.[ref]
For a single pair with fixed Förster distance R0​ and label separation r, the FRET efficiency is
E=1+(r/R0​)61​.
(1)
At r=R0​, half the FRET donor excitations transfer energy. At r=R0​/2, the model gives E=64/65≈98.46%; at r=2R0​, it gives E=1/65≈1.54%. The sixth power makes FRET especially useful near the Förster distance, with little sensitivity to further separation once transfer is already negligible. These are model calculations, not measurements of a particular pair.

Single-molecule Förster resonance energy transfer

Words: 26
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
single-molecule Förster resonance energy transfer records energy-transfer signals from individual donor–acceptor pairs. State occupancies and dwell times can constrain structural dynamics, provided dye photophysics, labeling geometry and time resolution are controlled.

FRET donor

Words: 37
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
A FRET donor supplies the initial excited state energy. Its fluorescence provides both an emission channel and a reference FRET donor lifetime when no FRET acceptor is present. Calling a label a FRET donor describes its role in a chosen pair, rather than a permanent classification of the molecule.

FRET acceptor

Words: 20
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
A FRET acceptor receives the excited state energy. Its absorption must support FRET spectral overlap with the FRET donor. FRET sensitized emission requires a fluorescent FRET acceptor; measuring a shortened FRET donor lifetime does not.

FRET efficiency (E)

Words: 740 Articles: 7
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
FRET efficiency is the probability that an excitation of the FRET donor ends in transfer to the FRET acceptor. Write kD​=kr​+knr​ for the total decay rate without a FRET acceptor, including radiative fluorescence and other decay, and kT​ for the transfer rate. For constant competing rates,
kT​=kD​(rR0​​)6,E=kD​+kT​kT​​=∫0∞​kT​e−(kD​+kT​)tdt.
(2)
The integral counts transfer events among all excitations in a population with exponential decay. Substitution gives the separation law above. This short rate argument assumes one FRET donor, one FRET acceptor, weak coupling, an available FRET acceptor, and fixed geometry during the decay; it is not a model for arbitrary interacting labels.[ref]

Multiple-acceptor FRET efficiency (En​=nE/[1+(n−1)E])

Words: 33
FRET efficiency OurBigBook.com
For n identical independent available FRET acceptors, transfer rates add. With single-pair FRET efficiency E, the donor exit probability is En​=nE/[1+(n−1)E]. It is not nE; competition with the donor's other decay routes must be retained.

Optimal Förster distance for two-state contrast (R0,opt​=AB​)

Words: 59
FRET efficiency OurBigBook.com
For FRET efficiency E(R)=1/[1+(R/R0​)6] and distinct positive reporter distances A and B, the absolute contrast is maximized at the geometric mean Förster distance R0​=AB​. Substitution of x=R06​ makes the derivative have the sign of A6B6−x2. The maximum is ∣B3−A3∣/(B3+A3) and the two efficiencies then sum to one. If A=B, every choice gives zero contrast and there is no unique optimum.

Förster distance (R0​)

Words: 387 Articles: 3
FRET efficiency OurBigBook.com
The Förster distance is the separation giving FRET efficiency E=1/2 for the specified pair and conditions. It is a property of the optical pair and its environment, rather than a universal length. In the usual pair model,
R06​∝κ2QD​Jn−4,
(3)
where κ2 is the FRET dipole orientation factor, QD​ the FRET donor quantum yield, J the FRET spectral overlap integral, and n the medium's refractive index. The omitted proportionality constant depends on the unit convention for J.[ref]
If FRET efficiency is known and those assumptions hold, inversion gives
r=R0​(E−1−1)1/6,dEdlogr​=−6E(1−E)1​.
(4)
The derivative explains why errors become troublesome near E=0 or E=1. An illustrative pair with R0​=4 nanometres and another with R0​=6 nanometres both give E=1/2 at their different Förster distances: knowing FRET efficiency alone does not determine r.
A heterogeneous population adds another ambiguity. In a synthetic example with equal weights per FRET donor excitation, efficiencies 0.1 and 0.9 average to 0.5, but their separations are 91/6R0​ and 9−1/6R0​. Their average separation is approximately 1.068R0​, whereas inverting the average FRET efficiency gives R0​. A measured emission ratio has additional detection weights; it need not equal even this excitation-weighted average. The transferable lesson is that an average readout does not uniquely specify a distribution of molecular separations.
FRET spectral overlap (J)

FRET spectral overlap quantifies overlap between the FRET donor's emission and the FRET acceptor's absorption as functions of wavelength λ. With normalized donor emission fD​ and acceptor molar absorption coefficient εA​, one convention is
∫0∞​fD​(λ)dλ=1,J=∫0∞​fD​(λ)εA​(λ)λ4dλ.
(5)
Matching two emission colors is insufficient: the receiving absorption matters.[ref]
FRET dipole orientation factor (κ2)
Words: 96
Förster distance OurBigBook.com
The FRET dipole orientation factor describes the geometry of the two transition electric dipoles relative to their separation. Let μ​D​, μ​A​ and r be their unit vectors. Then
κ2=[μ​D​⋅μ​A​−3(μ​D​⋅r)(μ​A​⋅r)]2,0≤κ2≤4.
(6)
For a separation along z, parallel electric dipoles along z give κ2=4, parallel ones along x give 1, and one along x with the other along y gives 0. Thus rotating a pair can change FRET efficiency without changing its separation.
The familiar value κ2=2/3 requires suitable rapid, independent orientation averaging during the FRET donor lifetime. Random orientations across a population of slowly rotating fluorescent proteins do not establish that dynamic condition. [ref]
FRET donor quantum yield (QD​)
Words: 36
Förster distance OurBigBook.com
The FRET donor quantum yield is the emitted fluorescence photon fraction per absorbed excitation photon, measured without a FRET acceptor. In the constant-rate model used for FRET efficiency, QD​=kr​/(kr​+knr​). Changing the FRET donor quantum yield changes the Förster distance, even if label positions remain fixed.

FRET donor lifetime (τD​,τDA​)

Words: 158
FRET efficiency OurBigBook.com
The FRET donor lifetime is the mean time spent in the excited state. For the constant-rate model of FRET efficiency,
τD​=kD​1​,τDA​=kD​+kT​1​,E=1−τD​τDA​​.
(7)
After a sufficiently brief excitation pulse, the FRET donor's fluorescence follows exponential decay, I(t)=I(0)e−t/τ. With the illustrative values τD​=4 nanoseconds and τDA​=2 nanoseconds, the inferred FRET efficiency is 1/2. Multiplying I(0) changes brightness without changing this decay time.
The lifetime comparison requires the same nontransfer decay rates in the reference and paired samples. Changes in the FRET donor's environment or a different nonradiative loss can also shorten the FRET donor lifetime. Heterogeneous FRET efficiencies or slowly varying FRET dipole orientation factors need an appropriate decay model; substituting any fitted average lifetime into the single-pair formula can mislead. [ref]

FRET sensitized emission

Words: 247 Articles: 1
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
FRET sensitized emission is FRET acceptor fluorescence caused by transfer from an excited FRET donor. Excite predominantly the FRET donor and collect both emission bands. Reduced FRET donor emission and increased FRET acceptor emission are useful together, but an acceptor-to-donor emission ratio is not automatically the FRET efficiency: brightness, label abundance and channel collection also enter.[ref]

FRET optical crosstalk

Words: 195
FRET sensitized emission OurBigBook.com
FRET optical crosstalk includes FRET donor emission leaking into the FRET acceptor detection band and direct excitation of the FRET acceptor by the nominal FRET donor excitation light. Measure donor-only, acceptor-only and unlabeled preparations with the same fluorescence microscope settings to separate these contributions and background.[ref]
An explicit linear measurement model makes the controls' purpose clear. Let D be background-subtracted donor-band counts under donor excitation, A acceptor-band counts under acceptor excitation, and F acceptor-band counts under donor excitation. Donor-only and acceptor-only preparations determine leakage coefficients a and b. Then
S=F−aD−bA
(8)
is a corrected FRET sensitized emission signal under the assumed linear, fixed optical response. In a synthetic example D=100, A=80, a=0.10, b=0.05 and F=34 give S=20. Even a nontransferring sample would give F=14 from these two contaminants. Correcting that signal still does not convert S into FRET efficiency without a calibration of excitation, yields and detection.
A biological comparison also needs a pairing-negative preparation and a known transferring positive preparation. Match label abundance and localization where possible; changing concentration can change encounter frequency as well as brightness. Check that attaching the labels preserves the protein function being investigated. Otherwise the measurement can faithfully report a perturbation introduced by the labels themselves.

FRET photobleaching controls

Words: 90
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
In FRET photobleaching controls, selectively destroy the FRET acceptor's ability to absorb and test whether FRET donor fluorescence increases. Under unchanged excitation and detection, complete loss of transfer, and an intact FRET donor,
E=1−ID,after​ID,before​​.
(9)
This is a destructive comparison. Monitor acceptor loss, donor-only samples exposed to the bleaching illumination, and an unbleached reference region or time trace. Incomplete bleaching, donor damage, movement or label replenishment violate the comparison. During ordinary recordings, gradual unequal bleaching can instead produce an apparent change of FRET without a biological change.[ref]

FRET proximity readout experiment

Words: 189
Förster resonance energy transfer OurBigBook.com
In Receptor sensitivity in bacterial chemotaxis by Victor Sourjik and others, Victor Sourjik and Howard C. Berg used a cyan fluorescent tag on CheZ as FRET donor and a yellow tag on CheY as FRET acceptor. Escherichia coli biological cells adhered to a polylysine-coated coverslip in a continuously perfused chamber. A fluorescence microscope excited them with a 442-nanometre laser; split emission bands fed two photon-counting detectors. The yellow/cyan emission ratio fell when attractant was added and rose when it was removed.[ref]
Saturating attractant increased cyan emission by an amount matching selective bleaching of the yellow tag: removing the FRET acceptor supported relief of FRET donor transfer. Strains carrying the tagged pair but lacking other chemotaxis machinery showed no stimulus-dependent emission ratio change. Weak illumination reduced bleaching drift.[ref]
The inference is a change in the population of nearby tagged CheY/CheZ pairs associated with CheY phosphorylation. It neither measures each pair's separation nor directly observes motor motion. The readout also averages many biological cells, and perfusion takes finite time. The pathway interpretation belongs to the chemotaxis FRET assay; the general measurement lesson is that molecular association can alter FRET while the geometry of an individual associated pair stays the same.
