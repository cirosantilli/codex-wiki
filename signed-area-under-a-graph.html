Signed area under a graph (∫ab​f(x)dx)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Integral
OurBigBook.com Words: 251 Articles: 1
For a<b and a real-valued function f with a finite Riemann integral or Lebesgue integral on [a,b], the signed area under a graph is its definite integral: geometric area above the horizontal axis contributes positively, and geometric area below it contributes negatively. If these two nonnegative areas are A+​ and A−​, then
∫ab​f(x)dx=A+​−A−​,∫ab​∣f(x)∣dx=A+​+A−​.
(16)
The second expression is the total geometric area under a graph. Taking the absolute value of the first expression does not usually give the second: cancellation has already removed information about how much area lies on each side.[ref]
For an exact example, take the polynomial function f(x)=x2−1 on [−2,2]. Its polynomial roots are −1 and 1: it is negative between them and positive on the two outer intervals. An antiderivative is F(x)=x3/3−x, so the fundamental theorem of calculus gives
∫−2−1​(x2−1)dx=34​,∫−11​(x2−1)dx=−34​,∫12​(x2−1)dx=34​.
(17)
Thus A+​=8/3 and A−​=4/3. The signed area under a graph is 4/3, whereas the total geometric area under a graph is 4. To compute the latter with antiderivatives, split at each sign-changing polynomial root and reverse the sign of the negative contribution.
Cancellation is useful information when the integrand represents a signed contribution. For the polynomial function f(x)=x on [−1,1], the two triangular areas give ∫−11​xdx=0, although ∫−11​∣x∣dx=1. Zero net signed area therefore means balanced positive and negative contributions, rather than an empty region.
The short algebraic justification uses the positive part f+​=max(f,0) and negative part f−​=max(−f,0): f=f+​−f−​ and ∣f∣=f+​+f−​. Their integrals give A+​ and A−​, and linearity of integration gives the difference and sum formulas. For Lebesgue integration, require both contributions to be finite to avoid the undefined subtraction ∞−∞.[ref]
