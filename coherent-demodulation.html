Coherent demodulation

 Home Electrical engineering Amplitude modulation
OurBigBook.com Words: 171
Coherent demodulation recovers a message by multiplying the received signal by a reference carrier wave of the same frequency and known relative wave phase, then passing the result through a low-pass filter. For the ideal AM model and reference 2cos(ωc​t+φ),
2s(t)cos(ωc​t+φ)=Aq(t)cosφ+Aq(t)cos(2ωc​t+φ).
(10)
A unity-gain ideal low-pass filter passing 0 and Ω but rejecting 2ωc​ and 2ωc​±Ω leaves Aq(t)cosφ. Such a separating cutoff exists because Ω<2ωc​−Ω. At φ=0, subtraction of A recovers Amcos(Ωt) exactly for any finite m≥0, including overmodulation, provided the transmitter and channel preserve this linear waveform. This is the signed factor, not its absolute value.
A constant phase error scales the recovered message by cosφ: a π/3 radian error halves it, and a π/2 error removes it. A known error with nonzero cosine can be compensated in gain; a frequency mismatch makes the relative wave phase drift and no longer gives constant attenuation. The lecture illustrates synchronous multiplication, filtering and phase error.[ref]
