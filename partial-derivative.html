Partial derivative (∂)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Calculus Multivariable calculus
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 14
A partial derivative measures the local response of a function to one input while its other inputs stay fixed. For a function f on an open subset of R2, its partial derivatives at (a,b) are the limits
fx​(a,b)=limh→0​hf(a+h,b)−f(a,b)​,fy​(a,b)=limk→0​kf(a,b+k)−f(a,b)​,
(3)
when the respective limits exist. Each is an ordinary derivative of a one-variable slice: x↦f(x,b) or y↦f(a,y). This is why the other variable is treated as a constant during partial differentiation.[ref]
For an original example, take the polynomial function
f(x,y)=x2+xy+2y2.
(4)
Holding y fixed gives fx​=2x+y, while holding x fixed gives fy​=x+4y. At (1,1), the height is f=4 and the two partial derivatives are fx​=3 and fy​=5. The values answer different experiments on the same mathematical model: change x alone, or change y alone.
The two exact slices through that point are
f(1+t,1)=4+3t+t2,f(1,1+t)=4+5t+2t2.
(5)
Dividing either height change by t=0 gives 3+t or 5+2t; taking the limit t→0 recovers the two slopes. At t=0.1, the finite height changes are 0.31 and 0.52, although their linear approximations are 0.3 and 0.5. A derivative measures the limiting response, rather than an exact finite change.
For a function of n variables, ∂j​f(a) varies only its jth coordinate: it is the same difference quotient along ej​, the jth standard basis vector. This gives a practical sensitivity analysis: one can compare how a mathematical model responds to changes in its individual inputs, provided the chosen system of units and the quantities held fixed are stated. Comparing the numerical sizes of two partial derivatives in different units does not by itself identify a physically preferable input.
Existence of the partial derivatives at one point is weaker than differentiability. The example under partial derivatives do not imply continuity shows how both coordinate tests can miss a bad diagonal approach. When the first partial derivatives are continuous in a neighborhood, they do give differentiability there, allowing the total differential to combine all small input changes.
