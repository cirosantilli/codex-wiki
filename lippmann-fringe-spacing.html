Lippmann fringe spacing (d=λ0​/(2n))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Lippmann plate
OurBigBook.com Words: 325
At normal incidence, let z measure distance from the reflector into a uniform transparent layer with refractive index n. For a monochromatic wave of vacuum wavelength λ0​, the layer wavenumber is k=2πn/λ0​. Represent the incident and returning electric fields by complex wave amplitudes with time factor e−iωt suppressed:
Ei​(z)=E0​e−ikz,Er​(z)=rE0​eikz,r=∣r∣eiϕ.
(49)
The time-averaged squared electric field, normalized to the incident contribution, is
⟨Ei2​⟩⟨E2⟩​=1+∣r∣2+2∣r∣cos(2kz+ϕ).
(50)
This electric exposure pattern has maxima separated by
2kd=2π,d=kπ​=2nλ0​​.
(51)
The wave phase of the reflector shifts the maxima's positions but leaves their spacing unchanged in this uniform-layer model. The electric pattern, rather than the zero net energy flux of an ideal standing wave, is the relevant exposure proxy. An imperfect mirror reduces its contrast: (Imax​−Imin​)/(Imax​+Imin​)=2∣r∣/(1+∣r∣2), where I denotes this exposure proxy.
Choose λ0​=600nm and n=1.5. Then the medium wavelength is 400nm and d=200nm. For an ideal phase-reversing reflector, r=−1 and the normalized pattern is 4sin2(kz): its first two maxima are at z=100 and 300nm. These are chosen calculations, not measured properties of a historical photographic emulsion or the optical response of Mercury.
For multiple mutually incoherent wavelengths, time averaging adds exposure contributions rather than their instantaneous electric fields. In a linearized recording model,
D(z)∝∫S(λ0​)q(λ0​)[1+∣r(λ0​)∣2+2∣r(λ0​)∣cos(λ0​4πn(λ0​)z​+ϕ(λ0​))]dλ0​.
(52)
Here S is incident spectral exposure density, q a wavelength-dependent recording sensitivity and D a model exposure response. An optical spectrum therefore writes overlapping depth oscillations, not necessarily visibly separated sheets. This is a useful connection to the Fourier transform: approximately, depth is paired with optical spatial frequency 2n/λ0​. Finite recording depth, varying sensitivity and nonlinear photographic processing prevent treating this expression as an exact inversion rule for an actual plate.
