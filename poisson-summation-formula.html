Poisson summation formula (∑n∈Z​f(n)=∑k∈Z​f​(k))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 999 Articles: 5
The Poisson summation formula equates sample sums of a function and its Fourier transform. Exchanging broad and narrow sums can save work. Use the convention
f​(ξ)=∫R​f(x)e−2πiξxdx.
(9)
For every Schwartz function f∈S(R),
n∈Z∑​f(n)=k∈Z∑​f​(k).​
(10)
Both series have absolute convergence. A sufficient hypothesis is that f is smooth and supx​(1+∣x∣)M∣f(r)(x)∣<∞ for all nonnegative integers M,r; its Fourier transform is then also a Schwartz function.[ref] This assumption controls both decay and regularity; mere membership of an L1 space does not control values at individual sampling points, as periodization of an integrable function illustrates.
Here ξ counts cycles per unit length. In the angular convention used elsewhere in Fourier analysis, f​(ω)=∫f(x)e−iωxdx, so f​(ξ)=f​(2πξ) and the right-hand side becomes ∑k​f​(2πk). Keeping the Fourier transform convention explicit prevents stray 2π factors. Changing the sample spacing leads to the Poisson summation formula for a Euclidean lattice.
