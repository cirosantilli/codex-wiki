so u=e−(k2+1)t. The inverse Fourier transform gives
u(x,t)=2πe−t​∫R​e−tk2+ikxdk.

Complete the square as −tk2+ikx=−t(k−ix/(2t))2−x2/(4t) and use the permitted complex-shift Gaussian integral. Thus the heat kernel with constant absorption is
u(x,t)=4πt​1​exp(−t−4tx2​),t>0.​

Its mass is e−t, consistent with constant absorption, and it tends to the Dirac delta as a distribution as t↓0.
