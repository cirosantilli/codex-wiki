Write Fp​,Fq​ evaluated at the fixed pair (p(s),q(s)). Differentiating in τ gives xτ​=Fp​, yτ​=Fq​, and uτ​=pFp​+qFq​, proving uτ​=pxτ​+qyτ​.
Differentiate the other parameter. The difference of the required sides is
us​−pxs​−qys​=(u0′​−px0′​−qy0′​)+τ(p′Fp​+q′Fq​).
(39)
The first bracket is zero by the prescribed initial compatibility condition, and the second is zero by differentiating F(p(s),q(s))=0. Thus us​=pxs​+qys​ as well. These calculations use the differentiability of the supplied parametric functions.
If the coordinate Jacobian determinant J=xs​yτ​−ys​xτ​ is nonzero, the inverse function theorem gives local coordinates (x,y). The chain rule then makes (ux​−p,uy​−q) solve a homogeneous two-by-two linear system with determinant J. Therefore
ux​=p,uy​=q.​
(40)
This is the method of characteristics for a first-order equation depending only on the gradient.
