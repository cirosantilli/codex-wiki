For negative ϵ the fast arrows reverse: the outer branches become repelling and the middle branch attracting. On the middle branch the slow equation is x˙=x/(1−x2), so the slow motion goes away from the saddle equilibrium at zero towards a fold.
At (3,1), F=3−9+1=−5, so x˙>0: the trajectory immediately escapes to the right. At (1,−2), F=1−1/3−2=−4/3, so it also moves rapidly right and escapes. In each case the large-x balance is x˙∼x3/(3∣ϵ∣), with y decreasing; it is not a bounded relaxation cycle.

These are the small-negative-ϵ limiting descriptions; the fold locations and fast landing point receive small corrections. The eigenvalues of L are π/2​, −π/2​ and zero, none equal to one. On E=span{1,cos2x} the map L−I is an invertible two-dimensional operator; on E⊥ it is −I. Hence it is invertible on all of L2(0,π). Thus (L−I)y=sinx has exactly one solution. This is the finite-rank Fredholm alternative; no range obstruction survives the nonresonant identity term.

The quantity inside a fixed interval changes by influx at its left endpoint minus outflux at its right endpoint:
dtd​∫ab​ρdx=q(ρ(a,t))−q(ρ(b,t)).
(13)
In a smooth region the fundamental theorem of calculus turns this into ∫ab​[ρt​+∂x​q(ρ)]dx=0. Since the interval is arbitrary, the local conservation law is ρt​+∂x​q(ρ)=0​.
For a jump at x=S(t), write the two smooth pieces using a step function. The singular part of ρt​ is −S′[ρ]δ(x−S) and that of ∂x​q is [q]δ(x−S). Cancellation gives the Rankine-Hugoniot condition
S′[ρ]=[q],S′=[q]/[ρ]when [ρ]=0.​
(14)
The same identity follows by applying conservation to a shrinking moving control interval across the shock.

c OurBigBook.com
Before intersection, the characteristic map is x=ξ+ρ0​(ξ)3t, with derivative 1+3ρ02​ρ0′​t. Because the initial profile decreases, higher-density characteristics behind move faster than lower-density characteristics ahead. Compression therefore forms shocks, which absorb more characteristics. The far states remain one and zero, so a shock joining their limiting traces has speed tending to [q]/[ρ]=1/4.
The precise large-scale interpretation uses ζ=x/t. The rescaled initial transition tends to a step, and entropy comparison for the convex flux gives
ρ(ζt,t)⟶1 for ζ<1/4,ρ(ζt,t)⟶0 for ζ>1/4.​
(18)
One way to see the comparison is to choose distant initial points beyond which the profile is within δ of each far state. Step profiles with those nearby states bracket it; their shock speeds tend to 1/4 as δ↓0, while their fixed initial locations disappear after division by t.
A fixed translation of the shock can persist. If the deviation from a reference step is integrable, its signed mass fixes a phase shift. Without such a tail assumption one should assert convergence on the x/t scale and S(t)/t→1/4, rather than claim that the exact line x=t/4 is the unshifted asymptote for every smooth initial transition.

The stated pointwise boundary limits do not imply uniqueness. A counterexample to homogeneous uniqueness is
w(x,y)=y2x​e−x/y=0.​
(32)
It is smooth for x,y>0. Writing η=x/y, differentiation gives
xwxx​=y2η(η−2)​e−η=wy​.
(33)
For fixed positive y, w→0 as x→0 and as x→∞; for fixed positive x, it also tends to zero as y↓0. Thus if u0​ is one solution, u0​+Cw satisfies the same equation and every printed boundary limit for any constant C. Part (d) supplies such a particular solution, so this is a genuine family of distinct solutions, not merely a formal obstruction.
This is corner-source nonuniqueness for a degenerate parabolic problem. The peak w(y,y)=1/(ey) diverges towards the missing corner (0,0); pointwise initial limits at each fixed x>0 do not exclude it. The preceding maximum argument fails to force uniqueness because a supremum over an entire past strip need not be attained or finite.
A customary bounded-solution condition restores the intended result. If the difference z of two solutions is bounded on every finite time strip, subtract δ(1+x+y). Its operator x∂x2​−∂y​ equals δ>0, excluding a positive past maximum; the linear barrier controls spatial infinity, and the homogeneous initial/boundary values control the parabolic boundary. Letting δ↓0 gives z≤0; applying the same argument to −z gives z=0. This proves uniqueness in that additional bounded class, which the printed pointwise limits alone do not specify.
