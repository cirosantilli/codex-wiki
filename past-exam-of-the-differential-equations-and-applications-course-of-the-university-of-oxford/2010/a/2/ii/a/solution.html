Use the cosine difference identity to separate the kernel:
k(x,t)=cos2xcos2t+21​sin2xsin2t.
(14)
Thus L has image E=span{c,s}, where c(x)=cos2x, s(x)=sin2x. Their squared norms in L2(0,π) are π/2 and they are orthogonal. Consequently
Lc=(π/2)c,Ls=(π/4)s.
(15)
Every nonzero eigenvalue must belong to this two-dimensional image, so the complete list of eigenvalues of finite multiplicity is
λ=π/2 with eigenfunctions Ac,λ=π/4 with eigenfunctions Bs,A,B=0.​
(16)
Each has multiplicity one. On E⊥ the operator vanishes, giving the zero eigenvalue infinite multiplicity. For example y(x)=1​ is a nonzero element of the kernel because both relevant integrals over the interval vanish.
