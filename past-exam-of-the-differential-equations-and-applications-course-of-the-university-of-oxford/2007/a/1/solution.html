Use dimensionless variables X=X∗​x, Y=Y∗​y, Z=Z∗​z, t=Tτ, with
Y∗​=b1​,Z∗​=mbg​,X∗​=rmblg​,T=rmklg​.​
(1)
Substitution gives the requested system, with dots now meaning differentiation with respect to τ, and
α=kab​,ϵ=l2g2rm2k​,δ=lgrk​,γ=b23c​.​
(2)
In particular the same scale for Y makes the first two coefficients of the cubic equal in magnitude.
Its derivative is f′(y)=1−2y+γy2. For 0<γ<1 its two simple zeros are
y±​=γ1±1−γ​​.​
(3)
The cubic increases below y−​, decreases between the two fold points of a critical manifold, and increases above y+​. It has a local maximum at y−​ and a local minimum at y+​.
After a rapidly decaying transient response, z≃f(y) when its relaxation is faster than the other variables. The reduced autonomous system is
x˙=α−y,y˙​=ϵx−f(y)​.​
(4)
For ϵ≪1, the fast y dynamics at fixed x attracts to the branches where f′(y)>0 and repels from the middle branch. On the low branch y<y−​<α, x increases slowly. At its fold point of a critical manifold x=f(y−​), the attracting low branch disappears and y jumps upward, with nearly constant x, to the high branch. On that branch y>y+​>α, x decreases slowly. At x=f(y+​) it jumps back to the low branch. These slow arcs and fast jumps form the singular relaxation cycle. For small positive ϵ, and parameters bounded away from the fold point of a critical manifold-endpoint cases, the nearby attracting periodic motion has the same pattern. The equilibrium (f(α),α) is on the repelling branch: its Jacobian matrix has determinant 1/ϵ>0 and trace −f′(α)/ϵ>0.
The limiting landing values are 3/γ−2y−​ after the upward jump and 3/γ−2y+​ after the downward jump, obtained from the third root of f(y)=f(y±​). Thus the two vertical segments in the phase plane plot really connect roots of the same fast equation.
If γ>1, f′ is strictly positive everywhere, so the cubic is monotone and has no fold points of a critical manifold. The reduced equilibrium is asymptotically stable. In fact
V=21​[x−f(α)]2+2ϵ​(y−α)2,V˙=−(y−α)[f(y)−f(α)]≤0.
(5)
The only invariant trajectory in the zero-derivative set is the equilibrium, so bounded reduced solutions converge to it; relaxation oscillations disappear.
The stronger condition δ≪ϵ​ matters during a fast jump: y then varies on the ϵ time scale, not on the slow unit scale. Only if z adjusts faster still may one keep z≃f(y) through that jump. Merely imposing δ≪1 would not justify this reduction on all pieces of the cycle. This is a three-time-scale cubic relaxation oscillator.
