For γ<0, the potential grows to infinity in both directions, so every nonstationary finite-energy trajectory is trapped between two turning points and is periodic; the zero-energy solution is stationary. The case γ=0 is the harmonic oscillator. The negative-γ comparison extends the parameter range initially stated in the source.
For γ>0, the origin lies in a local potential well, with barriers at x=±γ−1/2 of height 1/(4γ). A trajectory launched from the origin oscillates precisely when its initial energy is below that barrier:
∣v∣<2γ​1​.​

At equality the trajectory approaches a barrier equilibrium asymptotically, rather than being periodic; for example the positive branch is x=γ−1/2tanh(t/2​). Above the barrier the trajectory escapes the well. The source's inequality without the absolute value tacitly takes v≥0; for signed initial velocity the energy criterion requires ∣v∣.
Next set β=γ=0 and assume α>0. The energy H=(x˙2+x2)/2 satisfies H˙=−αx2x˙2≤0. This bounds the complete trajectory. The zero-dissipation set is the union of x=0 and x˙=0, and its only invariant trajectory is the origin: a nonzero point on either axis immediately leaves it under the differential equation. The LaSalle invariance principle therefore gives x(t)→0,x˙(t)→0​. If α=0 as well, damping is absent and that conclusion fails for nonzero v; the positive order-one damping assumption is essential.
