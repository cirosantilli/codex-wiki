There is no continuously differentiable solution realizing the first data set on the entire stated initial line. At (x,y)=(1/4,0), u=3/4, v​=3/8, ux​=1 and vx​=−3/8. The minus left eigenvector is (1,−8/3) and its eigenvalue is zero. The differential equation there would require ℓ−T​Ux​=ℓ−T​C, but its two sides are respectively 2 and 0. This is an explicit compatibility contradiction, not merely a loss of a preferred formula. Also, the proposed simple expression independent of y does not solve the equation for x<1, because then vx​<0 but v​>0.

Above the parabola the footpoint is η=(y−x2)/(1+4x)>0; below it the plus footpoint is η=y−x2<0. Each branch directly satisfies both equations and v>0. They agree continuously on y=x2, but their y derivatives are 0 and 2/(1+4x), respectively. Thus the continuation is piecewise classical and not continuously differentiable across the parabola. The source's globally continuously differentiable formulation needs this qualification. If only positive initial y is intended, the upper rational formula is a classical continuation throughout the positive quadrant, but the region y<x2 requires incoming information from y=0 and is not determined by that positive half of the initial line alone. This illustrates loss of differentiability from square-root hyperbolic source data.
