The stated pointwise boundary limits do not imply uniqueness. A counterexample to homogeneous uniqueness is
w(x,y)=y2x​e−x/y=0.​
(32)
It is smooth for x,y>0. Writing η=x/y, differentiation gives
xwxx​=y2η(η−2)​e−η=wy​.
(33)
For fixed positive y, w→0 as x→0 and as x→∞; for fixed positive x, it also tends to zero as y↓0. Thus if u0​ is one solution, u0​+Cw satisfies the same equation and every printed boundary limit for any constant C. Part (d) supplies such a particular solution, so this is a genuine family of distinct solutions, not merely a formal obstruction.
This is corner-source nonuniqueness for a degenerate parabolic problem. The peak w(y,y)=1/(ey) diverges towards the missing corner (0,0); pointwise initial limits at each fixed x>0 do not exclude it. The preceding maximum argument fails to force uniqueness because a supremum over an entire past strip need not be attained or finite.
A customary bounded-solution condition restores the intended result. If the difference z of two solutions is bounded on every finite time strip, subtract δ(1+x+y). Its operator x∂x2​−∂y​ equals δ>0, excluding a positive past maximum; the linear barrier controls spatial infinity, and the homogeneous initial/boundary values control the parabolic boundary. Letting δ↓0 gives z≤0; applying the same argument to −z gives z=0. This proves uniqueness in that additional bounded class, which the printed pointwise limits alone do not specify.
