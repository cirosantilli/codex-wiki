With A invertible, write the system as ux​+Muy​=d, M=A−1B. Real distinct eigenvalues of M give strict hyperbolicity; a real complete eigenbasis gives the usual diagonalizable hyperbolic form. The characteristic curves satisfy dy/dx=λi​. Left eigenvectors form characteristic dependent combinations wi​=ℓi​u; for constant coefficients they obey separate transport equations. Characteristic coordinates are functions constant on those curves, such as y−λi​x in the constant case. For variable coefficients extra derivatives of the eigenvectors can couple the combinations; local characteristic directions still follow the real matrix pencil. If A is singular one first chooses a noncharacteristic coordinate direction with an invertible coefficient combination.
For the specified constants,
M=(5−6​3−4​),d=(3−2​).
(26)
Its speeds are 2 and −1, with left eigenvectors (2,1) and (1,1). Hence
w+​=2u+v,(w+​)x​+2(w+​)y​=4,w−​=u+v,(w−​)x​−(w−​)y​=1.
(27)
The transported Riemann invariants are R+​=2u+v−4x,R−​=u+v−x​, constant on y−2x=constant and y+x=constant, respectively. Initial data give w+​(0,s)=s−sin(s/2) and w−​(0,s)=−es, so
w+​=y+2x−sin(y/2−x),w−​=−ey+x+x.
(28)
Invert these two combinations:
u=ey+x+y+x−sin(y/2−x),v=−2ey+x−y+sin(y/2−x).​
(29)
Both characteristic feet must lie on the supplied positive initial half-line. Thus
y−2x>0,y+x>0,equivalently y>max(2x,−x).​
(30)
Outside this sector at least one invariant needs unsupplied initial data. The closed expressions give one possible extension there but are not uniquely forced by the question's initial half-line.
