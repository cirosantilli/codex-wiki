The square-root reduction of a quadratic eikonal equation explains why two initial normal derivatives are possible. Set F=p2+q2−4u. Charpit's equations are x˙=2p, y˙​=2q, u˙=2p2+2q2=8u, p˙​=4p, q˙​=4q. Parametrize the initial semicircle by (x0​,y0​)=(coss,sins), −π/2<s<π/2. Tangential compatibility gives −p0​sins+q0​coss=0, and F=0 gives p02​+q02​=4. Thus there are two normal-gradient choices, p0​=2εcoss, q0​=2εsins, ε=±1.
Integration gives the requested parametric solutions
xyu​=[1+ε(e4t−1)]coss,=[1+ε(e4t−1)]sins,=e8t,p=2εe4tcoss,q=2εe4tsins.​​
(22)
For ε=+1, r=x2+y2​=e4t, so u+​=r2 on the whole right half-plane. For ε=−1, before focusing at the origin, r=2−e4t, so u−​=(2−r)2 on x>0, 0<r<2. The origin is a caustic for this inward family. The circle r=2 is approached only as characteristic time tends to negative infinity, where u and its gradient vanish. It cannot be crossed by this initial characteristic chart. Thus the initial data alone permit two branches; each is locally unique only after its normal-gradient sign is chosen.
On the added half-line write x0​=a≤0, y0​=1, u0​=1. Tangential differentiation gives p0​=0 and q0​=2δ, δ=±1. Its characteristics give
x=a,y=1+δ(e4t−1),u=e8t.​
(23)
The plus branch is u=y2 on x≤0,y>0; the minus branch is u=(2−y)2 on x≤0,y<2. On the positive y-axis, the semicircle traces are y2 and (2−y)2, respectively. Therefore only matching sign choices are continuous throughout their common positive-axis coverage. Mixed choices agree merely at the initial junction y=1.
For the matching-plus branch the data-covered open domain is
D+​={x>0}∪{y>0},u+​={x2+y2,y2,​x>0,x≤0, y>0.​​
(24)
For the matching-minus branch it is
D−​={x>0, x2+y2<4}∪{x<0, y<2}∪{x=0, 0<y<2}.​
(25)
Its formula is (2−x2+y2​)2 in the right portion and (2−y)2 in the left portion. The origin is excluded. The negative y-axis must also be a cut: the two formulas have different traces there, so including it would destroy continuity. Both matching solutions are C1 across the included positive-axis seam; generally their second transverse derivatives differ, which is compatible with this first-order problem. Values beyond these characteristic charts can sometimes be continued, but are not selected by the initial data through a nonsingular characteristic graph.
