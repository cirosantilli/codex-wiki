For fixed source (ξ,η)∈D, define the Dirichlet Green function by −Δx,y​G=δ(ξ,η)​ in D, G=0 on ∂D. It is harmonic away from the source and has singularity −(2π)−1log(x−ξ)2+(y−η)2​. The outward normal vector derivative is ∂n​G=n⋅∇x,y​G at a boundary point of arc-length parameter s.
Green's second identity gives ∫D​(uΔG−GΔu)=∫∂D​(u∂n​G−G∂n​u)ds. Substituting the two PDEs and the boundary data yields
−u(ξ,η)+∫D​fG=∫∂D​g∂n​G,u(ξ,η)=−∫∂D​g∂n​Gds+∫D​fGdxdy.​
(31)
The singular version follows by excising a small source disk and taking its radius to zero, or directly by the distributional identity.
For the upper half-plane use the opposite-sign image source at (ξ,−η):
G=4π1​log(x−ξ)2+(y−η)2(x−ξ)2+(y+η)2​.​
(32)
The image lies outside the domain, so it is harmonic inside; the true source has the required unit singularity, and the two distances agree at y=0, giving zero boundary value. On that boundary n=−ey​, so
∂n​G=−π[(s−ξ)2+η2]η​.
(33)
The assumed far-field decay makes the large semicircle contribution vanish. With f=0, the representation becomes
u(ξ,η)=πη​∫−∞∞​(ξ−s)2+η2g(s)​ds.​
(34)
Finally set s=ξ+ηtanθ, ds=ηsec2θdθ. The Jacobian matrix cancels the denominator, giving
u(ξ,η)=π1​∫−π/2π/2​g(ξ+ηtanθ)dθ.​
(35)
If g is even, the substitution s↦−s in the Poisson integral gives u(−ξ,η)=u(ξ,η). At η=0 this agrees with the continuous even boundary trace.
