Probability generating function of an independent sum (GX+Y​(s)=GX​(s)GY​(s))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Probability generating function
OurBigBook.com Words: 190
For nonnegative integer-valued independent random variables X,Y, factor the expected value of a product:
GX+Y​(s)=E[sXsY]=E[sX]E[sY]=GX​(s)GY​(s),∣s∣≤1.
(40)
The Cauchy product explains what this means for individual probability masses:
P(X+Y=n)=∑j=0n​P(X=j)P(Y=n−j).
(41)
Every way of splitting the total contributes once. This is convolution of independent random variables; multiplying probability generating functions performs the bookkeeping automatically.
For an original example, take independent X∼Bernoulli(0.3) and Y∼Binomial(2,0.5), using the Bernoulli distribution and binomial distribution. Then
GX​(s)=0.7+0.3s,GY​(s)=4(1+s)2​,
(42)
so
GX+Y​(s)=4(0.7+0.3s)(1+s)2​=0.175+0.425s+0.325s2+0.075s3.
(43)
Direct enumeration of the six pairs (x,y) gives the same probability masses. For example, total one collects (0,1) and (1,0), with contributions 0.7⋅0.5+0.3⋅0.25=0.425. The coefficients sum to one. The expected value is GX+Y′​(1)=1.3; since GX+Y′′​(1)=1.1, the variance is 1.1+1.3−1.32=0.71, agreeing with 0.3⋅0.7+2⋅0.5⋅0.5.
Uncorrelated random variables are insufficient for the product identity. For a concrete counterexample, let U be uniform on {−1,0,1} and set A=U+1, B=U2. Then Cov(A,B)=E[U3]=0, but P(A+B=0)=0, whereas GA​(0)GB​(0)=1/9. Zero covariance controls one mixed moment, not the full joint probability mass function.
