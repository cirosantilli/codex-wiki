Harmonic mean (H=n/(∑i​1/xi​))

OurBigBook.com Words: 449 Articles: 1
The harmonic mean of a nonempty finite list of positive real numbers is the multiplicative inverse of the arithmetic mean of their inverses:
H=∑i=1n​1/xi​n​,Hn​=∑i=1n​xi​1​.
(28)
For two inputs, H=2x1​x2​/(x1​+x2​). Replacing each input by H preserves the sum of inverses, making it useful when a fixed amount divided by a rate determines a cost or duration. This positive-input definition avoids division by zero; negative inputs need not retain the usual averaging or ordering properties. [ref].
Travel two legs of equal length d at positive speeds v1​,v2​, with no stops. Their travel times are d/v1​ and d/v2​, so the average speed is
vavg​=d/v1​+d/v2​2d​=1/v1​+1/v2​2​.
(29)
For two 60km legs at 30 and 60km/h, the times are two hours and one hour. The average speed is 120/3=40km/h. The same speeds maintained for one hour each instead cover 90km in two hours, giving the arithmetic mean 45km/h. A slower equal-distance leg occupies more time, so the data being averaged must include what is held equal. This is total distance traveled divided by elapsed time; a return journey can have zero average velocity while keeping a positive average speed. [ref].
The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality applied to the inverses gives H≤G; together with G≤A it yields the comparison in geometric mean. For two inputs the arithmetic–harmonic gap has the exact form
A−H=2(x1​+x2​)(x1​−x2​)2​≥0.
(30)
For 30 and 60 the gap is five. With one positive input held fixed and another approaching zero, the harmonic mean approaches zero too: a very slow fixed-distance leg dominates the travel time. Unequal leg lengths instead require a weighted harmonic mean.
