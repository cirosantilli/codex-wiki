Time-evolution operator (U(t))

 ... Quantum mechanics Quantum system Quantum state Observable Hamiltonian (quantum mechanics) Unitary time evolution
OurBigBook.com Words: 3k Articles: 21
The time-evolution operator maps an initial quantum state to its state at a later time. For a time-independent Hamiltonian operator H, it is the unitary operator U(t)=e−iHt/ℏ.
More generally, write ∣ψ(t)⟩=U(t,t0​)∣ψ(t0​)⟩. The time-evolution operator obeys
U(t0​,t0​)=I,U(t2​,t0​)=U(t2​,t1​)U(t1​,t0​),U(t,t0​)−1=U(t,t0​)†=U(t0​,t).
(46)
For a constant self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator, U(t,t0​)=exp[−iH(t−t0​)/ℏ]. With zero as the initial time, this gives the group law U(t+s)=U(t)U(s). Successive evolution is reversible; a measurement followed by conditioning on an outcome is a separate operation described by projective measurement.
For a time-dependent Hamiltonian operator, the defining equation is iℏ∂t​U(t,t0​)=H(t)U(t,t0​). In finite dimension, a continuous Hermitian H(t) still gives unitary time evolution, since
dtd​(U†U)=ℏi​U†H(t)U−ℏi​U†H(t)U=0.
(47)
The solution is a time-ordered exponential, with later factors acting on the left. One may replace it by exp[−i∫t0​t​H(s)ds/ℏ] when [H(s),H(s′)]=0 at all times; independently changing pulse axes generally requires time ordering.[ref]
