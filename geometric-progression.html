Geometric progression (an​=arn)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Real analysis Sequence and series Sequence
OurBigBook.com Words: 252 Articles: 1
A geometric progression is a sequence obtained by repeatedly multiplying by one fixed common ratio r:
a0​=a,an+1​=ran​,an​=arn(n≥0).
(7)
Here a and r may be real numbers or complex numbers. The recurrence is meaningful even when a=0 or r=0; expressing r as a ratio of successive terms requires a nonzero denominator. As a polynomial convention, r0=1, including at r=0.
For example, quantities 100,80,64,51.2,… retain four fifths of their preceding value at every step. The decrement changes, but the retained fraction does not. With the same initial value and common ratio 2, the terms are 100,200,400,800,…. This multiplicative structure is useful whenever the same proportional rule repeats: it turns many updates into the single exponentiation rn. An arithmetic progression instead repeats an additive increment.
For nonzero a, the modulus of an​ tends to zero when ∣r∣<1, stays fixed when ∣r∣=1, and grows without bound when ∣r∣>1. For a negative real common ratio, successive nonzero terms also alternate in sign. Thus the terms 1,−1/2,1/4,−1/8,… decay in size while crossing zero at every step. Adding the terms gives a geometric series; the individual terms and their accumulated partial sums are different sequences.
