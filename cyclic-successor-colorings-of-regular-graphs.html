Cyclic successor colorings of regular graphs

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Probabilistic combinatorics Lovász local lemma
OurBigBook.com Words: 176
For each fixed integer q≥2, every sufficiently high-degree finite regular graph has an assignment of q colors such that every vertex has a neighbour whose color is its successor modulo q. This assignment need not be a proper vertex coloring. If the degree is r, independent uniform vertex colors make a given vertex fail with probability (1−1/q)r. That event depends on its closed neighbourhood, and has at most r2 dependencies, since only centres within distance two matter. The symmetric Lovász local lemma therefore gives such an assignment whenever e(r2+1)(1−1/q)r≤1, which holds for all sufficiently large r.
Choose one successor neighbour from each vertex. The resulting directed graph has outdegree one and hence contains a directed cycle. Its color increases once modulo q on each step, so its length is divisible by q. Thus a nontrivial cycle of length a multiple of q is obtained when q≥3; for q=2 a directed two-cycle may simply traverse one undirected edge twice, so this last cycle inference needs a separate argument if an undirected simple cycle is required.
