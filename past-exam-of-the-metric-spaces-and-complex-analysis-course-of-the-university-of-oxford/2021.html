A metric space (X,d) is a complete metric space if every Cauchy sequence in X converges to a point of X. Explicitly, the hypothesis is
∀ε>0 ∃N ∀m,n≥N: d(xm​,xn​)<ε,
(1)
and the conclusion is that some x∈X satisfies d(xn​,x)→0.
It is a sequentially compact space if every sequence in X has a convergent subsequence whose limit of a sequence belongs to X.
It is a connected space if there are no disjoint nonempty open subsets U,V of X with X=U∪V. Here openness refers to the metric topology of X.
It is a path-connected space if, for every x,y∈X, there is a continuous map γ:[0,1]→X with γ(0)=x and γ(1)=y. Such a map is a path. Every path-connected space is a connected space: a separation would pull back along a path joining its two sides to a separation of the interval [0,1].

A norm on a real vector space X is a map ∥⋅∥:X→[0,∞) such that, for x,y∈X and λ∈R,
∥x∥=0⟺x=0,∥λx∥=∣λ∣∥x∥,∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥.
(15)
The last property is the triangle inequality; the middle property is absolute homogeneity of a norm. The induced metric is
d(x,y)=∥x−y∥.​
(16)
Positivity and separation follow from the norm axioms, symmetry from absolute homogeneity of a norm with λ=−1, and the triangle inequality from x−z=(x−y)+(y−z).

Equivalently, the zeros of a holomorphic function which is not identically zero cannot accumulate inside its domain.
Because f,g never vanish, their logarithmic derivatives are holomorphic. The holomorphic function
