Let (Fn​) be a Cauchy sequence for the Hausdorff distance, and put
δn​=supp,q≥n​ρ(Fp​,Fq​),An​=⋃m≥n​Fm​​.
(11)
The bounded set hypothesis makes δn​ finite, and the Cauchy sequence property gives δn​→0. Each An​ is nonempty and a closed set, with An+1​⊆An​.
We need estimates that survive taking the closure. For every nonempty F, the distance to a set is Lipschitz continuous with constant one: the triangle inequality gives dist(x,F)≤d(x,y)+dist(y,F), and interchanging x,y proves
∣dist(x,F)−dist(y,F)∣≤d(x,y).
(12)
For every m≥n, points of Fm​ have distance at most δn​ from Fn​. The same holds throughout their union and its closure, by this continuity. Since Fn​⊆An​,
ρ(Fn​,An​)≤δn​.
(13)
More generally, if m≥n, every point in ⋃k≥n​Fk​ is within δn​ of Fm​⊆Am​. Passing to its closure again, and using Am​⊆An​, gives
ρ(An​,Am​)≤δn​.
(14)
Choose strictly increasing indices nj​ with δnj​​≤2−j. Then (Anj​​) satisfies part (ii), so it converges in Hausdorff distance to the nonempty closed set G=⋂j​Anj​​=⋂n​An​, with ρ(Anj​​,G)≤21−j.
For n≥nj​, the triangle inequality gives the explicit full-sequence bound
ρ(Fn​,G)≤ρ(Fn​,Fnj​​)+ρ(Fnj​​,Anj​​)+ρ(Anj​​,G)≤2δnj​​+21−j≤22−j.
(15)
It tends to zero as j→∞. Therefore
X complete ⟹ (F(X),ρ) complete​.
(16)
This proves Completeness of the Hausdorff hyperspace using the proposed tail closures, rather than assuming their diameters shrink.
