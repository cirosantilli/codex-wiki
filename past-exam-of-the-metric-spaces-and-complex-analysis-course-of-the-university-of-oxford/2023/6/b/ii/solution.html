Let V=f(U), the right half-disc from part (i). Write w=reit with 0<r<1 and −π/2<t<π/2. Squaring doubles the angle and squares the radius, giving a holomorphic bijection
w⟼w2: V⟶W=D∖(−1,0].
(74)
Its inverse is the unique square root with positive real part, and its derivative 2w is nonzero on V. The cut includes zero, because 0∈/V.
Next, the Möbius transformation T(s)=i(1−s)/(1+s) is a conformal bijection from D onto H: it is the inverse Cayley transform between the half-plane and disk composed with s↦−s. For s=−t, 0≤t<1, it gives
T(−t)=i1−t1+t​.
(75)
Consequently T((−1,0])={iy:y≥1}. Since T is a bijection on the whole disc, deleting this interval deletes exactly that ray in the image.
The required function composition is therefore
g(z)=i1+f(z)21−f(z)2​=1−z22z​​.
(76)
Each component is a conformal bijection between the indicated domains. The only possible denominator zeros of the simplified expression are z=±1, outside U, and
g′(z)=(1−z2)22(1+z2)​=0(z∈U),
(77)
since i is on the excluded semicircle and −i is outside the upper half-plane. This also checks the local conformality of the conformal map from the exterior half-disc to a slit half-plane.
