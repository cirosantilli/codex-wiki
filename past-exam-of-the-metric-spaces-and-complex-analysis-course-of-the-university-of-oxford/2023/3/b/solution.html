A sufficient condition is that U is a connected space. Suppose, towards a contradiction, that ∣f∣ is the same constant R throughout U. If R=0, then f=0, contradicting f(a)=f(b).
If R>0, differentiate u2+v2=R2 in x and y, and use the Cauchy-Riemann equations from part (a). They give
uux​+vvx​=0,vux​−uvx​=0.
(32)
The coefficient matrix for (ux​,vx​) has determinant −(u2+v2)=−R2=0. Hence ux​=vx​=0, so f′=0 everywhere. A holomorphic function with zero complex derivative is locally constant: within a small disc, differentiate along a straight segment. Local constancy and connectedness imply constancy on U, again a contradiction.
Consequently,
U connected and f(a)=f(b) ⟹ ∃c,d∈U: ∣f(c)∣=∣f(d)∣​.
(33)
This is the constant-modulus holomorphic function principle. The extra hypothesis matters: on two disjoint open discs, define f=1 on one and f=−1 on the other. It is holomorphic and nonconstant, yet ∣f∣=1 everywhere.
