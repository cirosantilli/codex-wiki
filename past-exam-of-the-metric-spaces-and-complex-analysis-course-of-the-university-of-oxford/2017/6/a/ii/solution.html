Treat a straight line together with infinity as a generalized circle on the Riemann sphere. Its finite points have a real equation
A∣z∣2+Bz+Bz+C=0,A,C∈R.
(34)
Translations and nonzero complex scalings preserve circles and lines. Under z=1/w, multiplying the equation by ∣w∣2 gives
C∣w∣2+Bw+Bw+A=0,
(35)
again the equation of a circle or line. Nondegeneracy is preserved because the reciprocal map is a bijection of the sphere. The zero and infinite points are interpreted there, rather than discarded when multiplying denominators.

Thus Möbius translation-scaling-inversion factorization and the reciprocal transformation of a generalized circle prove every generalized circle maps to another generalized circle. A circle becomes a line exactly when its image contains infinity, that is, when the original circle contains the map's pole.
