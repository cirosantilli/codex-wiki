Write Mn​(f,g)=sup0≤t≤n​∣f(t)−g(t)∣. The given metric describes locally uniform convergence: d(fj​,f)→0 if and only if Mn​(fj​,f)→0 for every fixed n. Indeed,
2−nmin{Mn​(fj​,f),1}≤d(fj​,f),
(6)
so convergence in d forces Mn​→0. Conversely, split the defining series at N: its tail is at most ∑n>N​2−n=2−N, and its finitely many initial terms tend to zero.
If (fj​) is a Cauchy sequence in d, the same single-term estimate makes it a Cauchy sequence in the supremum norm on every [0,n]. The completeness of the continuous-map space in the uniform metric gives a continuous function Fn​ on [0,n] with fj​→Fn​ uniformly there. Uniqueness of the limit of a sequence gives Fn+1​∣[0,n]​=Fn​, so these functions define a single continuous function f on [0,∞). The preceding finite-head and tail argument proves d(fj​,f)→0. Thus the space is complete.
The constant functions fj​(t)=j have d(fj​,fk​)=1 whenever j=k. They have no convergent subsequence, so the space is not sequentially compact.
For fixed f,g, set γ(s)=(1−s)f+sg. On [0,n],
Mn​(γ(s),γ(s′))=∣s−s′∣Mn​(f,g)⟶0
(7)
as s′→s. The equivalence with locally uniform convergence proves that γ is a continuous path for d. Hence the space is path-connected and connected.
