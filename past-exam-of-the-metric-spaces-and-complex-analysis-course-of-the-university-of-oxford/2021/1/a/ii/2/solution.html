Distinct natural numbers have d(x,y)>1. Taking ε=1 in the Cauchy sequence condition forces every sufficiently late pair of terms to be equal. Every Cauchy sequence is therefore eventually constant and converges in N, proving completeness.
The sequence xn​=n has no convergent subsequence, since convergence would force its sufficiently late terms to lie within distance 1 of its limit of a sequence, hence to equal that limit. Thus sequential compactness fails.
Every singleton is an open ball of radius 1, so the metric topology is the discrete topology. For example, {1} and N∖{1} form a separation into two nonempty open sets, so X is not connected. It is consequently not path-connected either.
