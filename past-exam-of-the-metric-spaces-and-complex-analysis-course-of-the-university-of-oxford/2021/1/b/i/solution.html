The inverse tangent T(x)=arctanx is a homeomorphism from the real line with its Euclidean metric onto I=(−π/2,π/2) with its subspace topology: T and its inverse tan are continuous. It is also an isometry from (R,ρ) onto I, because ρ(x,y)=∣T(x)−T(y)∣. Consequently, for U⊆R,
U is d-open⟺T(U) is open in I⟺U is ρ-open.​
(8)
For a direct open ball interpretation, Bρ​(x,r)=T−1((T(x)−r,T(x)+r)∩I) is d-open by continuity of T. Conversely, continuity of tan at T(x) supplies a sufficiently small ρ-ball inside any prescribed d-ball about x. Thus both metrics generate exactly the same open sets.
