First verify that V0​ is a vector space. Sums and scalar multiples preserve continuity and the condition at zero. For every partition of an interval D, the triangle inequality and absolute homogeneity of a norm of the absolute value imply
V(f+g,D)≤V(f,D)+V(g,D),V(λf,D)=∣λ∣V(f,D).
(17)
Taking suprema yields
V(f+g)≤V(f)+V(g),V(λf)=∣λ∣V(f).
(18)
For λ=0 the second equality follows directly from V(0)=0. In particular, finite variation is preserved, so V0​ is closed under these operations.
Nonnegativity is immediate. For any t∈[0,1], use the partition of an interval (0,t,1), omitting repeated endpoints if necessary, to get
∣f(t)∣=∣f(t)−f(0)∣≤V(f).
(19)
Thus V(f)=0 forces f=0, and conversely f=0 has zero total variation of a function. These observations prove that ∥f∥=V(f) is a norm on V0​.
Applying the same bound to fn​−f gives
∥fn​−f∥∞​≤V(fn​−f)=∥fn​−f∥.​
(20)
Therefore convergence in the anchored variation norm implies uniform convergence on [0,1]. The anchoring condition f(0)=0 removes the nonzero constant functions which otherwise have zero total variation of a function.
