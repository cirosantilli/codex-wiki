Use the principal logarithm Logz=log∣z∣+iArgz with −π<Argz<π, whose branch cut is the negative real axis. For this choice,
F(z)=(z2+a2)Logz1​
(74)
has three simple poles away from the cut: z=1, z=ia, and z=−ia. The pole at 1, caused by Log1=0, must be included.
Choose 0<ε<min{1,a} and R>max{1,a}, and use a positively oriented keyhole contour around the negative real axis: the large circle is traversed counterclockwise, the upper bank from −R to −ε, the small circle clockwise, and the lower bank from −ε to −R. Formally, use radial segments at arguments ±(π−η), then let η↓0 with R,ε fixed. The function has continuous limiting values on each compact bank, which justifies this limit.
Put L=loga. The residues are
Res(F,1)=1+a21​,Res(F,ia)=2ia(L+iπ/2)1​,Res(F,−ia)=−2ia(L−iπ/2)1​.
(75)
The first follows from Logz=(z−1)+O((z−1)2); the other two follow from the simple zeros of z2+a2. Their sum is
Σ=1+a21​−2a(L2+π2/4)π​.
(76)
These expressions are valid also for a=1, since L±iπ/2 never vanish for real L.
On the outer circle, ∣Logz∣≥logR for R>1 and ∣z2+a2∣≥R2−a2. On the inner circle, ∣Logz∣≥∣logε∣ and ∣z2+a2∣≥a2−ε2. Thus the estimation lemma bounds the two arc integrals by
(R2−a2)logR2πR​⟶0,(a2−ε2)∣logε∣2πε​⟶0,
(77)
respectively, as R→∞ and ε↓0.
On the upper bank, z=−x+i0 gives Logz=logx+iπ; on the lower bank it gives logx−iπ. Accounting for the opposite orientations, their combined integral is
∫εR​x2+a21​(logx+iπ1​−logx−iπ1​)dx​=−2πi∫εR​(x2+a2)((logx)2+π2)dx​.​
(78)
The real improper integral converges: its integrand is bounded near zero by 1/(a2π2) and at infinity by 1/(π2x2). Taking the arc limits in the residue theorem gives −2πiI=2πiΣ, hence
∫0∞​(x2+a2)((logx)2+π2)dx​=2a((loga)2+π2/4)π​−1+a21​.​
(79)
The final subtraction is exactly the contribution of the pole at 1.
