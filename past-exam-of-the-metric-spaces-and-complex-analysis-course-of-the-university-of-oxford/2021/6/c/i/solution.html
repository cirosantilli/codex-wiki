There is no such map for the domain printed in the paper. The planar domain A=C∖[a,b] is not simply connected. For example, a sufficiently large positively oriented circle γ surrounds [a,b] and lies in A, while
∫γ​z−adz​=2πi=0.
(91)
The integrand is holomorphic on A. If A were simply connected, the Cauchy integral theorem would force this integral to be zero. A conformal bijection is a homeomorphism and preserves simple connectivity, whereas H is simply connected. Thus a conformal bijection A→H is impossible.
The explicit construction also pinpoints the missing point. Define
T(z)=z−bz−a​.
(92)
This Möbius transformation sends the removed line segment to the nonpositive real axis, together with infinity. More precisely, its image on the stated planar domain is
T(A)=(C∖(−∞,0])∖{1},
(93)
since T(z)=1 has no finite solution when a=b. Conversely, its inverse z=(bw−a)/(w−1) is defined for every w in this set and cannot lie on [a,b], proving this image description exactly.
Take the principal square root of T(z), characterized by positive real part, and then multiply by i. The result is the conformal map
F(z)=iz−bz−a​​: A⟶H∖{i}.​
(94)
Each stage is injective and onto the indicated image; the square root maps the slit plane to the right half-plane, with the point 1 omitted here. Hence this is a conformal bijection onto the punctured upper half-plane. Its inverse is
z=1+F2a+bF2​.
(95)
If the source is instead (C∪{∞})∖[a,b] on the Riemann sphere, the same map extends with F(∞)=i and becomes a conformal bijection onto all of H. Indeed, in the local coordinate u=1/z it has expansion F=i+2i​(b−a)u+O(u2), whose derivative at u=0 is nonzero. This gives the sphere version of the requested construction without silently changing the printed planar domain.
