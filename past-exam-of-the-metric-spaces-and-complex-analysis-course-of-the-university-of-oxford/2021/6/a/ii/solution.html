Use the univalent functions on D, namely the univalent functions F:D→C. Each has nonzero derivative, so its image F(D) is a domain conformally equivalent to D, and is therefore simply connected. This image is proper: otherwise F−1:C→D would be a bounded nonconstant entire function, contrary to the Liouville theorem.
Conversely, every proper simply connected domain G⊂C is F(D) for some such F, by taking the inverse of a Riemann mapping theorem bijection. Thus these functions parameterize all proper simply connected domains by their images. The complex plane itself is the exceptional simply connected domain and must be listed separately.
The parameterization has redundancy: two univalent functions F1​,F2​ have the same image if and only if F2​=F1​∘ϕ for an automorphism of the unit disk ϕ. All such automorphisms have the form
ϕ(z)=eiθ1−αzz−α​,∣α∣<1,θ∈R.
(81)
If an interior point w0​∈G is prescribed, the normalization F(0)=w0​ and F′(0)>0 real removes this redundancy. Equivalently one can describe these maps through their convergent Taylor series on D, imposing univalence on the resulting holomorphic functions.
