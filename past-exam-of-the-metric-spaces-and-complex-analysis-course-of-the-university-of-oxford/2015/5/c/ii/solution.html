Put D(z)=sinh2z+cosh2a and S=sinh(2a)>0. Traverse the rectangle −R→R→R+πi→−R+πi→−R with positive orientation of a contour, taking R>a. Part (i) shows that the only enclosed poles of f(z)=z/D(z) are
z+​=a+2πi​,z−​=−a+2πi​.
(43)
They are simple poles because
D′(z)=2sinhzcoshz=sinh(2z),D′(z+​)=−S,D′(z−​)=S.
(44)
The residue formula for a quotient at a simple denominator zero gives
Res(f,z+​)=−Sa+πi/2​,Res(f,z−​)=S−a+πi/2​.
(45)
Their imaginary terms cancel and their residues sum to −2a/S. Thus Cauchy's residue theorem gives
∫ΓR​​f(z)dz=−S4πia​.
(46)
The choice of numerator z makes the horizontal sides useful. The hyperbolic sine satisfies sinh(z+πi)=−sinhz, so D(x+πi)=D(x). Consequently the lower and upper contour integrals, with their actual orientations, add to
∫−RR​D(x)x​dx+∫R−R​D(x)x+πi​dx​=−πi∫−RR​D(x)dx​.​
(47)
It remains to justify discarding both vertical sides. On either side z=±R+iy, 0≤y≤π, the reverse triangle inequality and part (b)(ii) imply
∣D(z)∣≥∣sinhz∣2−cosh2a≥sinh2R−cosh2a.
(48)
This lower bound is positive for sufficiently large R. Also ∣z∣≤R+π and each vertical side has length π. The estimation lemma bounds the absolute value of the sum VR​ of the vertical contributions by
∣VR​∣≤sinh2R−cosh2a2π(R+π)​⟶0.
(49)
The real improper integral converges absolutely, since its integrand is continuous on the real axis and decays like 4e−2∣x∣ at infinity. Combining all four sides and taking R→∞ gives
−πi∫−∞∞​sinh2x+cosh2adx​=−sinh(2a)4πia​.
(50)
Therefore
∫−∞∞​sinh2x+cosh2adx​=sinh(2a)4a​,a>0.​
(51)
An independent real-variable check uses the hyperbolic tangent substitution u=tanhx: the integral becomes ∫−11​du/(cosh2a−u2sinh2a). Its antiderivative uses the inverse hyperbolic tangent, giving 2arctanh(tanha)/(sinhacosha)=4a/sinh(2a). As a↓0, this tends to 2, agreeing with ∫−∞∞​sech2xdx=2 and checking the factor of two.
