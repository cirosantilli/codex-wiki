If P had no root, the function h=1/P would be an entire function. The bound just proved makes h(z)→0 as ∣z∣→∞. On the remaining closed disk, continuity and the extreme value theorem make h bounded. Thus h is a bounded entire function, so Liouville's theorem makes it constant. Its limit at infinity forces that constant to be zero, impossible for a reciprocal.
Consequently P has a root in C. This proves the existence part of the fundamental theorem of algebra. The invoked Liouville theorem follows from the Cauchy integral formula: the Cauchy estimate ∣h′(w)∣≤M/r on arbitrarily large circles gives h′=0 when h is bounded by M.
