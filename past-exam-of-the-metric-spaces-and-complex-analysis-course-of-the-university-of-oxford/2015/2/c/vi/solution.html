Write C′=C∖{(1,1)} and V={1}×[0,1). Let L and R be the portions of C′ with first coordinate respectively less than and greater than one. Each is path-connected: move along a rational tooth to its corresponding open half of the top line, then horizontally there. By the density of the rational numbers, every point of V is a limit point of both L and R.
Use the elementary fact that adjoining any collection of limit points to a connected set preserves connectedness. Indeed, in a proposed separation, the connected subset lies on one side; relative openness would prevent any point on the other side from being a limit point of it. Therefore L∪V and R∪V are connected. They intersect in V, so their union C′ is connected.
There is, however, no path from (1,0) to (0,1). If γ(t)=(x(t),y(t)) were one, let t0​ be the first time at which y(t0​)=1. Such a first time exists by continuity and compactness of [0,1], and t0​>0. For 0≤t<t0​, membership in the comb forces x(t)∈Q. The continuous image of a connected space is connected, and a connected subset of Q is a singleton: an irrational number between any two distinct rational values would separate it. Hence x(t)=1 for t<t0​. By continuity, γ(t0​)=(1,1), which was deleted.
C′ is connected but not path-connected.​
