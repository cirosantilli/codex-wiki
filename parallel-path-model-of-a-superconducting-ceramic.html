Parallel-path model of a superconducting ceramic (Reff​=Rn​r/(Rn​+r))

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superconductivity High-temperature superconductivity Transport and magnetic tests of superconducting ceramics
OurBigBook.com Words: 388
Consider an original electrical circuit model between two equipotential cross-sections. A normal path has electrical resistance Rn​>0. Let b∈{0,1} indicate whether an additional path connects those same cross-sections; when connected it has electrical resistance r≥0. Ideal voltage contacts measure those cross-sections. This collapses a spatial network to two parallel branches; it is not a fit to the Bednorz-Müller experiment.
For r>0, both branches have the same electric potential difference V. Ohm's law and the Kirchhoff node law give I=V/Rn​+bV/r, hence
Reff​=⎩⎨⎧​Rn​,Rn​+rRn​r​,0,​b=0,b=1, r>0,b=1, r=0.​
(43)
The last case follows directly from the ideal short's constraint V=0, provided it can carry the imposed electric current below its superconducting critical current. It does not follow by substituting r=0 into V/r. A normal bridge in series with otherwise superconducting pieces belongs in r; superconducting grains alone need not short the measured interval.
Choose Rn​=1Ω. A connected bridge with r=0.01Ω gives Reff​=1/101Ω≃9.901mΩ, almost a hundredfold drop despite finite electrical resistance. An ideal connected superconducting filament instead gives zero in this model even when its volume is a small part of the specimen. Isolated superconducting islands can have the same superconducting phase fraction f while leaving b=0. For the illustrative disconnected geometry we stipulate that the normal conducting network retains Rn​.
To plot a chosen connectivity sequence, set b(f)=0 for f<fc​ and b(f)=1 for f≥fc​, with r=0 on the connected branch. The two displayed values of fc​ describe different chosen architectures. A real percolation calculation would need an explicit network and spatial distribution; the scalar phase fraction f does not determine b by itself.
An instrument with unresolved electric potential difference bounded by δV reports only Reff​≲δV/∣I∣. For chosen I=1mA and δV=100nV this floor is 10−4Ω, assuming offsets and noise are included in the bound. The figure clips smaller model values to that floor. Increasing ∣I∣ improves this formal bound only while Joule heating and the path's superconducting critical current remain controlled. None of these transport statements determines the superconducting phase fraction or establishes complete Meissner effect shielding.
