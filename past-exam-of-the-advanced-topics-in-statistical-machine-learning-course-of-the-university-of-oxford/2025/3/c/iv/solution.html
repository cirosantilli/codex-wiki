The source's assertion that the target input always gives the greatest reduction needs an additional hypothesis. The reduction formula above does not require that assertion. For a counterexample, use the linear kernel k(x,x′)=xx′ with σ2=1 and target x∗​=1. With no informative previous observations, v=1. This can also be arranged with n−1=1 by observing first at x1​=0, which has zero covariance with all latent values. Observing at the target gives reduction 1/2, whereas observing at xn​=2 gives

One sufficient extra condition is that the existing latent posterior variance be maximal at x∗​ among all candidate inputs. To prove it, let w=vn−1​(x) and r=Cov(f(x),f∗​∣y1:n−1​,θ). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives r2≤vw. If w≤v, then
