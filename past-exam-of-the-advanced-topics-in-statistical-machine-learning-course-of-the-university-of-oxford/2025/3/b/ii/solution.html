Condition throughout on the training inputs and x∗​. Interpret θ here as the fixed covariance function and noise hyperparameters. Assume observation errors ϵi​ are independent standard normal random variables, independent of the Gaussian process f, and write
Kij​=k(xi​,xj​),k∗​=(k(x1​,x∗​),…,k(xn​,x∗​))T,k∗∗​=k(x∗​,x∗​),C=K+σ2In​.

The independent-error assumption is the standard Gaussian process regression interpretation of the observation model; the single-observation marginal noise law alone would not specify cross-observation noise correlations. We take σ2>0 so that C is a positive-definite matrix. More generally the inverse formulas below apply whenever the indicated noisy covariance matrices are invertible.
The Gaussian process prior makes (f∗​,f) a Gaussian random vector. Adding the independent Gaussian noise vector to f preserves a multivariate normal distribution. Its mean is zero, its observation covariance matrix is C, and Cov(f∗​,yi​)=k(x∗​,xi​) because the noise is independent of f∗​. Therefore
(f∗​y​)​θ∼N(0,(k∗∗​k∗​​k∗T​C​))​.

A degenerate latent variance is allowed; the displayed multivariate normal distribution then denotes a Gaussian law even if it has no full-dimensional probability density.
