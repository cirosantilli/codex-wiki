Write h(θ)=logp(D∣θ)+logp(θ). Choose an interior maximum a posteriori estimate, a globally maximizing posterior mode
μ∈argmaxθ​h(θ),∇h(μ)=0.
(2)
Suppose h is twice continuously differentiable near μ and its negative Hessian matrix A=−∇2h(μ) is a positive-definite matrix. The second-order Taylor expansion is
h(θ)≃h(μ)−21​(θ−μ)TA(θ−μ).
(3)
Exponentiating and normalizing this local approximation gives the Laplace approximation
p(θ∣D)≃N(θ;μ,Σ),Σ=A−1=−[∇2h(μ)]−1​.
(4)
Thus the approximate mean is the posterior mode, and the approximate covariance matrix is the inverse local curvature. This normal approximation is useful when most posterior probability is concentrated around this mode. A boundary mode, singular curvature, or several substantial modes requires a modified approximation.
