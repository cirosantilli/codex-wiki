In this setting a positive-semidefinite kernel is an inner-product similarity: there exist a real Hilbert space H and a feature map φ:X→H such that
k(x,x′)=⟨φ(x),φ(x′)⟩H​​.
(26)
The feature map may be infinite-dimensional. Equivalently, k is symmetric and every finite kernel matrix is a positive semidefinite matrix. The equivalence follows by forming the canonical feature space of a positive-semidefinite kernel and taking its Hilbert space completion.

The closure property of positive-semidefinite kernels gives three constructions, each seen directly from the feature maps φ1​,φ2​.
  • Nonnegative sums: k=αk1​+βk2​, for α,β≥0. Its feature map is (α​φ1​(x),β​φ2​(x)) in the Hilbert space direct sum; expanding its inner product gives k.
  • Pointwise products: k(x,x′)=k1​(x,x′)k2​(x,x′). In the Hilbert tensor product, use φ(x)=φ1​(x)⊗φ2​(x): the inner product of two such elementary tensors is the product of their two inner products.
  • Input-dependent weighting: k(x,x′)=a(x)k1​(x,x′)a(x′) for any real-valued function a. Its feature map is a(x)φ1​(x), so signs of a are allowed. Nonnegative scalar multiplication is the special case of constant a; the weighting factor a(x)a(x′) is itself a rank-one positive-semidefinite kernel.
Another useful consequence is pullback: if h:X→Z and k0​ is a positive-semidefinite kernel on Z, then k0​(h(x),h(x′)) has feature map φ0​∘h.

A transformer block has two principal sublayers: multi-head attention and a positionwise feedforward neural network. Each sublayer has a residual connection and layer normalization. To make the components explicit, let X∈RL×dmodel​ contain the representations of L sequence positions. For head h, learned projections form
Qh​=XWhQ​,Kh​=XWhK​,Vh​=XWhV​.
(46)
The scaled dot-product attention output is
Ah​=softmax(dk​​Qh​KhT​​+B)Vh​,
(47)
where the softmax function acts along each row and B is an optional attention mask. For causal self-attention, disallowed future positions receive −∞ before the softmax function, giving zero attention weights. In self-attention, queries, keys, and values all come from the same input sequence. The multi-head attention output concatenates the heads and projects them:
MHA(X)=Concat(A1​,…,AH​)WO.
(48)
A standard post-normalization transformer block can then be written
U=LN(X+MHA(X)),Y=LN(U+FFN(U))​.
(49)
Here layer normalization normalizes the feature coordinates of each position separately, within each example, and applies learned scales and shifts. The positionwise feedforward neural network applies the same two affine maps and activation function at every position, for example
FFN(z)=ρ(zW1​+b1​)W2​+b2​.
(50)
Thus attention mixes information between positions, while the feedforward sublayer transforms each position's features. The residual connections support propagation through a deep stack, and dropout is often used during training. Pre-normalization variants move layer normalization before the sublayer. A model's positional encoding supplies order information to the sequence representation or attention mechanism; a decoder in an encoder-decoder model also has a cross-attention sublayer between its self-attention and feedforward neural network sublayers.

ii OurBigBook.com
A recurrent neural network processes positions through a recurrence such as ht​=F(ht−1​,xt​). Computing ht​ requires ht−1​, so the standard computation is sequential across sequence positions, although different examples in a batch can be processed in parallel. Information from position s to t must pass through t−s recurrent steps, and backpropagated gradients involve products of that many step Jacobian matrices. This creates vanishing gradients or exploding gradients and can make long-range dependencies difficult to learn. Gating can mitigate these difficulties while retaining the recurrent dependence.
In a transformer block, all queries, keys, and values for an available input sequence can be formed in parallel. The self-attention weights are computed by matrix multiplication, so positions within one layer can be processed together; layers themselves remain sequential. With unrestricted self-attention, one position can directly receive information from any other position in one layer, giving a constant-length route across distant positions rather than one proportional to their separation. With causal self-attention, there is the same direct route to any permitted earlier position.
A transformer model supports parallel training across known sequence positions and short routes for long-range dependencies. Even a causal model can compute all training positions together when the entire training sequence is supplied and the attention mask excludes future positions. However, ordinary autoregressive generation still generates one new token at a time. Full self-attention also constructs L2 pairwise scores, with quadratic time and storage in sequence length for the direct implementation; a standard recurrent neural network has linear sequential work in sequence length at fixed hidden width. Neither architecture guarantees that a useful long-range dependency will actually be learned.

Gaussian process regression supplies a predictive distribution and uncertainty estimates. A Gaussian process regression posterior gives both a posterior mean and a posterior variance, allowing credible intervals for the latent function and a posterior predictive distribution for future noisy observations. The kernel ridge regression estimator by itself specifies a point estimate.
With the same positive-semidefinite kernel and matching regularization, the Gaussian process regression posterior mean equals the kernel ridge regression prediction. Thus the main additional benefit is the explicit probability distribution of predictions, conditional on the chosen Gaussian process model and its hyperparameters, rather than an automatic improvement in point-prediction accuracy.

Additional observations weakly decrease the posterior variance at every fixed prediction input. Apply part (ii) repeatedly to add observations one at a time. An observation whose remaining conditional covariance with f∗​ is nonzero strictly decreases that posterior variance; one with zero remaining conditional covariance leaves it unchanged. Large observation noise reduces the information gained through the denominator vn−1​(xn​)+σ2.
For fixed Gaussian process hyperparameters and a Gaussian likelihood, the posterior variance depends on the observation locations and noise level, but not on the observed numerical outputs. Hence the reduction holds for each possible new output, rather than only on average. The predictive variance of a future noisy observation also decreases by the same amount because its extra independent noise variance is fixed. Refitting the hyperparameters after adding data can change the covariance function; the monotonicity statement compares conditioning under one fixed model.
