Limits of adiabatic demagnetization

 ... Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Thermodynamics Temperature Adiabatic demagnetization
OurBigBook.com Words: 343
Three distinct effects spoil indefinite T∝B cooling: extra entropy outside the spin subsystem, additional magnetic energy scales, and irreversible heat or work input. They can be distinguished with a thermodynamic derivative. For a smooth equilibrium Helmholtz free energy obeying dF=−SdT−mdB, a Maxwell relation gives
dS=TCB​​dT+(∂T∂m​)B​dB,(dBdT​)S​=−CB​T​(∂T∂m​)B​.
(13)
Here CB​ is the total heat capacity participating in the stage's thermal equilibrium. The ideal model has (∂m/∂T)B​=−CB,mag​/B. If its spins share temperature with a field-independent lattice and load of combined heat capacity Cother​>0, then
(dBdT​)Stotal​​=BT​CB,mag​+Cother​CB,mag​​.
(14)
The positive slope still means cooling when dB<0, but its smaller magnitude gives less cooling per fractional field reduction. For example, a low-temperature Debye model gives a lattice entropy proportional to T3. As that lattice entropy falls, the spins take up the released entropy; x can no longer remain constant. A weak enough coupling to neglect its equilibrium interaction energy can still be strong enough to exchange heat on the sweep timescale.
Interactions between magnetic moments, including exchange interaction and the fields of neighboring magnetic dipole moments, introduce energy scales that do not vanish with the applied B. Once they compete with kB​T or μB, the independent-site partition function fails. Correlations and possible magnetic ordering alter the available magnetic entropy and the cooling curve. Simply setting the external magnetic field to zero does not eliminate those interactions. Different refrigerants therefore need their own measured equilibrium properties; there is no universal final temperature from the applied-field ratio alone.
Finally, a real open heat switch has residual thermal conductance; supports, wires and radiation carry incoming heat. Field changes may also dissipate energy. Locally near thermal equilibrium, with incoming heat rate Q˙​leak​ and internal entropy production rate S˙prod​, the stage balance becomes
dtdS​=TQ˙​leak​​+S˙prod​,CB​T˙=−T(∂T∂m​)B​B˙+Q˙​leak​+TS˙prod​.
(15)
This uses a common stage temperature; a non-equilibrated load requires separate balances. The sweep must allow spin-lattice relaxation while remaining fast enough to limit accumulated parasitic heat. At fixed B, the same balance explains warming and finite hold time. Useful refrigeration is the controlled absorption of a load's heat during that finite interval, followed by a renewed isothermal process of magnetization to export its entropy.
