Siegert relation (g12(2)​(τ)=1+∣g12(1)​(τ)∣2)

OurBigBook.com Words: 254
For one selected polarization of waves, write the detected complex fields as E1​(t),E2​(t+τ) and their light intensities as Ij​=∣Ej​∣2, absorbing a common conversion constant. With stationary ensemble averages and nonzero Iˉj​=⟨Ij​⟩, define
g12(1)​(B,τ)=Iˉ1​Iˉ2​​⟨E1∗​(t)E2​(t+τ)⟩​,g12(2)​(B,τ)=Iˉ1​Iˉ2​⟨I1​(t)I2​(t+τ)⟩​.
(12)
Here second-order optical coherence concerns distinct detector channels, with the corresponding normal ordering in the quantum formulation. Independent detection has g(2)=1. Subtracting the means gives the normalized covariance
Iˉ1​Iˉ2​⟨δI1​(t)δI2​(t+τ)⟩​=g12(2)​(B,τ)−1.
(13)
Thus multiplying raw photocurrents contains a large mean-product contribution; multiplying their fluctuations isolates the small excess. Independent detector shot noise does not supply a mean cross-channel excess, although it increases measurement uncertainty.
The displayed Siegert relation requires a zero-mean, jointly circular complex Gaussian random field, the usual chaotic thermal light model. Circular means invariance under a common arbitrary complex phase rotation; in particular ⟨Ei​Ej​⟩=0. Apply the Gaussian moment pairing theorem to the real and imaginary parts:
⟨E1∗​E1​E2∗​E2​⟩=⟨∣E1​∣2⟩⟨∣E2​∣2⟩+∣⟨E1∗​E2​⟩∣2.
(14)
The remaining pairing vanishes by circularity. Division proves the Siegert relation. Gaussianity is an assumption about fourth moments, not a consequence of merely measuring first-order optical coherence; a coherent state or photon antibunching cannot be interpreted by this thermal formula.
For equal-intensity beams combined in a common optical mode with matched wave polarizations, interferometric visibility is V=∣g12(1)​∣. Consequently ideal thermal stellar intensity interferometry measures g(2)−1=V2, while the full g(2) includes the independent-count reference one. Unpolarized thermal light with two independent equally populated wave polarizations has half this excess: adding their covariances doubles the numerator while multiplying total means quadruples the denominator. Finite detector response further suppresses the peak and must be calibrated.[ref]
