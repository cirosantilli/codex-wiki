Planarity test across a cubic-to-tetragonal twin (p=(p1​,p2​,0),Π=p⊥ remains plane⟺p∥N)

Let B=diag(b,a,a), with a,b>0, a=b, and A=Rdiag(a,b,a) be a twin-related gradient with cosθ=2ab/(a2+b2), sinθ=κ(b2−a2)/(a2+b2), κ=±1. Their jump is (b2−a2)(−b,κa,0)⊗(1,κ,0)/(a2+b2).
A plane through the origin with normal p=(p1​,p2​,0) is spanned by e3​ and t=(−p2​,p1​,0). Both gradients map e3​ to the same direction, so the two image half-planes are coplanar exactly when At and Bt are parallel in their transverse section. Direct calculation gives
det2​(Bt,At)=a2+b2κab(b2−a2)​(p1​−κp2​)2.
(1)
Its nonzero prefactor makes planarity equivalent to p∥(1,κ,0), the twin normal. On that plane the two affine maps agree, proving sufficiency as well. The restriction on the third normal component matters; this statement is not a classification of every possible material plane.
