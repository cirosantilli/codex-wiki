Condensation, matter-wave coherence and superfluidity

Bose-Einstein condensation concerns occupation of a one-particle state. Matter-wave coherence concerns field correlations between positions or times. Superfluidity concerns a flow response, including suppression of dissipation under appropriate conditions. A density peak diagnoses neither a relative quantum phase between two separately prepared clouds nor a critical flow speed. These observables require different experiments.
For example, in a single-mode fixed-number Fock state, ⟨a^⟩=0 but ⟨a^†a^⟩=N. Writing ψ^​(r)=ϕ(r)a^ for that occupied mode gives
⟨ψ^​†(r)ψ^​(r′)⟩=Nϕ∗(r)ϕ(r′).
(39)
Thus vanishing mean field does not imply vanishing first-order matter-wave coherence. For two independent fixed-number modes, ⟨a^†b^⟩=0: there is no ensemble mean interference term establishing a fixed relative quantum phase. Interpreting fringes in an individual detection record requires considering correlations and the measurement; assigning a pre-existing definite relative quantum phase is unnecessary.
Wolfgang Ketterle's lecture describes a separate coherence test: two condensates expanded into one another and produced fringes. Selectively removing one condensate made the fringes disappear on alternating preparation cycles, testing against diffraction from the confinement. This compared two-cloud and one-cloud signals; it did not measure dissipationless flow.[ref]
