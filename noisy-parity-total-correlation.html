Noisy parity total correlation (TC(X,Y,Z)=1−h2​(ε))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Information theory Total correlation Parity constraint and total correlation
OurBigBook.com Words: 284
Keep X,Y as independent fair bits, introduce an independent Bernoulli random variable N with P(N=1)=ε, and set Z=X⊕Y⊕N. The extra bit flips the integer parity rule with probability ε. For each even-integer parity triple the probability is (1−ε)/4; for each odd-integer parity triple it is ε/4.
Every coordinate remains fair, and every pair remains independent for every 0≤ε≤1. For example, after fixing X, the fair independent Y makes Y⊕N fair regardless of ε. A pair's joint probability distribution therefore does not change as noise destroys the three-way relation.
Knowing X,Y, uncertainty in Z is exactly the uncertainty in N. The chain rule for information entropy gives
H(X,Y,Z)=H(X,Y)+H(Z∣X,Y)=2+h2​(ε),
(73)
where h2​ is binary entropy. Hence
TC(X,Y,Z)=1−h2​(ε).​
(74)
At ε=1/4, the result is approximately 0.188722 bits, although all three pairwise mutual informations are zero. At ε=1/2, all eight triples have probability 1/8, giving joint independence and zero total correlation. At ε=1, the complementary integer parity rule is deterministic again, so total correlation returns to one bit.
The example distinguishes "there is a dependence" from "a pair reveals it." Once X,Y are known together, Z is predictable at zero noise; knowing either one alone leaves a fair bit. Total correlation detects the joint departure from independence, but does not by itself specify which variables cause which others.
