Compact factorial triangular operator on l1 ((Tα)n​=∑m>n​(n!/m!)αm​)

Words: 94
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
The operator in the title is bounded and compact on the absolutely summable sequence space. The norm of a matrix operator on ℓ1 is the supremum of its absolute column sums: the triangle inequality gives one bound and coordinate unit vectors give the other. Here the column sum is cm​=∑n=1m−1​n!/m!≤2/m, using ∑n=1k​n!≤2k!. The latter inequality follows by induction. Retaining the first N columns gives a finite-rank operator and a remainder of norm at most 2/(N+1). A norm limit of finite-rank operators is a compact operator, proving the assertion. This column-decay method can prove compactness even when each row has infinitely many nonzero entries.

Quotient-distance criterion for compactness (T compact⟺infdimY<∞​∥πY​T∥=0)

Words: 106
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
For a bounded linear operator into a Banach space, compactness is equivalent to the displayed condition, where πY​ is the quotient map by a finite-dimensional subspace of the target. A finite net for the image of the unit ball gives such a subspace by taking its span. Conversely, if the image lies within ε of a finite-dimensional subspace, choose approximants there; they remain bounded because the original image is bounded. A finite net for these approximants gives a slightly larger finite net for the image. This proves total boundedness and hence compactness. This condition does not require choosing the approximants by a linear map, and therefore does not assume an approximation property for the Banach space.

Compact closed-ball image forces finite rank on c0 (S(Bc0​​) compact⟹rankS<∞)

Words: 106
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
Suppose a linear map S:c0​→Y has compact image of the closed unit ball. It is bounded, and each g∈Y∗ attains its maximum on this compact image. Thus S∗g attains its norm, so norm attainment on the vanishing sequence space implies S∗(Y∗)⊆c00​. The Baire category theorem applied to the inverse images of the first n coordinate spans makes this adjoint finite rank: these are closed linear subspaces covering the complete continuous dual space Y∗, so one has interior and is all of Y∗. Finally the Hahn-Banach theorem identifies kerS as the common kernel of S∗(Y∗), proving finite rank of S. Ordinary compact operators require only relative compactness of the ball image, so they need not have finite rank.

Block averaging compactness counterexample ((Tx)k​=mk−1/q​∑j∈Bk​​xj​)

Words: 114
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
For 1<p<∞, let q=p/(p−1) and partition the coordinates into consecutive finite blocks Bk​ of lengths mk​→∞. The displayed map defines a norm-one operator on the l-p sequence space. By Hölder's inequality, ∣(Tx)k​∣p≤∑j∈Bk​​∣xj​∣p, giving the upper norm bound. For the unit vector ej​ with j∈Bk​, Tej​=mk−1/q​ek​, so its norm tends to zero as j→∞.
Nevertheless, x(k)=mk−1/p​1Bk​​ has norm one and maps to ek​. Those images are pairwise distance 21/p apart. Thus T is not a compact operator, and has norm exactly one. Vanishing images of a chosen basis do not alone test compactness on ℓp for p>1. The map is the adjoint of the isometric block embedding (Uy)j​=mk−1/q​yk​, j∈Bk​, on ℓq.

Nonlinear compact operator (T:X→Y)

Words: 48
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
A nonlinear compact operator between Banach spaces is a continuous map that sends every bounded set to a relatively compact subset. Thus every bounded sequence of inputs has a subsequence whose images converge in the target norm. No linearity is assumed. On a bounded domain of definition it suffices that the entire image be relatively compact. For linear operators, this recovers the usual compact operator notion.

Compact L2 potential perturbation of the Dirichlet Laplacian (K=Mw​ΔD−1​,w∈Lp, p>n≥3)

Words: 74
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
On a bounded open set, let Tv∈W01,2​ be the weak solution of ΔTv=v. The energy estimate bounds T:L2→W01,2​. For p>n, put q=2p/(p−2)<2n/(n−2). The zero-boundary Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem gives compact embedding into Lq, and Hölder's inequality gives ∥wz∥2​≤∥w∥p​∥z∥q​. Hence Kv=wTv is compact on L2, without a regularity assumption on the boundary. Writing v=Δu makes Δu−wu=f equivalent to (I−K)v=f. The Fredholm alternative for a compact operator then identifies unique solvability with triviality of the homogeneous kernel.

Nondegenerate representations by compact operators

Words: 86
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
A nondegenerate star-algebra of compact operators decomposes its Hilbert space into irreducible closed reducing subspaces, each equivalence class occurring finitely many times. Pass to its norm closure. A nonzero compact positive element supplies a finite-rank spectral projection; a minimal projection in its finite-dimensional corner has scalar corner algebra. A cyclic vector in its range generates an irreducible reducing subspace. A maximal orthogonal family exhausts the space by nondegeneracy. Infinitely many equivalent copies would give orthogonal unit vectors whose images under some compact algebra element have equal nonzero norms, contradicting compactness.

Compact operators send weak convergence to norm convergence (fn​⇀f⟹Kfn​→Kf)

Words: 57
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
For a bounded operator K between Hilbert spaces, compactness is equivalent to fn​⇀f implying Kfn​→Kf in norm. The Uniform boundedness principle bounds a weakly convergent sequence. Relative compactness of its images and the unique possible weak limit then give norm convergence. Conversely, every bounded domain sequence has a weakly convergent subsequence, so the image of the unit ball is relatively compact.

Norm-closedness of compact operators

Words: 32
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
An operator norm limit of compact operators between Banach spaces is compact. Approximate the image of the unit ball by the image under one compact operator and transfer a finite epsilon-net across the small operator-norm error.

If Txj​=λj​xj​ for an infinite orthonormal sequence (xj​) and a compact operator T on a Hilbert space, then λj​→0. Otherwise a subsequence of the vectors Txj​ stays pairwise separated, contradicting compactness.

Finite-rank approximation theorem for compact operators on a Hilbert space

Words: 45
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
Every compact operator on a Hilbert space is an operator-norm limit of finite-rank operators. Cover the compact closure of the image of the unit ball by finitely many small balls, span their centres by a finite-dimensional space E, and approximate T by PE​T.

Fredholm alternative

Words: 129 Articles: 1
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
If T is compact and λ=0, then T−λI is injective exactly when it is surjective. Equivalently, every nonzero point of the spectrum of T is an eigenvalue. Compactness turns an approximate eigenvector sequence into a convergent eigenvector sequence.

Neumann compatibility from a shifted compact resolvent ((I−K)u=Kf,K=(L+I)−1)

Words: 89
Fredholm alternative OurBigBook.com
For a symmetric uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator with zero conormal flux and zero reaction, Lax-Milgram theorem constructs the shifted inverse K:L2→H1. The compact Rellich-Kondrachov theorem embedding makes it compact on L2, and symmetry makes it self-adjoint. If the homogeneous kernel consists of constants, then ker(I−K) is the constant line and K1=1. The Fredholm alternative makes solvability equivalent to Kf being orthogonal to constants, or ⟨Kf,1⟩=⟨f,K1⟩=∫f=0. The weak solution is unique up to an additive constant. For a disconnected domain the identical argument gives one compatibility condition per connected component.

Hilbert-Schmidt operator (T∈S2​)

Words: 936 Articles: 21
Compact operator OurBigBook.com
A bounded linear operator on a Hilbert space is Hilbert-Schmidt when ∑n​∥Ten​∥2<∞ for one, equivalently every, orthonormal basis. Every Hilbert-Schmidt operator is a compact operator.

Hilbert-Schmidt operators form a Hilbert space (S2​(X))

Words: 84
Hilbert-Schmidt operator OurBigBook.com
On a Hilbert space X with a countable Hilbertian basis (ek​), the Hilbert-Schmidt operators form a Hilbert space under ⟨S,T⟩=∑k​⟨Sek​,Tek​⟩, using the same linearity convention as the inner product on X. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality proves convergence of this sum. The finite-sum triangle inequality and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give ∥T∥≤∥T∥2​. Thus a Cauchy sequence (Tn​) in Hilbert-Schmidt norm converges in operator norm to a bounded linear operator T. For n,m≥N with ∥Tn​−Tm​∥2​≤ε, taking m→∞ in each finite column sum gives ∑k=1K​∥(Tn​−T)ek​∥2≤ε2 for every K. Taking the supremum over K proves Tn​−T∈S2​(X) and ∥Tn​−T∥2​≤ε, so T∈S2​(X) and completeness follows.

Finite column sum does not imply boundedness

Words: 80
Hilbert-Schmidt operator OurBigBook.com
An everywhere-defined linear operator T on an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space need not be bounded merely because ∑k​∥Tek​∥2<∞ for a Hilbertian basis (ek​). Let v=∑k≥1​2−kek​, extend {ek​:k≥1}∪{v} to a Hamel basis, and let the linear functional ϕ equal one on v and zero on every other basis vector. Then Tx=ϕ(x)e1​ has Tek​=0 for every k but Tv=e1​. For vN​=∑k=1N​2−kek​, v−vN​→0 while T(v−vN​)=e1​, proving discontinuity. Thus the definition of a Hilbert-Schmidt operator must require boundedness, or first define the operator on finite linear combinations and take its unique continuous extension.

Hilbert-Schmidt norm (∥T∥HS​=(∑n​∥Ten​∥2)1/2)

The Hilbert-Schmidt norm of a Hilbert-Schmidt operator is the square root of the displayed sum. The value is independent of the orthonormal basis, by the Parseval identity. For a finite matrix, it is the Frobenius norm.

Frobenius norm (∥A∥F​=(∑i,j​∣aij​∣2)1/2)

Words: 47
Hilbert-Schmidt norm OurBigBook.com
The Frobenius norm is the finite-matrix Hilbert-Schmidt norm. It equals tr(A∗A)​, is unchanged by unitary multiplication on either side, and is the square root of the sum of squared singular values. For unit vectors u,v, ∥uu∗−vv∗∥F2​=2(1−∣u∗v∣2), a useful sign-invariant measure of distance between rank-one orthogonal projection matrices.

Hilbert-Schmidt inner product (⟨A,B⟩HS​)

Words: 102 Articles: 2
Hilbert-Schmidt operator OurBigBook.com
For Hilbert-Schmidt operators A and B, define the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product by ⟨A,B⟩HS​=∑k​⟨Aek​,Bek​⟩ for a Hilbertian basis (ek​). With an inner product linear in its first argument this equals tr(B∗A); with an inner product conjugate-linear in its first argument it equals tr(A∗B). Its induced norm is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm, independently of that convention.

Trace-class duality (S1​(H)∗≅B(H))

Words: 69
Trace-class operator OurBigBook.com
The dual of the trace-class operators, and also of the normed space of finite-rank operators with the trace norm, is isometrically the space of bounded operators. The pairing is FB​(T)=Tr(BT). For Rx,y​z=⟨z,y⟩x, one has ∥Rx,y​∥1​=∥x∥∥y∥ and FB​(Rx,y​)=⟨Bx,y⟩, using an inner product linear in its first argument. A bounded functional on these rank-one operators gives a bounded sesquilinear form and hence a unique B by the Riesz representation theorem. The singular value decomposition proves ∥FB​∥=∥B∥ and the triangle inequality for the trace norm.

Fredholm determinant (det(I+K)=∏j​(1+λj​))

Trace-class operator OurBigBook.com
For a trace-class operator K on a Hilbert space, the Fredholm determinant is the convergent product over its eigenvalues, counted with algebraic multiplicity. For self-adjoint K, spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators reduces the product to a diagonal calculation. For ∥K∥<1, logdet(I+K)=∑n≥1​(−1)n+1tr(Kn)/n. This identity exposes the successive cumulants in a Gaussian quadratic exponential moment.
Fredholm determinant of an exponential commutator (det(eAeBe−Ae−B)=eTr[A,B])
Words: 65
Fredholm determinant OurBigBook.com
For bounded operators with trace-class commutator, the multiplicative commutator differs from identity by a trace-class operator. Along F(t)=etAeBe−tAe−B, the trace of its logarithmic derivative is the constant Tr[A,B]. This follows by writing eBAe−B−A as the integral of conjugates of [B,A] and using trace invariance under bounded similarities. Integrating the Fredholm determinant derivative from F(0)=I proves the formula. Individual traces of A and B need not exist.

Powers of a rank-one operator (Km=φ(a)m−1K(m≥1))
Words: 107 Articles: 1
Rank-one operator OurBigBook.com

Compact elliptic spectral theorem

On a closed manifold with a Riemannian metric the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator is self-adjoint with compact resolvent. Eigenvalue counting function (N(Λ)=#{j:λj​≤Λ})

Words: 114
Compact elliptic spectral theorem OurBigBook.com
For a nonnegative self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent, this function counts eigenvalues up to a given level with their multiplicities. For the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator on a closed n-dimensional Riemannian manifold, N(Λ)≤C(1+Λ)n/2. To prove this bound, multiply each vector in the low-eigenvalue subspace by a finite set of chart cutoffs and record its local Fourier coefficients of frequency at most R. The total number of retained coefficients is O(Rn), while the sum of discarded squared norms is at most CR−2(1+Λ)∥u∥22​ by Parseval identity and the energy bound. For R a sufficiently large multiple of 1+Λ​, vanishing of all recorded coefficients forces u=0. The recording map is injective and proves the dimension bound. Inverting this estimate gives λj​≥c(j+1)2/n−1.
