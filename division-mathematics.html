Division (mathematics, a/b)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic
OurBigBook.com Words: 718 Articles: 8
For real numbers a and b=0, division a/b asks for the unique number x satisfying bx=a. Equivalently, a/b=ab−1 uses the multiplicative inverse of b. This description separates the role of the denominator from the result: the quotient undoes a specified multiplication.
For example, 3/2 is the number whose double is three, so it is exactly 1.5. A second interpretation asks how many portions of size 2 fit into an amount 3; the answer is one and a half portions. Equal sharing and measuring with a specified portion size lead to the same quotient, although the units assigned to the result can differ.
In the natural numbers, exact division need not be possible. Euclidean division instead keeps a remainder: 17=5⋅3+2, with 0≤2<5. Thus seventeen objects form three complete groups of five with two left over. As a rational number, the same quotient is 17/5=3+2/5=3.4. The remainder and the fractional part describe the same unfinished group in different ways.
Division by zero cannot retain the unique-solution rule. For a=0, the equation 0x=a has no solution; for a=0, every real number x solves 0x=0, so there is no unique quotient. This explains both failures through multiplication, rather than assigning a/0 a value that would contradict ordinary arithmetic. [ref]
