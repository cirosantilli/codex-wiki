Magnetic field of a finite straight wire (B=−4πaμ0​I​[a2+s2​s​]s1​s2​​z)

Take a wire segment on the y axis, with source positions r′=(0,s,0) for s1​≤s≤s2​, and positive electric current along +y. Observe at r=(a,0,0), where a>0 is the perpendicular distance to the wire's supporting line. Choose x rightward and y upward, so +z points out of the page. Then
dℓ′=y​ds,R=ax−sy​,dℓ′×R=−azds.
(106)
Thus the magnetic field points into the page by the right-hand rule. The Biot-Savart law and an elementary antiderivative give
B=−4πμ0​Ia​z∫s1​s2​​(a2+s2)3/2ds​=−4πaμ0​I​[a2+s2​s​]s1​s2​​z.
(107)
For an original worked example, choose s1​=−0.12m, s2​=0.12m, a=0.05m, and I=3A. Use μ0​≈4π×10−7Tm/A for these rounded calculations. Each endpoint contributes 0.12/0.13=12/13, so
B=−0.05m(10−7Tm/A)(3A)​1324​z≈−11.08μTz.
(108)
These are calculated inputs and output, not laboratory data. The two endpoint angles measured from the perpendicular each have sine 12/13; this recovers the equivalent endpoint-angle formula without depending on a diagram's angle-sign convention.
For a segment centered on the perpendicular foot, s1​=−L, s2​=L. Its magnetic field magnitude is
B=2πaμ0​I​a2+L2​L​.
(109)
The limit L/a→∞ gives B∞​=μ0​I/(2πa), consistent with Ampère's circuital law for an infinite straight wire. In the example, B∞​=12μT and B/B∞​=12/13: the infinite-wire value overestimates the segment value by 1/12, or about 8.33% of the segment value. For a≫L, the isolated segment contribution instead behaves as B∼μ0​I(2L)/(4πa2). The complete localized electrical circuit has a different far-field behavior: return-path contributions cancel this leading segment term, leaving a magnetic dipole field when its magnetic dipole moment is nonzero. Segment and whole-circuit limits must be distinguished.
The SI units check directly: μ0​I/a has units (Tm/A)A/m=T, the tesla. The apparent divergence as a→0 is a failure of the thin-wire model near the conductor, not a prediction of infinite magnetic field inside a real wire. This calculation gives only this segment's contribution; include the return wires before comparing with an apparatus.
