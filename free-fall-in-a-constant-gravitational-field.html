Free fall in a constant gravitational field (s(t)=21​gt2,v(t)=gt)

Take downward displacement s as positive, assume a constant gravitational acceleration g>0, and release a point mass from rest at s=0. Integrating s¨=g twice gives
v(t)=gt,s(t)=21​gt2.
(3)
For a downward distance h>0, the arrival time and speed are
tfall​=g2h​​,varrival​=2gh​.
(4)
For the model values h=20m and g=9.81ms−2, these are approximately 2.0193s and 19.8091ms−1. Doubling the height multiplies both quantities by 2​; it does not double them. The same speed follows from the work-energy theorem, mgh=mv2/2.
For intervals of length Δt, the successive downward displacements are
s((j+1)Δt)−s(jΔt)=21​g(Δt)2(2j+1),j=0,1,2,….
(5)
Thus equal spacing in time produces increasingly separated positions. This is the odd-number pattern in Galileo's result. It assumes constant acceleration and release from rest; a nonzero initial velocity adds a linear term to s(t).
The approximation can be estimated for a spherical source of radius R and standard gravitational parameter μ. Writing g0​=μ/R2, the gravitational magnitude at height h is
g0​g(R+h)​=(1+Rh​)−2.
(6)
For h/R≪1, a Taylor expansion gives g(R+h)/g0​=1−2h/R+O((h/R)2). Small height relative to the source's radius makes nearly constant gravity plausible; negligible drag force remains a separate requirement.
