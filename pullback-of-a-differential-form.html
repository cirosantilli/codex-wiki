Pullback of a differential form (ϕ∗)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Differential form
OurBigBook.com Words: 123
A smooth map ϕ:M→N pulls a differential form on N back to one on M by applying its differential dϕ to each tangent vector argument. In particular, a parametrization of a curve pulls a one-form back to an ordinary integrand on an interval.
For α=xdy and the parabola ϕ(t)=(t,t2), 0≤t≤1, substitute x=t and dy=2tdt to obtain
ϕ∗α=2t2dt,∫ϕ​α=∫01​2t2dt=32​​.

The same endpoints gave 0 and 1 along the two square-edge paths in the introduction, so this third answer is consistent with path dependence. By contrast, ϕ∗d(xy)=d(t3)=3t2dt gives integral 1, the same endpoint difference as before.
The pullback of a differential form commutes with the wedge product of differential forms and the exterior derivative: ϕ∗(α∧β)=ϕ∗α∧ϕ∗β and ϕ∗(dα)=d(ϕ∗α). These identities make changes of coordinates and parametrizations compatible with differentiation and integration.
It is the alternating-tensor case of pullback.
