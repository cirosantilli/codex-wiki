The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators used here concerns a compact self-adjoint operator: its nonzero eigenvalues are real, have finite multiplicity and can accumulate only at zero; the orthogonal complement of its kernel is the closed span of its orthonormal eigenvectors. Self-adjointness is essential for this eigenbasis statement.
Let Kf be the unique zero-parameter weak solution. Uniqueness gives linearity. Testing with Kf and using coercivity gives ∥Kf∥H1​≤ν−1∥f∥2​. The Rellich-Kondrachov theorem makes the embedding H01​(Ω)↪L2(Ω) compact on this bounded C1 domain, so K is compact on L2. Symmetry gives
⟨Kf,g⟩=B[Kg,Kf]=⟨f,Kg⟩.
(6)
It is self-adjoint and positive. It is injective: if Kf=0, the weak equation pairs f to zero with all H01​ tests, dense in L2, so f=0. Moreover ⟨Kf,f⟩=B[Kf,Kf]>0 for f=0.
The parameter equation is equivalent to u=K(f+λu), that is
u−λKu=Kf.​
(7)
A solution of this operator equation in L2 lies in H01​, because its right-side representation is K(f+λu), and the equivalence follows in both directions. For λ≤0, the form B[u,v]−λ⟨u,v⟩ remains coercive; the preceding minimization argument gives a unique weak solution.
The spectral theorem now supplies a complete orthonormal basis (ϕj​) of L2 with Kϕj​=κj​ϕj​, κj​>0, and κj​→0. The elliptic eigenvalues are λj​=1/κj​>0, with finite multiplicity and tending to infinity, and Lϕj​=λj​ϕj​ weakly. If λ is not one of them, then
u=j∑​λj​−λ⟨f,ϕj​⟩​ϕj​.​
(8)
This converges even in H01​: its squared energy norm is ∑j​λj​∣⟨f,ϕj​⟩∣2/∣λj​−λ∣2, finite because the multiplier is bounded over j. Partial sums solve the weak equation with the corresponding L2 projections of f. Passing to the limit proves the equation for every test; the same coefficients force uniqueness. Thus the expansion represents the unique weak solution away from the elliptic eigenvalues.
