Weak convergence means ℓ(uj​)→ℓ(u) for every bounded linear functional ℓ∈X∗. Norm convergence implies this by ∣ℓ(uj​−u)∣≤∥ℓ∥∥uj​−u∥. The converse fails for coordinate unit vectors in ℓ2: their norms are one, but pairing with any square-summable sequence tends to zero, so they are weakly null.
For the subsequence assertion apply Gram-Schmidt process to a countable dense subset to choose a countable orthonormal basis (em​) of the separable Hilbert space and a bound M for the sequence. Repeated subsequence selection and diagonalization make every coefficient ⟨ujk​​,em​⟩ converge to a scalar am​. Bessel inequality gives ∑m=1N​∣am​∣2≤M2 for every N, so u=∑m​am​em​ exists in H with norm at most M. Pairings with finite linear combinations of the basis converge. Approximating any other test vector by such combinations and using the common norm bound proves ujk​​⇀u. The finite-dimensional case uses the same argument with a finite basis.
Apply this to every subsequence of the bounded L2 sequence. Any weakly convergent further subsequence has limit v with ∫ab​v=0, since interval indicators are L2 tests. The permitted fact gives v=0 almost everywhere. If the whole sequence failed to converge weakly to zero, some fixed test functional and subsequence would stay a positive distance from zero. Its weakly convergent further subsequence would then contradict the preceding conclusion. Thus the entire sequence converges weakly to zero in L2(0,1).
