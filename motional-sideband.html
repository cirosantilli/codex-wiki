Motional sideband (ωdrive​=ω0​, ω0​−ν, ω0​+ν)

A motional sideband is a transition that changes internal excitation and a motional quantum number together. Define the internal energy gap as ℏω0​>0. A carrier drives ∣g,n⟩↔∣e,n⟩; the first red sideband drives ∣g,n⟩↔∣e,n−1⟩; the first blue sideband drives ∣g,n⟩↔∣e,n+1⟩. Their resonant drive angular frequencies follow by subtracting these energy levels. For a Raman transition, the drive is a beam-difference frequency with a sign fixed by the selected internal transition.
Choose a real carrier Rabi angular frequency Ω defined so that resonant carrier excitation is sin2(Ωt/2). In the Lamb-Dicke regime, isolated first-sideband Hamiltonians in a rotating frame can be written, after choosing the pulse phase, as
Hr​=2ℏηΩ​(σ+​a+σ−​a†),Hb​=2ℏηΩ​(σ+​a†+σ−​a),σ+​=∣e⟩⟨g∣,σ−​=σ+†​.
(14)
These formulas use a rotating-wave approximation: neglected carrier and other-sideband terms oscillate rapidly compared with the retained coupling. The drive's effective width and sideband coupling must be small compared with ν; sufficient suppression of off-resonant carrier excitation must also be checked. The red interaction is a Jaynes-Cummings model with a motional quantum replacing a cavity photon. The blue interaction creates internal excitation and a motional quantum together; the external laser supplies energy. The driven-system rotating-frame Hamiltonian alone is not the total laboratory energy.
