Round-off error

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Numerical analysis
OurBigBook.com Words: 170
Round-off error arises when numbers or arithmetic results are rounded to a finite set of representable values. Its scale is associated with machine epsilon, and its effects depend on subsequent operations. Even a formula with zero truncation error can lose information. In decimal arithmetic rounded to three significant digits, 1.234 becomes 1.23 and 1.233 becomes 1.23: subtracting the stored numbers gives zero although the exact difference is 0.001. The subtraction exposes errors already present in its nearly equal operands.
This example also has a large condition number: independently perturbing the two inputs of a−b can produce a relative amplification (∣a∣+∣b∣)/∣a−b∣=2467. High sensitivity belongs to the input-output problem; numerical stability concerns the arithmetic used to solve it. Conversely, on a common binary system with 53 significant bits and rounding to nearest, (1016+1)−1016 evaluates to zero while (1016−1016)+1 evaluates to one. Exact arithmetic makes both one: changing summation order changes the accumulated roundoff error. MIT's summation lecture explains why numerical stability and the condition number must be assessed separately.[ref]
