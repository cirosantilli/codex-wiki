Maxwell equations

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism
OurBigBook.com Words: 7k Articles: 83
The Maxwell equations are the field equations of classical electromagnetism. In SI units, in vacuum with prescribed charge density ρ and current density J, they are
∇⋅E∇×E​=ρ/ϵ0​,=−∂t∂B​,​∇⋅B∇×B​=0,=μ0​J+μ0​ϵ0​∂t∂E​.​
(1)
Here E is the electric field in volts per metre, B the magnetic field in teslas, ρ in coulombs per cubic metre, and J in amperes per square metre. The constants are vacuum permittivity ϵ0​ and the vacuum value μ0​ of magnetic permeability. “Vacuum” specifies the field laws without a material response; “source-free” additionally means ρ=0 and J=0. Matter described by averaged fields instead requires Maxwell equations in matter and constitutive relations.
The first equation, Gauss's law, makes electric charge a source of electric flux. Gauss's law for magnetism sets the net magnetic flux through a closed surface to zero. The two curl equations, Faraday's law and the Ampère-Maxwell equation, couple changes of the electric field and magnetic field. Appropriate initial conditions and boundary conditions select a physical solution; the two divergence equations constrain the initial fields. [ref].
