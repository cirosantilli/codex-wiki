Sanger ladder reading

 Home Biology Genetics Molecular genetics DNA sequencing Sanger sequencing
OurBigBook.com Words: 481
Consider an original teaching template whose next eight positions, encountered after the sequencing primer, are
3′-TGCAATCG-5′.
(8)
The sequencing primer binds an upstream region omitted from this display; the template also continues beyond the eight positions shown. DNA polymerase encounters the displayed template from left to right. Applying base pairing at each successive position gives the extension
5′-ACGTTAGC-3′.
(9)
These eight letters are added material, not the entire primer-containing DNA molecule.
Here is the full endpoint accounting. A terminal letter denotes an incorporated terminating nucleotide; all preceding letters of that extension were supplied by ordinary deoxynucleoside triphosphates.

The shortest fragments migrate farthest, placing position 1 at the bottom. Reading upward, the lane labels spell A, C, G, T, T, A, G, C: the new strand in its 5′-to-3′ direction. The adjacent T bands at positions 4 and 5 encode two separate additions. A single unresolved T patch would leave that count uncertain. In the A lane, the position-6 fragment passed position 1 using ordinary dATP and stopped at position 6 using ddATP; it does not skip the intervening nucleotides.
The common sequencing primer is an offset. For primer length p, endpoint n has length p+n nucleotides. With an illustrative p=20, the drawn fragments would contain 21 through 28 nucleotides, and adjacent endpoints still differ by one. Subtracting the common p preserves their order because (p+n+1)−(p+n)=1. This supplies a short justification for Sanger ladder reading: nested fragments share a start, length orders their endpoints, and lane membership supplies each endpoint's letter. A missing early band cannot be relabeled position 1 without independent evidence about the starting point.
To report the original template region, first complement the readout in aligned orientation:
new extension:aligned template:​5′-ACGTTAGC-3′,3′-TGCAATCG-5′.​
(10)
If the requested answer is that template written 5′ to 3′, reverse the aligned template to obtain 5′-GCTAACGT-3′, the reverse complement of the readout. Reversing the readout alone would give CGATTGCA and would be wrong. Complementing without reversing gives the correct template letters in its displayed 3′-to-5′ direction, but not in the usual reporting convention. Specify the strand and its direction before transforming the answer.
