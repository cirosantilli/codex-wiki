Seminorm topology (τP​)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Functional analysis Topological vector space Locally convex space
OurBigBook.com Words: 269 Articles: 1
Given a nonempty family P of finite-valued seminorms on a real or complex vector space E, its seminorm topology has basic open zero neighbourhoods
U(p1​,…,pm​;ε1​,…,εm​)=⋂i=1m​{x∈E:pi​(x)<εi​},pi​∈P,εi​>0.
(4)
Their translates form a basis of a topology. Only finite intersections are required: an infinite intersection might impose a stronger topology. Equivalently, put q(x)=maxi​pi​(x)/εi​; then q is a seminorm and U={q<1}. See Günther Hörmann, Advanced Functional Analysis, section 4, neighbourhood systems from seminorms for the general construction.
Each defining seminorm is continuous, since ∣p(x)−p(y)∣≤p(x−y). A sequence satisfies xn​→x precisely when p(xn​−x)→0 for every p∈P; the same criterion holds for nets. Finitely many measurements can each meet a prescribed tolerance after a common index.
The seminorm axioms also establish the required joint continuity of vector addition and scalar multiplication:
p((x+y)−(x0​+y0​))≤p(x−x0​)+p(y−y0​),p(λx−λ0​x0​)≤∣λ∣p(x−x0​)+∣λ−λ0​∣p(x0​).
(5)
For a basic neighbourhood, apply these estimates to its finitely many seminorms; near λ0​ the absolute value ∣λ∣ is bounded. This gives a locally convex space even when some directions are invisible to every measurement.
