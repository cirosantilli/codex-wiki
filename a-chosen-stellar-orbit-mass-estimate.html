A chosen stellar-orbit mass estimate

Choose D=7.5kpc, α=0.110 arcseconds, P=18 years, e=0.70 and i=55 degrees. The small-angle conversion gives a=825AU. Taking one model year as 365.25 days gives μ≃2.30×1026m3s−2 and K≃1566km/s.
Dividing by the nominal solar mass parameter, (GM)⊙N​=1.3271244×1020m3s−2, gives 1.73×106 nominal solar masses. The nominal parameter is an exact conversion convention, rather than an assertion that the Sun's measured mass is exact.[ref]
The chosen periapsis radius is rp​=a(1−e)=247.5AU. Its corresponding Schwarzschild radius, rs​=2μ/c2, is about 0.0342AU. Thus this illustrative orbit remains roughly 7200 Schwarzschild radii from the centre at closest approach. Inferring a very concentrated mass from such an orbit is different from directly resolving its event horizon. Every number here follows from the chosen orbit, not from a fit to the actual Galactic-centre measurements.
