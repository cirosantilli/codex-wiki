Bijective monomial cusp parametrization with nonregular inverse

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic geometry Algebraic variety Affine algebraic set Morphism of affine varieties
OurBigBook.com Words: 104
The morphism A1→V(x4−y3) given by t↦(t3,t4) is bijective on points over an algebraically closed field, but is not an isomorphism. At a nonzero curve point, t=y/x is the unique inverse parameter, while the origin has unique preimage zero. The induced map on coordinate rings has image k[t3,t4]⊂k[t], which lacks the monomial t: all its monomial exponents have the form 3a+4b, with a,b≥0. Nor can t be regular at the cusp, since an equality t=P(t3,t4)/Q(t3,t4) with Q(0)=0 would produce a nonzero degree-one term in tQ and none in P. Thus pointwise bijectivity does not guarantee an algebraic inverse, even though the inverse is regular away from one singular point.
