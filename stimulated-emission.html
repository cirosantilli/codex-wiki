Stimulated emission

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Laser
OurBigBook.com Words: 471 Articles: 1
Stimulated emission is emission induced by incident light, in contrast with the unprompted decay in spontaneous emission. It supplies the amplification mechanism of a laser. [ref].
For an emitter with upper and lower energy values E2​>E1​, the resonant photon has frequency ν satisfying
ΔE=E2​−E1​=hν,
(38)
where h is the Planck constant. In the elementary photon picture, an incident photon stimulates an emitter in the upper quantum state to emit an additional photon into the same optical normal mode, with the same frequency, propagation direction and photon polarization. The emitter supplies the extra energy; the stimulating photon is not consumed. This builds the existing field and its optical coherence. An emitter in the lower quantum state can instead undergo photon absorption, so stimulated emission alone does not establish net optical gain.
For example, a 2.0eV gap corresponds to ν≈4.8360×1014Hz and a vacuum wavelength of about 620nm. One ideal stimulated emission event converts one incoming photon into two outgoing photons in the selected optical normal mode, adding 2.0eV of light energy while lowering the emitter's energy by that amount.
