Coblentz's cavity-radiation spectrum measurement

 ... Physics Branches of physics Astrophysics Radiative transfer Planck function Wien's displacement law
OurBigBook.com Words: 192
William Weber Coblentz's primary report describes an electrically heated porcelain cavity wound with platinum ribbon. Mirrors imaged its central radiating diaphragm onto a 0.6mm optical slit; a fluorite optical prism dispersed the beam onto a vacuum bolometer. Galvanometer deflections read the detector response. A resistance standard checked electrical sensitivity, while repeated readings checked stability. Sodium lines fixed the angular zero; a refractive index curve supplied wavelengths and slit-bandwidth corrections. Thermocouples were standardized at freezing points of zinc, antimony and copper. Drying the optical path reduced atmospheric absorption; prism scattering and temperature calibration remained limitations. These were Coblentz's measurements. His extraction of radiation constants assumed the Planck law, so it was not a model-independent proof of the spectral formula.[ref]
The inference to test is whether corrected spectra taken at independently determined temperatures have their Bλ​ maxima at a constant product λmax​T. Comparing raw galvanometer deflections at different optical prism settings would also compare different wavelength widths and optical losses. The response integral in blackbody peak and detector response explains why those corrections belong to the physical inference rather than merely to presentation.
