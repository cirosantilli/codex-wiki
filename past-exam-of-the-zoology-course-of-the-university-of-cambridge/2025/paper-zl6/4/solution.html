positional information allows cells to choose an appropriate developmental response for their location; positional memory allows a location-dependent identity to persist after the original signal has changed. Neither is identical to cell differentiation. Two connective-tissue cells can have similar current functions but different information about where they belong in a limb. Development must establish these relationships, while regeneration must reconstruct missing relationships from an incomplete stump.
In the classical positional information framework, a tissue first establishes coordinates and cells interpret them according to their competence and developmental history. A morphogen gradient is one possible coordinate system. Different thresholds can elicit different gene expression states, but concentration alone is not the entire signal: exposure duration, feedback, changing tissue geometry and the response network also matter. Thus the same positional signal can produce different differentiated structures in different competent tissues.[ref]
The vertebrate limb bud is patterned along three interacting axes: proximal-distal, anterior-posterior and dorsal-ventral. The apical ectodermal ridge supplies FGF signalling that supports distal outgrowth and survival. Its early removal causes distal truncation, but this does not uniquely demonstrate that cells measure time in a progress zone: cell loss, altered growth and loss of signals can also change the structures that remain.[ref] Distal FGF signals and proximal influences interact with Hox genes and other regulators to organize stylopod, zeugopod and autopod territories. One must distinguish a positional identity defect from a failure to supply enough cells to execute that identity.
Anterior-posterior patterning provides a more direct signal-relocation test. Grafting posterior zone of polarizing activity tissue to the anterior of a chick limb bud produces duplicated digit patterns. Sonic hedgehog is expressed in the posterior region, and anterior implantation of cells expressing it reproduces polarizing activity. This connects a localized molecular signal to a change in spatial identity, rather than merely correlating expression with posterior development. Digit pattern still depends on signal duration, growth and downstream regulation; a static concentration-to-digit lookup table is inadequate.[ref] Along the dorsal-ventral axis, dorsal Wnt7a promotes Lmx1b in mesenchyme, while ventral Engrailed-1 helps restrict dorsal signalling. Interactions among these systems allow outgrowth and coordinate specification to remain aligned.
Regeneration adds the problem of interpreting an amputation plane. An axolotl limb usually replaces structures distal to the cut: a wrist-level stump does not routinely add a new upper arm before rebuilding the hand. This implies that regenerative outgrowth retains or reconstructs level-specific information. A blastema contains proliferative progenitor cells beneath a specialized wound epithelium, but it is not a uniformly pluripotent mass. Transgenic graft and lineage experiments showed substantial memory of tissue origin and differences among progenitor cell populations. The distinction between tissue identity and spatial identity is essential: producing cartilage does not alone show that its proximal-distal address is correct.[ref]
Retinoid experiments show that positional identity can be reprogrammed. Treatment of distal axolotl amputations with vitamin A or related retinoids can generate proximal structures before distal ones, sometimes producing a much more complete limb from a distal stump. This is difficult to explain as simple acceleration of growth, because the identities and sequence of regenerated structures change. It supports proximalization of the responding tissue. Nevertheless, pharmacological reprogramming does not by itself prove that an endogenous retinoid gradient is the sole normal coordinate system; dose-dependent toxicity and effects on growth must be assessed separately.[ref]
Spatial confrontation supplies another test. In an accessory-limb assay, a wound with appropriate nerve support can be combined with tissue of a different circumferential identity. Productive anterior-posterior interactions can support a patterned extra outgrowth, whereas wound healing and proliferation alone need not generate a complete limb. In axolotl experiments, anterior FGF8 and posterior Sonic hedgehog activities were identified as interacting signals, and manipulating them could replace aspects of the normally required tissue confrontation. This connects positional disparity to a signalling circuit that promotes regeneration, without implying that FGF8 and SHH encode every spatial identity by themselves.[ref]
A rigorous molecular test would transplant permanently labelled connective-tissue cells between defined limb positions, score their contribution and spatial identity, and perturb a candidate positional regulator specifically in those cells. Compare position-matched grafts, rotated or mismatched grafts, sham operations and equal nerve support; measure survival and proliferation so that graft failure is not mistaken for lost positional identity. Assess skeletal pattern and lineage output as well as positional markers, with rescue of the candidate perturbation. Such experiments separate a stable address, responsiveness to local signals and a generic requirement for outgrowth. Development and regeneration reuse signalling pathways, but regeneration additionally depends on retained information, wound-specific competence and communication between cells carrying different positional histories.
