cell specification and morphogenesis can form a reciprocal causal loop: cell identity changes force production and tissue material properties, while the resulting geometry and loading alter signalling and differentiation. A molecular programme need not first assign every identity and then leave mechanics merely to implement a predetermined shape. Conversely, correlation between a new shape and a new marker does not establish that mechanical input instructed the fate. Developing intestine, avian skin and lung illustrate distinct mechanisms and experimental tests.
Cells generate forces through actomyosin contractility, transmit them through cell adhesion and the extracellular matrix, and respond through mechanotransduction. Changes in cell number, volume, polarity and matrix composition modify the mechanical problem. A deformed tissue can change ligand distribution, receptor exposure or intracellular signalling without any change in a ligand's production rate. One must therefore distinguish force generation, transmission and sensing. A contractility inhibitor can disrupt all three as well as cell division, so an abnormal organ alone is not a mechanistic explanation.
In the embryonic chick intestine, differentiation of surrounding smooth muscle changes the constraints on epithelial growth. Successive muscle layers restrict expansion in different directions, helping a growing epithelial–mesenchymal composite form ridges, zigzags and eventually villi through buckling. Muscle perturbations and artificial confinement support a causal role for physical boundary conditions rather than a purely epithelial prepattern. [ref] Here an initial differentiation event supplies a mechanical constraint that changes tissue shape. Equivalent external confinement is especially informative because it can replace a mechanical function without restoring all molecular products of the missing muscle.
The new geometry then feeds back into regional cell-state regulation. Epithelial Sonic hedgehog signals become concentrated beneath the curved villus tips, promoting mesenchymal BMP signalling; the resulting signals restrict the stem-cell-associated proliferative programme away from those tips. [ref] Thus spatially patterned signalling can arise from deformation of a source that was initially more uniform. The result concerns localization of a developmental stem-cell programme, not proof that every cell has irreversibly acquired one terminal identity. Comparing curved and flattened explants, measuring ligand production as well as local pathway activity, and changing pathway activity downstream of deformation distinguish altered geometry from a change in total ligand supply.
This chick mechanism should not be imposed unchanged on mouse intestine. Mesenchymal patterning and surface deformation have different temporal relationships in different species. The reusable principle is a feedback between differentiated supporting tissue, physical geometry and local signalling; the exact sequence is an empirical question. A useful control compares developmental stage and viability, since a tissue that has simply stopped developing may lack both folds and differentiation markers without either being the direct cause of the other.
Avian skin supplies a different route from mechanics to identity. Contractile dermal cells aggregate beneath the epidermis, mechanically compressing it. Experiments linked this dermal self-organization to activation of epidermal beta-catenin and initiation of the feather-follicle programme. Altering contractility or tissue mechanical conditions changed aggregate formation and pattern. [ref] This makes the feather placode an emergent epithelial–mesenchymal structure rather than requiring a complete molecular placode map to exist before any cell movement.
The important distinction is between beta-catenin's junctional and transcriptional roles. Redistribution of an adhesion-associated protein alone is not evidence for a fate change; nuclear activity and appropriate target expression, followed by placode development, provide a stronger chain. To test ordering, one can alter dermal force production while preserving epidermal viability, apply controlled compression, and perturb the epithelial response separately. Restoration of placode gene activity by a downstream intervention would place that activity after mechanical input, but would not necessarily rescue normal spacing. Molecular identity and periodic geometry are related outputs that can be experimentally separated.
In the developing lung, differences in how epithelial cells experience a common physical input help generate different differentiated states. live-cell imaging and perturbations showed that forces associated with fetal breathing movements and fluid displacement contribute to type I alveolar cell differentiation. A subset of progenitors protrudes toward mesenchyme through FGF10–FGFR2-dependent signalling; enrichment of apical myosin in those cells resists flattening and supports type II alveolar cell fate. [ref] Thus a local biochemical signal changes the cell's mechanical response, while loading helps determine whether it becomes thin and extended or retains the alternative morphology and differentiation programme.
A decisive lung experiment must therefore vary force and growth-factor signalling independently. If a receptor perturbation changes fate, it could act through competence, proliferation, survival or the physical protection from flattening. Cell tracking and early marker measurements distinguish transformation of an existing progenitor from selective loss of one population. Controlled loading, matched fluid and culture conditions, measurement of cell shape, and restoration of the relevant contractile response can separate these alternatives. Flattening alone is not a sufficient endpoint: the molecular and functional differentiation of the two alveolar classes must also be assessed.
These systems demonstrate three forms of coupling: differentiation changes a tissue constraint, geometry reorganizes an extracellular signal, and local signalling tunes a cell's response to a physical force. They need not all use the same sensor or transcription factor. Nor does mechanics replace biochemical information: the same deformation can elicit different responses in cells with different developmental competence. A fate programme can also alter adhesion or matrix properties and thereby change the mechanical input experienced by neighbouring cells, closing a mechanochemical feedback loop.
The strongest general strategy combines time-resolved live-cell imaging, fate or lineage reporters, a physical measurement and an independently targeted perturbation. Initial recoil after laser ablation reports load relative to resistance rather than an absolute force without a material model; substrate deformation and pressure measurements likewise need calibrated assumptions. Acute and tissue-restricted perturbations, sham procedures, independent genetic alleles and appropriate rescue help avoid generalized damage as the explanation. Fate and form are coupled through specific causal chains, but identifying a mechanically instructive step requires measuring both the physical intermediate and the resulting change in developmental state.

The Lgr5-expressing population is not always indispensable for short-term small-intestinal maintenance, but the unqualified claim of dispensability is misleading. Its contribution and its requirement depend on injury, timing, ablation efficiency and replacement by other cells. Three questions must be separated: whether Lgr5-expressing cells normally generate the epithelium, whether the Lgr5 receptor itself is necessary, and whether the tissue can replace the marker-defined cell state after its cells are removed.
In adult mouse small intestine, actively cycling crypt-base columnar cells include an Lgr5-expressing population that produces the major epithelial lineages. Inducible permanent lineage tracing from Lgr5-expressing cells generated long-lived epithelial contributions, demonstrating self-renewal and multilineage output. [ref] This identifies a functional stem-cell population under those conditions. It establishes normal contribution, however, not exclusive capacity or necessity. A lineage can supply most routine replacement while another population has latent capacity to take over after removal.
stem-cell ablation makes a different causal test. Engineering the human diphtheria toxin receptor into the Lgr5 locus allowed the marked population to be depleted. In the tested depletion regime, intestinal epithelial maintenance continued, and other cells could replace the missing stem-cell output; Bmi1-associated lineage experiments supported a reserve contribution. [ref] This result refutes a simple model in which immediate loss of all renewal must follow removal of the cycling Lgr5-positive compartment. It does not establish that no stem-like function is necessary, that the marker-defined state can never reappear, or that the same result holds indefinitely under every physiological challenge.
The relevant conclusion is about the experimental window and endpoint. Histologically recognizable crypts shortly after treatment are weaker evidence than sustained production of all lineages and preserved barrier or absorptive function. transit-amplifying cells and surviving differentiated cells can temporarily conceal a loss of long-term renewal. Conversely, if output continues beyond their expected persistence and previously unlabelled cells restore long-lived clones, genuine compensation is likely. Repeated observation should therefore quantify target-cell depletion, proliferation, crypt survival, lineage composition and the re-emergence of Lgr5 expression throughout treatment and recovery.
Interpretation also depends on whether reserve cells form a permanently distinct hierarchy or are recruited from existing descendants. Expression of a proposed reserve marker does not prove that it exclusively labels a quiescent stem-cell population. Drivers can label several states, marker expression can change after injury, and apparent populations can overlap. An ablation-induced contribution from a marker-labelled cell is evidence of regenerative potential in that labelled lineage; it need not reveal its normal developmental position. Labelling before injury and allowing the inducing reagent to clear are essential to avoid marking newly activated cells and mistaking them for the original source.
Committed descendants provide direct evidence for such flexibility. Enterocyte-lineage progenitors labelled through Alpi normally give short-lived absorptive descendants, yet after Lgr5-cell ablation they can produce persistent renewing lineages. [ref] This supports intestinal stem-cell plasticity rather than an absolutely one-way hierarchy. It should not be generalized to every mature cell on a villus: a progenitor already biased toward an absorptive fate and a terminally mature enterocyte are different starting populations. Clonal persistence and renewed multilineage output are stronger endpoints than transient expression of one stem-cell marker.
Further work connected recovery of the ablated intestinal stem-cell compartment to Ascl2-dependent dedifferentiation of absorptive and secretory precursors. [ref] Regenerative cells can initiate the returning programme before the full usual marker combination is restored. This makes stemness a context-dependent functional state as well as a useful marker-defined category. It also yields a testable limit to compensation: if a required reprogramming regulator is disabled, the presence of alternative surviving cells need not be sufficient for recovery. Targeted perturbation should be assessed alongside precursor survival so that failure to reprogramme is not confused with removal of the proposed source.
Dispensability changes markedly when another injury removes or disables the compensating population. Combining Lgr5-cell depletion with radiation-induced damage caused severe crypt loss and failure of robust regeneration, unlike the relatively preserved homeostasis after depletion alone. [ref] This demonstrates conditional necessity in the tested injury regime. It does not create a contradiction with compensation under ordinary maintenance: the two interventions alter different components of the regenerative system. Radiation affects multiple epithelial and niche populations, and its timing relative to ablation and recovery is therefore part of the explanation rather than a minor technical detail.
A factorial experiment makes that logic explicit. Compare control, Lgr5-cell depletion alone, radiation alone, and the combination, with matched ages, intestinal regions and observation times. Include receptor-negative animals receiving toxin, receptor-positive untreated controls, and independent confirmation of cell removal rather than relying solely on loss of the fluorescent reporter. Measure crypt number and clonogenic recovery, epithelial composition, apoptosis, inflammatory or stromal damage and longer-term function. Evidence that surviving untargeted cells generate new stem-cell clones is required to identify the replacement mechanism; normal tissue appearance does not identify the source.
The duration of targeting is also important. A lineage that first lacks the marker but later adopts Lgr5 expression may become sensitive to continued toxin treatment. A pulse of ablation followed by repopulation and sustained elimination of the re-emerging state consequently test different hypotheses. Escaper cells with incomplete receptor expression or recombination can expand after treatment and mimic recruitment from another lineage. Pre-existing lineage labels, spatial imaging and independent ablation strategies help distinguish rare survivors from true conversion, while acknowledging that every strategy can perturb the niche itself.
Finally, removing Lgr5-expressing cells is not equivalent to deleting the Lgr5 gene. The former removes entire cells, including their other molecular functions; the latter changes a receptor while leaving the cells and possible compensating pathways in place. Likewise, demonstrating organoid growth under supplied matrix and growth factors establishes competence under those conditions, not niche independence in vivo. Normal renewal, replacement capacity and receptor requirement must therefore remain separate conclusions.
The evidence supports a plastic, partly redundant renewal system: Lgr5-positive cells are major normal contributors, can be replaced under some maintenance conditions, and can become crucial when injury limits alternative sources. The scientifically useful claim is conditional dispensability of a defined population under a defined protocol, not general irrelevance of those cells or the stem-cell state they exemplify.

Quantitative lineage tracing shifted the interpretation of squamous renewal from a necessarily rigid stem-cell hierarchy toward population-level balance, while live-cell imaging and injury experiments revealed local coordination and context-dependent plasticity. The resulting picture is neither that every basal cell is identical nor that one universal hierarchy applies to every anatomical site. Methods measure different properties, and their assumptions help explain apparently conflicting conclusions.
A classical model of stratified epithelium places long-lived stem cells above a finite sequence of transit-amplifying cells, whose descendants leave the basal compartment and differentiate. Spatial columns of epidermal cells and slow label dilution were interpreted in terms of epidermal proliferative units. Such observations are compatible with a hierarchy, but do not establish it uniquely. A label-retaining cell may divide slowly, be terminally differentiated, or retain label because of assay kinetics. Conversely, an actively cycling stem cell can lose a dilution-sensitive label rapidly. Clonogenicity after culture or transplantation measures potential in a perturbed environment rather than necessarily identifying the cells that sustain undamaged tissue.
Sparse, inducible permanent clonal lineage tracing allowed the descendants of individual basal cells to be followed without relying on label retention. Clone numbers, basal-cell contents and differentiated descendants could be quantified at multiple chase times and compared with explicit stochastic models. In mouse epidermis, such analysis supported a model in which one progenitor class generated both renewing and differentiating daughters, with balance achieved across the population rather than one obligatory asymmetric division for every stem cell. [ref] Clones disappeared while surviving clones expanded, even though the tissue remained stable. That pattern is not evidence that disappearing clones were necessarily a separate short-lived class.
The bookkeeping is informative. Suppose a proliferative cell divides to give two proliferative daughters with probability r, one proliferative and one differentiating daughter with probability 1−2r, and two differentiating daughters with probability r, where 0≤r≤1/2. The expected number of proliferative daughters is:
2r+(1−2r)=1.
(1)
Thus the proliferative population can maintain itself even when individual divisions have different outcomes. In a simplified critical branching process with division rate λ, the effective rates of clone-size increase and decrease are both λr per proliferative cell. Starting from one such cell:
P(Nt​>0)=1+λrt1​,E[Nt​∣Nt​>0]=1+λrt.
(2)
The product remains one: increasing mean size among surviving clones can accompany unchanged expected labelled cell number across all initial clones. These formulae illustrate survivor conditioning; actual tissue geometry, noncycling basal cells, finite compartments and local interactions can require a different quantitative model.
Work on mouse oesophageal epithelium combined label-retention assays with lineage tracing and found that a single proliferative population could account for maintenance. After wounding, its outcomes became biased toward producing proliferating daughters, then returned toward balance after repair. [ref] Changing the division rate and changing the probability of daughter fates are different responses. More divisions with unchanged balance can accelerate replacement without expanding the renewing pool; biased outcomes can expand that pool. Neither observation alone requires conversion of a terminally differentiated cell into a stem cell.
Evidence for heterogeneity also remained important. Marker-selected tracing and proliferation analysis in mouse epidermis supported distinct slow-cycling stem and committed progenitor compartments. [ref] Such results should not be dismissed simply because a single-progenitor model fits another dataset. Anatomical site, promoter expression, induction dose, chase duration and which cells are scored can change the sampled population. A driver that preferentially labels a slow-cycling subset and a broadly induced driver need not produce the same clone distribution. Models should be compared against joint measurements of clone survival, cell cycle kinetics and labelled differentiated output rather than one convenient summary statistic.
Live intravital imaging added information that destructive sampling cannot supply. Tracking the same cells shows which event precedes which: division, loss of basal contact, movement into a suprabasal layer, or neighbour expansion. In adult mouse epidermis, local differentiation was followed by neighbour expansion and compensatory cell cycle progression, supporting a mechanism in which cell loss locally stimulates self-renewal. [ref] Balanced stochastic clone statistics therefore need not mean that cells make independent random decisions insulated from their neighbours. A spatially coordinated system can generate population-level fate balance and apparent randomness at the level of individual retrospective clones.
Modern molecular methods refine, but do not replace, those functional tests. single-cell RNA sequencing identifies heterogeneous molecular states and pathway activity; spatial measurements locate them relative to basement membrane, follicles or wounds. A trajectory inferred from transcriptomes is not a directly observed lineage, and an intermediate expression profile can reflect a transient response rather than a cell reversing fate. Combining molecular profiling with permanent labels, repeated imaging and appropriately timed perturbations distinguishes state similarity, ancestry and causal requirement. cell cycle state and injury-associated inflammation also need to be separated from a genuinely distinct renewing population.
Direct evidence for cell fate plasticity requires more than changing proliferation within an existing progenitor class. Pre-injury labelling of GATA6-expressing differentiated sebaceous-duct cells showed that their descendants could move into wounded interfollicular epidermis, regain basal renewing properties and produce a broader range of epidermal descendants. Long-term tracing and live observations supported dedifferentiation under the wound conditions. [ref] The key comparison is the fate of the same pre-labelled population in undamaged and damaged tissue. It is not sufficient to find Gata6-lineage fluorescence in a cell that has just begun expressing the marker after injury.
Several controls make such a claim persuasive. Verify the initial labelled cell identities before damage; allow the inducer to clear; exclude rare pre-labelled stem cells or reporter leakage; and demonstrate sustained renewal rather than transient basal residence. Imaging a labelled cell's transition and showing its later functional output is stronger than a static double-positive image. Exclude cell fusion and selective expansion of an unrecognized initial subpopulation where relevant. Perturbing a proposed reprogramming regulator should preserve source-cell viability, since losing the source is not the same as preventing its identity change.
The general conclusion is a flexible hierarchy of evidence. Label retention measures division history, clonal tracing measures descendant output, live-cell imaging measures event order and local interactions, and molecular profiling measures state. Together they support dynamic population self-renewal, site-dependent heterogeneity and injury-induced epithelial plasticity, while leaving normal maintenance distinct from regenerative potential. Claims about one mouse skin region or oesophagus should be tested independently before being generalized to every squamous tissue or to humans.

I would test whether the candidate changes digit identity while separating that effect from changes in limb axes, digit number, growth and survival. A convincing result would combine an early, position-appropriate requirement, a complementary gain-of-function phenotype and specific rescue, with positive evidence that an affected digit has adopted an alternative identity. Expression alone or an absent digit would not establish the hypothesis.
First define the experimental system and the identity being measured. In a chosen species and limb, map the normal skeletal pattern, digit positions, phalangeal structure, associated soft tissues and combinations of early molecular features. Record anatomical stage as well as chronological age. Serial imaging of condensations and later cartilage and bone distinguishes a developmental change from delayed ossification. Do not assume that a single marker labels the same identity in every digit or across all tetrapods: comparative digit transcriptomes have challenged universal digit-specific signatures. [ref] Identity should therefore be defined using several independent features in the actual experimental species.
Establish the candidate's spatial and temporal expression before, during and after the proposed identity-specification window. Use validated in situ hybridization, protein detection or an endogenous reporter, and compare anterior–posterior position, proximal–distal level and dorsal–ventral side. Determine whether expression is in mesenchyme, ectoderm, a signalling centre or differentiating cartilage. Co-expression with a digit marker suggests an association; expression preceding that marker makes an instructive role plausible but still does not demonstrate it. A reporter must be checked against endogenous expression and function, since insertion or overexpression can change dosage or cellular localization.
The normal patterning context supplies crucial controls. Posterior limb bud Sonic hedgehog signalling has a demonstrated ability to polarize the limb: ectopic expression can generate altered anterior–posterior patterns. [ref] The distal apical ectodermal ridge also supports outgrowth, while other signals establish dorsal–ventral properties. The candidate might alter one of these organizers and only indirectly alter digit identities. I would therefore measure organizer position and pathway activity, limb bud size and overall axis specification before treating an abnormal skeleton as a direct digit-identity phenotype.
For necessity, generate at least two independent loss-of-function alleles or validated independent targeting reagents, then establish that candidate protein or activity is actually lost. A constitutive lethal or globally malformed embryo is uninformative about a specific limb function. tissue-specific gene perturbation in limb mesenchyme or ectoderm, combined with temporal gene perturbation, can distinguish an early role in establishing the bud from a later role in assigning digit character. Include littermate or sibling controls, driver-only and inducer-only controls, and assess possible effects of the delivery method itself. Recombination, protein depletion and pathway-response delays must be measured rather than assumed to coincide with the time of induction.
The strongest predicted loss-of-function outcome is a reproducible change in a digit's position-associated character while a digit remains present in the corresponding lineage and location. A smaller digit, delayed cartilage, apoptosis or reduced progenitor number could instead explain the phenotype. I would measure cell proliferation, apoptosis, condensation number, local tissue movement and chondrogenic differentiation at early stages, before secondary degeneration. If a digit is missing, fate-map its prospective territory to determine whether cells died, failed to expand, contributed elsewhere or retained an alternative identity. That analysis can identify a real developmental requirement without overcalling it specification.
For sufficiency, activate the candidate at a normally negative position during the relevant window, using regulated expression near physiological levels. Compare equivalent expression after the window has closed. A position-appropriate identity transformation induced early but not late would support an instructive, competence-dependent function. A broad expansion of tissue or induction of ectopic organizer activity would require a different interpretation. Include vector and inactive-product controls where appropriate, verify dose and distribution, and avoid equating high-level toxicity with a patterned effect. Mosaic activation can reveal whether only expressing cells change or whether neighbouring cells respond through an induced signal.
Timing is particularly important because digit patterning integrates signal duration and tissue expansion. Lineage and perturbation studies of Shh supported an expansion-based temporal contribution to digit identity. [ref] An intervention can consequently change which cells experience a signal, for how long, or where they end up. I would permanently label candidate-expressing and neighbouring cells before perturbation and track them through condensation. Measure both pathway duration and cell displacement, rather than inferring an identity transformation merely from a digit's final ordinal position.
Rescue should restore the relevant candidate function with its appropriate timing and location. Re-expression of a targeting-resistant wild-type product that corrects the phenotype supports specificity. Domain mutants can test whether DNA binding, secretion or another biochemical activity is required, but their stability and localization must be confirmed. A rescue that restores total limb growth but leaves the identity pattern abnormal would separate these functions. Conversely, restoring a downstream organizer signal may rescue the phenotype while indicating that the candidate normally acts upstream rather than directly in digit cells.
To position the candidate mechanistically, combine its perturbation with controlled manipulation of established pathways. If the candidate requires a Shh response, ask whether its activity can bypass pathway inhibition or whether the candidate itself is required for a Shh-induced transformation. Such epistasis tests must use doses and stages that preserve a limb competent to respond. Transcript profiling can identify early consequences before overt shape changes. For a putative transcription factor, binding assays and a targeted perturbation of a candidate regulatory element can test direct regulation. Binding or differential expression alone is not proof that the target causes the identity phenotype.
Finally, analyse the data with the animal or independently manipulated limb as the biological replicate, accounting for the shared environment of digits within one limb. Randomize treatments, blind anatomical scoring and use predefined identity criteria. A contralateral limb is useful but not always independent if the perturbation is systemic or changes whole-embryo timing. Test reproducibility across developmental batches and, if arguing for a conserved function, validate it in another suitably staged species rather than assuming identical digit programmes.
Support for specification would require necessity, sufficiency and rescue tied to a defined spatial and temporal window, plus evidence for an alternative identity rather than simple tissue loss. Other outcomes would still be informative: they might place the gene in growth, competence, organizer maintenance or later differentiation instead of digit-identity assignment itself.

The looping heart tube emerges through staged recruitment and differentiation of regionally distinct progenitors. Developmental time changes a cell's competence and signalling environment, while anatomical position determines its neighbours, migration route and eventual contribution. The tube is therefore not produced by simultaneous differentiation of a homogeneous cardiac population, and chronological age alone does not specify a cell's developmental state.
The process begins before a recognizable heart exists. During gastrulation, cardiogenic mesoderm emerges from the primitive streak and moves into an anterior lateral environment, where interactions with neighbouring tissues help establish cardiac competence. Timed clonal labelling of Mesp1-expressing progenitors showed that populations labelled at different gastrulation times make different regional and lineage contributions to the mouse heart. [ref] This supports early regional segregation and restriction, rather than a single unrestricted precursor pool mixing uniformly until the heart tube forms. It does not mean that a pulse of marker expression is itself a complete autonomous clock: labelling time also selects cells at different positions and under different signalling conditions.
In mouse, the first heart field differentiates relatively early within the cardiac crescent and contributes substantially to initial myocardium, including much of the future left ventricle. Folding and convergence bring myocardial and endocardial components into the forming heart tube. Other cardiac progenitors remain outside the initial differentiated structure. Thus the location of a progenitor at one stage need not equal its later location in the organ; fate maps must follow tissue movement as well as the onset of a marker.
The second heart field maintains a proliferative reservoir that supplies additional cells as the tube elongates and loops. Contributions are made at the arterial pole of the heart and the venous pole of the heart, with different subregions supplying outflow, right-ventricular, atrial and inflow territories in the mouse. ISL1-associated lineage tracing and mutant analysis established major contributions from a population that proliferates before differentiating. [ref] ISL1 expression is consequently useful in the context of a dynamic lineage; it is not a permanent marker of one mature myocardial subtype. Species, timing and driver history must be specified when assigning exact heart-field boundaries.
Retention of progenitors outside the tube and their differentiation on entry solve different developmental tasks. If too many differentiate too early, the reservoir may be depleted before the organ receives enough cells; if they remain undifferentiated too long, the growing tube may lack functional myocardium. A small outflow region can therefore result from reduced specification, insufficient progenitor proliferation, failed migration, premature differentiation or poor survival. Counting cells only in the final tube cannot distinguish these explanations.
Signal timing supplies a direct reason for such context dependence. Experiments in zebrafish and embryonic stem-cell differentiation showed biphasic effects of Wnt signalling: activity at earlier and later stages had different consequences for cardiac specification. [ref] This does not imply that Wnt must always be completely absent from every later cardiac progenitor, because maintenance of specific progenitor domains has additional signalling requirements. Rather, the same pathway acts on different regulatory states as development proceeds. A timed activation or inhibition experiment must therefore define the precursor state and assay actual differentiated output instead of treating pathway activity as a timeless instruction to form heart.
Feedback also coordinates specification and expansion. In mouse, NKX2-5-dependent restraint of Bmp2/Smad1 signalling regulates the transition between early cardiac specification, second-heart-field proliferation and outflow development. Mutants showed increased specification-associated and progenitor programmes initially but later failed to sustain normal expansion; reducing Smad1 activity rescued important aspects of the phenotype. [ref] This is a particularly informative genetic interaction because it shows that stronger expression of an induction programme need not yield more successfully deployed differentiated myocardium. Pathway dose, time and location jointly determine the outcome.
Direct timing measurements show that addition to the tube can continue through new differentiation rather than division of existing cardiomyocytes alone. In zebrafish, fluorescent reporter maturation and photoconversion distinguished earlier differentiated cells from later contributions, identifying distinct phases and late addition at the arterial pole. [ref] photoconversion acts as a time stamp: a cell that first differentiates after conversion can carry a different reporter history from one already in the tube. Controls for incomplete conversion, fluorophore maturation, dilution and imaging damage are essential. Measuring cell cycle activity in parallel separates differentiation of recruited cells from proliferation of cells already present.
Position within the forming organ further shapes what differentiation means. myocardium, endocardium and supporting mesenchyme have different programmes and communicate across their interfaces. Along the tube, prospective chamber regions acquire different growth and functional properties from connecting or outflow territories; a generic cardiomyocyte marker does not establish chamber-specific maturation. The atrioventricular canal, for example, is not simply a less advanced sample of every future chamber cell. Regional differences can be actively maintained while the whole organ continues developing.
The left–right axis adds another positional dimension. Asymmetric PITX2-associated programmes link embryonic sidedness to organ morphogenesis, including heart looping. [ref] However, looping direction and myocardial differentiation are separable readouts. Reversed or abnormal geometry does not automatically imply failure to acquire cardiac identity, while a normally oriented but undersized tube can still contain an important differentiation defect. Three-dimensional imaging of sidedness, tube shape and regional markers is therefore needed to relate position to cell state rather than inferring one from the other.
An effective experimental design combines timed lineage induction, live-cell imaging and spatial molecular measurements. Compare matched morphological stages or somite counts as well as elapsed time, since a perturbation can delay the entire embryo. Apply pathway manipulations before and after progenitor specification, and measure proliferation, apoptosis, migration, cell entry and sarcomeric differentiation separately. A pulse-labelled lineage reveals descendants, whereas marker expression in a snapshot reveals current state; single-cell trajectories alone do not demonstrate ancestry. Tissue-restricted rescue can test whether a phenotype originates within a cardiac progenitor or in its signalling environment.
Finally, the onset of contraction and blood flow introduces new mechanical and physiological conditions during morphogenesis, but these should not be used to erase earlier temporal differences. A marker of contractile machinery, organized sarcomeres, beating, chamber-specific electrophysiology and adult-like maturation are progressively different endpoints. The relevant endpoint for emergence of the looping tube is appropriately timed regional deployment of differentiated cells, not completion of every mature cardiac property.
Time establishes competence and the sequence of recruitment; location supplies distinct signals, constraints and lineage destinations. Their interaction permits an early working tube to coexist with later progenitor addition and progressive regional specialization, and explains why the same molecular perturbation can have different effects at different stages or poles.

Core polarity factors define and maintain spatial domains; tissue-specific effectors translate those domains into different patterns of membrane growth, force, adhesion and cell arrangement. An apical–basal axis supplies a coordinate system rather than a complete geometric blueprint. Cells with the same orientation can form a flat sheet, a cyst, an elongated tube or a folded epithelium, depending on what is built and remodelled at each domain.
The conserved modules include the PAR polarity complex, with PAR-3, PAR-6, Cdc42-associated regulation and aPKC; the crumbs polarity complex; and basolateral regulators associated with the scribble polarity complex. Scaffolding, recruitment, phosphorylation and mutual antagonism help establish boundaries between domains. The components do not always form one invariant complex at one fixed position: PAR-3 can occupy junctional regions differently from other partners, and tissue-specific interactions modify their localization and activity. A shared component list therefore permits different spatial and temporal organizations.
External geometry also provides orientation. Contacts with neighbouring cells and the basement membrane, adhesion receptors and the extracellular matrix help define which surface should face a lumen and which should contact supporting tissue. Within the membrane plane, further anisotropy can organize elongation or neighbour exchange; planar cell polarity is distinct from the apical–basal axis. Thus a cell can retain apical-basal polarity yet change its planar orientation, dimensions or placement. Conversely, individually polarized cells can fail to align their apical surfaces into one common lumen.
Differential membrane allocation is one route from polarity to shape. Apical and basolateral surfaces can grow at different rates through polarized membrane trafficking, recycling and removal. Junction position sets the boundary between those domains, while actomyosin and adhesion alter their areas and mechanical coupling. As a simple geometric description, a cell with smaller apical than basal area can be wedge-shaped; this does not mean that its polarity factors directly encode a wedge. The resulting tissue curvature also depends on cell volume, height, neighbour arrangement and external constraints. The same apical contractile programme can bend a compliant sheet or generate tension in a constrained one.
Three-dimensional epithelial culture provides a clear mechanistic example. In MDCK cyst formation, a Rab11a/Rabin8/Rab8a pathway and the exocyst coordinate apical membrane delivery with PAR-3 and Cdc42 activity at an apical membrane initiation site. Perturbing components of this network disrupts formation of the organized apical surface and lumen. [ref] This connects polarity to a concrete transport process rather than treating a polarity marker as an explanation of shape. Matrix and cell-contact geometry help orient the site, while the trafficking machinery supplies the material needed to build it.
A separate output of related regulators is division orientation. In Caco-2 cysts, Cdc42 depletion disturbed orientation of the spindle apparatus and the normal placement of apical surfaces, producing multiple lumina even though individual cells retained apical-basal organization. [ref] The important distinction is tissue-level topology versus cell-level polarity. If daughters are placed incorrectly relative to an existing lumen, several locally polarized rosettes can form instead of one enclosure. One should therefore quantify angle of the spindle apparatus, daughter trajectories and lumen connectivity as well as the localization of a single apical marker. The MDCK trafficking and Caco-2 spindle experiments are complementary models, not interchangeable proof that every Cdc42 phenotype has the same immediate cause.
Tube dimensions provide another output. In the Drosophila trachea, perturbations of Yurt and Coracle affected tube dimensions, and genetic interactions with Crumbs linked these effects to control of the apical domain. Importantly, assays of the luminal matrix and its secretion distinguished this route from another pathway controlling tube size. [ref] Thus a core polarity regulator can affect apical surface extent without simply abolishing epithelial identity or causing every tube phenotype through failed secretion. Cell number, cell shape, apical area and luminal matrix organization must be measured independently before elongation or widening is assigned to a particular mechanism.
An elongated tube additionally requires anisotropic growth or rearrangement. Uniform expansion of every apical surface would enlarge an enclosure but would not uniquely specify a long narrow shape. Tissue-specific regulation of actomyosin, junction turnover, division and matrix resistance can translate the same domain organization into preferential extension. Altering the balance between apical and lateral surface growth can change diameter as well as length. This is why changing the dose or distribution of a core regulator can produce a dimensional phenotype even when the basic apical–basal distinction remains detectable.
Vascular lumen formation illustrates how the effector machinery changes with epithelial context. In the developing mouse aorta, imaging and perturbations connected endothelial contact organization to localization of CD34-family sialomucin, Moesin and actomyosin at the prospective lumen. VE-cadherin was required for early organization of luminal components, while VEGF-A acted at a different step involving cytoskeletal interactions and cell-shape change. [ref] The lumen forms between neighbouring cells rather than being explained solely by death of central cells. This is a case in which adhesion, specialized surface composition and active mechanics convert domain organization into a space; it need not duplicate the cyst mechanism in every detail.
The shared toolkit can also have stage-specific outputs. During initial epithelial assembly, establishing a first apical site is the dominant task. During growth, orienting divisions and supplying membrane preserve an existing architecture. During folding, changing junctional mechanics may become most important. A late perturbation can therefore alter shape without preventing initial polarity, whereas an early perturbation can collapse the entire tissue and conceal more specific downstream roles. Polarity-dependent signalling can also change proliferation or differentiation, so geometry can be altered indirectly through cell composition as well as directly through mechanics.
Experiments should exploit that modularity. Use acute, mosaic or tissue-restricted perturbations; measure viability, cell number and polarity before generalized disorganization; and rescue with constructs that separate localization, catalytic and binding functions. Combine endogenous reporters with live-cell imaging of membrane traffic, junctions, the spindle apparatus and luminal space. Domain-targeted rescue is especially informative if it restores one geometric output while leaving another defective. Loss of an apical marker, a multiple-lumen phenotype and an abnormally wide tube are different observations and should not all be reported simply as loss of polarity.
A limited polarity toolkit generates diverse shapes through variable localization, dosage, timing and coupling to tissue-specific effectors and boundary conditions. Its conservation explains shared organizing principles; its modular outputs explain why conservation does not imply identical epithelial geometry.
