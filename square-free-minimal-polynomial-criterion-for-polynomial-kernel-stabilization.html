Square-free minimal polynomial criterion for polynomial kernel stabilization (kerp(T)n=kerp(T) for all p,n≥1)

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Linear algebra Operator theory Minimal polynomial
OurBigBook.com Words: 138
For a linear operator on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field K, all polynomial kernels stabilize at their first power exactly when its minimal polynomial m has no repeated irreducible polynomial factor in K[X]. This formulation does not require those factors to be linear.
To prove it, the Bézout's identity for polynomials gives kerq(T)=kergcd(q,m)(T): a greatest common divisor is a combination of q and m, while q is its multiple. If m is square-free, gcd(pn,m)=gcd(p,m), proving the displayed equality. Conversely, if an irreducible factor f occurs to exponent e≥2, its primary decomposition theorem summand has minimal polynomial fe. On this summand f(T) is nonzero, while f(T)e=0. Thus the kernels of those two powers differ. An eigenbasis follows from this criterion precisely when the distinct factors of m are also linear over K; a real rotation through a right angle has square-free m=X2+1 but no real eigenvalues.
