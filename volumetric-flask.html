Volumetric flask

OurBigBook.com Words: 168
A volumetric flask prepares a chemical solution at a specified total volume, using a calibration mark.
Its mark specifies contained volume under its calibration conditions. In contrast, a volumetric pipette specifies a delivered portion. The narrow neck makes a small change of volume visible as a larger change of liquid height. The final chemical solution volume includes the solute; “dilute to 100mL” does not mean “add 100mL of water”.[ref]
For an original dilution example, a fully transferred 10.00mL aliquot of 0.100molL−1 acid contains 1.000mmol. Making the final total 100.0mL gives analytical concentration 0.0100molL−1. A subsequent 10.00mL aliquot contains 0.1000mmol and needs 1.000mL of 0.100molL−1 base for one-to-one neutralization. The entire diluted flask still needs 10.00mL of that base: dilution preserves the selected inventory but sampling selects a fraction of it.
Overfilling lowers the intended molar concentration. Removing some well-mixed chemical solution to restore the mark also removes solute, so it does not recover the intended inventory. Full transfer, known final volume, and mixing are separate requirements.
