Kruskal's algorithm worked example

 ... Area of mathematics Combinatorics Tree (graph theory) Spanning tree Minimum spanning tree Kruskal's algorithm
OurBigBook.com Words: 208
Consider this original connected graph on vertices A,B,C,D,E. Its undirected edges, already in increasing weight order, are
edgeweight​AB1​BC2​AC3​CD4​DE5​CE6​BE7.​​
(6)
Here AB means the unordered pair {A,B}; the weights are dimensionless costs. Accept AB and BC, with total 3. Reject AC: its endpoints already have the graph path A,B,C, so it would close the graph cycle A,B,C,A. Accept CD and DE, reaching all five vertices with four edges. The resulting minimum spanning tree is
T={AB,BC,CD,DE},w(T)=1+2+4+5=12.
(7)
Stop here: CE and BE need not be examined. If processing continued, both would be rejected because their endpoints are already in the same connected component of a graph.
Taking the three cheapest edges AB,BC,AC instead gives a graph cycle on A,B,C, leaving D,E isolated: it is neither a forest nor a spanning tree. Even taking the four cheapest edges adds only CD and leaves E isolated. A cheap infeasible selection cannot compete with a minimum spanning tree. For this small graph, checking all (47​)=35 four-edge subsets gives a direct certificate: among those that are spanning trees, exactly one has minimum cost 12, namely T. The general algorithm avoids this exhaustive search.
