The zero extension of H01 makes vm​ an element of H1(R3). The chain rule and change of variables give the exact scaling identities
∥∇vm​∥L2(R3)2​=∥∇um​∥L2(B)2​,∥vm​∥L2(R3)2​=rm−2​∥um​∥L2(B)2​.​
(49)
Indeed ∇vm​(x)=rm3/2​∇um​(rm​x) inside the expanded ball, and there is no boundary jump because the Sobolev trace is zero.
First take the printed convergence assertion as an assumption, as the question requests. It would imply ∥∇vm​∥2​→∥∇v∥2​>0. The strict inequality follows because an H1(R3) function with zero weak gradient is constant almost everywhere, and the only constant in L2(R3) is zero; this contradicts the asserted positivity of v. If um​ converged strongly in H1(B), its limit would have to be its weak limit zero from (iii), forcing ∥∇um​∥2​→0. The first scaling identity contradicts this. Thus the intended answer is
um​ does not converge strongly in H1(B).​
(50)
There is nevertheless a substantive printing error: the asserted strong convergence of vm​ in the full H1(R3) is false for these positive sources. Here is a check that does not rely on any assertion about the limiting profile. Put Rm​=rm−1​ and c=∣E∣−5/6. Rescaling the weak formulation gives
−Δvm​=cχE​in BRm​​,vm​=0on ∂BRm​​.
(51)
The spherical mean of a Poisson solution outside its source provides an exact obstruction. The total source is M=c∣E∣=∣E∣1/6>0. Let am​(s)=(4π)−1∫S2​vm​(sω)dω be its spherical mean. On 1<s<Rm​ the function is a harmonic function. The radial flux identity, obtained by testing against radial test functions or by the divergence theorem, gives −4πs2am′​(s)=M, while the zero boundary value gives am​(Rm​)=0. Hence
am​(s)=4πM​(s1​−Rm​1​)(1<s<Rm​).
(52)
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on each sphere, followed by spherical coordinates, now yields
∥vm​∥22​​≥4π∫1Rm​​s2am​(s)2ds=4πM2​(3Rm​​−1+Rm​1​−3Rm2​1​)⟶∞.​
(53)
Thus the printed sequence is not even bounded in the full H1 norm. The result on Poisson convergence on expanding balls in a homogeneous Sobolev space gives the appropriate corrected convergence in the homogeneous Sobolev space H˙1(R3), which controls the gradient in L2 and the function in L6, without requiring it to belong to L2.
For completeness, the desired nonconvergence of um​ has a direct proof that needs neither the incorrect claim nor its correction. Choose a nonnegative nonzero test function η∈Cc∞​(E), which exists since E is a domain containing the origin. The rescaled weak formulation and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give
0<c∫E​ηdx=∫R3​∇vm​⋅∇ηdx≤∥∇vm​∥2​∥∇η∥2​.

∥∇um​∥2​=∥∇vm​∥2​≥∥∇η∥2​∣E∣−5/6∫E​ηdx​>0.​
