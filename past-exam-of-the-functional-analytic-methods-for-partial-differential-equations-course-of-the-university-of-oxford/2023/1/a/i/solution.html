Choose a nonnegative mollifier ρ∈Cc∞​(R3) with ∫ρ=1, and put ρε​(x)=ε−3ρ(x/ε). Sobolev approximation by mollifiers in the Sobolev space under consideration states
uε​:=ρε​∗u∈C∞(R3)∩W1,6(R3),∥uε​−u∥W1,6​⟶0(ε↓0).​
(1)
In particular, it gives density of smooth functions in a Sobolev space. We prove convergence of both the function and all its first weak derivatives.
The usual smoothing properties of convolution give uε​∈C∞. For a test function ϕ∈Cc∞​(R3) and i∈{1,2,3}, Fubini's theorem and the definition of a weak derivative give
∫uε​(x)∂i​ϕ(x)dx​=∫ρε​(z)∫u(y)∂i​ϕ(y+z)dydz=−∫ρε​(z)∫∂i​u(y)ϕ(y+z)dydz=−∫(ρε​∗∂i​u)(x)ϕ(x)dx.​
(2)
The integrals are absolutely convergent: the compact supports of ρε​ and ϕ restrict y to a compact set, on which u and ∂i​u are integrable by Holder inequality. Consequently
∂i​uε​=ρε​∗∂i​u.
(3)
The approximate identity theorem in the Lp space L6 applies separately to u and ∂i​u. Thus
∥uε​−u∥6​→0,∥∂i​uε​−∂i​u∥6​→0(i=1,2,3).
(4)
Each convolution belongs to L6 by Young's convolution inequality. With the componentwise Sobolev norm, the sixth power of the required difference is the sum of these four sixth powers, proving the boxed claim. Any equivalent Sobolev norm gives the same convergence.
