Use the continuous representative of uˉ furnished by the one-dimensional Sobolev embedding theorem, and define u∗(x)=uˉ(∣x∣) on A. The one-dimensional Sobolev representative is absolutely continuous; Holder inequality applied to its fundamental theorem of calculus gives
∣uˉ(r)−uˉ(s)∣=​∫sr​uˉ′(t)dt​≤∣r−s∣5/6∥uˉ′∥L6(1,2)​.
(14)
Also ∥uˉ∥∞​≤∥uˉ∥6​+∥uˉ′∥6​: select a point where ∣uˉ∣ is no larger than its L6 norm on the unit-length interval and apply the same fundamental theorem of calculus. Thus the one-dimensional Hölder norm is controlled by ∥uˉ∥W1,6(1,2)​.
The two Hölder seminorms must be compared using the correct distances. By the reverse triangle inequality,
∣x−y∣5/6∣u∗(x)−u∗(y)∣​≤[uˉ]C0,5/6(1,2)​∣x−y∣5/6​∣x∣−∣y∣​5/6​≤[uˉ]C0,5/6(1,2)​.
(15)
Conversely, take x=re and y=se for any fixed unit vector e. Then ∣x−y∣=∣r−s∣, so taking suprema yields equality of the two Hölder seminorms. Their supremum norms are equal as well. Part (i) now gives
∥u∗∥C0,5/6(A)​=∥uˉ∥C0,5/6(1,2)​≤C∥uˉ∥W1,6(1,2)​≤C′∥u∥W1,6(A)​.​
(16)
The representative agrees with u almost everywhere, and the assignment is linear and injective on the radial Sobolev space. This proves the requested continuous embedding. The inner radius being positive makes the radial profile Sobolev norm comparable to its weighted radial version.
