On the unbounded domain R3, translations already prevent compactness in every indicated Hölder space. Choose a bump function η∈Cc∞​(B1​) with η(0)=1, put e1​=(1,0,0), and define
um​(x)=η(x−3me1​),m=1,2,….​
(9)
A change of variables in the Lebesgue integrals defining the Sobolev norm gives ∥um​∥W1,6(R3)​=∥η∥W1,6(R3)​, so the sequence is bounded. Each um​ is a smooth function of compact support and belongs to every Hölder space C0,γ(R3), 0<γ≤1: its bounded gradient gives the Lipschitz estimate for distances at most one, and its bounded values control larger distances.
For m=ℓ, the supports are disjoint, and at x=3me1​ we have um​(x)=1 and uℓ​(x)=0. Therefore
∥um​−uℓ​∥C0,γ​≥∥um​−uℓ​∥∞​≥1(0<γ≤1).
(10)
No subsequence can be a Cauchy sequence in any of these Hölder norms, hence none converges. This uses the translation mechanism for noncompactness of a Sobolev embedding by translation; concentration at the critical Hölder exponent alone would not rule out convergence at every smaller Hölder exponent.
