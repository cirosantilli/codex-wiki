For the earliest diffusive target encounter among independent searchers, with Np​ independent well-mixed searchers in volume V, the approximate first-arrival rate is Np​ka​/V, giving τ3​≃V/(Np​ka​). The target's effective reaction radius includes encounter geometry and is not uniquely determined by its contour length.
A numerical estimate needs temperature, cell volume and a capture-geometry convention, none of which is fixed numerically in the source. State representative estimates T=300K and V=1μm3. The Stokes–Einstein estimate for a sphere of radius 15nm in the stipulated viscosity is

This is an idealized 10−2-second-scale estimate, not a unique geometric prediction. Finite cell shape, crowded motion, accessibility and orientational restrictions can reduce the ideal capture rate.
If nonspecific binding merely sequesters searchers and their free fraction is 0.2, the three-dimensional capture hazard is reduced fivefold: τ≃τ3​/0.2≃0.067s for those estimates. If bound molecules instead search productively by sliding, a free-time fraction alone cannot determine the total arrival time or its sign of change. That is the distinct facilitated-search mechanism treated in c.
