RNA polymerase first recognizes and binds a gene promoter to form a closed complex with the local DNA duplex still paired. Local strand separation then produces a transcription bubble in an open promoter complex, making the template available for RNA synthesis. Recognition, binding, and opening are distinct events; observing binding alone does not demonstrate strand opening.
In magnetic tweezers, a DNA tether joins a fixed surface to a magnetic bead, with attachments that prevent free swivelling. The magnets pull upward and can impose turns; the bead height reports tether extension. For a torsionally constrained tether, linking number is partitioned into twist and writhe, Lk=Tw+Wr. If opening the transcription bubble removes helical twist while Lk is fixed, compensating twist or writhe appears elsewhere. In a supercoiled regime, changes in writhe change the extension, making an opening event visible as a bead-height step.
The sign provides an important control. On a positively supercoiled tether, local unwinding requires more positive compensating writhe and generally shortens the tether. On a negatively supercoiled tether, the same unwinding removes some negative writhe and generally lengthens it. A bending or compaction contribution can have the same sign in both cases, whereas the torsional contribution reverses. Comparing signs, together with an extension-versus-turns response measured for the tether, distinguishes local unwinding from mere binding-induced bending. These claims assume the tether remains torsionally constrained and lies in the calibrated supercoiled regime, rather than an arbitrary stretched-DNA regime.
