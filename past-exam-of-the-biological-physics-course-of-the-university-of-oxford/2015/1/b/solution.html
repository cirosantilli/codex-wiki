With zero initial messenger RNA and protein concentration, solving the two linear ordinary differential equations in sequence gives
XP,∗​EXP​(t)​=1−αR​−αP​αR​e−αP​t−αP​e−αR​t​≃1−e−αP​t.
(2)
The last expression applies after the short messenger RNA transient when αR​≫αP​. All three mean curves have this shape. At a fixed steady state, a larger translational burst size requires fewer transcription events: βR​=αP​XP,∗​/b. Thus b=400 gives infrequent large upward jumps followed by protein degradation, whereas b=3 gives many smaller jumps and a smoother trace.
Noise depends on molecule counts. If the fixed cell volume is V, write Nˉ=VXP,∗​. In the instantaneous-burst limit, independent Poisson process arrivals of geometrically distributed bursts, with count arrival rate λ=VβR​, and unit protein degradation give
varN=Nˉ(1+b),CV=VXP,∗​1+b​​.​
(3)
Indeed, the moment equations are dEN/dt=λb−αP​EN and dvarN/dt=λb(1+2b)+αP​EN−2αP​varN. With zero initial counts the variance becomes Nˉ[(1+b)−e−αP​t−be−2αP​t]. These equations explain both the approach to the steady state and the scatter about it.
For illustration only, V=1μm3 gives Nˉ=104 at the higher concentration: the two values of the coefficient of variation are 0.020 and 0.200. At the lower concentration, Nˉ=100 and b=3 gives 0.200. Decreasing the concentration by a factor of 100 therefore increases relative fluctuations tenfold, despite keeping small bursts. Absolute fluctuation sizes cannot be inferred without a cell volume. The original sketches below stipulate that volume; they are model realizations, not source data.[ref]
