Use the logic approximation for gene regulation: production is either β or zero, and every species has linear removal rate α. Put C=β/α and assume zero basal expression, inactive X before time zero, and all required thresholds strictly between 0 and C. The input is u(t)=1 for 0<t<T and zero otherwise. The common ordinary differential equation is c˙=βg−αc, with Boolean production gate g. Both networks therefore have
Y(t)=C(1−e−αt)(0<t<T),Y(t)=Ce−α(t−T)(t>T),
(9)
where the latter uses the stipulated attained steady state. Define the threshold-crossing delay
d(K)=−α−1ln(1−K/C),tr​=d(KYZ​),t1​=d(KYY1​).
(10)
Because KYY1​>KYZ​, t1​>tr​. In both networks, Y1​ stays zero until t1​ and then follows Y1​=C[1−e−α(t−t1​)] until the OFF response. Write KA​ for the activation threshold of Y1​ on Z in diagram (a), and KI​ for the level of Y1​ that blocks Y's repression in diagram (b); these thresholds were not supplied numerically.
Diagram (a). All three inputs X, Y and Y1​ must permit Z production. Since Y has already crossed its Z threshold before Y1​ starts, the final permission arrives at
ta​=t1​+d(KA​).
(11)
Thus Z=0 until ta​ and Z=C[1−e−α(t−ta​)] subsequently. Solving Z=C/2 gives
tZ,1/2​=d(KYY1​)+d(KA​)+αln2​​.
(12)
For the frequently adopted but extra convention that both required thresholds are C/2, this is 3ln2/α. At the OFF step, X's direct AND input disappears, so Z=Ce−α(t−T) immediately. Y also decays immediately. Y1​ remains at C while Y remains above KYY1​; it starts decaying at T+α−1ln(C/KYY1​). This is a coherent feed-forward loop persistence response: activation is delayed, while removal of the direct required input switches production off promptly.
Diagram (b). The production gate is gZ​=u[¬(Y>KYZ​)∨(Y1​>KI​)]. Z initially rises, Y switches Z production off at tr​, and Y1​ restores production at tb​=t1​+d(KI​). The continuous solution during the ON phase is
Z(t)=⎩⎨⎧​C(1−e−αt),C(1−e−αtr​)e−α(t−tr​),C+[Z(tb​)−C]e−α(t−tb​),​0<t<tr​,tr​<t<tb​,tb​<t<T.​
(13)
The derivative changes from positive to negative at tr​, and from negative to positive at tb​. Consequently the first temporary minimum following the initial pulse occurs at
tmin​=d(KYY1​)+d(KI​)​.
(14)
Its value is Z(tb​)=KYZ​e−α(tb​−tr​). At the OFF step Z again decays immediately because X is required for production, with Y and Y1​ behaving as above. If a required threshold is unattainable, these late switching events do not occur; equal production and removal rates alone do not fix threshold values.
The plotted thresholds are explicitly illustrative, with KYZ​/C=0.25, KYY1​/C=0.55 and KA​/C=KI​/C=0.45.
