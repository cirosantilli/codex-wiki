Identifying the fitted limiting extension with the polymer contour length gives
N≃0.64nm500nm​=781.25≃7.8×102​.
(41)
These are statistical links of the freely jointed chain, not necessarily chemical polymer repeat units. A measured maximum just before detachment need not equal the true contour length exactly, so the identification is itself an estimate.
The stated temperature request mixes two distinct quantities. At unchanged N and b, the true freely jointed chain maximum is geometric:
rmax​(100∘C)=Nb≃500nm​.
(42)
In contrast, the low-force relation ⟨z⟩=Nb2f/(3kB​T) predicts a ratio at the same small force, not a new maximum:
⟨z⟩298.15​⟨z⟩373.15​​=373.15298.15​≃0.799​.
(43)
Formally multiplying 500nm by this ratio gives about 400nm, but 500nm=Nb is outside the small-extension domain, so that is not a justified maximum-extension prediction. A concrete internally consistent counterexample uses the same chain at f=0.10pN: the low-force extensions are about 2.59nm and 2.07nm, while both contour lengths remain 500nm. Any temperature dependence of the segment length, solvent interactions, attachment strength or rupture force needs information absent from the model.
