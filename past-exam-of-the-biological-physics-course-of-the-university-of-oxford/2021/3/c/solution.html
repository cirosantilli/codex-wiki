Correction of the printed limits: ordinary positive polymer contour length requires integration from Rin​ to Rout​, because Rout​>Rin​ and the ring count is positive. For example, taking n(R)=1, Rout​=2 and Rin​=1 makes the printed outward-to-inward integral negative. Thus its sign cannot be retained as a physical length.
For hexagonally packed strands choose axial ring spacing s and radial layer spacing 3​s/2. With n=h/s and radial layer density 2/(3​s),
L=3​s2​∫Rin​Rout​​2πRsh​dR=3​s22πh​(Rout2​−Rin2​).
(17)
The same continuum replacement in b gives
ΔGelastic​=3​s22πlp​kB​Th​lnRin​Rout​​,Rin2​=Rout2​−2πh3​s2L​.
(18)
Eliminating the inner radius yields
ΔGelastic​=3​s2πlp​kB​Th​ln(Rout2​−3​s2L/(2πh)Rout2​​).​
(19)
The resisting packaging force is the marginal free energy per added polymer contour length, at fixed h, s and Rout​:
FL​=(∂L∂ΔGelastic​​)h,s,Rout​​=2Rin2​lp​kB​T​=2(Rout2​−3​s2L/(2πh))lp​kB​T​.​
(20)
The force opposing insertion is positive; its contribution to outward ejection has the same magnitude. The domain is 0≤L<2πhRout2​/(3​s2). The divergence as the inner hole closes reflects the singular curvature of ideal rings and marks breakdown of the continuum model. If spacing also relaxes, the full free energy must include interstrand interactions before optimization; simply varying s in this bending-only formula is insufficient.
