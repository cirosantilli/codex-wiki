Use the logic approximation for gene regulation, zero basal production and initially vanishing X, Y and Z. Let K=Kxy​=Kxz​=Kyz​=Kxx​/2, Y∞​=Z∞​=βy​/α, and assume the activated Q level exceeds the X activation threshold. The circuit combines a coherent feed-forward loop with negative autoregulation. While X is below its self-repression threshold it obeys X˙=βx​−αX, hence X first crosses K at
tX​=−α1​ln(1−βx​αK​).
(5)
Y production begins then. While X remains above K, Y=Y∞​[1−e−α(t−tX​)]. The AND gate for Z first becomes active when Y reaches K, so
Tmin​=tX​−α1​ln(1−βy​αK​)≃−α1​ln(1−βy​αK​).​
(6)
The approximation drops the short X build-up time because βx​≫βy​. Strictly the input must last longer than this onset time to produce a nonzero amount of Z. This requires βy​/α>K: at equality the threshold is approached only asymptotically, and below it Z never switches on. No numerical delay follows without the threshold-to-production ratio.
In the strong negative autoregulation limit, X is held close to Kxx​=2K; a smooth repression law regularizes the rapid switching of the ideal Boolean rule. After the input disappears at t0​, production of X stops and X≃2Ke−α(t−t0​). It takes (ln2)/α for X to fall below K. Until then Y remains at Y∞​>K and the Z AND gate stays on, so Z stays at Z∞​. After that Z decays by dilution, taking another (ln2)/α to halve. Therefore
tZ,1/2​−t0​≃α2ln2​.​
(7)
More generally replace the first ln2 by ln[X(t0​)/K]. This distinguishes disappearance of the external ligand from disappearance of the persistent X transcription factor.
