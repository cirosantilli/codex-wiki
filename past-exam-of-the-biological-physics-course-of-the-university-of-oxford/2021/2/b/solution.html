Put M=[Em​], r=VR​[R] and b=VB​[B]. Saturated CheR supplies a constant receptor methylation flux, while CheB removes it with Michaelis-Menten kinetics:
M˙=r−KB​+MbM​.
(8)
At a finite steady state, r(KB​+M0​)=bM0​, giving
M0​=VB​[B]−VR​[R]KB​VR​[R]​=b−rKB​r​.​
(9)
It requires 0<r<b and M0​ below the total available receptor pool. The steady state is stable because dM˙/dM=−bKB​/(KB​+M)2<0. If r≥b, the ideal equation has no finite positive steady state; actual methylatable sites would become exhausted.
Attractant binding reduces receptor signalling, reducing CheA-dependent phosphorylation of CheY and initially reducing tumbling frequency, so smooth runs become longer. A spatially uniform stimulus provides no preferred direction. Perfect adaptation means that after this transient the tumbling frequency returns exactly to its pre-step value despite continued attractant presence.
The equation above alone does not specify attractant occupancy or its influence on activity. If binding simply changes activity from M to fM with 0<f<1, without changing either modifying flux, M remains M0​ and the output remains fM0​: there is no adaptation. Thus the usual fall followed by recovery requires the additional feedback mechanism developed in c or d, rather than following from the stated Michaelis-Menten kinetics alone.
