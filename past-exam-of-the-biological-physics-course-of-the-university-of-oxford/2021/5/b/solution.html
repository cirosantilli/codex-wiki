Use the supplied −50kJmol−1 as a standard transformed Gibbs free energy of ATP hydrolysis, rather than replacing it with a different commonly quoted standard value. Approximate thermodynamic activities by concentrations divided by c∘=1M, at an explicitly assumed T=300K. The dimensionless reaction quotient is
Qh​=[ATP]/c∘([ADP]/c∘)([Pi​]/c∘)​=0.0016(0.0008)(0.010)​=0.005.
(33)
Consequently
ΔGh​=ΔGh∘​+RTlnQh​=−50.0−13.22=−63.22kJmol−1,
(34)
and the reverse synthesis cost is gATP​=63.22kJmol−1. Substituting bare millimolar numbers in this quotient without the standard state would give the wrong logarithm.
For outside-to-inside proton movement, the membrane potential is Δψ=−0.125V and pHin​−pHout​=0.5, so [H+]in​/[H+]out​=10−0.5. The molar electrochemical potential change is
Δμ​H​=FΔψ+RTln(10−0.5)=−F(0.125+F2.303RT​0.5).
(35)
Thus the available proton-motive force magnitude is 0.15476V, supplying 14.93kJmol−1 per transported proton. Assuming one proton per c-subunit per revolution, three ATP per revolution and no leakage, slippage or transport overhead,
ΔGturn​=3gATP​−nc​FΔp,nc​≥FΔp3gATP​​=12.70.​
(36)
The smallest integer permitting synthesis is therefore nc​=13​ in this ideal budget. A ring of thirteen gives only a small driving margin; inefficiency or sustained finite output could require a larger ring. The result is an energetic lower bound and near-reversible estimate, not a unique structural determination from these data. Reasonable temperatures near 300–310K leave the minimum integer thirteen. Additional membrane transport costs have been excluded explicitly.
