Put h=K/S, require 0<h<1, and use the dimensionless time s=αt. While X is produced, X/S=1−e−s; afterwards X/S=(1−e−αT)e−α(t−T). X crosses the activation threshold on its rising branch at
ta​=α−ln(1−h)​.
(19)
Symmetry keeps Y=Z. They initially receive production through X alone and need another interval ta​ of active input to reach K. If this happens while X is still above threshold, their mutual OR activation sustains production permanently. X remains above K until
tb​=T+α1​lnh1−e−αT​=α1​lnheαT−1​.
(20)
The persistence correction to a gene-circuit pulse threshold uses the limiting latch condition is tb​=2ta​, yielding
Tmin​=α1​ln(1+(1−h)2h​).​
(21)
For T>Tmin​, Y and Z cross their common threshold and approach S even after X decays away. For T<Tmin​ they either remain zero or make a subthreshold pulse and return to zero. Exact equality is sensitive to the convention for the ideal threshold, so robust switching requires a strict excess. At h=1/2, Tmin​=ln3/α, whereas Y and Z cross at 2ln2/α in absolute time.
The shorter required production duration reflects X's persistence after production stops. Requiring T≥2ta​ would incorrectly discard that tail when all activating ligands remain present. If K≥S, no finite pulse reaches the required feedback-active state. The second panel of the figure in solution contrasts pulses shorter and longer than ln3/α.
