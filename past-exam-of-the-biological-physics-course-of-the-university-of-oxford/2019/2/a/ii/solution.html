The intracellular recording by Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley shows the membrane voltage rising from a negative resting value to a positive overshoot, with the outside held at zero reference. The action potential therefore does more than transiently abolish a resting polarization: it reverses the sign across the membrane. A simple hypothesis that excitation only makes an otherwise potassium-dominated membrane equally leaky cannot explain that positive overshoot.
The result helped motivate a transient, selective change in membrane permeability, later resolved into rapid inward sodium current followed by outward potassium current. The positive sodium equilibrium potential calculated above makes the overshoot physically plausible, while potassium conductance favours a negative resting voltage and repolarization. The 1939 voltage trace itself did not identify individual channel proteins or establish the full later voltage-dependent gating mechanism; those conclusions required ionic substitution and voltage-clamp evidence.[ref]
