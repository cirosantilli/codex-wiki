Write B(t) for the probability of state B, so A(t)=1−B(t). Gain minus loss gives
B˙=kf​(1−B)−kb​B=kf​−(kf​+kb​)B.
(27)
With B(0)=0, the solution is
B(t)=kf​+kb​kf​​[1−e−(kf​+kb​)t].​
(28)
It rises monotonically from zero, initially with slope kf​, towards Beq​=kf​/(kf​+kb​) on time scale (kf​+kb​)−1. The first panel of the original figure below shows this shape for illustrative equilibrium probability 0.8.
The other equilibrium probability is Aeq​=kb​/(kf​+kb​), and their ratio is kf​/kb​. Applying detailed balance from part (a) gives
Aeq​Beq​​=e−(GB​−GA​)/(kB​T),
(29)
which is precisely the two-state Boltzmann distribution. It fixes the ratio but not the sum of the rates, so equilibrium measurements alone do not determine the relaxation time.
