The membrane capacitance is C=0.01A=6.346×10−12F. The required net inward electric charge is Q=CΔV=5.394×10−13C, or Q/e≃3.37×106 monovalent ions. If sodium supplies that net electric charge, Faraday constant F=NA​e gives
Δ[Na+]=FVCΔV​=1.780×10−5molL−1=0.0178mM=17.8μM.​
(16)
The final concentration is about 15.0178mM, only 0.119% above the starting value. Neglecting the end faces changes the estimate by 1%, giving about 17.6μM. Over 2ms the capacitive electric charge corresponds to mean net electric current Q/Δt≃0.270nA; the duration does not otherwise enter the total electric charge.
This is a net capacitive estimate, not a measurement of total sodium influx. Simultaneous outward potassium electric current makes actual sodium entry larger, and most bulk ions remain near their original concentrations during one spike. Long-term pumps restore gradients after repeated firing; they are not required to replace the entire intracellular sodium pool during the two-millisecond voltage rise.
