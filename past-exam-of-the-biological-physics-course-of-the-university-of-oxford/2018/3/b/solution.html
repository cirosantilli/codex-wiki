selective permeability sets the driving-force term. Potassium and sodium channels provide narrow coordinating pathways whose free energy balance of hydration and chemical binding favours the appropriate ion. voltage-gated potassium channel carbonyl coordination differs from the charged filter environment in canonical sodium channels, so selective permeation is not a pore-size ranking. Ion substitution shifts the reversal potential, and selective blockers or filter mutations alter the corresponding electric current. An open channel conducts down the electrochemical gradient; ATP-driven pumps maintain the gradients rather than pushing each ion through the pore.
Voltage-dependent activation sets channel availability. Charged residues in the S4-containing voltage-sensing domains move in the membrane electric field and couple to pore opening. A classical voltage-gated potassium channel is a tetramer; a voltage-gated sodium channel pore-forming polypeptide has four homologous domains. ion-channel gating current measurements and sensor-residue mutations connect electric charge motion to opening, while structures separate the sensor from the permeation pathway. The historical n4 and m3 variables describe kinetics phenomenologically and need not map one-to-one onto individual resolved structural movements.[ref][ref]
Time dependence, especially ion-channel inactivation, explains the different electric current waveforms. Sodium activation is rapid, but sustained depolarization reduces the availability variable h, so the sodium electric current decays despite maintained driving voltage. Fast sodium ion-channel inactivation involves intracellular machinery including the interdomain III–IV region. Potassium activation is slower, supplying delayed outward electric current; the classical delayed rectifier is relatively sustained on this time scale. Some voltage-gated potassium channel variants do show rapid N-terminal pore-blocking ion-channel inactivation or slower filter-associated ion-channel inactivation, which should not be assumed identical to sodium's mechanism. voltage clamp steps separate activation from decay; recovery protocols, single-channel recordings and targeted perturbations distinguish gating, ion-channel inactivation and ion conduction. Removal of the Shaker N-terminal blocker and restoration by a free peptide are particularly direct evidence for one voltage-gated potassium channel mechanism.[ref][ref]
