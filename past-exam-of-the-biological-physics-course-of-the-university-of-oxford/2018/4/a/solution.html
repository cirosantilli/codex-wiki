Use the source's explicit graded production rules. X is already present at a constant concentration; the input at zero activates it rather than starting accumulation of X. Assume its active level exceeds both relevant X thresholds, put αx​=αy​=αz​=α, let Ys​=βy​/α, and take 0<h=Kyz​/Ys​<1. Before the input Z is not produced, so use the initial steady value Z(0)=0.
For I1 incoherent feed-forward loop, activation of X starts Y production; for I4 incoherent feed-forward loop it stops Y production. Consequently
Y1​(t)=Ys​(1−e−αt),d1​=−α−1ln(1−h);Y4​(t)=Ys​e−αt,d4​=−α−1lnh.​
(18)
The two delays are the times for Y to cross its output-regulation threshold. In I1 incoherent feed-forward loop, initially absent Y permits the high output rate, then accumulated Y represses it. In I4 incoherent feed-forward loop, initially abundant Y activates the high output rate, then Y's decay removes that activation. Thus both have production βhigh​ before their respective delay and βlow​ afterwards.
With Zs​=βlow​/α and F=βhigh​/βlow​, their common piecewise response, with d=d1​ or d4​, is
Zs​Z(t)​={F(1−e−αt),1+[F(1−e−αd)−1]e−α(t−d),​0≤t≤d,t≥d.​​
(19)
The output is continuous at the production switch. There is an overshoot precisely when F(1−e−αd)>1; otherwise it rises monotonically to Zs​. For a threshold not extremely close to its limiting values and the supplied F≫1, a rapid pulse followed by relaxation to a lower nonzero level is expected. This can provide fast initial response or a transient strong signal, but it is not perfect adaptation to the original zero output.
The original plots below use the illustrative values h=1/4 and F=10. At h=1/2 the two Y curves are complementary and their output delays and Z curves are identical. These threshold values are examples, not extra source data. “AND” here must be read with the stipulated graded rates: replacing them by an ordinary all-or-nothing AND rule would give a different response.
