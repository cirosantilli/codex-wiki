At the final steady state, the ligands control activity as well as the amount of each factor. Assuming active X exceeds its thresholds and the high Y level exceeds Kyz​, the normalized output production rates are
Sx​0011​Sy​0101​I1 output rate00βhigh​βlow​​I4 output rate00βlow​βlow​​​
(22)
For I1 incoherent feed-forward loop, active X makes Y abundant, but Y can repress Z only if its own activating ligand is present. Without Sy​, Y is inactive and the output remains high. For I4 incoherent feed-forward loop, active X represses Y to below its threshold, so the output is low regardless of Sy​. If X is inactive, the direct permissive arm is absent and both outputs are zero even when Y is active.
Thus I1 incoherent feed-forward loop can retain two-input combinatorial control at steady state as well as generate the transient in part (a). I4 incoherent feed-forward loop loses steady-state sensitivity to Sy​ once X has suppressed Y, under these particular assumptions. That gives a functional reason the first motif may be more useful in many regulatory contexts. It is not a proof of an evolutionary frequency: promoter implementation, thresholds, noise, costs and other functions also influence how common a motif is. The argument follows the supplied rate table, including its nonzero βlow​, rather than a generic Boolean AND truth table.
