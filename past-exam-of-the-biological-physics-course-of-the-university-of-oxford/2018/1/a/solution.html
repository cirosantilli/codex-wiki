For the driven two-state rotary motor, use positive angular velocity ω>0 and extend all angular functions with period 2π. In the first half-turn the source figure has kAB​=k0​, kBA​=0, so the stationary equation is ωPA′​=−k0​PA​. In the second half-turn the rates are interchanged, giving ωPA′​=k0​(1−PA​). Hence
PA​(θ)={αe−βθ,1−Ce−β(θ−π),​0≤θ≤π,π≤θ≤2π,​β=ωk0​​.
(1)
Continuity at π gives 1−C=αe−βπ, while periodicity gives α=1−Ce−βπ. Solving the two linear conditions gives C=α and
α=1+e−πk0​/ω1​,β=ωk0​​,PA​(θ+π)=1−PA​(θ),PB​=1−PA​.​
(2)
In particular PA​(0)=PA​(2π)=α and PA​(π)=1−α, so the two pieces fill in the apparent endpoint gap in the printed piecewise formula. The probability decreases exponentially during the detachment half-cycle and rises exponentially during the attachment half-cycle; PB​ is its complement. Both remain between zero and one. They are state probabilities conditional on rotor angle, not separate angular densities normalized individually to one.
For ω≪k0​, attachment and detachment almost finish within their allotted half-turns: PA​ is nearly zero through most of 0<θ<π and nearly one through most of π<θ<2π, with angular boundary layers of width ω/k0​. For ω≫k0​, it stays near one half with a small nearly triangular modulation. The upper panels of the original figure in part (b) sketch both limits. At exactly zero speed the advective stationary problem is singular; these statements concern the positive-speed limit.
