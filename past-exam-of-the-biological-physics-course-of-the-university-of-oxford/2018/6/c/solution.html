A stable native conformation is favoured when its Gibbs free energy, including the protein and its surrounding solvent, is lower than that of competing conformational ensembles under the specified conditions:
ΔGfold​=ΔHfold​−TΔStotal​<0.​
(39)
Folding greatly reduces the chain's conformational entropy, an unfavourable contribution. It can nevertheless gain favourable hydrogen-bonding and packing interactions, van der Waals contacts, screened electrostatic interactions and solvent free energy. In aqueous globular proteins, burying nonpolar surface reduces the cost of exposing it to water; the hydrophobic effect is not an extra covalent attraction between all hydrophobic residues. Hydrogen bonds and ionic contacts must be judged relative to the interactions those groups made with solvent before folding, so merely counting bonds in the folded structure does not determine stability.
The protein primary structure determines which contacts and protein secondary structures can cooperate. A free-energy landscape biased towards compatible contacts permits many routes towards the native ensemble, rather than an exhaustive random search through every conformation. Folding funnels describe this bias, while barriers explain metastability and folding rates. Reversible denaturation/refolding experiments show that some proteins can recover native structure and activity when suitable conditions are restored, supporting a sequence-encoded thermodynamic tendency without asserting that every protein folds unaided.
“Stable” means an equilibrium preference and a useful lifetime, not one perfectly rigid shape. Native proteins fluctuate and can have several functional conformations; the unfolded ensemble remains populated with relative weight depending on its full free energy. Changes of temperature, pH, salt, solvent, ligands or pressure alter the balance, and molecular chaperones can prevent protein aggregation or assist kinetic access without supplying a different primary sequence. Membrane proteins require their appropriate environment, and intrinsically disordered proteins need not possess one stable folded state in isolation.
Thus a released stretched chain can gain configurational entropy on first recoiling yet later favour a more ordered native structure because the total free energy balance, including solvent, is different. Equilibrium stability, barriers to unfolding and resistance to irreversible protein aggregation are distinct properties. The existence of a native minimum alone does not guarantee rapid, reversible folding under every experimental condition.
