For the driven two-state rotary motor, use positive angular velocity ω>0 and extend all angular functions with period 2π. In the first half-turn the source figure has kAB​=k0​, kBA​=0, so the stationary equation is ωPA′​=−k0​PA​. In the second half-turn the rates are interchanged, giving ωPA′​=k0​(1−PA​). Hence
PA​(θ)={αe−βθ,1−Ce−β(θ−π),​0≤θ≤π,π≤θ≤2π,​β=ωk0​​.
(1)
Continuity at π gives 1−C=αe−βπ, while periodicity gives α=1−Ce−βπ. Solving the two linear conditions gives C=α and
α=1+e−πk0​/ω1​,β=ωk0​​,PA​(θ+π)=1−PA​(θ),PB​=1−PA​.​
(2)
In particular PA​(0)=PA​(2π)=α and PA​(π)=1−α, so the two pieces fill in the apparent endpoint gap in the printed piecewise formula. The probability decreases exponentially during the detachment half-cycle and rises exponentially during the attachment half-cycle; PB​ is its complement. Both remain between zero and one. They are state probabilities conditional on rotor angle, not separate angular densities normalized individually to one.
For ω≪k0​, attachment and detachment almost finish within their allotted half-turns: PA​ is nearly zero through most of 0<θ<π and nearly one through most of π<θ<2π, with angular boundary layers of width ω/k0​. For ω≫k0​, it stays near one half with a small nearly triangular modulation. The upper panels of the original figure in part (b) sketch both limits. At exactly zero speed the advective stationary problem is singular; these statements concern the positive-speed limit.

The source potential rises from zero to U0​ over the half-turn from s to s+π, then falls over the next half-turn. Its slopes are ±U0​/π. Torque is minus the angular derivative of potential; in the detached state UB​ is angle-independent and exerts no torque. A uniform set of motor phases therefore gives
T0​=πU0​​,T(s)=2πT0​​[−∫ss+π​PA​(θ)dθ+∫s+πs+2π​PA​(θ)dθ].​
(3)
Here U0​ is an energy and T0​ a torque because the angle is in radians. The average over a full turn divides by 2π, rather than by the bound-state occupation alone.
Differentiating the moving integration limits and using periodicity gives
dsdT​=πT0​​[PA​(s)−PA​(s+π)]=πT0​​[2PA​(s)−1].
(4)
The optimal phase of a two-state rotary motor follows on 0<s<π: PA​ decreases, so the stationary point is a maximum and satisfies
PA​(smax​)=PA​(smax​+π)=21​,smax​=β1​ln(2α)=2π​−β1​lncosh2πβ​.​
(5)
The crossing displaced by π is the corresponding minimum. For slow positive rotation, smax​≃(ω/k0​)ln2 lies just after the detachment switch. The potential then rises mainly while the motor is detached and falls mainly while it is bound. For fast rotation, smax​≃π/2−π2k0​/(8ω), with only a small phase-dependent occupation available to produce torque. The potential should still be shifted by this optimum, rather than aligned arbitrarily with a rate switch.

Sustained work requires the motor's chemical/ion-gradient energy supply, not extraction of work from a passive single thermal bath.

Initially folded structure can resist extension, then contacts break and more polypeptide polymer contour length becomes available. On releasing the tensile load, the extended chain first retracts towards less aligned conformations because they have much greater configurational multiplicity. Viscous friction and internal relaxation determine the time course; stored free energy is dissipated as heat. An unconstrained ideal chain does not shrink to a point: its mean vector is zero but its root mean square end-to-end length is bN​≃2.37nm for the supplied ideal-chain parameters.
A real polypeptide also has sequence-dependent interactions. If the original native structure is the stable state in the prevailing solvent and the molecule remains intact and capable of folding, it can subsequently refold, gaining favourable intramolecular and solvent free energy. The folded state is not selected by chain entropy alone, and formation of native contacts need not be instantaneous. Kinetic traps, proline isomerization, protein aggregation, misfolding, surface attachments or chemical damage may prevent complete recovery or produce hysteresis. Thus reversible refolding is a physically motivated possibility, not guaranteed simply by removal of force. This distinction connects the recoil of a stretched polymer to the thermodynamics discussed in Question 6(c).

selective permeability sets the driving-force term. Potassium and sodium channels provide narrow coordinating pathways whose free energy balance of hydration and chemical binding favours the appropriate ion. voltage-gated potassium channel carbonyl coordination differs from the charged filter environment in canonical sodium channels, so selective permeation is not a pore-size ranking. Ion substitution shifts the reversal potential, and selective blockers or filter mutations alter the corresponding electric current. An open channel conducts down the electrochemical gradient; ATP-driven pumps maintain the gradients rather than pushing each ion through the pore.
Voltage-dependent activation sets channel availability. Charged residues in the S4-containing voltage-sensing domains move in the membrane electric field and couple to pore opening. A classical voltage-gated potassium channel is a tetramer; a voltage-gated sodium channel pore-forming polypeptide has four homologous domains. ion-channel gating current measurements and sensor-residue mutations connect electric charge motion to opening, while structures separate the sensor from the permeation pathway. The historical n4 and m3 variables describe kinetics phenomenologically and need not map one-to-one onto individual resolved structural movements.[ref][ref]
Time dependence, especially ion-channel inactivation, explains the different electric current waveforms. Sodium activation is rapid, but sustained depolarization reduces the availability variable h, so the sodium electric current decays despite maintained driving voltage. Fast sodium ion-channel inactivation involves intracellular machinery including the interdomain III–IV region. Potassium activation is slower, supplying delayed outward electric current; the classical delayed rectifier is relatively sustained on this time scale. Some voltage-gated potassium channel variants do show rapid N-terminal pore-blocking ion-channel inactivation or slower filter-associated ion-channel inactivation, which should not be assumed identical to sodium's mechanism. voltage clamp steps separate activation from decay; recovery protocols, single-channel recordings and targeted perturbations distinguish gating, ion-channel inactivation and ion conduction. Removal of the Shaker N-terminal blocker and restoration by a free peptide are particularly direct evidence for one voltage-gated potassium channel mechanism.[ref][ref]
Together these properties produce n4gˉ​K​(V−EK​) and m3hgˉ​Na​(V−ENa​): selectivity fixes reversal potential, maximal electrical conductance fixes scale, and activation/inactivation variables supply voltage- and history-dependent open probability.

The equivalent circuit has membrane capacitance in parallel with electrical conductance–battery branches: voltage-dependent sodium and potassium conductances in series with their reversal potentials, a chloride electrical conductance in series with ECl​, and other leak conductances. For externally supplied electric current,
Cm​V˙=Iext​−gNa​(V,t)(V−ENa​)−gK​(V,t)(V−EK​)−gCl​(V−ECl​)−gL​(V−EL​).

Use the source's explicit graded production rules. X is already present at a constant concentration; the input at zero activates it rather than starting accumulation of X. Assume its active level exceeds both relevant X thresholds, put αx​=αy​=αz​=α, let Ys​=βy​/α, and take 0<h=Kyz​/Ys​<1. Before the input Z is not produced, so use the initial steady value Z(0)=0.
For I1 incoherent feed-forward loop, activation of X starts Y production; for I4 incoherent feed-forward loop it stops Y production. Consequently
Y1​(t)=Ys​(1−e−αt),d1​=−α−1ln(1−h);Y4​(t)=Ys​e−αt,d4​=−α−1lnh.​
(18)
The two delays are the times for Y to cross its output-regulation threshold. In I1 incoherent feed-forward loop, initially absent Y permits the high output rate, then accumulated Y represses it. In I4 incoherent feed-forward loop, initially abundant Y activates the high output rate, then Y's decay removes that activation. Thus both have production βhigh​ before their respective delay and βlow​ afterwards.
With Zs​=βlow​/α and F=βhigh​/βlow​, their common piecewise response, with d=d1​ or d4​, is
Zs​Z(t)​={F(1−e−αt),1+[F(1−e−αd)−1]e−α(t−d),​0≤t≤d,t≥d.​​
(19)
The output is continuous at the production switch. There is an overshoot precisely when F(1−e−αd)>1; otherwise it rises monotonically to Zs​. For a threshold not extremely close to its limiting values and the supplied F≫1, a rapid pulse followed by relaxation to a lower nonzero level is expected. This can provide fast initial response or a transient strong signal, but it is not perfect adaptation to the original zero output.
The original plots below use the illustrative values h=1/4 and F=10. At h=1/2 the two Y curves are complementary and their output delays and Z curves are identical. These threshold values are examples, not extra source data. “AND” here must be read with the stipulated graded rates: replacing them by an ordinary all-or-nothing AND rule would give a different response.

Use Y and Z as the variables appearing in the equations; the introductory reference to X and Y is a naming mismatch. With γ>0, n>0, a symmetric equilibrium has Y=Z=x and
x(1+xn)=γ.​
(23)
The left side increases continuously from zero to infinity because its derivative is 1+(n+1)xn>0. Thus there is exactly one positive symmetric solution for every allowed parameter pair, which verifies the implicit steady-state expression.
For γ≫1 and n≫1, look for one high component and one low component. If Y∼γ, the other equation gives Z=γ/(1+Yn)∼γ1−n≪1; that small value in turn gives Y=γ/(1+Zn)∼γ. Exchanging the components gives the alternative state:
(Y,Z)∼(γ,γ1−n)and(γ1−n,γ).​
(24)
For example the relative correction to the high value is of order γ−n(n−1), while the low value first has the ordinary denominator correction of relative order γ−n. The sharply repressive Hill function is therefore consistent with two alternatives, one factor high and the other low, which can encode a switch. Mutual repression makes a positive feedback loop: a high Y suppresses Z and thereby relieves repression of Y. Existence of alternative equilibria alone does not establish their stability; the explicit quadratic-Hill case in part (e) also permits that check.

In a bacterial transcription complex, RNA polymerase binds a promoter and locally separates the DNA strands into a transcription bubble. The template enters the active site in the 3′ to 5′ direction, while the complementary RNA grows 5′ to 3′. The displaced non-template strand passes around the bubble, downstream DNA re-forms a duplex, and the nascent RNA exits through a separate channel. A short RNA–DNA hybrid is maintained in an elongating complex; the first few nucleotides of an initiating complex do not yet make a full elongation-length hybrid.
The core enzyme has subunit composition α2​ββ′ω; association with a sigma factor forms a promoter-selective holoenzyme. Sigma recognizes promoter features and assists steps leading from a closed bound complex to the open complex. Different sigma factors redirect transcription towards different promoter classes. The familiar −10 and −35 elements are examples for a major bacterial sigma family, not a universal sequence rule for all sigma factors. Promoter escape changes these contacts, but complete sigma dissociation at one exact transcript length should not be assumed for every complex.
For the supplied non-template sequence, assuming its first base is the first transcribed position, the complementary template runs 3′-TCAGTAAGC-5′. The RNA has the coding-strand order with uracil replacing thymine. Therefore the first five nucleotides are
5′-AGUCA-3′.​

q=(s−1)2κ[sN+1−(N+1)s+N]​(s=1).​
(32)
The apparent singularity is removable: at s=1, the original sum gives q=κN(N+1)/2, which is 45κ for these nine bases.
At s≫1, the longest block dominates:
q≃κsN−1(1+s2​+s23​+⋯),qN=9​≃κs8.​
(33)
Conditional on at least one pair, the fully paired probability approaches one, with leading correction of order 2/s. If a fully unpaired reference state of weight one is included, the full partition function is Q=1+q, so a large propagation constant alone does not guarantee predominant duplex if κsN−1 is still very small.
The given sequence contains five A–T and four G–C pairs when fully complementary. Real duplex stability also depends strongly on stacking, sequence context, salt and temperature; it cannot be calculated just by counting two versus three hydrogen bonds. All else comparable, sequences with greater duplex propagation stability resist bubble opening more strongly, while easier-to-open, often A–T-rich start-site regions facilitate initiation. The homogeneous-s zipper demonstrates that principle but does not resolve a quantitative sequence-specific melting profile or polymerase's active stabilization of the open state.

PALM separates emitters in time. A sparse subset of photoactivatable or photoconvertible labels is switched on so that its diffraction-broadened images can be fitted individually; repeated activation, imaging and localization builds a map from many isolated detections. The positional uncertainty can be much smaller than the point spread function width because many photons estimate one image centre: ideally σloc​∼s/Nγ​​ for image width s and detected photon number Nγ​.
That is fluorescence localization precision, not automatically the structural resolution of the reconstructed cell. Pixelation, background, imperfect optics, drift, label linkage, blinking and labelling density matter. Repeated localizations of one molecule cannot fill unlabelled gaps; a crowded frame with overlapping images cannot simply be treated as many isolated emitters. For a static structural map, the structure must remain sufficiently stable while the many frames are accumulated, or its time dependence must be reconstructed explicitly.
For mobility in living cells, keep activated proteins observable for successive frames and link isolated positions into trajectories before bleaching. Repeating this for successive sparse subsets yields many trajectories without making the entire protein population simultaneously fluorescent. This is the sptPALM strategy.[ref]
For free isotropic two-dimensional diffusion with per-coordinate localization error σloc​ and instantaneous exposures,
⟨∣r(t+Δt)−r(t)∣2⟩=4DΔt+4σloc2​.​
(38)
Fit several lags or a full trajectory likelihood to infer D and its uncertainty. Finite exposure causes motion blur; for a uniform shutter of duration tE​≤Δt, a simple correction is 4D(Δt−tE​/3)+4σloc2​. Confinement, directed motion and switching binding states change the lag dependence. Short tracks, missed detections and false linking in crowded regions can mimic apparent heterogeneity, so mobility classes require explicit measurement-error and tracking controls. A trajectory map recovers dynamics; it is not identical to a static PALM density map.

b OurBigBook.com
The RNA world hypothesis proposes an early stage in which RNA-like molecules carried heritable sequence information and also performed important catalytic functions, before the present division of labour among DNA, RNA and proteins. Complementary base pairing supplies a route to template-directed information copying, while sequence-dependent RNA folding can create catalytic structures. This helps address the apparent circularity of requiring protein enzymes to copy genes whose expression already requires an elaborate protein-synthesis apparatus.
Modern biology supplies evidence for the necessary capacities. Ribozymes catalyse reactions, and self-splicing introns demonstrate RNA-dependent processing without a dedicated protein enzyme for that chemistry. The ribosome's peptidyl-transferase centre is built principally from RNA, with catalytic geometry located in its RNA core; structural work provides direct evidence that this central protein-synthesis chemistry is RNA based.[ref] RNA also remains central as messenger, adaptor and regulatory material. These observations make RNA catalysis and information storage physically plausible rather than assuming that all catalysts must be proteins.
They do not prove a complete historical sequence. A viable early system still needs plausible nucleotide supply and activation, polymerization, template copying with sufficient fidelity, product-strand separation, compatibility of catalytic and copying conditions, an energy source and some means of maintaining useful combinations. RNA's chemical lability and the challenge of separating paired products are constraints, not details supplied automatically by base pairing. The first hereditary chemistry might also have involved related polymers or cooperative RNA–peptide systems rather than an exclusively RNA organism.
The later advantages of DNA as a relatively stable storage medium and proteins as catalysts with chemically varied side chains help explain the present division of labour. The hypothesis is therefore a testable family of evolutionary scenarios supported by molecular capabilities and biological relics, not a direct observation of prebiotic life or proof that an isolated modern RNA already self-replicates autonomously.

A stable native conformation is favoured when its Gibbs free energy, including the protein and its surrounding solvent, is lower than that of competing conformational ensembles under the specified conditions:
ΔGfold​=ΔHfold​−TΔStotal​<0.​
(39)
Folding greatly reduces the chain's conformational entropy, an unfavourable contribution. It can nevertheless gain favourable hydrogen-bonding and packing interactions, van der Waals contacts, screened electrostatic interactions and solvent free energy. In aqueous globular proteins, burying nonpolar surface reduces the cost of exposing it to water; the hydrophobic effect is not an extra covalent attraction between all hydrophobic residues. Hydrogen bonds and ionic contacts must be judged relative to the interactions those groups made with solvent before folding, so merely counting bonds in the folded structure does not determine stability.
The protein primary structure determines which contacts and protein secondary structures can cooperate. A free-energy landscape biased towards compatible contacts permits many routes towards the native ensemble, rather than an exhaustive random search through every conformation. Folding funnels describe this bias, while barriers explain metastability and folding rates. Reversible denaturation/refolding experiments show that some proteins can recover native structure and activity when suitable conditions are restored, supporting a sequence-encoded thermodynamic tendency without asserting that every protein folds unaided.
“Stable” means an equilibrium preference and a useful lifetime, not one perfectly rigid shape. Native proteins fluctuate and can have several functional conformations; the unfolded ensemble remains populated with relative weight depending on its full free energy. Changes of temperature, pH, salt, solvent, ligands or pressure alter the balance, and molecular chaperones can prevent protein aggregation or assist kinetic access without supplying a different primary sequence. Membrane proteins require their appropriate environment, and intrinsically disordered proteins need not possess one stable folded state in isolation.
Thus a released stretched chain can gain configurational entropy on first recoiling yet later favour a more ordered native structure because the total free energy balance, including solvent, is different. Equilibrium stability, barriers to unfolding and resistance to irreversible protein aggregation are distinct properties. The existence of a native minimum alone does not guarantee rapid, reversible folding under every experimental condition.
