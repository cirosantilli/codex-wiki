On each branch the worm-like chain force-extension interpolation relates force to the extension of the already released chain. Fitting a sufficiently long branch estimates its polymer contour length L and, if the data constrain it independently, its persistence length Lp​. In practice Lp​ may be fixed across branches so that the changes in L are better identified. Consecutive fitted polymer contour lengths reveal how much additional chain became available at an protein denaturation event; using a justified polymer contour length per residue can then estimate residue counts. A projected folded length and the pulling geometry must be included before assigning a structural boundary.
Writing u=x/L, expansion at small extension gives
f=Lp​kB​T​(23​u+43​u2+⋯),keff​=dxdf​​0​=2Lp​L3kB​T​.
(20)
Near full extension, f∼kB​T/[4Lp​(1−x/L)2]. Larger L shifts a branch toward larger extension at fixed force. The effective flexural rigidity is κ=kB​TLp​, with units of force times length squared. This is entropic straightening of a worm-like chain; bond stretching and barrier rupture themselves are not included in the formula.
The persistence-length wording requires care. The three-dimensional model gives ⟨t(s)⋅t(0)⟩=e−s/Lp​. At s=Lp​, arccos⟨cosθ⟩=arccos(e−1)=68.4∘. This is not a statement that ⟨θ⟩=68.4∘. For example, angles zero and 90∘ with equal probabilities have mean angle 45∘, but arccos⟨cosθ⟩=60∘. The distinction prevents a false literal interpretation of the printed definition.
