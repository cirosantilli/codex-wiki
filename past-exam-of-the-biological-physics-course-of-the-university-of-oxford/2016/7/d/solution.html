Separate total methylated receptor m from its active portion a=f(c)m, where ligand-bound receptor is inactive and the unbound fraction supplies f(c)>0 at finite ligand concentration. In the Barkai-Leibler model, receptor demethylation depends on the active portion:
m˙=r−KB​+aba​.
(45)
Every accessible stable steady state therefore obeys
a∗​=b−rKB​r​,m∗​=a∗​/f(c).​
(46)
The output is independent of chemoattractant even though the methylated pool changes. A ligand step first lowers a, reducing its own reverse modification. Constant receptor methylation continues until a recovers. Local stability follows from ∂m˙/∂m=−bfKB​/(KB​+a)2<0. This is output-dependent negative feedback. [ref]
Changing CheR or CheB changes a∗​ and the recovery time, but the return to each new pre-step baseline remains exact over the model's operating range. Robust perfect adaptation does not mean invariant tumbling frequency under changes in enzyme abundance. Nor does it extend to f=0, b≤r or saturation of the finite receptor methylation capacity. If every receptor is permanently inactive, a=0 and the reverse flux cannot restore a positive baseline. The literal phrase that binding reduces activity to zero applies to bound receptors, not to every receptor under every finite stimulus.
If CheR instead follows Michaelis-Menten kinetics on unmethylated substrate, u=ET​−m, then
m˙=KR​+ET​−mr0​(ET​−m)​−KB​+aba​,a=f(c)m.
(47)
At steady state the forward flux becomes r0​(ET​−a/f)/(KR​+ET​−a/f) and generally depends on ligand through f. Thus ligand-independent output is lost. Near-perfect adaptation can remain when ET​−m≫KR​ so that CheR is effectively saturated, but Michaelis–Menten behavior outside that limit does not guarantee perfect adaptation. [ref]
