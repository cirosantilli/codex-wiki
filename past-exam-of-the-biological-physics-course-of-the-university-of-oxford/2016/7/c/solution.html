Let chemoattractant reduce the activity per methylated receptor by a factor f, with 0<f≤1, taking the initial factor as one. The active output is a=fm. If VB​ changes to VB′​ but receptor demethylation still acts on total methylated receptor, the new output is
a∗′​=fVB′​[B]−rKB​r​.
(43)
Demanding a∗′​=a∗​=KB​r/(VB​[B]−r) gives
VB′​[B]=fVB​[B]+(1−f)r,VB′​=fVB​+(1−f)[B]VR​[R]​.​
(44)
Reduced receptor demethylation lets methylated receptors accumulate to m∗′​=m∗​/f, compensating exactly for their reduced activity. During the transition the activity first falls, then rises as receptor methylation accumulates. This compensation works only if the required methylated concentration is accessible; in particular m∗​/f<ET​ in a finite receptor pool.
The required relation contains both enzyme concentrations. At fixed rates, increasing CheR raises the baseline receptor methylation and tumbling output, while increasing CheB lowers them. After such a change, the previous fine tuning generally no longer gives perfect adaptation. For example the required reverse capacity is b′=fb+(1−f)r, so changing r without retuning b′ changes the adapted output ratio. It is the precision of adaptation that is fragile here, in addition to the baseline itself changing. Changing all relevant capacities together in a way preserving the relation is a special exception, not generic robustness.
