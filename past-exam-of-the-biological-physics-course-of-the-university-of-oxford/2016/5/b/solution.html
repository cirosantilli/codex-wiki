Use the elastic-rod bending functional for an abstract inextensible worm-like chain,
Gb​=2κ​∫C(s′)2ds′,κ=lp​kB​T.
(22)
A circular ring of radius R has curvature 1/R and length 2πR, hence energy πκ/R. With n(Ri​) rings of each radius, add their energies:
Gb​=πlp​kB​Ti∑​Ri​n(Ri​)​.​
(23)
For a cylindrical annulus of height h, choose neighboring rings separated vertically by s. Then n=h/s, while adjacent radial layers are separated by 3​s/2. Replacing the radial sum by its continuum integral gives
L=3​s2​∫Rin​Rout​​2πRsh​dR=3​s22πh​(Rout2​−Rin2​),
(24)
and
Gb​=3​s22πlp​kB​Th​∫Rin​Rout​​RdR​=3​s22πlp​kB​Th​lnRin​Rout​​.
(25)
Eliminate the inner radius to obtain
Gb​=3​s2πlp​kB​Th​ln[Rout2​−3​Ls2/(2πh)Rout2​​].​
(26)
The dimensions are energy, and the expression is positive for 0<L<2πhRout2​/(3​s2). It tends to zero with L and diverges as the inner hole closes in this continuum model. The divergence signals the growing cost of forcing an inextensible chain to zero radius; it is not a physical prediction at arbitrarily small molecular scales. Interstrand, hydration and configurational contributions have been excluded, and disconnected ideal rings represent local curvature without modeling the connections between them.
