In bacterial transcription initiation, the RNA polymerase holoenzyme uses a promoter-specific sigma factor to recognize promoter elements and form a closed DNA-bound complex. Subsequent local DNA melting opens a transcription bubble, positioning the template strand in the active site: this is an open promoter complex. The enzyme can then initiate RNA synthesis and escape into elongation. LacI, the lac repressor, binds the lac operator overlapping or close to the promoter and can prevent productive promoter binding or its progression to an open complex; operator arrangement and DNA looping can strengthen repression. One should not assume every repressor acts at exactly the same microscopic step.
A high-resolution optical tweezers assay mechanically connects one trapped bead to RNA polymerase and another bead to an upstream or downstream DNA handle. Polymerase motion changes the tether length; measuring bead displacement and the calibrated restoring force reports elongation under a known load. A feedback force clamp can keep that load approximately constant. Reversing the tether geometry permits assisting and opposing loads. The left panel below shows the mechanical arrangement rather than a copied apparatus image.
A base-pair advance is only about 0.34nm along B-form DNA. Resolving that increment requires exceptional mechanical stability, suppression of drift and laser-pointing noise, calibration of trap and tether compliance, and sufficient temporal sampling to separate steps from thermal bead fluctuations. Position histograms, dwell detection and their spatial correlations can expose a repeated increment; one should also test nucleotide-dependent motion, pauses and backtracking. Stabilized dual-trap measurements combined with transcription-product analysis established approximately one base-pair advance per incorporated nucleotide.[ref]

∇2c=0,c(r0​)=0,c(∞)=CA​,c(r)=CA​(1−rr0​​).

J=4πr02​DA​drdc​​r0​​=4πDA​r0​CA​.
(2)

The term V/(D3​λ) is the bulk-diffusion search cost: increasing the effective length of DNA searched after each encounter enlarges the useful capture region and reduces this contribution. The term Lλ/D1​ is the accumulated sliding cost. Approximately L/λ DNA landing-and-sliding episodes, each taking order λ2/D1​, give Lλ/D1​. These are scaling estimates; geometric constants and correlated revisits are omitted by the supplied model.
Thus the facilitated target search is slow as λ→0, because too little sequence is examined per encounter, and slow at very large λ, because sliding and repeated local exploration take too long. A finite intermediate minimum balances 1D scanning against 3D relocation. The right panel of the figure in solution shows the two contributions and their sum. The model requires a useful range of sliding lengths below the full chromosome length, finite positive diffusion coefficients and effective relocation between episodes; it is not a claim that arbitrarily large λ is physically available.

Use a short inducer pulse to release DNA-bound fluorescent LacI, then remove extracellular IPTG rapidly and start the search clock after washout. The supplied entry, binding and dissociation times imply a synchronization uncertainty of order a few tenths of a second, which must be measured and separated from the search latency. If rebinding is faster than that dead time, the protocol alone cannot resolve the true mean without a kinetic correction or faster triggering.
Image individual cells rapidly enough to distinguish freely diffusing proteins from a persistent immobilized fluorescent spot at the single operator. Prelocate that locus from an independent position marker or a previous binding event. Score the first specific capture after each reset; controls lacking a functional target distinguish stable operator binding from nonspecific DNA residence. Correct for localization error, bleaching, blinking, missed brief events and target motion, and verify that the fluorescent tag does not substantially change the search kinetics.
With four independent freely searching molecules, each having an approximately exponential single-molecule target-arrival time of mean τ1​, the first-arrival survival is
S(t)=e−4t/τ1​,τ1​=4⟨tfirst​⟩.​
(10)
Thus report both the observed first capture among four and the inferred mean for one searcher. If the number of visible available molecules varies, use S(t)=exp[−∫0t​n(s)ds/τ1​] and account for censoring rather than blindly multiplying by four. Repeated resets and many cells estimate the distribution; deviations from an exponential test the independent well-mixed approximation. The rapid inducer manipulation defines a synchronization strategy, not an assumption that all subsequent intracellular search paths are identical.

The intracellular recording by Alan Hodgkin and Andrew Huxley shows the membrane voltage rising from a negative resting value to a positive overshoot, with the outside held at zero reference. The action potential therefore does more than transiently abolish a resting polarization: it reverses the sign across the membrane. A simple hypothesis that excitation only makes an otherwise potassium-dominated membrane equally leaky cannot explain that positive overshoot.
The result helped motivate a transient, selective change in membrane permeability, later resolved into rapid inward sodium current followed by outward potassium current. The positive sodium equilibrium potential calculated above makes the overshoot physically plausible, while potassium conductance favours a negative resting voltage and repolarization. The 1939 voltage trace itself did not identify individual channel proteins or establish the full later voltage-dependent gating mechanism; those conclusions required ionic substitution and voltage-clamp evidence.[ref]

Panel B steps the membrane from approximately −80 to −40mV and holds it there. The sodium current becomes inward, peaks rapidly, and decays almost to zero despite maintained depolarization. At −40mV the sodium driving force V−ENa​ is negative, consistent with the negative current convention. A conductance description is
INa​=NγpO​(V−ENa​),
(13)
where N is channel number, γ single-channel conductance and pO​ the open probability. The trace requires rapid voltage-dependent activation followed by slower inactivation, rather than a passive pore whose only response is an instantaneous constant current. Recovery normally requires repolarization, helping produce a refractory period and directional propagation of excitation.
A typical eukaryotic voltage-gated sodium channel contains four homologous membrane-spanning domains in one large pore-forming protein. Charged S4 helices sense voltage; pore-associated regions form a selective pathway and activation gate. Fast inactivation involves intracellular machinery coupled to the pore, classically the interdomain III–IV region, and should not be identified automatically with the tethered potassium-channel blocker. The pore permits rapid downhill electrochemical transport while maintaining selectivity against other ions.
Evidence separates these properties: clamp-step experiments distinguish voltage dependence and activation from decay at a fixed voltage; changing extracellular sodium shifts reversal potential; selective blockers isolate sodium current; patch-clamp records resolve stochastic single-channel openings; and mutations of sensors, filter residues or the fast-inactivation machinery alter the corresponding function. A single macroscopic trace alone cannot uniquely determine all microscopic rate constants.

dsdx​=H(h−z)−x,dsdy​=H(h−x)−y,dsdz​=H(h−y)−z,(x,y,z)(0)=(0,1,0).​

A fluorescent reporter associated with one phase of the repressilator should show repeated rises and falls in a tracked cell, with the same underlying oscillatory period but generally delayed and smoothed relative to instantaneous promoter activity. If mature GFP is fused to a repressor and is sufficiently fast to mature, fluorescence approximately tracks that protein's abundance. If GFP is a separately expressed reporter, its own production, maturation, degradation and dilution introduce an additional filter.
For example, G˙=βG​g(t)−δG​G converts a periodic gate g(t) into a periodic, phase-lagged concentration. Very stable GFP can accumulate a large baseline and obscure oscillations rather than reproducing the regulator trace exactly; maturation delay and bleaching also matter. Biological noise and cell-to-cell phase differences can make individual cycles irregular and wash out oscillations in a population average. The appropriate sketch is therefore a delayed, rounded oscillatory fluorescence trace, with amplitude and baseline depending on reporter turnover, rather than an unqualified copy of a Boolean gate.

After the load is removed, a reversibly stretched DNA molecule relaxes towards a thermally fluctuating coil, releasing stored free energy as heat through the surrounding fluid. There are many more disordered chain conformations than strongly aligned ones, so entropy favours retraction; viscous drag sets how rapidly it occurs. At long times an unconstrained ideal chain has zero mean end-to-end vector but a nonzero typical size, Rrms​=bN​; it does not contract to a point.

The hydrophobic core of a lipid bilayer makes transfer of a hydrated ion into bare lipid energetically costly. An ion channel supplies a water-accessible route across that barrier. Its narrow selectivity filter presents coordinating chemical groups that compensate for loss or rearrangement of the ion's hydration shell. Selectivity depends on the free-energy balance of hydration, coordination, electrostatics and geometry, rather than a sieve that passes all smaller ions.
A potassium-channel filter presents backbone carbonyl oxygens in a geometry favourable for potassium; smaller sodium does not automatically gain the same energetic compensation. Sodium and calcium channels use different filter architectures: charged side chains and their coordination environments favour particular ions, with ion occupancy and competing species also affecting permeation. In canonical vertebrate voltage-gated sodium channels, the DEKA selectivity ring illustrates chemical discrimination distinct from potassium-channel carbonyl coordination. The precise mechanism therefore depends on the channel family.
Rapid flux requires low successive free-energy barriers, not permanent binding of the preferred ion. Several occupied sites, nearby water and ion–ion interactions can facilitate passage without requiring the ion to cross the lipid. For scale, a 100pS open channel driven by 100mV carries 10pA, approximately 6×107 monovalent ions per second. A nanometre-scale route with favourable coordination can support such rapid, thermally driven motion. An ion channel is not an ATP-driven pump: net flux follows the electrochemical potential difference,
Δμ=kB​Tlncout​cin​​+ze(Vin​−Vout​),​
(45)
which vanishes at the species' Nernst equilibrium potential. ATP-consuming pumps maintain the gradients that make sustained channel currents possible.
Permeation selectivity and opening probability are separate properties. Voltage-sensor movement, ligand binding or mechanical tension can regulate access to the pore, while inactivation can terminate conduction during a maintained stimulus. Patch-clamp unitary currents establish discrete conducting states; reversal potentials under changed ionic compositions quantify selectivity. Structure determination locates the filter and gates, and mutations or specific blockers test their functional roles. Agreement between these measurements is stronger evidence than structure or current shape alone.

A charged biological molecule attracts counterions and repels ions of the same sign. Thermal diffusion opposes complete localization, producing a mobile ionic atmosphere whose charge partly cancels the molecule's electric field at larger distances. Solvent polarization also reduces the field through its permittivity, but that dielectric response is distinct from the mobile-ion screening cloud.

ni​(r)=ni∞​exp[−kB​Tzi​eϕ(r)​].
(46)
For ∣zi​eϕ∣≪kB​T, linearization about an electroneutral bulk gives

ϕ(r)=4πϵrQ​e−r/λD​.​

Fluorescence localization precision can be much finer than the image width: a fluorescent molecule produces a diffraction-broadened point spread function, but the centre of that distribution can be estimated much more precisely than its width. Fit the measured camera counts to a calibrated point-spread model with position, brightness and background parameters. For an ideal Gaussian image of standard deviation s and N independent detected photons, the centroid precision scales as
σloc​≃N​s​.​
(50)
Pixelation and background worsen this limit. A useful approximate variance for square pixels of width a and background rms noise b photons per pixel is
σloc2​≃Ns2+a2/12​+a2N28πs4b2​.
(51)
This estimate requires a correctly specified isolated image and appropriate noise model. Drift, optical aberration, dye motion, linkage offset, bleaching and motion blur introduce further errors; localization precision alone does not guarantee absolute positional accuracy.[ref]
In the Myosin V measurements, total internal reflection fluorescence microscopy reduced background and a two-dimensional Gaussian fit localized a single dye attached to a light chain with roughly 1.5nm precision. The motor's centre advances about 37nm per cycle. A hand-over-hand mechanism instead moves the rear head about 74nm past the stationary front head, exchanging their roles. A label displaced by x from the midpoint therefore gives alternating displacements 37+2x and 37−2x nm; near a head this tends towards alternating 74 and 0 nm. An inchworm model would give approximately uniform 37 nm label displacements. Resolved unequal alternating steps therefore distinguish the mechanisms, with interpretation depending on the label geometry.[ref]
For sub-diffraction imaging, arrange that only a sparse, spatially separable subset of fluorophores emits in each frame, localize those emitters, then combine many frames into a map. Photoactivation or reversible stochastic switching provides the sparse subsets. Distinct nearby molecules can then be localized at different times, circumventing overlap of their simultaneously emitted diffraction patterns. The achievable structural resolution depends on photon-limited precision, label density and linkage, repeated blinking, drift correction and specimen motion; arbitrarily many fits to the same dye do not supply missing labels elsewhere.
