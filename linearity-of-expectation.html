Linearity of expectation (E[aX+bY]=aE[X]+bE[Y])

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Expected value
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
For integrable random variables X1​,…,Xn​ on the same probability space and fixed real numbers a1​,…,an​, linearity of expectation states
E[∑i=1n​ai​Xi​]=∑i=1n​ai​E[Xi​].
(173)
The random variables can be dependent random variables. The formula follows by the linearity of integration; a finite linear combination of integrable random variables remains integrable. For example, if X takes −1,2,8 with probabilities 1/2,3/10,1/5, then X and 2X are dependent random variables, yet E[X+2X]=3E[X]=5.1.
An especially useful application counts how many events occur. Put Ii​=1Ai​​, an indicator random variable, and N=∑i​Ii​. Since E[Ii​]=P(Ai​), we get E[N]=∑i​P(Ai​). There is no need to compute the whole probability distribution of N, or to assume independence. For a uniform random permutation of n≥1 objects, each object stays fixed with probability 1/n, so the fixed point count of a uniform random permutation has expected value one for every n. [ref].
Linearity of expectation concerns addition and multiplication by fixed scalars. Products instead satisfy E[XY]=E[X]E[Y] under the hypotheses of expectation of a product of independent random variables. For the example above, E[X2]=14.5, whereas (E[X])2=2.89. Applying a nonlinear function before averaging can therefore change the answer substantially; use the law of the unconscious statistician to compute that average.
