Rayleigh distribution (R∼Rayleigh(σ),σ>0)

The Rayleigh distribution is the probability distribution of the Euclidean norm of a two-dimensional random vector with independent zero-mean normal random variables of equal variance as components. If X,Y∼N(0,σ2) are independent, then
R=X2+Y2​∼Rayleigh(σ),fR​(r)={σ2r​e−r2/(2σ2),0,​r≥0,r<0.​

Here σ is the standard deviation of each component, not of the radius R. At σ=1 this gives the unit-scale probability density function re−r2/2.[ref]
This geometry turns two signed errors into one nonnegative distance. For example, suppose horizontal and vertical positioning errors are independent N(0,4cm2) random variables. Their means both vanish, but the distance from the intended point is usually positive. The Rayleigh distribution with σ=2cm gives the probability of landing inside any centered disk. These are chosen model assumptions, rather than measurements of a particular instrument.
An apparently paradoxical feature is that the most likely location per unit area is the origin, while the radial mode is σ. A narrow annulus farther out has more area. Near zero that growing circumference outweighs the falling planar density; farther out the exponential function wins. Distinguishing densities measured against different elements of area or length resolves the paradox.
