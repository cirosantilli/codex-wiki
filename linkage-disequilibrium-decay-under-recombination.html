Linkage-disequilibrium decay under recombination (Dt+1​=(1−r)Dt​)

 Home Biology Genetics Population genetics Linkage disequilibrium
OurBigBook.com Words: 358
Genetic recombination erodes linkage disequilibrium by reshuffling alleles between haplotypes, while preserving marginal allele frequencies in the idealized model. Let fAB,t​ denote the haplotype frequency in the gamete pool of generation t, let p,q denote its marginal allele frequencies, and let r∈[0,1/2] be the constant recombination fraction between two autosomal genetic loci. Assume random mating within one population, nonoverlapping generations, fair Mendelian segregation, equal reciprocal gamete production, the same r in both sexes, and a deterministic population large enough to neglect genetic drift. Exclude natural selection, mutation and gene flow. Then
fAB,t+1​=(1−r)fAB,t​+rpq=fAB,t​−rDt​,Dt+1​=(1−r)Dt​,Dt​=(1−r)tD0​.
(8)
Here p and q remain constant: recombining changes which alleles occur together, not their total numbers. The other haplotype frequencies change by +rDt​ for Ab,aB and −rDt​ for ab. Consequently the joint probability distribution stays within its allowed bounds and approaches the product of its marginal distributions when r>0.
For the original positive example p=0.60,q=0.40,D0​=0.12, a recombination fraction r=0.10 gives D1​=0.108 and D10​≈0.04184. The negative example D0​=−0.12 has exactly the opposite sequence: genetic recombination reduces the magnitude without reversing the sign. With r=0, the initial linkage disequilibrium persists in this model; even independent assortment at r=1/2 only halves D each generation. A population association does not vanish after one generation merely because the genetic loci are unlinked.
The geometric sequence gives a generation scale: for r>0, the continuous interpolation reaches ∣D0​∣/2 at t1/2​=log(1/2)/log(1−r). Actual generations are integers; the first at or below half is ⌈t1/2​⌉. For small r, t1/2​≈(log2)/r. Because the denominator of allelic correlation stays fixed, rLD,t2​=(1−r)2trLD,02​; its decay is faster than the magnitude of Dt​.
A short derivation uses random mating to assign frequency 2fAB​fab​ to the double heterozygote AB/ab and 2fAb​faB​ to Ab/aB. Relative to r=0, each loses or gains an AB gamete fraction r/2, respectively. Their net contribution is r(fAb​faB​−fAB​fab​)=−rD. The other genotypes produce no net change in fAB​, proving the recurrence and, by iteration, its solution.
This temporal model does not identify elapsed generations from physical separation alone. The recombination fraction depends on genetic recombination, including recombination hotspots, while population stratification, natural selection, gene flow or genetic drift can create or maintain linkage disequilibrium. Repeated sampling across time would also require accounting for sample size and haplotype estimation. A cross-sectional plot of association against distance between genetic loci is therefore a different observation from the idealized trajectory plotted here.
