Dyadic slope martingale (Gn​(t)=2n(f((k+1)2−n)−f(k2−n)))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Martingale
OurBigBook.com Words: 84
For a real continuous function f on [0,1], let Gn​ be its secant slope on each length-2−n dyadic cell. Regard ([0,1],B,dt) as a probability space and use the filtration of half-open dyadic cells with the endpoint {1} as a separate null cell. The mean of the two child slopes equals the parent slope, so (Gn​) is a martingale. Its integral gives the linear interpolation of f on that grid. If f is Lipschitz continuous, these slopes are bounded by its Lipschitz constant; the Lp martingale convergence theorem then supplies a bounded integral density for f.
