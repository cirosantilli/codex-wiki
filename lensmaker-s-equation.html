Lensmaker's equation

 ... Physics Branches of physics Optics Geometrical optics Lens (optics) Thin lens
OurBigBook.com Words: 181
The lensmaker's equation connects focal length to the refractive index and shape of a thin lens. For a thin lens in air with surfaces shaped as parts of spheres, refractive index n, and incident light travelling to the right,
f1​=(n−1)(Ra​1​−Rb​1​).
(29)
Each signed radius of curvature is positive when its centre of curvature lies to the right of that surface, and negative when it lies to the left. A symmetric biconvex thin lens with n=1.5, Ra​=1m, and Rb​=−1m therefore has 1/f=0.5(1+1)m−1=1m−1, giving f=1m and optical power +1D. This is an ideal shape example consistent with the thin lens diagrams, not a specification of their finite thickness.
In a uniform surrounding medium of refractive index ns​ on both sides, replace n−1 by n/ns​−1. Thus the same shape reverses its focusing sign if its refractive index is lower than its surroundings: for n=1.5 and ns​=2, the same radii give 1/f=−0.5m−1, or f=−2m. Positive focal length therefore cannot be inferred from convex shape in every environment. A proof outline applies Snell's law with the small-angle approximation at each surface, adds their direction changes, and neglects the distance between them. [ref].
