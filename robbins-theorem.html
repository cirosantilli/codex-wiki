Robbins' theorem

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Foundations of mathematics Graph theory Orientation of a graph Strong orientation
OurBigBook.com Words: 158
A finite nonempty connected graph whose edges are undirected admits a strong orientation if and only if it has no bridge in a graph. This statement holds for finite multigraphs as well as finite simple graphs; self-loops do not help connect distinct vertices. The single-vertex case is allowed, with length-zero directed paths.[ref]
Necessity comes from the two sides separated by deleting a bridge in a graph: its chosen arrow crosses in only one direction. For sufficiency, the cited constructive proof uses depth-first search to find a cycle in a graph, orients it cyclically, contracts it, and repeats. Undoing the contractions replaces junctions by directed cycles, retaining two-way reachability. Contraction can produce multiple edges, so these must remain distinct. This is a construction outline; the input graph is finite and bridgeless, and remaining edges within contracted pieces can receive arbitrary directions.[ref]
