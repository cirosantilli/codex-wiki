Terrell rotation (sinθ=v/c)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Special relativity
OurBigBook.com Words: 98
The apparent rotation of a moving object arises when length contraction is combined with different light emission times for photons arriving at a camera together. For an edge-on plane lamina moving along x, let X,Y be its rest coordinates and put a distant camera on the negative y axis. Simultaneous reception requires t+Y/c=constant. Since x=X/γ+vt, its transverse image coordinate is X/γ−βY. This is the projection of a stationary lamina rotated through θ with cosθ=1/γ, sinθ=β. It describes a photographic projection; simultaneous laboratory positions retain length contraction. The simple angle formula assumes the transverse distant-camera geometry.
