A particle suspension is a dispersed population of solid particles carried by a fluid. Its dynamics couple particle transport, drag force, gravity, and the compensating motion of the carrier fluid.

Sedimentation

Words: 23 Articles: 1
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
Sedimentation is separation of suspended particles through a body-force-driven relative motion. In the Perrin sedimentation experiment, diffusion balances gravitational settling to produce a stationary height profile.

Monodisperse

Words: 27
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
A monodisperse particle suspension has a narrow distribution of particle sizes. Treating its particles as having a single radius simplifies the Perrin sedimentation experiment model; residual size variation can still affect the inferred Avogadro constant.

Density inversion in a settling suspension

Words: 33
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
More suspended heavy particles above fewer particles puts denser fluid above lighter fluid. Such a particle suspension may develop Rayleigh-Taylor instability and overturning in addition to one-dimensional kinematic sedimentation. A rarefaction solution alone does not establish hydrodynamic stability.

Heated particle-laden layer

Words: 41
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
A dilute particle suspension can supply both excess mass density and heat. To first order its equation of state is ρ/ρ0​=1+γϕ−θ, where ϕ is the particle volume fraction and θ is fractional thermal expansion. Particle deposition flux reduces ϕ, while particle heating increases θ. The competition can reverse the reduced gravity and create unstable density stratification.

Particle volume fraction (ϕ)

Words: 29
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
The particle volume fraction is the fraction of a representative mixture volume occupied by particles. Several particle species have fractions ϕi​, while the carrier-fluid fraction is 1−∑i​ϕi​.

A bidisperse particle suspension contains two distinguishable particle populations, commonly with different sizes, densities, or settling velocities.

Composition wave in a bidisperse suspension

Words: 30

When two species share the same local settling velocity, their total particle volume fraction supports a nonlinear kinematic wave, while their relative composition is transported as a contact-like wave at the common particle speed.

Settling velocity (W)

Words: 1k Articles: 21
Suspension (chemistry) OurBigBook.com
The settling velocity is a particle's vertical velocity relative to a stated frame. With height increasing upward, a sedimenting particle has W<0.

Settling-velocity radius scaling (Ws​∝r2 or r1/2)

Words: 45
Settling velocity OurBigBook.com
For spheres with the same density contrast, balance submerged weight, proportional to r3, against Stokes drag law, proportional to rWs​, to obtain Ws​∝r2. With constant drag coefficient, quadratic drag is proportional to r2Ws2​, giving Ws​∝r1/2. The two radius laws require their corresponding particle Reynolds number limits.

Particle Reynolds number (Rep​=2rρf​Ws​/μ)

Words: 38
Settling velocity OurBigBook.com
A Reynolds number based on particle diameter and relative speed in the carrier fluid. Small values justify Stokes drag law; a high-Reynolds-number regime with nearly constant drag coefficient instead gives quadratic drag. The drag law must be evaluated in the appropriate regime for every species.

Particle deposition flux (Js​=Ws​ϕb​)

Words: 46
Settling velocity OurBigBook.com
For downward settling velocity magnitude Ws​ and near-bed particle volume fraction ϕb​, a horizontal absorbing bed receives particle volume per area per time Js​=Ws​ϕb​. A bed inclined at angle ϑ to the horizontal receives Ws​ϕb​cosϑ per unit actual area, in the absence of resuspension or additional normal fluid motion.

Stokes settling velocity (Us​=2(ρp​−ρf​)ga2/(9μ))

Words: 321
Settling velocity OurBigBook.com
Take downward as positive and let gravity have magnitude g. For a rigid sphere of density ρp​ in otherwise stationary fluid of density ρf​, its weight minus buoyancy is 34​πa3(ρp​−ρf​)g. At terminal velocity, this balances the Stokes drag law:
6πμaUs​=34​πa3(ρp​−ρf​)g,Us​=9μ2(ρp​−ρf​)ga2​.
(1)
This signed downward settling velocity is negative for a buoyantly rising sphere and zero for equal densities. For fixed density contrast and dynamic viscosity, the submerged weight grows as a3 while linear drag at fixed speed grows as a; their balance gives the settling-velocity radius scaling Us​∝a2. Meanwhile,
Red​=μ2aρf​∣Us​∣​=9μ24ρf​∣ρp​−ρf​∣ga3​.
(2)
Thus doubling the radius multiplies the predicted Stokes settling speed by four and the particle Reynolds number by eight. This makes the formula useful for separating particles by size, while also providing its own check against extrapolation to large particles.
For a computed water example, take ρf​=1000 kgm−3, μ=1.00×10−3 Pas, ρp​=2500 kgm−3, g=9.81 ms−2, and a=10.0 μm. These are specified rounded inputs rather than a claim of exact water properties at every temperature. They give
Us​=3.27×10−4 ms−1=0.327 mms−1,Red​=10−31000(3.27×10−4)(20.0×10−6)​=0.00654.
(3)
The diameter is 20.0 μm, and both the net downward weight and the upward drag force are 6.16×10−11 N. At this terminal velocity, falling 1.00 mm takes 3.06 s. At a=20.0 μm the prediction is 1.308 mms−1 with Red​=0.05232; at a=50.0 μm it is 8.175 mms−1 with Red​=0.8175, which is no longer a convincing small-Reynolds number case. The figure labels these as predictions of the Stokes settling velocity formula and shows where its inertia check fails.
The calculation describes the eventual terminal velocity, not the release transient governed by particle acceleration and possibly the Unsteady Stokes equation. Walls, hydrodynamic interactions, departures from a rigid sphere, or a different surface condition require a different resistance. For sufficiently small particles, Brownian motion can obscure individual settling trajectories; the formula still specifies the gravitational drift within its assumptions. Hindered settling

Words: 750 Articles: 16
Settling velocity OurBigBook.com
Hindered settling is the reduction or modification of particle settling by the displaced carrier-fluid backflow and by interactions with other particles. A simple dilute mixture model gives every species the same volume-averaged backflow.

Kinematic sedimentation (ϕt​+∂z​j(ϕ)=0)

Words: 717 Articles: 15
Hindered settling OurBigBook.com
A one-dimensional particle suspension settling without particle diffusion or overturning obeys the scalar conservation law ϕt​+∂z​j(ϕ)=0. The upward flux is negative for downward settling. Characteristic curves, compressive shocks and rarefaction waves describe concentration transport rather than individual particle paths.
Hindered-settling flux inflection (f′′(s)=V0​α(1−s)α−2[2−(α+1)s])
Words: 63
Kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
For hindered settling with normalized particle volume fraction s, upward coordinate and downward isolated settling velocity magnitude V0​, the scalar conservation law has conservation law flux f(s)=−V0​s(1−s)α. For α>0, its convexity changes at s=2/(α+1) when this lies in the physical interval. This change distinguishes spreading rarefaction waves from compressing characteristic curves; a flux with an inflection can produce both a shock wave and a rarefaction wave in one transition. At α=0 the flux is linear and the profile simply translates.
Bidisperse kinematic sedimentation
Words: 142 Articles: 2
Kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
A particle suspension contains two particle species with separate particle volume fractions. In an independent-settling model, each species obeys its own flux conservation equation, while a shared stationary deposit can couple the jump conditions through its maximum total packing fraction. Such independence is an imposed model; general hindered settling need not decouple species.
Two-stage bidisperse batch sedimentation
Words: 52
Bidisperse kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
Initially, separate clearing fronts of fast and slow species coexist with a mixed deposition front. After the fast clearing front meets that deposit, the remaining suspension contains only the slow species. Its later sedimentation shock builds a pure-slow layer above the earlier mixed deposit. Final layer thicknesses and composition follow from separate particle-volume conservation.
Deposit composition from sedimentation jump conditions (s(pi​−qi​)=Wi​qi​(1−qi​))
Words: 44
Bidisperse kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
For a bidisperse kinematic sedimentation model with normalized suspension concentrations qi​, isolated speeds Wi​, and a stationary deposit with p1​+p2​=1, the upward deposit-front speed magnitude obeys s(pi​−qi​)=Wi​qi​(1−qi​). Adding gives s=∑i​Wi​qi​(1−qi​)/(1−∑i​qi​); substitution determines each deposited fraction. These are Rankine-Hugoniot conditions with a stationary compacted branch.
Sedimentation shock (U=[j]/[ϕ])
Words: 45
Kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
A moving discontinuity of particle volume fraction in kinematic sedimentation. Conservation of particle volume gives the Rankine-Hugoniot condition U[ϕ]=[j] in a consistently oriented coordinate. A clearing front and a deposit front can both be sedimentation shocks. A stationary deposit uses zero particle flux; its compacted branch need not obey the suspension flux law.

Particle mass conservation gives hc​=[ϕ1​(H−zm​)+ϕ2​zm​]/ϕmax​. Averaging concentration over the entire original container gives ϕˉ​=ϕmax​hc​/H. This average differs from the concentration inside the deposit itself.
Two-layer sedimentation with a compression shock

Kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
With upper concentration a and lower concentration 2a, the clearing, internal and deposit fronts have speeds −us​(1−a), −us​(1−3a) and 2us​a. If the lower two merge, the resulting deposit/upper-suspension front has speed us​a. Final deposit thickness is a(H+zm​) by mass conservation.
Triple-shock sedimentation point ((zm​,tc​,hc​)=(1+aH(1−a)​,us​(1+a)H​,1+a2aH​))
Words: 34
Two-layer sedimentation with a compression shock OurBigBook.com
For initial concentrations a above zm​ and 2a below it, all three straight shocks meet when zm​=H(1−a)/(1+a). The common time is H/[us​(1+a)] and height is 2aH/(1+a). Afterwards only a stationary deposit/clear-fluid boundary remains.
Quadratic hindered-settling flux (j(ϕ)=−us​ϕ(1−ϕ))
Words: 335 Articles: 6
Kinematic sedimentation OurBigBook.com
For downward settling speed us​(1−ϕ) in upward coordinate z, particle flux is j=−us​ϕ(1−ϕ). The concentration-characteristic speed is j′=us​(2ϕ−1) and differs from particle speed. Since j′′=2us​>0, decreasing concentration with height creates a compressive shock, while increasing concentration creates a rarefaction wave.
Parabolic-profile sedimentation shock
Words: 76
Quadratic hindered-settling flux OurBigBook.com
For ϕ0​(z)=ϕmax​(1−z2/h2)/3 on 0≤z≤h, with clear fluid above and no imposed bed discontinuity, the first compression of the smooth profile occurs at t=3h/(4Vs​) and z=h/4. The nascent shock enters clear fluid with initial signed speed −Vs​. Its downward speed subsequently decreases as the state behind it becomes more concentrated, until a boundary interaction changes the problem. This is characteristic breaking, not the time of initiation of a separately imposed packed-bed front.
Settling rarefaction fan (ϕ=21​[1+(z−zm​)/(us​t)])
Words: 181 Articles: 3
Quadratic hindered-settling flux OurBigBook.com
If lower initial concentration b is less than upper concentration a, the entropy solution expands into a fan with ϕ=[1+(z−zm​)/(us​t)]/2. Its characteristic speeds range from us​(2b−1) to us​(2a−1). The fan can be clipped by a clearing or deposition shock.
Early extinction of a settling rarefaction fan (a∗​=1−2/3​)
Words: 54
Settling rarefaction fan OurBigBook.com
For upper concentration a, lower a/2 and interface height 4H/5, both curved fronts meet inside the fan only if a≥1−2/3​. At smaller a, the deposition front reaches the fan upper edge first, then advances through uniform concentration a. The clearing front remains straight and final time is H(1−3a/5)/[us​(1−a)].

For upper concentration a, lower concentration b<a and L=H−zm​, define A=aL and D=(1−b)zm​. Once inside the settling rarefaction fan, clearing and deposition shocks obey zu​=zm​−us​t+2us​At​ and zd​=zm​+us​t−2us​Dt​. Last surviving characteristic of a settling fan (ϕ∗​=A​/(A​+D​))
Words: 47
Curved settling fronts within a rarefaction fan OurBigBook.com
If both shocks meet inside a settling rarefaction fan, tc​=(A​+D​)2/us​ and the last characteristic has concentration ϕ∗​=A​/(A​+D​). It runs from the initial internal interface to the final deposit point. This construction requires b≤ϕ∗​≤a; otherwise a front exits the fan before final deposition.
Settling shock speed (V=us​(ϕL​+ϕR​−1))
Words: 36
Quadratic hindered-settling flux OurBigBook.com
The Rankine-Hugoniot condition for the quadratic hindered-settling flux gives V=us​(ϕL​+ϕR​−1), with lower and upper states labelled L,R. It includes a downward clearing front (ϕR​=0), an upward deposition front (ϕL​=1), and a stationary final deposit/clear-fluid interface.
