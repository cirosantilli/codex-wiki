Frequency modulation

 Home Electrical engineering
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
Frequency modulation carries a message by changing the instantaneous frequency of a carrier wave. In the ideal model the wave amplitude stays constant: information changes the rate at which the wave phase advances. Write a real electrical signal and its chosen complex representation as
s(t)=Acosϕ(t),z(t)=Aeiϕ(t),A>0.
(11)
The wave envelope in this representation has modulus ∣z(t)∣=A throughout, although s(t) continually passes through zero. A smaller instantaneous value of s(t) therefore does not mean a smaller wave amplitude. The continuously unwrapped wave phase ϕ is measured in radians; its derivative is an angular frequency, while division by 2π gives frequency in cycles per second, or hertz.
For a message m(t) expressed as an electric potential difference, an ideal voltage-controlled oscillator has sensitivity Kf​ in hertz per volt and obeys
fi​(t)=fc​+Kf​m(t),ϕ(t)=ϕ(0)+2πfc​t+2πKf​∫0t​m(τ)dτ.
(12)
The integral is essential: putting the message directly into the wave phase gives phase modulation, whose instantaneous frequency instead depends on the message's derivative. Amplitude modulation changes the multiplying wave amplitude. These are different ways to encode the same kind of message signal.
An original slow illustration uses fc​=20Hz, message frequency fm​=1Hz, frequency deviation Δf=5Hz, and FM modulation index β=5. With zero initial wave phase,
ϕ(t)=2π⋅20t+5sin(2πt),s(t)=Acos(2π⋅20t+5sin(2πt)),fi​(t)=20+5cos(2πt).
(13)
Here t is in seconds. The instantaneous frequency stays positive, between 15 and 25Hz; the wave phase always advances. The oscillations bunch up where fi​ is large and spread out where it is small, while the wave envelope remains at A.
This separation is useful in a receiver: changing a positive multiplying wave amplitude does not change the zero crossings of cosϕ, so their timing still carries the message. Noise that moves those crossings, a vanishing wave amplitude, and frequency response distortion can still damage it. The surprising beauty of FM is that a constant wave envelope can conceal a rich frequency spectrum: the components interfere to change timing instead of the envelope's height.
