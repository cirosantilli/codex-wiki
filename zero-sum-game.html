Zero-sum game (∑i​ui​(s)=0)

OurBigBook.com Words: 966 Articles: 16
In a zero-sum game, the players' payoffs sum to zero at every pure strategy strategy profile. For two players this means u2​=−u1​: maximizing the column player's payoff is minimizing the row player's. A single payoff matrix A therefore specifies a finite two-player matrix game, and independent mixed strategies x,y give expected payoffs xTAy and −xTAy.
Matching pennies and the usual rock paper scissors are two-player zero-sum games. The asymmetric coordination example is not: its total payoff is 5 when choices match and 0 otherwise. Applying a two-player minimax theorem to general bimatrix games would discard this possibility of shared gains. For a two-player zero-sum game, however, optimal mixed strategies guarantee the value of a zero-sum game, and pairing any two opposing optimal strategies gives a Nash equilibrium.
