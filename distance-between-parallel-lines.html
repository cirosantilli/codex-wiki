Distance between parallel lines (d=∥d×v∥/∥v∥)

For two parallel lines a1​+sv and a2​+tv in three-dimensional Euclidean space, with v=0 and d=a2​−a1​, their shortest distance is
d=​d−v⋅vd⋅v​v​=∥v∥∥d×v∥​.​
(2)
Every joining displacement is d+(t−s)v. Its component perpendicular to v is fixed, and its parallel component can be cancelled by a suitable t−s. The Pythagorean theorem proves that this perpendicular component has the shortest Euclidean norm. The cross product expression follows from ∥d×v∥=∥d∥∥v∥sinθ. For coincident lines the distance is zero. The perpendicular-component formula also applies in any finite-dimensional Euclidean space, where a three-dimensional cross product need not be available.
