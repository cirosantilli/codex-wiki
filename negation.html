Negation (¬P)

 Home Computer science Theoretical computer science Boolean operation
OurBigBook.com Words: 108 Articles: 1
Logical negation exchanges zero and one: ¬0=1 and ¬1=0. For a numerical bit x, this is 1−x; applying logical negation twice restores x. It acts on the entire propositional formula in its scope. Parentheses therefore matter: ¬(P∨Q) says that neither condition holds, while (¬P)∨Q also accepts every case where Q holds. At (P,Q)=(1,1) their values are zero and one.
On a set, logical negation becomes taking a complement in a fixed universal set. In Boolean operations on events, “not even” selects {1,3,5}. “Not both even and at least four” selects {1,2,3,5}; it does not require both qualifications to fail. De Morgan's laws make the scope of these compound negations precise.
