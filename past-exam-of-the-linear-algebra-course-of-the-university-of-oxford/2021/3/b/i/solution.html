For the given nondegenerate bilinear form, define the linear map
Φ:V⟶V′,Φ(v)(u)=(u,v).
(35)
By part (a), Φ is a linear isomorphism. The paper's bilinear orthogonal complement uses U in the first argument; with this convention,
U⊥=Φ−1(U0),U0={f∈V′:f(u)=0 for every u∈U}.
(36)
Here U0 is the annihilator of a vector subspace U. Since Φ is a linear isomorphism, its restriction gives a linear isomorphism U⊥→U0.
For completeness, choose a basis u1​,…,uk​ of U and extend it to a basis u1​,…,un​ of V. In its dual basis u1∗​,…,un∗​, a linear functional vanishes on U exactly when its first k coefficients are zero. Therefore
U0=span{uk+1∗​,…,un∗​},dimU0=n−k.
(37)
Consequently
dimU⊥=dimU0=dimV−dimU,dimU+dimU⊥=dimV.​
(38)
The argument includes U=0 and U=V, and requires no symmetry assumption on the bilinear form.
