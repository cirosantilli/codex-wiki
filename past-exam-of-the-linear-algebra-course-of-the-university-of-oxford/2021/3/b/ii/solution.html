No. On the real vector space V=R2, use the symmetric bilinear form
((x,y),(s,t))=xs−yt.
(39)
Its matrix of a bilinear form is diag(1,−1), whose determinant is −1=0, so it is a nondegenerate bilinear form. Let
U=span{(1,1)}.
(40)
A vector (s,t) belongs to the bilinear orthogonal complement U⊥ exactly when s−t=0. Hence
U⊥=U,U∩U⊥=U=0,U+U⊥=U=V.​
(41)
In particular, the claimed direct sum fails. The isotropic vector (1,1) has zero self-pairing, although it pairs nontrivially with other vectors of V.
The dimension identity in part (i) counts both vector subspaces but does not force their intersection of vector subspaces to be zero. For a symmetric bilinear form, U∩U⊥ is the radical of a bilinear form restricted to U. Thus the precise criterion is
V=U⊕U⊥ ⟺ the restriction of the form to U is nondegenerate.​
(42)
Indeed, trivial intersection of vector subspaces and part (i) give a sum of vector subspaces of dimension dimV. A positive-definite bilinear form would guarantee this for every U; symmetry alone does not.
