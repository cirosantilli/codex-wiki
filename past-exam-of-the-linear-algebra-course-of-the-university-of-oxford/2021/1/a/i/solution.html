The quotient vector space V/U identifies two vectors v,w precisely when v−w∈U. Since U is an invariant subspace, T(U)⊆U. Thus
v+U=w+U ⟹ v−w∈U ⟹ T(v)−T(w)=T(v−w)∈U.
(1)
Consequently T(v)+U=T(w)+U: the output does not depend on the chosen representative. This proves that the induced linear map on a quotient vector space
T(v+U)=T(v)+U​
(2)
is well-defined. It is a linear map because, for scalars a,b,
T(a(v+U)+b(w+U))=T(av+bw)+U=aT(v+U)+bT(w+U).
(3)
