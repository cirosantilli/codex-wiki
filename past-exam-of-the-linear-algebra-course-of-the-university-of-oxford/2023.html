The primary decomposition theorem for linear maps starts with a linear map T:V→V on a finite-dimensional vector space over a field F. Factor its minimal polynomial as
mT​(x)=∏j=1r​pj​(x)aj​,
(1)
where the pj​ are distinct monic polynomials that are irreducible polynomials and aj​≥1. Define Vj​=kerpj​(T)aj​. Then
V=V1​⊕⋯⊕Vr​,T(Vj​)⊆Vj​,mT∣Vj​​​=pjaj​​​.
(2)
Thus the direct sum separates the irreducible polynomial factors into uniquely determined invariant subspaces. The statement works even when the minimal polynomial does not split into linear polynomials over F.

The dual space is the vector space of all linear functionals on V:
V′=HomC​(V,C)​.
(7)
Addition and scalar multiplication are defined pointwise. The dual map sends an output measurement back along T:
T′:V′→V′,T′(φ)=φ∘T,(T′φ)(v)=φ(Tv)​.
(8)
The function composition of these linear maps is linear, and T′ is linear in φ. This is the algebraic dual map, so its definition involves no complex conjugation or inner product.

For a vector subspace U⊆V, its annihilator of a vector subspace is
U0={φ∈V′:φ(u)=0 for every u∈U}​.
(9)
Let dimU=k and dimV=n. Extend a basis u1​,…,uk​ of U to a basis u1​,…,un​ of V, and let ε1,…,εn be the dual basis. Every linear functional has the unique expression φ=∑j=1n​φ(uj​)εj. It belongs to U0 precisely when its first k coefficients vanish. Consequently
U0=span{εk+1,…,εn},dimU+dimU0=dimV​.
(10)
The listed dual basis elements are linearly independent, so counting them proves the formula, including U=0 and U=V.

We prove by mathematical induction on n=dimV that every linear map on an n-dimensional complex vector space admits both triangular forms. For n=0 or 1 this is immediate. When n>1, the fundamental theorem of algebra supplies an eigenvector v; its span U=⟨v⟩ is an invariant subspace.
By parts (ii) and (iii), U0 has dimension n−1 and is an invariant subspace of the dual map T′. Apply the induction hypothesis to T′∣U0​ to choose a basis φ1​,…,φn−1​ with an upper triangular matrix. Extend it by a linear functional φn​ satisfying φn​(v)=1. Such a functional exists by basis extension, and cannot belong to U0. In this basis of V′, the matrix of T′ has the form
C=(B0​bλ​),
(13)
where B is an upper triangular matrix; here the last diagonal entry follows by evaluating T′φn​ at v, where Tv=λv.
There is a basis e1​,…,en​ of V with φi​(ej​)=δij​, since the dual basis construction gives a finite-dimensional bidual evaluation map isomorphism. In particular en​=v. The matrix transpose relationship for a dual map gives
[T](e1​,…,en​)​=CT,
(14)
a lower triangular matrix. Reversing this basis turns a lower triangular matrix into an upper triangular matrix. If R is the permutation matrix reversing the order, we have proved
[T](e1​,…,en​)​=CT lower triangular,[T](en​,…,e1​)​=RCTR upper triangular​.
(15)
This completes the induction by triangularization using dual spaces.

The increments Δi​=di​−di−1​ are positive integers by part (a)(i), are nonincreasing by part (a)(ii), and sum to dm​=4. Thus they form an integer partition of 4. The complete list is (4), (3,1), (2,2), (2,1,1), and (1,1,1,1).
To prove all five occur over every field, let Js​(0) be a Nilpotent Jordan block of size s. It sends each standard basis vector to its predecessor, with the first sent to zero, so
dimkerJs​(0)i=min(i,s).

For a block diagonal matrix, the nullities add. The following explicit nilpotent matrices therefore realize every possible sequence:
m12234​(d1​,…,dm​)(4)(3,4)(2,4)(2,3,4)(1,2,3,4)​realizing matrix04×4​J2​(0)⊕J1​(0)⊕J1​(0)J2​(0)⊕J2​(0)J3​(0)⊕J1​(0)J4​(0)​​​.

Direct matrix multiplication gives A2=0. Denote the columns by c1​,…,c4​; they satisfy
c3​=c1​−2c2​,c4​=−c1​+c2​.
(23)
Thus the matrix rank is at most two. Over the real numbers or complex numbers, the upper-left two-by-two minor is −3=0, so the matrix rank is exactly two. Every Jordan block has size at most two because A2=0, and each size-two Nilpotent Jordan block contributes one to the matrix rank. Consequently
J=J2​(0)⊕J2​(0)=​0000​1000​0000​0010​​​.
(24)
Equivalently the nullities are (2,4) and the minimal polynomial is x2.
There is a field qualification in the source: Question 2 initially allows an arbitrary field, and part (c) does not specify a new one. The preceding answer holds over every field whose characteristic is not three. In characteristic three, the first row vanishes and the other three rows all become (2,1,0,2), giving matrix rank one while A2=0. Under that reading the answer is instead

An adjoint operator of T:V→V is a linear map T∗:V→V satisfying
⟨Tu,w⟩=⟨u,T∗w⟩for all u,w∈V​.
(26)
This identity transfers T from the first input of the inner product to the second.

For a polynomial f(x)=∑j=0d​aj​xj, the defining identity and uniqueness of the adjoint operator give
f(T)∗=∑j=0d​aj​​(T∗)j.
(33)
Indeed, the adjoint operator of a sum is the sum of the adjoint operators, the adjoint of aT is aˉT∗, and the adjoint of a product AB is B∗A∗. Since the normal operator T commutes with T∗, each power of T commutes with each power of T∗. Multiplying the two finite sums therefore gives
f(T)f(T)∗=f(T)∗f(T)​.
(34)
Hence polynomials of a normal operator are normal, including constant polynomials.

Over the complex numbers, the fundamental theorem of algebra makes every irreducible polynomial factor fj​(x) equal to x−λj​. Part (b)(iv) gives
mT​(x)=∏j=1r​(x−λj​)
(43)
with distinct eigenvalues. The primary decomposition theorem for linear maps consequently expresses V as the direct sum of the actual eigenspaces Eλj​​=ker(T−λj​I), with no higher generalized eigenspaces needed. Part (ii) makes these summands mutually orthogonal.
Apply the Gram-Schmidt process to a basis within each eigenspace. Its linear combinations stay within that eigenspace, so all resulting vectors are still eigenvectors. Their union is orthonormal by part (ii) and spans V by the primary decomposition theorem for linear maps. Thus
V=j=1⨁r​Eλj​​,[T]B​=diag(λ1​IdimEλ1​​​,…,λr​IdimEλr​​​)​
(44)
for an orthonormal basis B of eigenvectors. This proves the finite-dimensional complex spectral theorem for normal operators by the requested decomposition argument.
