Ribose (C5​H10​O5​)

 Home Chemistry Chemical substance Carbohydrate Sugar
OurBigBook.com Words: 248
Ribose is a monosaccharide with molecular formula C5​H10​O5​. Its biologically familiar form is D-ribose, an aldopentose whose open-chain Fischer projection has CHO at the top, CH2​OH at the bottom, and hydroxy groups on the right at C2, C3, and C4. The opposite horizontal substituents are hydrogen. See McMurry, Organic Chemistry, 25.3: D-ribose and D-glucose configurations.
Read the drawing by counting groups: CHO contributes CHO, three CHOH groups contribute C3​H6​O3​, and CH2​OH contributes CH3​O. Their sum is C5​H10​O5​. The count checks composition; the right/right/right pattern additionally specifies the stereochemistry of D-ribose.
In RNA, ribose occurs as a ring within each nucleotide, joining a nucleobase to the phosphate-linked backbone. Its C2 position retains a hydroxy group, unlike deoxyribose in DNA. In a nucleotide, the sugar positions carry primes, so this is C2′. See Alberts et al.: nucleotide sugars in RNA and DNA. Free ribose in water is also predominantly cyclic, with several ring forms in chemical equilibrium and very little open-chain form; the Fischer projection is valuable for comparing configurations, not for estimating their abundances. See Mason et al., The Conformation of a Ribose Derivative in Aqueous Solution: A Neutron Scattering and Molecular Dynamics Study (2013), introduction.
