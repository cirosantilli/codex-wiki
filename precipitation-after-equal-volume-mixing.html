Precipitation after equal-volume mixing

OurBigBook.com Words: 340
Consider an original toy AB salt model with Ksp,c​=10−8M2 stipulated at fixed temperature and fixed activity coefficient product. This is a conditional concentration constant, not measured data for calcium carbonate or any identified material. A stock contains A cations and X anions; another contains Y cations and B anions. All four ions have unit electric charge magnitude. Only AB can form a solid; X and Y remain spectator ions. Assume full dissociation, no other chemical reactions, and a mixed volume equal to the sum of the initial volumes.
Mix two stocks, each of volume V,, each at molar concentration C of its indicated salt. Before any precipitation, the mixed volume is 2V, so
cA,0​=cB,0​=2VCV​=2C​,Qc,0​=4C2​.
(18)
Multiplying the two stock concentrations would overestimate the mixed ion product by a factor of four. The saturation threshold is C=2Ksp,c​​=2×10−4M.
If C=10−4M, mixing gives Qc,0​=2.5×10−9M2<Ksp,c​: no AB precipitate is favored. If C=4×10−4M, mixing gives Qc,0​=4×10−8M2>Ksp,c​. Suppose nucleation occurs and bulk AB solid remains at chemical equilibrium. Equal starting inventories and one-to-one stoichiometry require equal remaining concentrations, hence
cA,eq​=cB,eq​=Ksp,c​​=10−4M,2VnAB,s​​=2C​−10−4M=10−4M.
(19)
For two 10mL portions in the second case, each reacting ion starts with 4μmol; the final 20mL chemical solution retains 2μmol of each, and 2μmol of AB enters the solid. The respective X and Y inventories stay at 4μmol. Exactly half of each reacting inventory precipitates in this toy model.
The chemical equilibrium calculation adds a phase constraint to the inventory balance. Write x=nAB,s​/(2V) and c=C/2−x. In the equilibrated bulk model,
x≥0,c≥0,c2≤Ksp,c​,x(Ksp,c​−c2)=0.
(20)
When x>0, the last condition requires saturation; when x=0, the inventory can leave an undersaturated chemical solution. Together these give x=max{0,C/2−Ksp,c​​}. This excludes a persistent supersaturated solution by its explicit chemical equilibrium assumption; it does not model a nucleation delay.
