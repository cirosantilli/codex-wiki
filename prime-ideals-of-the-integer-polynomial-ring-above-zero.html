Prime ideals of the integer polynomial ring above zero (P∩Z=0⟹P=0 or P=(f) with primitive f)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Algebra Commutative algebra Prime ideal
OurBigBook.com Words: 194
The prime ideals P of Z[t] with P∩Z={0} are the zero ideal and the principal ideals (f) generated by nonconstant primitive polynomials that are irreducible in Q[t].
To prove the description, invert the nonzero integers to obtain the localization Q[t]. The extension of an ideal P consists of the polynomials f/n with f∈P and n>0. Its contraction is P: if nh∈P and n=0, the defining condition puts n∈/P, so primeness gives h∈P. The prime ideal correspondence for localization makes this extension a proper prime ideal of Q[t].
If P=0, its extension has a nonzero generator because Q[t] is a principal ideal domain. Dividing this generator by its rational polynomial content gives an integer-coefficient primitive polynomial f generating the same extended ideal. Divisibility descent by Gauss lemma for polynomials then gives P=(f) in Z[t]. The proper prime ideal (f) in Q[t] makes f irreducible there. Conversely, any such polynomial generates a prime ideal in Q[t] whose contraction is (f)⊂Z[t] and whose intersection with Z is zero.
The intersection condition is essential: (2,t) is a maximal ideal of Z[t], since its quotient ring is F2​, but it is not principal. A common divisor of 2 and t must be a unit, which would generate the whole ring.
