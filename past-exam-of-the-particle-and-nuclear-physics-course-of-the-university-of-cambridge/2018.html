For gamma decay, the nuclear gamma-ray multipole selection rules require the radiation's angular momentum L to satisfy the triangle inequality
∣Ji​−Jf​∣≤L≤Ji​+Jf​.
(5)
Here Ji​=Jf​=5/2, so 0≤L≤5. A real emitted photon has no monopole radiation mode, leaving L=1,2,3,4,5.
The two nuclear quantum states have the same parity. An electric multipole has parity factor (−1)L, while a magnetic multipole has factor (−1)L+1. Thus electric radiation requires even L and magnetic radiation odd L:
M1,E2,M3,E4,M5.​
(6)
In particular, an E0 nuclear transition cannot emit the single real photon requested here; processes such as internal conversion are different decay channels.
For usual nuclear gamma ray energies, kR≪1, where k=Eγ​/(ℏc) and R is the nuclear size. Higher multipole radiation orders bring extra powers of this small parameter, so M1 and E2 are the likely important contributions, with M3, E4 and M5 generally much smaller. Often M1 is dominant, but an allowed M1 matrix element can be hindered and an E2 transition can be collectively enhanced. The quantum numbers alone do not fix a numerical relative contribution or an unconditional ordering of the M1 and E2 rates.

The four tree-level diagrams for electron-positron annihilation into W-boson pairs are
s-channel γ∗,s-channel Z∗,t-channel νe​,s-channel h∗.​
(7)
The first two diagrams contain the electromagnetic interaction and weak interaction neutral electron currents and the gauge boson self-interaction vertices γW+W− and ZW+W−. The third has two weak charged current vertices joined by an electron neutrino propagator. In the fourth, the incoming electron-Positron pair couples to the Higgs boson, which then couples to W+W−.
The small factor is at the initial Higgs-electron vertex. With the usual electroweak vacuum value v≃246GeV, electron Yukawa suppression of Higgs production gives
ye​=v2​me​​≃2.9×10−6,ghee​=vme​​≃2.1×10−6.
(8)
These are tiny compared with ordinary electroweak interaction gauge couplings. The decay amplitude for the Higgs boson diagram carries this small coefficient, and its separate squared contribution carries its square. Thus the Higgs diagram is negligible because the electron Yukawa coupling is proportional to the electron mass. It vanishes in the massless-electron approximation, even though the Higgs boson-W boson coupling itself is not small. The remaining three gauge diagrams must be added coherently, including their interference.

Smooth-model fission energy. Use natural units c=1, so the quoted nuclear masses are expressed as rest energies. Conservation of mass number and atomic number fixes the second fragment to (A2​,Z2​)=((1−y)A,(1−y)Z). First omit nuclear pairing to compare a continuous range of y. Pairing can subsequently add δ1​+δ2​−δparent​ for integer fragments; it has no smooth parity assignment for arbitrary real y.
In the parent-minus-daughters mass difference, the nucleon rest energy and volume terms cancel. The nuclear symmetry energy also cancels because both fragments have the same Z/A:
A1​(A1​−2Z1​)2​+A2​(A2​−2Z2​)2​=[y+(1−y)]A(A−2Z)2​.
(9)
The remaining liquid-drop fission energy is
Q(y)=​aS​A2/3[1−y2/3−(1−y)2/3]+A1/3aC​Z2​[1−y5/3−(1−y)5/3].​​
(10)
Splitting increases the surface energy cost, but reduces nuclear Coulomb energy. Both contributions are symmetric under y↔1−y, so the symmetric split is stationary. Symmetry alone would not establish a maximum, since the two contributions compete.
For a full check, put S=aS​A2/3, x=aC​Z2/(aS​A) and t=y1/3+(1−y)1/3. On 0≤y≤1/2, t increases from 1 to 22/3. Expressing the two fractional-power sums through t gives
SQ​=1+x−3t2+2/t​+9x​(t5−5t2−5/t),dtd(Q/S)​=9t2t3−1​[5x(t3−1)−6].
(11)
The derivative can change sign only from negative to positive, so any interior stationary point in t is a minimum. The largest value is therefore either the no-split endpoint Q=0 or the symmetric split. Thus symmetric fission maximizes the positive liquid-drop energy release: whenever this smooth model permits an exothermic split, its largest release occurs at y=1/2.
There is a qualification to the paper's unqualified wording. For arbitrary low Z2/A, the symmetric split need not maximize Q; indeed it is a local minimum when Z2/A<(2/5)aS​/aC​=10. The symmetric-maximum conclusion is valid in the heavy, fission-favorable regime relevant here, rather than a universal property of the formula.
At y=1/2,
Qsym​=−aS​A2/3(21/3−1)+A1/3aC​Z2​(1−2−2/3).
(12)
Requiring Qsym​>0 gives
AZ2​>aC​aS​​1−2−2/321/3−1​=0.7218.0​0.3700390.259921​≃17.6.​
(13)
Barrier and fragment yields. Positive final-state Q value does not remove the fission barrier along a deformation path. Surface energy initially rises as the nucleus deforms; spontaneous fission then requires collective quantum tunnelling unless enough excitation is present. Its rate is exponentially sensitive to barrier height, width and collective inertia. Thus nuclei close to the exothermicity threshold can have immeasurably long spontaneous-fission mean lifetimes, while sufficiently heavier nuclei have smaller effective fission barriers or larger quantum tunnelling probabilities.
The fission-fragment mass distribution of low-excitation actinide fission is usually double peaked: complementary fragments have mass fractions roughly 0.4 and 0.6 rather than both exactly 1/2. Nuclear shell model effects and the fission dynamics help favor asymmetric fragments. Maximizing the final smooth-model energy release is not a calculation of the fragment yield. Thermal versus fast-neutron fission. The last comparison uses the supplied observed capture excitations, 6.5 and 4.8MeV, rather than replacing them by the preceding smooth-model estimates. Interpreting the quoted 6.2 and 6.6MeV activation energies as the barriers in the respective capture channels gives
6.5>6.2:thermal capture on 235U can lead to prompt fission,​
(18)
4.8<6.6:thermal capture on 238U is below the fission barrier.​
(19)
A simple neutron-induced fission threshold estimate for the second channel requires about 6.6−4.8=1.8MeV additional centre-of-mass kinetic energy. Sub-barrier tunnelling is possible, but it does not give the large thermal-neutron fission probability of the first channel.
The barrier labels in the original PDF need care: it assigns them to 235U and 238U, whereas the states formed after capture are 236U∗ and 239U∗. The comparison above uses the intended capture-channel interpretation. If the quoted numbers are literally barriers of the uncaptured target nuclei, the compound-nucleus barriers are missing and this numerical comparison does not follow from those data alone.
In a thermal reactor, neutron moderation slows fission neutrons so the large low-energy fission cross section of uranium-235 can support a nuclear chain reaction. Fuel composition, moderator choice and geometry must balance production against capture and leakage; capture resonances of uranium-238 during slowing are important losses from that neutron population. The reaction rate per target nucleus depends on the spectrum through ∫ϕ(E)σf​(E)dE, where ϕ(E) is differential neutron flux per unit energy. A fast spectrum can access the uranium-238 fission channel and changes this balance. Also, uranium-238 is a fertile material: capture followed by two beta-minus decays can produce plutonium-239, a fissile material, so capture can contribute to breeding even when it causes no immediate fission.

Colour and identical-quark spin. A spatial ground-state baryon has a symmetric ℓ=0 spatial wavefunction. For uuu, ddd or sss, the quark flavour state is also symmetric, and the total-spin 3/2 state of three spin-one-half quarks is symmetric. Without another degree of freedom, the whole state would be symmetric under exchange of identical fermions, contrary to the Pauli exclusion principle.
A three-quark colour singlet supplies a completely antisymmetric factor,
∣1c​⟩=6​1​ϵabc​∣abc⟩,
(20)
using the three color charge states. The remaining spatial-spin-flavour factor can then be symmetric. These baryons therefore provide evidence for an additional internal degree of freedom with at least three states, realized as colour in Quantum chromodynamics.
For the uu pair in uus, the spatial and quark flavour factors are symmetric under exchange of the two up quarks, while the colour factor is antisymmetric. The pair's spin state must consequently be symmetric. The identical-quark pair spin in a ground-state baryon is
Suu​=1,​
(21)
a triplet state. This holds whether the complete baryon has total spin 1/2 or 3/2.
Origin of the pair interaction. The terms in the baryon hyperfine mass formula are effective colour-magnetic spin-spin interactions, arising from gluon exchange between the quarks' colour-magnetic moments. Each magnetic moment brings an inverse constituent quark mass, explaining the factor 1/(mi​mj​). The constant A′ absorbs the strong coupling constant, colour factors and the spatial overlap. A common A′ is a constituent-model approximation.
Spin algebra and the two masses. Set ℏ=c=1, so each dimensionless spin angular momentum operator satisfies Si2​=3/4. Label the two up quarks 1 and 2 and the strange quark 3. Squaring S12​=S1​+S2​ gives
⟨S1​⋅S2​⟩=21​[S12​(S12​+1)−43​−43​]=41​.
(22)
Next square the total spin J=S12​+S3​:
⟨S1​⋅S3​+S2​⋅S3​⟩=21​[J(J+1)−2−43​]={−1,1/2,​J=1/2,J=3/2.​
(23)
Substituting these pair sums into the baryon hyperfine mass formula yields
Muus,1/2​=2mu​+ms​+4A′​(mu2​1​−mu​ms​4​),​
(24)
Muus,3/2​=2mu​+ms​+4A′​(mu2​1​+mu​ms​2​).​
(25)
Their difference is 3A′/(2mu​ms​). Repeating the argument for the identical ss pair in uss gives
Muss,1/2​=mu​+2ms​+4A′​(ms2​1​−mu​ms​4​),Muss,3/2​=mu​+2ms​+4A′​(ms2​1​+mu​ms​2​).
(26)
Thus the model predicts equal Sigma and Xi hyperfine splittings:
M(Ξ∗)−M(Ξ)M(Σ∗)−M(Σ)​=1.​
(27)
For a like-flavour comparison using exactly the supplied masses, the uus splitting is 1383−1189=194MeV and the uss splitting is 1532−1315=217MeV, giving 194/217=0.894. Averaging within the supplied isospin multiplets instead gives 192/215=0.893. The predicted equality captures the common scale but misses the observed ratio by about 11% in this table. Different spatial overlaps, and hence different effective A′ values, can account for departures from the common-coefficient approximation.
Radiative decay and recoil. In a radiative Sigma-to-Lambda transition, both baryons retain valence content uds. A photon couples to an electrically charged constituent while the final quarks are projected onto the lower baryon's spin-flavour wavefunction. The leading up- and down-quark diagrams are shown below; their contributions must be combined coherently. In the simplest model, the strange-quark spin operator alone cannot change the ud pair from its Sigma triplet to the Lambda singlet. The transition is an M1 multipole radiation transition, consistent with 1/2+→1/2+.
For the photon energy, let MΣ​=1193MeV and MΛ​=1116MeV in natural units. Four-momentum conservation gives equal opposite final momenta of magnitude Eγ​, so
MΣ​=Eγ​+MΛ2​+Eγ2​​,Eγ​=2MΣ​MΣ2​−MΛ2​​=74.5151MeV≃74.5MeV.​
(28)
The recoil kinetic energy of the Lambda baryon is 77−74.5151=2.4849MeV; assigning the entire mass difference to the photon would miss it.
Which baryons decay strongly? The open strong-decay thresholds for baryons require both sufficient energy and conserved quark flavour, including strangeness. The dominant strong decayers in the supplied table are
Δ−,Δ0,Δ+,Δ++;Σ∗−,Σ∗0,Σ∗+;Ξ∗−,Ξ∗0.​
(29)
The Delta baryon channels Δ→Nπ have threshold near 940+140=1080MeV, below 1232MeV. The excited Sigma baryons can decay as Σ∗→Λπ or Σπ, and the excited Xi baryons as Ξ∗→Ξπ, all with open thresholds and conserved strangeness. Their positive-parity baryon states can emit the negative-parity pion in an odd relative orbital angular momentum, with L=1 already allowing total J=3/2.
The last diagram illustrates Δ++(uuu)→p(uud)+π+(udˉ): a gluon creates a ddˉ pair and the quarks regroup into the two hadrons. No quark changes flavour at the gluon vertices. This is a representative strong pair-creation topology; quantitative hadronic decay rates require bound-state dynamics.
The Omega baryon is an important exception among the spin-3/2 states. Its S=−3 cannot become the S=−2 of Ξπ through the strong interaction. Its flavour-conserving ΞK threshold is at least 1315+500=1815MeV, above its 1673MeV mass, so it decays weakly. The ground-state strange octet also has no open strong channel: for example Σ→Λπ needs at least 1256MeV, and Λ→NK or Ξ→ΛK needs still more than the relevant initial mass. The neutral Sigma decays electromagnetically as above; the neutron and the other unstable octet members decay weakly, while the proton is stable within these processes.

The nuclear determination of ∣Vud​∣. A 0+→0+ beta decay transition has no nuclear parity change and can proceed with zero lepton orbital angular momentum. The allowed scalar operator gives a Fermi transition; the rank-one Gamow-Teller transition operator cannot connect total nuclear spins zero to zero. The strong isobaric-analog transitions used for precision measurements are superallowed beta decays. A generic 0+→0+ assignment alone would not guarantee superallowed strength if the states were nonanalog.
At the quark level the weak charged current converts d↔u and carries Vud​ in the amplitude. The nuclear Fermi operator is an isospin ladder operator, giving
M∝GF​Vud​MF​,Γ∝GF2​∣Vud​∣2∣MF​∣2f.​
(30)
Here f denotes the known phase space dependence, with the dimensional factors included in the proportionality constant. In the isospin-symmetric limit, MF​ between analog states is fixed by isospin algebra, for example ∣MF​∣2=2 for the usual T=1 analog transitions. A measured partial half-life and the computed phase space factor give the comparative half-life; after radiative corrections and isospin breaking corrections, its inverse determines ∣Vud​∣2. The decay rate measures a magnitude squared, not the complex phase of Vud​.
Ds decay diagrams and CKM comparisons. The original PDF has K+Kˉ0, with Kˉ0=sdˉ. The local TeX drops that overbar; using K0=dsˉ instead would give a different flavour channel. Use the PDF's neutral antikaon throughout.

In the third, ssˉ forms the phi meson and udˉ forms π+. The two possible final pairings have different colour contractions and hadronic matrix elements even when their weak CKM factors coincide.

What spin and parity establish. In either pseudoscalar-pseudoscalar final state, the total intrinsic spin is zero. Conservation of angular momentum for a spin-zero parent forces L=0, so the final parity is
Pf​=(−1)(−1)(−1)0=+1,
(36)
opposite to the parent 0− Ds meson. Consequently
Ds+​→K+π0, K+Kˉ0 require parity violation and are weak decays.​
(37)
For a vector meson plus a pion, the intrinsic final spin is one. Coupling to total J=0 requires L=1, giving Pf​=(−1)(−1)(−1)1=−1, equal to the parent parity. Thus spin and parity alone permit a parity-conserving ϕπ+ transition. That is the limited sense in which it is not necessarily weak from this information alone. In reality it is also weak: the initial charm flavour disappears, which the strong interaction and electromagnetic interaction conserve.

The requested top-lifetime estimate and its limitation. Under the instructed equal-coefficient Sargent's rule scaling, Γ∝m5 and the mean lifetime is inversely proportional to that fifth power. Converting both masses to GeV gives

It corresponds to cτ≃54fm and is longer than the hadronization time, roughly a few 10−24s. Taken literally, it would allow top-flavored hadrons to form; it does not establish the physical statement that the top decays before hadronizing.
The extrapolation fails because Sargent's rule uses a low-energy Fermi interaction approximation, whereas mt​>mW​+mb​ permits t→bW with an on-shell W boson. The physical leading decay width instead scales as
