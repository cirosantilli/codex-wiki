For the neutral quark-antiquark mesons here, C=(−1)L+S. Thus C(η′)=C(π0)=+1, while C(ρ0)=C(γ)=−1. The radiative ρ0γ mode conserves charge conjugation and admits a quark-line photon-emission diagram. Both components of the supplied ρ0 wavefunction contribute: the charge-weighted flavour overlap is proportional to Qu​−Qd​=1, so it does not cancel. Its photon energy is (mη′2​−mρ2​)/(2mη′​)≃165MeV.
The π0γ mode changes charge conjugation and, more decisively, a spin-zero particle cannot decay into another spin-zero particle and one real photon. In the parent rest frame the photon has helicity ±1, while the opposite-moving pion has helicity zero; the spin-zero two-body helicity selection rule cannot be satisfied. Its on-shell two-body amplitude vanishes even if a weak interaction violates C. A virtual photon subsequently making another pair is a different final state.
For π0ρ0, angular-momentum conservation requires relative orbital angular momentum L=1. Its parity is then negative, matching the parent, but its charge-conjugation eigenvalue is Cπ​Cρ​=−1: the two distinct self-conjugate particles are not interchanged by C, so there is no additional (−1)L factor in this C eigenvalue. The decay is forbidden by strong and electromagnetic charge-conjugation conservation despite being above threshold. A weak contribution would require CP violation as well. At the strong/electromagnetic accuracy of the diagram classification,
Γ(ρ0γ)>Γ(π0ρ0)=Γ(π0γ)=0​.

If extremely small weak CP-violating amplitudes are retained, π0ρ0 need not be exactly zero; the one-real-photon spin prohibition remains exact for π0γ.
