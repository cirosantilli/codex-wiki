The nuclear quadrupole vibration has five one-phonon magnetic substates, m=−2,−1,0,1,2. Bose-Einstein statistics restrict three identical phonons to the symmetric power S3(V2​), of dimension (35+3−1​)=35. Count occupation states (n−2​,…,n2​) with ∑nm​=3 and ∑mnm​=M. For nonnegative M the counts are
MdM​​05​14​24​33​42​51​61​70​​.
(3)
Each spin-J irreducible representation contributes one state at every projection from −J to J. magnetic-substate subtraction therefore gives multiplicity dJ​−dJ+1​: one each at J=0,2,3,4,6, and none at 1,5. Thus the three-quadrupole-phonon multiplet is
S3(V2​)=V0​⊕V2​⊕V3​⊕V4​⊕V6​,JP=0+,2+,3+,4+,6+​.
(4)
The dimension check is 1+5+7+9+13=35. The parity is positive because each constituent phonon has positive parity. Pairwise angular-momentum triangle rules alone would include states incompatible with complete boson symmetrization.

a OurBigBook.com
A bare gauge boson mass would spoil the electroweak gauge structure. In the Standard Model a complex Higgs doublet instead has a symmetry-breaking potential. Choose the convention
V=−μ2Φ†Φ+λ(Φ†Φ)2,Φ=2​1​(v+h0​),v2=μ2/λ.
(5)
The Higgs mechanism rearranges three scalar components into the longitudinal polarizations of the massive W bosons and Z bosons; the unbroken electromagnetic gauge symmetry leaves the photon massless. One physical scalar excitation, the Higgs boson h, remains.[ref] Using tree-level Feynman diagrams,
mW​=gv/2,mZ​=g2+g′2​v/2,mh2​=2λv2,mf​=yf​v/2​​.
(6)
These equations use natural units. Yukawa interactions generate charged-fermion masses and Higgs couplings proportional to those masses; the minimal model does not account for observed neutrino masses. The gauge kinetic term supplies Higgs couplings to W and Z pairs. The physical Higgs exchange also cancels the otherwise excessive high-energy growth of longitudinal vector-boson scattering. The mechanism does not fix the Yukawa couplings or the Higgs self-coupling, and most proton mass arises from strong-interaction dynamics rather than directly from the light-quark Higgs masses.
The mass-generation mechanism is a statement about the field and its vacuum expectation value; the observable boson is the excitation about that vacuum.

beta decay is a weak charged current process. At quark level d→uW−∗→ue−νˉe​, with amplitude proportional to GF​Vud​ at low momentum transfer. A neutron in a nucleus can thereby become a proton:
n→pe−νˉe​,p→ne+νe​,pe−→nνe​.
(7)
The second reaction denotes an energetically allowed nuclear conversion, not a decay of an isolated free proton. In a nuclear beta transition the mass number stays fixed and the atomic number changes by one. baryon number and electric charge are conserved; the neutrino or antineutrino supplies the missing energy, momentum and angular momentum. The three-body final state produces a continuous electron spectrum rather than a single electron energy. Neglecting neutrino mass and recoil, an allowed spectrum in electron total energy is
dEe​dΓ​∝F(Z,Ee​)pe​Ee​(E0​−Ee​)2(gV2​∣MF​∣2+gA2​∣MGT​∣2).
(8)
The Fermi function accounts for the electron's Coulomb interaction with the daughter; E0​ is the electron total-energy endpoint. forbidden beta decay adds momentum-dependent nuclear shape factors. The V-A interaction violates parity: it couples left-chiral fermion fields, not an equal mixture of left and right currents.
For allowed beta decay the leading leptons have zero orbital angular momentum. Fermi transitions have ΔJ=0; Gamow-Teller transitions have ΔJ=0,1 with 0→0 excluded. Both preserve nuclear parity. Higher lepton partial waves permit forbidden transitions with extra suppression. Nuclear matrix elements and phase space, as well as these selection rules, determine lifetimes. Particle-physics examples include free-neutron decay and pion beta decay, π+→π0e+νe​. semileptonic decays test quark mixing and the universality of the weak charged current. beta decay changes quark flavour through virtual W exchange; its continuous spectrum reflects an additional lepton in the final state.

nuclear radius scaling follows approximately R=r0​A1/3 with r0​≃1.2fm for a sharp matter-radius convention. Consequently the interior nucleon density is nearly independent of mass number: nuclear forces are short range and exhibit nuclear matter saturation. A diffuse nuclear charge distribution has a charge-weighted root mean square radius, not a hard surface. For a uniform sphere, rch​=3/5​R; charge radii and matter radii need not coincide, especially with neutron skins.
Elastic electron scattering measures the nuclear charge form factor. In a simple one-photon, spherical approximation, with q a wave number,
F(q)=Ze1​∫ρch​(r)eiq⋅rd3r,F(q)=1−6q2⟨r2⟩​+⋯.
(9)
The scattering cross section relative to a point-charge prediction is proportional to ∣F∣2, so its low-q slope constrains the radius and diffraction structure constrains the radial distribution. Finite momentum coverage and Coulomb distortion in electron scattering limit reconstruction. In Robert Hofstadter's experiment a magnetic spectrometer separated elastic and inelastic electron peaks; beam-charge normalization and closed-slit background checks prevented raw count structure from being mistaken for a nuclear charge form factor.[ref]
A useful intrinsic-shape description is R(θ,ϕ)=R0​[1+∑μ=−22​α2μ​Y2μ​(θ,ϕ)+⋯]. An intrinsic nuclear quadrupole moment Q0​=∫ρch​(3z2−r2)d3r distinguishes prolate and oblate positive-charge distributions by positive and negative signs. The laboratory spectroscopic nuclear quadrupole moment is state dependent: an I=0 state has zero laboratory quadrupole moment even when its intrinsic shape is deformed. nuclear rotational bands approximately follow EI​∝I(I+1); harmonic nuclear quadrupole vibrations instead have equally spaced phonon energies and multiphonon spin multiplets. Measured electric quadrupole transitions test the collective interpretation in addition to level energies. Radius probes spatial extent; quadrupole observables and collective spectra probe shape.

A Feynman diagram specifies a term in the perturbative scattering amplitude, not a measured microscopic trajectory. Assign external spinors or polarization vectors, an interaction factor to each Feynman vertex and a propagator to each internal line; impose four-momentum conservation and integrate over independent loop momenta. Sum amplitudes for indistinguishable alternatives, then square, sum final spins and average unpolarized initial spins. A decay rate uses dΓ=∣M∣2dΦn​/(2M) in natural units; a cross section also divides by incident flux. interference means probabilities of individual diagrams must not simply be added.
The electromagnetic fermion vertex is ffˉ​γ, with coupling proportional to eQf​γμ; it preserves fermion flavour. There is no elementary three-photon or four-photon vertex in quantum electrodynamics. The weak charged current has Wℓν and Wui​dj​ vertices, with a left-chiral projector and a Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix element at the quark vertex. The weak neutral current has Zffˉ​ vertices with vector and axial couplings; it includes neutrinos and is flavour diagonal at tree level. Electroweak gauge interactions also include WWγ, WWZ and quartic WWWW, WWZZ, WWZγ and WWγγ vertices. Higgs-fermion and Higgs-vector interactions complete the mass-related scalar sector.[ref]
Quantum chromodynamics has qqˉ​g vertices proportional to gs​γμTa, plus three-gluon and four-gluon Feynman vertices because gluons carry color charge. A quark-gluon vertex preserves quark flavour. Internal particles need not be on their mass shells, but electric charge and four-momentum are conserved at each vertex. The figures below show representative leading flavour topologies; binding and hadronization are implicit, and additional strong-interaction contributions can modify hadronic amplitudes.

The weak charged current spectator topology is c→qW+∗, W+∗→uqˉ​′, with the initial uˉ a spectator. The pair quˉ forms the negative meson and uqˉ​′ the positive meson. The amplitude contains Vcq∗​Vuq′​ in one common charged-current convention; complex conjugating all factors changes no rate classification. With cC​=cosθC​ and sC​=sinθC​, the CKM magnitudes are
modeπ+K−π+π−K+K−K+π−​(q,q′)(s,d)(d,d)(s,s)(d,s)​amplitude ordercC2​sC​cC​sC​cC​sC2​​​.
(10)
Thus π+K− is favoured, both equal-flavour pairs are singly Cabibbo suppressed, and K+π− is doubly suppressed. The corresponding parametric rate hierarchy is 1:tan2θC​:tan4θC​ after normalizing to the favoured mode. Cabibbo suppression counting alone does not rank the middle two: phase space, different matrix elements and hadronic final-state interactions matter. With identical constant matrix elements, the larger pion phase space would favour π+π−, whereas experimentally K+K− is faster. The empirical decreasing order is
Γ(π+K−)>Γ(K+K−)>Γ(π+π−)>Γ(K+π−)​.
(11)
As a later cross-check, HFLAV's 2024 averages give approximately 4.00%, 0.411%, 0.149% and 0.0138% respectively. The exact middle ordering is an experimental input, not a consequence of the supplied masses and Cabibbo angle alone.[ref]

The initial strangeness is −2. The Λπ0 final state has strangeness −1, so its allowed nonleptonic amplitude is weak. One representative topology is s→uW−∗ followed by W−∗→duˉ; spectator u,s combine with the produced d into the Λ, while uuˉ contributes to the neutral pion. The available rest energy is 1314.8−1115.7−135.0=64.1MeV.
The ΛKˉ0 state would conserve strangeness and could have a strong vertex, but its threshold exceeds the parent mass by 1115.7+497.6−1314.8=298.5MeV. It is kinematically forbidden. Coupling strength cannot rescue a closed decay channel.
The radiative channel also changes strangeness. It is a weak radiative hyperon decay, not an ordinary electromagnetic decay between states of the same flavour. A representative two-quark contribution is W exchange su→ud with a photon attached to a charged line; the other allowed attachments, hadronic intermediate states and short-distance loop contributions must be included for a complete amplitude. Its extra electromagnetic coupling and hadronic dynamics suppress it relative to the nonleptonic channel. Consequently
Γ(Λπ0)≫Γ(Λγ)>Γ(ΛKˉ0)=0​.
(12)
NA48 Collaboration measured a radiative branching fraction of about 1.16×10−3, corroborating the suppression rather than a purely electromagnetic interpretation.[ref]

For the neutral quark-antiquark mesons here, C=(−1)L+S. Thus C(η′)=C(π0)=+1, while C(ρ0)=C(γ)=−1. The radiative ρ0γ mode conserves charge conjugation and admits a quark-line photon-emission diagram. Both components of the supplied ρ0 wavefunction contribute: the charge-weighted flavour overlap is proportional to Qu​−Qd​=1, so it does not cancel. Its photon energy is (mη′2​−mρ2​)/(2mη′​)≃165MeV.
The π0γ mode changes charge conjugation and, more decisively, a spin-zero particle cannot decay into another spin-zero particle and one real photon. In the parent rest frame the photon has helicity ±1, while the opposite-moving pion has helicity zero; the spin-zero two-body helicity selection rule cannot be satisfied. Its on-shell two-body amplitude vanishes even if a weak interaction violates C. A virtual photon subsequently making another pair is a different final state.
For π0ρ0, angular-momentum conservation requires relative orbital angular momentum L=1. Its parity is then negative, matching the parent, but its charge-conjugation eigenvalue is Cπ​Cρ​=−1: the two distinct self-conjugate particles are not interchanged by C, so there is no additional (−1)L factor in this C eigenvalue. The decay is forbidden by strong and electromagnetic charge-conjugation conservation despite being above threshold. A weak contribution would require CP violation as well. At the strong/electromagnetic accuracy of the diagram classification,
Γ(ρ0γ)>Γ(π0ρ0)=Γ(π0γ)=0​.
(13)
If extremely small weak CP-violating amplitudes are retained, π0ρ0 need not be exactly zero; the one-real-photon spin prohibition remains exact for π0γ.

For electron-positron annihilation into a muon pair the gauge diagrams are s-channel photon and Z exchange. For electron-positron annihilation into electron neutrinos there is s-channel Z exchange and t-channel W exchange, with interference. For electron-positron annihilation into W-boson pairs there are s-channel photon and Z diagrams and a t-channel electron-neutrino diagram. All three gauge amplitudes are required for the proper high-energy cancellation. Electron-Yukawa-suppressed s-channel Higgs diagrams can also produce the massive muon or W pair; they vanish in the usual zero-electron-mass approximation used for the leading gauge comparison.
There is no energy-independent ordering. For unpolarized beams and inclusive Born cross sections the useful regimes are:
  • Below the muon threshold only the neutrino channel is open, ignoring its tiny mass threshold. Immediately above 2mμ​, muon phase space initially vanishes.
  • Well above 2mμ​ but well below the weak-boson masses, photon exchange gives σμμ​≃4πα2/(3s), whereas σνe​νˉe​​=O(GF2​s). Hence the muon channel dominates and real W-pair production is closed.
  • Near the Z pole, W pairs are still closed and the Z resonance dominates both open channels. The ratio is approximately Γ(Z→νe​νˉe​)/Γ(Z→μ+μ−)≃2, from their neutral-current couplings: neutrinos rank above muons. This is for one neutrino flavour.
  • At s​=2mW​≃160.806GeV the on-shell W-pair channel opens from zero phase space. Its position in the ordering changes across threshold and must be obtained from the summed amplitudes, rather than inferred solely from a weak/electromagnetic label.
  • Far above the weak masses, forward t-channel W exchange makes the inclusive electron-neutrino cross section approach a nonzero scale of order g4/mW2​. The W-pair cross section behaves as O[g4log(s/mW2​)/s] and the muon cross section as O(g4/s). Thus asymptotically the decreasing order is νe​νˉe​, then W+W−, then μ+μ−. Fixed angular cuts, beam spin polarization and radiative corrections modify these comparisons.
The Z-pole ratio follows [21​]2/[(−21​+sin2θW​)2+sin4θW​]≃1.99. Treating every weak reaction as uniformly slower misses both the resonance and forward exchange.

The partial decay width Γx​ is the energy width associated with decay of the compound nucleus back into the entrance channel x+X; by time reversal it also controls formation from that channel. The partial decay width Γy​ describes the selected exit channel y+Y. The total decay width sums all energetically accessible channels, including these: Γ=i∑​Γi​=ℏ/τ​. The branching fraction for channel i is Γi​/Γ. Widths have energy units, whereas Γi​/ℏ is a decay probability per unit time. The isolated-resonance formula assumes approximately constant widths over the resonance and neglects a nonresonant interfering background.

An unstable isolated level has survival amplitude a(t)=θ(t)e−iE0​t/ℏe−Γt/(2ℏ), because its survival probability falls as e−Γt/ℏ. Its energy-domain amplitude is proportional to
∫0∞​ei(E−E0​)t/ℏe−Γt/(2ℏ)dt=Γ/2−i(E−E0​)ℏ​.
(16)

(E−E0​)2+(Γ/2)2​.
(17)
At E−E0​=±Γ/2 the resonant factor is half its peak, so Γ is the full width at half maximum. exponential decay is the isolated-level approximation; energy-dependent thresholds or overlapping levels need a more general lineshape.

Unpolarized projectile and target spins supply (2Jx​+1)(2JX​+1) equally weighted spin orientations. An isolated compound-nucleus level of spin J has 2J+1 degenerate magnetic substates. Sum over these resonance substates and average over the entrance spins to obtain
g=(2Jx​+1)(2JX​+1)2J+1​​.
(18)
This resonance spin statistical factor is a statistical spin weight, not always a probability smaller than one: orbital angular momentum also participates in forming the resonance. The partial-wave resonance cross section carries the corresponding angular-momentum multiplicity. Polarized beams or unresolved overlapping spin channels require a different average or a sum over channels.
