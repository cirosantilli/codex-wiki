Read the line styles and fermion-number arrows in the original PDF: a wavy line is a photon, and a coiled line is a gluon. The valid fundamental interaction vertices are
QED: (a), (f).QCD: (b), (g).​
(5)
Both (a) and (f) have one electron-line arrow entering and one leaving a photon vertex; changing the drawing's orientation does not change the quantum electrodynamics interaction. The Quantum chromodynamics vertices (b) and (g) are the cubic and quartic gluon self-interactions.
The remaining cases fail for different reasons. Diagram (c) couples an isolated electron line to gluons and is not an allowed fermion vertex. Diagram (d) changes an electron to a muon at a photon vertex, violating the flavour-diagonal electromagnetic coupling. Diagram (e) couples a colourless electron to a gluon. Diagram (h) changes an up quark into a down quark at a gluon vertex, whereas Quantum chromodynamics preserves quark flavour; that assignment also fails electric charge conservation.
One measurement of the strong coupling constant uses the fraction of resolved three-jet events in e+e−→qqˉ​g: with a fixed jet-resolution definition, its leading ratio to two-jet production is proportional to αs​, with calculable higher-order corrections.
The intermediate current is γ∗ or Z∗, and the extra gluon can be emitted from either the quark or the antiquark. Both Feynman diagrams contribute coherently. The observed jets arise from hadronization; a resolved-jet definition is needed because arbitrarily soft or collinear radiation is not a separate measurable third jet.

a OurBigBook.com
Parity is full spatial reflection, x↦−x, represented by a parity operator P. A polar vector, such as momentum, reverses, whereas an axial vector, such as spin, does not. A state of definite parity has P∣ψ⟩=ηP​∣ψ⟩, with ηP​=±1 in the usual convention. A multiparticle state's parity includes both the product of its intrinsic parities and its orbital spatial factor, for example (−1)L for relative orbital angular momentum L.
An interaction conserves parity when its dynamics are invariant under this transformation, equivalently [H,P]=0 for a time-independent Hamiltonian. Parity violation means that a process and its fully reflected counterpart need not have the same probability. It is more than a left-right asymmetry in an unreflected laboratory apparatus.
The electromagnetic and strong sectors of the Standard Model conserve parity to the accuracy relevant here, taking the strong-sector Yang-Mills theta term to vanish. The weak charged current selects left-chiral fermions and right-chiral antifermions, as defined by fermion chirality; the reflected right-chiral charged current is not equally present. The neutral weak current also generally has different left- and right-chiral couplings. Consequently the weak sector violates parity; this does not mean that every individual weak decay must have opposite initial and final parity. For a massive particle, helicity and fermion chirality should be distinguished.

In the Wu experiment, Chien-Shiung Wu and collaborators studied beta-minus decay of polarized cobalt-60,
2760​Co⟶2860​Ni∗+e−+νˉe​.
(9)
A thin cobalt-60 layer on a cerium magnesium nitrate crystal was cooled by adiabatic demagnetization to a few millikelvin. A polarizing magnetic field produced nuclear polarization. An anthracene scintillation counter and photomultiplier detected the emitted electrons; angular gamma ray observations from the daughter de-excitation monitored whether the nuclei remained polarized.[ref]
The electrons were emitted preferentially opposite the parent nuclear spin. Reversing the nuclear polarization reversed the asymmetry, while warming the sample removed both polarization and the effect. Those controls distinguished a spin-correlated asymmetry from a fixed detector bias.
The relevant angular correlation is a polarized beta-decay angular distribution of the form
W(θ)∝1+Aβ​Pcv​cosθ,cosθ=J⋅p​e​.
(10)
Under parity, pe​→−pe​ but J→J, so the spin-momentum correlation changes sign. Parity conservation would require equal rates for these two directions relative to the same spin. The observed nonzero coefficient therefore demonstrated parity violation in beta decay. An unpolarized source would average this correlation to zero and could not provide the same test.

For both modes, a leading weak quark transition in K+=usˉ is sˉ→uˉ+W+, followed by W+→udˉ, with the original u acting as a spectator quark. This produces the four quarks needed for π+π0, using the uuˉ component of π0. Producing three pions additionally requires a ddˉ pair from strong hadronization. A representative quark-line diagram draws it as gluon emission and splitting.
The three-pion assignment is udˉ, udˉ, and duˉ, matching π+π+π−. The extra soft pair creation is a schematic representation of hadronization, not a controlled perturbative prediction of the relative decay rates. Both processes are first order in the effective weak interaction.
There is an explicit error or hypothetical assumption to address: the original PDF assigns 0+ to all these mesons. Taken literally, the two-pion final state has L=0, because all particles have zero spin and the initial J is zero. Its parity is (+1)2(−1)0=+1. For three spinless particles, use two Jacobi orbital angular momentum quantum numbers l,L. Coupling to total zero requires l=L, so the orbital parity (−1)l+L is +1 and the three-particle parity is also +1. Thus under the printed 0+ assignment, both channels conserve parity.
The named physical pions and kaons are instead pseudoscalar mesons with JP=0−. With those physical assignments,
P2π​=(−1)2(−1)0=+1,P3π​=(−1)3(−1)l+L=−1,PK​=−1.
(11)
Therefore
Physical mesons: K+→2π violates parity; K+→3π preserves parity.​
(12)
These two opposite-parity final states from the same physical kaon are a classic illustration of parity violation in a weak decay. The physical conclusion requires correcting the printed parity assignment explicitly.

A spin-zero pion has no preferred emission direction. Choose the antineutrino momentum along +z. A massless antineutrino has positive helicity, so its spin points along +z. The muon moves along −z, and total Jz​=0 requires its spin to point along −z:

Use a high-energy proton beam on a target to produce secondary pions, then select and focus the negative ones. A magnetic horn and a charge-selecting dipole system remove positive pions, limiting wrong-sign νμ​ contamination. Select a narrow range of magnetic rigidity; if additional species rejection is available, velocity or mass discrimination reduces negative kaon contamination at the same momentum.
The selected π− travel through an evacuated decay tunnel and undergo π−→μ−νˉμ​. Their forward Lorentz boost concentrates the antineutrinos within an angle of order 1/γπ​. Choose a tunnel long enough for substantial pion decay but short compared with the boosted muon decay length. Since τμ​≃2.2μs is much longer than τπ​≃26ns, this reduces secondary μ−→e−νˉe​νμ​ contamination.
Downstream magnets sweep the remaining charged particles into absorbers, and shielding stops residual hadrons and muons. The antineutrino beam continues straight through. Parent-particle collimation, charge selection, and a forward detector improve purity; neutral-kaon decays, remaining wrong-sign parents, and muon decays prevent perfect purity. Neutrinos themselves cannot be focused or charge-selected by these magnets. Neutrinos produced after stopped-muon decay or capture are much less forward and can be reduced by directional and energy selection.

There are three free-particle rest-energy terms and five nuclear corrections. In the paper's natural units, their meanings are:
  • Zmp​: the rest energy of Z free protons.
  • Zme​: the rest energy of the atomic electrons; including this term makes the result an atomic, rather than bare nuclear, mass. Small atomic binding energies are neglected.
  • (A−Z)mn​: the rest energy of the free neutrons.
  • a1​A, with a1​<0: the attractive volume term. The short-range nuclear force mainly binds each nucleon to nearby neighbours, so the leading attraction is extensive.
  • a2​A2/3>0: the surface correction. Surface nucleons have fewer neighbours and are less bound; surface area grows as A2/3 at approximately constant nuclear density.
  • a3​Z2/A1/3>0: the nuclear Coulomb energy cost of repelling protons, scaling as Z2/R with nuclear radius R∝A1/3.
  • a4​(A−2Z)2/A>0: the nuclear symmetry energy, penalizing unequal neutron and proton populations. The Pauli exclusion principle forces an excess species into higher single-particle states; strong-interaction effects also contribute.
  • δ(A)/A1/2: nuclear pairing. It lowers the mass for even-even nuclei, raises it for odd-odd nuclei, and vanishes for odd A in the stated model.
The sign convention is significant: the quantity labeled Ebind​ in the supplied mass formula is added to the free-particle masses and is normally negative for a bound nucleus. It is the negative of the conventional positive nuclear binding energy B:
M=Zmp​+Zme​+(A−Z)mn​+Ebind​,B=−Ebind​>0.​
(29)
The coefficients must therefore retain their printed signs; using positive B while still adding it to the masses would be inconsistent.

Thus the inferred potassium-argon dating age is about 2.5 billion years, consistent with the proposed 2.5×109-year age to the accuracy of the supplied inputs. Omitting the branching fraction would seriously underestimate the age. At exactly 2.5 billion years, the model predicts Ar​/K≃0.307, consistent with the rounded measured 0.3.
This dates closure of the mineral to argon loss and potassium exchange under the assumptions, not necessarily its original crystallization or the first formation of the Earth's crust. The depth alone is not an age measurement. No uncertainty or argon-retention history is supplied, so the agreement is a consistency check rather than proof of a particular geological event.
