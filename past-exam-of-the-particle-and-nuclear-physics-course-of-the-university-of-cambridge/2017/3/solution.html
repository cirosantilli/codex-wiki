Alpha lifetimes and competing beta decays. The Geiger-Nuttall law follows approximately from quantum tunnelling through the daughter’s Coulomb barrier. Increasing Qα​ shortens the barrier and greatly increases the decay rate; increasing the daughter charge makes the barrier harder to penetrate. A useful local parametrization is
log10​yTα​​≃aQα​/MeV​Zd​​+b,Zd​=Z−2.

a=88(1/4.07063​−1/5.51445​)log10​(1.4×1010/1.9)​≃1.606,b≃−59.92.

This gives the order-of-magnitude estimates
Tα​(228Ra)Tα​(228Ac)​∼4×108y,∼5×104y,​λα​λα​​∼6×10−17s−1,∼4×10−13s−1.​​
(18)
Here λα​=ln2/Tα​. The known beta-minus decay rates are approximately 3.8×10−9s−1 and 3.2×10−5s−1, respectively. Thus the reference alpha branching fractions are only about 1.6×10−8 and 1.4×10−8. Beta decay overwhelmingly removes the parents before alpha emission, explaining why the alpha branches are not seen in the stated chain.
These estimates are local extrapolations, not precision predictions. In particular, actinium is odd-odd, so extrapolating an even-even Geiger-Nuttall law neglects nuclear-structure and angular-momentum hindrance. Such effects can further suppress its alpha branch.
Allowed daughter spin and parity. The even-even parent in its ground state and the alpha particle both have JP=0+. The total final angular momentum must be zero, so coupling daughter spin JY​ to relative orbital momentum L requires JY​=L. Parity conservation gives +1=πY​(+1)(−1)L. The alpha-decay angular-momentum and parity selection rule is therefore
JYP​=0+,1−,2+,3−,4+,….​
(19)
These are the natural parity states. Restricting the list to even spin would be too strong; even-spin rotational bands are a further structural specialization.
Ground-state line and recoil. The nuclear alpha-decay recoil correction gives
Tα​=Qα​Md​+mα​Md​​.
(20)
For the thorium ground-state decay, this predicts Tα​≃4.0004MeV, with daughter recoil about 70.2keV. This is close to the highest measured line, 4.0112MeV: the difference is 10.8keV, or 0.27%. Conversely that line implies Qα​≃4.0816MeV. The quoted nuclear masses have only five decimal places in atomic mass units; rounding three such entries can shift their mass difference by up to about 14keV. Thus the requested approximate agreement is supported, but the rounded table does not reproduce the line energy exactly. Dropping daughter recoil would give an error around 70keV instead.
Excited levels. Relative to the highest line, the two alpha-energy deficits are 62.7 and 201.2keV. Multiplying by the common recoil factor 1+mα​/Md​≃232/228 gives
Ex,1​≃63.8keV,Ex,2​≃204.7keV,Ex,1​Ex,2​​=3.209.​
(21)
A deformed even-even nucleus has a ground-state nuclear rotational band with EJ​≃J(J+1)/(2J) and JP=0+,2+,4+,…. Its 4+ to 2+ excitation ratio is 20/6=10/3=3.333, with the observed ratio about 3.7% below this ideal value. The likely assignments are therefore 2+ and 4+ rotational excitations, with modest departures from a rigid constant-inertia rotor. The ratio supports this interpretation; it is not a unique spin measurement by itself.

λβ​(Ra)λβ​(Ac)​≃(0.0465752.12381​)5=1.97×108.
(23)
The measured rate ratio is the inverse half-life ratio,
λβ​(Ra)λβ​(Ac)​=6.15.75×365.25×24​=8.26×103.​
(24)
The naive fifth-power estimate is about 2.4×104 times larger. It correctly predicts the much faster actinium decay but fails quantitatively. The small radium endpoint is far below the electron rest mass, violating the negligible-mass approximation behind the fifth power; Coulomb distortion of the emitted electron, different nuclear matrix elements and different daughter excitations also matter. The rounded mass difference makes the very small radium Q especially uncertain. The appropriate comparison of individual branches uses the actual endpoint-dependent statistical factor and comparative half-life, not a universal fifth power of the ground-state release.
Beta selection rules and the four observed branches. The radium parent has 0+, so the ground-state 0+→3+ transition is a unique forbidden beta decay of second order: it preserves parity and requires three units of angular momentum. For the increasing daughter excitation energies, the endpoints decrease. The assignments are

(25)
For 0+→1+, zero lepton orbital angular momentum and lepton-pair spin one give an allowed beta decay of Gamow-Teller transition type. The 0+→1− branches need odd lepton orbital angular momentum, with lowest value one, and are forbidden beta decays of first order; they are not unique because they do not have the maximal transfer ΔJ=2 at that order.
The absence of a listed ground-state branch is consistent with its stronger forbiddenness. The two allowed branches carry 70% in total, and the nearly equal-endpoint pair favours the allowed branch by four to one. These trends support the classification. However, first-forbidden branches still carry 30%, and the lower-endpoint first-forbidden branch is stronger than the higher-endpoint one. Thus selection-rule labels alone do not explain all intensities: nuclear overlaps and the endpoint-dependent phase space are indispensable. “Forbidden” means suppressed at leading order, not impossible.
