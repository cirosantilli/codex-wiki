Evidence for quarks comes from several kinds of observation that test different aspects of their interpretation. The light meson and baryon spectra organize into approximate flavour multiplets with the charges and strangeness expected for qqˉ​ and qqq. In particular, the baryon octet and baryon decuplet encode the three light quark flavours and their spin couplings. This is an economical account of hadron quantum numbers, but spectroscopy alone would not establish that the constituents behave as localized scattering centers.
Deep inelastic scattering supplies that second test. In energetic electron–proton scattering the outgoing lepton measures the energy and momentum transferred through a virtual photon, while the broken-up target is summed over. Approximate Bjorken scaling means the structure functions depend primarily on x=Q2/(2P⋅q) rather than varying rapidly with the resolving momentum scale Q2. It indicates nearly pointlike constituents. The charge-squared weights and the second relation test the constituents' fractional charges and spin-half nature. Scaling violations reflect quark and gluon radiation rather than a failure of the constituent picture.[ref]
Particle jets give complementary final-state evidence. A high-energy quark produces a collimated spray of hadrons because confinement prevents it from emerging as an isolated particle. Back-to-back jets fit quark–antiquark production; three-jet events show hard gluon radiation. Their angular correlations test a spin-one radiated parton. This distinguishes constituents of hadrons from a literal observation of free fractional-charge particles.
Three independent arguments identify an additional threefold colour degree of freedom. First, the spin-aligned uuu state of a ground-state Δ++ would have symmetric space, spin and flavour factors. The Pauli exclusion principle requires an antisymmetric additional factor. The three-quark colour singlet supplies it. The same construction accommodates the spin-aligned sss state of the Ω− without altering its measured quantum numbers.

Each quark flavour has Nc​ distinguishable colour final states. With Nc​=3, the predicted plateaux for three, four and five accessible flavours are 2, 10/3 and 11/3; without the factor three they would be a third as large. The measured inclusive normalization is therefore a colour-counting test, with radiative and threshold corrections included in precision comparisons.[ref]
Third, the neutral pion's two-photon amplitude contains a quark-loop colour sum, proportional to Nc​(eu2​−ed2​); its rate and the other evidence are consistent with three colours. Quantum chromodynamics identifies this degree of freedom as a gauge charge, with eight gluons. Observed hadrons are colour singlets; the evidence for colour is its quantitative and symmetry consequences, not visible colours or isolated coloured quarks.
