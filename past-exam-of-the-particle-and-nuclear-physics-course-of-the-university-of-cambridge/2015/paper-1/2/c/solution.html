The deuteron is the bound proton–neutron system. Its ground state has JP=1+ and isospin T=0, with binding energy approximately 2.225 MeV and no excited bound state. The small binding compared with characteristic nuclear energy scales produces an extended tail. With reduced mass μ≃mN​/2, the asymptotic radial wavefunction decays as e−κr, where
κ=ℏ2μB​​,κ−1≃4.3 fm.
(11)
The decay length is not a sharp deuteron radius or the force range. It illustrates weak binding outside the region of strong attraction.
The observed total spin is consistent with aligned nucleon spins, S=1, and a predominantly L=0 spatial state, 3S1​. A central attraction identical in both spin–isospin channels would not distinguish this channel from a spin-singlet proton–neutron channel. In fact the triplet channel binds while the corresponding singlet channel does not. A realistic nuclear force must therefore distinguish these spin–isospin channels, for example through a term Vσ​(r)σp​⋅σn​, whose eigenvalues are +1 and −3 in triplet and singlet states. The absence of a bound dineutron and diproton reinforces the need to distinguish channels; proton–proton Coulomb repulsion is an additional effect, not a complete explanation of the singlet nuclear interaction.
The clearest evidence against a purely central force is the nonzero electric quadrupole moment. A pure isotropic S-wave charge distribution would give zero. The deuteron quadrupole evidence for a tensor force instead points to a nuclear tensor force,
VT​(r)S12​,S12​=3(σp​⋅r)(σn​⋅r)−σp​⋅σn​.
(12)
It depends on how the spins point relative to the line joining the particles. It preserves total J but couples orbital angular momenta differing by two, producing
∣d⟩=ru(r)​∣3S1​⟩+rw(r)​∣3D1​⟩.
(13)
Both terms have JP=1+, and their interference permits a quadrupole moment. The 1939 theoretical analysis explicitly connected the measured quadrupole moment with such directional dependence.[ref]
The nuclear magnetic moment provides a second check. A pure aligned-spin S wave would give approximately μp​+μn​≃0.880μN​, close to but larger than the observed deuteron value approximately 0.857μN​. The discrepancy is consistent with non-S-wave structure and interaction-current corrections; it is not by itself a model-independent measurement of a D-state probability. The deuteron D-state admixture depends on the adopted Hamiltonian and electromagnetic current. The magnetic moments and mass differences used for these scales are tabulated physical constants.[ref]
A useful schematic interaction therefore contains central, spin-dependent and tensor terms, and possibly spin–orbit terms in other channels. Long-range one-pion exchange naturally supplies a tensor component, with its finite range set by the pion mass. The deuteron establishes an attractive triplet channel and a noncentral tensor interaction; a spin-independent central potential is insufficient.
