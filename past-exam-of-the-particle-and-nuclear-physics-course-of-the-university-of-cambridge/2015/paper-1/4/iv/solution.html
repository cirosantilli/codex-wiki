The bracketed diagram energies are nuclear, so subtract one electron rest energy from each parent-to-daughter energy difference. The three kinetic-energy endpoints are
QN​=9.190−0.511=8.679 MeV,QNe​=16.26−2.25−0.511=13.499 MeV,QF​=2.25−0.511=1.739 MeV.
(32)
The observed partial-rate ratios are λN​/λNe​=0.19672 and λN​/λF​=0.24918.
To obtain the simple Sargent-law estimates, define an effective strength by λ=C∣Meff​∣2Q5, using the same normalization C. Then
∣Meff,Ne​∣2∣Meff,N​∣2​≃0.19672(8.67913.499​)5=1.79,​
(33)
∣Meff,F​∣2∣Meff,N​∣2​≃0.24918(8.6791.739​)5=8.05×10−5.​
(34)
Thus the two forbidden branches have effective strengths of the same broad order, whereas the N ground-state branch is highly suppressed relative to the allowed F branch after this approximate fifth-power kinematic scaling. The first comparison involves mirror transitions with related nuclear configurations; order-of-magnitude similarity is reasonable, without expecting exact equality from this estimate.
These effective strengths must not be confused with precisely extracted microscopic reduced nuclear matrix elements. Both N and Ne ground-state branches are first-forbidden unique, for which the first-forbidden unique beta-decay phase space contains an additional factor proportional to pe2​+Eν2​. In the massless, no-Coulomb limit this gives Q7, so using the same microscopic unique-forbidden normalization for those two branches instead yields
∣MU,Ne​∣2∣MU,N​∣2​≃0.19672(8.67913.499​)7=4.33.​
(35)
Comparing a unique-forbidden reduced operator directly with an allowed reduced operator also requires a convention for its powers of length and momentum. The number 8.05×10−5 is consequently an effective Sargent-normalized suppression, not a universal dimensionless ratio between different operator ranks. Electron-mass and opposite-sign Coulomb corrections matter, particularly for the low-energy F branch. The data justify approximate strength comparisons; they do not fix precision nuclear matrix elements without these extra ingredients.
