The mirror nuclei have interchanged neutron and proton numbers. Neglect their strong-interaction binding difference and describe them with the same uniformly charged sharp sphere of radius R. The nuclear Coulomb energy is
EC​(Z)=53​4πϵ0​RZ2e2​=53​RZ2αℏc​.
(26)
Changing one neutron into a proton decreases the nucleon rest-mass sum by mn​−mp​=1.294 MeV/c2, but raises the Coulomb energy. The diagram gives (MF​−MO​)c2=2.25 MeV for nuclear masses, so
2.25=−1.294+53​(92−82)Rαℏc​.
(27)
With the standard electromagnetic conversion αℏc≃1.44 MeVfm, the uniform-sphere Coulomb displacement energy estimate is
R=3.544(3/5)(17)(1.44)​ fm≃4.14 fm.​
(28)
This R is the uniform sphere's outer radius. If “charge radius” means the rms nuclear charge radius, the same distribution gives
⟨r2⟩​=3/5​R≃3.21 fm.​
(29)
The numerical result is an estimate within the charge-density model, not a model-independent radius measurement. A discrete-proton correction replaces Z2 by Z(Z−1), giving Δ[Z(Z−1)]=16 and R≃3.90 fm. The main answer above follows the literal continuum charged-sphere model. Strong isospin-breaking, nonuniform density and changes in the valence-nucleon distribution also limit the extraction.
