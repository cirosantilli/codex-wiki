The state marked X in F is the 1/2− analogue at 11.20 MeV excitation. Its absolute nuclear energy relative to O is 2.25+11.20=13.45 MeV. Hence
QX​=16.26−13.45−0.511=2.299 MeV.​
(36)
It is a same-spin, same-parity allowed beta decay between isobaric analog states. The large analogue Fermi overlap makes it a superallowed analogue transition, in the broad analogue-state terminology. Since J=1/2 rather than zero, this classification does not exclude a Gamow-Teller contribution.
For a transparent numerical estimate, take the Fermi part to dominate both this branch and the reference F-ground-state decay, neglect Coulomb and electron-mass corrections in their phase-space ratio, and use the Sargent rule. For Tz​=(N−Z)/2, Ne has T=Tzmagnitude​=3/2 and goes from Tz​=−3/2 to −1/2 in its analogue multiplet. The isospin ladder strength in analogue beta decay is therefore three. The F→O ground-state mirror transition in T=1/2 has Fermi strength one. Thus the analogue beta-decay branching estimate gives
λX​≃95 s3​(1.7392.299​)5=0.128 s−1,BX​≃(0.15 s)λX​=0.0191≃1.9%.​
(37)
This percent-level result is an estimate under the stated assumptions, not a unique consequence of the level diagram. More generally, if f denotes an allowed phase-space integral and S=gV2​∣MF​∣2+gA2​∣MGT​∣2, then
BX​=τF​τNe​​fF​fX​​SF​SX​​.
(38)
The diagram fixes the Fermi strengths but not both axial strengths. Including them and proper Positron phase-space functions changes the numerical result. Assuming equal total allowed strengths instead of the Fermi-only ratio three would give approximately 0.64%; that alternative makes a different nuclear-structure assumption. The rate enhancement from removing the forbiddenness explains why the X branch can be appreciable despite its much smaller endpoint than the Ne ground-state branch.
