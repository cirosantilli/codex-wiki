In the Breit-Wigner resonance cross section, E is the total centre-of-momentum energy, E0​ the resonance energy, and p either incident particle's center-of-momentum momentum magnitude. The total decay width is Γ=ℏ/τ, while Γi​ and Γf​ are partial widths into the entrance and specified exit channels. Their ratios to Γ are the corresponding branching fractions. The factor g averages over incident spin states and sums over the resonance's magnetic substates.
In natural units, ℏ=c=1, the resonant partial-wave cross-sectional scale is an area proportional to the squared reduced de Broglie wavelength, 1/p2. Equivalently a partial-wave scattering amplitude carries one factor 1/p, and its squared modulus supplies 1/p2. In SI area units the same scale is (ℏ/p)2; if p is expressed in energy units, multiply the final inverse-energy-squared answer by (ℏc)2.
For unpolarized spin-half incident muons, the unpolarized resonance spin factor is
g=(2sμ−​+1)(2sμ+​+1)2J+1​=42J+1​.
(14)
Hence gZ​=3/4 and gH​=1/4. The outgoing tau spins do not supply another independent multiplicity here: their allowed final states are already included in Γf​.
Near a narrow resonance, treat the smooth prefactor p−2 and the partial widths as constant across the line. At the peak the denominator is Γ2/4. Half its height occurs when (E−E0​)2+Γ2/4=Γ2/2, namely at E=E0​±Γ/2. Thus the full width at half maximum is Γ within the Breit-Wigner narrow-line approximation. A varying prefactor or convolution with the beam-energy distribution can modify an observed line shape.
The production amplitude has a muon pair joined to a Higgs propagator through their Yukawa coupling, followed by the bottom-quark pair at a second Yukawa vertex. The separate H→bbˉ graph is a tree-level Higgs–fermion vertex. There are no tree-level Hgg or Hγγ vertices in the renormalizable Standard Model: their lowest nonzero graphs are loops. A quark triangle, dominated by the top contribution, produces two gluons. Charged-fermion triangles contribute to two photons; charged-W loops also contribute and are dominant in the Standard Model amplitude. Photon permutations and the W contact topology belong to the full amplitude; the diagram shows these topologies rather than claiming one triangle alone is the entire gauge-invariant W contribution.[ref]
