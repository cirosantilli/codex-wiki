A ground-state baryon contains three quarks in a spatially symmetric L=0 wavefunction, giving positive parity. Each quark is a spin one-half fermion, so the complete wavefunction must be antisymmetric under interchange of its complete quark labels. A physical baryon is a colour singlet, with the antisymmetric colour factor
ψcolour​∝ϵabc​∣abc⟩.
(26)
Consequently the combined spin-and-flavour factor must be symmetric. The product notation in the question organizes the degrees of freedom; it need not mean that spin and flavour are separately unentangled. In particular the octet requires a correlated sum of products.
For the three light flavours, the flavour symmetry decomposition is
3⊗3⊗3=10S​⊕8M​⊕8M​⊕1A​.
(27)
The subscripts describe permutation symmetry, with M denoting mixed symmetry. The addition of angular momentum of three spin one-half particles gives
2⊗2⊗2=4S​⊕2M​⊕2M​.
(28)
The symmetric spin quartet has total J=3/2; the two spin doublets have J=1/2.
For J=3/2, the spin factor is symmetric, so the flavour factor must also be symmetric: this gives the spin-3/2 baryon decuplet. Its flavour content is four Δ states, three Σ∗ states, two Ξ∗ states, and one Ω state.
For J=1/2, the mixed-symmetry spin functions combine with the mixed-symmetry flavour functions to make one fully symmetric spin-flavour factor for each octet weight: this gives the spin-1/2 baryon octet, containing two nucleons, three Sigma baryons, one Lambda baryon, and two Xi baryons. The two mixed-symmetry octets in the raw flavour product are not two distinct ground-state octets; their permutation partners are correlated with the spin factors. An antisymmetric flavour singlet would require a fully antisymmetric spin factor to make a symmetric product, but three spin states drawn from a two-dimensional single-quark spin space have no such factor.
Equivalently, the symmetric three-particle representation of spin-flavour symmetry has dimension (38​)=56 and decomposes under flavour and spin into
56S​=(10,4)⊕(8,2),56=10×4+8×2.​
(29)
These are approximate multiplets: the larger strange quark mass breaks exact flavour symmetry and splits their energies.
