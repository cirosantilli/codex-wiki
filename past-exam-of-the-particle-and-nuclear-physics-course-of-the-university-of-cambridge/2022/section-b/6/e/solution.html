The table gives the Xi baryon mean lifetime τΞ0​=2.90×10−10s. At t1​=10−12s, an initially resting particle has survival probability
P(Ξ0 survives)=e−t1​/τΞ0​=0.99656.
(52)
Thus the most probable contents are one undecayed Ξ0. The initial momentum is unspecified. For a moving parent, time dilation replaces the denominator by γΞ​τΞ0​, making the survival probability at least as large. The times below are characteristic proper mean lifetimes; laboratory times depend on the particle velocities.
The principal first step is
Ξ0→Λπ0,BΞ​=0.99524.
(53)
It typically occurs on the 10−10s scale. The neutral pion almost immediately converts to two photons, with τπ0​=8.43×10−17s and Bγγ​=0.98823. The Lambda baryon has τΛ​=2.632×10−10s and two dominant routes.
In the charged-pion route,
Ξ0Λπ−μ−​→Λ+π0,π0→2γ,→p+π−,B=0.639,→μ−+νˉμ​,τπ−​=2.6033×10−8s,→e−+νˉe​+νμ​,τμ​≃2.197×10−6s.​
(54)
The muon lifetime is a standard additional input, not present in the supplied five table pages.[ref] The successive contents are approximately Λ+2γ, then p+π−+2γ, then p+μ−+νˉμ​+2γ. After a few muon lifetimes the dominant stable endpoint is
p+e−+νˉe​+νμ​+νˉμ​+2γ.​
(55)
The leading-path probability including the tabulated two-photon pion branch is
PA​≃(0.99524)(0.639)(0.98823)(0.999877)=0.6284.
(56)
Here the last factor is the tabulated dominant muonic charged-pion fraction; rare radiative corrections are being neglected in describing a fixed photon count. Before selecting pion decay channels, the probability of reaching the pπ−π0 history is 0.99524×0.639=0.63596.
In the neutral-pion route,
Ξ0→Λπ0,Λ→nπ0(B=0.358),π0π0→4γ.
(57)
This produces n+4γ by roughly the nanosecond scale. It is a long-lived intermediate state, not the final endpoint: the free neutron has τn​=879.4s in the original table and eventually undergoes beta-minus decay,
n→p+e−+νˉe​.
(58)
Thus after several thousand seconds the dominant stable endpoint is
p+e−+νˉe​+4γ,PB​≃(0.99524)(0.358)(0.98823)2=0.3480.​
(59)
Before selecting the two π0→2γ decays, the nπ0π0 history has probability 0.99524×0.358=0.35630. The familiar rough description is therefore a 64% charged-pion route and a 36% neutron route; including the specified leading pion branches gives approximately 62.8% and 34.8% for the two displayed endpoints. The remainder includes Dalitz decays and other rare channels, not a failure of probability conservation.
These are particle decay cascades, treating the separated final proton, electron, photons and neutrinos as stable asymptotic particles and neglecting subsequent collisions and rare extra radiation. An infinitely large box prevents escaping particles from being removed from the inventory. Neutrino labels specify their production flavours. Each lifetime describes a stochastic waiting time, so there is no finite sharp instant at which every unstable particle has certainly decayed.
