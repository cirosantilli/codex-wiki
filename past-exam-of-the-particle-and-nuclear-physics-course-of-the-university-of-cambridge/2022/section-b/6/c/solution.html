Both formal processes use one weak charged current transition, with a virtual W boson producing the lepton pair. Two representative lowest-order quark-line diagrams are shown below. They use the ddˉ component of π0; attachment to the antiquark line in the uuˉ component gives the other contribution to the same hadronic current.
In the first diagram, d→u+W− and W−→e−νˉe​, with dˉ a spectator quark. In the second, u→d+W+ and W+→e+νe​, again with a spectator dˉ. The diagram for the first process describes the coupling but cannot make an energetically forbidden on-shell decay occur.
From the original tables,
mπ0​=134.9768MeV,mπ+​=139.57039MeV,me​≃0.510999MeV.
(45)
Therefore the first process has Q value
Q(π0→π+e−νˉe​)=mπ0​−mπ+​−me​=−5.10459MeV<0,
(46)
and is forbidden for an isolated pion. The second has
Q(π+→π0e+νe​)=mπ+​−mπ0​−me​=4.08259MeV>0.
(47)
The table therefore lists only the second, pion beta decay, with
B(π+→π0e+νe​)=(1.036±0.006)×10−8.​
(48)
Its tiny branching fraction combines a weak interaction with an exceptionally small available energy. An allowed semileptonic transition has a decay width scaling approximately as GF2​∣Vud​∣2Δ5, where Δ=mπ+​−mπ0​, with electron-threshold and recoil corrections. The fifth power makes the few-MeV gap especially restrictive. Unlike direct π+→e+νe​, this semileptonic channel is not forced to have an amplitude proportional to me​; its primary extra suppression is the small phase space. Its approximately Cabibbo-favoured u↔d current does not supply a further large flavour suppression. The observed branching fraction corresponds to a partial decay rate of about (1.036×10−8)/(2.6033×10−8s)=0.398s−1, competing with the much faster dominant muonic decay.
