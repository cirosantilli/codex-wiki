The original table gives the dimensionless branching fractions
B(π0→e+e+e−e−)=(3.34±0.16)×10−5,B(π0→e+e−)=(6.46±0.33)×10−8.
(43)
Thus the four-lepton channel is about 517 times as frequent, despite its smaller available phase space.
The dominant neutral-pion interaction is with two photons. In double Dalitz decay, both are virtual and convert to separate electron-Positron pairs:
π0→γ∗γ∗→(e+e−)(e+e−).
(44)
This requires two electromagnetic conversions but does not force the whole final state into a helicity-suppressed two-fermion configuration. The two virtual photons and four final leptons allow the necessary angular momentum correlations.
For the two-lepton mode, a single virtual photon cannot mediate a 0−+ pion into e+e−: the electromagnetic vector current has the wrong quantum numbers. The leading electromagnetic mechanism involves two virtual photons attached to the same lepton line, with a loop integration. The resulting pseudoscalar two-lepton amplitude vanishes as me​→0, so its decay width has an additional helicity suppression factor proportional to me2​/mπ2​.
Both channels involve extra electromagnetic couplings relative to π0→γγ. The crucial distinction is the extra leptonic loop and helicity suppression of the two-lepton channel; counting final particles or couplings alone misses it. The pion-two-photon interaction itself already contains strong-interaction structure, so “extra loop” here compares the diagrams after representing that common interaction by an effective π0γγ vertex.
