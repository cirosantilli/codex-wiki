At energies far below the W mass, replace its propagator by a constant. The modern V-A interaction form of Fermi's contact theory has, in ℏ=c=1 units,
Heff​=2​GF​Vud​​[ψˉ​p​γμ(gV​−gA​γ5)ψn​][ψˉ​e​γμ​(1−γ5)ψν​]+h.c.
(12)
Nucleons are treated nonrelativistically within nuclear states. To leading order in their velocity, the vector charge and axial spatial current give the two nuclear operators
MF​=⟨f∣∑k​τk+​∣i⟩,MGT​=⟨f∣∑k​σk​τk+​∣i⟩,
(13)
where τ+ here means neutron-to-proton conversion. (i) The scalar Fermi operator permits ΔJ=0, including 0→0, without parity change. The rank-one Gamow–Teller operator permits ΔJ=0,1 without parity change, excluding 0→0. Both terms can contribute when the same nuclear states permit them; their strengths contain nuclear matrix elements, not just phase space.
(ii) Expand the lepton waves across the nucleus in powers of their momenta times nuclear radius. When pR/ℏ≪1, the constant spatial term gives allowed transitions. If its matrix element vanishes by spin/parity selection, higher terms supply lepton orbital angular momentum: “forbidden” means suppressed, not impossible. First-forbidden terms change parity and can have ΔJ=0,1,2; the rank-two ΔJ=2 case is unique first-forbidden. Higher orders carry additional small spatial factors and different energy-dependent shape factors. The contact approximation alone does not imply the allowed approximation.
For a single branch, neglect recoil and neutrino mass and first assume an energy-independent nuclear shape factor. Fermi's golden rule integrates the neutrino phase space with Eν​=Q−T, while the electron momentum volume is proportional to p2dp. Including Coulomb distortion,
N(p)dp=Cp2F(p)(Q−T)2dp,N′(p)=C​(Q−T)​.
(14)
This momentum-bin Kurie ordinate is linear in kinetic energy and vanishes at its endpoint. If instead using counts per unit kinetic energy, dN/dT∝F(p)pEe​(Q−T)2 in c=1 units; converting with dT/dp=p/Ee​ recovers the supplied momentum-bin definition. No extra factor of Ee​ should be left inside that definition of N′.
The graph's horizontal coordinate is T/(me​c2), not T in MeV. Its intercept is approximately 3.9–4.0, so
Q=Tmax​≃2.0MeV​.
(15)
For a ground-to-ground branch between bare nuclei, the emitted electron's rest energy must also be supplied:
[Mnuc​(91Y)−Mnuc​(91Zr)]c2≃Q+me​c2≃2.5MeV​.
(16)
For neutral atomic masses the mass difference instead equals approximately Q≃2.0MeV, neglecting small electron-binding corrections. The graph supports this approximate precision, not a precise evaluated isotope mass. Interpreting a branch to an excited daughter would require adding its excitation energy; the ground-state comparison assumes the ground-state branch intended here.
The shell-model prediction for yttrium-91 beta decay follows from shell filling: the proton orbitals above 28 fill 2p3/2​ with four protons and 1f5/2​ with six, reaching 38. Yttrium has its 39th proton in 2p1/2​, giving Jiπ​=1/2−. Its 52 neutrons include the closed 50-neutron core and a pair in 2d5/2​, coupled to zero in the simple paired ground state. Zirconium has 40 paired protons and 51 neutrons, leaving an unpaired 2d5/2​ neutron, so Jfπ​=5/2+. Therefore
∣ΔJ∣=2,πf​=−πi​:first-forbidden unique beta decay​.
(17)
The single-particle change is neutron d5/2​ to proton p1/2​; the parity change and rank two prevent an allowed Fermi or Gamow–Teller contribution.
The straight plotted line is a constant-shape idealization. For the shell-model transition just inferred, a leading unique-forbidden shape factor contains C(T)∝pe2​+pν2​ in c=1 units, with Coulomb/finite-size refinements. Consequently the uncorrected quantity specified in the paper is generally proportional to (Q−T)C(T)​, not exactly linear. A consistent shape-corrected Kurie plot also divides its ordinate by C(T)​. The source's exact straight-line construction and its forbidden-transition inference cannot both be exact with a Coulomb-only F; the approximate graphical endpoint can still be read as requested. Allowed and unique-forbidden spectral shapes are experimentally distinguishable.[ref]
