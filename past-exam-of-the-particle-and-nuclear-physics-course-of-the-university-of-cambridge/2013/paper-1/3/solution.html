For the lowest spatial state, orbital angular momentum is zero and the spatial wavefunction is symmetric. A three-quark colour singlet is antisymmetric, proportional to ϵabc​∣abc⟩. The Pauli exclusion principle for quarks therefore requires a symmetric spin–flavour factor. Colour and flavour are different degrees of freedom even though both use three-dimensional group representations.
The tensor product flavour decomposition is 3⊗3⊗3=10S​⊕8M​⊕8M​⊕1A​. Three spin-one-half particles have a symmetric spin-3/2 quartet and mixed-symmetry spin-1/2 sectors. The symmetric spin quartet combines with symmetric flavour factors to give a baryon decuplet. Mixed spin and mixed flavour factors instead combine to a symmetric spin–flavour state, giving one physical ground-state baryon octet, not two independent ground-state octets. Equivalently the symmetric spin-flavour SU6 representation has dimension 56=10×4+8×2. The antisymmetric flavour singlet would require a fully antisymmetric three-spin state, which does not exist for only two single-quark spin components.
The spin-one-half baryon octet contains (p,n), (Σ+,Σ0,Σ−), Λ and (Ξ0,Ξ−). The spin-three-halves baryon decuplet contains the four Δ charge states, three Σ∗ states, two Ξ∗ states and Ω−. Both have positive parity in this L=0 approximation. Strange-quark mass breaks the approximate flavour symmetry and splits multiplets.
In the baryon hyperfine mass formula, the constituent-mass sum accounts phenomenologically for common confinement energy. A colour-magnetic spin–spin interaction contributes a contact-type term proportional to Si​⋅Sj​/(mi​mj​): each magnetic moment supplies one inverse mass. The common colour and spatial-overlap factors are absorbed into A′. These are effective constituent masses, not short-distance current-quark masses. Use dimensionless spin operators with ℏ=1, as implicit in the supplied formula and coefficient.

With the supplied masses expressed in GeV units, the common mass is 1.261GeV/c2. The predicted values are
M(Σ+)=1.177GeV/c2,M(Σ∗+)=1.377GeV/c2​.
(10)
They are about 13 and 3MeV/c2 below the stated measurements, respectively. The predicted splitting is 201MeV/c2, compared with 190MeV/c2. The agreement is useful for such a simple model, but a common overlap constant neglects flavour-dependent spatial structure and other residual interactions.
For the weak decay, one contribution is s→uW−, W−→duˉ. The two spectator up quarks combine with d into the proton, and the new uuˉ supplies the neutral pion's corresponding component. This changes strangeness. In the strong decay, a gluon creates ddˉ; rearrangement gives uds and udˉ without changing strangeness.
The strong channel conserves parity: the positive-parity daughter baryon and negative-parity pion require odd relative orbital angular momentum, with a P wave sufficient. The weak channel need not conserve parity. Using the mean lifetime relation τ=ℏ/Γ,
τ(Σ+)≃8.2×10−11s,τ(Σ∗+)≃1.8×10−23s​.
(11)
The widths differ by about 4.5×1012, principally because one is weak and flavour changing and the other is an open strong decay. They are not comparable transitions differing only by a small spin factor.
