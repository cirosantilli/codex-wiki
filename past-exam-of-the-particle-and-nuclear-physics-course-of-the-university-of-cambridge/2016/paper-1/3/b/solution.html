Assume allowed beta decay, so the leading matrix element has negligible lepton-momentum dependence. Neglect nuclear recoil and neutrino mass. Let Q be the kinetic energy available to the electron and antineutrino, E0​=me​+Q, and E=p2+me2​​. The neutrino energy and momentum magnitude are k=E0​−E.
Fermi's golden rule weights accessible final states. After angular integration, the momentum-space factors are p2dp and k2dk. The energy-conserving Dirac delta function removes the neutrino integral:
dΓ=Cp2dp∫0∞​k2dkδ(E0​−E−k).
(18)
The constant includes the allowed nuclear matrix element, weak-coupling and angular factors. Including the usual Fermi function for electron-nucleus Coulomb distortion gives
dpdΓ​=CF(Z,E)p2(E0​−p2+me2​​)2,0≤p≤Q(Q+2me​)​.​
(19)
For the kinetic-energy spectrum, dp/dT=E/p instead yields
dTdΓ​=CF(Z,E)pE(Q−T)2.
(20)
The two distributions differ by a Jacobian; forgetting it changes the shape.
A pure fifth-power law additionally neglects electron mass and Coulomb distortion and keeps the nuclear matrix element fixed. Then E=p, E0​≃Q, and
Γ=C∫0Q​p2(Q−p)2dp=CQ5∫01​x2(1−x)2dx=30C​Q5.​
(21)
Two momentum powers come from each final particle and one from the remaining integration. This is the origin of the Sargent rule. Exact Q5 dependence requires the relativistic-electron, constant-matrix-element limit. For Q≪me​, the same approximation without Coulomb distortion has pE≃me​2me​T​ and instead scales as me3/2​Q7/2. Forbidden-transition factors, nuclear-structure changes and Coulomb distortion also modify the simple law.
