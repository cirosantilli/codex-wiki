In a pure L=0,S=1 stretched state both spins point along the total angular momentum, with no orbital contribution. The nuclear magnetic moment would be
μd(S)​=μp​+μn​=(2.793−1.913)μN​=0.880μN​.
(33)
The measured 0.857μN​ differs. More decisively, a pure S wave spatial state is isotropic and has zero electric quadrupole moment. Its nonzero value requires a nonspherical component of the quantum charge distribution.
Positive parity allows even L; coupling S=1 to J=1 permits L=0 and L=2. The state contains a deuteron D-state admixture:
∣ψd​⟩=1−PD​​∣3S1​⟩+PD​​∣3D1​⟩,
(34)
with radial wavefunctions implicit. A nuclear tensor force couples the orbital components. The quadrupole moment involves their interference as well as a D-state contribution, making it sensitive to a small admixture. This is a quantum angular correlation, not two particles permanently fixed in space.
In the nonrelativistic one-body magnetic-moment approximation,
μN​μd​​=(1−PD​)μs​+PD​(43​−21​μs​),μs​=μN​μp​+μn​​=0.880.
(35)
The D-state coupling has ⟨Sz​⟩=−1/2 and ⟨Lz​⟩=3/2 at Jz​=1; the proton supplies approximately half the relative orbital magnetic moment. Thus
PD​≃23​(0.880−21​)0.880−0.857​=0.040 in this approximation.​
(36)
The robust conclusion is a predominantly S wave state with a small tensor-induced D wave component. The 4% estimate is model dependent: exchange currents and other corrections also affect the magnetic moment, and a wavefunction component probability is not itself a uniquely measured observable.[ref]
