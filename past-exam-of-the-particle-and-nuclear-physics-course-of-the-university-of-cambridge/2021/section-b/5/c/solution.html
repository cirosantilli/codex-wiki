In the simple box-state counting used here, the final three-momentum lies in a spherical shell: dn=Cp2dp, where the common constant C contains the normalization volume and angular factor. Momentum conservation fixes the other final particle's momentum. Hence the density of states is
ρℓ​(E0​)=Cpℓ2​dE0​dpℓ​​.

With the paper's rounded masses,
Rnaive​=(19600−0.2519600−11236​)219600+0.2519600+11236​=0.28650.
(22)
By Fermi's golden rule, equal squared matrix elements make the rate ratio equal to this density ratio. Thus the naive constant-matrix-element estimate is R≃0.287, which favours the electronic decay by about 3.49 to one.
This is the particular noncovariant state-counting convention in the question. A calculation with the Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure places factors 1/(2E) in the measure and correspondingly normalizes its decay amplitude differently. Only the complete product of phase space and squared matrix element is convention-independent; mixing the two conventions would give a wrong rate.
