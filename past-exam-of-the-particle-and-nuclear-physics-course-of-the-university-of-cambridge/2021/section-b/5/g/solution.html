The weak charged current creates a negative charged lepton in a left-chiral state. For a massless particle, that would have negative helicity; part (e) instead requires positive helicity. A nonzero mass supplies the necessary wrong-helicity component. This helicity suppression is extremely strong for the fast electron, but much weaker for the slow muon.
For a positive-helicity charged lepton with spinors normalized to unit probability, the left-chiral weight is (1−βℓ​)/2. With the box-state normalization of part (c), this gives the useful consistent rate ratio
R=ρe​ρμ​​1−βe​1−βμ​​.
(27)
Indeed 1−βℓ​=2mℓ2​/(mπ2​+mℓ2​) cancels the extra energy-dependent factor in that density ratio, leaving the standard pion electron-to-muon decay ratio in inverted form:
R=me2​mμ2​​[1−me2​/mπ2​1−mμ2​/mπ2​​]2.​
(28)
Using the rounded masses in this paper gives R≃8185, close to the stated order of 8000. Precision predictions require more accurate masses and electromagnetic radiative corrections. The essential mechanism is the charged-lepton-mass dependence of the decay amplitude; simply counting available final momenta misses it.
