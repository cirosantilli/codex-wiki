The qualitative explanation is nuclear pairing: 40K is odd-odd (Z=19,N=21), while both 40Ar and 40Ca are even-even. At A=40, moving from the odd-odd to either even-even branch lowers the pairing contribution to the atomic mass by
40​(+11.2)−(−11.2)​=3.542MeV.
(32)
Two neighboring even-even nuclear isobars can therefore both be lower in mass than the intervening odd-odd member. The two possible directions are
1940​K→1840​Arby electron capture or positron emission,1940​K→2040​Caby beta-minus decay.
(33)
However, the particular coefficients printed in this paper do not quantitatively reproduce both allowed directions. Let D=mp​+me​−mn​≃−0.7823MeV. Using every supplied term, rather than pairing alone, gives
M(40,19)−M(40,18)M(40,19)−M(40,20)​=D+401/30.71(192−182)​+4023.3(22−42)​+40​22.4​≃+3.451MeV,=−D+401/30.71(192−202)​+4023.3(22−02)​+40​22.4​≃−1.443MeV.​
(34)
Thus this parametrization would forbid the calcium beta-minus branch. The physical atomic mass differences are instead approximately +1.505MeV to argon and +1.311MeV to calcium, so both physical directions are allowed.[ref]
The valid interpretation is therefore pairing explains how both directions can be possible, but the supplied smooth mass formula is insufficient to establish both for this isotope. The semi-empirical mass formula has MeV-scale residual errors and omits nuclear shell closure corrections; 40Ca has the especially stable Z=N=20 shell closures. One must use measured masses or a model with those corrections for a quantitative proof, rather than changing the printed numbers silently.
