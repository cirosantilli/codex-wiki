Chebyshev function (ϑ(x), ψ(x))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Number theory Arithmetic function Von Mangoldt function
OurBigBook.com Words: 863 Articles: 8
The two Chebyshev functions measure the total natural logarithm weight of prime numbers or of prime powers greater than one below a real cutoff x≥0:
ϑ(x)=∑p≤x​logp,ψ(x)=∑n≤x​Λ(n)=∑pk≤xk≥1​​logp.
(45)
Here p ranges over prime numbers, n over positive integers, and k over positive integers. The Chebyshev theta function ϑ, also written θ, weights each prime number once. The Second Chebyshev function ψ weights every positive power of each prime number separately. Both vanish for 0≤x<2; the inclusive cutoff makes them right-continuous functions and nondecreasing functions, constant between their jumps. [ref]
At x=10, the prime numbers are 2,3,5,7, while the extra prime powers greater than one are 4,8,9. Exactly,
ϑ(10)ψ(10)ψ(10)−ϑ(10)​=log2+log3+log5+log7=log210,=3log2+2log3+log5+log7=log2520,=2log2+log3=log12.​
(46)
At each prime number p, both Chebyshev functions jump by logp. At a prime power pk with k≥2, only the Second Chebyshev function jumps, by logp. At 6 and 10 neither jumps. Notice that the gap is already positive at 5 and 7: a shared jump does not remove the earlier contribution from 4.
Grouping the prime powers by their exponent gives, for x≥2,
ψ(x)=∑k=1⌊logx/log2⌋​ϑ(x1/k),ψ(x)−ϑ(x)=∑k=2⌊logx/log2⌋​ϑ(x1/k).
(47)
The floor function cutoff is exact: p≥2 forces 2k≤x whenever pk≤x. For each exponent, pk≤x is equivalent to p≤x1/k. An upper cutoff smaller than the lower one means an empty sum. [ref]
