Complex conjugate (z)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Complex analysis Complex number
OurBigBook.com Words: 251 Articles: 2
The complex conjugate of z=x+iy is z=x−iy. It keeps the real part and negates the imaginary part. In the complex plane, this is a reflection across the horizontal axis. Thus 3+4i and its complex conjugate 3−4i have the same modulus 5, with opposite arguments modulo 2π.
The product with the complex conjugate is a nonnegative real number:
zz=x2+y2=∣z∣2,z1​=∣z∣2z​(z=0).
(112)
In our example, (3+4i)(3−4i)=25 and 1/(3+4i)=(3−4i)/25=0.12−0.16i. Its modulus is 1/5. Geometrically, taking the multiplicative inverse reverses the argument and reciprocates the modulus; the reflected direction alone is only part of the operation.
Applying the complex conjugate twice returns the starting point. Moreover, a complex number equals its own complex conjugate exactly when its imaginary part is zero. The horizontal axis is therefore characterized algebraically by z=z.
The complex conjugate also recovers the two coordinates without reading them off explicitly:
Rez=2z+z​,Imz=2iz−z​.
(113)
For 3+4i, the numerator of the second expression is 8i, giving 4. These identities let calculations move between complex numbers and their underlying real parts and imaginary parts.
