Weak realization of a countable star (W=⋁n≥1​[0,1]n​)

 ... Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology Simplicial complex Geometric realization of a simplicial complex Weak topology of a simplicial realization
OurBigBook.com Words: 201
Identify the initial endpoints of countably many closed intervals to a vertex v, using the quotient topology. This is the geometric realization of the complex whose only edges are {v,un​}. A neighbourhood of v may contain a separately chosen initial interval [0,εn​) on each arm, with no positive lower bound on the εn​.
The space is not first countable. Given any proposed countable neighbourhood basis (Un​) at v, choose a point xn​ at coordinate tn​>0 on arm n with xn​∈Un​. This is possible by the interval topology on that arm. The set whose intersection with arm n is [0,tn​/2) is an open neighbourhood V of v, by the simplexwise definition. But xn​∈Un​∖V for every n, so no Un​ lies inside V. Consequently this weak star cannot embed in the plane, or in any metric space.
The terminal vertices form an infinite closed discrete subset: on each closed arm they meet only its terminal endpoint. Hence the star is not compact, by closed discrete subsets of compact spaces are finite.
