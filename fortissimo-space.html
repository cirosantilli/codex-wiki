Fortissimo space

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Topology Topological space
OurBigBook.com Words: 173
Choose an uncountable set X and a point a∈X. Every point other than a is isolated, and a set containing a is open precisely when its complement is countable. Equivalently, the closed sets are all subsets containing a together with all countable subsets. Arbitrary intersections and finite unions preserve this latter condition, so it defines a topology.
Every convergent sequence in this space is eventually equal to its limit. Indeed the set of its values different from its limit is countable and closed; its complement is an open neighbourhood of the limit. Eventual membership in this complement forces eventual equality. Nevertheless a is not isolated, because its singleton has uncountable complement. In particular sequence convergence alone does not determine this topology.
A real-valued function on this space is continuous exactly when {x:f(x)=f(a)} is countable. Sufficiency follows by considering the preimages of closed subsets of the real line: each either contains a or is a subset of that countable exceptional set. For necessity, each set {x:∣f(x)−f(a)∣≥1/n} is closed and excludes a, hence is countable; their countable union is the exceptional set.
