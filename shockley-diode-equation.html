Shockley diode equation (ID​=IS​(eVD​/(nVT​)−1))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Diode
OurBigBook.com Words: 410 Articles: 5
For a p-n junction at fixed temperature, the Shockley diode equation models its electric current as
ID​=IS​(expnVT​VD​​−1),VT​=qkB​T​.
(3)
Here IS​>0 is the diode saturation current, n>0 the diode ideality factor, VT​ the thermal voltage, kB​ the Boltzmann constant, and q>0 the elementary charge.[ref]
For an original model example, choose IS​=1nA, n=2, and T=300K. Using kB​=1.380649×10−23J/K and q=1.602176634×10−19C gives VT​=25.8520mV. Then VD​=0.600V gives ID​=0.109591mA, whereas VD​=0.800V gives ID​=5.24450mA. These are predictions of the declared model. There is no universal turn-on electric potential difference: solving the Shockley diode equation for the value needed at a chosen electric current gives
VD​=nVT​ln(1+IS​ID​​),ID​>−IS​.
(4)
At ID​≫IS​, increasing VD​ by 0.100V multiplies the electric current by approximately e0.100/(nVT​)=6.91772. At VD​=−0.300V the same model gives ID​=−0.996979nA. Its reverse branch approaches −IS​; it cannot describe diode reverse breakdown.
A constructed extension adds internal series electrical resistance Rs​ to a junction whose electric potential difference is Vj​. Applying the Kirchhoff cycle law gives
VD​=Vj​+ID​Rs​=nVT​ln(1+IS​ID​​)+ID​Rs​.
(5)
For Rs​=20Ω and the same IS​,n,T, sustaining ID​=5.24450mA needs VD​=0.904890V: 0.800V across the junction plus 0.104890V across its internal electrical resistance. The derivative
dID​dVD​​=ID​+IS​nVT​​+Rs​
(6)
shows why the current-voltage characteristic becomes nearly a straight line when the internal series electrical resistance dominates. This is still a fixed-temperature model; self-heating can change IS​, n and VT​. Real diodes also have leakage and charge-storage effects omitted here.[ref]
