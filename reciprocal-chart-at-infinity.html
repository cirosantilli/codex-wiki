Reciprocal chart at infinity (w=1/z,w(∞)=0)

 ... Analysis Complex analysis Riemann surfaces Riemann sphere Stereographic projection Holomorphic stereographic atlas of the sphere
OurBigBook.com Words: 229
The reciprocal chart makes infinity an ordinary coordinate origin. Its domain is the Riemann sphere minus the point z=0; w(∞)=0 and w(z)=1/z elsewhere. A neighbourhood UR​ of infinity becomes the open disc ∣w∣<1/R. In sphere coordinates this is exactly the cap
Z>R2+1R2−1​.
(31)
For R=2, the exterior ∣z∣>2 together with infinity becomes Z>3/5 and ∣w∣<1/2. The boundary itself is excluded from each open set.
The point z=2+i lies in this neighbourhood because ∣z∣=5​>2. Its reciprocal coordinate is w=(2−i)/5, with ∣w∣=1/5​<1/2, and its sphere height is Z=2/3>3/5. The negative imaginary part of w is the axis reversal required by the holomorphic stereographic atlas of the sphere.
For a complex-valued holomorphic function f defined outside a large disc, having a removable singularity at infinity means that g(w)=f(1/w) extends to a holomorphic function at w=0. This definition reduces questions at infinity to familiar questions at a finite point.[ref] For example,
f(z)=z+3z−2​,g(w)=1+3w1−2w​=1−5w+15w2−45w3+⋯,∣w∣<1/3.
(32)
The geometric series gives a Taylor series at the new origin, so f(∞)=1 and g′(0)=−5. The coefficient −5 is the derivative in the specified w coordinate. In contrast, f(z)=z2 becomes g(w)=w−2, displaying a pole of order two at infinity when the target is the complex plane. As a holomorphic map to the Riemann sphere, it uses the target reciprocal coordinate 1/f(1/w)=w2, which is regular at zero.
