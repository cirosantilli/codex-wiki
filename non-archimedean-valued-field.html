Non-Archimedean valued field ((K,∣⋅∣),∣x+y∣≤max(∣x∣,∣y∣))

 ... Mathematics Area of mathematics Arithmetic Non-Archimedean analysis Absolute value (algebra) Non-Archimedean absolute value
OurBigBook.com Words: 122
A field equipped with a Non-Archimedean absolute value is a non-Archimedean valued field. Its metric is d(x,y)=∣x−y∣ and satisfies the ultrametric inequality. The P-adic numbers give the basic mixed-characteristic examples. Completeness means that every Cauchy sequence for this metric has a limit in the field.
The sets R={x:∣x∣≤1} and m={x:∣x∣<1} are its valuation ring and maximal ideal. Multiplicativity and the strong triangle inequality prove that R is a ring and m is an ideal. A nonzero element of R is invertible in R exactly when its absolute value is one, since ∣x−1∣=∣x∣−1. Thus all nonunits are precisely m, proving that it is the unique maximal ideal and that R/m is a field. These definitions concern a valuation, independently of whether the field admits an ordering.
