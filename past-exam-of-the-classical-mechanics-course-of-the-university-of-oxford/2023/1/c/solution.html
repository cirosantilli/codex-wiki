Write w=1+2k​. The initial conditions on the normal mode coordinates are u(0)=v(0)=0 and u˙(0)=v˙(0)=k. Solving the two harmonic oscillator equations and recovering the original angles gives the exact solution within the harmonic approximation:
θ1​(t)=2k​(sint+wsinwt​),θ2​(t)=2k​(sint−wsinwt​).​
(12)
Their angular velocities are θ˙1​=k(cost+coswt)/2 and θ˙2​=k(cost−coswt)/2, which verify all four initial conditions.
To expose the beat, define Δ=w−1 and Ω=(w+1)/2. Replacing 1/w by 1 changes each angle by O(k2) uniformly in time. The sum-to-product identities then give
θ1​θ2​​=ksin(Ωt)cos(Δt/2)+O(k2),=−kcos(Ωt)sin(Δt/2)+O(k2),​θ˙1​θ˙2​​=kcos(Ωt)cos(Δt/2),=ksin(Ωt)sin(Δt/2).​
(13)
The displayed angular velocities are exact in the linear system. The absolute and signed beat envelopes have leading amplitudes k∣cos(Δt/2)∣ and k∣sin(Δt/2)∣. Because Δ=k+O(k2), at τ=π/k we have Δτ/2=π/2+O(k) and Ωτ=τ+π/2+O(k). Hence
θ1​(τ)θ2​(τ)​=O(k2),=ksinτ+O(k2),​θ˙1​(τ)θ˙2​(τ)​=O(k2),=kcosτ+O(k2).​​
(14)
The first simple pendulum has negligible oscillation amplitude. Its kinetic energy is T1​(τ)=O(k4), while the second has T2​(τ)=k2cos2τ/2+O(k3). Thus, to the retained order, all instantaneous kinetic energy belongs to the second simple pendulum. The fast phase can make that kinetic energy small: the whole initial energy k2/2 is transferred as the second simple pendulum's combined kinetic energy and gravitational potential energy, since
21​(θ˙2​(τ)2+θ2​(τ)2)=2k2​+O(k3),Vs​(τ)=O(k3).
(15)
This is energy transfer after an impulse in two weakly coupled oscillators. It does not require cosτ=1.
At 2τ, Δ(2τ)/2=π+O(k), so the beat has returned the oscillation to the first simple pendulum:
θ1​(2τ)θ2​(2τ)​=ksin(2τ)+O(k2),=O(k2),​θ˙1​(2τ)θ˙2​(2τ)​=kcos(2τ)+O(k2),=O(k2).​​
(16)
The energy localization returns, although the fast oscillation phase need not equal its initial phase. The exact first minimum of the leading beat envelope is at π/Δ, whose leading value is π/k. Keeping the slow phase Δt is essential: expanding sin(wt) at fixed t and then using t=O(1/k) would lose the energy exchange. Although angles are retained only to order k, their leading energies are of order k2.
