Living versus terminating chain populations

 Home Chemistry Polymer Polymerization Chain-growth polymerization
OurBigBook.com Words: 362
The defining idealization of living polymerization excludes chain-growth termination and irreversible chain transfer in chain polymerization. Fast, effective chain-growth initiation is an additional condition behind a nearly fixed growing-chain population, not a guarantee contained in the name alone.[ref] If all four chains in finite chain-growth bookkeeping remain active, completing the feed keeps C=A=4 and gives Xn​=10. Stipulate six further additions to each chain, obtaining (10,10,10,10). Other allocations with the same total also satisfy Xn​=10: living does not mean identical lengths.
Alternatively, stipulate that the first two length-four chains undergo chain-growth termination without joining, while the other two each receive twelve more monomer molecules. The final lengths are (4,4,16,16), with (C,A,S)=(4,2,40). The mean degree of polymerization is still 10, but two chains have stopped and two carry the remaining growth. The population variance of lengths is 36, compared with zero in the deliberately equal living allocation. These are synthetic populations, not measured molecular distributions and not a claim that all terminating processes are broader than all living processes. Indeed, assigning all twenty-four remaining additions to one still-active chain gives the living allocation (28,4,4,4), with the same mean but variance 108. Comparable start times and opportunities for chain-growth propagation are extra assumptions behind an expectation of more uniform lengths.
If the two terminated length-four chains instead join, their inactive product has length eight; with the same remaining additions the final lengths are (8,16,16). Now C=3, A=2, S=40 and Xn​=40/3. Thus chain-growth termination can change both which chains grow and which chains enter the denominator. Continuing chain-growth initiation, inefficient initiation, chain transfer in chain polymerization, and reactivation require their own counts. Predicting an entire length distribution additionally needs a specified reaction mechanism and growth statistics. A measured mean alone cannot establish living polymerization.
