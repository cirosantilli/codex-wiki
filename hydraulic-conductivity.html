Hydraulic conductivity (K=kρg/μ)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Porous-media flow Darcy law
OurBigBook.com Words: 85
Hydraulic conductivity is the coefficient in q=−K∇h, with units ms−1. Because the gradient of hydraulic head is dimensionless, hydraulic conductivity has the dimensions of velocity, but it is not the actual Darcy velocity unless the driving gradient has magnitude one. Intrinsic permeability of a porous medium k has units m2 and describes pore geometry; hydraulic conductivity also depends on mass density, dynamic viscosity and gravitational acceleration.[ref]
For k=10−12m2, ρ=1000kgm−3, g=9.81ms−2 and μ=10−3Pas, hydraulic conductivity is K=9.81×10−6ms−1. Doubling dynamic viscosity while keeping pore geometry, mass density and gravitational acceleration fixed halves hydraulic conductivity without changing intrinsic permeability of a porous medium. Thus a value quoted as a material's hydraulic conductivity needs its fluid conditions.
