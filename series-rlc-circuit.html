Series RLC circuit (Lq′′+Rq′+q/C=V(t))

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit RLC circuit
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 4
In a series RLC circuit, the same electric current i=q′ flows through the resistor with electrical resistance R, the inductor, and the capacitor. Choose q as the signed electric charge on the capacitor plate into which positive i flows. With terminal drops measured in that direction, Kirchhoff's voltage law gives
vR​=Ri,vL​=Li′,vC​=q/C,Lq′′+Rq′+q/C=V(t).
(20)
Here V(t) is the applied electric potential difference; assume constant L>0, C>0, and R>0. With the applied source set to zero and the loop kept closed, the free response satisfies
q′′+2αq′+ω02​q=0,α=2LR​,ω0​=LC​1​.
(21)
The angular frequency ω0​ is the value for lossless free oscillation; α is the decay parameter. The three possibilities are underdamped RLC response for α<ω0​, critically damped RLC response for α=ω0​, and overdamped RLC response for α>ω0​. [ref].
For a fair comparison, start each circuit with the same electric charge q(0)=q0​ and zero electric current q′(0)=0. Set τ=ω0​t and a=α/ω0​; then x=q/q0​ obeys d2x/dτ2+2adx/dτ+x=0. The plot compares a=0.5,1,2. These dimensionless curves apply to any L,C after rescaling time and electric charge.
The stored energy is the sum of magnetic energy and capacitor energy:
E=21​Li2+2Cq2​.
(22)
Differentiating and using the circuit equation gives the complete power balance,
E′=i(Li′+Cq​)=V(t)i−Ri2.
(23)
In the free response, E′=−Ri2≤0: Joule heating accounts exactly for the lost energy. For the plotted initial conditions, E(0)=q02​/(2C) and E/E(0)=x2+(dx/dτ)2. Since every free solution tends to zero for R>0,
∫0∞​Ri(t)2dt=E(0).
(24)
In the lossless limit R=0, energy stays constant and shuttles between the capacitor and inductor. Thus an oscillation of electric charge and a decrease of total energy are compatible statements.
The analogy with a damped harmonic oscillator is exact within these ideal models: electric charge corresponds to displacement, electric current to velocity, inductance to mass, 1/C to spring constant, and R to the coefficient of a resisting force proportional to velocity. The two stored energy terms play the roles of kinetic energy and elastic potential energy. [ref].
Differentiating the driven equation also gives Li′′+Ri′+i/C=V′. When using this equation to solve for electric current, retain the original constraint Li′(0)+Ri(0)+q(0)/C=V(0): the initial electric charge fixes the initial rate of change of electric current.
