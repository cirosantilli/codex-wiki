Vanishing moment (∫xrψ(x)dx=0)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Analysis Fourier analysis Wavelet
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 6
A vanishing moment is a cancellation condition: positive and negative parts of an analyzing function balance when weighted by a monomial. Having q vanishing moments means that the first q such balances hold, starting with the unweighted integral:
∫R​xrψ(x)dx=0,r=0,…,q−1,q≥1.
(30)
Consequently a wavelet ignores local polynomial trends of degree of a polynomial less than q. A smooth feature is close to its Taylor polynomial, so only the small Taylor remainder contributes to its detail coefficient. A jump discontinuity cannot be approximated this way across its edge; its jump response of a localized analyzing function can remain large relative to details from a smooth region.[ref]
These are ordinary absolutely convergent integrals, not a prescription for assigning values to divergent expressions. A sufficient assumption is
∫R​(1+∣x∣q−1)∣ψ(x)∣dx<∞.
(31)
It makes ψ and all xrψ for r<q integrable functions. Being square-integrable alone does not ensure these weighted integrals exist. For a square-integrable function with compact support, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality guarantees every such weighted integral is finite. Saying exactly q vanishing moments additionally requires an existing, nonzero next integral ∫xqψ(x)dx; the usual phrase “has q” allows more cancellation.
