Battery load power and efficiency (PL​=E2R/(R+r)2)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Electrical circuit Electric battery
OurBigBook.com Words: 277
In the constant-parameter battery internal resistance model, the source's available electrical power splits between the electrical load and internal dissipation:
EI=PL​VL​I​​+Pint​I2r​​,PL​=(R+r)2E2R​,Pint​=(R+r)2E2r​.
(48)
The stipulated 3V, 1Ω, 4Ω calculation gives PL​=1.44W, Pint​=0.36W, and EI=1.80W. A resistive electrical load converts its share to Joule heating; another electrical load might convert some of it to mechanical work or light. The source term is available electrical work per time, not a complete cell heat budget: reaction entropy can also matter.
Define the delivery efficiency relative to the source's available electrical work, rather than to all chemical energy or to a complete charging cycle:
η=EIPL​​=EVL​​=R+rR​.
(49)
Here η=0.80. The largest load power occurs at R=r: since (R+r)2≥4Rr,
PL​≤4rE2​,PL​​R=r​=2.25W,η​R=r​=21​.
(50)
Thus maximum load power and maximum delivery efficiency ask different questions. As R→∞, VL​→E and η→1, but I→0 and PL​→0: a high nearly unloaded voltage supplies almost no power. As R→0, the model gives VL​→0, PL​→0 and internal power tending to E2/r. Real high-current operation can invalidate constant r and E; the mathematical maximum does not establish a permissible operating point for an actual battery.
