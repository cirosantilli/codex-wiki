Stark effect of the hydrogen ground state

 Home Physics Mathematical physics Stark effect
OurBigBook.com Words: 249
The even, spherically symmetric hydrogen atom ground state has no permanent electric dipole, so a uniform electric field gives no first-order energy shift. The electric-dipole selection rules for hydrogen allow odd-parity excited states to mix in at second order, yielding a negative shift quadratic in the field.
Here there is a nondegenerate energy eigenvalue, so second-order nondegenerate perturbation theory applies. With H′=eEz, it gives

since the normalized radial probability density is 4ρ2e−2ρ, giving ⟨ρ2⟩0​=3 and ⟨ρ3⟩0​=15/2. Converting back to SI units gives the stated electric polarizability. The induced electric dipole moment is −∂ΔE/∂E=α1s​E and tends to zero with the electric field, unlike the selected degenerate branches' leading dipoles. Both calculations describe weak-field level shifts; a spatially uniform static electric field also allows eventual quantum tunnelling and ionization, which these real energy corrections do not calculate.
