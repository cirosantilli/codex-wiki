Helium-3 superfluidity

 Home Physics Branches of physics Statistical physics Superfluidity
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 9
Helium-3 superfluidity involves Cooper pairs of fermions; helium-4 atoms are bosons. Individual helium-3 atoms do not undergo Bose-Einstein condensation. [ref]
The helium-3 superfluid discovery experiment connects an unexpected pressure feature to a change in the state of a liquid. Its lesson is unusually instructive: finding a reproducible anomaly and identifying the material that changed are separate experimental achievements. Pomeranchuk cooling explains how the experiment reached its operating regime; an independent nuclear magnetic resonance channel distinguishes the phases.
