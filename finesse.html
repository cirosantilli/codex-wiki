Finesse (F=ΔνFSR​/ΔνFWHM​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Optical instrument Fabry–Pérot interferometer
OurBigBook.com Words: 543 Articles: 1
The finesse of a Fabry–Pérot interferometer is its free spectral range divided by a transmission peak's full width at half maximum. Large finesse means a narrow accepted frequency interval compared with the spacing between repeated orders. Here half maximum means T=1/2 of the ideal unit peak, with zero detector background subtracted; it does not mean halfway between the transmission minimum and maximum.
Write A=4R/(1−R)2 for the coefficient multiplying sin2(δ/2) in Fabry–Pérot transmission from multiple reflections. This coefficient is sometimes called the coefficient of finesse; it is not the finesse F. About a resonance δ=2πm, put ϵ=δ−2πm. Solving T=1/2 gives
sin2(ϵ/2)=A1​,ΔδFWHM​=4arcsin(2R​1−R​),
(9)
and, since one free spectral range advances the wave phase by 2π,
ΔνFSR​ΔνFWHM​​=π2​arcsin(2R​1−R​),F=2arcsin[(1−R)/(2R​)]π​.
(10)
These are exact for the ideal nondispersive model with constant R and linear frequency dependence of δ. The inverse sine requires (1−R)/(2R​)≤1, or R≥3−22​≃0.171573. At equality, neighbouring peaks merely touch half maximum at their shared minima: the nominal width is one full free spectral range and F=1. Isolated half-maximum peaks require the strict inequality. Below this threshold the transmission never falls to 1/2, so this peak-width definition supplies no finesse; at R=0 the transmission is flat.
For large reflectivity, the small-angle approximation arcsinx≃x gives
F≃1−RπR​​,ΔνFSR​ΔνFWHM​​≃πR​1−R​.
(11)
This is a high-reflectivity approximation, not an identity for every R. The original dimensionless examples below have exact full width at half maximum values obtained from the inverse sine formula.

The beauty of the narrowing is visible directly in the geometric series: large R preserves contributions from many round trips, so a small phase difference accumulates across many terms and spoils their addition. For the stipulated 10mm vacuum gap, R=0.95 gives an ideal full width at half maximum of about 244.792MHz. This instrumental width is not a measured spectral line width. A real scan also depends on the source profile, accepted angles, mirror shape, alignment, and scan stability. The University of Washington handout separately discusses mirror-reflectivity, mirror-flatness, and detector-pinhole contributions; increasing R alone cannot remove the others.[ref]
