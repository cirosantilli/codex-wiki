Nuclear spin-orbit splitting (j=l±21​)

 Home Physics Nuclear physics Atomic nucleus Nuclear shell model
OurBigBook.com Words: 227
The splitting of one-nucleon energy levels by their spin-orbit interaction. With J=L+S, the identity 2L⋅S=J2−L2−S2 gives, in a state of definite l,s=1/2,j,
⟨L⋅S⟩=2ℏ2​[j(j+1)−l(l+1)−43​]={ℏ2l/2,−ℏ2(l+1)/2,​j=l+1/2,j=l−1/2.​
(19)
The second branch requires l≥1. For the illustrative Hamiltonian operator Hso​=−aL⋅S/ℏ2 with a>0, the shifts are −al/2 and +a(l+1)/2. Thus the strong negative coefficient lowers the aligned j=l+1/2 branch. Its capacity is 2l+2; the raised partner holds 2l. Together they still hold 4l+2=2(2l+1) identical nucleons: the interaction rearranges states rather than creating them.
In this example the d5/2​ and d3/2​ shifts are −a and 3a/2, respectively, so the gap is 5a/2. The capacity-weighted mean shift vanishes:
6(−a)+4(3a/2)=0.
(20)
This is a useful check on both the sign and state counting. A seventh identical particle cannot occupy another substate of the already full lower level. In a real atomic nucleus, other radial and orbital angular momentum levels can interleave with this doublet, and a radial coupling need not give the same effective a for every orbital.
