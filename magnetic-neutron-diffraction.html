Magnetic neutron diffraction

 Home Physics Branches of physics Electromagnetism Magnetism Magnetic order
OurBigBook.com Words: 740 Articles: 2
Magnetic neutron diffraction uses scattering of neutrons by ordered magnetic moments to infer magnetic order. Neutrons also scatter from atomic nuclei, so a pattern can contain nuclear and magnetic contributions. A Bragg scattering peak encodes periodicity; it is not a photograph of an individual spin.
A minimal original calculation makes the extra periodicity visible. Consider identical nuclear scatterers at positions xn​=na, with equal neighboring separation a, and ordered magnetic moments alternating between +mA​ and −mB​. A two-site repeating block of length 2a has nuclear and magnetic scattering amplitudes, apart from common geometric factors,
FN​(q)=b(1+eiqa),FM​(q)=mA​−mB​eiqa,q=aπh​,h∈Z.
(117)
Here b is the common nuclear scattering coefficient and q the component of the scattering wave vector along the row. The magnetic expression assumes equally scattering point magnetic moments perpendicular to the scattering wave vector; real magnetic scattering measures the perpendicular component and has a momentum-dependent amplitude. This one-dimensional calculation is a periodicity illustration, not a finite-temperature solution for a chain.
For even h, FN​=2b and FM​=mA​−mB​. For odd h, FN​=0 and FM​=mA​+mB​. Thus equivalent opposing magnetic sublattices, mA​=mB​, cancel in forward scattering but reinforce at the odd positions of the doubled period. The intensity is proportional to the squared magnitude of the scattering amplitude, so changing an amplitude's sign does not make a negative intensity.
This calculation also exposes a useful difference between antiferromagnetism and ferrimagnetism: with mA​=2 and mB​=1, magnetic squared amplitudes are 1 at even h and 9 at odd h. Extra magnetic peaks can therefore occur in a ferrimagnet too. Identifying a real magnetic structure needs the full crystal basis structure factor, multiple reflections, and the allowed moment orientations, rather than a single extra peak.
