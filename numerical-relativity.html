3+1 decomposition of spacetime

Words: 1k Articles: 38
Numerical relativity OurBigBook.com
A 3+1 decomposition writes ds2=−α2dt2+γij​(dxi+βidt)(dxj+βjdt) in terms of lapse, shift, and spatial metric.
A spacetime foliation separates a spatial metric, lapse and shift. The ADM formalism uses this decomposition to express Einstein evolution and constraint equations in Hamiltonian form.

Normal scalar-field momentum (Π=nμ∂μ​ϕ)

Words: 92 Articles: 1
3+1 decomposition of spacetime OurBigBook.com
For a minimally coupled scalar field with spatial induced metric hij​, the field's derivative along the future unit normal is Π. If the shift vector enters as dxi−Nidt, then Π=(ϕ˙​+Ni∂i​ϕ)/N. The canonical momentum of the density is πϕ​=h​Π. Confusing this density with the normal derivative loses a volume factor.

Scalar-field stress projections (ρ,Ji​,Sij​)

Words: 47
Normal scalar-field momentum OurBigBook.com
For a minimally coupled scalar field, put q2=hijDi​ϕDj​ϕ using the spatial covariant derivative. Spatial and normal projections of its stress-energy tensor give ρ=Π2/2+q2/2+V, Ji​=−ΠDi​ϕ, and Sij​=Di​ϕDj​ϕ+hij​(Π2/2−q2/2−V). The trace-free stress is Di​ϕDj​ϕ−hij​q2/3, with coefficient one. This makes the scalar anisotropic stress second order in a spatial gradient expansion.

Unimodular spatial metric (hij​=aloc2​γij​,detγ=1)

Words: 34
3+1 decomposition of spacetime OurBigBook.com
In fixed spatial coordinates set aloc​=(deth)1/6 and γij​=aloc−2​hij​, so detγ=1. This volume-shape decomposition is useful for separating isotropic expansion and anisotropic shape. The determinant-defined scale depends on the coordinate volume convention.

Metric determinant in a 3+1 decomposition (detg=−α2detγ)

Words: 39
3+1 decomposition of spacetime OurBigBook.com
For a positive lapse function α, shift vector βi and spatial induced metric γij​, the block metric has g00​=−α2+γij​βiβj and g0i​=γij​βj. Its block determinant is g=−α2detγ. Therefore the metric volume tensor contracted with the future unit normal restricts to the spatial volume tensor: nμϵμijk​=detγ​[ijk].

Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity

Words: 266 Articles: 4
3+1 decomposition of spacetime OurBigBook.com
Using the lapse function N, shift vector Ni and spatial metric hij​, the Legendre transform in mechanics of the Einstein-Hilbert action gives, in units 16πG=1,
Hbulk​=∫d3xh​(NH+NiHi​),H=hπijπij​−π2/2​−(3)R,Hi​=−2Dj​(h​πji​​).
(1)
Here π=hij​πij and πij is the canonical momentum of the spatial metric. Varying lapse and shift imposes the Hamiltonian constraint and momentum constraint. An asymptotic time translation requires an Arnowitt-Deser-Misner energy boundary term. The bulk constraints generate gauge evolution; the nonzero asymptotic energy comes from the boundary.

Vanishing canonical Hamiltonian of a closed universe

Words: 51
Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity OurBigBook.com
On a compact spatial Cauchy hypersurface without boundary, the gravitational Hamiltonian consists entirely of constraints and vanishes on solutions. There is no asymptotic time-translation boundary charge to add. This is the canonical statement that a closed universe has zero total energy; it does not assert that local matter energy density or local curvature vanishes.

Momentum constraint (Hi​=−2Dj​(πji​/h​)=0)

Words: 79 Articles: 1
Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity OurBigBook.com
The momentum constraint is the equation obtained by varying the shift vector in the Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity. It expresses the tangential-normal projection of the Einstein field equations and generates spatial coordinate transformations. Writing pij=πij/h​ avoids ambiguities about covariant derivatives of tensor densities.
Scalar momentum constraint with positive extrinsic curvature (Dj​Kji​−Di​K=a2​∂i​(ψ′+HA))
Words: 44
Momentum constraint OurBigBook.com
For scalar metric potentials ds2=a2[−(1+2A)dτ2+2B,i​dτdxi+((1−2ψ)δij​+2E,ij​)dxidxj] and positive extrinsic curvature, δKij​=a−1[−(ψ′+HA)δij​+∂i∂j​(E′−B)]. The scalar shear terms cancel between its divergence and trace gradient. With ji​=−Pμi​Tμν​nν, the momentum constraint becomes 2∂i​(ψ′+HA)/a=−8πGji​. Reversing the convention for K reverses the geometric left side.

Canonical momentum of the spatial metric (πij=h​(Kij−Khij))

Words: 71
Hamiltonian formulation of general relativity OurBigBook.com
With the convention Kij​=(h˙ij​−Di​Nj​−Dj​Ni​)/(2N) for extrinsic curvature, differentiation of the gravitational Lagrangian gives πij=h​(Kij−Khij) in units 16πG=1. It is a weight-one tensor density. In three spatial dimensions, π=−2h​K, so
Kij=h−1/2(πij−21​πhij).
(2)
The lapse function and shift vector have no time derivatives in this action and consequently have vanishing conjugate momenta. Changing the sign convention for extrinsic curvature changes the corresponding momentum relation; it must not be silently combined with this formula.

For a unit timelike normal nμnμ​=−1, the spatial projection tensor annihilates nμ and projects every spacetime tensor onto a spatial hypersurface.

Spatial projector derivative identity (Pμρ​Pνσ​∇ν​Pλμ​=−nλKσρ​)

Words: 45
Spatial projection tensor OurBigBook.com
Differentiating the spatial projection tensor and projecting its derivative and covariant indices leaves −nλKσρ​, where K is the extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface. Contracting λ with ρ gives zero because K is transverse. This distinction separates the informative tensor identity, with a free normal index, from its vanishing trace. Hypersurface orthogonality implies symmetric extrinsic curvature.

Spatial metric sign for a unit timelike normal (hμν​=nμ​nν​−gμν​)

Words: 60
Spatial projection tensor OurBigBook.com
With signature +−−− and nμnμ​=1, the spatial projection tensor is Pμν​=δμν​−nμnν​. Projecting the spacetime metric tensor gives qμν​=gμν​−nμ​nν​, which is negative definite on spatial vectors. The positive spatial induced metric is hμν​=−qμν​. A plus sign in gμν​+nμ​nν​ belongs to a different normal/signature convention and is not transverse in this one. Fully projected covariant derivatives of either spatial metric vanish by metric compatibility.

Spatial projection tensor OurBigBook.com
The spatial covariant derivative projects the spacetime covariant derivative and every free tensor index onto a spatial hypersurface. It is the Levi-Civita connection of the induced spatial metric.
Metric compatibility of the spatial covariant derivative (Dλ​hμν​=0)
Words: 46
Spatial covariant derivative OurBigBook.com
For a unit timelike normal n, the induced metric is hμν​=gμν​+nμ​nν​. The spatial covariant derivative projects the derivative and all free indices with the spatial projection tensor Pμν​=δμν​+nμnν​. Metric compatibility gives ∇λ​hμν​=(∇λ​nμ​)nν​+nμ​∇λ​nν​; projecting kills the normal factor in each term. Thus the spatial covariant derivative is compatible with the induced metric.

Positive-expansion convention for extrinsic curvature (Kμν​=Pρμ​Pσν​∇ρ​nσ​)

Words: 56
Extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface OurBigBook.com
With a future unit timelike normal in signature (−,+,+,+), this convention makes the extrinsic curvature trace equal to the normal congruence's expansion, K=∇μ​nμ. An expanding flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric has Kij​=a′δij​ and K=3a′/a2. This is the opposite sign to the negative-expansion convention used in many 3+1 decomposition of spacetime formulas. Projection identities and momentum constraints must use the same convention throughout.

Spatial volume evolution identity ((∂t​−βi∂i​)γ​=γ​(∂i​βi−αK))

Words: 46
Extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface OurBigBook.com
In a 3+1 decomposition of spacetime with Kij​=−(∂t​γij​−Lβ​γij​)/(2α), contract the metric evolution with γij and use the Jacobi determinant derivative formula. Here γ=detγij​, K=γijKij​, α is the lapse function, and β is the shift vector. The identity separates spatial transport and coordinate compression from the geometric volume change caused by extrinsic curvature.

Extrinsic curvature with a negative shift (Kij​=−(h˙ij​+Di​Nj​+Dj​Ni​)/(2N))

Trace of extrinsic curvature with a negative shift (K=−2N1​(∂t​logγ+2Di​Ni))
Words: 49
Extrinsic curvature with a negative shift OurBigBook.com
For the threading convention dxi−Nidt and Kij​=−(γ˙​ij​+Di​Nj​+Dj​Ni​)/(2N), contract with γij. Metric compatibility gives γijDi​Nj​=Di​Ni, and the Jacobi determinant derivative formula gives γijγ˙​ij​=∂t​logdetγ. With zero shift, −K/3=(3N)−1∂t​logγ​ is the local volume Hubble parameter. Reversing the shift convention reverses both the normal's spatial component and the shift terms; the two conventions must not be mixed.

Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface

Words: 158 Articles: 2
Extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface OurBigBook.com
With signature (−,+,+,+), future unit normal n and Kab​=−Pca​Pdb​∇c​nd​, the Gauss–Codazzi equations become (⊥R)abcd​=Rabcd​+Kac​Kdb​−Kad​Kcb​ and (⊥R)abcd​nd=−Da​Kbc​+Db​Kac​, where in the second expression only the first three indices are projected. The intrinsic curvature and spatial covariant derivative use the spatial induced metric. The normal is timelike, so the normal-sign convention differs from a spacelike normal in a positive-definite ambient metric.
Contracted Codazzi equation with positive expansion (Dj​Kji​−Di​K=Pμi​Rμν​nν)
Words: 59
Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface OurBigBook.com
For the positive-expansion convention for extrinsic curvature, write ∇μ​nν​=Kμν​−nμ​aν​ with normal acceleration aν​=nρ∇ρ​nν​. The Ricci commutator applied to n gives the difference of its two second-derivative contractions. Projecting tangentially, the acceleration term in that commutator cancels exactly the acceleration term converting a spacetime divergence of K into its fully projected spatial covariant derivative. Einstein's equations then identify the displayed equation as the momentum constraint.
Vacuum Weyl tensors from hypersurface data (Eab​, Bab​)

For a Ricci-flat spacetime with the stated Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface and induced orientation ϵ~abc​=ndϵdabc​, the electric part of the Weyl tensor and magnetic part of the Weyl tensor satisfy
Eab​=Rab​+KKab​−Ka​cKcb​,Bab​=ϵ~a​cdDc​Kdb​.
(3)
The electric relation comes from the vanishing spatial projection of the Ricci tensor. The magnetic relation combines the normal curvature projection with the sign ϵacde​nc=−ϵ~ade​.

Hypersurface orthogonality implies symmetric extrinsic curvature (Kμν​=Kνμ​)

Time-symmetric conformally flat vacuum initial data (Kij​=0,γij​=ψ4δij​,Δψ=0)
Words: 86
Hamiltonian constraint OurBigBook.com
For a spatial initial slice with vanishing extrinsic curvature, the vacuum momentum constraint is automatic and the Hamiltonian constraint requires zero Ricci scalar. In three spatial dimensions, the indicated conformally flat metric has R=−8ψ−5Δψ, with a nonzero conformal factor. Thus a harmonic function supplies the data on its domain. A positive example is ψ=1+a/r on a punctured or exterior region. There is no nonconstant globally smooth example on all of Euclidean three-space tending to one at infinity, by the maximum principle for harmonic functions. Time symmetry of an initial slice does not imply that its entire evolved spacetime is static.

Indeed K2=9H2−6Hκ and Kij​Kji​=3H2−2Hκ to first order. The spatial Ricci scalar is 4ΔΦ; subtract the background equation 6H2=16πGρˉ​ and divide by four.

Harmonic coordinate

Words: 183 Articles: 6
Numerical relativity OurBigBook.com
A harmonic coordinate satisfies Δg​xλ=0 for a Riemannian metric, or □g​xλ=0 for a Lorentzian metric. Equivalently gμνΓμνλ​=0 or ∂μ​(∣detg∣​gλμ)=0. This common coordinate condition applies in both Riemannian geometry and general relativity.

Harmonic reduction of the vacuum Einstein equations (Rab​=−21​gμν∂μ​∂ν​gab​+Qab​(g,∂g))

Words: 46
Harmonic coordinate OurBigBook.com
With the Riemann curvature tensor convention Rμνρσ​=∂ρ​Γμνσ​−∂σ​Γμνρ​+⋯, harmonic coordinates remove the mixed second derivatives from the Ricci tensor. The remainder Qab​ contains at most first derivatives of the metric tensor. The vacuum Einstein field equations become a system of quasilinear partial differential equations with a Lorentzian wave equation principal part. Harmonic coordinate constraints must be maintained when using the reduced system.

ADM formalism

Words: 39
Numerical relativity OurBigBook.com
The Arnowitt-Deser-Misner formalism expresses general relativity as constrained Hamiltonian evolution of a spatial metric and its conjugate momentum after a 3+1 decomposition of spacetime. Lapse and shift enforce the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints. For an asymptotically flat spacetime its boundary Hamiltonian gives the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner energy.
