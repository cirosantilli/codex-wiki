Digital optical phase conjugation

 Home Physics Branches of physics Optics Physical optics Phase conjugation
OurBigBook.com Words: 215
Digital optical phase conjugation uses a wavefront sensor and a spatial light modulator to measure an incident field and launch a beam with the opposite spatial wave phase. A phase-only spatial light modulator illuminated uniformly generally does not reproduce the incident wave amplitude as well; exact γU∗ requires the appropriate wave amplitude and polarization of an electromagnetic wave too. The experimentally useful question is how much of the original optical mode can be recovered with the measured and controlled field components.
In 2010, Meng Cui and Changhuei Yang demonstrated digital optical phase conjugation through a prepared multiple scattering sample. They dried mixed-size polystyrene microspheres on cover glass, added immersion oil, and sealed them with another cover glass. A 532 nm laser supplied signal and reference beams. The signal crossed the sample between two objectives; the reference interfered with it on a charge-coupled device. Phase-stepped wave interference recovered the spatial wave phase; a calibrated spatial light modulator imposed its negative on the reference-derived return beam. The reference supplied playback power, without nonlinear mixing pumps. An optical camera read out the recovered signal. With the same sample and a zero-phase spatial light modulator control, the return instead produced a scattering of light pattern. This comparison supports mode recovery by spatial wave phase control, not merely increased illumination. A separate ordinary-mirror control tested source displacements. Finite sampling and collection limited recovery; the system controlled wave phase rather than the complete vector field. These restrictions distinguish demonstrated reconstruction from exact reversal of every scattered component.[ref]
