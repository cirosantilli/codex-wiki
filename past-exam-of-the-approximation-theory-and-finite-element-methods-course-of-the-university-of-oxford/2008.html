For n≥1, set p=21−nTn​. It is monic by part (a), and its supremum norm on [−1,1] is E=21−n. At the n+1 successive extreme points xj​=cos(jπ/n) it alternates between E and −E.

∥21−nTn​∥∞​≤∥q∥∞​.​
(3)

K(t)=n!1​X[(x−t)+n​],

The equioscillation theorem says that a best degree-at-most-n polynomial approximation to a continuous real function on [a,b] has n+2 ordered extreme-error points with alternating signs and equal absolute error. Conversely such alternation proves optimality. If the error is zero, the target is already a polynomial of the required degree.
For the single-point exchange in polynomial minimax approximation, start with n+2 ordered reference points tj​ and solve the linear system
u(tj​)−p(tj​)=(−1)jE,j=0,…,n+1
(23)
for the n+1 polynomial coefficients and E. Locate a point of largest absolute error on the interval. If its magnitude is no larger than ∣E∣ within the desired tolerance, alternation certifies the approximation.
Otherwise insert that point and delete one old reference point so that the ordered signs still alternate. If the new point lies between two reference points, delete the neighboring point with the same error sign. If it lies outside the reference range, either replace the adjacent same-sign endpoint or, for opposite sign, insert it and drop the far endpoint. Solve again and repeat. This is the one-point Remez algorithm; a global extreme-error search is required for its stopping certificate, rather than merely checking the old reference points.

The Sobolev space H1(0,1) consists of square-integrable functions whose weak first derivative is square-integrable, with norm ∥v∥H12​=∫01​(v2+v′2). Its one-dimensional representatives have well-defined endpoint traces.
Multiplying by v∈H1 and integrating by parts gives
∫01​fv=∫01​(u′v′+cosxuv)−u′(1)v(1)+u′(0)v(0).
(28)
The two Robin conditions give u′(0)=u(0) and u′(1)=−u(1), so define
a(u,v)=∫01​(u′v′+cosxuv)+u(0)v(0)+u(1)v(1),ℓ(v)=∫01​fv.
(29)
A weak solution is u∈H1(0,1) satisfying a(u,v)=ℓ(v) for every v∈H1(0,1). The boundary conditions are natural; no endpoint derivative is imposed in the trial space.
Since cosx≥cos1>0 on this interval,
a(v,v)≥cos1∥v∥H12​.​
(30)
This proves coercivity on the full space. Trace continuity and Cauchy-Schwarz give continuity of the form, so the Lax-Milgram theorem also ensures a unique weak solution.
