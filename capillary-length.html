Capillary length (ℓc​=γ/(ρg)​)

 Home Physics Branches of physics Fluid mechanics Surface tension
OurBigBook.com Words: 290
The capillary length measures when gravity and surface tension are comparably important in shaping a liquid interface. For a liquid of uniform mass density ρ, beneath a gas whose mass density is negligible, with uniform gravitational acceleration g>0 and constant surface tension γ>0,
ℓc​=ρgγ​​.
(13)
A deformation of height a and horizontal length scale L has hydrostatic pressure variation of order ρga and small-slope capillary pressure of order γa/L2. Equating these gives L∼ℓc​: surface tension smooths small features, whereas gravity matters for broad ones. For two resting fluids, replace ρ by their positive mass density difference Δρ in this static balance. This change alone does not give the two-fluid wave dynamics, because both fluids can have inertia. [ref]
With the stipulated illustrative parameters γ=0.072Nm−1, ρ=1000kgm−3, and g=9.81ms−2, the capillary length is ℓc​=2.71mm. These parameters define the ideal calculations below; they are not a measurement of a particular sample.
For a sinusoid with wavenumber k>0, the relevant horizontal scale is L=1/k, while its wavelength is λ=2π/k. Thus the Bond number for this scale and the reciprocal restoring-term ratio are
Bo1/k​=γk2ρg​=(kℓc​)21​=(2πℓc​λ​)2,gravity termcapillary term​=(kℓc​)2.
(14)
Equality is at kc​=1/ℓc​, hence λc​=2πℓc​=17.02mm for these parameters. Using L=λ instead produces a different numerical Bond number; state the length convention. The capillary length itself is not the equality wavelength. This distinction helps choose whether a measured ripple should be modeled as a surface gravity wave, a nearly pure capillary wave, or a gravity-capillary wave. [ref]
