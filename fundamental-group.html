Fundamental group (π1​(X,x0​))

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Algebraic topology
OurBigBook.com Words: 2k Articles: 34
The fundamental group π1​(X,x0​) consists of based loops modulo based homotopy. Multiplication follows one based loop and then the other; the constant based loop represents the identity, and path reversal gives the inverse. Passing to homotopy classes makes this operation associative, as explained by well-defined loop concatenation.
Identify the circle with S1={z∈C:∣z∣=1} and choose basepoint 1. Its based loops
γn​(s)=e2πins,0≤s≤1,n∈Z,
(25)
have winding number n: positive n counts counterclockwise turns, negative n counts clockwise turns, and n=0 gives the constant based loop. Every based loop on the circle is joined by a based homotopy to exactly one γn​. Consequently
π1​(S1,1)≅(Z,+),[γm​∗γn​]=[γm+n​].
(26)
This is Hatcher's Theorem 1.7 in Algebraic Topology, §1.1, pp. 29–30. For example, two counterclockwise turns followed by one clockwise turn represent 2+(−1)=1, rather than three turns. The winding graph in the figure above makes the signed arithmetic visible.
A proof outline uses the covering map q:R→S1, q(u)=e2πiu. By the path lifting theorem, a based loop has a unique lift starting at zero and ending at an integer n. Interpolating its lift to s↦ns produces a based homotopy to γn​. The homotopy lifting theorem for a covering map keeps that endpoint unchanged during a based homotopy, proving uniqueness.
In the filled disk, every based loop γ at x0​ has a based homotopy to the constant based loop:
K(s,t)=(1−t)γ(s)+tx0​.
(27)
Both endpoints remain x0​, and the convex set property of the disk keeps every intermediate point inside. Thus π1​(D2,x0​) is the trivial group. Together with the deformation retraction above, this computes three concrete answers: the filled disk has trivial fundamental group, while the circle and the annulus have infinite cyclic groups.
Maps have computable algebraic shadows too. The continuous map pn​:S1→S1, pn​(z)=zn, fixes 1 and sends γk​ to γnk​. Its induced group homomorphism is therefore k↦nk, and its mapping degree is n. For n=2 the image consists of even integers, so this map cannot be a homotopy equivalence. Composing p2​ with p−3​ gives p−6​: map composition multiplies degrees, whereas concatenating based loops adds winding numbers. Distinct degrees distinguish these maps up to homotopy; this calculation does not classify arbitrary topological spaces.
Historically, Poincaré's 1895 Analysis Situs introduced the fundamental group; its original §12 explicitly names it. The CNRS transcription of Poincaré's §12, Groupe fondamental provides a direct view of this early formulation.
