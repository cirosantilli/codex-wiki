Solar neutrino detection

Solar neutrino detection asks how an invisible electron neutrino from the Sun becomes a measurable laboratory record. The Homestake experiment stores a reaction product for later counting; Kamiokande records light promptly. The former measures an exposure-integrated rate, whereas the latter can retain event times and statistical source-direction information. These are complementary ways to connect stellar nuclear fusion with neutrino astronomy.
A detector samples a weighted part of a neutrino flux, rather than counting every passing neutrino. For a target population NT​, an ideal reaction rate is
R=NT​∫Emin​∞​ϕe​(E)σe​(E)dE,
(2)
where ϕe​(E) is the differential electron neutrino neutrino flux and σe​(E) is the relevant cross section. A detection efficiency belongs inside the integral if it depends on energy. Two detectors can therefore disagree in raw count rate while observing the same source. Interpreting a deficit requires both a source prediction and a detector response.
