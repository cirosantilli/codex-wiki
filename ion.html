Ion (q=ze)

 Home Chemistry
OurBigBook.com Words: 472 Articles: 9
An ion is a chemical atom or a group of chemical atoms with a nonzero net electric charge. A positive ion is a cation; a negative ion is an anion. The whole entity carries the electric charge: a hydronium ion is a group of four chemical atoms with net charge +e. This definition agrees with [ref].
For an ordinary ion containing only atomic nuclei and Electrons, let Np​ count all its protons and Ne​ all its Electrons. Each proton contributes the elementary charge +e, each Electron contributes −e, and neutrons contribute zero. Therefore
q=(Np​−Ne​)e=ze,z=Np​−Ne​.
(85)
The signed integer z is the net electric charge number. Removing an Electron increases z by one; adding an Electron decreases z by one. Changing the Electron count leaves the atomic number, and therefore the identity of the chemical element, unchanged.
For example, a neutral sodium chemical atom has 11 protons and 11 Electrons. Its cation Na+ still has 11 protons, but only 10 Electrons, so q=(11−10)e=+e. A neutral chlorine chemical atom has 17 protons and 17 Electrons; its anion Cl− has 17 protons and 18 Electrons, so q=(17−18)e=−e. These are particle inventories, not pictures of atomic orbitals. See 2.6 Ionic and Molecular Compounds (§2.6: ions and ionic compounds).
One sodium chloride formula unit accounts for one Na+ cation and one Cl− anion, whose net electric charges sum to zero. Ordinary solid sodium chloride is an extended arrangement of ions, rather than discrete NaCl molecules. This is a useful distinction: a neutral material can contain charged constituents, and its chemical formula tells us their ratio without claiming that each pair is a separate molecule.
Ion bookkeeping connects chemistry to electric current: moving ions can transport electric charge, while stationary ions cannot supply a transport current density. In electrochemistry, both signs of ions can contribute to the same conventional current. An ion opposing a fixed electric charge is called a counterion.
