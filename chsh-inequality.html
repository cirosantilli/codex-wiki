CHSH inequality

 Home Physics Branches of physics Quantum theory Foundations of quantum mechanics Bell theorem
OurBigBook.com Words: 1k Articles: 11
For local outcomes A0​,A1​,B0​,B1​∈{−1,1}, the Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt inequality is
∣E(A0​B0​)+E(A0​B1​)+E(A1​B0​)−E(A1​B1​)∣≤2.
(2)
An arbitrary no-signalling correlation can reach the algebraic maximum 4, while quantum correlations are bounded by 22​.
For a short proof of the local bound, fix a deterministic local hidden-variable model assignment. The combination is A0​(B0​+B1​)+A1​(B0​−B1​). Exactly one bracket vanishes and the other is ±2, so every assignment contributes ±2. Averaging with a single setting-independent probability distribution preserves the interval [−2,2]. Mixtures also cover stochastic local hidden-variable theories. Rejecting a different sample at each setting would invalidate precisely that common-average step.
The quantum bound follows just as economically. For Hermitian operators with Ai2​=Bj2​=I, the CHSH inequality operator C=A0​⊗(B0​+B1​)+A1​⊗(B0​−B1​) obeys C2=4I−[A0​,A1​]⊗[B0​,B1​]. Each commutator has operator norm at most two, giving ∥C∥≤22​. The Polarization CHSH count model attains this bound without allowing either station to control the distant marginal probability distribution.
