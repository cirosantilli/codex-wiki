For a horizontal field B(z)ex​ in constant downward gravity, equilibrium obeys (p+B2/(2μ0​))z​=−ρg. The displacement energy reduces to the displayed quadratic form. Perturbations independent of the field direction do not bend magnetic field lines and therefore have no magnetic tension penalty: an increasing mass density with height permits instability. A nonincreasing mass density gives nonnegative energy for all admitted incompressible perturbations. Along-field variation adds magnetic tension proportional to wavenumber squared, stabilizing sufficiently short waves when buoyancy is unfavorable.
