Ramsey family in the homogeneous-cone sense (∃M∈[N]ω[M]ω⊆A or [M]ω∩A=∅)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Combinatorics Ramsey theory Space of infinite subsets of the natural numbers
OurBigBook.com Words: 143
A family A⊆[N]ω is Ramsey in the homogeneous-cone sense if there is an infinite M⊆N such that either [M]ω⊆A or [M]ω∩A=∅. This is the existence of one infinite homogeneous cone. Requiring such a refinement inside every infinite initial ground set gives the stronger definition used for a Ramsey set of infinite subsets; requiring a homogeneous refinement in every finite-stem neighbourhood gives a completely Ramsey set.
These quantifiers should be distinguished. For example, the existence of a homogeneous cone on the even numbers does not constrain a family's restriction to the odd numbers. If E is the finite-symmetric-difference parity colouring class on the odd numbers, the family consisting of E alone has a homogeneous cone disjoint from it on the even numbers, but no homogeneous cone on the odd numbers. Every open set in the ordinary topology on infinite subsets has homogeneous cones inside all infinite ground sets, as follows by deciding finite stems through fusion.
