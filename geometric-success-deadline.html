Geometric success deadline (Nα​=⌈log(1−α)/log(1−p)⌉)

 ... Area of mathematics Probability and statistics Probability theory Probability distribution Discrete probability distribution Geometric distribution
OurBigBook.com Words: 193
How many attempts give at least a chosen success probability 0<α<1? For a positive-integer geometric distribution with 0<p<1, completion by attempt N means T≤N, so
P(T≤N)≥α⟺qN≤1−α⟺N≥logqlog(1−α)​,q=1−p.
(27)
The logarithm logq is negative, which reverses the inequality when dividing. The least integer meeting the target is therefore obtained with the ceiling function:
Nα​=⌈log(1−p)log(1−α)​⌉.
(28)
For our constructed independent retry mechanism with p=0.3 and α=0.95,
log(0.7)log(0.05)​≈8.3991,N0.95​=9.
(29)
To verify minimality directly, eight attempts give P(T≤8)=1−0.78=0.94235199<0.95, whereas nine give P(T≤9)=1−0.79=0.959646393≥0.95. Under the zero-based convention, the same completion event is F≤8, with failure to complete given by F≥9; saying F≤9 would allow ten attempts. A 95% deadline controls a tail probability, while the expected value 10/3 describes an average.
At p=1 success occurs on the first attempt, so Nα​=1; the logarithm formula is inapplicable. At p=0 no finite deadline achieves a positive target. For 0<p<1, no finite deadline guarantees success with probability one because qN>0. A retry cap at N produces the capped geometric waiting time min(T,N) as the number of attempts actually spent; reaching the cap can mean either success on the last allowed attempt or failure of all N attempts.
