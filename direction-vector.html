Direction vector (r=p+td)

 Home Mathematics Area of mathematics Geometry and topology Euclidean geometry Straight line
OurBigBook.com Words: 58
A nonzero vector d is a direction vector of a line if the line has a parametrization r=p+td, t∈R, for some point p. Replacing d by a nonzero scalar multiple describes the same line; dividing by its Euclidean norm gives a unit vector in one of the two orientations. A cross product of two nonparallel normal vectors supplies a direction vector for the intersection of their planes.
