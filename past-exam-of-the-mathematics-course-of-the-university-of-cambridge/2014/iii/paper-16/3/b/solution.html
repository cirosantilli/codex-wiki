The Symplectic Darboux theorem says that every point of a 2n-dimensional symplectic manifold has local coordinates (q1,…,qn,p1​,…,pn​) in which
ω=i=1∑n​dqi∧dpi​.​
(19)
First choose linear coordinates at the point so the form there is standard. The required symplectic basis can be constructed inductively: choose e,f with ω(e,f)=1, split off their span, and repeat on its nondegenerate symplectic orthogonal complement. Extend these coordinates to a chart centered at zero.
Let ω0​ be the constant standard form in this chart and η=ω−ω0​. The closed form η vanishes at zero. On a small star-shaped ball the radial Poincaré lemma supplies a primitive
αx​(v)=∫01​tηtx​(x,v)dt,dα=η.
(20)
Since η0​=0, this primitive is O(∣x∣2). The interpolating forms ωt​=ω0​+tη are nondegenerate on a common smaller ball, because they all agree with ω0​ at zero and t ranges over a compact interval.
Apply the local version of Moser's trick: solve ιXt​​ωt​=−α. The vector fields are O(∣x∣2) and fix zero. On a sufficiently small ball their flows exist for 0≤t≤1 and remain inside the coordinate chart; the quadratic bound makes their displacement smaller than the available margin. The same pullback calculation gives f1∗​ω=ω0​. Thus f1​ is a local symplectomorphism from the standard ball into M, and its inverse supplies the desired Darboux chart. There are no local symplectic invariants beyond dimension.
