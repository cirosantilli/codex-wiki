The Marsden-Weinstein theorem says that for a Hamiltonian group action, if c is a regular value fixed by the coadjoint action and G acts freely and properly on μ−1(c), then
Mc​=μ−1(c)/G
(28)
is a symplectic manifold with a unique form characterized by π∗ωc​=ι∗ω, where ι includes the level set and π is the quotient projection. Its dimension is dimM−2dimG.
Equip Cn+1 with the standard symplectic form
ωC​=∑j=0n​dxj​∧dyj​=2i​∑j=0n​dzj​∧dzˉj​.
(29)
Let the circle group act by eiθ⋅z=eiθz. Its generator is X=∑j​(−yj​∂xj​​+xj​∂yj​​), and
ιX​ωC​=−d(21​∣z∣2).
(30)
Consequently the moment map in our convention is μ(z)=−∣z∣2/2. It is invariant, hence equivariant because the circle group is abelian. The level μ−1(−1) is the sphere of radius 2​; it is regular, the circle group acts freely there, and properness follows from compactness of the group. The quotient is Complex projective space, by z↦[z], a scaled Hopf fibration. The Marsden-Weinstein theorem therefore produces a reduced symplectic form on CPn.
To identify it rather than only assert its existence, use the primitive
λ0​=21​∑j​(xj​dyj​−yj​dxj​)=4i1​∑j​(zˉj​dzj​−zj​dzˉj​),dλ0​=ωC​.

s∗λ0​=2iS1​∑j​(wˉj​dwj​−wj​dwˉj​)=2i1​(∂−∂ˉ)logS.
(32)
Differentiating, using ∂ˉ∂=−∂∂ˉ, gives
ωc​=s∗ωC​=d(s∗λ0​)=i∂∂ˉlogS=ωFS​.​
(33)
This is the Fubini-Study form from circle reduction. The unit sphere instead produces half this form; our radius 2​ is exactly what gives the 2π line-area normalization used above.
