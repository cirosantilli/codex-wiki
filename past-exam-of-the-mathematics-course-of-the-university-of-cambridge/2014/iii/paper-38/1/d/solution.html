Stop just before a large predictable coefficient would be used. Set
σj​=inf{t≥0:∣Kt+1​∣>j},
(5)
with the infimum of the empty set equal to infinity. Because Kt+1​ is Ft​-measurable, σj​ is a stopping time. The increment of the stopped martingale transform is
Yt∧σj​​−Y(t−1)∧σj​​=1{t≤σj​}​Kt​(Mt​−Mt−1​).
(6)
Its coefficient is predictable and bounded by j: the first coefficient exceeding j occurs at the step after stopping, and is never included. Part (c) makes Yσj​ a martingale. Since the finitely many Ks​ on any fixed finite horizon are finite almost surely, σj​↑∞ almost surely. Thus
Y is a discrete-time local martingale.​
(7)
This is predictable-coefficient localization of a martingale transform. Stopping after taking the large increment would not give the required bound.
