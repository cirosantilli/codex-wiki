A trading strategy chooses a vector Ht​ of holdings for the period (t−1,t], with Ht​ measurable with respect to Ft−1​. Its end-of-period wealth is Xt​=Ht​⋅Pt​. Rebalancing at date t is self-financing when
Ht+1​⋅Pt​=Ht​⋅Pt​;
(11)
new holdings cost exactly the value released by the old holdings. With fixed initial capital x, the equivalent gains identity is
Xt​=x+u=1∑t​Hu​⋅(Pu​−Pu−1​).​
(12)
A European contingent claim is a maturity-T payoff ξ measurable with respect to FT​. It is attainable if there exists a predictable self-financing strategy and an initial capital x for which XT​=ξ almost surely. Such a strategy is a replicating strategy, and x is its initial replication cost. Holdings are understood only up to the maturity being replicated.
