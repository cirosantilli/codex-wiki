First compute the Mordell-Weil group rank by two-isogeny descent. Here
E:y2=x(x2+i),E′:Y2=X(X2−4i).
(48)
The two-isogeny formula gives ϕ:E→E′. If u=1+i, then u4=−4, and
θ:E⟶E′,(x,y)⟼(u2x,u3y)
(49)
is an isomorphism over K. Its x-coordinate multiplier u2 is a square in K.
Use the two-torsion square-class homomorphism
α:E(K)⟶K×/K×2,α(P)=[x(P)],α(O)=1,α(T)=[i].
(50)
For any prime ideal of the Gaussian integers, if v(x)>0, then x2+i is a unit and 2v(y)=v(x); if v(x)<0, the term x3 has strictly smallest valuation and 2v(y)=3v(x). Hence every valuation of x is even. Since the Gaussian integers form a principal ideal domain, dividing by a square leaves a unit. Their units are {1,−1,i,−i}, whose square classes are {1,i} because −1=i2 and i is not a square in K. For the latter assertion, (a+bi)2=i with a,b∈Q would imply a2=b2 and 2ab=1, hence a2=1/2, impossible for rational a. Thus
α(E(K))={1,[i]}.
(51)
Both classes occur, at O and T. This is the unit square-class bound for two-isogeny descent.
Define α′ on E′ similarly, with α′((0,0))=[−4i]=[i]. Since θ multiplies nonexceptional x-coordinates by a square, and preserves the exceptional classes as well, its image is also {1,[i]}. The standard kernel identities in two-isogeny descent are
kerα=ϕ​E′(K),kerα′=ϕE(K).
(52)
Here ϕ​ is the dual isogeny and ϕ​ϕ=[2]. These identities can be checked directly from the formulas: x(ϕ​(X,Y))=(Y/(2X))2, and conversely a square x-coordinate lets the quadratic equation for a preimage be solved using the curve equation. For example, if x=r2=0, the equation for X is X2−(4x+2a)X+(a2−4b)=0, whose discriminant is 16(x2+ax+b)=16(y/r)2; the Y-coordinate then follows from the dual formula. The exceptional points satisfy the same completed square-class criterion.
The two-isogeny index formula over a number field keeps track of a small kernel factor:
[E(K):2E(K)]=δ#α(E(K))#α′(E′(K))​,δ=[kerϕ​:kerϕ​∩ϕE(K)].
(53)
In this case kerϕ​={O,T′}, where T′=(0,0), and T′∈/ϕE(K) because α′(T′)=[i]=1. Thus δ=2, and the index is 2⋅2/2=2. There is only one nonzero rational 2-torsion point on E: the other two would require x2=−i, and −i has the same nonsquare class as i. The Mordell-Weil theorem now gives
2r+1=[E(K):2E(K)]=2,
(54)
so r=0.
It remains to identify all torsion, rather than merely the rank. The elliptic-curve discriminant is −64i3, so the primes (3) and (2−i) have good reduction, with residue characteristics three and five. By the supplied point-count information their reduction groups have orders that are powers of two. The reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve is injective on prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion. Every odd-primary torsion subgroup therefore injects into a group of two-power order at at least one of these two primes, and must be zero. All torsion is two-primary.
Finally, if a point had order four, its double would be T. The elliptic-curve addition formula gives
x(2P)=4x(x2+i)(x2−i)2​.
(55)
For 2P=T the denominator is nonzero, so x2=i, impossible in K. A point of higher two-power order would have a multiple of order four, so it too is excluded. Thus the only torsion points are O,T. Together with rank zero,
E(Q(i))={O,(0,0)}≅Z/2Z.​
(56)
