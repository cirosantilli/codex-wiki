For a smooth map between manifolds ϕ:M→N, the four constructions go in the directions dictated by composition and the differential of a smooth map.
The pullback of a smooth function f∈C∞(N) is ϕ∗f=f∘ϕ, a function on M. The pushforward of a curve λ:I→M is ϕ∗​λ=ϕ∘λ, a curve in N with the same parameter interval.
For a tangent vector V∈Tp​M, its pushforward is ϕ∗​V=dϕp​(V)∈Tϕ(p)​N. Intrinsically, treating a tangent vector as a derivation on functions,
(ϕ∗​V)[f]=V[f∘ϕ].
(1)
For a covector η∈Tϕ(p)∗​N, the pullback of a covector is the dual linear map:
(ϕ∗η)p​(V)=ηϕ(p)​(dϕp​V).​
(2)
No inverse map is required. A pushed-forward field for a general map is a field along that map; it need not assign a unique vector to an image point with several preimages.

A type (0,s) covariant tensor is a multilinear form on s vectors. For arbitrary V1​,…,Vs​∈Tp​M, the pullback of a covariant tensor is
(ϕ∗ω)(V1​,…,Vs​)=ω(dϕV1​,…,dϕVs​).
(4)
Projection of all covariant slots means (Pω)(X1​,…,Xs​)=ω(PX1​,…,PXs​). Since every pushed-forward vector is already tangential,
[ϕ∗(Pω)](V1​,…,Vs​)​=ω(PdϕV1​,…,PdϕVs​)=ω(dϕV1​,…,dϕVs​).​
(5)
Equality on all arguments proves ϕ∗ω=ϕ∗(Pω). No antisymmetry is needed: this holds for every covariant tensor, not only for differential forms.

The pushforward of a contravariant tensor applies dϕp​ to each of its r vector slots. Choose a basis ei​ of Tp​M and expand
T=Ti1​⋯ir​ei1​​⊗⋯⊗eir​​.
(6)

P⊗r(ϕ∗​T)=ϕ∗​T.​
(7)

The two coordinate tangent vectors of the embedding are
eφ​=(−ρsinφ,ρcosφ,0),ez​=(0,0,1).
(8)
Their Euclidean inner products give the pullback of a Riemannian metric, equivalently the induced metric:
ϕ∗g=ρ2dφ2+dz2,γij​=(ρ20​01​).​
(9)
Locally set u=ρφ. The line element becomes du2+dz2, so the induced Riemann curvature tensor vanishes identically. This is the intrinsic flatness of a circular cylinder. In geometrized units, a mass is converted to a length by multiplying its SI value by G/c2, or to a time by multiplying by G/c3. Using the supplied constants, the solar mass becomes
M⊙(length)​≃1.47×103m,M⊙(time)​≃4.92×10−6s.​
(12)
Thus the characteristic scales are kilometres and a few microseconds. With potential zero at infinity, the Newtonian gravitational potential at the surface, in units of c2, is
Φ⊙​=−R⊙​c2GM⊙​​≃−2.12×10−6.​
(13)
Its very small magnitude is the relevant weak-field measure. In SI potential units the same number corresponds to about −1.91×1011m2s−2.

With vanishing extrinsic curvature, the momentum constraint is identically satisfied and the vacuum Hamiltonian constraint reduces to R=0. To evaluate that Ricci scalar, write ψi​=∂i​ψ, raise these intermediate indices with δij, and use γij=ψ−4δij. The Levi-Civita connection is
Γkij​=ψ2​(δki​ψj​+δkj​ψi​−δij​ψk).
(29)
Substitution into the stated curvature convention gives
Rij​=−ψ2​∂i​∂j​ψ−ψ2​δij​Δψ+ψ26​ψi​ψj​−ψ22​δij​∣∇ψ∣2.
(30)
Tracing with γij cancels the gradient-square terms:
R=−8ψ−5Δψ.​
(31)
Here the Laplacian and norm on the right are those of the flat Euclidean metric. Thus the time-symmetric conformally flat vacuum initial data constraints become
Δψ=0.​
(32)
The equivalence uses ψ=0. This identity is also the three-dimensional specialization of scalar curvature under conformal rescaling; locally the conformal exponent is 2log∣ψ∣, so either fixed nonzero sign of ψ gives the same metric.

On the punctured region r>0, choose a positive constant a and set
ψ(r)=1+ra​.​
(33)
For a radial function in three dimensions, Δψ=r−2∂r​(r2∂r​ψ). Since r2∂r​ψ=−a, this harmonic function has Δψ=0 at every r>0, is nonconstant and positive, and approaches one at infinity. It defines the requested time-symmetric conformally flat vacuum initial data on that punctured or exterior region. One often writes a=M/2, giving the time-symmetric spatial metric of the Schwarzschild metric in isotropic coordinates.
A puncture or inner boundary is necessary if global regularity was intended. A smooth harmonic function on all of R3 that tends to one at infinity must equal one everywhere: applying the maximum principle for harmonic functions to larger and larger balls bounds ∣ψ−1∣ by its arbitrarily small boundary values. The nonconstant example is therefore not a smooth solution through r=0, and no such globally regular example exists under those stronger assumptions.

∂uα∂L​=−2gαν​uν,∂xα∂L​=−∂α​gμν​uμuν.
(34)
The Euler-Lagrange equation is
gαν​dτduν​+∂ρ​gαν​uρuν−21​∂α​gμν​uμuν=0,
(35)
equivalently the affinely parametrized geodesic equation. For the static weak metric, its leading spatial connection coefficient is
Γi00​=−21​gij∂j​g00​=1−2Φ∂i​Φ​≃∂i​Φ.
(36)
All terms with two spatial velocities are suppressed by v2. Therefore d2xi/dτ2≃−∂i​Φ(dt/dτ)2.
Because t is cyclic, the same Euler-Lagrange equation gives (1+2Φ)dt/dτ=constant. On converting to coordinate time, the additional term (d2t/dτ2)(dxi/dt) is of order v2∇Φ and can be dropped. Hence
dt2d2x​=−∇Φ​

to leading order, the Newtonian limit of general relativity. Restoring SI units gives Φ=ΦSI​/c2, so the acceleration is −∇(c2Φ). Substituting L=1 from proper-time normalization before varying would incorrectly erase the dynamics.
