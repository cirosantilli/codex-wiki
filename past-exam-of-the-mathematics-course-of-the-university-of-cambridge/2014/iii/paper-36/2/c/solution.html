Apply 1−ϕB to the observed process and call the result Vt​. Then
Vt​=Zt​+Wt​−ϕWt−1​.
(16)
Its only nonzero covariance lags are
A=γV​(0)=σz2​+(1+ϕ2)σw2​,C=γV​(1)=−ϕσw2​.
(17)
Seek an invertible moving-average factor Vt​=εt​+ϑεt−1​ with Var(εt​)=ν. Matching these covariances requires ν(1+ϑ2)=A and νϑ=C. Solving gives
ν=2A+A2−4ϕ2σw4​​​,ϑ=−νϕσw2​​,ϕAR​=ϕ.​
(18)
These are the three requested parameters in the positive-sign moving-average convention. For nonzero total noise variance,
A−2∣C∣=σz2​+(1−∣ϕ∣)2σw2​>0,
(19)
so ∣ϑ∣<1 and the larger quadratic root gives the invertible factor.
Covariance matching alone would not identify arbitrary processes in distribution. To obtain an actual representation on the given space, define
εt​=(1+ϑB)−1Vt​=∑j≥0​(−ϑ)jVt−j​.
(20)
The series converges in L2. The spectrum of V is (A+2Ccosω)/(2π)=ν∣1+ϑe−iω∣2/(2π), so this filtered process has constant spectrum ν/(2π) and is white noise. Thus
(1−ϕB)Yt​=(1+ϑB)εt​,ε∼WN(0,ν).​
(21)
This is the invertible ARMA factorization of an AR(1)-plus-noise process. If σw2​=0, it reduces to AR(1); if ϕ=0, it reduces to white noise. If σz2​=0 and σw2​>0, then ϑ=−ϕ, the common factor cancels and Y=W. Therefore the orders are at most (1,1); no unnecessarily minimal-order claim is made in those degenerate cases.
