For a spherical stellar polytrope in hydrostatic equilibrium, combine dmr​/dr=4πr2ρ with dP/dr=−Gmr​ρ/r2 to eliminate the enclosed mass:
r21​drd​(ρr2​drdP​)=−4πGρ.
(1)
For n>0, put P=Kρ1+1/n, ρ=ρc​θn, P=Pc​θn+1 and r=aξ. Since ρ−1dP/dr=(n+1)Pc​θ′/(aρc​), choose
a2=4πGρc2​(n+1)Pc​​=4πG(n+1)K​ρc(1−n)/n​.
(2)
The Lane-Emden equation is then
ξ21​dξd​(ξ2θ′)=−θn.​
(3)
A regular center requires θ(0)=1 and θ′(0)=0, with positive chosen central mass density and pressure. Locally θ=1−ξ2/6+nξ4/120+⋯. The stellar surface is the first positive zero ξ1​, where the idealized external pressure is zero; retain the positive solution before it. Thus R=aξ1​ and the Lane-Emden mass formula is
M=4πa3ρc​ωn​,ωn​=−ξ12​θ′(ξ1​)=∫0ξ1​​ξ2θndξ.
(4)
The surface condition selects where to stop a centrally regular solution, rather than replacing its central regularity conditions.
For a polytrope of index zero, the density is constant and (ξ2θ′)′=−ξ2. Regularity gives
θ0​=1−ξ2/6,ξ1​=6​,R=6​a.​
(5)
The pressure is Pc​θ0​ and a2=Pc​/(4πGρc2​), so R2=3Pc​/(2πGρc2​). Index zero is the structural incompressible limit: the expression Kρ1+1/n is not itself defined at n=0.
For a polytrope of index one, set u=ξθ. The equation becomes u′′+u=0, while central regularity requires u(0)=0, u′(0)=1. Hence
θ1​=ξsinξ​,ξ1​=π,R=πa=2GπK​​,ω1​=π.​
(6)
The mass is 4π2a3ρc​, so it can change with central mass density while the radius stays fixed.

I=38π​∫0R​ρ(r)r4dr=38π​ρc​a5∫0ξ1​​ξ4θndξ.

For constant mass density this gives I0​=2MR2/5. For index one, integration by parts gives ∫0π​ξ3sinξdξ=π(π2−6), and therefore

These are axial moments of inertia, not the scalar second mass moment ∫r2dm.
For a finite-radius centrally regular stellar polytrope with 0<n<5 and n=1, eliminate ρc​ from R=aξ1​ and M=4πa3ρc​ωn​. The resulting polytropic mass-radius relation is
M=ARp(n),p(n)=n−1n−3​,A=4πωn​ξ1−p(n)​(4πG(n+1)K​)n/(n−1).​
(9)
Here ξ1​,ωn​ are dimensionless functions of n. At n=1 no such single-valued mass-as-a-power-of-radius relation exists at fixed K: the radius is fixed instead. At n=3 the exponent is zero and M=4πω3​(K/(πG))3/2 is independent of central mass density. For the incompressible case, M=(4πρc​/3)R3 at fixed mass density. Regular n≥5 solutions have no finite zero-pressure surface, so the finite-radius formula does not apply to them.
