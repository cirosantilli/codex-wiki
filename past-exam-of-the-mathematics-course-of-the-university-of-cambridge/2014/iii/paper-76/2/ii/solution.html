To find the threefold phase-locked equilibria, write C=Reiθ with R>0; separating real and imaginary parts gives
RT​=R(μ−R2)+R2cos3θ,θT​=−ω−Rsin3θ.​
(17)
A steady state satisfies
cos3θ0​=R0​R02​−μ​,sin3θ0​=−R0​ω​,
(18)
so
(μ−R02​)2+ω2=R02​.​
(19)
Writing y=R02​ and D=1+4μ−4ω2, the amplitude branches are
y±​=μ+21​±21​D​.​
(20)
There are two distinct positive roots for μ>ω2−1/4, except at (μ,ω)=(0,0), where y−​=0 and only the upper root is nonzero. Indeed their sum is positive in this range and their product is μ2+ω2. Below the fold condition there are none. At equality, there is one positive repeated root y=ω2+1/4, the saddle-node bifurcation limit.
For each positive amplitude, the sine and cosine determine 3θ0​ modulo 2π, giving three phases separated by 2π/3. Thus the source's “two states” means two amplitude branches modulo the threefold spatial symmetry. Generically there are six nonzero complex equilibria, three on each branch, not literally two.
The polar Jacobian matrix at an equilibrium is
J=(−μ−yω/R0​​3ωR0​3μ−3y​),trJ=2μ−4y,detJ=3y(2y−2μ−1).
(21)
On the lower branch, detJ=−3y−​D​<0, so it is a saddle equilibrium and unstable. On the upper branch, detJ=3y+​D​>0 and
trJ=−2(μ+1+D​)<0,
(22)
because existence implies μ>−1/4. Therefore every upper-branch equilibrium is asymptotically stable, and every lower-branch equilibrium is a saddle equilibrium, away from the degenerate endpoints. The eigenvalues are unchanged by the smooth polar coordinate transformation at R0​>0. The origin, not covered by those coordinates, has eigenvalues μ±iω and is stable for μ<0 and unstable for μ>0.
