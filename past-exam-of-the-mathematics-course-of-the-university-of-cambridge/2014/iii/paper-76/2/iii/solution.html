For ω=0, put μ=μ​ω2, C=ωz and τ=ωT, where z=C. Then CT​=ω2zτ​ and division by ω2 gives
zτ​+iz=z2+ω(μ​z−∣z∣2z).​
(23)
We may take ω>0 without loss of the dynamics by conjugating the original equation if necessary; otherwise τ reverses the time orientation. The scaling is singular at ω=0 and is not a transformation for that exactly zero-frequency case.
The Hamiltonian limit of three-to-one forcing drops the terms proportional to ω. For z=x+iy the remaining real system is
xτ​=y+x2−y2,yτ​=−x−2xy.
(24)
The proposed first integral is
H=x2+y2+2x2y−32​y3=∣z∣2+3iz3−z3​.​
(25)
Indeed xτ​=Hy​/2 and yτ​=−Hx​/2, hence Hτ​=0. The unperturbed equation is a planar Hamiltonian system, with a center equilibrium at the origin and three saddle equilibria at
(x,y)=(0,1),(3​/2,−1/2),(−3​/2,−1/2).
(26)
All saddle equilibria have H=1/3. The factorization
H−31​=(1+2y)[x2−3(y−1)2​]
(27)
shows that their central separatrix consists of the three sides of an equilateral triangle. Each level 0<H<1/3 inside this triangle is a closed periodic orbit. Indeed, inside the triangle 0<r<1 and ∂r​H=2r(1+rsin3θ)>0. Each ray from the origin therefore meets each such level once, giving a compact simple closed contour with no equilibrium point on it. The nonzero vector field traverses this contour periodically; the period grows without bound as the separatrix is approached. This supplies an infinite family, not a claim that every level outside the central region is closed.
Restore the small radial perturbation. Its exact effect on the first integral is
Hτ​=ω(μ​−r2)(xHx​+yHy​)=2ω(μ​−r2)r2(1+rsin3θ),r=∣z∣.
(28)
Consequently the continuum of Hamiltonian system orbits generally does not persist. The origin becomes a weak attracting focus for μ​<0 or a repelling focus for μ​>0, and the three hyperbolic saddle equilibria persist with perturbed stable manifold and unstable manifold. For a small positive μ​, outward drift on very small orbits balances cubic damping on somewhat larger ones, selecting a stable limit cycle rather than an arbitrary energy level. Near the center equilibrium H≃r2, so its leading radius is r≃μ​​ when μ​ is also small.
For a more general closed unperturbed orbit Γh​, the averaged area criterion for perturbed Hamiltonian cycles says that persistence requires the averaged energy drift ∮Γh​​Hτ​dτ to vanish. Since the unperturbed speed is ∣∇H∣/2, the planar divergence theorem converts this leading drift to
∮Γh​​Hτ​dτ=4ω[μ​∣Dh​∣−2∫Dh​​r2dA],
(29)
where Dh​ is the enclosed region. Isolated zeros select candidate periodic orbits; a drift changing from positive inside to negative outside gives an attracting limit cycle. The separatrix triangle has mean r2=1/4, so its leading flux changes sign at μ​=1/2. This marks the leading possible heteroclinic transition, with higher-order corrections needed to locate it precisely.
As a cycle approaches the saddle equilibria, long residence times and splitting of the heteroclinic cycle become important. Orbits can instead drift inward to the equilibrium point at the origin or leave the periodic island and approach one of the stable states with phase locking of the full canonical equation. Those upper-branch threefold phase-locked equilibria have C=O(1), so they lie outside the local C=O(ω) scaling. Thus the small perturbation gives energy selection, attracting or repelling oscillations, and possible switching/locking transitions; it does not preserve a conserved H or an infinite family of neutral periodic solutions. This qualitative picture does not assume all global parameter values have the same attractor.
