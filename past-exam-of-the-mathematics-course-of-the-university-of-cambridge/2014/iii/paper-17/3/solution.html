Write Lα=ω∧α, the Lefschetz operator of a Kähler manifold. The metric and volume form define the L2 inner product on smooth complex forms, and Λ=L∗ is its formal adjoint. Similarly, ∂ˉ∗ is the formal adjoint of the Dolbeault operator, characterized by ⟨∂ˉβ,α⟩=⟨β,∂ˉ∗α⟩. The Dolbeault Laplacian is
Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ.​
(13)
On the compact manifold without boundary, integration by parts gives
⟨Δ∂ˉ​α,α⟩=∥∂ˉα∥22​+∥∂ˉ∗α∥22​.
(14)
If the Laplacian vanishes, both terms are zero. Conversely, if both operators annihilate α, the defining formula annihilates it. Thus harmonicity is equivalent to being both ∂ˉ-closed and ∂ˉ∗-closed.
Because ω is closed and has type (1,1), ∂ˉω=0 and [L,∂ˉ]=0. The supplied identity from the Kähler identities gives [L,∂ˉ∗]=−i∂. With ordinary commutators for the even-degree operator L,
[L,Δ∂ˉ​]​=[L,∂ˉ]∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ[L,∂ˉ∗]+[L,∂ˉ∗]∂ˉ+∂ˉ∗[L,∂ˉ]=−i(∂ˉ∂+∂∂ˉ)=0.​
(15)
The last identity follows from d2=0. This also proves that the Lefschetz operator preserves harmonic forms.
For the cohomology map one can work directly with forms: ∂ˉ(ωk∧α)=ωk∧∂ˉα, since ω has even degree. It takes closed forms to closed forms and exact forms to exact forms. Therefore the kth power of L induces
ϕω,k​([α])=[ωk∧α].​
(16)
The bidegree is (p+k,q+k), including the zero groups outside the dimension range. No isomorphism claim is needed here.
Finally put θ=−i∂f, so ∂ˉθ=i∂∂ˉf=ω′−ω. Let
Tk​=∑j=0k−1​(ω′)j∧ωk−1−j.
(17)
Both Kähler forms are ∂ˉ-closed, so ∂ˉTk​=0. For a closed representative α,
(ω′)k∧α−ωk∧α=∂ˉ(θ∧Tk​∧α).
(18)
This primitive has type (p+k,q+k−1); the degree-one sign produces no additional term because Tk​∧α is closed. Hence
ϕω,k​=ϕω′,k​for every k≥1.​
(19)
This is the dependence of Lefschetz maps on the Dolbeault class.
