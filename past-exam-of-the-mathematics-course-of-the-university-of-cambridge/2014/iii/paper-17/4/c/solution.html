To prove (a) implies (c), shrink to a contractible coordinate neighborhood. The real Poincaré lemma gives a real one-form η with ω=dη. Write η=η1,0+η0,1. Because ω has type (1,1), ∂ˉη0,1=0 and ∂η1,0=0. The Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma supplies a smooth function ψ with η0,1=∂ˉψ. Reality of η gives η1,0=∂ψ​. Thus
ω=∂∂ˉψ+∂ˉ∂ψ​=∂∂ˉ(ψ−ψ​)=i∂∂ˉ(2Imψ).
(26)
Taking the real function f=2Imψ gives the local real potential for a closed (1,1)-form. Conversely, d(i∂∂ˉf)=0, by ∂2=∂ˉ2=0 and anticommutation. Hence (a) and (c) are equivalent, completing all three conditions.
For the unheaded radial continuation, take the real potential f as in (c). Rotation invariance makes f constant on every circle of radius r>0, so
u(t)=f(et/2),t∈R,​

fzzˉ​(z)=∣z∣2u′′(t)​=e−tu′′(t).

ω=iq(z)dz∧dzˉ,g=2q(z)(dx2+dy2).

At the origin, rotation invariance gives the finite Taylor expansion f(z)=f(0)+b∣z∣2+O(∣z∣4), with b=q(0). Alternatively continuity of q directly gives
limt→−∞​e−tu′′(t)=q(0).
(30)
The metric is positive away from the origin exactly when u′′(t)>0 for all finite t, and it is positive at the origin exactly when this limit is positive. Smoothness at the origin is already supplied by the original smooth potential. Consequently the positivity criterion for a radial Kähler potential is
u′′(t)>0 for every t∈R,t→−∞lim​e−tu′′(t)>0.​
(31)
Closedness is automatic from the potential, and positivity completes the Kähler condition. No completeness of this metric is asserted. The rotation-invariant Kähler potential on the complex plane is understood as real; if a complex radial potential is initially allowed with real ω, its smooth radial imaginary part is harmonic and hence constant, so that constant can be removed.
