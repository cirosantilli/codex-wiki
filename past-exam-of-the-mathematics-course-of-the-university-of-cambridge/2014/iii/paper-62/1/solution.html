The Schmidt decomposition theorem states that a normalized vector in a finite-dimensional tensor product HA​⊗HB​ has the form ∑j=1r​sj​∣uj​⟩∣vj​⟩, where the two families are orthonormal, sj​>0, ∑j​sj2​=1, and r≤min(dimHA​,dimHB​). Extending each family to an orthonormal basis for two qubits gives
∣ψ⟩=λ0​∣00⟩+λ1​∣11⟩,λi​≥0,λ02​+λ12​=1.
(1)
The nonzero Schmidt coefficients can be made positive by absorbing phases into the basis vectors. Both are positive exactly when the pure state is entangled. A product state has Schmidt rank one, so one coefficient is zero; the source's assertion of two positive coefficients for every pure state needs this exception.
Choose each local orthonormal basis independently as the computational basis. In that basis σz​=∣0⟩⟨0∣−∣1⟩⟨1∣. This is a local change of coordinates, implemented by separate unitary matrices, rather than a physical restriction on the original quantum state. The associated Pauli operators supply the other two local axes. Up to an irrelevant common phase, each unitary change of qubit basis corresponds to a rotation of its Bloch sphere.
Put s=2λ0​λ1​. The Schmidt-basis Pauli correlation tensor is diagonal. The Pauli operators σx​⊗σx​ exchange ∣00⟩ and ∣11⟩, while σy​⊗σy​ do so with minus signs, and σz​⊗σz​ leaves both fixed. Hence
⟨σx​⊗σx​⟩=s,⟨σy​⊗σy​⟩=−s,⟨σz​⊗σz​⟩=1.
(2)
Mixed components vanish: those containing one z and one transverse Pauli operator map the occupied basis vectors outside their span, while the xy and yx matrix elements are purely imaginary and cancel for real Schmidt coefficients. By bilinearity, for arbitrary real vectors,
P(a,b)=s(ax​bx​−ay​by​)+az​bz​.​
(3)
For a CHSH inequality test take unit measurement in quantum mechanics axes
a=ez​,a′=ex​,b=1+s2​ez​+sex​​,b′=1+s2​ez​−sex​​.
(4)
These CHSH axes for an entangled pure two-qubit state give
S=P(a,b)+P(a,b′)+P(a′,b)−P(a′,b′)=21+s2​>2(s>0).​
(5)
The local bound is two: for each hidden state, A(B+B′)+A′(B−B′) is ±2 when all four outcomes are ±1, and averaging cannot increase its absolute value. Thus every entangled pure two-qubit state violates a CHSH inequality, the content of Gisin's theorem. A product state has s=0 and does not violate it, so the unqualified final claim in the question is false for that case. The maximum 22​ occurs for equal Schmidt coefficients. This excludes local hidden-variable theories satisfying measurement independence, but does not permit faster-than-light signalling: the local reduced density matrix and its measurement in quantum mechanics probabilities are unchanged by the remote choice of axis.
