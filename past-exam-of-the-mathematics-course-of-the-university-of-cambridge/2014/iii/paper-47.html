Use the Minkowski metric diag(1,−1) and the angular field u=βϕ. The Euler-Lagrange equation becomes utt​−uxx​+m2sinu=0. For the dimensionless light-cone coordinates
ξ=2m​(x+t),η=2m​(x−t),
(1)
this is uξη​=sinu. This normalization keeps the coupling in the relation between the physical field and its angle, rather than silently setting β=1.
A Bäcklund transformation is a system of first-order differential relations that maps a solution to another solution. One convention for the Sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformation uses a nonzero parameter a and defines v=Ba​[u] by
∂ξ​2v+u​=a1​sin2v−u​,∂η​2v−u​=asin2v+u​.​
(2)
A compatible initial value or integration constant selects a particular transformed solution. Put w=(v+u)/2 and d=(v−u)/2. Differentiating gives wξη​=cosdsinw and dξη​=coswsind. Their sum and difference yield
vξη​=sin(w+d)=sinv,uξη​=sin(w−d)=sinu.
(3)
Thus compatibility of the first-order relations contains the field equations for both fields, and the transformed physical field v/β also solves Sine-Gordon theory.
The transformation provides a generating conservation law. On the branch close to the original field write v=u+2d. Its first equation is
sind=a(uξ​+dξ​).
(4)
It recursively determines a formal small-a expansion of d in local derivatives of u:
d=auξ​+a2uξξ​+a3(uξξξ​+61​uξ3​)+⋯.
(5)
At every subsequent order the new coefficient occurs linearly, so this recursion continues indefinitely. The second Bäcklund equation and the first imply the exact identity
∂η​P(a)=∂ξ​Q(a),P(a)=a21−cosd​,Q(a)=1−cos(u+d).​
(6)
Indeed ∂η​cosd=−asindsin(u+d) and ∂ξ​cos(u+d)=−a−1sindsin(u+d). Comparing powers of a therefore gives local conserved currents. If ∂η​Pj​=∂ξ​Qj​, our coordinate convention gives
∂t​(Pj​+Qj​)=∂x​(Pj​−Qj​),Ij​=∫R​(Pj​+Qj​)dx.
(7)
For localized fields approaching vacua at spatial infinity, the boundary flux vanishes and Ij​ is conserved. The formal expansion need not converge: each coefficient is a separately exact local conservation law.
For example P0​=uξ2​/2, Q0​=1−cosu, a light-cone combination of energy and momentum. The next coefficient is a derivative improvement, so it contributes no independent charge under the same decay conditions. At order a2, remove the improvement generated by ∂ξ​(uξ​uξξ​) and multiply by −2. One obtains the genuinely higher conservation law
∂η​(uξξ2​−41​uξ4​)=∂ξ​(cosuuξ2​).​
(8)
It can also be checked directly using uξη​=sinu. Continuing the recursion, and using the opposite light-cone construction, produces the local conserved-charge hierarchy of sine-Gordon theory, with infinitely many nontrivial higher-spin charges after derivative improvements are removed. This is the Bäcklund generating current for sine-Gordon conserved charges. The hierarchy is the characteristic field-theory form of classical integrability; an ordinary energy conservation law alone would not supply these constraints.
The same transformation constructs solutions rather than only currents. Starting from the vacuum u0​=0, its two first-order equations integrate to
ua​=4arctanEa​,Ea​=exp(ξ/a+aη+δa​).​
(9)
For a=eρ>0 the exponent is mcoshρx−msinhρt+δa​. This is a Sine-Gordon kink with velocity tanhρ, center set by δa​ and classical rest mass 8m/β2. Negative parameters can supply the corresponding opposite-orientation seeds.
The allowed Bianchi permutability for sine-Gordon Bäcklund transformations gives the two-step field algebraically. For vacuum seed and a=b,
tan4ua,b​​=b−aa+b​1+Ea​Eb​Eb​−Ea​​.​
(10)
Branches of the inverse tangent must be continued smoothly; its principal value alone does not specify the vacuum labels of a multi-kink field. All angles here are u=βϕ. In the physical-field version each field difference in the superposition formula carries β; the formula printed without it implicitly uses the β=1 angular-field convention.
To display two real scattering solutions, take 0<v<1, set Γ=(1−v2)−1/2, and put X=mΓx, Y=mΓvt. Choosing a=eρ, b=e−ρ with v=tanhρ and zero phase constants yields the Sine-Gordon kink-antikink scattering solution
uKKˉ​(x,t)=−4arctanvcoshXsinhY​.​
(11)
Choosing instead b=−e−ρ yields the Sine-Gordon two-kink solution
uKK​(x,t)=4arctancoshYvsinhX​.​
(12)
The latter has net angular winding 4π, while the former has zero net winding. At large positive or negative time they separate into localized kinks with velocities ±v. Repeated commuting transformations give general multi-soliton fields. Continuing the kink-antikink velocity to an imaginary value also gives a real Sine-Gordon breather:
uB​(x,t)=4arctan[ωm2−ω2​​cosh(m2−ω2​x)sinωt​],0<ω<m,
(13)
up to the irrelevant overall field sign and translations.
The scattering interpretation ties the construction to the conserved hierarchy. In an exact multi-soliton sector, the incoming species and rapidities reappear after the collision, up to permutation: the solitons change positions, not their asymptotic shapes or velocities, and the collision emits no radiation. In the two-kink example the large-time centers obey ∣X∣=∣Y∣−logv+o(1), so a right-moving trajectory acquires a shift −2logv/(mΓ). These shifts encode the interaction even though the collision is elastic. The commuting construction makes the net displacement in a many-soliton collision the sum of its pairwise displacements, independent of how the collisions are ordered; this is pairwise additivity of soliton shifts and the classical counterpart of factorized scattering.
Conservation of the entire hierarchy is much stronger than conservation of energy and momentum: its independent rapidity-weighted sums constrain the whole asymptotic soliton data and rule out particle production in this sector. Generic initial fields may also contain radiation scattering data, so this statement is about the exact soliton collisions, not a claim that every initial field is a pure soliton. The Bäcklund map unifies the conserved hierarchy, explicit soliton construction and elastic, pairwise scattering picture of classical integrability.

Take r>0 and m>0, the positive-energy sigma-model regime, and keep θ=0 for the energy calculation. For a static field the energy is
E=4r​∫R​[ϕx2​+sin2ϕαx2​+m2sin2ϕ]dx.
(14)
For either sign s=±1, complete the square:
E​=4r​∫[(ϕx​−smsinϕ)2+sin2ϕαx2​]dx+2rms​∫ϕx​sinϕdx=4r​∫[(ϕx​−smsinϕ)2+sin2ϕαx2​]dx+rmsT.​
(15)
Here ∫ϕx​sinϕdx=−[cosϕ]−∞+∞​=2T. Choose s to have the sign of T. The Bogomolny bound is therefore
E≥rm∣T∣.​
(16)
Saturation requires ϕx​=smsinϕ and sinϕαx​=0. Finite-energy vacua have sinϕ=0, so their endpoint cosine values are ±1 and this particular topological charge is T∈{−1,0,1}. This is the static kink bound in a mass-deformed spherical sigma model. Negative r would instead make the derivative energy unbounded below, so positivity is an essential physical convention.

For T=1, choose the positive Bogomolny equation and constant angular orientation α=α0​. Integrating dϕ/sinϕ=mdx gives
ϕ(x)=2arctanem(x−x0​),α(x)=α0​.​
(17)
This interpolates from ϕ(−∞)=0 to ϕ(+∞)=π, so T=−(−1−1)/2=1. Its useful profiles are sinϕ=sech[m(x−x0​)] and cosϕ=−tanh[m(x−x0​)]. It obeys the second-order field equation because differentiating ϕx​=msinϕ gives ϕxx​=m2sinϕcosϕ; the angular field equation holds since α is constant. Thus
E=M=rm.​
(18)
Both x0​ and α0​ are collective coordinates: translation and global angular rotation do not change the energy. This static solution is the zero-charge member of the rotating charged kink in a spherical sigma model.

Fix ϵ01=+1 with x0=t, x1=x. The Noether current for the unit shift of α is the derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to ∂μ​α:
jμ=2r​sin2ϕ∂μα−4πθ​ϵνμ∂ν​(cosϕ).​
(19)
The angular field equation is ∂μ​jμ=0. At θ=0 this gives the conserved Noether charge
Q0​=2r​∫R​sin2ϕα˙dx.​
(20)
For nonzero θ, ϵ10=−1, so j0 contains +θ∂x​(cosϕ)/(4π). Its integral is a boundary term:
Qθ​=Q0​+4πθ​[cosϕ]−∞+∞​=Q0​−2πθ​T.​
(21)
The theta term is locally a total derivative, so it does not change the bulk equations or the energy, but it changes the canonical charge by this topological shift. This is the theta-angle shift of a sigma-model kink charge. Reversing the convention for ϵ01 reverses the displayed theta shifts. Distinguishing the mechanical charge Q0​ from the canonical symmetry charge Qθ​ will be important for quantization.

The internal orientation α0​ is a periodic collective coordinate with period 2π. Its conjugate momentum is the integrated canonical charge. The Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization for a positively oriented circuit of this angle is, in units ℏ=1,
∮pα​dα=2πQθ​=2πℓ,ℓ∈Z.
(27)
There is no oscillator turning point and hence no half-integer Maslov correction for this cyclic coordinate. Equivalently, a wavefunction eiQθ​α0​ must be single valued. At θ=0, the allowed signed charges and leading semiclassical masses in the T=1 family are therefore
Q=ℓ∈Z,Mℓ​=mr2+ℓ2​.​
(28)
The signed integers include both rotation directions and the nonrotating ℓ=0 state. Every finite integer gives ∣ω∣<m.
For nonzero theta it is still the canonical charge that is integral, while the mechanical charge is shifted:
Qθ​=ℓ,Q0​=ℓ+2πθ​T,MT,ℓ​=mr2+(ℓ+2πθ​T)2​.​
(29)
For the stated family set T=1; the reversed kink gives the corresponding T=−1 family. This is the semiclassical charged-kink spectrum with a theta angle. One may equivalently describe the spectrum by fractional mechanical charges instead of integral canonical charges, but the two conventions must not be mixed. The theta periodicity follows by relabeling the integer when θ↦θ+2π. These are leading semiclassical energies; the quantization argument does not assert that quantum fluctuation corrections vanish.

Use the physical rapidity strip 0<Imϑ<π for poles of the two-body S-matrix, and write ϑ for scattering rapidity to distinguish it from the theta angle in question 2. A denominator in the kink-antikink product vanishes at
ϑ=iuk​,uk​=π(1−nk​),k=1,…,n−1.
(30)
No numerator cancels these poles. For two equal-mass constituents with rapidities χ±iuk​/2, their four-momentum vectors sum to
M(cosh(χ+iuk​/2),sinh(χ+iuk​/2))+M(cosh(χ−iuk​/2),sinh(χ−iuk​/2))=2Mcos(uk​/2)(coshχ,sinhχ).
(31)
This gives the relativistic bound-state mass from a rapidity pole. The ordered breather spectrum is
mk​=2Msin2nkπ​,kmax​=n−1.​
(32)
It increases strictly with k. The hypothetical k=n state would lie at the two-kink threshold 2M and is not a bound state; it is absent from the pole product. At these couplings M=mn/π and β2=8π/(n+1). In particular m1​→m at weak coupling n→∞. This is the Sine-Gordon breather spectrum at reflectionless couplings. At n=1 there are no breathers; the subsequent processes involving a physical B2​ require n≥3.
For two identical neutral B1​ particles, exchanging the two outgoing labels does not produce a distinguishable channel. In one spatial dimension the elastic final momenta are the incoming pair, up to interchange. Thus there is one scalar identical-particle amplitude, rather than separately observable transmission and reflection amplitudes.
Put a=π/(2n), so the basic amplitude uses sin(2a). Its poles in the physical strip occur at ϑ=2ia and ϑ=i(π−2a). The first is the direct bound-state pole. Choosing constituent rapidities χ±ia gives real total energy-momentum
p1​+p2​=2m1​cosa(coshχ,sinhχ).
(33)
Since m1​=2Msina,
2m1​cosa=2Msin(2a)=m2​.​
(34)
Both energy and momentum therefore match an on shell B2​ with rapidity χ. The complementary pole is its crossed-channel partner. At n=2 the would-be B2​ is a threshold state, so this physical fusion interpretation must not be imposed there.
The bound-state fusion of factorized S-matrices treats a bound particle as its on shell constituents with analytically continued rapidities. If equal-mass particles A,A fuse to C at relative rapidity iu, use constituent rapidities χ±iu/2. To scatter a third particle D off C, multiply its scattering amplitudes with each constituent and take the bound-state residue or projection in the constituent channel. For a scalar amplitude this gives
SC,D​(χ−χD​)=SA,D​(χ−χD​+iu/2)SA,D​(χ−χD​−iu/2).
(35)
For particles with internal indices, the product is projected using the bound-state coupling tensors. The heuristic reason is factorized scattering: conserved higher charges prevent particle production and fix the rapidity data, so the third particle scatters through the constituents by successive two-body processes. Consistency of different orders is the Yang-Baxter equation. Without integrability, an independent three-body interaction would invalidate this simple bootstrap product.
For B2​=(B1​B1​), the constituent shifts are ±ia, giving
S2,1​(ϑ)=S1,1​(ϑ+ia)S1,1​(ϑ−ia).​
(36)
It is useful to write this Sine-Gordon breather fusion amplitude in explicitly factorized form:
S2,1​(ϑ)=sinhϑ−isinasinhϑ+isina​sinhϑ−isin(3a)sinhϑ+isin(3a)​.​
(37)
To check the reduction, put z=sinhϑ. Multiplying the shifted factors gives numerator and denominator z2±2izsin(2a)cosa+sin2a−sin2(2a). Use sina+sin3a=2sin2acosa and sinasin3a=sin22a−sin2a to factor them as (z±isina)(z±isin3a). The product tends to one at large positive real rapidity, fixing its overall phase in this bootstrap convention.
For n≥3, the nearest pole to the real axis is ϑ=ia. It is simple and comes from the first factor. In the crossed, or t-channel, the momentum carried between the external particles is their difference. With the +,− metric its invariant is
t=m22​+m12​−2m2​m1​coshϑ.
(38)
At the pole, substitute the breather masses:
t​=4M2[sin2(2a)+sin2a−2sin(2a)sinacosa]=4M2sin2a=m12​​.​
(39)
The trigonometric identity is applied with angles 2a and a. Thus the exchanged one-particle state is the lightest breather B1​, on its mass shell. This is crossed-channel lightest-breather exchange. The external momenta at a bound-state pole are analytically continued; on shell here means the invariant mass relation and conservation of the continued energy-momentum, not a pole at real physical rapidity. At n=3 the more distant central factor has a double pole, but the nearest ia pole and its B1​ interpretation remain unchanged.
