The canonical geometric morphism C→Set has inverse image the constant-presheaf functor Δ and direct image the global sections functor
Γ(P)=Hom(1,P),Δ⊣Γ.
(34)
If the presheaf topos is a local topos, Γ is also the inverse image of a geometric morphism g:Set→C. This morphism has an extra left adjoint Δ. Apply part (iii) with source category 1 and target category C, using its idempotent-splitting hypothesis. Then g is induced by a functor 1→C, choosing an object C0​, and Γ is naturally evaluation at C0​.
Since evaluation at C0​ is Hom(yC0​,−), this says Hom(1,−)≅Hom(yC0​,−). Uniqueness of representing objects gives yC0​≅1. Thus C(C,C0​) is a singleton for every C: C0​ is terminal.
Conversely, if C0​ is terminal, yC0​=1 and Γ(P)=P(C0​). Evaluation at that object preserves finite limits and has a right Kan extension as right adjoint, so is an inverse image functor. Therefore, under the permitted idempotent-completeness assumption,
C is local if and only if C has a terminal object.​
(35)
