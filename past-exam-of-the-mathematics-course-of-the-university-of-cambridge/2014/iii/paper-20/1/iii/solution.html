OurBigBook.com Words: 309
Let Q be the full subcategory of quotients of decidable objects. It is closed under quotients, by composition of epimorphisms, and under small coproducts, by part (ii). It is also closed under subobjects: pull back a decidable cover D↠B along B′↪B. The resulting cover of B′ has domain a subobject of D, hence a decidable object.
The terminal object lies in Q. If D↠B and D′↠C are decidable covers, their product is an epimorphism D×D′↠B×C. Part (ii) makes its domain decidable. Thus products, and then equalizers as subobjects of products, remain in Q. The inclusion I:Q↪E preserves finite limits.
We construct a coreflective subcategory rather than claim that every object has a decidable cover. For X∈E, let qX↪X be the union of all subobjects of X which lie in Q. The Grothendieck topos is well-powered, so these subobjects form a set. Choose a decidable cover of each and take their coproduct. Its map to X has image qX, so qX is itself a quotient of a decidable object. Any map from an object of Q to X has image in Q, and therefore factors uniquely through qX. This gives
I⊣q,E(IB,X)≅Q(B,qX).
(9)
The induced idempotent comonad G=Iq on E preserves finite limits: q is a right adjoint and I preserves those limits. Its counit is the inclusion qX↪X, and q(qX)=qX.
The coalgebras of this comonad are exactly the objects of Q. A coalgebra structure is a section X→qX of the monic counit, forcing the counit to be an isomorphism; conversely an object already in Q has the unique such structure. Thus Q≃EG. Part (i) now gives
Eqd​ is a topos.​
(10)
This argument proves the required elementary-topos conclusion without presuming a small family of decidable generators for E.
