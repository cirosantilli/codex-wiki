A decidable object in a topos has a decomposition A×A=ΔA​⨿DA​, with DA​ representing inequality. In the internal logic of a topos, equality on A is decidable. These categorical complements behave well under pullback.
For a subobject B↪A, pull back the displayed decomposition along B×B↪A×A. The diagonal pulls back to ΔB​, while DA​ pulls back to its complement. Hence every subobject of a decidable object is decidable; the subobject itself need not be complemented in A.
For two decidable objects, the diagonals of A and B give four disjoint summands of (A×B)2, according as each coordinate pair is equal or unequal. The both-equal summand is ΔA×B​, and the other three give its complement. The terminal object is decidable, so induction gives closure under finite products, including the empty product.
For a family (Ai​)i∈I​ of decidable objects with an existing coproduct A=∐i​Ai​, products distribute over this coproduct, giving
A×A≅∐i,j∈I​(Ai​×Aj​).

The coproduct injections in a topos are disjoint. The diagonal consists of ΔAi​​ in each i=j summand, and has complement
(i∐​DAi​​)⨿​i=j∐​Ai​×Aj​​.​

Consequently every existing coproduct of decidable objects is decidable, including the initial object. In a Grothendieck topos all small coproducts exist. No assertion that arbitrary products preserve decidability is used.
