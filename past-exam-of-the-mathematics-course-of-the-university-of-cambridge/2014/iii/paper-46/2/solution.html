I=∫dt[x˙+p+​+x˙−p−​+x˙ipi​−2e​(−2p+​p−​+pi2​+μ2)].

In the light cone gauge x+=t, solve the mass-shell condition for p+​, assuming p−​=0. The reduced phase-space action is ∫dt(x˙−p−​+x˙ipi​−H), with
H=−p+​=−2p−​pi2​+μ2​=2p+pi2​+μ2​,p+=−p−​>0.​

The last equality selects the future-directed momentum sector and makes positivity transparent. With ℏ=1 and pa​=−i∂a​, the Schrödinger equation is
i∂+​Ψ=−2(−i∂−​)−Δ⊥​+μ2​Ψ.

The inverse acts only on Fourier modes with nonzero p−​. Multiplication by 2p−​ gives 2∂−​∂+​Ψ=(Δ⊥​−μ2)Ψ. Therefore
(□D​−μ2)Ψ=0,□D​=−2∂+​∂−​+Δ⊥​.​

The light-cone Hamiltonian thus gives the same Klein-Gordon equation as covariant quantization.
For the massive two-form field, take μ=0. Apply ∂n to its field equation. Antisymmetry of Fmnp​ makes ∂n∂mFmnp​=0, so
∂nAnp​=0.
(19)
Expanding F=dA, the other divergence terms vanish by this condition, leaving (□D​−μ2)Anp​=0. The light-cone decomposition of a massive two-form makes its dependent components explicit. The divergence equation is
−∂−​A+n​−∂+​A−n​+∂i​Ain​=0.
(20)
Taking n=− and n=i, respectively, gives
A+−​=−∂−−1​∂i​A−i​,A+i​=−∂−−1​∂+​A−i​+∂−−1​∂j​Aji​.​
(21)
The n=+ equation follows from these expressions: the two terms containing ∂+​∂i​A−i​ cancel and ∂i​∂j​Aji​=0. Consequently A−i​ and Aij​ are independent, each satisfying the Klein-Gordon equation with mass μ. The number of independent particle polarizations is
d+(2d​)=(2D−1​).​
(22)
This is the exterior square of the vector representation of the massive little group SO(D−1). In the analogous Proca equation, ∂mAm​=0 determines A+​ from A−​ and Ai​, leaving D−1 components. A massive field has no gauge freedom that would justify setting these longitudinal components to zero. If μ=0, instead use the two-form gauge field symmetry A↦A+dΛ: the light-cone gauge for a two-form removes A−m​, leaving (2D−2​) transverse particle polarizations. The massive and massless counts are different.
In the closed-string mode expansion, xm,pm​ are center-of-mass canonical variables, while αki​,αki​ are independent left- and right-moving transverse string oscillators. Their complex conjugates are α−ki​,α−ki​. The two zero-mode Lagrange multipliers impose the remaining mass-shell condition and closed-string level matching. The string level operators are
N=∑k>0​α−k​⋅αk​,N=∑k>0​α−k​⋅αk​.
(23)
Their quantum definitions use normal ordering. The symplectic terms in the phase-space action give
[xm,pn​]=iδnm​,[αmi​,αnj​]=mδijδm+n,0​,[αmi​,αnj​]=mδijδm+n,0​,
(24)
with all brackets between distinct sectors zero. The nonzero-index string oscillators obey αni†​=α−ni​ and similarly for the right-moving sector. Define the momentum-labelled oscillator vacuum by
αki​∣0;p⟩=αki​∣0;p⟩=0(k>0),pm​∣0;p⟩=pmlabel​∣0;p⟩.
(25)
For k>0, aki​=αki​/k​ has [aki​,alj†​]=δkl​δij. Hence
N=∑k,i​kaki†​aki​,[N,α−ki​]=kα−ki​.

Starting with N∣0;p⟩=0, a finite product with rki​ creation operators of mode k has eigenvalue ∑k,i​krki​. The Fock space is generated by these products; both level operators have nonnegative integer eigenvalues. This establishes the integer string oscillator level property. Subtracting their physical zero-mode constraints enforces N=N.
There is a distinction between the displayed classical zero modes and their quantum constraints. With the normal-ordering constant of a string a, these are
8πTp2​+N−a=0,8πTp2​+N−a=0.
(27)
At the massless first closed-string level, the states are
α−1i​α−1j​∣0;p⟩.
(28)
Their transverse polarization tensor splits into a symmetric trace-free part, an antisymmetric part, and its trace. These are the graviton, Kalb–Ramond field, and dilaton, with respective particle polarization counts d(d+1)/2−1, d(d−1)/2, and one. They have the transverse little group representations of massless particles. In a Lorentz-consistent bosonic string theory, the first chiral level is a massless vector, not a massive vector with one missing physical polarization; the closed-string products are therefore massless. This fixes a=1. Equivalently, regularized transverse zero-point energy gives a=(D−2)/24, and Lorentz consistency fixes the critical dimension of the bosonic string D=26.
It follows that the bosonic string mass spectrum is
MN2​=−p2=8πT(N−1)=α′4​(N−1),α′=2πT1​,N=N.​
(29)
The ground state has M02​=−8πT and is a tachyon; level one is massless; for N≥2 the mass is MN​=8πT(N−1)​. The masslessness claim uses the consistent quantum theory, rather than an unshifted reading of the classical L0​.
A massive two-form at closed-string level two is present. To see it without confusing it with the level-one massless Kalb–Ramond field, the level-two states in one chiral sector are
α−2i​∣0⟩,α−1i​α−1j​∣0⟩.
(30)
They have d+d(d+1)/2=(D−1)D/2−1 components and assemble into the symmetric traceless square S02​V of the massive little group vector space V=RD−1. The full closed-string level is S02​V⊗S02​V. For two symmetric trace-free matrices S,S, the map
(S,S)⟼[S,S]ab​=Sac​Scb​−Sac​Scb​
(31)
is an equivariant map onto antisymmetric matrices. To verify surjectivity, take S diagonal with distinct entries in positions a,b and S with only its symmetric ab entry nonzero. Their commutator gives the ab antisymmetric basis element. Finite-dimensional representations of the compact little group are completely reducible, so this quotient representation is also a subrepresentation. It has exactly (2D−1​) particle polarizations and is described by the massive field equation with μ2=8πT. At D=26 this gives 300 particle polarizations, consisting in light-cone coordinates of 24 components A−i​ and 276 components Aij​.
