The Vinogradov mean value is
Jk,r​(Z)=∫[0,1]r​​∑1≤z≤Z​e(θ1​z+⋯+θr​zr)​2kdθ.
(32)
By orthogonality of integer Fourier modes, it counts the ordered integer solutions of ∑i=1k​xij​=∑i=1k​yij​ for 1≤j≤r, with every coordinate between one and ⌊Z⌋. The diagonal solutions yi​=xi​ give Jk,r​(Z)≥⌊Z⌋k.
Put D=r(r+1)/2. The moment vector (∑i​xij​)j=1r​ has at most ∏j=1r​(k⌊Z⌋j+1)≪k,r​ZD possible values. If R(v) counts the tuples with vector v, then ∑v​R(v)=⌊Z⌋k and Jk,r​(Z)=∑v​R(v)2. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives Jk,r​(Z)≫k,r​Z2k−D. Combining the two lower bounds, with one common positive constant for large Z, yields
Jk,r​(Z)≥c(k,r)max{Zk,Z2k−r(r+1)/2}.​

Here is an explicit way that upper bounds enter the Vinogradov mean-value method for a bilinear exponential sum. Write U=∑x,y≤Z​e(∑j​αj​xjyj) and Lj​=k⌊Z⌋j. Two applications of the Holder inequality, followed by grouping equal differences of moment vectors, give
∣U∣4k2≪k,r​Z8k2−4kJk,r​(Z)2∏j=1r​∑∣v∣≤Lj​​min(2Lj​+1,2∥αj​v∥1​).
(34)
At integer αj​v the minimum is defined as 2Lj​+1; ∥u∥ means distance to the nearest integer. To explain the mean-value factor, let r(v) count pairs of k-tuples with prescribed moment difference. It is an autocorrelation of R, so r(v)≤∑w​R(w)2=Jk,r​(Z) by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The first Holder inequality groups the y tuples, and the second groups the x tuples, providing the two factors J. The remaining sums over moment differences are bounded by the displayed finite geometric series estimates. Good Vinogradov mean value upper bounds, together with rational approximation or spacing bounds for αj​, therefore give cancellation in U. The mean-value estimate alone does not force cancellation for arbitrary coefficients: when all αj​ are integers, U=⌊Z⌋2. For the paper take Z=N2/5 and the specified αj​.
