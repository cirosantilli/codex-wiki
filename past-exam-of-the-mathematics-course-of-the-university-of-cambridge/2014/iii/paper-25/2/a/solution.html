For a finite set of primes, expand the geometric factors: their product sums n−s over integers whose prime factors lie in that set. Let the finite sets increase through all primes. Absolute convergence permits passage to the limit and recovers the full Dirichlet series. Moreover ∑p,m≥1​∣p−ms∣/m<∞, so the logarithm converges and the product has no zeros there.

This is the product version of the three-four-one zero-free-region argument. It also proves there are no zeros on ℜs=1: if ζ(1+it0​)=0 for t0​=0, its factor has order at least four as σ↓1, while the real pole contributes only order minus three and the 2t0​ factor remains bounded. The displayed left side would tend to zero, a contradiction. At t0​=0 there is a pole, not a zero.
