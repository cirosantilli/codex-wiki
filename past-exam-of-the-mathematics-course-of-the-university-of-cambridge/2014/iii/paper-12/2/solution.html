Define compactly supported cohomology by

(5)
For K⊆L, the transition map is induced by the identity map of pairs (X,X∖L)→(X,X∖K). Equivalently, take the cochain complex of singular cochains that vanish on every chain contained in the complement of some compact set. Directed unions are exact, giving the same definition.
For R, the intervals [−a,a], a>0, are cofinal among compact subsets. The complement has two contractible components. The long exact sequence in relative cohomology contains the diagonal map Z→Z⊕Z, so its cokernel is H1(R,R∖[−a,a])≅Z, and all the other relative groups vanish. Enlarging the interval preserves the generator given by the difference of the two ends. Therefore
Hctq​(R;Z)={Z,0,​q=1,q=1.​​
(6)
For the compact-support comparison with a one-point compactification, write Y=X+. The assumed Hausdorff one-point compactification is compact; a compact subset K⊆X is closed in Y. The Excision theorem removes {∞} from the pair (Y,Y∖K), because its closure lies inside the open second member. Thus
Hq(X,X∖K)≅Hq(Y,Y∖K).
(7)
Complements of compact subsets of X are exactly the open neighbourhoods of ∞ in Y. The hypothesis supplies a cofinal family of contractible such neighbourhoods U. For every one, the long exact sequence of the pair identifies
Hq(Y,U)≅Hq(Y).
(8)
In degree zero, this is the kernel of evaluation on the component of ∞, identified with reduced cohomology by subtracting the constant value there. In degree one the map H0(Y)→H0(U)≅Z is surjective; in higher degrees the positive cohomology of U vanishes. These identifications are natural for inclusions of contractible neighbourhoods. Passing to the direct limit proves
Hct∗​(X)≅H∗(X+).​
(9)
For the specified disjoint union of lines, a compact subset meets only finitely many components and is bounded in each. Finite unions F×[−a,a], with F⊂Z finite, are cofinal. Applying the preceding relative calculation componentwise gives
Hctq​(Z×R;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​j∈Z⨁​Z,0,​q=1,q=1.​​
(10)
The one-point compactification W of this space is the Hawaiian earring: each line becomes a circle by adding the common point ∞, and every neighbourhood of ∞ contains all but finitely many whole circles. On the remaining finitely many circles it contains neighbourhoods of the common point. This describes exactly the shrinking-circle topology. In particular W is not locally contractible at ∞: every such neighbourhood contains a whole circle, whose generator remains nontrivial under the retraction W→S1 that collapses all the other circles.
For integral singular cohomology, the comparison does not hold. Here is a degree-two obstruction that takes account of the shrinking-circle topology. The standard rational summand in Hawaiian earring homology theorem gives a direct summand Q in H1​(W;Z). The universal coefficient theorem for cohomology injects
ExtZ1​(H1​(W;Z),Z)↪H2(W;Z).
(11)
The summand Q therefore contributes the nonzero Ext of the rationals with integer coefficients.
For completeness, this last algebraic assertion has an explicit proof. Present Q using generators an​=1/n! and relations an​−(n+1)an+1​=0, n≥1. The corresponding free resolution shows that ExtZ1​(Q,Z) is the cokernel of
∏n≥1​Z⟶∏n≥1​Z,(un​)⟼(un​−(n+1)un+1​).
(12)

u1​=∑j=1N​j!+(N+1)!uN+1​for every N.

For large N, the factorial sum exceeds ∣u1​∣ but is less than (N+1)!−∣u1​∣, making that congruence impossible. Thus the cokernel is nonzero. It follows that
