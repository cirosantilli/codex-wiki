Use integral cohomology and let k≥1. On the projective bundle Y=P(E) define the complex tautological line bundle
L={(ℓ,v):ℓ⊂Ex​ is a complex line, v∈ℓ}⊆π∗E.
(25)
Put t=e(L)∈H2(Y;Z), using the canonical complex orientation. On each fibre, t is the Euler class of the tautological line over CPk−1, so 1,t,…,tk−1 restrict to an integral basis of its cohomology.
Here is the finite-cover Leray-Hirsch theorem proof in this case. For each open set U⊆X, define
ΦU​:⨁i=0k−1​Hq−2i(U;Z)⟶Hq(π−1U;Z),(ui​)⟼∑i​π∗ui​⌣ti.
(26)
If E is trivial on U, its projective bundle is U×CPk−1 and L is pulled back from the tautological line on the second factor. The Künneth theorem makes ΦU​ an isomorphism, since the fibre has finite free integral cohomology. The same holds for every open subset of U.
Compactness of X provides a finite trivializing cover U1​,…,UN​. Induct on its size. If the result holds on V=U1​∪⋯∪UN−1​, it holds on UN​ and on V∩UN​, both lying in a trivializing chart. Form the diagram of Mayer–Vietoris sequences for the base, with the finitely many degree shifts on the left, and the total space on the right. Naturality of pullback and multiplication by the global even-degree classes ti makes the diagram commute. The Five lemma gives the isomorphism on V∪UN​. Thus
H∗(Y;Z)=i=0⨁k−1​π∗H∗−2i(X;Z)ti.​
(27)
This is the claimed free module statement, with its graded degree shifts made explicit.
The module basis expresses tk uniquely using the lower powers, with homogeneous coefficients. Define the Chern classes cj​(E)∈H2j(X;Z) by the unique relation
f(t)=tk−π∗c1​(E)tk−1+π∗c2​(E)tk−2+⋯+(−1)kπ∗ck​(E)=0.​
(28)
The pullbacks are suppressed when f is regarded as a polynomial over H∗(X). Evaluation at t gives a surjective map H∗(X)[T]→H∗(Y). Since f(T) is monic, monic polynomial division over a ring writes any polynomial as q(T)f(T)+r(T) with degree of r below k. If its evaluation is zero, module independence forces every coefficient of r to vanish. Hence the kernel is exactly the ideal generated by f, proving
H∗(P(E);Z)≅H∗(X;Z)[T]/(f(T)).​
(29)
The even-degree coefficients are central in the graded commutative algebra, so this division and ideal statement also apply when the base has odd-degree cohomology. Uniqueness of the coefficients proves their naturality under pullback, by pulling back the relation and using the same module basis. With the hyperplane convention u=−t, the relation has the usual all-positive Chern coefficients; the alternating signs here correspond to the tautological line itself.
Now suppose E=⨁i​Li​. The sections of a projective bundle si​:X→Y choose the line (Li​)x​⊂Ex​. They satisfy si∗​L≅Li​, so si∗​t=ei​ and pulling back the relation gives f(ei​)=0.
To obtain the full factorization over the possibly torsion-containing base ring, also use the associated open charts
Vi​={ℓ∈P(E):ℓ→(Li​)π(ℓ)​ has nonzero coordinate projection}.
(30)
They contain the images of the sections si​ and cover Y. Projection identifies L∣Vi​​ with π∗Li​∣Vi​​, so t−π∗ei​ restricts to zero on Vi​. The long exact sequence of the pair lifts this class to H2(Y,Vi​). The relative cup product of the k lifted classes lies in
H2k(Y,V1​∪⋯∪Vk​)=H2k(Y,Y)=0.
(31)
Its absolute image is ∏i​(t−π∗ei​), so that product vanishes. The polynomial ∏i​(T−ei​) is monic of degree k and lies in the kernel of evaluation. Subtracting the monic generator f(T) leaves degree below k, and module independence again makes the difference zero. Therefore
f(T)=i=1∏k​(T−ei​),cj​(E)=i1​<⋯<ij​∑​ei1​​⌣⋯⌣eij​​.​
(32)
The open-cover argument proves the factorization without a non-zero-divisor assumption on the differences ei​−ej​.
