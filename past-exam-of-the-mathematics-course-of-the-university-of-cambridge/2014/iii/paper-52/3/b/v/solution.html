For the scalar wave separation in Kerr spacetime, continue to use A=r2+a2 and s=sinθ. First verify the determinant in the hint. Direct multiplication of the covariant t,ϕ components gives
Σ2(gtt​gϕϕ​−gtϕ2​)​=−s2(Δ−a2s2)(A2−Δa2s2)−a2s4(A−Δ)2=−Δs2(A−a2s2)2=−Δs2Σ2.​
(31)
Hence the block determinant is −Δsin2θ. Inverting this block gives
gtt=−ΣΔA2−Δa2s2​,gtϕ=−ΣΔa(A−Δ)​,gϕϕ=ΣΔs2Δ−a2s2​.
(32)
The other inverse components are grr=Δ/Σ, gθθ=1/Σ, and −g​=Σs. The covariant wave operator on a scalar consequently has the divergence form
□Ψ=Σs1​∂μ​(Σsgμν∂ν​Ψ).

Let mϕ​ denote the azimuthal mode number, to distinguish it from the axial vector m. Insert the mode Ψ=e−iωt+imϕ​ϕR(r)Θ(θ) in the massless Klein-Gordon equation. Single-valuedness makes mϕ​ an integer. The t,ϕ derivatives give
Σ(−ω2gtt+2ωmϕ​gtϕ−mϕ2​gϕϕ)=Δ(Aω−amϕ​)2​+2aωmϕ​−a2ω2sin2θ−sin2θmϕ2​​.

With K(r)=Aω−amϕ​, division by the mode factor gives, on patches where RΘ=0,
R(ΔR′)′​+ΔK2​+2aωmϕ​−a2ω2+sinθΘ(sinθΘ′)′​+a2ω2cos2θ−sin2θmϕ2​​=0.
(35)
The radial and angular expressions must be opposite constants. Defining the separation constant as Λ, we obtain the two ordinary differential equations
sinθ1​dθd​(sinθdθdΘ​)+(a2ω2cos2θ−sin2θmϕ2​​+Λ)Θ=0,​
(36)
drd​(ΔdrdR​)+[Δ((r2+a2)ω−amϕ​)2​−a2ω2+2aωmϕ​−Λ]R=0.​
(37)
These equations also hold at zeros of a mode by continuity, without dividing there. Regular angular solutions are scalar spheroidal harmonics, with discrete Λ=Λℓmϕ​​(aω). For aω=0 the angular equation becomes the associated Legendre function equation, with Λ=ℓ(ℓ+1) and ℓ≥∣mϕ​∣, providing a useful check of the signs and normalization. The radial function here is exactly R in the chosen ansatz, without an additional factor of 1/r.
