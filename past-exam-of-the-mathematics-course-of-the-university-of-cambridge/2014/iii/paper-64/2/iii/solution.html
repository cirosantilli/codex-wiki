The literal assumptions suffice for the scalar quadratic fidelity problem. One can avoid regularity of level-set boundaries by moving only a clipped part of the minimizer. We prove the stronger jump-amplitude inequality for total variation denoising:
[u]νu​​([f]νu​​−[u]νu​​)≥0Hn−1-a.e. on Ju​.​
(12)
Comparison with the zero function gives finite energy and hence u∈L2. Here u=u^, and both differences use the same oriented BV traces on a hypersurface. Reversing the normal reverses both differences and leaves the inequality unchanged. Outside Jf​, the two traces of f agree, so the inequality would read −[u]2≥0 at a jump of u. It therefore gives the requested no-new-jumps property of total variation denoising in every dimension.
First use residual-preserving clipping of an ROF minimizer. For an integer M>0, put
w=TM​u=max(−M,min(M,u)),r=u−w,g=f−r=f−u+w.
(13)
The coarea formula for BV functions gives scalar total variation splitting under clipping:
TV(u)=TV(w)+TV(r).
(14)
Indeed the levels in (−M,M) contribute to w, while the levels outside that interval contribute to r. This uses the full signed coarea formula. For any z∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω), minimality of u and the triangle inequality for the total variation seminorm give
αTV(w)+αTV(r)+21​∥w−g∥22​≤αTV(z+r)+21​∥z−g∥22​≤αTV(z)+αTV(r)+21​∥z−g∥22​.
(15)
Cancel the tail variation. Thus w is a bounded ROF denoising minimizer for g∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω). The data g may still be unbounded. This is an exact reduction preserving w−g=u−f; it does not truncate the data and then pass to a limit of different reconstructions.
We next prove the jump-amplitude inequality for a bounded ROF minimizer, allowing its data to be unbounded. Fix a coordinate j, a nonnegative φ∈Cc∞​(Ω), and let Φt​ be the local flow of the smooth vector field Y=φej​. The flow is the identity near the domain boundary, preserves each line parallel to ej​, and satisfies Φ−t​=Φt−1​. Write
Tt​a=a∘Φt​,δt​a=Tt​a−a,Jt​=detDΦt​.
(16)
These maps are diffeomorphisms, with J±t​=1+O(t) and Jt​+J−t​−2=O(t2) uniformly.
The useful trace calculation is the BV jump-product limit with one bounded factor. For a∈BV(Ω) and b∈BV(Ω)∩L∞(Ω),
limt↓0​t1​∫Ω​δt​aδt​bdx=∫Jb​​[a]νb​​[b]νb​​φ∣νb​⋅ej​∣dHn−1.
(17)
The right side is integrable: ∣[b]∣≤2∥b∥∞​ and only the common jump part of a contributes. The same limit holds with both increments replaced by their negative-time increments, still dividing by positive t.
Here is why this calculation needs only one bounded factor. By the BV slicing theorem, almost every coordinate slice of a and b has one-sided representatives. On one such interval let μ=Da and use its right-continuous representative. Since φ≥0,
δt​a(x)=μ((x,Φt​(x)]).
(18)
Fubini's theorem rewrites the slice integral as
t1​∫δt​aδt​bdx=∫(t1​∫Φ−t​(s)s​δt​b(x)dx)dμ(s).
(19)
At each interior s, the inner expression tends to φ(s)(b(s+)−b(s−)); if φ(s)=0, it is zero. Its absolute value is at most 2∥b∥∞​∥φ∥∞​. Dominated convergence against ∣μ∣ therefore leaves just the measure atoms common to the two slices. The bound 2∥b∥∞​∥φ∥∞​∣Da∣ is also integrable over the transverse coordinates, by the BV slicing theorem. Integrating the slice jump sums gives the surface integral and its factor ∣νb​⋅ej​∣. Negative-time increments give the same product, because both slice differences reverse sign. At no point is a uniform bound on a across the slices required.
For 0<θ<1, use the mixed competitors
wt​=(1−θ)w+θTt​w.
(20)
The total variation under opposite smooth flows satisfies
TV(Tt​w)+TV(T−t​w)−2TV(w)=O(t2).
(21)
To see this for the entire vector Radon measure Dw=σw​∣Dw∣, the change of variables formula gives
TV(Tt​w)=∫Ω​∣cof(DΦ−t​)σw​∣d∣Dw∣.
(22)
For cofA=(detA)A−T, the two cofactor matrices expand as I±tB+O(t2), with the same B and opposite signs. Their norm expansions on ∣σw​∣=1 have cancelling linear terms. Integrating proves the estimate, including the absolutely continuous, jump and Cantor parts. The BV transformation formula is also given in Lemma 4.2 on differentiable regularizers; that lemma does not assume bounded data. The total variation seminorm is a convex function, so
TV(wt​)+TV(w−t​)−2TV(w)≤O(t2).
(23)
Thus minimality forces the sum of the two fidelity changes to have nonnegative limit after division by t.
It remains to evaluate that sum without bounding g. Put Fg​(v)=21​∥v−g∥22​. Exact expansion of the quadratic fidelity, together with change of variables, gives the opposite-flow fidelity identity for quadratic data:
Fg​(wt​)+Fg​(w−t​)−2Fg​(w)=−2θ(1−θ)​(∥δt​w∥22​+∥δ−t​w∥22​)+θ∫δt​gδt​wdx+o(t).
(24)
For completeness, the cross-term identity fixing its sign is
∫g(δt​w+δ−t​w)dx=−∫δt​gδt​wdx+∫g(1−J−t​)δ−t​wdx.
(25)
The last integral is o(t): ∥1−J−t​∥∞​=O(t), while
∫∣g∣∣δ−t​w∣dx≤K∥δ−t​w∥1​+2∥w∥∞​∫{∣g∣>K}​∣g∣dx⟶0.
(26)
Take t→0 first, then K→∞. Here g∈L1 and the local flow is strongly continuous in L1. The remaining Jacobian mass term is O(t2)∥w∥22​. This argument avoids multiplying an unbounded fidelity derivative by an uncontrolled derivative measure.
Apply the BV jump-product limit with one bounded factor with (a,b)=(g,w) and (w,w), and use minimality. For every nonnegative φ and every coordinate j,
0≤∫Jw​​([g][w]−(1−θ)[w]2)φ∣νw​⋅ej​∣dHn−1.
(27)
Let θ↓0. These are inequalities for finite signed Radon measures, so arbitrary nonnegative smooth tests imply nonnegativity of their densities. Since at least one coordinate of a unit normal is nonzero,
[w]([g]−[w])≥0Hn−1-a.e. on Jw​.
(28)
This proves the bounded-minimizer lemma with arbitrary BV∩L2 data.
Finally return to w=TM​u and g=f−u+w. At almost every finite approximate jump point of u, choose an integer M>max(∣u+∣,∣u−∣). The BV traces on a hypersurface commute with clipping, so w±=u±, r±=0 and g±=f± there. It is consequently a jump point of w, and its inequality is exactly [u]([f]−[u])≥0. A countable union over M removes all exceptional surface-null sets. Outside Jf​, the BV traces on a hypersurface of f agree almost everywhere. We conclude
Hn−1(Ju^​∖Jf​)=0for f∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω), Ω⊂Rn.​
(29)
The mechanism is exact scalar coarea splitting, paired smooth-flow variations, a one-bounded-factor BV trace limit, and localization through integer clipping levels. It works in every dimension under the printed hypotheses, without essential boundedness of f or u and without regularity of their level-set boundaries.
In total variation calibration notation, divz=(u−f)/α. Since this divergence is itself in the BV space, the proved inequality equivalently reads
[u]νu​​[divz]νu​​≤0.
(30)
Thus an upward output jump forces the appropriate nonpositive jump of the calibrated divergence. This is a consequence of the variational argument above, with common oriented BV traces on a hypersurface; no curvature of the rectifiable interface or differentiability of its normal is assumed.
