For t∈R, define the set functional
Ft​(E)=αPer(E;Ω)+∫E​(t−f)dx.
(9)
The ROF level-set formulation states that the unique minimizer of J(u)=∥u−f∥22​/2+αTV(u) has superlevel sets minimizing Ft​ for almost every t. Conversely, an admissible u with minimizing superlevel sets is the ROF minimizer. Thus the concise characterization is
u^ minimizes ROF⟺{u^>t}∈argEmin​Ft​(E) for a.e. t.​
(10)
The signed layer-cake identity for quadratic fidelity and the coarea formula for BV functions give
J(u)−21​∥f∥22​=∫R​[Ft​({u>t})−Ft​(Et0​)]dt,Et0​={Ω,∅,​t<0,t≥0.​
(11)
Indeed u2/2−fu=∫R​(t−f)(χ{u>t}​−χ{0>t}​)dt pointwise. Its absolute integral is bounded by u2/2+∣fu∣, so Fubini's theorem applies for u,f∈L2. The negative-level baseline is essential; integrating the unadjusted Ft​ would generally diverge.
Each Ft​ attains its minimum. A minimizing sequence of indicator functions has bounded L1 norm, and comparison with the empty set bounds its perimeter by an L1 forcing bound. Bounded-variation compactness supplies a limiting indicator function. The forcing integral converges by dominated convergence, while relative perimeter is sequentially lower semicontinuous.
For s<t, the supplied comparison lemma applied to (f−t)/α<(f−s)/α makes every selected minimizer At​ contained in As​ up to a null set. The comparison of perimeter minimizers with ordered forcing also follows directly: compare At​ with At​∩As​, compare As​ with At​∪As​, add and use submodularity of relative perimeter to obtain (t−s)∣At​∖As​∣≤0.
Select minimizers at rational levels, remove their countably many exceptional null sets, and reconstruct v(x)=sup{q∈Q:x∈Aq​}. The strict superlevel set is {v>t}=⋃q>t​Aq​. This union also minimizes Ft​: take q↓t, use monotone L1 convergence of its indicator functions and sequential lower semicontinuity, and note that m(t)=minE​Ft​(E) satisfies ∣m(t)−m(s)∣≤∣Ω∣∣t−s∣.
To justify finite energy before assuming it, clip this reconstruction to vM​∈[−M,M]. Comparison with the empty set gives αPer(Et​;Ω)≤∫Ω​∣t−f∣ uniformly on a bounded interval of levels. Since vM​=−M+∫−MM​χEt​​dt, pairing with compactly supported test-field divergences bounds its total variation seminorm by ∫−MM​Per(Et​;Ω)dt<∞. Thus vM​ belongs to the BV space before applying the coarea formula for BV functions. The layer-cake argument on the finite interval [−M,M] shows J(vM​)≤J(TM​w) for every finite-energy competitor w, where TM​ is clipping. In particular w=0 bounds the L2 norms and variations uniformly. Fatou's lemma excludes infinite values of v on a positive-measure set. Bounded-variation compactness and weak convergence in L2 identify an admissible limit v. For any fixed competitor, TM​w→w in L2 and its variation tends to that of w, by bounded-variation contraction under clipping and sequential lower semicontinuity. Thus J(v)≤J(w).
The quadratic fidelity is strictly convex, so every ROF minimizer equals v almost everywhere and has the selected minimizing superlevel sets. Conversely, for any u whose levels minimize Ft​, integrate the levelwise inequality against any competitor's levels in the displayed identity to obtain J(u)≤J(w). This proves both directions for signed as well as nonnegative data.
