A scale-invariant primordial potential has PΦ​(k)∝k−3. On subhorizon linear scales, the cosmological Poisson equation and the cold-dark-matter transfer function give δc​(k,a)∝D(a)k2T(k)Φprim​(k), where D(a)∝a during matter domination. Consequently
Pδc​​(k,a)∝D(a)2kT(k)2.
(36)
Large scales enter only after equality and have T(k)≃1. Small scales enter during radiation domination; their approximately logarithmic growth up to equality gives T(k)∝(keq​/k)2ln(k/keq​). Therefore
Pδc​​(k,z=1)∝D(1)2{k,k−3ln2(k/keq​),​H(z=1)≪k≪keq​,k≫keq​.​​
(37)
If the slow logarithm is suppressed in a rough sketch, the slopes are +1 and −3, with a turnover near keq​. The logarithmic correction is real and should not be mistaken for a different primordial spectral index. In the matter-only late-time approximation with D(0)=1, the cosmological redshift z=1 means a=1/2 and D(1)2=1/4; it changes the amplitude, not these asymptotic shapes. Neither the initial normalization nor cosmological parameters needed for an absolute power are supplied.
This is a schematic smooth interpolation with the derived asymptotes, not a precision transfer-function fit. The linear ideal-fluid model excludes baryonic acoustic structure and small-scale nonlinear evolution.
There is also a gauge and horizon qualification. For a mode still outside the Hubble radius at z=1, the printed Newtonian-gauge density has δc​≃−2Φ, and its formal dimensional spectrum is instead proportional to k−3. The conventional large-scale k branch describes modes that are large relative to the equality scale but already subhorizon at the observation time. Alternatively, the comoving matter density, Δc​=δc​+3Hθc​/k2 with θc​=∇⋅vc​, removes that constant gauge term and obeys Δc​=−2k2Φ/(3H2) in the growing matter solution. The usual matter-spectrum sketch can be continued to small k in this comoving-density convention. The dimensional spectrum requested here is P, rather than the dimensionless cosmological power spectrum k3P/(2π2), whose slopes would differ by three.
