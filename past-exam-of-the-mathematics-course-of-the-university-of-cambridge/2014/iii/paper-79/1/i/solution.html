The Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions says that, for each fixed δ>0, a sufficiently long interval cannot have a subset of density of a finite subset at least δ with no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Equivalently, the maximum size of a three-term-progression-free subset of [N]={1,…,N} is o(N). Here is a density increment proof, including its analytic ingredients.
Write e(t)=exp(2πit) and embed I=[N] into the cyclic group G=Z/(2N+1)Z of order M. For normalized Fourier analysis on a finite abelian group take h(r)=M−1∑x​h(x)e(−rx/M). The orthogonality of complex exponentials follows from a finite geometric series: its average is zero for a nonzero frequency and one for frequency zero. Expanding in these linear phases consequently proves both Fourier inversion on a finite group and the Parseval identity on a finite group. In particular,
Ex​∣h(x)∣2=∑r​∣h(r)∣2,Λ(h0​,h1​,h2​):=Ex,d​h0​(x)h1​(x+d)h2​(x+2d)=∑r​h0​(r)h1​(−2r)h2​(r).
(1)
The second identity follows by summing the frequency expansion over x,d: the two constraints leave precisely the frequencies (r,−2r,r). Since M is odd, multiplication by two permutes the frequencies. Also a congruence x+z=2y with x,y,z∈I is an equality in the integers, since ∣x+z−2y∣<M.
Let A⊆I have subset density α=∣A∣/N≥δ, and suppose it contains no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Its balanced indicator function of a finite subset is f=1A​−α1I​, extended by zero outside I. Then f​(0)=0. Counting endpoints with the same parity gives
Λ(1A​,1A​,1A​)=M2αN​,Λ(α1I​,α1I​,α1I​)=M2α3⌈N2/2⌉​.

If N≥4α−2, their difference in absolute value is at least α3N2/(4M2). Telescope the difference of the products into three terms, each containing f and two factors chosen from 1A​,α1I​. Put s=maxr​∣f​(r)∣. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Parseval identity on a finite group bound each term by sαN/M, since the squared normalized L2 norm of either other factor is at most αN/M. Therefore some nonzero frequency r satisfies
s≥12Mα2N​≥36α2​,​∑x∈I​f(x)e(−rx/M)​≥36α2N​.
(3)
This is the Fourier detection of a progression-free subset of an interval step; it has just been proved rather than assumed.
We now turn this Fourier coefficient on a finite abelian group into a density increment. Set Q=⌊N​⌋. Partition the unit interval into Q equal pieces and place the Q+1 fractional parts of 0,r/M,…,Qr/M in them. The pigeonhole principle gives 1≤q≤Q with ∥qr/M∥≤1/Q. This proves the particular Dirichlet approximation theorem needed here. Put κ=α2/(1000π) and L=⌊κQ⌋. For sufficiently large N depending only on δ, L≥κQ/2 and 2L≤Q. Each residue chain modulo q in I has at least Q elements. Split each such chain into arithmetic progressions with between L and 2L−1 elements, absorbing its final short remainder into the previous block.
The linear phase changes by at most 4πL/Q≤α2/250 within any block. Freezing the linear phase at the first element of each block therefore changes the preceding correlation by at most α2N/250, because ∣f∣≤1. The triangle inequality gives
∑P​​∑x∈P​f(x)​≥72α2N​.
(4)
The block sums of the balanced indicator function of a finite subset add to zero, so their positive total is half their absolute total. Some block consequently satisfies
∣P∣≥cα2N​,∣P∣∣A∩P∣​≥α+144α2​.​
(5)
Here c>0 is absolute, and shrinking it absorbs the floors. An affine parametrization of P identifies A∩P with a subset of [∣P∣] and preserves arithmetic progressions.
Repeat this Roth density-increment step. Every step raises the subset density by at least δ2/144, so fewer than T=⌈144/δ2⌉+1 steps are possible. All the lengths satisfy Nj+1​≥cδ2Nj​​ while above a threshold depending only on δ. Choose the initial N so large that this lower recursion stays above that threshold for T steps; this is possible by working backwards through finitely many squarings. We would then force a subset density greater than one. Thus every fixed positive density eventually forces a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. For an infinite subset of the positive integers with positive upper asymptotic density, apply the finite conclusion along initial intervals whose subset density is bounded away from zero; this gives the usual infinite formulation as well.
