For the angular reconstruction of a radial orbit transformation, choose the new angle by dθˉ/dτ=L/q2. The same L then supplies its centrifugal term. From dθ/dt=L/r2, the transformation is dθˉ/dθ=(r/q)(dq/dr); in the harmonic case this gives θˉ=θ/2 up to a constant. Keeping the old angle unchanged would not in general preserve the angular momentum used in the displayed transformed radial equation.
For the second transformation use the explicit displayed split of the energy terms. It assigns weight λ to E and 1−λ to μ/r; the surrounding printed prose interchanges these weights. Define the transformed quantities consistently by

This is the Kepler–isochrone radial transformation to the spherical isochrone model, up to the additive energy constant k.
The attractive branch of the Kepler–isochrone transformation requires a suitable parameter domain. The displayed square root needs λEEˉ>0, and the positive-root branch used above needs λE>0, Eˉ>0. An attractive regular isochrone also needs b≥0 and Mˉ>0. For bound original orbits a convenient domain is E<0, λ<0, Eˉ>0: it satisfies all these requirements and gives Eˉ−k=Eˉ/λ<0. For E>0 one may instead take 1/2<λ≤1, with Eˉ>0; these map to unbound attractive isochrone orbits. Other parameter choices can produce a repulsive Newtonian gravitational potential or a different branch, so no unrestricted real-parameter statement is justified. The singular algebraic case λ=0 is the harmonic transformation already handled separately. The angular reconstruction dθˉ/dτ=L/q2 likewise completes the isochrone radial solution into a central-force orbit.
