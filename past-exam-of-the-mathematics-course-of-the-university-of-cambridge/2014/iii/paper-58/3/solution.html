Use the paper's positive binding-energy convention U>0; the physical Newtonian gravitational potential energy is −U. Assume positive masses, an isolated system, and no collision during the time interval under consideration. Differentiate half the scalar moment of inertia, I, twice:

rj​⋅Fjk​+rk​⋅Fkj​=−rjk​Gmj​mk​​.

I¨=2T−U=T+E,E=T−U.​

B0​≤rU≤A0​,B0​=Gm(1)​m(2)​,A0​=GC.​

These constants show the minimum-separation binding-energy bounds: inverse binding energy is comparable to the nearest-pair distance, independently of configuration.
Similarly, every separation is at most R=maxrjk​ and at least one pair attains R. The pairwise identity gives the maximum-separation inertia bounds

Both lower bounds rely on actual extremal separations, rather than an arbitrary numerical lower or upper estimate for all the pair distances.
Negative energy does not imply a positive lower bound on the minimum separation. The printed request is false if it is meant to exclude close approaches or collisions; the trivial bound r≥0 says nothing of that kind. In fact E<0 and T≥0 give U=T−E≥∣E∣, which, combined with rU≤A0​, proves the negative-energy minimum-separation upper bound

An explicit negative-energy gravitational collision is a pair initially at rest with separation a. Its total energy is E=−Gm1​m2​/a<0. For reduced mass μ=m1​m2​/(m1​+m2​), its relative radial equation is
21​μr˙2−rGm1​m2​​=−aGm1​m2​​,r˙=−2G(m1​+m2​)(r1​−a1​)​.

tcoll​=∫0a​2G(m1​+m2​)(1/r−1/a)​dr​=π8G(m1​+m2​)a3​​.
(32)
Before that time the motion is a regular Newtonian solution, yet its separation has no positive infimum. Even at fixed negative energy, bound Kepler orbits with eccentricity approaching one have arbitrarily small pericentre distance. Thus a missing angular momentum or collision-exclusion hypothesis cannot be supplied by the energy sign alone.
For E>0, the instantaneous virial theorem gives I¨=T+E≥E. Integrating twice from any regular time t0​ gives
