Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2014 / iii / Paper 37 / 4 / c / Solution

Introduce nonnegative surplus variables
r1​=5x1​+6x2​+x3​−1,r2​=7x1​+5x2​+x3​−1,r3​=4x1​+3x2​+5x3​−1.
(16)
At the proposed starting basic feasible solution, the basic variables are x1​,r1​,r2​ and the nonbasic variables are x2​,x3​,r3​. Its simplex dictionary, with objective z=x1​+x2​+x3​, is
x1​r1​r2​z​=(1−3x2​−5x3​+r3​)/4,=(1+9x2​−21x3​+5r3​)/4,=(3−x2​−31x3​+7r3​)/4,=(1+x2​−x3​+r3​)/4.​
(17)
Increase x3​ to decrease z. The simplex ratio test gives limits 1/5 from x1​, 1/21 from r1​, and 3/31 from r2​. The smallest is 1/21, so x3​ enters and r1​ leaves. After this single pivot the dictionary is
x1​x3​r2​z​=(4+5r1​−r3​−27x2​)/21,=(1−4r1​+5r3​+9x2​)/21,=(8+31r1​−2r3​−75x2​)/21,=(5+r1​+4r3​+3x2​)/21.​
(18)
All objective coefficients of nonbasic variables are strictly positive. Thus the simplex method has reached its unique optimum:
x=(4/21,0,1/21),z=5/21.​
(19)
The feasible dual vector y=(1/21,0,4/21) has the same objective, providing an independent weak duality certificate. Normalization gives
p=(4/5,0,1/5),q=(1/5,0,4/5),v=21/5−5=−4/5.​
(20)
These are all the equilibria: the dual's strict slack in row two forces p2​=0, and the primal's strict slack in column two forces q2​=0. Equality of the two active row and column payoffs then fixes the displayed probabilities. The value is the first player's expected net loss; the second gains 4/5.
