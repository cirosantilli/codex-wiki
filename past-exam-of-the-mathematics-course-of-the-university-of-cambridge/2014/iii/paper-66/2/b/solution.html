The Dahlquist equivalence theorem states that a consistent linear multistep method is convergent for suitably consistent starting values exactly when it is zero-stable. The root condition for a multistep method requires every root of ρ to lie in the closed unit disk, with every unit-modulus root simple.
The roots are 1 and a. Thus ∣a∣≤1 is necessary; a=1 is excluded because it gives a double root at one. At a=−1, the two unit roots are distinct, so the endpoint is allowed. Combined with part (a), this proves
−1≤a<1for convergence.​
(14)
Assume a locally Lipschitz vector field, a smooth solution on the fixed time interval, a nearby solvable implicit branch and starting errors of the required order. The global order is three at a=−1/5 and two at the other convergent parameter values. Outside this interval, zero-step perturbations already grow through either an exterior root or a unit-root polynomial factor.
