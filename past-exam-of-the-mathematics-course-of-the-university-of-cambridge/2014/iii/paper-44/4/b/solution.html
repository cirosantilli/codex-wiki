 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2014 iii Paper 44 4 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 168
Factor the odd parameter on the left, δ=ϵs. The resulting left-acting BRST differential obeys the graded Leibniz rule
s(XY)=(sX)Y+(−1)∣X∣X(sY).
(26)
For the odd Grassmann field c, the bracket in the transformation is a graded commutator: [c,c]gr​=2c2, not the identically zero ordinary commutator of a matrix with itself. Thus sc=ic2, while sAμ​=Dμ​c, scˉ=h and sh=0.
On the ghost,
s2c=i[(sc)c−c(sc)]=i[ic2c−icc2]=0.
(27)
On the gauge field, variation of the connection and the adjoint covariant derivative gives
s2Aμ​=Dμ​(sc)−i[sAμ​,c]gr​=iDμ​(c2)−i[(Dμ​c)c+c(Dμ​c)]=0.
(28)
Here Dμ​ is even and therefore obeys the ordinary product rule. Also s2cˉ=sh=0 and s2h=0, without using any field equation; this is off-shell nilpotence supplied by the Nakanishi-Lautrup field.
Applying the graded Leibniz rule twice cancels the two cross terms:
s2(XY)=(s2X)Y+Xs2Y.
(29)
The square is consequently an even graded derivation. Since it vanishes on every generator, it vanishes inductively on every polynomial in the fields. Hence s2O=0 for every such operator. This genuine result is stronger than the automatic vanishing obtained by merely setting ϵ2=0; two independent transformation parameters also give a vanishing commutator.
