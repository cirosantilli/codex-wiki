An absolute value on a field is a map ∣⋅∣:K→R≥0​ satisfying

A Non-Archimedean absolute value satisfies the stronger ultrametric inequality ∣x+y∣≤max(∣x∣,∣y∣). Two equivalent absolute values induce the same topology, or equivalently differ by a positive real power. The trivial absolute value on a field takes value one on every nonzero element. The rational classification and the compactness criterion below concern nontrivial absolute values on a field; the trivial exceptions are given explicitly.

The valuation ring, its maximal ideal, and its residue field are
R={x:∣x∣≤1},m={x:∣x∣<1},k=R/m.

Suppose the absolute value on a field is nontrivial and R is compact. The ideal m is an open additive subgroup of R, so k is discrete; as a continuous image of a compact space it is finite. The subgroup m is also closed, since all its cosets are open, and is therefore compact. The continuous function ∣⋅∣ attains a maximum ρ on m, with 0<ρ<1. Choose π with ∣π∣=ρ. Then the positive values of v=−log∣⋅∣ have least element −logρ. Division with remainder in this additive subgroup of R proves v(K×)=v(π)Z. Thus K is a discretely valued field and π is a uniformizer.
Conversely, normalize the discrete valuation by v(π)=1. If k has q elements, R/πNR has qN elements. Each quotient therefore supplies a finite cover by balls of radius tending to zero. The ring R is a closed subset of the complete metric field K, hence complete and totally bounded, so it is compact. Equivalently,
