For this non-Archimedean local field, the Schwartz-Bruhat space S(F) consists of locally constant, compactly supported complex-valued functions. Choose a nontrivial continuous additive character ψ:F→C× of modulus one and an additive Haar measure dx. We use the plus-sign convention for the Fourier transform over a local field:
f​(y)=∫F​f(x)ψ(xy)dx.
(27)
Compact support makes the integral absolutely convergent. The additive character and the scale of the Haar measure are part of the definition; without them there is no canonical numerical transform.

Such a conductor exists: continuity puts the image of some additive ball inside an arc containing no nontrivial circle subgroup, so the character is trivial on that ball; nontriviality bounds the possible ball exponents below. Then Hn​=πFn​OF​ has volume Vq−n and character annihilator Hn⊥​=πFc−n​OF​.

Every Schwartz-Bruhat function is a finite linear combination of such coset indicators: compactness of its support supplies a common sufficiently small translation subgroup on which it is constant, and finitely many of its cosets cover the support. The transform of each indicator has compact support and is locally constant, since ψ has open kernel. Consequently f​∈S(F) for every f∈S(F).
Apply the transform again to an indicator. The same character-orthogonality calculation, together with (Hn⊥​)⊥=Hn​, gives

Choose the self-dual Haar measure, characterized here by V=qc/2. By linearity the desired Fourier inversion is
f​​(x)=f(−x).​
(31)
In particular, one may rescale any nontrivial additive character to make c=0, and then take V=1. For a concrete construction, start with ψ0​(x)=ψQp​​(TrF/Qp​​x), where the standard rational additive character has kernel Zp​. Its annihilator is the inverse different DF/Qp​−1​. If that ideal is πF−d​OF​, the rescaled character ψ(x)=ψ0​(πF−d​x) has OF⊥​=OF​. This supplies the stated normalization and also explains how the different ideal enters local Fourier analysis.
