Choose β with βp(p−1)=−p and put N=p(p−1). The Eisenstein polynomial TN+p shows that L=Qp​(β) has degree N, is totally ramified, and has uniformizer β. Its subfield Qp​(βp) is the field K from the previous part. Because p is odd,
a=−βp−1satisfiesap=p.
(18)
Thus L contains a and ζp​, and contains all roots aζpj​ of Tp−p. Conversely [Qp​(a):Qp​]=p, while [K:Qp​]=p−1. Their coprime degrees force their compositum to have degree N. It is contained in L and hence equals it. Therefore L is exactly the splitting field, not merely an extension containing it.
The Galois group has a normal subgroup H=Gal(L/K) of order p, acting by β↦ζpj​β. The (p−1)st roots of unity already lie in Qp​ by the Hensel lemma. The maps β↦ωβ, with ωp−1=1, supply a complement of order p−1. Its conjugation acts faithfully on H, so G≅Cp​⋊Fp×​.
Normalize vL​(β)=1. Total ramification and the uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups reduce the calculation to iG​(σ)=vL​(σβ−β). For nonidentity σ∈H,
iG​(σ)=1+vL​(ζpj​−1)=1+p,
(19)
since e(L/K)=p and vK​(ζpj​−1)=1. For σ∈/H, write σβ=ωζpj​β with ω=1. Its multiplier has residue ω=1, so iG​(σ)=1. The lower ramification numbering is therefore
G−1​=G0​=G,G1​=⋯=Gp​=H≅Cp​,Gp+1​=1.​
(20)
All later groups are trivial. The wild lower break is p, not one. In the upper ramification numbering, the Herbrand function sends this break to p/(p−1): G0=G, Gu=H for 0<u≤p/(p−1), and Gu=1 above it.
As an independent consistency check, the different exponent from ramification groups is (N−1)+p(p−1)=2N−1. The derivative of the Eisenstein polynomial gives the same answer, vL​(NβN−1)=N+(N−1), since vL​(p)=N.
