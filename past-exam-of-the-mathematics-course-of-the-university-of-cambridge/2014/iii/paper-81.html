Paper 81 OurBigBook.com
Use dimensionless spin angular momentum operators, so their commutators have no additional ℏ. A fully polarized state ∣F⟩=⨂n​∣S,S⟩n​ is an exact ground state of the Heisenberg ferromagnet, with E0​=−NJS2. On each bond the largest eigenvalue of Sn​⋅Sn+1​ is S2, obtained in the two-spin total-spin-2S sector. Hence the bondwise lower bound on the Hamiltonian is attained by this state.
The ferromagnetic ground-state multiplet has total spin NS. Applying the global lowering operator (Stot−​)r, r=0,…,2NS, generates its 2NS+1 magnetic states; their linear combinations are also ground states. Global spin rotations generate polarized ground states pointing in arbitrary directions and span the same multiplet. Uniform reorientation therefore costs no energy. Slowly varying orientations give gapless ferromagnetic magnons, with quadratic rather than linear long-wavelength dispersion. In the ordered thermodynamic ground state, the two broken spin generators are canonically paired because ⟨[Stotx​,Stoty​]⟩=i⟨Stotz​⟩=0, producing one type-B Goldstone boson. For a finite chain the zero-wavevector motion remains inside the exact ground multiplet; nonzero wavevectors have a finite-size spacing that vanishes as N−2. This is a ground-state statement, not a claim of finite-temperature long-range order in one dimension.

The Holstein–Primakoff transformation acts on the physical number-state subspace 0≤n≤2S. Its operator ordering matters: S+=2S−n^​a lowers the number first. Therefore
S+∣n⟩=n(2S−n+1)​∣n−1⟩,S−∣n⟩=(n+1)(2S−n)​∣n+1⟩,
(1)
while Sz∣n⟩=(S−n)∣n⟩. The square roots vanish at the appropriate ends, so the spin operators preserve the Holstein–Primakoff occupation constraint. The bosonic Fock space outside this subspace is not part of the spin-S representation.
These matrix elements give [Sz,S±]=±S±. The two products on ∣n⟩ are (n+1)(2S−n) and n(2S−n+1), respectively, so their difference is 2(S−n). Thus
[S+,S−]=2Sz,[Sz,S±]=±S±.​
(2)
Also (Sz)2+21​(S+S−+S−S+)=S(S+1)I. The transformation therefore has both the correct algebra and the correct spin Casimir operator, not just its small-occupation approximation.

For occupation much smaller than 2S, expand the square root in the Holstein–Primakoff transformation. To leading order Sn+​=2S​an​+O(S−1/2), Sn−​=2S​an†​+O(S−1/2) and Snz​=S−an†​an​. Consequently

(3)
Periodic boundaries count each occupation in two bonds. The linear spin-wave approximation is therefore
H=−NJS2+JSn∑​[2an†​an​−an†​an+1​−an+1†​an​]+O(S0).​
(4)
The displayed fluctuation Hamiltonian is bilinear and conserves boson number. The omitted terms are magnon interactions, quartic at the first omitted order. With occupation held of order one, they are smaller by 1/S than the order-S quadratic terms. The polarized vacuum has no zero-point depletion in this ferromagnetic expansion.

Use an​=N−1/2∑k​eiknak​ with k=2πj/N, choosing N distinct values modulo 2π in the Brillouin zone. The lattice spacing is one; for spacing a the dimensionless variable here would be ka. Orthogonality of the discrete Fourier modes diagonalizes the bilinear Hamiltonian:
H=E0​+∑k​εk​ak†​ak​,εk​=2JS(1−cosk)=4JSsin2(k/2).​

This is the nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion. The printed expression is missing a factor of four for the stated exchange Hamiltonian. An independent check uses one spin lowering at site n. Repeat the linear spin-wave approximation for bonds of lengths one and two. The polarized energy is E0​=−NS2(J1​+J2​) and each bond length contributes its own lattice Laplacian. Thus
εk​=2S[J1​(1−cosk)+J2​(1−cos2k)]=4S[J1​sin2(k/2)+J2​sin2k].​
(6)
The next-nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic magnon dispersion remains nonnegative for the stated positive exchanges and has its only zero at k=0 modulo 2π when J1​>0. At small k, εk​≃S(J1​+4J2​)k2: longer-range ferromagnetic exchange increases the stiffness.
At this harmonic order, a bosonic occupation configuration has energy E0​+∑k​εk​nk​. The one-magnon dispersion is also exact. General multi-magnon energies acquire the interactions omitted in the large-S expansion; the formula does not claim an exact diagonalization of the full interacting many-spin Hamiltonian.

Write V=L3 and N0​=N−Nex​, where Nex​=∑k=0​bk†​bk​. Macroscopic occupation permits b0​,b0†​≃N0​​, with relative commutator corrections of order 1/N0​. Keep the fixed-total-number correction before replacing n0​ by n.
The four-condensate term is V0​N0​(N0​−1)/(2V). Its leading expansion gives V0​N2/(2V)−nV0​Nex​. Terms with two condensate operators give normal scattering n(V0​+Vk​)bk†​bk​ and conversion of two condensate particles into opposite-momentum excitations, with coefficient nVk​/2. The nV0​ normal contribution cancels against the change of condensate energy at fixed N. Hence the number-conserving Bogoliubov quadratic Hamiltonian is
H≃2V​V0​n2+k=0∑​[(ϵk​+nVk​)bk†​bk​+2nVk​​(bk†​b−k†​+b−k​bk​)].​
(7)
Use a real even interaction transform, Vk​=V−k​, as for a real inversion-symmetric pair potential. The constant is the condensate interaction energy; the diagonal part combines particle kinetic energy with condensate scattering; the anomalous pair terms create and annihilate correlated opposite momenta while transferring particles to or from the condensate.
At fixed volume, counting replaced condensate operators gives quadratic vertices proportional to N0​, omitted cubic vertices proportional to N0​​ and quartic vertices with no condensate factor. There are also the finite-N0​ correction in N0​(N0​−1) and corrections from the condensate-number expansion. Their physical control parameter is small depletion, Nex​/N≪1, together with weak interaction; simply dropping them is not justified by low temperature in a strongly interacting gas. This is the weakly interacting Bose gas approximation.

Single-valued rotor wavefunctions are periodic in ϕ. The normalized eigenfunctions are ⟨ϕ∣n⟩=einϕ/2π​ with integer n, angular momentum ℏn and energy En​=ℏ2n2/(2I). The quantum rotor consequently has

The propagator is the quantum amplitude for evolving from one angular position to another. It evolves a wavefunction by Ψ(ϕF​,t)=∫02π​dϕI​K(ϕF​,ϕI​;t)Ψ(ϕI​,0). The path integral weights each trajectory by eiS/ℏ, with S=∫dtI(∂t​ϕ)2/2. Since the configuration space is a circle, paths must include all possible lifts of the final angle to the real line.
Take t=−iℏβ by Wick rotation. Let τ=tE​/ℏ, which runs from zero to β, and let the dot denote d/dτ. The dimensionless Euclidean exponent is then I∫0β​ϕ˙​2dτ/(2ℏ2). Tracing the kernel identifies final and initial angles modulo 2π, while the lift can change by 2πm. Thus the rotor winding-sector path integral is
Z=∫02π​dϕ0​m∈Z∑​∫ϕ(0)=ϕ0​ϕ(β)=ϕ0​+2πm​Dϕexp[−2ℏ2I​∫0β​dτϕ˙​2].​
(16)
The integration measure is fixed by the short-time free-rotor kernel. The dot on ϕ is essential; it is present in the PDF but lost in the TeX aid.

For smooth phase differences, −Jcos(ϕn+1​−ϕn​)=−J+J(ϕn+1​−ϕn​)2/2+⋯. After integrating the angular momenta in the same inverse-energy time coordinate τ, the coupled quantum rotor chain has the fluctuation action
S=∫0β​dτ∫dx[2ℏ2I​(∂τ​ϕ)2+2J​(∂x​ϕ)2].​
(23)
Unit spacing replaces the lattice sum and difference by their continuum limits. The constant energy −J per bond contributes an overall factor eβJN, suppressed when describing fluctuations. The phase is compact; the Gaussian action describes its smooth sector.
There is an imaginary-time normalization for a coupled rotor chain issue in the printed formula. Factoring out 1/ℏ2 from the action above places ℏ2J, not J, in its spatial bracket. The printed bracket is therefore correct in ℏ=1 units, or with its spatial coefficient redefined as Jbracket​=ℏ2JHamiltonian​. With the same dimensional J as in the Hamiltonian, it lacks that factor.
In physical real time the wave equation is I∂t2​ϕ=J∂x2​ϕ. The harmonic phase mode of a quantum rotor chain consequently has
ω(q)=J/I​∣q∣,E(q)=ℏJ/I​∣q∣.​
(24)
The lattice expression is ω(q)=2J/I​∣sin(q/2)∣. The mode is a gapless phase wave analogous to sound in a superfluid, with rotor angular momentum conjugate to its phase. In one dimension this harmonic regime has strong long-distance phase fluctuations rather than true continuous-symmetry long-range order; compact phase slips can change the infrared phase outside the smooth-phase approximation.

This identifies local instability of the paramagnetic state; the detailed transition order also depends on higher terms in the action.
In the isotropic ordered phase of itinerant ferromagnetism, continuous spin-rotation symmetry leaves a degenerate direction of magnetization. Its transverse fluctuations produce one gapless, quadratically dispersing mean-field itinerant ferromagnetic Goldstone mode, E(q)∝q2. The two broken spin generators form a canonical pair, as for the type-B Goldstone boson of the localized ferromagnet. The system also remains metallic, with exchange-split Fermi surfaces and low-energy spin-conserving particle-hole excitations. Longitudinal-amplitude fluctuations and the spin-flip continuum are distinct from the long-wavelength magnon; their energy and damping scales depend on the band structure. Spin anisotropy, if added, could gap the transverse mode, but it is absent from the stated Hamiltonian.
