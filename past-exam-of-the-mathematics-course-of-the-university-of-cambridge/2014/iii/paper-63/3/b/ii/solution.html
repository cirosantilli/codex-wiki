Write the final topological quantum order constants as a>0 and ε<1, to avoid confusing the allowed support diameter with the small time coefficient. For any initial operator AX​ of operator norm at most one,
⟨ψ0​∣AX​∣ψ0​⟩=⟨ψ1​∣AX​(−t)∣ψ1​⟩,
(31)
and similarly for the partner state. The minus sign follows from ∣ψ0​⟩=eitH∣ψ1​⟩ and the Heisenberg picture convention AX​(t)=eitHAX​e−itH. The Lieb-Robinson bound and its localization corollary apply to either time direction, using ∣t∣.
Choose diam(X)≤aL/4, t=τL with vτ≤a/8, and localization buffer l=aL/8. The enlarged support X′ obeys
diam(X′)≤diam(X)+2(v∣t∣+l)≤3aL/4<aL.
(32)
The Lieb-Robinson localization by Haar twirling corollary supplies an operator BX′​ approximating AX​(−t) with
δL​:=∥AX​(−t)−BX′​∥≤μvτL∣X∣e−μaL/16.
(33)
Do not assume that the approximate operator has norm at most one: it only has ∥BX′​∥≤1+δL​. Apply final-state local indistinguishability to BX′​/(1+δL​). The two approximation errors then give
​⟨ψ0​∣AX​∣ψ0​⟩−⟨ψ​0​∣AX​∣ψ​0​⟩​≤ε(1+δL​)+2δL​.
(34)
If the lattice has polynomially many sites in its diameter, N(L)=O(Lp), the error tends to zero uniformly in these supports. For sufficiently large L, take δL​≤(1−ε)/[2(ε+2)]. The initial pair is then indistinguishable within ε′=(1+ε)/2<1 on supports of diameter at most aL/4. Together with the exact orthogonality from condition (i), this proves the initial state is topologically ordered for sufficiently small linear-time coefficient. The constants for initial and final order need not be identical.
The backward preservation of local indistinguishability has an explicit finite-system qualification: it holds whenever the displayed L∣X∣e−μaL/16 error is small enough. The conventional bounded-density, fixed-dimensional lattice interpretation supplies that condition. The question leaves growth control implicit; for an arbitrary collection of qudits one cannot discard the ∣X∣ prefactor merely because L is large. This identifies exactly the assumption needed by the supplied localization proof.
