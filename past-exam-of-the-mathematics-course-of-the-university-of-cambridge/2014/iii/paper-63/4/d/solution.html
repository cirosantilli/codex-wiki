Put λ=2ks. For t≥0, apply a valid Lieb-Robinson bound to each commutator in the supplied comparison of the two evolutions. For a disjoint shell at support distance d,
∥[hZ​(τ),hY​]∥≤2∥hZ​∥∥hY​∥min(∣Z∣,∣Y∣)e−μd(eλτ−1).
(44)
In the usual uniform interaction-norm setting, truncation preserves the strength bound, so the same constants apply to Hd​. The given polynomial shell-weight hypothesis and eλτ−1≤eλt now give
∥eitHd​hZ​e−itHd​−eitHd−1​hZ​e−itHd−1​∥​≤C∥hZ​∥dαe−μd∫0t​eλτdτ≤C∥hZ​∥tdαe−μd+λt.​
(45)
Consequently
∥eitHd​hZ​e−itHd​−eitHd−1​hZ​e−itHd−1​∥=O(∥hZ​∥∣t∣dαe−μd+2ks∣t∣).​
(46)
The question explicitly supplies a bound for the full H; such a bound alone should not be assumed to transfer to Hd​. A full-evolution comparison for truncated dynamics avoids that additional assumption. The Duhamel comparison formula gives
∥τtH​(hZ​)−τtHj​​(hZ​)∥≤∑Y:d(Z,Y)>j​∫0∣t∣​∥[τσH​(hZ​),hY​]∥dσ.
(47)
Now use only the full-Hamiltonian bound, and sum the shell weights. Since ∑q≥d​qαe−μq=O(dαe−μd), the errors for j=d and j=d−1 are each bounded by O(∥hZ​∥∣t∣dαe−μd+λ∣t∣). The triangle inequality comparing both evolutions to τtH​(hZ​) proves the same boxed estimate from the stated full-system assumption. Negative times follow by the reverse-time Duhamel formula; the commutator bounds use ∣t∣.
For the interaction-term shells used to enforce H0​=hZ​, shell d corresponds to support distance d−1 for distinct terms. Replace the displayed commutator estimate by the coarser bound with eλτ, retaining the equal-time contribution for overlaps. Its distance factor is e−μ(d−1)=eμe−μd, and the fixed factor eμ is absorbed by the big-O constant. This supplies the same claimed shell estimate without incorrectly using a zero equal-time bound for overlapping terms.
