Use the Fourier transform convention w(E)=∫R​w(t)eitEdt. Since w≥0, it is real, so w(−E)=w(E)∗. The positive-frequency cutoff therefore also eliminates frequencies at or below −Δ. Normalization gives ∫w=w(0)=1.
For spectral filtering of Hamiltonian terms, choose
A(Z)=∫R​w(t)eitHhZ​e−itHdt.​
(35)
In an energy eigenbasis, its matrix elements are
⟨ϕi​∣A(Z)∣ϕj​⟩=w(Ei​−Ej​)⟨ϕi​∣hZ​∣ϕj​⟩.
(36)
At i=j=0 the frequency is zero, so the normalization preserves the ground-state expectation:
⟨ϕ0​∣A(Z)∣ϕ0​⟩=⟨ϕ0​∣hZ​∣ϕ0​⟩.​
(37)
The spectral filter is a positive weighted average of unitary conjugations; in particular the integral is bounded in operator norm by ∥hZ​∥. We can choose it even without an extra assumed tail bound: the evenization of a nonnegative bandlimited filter proved in part (e) produces another admissible spectral filter with the same type of positive-time decay. Make that choice consistently in all the filtered terms and shell definitions below.
