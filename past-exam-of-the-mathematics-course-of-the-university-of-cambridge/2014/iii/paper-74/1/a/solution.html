The absolute error estimate is

To see its uniformity, the slow-root error is O(ε2), while its magnitude is O(ε) and its coefficient is O(ε). The uniform error bound for nearby decaying exponentials therefore gives an O(ε2) contribution even for x=O(ε−1). The middle-root error is O(ε) with coefficient O(ε), again giving O(ε2). The fast-root error is O(1) with magnitude O(ε−1) and coefficient O(ε2), giving only O(ε3). Coefficient errors are O(ε2) or smaller. This estimate concerns y itself, rather than asserting the same uniform order for all derivatives or a relative error at its zero.
For the sketch, on x=εX one obtains
y(x)∼ε2(X−1+e−X).
(6)
Thus y∼x2/2 at the origin and rises smoothly after the fast layer. For x=O(1) it rises towards a plateau of height approximately ε/2, then decays on the much longer scale x=O(ε−1). The bulk maximum lies near x=log(4/ε)/(2−ε/2) and has height asymptotic to ε/2.
The sign of the parameter matters on an infinite interval. If ε→0− is allowed, the singular mode grows rather than decays. For each fixed x>0, its dominant contribution is y(x)∼ε2exp[(−1/ε+2)x]. The extra 2 in that exponent is needed for relative leading accuracy at fixed x. The positive-parameter uniform absolute bound and decaying sketch do not extend to that regime.
