Initially suppose 0<α<π/2, the usual oscillatory range. The oscillatory phase is nΦ(ϑ) with Φ=secαsinϑ−ϑ. Its stationary point is ϑ=α, since secαcosϑ=1. At that point
Φ(α)=tanα−α,Φ′′(α)=−tanα.
(9)
The stationary phase method therefore gives the Debye Bessel asymptotic
Jn​(nsecα)=πntanα2​​cos[n(tanα−α)−4π​]+O(n−3/2)​.
(10)
The error is additive for fixed positive α bounded away from π/2; this formula is not uniform as α→0, when the stationary point joins the endpoint and its curvature vanishes.
The printed condition α>0 alone includes other trigonometric branches. All defined fixed real cases can be covered as follows. If 0<∣cosα∣<1, put β=arccos∣cosα∣∈(0,π/2). Use the displayed formula with β instead of α, and multiply it by 1 if secα>0, or by (−1)n if secα<0. This follows from the integer-order parity Jn​(−x)=(−1)nJn​(x), which is also obtained from the defining integral by ϑ↦π−ϑ. If ∣cosα∣=1, the argument is ±n and the turning-point answer below applies, with the same parity factor. If cosα=0, the stated argument is undefined.
