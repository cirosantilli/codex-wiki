Keep the prescribed outer slip velocity fixed when perturbing the unsteady Prandtl equation. Cancelling the base equation and retaining terms linear in the disturbance gives the linearized unsteady Prandtl equation
u~t​+Uu~x​+Vu~y​+Ux​u~+Uy​v~=u~yy​,u~x​+v~y​=0.
(41)
The no-slip boundary condition supplies u~=v~=0 at the wall; the fixed outer velocity supplies u~→0 at infinity. There is no prescribed zero normal-velocity disturbance at infinity: a finite displacement-related value is permitted.
With the frozen parallel profile U=U(y), V=0, the coefficients are invariant under translations in x and t. Normal modes therefore separate those variables. For the mode convention used here the equations are
iα(U−c)u+U′v=u′′,iαu+v′=0.
(42)
Eliminate u=−v′/(iα) to obtain the Prandtl normal-mode equation
v′′′=iα[(U−c)v′−U′v]​,v(0)=v′(0)=0,v′(∞)=0,
(43)
with a bounded far-field v. Requiring v(∞)=0 would incorrectly eliminate the proposed outer profile. For real U and real α, complex conjugation of the equation and its boundary conditions replaces (α,c,v) by (−α,c∗,v∗). This proves the stated eigenvalue symmetry. The paired modes have the same temporal growth rate αImc, so choosing positive wavenumber loses no real physical disturbance.
Here the prescribed parallel profile is a local frozen-coefficient model. A nontrivial arbitrary U(y) with V=0 and constant outer slip is not generally an exact steady solution of the unforced unsteady Prandtl equation; its base equation would require U′′=0. The following stability calculation uses the simplifying local model stipulated for the mode analysis.
Set c0​=Uc​=U(yc​). Away from the critical point the dominant equation is (U−c0​)v0′​−U′v0​=0, whose solutions are multiples of U−c0​. Choosing the coefficient to be zero below yc​ and one above it satisfies the wall and tangential far-field conditions. The choice c0​=Uc​ makes v0​ continuous across the joining point; U′(yc​)=0 also makes its first derivative continuous. Its second derivative jumps. Thus it is a valid leading outer solution away from yc​, but viscosity must smooth the join in a critical layer in a shear flow.
Near the join, U−c0​∼(y−yc​)2/2. If the layer width is ℓ, matching gives v=O(ℓ2). The advection side of the mode equation scales as αℓ3 and v′′′ scales as ℓ−1. Balance gives ℓ4∼α−1=ε/k. Equivalently, the eigenvalue correction ε1/2c1​ balances (y−yc​)2, so the quarter-power critical layer has
p=41​,q=21​​,y−yc​=ε1/4Y,v=ε1/2w(Y)+⋯.
(44)
Substitution, retaining the leading terms and cancelling their common factor, gives
kYw−k(2Y2​−c1​)wY​=iwYYY​​.
(45)
At order ε1/2 outside the layer,
(U−c0​)v1′​−U′v1​=c1​v0′​.
(46)
A solution consistent with the chosen lower branch and the wall conditions is v1​=0 below the join. Above it a particular solution is v1​=−c1​; a multiple of U−c0​ represents an arbitrary amplitude renormalization and may be set to zero. Matching consequently requires
w→0(Y→−∞),w−(2Y2​−c1​)→0(Y→+∞)​.
(47)
The corresponding derivatives match as wY​∼Y, wYY​→1 on the positive side and as zero on the negative side. The matching statements require that growing homogeneous corrections be absent.
To symmetrize these different end conditions and remove k and i, choose
a=(ik)1/4=k1/4eiπ/8,z=aY,C=a2c1​,W=2a2w−(2z2​−C).
(48)
The polynomial A(z)=z2/2−C is itself a solution of the transformed homogeneous equation. Direct substitution, using a4=ik, gives
Wzzz​−(2z2​−C)Wz​+zW=0​,W∓(2z2​−C)→0(z→±∞).
(49)
The derivative is third order, as required by the original mode equation. Initially the two ends are along the rotated rays z=aY with real Y. Continuing them to the real z axis is compatible with the asymptotic end conditions: the decreasing homogeneous correction behaves exponentially as exp[−z2/(22​)] and still decreases throughout the rotation from angle π/8 to zero. Thus the supplied real-axis spectral normalization uses the same recessive conditions; a complex coordinate change should not be mistaken for a real stretching alone.
For each supplied real eigenvalue Cn​=(4n+7)/2​, the phase-speed correction is
c1​=k−1/2e−iπ/4Cn​,Imc=−2k​Cn​​ε1/2+o(ε1/2).
(50)
The mode factor has temporal magnitude eαImct. Therefore
γn​=αImc=−24n+7​α​+o(α​)​.
(51)
Among the listed indices, n=−2 gives positive growth γ∼α​/2; n=−1,1,2,… give decay. Hence the frozen non-monotone flow has high-wavenumber instability of a non-monotone Prandtl layer, with arbitrarily rapid linear growth as the positive wavenumber increases. The normal-mode calculation demonstrates linear instability; it is not by itself a claim about the final nonlinear state.
