For fixed x>0, set y=y0​+εy1​+ε2y2​+⋯ and impose the data at one at each order. The leading equation gives y0′​=0, hence y0​=1. At the next two orders,
x2y1′​=1,x2y2′​=2−x3​.
(28)
The boundary conditions y1​(1)=y2​(1)=0 give the three-term outer expansion
yout​=1+ε(1−x1​)+ε2(21​−x2​+2x23​)+⋯​.
(29)
Its ordering fails when ε/x=O(1), locating a boundary layer of thickness O(ε) at zero. Put x=εξ and y=Y0​(ξ)+εY1​(ξ)+⋯. The rescaled equation is
(1+ε)ξ2yξ​=y3+ε[ξ(y2−1)+2y2]−ε2ξ(y2+1).
(30)
Its leading equation is ξ2Y0′​=Y03​. Matching to the positive outer value one fixes the sign and integration constant:
Y0​=(ξ+2ξ​)1/2.
(31)
At the next order the bracket ξ(Y02​−1)+2Y02​ vanishes identically, leaving
ξ2Y1′​−ξ+23ξ​Y1​=−(ξ+2ξ​)3/2.
(32)
The general solution is Y1​=(1+kξ)ξ1/2/(ξ+2)3/2. In the overlap its expansion is
Y0​=1−ξ1​+2ξ23​+⋯,Y1​=k+ξ1−3k​+⋯.
(33)
The outer solution re-expressed on this scale has the corresponding terms 1−1/ξ+3/(2ξ2)+ε(1−2/ξ)+⋯. Thus matched asymptotic expansion determines k=1, consistently in both displayed orders, and the two-term inner expansion is
yin​(ξ)=(ξ+2ξ​)1/2+ε(ξ+2)3/2(1+ξ)ξ1/2​+⋯​.
(34)
This positive branch tends continuously to zero at x=0, with a square-root cusp. An infinite endpoint derivative is compatible with the degeneracy of the original equation's x2y′ coefficient.
