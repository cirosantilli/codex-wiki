The sources are independent odd Grassmann variables and anticommute with the Dirac field. Use K=γa∂a​+M and the same mostly-plus gamma matrices and Dirac adjoint as in the interaction calculation. The inverse KF−1​ is selected with vacuum Feynman i-epsilon prescription boundary conditions. With integral kernels and spinor contractions understood, the exponent can be completed to a square:
ψˉ​Kψ+ψˉ​J+Jˉψ=(ψˉ​+JˉKF−1​)K(ψ+KF−1​J)−JˉKF−1​J.
(28)
Translations preserve the Berezin integral, so the Gaussian generating functional for a Dirac field is
Z[J,Jˉ]=Z[0,0]exp[−i∫d4xd4yJˉ(x)KF−1​(x,y)J(y)]​.
(29)
The positions of the sources matter. To extract the Dirac propagator, use a left Grassmann derivative with respect to Jˉα​(x) followed by a right Grassmann derivative with respect to Jβ​(y):
SF,αβ​(x,y)=−Z[0,0]1​(δJˉα​(x)δL​Z​)δJβ​(y)δ​​J=Jˉ=0​=i(KF−1​)αβ​(x,y).
(30)
The leading minus sign removes the two insertion factors i2. It can also be checked by differentiating the quadratic source exponential: its ordered second derivative is −iZ[0,0]KF−1​.
For the Fourier transform eip⋅(x−y), K(p)=ip+M and the Clifford algebra gives
(ip+M)(M−ip)=p2+M2.
(31)
Therefore
SF​(x,y)=∫(2π)4d4p​eip⋅(x−y)p2+M2−i0i(M−ip)​​.
(32)
This equals ⟨0∣Tψ(x)ψˉ​(y)∣0⟩. The fermionic time ordering is explicitly
Tψα​(x)ψˉ​β​(y)=θ(x0−y0)ψα​(x)ψˉ​β​(y)−θ(y0−x0)ψˉ​β​(y)ψα​(x),
(33)
with the minus sign supplied by exchanging odd fields. The poles put positive energy forward in time and negative energy backward, which is the antiparticle contribution. Finally, Kx​SF​(x,y)=iIδ(4)(x−y) checks the numerator and overall sign. All formulas are distributional limits with the indicated boundary prescription.
