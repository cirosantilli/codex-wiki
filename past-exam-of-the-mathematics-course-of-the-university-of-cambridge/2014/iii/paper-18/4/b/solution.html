Put eA​=εFA​FηA​. This is a natural transformation F⇒F. Only the G-triangle GεD​ηGD​=1GD​ is assumed. Naturality gives two useful absorption identities:
GeA​ηA​εD​eGD​​=GεFA​GFηA​ηA​=GεFA​ηGFA​ηA​=ηA​,=εD​εFGD​FηGD​=εD​FGεD​FηGD​=εD​.​
(15)
For the second equality in the last line use naturality of ε at εD​, and then the assumed G-triangle. Naturality of ε at FηA​ and of η at ηA​ now gives
eA2​​=εFA​εFGFA​FGFηA​FηA​=εFA​εFGFA​FηGFA​FηA​=εFA​eGFA​FηA​=eA​.​
(16)
Hence e is an idempotent in the functor category: this is the one-triangle adjunction idempotent.
For the splitting of an idempotent morphism, suppose this idempotent morphism splits as natural transformations r:F⇒H and s:H⇒F, with sr=e and rs=1H​. Define
ηA′​=GrA​ηA​,εD′​=εD​sGD​.
(17)
The first absorption identity gives
GεD′​ηGD′​=GεD​GeGD​ηGD​=1GD​.
(18)
For the other triangle, naturality of s at ηA′​ and of ε at rA​ gives
εHA′​HηA′​​=εHA​sGHA​HηA′​=εHA​FηA′​sA​=εHA​FGrA​FηA​sA​=rA​εFA​FηA​sA​=rA​eA​sA​=1HA​.​
(19)
Thus the triangle identities for an adjunction prove H⊣G.
Conversely, suppose H⊣G, with unit ψ:1C​⇒GH and counit φ:HG⇒1D​. The unit has target GH, as its type requires. Define
sA​=φFA​HηA​:HA→FA,rA​=εHA​FψA​:FA→HA.​
(20)
These are natural transformations. Transposition under H⊣G gives GsA​ψA​=ηA​. Independently, naturality of η at ψA​ and the assumed G-triangle give
GrA​ηA​=GεHA​GFψA​ηA​=GεHA​ηGHA​ψA​=ψA​.
(21)
The transpose of rA​sA​ is therefore GrA​GsA​ψA​=ψA​, the transpose of 1HA​. Injectivity of the hom-set bijection implies rA​sA​=1HA​. Naturality of ε at sA​ gives
sA​rA​=εFA​FGsA​FψA​=εFA​F(GsA​ψA​)=εFA​FηA​=eA​.
(22)
Consequently G has a left adjoint if and only if e splits. This is the criterion for splitting a one-triangle adjunction idempotent. The argument gives both the explicit splitting and the new unit and counit, without assuming the other triangle for F.
