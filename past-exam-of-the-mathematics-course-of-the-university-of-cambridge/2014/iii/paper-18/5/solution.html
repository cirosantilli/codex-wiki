For A∈C, the comma category (A↓G) has objects (D,u) with D∈D and u:A→GD. A morphism (D,u)→(D′,u′) is h:D→D′ satisfying Ghu=u′. Identities and composition are those of D, and functoriality of G verifies the condition under composition.
The Freyd general adjoint functor theorem states: if D is a locally small category with all small categorical limits, and C is locally small, then G:D→C has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves small limits and satisfies the solution-set condition. The latter means that for each A there is a set-indexed family ui​:A→GDi​ such that every u:A→GD equals Ghui​ for some i and some h:Di​→D.
For necessity, use the standard result that a right adjoint preserves categorical limits. If F⊣G, the singleton family containing the unit of an adjunction ηA​:A→GFA is a solution set, since transposition gives u=GhηA​ for a unique h:FA→D.
For sufficiency, use the following standard limit fact: if D is complete and G preserves limits, the projection (A↓G)→D creates small limits. Indeed, a compatible family A→GDj​ induces a unique arrow into G(limDj​), and this makes the underlying limit a limit in the comma category. The comma category is locally small because each of its hom-sets is a subset of a hom-set of D. Its solution family is a weakly initial set.
We prove the remaining initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set. In any locally small category K with all small limits and a weakly initial set (Ki​), form W=∏i​Ki​. It is weakly initial: for any X, some Ki​→X exists and may be composed with the projection W→Ki​. The empty family cannot be weakly initial in a nonempty complete category, which has a terminal object.
The set End(W) is small. Form a simultaneous equalizer e:E→W of every endomorphism of W and 1W​; thus
ue=efor every u:W→W.
(23)
This equalizer exists by completeness, for example as the equalizer of two maps W⇉WEnd(W). The object E is still weakly initial, since it maps to W.
Given a,b:E→X, take their equalizer j:Y→E. Weak initiality of W gives t:W→Y. Since ejt is an endomorphism of W, we have ejte=e, and cancellation of the monomorphism e gives jte=1E​. Thus j is a split epimorphism as well as a monomorphism, so it is an isomorphism. From aj=bj follows a=b. There is at least one map E→X by weak initiality, so E is initial.
Apply this lemma to every (A↓G) and choose its initial object (FA,ηA​). For v:A→A′, initiality gives the unique Fv:FA→FA′ satisfying GFvηA​=ηA′​v. Uniqueness proves the functor laws. The same initiality gives natural bijections
D(FA,D)≅C(A,GD),h⟼GhηA​.
(24)
Hence F⊣G, completing the theorem without invoking another adjoint functor theorem.
