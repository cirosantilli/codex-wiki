The covariant form of the Yoneda lemma says that for a locally small category C, a functor F:C→Set and an object A, there is a bijection, natural in A and F,
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A).
(2)
Explicitly its two directions are
α⟼αA​(1A​),x⟼αx,αBx​(f)=F(f)(x).​
(3)
For u:B→B′, functoriality gives F(u)αBx​(f)=F(uf)(x)=αB′x​(uf), so αx is a natural transformation. Conversely, naturality of α at f:A→B gives αB​(f)=F(f)(αA​(1A​)). This proves that the displayed maps are inverses.
Naturality in F follows because postcomposition by θ:F⇒F′ sends x to θA​(x). For u:A′→A, precomposition of transformations by C(u,−) sends αx to αF(u)(x). This also verifies naturality in the representing object.
