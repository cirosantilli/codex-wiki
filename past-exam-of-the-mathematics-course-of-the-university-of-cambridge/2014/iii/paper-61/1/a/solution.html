Use the positive-sign quantum Fourier transform
FN​∣x⟩=N​1​∑j=0N−1​e2πijx/N∣j⟩.
(1)
Orthogonality of the finite-group characters makes FN​ a unitary operator. Here r is the least positive period. The injectivity within one period implies r∣N: write N=qr+s, 0≤s<r. Periodicity gives f(s)=f(N)=f(0), and injectivity forces s=0. Put L=N/r.
Start with two available computational basis states ∣0⟩∣0⟩. Apply FN​ to the first register and query the modular-addition quantum oracle:
∣0⟩∣0⟩⟼N​1​∑x​∣x⟩∣0⟩⟼N​1​∑x​∣x⟩∣f(x)⟩.
(2)
Measure the second register. For some t∈{0,…,r−1}, the first register becomes the normalized coset state
L​1​∑h=0L−1​∣t+hr⟩.
(3)
The quantum Fourier transform of a periodic coset state is
r​1​∑s=0r−1​e2πist/r​rsN​⟩.
(4)
Indeed, the inner geometric sum vanishes unless the Fourier label is a multiple of N/r. Measuring that label gives j=sN/r for a uniformly random s. Compute, by the Euclidean algorithm,
R=gcd(j,N)N​=gcd(s,r)r​.
(5)
Thus exact period recovery from a Fourier sample succeeds whenever s is coprime to r, and
Pr(R=r)=rφ(r)​=Ω(loglogN1​)​.
(6)
Here φ is the Euler totient function. The classical number-theory input is the totient lower bound from the Mertens product: for sufficiently large m, φ(m)/m≥c/loglogm for an absolute c>0, with the finitely many small cases handled separately. For r=1 recovery is certain; for r=2 its probability is 1/2. The useful asymptotic probability statement is a lower bound, hence Ω notation; it can be much larger for particular periods, for example a prime period.
There is one oracle query, two Fourier transforms and two measurements. The final greatest common divisor and division use polynomial time in logN. This meets the specified primitive-operation model without assuming that an arbitrary-N Fourier gate is free in another gate model. If a constant success probability is wanted, repeat independently O(loglogN) times and return the least common multiple of the candidates: each candidate divides r, and one successful sample makes that least common multiple exactly r.
