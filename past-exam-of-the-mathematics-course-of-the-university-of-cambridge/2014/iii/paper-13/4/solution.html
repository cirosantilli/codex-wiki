Order the index set of the open cover U=(Ua​). The Čech cochain groups are
Cˇp(U,F)=∏a0​<⋯<ap​​F(Ua0​​∩⋯∩Uap​​),
(24)
and their differential is the alternating sum of restrictions:
(δc)a0​…ap+1​​=∑j=0p+1​(−1)jca0​…aj​…ap+1​​∣Ua0​​∩⋯∩Uap+1​​​.
(25)
Terms obtained by deleting two indices cancel in pairs, so δ2=0. The Čech cohomology is Hˇp=kerδ/imδ, with zero incoming differential in degree zero. Its zeroth group is the group of global sections by sheaf gluing.
The acyclic cover theorem applies if every nonempty finite intersection is acyclic for F. For a quasi-coherent sheaf on a variety it is sufficient that every such intersection is affine; on a separated variety, any affine open cover has this property. On Pn, take the n+1 standard charts Ui​=D+​(Xi​). Every intersection is a principal open in an affine chart, hence affine. The Čech complex has no terms above degree n, proving
Hi(Pn,F)=0(i>n).​
(26)
This is the cohomological dimension bound from an affine cover and requires only quasi-coherence, not finite generation.
For the remaining projective calculations assume n≥1. There is a necessary zero-dimensional exception to the negative-twist assertion: P0 is a point, every twist is trivial there, and H0(P0,O(m))≅k even for m<0.
Put S=k[X0​,…,Xn​] with its usual grading. The twisting sheaf on projective space is the sheaf associated with the shifted graded module S(m); on an intersection UI​=⋂i∈I​Ui​ its sections are
O(m)(UI​)=(S∏i∈I​Xi​​)m​.
(27)
On Ui​ a generator is ei​=Xim​, with transition ej​=(Xj​/Xi​)mei​. These are regular units on overlaps and satisfy the cocycle relation, so they glue an invertible sheaf for every integer m, including negative m.
A global section is a compatible family of these homogeneous fractions, hence a single element of degree m in ⋂i​SXi​​⊆FracS. For n≥1, X0​ and X1​ are relatively prime in the unique factorization domain S, so SX0​​∩SX1​​=S; therefore the full intersection is S. It follows that
H0(Pn,O(m))≅Sm​={homogeneous polynomials of degree m,0,​m≥0,m<0.​​
(28)
For m≥0 its dimension is (nn+m​). The proof uses all-chart compatibility; regularity on one chart alone would permit poles on its complement.
If some di​=0, the ideal (Xidi​​) contains 1, so the requested containment is immediate. Otherwise every di​≥1. A monomial X0a0​​⋯Xnan​​ of degree ∑i​di​ must have ai​≥di​ for some i: if not, its total degree would be at most ∑i​(di​−1)<∑i​di​. Thus every degree-∑i​di​ monomial, and hence every homogeneous polynomial of that degree, belongs to (X0d0​​,…,Xndn​​). This is monomial containment in an ideal of coordinate powers.
For n>0, a top-degree Čech cochain for O is a degree-zero Laurent polynomial on the full intersection. Write it with a common denominator as
X0d0​​⋯Xndn​​P​,degP=∑i​di​.
(29)
The containment just proved gives P=∑i​Qi​Xidi​​, with homogeneous Qi​ of degree ∑j​dj​−di​. Consequently
∏j​Xjdj​​P​=∑i​∏j=i​Xjdj​​Qi​​.
(30)
The ith term is regular on the intersection omitting Ui​, and has degree zero. Give it the sign (−1)i in that component of the preceding Čech cochain. Its coboundary is the original fraction. Every top cochain is thus a coboundary, and
Hn(Pn,O)=0(n>0).​
(31)
This is top Čech cohomology from missing-denominator monomials.
To obtain the negative-twist bound by induction on dimension, let H≅Pn−1 be a hyperplane, with inclusion j. Its equation gives the hyperplane exact sequence for twisting sheaves
0⟶O(m−1)⟶O(m)⟶j∗​OH​(m)⟶0.
(32)
The associated long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology and sheaf cohomology under a closed inclusion give
Hn−1(H,OH​(m))⟶Hn(Pn,O(m−1))⟶Hn(Pn,O(m))⟶0.
(33)
For n=1, restriction H0(P1,O)→H0(H,OH​) is the surjection k→k. Exactness and the already proved H1(P1,O)=0 give H1(P1,O(−1))=0. The displayed surjections then give the same vanishing for every m≥0. This handles the point hyperplane without making a false negative-twist claim on P0.
For n≥2, the induction hypothesis in dimension n−1 gives Hn−1(H,OH​(m))=0 whenever m≥−(n−1). Therefore consecutive top-degree groups are isomorphic for m≥1−n. Starting with Hn(Pn,O)=0 and stepping down through m=0,−1,…,1−n reaches Hn(Pn,O(−n))=0. Stepping upward proves all positive twists too. We conclude
Hn(Pn,O(−r))=0for all integers r≤n, n>0.​
(34)
This is top-twist vanishing by hyperplane induction.
Finally, on U0​ write xa​=Xa​/X0​ and ω0​=dx1​∧⋯∧dxn​. On Ui​, let ωi​ be the wedge of d(Xa​/Xi​) for a=i, taken in increasing index order. For i>0, differentiating X0​/Xi​=xi−1​ and Xa​/Xi​=xa​/xi​ gives
ωi​=(−1)ixi−n−1​ω0​.​
(35)
In the wedge, all terms involving a second copy of dxi​ disappear; the surviving powers are xi−2​ from d(xi−1​) and xi−1​ from the other n−1 differentials. Moving dxi​ into its original position produces the stated sign. Rescale each local generator by ηi​=(−1)iωi​. Then ηj​=(Xj​/Xi​)−n−1ηi​ on every overlap, exactly the transition of the twist with m=−n−1. This canonical-form transition on projective space proves the canonical bundle of projective space:
ΩPnn​≅OPn​(−n−1).​
(36)
For line bundles the Serre duality pairing becomes
Hi(Pn,O(m))∗≅Hn−i(Pn,O(−m−n−1)).
(37)
In particular Hn(O(−r)) is dual to H0(O(r−n−1)), which is zero for r≤n, precisely the independently obtained bound. The case r=0 recovers Hn(O)=0, and Hn(O(−n−1))≅k matches H0(O)≅k. Thus the calculated transition functions, global sections and top-degree vanishing agree with Serre duality.
