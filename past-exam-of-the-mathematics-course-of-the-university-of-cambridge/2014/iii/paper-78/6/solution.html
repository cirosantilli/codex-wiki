Introduce water dynamic viscosity μ and the net driving liquid-pressure difference ΔP=p0​−pf​>0. If pc​>0 denotes capillary suction relative to an ambient gas pressure pa​, then pf​=pa​−pc​ and ΔP=p0​−pa​+pc​. If pc​ is instead the prescribed liquid front pressure, use ΔP=p0​−pc​. This makes the capillary sign convention explicit.
Hemispherical front and injection flux. A hemispherical surface at radius r has area 2πr2. Quasi-steady mass conservation makes the total volume flux independent of r in the saturated region, so Darcy's law gives
ur​(r)=2πr2Q(t)​=−μk​pr​,ΔP=2πkμQ​(Rs​1​−R1​).
(33)
The changing pore volume is 2πϕ(R3−Rs3​)/3, giving Q=2πϕR2R˙. Hence
Q(R)=μ2πkΔPRs​​R−Rs​R​,R˙=μϕR(R−Rs​)kΔPRs​​.​
(34)
For the initially dry idealization R(0)=Rs​, integration gives the implicit front law
t=kΔPRs​μϕ​(3R3​−2Rs​R2​+6Rs3​​),R≥Rs​.​
(35)
Its right-hand side increases for R>Rs​, defining one advancing radius and thus the flux implicitly in time. Equivalently, with r=R/Rs​ and t∗​=μϕRs2​/(kΔP), t/t∗​=r3/3−r2/2+1/6. The infinite initial flux is an idealization of zero wetted resistance; inertia, finite inlet geometry and pore-scale effects regularize its earliest stage.
For a thin wetted shell ℓ=R−Rs​≪Rs​, the law reduces to
ℓ∼(μϕ2kΔPt​)1/2,Q∼2πRs2​(2μtkΔPϕ​)1/2.
(36)
For R≫Rs​, the hemispherical capillary imbibition model instead predicts
R∼(μϕ3kΔPRs​t​)1/3,Q⟶Q∞​=μ2πkΔPRs​​,​
(37)
with Q/Q∞​=1+Rs​/R+O((Rs​/R)2). Radial spreading makes the hydraulic resistance approach a finite value, while the increasing front area reduces its advance speed.
Comparison with one-dimensional imbibition. Under the same constant-driving-pressure, negligible-gravity assumptions, a planar front at depth z has Darcy flux q=kΔP/(μz) and storage law ϕz˙=q. Therefore
z∼(μϕ2kΔPt​)1/2,q∼(2μtkΔPϕ​)1/2.​
(38)
A fixed-area one-dimensional flux decays as t−1/2, whereas the hemispherical total flux tends to a constant and its radius grows as t1/3. If purely vertical imbibition includes gravity, upward penetration eventually approaches a capillary-rise height and downward penetration has a different gravity-driven limit. The hemispherical assumption itself neglects that directional gravity effect, so the power-law comparison is within its capillary-dominated regime.
Evaporation-limited steady radius. Take Fe​ as volumetric water loss per unit front area per unit time. At equilibrium the incoming flux equals 2πR2Fe​, giving
R(R−Rs​)=μFe​kΔPRs​​,R∗​=2Rs​+Rs2​+4kΔPRs​/(μFe​)​​.​
(39)
If the stated loss is a mass flux, replace Fe​ by Fe​/ρw​. Porosity cancels from this equilibrium but affects the time to approach it. The incoming flux decreases with R while the evaporating area increases, so the equilibrium is stable in this model. As Fe​→0, R∗​∼[kΔPRs​/(μFe​)]1/2; sufficiently large radii can invalidate the negligible-gravity or semi-infinite homogeneous-medium assumptions.
