Use the standard Euclidean convention e−SE​ and restore the curvature normalization explicitly. Let φ be the conventional dimensionless dilaton, whose worldsheet term is
Sφ​=4π1​∫Σ​d2σh​φR(2).
(12)
For constant φ0​, the Gauss-Bonnet theorem gives Sφ​=φ0​χ(Σ). A closed surface of genus g has Euler characteristic χ=2−2g, so its contribution is weighted by
e−Sφ​=e(2g−2)φ0​=gs2g−2​.
(13)
Thus
gs​=eφ0​.​
(14)
This is the dilaton normalization and Euler-characteristic weighting of string perturbation theory. If the unit curvature coefficient in the printed expression is retained literally in this Euclidean convention, then φ=4πΦ and gs​=e4πΦ0​. If Φ denotes the conventional dilaton, the usual omitted 1/(4π) is understood and gs​=eΦ0​. One must specify that normalization before exponentiating the field. Lorentzian overall signs are fixed consistently by Wick rotation.
For the sigma-model beta function, use
SE​=4πα′1​∫d2σgab​(X)∂μ​Xa∂μ​Xb
(15)
with vanishing two-form and dilaton. Perform the background field expansion of a string sigma model covariantly by setting X=expXˉ​η. In Riemann normal coordinates, the quadratic fluctuation action is
S(2)=4πα′1​∫d2σ[gab​Dμ​ηaDμ​ηb−Racbd​ηaηb∂μ​Xˉc∂μ​Xˉd].
(16)
The covariant derivative contains the pulled-back target connection. This expansion accounts for the connection vertices as well as the normal-coordinate metric expansion; keeping only a single metric tadpole would not give a covariant answer.
Integrating the Gaussian fluctuations gives 21​TrlogΔ, where
Δab​=−D2δab​−Racbd​∂μ​Xˉc∂μ​Xˉd.
(17)
Contracting the curvature vertex with the short-distance propagator traces Racad​ to the Ricci tensor Rcd​. In dimensional regularization at d=2−ϵ, the logarithmic integral is ∫ddp/(2π)d(p2+μ2)−1=1/(2πϵ)+O(1). With the stated curvature convention the divergent effective action is
Γdiv(1)​=−4πϵ1​∫Rab​(Xˉ)∂μ​Xˉa∂μ​Xˉb.
(18)
It is cancelled by the metric counterterm δgab​=α′Rab​/ϵ. Equivalently, write gbare=μ−ϵ(g+α′R/ϵ+⋯). Differentiating the bare coupling at fixed value gives the one-loop metric beta function of a string sigma model
βabg​=μdμdgab​​=α′Rab​+O(α′2).​
(19)
This loop is a loop of two-dimensional fluctuation fields: its expansion is in α′, not a change of worldsheet genus or an extra power of gs​. The sign here defines the renormalization scale to increase toward the ultraviolet. For a round target sphere, positive Ricci curvature makes its squared radius increase with that scale, consistent with the usual asymptotic freedom of the inverse-radius sigma-model coupling. With the printed kinetic coefficient 1/2 taken literally, 2πα′=1 and the leading coefficient is Rab​/(2π). Requiring the metric beta function to vanish gives Rab​=0 at this order. Full bosonic-string Weyl consistency also requires the other anomaly coefficients, including the critical-dimension condition when the dilaton vanishes.
For T-duality, choose coordinates (y,xi) adapted to the isometry and use α′=1 units for the local Buscher rules. Assume gyy​=0, with a spacelike isometry for the usual unitary circle duality. In light-cone worldsheet coordinates take the convention that E=g+B multiplies ∂+​Xa∂−​Xb. Gauge translations of y, replace ∂±​y by A±​, gauge fix y=0, and impose flatness with a multiplier y​. The relevant first-order Lagrangian is
L=​gyy​A+​A−​+gyi​(A+​∂−​xi+∂+​xiA−​)+gij​∂+​xi∂−​xj+y​(∂+​A−​−∂−​A+​).​
(20)
Integrating out y​ gives a locally pure-gauge connection and hence the original model. Instead integrate the last term by parts and solve the algebraic equations for A±​:
A+​=gyy​∂+​y​−gyi​∂+​xi​,A−​=−gyy​∂−​y​+gyi​∂−​xi​.
(21)
Substitution gives
L′=​gyy​1​∂+​y​∂−​y​+(gij​−gyy​gyi​gyj​​)∂+​xi∂−​xj+gyy​gyi​​(∂+​y​∂−​xi−∂+​xi∂−​y​).​
(22)
Reading its symmetric and antisymmetric parts yields the Buscher dual of a metric with vanishing two-form:
gyy′​=gyy​1​,gyi′​=0,gij′​=gij​−gyy​gyi​gyj​​,​
(23)
Byi′​=gyy​gyi​​,Biy′​=−Byi′​,Bij′​=0.​
(24)
The spectator metric is the Schur complement of gyy​. Reversing the orientation of y​ reverses the displayed mixed two-form signs; our multiplier and E=g+B conventions fix them.
The metric rules arise already from the classical algebraic elimination. For quantum equivalence the regulated Gaussian measure also produces the Buscher dilaton shift
φ′=φ−21​loggyy​.
(25)
For a compact isometry the multiplier periodicity and flat-connection sectors implement the exchange of momentum and winding. On a circle, restoring dimensions gives R′=α′/R. Thus the calculation derives the local dual background, while the measure and global sectors explain how it becomes a string-theory duality.
