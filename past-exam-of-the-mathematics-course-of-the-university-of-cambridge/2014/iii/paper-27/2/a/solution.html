Let Vt​=⟨M⟩t​, and first suppose V∞​=∞ almost surely in addition to strict increase. The Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem says that
Tu​=inf{t:Vt​>u},Gu​=FTu​​,Bu​=MTu​​
(9)
define a Brownian motion B in the time-changed filtration and give
Mt​=BVt​​.​
(10)
Each Tu​ is a stopping time and is finite. Continuity and strict increase of V make u↦Tu​ continuous, VTu​​=u and TVt​​=t.
Here are the martingale details behind this inverse-clock proof of the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem. Stopping a continuous local martingale when its bracket reaches n makes it an L2-bounded continuous martingale. This follows from the stopped Itô isometry or from the p=2 estimate proved in Question 1(a), applied after localization. In particular it is uniformly integrable, and optional sampling is valid even at an unbounded stopping time by taking limits. Applying this to M stopped at Tn​ shows that (MTu∧n​​)u≥0​ is a martingale. Thus B is a continuous local martingale. Time-changing M2−V in the same way shows Bu2​−u is a local martingale, so ⟨B⟩u​=u.
For completeness, the Lévy characterization of Brownian motion follows directly from the Itô formula. If a continuous local martingale N, starting at zero, has bracket u, then
exp(iθNu​+2θ2u​)
(11)
is a complex local martingale. Its modulus is bounded on each deterministic finite horizon, so it is a true martingale there. Consequently
E[eiθ(Nu​−Nv​)∣Gv​]=e−θ2(u−v)/2(v≤u).
(12)
Conditional characteristic functions give Gaussian increments independent of the past. Iterating this identity gives independent increments, and continuity completes the Brownian characterization. The identical vector argument proves the Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion when the bracket matrix is uI.
The printed strict-increase hypothesis does not imply V∞​=∞. For example, Mt​=∫0t​e−sdWs​ has strictly increasing bracket (1−e−2t)/2. To state the theorem under exactly the printed hypothesis, allow an independent enlargement of the probability space if the terminal clock L=V∞​ can be finite.
On {L<∞} the martingale M has a finite terminal limit. Indeed, stopping at each bracket level n gives an L2-bounded continuous martingale which converges; on {V∞​<n} the stopped process is the original one. This proves the finite-bracket convergence lemma. Set Tu​=∞ when u≥L and continue Nu​=MTu​​ by that terminal limit. The optional-sampling argument just given makes N a continuous local martingale with bracket u∧L. Moreover L is a stopping time in Gu​=FTu​​.
On a product extension add an independent Brownian motion β in clock time, and put
Bu​=Nu​+∫0u​1{s>L}​dβs​.
(13)
The two summands have zero quadratic covariation, and their brackets are u∧L and (u−L)+. Thus ⟨B⟩u​=u; the proved characterization makes B Brownian. Since Vt​<L at every finite t when L is finite, Mt​=BVt​​ still holds. This is the finite-lifetime extension of the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem. An infinite clock gives Brownian motion on the original space; a finite clock may require the independent extension.
