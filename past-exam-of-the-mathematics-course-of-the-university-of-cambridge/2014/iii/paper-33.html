For the normal linear model, the log-likelihood is
ℓ(β,σ2)=−2n​log(2πσ2)−2σ2(Y−Xβ)T(Y−Xβ)​.
(1)
The full column rank of the design matrix makes XTX invertible. Differentiating in β gives the normal equations XTXβ​=XTY. With the minimized residual sum of squares substituted, differentiation in σ2 gives
β​=(XTX)−1XTY,σ2=nRSS​.​
(2)
These are the maximum-likelihood estimators almost surely; RSS>0 almost surely when σ2>0 and n>p. The linear transformation of the multivariate normal distribution yields
β​∼Np​(β,σ2(XTX)−1).​
(3)
Define the hat matrix H=X(XTX)−1XT. The fitted values are Y=HY=Xβ​, the regression residuals are e=Y−Y=(I−H)Y, and RSS=eTe. The orthogonal projection I−H has rank n−p and annihilates Xβ. By Cochran's theorem,
RSS/σ2∼χn−p2​,β​ is independent of RSS.​
(4)
Consequently E(σ2)=(n−p)σ2/n, with bias −pσ2/n. The unbiased estimator is
σ2=n−pRSS​.​
(5)
Its square root, the reported residual standard error, estimates σ; the assertion of unbiasedness applies to the variance, not generally to its square root.
For the paper-strength analysis, let hi​ be the measured percentage and zi​=hi​−h. Both normal linear models use independent errors of common variance σ2. Their mean functions are β0​+β1​zi​ for lm1, and β0​+β1​zi​+β2​zi2​ for lm2, with separately fitted coefficients. The reported residual standard errors are 11.82 and 4.42, respectively.

A generalized linear model specifies independent responses in an exponential dispersion family, their means μi​=E(Yi​), and a link function connecting those means to a linear predictor. In the common notation,
fi​(yi​)=exp{ai​ϕyi​θi​−b(θi​)​+ci​(yi​,ai​ϕ)},μi​=b′(θi​),Var(Yi​)=ai​ϕV(μi​),

A 100-year return level is a flow threshold whose probability of being exceeded in a given year is one percent, under a stationary model for annual extremes. Equivalently, its long-run average waiting time between years with an exceedance is one hundred years. It does not imply that exceedances occur regularly one hundred years apart, or that one is guaranteed in any particular hundred-year interval. This definition uses yearly exceedance events even when the underlying observations are daily maxima.

e OurBigBook.com
This is the generalized Pareto distribution for the excess X−u, with scale σ>0 and shape ξ. Its support requires 1+ξ(x−u)/σ>0. The Pickands-Balkema-de Haan theorem says that, for distributions in an appropriate extreme-value domain of attraction, the conditional distribution of excesses above a sufficiently high threshold approaches a generalized Pareto form. This makes it the natural peaks-over-threshold method model; it is an asymptotic justification, not an assertion that every threshold is sufficiently high.
For ξ>0, the tail decays as a power and has no finite upper endpoint, giving a heavy tail. For ξ=0, the limiting distribution is exponential, with survival probability exp{−(x−u)/σ}. For ξ<0, there is a finite upper endpoint u−σ/ξ, giving a bounded tail. Thus the sign of the shape parameter distinguishes heavy, exponential-type and bounded tails.

Consider a competing interpolant f and set h=f−g. At each spline knot, h(xi​)=0. If its second derivative roughness penalty is infinite there is nothing to prove, so assume f′′∈L2. Its absolutely continuous first derivative makes h′ absolutely continuous as well, with h′′ defined almost everywhere.
On each knot interval the natural cubic spline has constant third derivative. Integrating by parts once gives
∫xi​xi+1​​g′′h′′=[g′′h′]xi​xi+1​​−g(3)∣(xi​,xi+1​)​[h]xi​xi+1​​.
(27)
The last term vanishes because h is zero at both endpoints. Summing over intervals cancels the interior boundary terms, since g′′ and h′ are continuous at the knots. The exterior terms vanish because g′′(x1​)=g′′(xn​)=0. Hence ∫g′′h′′=0. Expanding the square now gives
α∫x1​xn​​(f′′)2=α∫x1​xn​​(g′′)2+α∫x1​xn​​(h′′)2 ≥α∫x1​xn​​(g′′)2.​
(28)
Since α>0, equality requires h′′=0 almost everywhere. Absolute continuity then makes h affine. It has at least two distinct zeros because n≥2, so h=0. The natural cubic spline is the unique minimizer. This proves the minimum roughness property with absolutely continuous first derivatives, covering the weaker regularity in the question rather than assuming competitors are twice continuously differentiable. The case of two knots is included: the minimizing spline is their straight-line interpolant.

Its observed probability mass function is πi​+(1−πi​)e−μi​ at zero and (1−πi​)e−μi​μiyi​​/yi​! at a positive count. The zero-mixing probability here is explicitly πi​; it is distinct from the weight of the count component.
For the expectation-maximization algorithm, start with positive μi​ and 0<πi​<1. At iteration k, the E-step computes
τi(k)​=E(Zi​∣yi​)=⎩⎨⎧​πi(k)​+(1−πi(k)​)e−μi(k)​πi(k)​​,0,​yi​=0,yi​>0.​​
(35)
This distinguishes structural zeros from Poisson-generated zeros. Up to terms independent of the new parameters, the expected complete-data log-likelihood is
Q=∑i​{τi​logπi​+(1−τi​)log(1−πi​)}+∑i​(1−τi​){yi​logμi​−μi​}.
(36)
It separates into a fractional-response logistic fit and a weighted Poisson fit. Because treatment is binary, each component is saturated over its two treatment groups, so the M-step has closed forms.
For j=0,1, let Ij​={i:xi​=j}, nj​=∣Ij​∣, Sj​=∑i∈Ij​​τi​, and Cj​=∑i∈Ij​​yi​. The M-step score equations give
πj(k+1)​=nj​Sj​​,μj(k+1)​=nj​−Sj​Cj​​.​
(37)
Here ∑(1−τi​)yi​=Cj​, since every positive count has τi​=0. The explicit coefficient updates are
γ0(k+1)​γ1(k+1)​β0(k+1)​β1(k+1)​​=logit(π0(k+1)​),=logit(π1(k+1)​)−γ0(k+1)​,=logμ0(k+1)​,=logμ1(k+1)​−β0(k+1)​.​​
(38)
Iterate the E- and M-steps until the observed log-likelihood and parameter estimates stabilize. This is the EM algorithm for zero-inflated Poisson regression with explicit binary-group updates; merely naming two regression routines would not supply these expressions. Both treatment groups must be represented for both contrasts to be identifiable. Zero fitted group means or endpoint mixing probabilities are boundary solutions, interpreted through limits of the log or logit coefficients. As with other mixture models, multiple starts help distinguish competing stationary solutions; EM increases the likelihood but does not guarantee a global maximum from an arbitrary start.

There are two different effect scales in this zero-inflated Poisson regression. The count component describes the mean μ conditional on belonging to the susceptible component, including its possible zero outcomes. Its logarithmic link function coefficients exponentiate to ratios of those conditional means. The zero component describes the structural-zero probability π; its logit coefficients exponentiate to odds ratios, not probability ratios.
Holding the other covariates fixed, Centre B versus Centre A changes the susceptible count mean by the factor
e0.434635=1.5444,​
(39)
an increase of about 54.4%. It simultaneously multiplies the odds of being a structural zero by
e0.6715=1.9571.​
(40)
