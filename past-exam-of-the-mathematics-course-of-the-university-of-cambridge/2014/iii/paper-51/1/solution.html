Let P=(ωab) be a smooth antisymmetric matrix field. It defines a Poisson bivector through
{f,g}=ωab(x)∂a​f∂b​g,f,g∈C∞(U).
(1)
This Poisson bracket is bilinear, antisymmetric and a derivation in each argument. It defines a Poisson manifold when it also satisfies the Jacobi identity. Apply that identity in the form {{f,g},h}+{{g,h},f}+{{h,f},g}=0 to the coordinate functions. Since {xa,xb}=ωab, it gives
Jabc:=ωdc∂d​ωab+ωdb∂d​ωca+ωda∂d​ωbc=0.​
(2)
This is the coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector. It is also sufficient: in the Jacobi identity for arbitrary functions the terms containing second derivatives cancel by antisymmetry, leaving Jabc(∂a​f)(∂b​g)(∂c​h). Thus the coordinate condition captures the whole obstruction.
Now suppose the Poisson bivector is nondegenerate. Write S=P−1, so Sab​ωbc=δac​, and define the 2-form
Ω=21​Sab​dxa∧dxb.
(3)
An overall minus sign in identifying the symplectic form depends on the convention for Hamiltonian vector fields; it does not affect the closure argument. Differentiating the inverse matrix gives
∂d​Sij​=−Sia​(∂d​ωab)Sbj​=Sia​Sjb​∂d​ωab.
(4)
Contract the coordinate Jacobi identity with Sia​Sjb​Skc​. Antisymmetry gives ωdcSkc​=−δkd​, and similarly for the other terms, hence
Sia​Sjb​Skc​Jabc=−{∂k​Sij​+∂j​Ski​+∂i​Sjk​}=0.
(5)
The cyclic expression is precisely the coefficient of the exterior derivative dΩ. Therefore
dΩ=0.​
(6)
Since S is antisymmetric and nondegenerate, Ω is a symplectic form. This proves the closure of the inverse of a nondegenerate Poisson bivector. The matrix entries ωab are scalar functions; the codomain in the source's matrix description should be read as the space of antisymmetric matrices, rather than a vector-valued individual entry.
