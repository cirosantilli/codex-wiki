Let Mh​,Rh​ describe the halo and let v=vvir​. The cosmic baryon fraction is fb​=Ωb​/Ωm​=0.20, so the specified settled fraction gives Md​=0.10Mh​. The specific angular momentum constraint at the disk edge is
Rd​vd​=fj​Rh​v,fj​=0.10.
(5)
To infer an actual circular speed one must specify the disk's radial mass distribution: enclosed mass does not determine a disc rotation curve. Introduce a finite geometry coefficient κ by vd2​=κGMd​/Rd​. A rounded, centrally concentrated disk permits a monopole estimate κ≃1 at its outer edge; it is an explicit approximation, not the spherical shell theorem applied exactly to a razor-thin disk. Ignore the force of the unsettled baryons as well as the dark matter within the disk. Using v2=GMh​/Rh​, the angular-momentum estimate of a self-gravitating galactic disk gives
Rh​Rd​​=κfd​fj2​​=κ0.10​,vvd​​=fj​κfd​​=κ,fd​=0.10.​
(6)
An exact disk answer cannot be fixed by total disk mass alone; the dependence on κ records that missing input.
For the numerical virial radius of a dark-matter halo, adopt mean density Δc​=200 times the critical density at the formation epoch. This conventional definition gives
Mh​=34π​Δc​8πG3H2​Rh3​,v=HRh​Δc​/2​.
(7)
The supplied expansion law gives H(3)=286.49kms−1Mpc−1, and hence Rh​=v/(10H)=97.74kpc. Taking κ=1 yields
Rd​≃9.77kpc,vd​≃280kms−1.​
(8)
The corresponding virial mass of a dark-matter halo is Mh​≃1.78×1012M⊙​, and Md​≃1.78×1011M⊙​. Other overdensity conventions give Rd​∝Δc−1/2​ at fixed H,v,κ.
Interpret the wavelength separation as an observed-frame local mean near the redshift in question. Since Lyman-alpha absorption appears at λobs​=λα​(1+z), the incidence is
dzdNabs​​≃Δλobs​λα​​=2.
(9)
Assume one counted absorption system per intercepted disk, no unrelated forest systems or missed absorbers, unity neutral covering fraction, and a locally slowly varying population. Let σabs​ be the proper interception cross-section and nc​ the comoving number density. The proper density is np​=nc​(1+z)3, and the proper line element along the light path is dlp​=cdz/[H(z)(1+z)]. Thus the absorber incidence and comoving number density relation is
dzdNabs​​=nc​σabs​H(z)c(1+z)2​.​
(10)
For a thin circular disk, the projected area is πRd2​∣cosi∣. Isotropically oriented normals have ⟨∣cosi∣⟩=1/2, so the random-orientation absorbing-disk cross-section is σabs​=πRd2​/2. Consequently
nc​≃0.796Mpc−3for random orientations, κ=1, Δc​=200.​
(11)
If all disks are taken face-on instead, nc​≃0.398Mpc−3. Generally the random-orientation result scales as κ2(Δc​/200) and is divided by the neutral covering fraction. The finite mean redshift spacing is sizable, so a precision inference would integrate the incidence over the actual redshift interval rather than identify it with one local value.
There is also a halo abundance mass-budget bound on this formal result. The present mean matter density for these parameters is ρˉ​m,0​=Ωm​3H02​/(8πG)≃3.40×1010M⊙​Mpc−3. Distinct haloes of this mass cannot have nc​Mh​>ρˉ​m,0​, even if every matter particle belonged to them. Yet the inferred random-orientation population has
ρˉ​m,0​nc​Mh​​≃42,nc,max​=Mh​ρˉ​m,0​​≃0.019Mpc−3.
(12)
Even the face-on estimate exceeds this bound by about twenty-one. The numerical incidence result is conditional; the supplied population assumptions are not cosmologically consistent under this standard virial and compact-disk estimate. A larger neutral-gas absorption radius, a different absorber population, or different physical inputs are needed. For example, random orientations would require an absorbing radius at least about 63kpc merely to reach the all-matter upper bound, much larger than the calculated centrifugal radius. This check does not change the algebraic answer, but prevents treating it as a realizable population.
