Along an isolated two-phase coexistence line, two distinct stable minima have equal free energy. If this line ends at an ordinary interior endpoint without encountering another phase, the distinction between the two phases must disappear: their minima and the intervening barrier merge. For separated nondegenerate minima, their stationary values are smooth functions of the controls. The difference ΔV=V(m1​)−V(m2​) has ∂h​ΔV=−m1​+m2​=0. The implicit function theorem therefore continues the equation ΔV=0 as a local coexistence curve. It cannot end while those distinct stable minima persist.
At the merger the potential satisfies
V′(mc​)=V′′(mc​)=V(3)(mc​)=0,V(4)(mc​)>0.​
(5)
The third-derivative condition is essential: a stationary zero-curvature point with nonzero third derivative is an inflection, not a stable equilibrium. Shifting to ψ=m−mc​ gives an ordinary quartic Landau free energy with two independent control directions, temperature-like and field-like. The curvature and the order-parameter discontinuity vanish, the magnetic susceptibility diverges, and the correlation length diverges. The endpoint is therefore associated with a continuous transition. This is the critical endpoint of a quartic Landau free energy mechanism.
In the symmetric quartic example, the coexistence line is h=0,r<0. Its jump 2−r/u​ vanishes at (r,h)=(0,0), where V=um4/4. This supplies a concrete realization. The word “must” requires the stated ordinary-endpoint assumptions: a first-order line can instead meet a third phase, a multicritical point, a boundary of parameter space, or continue without a finite endpoint. A spinodal point alone is not an equilibrium critical endpoint.
