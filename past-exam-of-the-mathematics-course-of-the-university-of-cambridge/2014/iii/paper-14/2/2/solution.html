The second assertion is true. Separate the points pi​ in the circle direction by a small isotopy if necessary. Cutting S1×S at fibers just before and after pi​×γi​ exposes two copies of S. The surface framing is precisely the stated push-off pi′​×γi​: the two normal directions give homotopic nonzero normal fields along the curve.
For a single fiber-curve surgery, a cut and regluing of these copies by a Dehn twist changes the curve identified with the transverse meridian by one copy of the surface longitude. In the usual convention that negative surface-framed surgery produces a positive Dehn twist, the filling slope μ+λ implements τγi​−1​. One can see the coefficient locally by tracing a transverse arc across the twist annulus: it acquires one reverse turn along γi​, and reversing this cut-and-glue identifies the compressible slope as μ+λ. Reversing the circle parameter reverses the monodromy convention, without changing the existence of the fiber bundle.
Every filled block is therefore a product of S with an interval, with a modified endpoint identification. Their cyclic assembly is the mapping torus of the product of these inverse Dehn twists, in the order of the pi​ along the oriented circle. It has the original closed topological surface S as fiber, hence fibers over the circle. Intersections between the γi​ do not obstruct this argument: their twists occur in different fibers, and their ordered product need not commute.
