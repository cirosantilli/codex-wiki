Use κ-completeness in its usual forcing sense: every decreasing chain in a partial order of stronger conditions of length less than κ has a common stronger bound. Let f˙​∈M and choose p∈G forcing that it functions the ground ordinal α<κ into the ground set B. Below any stronger condition r, recursively decide each value of f˙​ in order. At a successor step the deciding conditions are dense, and at a limit stage use κ-completeness. After all α steps take another common bound. The recursion and its choices can be performed in M, using ground choice and closure, and it records a function g:α→B in M.
Thus below every r stronger than p there is a condition forcing f˙​=gˇ​ for some ground g. The set D of such whole-function deciding conditions belongs to M and is dense below p. Genericity with p∈G makes G meet D: adjoin the conditions incompatible with p to obtain a globally dense subset of a forcing order, and use directedness to rule out the incompatible alternative. A condition in G∩D then gives f=g∈M.
The reverse inclusion follows because ground functions remain functions with the same domain and values. Therefore
(αB)M=(αB)M[G].​
(38)
This closed forcing adds no short ground-valued sequences argument needs density of complete decisions. A single arbitrarily constructed lower bound need not belong to G, and would not by itself prove the claim.
