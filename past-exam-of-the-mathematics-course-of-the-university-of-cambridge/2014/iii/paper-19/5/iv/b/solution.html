For every infinite α<κ, the generic union at coordinate α gives a function gα​:ω→α. The requirement to assign (α,n) is dense for each n, and the requirement to use any specified value β<α is dense by assigning it at a fresh natural-number position. Therefore gα​ is a surjection, and every ordinal below κ becomes countable.
The κ-chain condition preserves the regularity of κ. For a direct verification, a forcing name for a function from some μ<κ into κ has fewer than κ possible values at each coordinate, using a maximal deciding forcing antichain. The union of these possible-value sets has size less than κ by regularity and is bounded in κ. No such function can be cofinal. In particular κ remains uncountable, while every smaller ordinal is countable. Hence
κ=ℵ1M[H]​.​
(35)
This is the finite Lévy collapse to omega-one.
