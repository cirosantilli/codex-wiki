All cardinalities in this part are first computed in M. The forcing has size κ. A family of κ finite domains has a κ-sized Delta-system, since κ is regular. There are fewer than κ possible value assignments on its finite root: each coordinate (α,n) allows fewer than κ values. Regularity lets us thin to two conditions, indeed κ many, with identical root assignments. Their union is a condition, proving the κ-chain condition.
Consequently every maximal forcing antichain has cardinality less than κ, but there is no one compulsory cardinality. For any nonzero cardinal μ<κ, the single-coordinate conditions assigning the values ξ<μ at (μ,0) form an forcing antichain of size μ. It is maximal: a condition already assigning that coordinate is compatible with its matching value, and a condition not assigning it is compatible with every allowed value. Thus every such size occurs, including singleton maximal forcing antichains. These are the maximal-antichain sizes in the finite Lévy collapse.
The paper writes p≤q for p⊆q. We use this printed weaker-first convention: q extends p and is stronger. Compatibility and generic meeting arguments below always refer to common extensions, so do not accidentally reverse the convention.
