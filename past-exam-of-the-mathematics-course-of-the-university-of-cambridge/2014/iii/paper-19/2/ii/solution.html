The bounding number b is the least size of an unbounded family in (ωω,≤∗). The almost disjointness number a is the least size of an infinite maximal almost disjoint family on omega of infinite subsets of ω. Requiring the family to be infinite excludes trivial finite maximal partitions.
A countable family {fi​:i<ω} is bounded by g(n)=1+maxi≤n​fi​(n). Thus ℵ1​≤b.
Suppose an infinite almost disjoint family on omega A has size less than b. Choose distinct members An​ and put Cn​=An​∖⋃i<n​Ai​. These are infinite and pairwise disjoint. For each B∈A, define hB​(n)=max((B∩Cn​)∪{0}) whenever the intersection is finite. If B=An​ at the exceptional index n, set hB​(n)=0. These are the only possible infinite intersections. A single g eventually dominates every hB​, because there are fewer than b of them. Pick xn​∈Cn​ with xn​>g(n) and put X={xn​:n<ω}.
For B not among the selected An​, only finitely many xn​ can lie in B. The same is true for B=Aj​, with its single exceptional index j ignored. Thus X is infinite and almost disjoint from every member of A, so the family was not maximal. This bounding-to-almost-disjointness inequality proves b≤a.
Finally start with an infinite pairwise disjoint family and use Zorn's lemma to extend it to a maximal almost disjoint family on omega. It is a family of subsets of ω, so has cardinality at most 2ℵ0​. Therefore
ℵ1​≤b≤a≤2ℵ0​.​
