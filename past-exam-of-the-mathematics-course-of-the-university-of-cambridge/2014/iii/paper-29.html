A compact H-hull is a bounded set K⊂H that is closed relative to the complex upper half-plane and whose complement D=H∖K is a simply connected domain. Its Euclidean closure is compact in H; “compact” here does not require separation from the real axis. One may equivalently describe the closed hull in H, with its real-boundary convention understood. Its mapping-out function is the unique conformal map to H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity.
Use the capacity convention hcap(Kt​)=2t. A chordal Schramm–Loewner evolution from 0 to infinity is the hull family of the Chordal Loewner equation
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−Ut​2​,g0​(z)=z,Ut​=κ​Wt​,​
(1)
where W is a real standard Brownian motion and κ≥0. The points with interior-point swallowing time for a Loewner chain at most t constitute Kt​. For each t, gt​ maps H∖Kt​ conformally onto H and has expansion gt​(z)=z+2t/z+O(z−2). The case κ=0 is the deterministic vertical-slit chain.
First identify the relevant transformed Loewner driving functions directly. For λ>0, define
g​t​(z)=λ−1gλ2t​(λz).
(2)
Differentiation gives ∂t​g​t​=2/(g​t​−λ−1Uλ2t​). Its initial value is z, its domain is H∖λ−1Kλ2t​, and its expansion is z+2t/z+O(z−2). Thus its driving function is λ−1Uλ2t​, by uniqueness of the differential equation and of the hydrodynamically normalized map. Brownian scaling proves
(λ−1Kλ2t​)t≥0​ =d (Kt​)t≥0​.​
(3)
This is capacity-parametrized scale invariance of a Loewner chain.
For a fixed time t, map the future remaining domains by z↦gt​(z)−Ut​. The corresponding hulls Ks​ are specified without any boundary-image ambiguity by
H∖Ks​=gt​(H∖Kt+s​)−Ut​.
(4)
Their mapping-out functions are
g​s​(z)=gt+s​(gt−1​(z+Ut​))−Ut​.
(5)
At s=0 this is the identity. Differentiating at a point in its domain yields
∂s​g​s​(z)=g​s​(z)−(Ut+s​−Ut​)2​.
(6)
The Laurent series is z+2s/z+O(z−2). Hence the transformed Loewner driver is exactly Ut+s​−Ut​, with the original capacity clock unchanged. This proves the composition rule for chordal Loewner driving functions, rather than assuming an identification of transforms.
The Brownian motion increments after t are independent of its past and have the original law. Since past hulls are measurable functions of the past Loewner driver, conditionally on the past, the centered mapped future is an independent copy of the original chain. This is the domain Markov property of a chordal Loewner chain. At an almost surely finite stopping time it follows likewise from the Strong Markov property.
For the converse, a precise class is essential: take capacity-parametrized Loewner chains generated by a continuous real Loewner driving function U, with U0​=0, and require the two displayed hull properties, including independence from the entire hull past in the domain Markov property. Within this class they characterize SLEκ​, for some κ≥0.
To see why the hull assertions determine the Loewner driver assertions, the hull domains determine their normalized maps uniquely. For any surviving point,
Ut​=gt​(z)−∂t​gt​(z)2​.
(7)
The left time derivative suffices for t>0, since U is continuous. A sufficiently high point survives up to any given finite time, so points in, n∈N, suffice to reconstruct the Loewner driver locally and measurably from the hull past. Thus the driving-function reconstruction for a chordal Loewner chain identifies the two past filtrations and makes the preceding scale and composition computations reversible.
The domain Markov property therefore gives stationary independent increments for U. Its continuity makes it a continuous Lévy process. Its classification gives Ut​=bt+κ​Wt​: in the Lévy–Khintchine formula continuity removes the jump measure, leaving characteristic function exp(t(ibu−κu2/2)). Driver scaling would turn the drift b into λb, so invariance for every λ forces b=0. The diffusion coefficient is unchanged. This proves the scale-and-domain-Markov characterization of SLE with its regularity and parametrization hypotheses stated explicitly.

Set β=π/α and use the branch ϕ(z)=zβ with argument in (−α/2,α/2). This is a conformal map from the wedge to the right half-plane, fixes the starting point 1, and sends the outer circle of radius r to that of radius
R=rπ/α.​
(8)
The two wedge sides map to the imaginary axis.
By conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion, the image of the stopped path is planar Brownian motion after the increasing conformal Brownian clock A(t)=∫0t​∣ϕ′(Bs​)∣2ds. This clock does not change which boundary portion is reached first. Localization away from the vertex justifies the map even when its derivative is unbounded there; the vertex is a polar point for planar Brownian motion and has zero hitting probability from 1.
Consequently
P1​{B[0,T(r)]⊂Wα​}=P1​{T(R)<S},
(9)
where the probability on the right is for the right half-plane. The power-map reduction for Brownian exit from a wedge also works at α=2π, when the wedge is the plane slit along the negative real axis.

Let E+​ and E−​ denote positive and negative real part at the circular exit. On {S<T(r)}, reflect the portion of the planar Brownian motion after S by z↦−z. This reflection fixes the imaginary axis and preserves distances from the origin. The Strong Markov property and reflection symmetry show that the resulting path has the same law, its circular exit time is unchanged, and E+​ is exchanged with E−​. Therefore
P1​(S<T(r),E+​)=P1​(S<T(r),E−​).
(10)
An exit with negative real part must first cross the imaginary axis. An exit before S has positive real part. The two points ir,−ir have zero circular exit probability, since circular harmonic measure has no atoms. Hence
P1​(E+​)P1​(E−​)​=P1​(T(r)<S)+P1​(S<T(r),E+​),=P1​(S<T(r),E−​).​
(11)
Subtracting proves
P1​(T(r)<S)=P1​(E+​)−P1​(E−​).​
(12)
This is the reflection identity for Brownian exit from a half-disc.

ψa​(w)=1−aww−a​,

The endpoints of the right semicircle satisfy

P1​(E+​)=2π2φ​=21​+π2​arctan(1/r).

Write D=H∖K. The mapping-out function is a conformal map gK​:D→H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity
gK​(z)=z+zaK​​+O(z−2)(z→∞).​
(18)
Equivalently, gK​(z)−z→0. The Riemann mapping theorem gives a map onto the half-plane. Since K is bounded, infinity has an analytic real-boundary neighborhood. After sending its image to infinity, the Schwarz reflection principle gives a Laurent series az+b+O(z−1) there, with a>0 and b real. A real affine automorphism removes a and b, giving the stated normalization. Reflection also makes aK​ real.
If g1​,g2​ both have this normalization, g2​g1−1​ is a conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane. Such maps are real Möbius transformations. The expansion forces it to fix infinity, have leading coefficient 1, and have zero translation. It is therefore the identity. Thus the normalized mapping-out function exists and is unique.

The half-plane capacity is the Laurent coefficient
hcap(K)=aK​=y→∞lim​iy(gK​(iy)−iy).​
(19)
It is real and nonnegative. For planar Brownian motion starting at z∈D, let τD​ be its first exit from D. The Brownian representation of half-plane capacity is
hcap(K)=y→∞lim​yEiy​[ImBτD​​].​
(20)
One may also state the underlying harmonic identity, valid throughout D,
ImgK​(z)=Imz−Ez​[ImBτD​​].
(21)
The process is killed at the hull or real boundary; real-boundary exits contribute zero. These are the Brownian characterizations, with the normalization that a Chordal Loewner equation at speed 2 produces capacity 2t.

c OurBigBook.com
Let f=gK−1​. The reflection at infinity used in part (a) gives analytic expansions there for both inverse maps. In particular,
f(w)=w−waK​​+O(w−2),
(22)
uniformly for large w∈H, including approach close to the real axis.
Fix R<∞. If gK​ were unbounded on D∩{∣z∣≤R}, there would be points zn​ there with wn​=gK​(zn​) tending to infinity in modulus. The inverse expansion would imply zn​=f(wn​)=wn​+O(1/wn​), contradicting boundedness of zn​. Thus gK​ is bounded on every bounded portion of its domain, including points arbitrarily near a rough hull boundary. This is the inverse-at-infinity criterion for local boundedness of a mapping-out function.
On the region ∣z∣>R for sufficiently large R, the expansion of gK​ gives gK​(z)−z=O(1/z). On the remaining bounded region, the preceding bound for gK​ and the bound for z give a finite bound for their difference. Therefore
z∈Dsup​∣gK​(z)−z∣<∞.​
(23)
The argument uses reflection near infinity only. It does not assume that the real part of the map extends continuously at every point of an arbitrary hull; the sharp displacement bound for a compact H-hull is a further quantitative version of this boundedness.

Set v(z)=ImgK​(z), a positive harmonic function on D. First justify its boundary behavior. For zn​→p∈∂D with p finite, part (c) makes gK​(zn​) bounded. Any subsequential limit w lies in H. If Imw>0, continuity of the inverse inside H would give p=gK−1​(w)∈D, a contradiction. Thus
v(z)⟶0as z→p∈∂D.
(24)
This boundary degeneration under a mapping-out function is valid without a smooth or locally connected hull boundary.
The harmonic function u(z)=v(z)−Imz consequently has nonpositive finite-boundary values. Its value tends uniformly to zero at infinity, by the Laurent series. On the bounded domain D∩{∣z∣<R}, the maximum principle for harmonic functions bounds u by max(0,εR​), where εR​=supD∩{∣z∣=R}​∣gK​(z)−z∣→0. Let R→∞ with z fixed. We obtain
ImgK​(z)≤Imz.​
(25)
This is the height contraction of a hydrodynamically normalized mapping-out function. The exhaustion controls infinity explicitly, which is necessary when using a maximum principle on an unbounded domain.

Here the otherwise undefined printed domain H must mean D=H∖K. Let the Brownian motion start at z∈D, and let τR​ stop it on reaching height R or leaving D. Take R>max(Imz,supK​Imw). This stopping time is finite almost surely, since it is no later than exit of its imaginary coordinate from (0,R).
By part (d), 0≤v(w)≤R in the stopped domain. Its finite-boundary values vanish except on the top boundary, by the argument of part (d). Localization and the optional stopping theorem for this bounded harmonic martingale therefore give
v(z)=Ez​[v(BτR​​)1{ImBτR​​=R}​].
(26)
Write pR​=Pz​(ImBτR​​=R) and use the uniform displacement bound M from part (c). On the top boundary, ∣v(w)−R∣≤M, hence
∣v(z)−RpR​∣≤MpR​.
(27)
The stopped imaginary coordinate is itself a bounded martingale. Since its exit height is nonnegative,
Imz=Ez​[ImBτR​​]≥RpR​.
(28)
Consequently ∣v(z)−RpR​∣≤MImz/R→0, proving the high-level escape representation of a mapping-out height:
ImgK​(z)=R→∞lim​RPz​(ImBτR​​=R).​
(29)
If H instead meant the whole upper half-plane, the right side would always be Imz. For example, the mapping-out function of a vertical slit K=(0,ia] is gK​(w)=w2+a2​ with branch asymptotic to w; at z=iy, y>a, its height is y2−a2​<y. This demonstrates why killing on the hull is essential to the printed formula.

Use Ut​=2Wt​ and write Zt​=Xt​+iYt​ up to its interior-point swallowing time for a Loewner chain Tz​. The Chordal Loewner equation gives
dZt​=Zt​2​dt−2dWt​,dYt​=−∣Zt​∣22Yt​​dt.
(30)
The branch of the complex logarithm with argument in (0,π) is well-defined while t<Tz​. The Itô formula yields the crucial cancellation
dlogZt​=Zt​1​dZt​−2Zt2​1​d[Z]t​=−Zt​2​dWt​.​
(31)
The 2/Zt2​ drift is cancelled by the quadratic variation term, because κ=4. Thus both Lt​=log∣Zt​∣ and θt​=argZt​ are continuous local martingales, with
dLt​=−∣Zt​∣22Xt​​dWt​,dθt​=∣Zt​∣22Yt​​dWt​.
(32)
This is the logarithmic martingale for SLE4.
We justify absence of a finite swallowing time rather than presuming that the logarithm survives forever. On a finite horizon H, 0<Yt​≤Imz. Also ∣Xt​∣ is bounded pathwise before Tz​∧H. Indeed, while ∣Xt​∣≥1 its drift 2Xt​/∣Zt​∣2 has absolute value at most 2; on each excursion outside [−1,1], integrate from its starting point and bound the Brownian oscillation on [0,H]. For example,
∣Xt​∣≤max(1,∣Rez∣)+2H+4sups≤H​∣Ws​∣.
(33)
Therefore Lt​ is bounded above pathwise on this interval.
Suppose Tz​≤H. By the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem, L is a Brownian motion run at its own quadratic variation. If that clock diverged as t↑Tz​, Brownian oscillation would make L unbounded above, contradicting the preceding bound. The one-sided bound criterion for a martingale clock therefore gives a finite clock limit and a finite real limit for Lt​. Hence ∣Zt​∣ is bounded away from zero near Tz​.
Now Yt​=Y0​exp(−2∫0t​∣Zs​∣−2ds) has a strictly positive limit at Tz​. The drift in X is integrable there, so continuity of W gives a finite limit for X as well. The limiting point is in H and away from the Loewner driver singularity, and the differential equation extends past Tz​, a contradiction. Thus Tz​=∞ almost surely. A point of the Loewner trace at a finite time belongs to that time's hull, so
P{z∈γ∗}=0.​
(34)
This proves the fixed-interior-point avoidance of SLE4 without using simplicity as an input.
The angle remains in (0,π) at all finite times. The bounded local martingale criterion upgrades its local martingale equation to a genuine martingale:
E[θt​∣Fs​]=θs​,Eθt​=argz.​
(35)
In particular this is the SLE4 angle martingale, and it converges almost surely and in L1 by bounded martingale convergence theorem.
It remains to identify the limiting angle using the assumed simple path tending to infinity. Orient that path from 0 to infinity. Its left component is the one adjacent to the negative real half-axis. Under gt​, the left boundary of the slit domain maps to (−∞,Ut​) and the right boundary to (Ut​,∞). The harmonic measure of the former as seen from z is
πarg(gt​(z)−Ut​)​=πθt​​.
(36)
This follows by conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion: in the upper half-plane, arg(w−Ut​)/π is the bounded harmonic function with values 1 on the left half-axis and 0 on the right.
To justify the limiting boundary classification, condition on a simple proper realization of the path and use an independent planar Brownian motion from z. It exits the upper half-plane in finite time almost surely, so its path up to that time is compact. The curve tends to infinity, so its intersection with this compact set is contained in a finite initial curve segment. Once that segment has been drawn, the Brownian path exits the slit domain through its left boundary exactly when z is in the final left component: a path from that component cannot reach the right boundary without crossing the curve, and the reverse assertion holds on the right. Endpoints have zero harmonic measure. Bounded convergence of these exit indicators proves
θt​⟶π1{z is in the left component}​.
(37)
Taking expectations in the bounded angle martingale gives the SLE4 left-passage probability
P{z is in the left component}=πargz​=21​−π1​arctan(ImzRez​).​
(38)
For the imaginary axis it is 1/2; near the negative real axis it tends to 1, fixing the orientation of “left”.
