The closest point theorem in a Hilbert space says that, for every nonempty closed convex set C⊂H and x∈H, there is exactly one p∈C minimizing ∥x−p∥.
Put d=infy∈C​∥x−y∥ and choose yn​∈C with ∥x−yn​∥→d. The midpoint belongs to C because it is a convex set. The parallelogram law gives
∥yn​−ym​∥2=2∥x−yn​∥2+2∥x−ym​∥2−4​x−2yn​+ym​​​2≤2∥x−yn​∥2+2∥x−ym​∥2−4d2⟶0.
(10)
Thus (yn​) is a Cauchy sequence. Completeness of the Hilbert space and closedness of C give a limit p∈C, with ∥x−p∥=d. Applying the same identity to two minimizers gives their squared distance at most zero, proving uniqueness.
The resulting projection is characterized by
Re⟨x−p,z−p⟩≤0(z∈C).​
(11)
Indeed, differentiate ∥x−p−t(z−p)∥2 at t=0+; the minimum there gives the inequality. Conversely, expanding ∥x−z∥2 proves minimality from this inequality. For a closed linear subspace, both signs of each direction are allowed, so x−p is orthogonal to that subspace: this recovers the orthogonal projection.
