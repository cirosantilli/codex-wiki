To specify a limit, fix a number c∈(ur​,ul​) independently of ε and normalize vε​(0)=c. If V′=Q(V) and V(0)=c, uniqueness gives vε​(s)=V(s/ε). Consequently

At x=σt the normalized profile equals c for every ε. This single-line value is immaterial to the weak solution. Convergence holds pointwise off the line and in Lloc1​ by bounded convergence; it cannot be uniform across a nonzero jump. The transition has thickness of order ε.
This is the vanishing viscosity approximation to a compressive entropy shock. The Rankine-Hugoniot condition makes the step a weak solution of the inviscid scalar conservation law, while F′(ur​)<σ<F′(ul​) means characteristic curves enter the shock from both sides. For every smooth convex function η used as an entropy, with entropy flux for a scalar conservation law q′=η′F′, the viscous equation gives
∂t​η(u)+∂x​q(u)=ε∂xx​η(u)−εη′′(u)ux2​≤ε∂xx​η(u).
(56)
Against compactly supported tests the right side tends to zero, since uε​ stays in [ur​,ul​]. Passing to the Lloc1​ limit yields the entropy inequality, explaining the direction selected by positive viscosity.
A translation must be fixed to obtain this particular limit. An ε-dependent translate V((s−aε​)/ε) can converge to a shock at a different location, to a constant if its center escapes, or fail to converge if the centers oscillate. Thus existence of profiles alone does not specify a single vanishing-viscosity limit without a phase normalization.
