The bounded-variation space carries the norm ∥u∥BV​=∥u∥L1(Ω)​+∣Du∣(Ω). Its weak-star convergence in BV is characterized by
uk​→u in L1(Ω),Duk​⇀∗Du in M(Ω;Rn).​
(1)
The second condition means convergence of the vector measure pairings against every φ∈C0​(Ω;Rn). Equivalently, strong convergence in L1 together with supk​∣Duk​∣(Ω)<∞ suffices: integration by parts identifies the limit on smooth compactly supported tests, and uniform approximation extends this to C0​ tests.
The bounded-variation compactness theorem says that
ksup​(∥uk​∥L1(Ω)​+∣Duk​∣(Ω))<∞​
(2)
guarantees a subsequence convergent in weak-star convergence in BV on a bounded Lipschitz domain. This is the uniform criterion for relative sequential compactness. If asking only for the existence of one convergent subsequence, the exact condition is the existence of a BV-bounded subsequence, equivalently liminfk​∥uk​∥BV​<∞. The entire sequence need not be bounded: alternating zero functions and constants tending to infinity gives a simple example. Conversely, a convergent subsequence has bounded L1 norm and bounded total variation seminorm by the Uniform boundedness principle for its derivative-measure pairings.

Use a bounded BV extension operator for the bounded Lipschitz domain. Extend uk​ to wk​∈BV(Rn), supported in one fixed compact set, with ∥wk​∥BV(Rn)​≤CΩ​∥uk​∥BV(Ω)​. Such an extension is obtained by reflection in Lipschitz boundary charts, a partition of unity and a fixed cutoff function. A naive zero extension must also charge its boundary-trace jump; it cannot discard that contribution.
Let M bound these norms and let wk,ϵ​=wk​∗ρϵ​ be their mollifications. The BV mollification error estimate gives
∥wk,ϵ​−wk​∥1​≤ϵ∣Dwk​∣(Rn)≤ϵM.
(3)
The variation measure here is on Rn. The printed lemma's ∣Dw∣(Ω) needs this correction unless it also assumes all the derivative mass lies in Ω: a nonconstant bump supported outside Ω disproves its literal wording. The correct estimate follows by averaging the BV translation estimate ∥w(⋅−h)−w∥1​≤∣h∣∣Dw∣(Rn) over a mollifier supported in ∣h∣≤ϵ.
For each fixed ϵ>0, convolution gives uniform bounds
∥wk,ϵ​∥∞​≤M∥ρϵ​∥∞​,∥∇wk,ϵ​∥∞​≤M∥∇ρϵ​∥∞​.
(4)
The mollifications have common compact support and are uniformly equicontinuous. The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem supplies a uniformly convergent subsequence at each scale ϵ=1/m. A diagonal subsequence converges at every one of those scales. For two late members of that subsequence,
∥wk​−wℓ​∥1​≤2M/m+∥wk,1/m​−wℓ,1/m​∥1​.
(5)
First choose large m, then late k,ℓ; the sequence is a Cauchy sequence in L1. Its limit w belongs to the BV space because the total variation seminorm on a domain is sequentially lower semicontinuous. Uniform derivative bounds and integration by parts give Dwk​⇀∗Dw. Restriction to Ω proves the required weak-star convergence in BV.

Apply the direct method in the calculus of variations to J(u)=∥f−Ku∥1​+α∣Du∣(Ω). Since J(0)=∥f∥1​, take a minimizing sequence with J(uk​)≤C:=∥f∥1​+1. Then
∣Duk​∣(Ω)≤C/α,∥Kuk​∥1​≤∥f∥1​+C,∥uk​∥1​≤∥K−1∥(∥f∥1​+C).
(6)
Thus the sequence is bounded in the bounded-variation space. The bounded-variation compactness gives a subsequence converging in the strong convergence sense in L1 to u∈BV(Ω). Boundedness of the linear operator K gives Kuk​→Ku strongly in L1, so the residual norm converges. The variation term is sequentially lower semicontinuous, hence
J(u)≤kliminf​J(uk​)=v∈BV(Ω)inf​J(v).​
(7)
Therefore u attains the infimum. The useful inverse hypothesis is the lower bound ∥u∥1​≤CK​∥Ku∥1​; a bounded inverse on the range already suffices for this proof. No uniqueness follows from the nonsquared L1 fidelity.

For a scalar u∈BV(Ω), put Et​={x∈Ω:u(x)>t}. The coarea formula for BV functions is the measure identity
∣Du∣(A)=∫R​Per(Et​;A)dt​
(8)
for every Borel A⊆Ω. For almost every real t, Et​ is a finite-perimeter set, with Per(Et​;A)=∣DχEt​​∣(A). In particular TV(u)=∫R​Per(Et​;Ω)dt. This is relative perimeter: it counts interfaces inside Ω, and does not add the boundary jump of a zero extension.

ii OurBigBook.com
For t∈R, define the set functional
Ft​(E)=αPer(E;Ω)+∫E​(t−f)dx.
(9)
The ROF level-set formulation states that the unique minimizer of J(u)=∥u−f∥22​/2+αTV(u) has superlevel sets minimizing Ft​ for almost every t. Conversely, an admissible u with minimizing superlevel sets is the ROF minimizer. Thus the concise characterization is
u^ minimizes ROF⟺{u^>t}∈argEmin​Ft​(E) for a.e. t.​
(10)
The signed layer-cake identity for quadratic fidelity and the coarea formula for BV functions give
J(u)−21​∥f∥22​=∫R​[Ft​({u>t})−Ft​(Et0​)]dt,Et0​={Ω,∅,​t<0,t≥0.​
(11)
Indeed u2/2−fu=∫R​(t−f)(χ{u>t}​−χ{0>t}​)dt pointwise. Its absolute integral is bounded by u2/2+∣fu∣, so Fubini's theorem applies for u,f∈L2. The negative-level baseline is essential; integrating the unadjusted Ft​ would generally diverge.
Each Ft​ attains its minimum. A minimizing sequence of indicator functions has bounded L1 norm, and comparison with the empty set bounds its perimeter by an L1 forcing bound. Bounded-variation compactness supplies a limiting indicator function. The forcing integral converges by dominated convergence, while relative perimeter is sequentially lower semicontinuous.
For s<t, the supplied comparison lemma applied to (f−t)/α<(f−s)/α makes every selected minimizer At​ contained in As​ up to a null set. The comparison of perimeter minimizers with ordered forcing also follows directly: compare At​ with At​∩As​, compare As​ with At​∪As​, add and use submodularity of relative perimeter to obtain (t−s)∣At​∖As​∣≤0.
Select minimizers at rational levels, remove their countably many exceptional null sets, and reconstruct v(x)=sup{q∈Q:x∈Aq​}. The strict superlevel set is {v>t}=⋃q>t​Aq​. This union also minimizes Ft​: take q↓t, use monotone L1 convergence of its indicator functions and sequential lower semicontinuity, and note that m(t)=minE​Ft​(E) satisfies ∣m(t)−m(s)∣≤∣Ω∣∣t−s∣.
To justify finite energy before assuming it, clip this reconstruction to vM​∈[−M,M]. Comparison with the empty set gives αPer(Et​;Ω)≤∫Ω​∣t−f∣ uniformly on a bounded interval of levels. Since vM​=−M+∫−MM​χEt​​dt, pairing with compactly supported test-field divergences bounds its total variation seminorm by ∫−MM​Per(Et​;Ω)dt<∞. Thus vM​ belongs to the BV space before applying the coarea formula for BV functions. The layer-cake argument on the finite interval [−M,M] shows J(vM​)≤J(TM​w) for every finite-energy competitor w, where TM​ is clipping. In particular w=0 bounds the L2 norms and variations uniformly. Fatou's lemma excludes infinite values of v on a positive-measure set. Bounded-variation compactness and weak convergence in L2 identify an admissible limit v. For any fixed competitor, TM​w→w in L2 and its variation tends to that of w, by bounded-variation contraction under clipping and sequential lower semicontinuity. Thus J(v)≤J(w).
The quadratic fidelity is strictly convex, so every ROF minimizer equals v almost everywhere and has the selected minimizing superlevel sets. Conversely, for any u whose levels minimize Ft​, integrate the levelwise inequality against any competitor's levels in the displayed identity to obtain J(u)≤J(w). This proves both directions for signed as well as nonnegative data.

The literal assumptions suffice for the scalar quadratic fidelity problem. One can avoid regularity of level-set boundaries by moving only a clipped part of the minimizer. We prove the stronger jump-amplitude inequality for total variation denoising:
[u]νu​​([f]νu​​−[u]νu​​)≥0Hn−1-a.e. on Ju​.​
(12)
Comparison with the zero function gives finite energy and hence u∈L2. Here u=u^, and both differences use the same oriented BV traces on a hypersurface. Reversing the normal reverses both differences and leaves the inequality unchanged. Outside Jf​, the two traces of f agree, so the inequality would read −[u]2≥0 at a jump of u. It therefore gives the requested no-new-jumps property of total variation denoising in every dimension.
First use residual-preserving clipping of an ROF minimizer. For an integer M>0, put
w=TM​u=max(−M,min(M,u)),r=u−w,g=f−r=f−u+w.
(13)
The coarea formula for BV functions gives scalar total variation splitting under clipping:
TV(u)=TV(w)+TV(r).
(14)
Indeed the levels in (−M,M) contribute to w, while the levels outside that interval contribute to r. This uses the full signed coarea formula. For any z∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω), minimality of u and the triangle inequality for the total variation seminorm give
αTV(w)+αTV(r)+21​∥w−g∥22​≤αTV(z+r)+21​∥z−g∥22​≤αTV(z)+αTV(r)+21​∥z−g∥22​.
(15)
Cancel the tail variation. Thus w is a bounded ROF denoising minimizer for g∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω). The data g may still be unbounded. This is an exact reduction preserving w−g=u−f; it does not truncate the data and then pass to a limit of different reconstructions.
We next prove the jump-amplitude inequality for a bounded ROF minimizer, allowing its data to be unbounded. Fix a coordinate j, a nonnegative φ∈Cc∞​(Ω), and let Φt​ be the local flow of the smooth vector field Y=φej​. The flow is the identity near the domain boundary, preserves each line parallel to ej​, and satisfies Φ−t​=Φt−1​. Write
Tt​a=a∘Φt​,δt​a=Tt​a−a,Jt​=detDΦt​.
(16)
These maps are diffeomorphisms, with J±t​=1+O(t) and Jt​+J−t​−2=O(t2) uniformly.
The useful trace calculation is the BV jump-product limit with one bounded factor. For a∈BV(Ω) and b∈BV(Ω)∩L∞(Ω),
limt↓0​t1​∫Ω​δt​aδt​bdx=∫Jb​​[a]νb​​[b]νb​​φ∣νb​⋅ej​∣dHn−1.
(17)
The right side is integrable: ∣[b]∣≤2∥b∥∞​ and only the common jump part of a contributes. The same limit holds with both increments replaced by their negative-time increments, still dividing by positive t.
Here is why this calculation needs only one bounded factor. By the BV slicing theorem, almost every coordinate slice of a and b has one-sided representatives. On one such interval let μ=Da and use its right-continuous representative. Since φ≥0,
δt​a(x)=μ((x,Φt​(x)]).
(18)
Fubini's theorem rewrites the slice integral as
t1​∫δt​aδt​bdx=∫(t1​∫Φ−t​(s)s​δt​b(x)dx)dμ(s).
(19)
At each interior s, the inner expression tends to φ(s)(b(s+)−b(s−)); if φ(s)=0, it is zero. Its absolute value is at most 2∥b∥∞​∥φ∥∞​. Dominated convergence against ∣μ∣ therefore leaves just the measure atoms common to the two slices. The bound 2∥b∥∞​∥φ∥∞​∣Da∣ is also integrable over the transverse coordinates, by the BV slicing theorem. Integrating the slice jump sums gives the surface integral and its factor ∣νb​⋅ej​∣. Negative-time increments give the same product, because both slice differences reverse sign. At no point is a uniform bound on a across the slices required.
For 0<θ<1, use the mixed competitors
wt​=(1−θ)w+θTt​w.
(20)
The total variation under opposite smooth flows satisfies
TV(Tt​w)+TV(T−t​w)−2TV(w)=O(t2).
(21)
To see this for the entire vector Radon measure Dw=σw​∣Dw∣, the change of variables formula gives
TV(Tt​w)=∫Ω​∣cof(DΦ−t​)σw​∣d∣Dw∣.
(22)
For cofA=(detA)A−T, the two cofactor matrices expand as I±tB+O(t2), with the same B and opposite signs. Their norm expansions on ∣σw​∣=1 have cancelling linear terms. Integrating proves the estimate, including the absolutely continuous, jump and Cantor parts. The BV transformation formula is also given in Lemma 4.2 on differentiable regularizers; that lemma does not assume bounded data. The total variation seminorm is a convex function, so
TV(wt​)+TV(w−t​)−2TV(w)≤O(t2).
(23)
Thus minimality forces the sum of the two fidelity changes to have nonnegative limit after division by t.
It remains to evaluate that sum without bounding g. Put Fg​(v)=21​∥v−g∥22​. Exact expansion of the quadratic fidelity, together with change of variables, gives the opposite-flow fidelity identity for quadratic data:
Fg​(wt​)+Fg​(w−t​)−2Fg​(w)=−2θ(1−θ)​(∥δt​w∥22​+∥δ−t​w∥22​)+θ∫δt​gδt​wdx+o(t).
(24)
For completeness, the cross-term identity fixing its sign is
∫g(δt​w+δ−t​w)dx=−∫δt​gδt​wdx+∫g(1−J−t​)δ−t​wdx.
(25)
The last integral is o(t): ∥1−J−t​∥∞​=O(t), while
∫∣g∣∣δ−t​w∣dx≤K∥δ−t​w∥1​+2∥w∥∞​∫{∣g∣>K}​∣g∣dx⟶0.
(26)
Take t→0 first, then K→∞. Here g∈L1 and the local flow is strongly continuous in L1. The remaining Jacobian mass term is O(t2)∥w∥22​. This argument avoids multiplying an unbounded fidelity derivative by an uncontrolled derivative measure.
Apply the BV jump-product limit with one bounded factor with (a,b)=(g,w) and (w,w), and use minimality. For every nonnegative φ and every coordinate j,
0≤∫Jw​​([g][w]−(1−θ)[w]2)φ∣νw​⋅ej​∣dHn−1.
(27)
Let θ↓0. These are inequalities for finite signed Radon measures, so arbitrary nonnegative smooth tests imply nonnegativity of their densities. Since at least one coordinate of a unit normal is nonzero,
[w]([g]−[w])≥0Hn−1-a.e. on Jw​.
(28)
This proves the bounded-minimizer lemma with arbitrary BV∩L2 data.
Finally return to w=TM​u and g=f−u+w. At almost every finite approximate jump point of u, choose an integer M>max(∣u+∣,∣u−∣). The BV traces on a hypersurface commute with clipping, so w±=u±, r±=0 and g±=f± there. It is consequently a jump point of w, and its inequality is exactly [u]([f]−[u])≥0. A countable union over M removes all exceptional surface-null sets. Outside Jf​, the BV traces on a hypersurface of f agree almost everywhere. We conclude
Hn−1(Ju^​∖Jf​)=0for f∈BV(Ω)∩L2(Ω), Ω⊂Rn.​
(29)
The mechanism is exact scalar coarea splitting, paired smooth-flow variations, a one-bounded-factor BV trace limit, and localization through integer clipping levels. It works in every dimension under the printed hypotheses, without essential boundedness of f or u and without regularity of their level-set boundaries.
In total variation calibration notation, divz=(u−f)/α. Since this divergence is itself in the BV space, the proved inequality equivalently reads
[u]νu​​[divz]νu​​≤0.
(30)
Thus an upward output jump forces the appropriate nonpositive jump of the calibrated divergence. This is a consequence of the variational argument above, with common oriented BV traces on a hypersurface; no curvature of the rectifiable interface or differentiability of its normal is assumed.

The structure theorem for functions of bounded variation is the measure representation of the distributional derivative: for u∈BV(Ω) there is a unique finite vector Radon measure Du with
∫Ω​udivφdx=−∫Ω​φ⋅dDu,∣Du∣(Ω)=TV(u)​
(31)
for every φ∈Cc1​(Ω;Rn). It also has a polar decomposition of a vector measure Du=σu​∣Du∣ with ∣σu​∣=1 for ∣Du∣-almost every point.
To prove it, let L(φ)=−∫udivφ. The test-function definition of total variation seminorm, applied to both signs, gives ∣L(φ)∣≤TV(u)∥φ∥∞​. Compactly supported smooth vector fields are uniformly dense in C0​(Ω;Rn), so L extends uniquely to a bounded functional there. The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem, applied componentwise, gives the unique vector measure Du. The operator norm of L is exactly the defining variation supremum; the vector-measure dual norm is ∣Du∣(Ω), proving equality. Conversely any finite measure satisfying the identity bounds that supremum, so this also characterizes membership in the BV space.
The Radon-Nikodym theorem applied to the components relative to ∣Du∣ gives σu​; the definition of the variation measure forces ∣σu​∣=1 almost everywhere. If u is smooth, ordinary integration by parts gives Du=∇uLn. Compact support of the test field removes any boundary contribution; no boundary regularity is needed for this representation statement.

First apply Lebesgue decomposition to the derivative measure relative to Ln:
Du=Dau+Dsu,Dau=∇uLn,Dsu⊥Ln.
(32)
Here ∇u is the almost-everywhere approximate gradient, rather than an assertion that u belongs to W1,1. Split the singular part into its jump part of a BV derivative and Cantor part of a BV derivative:
Du=∇uLn+(u+−u−)νu​Hn−1⌊Ju​+Dcu.​
(33)
An approximate jump point has a unit normal νu​ and distinct finite BV traces on a hypersurface u+,u−, obtained as mean limits on the corresponding two half-balls. Their set Ju​ is the jump set of a BV function, countably (n−1)-rectifiable. Reversing the normal swaps the BV traces on a hypersurface and leaves the displayed measure unchanged. The approximate discontinuity set Su​ differs from Ju​ only by an Hn−1-null set.
The remaining Dcu=Dsu−Dju is singular to Lebesgue measure and gives zero mass to every set with sigma-finite Hn−1 measure. It is diffuse rather than a second jump contribution. In one dimension the three parts are illustrated by an affine function, a step function and the Cantor function, respectively. Countably many jumps therefore do not imply that the singular derivative has no Cantor part of a BV derivative.

Put μ=Du, a finite signed Radon measure, and define
Fl​(t)=μ((a,t)),Fr​(t)=μ((a,t]).
(34)
For φ∈Cc1​((a,b)), Fubini's theorem for the signed measure gives
−∫ab​Fl​(t)φ′(t)dt=−∫(a,b)​[∫sb​φ′(t)dt]dμ(s)=∫φdμ.
(35)
Thus DFl​=μ=Du. A distribution on a connected interval with zero derivative is constant: any compactly supported test of integral zero is the derivative of a compactly supported test, so it pairs to zero with u−Fl​. Choose the resulting constant c. Then
ul​(t)=c+μ((a,t)),ur​(t)=c+μ((a,t])​
(36)
are one-sided representatives of a one-dimensional BV function. The first equals u almost everywhere. Their difference is μ({t}), nonzero at at most countably many points, so the second also equals u almost everywhere.
Finite-measure continuity applied to ∣μ∣ proves Fl​(s)→Fl​(t) as s↑t, and Fr​(s)→Fr​(t) as s↓t. Moreover the opposite one-sided limits are Fl​(t+)=Fr​(t) and Fr​(t−)=Fl​(t). Consequently both representatives are continuous exactly where μ({t})=0. For each positive integer m, there are only finitely many measure atoms of magnitude at least 1/m, since their total magnitudes are bounded by ∣μ∣((a,b)). Their union is countable, proving the requested discontinuity bound. This does not require the jump points to be isolated; they can be dense.
The hint's one-dimensional statement is consistent with this construction: zero H0 measure means an empty set, so Su​⊆Ju​. This concerns approximate discontinuities of the BV space class; arbitrary changes of representative at points could create artificial pointwise discontinuities. The interval-mass representatives above remove that ambiguity and give the required one-sided continuity directly.
