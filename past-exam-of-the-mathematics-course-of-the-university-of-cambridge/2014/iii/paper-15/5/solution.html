Use the given orientation throughout, and the usual convention that the manifold has no boundary. In an oriented manifold chart the metric volume form is
ωg​=det(gij​)​dx1∧⋯∧dxn.
(18)
If y is another oriented chart and A=∂x/∂y, then gy​=Atgx​A, so detgy​​=(detA)detgx​​ because detA>0. This is exactly the transformation of the top exterior product. The formulas therefore agree on overlaps and define a smooth positive volume form. Equivalently it takes value one on any positively oriented orthonormal tangent frame.
The metric induces an inner product on p-forms by making the increasing exterior products of an orthonormal coframe orthonormal. The Hodge star operator is the unique pointwise linear map ∗:ΛpT∗M→Λn−pT∗M satisfying
η∧∗θ=⟨η,θ⟩g​ωg​.
(19)
For an increasing multi-index I, ∗eI is the complementary wedge with the sign making eI∧∗eI=ωg​. Swapping the blocks of p and n−p factors introduces (−1)p(n−p). Therefore
∗2=(−1)p(n−p)idon Ωp(M).​
(20)
In particular ∗1=ωg​ and ∗ωg​=1.
For compactly supported smooth f, the (n−1)-form f∗α has compact support. Stokes theorem and the graded Leibniz rule give
0=∫M​d(f∗α)=∫M​df∧∗α+∫M​fd∗α.
(21)
A top form τ equals (∗τ)ωg​. Consequently this Hodge integration by parts for one-forms becomes exactly
∫M​(−f∗d∗α)ωg​=∫M​⟨df,α⟩g​ωg​.
(22)
Compact support of f is enough; α need not itself have compact support.
Define the codifferential on p-forms by δ=(−1)n(p+1)+1∗d∗ and the Hodge Laplacian by Δ=dδ+δd. A harmonic differential form is a smooth form in kerΔ. On functions this is the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator, Δf=δdf=−divg​gradg​f. This sign convention is required by the product identity; it is the negative of the divgrad convention also commonly used for the Laplace-Beltrami operator.
For a p-form β, the square of the Hodge star operator and the codifferential formula give
δ(∗β)=(−1)p+1∗dβ,d(∗β)=(−1)p∗δβ.
(23)
Applying the second formula to dβ and the first to δβ gives
Δ(∗β)=(−1)p+1d(∗dβ)+(−1)pδ(∗δβ)=∗δdβ+∗dδβ=∗Δβ.
(24)
Thus Hodge star commutes with the Hodge Laplacian. Since ∗ is invertible, β is harmonic if and only if ∗β is harmonic. This holds without compactness; we have not used the generally false noncompact implication that a harmonic form must be closed and coclosed.
For a smooth function h and one-form α, the same graded Leibniz rule gives δ(hα)=hδα−∗(dh∧∗α)=hδα−⟨dh,α⟩g​. Apply this to d(f1​f2​)=f2​df1​+f1​df2​ to obtain the product rule for the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator
Δ(f1​f2​)=f2​Δf1​+f1​Δf2​−2⟨df1​,df2​⟩g​.​
(25)
The Hodge decomposition theorem states that on a compact oriented boundaryless Riemannian manifold, smooth forms have the L2-orthogonal decomposition
Ωp(M)=Hp(M)⊕dΩp−1(M)⊕δΩp+1(M),Hp(M)=kerΔ,
(26)
and each de Rham cohomology class has a unique harmonic differential form representative. To spell out the last conclusion, a harmonic form is closed and coclosed because ⟨Δη,η⟩=∥dη∥2+∥δη∥2. If a closed form decomposes as h+du+δv, then dδv=0; integration by parts gives ∥δv∥2=⟨v,dδv⟩=0. Thus it represents h. A harmonic exact form has zero norm by adjointness, proving uniqueness. The analytic existence of the decomposition is the stated Hodge decomposition theorem.
Now let M be compact, connected and oriented. A harmonic function satisfies 0=⟨f,Δf⟩=∥df∥2, so it is constant. The Hodge star operator identifies H0(M) with Hn(M), hence Hn(M)=Rωg​. A Riemannian metric exists by Question 3 even if none was initially chosen. Using the harmonic representative of each class, we obtain the top de Rham cohomology of a compact connected oriented manifold
HdRn​(M)≅R,[ωg​] spans it.​
(27)
The class is nonzero also directly from Stokes theorem, since ∫M​ωg​>0 while every exact top form has zero integral. Boundarylessness matters: a compact interval has zero first de Rham cohomology.
