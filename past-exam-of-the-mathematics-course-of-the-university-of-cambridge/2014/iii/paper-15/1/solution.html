The exterior derivative is an R-linear map d:Ωp(M)→Ωp+1(M), agrees with the differential df of a smooth function, satisfies the graded Leibniz rule d(α∧β)=dα∧β+(−1)pα∧dβ for α∈Ωp(M), and has d2=0. These properties determine it locally and hence globally. To see locality directly, if a differential form α vanishes near x, choose a smooth bump function χ equal to one near x and supported where α=0. The identity d(χα)=dχ∧α+χdα gives (dα)x​=0. Thus global forms can be computed using local extensions in a manifold chart.
In coordinates write α=∑I​aI​dxi1​∧⋯∧dxip​. Since d(dxi)=d2xi=0, the graded Leibniz rule forces
dα=∑I,j​∂j​aI​dxj∧dxi1​∧⋯∧dxip​.
(1)
This is the uniqueness of the exterior derivative from its axioms. The coordinate formula also establishes existence: it has the stated properties, and the chain rule shows that coordinate changes give the same operator.
The de Rham cohomology is the real quotient vector space
HdRp​(M)=im(d:Ωp−1(M)→Ωp(M))ker(d:Ωp(M)→Ωp+1(M))​.
(2)
Thus a cohomology class records a closed differential form modulo an exact differential form. In degree zero there are no exact forms; closed functions are locally constant. The Poincaré lemma says that every closed form of positive degree on a star-shaped open subset of Rn is exact, and hence that positive-degree closed forms are locally exact on a smooth manifold.
For the first de Rham cohomology of the two-sphere, let U=S2∖{N} and V=S2∖{S}. Stereographic projection identifies each with R2, so a closed one-form α has primitives fU​,fV​. On the connected overlap U∩V, the derivative of fU​−fV​ is zero, so this difference is a constant. Subtracting that constant from fU​ makes the primitives agree. They glue to a global smooth primitive. Therefore HdR1​(S2)=0.
Let q:S2→RP2 be the double covering map and a the antipodal map. The pullback of a differential form identifies forms downstairs with a-invariant forms upstairs. Averaging (η+a∗η)/2 commutes with d. If an invariant form is exact upstairs, averaging its primitive proves it exact downstairs. Conversely an invariant cohomology class has an invariant representative by the same averaging. This proves the de Rham cohomology of a finite quotient identification HdRp​(RP2)≅HdRp​(S2)a∗. In degree zero the sphere is connected and a∗ fixes constants; degree one is zero; in degree two the granted action is multiplication by −1, whose invariant real subspace is zero. Forms of degree greater than two vanish. Hence
HdRp​(RP2)={R,0,​p=0,p>0.​​
(3)
This is real de Rham cohomology; it does not detect the integral two-torsion of the real projective plane.
