Choose the time dependence e−iωt and measure distances in background-wavenumber units. Thus L0​=Δ+1, the incident field satisfies L0​ψi​=0, and the scattering potential convention is V=n2−1, giving L0​ψ=−Vψ. To match the minus sign in the paper's integral, take the outgoing Green function to satisfy L0​G=δ. This is the negative of the frequently used outgoing Helmholtz equation fundamental solution satisfying L0​G=−δ. In physical coordinates one restores k02​ in the scattering potential, or absorbs it in the integral kernel; the sign convention must remain consistent.
On a region where the incident and total fields are nonzero, introduce the logarithmic wave perturbation
ψ=ψi​eχ,χ=log(ψ/ψi​).
(1)
Choose a continuous logarithm branch connected to the unperturbed field. Substitution into the Helmholtz equation gives
Li​χ+∇χ⋅∇χ=−V,Li​=Δ+2ψi​∇ψi​​⋅∇.
(2)
The quadratic term uses the complex bilinear dot product, not the squared modulus of the gradient. Write V=εV1​ and χ=εχ[1]+⋯. The first-order Rytov approximation discards that quadratic term. Since L0​(ψi​χ)=ψi​Li​χ, the outgoing first correction is
χ1​(r)=−ψi​(r)1​∫G(r,r′)V(r′)ψi​(r′)dr′,
(3)
and hence
ψ1(R)​(r)=ψi​(r)exp[−ψi​(r)1​∫G(r,r′)V(r′)ψi​(r′)dr′].​
(4)
The validity of the first Rytov approximation concerns the omitted logarithmic correction. Its next contribution satisfies Li​χ2​=−∇χ1​⋅∇χ1​, so a useful explicit criterion is that the outgoing solution χ2​ be small in the region of interest. Weak refractive index contrast and small wave phase gradients on a wavelength scale provide the usual perturbative regime, with weak amplitude fluctuations and no strong focusing or zeros that destroy the logarithm. A sufficient local source comparison is ∣∇χ1​⋅∇χ1​∣≪∣V∣ where the scattering potential is nonzero, together with control of propagation of that error. This is not a universal pointwise test at zeros of V.
Small accumulated wave phase is not required in the same way as in a linear field approximation: the exponential retains that wave phase accumulation. Large gradients, strong multiple-scattering amplitude effects or field zeros can still invalidate the Rytov approximation. The integrals also require a finite scattering region or appropriate convergence conditions.
