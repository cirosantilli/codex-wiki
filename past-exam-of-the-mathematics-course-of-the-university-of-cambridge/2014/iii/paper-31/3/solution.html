First derive the survival renewal equation for a classical risk model. Before the first claim, capital rises deterministically. The first interarrival time has exponential distribution of rate λ, independently of the claim size. Conditioning on its time and size, and using the Markov property after that claim, gives
φ(u)=∫0∞​λe−λt∫0u+ct​φ(u+ct−x)f(x)dxdt.
(18)
A claim larger than the capital available then causes immediate ruin and contributes zero. There is almost surely a first claim because λ>0.
Put g(v)=∫0v​φ(v−x)f(x)dx. Changing variables v=u+ct yields
φ(u)=cλ​eλu/c∫u∞​e−λv/cg(v)dv.
(19)
Since 0≤g≤1, this representation makes φ locally absolutely continuous, and differentiation gives, at least almost everywhere,
cφ′(u)=λφ(u)−λ∫0u​φ(u−x)f(x)dx.
(20)
Integrate from 0 to u. The integrands are nonnegative, so Tonelli theorem permits interchange in the double integral. In particular
∫0u​∫0v​φ(v−x)f(x)dxdv=∫0u​φ(w)F(u−w)dw.
(21)
Subtracting this from ∫0u​φ(w)dw, changing variables once more, and using the permitted boundary value gives
φ(u)=1−cλμ​+cλ​∫0u​φ(u−x)(1−F(x))dx.​
(22)
The constant is the zero-capital survival probability. Positive relative safety loading means c>λμ, so it is positive. The convolution kernel is a multiple of the integrated tail distribution density and has total mass λμ/c<1, making this a defective renewal equation.
For the individual portfolios define ρi​=λi​μi​/ci​. Under the positive-loading convention of the question, 0<ρi​<1. At zero capital, portfolio i survives with probability 1−ρi​. The independence of random variables of the entire portfolio processes makes their ultimate survival events independent. Consequently
P(all individual portfolios survive)=i=1∏n​(1−ci​λi​μi​​).​
(23)
The exponential form is not needed for this zero-capital probability under positive loading; only the claim mean enters. For completeness, the company description does not separately repeat positive loading for every portfolio. If nonpositive loadings are allowed, certain ruin with nonpositive loading and finite claim variance instead gives
P(all individual portfolios survive)=i=1∏n​(1−ρi​)+​.​
(24)
Here the positive part sets a nonpositive factor to zero. To justify the additional case, observe capital at claim times: its independent increments are ci​T−X, with mean ci​/λi​−μi​. A negative mean sends their partial sums to −∞ by the strong law of large numbers. At zero mean, these increments have finite nonzero variance. The central limit theorem gives P(Sk​<−K)→1/2 for each fixed K, so the probability of unboundedness below is at least 1/2. That event is unchanged by altering finitely many increments and hence is a tail event; the Kolmogorov zero-one law makes its probability one. Ruin therefore occurs almost surely also at zero loading.
For the merged claims, Poisson superposition of insurance portfolios gives total arrival rate λ=∑i​λi​. Each arrival is from portfolio i with probability λi​/λ, independently of other arrival labels. Thus its claim-size mixture distribution has density
f(x)=i=1∑n​λλi​​μi​1​e−x/μi​,x>0.​
(25)
The weights sum to one, so this density integrates to one. An independent transform verification uses the aggregate for one accounting period. If Mi​(r)=(1−μi​r)−1, then its moment-generating function is
∏i​exp(λi​(Mi​(r)−1))=exp(λ[∑i​λλi​​Mi​(r)−1]).
(26)
This is precisely a compound Poisson distribution with parameter λ and the displayed claim-size law. The transform identity can safely be read at r≤0; positive arguments must be below the relevant poles.
Premium incomes and initial capitals add. Therefore the merged premium rate is C=∑i​ci​, the merged initial capital is zero, and its mean claim size is
μ​=∑i​λλi​​μi​,λμ​=∑i​λi​μi​.
(27)
Its zero-capital survival probability is consequently
P(merged portfolio survives)=1−∑i​ci​∑i​λi​μi​​=i∑​Cci​​(1−ρi​).​
(28)
Under the question's positive-loading context this is a premium-weighted average of the individual survival probabilities, rather than their product. If arbitrary individual loadings are admitted, recompute the loading of the merged portfolio; its finite-variance mixture law gives the complete formula
P(merged portfolio survives)=(1−∑i​ci​∑i​λi​μi​​)+​.​
(29)
An individually nonpositive loading does not force merged ruin when aggregate premiums still exceed aggregate expected claims. The survival probability under risk pooling is at least their product because a product of numbers in [0,1] is at most each factor. Pooling allows one portfolio's surplus to cover another's deficit, so merged survival does not require every original portfolio to remain solvent separately.
