For ℜs>1, the Riemann zeta function is the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series ζ(s)=∑n≥1​n−s. Apply Abel summation to the tail, with counting function ⌊w⌋−⌊x⌋. The upper boundary term tends to zero, giving
∑n>x​n−s=−⌊x⌋x−s+s∫x∞​⌊w⌋w−s−1dw.
(1)
Substitute ⌊w⌋=w−{w} and integrate the w term. This proves the fractional-part continuation formula for the Riemann zeta function
ζ(s)=n≤x∑​n−s+s−1x1−s​+{x}x−s−s∫x∞​{w}w−s−1dw.​
(2)
The endpoint convention is valid whether or not x is an integer. Since 0≤{w}<1, the last integral converges locally uniformly for ℜs>0, including after differentiation on compact subsets. It therefore defines a holomorphic function there. The other terms are entire except for x1−s/(s−1). By the identity theorem for holomorphic functions, the formula supplies a meromorphic continuation with exactly one pole in ℜs>0: a simple pole at s=1 of residue one.

For the Hardy-Littlewood approximation to the Riemann zeta function, take f(w)=−tlogw/(2π). On w≥x≥∣t∣/π, ∣f′(w)∣≤1/2 and f′ is monotone. It approximates the Riemann zeta function by a finite Dirichlet polynomial, transfers exponential sum estimates to bounds in the critical strip, yields elementary near-one bounds for ζ and its derivative, and supports estimates for the mean value of Dirichlet polynomials and numerical calculations.

Here is a quantitative local form of the Landau zero-free-region theorem. Let t≥2, 0<η≤1/4, M≥2, and suppose ∣ζ(z)∣≤M on the two closed discs of radius η centred at 1+η/4+it and 1+η/4+2it. Then every zero β+it satisfies
1−β≥1+log(M/η)cη​​
(21)
for an absolute positive c. In particular, if upper bounds of this form hold locally for every large height, they give a zero-free region of this width. The discs are away from the pole, and the Euler product excludes zeros to the right of one.
We state precisely the permitted local logarithmic-derivative lemma. If f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of ∣z−z0​∣≤R, f(z0​)=0, and ∣f∣≤M, then for ∣z−z0​∣≤R/3 away from zeros,
f(z)f′(z)​=∑∣ρ−z0​∣≤R/2​z−ρ1​+O(R1+log(M/∣f(z0​)∣)​).
(22)
The zeros are counted with multiplicity. This standard disc estimate, which may be assumed here, follows by factoring nearby zeros and applying a Cauchy estimate for derivatives to the remaining logarithm. When every zero has real part at most one and ℜz>1, the zero terms have nonnegative real parts, giving the required lower bound. The estimate with fixed radius ratios is also recorded as Lemma 24.17 in Montgomery and Vaughan's general treatment.
The reciprocal Euler product gives ∣ζ(1+η/4+ijt)∣≥1/ζ(1+η/4)≫η for j=1,2. Put B=1+log(M/η). The lemma's error on both discs is therefore O(B/η). Let d=1−β. If d≥η/24, the desired conclusion already holds after reducing c. Otherwise β+it is among the local zeros and, for 1<σ≤1+η/4,
