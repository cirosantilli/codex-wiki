Let h=logψ, with ψ>0, and set f=2αhθ​. Direct differentiation gives
fZ​−ffθ​−αfθθ​=2α∂θ​{hZ​−αhθθ​−αhθ2​}.
(35)
The heat equation ψZ​=αψθθ​ implies hZ​=α(hθθ​+hθ2​), so the bracket vanishes. Conversely its being independent of θ can be absorbed into a Z-dependent multiplicative normalization of ψ, leaving f unchanged. This proves the Cole-Hopf transformation with the positive sign appropriate to the negative-flux Burgers convention. Although the algebra works for nonzero α, the given forward Gaussian function diffusion kernel and a physical vanishing-viscosity limit require α>0.
Take U,L>0 for the stated Burgers N-wave. Integrating ∂θ​logψ(0,θ)=f0​(θ)/(2α) and normalizing the exterior value to one gives
ψ0​(θ)={exp{U(L2−θ2)/(4α)},1,​∣θ∣<L,∣θ∣≥L.​
(36)
This function is continuous at ±L; its logarithmic derivative has the specified jumps. Convolution with the heat kernel is positive and solves the heat equation for Z>0. Splitting the integral into the exterior baseline and the interior correction yields
ψ=1−Iα​(θ,L,Z)+4παZ​eUL2/(4α)​∫−LL​exp{−4αUφ2​−4αZ(φ−θ)2​}dφ.
(37)
Put a=1+UZ. Completing the square gives
Uφ2+Z(φ−θ)2​=Za​(φ−aθ​)2+aUθ2​.
(38)
Changing the interior integration variable to η=aφ changes its limits to ±La and the Gaussian function width to Za. The Jacobian and normalization leave the factor a−1/2. Thus the Cole-Hopf solution for a Burgers N-wave is
ψ=1−Iα​(θ,L,Z)+Iα​(θ,La,Za)ψ​,ψ​=a−1/2exp{4αU​(L2−aθ2​)},f=2α∂θ​logψ.​
(39)
Here Iα​ is the normalized Gaussian function mass of its indicated interval. Its explicit error function representation is
Iα​(θ,b,w)=21​{erf(4αw​b−θ​)+erf(4αw​b+θ​)}.
(40)
For fixed w>0, the Gaussian function concentrates at φ=θ as α↓0. Consequently
Iα​(θ,L,w)⟶H(L2−θ2)​
(41)
away from the endpoints; at θ=±L the limit is 1/2. The transition layer has width O(αw​). This is an approximate identity argument, not a uniform step approximation across the endpoints.
For fixed Z>0 and ∣θ∣≪L, both interval masses tend to one, and the exponentially large positive ψ​ dominates the exterior correction. Its logarithmic derivative therefore gives
f(Z,θ)≃−1+UZUθ​(∣θ∣≪L, α↓0).​
(42)
For ∣θ∣≫La, both interval masses are exponentially small and the weighted interior integral is also negligible, while the exterior contribution tends to one. Therefore f(Z,θ)≃0​ in the specified far exterior.
An exponentially weighted Gaussian function tail should not be discarded solely because its unweighted interval mass tends to zero. In fact, comparing the order-one exterior term with ψ​ gives the sharper inviscid Burgers N-wave fronts ∣θ∣=L1+UZ​, not La. Away from these fronts, the vanishing-viscosity limit is
f(Z,θ)⟶{−Uθ/(1+UZ),0,​∣θ∣<L1+UZ​,∣θ∣>L1+UZ​.​
(43)
Inside ∣θ∣<La, this follows from the sign of L2−θ2/a in the exponential. Outside La, the constrained Gaussian function maximum lies at an interval endpoint and has negative exponent. The right shock wave's speed is UL/(2a​), equal both to the derivative of La​ and to minus half the sum of its two limiting states; the left shock wave is its reflection. This checks consistency with the Rankine-Hugoniot condition and shows that the requested near-center and far-exterior approximations are compatible with the full entropy limit.
