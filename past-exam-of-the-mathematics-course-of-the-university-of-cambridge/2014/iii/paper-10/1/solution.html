(A) For a finite point set P and a family C of distinct unit circles in R2, the Szemerédi–Trotter theorem for unit circles states
I(P,C)≤C(∣P∣2/3∣C∣2/3+∣P∣+∣C∣)​
(1)
with an absolute constant C. Here I counts point-circle incidences. Distinctness matters: repeated copies of the same circle are not separate members of this geometric family. A dilation gives the same bound for circles of any one fixed positive radius, with the same constant.
(B) Write N=∣P∣ and D=∣Δ(P)∖{0}∣. The comparison is a bound on the cardinality of the distinct-distance set, rather than on the set itself. For each positive distance ρ, take the N circles of radius ρ centred at points of P. Their incidences between points and curves count exactly the ordered pairs (p,q) at distance ρ. After dilation by 1/ρ, part (A) bounds this number by
C(N4/3+2N)≤C1​N4/3(N≥1).
(2)
Every ordered pair of distinct points contributes to exactly one of these counts. Thus the unit-circle method for a distinct-distance lower bound gives
N(N−1)≤C1​DN4/3.

(4)
For N=1 the distance set is {0} and the conclusion holds after adjusting the absolute constant; the empty set causes no difficulty.
