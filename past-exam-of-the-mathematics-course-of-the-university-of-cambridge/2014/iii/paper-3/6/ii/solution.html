For the Kronecker quiver representation with arrows I and N=(00​10​), an endomorphism (P,Q) satisfies Q=P and PN=NP. Solving the second equation gives
EndQ​(X)={(a0​ba​):a,b∈k}≅k[t]/(t2).
(17)
Thus this representation is indecomposable but is not a brick: its endomorphism ring is a local endomorphism ring, while the nonzero endomorphism N is nilpotent.
For a general indecomposable non-brick, the proof of Ringel lemma on bricks finds a proper indecomposable submodule with nonzero self-extensions. Repetition in strictly decreasing dimension reaches a brick module Y⊂X with ExtQ1​(Y,Y)=0. The linked proof supplies the minimal-rank, retraction and hereditary-extension steps.
Now assume the Tits form of a quiver is positive definite. If an indecomposable X were not a brick, this Y would give the contradiction
0<q(dimY)=1−dimExtQ1​(Y,Y)≤0.
(18)
Hence X is a brick. For its nonzero dimension vector n, positivity and integrality then imply
0<q(n)=1−dimExtQ1​(X,X)≤1,q(n)=1,ExtQ1​(X,X)=0​.
(19)
Thus every indecomposable in this case is a rigid brick. This deduction uses the Ringel lemma on bricks and the Ringel form, without requiring the full Gabriel theorem.
