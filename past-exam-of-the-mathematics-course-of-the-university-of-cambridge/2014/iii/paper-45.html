Use the metric gμν=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and the usual Dirac basis, with (γ0)†=γ0 and (γi)†=−γi. In this solution the sign of the chirality matrix is the one specified in the paper, γ5=−iγ0γ1γ2γ3. Define t0​=1 and ti​=−1, with no summation in componentwise transformation formulas.
Complex conjugation changes the explicit −i to +i. Three spatial gamma matrices each contribute another minus sign, so
Bγ5∗B−1=iγ0(−γ1)(−γ2)(−γ3)=−iγ0γ1γ2γ3=γ5​.
(1)
Also (γ5)2=1 and {γ5,γμ}=0 by the Clifford algebra.
The quantum time-reversal operator is antiunitary: it conjugates numerical coefficients, including the Dirac spinors and plane-wave exponentials in the mode expansion of a Dirac field. To fix the spin phase explicitly, put ϵs​=(−1)1/2−s and use
T^bs(p)T^−1=ϵs​b−s(pT​),T^ds†(p)T^−1=ϵs​d−s†(pT​).
(2)
This convention gives T^2=−1 on a one-fermion state, since ϵs​ϵ−s​=−1. In the transformed expansion, change variables from (p,s) to (pT​,−s). The Lorentz-invariant phase-space measure is unchanged, and pT​⋅x=−p⋅xT​. The coefficient of bs(p)e−ip⋅xT​ is therefore
ϵ−s​u−s∗(pT​)=−ϵs​u−s∗(pT​)=γ5Cus(p).
(3)
The same calculation applies to the antiparticle coefficient. Thus, with this explicitly fixed spin convention,
B=γ5C,T^ψ(x)T^−1=Bψ(xT​).​
(4)
The spin phase in the Dirac time-reversal matrix matters here: a common change of the one-particle time-reversal phase replaces B by −B; the phase-independent relation is B∝γ5C. It changes neither the defining conjugation property nor any bilinear result below. Normalize B to be a unitary matrix. In the Dirac basis, γ0 is real and commutes with B, so transforming the Dirac adjoint gives
T^ψˉ​(x)T^−1=(Bψ(xT​))†γ0∗=ψ†(xT​)B−1γ0=ψˉ​(xT​)B−1​.
(5)
Here complex conjugation of the matrix defining the Dirac adjoint is essential. An explicit realization consistent with the sign of γ5 used here is C=iγ2γ0 and B=γ1γ3; it has B−1=−B, so conjugation by B and by B−1 coincides. The usual changes of Dirac spinor basis by unitary matrices carry the adjoint and time-reversal matrix with them.
For the charge-conjugation matrix, the gamma matrix adjoint and transpose identities give γμT=tμ​γμ∗. Consequently BγμTB−1=γμ. Since C=γ5B in the chosen phase convention,
CγμTC−1=γ5BγμTB−1γ5=γ5γμγ5=−γμ​.
(6)
This proof uses a temporal Hermitian matrix and spatial skew-Hermitian matrices explicitly; the transpose identity is preserved when C is transformed appropriately with the gamma matrices.
For the Fermi interaction, let Vpnμ​=pˉ​γμn and Apnμ​=pˉ​γμγ5n. In the displayed Dirac basis, the inverse version of the conjugation relation also holds. Antiunitarity, B−1γμ∗B=tμ​γμ and B−1γ5∗B=γ5 give
T^Vpnμ​(x)T^−1=tμ​Vpnμ​(xT​),T^Apnμ​(x)T^−1=tμ​Apnμ​(xT​).
(7)
The leptonic weak charged current has the same component signs. In the contraction of leptonic and hadronic currents, the two tμ​ factors cancel. Its two independent operators thus keep their form while their coefficients become gV∗​ and gA∗​. The Hermitian-conjugate term transforms separately; its presence does not remove a relative complex phase between the vector and axial couplings.
With all intrinsic phases fixed to one and a real positive Fermi constant, invariance requires real coefficients in that convention. A common phase of gV​ and gA​ can instead be absorbed into a rephasing of the nucleon fields, and hence into their intrinsic time-reversal phases. The convention-independent condition, for gV​=0, is
Im(gA​gV∗​)=0,gA​/gV​∈R.​
(8)
This is the relative weak phase condition for time reversal. If one coefficient vanishes, there is no relative phase to constrain; the remaining common phase can be removed. A nonreal ratio violates time-reversal symmetry even though the Lagrangian density includes its Hermitian conjugate.

Take Dμ​=∂μ​+iKμ​, with the Pauli matrices acting on the Higgs doublet. For electroweak hypercharge Y=1/2,
Kμ​=2g​τaWμa​+2g′​Bμ​I2​=21​(gWμ3​+g′Bμ​g(Wμ1​+iWμ2​)​g(Wμ1​−iWμ2​)−gWμ3​+g′Bμ​​).
(9)
This specifies every component of the gauge covariant derivative for the electroweak interaction. Expanding the gauge-covariant kinetic term makes its derivative, trilinear and quartic interactions explicit:
(Dμ​ϕ)†Dμϕ=(∂μ​ϕ)†∂μϕ+i[(∂μ​ϕ)†Kμϕ−ϕ†Kμ​∂μϕ]+ϕ†Kμ​Kμϕ,
(10)
where the Pauli matrix multiplication law gives
ϕ†Kμ​Kμϕ=4g2​Wμa​Waμϕ†ϕ+4g′2​Bμ​Bμϕ†ϕ+2gg′​Wμa​Bμϕ†τaϕ.
(11)
The antisymmetric Pauli contribution vanishes because Wμa​Wbμ is symmetric in a,b. Reversing the sign convention for Dμ​ reverses the linear gauge interactions consistently, without changing the masses.
A nonzero vacuum expectation value requires μ2<0. Minimizing the Higgs potential gives v2=−μ2/λ. By an gauge transformation choose
⟨ϕ⟩=2​1​(v0​),ϕ(x)=2​1​(v+h(x)0​)
(12)
in unitary gauge. The electroweak doublet gauge-boson mass matrix follows by inserting the vacuum expectation value in the gauge-covariant kinetic term:
Lmass​=8v2​[g2(Wμ1​W1μ+Wμ2​W2μ)+(gWμ3​−g′Bμ​)2].
(13)
Define the charged electroweak gauge bosons and the neutral rotation through the Weinberg angle by
Wμ±​=2​Wμ1​∓iWμ2​​,sW​=g2+g′2​g′​,cW​=g2+g′2​g​,
(14)
Zμ​=cW​Wμ3​−sW​Bμ​,Aμ​=sW​Wμ3​+cW​Bμ​,
(15)
with inverse Wμ3​=cW​Zμ​+sW​Aμ​ and Bμ​=−sW​Zμ​+cW​Aμ​. Then
Lmass​=mW2​Wμ+​W−μ+21​mZ2​Zμ​Zμ,mW​=2gv​,mZ​=2vg2+g′2​​,mA​=0.​
(16)
The factors differ because W+ and W− are conjugate fields whereas Z is real. The massless photon corresponds to the unbroken Lie algebra generator Q=T3​+Y, which annihilates ⟨ϕ⟩. Thus three of the four real gauge bosons acquire mass, with mW​=mZ​cW​. The three would-be Goldstone bosons provide their longitudinal polarizations; the remaining scalar h is the Higgs boson.
Introduce the left-handed lepton doublet L=(νeL​,eL​)T, with YL​=−1/2, and the right-handed singlet eR​, with YR​=−1. Use the chiral projectors PL​=(1−γ5)/2 and PR​=(1+γ5)/2 in the course's convention. The minimal Standard Model has no right-handed neutrino. The gauge-invariant fermion terms are
Lℓ​=Lˉiγμ(∂μ​+i2g​τaWμa​−i2g′​Bμ​)L+eˉR​iγμ(∂μ​−ig′Bμ​)eR​−(ye​LˉϕeR​+ye∗​eˉR​ϕ†L).
(17)
The doublet contraction in the Yukawa interaction is a singlet, and its total hypercharge is +1/2+1/2−1=0. A bare term eˉL​eR​ would fail electroweak gauge invariance. The gauge covariant derivatives above already give all the requested fermion-gauge couplings. In terms of mass eigenstates, e=gsW​=g′cW​ and they become
LCC​=−2​g​(Wμ+​νˉeL​γμeL​+Wμ−​eˉL​γμνeL​),Lem​=+eAμ​eˉγμe,
(18)
LZ​=−cW​g​Zμ​[21​νˉeL​γμνeL​+(−21​+sW2​)eˉL​γμeL​+sW2​eˉR​γμeR​].
(19)
Thus the weak charged current is chiral and the neutrino has zero electric charge. The gauge-invariant electron Yukawa mass follows from the Yukawa interaction after electroweak symmetry breaking:
−2​v+h​(ye​eˉL​eR​+ye∗​eˉR​eL​).
(20)
Rephase eR​ to make ye​ real and positive. It gives
me​=2​∣ye​∣v​,Le,h​=−me​eˉe−vme​​heˉe.​
(21)
The electron Dirac mass is therefore compatible with the original gauge symmetry through the Higgs mechanism. The neutrino remains massless in this minimal renormalizable lepton sector.

The strong-interaction matrix element between two spin-zero pseudoscalar mesons has only pμ and kμ available. The product of the two intrinsic parities is positive. An axial current would require a pseudovector constructed from these momenta, but an expression involving the Levi-Civita symbol needs three independent four-vectors and therefore vanishes. This is a consequence of parity conservation in the hadronic matrix element, not of parity conservation in the weak interaction. The vector current can have the two independent structures p+k and p−k. Its coefficients are Lorentz scalars; with p2=mK2​ and k2=mπ2​ fixed, their only varying invariant is s=q2. Hence
Hμ=⟨π+(k)∣uˉγμs∣Kˉ0(p)⟩=(p+k)μf+​(s)+qμf−​(s).
(22)
These are the pseudoscalar-to-pseudoscalar form factors. With relativistically normalized states they are dimensionless. The vanishing axial matrix element and this decomposition explain the two equalities separately.
Write q1​ and q2​ for the outgoing electron and antineutrino momenta. From the Fermi interaction, an invariant scattering amplitude, up to an irrelevant overall sign or phase, is
M=2​GF​Vus​​uˉ(q1​)γμ​(1−γ5)v(q2​)[(p+k)μf+​(s)+qμf−​(s)].
(23)
The CKM matrix element Vus​ multiplies the quark current in the convention of this interaction. Let ℓμ​=uˉ(q1​)γμ​(1−γ5)v(q2​). For massless leptons, the massless Dirac equation and chirality matrix anticommutation give
qμℓμ​=uˉ(q1​)(q1​+q2​)(1−γ5)v(q2​)=0.
(24)
In the second term move q2​ through the chiral projector before applying q2​v=0. Since p+k=2p−q, this transverse massless leptonic current gives
M=2​GF​Vus​f+​(s)pμℓμ​.​
(25)
The disappearance of f−​ uses the massless approximation; for a massive charged lepton its contraction is proportional to the lepton mass.
Use the fermion spin sum and the supplied gamma matrix trace identities. The symmetric part of the leptonic tensor is
Lμν​=8(q1μ​q2ν​+q1ν​q2μ​−gμν​q1​⋅q2​)+Lμνantisym​.
(26)
The Levi-Civita symbol term is antisymmetric and drops out when contracted with pμpν. Thus
∑spins​∣M∣2=16GF2​∣Vus​∣2∣f+​(s)∣2[2(p⋅q1​)(p⋅q2​)−mK2​(q1​⋅q2​)].
(27)
There is no initial-spin average because the kaon is spinless. For the integrated massless leptonic tensor, keep every factor of 2π explicit and define the unnormalized two-lepton Lorentz-invariant phase space
Iμν​=∫q10​d3q1​​q20​d3q2​​δ(4)(q−q1​−q2​)q1μ​q2ν​=3π​qμ​qν​+6π​gμν​s.
(28)
The leptons are massless, so qi0​=∣qi​∣. Its trace is gμνIμν​=πs. Therefore
2pμpνIμν​−mK2​gμνIμν​=32π​[(p⋅q)2−mK2​s].
(29)
The three Lorentz-invariant phase-space measures and their momentum delta function contribute 1/[8(2π)5], in addition to 1/(2mK​) in the decay rate. Combining them with the spin sum gives
Γ=48π4mK​GF2​∣Vus​∣2​∫k0d3k​[(p⋅q)2−mK2​q2]∣f+​(q2)∣2,A=48π4mK​GF2​∣Vus​∣2​.​
(30)
This massless semileptonic pseudoscalar decay rate uses a two-lepton integral over future-timelike q and the pion integral is restricted to the physically allowed region. The null endpoint follows by continuity. The coefficient has mass dimension −5, so the complete expression has mass dimension one, as a decay rate must in natural units.
In the kaon centre-of-momentum frame, put E=k0 and κ=∣k∣. Then
s=mK2​+mπ2​−2mK​E,(p⋅q)2−mK2​s=mK2​(E2−mπ2​)=mK2​κ2.
(31)
The dimensionally consistent Källén function is
λ(s,mK2​,mπ2​)=s2+mK4​+mπ4​−2smK2​−2smπ2​−2mK2​mπ2​,κ=2mK​λ​​.
(32)
The pion-only mass term must have fourth power: the second power printed in the PDF is dimensionally inconsistent. This repair also follows directly from squaring E=(mK2​+mπ2​−s)/(2mK​). Angular integration and the change of variable give
k0d3k​=4πEκ2​dκ=−mK​2πκ​ds=−mK2​πλ​​ds,
(33)
where the negative sign reverses the endpoints. Combining this with the bracket λ/4 yields
Γ=192π3mK3​GF2​∣Vus​∣2​∫0(mK​−mπ​)2​dsλ(s,mK2​,mπ2​)3/2∣f+​(s)∣2.​
(34)
Thus B=GF2​∣Vus​∣2/(192π3mK3​), a=0, b=(mK​−mπ​)2​. The lower limit is the minimum invariant mass of two massless leptons; at the upper limit the pion is at rest. The coefficient has mass dimension −7, while dsλ3/2 has dimension eight. The Källén function also shows why the differential decay rate vanishes at zero pion momentum.

At one loop, writing t=logμ makes the renormalization-group beta function dgi​/dt=bi​gi3​. For bi​>0 a positive running coupling grows towards high energies and decreases towards the infrared. Extrapolation of the one-loop expression gives a finite ultraviolet Landau pole; perturbation theory fails before it reaches that pole. For bi​<0, the coupling decreases towards high energies, giving asymptotic freedom, and grows towards the infrared. The formal infrared pole identifies a strong-coupling scale, where the weak-coupling approximation no longer applies. These conclusions concern the small-coupling branch; a pole in the perturbative solution does not establish a pole in the exact theory. If bi​=0, higher-order terms decide the running.
From αi​=gi2​/(4π),
dlogμdαi​​=8πbi​αi2​,dlogμdαi−1​​=−8πbi​.
(35)
Integrating the one-loop beta function gives
αi−1​(μ)=αi−1​(mZ​)−8πbi​logmZ​μ​,αi​(μ)=1−8πbi​αi​(mZ​)log(μ/mZ​)αi​(mZ​)​.​
(36)
These formulas retain the same particle content and neglect threshold corrections throughout the interval. For bi​>0, the formal pole is μ=mZ​exp[1/(8πbi​αi​(mZ​))]; for bi​<0 the analogous scale lies below mZ​.
For one-loop normalized hypercharge unification, the normalized hypercharge coupling is αYGUT​=(5/3)α1​, so its inverse and one-loop slope are (3/5)α1−1​ and (3/5)b1​. Define
U1​=53​α1−1​(mZ​),U2​=α2−1​(mZ​),U3​=α3−1​(mZ​),LG​=logmZ​MGUT​​.
(37)
Equality of the three normalized inverses at the unification scale gives
U1​−U2​=8π(53​b1​−b2​)LG​,U3​−U2​=8π(b3​−b2​)LG​.
(38)
Eliminating LG​ proves
α3−1​(mZ​)=α2−1​(mZ​)+(3/5)b1​−b2​b3​−b2​​[53​α1−1​(mZ​)−α2−1​(mZ​)].​
(39)
This expression assumes (3/5)b1​=b2​. If these slopes coincide, unification first requires U1​=U2​, and the displayed division is unavailable. A unification scale above mZ​ additionally requires the inferred LG​ to be positive. The relation is a consistency condition under the stated one-loop assumptions, not proof that the measured couplings unify without threshold effects.
For the two-loop running coupling, the claimed logarithmic asymptotic concerns the asymptotically free branch with β0​>0 and μ/Λ→∞. Set
y=a−1,c=β0​β1​​,L=logΛμ​.
(40)
The differential equation becomes dy/dL=β0​+β1​/y=β0​(1+c/y). Separating variables yields
y−clog∣y+c∣=β0​L+K.
(41)
A change of the strong-coupling scale Λ absorbs any additive constant K. Choose that scale so that K=−clogβ0​. On the large positive-y branch the exact implicit relation is then
y−clogβ0​y+c​=β0​L.
(42)
It first gives y∼β0​L. Substituting this back into the logarithm gives y=β0​L+clogL+o(1). To determine the error rather than assume it, write y=β0​L+clogL+r(L). Expansion of the exact implicit relation yields
r(L)=β0​Lc[clogL+c+r(L)]​+O(L2(logL)2​)=β0​c2​LlogL+1​+O(L2(logL)2​).
(43)
Consequently the mathematically correct two-loop asymptotic is
a−1(μ)=β0​L+β0​β1​​logL+O(LlogL​).​
(44)
More precisely, the next term is β12​(logL+1)/(β03​L)​. For β1​=0 this is not O(1/L): multiplying the remainder by L makes it grow as (β12​/β03​)logL. No fixed change of Λ can remove this term. Such a change adds a constant to L and changes only constant or 1/L contributions, rather than the coefficient of logL/L. Thus the two leading terms requested are correct, but the literal remainder printed in the PDF is too small. This is the two-loop inverse-coupling logarithmic remainder.
If β1​=0, the one-loop result y=β0​L is exact after choosing Λ. If β0​=0, the displayed expansion is undefined and the differential equation instead gives y2=2β1​logμ+constant. For β0​<0, a positive weak coupling does not approach zero at arbitrarily large μ along the branch used above. The asymptotic assumptions therefore matter as well as the remainder.
