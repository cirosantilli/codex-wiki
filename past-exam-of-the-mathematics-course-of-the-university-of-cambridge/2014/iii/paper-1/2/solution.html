A prime ideal p is minimal over I if I⊆p and no strictly smaller prime contains I. An associated prime of a module is an annihilator of an individual nonzero element which happens to be prime. For the quotient module this reads
AssR​(R/I)={AnnR​(aˉ):0=aˉ∈R/I, AnnR​(aˉ) prime},​
(6)
where AnnR​(aˉ)=(I:a)={r∈R:ra∈I}. This is the annihilator of an individual element, rather than necessarily the annihilator of the whole quotient.
Minimal primes exist because I is proper. First choose a maximal ideal containing I. Within the primes contained in it and containing I, an intersection of any decreasing chain is again prime. Indeed, if ab is in the intersection and a is absent from one member, then b belongs to that member and to every smaller member; it also belongs to every larger member. The intersection is still proper and contains I. Zorn's lemma, applied with reverse inclusion, produces a minimal prime. This proves existence of minimal primes over a proper ideal, even without the Noetherian hypothesis.
Now fix a minimal prime p and put A=R/I. The localization at a prime ideal Ap​ is nonzero and is a Noetherian local ring. By prime ideal correspondence for localization, its only prime is pRp​/IRp​. Write this maximal ideal as n. It is the nilradical; since it is finitely generated and each generator is nilpotent, some power of n is zero. Explicitly, if its generators have nilpotence exponents e1​,…,er​, every product of degree 1+∑i​(ei​−1) vanishes.
Choose the smallest s≥1 such that ns=0, and choose a nonzero element in ns−1; when n=0, choose 1. Its annihilator over Rp​ is exactly pRp​. Represent it as a/u with u∈/p. Multiplication by the unit u shows that a/1 has the same annihilator and remains nonzero.
Let p1​,…,pr​ generate p in R. For each i, the equality pi​a/1=0 supplies ui​∈/p such that ui​pi​aˉ=0 in A. Put u=∏i​ui​ and bˉ=uaˉ. Its localization is nonzero, so bˉ=0, and every element of p kills it. Conversely, an element outside p becomes a unit and cannot kill the nonzero element bˉ/1. Therefore
AnnR​(bˉ)=p,∅=MinR​(I)⊆AssR​(R/I).​
(7)
The key step in minimal primes are associated primes is clearing denominators for a finite generating set of p; that is where Noetherianity is used.
For an embedded associated prime, take R=k[x,y] and I=(x2,xy). Its radical is (x), so (x) is its unique minimal prime. The nonzero class of x satisfies
AnnR​(xˉ)=(I:x)=(x,y).
(8)
Indeed, rx∈x(x,y) is equivalent to r∈(x,y) by cancellation in the polynomial domain. Thus (x,y) is associated but is not minimal, since (x)⊊(x,y). For comparison, AnnR​(yˉ​)=(x), exhibiting the minimal associated prime as well.
