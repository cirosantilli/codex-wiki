Every element of Γ1​(p) has trace congruent to two. An effective elliptic element of order two or three has trace zero or ±1 in a lift, so neither is possible for p≥5. Hence r2​=r3​=0.
Represent a modular cusp by a primitive column (a,c), modulo sign. Its reduction is a nonzero vector in Fp2​ modulo sign, and the unipotent subgroup acts by (a,c)↦(a+bc,c). For c=0, the nonzero values of a give (p−1)/2 orbits. For c=0, a varies freely and c modulo sign gives another (p−1)/2 orbits. Thus there are p−1 modular cusps. The reduction classification is sufficient as well as necessary: completing two primitive columns to determinant-one matrices and adjusting the second columns by a translation makes congruent columns related by Γ(p).
