Use the three left-coset representatives I,S,ST for Γ0​(2)\SL2​(Z). If a nonzero weight-four cusp form h existed, its coset norm of a modular form
H=h(h∣4​S)(h∣4​ST)
(13)
would be a nonzero weight-twelve modular form for the full group: right multiplication permutes its factors. At infinity h has order at least one in q. The other two factors correspond to the zero modular cusp of cusp width two and each have order at least 1/2 in q. Thus H has order at least two there.
The ratio H/Δ is weight zero, holomorphic on the half-plane because Δ has no zeros there, and holomorphic at the modular cusp with value zero. It descends to a holomorphic function on the compact full modular curve. Such a function is constant, hence zero, contradicting H=0. Therefore
S4​(Γ0​(2))=0.​
(14)
Constant terms at the two modular cusps define a linear map M4​(Γ0​(2))→C2 whose kernel is this modular cusp space. It is injective, so the dimension is at most two. The forms E4​(τ) and E4​(2τ) are holomorphic weight-four forms for this group. For the second, the same conjugation used in 1(c) proves transformation, and E4​(2Sτ)∣ after the weight factor is 2−4E4​(τ/2), proving holomorphy at zero. Their constant terms are both one but their q coefficients are respectively 240 and zero, so they are independent. We obtain
M4​(Γ0​(2))=CE4​(τ)⊕CE4​(2τ),dimM4​(Γ0​(2))=2.​
(15)
This is the weight-four Eisenstein basis at level two.
