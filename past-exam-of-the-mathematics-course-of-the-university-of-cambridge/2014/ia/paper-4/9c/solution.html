First apply momentum conservation to the rocket and the infinitesimal amount of expelled gas. During dt, the exhaust mass is αdt and its inertial velocity, to first order, is v−u. With no dust, the momentum balance is
mv=(m−αdt)(v+dv)+αdt(v−u)+o(dt).
(38)
It gives the rocket equation mv˙=αu​, while m˙=−α.
For a dust-collecting rocket, the incoming dust mass is βvdt with zero initial momentum. The same balance has rocket mass m+(βv−α)dt after the interval, so
mv=(m+(βv−α)dt)(v+dv)+αdt(v−u)+o(dt).
(39)
Consequently
m˙=βv−α,mv˙=αu−βv2.​
(40)
The second term in the acceleration equation accounts for the momentum required to bring collected dust up to rocket speed.
Set λ=α/(βu)​ and V=λu, so αu=βV2. As long as the engines operate and 0≤v<V, the velocity increases and time can be eliminated:
dvdlogm​=V2−v2v−λ2u​=−2(V−v)λ−1​−2(V+v)λ+1​.
(41)
Integrating from v=0, m=m0​, gives
logm0​m​=2λ−1​logVV−v​−2λ+1​logVV+v​.
(42)
Thus the required mass–velocity relation is
m=λm0​u(λu+v)λ+1(λu−v)λ−1​​.​
(43)
If λ>1, this formula would give m→0 as v→V−. In this interval, m˙=β(v−λ2u)<0. A real rocket retains positive body mass even after its fuel is gone, and collected dust only increases that residual mass. It therefore cannot follow this branch all the way to zero mass: its fuel must run out while v<λu. Indeed the hypothetical time integral dt/dv=m/[β(V2−v2)] is integrable at V when λ>1, so even the formal zero-mass endpoint would occur at finite time.
If 0<λ<1, the same relation instead gives m→∞ as v→V−. The mass first decreases, reaches its minimum at v=λ2u, and then increases because dust arrives faster than fuel is expelled. The time integral now diverges at V: under indefinitely maintained thrust, v approaches λu asymptotically while stored dust grows without bound. At that limiting speed, thrust αu balances the dust-loading term βv2, and m˙→βuλ(1−λ)>0. This is the physical reason the total rocket mass no longer signals fuel exhaustion. A rocket with a finite initial fuel supply still runs out at finite time t=Mfuel​/α and before reaching V; increasing total mass does not create new fuel.
