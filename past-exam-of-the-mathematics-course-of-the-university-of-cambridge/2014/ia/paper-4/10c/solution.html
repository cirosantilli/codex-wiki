For any vector a, differentiating its components and the rotating basis gives the rotating-frame derivative formula
(dtda​)S​=(dtda​)S′​+ω×a.
(44)
Apply it twice to the position x, with constant angular velocity ω. The inertial acceleration is
aS​=x¨+2ω×x˙+ω×(ω×x),

where dots denote rotating-frame derivatives. Newton's second law therefore becomes
mx¨=F−2mω×x˙−mω×(ω×x).​
(46)
The last terms are the Coriolis force and the force corresponding to centrifugal acceleration.
For the particle on a uniformly rotating inclined plane, choose orthonormal vectors e1​ along the horizontal ξ axis and e2​ up the slope, and set e3​=e1​×e2​, the upward normal vector. Then ez​=sinθe2​+cosθe3​ and

−2ω×x˙=2ωη˙​cosθe1​−2ωξ˙​cosθe2​+2ωξ˙​sinθe3​,

−ω×(ω×x)=ω2ξe1​+ω2ηcos2θe2​−ω2ηsinθcosθe3​.
(49)
The normal rotating-frame acceleration is zero, so the normal reaction is not generally mgcosθ; it satisfies
N=m(gcosθ−2ωξ˙​sinθ+ω2ηsinθcosθ).
(50)
Projecting the same equation onto the two tangential axes gives exactly
ξ¨​=ω2ξ+2ωη˙​cosθ,η¨​=ω2ηcos2θ−2ωξ˙​cosθ−gsinθ.​
(51)
Multiply the equations by ξ˙​ and η˙​ and add. The Coriolis force terms cancel, as expected because this force does no work relative to the rotating frame. The normal reaction also does no work along the plane. The conserved quantity is rotating-frame kinetic energy plus gravitational and centrifugal potential energy:
E′=2m​(ξ˙​2+η˙​2)+mgηsinθ−2mω2​(ξ2+η2cos2θ)=constant.​

This need not be the conserved inertial energy, since the externally driven rotating plane can exchange energy with the particle.
