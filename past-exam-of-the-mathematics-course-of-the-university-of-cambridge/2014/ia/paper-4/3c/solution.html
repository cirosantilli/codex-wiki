In classical charged particle in a uniform magnetic field motion, the Lorentz force and Newton's second law give
mv˙=qv×B.
(8)
Assume B=0 and choose an orthonormal coordinate system with B=Bez​, B=∣B∣. Write Ω=qB/m, retaining the sign of the electric charge. The velocity equations are
v˙x​=Ωvy​,v˙y​=−Ωvx​,v˙z​=0.
(9)
Thus the parallel velocity is constant, and the perpendicular velocity rotates at constant angular frequency. Choosing the time origin and perpendicular axes conveniently gives
vx​=v⊥​cosΩt,vy​=−v⊥​sinΩt,vz​=v∥​.
(10)
Integrating the velocity, one obtains
x=xc​+Ωv⊥​​sinΩt,y=yc​+Ωv⊥​​cosΩt,z=z0​+v∥​t.
(11)
The first two coordinates describe a circle of radius mv⊥​/(∣q∣B), while the third advances uniformly. This is helical motion in a uniform magnetic field: the helix has axis parallel to B through the guiding centre. Its cyclotron frequency is
ωc​=m∣q∣B​​,
(12)
and its signed angular velocity vector about that axis is −qB/m. The minus sign follows from the ordering v×B in the Lorentz force. The helix degenerates to a circle when v∥​=0 and to a straight line when v⊥​=0. If q=0 or B=0, there is only straight-line motion. In a relativistic extension with fixed Lorentz factor γ, the frequency becomes ∣q∣B/(γm); the classical result above uses mv as momentum.
