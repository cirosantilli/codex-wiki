A real sequence (xn​) has limit of a sequence x when
∀ε>0 ∃N∈N ∀n≥N:∣xn​−x∣<ε.
(17)
A series ∑n≥1​xn​ is a convergent series with sum s when its partial sums SN​=∑n=1N​xn​ have limit of a sequence s.
For the first comparison test for series, either of the two allowed bounds implies 0<xn​≤an​+bn​. Hence
0<∑n=1N​xn​≤∑n=1∞​an​+∑n=1∞​bn​<∞.
(18)
The partial sums form a monotone bounded sequence, so they converge. The series ∑xn​ is convergent.
For the exponent 2, use 1/n2≤1/[n(n−1)]=1/(n−1)−1/n for n≥2. The resulting telescoping series bounds every partial sum by 2, proving that the series converges by the monotone bounded sequence property. To prove divergence of the harmonic series, group the terms with 2j<n≤2j+1: their sum is at least 2j/2j+1=1/2. Thus its partial sums are unbounded. If α≤1, then n−α≥n−1, so the comparison test for series proves
n≥1∑​n−2<∞,n≥1∑​n−α=∞(α≤1).​
(19)
For the weighted square assumption, the finite-dimensional Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives, for every N,
∑n=1N​xn​=∑n=1N​(nxn​)n1​≤(∑n=1N​n2xn2​)1/2(∑n=1N​n21​)1/2≤(∑n≥1​n2xn2​)1/2(∑n≥1​n−2)1/2<∞.
(20)
Again the positive partial sums form a monotone bounded sequence. This is the weighted square roots of a summable sequence argument, with an​=n2xn2​. The weighted square condition implies ∑xn​<∞.
The converse is false. Take xn​=n−3/2. Each block 2j≤n<2j+1 contributes at most 2j2−3j/2=2−j/2, and the resulting geometric series converges. But n2xn2​=1/n, whose harmonic series diverges. This proves the counterexample without assuming the full P-series criterion.
