Use N={1,2,…}. If a bijection f:N→N maps S onto the even natural numbers, its restriction is a bijection from S onto an infinite set. Moreover, its restriction to the complement of S maps onto the odd natural numbers. Both sets are therefore infinite.
Conversely, suppose that S and its complement are infinite. Applying the increasing enumeration of an infinite subset of natural numbers to each gives
S={s1​<s2​<⋯},N∖S={t1​<t2​<⋯}.
(29)
For completeness, successively choose the least unused member. The process never stops because the set is infinite, and it lists every member: an omitted member would have infinitely many selected natural numbers below it, which is impossible. Define
f(sj​)=2j,f(tj​)=2j−1.​
(30)
This is injective on each list, their images are disjoint, and together their images cover N. Thus it is a bijection and maps S onto 2N. Both infinitude conditions are necessary and sufficient. If a convention includes zero in N, index each list from zero and use 2j, 2j+1 instead.
