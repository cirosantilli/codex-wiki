The first coordinate of the vector triple product is

The other two coordinates follow by cyclically permuting the indices, proving the identity.
A point on the line has the form r=b+sm. Substituting in the plane equation gives s(m⋅n)=(a−b)⋅n. When m⋅n=0 the parameter, and therefore the intersection point, is unique. The vector triple product gives n×(b×m)=(n⋅m)b−(n⋅b)m, so the unique intersection is
r=b+m⋅n(a−b)⋅n​m=m⋅n(a⋅n)m+n×(b×m)​.​
(10)
For the parallel case m⋅n=0, the line is entirely in the plane if (b−a)⋅n=0, and otherwise has no intersection. As usual, a genuine line and plane require nonzero direction and normal vectors. This is the line-plane intersection criterion.
