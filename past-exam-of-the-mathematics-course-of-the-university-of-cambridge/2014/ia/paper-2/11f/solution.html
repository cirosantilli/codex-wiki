Values of a conditional expectation on zero-probability conditioning events do not affect the sum. An unrestricted signed g whose expectation is undefined is outside this assertion.
Write M(x)=E[N(x)]. The waiting time for two increments larger than 1/2 dominates N(x) for 0≤x≤1 and has mean 4, so M is finite. Conditioning on the first increment, one step is always consumed; if that increment is below x, the remaining independent sequence has the original law. Thus first-step analysis and the tower property of conditional expectation give
M(x)=1+∫0x​M(x−u)du=1+∫0x​M(s)ds.
(35)
This first makes M continuous and then gives M′=M, with M(0)=1. Hence the uniform-sum crossing time below one has
E[N(x)]=ex,0≤x≤1.​
(36)
An independent explanation uses the tail-sum formula: P(N(x)>n)=P(U1​+⋯+Un​≤x)=xn/n! for x≤1, since this region is an n-dimensional simplex inside the unit cube. Summing from n=0 gives ex. In particular the strict-crossing convention gives N(0)=1 almost surely and EN(1)=e.
