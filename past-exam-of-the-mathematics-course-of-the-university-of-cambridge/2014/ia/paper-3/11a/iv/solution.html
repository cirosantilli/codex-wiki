Rotational symmetry of the vector field means F(rn)=h(r)n: rotations fixing n force the field to be parallel to n, and rotations between directions make its magnitude depend only on r. Its flux integral over the radius-r sphere is 4πr2h(r). Applying the flux identity from part (i) to the ball, with the spherically symmetric source, gives
4πr2h(r)=4π∫0r​f(s)s2ds.
(51)
Therefore the origin-regular spherical Poisson flux law is
F(x)=∣x∣3x​∫0∣x∣​f(s)s2ds.​
(52)
Only source values at s≤∣x∣ occur. Changing the exterior source can change the scalar potential by an interior constant, but cannot change this radial gradient. Regularity on all of R3, inherited from the Poisson equation setting, matters: on a punctured domain alone an additional Cx/r3 would have zero divergence away from the origin and would not be fixed by the local source. It is excluded here by the origin regularity needed for the ball flux identity.
