OurBigBook.com Words: 127
Under a proper rotation matrix R, invariance of a Cartesian second-rank tensor reads T=RTRT, equivalently TR=RT. The half-turn Rz​(π)=diag(−1,−1,1) forces all entries mixing the z direction with the xy plane to vanish. Thus T consists of a planar 2×2 block B and the entry T33​=c.
Commutation with the planar quarter-turn J=(01​−10​) gives B=aI+bJ. This block already commutes with every planar rotation. Now use the half-turn Rx​(π)=diag(1,−1,−1): on the planar block its conjugation fixes I and sends J to −J, so invariance forces b=0. Therefore
T=diag(a,a,c),tij​=αδij​+βδi3​δj3​,α=a, β=c−a.​
(54)
The formula uses the Kronecker delta and is sufficient as well as necessary: rotations preserving the unoriented z-axis fix both I and e3​e3T​. This is an axially invariant second-rank tensor with the planar antisymmetric part removed by the horizontal half-turn.
