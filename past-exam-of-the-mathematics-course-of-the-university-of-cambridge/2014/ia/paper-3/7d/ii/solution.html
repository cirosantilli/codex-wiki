The Möbius transformations are bijections, so they preserve which coordinates of a triple are equal. Conversely, part (i) implies transitivity within each equality pattern. For a pattern with fewer than three distinct entries, extend the chosen distinct points to a triple before applying part (i); this works also for p=2, when the projective line has exactly three points. There are therefore five orbits of a group action.
We first count ∣GL2​(Fp​)∣. Its first column can be any nonzero vector, giving p2−1 choices. The second must be outside the first column's one-dimensional span, giving p2−p choices. Hence
∣G∣=(p2−1)(p2−p)=p(p−1)2(p+1).

For the all-equal pattern take (∞,∞,∞). Its stabilizer subgroup consists of upper triangular matrices

of order p(p−1)2. Its group orbit has order p+1.
The three exactly-two-equal patterns have representatives (∞,∞,0), (∞,0,∞) and (0,∞,∞). Each stabilizer subgroup fixes 0 and ∞ individually, so it is the diagonal subgroup of order (p−1)2. Each group orbit has order p(p+1); their repeated-coordinate positions keep these three orbits distinct.
For the all-distinct pattern use (0,1,∞). Its stabilizer subgroup is the scalar subgroup of order p−1, and the group orbit has order (p+1)p(p−1).
