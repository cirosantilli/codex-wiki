Substitute y(x)=∑j≥0​cj​xj into the Legendre differential equation. Matching the coefficient of xj gives
cj+2​=(j+2)(j+1)j(j+1)−n(n+1)​cj​.​
(57)
Choose the parity of n: start with nonzero c0​ for even n, or nonzero c1​ for odd n, and set the other parity to zero. The numerator is nonzero for every preceding index of that parity below n, so cn​=0, but at j=n it vanishes. All higher coefficients are zero. This constructs a nonzero polynomial solution of degree exactly n.
Its value at x=1 cannot be zero. If it had a zero there of multiplicity k≥1, write y=(x−1)kR with R(1)=0. The lowest-order term of (1−x2)y′′−2xy′ would be −2k2R(1)(x−1)k−1, whereas n(n+1)y starts at order k. The differential equation could not hold. Hence the polynomial can be scaled to satisfy Pn​(1)=1.
The resulting Legendre polynomials begin
P0​(x)=1,P1​(x)=x,P2​(x)=21​(3x2−1).​
(58)
Each satisfies both the differential equation and the specified normalization.
