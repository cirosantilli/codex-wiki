For a linear map T:V→W with V finite-dimensional, the rank-nullity theorem states
dimV=dimkerT+dimimT.​
(1)
To prove it, choose a basis v1​,…,vk​ of the kernel of a linear map and use basis extension to obtain v1​,…,vn​ as a basis of V. The vectors Tvk+1​,…,Tvn​ span the image of a linear map: applying T to a basis expansion kills the first k terms. They are also linearly independent. Indeed, if ∑j>k​aj​Tvj​=0, then ∑j>k​aj​vj​∈kerT, so it is a combination of v1​,…,vk​. Independence of the full basis forces every aj​=0. Consequently dimimT=n−k, proving the theorem.
Here the image of a linear map has dimension r∈{0,1,2,3}, since it lies in R3. Thus
dimkerα∈{2,3,4,5}.​
(2)
Every possibility occurs: for 0≤r≤3, map (x1​,…,x5​) to (x1​,…,xr​,0,…,0)∈R3. This map has rank r and kernel dimension 5−r.
