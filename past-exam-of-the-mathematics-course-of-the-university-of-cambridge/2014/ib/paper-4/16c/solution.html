Along a sufficiently smooth stationary curve, differentiation and the Euler-Lagrange equation give the Beltrami identity:
dxd​(f−y′fy′​)=fy​y′−y′dxd​fy′​=0,f−y′fy′​=constant.​
(49)
The cancellation uses the absence of explicit x dependence.
For an axisymmetric surface of revolution, its area is I[y]=2π∫−ll​y1+y′2​dx. A smooth positive-radius minimizer is stationary, so the Beltrami identity, with the constant factor omitted, gives
1+y′2​y​=k>0.
(50)
Equivalently the Euler-Lagrange equation gives yy′′=1+y′2, whence y′′=y/k2. The first integral selects
y=kcosh((x−x∗​)/k).
(51)
Equal radii at ±l imply x∗​=0 (subtract the two cosh values), giving
y(x)=kcosh(x/k),r=kcosh(l/k).​
(52)
This is a catenoid. The conclusion is a necessary form for a smooth connected minimizer; it does not declare every stationary catenoid a minimum.
Put a=l/k>0. The boundary equation is r/l=cosha/a. Its derivative is (asinha−cosha)/a2, so its unique critical point solves atanha=1. The left side increases strictly from zero to infinity. Thus cosha/a decreases to a unique minimum and then increases, diverging as a↓0 and a→∞. There are exactly two positive values of k when
r/l>mina>0​acosha​≃1.50888.
(53)
At equality there is one value, and below it there is none.
To match the printed second variation convention, write I[y+εη]=I[y]+εδI+ε2δ2I+o(ε2); its δ2I is the quadratic coefficient, half the second derivative with respect to ε. Expanding the area integrand gives
δ2I=π∫−ll​[(1+y′2)3/2y​η′2+21+y′2​y′​ηη′]dx.
(54)
On the catenoid these coefficients are ksech2(x/k) and 2tanh(x/k). Integrate the mixed term by parts; the boundary term is zero because η(±l)=0. Since (tanh(x/k))′=sech2(x/k)/k, this yields
δ2I=π∫−ll​(kη′2−kη2​)sech2(x/k)dx.​
(55)
This is the axisymmetric second variation of a catenoid, with the explicitly stated quadratic-coefficient convention.
