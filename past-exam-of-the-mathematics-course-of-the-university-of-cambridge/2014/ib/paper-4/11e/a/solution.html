With the usual commutative, unital conventions, the chain is
field ⟹ principal ideal domain ⟹ unique factorization domain ⟹ integral domain.​
(31)
A field is an integral domain, and every nonzero ideal contains an invertible element and is consequently the whole ring. Its only ideals are (0) and (1), so it is a principal ideal domain.
To prove the middle implication, first show that a principal ideal domain has the ascending chain condition on ideals. The union of an ascending chain is an ideal, hence is (a); its generator belongs to one member of the chain, and all later members equal (a). Now suppose a nonzero nonunit admitted no factorization into irreducible elements. It would factor into two nonunits, at least one of which again has no such factorization. Repeating would produce a strictly increasing chain of principal ideals: if a=bc with c a nonunit, (a)⊊(b), since equality and cancellation would make c invertible. This contradicts the ascending chain condition. Existence of factorization follows. By the permitted fact that every irreducible element is a prime element in a principal ideal domain, any irreducible factor in one factorization divides a factor in the other; cancellation then proves uniqueness up to units and order. This is a unique factorization domain. The final implication is part of the definition of a unique factorization domain.
None of the adjacent implications reverses. The principal ideal domain Z is not a field, since 2 is not invertible. The polynomial ring Q[x,y] is a unique factorization domain, by applying the polynomial extension theorem for unique factorization domains twice, but its ideal (x,y) is not principal: a generator would divide both x and y, so would be a unit, whereas every member of this ideal vanishes at the origin. Finally, Z[−5​] is an integral domain as a subring of C, but is not a unique factorization domain. Its multiplicative norm is N(a+b−5​)=a2+5b2. There is no element of norm 2 or 3. Hence 2,3,1+−5​,1−−5​, of norms 4,9,6,6, are all irreducible: a nontrivial factorization would require one of those missing norms. The only units have norm one and are ±1. The two factorizations
6=2⋅3=(1+−5​)(1−−5​)
(32)
are therefore not related by associates and reordering.
