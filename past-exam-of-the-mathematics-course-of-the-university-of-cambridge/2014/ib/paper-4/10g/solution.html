The algebraic dual space is V∗=HomR​(V,R), the vector space of all real linear functionals. For a finite basis e1​,…,en​, define the dual basis by ei∗​(∑j​aj​ej​)=ai​, so ei∗​(ej​)=δij​. Every linear functional satisfies
λ=∑i=1n​λ(ei​)ei∗​.
(27)
This equality follows by evaluating both sides on each vector of the basis. It proves spanning directly. If ∑i​bi​ei∗​=0, evaluation on ej​ gives bj​=0, proving linear independence without assuming a theorem on dual dimensions.
For a vector subspace U, its annihilator of a vector subspace is
U∘={λ∈V∗:λ(u)=0 for all u∈U}.
(28)
Extend a basis e1​,…,er​ of U to a basis of V. Then U∘ has basis er+1∗​,…,en∗​, so dimU∘=n−dimU​.
The dual map of α:V→W is α∗:W∗→V∗, λ↦λ∘α. In the corresponding dual bases its matrix is the transpose of the matrix for α. A matrix and its transpose have the same rank, giving rankα∗=rankα. Directly,
α∗λ=0 ⟺ λ(αv)=0 for all v ⟺ λ∈(imα)∘.
(29)
Thus kerα∗=(imα)∘​. Also every λ∘α vanishes on kerα, so imα∗⊆(kerα)∘. Both spaces have dimension rankα by the preceding annihilator of a vector subspace formula and rank-nullity theorem, proving
imα∗=(kerα)∘.​
(30)
Alternatively, a linear functional vanishing on the kernel descends to the image of α and can be extended to W by extending a basis.
For the infinite-dimensional polynomial ring, the coefficient functionals Li​ are linearly independent: evaluate a finite relation on each monomial. However, they do not span the algebraic dual space. The linear functional p↦p(1) takes value one on every monomial, while any finite combination of the Li​ vanishes on every sufficiently high-degree monomial. Thus the Li​ do not form a basis of V∗. An infinite formal sum of coefficient functionals is not a finite linear combination.
