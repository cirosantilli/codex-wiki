For any distinct nodes, the polynomial q(x)=∏j=1ν​(x−cj​)2 has degree 2ν, is nonnegative, and vanishes at every node. The quadrature rule sum for q is zero, but ∫0π​wqdx>0 because the weight is positive and q is positive except at finitely many points. Therefore no such rule is exact for all polynomials of degree at most 2ν, irrespective of the chosen weights.

To prove cubic exactness, divide any cubic as f=ap2​+r, with a,r of degree at most one. The integral of ap2​ is zero by orthogonality, and its nodal values vanish; the remaining linear polynomial is integrated exactly. This is the two-node Gaussian quadrature with sine weight; the argument proves its Gaussian quadrature property without assuming a general quadrature theorem.
