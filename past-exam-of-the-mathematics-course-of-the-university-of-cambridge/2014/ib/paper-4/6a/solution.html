For a normalized state and a self-adjoint operator Q with finite second moment, the quantum uncertainty is defined by ΔQ=∥(Q−⟨Q⟩)ψ∥. Since ⟨Q⟩ is real,
(ΔQ)2=⟨(Q−⟨Q⟩)2⟩=⟨Q2⟩−⟨Q⟩2​.
(14)
In an energy eigenstate of the quantum harmonic oscillator,
E=⟨H⟩=2m(Δp)2+⟨p⟩2​+2mω2​((Δx)2+⟨x⟩2).
(15)
Discarding the nonnegative squared means proves the stated lower bound. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle is ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2, following from [x,p]=iℏ. Applying the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality to the two variance contributions gives
E≥2m(Δp)2​+2mω2(Δx)2​≥ωΔpΔx≥2ℏω​​.
(16)
Here ω>0 is the oscillator frequency. The bound is the ground-state energy; equality requires zero means and the appropriate minimum-uncertainty width.
