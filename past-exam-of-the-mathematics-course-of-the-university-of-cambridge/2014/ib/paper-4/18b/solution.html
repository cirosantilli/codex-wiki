Apply mass conservation to a fixed horizontal region D. The layer mass is ρ∫D​hdxdy and the outward horizontal flux is ρ∫∂D​h(u,v)⋅nds. The divergence theorem and arbitrary choice of D give
ht​+(hu)x​+(hv)y​=0.​
(64)
This depth-integrated law retains free-surface motion; neglecting the vertical velocity in horizontal momentum is not the assumption that the depth cannot change.
Take the free-surface pressure to be constant. hydrostatic pressure gives p=patm​+ρg(h−z), so the horizontal pressure forces are −gηx​,−gηy​. Linearization about a resting layer gives the linearized shallow water equations
ut​−fv=−gηx​,vt​+fu=−gηy​,ηt​+h0​(ux​+vy​)=0.
(65)
Taking the curl of the momentum equations yields ζt​=−f(ux​+vy​). Combining with continuity gives the linearized shallow-water potential-vorticity anomaly
∂t​(ζ−h0​f​η)=0,Q=Q0​(x,y).​
(66)
For Q0​=0, ζ=fη/h0​. Taking the divergence of momentum gives ∂t​(ux​+vy​)−fζ=−g∇h2​η. Eliminate the divergence with continuity to obtain
ηtt​−gh0​∇h2​η+f2η=0.​
(67)
For θ=k(x−ct), substitution of η=η0​cosθ gives the inertia-gravity wave dispersion relation
ω2=gh0​k2+f2,ω=kc,c2=gh0​+f2/k2.​
(68)
The associated purely oscillatory velocity field is
u=Ucosθ,v=Vsinθ,U=h0​cη0​​,V=kh0​fη0​​=ωf​U.
(69)
These formulas satisfy all three linearized equations and the zero-anomaly condition. A separately added spatially uniform inertial oscillation is not part of this monochromatic wave.
To first order in amplitude evaluate the wave at a particle's equilibrium coordinate x∗​, so θ∗​=kx∗​−ωt. Integrating its velocity gives displacements relative to the orbit centre
ξ=−ωU​sinθ∗​,υ=ωV​cosθ∗​,(U/ω)2ξ2​+(V/ω)2υ2​=1.​
(70)
These are particle ellipses for rotating shallow-water waves, with axis ratio V/U=f/ω<1. For the stipulated positive signs, the velocity at phases 0,π/2,π,3π/2 points respectively right, up, left, down, with magnitudes U,V,U,V. The particle orbits are clockwise as time advances, since the phase decreases.
