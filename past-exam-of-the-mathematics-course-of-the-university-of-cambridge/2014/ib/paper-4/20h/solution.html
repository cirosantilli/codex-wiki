On a directed flow network with capacities ce​, choose flows 0≤fe​≤ce​ satisfying inflow equals outflow at every vertex except source s and sink t, and maximize the net source outflow ∣f∣. For a cut of a flow network S containing s but not t, its capacity is ∑u∈S,v∈/S​cuv​. The max-flow min-cut theorem states
fmax​∣f∣=s∈S, t∈/Smin​c(S,Sc).​
(78)
Every feasible flow is bounded above by every cut capacity; equality for a feasible flow and a cut proves optimality.
The Ford-Fulkerson algorithm starts with a feasible flow, for instance zero. A forward edge has residual capacity cuv​−fuv​ and its reverse edge has residual capacity fuv​. Find a source-to-sink augmenting path in this residual network, and increase the flow along it by the minimum residual capacity on the path, reducing original-edge flows when reverse edges are used. Repeat until no such path exists. The vertices reachable from the source then define a saturated minimum cut of a flow network. With integer capacities and zero initial flow, all residual capacities and augmentations are integers. Each augmentation increases ∣f∣ by at least one, while ∣f∣ is bounded by the finite sum of source capacities, so termination takes finitely many steps.

All road capacities are respected, and direct summation verifies flow conservation at each roundabout. The original flow value is 90. The cut separating B from every other vertex consists of its two incoming roads and has capacity 40+50=90. Therefore the original maximum is 90 vehicles per minute.
To impose a vertex capacity, split R2​ into R2−​ and R2+​. Send its incoming roads to R2−​ and its outgoing roads from R2+​, and add a directed internal edge R2−​→R2+​ of capacity 20. Every unit passing through the roundabout must traverse this internal edge. This gives the standard maximum flow with vertex capacities construction.
Starting from zero, apply the Ford-Fulkerson algorithm with the following augmenting paths in order. First use A→R1​→R3​→B: its residual capacities are 60,10,40, so augment by 10. Next use A→R4​→R5​→B: the residual capacities are 50,30,50, so augment by 30. Finally use A→R1​→R2−​→R2+​→R3​→B: the residual capacities at this stage are 50,30,20,40,30, so augment by 20. No reverse edge is needed in this valid run. The resulting original-road flows are precisely the after-storm column above, and the internal edge carries 20.
For termination and optimality, take the source-side set {A,R1​,R4​,R2−​}. Its outgoing edges are R1​→R3​, R4​→R5​ and R2−​→R2+​, of capacities 10,30,20. They are all saturated, and no positive-flow original edge enters this set. Consequently no residual path leaves the set, and its cut capacity equals the constructed flow value. The new maximum is therefore
60 vehicles per minute.​
