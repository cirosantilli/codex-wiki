For a linear map T:V→W with V finite-dimensional, the rank-nullity theorem states
dimV=dimkerT+dimimT.​
(1)
To prove it, choose a basis v1​,…,vk​ of the kernel of a linear map and use basis extension to obtain v1​,…,vn​ as a basis of V. The vectors Tvk+1​,…,Tvn​ span the image of a linear map: applying T to a basis expansion kills the first k terms. They are also linearly independent. Indeed, if ∑j>k​aj​Tvj​=0, then ∑j>k​aj​vj​∈kerT, so it is a combination of v1​,…,vk​. Independence of the full basis forces every aj​=0. Consequently dimimT=n−k, proving the theorem.
Here the image of a linear map has dimension r∈{0,1,2,3}, since it lies in R3. Thus
dimkerα∈{2,3,4,5}.​
(2)
Every possibility occurs: for 0≤r≤3, map (x1​,…,x5​) to (x1​,…,xr​,0,…,0)∈R3. This map has rank r and kernel dimension 5−r.

An even permutation in the alternating group A6​ has one of the cycle types listed below. In the symmetric group, a type with mj​ cycles of length j has centralizer order ∏j​jmj​mj​! and class size 6!/∏j​jmj​mj​!: commuting permutations can rotate each cycle and exchange equal-length cycles.
An S6​ conjugacy class remains one A6​ class if its centralizer contains an odd permutation. Otherwise it splits into two equal classes, since the centralizer is already contained in the index-two subgroup A6​. This is the alternating conjugacy class splitting criterion. For the nonsplitting nonidentity types here, odd commuting permutations are respectively a constituent transposition, a transposition of fixed points, the interchange of two 3-cycles, and the constituent 4-cycle. For a 5-cycle with one fixed point, the centralizer is its cyclic group of order five and contains only even permutations.

A function f:E→R has uniform continuity if for every ε>0 there is a δ>0 such that, for all x,y∈E, ∣x−y∣<δ implies ∣f(x)−f(y)∣<ε. The same δ must work everywhere in E.
The product need not be uniformly continuous. Both f(x)=x and g(x)=x are Lipschitz continuous, hence uniformly continuous on R, but their product is x2. For xn​=n and yn​=n+1/n, the input separation tends to zero while
∣yn2​−xn2​∣=2+n21​≥2.
(5)
This contradicts uniform continuity. The issue is captured by products of unbounded uniformly continuous functions; bounded factors would give a uniform product estimate.
A uniformly continuous function on (0,1) is bounded. Take δ for output tolerance one and cover (0,1) by finitely many intervals of radius less than δ, centred at points c1​,…,cm​ in (0,1). Every x is within δ of some cj​, so ∣f(x)∣≤1+maxj​∣f(cj​)∣. This proves the general principle that a uniformly continuous function on a totally bounded set is bounded.
The reciprocal-cosine function is not uniformly continuous. Take
xn​=2πn1​,yn​=(2n+1)π1​.
(6)
Both sequences lie in (0,1), ∣xn​−yn​∣→0, but the two function values are 1 and −1. Their difference is always two, violating the sequential criterion for uniform continuity.

Define the determinant by the signed permutation sum
detA=∑σ∈Sn​​sgn(σ)∏i=1n​ai,σ(i)​.
(25)
It is multilinear and alternating in the rows, and likewise in the columns. Row multilinearity follows because every product contains exactly one entry from each row; a swap changes the permutation sign, and equal rows give cancellation in pairs. Applying the same argument to the transpose proves the column properties.
Consequently, for either rows or columns, a swap multiplies the determinant by −1; multiplication of one row or column by c multiplies it by c; addition of a multiple of another row or column leaves it unchanged. For the last rule, multilinearity separates the original determinant and a determinant with two equal rows or columns, which is zero. These arguments also cover singular matrices and zero scaling.

Let D be a finite integral domain. For any a=0, multiplication ma​:D→D is injective: ax=ay implies a(x−y)=0, hence x=y because there are no zero divisors. When the shortest angular separation is zero, the fastest path is the meridian ϕ=constant; it is a vertical straight line on the cylinder and cannot be represented by finite A,B as a graph over ϕ. The poles correspond to z=±∞ and cannot be reached in finite time with this speed law.
