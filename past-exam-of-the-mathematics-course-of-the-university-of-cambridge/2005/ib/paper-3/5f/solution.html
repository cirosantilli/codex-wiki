A real harmonic function on a planar open set is a twice continuously differentiable function satisfying fxx​+fyy​=0. The ordered pair (f,g) consists of harmonic conjugates when f+ig is holomorphic; the corresponding Cauchy-Riemann equations are fx​=gy​ and fy​=−gx​.
To prove composition of harmonic conjugate pairs, denote P=p(u,v) and Q=q(u,v), and assume the inner pair's image lies in the outer pair's domain. Their equations are ux​=vy​, uy​=−vx​, pu​=qv​ and pv​=−qu​. The chain rule gives
Px​=pu​ux​+pv​vx​=pu​ux​−qu​vx​=Qy​,
(10)
Py​=pu​uy​+pv​vy​=−pu​vx​−qu​ux​=−Qx​.
(11)
Thus the composite satisfies the Cauchy-Riemann equations. Its components are C2, and differentiating gives Pxx​+Pyy​=Qyx​−Qxy​=0 and similarly Qxx​+Qyy​=0. The composite pair is harmonic conjugate, including when the inner map has a critical point.
