Project from the north pole N=(0,0,1) along the line through a sphere point onto the equatorial plane Z=0, identified with C. Parameterizing this line gives
π(X,Y,Z)=1−ZX+iY​,π(N)=∞​.
(25)
The inverse stereographic projection is
(X,Y,Z)=1+∣z∣2(2Rez,2Imz,∣z∣2−1)​.
(26)
Negating the three sphere coordinates consequently sends z to −1/zˉ, with 0,∞ interchanged. This proves the antipodal stereographic coordinate relation.
A nonidentity Möbius transformation T(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d) has ad−bc=0. For c=0, its fixed points are the roots of cz2+(d−a)z−b=0, giving one or two distinct points. If c=0 and a=d, there is one finite root and the fixed point infinity. If c=0, a=d, and b=0, it is a translation and only infinity is fixed. The remaining case is the identity, already excluded. Thus the claim includes fixed points at infinity, not just finite solutions of the quadratic.
A nontrivial rotation of the sphere fixes exactly the two poles on its axis, which are antipodal. It therefore gives exactly two fixed stereographic points with z2​=−1/zˉ1​. Here the nonzero rotation angle is understood modulo 2π; an angle that is a whole multiple of 2π would be the identity.
Conversely, use a sphere rotation whose Möbius map S sends the two antipodal fixed points to 0,∞. The conjugate STS−1 fixes both, hence equals z↦az for some a=0. If ∣a∣=1, write a=eiθ; the inverse stereographic formula shows that this rotates X+iY while leaving Z fixed. Conjugating back by the rotation S gives a sphere rotation. If ∣a∣<1, anz→0 for every finite z. If ∣a∣>1, anz→∞ for every z=0, in the sphere topology. Pulling these conclusions back proves the antipodal fixed-point classification of Möbius transformations: either the map is a sphere rotation or one fixed point attracts every point except the other fixed point.
