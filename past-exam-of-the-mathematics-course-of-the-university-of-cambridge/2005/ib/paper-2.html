A group automorphism is a bijective group homomorphism from G to itself. The multiplication in Aut(G) is composition: (αβ)(g)=α(β(g)). Composition is associative, the identity map is the identity element, and each inverse bijection is again a homomorphism, so these maps form the automorphism group.
For conjugation by h, multiplication is preserved because
h(g1​g2​)h−1=(hg1​h−1)(hg2​h−1).
(6)
Conjugation by h−1 is its inverse, so ψ(h) is an group automorphism. Moreover
ψ(h1​h2​)(g)=h1​h2​gh2−1​h1−1​=ψ(h1​)(ψ(h2​)(g)).
(7)
Hence ψ:G→Aut(G) is a homomorphism, with image the inner automorphisms. Its group homomorphism kernel is the group center of G, since ψ(h) is the identity precisely when hg=gh for every g.

A function f:I→R is uniformly continuous if
∀ε>0 ∃δ>0 ∀x,y∈I:∣x−y∣<δ⟹∣f(x)−f(y)∣<ε.
(8)
The same δ must work at every point of I.
Every uniformly continuous function on (0,1) is bounded. Choose the tolerance δ for ε=1 and an integer N with 1/N<δ. The finitely many centers cj​=(j+1/2)/N, 0≤j<N, cover the interval by cells of radius 1/(2N). For each x, one center satisfies ∣f(x)−f(cj​)∣<1. Thus ∣f(x)∣≤1+maxj​∣f(cj​)∣. This is the uniformly continuous function on a totally bounded set is bounded argument; closedness of the interval is unnecessary.
The reciprocal function is not uniformly continuous on (0,1). It is unbounded, already contradicting the preceding result. Directly, xn​=1/(n+1) and yn​=1/(n+2) have distance tending to zero while ∣1/xn​−1/yn​∣=1.
The oscillating function sin(1/x) is not uniformly continuous either, despite being bounded. Take
xn​=2πn+π/21​,yn​=2πn+3π/21​.
(9)
Their distance tends to zero, but their function values are 1 and −1. The fixed output gap of two contradicts the uniform-continuity condition, for example with ε=1.

Suppose the union W has a separation W=A∪B into disjoint, nonempty, relatively open subsets. Each connected Uj​ must lie entirely in one of them: otherwise its intersections with A and B would separate Uj​ in its subspace topology. In particular U1​ lies on one side, say A. Every other Uj​ intersects U1​, so it cannot lie in B and must also lie in A. Then W⊆A, contradicting B=∅. The union is connected. This is the connected union with a connected hub argument.
For path-connectedness, take any x∈Ui​ and y∈Uj​. Choose a∈Ui​∩U1​ and b∈Uj​∩U1​. There is a path from x to a inside Ui​, a path from a to b inside U1​, and a path from b to y inside Uj​. Concatenate the three paths on successive thirds of [0,1]. They agree at the joining points, so the resulting map is continuous and remains in W. The union is path-connected. There need not be a single point common to all the subsets.

In a finite two-person zero-sum game the row player chooses i, the column player chooses j, and the row player receives aij​ while the column player receives −aij​. The row player maximizes that payoff and the column player minimizes it. Mixed strategies are probability vectors x∈Rm and y∈Rn, giving expected row payoff xTAy.
Against a fixed row mixture, the worst column is a pure column, so the row problem introduces its guaranteed payoff v:
x,vmax​v:x≥0,1Tx=1,ATx≥v1.​
(23)
Similarly the column player minimizes an upper guarantee w:
y,wmin​w:y≥0,1Ty=1,Ay≤w1.​
(24)
The values v,w are unrestricted real variables; no positivity shift of the matrix is required.
To see the linear programming duality explicitly, introduce nonnegative multipliers y for the first problem's payoff inequalities and multiplier w for its normalization. Its upper-bound Lagrangian is
L=v+yT(ATx−v1)+w(1−1Tx)=w+v(1−1Ty)+xT(Ay−w1).
(25)
The supremum over free v and nonnegative x is finite precisely when 1Ty=1 and Ay≤w1, and is then w. Minimizing it gives the displayed column problem, proving that the programs are a dual pair.
A sufficient optimality certificate is a pair of probability vectors and a common value v∗​ satisfying
Ay≤v∗​1,ATx≥v∗​1.​
(26)
Equivalently, both programs are feasible with equal values. Positive-probability rows then have (Ay)i​=v∗​ and positive-probability columns have (ATx)j​=v∗​, the complementary slackness conditions. These conditions certify a saddle point and hence optimal mixed strategies for both players.
