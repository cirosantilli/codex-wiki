The image is a ring torus: a circle of radius b is rotated about the vertical axis at distance a from that axis. Put r=x2+y2​. Since a>b, every image point has r>0, and the image is precisely
T={(x,y,z):(r−a)2+z2=b2}.
(2)
Conversely, on this level set the pairs ((r−a)/b,z/b) and (x/r,y/r) are unit vectors, so choosing their angles gives a preimage.
The specified angular rectangle removes two closed circles from the torus. More explicitly its image is
U=T∖(Cu​∪Cv​),Cu​={z=0, r=a+b}∩T,Cv​={y=0, x>0}∩T.
(3)
Here Cu​ is the outer equatorial circle and Cv​ is a meridian. The given openness property makes U open in T. On U, the angles of (r−a)+iz and x+iy are uniquely chosen in (0,2π); their continuous argument branches give the inverse (x,y,z)↦(u,v). Thus the restriction is a homeomorphism onto U, and the inverse is locally smooth.
For the immersion condition, the two derivatives have inner products
σu​⋅σu​=b2,σv​⋅σv​=(a+bcosu)2,σu​⋅σv​=0.
(4)
They are linearly independent everywhere, since a+bcosu≥a−b>0. Hence this is a smooth embedded surface parametrization.
The angular cuts do not represent singularities of the embedded torus of revolution. To give a direct proof covering all points, define F=(x2+y2​−a)2+z2−b2 on the open region r>0. On T,
∇F=2((r−a)rx​,(r−a)ry​,z),∣∇F∣=2b>0.
(5)
The implicit function theorem therefore describes T near every point as a smooth graph of two coordinates. The whole torus is a smooth embedded surface.
