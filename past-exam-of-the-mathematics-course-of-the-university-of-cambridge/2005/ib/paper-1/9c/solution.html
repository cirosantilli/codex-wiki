Let dimV=n and dimW=k. Extend a basis w1​,…,wk​ of the vector subspace W to w1​,…,wn​ of V, and let f1​,…,fn​ be its dual basis. Every linear functional is uniquely ∑i​ci​fi​. It vanishes on W exactly when c1​=⋯=ck​=0. Thus the annihilator of a vector subspace has basis fk+1​,…,fn​, and
dimα(W)=n−k​.
(29)
To apply this to a matrix, regard its rows as elements of (Rn)∗ and let R be their linear span. Elementary invertible row and column operations reduce a rank-r matrix to a block identity diag(Ir​,0), showing that dimR=r. The equations require that every functional in R vanish on x. The canonical identification Rn≅((Rn)∗)∗ sends x to evaluation f↦f(x): it is injective because coordinate functionals separate points, and is bijective by equal dimensions. Consequently the solution vector space is the annihilator of R under this identification. The dimension formula just proved gives
dimkerA=n−r​.
(30)
A basis of this kernel consists of exactly n−r linearly independent solutions, and every solution is their linear combination.
