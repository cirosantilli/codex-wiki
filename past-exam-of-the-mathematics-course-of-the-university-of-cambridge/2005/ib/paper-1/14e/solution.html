This is a Poisson equation on a disk with angular forcing. The series converges uniformly on the closed disk: for n≥3 each term is bounded by a constant times n−3. Its first spatial derivatives also converge uniformly. For instance the radial derivative and r−1 times the angular derivative of each term are bounded by constants times n−2; the n=1 harmonic term is smooth in Cartesian coordinates. It defines a function with zero boundary data and the required weak formulation. Applying the Laplacian to finite partial sums gives the Fourier partial sums of f, which converge in L2; passing to the limit against smooth compactly supported test functions verifies the Poisson equation. It holds classically off the jump rays and the centre, where the forcing is locally constant. It cannot be interpreted as a global classical equation with continuous second derivatives for discontinuous forcing. Uniqueness follows from the zero-boundary Dirichlet energy identity for the difference of two solutions.
