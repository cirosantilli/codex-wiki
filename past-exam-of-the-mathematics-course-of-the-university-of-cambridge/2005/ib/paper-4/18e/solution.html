Use the standard ideal-fluid assumptions underlying the stated relation: both fluids are incompressible, inviscid and unbounded away from the interface, with no surface tension. An irrotational flow has uj​=∇ϕj​, and incompressibility gives Δϕj​=0 in each fluid. The unsteady Bernoulli equation is
ϕj,t​+21​∣∇ϕj​∣2+ρj​pj​​+gz=Cj​(t),
(51)
where the arbitrary time functions can be absorbed into the potentials. On z=ζ(x,t) the material-interface and normal-stress conditions are
ζt​+ϕj,x​ζx​=ϕj,z​(j=1,2),p1​=p2​.
(52)
Disturbance velocities decay as z→±∞. The equilibrium pressures are p∗​−ρj​gz.
Linearize at the resting flat interface. The equations become Δϕj​=0, ζt​=ϕj,z​ on z=0, and
ρ1​(ϕ1,t​+gζ)=ρ2​(ϕ2,t​+gζ).
(53)
For k>0, write ζ=ηei(kx−ωt). Decay selects ϕ1​=A1​e−kzei(kx−ωt) above and ϕ2​=A2​ekzei(kx−ωt) below. The kinematic conditions give A1​=iωη/k and A2​=−iωη/k. Substituting into the dynamic condition gives
ρ1​(ω2/k+g)η=ρ2​(−ω2/k+g)η,ω2=ρ2​+ρ1​ρ2​−ρ1​​gk.​
(54)
For a signed wave number replace k by ∣k∣. A heavier lower fluid gives stable interfacial gravity waves; a heavier upper fluid gives ω2<0 and Rayleigh-Taylor instability. The density ordering is not silently assumed positive in the derivation.
