In Cartesian coordinates the magnetic field is (bx,by,−2bz). Its divergence is b+b−2b=0, and all components of its curl vanish. Thus it satisfies the static vacuum magnetic equations ∇⋅B=0 and ∇×B=0, with no electric current in the region.
Fix clockwise orientation as viewed from above, looking down the positive z-axis. Its positive area normal is −ez​. The loop's external magnetic flux is therefore
ΦB​=2πba2z=Kz,K=2πba2.​
(57)
With the opposite normal its flux has the opposite sign. On the circle B=baer​−2bzez​ and clockwise line element is dℓ=−aeφ​dφ. The Lorentz force element is
Idℓ×B=Iba2ez​dφ+2Ibazer​dφ.
(58)
The radial terms cancel around the circle, so
FB​=KIez​.​
(59)
This fixes the current/flux/force signs together.
Neglect self-inductance initially, so Faraday's law and Ohm's law give RI=−Kz˙. Including gravity then gives
mz¨=−mg−RK2​z˙.​
(60)
For R>0 and K=0, the terminal solution is z=z0​−vt with
v=K2mgR​=4π2b2a4mgR​.​
(61)
The general velocity relaxes exponentially to −v on time scale mR/K2. In the terminal state I=Kv/R, and the Joule-heating rate is
I2R=RK2v2​=mgv=−dtd​(mgz).
(62)
Thus all the lost gravitational potential energy becomes resistive heat when kinetic energy is constant.
Within the zero-inductance approximation v→0 as R→0; finite-speed motion would require an unbounded current. This limit is singular, so it is important to describe the physical ideal-conductor limit separately. With finite self-inductance L, the circuit equation is LI˙+RI=−Kz˙. At R=0, total linked flux LI+Kz is conserved and there is no Joule heating. If I=0 initially at z0​, then
I=−LK​(z−z0​),mz¨=−mg−LK2​(z−z0​).
(63)
This lossless limit of magnetic loop braking has undamped oscillations about z0​−mgL/K2, with magnetic energy storing and returning mechanical energy. It has no generic dissipative terminal descent. Thus the vanishing terminal speed is the formal resistive-model limit, not a justification for dropping inductance in a perfectly conducting moving loop.
