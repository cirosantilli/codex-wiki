Its self-transition at zero has probability q>0, so it is aperiodic. It is the reflected chain with geometric downward jumps.

The separate boundary equation at i=1 is h1​=q2+pqh1​+ph2​. It must not be replaced by the linear recurrence relation with h0​=1: the absorbing hitting convention at zero is different from applying a first-step return equation there.
For p=q, the linear recurrence relation has roots 1 and r=q/p, so hi​=A+Bri for i≥1. The boundary equation gives B=r(1−A). If p<1/2, then r>1 and boundedness of probabilities forces B=0, hence A=1. If p=1/2, the repeated-root solution is A+Bi; boundedness again gives B=0 and the boundary equation gives A=1.
For p>1/2, r<1 and the nonnegative harmonic candidate hi​=ri+1, together with h0​=1, satisfies every first-step equation. Hitting-by-time-n probabilities start from zero off the target and iterate those equations, so by induction they are bounded above by this candidate. Taking their increasing limit gives hi​≤ri+1. But the general bounded solution has limit A≥0 as i→∞, forcing A=0 under that bound. Thus
hi​={1,(q/p)i+1,​p≤1/2,p>1/2,​i≥1.​
(74)
In particular the transient exponent is i+1, fixed by the original boundary equation.
The probability of a subsequent return to zero when starting there is f00​=q+ph1​. It is one for p≤1/2, so the Markov chain is recurrent there. For p>1/2 it equals q+p(q/p)2=q/p<1, so the irreducible Markov chain is transient.
