Suppose the union W has a separation W=A∪B into disjoint, nonempty, relatively open subsets. Each connected Uj​ must lie entirely in one of them: otherwise its intersections with A and B would separate Uj​ in its subspace topology. In particular U1​ lies on one side, say A. Every other Uj​ intersects U1​, so it cannot lie in B and must also lie in A. Then W⊆A, contradicting B=∅. The union is connected. This is the connected union with a connected hub argument.
For path-connectedness, take any x∈Ui​ and y∈Uj​. Choose a∈Ui​∩U1​ and b∈Uj​∩U1​. There is a path from x to a inside Ui​, a path from a to b inside U1​, and a path from b to y inside Uj​. Concatenate the three paths on successive thirds of [0,1]. They agree at the joining points, so the resulting map is continuous and remains in W. The union is path-connected. There need not be a single point common to all the subsets.
