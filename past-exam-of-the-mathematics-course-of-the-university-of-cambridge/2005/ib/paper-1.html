1C OurBigBook.com
The minimal polynomial mβ​(t) of the linear map β is the unique monic polynomial of smallest degree satisfying mβ​(β)=0. An annihilating polynomial exists because the powers I,β,β2,… lie in the finite-dimensional vector space End(V) and are therefore linearly dependent. If two monic annihilating polynomials had the same least degree, their difference would be an annihilating polynomial of smaller degree, so they coincide.
Write mβ​(t)=a0​+tq(t). If a0​=0, evaluation at the linear map gives βq(β)=−a0​I. Since β commutes with every polynomial in itself, this is a two-sided inverse:
β−1=−a0​q(β)​​.
(1)
Conversely, if β is an invertible linear map and a0​=0, then mβ​(t)=tq(t) and multiplication of βq(β)=0 by β−1 gives q(β)=0, contradicting the defining least degree. Thus invertibility is equivalent to a nonzero constant term of the minimal polynomial. In the zero-dimensional case the minimal polynomial is 1 and the same conclusion holds.

The image is a ring torus: a circle of radius b is rotated about the vertical axis at distance a from that axis. Put r=x2+y2​. Since a>b, every image point has r>0, and the image is precisely

U=T∖(Cu​∪Cv​),Cu​={z=0, r=a+b}∩T,Cv​={y=0, x>0}∩T.

For a two-dimensional incompressible flow u=(u,v), the continuity equation is ux​+vy​=0. Locally, or globally on a simply connected fluid region, this permits a streamfunction with
u=ψy​,v=−ψx​.
(14)
Indeed, the one-form −vdx+udy is closed by incompressibility and hence is locally the differential of ψ. Conversely these expressions automatically give zero divergence by equality of mixed derivatives.
A streamline at a fixed time is tangent to the instantaneous velocity field. Along it,
dsdψ​=∇ψ⋅u=ψx​ψy​−ψy​ψx​=0.
(15)

At a stagnation point, the tangent is degenerate and distinct branches of a level set need not form a single streamline. For unsteady flow this argument fixes time: it does not identify streamlines with particle pathlines.

Under the null hypothesis of equal population probabilities, the three counts have a multinomial distribution with probabilities 1/3 and expected counts 100 each. The Pearson chi-squared goodness-of-fit test uses
X2=∑j=13​100(Oj​−100)2​=100(−8)2+(−11)2+192​=5.46​.
(24)
No probabilities have been estimated from the data, and the three counts sum to a fixed total. Hence the null reference chi-squared distribution has 3−1=2 statistical degrees of freedom. The supplied 5% upper critical value is 5.99, and 5.46<5.99. At the 5% significance level there is insufficient evidence to reject equal popularity.
The asymptotic P-value is Pr(χ22​≥5.46)=e−5.46/2≈0.0652. The expected counts are all large, so the approximation is appropriate if the sampled choices are independent and representative of this branch. The conclusion depends on the chosen significance level; for instance, a 10% test would reject. Associate free real dual variables ui​ and vj​ with the row and column equality constraints. The Lagrangian for the transportation problem is
L(x,u,v)=∑i​ai​ui​+∑j​bj​vj​+∑i,j​(cij​−ui​−vj​)xij​.
(25)
Its infimum over xij​≥0 is finite exactly when ui​+vj​≤cij​ for every pair. The linear programming duality therefore gives
maximize i∑​ai​ui​+j∑​bj​vj​subject to ui​+vj​≤cij​,ui​,vj​∈R​.
(26)
The dual variables have no sign restrictions because the primal constraints are equalities.
A feasible shipment x is optimal if and only if there exist feasible transportation dual potentials satisfying complementary slackness:
xij​(cij​−ui​−vj​)=0for every i,j​.
(27)
Thus every occupied route must have ui​+vj​=cij​. To see sufficiency explicitly, the primal minus dual objective is
∑i,j​xij​(cij​−ui​−vj​)≥0,
(28)
and complementary slackness makes it zero. This is the weak duality certificate. Necessity follows from strong duality for the feasible, bounded linear program. If the total supply M is positive, xij​=ai​bj​/M gives feasibility; if M=0, x=0 does. The feasible shipments form a closed bounded set, so the primal optimum is attained. This also covers zero supplies and demands. The one redundant equality merely leaves the dual gauge freedom (u,v)↦(u+t,v−t).

Let dimV=n and dimW=k. Extend a basis w1​,…,wk​ of the vector subspace W to w1​,…,wn​ of V, and let f1​,…,fn​ be its dual basis. Every linear functional is uniquely ∑i​ci​fi​. It vanishes on W exactly when c1​=⋯=ck​=0. Thus the annihilator of a vector subspace has basis fk+1​,…,fn​, and

To apply this to a matrix, regard its rows as elements of (Rn)∗ and let R be their linear span. Elementary invertible row and column operations reduce a rank-r matrix to a block identity diag(Ir​,0), showing that dimR=r. The equations require that every functional in R vanish on x. The canonical identification Rn≅((Rn)∗)∗ sends x to evaluation f↦f(x): it is injective because coordinate functionals separate points, and is bijective by equal dimensions. dimkerA=n−r​.
(30)
A basis of this kernel consists of exactly n−r linearly independent solutions, and every solution is their linear combination.

Let G act on its n left cosets by g⋅(xH)=gxH. This is well-defined and gives a group homomorphism ρ:G→Sn​. Its kernel K is a normal subgroup. An element lies in this kernel exactly when it fixes every coset, so
K=⋂x∈G​xHx−1⊂H.
(31)
This is the normal core of a subgroup. By the first isomorphism theorem, G/K≅ρ(G), a subgroup of Sn​. The Lagrange theorem therefore gives
[G:K]=∣ρ(G)∣ divides n!​.
(32)
In particular K has finite index even when G is infinite.
Now let H be generated by an element of order q in a group of order pq. Then ∣H∣=q and [G:H]=p. The preceding result gives a normal K⊂H with [G:K]∣p!. Since H has prime order, its subgroup K is either {e} or all of H. The first choice would imply pq∣p!, which is impossible because the prime q>p divides none of the factors of p!. Therefore K=H, proving that every subgroup generated by an element of order q is normal. No classification of groups of order pq is needed.

The sum of the two metrics is nonnegative and symmetric. It vanishes exactly when both coordinate distances vanish, hence exactly when the two points agree. Adding the two component triangle inequalities gives the triangle inequality on the Cartesian product, so the displayed function is a metric. Moreover,

To prove sequential compactness directly from compactness, take a sequence (xn​) in X. A simple hypothesis specifies the entire probability distribution. A critical region C is the set of observations for which the null hypothesis is rejected. The size of a statistical test is supθ∈H0​​Prθ​(X∈C), which reduces to Pr0​(C) for a simple hypothesis. The power function is Prθ​(C) as a function of the true parameter or distribution. At a specified alternative, the Type II error probability is Prθ​(Cc)=1−Prθ​(C), the chance of failing to reject a false null hypothesis.
