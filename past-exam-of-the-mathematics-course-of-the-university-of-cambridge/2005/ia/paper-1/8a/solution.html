Use matrix notation and set s=vTv>0, t=vTTv, τ=trT. Contracting the proposed decomposition with v and taking its trace, using the stipulated transverse and trace conditions, gives
Tv=(A+Bs)v+sC,t=sA+s2B,τ=3A+sB.
(27)
These relations determine both scalars and the transverse vector uniquely:
A=21​(τ−st​),B=2s23t−sτ​,C=sTv−(t/s)v​.​
(28)
The last expression obeys CTv=0 directly.
To construct and check the remaining symmetric matrix, use the orthogonal projection matrix P=I−vvT/s onto v⊥, and define
D=PTP−AP.​
(29)
Both terms are symmetric, Dv=0, and
trD=tr(TP)−AtrP=τ−st​−2A=0.
(30)
Expand T=(P+Q)T(P+Q) with Q=vvT/s. The mixed terms are PTQ=CvT and QTP=vCT, while QTQ=(t/s2)vvT and PTP=AP+D. Consequently
T=AI+(s2t​−sA​)vvT+CvT+vCT+D,
(31)
and the displayed coefficient equals B. This proves existence as well as the axis decomposition of a symmetric matrix formulas.
For the dimension count, choose an orthonormal basis whose third axis is parallel to v. The vector C has two transverse components. The conditions Dv=0 and symmetry set the third row and column of D to zero, leaving a symmetric 2×2 block. Its zero trace leaves two independent entries, of the form (de​e−d​). Together with the two independent scalars A,B, the total is 1+1+2+2=6 parameters, exactly 3(3+1)/2, the dimension of real symmetric 3×3 matrices. The recovery formulas also prove uniqueness, so this is an actual parameterization without redundant degrees of freedom.
