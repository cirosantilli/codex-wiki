The Wilson theorem says that (p−1)!≡−1(modp) for a prime number p. Thus 18⋅17!≡−1(mod19), and since 18≡−1, we get 17!≡1. The Fermat little theorem says bp−1≡1(modp) when p does not divide b. Applying it to 3 gives 318≡1, so 316 is the inverse of 9 modulo 19. Since 9⋅17=153≡1, the required residue is
a=17.​
(1)
This is the unique representative in {1,…,19}.
For the simultaneous integer congruences, the modulus-2 condition is already implied by x≡3(mod4). Write x=3+4k; the modulus-3 condition becomes k≡1(mod3), hence x=7+12t. The modulus-5 condition then gives 2+2t≡4, or t≡1(mod5). Therefore
x=19+60j,j∈Z.​
(2)
Every such integer satisfies all four integer congruences; reversing the substitutions shows that there are no others. The uniqueness modulo 60 also follows from the Chinese remainder theorem applied to the coprime moduli 3,4,5.
