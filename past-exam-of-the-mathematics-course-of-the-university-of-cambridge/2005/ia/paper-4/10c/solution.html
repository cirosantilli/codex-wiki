The rim-mass wheel has moment of inertia I=MR2. A uniform solid cylinder has axial moment 21​mS2, so the roller of mass M/2 has J=MS2/4. No slip gives equal contact speeds, Rω=Sσ, when ω,σ denote angular-speed magnitudes. Therefore
I=MR2,J=4MS2​,ωσ​=SR​.​
(38)
With parallel signed axes the two angular velocities have opposite signs. Their kinetic energies add regardless of that convention:
21​Iω2+21​Jσ2=21​[I+J(R/S)2]ω2=21​Kω2,K=45​MR2.​
(39)
The reflected inertia of a no-slip wheel and roller must be used because accelerating the wheel also accelerates the roller. The transmitted contact forces redistribute energy without dissipating it. For an applied wheel torque T, the input power is Tω, so the work-energy theorem gives Kωω˙=Tω, or Kω˙=T; this also holds at rest by continuity or the constrained equation of motion.
Assume the driving constants Q,Ω are positive. With the speed-dependent torque,
Kω˙=Q(1−ω/Ω),ω(0)=0.
(40)
Separating this linear equation gives
ω(t)=Ω(1−e−Qt/(KΩ)).​
(41)
For the fan, its resisting roller torque dissipates power γσ3. Expressing this in the wheel coordinate gives a resisting wheel torque γ(R/S)3ω2. Thus, with Γ=γ(R/S)3,
Kω˙=Q(1−ω/Ω)−Γω2.
(42)
The right-hand side is positive at zero, strictly decreasing for nonnegative ω, and has a unique positive zero; the wheel starting at rest approaches this terminal angular speed. Hence
ωmax​=Q/Ω+(Q/Ω)2+4γQ(R/S)3​2Q​,vrim,max​=Rωmax​.​
(43)
For a nonzero fan drag this is less than Ω, and as γ→0 it tends to Ω. The cubed radius ratio is essential: one factor arises from converting roller power to wheel torque, in addition to the two factors in σ2.
