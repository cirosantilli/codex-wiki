First E=ker(A+I) is a linear subspace. To prove it is nonzero, use orthogonality and detA=−1:
det(A+I)=detAdet(I+A−1)=−det(I+AT)=−det(A+I).
(23)
Hence det(A+I)=0. If u∈E then Au=−u also implies ATu=−u. For x⊥E,
⟨Ax,u⟩=⟨x,ATu⟩=−⟨x,u⟩=0,
(24)
so E⊥ is invariant. If E had dimension two, its one-dimensional orthogonal complement would be invariant and A would act there as a scalar η=±1. The determinant would be (−1)2η=−1, so η=−1, forcing A=−I. Dimension three also forces A=−I. Both contradict the hypothesis. Consequently
dimE=1.​
(25)
Choose an orthonormal basis beginning with a unit vector in E and followed by a basis of Π=E⊥. In this basis the orthogonal matrix has block form
A∼(−10​0R​),RTR=I2​,detR=1.
(26)
The first column of R is some (cosθ,sinθ)T. Its perpendicular unit second column with positive orientation must be (−sinθ,cosθ)T. Thus the restriction is the plane rotation
R=(cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​).
(27)
Trace invariance gives trA=−1+2cosθ, proving
cosθ=21​(trA+1).​
(28)
A Euclidean reflection in Π fixes that plane pointwise and reverses E, so it occurs exactly when R=I2​. This is equivalent to cosθ=1, hence exactly to trA=1. Finally,
det(A−I)=(−2)det(R−I2​)=−2((cosθ−1)2+sin2θ)=4(cosθ−1)​.
(29)
This exhibits the three-dimensional improper orthogonal transformation as a rotation about the axis E composed with Euclidean reflection in Π.
