Assume sufficient smoothness and a regular boundary for the integrations below, with n outward. Put δ=w−ϕ, so δ=0 on S. Expanding the Dirichlet energy gives
∫V​∣∇w∣2=∫V​∣∇ϕ∣2+2∫V​∇ϕ⋅∇δ+∫V​∣∇δ∣2.
(30)
By Green's first identity, the middle integral is
∫V​∇ϕ⋅∇δ=∫S​δ∂n​ϕ−∫V​δΔϕ=0.
(31)
Thus the Dirichlet principle follows with an explicit remainder:
∫V​∣∇w∣2−∫V​∣∇ϕ∣2=∫V​∣∇(w−ϕ)∣2≥0.​
(32)
Equality means w−ϕ is constant on each connected component; its zero boundary value forces that constant to be zero.
Now let w solve the heat equation and set E(t)=∫V​∣∇w∣2dV. Differentiation followed by Green's first identity gives
E′(t)=2∫V​∇w⋅∇wt​=2∫S​wt​∂n​w−2∫V​wt​Δw.
(33)
Under time-independent Dirichlet boundary conditions, wt​=0 on S, while wt​=Δw in V. Therefore
E′(t)=−2∫V​(Δw)2dV≤0.​
(34)
If equality holds at a particular time, continuity makes Δw=0 throughout V at that time. Its boundary values are still f, so uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem implies w=ϕ. Conversely w=ϕ has Δw=0 and gives equality. This proves the full equality criterion for Dirichlet energy dissipation for the heat equation.
For the dynamic boundary condition for the heat equation, substitute wt​=−α∂n​w directly into the boundary term. The interior equation is unchanged, so
E′(t)=−2∫V​wt2​dV−2∫S​α(∂n​w)2dS≤0.​
(35)
This is Dirichlet energy dissipation with a dynamic heat boundary condition. It remains valid when α vanishes, because no division by α is used. The last part preserves energy monotonicity; its stationary states need not have the earlier prescribed Dirichlet data. For example, with strictly positive α on a connected boundary, a stationary harmonic state has zero outward normal derivative and is constant, as follows by applying Green's first identity to that state itself.
