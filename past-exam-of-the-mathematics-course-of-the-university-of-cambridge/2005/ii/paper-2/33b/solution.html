For a normalized trial state in the quadratic-form domain of a self-adjoint Hamiltonian operator bounded below, its expectation of H is at least the ground energy E0​. Minimizing this Rayleigh quotient over a tractable trial family therefore supplies an upper bound.
For the two states, set a±​=(a1​±a2​)/2​. Their quotient is
(1+s)a+2​+(1−s)a−2​(E+ϵ)a+2​+(E−ϵ)a−2​​.
(116)
The strict assumption ϵ<sE excludes ∣s∣=1, since equality in the overlap Cauchy-Schwarz bound would then force the two states to be proportional and ϵ=sE. Thus ∣s∣<1. The two generalized eigenvalues are (E±ϵ)/(1±s), and their difference has the sign of ϵ−sE. The symmetric trial is the lower one, so
E0​≤1+sE+ϵ​.​
(117)
For the single attractive delta well, the supplied wavefunction has norm one because ∫λe−2λ∣x∣dx=1. Its kinetic energy in quadratic-form notation is ℏ2∫∣ψ1′​∣2/(2m)=ℏ2λ2/(2m), and its delta potential energy is −K∣ψ1​(0)∣2=−Kλ. With λ=mK/ℏ2 this gives
EB​=−Kλ/2.​
(118)
Its derivative jump is ψ1′​(0+)−ψ1′​(0−)=−2λψ1​(0), exactly the delta boundary condition, so it is an eigenstate. A decaying bound state on the two half-lines must have a common decay rate fixed by this same jump; there is only this one negative-energy state, confirming it is the ground state.
For R>0, integrate the overlap on the three regions x<0, 0<x<R, x>R. Put a=λR. The result is
s=(1+a)e−a,E=EB​−Kλe−2a,ϵ=EB​s−Kλe−a.​
(119)
The second equality is the single-well energy plus the extra delta expectation; the third follows by applying either single-well eigenvalue equation in the cross matrix element. Moreover ϵ−sE=Kλe−a[(1+a)e−2a−1]<0, so the preceding bound applies. Substitution gives
E0​(R)≤EB​[1+1+(1+λR)e−λR2e−λR(1+e−λR)​].​
(120)
For widely separated wells, each localized orbital is close to an isolated-well ground state and the low-energy subspace is accurately their span; the symmetric combination captures the leading tunneling energy lowering. In the limit both exact and trial energies approach EB​. This illustrates electronic molecular binding: sharing an orbital between attractive centers can lower its energy below the separated-center value. A full equilibrium bond length would also require the repulsion of the positive centers.
