Each scan() command reads a numeric vector from the entered data: the first creates the nine counts, and the second their nine predictor values. The last command fits a generalized linear model with the Poisson distribution and its default log link, including an intercept, then prints a summary. Thus the model is independent counts with means μj​ satisfying
logμj​=b0​+b1​ij​,μ​j​=exp(1.363+0.3106ij​).
(12)
The coefficients are maximum-likelihood estimates, and the reported standard errors come from the inverse fitted Fisher information matrix. Increasing the predictor by one multiplies the fitted mean by e0.3106≃1.364, about a 36.4% increase. The intercept corresponds to a mean e1.363≃3.91 at predictor zero, outside the observed range. Approximate coefficient-to-standard-error ratios are 6.17 and 8.13; the slope is strongly different from zero under the assumed model. A 95% Wald confidence interval for the slope is about [0.236,0.385], or [1.266,1.470] for the multiplicative change.
The deviance is twice the log-likelihood advantage of the saturated model over the fitted model. For these counts it is
D=2∑j=19​[nj​logμ​j​nj​​−(nj​−μ​j​)],
(13)
with the logarithmic term interpreted as zero when a count is zero. The saturated model has nine freely fitted means; the fitted model has two independent regression coefficients, so the residual degrees of freedom are 9−2=7.
Under an adequate Poisson model and the usual large-sample approximation, D is compared with χ72​. The reported 13.218 is rather large relative to seven, but below the 5% upper critical value about 14.07; its approximate upper-tail probability is 0.067. Thus there is some indication of lack of fit or overdispersion, but not a rejection at the 5% level on this diagnostic. The deviance-to-d.f. ratio is about 1.89; it does not by itself prove an overdispersed model, and the chi-square approximation is imperfect for small counts.
There is one inconsistency in the supplied numerical output. Independently solving the Poisson score equations for the displayed nine counts gives b0​=1.363454, b1​=0.310563 and deviance 13.217971, agreeing with the printed estimates and deviance. But [XTdiag(μ​)X]−1 gives standard errors approximately 0.221295 and 0.030850. In particular the printed slope standard error 0.0382 is not the standard Poisson-model value for these data. The interpretations above describe the supplied numbers; the directly recomputed slope ratio is 10.07 and its normal-approximation 95% interval is about [0.2501,0.3710]. The conclusion of a strong increasing trend is unchanged.
