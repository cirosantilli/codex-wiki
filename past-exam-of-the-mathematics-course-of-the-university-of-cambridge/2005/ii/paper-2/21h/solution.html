The Seifert-van Kampen theorem states that if a space is covered by path-connected open sets U,V with path-connected intersection containing a basepoint, then its fundamental group is the free product π1​(U)∗π1​(V) modulo the normal subgroup generated by identifying the two images of each loop in U∩V. More generally it is the corresponding pushout of groups.
A wedge of two circles has fundamental group F(a,b), the free group on their two loops: apply the theorem to thickened circle neighborhoods whose intersection is contractible and use π1​(S1)=Z. Represent the torus by a square with opposite edges identified. Remove an interior disk; the remainder deformation retracts onto this wedge. Replacing the disk attaches a two-cell whose boundary follows aba−1b−1. A disk neighborhood and the punctured torus have annular intersection, and van Kampen kills precisely that boundary loop. Hence
π1​(T)=⟨a,b∣aba−1b−1=1⟩≅Z2.​
(70)
A torus with a point removed retracts to a torus with a small open disk removed, and then to the same wedge. Thus
π1​(T∖{p})≅F2​.​
(71)
For the connected sum, remove a disk from each torus and glue their boundary circles with reversed orientations. The two pieces have free groups F(a,b) and F(c,d) and their overlap is an annulus. Its boundary generators correspond to [a,b] and [c,d]−1, where [a,b]=aba−1b−1. Therefore
π1​(T#T)=⟨a,b,c,d∣[a,b][c,d]=1⟩.​
(72)
The boundary relation is essential: this group is neither the free group on four generators nor the abelian group Z4.
