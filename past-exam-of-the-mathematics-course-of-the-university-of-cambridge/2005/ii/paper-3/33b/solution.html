A Bravais lattice is the set of all integer combinations of linearly independent primitive translation vectors. Its reciprocal lattice consists of g satisfying g⋅l∈2πZ for every translation l; equivalently eig⋅l=1.
The original PDF's third family has a bare j, not n2​j. Taken literally, the listed set is not the claimed Bravais lattice: the claimed primitive vector a(i+k)/2 does not occur. Its half-integer first and third coordinates force it into that third family, whose second coordinate is always a, not zero. The intended repair is j↦n2​j in the third family. The following conclusions apply to that corrected set.
The four corrected families are exactly
L=2a​{(h,k,l)∈Z3:h+k+l is even}.
(66)
The allowed parity patterns are all even, or exactly two odd coordinates. An integer combination of the proposed primitive vectors has coordinates (a/2)(m2​+m3​,m1​+m3​,m1​+m2​), so lies in this set. Conversely, solve
m1​=21​(k+l−h),m2​=21​(h+l−k),m3​=21​(h+k−l).
(67)
All three are integers precisely when h+k+l is even. This proves that the corrected set is the face-centered cubic lattice with the proposed primitive basis.
Direct dot products give ai​⋅bj​=2πδij​ for
b1​=a2π​(−1,1,1),b2​=a2π​(1,−1,1),b3​=a2π​(1,1,−1).​
(68)
These vectors generate the entire reciprocal lattice, since the coefficients of an arbitrary reciprocal vector in this basis are its integer pairings with the ai​. Its vectors are (2π/a)(h,k,l) with all three coordinates of the same parity, the body-centered cubic lattice.
In Bragg scattering, translating a scattering atom by l changes the amplitude by ei(k−k′)⋅l. Constructive interference from every lattice translate requires this factor to be one, so k′−k=g is a reciprocal lattice vector. For elastic scattering, ∣k′∣=∣k∣; the chord between these two vectors has length 2∣k∣sin(θ/2). Therefore
sin(θ/2)=2∣k∣∣g∣​.​
(69)
The shortest nonzero reciprocal vectors are the (111) family of length (2π/a)3​, followed by the (200) family of length (2π/a)2. Identical atoms on this Bravais lattice introduce no additional basis extinction. If both families are accessible at the incident wavelength, their scattering angles obey sin(θ1​/2)/sin(θ2​/2)=3​/2​.
