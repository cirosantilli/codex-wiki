For 0<q<1, a finite positive equilibrium exists exactly when aqL>b, and then
z∗​=(aqL−b)/[a(1−qL)].​
(7)
The derivative of the ratio's vector field at z∗​ is −(aqL−b)<0, so this equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable; every positive initial ratio tends to it. The critical real length is Lc​=log(b/a)/logq, and the admissible integer lengths satisfy L<Lc​. Thus the largest positive integer length, if one exists, is Lmax​=⌈Lc​⌉−1​. At equality the equilibrium is zero, so rounding down without accounting for the strict inequality can be wrong. If q=1, mutants are never produced from masters and the ratio grows without a finite positive equilibrium; the finite threshold formula concerns imperfect copying.
