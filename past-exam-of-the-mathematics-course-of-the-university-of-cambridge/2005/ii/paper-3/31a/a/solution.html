There is a literal inconsistency in the stated Riemann-Hilbert problem: a jump matrix S(k) tending to zero is incompatible with μ±​→I. Along the real axis the conjugating exponentials are unitary, so the right side of the jump tends to zero while the left side tends to I. No normalized solution satisfies the printed decay condition. The intended condition is S(k)→I, or replacement of S in the jump by I+S when S denotes its decaying part.
For this corrected problem assume a regular, invertible, pole-free solution, differentiable in x,t, with the required expansion uniformly at infinity. These conditions matter: arbitrary jump data need not be solvable, and poles would contribute extra terms. Write σ=σ3​ and μ=I+μ1​/k+μ2​/k2+⋯. Matching the nondecaying coefficients in the two differential expressions yields
Q=i[σ,μ1​],A=2i[σ,μ1​]=2Q,B=2i[σ,μ2​]−Aμ1​.​
(57)
The first expression makes Q off-diagonal.
These matches really establish the differential equations, rather than just formal identities. Let G=e−i(kx+2k2t)σS(k)ei(kx+2k2t)σ. It satisfies Gx​=−ik[σ,G] and Gt​=−2ik2[σ,G]. Differentiating μ+​=μ−​G consequently shows that
Rx​=(μx​+ik[σ,μ]−Qμ)μ−1,Rt​=(μt​+2ik2[σ,μ]−(kA+B)μ)μ−1
(58)
have matching boundary values. They therefore extend holomorphically across the contour. The chosen coefficients make both residuals O(k−1) at infinity. Their absence of poles and Liouville theorem imply Rx​=Rt​=0, proving the required Lax pair under the corrected hypotheses. This is matrix NLS reconstruction from a normalized Riemann-Hilbert problem.
