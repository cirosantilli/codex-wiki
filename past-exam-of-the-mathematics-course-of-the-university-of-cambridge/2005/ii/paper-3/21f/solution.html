The continuous dual space X∗ consists of bounded complex-linear functionals ϕ:X→C, with ∥ϕ∥=sup∥x∥≤1​∣ϕ(x)∣. For 1≤s<∞, ℓs consists of sequences with ∑j​∣xj​∣s<∞, norm (∑j​∣xj​∣s)1/s; ℓ∞ consists of bounded sequences with supremum norm.
For a∈ℓp, define ϕa​(x)=∑j​aj​xj​ on ℓq. Hölder's inequality gives absolute convergence and ∥ϕa​∥≤∥a∥p​. For a finite truncation, choose xj​=aj​​∣aj​∣p−2/(∑j≤N​∣aj​∣p)1/q for j≤N, zero elsewhere, interpreting the numerator as zero when aj​=0. Its q-norm is one and its pairing equals (∑j≤N​∣aj​∣p)1/p. Letting N grow proves equality of the norms.
Conversely, for a bounded ϕ let aj​=ϕ(ej​). The same finite test vector gives ∑j≤N​∣aj​∣p≤∥ϕ∥p, so a∈ℓp. Finite truncations converge in ℓq, and continuity gives ϕ(x)=∑j​aj​xj​. Thus (ℓq)∗≅ℓp isometrically.​
For ℓ1, boundedness gives ∣aj​∣≤∥ϕ∥, while any bounded sequence defines an absolutely convergent pairing bounded by ∥a∥∞​∥x∥1​. Density of finite sequences again proves surjectivity, and single-coordinate tests give norm equality. Hence (ℓ1)∗≅ℓ∞ isometrically.​ The reversed argument fails for ℓ∞: finite sequences are not dense in its supremum norm; their closure is c0​. For example the limit functional on convergent sequences extends by the Hahn-Banach theorem to ℓ∞, vanishes on every ej​, yet takes value one on the constant-one sequence. It cannot be represented by pairing with an ℓ1 coefficient sequence.
