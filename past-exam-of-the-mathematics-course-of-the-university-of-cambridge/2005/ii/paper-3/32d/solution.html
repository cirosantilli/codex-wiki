Addition of two spin-one angular momenta gives J=0,1,2, with M=−J,−J+1,…,J. To obtain the J=1 multiplet, its highest-weight state must have M=1 and be killed by J+​=J+(1)​+J+(2)​. On the two possible product states, the raising operator gives the same multiple of ∣1;1⟩, so their difference is required. Normalization and successive lowering give
∣1,1⟩∣1,0⟩∣1,−1⟩​=2​∣1;0⟩−∣0;1⟩​,=2​∣1;−1⟩−∣−1;1⟩​,=2​∣0;−1⟩−∣−1;0⟩​.​​
(64)
For example J−​∣1,1⟩=∣1;−1⟩−∣−1;1⟩=2​∣1,0⟩, and lowering once more gives the last expression. These states are all antisymmetric under interchanging the two constituents.
Identical spin-one particles are bosons, so the total state must be symmetric. Its J=1 spin factor is antisymmetric and must therefore multiply an antisymmetric spatial factor. Two particles in the same one-particle orbital have zero antisymmetric spatial state. The lowest allowed choice is
2​ψ1​(rA​)ψ2​(rB​)−ψ2​(rA​)ψ1​(rB​)​∣1,M⟩,E=E1​+E2​​,M=−1,0,1.
(65)
For distinguishable particles there is no exchange-symmetry constraint. Both can occupy the ground orbital, giving ψ1​(rA​)ψ1​(rB​)∣1,M⟩ and lowest energy 2E1​​, again with three spin states.
