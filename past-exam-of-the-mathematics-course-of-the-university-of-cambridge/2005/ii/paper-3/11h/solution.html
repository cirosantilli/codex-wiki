Write a reduced positive definite binary quadratic form as [a,b,c]=ax2+bxy+cy2, with ∣b∣≤a≤c and b≥0 on the reduction boundaries. Since 4ac−b2=20, reduction gives 3a2≤20, so a=1 or two. The middle coefficient is even. For a=1, only b=0 is possible, yielding c=5. For a=2, b=0 would give a noninteger c, while b=±2 gives c=3; the boundary convention selects b=2. Thus the two reduced forms are
x2+5y2,2x2+2xy+3y2.​

A positive odd integer n is properly represented by some integer form of discriminant d exactly when b2≡d(mod4n) has a solution. For a specified proper equivalence class, the more precise criterion is that [n,b,(b2−d)/(4n)] belong to that class for some such b. Indeed, a primitive representing vector extends to an SL2​(Z) basis change, making the leading coefficient n; conversely that form represents n at the primitive vector (1,0).
For primes p∤10, the existence criterion is (−20/p)=1, because the congruence modulo four is compatible with an even middle coefficient. Quadratic reciprocity gives (−20/p)=(−1/p)(p/5), so the split possibilities are p≡1,3,7,9(mod20). Any proper representing form reduces to one of the two listed forms. Odd values of x2+5y2 are one modulo four; odd values of 2x2+2xy+3y2 require odd y and are three modulo four. These distinguish the two classes. Consequently
p=x2+5y2 for a prime p=2,5⟺p≡1 or 9(mod20).​
(17)
A representation of a prime is automatically proper: a common divisor of x,y would have its square divide p.
