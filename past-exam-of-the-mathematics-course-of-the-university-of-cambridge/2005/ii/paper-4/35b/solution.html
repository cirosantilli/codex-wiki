There is a sign inconsistency in the printed starting energy: its kinetic term uses ∇+iqs​A/ℏ, while the requested field equation, current and later zero-covariant-derivative condition use ∇−iqs​A/ℏ. Both conventions can be derived explicitly. Set Dη​=∇−iηqs​A/ℏ for η=±1; the intended later equations take η=1, whereas the literal printed energy takes η=−1.
The magnetic term is B2/(2μ0​), because Ak,j​(Ak,j​−Aj,k​)=∣∇×A∣2. Vary ψ∗ independently with compactly supported variations. Integration by parts in the kinetic term gives
−2ms​ℏ2​Dη2​ψ+(α+β∣ψ∣2)ψ=0​.
(134)
In particular η=1 gives the requested equation. Its conjugate follows from variation in ψ.
For variation in A, δDη​ψ=−iηqs​δAψ/ℏ. The kinetic variation is −jη​⋅δA, where
jη​=ms​ηqs​ℏ​Im(ψ∗∇ψ)−ms​qs2​​∣ψ∣2A.
(135)
The magnetic variation is (∇×B)⋅δA/μ0​ after integration by parts. Therefore
∇×B=μ0​jη​​.
(136)
For η=1 this current equals the two displayed forms in the question. For the literal plus-sign energy, η=−1, the phase-gradient current term has the opposite sign and the field equation contains D−12​. Thus the requested set follows after correcting that one energy sign, not from silently varying inconsistent expressions. This is the gauge-coupling sign in Ginzburg-Landau superconductivity.
Now use the intended convention and write ψ=ns​​eiθ with ns​>0. Dividing D1​ψ=0 by ψ separates real and imaginary parts:
∇ns​​=0,ℏ∇θ=qs​A​.
(137)
Thus ns​ is constant on each connected region. The remaining field equation requires α+βns​=0, so for T<Tc​,
ns​=−α/β>0​.
(138)
The current is j=(qs​ns​/ms​)(ℏ∇θ−qs​A)=0. Taking the curl locally gives B=0, hence also solves the Maxwell equation.
For a closed curve on which the order parameter is nonzero and D1​ψ=0, Stokes' theorem gives
Φ=∮C​A⋅dl=qs​ℏ​∮C​∇θ⋅dl=qs​ℏ​[θ]C​.
(139)
Single-valuedness of ψ permits phase change 2πN, so the flux quantum magnitude is h/∣qs​∣ (for Cooper pairs, h/(2∣e∣)). There is an important geometric qualification: if the entire spanning surface lies in the smooth zero-field superconducting region assumed above, its flux and the total boundary winding are zero. Nonzero quantized flux occurs through a hole or vortex core where those assumptions do not hold over the spanning surface. More generally, without j=0 along the curve, the exact quantized quantity is the fluxoid
∮C​(A+qs2​ns​ms​​j)⋅dl=qs​h​N​.
(140)
Thus fluxoid and flux quantization in a superconductor are related but are not interchangeable without the current condition.
