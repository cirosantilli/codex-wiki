The Dirichlet series for the Riemann zeta function converges for ℜz>1. In the Hankel integral representation the branch is a branch of tz−1, so the argument restriction concerns t, not the parameter z. Use the principal branch cut along the negative real axis and a positive Hankel loop with a fixed small indentation around zero. Its integral I(z) is an entire function of z, since the tails have exponential decay and the fixed contour avoids zero. Multiplication by Γ(1−z)/(2πi) gives the meromorphic continuation of ζ: it is directly defined away from positive integers, has a pole at 1, and the apparent singularities at 2,3,… are interpreted by limits.

Initially take ℜz<0, so the series on the right converges as N→∞ and the large arcs can be discarded under the given assumption.
