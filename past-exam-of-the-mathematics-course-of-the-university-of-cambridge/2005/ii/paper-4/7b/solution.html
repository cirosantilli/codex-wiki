The fixed points are (0,0) and (±1,1). The Jacobian matrix is
J(x,y)=(1−y2x​−x−1​).
(12)
At the origin its eigenvalues are 1,−1, so it is a saddle with stable direction the y-axis. At either other fixed point, the characteristic polynomial is λ2+λ+2, giving eigenvalues (−1±i7​)/2. Thus both are stable foci. The nullclines are x=0, y=1 and y=x2. In the right half-plane x grows below y=1 and decreases above it; in the left half-plane these horizontal arrows reverse. Vertically, y increases below y=x2 and decreases above it.
A global argument determines the requested omega-limit sets, rather than guessing from the local phase portrait. For x=0, use the quadratic-logarithmic Lyapunov function for a feedback system
L=x2−1−log(x2)+(y−1)2,L˙=−2(y−1)2.
(13)
Its sublevel sets are compact and bounded away from x=0, so trajectories starting in either half-plane exist for all positive time and stay in such a set. Since L decreases, it tends to a limit, and is constant on the omega-limit set. Every point of that set therefore has y=1. Invariance then requires y˙​=x2−1=0, so only the corresponding focus can occur. Also x(t)=x(0)exp(∫0t​(1−y(s))ds) preserves its sign.
Consequently
ω(2,−1)={(1,1)},ω(x,y)={(0,0)} ⟺ x=0​.
