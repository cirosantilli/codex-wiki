For inviscid barotropic flow, conservation gives ρt​+∇⋅(ρu)=0 and ρ(ut​+u⋅∇u)=−∇p. Linearizing about uniform rest ρ0​,p0​ gives
ρt′​+ρ0​∇⋅u=0,ρ0​ut​=−∇p′,p′=c02​ρ′,c02​=(dp/dρ)ρ0​​​,
(149)
with the derivative adiabatic for ordinary sound.
Taking curl gives ∂t​(∇×u)=0. Therefore the acoustic velocity is irrotational if the initial vorticity is zero. Uniform rest as the background does not alone forbid a superposed stationary vortical perturbation: for example u=ε(siny,0,0), ρ′=p′=0 solves the linearized equations but has nonzero curl. Thus the printed irrotational conclusion requires the usual restriction to the longitudinal acoustic mode. In that mode set u=∇ϕ and absorb a time-only gauge into ϕ, so p′=−ρ0​ϕt​. Continuity gives
ϕtt​−c02​Δϕ=0​.
(150)
This separates the sound wave from the stationary vorticity mode in linear acoustics.

∂t​e+∇⋅j=0​.

For any progressive plane wave, not necessarily sinusoidal, let ϕ=F(ξ) with ξ=k⋅x−c0​t. Then u=F′(ξ)k and p′=ρ0​c0​F′(ξ). Its kinetic and compressional energy densities both equal ρ0​(F′)2/2, so
j=c0​ke​.
(153)
The energy is transported at the sound speed in the propagation direction. A superposition of oppositely traveling waves can form a standing wave and need not have this pointwise equality; the statement concerns a single progressive wave.
For a spherical outgoing wave, ϕ=F(r−c0​t)/r. Its radial velocity and pressure are
ur​=rF′​−r2F​,p′=ρ0​c0​rF′​.
(154)
Therefore
eK​=2ρ0​​(rF′​−r2F​)2,eP​=2ρ0​​(rF′​)2​.
(155)
They are generally unequal because of the additional near-field velocity term; their difference is ρ0​[F2/r4−2FF′/r3]/2. They agree only to leading far-field order, or for special profile values. This is the near-field energy imbalance in a spherical acoustic wave.
