An element x of an extension L/K is an algebraic element over K when some nonzero polynomial in K[t] vanishes at x. Let its monic minimal polynomial have degree d. Polynomial division shows K[x] is spanned by 1,x,…,xd−1, and minimality makes these vectors independent. The minimal polynomial is irreducible; for any nonzero q(x), Bezout's identity between q and that polynomial gives a polynomial representative for q(x)−1. Thus K[x] is a field, equals K(x), and has degree d.
Conversely, if K(x) has finite vector-space dimension d, then 1,x,…,xd are linearly dependent over K, giving a nonzero annihilating polynomial. Hence
x algebraic over K ⟺ [K(x):K]<∞.​
(42)
An algebraic extension is one in which every element is algebraic. Suppose M/L and L/K are algebraic, and take α∈M. A polynomial relation for α over L uses only finitely many coefficients a1​,…,ar​. Each is algebraic over K, so adjoining them successively gives a finite extension E=K(a1​,…,ar​), by the proved criterion and the tower law. The same relation makes α algebraic over E, so E(α)/E is finite. Therefore E(α)/K is finite, and its subspace K(α) is finite-dimensional. The criterion makes α algebraic over K. Since α was arbitrary, M/K is algebraic.
