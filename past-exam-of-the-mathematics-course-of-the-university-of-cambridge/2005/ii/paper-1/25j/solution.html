A pi-system is a collection closed under finite intersections. The uniqueness theorem says that two probability measures agreeing on a generating pi-system agree on its generated sigma-field. For finite measures one also fixes the total mass; for a sigma-finite version one requires a covering sequence from the pi-system with finite common masses. Adding the whole space to a pi-system of events causes no change to its generated sigma-field and preserves the stated independence condition.
Fix H∈H. On σ(G) the finite measures A↦P(A∩H) and A↦P(A)P(H) agree on G and on the whole space. Uniqueness makes them agree for every A∈σ(G). Now fix such an A. On σ(H) the measures B↦P(A∩B) and B↦P(A)P(B) agree on H and the whole space; a second application of uniqueness proves
P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B)(A∈σ(G), B∈σ(H)).​
(60)
Thus the two generated sigma-fields are independent. The extension is done one argument at a time, not by assuming the required independence for arbitrary unions.
For the independent random variables, let G be the rectangles ⋂i=1m​{Yi​∈Bi​} and let H be the analogous rectangles in the Zj​. Both are pi-systems. Joint independence of the full list factors each combined rectangular probability into the product of the individual probabilities, hence into P(G)P(H). Their generated sigma-fields are exactly σ(Y1​,…,Ym​) and σ(Z1​,…,Zn​). The proved result therefore gives independence of the two vector-generated sigma-fields.
