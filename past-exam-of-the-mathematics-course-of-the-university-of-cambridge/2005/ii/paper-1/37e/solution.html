The isotropic elastic displacement equation is ρu¨=(λ+μ)∇(∇⋅u)+μ∇2u. With u=∇ϕ+∇×ψ, its dilational and shear potentials satisfy
ϕtt​=cP2​∇2ϕ,ψtt​=cS2​∇2ψ,cP2​=(λ+2μ)/ρ,cS2​=μ/ρ.​
(104)
For the specified two-dimensional potential, ux​=ϕx​+ψy​, uy​=ϕy​−ψx​.
Choose a real wave number k>0 and put ω=kc. Decay into y<0 requires the combinations
ϕ=Aepyei(kx−ωt),ψ=Beqyei(kx−ωt),p=k1−c2/cP2​​,q=k1−c2/cS2​​,
(105)
with 0<c<cS​. Real parts give the physical fields. Then ux​=ikAepy+qBeqy and uy​=pAepy−ikBeqy, times the common propagating factor. The free surface requires σxy​=μ(ux,y​+uy,x​)=0 and σyy​=λ∇⋅u+2μuy,y​=0. At zero these become
2ikpA+(k2+q2)B=0,(k2+q2)A−2ikqB=0.
(106)
A nonzero pair exists precisely when
(k2+q2)2=4k2pq,(2−s2)2=41−s2​1−κs2​,s=c/cS​, κ=cS2​/cP2​.​
(107)
For such a root, one can choose A=0 and B=−2ikpA/(k2+q2), providing the requested wave explicitly. Squaring and excluding the static zero root gives the equivalent Rayleigh polynomial
s6−8s4+(24−16κ)s2−16(1−κ)=0,0<s<1.
(108)
The physical positive root is unique as allowed in the question. All factors of k cancel in the speed equation, so the Rayleigh surface wave is nondispersive, with c determined only by the material constants. The potentials decay exponentially over depths of order k−1.
