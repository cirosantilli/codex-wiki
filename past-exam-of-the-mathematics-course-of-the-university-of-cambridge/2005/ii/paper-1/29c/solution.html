For a regular defining function ϕ, the hypersurface is non-characteristic when the characteristic vector has nonzero normal component:
x2​ϕx1​​−x1​ϕx2​​+aϕx3​​=0on Sϕ​.​
(72)
For ϕ=x3​ this is precisely a=0. The defining function must also have nonzero gradient so that it actually defines a hypersurface.
The method of characteristics gives x˙1​=x2​, x˙2​=−x1​, x˙3​=a, u˙=u. Starting at (ξ1​,ξ2​,0), the first two coordinates rotate clockwise through characteristic time s and x3​=as. Solving back for the initial point gives
u(x1​,x2​,x3​)=ex3​/af(x1​cosax3​​−x2​sinax3​​, x1​sinax3​​+x2​cosax3​​).​
(73)
It meets the data and is C1 when f is C1.
For the rotationally invariant data, u=(x12​+x22​)ex3​/a. As a→0+ it tends to zero for fixed x3​<0, diverges to positive infinity for fixed x3​>0 and nonzero radius, and retains the initial value on x3​=0. It is identically zero on the vertical axis. There is no regular limit across the initial plane. At a=0 that plane is characteristic and the data would need to satisfy x2​fx1​​−x1​fx2​​=f; the chosen radial data violate this except at the origin, explaining the singular limit.
