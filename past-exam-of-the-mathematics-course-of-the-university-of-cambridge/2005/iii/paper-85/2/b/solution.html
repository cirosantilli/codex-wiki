Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2005 / iii / Paper 85 / 2 / b / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 201
Give U(g) its degree filtration. The Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt theorem identifies its associated graded ring with Symk​g, a polynomial ring in dimk​g variables. The Hilbert basis theorem makes this graded algebra Noetherian on both sides.
Here is the required transfer, including why a finite list of symbols gives actual generators. For a right ideal I of a nonnegatively filtered ring A, its induced graded ideal grI is a homogeneous right ideal of grA. Choose finite homogeneous generators and lift them to u1​,…,um​∈I. For u∈I of degree d, express its leading symbol as a graded right linear combination of these symbols, using homogeneous coefficients of complementary degrees. Lift the coefficients to A and subtract the corresponding ∑i​ui​ai​. The remainder has degree strictly below d. Induction, terminating because degrees are nonnegative, proves I=∑i​ui​A. The identical argument with coefficients on the left treats left ideals.
This ascending filtered-graded transfer of Noetherianity proves that U(g) is right and left Noetherian. A quotient ring inherits either property: pull an ascending ideal chain back to the original ring, or take images of ideal generators. Part (a) therefore gives R right and left Noetherian. Alternatively, under the broader almost commutative algebra convention, its commutative finitely generated associated graded ring is directly a quotient of a finite-variable polynomial ring, and the same leading-symbol argument proves the conclusion.
