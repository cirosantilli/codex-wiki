Take rings and modules to be unital. Let Jℓ​ denote the intersection of the maximal left ideals. For each nonzero vector v of a simple module M on the left, the map R→M, r↦rv, is onto and its kernel is a maximal left ideal. Consequently Jℓ​ annihilates every simple module on the left. Conversely, annihilating R/L forces an element to belong to the maximal left ideal L. Thus
Jℓ​=⋂M simple left​AnnR​(M),
(1)
which shows that Jℓ​ is a two-sided ideal.
For j∈Jℓ​ and r∈R, the left ideal R(1−rj) cannot be proper: a containing maximal left ideal would also contain rj and hence 1. Therefore u(1−rj)=1 for some u∈R. Here u=1+urj also differs from 1 by an element of Jℓ​, so it too has a left inverse v. Multiplying the first equality by v gives 1−rj=v; hence (1−rj)u=1. We have proved that 1−rj is a unit, not just left invertible.
The identity
(1−ba)−1=1+b(1−ab)−1a
(2)
is verified by multiplying on either side, using (1−ab)−1(1−ab)=(1−ab)(1−ab)−1=1. It implies that 1−jr is a unit as well. If j were outside a maximal right ideal I, then I+jR=R, so 1−jr∈I for some r. A proper right ideal cannot contain a unit, giving a contradiction. Thus Jℓ​ belongs to every maximal right ideal. Applying the same argument to the opposite ring proves the reverse inclusion. This proves the Jacobson radical is independent of handedness:
J(R)=L maximal left⋂​L=I maximal right⋂​I.​
(3)
For the zero ring the intersections are interpreted as the whole ring, so the equality remains valid.
If a two-sided ideal N satisfies Nm=0, then for x∈N and r∈R, (rx)m=0. The finite geometric series ∑i=0m−1​(rx)i inverts 1−rx. The unit criterion for the Jacobson radical, also proved above by the maximal-ideal argument, gives N⊆J(R). This assertion concerns a nilpotent ideal; arbitrary nilpotent elements of a noncommutative ring need not lie in its Jacobson radical.
For the formal power series ring over the p-adic integers, a series f=∑i≥0​ai​ti is a unit exactly when a0​ is a unit of Zp​. Necessity follows by comparing constant coefficients. If a0​ is invertible, construct the inverse recursively by b0​=a0−1​ and bm​=−a0−1​∑i=1m​ai​bm−i​. Therefore the nonunits are exactly the series whose constant coefficient lies in pZp​. They form the unique maximal ideal (p,t), and the quotient is Fp​. Hence
J(Zp​[[t]])=(p,t).​
(4)
