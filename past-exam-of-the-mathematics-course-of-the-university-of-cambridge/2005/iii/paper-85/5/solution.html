In a commutative ring, if xm=0, then the principal ideal (x) satisfies (x)m=0. Thus a semiprime ring has no nonzero nilpotent elements. Conversely, if Im=0 for an ideal I, then every x∈I satisfies xm=0; absence of nonzero nilpotent elements forces I=0. Hence commutative semiprimeness is exactly reducedness.
For a prime ideal P, let S=R∖P. Every ideal I of RP​=S−1R is the extension of its contraction Ic: if r/s∈I, multiply by the unit s/1 to get r/1∈I, so r∈Ic. If R is Noetherian, write Ic=(r1​,…,rm​); their images generate I. This proves that RP​ is Noetherian.
The extended ideal PRP​ is proper. Indeed 1=p/s would imply u(s−p)=0 for some u∈/P, and then us=up∈P, contradicting primeness and u,s∈/P. A fraction with numerator outside P is invertible, with inverse s/r. A fraction with numerator in P lies in the proper ideal PRP​ and cannot be invertible. Thus the nonunits are precisely PRP​, making it the unique maximal ideal. Therefore
RP​ is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal PRP​.​

To prove reducedness is detected by prime localizations, let x be a nilpotent element of R. Its image in every RQ​ is zero because these localizations are semiprime, hence reduced. If x=0, its annihilator Ann(x) is a proper ideal; choose a maximal ideal Q containing it. The zero criterion for localization says x/1=0 in RQ​ only if sx=0 for some s∈/Q, which would put s in Ann(x)⊆Q, a contradiction. Hence x=0, and R is semiprime. This argument does not need Noetherianity.
Primeness is not detected in the same way. Take R=k×k. Its two prime ideals are P1​=0×k and P2​=k×0, and its corresponding localizations are both isomorphic to the field k. To see these are all the prime ideals, the orthogonal idempotents e1​,e2​ have product zero, so a prime ideal contains one of them and is then the kernel of the other projection. Both localizations are prime rings, but R is not prime: e1​,e2​=0 and e1​e2​=0.
