The spatial coordinates are cyclic because the flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker metric depends only on time. Differentiating the printed affine Lagrangian gives the canonical momentum
Pic​=−mgiν​x˙ν=−ma2x˙i.
(1)
The Euler-Lagrange equations give dPic​/dτ=∂L/∂xi=0, so
Pc=constant.​
(2)
This is the conserved quantity associated with spatial translation invariance. The overall minus sign in the stated action makes this canonical covector opposite to the usual future-directed mechanical momentum; it does not alter the geodesic trajectories or the conservation result. The canonical momentum sign in the affine particle action will be kept explicit below.

For tangent vectors X,Y on a slice, the extrinsic curvature is K(X,Y)=g(∇X​n,Y). Differentiating g(n,Y)=0 and using metric compatibility gives K(X,Y)=−g(n,∇X​Y). Since the connection is torsion-free,

Double contraction of the projected Gauss equation, with the timelike-normal sign and curvature convention in the question, gives
R(3)=qμρqνσRμνρσ(4)​−K2+Kμν​Kμν.
(18)
Here qμν=gμν+nμnν. Expanding the two projectors gives
qμρqνσRμνρσ(4)​=R(4)+2Rμν(4)​nμnν.
(19)
The four-normal contraction vanishes by Riemann antisymmetry. Because gμν​nμnν=−1, the right side is 2Gμν(4)​nμnν. Rearranging proves the Hamiltonian constraint identity:
Gμν(4)​nμnν=21​[R(3)+K2−Kμν​Kμν].​
(20)
For flat FRW, R(3)=0, K2=9H2 and Kμν​Kμν=3H2, so Gnn​=3H2. Einstein's equation is therefore the first Friedmann equation, 3H2=8πGρ, or 3H2=8πGρ+Λ if a cosmological constant is placed separately in Einstein's equation.
The scalar R(3) measures intrinsic spatial curvature, K2 measures squared volume expansion, and the norm of Kij​ measures directional expansion including shear. More generally Kij​=Kqij​/3+sij​ with trace-free sij​ gives Kij​Kij=K2/3+sij​sij. The shear vanishes in FRW, leaving the positive 3H2 contribution. This is an instantaneous constraint on initial data, not the separate acceleration equation.

Use H=a′/a and the Fourier convention ∇→ik. A valid linearization requires all components of hij​ to be small: the printed small-determinant condition alone is insufficient, as the matrix diag(1,1,0) has zero determinant without being small. This is small determinant does not imply a small metric perturbation.
Define the density contrast δ=(ρ−ρˉ​)/ρˉ​ and the scalar velocity potential by vi=∂iθ, or vki​=ikiθk​. Hence ∇⋅v=−k2θ. To first order, u0=a−1, ui=a−1vi and gij=a−2(δij−hij). The product uiuj is second order. Substitution into the perfect fluid stress-energy tensor gives
T00=a−2ρˉ​(1+δ),T0i=a−2(1+w)ρˉ​ikiθ,Tij=a−2wρˉ​[(1+δ)δij−hij].​
(21)
Here the equation of state is used locally, so the pressure perturbation is wρˉ​δ.
The contraction in ∇μ​T0μ=0 is
∂μ​T0μ+Γμν0​Tνμ+Γμνμ​T0ν=0.
(22)
Using the displayed connections gives, through first order,
ρ′+3H(ρ+P)+(ρˉ​+Pˉ)(∇⋅v+21​h′)=0.
(23)
In particular Γμ0μ​=4H+h′/2; the spatial connection contraction adds 3HP+Pˉh′/2, while differentiating a−2ρ supplies −2Hρ. Subtract the background relation ρˉ​′+3H(1+w)ρˉ​=0. The background terms multiplying δ cancel, leaving
δ′−(1+w)k2θ+21​(1+w)h′=0.​
(24)
For comoving cold dark matter, w=0 and θ=0, so δc′​=−h′/2 and δc​+h/2 is constant at each spatial point. The proper-volume element of a comoving cell is a3det(I+h)​d3x=a3(1+h/2)d3x. Conserved mass in the cell then gives the density change opposite to the perturbation of its volume. In comoving synchronous coordinates the linear CDM density change is a volume change, not transport across cell boundaries.

There is a conformal-time normalization error in the printed density parameter. For physical background density, H=H/a, so the cosmological density parameter is
Ωc​=3H28πGρˉ​c​​=3H28πGa2ρˉ​c​​.
(25)
The printed definition lacks the numerator's a2. The scale factor in a conformal-time density parameter is essential for Ωc​≃1 in the intended flat matter-dominated approximation.
During matter domination, pressureless matter supplies both the expansion and the gravitational source: a∝τ2, H=2/τ and Ωc​≃1 when CDM is taken to constitute that matter. Thus
δc′′​+τ2​δc′​−τ26​δc​=0.
(26)
Try δc​∝τp. Its characteristic polynomial is p(p−1)+2p−6=(p−2)(p+3), giving
δc​=Cg​τ2+Cd​τ−3=Dg​a+Dd​a−3/2.​
(27)
Radiation-era photon pressure and radiation perturbations affect the metric source, and matter alone does not dominate the expansion, so this matter-only closure and its power laws are inadequate before equality. At late acceleration the growth also ceases to follow a∝τ2 and slows. The question's claim that the differential form itself is valid only in matter domination is too strong: for subhorizon pressureless matter with smooth dark energy it still applies with the correct time-dependent H and Ωm​. Indeed, the pressureless growth equation in proper time is δ¨+2Hδ˙−4πGρˉ​m​δ=0 for smooth dark energy; substituting d/dtproper​=a−1d/dτ gives δ′′+Hδ′−4πGa2ρˉ​m​δ=0, the same conformal differential form. It is the matter-dominated coefficients and simple solutions that are restricted to the stated interval.
Outside the radiation-era horizon, the regular growing adiabatic mode has nearly constant curvature, while its comoving-synchronous density perturbation begins at gradient order k2τ2. Equivalently its metric trace grows as τ2 and the volume relation in (i) gives δc​∝τ2. This is a relativistic superhorizon solution, not instantaneous Newtonian communication between causally disconnected regions. Inside the horizon, pressure makes the dominant radiation oscillate and its gravitational potential decay, while CDM self-gravity is still subdominant. CDM then grows only weakly, logarithmically in the more accurate Mészáros effect; the stated freezing approximation replaces that slow growth by a constant.
Assume all modes being compared start outside the horizon at common τi​, and define τh​=2π/k, keq​=2π/τeq​ and G0​=(τ0​/τi​)2. Modes with k≤keq​ never undergo the radiation-era frozen interval, so their growth is G0​. For k>keq​, multiply growth before horizon entry, freezing until equality, and subsequent matter growth:
T(k)=(τi​τh​​)2(τeq​τ0​​)2=G0​(kkeq​​)2.
(28)
Thus the frozen-radiation approximation to the matter transfer function is
T(k)≃G0​{1,(keq​/k)2,​k≤keq​,k>keq​.​​
(29)
This T includes total growth because the question defines it directly between two density contrasts. The usual transfer normalized to one on large scales is T/G0​.
Power is the variance of a Fourier amplitude, so P0​=∣T∣2Pi​. The prescribed primordial density spectrum gives
P0​(k)≃AG02​{k,keq4​k−3,​k≤keq​,k>keq​.​​
(30)
It turns over at the comoving equality-horizon scale 2π/keq​=τeq​. The turnover and reduced small-scale growth help organize the broad spectrum of galaxy clustering, clusters and superclusters; they do not select one unique object size. The dimensionless power k3P0​/(2π2) is approximately flat above equality in this crude approximation, so a decreasing dimensional spectrum does not imply no small-scale structures. Restoring radiation-era logarithmic growth adds logarithmic corrections to the small-scale tail. Baryon acoustic features, a smooth equality transition, late growth suppression and nonlinear halo formation are absent from this simple linear broken-power approximation.

For an adiabatic mode the perturbation of baryon number per photon is zero. Since nγ​∝T3, ργ​∝T4 and nonrelativistic ρb​∝nb​, the common temperature/entropy perturbation gives δb​=3δT/T and δγ​=4δT/T. Hence δγ​=4δb​/3.
Let q=ane​σT​, H=a′/a and R=4ρˉ​γ​/(3ρˉ​b​). In tight coupling, the relative velocity is small but its drag force need not vanish, so it must be eliminated before setting the two velocities equal. Multiply the photon Euler equation by R and add the baryon Euler equation. Their opposite drag terms cancel, even with the common drag-prefactor typo in the printed equations. The result at common velocity potential θ is
(1+R)θ′+Hθ+R(δγ​/4−σγ​)=0.
(31)
Together with continuity this gives
δγ′​=34​k2θ,θ′=−3cs2​(41​δγ​−σγ​+RH​θ),​
(32)
cs2​=3(1+R)R​=31​(1+4ρˉ​γ​3ρˉ​b​​)−1.​
(33)
The same photon-baryon sound speed follows from δP=δργ​/3 and δρ=δργ​+δρb​: using the adiabatic density relation gives δP/δρ=cs2​.
The Euler bracket in the original PDF prints a/a′; the derivation requires a′/a=H. Its baryon continuity equation also needs a prime on δb​. Finally, for the defined velocity potential v=ikθ, physical Thomson drag is −q(θγ​−θb​), without an extra k−2: taking a velocity divergence introduces −k2 on both sides, which cancels on returning to the potential equation. This is photon opacity normalization for a velocity potential. These errors should not be concealed by reproducing a dimensionally inconsistent equation.

δγ′′​=34​k2θγ′​=−3k2​δγ​+34​k2σγ​.

(35)

δγ′′​+154​τc​k2δγ′​+3k2​δγ​=0.​
(36)
To solve it, put Γ=4k2τc​/15 and δγ​=uexp[−21​∫τΓ(s)ds]. Direct differentiation, including both derivatives of the prefactor, gives

δγ​(k,τ)≃[A(k)cos3​kτ​+B(k)sin3​kτ​]exp[−kD2​(τ)k2​],​

Let q∗​=kτdec​/3​ and ED​=exp(−k2/kD2​) at last scattering. For random oscillator amplitudes, define spectra PA​,PB​ and cross-spectrum PAB​ using the same Fourier normalization as the density spectrum. Averaging the solution's squared modulus gives
Pδγ​​=ED2​[PA​cos2q∗​+PB​sin2q∗​+2RePAB​sinq∗​cosq∗​].
(40)
Consequently the intrinsic-temperature source has variance spectrum Pδγ​​/16. A common acoustic phase is necessary for pronounced peaks in the variance. Equal independent sine/cosine amplitudes would erase them. A regular growing adiabatic mode begins with negligible velocity outside the sound horizon and selects approximately the cosine phase, B≃0, preserving a cos2q∗​ sequence. This illustrates coherent initial phases are needed for acoustic variance peaks.
From continuity, θγ​=3δγ′​/(4k2). Ignoring the small damping correction in this velocity relation, the coherent cosine solution gives
θγ​≃−4k3​​AED​sinq∗​,vγ​=ikθγ​.
(41)
The Doppler CMB anisotropy source therefore has directional variance 3μ2PA​ED2​sin2q∗​/16, where μ=n^⋅k^. Its velocity maxima lie between density maxima: compression and bulk motion are a quarter acoustic period apart.
Angular projection must also be included. Let χ∗​=τ0​−τdec​, assume a thin last-scattering surface, and choose the emission phase eikχ∗​μ. The coherent intrinsic-plus-Doppler radial response is proportional to
Θℓ​(k)=4ED​​[cosq∗​jℓ​(kχ∗​)−3​sinq∗​jℓ′​(kχ∗​)].
(42)
The derivative follows from iμeixμ=∂x​eixμ and the velocity sign above. A reversed line-of-sight convention reverses the corresponding Doppler sign consistently. The CMB angular power spectrum is Cℓ​=(2/π)∫k2dkPA​(k)∣Θℓ​(k)∣2 in this simplified model. Squaring the combined response keeps its correlated density–velocity cross term; adding two independent angular powers would discard that correlation. The derivative Bessel projection also spreads the Doppler contribution differently from the density contribution.
The sound horizon here is rs​≃τdec​/3​. Intrinsic acoustic extrema occur near krs​=nπ, mapping roughly to ℓn​≃nπχ∗​/rs​, with interleaved velocity extrema and projection broadening. Cosmic microwave background diffusion damping suppresses both contributions exponentially at k≳kD​, or multipoles ℓ≳kD​χ∗​. Modes outside the sound horizon have no completed acoustic oscillation.
The metric integral represents gravitational redshift, including contributions that in another gauge are separated into endpoint Sachs-Wolfe effects and Integrated Sachs-Wolfe effects. It is not correct to call the entire synchronous metric integral only a late-time integrated effect. Large-scale metric/curvature fluctuations remain important where photon pressure cannot redistribute matter causally and produce the broad large-angle signal rather than just the pressure-oscillation sequence. Potential evolution during radiation-to-matter transition also drives and shifts acoustic amplitudes near horizon entry; late accelerating-era evolution adds a large-angle integrated signal. These effects are excluded by the oscillator's instruction to ignore metric perturbations. Even if the Newtonian-gauge potential is constant in matter domination, synchronous hij′​ need not vanish, and the complete observable requires the consistent density, velocity and metric combination.
