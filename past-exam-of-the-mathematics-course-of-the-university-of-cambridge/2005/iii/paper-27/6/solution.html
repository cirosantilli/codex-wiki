A measurable cardinal is an uncountable cardinal κ carrying a kappa-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter U on κ. An elementary embedding j:M→N preserves all first-order formulas with parameters:
M⊨φ(a1​,…,ar​)⟺N⊨φ(j(a1​),…,j(ar​)).
(10)
For a measurable κ, the Łoś theorem gives an elementary map from the universe into its ultrapower. Countable completeness makes that ultrapower well founded, so the Mostowski collapse gives a transitive target M and an ultrapower embedding j:V→M. The least moved ordinal, its critical point of an elementary embedding, is κ: every function into an ordinal below κ is constant on a U-large set by kappa-completeness, whereas the identity function on κ represents an ordinal above every constant ordinal below κ and below the image of κ. Conversely a suitably available embedding with critical point κ defines the measure U={X⊆κ:κ∈j(X)}.
The identity V→V is elementary. In ZFC there is no nonidentity elementary class embedding j:V→V, by the Kunen inconsistency theorem. Here the embedding is a class for which restrictions to sets and the critical sequence are available, as in the usual class-theoretic formulation. The axiom of choice is part of this conclusion; it is not a claimed contradiction for the choice-free Reinhardt-cardinal hypothesis.
Here is the stationary-set proof. A nonidentity j has a least moved ordinal κ; if all ordinals were fixed, induction on rank would fix every set. Put κ0​=κ, κn+1​=j(κn​) and λ=supn<ω​κn​. Since j fixes ω and shifts this sequence, j(λ)=λ. It therefore fixes the regular successor cardinal δ=λ+. The stationarity of ordinals of prescribed cofinality gives the stationary set
S={β<δ:cf(β)=ω}.
(11)
Use the stationary partition at a successor cardinal to split S into κ disjoint stationary pieces ⟨Sα​:α<κ⟩, merging pieces if necessary. Applying j gives a partition ⟨Tα​:α<j(κ)⟩ of the same S into stationary pieces. The additional piece Tκ​ is stationary.
The closure points
C={β<δ:(∀γ<β) j(γ)<β}
(12)
form a club set. For unboundedness, repeatedly close a bound under the values of j below it and take the supremum of the resulting countable sequence; regularity of δ keeps it below δ. Closure under limits is immediate. Choose β∈C∩Tκ​ and an increasing sequence ⟨βn​:n<ω⟩ cofinal in β. Then
j(β)=supn<ω​j(βn​)≤β,
(13)
while every ordinal satisfies j(β)≥β, so j(β)=β. There is an α<κ with β∈Sα​. Since j(α)=α, elementarity gives β=j(β)∈j(Sα​)=Tα​, contrary to disjointness from Tκ​.
