An ultraproduct assembles a family of nonempty first-order structures ⟨Mi​:i∈I⟩ in one fixed language, using an ultrafilter U to specify which coordinate statements hold almost everywhere. Its universe is
∏i∈I​Mi​/U,f∼U​g ⟺ {i:f(i)=g(i)}∈U.
(8)
Functions are interpreted coordinatewise, and an r-ary relation holds on [f1​],…,[fr​] when its coordinate truth set belongs to U. Changing representatives changes such a truth set only off a U-large set, so these definitions are well defined. A principal ultrafilter simply recovers its distinguished factor; a nonprincipal ultrafilter can produce new elements and structures.
The fundamental result is the Łoś theorem:
∏i​Mi​/U⊨φ([f1​],…,[fr​])⟺{i:Mi​⊨φ(f1​(i),…,fr​(i))}∈U.
(9)
Prove it by structural induction on first-order formulas. Atomic formulas are the definition. Intersections handle conjunction, while exactly one of a set and its complement belongs to an ultrafilter, handling negation. For the existential step, if the coordinate existential truth set is in U, use choice to select witnesses there and arbitrary values elsewhere; their product function is an ultraproduct witness. Conversely a product witness makes the coordinate existential truth set contain a U-large set. Other connectives and universal quantification follow.
In an ultrapower, all factors are one structure M. The diagonal map a↦[constant a] is an elementary embedding by the Łoś theorem. For a nonprincipal U on ω, the class of n↦n in an ultrapower of the standard natural numbers is larger than every constant natural number, since each tail belongs to U. This gives a nonstandard model of Peano arithmetic. Similarly, an ultraproduct of finite fields Fpi​​ with distinct primes tending to infinity has characteristic zero: for every fixed positive integer m, the equation m1=0 fails in all sufficiently late factors. An infinite structure can therefore arise from finite factors.
Ultraproducts prove the compactness theorem. If each finite subset Δ of a theory T has a model MΔ​, index these models by [T]<ω. The cones {Δ:φ∈Δ} have the finite intersection property. Extend their filter to an ultrafilter using the ultrafilter lemma. The Łoś theorem makes the resulting ultraproduct satisfy every φ∈T. This construction also gives elementary extensions realizing prescribed finitely satisfiable types.
A useful stronger property is countable saturation of an ultraproduct over omega. Let a countable type {φn​(x,aˉn​):n<ω} be finitely satisfiable in an ultraproduct over a nonprincipal U on ω. Choose coordinate representatives of its parameters. Let En​ be the coordinates where the first n formulas have a simultaneous witness; then En​∈U, the En​ decrease, and we can replace En​ by En​∩[n,∞). At coordinate i, choose a witness to the first m(i) formulas, where m(i) is the largest n≤i with i∈En​, taking an arbitrary value if there is none. For each fixed n, the set where m(i)≥n is U-large. Its class realizes the entire type. This proof uses countable incompleteness of U, not arbitrary completeness or automatic saturation at larger cardinals.
Finally, complete ultrafilters connect ultraproducts to large cardinals. A countably complete ultrafilter prevents an externally infinite descending membership chain in an ultrapower of a well-founded universe: intersect the countably many coordinate truth sets and obtain an impossible descending chain at one coordinate. A kappa-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter on κ consequently supplies the ultrapower embedding associated with a measurable cardinal. Thus the same construction supports both elementary compactness arguments and large-cardinal embeddings, with the completeness hypothesis deciding which well-foundedness and closure properties survive.
