Work over the finite field F2​, and write Ai​ for row i of the PDF's 12×12 matrix. The first row has Hamming weight eleven; all other rows have Hamming weight seven. To check their intersections economically, index the last eleven columns modulo eleven. The last eleven rows, after deleting the first column, are the successive cyclic shifts of the set D={0,1,3,4,5,9}. Direct calculation gives
tD∩(D+t)​1{1,4,5}​2{0,3,5}​3{1,3,4}​4{4,5,9}​5{3,5,9}​​.
(1)
The intersection sizes for −t equal those for t. Thus two different rows among A2​,…,A12​ meet in four positions, including their shared first coordinate, whereas A1​ meets each of these rows in six positions. All these intersection sizes are even numbers, while each row weight is an odd number. Consequently AAT=I. The first row and column agree, and the entry of the last eleven-by-eleven block depends only on the sum of its row and column indices modulo eleven, because successive rows are left shifts. Thus A is a symmetric matrix, so A2=I.
The generator matrix G=(I∣A) has rank twelve, and
GGT=I+AAT=0.
(2)
Hence its linear code C lies in its dual code C⊥. Both have dimension twelve, so C=C⊥: it is a self-dual code. Moreover AG=(A∣A2)=(A∣I), and A is an invertible matrix, so this is another generator matrix for the same code. In the column parity-check matrix convention of the question,
C=C⊥,G′=(A∣I),H=(IA​),GH=0.​
(3)
The matrix H has rank twelve, so its nullspace condition cH=0 characterizes C exactly.
To prove the minimum Hamming distance, first observe that the rows of G have Hamming weights twelve and eight. For binary vectors,
wt(v+w)=wt(v)+wt(w)−2∣supp(v)∩supp(w)∣.
(4)
Every partial sum of generator rows is orthogonal to every further generator row, by GGT=0. Its intersection with that row therefore contains an even number of positions. Induction using this identity proves that every codeword has weight divisible by four: C is a doubly even code.
The systematic-matrix proof of extended Golay distance now excludes weight four. Write a codeword as (u,uA) and put k=wt(u). If its total weight were four, 1≤k≤4. For k=1, the right half is a row of A and has weight seven or eleven. For k=2, the right half is the sum of two distinct rows; the intersection counts above give weight 11+7−2⋅6=6 or 7+7−2⋅4=6. For k=3, the right half would be a unit vector, but then u=(uA)A would be a row of A, of weight seven or eleven. For k=4, the right half would vanish, contradicting invertibility of A. Thus no nonzero word has weight four. Every nonzero weight is consequently at least eight, and any generator row other than the first attains eight. Since the minimum Hamming distance of a linear code is its least nonzero weight,
d(C)=8,C has parameters [24,12,8].​
(5)
Define the length-23 binary Golay code C23​ as the punctured code obtained by deleting the first coordinate of this extended binary Golay code. This puncturing is injective on C, because a nonzero word in its kernel would have weight one. Thus dimC23​=12, and deleting one coordinate gives d(C23​)≥7. The sum of the first two generator rows has weight eight and first coordinate one, so its puncture has weight seven. Therefore d(C23​)=7. In fact any coordinate can be punctured: every left coordinate except the first occurs in a weight-eight generator row, the first occurs in the sum just used, and every right coordinate occurs in at least one of the weight-eight generator rows.
The radius-three Hamming balls around the 212 words of C23​ are disjoint, since two centers in intersecting balls would have Hamming distance at most six. Each ball contains
∑j=03​(j23​)=1+23+253+1771=2048=211
(6)
words. Their combined size is 212211=223, the size of the entire binary Hamming space. Hence
C23​ is a perfect [23,12,7] code, correcting three errors.​
(7)
For decoding in C, write the received word as y=c+(p,q), where (p,q) is the unknown error in its two halves. Its syndrome is
s=yH=p+qA,t=sA=pA+q.
(8)
Let ei​ denote the length-twelve unit vector. For bounded-distance decoding, the three-error syndrome decoding of extended Golay code is the following finite search, returning the first successful candidate error:
  • If wt(s)≤3, return (s,0).
  • Otherwise, if some i satisfies wt(s+Ai​)≤2, return (s+Ai​,ei​).
  • Otherwise, compute t=sA; if wt(t)≤3, return (0,t).
  • Otherwise, if some i satisfies wt(t+Ai​)≤2, return (ei​,t+Ai​).
  • If no test succeeds, declare that no error of weight at most three has this syndrome.
Every returned candidate has total Hamming weight at most three and the required syndrome. Conversely, an error of total weight at most three has a half of weight zero or one. The tests exhaust respectively q=0, q=ei​, p=0, and p=ei​, so they find every such error. Two distinct successful candidates would differ by a nonzero codeword of weight at most six, contradicting d(C)=8. Thus the result is unique, and the decoded word is y+(p,q). The condition wt(s)≥3 still allows the first test when the weight is exactly three; for larger syndrome weight begin with the second test. A large syndrome weight alone does not imply more than three errors.
The decoder corrects every error of weight at most three; failure means that the received word is outside all radius-three decoding balls. Unlike its puncture, C is not a perfect code: its radius-three balls cover only 212(1+24+276+2024)=212⋅2325<224 words. Without the three-error assumption, a successful test identifies a nearby codeword but need not identify the transmitted word.
For an arbitrary received word, a complete minimum-distance decoding fallback is to enumerate q∈F212​ and minimize wt(s+qA)+wt(q). Every error with the observed syndrome has exactly the form (s+qA,q), so a minimizer gives a nearest codeword y+(s+qA,q). Report all minimizers if there is a tie. This also decodes outside the guaranteed radius in the nearest-word sense, while making no unsupported claim that the transmitted word is determined. Thus a syndrome of weight at least three alone is insufficient for guaranteed recovery; either the three-error hypothesis or a choice among nearest words is needed.
