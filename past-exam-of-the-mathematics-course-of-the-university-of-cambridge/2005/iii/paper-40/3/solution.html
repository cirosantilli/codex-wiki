For a finite bimatrix game, let A,B∈Rr×s be the two players' payoff matrices. A pair of mixed strategies (p,q)∈Δr​×Δs​ is a Nash equilibrium if
pTAq≥(p′)TAqfor all p′∈Δr​,pTBq≥pTBq′for all q′∈Δs​.
(11)
Here Δr​ is the probability simplex on the first player's pure actions, and similarly for Δs​. The two payoff matrices need not be negatives of each other. Nash's theorem asserts that every finite two-person game has an equilibrium in mixed strategies.
We prove it using the Brouwer gain-map proof of bimatrix equilibrium. Put u=pTAq, v=pTBq and define the positive pure-deviation gains
gi​(p,q)=max{0,(Aq)i​−u},hj​(p,q)=max{0,(pTB)j​−v}.
(12)
Write G=∑i​gi​, H=∑j​hj​ and define
T(p,q)=((1+Gpi​+gi​​)i=1r​,(1+Hqj​+hj​​)j=1s​).
(13)
Every coordinate is nonnegative and each player's coordinates sum to one. The map is continuous, since the payoffs and positive-part function are continuous and both denominators are at least one. Thus T is a continuous self-map of the nonempty compact convex set Δr​×Δs​. The Brouwer fixed-point theorem supplies a fixed point (p,q).
At that fixed point the first coordinate equations give gi​=Gpi​. Suppose G>0. For every i with pi​>0, this makes gi​>0, so (Aq)i​−u=gi​=Gpi​. But averaging the payoff excess over the support gives
0=∑i​pi​((Aq)i​−u)=G∑i​pi2​>0,
(14)
a contradiction. Therefore G=0, and every gi​ is zero. Each pure action has payoff at most u, so every mixed deviation, being a convex combination of pure actions, has payoff at most u too. The same argument for hj​=Hqj​ gives H=0 and excludes every unilateral deviation by the second player. The fixed point is therefore a Nash equilibrium, proving the stated theorem without a zero-sum assumption.
