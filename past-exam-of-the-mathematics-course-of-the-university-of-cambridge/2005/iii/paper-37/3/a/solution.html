Yes: a deterministic LOCC protocol exists for every allowed value of the parameter. Here is an explicit implementation, so existence does not depend on merely quoting a pure-state conversion theorem.
First convert the input Bell state to ∣Φ+⟩=(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​ by local unitary operations:
(ZA​⊗XB​)∣Ψ−⟩=∣Φ+⟩.
(25)
Write c=cosϕ and s=sinϕ. Alice performs the two-outcome measurement in quantum mechanics with Kraus operators
M0​=(c0​0s​),M1​=(s0​0c​).
(26)
They define a physical measurement in quantum mechanics because
M0†​M0​+M1†​M1​=(c2+s2)I=I.
(27)
Acting on ∣Φ+⟩, the unnormalized branch vectors are
(M0​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​c∣00⟩+s∣11⟩​,(M1​⊗I)∣Φ+⟩=2​s∣00⟩+c∣11⟩​.
(28)
Both branches have probability 1/2. Alice communicates the outcome to Bob. If it is one, both apply the Pauli X gate; this turns the normalized second branch into c∣00⟩+s∣11⟩. If it is zero, neither applies this correction. Finally Bob applies a Pauli X gate in either branch, obtaining
c∣01⟩+s∣10⟩.​
(29)
Every outcome produces the same target pure state, so this is deterministic local operations and classical communication, not a successful branch selected by postselection. This two-outcome LOCC dilution of a Bell pair remains valid at ϕ=0, where the output is a product state, and at ϕ=π/4, where it is another Bell state.
The target Schmidt coefficients are c,s, while those of the input are both 1/2​. For an interior value 0<ϕ<π/4, local unitary operations alone could not change these Schmidt coefficients; the local measurement in quantum mechanics is the step that reduces the entanglement.
