Let a density operator on a Hilbert space HS​ have the spectral decomposition
ρS​=∑j=1r​λj​∣j⟩S​⟨j∣,λj​>0,∑j​λj​=1.
(5)
Introduce an auxiliary Hilbert space HR​ with an orthonormal basis containing r vectors ∣j⟩R​, and set
∣Ω⟩SR​=∑j=1r​λj​​∣j⟩S​∣j⟩R​.
(6)
This vector has norm one. Its partial trace is
TrR​∣Ω⟩⟨Ω∣=∑j,k​λj​λk​​∣j⟩S​⟨k∣⟨k∣j⟩R​=ρS​.
(7)
This explicitly constructs a purification of a density operator. The same construction works for an arbitrary trace-class density operator: its nonzero eigenvalues form a finite or countable summable family, and the displayed vector converges in the Hilbert space norm. The entropy inequality below is understood with finite Von Neumann entropies, avoiding undefined differences of infinities.
Apply this construction with S=AB to obtain a joint pure state on ABC. The Schmidt decomposition across AB:C, A:BC and B:AC respectively gives
S(AB)=S(C),S(A)=S(BC),S(B)=S(AC).
(8)
Now apply Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy to the reduced density matrices on BC and AC:
S(A)=S(BC)≤S(B)+S(C),S(B)=S(AC)≤S(A)+S(C).
(9)
These inequalities say S(A)−S(B)≤S(C) and S(B)−S(A)≤S(C). Replacing S(C) by S(AB) proves the Araki–Lieb inequality:
∣S(A)−S(B)∣≤S(A,B).​
(10)
The subadditivity used here concerns the actual reduced density operators, rather than statistical independence. In particular it follows from nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy, since
D(ρBC​∥ρB​⊗ρC​)=S(ρB​)+S(ρC​)−S(ρBC​)≥0,
(11)
and similarly for AC. Thus no independence assumption on the purification of a density operator is needed.
