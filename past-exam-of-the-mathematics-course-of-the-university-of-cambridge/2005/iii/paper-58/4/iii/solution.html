The Pauli X gate interchanges the two basis quantum states. Because the bit-flip outcome is a classical unobserved error rather than a coherent superposition, the quantum bit-flip channel gives
ρ2​=(1−p)∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣+pX∣ψ⟩⟨ψ∣X.​
(46)
For ∣ψ⟩=α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩, its explicit density matrix is
ρ2​=((1−p)∣α∣2+p∣β∣2(1−p)βα∗+pαβ∗​(1−p)αβ∗+pβα∗(1−p)∣β∣2+p∣α∣2​).
(47)
For a pure reference quantum state, the square of the paper's quantum fidelity is F2=⟨ψ∣ρ2​∣ψ⟩. Therefore
F2=1−p+p∣⟨ψ∣X∣ψ⟩∣2.
(48)
With ∣ψ⟩=cos(θ/2)∣0⟩+eiϕsin(θ/2)∣1⟩, the expectation is ⟨X⟩=sinθcosϕ, the x component of the Bloch vector. Hence
F2(θ,ϕ)=1−p+psin2θcos2ϕ.
(49)
“All possible quantum states” is interpreted as the rotationally invariant uniform pure-qubit average, with measure dμ=sinθdθdϕ/(4π). Thus
⟨F2⟩​=1−p+4πp​∫0π​sin3θdθ∫02π​cos2ϕdϕ=1−p+4πp​⋅34​⋅π,​
(50)
so the average pure-qubit fidelity of a bit-flip channel is
⟨F2⟩=1−32p​.​
(51)
Equivalently, rotational symmetry gives ⟨rx2​⟩=⟨ry2​⟩=⟨rz2​⟩=1/3, since a pure-state Bloch vector has unit length. The X eigenstates are unchanged as density matrices and have fidelity one, while a computational basis quantum state has F2=1−p; these provide checks on the angular expression. A uniform distribution in θ rather than in solid angle would produce the wrong ensemble average. The requested quantity is the mean squared fidelity, not the square of the mean unsquared fidelity.
