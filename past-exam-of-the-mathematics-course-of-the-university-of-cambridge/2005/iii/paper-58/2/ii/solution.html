Write ϕ=2πn/N+ϵ, with ∣ϵ∣≤δ≪2π/N. Decode the measured bits as above. The probability amplitude of label m is
am​=N1​∑k=0N−1​eikΔm​,Δm​=ϕ−N2πm​.
(19)
Using the finite geometric series,
am​=Nei(N−1)Δm​/2​sin(Δm​/2)sin(NΔm​/2)​,P(m)=N2sin2(Δm​/2)sin2(NΔm​/2)​.
(20)
Interpret removable singularities by continuity. In particular, near-grid quantum phase estimation returns the nearby exact label nmodN with probability
P(n)=N2sin2(ϵ/2)sin2(Nϵ/2)​=1−12N2−1​ϵ2+O(N4ϵ4).​
(21)
This is near one under the stated small-offset assumption, but not exactly one when ϵ=0.
There is also a rigorous error bound. Taking the squared modulus of the sum gives
1−P(n)=N21​∑k,l=0N−1​[1−cos((k−l)ϵ)]≤2N2ϵ2​∑k,l=0N−1​(k−l)2=12N2−1​ϵ2,
(22)
where 1−cosx≤x2/2 and the elementary sums of k and k2 evaluate the last expression. Hence
P(ϕ​=N2π(nmodN)​)≥1−12N2−1​δ2.​
(23)
On that event the circular quantum phase error is at most δ. For other labels, the exact probabilities are
P(m)=N2sin2(ϵ/2+π(n−m)/N)sin2(Nϵ/2)​,m=nmodN.
(24)
Their total is 1−P(n)=O(N2δ2), describing the leakage from the perfect solution. The physical output bit string remains reversed in the printed circuit; this changes its decoding, not these probabilities.
