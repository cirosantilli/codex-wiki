∣k⟩∣u⟩⟼∣k⟩Uk∣u⟩=eikϕ∣k⟩∣u⟩.

Thus the register quantum state is N−1/2∑k​eikϕ∣k⟩, while the target remains ∣u⟩. Define the quantum Fourier transform by FN​∣m⟩=N−1/2∑k​e2πikm/N∣k⟩. Its inverse gives
N1​∑m=0N−1​(∑k=0N−1​eik(ϕ−2πm/N))∣m⟩.

When ϕ=2πn/N, the inner finite geometric series equals N if m≡n(modN) and zero otherwise. Therefore exact quantum phase estimation gives
m=nmodNwith probability one,ϕ​=N2πm​(mod2π).​
(16)
The quantum phase, rather than an unrestricted integer n, is recovered modulo 2π.
The printed circuit has no bit-reversal swaps. Its top control uses U2t−1, and its measured top wire gives the least significant output bit. Write nmodN=∑j=0t−1​2jbj​. Before the top wire's final Hadamard gate, its relative quantum phase is 2t−1ϕ=πn, so that gate produces b0​. The controlled inverse quantum phase gates then subtract its contribution from lower wires. More generally, before the final Hadamard gate on wire j, the relative quantum phase is
2j+12π​(n−∑h=0j−1​2hbh​)=πbj​(mod2π),
(17)
so that wire gives bj​ deterministically. For the three-wire drawing, the correct interpretation is
nmod8=j0​+2j1​+4j2​.​
(18)
Equivalently, its Fourier-inversion circuit is RFN†​, where R reverses the register bits. Relabeling the measured bits or appending swaps gives the usual integer output. This bit convention matters, for example, when quantum phase π produces top-to-bottom bits 001, representing the label four rather than one.
