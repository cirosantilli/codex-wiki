For based maps α:Sp→Y and β:Sq→Y, use the standard cell decomposition
Sp×Sq=(Sp∨Sq)∪w​Dp+q.
(8)
The attaching map w:Sp+q−1→Sp∨Sq is obtained from the boundary of Dp×Dq, collapsing the appropriate coordinate boundaries. The geometric Whitehead product is the homotopy class
[α,β]W​=[(α∨β)∘w]∈πp+q−1​(Y).
(9)
Orient the product cell consistently with the ordinary product orientation. Its class depends only on the two homotopy classes, and it is natural: f∗​[α,β]W​=[f∗​α,f∗​β]W​.
For p,q≥2 it is additive in both variables:
[α+α′,β]W​=[α,β]W​+[α′,β]W​,[α,β+β′]W​=[α,β]W​+[α,β′]W​.
(10)
Thus it is bilinear over Z, and after rationalization of a topological space over Q. In the geometric convention its symmetry is
[α,β]W​=(−1)pq[β,α]W​.
(11)
For a genuine graded Lie convention, put a class in πp​Y in shifted degree p−1 and set {α,β}=(−1)p[α,β]W​. Equivalently transport the Samelson product through reduced suspension and loop space adjunction. Writing ∣α∣=p−1, this bracket has degree zero, satisfies
{α,β}=−(−1)∣α∣∣β∣{β,α},
(12)
and has the graded Jacobi identity
(−1)∣α∣∣γ∣{α,{β,γ}}+(−1)∣β∣∣α∣{β,{γ,α}}+(−1)∣γ∣∣β∣{γ,{α,β}}=0.
(13)
These are two equivalent sign presentations of the same Whitehead product. In particular the square of an odd-dimensional geometric class is two-torsion, whereas an even-dimensional sphere class can have a nonzero rational square. Reduced suspension kills Whitehead products, since the suspended attaching map of the product cell is null-homotopic. No proofs of these remaining algebraic properties are needed here.
Bilinearity has a specific fundamental-group exception. If both inputs lie in π1​Y, the attaching loop of the torus is the commutator, so the operation is ghg−1h−1, with the possible inverse corresponding to reversal of its boundary orientation. This is not a homomorphism in either input in a general nonabelian group.
If one input is g∈π1​Y and the other is β∈πq​Y, q≥2, the product records the action difference g⋅β−β, up to the fixed overall boundary sign. Choose the action convention matching that boundary orientation and write δg​β=g⋅β−β. Then
δg​(β+γ)=δg​β+δg​γ,δgh​β=δg​β+g⋅(δh​β).
(14)
The second formula is a crossed-additivity law, not ordinary additivity in g. It follows by expanding gh⋅β−β=g⋅(h⋅β−β)+(g⋅β−β). Consequently the higher-homotopy variable remains additive, while the fundamental-group variable is generally not. If the action is trivial the mixed product is zero. This explains exactly where the usual bilinearity statement fails.
