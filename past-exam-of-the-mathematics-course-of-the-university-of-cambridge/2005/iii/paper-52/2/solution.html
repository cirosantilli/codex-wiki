With signature (+−−−), the momentum-space Feynman rules for phi-fourth theory follow by expanding the interaction exponential and using free-field Wick contractions. Every internal scalar propagator supplies i/(k2−m02​+i0), every quartic Feynman vertex supplies −iλ0​, and each vertex conserves four-momentum. Integrate every independent loop momentum with d4k/(2π)4 and divide by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor S. Equivalently, assign momenta to all lines and attach (2π)4δ4(∑k) at each vertex before eliminating constrained integrations. A connected amputated graph therefore has the form
S(−iλ0​)Nv​​∫∏r=1L​(2π)4d4ℓr​​∏j=1Nl​​kj2​−m02​+i0i​,
(13)
with the remaining overall momentum-conservation delta function understood. For an unamputated correlation function, retain the external propagators; scattering amplitudes instead use LSZ reduction. The factor 4! in the interaction cancels the number of ways to attach the four identical fields at a vertex, leaving the stated vertex factor.
For a connected Feynman diagram, L=Nl​−Nv​+1. The superficial degree of divergence is the power under simultaneous large rescaling of all independent loop momenta before considering cancellations or divergent subgraphs. Since each integration contributes four powers and each internal propagator removes two,
D=4L−2Nl​=2Nl​−4Nv​+4.
(14)
Counting vertex half-edges gives 4Nv​=2Nl​+Ne​. Substitution yields
D=4−Ne​.​
(15)
The connectedness assumption matters: a disconnected interaction graph with C components instead has the corresponding count D=4C−Ne​. Tree graphs have no loop integral even when this formal power count is nonnegative.
In four dimensions the possible primitively divergent proper vertices have Ne​=2 or 4, with D=2 or 0; odd vertices vanish by the ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry. The divergent local structures are a mass term, a kinetic term and a quartic coupling term. If vacuum diagrams are retained, a vacuum-energy term is also required. Thus one can renormalize using a finite family of operators already allowed by the theory's symmetries: four-dimensional phi-fourth theory is perturbatively renormalizable. The superficial count alone is not an all-orders convergence proof. After divergent subgraphs are subtracted with these counterterms, higher-point graphs have no new overall ultraviolet divergence; a negative overall D does not by itself exclude subdivergences.

For the two-point function, the free propagator is order one, while its first interacting correction is the tadpole diagram, with Nv​=Nl​=L=1, Ne​=2, S=2 and D=2. Its loop integral behaves as ∫d4k/k2 and is quadratically divergent. For the connected four-point function, the lowest graph is the order-λ0​ contact vertex, with Nv​=1,Nl​=L=0 and formal D=0 but no loop divergence. The first loop correction comprises the s,t,u bubble diagrams. Each has Nv​=2,Nl​=2,L=1, Ne​=4, S=2 and D=0, matching its logarithmic integral ∫d4k/(k2)2.
If the full four-point correlation function rather than its connected part is considered, there is also an order-one contribution: the sum of the three Wick contractions into two free propagators, with pairings (12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23). Explicitly, G0(4)​=G12​G34​+G13​G24​+G14​G23​, with Gij​ the free scalar propagator between insertions i,j. These disconnected pairings are drawn in the bottom row of the plate. They contain no loop integral and are not contributions to the proper four-point vertex.
A six-point amplitude in phi-fourth theory has a one-loop triangle Feynman diagram with two external legs at each of three vertices. It has Nv​=3,Nl​=3,L=1, Ne​=6, and D=−2. For a fixed external-leg partition its loop integral contains three scalar denominators, for example
∫(2π)4d4k​(k2−m02​+i0)((k+q1​)2−m02​+i0)((k−q2​)2−m02​+i0)1​.
(16)
After Wick rotation, its ultraviolet radial behavior is ∫∞dkk3/k6=∫∞dk/k3, which converges. The triangle has no proper loop subgraph, so no ultraviolet subdivergence is hidden in this example. It generates no new six-field counterterm. Nonzero mass avoids a separate infrared issue; with massless propagators exceptional external momenta can produce infrared singularities without changing this ultraviolet conclusion.
The bare mass is a coefficient in the regulated action, whereas the physical mass is the pole position of the full propagator. Write the amputated two-point insertion as −iΣ(p2). Summing its insertions gives
G(p)=p2−m02​−Σ(p2)+i0i​,m2=m02​+Σ(m2).
(17)
At order λ0​, only the tadpole contributes. Its factor one half follows directly from Wick pairing: of the four fields at the vertex, 4⋅3 choices attach the two labeled external lines and the remaining pair closes the loop, so 12/4!=1/2. Thus
−iΣ(1)=2−iλ0​​∫(2π)4d4k​k2−m02​+i0i​.
(18)
Rotate k0=ikE0​ through the Feynman contour. The i from the measure and the i in the propagator, together with the denominator sign, give a positive Euclidean integral. With spherical cutoff ∣kE​∣<Λ,
Σ(1)=2λ0​​(2π)42π2​∫0Λ​k2+m02​k3dk​=2(4π)2λ0​​[Λ2−m02​log(1+m02​Λ2​)].
(19)
This is the Wick-rotated cutoff tadpole mass shift, and it is independent of external momentum. Hence to first order,
m2=m02​+2(4π)2λ0​​[Λ2−m02​logm02​Λ2+m02​​]+O(λ02​).​
(20)
For a positive stable quartic coupling the first-order shift is positive. Keeping the measured pole fixed while changing the cutoff requires adjusting the bare mass. At m0​=0 the bracket is understood by its finite limit Λ2. There is no momentum-dependent wave-function correction from this one-loop tadpole; such corrections begin at higher order.
