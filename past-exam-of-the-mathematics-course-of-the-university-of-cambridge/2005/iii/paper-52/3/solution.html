Set ϵ=4−n and a=3/(16π2)=−C. The kinetic term gives the scalar mass dimension (n−2)/2, so the quartic bare coupling has mass dimension 4−n=ϵ. A dimensionless renormalized coupling therefore comes with the factor μϵ=(μ2)2−n/2.
The order-λR2​ proper four-point corrections are precisely the three s,t,u bubble diagrams in the diagram plate, with external partitions (12∣34),(13∣24),(14∣23). Each carries a symmetry factor one half. Its two-propagator integral has logarithmic ultraviolet divergence in four dimensions, represented in dimensional regularization by a simple pole at n=4. That pole's residue is independent of external momenta, so a local quartic counterterm cancels it. The three channels add, rather than describing three separate couplings. The two-point tadpole has no momentum dependence, so it does not supply a one-loop field-normalization correction to the four-point coupling. The first momentum-dependent field correction contributes to this relation only at higher order.
With the supplied residue C=−a, the subtraction relation consequently reads
λ0​=μϵ(λR​+ϵa​λR2​+O(λR3​)).
(21)
A possible scheme-dependent finite subtraction does not change the displayed four-dimensional one-loop coefficient. Keep the regulator and bare coupling fixed while varying the renormalization scale, and define β(λR​)=dλR​/dlogμ. Differentiation gives
0=ϵ(λR​+ϵa​λR2​)+β(λR​)(1+ϵ2a​λR​)+O(λR3​).
(22)
To retain all quadratic terms, put β=−ϵλR​+bλR2​+O(λR3​). The engineering term times the pole factor contributes −2aλR2​, and the remaining quadratic coefficient is a−2a+b. It vanishes only when b=a. Thus
β(λR​)=−(4−n)λR​+16π23​λR2​+O(λR3​).​
(23)
Dropping the product of the engineering term and pole factor would give the wrong sign for the loop term.
At n=4, separation of the one-loop renormalization-group flow gives
λR2​dλR​​=adlogμ,λR​(μ)1​=λs​1​−alogμs​μ​.
(24)
Therefore
λR​(μ)=1−16π23λs​​log(μ/μs​)λs​​.​
(25)
This is the solution of the truncated one-loop equation, not an exact all-orders running law. It applies while the coupling remains sufficiently weak; its Landau pole is at μ=μs​exp[16π2/(3λs​)] for λs​>0.
A renormalization-group fixed point has β(λ∗)=0. For t=log(μ/μs​) and a small perturbation δ=λR​−λ∗, linearization gives
dtdδ​=β′(λ∗)δ+O(δ2),δ(t)≈δ(0)eβ′(λ∗)t.
(26)
Thus β′(λ∗)<0 means attraction as μ→∞, or ultraviolet stability; β′(λ∗)>0 means attraction as μ→0, or infrared stability. If the derivative vanishes, higher terms decide marginal stability. These statements concern the specified coupling direction; other couplings or the mass can be relevant even when this direction is stable.
For ϵ>0, the quadratic renormalization-group beta function has zeros 0 and ϵ/a. At the nonzero zero,
λ∗=aϵ​,β′(λ∗)=−ϵ+2aaϵ​=ϵ>0.
(27)
Consequently
λ∗=(4−n)316π2​is infrared stable in the quartic direction.​
(28)
This is the weak-coupling Wilson-Fisher fixed point, perturbatively controlled near four dimensions. Reaching its full critical limit also requires tuning the mass, the thermal relevant direction at the Wilson-Fisher fixed point.
At n=4, this nonzero fixed point merges with the Gaussian fixed point. Although the derivative at zero vanishes, β=aλR2​>0 for a positive weak coupling, so the running solution tends to zero logarithmically toward the infrared. The four-dimensional critical long-distance limit is Gaussian: the quartic interaction is marginally irrelevant, rather than flowing to a nonzero weakly interacting infrared fixed point. This is the Gaussian infrared limit of positive four-dimensional quartic coupling. It is not ultraviolet asymptotic freedom; the positive coupling grows in the ultraviolet. Nor does it say a massive theory has no interactions at finite energy: the critical or massless scaling regime is the one described by this limiting flow. Extrapolating the one-loop law to arbitrarily high cutoff at fixed positive low-energy coupling encounters its Landau pole; avoiding that pole as the cutoff is removed forces the perturbative coupling toward zero. This motivates the usual triviality conclusion, but the one-loop calculation alone is not a nonperturbative theorem excluding all possible strongly coupled ultraviolet completions.
