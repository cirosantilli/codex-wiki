Use signature (+−−−) and covariant one-particle state normalization ⟨p′∣p⟩=(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−p′). The future scattering condition uses the outgoing asymptotic scalar field:
ϕ(x)⟶Z​ϕout​(x)(x0→+∞),(∂2+m2)ϕout​=0.​
(1)
As with the incoming condition, this is understood in suitably smeared scattering matrix elements, not as unrestricted pointwise strong convergence of interacting and free local operators.
The free incoming field has expansion
ϕin​(x)=∫(2π)32Ek​d3k​[ain​(k)e−ikx+ain†​(k)eikx],[ain​(k),ain†​(p)]=(2π)32Ek​δ3(k−p).
(2)
The creation operator defines ∣p⟩=ain†​(p)∣0⟩, with ain​∣0⟩=0, so ⟨p∣ϕin​(x)∣0⟩=eipx. On the other hand, translation invariance gives
ϕ(x)=eiPxϕ(0)e−iPx,⟨p∣ϕ(x)∣0⟩=eipx⟨p∣ϕ(0)∣0⟩.
(3)
Matching the stable one-particle state component in the asymptotic condition fixes the constant overlap. Choose its state phase so that it is real and nonnegative. Then
⟨p∣ϕ(0)∣0⟩=Z​.​
(4)
The same stable particle appears in the outgoing description, with compatible one-particle phases. Thus Z is the squared overlap measured by wave-function renormalization.
Insert a complete set of four-momentum eigenstates between the fields. For x0>0, translation invariance and Hermiticity give
⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(0)∣0⟩=∑α​e−ipα​x∣⟨α∣ϕ(0)∣0⟩∣2.
(5)
For x0<0, reversing the time-ordered product gives the same sum with eipα​x. Therefore, with continuous-state integrals implicit in the completeness sum,
iΔ(x)=∑α​∣⟨α∣ϕ(0)∣0⟩∣2[θ(x0)e−ipα​x+θ(−x0)eipα​x].
(6)
Here θ is the Heaviside step function.
For the usual spectral representation, take a Lorentz-invariant vacuum, a positive-norm Hilbert space and the future-cone spectrum condition. Also take ⟨0∣ϕ∣0⟩=0, as for the unbroken real scalar field, or subtract this constant and use the connected correlation function. Otherwise the vacuum intermediate state adds a disconnected constant not represented by the ordinary mass-shell measure below.
Let W(x)=⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(0)∣0⟩ for that centered field, and use the Fourier transform convention W(x)=∫d4p(2π)−4e−ipxW(p). Define the Källén–Lehmann spectral density by the distributional equality
W(p)=(2π)4∑α​∣⟨α∣ϕ(0)∣0⟩∣2δ4(p−pα​)=2πθ(p0)ρ(p2).
(7)
Lorentz invariance makes the weight on each future mass shell a function or measure of p2; the spectrum condition gives p2≥0. Its nonnegativity follows from the displayed squared matrix elements. Thus
W(x)=∫0∞​dσρ(σ)∫(2π)32p2+σ​d3p​e−ipx.
(8)
Applying time ordering to this expression mass shell by mass shell proves the Källén–Lehmann spectral representation:
Δ(x)=∫0∞​dσρ(σ)ΔF​(x,σ),ΔF​(p,σ)=p2−σ+i01​.​
(9)
The factor i is outside ΔF​ under the convention in the question.
The stable one-particle state part of completeness is ∫d3p[(2π)32Ep​]−1∣p⟩⟨p∣. Its overlap is Z​, so its contribution to W is Z times the free mass-m two-point function. Hence
ρ(σ)=Zδ(σ−m2)+ρrest​(σ),ρrest​≥0.​
(10)
Other isolated particles as well as multiparticle states can belong to ρrest​. The full scalar propagator accordingly has isolated pole Z/(p2−m2+i0), making Z its pole residue.
Finally use canonical field normalization: the canonical momentum is ϕ˙​ and the equal-time canonical commutation relation is [ϕ˙​(t,x),ϕ(t,y)]=−iδ3(x−y). The vacuum commutator C(x)=W(x)−W(−x) has the same spectral decomposition. For each free mass shell,
∂x0​Cσ​(x)∣x0=0​=−2i​∫(2π)3d3p​(eip⋅x+e−ip⋅x)=−iδ3(x),
(11)
independently of σ. Comparing with the interacting equal-time commutator gives the canonical scalar spectral sum rule:
∫0∞​ρ(σ)dσ=1,0≤Z≤1.​
(12)
For the particle genuinely interpolated by the stated asymptotic field, Z>0; the general nonnegative bound allows a field with zero overlap. The total weight and the upper bound require this canonical normalization and a well-defined distributional equal-time limit. They do not follow from the asymptotic condition alone: rescaling ϕ rescales both Z and the entire spectral measure.
