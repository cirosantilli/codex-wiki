Take the leading real field to be Aei(kc​x+ω0​t)+Bei(−kc​x+ω0​t)+complex conjugate. A spatial translation gives (A,B)↦(eikc​δA,e−ikc​δB); a time translation gives (A,B)↦(eiψA,eiψB); spatial reflection exchanges A and B.
For a monomial AjAkBlBm in the first amplitude equation, covariance requires j−k+l−m=1 and j−k−l+m=1. Hence j−k=1, l−m=0. At linear order only A is allowed, no quadratic monomial satisfies these conditions, and the cubic possibilities are A2A and ABB. Reflection fixes the coefficients in the second equation. Removing the small common linear frequency correction by a rotating frame makes μ real. This derives the two cubic amplitude equations, rather than merely postulating them.
Let r=∣A∣2, s=∣B∣2. Taking real parts gives
r˙=2r(μ−βr​r−γr​s),s˙=2s(μ−βr​s−γr​r).
(35)
A travelling wave has (r,s)=(μ/βr​,0), or its reflection, whenever μ/βr​>0. Its phase evolves at −βi​μ/βr​, shifting the physical oscillation frequency. The nonzero-amplitude radial eigenvalue is −2μ, while the absent mode has real growth rate μ(1−γr​/βr​). Therefore, on the supercritical side,
stable travelling wave: μ>0,βr​>0,γr​>βr​.​
(36)
The phase direction is neutral because of the symmetry, so stability means orbital stability. A branch with μ<0 is radially unstable, whatever the transverse coefficient.
A standing wave has r=s=h=μ/(βr​+γr​)>0. Its two phases evolve together at −(βi​+γi​)h; the relative phase fixes the spatial position of the standing pattern. In the two intensity directions the eigenvalues are −2μ and −2h(βr​−γr​). Thus
stable standing wave: μ>0,βr​+γr​>0,βr​>γr​.​
(37)
Again the symmetry phases are neutral and the conclusion is orbital stability. Equality βr​=γr​ leaves a continuum of mixed intensities at cubic order; equality βr​+γr​=0 removes cubic standing-wave saturation. These degeneracies require higher-order terms and cannot be classified by the cubic truncation. The zero state is linearly stable for μ<0 and unstable for μ>0.
