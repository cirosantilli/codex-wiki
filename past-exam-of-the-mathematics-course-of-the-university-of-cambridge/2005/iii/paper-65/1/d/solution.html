There is an important chain rule qualification in this part. The printed value of γ follows from the expansion. Its printed value of α follows only if transverse differentiation of the translated correction profiles is omitted. For a consistent translation-covariant phase expansion, that omission is generally nonzero.
At the leading transverse threshold let
LC1​=2Bw0′​,LC2​=2Bw0′′​,⟨w0′​Cj​⟩=0,
(10)
where the last conditions fix the phase gauge. Put p=ϕY​, r1​=ϕYY​, s1​=ϕYYY​ and t1​=ϕYYYY​, and use w2​=C1​(ξ)r1​+C2​(ξ)p2. At order ε4 the Fredholm solvability condition gives
NϕT​=λNr1​−2⟨w0′​BDY2​w2​⟩−⟨w0′​DY4​w0​⟩−3⟨w0′​w0​w22​⟩.
(11)
The first two contributions follow directly by differentiating:
DY2​w2​=DY4​w0​=​C1​t1​+2(C1′​+C2​)ps1​+(C1′​+2C2​)r12​+(C1′′​+5C2′​)p2r1​+C2′′​p4,w0′​t1​+w0′′​(4ps1​+3r12​)+6w0′′′​p2r1​+w0′′′′​p4.​
(12)
Choose the reflection-symmetric roll with w0​ even. Then C1​ is odd and C2​ even. Projection onto the odd translation mode eliminates ps1​, r12​ and p4, leaving exactly the symmetry-allowed phase modulation equation. Its coefficients are
γ=N⟨w0′​(2C1​+2C1′′​+w0′​)⟩​,​
(13)
and
αcov​=N6⟨w0′​(w0′′′​+w0​C1​C2​)⟩+2⟨w0′​B(C1′′​+5C2′​)⟩​.​
(14)
If one freezes Cj​ at the unshifted coordinate when taking transverse derivatives, ∂Y2​w2​=C1​t1​+2C2​(r12​+ps1​), and its projection contributes only the γ term. The other two projected terms then give the printed expression αprint​=6⟨w0′​(w0′′′​+w0​C1​C2​)⟩/N. But freezing these profiles is inconsistent with the translated w0​ in L for a general finite phase displacement. The printed expression needs the extra assumption ⟨w0′​B(C1′′​+5C2′​)⟩=0, which is not an identity.
A small-amplitude calculation demonstrates the difference. For a critical roll of leading amplitude η and fast wavenumber κ, the steady equation and J=0 give
w0​κC1​C2​​=ηcos(κξ)−256η3​cos(3κξ)+O(η5),=1−163849η4​+O(η6),r=43​η2+O(η4),=10243η3​sin(3κξ)+O(η5),=1024η3​[−3cos(κξ)+9cos(3κξ)]+O(η5).​
(15)
The third harmonic coefficient in w0​ follows by dividing its cubic forcing −η3cos(3κξ)/4 by the fast eigenvalue −64. Substituting in LCj​ gives the displayed correction profiles; the near-zero first-harmonic eigenvalue in the even problem must be retained to obtain the first harmonic of C2​. Periodic orthogonality then gives
⟨w0′​B(C1′′​+5C2′​)⟩=32768243η6​+O(η8),αcov​−αprint​=8192243η4​+O(η6).
(16)
Thus the supplied nonlinear coefficient is not generally an exact consequence of the finite-amplitude translated-roll expansion. The corrected coefficient above, the printed calculation under its additional freezing assumption, and the genuine near-threshold phase modulation equation are all distinguished explicitly.
