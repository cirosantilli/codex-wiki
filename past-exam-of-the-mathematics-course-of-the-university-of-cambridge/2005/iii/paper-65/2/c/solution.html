The quadratic discrete predator-prey map equals the identity plus the vector field of part (a), so its admissible fixed points are the same O,E,P. Their Jacobian matrices are the identity plus the corresponding continuous-time Jacobian matrices, but map stability requires eigenvalues inside the unit circle rather than the left half-plane.
At O the multipliers are 1/a,0: it is a sink for a>1 and a saddle for a<1. At E they are 2−1/a and (1−a)/b. Therefore
E is a sink⟺31​<a<1,b>1−a.​
(21)
For a<1/3, the prey multiplier is below −1: E is a saddle when b>1−a and a repeller when b<1−a. For 1/3<a<1, b<1−a makes it a saddle. The +1 crossings at a=1 and a+b=1 are the two transcritical bifurcations already encountered. The crossing at a=1/3 is a prey period-doubling bifurcation.
At positive coexistence,
J(P)=(1−b/ac/b​−b1​),T=2−b/a,D=(1−2b)/a.
(22)

(1−s)2p(1−s1+s​)=(1−T+D)+2(1−D)s+(1+T+D)s2.
(23)
The open unit disk maps to Res<0. A real quadratic has both roots there precisely when all three displayed coefficients have the same positive sign. Since 1−T+D=c>0, the coexistence fixed point is a sink precisely when
21−a​<b<min(1−a,a+31​).​
(24)
This region exists only for a>1/9. In the coexistence triangle a+b<1, put K=1+T+D=(3a+1−3b)/a. If K<0, one root lies below −1 and the other in (−1,1), so P is a saddle. If K>0 but D>1, it is a repeller; if K>0,D<1, it is a sink. These signs give the complete local fixed-point partition, including real and complex multipliers.
The coexistence period-doubling bifurcation lies at b=a+1/3; its segment adjoining the sink region has 1/9<a<1/3. The other multiplier there is 2−1/(3a) and lies strictly inside the unit circle on this segment. This coexistence flip is subcritical on that segment. To calculate its direction, normalize the −1 eigenvector by qf​=(1,(2−b/a)/b)T and the left eigenvector by pf​qf​=1. The symmetric quadratic derivative B is given explicitly in part (d). Eliminating the quadratic term from the one-dimensional centre manifold map gives the effective cubic flip coefficient
cf​=21​pf​B(qf​,(I−J)−1B(qf​,qf​))=−(3a+1)2(9a−1)9​<0.
(25)
For a scalar reduced map u′=(−1+δ)u+cf​u3+⋯, its second iterate satisfies u′′−u=−2δu−2cf​u3+⋯. Thus u2=−δ/cf​>0 on the stable-fixed-point side δ>0, and the two-cycle multiplier is 1+4δ+⋯>1. An unstable coexistence two-cycle meets the stable fixed point as b increases to the flip curve. The other multiplier remains inside the unit circle. The complex unit-modulus crossing lies at a+2b=1, with 1/9<a<1. The two curves meet at (a,b)=(1/9,4/9) with a double multiplier −1. The prey flip and predator invasion curves meet at (1/3,2/3) with multipliers −1,+1.
The sketches mark the sink regions and all these local thresholds. They are a local atlas, not a claim that the map has only these global attractors. Indeed the full nonnegative quadrant is not invariant: x′=x(1−x−ay)/a becomes negative when x+ay>1. On the prey axis the logistic map already supplies new dynamics. Its nontrivial two-cycle, obtained by factoring F2(x)−x after removing the fixed-point factors, is
x±​=21+a±(1−3a)(1+a)​​,a<31​.
(26)
Its longitudinal multiplier is 4+2/a−1/a2, so it is stable on the axis for 1/(1+6​)<a<1/3. The transverse multiplier is x+​x−​/b2=a(1+a)/b2, making the cycle stable in the plane when additionally b>a(1+a)​. Further flips and more complicated logistic dynamics show why the differential equation's three global regions cannot be transferred unchanged to its unit-step map.
