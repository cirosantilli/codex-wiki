Put a=σ13​, b=σ23​, w=v3​, and write fa​=∂f/∂a, fb​=∂f/∂b. In antiplane perfect plasticity, force balance and the fixed yield surface give
a,1​+b,2​=0,f(a,b)=k.
(56)
Differentiate the second relation in x2​. Along the proposed characteristic curve,
dsda​=α(−fb​a,1​+fa​a,2​)=α(fb​b,2​+fa​a,2​)=0.
(57)
Similarly, differentiating the yield relation in x1​ gives
dsdb​=α(−fb​b,1​+fa​b,2​)=−α(fb​b,1​+fa​a,1​)=0.
(58)
The associated flow rule and ϵ˙i3​=w,i​/2 give w,1​=2λ˙fa​, w,2​=2λ˙fb​. Consequently
dsdw​=2αλ˙(−fb​fa​+fa​fb​)=0.
(59)
Thus both shear stresses and the antiplane velocity are constant on each characteristic. Assume a regular yield gradient (fa​,fb​)=0. Since the stress is constant on a characteristic, its tangent (−fb​,fa​) has fixed direction, so the curves are straight lines, apart from arbitrary reparameterization by α.
In a centred antiplane plastic fan those lines emanate from the crack tip. Hence
a=a(ϕ),b=b(ϕ),w=w(ϕ),
(60)
and a radial line at angle ϕ from the x1​ axis must have
(cosϕ,sinϕ)∥(−fb​,fa​),fa​cosϕ+fb​sinϕ=0.
(61)
The crack faces have normal in the x2​ direction, so traction freedom requires b=0 there. The fan joins the adjacent constant crack-face stress region along the characteristic carrying that stress. Its boundary line therefore satisfies
tanϕb​=−fb​fa​​at b=0,​
(62)
with the direction interpreted directly when the denominator vanishes. The original PDF reverses this ratio. Its displayed ratio is dx1​/dx2​, whereas the tangent of an angle from the x1​ axis is dx2​/dx1​. The distinction cannot be removed while retaining both its stated characteristic direction and its angle convention.
For the elliptic yield surface, with A,B>0,
fa​=2a/A2,fb​=2b/B2.
(63)
On the forward fan −π/2<ϕ<π/2, choose the loading branch b>0. The radial tangent condition gives a/A2=−(b/B2)tanϕ. Substituting in the yield equation and choosing the positive square root gives exactly
a=(B2+A2tan2ϕ)1/2−A2tanϕ​,b=(B2+A2tan2ϕ)1/2B2​.​
(64)
For the endpoints, use the equivalent nonsingular expressions
a=−H(ϕ)A2sinϕ​,b=H(ϕ)B2cosϕ​,H(ϕ)=(A2sin2ϕ+B2cos2ϕ)1/2.
(65)
They give (a,b)=(−A,0) on the upper boundary ray and (A,0) on the lower one. The correct boundaries are ϕ=±π/2, the vertical line through the tip. At either of these stress states fb​=0 and fa​=0, so the characteristic tangent is vertical. The printed boundary ratio would instead give a horizontal line, providing a concrete counterexample to that intermediate assertion.
Extend the upper and lower rear sectors by the constant stresses (−A,0) and (A,0) respectively; these satisfy the traction-free crack faces and match the fan stresses continuously. The reversed loading has all stresses negated. To check equilibrium within the forward fan, differentiation gives
a,ϕ​=−H3A2B2cosϕ​,b,ϕ​=−H3A2B2sinϕ​,
(66)
and hence a,1​+b,2​=(−sinϕa,ϕ​+cosϕb,ϕ​)/r=0. The yield equation is satisfied identically as well.
Finally ∇w=w′(ϕ)eϕ​/r, while (fa​,fb​)=2eϕ​/H. The associated flow rule reduces to
λ˙=4rH(ϕ)w′(ϕ)​,w′(ϕ)≥0​
(67)
for this stress branch. Any such angular velocity field is constant along the rays and has constant values in the adjacent rear sectors. Its magnitude and angular variation require velocity or loading data beyond the specified traction-free faces; the local stress fan alone does not determine them.
