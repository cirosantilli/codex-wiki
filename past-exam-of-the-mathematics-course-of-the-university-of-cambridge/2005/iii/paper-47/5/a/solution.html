The Metropolis–Hastings algorithm is useful when a target posterior can be evaluated up to a normalizing constant but direct independent sampling or simple conditional sampling is unavailable. It constructs a Markov chain with that posterior as invariant law. After adequate convergence, averages of functions of the chain estimate posterior expectations, and the retained draws approximate marginal distributions, quantiles and credible intervals.
For the Random-walk Metropolis algorithm, at current θ propose θ′=θ+Z, where Z has a symmetric distribution, often N(0,Σ). With a symmetric proposal the acceptance probability is
a(θ,θ′)=min{1,π(θ′∣X)/π(θ∣X)}.​
(38)
Accept the proposal when an independent uniform is below this probability; otherwise retain the current value. For a nonsymmetric proposal q(θ′∣θ), include q(θ∣θ′)/q(θ′∣θ) in the ratio. Detailed balance follows because the accepted flow is the minimum of the two proposed directional flows; rejection supplies the remaining self-transition probability. Irreducibility, appropriate recurrence and aperiodicity are also needed for convergence.
In the displayed trace plots, the first parameter moves in small increments with pronounced long-term wandering and a shift from its early region to a later region. This is consistent with an undersized proposal or a strongly correlated slow direction. Measure its acceptance rate: if it is very high, increase that coordinate's proposal variance. The second parameter moves rapidly within regions but changes its level around iteration 4500; that is evidence against treating the full run as a settled stationary sample, and it suggests investigating dependence with the first parameter or separated posterior regions. Its local scale may already be reasonable, so a blanket reduction of all proposal variances would be unwarranted. The third parameter has conspicuous long plateaus, indicating many repeated states: if the recorded acceptance is low, reduce its proposal variance; also check constraints and dependence before deciding that scale alone is the cause.
Rerun with coordinate-specific scale changes, inspect joint dependence, consider a covariance-based block proposal, and run longer chains from dispersed starting points. The MCMC trace diagnosis and proposal tuning should include actual acceptance rates and effective sample sizes; the three marginal trace plots do not by themselves identify correlations or prove convergence.
