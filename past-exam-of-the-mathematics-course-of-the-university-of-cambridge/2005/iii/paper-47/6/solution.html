For minimizing an energy F(θ), simulated annealing at temperature T>0 uses the Boltzmann distribution πT​(θ)∝e−F(θ)/T. At each temperature run a Metropolis–Hastings algorithm or Gibbs transition preserving that law, then reduce T. With a symmetric proposal, accept θ′ with probability min{1,e−[F(θ′)−F(θ)]/T}: downhill moves are accepted and uphill moves remain possible. Keep the best state encountered. As temperature decreases, equilibrium mass favors global minima, provided the target is proper and the relevant concentration hypotheses hold. An arbitrary finite cooling run does not by itself prove global convergence.
Let s=∑i=1m​xi​, xˉ=s/m and S=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2. For the Poisson distribution model the observations must be nonnegative integers. Its log-likelihood is
ℓP​(λ)=−mλ+slogλ−∑i​log(xi​!).
(46)
For s>0, differentiation gives ℓP′​=−m+s/λ and ℓP′′​=−s/λ2<0. If s=0, the likelihood is decreasing and the maximizer is the boundary value zero. For the normal distribution model write v=σ2>0, giving
ℓN​(μ,v)=−2m​log(2πv)−2vS+m(μ−xˉ)2​.
(47)
For each v, this is maximized at μ=xˉ, and differentiating there gives v=S/m. Hence
λ=xˉ,μ​=xˉ,σ2=S/m.​
(48)
The normal variance estimate has denominator m, not m−1. A finite positive normal maximum requires S>0; if all observations coincide, the likelihood is unbounded as μ=xˉ,v↓0. The normal annealing calculation below assumes S>0.
To maximize likelihood, choose the energy −ℓ, so the annealing law is L1/T, not L−1/T. This sign is important in the Poisson request: literally inserting a positive log-likelihood into a minimizing Boltzmann law would give a nonnormalizable probability density function growing like emλ/T at infinity. We use likelihood-power annealing with the maximizing sign.
For the normal distribution model, take base measure dμdv. The joint annealing probability density function is
πT​(μ,v)∝v−m/(2T)exp[−2TvS+m(μ−xˉ)2​].
(49)
Completing the square gives the Gibbs sampling conditional for μ. Matching the conditional v probability density function to the supplied inverse-gamma distribution convention gives
μ∣vv∣μ​∼N(xˉ,Tv/m),∼InvGamma(2Tm​−1,2TS+m(μ−xˉ)2​).​​
(50)
Integrating out μ contributes a factor v​, so the marginal law is
v∼InvGamma(2Tm​−23​,2TS​).
(51)
Thus this joint Boltzmann law is proper for T<m/3. Its variance marginal and the normal conditional both concentrate at the stated estimates as T↓0. For example, for T<m/7 its marginal mean and variance are
ET​v=m−5TS​,VarT​(v)=(m−5T)2(m−7T)2TS2​.

There is a direct convergence proof for the actual changing-temperature Gibbs algorithm, beyond convergence of its equilibrium probability densities. Start at a finite deterministic v0​>0, choose deterministic 0<Tn​≤m/10 with Tn​→0, draw μn​ conditional on vn−1​, then draw vn​ conditional on μn​ using the two boxed distributions. Conditional normal moments give
E[(μn​−xˉ)2∣vn−1​]=Tn​vn−1​/m,E[(μn​−xˉ)4∣vn−1​]=3(Tn​vn−1​/m)2.
(53)
The inverse-gamma distribution first and second moments consequently yield
Evn​=m−4Tn​S+Tn​Evn−1​​,
(54)
and
Evn2​=(m−4Tn​)(m−6Tn​)S2+2STn​Evn−1​+3Tn2​Evn−12​​.
(55)
In the first recursion the coefficient of the preceding mean is at most 1/6; in the second the coefficient of the preceding second moment is at most 1/8. The means therefore stay bounded, and then so do the second moments. Letting Tn​→0 in these formulas gives Evn​→S/m and Evn2​→(S/m)2. Together with E(μn​−xˉ)2=Tn​Evn−1​/m→0, this proves
(μn​,vn​)⟶(xˉ,S/m)in mean square and hence in probability.​

This Gaussian likelihood Gibbs annealing convergence uses the explicit conditional structure and does not assume that one Gibbs sampling sweep is an exact equilibrium draw.
For the Poisson distribution model, the likelihood-power law with respect to dλ has the normalized probability density function
πT​(λ)=Γ(s/T+1)(m/T)s/T+1​λs/Te−mλ/T(λ>0),

The mean tends to xˉ and the variance to zero, proving convergence to a point mass at the Poisson maximum-likelihood estimate, including the zero-boundary case.
The Akaike information criterion is 2k−2ℓ(θ), counting one parameter for Poisson and two for normal. With 0log0=0 and S>0, the two forms are
AICP​=2+2s−2slog(s/m)+2i∑​log(xi​!),​
(59)
and
AICN​=4+m[log(2πS/m)+1].​
(60)
Choose the smaller criterion. Likelihood constants that differ between models, including the factorial and 2π terms, cannot be discarded.
For a fully specified trans-dimensional annealing for penalized likelihood, let M=P or N and define
EP​(λ)=2−2ℓP​(λ),EN​(μ,v)=4−2ℓN​(μ,v).
(61)
Use the combined target, relative to counting measure on models and Lebesgue measure on their parameters,
ΠT​(M,ϑ)∝wM​e−EM​(ϑ)/(2T)=wM​e−kM​/TLM​(ϑ)1/T,
(62)
where wP​,wN​>0 are fixed reference weights. For S>0 and 0<T<m/3 both model components are integrable. The factor 2T is a convenient temperature convention keeping the within-model laws exactly those already derived. The extra factor e−kM​/T makes the zero-temperature objective AIC rather than unpenalized likelihood.
Use, for example, probability 1/2 for a model-change attempt in either model and probability 1/2 for a within-model update. Within the Poisson distribution model draw a fresh gamma value from its boxed law; within the normal distribution model perform the boxed Gibbs sampling sweep. For a Poisson-to-normal proposal, draw u∼q(u)=N(0,τ2) with a fixed τ2>0, and set
(μ,v)=(λ+u,λ).
(63)
The inverse normal-to-Poisson proposal is λ=v with reverse auxiliary u=μ−v. This dimension matching uses one auxiliary variable to augment the one-dimensional Poisson parameter space. The absolute Jacobian determinant is
J=​det(11​10​)​=1.
(64)
If the forward model-jump selection probability is b and the reverse probability is d, the reversible-jump Markov chain Monte Carlo acceptance ratio is
RP→N​=wP​bq(u)wN​d​exp[−2TEN​(λ+u,λ)−EP​(λ)​]J.​
(65)
Accept with probability min(1,RP→N​). For the reverse move compute λ=v,u=μ−v and use
RN→P​=wN​dwP​bq(μ−v)​exp[−2TEP​(v)−EN​(μ,v)​]J−1.​
(66)
For the suggested equal selection probabilities, b=d=1/2 and those factors cancel. The probability density function q must still be included, despite symmetry of its normal distribution: it is an auxiliary draw in only the forward dimension-increasing proposal. Pairing a move with its inverse makes the accepted forward and reverse probability flows equal after the change of variables, proving detailed balance for each fixed-temperature model-switch kernel.
Cool through proper temperatures, allow enough model and parameter exploration at each stage, and retain the smallest observed EM​. The minima of the two energies are exactly the two AIC values above. Low-temperature equilibrium mass concentrates on the better criterion and its fitted parameters; if they tie, the minimizing set contains both models. Unlike the direct normal Gibbs argument, this multimodel exploration claim needs an appropriate cooling/mixing justification and is not guaranteed by an arbitrary short run.
Finally, an exact statistical comparison must put both likelihoods on the same observable and reference-measure convention. A Poisson distribution model is a probability mass function on counts, whereas the displayed normal distribution model is a continuous probability density function. The formulas above are the question's formal Gaussian working-likelihood comparison. A coherent exact normal approximation for count observations can instead use probabilities of rounding intervals; that changes its likelihood and generally loses the simple analytic normal estimates. This distinction matters when interpreting the numerical AIC difference, while leaving the dimension-matching and annealing construction applicable to properly specified likelihoods.
