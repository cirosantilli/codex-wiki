Let Φ(z)=1−ϕ1​z−⋯−ϕp​zp. The causality root criterion for an autoregressive model,
Φ(z)=0for ∣z∣≤1,​
(9)
is a sufficient condition for a weakly stationary process solution, and gives the causal solution Xt​=∑j≥0​ψj​εt−j​. Indeed 1/Φ is analytic in a disk of radius greater than one, so its Taylor coefficients ψj​ decay geometrically. The series therefore has mean-square convergence and defines a linear filter of a stationary time series; multiplying by Φ verifies the equation.
For the specified order-two model, put φ=(1+5​)/2. Since φ−φ−1=1,
Φ(z)=1−αz−α2z2=(1−αφz)(1+αz/φ).
(10)
When α=0, its roots are 1/(αφ) and −φ/α. Both have modulus greater than one exactly when ∣α∣<1/φ. The case α=0 is white noise and satisfies the same inequality. Consequently
α0​=25​−1​,∣α∣<α0​.​
(11)
This is the causal stationarity condition selected above. Without causality, a two-sided autoregressive model can also have a noncausal stationary autoregression when its polynomial has no unit-circle zeros. Thus the inequality is not a necessary condition for all conceivable weak stationarity solutions; the causal interpretation is the one required by the subsequent innovations and forecasting questions.
To find the Wold representation, expand 1/Φ(z)=∑j≥0​ψj​zj. Matching coefficients gives ψ0​=1, ψ1​=α and ψj​=αψj−1​+α2ψj−2​. Hence
Xt​=j=0∑∞​Fj+1​αjεt−j​,Fj+1​=5​φj+1−(−1/φ)j+1​.​
(12)
Here F1​=F2​=1 are the Fibonacci numbers. The coefficient formula also follows by partial fractions in the displayed factorization. Absolute summability follows from ∣αφ∣<1 and ∣α/φ∣<1. The past observation span equals the past noise span, because the causal series gives one inclusion and εt​=Xt​−αXt−1​−α2Xt−2​ gives the reverse inclusion. Thus these noises are the linear innovations, and there is no additional deterministic component. This is the Fibonacci-coefficient autoregression.
The best linear prediction from an infinite past is the orthogonal projection onto the closed span of XT​,XT−1​,…. Future white noise is orthogonal to that span. Substitution in the recursion therefore gives
XT+1​=αXT​+α2XT−1​,XT+2​=2α2XT​+α3XT−1​.​
(13)
The respective errors are εT+1​ and αεT+1​+εT+2​, so their mean squared errors are σ2 and (1+α2)σ2. No normal distribution assumption is needed for these linear forecasts.
