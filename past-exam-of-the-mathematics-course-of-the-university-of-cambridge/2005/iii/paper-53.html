Write t=logμ and use αi​=gi2​/(4π). The chain rule turns the renormalization-group beta function into
dtdαi​​=2πgi​​dtdgi​​=2πβi​​αi2​,dtdαi−1​​=−2πβi​​.
(1)
Integrating gives the one-loop inverse gauge coupling evolution
αi−1​(μ)=αi−1​(MZ​)−2πβi​​logMZ​μ​.​
(2)
This expression applies over a range with unchanged active field content and in the perturbative regime. It uses logμ, not logμ2.
For gauge coupling unification, define the normalized inverse couplings A1​=3/(5α1​), A2​=1/α2​, A3​=1/α3​, and slopes b1​=(3/5)β1​, b2​=β2​, b3​=β3​. At the common scale each inverse coupling is AU​. With L=log(MGUT​/MZ​),
Ai​(MZ​)=AU​+2πbi​​L.
(3)
Subtract the equations for factors 1 and 2 to solve L=2π(A1​−A2​)/(b1​−b2​), then insert this into the difference of factors 3 and 2:
α3​(MZ​)1​=α2​(MZ​)1​+(3/5)β1​−β2​β3​−β2​​[5α1​(MZ​)3​−α2​(MZ​)1​].​
(4)
This one-loop normalized hypercharge unification relation requires b1​=b2​. A scale above MZ​ also requires the inferred L to be positive. If those two slopes coincide, unification instead requires A1​=A2​ and their difference cannot fix the scale.
In the convention Q=T3​+Y, one matter generation and one Higgs doublet have the following Standard Model representations. Spin is in units of ℏ; L/R describe chirality, not the spin projection. The U(1)Y​ entry is its hypercharge eigenvalue, defining the one-dimensional representation eiYω.

There is no right-handed neutrino in the minimal field content being counted. Right-handed matter can alternatively be written as left-handed charge-conjugate Weyl fermions, in conjugate colour representations with opposite hypercharges. The quadratic indices and squared charges in the beta coefficients are unchanged.
For SU(3), one generation supplies two colour fundamentals from QL​ and one each from uR​,dR​. With three generations there are nf​=3(2+1+1)=12 Weyl fermions counted as fundamental copies, and no coloured scalar. Thus
β3​=−311​3+31​12=−7​.
(5)
For SU(2), the three colours of QL​ give three weak-doublet copies and LL​ gives one. There are nf​=3(3+1)=12 doublets across three generations and ns​=1 complex Higgs doublet, giving
β2​=−311​2+31​12+61​=−619​​.
(6)
These are spectator multiplicities in gauge beta functions: the colour index multiplies the weak contribution, while the weak index multiplies the colour contribution. Each two-component fermion is counted once; it must not be counted again as a separate antiparticle.
For U(1)Y​, sum squared hypercharges over all colour and weak components. One generation gives
∑f,one generation​Yf2​=6(61​)2+3(32​)2+3(−31​)2+2(−21​)2+(−1)2=310​.
(7)
The two complex Higgs components give ∑s​Ys2​=2(1/2)2=1/2. There is no Abelian gauge self-interaction contribution. Therefore
β1​=32​3310​+31​21​=641​​,(β1​,β2​,β3​)=(641​,−619​,−7).​
(8)
The Higgs is counted only once for the whole model, whereas the fermionic generation is repeated three times. These are the Standard Model one-loop gauge coefficients in the unnormalized hypercharge convention. The grand-unified normalization instead has b1​=41/10. The ratio of slopes in the unification relation is (β3​−β2​)/(b1​−β2​)=−115/218.

The local matrix U(x) is a gauge transformation in the representation acting on the matter field. With Hermitian Lie algebra generators it can be written locally in spacetime, and pointwise for SU(3), as
U(x)=exp[iωa(x)Ta],[Ta,Tb]=ifabc​Tc.
(9)
The real functions ωa can instead include a factor of g by redefining the transformation parameters. The gauge covariant derivative convention in this paper is Dμ​=∂μ​+igAμ​. Inserting the stated transformed Aμ​ shows explicitly that the ∂μ​U term cancels, giving Dμ′​(Uψ)=UDμ​ψ.
In QCD, each quark field is in the colour fundamental representation 3, with ψi↦Uij​ψj. Thus its colour generators are eight 3×3 matrices, conventionally Ta=λa/2 for the Gell-Mann matrices; their number is not their matrix dimension. The eight gluons themselves transform in the adjoint representation of SU(3).
With tr(TaTb)=δab/2, the pure Yang-Mills theory has Lagrangian LYM​=−41​Fμνa​Faμν=−21​tr(Fμν​Fμν). Put Gμνa​=∂μ​Aνa​−∂ν​Aμa​ and Cμνa​=fabc​Aμb​Aνc​. Since F=G−gC,
LYM​=−41​Gμνa​Gaμν+2g​Gμνa​Caμν−4g2​Cμνa​Caμν.
(10)
The cross term has two derivative contributions. Interchange μ,ν and then b,c in the second; antisymmetry of fabc​ makes it equal to the first. The cubic and quartic Yang-Mills self-interactions are therefore
L3​=gfabc​(∂μ​Aνa​)AbμAcν,L4​=−4g2​fabc​fade​Aμb​Aνc​AdμAeν.​
(11)
The positive cubic sign follows from the negative gfAA term in the given Yang-Mills field strength; changing the covariant-derivative convention consistently changes intermediate signs.
For the left-handed quark doublet QL​=(uL​,dL​)T, the hypercharge is Y=1/6 and the weak generators are σα/2. Its electroweak term is
LQL​​=Q​L​iγμ[∂μ​+2ig​σαAμα​+6ig′​Bμ​]QL​.​
(12)
The Pauli matrices give diagonal entries ±A3 and off-diagonal entries A1∓iA2. Expanding the term gives
LQL​​=​uˉL​iγμ∂μ​uL​+dˉL​iγμ∂μ​dL​−6g′​Bμ​(uˉL​γμuL​+dˉL​γμdL​)−2g​Aμ3​(uˉL​γμuL​−dˉL​γμdL​)−2g​[(Aμ1​−iAμ2​)uˉL​γμdL​+(Aμ1​+iAμ2​)dˉL​γμuL​].​
(13)
In particular the off-diagonal weak charged current coupling is −g(Wμ​uˉL​γμdL​+Wμ∗​dˉL​γμuL​)/2​.
Write sW​=sinθW​, cW​=cosθW​. Invert the neutral-field rotation: Aμ3​=cW​Zμ​+sW​Aμ​, Bμ​=cW​Aμ​−sW​Zμ​. The original PDF gives tanθW​=g′/g, so
e=gsinθW​=g′cosθW​.​
(14)
The photon coefficients inside the derivative are gsW​/2+g′cW​/6=2e/3 for uL​, and −gsW​/2+g′cW​/6=−e/3 for dL​. Multiplication by the i in the kinetic Lagrangian gives the requested electromagnetic coupling of a weak multiplet:
Lem​=−32​euˉL​Aμ​γμuL​+31​edˉL​Aμ​γμdL​.​
(15)
Equivalently it is −eAμ​Q​L​γμ(T3​+Y)QL​, making the electric charges explicit. The remaining neutral terms are −(g/cW​)Zμ​{(1/2−2sW2​/3)uˉL​γμuL​+(−1/2+sW2​/3)dˉL​γμdL​}. The TeX's missing prime in the mixing-angle ratio and truncated first Pauli matrix are conversion errors, not physical assumptions.

At momentum transfers small compared with MW​, the two weak charged current vertices each carry g/(22​) when written with (1−γ5​), and the W boson propagator reduces to a local factor proportional to 1/MW2​. Matching their product to the Fermi interaction gives, at tree level,
2​GF​​=8MW2​g2​,GF​=2​v21​(MW​=gv/2).​
(16)
The Fermi constant is thus determined by the electroweak coupling and symmetry-breaking scale; the four-fermion description is a low-energy effective interaction.
Only the cross term JL†​JH​ can annihilate the pion and create the stated charged-lepton/antineutrino pair. Its leptonic matrix element is uˉe​(k)γμ(1−γ5​)vν​(q). Lorentz covariance permits a vacuum-to-spin-zero current matrix element only proportional to pμ. At rest this has only a temporal component. The vacuum is parity-even and the pion is parity-odd, while VH0​ is parity-even, so ⟨0∣VH0​∣π⟩ equals its own negative and vanishes. Lorentz covariance then makes the full vector matrix element vanish. The temporal axial current is parity-odd and is allowed: this is the vacuum-to-pseudoscalar current selection rule.
The minus sign in JH​=VH​−AH​ cancels the interaction's minus sign, up to an irrelevant common S-matrix phase. Therefore
M=2​GF​​uˉe​γμ(1−γ5​)vν​⟨0∣AHμ​∣π−⟩=iGF​Fπ​cosθC​pμ​uˉe​γμ(1−γ5​)vν​.
(17)
The pion decay constant convention here includes the given 2​ in its current matrix element. Momentum conservation and the external Dirac equations give p=k+q, uˉe​k=me​uˉe​, qvν​=0. Since q(1−γ5​)=(1+γ5​)q, the neutrino term vanishes and
M=iGF​Fπ​cosθC​me​uˉe​(1−γ5​)vν​.​
(18)
This is helicity suppression: the amplitude vanishes in the zero charged-lepton-mass limit despite the larger available phase space.
For the spin sum, the Dirac adjoint of 1−γ5​ is 1+γ5​, and the completeness relations give
s∑​∣M∣2​=GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​tr[(k+me​)(1−γ5​)q(1+γ5​)]=2GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​tr[(k+me​)q(1+γ5​)]=8GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​k⋅q=4GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​(mπ2​−me2​).​
(19)
The chiral projectors automatically eliminate the unused neutrino helicity when the full massless completeness relation is inserted. There is no initial spin average for a spin-zero pion.
In the pion rest frame, let r=∣k∣=∣q∣, E=r2+me2​​. After integrating the spatial delta function and angles, the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space becomes
Φ2​=4π1​∫0∞​Err2​δ(mπ​−E−r)dr.
(20)
The root is r∗​=(mπ2​−me2​)/(2mπ​) and E∗​=(mπ2​+me2​)/(2mπ​). The absolute derivative of the delta-function argument is r∗​/E∗​+1=mπ​/E∗​. Thus Φ2​=r∗​/(4πmπ​). Multiplying by the spin sum and the initial factor 1/(2mπ​) yields the leptonic pseudoscalar decay width
Γ(π−→e−νˉe​)=4πGF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​​mπ​me2​(1−mπ2​me2​​)2.​
(21)
If the decay constant were instead defined by a matrix element ifπ​pμ without 2​, then fπ​=2​Fπ​ and the width coefficient would be fπ2​/(8π); mixing these conventions would introduce a factor-of-two error.
The same derivation for a muon cancels the common hadronic and weak factors in the pion electron-to-muon decay ratio:
Re/μ(0)​=mμ2​me2​​(1−mμ2​/mπ2​1−me2​/mπ2​​)2≈1.28×10−4.​
(22)
The numerical value uses approximate masses 0.511,105.66,139.57 MeV. Agreement with the corresponding Standard Model prediction tests lepton universality and the chiral, helicity-suppressing structure of the weak charged current, rather than the poorly calculable hadronic decay constant. A difference in the electron and muon weak couplings would multiply the ratio by their squared coupling ratio. Precision agreement must be assessed after radiative corrections, with matching photon-inclusive experimental definitions; the displayed tree-level ratio is not itself the radiatively corrected precision prediction.

An antilinear involution need not preserve norms, so the required physical assumption is that momentum reversal maps a complete normalized basis to a normalized basis. For a spinless sector take T^∣p⟩=η(p)∣−p⟩ with ∣η∣=1 and relativistic normalization ⟨p∣q⟩=(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−q). Any extra degeneracy labels must likewise be mapped by a norm-preserving permutation or unitary matrix. The condition T^2=1 requires η(−p)η(p)∗=1 in the scalar basis.
For arbitrary normalizable wave packets ∣ϕ⟩=∫dΠp​f(p)∣p⟩ and ∣ψ⟩=∫dΠp​h(p)∣p⟩, where dΠp​=d3p/[(2π)32Ep​], antilinearity conjugates their coefficients. Using the normalized reversed basis gives
⟨T^ϕ∣T^ψ⟩=∫dΠp​f(p)h(p)∗∣η(p)∣2=[∫dΠp​f(p)∗h(p)]∗.
(23)
Completeness proves ⟨T^ϕ∣T^ψ⟩=⟨ϕ∣ψ⟩∗ for all states, so this quantum time-reversal operator is antiunitary. The same proof with sums over extra labels uses the unitarity of their reversal matrix.
For a Hermitian interaction-picture Hamiltonian, the condition of time-reversal symmetry is
T^V(t)T^−1=V(−t),V(t)†=V(t).​
(24)
Expand the scattering matrix as a Dyson series, using ordered integration rather than commuting operators:
S=1+∑n≥1​(−i)n∫t1​>⋯>tn​​dt1​⋯dtn​V(t1​)⋯V(tn​).
(25)
Conjugation by T^ conjugates (−i)n to in but preserves the product order. Substitute sj​=−tj​; the integration domain becomes s1​<⋯<sn​ and
T^ST^−1=1+∑n≥1​in∫s1​<⋯<sn​​ds1​⋯dsn​V(s1​)⋯V(sn​).
(26)
On the other hand, taking the adjoint of the original series conjugates its coefficient and reverses the operator order. Relabel s1​=tn​,…,sn​=t1​ in each adjoint integral; it is exactly the same series. Thus
T^ST^−1=S†.​
(27)
Equivalently, time ordering turns into anti-time ordering after reversing the time arguments. One can first use a finite symmetric time interval and the same time-symmetric switching prescription before taking the scattering limit; the argument never assumes [V(t),V(s)]=0.
Antiunitarity relates amplitudes by
⟨T^f∣T^ST^−1∣T^i⟩=⟨f∣S∣i⟩∗.
(28)
Using T^ST^−1=S† and taking a complex conjugate gives the time-reversal reciprocity of the scattering matrix
⟨f∣S∣i⟩=⟨T^i∣S∣T^f⟩.​
(29)
The reverse process starts with the reversed final state and ends with the reversed initial state. Intrinsic state phases can appear when these reversed states are rewritten in a separately chosen basis, but the corresponding squared amplitudes agree. Equal amplitudes do not mean identical numerical cross sections when the reversed process has different flux or phase-space factors.
For a spin-zero complex scalar field, choose the transformation convention T^ϕ(t,x)T^−1=ηT​ϕ(−t,x). Its mode expansion of a free field is
ϕ(t,x)=∫dΠp​[a(p)e−iEp​t+ip⋅x+b†(p)eiEp​t−ip⋅x].
(30)
Apply T^ to the expansion. Its antiunitarity conjugates both numerical plane waves, giving coefficients T^aT^−1 multiplying eiEp​t−ip⋅x and T^b†T^−1 multiplying e−iEp​t+ip⋅x. In ηT​ϕ(−t,x), replace p by −p to obtain precisely these same plane waves. Comparing independent modes proves time reversal of a complex scalar field:
T^a(p)T^−1=ηT​a(pP​),T^b†(p)T^−1=ηT​b†(pP​),pP​=(Ep​,−p).​
(31)
Taking adjoints gives T^b(p)T^−1=ηT∗​b(pP​). Positive energy is preserved, and an annihilation operator remains an annihilation operator. Time reversal does not exchange particles with antiparticles.
The coefficient ηT​ is an intrinsic time-reversal phase. Applying T^ twice gives T^2aT^−2=ηT∗​ηT​a, so ∣ηT​∣=1; it need not be restricted to ±1. Write ηT​=eiϑ and redefine a′=eiϑ/2a. Because the phase is conjugated by T^,
T^a′T^−1=e−iϑ/2ηT​a(pP​)=a′(pP​).
(32)
Thus the scalar intrinsic phase can be absorbed into the annihilation-operator convention. Redefining b′=e−iϑ/2b simultaneously implements the corresponding whole-field rephasing ϕ′=eiϑ/2ϕ and removes the phase from both mode transformations.
