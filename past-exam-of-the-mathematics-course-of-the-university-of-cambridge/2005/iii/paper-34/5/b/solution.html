Fix a closed ball B(z,r)​⊂D, independently choose a radius R with density nsn−1/rn on (0,r), and let S be the first time the Brownian motion from z reaches that radius. This identity is valid in the extended nonnegative reals; no finiteness was assumed to derive it.
If gD​(z)<∞, the integral over B(z,r) is finite. For any y∈B(z,r) choose ρ>0 with B(y,ρ)​⊂B(z,r). The same identity at y has a finite right side because its integral is over a subset of the integrable ball. Thus gD​(y)<∞. The set F={z∈D:gD​(z)<∞} is therefore open relative to D.
It is also relatively closed. If zj​∈F converges to y∈D, choose r>0 with B(y,3r)​⊂D. For large j, ∣zj​−y∣<r, and B(zj​,2r)​⊂D. The preceding argument shows that every point in B(zj​,2r), including y, belongs to F. Hence F is closed in D. Since F is nonempty and D is connected, gD​(y)<∞ for every y∈D. This proves finiteness propagation of Brownian mean exit times without assuming beforehand that gD​ solves a smooth Poisson equation.
