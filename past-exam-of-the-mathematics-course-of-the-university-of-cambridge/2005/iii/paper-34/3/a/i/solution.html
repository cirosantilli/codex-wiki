For each sample point put An​=max0≤k<2n​∣X(k+1)2−n​−Xk2−n​∣. First take s<t in the dyadic rationals, set h=t−s, and choose N≥0 with 2−N≤h<21−N. The left grid approximations sN​=2−N⌊2Ns⌋ and tN​=2−N⌊2Nt⌋ are at most two grid steps apart, so ∣XtN​​−XsN​​∣≤2AN​.
For either endpoint r, successive left approximations rn​,rn+1​ are equal or one step apart on the level-(n+1) grid. Since r is dyadic these approximations eventually equal r exactly. The triangle inequality therefore gives
∣Xt​−Xs​∣≤2∑n≥N​An​≤2−Nα2∑n≥N​2nαAn​≤hαKα​.
(11)
The second inequality is valid also for α=0. Thus the required bound holds simultaneously for every dyadic pair whenever Kα​ is finite; if it is infinite the inequality holds in the extended sense. For s=t the left side is zero. This is dyadic increment chaining, with the stated constant two.
