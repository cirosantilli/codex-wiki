The dyadic slope martingale is uniformly bounded by L. The martingale convergence theorem gives an almost-sure limit f˙​, and dominated convergence gives L1 convergence. Define f˙​=0 on the null exceptional set and at the endpoint. This gives a measurable representative with ∣f˙​∣≤L everywhere.
If a,b are dyadic rationals, then for every sufficiently fine grid the integral telescopes:
∫ab​Xn​(x)dx=∑[u,v)⊂[a,b)​(f(v)−f(u))=f(b)−f(a).
(9)
Pass to the limit by L1 convergence. For arbitrary endpoints choose dyadic aj​→a, bj​→b with aj​≤bj​. The error in the integrals is at most L(∣aj​−a∣+∣bj​−b∣), and Lipschitz continuity controls the error in the endpoint values by the same expression. Therefore
∫ab​f˙​(x)dx=f(b)−f(a)(0≤a≤b≤1),∥f˙​∥∞​≤L.​
(10)
This constructs the bounded integral density directly, without assuming in advance that a Lipschitz function has an almost everywhere derivative.
