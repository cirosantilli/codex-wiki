For a chiral superfield of mass dimension one, a renormalizable quantum field theory has a quadratic Kähler potential and a superpotential of degree at most three. Before canonical normalization one may write
K=ZΦ†Φ+[k1​Φ+k2​Φ2+h.c.]+k0​,Z>0,
(6)
with real k0​, and
W=w0​+ℓΦ+2m​Φ2+3g​Φ3.
(7)
The purely holomorphic terms in K, their conjugates and its constant have vanishing full superspace integration; they are Kähler transformations of the global theory. Rescaling the chiral superfield sets Z=1, so the physically general kinetic choice is K=Φ†Φ up to these terms. The constant superpotential in global supersymmetry also has no component effect. The dimensions of ℓ,m,g are respectively two, one and zero.
To eliminate the linear term, translate the complex chiral superfield as Φ=Φ′+s. The shifted linear coefficient is
W′(s)=ℓ+ms+gs2.
(8)
If g=0, this quadratic has a root over the complex numbers; if g=0 but m=0, take s=−ℓ/m. The resulting linear term vanishes and the new quadratic coefficient is m+2gs. The canonical Kähler potential changes only by holomorphic, antiholomorphic and constant terms, which do not change the global kinetic action. This is the superpotential critical-point shift.
The claim needs an exception: if m=g=0 but ℓ=0, the superpotential is purely linear and no invertible translation can remove it. More generally an invertible regular field redefinition cannot turn a nowhere-zero derivative into a critical point. The theory with W=ℓΦ has F=−ℓˉ, a constant positive scalar potential ∣ℓ∣2, and flat F-term breaking with a linear superpotential. Below, m and g denote the quadratic/cubic coefficients after the valid shift, as intended by the question.
Expand Φ=ϕ+2​θψ+θ2F in chiral coordinates. The highest component of W(Φ) is W′(ϕ)F−21​W′′(ϕ)ψψ, while the full-superspace kinetic term contains F∗F. Thus the auxiliary field part of the component Lagrangian is
LF​=F∗F+(mϕ+gϕ2)F+(mˉϕˉ​+gˉ​ϕˉ​2)F∗.
(9)
Varying F and F∗ independently gives
F∗=−(mϕ+gϕ2),F=−(mˉϕˉ​+gˉ​ϕˉ​2).
(10)
Substitution gives LF​=−∣mϕ+gϕ2∣2, and hence the F-term scalar potential
V(ϕ)=∣mϕ+gϕ2∣2.​
(11)
It is nonnegative and vanishes at ϕ=0, and also at ϕ=−m/g when g=0. These are supersymmetric vacua, since the auxiliary field vanishes. Thus the quadratic/cubic model does not spontaneously break supersymmetry. If m=0 but g=0, zero is the sole critical point; if both vanish, every ϕ is a zero-energy vacuum. Nonzero m,g give the usual two vacua, coinciding when m=0.
For the loop argument, promote the coefficients to background chiral spurions. One convenient pair of independent formal U(1) charge assignments is
U(1)U(1)R​​Φ12​m−2−2​g−3−4​θ01​W02​​.
(12)
Both symmetries act nontrivially on all three of Φ,m,g; the second is an R-symmetry. The superspace integration measure d2θ has R-charge −2, so both tree monomials give invariant actions. These are spurionic selection rules, not necessarily actual symmetries after fixing numerical couplings. The R-row is an ordinary-charge redefinition of the usual Wess–Zumino spurion charge assignment.
A local Wilsonian superpotential is a holomorphic function of the chiral fields and chiral spurions. The ratio z=gΦ/m is neutral under both charge assignments and has dimension zero, whereas mΦ2 has exactly the charges and dimension of W. The general symmetry-allowed form, on a patch with m=0 and up to a physically irrelevant global constant, is therefore
Weff​=mΦ2f(mgΦ​),​
(13)
with f holomorphic where that description is regular. Symmetry by itself does not determine f.
For a regular perturbative local Wilsonian effective action retaining the same elementary field and an infrared cutoff, expand f(z)=∑n​cn​zn. Its term of order n is cn​gnm1−nΦn+2. Negative n are singular in g, while n≥2 are singular in m; these are excluded by perturbative holomorphic regularity at the free-coupling limits. Thus only the quadratic and cubic terms survive. Their regular holomorphic coefficients cannot acquire coupling-dependent invariant corrections. At g=0 the quadratic theory is Gaussian, fixing c0​=1/2 exactly. A contribution linear in g to the cubic vertex has only one cubic interaction: three external lines exhaust that vertex, leaving no contraction to form a loop. Thus c1​=1/3 as well in the present normalization. This supplies the holomorphy argument for superpotential non-renormalization rather than concluding non-renormalization from symmetry alone.
The Kähler potential can still undergo wave-function renormalization. If its quadratic coefficient becomes Z, canonical normalization gives mc​=m/Z and gc​=g/Z3/2, so physical masses and interactions can run despite superpotential non-renormalization. More generally a positive nonsingular effective Kähler metric replaces V by Kϕϕˉ​∣W′(ϕ)∣2 and does not remove its zero-energy critical points. Infrared-singular nonlocal one-particle-irreducible terms and eliminating the entire massive field are outside this Wilsonian statement.
