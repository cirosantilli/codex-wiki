R-parity is the multiplicative Z2​ quantum number
Rp​=(−1)3(B−L)+2s,​
(4)
where B,L,s are baryon number, lepton number and spin. Ordinary Standard Model particles have Rp​=+1 and their superpartners have Rp​=−1. Gauge invariance alone allows MSSM superpotential terms LLEc, LQDc, UcDcDc and LHu​. Simultaneous baryon- and lepton-number violation can induce rapid proton decay. Exact R-parity forbids these renormalizable terms, since the associated matter parity is odd; it does not forbid every higher-dimensional proton-decay operator, for example QQQL.
Two consequences are superpartner production in pairs from ordinary-particle initial states and stability of the lightest supersymmetric particle: a single parity-odd particle cannot decay only into parity-even particles. Cascades therefore end in the lightest supersymmetric particle; if it is neutral and weakly interacting, it produces missing-energy signatures.
A continuous R-symmetry rotates θ and the supercharges. With R(θ)=1, the superpotential has R-charge two and the fermion in a chiral superfield has one less charge than its scalar. R-parity is only a discrete restriction, represented by combining matter parity with θ↦−θ, and need not preserve a full continuous R-symmetry. For example, a gaugino Majorana mass term violates an unbroken continuous R-symmetry but preserves R-parity. Thus proton protection by parity and continuous R-charge selection rules are distinct requirements.
