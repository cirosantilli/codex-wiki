Sending a matrix to its last column gives the special orthogonal sphere fibration
SO(n−1)⟶SO(n)⟶Sn−1,n≥2.
(1)
The action is transitive and the stabilizer of that column is SO(n−1). Local orthonormal frames, obtained by Gram-Schmidt process near a chosen unit vector, give local sections and hence local bundle trivializations. This allows the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration to be applied.
At the bottom of the induction, SO(1) is a point and SO(2) is the rotation circle S1. For SO(3), identify R3 with imaginary quaternions. A unit quaternion acts by v↦qvq−1, preserving length and orientation. Every rotation through angle θ about a unit imaginary axis u comes from q=cos(θ/2)+usin(θ/2). The kernel is precisely {1,−1}, since a quaternion commuting with all imaginary quaternions is real. Thus this gives the twofold covering space S3→SO(3). Its simply connected total space is the universal cover, yielding π1​SO(3)=Z/2. Coverings induce isomorphisms on higher homotopy groups, so π2​SO(3)=π2​S3=0 and π3​SO(3)=π3​S3=Z.
For n≥4, both π1​Sn−1 and π2​Sn−1 vanish. The Serre fibration sequence therefore identifies π1​SO(n−1) with π1​SO(n) and propagates vanishing of π2​. Path-connectedness also propagates from connected fiber and base. Consequently the full low-degree answer is
π0​SO(n)={∗},π2​SO(n)=0(n≥1),π1​SO(n)=⎩⎨⎧​0,Z,Z/2,​n=1,n=2,n≥3.​​
(2)
Here π0​ records the single connected component.
For the remaining third group use SO(3)→SO(4)→S3. The relevant exact segment, using the supplied fourth homotopy group, is
Z/2⟶Z⟶π3​SO(4)⟶Z⟶0.
(3)
The first homomorphism is zero since a torsion group cannot map nontrivially to Z. Thus it reduces to a short exact sequence 0→Z→π3​SO(4)→Z→0. These groups are abelian and the final free group has a lift of its generator, so the sequence splits. Therefore
π3​SO(1)=π3​SO(2)=0,π3​SO(3)=Z,π3​SO(4)=Z⊕Z.​
(4)
These are the low homotopy groups of special orthogonal groups.
