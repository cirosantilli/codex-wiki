Put t=−30​ and K=Q(t). The ring of integers is Z[t], and the fundamental discriminant is DK​=−120. For a reduced positive definite binary quadratic form (a,b,c) of discriminant −120, reduction gives
∣b∣≤a≤c,b2−4ac=−120,1≤a≤120/3​<7.
(41)
The coefficient b is even; checking 1≤a≤6 and ∣b∣≤a, with the usual nonnegative sign choice at boundary equalities, leaves precisely
(1,0,30),(2,0,15),(3,0,10),(5,0,6).
(42)
The correspondence between proper quadratic forms and ideal classes therefore gives hK​=4. All four classes equal their inverses, since inversion replaces b by −b. Hence
Cl(K)≅C2​×C2​.
(43)
The three nontrivial classes may be represented by the ramified prime ideals p2​=(2,t), p3​=(3,t) and p5​=(5,t). They square to principal ideals and satisfy p2​p3​p5​=(t). None is principal: an integral generator of norm 2, 3 or 5 would require a2+30b2 to equal that number, which is impossible. The relation then makes the three classes distinct, consistently with the four-form calculation.
Consider
H=Q(2​,−3​,5​).
(44)
The three rational square classes are independent, so [H:Q]=8. Their radical product is −30​, so K⊂H and [H:K]=4; the extension H/K is abelian. To verify that it is unramified, rather than assuming that radical adjunctions are harmless at 2,3,5, use the conductor-discriminant formula. The seven quadratic subfields have fundamental discriminants
8, −3, 5, −24, 40, −15, −120.
(45)
The formula gives
∣DH​∣=8⋅3⋅5⋅24⋅40⋅15⋅120=1204.
(46)
The discriminant tower identity now gives
∣DH​∣=∣DK​∣[H:K]NK/Q​(dH/K​),NK/Q​(dH/K​)=1.
(47)
Thus the relative discriminant is the unit ideal and no finite prime ramifies. The base has no real places, so there is no infinite ramification to check. This unramified abelian degree-four extension has degree hK​, and part (i) proves
HK​=Q(2​,−3​,5​).​
(48)
This is the Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus thirty.
Next translate prime representation into an ideal class. For p∤30, we claim
p=2x2+15y2 for integers x,y⟺some prime P of norm p has [P]=[p2​].
(49)
If a representation exists, α=2x+yt lies in p2​ and has NK/Q​α=2p. The integral ideal (α)p2−1​ has norm p, so it is a prime ideal P. Its class is [p2​]−1=[p2​]. Conversely, if [P]=[p2​], the product p2​P is principal, say (α). Since this ideal lies in p2​, its generator has the form α=2x+yt with integers x,y. Taking norms gives 4x2+30y2=2p, proving the reverse implication. This is prime representation via an ideal class, and it proves sufficiency as well as necessity.
Identify the required class in Gal(H/K) through the Artin map. We can use the explicit representation 17=2+15: 2+t has norm 34, so the prime above 17 obtained from (2+t)p2−1​ has class [p2​]. Its Frobenius in the multiquadratic extension acts on the three radicals with signs
((172​),(17−3​),(175​))=(1,−1,−1).
(50)
Their product is one, so this automorphism fixes K. Because H/Q is abelian, for any unramified rational prime p that splits in K, the Frobenius at either prime of K of norm p is its rational Frobenius restricted to H/K. The Artin isomorphism therefore makes the required class condition equivalent to
(p2​)=1,(p−3​)=−1,(p5​)=−1.
(51)
Conversely, these signs already imply splitting in K, since their product is (p−30​)=1. No extra splitting condition is missing.
The supplementary law for 2, quadratic reciprocity for −3 and 5, and then the Chinese remainder theorem turn these sign conditions into
p≡1 or 7(mod8),p≡2(mod3),p≡2 or 3(mod5).
(52)
Their four simultaneous residue classes modulo 120 are 17,23,47,113. Finally, 2 is represented by (x,y)=(±1,0), whereas 3 and 5 cannot be represented: y=0 gives a value at least 15, and y=0 gives twice a square. We have proved the complete criterion
p=2x2+15y2 is soluble⟺p=2 or p≡17,23,47,113(mod120).​
(53)
