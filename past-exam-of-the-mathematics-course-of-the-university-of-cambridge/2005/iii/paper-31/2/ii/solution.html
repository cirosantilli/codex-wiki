For a,b∈Qp×​, the quadratic Hilbert symbol (a,b)p​ is one if b is a field norm from Qp​(a​), and minus one otherwise; a square a gives the trivial extension and symbol one. It depends only on the two square classes. Equivalently, the conic z2=ax2+by2 has a nonzero rational point precisely when the symbol is one. Interchanging a and b in the conic gives (a,b)p​=(b,a)p​. The Hilbert norm residue symbol is a group homomorphism in each argument; for nonsquare a, the cyclic local norm index makes its group kernel a subgroup of index two. The resulting pairing on square classes is nondegenerate. Also
(a,−a)p​=1,(a,a)p​=(a,−1)p​,
(16)
since N(a​)=−a. In the language of local class field theory, (a,b)p​=Artp​(b)(a​)/a​; in the Brauer group, it records whether the quaternion cyclic algebra (a,b) splits. These are compatible norm-obstruction descriptions.
An unramified extension of local fields of degree two has norm group consisting precisely of elements with even valuation. Indeed,
vK​(Nz)=2vL​(z),
(17)
and the norms on units are surjective. For the latter assertion, the norm on the finite residue fields is surjective. On successive higher principal-unit groups, the congruence
N(1+πrt)≡1+πrTrκL​/κK​​(tˉ)(modπr+1)
(18)
allows one to correct a proposed unit norm one digit at a time: the residue field trace is surjective, and completeness makes the corrections converge. An unramified quadratic field is unique up to isomorphism. Therefore, if d is its defining nonsquare class,
(d,b)p​=(−1)vp​(b).​
(19)
For odd p, choose the nonsquare unit u0​ from part (i). The polynomial T2−u0​ has irreducible separable reduction, so it defines the unramified quadratic extension. Thus
Δ1​={1,u0​},Δ2​={1,u0​}.
(20)
The trivial class is included in Δ2​, since a degree-one extension is unramified. The two units pair trivially, while (u0​,p)p​=−1. Any class outside Δ1​ has odd valuation and consequently pairs nontrivially with u0​. This proves directly
Δ1⊥​=Δ2​,Δ2⊥​=Δ1​(p=2).​
(21)
For p=2, the unit square classes from part (i) give Δ1​={1,−1,5,−5}. The element ω=(1+5​)/2 satisfies T2−T−1; its reduction T2+T+1 is irreducible and separable over F2​. This shows that Q2​(5​) is unramified of degree two, giving
Δ1​={1,−1,5,−5},Δ2​={1,5}.
(22)
For a fully explicit pairing check, use the generators (−1,5,2). We have (−1,5)2​=1 and (−1,2)2​=1, since 5=N(1+2i) and 2=N(1+i) from Q2​(i). But (−1,−1)2​=−1: a norm equal to −1 would be a sum of two squares. If either summand had negative valuation, the sum would have negative valuation (equal negative valuations give valuation 1−2r<0); if both were integral, their squares could not sum to −1≡3(mod4). Finally, the unramified norm criterion gives (5,2)2​=−1. The identity (a,b)2​=(b,a)2​ and (a,a)2​=(a,−1)2​ determine the remaining entries. Writing (a,b)2​=(−1)B(a,b), the exponent matrix is
B=​100​001​010​​in the basis (−1,5,2).
(23)
For b=(−1)s5t2r, annihilating both generators −1,5 of Δ1​ requires s=r=0, so b∈{1,5}. Annihilating 5 alone requires r=0, so b is a unit class. Hence
Δ1⊥​=Δ2​,Δ2⊥​=Δ1​(p=2).​
(24)
These are the Hilbert-symbol annihilators of units and unramified square classes. Annihilators here are taken with respect to this pairing, or equivalently in the character group of a finite abelian group after identifying square classes with their Hilbert-symbol characters.
