Fix z∈K. If z∈∂K, the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise z is in the interior of K. If f(z)=0, nonnegativity of the boundary supremum suffices. For f(z)=0, the complex Hahn-Banach theorem gives a bounded linear functional ℓ:A→C with ∥ℓ∥=1 and ℓ(f(z))=∥f(z)∥. The scalar function g=ℓ∘f is holomorphic.
Let V be the connected component of the interior of K containing z. It is bounded, its closure is contained in K, and ∂V⊆∂K. To see the last inclusion, a boundary point of V lying in the interior of K would have a small connected open ball in that interior meeting V; the ball would belong to the same component, contradicting that it is a boundary point. The scalar maximum modulus principle on a bounded domain therefore gives
∥f(z)∥=∣g(z)∣≤supw∈∂V​∣g(w)∣≤supw∈∂K​∥f(w)∥.

For completeness, this boundary form of the maximum modulus principle follows by taking the maximum on the compact closure of V. An interior maximum makes g constant on V, in which case continuity gives the same value on its nonempty boundary. Thus the norm maximum principle for a Banach-space-valued holomorphic function applies to arbitrary compact K, even when it is disconnected or has nonsmooth boundary:
∥f(z)∥≤w∈∂Ksup​∥f(w)∥.​
