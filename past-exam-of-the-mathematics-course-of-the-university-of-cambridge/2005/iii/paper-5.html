Here is an explicit finite-exponent proof of the remaining Generalized Holder inequality. Put qi​=pi​/r, so ∑i​1/qi​=1, and normalize hi​=∣fi​∣r/∥fi​∥pi​r​. If one norm is zero the product vanishes almost everywhere. Otherwise ∫hiqi​​=1. The weighted arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, applied to hiqi​​ with weights 1/qi​, gives
∏i​hi​≤∑i​qi​hiqi​​​.
(1)
Integrating gives ∫∏i​hi​≤1. If only one finite exponent remains, its weight is one and the same argument is an equality. Restoring the normalization and taking the rth root proves
∥f1​⋯fn​∥r​≤i=1∏n​∥fi​∥pi​​.​
(2)
In particular the product belongs to Lr.
For the projection estimate, use the three-dimensional functional Loomis-Whitney inequality
∫R3​(g1​(x2​,x3​)g2​(x1​,x3​)g3​(x1​,x2​))1/2dx≤∏j=13​(∫R2​gj​)1/2.

This permitted three-dimensional result also follows directly by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality: integrate the square root of g1​g2​ in x3​, obtaining the square root of the product of their marginal integrals, and then apply Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in (x1​,x2​) against g3​​.
Let fj​ be any nonnegative integrable function of the three coordinates excluding xj​. For j=1,2,3, define Gj​ by integrating fj​ in x4​. First Hölder's inequality with three equal exponents yields
∫R​∏j=13​fj​(xj​)1/3dx4​≤∏j=13​Gj1/3​.

The fourth factor f4​(x1​,x2​,x3​) is independent of x4​. A further Hölder's inequality with exponents 3 and 3/2, followed by the three-dimensional estimate, therefore gives
∫R4​j=1∏4​fj​(xj​)1/3dx​≤(∫R3​f4​)1/3(∫R3​j=1∏3​Gj1/2​)2/3≤j=1∏4​(∫R3​fj​)1/3.​

Read equality of the two positive series as equality of a finite total M, so the sequences are summable. This is the majorization of summable sequences setting. If equality is instead allowed to mean ∞=∞, the assertion is false: the strictly decreasing sequences xi​=1+1/(i+1) and yi​=2+1/(i+1) satisfy every partial-sum inequality and have divergent equal extended totals. But a normalized nonnegative row of any doubly stochastic matrix averages numbers all at least 2, and cannot give xi​<2. We prove the intended finite-total assertion constructively.
Since yj​→0 and 0<x1​≤y1​, there is a finite k such that yk​≥x1​>yk+1​. Put
t=yk​−yk+1​x1​−yk+1​​∈(0,1],u=yk​+yk+1​−x1​.
(7)
The two-coordinate stochastic averaging matrix sends (yk​,yk+1​) to (x1​,u). Move the first of these values to the front by a finite permutation, leaving the other values in decreasing order. The tail is
z=(y1​,…,yk−1​,u,yk+2​,yk+3​,…),
(8)
which is decreasing and strictly positive because yk+1​≤u≤yk​.
We need the residual majorization in order to repeat the construction. For n<k, the first n values of z are at least x1​, while every target-tail value is at most x1​. Thus
∑j=1n​zj​≥nx1​≥∑i=2n+1​xi​.
(9)
For n≥k, the residual initial sum is the old initial sum with x1​ removed:
∑j=1n​zj​=∑j=1n+1​yj​−x1​≥∑i=2n+1​xi​.
(10)
The tail totals also agree, both equalling M−x1​. Therefore the same step applies to (x2​,x3​,…) and z.
Iterating, let P(m) be the product of the first m finite permutations and embedded averaging matrices. Each P(m) is a doubly stochastic matrix, each row is a finitely supported sequence, and the first m entries of P(m)y are x1​,…,xm​. Later operations act only on tail rows, so row i becomes permanently fixed once m≥i. Define pij​ to be that stabilized row. Then
pij​≥0,∑j​pij​=1,xi​=∑j​pij​yj​.
(11)
For every fixed column j and every m, its first m stabilized entries sum to at most one, since they are entries of column j in P(m). Consequently sj​=∑i​pij​≤1. By Tonelli's theorem,
M=∑i​xi​=∑j​yj​sj​,0=∑j​yj​(1−sj​).
(12)
All yj​ are strictly positive and every summand in the last expression is nonnegative, so sj​=1 for every j. Thus both the row sums and the column sums are one, and
x=Pywith P doubly stochastic.​
(13)
The finite-total argument is what prevents loss of column mass in the infinite construction. This completes the doubly stochastic realization of summable-sequence majorization.

A nonnegative sublinear operator satisfies S(f+g)≤S(f)+S(g) and S(cf)=∣c∣S(f) almost everywhere. It has weak type (1,1) if there is a finite constant C, independent of the function and threshold, such that
λ{x:S(f)(x)>α}≤αC​∥f∥L1(R)​for every f∈L1(R), α>0.​
(14)
For a signed or complex output use ∣S(f)∣ in the level set. The bound controls the distribution of large values; it does not assert that S(f) is integrable.

Let Uα​={x:mu​(f)(x)>α}. It is open: it is the union of all open intervals I for which ∫I​∣f∣>α∣I∣. This also proves measurability of the uncentered maximal function. Triangle inequality inside each average proves sublinearity and absolute homogeneity.
Take a compact subset K⊂Uα​, and choose finitely many such intervals covering it. The one-dimensional Wiener covering lemma selects pairwise disjoint intervals I1​,…,IN​ whose concentric triples cover K. For a finite family this follows by choosing a longest remaining interval and discarding all intervals intersecting it: each discarded interval has no greater length and lies in the triple of the chosen one. Thus
λ(K)≤3∑j=1N​∣Ij​∣<α3​∑j=1N​∫Ij​​∣f∣≤α3​∥f∥1​.
(21)
Taking the supremum over compact subsets, by inner regularity of Lebesgue measure, gives
λ{mu​(f)>α}≤α3​∥f∥1​.​
(22)
This establishes weak type (1,1). It is the one-dimensional uncentered maximal weak-type inequality for the finite measure ∣f(t)∣dt; the constant need not be optimal for this question.

For r>0, define the forward and backward one-sided interval averaging operators
Ar+​f(x)=r1​∫xx+r​f(t)dt,Ar−​f(x)=r1​∫x−rx​f(t)dt,
(23)
and set A0±​f=f. Each positive-radius average is bounded in absolute value by mu​(f)(x). Although x is an endpoint of its averaging interval, enlarge that interval slightly so that x is interior, apply the maximal bound and let the enlargement shrink to zero.
To dominate the limiting identity too without assuming the differentiation result we are proving, use the sublinear operator
S(f)=mu​(f)+∣f∣.
(24)
The maximal estimate and Chebyshev inequality imply
λ{S(f)>α}≤λ{mu​(f)>α/2}+λ{∣f∣>α/2}≤α8​∥f∥1​.
(25)
Thus S has weak type (1,1) and dominates every Ar±​, including r=0.
Smooth compactly supported functions are dense in L1, obtained by truncating and then smoothing with a compactly supported approximate identity. For each such continuous function both averages tend to its value at every point. Applying part (ii) to the two families, and intersecting their full-measure convergence sets, gives Ar±​f(x)→f(x) almost everywhere.
The forward difference quotient of the primitive is Ar+​f(x), and the backward quotient is Ar−​f(x):
rF(x+r)−F(x)​=Ar+​f(x),−rF(x−r)−F(x)​=Ar−​f(x).
(26)
They have the same limit, proving differentiability almost everywhere, with the stronger conclusion
F′(x)=f(x)almost everywhere.​
(27)
This proves differentiation of an indefinite Lebesgue integral directly from the maximal estimate and density, without assuming mu​(f)≥∣f∣ as an unproved prerequisite.

Consider the complex-linear two-coordinate map H(x,y)=(x+y,x−y). The triangle inequality shows that its operator norm from ℓ1 to ℓ∞ is one; equality is attained at (1,0). The complex parallelogram identity
∣x+y∣2+∣x−y∣2=2(∣x∣2+∣y∣2)
(36)
shows that its norm from ℓ2 to ℓ2 is 2​. Interpolate these endpoint bounds by the Riesz-Thorin theorem with parameter η=2/p. Then
p′1​=1−2η​,p1​=2η​,∥H:ℓp′→ℓp∥≤(2​)η=21/p.
(37)
Dividing the output norm by 21/p proves the scalar estimate:
(2∣x+y∣p+∣x−y∣p​)1/p≤(∣x∣p′+∣y∣p′)1/p′.​
(38)
Apply it pointwise to f,g and integrate its pth power. With r=p/p′=p−1>1, this gives
(2∥f+g∥pp​+∥f−g∥pp​​)1/p≤​∣f∣p′+∣g∣p′​r1/p′​.
(39)
By Minkowski inequality, the expression on the right is at most
(​∣f∣p′​r​+​∣g∣p′​r​)1/p′=(∥f∥pp′​+∥g∥pp′​)1/p′.
(40)
Therefore the requested function inequality holds, namely the dual-exponent Clarkson inequality.

First use independent fair signs εi​∈{−1,1}, the Rademacher distribution convention for Bernoulli random variables in this setting. Let Ω={−1,1}d with uniform probability, write s(ω)=∑i​ωi​ai​ and F(ω)=∥s(ω)∥. We prove the sharp Rademacher second-moment inequality by a complete finite-cube calculation.
For a function on this cube define the coordinate-flip generator on a hypercube
Lu(ω)=21​∑i=1d​(u(ω(i))−u(ω)),
(41)
where ω(i) flips just coordinate i. The Walsh characters χA​(ω)=∏i∈A​ωi​ form an orthonormal basis: distinct characters have expectation-zero product, and there are 2d of them. Flipping a coordinate in A changes its sign and other flips leave it unchanged, so LχA​=−∣A∣χA​. Expand the real function F as ∑A​F(A)χA​. Since F(−ω)=F(ω), changing variables ω↦−ω shows that F(A)=0 whenever ∣A∣ is odd. Thus
−E[FLF]=∑A​∣A∣F(A)2≥2∑A=∅​F(A)2=2Var(F).
(42)
This derives the even-function spectral gap on a hypercube, rather than assuming it.
At each vertex with s(ω)=0, choose a norming functional ℓω​ of norm one satisfying ℓω​(s(ω))=F(ω), supplied by the Hahn-Banach theorem. For complex E take real parts in the following inequalities. At a vertex with s(ω)=0, use the zero functional. The supporting inequality for the norm gives
F(ω(i))−F(ω)≥Reℓω​(s(ω(i))−s(ω)).
(43)
But Ls=−s, since every coefficient is a degree-one Walsh character. Summing the preceding inequalities with factor 1/2 therefore proves LF(ω)≥−F(ω). As F≥0,
2(EF2−(EF)2)≤−E[FLF]≤EF2.
(44)
Rearranging and taking square roots yields
​i∑​εi​ai​​L2(E)​≤2​​i∑​εi​ai​​L1(E)​.​
(45)
No completeness of E is needed. The constant is sharp: with two identical nonzero scalar coefficients, the absolute sum is zero with probability 1/2 and twice the coefficient magnitude with probability 1/2.
If Bernoulli instead means fair {0,1} variables bi​, write bi​=(1+εi​)/2 and a=∑i​ai​. Add an independent fair sign ε0​ and apply the proved estimate to Y=ε0​a+∑i​εi​ai​. Conditional on either sign of ε0​, ∥Y∥ has the same distribution as ∥a+∑i​εi​ai​∥, by symmetry of the sign sum. Hence ∥Y∥Lp(E)​=2∥∑i​bi​ai​∥Lp(E)​ for p=1,2, proving the same bound in that convention.
The constant is documented in the primary sharp-constant study.
Fairness is essential. For a general {0,1} Bernoulli random variable with success probability 0<t<1/2, a single nonzero scalar coefficient gives L2/L1=1/t​>2​. Thus the printed term cannot be read as permitting arbitrary Bernoulli probabilities.
