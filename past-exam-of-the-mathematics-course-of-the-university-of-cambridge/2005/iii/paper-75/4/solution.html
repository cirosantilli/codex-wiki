Use incompressible ideal MHD at scales above the ion skin depth, a strong uniform mean field, and a statistically steady local energy cascade with mean flux ϵ per unit mass. Assume balanced counterpropagating wave populations, comparable velocity and magnetic amplitudes in velocity units, and no additional scale-dependent alignment or intermittency correction. Write b=δB/4πρ​ and introduce the fluctuation Elsässer variables z±=u±b. Their equations are
∂t​z±∓vA​∂∥​z±+(z∓⋅∇)z±=−∇Π,∇⋅z±=0.
(40)
Only opposite populations distort one another. A single population has no leading nonlinear self-interaction, so the balance assumption is essential to a one-amplitude cascade estimate.
Let δzl​ denote the typical balanced amplitude and let l⊥​,l∥​ be coherence lengths relative to the local mean field. The Alfvén wave time and eddy turnover time are
τA​=vA​l∥​​,τnl​=δzl​l⊥​​,χ=τnl​τA​​.
(41)
For χ≪1, one counterpropagating encounter changes the packet fractionally by order χ. Assume phases decorrelate between encounters. After N encounters, random accumulation is of order N​χ; an order-one change requires N∼χ−2. The weak wave cascade time is therefore
τcas​∼NτA​=τA​τnl2​​.
(42)
Weakness by itself does not prove this random walk estimate; phase decorrelation and the interaction geometry are additional assumptions.
For the Iroshnikov-Kraichnan spectrum, impose isotropy, l∥​∼l⊥​∼l, in addition to δzl​≪vA​. Then τA​=l/vA​≪τnl​=l/δzl​ and
τcas​∼δzl2​vA​l​,ϵ∼τcas​δzl2​​∼vA​lδzl4​​.
(43)
Hence δzl​∼(ϵvA​l)1/4. A local shell-integrated turbulent energy spectrum has δzl2​∼kE(k), k∼l−1, so
EIK​(k)∼(ϵvA​)1/2k−3/2.​
(44)
The isotropy is imposed phenomenologically; a strong guide field actually distinguishes directions and does not force this isotropic picture. The spectrum is not an automatically valid asymptotic result for every strongly magnetized cascade.
For the anisotropic weak Alfvénic cascade, keep l∥​ approximately fixed while cascading to smaller l⊥​, and require χ≪1. This geometry follows from resonant three-wave Alfvén interactions: frequency conservation together with parallel-wavenumber conservation makes one member of an oppositely propagating resonant triad have zero parallel wavenumber, so the propagating modes retain their parallel wavenumber in the leading resonant transfer. Thus there is no leading parallel cascade in this ideal weak-wave ordering. Using the fixed parallel scale,
τcas​∼δzl2​l∥​vA​l⊥2​​,ϵ∼vA​l⊥2​δzl4​l∥​​.
(45)
Therefore δzl​∼(ϵvA​/l∥​)1/4l⊥1/2​. For the one-dimensional perpendicular spectrum, δzl2​∼k⊥​E⊥​(k⊥​) gives
E⊥,weak​(k⊥​)∼(l∥​ϵvA​​)1/2k⊥−2​.​
(46)
Unlike the isotropic estimate, the parallel length does not decrease with the perpendicular length. Since χ=δzl​l∥​/(vA​l⊥​)∝l⊥−1/2​, the interactions become stronger down the cascade. If χ0​≪1 at outer perpendicular scale L⊥​, the weak approximation breaks at l⊥,∗​∼L⊥​χ02​. This is the weak-to-strong transition of an Alfvénic cascade.
For Goldreich–Sridhar turbulence, abandon the weak-collision estimate and impose critical balance, τA​∼τnl​. The local cascade is strong, with transfer time of order one eddy turnover time. Then
ϵ∼l⊥​δzl3​​,δzl​∼(ϵl⊥​)1/3,E⊥,GS​(k⊥​)∼ϵ2/3k⊥−5/3​.​
(47)
The parallel scale is set dynamically, not held fixed:
l∥​∼vA​ϵ−1/3l⊥2/3​,k∥​∼vA​ϵ1/3​k⊥2/3​.​
(48)
At smaller scales l∥​/l⊥​ grows, so the eddies are increasingly elongated along the local magnetic field. Strong turbulence does not mean δzl​∼vA​: small transverse amplitudes can still be strong when the eddies are sufficiently anisotropic to satisfy critical balance. Imbalance, alignment or nonlocal interactions would require different cascade reasoning.
Finally, in the usual isotropic hydrodynamic Kolmogorov 1941 theory inertial-range argument, the only relevant dimensional quantities are ϵ and k. With [ϵ]=L2T−3 and [E]=L3T−2, writing E∝ϵakb forces a=2/3, b=−5/3. This uses the physical assumptions that outer-scale, viscous and intermittency effects do not supply another leading dependence; dimensions alone are not a proof of those assumptions.
A magnetic guide field supplies the independent speed vA​, and anisotropy adds independent parallel lengths. Even before adding anisotropy, dimensions permit
E(k)=ϵ2/3k−5/3Φ(vA​ϵ−1/3k1/3),​
(49)
with arbitrary dimensionless Φ. For example Φ(x)∝x1/2 reproduces the isotropic Iroshnikov-Kraichnan spectrum estimate. This dimensional freedom of an MHD spectrum explains why propagation, decorrelation, anisotropy and cascade-time assumptions must select among the spectra: purely dimensional considerations do not select a unique MHD exponent.
