The preceding constructions lead to the Iwasawa theorem on local cyclotomic units. Its significance is that an explicitly calculated local unit quotient is governed by a p-adic L-function; class field theory then suggests that the same analytic object should govern global arithmetic. The latter prediction is the Iwasawa main conjecture, rather than a consequence of the local calculation alone.
Fix compatible roots of unity ζpn​ and put Kn​=Qp​(ζpn​), πn​=ζpn​−1. Let V∞​ be the norm limit of the local principal units. Take its even part under complex conjugation to obtain U∞1​, the local principal-unit limit in the real tower. Let C∞1​ be the closed subgroup generated by norm-compatible real cyclotomic units, with their finite-order residue factors removed. Write
G=Zp×​/{±1},ΛG​=Zp​[[G]],I(G)=ker(ΛG​⟶Zp​).
(23)
Here the last map is augmentation. Normalize the p-adic zeta pseudomeasure ζp​ by
∫G​xkdζp​=(1−pk−1)ζ(1−k)=−(1−pk−1)kBk​​(k≥2 even).
(24)
The expression xk is well defined on G for even k. A pseudomeasure lies in the total fraction ring of the Iwasawa algebra; the pole on its trivial finite-character component is removed by the augmentation ideal, so I(G)ζp​⊂ΛG​. In this normalization the local theorem is
U∞1​/C∞1​≃ΛG​/(I(G)ζp​).​
(25)
The following steps outline its proof and explain where the earlier questions enter.
First encode local units by Coleman power series. Evaluation at πn​ is surjective from R to OKn​​, and the product defining the Coleman norm operator gives
(Nf)(πn−1​)=NKn​/Kn−1​​(f(πn​))(n≥2).
(26)
Thus norm-fixed series give norm-compatible sequences of local units. Conversely, lift um​ from such a sequence to f(m)∈R× and take gm​=Nm−rm​f(m), where rm​=⌊m/2⌋. This has gm​(πrm​​)=urm​​. The estimates in Question 2 give Ngm​/gm​∈1+pm−rm​+1R. Consequently for fixed n≤rm​, the series Nrm​−ngm​ differs from gm​ modulo pm−rm​+1 and its value at πn​ is un​. The compactness of R in the coarser of the topologies on integral formal power series supplies a convergent subsequence. Continuity of the Coleman norm operator and of evaluation shows that its limit is norm-fixed and takes every value un​. It is a unit because the original principal units have residue 1. Uniqueness follows from the Weierstrass preparation theorem: a nonzero series in R has only finitely many zeros in the open unit disk, whereas the distinct πn​ give infinitely many interpolation points. This constructs the Coleman power series fu​.
Next turn its multiplicative information into an integral p-adic measure. For a norm-fixed unit f, use the Frobenius-corrected logarithm
L(f)=p1​log(ϕ(f)fp​).
(27)
The ratio belongs to 1+pR by reduction modulo p, and the logarithm divided by p is integral because p is odd. Equivalently L(f)=(1−ϕ/p)logf, interpreting the constant coefficient with the p-adic logarithm on units. The averaging identity for the logarithm is understood on the open p-adic unit disk, where the translated logarithmic series converge; the corrected result itself lies in R. The logarithmic product formula and Question 1 give
Slogf=p1​log(Nf),SL(f)=p1​log(Nf/f)=0.
(28)
Under the Amice transform, the root-of-unity average projects a p-adic measure onto its restriction to pZp​: the averaged factor (1+T)x survives exactly for x∈pZp​. Consequently SL(f)=0 says that λ(L(f)) is supported on Zp×​. This gives a Galois group-equivariant homomorphism from local principal units to the Iwasawa algebra of Zp×​.
The integral lifting step in this proof is the Coleman unit-measure exact sequence
0⟶Zp​(1)⟶V∞​Col​Zp​[[Zp×​]]m1​​Zp​(1)⟶0,m1​(μ)=∫xdμ.
(29)
Here Col(u)=λ(L(fu​)). The last map is surjective, since m1​(δ1​)=1, and it has the cyclotomic action on its target. Its composition with Col is zero: Dϕ=pϕD implies DL(f)(0)=0. The kernel on the unit side consists of norm-compatible p-power roots of unity. To see the shape of the kernel, write z=log(1+T); the equation L(f)=0 becomes h(pz)=ph(z) for h(z)=logf(ez−1), so only its linear term can survive. Integral solutions are f=(1+T)c, c∈Zp​, after excluding finite residue factors by the principal unit condition. For the image, use the following explicit integral lifting of the corrected cyclotomic logarithm.
Take a p-adic measure μ on the units with first moment zero, and write g for its Amice transform. Then Sg=0 and g′(0)=0. Put H=exp(pg)∈1+pR and solve fp=Hϕ(f) with f(0)∈1+pZp​. The constant equation is solved by f0​=exp(pg0​/(p−1)). In degree one the terms involving f1​ cancel, and the remaining condition is H1​=0, exactly g1​=0; choose any f1​∈Zp​. For n≥2, after fixing the lower coefficients, the coefficient multiplying the unknown fn​ is
pf0p−1​(1−pn−1).
(30)
The other terms are divisible by p: for the already integral truncated series, fp≡ϕ(f)(modp), while H≡1(modp). Division therefore gives an integral fn​, since f0​ and 1−pn−1 are units. Recursion constructs f∈R× with L(f)=g. Now pSg=log(Nf/f)=0, and Nf/f∈1+pR by Question 2. The p-adic logarithm is injective there, so Nf=f. This supplies the desired Coleman power series of a norm-compatible sequence of local units. The freely chosen linear coefficient accounts precisely for the kernel (1+T)c. It proves the integral surjectivity step, including the integrality that rational inversion of 1−ϕ/p would leave unproved.
Complex conjugation acts by −1 on both copies of Zp​(1). As p is odd, taking the even part is exact and removes both end terms. It follows that Col:U∞1​≃ΛG​. The integral exact sequence is essential here: the support calculation alone would not prove this isomorphism.
It remains to calculate the image of the cyclotomic units. For a,b∈Zp×​, consider the symmetric cyclotomic unit series
fa,b​(T)=X(b−a)/21−Xb1−Xa​.
(31)
The quotient has an invertible constant term a/b, because both numerator and denominator have a simple zero at T=0. The factor X(b−a)/2 makes the expression unchanged under X↦X−1. The product over the roots of unity gives Nfa,b​=fa,b​; one may verify this first for integer a,b prime to p and then extend by continuity. Dividing by the norm-compatible Teichmuller representative makes the series correspond to principal units and does not change its logarithm. The generating function for the Bernoulli numbers, with z=logX, gives
logfa,b​(ez−1)=log(a/b)+∑k≥2k even​​kk!(ak−bk)Bk​​zk.
(32)
The symmetric factor cancels the linear term. Applying 1−ϕ/p, and then the moment formula of Question 3, yields
∫xkdCol(fa,b​)=(1−pk−1)(ak−bk)kBk​​=∫xkd(([b]−[a])ζp​)
(33)
for even k≥2. These moments determine an even p-adic measure on the units: polynomials are dense by the Mahler theorem, even projection preserves density, and multiplication by x2 is invertible on the units, so the positive even-degree polynomials are dense there as well. Thus
Col(fa,b​)=([b]−[a])ζp​.
(34)
The closed ideal generated by all differences [b]−[a] is I(G). The closed subgroup generated by the corresponding cyclotomic units, including its Galois group translates, therefore has image exactly I(G)ζp​. Taking the quotient of the isomorphism U∞1​≃ΛG​ proves the boxed local theorem.
Finally introduce global arithmetic. Let E∞1​ be the closure of norm-compatible global units in the real tower, let X∞+​ be its p-ramified Iwasawa module, and let Y∞+​ be its unramified Iwasawa module. With compatible unit and splitting conventions, class field theory gives the class-field unit sequence; quotienting its unit terms by C∞1​ gives
0⟶E∞1​/C∞1​⟶U∞1​/C∞1​⟶X∞+​⟶Y∞+​⟶0.
(35)
In the cyclotomic tower the prime above p is principal, so the unramified class-field convention also makes it split. On the torsion character components, multiplicativity of characteristic ideals implies
char(U∞1​/C∞1​)char(Y∞+​)=char(E∞1​/C∞1​)char(X∞+​).
(36)
The local theorem has already calculated the first factor analytically. Equality of the global unit and class-group factors would therefore identify the characteristic ideal of X∞+​ with the same ideal of the p-adic L-function. That is the content predicted by the Iwasawa main conjecture. More explicitly, for a nontrivial even finite character χ, the character component of I(G) is the whole component and the prediction is
charΛχ​​(Xχ+​)=(gχ​),gχ​((1+p)1−s−1)=Lp​(χ,s).​
(37)
Here Λχ​ is the power-series algebra of the pro-p part with coefficients in the character-value ring. The trivial component requires the augmentation correction for the zeta pole. Equivalently the characteristic ideals of E∞1​/C∞1​ and Y∞+​ agree componentwise. A formulation using odd class-group characters must include the appropriate cyclotomic twist and inversion, as in Kummer reflection in Iwasawa theory. The motivation is an analytic formula for local units together with a global class-field exact sequence; the global equality is the additional arithmetic assertion.
The related class-number growth theorem illustrates why passage to an Iwasawa module is powerful. For the cyclotomic Zp​-extension, let pen​ be the p-part of the class number at level n. The unramified Iwasawa torsion theorem, the Iwasawa module structure theorem and the class-group control relations give, for sufficiently large n,
en​=μpn+λn+ν.
(38)
Here μ,λ are the Iwasawa invariants. To see the two growth terms, use a topological generator γ and, after a fixed sufficiently high level n0​, the norm polynomial νn,n0​​=(γpn−1)/(γpn0​−1). An elementary summand Λ/(pa) contributes a(pn−pn0​) to the length of its quotient by this polynomial. A summand associated with a distinguished polynomial of degree d contributes dn plus a constant: at each root α the valuation of the norm polynomial is n plus a fixed constant for large n. If 1+α is a root of unity already killed at level n0​, its value is instead exactly pn−n0​ and gives the same linear contribution. The finite errors in the structure and control maps stabilize and contribute to ν. Thus module structure explains growth, while the Iwasawa main conjecture goes further by identifying the characteristic series of an Iwasawa module themselves with analytic ones.
