Let Mf​ be the matrix of multiplication by f in the basis from Question 1. Define the Coleman norm operator by the norm for a finite free ring extension:
ϕ(Nf)=det(Mf​).​
(5)
The entries lie in ϕ(R), and ϕ:R→ϕ(R) is an isomorphism, so this determines Nf uniquely. If f is a unit, Mf​ is invertible and its determinant is a unit. Also Mfg​=Mf​Mg​, giving N(fg)=NfNg. Splitting the conjugates, or diagonalizing multiplication after adjoining the roots of unity and inverting p, gives the equivalent formula
ϕ(Nf)(T)=∏ζ∈μp​​f(ζ(1+T)−1).
(6)
In particular, this definition has the required target R×, rather than merely a ring with extra roots of unity in its coefficients.
For the requested ring congruence, observe that the reduction of ϕ is the injective map g​(T)↦g​(Tp). If ϕ(g)∈pkR, its reduction first gives g=pg1​. Then ϕ(g1​)∈pk−1R; repeat to obtain g∈pkR. Applying this to g=f−1 proves
ϕ(f)≡1(modpkR) ⟹ f≡1(modpkR).​
(7)
This congruence reflection for Frobenius substitution is valid for all f∈R, without assuming f is a unit.
A further property needed for the fixed points is Coleman norm contraction. If g=1+pkh with k≥1, expand the determinant:
ϕ(Ng)=det(I+pkMh​)=1+pkTr(Mh​)+∑j=2p​pkjej​(Mh​).
(8)
Here ej​(Mh​)∈ϕ(R) is the coefficient of degree j in det(I+tMh​). The trace formula from Question 1 makes the linear term divisible by pk+1; every later term is divisible by p2k and 2k≥k+1. Reflecting the resulting ring congruence through ϕ gives
N(1+pkR)⊆1+pk+1R.
(9)
If Nf=f and f∈1+pR, iteration gives f∈1+pkR for every k. Since ⋂k​pkR=0, f=1.
To identify all fixed points, we also need Nf≡f(modpR) for every unit f. In characteristic p, the extension Fp​[[T]]/Fp​[[Tp]] has degree p and its norm for a finite free ring extension is f​↦f​p. Indeed, after passing to fraction fields it is a purely inseparable field extension of degree p, so multiplication has only the eigenvalue f​, with multiplicity p, after splitting. Its determinant is f​p. Therefore
ϕ(Nf)​=f​p=ϕ(f)​,
(10)
and congruence reflection for Frobenius substitution gives Nf≡f(modpR).
Now choose any lift f0​∈R× of a given a∈A× and put fn​=Nnf0​. Multiplicativity gives
fn​fn+1​​=Nn(f0​Nf0​​)∈1+pn+1R.
(11)
Thus (fn​) is a Cauchy sequence for the finer of the topologies on integral formal power series and converges to a unit w(a) reducing to a. The Coleman norm operator is continuous: multiplication matrices and their determinants are continuous, and the finite-free coordinate decomposition and its inverse preserve the p-adic filtration defining the finer of the topologies on integral formal power series. Hence Nw(a)=w(a). If two fixed units reduce to a, their quotient is fixed and belongs to 1+pR, so it is 1 by the preceding argument. This proves uniqueness, independence of the initial lift, and multiplicativity of the Coleman norm-fixed lift. In particular,
W ∼ reduction​A×,a⟼w(a)=n→∞lim​Nnf0​ in the inverse direction.​
(12)
Every unit f∈R consequently has a unique factorization f=w(f​)v with v∈1+pR, giving the canonical Coleman decomposition of power-series units:
R×≃W×(1+pR),f⟼(w(f​),w(f​)f​).​
(13)
The final assertion as printed is false. The original PDF identifies a ring with a product of unit groups. With multiplication, A has a zero and is not a group. With addition, it has exponent p, whereas W×A× has a nonidentity element of order 2: −1∈W because p is odd and N(−1)=(−1)p=−1. Thus neither interpretation makes the printed assertion true.
