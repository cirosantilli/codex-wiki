Let t,h be the thermal and magnetic scaling fields, with eigenvalues byt​ and byh​. After discarding irrelevant variables whose vanishing is nonsingular, the homogeneous singular free-energy density satisfies
fs​(t,h)=b−Dfs​(byt​t,byh​h).
(40)
Choose b=∣t∣−1/yt​. Then
fs​(t,h)=∣t∣D/yt​F±​(h∣t∣−yh​/yt​),ξ(t,h)=∣t∣−1/yt​X±​(h∣t∣−yh​/yt​).
(41)
Two thermal derivatives give the heat-capacity critical exponent; one and two field derivatives give the order-parameter critical exponent and magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent. The correlation-length critical exponent follows directly from the length rescaling. Thus
ν=yt​1​,α=2−yt​D​,βm​=yt​D−yh​​,γ=yt​2yh​−D​.​
(42)
At t=0, choose b=∣h∣−1/yh​. Differentiating fs​(0,h)∝∣h∣D/yh​ gives M∝sgn(h)∣h∣(D−yh​)/yh​, so δ=yh​/(D−yh​). The order parameter has scaling dimension xM​=D−yh​; therefore its critical connected correlation function has power r−2xM​. Comparing with r−(D−2+η) yields
δ=D−yh​yh​​,η=D+2−2yh​.​
(43)
These formulas express the requested critical exponents in terms of the relevant scaling exponents. Their hyperscaling assumptions must be respected: above an upper critical dimension, a dangerously irrelevant coupling can invalidate the free-energy homogeneity used here. Applying the formulas to the Gaussian thinning transformation in the root solution gives the requested Gaussian indices and their physical qualifications.
