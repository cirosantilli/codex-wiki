Critical exponents describe leading singular powers as the reduced temperature t or conjugate field approaches zero. With βm​ used for the order-parameter critical exponent to avoid confusion with inverse temperature, the definitions include
Cs​∼∣t∣−α,M(t,0)∼(−t)βm​,χ∼∣t∣−γ,ξ∼∣t∣−ν,M(0,h)∼sgn(h)∣h∣1/δ.
(9)
At criticality the connected correlation function behaves as G(r)∼r−(D−2+η). These definitions refer to the singular part and allow additional logarithms at marginal dimensions.
For an ordinary scalar Landau free energy, the equation of state is h=rM+uM3. Below the transition M2=−r/u, giving βm​=1/2. Differentiating the equation of state gives χ−1=r+3uM2: it equals r above and −2r below the transition, so γ=1. The quadratic fluctuation kernel is κk2+r in the symmetric phase, giving correlation length ξ=κ/r​ and ν=1/2. At zero mass the momentum-space correlation function is proportional to k−2, so η=0. The minimized free energy and critical equation of state give α=0 and δ=3, as calculated in the root solution.
At the tricritical point, u=0 and h=rM+vM5. Then M∼(−r)1/4, and χ−1=r+5vM4 is r above and −4r below the transition. The gradient kernel still gives ν=1/2 and η=0. Therefore
(α,βm​,γ,δ,ν,η)ordinary​=(0,1/2,1,3,1/2,0),(α,βm​,γ,δ,ν,η)tricritical​=(1/2,1/4,1,5,1/2,0).​
(10)
These are mean-field critical exponents. A systematic derivation incorporating fluctuations uses the renormalization group, extracts the relevant scaling eigenvalues, and differentiates the singular free-energy density, as in Question 3.
