For r>0, define the forward and backward one-sided interval averaging operators
Ar+​f(x)=r1​∫xx+r​f(t)dt,Ar−​f(x)=r1​∫x−rx​f(t)dt,
(23)
and set A0±​f=f. Each positive-radius average is bounded in absolute value by mu​(f)(x). Although x is an endpoint of its averaging interval, enlarge that interval slightly so that x is interior, apply the maximal bound and let the enlargement shrink to zero.
To dominate the limiting identity too without assuming the differentiation result we are proving, use the sublinear operator
S(f)=mu​(f)+∣f∣.
(24)
The maximal estimate and Chebyshev inequality imply
λ{S(f)>α}≤λ{mu​(f)>α/2}+λ{∣f∣>α/2}≤α8​∥f∥1​.
(25)
Thus S has weak type (1,1) and dominates every Ar±​, including r=0.
Smooth compactly supported functions are dense in L1, obtained by truncating and then smoothing with a compactly supported approximate identity. For each such continuous function both averages tend to its value at every point. Applying part (ii) to the two families, and intersecting their full-measure convergence sets, gives Ar±​f(x)→f(x) almost everywhere.
The forward difference quotient of the primitive is Ar+​f(x), and the backward quotient is Ar−​f(x):
rF(x+r)−F(x)​=Ar+​f(x),−rF(x−r)−F(x)​=Ar−​f(x).
(26)
They have the same limit, proving differentiability almost everywhere, with the stronger conclusion
F′(x)=f(x)almost everywhere.​
(27)
This proves differentiation of an indefinite Lebesgue integral directly from the maximal estimate and density, without assuming mu​(f)≥∣f∣ as an unproved prerequisite.
