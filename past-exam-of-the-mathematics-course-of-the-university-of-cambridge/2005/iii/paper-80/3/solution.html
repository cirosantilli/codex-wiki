Balanced variables and PV derivation. Write the disturbance reduced pressure as p′/ρ0​=f0​ψ. Leading geostrophic balance gives (ug​,vg​)=(−ψy​,ψx​), and leading hydrostatic balance gives buoyancy anomaly σ′=pz′​/ρ0​=f0​ψz​. The geostrophic material derivative is

(23)
It includes horizontal advection, not an independent prognostic vertical velocity. The appropriate regime has small Rossby number, small stratified Froude number, thin aspect ratio and slow evolution; a common scaling has [NH/(f0​L)]2 of order one and βL/∣f0​∣≪1.
Only the vertical rotation component is retained in the traditional approximation. In the horizontal momentum balance, the horizontal part of the rotation vector multiplies the small vertical velocity; in vertical momentum, its contribution is small compared with leading hydrostatic pressure and buoyancy in the thin-layer scaling. Away from the equator these corrections are smaller by a factor of order (H/L)cotλ. They need not be small in equatorial or strongly nonhydrostatic settings, so the approximation has a definite regime of validity.

A northward parcel displacement has q′=−βξy​: it retains the smaller background PV of its origin. The inverted geostrophic velocity associated with a positive PV crest is northward on its eastern flank and southward on its western flank. Advection of the background PV then decreases the anomaly on the eastern flank and increases it on the western flank, shifting the crest westward. This is a PV-restoring mechanism, not a westward mean current.

If U<0 or U>β/k2, define

Exclude exponential growth at infinity. The unique decaying forced component is
ψ′(x,z)=f0​λN2b0​​e−λzsinkx,σ′=−N2b0​e−λzsinkx,w′=Ukb0​e−λzcoskx.​

At the excluded value U=β/k2, a nonzero prescribed bottom derivative cannot be matched by a bounded constant/linear vertical solution; the steady idealization is singular. At U=0 the boundary is not crossed and there is no forced response in the linear nonzero-k sector: ψ′=0, rather than an expression obtained by dividing by U. Uniqueness throughout refers to the prescribed forced Fourier component, with the reference state fixed and no independently incident waves or added mean-state changes.
The propagation criterion explains why topographically forced planetary Rossby waves can carry energy into a moderately westerly stratosphere, while easterlies and sufficiently strong westerlies block their vertical propagation. Larger horizontal scales more readily meet the criterion. Real dissipation, height-dependent winds and critical levels modify the simple solution and allow wave–mean-flow interaction; the ideal constant-coefficient calculation establishes the propagation filter, not a complete theory of stratospheric evolution.
