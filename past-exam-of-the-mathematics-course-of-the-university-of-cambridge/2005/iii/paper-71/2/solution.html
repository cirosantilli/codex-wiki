Linearizing the ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation about a static magnetic field and using u′=∂t​ξ gives the fluid Lagrangian displacement-generated magnetic field perturbation b=∇×(ξ×B). The two solenoidal vector field conditions reduce the curl identity to
bi​=Bj​∂j​ξi​−ξj​∂j​Bi​.​
(9)
Its divergence vanishes because it is a curl. Directly, the two products of first derivatives in ∂i​bi​ cancel after exchanging their indices, while the remaining terms differentiate ∇⋅ξ or ∇⋅B, both zero. Similarly the integrated linear continuity equation gives ρ′=−∇⋅(ρξ), so ρ′=−ξ⋅∇ρ​.
Let Π=p+B2/(2μ0​) be the magnetohydrodynamic total pressure. Magnetostatic equilibrium and the momentum equation in the linearized ideal magnetohydrodynamic equations are
∂i​Π=−ρ∂i​Φ+μ0−1​Bj​∂j​Bi​,
(10)
−σ2ρξi​=−∂i​π′−ρ′∂i​Φ+μ0−1​(Bj​∂j​bi​+bj​∂j​Bi​),π′=p′+B⋅b/μ0​.
(11)
There is no perturbed gravitational potential here because the field is fixed. Multiply by ξi∗​ and integrate. The pressure term vanishes by the assumed boundary cancellation and ∇⋅ξ∗=0. Thus
σ2I=−∫ξi∗​ξj​ρ,j​Φ,i​dV+μ0−1​M,I=∫ρ∣ξ∣2dV,
(12)
where M=−∫ξi∗​(Bj​∂j​bi​+bj​∂j​Bi​)dV.
Integrate the first magnetic term along B, using ∇⋅B=0, then insert the expression for b. This gives the positive term ∫∣(B⋅∇)ξ∣2dV and two mixed derivative terms. For clarity, the troublesome term transforms as
−∫(Bk​∂k​ξi∗​)ξj​∂j​Bi​=∫ξi∗​Bk​(∂k​ξj​)∂j​Bi​+∫ξi∗​ξj​Bk​∂k​∂j​Bi​.
(13)
The first term on the right cancels the other mixed term. Combining the remaining derivative with the term containing ∂j​Bk​∂k​Bi​ gives
M=∫∣(B⋅∇)ξ∣2dV+∫ξi∗​ξj​∂j​(Bk​∂k​Bi​)dV.
(14)
Differentiating the equilibrium relation now combines the gradient of mass density and magnetostatic equilibrium terms:
−ρ,j​Φ,i​+μ0−1​∂j​(Bk​∂k​Bi​)=Π,ij​+ρΦ,ij​.
(15)
We obtain the incompressible magnetic displacement energy identity
σ2I=∫ξi∗​ξj​Π,ij​dV+∫ρξi∗​ξj​Φ,ij​dV+μ0​1​∫∣(B⋅∇)ξ∣2dV.​
(16)
The two Hessian matrices are real symmetric, making their Hermitian form evaluations real. The last term is a squared norm, and I>0 for a nonzero displacement in fluid with positive mass density. Thus σ2 is real​. It may be negative, in which case one temporal branch has exponential growth; real squared frequency is not itself a proof of stability.
For the atmosphere with a horizontal magnetic field, Φ=gz has zero Hessian matrix and the equilibrium equation is
dzd​(p+2μ0​B2​)=−ρg.​
(17)
Consequently the interchange stability of an incompressible magnetized atmosphere is governed by the numerator
Q[ξ]=−g∫ρz​∣ξz​∣2dV+μ0​1​∫B2∣∂x​ξ∣2dV.
(18)
For ξz​=sinky, the magnetic tension term is zero: these fluid Lagrangian displacements interchange neighboring magnetic field lines without varying along them. A mass density increasing with height gives negative energy and instability; decreasing mass density gives positive energy for these vertical interchanges. If ρz​≤0 everywhere, the displayed form is nonnegative for every admitted incompressible displacement. If the gradient changes sign, suitably localized divergence-free interchange trials detect an unstable inverted layer; For example, take ξz​=f(z)eiky and ξy​=if′(z)eiky/k, with f supported in that layer. Their divergence is zero, and neither component varies along the magnetic field.
For ξz​=sinkx, magnetic field lines are bent. Averaging over a full horizontal wavelength gives the trial Rayleigh quotient
IQ​=∫ρdz−g∫ρz​dz+(k2/μ0​)∫B2dz​,
(19)
under the stipulated admissible boundary/integrability conditions. It is a trial quotient, not a claim that this displacement is an exact eigenfunction for every profile. mass density decreasing upward is stable here too. For inverted mass density, magnetic tension can stabilize sufficiently short waves along the magnetic field, but does not remove the transverse interchange instability. Locally the two contributions have the familiar scale −gρz​/ρ+k2vA2​, with the Alfvén speed vA​=B/μ0​ρ​.
