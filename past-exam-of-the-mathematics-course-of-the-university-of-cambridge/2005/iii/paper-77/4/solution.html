Write G=Δρg>0 and take τ>0 in the positive x direction. Removing the ambient hydrostatic pressure leaves excess pressure G(h−z), so the horizontal pressure gradient in the dense gravity current is G∇h. The lubrication approximation gives
μuzz​=Ghx​,μvzz​=Ghy​.
(45)
The no-slip boundary condition at z=0 and the imposed upper shear stress require u(0)=v(0)=0, μuz​(h)=τ and μvz​(h)=0. Integration yields
u=μτ​z+μG​hx​(2z2​−hz),v=μG​hy​(2z2​−hz).
(46)
Consequently the depth-integrated volume flux is
q=2μτh2​ex​−3μGh3​∇h.
(47)
Using conservation of mass, ht​+∇⋅q=0, proves the evolution equation for a shear-driven viscous gravity current:
ht​+2μτ​(h2)x​=3μG​∇⋅(h3∇h).​
(48)
The gravity term smooths thickness gradients, while the imposed shear transports the current downstream.
The approximations need more than a small ordinary Reynolds number. With thickness H, horizontal scale L, dense-fluid density ρc​=ρ+Δρ and typical horizontal speed V, sufficient small parameters are
LH​≪1,μLρc​VH2​≪1,GHLμV​≪1.​
(49)
The first neglects horizontal viscous derivatives relative to vertical ones. The second compares convective inertia with vertical viscous stress: it is the reduced Reynolds number, ReH​(H/L), rather than a requirement that ReH​ itself be small. The third keeps viscous normal stress small relative to the excess hydrostatic pressure GH. Here V is of order τH/μ+GH3/(μL), so this last condition requires τ/(GL)≪1 together with (H/L)2≪1. Explicit sufficient inertia conditions are ρc​τH3/(μ2L)≪1 and ρc​GH5/(μ2L2)≪1. If an independently imposed unsteady time scale is T, also require ρc​H2/(μT)≪1; for T∼L/V this is the same reduced-inertia condition.
Set A=τ/(2μ) and D=G/(3μ). Far downstream, the transverse scale Y=yN​ is much smaller than x, so the downstream gravity-flux divergence is smaller than the transverse one by order (Y/x)2. For the steady flow, away from the source,
A(h2)x​=D(h3hy​)y​,Q=A∫−YY​h2dy.
(50)
The latter expresses conservation of the prescribed point-source volume flux. In scaling form, Q∼AH2Y and AH2/x∼DH4/Y2. Eliminating H gives Y3∼DQx/A2, proving yN​∝x1/3 and H∝x−1/6.
For the exact downstream similarity solution, put w=h2. Since h3hy​=(w2)y​/4, the reduced equation is the porous medium equation
wx​=4AD​(w2)yy​.
(51)
Seek w=x−1/3F(η) with η=y/x1/3. Substitution gives
−3A​(F+ηF′)=4D​(F2)′′.
(52)
Integrate once, using symmetry and zero transverse flux at η=0, to obtain −AηF/3=DFF′/2. Where F>0, this gives F′=−2Aη/(3D), hence F=A(ηN2​−η2)/(3D). Vanishing thickness fixes its support. The volume-flux condition then reads
Q=3DxA2​∫−YY​(Y2−y2)dy=9Dx4A2Y3​.
(53)
Thus the downstream profile and half-width are
yN​(x)h(x,y)​=(τ23μΔρgQ​x)1/3,=[2Δρgxτ​(yN​(x)2−y2)]1/2(∣y∣<yN​),​​
(54)
with h=0 outside that interval. This is the downstream similarity of a shear-driven gravity current. The square-root edge is the formal outer thin-layer solution; arbitrarily close to that edge its slope is not small, so the local front requires a separate description. This does not change the downstream scaling and flux calculation.
Near the source the two horizontal scales are comparable, say ℓ, so the far-downstream reduction cannot locate the upstream nose. In the upstream direction the downstream shear flux AH2 must balance the upstream gravity flux of order DH4/ℓ. Hence ℓ∼(D/A)H2. The total discharge through a region of width ℓ is Q∼AH2ℓ, giving H4∼Q/D. Combining these estimates gives the upstream reach scale
ℓup​ is of order τμΔρgQ​​.​
(55)
The shear advects liquid toward positive x, while the hydrostatic thickness gradient drives liquid back toward negative x; their balance stops further upstream spreading. A numerical prefactor requires the full two-dimensional near-source free-boundary solution and cannot be fixed by these scaling arguments alone.
