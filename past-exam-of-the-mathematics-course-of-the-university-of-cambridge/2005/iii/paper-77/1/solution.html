For a Newtonian fluid, write σ=−pI+2μe, where e=(∇u+∇uT)/2 is the rate-of-strain tensor. The Stokes equation without body force gives ∂j​σij​=0. Since the stress tensor is symmetric, its contraction with the antisymmetric part of the velocity gradient is zero. Thus
∂j​(ui​σij​)=σij​∂j​ui​=σ:e.
(1)
The divergence theorem proves the boundary-work identity
D=∫V​σ:edV=∫∂V​u⋅σndS.​
(2)
For an incompressible flow, tre=0, so D=2μ∫V​e:edV≥0 is precisely the viscous dissipation.
Apply this identity to the particle-free volume and then to the fluid outside the added particle. On the rigid particle, the velocity has the form V+Ω×x. Its contribution to the boundary work is V⋅F+Ω⋅T, which vanishes because the particle is force-free and torque-free. The outer velocity is unchanged. Subtracting the two boundary-work identities therefore gives
D′=∫∂V0​​U0​⋅σ′ndS.​
(3)
To move this expression to the particle surface, apply the Lorentz reciprocal theorem to (u0​,σ0​) and (u′,σ′) in the punctured fluid domain. For completeness, its local proof is
∇⋅(u0​⋅σ′−u′⋅σ0​)=2μe0​:e′−2μe′:e0​=0.
(4)
On the outer boundary u′=0. Let N=−n be the particle-outward normal, opposite to the fluid-outward normal there. Integrating the preceding divergence identity gives
D′=∫A​(u0​⋅σ′−u′⋅σ0​)NdS.​
(5)
This expression includes the removal of the original fluid inside the particle; that removal must not be counted separately again.
For a sphere in a uniform straining Stokes flow, E is symmetric and trace free, and u0​=Ex. Symmetry gives zero rigid translation and rotation. Put H=x⋅Ex and r=∣x∣. Use the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation printed on the paper, with the decaying harmonic potentials
Φ′=Cr3a3Ex​,χ′=Br5a5H​.
(6)
Their harmonicity follows from xi​/r3=−∂i​(1/r) and from H/r5 being a trace-free second derivative of 1/r. Direct differentiation gives
u′=−3Cr5a3Hx​+Ba5(r52Ex​−r75Hx​).
(7)
At r=a, the no-slip boundary condition requires u′=−Ex. Matching the independent Ex and Hx terms fixes B=−1/2 and C=5/6. The disturbance velocity and pressure are consequently
u′=−r5a5​Ex−2r55a3​(1−r2a2​)Hx,p′=−r55μa3H​.​
(8)
The pressure follows from p′=2μ∇⋅Φ′. The disturbance decays, the total velocity vanishes at the sphere, and the harmonic representation verifies the Stokes equation. The boundary traction has zero total force by odd symmetry, and zero total torque because E is symmetric, confirming the assumed force-free and torque-free motion.
At the sphere u′=−u0​, so the extra dissipation due to a rigid inclusion becomes ∫A​u0​⋅(σ0​+σ′)NdS. Contracting the supplied total stress tensor with N cancels its terms proportional to (N⋅EN)N, leaving 5μEN. Thus
D′=5μa∫A​∣EN∣2dS.
(9)
Using ∫A​Ni​Nj​dS=4πa2δij​/3 proves the single-sphere dissipation increase
D′=320π​μa3E:E.​
(10)
One may perform the comparison in a large bounded vessel with prescribed straining velocity and then take its size to infinity; the given near-sphere approximation makes this limit compatible with the fixed-outer-velocity definition of D′.
The sphere number density is ns​=ϕ/(4πa3/3)=3ϕ/(4πa3). Neglecting hydrodynamic interactions, the additional viscous dissipation per unit volume is ns​D′=5μϕE:E. Adding the particle-free value 2μE:E gives
2μeff​E:E=2μE:E+5μϕE:E,μeff​=μ(1+25​ϕ).​
(11)
This is the Einstein viscosity formula for a dilute suspension, to first order in the particle volume fraction.
