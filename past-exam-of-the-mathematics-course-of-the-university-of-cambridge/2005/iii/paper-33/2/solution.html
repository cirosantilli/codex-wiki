For discrete random variables, write p(u,v) for the joint probability mass function and pU​(u) for its marginal. The conditional entropy is the average of the entropies of the conditional distributions:
h(V∣U)=−∑u:pU​(u)>0​∑v​p(u,v)logp(v∣u),p(v∣u)=pU​(u)p(u,v)​.
(7)
Conditional distributions at zero-probability values of u can be chosen arbitrarily; they contribute nothing. Here h denotes Shannon entropy, with one common logarithm base and 0log0=0.
For p(u,v)>0, use logp(u,v)=logpU​(u)+logp(v∣u) and sum with weight −p(u,v). Summing out v in the first term gives
h(U,V)=h(U)+h(V∣U)​.
(8)
For countable alphabets, the entropy contributions are nonnegative, so the equality remains valid with extended values by summing nonnegative terms. Applying this identity successively to the prefix (X1​,…,Xi−1​) and Xi​ proves the chain rule for information entropy:
h(X1​,…,Xn​)=i=1∑n​h(Xi​∣X1​,…,Xi−1​)​.
(9)
For i=1, the conditioning tuple is empty and the term is h(X1​).
For the subset inequalities, first suppose the marginal entropies are finite, so all joint entropies below are finite by the chain rule and the assumed conditioning reduces entropy property. Put H=h(X1​,…,Xn​), and let H−i​ be the joint entropy with coordinate i omitted. Apply the two-variable chain rule in the order (X[n]∖{i}​,Xi​) to get
H−H−i​=h(Xi​∣X[n]∖{i}​)≤h(Xi​∣X1​,…,Xi−1​).
(10)
The inequality holds because the prefix conditioning set is contained in the set of all other coordinates. Summing over i and using the chain rule gives
nH−∑i=1n​H−i​≤H,so∑i=1n​H−i​≥(n−1)H.
(11)
This proves Han's entropy inequality. In terms of the normalized subset entropy, hn(n)​=H/n and hn−1(n)​=∑i​H−i​/[n(n−1)], hence hn(n)​≤hn−1(n)​​ for n≥2.
Apply the same result to each k-element coordinate set S. It gives
(k−1)h(XS​)≤∑i∈S​h(XS∖{i}​).
(12)
When this is summed over all k-subsets, each fixed (k−1)-subset T occurs once for each possible added coordinate, namely n−k+1 times. Therefore
(k−1)∑∣S∣=k​h(XS​)≤(n−k+1)∑∣T∣=k−1​h(XT​).
(13)
Substitute ∑∣S∣=k​h(XS​)=k(kn​)hk(n)​ and the analogous identity at k−1. The binomial coefficient identity k(kn​)=(n−k+1)(k−1n​) cancels the common positive factor and gives
hk(n)​≤hk−1(n)​(2≤k≤n)​.
(14)
This is the monotonicity of normalized subset entropies, obtained from the local k-coordinate inequality by explicit counting.
If some marginal entropy is infinite, each subset average contains at least one subset including that coordinate. The entropy of such a subset is at least that marginal entropy, by the nonnegative conditional-entropy chain rule, so every hk(n)​ is infinite. The inequalities then hold in the extended sense. Otherwise the finite-entropy proof applies. For independent random variables the averages are all n−1∑i​h(Xi​); repeated copies of one variable instead give hk(n)​=h(X1​)/k, illustrating the entropy reduction caused by redundancy.
