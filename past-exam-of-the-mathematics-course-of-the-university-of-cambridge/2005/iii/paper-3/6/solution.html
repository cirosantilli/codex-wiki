The kernel intersection theorem for Specht modules uses maps that move entries between adjacent rows. For 1≤i<ℓ(μ) and 0≤v<μi+1​, let μ(i,v) replace the row lengths (μi​,μi+1​) by (μi​+μi+1​−v,v), keeping every other length unchanged. This is allowed to be a composition rather than a decreasing partition of an integer, and a zero row can be omitted. For a tabloid with row sets Rj​, define
ψi,v​:Mμ⟶Mμ(i,v)
(27)
by summing over all v-subsets B⊂Ri+1​: the new row i+1 is B, the new row i is Ri​∪(Ri+1​∖B), and all other rows are unchanged. These are module homomorphisms, since relabelling commutes with choosing subsets. The theorem is
Sμ=i=1⋂ℓ(μ)−1​v=0⋂μi+1​−1​kerψi,v​.​
(28)
It holds over every field. Column cancellation shows the inclusion of Sμ in each kernel; the full theorem identifies the common kernel with the Specht module.
For completeness, the semistandard homomorphism theorem concerns a partition of an integer λ, a composition α, and fillings T of shape λ with αj​ occurrences of j. Such a filling gives ΘT​:Mλ→Mα: replace its labels by the numbered entries of an input tableau, and sum the output tabloids over distinct permutations of the labels within each input row. Label j assigns an entry to output row j. The use of distinct assignments avoids dividing by any factorial. Let ΘT​ be its restriction to Sλ. The restrictions indexed by Semistandard Young tableaux are linearly independent. They form a basis of HomFSn​​(Sλ,Mα) when p=2, or when λ is two-regular. In characteristic two for a two-singular shape they need not span; omitting this exception would make the statement incorrect.
We now prove the invariant vector criterion for a Specht module directly from the first theorem. The group acts transitively on the tabloid basis of Mμ, so any invariant vector has all coefficients equal. Thus (Mμ)Sn​=Fzμ​, where zμ​ is the sum of all its tabloids and is nonzero over every field. Fix i,v, write a=μi​, b=μi+1​, and count preimages of one output tabloid. The v retained entries are prescribed; among the a+b−v entries of the enlarged row, exactly a must have come from its old row. Hence
ψi,v​(zμ​)=(aa+b−v​)zμ(i,v)​.
(29)
The common-kernel criterion becomes
zμ​∈Sμ⟺(ja+j​)≡0(modp)(1≤j≤b)
(30)
for every adjacent pair, where j=b−v.
Suppose p>0, and let t be least with b<pt. If a≡−1(modpt), its lowest t base-p digits are all p−1. For any 0<j<pt, adding j to a creates a carry at the lowest nonzero digit of j. At that position the digit of a+j is smaller than the corresponding digit of j. Lucas's theorem therefore gives (ja+j​)≡0. Conversely, if one of the first t digits ad​ is not p−1, take j=pd. Minimality of t gives pd≤b, while addition at that digit creates no carry. Lucas then yields
(pda+pd​)≡ad​+1≡0(modp).
(31)
This proves the claimed equivalence, and therefore
(Sμ)Sn​=0⟺μi​≡−1(modpti​) for every i,ti​=min{t≥0:μi+1​<pt}.​
(32)
The invariant space, when present, is one-dimensional. At the last row put μi+1​=0; then ti​=0 and the condition is vacuous. This congruence formulation is for positive characteristic. In characteristic zero the positive binomial coefficients cannot vanish, so an invariant vector exists precisely for the one-row shape (n).
Finally suppose μ is regular. It has a unique simple head Dμ, by Question 1. The given sign-twisted duality of Specht modules implies
HomSn​​(Sμ,sgn)≅HomSn​​((Sμ′)∗,F)≅HomSn​​(F,Sμ′).
(33)
Thus Dμ is sign exactly when the conjugate shape μ′ satisfies the invariant congruences.
Here is an explicit solution of those congruences under regularity. Write the positive column heights of μ as a1​≥⋯≥al​>0, so μ′=(a1​,…,al​). Regularity says ai​−ai+1​<p, including al​−al+1​=al​<p, because this difference counts rows of length i. Starting with 0<al​<p, the congruence for its preceding row forces al−1​≡−1(modp). Since al​≤al−1​<al​+p≤2p−1, the only possibility is al−1​=p−1. Repeating upwards forces every earlier height to be p−1. Consequently
μ′=((p−1)q,r),n=q(p−1)+r,0≤r<p−1,
(34)
where a zero final part is omitted. Conversely this shape satisfies every invariant congruence, so its conjugate is the desired regular label of the modular sign representation:
μ=((q+1)r,qp−1−r),n=q(p−1)+r,quad0≤r<p−1.​
(35)
Zero parts are omitted. For p=2 this is (n), as it should be because sign is trivial. In characteristic zero the answer is (1n).
