Use [λ] for the ordinary irreducible character of S∣λ∣​. Multiplication of different degrees is the product in the induction ring of symmetric-group characters: it induces an external tensor product of group representations. For α⊆ν, the skew Young diagram ν/α is the set of cells of ν outside α. A filling of it is a semistandard Young tableau when entries weakly increase along rows and strictly increase down columns. Its content β means that i occurs βi​ times. Its reading word takes each row right to left and the rows top to bottom. A lattice word has, in every initial segment, at least as many i's as (i+1)'s for every i.
The Littlewood–Richardson rule says
[α][β]=∑ν⊢∣α∣+∣β∣​cαβν​[ν],
(18)
where the Littlewood–Richardson coefficient cαβν​ counts such skew semistandard fillings with content β and lattice reading word. If α is not contained in ν, the coefficient is zero. Over characteristic zero this is an actual direct-sum multiplicity statement; over other fields the analogous statement describes a Specht filtration, not necessarily a decomposition into simple modules.
For the required coefficient, the five skew cells are
(1,4),(1,5),(2,3),(3,2),(4,1).
(19)
Their columns are all distinct. Content (3,2) uses three 1's and two 2's. The first reading letter must be 1 by the lattice word condition, and row monotonicity forces both top cells to be 1. The remaining three cells, in reading order, can then be filled by 122, 212, or 221. The resulting words 11122, 11212, 11221 all satisfy the lattice inequalities, and exhaust the possibilities. Thus
c(3,2,1),(3,2)(5,3,2,1)​=3.​
(20)
For the determinantal form of a symmetric-group character, set [0]=1 in degree zero and [r]=0 for r<0. The one-row character [r] is trivial. The one-row specialization of the Littlewood–Richardson rule is the Pieri rule: multiplication by [r] adds a horizontal strip of r cells, since all entries are 1 and no column can contain two of them.
Here is an explicit determinant argument, so that the desired form is genuinely deduced. Under the Frobenius characteristic map, [r] corresponds to the complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial hr​, whose monomials enumerate weakly increasing sequences of r entries. Fix N variables and l rows. Let a directed lattice path run right or up from Ai​=(−i,1) to Bj​=(λj​−j,N), and assign weight xk​ to a horizontal step at height k and weight one to a vertical step. The sum of its weights is hλj​−j+i​. Expanding the determinant of this path matrix sums signed families connecting the sources to a permutation of the destinations.
Cancel intersecting families as follows. Choose the first intersection in a fixed order of vertices, and then the first pair of paths meeting there. Exchange their tails after that vertex. The total weight is unchanged, while the endpoint permutation changes by a transposition, reversing the sign. Choosing the intersection in this fixed order makes the operation an involution. The surviving families are nonintersecting; the ordered sources and ordered destinations force the identity endpoint pairing. Record the heights of the horizontal steps of path i as row i of a tableau. Rows weakly increase, and nonintersection of successive paths is precisely strict increase down the shared columns. Conversely every semistandard Young tableau gives one surviving path family. Thus the determinant has the semistandard-tableau generating function sλ​.
Young's rule identifies this generating function with the characteristic of [λ]: its coefficient at a monomial of weight μ is Kλμ​, the multiplicity obtained by pairing with the Young permutation module of type μ. The Frobenius characteristic map is injective and respects induction products. Transposing the path matrix does not change its determinant, so
[λ]=det([λi​+j−i])1≤i,j≤l​.​
(21)
This is the character version of the Jacobi–Trudi identity.
For the example the matrix is
[3,2,2]=det​[3][1][0]​[4][2][1]​[5][3][2]​​.
(22)
Expanding gives the concise answer
[3,2,2]=[3][2]2−[3]2[1]−[4][1][2]+[4][3]+[5][1]2−[5][2].​
(23)
At the identity the six induction-product dimensions are 210,140,105,35,42,21, respectively; their alternating sum is 21, agreeing with the hook-length formula for (3,2,2).
