Use natural units and the unreduced Planck mass MP​=G−1/2; it is 8π​ times the reduced Planck mass. A homogeneous canonical inflaton has ρϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2+V and Pϕ​=ϕ˙​2/2−V. Vacuum domination by a scalar field requires ϕ˙​i2​/2≪V(ϕi​), with other matter and the curvature term negligible in the Friedmann equation. The equation-of-state parameter is then near −1, and the Friedmann acceleration equation gives accelerating expansion.

ϵV​=16πMP2​​(VV′​)2≪1,∣ηV​∣=​8πMP2​​VV′′​​≪1.
(14)
For the quartic scalar potential,
ϵV​=πϕ2MP2​​,ηV​=2πϕ23MP2​​.
(15)
Thus ∣ϕi​∣≫MP​ supplies a broad slow-roll range when λ≪1. At an initial density of order the Planck density, potential domination gives
ϕi2​∼λ​2MP2​​,Vi​∼MP4​,​
(16)
so both potential slow-roll parameters are small for small λ. Small initial kinetic energy is a separate condition; the height of the scalar potential alone does not enforce it. These classical equations describe the post-Planck evolution under the model assumptions, rather than resolving quantum gravity at the initial endpoint.
Take ϕi​>0; the negative branch follows by the symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ. Dropping kinetic energy from the Friedmann equation and acceleration from the inflaton equation of motion gives

ϕ(t)≃ϕi​e−b(t−ti​),a(t)≃ai​exp[MP2​πϕi2​​(1−e−2b(t−ti​))].​

The exact end of accelerating expansion is ϵH​=−H˙/H2=1, equivalently ϕ˙​2=V for the scalar-dominated flat model. The quartic-inflation endpoint estimate uses the leading potential condition ϵV​≃1, giving
∣ϕe​∣∼MP​,∣ϕe​∣≃π​MP​​ using ϵV​=1.​
(19)
The numerical coefficient is an endpoint estimate: the slow-roll approximation breaks down there. Imposing ∣ηV​∣=1 estimates when slow roll loses accuracy and gives a different order-one coefficient, not a different exact acceleration condition.
The slow-roll e-fold count is
N≃MP2​8π​∫ϕe​ϕi​​V′V​dϕ=MP2​π​(ϕi2​−ϕe2​).

N∼λ​2π​−1,ai​ae​​∼exp(λ​2π​−1).​
(21)
For small λ the dominant term is enormous; the order-one endpoint uncertainty does not affect that conclusion.
To estimate the reheating temperature, assume prompt, efficient reheating converts the end-of-inflation energy into a thermal radiation bath. With the same endpoint convention, Ve​≃λMP4​/(4π2), and equating this to the radiation energy density gives
30π2​g∗​TR4​∼4π2λMP4​​,TR​∼(2π4g∗​15λ​)1/4MP​∼λ1/4g∗−1/4​MP​.​
(22)
The quartic-inflation prompt-reheating estimate fixes the scaling; the endpoint kinetic energy changes the coefficient by order one. Without an inflaton coupling and its thermalization history, V alone cannot specify the actual reheating temperature.
Finally, during cosmic inflation the comoving Hubble radius decreases:
dtd​aH1​=−a1−ϵH​​<0.
(23)
An initially causally connected patch is enlarged enough that the present observable region can lie within it. Its portions may subsequently be far outside one another's Hubble radii while retaining their common earlier thermal history. This solves the horizon problem if the number of e-folds is sufficiently large, conventionally of order sixty for the relevant thermal history; N∼2π/λ​ can readily exceed that. The necessary count depends on reheating and later expansion. The causal mechanism and the conversion back to a hot universe are discussed in David Tong's account of inflation.
