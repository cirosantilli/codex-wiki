The nonexistence conclusion concerns nonconstant fields: a constant scalar-field vacuum with U=0 is a zero-energy critical point of an energy functional in every dimension. Assume the potential is smooth, so the classical Euler-Lagrange equation is well defined. Write the two nonnegative energy terms as
T=21​∫∣∇ϕ∣2dDx,V=∫U(ϕ)dDx.
(1)
For the admissible Derrick scaling ϕλ​(x)=ϕ(λx),
E(ϕλ​)=λ2−DT+λ−DV.
(2)
Stationarity at λ=1 gives the Derrick virial identity
(2−D)T−DV=0.​
(3)
For D>2, both terms have nonpositive sign, forcing T=V=0 and hence a constant vacuum. For D=2, scaling alone only proves V=0; the gradient term is scale invariant, so it must not simply be declared zero.
The two-dimensional flat-target Derrick obstruction supplies that remaining step. Since U≥0 and its integral is zero, continuity gives U(ϕ(x))=0 everywhere; smooth nonnegative U has U′(ϕ(x))=0 at those values. The field equation Δϕ=U′(ϕ) therefore becomes Δϕ=0. Each ∂i​ϕ is an entire harmonic function in L2(R2). Its mean value property and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality give, for every radius R,
∣∂i​ϕ(x0​)∣≤πR2​∥∂i​ϕ∥L2(R2)​​.
(4)
Let R→∞ to get ∂i​ϕ=0. Thus D=2 also has only constant stationary field configurations of finite energy. The unconstrained real scalar target is essential in this argument; the spherical target in Question 2 has different field equations.
In D=1 the field equation is ϕ′′=U′(ϕ). Multiplication by ϕ′ gives the first integral
21​(ϕ′)2−U(ϕ)=K.
(5)
Finite energy makes 21​(ϕ′)2+U(ϕ) an integrable nonnegative function, so along some sequence xn​→+∞ it tends to zero. Evaluating the constant first integral on that sequence gives K=0. Choose
W(u)=∫u∗​u​2U(s)​ds,U(u)=21​(W′(u))2.​
(6)
Consequently (ϕ′)2=2U(ϕ) and
dxdϕ​=±dϕdW​.​
(7)
For a nonconstant entire solution the sign is fixed. Indeed a finite-x zero of ϕ′ would have U(ϕ)=U′(ϕ)=0; uniqueness of the smooth second-order equation with initial data (ϕ,ϕ′)=(v,0) would make the solution the constant vacuum. Thus ϕ′ never vanishes in a nonconstant solution. Conversely a smooth solution of the displayed first-order equation satisfies ϕ′′=U′(ϕ) wherever it is nonconstant.
Under the usual soliton boundary conditions ϕ(x)→v±​ as x→±∞, with v±​ in the vacuum manifold {U=0}, the two endpoint components label the topological sector. For isolated scalar-field vacua this is an ordered pair of vacuum labels, not necessarily one universally normalized integer. More explicitly, for any smooth F, the topological current
jFμ​=ϵμν∂ν​F(ϕ),∂μ​jFμ​=0,QF​=∫jF0​dx=F(v+​)−F(v−​)
(8)
is identically conserved, taking ϵ01=1 and boundary conditions fixed during evolution. The choices F(u)=u and F(u)=W(u) give the field-difference charge and the kink energy charge. The square completion for a one-dimensional kink gives
E=21​∫(ϕ′∓W′(ϕ))2dx±[W(v+​)−W(v−​)],E≥∣W(v+​)−W(v−​)∣.​
(9)
The first-order critical solution saturates this Bogomolny bound. A nonzero difference between endpoint vacuum components prevents continuous deformation to a vacuum while those boundary conditions remain fixed. Finite energy alone need not give finite endpoint values for arbitrary potentials, so these topological labels use the stated vacuum boundary conditions.
To evade the scalar-only obstruction, introduce a gauge field and a charged, generally multicomponent Higgs field. For a Yang-Mills-Higgs energy write E=TH​+V+EB​, where TH​ contains ∣Di​Φ∣2 and EB​ contains ∣Fij​∣2. Rescale Φλ​(x)=Φ(λx) and Ai,λ​(x)=λAi​(λx). Then Di​Φ scales by λ, while Fij​ scales by λ2, so
Eλ​=λ2−DTH​+λ−DV+λ4−DEB​,(2−D)TH​−DV+(4−D)EB​=0.​
(10)
The magnetic term supplies the missing opposing scaling power. In D=2 the relation is EB​=V, permitting Abelian Higgs vortices; in D=3 it is EB​=TH​+3V, permitting 't Hooft-Polyakov monopoles. In the monopole Bogomolny limit V=0, Bi​=±Di​Φ achieves EB​=TH​. Also, asymptotic ordinary derivatives need not vanish when a Higgs phase winds: the relevant vanishing quantity is the gauge covariant derivative. This allows nontrivial boundary winding with finite energy. Merely appending a gauge potential to a neutral one-component real field would not by itself furnish that Higgs mechanism.

Enforce the unit-vector constraint using a Lagrange multiplier Λ(x):
E=21​∫[∂i​ϕa∂i​ϕa+Λ(ϕaϕa−1)]d2x.
(11)
Integration by parts in a compactly supported variation gives −Δϕa+Λϕa=0. Contracting with ϕa gives Λ=ϕbΔϕb, hence
Δϕa−(ϕbΔϕb)ϕa=0.​
(12)
Differentiating ϕaϕa=1 twice also gives ϕ⋅Δϕ=−∣∇ϕ∣2, so an equivalent equation is Δϕ+∣∇ϕ∣2ϕ=0. These are the harmonic map equations for the O3 nonlinear sigma model.
For arbitrary maps, the printed finite-energy compactification assertion is too strong. The following counterexample shows that finite sigma-model energy need not give a limit at infinity. Outside a large disk, set
ϕ(r,ϑ)=(sin(loglogr),0,cos(loglogr)).
(13)
Interpolate smoothly to a constant inside the disk. There is no angular dependence, and the exterior energy is
21​∫r≥R​∣∇ϕ∣2d2x=π∫R∞​rlog2rdr​=logRπ​<∞.
(14)
Nevertheless the field keeps rotating and has no limit as r→∞. Thus energy integrability alone does not prove a continuous map on the one-point compactification.
The usual sigma-model lump sector adds the boundary condition ϕ(x)→ϕ∞​∈S2 uniformly as ∣x∣→∞. Defining the value at the added point to be ϕ∞​ then gives a continuous map S2→S2: neighborhoods of the point at infinity are precisely complements of compact subsets of the plane, and uniform convergence supplies continuity there. For the topological degree calculation using differential forms below, take a smooth compactified map, as in the regular lump sector. This boundary/regularity information is additional to finite energy for unrestricted fields.
Write ai​=∂i​ϕ and bi​=ϵij​ϕ×∂j​ϕ. Since ϕ⋅∂i​ϕ=0 and ∣ϕ∣=1,
∑i​∣bi​∣2=∑i​∣ai​∣2,∑i​ai​⋅bi​=−2ϕ⋅(∂1​ϕ×∂2​ϕ).
(15)
Also the given double-index charge reduces to
Q=4π1​∫ϕ⋅(∂1​ϕ×∂2​ϕ)d2x.
(16)
For s=±1, expanding the nonnegative square gives
∫∑i​∣ai​+sbi​∣2d2x=4E−16πsQ.
(17)
Therefore the Bogomolny degree bound for the O3 sigma model is
E≥4π∣Q∣.​
(18)
The charge integral is absolutely convergent already for finite-energy fields, because ∣ϕ⋅(∂1​ϕ×∂2​ϕ)∣≤21​(∣∂1​ϕ∣2+∣∂2​ϕ∣2). The inequality itself consequently does not need the compactification hypothesis. Equality requires ∂i​ϕ+sϵij​ϕ×∂j​ϕ=0 for s chosen to have the sign of Q.
For the topological interpretation, the standard oriented area form on the target sphere is
ω=21​ϵabc​yadyb∧dyc,∫S2​ω=4π.
(19)
Its pullback is ϕ∗ω=ϕ⋅(∂1​ϕ×∂2​ϕ)dx1∧dx2. The topological degree is defined by ϕ∗​[S2]=deg(ϕ)[S2] in H2​(S2;Z)≅Z. Pairing with ω gives
∫S2​ϕ∗ω=deg(ϕ)∫S2​ω,Q=deg(ϕ)∈Z.​
(20)
Equivalently, the integer is the signed number of preimages of a regular value. It is invariant under smooth homotopy: if Φ:S2×[0,1]→S2 is that homotopy, then Stokes theorem and dω=0 make the difference between the two charge integrals equal to ∫S2×[0,1]​d(Φ∗ω)=0. This identifies the charge as the degree charge of an O3 sigma-model lump, rather than merely an arbitrary energy integral.

Use Euclidean coordinates (x1,x2,x3,x4) and orientation dx1∧dx2∧dx3∧dx4. For Dμ​=∂μ​+Aμ​, the gauge curvature is Fμν​=[Dμ​,Dν​]. The ASDYM equations are F=−∗F, equivalently
F12​+F34​=0,F13​−F24​=0,F14​+F23​=0.
(21)
Put w=x1+ix2, z=x3+ix4, with ∂w​=(∂1​−i∂2​)/2 and similarly for z. The bars label the complex conjugate coordinates on the Euclidean real slice; after complexification they can be treated as independent coordinates. A Lax pair for the anti-self-dual Yang-Mills equations is
(Dw​−λDzˉ​)Ψ=0,(Dz​+λDwˉ​)Ψ=0,λ∈CP1.​
(22)
At infinity rescale the operators and use the other affine coordinate. Their commutator is
[Dw​−λDzˉ​,Dz​+λDwˉ​]=Fwz​+λ(Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​)+λ2Fwˉzˉ​.
(23)
Compatibility for every λ is therefore precisely Fwz​=Fwˉzˉ​=0 and Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=0. To verify the real component signs,
Fwz​=41​{F13​−F24​−i(F14​+F23​)},Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=2i​(F12​+F34​).
(24)
The conjugate equation supplies the remaining real components. This is compatibility of an overdetermined system, not an additional field equation on Ψ for only one value of the spectral parameter.
Impose invariance under translations in x1,x2,x3, writing t=x4. In a symmetry-adapted gauge the potentials depend only on t. A further t-dependent Yang-Mills gauge transformation sets A4​=0 locally, by solving the usual parallel transport ordinary differential equation; it does not reintroduce spatial dependence. Then
Fab​=[Aa​,Ab​],Fa4​=−A˙a​.
(25)
The three real equations above become A˙1​=[A2​,A3​], A˙2​=[A3​,A1​] and A˙3​=[A1​,A2​], namely
A˙a​=21​ϵabc​[Ab​,Ac​].​
(26)
These are the Nahm equations. The orientation chosen above gives the requested plus sign; reversing orientation exchanges self-duality of gauge curvature and anti-self-duality of gauge curvature. Translation invariance here is a local dimensional reduction and is not a claim of finite four-dimensional action over all three translation directions.
To prove conservation, abbreviate P=A1​+iA2​, Q=A1​−iA2​ and R=A3​, so A(λ)=P+2Rλ−Qλ2 and B(λ)=−iR+iQλ. Expanding the commutator, including the cancellation of the cubic term, gives
[A(λ),B(λ)]=−i[P,R]+i[P,Q]λ+i[R,Q]λ2.
(27)
The Nahm equations give
P˙=−i[P,R],2R˙=i[P,Q],−Q˙​=i[R,Q].
(28)
Thus the polynomial Lax representation of the Nahm equations is
A˙(λ)=[A(λ),B(λ)].​
(29)
Take a finite-dimensional matrix representation of the Lie algebra and its complexification. For every positive integer p, differentiating the product and using cyclicity of the matrix trace gives
dtd​Tr(A(λ)p)=pTr(A(λ)p−1[A(λ),B(λ)])=p{Tr(ApB)−Tr(BAp)}=0.
(30)
This proves the trace invariants of a Lax equation directly. Since Tr(A(λ)p) is a polynomial of degree at most 2p in the affine coordinate λ, its derivative vanishes identically only if the derivative of every coefficient vanishes. Therefore every trace-polynomial coefficient is independent of t. At projective infinity these polynomials are sections of O(2p); they need not be constant functions of λ on the whole projective sphere.

Represent affine complexified Minkowski space by complex 2×2 matrices xAA′, with the conformal metric characterized by det(dx). Let an element of twistor space be Z=(ωA,πA′​)∈C4, and identify nonzero twistors under Z∼cZ, c=0. The compact projective twistor space is CP3. For affine space-time the corresponding open domain is the affine projective twistor space
PTaff​={[ω,π]:π=0}=CP3∖CP∞1​≅Tot(O(1)⊕O(1)).​
(31)
Use the twistor incidence relation in the convention ωA=xAA′πA′​; the more usual factor i can be absorbed into the complex coordinate x. A point x determines a twistor line
Lx​={[xπ,π]:[π]∈CP1}≅CP1.
(32)
In the two projective charts, fibre coordinates ω/π0′​ and ω/π1′​ differ by division by λ=π1′​/π0′​, giving the two O(1) summands. Two distinct space-time points have intersecting lines precisely when (x−y)π=0 for some nonzero π, equivalently det(x−y)=0; this recovers conformal null separation.
The twistor correspondence is the double fibration
MC​p​F=MC​×CP1  q ​ PTaff​,q(x,[π])=[xπ,π].
(33)
For a fixed twistor, the incidence solutions form an affine alpha-plane, a two-dimensional totally null plane. Its tangent distribution on F is generated by
VA​=πA′∂AA′​,πA′=ϵA′B′πB′​.
(34)
These vector fields annihilate the incidence coordinates, since πA′πA′​=0, and commute. This distribution is the geometric source of the linear system in the Penrose-Ward correspondence.
For the inverse construction, take a rank-r holomorphic vector bundle E on the twistor domain corresponding to a space-time open set U. The crucial hypothesis is
E∣Lx​​≅O⊕rfor every x∈U.​
(35)
These are line-trivial bundles in the Ward correspondence. It is not enough to specify an arbitrary holomorphic vector bundle, nor just zero First Chern class on its lines: O(1)⊕O(−1) has degree zero but is nontrivial. A line with a nontrivial splitting is a jumping line and does not support the regular linewise construction there. Thus a globally nonsingular field on all affine complexified space-time requires triviality on all its relevant lines; a local construction uses only the lines over U.
Choose holomorphic vector bundle bundle frames on two twistor patches, and let P be the invertible transition matrix on their overlap. Pull it back to F. Because it comes from affine projective twistor space, it is constant along the q-fibres:
VA​P(x,π)=0.
(36)
Triviality on Lx​ supplies a global holomorphic bundle frame on that line. Comparing this global bundle frame with the two local bundle frames produces a holomorphic splitting of a twistor patching matrix,
P(x,λ)=Ψ−​(x,λ)−1Ψ+​(x,λ),
(37)
where Ψ+​ and Ψ−​ are holomorphic and invertible near the two affine patches of the complex projective line. The splitting can be chosen holomorphically in x locally: the restrictions have r independent global sections and H1(CP1,O⊕r)=0, so these bundle frames extend through a neighborhood of the given line. This explains where regularity and linewise triviality enter the construction.
Differentiate the factorization using VA​P=0. Multiplication by the invertible factors gives
(VA​Ψ+​)Ψ+−1​=(VA​Ψ−​)Ψ−−1​.
(38)
Define their common negative to be AA​. It is regular on both projective patches and homogeneous of degree one in π, because VA​ has that degree while the bundle frames have degree zero. Consequently
AA​(x,π)=πA′AAA′​(x).
(39)
This last conclusion is elementary: on one affine chart its entries are entire in λ; regularity at infinity after dividing by the transition factor λ allows at most linear growth, so each entry is affine in λ. Equivalently, a global section of O(1) is a linear homogeneous polynomial. We have thereby recovered a gauge connection DAA′​=∂AA′​+AAA′​ on the space-time bundle whose fibre is H0(Lx​,E∣Lx​​).
The splitting factors now obey
πA′DAA′​Ψ±​=0.​
(40)
Their invertibility implies the compatibility conditions
πA′πB′FAA′BB′​=0for every [π].
(41)
In the spinor decomposition of gauge curvature,
FAA′BB′​=ϵAB​ΦA′B′​+ϵA′B′​ΦAB​,
(42)
the two spinors are symmetric. The antisymmetric primed factor vanishes when contracted with πA′πB′, leaving ϵAB​πA′πB′ΦA′B′​=0. A quadratic polynomial that vanishes for every spinor has every coefficient zero, so ΦA′B′​=0. With the orientation convention used here, this is exactly anti-self-duality of gauge curvature, hence the ASDYM equations. This establishes the field equation from the holomorphic data, rather than assuming it as an extra input.
The construction naturally gives a gauge class. If another linewise splitting is chosen, Ψ±′​Ψ±−1​ agrees on the overlap and is holomorphic on the entire compact complex projective line; its entries are therefore independent of λ. Write this common matrix as g(x)−1. Then
AAA′′​=g−1AAA′​g+g−1∂AA′​g,
(43)
the ordinary Yang-Mills gauge transformation. Changing the original holomorphic vector bundle bundle frames or replacing E by an isomorphic bundle produces the same space-time gauge class. A determinant trivialization or another holomorphic structure-group reduction gives the corresponding restriction from GL(r,C) to SL(r,C) or another complex gauge group.
For perspective, the forward direction starts from an ASD gauge connection and uses its flat gauge covariant derivative along each alpha-plane to descend parallel sections to affine projective twistor space. The reconstructed linear system is exactly that parallel-section system. Going back through it recovers the original line-trivial bundle, while changing space-time bundle frames amounts to Yang-Mills gauge transformation. Thus, locally and with the stated regularity and triviality conditions, holomorphic line-trivial bundles determine solutions of the ASDYM equations uniquely up to gauge.
No unitary reality condition is imposed on complexified Minkowski space in this question. Obtaining real Euclidean gauge fields requires the compatible antiholomorphic twistor involution and a bundle reality structure; obtaining finite-action instantons requires further global and boundary conditions. Those are additional restrictions on the complex correspondence, not automatic consequences of having any holomorphic vector bundle on projective twistor space.
