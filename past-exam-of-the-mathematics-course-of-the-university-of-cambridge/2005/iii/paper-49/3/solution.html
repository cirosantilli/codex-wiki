Split the free interaction picture field as ϕ=ϕ(+)+ϕ(−), where the first part contains annihilation operators and the second creation operators. Time ordering places the operator at the later time on the left, while normal ordering places all creation operators on the left. For x0>y0, commuting the annihilation part of the first field through the creation part of the second gives
ϕ(x)ϕ(y)=:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):+[ϕ(+)(x),ϕ(−)(y)].
(14)
The commutator is the c-number
[ϕ(+)(x),ϕ(−)(y)]=∫(2π)3d3p​2Ep​e−ip⋅(x−y)​.
(15)
For y0>x0, the same calculation interchanges x,y. Combining the two time orders proves the two-field Wick contraction identity
Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)=:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):+DF​(x−y)​,
(16)
where
DF​(x−y)=⟨0∣Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+i0ie−ip⋅(x−y)​.

The equality with the four-dimensional integral follows by closing the energy contour at the positive-energy pole for x0>y0 and the negative-energy pole for the reverse order. This fixes the scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription; in this convention DF​ itself includes the factor i.
Because these interactions have no derivatives, Hint​=−∫Lint​d3x. Expand the Dyson series
S=Texp(i∫d4z[3!μ​ϕ(z)3−4!λ​ϕ(z)4]).

Apply the Wick theorem to each term together with the external insertions. Every paired field contributes a propagator, and the remaining normal-ordered vacuum expectation vanishes unless no fields remain. Representing the propagators by Fourier integrals and integrating each vertex position gives the four-momentum conservation delta function. The 3! and 4! contractions into labelled vertex slots cancel the factorials in the interaction density. The resulting scalar-field cubic and quartic Feynman vertices are
propagator: p2−m2+i0i​,cubic vertex: iμ,quartic vertex: −iλ​.
(19)
Each vertex also has (2π)4δ(4)(∑p), each independent internal momentum is integrated with d4p/(2π)4, and repeated equivalent contractions give the usual Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. For the unamputated correlation functions, retain the propagators joining external insertions to vertices. The displayed unnormalized numerator includes disconnected vacuum bubbles; dividing by ⟨0∣S∣0⟩ cancels them. Amputated scattering amplitudes additionally use the LSZ reduction formula.
For stability, examine the classical scalar potential
V(ϕ)=21​m2ϕ2−6μ​ϕ3+24λ​ϕ4.
(20)
The tree-level boundedness criterion is λ>0, with arbitrary real cubic coupling. If λ=0, boundedness requires μ=0 and m2≥0; if λ<0, or λ=0 with μ=0, the potential is unbounded below. Thus a positive quartic coupling permits a stable ground-state vacuum, although it need not be the perturbative vacuum at ϕ=0.
For λ>0 and m2≥0, the stability of a cubic-quartic scalar potential determines when zero is a global minimum:
V(ϕ)=ϕ2[24λ​(ϕ−λ2μ​)2+2m2​−6λμ2​].
(21)
The square bracket is nonnegative for every field value exactly when
μ2≤3λm2​.
(22)
For a nontrivial equality case there is a second degenerate minimum at ϕ=2μ/λ. If m2>0 and μ2>3λm2, zero remains a local minimum but is a false vacuum, with a lower minimum elsewhere. If m2=μ=0 and λ>0, the origin is the quartic minimum despite its vanishing quadratic curvature. These are classical or tree-level stability statements; renormalized couplings and the quantum effective potential refine the quantum analysis.
