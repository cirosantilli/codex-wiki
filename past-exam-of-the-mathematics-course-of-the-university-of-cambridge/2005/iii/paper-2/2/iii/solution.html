The printed phrase “not reducible” is a misprint: the requested nonzero complementary submodules establish that this tensor square is reducible. The natural reason is that the flip τ(v⊗w)=w⊗v commutes with the Lie algebra representation. Its two eigenspaces are the symmetric square and the exterior square, giving
V⊗V=Sym2V⊕Λ2V,dimSym2V=10,dimΛ2V=6.​
(24)
In the symmetric square, the four weights ±2Li​ and four weights ±L1​±L2​ each occur once, while zero occurs twice. In the exterior square, the four weights ±L1​±L2​ each occur once and zero occurs twice; the ±2Li​ are absent because repeated wedge factors vanish. These are the two requested complementary weight diagrams.
More precisely, the tensor-square decomposition of the defining symplectic representation identifies Sym2V with the irreducible adjoint module L(2ω1​). The identification is an intertwiner sending a symmetric tensor vw to the element of the symplectic Lie algebra x↦ω(v,x)w+ω(w,x)v. For the exterior square, symplectic contraction of an exterior square has a five-dimensional kernel, the primitive exterior square, and the invariant tensor Ω=e1​∧e3​+e2​∧e4​ supplies a complementary line. Thus the full irreducible decomposition is
V⊗V=L(2ω1​)⊕L(ω2​)⊕L(0),16=10+5+1.​
(25)
Here ω1​=L1​ and ω2​=L1​+L2​. The two zero weights in the exterior square split into one primitive zero weight and the invariant line.
