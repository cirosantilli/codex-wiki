If the random k-out graph with k=2 is disconnected, one of its connected components of a graph has size 3≤s≤n/2. The lower bound follows because every vertex chooses two distinct neighbours inside its component. For a specified s-set S, absence of crossing edges requires every choice at a vertex in S to stay in S, and every choice outside to stay outside. Independence of choices at different vertices gives exactly
Pr(S has no crossing edge)=[(2n−1​)(2s−1​)​]s[(2n−1​)(2n−s−1​)​]n−s.
(13)
For 3≤s≤n/2, the two ratios are at most (s/n)2 and (1−s/n)2. Indeed each of the factors (s−j)/(n−j), j=1,2, is at most s/n, and the complementary statement is identical. Put t=s/n. The binomial coefficient bound (sn​)≤t−s(1−t)−(n−s) follows by bounding the probability of s successes in Bin(n,t) by one. Thus the union bound gives
Pr(G2-out​ disconnected)≤∑s=3⌊n/2⌋​ts(1−t)n−s≤∑s=3⌊n/2⌋​(s/n)s.
(14)
For s≤n​, the last summands are at most n−s/2, whose sum from three tends to zero. For s>n​, they are at most 2−s, again summing to zero. Therefore
Pr(G2-out​ connected)=1−o(1).​
(15)
The proof bounds all possible small components rather than assuming the dependent undirected edges behave like a binomial random graph.
