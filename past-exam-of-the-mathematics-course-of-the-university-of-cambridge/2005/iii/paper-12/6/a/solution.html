Draw n independent pairs of independent uniform random variables on [0,1]. In each pair call the minimum L and maximum R, then relabel whole pairs so R1​<⋯<Rn​. Merge all endpoints in (Ri−1​,Ri​] into vertex i, with R0​=0, and join the blocks containing the two endpoints of each pair, orienting from the right endpoint to the left. Self-loops are retained. This gives the uniform linearized chord diagram representation.
Before ordering, a pair has joint density 2 on 0<L<R<1. Consequently its maximum has distribution Pr(R≤r)=r2 and density 2r; conditional on R=r, its minimum is uniform on [0,r]. Independence and relabelling give the equivalent precise description

Every one of the (2n)! relative orderings of the independently drawn labelled endpoints has the same probability. After forgetting pair labels and within-pair labels, each matching of 2n ordered points is produced by 2nn! orderings. Thus its matching is uniform among the (2n−1)!! pairings.
To prove equivalence with the recursive graph law, remove the last endpoint and its mate from a uniform pairing. The remaining ordered points have a uniform (n−1)-pairing, and the mate's old rank is uniform among the 2n−1 possible ranks, independently of that smaller pairing. Conversely insert the mate into a uniformly chosen one of its 2n−1 gaps, and pair it with the new final endpoint. For each old vertex block, the number of gaps assigned to it is exactly the number of endpoints already in that block, hence its old total vertex degree. These are the gaps immediately before each endpoint of that block. The last gap, after the last old endpoint, makes both new endpoints belong to the new final block and produces a self-loop. Therefore the attachment probabilities are di​/(2n−1) for old vertices and 1/(2n−1) for the new one. Induction proves the continuous construction and the recursive LCD model have the same distribution. Inserting a left endpoint into an old block raises exactly that vertex's vertex degree without splitting any old block.
