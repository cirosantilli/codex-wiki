Let ρr​ be a constant reference density close to the ambient density, and let ρp​ denote the mean plume density. The Boussinesq approximation permits full-cross-section kinematic fluxes
Q=∫A​wdA,M=∫A​w2dA,F=∫A​bwdA,b=gρr​ρa​(z)−ρp​​.​
(8)
Thus Q is the specific mass flux, equal to volume flux at this order; M is the specific vertical momentum flux; and F is the buoyancy flux. Physical mass and momentum fluxes are ρr​Q and ρr​M. These definitions include the full circular area. If factors of π are removed in a course's flux convention, divide Q,M,F by π throughout rather than mixing normalizations. The PDF's source density is ρ0​, distinct from ambient ρa​(z); the TeX changes the source symbol incorrectly.
For the top-hat plume model, let the plume radius be r(z) and let w,b be uniform inside it. Then
Q=πr2w,M=πr2w2,F=πr2wb,w=QM​,r=πM​Q​.
(9)
The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis supplies the necessary closure: the inward edge velocity is ve​=αe​w, with constant entrainment coefficient αe​>0. Entrainment across a cylindrical slice gives Q′=2πrαe​w=2αe​π​M​. The entrained ambient has negligible upward momentum. The vertical buoyancy force on the slice is πr2b=F/w, hence M′=FQ/M.
For the buoyancy balance, let R=∫A​ρp​wdA be the transported density scalar. Entrainment gives R′=ρa​Q′. Differentiate F=g(ρa​Q−R)/ρr​ to get F′=gρa′​Q/ρr​. Therefore the closed flux equations are
Q′=cM​,M′=MFQ​,F′=−N2Q,c=2αe​π​,N2=−ρr​g​ρa′​.​
(10)
Here N is the buoyancy frequency when the ambient is stably stratified. These are the Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient equations in the explicitly stated full-area convention. Stable stratification consumes positive buoyancy flux as the plume rises. The top-hat assumption alone would not determine Q′ without an entrainment law.
For uniform ambient density, F=F0​ is constant. The source volume Q0​ becomes negligible when accumulated entrainment ∫0z​cM(s)​ds is much greater than Q0​. In a jet-like region, M≃M0​, so the criterion is z≫Q0​/(cM0​​). In a buoyancy-dominated far field with Q≃CQ​z5/3, it is z≫(Q0​/CQ​)3/5, with CQ​ determined below. These are the two source-volume lengths of a turbulent plume. They are not the jet length, which measures the persistence of imposed source momentum. A nonzero source volume can also appear as a plume virtual origin; it is not valid to discard it at every distance simply because the source is geometrically small.
For Q0​=M0​=0, F0​>0, seek Q=CQ​z5/3, M=CM​z4/3. The two flux equations give CQ​=(3c/5)CM​​ and CM3/2​=9cF0​/20. Thus the pure-plume solution is
F=F0​,M=(209cF0​​)2/3z4/3,Q=53c​(209cF0​​)1/3z5/3.​
(11)
For completeness its directly measurable top-hat fields are
r=56αe​​z,w=Kp​z−1/3,b=34​Kp2​z−5/3,Kp3​=48παe2​25F0​​.
(12)
This is an axisymmetric pure plume. In this regime the explicit source-volume criterion is
z≫LQplume​=(243c4F0​2500Q03​​)1/5.
(13)
For Q0​=F0​=0, M0​>0, the buoyancy vanishes and momentum is constant. The pure-jet solution is
F=0,M=M0​,Q=cM0​​z,w=czM0​​​,r=2αe​z.​
(14)
This is the jet limit of the top-hat plume model. Both ideal zero-volume sources have singular speeds at z=0; they model regions outside a finite source where entrainment has already made the source-volume correction small.
When M0​ and F0​ are both positive, use the jet solution near the source and the pure-plume solution far above it, with an appropriate virtual-origin correction. To quantify this transition, eliminate z:
dQdM​=cM3/2F0​Q​,M5/2−4c5F0​​Q2=M05/2​−4c5F0​​Q02​.​
(15)
The conserved quantity is the plume flux-balance invariant. With negligible Q0​, comparison of its two terms and Q≃cM0​​z gives a transition height of order M03/4​/(c​F0​​). The exact connection, including finite Q0​, is the finite-source forced-plume quadrature
z=c1​∫Q0​Q(z)​[M05/2​+4c5F0​​(q2−Q02​)]−1/5dq,​
(16)
with M then recovered from the invariant. A positive integrand gives the physical increasing-volume branch. As Q grows, the source constant is negligible relative to Q2, proving attraction to pure plume similarity. One matches the two asymptotic regimes or uses this quadrature; the jet and plume solutions cannot be linearly superposed because their governing flux equations are nonlinear.
