The Boussinesq approximation assumes that the fractional mass density variations are small. Replace density by a constant reference value in inertia, pressure scaling and mass conservation, but retain its small anomaly in the gravitational force: the large hydrostatic force otherwise cancels, leaving precisely the buoyancy-driving term. For temperature-driven motion, write ρ=ρr​[1−α(T−Tr​)] with ∣α(T−Tr​)∣≪1. The resulting velocity is an incompressible flow; compressional heating and acoustic effects are omitted in the shallow, low-compressibility regime.
For this convection problem, use the strong Boussinesq approximation in the constant-property sense: the thermal expansion coefficient, kinematic viscosity, thermal diffusivity and heat capacity are also independent of temperature over the layer. This additional assumption makes the conduction profile linear and the perturbation coefficients constant. This usage of “strong” is the convection convention described in courses.physics.ucsd.edu/2021/Spring/physics218c/Spiegel%20Convection.pdf . Some stratified-flow literature uses “strong” instead to emphasize a globally constant reference density; that condition is also satisfied here. The equations below specify precisely which assumptions are being used.
Introduce the thermal diffusivity κ and positive thermal expansion coefficient α, which are necessary material data even though the question names only ν explicitly. Measure z upward from the bottom. The conductive state of Rayleigh-Bénard convection is Tb​=T0​+ΔT(1−z/d) with zero velocity. Let θ=T−Tb​ and let π be perturbation pressure divided by the reference density, after subtracting the hydrostatic base pressure. Linearizing gives
(∂t​−ν∇2)u=−∇π+gαθez​,∇⋅u=0,(∂t​−κ∇2)θ=dΔT​w.
(1)
The sign in the last equation is positive because upward motion carries warmer base-state fluid into cooler surroundings. Taking the divergence of momentum gives ∇2π=gαθz​. Apply a Laplacian to its vertical component and subtract the vertical derivative of that pressure identity. This eliminates pressure:
(∂t​−ν∇2)∇2w=gα∇h2​θ,∇h2​=∂x2​+∂y2​.
(2)
Apply the temperature operator to obtain the vertical-velocity equation
(∂t​−κ∇2)(∂t​−ν∇2)∇2w=dgαΔT​∇h2​w.​
(3)
This is the pressure- and temperature-eliminated linear stability analysis equation for Rayleigh-Bénard convection.
At each boundary, impermeability gives w=0, the stress-free boundary condition gives wzz​=0, and perfect thermal conduction gives θ=0. To obtain the second condition, zero tangential traction gives ∂z​uh​+∇h​w=0; since w vanishes identically along the boundary, differentiate horizontal incompressibility to get wzz​=0. For the fully eliminated velocity equation, θ=0 and the preceding momentum relation also imply wzzzz​=0 there. Thus suitable formulations are
w=wzz​=0,θ=0(z=0,d),​
(4)
or, for the sixth-order vertical equation alone, w=wzz​=wzzzz​=0 at both boundaries. Fixed boundary temperature means a zero perturbation there, not zero perturbation heat flux.
Use normal modes w=Wsin(nπz/d)eσt+ik⋅xh​, n≥1, k=∣k∣>0. Put K2=k2+n2π2/d2. Substitution yields the stress-free convection growth-rate polynomial
(σ+νK2)(σ+κK2)K2=dgαΔT​k2.​
(5)
At stationary marginal stability, σ=0. In terms of the Rayleigh number Ra=gαΔTd3/(νκ) and dimensionless horizontal wave number a=kd, the free-slip convection neutral curve is
Ran​(a)=a2(a2+n2π2)3​.
(6)
Writing y=a2 and differentiating gives dlogRan​/dy=3/(y+n2π2)−1/y. Its zero is a2=n2π2/2, with minimum 27n4π4/4. The smallest vertical mode is n=1, so the critical temperature difference is
Rac​=427π4​,ΔTmax​=4gαd327π4νκ​.​
(7)
The first unstable horizontal wavelength is 22​d. To check that an oscillatory mode cannot destabilize earlier, expand the growth-rate equation: its linear coefficient is (ν+κ)K2>0, and its constant term changes sign exactly at the stationary neutral curve. For positive temperature difference the discriminant is (ν−κ)2K4+4gαΔTk2/(dK2)>0, so the crossing root is real. Below the global critical value every convection mode decays; at criticality the selected mode is marginal; above it some modes grow and eventually require nonlinear advection, producing convective heat transport. This is exchange of stabilities in stress-free convection.
Strictly, the linearized equations remain the correct small-amplitude equations on either side of onset. What fails above ΔTmax​ is stability of the conductive state and long-time validity of a small-perturbation description, not the algebra of linearization. The threshold also presumes that the required small density variations and constant-property approximation hold at that temperature difference.
