The usual compact closed-manifold hypothesis is needed for the first assertion, or a proper bounded-below Morse exhaustion in an appropriate noncompact version. As literally printed without any such condition, f(x)=x on R is a Morse function with no critical points, while b0​=1. We prove the intended closed-manifold statement, which applies to the surface product later in the question.
By the Morse handle-attachment theorem, crossing an index-i critical point attaches an i-handle, and no topological change occurs between critical levels. Retraction to the core disks of handles gives a CW complex model with ci​ cells in dimension i. Its cellular chain complex has Ci​≅Zci​. Their weak form is ci​≥bi​. To see the algebra behind the strong form, put ri​=rankQ​∂i​ in the Morse chain complex, with r0​=rm+1​=0. The kernel-image dimensions give ci​=bi​+ri​+ri+1​, so the alternating difference at j is exactly rj+1​≥0. Conversely, given the strong inequalities, denote those alternating differences by Sj​, with S−1​=0. Then cj​−bj​=Sj​+Sj−1​≥0, proving that they imply every weak inequality.
They are not equivalent as numerical restrictions. For the Betti numbers b=(1,0,0,1) of S3, the formal counts c=(2,0,0,2) satisfy all weak inequalities and the Euler equality, but at j=1 give c1​−c0​=−2<−1=b1​−b0​. They therefore cannot be the counts of a Morse function on S3; the strong inequalities detect that additional obstruction.
If all the strong inequalities are equalities, all ri​ vanish and ci​=bi​. An integer boundary matrix with rational rank zero is the zero matrix. Thus the integral complex itself has zero differential, not merely its rationalization. Consequently
Hi​(M;Z)≅Zci​=Zbi​(0≤i≤m),​

and higher homology vanishes. In particular there is no homology torsion. This is an integral consequence of a Perfect Morse function, rather than just an assertion about ranks.
For Σg​, the integral homology is free with Betti numbers (1,2g,1). The Künneth theorem therefore has no torsion terms, and the product's Poincaré polynomial is

Choose a surface Morse function h with one minimum, 2g saddles and one maximum. It can be built from one closed disk, the standard 2g one-handles, and a closing closed disk, using the local forms u2+v2, −u2+v2, and −u2−v2 at the handle centres. Regular collars interpolate between the handles without new critical points. Now set F(x,y)=h(x)+h(y). Its critical points are the ordered pairs of those of h, and its Hessian matrix is a block direct sum; the indices add. This product of Morse functions has exactly (2g+2)2 critical points and realizes every displayed Betti count. Small bumps constant near the critical pairs can separate their values without altering the Hessians or creating new critical points.
The same minimum does not apply to arbitrary smooth functions. Here is an explicit finite-critical-point counterexample, avoiding any assumption that degeneracy leaves the Morse lower bound intact. On T2=(R/πZ)2, take
h0​(u,v)=sinusinvsin(u−v).
(9)
It is well defined because shifting either coordinate by π reverses two of its factors. Its derivatives are sinvsin(2u−v) and sinusin(u−2v). If either sinu or sinv vanishes, both derivatives vanish only at (0,0). Otherwise 2u−v and u−2v must be multiples of π, giving exactly (π/3,2π/3) and (2π/3,π/3) in addition to the origin. At the first point the Hessian is 3​(1−1/2​−1/21​), positive definite; at the second it is its negative. They are a minimum and maximum. The origin is an isolated degenerate monkey saddle, with leading term uv(u−v). Thus this three-critical-point function on a torus has exactly three critical points.
For g>1, perform connected-sum gluing of functions between this torus and a perfect Morse surface of genus g−1: delete a small neighbourhood of the torus maximum and of the other surface's minimum, and join their regular boundary circles by a monotone cylinder. Collar coordinates ensure nonzero differential throughout the neck, so precisely those two critical points are lost and none are added. The result on Σg​ has 3+[2(g−1)+2]−2=2g+1 critical points, retaining the degenerate point. For g=1, simply use h0​. Calling this smooth function h and the earlier perfect Morse function h, the smooth product h(x)+h(y) has
(2g+1)(2g+2)<(2g+2)2​

critical points. This explicitly proves the negative answer to the unrestricted smooth-minimum question; it does not assert that this smaller illustrative count is sharp.
