Choose real constants a0​<a1​<⋯<am​ and define the smooth homogeneous quotient
f([x0​:⋯:xm​])=∑j=0m​xj2​∑j=0m​aj​xj2​​.​
(16)
It is unchanged by nonzero rescaling, so it is well defined on Real projective space. On the unit sphere its differential vanishes on the tangent space precisely when Ax is proportional to x, where A=diag(a0​,…,am​). The entries are distinct, so the only critical projective lines are pi​=[ei​]. In the affine chart xi​=1, write uj​=xj​/xi​ for j=i. Then
f=ai​+1+∑j=i​uj2​∑j=i​(aj​−ai​)uj2​​.
(17)
Its Hessian at zero is diagonal with entries 2(aj​−ai​). Exactly i are negative, and none vanish. Thus this is a Morse function with one critical point pi​ of index i for every 0≤i≤m.
Choose the quotient of the round metric on the unit sphere. The downward gradient flow has sphere equation x˙j​=−2(aj​−f(x))xj​ and projective solution
[x0​(t):⋯:xm​(t)]=[e−2a0​tx0​(0):⋯:e−2am​txm​(0)].
(18)
Normalization to the unit sphere supplies the common positive factor. As t→+∞, the smallest index with nonzero coordinate dominates; as t→−∞, the largest dominates. Consequently
Wu(pi​)={xi​=0, xj​=0 (j>i)}≅Ri,Ws(pi​)={xi​=0, xj​=0 (j<i)}≅Rm−i.
(19)
These are its unstable manifolds and stable manifolds. If j≥k, the tangent directions in Wu(pj​) allow coordinates 0,…,j, and those in Ws(pk​) allow k,…,m, modulo the common projective line. Together they span the full tangent space at every intersection. If j<k the intersection is empty. Thus the flow is a Morse-Smale gradient flow, with no generic perturbation needed.
Orient each unstable manifold using its affine coordinates. The Morse-Smale complex has Ci​=Zpi​; its differential counts oriented unparametrized connecting trajectories of index difference one. A trajectory from pi​ to pi−1​ has only coordinates xi−1​,xi​ nonzero, and there are exactly two, distinguished by the sign of xi−1​/xi​. Each sign branch is a single orbit modulo time translation.
To calculate their signs rather than merely count two, use the characteristic closed disk of the unstable cell: the closed upper hemisphere of Si with xi​≥0, followed by projection onto RPi. Its interior is Wu(pi​), and its boundary map is the antipodal double cover Si−1→RPi−1. After collapsing RPi−2, its degree on the (i−1)-cell is the sum of the degrees of the two sheets. Comparing the sheets by the antipodal map on Si−1 gives relative orientation (−1)i: it is induced by −I on Ri, whose determinant is (−1)i. Thus the two trajectory signs add to 1+(−1)i, up to the choice of generator orientations. The projective quadratic Morse complex is therefore
Ci​=Z (0≤i≤m),∂i​={2,0,​i even,i odd.​​
(20)
In particular ∂1​=0, as required for the connected one-dimensional skeleton. The signed trajectory count agrees with this attaching-map incidence calculation, so it computes integral rather than only mod-two homology.
For m≥1, taking kernels modulo images gives
Hk​(RPm;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/2,Z,0,​k=0,0<k<m and k odd,k=m and m odd,otherwise.​​
(21)
For example, an odd interior-degree generator has zero outgoing differential but twice itself is the image from the next degree; an even positive-degree differential is injective. At the top there is no incoming differential, leaving a copy of Z exactly when m is odd. For m=0 the space is one point and only H0​=Z remains. This recovers the cellular homology of real projective space by explicitly constructing its Morse-Smale complex and signs.
