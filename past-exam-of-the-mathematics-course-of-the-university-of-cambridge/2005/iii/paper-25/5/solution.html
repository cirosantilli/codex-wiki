The smooth h-cobordism theorem states: if (W;M0​,M1​) is a compact smooth h-cobordism, dimW≥6, and its connected boundary manifolds are simply connected, then there is a diffeomorphism W≅M0​×[0,1] restricting to the prescribed identification on M0​. In particular M0​ and M1​ are diffeomorphic. Being an h-cobordism means that both inclusions Mi​↪W are homotopy equivalences; their fundamental groups then also identify with that of W.
The homotopy-equivalence condition is necessary for a trivial cobordism: each end of a cylinder is a deformation retract, by (x,t)↦(x,(1−s)t) for the incoming end and (x,t)↦(x,(1−s)t+s) for the outgoing end. Its relative homology with respect to either end is consequently zero. Compactness is also necessary for a product with a closed compact end. The dimensional bound and simple connectivity are not necessary for an individual cobordism to be a product. The cylinder S2×[0,1] is a simply connected trivial cobordism of dimension three; and, in the theorem's dimension range, (S1×S4)×[0,1] is a six-dimensional trivial cobordism with fundamental group Z. These prove the two non-necessity assertions explicitly, while the inclusion argument proves necessity of the h-condition.
For the remaining claims, use a cobordism Morse function: its two regular boundary levels are zero and one, and all critical points are interior. An arbitrary function with unrelated boundary values would not have the relative handle-count property. The relative Morse chain complex for (W,M0​) has one generator per critical point, in its Morse index. Thus
χ(W,M0​)=∑r∈Crit(f)​(−1)ind(r).
(22)
A trivial cobordism has zero relative homology, hence zero relative Euler characteristic. Modulo two the displayed sum is the number of critical points. An odd number makes it odd, and therefore nonzero. This proves an odd-critical-point cobordism is not trivial.
With precisely two critical points of indices λ≤μ, a trivial cobordism requires the two-generator relative complex to be acyclic over Z. Equal indices leave a rank-two homology group; a gap of more than one leaves both generators with zero possible differential. Therefore μ=λ+1 is necessary. The remaining two-term complex is
0⟶Zq×a​Zp⟶0.
(23)
Choose a transverse gradient-like vector field. A nonzero differential requires the index-μ point to be above the index-λ point; otherwise no descending connecting trajectories exist. In a common regular level between them, the belt sphere SU​(p) has dimension d−λ−1, and the attaching sphere SL​(q) has dimension μ−1=λ, where d=dimW. Their dimensions add to d−1, the level's dimension, so their transverse intersection consists of points. Each intersection is one descending trajectory from q to p, modulo time translation. Orient the attaching sphere and use an orientation of a vector space on the handle core to coorient the belt sphere; the resulting signed count is the cellular boundary coefficient
a=SU​(p)⋅SL​(q).
(24)
The two homology groups are the kernel and cokernel of multiplication by a. They vanish precisely when a is a unit of Z. Consequently
μ=λ+1,SU​(p)⋅SL​(q)=±1​
(25)
are necessary. This proves the two-critical-point obstruction to a trivial cobordism; it makes an algebraic incidence statement and does not assume that an algebraic count of one automatically supplies geometric handle cancellation.
For an explicit nontrivial cobordism from S3 to itself, attach two zero-framed two-handles to the outgoing end of S3×[0,1] along the standard Hopf link. Its two components are unknots with linking number one. This realizes two successive (2,2) surgeries, as suggested by the hint. The outgoing boundary is again S3, and this can be checked geometrically: the Hopf-link exterior is T2×[0,1], and each component's zero-framing longitude is the other's meridian. Filling along these two slopes glues two solid tori with meridians intersecting once, the standard genus-one Heegaard decomposition of S3. Thus zero surgery on the Hopf link does return the three-sphere, not just an unspecified homology sphere.
The trace has only the two added index-two handles. Its relative cellular complex has C2​(W,S3)=Z2 and all other groups zero, so
H2​(W,S3;Z)=Z2=0.​
(26)
It cannot be a product cylinder. Equivalently this trace is S2×S2 with two disjoint open four-balls removed: the usual handle presentation of S2×S2 has one zero-handle, the two zero-framed Hopf-linked two-handles, and one four-handle. Removing the bottom and top balls exposes the two three-sphere ends. The relative-homology calculation alone already proves the requested cobordism is nontrivial.
