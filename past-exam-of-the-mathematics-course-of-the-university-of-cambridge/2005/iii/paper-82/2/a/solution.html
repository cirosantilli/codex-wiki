Assume the coefficients are sufficiently differentiable in the region considered, with no material discontinuity or caustic at the front. Let t=T(x) describe arrival time, p=∇T the slowness vector, and n the unit elastic wave propagation normal. For any field, its jump is the value immediately behind the front minus that immediately ahead. If the first nonzero jump occurs in the kth time derivative, k≥2, write

The restriction k≥2 lets displacement field and its first time derivative remain continuous and avoids an imposed impulsive source at the front.

∂q​[ρα(a[k])2nq​]=0.​
(21)
The wave amplitude here is real for a real jump; a complex wave amplitude uses the corresponding modulus-squared law. An elastic ray follows dx/ds=n, with arclength s and dt/ds=1/α. If J is the infinitesimal cross-sectional area of a neighboring ray tube, dlogJ/ds=∇⋅n. Thus ραa2J is constant along that tube, the ray-tube conservation for a P-wave jump. This defines the geometrical spreading and shows why the wave amplitude changes with material properties even before attenuation is considered. At a caustic the simple ray-tube wave amplitude is singular and a uniform local description is needed.
