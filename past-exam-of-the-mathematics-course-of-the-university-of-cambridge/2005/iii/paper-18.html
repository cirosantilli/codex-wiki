Sending a matrix to its last column gives the special orthogonal sphere fibration
SO(n−1)⟶SO(n)⟶Sn−1,n≥2.
(1)
The action is transitive and the stabilizer of that column is SO(n−1). Local orthonormal frames, obtained by Gram-Schmidt process near a chosen unit vector, give local sections and hence local bundle trivializations. This allows the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration to be applied.
At the bottom of the induction, SO(1) is a point and SO(2) is the rotation circle S1. For SO(3), identify R3 with imaginary quaternions. A unit quaternion acts by v↦qvq−1, preserving length and orientation. Every rotation through angle θ about a unit imaginary axis u comes from q=cos(θ/2)+usin(θ/2). The kernel is precisely {1,−1}, since a quaternion commuting with all imaginary quaternions is real. Thus this gives the twofold covering space S3→SO(3). Its simply connected total space is the universal cover, yielding π1​SO(3)=Z/2. Coverings induce isomorphisms on higher homotopy groups, so π2​SO(3)=π2​S3=0 and π3​SO(3)=π3​S3=Z.
For n≥4, both π1​Sn−1 and π2​Sn−1 vanish. The Serre fibration sequence therefore identifies π1​SO(n−1) with π1​SO(n) and propagates vanishing of π2​. Path-connectedness also propagates from connected fiber and base. Consequently the full low-degree answer is
π0​SO(n)={∗},π2​SO(n)=0(n≥1),π1​SO(n)=⎩⎨⎧​0,Z,Z/2,​n=1,n=2,n≥3.​​
(2)
Here π0​ records the single connected component.
For the remaining third group use SO(3)→SO(4)→S3. The relevant exact segment, using the supplied fourth homotopy group, is
Z/2⟶Z⟶π3​SO(4)⟶Z⟶0.
(3)
The first homomorphism is zero since a torsion group cannot map nontrivially to Z. Thus it reduces to a short exact sequence 0→Z→π3​SO(4)→Z→0. These groups are abelian and the final free group has a lift of its generator, so the sequence splits. Therefore
π3​SO(1)=π3​SO(2)=0,π3​SO(3)=Z,π3​SO(4)=Z⊕Z.​
(4)
These are the low homotopy groups of special orthogonal groups.

An immersion M4→R5 would inject TM into the trivial rank-five bundle. Taking its orthogonal complement gives a normal real line bundle ν with TM⊕ν≅ε5. The Whitney product formula for Stiefel–Whitney classes requires
w(TM)w(ν)=1.
(5)
The real line bundle need not be orientable, but it has no Stiefel–Whitney classes above degree one.
Let a,b be the pulled-back degree-one generators from the two real projective planes. Their mod-two cohomology ring, by the Künneth theorem, is F2​[a,b]/(a3,b3). To compute the tangent classes, at a line L the projective tangent space is Hom(L,L⊥). Adding Hom(L,L)≅R gives Hom(L,R3)≅3L, with the real line identified with its dual using a metric. Thus the stabilized tangent bundle of real projective space gives TRP2⊕ε1≅3γ and
w(TRP2)=(1+a)3=1+a+a2.
(6)
The tangent bundle of the product is the direct sum of the pulled-back tangent bundles. Since (1+a+a2)(1+a)=1 in this truncated ring, and similarly for b,
w(TM)−1=(1+a)(1+b)=1+a+b+ab.
(7)
The degree-two class ab is nonzero by the product-ring basis. It cannot be a class of the rank-one bundle ν. Equivalently w1​(TM)2+w2​(TM)=ab=0, violating the codimension-one Stiefel–Whitney immersion obstruction. Hence RP2×RP2 admits no immersion in R5.

Apply the splitting principle for complex vector bundles simultaneously to E and F. This can be implemented by successive projective bundle pullbacks; the projective bundle theorem makes their composite pullback injective on integral cohomology. After pullback write E=L1​⊕L2​ and F=M1​⊕M2​, with Chern roots x1​,x2​ and y1​,y2​. Let
A=x1​+x2​=c1​(E),C=x1​x2​=c2​(E),B=y1​+y2​=c1​(F),D=y1​y2​=c2​(F).
(8)
Since the First Chern class of a tensor product of complex line bundles is the sum of their first Chern classes, the four roots of E⊗F are xi​+yj​, 1≤i,j≤2.
Compute their six pairwise products integrally. The two pairs within a fixed xi​ row contribute
∑i=12​(xi​+y1​)(xi​+y2​)=x12​+x22​+AB+2D=A2−2C+AB+2D.
(9)
The four pairs between the two rows contribute
∑j,k=12​(x1​+yj​)(x2​+yk​)=4C+2AB+B2.
(10)
Their sum is the second elementary symmetric polynomial of the tensor roots. Injectivity of the pullback returns the resulting identity to X, giving
c2​(E⊗F)=c1​(E)2+3c1​(E)c1​(F)+c1​(F)2+2c2​(E)+2c2​(F).​
(11)
Products here are cup products in integral cohomology. This second Chern class of a tensor product of rank-two bundles formula uses no division by two and therefore holds without assuming the integral cohomology of X is torsion-free.

Let p:E→Sr be the Serre fibration. For r≥1, cover the base by U=Sr∖{s} and a small open ball V about s. Write A=p−1U and B=p−1V. The contractible-base assumption gives A≃F and identifies the pair (B,A∩B), up to fiberwise homotopy, with (F×V,F×(V∖{s})).
Excision for the open cover E=A∪B gives Hi​(E,A)≅Hi​(B,A∩B). The relative base pair has
Hj​(V,V∖{s};Z)={Z,0,​j=r,j=r,​
(12)
by the deformation retraction of the punctured ball onto Sr−1 and its relative homology sequence. The relative Künneth theorem therefore gives
Hi​(E,A;Z)≅Hi−r​(F;Z).
(13)
There is no Tor term because the only nonzero base group is free. The long exact sequence of a pair, replacing A by its homotopy-equivalent fiber, now becomes
⋯⟶Hi​(F;Z)⟶Hi​(E;Z)⟶Hi−r​(F;Z)⟶Hi−1​(F;Z)⟶⋯.​
(14)
The last arrow is the relative boundary transported through these identifications; it can encode twisting, so it has not been set to zero. This proves the relative homology of a fibration over a sphere sequence. In the dimension-zero case, the total space is the disjoint union of the two fibers and the same formula is the split sequence for that union.

Use the based path space and the path-loop fibration ΩSr→PSr→Sr, where paths begin at a fixed basepoint and the projection evaluates their final endpoint. The total space is contractible: the homotopy γ(t)↦γ(st), s↓0, contracts every path to the constant path. The fiber is path-connected because Sr is simply connected. Hence H0​(ΩSr)=Z and all positive homology of PSr vanishes.
The exact sequence from part (i), for i≥2, gives Hi−r​(ΩSr)≅Hi−1​(ΩSr). Put d=r−1. Taking negative-degree homology to be zero, this says Hj​≅Hj−d​ for every j≥1. Starting with H0​=Z determines every group:
Hj​(ΩSr;Z)={Z,0,​j≥0 and d∣j,otherwise.​​
(15)
There are no torsion groups hidden in the recurrence, since each step is an actual integral isomorphism. This is the integral homology of the loop space of a sphere.
Choose the unit map η:Sr−1→ΩSr adjoint to the identity under ΣSr−1≅Sr. Loop adjunction gives πj​(ΩSr)≅πj+1​(Sr), so the loop space is (r−2)-connected and πr−1​(ΩSr)≅Z. The adjoint of η is the degree-one identity, so η sends the generator of πr−1​(Sr−1) to that generator. The Hurewicz theorem for both (r−2)-connected spaces, and its naturality, then show that η∗​ is an isomorphism on Hr−1​. It also is on H0​, since both spaces are connected. All other positive degrees below 2r−2 vanish in both spaces. Thus
η∗​:Hj​(Sr−1;Z)≅​Hj​(ΩSr;Z),j≤2r−3.​
(16)
This proves the required existence with a specific map, without using the suspension theorem in advance.

Use the unit η just constructed and replace it by the inclusion A=Sr−1↪Y=Cyl(η) into its mapping cylinder; Y≃ΩSr. Put m=2r−3. The homology isomorphisms from question 4(ii) and the relative exact sequence give Hj​(Y,A;Z)=0 for j≤m.
Both A and Y are simply connected for r≥3. For the degree-two start, the absolute Hurewicz theorem identifies their π2​ with H2​; naturality and the homology isomorphism show η∗​ is an isomorphism on π2​, so π2​(Y,A)=0. The relative fundamental group also vanishes. If there were a first nonzero relative homotopy group in some degree 3≤k≤m, the Relative Hurewicz theorem for a (k−1)-connected simply connected pair would identify it with Hk​(Y,A), which is zero. Induction therefore gives πj​(Y,A)=0 for all j≤m.
The relative homotopy sequence now makes η∗​:πi​(Sr−1)→πi​(ΩSr) an isomorphism when i+1≤m, and a surjection at i=m. Under loop-suspension adjunction, the adjoint of η∘a is Σa, so this particular isomorphism is the suspension homomorphism. Consequently the requested Freudenthal suspension theorem is
πi​(Sr−1) Σ ​πi+1​(Sr) is an isomorphism for r≥3, i≤2r−4.​
(17)
For i=0 this is read as the identification of connected-component sets; all degree-one groups here also vanish. The proof is the homology comparison proof of Freudenthal suspension.
To compute the specified unstable groups, S1 has contractible universal cover, so π2​(S1)=0. The Hopf fibration S1→S3→S2 has exact segment 0=π3​S1→π3​S3→π3​S2→π2​S1=0, giving π3​S2=Z. The supplied starting value is π4​S3=Z/2. To pass from the group over Sn−1 to that over Sn, use r=n,i=n; the condition n≤2n−4 holds exactly when n≥4. Thus every later step is an isomorphism, and
πn+1​(Sn)=⎩⎨⎧​0,Z,Z/2,​n=1,n=2,n≥3.​​
(18)
This is the first homotopy group above the dimension of a sphere. The stable range deliberately excludes the step from n=2 to n=3, so the different groups there do not conflict with the theorem.

ii OurBigBook.com
The orthogonal group acts transitively on unoriented lines in Rn. The stabilizer of the line spanned by the first coordinate is
H={diag(ϵ,A):ϵ∈O(1), A∈O(n−1)}≅O(1)×O(n−1).​
(19)
Thus G/H=RPn−1; using only O(n−1) would stabilize a vector and give a sphere instead. For the universal contractible free G-space EG, the associated bundle EG/H→EG/G gives the Serre fibration G/H→BH→BG. Its total space identifies up to homotopy with BO(1)×BO(n−1).
If n is odd, n−1 is even and the rational cohomology of RPn−1 is just Q in degree zero. This follows from the integral projective cellular differentials, alternating zero and multiplication by two, with the even-dimensional top class killed rationally. The cohomological Serre spectral sequence therefore has only the row
E2p,0​=Hp(BO(n);Q),
(20)
with all other rows zero. The local system in degree zero is trivial because the fiber is connected. There can be no differential or extension, and the multiplicative edge map H∗(BO(n);Q)→H∗(BH;Q) is an isomorphism of rings.
Also BO(1)=RP∞ has rational cohomology Q in degree zero only, by the same cellular calculation. The Künneth theorem identifies H∗(BH;Q) with H∗(BO(n−1);Q). The inclusion fixing the first coordinate factors through H, setting its O(1) coordinate to one; hence the resulting isomorphism is the actual restriction along this inclusion:
H∗(BO(n);Q)≅​H∗(BO(n−1);Q)(n odd).​
(21)
This includes n=1, with BO(0) a point, and proves rational odd-rank stabilization of orthogonal classifying spaces.
For even n, the fiber is odd-dimensional real projective space and has a further rational class in degree n−1. The spectral sequence has a possible additional row, now with determinant-sign local coefficients: an orientation-reversing orthogonal matrix acts with degree −1 on the covering sphere and hence on the odd-dimensional projective space. Therefore the one-row collapse argument is unavailable; this top row must not be treated as automatically untwisted.
There is an actual failure of injectivity in every even rank. Write n=2m. The universal class pm​∈H4m(BO(2m);Q) is nonzero: pull the universal bundle back to the sum of the real underlying bundles of m complex lines over (CP∞)m. If their first Chern classes are xj​, the Pontryagin formula pm​(E)=(−1)mc2m​(E⊗C) gives pm​=∏j​xj2​=0. On BO(2m−1) the restricted bundle is ε1⊕γ2m−1​; its top complex Chern class c2m​ vanishes by the rank bound and the trivial summand. Thus pm​ restricts to zero. The natural restriction is not injective for even n, as the extra fiber cohomology already warns.
