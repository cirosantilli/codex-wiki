For statistical homogeneity and statistical isotropy, with the paper's finite-volume Fourier normalization,

At zero the integrand behaves as un+2, requiring n>−3; at infinity its oscillatory integral converges for n<−1. Thus in the ordinary convergent range −3<n<−1, the scale-free density correlation transform has
In​=Γ(n+2)sin2π(n+2)​>0,I−2​=2π​,γ=n+3,r0γ​=2π2VCIn​​.​
(24)
The gamma function expression follows by introducing e−ϵu, evaluating the complex Laplace integral where it converges absolutely at zero, and continuing within the convergent oscillatory range; its removable singularity at n=−2 is handled by the limit. Absolute convergence of the original correlation integral requires the smaller range −3<n<−2.
The exponent relation is a useful scaling statement beyond that interval, but a pure primordial n≃1 spectrum over all wavenumbers has no ordinary unsmoothed Fourier correlation integral. Physical transfer functions, cutoffs or smoothing must be specified, and a distributional extension need not yield a positive r0​ power law. Even within the convergent interval, the point variance is ultraviolet divergent without smoothing; the finite result here is at nonzero separation.
For a representative present-day ordinary galaxy sample over roughly 0.1–10h−1Mpc, the familiar approximate fit is
r0​≃5h−1Mpc,γ≃1.8,​
(25)
where H0​=100hkms−1Mpc−1. These are empirical galaxy clustering parameters, not universal matter-spectrum constants; luminosity, color, selection and scale change the fit, as shown in the galaxy correlation measurements. A formal power law of that slope corresponds to n≃−1.2 on those scales, not to the nearly scale-invariant primordial spectral index.
