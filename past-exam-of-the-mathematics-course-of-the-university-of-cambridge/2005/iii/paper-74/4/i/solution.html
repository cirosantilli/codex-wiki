In the hydrogen–helium approximation, take helium to be helium-4, and assume essentially all neutrons remaining at the onset of efficient nuclear burning enter it. Each helium-4 nucleus contains two neutrons and two protons, hence
nHe​=2nn​​,nH​=np​−nn​,nB​=np​+nn​.
(28)
Here np​,nn​ count constituent nucleons, not just free particles after burning. Neglecting the small nucleon mass difference and nuclear binding-energy correction, the helium mass is 4mu​nHe​ and the total baryon mass is mu​nB​. Thus neutron-limited helium synthesis gives
Yp​=nB​4nHe​​=np​+nn​2nn​​=2(1+nn​np​​)−1.​
(29)
For nn​/np​≃1/7, this yields Yp​≃1/4 and the hydrogen mass fraction is Xp​≃3/4. The assumptions require np​≥nn​ and negligible free neutrons, deuterium and helium-3: the phrase hydrogen and helium must here mean predominantly hydrogen-1 and helium-4, not an arbitrary mixture of helium isotopes.
