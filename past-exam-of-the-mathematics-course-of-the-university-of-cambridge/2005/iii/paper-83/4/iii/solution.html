At fixed particle numbers, the perturbations satisfy ∫δni​d3x=0. Expanding the uniform interaction energy, its linear term is ∫(μ1​δn1​+μ2​δn2​)d3x=0, and its exact quadratic change in this contact-interaction approximation is

Hence the miscibility criterion for a two-component condensate is
U11​>0,U22​>0,U122​<U11​U22​.​
(44)
These are precisely strict positive definiteness of the interaction matrix, and make every nonzero admissible long-wavelength density perturbation cost energy. With both equilibrium densities positive, a negative eigenvalue would instead allow a small sinusoidal perturbation along its eigenvector, with zero mean and negative energy change. The omitted positive gradient term cannot cure arbitrarily long wavelengths in an unbounded system.
If cross-repulsion exceeds the geometric mean of the self-repulsions, the unstable direction favors separation of species; sufficiently strong cross-attraction favors a joint density increase and collapse. Equality gives a zero-cost direction at this order, so it is marginal rather than strictly stable. Constraints near an absent component or finite-system wavelength restrictions require a separate boundary analysis.
