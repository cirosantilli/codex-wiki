Use H=a′/a and the Fourier convention ∇→ik. A valid linearization requires all components of hij​ to be small: the printed small-determinant condition alone is insufficient, as the matrix diag(1,1,0) has zero determinant without being small. This is small determinant does not imply a small metric perturbation.
Define the density contrast δ=(ρ−ρˉ​)/ρˉ​ and the scalar velocity potential by vi=∂iθ, or vki​=ikiθk​. Hence ∇⋅v=−k2θ. To first order, u0=a−1, ui=a−1vi and gij=a−2(δij−hij). The product uiuj is second order. Substitution into the perfect fluid stress-energy tensor gives
T00=a−2ρˉ​(1+δ),T0i=a−2(1+w)ρˉ​ikiθ,Tij=a−2wρˉ​[(1+δ)δij−hij].​
(21)
Here the equation of state is used locally, so the pressure perturbation is wρˉ​δ.
The contraction in ∇μ​T0μ=0 is
∂μ​T0μ+Γμν0​Tνμ+Γμνμ​T0ν=0.
(22)
Using the displayed connections gives, through first order,
ρ′+3H(ρ+P)+(ρˉ​+Pˉ)(∇⋅v+21​h′)=0.
(23)
In particular Γμ0μ​=4H+h′/2; the spatial connection contraction adds 3HP+Pˉh′/2, while differentiating a−2ρ supplies −2Hρ. Subtract the background relation ρˉ​′+3H(1+w)ρˉ​=0. The background terms multiplying δ cancel, leaving
δ′−(1+w)k2θ+21​(1+w)h′=0.​
(24)
For comoving cold dark matter, w=0 and θ=0, so δc′​=−h′/2 and δc​+h/2 is constant at each spatial point. The proper-volume element of a comoving cell is a3det(I+h)​d3x=a3(1+h/2)d3x. Conserved mass in the cell then gives the density change opposite to the perturbation of its volume. In comoving synchronous coordinates the linear CDM density change is a volume change, not transport across cell boundaries.
