For H≤G of finite subgroup index, let H′=[H,H] and choose a right-coset transversal T for the cosets Ht. For each g∈G write
tg=h(t,g)tg​,h(t,g)∈H,tg​∈T.
(18)
The transfer homomorphism is
VG,H​(g)=t∈T∏​h(t,g)(modH′) ∈H/H′.​
(19)
The product is taken in the abelianization, so its order is irrelevant. If another transversal has representative at​t for Ht, with at​∈H, its factor is at​h(t,g)atg​−1​. The map t↦tg​ permutes T, so the extra factors cancel in H/H′. This proves independence of the transversal. Also
h(t,g1​g2​)=h(t,g1​)h(tg1​​,g2​).
(20)
Multiplying over t and again permuting the second indices proves V(g1​g2​)=V(g1​)V(g2​). Thus it is indeed a group homomorphism.
The Burnside transfer theorem states that if a Sylow p-subgroup P of a finite group satisfies P⊆Z(NG​(P)), then G has a normal p-complement: a normal subgroup of order ∣G∣/∣P∣. The hypothesis first makes P abelian. It also makes fusion inside P trivial. To prove this, suppose u,v∈P and ug=v, using ug=g−1ug. Both P and Pg lie in CG​(v): the former because P is abelian, and the latter because v∈Pg. They are Sylow subgroups of that centralizer. Choose c∈CG​(v) with (Pg)c=P. Then gc∈NG​(P) and ugc=vc=v, while the central-normalizer hypothesis gives ugc=u. Thus u=v.
Evaluate transfer to the abelian group P on u∈P. In the action of u on right cosets, a cycle of length r contributes
∏j=0r−1​tj​utj+1−1​=t0​urt0−1​∈P,
(21)
with tr​=t0​. This is a conjugate of ur inside P and hence equals ur by the fusion argument. Summing the cycle lengths therefore gives
VG,P​(u)=u[G:P].
(22)
The exponent is coprime to p, so this power map is an automorphism of the finite abelian p-group P. Transfer is consequently onto P, and its kernel is normal of subgroup index ∣P∣ and of order prime to p. This proves the theorem. Notice also that a normal p-complement consists exactly of the elements of prime-to-p order: they map trivially to the p-group quotient, while all elements of the complement have such order. It is therefore unique and a characteristic subgroup.
Now let G≤Ap​ be primitive of degree p=2q+1, with q prime. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of a faithful primitive group action is transitive: its orbits form a G-invariant block partition, and singleton orbits would make it trivial. Such a subgroup has order divisible by p. A Sylow p-subgroup P has order p, since p! contains only one factor p. The normalizer of a regular p-cycle in Sp​ is the affine group x↦ax+b on Fp​, of order p(p−1). Translations are even, whereas a generating multiplier gives a (p−1)-cycle and is odd. Its normalizer in Ap​ therefore has order p(p−1)/2=pq.
Suppose G is not simple and choose 1<N◃G proper. It is transitive, so we can choose P≤N. The Frattini argument gives G=NNG​(P): for each g, the subgroup Pg is a Sylow subgroup of N and can be conjugated back to P within N. Since NG​(P) contains P and lies in the order-pq normalizer, it has order p or pq. If its order were p, the Burnside theorem applied to G would give a normal p-complement. A nontrivial such complement cannot be transitive because its order is prime to p, contradicting primitivity. It must be trivial, giving G=P, contrary to the choice of N. Thus ∣NG​(P)∣=pq.
The intersection NN​(P) again has order p or pq. If it had order pq, the whole normalizer would lie in N and the Frattini equality would give G=N. Hence NN​(P)=P. Applying Burnside transfer within N gives its normal p-complement K. By the uniqueness argument, K is a characteristic subgroup of N, and so normal in G. Primitivity again forces this prime-to-p subgroup to be trivial. Thus N=P is normal in G, and G=NG​(P) has order pq. The even primitive groups of safe-prime degree conclusion is
G is simple, or ∣G∣=pq.​
(23)
