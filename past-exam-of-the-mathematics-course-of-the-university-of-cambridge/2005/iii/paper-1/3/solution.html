Choose a minimal nontrivial normal subgroup L of K itself. Every conjugate Lg for g∈G is also a minimal normal subgroup of K, since K is normal in G. Distinct such subgroups intersect trivially: their intersection is normal in K and minimality forces either equality or trivial intersection. Moreover [Li​,Lj​]⊆Li​∩Lj​=1, so distinct ones commute.
Choose a maximal family of these conjugates whose product M=L1​×⋯×Lr​ is direct. For any further conjugate Lg, its intersection with M is normal in K and is either all of Lg or trivial. In the latter case it commutes with all the existing factors and could be adjoined to the direct product of groups, contrary to maximality. Thus every conjugate is contained in M. The product of all conjugates is consequently M, which is nontrivial and normal in G. Minimality of K as a G-normal subgroup gives M=K.
Any normal subgroup of a factor Li​ is normal in K: the other direct factors commute with it, and Li​ normalizes it. Minimality of Li​ as a K-normal subgroup makes it simple. All factors are isomorphic because they are conjugate to L. This proves the direct-product structure of a finite minimal normal subgroup:
K≅Srfor a finite simple group S.​
(11)
An abelian simple group is cyclic of prime order, so the abelian case is elementary abelian. Now let H be a maximal proper subgroup of a nonabelian simple group X. It is nontrivial, since X has nontrivial proper cyclic subgroups. Choose a minimal normal subgroup K of H. Then H≤NX​(K). This normalizer cannot be X, since that would make 1<K≤H<X normal in X. Maximality gives
H=NX​(K),K≅Sr.​
(12)
For each of the two groups in this question, the allowed absence of proper nonabelian simple subgroups makes the factors of such a K cyclic of prime order. Hence it suffices to examine normalizers of nontrivial elementary abelian subgroups.
In A5​, the possible primes are two, three and five. The Sylow subgroups for the last two primes are cyclic of orders three and five. Every involution is a double transposition; for (12)(34) the centralizer in A5​ is
{1,(12)(34),(13)(24),(14)(23)},
(13)
the Klein four-group fixing point five. Thus an elementary abelian 2-subgroup is cyclic of order two or a Klein four-group. The normalizer of a cyclic order-two subgroup equals its centralizer and has order four, so is contained in a point stabilizer of order twelve. A Klein four-group's common fixed point is intrinsic, and the full point stabilizer A4​ normalizes it; its normalizer is therefore exactly that stabilizer.
An order-three subgroup has support a triple. Its normalizer preserves the triple and the remaining pair, and is (S3​×S2​)∩A5​, of order six. An order-five subgroup acts regularly on all five points. Label the points by F5​, with the subgroup acting by translations. Its normalizer in S5​ is the twenty affine maps x↦ax+b. The translations are even, whereas multiplication by a generator of F5×​ is a four-cycle and is odd. Exactly half these maps are even, so the normalizer in A5​ has order ten.
The only candidates for maximal subgroups are thus the normalizers of orders twelve, ten and six. They are all maximal: none can lie in a larger candidate, except potentially an order-six subgroup in an order-twelve A4​. But an index-two subgroup of A4​ would contain every order-three element; there are eight such elements, plus the identity, already more than six. Hence that containment is impossible. Point stabilizers are conjugate; order-three and order-five subgroup normalizers each form one class by the Sylow theorems. Therefore the maximal subgroups of A5 form precisely
three conjugacy classes, of orders 12,10,6.​
(14)
Their numbers are five, six and ten respectively, since these proper maximal subgroups are self-normalizing in the simple group.
For X=GL3​(2), choosing independent columns gives ∣X∣=(8−1)(8−2)(8−4)=168. If t is an involution, write t=I+N. Field characteristic two gives N2=0, and imN⊆kerN in dimension three forces its nonzero rank to be one. Thus t=I+vf, with nonzero vector v, nonzero linear functional f and f(v)=0. This transvection fixes the plane kerf and has image line ⟨v⟩. Every flag consisting of an incident line and plane occurs, giving 7⋅3=21 involutions, all conjugate. Its centralizer, and hence the normalizer of its cyclic subgroup, is the flag stabilizer of order 168/21=8. It lies in both the line and plane stabilizers of order 24, so cannot be maximal.
For the elementary abelian subgroups in GL3 over F2, consider commuting transvections I+vf, I+wh. Their rank-one parts satisfy
vf(w)h=wh(v)f.
(15)
If v,w are independent, equality forces both sides zero; thus f,h vanish on ⟨v,w⟩ and are the same nonzero functional. If v=w, they have a common image line. Consequently the two types of order-four elementary abelian subgroup are
KL​={I+vf:f(v)=0},KW​={I+wf:w∈W=kerf}.
(16)
No extra transvection can commute with all elements of KL​: if its image were different, its functional would have to equal each of the two independent functionals occurring in KL​. Dually, the same holds for KW​. Thus these exhaust the elementary abelian 2-subgroups of rank at least two. The normalizer of KL​ is the line stabilizer and the normalizer of KW​ is the plane stabilizer, each of order 168/7=24. They give different conjugacy classes because their common fixed spaces have dimensions one and two. All line stabilizers are conjugate, as are all plane stabilizers.
For an order-three element, its minimal polynomial is (X−1)(X2+X+1): it has a fixed line and an invariant complementary irreducible plane. Its normalizer preserves both and is the full GL2​(2) on that plane, of order six, since the order-three subgroup is normal in GL2​(2). This normalizer lies in the corresponding line and plane stabilizers, so is not maximal.
An order-seven element is irreducible. Over the finite field F2​ one has X7−1=(X−1)(X3+X+1)(X3+X2+1), and both cubic factors are irreducible because neither has a root in F2​. Its minimal polynomial divides this square-free product and cannot be just X−1, since the element is not the identity. It therefore contains a cubic factor; the three-dimensional space forces that irreducible cubic to be the whole minimal polynomial. Identify the space with F8​ and the element with multiplication by a generator α of F8×​. Its centralizer consists of the seven nonzero multiplication maps. A normalizer induces an F2​-automorphism of this field, so it sends α to α, α2 or α4. All three possibilities occur via the field automorphisms x↦x2j. Therefore the normalizer of the Singer cycle has order 7⋅3=21, and all such normalizers are conjugate by the Sylow theorems.
The maximal-normalizer reduction leaves exactly the two order-twenty-four stabilizer types and the order-twenty-one type. Each is maximal because it cannot be contained in any larger remaining candidate; equal-order subgroups cannot properly contain one another and 21 does not divide 24. Thus
GL3​(2) has exactly three maximal-subgroup classes, of orders 24,24,21.​
(17)
