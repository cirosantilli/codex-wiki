Buoyancy and the convective criterion. Consider a small fluid parcel displaced outward by δr. Assume that sound waves keep its pressure equal to the ambient pressure, while heat exchange is slow enough that its displacement is an adiabatic process. Composition is uniform, so the parcel and its surroundings have the same mean molecular weight. For an ideal gas, P∝ρT, and an adiabatic parcel obeys P∝ργ. If δlnP<0 is the pressure change on moving outward, then
δlnρparcel​=γ1​δlnP,δlnρambient​=(1−∇)δlnP.
(1)
The density excess of the parcel at its new location is therefore
δlnρparcel​−δlnρambient​=[∇−γγ−1​]δlnP.
(2)
If ∇>(γ−1)/γ, this excess is negative: the outward-displaced parcel is lighter and accelerates farther outward. A downward displacement similarly continues downward. Conversely, a smaller gradient gives a restoring buoyancy force. The Schwarzschild criterion is thus stability for ∇<∇ad​ and instability for ∇>∇ad​, where the adiabatic temperature gradient is ∇ad​=(γ−1)/γ. For a monatomic gas,
γ=5/3⟹∇ad​=2/5.​
(3)
A composition gradient would require the additional density effect in the Ledoux criterion; it is absent in this calculation.
Opacity and pressure. The supplied opacity is characteristic of a cool hydrogen-rich stellar atmosphere dominated by negative hydrogen ion opacity. Mostly neutral hydrogen can attach electrons supplied by partially ionized metals; bound-free and free-free absorption involving the resulting H− ions produces a steep positive temperature dependence. The weak density dependence and strong temperature sensitivity can be represented by local power-law fits. At fixed composition the given pressure form is equivalent to κ∝ρ1/2T17/2, because P∝ρT. This is an approximate fit over the relevant cool-atmosphere regime, not an exact opacity law at all temperatures; molecular opacity or hydrogen ionization eventually changes the regime.
For a geometrically thin atmosphere take the surface gravity of a star to be constant, gs​=GM/R2. Combining dP/dr=−ρgs​ with dτ/dr=−κρ gives hydrostatic equilibrium in optical depth,
dτdP​=κ0​P1/2T8gs​​.
(4)
The grey atmosphere relation gives T8=Te8​(3τ+2)2/16. With negligible external pressure, P(0)=0, integration yields
P3/2(τ)​=κ0​Te8​24gs​​∫0τ​(3u+2)2du​=κ0​R2Te8​8GM​(21​−3τ+21​).​
(5)
An imposed nonzero external pressure would add P(0)3/2; the boundary assumption is what fixes the integration constant here.
The factor in parentheses is 3τ/[2(3τ+2)]. Hence
dτdlnT​=4(3τ+2)3​,dτdlnP​=3τ(3τ+2)4​,∇rad​=169τ​.
(6)
The grey-atmosphere convection onset with eighth-power temperature opacity occurs when this reaches 2/5, so
τc​=4532​.​
(7)
The radiative continuation below this boundary would be unstable. Efficient convection instead keeps the deeper star approximately adiabatic.

T=Rμ​K3/5P2/5,K∝T5/3P−2/3
