Let μ be the mutation rate per chromosomal segment per generation, and use the population-scaled parameter θ=4Ne​μ. In 2Ne​-generation coalescent units, mutations occur on each ancestral lineage at rate θ/2. Independent mutations on all branches of a given phylogenetic tree superpose to give a Poisson distribution with mean θL/2.
Under the infinite sites mutation model, each mutation occurs at a distinct position, and every mutation below the sample's common ancestor separates a nonempty proper subset of sampled chromosomes from the remainder. Thus the mutation count is exactly the segregating site count, giving
S∣L,θ∼Poisson(2θL​).​
(26)
By the law of total expectation and the previous branch-length formula,
E[S∣θ]=E[2θL​]=θan​.​
(27)
This also gives the Watterson estimator S/an​ as an unbiased moment estimator of θ. Here all sample segregating sites are counted, including singletons; filtering sites by a minimum sample frequency or by external SNP discovery would introduce genetic-study ascertainment and change this calculation.
