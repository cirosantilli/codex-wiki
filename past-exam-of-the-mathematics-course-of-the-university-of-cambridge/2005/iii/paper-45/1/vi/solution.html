Put N=n0​+n1​+n2​ and s=2x=2θ(1−θ). The usual recombination fraction range 0≤θ≤1/2 gives 0≤s≤1/2, and the preceding likelihood function becomes
L(s)∝s2n0​+n1​(1−s)2n2​+n1​.​
(12)
To see its relation to nonparametric linkage analysis, use Bayes' theorem and the constant disease probability 1/16 to obtain the affected-pair marker-sharing probabilities
(z0​,z1​,z2​)=(1/16L0​/4​,1/16L1​/2​,1/16L2​/4​)=(s2,2s(1−s),(1−s)2).
(13)
Their multinomial likelihood has exactly the same s-dependent kernel, since its extra factor 2n1​ is constant. Set A=2n0​+n1​ and B=2n2​+n1​. Then A+B=2N, and B is the total number of shared ancestral copies. At fixed N, the Fisher-Neyman factorization theorem makes B a sufficient statistic. The observed mean shared proportion is p​=B/(2N), and the binomial likelihood kernel gives
s=min(2NA​,21​),θ=21−1−2s​​.​
(14)
The unlinked null is s=1/2, whereas linkage corresponds to s<1/2, or expected shared proportion 1−s>1/2. If p​≤1/2, the constrained likelihood is maximized at the unlinked null. For p​>1/2, its log likelihood ratio is
2N{p​log(2p​)+(1−p​)log(2(1−p​))},
(15)
whose derivative in p​ is 2Nlog[p​/(1−p​)]>0. Hence it orders samples exactly by excess IBD sharing. Under this fully penetrant recessive model, parametric linkage testing and a one-sided test of the overall IBD-sharing proportion use the same information. This is allele-sharing sufficiency for recessive linkage, not a general equivalence for all disease models. Use limiting values for terms such as 0log0, and assume N>0 for the estimators.
