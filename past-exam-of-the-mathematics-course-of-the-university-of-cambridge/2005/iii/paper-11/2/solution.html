Use the equitable form of the Szemerédi regularity lemma. For 0<ε<1/2 and a prescribed lower bound m0​, there are M,n0​ such that every sufficiently large graph admits a set partition
V(G)=V0​⊔V1​⊔⋯⊔Vk​,m0​≤k≤M,∣V0​∣≤εn,∣V1​∣=⋯=∣Vk​∣,
(11)
with at most εk2 unordered irregular pairs among the nonexceptional cells. A uniform pair here means a regular pair of vertex sets. Larger values of ε follow from a smaller parameter, so the stated restriction loses nothing.
For the proof, retain the exceptional vertices as singleton cells in an auxiliary set partition P. Define its regularity energy by the ordered sum
q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∣A∣∣B∣d(A,B)2.
(12)
For diagonal rectangles the adjacency matrix of a graph defines d(A,A); diagonal terms are included. Thus 0≤q≤1. If A×B is split into rectangles of densities duv​ and sizes wuv​, their weighted mean is d=d(A,B). Expanding squares proves the refinement variance identity for regularity energy:
∑u,v​wuv​duv2​−∣A∣∣B∣d2=∑u,v​wuv​(duv​−d)2≥0.
(13)
Every refinement therefore increases or preserves energy.
Suppose equal cells Vi​,Vj​ of size m are ε-irregular. Choose witnesses X⊆Vi​, Y⊆Vj​ of sizes at least εm, whose density differs from d(Vi​,Vj​) by more than ε. Refine the cells so that these witnesses are unions of refined atoms. Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality inside X×Y gives an increase, for this oriented rectangle, greater than
n2∣X∣∣Y∣​∣d(X,Y)−d(Vi​,Vj​)∣2≥n2ε4m2​.
(14)
Choose witnesses simultaneously for every irregular pair. Each original cell is cut by at most k−1 witness sets and has at most 2k atoms. If more than εk2 unordered pairs are irregular, both orientations contribute, giving
Δq>2ε5(nkm​)2≥2ε5​,
(15)
as long as the exceptional set has at most n/2 vertices.
To restore equal sizes without sacrificing this increase, use equalization with a controlled exceptional set. Cut every witness atom into blocks of size ℓ=⌊m/4k⌋, leaving fewer than ℓ unused vertices in each atom. Make each unused vertex a singleton exceptional cell. This is a further refinement, so there is no energy loss. The number newly exceptional is at most
k2kℓ≤km2−k≤n2−k.
(16)
When m≥2⋅4k, the new nonexceptional cell count k′ is at most 4k4k: indeed ℓ≥m/(2⋅4k) and n/m≤2k. It is at least k, since each old cell supplies at least 4k−2k≥1 blocks.
Let L=⌈2ε−5⌉+1. Choose the initial count k0​≥m0​ so large that L2−k0​≤ε/2, and begin with k0​ equal cells and fewer than k0​ leftover vertices. Take n0​ large enough that these initial leftovers are at most εn/2. Iterate the bound k↦4k4k for L rounds to obtain a finite number M. Increase n0​ further to ensure n≥4M4M. Then every cell size encountered satisfies m≥n/(2M)≥2⋅4k, so every equalization is legitimate. The total exception budget is at most εn, because the initial losses use half the budget and all later losses sum to at most Ln2−k0​. Energy cannot increase by more than one, so L failing rounds are impossible. The process must stop with at most εk2 irregular pairs. This proves the Szemerédi regularity lemma, including the bounded cell count, equitability and exceptional-set control.
For the dense-pair application, apply this proof with η=1/100 and m0​=100. Suppose every η-regular pair of nonexceptional cells has density at most 1/4. Edges touching V0​ number at most ηn2. Edges inside nonexceptional cells number at most km2/2≤n2/(2m0​). The at most ηk2 irregular cell pairs contribute at most ηn2. Finally the regular cross-cell pairs contribute at most k(k−1)m2/8≤n2/8. Therefore
e(G)≤(81​+2η+2m0​1​)n2=203​n2<61​n2,

a contradiction. Some regular pair (U,W) consequently has density greater than 1/4. It is also a 1/8-uniform pair, since subsets of relative size at least 1/8 qualify in the stronger η-regularity condition. The common cell size satisfies m≥(1−η)n/M≥n/(2M). Thus the dense regular pair from edge surplus gives
∣U∣=∣W∣≥cn,d(U,W)>1/4,(U,W) is 1/8-uniform.​

This first proves the assertion for n≥n0​. For the finitely many smaller possible orders, choose the endpoints of any edge as singleton sets: the pair has density one and is regular for every positive parameter. Taking c=min{1/(2M),1/n0​}>0 includes these cases as well.
