Write Kj​(t) for a balanced complete multipartite blow-up, with j parts each containing t vertices. Its occurrence is a subgraph condition, so additional internal edges in the host are harmless. The coefficient multiplying logn is essential: the PDF has t=⌊dlogn⌋, although it is lost in the converted TeX.
First prove the common neighbourhood from bipartite density estimate. Suppose a bipartite graph has parts of sizes m,N and at least βmN edges. For an integer s≤βm/2, count incidences between s-subsets of the first part and their common neighbours in the second. The discrete convexity of k↦(sk​), with value zero for k<s, gives

We prove the logarithmic clique blow-up from minimum degree by induction on r. When ε≥1/r, the degree hypothesis is impossible for a loopless graph, so any positive d works vacuously. Assume 0<ε<1/r. For r=1, take two copies of the vertex set and join the copies of adjacent vertices. This bipartite graph has edge density of a bipartite graph at least ε. Choose
d(1,ε)≤2log(2/ε)1​.

For large n, m≥d′logn/2 and t=⌊dlogn⌋≤βm/2. The estimate gives t selected cliques and at least n​≥t common vertices. The selected cliques supply t vertices in each of the original r parts, and their common vertices supply the final part. No common vertex belongs to a selected clique, again because there are no loops. All required cross-part edges are present. The Erdős-Stone theorem states that for a fixed graph H with chromatic number r+1≥2,
ex(n,H)=(1−r1​+o(1))(2n​).​

The lower bound comes from the Turán graph Tr​(n), which cannot contain an (r+1)-chromatic subgraph. To obtain the upper bound from our minimum-degree result, suppose e(G)≥(a+η)n2/2, where a=1−1/r and 0<η<1−a. Repeatedly delete a vertex of degree less than b times the current order, where b=a+η/2, until no such vertex remains. If the remaining order is m, then
e(G)≤2b​(n2−m2+n)+2m2​,(1−b)m2≥2η​n2−bn.

Thus m≥cη​n for large n, with cη​>0, and the remaining subgraph has minimum degree of a graph at least (a+η/2)m. It contains Kr+1​(⌊dlogm⌋). A proper graph colouring of H places its vertices in the r+1 parts once their size exceeds ∣V(H)∣. This contradicts H-freeness. Adjusting a fixed positive density margin absorbs the difference between n2/2 and (2n​), proving the upper bound.
The six-vertex forbidden graph is the octahedral graph K2,2,2​: the deleted matching in a graph gives its three independent pairs. Its chromatic number is three, so
n→∞lim​(2n​)ex(n,F)​=21​.​

The printed notation uses H for the extremal-number argument while its defining condition forbids F; the answer uses that explicitly defined forbidden graph. For the strict finite inequality, take T2​(n) and add one edge inside its larger part, which has at least two vertices for n≥3. Every triangle in a graph then uses this added edge. However, K2,2,2​ contains two vertex-disjoint triangles in a graph, obtained by taking one vertex from each pair and then the other three. It therefore cannot occur in the constructed graph. Hence
