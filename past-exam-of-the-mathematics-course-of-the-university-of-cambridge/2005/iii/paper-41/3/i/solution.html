Expanding the ordinary least squares objective gives
Q(β)=YTY−2βTXTY+βTXTXβ,∇Q=2XT(Xβ−Y),∇2Q=2XTX.
(12)
For a nonzero vector v, vTXTXv=∥Xv∥2>0, since the design matrix has matrix rank p. The Hessian matrix is therefore positive-definite, and Q is strictly convex. Solving its normal equations gives the unique least-squares estimator:
β​=(XTX)−1XTY.​
(13)
Indeed, expansion about this stationary point and XT(Y−Xβ​)=0 give Q(β)=Q(β​)+(β−β​)TXTX(β−β​), which also proves the global minimum directly.
Define the hat matrix H=X(XTX)−1XT. It is a symmetric matrix and an idempotent linear map, since HT=H and H2=H, and its image is the column space of X. Thus H is the orthogonal projection onto the fitted mean space. The fitted values are HY and the regression residual vector is (I−H)Y. Because I−H is also a symmetric matrix and an idempotent linear map,
Q(β​)=YT(I−H)T(I−H)Y=YT(I−H)Y.​
(14)
These minimization and projection identities do not need errors with a normal distribution; the normal linear model becomes relevant to the distributional calculations.
