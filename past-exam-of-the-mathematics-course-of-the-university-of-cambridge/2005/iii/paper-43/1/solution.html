Condition on the claim count, defining the empty sum to be zero. Independence gives E[S∣N=k]=kEX1​. By the law of total expectation,
ES=ENEX1​.​
(1)
For nonnegative claims this also follows from Tonelli theorem; for signed claims finite EN and E∣X1​∣ ensure integrability. Conditional independence likewise gives
E[etS∣N=k]=∏j=1k​E[etXj​]=MX1​​(t)k,MS​(t)=GN​(MX1​​(t)).​
(2)
This random-sum transform identity holds wherever the composed series is finite, or as an extended nonnegative expectation. It is not permission to use an algebraic continuation outside the convergence domain.
For the zero-based geometric distribution, summing the geometric series gives
GN​(z)=1−qzp​,EN=GN′​(1)=pq​.
(3)
The exponential distribution of mean μ has MX1​​(t)=(1−μt)−1 for t<1/μ. Therefore
ES=pqμ​,MS​(t)=1−q/(1−μt)p​=p−μtp(1−μt)​(t<p/μ).​
(4)
The stricter bound t<p/μ is necessary: the geometric series requires qMX1​​(t)<1. At and above p/μ that series diverges; at and above 1/μ even one claim has an infinite transform. Thus the displayed rational expression represents the moment-generating function only on its stated domain.
Since all claims are strictly positive, S=0 exactly when N=0, so P(S=0)=p. Splitting the transform gives
MS​(t)=p+qp/μ−tp/μ​.
(5)
Uniqueness of the moment-generating function identifies a mixture distribution with a zero atom of weight p and an exponential positive component of weight q and rate p/μ. In particular,
P(S=0)=p,fS​(x)=μqp​e−px/μ(x>0).​
(6)
The density integrates to q, not one. One can also verify it directly: conditional on N=k≥1, the sum has Erlang distribution density xk−1e−x/μ/[μk(k−1)!]. Multiplying by pqk and summing over k gives (pq/μ)e−x/μeqx/μ, the same density. This is the zero-based geometric sum of exponential variables.
Under excess of loss reinsurance, the ceded amount for each claim is (Xj​−M)+​, and the monthly reduction is their random sum. The tail integral formula for moments gives
E(X1​−M)+​=∫M∞​P(X1​>x)dx=μe−M/μ.
(7)
Conditioning on N again yields
Ej=1∑N​(Xj​−M)+​=pqμ​e−M/μ.​
(8)
The retained payment is ∑j=1N​min(Xj​,M); it is generally not min(S,M).
Under aggregate stop loss reinsurance, the reduction is (S−M)+​. The aggregate tail is P(S>x)=qe−px/μ for x≥0, so
E(S−M)+​=∫M∞​qe−px/μdx=pqμ​e−pM/μ.
(9)
The prefactor is positive. Equality with the per-claim recovery is equivalent to pM=M, giving the unique reinsurance retention
M=pM​.​
(10)
This is the relation for equal expected recoveries from excess of loss and stop loss; since p<1, the aggregate reinsurance retention exceeds the individual reinsurance retention. These are reductions in claim payouts, before any reinsurance premium is deducted.
