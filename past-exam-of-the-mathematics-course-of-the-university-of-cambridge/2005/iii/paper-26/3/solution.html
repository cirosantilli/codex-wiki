For a locally small category C, an object A, and a functor F:C→Set, the covariant form of the Yoneda lemma is the natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A),α⟼αA​(1A​).
(7)
For x∈F(A) define αBx​(f)=F(f)(x), where f:A→B. For u:B→D, functoriality gives F(u)αBx​(f)=F(uf)(x)=αDx​(uf), so this is a natural transformation. Conversely, naturality of α at f:A→B gives αB​(f)=F(f)αA​(1A​). The two constructions are inverse. They are natural in F, because a natural transformation F⇒G commutes with evaluation. They are also natural in A: an arrow v:A′→A induces C(A,−)→C(A′,−) by precomposition, and the resulting map Nat(C(A′,−),F)→Nat(C(A,−),F) corresponds to F(v):F(A′)→F(A). Applying the result to the opposite category gives Nat(C(−,A),H)≅H(A) for H:Cop→Set.
Now let C be a small category, and let F,G:C→Set. Finite products in a category in [C,Set] exist pointwise; the terminal functor is the constant singleton functor. Define the exponential of covariant set-valued functors by
(GF)(C)=Nat(C(C,−)×F,G).
(8)
Smallness ensures that these natural transformations form a set. For u:C→D, define (GF)(u)(α) at E by
((GF)(u)(α))E​(v,x)=αE​(vu,x),v:D→E.
(9)
Precomposition respects identities and composition, so this defines a functor. Its evaluation map of an exponential object is
evC​:(GF)(C)×F(C)→G(C),(α,x)↦αC​(1C​,x).
(10)
To check naturality, for u:C→D both sides give αD​(u,F(u)x): one side uses naturality of α, and the other uses the defining action of GF.
Here is the full universal property. Given θ:H×F⇒G, define θ:H⇒GF by
(θC​(h))D​(v,x)=θD​(H(v)h,x),v:C→D.
(11)
For w:D→E, naturality of θ makes this commute with G(w), so θC​(h) is a natural transformation. Replacing h by H(u)h is the same as precomposing v by u, proving naturality in C. Conversely, β:H⇒GF gives θC​(h,x)=βC​(h)C​(1C​,x). Starting with θ recovers it immediately. Starting with β, its naturality at v:C→D yields
βD​(H(v)h)D​(1D​,x)=βC​(h)D​(v,x),
(12)
so it too is recovered. The formulas are natural in H and G, giving the required adjunction (−)×F⊣(−)F. Therefore the covariant functor category is cartesian closed. This proof supplies the actual exponential and does not need a colimit presentation of its arguments.
Finally suppose C itself is a small category which is a Cartesian closed category. In its presheaf category, the same construction, applied to Cop, gives
((YB)YA)(C)​=Nat(YC×YA,YB)≅Nat(Y(C×A),YB)≅C(C×A,B)≅C(C,BA)=Y(BA)(C).​
(13)
The first isomorphism uses the product's universal property: a map into C×A is precisely a pair of maps into C and A. The next is the Yoneda lemma, and the last is the exponential adjunction in C. Each step is natural in C, A, and B. Under these identifications, evaluation corresponds to Y of the evaluation BA×A→B. Hence the Yoneda embedding preserves exponentials, with the natural identification Y(BA)≅(YB)YA​.
