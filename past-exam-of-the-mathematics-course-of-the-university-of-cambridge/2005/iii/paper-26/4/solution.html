For an adjunction L:C⇄D:R, write its adjunction unit as η and its adjunction counit as ε. It is an idempotent adjunction when the induced monad has invertible multiplication μ=RεL:RLRL⇒RL. The dual condition is invertibility of the induced comonad comultiplication δ=LηR:LR⇒LRLR.
To prove equivalence of these conditions, suppose μ is invertible. The two unit identities imply Tη=ηT=μ−1, where T=RL. For any algebra for a monad a:TA→A, naturality gives
ηA​a=TaηTA​=TaTηA​=T(aηA​)=1TA​,
(14)
while aηA​=1A​. Thus ηA​ is invertible. For each D∈D, the arrow RεD​:T(RD)→RD is a monad algebra action, so ηRD​ is invertible. Applying L proves δD​=LηRD​ invertible. Apply this implication to the opposite adjunction, interchanging the roles of the monad and comonad, to obtain the converse. Therefore idempotence is self-dual.
The open-set frame O(X) is ordered by inclusion. Its arbitrary joins are unions; arbitrary meets are interiors of intersections. Finite intersections are already open, so finite meets are ordinary intersections, including the empty meet X. Distributivity of intersection over arbitrary unions proves the frame law. If f:X→Y is a continuous map, inverse image
f−1:O(Y)→O(X)
(15)
preserves arbitrary unions and finite intersections, including ∅ and the entire space. It is therefore a frame homomorphism. Since (gf)−1=f−1g−1 and 1X−1​=1O(X)​, this defines O:Top→Frmop.
Let 2={0<1} and let P(A) be the set of points of a frame A, namely frame homomorphisms A→2. For a∈A, put Ua​={p:p(a)=1}. Preservation of joins and finite meets gives
U0​=∅,U1​=P(A),Ua∧b​=Ua​∩Ub​,U⋁i​ai​​=⋃i​Uai​​.

In the last equality, a join in 2 is 1 precisely when at least one summand is 1, also covering the empty family. Thus the sets Ua​ are all the opens of a topology, rather than merely a proposed basis.
For a frame homomorphism h:A→B, define P(h):P(B)→P(A) by p↦p∘h. Since P(h)−1(Ua​)=Uh(a)​, it is continuous. Composition and identities follow from composition of functions, giving P:Frmop→Top.
The frame-point adjunction is the natural bijection
Top(X,P(A))≅Frm(A,O(X))=Frmop(O(X),A).
(17)
A continuous map f:X→P(A) determines hf​(a)=f−1(Ua​); the displayed identities for Ua​ show that hf​ is a frame homomorphism. Conversely a frame homomorphism h:A→O(X) determines fh​(x)(a)=1 exactly when x∈h(a). Membership in unions and finite intersections proves that fh​(x) is a frame homomorphism to 2, and fh−1​(Ua​)=h(a) proves continuity. These constructions are inverse. Precomposing in X becomes inverse image of open sets; precomposing a frame homomorphism becomes composition of frame points. Hence the bijection is natural in both arguments and proves O⊣P.
The adjunction unit is
ηX​:X→P(O(X)),ηX​(x)(V)=1 ⟺ x∈V.

The multiplication of the induced monad PO at X is P(eO(X)​), which is invertible by this calculation. Consequently the frame-point adjunction is idempotent. This does not assert that every ηX​ is a homeomorphism: it says that applying the point-space construction a second time makes no further change.
