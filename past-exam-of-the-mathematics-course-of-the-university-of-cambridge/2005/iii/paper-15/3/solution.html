An orthogonal structure on a real vector bundle E of rank r is a choice of local frames whose transition matrices lie in the orthogonal group O(r). In such frames declare
h(∑i​ai​ei​,∑i​bi​ei​)=∑i​ai​bi​.
(13)
Orthogonal transitions preserve this expression, so it defines a smooth positive-definite fiber metric. Conversely, a fiber metric turns any local frame into a smooth orthonormal frame by the Gram-Schmidt process; the resulting transition matrices are orthogonal. The two constructions are inverse, giving the required equivalence.
An orthogonal local trivialization E∣U​≅U×Rr identifies each fiber isometrically with standard Euclidean space. Equivalently, its coordinate frame satisfies h(ei​,ej​)=δij​. This is local and does not assert the existence of a global frame.
Every real vector bundle over a smooth manifold admits a fiber metric. Choose a trivializing open cover and local Euclidean metrics hi​. The partition of unity theorem for a Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold supplies nonnegative smooth functions ρi​, with locally finite supports contained in the respective cover members, such that ∑i​ρi​=1. Set
h=∑i​ρi​hi​,
(14)
extending each weighted metric by zero off its chart. Local finiteness makes this smooth. For every nonzero fiber vector v, at least one positive weight gives h(v,v)=∑i​ρi​hi​(v,v)>0. Thus h is a fiber metric and provides an orthogonal structure.
One definition of an orthogonal connection is that its covariant derivative preserves the fiber metric:
X(h(s,t))=h(∇X​s,t)+h(s,∇X​t)
(15)
for all vector fields X and smooth sections of a vector bundle s,t. Another is that its parallel transport along every smooth curve is an isometry between the endpoint fibers.
For the first implication, take parallel sections s,t along a curve. Their covariant derivatives vanish, so the displayed identity makes h(s,t) constant: parallel transport preserves a fibre metric. Conversely, if parallel transport preserves the inner product, choose parallel extensions of arbitrary initial fiber vectors along a curve with prescribed initial tangent X. Differentiating their constant inner product gives (∇X​h)(s,t)=0 at the initial point. Since X,s,t were arbitrary, the connection is metric-compatible.
The equivalent local description is that the connection matrix in an orthonormal frame is skew-symmetric. Writing ∇ej​=∑i​Aij​ei​ in an orthogonal trivialization, differentiate h(ei​,ej​)=δij​ to obtain
Aij​+Aji​=0,AT=−A​.
(16)
Conversely this matrix identity gives the metric-preservation formula by expanding arbitrary sections. It is the orthogonal group connection description of the same condition.
