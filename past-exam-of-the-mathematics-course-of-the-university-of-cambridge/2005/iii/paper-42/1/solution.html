The profile likelihood for the parameter of interest replaces the nuisance parameter by its constrained best fit: Lp​(ψ)=supλ​L(ψ,λ), with ℓp​(ψ)=ℓ(ψ,λψ​). It is not in general a normalized sampling density. A conditional likelihood instead uses the sampling law conditional on a statistic carrying the nuisance dependence. In the canonical exponential family here, conditioning on S removes λ because the factor involving λ is constant over the conditioning fibre.
Let Tˉ1​=n−1∑i​τ1​(yi​) and let the observed S equal s. For the cumulant-generating function of τ2​(Y) at fixed ψ,
K(t)=d(ψ,λ+t)−d(ψ,λ).
(1)
The saddlepoint solves K′(t)=s, equivalently dλ​(ψ,λψ​)=s with λψ​=λ+t. This is also the constrained likelihood equation, since ℓλ​=n(s−dλ​). Assume an interior saddle, dλλ​>0, and the usual smooth nonlattice conditions under which S has a density. Exponential tilting to λψ​ makes s the tilted mean. A local normal approximation gives tilted density {n/(2πdλλ​(ψ,λψ​))}1/2 at that mean; undoing the tilt gives the saddlepoint density approximation
f​S​(s;ψ,λ)=[2πdλλ​(ψ,λψ​)n​]1/2exp{n[d(ψ,λψ​)−d(ψ,λ)−(λψ​−λ)s]}.​
(2)
The factor n1/2 is for the sample mean, rather than the sum. A transform-based derivation gives the same expression by expanding K to second order at its stationary point.
The joint log-likelihood is nψTˉ1​+nλs−nd(ψ,λ)−∑i​Q(yi​). Subtracting logf​S​(s;ψ,λ) cancels every appearance of the original λ, leaving
ℓc​(ψ)=nψTˉ1​+nλψ​s−nd(ψ,λψ​)−∑i​Q(yi​)+21​logdλλ​(ψ,λψ​)−21​log2πn​.
(3)
Any change from the ambient density to a conditional density on S=s contributes only a data-dependent Jacobian determinant. Thus, up to a constant independent of ψ, the saddlepoint conditional likelihood adjustment is
ℓc​(ψ)=ℓp​(ψ)+B(ψ),B(ψ)=21​logdλλ​(ψ,λψ​).​
(4)
Equivalently B=21​logjλλ​(ψ,λψ​) up to the constant −21​logn, where jλλ​=ndλλ​ is the nuisance observed information. The adjustment has a positive sign. The printed exponential-family form alone does not guarantee a density for S: for example a continuous Y can have τ2​(Y)=1{Y>0}​. In a lattice case one must approximate the probability mass of the sum instead; the displayed density calculation uses the regular continuous interpretation.
