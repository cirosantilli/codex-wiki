An Edgeworth expansion refines the central limit theorem by incorporating higher cumulants. For iid centred observations with variance σ2, standardized sum Zn​=∑i​Xi​/(σn​), standardized third cumulant γ1​=κ3​/σ3 and fourth cumulant γ2​=κ4​/σ4, its central-region density expansion is
fZn​​(z)=ϕ(z)[1+6n​γ1​​H3​(z)+n1​{24γ2​​H4​(z)+72γ12​​H6​(z)}]+o(n−1),
(30)
under sufficient moment and smoothness/nonlattice conditions. Here H3​=z3−3z, H4​=z4−6z2+3, H6​=z6−15z4+45z2−15 are probabilists' Hermite polynomials. Expanding the characteristic exponent gives the cubic and quartic terms, while squaring the cubic term in the exponential gives the H6​ correction. Integrating the first correction, using (ϕHk−1​)′=−ϕHk​, gives
P(Zn​≤z)=Φ(z)+6n​γ1​​(1−z2)ϕ(z)+O(n−1).​
(31)
Thus skewness determines the first departure from the normal approximation; inversion gives a Cornish-Fisher expansion for quantiles. These are asymptotic expansions, not guaranteed nonnegative densities in extreme tails. Lattice data require continuity/lattice corrections, and moment assumptions alone do not justify a uniform smooth-density expansion.
