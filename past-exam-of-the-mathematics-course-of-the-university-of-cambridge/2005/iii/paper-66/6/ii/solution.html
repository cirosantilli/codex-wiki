The corresponding subdivision surface interrogation needs mesh connectivity and patch adjacency, boundary and crease flags, local coordinate charts with transition maps, and subdivision into smaller patches with their original-chart mappings. Its evaluation enquiry returns a limit point, tangent derivatives and a normal vector; second derivatives are supplied only where the limit is sufficiently smooth. Certified bounding volumes and patch approximation errors allow a hierarchy for intersection and proximity searches. Boundary-curve enquiries complete the interface for clipping.
On regular parts of a subdivision surface, these evaluations may use its equivalent tensor-product or box spline patch. Near an extraordinary subdivision vertex, the local subdivision matrix determines the limit position through its constant left eigenvector, and its tangent modes determine the normal vector under the scheme's characteristic map of a subdivision surface regularity conditions. Refinement alone does not establish those conditions. A subdivision surface may have a well-defined tangent plane there without possessing second derivatives or Gaussian curvature. An interrogation interface must distinguish these cases and can work on surrounding regular charts when a higher derivative is unavailable at the vertex itself.
Together, subdivision, bounds, topology and limit evaluation support ray–subdivision surface intersection: bounds eliminate irrelevant patches; surviving patches are refined, and regular candidate intersections are corrected using position and first derivatives. The returned intersection is a point of the limit subdivision surface with an error certificate, rather than automatically a point on the last control mesh.
