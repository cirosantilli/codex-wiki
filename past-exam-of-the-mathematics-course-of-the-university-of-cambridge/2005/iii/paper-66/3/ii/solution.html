There are no numerical continuity bounds determined by w and a alone: the subdivision mask coefficients matter. First check constant reproduction, ∑j​Mϵ−aj​=1 for every residue ϵ, and convergence. For the usual stationary scalar scheme, two useful calculations are a uniform contraction test and a spectral obstruction test. Higher smoothness is tested with derived difference subdivision masks.

and test maxϵmodap​∑j​∣(Br[p]​)ϵ−apj​∣<1. This proves convergence of the constant-preserving derivative scheme to a continuous limit. One justification is to compare consecutive piecewise-linear interpolants: their difference is bounded by a constant times the maximum adjacent-control difference, and the geometrically decreasing bound is summable. To identify the limit as P(r), use fixed-degree Cardinal B-spline interpolants of the original refined controls. Their rth derivatives are sums of the scaled finite differences; convergence of both functions and derivatives gives P∈Cr. Testing successive r supplies a guaranteed lower bound.
An upper continuity bound comes from an observable noncontracting shape mode. Build finite local subdivision matrices for Br​ on a refinement-invariant control neighborhood. For a digit ϵ their entries are (Aϵ​)ij​=(Br​)ϵ+i−aj​, since the new index is ak+ϵ+i. Restrict to the realizable difference space and discard modes that do not affect the limit. A word ϵ1​,…,ϵp​ corresponds to a nested parameter location. If its product has an observable eigenvalue λ with ∣λ∣>1, repetition of this word makes some scaled derivative differences grow rather than tend to zero. For a stable limit representation, Cr regularity would force these differences to vanish; hence general-position data cannot have Cr continuity. A modulus-one mode requires examination of whether it actually persists, rather than an automatic rounding rule.
The two calculations can be organized through the joint spectral radius ρ​ of these restricted matrices:

These norm and spectral bounds for subdivision regularity make the test computable from the subdivision mask. The right bound certifies all-word contraction when it is less than one; the left bound detects a repeated-word obstruction when it exceeds one. Longer products improve the tests. A single matrix's spectrum is not a substitute for controlling arbitrary products. The product estimates also appear in Charina's analysis of subdivision regularity; the strict contraction and observable-mode arguments above explain their respective roles.
