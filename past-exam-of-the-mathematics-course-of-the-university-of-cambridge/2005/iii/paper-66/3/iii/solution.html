A norm estimate controls the worst possible refined differences for every choice of the initial controls. Submultiplicativity turns a length-p contraction into a bound for arbitrary long products, so it proves a certain number of continuous derivatives. Its absolute values can lose cancellations; failure of the estimate is inconclusive. It therefore gives a lower bound on smoothness.
Conversely, an observable eigenmode of a repeated local product exhibits a particular sequence of shrinking neighborhoods with inadequate decay. General-position controls have a nonzero coefficient in that mode, so greater derivative continuity is impossible. Special symmetric or polynomial data may suppress the bad mode; this is why the general-position qualification matters. The obstruction gives an upper bound on smoothness, while failure to find an obstruction proves nothing. A lower bound on spectral growth becomes an upper bound on smoothness because large surviving differences correspond to rough behavior.
For example, the binary Cardinal cubic B-spline subdivision mask has symbol M(z)=(1+z)4/8, up to an irrelevant index shift. At derivative order r=2, the first-difference subdivision mask is B2​=(1+z)/2, whose maximum parity sum is 1/2: the limit is at least C2. At r=3, B3​=1, and the surviving difference mode does not decay. For generic controls the third derivative is piecewise constant with nonzero jumps, so the subdivision curve is not C3. Here the lower and upper tests determine the exact integer continuity, but they need not coincide for an arbitrary subdivision mask. Stability, sum rules and borderline modes must be handled explicitly before interpreting any numerical estimate as a continuity level.
