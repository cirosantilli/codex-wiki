Hold the metric fixed when varying the gauge potential, put F=dA, and take variations with compact support or with appropriate fixed boundary data. The Hodge star operator then gives
δSkin​=−∫d(δA)∧∗F=−∫δA∧d∗F−∫d(δA∧∗F).
(5)
For the Abelian Chern-Simons 5-form, the two occurrences of F commute as even-degree differential forms. Since dF=0,
δ(A∧F∧F)=δA∧F∧F+2A∧d(δA)∧F=3δA∧F∧F−2d(A∧δA∧F).
(6)
The factor three comes from all three appearances of A, including those hidden in F=dA. The bulk variation is therefore
δS=∫δA∧(−d∗F+3cF∧F)+boundary terms,
(7)
and the Abelian Chern-Simons five-form field equation is
d∗F=3cF∧F,dF=0.​
(8)
Under an Abelian gauge transformation A↦A+dλ, the gauge field strength stays fixed and the action changes by
ΔS=c∫dλ∧F∧F=c∫d(λF∧F)=c∫∂M​λF∧F.
(9)
Thus the local integrand is not gauge invariant, but its change is a boundary term by the Generalized Stokes theorem. With the stated boundary conditions the variational bulk equations are unchanged; they depend only on F and the metric, so they are manifestly gauge invariant. On a spacetime with boundary, gauge transformations nonzero at the boundary require boundary conditions or boundary degrees of freedom; the bulk claim does not assert invariance of every possible boundary action.

The Chern-Simons 5-form uses only wedge products of differential forms and orientation, with no metric or Hodge star operator. Its bulk metric derivative is zero, so the Chern-Simons term contributes no bulk stress-energy tensor. The displayed tensor is generally not traceless in five dimensions: Tμμ​=−Fρσ​Fρσ/4.
