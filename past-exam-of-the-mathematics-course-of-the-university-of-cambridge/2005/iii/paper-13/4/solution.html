Let π:E→B be an oriented real vector bundle of rank r over a paracompact base. A Thom class is a class U∈Hr(E,E∖B;Z) restricting on every oriented fiber pair (Rr,Rr∖0) to its positive generator. The Thom isomorphism theorem states that this class exists uniquely with those fiber restrictions and that
Hq(B;Z) ≅ ​Hq+r(E,E∖B;Z),a⟼π∗a⌣U.​
(13)
Here B is identified with the zero section. Equivalently use the disk/sphere bundle pair. The compact-support version is Hcq​(B;Z)≅Hcq+r​(E;Z), with fiberwise compact support in the Thom construction. If the bundle is not oriented, the integral assertion requires the corresponding orientation local system.
For a closed co-oriented embedded submanifold Y of codimension r, co-orientation means an orientation of its normal bundle. A tubular neighborhood identifies a neighborhood of Y with that bundle. Its Thom class, transferred by excision, is a class in Hr(M,M∖Y;Z). For compact Y this maps into the direct limit defining compactly supported cohomology, giving εY​∈Hcr​(M;Z)​. The construction is independent of the tubular neighborhood and uses the chosen co-orientation. In an oriented ambient manifold it is the Poincare dual of the oriented submanifold. Here “closed submanifold” has the usual compact-without-boundary meaning; if it meant merely a closed subset and Y were noncompact, the supported relative class would still exist but need not have compact support.
We next prove the fixed-point implication without imposing an unprinted orientability assumption on M. Set
L(f)=∑k​(−1)ktr(f∗:Hk(M;Q)→Hk(M;Q)).
(14)
Use a finite triangulation of the closed smooth manifold. The elementary Hopf trace identity says that the alternating trace of a chain endomorphism equals its alternating trace on homology. To verify it, split each finite-dimensional chain space as boundaries, a complement representing homology, and a complement mapping isomorphically onto the preceding boundaries. The traces on these two boundary summands cancel in adjacent degrees, leaving exactly the homology traces. The cohomology traces equal the homology traces because the induced maps are dual over Q.
If f has no fixed points, compactness gives δ=minx​d(x,f(x))>0. Choose a triangulation K of mesh less than δ/4 and a sufficiently fine subdivision K′ admitting a simplicial approximation F:K′→K uniformly within δ/4 of f. The subdivision chain map s:C∗​(K)→C∗​(K′) sends each simplex to simplices inside it. For a vertex v of such a small simplex, d(v,F(v))>3δ/4, so F(v) cannot lie in the original coarse simplex. Consequently F#​s has zero diagonal coefficient on every coarse simplex. Its trace vanishes in every degree, while it induces f∗​ on homology. The trace identity yields L(f)=0. Taking the contrapositive proves
L(f)=0 ⟹ f has a fixed point.​
(15)
This is the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem, established here rather than invoked. For oriented M the same identity is the graph-diagonal formula for the Lefschetz number: L(f)=⟨(1,f)∗εΔ​,[M]⟩, which vanishes when the graph misses the diagonal. Finally, homotopic maps induce the same cohomology maps, so their Lefschetz numbers agree.
