For a based space (X,x0​) and k≥1, the homotopy group πk​(X,x0​) is the set of based homotopy classes of maps (Ik,∂Ik)→(X,x0​), equivalently based maps Sk→X. Concatenating along one coordinate defines its group operation; reversing that coordinate gives the inverse. The group is abelian for k≥2. The symbol π0​(X) records path components and is generally only a pointed set. An Eilenberg–MacLane space K(G,n) is a connected CW space with πn​=G and all other positive homotopy groups zero; for n>1 the group G must be abelian.
Projection onto coordinates gives
πk​(X×Y)≅πk​(X)×πk​(Y):
(17)
a map to a product is exactly a pair of maps, and a based homotopy to the product is exactly a pair of based homotopies. These identifications respect concatenation. Thus K(G,n)×K(H,n) has exactly the homotopy groups required of K(G×H,n). Taking CW models and using the allowed uniqueness of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces gives
K(G,n)×K(H,n)≃K(G×H,n).​
(18)
Let p be prime. Take S∞ in complex coordinates, with Cp​ acting by scalar multiplication by a primitive pth root of unity. This is a free action. Every map from a finite-dimensional sphere lands in a finite stage and becomes null-homotopic in a sufficiently higher sphere, so S∞ is weakly contractible; the Whitehead theorem for CW complexes makes it contractible. Therefore the infinite lens space S∞/Cp​ has contractible universal cover, fundamental group Cp​, and no higher homotopy: it is a model of K(Cp​,1).
Here is the group cohomology computation, including the ring structure. For the cyclic group generated by g, the standard free resolution alternates the maps g−1 and N=1+g+⋯+gp−1. Applying HomZ[Cp​]​(−,Fp​) makes both maps zero, so each cohomology degree is one-dimensional. The period-two identity chain map represents a class b in degree two, and cup multiplication by this periodicity class is an isomorphism from each degree to the degree two higher. For odd p, a nonzero degree-one class a has a2=0 by graded commutativity, while bj and abj are nonzero by periodicity. For p=2 the model is homotopy equivalent to RP∞, whose ring is the infinite version of that computed in question 2. Consequently the mod-p cohomology ring of a cyclic group is
H∗(K(Cp​,1);Fp​)={F2​[t],Λ(a)⊗Fp​[b],​p=2,∣t∣=1,p odd,∣a∣=1, ∣b∣=2.​​
(19)
The standard results used are the contractible-cover description of a K(G,1), its identification of singular cohomology with group cohomology, and the cyclic periodic resolution with cup-periodicity just stated.
The product equivalence and the assumed Künneth theorem over the field give
H∗(K(Cp​×Cp​,1);Fp​)={F2​[t1​,t2​],Λ(a1​,a2​)⊗Fp​[b1​,b2​],​p=2,p odd.​​
(20)
The tensor product is graded: for odd p, a1​a2​=−a2​a1​ and each ai2​=0. The dimensions in degree d are d+1: for p=2 count t1i​t2d−i​; for odd p the generating series is (1+t)2/(1−t2)2=(1−t)−2.
If an n-dimensional CW complex has exactly one cell in dimension n+1, then Cn+1(X;Fp​)=Fp​. Cellular cohomology is the quotient of the kernel of its outgoing coboundary by the image of the incoming one, a quotient of a subspace of this one-dimensional vector space. Thus
Hn+1(X;Fp​) is either 0 or Fp​.​
(21)
This one-cell bound on next-degree cohomology is valid even with infinitely many higher cells; the printed finite-per-dimension hypothesis is more than it needs.
Now a free finite-group action makes Sn→M=Sn/G a covering. For n>1 the sphere is simply connected, so π1​(M)=G, πi​(M)=0 for 1<i<n, and πn​(M)=Z. The covering also identifies every higher homotopy group with that of Sn. Use the homotopy-group effect of attaching higher cells: attaching (i+1)-cells, i≥2, preserves homotopy groups below i and quotients πi​ by the subgroup generated by the attaching classes and their fundamental-group translates. Attach a single (n+1)-cell along a generator of πn​(M); its translates do not require additional cells since the original generator already generates the underlying group. This kills πn​ without changing π1​ or the lower groups.
Successively in each higher degree attach cells representing generators of the remaining homotopy group. In the union every higher homotopy group vanishes, since a map and its relevant homotopy have compact domain and are handled at a finite stage. The result is a K(G,1) with exactly one (n+1)-cell. No finiteness assumption on the number of later cells is needed for the preceding cochain bound. If desired, one can retain finitely many per degree using the Serre finiteness theorem for homotopy groups: a simply connected CW complex of finite type has finitely generated homotopy groups. At each finite stage the universal cover is simply connected and finite type, since G is finite; choosing finitely many generators then preserves finite type inductively. The first stage needs just the stated πn​(Sn)=Z.
If G=Cp​×Cp​, the resulting K(G,1) would have dimHn+1≤1 by its single cell, but its already computed cohomology has dimension n+2>1 in that degree. This contradiction proves
G≅Cp​×Cp​for every prime p.​
(22)
This is the free sphere actions exclude elementary abelian subgroups of rank two obstruction.
