Let C∗​(X) be the integral singular chain complex. Augment it by the homomorphism C0​(X)→Z sending every point simplex to one. The homology of this augmented complex is reduced homology: for a nonempty space, it agrees with ordinary homology in positive degrees and H0​(X) is the kernel of H0​(X)→Z. The relative homology H∗​(X,A) is the homology of C∗​(X)/C∗​(A); a relative cycle has boundary in A, and chains differing by a boundary or a chain in A represent the same class.
Excision theorem: if U⊂A⊂X and U⊂intX​A, the inclusion (X∖U,A∖U)↪(X,A) induces isomorphisms on all relative homology groups. In particular, for a nonempty CW subcomplex A, choose a neighborhood N deforming onto A, with the deformation fixing A. Such neighborhoods exist for CW pairs. Then N/A is contractible. The long exact sequence of a pair gives H∗​(X,A)≅H∗​(X,N) and H∗​(X/A,{∗})≅H∗​(X/A,N/A). Excision removes A and the collapsed point respectively; the resulting pairs (X∖A,N∖A) and ((X/A)∖{∗},(N/A)∖{∗}) are homeomorphic. Consequently
H∗​(X,A;Z)≅H∗​(X/A;Z).​
(1)
Here the nonempty-subcomplex condition is the usual quotient convention in this statement. If A=∅, use the pointed cofiber X+​ with a disjoint basepoint; the literal unpointed quotient X/∅=X would not give the asserted degree-zero identity.
For one embedded circle, its long exact sequence of a pair reduces to
0⟶Z⟶H2​(Σg​,A)⟶Zi∗​​Z2g⟶H1​(Σg​,A)⟶0,H0​(Σg​,A)=0.
(2)
A separating circle bounds a subsurface, so its homology class is zero. A nonseparating circle has a transverse closed curve meeting it once, showing that its class is nonzero and primitive. Thus i∗​ is either zero or an injection with torsion-free cokernel. The resulting possibilities are
[A]=0[A]=0​H2​(Σg​,A)Z2Z​H1​(Σg​,A)Z2gZ2g−1​​​
(3)
with all other relative groups zero. These ranks determine whether the circle is null-homologous.
For g≥2, a disk boundary and a separating circle bounding a once-punctured torus have the same relative groups. The first is null-homotopic; the second is not. For a direct verification, use the surface-group presentation ⟨a1​,b1​,…,ag​,bg​∣∏i​[ai​,bi​]=1⟩. Send a1​,b1​ to free generators u,v, send a2​,b2​ to v,u, and all remaining generators to one. The relation maps to [u,v][v,u]=1, so this defines a homomorphism to the free group. The separating circle represents [a1​,b1​] and maps to the nonempty reduced word uvu−1v−1; it is therefore not null-homotopic. This proves the requested failure to detect nullhomotopy. There is a necessary low-genus qualification: on Σ0​ or Σ1​, an embedded circle is nullhomologous exactly when it is nullhomotopic. On the sphere every circle bounds a disk; on the torus every essential embedded circle is nonseparating. The printed nondetection assertion therefore needs g≥2.
For two disjoint embedded circles let r be the rank of their image in H1​(Σg​), and let c be the number of connected components of the cut surface. Removing open annular neighborhoods and applying excision gives the compact cut surface W relative to its boundary. Poincare-Lefschetz duality gives H2​(W,∂W)≅H0(W)=Zc and H1​(W,∂W)≅H1(W), which is free. Thus there is no hidden torsion. The long exact sequence gives c=3−r, and the Euler characteristic of the pair is 2−2g, giving
H2​(Σg​,A)=Z3−r,H1​(Σg​,A)=Z2g+1−r,Hj​=0 (j=1,2).​
(4)
The possibilities are r=0,1,2: two separating circles give zero; a nonseparating circle with a disk boundary, or two parallel essential circles, give one; circles around two different handles give two. For genus zero only r=0 occurs; for genus one r=0,1 occur; for every genus at least two all three occur. This is relative homology of a surface modulo disjoint circles.
