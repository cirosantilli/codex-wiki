A suitable large sieve statement is the following. If αj​ are separated modulo one by at least 0<δ≤1 and an​ is supported on N consecutive integers, then
∑j​∣∑n​an​e(nαj​)∣2≤(8N+δ−1)∑n​∣an​∣2.
(52)
The sharper classical constant N−1+δ−1 also suffices, but is unnecessary for the requested bound. This is the exponential-sum large sieve.
Put M=∣A∣, S(α)=∑n∈A​e(nα), and Ap​(r)=#{n∈A:n≡r(modp)}. By the orthogonality of roots of unity,

All fractions h/p, 1≤h<p, p≤X, are distinct reduced fractions. Their circular spacing is at least X−2, because two distinct fractions of these denominators differ modulo one by at least 1/(pp′). Apply the large sieve with an​=1A​(n) and δ=X−2, and sum the preceding lower bounds:
M2∑p≤X​pk(p)​≤∑p≤X​∑h=1p−1​∣S(h/p)∣2≤(8N+X2)M.
(55)

∣A∣≤(8N+X2)​p≤X∑​pk(p)​​−1.​
(56)
When the denominator is zero this asserts no restriction; that degenerate case is interpreted as a vacuous bound.
For the least-nonresidue application, first establish the positive density of power-smooth integers. Fix η=1/k, with an integer k≥2, and let H tend to infinity. Choose ordered primes, with repetitions allowed, in the interval
Hη−η2≤ℓ1​,…,ℓk​≤Hη.
(57)
Their product d satisfies H1−η≤d≤H. Every number md≤H has m≤Hη, so is Hη-smooth. By partial summation of the Prime number theorem, or the Mertens theorem for reciprocal primes,
∑Hη−η2≤ℓ≤Hη​ℓ1​⟶logη−η2η​=−log(1−η)>0.
(58)
The number of ordered representations (m,ℓ1​,…,ℓk​) is at least
2H​(∑Hη−η2≤ℓ≤Hη​ℓ1​)k≫η​H,
(59)
because ⌊H/d⌋≥H/(2d) for 1≤d≤H.
This representation count must not be confused with a count of distinct integers. A number at most H has at most ⌊1/(η−η2)⌋ prime factors in that interval, counted with multiplicity. There are therefore only a bounded number, depending on η, of ordered k-tuples which can occur as divisors of it; the cofactor is then fixed. Dividing by this bounded multiplicity proves
#{m≤H:m is Hη-smooth}≥κη​H
(60)
for a fixed κη​>0 and all sufficiently large H.
Now work on a dyadic prime interval Q<p≤2Q and let EQ​ be the primes there with n(p)>p0.01. Take H=Q2 and η=1/200, so Hη=Q0.01. Let A be the Q0.01-smooth integers at most Q2. The density lemma gives ∣A∣≥κQ2 for an absolute κ>0.
For each p∈EQ​, every prime factor of a member of A is at most Q0.01≤p0.01<n(p) and is smaller than p. Hence it is a nonzero quadratic residue modulo p. Multiplicativity of the Legendre symbol makes every member of A a nonzero quadratic residue modulo p. It therefore avoids all (p−1)/2 quadratic-nonresidue classes. In the sieve inequality set k(p)=(p−1)/2 at exceptional primes and zero at every other prime up to X=2Q. For odd p, k(p)/p≥1/3, so
3∣EQ​∣​≤∑p≤2Q​pk(p)​≤∣A∣8Q2+(2Q)2​≤κ12​.
(61)
Thus each dyadic interval has at most 36/κ exceptional primes. The finitely many small intervals can be absorbed into this absolute bound. Summing the O(logN) intervals up to N proves the large-sieve logarithmic exceptional-prime bound for least quadratic nonresidues:
#{p≤N:n(p)>p0.01}≤ClogN.​
(62)
The least nonresidue is normally defined for odd primes; any convention at the prime two changes the count by at most one.
