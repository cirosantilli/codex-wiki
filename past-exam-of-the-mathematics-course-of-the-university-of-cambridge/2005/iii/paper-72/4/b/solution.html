Spherical symmetry fixes the orbital plane and conserves the vector angular momentum, while the autonomous specific orbital energy is also conserved.

For a noncircular bound orbit 0<e<1; its specific orbital energy is E=μ2(e2−1)/[2(L2−2μa)]. The effective potential [L2−2μa]/(2r2)−μ/r has a centrifugal barrier and a minimum in this case. If L2−2μa≤0, the oscillator frequency is zero or imaginary and there is no regular finite-radius bound oscillation: the attractive centre lacks a centrifugal barrier. Unbound branches with e≥1 are not closed ellipselike trajectories.

An irrational ν advances the apsidal direction through a dense set of angles and gives a nonclosed rosette, with only the four regular spherical invariants needed to label its two-dimensional closure.
To see the additional resonant label explicitly, let uc​=μ/(L2−2μa) and w=u−uc​−iu′/ν. Then w=Ceiν(ψ−ψ0​), so on a family with fixed resonant L,
wqe−ipψ=Cqe−ipψ0​.​

Its argument fixes the remaining apsidal phase modulo the symmetry of the closed curve; its magnitude is already determined by specific orbital energy and L. Choosing reference axes in the fixed orbital plane makes this single-valued under ψ↦ψ+2π. This is the fifth independent orbit label on the resonant family described by closed orbits with an inverse-cube perturbation.
For a=0, ν varies with L. The orbital resonance condition holds only on selected angular momentum surfaces, so this construction is not a fifth global smooth integral on an open set of arbitrary nearby L. If the question asks for a maximally superintegrable Hamiltonian system on an open region of noncircular bound orbits, the required case is a=0: then ν=1 for every L, and the conserved Laplace-Runge-Lenz vector A=v×L−μr fixes the apsidal direction globally. The relations A⋅L=0 and A2=μ2+2EL2 leave five independent constants. For the usual orbit-wise interpretation, noncircular bound orbits with rational ν have five isolating labels; irrational ν has four. Circular orbits are closed for any allowed ν but have no independent apsidal phase, so the generic noncircular count cannot simply be applied to them.
For completeness, regular unbound branches are different. When e≥1 and ν>0, positive radius restricts the radial phase to ∣ν(ψ−ψ0​)∣<arccos(−1/e)≤π. The apsidal invariant on an unbound central-force branch is
exp(iψ−νi​Argw)=eiψ0​.
(40)
The principal argument is continuous on this scattering branch because it never reaches an apocentre across the negative-real-axis cut. Thus the periapsis direction can be a fifth single-valued scattering label for any positive ν, including irrational ν; the rationality condition applies to the noncircular bound case. For plunging or collision branches with L2−2μa≤0, local trajectory labels can still be defined away from the singular centre, but the preceding periodic-orbit criterion is inapplicable and no smooth continuation through r=0 is asserted.
