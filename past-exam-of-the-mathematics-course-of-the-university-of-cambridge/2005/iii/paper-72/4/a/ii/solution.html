In the distinction relevant here, an integral of motion is a single-valued function I(x,v) of instantaneous phase space, with no explicit time dependence, constant along the orbit. In an autonomous Hamiltonian system this is {I,H}=0, where the bracket is the Poisson bracket. An integral must be defined consistently on the phase space region under discussion; an arbitrary multivalued formula obtained by solving one trajectory does not supply a global integral. Some conventions call explicitly time-dependent conserved functions integrals too; the stationary interpretation is needed for the following counts.
