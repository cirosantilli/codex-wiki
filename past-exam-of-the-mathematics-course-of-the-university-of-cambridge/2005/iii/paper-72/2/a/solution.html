Take V=∣V∣ to be the test body's instantaneous speed relative to the nonstreaming background, rather than an ensemble root mean square speed. Use a local isotropic galactic distribution function f(v) normalized as a number distribution, and assume independent encounters and a large, approximately constant Coulomb logarithm in stellar dynamics.
For a background particle with relative incoming velocity w=V−v, a gravitational scattering angle χ obeys
tan(χ/2)=bw2G(M+m)​=bb90​​.

In the centre-of-momentum frame, the test body's share of the relative velocity is m/(M+m). The azimuthal average cancels transverse changes, leaving a change parallel to the incoming relative velocity,
ΔV∥​=−M+mm​(1−cosχ)w=−M+m2m​1+b2/b902​w​.

The number of encounters per unit time in d3v and impact interval db is f(v)d3vw2πbdb. Integrating the longitudinal change from b=0 to bm​ gives
V˙=−2πG2m(M+m)∫f(v)w3w​log(1+G2(M+m)2bm2​w4​)d3v.

At leading logarithmic order replace the logarithm by 2lnΛ, evaluated at a representative speed. For isotropic f, the velocity-shell identity for dynamical friction is
∫f(v)∣V−v∣3V−v​d3v=V34πV​∫0V​f(v)v2dv.
(12)
One proof takes the divergence with respect to V: the inverse-square vector field has divergence 4πδ(3)(V−v), whose velocity integral is 4πf(V), and rotational symmetry plus the divergence theorem determines the radial field. Exterior velocity shells give zero field. Consequently the systematic Chandrasekhar dynamical friction is antiparallel to motion, with
V˙=−V216π2G2m(M+m)lnΛ​∫0V​f(v)v2dv.
(13)
In the massive-test-body approximation M≫m, this becomes
V˙=−V216π2G2mMlnΛ​∫0V​f(v)v2dv,Λ=GMbm​V2​.​
(14)
The finite-test-mass dynamical friction coefficient shows the needed qualification: M>m alone does not make M+m=M. The quoted expression also neglects diffusion and the velocity dependence of the logarithmic cutoff. For the lowered Maxwellian velocity distribution, set f=0 for v>ve​; continuing the printed subtraction beyond escape would give a negative distribution. If V>ve​, the integral stops at ve​.
