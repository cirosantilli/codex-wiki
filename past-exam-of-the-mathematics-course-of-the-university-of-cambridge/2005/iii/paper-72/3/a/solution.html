Interpret the barred velocities as second moments of a steady nonstreaming collisionless stellar system, and write σr2​=vr2​​. In an isotropic model the three component dispersions agree, so the Spherical Jeans equation becomes
drd(ρσr2​)​=−r2ρGM(r)​.

(27)
For k<1 and r≪b, the integral is dominated by outer radii. Therefore
σr2​∼Crk,C=APb​​+(3−k)(1−k)2πGA​b2−2k.​
(28)
The coefficient cannot be determined from the local density cusp alone. Extending the power law to infinity gives a divergent pressure integral when k<1, so an outer scale or boundary condition is essential. For the usual shallow cusp 0<k<1, the central velocity dispersion tends to zero even though the density diverges.
For 1<k<3, inner radii dominate as r/b→0; equivalently one can send b→∞ with vanishing boundary pressure to obtain
σr2​∼(3−k)(k−1)2πGρ0​r0k​​r2−k=2(k−1)Vc2​(r)​.​
(29)
Thus the central velocity dispersion decreases for 1<k<2, is constant at k=2, and diverges for 2<k<3. At the omitted threshold k=1, σr2​=(Pb​/A)r+2πGArln(b/r). For k≥3 the assumed central density law has infinite enclosed mass, so the same unmodified model cannot determine a finite velocity dispersion. These boundary and domain restrictions are the boundary dependence of an isotropic self-gravitating cusp; specifying density without them does not specify a physical finite galaxy.
