First correct the group-ring condition in the PDF. For an infinite group, a left-invariant element of ZΓ would have constant coefficients on the whole group; finite support then forces it to be zero. Hence H0(Γ,ZΓ)=0. For a Poincare duality group of positive dimension, the correct condition is
Hk(Γ,ZΓ)=0 (k=n),Hn(Γ,ZΓ)=D≅Z as an abelian group.​
(19)
It must be accompanied by the finiteness condition: the trivial module has a finite-length resolution by finitely generated projective modules. The module D has a right group action, possibly an orientation-sign action. The degree-zero exception only applies to the trivial PD0 group, not simultaneously to positive-dimensional groups.
Here is how the duality follows. Put R=ZΓ and take a length-n projective resolution P∗​→Z. Dualize to P∗=HomR​(P∗​,R). Its cohomology is zero except in degree n, where it is D. Reversing degrees therefore makes Qj​=Pn−j∗​ a projective right-R resolution of D. Finite generation and projectivity give, for every left R-module V, an isomorphism of cochain complexes
HomR​(P∗​,V)≅P∗⊗R​V.
(20)
Taking cohomology and reversing the grading yields
Hk(Γ,V)≅Torn−kR​(D,V).
(21)
Since D has underlying abelian group Z, its action is a sign character ω:Γ→{±1}. Twisting the resolution by this character identifies the right side with group homology having coefficient module D⊗Z​V, with diagonal left action γ(d⊗v)=ω(γ)d⊗γv. Thus
Hk(Γ,V)≅Hn−k​(Γ,D⊗V).​
(22)
For an orientable Poincare duality group, D is trivial and this is untwisted Poincare duality. The natural isomorphism can equivalently be realized by cap product with the group fundamental class. The reversed finite projective complex explains why the top group-ring condition produces duality for every coefficient module, rather than just matching integral Betti numbers.
Now let H have finite index in Γ. There is an isomorphism of left Γ-modules
ZΓ≅CoindHΓ​(ZH).
(23)
One explicit map sends r∈ZΓ to the H-linear function g↦prH​(gr), where prH​ retains only terms in H. Finite index ensures that all such functions arise in this way. Shapiro's lemma gives the isomorphism
Hk(Γ,ZΓ)≅Hk(H,ZH)
(24)
as abelian groups. If Γ is a Poincare duality group, restricting its finite projective resolution to H preserves projectivity and finite generation: the group ring is finite free over ZH. This proves that H is PDn.
For the converse one needs the usual ambient torsion-free hypothesis. The standard finite-index resolution lemmas say that type FP∞​ is preserved under finite-index passage, and that a torsion-free group of finite virtual cohomological dimension has the same integral cohomological dimension as a finite-index subgroup. Thus if H is PDn and Γ is torsion-free, Γ has a finite resolution by finitely generated projective modules of length n. The preceding explicit coinduction and Shapiro calculation transfers exactly the required group-ring cohomology, and proves the finite-index invariance of Poincare duality for torsion-free groups.
Without that hypothesis the claimed converse is false. Take Γ=Z×C2​ and H=Z×1. Then H is index two and is PD1, while Γ contains torsion. Its integral cohomological dimension of a group is infinite: restriction of any putative finite projective resolution to C2​ would contradict the nonzero cohomology H2j(C2​,Z)=Z/2 for every j≥1. Also, orientability need not ascend: the torus subgroup of the Klein-bottle group has trivial orientation action, whereas the full group has a nontrivial one. The invariant theorem concerns the possibly twisted notion of PDn.
Finally, a closed aspherical surface has a two-dimensional contractible universal cover and exactly this compact-support duality, so its surface group is PD2. Conversely the algebraic condition forces the same local duality dimension and, in the orientable case, a nonsingular skew pairing on first cohomology, the familiar algebra of a closed orientable surface. Infinite-index subgroups behave like noncompact surfaces rather than closed ones. These are strong reasons to expect a surface group; in fact the PD2 classification theorem confirms that every integral PD2 group is the fundamental group of a closed aspherical surface. This includes the torus and, with orientation twist, the Klein bottle; the sphere and projective plane do not give PD2 groups.
