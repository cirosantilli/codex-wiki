A topological spectrum can be described sequentially by based spaces Ej​, j≥0, and structure maps
σj​:S1∧Ej​⟶Ej+1​.
(1)
Iterating these maps gives compatible maps Sk−j∧Ej​→Ek​. Equivalently, an indexed prespectrum on a universe for spectra U has spaces E(V) and unital associative structure maps SW⊖V∧E(V)→E(W) for finite-dimensional inclusions V⊂W⊂U. Here SA denotes the one-point compactification of the real inner product space A.
A map of topological spectra u:D→E is a family of based maps uj​:Dj​→Ej​ satisfying
uj+1​σjD​=σjE​(1∧uj​).
(2)
The indexed version requires the same compatibility for every inclusion of indexing subspaces. These are point-set maps, before passing to spectrum homotopy or the stable homotopy category.
An Omega-spectrum is a topological spectrum whose adjoint structure maps
Ej​⟶ΩEj+1​
(3)
are weak homotopy equivalences. Consequently πk​E=πk+j​Ej​ whenever k+j≥0, using the structure identifications. In the genuine indexed point-set convention, the word spectrum already includes the stronger condition that E(V)→ΩW⊖VE(W) is a homeomorphism; a general system without that condition is called an indexed prespectrum. Its associated spectrum is obtained by spectrification. The sequential and indexed conventions present the same stable theory.
A cell spectrum is built from the zero topological spectrum by successive stable cell attachments. Concretely, attach a free spectrum on the inclusion Sm−1↪Dm at some level j by a pushout along its boundary map; its quotient is the stable cell sphere Σm−jS. Iterating such attachments and taking the colimit gives a cell spectrum, with successive quotients wedges of integer suspensions of spectra of the sphere spectrum. Negative cell degrees are allowed by choosing j>m. In the genuine indexed model apply spectrification to this cellular prespectrum. The compatibility of the structure maps is part of both the objects and their maps.

Use derived smash products of spectra and replace a based space by a based CW complex when necessary. The reduced represented homology theory of E is
Ek​(X)=πk​(E∧Σ∞X),k∈Z.​
(4)
Thus represented homology theory is covariant in X: a based map induces a map of suspension spectra, and hence a map on the stable homotopy groups of the displayed smash product of spectra. In a well-based sequential model the same definition is
Ek​(X)=colimj​πk+j​(Ej​∧X),
(5)
with j sufficiently large and transition maps given by suspension followed by the structure map.
The reduced represented cohomology theory is
Eq(X)=[Σ∞X,ΣqE]st​,q∈Z.​
(6)
The brackets denote maps in the stable homotopy category. This is contravariant in X, by precomposition. For the Omega-spectrum E it is also the group of based homotopy classes [X,Eq​]∗​, where for negative q we take Eq​=Ω−qE0​. More generally, for j≥max(q,0) the Omega-spectrum identifications give Eq(X)=[Σj−qX,Ej​]∗​; the shifts and loops supply its abelian group structure. In particular
Ek​(S0)=πk​E,Eq(S0)=π−q​E.
(7)
All these definitions are reduced: the basepoint itself contributes zero.

Let Mapsp​(D,E) be the space of point-set maps of topological spectra. Its underlying set is the first set in the comparison. A member is a compatible collection Dj​→Ej​, rather than independently chosen maps at the levels.
Because the source is cellular and the target is an Omega-spectrum, taking spectrum homotopy classes gives all maps in the stable homotopy category:
[D,E]st​=π0​Mapsp​(D,E).​
(8)
Thus the first set maps onto the second. Two point-set maps have the same stable class precisely when they are spectrum-homotopic, using the cellular cylinder and fibrant target. The homotopy must commute with the structure maps throughout; unrelated levelwise homotopies are not the definition.

A stable map u:D→E induces a degree-preserving additive natural transformation of represented cohomology theories by postcomposition with Σqu in degree q. This transformation commutes with the suspension isomorphisms. The resulting map
[D,E]st​⟶NatΣ​(D∗,E∗)
(9)
is onto, but need not be one-to-one. Its kernel consists of hyperphantom maps of spectra: maps u for which uv=0 for every stable map
v:Σ−jΣ∞X⟶D,
(10)
for every based space X and every j≥0. Equivalently, they induce zero in all degrees of represented cohomology theory on all based spaces. Therefore
NatΣ​(D∗,E∗)≅[D,E]st​/HPh(D,E).​
(11)
This condition tests arbitrary suspension spectra with negative shifts. Merely testing finite spectra defines a phantom map of spectra, which is a weaker condition. In particular the homology conclusion in Question 5 should not be substituted for the cohomology condition here.

The Yoneda lemma, applied to the representing Omega-spectrum spaces in each degree, makes the comparison more explicit. Put
Tj​=Σ−jΣ∞Dj​.
(12)
The structure map of D supplies Tj​→Tj+1​, and D is the homotopy colimit of spectra of this sequence. The inverse-tower transition on maps into E is precomposition with Tj​→Tj+1​. Under the suspension spectrum adjunction it takes [Dj+1​,Ej+1​]∗​ to [Dj​,Ej​]∗​ by taking the adjoint through Dj​→ΩDj+1​ and the Omega-spectrum identification Ej​≃ΩEj+1​.
Consequently the maps of represented cohomology theories are exactly the compatible degreewise operations:
NatΣ​(D∗,E∗)≅jlim​[Dj​,Ej​]∗​.​
(13)
Only nonnegative degrees need be listed, since the suspension compatibility recovers the negative degrees. The Milnor exact sequence for maps of spectra gives the sharper description
0⟶limj1​[ΣDj​,Ej​]∗​⟶[D,E]st​⟶limj​[Dj​,Ej​]∗​⟶0.
(14)
Thus HPh(D,E)≅limj1​[ΣDj​,Ej​]∗​, with bonding maps coming from the same telescope. For an inverse tower Aj​ with maps rj​:Aj+1​→Aj​, limAj​ is the kernel and lim1Aj​ is the cokernel of
∏j​Aj​⟶∏j​Aj​,(aj​)⟼(aj​−rj​(aj+1​)).
(15)
The first comparison quotients by spectrum homotopy; the second quotients by hyperphantom maps. These are different identifications.

Write fx​ for the linear isometry specified at x. Choose a cofinal sequence Vi​ from the indexing family I, and write the target part of the supplied subordinate flag for a family of linear isometries as Wi​. After a cofinal enlargement it has
Vi​⊂Vi+1​,Wi​⊂Wi+1​,fx​(Vi​)⊂Wi​(x∈X).
(16)
Both flags exhaust their universe for spectra. Compactness of the parameter space ensures that a finite target stage can contain all images of each fixed source stage.
Let ξi​ be the vector bundle on X with fiber
(ξi​)x​=Wi​⊖fx​(Vi​).
(17)
Here ⊖ is the orthogonal complement in the indicated ambient subspace. Its rank is dimWi​−dimVi​. Let Th(ξi​) be its Thom space, the disk bundle modulo its sphere bundle, so that every fiber is compactified and all its points at infinity become the single basepoint. For the zero bundle this convention gives Th(0X​)=X+​. Define a target indexed prespectrum on the flag by
P(Wi​)=Th(ξi​)∧E(Vi​).
(18)
The smash product here is of based spaces, not yet the derived smash product of spectra.
To specify all structure maps, for i≤j use the continuous fiberwise orthogonal isomorphism
(Wj​⊖Wi​)⊕(ξi​)x​≅(ξj​)x​⊕fx​(Vj​⊖Vi​).
(19)
Both sides identify with the complement of fx​(Vi​) in Wj​. In particular this is the isometry obtained by orthogonally projecting onto fx​(Vj​⊖Vi​) and its complement; it is not an assertion that the displayed summands coincide separately. Pass to fiberwise one-point compactifications, identify the last summand with Vj​⊖Vi​ using fx−1​, and apply the source structure map. This defines
SWj​⊖Wi​∧P(Wi​)⟶P(Wj​).
(20)
More explicitly, decompose z+w∈Wj​⊖fx​(Vi​), where z∈Wj​⊖Wi​ and w∈(ξi​)x​, as w′+fx​(v) with w′∈(ξj​)x​ and v∈Vj​⊖Vi​. Send its pair with e∈E(Vi​) to the pair (w′,σVi​,Vj​​(v,e)) over x. The notation for finite vectors extends to the compactifications, sending infinities and basepoints to the basepoint. Orthogonal decomposition and associativity of the source structure maps prove the prespectrum identities.
Extend the indexed prespectrum from the cofinal flag to the target indexing family and apply spectrification L. The point-set definition is
X⋉f​E=LP.​
(21)
On a map of topological spectra u:E→E′ it is induced at stage i by 1Th(ξi​)​∧u(Vi​). Thus the prescribed isometry family, indexing family and flag have all been used. A common cofinal refinement identifies the resulting objects in the stable homotopy category; the point-set model retains the chosen flag. If f is a constant standard inclusion, the complement bundles are trivial and this recovers the usual reindexed X+​∧E.

The associativity of twisted half-smash products follows from an actual isomorphism of the complement bundles. First work over compact parameter spaces, so that a common choice of flags can be made. Choose source, intermediate and target stages Vi​,Wi​,Zi​ satisfying
fx​(Vi​)⊂Wi​,gy​(Wi​)⊂Zi​.
(22)
For the two twisted half-smash products and their composite the relevant vector bundles have fibers
ξx​=Wi​⊖fx​(Vi​),ηy​=Zi​⊖gy​(Wi​),ζy,x​=Zi​⊖gy​fx​(Vi​).
(23)
Pull the first two bundles back to Y×X. Since gy​ is a linear isometry, the decomposition of Zi​ into gy​(Wi​) and its orthogonal complement gives the fiberwise vector bundle isomorphism
ζy,x​=ηy​⊕gy​(ξx​).​
(24)
Its inverse is given by the two orthogonal projections. The isomorphism varies continuously with (y,x), and the ranks agree:
dimZi​−dimVi​=(dimZi​−dimWi​)+(dimWi​−dimVi​).
(25)
The one-point compactification of an external orthogonal direct sum gives the smash product of its Thom spaces. Using gy​ to identify gy​(ξx​) with ξx​, the isomorphism therefore gives
Th(ζ)∧E(Vi​)≅Th(η)∧Th(ξ)∧E(Vi​).
(26)
The left side is a stage of (Y×X)⋉h​E and the right side is the corresponding two-step prespectrum presentation of Y⋉g​(X⋉f​E).
These stage comparisons commute with structure maps. Indeed, increasing a stage decomposes a suspension vector into its component in gy​fx​(Vj​⊖Vi​) and the two residual complement components. In either construction the same source component is pulled back to Vj​⊖Vi​ and used in the same structure map of E; the residual components are left in the same final complement. Iterated orthogonal decomposition gives the same result whether the intermediate complement is separated first or last. They also commute with maps of E, since the comparison only rearranges the suspension coordinates.
For clarity about the intervening spectrification, maps out of LP into a genuine indexed spectrum T are precisely compatible prespectrum maps P→T. At stage i, adjunction from the Thom coordinate identifies such a map with a continuous family
E(Vi​)⟶Ω(ξi​)x​T(Wi​)≅T(fx​(Vi​)),
(27)
where the last identification is the genuine indexed spectrum structure of T. Compatibility between stages is exactly compatibility of this family with the source structure maps. Thus maps out of the twisted construction are continuous families of maps over the specified linear isometries. A family of such maps over g out of X⋉f​E is, by applying this universal property a second time, exactly a family of maps out of E over gy​fx​. This is the same orthogonal-coordinate correspondence just constructed; it respects the identifications imposed by each spectrification. Consequently it descends from the prespectrum presentations to the associated spectra. Common cofinal refinements remove any incompatibility between the independently chosen flags.
The printed hypothesis does not require Y to be compact. For an arbitrary compactly generated parameter space Y, use the same construction on compact test spaces mapping into Y. Each such family admits the common flags above, and the comparisons agree under restriction because their formula is the canonical orthogonal projection. They therefore assemble into the continuous global comparison in the compactly generated point-set construction. Equivalently, the preceding universal-property correspondence is valid for arbitrary Y and does not impose a uniform finite target stage on all of it. Passing to cellular models for the derived construction gives the requested natural isomorphism
Y⋉g​(X⋉f​E)≅(Y×X)⋉h​Ein the stable homotopy category.​
(28)

In the stable homotopy category, a ring spectrum is a unital associative monoid object for the smash product of spectra. Thus it consists of a topological spectrum E with multiplication and unit
μ:E∧E⟶E,η:S⟶E,
(29)
where S is the sphere spectrum, and the identities are
μ(μ∧1)=μ(1∧μ),μ(η∧1)=1E​=μ(1∧η).
(30)
The canonical associativity and unit identifications of the symmetric monoidal category are understood in these formulas. All identities are identities of maps in the stable homotopy category.
A commutative ring spectrum also satisfies
μτ=μ,​
(31)
where τ:E∧E→E∧E interchanges the two factors. This is the ring-object convention; a coherently structured ring spectrum has additional point-set or higher-homotopy data. In particular homotopy-category commutativity alone is not an assertion of such a coherent enhancement.

Put A=Σ∞X+​ and H=F(A,E)=F(X+​,E). The diagonal of X and its map to a point induce
Δ:A⟶A∧A,ϵ:A⟶S.
(32)
Here the identification (X×X)+​≅X+​∧X+​ explains the target of Δ. These maps satisfy the coassociativity, cocommutativity and counit identities, since their underlying maps send x to repeated copies of the same x.
Use the defining adjunction of the function spectrum to define m:H∧H→H. Its adjoint is the composite
H∧H∧A1∧Δ​H∧H∧A∧Ainterchange​(H∧A)∧(H∧A)ev∧ev​E∧Eμ​E.
(33)
Define u:S→H to have adjoint
Aϵ​Sη​E.
(34)
Thus m is pointwise multiplication, while u is the constant unit. These are genuine stable maps, defined through the internal function spectrum, so the argument does not presume that stable functions have ordinary points.
To verify associativity, take the adjoints of m(m∧1) and m(1∧m). Both are maps H∧3∧A→E. Both use the triple diagonal A→A∧3, evaluate the three copies of H, and multiply the three outputs. The triple diagonals agree by coassociativity; the output maps agree by associativity of the ring spectrum E. The function spectrum adjunction therefore makes the original two maps equal. For the left unit, the adjunct of m(u∧1) uses (ϵ∧1)Δ=1A​ and μ(η∧1)=1E​, leaving exactly evaluation. Its adjoint is 1H​. The same calculation with (1∧ϵ)Δ proves the right unit. Hence
F(X+​,E) is a ring spectrum.​
(35)
If E is a commutative ring spectrum, cocommutativity of Δ proves that this function spectrum is commutative as well.

Take a based CW complex model for X. Since it is (n−1)-connected and n≥2, its reduced ordinary homology vanishes below degree n, with arbitrary constant coefficients. This also follows from a CW complex model with no non-basepoint cells below degree n. The connective spectrum E has πq​E=0 for q<0.
Filter X by its skeleta. The quotients of consecutive stages are wedges of spheres, so the exact sequences in represented homology theory give the homological Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence
Ep,q2​=Hp​(X;πq​E)⟹Ep+q​(X),dr​:Ep,qr​⟶Ep−r,q+r−1r​.
(36)
It is first quadrant because E is a connective spectrum. In a fixed total degree it converges with a finite filtration: cells of sufficiently high dimension cannot contribute in that degree or affect it through a boundary, by the same connectivity bound.
In total degree n, every term with p<n is zero, and terms with q<0 are zero. The only possible term is
En,02​=Hn​(X;π0​E)=Hn​(X;Z).
(37)
No differential can leave it: the target has spatial degree n−r<n. No differential can enter it: its source would be (n+r,1−r), whose coefficient degree is negative for every r≥2. Thus it survives unchanged and is the only filtration quotient of En​(X); there is no extension problem. The ordinary Hurewicz theorem now yields
En​(X)≅Hn​(X;Z)≅πn​(X).​
(38)
The isomorphism uses the stated identification π0​E≅Z and is natural in X. This is the bottom-degree generalized Hurewicz isomorphism.

Choose basepoints and replace the spaces and map by CW complex models. If the hypothesis is stated for unreduced represented homology theory, the basepoint coefficient summands split naturally, so it also gives an isomorphism in reduced represented homology theory. Let C be the homotopy mapping cone of f. Its reduced represented homology theory is zero in every degree, by the exact sequence of the corresponding cofiber sequence of spectra.
The mapping cone C is a simply connected space: the mapping cylinder retracts onto Z, and coning off the path-connected space Y adds no fundamental-group generators; the van Kampen calculation gives the quotient of π1​Z by the image of π1​Y, which is trivial. Suppose some positive-degree integral homology group of C were nonzero, and let n be the least such degree. Then n≥2. Starting with π1​C=0, the Hurewicz theorem shows inductively that πi​C=0 for i<n: at each first possible degree i, its homotopy group is identified with the zero group Hi​(C;Z). It then identifies
πn​C≅Hn​(C;Z)=0.
(39)
Applying the result of part (a) to the (n−1)-connected based space C gives En​(C)≅πn​C=0, contradicting the vanishing of its represented homology theory.
Thus C is integrally acyclic, and the ordinary homology exact sequence says that f is an integral homology isomorphism. The allowed homological Whitehead theorem for simply connected spaces makes the map of CW complex models a homotopy equivalence. Returning to the original spaces therefore gives
f is a weak homotopy equivalence.​
(40)
The simply connected hypothesis is used both in the first-nonzero-degree Hurewicz theorem argument and in the final homological Whitehead theorem.

Choose a small skeleton F of the finite spectra, including every integer suspension. There is such a set of representatives: a finite list of stable cells and their attaching maps specifies a finite cell model, and the possible attaching maps form sets. For every F∈F and every stable map v:F→D, take one copy of F and form
X=⋁(F,v)​F.
(41)
Define the evaluation map e:X→D by taking its restriction to the (F,v) summand to be v. Complete it to the cofiber sequence of spectra
Xe​Dp​E⟶ΣX.
(42)
This is the universal evaluation phantom map construction.
First, p is nonzero. If p=0, applying [D,−]st​ to the cofiber sequence of spectra gives the exact portion
[D,X]st​e∗​​[D,D]st​p∗​​[D,E]st​.
(43)
Since p∗​(1D​)=p=0, exactness supplies s:D→X with es=1D​. Thus D is a retract of X. This is also a wedge summand: complete s to a cofiber sequence of spectra D→X→C′→ΣD. The left inverse e makes this triangle split, giving X≅D∨C′ in the stable homotopy category. One can see the splitting directly from exactness: the connecting map is zero because e extends 1D​ across s, and consequently the quotient map X→C′ admits a section. This contradicts the assumption on D. Hence p=0.
For any finite spectrum F and any v:F→D, its chosen representative has a summand inclusion ιv​:F→X with eιv​=v, after transferring along the representative isomorphism. Consecutive maps of a cofiber sequence of spectra compose to zero, so
pv=peιv​=0.
(44)
Thus p is a phantom map of spectra. To establish the requested conclusion on all spaces, rather than just coefficient groups, let K first be a finite based CW complex. Put A=Σ∞K. The Spanier-Whitehead dual A∨=F(A,S) is a finite spectrum. The evaluation and coevaluation adjunction of Spanier-Whitehead duality identifies
Dk​(K)=πk​(D∧A)≅[ΣkA∨,D]st​.
(45)
Under this identification p∗​ is postcomposition with p. Its source is a finite spectrum, including when k is negative, so the calculation pv=0 proves that p∗​ is zero on Dk​(K) for every k.
Now let Y be an arbitrary based CW complex. It is the filtered union, or filtered homotopy colimit of spaces, of its finite based subcomplexes K. The suspension spectrum functor and smash product of spectra preserve that homotopy colimit. The shifted sphere spectrum is compact, so taking stable homotopy groups gives
Dk​(Y)=colimK⊂Y finite​Dk​(K),Ek​(Y)=colimK⊂Y finite​Ek​(K).
(46)
Every element of the first group therefore comes from a finite K, where p∗​ has just been proved zero. Consequently p∗​ is zero on Y as well. A based CW complex replacement extends the conclusion to arbitrary based spaces in the homotopy category. We have constructed
p:D⟶E,p=0 stably,p∗​:D∗​(Y)⟶E∗​(Y) is zero for every Y.​
(47)
This uses continuity of represented homology theory, not a continuity assertion for represented cohomology theory; the latter need not hold.
