Put At​=[M]t​. Its continuous, strictly increasing paths start at zero and tend to infinity. Thus the inverse Ts​ is finite and continuous, and
{Ts​≤u}={Au​≥s}∈Fu​,
(24)
so each Ts​ is a stopping time. Set Gs​=FTs​​ and βs​=MTs​​. This is the inverse-clock construction in the Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem.
To verify its martingale property, stop M at times τn​ bounding its path, its bracket and time itself. The stopped M and M2−A are bounded martingales. Optional sampling at Ts​∧τn​ and Tt​∧τn​ shows that the time-changed process, stopped at Aτn​​, is a martingale and that its square minus the stopped clock s∧Aτn​​ is a martingale. These clock stopping times tend to infinity because A∞​=∞. Therefore β is a continuous local martingale with β0​=0 and quadratic variation [β]s​=s. The Lévy characterization of Brownian motion identifies it as a Gs​-Brownian motion.
Strict monotonicity and continuity give ATs​​=s and TAt​​=t, so
Mt​=β[M]t​​​.

The inverse-clock argument proves the asserted representation; the random clock need not be independent of the Brownian motion it produces.
