Let x have a dense forward orbit O={fj(x):j≥0}, and suppose that f is surjective. Every tail Ok​={fj(x):j≥k} is then dense. Indeed, continuity gives
fk(O)⊆fk(O)​=Ok​​,
(8)
and the left side equals fk(X)=X by surjectivity.
Given nonempty open sets U,V, choose k≥0 with fk(x)∈U. The dense tail Ok+1​ supplies j≥k+1 with fj(x)∈V. Taking n=j−k≥1 gives fj(x)∈fn(U)∩V. This proves the required positive-time intersection property.
The result can fail without surjectivity. Take the compact metric space
X={0}∪{1/j:j≥1},qquadf(0)=0,qquadf(1/j)=1/(j+1),
(9)
with the metric induced from the real line. All nonzero points are isolated, so continuity there is automatic; at zero, f(1/j)→0=f(0) proves continuity. The forward orbit of 1 is {1,1/2,1/3,…}, whose closure is X, so this is point transitivity. But f(X)=X∖{1}. The singletons U={1/2} and V={1} are nonempty open sets, and for every n≥1,
fn(U)={1/(n+2)},qquadfn(U)∩V=∅.
(10)
The isolated initial point can be visited only at the start of the dense forward orbit; deleting that initial segment destroys density.
