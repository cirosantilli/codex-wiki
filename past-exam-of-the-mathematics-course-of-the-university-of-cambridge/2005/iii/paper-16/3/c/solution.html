Write the hyperbolic toral automorphism as TA​([v])=[Av] on T2=R2/Z2, where A∈GL(2,Z). Its determinant is ±1. The eigenvalues cannot be a nonreal conjugate pair: their product would have absolute value one, forcing each to have absolute value one, contrary to hyperbolicity. The same product condition and hyperbolicity imply two distinct real eigenvalues, labelled so that
0<∣λs​∣<1<∣λu​∣,qquad∣λs​λu​∣=1.
(19)
We will prove
htop​(TA​)=log∣λu​∣.​
(20)
This is the entropy of a hyperbolic toral automorphism; the absolute value is important when the expanding eigenvalue is negative.
Choose real eigenvectors vs​,vu​ and the adapted norm ∥avs​+bvu​∥∗​=max(∣a∣,∣b∣). Its quotient metric on the torus is
d([v],[w])=minℓ∈Z2​∥v−w−ℓ∥∗​.
(21)
It is a compatible metric, so part (a) allows its use. Let ρ=min0=ℓ∈Z2​∥ℓ∥∗​>0 and D=∥A∥∗​=∣λu​∣. Fix ϵ>0 small enough that 2ϵ<ρ and (D+1)ϵ<ρ.
If [v] lies in the Bowen ball Bn​(0,ϵ), each TAk​[v] has a unique lift zk​ with ∥zk​∥∗​<ϵ. Uniqueness follows because two such lifts differ by a lattice vector of norm less than 2ϵ<ρ. Moreover, zk+1​−Azk​ is a lattice vector, and
∥zk+1​−Azk​∥∗​<(D+1)ϵ<ρ.
(22)
It must be zero. Thus zk​=Akz0​: there is no hidden lattice jump between successive lifted iterates.
Write z0​=avs​+bvu​. The simultaneous bounds ∥Akz0​∥∗​<ϵ for 0≤k<n are exactly
∣a∣<ϵ,qquad∣b∣<ϵ∣λu​∣−(n−1).
(23)
The stable coordinate is largest at time zero and the unstable coordinate at time n−1. Conversely, every vector in this rectangle satisfies all the Bowen ball bounds. The quotient is injective on this rectangle by 2ϵ<ρ. If J=∣det(vs​,vu​)∣, its normalized Haar measure is therefore
ν(Bn​(0,ϵ))=4Jϵ2∣λu​∣−(n−1).
(24)
The quotient metric is translation invariant and TA​ is linear, so dn​(x,y)=dn​(0,y−x); every Bowen ball has this same measure, independently of its centre.
Take an ϵ-separated set of largest cardinality sn​(d,ϵ). It is maximal under inclusion, so its radius-ϵ Bowen balls cover the torus: a point outside all these balls could be added. The covering bound gives
sn​(d,ϵ)≥4Jϵ21​∣λu​∣n−1.
(25)
Its radius-ϵ/2 Bowen balls are disjoint, by the triangle inequality for dn​. Their measures sum to at most one, giving
sn​(d,ϵ)≤Jϵ21​∣λu​∣n−1.
(26)
Taking logarithms, dividing by n, and letting n→∞ yields hd​(TA​,ϵ)=log∣λu​∣ for every sufficiently small ϵ. The limit as ϵ↓0 proves the answer. Contraction in the stable direction contributes no exponential growth; only expansion in the unstable direction does.
