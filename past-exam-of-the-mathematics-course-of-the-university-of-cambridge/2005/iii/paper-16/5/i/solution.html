Here is an alternative route for the ergodicity step using the Birkhoff ergodic theorem. After invariance has been proved, the theorem identifies the almost-everywhere limit of An​φ with Eμ​[φ∣I]. The hypothesis therefore gives
Eμ​[φ∣I]=∫φdμ
(41)
for every continuous function φ. For an invariant Borel set B, approximate h=1B​ in L1 by such a φ. Since h is measurable with respect to the invariant sigma-algebra, its conditional expectation is h itself. The L1 contraction of conditional expectation gives
∥h−μ(B)∥1​≤∥Eμ​[h−φ∣I]∥1​+​∫(φ−h)dμ​≤2∥h−φ∥1​.
(42)
Arbitrarily accurate continuous approximation forces h=μ(B) almost everywhere. Thus this use of the theorem reaches the same zero-or-one criterion as the direct averaging proof above, without assuming in advance that μ is ergodic.
