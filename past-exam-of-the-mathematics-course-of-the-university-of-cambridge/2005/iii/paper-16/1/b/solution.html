Write Wn​(i,j) for the number of locally admissible words (x0​,…,xn​) with x0​=i and xn​=j. Here local admissibility means Axr​xr+1​​=1 for each consecutive pair; length counts symbols, so there are n transitions. For n=0, the single-symbol word gives W0​(i,j)=δij​=(A0)ij​, and for n=1 we have W1​(i,j)=Aij​.
To append the last symbol j, choose the penultimate symbol k. Every choice gives a locally admissible word exactly when its length-n prefix is locally admissible and Akj​=1. Different prefixes or penultimate symbols give different words, and every admissible word is obtained once. Therefore
Wn+1​(i,j)=∑k=1m​Wn​(i,k)Akj​.
(2)
This is precisely the rule for matrix multiplication. Mathematical induction consequently gives
Wn​(i,j)=(An)ij​.​
(3)
Equivalently, expanding the matrix entry sums the products Aix1​​Ax1​x2​​⋯Axn−1​j​ over all intermediate symbols; each product is the indicator function of one allowed transition chain.
The intended meaning of “allowed” here is local admissibility. If instead it means a word that actually occurs in some two-sided sequence in ΣA​, the assertion needs an additional extension assumption. For example, A=(00​10​) permits the word (1,2) and has (A)12​=1, but ΣA​ is empty because no symbol can continue after 2. Having a nonzero entry in every row and every column is sufficient: one can successively choose a predecessor and a successor to extend every locally admissible word indefinitely in both directions. No such assumption is needed for the local counting formula.
