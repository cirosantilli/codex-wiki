The printed phrase “not reducible” is a misprint: the requested nonzero complementary submodules establish that this tensor square is reducible. The natural reason is that the flip τ(v⊗w)=w⊗v commutes with the Lie algebra representation. Its two eigenspaces are the symmetric square and the exterior square, giving
V⊗V=Sym2V⊕Λ2V,dimSym2V=10,dimΛ2V=6.​
(24)
In the symmetric square, the four weights ±2Li​ and four weights ±L1​±L2​ each occur once, while zero occurs twice. In the exterior square, the four weights ±L1​±L2​ each occur once and zero occurs twice; the ±2Li​ are absent because repeated wedge factors vanish. These are the two requested complementary weight diagrams.
More precisely, the tensor-square decomposition of the defining symplectic representation identifies Sym2V with the irreducible adjoint module L(2ω1​). The identification is an intertwiner sending a symmetric tensor vw to the element of the symplectic Lie algebra x↦ω(v,x)w+ω(w,x)v. For the exterior square, symplectic contraction of an exterior square has a five-dimensional kernel, the primitive exterior square, and the invariant tensor Ω=e1​∧e3​+e2​∧e4​ supplies a complementary line. Thus the full irreducible decomposition is
V⊗V=L(2ω1​)⊕L(ω2​)⊕L(0),16=10+5+1.​

Here ω1​=L1​ and ω2​=L1​+L2​. The two zero weights in the exterior square split into one primitive zero weight and the invariant line.

Write u=∑λ​aλ​e(λ) with finite support. The root reflection is an involution, and sα​u=−u implies asα​λ​=−aλ​. Any fixed weight has coefficient zero. For each nonfixed orbit choose its representative λ with
m=λ(Hα​)>0,sα​λ=λ−mα.
(36)
Integrality of m follows because λ belongs to the weight lattice; positivity is ensured by our choice of orbit representative. Pairing the orbit coefficients gives
u=∑chosen λ​aλ​[e(λ)−e(λ−mα)].
(37)
For each pair, elementary finite telescoping shows
e(λ)−e(λ−mα)=(1−e(α))[−∑r=1m​e(λ−rα)].
(38)
There are finitely many chosen orbits, and each inner sum is finite. This proves the root-binomial divisibility of reflection anti-invariants:
1−e(α)u​=−chosen λ∑​aλ​r=1∑λ(Hα​)​e(λ−rα)∈Z[ΛW​].​
(39)
For precision, the geometric-series expression is interpreted coefficientwise. Its coefficient at a weight ν is ∑n≥0​aν−nα​, which is a finite sum because u has finite support. On each coset of Zα, reflection pairs the coefficients with opposite signs, so their total is zero. These cumulative sums vanish both before the smallest occupied weight and after the largest occupied weight. Only finitely many cosets are occupied, hence the product of the formal series with u has finite support and equals the boxed quotient.
The individual terms e(nα)u need not vanish for large n. For example, u=e(α)−e(−α) is anti-invariant, and its quotient is −1−e(−α). Thus “finite sum” means a finite result after coefficientwise cancellation, not an actually truncated geometric series. No analytic convergence assumption is involved.

Work over C with finite-dimensional Lie algebras and Lie algebra representation spaces. The adjoint form of Engel theorem is
g is nilpotent ⟺adx is nilpotent for every x∈g.​
(40)
Here a nilpotent Lie algebra has terminating lower central series, γ1​g=g, γj+1​g=[g,γj​g]. We prove the stronger linear statement: if V=0, L⊆gl(V) and every member of L is a nilpotent endomorphism, then there is a nonzero v∈V killed by all of L, and L can be simultaneously represented by strictly upper triangular matrices.
For the proof of Engel theorem by induction and normalizers, first establish a useful nilpotence fact. If xm=0, left and right multiplication by x on End(V) commute. Expanding their difference gives
(adx)N(T)=∑j=0N​(−1)j(jN​)xN−jTxj.
(41)
For N=2m−1, every summand contains a power of x at least m, so (adx)2m−1=0. Restrictions and induced quotient maps remain nilpotent.
Now prove the common-kernel statement, the Engel lemma, by induction on dimL. The zero algebra is immediate. Choose a maximal proper Lie subalgebra M. Its action on L/M by commutators consists of nilpotent maps by the preceding fact. Applying the lower-dimensional induction hypothesis to its image gives a nonzero coset y+M with [M,y]⊆M. Hence
M⊊NL​(M),NL​(M)={z∈L:[z,M]⊆M}.
(42)
The normalizer of a Lie subalgebra is a Lie subalgebra by the Jacobi identity. Maximality implies NL​(M)=L, so M is an ideal of a Lie algebra. Moreover L/M has no proper nonzero Lie subalgebra, and every one-dimensional subspace is a Lie subalgebra. Thus dimL/M=1.
Induction also provides a nonzero common annihilator
W={v∈V:mv=0 for all m∈M}.
(43)
It is stable under L: for m∈M, x∈L, v∈W,
m(xv)=x(mv)+[m,x]v=0,
(44)
because [m,x]∈M. Choose x representing a basis of L/M. The restriction of the nilpotent endomorphism x to the nonzero space W has nonzero kernel. A vector in this kernel is killed by both M and x, hence by all of L. This finishes the induction. When an action is not faithful, induction is applied to its image, whose dimension is no larger than dimM; no faithfulness assumption is hidden.
Starting with the common-kernel line, apply the same statement to each successive quotient Lie algebra representation space. It constructs a complete flag of invariant subspaces
0=V0​⊂V1​⊂⋯⊂Vd​=V,dimVj​=j,LVj​⊆Vj−1​.
(45)
An adapted basis represents every member of L by a strictly upper triangular matrix. Every product of d such matrices is zero, so every iterated commutator with d entries is zero as well.
Finally, if every adx on g is nilpotent, apply the linear statement to the Adjoint representation. With d=dimg, every product of d adjoint operators vanishes, so γd+1​g=0. Conversely, if γc+1​g=0, then (adx)cy=0 for all x,y, proving the other implication. This proves Engel's theorem and its simultaneous-triangularization form. The hypothesis concerns every element of the algebra, not merely a chosen set of generators.

Work with a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra. The Dynkin diagram is obtained from its intrinsic root system, with several structure theorems entering the construction.
First choose a Cartan subalgebra h. The semisimple structure theory gives the root-space decomposition
g=h⊕⨁α∈Φ​gα​,gα​={X:[H,X]=α(H)X for all H∈h}.
(63)
The nonzero roots span h∗, root spaces have dimension one, and every root has its negative. The rank is r=dimh.
Next use the Killing form. Its nondegeneracy on h and its positive-definite restriction to the real coroot span identify the real root span E=RΦ with a Euclidean space. Explicitly, if tλ​=B−1(λ), put (λ,μ)=B(tλ​,tμ​). The sl2 subalgebra associated with a root supplies its normalized coroot Hα​ and the integral pairing
β(Hα​)=(α,α)2(β,α)​∈Z.
(64)
The root-string theorem makes the reflection sα​(β)=β−β(Hα​)α permute Φ. Together with the absence of multiples other than ±α, this shows that Φ is a finite reduced root system and a crystallographic root system. These properties come from semisimple Lie algebra theory, not from the diagram's definition.
Choose a vector in E that is not perpendicular to any root. Define the positive roots by positive inner product with this vector. The simple roots are those positive roots that cannot be expressed as sums of two positive roots. Root-system theory shows that they form a basis Δ={α1​,…,αr​}, and each root is an integer combination of them with coefficients all of one sign. Distinct simple roots have nonpositive inner product.
Use the standard Cartan matrix convention
Aij​=αi​(Hαj​​)=(αj​,αj​)2(αi​,αj​)​.
(65)
There is one diagram vertex for each simple root. For i=j, the integral off-diagonal entries are nonpositive and
Aij​Aji​=4cos2θij​∈{0,1,2,3}.
(66)
Connect the two vertices with respectively zero, one, two or three edges. The corresponding angles are 90∘,120∘,135∘,150∘. A multiple edge carries an arrow toward the shorter root. For joined vertices, the length ratio is determined by Aij​/Aji​=(αi​,αi​)/(αj​,αj​); hence the diagram records both angle and relative length.
For example, sl3​ has simple roots L1​−L2​ and L2​−L3​, giving two equal-length vertices joined by one edge: type A2​. For sp4​, take α1​=L1​−L2​ and α2​=2L2​. Then
A=(2−2​−12​),
(67)
so the two vertices have a double edge directed toward α1​, the shorter root: type C2​.
Finally, orthogonal irreducible components of the root system correspond to the simple ideals of a Lie algebra in the semisimple Lie algebra, so its diagram is the disjoint union of their connected diagrams. Conjugacy of Cartan subalgebras and the Weyl group action on choices of positive roots make the resulting diagram independent of these choices up to isomorphism. Thus the vertices, edge multiplicities, arrows and connected components encode the simple-root geometry and the simple-ideal decomposition.
