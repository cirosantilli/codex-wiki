Let I⊆OX​ be the ideal sheaf of Y. The formal completion of a scheme along Y has underlying topological space ∣Y∣ and topological structure sheaf
X/Y​=(∣Y∣, n≥0lim​​(OX​/In+1)∣∣Y∣​).​
(1)
The topology is the adic topology given by the kernels of the projections to the successive infinitesimal neighbourhoods Xn​=(∣Y∣,OX​/In+1). In the locally Noetherian scheme setting of these examples this is a formal scheme. On a Noetherian affine scheme, with Y=V(I)⊆SpecA, it is the formal spectrum SpfA, where A=lim​n​A/In+1. Thus the completion retains all orders of infinitesimal information normal to Y.
Here Z=D(t)=Speck[q,t,t−1] is an open subscheme of X. The relevant affine completions are
X/(q=0)​=Spf(k[t][[q]]),Z/(q=0)​=Spf(k[t,t−1][[q]]),
(2)
with the q-adic topology. The complement of t=0 in the first formal scheme is its formal open D(t). The sections on this open are the completed localization of an adic ring:
Γ(D(t),OX/(q=0)​​)​=n≥0lim​​(k[t,q]/(qn+1))[t−1]=n≥0lim​​k[t,t−1,q]/(qn+1)=k[t,t−1][[q]].​
(3)
The same calculation on every smaller principal open subset identifies the structure sheaves, not just their global sections. Equivalently, every infinitesimal neighbourhood of Z∩(q=0) is precisely the restriction to D(t) of the corresponding neighbourhood in X. By formal completion commutes with restriction to an open subscheme,
Z/(q=0)​ ≅ X/(q=0)​∖(t=0).​
(4)
The isomorphism is canonical and respects the morphisms to Spfk[[q]].
It would give the wrong answer to replace the formal-open structure sheaf by the ordinary localization of a ring k[t][[q]][t−1]. That ring has a uniform bound on the negative powers of t across the coefficients of any one element, whereas
∑n≥0​qnt−n ∈ k[t,t−1][[q]]
(5)
has no such bound. This difference between ordinary and completed localization does not obstruct the isomorphism of formal schemes; it explains why the completed localization is essential.

Use the toric scheme over a base ring construction over Z. Take the cocharacter lattice of an algebraic torus N=Z2 and dual character lattice of an algebraic torus M=Hom(N,Z). For every i∈Z, set
vi​=(i,1),ρi​=R≥0​vi​,σi​=R≥0​vi​+R≥0​vi+1​.
(6)
These cones in toric geometry, together with their rays and the zero cone, form an infinite fan in toric geometry. Adjacent two-dimensional cones meet in their common ray; all other pairs meet only at zero. Thus the face and intersection axioms hold even though there are infinitely many charts.
The consecutive vectors vi​,vi+1​ are a basis of N, since their determinant is −1. The dual cone has integral generators
ai​=(1,−i),bi​=(−1,i+1),
(7)
with ⟨ai​,vi​⟩=0, ⟨ai​,vi+1​⟩=1, ⟨bi​,vi​⟩=1, ⟨bi​,vi+1​⟩=0. Its integral points form the free commutative monoid Nai​+Nbi​. Hence the associated semigroup algebra chart is simply
Ui​=SpecZ[σi∨​∩M]=SpecZ[xi​,yi​].
(8)
On the dense algebraic torus, write t=χ(1,0) and q=χ(0,1). The chart coordinates are
xi​=tq−i,yi​=t−1qi+1,q=xi​yi​.​
(9)
Although these expressions use inverses on the torus, xi​,yi​,q are regular on the indicated charts.
The common-ray chart of Ui​ and Ui+1​ is D(yi​)⊂Ui​, identified with D(xi+1​)⊂Ui+1​ by
xi+1​=yi−1​,yi+1​=xi​yi2​.​
(10)
The inverse is yi​=xi+1−1​, xi​=yi+1​xi+12​. Nonadjacent charts meet in the common dense torus, and all transition maps agree there with the same characters of an algebraic torus t,q, so their cocycle identities follow. By gluing of schemes along open subschemes, these charts construct a scheme X.
The lattice projection (a,b)↦b carries every σi​ into the nonnegative ray of the fan of the affine line. It therefore defines a toric morphism
f:X⟶SpecZ[q].
(11)
Explicitly its pullback on each chart sends q to xi​yi​. The adjacent-chart formula preserves that product, so the local morphisms glue.
Compute its scheme-theoretic fibre over q=0:
Y∩Ui​=SpecZ[xi​,yi​]/(xi​yi​).
(12)
This is the union of the two coordinate affine lines, each with multiplicity one. Let Di​ denote the toric divisor corresponding to ρi​. Its part in Ui​ is yi​=0, with coordinate xi​; its part in Ui−1​ is xi−1​=0, with coordinate yi−1​. On the overlap these coordinates satisfy xi​=yi−1−1​. Consequently these two affine lines glue to
Di​≅PZ1​.
(13)
The neighbouring components Di​,Di+1​ meet at the section pi​=(xi​,yi​)=(0,0), isomorphic to SpecZ. No other components meet. Every special-fibre chart has exactly these two branches and no embedded or extra components. Thus
Y=⋯∪D−1​∪D0​∪D1​∪⋯,Di​≅PZ1​,Di​∩Dj​=∅ if ∣i−j∣>1.​
(14)
The union is an infinite chain, not a finite-type curve disguised as one: its infinitely many components account for the infinitely many toric charts.
Over the principal open subset D(q), both xi​ and yi​ are units because their product is q. Every chart then becomes the same torus chart:
Z[xi​,yi​,(xi​yi​)−1]​=Z[q,q−1,xi​,xi−1​]=Z[q,q−1,t,t−1],t=xi​qi.​
(15)
The transition maps identify the coordinate t exactly, so
X×Z[q]​Z[q,q−1]≅Gm,Z[q,q−1]​.​
(16)
This is the generic region described in the question; over the actual generic point of SpecZ[q] its fibre is Gm,Q(q)​.
The construction is also flat. Indeed, each node chart has a free Z[q]-module basis
1,xia​ (a≥1),yib​ (b≥1).
(17)
Every monomial xiu​yiv​ reduces uniquely by taking out qmin(u,v), and the resulting monomials are independent. Thus the chart morphisms are flat morphisms. This verifies directly that the semistable infinite chain from a toric fan is a degeneration of the multiplicative group scheme rather than an unrelated special fibre.

Write X=X/Y​. The formal completion of a scheme restricts on each chart to
Ui​=SpfAi​,Ai​=lim​n≥0​Z[xi​,yi​]/((xi​yi​)n+1),q=xi​yi​.
(18)
Thus X is a formal scheme over SpfZ[[q]]. The ordinary torus D(q) has disappeared from its underlying space: every point of X lies on Y. In particular, only adjacent completed charts intersect, and their two possible branch overlaps inside any one chart are disjoint.
Construct the action using the integral lattice automorphism
S:N⟶N,S(a,b)=(a+3b,b).
(19)
It carries vi​ to vi+3​ and σi​ to σi+3​, so induces a toric morphism g:X→X that is an automorphism. It preserves the lattice projection to the second coordinate, hence preserves q and Y. On the dense algebraic torus it is
(t,q)⟼(q3t,q).
(20)
In the local coordinates this sends Ui​ to Ui+3​ with
g∗(xi+3​)=xi​,g∗(yi+3​)=yi​.
(21)
These identities show regularity on every chart without requiring q to be invertible there. They also make the action continuous in the q-adic topology, so it extends to X. Its powers shift Di​ to Di+3k​; no nonzero power is the identity or fixes a point of the formal support. Therefore G=⟨g⟩≅Z is a nontrivial infinite cyclic group acting freely.
Construct the quotient explicitly, using three representative charts
Vi​=Spf(lim​n​Z[Xi​,Yi​]/((Xi​Yi​)n+1)),i=0,1,2,
(22)
with q=Xi​Yi​. Glue their branch opens cyclically, taking subscripts modulo three, by
D(Yi​)⊂Vi​ ≅ D(Xi+1​)⊂Vi+1​,Xi+1​=Yi−1​,Yi+1​=Xi​Yi2​.​
(23)
These are genuine isomorphisms of formal schemes. For example, the completed localization of an adic ring on the first open is
Γ(D(Yi​),O)=Z[Yi​,Yi−1​][[q]],
(24)
where Xi​=q/Yi​. On the second it is Z[Xi+1​,Xi+1−1​][[q]], and the gluing sends Xi+1​ to Yi−1​ and Yi+1​ to qYi​. Thus it preserves q and is continuous, with a continuous inverse.
Inside Vi​ we have
D(Xi​)∩D(Yi​)=D(q)=∅.
(25)
Consequently there are no triple-overlap conditions left to check: the two branch identifications in a chart have disjoint domains. By gluing of schemes along open subschemes in its formal version, the three charts produce a formal scheme C over SpfZ[[q]]. Equivalently, reduce the same gluing modulo qn+1 for every n; this constructs compatible schemes Cn​, all on the same underlying space, with Cn​=Cn+1​×SpecZ[q]/(qn+1). Their inverse system has precisely the displayed formal charts.
For every integer i, map Ui​ isomorphically to Viˉ​ by identifying its coordinates with the corresponding capital-letter coordinates. The adjacent-chart formulas agree with the cyclic identifications, so these maps glue to
π:X⟶C.
(26)
They are invariant under g, and π−1(Vi​) is the disjoint union of the translated opens Ui+3k​, each mapping isomorphically onto Vi​. The group permutes these copies transitively. Thus the fibres of π are exactly the G-orbits, and invariant sections on this inverse image are exactly the sections on Vi​.
To verify the quotient property also for morphisms, take a G-invariant morphism h:X→T. Its restrictions to U0​,U1​,U2​ agree on the adjacent branch opens. On the closing overlap they agree because the translated chart U3​ has the same restriction as U0​ under g. Hence they descend to a unique morphism C→T. This proves that the gluing is the cyclic formal quotient of the infinite toric chain:
C≅X/Y​/⟨g⟩,g:(t,q)↦(q3t,q).​
(27)
Its fibre modulo q is a triangle of three projective lines over Z, each meeting its two neighbours at its two boundary sections. More generally, a shift by any m≥3 gives an m-gon. Completion is important here: translates of a formal chart are disjoint, whereas all ordinary toric charts still share the generic torus.

Let n=rankZ​M. Work with commutative rings and take a quotient to mean a surjection p:M⊗Z​R↠Q, considered up to an isomorphism of Q commuting with p. Equivalently, retain its kernel as a submodule of M⊗R. Here a rank-r locally free module is finite locally free, or equivalently a finite projective module of constant rank r. A ring homomorphism R→R′ sends the quotient to
M⊗R′↠Q⊗R​R′.
(28)
The tensor product of modules is right exact and preserves finite projectivity, so this defines the functorial action. We prove scheme representability in the usual functor of points sense:
F(R)≅HomZ​(SpecR,Grr​(M)),​
(29)
naturally in R. The Grassmannian of locally free quotients will be constructed explicitly.
Assume first 0<r<n, and choose a basis e1​,…,en​ of M. For every ordered subset I={i1​<⋯<ir​}⊆{1,…,n}, consider those quotients for which p(ei1​​),…,p(eir​​) are a basis of Q. This choice gives a unique identification Q≅Rr, in which the quotient is represented by an r×n matrix AI​ whose columns indexed by I are the identity matrix. Its remaining r(n−r) entries are arbitrary elements of R. Thus this subfunctor is represented by the affine scheme
UI​=SpecZ[akj​:1≤k≤r, j∈/I]≅AZr(n−r)​.
(30)
There is no further change-of-basis ambiguity: the selected quotient images already specify its basis uniquely.
For another subset J, write AI(J)​ for the square submatrix consisting of the J columns. The same quotient lies in the J chart exactly when its determinant is a unit. Consequently the chart overlap is the principal open subset
UIJ​=D(δIJ​)⊆UI​,δIJ​=detAI(J)​.
(31)
On that overlap the matrix normalized in the J basis is
AJ​=(AI(J)​)−1AI​.​
(32)
The entries are regular on D(δIJ​) by the adjugate matrix. Conversely, the I columns of AJ​ form (AI(J)​)−1, so normalization back to I recovers AI​. The transition is therefore an isomorphism UIJ​≅UJI​.
The cocycle identity follows from the same matrix computation, not just an assertion about bases. On a triple overlap, normalization first to J and then to K gives
(AJ(K)​)−1AJ​​=((AI(J)​)−1AI(K)​)−1(AI(J)​)−1AI​=(AI(K)​)−1AI​.​
(33)
This is direct normalization from I to K. By gluing of schemes along open subschemes, the standard affine charts of the quotient Grassmannian therefore glue to a scheme G over Z.
The universal matrices also glue a universal quotient. On UI​, use the surjection
M⊗Z​OUI​​ AI​ ​OUI​⊕r​.
(34)
On overlaps, identify its targets by (AI(J)​)−1, precisely the transition matrix above. The cocycle identity glues these free sheaves into a rank-r locally free sheaf Q and glues the maps into M⊗OG​↠Q. Its kernel S is locally free of rank n−r: on UI​ an explicit basis is
ej​−∑k=1r​akj​eik​​,j∈/I.
(35)
Hence we have the universal exact sequence
0⟶S⟶M⊗Z​OG​⟶Q⟶0,rankS=n−r,rankQ=r.​
(36)
It remains to verify that these charts capture every quotient, including a nonfree projective target. Given p:Rn↠Q, define VI​⊆SpecR as the locus where the selected images p(ei​), i∈I, form a basis. This is open: locally trivialize the locally free sheaf associated with Q, and it is the nonvanishing locus of the corresponding maximal minor. The sets VI​ cover SpecR. Indeed, at every prime p, the images of all n vectors span the r-dimensional residue-field quotient, so some r of them form a basis there. Their determinant is a unit in Rp​, hence they form a basis over that local ring and on an open neighbourhood of p.
On VI​, trivialize Q using exactly those selected images. The remaining columns give regular functions and hence a morphism VI​→UI​. On VI​∩VJ​, the normalized matrices satisfy the displayed transition formula, so the local morphisms glue to a morphism
fp​:SpecR⟶G.
(37)
Its pullback of the universal quotient is the original quotient, because this is true in each of the specified bases. Conversely, any morphism f:SpecR→G pulls back the universal sequence to a rank-r locally free quotient. On each inverse-image chart its matrix is exactly the normalized matrix used to reconstruct f, so the two constructions are inverse. They are unchanged by an isomorphism commuting with the quotient map, and they commute with every base change R→R′. This proves the natural bijection and therefore
G=Grr​(M) represents F.​
(38)
Taking kernels over a field identifies this with the usual Grassmannian of (n−r)-dimensional subspaces of M⊗k. The construction itself does not assume the target module is globally free.
For r=0, the only quotient is zero; for r=n, a surjection to a locally free module of the same rank is an isomorphism, as can be checked locally by its determinant. Both endpoint functors are represented by SpecZ. If r>n, no such quotient exists over a nonempty test scheme, and the representing scheme is empty. Finally, scheme representability does not mean representation by a single ring: already r=1,n=2 gives PZ1​, which is not affine. This is the standard algebraic-geometric meaning of representability for the functor on rings here.
