Use target signature (−,+,…,+) and a closed-string spatial coordinate 0≤σ≤2π. Write Y for the compact coordinate and XA for the noncompact coordinates. In conformal gauge, the wave equation factorizes, so every coordinate is a sum of a function of τ+σ and a function of τ−σ. The closed-string boundary conditions are
XA(τ,σ+2π)=XA(τ,σ),Y(τ,σ+2π)=Y(τ,σ)+2πwR,w∈Z.
(1)
Thus the derivatives of each chiral part are periodic and have integer Fourier modes; only their worldsheet zero modes can produce the winding displacement. Integrating these Fourier series gives the closed-string mode expansion
XA=xA+α′pAτ+i2α′​​∑m=0​mαmA​e−im(τ−σ)+αmA​e−im(τ+σ)​,
(2)
Y=y+α′Rn​τ+wRσ+i2α′​​∑m=0​mαmY​e−im(τ−σ)+αmY​e−im(τ+σ)​.
(3)
The oscillator reality conditions are αm†​=α−m​ and similarly for the tilded modes. The coefficient of τ follows by integrating the canonical momentum density Y˙/(2πα′). Single-valued wavefunctions of the center coordinate y∼y+2πR have momentum n/R, with n∈Z. Equivalently, the compact chiral worldsheet zero modes are α′pL​(τ+σ)/2 and α′pR​(τ−σ)/2, where
pL​=Rn​+α′wR​,pR​=Rn​−α′wR​.​
(4)
Here tildes denote the left-moving sector. This convention will also fix the sign in compact-circle closed-string level matching.
Choose a noncompact light-cone pair and impose light-cone gauge in string theory X+=x++α′p+τ, with p+=0. The Virasoro constraints (∂±​X)2=0 then solve for ∂±​X− in terms of the transverse coordinates. For example, with ∂±​=∂τ​±∂σ​, one has ∂±​X−=(∂±​Xi)2/(2α′p+). Thus there are two independent transverse oscillator Fock spaces, including Y among the transverse coordinates. Quantization gives [αmi​,αkj​]=mδijδm+k,0​, and the same relation for the tilded oscillators, with the two sectors commuting. Lorentz consistency gives the critical dimension of the bosonic string D=26 and string intercept one in each sector. Accordingly
N=∑m>0​α−mi​αmi​,N=∑m>0​α−mi​αmi​

count 24 transverse oscillator species. The two zero-mode constraints give the lower-dimensional bosonic string mass spectrum
M2=pR2​+α′4​(N−1)=pL2​+α′4​(N−1).
(6)
Taking their difference and average yields
N−N+nw=0,M2=R2n2​+α′2w2R2​+α′2​(N+N−2).​

Every choice of transverse string oscillators and integer charges satisfying this closed-string level matching is a physical string state in light-cone gauge. The eliminated longitudinal oscillators do not supply additional polarizations.
To classify all massless states, positivity of the two compact terms implies N+N≤2. When the sum is two, masslessness forces n=w=0, and closed-string level matching then forces N=N=1. The states α−1i​α−1j​∣0⟩ have 242=576 polarizations. In 25 noncompact dimensions, their decomposition gives a graviton, a Kalb–Ramond field, a dilaton, two Abelian gauge vectors from the metric and two-form with one compact index, and the radius scalar field. Their physical polarization counts are
275+253+2⋅23+1+1=576.
(8)
The first two numbers are the symmetric traceless second-rank tensor and antisymmetric second-rank tensor of the massless little group SO(23).
When N+N=1, closed-string level matching requires ∣nw∣=1. The compact contribution obeys
R2n2​+α′2w2R2​≥α′2∣nw∣​=α′2​,
(9)
and equality occurs precisely at R=α′​. The charges n=w=±1 have (N,N)=(1,0); the charges n=−w=±1 have (N,N)=(0,1). Each charge has 24 polarizations supplied by its single excited sector. At a general radius these have
M2=(α′R​−R1​)2.
(10)
Finally, N=N=0 requires nw=0. The exceptional massless ground states of a bosonic circle occur at
R=2∣n∣α′​​(w=0,n=0),R=∣w∣2α′​​(n=0,w=0).
(11)
Each charge is a scalar field. These special radii must be included if “general radius” is to mean a complete classification, rather than only a generic radius. At a generic radius the massless spectrum is the neutral 576-state spectrum above; at exceptional radii one adds exactly the states just listed. The neutral ground state remains tachyonic.
The map
R′=Rα′​,(n′,w′)=(w,n)
(12)
sends pL​ to pL​ and pR​ to −pR​. Reversing all right-moving compact string oscillators, αmY​↦−αmY​, preserves their commutators and oscillator number; the left-moving oscillators are unchanged. Both the mass formula and compact-circle closed-string level matching are therefore unchanged, with a one-to-one map of the complete oscillator basis. This proves T-duality of the full spectrum, including winding, massive and tachyonic states.
At the self-dual circle the four single-oscillator charge families give 4⋅24=96 new massless states. Each family decomposes into a 25-dimensional gauge vector with 23 polarizations and one scalar field. There are also four massless ground states, with charges (±2,0) and (0,±2). Thus the massless spectrum at the bosonic self-dual circle has
576+96+4=676 polarizations:4 extra vectors and 8 extra scalars.​

(14)

Multiplying these currents by a noncompact oscillator produces six gauge vectors in total. The nine scalar string vertex operators JLa​JRb​ form the (3,3) representation, accounting for the radius scalar field and eight new scalar fields. The remaining neutral fields are the graviton, two-form and dilaton.
A radius deformation as a Higgs mechanism is the expectation value of the Cartan–Cartan scalar string vertex operator JL3​JR3​∝∂YL​∂ˉYR​. Its stabilizer is U(1)L​×U(1)R​, so
SU(2)L​×SU(2)R​⟶U(1)L​×U(1)R​,mW​=​α′R​−R1​​.​
(15)

The other four extra scalar fields have masses 4/R2−4/α′ or 4R2/α′2−4/α′ and need not remain massless; one pair becomes tachyonic on either side of the self-dual point. Together with the original bosonic tachyon, this means that the gauge-symmetry interpretation is formal and does not assert a stable bosonic-string vacuum.

Take 0≤σ≤π and split the coordinates into a=0,…,p along the branes and I=p+1,…,25 transverse to them. In conformal gauge the wave equation permits a separated mode e−iωτf(σ) with f′′+ω2f=0. For Neumann boundary conditions at both ends, f′(0)=f′(π)=0 selects cosmσ and integer m. The zero-frequency solution is independent of σ, with a constant position and a term linear in time. Therefore the open-string mode expansion is
Xa=xa+2α′kaτ+i2α′​∑m=0​mαma​​e−imτcosmσ.
(16)
The canonical momentum density integrated over this interval is ka.
For Dirichlet boundary conditions, subtract the static interpolation between fixed endpoint positions y0I​ and yπI​. The remaining field vanishes at both ends, selecting sinmσ with integer m and excluding a momentum worldsheet zero mode. The expansion is
XI=y0I​+πyπI​−y0I​​σ+2α′​m=0∑​mαmI​​e−imτsinmσ.​
(17)
Here αmI†​=α−mI​. There is no factor i in the sine series with this reality convention: changing m to −m reverses both the sine and the denominator. These are the integer-mode expansion with parallel D-brane endpoints; they are not the half-integer modes of a coordinate with a Neumann condition at one endpoint and a Dirichlet condition at the other.
The endpoints can move on (p+1)-dimensional worldvolumes but are fixed in the transverse directions. Thus these are open strings attached to D-branes, or stretched between parallel D-branes at y0​ and yπ​. The tangential momenta describe propagation on the brane; the transverse positions describe its embedding. Let L=∣yπ​−y0​∣. The linear spatial worldsheet zero mode contributes L2/(4π2α′) to L0​. Quantization with string intercept one gives
M2=4π2α′2L2​+α′N−1​.​
(18)
The first term is also the square of the classical stretched-string energy TL, since the string tension is T=1/(2πα′).
For two coincident D3-branes, label the ends of an oriented open string by i,j∈{1,2}. The four endpoint combinations give Chan-Paton factors λij​, which form the full algebra of 2×2 matrices. Under a change of brane basis these transform as λ↦UλU−1, the adjoint action of U(2). At L=0, the first oscillator level is massless. Its tangential polarization supplies a four-dimensional vector Aa​, while the 22 transverse polarizations supply real adjoint scalar fields ΦI. This is the bosonic D3-brane low-energy field content.
The endpoint matrices multiply in their boundary order in disk amplitudes. The difference between the two orders in the three-vector interaction gives the matrix commutator; the kinematic factor is the one-derivative Yang-Mills theory cubic vertex. Factorization and the vector gauge redundancy then supply the quartic interaction with the same commutator. Equivalently, the leading low-energy action is the dimensional reduction of 26-dimensional Yang-Mills theory, with
Fab​=∂a​Ab​−∂b​Aa​−i[Aa​,Ab​],Da​ΦI=∂a​ΦI−i[Aa​,ΦI],
(19)
Slow​=gYM2​1​∫d4xTr(−41​Fab​Fab−21​Da​ΦIDaΦI+41​[ΦI,ΦJ][ΦI,ΦJ])+⋯.
(20)
For Hermitian scalar fields the last displayed term corresponds to a nonnegative potential, because their commutators are anti-Hermitian. Higher-derivative terms are suppressed at energies well below 1/α′​. Ignoring the tachyon, the gauge sector is four-dimensional U(2) Yang-Mills theory, accompanied by 22 adjoint scalar fields. It is not pure Yang-Mills theory, nor the supersymmetric six-scalar D3-brane theory of a ten-dimensional superstring.
Separate the two branes and use the D-brane scalar-position normalization
⟨ΦI⟩=2πα′1​(y1I​0​0y2I​​).
(21)
A Yang-Mills gauge transformation preserves this expectation value only when it is diagonal if y1​=y2​. Thus the unbroken group is U(1)×U(1). The scalar field kinetic term supplies a mass for the off-diagonal vectors, since
[Aa​,⟨ΦI⟩]12​=2πα′y2I​−y1I​​(Aa​)12​.
(22)
Summing over I gives m122​=L2/(4π2α′2), exactly the mass of the stretched N=1 string. The two diagonal gauge vectors remain massless. This identifies brane separation with an adjoint Higgs mechanism, rather than an explicit deletion of the off-diagonal strings.
For the ground state of an open string joining the two different branes, N=0, so the bosonic stretched-string tachyon threshold is
M02​<0⟺L<2πα′​.​
(23)
At equality it is massless, and above it the stretched ground state has positive mass squared. The same-brane ground strings still have M02​=−1/α′; this calculation removes only the stretched-string instability, not the original instability of the bosonic branes.

The Polyakov action has independent worldsheet metric γab​ and embedding fields Xμ. Varying the metric before imposing a gauge gives
Tab​=α′1​(∂a​X⋅∂b​X−21​γab​γcd∂c​X⋅∂d​X)=0.
(24)
Worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations allow conformal gauge γab​=e2ωηab​ locally. The Weyl factor then cancels out of the classical action, leaving
SX​=4πα′1​∫dτdσ(X˙2−X′2),(∂τ2​−∂σ2​)Xμ=0.
(25)
These are free two-dimensional scalar fields, but the surviving metric equations require
(X˙+X′)2=(X˙−X′)2=0.​
(26)
Equivalently, X˙2+X′2=0 and X˙⋅X′=0. Gauge fixing does not license dropping these Virasoro constraints.
For an ordinary open string, the boundary conditions relate the two chiral sectors. With α0μ​=2α′​kμ, their independent Fourier generators are
Lm​=21​∑r∈Z​αm−r​⋅αr​.
(27)
The classical oscillator Poisson brackets {αmμ​,αnν​}=−imημνδm+n,0​ give the classical Virasoro constraint algebra
{Lm​,Ln​}=−i(m−n)Lm+n​.
(28)
Thus they are first-class constraints. In the quantum theory one applies normal ordering to the product and uses [αmμ​,αnν​]=mημνδm+n,0​. The oscillator commutator gives
[Lm​,αnμ​]=−nαm+nμ​,[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12D​(m3−m)δm+n,0​.
(29)
For example, for m>0 the extra vacuum term is (D/2)∑r=1m−1​r(m−r)=D(m3−m)/12. This exhibits the Virasoro central extension rather than concealing it in a classical constraint equation. The matter central charge is D; the worldsheet ghost fields have charge −26. Quantum consistency sets D=26 and the string intercept to one.
In old covariant string quantization the physical-state Virasoro conditions for an open string are
Lm​∣Ψ⟩=0(m>0),(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0,L0​=α′k2+N.
(30)
Physical string states are then identified modulo physical null string states. One does not impose both positive and negative generators as annihilation conditions: their central extension would obstruct that prescription. The resulting null-state quotient of a string agrees with the transverse light-cone spectrum at nonzero momentum.
At N=0, the oscillator vacuum ∣k⟩ is a single spacetime scalar field with α′M2=−1: the bosonic tachyon. At N=1, write ∣Ψ⟩=ϵμ​α−1μ​∣k⟩. The zero-mode equation gives k2=0, and L1​ gives k⋅ϵ=0. The null descendant L−1​∣k⟩=2α′​k⋅α−1​∣k⟩ identifies ϵ∼ϵ+λk. This leaves 24 physical polarizations of a massless gauge vector.
At N=2, use the level-two open-string polarization decomposition
∣Ψ⟩=(21​hμν​α−1μ​α−1ν​+bμ​α−2μ​)∣k⟩,hμν​=hνμ​.
(31)
The mass is M2=1/α′. The oscillator commutators give all the nontrivial positive-mode conditions:
2α′​kμhμν​+2bν​=0,21​hμμ​+22α′​k⋅b=0.
(32)
Higher positive modes annihilate this level. To see the quotient explicitly, the 25 transverse-parameter null states L−1​(ξ⋅α−1​∣k⟩), with k⋅ξ=0, shift
Δhμν​=2α′​(kμ​ξν​+kν​ξμ​),Δbμ​=ξμ​.
(33)
The additional level-two scalar Virasoro null state (L−2​+23​L−12​)∣k⟩ shifts
Δhμν​=ημν​+6α′kμ​kν​,Δbμ​=25​2α′​kμ​.
(34)
Substitution into the two physical equations verifies the null-state conditions; the second residual is (D−26)/2. Because k is timelike, these null shifts span all components of b, so choose a representative with b=0. The physical conditions then say kμhμν​=0 and hμμ​=0. In the rest frame this is a traceless second-rank tensor in 25 spatial dimensions, with 25⋅26/2−1=324 polarizations. The light-cone basis independently gives 24 states α−2i​∣k⟩ and 24⋅25/2=300 symmetric states α−1i​α−1j​∣k⟩. Hence
α′M2=−1,0,1:1 scalar,24 vector polarizations,324 massive spin-two polarizations.​
(35)
For the fermionic extension, an explicit convention prevents ambiguity in the signs. Keep worldsheet signature (−,+) and use
ρ0=(0i​−i0​),ρ1=(0i​i0​),{ρa,ρb}=−2ηab.
(36)
Take ψ=(ψ1​,ψ2​)T and the constant parameter ϵ=(ϵ1​,ϵ2​)T to be real Grassmann-odd Majorana components, with ψˉ​=ψTρ0. Put ∂±​=∂τ​±∂σ​. Apart from the common factor 1/(4πα′), the combined Lagrangian is
L=∂+​X⋅∂−​X+iψ1​⋅∂+​ψ1​+iψ2​⋅∂−​ψ2​.
(37)
The rigid transformations can be written
δX=ϵˉψ,δψ=−iρa∂a​Xϵ,
(38)
or, in components,
δX=i(ϵ2​ψ1​−ϵ1​ψ2​),δψ1​=−ϵ2​∂−​X,δψ2​=ϵ1​∂+​X.
(39)
For the ϵ2​ part, the bosonic variation is iϵ2​(∂+​ψ1​⋅∂−​X+∂+​X⋅∂−​ψ1​), while the fermionic variation is −iϵ2​∂−​X⋅∂+​ψ1​+iϵ2​ψ1​⋅∂+​∂−​X. Anticommuting the constant parameter past the fermion is essential in the second term. The first terms cancel. The other chiral component works in the same way, giving the chiral proof of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry
δL=iϵ2​∂−​(ψ1​⋅∂+​X)−iϵ1​∂+​(ψ2​⋅∂−​X).​
(40)
Thus the action is invariant off shell up to a boundary term. The transformation is a genuine supersymmetry: on X its commutator for parameters ϵ,ζ is the translation
[δϵ​,δζ​]X=2i(ϵ2​ζ2​∂−​+ϵ1​ζ1​∂+​)X.
(41)
On ψ the same translation follows using ∂+​ψ1​=0 and ∂−​ψ2​=0; the remaining cross terms are proportional to these Dirac equations. This is on-shell closure of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry. On a surface with boundary, compatible endpoint conditions must make the boundary flux vanish. On a compact worldsheet, a constant parameter must preserve the spin structure; the local identity alone does not make a constant transformation compatible with every antiperiodic sector.
The fermions contribute to the worldsheet stress-energy tensor and produce a fermionic superconformal current. In holomorphic normalization with ψμ(z)ψν(w)∼ημν/(z−w),
T=−α′1​:∂X⋅∂X:−21​:ψ⋅∂ψ:,qquadG=iα′2​​:ψ⋅∂X:.
(42)
The free-field contractions give G(z)G(w)∼(2c/3)/(z−w)3+2T(w)/(z−w) and conformal weight 3/2 for G, where c=D+D/2=3D/2. Consequently the modes obey the N=1 super-Virasoro algebra
[Lm​,Ln​][Lm​,Gr​]{Gr​,Gs​}​=(m−n)Lm+n​+12c​(m3−m)δm+n,0​,=(2m​−r)Gm+r​,=2Lr+s​+3c​(r2−41​)δr+s,0​.​
(43)
Dropping the central terms gives the classical graded constraint algebra. Integer r gives the Ramond sector; half-integer r gives the Neveu–Schwarz sector. In the displayed conformal-plane convention G02​=L0​−c/24 in the Ramond sector. Shifting to the Ramond vacuum-normal-ordered zero mode gives the usual Ramond supercurrent zero-mode square G02​=L0(R)​.
Rigid symmetry of matter already supplies these currents, but promoting their vanishing to new gauge constraints requires coupling to worldsheet supergravity and varying its worldsheet gravitino. In superconformal gauge one retains T=G=0. The fermionic current constraints and their physical-state conditions extend the bosonic Virasoro conditions. The ghost charges are −26+11=−15, so cancellation with 3D/2 gives the critical dimension of the RNS superstring D=10; the NS and Ramond intercepts are respectively 1/2 and zero. This distinguishes the global supersymmetry established above from its locally gauged string completion.

Use the critical ten-dimensional RNS string and light-cone gauge in string theory, leaving eight transverse bosons and eight transverse fermions. The bosonic oscillators α−mi​ have integer m>0. In the Neveu–Schwarz sector the fermionic oscillators b−ri​ have positive half-integer r, while in the Ramond sector the fermionic oscillators d−mi​ have integer m, including zero modes. Their anticommutators are {bri​,bsj​}=δijδr+s,0​ and {dmi​,dnj​}=δijδm+n,0​. The level and mass formulas are
NNS​NR​​=m>0∑​α−mi​αmi​+r>0∑​rb−ri​bri​,=m>0∑​(α−mi​αmi​+md−mi​dmi​),​α′M2α′M2​=NNS​−21​,=NR​.​
(44)
Before projection the NS ground state is a tachyon of mass squared −1/(2α′). Its oscillator tower describes spacetime bosons. The Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra {2​d0i​,2​d0j​}=2δij makes the Ramond vacuum a 16-dimensional spinor of SO(8), split into the two chiral eight-dimensional spinors. The Ramond tower describes spacetime fermions; there is no Ramond tachyon.
Choose the supersymmetric GSO projection. In the NS sector it keeps states with an odd number of fermionic creation operators, eliminating the zero-oscillator tachyon. In the Ramond sector it fixes the combined parity of the zero-mode chirality and the nonzero-mode fermion number. Thus the Ramond vacuum retains one chirality; adding an odd number of nonzero-mode fermions requires the opposite zero-mode chirality. The projected towers both have α′M2=0,1,2,…. Here the counts refer to oscillator polarizations for a single endpoint multiplicity; any common Chan-Paton multiplicity multiplies both counts.
At the first retained mass level, M2=0, the NS states are b−1/2i​∣0;k⟩ for i=1,…,8, furnishing the eight polarizations of the ten-dimensional gauge vector. The Ramond ground state is one chiral spinor with eight physical polarizations. Hence
M2=0:8 bosons=8 fermions.​
(45)
These form the massless supersymmetric vector multiplet.
At the next mass level, α′M2=1, the NS level is 3/2. The odd-fermion condition leaves exactly
b−3/2i​∣0⟩,α−1i​b−1/2j​∣0⟩,b−1/2i​b−1/2j​b−1/2k​∣0⟩(i<j<k).
(46)
Their counts are 8, 8⋅8=64 and (38​)=56. Anticommutation requires distinct indices in the third family. There are no other partitions of 3/2 with odd fermion number, so these are all 128 bosons. They assemble into the massive little-group SO(9) representations of dimensions 44 and 84: a traceless symmetric two-tensor and a three-form.
At Ramond level one, the only possibilities are α−1i​∣s⟩ and d−1i​∣s′⟩. The spinors ∣s⟩ and ∣s′⟩ each have eight components and have opposite zero-mode chirality because the fermionic oscillator reverses the GSO parity. Consequently there are 64+64=128 fermions. They form a gamma-traceless vector-spinor of the massive little group: a vector-spinor has 9⋅16 components and its gamma trace removes 16. Therefore the first massive level of the open RNS string has
α′M2=1:8+64+56=128 bosons=64+64=128 fermions.​
(47)
This is the open-string mass normalization; the same chiral oscillator count used in a closed string would correspond to α′M2=4.

F(q)=∑N≥0​dN​qN=∏m≥1​(1−qm)−24.

The two sectors are independent, while closed-string level matching restricts their oscillator numbers to the same N. Thus the number of states at the closed-string mass level is gN​=dN2​, not d2N​.

F(e−β)∼(2πβ​)12exp(β4π2​−β).

dN​∼2​1​(N−1)−27/4e4πN−1​,gN​∼21​(N−1)−27/2e8πN−1​.​
(54)
Replacing N−1 by N gives the equivalent usual leading asymptotic. The power −27/2 matters: the two-sector degeneracy is not just an unspecified exponential.
Since M=2(N−1)/α′​, the exponential is eβH​M with
βH​=4πα′​,TH​=4πα′​1​.​
(55)
Here Boltzmann's constant is one. Coarse-graining the levels into a density per unit rest mass introduces the Jacobian dN/dM=α′M/2. Consequently
ρ(M)∼CM−26eβH​M,C=225α′−25/2.
(56)
This is distinct from the degeneracy per level, whose power in M is −27. The internal-state contribution to a canonical partition function behaves at large mass as
Zinternal​(β)∼∫∞dMCM−26e−(β−βH​)M.
(57)
It converges for β>βH​ and diverges for β<βH​. At β=βH​, this particular internal rest-mass integral converges because of the power M−26; one should not discard the prefactor and claim divergence there without specifying the full thermodynamic ensemble. For example, including continuous momenta in 25 spatial dimensions multiplies the high-mass integrand by a factor proportional to M25/2, still leaving convergence of this ideal one-string integral at the endpoint, although sufficiently high derivatives are singular. Multi-string effects, volume and interactions affect the detailed limiting thermodynamics.
The leading microcanonical entropy grows as S(E)∼βH​E, with logarithmic corrections, so increasingly large energies are stored in increasingly excited, long strings rather than in an arbitrarily hot ordinary gas. The Hagedorn temperature is the boundary of canonical convergence from above in inverse temperature; extrapolating the free-string canonical description to T>TH​ fails. This counting does not settle the interacting phase above that scale. Also, the bosonic ground-state tachyon is a separate vacuum instability; the high-level asymptotic is meaningful as a formal spectrum calculation without asserting a stable bosonic thermal vacuum.

The starting point is a physical massless closed-string state
ϵμν​α−1μ​α−1ν​∣k⟩,k2=0.
(58)
Its symmetric traceless part is a spacetime spin-two field, the graviton; the antisymmetric part is the Kalb–Ramond field, and the scalar field part gives the dilaton. The physical-state conditions impose transverse polarizations, and longitudinal null states identify
ϵμν​∼ϵμν​+kμ​ξν​+kν​ξμ​
(59)
for the symmetric component. In position space this is the linearized diffeomorphism δhμν​=∂μ​ξν​+∂ν​ξμ​. Thus the massless spin-two state has the gauge redundancy required of a gravitational field, rather than an arbitrary massive tensor interaction.
Closed-string amplitudes factorize on this graviton pole. Its leading soft coupling is universal and couples to the energy-momentum tensor of the other string states. Decoupling longitudinal polarizations enforces the gravitational Ward identities. The two-derivative cubic graviton vertex extracted from the low-momentum three-point amplitude is the cubic vertex of the Einstein-Hilbert action; continuing these Ward identities requires the nonlinear diffeomorphism-invariant completion. A complementary way to derive the same dynamics is to demand quantum consistency of propagation in a curved background.
Replace the flat target metric in the worldsheet action by Gμν​(X), and allow the two-form and dilaton backgrounds. After Euclidean continuation, their couplings have the schematic normalization
Sσ​=4πα′1​∫d2σ(h​habGμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+iεabBμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+α′h​Φ(X)R(2)).
(60)
These backgrounds are couplings of a string nonlinear sigma model. Quantum Weyl invariance requires their Weyl anomaly coefficients to vanish. To first order in α′, the metric equation is
0=βμνG​=α′(Rμν​+2∇μ​∇ν​Φ−41​Hμρσ​Hν​ρσ)+O(α′2),H=dB.
(61)
The dilaton and two-form equations accompany it. In a critical-dimensional background with H=0 and constant Φ, the leading metric equation is Rμν​=0, precisely the vacuum Einstein field equations. Matter and other massless string fields supply the corresponding stress-energy when retained. This argument also explains why arbitrary curved target metrics are not consistent string backgrounds.
The equations arise from the leading string-frame massless effective action
Seff​=2κ21​∫dDx−G​e−2Φ(R[G]+4(∇Φ)2−121​Hμνρ​Hμνρ+O(α′)).
(62)
A constant-dilaton expansion of its curvature term directly gives the Einstein-Hilbert kinetic and self-interaction terms. For a varying dilaton, introduce the Einstein-frame metric GμνE​=e−4Φ/(D−2)Gμν​. The Weyl rescaling of the metric and the corresponding transformation of the Ricci scalar and an integration by parts give
Seff​=2κ21​∫dDx−GE​​(R[GE​]−D−24​(∇E​Φ)2−121​e−8Φ/(D−2)HE2​+O(α′)).
(63)
This displays ordinary Einstein gravity coupled to the dilaton and two-form. Gravity need not be the only low-energy field: those fields, compactification moduli and gauge fields have to be retained or stabilized as appropriate. Four-dimensional gravity additionally requires compactification from the critical target dimension, with its Newton constant depending on the compactification volume.
The approximation is controlled when external energies obey Eα′​≪1 and curvatures obey α′∣Rμνρσ​∣≪1. Massive string modes then produce suppressed higher-derivative interactions, such as higher-curvature corrections. Small string coupling gs​=eΦ0​ also suppresses higher-genus quantum corrections; dimensionally GD​ scales as gs2​α′(D−2)/2. Thus Einstein gravity is the universal leading two-derivative dynamics of the massless closed-string spin-two field, with additional massless matter, string-length corrections and quantum corrections systematically controlled.
For the bosonic theory this is the massless-sector effective description in 26 dimensions; the tachyon means that it is not by itself a proof of a stable vacuum. Tachyon-free supersymmetric string theories have the same massless graviton mechanism in ten dimensions before compactification. Removing the tachyon and obtaining a realistic four-dimensional model require extra physical input beyond the emergence of the Einstein action.
