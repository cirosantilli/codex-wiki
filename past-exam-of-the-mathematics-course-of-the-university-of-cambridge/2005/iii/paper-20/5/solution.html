Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2005 / iii / Paper 20 / 5 / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 1k
Use an oriented geodesic hyperbolic 2s-gon D with paired edges of equal length. Identify each pair by a length-preserving map reversing the boundary directions, to make an oriented base surface M0​ with possibly singular vertices. Assign to the s edge pairs generators A1​,…,As​ of T, with inverse labels for reversed crossings. For each g∈T, take a copy Dg​ and glue its positively labelled edge of type k to the corresponding mate in DgAk​​. The generators make the glued surface M connected. Left multiplication Dg​↦Dhg​ commutes with these right-labelled gluings and gives the T action with quotient M0​.
Existence for an arbitrary finite group can even be arranged with no cone singularities. If T has d generators, take h≥max{2,d} and a regular hyperbolic polygon with 4h edges, all angles π/(2h), paired with side word ∏i=1h​ai​bi​ai−1​bi−1​. Its vertices form one cycle of angle 2π, giving a smooth base of genus h. Label the ai​ by the generators of T, padded by identities, and every bi​ by the identity. The single vertex word is ∏i​[Ai​,1]=1, so every lifted vertex also has angle 2π. The resulting connected ordinary normal covering map has degree ∣T∣ and deck group T.
This is the paired-polygon construction of finite surface covers. Interior and open-edge points are regular hyperbolic points; possible singularities occur only at vertices. When the base has cone points, the map is an orbifold covering and may be branched as a map between the underlying topological surfaces. It should not be called an unbranched manifold covering at those branch points.
A vertex cycle of a paired polygon is the collection of vertices identified to one point on M0​. Let r be the number of cycles, θj​ the sum of the polygon angles in cycle j, and Cj​∈T the gluing word recorded on going around it. If qj​=∣Cj​∣ is the order of that element, a lift closes after qj​ turns. It has angle qj​θj​, and there are ∣T∣/qj​ such lifted vertices. The base and lifted cone orders, when they are integer orbifold orders, are
mj​=θj​2π​,mj​=qj​θj​2π​=qj​mj​​.​
(24)
An orbifold homomorphism requires qj​∣mj​. The lifted point is smooth exactly when qj​θj​=2π, equivalently qj​=mj​ in that case. For arbitrary cone metrics the angle formulas remain valid even when an angle does not correspond to an integer orbifold order.
Counting faces, edges and vertices gives the Euler characteristic of a paired-polygon surface cover:
χ(M0​)=1−s+r,χ(M)=∣T∣(1−s+j=1∑r​qj​1​).​
(25)
These are ordinary topological Euler characteristics, rather than orbifold Euler characteristics. If the lifted surface is smooth, the equivalent orbifold formula is
χ(M)=∣T∣χorb​(M0​),χorb​(M0​)=χ(M0​)−∑j​(1−mj​1​).
(26)
For a closed oriented smooth surface, χ=2−2g defines its genus. These formulas also follow from the Riemann-Hurwitz formula by subtracting the ramification deficits ∣T∣−∣T∣/qj​ over each base vertex.
For U≤T, the quotient map M→M1​=U\M is an ordinary normal covering if and only if U acts freely. All possible point stabilizers in this construction are conjugates of ⟨Cj​⟩, so the concrete necessary and sufficient condition is
U∩g⟨Cj​⟩g−1={1}for every j and every g∈T.​
(27)
This is the freeness criterion for a subgroup of a polygon-cover deck group. Under it the deck group is U, the degree is ∣U∣, and
χ(M1​)=∣U∣χ(M)​.​
(28)
There is no requirement U◃T for this map. That different condition characterizes normality of the intermediate cover M1​→M0​. If fixed points are retained and one instead speaks of an orbifold covering, the quotient is regular as an orbifold cover, but ordinary Euler characteristic need not divide by ∣U∣; orbifold Euler characteristic does.
For the low-genus examples take
T=GL(3,F2​)=PSL(3,2),∣T∣=(8−1)(8−2)(8−4)=168.
(29)
Let U1​ be a nonzero-vector stabilizer and U2​ a plane stabilizer. Their actions have seven points, so ∣U1​∣=∣U2​∣=24 and [T:Ui​]=7. They are almost conjugate subgroups: the numbers of fixed nonzero vectors for a matrix A and its dual action A−T are both 2dimker(A−I)−1. Thus the two coset permutation characters agree, which is equivalent to Gassmann equivalence. They are not conjugate: a point stabilizer has a common fixed nonzero vector, whereas a plane stabilizer fixes no nonzero vector globally. In particular, since 7∤24, neither subgroup contains any nonidentity element of a cyclic subgroup of order seven. All cone monodromies of exact order seven therefore act freely on the intermediate covers.
For genus three, start with a hyperbolic triangle whose three angles are π/7 and double it across one edge. The resulting quadrilateral D has angles
π/7, 2π/7, π/7, 2π/7.​
(30)
The side pairings produce three base vertex cycles, each of total angle 2π/7, and an underlying sphere. Choose generators A,B with ∣A∣=∣B∣=∣AB∣=7; the three vertex words are A,B,(AB)−1. Hence s=2, r=3 and all qj​=7. The full cover is smooth, and
χ(M)=168(−1+73​)=−96,χ(Ui​\M)=−96/24=−4,gi​=3.​
(31)
The hyperbolic polygon area of D is 8π/7, so the seven-sheeted surfaces have area 8π, agreeing with Gauss-Bonnet. Their equal spectra follow from the Sunada theorem. For complete numerical generator data, one possible pair is
A=​001​011​110​​,B=​010​001​101​​over F2​.
(32)
These generate all of T and have the required orders. This construction establishes the requested isospectrality; it does not establish nonisometry. In the constant-curvature triangle example a lifted reflection can identify the point and plane covers, as in reflection intertwining of triangle-cover coset actions. Breaking that symmetry with a generic metric gives nonisometric genus-three surfaces, but then the metric need not have constant curvature. Those are distinct assertions.
For genus four, use the cone-torus construction of genus-four Sunada surfaces. Take a regular hyperbolic quadrilateral with four angles
π/14, π/14, π/14, π/14​
(33)
and pair opposite sides in the usual torus pattern a,b,a−1,b−1. All four vertices belong to one cycle, whose total angle is 2π/7; the underlying base is a torus with one cone point. Choose A,B of order four generating T, with commutator [A,B] of order seven. For example,
A=​001​011​100​​,B=​001​010​110​​,∣A∣=∣B∣=4,∣[A,B]∣=7.
(34)
Only the commutator is a cone monodromy, so the order-four side labels do not create cone points of order four. Here s=2, r=1, q1​=7, giving
χ(M)=168(−1+71​)=−144,χ(Ui​\M)=−144/24=−6,gi​=4.​
(35)
The quadrilateral has area 12π/7, and each seven-sheeted smooth quotient has area 12π. Again the order-seven cone stabilizer meets neither Ui​ nontrivially, so the metrics are smooth and the Sunada theorem gives identical spectra. The required group and subgroup orders are therefore 168 and 24 in both constructions; the genus-three side-generator orders are 7 and 7, and the displayed genus-four side-generator orders are 4 and 4, with cone-word order 7 in both cases.
