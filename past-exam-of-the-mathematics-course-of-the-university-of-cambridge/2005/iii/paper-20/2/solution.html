For a smooth function f and the Levi-Civita connection ∇, the Riemannian Hessian is the covariant two-tensor
Hessg​f(X,Y)=(∇X​df)(Y)=X(Yf)−df(∇X​Y).
(4)
The connection rules show that this is linear over smooth functions in both vector-field arguments, so it is a tensor. Torsion-freeness gives its symmetry:
Hessg​f(X,Y)−Hessg​f(Y,X)=[X,Y]f−df(∇X​Y−∇Y​X)=0.
(5)
Its metric trace defines the paper's Laplace-Beltrami operator, Δ=divg​gradg​; its eigenvalues on a closed manifold are nonpositive. We use P=−Δ when listing nonnegative eigenvalues or writing e−tP.
At p, choose an orthonormal basis e1​,…,ed​ of the tangent space and unit-speed geodesics γi​(t)=expp​(tei​). Differentiating twice along a curve gives
dt2d2​f(γi​(t))=Hessg​f(γ˙​i​,γ˙​i​)+df(∇γ˙​i​​γ˙​i​).
(6)
The covariant acceleration vanishes on a geodesic. Taking the trace at t=0 proves the geodesic trace formula for the Laplace-Beltrami operator:
Δf(p)=i=1∑d​(f∘γi​)′′(0)=h→0lim​h2∑i=1d​[f(expp​(hei​))+f(expp​(−hei​))−2f(p)]​.​
(7)
The last equality is Taylor's formula along the geodesics, and expresses the Laplacian entirely through nearby function values.
For stabilization, let X1​,X2​ be the assumed isospectral nonisometric four-dimensional tori, and let k=n−4>0. Take the same small flat torus Fε​=Rk/εZk as an extra factor. On a Riemannian product,
PXi​×Fε​​=PXi​​⊗I+I⊗PFε​​.
(8)
If ϕj​ and ψℓ​ are eigenfunctions with eigenvalues λj​ and μℓ​, then ϕj​ψℓ​ has eigenvalue λj​+μℓ​. Products of orthonormal eigenbases are complete in the product L2 space, so this lists all eigenvalues with multiplicities, including coincidences between different sums. Consequently the two products are isospectral.
One must also prove that multiplying by a common factor has not accidentally made the two manifolds isometric. Choose
0<ε<δ<min{inj(X1​),inj(X2​)}.
(9)
The injectivity radii are positive because the factors are compact. A closed product geodesic of length less than δ projects to a closed geodesic of length less than δ in Xi​. Such a projected geodesic must be constant: otherwise its initial vector over one period would be a nonzero vector of norm below the injectivity radius mapping back to its starting point under the exponential map. Thus all sufficiently short closed geodesics lie in the Fε​ directions. Conversely the k coordinate circles, of length ε, pass through every point and span those directions.
It follows that the smooth distribution tangent to the small flat-torus factor is characterized intrinsically by the span of tangent vectors to closed geodesics of length below δ. A hypothetical product isometry would preserve this smooth distribution and its orthogonal complement. The latter has leaves Xi​×{q}; the isometry and its inverse take whole leaves to whole leaves, thereby giving an isometry X1​≅X2​, a contradiction. This proves isospectral stabilization by a small flat torus:
X1​×Fε​ and X2​×Fε​ are isospectral nonisometric n-tori for every n>4.​
(10)
This argument works even without assuming that the original four-dimensional torus metrics are flat.
