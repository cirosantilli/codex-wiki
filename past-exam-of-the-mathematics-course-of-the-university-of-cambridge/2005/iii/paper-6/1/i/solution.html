The Baire category theorem states that in a complete metric space, a countable intersection of open dense sets is dense. Equivalently, a nonempty complete metric space cannot be a countable union of nowhere dense sets, where a set is nowhere dense if its closure has empty interior.
Here is the proof. Let G1​,G2​,… be open dense sets and let U be any nonempty open set. Choose a point a1​∈U∩G1​ and a radius 0<r1​<1/2 such that the closed ball B1​=B(a1​,r1​) lies inside U∩G1​. Inductively choose am+1​ in the nonempty open set B(am​,rm​)∩Gm+1​, and then a radius 0<rm+1​<2−m−1 such that
Bm+1​=B(am+1​,rm+1​)⊆B(am​,rm​)∩Gm+1​.
(1)
Thus the closed balls are nested and their radii tend to zero. For j,k≥m, both centers lie in Bm​, so d(aj​,ak​)≤2rm​. They form a Cauchy sequence. Completeness gives a limit a, and closedness puts it in every Bm​. Consequently a∈U∩⋂m​Gm​. As U was arbitrary, the intersection is dense.
To obtain the equivalent formulation, take complements of the closures of the nowhere dense sets; those complements are open dense. Conversely, complements of open dense sets are closed with empty interior. In particular, a countable closed cover of a nonempty complete metric space must contain a set with nonempty interior.
