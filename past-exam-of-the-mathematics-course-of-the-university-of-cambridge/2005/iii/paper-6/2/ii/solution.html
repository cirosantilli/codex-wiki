We prove the extension by Zorn's lemma, retaining the full domination condition. Consider all pairs (Y,S) extending (Y0​,T0​), with Y a subspace and S linear, satisfying S(y)≤p(x+y)−q(x) for every x∈X,y∈Y. Order these pairs by extension. A chain has an upper bound: take the union of its subspaces and maps. The union is a subspace, and its map is well defined and linear because any finitely many vectors lie together in one member of the chain. Its domination is inherited there. Thus a maximal extension exists.
Suppose its domain Y is proper, and choose z∈/Y. A linear extension to Y+Rz has the form Sa​(y+tz)=S(y)+ta. Define the admissible values for a sandwich extension by
A=supx′,y′​{S(y′)−p(x′+y′−z)+q(x′)},
(17)
B=infx,y​{−S(y)+p(x+y+z)−q(x)},
(18)
with x,x′∈X and y,y′∈Y. Part (i) shows every quantity in the supremum is at most every quantity in the infimum, hence A≤B. Both bounds are real and finite: choosing zero vectors provides A≥−p(−z) and B≤p(z), and the pairwise comparison then bounds them from the other sides. Choose a∈[A,B].
For t>0, positive homogeneity and the upper bound on a, used with x/t,y/t, give
S(y)+ta≤t[p(x/t+y/t+z)−q(x/t)]=p(x+y+tz)−q(x).
(19)
For t=−s<0, the lower bound on a applied to x/s,y/s gives
a≥S(y/s)−p(x/s+y/s−z)+q(x/s),
(20)
which rearranges to S(y)−sa≤p(x+y−sz)−q(x). For t=0 this is the existing domination. Thus Sa​ is a legitimate larger extension, contradicting maximality. The maximal domain is therefore all of X; call its map T.
Setting the first vector in the domination to zero yields T(v)≤p(v). Setting it to v and the domain vector to −v yields −T(v)≤−q(v). Consequently
T∣Y0​​=T0​,T(y)≤p(x+y)−q(x),q(v)≤T(v)≤p(v).​
(21)
The one-dimensional extension has checked both signs of its coefficient; positive homogeneity alone does not permit treating negative coefficients as positive ones.
