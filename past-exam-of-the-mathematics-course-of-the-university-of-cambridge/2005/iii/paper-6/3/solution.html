Use a nonzero complex unital Banach algebra B with submultiplicative norm, and write its unit as e. The complex scalar field is the usual convention in this spectral statement; for a real algebra one uses its complexification. We derive the needed analytic facts from norm-convergent series and scalar complex analysis.
If ∥a∥<1, completeness makes the Neumann series ∑j=0∞​aj converge. Multiplying its finite partial sums by e−a gives e−am+1, which tends to e. Hence its sum is the inverse of e−a. If b is invertible, then b+h=b(e+b−1h) is invertible whenever ∥b−1h∥<1. This proves that the invertible elements form an open set and that inversion is locally norm-continuous.
Define the Banach algebra spectrum and the resolvent of an element by

Thus σ(x) is bounded by ∥x∥ and is closed, because invertibility is open. Also R(λ)→0 in norm at infinity; for example its norm is at most ∥e∥/(∣λ∣−∥x∥), since ∥e∥≥1 in a nonzero submultiplicative unital algebra. The standard normalization ∥e∥=1 is not needed.

This series and its differentiated series converge uniformly on every smaller disk. It therefore proves R is a Banach-space-valued holomorphic function, establishing complex differentiability in the Banach-space norm and gives R′=−R2, without assuming a Banach-algebra-valued analytic theorem. In particular every continuous complex linear functional ϕ makes ϕ(R(λ)) a scalar holomorphic function.
The Banach algebra spectrum is nonempty. Otherwise R would be defined and analytic everywhere. Its local norm continuity and decay at infinity make it globally bounded. For every continuous linear functional ϕ, the scalar entire function ϕ(R(λ)) is bounded and tends to zero at infinity, so it is identically zero: the scalar Cauchy estimate for its derivative on circles of radius r is M/r, which tends to zero, and the limiting value then fixes its constant as zero. Such functionals separate points by the Hahn-Banach theorem. Explicitly a bounded real functional can be extended from the real span of any nonzero element using its norm, and complexifying it by ϕ(v)=ℓ(v)−iℓ(iv) gives a bounded complex linear functional nonzero on that element. Therefore R itself would be zero, contradicting (λe−x)R=e. We have proved the Banach algebra spectrum is a nonempty compact subset of the complex plane.

For the first example take the commutative algebra of dual numbers B=C[ε]/(ε2) with norm ∥a+bε∥=∣a∣+∣b∣. Completeness follows from finite dimension, and the product estimate makes the norm submultiplicative. For x=ε, x=0 but x2=0, so ρ(x)=0. Directly, λe−ε has inverse λ−1e+λ−2ε for λ=0, and its Banach algebra spectrum is {0}. For the second example take B=C with its absolute-value norm and x=1. Its Banach algebra spectrum is {1}, giving ρ(x)=∥x∥=1.
