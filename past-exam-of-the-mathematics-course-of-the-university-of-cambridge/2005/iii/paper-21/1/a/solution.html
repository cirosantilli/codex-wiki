OurBigBook.com Words: 395
Use projectivization by quotients, and set
E=⨁i=1n​OP1​(ai​),F=PP1​(E),π:F→P1.
(1)
The fibre over t parametrizes one-dimensional quotients of Et​, so it is Pn−1. The standard line bundles are
L=π∗OP1​(1),M=OF​(1).
(2)
Thus L is the fiber class of a rational normal scroll of the ruling and M is the universal quotient line bundle of π∗E. In additive divisor notation we also write L,M for their first Chern classes. For n≥2 these generate the Picard group; for n=1, F=P1 and M=a1​L. This fixes the sign convention for the rational normal scroll.
Put s=∑i​ai​. The projective bundle formula for Chow groups gives
A∗(F)=Z[L,M]/(L2, Mn−sLMn−1).
(3)
Here the first relation comes from the one-dimensional base, and the second is the projective-bundle relation with c1​(E)=sL and higher Chern classes zero on the base. Integration over a fibre gives LMn−1=1. Therefore all top-degree mixed intersection numbers are
Mn=s,LMn−1=1,LjMn−j=0(2≤j≤n).​
(4)
In particular, for integers u,v,
(uM+vL)n=uns+nun−1v.
(5)
For the canonical divisor of a smooth variety, the relative Euler sequence
0→OF​→π∗E∗⊗OF​(M)→TF/P1​→0
(6)
has determinant OF​(nM−sL). Combining its dual determinant with KP1​=−2L gives
KF​=−nM+(s−2)L.​
(7)
A common twist does not change the abstract projective bundle. Choose an integer b such that di​=ai​+b≥1 for every i. The complete linear system of divisors ∣M+bL∣ has
h0(F,M+bL)=h0(P1,E(b))=∑i​(di​+1)=s+nb+n.
(8)
Its map to PN, where N=s+nb+n−1, has coordinate blocks
λi​s0di​​, λi​s0di​−1​t0​, …, λi​t0di​​(1≤i≤n).
(9)
Here [s0​:t0​] is a point of the base and the λi​ are quotient coordinates in a local trivialization of E(b). These expressions have the correct transition factors and define the global map. Any nonzero block recovers the base point through its degree-di​ rational normal curve. Once that point is known, the blocks recover the projective fibre coordinates. On the open where the first coordinate of a nonzero block is nonzero, the ratio of the next coordinate to the first recovers t0​/s0​; the analogous last-coordinate chart recovers s0​/t0​. The remaining coordinate ratios then recover the fibre coordinates regularly. Thus the map identifies F with its closed image and is a projective embedding, not merely a pointwise injection.
The hyperplane class on this image is M+bL, so
degF=(M+bL)n=s+nb,codimF=N−n=s+nb−1.
(10)
Consequently every such natural embedding has
degF=codimF+1.​
(11)
The tautological choice b=0 is an embedding when all ai​>0. If the ai​ are only nonnegative, ∣M∣ is basepoint-free but can contract the projective subbundle arising from zero summands; one must distinguish that scroll image from the smooth abstract scroll.
