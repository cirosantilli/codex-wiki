Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2005 / iii / Paper 21 / 2 / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 622
Work over C, and let C be a smooth projective curve of genus g≥3 and gonality three. A trigonal curve has a basepoint-free degree-three pencil, giving a finite morphism f:C→P1 of degree three. It is not hyperelliptic, so its canonical map is an embedding. Let Dt​=f−1(t), including its scheme multiplicities. The complete pencil has h0(Dt​)=2, and Riemann-Roch theorem gives
h0(KC​−Dt​)=g−2.
(20)
Thus the quotient of canonical sections evaluated on Dt​ has dimension two, and Dt​ spans a line in Pg−1. This argument includes ramified fibres, rather than treating only three distinct points.
These two-dimensional quotients vary in a globally generated rank-two bundle E on P1. More explicitly, the image of
H0(C,KC​)⊗OP1​⟶f∗​KC​
(21)
has constant rank two by the preceding dimension count, so it is a subbundle. The equality of the rank at every fibre justifies the bundle construction. On global sections it contains every canonical section, and therefore H0(P1,E)=H0(C,KC​). The Birkhoff–Grothendieck theorem gives
E=O(a1​)⊕O(a2​),0≤a1​≤a2​.
(22)
Global generation forces nonnegative splitting degrees. Since h0(E)=a1​+a2​+2=g,
a1​+a2​=g−2.​
(23)
The natural map from f∗E to KC​ is a surjection at every point, since the canonical system has no base points. By projectivization by quotients it gives a map C→F=P(E) over P1, with M∣C​=KC​. Its composition with the tautological map of F is the canonical embedding of C, so C→F is itself a closed embedding. Each fibre of the ruling maps to the spanning line of Dt​; their union is the canonical scroll of a trigonal curve, and the ruling restricts to the given g31​.
Put a=a2​−a1​. Tensoring E by O(−a2​) identifies the abstract ruled surface with Fa​=P(O(−a)⊕O). The section coming from the quotient onto the smaller summand has self-intersection −a and will be denoted by B. In this normalized presentation its tautological class is B; restoring the twist gives
M=B+a2​L.​
(24)
This class gives the scroll map. It is an embedding if a1​>0. When a1​=0, it contracts B; thus the printed embedding description needs this positivity qualification. For example, in genus three the ruled model is F1​ and its image is P2; in genus four the type (0,2) has a quadric-cone image. In both cases the smooth curve and its canonical image are still described by the construction above.
As a divisor on F, the curve meets each fibre in degree three, so C=3M+tL for an integer t. The scroll formula gives
KF​=−2M+(g−4)L.
(25)
By adjunction formula and M∣C​=KC​,
(KF​+C−M)∣C​=(g−4+t)L∣C​=0.
(26)
Taking degree on C uses deg(L∣C​)=3 and gives t=4−g. Consequently
C∈∣3M+(4−g)L∣=∣3B+(3a2​+4−g)L∣=∣3B+(a+a2​+2)L∣.
(27)
Writing d=a+a2​+2, this is
C∈∣3B+dL∣,d=2g+3a+2​.​
(28)
A divisor rB+dL with r≥0 on Fa​ is basepoint-free when d≥ra. Indeed, its pushforward along the ruling splits into
⨁j=0r​OP1​(d−ja);
(29)
if each degree is nonnegative, sections generate these coefficient functions, and the fibre monomials generate at every point. In particular 3B+dL is basepoint-free when d≥3a. The Bertini smoothness theorem then makes its general member nonsingular, including the equality case, where the general member avoids B.
Conversely, if d<3a, then (3B+dL)⋅B=d−3a<0, so every effective member contains B. Because the canonical scroll has a1​≥0,
d=2a+a1​+2>2a.
(30)
The residual system ∣2B+dL∣ is basepoint-free. Its general member R does not contain B and intersects it in
R⋅B=d−2a=a1​+2>0.
(31)
Thus a general divisor in the original system is B+R with intersecting components and is singular at an intersection point. The fixed component occurs exactly once for a general member, since R is not forced to contain it. This proves both directions, with the canonical-scroll hypotheses retained:
∣3B+(a+a2​+2)L∣ has a nonsingular general member⟺d≥3a⟺3a≤g+2.​
(32)
This is also the bound on the Maroni invariant. Merely observing a fixed component would not by itself prove singularity; the positive intersection with its residual divisor is essential.
