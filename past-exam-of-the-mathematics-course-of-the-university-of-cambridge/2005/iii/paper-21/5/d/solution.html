One useful version is the effective global Torelli theorem for K3 surfaces. Let X,Y be complex K3 surfaces and let
ϕ:H2(Y,Z)∼​H2(X,Z)
(75)
preserve the integral cup-product pairing and the Hodge decomposition. Suppose that ϕ carries some Kähler class of Y to a Kähler class of X. Then
there is a unique isomorphism f:X∼​Y such that f∗=ϕ.​
(76)
Conversely every isomorphism has these properties. In particular, for projective K3 surfaces an integral Hodge isometry carrying an ample polarization to an ample polarization is induced by a unique isomorphism of the polarized surfaces. This is the effective global Torelli statement.
The Kähler-cone condition is necessary if the prescribed isometry itself is to be induced by an isomorphism. If δ is the class of an effective smooth rational (−2)-curve, the reflection
rδ​(x)=x+(x,δ)δ
(77)
is integral, preserves the pairing, and preserves the Hodge decomposition because δ has type (1,1). It sends δ to −δ. An automorphism preserves effectivity, whereas −δ is not effective: it has negative intersection with an ample class. Thus this Hodge isometry is not an automorphism pullback. Requiring an isometry to respect the Kähler chamber removes precisely this kind of obstruction.
