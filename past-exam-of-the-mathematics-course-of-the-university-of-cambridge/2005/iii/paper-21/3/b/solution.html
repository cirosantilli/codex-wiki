Take D nef and big on a complex K3 surface, and put g=1+D2/2. The K3 linear system dichotomies are successive alternatives, so each later statement assumes that the exceptional cases in the earlier ones have been excluded.
The first dichotomy separates basepoint-free systems from monogonal linear systems on a K3 surface. In the exceptional case,
D=mE+Γ,m≥2,E2=0,EΓ=1,Γ2=−2,∣D∣=Γ+∣mE∣.
(39)
Otherwise ∣D∣ is basepoint-free. There are no isolated base points left after excluding the fixed-section case; this is the first dichotomy.
For a basepoint-free system, the second dichotomy separates hyperelliptic linear systems on a K3 surface from birational maps to normal images. The degree-two cases are characterized by
D2=2,orE2=0, E an elliptic pencil, DE=2,orD=2B, B2=2, ∣B∣ basepoint-free.
(40)
Their images are surfaces of minimal degree: the plane, a rational normal scroll, or the Veronese surface. In the birational case, the map is an embedding away from the (−2)-curves Γ with DΓ=0, which it contracts to Du Val singularities. Thus “birational” does not assert an embedding of the original surface. These are the degree-two and birational projective-model alternatives.
In the third dichotomy, the birational image is either defined scheme-theoretically by quadrics or has an exceptional cubic requirement. The exceptional threefold containing it is P3 for g=3, a quadric hypersurface in P4 for g=4, a rational normal threefold scroll in the higher-genus trigonal case, or the cone over the Veronese surface in the plane-quintic case. For g≥5, trigonal sections come from an elliptic pencil with DE=3; the plane-quintic exception has
g=6,D=2B+Γ,B2=2, BΓ=1, Γ2=−2,∣B∣ basepoint-free.
(41)
All other birational cases have homogeneous ideal generated by quadrics. For g≥4, the exceptional ideals are generated by quadrics and cubics. For g=3, the image is a quartic hypersurface in P3 and its ideal needs its degree-four defining equation; that case must not be included in the cubic-generation assertion. The geometric alternatives, including the low-genus exceptions, are the plane-quintic exception to the third dichotomy; the higher-genus trigonal conclusion is also stated in the projective-model theorem.
The point of the final exception is that a plane quintic is not a trigonal curve. Its canonical model is the degree-two Veronese embedding of the plane curve, and its quadrics retain an ambient Veronese surface. Accordingly, one must include the cone-over-Veronese case instead of calling every failure of quadratic generation “trigonal.”
