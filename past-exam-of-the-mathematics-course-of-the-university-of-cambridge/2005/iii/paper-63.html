A smooth fiber bundle π:E→M with typical fiber F is locally a product: every base point has a neighborhood U and a local trivialization π−1(U)≅U×F commuting with projection to U. Its overlap maps act smoothly on F. A principal bundle with structure Lie group G has a free right G-action, each fiber is one orbit, and the local trivializations are equivariant for right multiplication on G. A smooth section of a fiber bundle is a map s:M→E with π∘s=idM​.
For a principal bundle, a global section of a fiber bundle gives the map
Φ:M×G⟶P,Φ(x,g)=s(x)g.
(1)
The free transitive action on each fiber makes Φ bijective. In a local trivialization, write s(x)=(x,a(x)); then Φ(x,g)=(x,a(x)g) and its inverse is (x,k)↦(x,a(x)−1k). Both are smooth and Φ is equivariant. Conversely, a product principal bundle has the section x↦(x,e). This proves a principal bundle is trivial exactly when it has a global section. The argument depends on the free transitive action: an arbitrary bundle section need not trivialize its bundle.
For an n-dimensional smooth manifold, the frame bundle has fiber over x consisting of ordered bases of Tx​M, equivalently linear isomorphisms u:Rn→Tx​M. Right composition by GL(n,R) makes it a principal bundle. If a metric has signature (p,q), its pseudo-orthonormal frame bundle consists of the isometries u:Rp,q→Tx​M, with structure group O(p,q). In the positive-definite case this is the usual orthonormal frame bundle. Choosing space and time orientations reduces the Lorentzian structure group to its corresponding connected subgroup.
For Minkowski spacetime, use a fixed origin and a standard Lorentzian frame. Every pair consisting of a point and a pseudo-orthonormal frame is the image of this reference pair under a unique affine isometry. Thus, for the full groups,
Frη​(R1,3)≅ISO(1,3)=R1,3⋊O(1,3),R1,3≅ISO(1,3)/O(1,3).​
(2)
Here the first expression denotes the total space of the orthonormal frame bundle, not merely the Lorentz group. Projection sends an affine isometry to the image of the reference point, and the right action of the stabilizer changes its frame.
Realize de Sitter spacetime of radius ℓ as ηAB​XAXB=ℓ2 in R1,4. The ambient orthogonal group is transitive on this hyperboloid. Its point stabilizer acts on the Lorentzian tangent space as O(1,3); choosing a tangent orthonormal frame removes that stabilizer completely. Consequently
dS4​≅O(1,4)/O(1,3),O(dS4​)≅O(1,4)⟶dS4​.​
(3)
For oriented, time-oriented frames, replace these groups by SO0​(1,4) and SO0​(1,3), and similarly use the proper orthochronous Poincaré group for Minkowski spacetime. The isometries are uniquely determined by their action on a point and its frame, which explains these bundle identifications.
Finally, choose a basis v1​,…,vn​ of the Lie algebra Te​G. The left-invariant vector fields Xi​(g)=(dLg​)e​vi​ form a basis at every g, because Lg​ is a diffeomorphism. They give a global section of a fiber bundle of the frame bundle, so that bundle is trivial. More explicitly,
TG≅G×g,(g,v)⟼(dLg​)e​v.​
(4)
Thus every Lie group is a parallelizable manifold; connectedness is not needed for this construction.

δSkin​=−∫d(δA)∧∗F=−∫δA∧d∗F−∫d(δA∧∗F).

δ(A∧F∧F)=δA∧F∧F+2A∧d(δA)∧F=3δA∧F∧F−2d(A∧δA∧F).
(6)

δS=∫δA∧(−d∗F+3cF∧F)+boundary terms,
(7)

d∗F=3cF∧F,dF=0.​

ΔS=c∫dλ∧F∧F=c∫d(λF∧F)=c∫∂M​λF∧F.

Differentiate the product with the first factor close to the identity. A small displacement of its x′ coordinate adds to x; a small y′ displacement adds to y; and a small z′ displacement changes (x,y,z) by (−x,y,1) times that displacement. The infinitesimal left actions therefore give the right-invariant vector fields
R1​=∂x​,R2​=∂y​,R3​=∂z​−x∂x​+y∂y​.
(13)
For right multiplication, regard (x′,y′,z′) as the fixed point and the second factor as infinitesimal. Its displacements are (e−z′δx,ez′δy,δz); relabeling the coordinates of the fixed point gives the left-invariant vector fields
L1​=e−z∂x​,L2​=ez∂y​,L3​=∂z​.
(14)
The naming distinction matters: generators of left multiplication are right invariant, whereas generators of right multiplication are left invariant.
All mixed Lie brackets of vector fields vanish. For example, R3​(e−z)=−e−z cancels L1​(−x)=−e−z in [L1​,R3​], and R3​(ez)=ez cancels L2​(y)=ez in [L2​,R3​]. [Li​,Rj​]=0for every i,j.​

A symplectic manifold is a smooth manifold P with a closed, nondegenerate two-form ω. Nondegeneracy forces dimP=2n. Examples are R2n with ω=∑i​dqi∧dpi​, an oriented surface with a nowhere-zero area form, and every cotangent bundle with its canonical form.
A Poisson manifold has a bivector Π such that {f,g}=Π(df,dg) is an antisymmetric bilinear Poisson bracket, is a derivation in each argument, and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Its rank need not be full or constant. The zero bivector gives a Poisson manifold on any smooth manifold, including an odd-dimensional or nonorientable one. A nonzero example on R3 is the Lie-Poisson bracket {xi​,xj​}=ϵijk​xk​: it has rank two off the origin and rank zero at the origin, so it does not arise from a symplectic form on the whole three-dimensional space. Even on R2, Π=x∂x​∧∂y​ is Poisson, since its Jacobi condition is a three-vector and hence vanishes, but its degeneracy on x=0 prevents it from being the inverse of a global symplectic form. A manifold carrying a degenerate Poisson structure might separately admit some other symplectic structure; these are distinct claims.
At each point of a symplectic manifold, linear nondegeneracy gives a basis with ω=∑i​ei∧fi. Its top wedge product of differential forms is
n!ωn​=e1∧f1∧⋯∧en∧fn=0.
(28)
Thus ωn/n! is a global nowhere-zero volume form and selects a consistent symplectic orientation. This proves every symplectic manifold is orientable without choosing a global frame.
Let π:T∗M→M and let a point of the cotangent bundle be (x,p) with p∈Tx∗​M. Define the canonical Liouville one-form intrinsically by θ(x,p)​(V)=p(dπ(V)). In local coordinates it is θ=pi​dqi, so
ω=−dθ=dqi∧dpi​.
(29)
This definition is global, dω=0 by d2=0, and its coordinate matrix has invertible blocks. Hence T∗M is symplectic for every smooth M, even if M itself is not orientable.
The coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector is
Πiℓ∂ℓ​Πjk+Πjℓ∂ℓ​Πki+Πkℓ∂ℓ​Πij=0.​
(30)
Necessity follows by applying the Jacobi identity to three coordinate functions. For arbitrary functions, the second-derivative terms in the Jacobiator cancel by antisymmetry of Π; the terms left are exactly this coefficient multiplying fi​gj​hk​, proving sufficiency.
For a symplectic form, take ιXf​​ω=df and {f,g}=ω(Xf​,Xg​). With these signs the matrix of Π is −ω−1. Differentiation gives ∂ℓ​Π=Π(∂ℓ​ω)Π. The cyclic expression above consequently equals
−ΠiaΠjbΠkc(∂a​ωbc​+∂b​ωca​+∂c​ωab​),
(31)
which vanishes because dω=0. This directly proves that a symplectic manifold defines a Poisson manifold.

Use ιXf​​ω=df and {f,g}=ω(Xf​,Xg​). For an actual infinitesimal left Lie group action, define ξP​(p)=dtd​​0​exp(tξ)⋅p. Its fundamental vector fields obey [ξP​,ηP​]=−[ξ,η]P​ with this convention.
Existence and its global obstruction. Preservation of the symplectic form gives LξP​​ω=0. By Cartan's magic formula, ιξP​​ω is closed. Locally it equals dμξ​; globally this requires its class in HdR1​(P) to vanish. If it does for every ξ, choose potentials linearly in ξ and define
⟨μ(p),ξ⟩=μξ​(p),dμξ​=ιξP​​ω.
(32)
This gives the component Hamiltonians of a weakly Hamiltonian action. The assumption in the question does not alone guarantee a global moment map: on T2 with ω=dx∧dy, translation in x preserves ω, but its contraction is dy, whose integral around the y circle is nonzero. It is not an exact one-form. Thus no global real-valued component Hamiltonian exists for that circle action. The intended construction is valid locally, or globally with this exactness condition, for example if HdR1​(P)=0.
Matching the Lie algebra. On each connected component, the moment-map equivariance obstruction is the constant
c(ξ,η)={μξ​,μη​}−μ[ξ,η]​.
(33)
Indeed, the identities [Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​ and [ξP​,ηP​]=−[ξ,η]P​ show that its differential vanishes. The Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket says
c([ξ,η],ζ)+c([η,ζ],ξ)+c([ζ,ξ],η)=0.
(34)
Changing μξ​ by a linear constant b(ξ) changes c to c−b([ξ,η]). Consequently the necessary and sufficient infinitesimal condition is the cocycle must be a coboundary, so that the constants can be chosen with
{μξ​,μη​}=μ[ξ,η]​.​
(35)
Equivalently its class in H2(g,R) vanishes. For a connected group this infinitesimal equivariance integrates to equivariance under its coadjoint action. A disconnected group can impose additional conditions. Here “coincides” means representing the specified Lie brackets; it does not guarantee injectivity when the group action has ineffective generators.
Proof using the Killing form. A nondegenerate Killing form B makes g semisimple. First, it is perfect: the B-orthogonal complement of [g,g] is the center by invariance, and the center is zero by nondegeneracy. For symplectic fields,
ι[ξ,η]P​​ω=d[ω(ξP​,ηP​)]
(36)
by Cartan's magic formula and the antihomomorphism sign. Every commutator therefore has an exact contraction. Perfectness supplies component Hamiltonians for every generator, even when P is not simply connected: this is Hamiltonian existence for a semisimple symplectic action.
It remains to eliminate the cocycle, not merely to name an existence theorem. Define a linear operator D on g by B(Dξ,η)=c(ξ,η). Antisymmetry of c makes D skew for B. Combining its cocycle identity with invariance of B gives
D[ξ,η]=[Dξ,η]+[ξ,Dη].
(37)
Choose Z∈g by B(Z,ξ)=tr(Dadξ​), possible because B is nondegenerate. Since D is a derivation, [D,adξ​]=adDξ​. Cyclicity of the matrix trace then gives
B([Z,ξ],η)​=B(Z,[ξ,η])=tr(D[adξ​,adη​])=tr([D,adξ​]adη​)=B(Dξ,η).​
(38)
Thus D=adZ​, and c(ξ,η)=B(Z,[ξ,η]). Taking b(ξ)=B(Z,ξ) removes c, proving the required condition. This applies to semisimple Lie groups, including SU(n) for n≥2, SO(3) and SL(n,R) for n≥2. It is nondegeneracy, not positive definiteness, that matters. Groups with a nonzero central Lie algebra, such as U(3), do not satisfy this Killing-form criterion, although particular actions can still have equivariant moment maps.
The isotropic oscillator. On T∗R3, put
zj​=2​xj​+ipj​​,ω=i∑j​dzj​∧dzˉj​,H=z†z.
(39)
The action z↦Uz, U∈U(3), preserves the Hermitian norm and the symplectic form, and therefore preserves H. A Hermitian matrix A parametrizes the Lie algebra generator ξ=−iA. Its unitary moment map of an isotropic oscillator is
μA​=z†Az,XμA​​z=−iAz.
(40)
Since {zj​,zˉk​}=−iδjk​, direct differentiation yields
{μA​,μB​}=−iz†[A,B]z=μ−i[A,B]​.​
(41)
The matrix −i[A,B] is Hermitian and corresponds precisely to [ξA​,ξB​]; no central defect remains. The full moment map can be identified, using the pairing with Hermitian matrices, with the rank-one matrix zz†. The central generator A=I gives μI​=H, whose flow is z(t)=e−itz(0). Globally U(3)=(SU(3)×U(1))/Z3​, so the “U(1) factor” refers to this central circle, rather than a literal direct-product decomposition.
The real orthogonal subgroup acts geometrically by (x,p)↦(Rx,Rp), the cotangent lift of a diffeomorphism of R3. If K is real antisymmetric and A=iK, then μA​=−xTKp. For Kv=Ω×v, this is Ω⋅(x×p), so its three components are the usual angular momentum Li​=ϵijk​xj​pk​.
The remaining six independent Hermitian directions can be taken as real symmetric matrices. Their moment functions span
Qij​=21​(xi​xj​+pi​pj​),Qij​=Qji​.
(42)

Their flows mix positions and momenta and are not cotangent lifts of the obvious spatial rotation action: for a real symmetric A, x˙=Ap and p˙​=−Ax, rather than x˙=Kx, p˙​=Kp. All nine quadratic functions commute with H, since the entire unitary group action preserves the oscillator Hamiltonian.

Geometric quantization. A classical symplectic manifold supplies observables as real functions, their Poisson bracket, and Hamiltonian flows. Geometric quantization seeks a Hilbert space of quantum states and operators that encode this structure, including 1=I and a commutator corresponding to the Poisson bracket. A direct prescription cannot make every classical product become an operator product without qualification; operator ordering and the choice of physically admissible observables matter. The aim is to build the state space and operators from the geometry rather than from a preferred coordinate chart.
For prequantization, with ιXf​​ω=df, choose a Hermitian line bundle L→P with a unitary connection on a vector bundle of curvature
F∇​=ℏi​ω.
(43)
Its first Chern class requires the integrality condition
[ω/(2πℏ)] lies in the image of H2(P;Z)→HdR2​(P).​
(44)
Equivalently the symplectic period on every closed integral two-cycle is a multiple of 2πℏ. Conversely an integral class supplies a line bundle, and a unitary connection can be adjusted by a one-form to have the required curvature. Locally choose θ with ω=−dθ; then ∇=d−iθ/ℏ. Potentials differing by an exact one-form change the local phase of the section. Integrality is precisely what makes these local descriptions glue consistently.
On sections the Kostant-Souriau prequantum operator is
f​=−iℏ∇Xf​​+f.
(45)
The curvature identity [∇X​,∇Y​]=∇[X,Y]​+F∇​(X,Y), together with [Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​, gives
[f​,g​]=iℏ{f,g}​.
(46)
Indeed the derivative part of the commutator is ℏ2∇X{f,g}​​, the curvature part is −iℏ{f,g}, and the two multiplication/derivative cross terms total 2iℏ{f,g}. These combine to the displayed operator identity. The prequantum inner product uses the Hermitian fiber metric and the symplectic volume form ωn/n!, with appropriate square-integrability and operator-domain conditions.
This is not yet the desired quantum state space: a prequantum section depends on all 2n phase-space variables, whereas a wavefunction normally depends on only n configuration variables. A polarization in geometric quantization is an involutive Lagrangian rank-n subbundle of TC​P. Polarized sections obey ∇X​s=0 along that distribution. The symplectic form vanishes on it, so its restricted curvature vanishes and these differential conditions are locally compatible.
For T∗Q, the vertical polarization is spanned locally by ∂pi​​. With θ=pi​dqi, polarized sections depend only on q, and the basic prequantum operators become
q​i=qi,p​i​=−iℏ∂qi​.​
(47)
Only observables whose Hamiltonian flows preserve the polarization act directly on this polarized space. A generic quadratic kinetic energy does not preserve the vertical polarization, so obtaining its quantum operator needs an additional construction rather than simply restricting every prequantum operator. A compatible complex structure can instead provide a Kähler polarization, yielding holomorphic-type states, as for oscillator variables. The Blattner-Kostant-Sternberg pairing compares suitable state spaces of different polarizations and can implement some transported observables.
Finding a useful global polarization is a genuine problem: it may not exist as a nonsingular real foliation; leaf holonomy can obstruct globally nonzero flat sections; singular leaves and compact leaves impose further conditions. A metaplectic correction uses half-forms to improve the inner product and coordinate transformation law and account for familiar zero-point shifts, when its additional square-root data exist. Thus prequantum integrality, polarization, Hilbert-space completion and observable domains are separate steps, each with geometric constraints.
Applications of Stokes's theorem. For an oriented manifold W with boundary and a differential form α of degree dimW−1,
∫W​dα=∫∂W​α
(48)
with the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. Locally the identity is integration by parts in a half-space; a partition of unity reduces the general manifold to that local calculation, and contributions along internal patch boundaries cancel. The Generalized Stokes theorem unifies the fundamental theorem of calculus, circulation/flux formulas and the relation between local conservation laws and conserved integrated charges.
For a charged brane with worldvolume W of dimension p+1, let X:W→M be its embedding, γ=X∗g its induced worldvolume metric, and A a (p+1)-form gauge potential with F=dA. Take
S[X]=−Tp​∫W​−γ​dp+1σ+q∫W​X∗A.

Under A↦A+dΛ, the Wess-Zumino brane coupling changes by q∫∂W​X∗Λ. It is invariant for a closed worldvolume, or for suitable boundary conditions; open branes can require compensating boundary terms.
For an embedding variation V=δX, differentiation of the pullback and Cartan's magic formula give
δ∫W​X∗A=∫W​X∗(LV​A)=∫W​X∗(ιV​F)+∫∂W​X∗(ιV​A).
(50)
Thus the interior force depends only on the gauge field strength. In components, define the worldvolume wave-map/mean-curvature vector by
Kμ=−γ​1​∂a​(−γ​γab∂b​Xμ)+Γρσμ​γab∂a​Xρ∂b​Xσ.
(51)
Varying the area term and integrating by parts gives δSarea​=Tp​∫W​−γ​Kμ​Vμ. Combining the two variations yields the field-strength force on a charged brane equation

It is gauge invariant because A no longer occurs. Its tangential projection vanishes identically by antisymmetry, consistently with worldvolume reparametrization invariance. At p=0, the chosen Lorentzian worldline convention has K=−X¨ in proper time and recovers the usual Lorentz-force sign.
For topological conservation, let the spatial manifold compactify to a closed oriented n-manifold Σ, and let a field U:Σ→N take values in a closed oriented n-dimensional target with normalized volume form Ω, ∫N​Ω=1. Then
Q=∫Σ​U∗Ω=degU∈Z.
(53)
This is the degree of a map between oriented manifolds: for a regular value it is the signed sum of inverse images. To relate the integral to that count, use a normalized target form supported near a regular value and change variables on its inverse-image neighborhoods. Any two normalized top forms on a connected closed oriented target differ by an exact form, whose pulled-back integral vanishes by the Generalized Stokes theorem. Hence the value is independent of this localization and equals the integer signed count.
If U varies smoothly in time, its spacetime pullback j=U∗Ω satisfies dj=U∗(dΩ)=0. Apply Stokes theorem to the spacetime slab between two slices: the difference of their charges is minus the flux through the spatial boundary. With no flux at infinity, Q is conserved independently of the field equations. Equivalently a smooth homotopy cannot change the degree. Singularities, altered boundary conditions or nonzero boundary flux are the ways the argument can fail.
For the Skyrme model, finite-energy fields approach U=I at spatial infinity, so R3 compactifies to S3 and U:S3→SU(2)≅S3. With the conventional target orientation choose
Ω=−24π21​tr(U−1dU)∧3.
(54)
This is a normalized closed target volume form. The resulting topological baryon number in the Skyrme model is
B=−24π21​∫R3​ϵijktr(Li​Lj​Lk​)d3x∈Z,Li​=U−1∂i​U.​
(55)
The corresponding identically conserved spacetime current is jμ=−(24π2)−1ϵμνρσtr(Lν​Lρ​Lσ​) in oriented flat coordinates. For the hedgehog U=cosf(r)I+ix⋅σsinf(r) with f(0)=π and f(∞)=0, substitution gives
B=−π2​∫0∞​f′(r)sin2f(r)dr=−π1​[f−21​sin2f]0∞​=1.
(56)
The Skyrmion therefore supplies a concrete topologically conserved unit charge. Topological conservation alone does not guarantee a stable finite-size static solution: the positive Skyrme term, with four derivatives, opposes collapse of the two-derivative energy. Under size rescaling, these contributions behave as E2​∝λ and E4​∝λ−1, allowing a nonzero equilibrium size while preserving the degree.
