For fixed t, put gt​(u)=(u−t)+k−1​, a truncated power function. Begin with distinct increasing knots. The two interpolation polynomials ℓi​(⋅,t) and ℓi+1​(⋅,t) agree at their k−1 shared knots. Their difference has degree at most k−1, so
ℓi+1​(x,t)−ℓi​(x,t)=ci​(t)∏j=1k−1​(x−ti+j​)=ci​(t)ωi​(x).
(19)
For k=1 the empty product is one and the same assertion holds for constant interpolants. The leading coefficient of a degree-at-most-k−1 interpolant on k nodes is its order-k−1 divided difference, by the Newton interpolation polynomial. Therefore
ci​(t)=[ti+1​,…,ti+k​]gt​−[ti​,…,ti+k−1​]gt​=(ti+k​−ti​)[ti​,…,ti+k​]gt​=Ni​(t).
(20)
This proves the Lee interpolation identity
ωi​(x)Ni​(t)=ℓi+1​(x,t)−ℓi​(x,t).​
(21)
The identity holds for every real x,t, with the usual convention for the order-zero truncated power. Admissible repeated knots are handled by the corresponding confluent divided differences and consistent one-sided limits at breakpoints; taking these limits preserves the Lee interpolation identity.
Sum over i to telescope:
∑i=1n​ωi​(x)Ni​(t)=ℓn+1​(x,t)−ℓ1​(x,t).
(22)
If tk​<t<tn+1​, the first k knot values of gt​ are zero, so ℓ1​=0. At the last k knots tn+1​,…,tn+k​, the function gt​ agrees with the polynomial (u−t)k−1, so uniqueness of polynomial interpolation gives ℓn+1​(x,t)=(x−t)k−1. Thus the Marsden identity is
(x−t)k−1=i=1∑n​ωi​(x)Ni​(t),tk​<t<tn+1​,x∈R.​
(23)
