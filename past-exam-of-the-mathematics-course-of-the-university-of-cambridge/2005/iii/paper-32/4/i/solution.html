The companion curve is E′:Y2=X(X2−2aX+a2−4b). The two-isogeny formula gives maps ϕ:E→E′ and ψ:E′→E satisfying ψϕ=[2].
For the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem in this setting, use the two-torsion square-class homomorphism
α(O)=1,α((0,0))=[b],α((x,y))=[x]∈Q∗/Q∗2,
(25)
and its counterpart α′ on E′. These are homomorphisms: a nonvertical line intersecting the cubic in three points has the product of their x-coordinates a square, by comparison of the constant term of the intersection cubic. For a line through (0,0), the product of the other two coordinates is b, explaining the special value there. The kernels are ψE′(Q) and ϕE(Q), respectively, as follows by solving the elliptic isogeny preimage quadratic and testing its discriminant for a square.
The prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent makes both images finite. Indeed at a prime not dividing b, a negative valuation of x makes the leading cubic term dominate, giving 2v(y)=3v(x); a positive valuation makes x2+ax+b a unit, giving 2v(y)=v(x). In either case v(x) is even. Thus the square class of x is supported only on primes dividing b, together with a possible sign. The same argument uses b′=a2−4b on E′. Since 2E=ψϕE, the two finite elliptic isogeny quotients imply that E(Q)/2E(Q) is finite.
Finite generation still needs a descent on height. Let hx​ be the logarithmic height of the x-coordinate. The duplication formula has a degree-four rational x-coordinate map with coprime numerator and denominator. Polynomial height estimates, with the resultant supplying the lower bound, give the height growth under a morphism of the projective line relation hx​(2P)=4hx​(P)+O(1). Thus the convergent telescoping limit
h(P)=21​limn→∞​4−nhx​(2nP)
(26)
defines the canonical height of an elliptic curve, without assuming finite generation. It is nonnegative, satisfies h(2P)=4h(P) and the height parallelogram identity, and differs from hx​/2 by a bounded amount. Consequently its bounded subsets of rational points are finite by the Northcott theorem.
