The Polyakov action has independent worldsheet metric γab​ and embedding fields Xμ. Varying the metric before imposing a gauge gives
Tab​=α′1​(∂a​X⋅∂b​X−21​γab​γcd∂c​X⋅∂d​X)=0.
(24)
Worldsheet diffeomorphisms and Weyl transformations allow conformal gauge γab​=e2ωηab​ locally. The Weyl factor then cancels out of the classical action, leaving
SX​=4πα′1​∫dτdσ(X˙2−X′2),(∂τ2​−∂σ2​)Xμ=0.
(25)
These are free two-dimensional scalar fields, but the surviving metric equations require
(X˙+X′)2=(X˙−X′)2=0.​
(26)
Equivalently, X˙2+X′2=0 and X˙⋅X′=0. Gauge fixing does not license dropping these Virasoro constraints.
For an ordinary open string, the boundary conditions relate the two chiral sectors. With α0μ​=2α′​kμ, their independent Fourier generators are
Lm​=21​∑r∈Z​αm−r​⋅αr​.
(27)
The classical oscillator Poisson brackets {αmμ​,αnν​}=−imημνδm+n,0​ give the classical Virasoro constraint algebra
{Lm​,Ln​}=−i(m−n)Lm+n​.
(28)
Thus they are first-class constraints. In the quantum theory one applies normal ordering to the product and uses [αmμ​,αnν​]=mημνδm+n,0​. The oscillator commutator gives
[Lm​,αnμ​]=−nαm+nμ​,[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12D​(m3−m)δm+n,0​.
(29)
For example, for m>0 the extra vacuum term is (D/2)∑r=1m−1​r(m−r)=D(m3−m)/12. This exhibits the Virasoro central extension rather than concealing it in a classical constraint equation. The matter central charge is D; the worldsheet ghost fields have charge −26. Quantum consistency sets D=26 and the string intercept to one.
In old covariant string quantization the physical-state Virasoro conditions for an open string are
Lm​∣Ψ⟩=0(m>0),(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0,L0​=α′k2+N.
(30)
Physical string states are then identified modulo physical null string states. One does not impose both positive and negative generators as annihilation conditions: their central extension would obstruct that prescription. The resulting null-state quotient of a string agrees with the transverse light-cone spectrum at nonzero momentum.
At N=0, the oscillator vacuum ∣k⟩ is a single spacetime scalar field with α′M2=−1: the bosonic tachyon. At N=1, write ∣Ψ⟩=ϵμ​α−1μ​∣k⟩. The zero-mode equation gives k2=0, and L1​ gives k⋅ϵ=0. The null descendant L−1​∣k⟩=2α′​k⋅α−1​∣k⟩ identifies ϵ∼ϵ+λk. This leaves 24 physical polarizations of a massless gauge vector.
At N=2, use the level-two open-string polarization decomposition
∣Ψ⟩=(21​hμν​α−1μ​α−1ν​+bμ​α−2μ​)∣k⟩,hμν​=hνμ​.
(31)
The mass is M2=1/α′. The oscillator commutators give all the nontrivial positive-mode conditions:
2α′​kμhμν​+2bν​=0,21​hμμ​+22α′​k⋅b=0.
(32)
Higher positive modes annihilate this level. To see the quotient explicitly, the 25 transverse-parameter null states L−1​(ξ⋅α−1​∣k⟩), with k⋅ξ=0, shift
Δhμν​=2α′​(kμ​ξν​+kν​ξμ​),Δbμ​=ξμ​.
(33)
The additional level-two scalar Virasoro null state (L−2​+23​L−12​)∣k⟩ shifts
Δhμν​=ημν​+6α′kμ​kν​,Δbμ​=25​2α′​kμ​.
(34)
Substitution into the two physical equations verifies the null-state conditions; the second residual is (D−26)/2. Because k is timelike, these null shifts span all components of b, so choose a representative with b=0. The physical conditions then say kμhμν​=0 and hμμ​=0. In the rest frame this is a traceless second-rank tensor in 25 spatial dimensions, with 25⋅26/2−1=324 polarizations. The light-cone basis independently gives 24 states α−2i​∣k⟩ and 24⋅25/2=300 symmetric states α−1i​α−1j​∣k⟩. Hence
α′M2=−1,0,1:1 scalar,24 vector polarizations,324 massive spin-two polarizations.​
(35)
For the fermionic extension, an explicit convention prevents ambiguity in the signs. Keep worldsheet signature (−,+) and use
ρ0=(0i​−i0​),ρ1=(0i​i0​),{ρa,ρb}=−2ηab.
(36)
Take ψ=(ψ1​,ψ2​)T and the constant parameter ϵ=(ϵ1​,ϵ2​)T to be real Grassmann-odd Majorana components, with ψˉ​=ψTρ0. Put ∂±​=∂τ​±∂σ​. Apart from the common factor 1/(4πα′), the combined Lagrangian is
L=∂+​X⋅∂−​X+iψ1​⋅∂+​ψ1​+iψ2​⋅∂−​ψ2​.
(37)
The rigid transformations can be written
δX=ϵˉψ,δψ=−iρa∂a​Xϵ,
(38)
or, in components,
δX=i(ϵ2​ψ1​−ϵ1​ψ2​),δψ1​=−ϵ2​∂−​X,δψ2​=ϵ1​∂+​X.
(39)
For the ϵ2​ part, the bosonic variation is iϵ2​(∂+​ψ1​⋅∂−​X+∂+​X⋅∂−​ψ1​), while the fermionic variation is −iϵ2​∂−​X⋅∂+​ψ1​+iϵ2​ψ1​⋅∂+​∂−​X. Anticommuting the constant parameter past the fermion is essential in the second term. The first terms cancel. The other chiral component works in the same way, giving the chiral proof of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry
δL=iϵ2​∂−​(ψ1​⋅∂+​X)−iϵ1​∂+​(ψ2​⋅∂−​X).​
(40)
Thus the action is invariant off shell up to a boundary term. The transformation is a genuine supersymmetry: on X its commutator for parameters ϵ,ζ is the translation
[δϵ​,δζ​]X=2i(ϵ2​ζ2​∂−​+ϵ1​ζ1​∂+​)X.
(41)
On ψ the same translation follows using ∂+​ψ1​=0 and ∂−​ψ2​=0; the remaining cross terms are proportional to these Dirac equations. This is on-shell closure of rigid worldsheet supersymmetry. On a surface with boundary, compatible endpoint conditions must make the boundary flux vanish. On a compact worldsheet, a constant parameter must preserve the spin structure; the local identity alone does not make a constant transformation compatible with every antiperiodic sector.
The fermions contribute to the worldsheet stress-energy tensor and produce a fermionic superconformal current. In holomorphic normalization with ψμ(z)ψν(w)∼ημν/(z−w),
T=−α′1​:∂X⋅∂X:−21​:ψ⋅∂ψ:,qquadG=iα′2​​:ψ⋅∂X:.
(42)
The free-field contractions give G(z)G(w)∼(2c/3)/(z−w)3+2T(w)/(z−w) and conformal weight 3/2 for G, where c=D+D/2=3D/2. Consequently the modes obey the N=1 super-Virasoro algebra
[Lm​,Ln​][Lm​,Gr​]{Gr​,Gs​}​=(m−n)Lm+n​+12c​(m3−m)δm+n,0​,=(2m​−r)Gm+r​,=2Lr+s​+3c​(r2−41​)δr+s,0​.​
(43)
Dropping the central terms gives the classical graded constraint algebra. Integer r gives the Ramond sector; half-integer r gives the Neveu–Schwarz sector. In the displayed conformal-plane convention G02​=L0​−c/24 in the Ramond sector. Shifting to the Ramond vacuum-normal-ordered zero mode gives the usual Ramond supercurrent zero-mode square G02​=L0(R)​.
Rigid symmetry of matter already supplies these currents, but promoting their vanishing to new gauge constraints requires coupling to worldsheet supergravity and varying its worldsheet gravitino. In superconformal gauge one retains T=G=0. The fermionic current constraints and their physical-state conditions extend the bosonic Virasoro conditions. The ghost charges are −26+11=−15, so cancellation with 3D/2 gives the critical dimension of the RNS superstring D=10; the NS and Ramond intercepts are respectively 1/2 and zero. This distinguishes the global supersymmetry established above from its locally gauged string completion.
