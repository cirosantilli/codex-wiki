Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2005 / iii / Paper 54 / 4 / a / Solution

Use the critical ten-dimensional RNS string and light-cone gauge in string theory, leaving eight transverse bosons and eight transverse fermions. The bosonic oscillators α−mi​ have integer m>0. In the Neveu–Schwarz sector the fermionic oscillators b−ri​ have positive half-integer r, while in the Ramond sector the fermionic oscillators d−mi​ have integer m, including zero modes. Their anticommutators are {bri​,bsj​}=δijδr+s,0​ and {dmi​,dnj​}=δijδm+n,0​. The level and mass formulas are
NNS​NR​​=m>0∑​α−mi​αmi​+r>0∑​rb−ri​bri​,=m>0∑​(α−mi​αmi​+md−mi​dmi​),​α′M2α′M2​=NNS​−21​,=NR​.​
(44)
Before projection the NS ground state is a tachyon of mass squared −1/(2α′). Its oscillator tower describes spacetime bosons. The Ramond zero-mode Clifford algebra {2​d0i​,2​d0j​}=2δij makes the Ramond vacuum a 16-dimensional spinor of SO(8), split into the two chiral eight-dimensional spinors. The Ramond tower describes spacetime fermions; there is no Ramond tachyon.
Choose the supersymmetric GSO projection. In the NS sector it keeps states with an odd number of fermionic creation operators, eliminating the zero-oscillator tachyon. In the Ramond sector it fixes the combined parity of the zero-mode chirality and the nonzero-mode fermion number. Thus the Ramond vacuum retains one chirality; adding an odd number of nonzero-mode fermions requires the opposite zero-mode chirality. The projected towers both have α′M2=0,1,2,…. Here the counts refer to oscillator polarizations for a single endpoint multiplicity; any common Chan-Paton multiplicity multiplies both counts.
At the first retained mass level, M2=0, the NS states are b−1/2i​∣0;k⟩ for i=1,…,8, furnishing the eight polarizations of the ten-dimensional gauge vector. The Ramond ground state is one chiral spinor with eight physical polarizations. Hence
M2=0:8 bosons=8 fermions.​
(45)
These form the massless supersymmetric vector multiplet.
At the next mass level, α′M2=1, the NS level is 3/2. The odd-fermion condition leaves exactly
b−3/2i​∣0⟩,α−1i​b−1/2j​∣0⟩,b−1/2i​b−1/2j​b−1/2k​∣0⟩(i<j<k).
(46)
Their counts are 8, 8⋅8=64 and (38​)=56. Anticommutation requires distinct indices in the third family. There are no other partitions of 3/2 with odd fermion number, so these are all 128 bosons. They assemble into the massive little-group SO(9) representations of dimensions 44 and 84: a traceless symmetric two-tensor and a three-form.
At Ramond level one, the only possibilities are α−1i​∣s⟩ and d−1i​∣s′⟩. The spinors ∣s⟩ and ∣s′⟩ each have eight components and have opposite zero-mode chirality because the fermionic oscillator reverses the GSO parity. Consequently there are 64+64=128 fermions. They form a gamma-traceless vector-spinor of the massive little group: a vector-spinor has 9⋅16 components and its gamma trace removes 16. Therefore the first massive level of the open RNS string has
α′M2=1:8+64+56=128 bosons=64+64=128 fermions.​
(47)
This is the open-string mass normalization; the same chiral oscillator count used in a closed string would correspond to α′M2=4.
