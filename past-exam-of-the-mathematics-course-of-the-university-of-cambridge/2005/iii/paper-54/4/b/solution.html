Count oscillator polarizations at fixed center-of-mass momentum. In a critical closed bosonic string, each chiral sector has 24 transverse oscillator species. A species at frequency m can be occupied 0,1,2,… times, so its contribution is (1−qm)−1. The full chiral generating function is therefore
F(q)=∑N≥0​dN​qN=∏m≥1​(1−qm)−24.
(48)
The two sectors are independent, while closed-string level matching restricts their oscillator numbers to the same N. Thus the number of states at the closed-string mass level is gN​=dN2​, not d2N​.
Put q=e−β, with β>0. The Dedekind eta function obeys
η(2πiβ​)=e−β/24∏m≥1​(1−e−βm),η(−1/τ)=−iτ​η(τ).
(49)
The transformed argument 2πi/β has large imaginary part, so its product approaches one and η(2πi/β)∼e−π2/(6β). Hence
F(e−β)∼(2πβ​)12exp(β4π2​−β).
(50)
This establishes the singular exponential as well as its power prefactor. The corrections to the transformed eta product are exponentially small in 1/β.
The Cauchy coefficient formula, followed by q=e−β, gives a steepest-descent integral with leading form
dN​∼2πi1​∫dβ(2πβ​)12exp((N−1)β+β4π2​).
(51)

β∗​=n​2π​,S(β∗​)=4πn​,S′′(β∗​)=πn3/2​.
(52)

2πS′′(β∗​)​(β∗​/2π)12​=2​1​n−27/4.

The point q=1 supplies the largest exponential contribution. At another fixed root of unity of order k>1, the corresponding modular singularity gives a smaller exponential e4πn​/k, so it does not change this leading asymptotic. Squaring the chiral result gives the large-level degeneracy of a closed bosonic string
dN​∼2​1​(N−1)−27/4e4πN−1​,gN​∼21​(N−1)−27/2e8πN−1​.​
(54)
Replacing N−1 by N gives the equivalent usual leading asymptotic. The power −27/2 matters: the two-sector degeneracy is not just an unspecified exponential.
Since M=2(N−1)/α′​, the exponential is eβH​M with
βH​=4πα′​,TH​=4πα′​1​.​
(55)
Here Boltzmann's constant is one. Coarse-graining the levels into a density per unit rest mass introduces the Jacobian dN/dM=α′M/2. Consequently
ρ(M)∼CM−26eβH​M,C=225α′−25/2.
(56)
This is distinct from the degeneracy per level, whose power in M is −27. The internal-state contribution to a canonical partition function behaves at large mass as
Zinternal​(β)∼∫∞dMCM−26e−(β−βH​)M.
(57)
It converges for β>βH​ and diverges for β<βH​. At β=βH​, this particular internal rest-mass integral converges because of the power M−26; one should not discard the prefactor and claim divergence there without specifying the full thermodynamic ensemble. For example, including continuous momenta in 25 spatial dimensions multiplies the high-mass integrand by a factor proportional to M25/2, still leaving convergence of this ideal one-string integral at the endpoint, although sufficiently high derivatives are singular. Multi-string effects, volume and interactions affect the detailed limiting thermodynamics.
The leading microcanonical entropy grows as S(E)∼βH​E, with logarithmic corrections, so increasingly large energies are stored in increasingly excited, long strings rather than in an arbitrarily hot ordinary gas. The Hagedorn temperature is the boundary of canonical convergence from above in inverse temperature; extrapolating the free-string canonical description to T>TH​ fails. This counting does not settle the interacting phase above that scale. Also, the bosonic ground-state tachyon is a separate vacuum instability; the high-level asymptotic is meaningful as a formal spectrum calculation without asserting a stable bosonic thermal vacuum.
