Take positive volumetric flow rate in the direction of increasing x. For an arterial pressure wave, the characteristic admittance of an arterial wave gives
qB​=Y(PB​E−−PBR​E+).​
(1)
The backward wave contributes the opposite flow sign. Write pj​ for each junction's complex pressure amplitude, suppressing the common eiωt factor, and put r=RY, Cβ​=cosβ, Sβ​=sinβ. The topology is the two paths K→L→N and K→M→N; their intermediate and common distal junctions each have a hydraulic resistance. The coordinate convention in the source puts the upstream end at −ℓ and the downstream end at zero in each segment. Consequently the wave transfer matrix of an arterial segment is
(pu​qu​​)=(Cβ​iYSβ​​iSβ​/YCβ​​)(pd​qd​​).
(2)
Equivalently qu​=Y(Cβ​pu​−pd​)/(iSβ​) and qd​=Y(pu​−Cβ​pd​)/(iSβ​), with their continuous limits understood when Sβ​=0.
Symmetry gives pL​=pM​=ps​. Distal mass conservation and the resistive relation QN​=pN​/R give
2iSβ​Y​(ps​−Cβ​pN​)=RpN​​,ps​=(Cβ​+2riSβ​​)pN​.
(3)
At L, the incoming flow supplies both its bed and segment D, so
pK​=(2Cβ​+riSβ​​)ps​−pN​.
(4)
Choose the incident amplitude PA​ at K. If PAR​ is the reflected amplitude in vessel A, pressure continuity equation gives pK​=PA​+PAR​, while inlet flow is 2Y(PA​−PAR​)=2Y(2PA​−pK​). Equating this to the two outgoing segment flows yields
ps​=eiβpK​−2iSβ​PA​.
(5)
Eliminating ps​,pK​ from these three relations gives the wave transmission through a resistively loaded arterial loop result
QN​=PA​RY2eiβ(2RY+1)2+e−iβ(2RY−1)8e−iβ​.​
(6)
For large r at fixed phase, pN​=2PA​e−2iβ+O(r−1) and pM​=Cβ​pN​+O(r−1). Hence
QN​∼R2PA​​e−2iβ,QM​=QL​∼R2PA​​e−2iβcosβ.​
(7)
These leading terms must not be used as nonzero denominators near a phase at which the normal intermediate flow vanishes.
