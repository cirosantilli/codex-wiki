Use the pressure scale ρU2, and denote the scaled transverse velocity by v. The steady incompressible flow equations under the prescribed coordinate scaling are

(27)
The no-slip boundary condition is u=v=0 at y=1 and at the lower wall y=ϵF(x), taking F=0 upstream. Far upstream, u→U0​=6y(1−y), v→0, and px​→−12λ/R. These are the dimensional Navier-Stokes equations divided by the streamwise and transverse inertial scales, respectively; the transverse equation's λ−2 is essential.
A consistent nonlinear wall-layer regime can be chosen as
Rλ​=ϵ3,R1/7≪λ≪R,ϵ≪1.​
(28)
Near a wall the oncoming plane Poiseuille flow is linear in distance, so a layer of thickness ϵ has u=O(ϵ) and v=O(ϵ2). Its inertia is O(ϵ2); its transverse viscous term is (λ/R)O(ϵ−1)=O(ϵ2), explaining the distinguished balance. The resulting pressure perturbation is O(ϵ2). In the core the induced transverse pressure variation is O(ϵ/λ2), negligible compared with that pressure perturbation when λ2ϵ≫1, equivalently λ≫R1/7. Also λ≪R makes both the indentation and the viscous boundary layer thin. Streamwise viscous derivatives are smaller still. Thus the core perturbation is inviscid at first order and pressure is common to the two wall layers at their leading order.
Write u=U0​+ϵu1​+⋯ and v=ϵv1​+⋯. The core pressure gradient only enters at order ϵ2, so first-order continuity equation and streamwise momentum give
u1x​+v1y​=0,U0​u1x​+U0′​v1​=0.

Matching to the core near the lower wall gives U∼6[z+F+A], so Hlower​=F+A.
For the upper boundary layer, set y=1−ϵz, u=ϵU, v=−ϵ2V. The same equations, wall data and common P(x) result, but the core matching gives U∼6[z−A], so Hupper​=−A. The two pressure-driven wall-layer problems have the same upstream shear, wall conditions and pressure forcing. Under the question's uniqueness assumption their solutions coincide, and their displacement limits must coincide. Thus the symmetric viscous interaction in an indented Poiseuille channel gives
F+A=−A,A=−F/2,Hlower​=Hupper​=F/2.​
