A uniform spatial translation of the rolls changes ϕ by a constant, so a local phase modulation equation cannot depend on ϕ itself. Reflection across the transverse axis acts as Y↦−Y; reflection across the roll coordinate acts as ϕ↦−ϕ, after choosing a reflection-symmetric roll profile. The right-hand side must therefore be odd in ϕ and even under Y reflection.
At the lowest orders, ϕYY​ and ϕYYYY​ have these symmetries. The terms ϕY2​, ϕY​ϕYYY​ and ϕYY2​ are even in ϕ and are forbidden; ϕY3​ is odd under Y reflection and is forbidden. The first nonlinear derivative term at this order is ϕY2​ϕYY​. Near the transverse threshold use the quartic-time transverse phase scaling: the small coefficient of ϕYY​, the fourth derivative and this cubic term have the same asymptotic order. This gives
ϕT​=λϕYY​−γϕYYYY​−αϕY2​ϕYY​.​
(4)
The symmetry arguments determine the allowed terms, not their coefficients. A well-posed short-wave regularization requires γ>0. The zigzag instability occurs for λ<0, since a Fourier mode grows at −λk2−γk4. With p=ϕY​, differentiating gives
pT​=∂Y2​(λp−γpYY​−3α​p3).
(5)
For λ,α<0 the preferred uniform slopes satisfy p2=3λ/α; their slope diffusion coefficient is λ−αp2=−2λ>0. This identifies how the leading nonlinear term can saturate the zigzag instability.

Write c=(1−a−b)/a. The admissible equilibrium points are O=(0,0), E=(1−a,0) when a≤1, and P=(b,c) when a+b≤1. The Jacobian matrix is
J(x,y)=((1−a−2x)/a−yy/b​−xx/b−1​).
(17)
At O the eigenvalues are (1−a)/a and −1; at E they are −(1−a)/a and (1−a−b)/b. At a positive coexistence equilibrium point,
J(P)=(−b/ac/b​−b0​),trJ=−b/a<0,detJ=c>0.
(18)
It is therefore asymptotically stable. It is a node for b2>4a(1−a−b), a focus for the reverse inequality, and has repeated real eigenvalues at equality. The node–focus change does not alter the attracting equilibrium point's topology.
There are three open physical parameter regions:
  • a>1: extinction. Only O is admissible and it is a sink.
  • 0<a<1, a+b>1: predator extinction. O is a saddle, and E is a sink.
  • a+b<1: coexistence. O and E are saddles on the boundary, while P is a sink.
The axes are invariant. On x=0, y decays exponentially; on y=0, prey obey the logistic differential equation. Positivity follows because each right-hand side has its population as a factor. Also x is bounded by logistic comparison, while W=x+by satisfies W˙=(1−a)x/a−x2/a−by, so all forward trajectories are bounded. The Dulac function 1/(xy) has divergence −1/(ay)<0 in the interior, excluding periodic orbits by the Bendixson-Dulac criterion.
In the extinction region, x˙≤−(a−1)x/a, and then y˙​/y=x/b−1 is eventually negative. In the prey-only region, the upper bound limsupx≤1−a<b implies predator decay and convergence of interior prey to 1−a. In the coexistence region the Lyapunov function
V=x−b−blog(x/b)+b[y−c−clog(y/c)]
(19)
has V˙=−(x−b)2/a≤0. Its positive-quadrant sublevel sets are compact, and the only invariant subset with x=b also has y=c. The LaSalle invariance principle proves convergence of every interior trajectory to P. These facts justify the complete qualitative phase portraits, not just their local arrows.
At a=1, O and E meet in a transcritical bifurcation; positive prey decay algebraically. At a+b=1 with a<1, E and P exchange stability. On its centre manifold, x=b−ay+O(y2) gives y˙​=−ay2/b+O(y3), so the physically positive side is still attracting. There is no interior Hopf bifurcation since the coexistence trace never vanishes.

For ν=0, the undivided equations must be used; a nonzero equilibrium point in this fixed rotating frame requires V=0 and z=μ/U>0. If U=V=0, nonzero equilibrium points require μ2=ν2 and a compatible phase, with no cubic amplitude selection.
Strong enough forcing can overcome the nonlinear frequency shift, lock the sum phase to the external signal and produce a standing wave at half the forcing frequency. The difference phase remains free through spatial translation. The amplitude inequality is an existence condition, not a stability theorem: attraction requires the linearized forced amplitude equations, and nonlinear coefficients have not been restricted enough here to promise stability of every root.
