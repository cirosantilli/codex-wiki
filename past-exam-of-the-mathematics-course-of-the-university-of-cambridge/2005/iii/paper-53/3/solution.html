At momentum transfers small compared with MW​, the two weak charged current vertices each carry g/(22​) when written with (1−γ5​), and the W boson propagator reduces to a local factor proportional to 1/MW2​. Matching their product to the Fermi interaction gives, at tree level,
2​GF​​=8MW2​g2​,GF​=2​v21​(MW​=gv/2).​
(16)
The Fermi constant is thus determined by the electroweak coupling and symmetry-breaking scale; the four-fermion description is a low-energy effective interaction.
Only the cross term JL†​JH​ can annihilate the pion and create the stated charged-lepton/antineutrino pair. Its leptonic matrix element is uˉe​(k)γμ(1−γ5​)vν​(q). Lorentz covariance permits a vacuum-to-spin-zero current matrix element only proportional to pμ. At rest this has only a temporal component. The vacuum is parity-even and the pion is parity-odd, while VH0​ is parity-even, so ⟨0∣VH0​∣π⟩ equals its own negative and vanishes. Lorentz covariance then makes the full vector matrix element vanish. The temporal axial current is parity-odd and is allowed: this is the vacuum-to-pseudoscalar current selection rule.
The minus sign in JH​=VH​−AH​ cancels the interaction's minus sign, up to an irrelevant common S-matrix phase. Therefore
M=2​GF​​uˉe​γμ(1−γ5​)vν​⟨0∣AHμ​∣π−⟩=iGF​Fπ​cosθC​pμ​uˉe​γμ(1−γ5​)vν​.
(17)
The pion decay constant convention here includes the given 2​ in its current matrix element. Momentum conservation and the external Dirac equations give p=k+q, uˉe​k=me​uˉe​, qvν​=0. Since q(1−γ5​)=(1+γ5​)q, the neutrino term vanishes and
M=iGF​Fπ​cosθC​me​uˉe​(1−γ5​)vν​.​
(18)
This is helicity suppression: the amplitude vanishes in the zero charged-lepton-mass limit despite the larger available phase space.
For the spin sum, the Dirac adjoint of 1−γ5​ is 1+γ5​, and the completeness relations give
s∑​∣M∣2​=GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​tr[(k+me​)(1−γ5​)q(1+γ5​)]=2GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​tr[(k+me​)q(1+γ5​)]=8GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​k⋅q=4GF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​me2​(mπ2​−me2​).​
(19)
The chiral projectors automatically eliminate the unused neutrino helicity when the full massless completeness relation is inserted. There is no initial spin average for a spin-zero pion.
In the pion rest frame, let r=∣k∣=∣q∣, E=r2+me2​​. After integrating the spatial delta function and angles, the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space becomes
Φ2​=4π1​∫0∞​Err2​δ(mπ​−E−r)dr.
(20)
The root is r∗​=(mπ2​−me2​)/(2mπ​) and E∗​=(mπ2​+me2​)/(2mπ​). The absolute derivative of the delta-function argument is r∗​/E∗​+1=mπ​/E∗​. Thus Φ2​=r∗​/(4πmπ​). Multiplying by the spin sum and the initial factor 1/(2mπ​) yields the leptonic pseudoscalar decay width
Γ(π−→e−νˉe​)=4πGF2​Fπ2​cos2θC​​mπ​me2​(1−mπ2​me2​​)2.​
(21)
If the decay constant were instead defined by a matrix element ifπ​pμ without 2​, then fπ​=2​Fπ​ and the width coefficient would be fπ2​/(8π); mixing these conventions would introduce a factor-of-two error.
The same derivation for a muon cancels the common hadronic and weak factors in the pion electron-to-muon decay ratio:
Re/μ(0)​=mμ2​me2​​(1−mμ2​/mπ2​1−me2​/mπ2​​)2≈1.28×10−4.​
(22)
The numerical value uses approximate masses 0.511,105.66,139.57 MeV. Agreement with the corresponding Standard Model prediction tests lepton universality and the chiral, helicity-suppressing structure of the weak charged current, rather than the poorly calculable hadronic decay constant. A difference in the electron and muon weak couplings would multiply the ratio by their squared coupling ratio. Precision agreement must be assessed after radiative corrections, with matching photon-inclusive experimental definitions; the displayed tree-level ratio is not itself the radiatively corrected precision prediction.
