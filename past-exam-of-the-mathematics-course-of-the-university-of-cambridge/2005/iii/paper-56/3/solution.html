Use Euclidean coordinates (x1,x2,x3,x4) and orientation dx1∧dx2∧dx3∧dx4. For Dμ​=∂μ​+Aμ​, the gauge curvature is Fμν​=[Dμ​,Dν​]. The ASDYM equations are F=−∗F, equivalently
F12​+F34​=0,F13​−F24​=0,F14​+F23​=0.
(21)
Put w=x1+ix2, z=x3+ix4, with ∂w​=(∂1​−i∂2​)/2 and similarly for z. The bars label the complex conjugate coordinates on the Euclidean real slice; after complexification they can be treated as independent coordinates. A Lax pair for the anti-self-dual Yang-Mills equations is
(Dw​−λDzˉ​)Ψ=0,(Dz​+λDwˉ​)Ψ=0,λ∈CP1.​
(22)
At infinity rescale the operators and use the other affine coordinate. Their commutator is
[Dw​−λDzˉ​,Dz​+λDwˉ​]=Fwz​+λ(Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​)+λ2Fwˉzˉ​.
(23)
Compatibility for every λ is therefore precisely Fwz​=Fwˉzˉ​=0 and Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=0. To verify the real component signs,
Fwz​=41​{F13​−F24​−i(F14​+F23​)},Fwwˉ​+Fzzˉ​=2i​(F12​+F34​).
(24)
The conjugate equation supplies the remaining real components. This is compatibility of an overdetermined system, not an additional field equation on Ψ for only one value of the spectral parameter.
Impose invariance under translations in x1,x2,x3, writing t=x4. In a symmetry-adapted gauge the potentials depend only on t. A further t-dependent Yang-Mills gauge transformation sets A4​=0 locally, by solving the usual parallel transport ordinary differential equation; it does not reintroduce spatial dependence. Then
Fab​=[Aa​,Ab​],Fa4​=−A˙a​.
(25)
The three real equations above become A˙1​=[A2​,A3​], A˙2​=[A3​,A1​] and A˙3​=[A1​,A2​], namely
A˙a​=21​ϵabc​[Ab​,Ac​].​
(26)
These are the Nahm equations. The orientation chosen above gives the requested plus sign; reversing orientation exchanges self-duality of gauge curvature and anti-self-duality of gauge curvature. Translation invariance here is a local dimensional reduction and is not a claim of finite four-dimensional action over all three translation directions.
To prove conservation, abbreviate P=A1​+iA2​, Q=A1​−iA2​ and R=A3​, so A(λ)=P+2Rλ−Qλ2 and B(λ)=−iR+iQλ. Expanding the commutator, including the cancellation of the cubic term, gives
[A(λ),B(λ)]=−i[P,R]+i[P,Q]λ+i[R,Q]λ2.
(27)
The Nahm equations give
P˙=−i[P,R],2R˙=i[P,Q],−Q˙​=i[R,Q].
(28)
Thus the polynomial Lax representation of the Nahm equations is
A˙(λ)=[A(λ),B(λ)].​
(29)
Take a finite-dimensional matrix representation of the Lie algebra and its complexification. For every positive integer p, differentiating the product and using cyclicity of the matrix trace gives
dtd​Tr(A(λ)p)=pTr(A(λ)p−1[A(λ),B(λ)])=p{Tr(ApB)−Tr(BAp)}=0.
(30)
This proves the trace invariants of a Lax equation directly. Since Tr(A(λ)p) is a polynomial of degree at most 2p in the affine coordinate λ, its derivative vanishes identically only if the derivative of every coefficient vanishes. Therefore every trace-polynomial coefficient is independent of t. At projective infinity these polynomials are sections of O(2p); they need not be constant functions of λ on the whole projective sphere.
