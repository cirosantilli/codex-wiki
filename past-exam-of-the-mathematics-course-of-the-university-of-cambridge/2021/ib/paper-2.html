A subset S⊆M freely generates M when every m∈M has a unique expression
m=∑s∈S​rs​s
(46)
with rs​∈R and only finitely many nonzero coefficients. Equivalently, S is a basis of a module and M is the free module on S.
If S freely generates M and f:S→N is any function, define
ϕ(∑s​rs​s)=∑s​rs​f(s).
(47)
Unique coordinates make ϕ well defined; it is an R-module homomorphism and is the only extension of f. Conversely, apply the proposed universal property to the free module F=R(S) and the inclusion of S into M. It gives maps F→M and M→F extending the corresponding functions on S. Uniqueness makes both composites identity maps, so M≅F and S freely generates M. This is the universal property of a free module.
Now let T generate the free module M and ∣T∣=m. Choose a maximal ideal m of the nontrivial ring R. Then k=R/m is a field, and
M/mM
(48)
is a k-vector space. The images of a basis S of M form a vector-space basis, while the images of T span it. Therefore
∣S∣=dimk​(M/mM)≤m.
(49)
In particular S is finite. Applying this result in both directions to two finite bases shows that they have equal cardinality, the rank of a free module rkM.
A Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain. By the submodule theorem for free modules over a principal ideal domain, every submodule N of the finite-rank free module M is free. Since a basis of M generates it, the preceding inequality applied in the standard proof gives
