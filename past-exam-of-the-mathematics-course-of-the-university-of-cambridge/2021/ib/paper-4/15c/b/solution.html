The Hamiltonian is the sum of three commuting one-dimensional harmonic-oscillator Hamiltonians. Its product eigenstates are
Ψnx​ny​nz​​(x,y,z)=ψnx​​(x)ψny​​(y)ψnz​​(z),
(113)
with energies
Enx​ny​nz​​=ℏω(nx​+ny​+nz​+23​)​.
(114)
Equivalently, the level with N=nx​+ny​+nz​ has energy EN​=ℏω(N+3/2), as in the three-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator.
Up to normalization, the ground-state wavefunction is
Ψ000​=exp[−2ℏMω​(x2+y2+z2)].
(115)
It is radial, so ∇Ψ000​ is parallel to the position vector. Since the orbital angular momentum is L=−iℏr×∇, every component of L annihilates this state. Therefore
Lz​Ψ000​=0,L2Ψ000​=0,
(116)
and the ground state has ℓ=m=0.
The first excited level has N=1 and is spanned by
xΨ000​,yΨ000​,zΨ000​.
(117)
The state
Φ0​=zΨ000​
(118)
satisfies Lz​Φ0​=0. Applying the ladder operators gives
L+​Φ0​=−ℏ(x+iy)Ψ000​,L−​Φ0​=ℏ(x−iy)Ψ000​.
(119)
Hence convenient m=±1 eigenstates are
Φ±1​=(x±iy)Ψ000​​,
(120)
up to normalization and irrelevant overall phases.
Finally, the isotropic Hamiltonian is rotationally invariant. The rotational invariance of a central-potential Hamiltonian gives
[H,Lz​]=[H,L2]=[Lz​,L2]=0.
(121)
These commuting self-adjoint operators can be simultaneously diagonalized within each energy eigenspace, which is why joint eigenstates of H,L2,Lz​ must exist.
