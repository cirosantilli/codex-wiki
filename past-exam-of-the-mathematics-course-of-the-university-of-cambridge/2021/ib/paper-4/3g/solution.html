The hypothesis says that f has a zero of order k at a, so write
f(z)=(z−a)kg(z),g(a)=0,
(15)
with g holomorphic near a. Differentiating gives
f′(z)=(z−a)k−1(kg(z)+(z−a)g′(z)).
(16)
Choose δ>0 so small that the closed disc D(a,δ) lies in the domain, g never vanishes there, and the parenthesized factor never vanishes there. Thus a is the only critical point of f in that disc.
On the boundary circle, f is nonzero. Set
ε=min∣z−a∣=δ​∣f(z)∣>0.
(17)
If 0<∣b∣<ε, then ∣b∣<∣f(z)∣ on the boundary. Rouché's theorem applied to f and f−b says that f−b has the same number of zeros in D(a,δ) as f, namely k, counted with multiplicity.
None of these zeros is a because b=0, and none is a critical point because f′ has no other zero in the disc. Every zero of f−b is therefore simple. Hence there are exactly
k distinct z∈D(a,δ) with f(z)=b​.
(18)
