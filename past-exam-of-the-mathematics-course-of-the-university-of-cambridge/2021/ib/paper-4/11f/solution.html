An abstract smooth surface is a Hausdorff second-countable topological space with an atlas of charts to open subsets of R2 whose transition maps are smooth. It is orientable when it has such an atlas for which every transition map has positive Jacobian determinant. A map f:S1​→S2​ is a smooth map when every coordinate representation
ψ∘f∘ϕ−1
(68)
is smooth wherever it is defined.
The map a:C→C is a smooth involution with no fixed point: the equation
(x,y,z)=(−x,−y,−z)
(69)
would force x=y=0, which is impossible on x2+y2=1. For each p∈C, choose a sufficiently small coordinate neighbourhood Up​ such that
Up​∩a(Up​)=∅.
(70)
Then the quotient projection restricts to a homeomorphism
π∣Up​​:Up​⟶π(Up​).
(71)
Transporting a smooth chart ϕp​:Up​→R2 across this homeomorphism gives the quotient chart
ϕ​p​=ϕp​∘(π∣Up​​)−1.
(72)
On an overlap, a lift lies either in another chosen neighbourhood or in its image under a. The corresponding transition map is therefore a transition map on C, possibly composed with the diffeomorphism a, and is smooth. Since this is a free action of the finite group {1,a}, the quotient is Hausdorff and second countable. This constructs the smooth quotient by a free finite group action, and in these charts π is locally the identity. Hence π is a local diffeomorphism, in particular smooth.
To test orientability, parametrize the cylinder by
F(θ,z)=(cosθ,sinθ,z).
(73)
The involution acts in these coordinates as
(θ,z)⟼(θ+π,−z),
(74)
whose derivative has determinant −1. Thus a is an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of the cylinder.
If the quotient surface S were orientable, its orientation would pull back through the local diffeomorphism π to an orientation of C. The identity π∘a=π would then force a to preserve that pulled-back orientation, contradicting the negative determinant above. Therefore
S is not orientable​.
(75)
