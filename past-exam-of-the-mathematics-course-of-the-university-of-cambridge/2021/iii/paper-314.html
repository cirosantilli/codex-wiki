In a static spherical star, hydrostatic equilibrium and the Poisson equation give
drdp​=−ρg,g=drdΦ​=r2Gm(r)​,drdm​=4πr2ρ​,
(1)
or equivalently r−2d(r2g)/dr=4πGρ.
Let ξ be the fluid displacement field, so the velocity perturbation is δu=∂t​ξ. Linearizing the ideal-fluid momentum equation about the static state and cancelling the background hydrostatic terms gives
ρ∂t2∂2ξ​=−ρ∇δΦ−δρ∇Φ−∇δp​.
(2)
Conservation of mass says that the Lagrangian density perturbation is ΔL​ρ=−ρ∇⋅ξ. The relation between Eulerian and Lagrangian fluid perturbations and adiabatic compression gives, with Δ=∇⋅ξ,
δρ=−ρΔ−ξ⋅∇ρ,δp=−γpΔ−ξ⋅∇p​,
(3)
while linearized self-gravity gives ∇2δΦ=4πGδρ​.
For the stated spherical harmonic displacement, the radial divergence is r−2d(r2ξ​r​)/dr, while ∇ξ​h​ is radial and orthogonal to the angular gradient of Ylm​. Hence
Δ=r21​drd​(r2ξ​r​)−kh2​ξ​h​,kh2​=r2l(l+1)​​.
(4)
Equating the radial and horizontal coefficients of Ylm​ and ∇Ylm​, and applying the separated Laplacian in spherical coordinates to δΦ(r)Ylm​, gives
−ρω2ξ​r​=−ρdrdδΦ​−gδρ​−drdδp​​​,
(5)
−ρω2ξ​h​=−ρδΦ−δp​​,
(6)
δρ​=−ρΔ−ξ​r​drdρ​,δp​=−γpΔ−ξ​r​drdp​​,
(7)
r21​drd​(r2drdδΦ​)−kh2​δΦ=4πGδρ​​.
(8)
Define the stellar buoyancy frequency by
N2=g(γp1​drdp​−ρ1​drdρ​)​.
(9)
Eliminating Δ between the density and pressure perturbations gives
δρ​=γpρ​δp​+gρN2​ξ​r​.
(10)
Substitution in the radial equation, followed by use of dp/dr=−ρg, yields
(ω2−N2)ξ​r​=drd​(δΦ+ρδp​​)−gρN2δp​​​.
(11)
Regular spherical profiles near the center have ρ=ρc​+O(r2) and p=pc​+O(r2), while m(r)=4πρc​r3/3+O(r5) and hence g=O(r). Both logarithmic gradients in the definition of N2 are O(r), so N2=Ar2+O(r4). A Sun-like radiative central stratification is stable, making A>0.

Integrating the three conservation laws across a stationary normal shock wave gives the Rankine-Hugoniot conditions for a perfect gas
ρ1​ux1​=ρ2​ux2​,
(16)
p1​+ρ1​ux12​=p2​+ρ2​ux22​,
(17)

ux2​ux1​​=ρ1​ρ2​​=(γ−1)Mx12​+2(γ+1)Mx12​​​,

The divergence-free poloidal field can be represented by the poloidal magnetic flux function
Bp​=∇ψ×∇ϕ=−r1​eϕ​×∇ψ.
(41)
In a steady axisymmetric ideal magnetohydrodynamics flow, the azimuthal component of ∇×(u×B)=0 makes up​ parallel to Bp​. Write
ρup​=kBp​​.
(42)
Mass conservation and ∇⋅B=0 then imply B⋅∇k=0, so the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading k=k(ψ) is constant along each magnetic line.
The poloidal part of u×B is
u×B=r1​(uϕ​−ρkBϕ​​)∇ψ.
(43)
Its curl vanishes only if its coefficient is a flux function, giving the field-line angular velocity
ruϕ​​−rρkBϕ​​=ω(ψ)​.
(44)
The azimuthal component of momentum conservation is a divergence of matter and magnetic angular-momentum flux. Dividing its field-line constant by the mass loading yields the magnetohydrodynamic angular-momentum invariant
ruϕ​−μ0​krBϕ​​=ℓ(ψ)​.
(45)
The conservative total-energy equation similarly gives the magnetohydrodynamic Bernoulli invariant
21​∣u∣2+Φ+h−μ0​krωBϕ​​=ϵ(ψ)​.
(46)
Finally, the entropy advection equation and up​∥Bp​ imply s=s(ψ). Thus k,ω,ℓ,ϵ, and s are constant along each magnetic field line.

Introduce the squared poloidal Alfvén number
MA2​=Bp2​μ0​ρup2​​=ρμ0​k2​.
(47)
Solving the two linear azimuthal invariants gives
uϕ​=MA2​−1MA2​ℓ/r−rω​​,
(48)
Bϕ​=MA2​−1μ0​k(ℓ/r−rω)​​.
(49)
At an Alfvén surface, MA2​=1. Smooth passage through the apparent singularity requires both numerators to vanish at the same cylindrical radius rA​, giving the Alfvén-surface regularity condition for an axisymmetric wind
ℓ=ωrA2​​.
(50)
The finite limiting values of uϕ​ and Bϕ​ then follow by l'Hopital's rule from the local variation of MA​ and r along the field line; the algebraic invariants alone fix their combination rather than each value separately at the critical point.
