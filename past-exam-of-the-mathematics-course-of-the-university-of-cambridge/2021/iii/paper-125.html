The Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve over a field K has the general form
y2+a1​xy+a3​y=x3+a2​x2+a4​x+a6​,
(1)
with nonzero elliptic-curve discriminant. Admissible change of Weierstrass coordinates relates two such equations defining isomorphic pointed curves over K:
x=u2x′+r,y=u3y′+u2sx′+t,
(2)
where u∈K× and r,s,t∈K.
If charK=2,3, completing the square and translating x simplify every equation. Short Weierstrass form is
y2=x3+Ax+B,Δ=−16(4A3+27B2)=0.
(3)
Two short equations are K-isomorphic precisely when, for some u∈K×,
A′=u4A,B′=u6B;
(4)
the isomorphism from the first curve to the second is (x,y)↦(u2x,u3y).

A twist of an elliptic curve E/Q is an elliptic curve that becomes isomorphic to E over Q​. Write
E:y2=x3+Ax+B.
(5)
For d∈Q×, its quadratic twist may be written
Ed:y2=x3+d2Ax+d3B.
(6)
Over Q(d​), the map (x,y)↦(d−1x,d−3/2y) identifies Ed with E.
The hypothesis j(E)=0,1728 implies
AutQ​​(E)={±1}.
(7)
Consequently twists are classified by
H1(GQ​,{±1})≃Hom(GQ​,{±1})≃Q×/(Q×)2,
(8)
where the last identification is Kummer theory. In the explicit equations, Ed≃Q​Ee exactly when d/e is a rational square. Every nonzero rational square class has a unique square-free integer representative, including its sign, so the twists are parametrized by the nonzero square-free integers.

If every point of E[n] is rational, the Weil pairing
en​:E[n]×E[n]⟶μn​
(9)
and its nondegeneracy imply that every nth root of unity belongs to Q. The only roots of unity in Q are ±1, so n≤2. Since the question assumes n≥2, only n=2 is possible. It does occur: any nonsingular equation
y2=(x−a)(x−b)(x−c)
(10)
with distinct a,b,c∈Q has all four points of E[2] rational.

Mod-three Galois representation of an elliptic curve is the representation
ρ3​:GQ​⟶GL(E[3])≃GL2​(F3​)
(11)
in this case. Twisting by the quadratic character χd​ replaces it by χd​ρ3​. Thus Ed has a rational point of order three exactly when E[3] contains a nonzero vector v satisfying
ρ3​(σ)v=χd​(σ)v
(12)
for every σ∈GQ​: the line F3​v is a one-dimensional Galois subrepresentation with character χd​.
The two-dimensional representation E[3] has at most two distinct one-dimensional characters among its Jordan–Hölder factors. Therefore at most two quadratic characters χd​, and hence at most two rational isomorphism classes of twists, can have a rational point of order three.

A one-dimensional commutative formal group law over OK​ is a power series F(X,Y)∈OK​[[X,Y]] satisfying
F(X,0)=X,F(0,Y)=Y,F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)),F(X,Y)=F(Y,X).
(15)
For r≥1, the ideal πrOK​ becomes a group, denoted F(πrOK​), under x+F​y=F(x,y); convergence follows because both inputs lie in the maximal ideal.
Over the characteristic-zero field K, there is a unique formal logarithm
logF​(T)=T+O(T2)
(16)
satisfying logF​(F(X,Y))=logF​(X)+logF​(Y). Its coefficients have bounded denominator growth, so for sufficiently large r both logF​ and its inverse formal group exponential converge on πrOK​ and preserve that ideal. They give
F(πrOK​)≃(πrOK​,+)≃(OK​,+),
(17)
where the final isomorphism is multiplication by π−r.

For good reduction at p, the reduction of an elliptic curve gives a map
E(Qp​)⟶E(Fp​)
(19)
whose kernel is its formal group of an elliptic curve, and the torsion in that kernel is p-primary. Hence the prime-to-p part of any torsion subgroup injects into E(Fp​).
At p=2, the odd part of E(Q)tors​ must divide 4, so it is trivial. The torsion group is therefore a 2-group. At p=3, all of this group has order prime to 3 and injects into a group of order 7, so it too is trivial. Thus
E(Q)tors​=0.​
(20)

Hasse theorem and zeta functions

Words: 156
Solution OurBigBook.com
Let E be an elliptic curve over Fq​ and let π be its Frobenius isogeny. The fixed points of π are exactly E(Fq​), and separability of 1−π gives
#E(Fq​)=deg(1−π)=q+1−a,
(38)
Here Trace of Frobenius is the integer a. Since degπ=q, the quadraticity of degree gives, for every pair of integers m,n,
0≤deg(m−nπ)=m2−amn+qn2.
(39)
If the discriminant a2−4q were positive, this homogeneous quadratic would be negative for some real ratio m/n, hence for a nearby rational ratio and then for some pair of integers. Therefore
a2≤4q.
(40)
This is the Hasse theorem for elliptic curves,
∣#E(Fq​)−(q+1)∣≤2q​.
(41)
The Frobenius isogeny satisfies
π2−aπ+q=0.
(42)
If α,β are the roots of X2−aX+q, then ∣α∣=∣β∣=q​ and
#E(Fqn​)=qn+1−αn−βn.
(43)
The zeta function of an elliptic curve over a finite field is
Z(E/Fq​,T)=exp(∑n≥1​#E(Fqn​)nTn​).
(44)
Substitution of the point-count formula and −log(1−z)=∑n≥1​zn/n gives the rational function
Z(E/Fq​,T)=(1−T)(1−qT)(1−αT)(1−βT)​=(1−T)(1−qT)1−aT+qT2​.
(45)
The bounds ∣α∣=∣β∣=q​ are the Riemann hypothesis for an elliptic curve over a finite field. The relations αβ=q and the displayed formula also give the functional equation
Z(E/Fq​,1/(qT))=Z(E/Fq​,T).
(46)

Galois cohomology and weak Mordell-Weil

Words: 199
Solution OurBigBook.com
For a discrete module M over the absolute Galois group GK​, the first Galois cohomology group is
H1(K,M)=Z1(GK​,M)/B1(GK​,M),
(47)
where a one-cocycle satisfies c(στ)=c(σ)+σc(τ) and a coboundary has the form c(σ)=σm−m.
For an integer n≥2, the Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve
0⟶E[n]⟶E(K)[n]​E(K)⟶0
(48)
produces the injective Kummer map of an elliptic curve
δ:E(K)/nE(K)↪H1(K,E[n]).
(49)
Explicitly, if nQ=P, then δ(P) is represented by σ↦σQ−Q.
For every completion Kv​ there is a local Kummer map. The n-Selmer group is
Sel(n)(E/K)={c∈H1(K,E[n]):cv​∈imδv​ for every v}.
(50)
It fits into
0⟶E(K)/nE(K)⟶Sel(n)(E/K)⟶Sha(E/K)[n]⟶0,
(51)
where Sha(E/K) is the Tate–Shafarevich group.
Only finitely many places divide n, are places of bad reduction, or are Archimedean. Outside this finite set S, every Selmer class is unramified. Since the finite Galois module E[n] has finite order, there are only finitely many E[n]-valued cohomology classes unramified outside S; equivalently, the relevant finite extensions have bounded degree and ramification, and their number is finite by the Hermite–Minkowski theorem. Hence Sel(n)(E/K) is finite, and its subgroup E(K)/nE(K) is finite. This is the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem. Combined with height descent, which chooses representatives of bounded height in the finitely many cosets modulo nE(K), it yields the finite generation asserted by the Mordell-Weil theorem.
