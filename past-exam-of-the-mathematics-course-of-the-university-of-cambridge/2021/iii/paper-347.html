At distance r, isotropic luminosity L produces radiative acceleration arad​=κL/(4πr2c) in material of opacity κ. In fully ionized hydrogen, one Electron supplies a Thomson scattering cross-section σT​ for approximately one proton mass of material, so κ=σT​/mp​. Balancing radiation against the Newtonian gravitational field of the black hole gives the Eddington luminosity
LEdd,H​=σT​4πGMBH​mp​c​​.
(1)
Below this luminosity gravity can confine optically thin ionized hydrogen; above it, electron scattering alone accelerates the gas outward.
An electron--positron pair has mass 2me​ and total scattering cross-section 2σT​, so its opacity is σT​/me​. Therefore
LEdd,pair​=σT​4πGMBH​me​c​=mp​me​​LEdd,H​​.
(2)
The limit is lower because the radiative force per unit inertial mass is larger in a pair plasma.
For a cloud with arbitrary constant opacity per unit mass,
LEdd​=κ4πGMBH​c​​.
(3)
Suppose the luminosity jumps to a constant L>LEdd​ while the cloud is at rest at r0​. With Γ=L/LEdd​, its radial acceleration is
r¨=r2GMBH​​(Γ−1).
(4)
Multiplication by r˙ and integration from r0​ to infinity gives conservation of energy
21​v∞2​=r0​GMBH​​(Γ−1),v∞​=[r0​2GMBH​​(LEdd​L​−1)]1/2​.
(5)
This result assumes that L and κ remain constant and that the cloud stays optically thin.
