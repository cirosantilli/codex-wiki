A prime model of a complete theory T is a model M⊨T for which there is an elementary embedding M→N into every model N⊨T.

Enumerate the countable atomic model as M={a0​,a1​,…} and fix any N⊨T. We recursively construct finite partial elementary maps fn​ from the first n elements of M into N.
Suppose fn​(aˉ)=bˉ. Since M is atomic, choose a formula θ(xˉ,y) isolating tp(aˉ,an​). Its existential consequence ∃yθ(xˉ,y) belongs to tp(aˉ), so partial elementarity gives
N⊨∃yθ(bˉ,y).
(1)
Choose a witness bn​. Because θ isolates the complete joint type, extending fn​ by an​↦bn​ remains partial elementary. The union of the recursive maps is an elementary embedding M→N. Thus every countable atomic model is a prime model, proving the countable atomic model is prime result.

The Fundamental theorem of stability says that, for a complete theory with infinite models, the following are equivalent:
  • T is stable;
  • no formula has the order property;
  • every complete type over every model of T is a definable type.

Solution

Words: 99
b OurBigBook.com
Let λ=∣T∣ and choose an infinite cardinal κ satisfying κλ=κ, for example κ=2λ. Take M⊨T of cardinality κ. By assumption, every p∈S1​(M) is definable.
For each parameter-free formula scheme φ(x;y), the definition dp​φ(y) is a formula with finitely many parameters from M. There are at most κ such formulas. A definable type is completely determined by choosing one definition for each of the at most λ formula schemes, so
∣S1​(M)∣≤κλ=κ.
(2)
The same count applies to every model of cardinality κ, and therefore T is κ-stable. Hence T is a stable theory.

Every model of T becomes an L0​-structure by forgetting the symbols outside L0​, so T0​ has an infinite model. If φ is an L0​-sentence, completeness of T gives either T⊨φ or T⊨¬φ. Accordingly either φ∈T0​ or ¬φ∈T0​. The two cannot both occur because T is consistent. Therefore T0​ is a complete L0​-theory with infinite models.

No. Let L have unary predicates P,Q and a unary function f. Let T say that P and Q partition the universe into two infinite sets and that f is an involution mapping P bijectively onto Q. This is a complete theory. In every infinite cardinal κ, a model of total size κ has ∣P∣=∣Q∣=κ, and any two such bijections are isomorphic. Hence T is κ-categorical for every infinite κ.
Take L0​={P,Q}. The reduct theory merely says that P and Q are two infinite parts. At any uncountable κ, it has one model with ∣P∣=ℵ0​ and ∣Q∣=κ, and another with ∣P∣=∣Q∣=κ. They are not isomorphic. Therefore T0​ is not κ-categorical, giving the reduct need not preserve uncountable categoricity counterexample.

A theory T has quantifier elimination when every first-order formula is equivalent modulo T to a quantifier-free formula with the same free variables.

b

Words: 151 Articles: 1
4 OurBigBook.com

Solution

Words: 151
b OurBigBook.com
Use the partial-isomorphism criterion for quantifier elimination: it is enough that every finite partial embedding between sufficiently saturated models have the one-point extension property.
Let f:A→B be a finite partial order isomorphism between models M,N of the theory of dense linear order without endpoints, and take c∈M∖A. The finite set A partitions M into the points of A, the intervals between consecutive elements, and the two outer rays. The order relations between c and A identify one of those intervals or rays. The image B determines the corresponding interval or ray in N. Density supplies a point there when it is bounded, and the absence of endpoints supplies one in either outer ray. Choose such a point d; then f∪{(c,d)} remains a partial order isomorphism.
The same argument works in the reverse direction. A back-and-forth method therefore extends finite partial order isomorphisms, making them partial elementary. The criterion proves quantifier elimination for dense linear orders without endpoints.
