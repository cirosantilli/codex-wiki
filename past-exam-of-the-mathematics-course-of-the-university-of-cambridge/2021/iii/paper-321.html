Inside the innermost stable circular orbit, nearly circular motion is unstable and gas enters the plunging region of a black-hole accretion disk. Its inflow time becomes shorter than the time on which internal stress can communicate angular momentum back to the disk, motivating the zero-torque inner boundary condition at rISCO​.
In a steady state, the given diffusion equation implies
r1/2drd​(r1/2νˉΣ)=C.
(1)

νˉΣ=3πM˙​[1−C0​r−1/2].
(2)
Zero torque means νˉΣ=0 at r=rISCO​, so C0​=rISCO1/2​. Thus the Keplerian accretion disk relation is
νˉΣ=M˙f(r)​,f(r)=3π1​[1−(rrISCO​​)1/2]​.

For the Keplerian shearing sheet velocity U=−(3/2)Ωxey​, its material acceleration vanishes because U⋅∇U=0. The x component of the Coriolis acceleration is −3Ω2x, which is cancelled by −∇Φt​=+3Ω2xex​ with the signs in the stated equation; constant P0​ supplies no force. The flow is also incompressible.
Let u=U+u′ and h′=P′/ρ. Axisymmetry removes U⋅∇u′, while u′⋅∇U=−(3/2)Ωux′​ey​. Keeping first-order terms gives
∂t​ux′​=−∂x​h′+2Ωuy′​​,∂t​uy′​=−21​Ωux′​​,
(46)
∂t​uz′​=−∂z​h′​,∂x​ux′​+∂z​uz′​=0​.
(47)
Differentiate the momentum equations in time and use incompressibility to eliminate h′. The radial velocity obeys
∂t2​(∇2ux′​)+Ω2∂z2​ux′​=0​.
(48)
For a plane wave proportional to ei(kx​x+kz​z−ωt), this gives the inertial wave dispersion relation
ω2=Ω2kx2​+kz2​kz2​​​.

When kx​=0 and kz​=0, incompressibility forces uz′​=0, and the vertical momentum equation then forces h′=0. The remaining motion has ω=±Ω and satisfies uy′​=∓iux′​/2: each horizontal layer executes an epicyclic motion, with the phase varying vertically but no pressure or vertical-velocity perturbation.
