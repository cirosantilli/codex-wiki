Consider the graph
Γ={(x,f(x)):1≤x≤N}⊆Z2.
(47)
The hypothesis says exactly that Γ has at least δN3 additive quadruples. By the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, it contains Γ′ with ∣Γ′∣≫δ​N and ∣Γ′+Γ′∣≪δ​∣Γ′∣.
Part b gives a Freiman s-isomorphism from Γ′ to a set D⊆Z, where it is enough to take any fixed s≥2. The set D has bounded doubling, with a bound depending only on δ. Part c therefore provides a proper generalized arithmetic progression Q of rank Oδ​(1) such that
∣D∩Q∣≫δ​∣D∣≫δ​∣Q∣.
(48)
Write Q as a proper parameter box. Since ∣Q∣≫δ​N, one of its Oδ​(1) side lengths tends to infinity with N. Averaging over all lines parallel to that side gives a line on which D∩Q has density bounded below in terms of δ. The Szemerédi theorem quoted in the question then gives, once N is sufficiently large in terms of k and δ, a nonconstant k-term arithmetic progression in D.
The inverse Freiman isomorphism sends it to a k-term arithmetic progression in Γ′⊆Γ, because each relation between three consecutive terms is an additive-quadruple relation. Write this progression as
(x0​,y0​), (x0​+d,y0​+e),…,(x0​+(k−1)d,y0​+(k−1)e).
(49)
Its first-coordinate difference cannot be zero: the graph of a function has only one point above each x. Hence d=0. On the nonconstant progression
P={x0​,x0​+d,…,x0​+(k−1)d}⊆{1,…,N},
(50)
we have
f(x)=de​x+(y0​−de​x0​).
(51)
Taking a=e/d and b=y0​−(e/d)x0​, both in Q, proves the claim.
