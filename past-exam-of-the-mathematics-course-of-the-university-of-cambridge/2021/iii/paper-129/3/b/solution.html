Put N=pn, α=∣A∣/N, and use normalized Fourier analysis on a finite abelian group. If F=1A​∗1A​ denotes unnormalized convolution and h=N−1F, then
h(γ)=1A​​(γ)2,∑γ​∣h(γ)∣=∑γ​∣1A​​(γ)∣2=α
(27)
by the Parseval identity.
Sample k=O(mε−2) characters independently, choosing γ with probability ∣h(γ)∣/α, and attach the phase of h(γ) to the sampled character. The Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality, followed by averaging over x, shows that some sampled Fourier sum g satisfies
∥h−g∥L2m(E)​≤2εα​.
(28)
This is the sampling argument recorded in the finite-field character approximation principle.
Let V be the intersection of the kernels of the sampled characters. It is a vector subspace of codimension at most k, and τt​g=g for every t∈V. The triangle inequality therefore gives
∥τt​h−h∥L2m(E)​≤2∥h−g∥L2m(E)​≤εα.
(29)
Returning from normalized convolution and normalized norm to F and the counting norm multiplies the right side by N1+1/(2m). Hence
∥τt​F−F∥2m​≤ε∣A∣N1/(2m)=ε∣A∣pn/(2m)
(30)
for every t∈V, proving the finite-field convolution almost-periodicity theorem.
