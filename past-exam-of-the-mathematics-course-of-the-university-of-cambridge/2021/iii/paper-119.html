For a locally small category C, an object A, and a functor F:C→Set, the covariant Yoneda lemma is the natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)⟶F(A),α⟼αA​(1A​).
(1)
Its inverse sends x∈F(A) to the natural transformation whose component at B maps f:A→B to F(f)(x).
Suppose now that C is a small category. For F:C→Set, form the coproduct in a category
P=∐(A,x), x∈F(A)​C(A,−).
(2)
The Yoneda lemma associates to every summand the natural transformation determined by x, and these transformations combine to a map p:P→F. At an object B, the element y∈F(B) is the image of 1B​ in the summand indexed by (B,y), so p is a pointwise epimorphism in a functor category. Each representable functor C(A,−) is a projective object in a category, since
Nat(C(A,−),−)≅evA​
(3)
and evaluation preserves pointwise epimorphisms. A coproduct of projectives is projective, so P is the required projective object. This is the projective cover of a set-valued functor by representables.
We next prove the three equivalent conditions. If every morphism of C is a monomorphism, then for f:B→C and every A, postcomposition
C(A,B)⟶C(A,C),g⟼fg
(4)
is injective. Thus every covariant representable functor is a monofunctor. Conversely, taking A=B shows that injectivity for every representable implies that fg=fh forces g=h, so every f is monic.
If all representables are monofunctors, the object P above is a monofunctor because a coproduct of injective functions is injective. Hence every F is an epimorphic image of a monofunctor. Conversely, suppose every functor is an epimorphic image of a monofunctor and apply this to a representable R. Choose an epimorphism q:M↠R with M a monofunctor. Since R is projective, 1R​ lifts to s:R→M with qs=1R​. Thus R is a retract in a category of M. Every retract of a monofunctor is a monofunctor: if R(f)x=R(f)y, then injectivity of M(f) applied to s(x) and s(y) gives s(x)=s(y), and applying q gives x=y. This completes the equivalence.
Finally, every functor C→Set is a monofunctor exactly when every morphism of C is a split monomorphism. The forward implication is immediate because every functor preserves a left inverse. For the converse, fix f:A→B and form a quotient of C(A,−)⊔1 by identifying the distinguished point with every arrow of the form kf:A→X, where k:B→X. In the resulting functor Q, the two elements [1A​] and ∗ of Q(A) have equal images under Q(f) because [f]=∗. If every functor is a monofunctor, Q(f) is injective, so [1A​]=∗. By construction this means 1A​=rf for some r:B→A. Thus f is split monic. Equivalently, every morphism of C must be an absolute monomorphism.

For an adjoint functor pair F:D⇄C:G, the unit and counit of an adjunction are
η:1D​⇒GF,ε:FG⇒1C​.
(5)
Under the adjunction bijection, ηA​ corresponds to 1FA​ and εB​ corresponds to 1GB​. They satisfy the triangle identities
εFA​F(ηA​)=1FA​,G(εB​)ηGB​=1GB​.
(6)
Conversely, natural transformations with these identities recover the adjunction through the mutually inverse maps
f:FA→B⟼G(f)ηA​,g:A→GB⟼εB​F(g).
(7)
The fully faithful adjoint criterion gives the first equivalence directly. If F is full and faithful, there is a unique rA​:GFA→A with F(rA​)=εFA​; the triangle identity and faithfulness show that rA​ and ηA​ are inverse, so the unit is an isomorphism. If the unit is an isomorphism, the displayed adjunction bijection shows that
D(A,A′)⟶C(FA,FA′)
(8)
is bijective, so F is full and faithful. This also proves that either condition gives a natural isomorphism GF≅1D​.
For the remaining direction, suppose merely that GF is naturally isomorphic to the identity. Transport the monad induced by an adjunction along this isomorphism. Its underlying endofunctor is then the identity, its unit is a natural endomorphism u:1⇒1, and its multiplication is a natural endomorphism m:1⇒1 with mu=1. Naturality makes uA​ commute with mA​, so also um=1. Hence the transported unit, and therefore η, is an isomorphism. The three conditions are equivalent.
Now let F⊣G⊣H. If F is full and faithful, then GF≅1D​. For A,B∈D, the two adjunctions give natural bijections
D(B,GHA)≅C(FB,HA)≅D(GFB,A)≅D(B,A).
(9)
The Yoneda lemma therefore gives GHA≅A, naturally in A. The fully faithful adjoint criterion applied to G⊣H shows that H is full and faithful. Conversely, if H is full and faithful, then GH≅1D​ and
D(GFA,B)≅C(FA,HB)≅D(A,GHB)≅D(A,B).
(10)
Another application of the Yoneda lemma gives GFA≅A, so F is full and faithful. Thus F is full and faithful exactly when H is.
Assume henceforth that G is full and faithful. Then the counit ε:FG→1C​ and unit α:1C​→HG are natural isomorphisms. Consider
θ1​=(αF)−1(Hη),θ2​=(Fβ)(εH)−1:H⇒F.
(11)
Applying the faithful functor G, then using naturality and the four triangle identities, turns both composites into
GHβ​1D​η​GF.
(12)
Therefore θ1​=θ2​; denote their common value by θ, the double-adjoint comparison transformation.
The pointwise monicity criterion is clearest from the following natural square, in which both vertical maps are bijections:
C(B,HA)↓D(GB,A)​θA​∘−​F​​C(B,FA)↓C(FGB,FA).​
(13)
The left vertical map is the adjunction G⊣H, while the right one precomposes with the isomorphism εB​:FGB→B. Thus every θA​ is a monomorphism exactly when F is faithful on all morphisms GB→A, namely morphisms whose domains lie in the image of G.
Dually, the natural square
C(FA,B)↓D(A,GB)​−∘θA​​H​​C(HA,B)↓C(HA,HGB)​
(14)
has bijective vertical maps, using F⊣G on the left and the isomorphism αB​:B→HGB on the right. Hence every θA​ is an epimorphism exactly when H is faithful on all morphisms A→GB, namely morphisms whose codomains lie in the image of G.

A cocone under a diagram D:J→C with vertex X is a natural family λj​:Dj→X, so λk​D(u)=λj​ for every u:j→k. A colimit of D is an initial such cocone: for every cocone λ:D⇒ΔX, there is a unique h:colimD→X with hιj​=λj​ for all j.
Let F:I→J be a final functor and let λi​:D(Fi)→X be a cocone under DF. For each j, choose an object (i,u:j→Fi) of the nonempty comma category (j↓F) and define
λˉj​=λi​D(u):D(j)→X.
(15)
A morphism (i,u)→(i′,u′) in the comma category gives F(v)u=u′, and the cocone identity shows that the two resulting maps are equal. Because (j↓F) is a connected category, a zigzag proves independence of the chosen object. The same construction applied after a morphism j→k proves naturality, so λˉ is a cocone. Any extension must have this value because it must satisfy the cocone identity along u, proving uniqueness. This is cocone extension along a final functor.
If L is a colimit of DF, its universal cocone extends uniquely to D. Restriction and extension give mutually inverse correspondences between cocones from D and from DF, so the extended cocone is a colimit of D. Therefore the existence of all colimits of shape I implies the required colimits of shape J.
Now suppose J is a sifted category. For D,E:J→Set, the product functor −×A is a left adjoint, so it preserves colimits. Applying this once in each variable gives
(colimj∈J​Dj​)×(colimk∈J​Ek​)≅colim(j,k)∈J×J​(Dj​×Ek​).
(16)
The diagonal Δ:J→J×J is final, so the right side is
colimj∈J​(Dj​×Ej​).
(17)
Thus colimJ​ preserves binary products in a category. Since J is connected, the colimit of the constant singleton diagram is a singleton, so it also preserves the terminal object. It therefore preserves finite products.

A monad on C is an endofunctor T with natural transformations
η:1C​⇒T,μ:T2⇒T
(18)
satisfying μTμ=μμT and μTη=1T​=μηT. Its Eilenberg-Moore category CT has algebras for a monad (A,a:TA→A) satisfying
aηA​=1A​,aT(a)=aμA​,
(19)
and morphisms of algebras for a monad f:(A,a)→(B,b) satisfying fa=bT(f).
The Kleisli category CT​ has the objects of C and
CT​(A,B)=C(A,TB).
(20)
Its identity is ηA​, while the composite of f:A→TB and g:B→TC is μC​T(g)f. The free functor into a Kleisli category FT​:C→CT​ is the identity on objects and sends f:A→B to ηB​f.
On the functor category [D,C], postcomposition gives the pointwise monad on a functor category
T∗​(F)=TF,(η∗​)F​=ηF,(μ∗​)F​=μF.
(21)
The monad laws hold componentwise. A T∗​-algebra is a functor F:D→C with a natural transformation a:TF→F whose components are T-algebras. Naturality says precisely that every F(u) is an algebra morphism. Hence sending (F,a) to the lifted functor D→CT gives an isomorphism, and in particular an equivalence,
[D,C]T∗​≃[D,CT].
(22)
On [C,D], precomposition gives the precomposition monad on a functor category
T∗(G)=GT,(η∗)G​=Gη,(μ∗)G​=Gμ.
(23)
A T∗-algebra is a natural transformation a:GT→G satisfying aGη=1G​ and a(aT)=aGμ. From it define Gˉ:CT​→D by
Gˉ(A)=G(A),Gˉ(f:A→TB)=aB​G(f).
(24)
The two algebra laws say exactly that Gˉ preserves identities and Kleisli composition, and GˉFT​=G. Conversely, a factorization G=GˉFT​ gives
aA​=Gˉ(1TA​:TA→TA),
(25)
where 1TA​ represents a Kleisli arrow TA→A. These constructions are inverse on objects and morphisms, so
[C,D]T∗≃[CT​,D].​
(26)

A regular category has finite limits, every morphism factors through its image of a morphism in a regular category as a regular epimorphism followed by a monomorphism, and regular epimorphisms are stable under every pullback in a category. A cover is a strong epimorphism. Every regular epimorphism is strong: if e is the coequalizer of r,s and a square has e on the left and a monomorphism m on the right, monicity shows that the upper arrow coequalizes r,s. It therefore factors through e, and the epimorphism property of e shows that this factor is the required diagonal. Conversely, factor a strong epimorphism f as f=me with e regular epic and m monic. The lifting property gives a two-sided inverse to m, so m is an isomorphism and f is regular epic. Thus regular epimorphisms and covers coincide.
Let L:C→D be the left-exact reflector and let m:A′↪A. Since L preserves finite limits, Lm is monic. Define cA​(A′) as the pullback
cA​(A′)↓A​⟶ηA​​​LA′↓LmLA.​
(27)
Naturality of the unit supplies a map A′→cA​(A′) over A, proving A′≤cA​(A′). A factorization A′≤A′′ induces LA′≤LA′′ and therefore cA​(A′)≤cA​(A′′), so cA​ is order-preserving.
Apply L to the defining pullback. Left exactness and the fact that LηA​:LA→LLA is an isomorphism identify L(cA​(A′)) with LA′. Pulling back once more therefore gives
cA​(cA​(A′))≅cA​(A′).
(28)
For a map f:B→A, left exactness identifies L(f∗A′) with (Lf)∗(LA′). Pasting the two pullback squares then yields
cB​(f∗A′)≅f∗cA​(A′),
(29)
so this closure operation induced by a left-exact reflector commutes with pullback.
Assume A lies in D, so ηA​ is an isomorphism. If A′ also lies in D, its unit is an isomorphism and the defining square gives cA​(A′)≅A′. Conversely, if A′ is closed, that square expresses A′ as a finite limit of A, LA′, and LA, all fixed by L. Fixed objects of a left-exact reflective subcategory are closed under finite limits, so A′ belongs to D.
Finally suppose C is regular. The fixed objects have finite limits. For f:A→B in D, factor it in C as
Ae​Im​B.
(30)
Applying L gives ALe​LILm​B. The map Le is regular epic because a left adjoint preserves the coequalizer presenting e, and Lm is monic because L is left exact. Thus D has image factorizations. Their image subobject is the closure cB​(I). A map in D is regular epic exactly when this closure is all of its codomain. Images in C commute with pullback, and the closure operation also commutes with pullback, so this condition is pullback-stable. Hence D is regular, as stated by the left-exact reflective subcategory of a regular category theorem.

In a pointed category, a normal monomorphism is a monomorphism that is the kernel in a category of some morphism. Suppose m:M↪A is the kernel of f:A→B, and let q:A→Q be the cokernel in a category of m. Since fm=0, there is a unique fˉ​:Q→B with f=fˉ​q. If qx=0, then fx=fˉ​qx=0, so the universal property of m=kerf factors x uniquely through m. Therefore m=kerq=ker(cokerm). The converse is immediate: if m is the kernel of its cokernel, it is the kernel of a morphism and hence normal.
An abelian category is an additive category with kernels and cokernels in which every monomorphism is normal and every epimorphism is a conormal epimorphism. Finite biproducts and kernels give finite limits. The image and coimage in an abelian category give every f:A→B the canonical factorization
A↠coimf∼​imf↪B,
(31)
and the middle map is an isomorphism. The first map is a cokernel and therefore a regular epimorphism. Every epimorphism in an abelian category is the cokernel of its kernel, and epimorphisms are stable under pullback; consequently regular epimorphisms are pullback-stable. This proves that every abelian category is regular.
Define the additive indexing category for chain complexes Z as follows. Its objects are the integers and
Z(n,p)={Z,0,​p=n or p=n−1,otherwise.​
(32)
Let the generator of Z(n,n) be 1n​ and the generator of Z(n,n−1) be ∂n​. Composition is bilinear, the 1n​ are identities, and
∂n−1​∂n​=0
(33)
because the target hom-group Z(n,n−2) is zero. An additive functor C:Z→A chooses objects Cn​=C(n) and differentials dn​=C(∂n​) satisfying dn−1​dn​=0, hence a complex in an abelian category. Conversely every chain complex defines this unique additive functor.
For self-duality, put Zn​=kerdn​, Bn​=imdn+1​, and let q:Cn​→Qn​=cokerdn+1​. Since dn​dn+1​=0, there is a unique dˉn​:Qn​→Cn−1​ with dˉn​q=dn​. The image-to-kernel factorization gives a canonical isomorphism
Hn​(C∙​)=coker(Bn​↪Zn​)≅ker(dˉn​:Qn​→Cn−1​).
(34)
Passing to the opposite category exchanges kernels with cokernels and images with coimages. The usual construction in Aop is therefore the expression on the right, which is canonically the original homology object. This proves the self-duality of homology.
