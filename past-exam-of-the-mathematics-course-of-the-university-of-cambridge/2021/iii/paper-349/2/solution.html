Stars that cross the two nearby Lagrange points are no longer bound to the satellite. Small energy and angular-momentum offsets place them on slightly different host orbits, producing one leading and one trailing tidal tail. Differential orbital frequency stretches these streams around the host, while epicyclic motion can create density clumps. The tail's position and velocity structure retain information about the satellite orbit and host potential.
