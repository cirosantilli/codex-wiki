Interstellar dust both hides and reveals galaxies. Absorption and scattering remove short-wavelength photons from a line of sight, causing interstellar extinction, reddening, anisotropic scattering, and polarization. These effects obscure embedded star formation, bias luminosities, colours, stellar masses, and star-formation rates, and can make an edge-on or dusty galaxy appear older and fainter. Dust also converts absorbed ultraviolet and optical power into far-infrared and submillimetre thermal radiation. That reradiation exposes otherwise hidden star formation, constrains dust temperature and mass, and helps map cold molecular material; polarization traces magnetic-field orientation. Grain surfaces also catalyse molecular-hydrogen formation, while photoelectric emission from grains heats interstellar gas.
