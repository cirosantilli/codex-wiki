Let the unperturbed interface rise at pore velocity U/ϕ, and write its displacement as
η=η​eiαx+σt.
(1)
In each fluid, Darcy's law and incompressible flow imply
uj​=−μj​k​(∇pj​+ρj​gz),∇2pj​=0.
(2)
The decaying pressure perturbations are proportional to e−α∣z∣. Continuity of normal velocity, the kinematic boundary condition ϕηt​=un′​, and continuity of pressure give
σ=ϕαU​[M+(μ1​+μ2​)U(ρ2​−ρ1​)kg​]​,M=μ2​+μ1​μ2​−μ1​​.
(3)
Thus a less mobile displaced fluid, μ2​>μ1​, and a denser fluid above a lighter one both drive the Saffman–Taylor instability.
For immiscible fluids, the Young–Laplace equation adds the pressure jump −γηxx​. The dispersion relation becomes
σ(α)=ϕαU​[A−Cα2]​,
(4)
where
A=M+(μ1​+μ2​)U(ρ2​−ρ1​)kg​,C=(μ1​+μ2​)Uγk​.
(5)
If A>0, the unstable band is 0<α<A/C​ and differentiation gives
αmax​=3CA​​​.
(6)
Define the signed characteristic buoyancy velocity
Ub​=μ2​k(ρ2​−ρ1​)g​.
(7)
Since μ2​/(μ1​+μ2​)=(1+M)/2 and Ca=μ2​U/γ,
αmax​=[3k(1+M)2Ca​(M+21+M​UUb​​)]1/2​.
(8)
This expression applies when the quantity in the final parentheses is positive.
Every growth curve starts at the origin. For equal densities its initial slope is proportional to M; for ρ2​>ρ1​ buoyancy shifts that slope upward, while for ρ2​<ρ1​ it shifts it downward. When A>0, the curve rises to one positive maximum and then crosses zero before its stabilizing −α3 capillary tail. When A≤0, every nonzero wavenumber decays.
For a prescribed nonzero wavenumber, neutral stability requires
(μ2​−μ1​)U+(ρ2​−ρ1​)kg−γkα2=0,
(9)
or
ρ2​=ρ1​−kg(μ2​−μ1​)U​+gγα2​​.
(10)
In the quasistatic limit U→0, viscosity contrast disappears and this reduces to the capillary Rayleigh-Taylor instability threshold ρ2​−ρ1​=γα2/g. Without surface tension, neutral stability in that limit simply requires equal densities.

Let
κ=ρcp​k​,κs​=ρcp​ks​​,h(t)=2λκt​.
(11)
The Neumann solution of the Stefan problem in the ice and substrate is
Ti​(z,t)=T0​+(Tm​−T0​)erfλerf(z/(2κt​))​(0<z<h),
(12)
and
Ts​(z,t)=Ts​+(T0​−Ts​)erfc(2κs​t​−z​)(z<0).
(13)
Continuity of heat flux at the contact gives, with r=ks​/k​,
erfλTm​−T0​​=r(T0​−Ts​),
(14)
and therefore
T0​=1+rerf(λ)Tm​+rerf(λ)Ts​​​.
(15)
At the ice–water interface, the water is isothermal at Tm​. The Stefan condition ρLh˙=kTi,z​(h−) gives
Sλ=Tm​−Ts​Tm​−T0​​π​erfλe−λ2​,S=cp​(Tm​−Ts​)L​.
(16)
Eliminating T0​ yields the implicit equation
π​Sλeλ2(1+rerfλ)=r​,
(17)

If ks​≫k, then r≫1, T0​∼Ts​, and
Sλ∼π​erfλe−λ2​​.
(18)
The highly conducting substrate acts as a reservoir fixed near its initial cold temperature, giving the usual one-phase Stefan problem.

(19)
Here heat removal through the poorly conducting substrate is rate limiting, and only a small temperature drop is needed across the much more conducting ice.

Measure z upward from the heat exchanger and let the steady ice front be at z=h>0. In the exchanger frame, salt in the liquid satisfies the advection-diffusion equation
−VCz​=DCzz​.
(20)
The decaying solution and the prescribed total salt mass are
C(z)=Ci​e−V(z−h)/D,S=ρ∫h∞​C(z)dz,
(21)
so
Ci​=ρDSV​​.
(22)
The linear liquidus condition fixes the interface temperature as
Ti​=Tm​−mCi​=Tm​−ρDmSV​​.
(23)
A solid layer between the exchanger and the interface can therefore exist only if
Tc​<Tm​−ρDmSV​​.
(24)
Write ϑ=T−T∞​. Heat advection, conduction, and environmental loss give
ϑ′′+κV​ϑ′−kF​ϑ=0.
(25)
Its characteristic exponents are
r±​=−2κV​±4κ2V2​+kF​​,r+​>0>r−​.
(26)
Below the exchanger and above the ice front, boundedness gives
T−​(z)=T∞​+(Tc​−T∞​)er+​z(z<0),
(27)
Tl​(z)=T∞​+(Ti​−T∞​)er−​(z−h)(z>h).
(28)
In 0<z<h, the ice temperature is
T+​(z)=T∞​+Aer+​z+Ber−​z,
(29)
where
A+B=Tc​−T∞​,Aer+​h+Ber−​h=Ti​−T∞​.

gives one scalar equation for the steady height h, which can be solved numerically. The heat flux may jump at z=0 because the exchanger supplies the required localized cooling.
The local equilibrium freezing temperature ahead of the front is
TL​(z)=Tm​−mC(z).
(32)
Because Tl​(h)=TL​(h), constitutional supercooling begins when the actual liquid-temperature gradient at the interface is smaller than the liquidus gradient:

Using the solutions above, this is

At criticality the inequality is an equality. If F/k≪V2/κ2, then q∼V/κ and, for κ>D,
Vc​∼mS(κ−D)ρD2(T∞​−Tm​)​​.

(36)
This curve behaves as S−1/2 for small S and approaches DF/k​ for large S. Supercooling occurs above the corresponding critical curve, where solute rejection steepens the liquidus faster than heat transport raises the actual temperature.

Take the rock at z=0, the till–ice interface at z=h, and the ice surface at z=h+H. The common leading hydrostatic pressure has horizontal gradient
px​=ρgHx​.
(37)
The thin-film momentum equations are
λμut,zz​=px​,μui,zz​=px​.
(38)
Impose no slip at the rock, continuity of velocity and shear stress at z=h, and zero shear at the ice surface. Expanding for h/H≪1 gives the till flux and ice flux
qt​=−2λμρg​Hh2Hx​,
(39)
qi​=−3μρg​(1+λH3h​)H3Hx​.
(40)
The basal shear stress is
τ0​=−ρgHHx​+o(ρgHHx​).
(41)
Applying mass conservation separately to the ice and till, with erosion source Eτ0​, gives
Ht​=3μρg​∂x​[(1+λH3h​)H3Hx​]​,
(42)
and
ht​=2λμρg​∂x​(Hh2Hx​)−ρgEHHx​​.

In a steady state qi​=q0​. Balancing the two terms in the till equation over length L gives
λμρg​LH2h2​∼ρgEH2,

h∼(λμEL)1/2​,

(λHh​)4∼λ2q0​μE2Lρg​=E.

q0​=−λμρg​H2hHx​.

Integrating the steady till equation from x=0, where H=H0​ and h=0, gives
