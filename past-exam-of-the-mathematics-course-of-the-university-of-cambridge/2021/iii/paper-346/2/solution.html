The Schechter function has logarithmic slope α≃−1 for M≪M∗​ and an exponential cutoff for M≫M∗​. Thus a log-log plot is approximately a straight line of slope −1 at low mass and bends sharply downward near M∗​.
For a collisionless self-gravitating system, define the inertia tensor
Iij​=∫ρxi​xj​dV.
(16)
Two time derivatives, the collisionless Boltzmann equation, and integration by parts give the tensor virial theorem
21​I¨ij​=2Kij​+Wij​,
(17)
where
Kij​=21​∫ρ⟨vi​vj​⟩dV,Wij​=−∫ρxi​∂j​ΦdV.
(18)
For an isolated steady state, or a long-time average of a bounded system, I¨ij​=0 and surface terms vanish, so
2Kij​+Wij​=0​.
(19)
Taking the trace gives 2K+W=0. Therefore
E=K+W=−K=2W​​.
(20)
Introduce the gravitational radius by W=−GM2/R. Virial equilibrium gives
EI​=−21​MI​⟨vI2​⟩=−2RI​GMI2​​​.
(21)
Assume dissipationless accretion, negligible escaping mass, no external work, and final re-virialization. The accreted systems bring
EA​=−21​MA​⟨vA2​⟩.
(22)
With η=MA​/MI​ and ϵ=⟨vA2​⟩/⟨vI2​⟩,
EF​=EI​+EA​=−21​MI​⟨vI2​⟩(1+ϵη)​.
(23)
Since MF​=MI​(1+η) and EF​=−MF​⟨vF2​⟩/2,
⟨vI2​⟩⟨vF2​⟩​=1+η1+ηϵ​​.
(24)
Using E=−GM2/(2R) also gives
RI​RF​​=1+ηϵ(1+η)2​​.
(25)
Many minor galaxy mergers have dynamically cold accreted material, ϵ≪1. If they double the mass, η=1, then RF​/RI​≃4, and
ρˉ​I​ρˉ​F​​=(RF​/RI​)3MF​/MI​​=432​=321​.
(26)
The steep low-mass side of the Schechter function supplies many small satellites. Repeated dry galaxy mergers can therefore add stars mainly at large radius, increasing size much faster than mass and lowering mean density. This explains how compact, dense redshift-two galaxies can evolve into larger present-day elliptical galaxies without requiring comparable in-situ star formation.
