the chord-and-tangent group law gives, for distinct nonopposite points P=(x1​,y1​) and Q=(x2​,y2​),
λ=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​,x(P+Q)=λ2−x1​−x2​,y(P+Q)=−y1​+λ(x1​−x(P+Q)).
(25)
For doubling, replace the slope by
λ=2y1​3x12​+a​.
(26)
The point at infinity is the identity and −(x,y)=(x,−y).
Let the x-coordinates of P,Q,P+Q,P−Q be x1​,x2​,x3​,x4​. Applying the addition formula with Q and −Q gives
x3​+x4​=(x2​−x1​)2(y2​−y1​)2+(y2​+y1​)2​−2(x1​+x2​).
(27)
Substituting yi2​=xi3​+axi​+b and simplifying yields
x3​+x4​=(x1​−x2​)22(x1​x2​+a)(x1​+x2​)+4b​.
(28)
Multiplying the two addition formulas and eliminating y1​y2​ in the same way gives
x3​x4​=(x1​−x2​)2(x1​x2​−a)2−4b(x1​+x2​)​.
(29)
These identities are the algebraic source of two parallelogram laws. Applied to pullbacks of the pole divisor of x, they imply
deg(φ+ψ)+deg(φ−ψ)=2degφ+2degψ
(30)
for isogenies of elliptic curves. Together with deg(nφ)=n2degφ, this makes the degree a quadratic form. Applied to the Absolute logarithmic Weil height of the four x-coordinates, with bounded terms removed by passage to the limit defining the canonical height of an elliptic curve, they similarly give
h(P+Q)+h(P−Q)=2h(P)+2h(Q),h(nP)=n2h(P).​
(31)
