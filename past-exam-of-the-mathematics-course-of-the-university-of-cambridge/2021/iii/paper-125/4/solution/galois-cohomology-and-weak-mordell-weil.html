For a discrete module M over the absolute Galois group GK​, the first Galois cohomology group is
H1(K,M)=Z1(GK​,M)/B1(GK​,M),
(47)
where a one-cocycle satisfies c(στ)=c(σ)+σc(τ) and a coboundary has the form c(σ)=σm−m.
For an integer n≥2, the Kummer exact sequence of an elliptic curve
0⟶E[n]⟶E(K)[n]​E(K)⟶0
(48)
produces the injective Kummer map of an elliptic curve
δ:E(K)/nE(K)↪H1(K,E[n]).
(49)
Explicitly, if nQ=P, then δ(P) is represented by σ↦σQ−Q.
For every completion Kv​ there is a local Kummer map. The n-Selmer group is
Sel(n)(E/K)={c∈H1(K,E[n]):cv​∈imδv​ for every v}.
(50)
It fits into
0⟶E(K)/nE(K)⟶Sel(n)(E/K)⟶Sha(E/K)[n]⟶0,
(51)
where Sha(E/K) is the Tate–Shafarevich group.
Only finitely many places divide n, are places of bad reduction, or are Archimedean. Outside this finite set S, every Selmer class is unramified. Since the finite Galois module E[n] has finite order, there are only finitely many E[n]-valued cohomology classes unramified outside S; equivalently, the relevant finite extensions have bounded degree and ramification, and their number is finite by the Hermite–Minkowski theorem. Hence Sel(n)(E/K) is finite, and its subgroup E(K)/nE(K) is finite. This is the Weak Mordell-Weil theorem. Combined with height descent, which chooses representatives of bounded height in the finitely many cosets modulo nE(K), it yields the finite generation asserted by the Mordell-Weil theorem.
