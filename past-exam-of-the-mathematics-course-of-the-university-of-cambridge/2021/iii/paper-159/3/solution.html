The irregularity of an algebraic surface and the geometric genus of an algebraic surface are respectively
q(X)=h1(X,OX​)=h0(X,ΩX1​),pg​(X)=h0(X,KX​)=h2(X,OX​).
(15)
If π:X′→X is the blowup at a point with exceptional divisor E, then
KX′​=π∗KX​+E.
(16)
A holomorphic two-form on X pulls back to one on X′. Conversely, a holomorphic two-form on X′ descends across E: locally it is a form on the punctured smooth surface X∖{p}, and its coefficients extend over the codimension-two point p. Equivalently, π∗​KX′​=KX​. Pullback is therefore an isomorphism
H0(X,KX​)≅H0(X′,KX′​),
(17)
which proves the birational invariance of the geometric genus of a surface in this case.
Let X=C×D, where both smooth projective curves have positive genus. The Künneth theorem gives the irregularity of a product of curves
q(X)=g(C)+g(D)>0.
(18)
If a surface is a hypersurface in projective space, dimension forces it to be a smooth hypersurface Y⊆P3. From
0⟶OP3​(−d)⟶OP3​⟶OY​⟶0
(19)
and the intermediate cohomology vanishing for line bundles on projective space, one obtains H1(Y,OY​)=0. Thus every such hypersurface has irregularity zero, whereas X has positive irregularity. Hence C×D is not isomorphic to a hypersurface. This is the product of positive-genus curves is not a projective hypersurface obstruction.
The Albanese variety Alb(X) is the universal abelian variety receiving a pointed morphism from X, and
dimAlb(X)=q(X).
(20)
Let the smooth image curve of the Albanese morphism be C of genus g. Pullback of holomorphic one-forms along the dominant map X→C is injective, giving g≤q(X). On the other hand, the universal property of the Jacobian extends C→Alb(X) to a homomorphism
Jac(C)⟶Alb(X).
(21)
The Albanese image generates the entire Albanese variety, so this homomorphism is surjective and q(X)≤dimJac(C)=g. Therefore the Irregularity from a smooth Albanese curve image is
q(X)=g​.
(22)
