For a based space (X,x0​), the homotopy group
πk​(X,x0​)=[(Sk,∗),(X,x0​)]∗​
(1)
is the set of based homotopy classes, with its usual concatenation operation. A map f:X→Y is a weak homotopy equivalence when it induces a bijection on path components and an isomorphism
f∗​:πk​(X,x)∼​πk​(Y,f(x))
(2)
for every k≥1 and every basepoint x. It is an n-connected map when it is bijective on πk​ for k<n and surjective on πn​; equivalently, every homotopy fiber is (n−1)-connected.
A CW complex is built from a discrete set of zero-cells by successively attaching k-discs along maps from their boundary spheres, with the weak topology and closure-finiteness conditions. Its filtration by skeleta is the CW filtration.
For any space X, form its singular simplicial set SingX. Its geometric realization of a simplicial set is a CW complex, with one cell for each nondegenerate singular simplex, and evaluation gives
ϵ:∣SingX∣⟶X.
(3)
The Simplicial approximation theorem identifies based maps and homotopies from finite simplicial spheres into ∣SingX∣ with singular simplices in X. Consequently ϵ induces a bijection on components and isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. Thus every space admits a CW approximation.
The vanishing assumptions do not permit removal of all n-cells. Take n=2 and
X=K(Z/r,1),r>1.
(4)
Then π2​(X)=0, and homology of a finite cyclic group gives H2​(X;Z)=0. If a connected CW complex Y had no two-cells, attaching cells of dimension at least three would not change the fundamental group of its one-skeleton. Hence π1​(Y) would be a free group. A weak equivalence Y→X would instead give π1​(Y)≅Z/r, which is nontrivial and finite and therefore not free. No such Y exists.
