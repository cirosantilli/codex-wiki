At θ=1, the two saddles have receded to infinity and the ordinary quadratic saddle approximation is nonuniform. One should first use an asymptotic expansion of the phase and amplitude for large z:
z2−1​=z−2z1​+O(z−3)(z→+∞),
(11)
so
Φ(z)=−2z1​+O(z−3).
(12)
The phase varies on the scale z=O(λ) rather than in an O(λ−1/2) neighbourhood of a finite saddle. Rescaling z=λZ in the positive and negative tails, retaining the corresponding large-z amplitude, and matching these tail integrals to the finite part of the deformed contour produces the leading approximation. This is an endpoint at infinity problem; a uniform calculation may equivalently begin from the θ<1 saddle representation and take the coalescing-at-infinity limit.
