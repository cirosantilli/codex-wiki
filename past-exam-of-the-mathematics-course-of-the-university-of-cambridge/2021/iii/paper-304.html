With Euclidean source convention +∫ddxJa​ϕa​, the generating functional is
Z[J]=∫Dϕexp[−S[ϕ]+∫ddxJa​(x)ϕa​(x)].
(1)
Here Ja​(x) is a nondynamical source. Its functional derivatives insert fields:
Z[0]1​δJa1​​(x1​)⋯δJan​​(xn​)δnZ[J]​​J=0​=⟨ϕa1​​(x1​)⋯ϕan​​(xn​)⟩.
(2)
This is why Z “generates” correlation functions.
With the notation of the question, W[J]=−logZ[J] is the Euclidean connected generating functional; it is distinct from a Wilsonian effective action. Define the classical field
Φa​(x)=⟨ϕa​(x)⟩J​=−δJa​(x)δW​.
(3)
The quantum effective action is the Legendre transform
Γ[Φ]=W[J]+∫ddxJa​(x)Φa​(x)​,
(4)
where J is eliminated in favor of Φ. Its first derivative is
δΦa​(x)δΓ​=Ja​(x),
(5)
so at zero source the quantum expectation value is a stationary point of Γ. Moreover,
∫ddzΓac(2)​(x,z)Gcb​(z,y)=δab​δ(d)(x−y),
(6)
where Gab​=⟨ϕa​ϕb​⟩J,conn​ is the exact connected correlation function. Thus the second derivative of Γ is the inverse exact propagator.
Perturbatively, Z[J] sums all Feynman diagrams, including disconnected ones. The exponential formula for combinatorial structures says that its logarithm selects connected Feynman diagrams, so W sums connected diagrams with the sign dictated by the convention above. The Legendre transform removes diagrams that disconnect when one internal line is cut; consequently Γ sums one-particle-irreducible Feynman diagrams. Equivalently, every connected diagram is a tree assembled from one-particle-irreducible vertices and full propagators, and the Legendre transform inverts that tree construction.
Now let Ja′​=Jb​Uba​. Then
Z[J′]=∫Dϕe−S[ϕ]+∫Jb​Uba​ϕa​.
(7)
Changing variables to ϕb′​=Uba​ϕa​ and using invariance of both the action and functional measure gives Z[J′]=Z[J], hence W[J′]=W[J]. In the Legendre transform, the pairing obeys
Ja​(Uab​Φb​)=(Jb​Uba​)Φa​.
(8)
Changing the source variable and using the invariance of W therefore gives
Γ[UΦ]=Γ[Φ]​.
(9)
The symmetry of the classical action and measure is inherited by the full quantum effective action when it has no quantum anomaly.

For incoming momenta, the Euclidean momentum-space Feynman rules are
  • a ϕ propagator (p2+m02​)−1;
  • a χ propagator (p2+M02​)−1;
  • a vertex with two ϕ legs and one χ leg equal to −λ0​(2π)dδ(d)(p1​+p2​+p3​);
  • one integration ∫ddℓ/(2π)d for each loop momentum, with division by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor.
The one-loop one-particle-irreducible diagrams with at most three external legs can be classified by their external species. There is a one-point χ tadpole diagram made from a ϕ loop. There are two two-point bubble diagrams: the ϕ self-energy has one internal ϕ and one internal χ, while the χ self-energy has two internal ϕ lines and symmetry factor 1/2. For three external legs, three vertices make a triangle: one triangle corrects the ϕϕχ vertex and contains two internal ϕ lines and one internal χ line; another has three external χ legs and a ϕ loop, generating a χ3 interaction. The symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ forbids amplitudes with an odd number of external ϕ legs. The classical ϕϕχ vertex is the corresponding tree-level three-point diagram.

To make the two-point function finite, write ϕ0​=Zϕ1/2​ϕ, express m02​ in terms of a renormalized mass and a mass counterterm, and choose the pole parts of δZϕ​ and δm2 to cancel Bp2/ϵ and A/ϵ. The superficial degree of divergence counts the ultraviolet power before subdivergences and symmetry cancellations are considered. At d=6, a connected graph made from cubic vertices has
D=6L−2I=6−2E,
(18)
where E is its number of external legs. Hence one-, two-, and three-point functions can have quartic, quadratic, and logarithmic superficial divergences, whereas graphs with more external legs are superficially convergent.
Full renormalization also requires the χ mass and wave-function counterterms from its two-point function, a linear χ counterterm cancelling the tadpole, a coupling counterterm from the divergent ϕϕχ triangle, and a χ3 counterterm from the three-χ triangle. A vacuum-energy counterterm removes divergent vacuum diagrams. These are precisely the local operators allowed by power counting in quantum field theory and the exact ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry.

The quadratic Yang-Mills operator has zero directions along gauge orbits, so it has no propagator until one chooses a gauge fixing. The Faddeev-Popov determinant generated by this choice is represented by anticommuting Faddeev-Popov ghost fields, which cancel unphysical gauge-field contributions in loop calculations. A Nakanishi-Lautrup field imposes the gauge condition algebraically and lets the gauge-fixing plus ghost action be written as a BRST-exact term with off-shell nilpotency.
