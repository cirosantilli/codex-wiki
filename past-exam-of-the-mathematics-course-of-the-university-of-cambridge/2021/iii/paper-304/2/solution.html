For incoming momenta, the Euclidean momentum-space Feynman rules are
  • a ϕ propagator (p2+m02​)−1;
  • a χ propagator (p2+M02​)−1;
  • a vertex with two ϕ legs and one χ leg equal to −λ0​(2π)dδ(d)(p1​+p2​+p3​);
  • one integration ∫ddℓ/(2π)d for each loop momentum, with division by the Feynman-diagram symmetry factor.
The one-loop one-particle-irreducible diagrams with at most three external legs can be classified by their external species. There is a one-point χ tadpole diagram made from a ϕ loop. There are two two-point bubble diagrams: the ϕ self-energy has one internal ϕ and one internal χ, while the χ self-energy has two internal ϕ lines and symmetry factor 1/2. For three external legs, three vertices make a triangle: one triangle corrects the ϕϕχ vertex and contains two internal ϕ lines and one internal χ line; another has three external χ legs and a ϕ loop, generating a χ3 interaction. The symmetry ϕ↦−ϕ forbids amplitudes with an odd number of external ϕ legs. The classical ϕϕχ vertex is the corresponding tree-level three-point diagram.

To make the two-point function finite, write ϕ0​=Zϕ1/2​ϕ, express m02​ in terms of a renormalized mass and a mass counterterm, and choose the pole parts of δZϕ​ and δm2 to cancel Bp2/ϵ and A/ϵ. In the minimal subtraction scheme no additional finite pieces are removed. The physical mass is the pole mass, so with the self-energy convention above it obeys
mphys2​=m2(μ)−ΠMS,fin​(−mphys2​,μ)​.
(17)
The explicit μ dependence of the finite self-energy cancels the running of m(μ), leaving mphys​ independent of the renormalization scale.
The superficial degree of divergence counts the ultraviolet power before subdivergences and symmetry cancellations are considered. At d=6, a connected graph made from cubic vertices has
D=6L−2I=6−2E,
(18)
where E is its number of external legs. Hence one-, two-, and three-point functions can have quartic, quadratic, and logarithmic superficial divergences, whereas graphs with more external legs are superficially convergent.
Full renormalization also requires the χ mass and wave-function counterterms from its two-point function, a linear χ counterterm cancelling the tadpole, a coupling counterterm from the divergent ϕϕχ triangle, and a χ3 counterterm from the three-χ triangle. A vacuum-energy counterterm removes divergent vacuum diagrams. These are precisely the local operators allowed by power counting in quantum field theory and the exact ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry.
