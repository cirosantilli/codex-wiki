For a based space (X,x0​), the homotopy group
πk​(X,x0​)=[(Sk,∗),(X,x0​)]∗​
(1)
is the set of based homotopy classes, with its usual concatenation operation. A map f:X→Y is a weak homotopy equivalence when it induces a bijection on path components and an isomorphism
f∗​:πk​(X,x)∼​πk​(Y,f(x))
(2)
for every k≥1 and every basepoint x. It is an n-connected map when it is bijective on πk​ for k<n and surjective on πn​; equivalently, every homotopy fiber is (n−1)-connected.
A CW complex is built from a discrete set of zero-cells by successively attaching k-discs along maps from their boundary spheres, with the weak topology and closure-finiteness conditions. Its filtration by skeleta is the CW filtration.
For any space X, form its singular simplicial set SingX. Its geometric realization of a simplicial set is a CW complex, with one cell for each nondegenerate singular simplex, and evaluation gives
ϵ:∣SingX∣⟶X.
(3)
The Simplicial approximation theorem identifies based maps and homotopies from finite simplicial spheres into ∣SingX∣ with singular simplices in X. Consequently ϵ induces a bijection on components and isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. Thus every space admits a CW approximation.
The vanishing assumptions do not permit removal of all n-cells. Take n=2 and
X=K(Z/r,1),r>1.
(4)
Then π2​(X)=0, and homology of a finite cyclic group gives H2​(X;Z)=0. If a connected CW complex Y had no two-cells, attaching cells of dimension at least three would not change the fundamental group of its one-skeleton. Hence π1​(Y) would be a free group. A weak equivalence Y→X would instead give π1​(Y)≅Z/r, which is nontrivial and finite and therefore not free. No such Y exists.

A map p:E→B is a Serre fibration when it has the homotopy lifting property for every disc: given g:Dk→E and H:Dk×I→B with pg=H(−,0), there is a lift H:Dk×I→E satisfying H(−,0)=g and pH=H.
Fix b0​∈B, e0​∈F=p−1(b0​), and write i:F↪E. The long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration is
⋯→πk​(F,e0​)i∗​​πk​(E,e0​)p∗​​πk​(B,b0​)∂​πk−1​(F,e0​)→⋯.
(5)
The first two maps are induced by inclusion and projection. To define ∂, represent a class of πk​(B) by a map of pairs (Dk,Sk−1)→(B,b0​), lift it beginning at e0​ along radial paths, and restrict the lift to Sk−1; that restriction lies in F. At the bottom, exactness continues through the pointed sets π1​(B)→π0​(F)→π0​(E)→π0​(B).
Every fiber bundle is a Serre fibration. Pull a bundle back along H:Dk×I→B; a lift of H is the same as a section of this pullback extending the section over Dk×{0} supplied by g. Since Dk×I is compact, finitely many bundle charts cover it. A Lebesgue-number subdivision of I, followed by a finite subdivision of Dk, makes each resulting prism lie in one chart. In a trivialization U×F→U, extend the section across a prism by keeping its F-coordinate constant along the interval direction. Proceed prism by prism and time-slab by time-slab; on an already treated face use its prescribed coordinate, and the transition functions ensure agreement on overlaps. The resulting sections glue to the required lift H.
Now consider i:RPn↪CPn. The target is simply connected, so i∗​ is zero on π1​. For k≥2, every based map Sk→RPn lifts through the double cover to Sn. The composite
Sn⟶RPni​CPn,x⟼[Cx],
(6)
lifts through the Hopf fibration S2n+1→CPn to the real-coordinate inclusion Sn↪S2n+1. This inclusion is null-homotopic because πn​(S2n+1)=0. Hence i∗​ is zero on every πk​, including the cases n=1 and k≥2 where the source groups already vanish.

Because Sn is simply connected for n≥2, the homological Serre spectral sequence has constant coefficients and only two nonzero columns:
Ep,q2​=Hp​(Sn;Hq​(F))={Hq​(F),0,​p=0,n,otherwise.​
(7)
Its only possible nonzero differential is
dn​:En,in​⟶E0,i+n−1n​.
(8)
Using the orientation generator of Hn​(Sn) to identify both columns with H∗​(F) defines the Wang homomorphism
Δ:Hi​(F)⟶Hi+n−1​(F).
(9)
The kernel and cokernel descriptions of the two surviving columns splice with the filtration of H∗​(E) to give the Wang sequence over a sphere
⋯→Hj​(F)→Hj​(E)→Hj−n​(F)Δ​Hj−1​(F)→Hj−1​(E)→⋯.
(10)
Let Fm​ be the homotopy fiber of a degree-m map fm​:Sn→Sn. Apply this sequence to the fibration
Fm​⟶Snfm​​Sn.
(11)
At the bottom, the map between the two copies of Hn​(Sn) is multiplication by m. It follows that
Hj​(Fm​;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/m,0,​j=0,j=k(n−1) for some k≥1,otherwise.​
(12)
The first torsion group can also be seen from πn−1​(Fm​)≅Z/m and the Hurewicz theorem; the Wang map then propagates it periodically.
The universal coefficient theorem for cohomology gives
Hj(Fm​;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z/m,0,​j=0,j=k(n−1)+1 for some k≥1,otherwise.​
(13)
Every product of two positive-degree classes is zero. For n>2, this follows immediately because the sum of two degrees of the form k(n−1)+1 is not of that form. For n=2, degree counting does not suffice, since Hj(Fm​)≅Z/m for every j≥2. Use instead the multiplicative cohomological Serre spectral sequence for
ΩS2⟶Fm​⟶S2.
(14)
Its transgression in degree one is multiplication by m. Every positive integral cohomology class that survives lies in filtration two, while the product of two such classes lies in filtration four; the base has dimension two, so filtration four is zero. Thus the reduced cohomology is a square-zero ideal, and
H∗(Fm​;Z)=Z⊕⨁k≥1​(Z/m)[k(n−1)+1]
(15)
as a graded ring, with zero multiplication on the second summand.

The space K(Z/n,1) is a classifying space B(Z/n). The periodic resolution of a finite cyclic group gives
H∗(K(Z/n,1);Z)≅Z[c]/(nc),∣c∣=2.
(16)
Thus positive odd cohomology vanishes and every positive even group is Z/n.
Let p be odd. If p∤n, transfer makes multiplication by n both zero and invertible on positive-degree cohomology, so
H∗(K(Z/n,1);Fp​)=Fp​.
(17)
If p∣n, restriction to the cyclic Sylow p-subgroup and transfer give
H∗(K(Z/n,1);Fp​)≅Λ(u)⊗Fp​[v],∣u∣=1,∣v∣=2.
(18)
For n=p, one may take v=βu, where β is the mod-p Bockstein homomorphism.
For the second part put A=Z/p and X=K(A,1)×K(A,1). The long exact homotopy sequence of the homotopy fibre of f:X→K(A,2) gives
1⟶A⟶G:=π1​(F)⟶A2⟶1
(19)
and πk​(F)=0 for k>1. Therefore F is a K(G,1) and ∣G∣=p3.
Regard it as the fibration
K(A,1)⟶F⟶K(A,1)2.
(20)
Write
H∗(K(A,1)2;Fp​)=Λ(u1​,u2​)⊗Fp​[v1​,v2​]
(21)
and write w,t for the degree-one and degree-two generators of the fibre. The fibration is classified by u1​u2​, so in its cohomological Serre spectral sequence
d2​(w)=u1​u2​.
(22)
The Kudo transgression theorem and the mod-p Bockstein give
d3​(t)=β(u1​u2​)=v1​u2​−u1​v2​=0.
(23)
Consequently H1(F;Fp​) has basis u1​,u2​, while H2(F;Fp​) has the four surviving classes represented by
v1​,v2​,u1​w,u2​w.
(24)
If G were abelian, an abelian group of order p3 mapping onto A2 would be either A3 or Z/p2×A. The first has three-dimensional H1(−;Fp​); the second has two-dimensional H1 but three-dimensional H2. Both contradict the dimensions just calculated. Hence G is nonabelian.

Let i:F↪E. A pair
(x,y)∈Hr(F;F2​)×Hr+1(B;F2​)
(25)
is transgressive pair when, in the long exact sequence of the pair (E,F),
δx=π∗y∈Hr+1(E,F;F2​),
(26)
where Hr+1(B,b0​) is identified with reduced cohomology. In the Serre spectral sequence, this says that x survives to the transgression and
dr+1​(x)=y
(27)
under the edge identifications, modulo the usual earlier-differential indeterminacy.
The Kudo transgression theorem says that if (x,y) is transgressive and 0≤j≤r, then
(Sqjx,Sqjy)
(28)
is transgressive. To prove it, use relative Steenrod squares. Naturality gives
Sqj(π∗y)=π∗(Sqjy),
(29)
and stability, equivalently compatibility with the suspension isomorphism, makes squares commute with the connecting map:
Sqj(δx)=δ(Sqjx).
(30)
Applying Sqj to δx=π∗y proves the theorem. The properties used are naturality, stability, additivity, and the instability conditions Sqjz=0 for j>∣z∣ and Sq∣z∣z=z2.
Let x∈H1(RP∞;F2​) be the generator. Instability gives
Sq(x)=Sq0x+Sq1x=x+x2.
(31)
The Cartan formula says the total square is multiplicative, so
Sq(xk)=(x+x2)k=∑j=0k​(jk​)xk+j.
(32)
Comparing components yields the complete formula
Sqj(xk)=(jk​)xk+j​,
(33)
with the binomial coefficient reduced modulo two.
Finally, Sq1 is the Bockstein homomorphism associated with
0⟶Z/2⟶Z/4⟶Z/2⟶0.

If a mod-two cocycle representing y is lifted to an integral cochain A, write dA=2B. Then B modulo two represents Sq1y. But dB=0, because integral cochains are torsion-free and 2dB=d2A=0. Thus B itself is a cocycle lift, so its Bockstein vanishes. Therefore
Sq1Sq1(y)=0
(35)
