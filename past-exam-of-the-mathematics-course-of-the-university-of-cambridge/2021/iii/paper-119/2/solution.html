Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2021 / iii / Paper 119 / 2 / Solution

For an adjoint functor pair F:D⇄C:G, the unit and counit of an adjunction are
η:1D​⇒GF,ε:FG⇒1C​.
(5)
Under the adjunction bijection, ηA​ corresponds to 1FA​ and εB​ corresponds to 1GB​. They satisfy the triangle identities
εFA​F(ηA​)=1FA​,G(εB​)ηGB​=1GB​.
(6)
Conversely, natural transformations with these identities recover the adjunction through the mutually inverse maps
f:FA→B⟼G(f)ηA​,g:A→GB⟼εB​F(g).
(7)
The fully faithful adjoint criterion gives the first equivalence directly. If F is full and faithful, there is a unique rA​:GFA→A with F(rA​)=εFA​; the triangle identity and faithfulness show that rA​ and ηA​ are inverse, so the unit is an isomorphism. If the unit is an isomorphism, the displayed adjunction bijection shows that
D(A,A′)⟶C(FA,FA′)
(8)
is bijective, so F is full and faithful. This also proves that either condition gives a natural isomorphism GF≅1D​.
For the remaining direction, suppose merely that GF is naturally isomorphic to the identity. Transport the monad induced by an adjunction along this isomorphism. Its underlying endofunctor is then the identity, its unit is a natural endomorphism u:1⇒1, and its multiplication is a natural endomorphism m:1⇒1 with mu=1. Naturality makes uA​ commute with mA​, so also um=1. Hence the transported unit, and therefore η, is an isomorphism. The three conditions are equivalent.
Now let F⊣G⊣H. If F is full and faithful, then GF≅1D​. For A,B∈D, the two adjunctions give natural bijections
D(B,GHA)≅C(FB,HA)≅D(GFB,A)≅D(B,A).
(9)
The Yoneda lemma therefore gives GHA≅A, naturally in A. The fully faithful adjoint criterion applied to G⊣H shows that H is full and faithful. Conversely, if H is full and faithful, then GH≅1D​ and
D(GFA,B)≅C(FA,HB)≅D(A,GHB)≅D(A,B).
(10)
Another application of the Yoneda lemma gives GFA≅A, so F is full and faithful. Thus F is full and faithful exactly when H is.
Assume henceforth that G is full and faithful. Then the counit ε:FG→1C​ and unit α:1C​→HG are natural isomorphisms. Consider
θ1​=(αF)−1(Hη),θ2​=(Fβ)(εH)−1:H⇒F.
(11)
Applying the faithful functor G, then using naturality and the four triangle identities, turns both composites into
GHβ​1D​η​GF.
(12)
Therefore θ1​=θ2​; denote their common value by θ, the double-adjoint comparison transformation.
The pointwise monicity criterion is clearest from the following natural square, in which both vertical maps are bijections:
C(B,HA)↓D(GB,A)​θA​∘−​F​​C(B,FA)↓C(FGB,FA).​
(13)
The left vertical map is the adjunction G⊣H, while the right one precomposes with the isomorphism εB​:FGB→B. Thus every θA​ is a monomorphism exactly when F is faithful on all morphisms GB→A, namely morphisms whose domains lie in the image of G.
Dually, the natural square
C(FA,B)↓D(A,GB)​−∘θA​​H​​C(HA,B)↓C(HA,HGB)​
(14)
has bijective vertical maps, using F⊣G on the left and the isomorphism αB​:B→HGB on the right. Hence every θA​ is an epimorphism exactly when H is faithful on all morphisms A→GB, namely morphisms whose codomains lie in the image of G.
