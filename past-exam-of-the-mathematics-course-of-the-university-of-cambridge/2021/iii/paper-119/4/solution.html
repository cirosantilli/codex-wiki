A monad on C is an endofunctor T with natural transformations
η:1C​⇒T,μ:T2⇒T
(18)
satisfying μTμ=μμT and μTη=1T​=μηT. Its Eilenberg-Moore category CT has algebras for a monad (A,a:TA→A) satisfying
aηA​=1A​,aT(a)=aμA​,
(19)
and morphisms of algebras for a monad f:(A,a)→(B,b) satisfying fa=bT(f).
The Kleisli category CT​ has the objects of C and
CT​(A,B)=C(A,TB).
(20)
Its identity is ηA​, while the composite of f:A→TB and g:B→TC is μC​T(g)f. The free functor into a Kleisli category FT​:C→CT​ is the identity on objects and sends f:A→B to ηB​f.
On the functor category [D,C], postcomposition gives the pointwise monad on a functor category
T∗​(F)=TF,(η∗​)F​=ηF,(μ∗​)F​=μF.
(21)
The monad laws hold componentwise. A T∗​-algebra is a functor F:D→C with a natural transformation a:TF→F whose components are T-algebras. Naturality says precisely that every F(u) is an algebra morphism. Hence sending (F,a) to the lifted functor D→CT gives an isomorphism, and in particular an equivalence,
[D,C]T∗​≃[D,CT].
(22)
On [C,D], precomposition gives the precomposition monad on a functor category
T∗(G)=GT,(η∗)G​=Gη,(μ∗)G​=Gμ.
(23)
A T∗-algebra is a natural transformation a:GT→G satisfying aGη=1G​ and a(aT)=aGμ. From it define Gˉ:CT​→D by
Gˉ(A)=G(A),Gˉ(f:A→TB)=aB​G(f).
(24)
The two algebra laws say exactly that Gˉ preserves identities and Kleisli composition, and GˉFT​=G. Conversely, a factorization G=GˉFT​ gives
aA​=Gˉ(1TA​:TA→TA),
(25)
where 1TA​ represents a Kleisli arrow TA→A. These constructions are inverse on objects and morphisms, so
[C,D]T∗≃[CT​,D].​
(26)
