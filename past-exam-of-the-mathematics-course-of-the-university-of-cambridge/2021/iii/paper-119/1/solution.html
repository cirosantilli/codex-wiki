For a locally small category C, an object A, and a functor F:C→Set, the covariant Yoneda lemma is the natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)⟶F(A),α⟼αA​(1A​).
(1)
Its inverse sends x∈F(A) to the natural transformation whose component at B maps f:A→B to F(f)(x).
Suppose now that C is a small category. For F:C→Set, form the coproduct in a category
P=∐(A,x), x∈F(A)​C(A,−).
(2)
The Yoneda lemma associates to every summand the natural transformation determined by x, and these transformations combine to a map p:P→F. At an object B, the element y∈F(B) is the image of 1B​ in the summand indexed by (B,y), so p is a pointwise epimorphism in a functor category. Each representable functor C(A,−) is a projective object in a category, since
Nat(C(A,−),−)≅evA​
(3)
and evaluation preserves pointwise epimorphisms. A coproduct of projectives is projective, so P is the required projective object. This is the projective cover of a set-valued functor by representables.
We next prove the three equivalent conditions. If every morphism of C is a monomorphism, then for f:B→C and every A, postcomposition
C(A,B)⟶C(A,C),g⟼fg
(4)
is injective. Thus every covariant representable functor is a monofunctor. Conversely, taking A=B shows that injectivity for every representable implies that fg=fh forces g=h, so every f is monic.
If all representables are monofunctors, the object P above is a monofunctor because a coproduct of injective functions is injective. Hence every F is an epimorphic image of a monofunctor. Conversely, suppose every functor is an epimorphic image of a monofunctor and apply this to a representable R. Choose an epimorphism q:M↠R with M a monofunctor. Since R is projective, 1R​ lifts to s:R→M with qs=1R​. Thus R is a retract in a category of M. Every retract of a monofunctor is a monofunctor: if R(f)x=R(f)y, then injectivity of M(f) applied to s(x) and s(y) gives s(x)=s(y), and applying q gives x=y. This completes the equivalence.
Finally, every functor C→Set is a monofunctor exactly when every morphism of C is a split monomorphism. The forward implication is immediate because every functor preserves a left inverse. For the converse, fix f:A→B and form a quotient of C(A,−)⊔1 by identifying the distinguished point with every arrow of the form kf:A→X, where k:B→X. In the resulting functor Q, the two elements [1A​] and ∗ of Q(A) have equal images under Q(f) because [f]=∗. If every functor is a monofunctor, Q(f) is injective, so [1A​]=∗. By construction this means 1A​=rf for some r:B→A. Thus f is split monic. Equivalently, every morphism of C must be an absolute monomorphism.
