Let p∈ProjT. Some homogeneous t∈T+​ lies outside p. Choose N with Ndegt≥d0​. Since φNdegt​ is surjective, tN=φ(s) for some homogeneous s∈S+​; primality gives tN∈/p. Thus p∈U, and U=ProjT.
Fix a positive-degree homogeneous s∈S and write q=φ(s). The map
(Ss​)0​⟶(Tq​)0​
(6)
is surjective: after multiplying the numerator and denominator of any degree-zero fraction by a sufficiently large power of q, its numerator has degree at least d0​ and therefore lifts through φ. It is injective by the same device: if a/sn maps to zero, then qmφ(a)=0 for some m, and after increasing m the injectivity of φ in degree (m+n)degs gives sma=0. Hence the displayed map is a ring isomorphism.
The opens D+​(q) obtained in this way cover ProjT, and on every one of them f is an isomorphism onto D+​(s). The inverses agree on overlaps, so f is an isomorphism of schemes. This proves the Invariance of Proj under an eventual graded isomorphism.

Put
R=k[x,y,z,w]/(xy−zw).
(7)
Since
R/(x,z)≅k[y,w]
(8)
is an integral domain, (x,z) is a prime ideal. Moreover dimR=3 and dimR/(x,z)=2, so its height of an ideal is one. Therefore D=V(x,z) is a prime Weil divisor.
Localizing at x eliminates y and gives
Rx​≅k[x,z,w,x−1],
(9)
a unique factorization domain. The Nagata theorem for divisor class groups says that Cl(R) is generated by the height-one primes containing x. Since
R/(x)≅k[y,z,w]/(zw),
(10)
these are D=V(x,z) and E=V(x,w). Both occur with multiplicity one in the principal divisor
div(x)=D+E.
(11)
The units of Rx​≅k[z,w][x,x−1] are exactly cxn with c∈k∗ and n∈Z. Consequently the only relation supplied by localization is [D]+[E]=0, and
Cl(X)≅Z([D]+[E])Z[D]⊕Z[E]​≅Z[D].
(12)
This is the divisor class group of the three-dimensional affine quadric cone.

The two pairs of regular functions
[x:w]and[z:y]
(16)
define maps to the projective line on the loci where their respective coordinates do not vanish simultaneously. Those loci cover Y, since simultaneous failure would force x=y=z=w=0. On their overlap the equation xy=zw says that the two projective points are equal. They therefore glue to a morphism
f:Y⟶Pk1​.
(17)
Let [A:B] be the homogeneous coordinates on Pk1​. The pullback of the hyperplane divisor V(A) has local equation x on the first chart and z on the second. It is therefore exactly DY​=V(x,z). Compatibility of the pullback of a sheaf of modules with the line bundle associated to a divisor gives
f∗OPk1​​(1)≅OY​(DY​).
(18)
This is the ruling morphism of the punctured three-dimensional affine quadric cone associated with DY​.

Because X is a Noetherian scheme, choose a finite affine cover X=⋃i​Ui​. Every intersection Ui​∩Uj​ is quasi-compact, so choose a finite affine cover Ui​∩Uj​=⋃k​Uijk​. If ji​:Ui​↪X and jijk​:Uijk​↪X are the inclusions, the sheaf axiom gives an exact sequence
0⟶F⟶⨁i​(ji​)∗​(F∣Ui​​)⟶⨁i,j,k​(jijk​)∗​(F∣Uijk​​),
(23)
where the last arrow is the difference of the two restrictions to each overlap chart.
Applying the left-exact direct image sheaf functor identifies f∗​F with the kernel of
⨁i​(fji​)∗​(F∣Ui​​)⟶⨁i,j,k​(fjijk​)∗​(F∣Uijk​​).
(24)
Every map Ui​→Y and Uijk​→Y is a morphism between affine schemes. Part (a) shows that all sheaves in the two finite sums are quasi-coherent sheaves. Kernels of morphisms of quasi-coherent sheaves on the affine scheme Y are quasi-coherent, because they correspond to kernels of module homomorphisms. Therefore f∗​F is quasi-coherent. This is the quasi-coherence of direct image under a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism in the present Noetherian case.

The kernel of the restriction map
Γ(X,F)⟶Γ(X∖Z,F)
(25)
consists exactly of sections whose germs vanish outside Z, namely ΓZ​(X,F). This proves exactness. If F is a flasque sheaf, the restriction map is surjective by definition.
Now let 0→F1​→F2​→F3​→0 be exact. The global section functor is left exact, so a section of ΓZ​(X,F2​) mapping to zero lifts uniquely to a global section of F1​. Its germs outside Z vanish because F1​→F2​ is injective at each stalk of a sheaf. It therefore lies in ΓZ​(X,F1​), proving exactness of the supported-section sequence.
Suppose in addition that F1​ is flasque and take s3​∈ΓZ​(X,F3​). Surjectivity on global sections gives a lift s2​∈Γ(X,F2​). On X∖Z, its restriction comes from some t1​∈Γ(X∖Z,F1​) by left exactness. Extend t1​ to t1​∈Γ(X,F1​) using flasqueness. Then s2​−t1​ maps to s3​ and vanishes outside Z, proving surjectivity on the right.

Write A=k[x,y], X=SpecA, and U=X∖Z. Since A is an integral domain, no nonzero global section is supported only at the origin, so
HZ0​(X,OX​)=0.

0⟶Ax​⊕Ay​(a,b)↦a−b​Axy​⟶0.

Consequently
H0(U,OU​)=A,H1(U,OU​)=Ax​+Ay​Axy​​,Hi(U,OU​)=0(i≥2).
