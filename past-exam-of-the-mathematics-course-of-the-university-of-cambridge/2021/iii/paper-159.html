The Picard group Pic(X) is the set of isomorphism classes of line bundles on X, with tensor product as addition, the trivial bundle as zero and the dual bundle as inverse. On a smooth projective surface, line bundles can be represented by divisors. The intersection pairing on the Picard group of a surface is the symmetric bilinear map
Pic(X)×Pic(X)⟶Z,([D],[E])⟼D⋅E,
(1)
obtained by moving the divisors into proper position and counting their intersections with multiplicity.
The surface
PP1​(O⊕O(1))
(2)
is the first Hirzebruch surface F1​. If S is its negative section and F a fiber of the ruling, its Picard lattice of a Hirzebruch surface is
Pic(F1​)=ZS⊕ZF,S2=−1,quadS⋅F=1,quadF2=0.
(3)
Now let π:X→P2 be the given nonisomorphic birational morphism. A birational morphism between smooth projective surfaces factors as a nonempty sequence of point blowups. Let H=π∗[line], and take the total transform E on X of the exceptional curve of the first blowup. The intersection formula for blowing up a surface gives
H2=1,qquadE2=−1,qquadH⋅E=0.
(4)
Therefore the nonzero Picard group element H+E satisfies
(H+E)2=1−1=0.
(5)
This is the isotropic divisor from a nontrivial birational morphism to the projective plane.
For a morphism ϕ:P2→Pn, the pullback of the hyperplane bundle has the form
ϕ∗OPn​(1)≅OP2​(d)
(6)
for an integer d≥0. If a line ℓ is contracted to a point, this bundle restricts trivially to ℓ, whereas
OP2​(d)∣ℓ​≅OP1​(d).
(7)
Its degree is therefore zero, so d=0. The homogeneous sections defining ϕ are then constants, and ϕ is constant. This proves the morphism from the projective plane contracting a line criterion.
Finally choose an integer r>C2 and blow up r distinct points of the smooth curve C. For the resulting morphism π:X′→X, let E1​,…,Er​ be the exceptional curves. The strict transform is
C′=π∗C−∑i=1r​Ei​,
(8)
and the self-intersection after blowing up points on a smooth curve formula gives
(C′)2=C2−r<0.
(9)
Blowing up a smooth point of a smooth curve does not change that curve itself, so π∣C′​:C′→C is an isomorphism.

A minimal algebraic surface is a smooth projective surface containing no exceptional curve of the first kind, namely no smooth rational curve of self-intersection −1. An abelian surface contains no rational curve because every morphism from P1 to an abelian variety is constant. It therefore has no (−1)-curve and is minimal.
For every n≥2, the Minimal Hirzebruch surface Fn​ is a rational minimal surface. These surfaces are pairwise nonisomorphic: the negative section is the unique irreducible curve of negative self-intersection and has square −n, so an isomorphism would recover n. Thus there are infinitely many nonisomorphic minimal rational surfaces.
A K3 surface is a smooth projective surface X with KX​≅OX​ and H1(X,OX​)=0. For a smooth curve C⊂X of geometric genus g, the adjunction formula gives
2g−2=C⋅(C+KX​)=C2,
(10)
and hence
C2=2g−2​.
(11)
An elliptic surface is a smooth projective surface with a morphism to a smooth curve whose generic fiber is a smooth genus-one curve. If E is an elliptic curve, projection
P1×E⟶P1
(12)
is an elliptic fibration. Its canonical bundle is pulled back from KP1​, so every positive pluricanonical space vanishes and the Kodaira dimension is −∞. This supplies the requested negative-Kodaira-dimension example.
For an elliptically fibered K3 surface, choose a smooth quartic X⊆P3 containing a line L. The canonical bundle of a smooth projective hypersurface formula makes KX​ trivial, and the standard cohomology sequence gives H1(X,OX​)=0, so X is K3. The pencil of planes through L cuts X into L plus a residual plane cubic. The residual linear system ∣H−L∣ is basepoint-free, has square zero and defines a morphism X→P1 whose generic fiber is a smooth plane cubic. This is the Elliptic K3 surface from a quartic containing a line.
A surface of general type is a smooth projective surface of Kodaira dimension two. Let B⊆P2 be a smooth plane curve of degree eight and let
π:X⟶P2
(13)
be the degree-two cover branched along B. The branch-cover canonical-bundle formula gives
KX​=π∗(KP2​+4H)=π∗H.
(14)
This divisor is ample, so X is a double plane of general type and π is the required finite morphism of degree two.

The irregularity of an algebraic surface and the geometric genus of an algebraic surface are respectively
q(X)=h1(X,OX​)=h0(X,ΩX1​),pg​(X)=h0(X,KX​)=h2(X,OX​).
(15)
If π:X′→X is the blowup at a point with exceptional divisor E, then
KX′​=π∗KX​+E.
(16)
A holomorphic two-form on X pulls back to one on X′. Conversely, a holomorphic two-form on X′ descends across E: locally it is a form on the punctured smooth surface X∖{p}, and its coefficients extend over the codimension-two point p. Equivalently, π∗​KX′​=KX​. Pullback is therefore an isomorphism
H0(X,KX​)≅H0(X′,KX′​),
(17)
which proves the birational invariance of the geometric genus of a surface in this case.
Let X=C×D, where both smooth projective curves have positive genus. The Künneth theorem gives the irregularity of a product of curves
q(X)=g(C)+g(D)>0.
(18)
If a surface is a hypersurface in projective space, dimension forces it to be a smooth hypersurface Y⊆P3. From
0⟶OP3​(−d)⟶OP3​⟶OY​⟶0
(19)
and the intermediate cohomology vanishing for line bundles on projective space, one obtains H1(Y,OY​)=0. Thus every such hypersurface has irregularity zero, whereas X has positive irregularity. Hence C×D is not isomorphic to a hypersurface. This is the product of positive-genus curves is not a projective hypersurface obstruction.
The Albanese variety Alb(X) is the universal abelian variety receiving a pointed morphism from X, and
dimAlb(X)=q(X).
(20)
Let the smooth image curve of the Albanese morphism be C of genus g. Pullback of holomorphic one-forms along the dominant map X→C is injective, giving g≤q(X). On the other hand, the universal property of the Jacobian extends C→Alb(X) to a homomorphism
Jac(C)⟶Alb(X).
(21)
The Albanese image generates the entire Albanese variety, so this homomorphism is surjective and q(X)≤dimJac(C)=g. Therefore the Irregularity from a smooth Albanese curve image is
q(X)=g​.
(22)
