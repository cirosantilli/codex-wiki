The space of test functions is
D(R)=Cc∞​(R).
(1)
A sequence φm​ converges to φ in D(R) when all supports lie in one compact set K and
supx∈K​∣φm(j)​(x)−φ(j)(x)∣⟶0
(2)
for every nonnegative integer j. The distribution space D′(R) is the continuous dual space of D(R), and um​→u in D′ means
⟨um​,φ⟩⟶⟨u,φ⟩
(3)
for every test function φ.
If a linear form u is continuous, it clearly maps every null sequence to a scalar sequence tending to zero. Conversely, suppose it has this sequential property. For each compact K, its restriction to the Fréchet space DK​ must be continuous: otherwise, for every m one could choose φm​∈DK​ such that
max0≤j≤m​∥φm(j)​∥∞​≤m1​,∣⟨u,φm​⟩∣≥1.
(4)
Then φm​→0 in D but its images do not tend to zero, a contradiction. Continuity on every DK​ is precisely continuity for the strict inductive limit topology of D, so u∈D′.
Use the convention τh​φ(x)=φ(x−h). Translation and the distributional derivative are defined by
⟨τh​u,φ⟩=⟨u,τ−h​φ⟩,⟨u′,φ⟩=−⟨u,φ′⟩.
(5)
Translation, differentiation, and multiplication by −1 are continuous maps on D, so these formulas define continuous linear functionals and hence distributions.
For fixed φ, the difference quotient satisfies
hτh​φ−φ​⟶−φ′in D.
(6)
Therefore
⟨hτ−h​u−u​,φ⟩=⟨u,hτh​φ−φ​⟩⟶−⟨u,φ′⟩=⟨u′,φ⟩,
(7)
which proves u′=limh→0​(τ−h​u−u)/h in D′.
For −1<λ<0, integration by parts after subtracting the value at zero gives
⟨(x+λ​)′,φ⟩=∫0∞​[φ(x)−φ(0)]λxλ−1dx​.
(8)
The subtraction makes the integrand locally integrable at zero, and xλ→0 handles the other boundary.
The analogous Hadamard finite-part integral is
⟨(logx+​)′,φ⟩=∫01​xφ(x)−φ(0)​dx+∫1∞​xφ(x)​dx​.
(9)
Indeed, integrating from ε and combining the boundary term φ(ε)logε with the divergent constant part of the integral gives this limit.
Because x+λ​ is a locally integrable function, its first distributional derivative has order at most one. It is not of order zero. Choose ψ∈D((0,1)) with ∫01​λtλ−1ψ(t)dt=0 and put ψε​(x)=ψ(x/ε). The sup norms stay bounded while
⟨(x+λ​)′,ψε​⟩=ελ∫01​λtλ−1ψ(t)dt
(10)

A phase function is a real smooth function
Φ:X×(Rk∖{0})→R
(11)
that is positively homogeneous of degree one in θ and has no critical point in all variables:
Φ(x,tθ)=tΦ(x,θ),∇x,θ​Φ(x,θ)=0.
(12)
The symbol class Sym(X,Rk;N) consists of all a∈C∞(X×Rk) such that, for every compact K⋐X and multi-indices α,β,
∣Dxα​Dθβ​a(x,θ)∣≤CK,α,β​(1+∣θ∣)N−∣β∣.
(13)
For a cutoff χ∈Cc∞​(Rk) equal to one near zero, define the oscillatory integral by
⟨IΦ​(a),ψ⟩=ε↓0lim​∫X​∫Rk​eiΦ(x,θ)a(x,θ)ψ(x)χ(εθ)dθdx​.
(14)
Repeated integration by parts makes the limit meaningful and independent of the cutoff.
The singular support is the complement of the largest open set on which the distribution is represented by a smooth function. Suppose x0​ does not belong to
CΦ​={x:∇θ​Φ(x,θ)=0 for some θ=0}.
(15)
On a sufficiently small neighborhood of x0​, homogeneity and compactness of the unit sphere give a lower bound for ∣∇θ​Φ∣. The differential operator
L=i∣∇θ​Φ∣2∇θ​Φ⋅∇θ​​
(16)
satisfies LeiΦ=eiΦ. Repeatedly transferring L to the amplitude lowers its symbol order until the integral and all its x-derivatives converge absolutely. Hence IΦ​(a) is smooth near x0​, proving
singsuppIΦ​(a)⊆CΦ​​.
(17)
For the stated distribution on R3, rotational symmetry lets us align the polar axis with x and write ρ=∣x∣. The angular integral is
∫02π​∫0π​eirρcosθsinθdθdϕ=4πrρsin(rρ)​.
(18)
Therefore, for ρ>0,
u(x)​=2π1​ρ4π​∫0∞​1+r2rsin(rρ)​dr=ρπe−ρ​​.​
(19)
The original amplitude is not Lebesgue integrable in k, so this computation illustrates how oscillation assigns a distribution to a divergent ordinary integral. The result is smooth away from the origin and has singular support {0}. It is a constant multiple of the three-dimensional Yukawa potential, satisfying (−Δ+1)u=4π2δ0​ with the normalization used in the question.

Using multi-index notation, the Schwartz space is
S(Rn)={φ∈C∞:pα,β​(φ)=supx​∣xαDβφ(x)∣<∞ for all α,β}.
(25)
Its topology is generated by the displayed seminorms. The tempered distribution S′(Rn) is its continuous dual, with weak convergence defined by convergence of every pairing with a Schwartz function.
For the convention
φ​(λ)=∫Rn​e−iλ⋅xφ(x)dx,
(26)

Dλα​φ​=(−ix)αφ​,λβφ​=(−iDx​)βφ​.
(27)
These identities bound every Schwartz seminorm of φ​ by finitely many seminorms of φ, proving continuity. The Fourier inversion theorem gives the continuous inverse
φ(x)=(2π)n1​∫eiλ⋅xφ​(λ)dλ,
(28)
so the Fourier transform is a continuous isomorphism of S. By duality,
⟨u,φ⟩=⟨u,φ​⟩
(29)
defines a continuous isomorphism of S′.
