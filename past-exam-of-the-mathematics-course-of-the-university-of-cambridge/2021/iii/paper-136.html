One form of the Hensel lemma is the following. Let R be a complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal m, and let f∈R[X]. If a0​∈R satisfies
f(a0​)≡0(modm),f′(a0​)≡0(modm),
(1)
then there is a unique a∈R with f(a)=0 and a≡a0​(modm).
Define an+1​=an​−f(an​)/f′(an​). Since f′(an​) remains a unit, Taylor expansion gives
f(an+1​)≡0(modf(an​)2),
(2)
so the valuations of the errors at least double. The corrections tend to zero, making (an​) a Cauchy sequence; completeness gives a limit a, and continuity gives f(a)=0. If a,b are two such roots, then
0=f(a)−f(b)=(a−b)(f′(a0​)+u)
(3)
with u∈m, so the second factor is a unit and a=b.

Put f(X)=X3+5X−12. Any root in Qp​ is a p-adic integer: if its valuation were negative, X3 would be the unique term of least valuation.
For p=2, reduction modulo two has roots zero and one. The root zero is simple because f′(0)=5 is odd, so it lifts uniquely. An odd integer x satisfies x2≡1(mod8), and hence
f(x)=x(x2+5)−12≡6x−4≡0(mod8).
(4)
Thus there is no odd 2-adic root and the number of roots is one.
For p=3,
f(X)≡X3−X=X(X−1)(X+1)(mod3).
(5)
All three roots are simple because f′(X)=3X2+5≡2(mod3). Each lifts uniquely, giving three roots in Q3​.
For p=5, reduction gives X3−2, whose unique root is X=3; it is simple because 3X2≡0(mod5). It lifts uniquely, so there is one root in Q5​.

For odd p there is a decomposition
Qp×​≅pZ×μp−1​×(1+pZp​)
(6)
into the valuation factor, the Teichmuller representative factor, and the group of principal units. If x is a ((p−1)m)th power for every m coprime to p, its valuation is divisible by every such (p−1)m, and is therefore zero. Its residue in Fp×​ is a (p−1)st power, hence is one. Thus x∈1+pZp​.
Conversely, exponentiation by any integer n coprime to p is an automorphism of 1+pZp​. This follows either from the principal-unit logarithm, under which it becomes multiplication by n, or by applying the Hensel lemma to Yn−x. Since (p−1)m is coprime to p, every x∈1+pZp​ has a ((p−1)m)th root for every allowed m.

The non-Archimedean part of the Ostrowski theorem says that every nontrivial Non-Archimedean absolute value on Q is equivalent to the p-adic absolute value for a unique prime p.
Indeed, ∣n∣≤1 for every integer n. Nontriviality gives a prime p with ∣p∣<1, and there cannot be two such primes because the Bezout identity would make 1 a sum of two terms of absolute value below one. If a is coprime to p, another Bezout identity shows ∣a∣=1. Consequently
∣x∣=∣p∣vp​(x)
(7)
for every x∈Q×, which is a positive real power of ∣x∣p​.
More generally, the Non-Archimedean absolute values on a number field K are indexed, up to equivalence, by the nonzero prime ideals p⊂OK​. The value attached to p is
∣x∣p​=c−vp​(x),c>1.
(8)
To prove completeness of the list, restrict an absolute value to Q and obtain a rational prime p. Its valuation ring contains OK​ away from a unique prime above p; equivalently, its center
p={a∈OK​:∣a∣<1}
(9)
is a nonzero prime ideal. Since OK,p​ is a discrete valuation ring, every x∈K× is a unit times a power of a uniformizer, so the given value is equivalent to the displayed p-adic value. An absolute value trivial on Q is trivial on the algebraic extension K, so no further cases occur.

Choose a K-basis of L. Any extension ∣⋅∣L​ defines a norm on the finite-dimensional K-vector space L, and all norms on such a space over a complete valued field induce the same topology. Hence any two extensions induce the same topology and are equivalent absolute values. The coordinate sup norm is complete because K is complete, so the equivalent topology defined by ∣⋅∣L​ is complete as well. This proves the unique extension of an absolute value to a finite extension and the completeness of L.
Completeness is essential. Give K=Q its 5-adic value and take L=Q(i). Since
5=(2+i)(2−i)
(10)
in Z[i], the two primes (2+i) and (2−i) define two inequivalent extensions. For example, (2+i)/(2−i) has positive valuation for one and negative valuation for the other. This is the valuation ring need not equal the integral closure over a noncomplete field example.

For a∈k and each n, choose a lift xn​∈OK​ of the unique pnth root ap−n, which exists because k is a perfect field. Define
[a]=limn→∞​xnpn​.
(11)
Changing xn​ by an element of the maximal ideal changes its pnth power by an element whose valuation tends to infinity, so the limit exists and is independent of all choices. In characteristic p, the Frobenius endomorphism satisfies (x+y)pn=xpn+ypn, making [⋅] a ring homomorphism lifting the identity on k. If s:k→OK​ is any other such section, then
s(a)=s(ap−n)pn
(12)
for every n, and the same limiting construction forces s(a)=[a]. This proves uniqueness of the Teichmuller lift.
Choose a uniformizer t. Repeatedly subtracting the lift of the residue and dividing by t gives every x∈OK​ a unique convergent Teichmuller expansion
x=∑j≥0​[aj​]tj.
(13)
Because the lift is a ring map, this identifies OK​ with k[[t]] and its fraction field with the Laurent series field k((t)). This is the equal-characteristic complete discretely valued field classification.
If K is locally compact, its compact valuation ring has only finitely many disjoint residue-class balls. Thus k is finite, as also follows from the local compactness criterion for a complete non-Archimedean field.

Suppose first that L=K(α) and the degree-n minimal polynomial of α is an Eisenstein polynomial. It is irreducible, α is a uniformizer of L, and its valuation shows that e(L/K)≥n=[L:K]. Equality follows, so L/K is a totally ramified extension.
Conversely, suppose L/K is totally ramified of degree n and choose a uniformizer α of L. The field K(α) already has ramification index at least n, so it equals L. Let
f(X)=Xn+an−1​Xn−1+⋯+a0​
(18)
be the minimal polynomial. All conjugates of α have L-valuation one. Each ai​ with i<n is an elementary symmetric polynomial in products of at least one conjugate and therefore has positive L-valuation. Since valuations of elements of K are multiples of n, every ai​ lies in the maximal ideal of OK​. Moreover
vK​(a0​)=vK​(NL/K​(α))=1,
(19)
so a0​ is not divisible by the square of that ideal. Thus f is Eisenstein, proving the Eisenstein generator of a totally ramified extension criterion.

The Local Kronecker-Weber theorem says that every finite abelian extension of Qp​ is contained in a cyclotomic extension obtained by adjoining roots of unity. It identifies the totally ramified cyclotomic part through
Gal(Qp​(ζp∞​)/Qp​)≅Zp×​
(25)
and the maximal unramified part through its Frobenius generator.
Choose the normalization in which a uniformizer maps to arithmetic Frobenius. For x=pru with r∈Z and u∈Zp×​, define ArtQp​​(x) to act by Frobr on the maximal unramified extension and by
ζpn​⟼ζpn,u−1​
(26)
on every p-power root of unity. These compatible actions define an element of the abelian Weil group, because its residue action is an integral power of Frobenius. The resulting continuous homomorphism
ArtQp​​:Qp×​⟶W(Qpab​/Qp​)
(27)
is the Local Artin map; reversing both Frobenius conventions replaces the displayed inverse by the corresponding opposite normalization.

A one-dimensional commutative formal group law over a ring R is a series F(X,Y)∈R[[X,Y]] satisfying
F(X,0)=X,F(X,Y)=F(Y,X),F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)).
(28)
The identity axiom gives F(X,Y)=X+Y+ terms of total degree at least two. Seek
i(X)=−X+a2​X2+a3​X3+⋯.
(29)
After a2​,…,an−1​ have been chosen, the coefficient of Xn in F(X,i(X)) is an​ plus a known expression in the earlier coefficients. There is therefore a unique choice of an​ making it zero. Recursion constructs the formal inverse i(X) with F(X,i(X))=0 and i(X)≡−X(modX2).

The formal additive group and formal multiplicative group have laws
Ga​(X,Y)=X+Y,Gm​(X,Y)=X+Y+XY.
(30)
If R is an algebra over a field whose scalar field is Q, the series
h(X)=exp(X)−1
(31)
has linear coefficient one and satisfies
h(X+Y)=h(X)+h(Y)+h(X)h(Y).
(32)
It is therefore the exponential isomorphism between the additive and multiplicative formal groups, with inverse log(1+X).

Repeated formal addition gives
[n]Ga​​(X)=nX,[n]Gm​​(X)=(1+X)n−1
(33)
for positive n, and the same formulas extend to all n∈Z using the formal inverse.
Now let R be a field of characteristic p>0 and let h:Ga​→Gm​ be a homomorphism. Compatibility with multiplication by p gives
h([p]Ga​​(X))=[p]Gm​​(h(X)).
(34)
The left side is h(0)=0, while the Frobenius identity gives the right side as
(1+h(X))p−1=h(X)p.
(35)
The formal power series ring over a field is an integral domain, so h(X)p=0 implies h=0. Hence there are no nonzero homomorphisms from the formal additive group to the formal multiplicative group.
