The weak Hilbert Nullstellensatz says that if a field is a finitely generated algebra over k, then it is a finite algebraic extension of k. Since every maximal ideal is prime, the nilradical N(R) is contained in the Jacobson radical J(R).
Conversely, let f be nonnilpotent. Then Rf​=0, and it is a finitely generated k-algebra, so it has a maximal ideal n. Its contraction p to R avoids f. Moreover,
R/p↪Rf​/n
