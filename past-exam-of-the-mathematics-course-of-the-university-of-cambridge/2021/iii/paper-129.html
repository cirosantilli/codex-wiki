The Bourgain bound for three-term-progression-free sets states that, if N=∣G∣ is odd and A⊆G contains no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression, then
∣A∣≪N(logNloglogN​)1/2.
(21)
Here is how it follows from the standard Bohr-set density increment lemma. Begin with B0​=G and relative density α0​=α=∣A∣/N. Whenever the lemma gives its second alternative, replace the current set by the denser translate inside the smaller regular Bohr set. The density changes by
αi+1​≥(1+cαi​)αi​,
(22)
so this can happen only O(α−1) times. Throughout the iteration the rank is O(α−1), the width is at least αO(α−1), and the elementary lower bound for the size of a Bohr set gives
∣Bi​∣≥αO(α−2)N.
(23)
At the terminal stage the first alternative of the density-increment lemma holds. Combining it with the last display yields
α−2log(1/α)≪logN.
(24)
If α<1/logN, Bourgain's bound is already true. Otherwise log(1/α)≪loglogN, and rearranging proves
α≪(logNloglogN​)1/2.​
(25)

h(γ)=1A​​(γ)2,∑γ​∣h(γ)∣=∑γ​∣1A​​(γ)∣2=α

Sample k=O(mε−2) characters independently, choosing γ with probability ∣h(γ)∣/α, and attach the phase of h(γ) to the sampled character. The Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequality, followed by averaging over x, shows that some sampled Fourier sum g satisfies
∥h−g∥L2m(E)​≤2εα​.
(28)
This is the sampling argument recorded in the finite-field character approximation principle.

∥τt​h−h∥L2m(E)​≤2∥h−g∥L2m(E)​≤εα.

The Dense Bogolyubov-Ruzsa lemma says that, for every α>0, if S has density at least α in a cyclic group of prime order, then 2S−2S contains a proper generalized arithmetic progression of rank Oα​(1) and size Ωα​(∣G∣).
Now suppose A⊆Z and ∣A+A∣≤K∣A∣. The Ruzsa modelling lemma, taken at a sufficiently high fixed Freiman order, supplies A′⊆A with ∣A′∣≥∣A∣/2 and a Freiman isomorphism from A′ to a subset S⊆Z/qZ, where q is prime, q=OK​(∣A∣), and ∣S∣/q≫K​1. Apply the dense Bogolyubov-Ruzsa lemma to S and transfer the resulting progression back through the Freiman model. We obtain a proper progression
P0​⊆2A′−2A′⊆2A−2A
(42)
of rank OK​(1) and size ∣P0​∣≫K​∣A∣.
The Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality gives
∣A+P0​∣≤∣3A−2A∣≤K5∣A∣.
(43)
There are ∣A∣∣P0​∣ pairs (a,p) with a∈A and p∈P0​, distributed among the sums in A+P0​. Some x therefore has at least K−5∣P0​∣≫K​∣A∣ representations x=a+p. Equivalently,

Consider the graph
Γ={(x,f(x)):1≤x≤N}⊆Z2.
(47)
The hypothesis says exactly that Γ has at least δN3 additive quadruples. By the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, it contains Γ′ with ∣Γ′∣≫δ​N and ∣Γ′+Γ′∣≪δ​∣Γ′∣.
Part b gives a Freiman s-isomorphism from Γ′ to a set D⊆Z, where it is enough to take any fixed s≥2. The set D has bounded doubling, with a bound depending only on δ. Part c therefore provides a proper generalized arithmetic progression Q of rank Oδ​(1) such that
∣D∩Q∣≫δ​∣D∣≫δ​∣Q∣.
(48)
Write Q as a proper parameter box. Since ∣Q∣≫δ​N, one of its Oδ​(1) side lengths tends to infinity with N. Averaging over all lines parallel to that side gives a line on which D∩Q has density bounded below in terms of δ. The Szemerédi theorem quoted in the question then gives, once N is sufficiently large in terms of k and δ, a nonconstant k-term arithmetic progression in D.
The inverse Freiman isomorphism sends it to a k-term arithmetic progression in Γ′⊆Γ, because each relation between three consecutive terms is an additive-quadruple relation. Write this progression as
(x0​,y0​), (x0​+d,y0​+e),…,(x0​+(k−1)d,y0​+(k−1)e).
(49)
Its first-coordinate difference cannot be zero: the graph of a function has only one point above each x. Hence d=0. On the nonconstant progression
P={x0​,x0​+d,…,x0​+(k−1)d}⊆{1,…,N},
(50)
we have
f(x)=de​x+(y0​−de​x0​).
(51)
Taking a=e/d and b=y0​−(e/d)x0​, both in Q, proves the claim.
