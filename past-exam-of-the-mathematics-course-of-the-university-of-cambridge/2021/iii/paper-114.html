Choose the intersection point p=[(θ0​,θ1​)] as the zero-cell. Let a be the horizontal one-cell S1×{θ1​} and b the vertical one-cell {θ0​}×S1. The usual CW complex structure on the torus has one two-cell c attached by the word aba−1b−1, and the extra disk gives a two-cell d attached along b with degree one. Collapsing a leaves one zero-cell p, one one-cell b, and the two two-cells c,d. The cellular boundary formula gives
C2​≅Zc⊕Zd,C1​≅Zb,∂2​(c)=0,quad∂2​(d)=b.
(1)
Therefore
Hi​(X;Z)≅{Z,0,​i=0,2,otherwise.​
(2)
The Excision theorem says that if Z⊆A⊆X and Z⊆intA, then inclusion induces
Hi​(X∖Z,A∖Z)∼​Hi​(X,A).
(3)
It lets us compute local homology in arbitrarily small neighborhoods.
Let C⊆X be the image of {θ0​}×S1. At a point of X∖C, a neighborhood is a disk, whose link is a circle. At a point of C∖{p}, three half-disks meet along their diameters, and the link is a Theta graph, with first homology Z2. At p, the two folds created by collapsing the horizontal circle give a dumbbell graph as link: two circles joined by an interval. Its first homology is again Z2. The local homology from a link therefore gives
Hi​(X,X∖{x};Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z2,Z,0,​i=2 and x∈C,i=2 and x∈/C,otherwise.​
(4)
Because local homology is invariant under a homeomorphism, every self-homeomorphism of X preserves the rank-two locus C.
It must also preserve p. Indeed, p is the unique point of C whose sufficiently small local link is a dumbbell graph; every other point of C has a theta-graph link. The connecting edge of a dumbbell graph is a bridge in a graph, whereas no edge of a theta graph is a bridge, so these local topological types are distinct.

Equivalently, if an integral cochain a lifts a modulo-m cocycle and δa=mb, then β​[a]=[b] and β[a]=[bmodm].

δ(a⌣c)=δa⌣c+(−1)∣x∣a⌣δc.

β(x⌣y)=β(x)⌣y+(−1)∣x∣x⌣β(y).

Now assume that the stated closed five-manifold M exists. Its top integral cohomology makes it connected and orientable. The long exact sequence from a coefficient sequence for multiplication by p shows that
β​:H2(M;Z/p)∼​H3(M;Z)

and that reduction H3(M;Z)→H3(M;Z/p) is an isomorphism. Hence
β:H2(M;Z/p)∼​H3(M;Z/p).

For an oriented real rank-r vector bundle π:E→X, let uE​∈Hr(D(E),S(E);Z) be its Thom class. If s:X→D(E) is the zero section, the Euler class of a vector bundle is
e(E)=s∗uE​∈Hr(X;Z).
(21)
Let u generate H2k(S2k;Z). The Poincaré-Hopf theorem gives
e(TS2k)=χ(S2k)u=2u.
(22)
For every integer m, choose a map fm​:S2k→S2k of degree m. Naturality of the Euler class gives
e(fm∗​TS2k)=fm∗​e(TS2k)=2m,u.
(23)
Thus every even class belongs to E2k​(S2k).
More generally, every class a∈H2k(M;Z) on a closed 2k-manifold is f∗u for some map f:M→S2k, by the realization of top-dimensional cohomology by a sphere map. Pulling back TS2k gives
2a=e(f∗TS2k),
(24)
so 2H2k(M;Z)⊆E2k​(M). The inclusion can be strict: every complex line bundle on S2 has an underlying oriented real two-plane bundle, and these bundles realize every integral Euler class. Hence
2H2(S2;Z)⊊E2​(S2)=H2(S2;Z).
(25)
The odd-dimensional analogue fails. The Euler class of an oriented odd-rank vector bundle is two-torsion, whereas H2k+1(S2k+1;Z)≅Z is torsion-free. Thus every oriented rank-(2k+1) bundle on S2k+1 has zero Euler class, and the nonzero subgroup 2Z cannot lie in E2k+1​(S2k+1).
Finally, if E and F are oriented bundles of ranks k and l, orient E⊕F by the ordered sum. The Whitney product formula for Euler classes states
e(E⊕F)=e(E)⌣e(F).
(26)
Therefore cup product restricts to the asserted map Ek​(X)⊗El​(X)→Ek+l​(X).
It need not be injective. For X=S2 and k=l=2, one has E2​(S2)=Z, so the source contains Z⊗Z≅Z, but H4(S2)=0 and the map is zero. It need not be surjective either. For X=S2 and k=l=1, every oriented real line bundle is trivial, so both degree-one Euler-class sets vanish, while E2​(S2)=Z.
