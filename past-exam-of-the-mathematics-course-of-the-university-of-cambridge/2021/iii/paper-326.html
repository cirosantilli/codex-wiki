A problem is well posed in the sense of Hadamard when a solution exists for every admissible datum, is unique, and depends continuously on the datum. It is ill posed if any one of these three properties fails.
For the inverse problem Au=f, a regularization of an inverse problem is a family of bounded maps Rα​:Y→X that approximate the generally unbounded Moore–Penrose inverse of an operator A†. It is a convergent regularization of an inverse problem if there is a parameter rule α=α(δ,fδ) such that
α(δ,fδ)⟶0,sup∥fδ−f∥≤δ​∥Rα(δ,fδ)​fδ−A†f∥⟶0
(1)
as δ↓0 for every f∈D(A†).

Let (σi​,xi​,yi​) be the singular system of a compact operator. Since Pn​xi​=xi​ for i≤n and Pn​xi​=0 for i>n, the truncated operator Tn​=APn​ has the finite singular value decomposition
Tn​xi​=σi​yi​(i≤n),Tn​xi​=0(i>n).
(2)
Consequently
Tn†​y=∑i=1n​σi​⟨y,yi​⟩​xi​=A†Qn​y,
(3)
so Tn†​=A†Qn​​.
For completeness, Tn†​Tn​=Pn​ on (kerA)⊥ and Tn​Tn†​=Qn​. These are self-adjoint orthogonal projectors, and hence

(4)
Thus all four Penrose equations hold, which verifies the formula independently of the singular expansion.

A Bayesian inverse problem consists of a prior distribution μ0​ on the unknown u∈X, a reference measure on the data space Y, and a jointly measurable likelihood L(u;y) such that L(u;⋅) is a probability density function for μ0​-almost every u. For observed data y, Bayes' theorem defines the posterior distribution by
dμ0​dμy​(u)=Z(y)L(u;y)​,Z(y)=∫X​L(u;y)dμ0​(u)​,
(27)
provided 0<Z(y)<∞.

The problem is a well-posed Bayesian inverse problem in total variation when a unique posterior μy exists for every y∈Y and the posterior map is continuous:

d OurBigBook.com
The following four assumptions are sufficient, with statements understood for μ0​-almost every u and every y:
  • L(u;⋅) is a strictly positive probability density function on the data space.
  • L(⋅;y)∈L1(X,μ0​).
  • There is one h∈L1(X,μ0​) such that L(u;y)≤h(u) for all y.
  • For each relevant u, the map y↦L(u;y) is continuous.
The first two assumptions give 0<Z(y)<∞. The last two allow the dominated convergence theorem to prove L1(μ0​) continuity of the normalized posterior density, which is equivalent to continuity in total variation distance.
