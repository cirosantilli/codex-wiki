Let N=(0,…,0,1) and S=(0,…,0,−1) be the poles of Sn. The open sets UN​=Sn∖{N} and US​=Sn∖{S} cover the sphere. Stereographic projection gives coordinate charts
ϕN​(r)=1−rn+1​(r1​,…,rn​)​,ϕS​(r)=1+rn+1​(r1​,…,rn​)​
(1)
from these sets to Rn. Their inverses are
ϕN−1​(x)=(1+∣x∣22x​,1+∣x∣2∣x∣2−1​),ϕS−1​(y)=(1+∣y∣22y​,1+∣y∣21−∣y∣2​).
(2)
On the overlap, the transition map is
y=ϕS​ϕN−1​(x)=∣x∣2x​​,x=0,
(3)
which is a smooth diffeomorphism of Rn∖{0}. These two compatible charts make Sn a smooth manifold of dimension n.
Now let G be an m-dimensional Lie group and choose a basis e1​,…,em​ of its tangent space Te​G at the identity. Define
Ei​(g)=(dLg​)e​ei​,
(4)
where Lg​ is Left translation on a Lie group. Smoothness of multiplication makes each Ei​ a smooth left-invariant vector field, and invertibility of (dLg​)e​ makes E1​(g),…,Em​(g) a basis of Tg​G at every point. Thus the Ei​ form a global frame and every Lie group is a parallelizable manifold.
The columns of a matrix in the special unitary group SU(2) are orthonormal and its determinant is one. Consequently every element has the unique form
U=(z1​z2​​−z2​z1​​),∣z1​∣2+∣z2​∣2=1​.
(5)
The pair (z1​,z2​)∈C2≅R4 therefore identifies SU(2) diffeomorphically with S3. The Lie-group construction then proves that S3 is parallelizable; this is the SU(2) as the three-sphere identification.
Another example is S1, which is the Lie group U(1). Explicitly, at (x,y)∈S1 the vector
E(x,y)=−y∂x∂​+x∂y∂​
(6)
is smooth, tangent, and nowhere zero, so it is a global one-vector frame. Thus S1 is another parallelizable sphere.
