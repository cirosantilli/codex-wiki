An encounter can occur only near an orbital node of the comet's inclined orbit. Its tangential velocity projected into the planet's plane is reduced by cosIc​, so the relative-speed calculation becomes
Δvpc2​=vp2​[3−ac​ap​​−2ap​ac​​(1−ec2​)​cosIc​]​.
(19)
The missing projected component is a vertical relative velocity, so the moon encounter is three-dimensional: b becomes a vector in the impact plane, and a collision also requires the trajectory to pass close to the moon's orbital plane. Once the total asymptotic speed is used, the two-body gravitational focusing formulae in parts (v) and (vi) retain their form, but the geometrical collision probability is lower.

The same absorption and re-emission that produce radial radiation pressure also produce Poynting–Robertson drag. This velocity-dependent force removes specific orbital energy and angular momentum, destroys the exact Jacobi constant, and turns the formal equilibrium points into slowly drifting configurations. Stellar-wind drag can provide a similar correction.

i OurBigBook.com
For a spherical grain of diameter D, density ρd​, and radiation-pressure efficiency Qpr​, comparison of the stellar momentum flux with Newton's law of universal gravitation gives the radiation-pressure coefficient
β(D)=8πcGM∗​ρd​D3L∗​Qpr​(D)​​.
(38)
For D much larger than the characteristic stellar wavelength, Qpr​≃1, so β∝D−1. When D becomes comparable to the optical wavelength, diffraction and the grain's composition make Qpr​ size dependent and β reaches a broad maximum. Deep in the Rayleigh scattering regime, absorption can give Qpr​∝D and hence nearly constant β, while scattering alone gives Qpr​∝D4 and hence β∝D3. A realistic curve therefore rises roughly as D−1 toward micron sizes, turns over near the stellar spectral peak, and falls or flattens for still smaller grains.

At periapsis the release velocity is tangential. A grain with β=1 feels no net stellar inverse-square force and therefore moves on the tangent line. A grain with β<1 retains an inward acceleration and bends toward the star; a grain with β>1 feels a net outward acceleration and bends away from it. At the common observation time, joining these positions in increasing β gives the synchrone shown below. It starts near the low-β orbital trajectory, passes through the force-free β=1 position, and extends outward through the repelled high-β grains.

For a fixed β, release points from f=−π/2 through the current f=π/2 generate a syndyne. The newest grain is still at the comet, whereas earlier grains have had longer to separate. A cometary dust tail is a superposition of synchrones and syndynes, rather than a material line emitted in one fixed direction. For grains larger than the stellar wavelength, β∝D−1: small grains have large β, are displaced rapidly in the anti-stellar direction, and make a relatively straight broad tail. Large grains have small β, remain closer to the comet's Kepler orbit, and make a more strongly curved dust trail. The observed position angle consequently depends on grain size, release time, orbital phase, and projection onto the plane of the sky.

r¨1​=−r13​G(M∗​+M1​)​r1​+GM2​[∣r2​−r1​∣3r2​−r1​​−r23​r2​​].

Expanding the disturbing function in orbital elements gives a Fourier series of the form

Solution

Words: 98
iii OurBigBook.com
The expansion separates naturally into three timescales:
  • A short-period orbital perturbation contains rapidly varying mean longitudes. It describes individual conjunction kicks and usually averages to zero away from a commensurability.
  • A resonant orbital perturbation has a nearly stationary integer combination of mean longitudes near a mean-motion resonance. Successive conjunctions then occur at correlated phases, so their effects accumulate and the resonant argument may librate.
  • A secular perturbation survives averaging over both mean longitudes. It drives slow apsidal precession, nodal precession, and exchange of orbital eccentricity or orbital inclination over many orbital periods.
Short-period terms matter for instantaneous osculating elements and encounters, resonant terms near period commensurabilities, and secular terms for long-term evolution away from strong resonance.

Solution

Words: 157
v OurBigBook.com
Just after M2​ crosses its ascending node, it lies above the nearly fixed orbital plane of M1​. At a nearby conjunction, M1​ is inward of M2​, because r1​<r2​. The perturbing acceleration toward M1​ therefore has an inward component and a downward normal component. In the view along the node toward the star, the downward component opposes M2​'s upward vertical velocity and reduces its orbital inclination; in the face-on view, the inward component perturbs its orbital energy and shifts the conjunction phase. A conjunction before the ascending node reverses the relation between the normal force and vertical velocity. Repeated phase-correlated impulses therefore provide the restoring dynamics of an inclination-type mean-motion resonance.
