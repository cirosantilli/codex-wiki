With the convention
ιXft​​​ω=−dft​,
(14)
the time-dependent Hamiltonian vector field is uniquely determined because ω is nondegenerate. If ϕt​ is its local flow, Cartan's magic formula gives
dtd​ϕt∗​ω=ϕt∗​LXft​​​ω=ϕt∗​(dιXft​​​ω+ιXft​​​dω)=0.

Thus ϕt∗​ω=ω wherever the flow is defined, which is the Hamiltonian flow preserves the symplectic form property.
Write v=(a,b) and ω0​=dx∧dy. The linear Hamiltonian
H0​(x,y)=bx−ay
(16)
has Hamiltonian vector field XH0​​=v. Choose a smooth cutoff ρ that is one on B(r+∣v∣) and zero outside B(r+∣v∣+ϵ), and put H=ρH0​. Every trajectory beginning in B(r) and following v remains in B(r+∣v∣) for time 0≤t≤1, so the time-one map translates that ball by v. Outside B(r+∣v∣+ϵ) its vector field vanishes, so the map is the identity. This is a compactly supported Hamiltonian translation and hence a compactly supported symplectomorphism.
For the connected-sum construction, choose a Darboux chart about the unique transverse intersection and straighten the two Lagrangian sheets to R2 and iR2 in C2. Remove small disks from the two sheets and join their boundary circles by the standard Lagrangian neck
(t,u)⟼(a(t)u,b(t)u),u∈S1,
(17)
in Rx2​⊕Ry2​, where (a(t),b(t)) follows a smooth arc from one positive coordinate ray to the other and agrees with those rays near its ends. Its pullback of ∑j​dxj​∧dyj​ vanishes because u⋅u′=0. Gluing this neck to the unchanged surfaces performs Lagrangian surgery. Topologically it is their connected sum, so it gives a Lagrangian connected sum
Σg1​​#Σg2​​≅Σg1​+g2​​.
(18)
Finally, choose an immersed circle γl​:S1→R2 with exactly l transverse double points and no other multiple points; one may add l small figure-eight kinks to an embedded circle. Let c:S1→R2 be an embedded circle and define
ι(s,t)=(γl​(s),c(t)).
(19)
This Product Lagrangian immersion satisfies ι∗ω0​=0. If p is a double point of γl​, its two local branches times c(S1) meet along {p}×c(S1). Their tangent spaces intersect precisely in the tangent line to that circle, so the intersection is clean. The l double points therefore give exactly l disjoint clean self-intersection circles.
Near each clean circle, perturb one Lagrangian sheet by the graph of δdh in its Weinstein neighborhood, where h:S1→R is a Morse function with one minimum and one maximum. The clean circle is replaced by two transverse double points. Resolve both by Lagrangian surgery. Each resolution attaches one one-handle and lowers the Euler characteristic by two, so resolving all l clean circles changes the Euler characteristic of the original torus from zero to
0−2(2l)=−4l.
(20)
Choose the orientation-reversing neck at one double point; the resulting connected surface is nonorientable, while all the surgeries remove their double points and leave an embedding. Thus the Givental construction of nonorientable Lagrangian surfaces gives a closed connected nonorientable surface of Euler characteristic −4l Lagrangian embedded in (R4,ω0​).
