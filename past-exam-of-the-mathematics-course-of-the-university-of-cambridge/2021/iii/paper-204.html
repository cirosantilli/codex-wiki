After the first of the four possible steps, a self-avoiding walk has at most three choices at every stage because it cannot immediately reverse its preceding step. Thus bn​≤4⋅3n−1 and κ≤3. On the other hand, every sequence of n north or east steps is self-avoiding, so bn​≥2n and κ≥2.

In site percolation on the three-dimensional cubic lattice, each vertex v∈Z3 is independently open with probability p and closed with probability 1−p. Thus Pp​ is the Bernoulli product measure on {0,1}Z3, and open paths are nearest-neighbour paths all of whose vertices are open.

Use the standard theorem that supercritical Bernoulli percolation on Z3 has a unique infinite open cluster almost surely. Fix p>pc​ and choose q∈(pc​,p). Almost surely ηq​ has an infinite cluster somewhere. On Ip​, the origin and that cluster lie in the unique infinite ηp​-cluster, so a finite ηp​-open path joins them. Almost surely every label on this finite path is strictly below p; increasing q to some r<p above those finitely many labels makes the origin percolate in ηr​. Hence Ip​⊆{M<p} up to a null event, and part e gives left continuity at p. Together with part c, θ is continuous on (pc​,1].

The contact process on Z has states ξt​(x)∈{0,1}. Each infected site recovers at rate μ, and infection passes across each oriented nearest-neighbour edge at rate λ. For the graphical representation of the contact process, put independent rate-μ recovery marks on each vertical time line and independent rate-λ infection arrows on each oriented nearest-neighbour edge. Then y∈ξtA​ exactly when a forward path from some (x,0) with x∈A reaches (y,t) by moving upward, following arrows, and avoiding recovery marks.
If A⊆B, every path starting in A also starts in B, so ξtA​⊆ξtB​. A path starts in A∪B exactly when it starts in one of the two sets, which proves additivity of the contact process:
ξtA∪B​=ξtA​∪ξtB​.
(16)
The event ξtA​∩B=∅ says that a graphical path runs from A×{0} to B×{t}. Reflecting the time interval about t/2 and reversing every arrow preserves the joint law of the independent Poisson processes, and the path now runs from B to A. This proves duality of the contact process:
Pλ,μ​(ξtA​∩B=∅)=Pλ,μ​(ξtB​∩A=∅).
