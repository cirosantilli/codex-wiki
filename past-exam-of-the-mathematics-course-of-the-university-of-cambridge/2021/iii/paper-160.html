Expand the Column antisymmetrizer of a Young tableau:
bv​{w}=∑g∈C(v)​sgn(g){gw}.
(1)
If two entries in one column of v lie in the same row of w, their transposition belongs to both C(v) and the row stabilizer of w, so the terms cancel in pairs. The assumption bv​{w}=0 therefore says that every row of w meets every column of v in at most one entry.
The first row of w has λ1​ entries, while v has exactly λ1​ nonempty columns. It must consequently contain exactly one entry from each column of v. Permuting within each column puts these entries in the first row positions of v. Delete the matched first rows and repeat the argument on the remaining Young diagram. The product of the resulting column permutations is an element h∈C(v) for which the row sets of hv are those of w. Thus
h{v}={w}​.
(2)
This is the nonzero column antisymmetrizer criterion.

The James submodule theorem says that for every FSn​-submodule U≤Mλ, either
Sλ⊆UorU⊆(Sλ)⊥,
(3)
where orthogonality is taken with respect to the tabloid bilinear form.
Fix a λ-tableau t. Part a shows that for every tabloid {s}, the vector bt​{s} is either zero or a signed copy of the polytabloid e(t). Comparing the coefficient of {t} gives the precise identity
bt​u=⟨u,e(t)⟩e(t)(u∈Mλ).
(4)
If U⊈(Sλ)⊥, choose u∈U and a tableau t with ⟨u,e(t)⟩=0. Since U is a submodule, the identity puts e(t) in U. Every polytabloid of shape λ is an Sn​-translate of e(t), so their span Sλ lies in U. If no such u,t exist, then by definition U⊆(Sλ)⊥. This proves the theorem over the arbitrary field F.

The row stabilizer of the transposed tableau is R(t′)=C(t). For r∈R(t′), the polytabloid satisfies
re(t)=sgn(r)e(t),re(u)=sgn(r)e(u).

gre(t)⊗gre(u)=ge(t)⊗ge(u).

The restriction form of the restriction branching rule for a symmetric group is
ResSn−1​Sn​​Sλ≅μ∈λ−⨁​Sμ​,
(18)
where λ− contains the distinct partitions obtained by deleting one Removable node of a Young diagram. In particular, the restriction is multiplicity-free.

ψλ=∑π∈SN​​sgn(π)ξλ−id+π.
(19)

ResSn−1​Sn​​ψλ=∑i​ψλ−ϵi​.
(20)

Suppose χαχβ is irreducible. Since symmetric-group characters are real,
1=⟨χαχβ,χαχβ⟩=⟨χαχα,χβχβ⟩.
(30)
Both self-products contain the trivial character once. They can therefore have no other common irreducible constituent. By part i, the standard character occurs in the two self-products with multiplicities ∣α−∣−1 and ∣β−∣−1. Hence one of these numbers is zero; say ∣α−∣=1.
A partition has exactly one removable node precisely when all its nonzero rows have equal length, so α=(ab) is rectangular. Since ab=n and n is prime, either a=1 or b=1. Thus α=(1n) or (n). The same argument applies with α and β interchanged, proving the prime-degree irreducible Kronecker product criterion for a symmetric group.

Trace the southeast boundary of the hook of a Young diagram based at (i,j). At each horizontal boundary step record the hook length hi,y​ of the cell in row i above that step. At each vertical step ending beside row x, record hi,j​−hx,j​. Starting at the northeast end and moving to the southwest end, these records increase by one from 1 to hi,j​; horizontal and vertical steps are disjoint and account for every step. Therefore the Hook-interval decomposition at a Young-diagram cell is
{1,…,hi,j​}={hi,y​:j≤y≤λi​}⊔{hi,j​−hx,j​:i<x≤λj′​}.​
(31)

The supplied row-hook formula is
Hi​(λ)={1,…,hi​}∖{hi​−hj​:i<j≤m}.
(32)
Consequently h∈Hi​(λ) exactly when hi​−h≥0 and hi​−h is not one of hi+1​,…,hm​. Since hi​−h≤hi​<hj​ for j<i, this is equivalent to hi​−h∈/X. We have proved the hook criterion in a beta set
h∈Hi​(λ)⟺hi​−h≥0 and hi​−h∈/X​.

An ek-runner abacus separates bead positions by their residue modulo ek. Write such a residue in base e as
a0​+a1​e+⋯+ak−1​ek−1.
(44)
Taking one e-quotient sorts beads by a0​ and divides their positions by e; applying the operation again sorts by a1​, and so on. After k stages, the iterated construction has selected exactly the same ek residue classes as the single ek-quotient. The two conventional orderings may list the base-e digits in opposite order, producing only a permutation of components.
Equivalently, induction on k applies the same argument to every component of Qe​(λ) and identifies the resulting ek+1 runner partitions. Hence the iterated quotient equals a power quotient up to permutation statement is
Qek​(λ) is a permutation of TQe​(λ)k​​.

Each carry removes p units from one column and adds one unit to the next, decreasing the total digit sum by p−1.
