The short exact coefficient sequence
0⟶Z×m​Z⟶Z/m⟶0
(12)
induces the integral Bockstein homomorphism
β​:Hi(X;Z/m)⟶Hi+1(X;Z).
(13)
If ρ:Hi+1(X;Z)→Hi+1(X;Z/m) is reduction modulo m, then
β=ρ∘β​.
(14)
Equivalently, if an integral cochain a lifts a modulo-m cocycle and δa=mb, then β​[a]=[b] and β[a]=[bmodm].
For integral lifts a,c of classes x,y, the cup product coboundary formula is
δ(a⌣c)=δa⌣c+(−1)∣x∣a⌣δc.
(15)
Dividing by m and reducing modulo m proves the Bockstein derivation rule
β(x⌣y)=β(x)⌣y+(−1)∣x∣x⌣β(y).
(16)
Now assume that the stated closed five-manifold M exists. Its top integral cohomology makes it connected and orientable. The long exact sequence from a coefficient sequence for multiplication by p shows that
β​:H2(M;Z/p)∼​H3(M;Z)
(17)
and that reduction H3(M;Z)→H3(M;Z/p) is an isomorphism. Hence
β:H2(M;Z/p)∼​H3(M;Z/p).
(18)
The same coefficient sequence gives H4(M;Z/p)=0.
Choose 0=x∈H2(M;Z/p) and put y=βx=0. By Poincare duality over Z/p, the pairing
H2(M;Z/p)⊗H3(M;Z/p)⟶H5(M;Z/p)
(19)
is nondegenerate. Both factors are one-dimensional, so x⌣y=0. But x⌣x=0 because H4(M;Z/p)=0, while the derivation rule and graded commutativity of the cup product give
0=β(x⌣x)=y⌣x+x⌣y=2x⌣y.
(20)
This contradicts p>2. Therefore no such manifold exists.
