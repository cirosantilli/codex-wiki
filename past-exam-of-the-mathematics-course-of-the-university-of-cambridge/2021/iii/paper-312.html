a OurBigBook.com
The operator ϕ˙​2ϕ is not invariant under a scalar-field shift symmetry because one field is undifferentiated. An arbitrary large coefficient would therefore radiatively generate equally unsuppressed nonderivative operators, including a mass and a steep scalar potential, and would spoil the approximate shift symmetry and flat potential needed for single-field slow-roll inflation. In a technically natural slow-roll model its coefficient must consequently be small, so it does not produce parametrically large primordial non-Gaussianity. Treated merely as an effective spectator-field interaction, it does generate the tree-level primordial bispectrum computed below, whose size is controlled by the dimensionless interaction strength at the Hubble scale; taking that strength large abandons the controlled slow-roll premise.

The cosmological continuity equation expresses conservation of dark-matter mass: δ is the density contrast, v is the peculiar velocity, and a prime denotes a conformal time derivative. The cosmological Euler equation expresses momentum conservation: H=a′/a is the conformal Hubble rate, ϕ is the peculiar gravitational potential, ρ is the density, and σij​ is the velocity-dispersion tensor of collisionless matter. The terms are respectively Hubble drag, convective acceleration, gravity, and velocity-dispersion stress.
For curl-free flow, introduce the peculiar-velocity divergence θ=∇⋅v and set σij​=0. A Fourier transform of the nonlinear continuity term gives
δ′(k)+θ(k)=−∫(2π)3d3q​α(q,k−q)θ(q)δ(k−q),
(15)
with the alpha mode-coupling kernel
α(k1​,k2​)=k12​(k1​+k2​)⋅k1​​​.
(16)
Taking the divergence of the Euler equation gives the quadratic velocity kernel
β(k1​,k2​)=2k12​k22​∣k1​+k2​∣2(k1​⋅k2​)​​.
(17)
This is the beta mode-coupling kernel; its symmetry follows from the two velocity factors.

The conventional vertical variable is Dℓ​=ℓ(ℓ+1)Cℓ​/(2π) in μK2, plotted against the dimensionless angular multipole ℓ. The Sachs-Wolfe effect supplies a nearly flat large-angle plateau for ℓ≲30. The total spectrum has its first acoustic peak near ℓ≃220 with Dℓ​ of order 5×103μK2, followed by further Cosmic microwave background acoustic peaks; the Doppler CMB anisotropy is phase shifted relative to the photon-density oscillation. At ℓ≳1000, Cosmic microwave background diffusion damping lets photons random-walk across perturbations during recombination and produces the rapidly falling damping tail.
