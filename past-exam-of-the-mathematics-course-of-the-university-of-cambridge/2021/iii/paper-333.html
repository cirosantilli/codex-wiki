The basic state has velocity field
U=(0,Λx,0)
(1)
and buoyancy B=N2z. For disturbances independent of y, the linearized equations are
ut​−fv=−px​,vt​+(f+Λ)u=0,wt​=−pz​+b,
(2)
bt​+N2w=0,ux​+wz​=0.
(3)
Substituting a plane wave proportional to exp[i(kx+mz−ωt)] and eliminating p, v, and b gives the dispersion relation
ω2=k2+m2N2k2+f(f+Λ)m2​.
(4)
Thus the requested coefficients are
f​2=f(f+Λ),g​=N2.
(5)
An instability exists precisely when some wavenumber pair makes ω2<0. Since the Brunt–Väisälä frequency satisfies N2>0, this is possible exactly when
f(f+Λ)<0.
(6)
The basic relative vorticity is Λez​, so its absolute vorticity is (f+Λ)ez​. Its Ertel potential vorticity is therefore
Q=(f+Λ)N2.
(7)
The instability criterion can consequently be written as fQ<0: the vertical absolute vorticity has the opposite sign to the planetary vorticity. This is inertial instability, approached most directly by disturbances with ∣m/k∣≫1.

Now let the basic buoyancy be
B=M2x+N2z.
(8)
Thermal-wind balance requires the basic along-front velocity to have vertical shear Vz​=M2/f, so
U=(0,Λx+fM2​z,0).
(9)
The basic absolute vorticity and buoyancy gradient are
ωa​=(−fM2​,0,f+Λ),∇B=(M2,0,N2),
(10)
and hence
Q=ωa​⋅∇B=(f+Λ)N2−fM4​.
(11)
For the prescribed disturbance the buoyancy perturbation vanishes, so the linearized buoyancy equation and incompressibility give
M2u+N2w=0,ku+mw=0.
(12)
The wavevector must therefore satisfy
km​=M2N2​;
(13)
the disturbance velocity lies along a basic isopycnal. The along-front momentum equation becomes
−iωv+(f+Λ−fN2M4​)u=0=−iωv+N2Q​u.
(14)
Projecting the remaining momentum equations onto the divergence-free direction eliminates the pressure and yields
ω2=N2fQ​k2+m2m2​=N4+M4fQN2​.
(15)
Thus this isopycnal disturbance grows if and only if fQ<0. Geometrically, Q is the component of the absolute vorticity along the buoyancy gradient, multiplied by ∣∇B∣; the horizontal buoyancy gradient reduces that component through the term −M4/f. When M=0, the result reduces to the most unstable, nearly horizontal-wavevector limit of part i.

Represent a horizontal vector (u,v) by the complex number W=u+iv, let s=sgnf, and define the two Ekman layer depths and drag coefficients by
δj​=∣f∣2νj​​​,rj​=δj​ρj​νj​​=ρj​2νj​∣f∣​​,j∈{a,o}.
(16)
The atmospheric and oceanic departures from their respective geostrophic flow satisfy
νj​Wj,zz​=ifWj​.
(17)
The solutions that decay away from the ice are
Wa​−Wa∞​=(Wi​−Wa∞​)exp[−δa​(1+is)z​],z>0,
(18)
and
Wo​−Wo∞​=(Wi​−Wo∞​)exp[δo​(1+is)z​],z<0.
(19)
Here Wa∞​ and Wo∞​ denote the atmospheric and oceanic geostrophic velocities.
The viscous stress exerted on the ice by the atmosphere and ocean is, respectively,
τa​=(1+is)ra​(Wa∞​−Wi​),τo​=(1+is)ro​(Wo∞​−Wi​).
(20)
Because the ice is an infinitesimally thin, freely moving sheet, its horizontal force balance is τa​+τo​=0. The common Ekman turning factor cancels, leaving
Wi​=ra​+ro​ra​Wa∞​+ro​Wo∞​​​.
(21)
Thus the ice moves along the weighted mean of the two geostrophic currents. In particular, as νa​→0 one has ra​→0 and Wi​→Wo∞​: an atmosphere with vanishing viscosity transmits no finite stress to the ice.
The atmospheric Ekman transport relative to its geostrophic current is
Ma​=∫0∞​(Wa​−Wa∞​)dz=1+isδa​​(Wi​−Wa∞​)=−2(ra​+ro​)δa​ro​​(1−is)(Wa∞​−Wo∞​).
(22)
The atmospheric stress is
τa​=ra​+ro​(1+is)ra​ro​​(Wa∞​−Wo∞​).
(23)
If the two geostrophic currents are parallel but unequal, both directions are obtained by rotating their velocity difference: in the Northern Hemisphere the stress lies 45∘ anticlockwise from Wa∞​−Wo∞​, while the atmospheric transport lies 45∘ clockwise from its negative. Both rotations reverse in the Southern Hemisphere. If the two currents are identical, the shear, stress, and relative Ekman transport all vanish.

∂t​(∇h2​ϕn​−cn2​f02​​ϕn​)+β∂x​ϕn​=Fn​(x,y)H(t).

The method of characteristics shows that a point is affected only after a westward characteristic from the forcing region reaches it. At a western observation point X1​, during the interval
C0​t>LX​>C1​t,
(42)
the fast barotropic signal has arrived but the first and higher baroclinic signals have not. Consequently
ψ(X1​,y,z,t)≃ϕ0​(X1​,y,t)P0​(z),
(43)
which is nearly independent of depth. At the eastern point X2​, no westward Rossby-wave characteristic arrives from the forcing region, so the disturbance remains zero under the stated initial and radiation conditions.
The propagation speed derived in part v is Cn​=βcn2​/f02​. Thus the question's notation c0​t>LX​>c1​t must be read as a comparison with these modal Rossby signal speeds; using the gravity-wave speeds cn​ literally would not describe the long-wave equation derived above.

In a steady state the time derivative of quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity vanishes, leaving the local balance
βψx​=F.
(44)
Choose the undisturbed state immediately east of the compact forcing as the integration condition. The solution is then
ψ(x,y,z)=⎩⎨⎧​−β1​∫x∞​G(x′,y)dx′,0,​−D<z<0,−H<z<−D.​​
(45)
The discontinuity in this ideal expression reflects the discontinuous prescribed forcing at z=−D; a vertically smooth forcing gives the corresponding smooth vertical profile.
The adjustment begins with the rapidly propagating barotropic mode and is therefore initially almost depth-independent. Successively slower baroclinic modes then arrive from the forcing region. Their Fourier series in the vertical normal modes progressively reconstructs the forcing's upper-layer profile, tending to the stated steady solution while points to the east remain unaltered.

i OurBigBook.com
Let every perturbation be proportional to exp[i(kx+mz−ωt)] and define the intrinsic frequency
ω=ω−kU.
(46)
Eliminating pressure with incompressibility, and then eliminating buoyancy, gives the internal gravity wave dispersion relation
ω2=k2+m2N2k2​​,ω=kU±k2+m2​N∣k∣​.
(47)
The vertical group velocity is
cgz​=∂m∂ω​=−k2+m2ωm​.
(48)
Thus energy propagates upward when ωm<0 and downward when ωm>0. Equivalently, on the positive-intrinsic-frequency branch upward propagation requires m<0, while on the negative branch it requires m>0.

The exact no-penetration condition on the stationary boundary is that the velocity be tangent to it. Linearized at z=0, it is
w′(x,0)=Udxdh​=−Ukh0​sinkx.
(49)
The terrain is stationary, so ω=0 and ω=−kU. The dispersion relation gives
m2=U2N2​−k2.
(50)
A vertically propagating wave therefore exists if and only if
∣kU∣<N.
(51)
The upward radiation condition ωm<0 selects km>0 for U>0. Taking k>0 gives m>0. Lines of constant phase satisfy
kx+mz=constant,
(52)
so they tilt upstream as height increases. The discarded sign of m gives the mirror-image phase tilt and downward energy propagation.

The force on the mean flow and the wave-activity tendency obey
−∂z​u′w′=∂z​FA​=−∂t​A.
(62)
Hence growth of positive wave activity produces a negative mean-flow force, while decay deposits negative momentum into the mean flow. Since part iii found a negative force for a growing, upward-radiating wave with U>0, its wave activity is positive.
