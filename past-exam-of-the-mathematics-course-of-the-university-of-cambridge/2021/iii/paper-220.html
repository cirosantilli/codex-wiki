A compact real tree is a compact metric space (T,d) such that any x,y∈T are joined by a unique arc, and that arc is isometric to [0,d(x,y)]. The multiplicity of a point in a real tree a∈T is the number of connected components of T∖{a}.

For nonempty compact subsets A,B of a metric space (Z,d), the Hausdorff distance is
dHZ​(A,B)=max{supa∈A​infb∈B​d(a,b),supb∈B​infa∈A​d(a,b)}.
(3)
For compact metric spaces X,Y, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance is
dGH​(X,Y)=infZ,φ,ψ​dHZ​(φ(X),ψ(Y)),
(4)
where φ and ψ range over isometric embeddings into a common metric space Z.
The collection of compact real trees is not compact in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology. Indeed, the intervals Tn​=[0,n] are compact real trees and
∣diam(X)−diam(Y)∣≤2dGH​(X,Y).
(5)
Their diameters are unbounded, so (Tn​) has no convergent subsequence in the Gromov-Hausdorff topology.

Choose a root o∈T and finite sets Fn​⊂T whose union is dense, arranging that Fn​ is a 2−n-net and Fn​⊂Fn+1​. Let Tn​ be the finite subtree spanned by o and Fn​. A depth-first contour traversal of Tn​, recording distance from o, gives a continuous excursion gn​ whose real tree encoded by an excursion is Tn​.
The traversals may be chosen compatibly: when passing from Tn​ to Tn+1​, insert the new branch traversals into small time intervals at their attachment points. Since every new component has height at most 2−n+1, choose the time changes so that
∥gn+1​−gn​∥∞​≤2−n+2.
(6)
After harmlessly taking a faster sequence of nets, these errors are summable. Hence (gn​) is Uniformly Cauchy and converges uniformly to a continuous g:[0,1]→R+​ with g(0)=g(1)=0.
The net property gives dGH​(Tn​,T)→0. By the stated continuity of excursion coding, dGH​(Tgn​​,Tg​)→0. Since Tgn​​ is isometric to Tn​, uniqueness of limits in the Gromov-Hausdorff distance implies that Tg​ is isometric to T. This proves the excursion coding theorem for compact real trees.

The head of the Brownian snake driven by g is the centered Gaussian process (Zt​)0≤t≤1​ with covariance function E[Zs​Zt​]=mg​(s,t). Part i shows that these finite-dimensional distributions exist consistently. Moreover,

The set Qn​ consists of rooted planar maps with n faces, every face having degree four. The set Qn∙​ consists of these quadrangulations with an additional distinguished vertex.
For the trivial bijection between planar maps and quadrangulations, start from a rooted planar map with n edges. Put a new vertex in every face and join it to the original vertex at every incident corner. Delete the original edges. The two endpoints of each deleted edge and the new vertices in its two adjacent faces bound a quadrangular face, so the result lies in Qn​. Its bipartition distinguishes old from new vertices and reconstructs the original map. With the standard root convention this is a bijection, and hence
#Qn​=#Mn​.​
(11)

Consider an occurrence counted by Gm​. Before the next counted occurrence, the exploration must first take a downward step of the simple symmetric random walk, which has probability 1/2, and then choose the decrement −1 among the three equally likely values of ξ, which has probability 1/3. Thus it terminates the visits to the current record value with probability
p=21​31​=61​.
(12)
The Strong Markov property at successive counted occurrences makes these trials independent. Therefore Gm​ has the geometric distribution on {1,2,…} with parameter 1/6, and
P(Gm​≥j)=(1−61​)j−1​
(13)

A compact H-hull is a bounded relatively closed set A⊂H for which H∖A is a simply connected domain. Its mapping-out function of a compact H-hull is the unique conformal map gA​:H∖A→H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity
gA​(z)=z+za​+O(∣z∣−2).
(17)
Its half-plane capacity is hcap(A)=a.

Fix x∈R∖{0} and, before x is swallowed, put Xt​=(gt​(x)−Ut​)/κ​. The Chordal Loewner equation and Ut​=κ​Bt​ show, after changing the sign of the Brownian motion, that
dXt​=dWt​+Xt​2/κ​,dt.
(26)
Thus X is a Bessel process of dimension
δ=1+κ4​.
(27)
A Bessel process hits zero exactly when δ<2, which here is equivalent to κ>4. Hitting zero is precisely the swallowing of a nonzero boundary point by the SLE hull. For κ≤4 the trace is simple and swallows no such point; for κ>4 swallowing occurs through a boundary contact. Hence the trace intersects ∂H∖{0} exactly when κ>4.

ii OurBigBook.com
For κ≤4, part i gives no nonzero boundary intersection. For κ>4, part i gives a boundary hit almost surely. After any hit, map out the past hull and recenter at the current tip. The Conformal Markov property of SLE says that the future is again an SLE in the remaining domain. Applying part i repeatedly and using Scaling invariance of SLE produces another boundary hit after every finite number of hits. Therefore there are almost surely infinitely many.

For 0<κ≤4, the trace meets the real boundary only at its starting point, so the intersection has zero Lebesgue measure. Suppose 4<κ<8. A boundary point swallowed by an interval need not itself lie on the trace. The stated fact that P(τx​=τy​)>0 for 0<x<y, combined with the Strong Markov property and Scaling invariance of SLE at successively nested swallowed intervals, implies that a fixed deterministic x=0 is swallowed in an interval rather than hit by the trace with probability one. Equivalently,
P{x∈γ([0,∞))}=0.

The Conformal Markov property of SLE states that, conditional on the hull Kt​, the image under gt​−Ut​ of the future hull has the same law as the original hull and is independent of the past.
For a Loewner chain with continuous driver U, this property says that Ut+s​−Ut​ is independent of the past and has the same distribution as Us​. Thus U has stationary increments and independent increments. Every continuous process with those properties is a Brownian motion with drift, so Ut​=at+σBt​. Conformal scale invariance gives
(r−1Ur2t​)t≥0​=d(Ut​)t≥0​,
(30)
which forces a=0. Writing κ=σ2 yields
Ut​=κ​Bt​​.
(31)

The Locality property of SLE says the following. Let D⊂H be simply connected and agree with H in a neighborhood of 0, and let ψ:D→H be conformal with ψ(0)=0. The image under ψ of an SLE6​ in H, stopped when it first leaves D, has the law of an SLE6​ in H, up to the corresponding stopping time and a change of half-plane-capacity parameterization.

Explore the two stopped curves in the opposite order. Begin with an SLE6​ from 1 to ∞, stopped on leaving B(1,1), and then, in its unbounded complementary component, draw an SLE6​ from −1 to ∞, stopped on leaving B(−1,1). Before their respective stopping times, each curve is separated from the neighborhood in which the other hull changes the domain. The Locality property of SLE therefore says that mapping out the other stopped hull does not change either stopped marginal law.
The two exploration orders consequently define the same joint law for the pair of stopped hulls. Disintegrating this joint law with respect to the second curve shows that, conditional on γ2​∣[0,τ2​]​, the first curve is an SLE6​ in the unbounded component of
H∖γ2​([0,τ2​])
(35)
from −1 to ∞, stopped when it leaves B(−1,1), as required.

Choose a Möbius transformation of H that fixes 0 and exchanges x with ∞. By Conformal invariance of SLE, it transforms an SLE6​ from 0 to x into one from 0 to ∞. Until x is disconnected from infinity, the discrepancy between the two target domains lies beyond the component visible from the growing tip. The Locality property of SLE therefore makes the two initial curve laws identical up to that disconnection time. Hence an SLE6​ from 0 to x, stopped at τx​, has the law of an SLE6​ from 0 to ∞ stopped when it disconnects x from infinity.
