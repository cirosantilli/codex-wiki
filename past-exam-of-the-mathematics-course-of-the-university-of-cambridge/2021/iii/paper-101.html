a OurBigBook.com
Suppose every submodule of M is finitely generated. For an ascending chain N1​⊆N2​⊆⋯, its union N is a submodule. Finitely many generators of N all lie in one Nr​, so N=Nr​ and the chain stabilizes. Thus the ascending chain condition holds.
If the ascending chain condition holds, any nonempty collection of submodules has a maximal member: otherwise, starting from one member and repeatedly choosing a strictly larger one constructs a nonstationary ascending chain.
Finally, assume the maximal condition. Among the finitely generated submodules of a given N⊆M, choose a maximal one F. If F=N, then F+Rx is a larger finitely generated submodule for any x∈N∖F, a contradiction. Hence every N is finitely generated. These are the three equivalent characterizations of a Noetherian module.

If M is Noetherian, every submodule of N is a submodule of M, and submodules of M/N correspond to submodules of M containing N; hence both are Noetherian.
Conversely, suppose N and M/N are Noetherian. For any P⊆M, the intersection P∩N is finitely generated and the image (P+N)/N is finitely generated. Lifting generators of the image and applying part i to
0⟶P∩N⟶P⟶(P+N)/N⟶0
(2)
shows that P is finitely generated. Thus M is Noetherian.

The weak Hilbert Nullstellensatz says that if a field is a finitely generated algebra over k, then it is a finite algebraic extension of k. Since every maximal ideal is prime, the nilradical N(R) is contained in the Jacobson radical J(R).
Conversely, let f be nonnilpotent. Then Rf​=0, and it is a finitely generated k-algebra, so it has a maximal ideal n. Its contraction p to R avoids f. Moreover,
R/p↪Rf​/n
(18)
is a finitely generated k-domain inside a finite algebraic extension of k, hence is itself a field. Thus p is maximal and does not contain f. Therefore f∈/J(R), proving
N(R)=J(R).​
(19)

An R-module F is flat when −⊗R​F preserves injections, equivalently all finite exact sequences. Since −⊗R​R is naturally the identity functor, R is flat. A free module is a direct sum of copies of R, and tensor products commute with direct sums, so every free module is flat.
As an R-module,
R[X]=⨁n≥0​RXn
(23)
is free. Therefore the polynomial algebra R[X] is a flat R-algebra.

If x,y are integral over A, then A[x,y] is finite over A: it is generated by finitely many monomials xiyj. Multiplication by x+y, x−y, or xy is an endomorphism of this finite module, so the determinant trick gives a monic annihilating polynomial. Hence the integral elements form a subring C containing A.
The integral closure of A in B is this subring C. The ring A is integrally closed in B when C=A, and B is integral over A when C=B.

The going-up theorem states: if B is integral over A, p1​⊆p2​ are primes of A, and q1​ lies over p1​, then some prime q2​⊇q1​ lies over p2​.
Pass to A/p1​⊆B/q1​, which remains integral, and localize at the complement of p2​/p1​. The lying-over theorem supplies a prime of the localized upper ring over the maximal ideal of the localized lower ring. Contracting it to B/q1​, and then pulling it back to B, gives the required q2​.

The height htp is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of primes ending at p. The Krull principal ideal theorem says that in a Noetherian ring every prime minimal over a principal proper ideal has height at most one.
Induct on n. If p is minimal over I=(x1​,…,xn​), choose a prime q⊆p minimal over (x1​,…,xn−1​) and localize appropriately. In R/q, the prime p/q is minimal over the principal ideal generated by xn​, so its relative height is at most one. Induction gives htq≤n−1, hence
htp≤n.​
(40)

Write p=(x). The principal ideal theorem and the hypothesis give htp=1. We use the standard principal prime in a Noetherian local ring lemma: a principal prime of positive height in a Noetherian local ring is generated by a nonzerodivisor and is the unique minimal prime above zero. The lemma follows by applying the associated-prime description of zero divisors and Nakayama's lemma to ann(x); if a nonzero annihilator or another minimal component existed, the principal prime would have height zero.
Here is the needed argument directly. For every n≥1,
ann(xn)⊆p.
(41)
Indeed, if yxn=0 with y∈/p, then y is a unit in Rp​, so xn=0 there. The maximal ideal pRp​=(x) would then be nilpotent, making Rp​ zero-dimensional, contrary to htp≥1.
Now suppose xy=0. The displayed containment gives y=xy1​, then x2y1​=0 gives y1​=xy2​, and inductively
y=xnyn​,xn+1yn​=0.
(42)
Because R is Noetherian, the ascending chain ann(x)⊆ann(x2)⊆⋯ stabilizes, say at n=N. Then yN​∈ann(xN+1)=ann(xN), and hence y=xNyN​=0. Thus x is a nonzerodivisor.
Let N=⋂n≥0​xnR. It is a finitely generated ideal. If y=xz∈N, then xz∈xn+1R for every n; cancellation of the nonzerodivisor x gives z∈xnR for every n. Hence N=xN, and Nakayama lemma gives N=0 because x lies in the maximal ideal.
Every nonzero element consequently has a finite x-adic order. If nonzero a,b satisfied ab=0, write a=xra′ and b=xsb′ with a′,b′∈/(x). Cancelling xr+s gives a′b′=0, contradicting primality of (x). Equivalently, (0) is prime, so
R is an integral domain.​
(43)

R∗=⨁n≥0​Intn,M∗=⨁n≥0​Mn​tn.

If the filtration is stable from degree ℓ, then M∗ is generated over R∗ by finite generating sets for M0​,…,Mℓ​. Conversely, let homogeneous elements of degrees at most ℓ generate M∗. In every degree n>ℓ, each expression for an element of Mn​ uses a positive-degree coefficient from R∗, so Mn​=IMn−1​. Hence finite generation is equivalent to stability.
