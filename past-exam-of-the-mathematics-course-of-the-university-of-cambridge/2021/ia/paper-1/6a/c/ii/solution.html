Each entry of adj(M−tI) is, up to sign, the determinant of an (n−1)×(n−1) minor whose entries are affine polynomials in t. The Leibniz formula for determinants therefore makes each adjugate entry a polynomial in t of degree at most n−1.
For arbitrary A,B, choose positive sequences sj​,tj​→0 for which A−sj​I and B−tj​I are nonsingular. The nonsingular identity from part (b) applies to their product:
adj((A−sj​I)(B−tj​I))=adj(B−tj​I)adj(A−sj​I).
(37)
Every entry is a polynomial, hence continuous, in the matrix entries. Letting j→∞ proves
adj(AB)=adj(B)adj(A)​
(38)
for all square matrices.
