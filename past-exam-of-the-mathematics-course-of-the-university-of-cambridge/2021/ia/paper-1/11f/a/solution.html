The Taylor theorem with Lagrange remainder states that if f is n times differentiable between a and x, then some ξ between a and x satisfies
f(x)=∑j=0n−1​j!f(j)(a)​(x−a)j+n!f(n)(ξ)​(x−a)n.
(74)
Let Tn−1​ denote the displayed polynomial and choose K so that
F(t)=f(t)−Tn−1​(t)−K(t−a)n
(75)
satisfies F(x)=0. By construction,
F(a)=F′(a)=⋯=F(n−1)(a)=0.
(76)
Starting with the two zeros a,x and applying Rolle theorem repeatedly, there is a ξ between them with F(n)(ξ)=0. Since
F(n)(ξ)=f(n)(ξ)−n!K,
(77)
we have K=f(n)(ξ)/n!, proving the theorem.
