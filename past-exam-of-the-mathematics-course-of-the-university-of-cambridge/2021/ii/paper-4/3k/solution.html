For Rabin cryptosystem, choose primes p,q≡3(mod4), publish N=pq, and encrypt x as x2modN. The factors let the receiver take square roots modulo p and q and combine them using the Chinese remainder theorem; redundancy identifies the intended one of four roots.
For RSA cryptosystem, choose N=pq, select e coprime to φ(N) and d with ed≡1(modφ(N)). Encrypt x as xe and decrypt by raising to d modulo N. Rabin inversion is provably equivalent to factoring, while ordinary RSA lacks that reduction; Rabin's disadvantage is its fourfold decryption ambiguity.
