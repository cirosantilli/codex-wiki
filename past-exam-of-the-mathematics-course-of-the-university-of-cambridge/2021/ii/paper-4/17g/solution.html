Hall marriage theorem states that a bipartite graph with parts A,B has a matching saturating A iff ∣N(S)∣≥∣S∣ for every S⊆A. Starting with a maximum matching, an unmatched vertex and its alternating reachable set would violate Hall unless an augmenting path exists; flipping along that path increases the matching, proving sufficiency.
Every vertex cover meets each edge of a matching, so γ≤β. Endpoints of a maximal matching cover every edge, so β≤2γ. A disjoint union of k triangles has (γ,β)=(k,2k). For T3​(30), γ=15 and β=30−10=20. In bipartite graphs the alternating-path proof constructs a cover of size equal to a maximum matching, giving König theorem.
The chromatic index is the minimum number of matchings partitioning the edges. Label vertices of K2r​ by the vector space over F2​; for each nonzero a, pair x with x+a. These 2r−1 perfect matchings partition all edges, while degree gives the matching lower bound, so χ′=n−1.
