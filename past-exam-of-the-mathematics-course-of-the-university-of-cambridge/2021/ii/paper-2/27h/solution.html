For a nonnegative measurable f, define
fn​(x)=2−n⌊2nmin(f(x),n)⌋.
(127)
Each fn​ takes only finitely many values, its level sets are measurable, and fn​(x)→f(x) pointwise. Thus measurable nonnegative functions are pointwise limits of simple functions.
If f is integrable, the same functions satisfy 0≤fn​≤f and converge pointwise. The dominated convergence theorem gives
∫E​∣fn​−f∣dμ⟶0.
(128)
They are integrable because they are bounded above by f.
If 0≤f≤M, instead set
gn​=2−n⌊2nf⌋.
(129)
This has finitely many values and
0≤f−gn​<2−n
(130)
everywhere, so gn​→f uniformly.
