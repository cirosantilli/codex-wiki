Let Nj​(A) be the number of particles of gas j in a bounded Lebesgue measurable set A. Because Lebesgue measure is diffuse, two independent spatial Poisson point processes have no common particle almost surely. Hence the mixture count is
N(A)=N1​(A)+N2​(A).
(347)
Writing λj​=zj​∣A∣, independence gives the probability generating function
E[sN(A)]​=E[sN1​(A)]E[sN2​(A)]=exp(λ1​(s−1))exp(λ2​(s−1))=exp((z1​+z2​)∣A∣(s−1)).​
(348)
Thus
N(A)∼Pois((z1​+z2​)∣A∣).
(349)
If A1​,…,Am​ are pairwise disjoint, the family
{Nj​(Ar​):j=1,2, 1≤r≤m}

These two facts prove directly that the mixture is a Poisson point process. By the Superposition theorem for Poisson point processes, its activity is
