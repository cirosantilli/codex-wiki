If some Ti​ were inconsistent, it would prove every formula by the classical principle of explosion. Because Ti​ is a deductively closed set of formulae, it would then contain every formula and could have no proper extension. This contradicts Ti​⊊Ti+1​. Thus every Ti​ is consistent.
Let
T=⋃i=0∞​Ti​.
(159)
If T were inconsistent, the finite character of formal proofs would give a finite inconsistent subset R⊆T. The chain is increasing, so all finitely many members of R lie in some common TN​. Then TN​ would be inconsistent, a contradiction. Hence T is consistent.
If T⊢φ, a proof again uses only finitely many assumptions from T, all lying in some TN​. Thus
TN​⊢φ.
(160)
Since TN​ is deductively closed, φ∈TN​⊆T. Therefore T is deductively closed.
Finally, suppose that T were finitary. Part (b) would provide a finite R⊆T such that R⊢T. Choose N with R⊆TN​. Then TN​⊢T; since TN​ is deductively closed, this implies T⊆TN​. But
TN​⊊TN+1​⊆T,
(161)
a contradiction. Consequently T is not finitary, proving the increasing union of deductively closed sets.
