With unit constant bending coefficients and unit length, the quadratic elastic energy is
E=21​∫01​(y′′2+z′′2−F(y′2+z′2))dx.
(51)
To find the first loss of positivity, put v=y′. The clamped conditions give v(0)=v(1)=0 and ∫01​vdx=0. Extend v periodically with period one. The sharp periodic Wirtinger inequality gives ∫01​v′2dx≥4π2∫01​v2dx. For completeness, expand the mean-zero periodic v in its Fourier series ∑n≥1​(an​cos2πnx+bn​sin2πnx). Parseval identity gives
∫01​v2dx=21​∑n≥1​(an2​+bn2​),∫01​v′2dx=21​∑n≥1​(2πn)2(an2​+bn2​),
(52)
which proves the inequality and its equality condition. The same holds for z′. Consequently
E≥24π2−F​∫01​(y′2+z′2)dx.
(53)
For F<4π2, the straight filament is the unique minimum. Equality in the periodic Wirtinger inequality has v=acos2πx+bsin2πx; the endpoint value v(0)=0 eliminates a. Integration and the position boundary conditions therefore give the first buckling load and modes:
Fc​=4π2,y=α(1−cos2πx),z=β(1−cos2πx).​
(54)
At this load every such pair has zero quadratic elastic energy and solves the Euler-Lagrange equations. For F>Fc​ these modes give negative energy, signaling instability of the straight configuration. The quadratic small-slope model does not select a finite post-buckling amplitude; nonlinear terms would be needed for that. This is Euler buckling of an elastic filament.
