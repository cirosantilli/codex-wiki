Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2016 / ib / Paper 2 / 9H / Solution

Introduce nonnegative slack variables s1​,s2​,s3​ and start the simplex algorithm at x=y=z=0, with slacks 9,8,2. The objective's largest positive entering coefficient belongs to y. Its ratio test is 9/3=3, 8/2=4, 2/1=2, so s3​ leaves and y enters.
Solve that pivot row for y and substitute into the other rows and the objective v:
ys1​s2​v​=2−2x−3z−s3​,=3−2x−z+3s3​,=4−x+2z+2s3​,=10−7x−15z−5s3​.​
(23)
This is a feasible simplex dictionary at nonbasic variables x=z=s3​=0. All their objective coefficients are negative, so no feasible increase can improve the objective. Thus
(x,y,z)=(0,2,0),vmax​=10.​
(24)
The negative reduced coefficients also prove uniqueness of this optimum.
The dual linear program, with nonnegative variables u1​,u2​,u3​, is
minimize 9u1​+8u2​+2u3​,8u1​+5u2​+2u3​≥3,3u1​+2u2​+u3​≥5,10u1​+4u2​+3u3​≥0.​
(25)
The feasible choice (u1​,u2​,u3​)=(0,0,5)​ has objective 10. Weak duality proves that it and the primal point are optimal. Complementary slackness also forces u1​=u2​=0 because the first two primal slacks are positive, and then the positive y forces u3​=5.
