Rouché's theorem. Suppose f,g are holomorphic functions on a neighbourhood of the closure of a bounded domain enclosed by a simple, positively oriented, piecewise smooth curve. If ∣g(z)∣<∣f(z)∣ on the boundary, then f and f+g have the same number of zeros inside, counted with multiplicity.
Apply Rouche theorem with f(z)=z4+3 and g(z)=eiz on the first-quadrant quarter-disc of radius R=2​. On the two radial segments, z4 is nonnegative real, so ∣f∣≥3, whereas ∣g∣=e−Imz≤1. On the circular arc,
∣f(z)∣≥∣z∣4−3=1.
(10)
At every arc point with positive imaginary part, ∣g∣<1, giving the strict inequality; at the remaining endpoint z=R, ∣f∣=7>1=∣g∣. Thus the strict boundary inequality holds everywhere, including the origin.
The four zeros of f have modulus 31/4 and arguments π/4,3π/4,5π/4,7π/4. Exactly one lies inside the chosen domain. Rouche theorem gives exactly one zero of f+g there, counted with multiplicity, so that zero is simple. Finally any zero in the open first quadrant satisfies
∣z∣4=∣3+eiz∣≤3+e−Imz<4.
(11)
There can be no additional zero outside the quarter-disc. Hence
Exactly one zero lies in the first quadrant, and ∣z0​∣<2​.​
