Put κ=a/b​>0. Solving the interior Euler-Lagrange equation and imposing both V(0)=0 and V˙(0)=0 yields V=C(coshκt−1), with λ=−2aC. The distance constraint fixes
Vformal​(t)=sinh(κT)/κ−TL(coshκt−1)​.​
(76)
The denominator is positive because sinhz>z for z>0. This is the requested solution of the differential equation with the stated initial conditions.
As a minimum-energy problem with free terminal speed, the printed conditions do not admit a smooth minimizer. The formal profile has V˙(T)=Cκsinh(κT)>0, violating the natural terminal condition from part (a). Fixing the initial acceleration restricts η′(0), but it does not restrict the independently variable η(T) and therefore does not remove that condition.
For clarity, without the extra initial-acceleration restriction the actual unique minimizing profile is
V∗​(t)=T−tanh(κT)/κL​(1−cosh(κT)cosh[κ(T−t)]​).​
(77)
It satisfies V∗​(0)=0, V˙∗​(T)=0, the distance constraint and the interior equation, but V˙∗​(0)>0. For any perturbation preserving the distance and initial speed, the first variation about V∗​ is zero, and
E[V∗​+η]−E[V∗​]=∫0T​(aη2+bη˙​2)dt≥0,
(78)
with equality only for η=0. Its energy is aL2/[T−tanh(κT)/κ].
This same energy is the unattained infimum under the extra condition V˙(0)=0. Indeed multiply V∗​ by 1−e−t/ε and rescale by a factor tending to one to restore total distance L. These smooth nonnegative profiles have both initial values zero and converge to V∗​ in value and derivative square integrals as ε↓0. Their energies tend to E[V∗​], but uniqueness of V∗​ precludes attaining it with zero initial acceleration. This is nonattainment under an initial derivative constraint.
