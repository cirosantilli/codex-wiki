The algebraic dual map is δ=α∨:W∗→V∗, defined by δ(φ)=φ∘α. The annihilator of a vector subspace U⊆V is
U∘={λ∈V∗:λ(u)=0 for all u∈U}.
(39)
Every functional φ∘α vanishes on kerα, so imδ⊆(kerα)∘. Conversely, let λ∈(kerα)∘. Define a functional on imα by λ(αv)=λ(v). It is well defined because two preimages differ by an element of kerα. Extend a basis of imα to one of W and extend the functional arbitrarily, for instance by zero on the extra basis vectors. The resulting φ∈W∗ satisfies δφ=λ.
This is the dual image and kernel annihilator identity. Therefore
imδ=(kerα)∘.​
(40)
The basis extension theorem also gives dimU∘=dimV−dimU. By the rank-nullity theorem,
dimimδ=dimV−dimkerα=dimimα.​
(41)
Apply rank-nullity theorem to both maps, using dimW∗=dimW, to obtain
dimkerα−dimkerδ=dimV−dimW.​
(42)
Over C, this is the algebraic dual, without complex conjugation; the adjoint operator in part (b) uses the inner products instead.
