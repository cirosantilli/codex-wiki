Let T be a closed triangle contained, together with a neighbourhood, in the domain of a holomorphic function f. We prove Cauchy theorem for a triangle without assuming continuity of f′.
Join the edge midpoints to subdivide T into four triangles, each with half its diameter and perimeter. Their positively oriented contour integrals sum to the integral around T, because the internal edges cancel. At least one subtriangle has integral of modulus at least one quarter of the original. Repeat that choice to obtain nested closed triangles Tn​ satisfying
​∫∂T​f(z)dz​≤4n​∫∂Tn​​f(z)dz​,dn​=2−nd0​,Pn​=2−nP0​.
(40)
Their intersection is a single point z∗​, by compactness and shrinking diameter. complex differentiability at a point at z∗​ gives
f(z)=f(z∗​)+f′(z∗​)(z−z∗​)+(z−z∗​)r(z),r(z)→0.
(41)
The first two terms have polynomial primitives, hence integrate to zero around each triangle. For sufficiently large n, ∣r∣<ε on Tn​, so the complex line integral estimate gives
​∫∂Tn​​f(z)dz​≤εdn​Pn​.
(42)
Combining the bounds gives ∣∫∂T​f(z)dz∣≤εd0​P0​. Since ε is arbitrary,
∫∂T​f(z)dz=0.​
(43)
The orientation can be reversed with no change to the conclusion.
