For fixed θ>0, the central limit theorem gives
θ​n​(X−θ)​ d​ N(0,1).
(86)
The weak law of large numbers gives X→θ, so Slutsky theorem permits replacing θ in the standard error by X. With z=z0.025​≈1.96, an approximate 95% confidence interval is
​X−znX​​,X+znX​​​.​
(87)
The lower endpoint may be truncated at zero because the parameter is nonnegative. This is a large-sample normal approximation for fixed positive θ; an all-zero or very sparse sample does not justify the plug-in normal approximation. At the degenerate parameter value zero, every observation is zero almost surely.
