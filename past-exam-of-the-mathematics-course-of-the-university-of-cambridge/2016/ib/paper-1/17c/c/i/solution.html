Write the conservative body force per unit mass as −∇Φ. For a fluid of constant density ρ, the Euler equations for an inviscid fluid and the incompressible flow condition are
∂t​u+(u⋅∇)u=−∇(ρp​+Φ),∇⋅u=0.
(89)
Using part (a), these become
∂t​u−u×ω=−∇H,H=21​∣u∣2+ρp​+Φ.
(90)
For irrotational flow, locally choose a velocity potential with u=∇ϕ. Then ∇(∂t​ϕ+H)=0, so the unsteady Bernoulli integral is
∂t​ϕ+21​∣u∣2+ρp​+Φ=C(t).​
(91)
The constant is spatially uniform on a connected region where the velocity potential is defined; its time dependence can be absorbed into the additive time-dependent gauge of ϕ. A global single-valued velocity potential requires an appropriate topological condition, such as a simply connected domain; without it, the integral is locally valid. In a connected steady irrotational region, ∇H=0 directly gives one spatial Bernoulli constant even without choosing a global potential.
