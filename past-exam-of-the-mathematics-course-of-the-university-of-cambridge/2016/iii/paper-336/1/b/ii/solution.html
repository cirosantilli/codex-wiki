Let b=eiθ and use the method of steepest descents with phase Φ(z)=−z2. For z=x+iy, ReΦ=y2−x2 and ImΦ=−2xy. The simple saddle point is z=0: the real axis is its descending path and the imaginary axis its ascending path. The quadratic complex endpoint descent path from b satisfies Im(z2)=sin2θ and can be parametrized by
z(s)2=b2+s,s≥0,
(10)
with the square-root branch chosen continuously from b. Increasing s reduces the real part of the exponent by λs.
For 0≤θ<π/2, that endpoint path goes to positive real infinity. Deform the original contour into the real path from −d through the saddle point towards +∞, followed backwards along the endpoint descent path to b. The integrand is an entire function, so finite contour deformations are legitimate; connecting arcs in the far decaying sector vanish. If Tb​ denotes the oriented integral from b to that infinity, this gives
f=λπ​​−Td​−Tb​,Td​∼2​λe−λ/2​,Tb​∼2λbe−λb2​.
(11)
The last expression follows by setting z2=b2+s: dz/ds=1/(2z), so the endpoint integral is e−λb2∫0∞​e−λs/[2z(s)]ds. This derives its phase and orientation directly.
For π/2<θ≤π, the descent path from b goes to negative real infinity. Deform from −d towards that infinity and then backwards along this endpoint path to b. There is no saddle point segment, and
f=−Td​−Tb​,Tb​∼2λbe−λb2​
(12)
uses the same formula with the negative square-root branch. At θ=π/2 the contour can run from −d to zero and then up the imaginary axis. It gives a half-Gaussian contribution and the dominant imaginary endpoint term ieλ/(2λ). The change in the subdominant saddle point contribution is a Stokes phenomenon; it does not change the leading endpoint term on either side of that angle.
The Gaussian saddle and endpoint dominance by angle follows by comparing exponential sizes and gives all fixed-angle leading behaviours:
f(θ,λ)∼⎩⎨⎧​π/λ​,−2λeiθe−λe2iθ​,−λe−λ/2​[2​1​+2e5πi/6ei3​λ/2​],−2​λe−λ/2​,​0≤θ≤π/4,π/4<θ<5π/6,θ=5π/6,5π/6<θ≤π.​​
(13)
At θ=π/4, the endpoint term has magnitude O(λ−1), so the saddle point term of order λ−1/2 still dominates. In the middle interval the endpoint term grows exponentially for π/4<θ<3π/4, is oscillatory of order λ−1 at 3π/4, and decays exponentially for 3π/4<θ<5π/6. On the latter interval it nevertheless decays more slowly than the fixed-endpoint term. Equality of their exponential rates means cos2θ=1/2 on the left half-plane, hence θ=5π/6. At that angle both terms are required; their coefficient magnitudes are unequal, so their sum has no complete destructive cancellation.
These fixed-angle asymptotic expansions are not uniform if θ is allowed to vary with λ near a dominance transition. The endpoint/saddle balance near π/4 involves λ(θ−π/4)=O(logλ); the two-endpoint competition near 5π/6 has an angular scale O(λ−1).
