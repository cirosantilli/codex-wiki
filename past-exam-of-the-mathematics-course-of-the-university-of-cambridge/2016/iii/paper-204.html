In bond percolation on an undirected graph G=(V,E), a configuration is ω∈{0,1}E. The edge e is open when ωe​=1 and closed when ωe​=0. Under Pp​, the coordinates are independent and identically distributed random variables with the Bernoulli distribution of parameter p. For a countable edge set, use the product sigma-algebra and the product measure
Pp​=⨂e∈E​Bernoulli(p).
(1)
The open edges form a random subgraph with the original graph vertices. Its connected components of a graph are the percolation clusters. Write x↔y when an open graph path joins x to y; a zero-length graph path is allowed, so x↔x always holds. The randomness is in the independently open bonds; all vertices remain present.

Order configurations coordinatewise: ω≤ω′ means ωe​≤ωe′​ for every edge. An increasing event A is a measurable set such that ω∈A and ω≤ω′ imply ω′∈A. Opening additional edges cannot destroy an increasing event. The Harris-FKG inequality, the product measure version of the FKG inequality, states
Pp​(A∩B)≥Pp​(A)Pp​(B)​
(2)
for two increasing events. Equivalently, bounded coordinatewise increasing random variables f,g satisfy Ep​(fg)≥Ep​fEp​g. The event inequality also holds for two decreasing events, by taking complements.
For disjoint occurrence of increasing events, a finite open edge set K witnesses A in ω if every configuration agreeing with ω on K belongs to A. Since A is an increasing event and all edges of K are open, this says that the configuration with exactly K open already forces A. Define A□B to consist of configurations admitting disjoint finite witnesses K,L for A,B. For events depending on finitely many edges, this is the usual disjoint-occurrence definition; it also applies to finite-connection events on the infinite graph. The Van den Berg-Kesten inequality states
Pp​(A□B)≤Pp​(A)Pp​(B)​.
(3)
On a countable graph, its finite-witness version follows by taking increasing unions over finite edge sets. The Harris-FKG inequality concerns simultaneous occurrence, whereas the Van den Berg-Kesten inequality requires separate certificates using disjoint coordinates.

Use integer graph vertices in the boxes: Λn​=[−n,n]2∩Z2, and set Λ−1​=∅. Thus ∂Λ0​={0}, g0​=1, and ∣∂Λm​∣=8m for m≥1.
If 0↔∂Λm+n​, erase loops from an open graph path to obtain a self-avoiding walk. Let x be its first graph vertex on ∂Λm​. Split at x. The prefix certifies Ax​={0↔x}, while a segment of the suffix certifies Bx​={x↔x+∂Λn​}: the final graph vertex is at supremum norm distance at least n from x, so the suffix first hits that translated boundary. The two certificates use disjoint edges. Therefore the BK boundary-splitting estimate, the union bound, the Van den Berg-Kesten inequality, and translation invariance give
gm+n​​≤x∈∂Λm​∑​Pp​(Ax​□Bx​)≤x∈∂Λm​∑​Pp​(Ax​)Pp​(Bx​)≤∣∂Λm​∣gm​gn​.​
(4)
The cases m=0 or n=0 follow directly from g0​=1, so this proves the bound including the endpoints.
Here is an explicit way to remove the polynomial boundary factor. For p>0 put bn​=32n2gn​, n≥1. By symmetry in m,n, assume m≤n. Then
bm​bn​bm+n​​≤4mn2(m+n)2​≤m1​≤1.
(5)
Consequently an​=logbn​ is a subadditive sequence. The Fekete lemma states that any real subadditive sequence satisfies liman​/n=infn≥1​an​/n, possibly −∞. In this case the direct horizontal open graph path gives gn​≥pn, so this limit of a sequence is bounded below by logp. Since gn​≤1, its upper bound is zero. Finally,
nloggn​​=nan​​−nlog(32n2)​

has the same limit of a sequence. Thus the percolation one-arm decay rate exists and
γ=n→∞lim​gn1/n​∈[p,1]​.
(7)
For p=0, every gn​ with n≥1 is zero and γ=0. No exponential-decay theorem or assumption that p is below the percolation critical probability is needed.

The union bound gives gk​≤∑x∈∂Λk​​Pp​(0↔x). Hence some boundary graph vertex has percolation two-point connection probability at least gk​/(8k). The rotations and reflections of the square lattice let us choose such a graph vertex as x=(k,j), −k≤j≤k. Reflection in the vertical line through x sends 0 to e2k​=(2k,0) and fixes x. Therefore
Pp​(x↔e2k​)=Pp​(0↔x).
(8)
The Harris-FKG inequality applied to these two increasing events proves the reflection lower bound for two-point percolation:
h2k​≥Pp​(0↔x, x↔e2k​)≥(8kgk​​)2.
(9)
Every graph path from 0 to e2k​ reaches ∂Λ2k​, so h2k​≤g2k​. Taking roots gives
(8kgk​​)1/k≤h2k1/(2k)​≤g2k1/(2k)​.

An n-step self-avoiding walk on a graph G=(V,E) is a sequence (v0​,v1​,…,vn​) of n+1 distinct graph vertices such that {vi−1​,vi​}∈E for 1≤i≤n. It has n edges and no repeated vertex, including its starting graph vertex. The 0-step self-avoiding walk consists of its starting graph vertex alone.

Represent the doubly infinite ladder graph by Z×{0,1}, with horizontal edges between successive columns and a vertical rung at each column. An allowed self-avoiding walk is coded by a word in R,V. Two successive V steps revisit the preceding graph vertex and are forbidden. Conversely, every word without consecutive V steps is a self-avoiding walk: horizontal steps strictly increase the column, and a column is visited vertically at most once. This establishes a bijection, not merely an upper bound.
For the directed ladder self-avoiding walk count, σ0​=1, σ1​=2, and for n≥2 a word either ends in R, or ends in RV. Removing that final block gives
σn​=σn−1​+σn−2​,σn​=Fn+2​​.
(12)
Let φ=(1+5​)/2 be the golden ratio, and ψ=(1−5​)/2. The Binet formula for the Fibonacci number yields
σn​=5​φn+2−ψn+2​=5​φn+2​(1−(φψ​)n+2).
(13)
Since ∣ψ∣<φ, taking nth roots gives limn→∞​σn1/n​=φ. This use of the golden-ratio symbol is independent of the connection rate in Question 1.

Let cn​ count n-step self-avoiding walks on the square lattice from one fixed graph vertex. Splitting a self-avoiding walk after m steps and dropping the avoidance constraint on its suffix gives cm+n​≤cm​cn​. The Fekete lemma therefore gives the connective constant μ=limcn1/n​. The planar dual graph of the square lattice is a translated copy of it, so its connective constant is also μ.
Put q=1−p and assume qμ<1. Choose a>μ with qa<1. The definition of the connective constant provides a finite K such that cj​≤Kaj for every j≥0. A simple dual graph cycle of length ℓ surrounding 0 has a graph vertex in a box of radius ℓ+1: its diameter is at most ℓ, and its coordinate ranges straddle the origin. Choose such a graph vertex as the starting point, orient the graph cycle, and omit its closing edge. The remaining self-avoiding walk has length ℓ−1. Consequently the number Nℓ​ of these graph cycles obeys
Nℓ​≤K0​ℓ2cℓ−1​≤K1​ℓ2aℓ−1
(14)
for fixed finite constants. A specified graph cycle is open in dual bond percolation, equivalently all its crossed primal edges are closed, with probability qℓ. Hence
∑ℓ≥L​Nℓ​qℓ⟶0as L→∞.
(15)
To make this tail argument valid for every qμ<1, rather than only extremely small q, use a finite modification of Bernoulli percolation. Condition all primal edges within ΛN​ to be open. This event has positive probability for p>0. If the resulting percolation cluster of 0 is finite, its outer boundary contains a simple closed dual graph cycle surrounding the whole box. Such a graph cycle has length tending to infinity with N and crosses no forced-open edge. Under the conditioning its remaining edge states retain the original independent law, so its probability is still qℓ. Choose N so that the above tail is less than 1/2. The conditional probability that 0 belongs to an infinite percolation cluster is then at least 1/2, and thus θ(p)>0.
This connective-constant Peierls bound proves that every p>1−μ−1 is above or at the onset of positive percolation probability. Taking the infimum gives
pc​≤1−μ−1​.
(16)
The case p=1 is immediate. This proof uses the Peierls argument, without assuming the exact Harris-Kesten theorem.

The cubic lattice has graph vertices Zd and an edge between x,y when ∑i​∣xi​−yi​∣=1. In independent site percolation, every graph vertex is open with probability p and closed with probability 1−p, independently. The open subgraph contains only open graph vertices and the edges between them. Let Cp​(0) be the open connected component of a graph containing 0, with Cp​(0)=∅ when 0 is closed. The percolation probability is
θ(p)=Pp​(∣Cp​(0)∣=∞)=Pp​(0↔∞)​.
(20)
Since the cubic lattice is a locally finite graph, the König infinity lemma identifies an infinite open connected component of a graph with the existence of an infinite open graph ray from the origin. In particular, the origin itself must be open. The percolation critical probability is pc​=inf{p:θ(p)>0}.

Let An​ be the event that the origin has an open graph path to the boundary of [−n,n]d∩Zd. The graph path can be stopped on its first boundary visit, so An​ depends on only finitely many sites. Its probability fn​(p)=Pp​(An​) is a finite sum of terms pa(1−p)b, and is therefore a continuous function of p. Also An+1​⊆An​, and the König infinity lemma gives ⋂n​An​={0↔∞}. By continuity from above of a measure,
θ(p)=infn≥1​fn​(p).
(21)
The monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation described below shows that θ is nondecreasing. For p<1, its right limit of a function θ(p+) exists and is at least θ(p). For every fixed n,
θ(p+)≤limr↓p​fn​(r)=fn​(p).
(22)
Taking the infimum in n proves θ(p+)=θ(p). This proves right continuity of percolation probability throughout [0,1); at 1 right continuity is understood relative to the parameter domain. The argument does not interchange two uncontrolled limits.

Use the probability space ([0,1]Zd,F,P) with the product measure of the uniform distribution on [0,1]. Its coordinate random variables Uv​ are independent and identically distributed random variables. Define simultaneously for every parameter
Xp​(v)=1{Uv​≤p}​,p∈[0,1], v∈Zd​.
(23)
For r≤s, the indicator function inequality Xr​(v)≤Xs​(v) holds pointwise at every graph vertex. For fixed p, each coordinate has the Bernoulli distribution with parameter p, and the coordinates are independent because they are functions of distinct independent Uv​. Thus every marginal configuration is exactly the required site percolation model. This is a continuum-indexed family of processes, all on one probability space, and is the standard monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation.

Work on the common probability space from part (c). For a configuration of all the uniforms, the set S={r∈[0,1]:Ir​ occurs} is an upper interval, possibly with or without its lower endpoint. It is nonempty because I1​ occurs, and the coupled onset parameter for origin percolation is M=infS. For 0<p≤1,
⋃r<pr∈Q∩[0,1]​​Ir​={M<p}.
(24)
Indeed, occurrence at some r<p implies M<p. Conversely, if M<p, the definition of infimum yields occurrence at some s<p, and a rational r between s and p also satisfies Ir​. This also proves measurability of M through its strict sublevel sets.
Choose a rational sequence increasing to p. By continuity from below of a measure and the monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation,
limr↑p​θ(r)=P(M<p).
(25)
Moreover, {M<p}⊆Ip​⊆{M≤p}. Hence Ip​ is the disjoint union of {M<p} and Ip​∩{M=p}, giving
r↑plim​θ(r)=θ(p)−P(Ip​∩{M=p})​.
(26)
It is important to retain the intersection with Ip​: occurrence at the infimum is not automatic. At p=0 there is no left limit within the parameter domain, so the displayed left-limit assertion is for p>0.

Use the following general result, including its site version: independent site percolation on Zd has almost surely at most one infinite percolation cluster at each fixed parameter; for every fixed r>pc​ it has exactly one almost surely. This is the uniqueness of the infinite percolation cluster, also called the Burton-Keane theorem. For d≥2, the additional general fact pc​<1 ensures a choice of a parameter strictly between pc​ and 1. In dimension one, pc​=1 and the asserted interval is empty.
Fix p∈(pc​,1] and choose q∈(pc​,p). Let Cq​ be the unique infinite open connected component of a graph at q. Under the monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation, it is contained in the unique infinite open connected component of a graph at p. On Ip​, the origin and Cq​ therefore lie in that same connected component of a graph, and a finite p-open graph path connects 0 to some graph vertex of Cq​.
For this fixed deterministic p, the countable set of uniforms satisfies Uv​=p at every graph vertex almost surely. Thus all uniforms on that finite graph path are strictly less than p, including its endpoints. If t is their maximum, choose r with max(q,t)<r<p. The graph path is then r-open, and its endpoint remains connected to infinity through Cq​. Consequently Ir​ occurs and M<p. We have proved
P(Ip​∩{M=p})=0.
(27)
Part (d) gives left continuity at this p; part (b) supplies right continuity when p<1. The same finite-path proof gives left continuity at p=1, since all uniforms are strictly less than 1 almost surely. Thus θ is continuous on (pc​,1], without asserting continuity at pc​. This is supercritical continuity of percolation probability.

Write H(R) and V(R) for the horizontal and vertical open-crossing increasing events of a rectangle in the rotated square lattice. At parameter 1/2, planar duality for rectangle crossings says that failure of H is exactly a closed dual vertical crossing. Quarter-turn symmetry and the boundary convention of the rotated rectangles identify the two alternatives on a square. Thus its crossing probability is 1/2; a rectangle obtained by a harmless one-step boundary adjustment has crossing probability at least 1/2. This is the finite-domain exact self-dual rectangle crossing probability, not an assumption about infinite percolation clusters.
We first prove a RSW reflection extension lemma. In coordinate units adapted to the rotated drawing, take S=[0,n]2, T=[−n,n]2, and R=[−n,2n]×[−n,n]. Reveal the rightmost open vertical crossing Γ of S, when one exists, using only its edges and the region to its right. Reflect Γ across the horizontal axis. The part D of T to the left of these two paths remains unexposed and has the independent parameter-1/2 law. A horizontal crossing of T must reach their union. Its probability is at least 1/2. Reflection symmetry and the union bound give a probability at least 1/4 of reaching the upper path Γ through D. Conditional opening of the edges on Γ can only help. Since V(S) has probability at least 1/2, the increasing event B that a vertical crossing of S is connected inside T to its left side has probability at least 1/8.
Reflect this construction in the vertical midline of S to obtain an increasing event B′ of the same probability, connecting a vertical crossing of S to the right side of [0,2n]×[−n,n]. On H(S)∩B∩B′, the horizontal crossing of S meets both vertical crossings, so all three graph paths join and give H(R). The Harris-FKG inequality now yields
P1/2​(H(R))≥21​(81​)2=1281​.
(28)
The exploration only conditions the exposed side of the extremal graph path; reflecting a drawn path does not assert that its reflected edges are open. This distinction is what makes the extension argument valid.
To pass from aspect ratio 3/2 to 3, put four rectangles of width 3h/2 and height h next to each other, with successive left boundaries separated by h/2. Adjacent rectangles overlap in a square of side h. Require horizontal crossings of the four rectangles and vertical crossings of the three overlap squares. Every consecutive pair of horizontal crossings meets the vertical crossing in its overlap; together they form a horizontal crossing of width 3h. The Harris-FKG inequality gives a strictly positive bound, for these compatible scales, of
c∗​=(1/128)4(1/2)3.
(29)
The construction works on the rotated square lattice with the boundary sites used in the source drawing. Integer roundings do not create a scale restriction: for an odd height use the largest smaller compatible even height, and use eight horizontal rectangles and seven overlap squares, whose union has auxiliary aspect ratio 5, so that its width exceeds the desired 3m width for all sufficiently large m. Truncate its horizontal crossing on first reaching the desired right side. The number of extra rectangles and overlap-square crossings is bounded independently of m, and every factor is bounded below by the same positive constants. The finitely many smallest rectangles each have positive crossing probability, since a specified finite open graph path suffices. Therefore a constant c>0 works at every integer scale. Taking τ=c/2 ensures the requested strict inequality:
m≥1inf​P1/2​(C3m,m​)≥c>τ>0​.
(30)
This proves the required instance of the Russo-Seymour-Welsh theorem through reflection, exploration and gluing. The parameter in this calculation is 1/2.

First obtain a polynomial critical one-arm upper bound from part (a). Fix a sufficiently large integer radius r. Use rotated-coordinate boxes Br​=[−r,r]2 for this geometric construction, in the axes of part (a). Surround Br​ by a square ring inside B3r​. It can be assembled from the four strips
[−3r,3r]×[r,3r],[−3r,3r]×[−3r,−r],[−3r,−r]×[−3r,3r],[r,3r]×[−3r,3r].
(31)
The first two require horizontal dual crossings; the last two require vertical dual crossings. Their corner overlaps are squares, so the adjacent long crossings intersect there and their union contains a dual graph cycle surrounding the inner box. Translate the strips by the half-lattice displacement of the planar dual graph and adjust the endpoints by one lattice unit if needed. All aspect ratios remain bounded. Part (a) and the same overlap gluing give a uniform lower bound c1​>0 for each dual crossing at 1/2. Applying the Harris-FKG inequality to these decreasing events in the primal configuration gives a closed dual circuit with probability at least δ=c14​>0. We may replace δ by a smaller number in (0,1).
Use the independent annular barriers for percolation at radii rj​=r0​10j. Their supporting edge sets are disjoint, so the circuit events are independent. In the original unrotated coordinates, B3r​ lies inside Λ5r​ and the ring still separates the origin from infinity. These fixed geometric comparisons let us bound the original gN​. An open graph path from 0 to distance N must avoid every such closed dual circuit inside ΛN​. Thus, for some constants C<∞ and α>0,
gN​(1/2)≤(1−δ)⌊log10​(N/(5r0​))⌋+1≤CN−α,α=log10−log(1−δ)​>0,
(32)
where the first bound is used only when at least one of the indicated rings fits; increasing C handles smaller N.
Now let ε=p−1/2>0 and use the monotone coupling of Bernoulli percolation on edges. The event {0↔∂ΛN​} only needs edges whose endpoints lie in ΛN​, because a first boundary hit gives an internal graph path. There are exactly 4N(2N+1) such edges. Each changes state between parameters 1/2 and p with probability ε. The union bound therefore gives the finite-box comparison for percolation parameters
θ(p)≤gN​(p)≤gN​(1/2)+4N(2N+1)ε≤CN−α+12N2ε.
(33)
For sufficiently small ε, choose N=⌊ε−1/(α+2)⌋, so that N is at least half the unrounded value. Both terms then have order εα/(α+2). In particular,
θ(p)≤A(p−1/2)β,β=α+2α​>0​
(34)
for a finite A. Increase A to cover the remaining compact range of parameters by θ(p)≤1. This near-critical percolation power upper bound requires only a positive crossing constant, not the exact value of a critical exponent. It also implies θ(1/2)=0 through the critical one-arm probability bound.
