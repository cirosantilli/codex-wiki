Choose the Hopf map η and orientations so its Hopf invariant is one. The cellular cohomology of Yd​ is Z in degrees 0,2,4 and zero otherwise. Let x,z be generators in degrees two and four. Precomposing η with a degree-d self-map of S3 represents dη. The induced map of mapping cones is the identity on the two-cell and has degree d on the four-cell. Since the Hopf mapping cone has cup square equal to its top generator, naturality of the cup product gives x2=dz. Equivalently, precomposition scales the Hopf invariant by degree. Hence
H∗(Yd​;Z)=Z[x,z]/(x2−dz,xz,z2),∣x∣=2,∣z∣=4​.
(7)
The coefficient is d, not d2: multiplication in π3​(S2) is being used, rather than postcomposition by a degree-d map of the target sphere.
For X=Xd,f​, the only nonzero cellular boundary is C4​→C3​, multiplication by f. Thus its cellular cohomology gives
Hq(X;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,ker(f:Z→Z),Z/fZ,0,​q=0,2,q=3,q=4,otherwise.​
(8)
Let x again generate H2, and let z be the class of the four-cell cochain. Collapsing the three-sphere gives a map X→Yd​ which pulls the degree-two generator back to x and the degree-four generator back to z. Hence x2=dz, now interpreted modulo f. Every other product of positive-degree classes vanishes by dimension. This is the cohomology ring of a Hopf attachment with a sphere summand. More explicitly, if f=0,
H∗(X;Z)=Z[x,z]/(fz,x2−dz,xz,z2)​.
(9)
If f=0, there is additionally a generator y of degree three, with xy=y2=yz=0. The displayed description of the groups uses Z/0Z=Z, so the zero case is included. In particular, a nonzero f kills the degree-three cohomology but can leave torsion in degree four.
There are no one-cells, and attaching cells of dimension at least three does not change π1​. Thus X is simply connected. The Hurewicz theorem in degree two gives
π1​(X)=0,π2​(X)≅H2​(X;Z)≅Z​.
(10)
To compute π3​, choose a map g:X→CP∞=K(Z,2) representing x. This Eilenberg–MacLane space can be realized by the classifying space of S1. On S2 the map is the standard inclusion; on S3 it is constant, and it extends over the four-cell since π3​(K(Z,2))=0. Its homotopy fibre T is the pullback of the universal circle bundle S∞→CP∞. The map on π2​ is an isomorphism, so the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration gives π1​(T)=π2​(T)=0 and π3​(T)≅π3​(X). Therefore the Hurewicz theorem identifies the latter with H3​(T;Z).
Over W=S2∨S3, the circle-bundle total space is
P=S3∪S1​(S1×S3).
(11)
The first summand is the Hopf fibration total space over S2, and the second is the trivial bundle over S3. Their intersection is the fibre over the wedge point. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives H3​(P)=ZA⊕ZB, with generators from the two three-spheres. These are the lifts of η1​ and i2​. The circle bundle over W also has a 2-connected total space, so lifting and the Hurewicz theorem identify dη1​+fi2​ with dA+fB.
Over the attached four-disc the bundle is trivial. The resulting relative pair has the homology of (S1×D4,S1×S3), so H4​(T,P)=Z and H3​(T,P)=0. Its boundary map sends a generator to the lift of the attaching map, namely dA+fB. The long exact sequence in relative homology now gives
H3​(T)=⟨dA+fB⟩ZA⊕ZB​.
(12)
Applying Smith normal form to this one relation proves the third homotopy group of a Hopf attachment with a sphere summand:
π3​(Xd,f​)≅Z⊕Z/gcd(d,f)Z​.
(13)
Since d>0, the greatest common divisor is positive even when f=0. This derivation uses the required map to an Eilenberg–MacLane space and determines the extension, rather than merely the orders of its pieces.
