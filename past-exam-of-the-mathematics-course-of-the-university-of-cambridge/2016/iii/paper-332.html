Take z positive downwards from the roof. Write T0​=Tm​−mC0​, CB​=(Tm​−TB​)/m, ΔT=T0​−TB​=m(CB​−C0​)>0 and ΔC=CB​−C0​. The cooled magma follows the decreasing liquidus from (C0​,T0​) towards (CB​,TB​); the initially superheated liquid starts at (C0​,T∞​) above that liquidus. The solid has zero solute concentration. At each temperature in the mushy layer, a horizontal tie line joins the pure solid to the residual liquid on the liquidus. The lever rule gives the liquid fraction from the bulk concentration. Since TB​>TE​, this path stops before the eutectic temperature and does not cross the eutectic composition.
The porosity ϕ denotes the liquid fraction, so 1−ϕ is the solid fraction. Equal phase mass densities remove volume-change flow; the stable density stratification suppresses convection. In the stagnant mushy-layer model, define bulk specific enthalpy and bulk solute concentration by
E=cp​T+Lϕ,B=ϕC+(1−ϕ)Cs​=ϕC.
(1)
The arbitrary reference of specific enthalpy has been omitted. The two conservation laws in 0<z<h(t) are
Tt​+cp​L​ϕt​=κTzz​,(ϕC)t​=0,T=Tm​−mC.​
(2)
Here κ=kT​/(ρcp​) is the thermal diffusivity, with kT​ the thermal conductivity. The positive Lϕt​ term records increased specific enthalpy on melting; during freezing it supplies latent heat. In the liquid z>h(t), the heat equation and solute diffusion equation are
Tt​=κTzz​,Ct​=DCzz​,ϕ=1,
(3)
where D is the solutal diffusivity. Neglecting solute diffusion inside the mushy layer does not permit dropping DCzz​ in the liquid at this stage.
Use T(0,t)=TB​, liquid T→T∞​ and C→C0​ as z→∞, and initially h=0, T=T∞​, C=C0​ for z>0. These are the half-space boundary conditions appropriate before cooling reaches the bottom of the chamber. For a closed chamber of finite depth H, replace the far-field conditions by the specified bottom conditions, for example Tz​=Cz​=0 at an insulated impermeable floor; the half-space similarity solution then ceases to apply when the thermal penetration depth approaches H.
At z=h(t), let Ci​ and Ti​=Tm​−mCi​ be the common liquid concentration and temperature, and let ϕi​ be the limiting porosity on the mush side. Continuity of T,C, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for solute, and the interfacial conservation of energy give
−DCz​(h+,t)=h˙(1−ϕi​)Ci​,κ[Tz​(h−,t)−Tz​(h+,t)]=cp​L​h˙(1−ϕi​).
(4)
The first condition has the sign of solute rejection into the liquid. For the sharp-edge stagnant mushy-layer model with nonzero liquid solutal diffusivity, close the free-boundary problem by marginal equilibrium at a mush-liquid boundary:
Tz​(h+,t)=−mCz​(h+,t),T(z,t)≥Tm​−mC(z,t)(z>h).
(5)
This prevents constitutional supercooling immediately ahead of the mushy layer. A small solid fraction jump at its edge is allowed: imposing ϕi​=1 as well as these finite-D conditions would generally overdetermine the reduced model. At the roof, C=CB​ follows from the liquidus, and no additional solute-flux boundary condition is needed in the diffusion-free mush.
To obtain the large-concentration similarity solution, put
θ=ΔTT−T0​​,θ∞​=ΔTT∞​−T0​​>0,C=ΔCC0​​,S=cp​ΔTL​,r=κD​,η=2κt​z​,h=2λκt​.
(6)
The positive superheat specifies the physically intended liquid initial state. On a similarity solution, Ci​,ϕi​ are constant, so every newly incorporated level leaves the same constant B=ϕi​Ci​. Write Ci​=C0​+ΔCδ and B=ΔC(C+a). Then throughout the mushy layer
C=C0​−ΔCθ,ϕ=C−θC+a​,[1+S(C−θ)2C+a​]θt​=κθzz​.
(7)
For a,θ=O(1), C≫1, and S/C=O(1), the effective heat capacity is constant to relative error O(C−1). Thus set
Ω=1+S/C​,Ω2θt​=κθzz​.​
(8)
Integration of the similarity solution ordinary differential equation gives the leading analytical profiles
θm​=−1+(1−δ)erf(Ωλ)erf(Ωη)​,θl​=θ∞​−(θ∞​+δ)erfcλerfcη​.​
(9)
The temperature is T=T0​+ΔTθ. In the mushy layer, the concentration and porosity are the displayed C,ϕ; in the liquid,
Cl​=C0​+ΔCδerfc(λ/r​)erfc(η/r​)​,ϕl​=1.​
(10)
These retain the liquid solutal boundary layer. For completeness, finite-r values of δ,a,λ are determined analytically, to the same large-C order, by setting
K(x)=erfcxe−x2​,Ar​=π​λr​K(λ/r​)​,δ=K(λ/r​)/r​−K(λ)θ∞​K(λ)​,b=δAr​,a=δ−b,
(11)
and solving
(1−δ)erf(Ωλ)Ωe−Ω2λ2​−(θ∞​+δ)K(λ)=π​CS​λb.
(12)
The solute jump gives ϕi​=1−b/(C+δ) exactly; replacing C by C+δ on the right of the last equation retains that jump's next-order factor, but does not make the linearized thermal profiles exact at finite C. The branch has 0≤δ<1, 0<ϕ≤1. All these profiles are leading asymptotic expressions, not exact solutions of the nonlinear finite-C heat equation.
For r→0 at fixed positive λ, the complementary error function asymptotic gives K(λ/r​)∼π​λ/r​, hence δ=O(r), b=O(r), a→0, and ϕi​→1. The result simplifies to
θm​=−1+erf(Ωλ)erf(Ωη)​,θl​=θ∞​(1−erfcλerfcη​),Cm​=C0​−ΔCθm​,ϕm​=C−θm​C​,Cl​=C0​,h(t)=2λκt​.​​
(13)
In particular, ϕ(0,t)=C0​/CB​=C/(C+1) and ϕ(h−,t)=1. There is no finite interfacial latent heat jump in this limit: the latent heat is released continuously throughout the mushy layer. Matching the two temperature gradients gives
erf(Ωλ)Ωe−Ω2λ2​=erfcλθ∞​e−λ2​,Ωeλ2erfcλ=θ∞​eΩ2λ2erf(Ωλ).​
(14)
The printed final equation is missing the square on Ω in the exponential. The corrected exponent follows directly from differentiating erf(Ωη) and is consistent with Ω2=1+S/C. The positive root is unique: the mush-side gradient decreases from infinity to zero with λ, whereas θ∞​K(λ) increases. The zero-superheat limit is singular and is not described by a finite positive λ in this two-region construction.

Choose the positive x direction along the background flow, so U>0; reverse x if necessary. Let z measure depth below the impermeable roof, and set Δρ=ρw​−ρc​>0. The deep ambient water has pw​=p0​(x)+ρw​gz. Continuity of pressure at z=h and hydrostatic pressure inside the buoyant CO2 give
pc​=p0​(x)+Δρgh+ρc​gz,uD​=−μk​∂x​pc​=U−Kb​hx​,Kb​=μkΔρg​.
(15)
Here k is permeability of a porous medium and μ is dynamic viscosity. Since h/H≪1, the ambient return-flow correction to the imposed pressure gradient is higher order. The small aspect ratio supplies the hydrostatic pressure approximation; capillary trapping, dissolution, compressibility and dispersion are omitted in this sharp-interface model.
The depth-integrated Darcy flux is q=Uh−Kb​hhx​. The actual stored fluid volume is ϕh per unit horizontal area, where ϕ is the aquifer porosity. Thus the porous gravity current with background flow satisfies
ϕht​+∂x​(Uh−Kb​hhx​)=Qδ0​(x),ht​+uhx​=a(hhx​)x​+Jδ0​(x),​
(16)
with u=U/ϕ, a=Kb​/ϕ and J=Q/ϕ. Both Q and the later V denote actual injected volume per unit transverse width. Away from injection, the equation is an advected porous medium equation. At each finite front impose h=0 and vanishing outgoing volume flux per unit width. At the injection point h is continuous and q(0+)−q(0−)=Q. For an initially empty aquifer, ϕ∫hdx=Qt. The advective speed is U/ϕ, not U.
For a constant-flux porous gravity current, write characteristic depth Hc​ and extent Lc​ temporarily. At early times volume conservation gives Hc​Lc​∼Jt; gravity spreading gives Lc2​/t∼aHc​. Hence
Lc​∼(aJ)1/3t2/3,Hc​∼(J2/a)1/3t1/3.​
(17)
Background advection becomes comparable when Lc​∼ut, so the transition scales are
tc​=u3aJ​=U3ϕKb​Q​,ℓc​=utc​=U2Kb​Q​,hc​=uJ​=UQ​.​
(18)
For t≪tc​, the leading similarity solution is symmetric:
h=(J2/a)1/3t1/3F(η),η=(aJ)1/3t2/3x​.
(19)
For 0<η<ηN​, its model ordinary differential equation and boundary conditions are
(FF′)′+32​ηF′−31​F=0,−F(0)F′(0+)=21​,F(ηN​)=0.​
(20)
Choose the nonnegative finite-front branch, reflect evenly in x, and require FF′→0 at the front. The factor 1/2 divides the injection equally between the two halves. Integration of the ordinary differential equation gives 2∫0ηN​​Fdη=1; the limiting front slope is F′(ηN−​)=−2ηN​/3. This boundary-value problem determines the initially symmetric shape without assuming an unjustified elementary formula. Independent numerical integration gives ηN​≃1.17619 in this normalization.
For t≫tc​, upstream storage approaches a finite steady value. With no upstream leakage, q=0 for x<0, giving hx​=U/Kb​ wherever h>0. Downstream the uniform interior carries q=Q, so
hint​=UQ​,x−​=−U2Kb​Q​,hup​(x)=Kb​U​(x−x−​)(x−​≤x≤0).​
(21)
The upstream volume flux vanishes even though local Darcy velocity contributions from background flow and buoyancy cancel. The upstream stored volume is ϕhint​∣x−​∣/2, a finite constant. The downstream plateau therefore extends a distance ut+O(ℓc​), and its front advances at u to leading order.
The diffusive nose of an advected porous gravity current has width
w(t)∼ahint​t​,xN​(t)=ut+O(ahint​t​)+O(ℓc​).​
(22)
In a frame translating at u, balance the time derivative against the nonlinear gravity diffusion. With η=(x−xc​(t))/ahint​t​ and xc​(t)=ut+O(ℓc​), the leading transition is h=hint​F(η), with
(FF′)′+2η​F′=0,F(−∞)=1,F(ηN​)=0.
(23)
Its finite front has F∼(ηN​/2)(ηN​−η) as η↑ηN​. Independent numerical integration of this boundary-value problem gives ηN​≃1.23849, so a more accurate leading nose position is xN​≃xc​+1.23849ahint​t​ and its local shape is F≃0.619247(1.23849−η). The entire transition has no elementary closed form. A useful explicit, approximate shape is the volume-preserving linear ramp
h≃hint​⎩⎨⎧​1,(1−η)/2,0,​η≤−1,−1<η<1,η≥1.​​
(24)
This gives xN​≃xc​+ahint​t​. Its ramp width is 2ahint​t​, and its edge obeys the exact kinematic boundary condition x˙N​=u−ahx​(xN−​,t) to the displayed order. It also has the same area as a sharp step at xc​. This is an approximate ramp closure, not an exact solution of the nonlinear nose ordinary differential equation. A constant phase shift xc​=ut−∣x−​∣/2 balances the finite upstream storage within this ramp approximation; the full solution's subleading matching may alter constant offsets.
For a rapid injection of total volume V, remove the source for t>0 and introduce ξ=x−ut. The advected constant-volume porous gravity current obeys ht​=a(hhξ​)ξ​ and ∫hdξ=M=V/ϕ. A similarity solution has depth proportional to t−1/3 and radius proportional to t1/3. Direct substitution gives the compactly supported Barenblatt solution
h(x,t)=6at[R(t)2−(x−ut)2]+​​,R(t)=(2ϕ9aVt​)1/3.​
(25)
Indeed ∫−RR​(R2−ξ2)/(6at)dξ=2R3/(9at)=V/ϕ. Its centre translates at the pore speed u, its two edges are ut±R(t), and its maximum depth decays as t−1/3. The expression is exact for a point release within this reduced model; finite-width initial data approach this similarity solution at late times. If U=0, the constant-rate current remains in the symmetric spreading regime and the constant-volume solution has a stationary centre.

Take z upward from the horizontal bed and measure x away from an ice divide. In the shallow-ice approximation, the hydrostatic pressure is p=pa​+ρg(h−z) and the horizontal balance for a Newtonian fluid is
μuzz​=px​=ρghx​.
(26)
The upper stress-free boundary condition gives uz​(h)=0. The resisting basal traction is −μβub​, so the fluid-side shear stress satisfies μuz​(0)=μβub​. This is a Navier slip boundary condition with slip length ℓs​=1/β. Integrating twice gives
u(z)=−μρg​hx​(βh​+hz−2z2​),ub​=−μβρg​hhx​.​
(27)
The first term is the uniform basal sliding contribution, while the quadratic term is internal viscous deformation. Both are positive where the thickness decreases downstream.
The shallow-ice flux with linear basal drag is
q=∫0h​udz=−μρg​(3h3​+βh2​)hx​,ht​=μρg​∂x​[(3h3​+βh2​)hx​].​
(28)
No ice accumulation or ice ablation appears. For a symmetric glacier, work on one half, 0≤x≤xN​(t), with q(0,t)=0, h(xN​,t)=0 and no outgoing volume flux. Its conserved half-volume per unit width is Vg​=∫0xN​​hdx; the full glacier has volume 2Vg​. This specifies the volume convention rather than silently supplying an unspecified value. For a general finite release on the whole line, the late similarity solution is centred on its conserved centre of mass and uses half its total volume for Vg​.
The internal-shear and linear basal drag law mobilities are equal at H∗​=3/β. Suitable vertical, horizontal and temporal scales are
H∗​=β3​,L∗​=H∗​Vg​​=3βVg​​,T∗​=ρgH∗3​3μL∗2​​=81ρgμβ5Vg2​​.​
(29)
Write h=H∗​H, x=L∗​X, t=T∗​τ. Then
Hτ​=∂X​[(H3+H2)HX​],∫0XN​​HdX=1.​
(30)
The initial typical thickness is H≃10, the initial extent is of order L∗​/10, and its shear-controlled spreading time is of order T∗​/105. The shallow-ice approximation also requires small aspect ratio: in particular the initial thickness and extent must satisfy approximately 100H∗​/L∗​≪1. The equations model the broad shallow bulk; the very tip can require physics beyond that approximation.
The two limiting equations are porous medium equations of the form Hτ​=(HnHX​)X​, with n=3 for a thick shear-dominated glacier and n=2 for a thin sliding-dominated glacier. Volume conservation and balance of the time derivative give exponent α=1/(n+2). Set H=τ−αf(η), η=Xτ−α. One integration, using zero volume flux at the ice divide, gives fnf′=−αηf, hence
f(η)=[cn​(ηn2​−η2)]1/n,cn​=2(n+2)n​.
(31)
The planar volume-conserving nonlinear-diffusion similarity is therefore
Hn​(X,τ)=[τcn​​(ηn2​τ2/(n+2)−X2)]+1/n​,XN​=ηn​τ1/(n+2).​
(32)
The Beta function fixes its constants:
In​=∫01​(1−s2)1/nds=21​B(21​,1+n1​),ηn​=(cn−1/n​/In​)n/(n+2).
(33)
Consequently the explicit early shear and late sliding limits are
Hearly​Hlate​​=[10τ3​(η32​τ2/5−X2)]+1/3​,=2τ​1​[η22​τ1/2−X2]+1/2​,​η3​η2​​≃1.41124,=8/π​.​​
(34)
Their centre depths are A3​τ−1/5 with A3​≃0.842252, and A2​τ−1/4 with A2​=2/π​. Thus the early extent grows as t1/5 and the late extent as t1/4. These are asymptotic regime profiles; they solve the respective limiting equations, not the full sum of both mobilities. For arbitrary finite-width initial data they are appropriate after the corresponding spatial relaxation, with a virtual time origin depending on that data.
The shear-to-slip transition in a shallow ice current occurs at thickness of order H∗​. Using equality at the centre of the early similarity solution gives
τtr​≃A35​=10I32​3​≃0.42385.​
(35)
If the initial profile is already approximated by that early similarity solution and its centre depth is 10, the virtual age is τ0​=A35​/105≃4.2385×10−6, and the elapsed estimate is τtr​−τ0​. Other reasonable definitions of typical thickness change the order-one coefficient; the initial thickness alone does not uniquely determine an exact transition time or an exact initial profile. The robust nondimensional estimate is a transition time of order one in the T∗​ scale.
Finally, let a local nose translate steadily at positive speed v and write s=xN​−x≥0. This is a local traveling nose of a glacier with linear basal drag; the speed of the globally spreading glacier changes slowly with time. The local mass conservation equation integrates to q=vh, since both q and h vanish at the front. Therefore
v=μρg​(3h2​+βh​)dsdh​,s=μvρg​(9h3​+2βh2​).​
(36)
The right side is strictly increasing, giving a unique positive thickness at every s>0. With S=XN​−X and c=dXN​/dτ, the same relation reads cS=H3/3+H2/2. Its limiting profiles are
h∼(ρg2μβvs​)1/2(h≪3/β),h∼(ρg9μvs​)1/3(h≫3/β).​
(37)
The square-root tip reflects basal sliding; the cube-root thicker region reflects internal viscous deformation. Even during the early thick regime, the very nose is thin and has the square-root inner form. At late times that sliding balance governs nearly the whole profile. The dimensionless crossover thickness is H=1 and its distance is S=5/(6c); the early thick cube-root outer profile matches this smaller sliding region. The diverging geometric slope at the idealized tip also marks the local limit of the long-wave shallow-ice approximation.
