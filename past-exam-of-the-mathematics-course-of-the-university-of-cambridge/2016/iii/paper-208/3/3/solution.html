The edge cases allowed by S∈N∗ matter: Y is stationary when S=1, because the mean is then constant, and Z is stationary for S=1 or S=2. In the latter case multiplying iid centered Gaussian white noise by (−1)t leaves its iid distribution unchanged. For every S≥3, c02​=1 but c12​<1, so Z is nonstationary. For S>1, Y is nonstationary because ct​ is not constant.
Use the seasonal difference operator ΔS​=1−BS. Since ct​=ct−S​,
ΔS​Yt​=εt​−εt−S​.​
(36)
This is a stationary moving-average model of iid Gaussian white noise. Its covariance is 2σ2 at lag 0, −σ2 at lags ±S, and zero otherwise.
For the variance-modulated process the same operation gives
ΔS​Zt​=ct​(εt​−εt−S​).​
(37)
Its variance is 2σ2ct2​ and its covariance at lag S is −σ2ct2​. Therefore it remains nonstationary for S≥3, and the operation doubles the marginal variance at nonzero seasons. Seasonal differencing does not remove periodic variance: it removes a periodic mean, but the periodic variance generally remains. Practical alternatives are a periodic model or seasonal variance standardization; at seasons with ct​=0 the observations are deterministically zero, so division by ct​ is not possible there. For the already stationary special cases S=1,2, filtering preserves stationarity.
A periodically correlated process with period S has its mean and two-time covariance unchanged when both times are shifted by S. For Z, the mean is zero and
Cov(Zt+S​,Zs+S​)=σ2ct+S​cs+S​1{t=s}​=Cov(Zt​,Zs​).
(38)
Hence Z is a periodically correlated process. Its covariance period can be smaller than S: when S is even, ct+S/22​=ct2​, so S/2 is already a covariance period.
