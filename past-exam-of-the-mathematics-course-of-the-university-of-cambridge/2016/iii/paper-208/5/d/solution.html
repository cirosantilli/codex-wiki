The printed identity needs an expectation on its right-hand side. For fixed r≥1, condition on X2​,…,Xr​ and use independence of X1​:
P(Sr​≤x)=E[F(x−i=2∑r​Xi​)].​
(63)
The expression inside this expectation is generally random and cannot equal the unconditional probability by itself.
For each independent Monte Carlo method replicate, draw Rj​ from its given distribution. If Rj​=r≥1, draw r−1 independent F-variables, form Wj​=∑i=2r​Xj,i​ and set Hj​=F(x−Wj​). For r=1 the empty sum is 0. If R may equal zero, use Hj​=1{x≥0}​ when Rj​=0, since S0​=0. The law of total expectation gives
lN​=N−1j=1∑N​Hj​,ElN​=P(SR​≤x).​
(64)
Since 0≤Hj​≤1, the estimator has statistical consistency by the strong law of large numbers. It is the conditional Monte Carlo estimator obtained by Rao-Blackwellization of the direct indicator 1{SR​≤x}​, and the law of total variance gives Var(Hj​)≤l(1−l).
This version assumes that the cumulative distribution function F can be evaluated. An easy sampler alone does not automatically supply an easy cumulative distribution function evaluation. If only sampling is available, use the direct indicator estimator, or replace each conditional cumulative distribution function by the average of several independent indicators 1{Xj,1,k​≤x−Wj​}​; the nested version remains an unbiased estimator. With L such inner draws its variance per outer replicate is Var(Hj​)+L−1E[Hj​(1−Hj​)].
