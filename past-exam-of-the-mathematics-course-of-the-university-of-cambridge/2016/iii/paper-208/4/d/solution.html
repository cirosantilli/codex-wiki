Use v=σ2 as the variance parameter, with prior density relative to dμdv. Let xˉ=n−1∑xi​ and S=∑(xi​−xˉ)2. Multiplying the prior by the likelihood function gives the unnormalized Bayesian posterior
π(μ,v∣x)∝v−n/2−1exp[−2vS+n(μ−xˉ)2​],v>0.​
(52)
The printed request for an “improper posterior” should not be read as a claim that this posterior is always improper. Although the prior is improper, integrating over μ gives a marginal kernel v−(n+1)/2e−S/(2v), which is integrable exactly when n>1 and S>0. Under these usual nondegenerate-data conditions it is a proper posterior. If those conditions fail, the displayed kernel is improper and there is no posterior probability distribution for a sampler to target.
Completing the square and using the inverse-gamma distribution give the full conditionals
μ∣v,x∼N(xˉ,v/n),v∣μ,x∼IG(2n​,2S+n(μ−xˉ)2​).​
(53)
The inverse-gamma distribution convention is density proportional to v−α−1e−β/v. Equivalently the precision satisfies
τ=1/v∣μ,x∼Gamma(2n​,2S+n(μ−xˉ)2​),​
(54)
using shape and rate, as in the question.
A Gibbs sampler starts from v(0)>0 and alternates
μ(k+1)∼N(xˉ,v(k)/n),τ(k+1)∼Gamma(n/2,21​∑i​(xi​−μ(k+1))2),v(k+1)=1/τ(k+1).
(55)
This is the normal mean-variance posterior with a log-uniform variance prior. For a marginal check, v∣x∼IG((n−1)/2,S/2): integrating out μ reduces the shape by 1/2. This marginal shape is different from the full-conditional shape n/2.
