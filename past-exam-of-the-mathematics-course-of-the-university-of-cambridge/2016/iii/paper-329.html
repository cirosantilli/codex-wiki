Let e=(∇u+∇uT)/2 be the rate-of-strain tensor and σ=−pI+2μe the Newtonian fluid stress tensor. The viscous dissipation is D[u]=2μ∫V​e:edV. The Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow compares the Stokes flow with every sufficiently regular incompressible flow in the same domain having the same prescribed boundary velocity, with no body force or with a conservative body force absorbed into the fluid pressure. A trial field need not satisfy the Stokes flow equations. Write it as u+w, with ∇⋅w=0 and w=0 on the boundary. Integration by parts, the symmetry of the Newtonian fluid stress tensor, and ∇⋅σ=0 give
2μ∫V​e(u):e(w)dV=∫V​σ:∇wdV=∫∂V​w⋅σndS−∫V​w⋅∇⋅σdV=0.
(1)
Consequently the Stokes flow minimizes dissipation:
D[u+w]−D[u]=2μ∫V​e(w):e(w)dV≥0.​
(2)
Equality requires a rigid body motion of w, which the prescribed boundary eliminates. Extending the flow inside the inserted particle as its rigid body velocity produces an admissible comparison field with zero internal rate-of-strain tensor; hence the extra dissipation due to a rigid inclusion is nonnegative.
Here and below n on A points into the particle, so the traction σn represents force exerted by the particle on the fluid. Apply the Lorentz reciprocal theorem for Stokes flow in the fluid region to u and the restriction of u0​. The equal outer boundary velocities yield
∫∂V​V0​⋅(σ−σ0​)ndS=∫A​(u⋅σ0​n−u0​⋅σn)dS.
(3)
The boundary-work formula for viscous dissipation therefore gives D′=∫A​[(u−u0​)⋅σn+u⋅σ0​n]dS. The last integral is zero: σ0​ is symmetric and divergence-free throughout the particle's original volume, so its total force and torque there vanish, and u on A is a rigid body velocity. This also allows the sign of this zero-work term to be reversed, giving exactly
D′=∫A​(u⋅σ−u0​⋅σ−u⋅σ0​)⋅ndS.​
(4)
With the particle's centre as origin, define F=∫A​σndS, G=∫A​x×(σn)dS and the particle stresslet tensor S=−21​∫A​[x(σn)+(σn)x]dS. Contracting the linear background velocity with the traction gives
D′=F⋅(U−U0​)+G⋅(Ω−Ω0​)+S:E0​.​
(5)
The background rate-of-strain tensor E0​ is symmetric and trace-free; the trace of this unprojected particle stresslet tensor does not affect the contraction.
For the straight-rod resistance in a linear flow, write ΔU=U−U0​, ΔΩ=Ω−Ω0​, M=I−pp/2, and X=sp, −L≤s≤L. The slender-body force density becomes f=CM[ΔU+s(ΔΩ×p−E0​p)]. The integrals of s and s2 are 0 and 2L3/3, respectively, so
F=2CLMΔU,G=32CL3​[(I−pp)ΔΩ−p×E0​p].​
(6)
For a force-free, torque-free rod, ΔU=0 and the perpendicular component of ΔΩ is p×E0​p. Axial spin is not determined by this leading, zero-thickness slender-body theory; it has no effect on p. Put ap​=p⋅E0​p. The vector identity (p×E0​p)×p=E0​p−ap​p gives
f=−2C​sap​p,p˙​=Ω0​×p+E0​p−ap​p.​
(7)
The force-free straight-rod orientation equation combines the background rotation with the part of strain that turns the rod; removing ap​p preserves its unit length. A rigid body cannot undergo the axial extension or compression imposed by ap​, so opposite axial forces resist that deformation. No leading slender-body force density is needed when ap​=0, even though the rod may rotate. Its particle stresslet tensor and viscous dissipation are
S=3CL3​ap​pp,D′=3CL3​ap2​.​
(8)
In the simple shear flow, Ω0​=−γez​/2, (E0​)xy​=(E0​)yx​=γ/2 and ap​=γcosθsinθ. Thus the rod excess dissipation in shear is
D′(θ)=12CL3γ2​sin2(2θ),θ˙=−γsin2θ.​
(9)
The maxima at θ=π/4,3π/4 correspond to strongest axial extension and compression. The zeros at 0,π/2,π correspond to zero axial strain: a rod aligned with the velocity or its gradient needs no leading force at that instant. For γ>0, cotθ=γt with the continuous branch θ:π→0 and θ(0)=π/2. Reversing the shear flow reverses the traversal. Substitution gives
D′(t)=3CL3γ2​[1+(γt)2]2(γt)2​,∫−∞∞​D′(t)dt=6πCL3∣γ∣​.​
(10)
The t−2 tails make the total finite; the instantaneous zero at t=0 lies between two maxima.
For a dilute rod suspension, the added bulk stress is the number density times the orientation average of the particle stresslet tensor. In this ideal infinitely slender, non-interacting model, rods approach alignment with the shear flow and their excess viscous dissipation decays. A real finite-aspect-ratio rod continues to tumble; rotational diffusion and interactions also maintain a spread of orientations. One therefore expects increased effective viscosity, with alignment offering a mechanism for reduced excess viscosity at strong shear flow. This is a qualitative expectation, not a claim that a finite rod suspension has zero steady excess stress.

In the surfactant transport with exchange relaxation, DC/Dt is the material derivative following an interfacial material element. The term −C∇s​⋅us​ accounts for dilution by tangential stretching, while −C(u⋅n)∇s​⋅n accounts for changing area through normal motion and curvature. The surface diffusion term redistributes surfactant down concentration gradients. The exchange term relaxes its concentration towards C0​, through adsorption or desorption from a surrounding reservoir, on time scale k−1.
In the bubble frame the spherical interface is stationary and impermeable. A steady material derivative is us​⋅∇s​C′. For a linear response in the rising speed this and C′∇s​⋅us​ are second order, whereas stretching of C0​ is first order. Thus
(Ds​Δs​−k)C′=C0​∇s​⋅us​.​
(11)
Rotational symmetry, linear response to U, and zero normal velocity allow only us​=APU, where P=I−nn is the surface tangent projector. This is the dipolar, axisymmetric sector; axisymmetry alone, without the linear-response assumption, would allow other angular dependence.
On a sphere n=x/r and ∂j​ni​=(δij​−ni​nj​)/r. Applying the surface tangent projector to the derivative gives the sphere surface derivative identities
∇s​n=P/a,∇s​⋅n=2/a,Δs​n=−2n/a2,∇s​⋅(PU)=−2(U⋅n)/a.​
(12)
For example, taking a second surface divergence of the first identity yields the third. Equivalently, the components of n are degree-one spherical harmonics. Hence a dipolar surfactant distribution solves the linear equation:
C′=BU⋅n,B=ka2+2Ds​2AC0​a​.​
(13)
The required smallness condition is 2∣A∣Ua/(ka2+2Ds​)≪1. Surface diffusion or exchange must smooth the concentration faster than it is redistributed by the actual interfacial velocity. We require k≥0, Ds​≥0 and ka2+2Ds​>0; with neither process there is no such steady linear balance at nonzero A. A small capillary number μU/γ0​ also justifies the spherical approximation.
Take the normal from the inner bubble to the outer liquid, define [σ]−+​=σ+​−σ−​, and set κ=∇s​⋅n. The interfacial stress balance with variable surface tension is
[σ]−+​n=γκn−∇s​γ.​
(14)
Because ∇s​C′=BPU/a and γ=γ0​−γ1​C′, its tangential part becomes
P[σ]−+​n=a6μAλ​PU,λ=3μ(ka2+2Ds​)γ1​C0​a​.​
(15)
This is a Marangoni stress: surface-tension gradients oppose the clean-bubble circulation.
Use the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation, u=∇(x⋅Φ+χ)−2Φ and p=2μ∇⋅Φ. The constant vector potential gives u→−U. In the decaying, linear axisymmetric sector, a vector monopole and scalar dipole provide the two constants needed at the sphere; other equivalent potential choices amount to a representation freedom. The PDF potential contains the factor a omitted in the TeX aid. With that factor restored, the exterior velocity is
u=−U−αa(rU​+r3(U⋅x)x​)+βa3(−r3U​+r53(U⋅x)x​),p=−r32μαaU⋅x​.
(16)
At the interface, no penetration gives β−α=1/2, and the tangential velocity gives A=−(1+α+β). Using the traction coefficient printed in equation (3), the tangential stress condition gives 2β=Aλ. The requested algebraic answers are therefore
Aprinted​=−2(1+λ)1​,αprinted​=−4(1+λ)2+3λ​,βprinted​=−4(1+λ)λ​.​
(17)
There is a factor-of-two error in the PDF traction. Direct differentiation of the preceding velocity with the Newtonian fluid stress tensor gives
σn=a6μ​[βU+(α−3β)(U⋅n)n],
(18)
rather than its printed coefficient 12μ/a. In particular the tangential traction is 6μβPU/a. Keeping the same definition of λ, the physically consistent translating surfactant-coated bubble instead has β=Aλ and
A=−2(1+2λ)1​,α=−2(1+2λ)1+3λ​,β=−2(1+2λ)λ​.​
(19)
The two sets must not be silently conflated. As a useful check, the no-slip limit has the usual total Stokes flow drag 6πμaU with the corrected traction; the printed traction would double it.
Both versions give A=−1/2, α=−1/2, β=0 when λ→0: the interface is tangentially stress-free and circulates as a clean inviscid bubble. For λ→∞, A→0, α→−3/4, β→−1/4: the interface is immobile in the bubble frame and behaves as a no-slip sphere. The Marangoni stress remains finite because λA has a nonzero limit. The concentration smallness condition still has to hold; large λ is not by itself a complete linearization criterion.
The normal component is balanced by the inner gas pressure and the normal capillary stress γκ, including the variation of γ, together with the hydrostatic pressure difference responsible for buoyancy. A spatially uniform inner pressure alone cannot balance the dipolar traction. To make the normal stress balance on a translating bubble explicit, let Δρb​ be the outer-minus-inner mass density. After removing the equilibrium Laplace pressure, the corrected outer normal traction has dynamic part −3μ(1+λ)(U⋅n)/[a(1+2λ)]. Its degree-one balance is
Δρb​gacosϑ−a(1+2λ)3μ(1+λ)​Ucosϑ=−a2γ1​B​Ucosϑ.
(20)
Thus, if a terminal speed is desired, U=Δρb​ga2(1+2λ)/[3μ(1+3λ)], interpolating between the clean-bubble and rigid-sphere speeds. A degree-one shape displacement is merely a translation of the sphere, so the balance determines its rise speed, rather than a dipolar shape distortion.

For a long finite blade with negligible under-blade flux, fluid accumulates upstream and escapes sideways around the ends. The finite-blade side-drainage pile has h≫1 in a broad upstream region, while its near wake is strongly depleted; far from the blade the incoming film still has thickness one. The schematic in the preceding figure shows the pile, incoming flow, and escape around the ends.
Away from the nose and end regions, the streamwise flux nearly cancels: −h−h3hx​/3≃0. This is the deep part of the infinite-blade gravity pile, so h3=9[xN​(y)−x] is the appropriate local profile. Write d1​=ρ0​−ρ1​>0, d2​=ρ2​−ρ0​>0, and choose the common undisturbed interface pressure P. The hydrostatic pressure is p1​=P−ρ1​gz in the upper fluid and p2​=P−ρ2​gz in the lower fluid. Continuity of hydrostatic pressure across both current interfaces gives
p0​=P−ρ0​gz+d1​gh1​=P−ρ0​gz−d2​gh2​,d1​h1​=−d2​h2​.​
(31)
Thus h1​=d2​h/(d1​+d2​) and h2​=−d1​h/(d1​+d2​). This isostatic depth partition of an interfacial current expresses local vertical buoyancy balance: a displaced column with an imbalance would move vertically until the partition was restored. With the hydrostatic pressure P−ρ0​gz subtracted, the pressure within the current is Δρgh, where
Δρ=ρ2​−ρ1​(ρ0​−ρ1​)(ρ2​−ρ0​)​,f=−Δρghr​er​.​
(32)
The equivalent body force is outward for a profile decreasing with r. Its magnitude is Δρg∣hr​∣; the derivative without an absolute value is a signed quantity.
For an ambient-controlled interfacial plug current, integrating the body force through depth gives radial force per area O(ΔρgH2/R). The ambient Stokes flow varies over distance R, exerting traction O(μu/R), so u∼ΔρgH2/μ. The current's internal shear supports the ambient traction with a velocity difference Δu∼(μu/R)H/(λμ); Δu/u∼H/(λR)≪1 requires λ≫H/R. The force per area from its in-plane viscous extension is O(λμuH/R2), negligible against ambient traction when λ≪R/H. Together these give the viscosity window for an interfacial plug current
H/R≪λ≪R/H.​
(33)
Fixed volume gives V∼HR2, and the spreading velocity has the scale R˙. Thus R˙∼ΔρgV2/(μR4) and
R∼At1/5,A∼(ΔρgV2/μ)1/5.​
(34)

Regularity and no source at the centre remove the integration constant. Therefore the radial plug velocity is linear:
U(η)=η/5,u=r/(5t).​

For the disc-traction analogy for an interfacial current, subtract the imposed straining flow from the stationary-disc solution. The perturbation has velocity −Erer​ on the disc and zero far-field velocity. Reverse its sign: the resulting Stokes flow is driven by a radially expanding disc with velocity Erer​ in otherwise quiescent fluid. The background straining flow contributes no radial shear traction on z=0, so the given disc traction changes sign. This step uses Linearity of Stokes flow, Uniqueness of Stokes flow, and the no-slip boundary condition. The thin plug current has the same leading boundary velocity and hence the same ambient resistance. Each face contributes −8μEr/[πR2−r2​]; both faces must be counted.
