Let w=K∗Kv∈∂J(u†) from the normal-operator source condition, and set e=u−u†. Since u† is a least-squares solution, the normal equation for a linear inverse problem gives K∗(Ku†−f)=0. Expand the objective difference and use the Bregman distance definition:
Fα​(u)−Fα​(u†)​=21​∥Ke∥2+α[J(u)−J(u†)]=21​∥Ke∥2+α⟨Kv,Ke⟩+αDJw​(u,u†)=21​∥K(e+αv)∥2+αDJw​(u,u†)−2α2​∥Kv∥2.​

The cross term with the residual of the least-squares solution vanishes by the normal equation; exact data are not required. Compare the minimizing uα​ with z=u†−αv. At z, the completed square is zero. Dividing the resulting inequality by α proves the stronger shifted-comparator Bregman bound
DJw​(uα​,u†)+2α1​∥K(uα​−u†+αv)∥2≤DJw​(u†−αv,u†),w=K∗Kv.​
(22)
Dropping the nonnegative squared term gives the requested estimate. If the comparator lies outside the effective domain of J, its distance is infinite and the inequality is valid but gives no finite error bound.
