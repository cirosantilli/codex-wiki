The repeated-root unstable heat boundary mode gives a useful sign check on the dynamic boundary condition for the heat equation. If a>0, (p​−a)2 has a genuine double pole at p=a2 on the physical branch, and the exact homogeneous mode e−ax+a2t satisfies both the heat equation and the printed boundary condition. Generic data can also excite a tea2t contribution. A contour deformation must retain this repeated-pole contribution. If a<0, the putative root p​=a is outside the chosen branch and is not a physical pole. At a=0, J0​=erfc(x/(2t​)), as expected when the boundary trace satisfies b′(t)=f(t).
