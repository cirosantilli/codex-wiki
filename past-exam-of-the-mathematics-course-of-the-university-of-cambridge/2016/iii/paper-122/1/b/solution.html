Define the length of a formal tensor expression recursively by
∣I∣=0,∣x∣=1,∣u⊗v∣=∣u∣+∣v∣.
(2)
The canonical strict monoidal functor Q:Fbr→B sends u to ∣u∣, sends every associator and unitor to an identity braid, and sends cu,v​ to the block braiding c∣u∣,∣v∣​. The pentagon and triangle become identity equations; the braiding axioms become the corresponding block-braid equations. Thus the assignment respects the defining relations, and
Q(u⊗v)=Qu+Qv,QI=0
(3)
on the nose. It is a braided monoidal functor with identity comparison maps.
To prove that Q is an equivalence of categories, choose a standard parenthesization x[n] of n copies of x, with x[0]=I. On x[n], define the image of σi​ by canonically exposing the ith and (i+1)st factors, applying cx,x​ there, and restoring the chosen parentheses. The monoidal coherence theorem makes this independent of the structural rebracketing. Crossings on disjoint pairs commute by the tensor interchange law. The adjacent braid group relations follows from the hexagon laws and naturality of the braiding, as in the Yang–Baxter calculation below. Hence these assignments give group homomorphisms Bn​→Aut(x[n]) and a functor S:B→Fbr.
Equip S with the canonical rebracketing maps x[m]⊗x[n]→x[m+n]. Their monoidal functor axioms follow from the monoidal coherence theorem; the block-braiding compatibility follows by iterating the two hexagon laws. Therefore S is a strong monoidal functor compatible with the braiding.
We have QS=1B​, including its comparison maps. For every formal expression u, there is a canonical structural isomorphism
θu​:x[∣u∣]⟶u
(4)
obtained by rebracketing and inserting the units appearing in u. These maps form a natural isomorphism SQ⇒1Fbr​. To check naturality, it suffices to check the generating morphisms: for associators and unitors it is exactly monoidal coherence; for cu,v​ it follows from the hexagon expansion into the elementary crossings defining S. Composition and tensor product then preserve the equation. The same structural coherence shows that θ is monoidal.
Thus Q has a specified quasi-inverse and is the required equivalence:
Q:Fbr≃Bis braided and strict monoidal.​
(5)
Only ordinary monoidal coherence, together with the defining braiding axioms, has been used; a separate braided coherence theorem is unnecessary.
