First address the unnumbered preliminaries. The Yoneda lemma gives a natural transformation bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A),τ⟼τA​(1A​).​
(5)
The element x∈F(A) corresponds to τB​(f)=F(f)(x) for f:A→B. These bijections are natural in A and F; the contravariant form replaces C(A,−) by C(−,A).
Let E=(1↓F) be the category of elements. Its objects are (A,x) with x∈F(A); an arrow u:(A,x)→(B,y) is u:A→B with F(u)(x)=y. Since C is a small category, E is small. Define a diagram in a category D:Eop→[C,Set] by
D(A,x)=C(A,−).
(6)
For the opposite of u, the map C(B,−)→C(A,−) is precomposition with u. The Yoneda lemma gives a cocone under a diagram D→F whose (A,x) component sends f:A→B to F(f)(x).
For any G, a competing cocone under a diagram amounts, by the Yoneda lemma, to elements zA,x​∈G(A) satisfying
G(u)(zA,x​)=zB,F(u)(x)​.
(7)
This is exactly the naturality condition for αA​:F(A)→G(A) defined by αA​(x)=zA,x​. It gives a unique natural transformation α:F→G factoring the cocone. Thus the universal property of a colimit proves the canonical colimit presentation of a covariant set-valued functor:
F≅(A,x)∈Eopcolim​C(A,−).​
(8)
The opposite on E is essential for the variance of the representable functors.
We now prove the four conditions equivalent by the cycle (i)⇒(ii)⇒(iii)⇒(iv)⇒(i). Suppose F is a finite-limit-preserving set-valued functor. For a finite diagram in a category in the comma category (S↓F), write its objects as (Ai​,si​:S→F(Ai​)). Take L=limi​Ai​ in C. Since F preserves this finite limit, the compatible maps si​ determine a unique s:S→F(L) whose composites with F(L)→F(Ai​) are si​. The pair (L,s) has the required categorical limit property in (S↓F), by the same universal properties. For the empty diagram, F(1) is a singleton, so there is exactly one map S→F(1); this provides the terminal object. Hence (i)⇒(ii) for every set S.
