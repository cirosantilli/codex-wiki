Use the nonnegative sign convention Δ=−divgrad for the Laplace-Beltrami operator; the ambient Laplacian has the same sign convention. Changing both signs changes the signs of the eigenvalues below. Let e1​,…,en​ be an orthonormal basis of Tp​M, and let γi​ be the unit-speed geodesic with γi​(0)=p and γ˙​i​(0)=ei​. The geodesic Hessian formula gives
Δf(p)=−i=1∑n​dt2d2​f(γi​(t))​t=0​.​
(1)
Indeed, the second derivative along a geodesic is Hessf(ei​,ei​), because its covariant acceleration is zero. Taking the trace of the Hessian matrix in this orthonormal basis gives the displayed Laplace-Beltrami operator.
For the sphere, put x=rω, where ω∈Sd, and write F(r,ω) for a smooth ambient function. The Euclidean metric becomes dr2+r2gSd​ and its volume form is rddrdω. The coordinate formula for the Laplace-Beltrami operator therefore gives the polar-coordinate Laplacian identity
ΔF=−∂r2​F−rd​∂r​F+r21​ΔSd​F.
(2)
For f(ω)=F(1,ω), restriction to r=1 yields
ΔSd​f=(ΔF+∂r2​F+d∂r​F)​r=1​.​
(3)
This relation also has a direct geodesic proof. At a point p∈Sd, complete an orthonormal basis of the tangent space by the radial vector p. Each great-circle geodesic γi​(t)=pcost+ei​sint satisfies γ¨​i​(0)=−p. The chain rule gives (F∘γi​)′′(0)=D2F(ei​,ei​)−∂r​F. Summing and comparing with the ambient trace gives precisely the radial correction above.
Let Pℓ​ be the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree ℓ on Rd+1, and let Hℓ​ be its subspace of harmonic polynomials. For H∈Hℓ​, homogeneity gives H(rω)=rℓH(ω). Substituting in the polar-coordinate Laplacian identity proves that its restriction h is a spherical harmonic with
ΔSd​h=ℓ(ℓ+d−1)h.
(4)
Restriction is injective on Hℓ​: if h=0, homogeneity makes H zero away from the origin, hence everywhere.
To count and exhaust these eigenfunctions, use the harmonic decomposition of homogeneous polynomials
Pℓ​=Hℓ​⊕r2Pℓ−2​,Pj​=0 for j<0.
(5)
Here is an algebraic proof rather than an assumption about the spectrum. On polynomials put the Fischer inner product ⟨xα,xβ⟩F​=α!δαβ​. Multiplication by xi​ is adjoint to ∂i​, so multiplication by r2 is adjoint to L=∑i​∂i2​. In finite-dimensional inner product spaces, the orthogonal complement of the image of multiplication by r2 is kerL=Hℓ​. This proves the decomposition. Multiplication by r2 is injective, so
dimHℓ​=(dℓ+d​)−(dℓ+d−2​),
(6)
where the second term is zero for ℓ<2.
Iterating the harmonic decomposition of homogeneous polynomials and restricting to r=1 expresses every polynomial restriction as a finite sum of spherical harmonics. Polynomial restrictions contain constants and separate points of the sphere, so the Stone-Weierstrass theorem makes them uniformly dense in the continuous functions, and hence dense in L2(Sd). The Laplace-Beltrami operator is self-adjoint, and eigenfunctions with different eigenvalues are orthogonal. If a smooth eigenfunction had an eigenvalue not in the list, it would be orthogonal to a dense subspace and would vanish. If it had a listed eigenvalue, subtracting its orthogonal projection onto the corresponding finite-dimensional Hℓ​∣Sd​ gives the same contradiction. Thus, for d≥1, the spectrum of the Laplacian on a sphere is
λℓ​=ℓ(ℓ+d−1),mℓ​=(dℓ+d​)−(dℓ+d−2​),ℓ=0,1,2,….​
(7)
In particular, the zero eigenvalue has multiplicity one; on S1 each positive eigenvalue ℓ2 has multiplicity two; on S2 the multiplicity is 2ℓ+1.
