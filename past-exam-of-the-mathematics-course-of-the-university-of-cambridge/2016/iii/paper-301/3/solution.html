Use ηab​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and ℏ=1. The free real scalar field has action
S=21​∫d4x[∂a​ϕ∂aϕ−m2ϕ2].
(21)
The canonical momentum is π=∂L/∂ϕ˙​=ϕ˙​, and the Hamiltonian operator is obtained by the Legendre transform of the density:
H=21​∫d3x[π2+(∇ϕ)2+m2ϕ2].
(22)
Canonical quantization promotes ϕ and π to Hermitian operator-valued fields and imposes the equal-time canonical commutation relations
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδ3(x−y),[ϕ(t,x),ϕ(t,y)]=[π(t,x),π(t,y)]=0.
(23)
Their Heisenberg equation of motion gives ϕ˙​=π, π˙=∇2ϕ−m2ϕ, hence the Klein-Gordon equation (□+m2)ϕ=0.
Let Ep​=p2+m2​ and p⋅x=Ep​t−p⋅x. The Hermitian field mode expansion is
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​[a(p)e−ip⋅x+a†(p)eip⋅x].
(24)
The scalar field oscillator inversion extracts
a(p)=eiEp​t∫d3xe−ip⋅x[2Ep​​​ϕ(t,x)+2Ep​​i​π(t,x)],
(25)
and its adjoint obtained by Hermitian conjugation extracts a†. Substitute these expressions into the equal-time canonical commutation relation. The two mixed field-momentum terms give
[a(p),a†(q)]​=ei(Ep​−Eq​)t21​(Eq​Ep​​​+Ep​Eq​​​)(2π)3δ3(p−q)=(2π)3δ3(p−q)​.​
(26)
For [a(p),a(q)], the corresponding coefficient is a difference of those square roots and multiplies δ3(p+q); it vanishes because Ep​=E−p​. Taking the adjoint gives [a†,a†]=0. Thus these are bosonic annihilation operators and creation operators. The vacuum satisfies a(p)∣0⟩=0, and normal ordering gives :H:=∫d3pEp​a†(p)a(p)/(2π)3, after removing the constant zero-point energy. A one-particle excitation has energy Ep​ and spin zero.
The Feynman propagator GF​(x−y)=⟨0∣Tϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩ is the vacuum expectation value of the time-ordered product of two field insertions. For x0>y0, the field at y creates a one-particle excitation from the vacuum and the field at x annihilates it; the opposite time ordering reverses the roles. It is a propagation amplitude and correlation function of vacuum fluctuations, rather than a transition probability. From the oscillator expansion, only the a-a† contraction survives, so with t=x0−y0 and r=x−y,
GF​(t,r)​=∫(2π)3,2Ep​d3p​[θ(t)e−iEp​t+ip⋅r+θ(−t)eiEp​t−ip⋅r]=∫(2π)3,2Ep​d3p​eip⋅re−iEp​∣t∣.​
(27)
Here θ is the Heaviside step function, and changing p to −p in the second term gives the last line.
For the Fourier transform convention GF​(ω,p)=∫dtd3reiωt−ip⋅rGF​(t,r), insert a positive damping factor e−ϵ∣t∣ and integrate the positive and negative half-lines separately:
GF​(ω,p)=limϵ↓0​2Ep​i​[ω−Ep​+iϵ1​−ω+Ep​−iϵ1​]=ω2−p2−m2+i0i​​.
(28)
The last equality is a distribution identity; the infinitesimals in the partial fractions need not have the same finite magnitude as the infinitesimal in the combined denominator. The Feynman i-epsilon prescription means that the positive-frequency pole is just below the real energy axis and the negative-frequency pole is just above it. Closing the contour integral below for t>0, and above for t<0, reproduces the oscillator result by the residue theorem. This prescription fixes which homogeneous solutions are added to the Green function. As an independent normalization check, e−iE∣t∣/(2E) has derivative jump −i at t=0, so
(□+m2)GF​(x)=−iδ4(x).
(29)
It is the expectation-value convention for GF​, including the numerator i, that determines this source normalization.
The vacuum is a centered free Gaussian state. The Wick theorem expresses the four-field time-ordered product as the sum of all pair Wick contractions plus terms containing a normal-ordered product. The latter terms have zero vacuum expectation value. There are exactly three complete pairings, with no fermionic signs for this bosonic field. Thus the free scalar four-point function is
⟨0∣Tϕ(x1​)ϕ(x2​)ϕ(x3​)ϕ(x4​)∣0⟩=​GF​(x1​−x2​)GF​(x3​−x4​)+GF​(x1​−x3​)GF​(x2​−x4​)+GF​(x1​−x4​)GF​(x2​−x3​).​​
(30)
These formulas describe canonical quantization of a real scalar field; in particular, a real scalar field uses a single oscillator family rather than independent charged-particle and antiparticle families.
