Every nonzero complex unital normed division algebra is algebraically and topologically isomorphic to C. This is the Gelfand-Mazur theorem; completeness is not needed in its statement. The bidual range condition implies both weak compactness and the stronger continuity of T∗. First assume T∗∗(X∗∗)⊆JY​Y. The Banach-Alaoglu theorem makes BX∗∗​ weak-star compact, and T∗∗ is weak-star continuous. Hence its image is compact for the weak-star topology in Y∗∗. Because it lies in JY​Y, it corresponds to a weakly compact subset C⊆Y under JY−1​.
The identity T∗∗JX​=JY​T gives T(BX​)⊆C. A weakly compact set in a Hausdorff space is closed for the weak topology, so C is also norm-closed and contains the norm closure of T(BX​). That closure is a convex set closed in the norm topology, hence weakly closed by the Mazur theorem. It is therefore a weakly closed subset of the weakly compact C, and is a weakly compact set. This proves (ii)⟹(i).
For (ii)⟹(iii), fix F∈X∗∗. By the range assumption there is yF​∈Y with T∗∗F=JY​yF​. For every y∗∈Y∗,
F(T∗y∗)=(T∗∗F)(y∗)=y∗(yF​).
(60)
The right side is a defining weak-star continuous evaluation on Y∗. Since the weak topology on X∗ is determined by all F∈X∗∗, every scalar coordinate of T∗ for that topology is weak-star continuous. Therefore
T∗:(Y∗,w∗)⟶(X∗,w) is continuous.​

Weak-star-to-weak continuity of the adjoint implies its weak compactness. By the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, BY∗​ is compact for the weak-star topology. Under the assumed continuous map T∗:(Y∗,w∗)→(X∗,w), its image T∗(BY∗​) is weakly compact in X∗.
This image is closed for the weak topology, and hence for the norm topology, because the weak topology is Hausdorff and weaker than the norm topology. Thus its norm closure is the same weakly compact set. By the definition of a weakly compact operator, T∗ is weakly compact, proving (iii)⟹(iv).

Weak compactness of the adjoint forces weak compactness of the original operator. Assume T∗:Y∗→X∗ is weakly compact. Apply the already proved (i)⟹(ii) to this operator, rather than assuming the adjoint equivalence in advance. It gives
T∗∗∗(Y∗∗∗)⊆JX∗​(X∗).
(62)
Take ψ∈Y∗∗∗ annihilating JY​Y, so ψ(JY​y)=0 for every y∈Y. Write T∗∗∗ψ=JX∗​f for some f∈X∗. For every x∈X,
f(x)=(JX∗​f)(JX​x)=(T∗∗∗ψ)(JX​x)=ψ(T∗∗JX​x)=ψ(JY​Tx)=0.
(63)
Hence f=0 and T∗∗∗ψ=0. It follows that
ψ(T∗∗F)=0(F∈X∗∗)
(64)
for every ψ annihilating JY​Y.
The vector subspace JY​Y is closed in the norm topology in Y∗∗ because Y is complete and JY​ is an isometry. The Hahn-Banach theorem says that any point outside a closed linear subspace can be separated from it by a bounded linear functional vanishing on that subspace. Applying this in Y∗∗ shows that an element annihilated by all such ψ∈Y∗∗∗ must belong to JY​Y. Therefore T∗∗F∈JY​Y for every F, proving (iv)⟹(ii). The reverse implications above now establish all four equivalences, including weak compactness of an operator and its adjoint:
T weakly compact ⟺ T∗∗(X∗∗)⊆JY​Y ⟺ T∗ is w∗-to-w continuous ⟺ T∗ weakly compact.​

X or Y reflexive⟹every bounded T:X→Y is weakly compact.​
