The operator bs(p) is a fermionic annihilation operator for a particle with momentum p and spin label s; ds†(p) is a fermionic creation operator for the corresponding antiparticle. The c-number Dirac spinors us(p) and vs(p) solve the positive-energy particle and antiparticle equations respectively. They supply the spinor wavefunctions in the mode expansion of a Dirac field; they are not creation or annihilation operators.
Apply the specified parity transformation to the two operators in the mode expansion of a Dirac field:
Pψ(x)P−1=ηP​∑p,s​[bs(pP​)us(p)e−ip⋅x−ds†(pP​)vs(p)eip⋅x].
(1)
Relabel k=pP​. The momentum sum is unchanged, p⋅x=k⋅xP​, and the spinor identities give us(p)=γ0us(k) and vs(p)=−γ0vs(k). The two minus signs in the antiparticle term cancel. Therefore
Pψ(x)P−1=ηP​γ0ψ(xP​).​
(2)
Taking the Hermitian conjugate and using (γ0)†=γ0, (γ0)2=1 in the Dirac adjoint yields
Pψˉ​(x)P−1=ηP∗​ψˉ​(xP​)γ0.​
(3)
The unit-modulus intrinsic-parity phase cancels from all neutral fermion bilinears.

Use the printed gamma matrix conventions throughout, in particular γ5=−iγ0γ1γ2γ3. The transpose identity for γ0 gives Cγ0=−γ0C, and its Hermitian conjugate gives the corresponding identity for C†. Thus the Dirac adjoint of the charge-conjugated field is
ψˉ​C=ηC∗​ψTγ0C†γ0=−ηC∗​ψTC†.
(4)
Reordering the anticommuting components of the fermion bilinear supplies a minus sign:
ψˉ​CψC=−ψTC†Cψˉ​T=ψˉ​(C†C)Tψ.
(5)
Invariance for arbitrary fields therefore requires C†C=I, so the charge-conjugation matrix is a unitary matrix. This is the required normalization constraint; it is not a bosonic reordering calculation.
For a spinor matrix Γ, the same calculation and the given C−1=CT give
(ψˉ​Γψ)C=ψˉ​(C−1ΓC)Tψ=ψˉ​C−1ΓTCψ.
(6)
With Vμ=ψˉ​γμψ and Aμ=ψˉ​γμγ5ψ, the supplied transpose identities imply
C−1γμTC=−γμ,C−1(γμγ5)TC=−γ5γμ=γμγ5.
(7)
Consequently the vector current is C-odd and the axial current is C-even: Vμ↦−Vμ and Aμ↦Aμ.
For parity, put Λ=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). The gamma matrix identities γ0γμγ0=Λμν​γν and γ0γ5γ0=−γ5 give
Vμ(x)⟼P​Λμν​Vν(xP​),Aμ(x)⟼P​−Λμν​Aν(xP​).​
(8)
Thus V0 is P-even and V P-odd, whereas A0 is P-odd and A P-even. The axial current transforms as a pseudovector.

Keep the color matrix intact: Aμ​=Aμa​Ta. Under parity, invariance of its contraction with the vector current requires
Aμ​(x)⟼P​Λμ​νAν​(xP​).​
(9)
Under charge conjugation, the fermionic reordering in part (b) transposes the color matrix as well as the spinor matrix. For a fixed color matrix M, ψˉ​Mγμψ becomes −ψˉ​MTγμψ. Hence invariance of the interaction requires
Aμ​(x)⟼C​−Aμ​(x)T.​
(10)
This is not a rule that changes the sign of every individual color component: transposition also acts on the generators of the fundamental representation.
Write the gauge field strength as Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​+ig[Aμ​,Aν​]. Since [AT,BT]=−[A,B]T, the charge conjugation rule gives Fμν​↦−FμνT​. Including the coordinate reflection in CP symmetry,
Fμν​(x)⟼CP​−Λμ​αΛν​βFαβ​(xP​)T.​
(11)
For generators normalized by Tr(TaTb)=κδab, the color contraction equals κ−1Tr(Fμν​Fρσ​). The two charge-conjugation minus signs cancel, and transposition reverses the order inside the trace without changing it. The Levi-Civita symbol acquires detΛ=−1 under the remaining reflection. Thus
Lθ​(x)⟼CP​−Lθ​(xP​).​
(12)
The Yang-Mills theta term is CP-odd; a generic fixed nonzero θ produces CP violation. Setting θ=0 removes this source. Although the density is a total derivative, it can affect the quantum theory through nontrivial topological sectors; the local transformation test is not rendered irrelevant by that fact.

An experimentally observed example is KL​→π+π−, a decay of the long-lived neutral kaon into a CP-even two-pion state. Without CP violation, the long-lived state would be CP-odd and this decay would be forbidden.
For the CKM matrix mechanism of CP violation, three quark generations are the minimum. Rephasings remove every complex phase for one or two generations, while three generations leave one physical CP-violating phase. This count concerns weak quark mixing. The Yang-Mills theta term in part (c) is a separate possible source of CP violation and does not require three generations.

Let V(ϕ)=m2ϕ2/2+λ(ϕ2)2/4, where ϕ2=∑i​ϕi2​. Its stationary-point equation is (m2+λϕ2)ϕi​=0.
For m2>0, the unique minimum is ϕ=0. The quadratic mass term is m2δij​, so there are n real scalar fields, all of mass m2​, and the global special orthogonal group SO(n) remains unbroken.
For m2<0, write v2=−m2/λ. The vacuum manifold is the sphere ϕ2=v2. Choose ⟨ϕ⟩=ven​. The transformations preserving this vacuum expectation value rotate the first n−1 components, giving SO(n)→SO(n−1). At this vacuum the potential's Hessian matrix is
∂ϕi​∂ϕj​∂2V​​ven​​=2λv2δin​δjn​.
(13)
Writing ϕ=(π1​,…,πn−1​,v+η), the radial mode η therefore has
mη2​=2λv2=−2m2,mπa​2​=0(a=1,…,n−1).​
(14)
These are one massive radial scalar and n−1 physical Goldstone bosons. The Goldstone theorem applies to the broken global generators; the flat angular directions of the vacuum manifold explain their zero masses. The spontaneous symmetry breaking is a choice of vacuum, not a change in the invariant Lagrangian. For n=1 there are no continuous angular directions: only the radial scalar remains, and the potential's discrete ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry is broken.

The three real components form the Adjoint representation of SU(2). Around a nonzero vacuum, a local gauge transformation rotates the direction of ϕ(x) to the third axis. In this unitary gauge, the two angular fields are removed from the scalar parametrization and become longitudinal polarizations of two gauge bosons, through the Higgs mechanism. Thus ϕ=(0,0,v+η)T describes the perturbative physical fluctuations, with v2=−m2/λ.
The subgroup generated by t3 fixes ve3​, giving SU(2)→U(1). Define the physical vector fields
Aμ​=Bμ3​,Wμ±​=2​Bμ1​∓iBμ2​​.
(15)
The gauge covariant derivative on the radial configuration is
Dμ​ϕ=​−gBμ2​(v+η)gBμ1​(v+η)∂μ​η​​,
(16)
so the scalar kinetic term becomes 21​(∂η)2+g2(v+η)2Wμ+​W−μ.
Here is an exact form of the Lagrangian in these physical fields, up to its additive vacuum constant. Define
fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​,Gμν±​=(∂μ​±igAμ​)Wν±​−(∂ν​±igAν​)Wμ±​,
(17)
and
Hμν​=fμν​+ig(Wμ+​Wν−​−Wμ−​Wν+​).
(18)
The minus sign in the printed non-Abelian gauge field strength gives precisely these signs. Then
L=−41​Hμν​Hμν−21​Gμν+​G−μν+21​∂μ​η∂μη+g2(v+η)2Wμ+​W−μ−λv2η2−λvη3−4λ​η4.​
(19)
Its quadratic terms give mA​=0, mW+​=mW−​=gv, and mη​=2λ​v. The two massive vectors have six real polarization degrees of freedom in total; the massless vector has two and the scalar one. Their total of nine equals the original six vector plus three scalar degrees of freedom.
Under the surviving U(1), W+ and W− have opposite charges and η is neutral. The Yang-Mills theory supplies AW+W− and AAW+W− couplings and quartic charged-vector self-interactions. The scalar term supplies 2g2vηW+W− and g2η2W+W−, together with the cubic and quartic scalar self-interactions. There is no direct ηAA term.
With suitable fermions, this spectrum offers a massless photon and massive charged mediators of short-range weak charged currents, so it captures part of the weak/electromagnetic pattern. It differs crucially from the electroweak interaction of the Standard Model: the latter gauges SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​, uses a complex Higgs doublet, and breaks to U(1)em​. Three rather than two generators are broken, leaving a massive neutral Z boson as well as W± and a photon. Its charge operator Q=T3+Y and independent hypercharge coupling give a nontrivial weak mixing angle. The present theory has no massive neutral mediator, no such mixing angle, and does not reproduce the observed chiral fermion charge assignments or their usual Yukawa couplings from the standard doublet/singlet assignments.

The Yukawa matrices for up-type and down-type quarks are diagonalized by separate unitary changes of basis. Let uLweak​=Uu​uLmass​ and dLweak​=Ud​dLmass​. The generation-diagonal weak charged current in the weak basis becomes
uˉLweak​γμdLweak​=uˉLmass​γμ(Uu†​Ud​)dLmass​,VCKM​=Uu†​Ud​.​
(20)
Thus the CKM matrix records the mismatch between the two left-handed mass bases, not an additional non-unitary interaction.
An N×N unitary matrix has N2 real parameters. The 2N quark-field phases can remove 2N−1 phases from V: the common phase changes neither current. This leaves (N−1)2 physical real parameters, comprising N(N−1)/2 mixing angles and (N−1)(N−2)/2 CP-violating phases. Hence three generations give four parameters: three angles and one phase; four generations give nine: six angles and three phases. This count assumes the usual nondegenerate masses, so that extra mass degeneracies do not enlarge the allowed basis transformations.

The tree-level Feynman diagram contains one weak charged current vertex. Fermion arrows point along fermion-number flow, so the outgoing antiquark arrow points toward the vertex.
For one fixed matching color, the scattering amplitude is
M=22​g​Vqq′​ϵμ​(p)uˉ(k)γμ(1−γ5)v(k′).
(21)
An overall phase from the vertex has no effect on the width. The fermion spin sums give ∑uuˉ=k, ∑vvˉ=k′ in the massless approximation. For the three initial polarizations, use a spin average of 1/3. The gamma matrix trace identities give
Tμν=Tr[kγμ(1−γ5)k′γν(1−γ5)]=8(kμk′ν+kνk′μ−gμνk⋅k′+iϵμνρσkρ​kσ′​).
(22)
The sign of the last term follows the PDF's γ5 convention and does not affect this decay: the polarization sum is symmetric. Since p=k+k′ and the daughter masses vanish, pμ​Tμν=0 and k⋅k′=MW2​/2. Therefore
∣M∣2​one color​=24g2∣Vqq′​∣2​(−gμν​+MW2​pμ​pν​​)Tμν=3g2MW2​​∣Vqq′​∣2.
(23)
In the rest frame, the two-body Lorentz-invariant phase space integrates to 1/(8π): after the spatial delta function sets k′=−k, its radial delta function fixes ∣k∣=MW​/2. Explicitly,
∫dΦ2​=16π21​∫dΩ∫0∞​dkδ(MW​−2k)=8π1​.
(24)
The decay formula consequently gives
Γone color​=16πMW​∣M∣2​​=48πg2MW​​∣Vqq′​∣2=6π2​GF​MW3​​∣Vqq′​∣2.​
(25)
This is the printed expression, interpreted for one color. For a physical quark-flavor channel there are three orthogonal final color states. Their probabilities add; no initial color average is present for a colorless W boson. Thus
ΓW+→qqˉ​′​=Nc​6π2​GF​MW3​​∣Vqq′​∣2,Nc​=3.​
(26)
The PDF's formula omits this color multiplicity if read as the ordinary inclusive flavor width. It is also the familiar normalization for a colorless lepton channel.
The six physically accessible quark combinations are udˉ,usˉ,ubˉ,cdˉ,csˉ,cbˉ. A real on-shell W boson cannot produce a top quark; the massless approximation is applied to the accessible daughters and does not open a physically forbidden top channel. CKM matrix unitarity gives ∑q′=d,s,b​∣Vuq′​∣2=∑q′=d,s,b​∣Vcq′​∣2=1. Therefore the physical color-summed hadronic width at this order is
Γhad​=π2​GF​MW3​​.​
(27)
If the printed one-color convention is retained for every channel, its sum is instead GF​MW3​/(3π2​). In the artificial theory where all three up-type flavors, including the top, are kinematically massless, there would be nine channels and the physical sum would be 9GF​MW3​/(6π2​); that is not the on-shell Standard Model channel list.

The weak charged current creates a left-handed neutrino and a right-handed outgoing positron in the massless limit. In the parent's rest frame their momenta are opposite, so these two fixed helicities give total angular-momentum projection of magnitude one along their flight axis. A spin-one W boson can supply that projection, and its leptonic width remains nonzero:
Γ(W+→e+νe​)​me​=0​=6π2​GF​MW3​​.​
(28)
A spin-zero pion cannot produce the same helicity configuration. The forbidden component is rescued only by a charged-lepton mass insertion that reverses its helicity, giving helicity suppression.
This can also be seen directly from the pion decay constant. Lorentz symmetry gives a vacuum-to-pion axial current matrix element proportional to fπ​pπμ​. Contracting pπ​=ke​+kν​ with the leptonic current gives
Mπ​ ∝ fπ​uˉν​(kν​+ke​)(1−γ5)ve​=−me​fπ​uˉν​(1+γ5)ve​,
(29)
using the massless neutrino equation and (ke​+me​)ve​=0. Hence Mπ​∝me​ and Γπ​∝me2​→0. The W boson polarization vector is not constrained to be proportional to the total momentum, so its amplitude has no analogous compulsory mass factor.

Both the left-handed Electron and the electron neutrino participate in weak charged currents through W±, and both have weak neutral currents mediated by the Z boson. Their electromagnetic interactions differ: the Electron has electric charge −e and couples to the photon, while the neutrino is electrically neutral and has no tree-level electromagnetic coupling.
In the minimal massless-neutrino Standard Model, the active neutrino has only a left-handed particle state and a right-handed antiparticle state. The Electron has both chiralities: its right-handed component has electromagnetic interactions and a weak neutral current, but no weak charged current. Its nonzero mass comes from a Yukawa coupling to the Higgs field; the minimal massless-neutrino model contains no right-handed neutrino for an analogous Dirac mass term.
Observed neutrino oscillations establish nonzero neutrino mass-squared differences. Atmospheric muon-neutrino disappearance depends on travel distance and energy, and solar electron neutrinos convert into other active flavors; reactor and accelerator oscillations provide complementary evidence. In propagation, the relative phase of two mass eigenstates is Δmij2​L/(2E). If all masses were zero there would be no such relative phase. Thus at least two neutrino mass eigenstates are nonzero in the three-neutrino description with two independent measured splittings. A flavor state is a superposition of mass eigenstates, so the evidence does not assign a single definite mass to a flavor label.

The two tree-level Feynman diagrams have t-channel W boson exchange. The antiquark process reverses the fermion-flow arrows on the lower line.
Each vertex contributes g/(22​) multiplying a chiral current. At momentum transfer ∣q2∣≪MW2​, the gauge-boson propagator is approximated by its momentum-independent term gαβ​/MW2​, up to the overall phase. The longitudinal term does not contribute to these conserved massless currents. Thus the coefficient is g2/(8MW2​)=GF​/2​, giving the Fermi interaction. For the quark and antiquark amplitudes, apart from irrelevant overall phases,
Md​=2​GF​​[uˉ(p′)γα(1−γ5)u(p)][uˉ(k′)γα​(1−γ5)u(k)],
(30)
and
Muˉ​=2​GF​​[uˉ(p′)γα(1−γ5)u(p)][vˉ(k)γα​(1−γ5)v(k′)].
(31)
The low-energy operator form is Leff​=−(GF​/2​)(eˉγα(1−γ5)νe​)(uˉγα​(1−γ5)d)+h.c.; its external spinor matrix elements are the displayed products.
For an unpolarized initial parton, average over its two spin states. There is no factor 1/2 for the incoming active neutrino, which has one allowed helicity. The initial color average cancels the final color sum, so there is no surviving color multiplicity in this parton cross section. Define
Tαβ(a,b)=Tr[aγα(1−γ5)bγβ(1−γ5)]=8(aαbβ+aβbα−gαβa⋅b+iϵαβρσaρ​bσ​).
(32)
The symmetric and antisymmetric parts have zero cross contraction. Their separate contractions are 128[(a⋅c)(b⋅d)+(a⋅d)(b⋅c)] and 128[(a⋅c)(b⋅d)−(a⋅d)(b⋅c)]. Thus the given Levi-Civita symbol identity yields
Tαβ(a,b)Tαβ​(c,d)=256(a⋅c)(b⋅d).
(33)
Including the coefficient GF2​/2 and the initial-parton spin average gives
∣Md​∣2​=64GF2​(p⋅k)(p′⋅k′),∣Muˉ​∣2​=64GF2​(p⋅k′)(p′⋅k).
(34)
Massless kinematics and q=p−p′=k′−k imply p⋅k=p′⋅k′=s/2. From yq​=k⋅q/(k⋅p), p′⋅k=p⋅k′=s(1−yq​)/2. Therefore
∣Md​∣2​=16GF2​s2,∣Muˉ​∣2​=16GF2​s2(1−yq​)2.
(35)
To reduce the supplied phase-space formula, use the massless center-of-mass flux 2s and dΦ2​=dΩ/(32π2). This gives dσ/dΩ=∣M∣2​/(64π2s). In that frame yq​=(1−cosϑ)/2, so dΩ/dyq​=4π. Hence
A(s)=πs​,B(s,yq​)=πs​(1−yq​)2,0≤yq​≤1.​
(36)
The antiquark channel's angular factor is a consequence of the chiral current and the reversed lower-line spinor order, rather than a color factor.

In the parton model, the incoming parton has k=ξPH​ and the outgoing massless parton has k′=ξPH​+q. Neglecting PH2​ and imposing its on-shell condition gives
0=(ξPH​+q)2=2ξPH​⋅q+q2,ξ=2PH​⋅q−q2​=x.​
(37)
Thus the Bjorken scaling variable measures the struck parton's longitudinal momentum fraction in this approximation. The inelasticity is unchanged when the hadron momentum is replaced by its parton's collinear momentum:
yq​=ξPH​⋅pξPH​⋅q​=PH​⋅pPH​⋅q​=y.​
(38)
Let SH​=(p+PH​)2≃2p⋅PH​. The partonic invariant in part (b) is s(ξ)=ξSH​, and Q2=−q2=xySH​. Insert its two parton cross sections into the parton distribution function convolution:
dydσH​​=πGF2​SH​​∫01​ξ[qd​(ξ)+(1−y)2quˉ​(ξ)]dξ.
(39)
Since the event's measured x fixes ξ=x, the integrand is the cross-section density in x. No additional Jacobian from yq​ is needed because yq​=y. Therefore
dydxd2σH​​=πGF2​SH​​x[qd​(x)+(1−y)2quˉ​(x)].​
(40)
Equivalently SH​=2Eν​EH​(1−cosϑνH​) in the chosen massless-hadron frame. The explicit factor x comes from the partonic energy s=xSH​, not from redefining the parton distribution functions. This expression uses the stated two-flavor and low-energy approximations, with masses and generation mixing omitted.
