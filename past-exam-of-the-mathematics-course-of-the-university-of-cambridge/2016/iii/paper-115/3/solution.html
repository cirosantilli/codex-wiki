A smooth vector field along a map γ:[a,b]→M is a smooth section of the pullback tangent bundle γ∗TM: it assigns V(t)∈Tγ(t)​M, with smooth coefficients in each local frame. It need not be the restriction of one ambient vector field, since a curve may return to the same point with different values of V.
An affine connection, also called a Koszul connection, induces a pullback connection on this bundle. In coordinates, write V=Vi(t)∂i​ and ∇∂j​​∂k​=Γijk​∂i​. Its covariant derivative along a curve is
Dt​V=(V˙i+Γijk​(γ(t))γ˙​jVk)∂i​.
(18)
The connection transformation law, or the Leibniz rule applied to a change of frame, makes this expression independent of the frame. In particular it is defined even when γ˙​=0; it differentiates the section's coefficients as well as the frame.
The field is parallel precisely when Dt​V=0. In a frame along a coordinate segment this is the linear ordinary differential equation
V˙i=−Aik​(t)Vk,Aik​(t)=Γijk​(γ(t))γ˙​j(t).
(19)
The Picard-Lindelöf theorem gives a unique solution with prescribed initial value. Smooth coefficients on compact subintervals are bounded, and the usual norm estimate for a linear ordinary differential equation prevents finite-time blowup there. A finite sequence of coordinate segments covers the image of the compact interval [a,b]; solve successively and use uniqueness on overlaps. This gives a unique parallel field on the whole interval for every Va​∈Tγ(a)​M.
Define parallel transport by τt​(Va​)=V(t). Linearity of the equation and uniqueness give τt​(cVa​+dWa​)=cτt​(Va​)+dτt​(Wa​). Solving along the reversed curve supplies its inverse. Therefore each τt​:Tγ(a)​M→Tγ(t)​M is a linear isomorphism.
For two affine connections, put Δ(X,Y)=∇X​Y−∇X​Y. Their rules give
Δ(fX,Y)=fΔ(X,Y),Δ(X,fY)=fΔ(X,Y),
(20)
since the two X(f)Y terms cancel. The tensoriality of this operation proves that the difference of affine connections is a tensor, of type (1,2), with smooth coordinate coefficients Γijk​−Γijk​.
A parametrized geodesic satisfies Dt​γ˙​=0, or the geodesic equation
γ¨​i+Γijk​(γ)γ˙​jγ˙​k=0.
(21)
With vi=γ˙​i, this becomes the first-order system γ˙​i=vi, v˙i=−Γijk​(γ)vjvk. Its right side is smooth, so the Picard-Lindelöf theorem gives a unique local solution for each initial point and tangent vector. This establishes uniqueness with the parametrization fixed.
The two accelerations differ by
Dt​γ˙​−Dt​γ˙​=Δ(γ˙​,γ˙​).
(22)
If Δ(v,v)=0 for every tangent vector, either acceleration vanishes exactly when the other does. Conversely, start the ∇-geodesic with arbitrary initial vector v at an arbitrary point. If it is also a ∇-geodesic with the same parameter, evaluating this identity initially gives Δ(v,v)=0. Hence
same parametrized geodesics⟺Δ(v,v)=0 for every v​.
(23)
Polarization makes the latter condition equivalent to Δ(u,v)+Δ(v,u)=0: parametrized geodesics determine the symmetric part of an affine connection. In particular torsion-free connections are determined by their parametrized geodesics, since their difference is also symmetric and must then vanish.
