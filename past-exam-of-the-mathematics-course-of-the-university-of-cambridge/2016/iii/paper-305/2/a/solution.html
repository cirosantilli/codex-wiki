Let V(ϕ)=m2ϕ2/2+λ(ϕ2)2/4, where ϕ2=∑i​ϕi2​. Its stationary-point equation is (m2+λϕ2)ϕi​=0.
For m2>0, the unique minimum is ϕ=0. The quadratic mass term is m2δij​, so there are n real scalar fields, all of mass m2​, and the global special orthogonal group SO(n) remains unbroken.
For m2<0, write v2=−m2/λ. The vacuum manifold is the sphere ϕ2=v2. Choose ⟨ϕ⟩=ven​. The transformations preserving this vacuum expectation value rotate the first n−1 components, giving SO(n)→SO(n−1). At this vacuum the potential's Hessian matrix is
∂ϕi​∂ϕj​∂2V​​ven​​=2λv2δin​δjn​.
(13)
Writing ϕ=(π1​,…,πn−1​,v+η), the radial mode η therefore has
mη2​=2λv2=−2m2,mπa​2​=0(a=1,…,n−1).​
(14)
These are one massive radial scalar and n−1 physical Goldstone bosons. The Goldstone theorem applies to the broken global generators; the flat angular directions of the vacuum manifold explain their zero masses. The spontaneous symmetry breaking is a choice of vacuum, not a change in the invariant Lagrangian. For n=1 there are no continuous angular directions: only the radial scalar remains, and the potential's discrete ϕ↦−ϕ symmetry is broken.
