Use the orientation selected by the volume form, and let volg​ be its normalized Riemannian volume form. The Euclidean metric induces an inner product on the exterior algebra of covectors. The Hodge star operator is the unique map from p-forms to (4−p)-forms satisfying
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩g​volg​.
(1)
For an oriented orthonormal coframe e1,…,e4, it sends a basis wedge to the complementary wedge with the sign of the permutation that restores e1∧⋯∧e4. Applying the Hodge star operator twice exchanges blocks of p and 4−p covectors, so
∗2=(−1)p(4−p),∗2=1on two-forms.​
(2)
Here the supplied volume is interpreted in the usual metric-normalized sense. If instead one defines the operator with an arbitrary unnormalized vol=fvolg​, that operator is f∗g​ and its square on two-forms is f2. The usual self-duality statements use the metric-normalized Hodge star operator, with the supplied volume specifying orientation.
The projections P±​=(1±∗)/2 give the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms:
Λ2=Λ+2​⊕Λ−2​,α±​=21​(α±∗α),∗α±​=±α±​.​
(3)
Both spaces have dimension three. With e4=dt, bases are
Σi±​=ei∧dt ± 21​ϵijk​ej∧ek,i=1,2,3.
(4)
The Hodge star operator is an orthogonal involution on two-forms and therefore is self-adjoint. For a self-dual two-form H and an anti-self-dual two-form G,
⟨H,G⟩=⟨∗H,G⟩=⟨H,∗G⟩=−⟨H,G⟩=0.
(5)
Consequently
H∧G=⟨H,∗G⟩volg​=0.​
(6)
This is the wedge orthogonality of opposite-duality two-forms.
For the Yang-Mills action, take an anti-Hermitian special unitary group connection and the fundamental matrix trace, so the positive invariant pairing on its Lie algebra is ⟨X,Y⟩=−tr(XY). Write its gauge curvature as F=F+​+F−​ using the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms, and define
∥F±​∥2=−∫tr(F±​∧∗F±​),SYM​=−gYM2​1​∫tr(F∧∗F).
(7)
Cross terms vanish by the wedge orthogonality of opposite-duality two-forms. Thus
SYM​=gYM2​∥F+​∥2+∥F−​∥2​,∫tr(F∧F)=−∥F+​∥2+∥F−​∥2.
(8)
With this anti-Hermitian convention the Second Chern number is
k=c2​(E)[S4]=8π21​∫tr(F∧F).
(9)
For example, this normalization follows by expanding det(1+iF/(2π)) and using trF=0. Interpreting the integral as an integer Second Chern number on R4 assumes the usual decay and gauge behavior that allow extension over the point at infinity. The following norm inequality itself does not require integrality:
SYM​≥gYM2​8π2​∣k∣.​
(10)
The Yang-Mills instanton Bogomolny bound is saturated precisely when one component vanishes: F=∗F or F=−∗F. In the stated convention the self-dual case has k≤0 and the anti-self-dual case has k≥0. Reversing orientation, or defining topological charge with the opposite sign, reverses this assignment while leaving the absolute-value bound unchanged.
For the self-dual Yang-Mills equations in temporal gauge, choose volg​=dx1∧dx2∧dx3∧dt and ϵ123​=1. The relevant Hodge star operator identities are
∗(dt∧dxi)=−21​ϵijk​dxj∧dxk,∗(dxj∧dxk)=−ϵijk​dt∧dxi.
(11)
Writing F=Fti​dt∧dxi+21​Fjk​dxj∧dxk, the self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=∗F give
Fti​=−21​ϵijk​Fjk​.
(12)
In temporal gauge, At​=0, so the gauge curvature component is Fti​=∂t​Ai​−∂i​At​+[At​,Ai​]=∂t​Ai​. Hence
∂t​Ai​=−21​ϵijk​Fjk​,Fjk​=∂j​Ak​−∂k​Aj​+[Aj​,Ak​].​
(13)
The minus sign follows from placing dt last in the orientation and first in the mixed curvature component.

The real vector space of symmetric matrices has dimension n(n+1)/2. On its invertible part the derivative of the determinant is
D(det)B​(C)=det(B)tr(B−1C).
(14)
At a determinant-one symmetric matrix, this derivative is nonzero: the symmetric direction C=B gives value n. The regular level set theorem therefore yields
dimM=2n(n+1)​−1,TB​M={C=CT:tr(B−1C)=0}.​
(15)
This applies to every signature component, not only positive-definite matrices.
The special linear congruence action on symmetric matrices preserves symmetry because (ABAT)T=ABAT, and preserves determinant because det(ABAT)=(detA)2detB=1. The identity acts trivially and
A1​⋅(A2​⋅B)=A1​A2​B(A1​A2​)T=(A1​A2​)⋅B.
(16)
These verify a smooth left Lie group action of the special linear group. By Sylvester's law of inertia, it also preserves the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues.
For n=2, parametrize the symmetric matrices by
B=(t+xy​yt−x​),detB=t2−x2−y2.
(17)
Thus M is exactly the two-sheeted hyperboloid
t2−x2−y2=1,t>0 or t<0.​
(18)
The two sheets consist respectively of positive-definite and negative-definite matrices. Each is preserved by the special linear congruence action on symmetric matrices. They are individually transitive: on the positive sheet B=AAT with A=B1/2 and detA=1; on the negative sheet use B=−AAT. The stabilizer of either I or −I is SO(2).
Differentiating the left Lie group action along exp(sX) gives the fundamental vector field
uX​(B)=XB+BXT.
(19)
For the three matrices in the question, their coordinate components are
u1​u2​u3​​=y∂t​+y∂x​+(t−x)∂y​,=2x∂t​+2t∂x​,=y∂t​−y∂x​+(t+x)∂y​.​
(20)
A sign convention matters here. With the usual Lie bracket of vector fields [U,V]f=U(Vf)−V(Uf), the fundamental fields of this left action obey [uX​,uY​]=−u[X,Y]​. Indeed, for linear coordinate fields uX​(z)=RX​z, the bracket has coefficient (RY​RX​−RX​RY​)z. To obtain a Lie algebra representation rather than an anti-representation, use the infinitesimal left-action sign convention
vX​(B)=dsd​​s=0​e−sXBe−sXT=−uX​(B).
(21)
An explicit answer is therefore
v1​v2​v3​​=−y∂t​−y∂x​+(x−t)∂y​,=−2x∂t​−2t∂x​,=−y∂t​+y∂x​−(t+x)∂y​.​​
(22)
These are tangent to the two-sheeted hyperboloid: applying each to Q=t2−x2−y2 gives zero. For example,
v1​(Q)=−2ty+2xy−2y(x−t)=0.
(23)
They can be written on either sheet using t=±1+x2+y2​:
v1​=−y∂x​+(x−t)∂y​,v2​=−2t∂x​,v3​=y∂x​−(t+x)∂y​.
(24)
Here the omitted ∂t​ component is determined by tangency, and derivatives of t(x,y) must be included when computing brackets in these coordinates.
Direct matrix multiplication gives
[τ2​,τ1​]=2τ1​,[τ2​,τ3​]=−2τ3​,[τ1​,τ3​]=τ2​.
(25)
Direct differentiation of the displayed vector fields gives exactly
[v2​,v1​]=2v1​,[v2​,v3​]=−2v3​,[v1​,v3​]=v2​.​
(26)
For instance, in ambient (t,x,y) coordinates, [v1​,v3​]=(−2x,−2t,0)=v2​. These are the defining relations of the sl2 Lie algebra. The three fields are linearly independent over constant real coefficients: if av1​+bv2​+cv3​ vanishes on a sheet, its ∂x​ coefficient (−a+c)y−2bt forces b=0,c=a, and its ∂y​ coefficient then equals −2at, forcing a=c=0. Thus the representation is faithful. Their pointwise span need only have dimension two, consistent with the dimension of each sheet.

A principal bundle separates the geometry of internal symmetry from a chosen local gauge potential. Let π:P→X be a principal G-bundle with a free right Lie group action. Each fiber is a copy of G, but generally no single identification works globally. A principal connection tells us how to compare fibers over nearby base points. Its physical interpretation is a prescription for parallel transport of internal states.
For ξ∈g, the vertical fundamental vector field is ξp#​=dsd​​0​pexp(sξ). A principal connection is a Lie algebra-valued one-form A on P satisfying
A(ξ#)=ξ,Rg∗​A=Adg−1​A.​
(27)
Equivalently, the horizontal distribution of a principal connection Hp​=kerAp​ complements the vertical tangent space and is preserved by right translation. Given a curve in X and an initial point in its fiber, there is a unique horizontal lift. For a closed curve its endpoint differs from its start by a group element: the holonomy of a connection. Thus a principal connection contains both infinitesimal and global transport information.
Choose local sections sα​:Uα​→P. Their local principal connection forms Aα​=sα∗​A are the usual gauge potentials. If sβ​=sα​gαβ​ on an overlap, equivariance and reproduction of vertical generators give
Aβ​=gαβ−1​Aα​gαβ​+gαβ−1​dgαβ​.​
(28)
This inhomogeneous law means that a gauge potential is not an ordinary globally defined tensor. Its local formulas glue to a global principal connection. The same law describes changing a local section by a gauge function. For example, to impose temporal gauge locally, solve ∂t​g=−At​g so the transformed time component vanishes.
The curvature of a principal connection is
F=dA+21​[A∧A],Fα​=dAα​+Aα​∧Aα​.​
(29)
Unlike the principal connection form, its curvature is horizontal and equivariant. Consequently Fβ​=gαβ−1​Fα​gαβ​, and the gauge curvature is globally a two-form on X with values in the adjoint bundle adP=P×Ad​g. The curvature measures the failure of horizontal directions to close under brackets: for horizontal lifts U,V, F(U,V)=−A([U,V]). A flat principal connection has an integrable horizontal distribution, although nontrivial global holonomy of a connection can remain around noncontractible curves.
The covariant exterior derivative on adjoint bundle-valued forms is locally DA​=d+[A,⋅]. Expanding F=dA+A∧A and using d2=0 yields the Bianchi identity
DA​F=0.​
(30)
For a representation ρ of G, the same principal connection induces transport in the corresponding associated vector bundle; locally its covariant derivative is d+ρ∗​(A). This explains why charged matter and the gauge curvature use the same gauge potential.
Now put an oriented Riemannian metric on a four-dimensional base and take G=SU(n). Use anti-Hermitian matrices with positive pairing −tr(XY), as in the preceding solution. The Yang-Mills action is
S[A]=−gYM2​1​∫X​tr(F∧∗F).
(31)
Its gauge invariance follows from curvature conjugation and invariance of the trace. Under a compactly supported variation a=δA, δF=DA​a. Integration by parts therefore gives
δS=−gYM2​2​∫X​tr(a∧DA​∗F),DA​∗F=0.​
(32)
This is a second-order equation for the gauge potential. The self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=∗F and the Anti-self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=−∗F are first-order equations. Either implies the full Yang-Mills equations immediately, since DA​∗F=±DA​F=0 by the Bianchi identity. This is self-duality implies Yang-Mills equations.
A Yang-Mills instanton is a smooth finite-action Euclidean solution with self-dual or anti-self-dual gauge curvature. Its defining first-order condition depends on the principal connection, the metric and the orientation. Since the Hodge star operator on two-forms is unchanged under g↦e2fg in four dimensions, self-duality and the Yang-Mills action are conformally invariant. In particular Euclidean solutions can be studied through conformal compactification, with appropriate behavior at infinity.
The global topology is encoded by Chern-Weil theory. Since trF=0, the Second Chern form and Second Chern number are
c2​(A)=8π21​tr(F∧F),k=∫X​c2​(A)∈Z
(33)
on a compact oriented four-manifold. The Bianchi identity makes c2​(A) closed. More explicitly, under a connection variation,
δtr(F∧F)=2tr(DA​a∧F)=2dtr(a∧F).
(34)
Thus the integrated Second Chern number is independent of the principal connection on a fixed bundle, with the usual fixed-boundary condition on a noncompact base. The space of connections is affine, so integrating this variation along a straight path also proves that their characteristic forms differ by an exact form.
Locally the same characteristic form has a Chern-Simons 3-form primitive:
tr(F∧F)=dtr(A∧dA+32​A∧A∧A).
(35)
On R4 the underlying bundle is trivial, but a finite-action instanton with the standard extendible behavior at infinity can define a nontrivial bundle after adding the point at infinity. The transition map on an equatorial S3 takes values in SU(n); for n≥2, its homotopy class lies in π3​(SU(n))≅Z. The boundary integral of the Chern-Simons 3-form computes that integer with the chosen sign convention. This reconciles a local matrix-valued gauge potential on R4 with nonzero global Second Chern number on S4.
Finally the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms produces the Yang-Mills instanton Bogomolny bound
S[A]≥gYM2​8π2​∣k∣.​
(36)
Equality holds exactly when the opposite-duality component of the gauge curvature vanishes. Thus a Yang-Mills instanton is an absolute action minimum in its fixed topological sector, not merely a stationary solution. In this trace convention self-dual instantons have k<0 and anti-self-dual instantons have k>0. In the zero sector, a self-dual or anti-self-dual finite-action solution has S=0 and hence F=0.
Different principal connections related by bundle gauge transformations describe the same physical configuration. Fixing k and taking the quotient of instanton solutions by this gauge equivalence of principal connections gives the instanton moduli space. Near an anti-self-dual solution, write a variation as a∈Ω1(X,adP). The linearized instanton equation and infinitesimal gauge transformations are
P+​DA​a=0,a∼a+DA​ε.
(37)
A local gauge condition DA∗​a=0 removes this redundancy. The combined operator DA∗​⊕P+​DA​ is elliptic: for nonzero covector ξ, its principal symbol a↦(ιξ♯​a,P+​(ξ∧a)) has zero kernel. Choose ξ=dt; the first component removes at​, and the self-dual projection of dt∧a removes the three remaining components. This connects the instanton moduli space to geometric analysis, while the characteristic number and transport law explain its topological and physical meaning.

A Poisson bivector is a smooth antisymmetric contravariant two-tensor
ω=21​ωij(x)∂i​∧∂j​
(38)
whose bracket on smooth functions,
{F,G}=ω(dF,dG)=ωij∂i​F∂j​G,​
(39)
satisfies the Jacobi identity. Bilinearity and antisymmetry are immediate, and the product rule makes the bracket a derivation in each argument. Together these properties define a Poisson manifold. Unlike a symplectic form, the Poisson bivector need not be nondegenerate.
Apply the Jacobi identity to the coordinate functions. Since {xi,xj}=ωij,
0={xi,{xj,xk}}+{xj,{xk,xi}}+{xk,{xi,xj}}=∑m​(ωim∂m​ωjk+ωjm∂m​ωki+ωkm∂m​ωij).
(40)
Changing ωim to −ωmi and rearranging the three summands gives the printed coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector:
m∑​(ωmi∂m​ωjk+ωmk∂m​ωij+ωmj∂m​ωki)=0.​
(41)
This is also sufficient: expanding the Jacobiator of three arbitrary smooth functions, all terms involving second derivatives cancel in pairs by antisymmetry. The remaining coefficient of ∂i​F∂j​G∂k​H is the coordinate-function Jacobiator displayed above.
For a Lie algebra, the natural global space carrying the proposed linear bracket is its dual space g∗. If vi is the chosen basis, define its linear coordinate function by xi(ℓ)=ℓ(vi). The Lie-Poisson bracket is
{F,G}(ℓ)=ℓ([dFℓ​,dGℓ​])=ckij​xk∂i​F∂j​G,​
(42)
where dFℓ​,dGℓ​ are elements of g=(g∗)∗. On a general manifold the same coordinate expression gives a local construction; a global one requires compatible transition rules. The use of g∗ supplies that compatibility intrinsically.
Here ωij=crij​xr and ∂m​ωjk=cmjk​. The left side of the coordinate Jacobi condition for a Poisson bivector is therefore
xr∑m​(crmi​cmjk​+crmk​cmij​+crmj​cmki​)=−xr∑m​(cmjk​crim​+cmij​crkm​+cmki​crjm​)=0.
(43)
The final coefficient is the negative of the coefficient of vr in the Lie algebra identity [vi,[vj,vk]]+[vj,[vk,vi]]+[vk,[vi,vj]]=0. Thus the Lie-Poisson bracket satisfies the Jacobi identity.
It remains to find the Lie algebra structure constants for the printed rotation fields. Distinguish their original spatial coordinates (x,y,z) from the coordinates (x1,x2,x3) on the dual space. Use the conventional Lie bracket of vector fields. Their component vectors are (0,−z,y), (z,0,−x) and (−y,x,0), respectively. For example,
[v1,v2]=(y,−x,0)=−v3.
(44)
Similarly,
[v2,v3]=−v1,[v3,v1]=−v2,ckij​=−ϵijk​.​
(45)
The negative sign is essential: these are the fundamental fields of a left rotation action with the stated Lie bracket of vector fields, and consequently have the infinitesimal left-action sign convention discussed above. The first field here has component y∂z​, as printed in the PDF.
The Lie-Poisson bracket on the dual space consequently has
{x1,x2}=−x3,{x2,x3}=−x1,{x3,x1}=−x2.
(46)
For the evolution convention F˙={F,H}, the Hamiltonian function has derivatives (2Ax1,2Bx2,2Cx3). Substitution gives the quadratic rotational Lie-Poisson dynamics
x˙1x˙2x˙3​=2(C−B)x2x3,=2(A−C)x3x1,=2(B−A)x1x2.​​
(47)
These are Euler-type Hamilton's equations on a noncanonical Poisson manifold. When A=1/(2I1​), B=1/(2I2​) and C=1/(2I3​) with positive principal inertias, they are the Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body in body angular-momentum coordinates. As a check, H is conserved by antisymmetry of the Poisson bracket, and C0​=(x1)2+(x2)2+(x3)2 is a Casimir function of a Poisson manifold. Direct differentiation of C0​ in the three equations cancels the terms 4[(C−B)+(A−C)+(B−A)]x1x2x3. The Hamiltonian flow therefore lies on both an energy level and a sphere, a symplectic leaf of this signed rotational bracket.
