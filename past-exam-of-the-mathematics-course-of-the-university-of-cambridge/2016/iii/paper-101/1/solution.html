The Laurent polynomial ring is a localization of a ring:
R=k[X1​,X2​]X1​X2​​.
(1)
The Hilbert basis theorem makes k[X1​,X2​] a Noetherian ring, and Localization of a Noetherian ring preserves this property. Thus R is Noetherian​.
For separating finite Laurent support by an integer weight, take a nonzero Laurent polynomial r and let F⊂Z2 be its finite set of exponent pairs with nonzero coefficients. Choose an integer N such that
N>maxm,m′∈F​∣m1​−m1′​∣,
(2)
where the maximum is zero if F has only one member. Set (a1​,a2​)=(1,N). If m1​+Nm2​=m1′​+Nm2′​, then
N∣m2​−m2′​∣=∣m1​−m1′​∣<N,
(3)
so m2​=m2′​ and then m1​=m1′​. Consequently all powers of T in θ(1,N)​(r) are distinct. The linear independence of the Laurent monomials prevents cancellation, giving
θ(1,N)​(r)=0.​
(4)
The choice depends on the finite support of r; a single fixed substitution need not detect every nonzero element.
For Noetherianity of subalgebras of a one-variable Laurent polynomial ring, let A be a unital k-subalgebra of k[T,T−1]. If A=k, it is already a Noetherian ring. Otherwise we find a nonconstant f∈A and an ambient ring B containing A such that B is a finitely generated module over k[f].
If A⊆k[T], choose a nonconstant polynomial f=ad​Td+⋯+a0​ in A, where ad​=0. Then T is an integral element over k[f], since it satisfies the monic polynomial equation
Td+ad​ad−1​​Td−1+⋯+ad​a1​​T+ad​a0​−f​=0.
(5)
Thus B=k[T] is a finitely generated module over k[f], generated by 1,T,…,Td−1. The case A⊆k[T−1] is identical after replacing T by T−1.
In the remaining case A is contained in neither of these two polynomial rings. There is an element with a positive exponent and an element with a negative exponent. If either already has both, use it as f. Otherwise their sum has both, since the positive powers of the first and the negative powers of the second cannot cancel. We obtain
f=∑j=−pq​aj​Tj,p,q>0,a−p​aq​=0.
(6)
Multiplying this identity by Tp gives a polynomial equation for T with leading coefficient aq​:
∑j=−pq​aj​Tj+p−fTp=0.
(7)
Dividing by aq​ makes it monic. Similarly, multiplication by T−q gives a monic equation for T−1 after division by a−p​. Both generators are therefore integral elements over k[f], so B=k[T,T−1] is a finitely generated module over k[f].
In all three cases k[f] is a Noetherian ring, as a quotient of a one-variable polynomial ring. The subalgebra A⊆B is also a k[f]-submodule, so it is a Noetherian module. Every ideal of A is a k[f]-submodule of A and is therefore finitely generated over k[f], hence over A. This proves
Every k-subalgebra of k[T,T−1] is Noetherian.​
(8)
For the final request, take the following subalgebra of the two-variable Laurent polynomial ring:
A=k+X1​k[X1​,X2​]=k[X1​,X1​X2​,X1​X22​,…].​
(9)
Let J=X1​k[X1​,X2​], an ideal of A. We have
A/J≅k,J2=X12​k[X1​,X2​],J/J2≅X1​k[X2​]
(10)
as k-vector spaces. In particular, the classes of X1​X2j​, for j≥0, are linearly independent. If J had finitely many generators as an A-module, their classes would span J/J2 over A/J=k, contradicting its infinite dimension. Hence J is not finitely generated and A is not a Noetherian ring. This illustrates why the preceding result is special to one Laurent variable.
