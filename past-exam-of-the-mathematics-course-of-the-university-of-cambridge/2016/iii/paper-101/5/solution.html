The Artin-Rees lemma states that, for a Noetherian ring R, an ideal I, a finitely generated module M, and a submodule N⊆M, there is c≥0 such that
IjM∩N=Ij−c(IcM∩N)for every j≥c.​
(44)
Thus the filtration on N induced from the I-adic filtration of M is eventually determined by one of its terms.
For the proof, form the Rees ring and its Rees module:
R(I)=⨁j≥0​Ijtj⊆R[t],RI​(M)=⨁j≥0​IjMtj.
(45)
Since R is a Noetherian ring, write I=(a1​,…,as​). Then R(I)=R[a1​t,…,as​t], so it is a Noetherian ring by the Hilbert basis theorem. Generators of M in degree zero generate RI​(M) as a module over R(I), so this is a Noetherian module.
The graded submodule
L=⨁j≥0​(IjM∩N)tj
(46)
is therefore finitely generated. Choose homogeneous generators zα​tdα​ with dα​≤c for some c. For j≥c, taking degree-j components gives
IjM∩N=∑α​Ij−dα​zα​=Ij−c∑α​Ic−dα​zα​.
(47)
The same generators show that the final sum is exactly IcM∩N. This proves the Artin-Rees lemma. If L=0, one may simply take c=0.
Now let
K=⋂j≥1​IjM.
(48)
It is a submodule of the Noetherian module M, hence a finitely generated module. Apply the Artin-Rees lemma with N=K and j=c+1. Since K is contained in every IjM (including I0M=M), its conclusion reduces to
K=IK.
(49)
Write generators as y1​,…,yh​. There is a matrix A=(aij​) with entries in I such that yi​=∑j​aij​yj​. Multiplication by the adjugate of Ih​−A shows that
det(Ih​−A)yi​=0for all i.
(50)
The determinant is congruent to 1 modulo I, so it is 1+r for some r∈I. This determinant trick produces a single such element annihilating all of K, and in particular annihilating each m∈K. If K=0, use r=0.
Conversely, if (1+r)m=0 with r∈I, then m=−rm. Iterating gives m=(−r)jm∈IjM for every j≥1. Hence the exact description is
j≥1⋂​IjM={m∈M:some r∈I satisfies (1+r)m=0}.​
(51)
In a local ring with I contained in the maximal ideal, every 1+r is a unit, so this also yields the usual vanishing form of the Krull intersection theorem.
For a failure without the Noetherian ring hypothesis, let D=Z[1/2]≥0​ and use the semigroup algebra
R=k[tq:q∈D]=⋃h≥0​k[t1/2h].
(52)
Its elements are finite sums of formal monomials tq, with multiplication tqtq′=tq+q′. Each ring in the union is a polynomial ring and the inclusions are injective, so R is an integral domain. It is not a Noetherian ring, since
(t)⊊(t1/2)⊊(t1/4)⊊⋯.
(53)
Indeed the generator on the left is the square of the next generator, while division in the opposite direction would require a negative exponent, which is unavailable in R.
Let I be the ideal of elements with zero constant term, equivalently
I=(tq:q∈D, q>0).
(54)
It is proper because taking the constant term gives R/I≅k. Every generator tq is (tq/2)2, with tq/2∈I, so I2=I. Thus this is a nonzero idempotent ideal, with Ij=I for every j≥1. Take
m=t∈j≥1⋂​Ij.​
(55)
For every r∈I, the constant term of 1+r is 1, so 1+r=0. Since R is an integral domain and t=0, we have (1+r)t=0. This gives all three requested objects and proves that no element of the required form annihilates m.
