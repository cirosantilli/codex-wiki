Put P(t)=∑n≤t/(2π)​​n−1/2−it. The given bound for the Riemann zeta function, together with (a+b)4≤8(a4+b4) for a,b≥0, gives
∫1T​∣ζ(1/2+it)∣4dt≪∫1T​∣P(t)∣4dt+logT.
(43)
The integral over [0,1] is bounded, since the Riemann zeta function has no pole on this compact segment. It remains to estimate the fourth moment of the Riemann zeta function through this moving Dirichlet polynomial cutoff.
Let L=⌊T/(2π)​⌋ and Y=L2. In the expansion of ∣P(t)∣4, a quadruple n1​,n2​,n3​,n4​≤L occurs precisely for
t≥t0​=max(1,2πn12​,2πn22​,2πn32​,2πn42​).
(44)
Its oscillatory factor is eitlog(n3​n4​/(n1​n2​)), and its weight is (n1​n2​n3​n4​)−1/2. When n1​n2​=n3​n4​, its integral has length at most T. When these products differ, its integral over [t0​,T] has absolute value at most 2/∣log(n3​n4​/(n1​n2​))∣. Thus the moving cutoff affects the lower endpoint but preserves the off-diagonal bound from part (a).
Write rL​(m)=#{(a,b):a,b≤L, ab=m}. The divisor function d(m) bounds rL​(m). Grouping the off-diagonal majorant by the two products and using the weighted row-sum argument in part (a), with coefficient rL​(m) and σ=1/2, bounds it by
O(log(2Y)∑m≤Y​rL​(m)2)≪log(2Y)∑m≤Y​d(m)2≪Ylog4(2Y)≪T(logT)4.
(45)
This uses only the given divisor-square summatory bound. For bounded T, the desired conclusions follow by boundedness on compact segments, so these estimates may be read for large T with Y≥2. We have proved the requested diagonal-plus-error estimate:
∫0T​∣ζ(1/2+it)∣4dt≪Tn1​,n2​,n3​,n4​≤T/(2π)​n1​n2​=n3​n4​​∑​n1​n2​n3​n4​​1​+T(logT)4.​
(46)
The diagonal sum equals ∑m≤Y​rL​(m)2/m≤∑m≤Y​d(m)2/m. If D(x)=∑m≤x​d(m)2≪xlog3(2x), then partial summation gives
∑m≤Y​md(m)2​=YD(Y)​+∫1Y​x2D(x)​dx≪log4(2Y).
(47)
Consequently the full fourth-moment bound is
∫0T​∣ζ(1/2+it)∣4dt≪T(logT)4(T≥2).​
