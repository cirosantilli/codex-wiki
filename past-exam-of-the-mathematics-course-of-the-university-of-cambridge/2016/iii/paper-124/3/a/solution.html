Use the convention that a z-sieved number is a positive integer with no prime factor strictly below z; 1 is included. Such integers are also called rough numbers. Using instead exclusion of primes at most z changes an endpoint convention, not the following fixed-u asymptotic.
The Buchstab function is the continuous function w:[1,∞)→R determined by
w(u)=u1​(1≤u≤2),dud​(uw(u))=w(u−1)(u>2).​

Successive integration over intervals of length one determines the Buchstab function uniquely from its initial values.
A standard fixed-u form of Buchstab theorem states that, for each fixed u>1, as z→∞,
Φ(zu,z)∼logzzu​w(u).​
(26)
For 1<u≤2, this agrees with the Prime number theorem, since apart from an immaterial endpoint the z-sieved numbers in this range are 1 and the primes from z to zu. The restriction u>1 matters: at u=1 the count is bounded, so the displayed asymptotic does not hold there.
