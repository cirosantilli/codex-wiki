Let T=10Gyr and let t denote present stellar age, rather than time measured from the galaxy's birth. With a fixed initial mass function, constant star formation rate also gives a constant number of births per unit time, as in the birth-age distribution under constant star formation. Counting the supplied stellar evolution model's remnants as part of the population, ages have a uniform distribution on [0,T]. Integrating the constant birth rate therefore gives
Y(t)=Tt​=0.1Gyrt​(0≤t≤T).​
(1)
Outside this interval the cumulative fraction is zero or one as appropriate.
Write m=M/M⊙​ for the birth-mass parameter and Mmin​=0.2M⊙​. The normalization cancels when integrating the initial mass function:
X(M)=∫Mmin​∞​kM⊙3​M′−3dM′∫M∞​kM⊙3​M′−3dM′​=M2Mmin2​​.
(2)
Thus the mass-tail fraction is
X(M)=m20.04​(m>0.2).​
(3)
It is one at and below the lower cutoff. The normalization constant has no effect on any number fraction.
For evolutionary-state selection by stellar lifetimes, a red giant has completed its main sequence lifetime but not its giant lifetime. Its stellar lifetime regions in a mass-age diagram are bounded by
m28Gyr​<t<m210Gyr​,0<t<T,m≥0.2.​
(4)
The lower boundary meets t=T at m=0.8​ and the upper boundary meets it at m=1. A white dwarf lies above the upper lifetime curve, within the same age interval.
The probability integral transform, applied to the decreasing mass-tail coordinate, makes X uniform on (0,1): Pr(X<x)=x. The independent birth age gives Y=t/T uniform on (0,1), so number fractions are areas in a unit square. Since m−2=25X, the red giant region becomes 20X<Y<25X, a triangle with vertices (0,0), (1/25,1) and (1/20,1). Its area is 21​(1/20−1/25)=1/200. The white dwarf region Y>25X is a triangle of area 21​(1/25)=1/50. Hence the present individual-star fractions are
Pr(G)=0.005=0.5%,Pr(W)=0.02=2%.​
(5)
The rest are on the main sequence under the given toy lifetime model.
For the binary stars, draw two independent mass-tail coordinates X1​,X2​, but only one shared age coordinate Y: components born together are coeval. The coeval binary population therefore occupies a uniform unit cube (X1​,X2​,Y). At a fixed age Y=y, the state probabilities for either component are
g(y)=Pr(G∣y)=100y​,w(y)=Pr(W∣y)=25y​=4g(y),s(y)=1−20y​.
(6)
Their evolutionary states have conditional independence given y. They generally do not have unconditional independence, because sharing an age correlates their states. The binary fractions below are slice areas averaged over 0<y<1.

Use a circular orbit, as in the stated Roche lobe approximation, with orbital angular speed Ω and negligible stellar spin. The center of mass distances are a1​=aM2​/M and a2​=aM1​/M. Summing the two orbital angular momenta gives
J=(M1​a12​+M2​a22​)Ω=MM1​M2​​a2Ω.​
(13)
Kepler's third law, Ω2a3=GM, makes this J=M1​M2​Ga/M​. In conservative mass transfer, both M and J are fixed, while dM2​=−dM1​. Thus
dlnM1​dlnM2​​=−q,dlnM1​dlna​=2(q−1).
(14)
The Roche-lobe radius response exponent is consequently
ζL​=dlnM1​dlnRL​​=2(q−1)+31​=2q−35​.
(15)
During a dynamical mass-loss perturbation, the deep interior and luminosity do not have time to change. The supplied giant structure therefore has stellar radius response exponent ζ∗​=−0.27. Its fractional overfill changes by dln(R/RL​)=(ζ∗​−ζL​)dlnM1​. The binary mass ratio uses donor mass divided by accretor mass. Since dlnM1​<0, self-limiting Roche-lobe overflow requires ζ∗​>ζL​. Hence the dynamical stability condition is
q<qcrit​=25/3−0.27​=0.6983≃0.7.​
(16)
At equality the linear restoring response vanishes. This conservative mass-transfer critical mass ratio uses the given radius exponent, rather than imposing the different fully convective −1/3 approximation.
The initially more massive component normally evolves first. If it first overflowed only after acquiring the given giant response, it would have q>1 and the mass loss would be dynamically unstable: its radius grows while its Roche lobe initially contracts. Starting overflow earlier can avoid this outcome. A main sequence or early post-main-sequence donor star can have a radiative envelope with a stabilizing contraction response. Transfer then reverses the binary mass ratio before the donor develops the giant structure. This is the route to an Algol binary through Case A mass transfer during core hydrogen burning, or early case B mass transfer after core hydrogen exhaustion but before a deep giant envelope develops.
For the initial Roche-lobe overflow to occur before the base of the giant branch, its lobe must be smaller than the base-of-branch stellar radius. Combining RL​=0.462a(M1​/M)1/3 with Kepler's third law cancels the companion dependence:
Pi​<2π[0.4623GM1​RBGB3​​]1/2.
(17)
Writing m1​=M1​/M⊙​, substitute the mass-radius relation to obtain the pre-giant period limit:
Pi​<P0​m12​,P0​=2π[GM⊙​(1.68/0.462)3R⊙3​​]1/2≃0.803days.​
(18)
We use the specified 0.8days below. This is a pre-giant Roche-lobe-filling period limit, not a condition on the current stripped donor's radius.
At fixed total mass, Kepler's third law gives P∝a3/2, while fixed orbital angular momentum gives a∝(M1​M2​)−2. Therefore the conservative period invariant is
P(M1​M2​)3=constant.​
(19)
To find the smallest possible pre-giant period ratio, use solar-unit masses m1​,m2​ and fixed m=m1​+m2​. Let K=P(m1​m2​)3. Along this period-product invariant for conservative mass transfer,
F(m1​)=m12​P​=m15​(m−m1​)3K​.
(20)
The denominator has logarithmic derivative 5/m1​−3/(m−m1​), with a strictly negative derivative of its own. Its unique maximum, hence the unique minimum of F, occurs at
m1​=85​m,m2​=83​m.​
(21)
Since F diverges at either endpoint, this is the global minimum period-to-donor-mass ratio for conservative evolution.
For the quoted Algol binary, K=3(1×3)3=81days and m=4. Choosing the minimizing progenitor gives m1,i​=2.5, m2,i​=1.5 and
Pi​=(2.5×1.5)381​=1.536days,Fmin​=0.24576days<0.8days.
(22)
Its pre-giant upper limit is 0.8(2.5)2=5days, comfortably above this initial period. Subsequent conservative mass transfer of 1.5M⊙​ produces the stated masses and period. Thus the data permit the proposed early-overflow formation route.
For RT Lac, instead K=5(0.5×1.5)3=2.109375days and m=2. At its most favorable conservative progenitor, m1,i​=1.25, m2,i​=0.75 and
Pi​=2.56days,Fmin​=1.6384days>0.8days.
(23)
No conservative progenitor meets the pre-giant overflow condition. An initially more massive giant donor also fails the dynamical-stability condition derived above. Within this stable Algol-type formation model, the current RT Lac system therefore requires nonconservative mass transfer.
A plausible history is substantial envelope loss from the system, through a stellar wind or escaping overflow, possibly with a common envelope episode. Escaping matter also carries orbital angular momentum, so the total-mass and period-product invariants no longer constrain its initial orbit. A more massive progenitor can then shed its envelope and reach the present low donor mass without requiring the excluded conservative sequence. The supplied final data do not determine a unique mass-loss or angular-momentum-loss history.

For constant component masses, the center of mass frame has r1​=(M2​/M)r and r2​=−(M1​/M)r. Newton's law of universal gravitation gives the relative equation r¨=−GMr/r3. Substitution in the component angular momenta yields
J=μh=μr×r˙,h˙=r×r¨=0.​
(24)
Here μ=M1​M2​/M is the reduced mass, while h is specific angular momentum.
The printed total-energy expression lacks a factor of μ in its kinetic term. With the stated physical separation coordinate, the dimensionally consistent total two-body orbital energy is
E=2μ​∣r˙∣2−rGMμ​=με,ε=21​∣r˙∣2−rGM​.​
(25)
The second expression is specific orbital energy; these two conventions must not be mixed. Its conservation follows directly:
E˙=μr˙⋅(r¨+r3GMr​)=0.
(26)
For the eccentricity vector, differentiate GMe=r˙×h−GMr. Since h is constant,
GMe˙=−r3GM​r×(r×r˙)−GM[rr˙​−r3r(r⋅r˙)​]=0.
(27)
The cancellation is the vector triple product identity. Thus all three corrected Kepler integrals are conserved. Dotting the eccentricity relation with r also gives the useful orbit identity
e⋅r=GMh2​−r.​
(28)
For ballistic streamline focusing by a moving star, work in the star's rest frame and take the incoming gas to have velocity vex​, with downstream behind the star at x>0. A streamline with impact parameter d>0 approaches from (−∞,d,0). Its upstream specific angular momentum and eccentricity vector are
h=−dvez​,e=ex​+GMdv2​ey​.
(29)
At its downstream axis crossing, r=bex​. The orbit identity gives b=d2v2/(GM)−b, hence the collision distance is
b=2GMd2v2​.​
(30)
At that point, hz​=buy​=−dv, while the y component of GMe=u×h−GMr gives dvux​=dv2. Therefore
u=vex​−dv2GM​ey​.
(31)
A symmetric streamline with opposite impact parameter has the opposite transverse velocity. Their shock wave removes the opposing transverse motion while preserving the common downstream component. Thus the remaining velocity is vex​ in the star frame. In the frame where the undisturbed medium is stationary, adding the star's velocity gives zero velocity immediately after this idealized collision.
After transverse kinetic energy is dissipated, the remaining specific orbital energy is εafter​=v2/2−GM/b. It is negative if b<2GM/v2, or equivalently if the upstream impact parameter satisfies d<dcap​=2GM/v2. This is the post-shock capture criterion in ballistic accretion; bound axial streams can return to the star. The captured incident mass flux through the corresponding disk gives the ballistic Bondi--Hoyle--Lyttleton accretion rate:
M˙=πdcap2​ρv=v34π(GM)2ρ​.​
(32)
This is the pressureless, dissipative capture model, with the shock's transverse energy unavailable to unbind the wake.
For wind mass transfer in a binary star, steady isotropic donor mass loss gives the local wind mass density at the companion:
ρw​(a)=4πa2vw​−M˙1​​.
(33)
A fast wind has vw​≫2πa/P and a capture scale small compared with a, so its relative incident speed is vw​ to leading order. Apply the same Bondi--Hoyle--Lyttleton accretion rate with accretor mass M2​ to obtain
M˙2,acc​=(−M˙1​)a2vw4​G2M22​​.​
(34)
Finally, Kepler's third law eliminates the unquoted separation, a=[G(M1​+M2​)P2/(4π2)]1/3, giving the rate in terms of the stated orbital period:
M˙2,acc​=(−M˙1​)(M1​+M2​)2/3vw4​(2πG/P)4/3M22​​.​
(35)
Equivalently, the fast-wind accretion fraction in a circular binary is [M2​/(M1​+M2​)]2(vorb​/vw​)4, with vorb​=2πa/P the relative circular orbital speed. Its smallness is consistent with using an almost undisturbed isotropic stellar wind.
