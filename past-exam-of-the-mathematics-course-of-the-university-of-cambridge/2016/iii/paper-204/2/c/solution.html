Let cn​ count n-step self-avoiding walks on the square lattice from one fixed graph vertex. Splitting a self-avoiding walk after m steps and dropping the avoidance constraint on its suffix gives cm+n​≤cm​cn​. The Fekete lemma therefore gives the connective constant μ=limcn1/n​. The planar dual graph of the square lattice is a translated copy of it, so its connective constant is also μ.
Put q=1−p and assume qμ<1. Choose a>μ with qa<1. The definition of the connective constant provides a finite K such that cj​≤Kaj for every j≥0. A simple dual graph cycle of length ℓ surrounding 0 has a graph vertex in a box of radius ℓ+1: its diameter is at most ℓ, and its coordinate ranges straddle the origin. Choose such a graph vertex as the starting point, orient the graph cycle, and omit its closing edge. The remaining self-avoiding walk has length ℓ−1. Consequently the number Nℓ​ of these graph cycles obeys
Nℓ​≤K0​ℓ2cℓ−1​≤K1​ℓ2aℓ−1
(14)
for fixed finite constants. A specified graph cycle is open in dual bond percolation, equivalently all its crossed primal edges are closed, with probability qℓ. Hence
∑ℓ≥L​Nℓ​qℓ⟶0as L→∞.
(15)
To make this tail argument valid for every qμ<1, rather than only extremely small q, use a finite modification of Bernoulli percolation. Condition all primal edges within ΛN​ to be open. This event has positive probability for p>0. If the resulting percolation cluster of 0 is finite, its outer boundary contains a simple closed dual graph cycle surrounding the whole box. Such a graph cycle has length tending to infinity with N and crosses no forced-open edge. Under the conditioning its remaining edge states retain the original independent law, so its probability is still qℓ. Choose N so that the above tail is less than 1/2. The conditional probability that 0 belongs to an infinite percolation cluster is then at least 1/2, and thus θ(p)>0.
This connective-constant Peierls bound proves that every p>1−μ−1 is above or at the onset of positive percolation probability. Taking the infimum gives
pc​≤1−μ−1​.
(16)
The case p=1 is immediate. This proof uses the Peierls argument, without assuming the exact Harris-Kesten theorem.
