The reference Brownian motion must start at x, and both laws must use the same stopped-path rule. Under the reference probability measure let Rs​=x+Ws​, let τ be its exit time from (a,b), and put T=t∧τ and c=(δ−1)/2. The Girsanov density for a stopped Bessel process, as a functional of the stopped reference path, is
D(R)=exp{c∫0T​Rs​dWs​​−2c2​∫0T​Rs2​ds​}.​
(16)
Use the predictable process θs​=cRs−1​1{s≤τ}​, with any harmless choice at the endpoint. On [0,t], ∫θs2​ds≤c2t/a2. The Novikov condition holds; under the density D, the Girsanov theorem makes Ws​=Ws​−∫0s​θu​du a Brownian motion. Before exit, dRs​=cRs−1​ds+dWs​, which is the required stochastic differential equation. The drift is a locally Lipschitz function on (0,∞), so pathwise uniqueness and uniqueness in law hold up to this exit time.
For a version involving just the endpoint and an ordinary integral, the Itô formula for logR gives

Thus
D(R)=(xRT​​)(δ−1)/2exp{−8(δ−1)(δ−3)​∫0T​Rs2​ds​}.​

The stopped density is measurable from the stopped path, so it is also the Radon-Nikodym derivative of the stopped-path laws. The stopping times in this formula are evaluated on the reference path, rather than taken from a different realization of X. A zero-start reference Brownian motion would have a different deterministic initial value and could not dominate this law.
