The Lévy characterization of Brownian motion states that an adapted continuous real process M with M0​=0 is standard Brownian motion relative to (Ft​) if and only if it is a continuous local martingale and [M]t​=t. The multidimensional version replaces the bracket identity by [Mi,Mj]t​=δij​t.
For the converse direction, a standard Brownian motion is a continuous martingale, and its independent centered increments give quadratic variation t. Distinct independent coordinates have zero quadratic covariation.
For the substantive direction, fix a vector θ∈Rd. The Itô formula applied to
Et​(θ)=exp{iθ⋅Mt​+21​∣θ∣2t}
(31)
shows that its drift is zero: the second-order term is −21​∣θ∣2Et​dt and cancels the time derivative. Thus E(θ) is a complex local martingale. On [0,T] its modulus is the deterministic value e∣θ∣2t/2≤e∣θ∣2T/2; localization and the dominated convergence theorem make it a true martingale. Hence
E[eiθ⋅(Mt​−Ms​)∣Fs​]=e−21​∣θ∣2(t−s).​
(32)
This conditional characteristic function is the deterministic characteristic function of N(0,(t−s)I). For A∈Fs​, multiplying by 1A​ and taking expectations, then using uniqueness of characteristic functions, proves that Mt​−Ms​ has this multivariate normal distribution and is independent of Fs​. Iterated conditioning gives independent increments with Gaussian distributions. Continuity, the zero initial value and these increment laws are the definition of standard Brownian motion. The case d=1 proves the requested statement; the vector form will also be used below.
