For each ball, the event of landing in Sj​ is a Bernoulli distribution trial. Under the null hypothesis its probability is ε. The Hoeffding lemma for a Bernoulli distribution variable gives Ees(B−p)≤es2/8. Multiplication of the moment-generating functions of the independent random variables gives Ees(X−np)≤ens2/8. Because the scalar signal and the noise vector are independent random variables with normal distributions, their sum is a Gaussian random vector. Its expected value is zero, and the covariance matrix is
ΣS​=Id​+θu(S)u(S)⊤,Pθ,S​=N(0,ΣS​).​

Put ℓ=log(d/δ) and r=ℓ/n​≤1. Use the quadratic scan statistic Λ and reject when Λ>1+4r. Under the null hypothesis, each nu(T)⊤Σu(T) has the chi-squared distribution with n degrees of freedom. The chi-squared concentration inequality gives P0⊗n​(Λ>1+2r+2r2)≤de−ℓ=δ, by the union bound. Since 2r+2r2≤4r, the proposed Type I error is at most δ.
For the true set S, nu(S)⊤Σu(S)/(1+θ) has the same chi-squared distribution. We need a lower-tail bound that remains positive even when ℓ/n is close to one. Set a=e−4r. The chi-squared Chernoff lower-tail bound gives P(V≤na)≤exp[−n(a−1−loga)/2]≤e−nr2=e−ℓ: indeed e−4r−1+4r≥2r2 for 0≤r≤1. To verify this last inequality, its derivative is 4(1−r−e−4r); the expression in parentheses is strictly concave, starts at zero, and changes sign once, so the minimum occurs at an endpoint, where the inequality holds.
The function g(r)=(1+4r)e4r−1 is convex with g(0)=0, so g(r)≤rg(1)<300r on this interval. Consequently θ>300r implies (1+θ)e−4r>1+4r. Failure to reject then requires V≤na, giving Type II error at most e−ℓ≤δ, uniformly in S. An explicit, deliberately conservative answer is
ψ=1{Λ>1+4log(d/δ)/n​}​,C=300.​
(9)
The sharp constant is unnecessary; the detection scale is log(d/δ)/n​.

Each row of the oriented incidence matrix records one signed endpoint difference, hence
s(θ)=∥Dθ∥1​.​
(13)
Reversing an edge changes the sign of its row, and permuting the edges permutes rows; neither operation changes the sum of absolute differences. With the usual variance-proxy convention for a sub-Gaussian random vector, every column inner product is a sub-Gaussian random variable with proxy at most τ2. The two-sided Chernoff bound and union bound imply
∥A⊤g∥∞​≤τ2log(2m/δ)​≤2​τ(log(2m)​+log(1/δ)​)​

The printed coefficient-one bound is false under this convention. Take k=m=1, A=[1], and g=±τ with equal probability. This Rademacher random variable has the stipulated variance proxy, but for δ=0.99 the printed threshold is less than τ, whereas ∣g∣=τ surely. Even a general sub-Gaussian assumption does not imply Gaussian concentration inequality for arbitrary Lipschitz functions. Under the stronger convention Ee⟨a,g⟩≤eτ2∥a∥22​/4, the same union bound proves the printed constants. That convention, however, differs from the binomial variance proxy used in 1(b).

For a symmetric matrix H with simple largest eigenvalue and spectral gap γ, the Davis-Kahan curvature lemma says 2γ​∥uu⊤−vv⊤∥F2​≤tr(H(vv⊤−uu⊤)), where v is its leading unit eigenvector. Applying this to a leading eigenvector v of H+W, the maximal Rayleigh quotient and duality of the operator norm and trace norm give ∥vv⊤−vv⊤∥F​≤22​∥W∥op​/γ; the projector difference has matrix rank at most two. This is a rank-one eigenprojector perturbation bound.
Here W=M−M0​=A−A0​ has independent centered bounded upper-triangular entries, apart from symmetry. The spectral norm bound for a centered Bernoulli adjacency matrix gives E∥W∥op​≤C0​n​. Taking H=M0​ and γ=tn​/2 therefore gives, for the largest-eigenvalue estimator,
E∥vv⊤−vv⊤∥F​≤t42​C0​​.​
(20)
The printed argmin selects the wrong end of the spectrum. For a minimizing unit eigenvector u, compare its Rayleigh quotient with any unit vector orthogonal to v. This yields γ∣u⊤v∣2≤2∥W∥op​, and hence E∣u⊤v∣2≤4C0​/t. Its mean projector error is at least 2​(1−4C0​/t), using 1−x​≥1−x. Along t=n1/4 with sufficiently large even n, the printed parameter restriction holds and this lower bound tends to 2​, contradicting an error of order 1/t. Replace argmin by argmax, or negate the entire matrix before minimizing.

For partitions, the appropriate Hamming distance between unlabelled bipartitions is Δ(T,S)=min{∣T△S∣,∣T△Sc∣}. It counts vertices assigned to the wrong group after the better global exchange of the two group names. The printed signed-indicator formula does not implement this exchange: negating a zero-one indicator is not taking its complement. It must be read as this partition distance, or written with the ±1 membership vectors.
Use the corrected leading eigenvector estimator from 4(b), and orient its sign to minimize ∥v−σv∥2​. At every wrongly signed coordinate, this difference has magnitude at least 1/n​. Therefore the sign rounding bound for a unit eigenvector gives
Δ(S,S)≤nminσ​∥v−σv∥22​≤n∥vv⊤−vv⊤∥F2​≤2n∥vv⊤−vv⊤∥F​.

The Kullback-Leibler divergence of a product measure is the sum of the individual divergences, by expanding the logarithm of the likelihood ratio and taking its expected value. Put q=1/2−t/n​ and define W(S) to be the unordered pairs lying within one group, while C(S) is the set of unordered pairs crossing between groups. Then
DKL​(PS​∥PS′​)=∑e∈W(S)∖W(S′)​DKL​(Ber(1/2)∥Ber(q))+∑e∈W(S′)∖W(S)​DKL​(Ber(q)∥Ber(1/2)).
(22)
Thus choosing the printed ∂S to mean W(S) gives its order of the summands. If ∂S denotes the usual graph cut C(S), their two orders must be interchanged. Each unordered edge is counted once.
The inequality logx≤x−1 gives
DKL​(Ber(p)∥Ber(q))≤p(qp​−1)+(1−p)(1−q1−p​−1)=q(1−q)(p−q)2​.​
(23)
Since 1/3<q<1/2, each changed-edge divergence is at most 9t2/(2n). Summing over at most n(n−1)/2 pairs gives DKL​(PS​∥PS′​)≤9nt2/4. This is the information scale needed for Fano's inequality.

In natural logarithms, Fano's inequality for a uniform index J among N alternatives states P(J=J)≥1−(I(J;G)+log2)/logN. The mutual information is at most the average Kullback-Leibler divergence to any fixed reference law. With the supplied packing, nearest-neighbour decoding turns partition error below half the separation into correct decoding. Thus the packing alone proves a bound at radius n(1/4−ε/2), with the unavoidable log2 term. It does not automatically prove the much larger printed radius.
A list-decoding Fano inequality supplies the intended near-half error threshold. Assume 0<ε<1/4, since otherwise that threshold is nonpositive and its event is automatic. Take a uniform prior on all N=(n/2n​)/2 unlabelled balanced partitions, and let r=n(1/2−2ε). A Hamming ball of radius less than r around any estimated partition contains at most B=∑j<r​(jn​) such partitions. This follows by selecting the representative closer to the estimated group; each unlabelled partition contributes at most one such representative because r<n/2. The entropy bound for a Hamming ball gives logB≤nh(r/n), where h is the binary entropy function measured in natural logarithms. Its curvature gives log2−h(1/2−2ε)≥8ε2. Also (n/2n​)≥2n/(n+1), so
log(N/B)≥8ε2n−log(2(n+1)).
(24)
Use the all-1/2 independent-edge law P∗​ as a reference. Only the n2/4 crossing pairs differ; the preceding Bernoulli distribution divergence estimate gives DKL​(PS​∥P∗​)≤nt2. Hence I(J;G)≤nt2. Whenever the denominator is positive, the list-decoding Fano inequality proves the finite-sample answer
Sinf​Smax​PS​{Δ(S,S)≥r}≥1−8ε2n−log(2(n+1))nt2+log2​.​
(25)
For 4ε2n≥log(2(n+1)), this is at least 1−t2/(4ε2)−log2/(4ε2n). If in addition nt2≥log2, it implies the intended form with c′=1/2. This proves the claimed information-theoretic scale with its needed finite-sample qualifications.
As a uniform finite-sample assertion for every positive t, the printed form is false. At t=0 every partition has the same data law, and a uniformly random balanced guess has a positive chance of being correct, giving error strictly below one. Such randomness can also be extracted from the finite graph sample: assign distinct graph outcomes to the finitely many balanced partitions; under the common law every selected outcome has positive probability. The worst error remains strictly below one for sufficiently small positive t, by continuity. The printed lower bound tends to one as t↓0 for any fixed universal c′, which contradicts this. The entropy correction cannot be dropped without an asymptotic or signal-size condition.
