For 0<α<1, an averaged operator has the form
T=(1−α)I+αR​
(15)
for some nonexpansive mapping R, meaning ∥Rz−Rw∥2​≤∥z−w∥2​ for all z,w. The word averaging refers to a convex combination with the identity. It does not mean averaging function values over a neighborhood.
An equivalent and useful averaged-operator inequality is
∥Tz−Tw∥22​≤∥z−w∥22​−α1−α​∥(I−T)z−(I−T)w∥22​​.
(16)
To see it, put d=z−w and r=Rz−Rw and expand
∥(1−α)d+αr∥22​=(1−α)∥d∥22​+α∥r∥22​−α(1−α)∥d−r∥22​.
(17)
Use ∥r∥2​≤∥d∥2​ and (I−T)z−(I−T)w=α(d−r). Conversely, substituting R=[T−(1−α)I]/α into the same expansion proves its nonexpansiveness from the displayed inequality. The strict range 0<α<1 matters for convergence of unrelaxed iterations: a general nonexpansive mapping such as T=−I can have nonconvergent alternating iterates.
