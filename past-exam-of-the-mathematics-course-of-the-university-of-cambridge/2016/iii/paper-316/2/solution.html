The normalization of the circular restricted three-body problem implies μ1​+μ2​=1, by Kepler's third law. The two bodies have barycentric positions (−μ2​,0) and (μ1​,0). Consequently
r1​=(x+μ2​)2+y2​,r2​=(x−μ1​)2+y2​​.
(11)
Differentiate the effective potential directly:
Ux​=x−r13​μ1​(x+μ2​)​−r23​μ2​(x−μ1​)​,Uy​=y−r13​μ1​y​−r23​μ2​y​.
(12)
Since x=μ1​(x+μ2​)+μ2​(x−μ1​) and y=(μ1​+μ2​)y, these become
Ux​=μ1​(1−r1−3​)(x+μ2​)+μ2​(1−r2−3​)(x−μ1​),Uy​=μ1​(1−r1−3​)y+μ2​(1−r2−3​)y​.
(13)
On y=0, centrifugal acceleration is the straight line acf​=x. Each gravitational term points towards its source, diverges at that source, and tends to zero far away. Let f(x)=Ux​(x,0); away from the sources,
f′(x)=1+∣x+μ2​∣32μ1​​+∣x−μ1​∣32μ2​​>0.
(14)
Thus the net acceleration is strictly increasing on each of the three intervals separated by the bodies. On the left interval f runs from −∞ to +∞; on the middle interval it runs from −∞ just right of M1​ to +∞ just left of M2​; on the right interval it runs from −∞ to +∞. The intermediate value theorem and strict monotonicity give exactly three collinear Lagrange points, L3​,L1​,L2​ in increasing x order. The sketch plots gravity and centrifugal acceleration separately; their intersections with agrav​=−x locate the equilibria.
Write xL2​=μ1​+δ, so r2,L2​=δ and r1,L2​=1+δ. The exact right-hand equilibrium equation is
0=μ1​+δ−(1+δ)2μ1​​−δ2μ2​​=(1+2μ1​)δ−δ2μ2​​+O(δ2).
(15)
As μ2​→0, μ1​→1, so 3δ3≃μ2​. Therefore the distance is the leading Hill radius in units of the binary separation:
r2,L2​≃α=(3M1​M2​​)1/3​.
(16)
Replacing M1​+M2​ by M1​ changes only higher-order terms. In dimensional units the right-hand side is multiplied by the binary separation.
Use the displacement vector q=(X,Y,X˙,Y˙)T, where X=x−xL2​ and Y=y. The absolute coordinate x cannot be the first entry of a homogeneous linear system about L2​; it must be translated. At L2​, reflection symmetry gives Uxy​=0, while the Hessian matrix has
Uxx​=1+2B,Uyy​=1−B,B=(1+δ)3μ1​​+δ3μ2​​​.
(17)
To first order in ∣X∣/α and ∣Y∣/α, the Coriolis acceleration then gives
q˙​=Aq,A=​001+2B0​0001−B​100−2​0120​​.
(18)
This is the linearization at L2. The expression for B is exact at the true equilibrium; in the small-secondary limit, μ2​/δ3≃3 and μ1​/(1+δ)3≃1, so B≃4.
For an eigenmode proportional to eλt, eliminate the velocity coordinates. The characteristic polynomial is
[λ2−(1+2B)][λ2−(1−B)]+4λ2=λ4+(2−B)λ2+(1+2B)(1−B)=0.
(19)
Thus
λ2=2B−2±B(9B−8)​​.
(20)
For B≃4, the four eigenvalues are
λ=±1+27​​,λ=±i27​−1​​.
(21)
There is an exponentially growing eigenmode, so L2​ is an unstable saddle-centre equilibrium. A generic displacement has an unstable component whose distance grows as e1+27​​t. The L2 escape e-folding time is
τgrow​≃nbin​1+27​​1​≃nbin​0.399​≃0.0634Pbin​​.
(22)
This is a local exponential timescale, not the time to reach a prescribed distance from an unspecified initial offset. Starting with unstable amplitude q0​, reaching q1​ within the linear neighborhood takes approximately τgrow​log(q1​/q0​). Initial data exactly on the centre-stable subspace need not grow forward at this linear order; the existence of the growing eigenmode already proves instability.
