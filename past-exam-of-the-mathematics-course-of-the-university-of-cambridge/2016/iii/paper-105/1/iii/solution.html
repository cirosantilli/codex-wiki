To obtain a contraction mapping, use the weighted holomorphic norm on a shrinking time domain. Its finite-norm space consists of holomorphic functions on Cα​ with zero initial value; the apparent quotient at t=0 is interpreted by a limit. Completeness follows because convergence in this norm implies locally uniform convergence of holomorphic functions, including near t=0, and the limiting pointwise bounds give convergence in the norm.

Finally, near each real initial point, a real analytic function g has a holomorphic extension G. Apply the same construction to ϕ=G(x)+v with v(x,0)=0 and source iG′(x), restricting the discs if necessary. This gives a local real analytic function of (x,t) with the prescribed initial value. Equivalently the solution is ϕ(x,t)=G(x+it)​ wherever the extension is defined.
