A contraction semigroup on a Banach space X consists of bounded linear operators S(t) with S(0)=I, S(t+s)=S(t)S(s), ∥S(t)∥≤1, and S(t)x→x as t↓0 for each x∈X. Its infinitesimal generator of a semigroup is
D(A)={x:limh↓0​hS(h)x−x​ exists in X},Ax=limh↓0​hS(h)x−x​.
(20)
For density, Yosida averaging of a semigroup gives xh​=h−1∫0h​S(s)xds. Taking the difference quotient of this Bochner integral shows that xh​∈D(A) and Axh​=(S(h)x−x)/h. Strong continuity gives xh​→x, so D(A) is dense.
For closedness, the semigroup restricted to its generator domain satisfies
S(t)x−x=∫0t​S(s)Axds(x∈D(A)).
(21)
If xn​→x and Axn​→y, pass to the limit in this identity. After dividing by t, strong continuity gives (S(t)x−x)/t→y. Hence x∈D(A) and Ax=y. This proves generator of a strongly continuous semigroup is closed and densely defined.
The contraction form of the Hille-Yosida theorem states that a linear operator A generates a contraction semigroup if and only if it is densely defined and closed, every real λ>0 lies in its resolvent set, and
∥(λ−A)−n∥≤λ−n(n≥1).
(22)
Here the n=1 bound implies the others by taking powers of the same bounded resolvent.
The H1 space is H1(R)={u∈L2:u′∈L2} with weak derivative u′ and squared norm ∥u∥22​+∥u′∥22​. The displayed weighted space is the one-dimensional harmonic oscillator form domain, with inner product
⟨u,v⟩X​=∫R​uvˉ+u′v′+x2uvˉdx.
(23)
If un​ is Cauchy in X, it converges in H1 to u, and xun​ converges in L2 space to some w. On each bounded interval, multiplication by x is bounded, so w=xu there. Thus xu∈L2 and convergence holds in X, proving completeness and the Hilbert space property. Equivalently this is closedness of the multiplication operator by x.
For the Sobolev characterization by bounded difference quotients, if f∈H1 then
Dh​f=∫01​f′(⋅+θh)dθ,∥Dh​f∥2​≤∥f′∥2​,Dh​f⟶f′ in L2.
(24)
The last assertion uses continuity of translation of a function in L2 space. Conversely, if f∈L2 and the quotients are uniformly bounded for 0<∣h∣<1, take a weakly convergent subsequence as h→0. For every test function v,
∫Dh​fvdx=∫f(x)hv(x−h)−v(x)​dx⟶−∫fv′dx.
(25)
The weak limit is therefore the weak derivative f′∈L2. This proves the characterization.
