Write V={1,…,p}. The global Markov property for a directed acyclic graph says that, for every three pairwise disjoint vertex sets A,B,C,
A and B are D-separated by C in G⟹ZA​⊥⊥ZB​∣ZC​.
(1)
Thus graphical D-separation implies probabilistic conditional independence. Causal minimality means that P has this global Markov property for a directed acyclic graph, but has it for no proper subgraph of G obtained by deleting edges while keeping all vertices. Faithfulness of a directed acyclic graph means that the displayed implication is an equivalence: the conditional independences of P are exactly the D-separations of G. In particular it includes the global Markov property for a directed acyclic graph.
A causally minimal distribution can nevertheless have an extra independence. Here is a causally minimal nonfaithful Gaussian model. Take three independent random variables E1​,E2​,E3​, each with the standard normal distribution, and set
Z1​=E1​,Z2​=Z1​+E2​,Z3​=Z2​−Z1​+E3​.
(2)
Use the Directed acyclic graph with edges 1→2, 1→3 and 2→3. The equations give a directed acyclic graph factorization, so the global Markov property for a directed acyclic graph holds. Alternatively this complete acyclic graph imposes no nontrivial D-separations. But Z3​=E2​+E3​ is independent of Z1​=E1​, even though the direct edge 1→3 prevents their D-separation. Hence faithfulness of a directed acyclic graph fails.
To check causal minimality explicitly, the covariance matrix is
Σ=​110​121​012​​.
(3)
The linear transformation from (E1​,E2​,E3​) to (Z1​,Z2​,Z3​) is invertible, so this is a nonsingular multivariate normal distribution. Deleting 1→2 would require Z1​⊥Z2​, whereas Cov(Z1​,Z2​)=1. Deleting 1→3 would require Z1​⊥Z3​∣Z2​, whereas the conditional covariance is
Cov(Z1​,Z3​∣Z2​)=0−21⋅1​=−21​.
(4)
Deleting 2→3 would require Z2​⊥Z3​∣Z1​, whereas its conditional covariance is 1−1⋅0=1. By Gaussian conditional independence, each nonzero conditional covariance rules out the required independence. Every proper subgraph is contained in a graph obtained by a single edge deletion, and further deletion can only add Markov restrictions. Thus no proper subgraph is Markov for P, proving causal minimality.
For the Markov blanket, use its standard convention that the focal vertex is excluded:
B=mb(k)=(pa(k)∪ch(k)∪pa(ch(k)))∖{k}.
(5)
We prove Markov blanket D-separation by examining an arbitrary path from k to v∈/B∪{k}. If its first edge points into k, its next vertex is a parent of k, hence a conditioned noncollider in B, and the path is blocked. Otherwise the path starts k→h at a child h∈B. If the next edge points out of h, then h is again a conditioned noncollider. If it points into h, the path begins k→h←u. Although conditioning on the collider h opens that local segment, u is another parent of h, hence belongs to B. The outgoing arrow u→h makes u a noncollider on the path, so conditioning on u blocks it. Since v is outside B, none of these blocking vertices can be the endpoint.
Every path is therefore blocked by B. Applying the global Markov property for a directed acyclic graph gives
Zk​⊥⊥ZV∖(B∪{k})​∣ZB​.​
(6)
This proves the requested screening property without any positivity assumption on P.
Finally assume faithfulness of a directed acyclic graph and let A have the stated screening property. Then k and every vertex outside A∪{k} must be D-separated by A. If a parent or child h of k were missing from A, the one-edge path between them would be active, a contradiction. Thus every parent and child is in A. If another parent u of a child h were missing from A, the path k→h←u would be active: its only interior vertex is the collider h, already in A. This is again a contradiction. Consequently
A⊇mb(k).​
(7)
Under faithfulness the graphical blanket is contained in every screening set. This is the minimal Markov blanket under faithfulness; the preceding argument also shows that the blanket itself is a screening set.
