We construct the output presentation explicitly, using HNN extensions and an amalgamated free product. Throughout, P also denotes the group given by the input finite group presentation, and X={x1​,…,xn​}. Every word introduced below is a literal computable word in the displayed generators; testing whether w=1 is never part of the construction.
First fix the auxiliary Higman group
Q=⟨a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​∣ai​ai+1​ai−1​=ai+12​ (imod4)⟩.
(49)
We need two facts: ⟨a1​,a3​⟩ is a free group of rank two, and the normal closure of a1​ is Q. The latter follows immediately from the relations: setting a1​=1 forces successively a2​=a22​, a3​=a32​, and a4​=a42​, hence all three are 1.
Here is a normal-form verification of the first fact. The three-generator group
Q123​=⟨a1​,a2​,a3​∣a1​a2​a1−1​=a22​, a2​a3​a2−1​=a32​⟩
(50)
is an amalgamated free product of two Baumslag-Solitar groups BS(1,2) along their infinite cyclic subgroups generated by a2​. Their base cyclic groups embed by Britton's lemma, so this amalgamation is legitimate. Nonzero powers of a1​ in the first factor lie outside ⟨a2​⟩, as shown by the homomorphism recording the exponent of its stable letter a1​. Nonzero powers of a3​ in the second factor also lie outside ⟨a2​⟩: the stable-letter exponent map first forces a putative equality a3r​=a2s​ to have s=0, and then injectivity of the base cyclic group forces r=0. The normal form theorem for an amalgamated free product therefore makes ⟨a1​,a3​⟩ a rank-two free group in Q123​.
The same argument applies to Q341​, formed from the other two cyclic conjugation relations. The full Q is Q123​∗⟨a1​,a3​⟩​Q341​, so that rank-two free group embeds in Q as well. In particular a1​ has infinite order.
Now take the free product
D=P∗F(z,d),c=[w,z]=wzw−1z−1,C=d−1cd,
(51)
and set
g0​=z,g1​=d,gi+1​=xi​z(1≤i≤n).
(52)
Introduce n+2 letters u0​,…,un+1​, and define the finite presentation
H=⟨X,z,d,u0​,…,un+1​∣R, uj−1​cuj​=gj​ (0≤j≤n+1)⟩.
(53)
Finally impose the Higman relations and identify
C=a1​,uj​=a3j+1​a1​a3−(j+1)​(0≤j≤n+1).
(54)
Thus the algorithm outputs
P(w)=⟨​X,z,d,u0​,…,un+1​,a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​∣ R,uj−1​cuj​=gj​ (0≤j≤n+1),ai​ai+1​ai−1​=ai+12​ (imod4),C=a1​,uj​=a3j+1​a1​a3−(j+1)​ (0≤j≤n+1)⟩.​​
(55)
The symbols c,C,gj​ in this display are abbreviations for the explicit words above, not extra generators. Renaming the finitely many new generators to avoid X makes this a uniform effective construction.
