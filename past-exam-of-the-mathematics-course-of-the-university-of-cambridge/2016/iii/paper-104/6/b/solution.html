OurBigBook.com Words: 187
An isomorphism-invariant Markov property of finitely presented groups ρ has two finitely presented witnesses: a group A+​ with ρ, and a group A−​ which cannot embed in any finitely presented group having ρ. In symbols,
A+​ has ρ,A−​↪B⟹B does not have ρ.​
(61)
The second condition is an obstruction to embedding, stronger than merely saying that A−​ itself fails the property.
Let U be a fixed finitely presented group with unsolvable word problem for a group. Form the finitely presented free product P=U∗A−​. Given a word w in the generators of U, apply part (a) to this P and w, and output the finite presentation of
B(w)=A+​∗P(w).
(62)
If w=1 in U, it is also 1 in P, so P(w) is trivial and B(w)≅A+​ has ρ. If w=1 in U, its image stays nontrivial in the free product P, and P embeds in P(w). Therefore A−​ embeds in B(w), which cannot have ρ. We have the effective equivalence
B(w) has ρ⟺w=1 in U.​
(63)
An algorithm recognizing whether an arbitrary finite group presentation has ρ would decide the unsolvable word problem for a group U. This contradiction proves the Adian–Rabin theorem: no Markov property of finitely presented groups is algorithmically decidable.
