The HNN extension in the convention used here is
G∗ϕ​=⟨G,t∣tat−1=ϕ(a) for every a∈A⟩.
(9)
The new generator t is its stable letter, and A,B are its associated subgroups of an HNN extension. We prove the normal form theorem for an HNN extension, which also proves that the natural map G→G∗ϕ​ is injective.
Set C+​=A, C−​=B, ψ+​=ϕ, and ψ−​=ϕ−1. For each sign ϵ, choose a right coset transversal Rϵ​ for Cϵ​\G, containing 1 as the representative of Cϵ​. Thus every g∈G has a unique decomposition g=cr, with c∈Cϵ​ and r∈Rϵ​. A normal form is
g0​tϵ1​r1​⋯tϵk​rk​,ri​∈Rϵi​​,ri​=1 if i<k and ϵi+1​=−ϵi​,​
(10)
where g0​∈G is unrestricted and the case k=0 is just g0​.
Here is a constructive proof. Let N be the set of these formal sequences. Left multiplication by h∈G changes only g0​ to hg0​. To prepend tϵ, uniquely decompose g0​=cr using Rϵ​ and use
tϵcr=ψϵ​(c)tϵr.
(11)
If the old first stable letter is t−ϵ and r=1, cancel this pair and multiply ψϵ​(c) into the now-leading coefficient r1​. Otherwise retain the new first stable letter. Both cases give a sequence in N; after a cancellation the leading coefficient is unrestricted, so no further normalization is needed at that end. Prepending the letters of a word from right to left proves existence.
These operations define permutations of N. The base-group operations satisfy Lh​Lh′​=Lhh′​. The operations Lt+​ and Lt−​ are inverse: if prepending did not cancel, the reverse operation decomposes the new leading coefficient ψϵ​(c) with representative 1 and cancels the newly inserted letter; if it did cancel, the reverse operation decomposes ψϵ​(c)r1​ with representative r1​, restoring the original prefix. The normal-form restriction rules out an unwanted second cancellation in the latter case.
For a∈A, decomposing ag0​ uses the same representative as decomposing g0​, and changes its subgroup coefficient from c to ac. Both the cancelling and noncancelling cases therefore give
Lt​La​=Lϕ(a)​Lt​.
(12)
Thus all defining relations act identically on N, and we obtain a group action of the presented HNN extension. Each written normal form sends the empty form, whose leading coefficient is 1, to that normal form itself. Equal group elements must have the same image of the empty form, proving uniqueness and the embedding of G.
A reduced sequence in an HNN extension is a word g0​tϵ1​g1​⋯tϵk​gk​ containing no pinch in an HNN extension: no tat−1 with a∈A and no t−1bt with b∈B. Equivalently, whenever ϵi+1​=−ϵi​, one requires gi​∈/Cϵi​​.
Britton's lemma states that
a reduced sequence with k>0 is not the identity.​
(13)
More strongly it cannot represent an element of G. To prove this from the normal form theorem for an HNN extension, normalize the coefficients from right to left. Splitting gi​=cr moves ψϵi​​(c) to the coefficient immediately on its left. If the preceding stable letter has the opposite sign, this transported factor belongs to the subgroup relevant to that inverse pair; multiplying by it cannot turn a coefficient outside that subgroup into one inside it. If the signs agree, cancellation is impossible anyway. Hence no stable letter disappears during normalization of a reduced sequence. Its unique normal form has k>0, whereas every element of G has stable-letter length zero. This proves Britton's lemma. The same proof works with finitely many stable letters, with pinches requiring the same letter and its inverse.
