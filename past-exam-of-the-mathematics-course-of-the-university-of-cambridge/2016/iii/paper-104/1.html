The free product G1​∗G2​ is obtained by taking disjoint copies of the elements of the two groups as generators and imposing precisely their internal multiplication relations. Equivalently, if Gi​=⟨Xi​∣Ri​⟩ with disjoint Xi​, then
G1​∗G2​=⟨X1​⊔X2​∣R1​⊔R2​⟩.
(1)
A nontrivial free product has both factors different from the trivial group.
The normal form theorem for a free product says that every element has a unique expression
g1​g2​⋯gr​,gj​∈Gϵj​​∖{1},ϵj​=ϵj+1​,​
(2)
where the empty expression represents the identity. The gj​ are called syllables in a free product. Thus nonempty alternating products cannot be the identity, and the natural maps of the factors into the free product are injective.
For existence, multiply adjacent syllables from the same factor and delete identity syllables until the word alternates. Each change uses a defining relation and decreases the number of syllables. To establish uniqueness without assuming that these reductions are confluent, let W be the set of alternating nonidentity syllable sequences, including the empty sequence. For h∈Gi​, define a permutation Lh​ of W by prepending h, multiplying into the first syllable if it belongs to Gi​, and deleting it if that product is the identity; for h=1, do nothing.
The multiplication law inside Gi​ gives Lh​Lk​=Lhk​ for h,k∈Gi​, and Lh−1​=Lh−1​. This can be checked at the first syllable: if a product deletes it, the next syllable belongs to the other factor and is treated as a new first syllable. Hence the defining relations give a group action of G1​∗G2​ on W. An alternating word g1​⋯gr​ sends the empty sequence to its own syllable sequence. Two such words representing the same group element induce the same permutation, and therefore have identical sequences. This proves the normal form theorem for a free product.
The universal property of a free product states that, for group homomorphisms fi​:Gi​→H, there is a unique group homomorphism f:G1​∗G2​→H extending both. Explicitly,
f(g1​⋯gr​)=fϵ1​​(g1​)⋯fϵr​​(gr​).​
(3)
The internal multiplication relations are respected because each fi​ is a group homomorphism, so this assignment descends from words to the presented group. Alternatively, multiplication of two normal forms is concatenation followed by precisely those internal relations, which do not change its value in H. Uniqueness holds because the two factors generate the free product.
