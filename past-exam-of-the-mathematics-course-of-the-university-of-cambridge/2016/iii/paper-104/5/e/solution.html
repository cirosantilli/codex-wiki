Introduce stable letters ri​ for i∈I and ℓj​ for j∈J, using ri−1​(⋅)ri​ and ℓj−1​(⋅)ℓj​ to implement the maps. Thus KM​ is the multiple HNN extension of K with the finite presentation
KM​=⟨x,y,t,ri​,ℓj​∣​[x,y]=1,ri−1​xmri​=xm2,ri−1​ymri​=y,ri−1​t(ai​,bi​)ri​=t(ci​,0),ℓj−1​xmℓj​=x,ℓj−1​ymℓj​=ym2,ℓj−1​t(aj​,bj​)ℓj​=t(0,cj​)⟩.​

Here each family ranges over its finite instruction set. The enlarged subgroup denoted by ⟨t⟩′ is
L=⟨t⟩′=⟨t,ri​,ℓj​:i∈I,j∈J⟩≤KM​.​
(39)
The prime means this enlarged subgroup, not the commutator subgroup or the unrestricted normal closure of t.
The key relationship is
(r,s)∈H0​(M)⟺t(r,s)∈L(r,s≥0).​
(40)
Here is a justification that also identifies the relevant intersection. Set
TH​=⟨t(r,s):(r,s)∈H0​(M)⟩≤TK​,BH​=⟨TH​,ri​,ℓj​⟩.
(41)
The basis calculation in the preceding part shows that intersections of TH​ with associated subgroups are generated by exactly the halting basis elements whose indices satisfy the appropriate congruences. Since the machine is deterministic and (0,0) is terminal, halting at (0,0) is equivalent for the two ends of every instruction edge. Therefore ϕi​ and φj​ map these intersections onto the corresponding intersections in their targets. The same assertion holds on the integer lattices after declaring pairs with a negative coordinate nonhalting: the bounds on a,b,c imply that each coordinate's sign is preserved by a transition and its inverse.
Apply Britton's lemma to a word in BH​ which represents an element of K. If it has stable letters, it must have a pinch. The base coefficient of that pinch lies in TH​ and an associated subgroup, and the intersection property just proved makes its transported image lie in TH​ again. Successive pinch removals therefore keep all base coefficients in TH​, and eventually remove every stable letter. It follows that
K∩BH​=TH​.
(42)
Finally, t=t(0,0) belongs to TH​, so L⊆BH​. In the opposite direction, induction backwards along any computation ending at (0,0) shows that every halting basis element lies in L: if t(r′,s′) for the next configuration is in L, then the transition identity expresses t(r,s) as ri​t(r′,s′)ri−1​ or ℓj​t(r′,s′)ℓj−1​. Thus BH​=L and
K∩L=TH​.
(43)
Because TH​ is generated by a subset of a free basis of a group of TK​, a single basis element t(r,s) belongs to it exactly when (r,s) is a halting index. This proves the boxed relationship.
