For M>a≥0 and N>b≥0, define
Ka,bM,N​=⟨xM,yN,t(a,b)⟩≤K.​
(31)
The normal form theorem for a free product gives
Ka,bM,N​≅⟨X,Y,T∣XY=YX⟩≅Z2∗Z,
(32)
where X,Y,T map to xM,yN,t(a,b). To see injectivity, expand an alternating word in ⟨xM,yN⟩ and powers of t(a,b). The conjugating x,y factors commute with the lattice syllables, so each intervening nonidentity lattice syllable stays nonidentity in the expanded free-product normal form.
Its intersection with TK​ is the free group with basis
Ka,bM,N​∩TK​=⟨t(Mu+a,Nv+b):u,v∈Z⟩.
(33)
This follows by moving all xM,yN factors to the right; the zero-exponent lattice remainder characterizes membership in the kernel.
The required isomorphisms are specified on the three generators by
ϕi​:xm↦xm2,ym↦y,t(ai​,bi​)↦t(ci​,0),​
(34)
and
φj​:xm↦x,ym↦ym2,t(aj​,bj​)↦t(0,cj​).​
(35)
Each source and target has the same presentation ⟨X,Y,T∣[X,Y]=1⟩, and each displayed assignment identifies its three abstract generators. Hence these are genuine isomorphisms, not merely maps of generating sets.
On the kernel basis they give exactly the machine transitions:
ϕi​(t(mu+ai​,mv+bi​))=t(m2u+ci​,v),
(36)
φj​(t(mu+aj​,mv+bj​))=t(u,m2v+cj​).
(37)
For example, rewrite the first input as x−muy−mvt(ai​,bi​)xmuymv and apply ϕi​ to each factor. These identities also hold for negative u,v, since they are identities in the groups.
