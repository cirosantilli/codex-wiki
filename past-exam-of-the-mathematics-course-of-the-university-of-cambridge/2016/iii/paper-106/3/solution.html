The Radon-Nikodym theorem for positive measures says: if μ and ν are sigma-finite measures on the same measurable space, and ν is absolutely continuous with respect to μ, then there is a nonnegative measurable function g, unique μ-almost everywhere, such that

The function g=dν/dμ is the Radon-Nikodym derivative. For a finite signed or complex measure ν of finite total variation norm of a measure, absolutely continuous with respect to a sigma-finite μ, the corresponding density belongs to L1(μ). This follows by applying the positive theorem to the positive and negative parts of the real and imaginary parts of ν.
