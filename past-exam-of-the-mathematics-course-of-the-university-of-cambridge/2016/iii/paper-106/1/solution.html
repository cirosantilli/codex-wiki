Invertibility is stable under sufficiently small perturbations, and inversion is continuous. Let 1 be the identity of the complex unital Banach algebra A, with its submultiplicative algebra norm. For ∥h∥<1, completeness makes the Neumann series
(1−h)−1=∑n=0∞​hn
(1)
converge in A. Multiplying either side of the partial sums by 1−h gives 1−hN+1, which tends to 1, proving that the sum is a two-sided inverse.
Fix a∈G(A), the group of invertible elements of a Banach algebra. If ∥b−a∥<∥a−1∥−1, then
b=a(1+a−1(b−a)),∥a−1(b−a)∥<1,
(2)
so the Neumann series makes b invertible. This proves that G(A) is open. It also gives a local bound
∥b−1∥≤1−∥a−1∥∥b−a∥∥1∥∥a−1∥​.
(3)
We retain ∥1∥ here so the estimate does not silently assume a normalized identity. The inverse identity
b−1−a−1=b−1(a−b)a−1
(4)
then implies
∥b−1−a−1∥≤∥b−1∥∥b−a∥∥a−1∥⟶0(b→a).
(5)
This proves continuity of inversion in a Banach algebra.
For a unital Banach algebra, the spectrum of an element is
σA​(x)={λ∈C:λ1−x∈/G(A)}.
(6)
For a nonunital Banach algebra, use its unitization of an algebra A+=A⊕C with multiplication and norm
(a,λ)(b,μ)=(ab+λb+μa,λμ),∥(a,λ)∥=∥a∥+∣λ∣.
(7)
It is a unital Banach algebra, with identity (0,1), and define σA​(x)=σA+​((x,0)). In this nonunital convention, 0 belongs to the spectrum: the scalar coordinate of (x,0) is zero, so it cannot be invertible in A+.
The spectrum is nonempty and compact. It suffices to work in a nonzero unital Banach algebra, since unitization reduces the other case to this one. The group of invertible elements of a Banach algebra is open, so the complement of the spectrum of an element is open. For ∣λ∣>∥x∥, the Neumann series gives
R(λ)=(λ1−x)−1=λ1​∑n=0∞​(λx​)n,∥R(λ)∥≤∣λ∣−∥x∥∥1∥​.
(8)
Thus σA​(x) is closed and lies in {λ:∣λ∣≤∥x∥}, hence is compact.
For nonemptiness of the Banach-algebra spectrum, suppose that R were defined on all of C. At any λ0​, factoring λ1−x=(λ0​1−x)(1+(λ−λ0​)R(λ0​)) gives a locally convergent power series
R(λ)=∑n=0∞​(−1)n(λ−λ0​)nR(λ0​)n+1.
(9)
For every bounded linear functional ℓ∈A∗, the scalar function ℓ(R(λ)) is therefore entire. The bound at infinity makes it bounded outside a disk, while continuity makes it bounded on the disk. By the Liouville theorem, it is constant; since it tends to zero at infinity, it is identically zero. The version of the Hahn-Banach theorem used here says that bounded linear functionals separate points of a normed vector space: if z=0, there is ℓ with ℓ(z)=0. Consequently R(λ)=0 for every λ, contradicting (λ1−x)R(λ)=1. This proves the assertion.
Every nonzero complex unital normed division algebra is algebraically and topologically isomorphic to C. This is the Gelfand-Mazur theorem; completeness is not needed in its statement. Let D be a normed division algebra, and take its completion of a normed space using the algebra norm to obtain a unital Banach algebra D. Submultiplicativity extends multiplication continuously to the completion, and the original identity remains its identity.
For x∈D, the spectrum of an element of x in D contains some λ. If x−λ1=0 in D, the division-algebra assumption supplies an inverse in D, which is still an inverse in D. This contradicts λ∈σD​(x). Therefore x=λ1. The map λ↦λ1 is a bijective complex algebra homomorphism, and
∥λ1∥=∣λ∣∥1∥.
(10)
It and its inverse are continuous; when ∥1∥=1, it is an isometry.
A complete algebra norm on a function algebra dominates the supremum norm, even before continuity of point evaluations is known. Let A be the given algebra of functions. Form the same artificial unitization A+ even if A already has an identity. For each t∈K, the algebraic map
χt​:A+→C,χt​(f,λ)=f(t)+λ
(11)
is a unital multiplicative complex linear functional. No continuity has been assumed. Since χt​(f−f(t)1)=0, the element f−f(t)1 cannot be invertible: applying χt​ to an inverse equation would give 0=1. Hence f(t)∈σA+​(f), and the spectrum bound already proved yields
∣f(t)∣≤∥f∥(t∈K).
(12)
Taking the supremum proves the supremum bound for a complete function-algebra norm:
t∈Ksup​∣f(t)∣≤∥f∥.​
(13)
In particular every f is bounded, and every point evaluation is a bounded linear functional of norm at most 1. This is an instance of automatic continuity of characters: a character of an algebra on a Banach algebra is bounded because its value at any element belongs to that element's spectrum.
