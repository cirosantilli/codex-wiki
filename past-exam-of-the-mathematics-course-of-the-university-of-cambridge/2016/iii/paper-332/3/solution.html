Take z upward from the horizontal bed and measure x away from an ice divide. In the shallow-ice approximation, the hydrostatic pressure is p=pa​+ρg(h−z) and the horizontal balance for a Newtonian fluid is

u(z)=−μρg​hx​(βh​+hz−2z2​),ub​=−μβρg​hhx​.​
(27)
The first term is the uniform basal sliding contribution, while the quadratic term is internal viscous deformation. Both are positive where the thickness decreases downstream.

No ice accumulation or ice ablation appears. For a symmetric glacier, work on one half, 0≤x≤xN​(t), with q(0,t)=0, h(xN​,t)=0 and no outgoing volume flux. Its conserved half-volume per unit width is Vg​=∫0xN​​hdx; the full glacier has volume 2Vg​. This specifies the volume convention rather than silently supplying an unspecified value. For a general finite release on the whole line, the late similarity solution is centred on its conserved centre of mass and uses half its total volume for Vg​.

The equations model the broad shallow bulk; the very tip can require physics beyond that approximation.
The two limiting equations are porous medium equations of the form Hτ​=(HnHX​)X​, with n=3 for a thick shear-dominated glacier and n=2 for a thin sliding-dominated glacier. These are asymptotic regime profiles; they solve the respective limiting equations, not the full sum of both mobilities. For arbitrary finite-width initial data they are appropriate after the corresponding spatial relaxation, with a virtual time origin depending on that data.
The shear-to-slip transition in a shallow ice current occurs at thickness of order H∗​. Using equality at the centre of the early similarity solution gives

Other reasonable definitions of typical thickness change the order-one coefficient; the initial thickness alone does not uniquely determine an exact transition time or an exact initial profile. The robust nondimensional estimate is a transition time of order one in the T∗​ scale.
Finally, let a local nose translate steadily at positive speed v and write s=xN​−x≥0. This is a local traveling nose of a glacier with linear basal drag; the speed of the globally spreading glacier changes slowly with time. The local mass conservation equation integrates to q=vh, since both q and h vanish at the front. Therefore
v=μρg​(3h2​+βh​)dsdh​,s=μvρg​(9h3​+2βh2​).​
(36)
The right side is strictly increasing, giving a unique positive thickness at every s>0. With S=XN​−X and c=dXN​/dτ, the same relation reads cS=H3/3+H2/2. Its limiting profiles are
h∼(ρg2μβvs​)1/2(h≪3/β),h∼(ρg9μvs​)1/3(h≫3/β).​
(37)
The square-root tip reflects basal sliding; the cube-root thicker region reflects internal viscous deformation. Even during the early thick regime, the very nose is thin and has the square-root inner form. At late times that sliding balance governs nearly the whole profile. The dimensionless crossover thickness is H=1 and its distance is S=5/(6c); the early thick cube-root outer profile matches this smaller sliding region. The diverging geometric slope at the idealized tip also marks the local limit of the long-wave shallow-ice approximation.
