Take z positive downwards from the roof. Write T0​=Tm​−mC0​, CB​=(Tm​−TB​)/m, ΔT=T0​−TB​=m(CB​−C0​)>0 and ΔC=CB​−C0​. The cooled magma follows the decreasing liquidus from (C0​,T0​) towards (CB​,TB​); the initially superheated liquid starts at (C0​,T∞​) above that liquidus. The solid has zero solute concentration. At each temperature in the mushy layer, a horizontal tie line joins the pure solid to the residual liquid on the liquidus. The lever rule gives the liquid fraction from the bulk concentration. Since TB​>TE​, this path stops before the eutectic temperature and does not cross the eutectic composition.
The porosity ϕ denotes the liquid fraction, so 1−ϕ is the solid fraction. Equal phase mass densities remove volume-change flow; the stable density stratification suppresses convection. In the stagnant mushy-layer model, define bulk specific enthalpy and bulk solute concentration by
E=cp​T+Lϕ,B=ϕC+(1−ϕ)Cs​=ϕC.
(1)
The arbitrary reference of specific enthalpy has been omitted. The two conservation laws in 0<z<h(t) are
Tt​+cp​L​ϕt​=κTzz​,(ϕC)t​=0,T=Tm​−mC.​
(2)
Here κ=kT​/(ρcp​) is the thermal diffusivity, with kT​ the thermal conductivity. The positive Lϕt​ term records increased specific enthalpy on melting; during freezing it supplies latent heat. In the liquid z>h(t), the heat equation and solute diffusion equation are
Tt​=κTzz​,Ct​=DCzz​,ϕ=1,
(3)
where D is the solutal diffusivity. Neglecting solute diffusion inside the mushy layer does not permit dropping DCzz​ in the liquid at this stage.
Use T(0,t)=TB​, liquid T→T∞​ and C→C0​ as z→∞, and initially h=0, T=T∞​, C=C0​ for z>0. These are the half-space boundary conditions appropriate before cooling reaches the bottom of the chamber. For a closed chamber of finite depth H, replace the far-field conditions by the specified bottom conditions, for example Tz​=Cz​=0 at an insulated impermeable floor; the half-space similarity solution then ceases to apply when the thermal penetration depth approaches H.
At z=h(t), let Ci​ and Ti​=Tm​−mCi​ be the common liquid concentration and temperature, and let ϕi​ be the limiting porosity on the mush side. Continuity of T,C, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition for solute, and the interfacial conservation of energy give
−DCz​(h+,t)=h˙(1−ϕi​)Ci​,κ[Tz​(h−,t)−Tz​(h+,t)]=cp​L​h˙(1−ϕi​).
(4)
The first condition has the sign of solute rejection into the liquid. For the sharp-edge stagnant mushy-layer model with nonzero liquid solutal diffusivity, close the free-boundary problem by marginal equilibrium at a mush-liquid boundary:
Tz​(h+,t)=−mCz​(h+,t),T(z,t)≥Tm​−mC(z,t)(z>h).
(5)
This prevents constitutional supercooling immediately ahead of the mushy layer. A small solid fraction jump at its edge is allowed: imposing ϕi​=1 as well as these finite-D conditions would generally overdetermine the reduced model. At the roof, C=CB​ follows from the liquidus, and no additional solute-flux boundary condition is needed in the diffusion-free mush.
To obtain the large-concentration similarity solution, put
θ=ΔTT−T0​​,θ∞​=ΔTT∞​−T0​​>0,C=ΔCC0​​,S=cp​ΔTL​,r=κD​,η=2κt​z​,h=2λκt​.
(6)
The positive superheat specifies the physically intended liquid initial state. On a similarity solution, Ci​,ϕi​ are constant, so every newly incorporated level leaves the same constant B=ϕi​Ci​. Write Ci​=C0​+ΔCδ and B=ΔC(C+a). Then throughout the mushy layer
C=C0​−ΔCθ,ϕ=C−θC+a​,[1+S(C−θ)2C+a​]θt​=κθzz​.
(7)
For a,θ=O(1), C≫1, and S/C=O(1), the effective heat capacity is constant to relative error O(C−1). Thus set
Ω=1+S/C​,Ω2θt​=κθzz​.​
(8)
Integration of the similarity solution ordinary differential equation gives the leading analytical profiles
θm​=−1+(1−δ)erf(Ωλ)erf(Ωη)​,θl​=θ∞​−(θ∞​+δ)erfcλerfcη​.​
(9)
The temperature is T=T0​+ΔTθ. In the mushy layer, the concentration and porosity are the displayed C,ϕ; in the liquid,
Cl​=C0​+ΔCδerfc(λ/r​)erfc(η/r​)​,ϕl​=1.​
(10)
These retain the liquid solutal boundary layer. For completeness, finite-r values of δ,a,λ are determined analytically, to the same large-C order, by setting
K(x)=erfcxe−x2​,Ar​=π​λr​K(λ/r​)​,δ=K(λ/r​)/r​−K(λ)θ∞​K(λ)​,b=δAr​,a=δ−b,
(11)
and solving
(1−δ)erf(Ωλ)Ωe−Ω2λ2​−(θ∞​+δ)K(λ)=π​CS​λb.
(12)
The solute jump gives ϕi​=1−b/(C+δ) exactly; replacing C by C+δ on the right of the last equation retains that jump's next-order factor, but does not make the linearized thermal profiles exact at finite C. The branch has 0≤δ<1, 0<ϕ≤1. All these profiles are leading asymptotic expressions, not exact solutions of the nonlinear finite-C heat equation.
For r→0 at fixed positive λ, the complementary error function asymptotic gives K(λ/r​)∼π​λ/r​, hence δ=O(r), b=O(r), a→0, and ϕi​→1. The result simplifies to
θm​=−1+erf(Ωλ)erf(Ωη)​,θl​=θ∞​(1−erfcλerfcη​),Cm​=C0​−ΔCθm​,ϕm​=C−θm​C​,Cl​=C0​,h(t)=2λκt​.​​
(13)
In particular, ϕ(0,t)=C0​/CB​=C/(C+1) and ϕ(h−,t)=1. There is no finite interfacial latent heat jump in this limit: the latent heat is released continuously throughout the mushy layer. Matching the two temperature gradients gives
erf(Ωλ)Ωe−Ω2λ2​=erfcλθ∞​e−λ2​,Ωeλ2erfcλ=θ∞​eΩ2λ2erf(Ωλ).​
(14)
The printed final equation is missing the square on Ω in the exponential. The corrected exponent follows directly from differentiating erf(Ωη) and is consistent with Ω2=1+S/C. The positive root is unique: the mush-side gradient decreases from infinity to zero with λ, whereas θ∞​K(λ) increases. The zero-superheat limit is singular and is not described by a finite positive λ in this two-region construction.
