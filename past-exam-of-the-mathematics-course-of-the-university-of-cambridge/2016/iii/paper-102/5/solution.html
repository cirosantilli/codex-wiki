An abstract root system, in the reduced crystallographic convention, is a finite subset Φ of a finite-dimensional real Euclidean space E satisfying the following conditions:
  • 0∈/Φ and Φ spans E.
  • For each α∈Φ, Φ∩Rα={α,−α}; this is the reduced condition.
  • The root reflection sα​(v)=v−2(v,α)α/(α,α) preserves Φ.
  • Every Cartan integer ⟨β,α∨⟩=2(β,α)/(α,α) is an integer.
The root system is irreducible if it is not a union of two nonempty mutually orthogonal root systems. The integrality condition is essential for the finite angle list below; arbitrary finite reflection systems need not satisfy it.
The possible angles are 0, π/6, π/4, π/3, π/2, 2π/3, 3π/4, 5π/6, and π. For roots α,β, put

For the last column, the possible absolute pairs (∣a∣,∣b∣) are (1,1), (1,2), and (1,3), up to interchange, and a/b=(β,β)/(α,α). Parallel roots are equal or opposite by reducedness, giving 0 or π. Thus the list is exhaustive, and the rank-two root systems realize each nonparallel possibility.
Now suppose two simple roots α,β have angle 5π/6. Distinct simple roots have nonpositive inner product. Here is a proof from their defining sign property: if (δ,ϵ)>0, then sδ​(ϵ)=ϵ−nδ with a positive Cartan integer n. This would be a root whose coefficients in the root basis include both a positive and a negative coefficient, which is impossible. Therefore, after normalizing the simple roots to unit length, any nonzero off-diagonal inner product is at most −1/2, by the angle list.
Let u=α/∥α∥, v=β/∥β∥, so (u,v)=−3​/2. If there were a third simple root with unit vector w, write r=(u,w)≤0 and s=(v,w)≤0. Independence of the simple roots makes their Gram matrix positive definite, so

If either r or s were nonzero, its absolute value would be at least 1/2. Since rs≥0, the displayed determinant would then be nonpositive. Thus r=s=0. Every other simple root is orthogonal to both α and β: a triple bond isolates a G2 component.
Indeed, the root reflections in α,β preserve their plane and fix its orthogonal complement; the root reflections in the other simple roots preserve that complement and fix the plane. The given facts that these reflections generate the Weyl group and that every root is Weyl group-conjugate to a simple root imply that every root lies entirely in one of those two orthogonal subspaces. If there were any other simple root, this would make Φ reducible. Therefore irreducibility forces rank two.
Up to interchanging the simple roots, take α short and β long. The squared-length ratio is 3. Choose a scale and orthonormal coordinates so that
α=(1,0),β=(−23​,23​​).
(59)
The Cartan integers are ⟨β,α∨⟩=−3 and ⟨α,β∨⟩=−1. In coefficient coordinates mα+nβ, the two root reflections are
sα​(m,n)=(−m+3n,n),sβ​(m,n)=(m,m−n).
(60)
Their orbits of α and β are, respectively,
{±α,±(α+β),±(2α+β)},{±β,±(3α+β),±(3α+2β)}.
(61)
These sets are closed under both displayed reflections; for example sβ​(α)=α+β, sα​(α+β)=2α+β, sα​(β)=3α+β, and sβ​(3α+β)=3α+2β. The assumption that every root is conjugate to a simple root now determines all twelve roots, with no further ones possible.
For completeness, this twelve-element set really is a root system. It is finite, spans the plane, and is reduced. The six short roots have length 1 and directions spaced by π/3; the six long roots have length 3​ and interleaved directions, also spaced by π/3. Their mutual Cartan integers are integral: within either length class they are 0, ±1, or ±2 as applicable, and between the two length classes they are 0, ±1, or ±3. Closure under all root reflections follows by conjugating the two simple reflections, since every listed root lies in a simple-root orbit. The nonorthogonal simple roots make it irreducible. We have therefore proved existence as well as uniqueness, up to isometry and overall scale:
The unique irreducible type is the G2​ root system.​
(62)
