Start with the tensor product of Lie algebra representations, whose action is
x⋅(v⊗w)=(xv)⊗w+v⊗(xw).
(39)
Writing Tx​=ρ(x)⊗I+I⊗ρ(x), the two tensor factors commute, so [Tx​,Ty​]=T[x,y]​. This verifies the Lie algebra representation identity over every field.
The exterior square and symmetric square are the quotient vector spaces
⋀2V=(V⊗V)/⟨v⊗v:v∈V⟩,S2V=(V⊗V)/⟨v⊗w−w⊗v:v,w∈V⟩.
(40)
In the exterior square, expanding (v+w)⊗(v+w) shows that v∧w=−w∧v, including in characteristic two. Both defining relation spaces are invariant under the tensor product action: x(v⊗v)=xv⊗v+v⊗xv is an exterior relation, and the image of a symmetric relation is a sum of symmetric relations. Thus the quotient actions are well-defined and satisfy
x(v∧w)=xv∧w+v∧xw,x(vw)=(xv)w+v(xw).
(41)
For the printed basis v1​,…,vn​, bases are vi​∧vj​ with i<j and vi​vj​ with i≤j. Their dimensions are n(n−1)/2 and n(n+1)/2 respectively.
If 2 is invertible in k, V⊗V≅⋀2V⊕S2V as representations. Define the flip τ(v⊗w)=w⊗v. It commutes with the Lie algebra action and satisfies τ2=I. Therefore
P+​=2I+τ​,P−​=2I−τ​
(42)
are complementary invariant linear projections. The maps
vw⟼2v⊗w+w⊗v​,v∧w⟼2v⊗w−w⊗v​
(43)
identify S2V with imP+​ and ⋀2V with imP−​. Their inverses are the corresponding quotient maps restricted to these subspaces. This proves the assertion for every field of odd characteristic, and also for characteristic zero.
Over every field, S2(V⊕W)≅S2V⊕S2W⊕(V⊗W). The symmetric square of a direct sum isomorphism sends the first two summands into products within V and within W, and sends v⊗w to the mixed product vw. If vi​ and wj​ are bases, the monomial basis of S2(V⊕W) is the disjoint union
{vi​vj​:i≤j},{wi​wj​:i≤j},{vi​wj​:all i,j}.
(44)
Thus the map is bijective, with no division by 2 needed. The Leibniz rule for the action preserves each of these three summands and agrees with its usual Lie algebra representation action, proving equivariance.
For g=sl(3), V=Γ0,0​ is trivial and W=Γ2,1​ has dimension 15. Hence
S2(V⊕W)≅Γ0,0​⊕Γ2,1​⊕S2Γ2,1​.
(45)
It remains to find the irreducible representations in the symmetric square of the sl3 representation of highest weight (2,1). We give the formal character calculation explicitly.
Let E=C3 be the defining special linear Lie algebra representation. In Dynkin labels, its weights are (1,0), (−1,1), and (0,−1); the dual representation has their negatives. The equivariant contraction
c:S2E⊗E∗⟶E,c(uv⊗f)=f(u)v+f(v)u
(46)
is surjective. Its kernel has dimension 18−3=15. The tensor e12​⊗e3∗​ is a highest-weight vector of highest weight (2,1) in that kernel. By the Weyl complete reducibility theorem, the kernel contains the irreducible representation Γ2,1​, whose Weyl dimension formula gives dimension 15; therefore the kernel equals Γ2,1​. This yields
χW​=χS2E​χE∗​−χE​.
(47)
Multiplying the six weights of S2E by the three weights of E∗ and subtracting those of E gives the following full weight multiplicity list:
multiplicity12​weights in Dynkin labels(2,1),(3,−1),(2,−2),(1,−3),(0,2),(−1,−2),(−2,3),(−2,0),(−3,2)(1,0),(0,−1),(−1,1)​​
(48)
The multiplicities sum to 9+2⋅3=15.
For any finite-dimensional weight-space decomposition, a weight μ of multiplicity mμ​ contributes mμ​(mμ​+1)/2 to weight 2μ in its symmetric square. Distinct weights μ,ν contribute mμ​mν​ to μ+ν. Equivalently,
χS2W​(z)=2χW​(z)2+χW​(z2)​.
(49)
Applying this to the displayed list gives all dominant weight multiplicities in the second column below. The remaining columns are the weight multiplicities of the candidate irreducible representations:
weight(4,2)(5,0)(2,3)(3,1)(0,4)(1,2)(2,0)(0,1)​S2W11132589​Γ4,2​11121233​Γ3,1​00010122​Γ0,4​00001111​Γ1,2​00000112​Γ2,0​00000011​​
(50)
For an explicit way to compute each irreducible column, set z1​z2​z3​=1 and use the Weyl character formula in the form
χa,b​(z1​,z2​,z3​)=det(z12​z22​z32​​z1​z2​z3​​111​)det​z1a+b+2​z2a+b+2​z3a+b+2​​z1b+1​z2b+1​z3b+1​​111​​​.
(51)
A monomial z1n1​​z2n2​​z3n3​​ has Dynkin labels (n1​−n2​,n2​−n3​). Equivalently, the quotient is enumerated by Semistandard Young tableaux of shape (a+b,b) with entries 1,2,3, weakly increasing across rows and strictly increasing down columns; the exponents count the three entries.
The five irreducible columns sum to the S2W column. These are all its dominant weights, and all five candidate characters have no other dominant weights. Every Weyl group orbit meets the dominant chamber, and weight multiplicities are constant on Weyl group orbits. Thus the table proves equality of the full formal characters, and the Weyl complete reducibility theorem gives
S2Γ2,1​≅Γ4,2​⊕Γ3,1​⊕Γ0,4​⊕Γ1,2​⊕Γ2,0​.​
(52)
The Weyl dimension formula checks the result:
60+24+15+15+6=120=215⋅16​.
(53)
Consequently the requested decomposition is
S2(Γ0,0​⊕Γ2,1​)≅Γ0,0​⊕Γ2,1​⊕Γ4,2​⊕Γ3,1​⊕Γ0,4​⊕Γ1,2​⊕Γ2,0​.​
(54)
Its total dimension is 1+15+120=136=16⋅17/2.
