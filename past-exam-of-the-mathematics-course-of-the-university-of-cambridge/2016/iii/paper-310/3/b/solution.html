Let T−​ be the residual gas temperature immediately before decay. This is the Td​ that enters the target abundance formula, not the higher reheating temperature after decay. Before the decay, separately conserved relic number and gas entropy give the conserved cosmological relic abundance Y=nϕ​/s−​. The cold nonrelativistic relic has negligible kinetic energy, hence
ρϕ​≃mnϕ​=mYs−​=mc1​g⋆​YT−3​.
(37)
Assume its density dominates over that of the residual gas and that curvature and dark energy are negligible in the Friedmann equation. Using the reduced Planck mass MPl2​=(8πG)−1 gives 3MPl2​Hd2​≃ρϕ​(td−​). Under the allowed sudden decay of a dominant nonrelativistic relic convention Hd​≃Γ=td−1​, the temperature immediately before decay is
Td3​=T−3​≃c1​g⋆​Ymtd2​3MPl2​​.​
(38)
The coefficient inherits the approximation Hd​≃1/td​: the result is a sudden-decay estimate, not an exact age relation for a matter-only universe. If td​ were instead the exact cosmic age in an uninterrupted spatially flat matter era, Hd​=2/(3td​) would replace the numerator 3MPl2​ by 4MPl2​/3.
The assumptions also require thermal decoupling in cosmology and negligible prior decay so that Y remains conserved, a fixed relativistic count g⋆​, and rapid thermalization after the energy release. In the same approximation, the reheating temperature T+​ follows from c2​g⋆​T+4​≃3MPl2​/td2​:
T+​≃(c2​g⋆​td2​3MPl2​​)1/4.
(39)
Its absence of Y at fixed decay rate makes clear why the two temperatures must be distinguished.
