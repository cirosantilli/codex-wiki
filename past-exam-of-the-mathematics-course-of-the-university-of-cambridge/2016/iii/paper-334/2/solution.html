Use u=∂y​ψ and v=−∂x​ψ in the frame translating with the sheet. At each instant, the incompressible Stokes equations are
−∇p+μ∇2u=0,∇⋅u=0.
(12)
Taking their curl gives the biharmonic stream function for planar Stokes flow:
∇4ψ=0​
(13)
in the fluid above the actual sheet. The no-slip boundary condition is imposed on material points, whose label is x and whose actual horizontal position is xs​. With q=x−t,
ψy​(xs​,ys​,t)=−3ϵacos3q,ψx​(xs​,ys​,t)=ϵbcosq.
(14)
The second sign follows from v=∂t​ys​=−ϵbcosq. Far above the sheet,
ψy​→U,ψx​→0,
(15)
with bounded velocity and no imposed mean pressure gradient. The positive far-field velocity arises because the sheet moves with laboratory velocity −Uex​. All fields are 2π-periodic in x; an additive function of time in ψ is a harmless gauge. These conditions formulate the Taylor swimming sheet problem with both types of travelling deformation.
Expand U=ϵU1​+ϵ2U2​+⋯ and ψ=ϵψ1​+ϵ2ψ2​+⋯. At first order the boundary can be evaluated at y=0:
∇4ψ1​=0,ψ1y​(x,0,t)=−3acos3q,ψ1x​(x,0,t)=bcosq.
(16)
The far-field conditions are ψ1y​→U1​ and ψ1x​→0. For a nonzero Fourier mode of wavenumber k, bounded velocity selects (E+Gy)e−kyeikq; the exponentially growing modes are excluded. Matching the modes k=1 and k=3 gives
ψ1​=b(1+y)e−ysinq−3aye−3ycos3q​.
(17)
An additive gauge has been set to zero. The first term is a transverse mode of a Taylor swimming sheet, and the second is a longitudinal mode of a Taylor swimming sheet. Their velocities decay at infinity. The mean first-order boundary velocity is zero, and the bounded zero Fourier mode is a constant plus a multiple of y, so U1​=0.
For the second-order Taylor-expanded no-slip boundary condition, the first-order displacement is
ξ=(asin3q,bsinq).
(18)
There is no explicit second-order material velocity in the prescribed motion. Hence, with all first-order derivatives evaluated at (x,0,t),
∇4ψ2​=0,u2​=−ξ⋅∇u1​,v2​=−ξ⋅∇v1​,
(19)
where uj​=ψjy​ and vj​=−ψjx​. Differentiation of the first-order solution gives
u1x​=9asin3q,u1y​=18acos3q−bsinq,v1x​=bsinq,v1y​=−9asin3q
(20)
on the flat reference boundary. Therefore
u2​(x,0,t)=−9a2sin23q−18absinqcos3q+b2sin2q,v2​(x,0,t)=8absinqsin3q​.
(21)
The boundary conditions for the streamfunction are ψ2y​=u2​ and ψ2x​=−v2​. Their far-field counterparts are ψ2y​→U2​ and ψ2x​→0.
Products of the two distinct first-order Fourier modes generate only the nonzero wavenumbers 2,4,6, as well as a mean. Thus the bounded general solution compatible with these data has the form
ψ2​=C0​(t)+U2​y+Re⎩⎨⎧​k∈{2,4,6}∑​(Ek​+Gk​y)e−kyeikq⎭⎬⎫​​.
(22)
The discarded zero-mode terms y2,y3 would give unbounded velocity, and the discarded nonzero modes grow exponentially. The zero-mean normal velocity in periodic half-space Stokes flow is consistent with a bounded periodic flow and no far-field vertical flux. For example, integrating ψ2x​=−v2​ along the boundary yields
ψ2​(x,0,t)=−2absin2q+absin4q+C0​(t).
(23)
This fixes the Ek​; the nonconstant part of u2​ fixes Gk​−kEk​. All coefficients are thereby determined, but none is needed to find the mean swimming speed.
Indeed, mean boundary velocity determines Taylor-sheet swimming speed. The mean Fourier mode of ψ2​ is C0​+U2​y, so its horizontal velocity is the same at the boundary and infinity. Fourier orthogonality of sheet swimming modes removes the mixed product, giving
U2​=⟨u2​(x,0,t)⟩x​=2b2−9a2​,U=2ϵ2​(b2−9a2)+O(ϵ3)​.
(24)
Here ⟨⋅⟩x​ is the average over one spatial period; a temporal average gives the same answer for these travelling waves. The laboratory swimming velocity is −Uex​. The transverse wave drives motion opposite to its direction of propagation, whereas the longitudinal wave drives it in the propagation direction. Their wavenumbers enter quadratically. Thus b/a=3 cancels the swimming speed at order ϵ2. This different-wavenumber cancellation in sheet swimming is not a claim that all higher-order swimming terms vanish.
