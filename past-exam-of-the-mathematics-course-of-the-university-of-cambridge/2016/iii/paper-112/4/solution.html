Let Xs​ count the copies of the complete graph Ks​ in the binomial random graph Gn,p​. By linearity of the expected value and independence of distinct edges,
EXs​=(sn​)p(2s​).
(49)
Throughout, n is sufficiently large that k=⌊loglogn⌋≥2. We have k→∞, k=O(loglogn) and klogk=o(logn).
First, since p=n−2/k,
EXk+1​=(k+1n​)n−(k+1)≤(k+1)!1​⟶0.
(50)
The first moment method proves P(Xk+1​>0)=o(1).
For the existence of a k-clique, put X=Xk​ and μ=EX. Since p(2k​)=n−(k−1),
μ=k!n​∏i=0k−1​(1−ni​)=k!n​(1+o(1))⟶∞.
(51)
This alone is insufficient: we must control the variance via the overlap formula for the variance of a clique count. Let IS​ indicate that a given k-set S spans a clique. If ∣S∩T∣=j, the two copies share (2j​) edges, so
E(IS​IT​)=p2(2k​)−(2j​).
(52)
For j=0,1 their edge sets are disjoint and the covariance vanishes, even when they share one vertex. Counting ordered pairs of k-sets gives the exact formula
μ2VarX​=∑j=2k​(kn​)(jk​)(k−jn−k​)​(p−(2j​)−1).
(53)
This includes the diagonal case j=k.
To bound the overlap factor, regard it as the probability that a uniformly chosen k-set T intersects a fixed S in exactly j vertices. A union bound over the possible specified j-subsets of S yields
(kn​)(jk​)(k−jn−k​)​≤(jk​)(n)j​(k)j​​≤(n−kk2​)j≤(n2k2​)j.
(54)
Here (a)j​=a(a−1)⋯(a−j+1) is a falling factorial, and the last bound holds for n≥2k. Therefore
μ2VarX​≤∑j=2k​(2k2)jn−j+j(j−1)/k=∑j=2k​(2k2)jn−j(k−j+1)/k.
(55)
For 2≤j≤k, the concave quadratic j(k−j+1) is minimized at an endpoint. Its endpoint values are 2(k−1) and k, both at least k. Thus every power of n in the last sum is at most n−1, and
μ2VarX​≤nk(2k2)k​=o(1),
(56)
because the logarithm of its numerator is O(klogk)=o(logn). The second moment method, or Chebyshev's inequality directly, gives P(X=0)=o(1). Finally, every larger clique contains a (k+1)-clique, so the absence of Kk+1​ excludes all larger sizes. The clique number is exactly k with high probability:
P(ω(Gn,p​)=k)=1−o(1).​
