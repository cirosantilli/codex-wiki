If β<ω1​ is a nonzero limit ordinal, this cofinality is ω, so ℵβ​ is a singular cardinal. The other infinite cardinal numbers below ℵω1​​ are successor-indexed or ℵ0​, and are regular cardinals. For a successor cardinal μ+, a cofinal sequence of length at most μ would express μ+ as a union of at most μ sets of size at most μ, contradicting infinite cardinal arithmetic.
There are ℵ1​ nonzero countable limit ordinals, and each countable initial segment contains only countably many. In increasing order they therefore have order type ω1​. These are precisely the indices of the uncountable singular cardinals below ℵω1​​. Hence every σα​ with α<ω1​ has countable cofinality, and the next one is ℵω1​​, whose cofinality is ω1​<ℵω1​​.
