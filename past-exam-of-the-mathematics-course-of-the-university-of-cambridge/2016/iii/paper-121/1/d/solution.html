The singular cardinal enumeration is strictly increasing and satisfies σα​≥α for every ordinal α; the latter follows by transfinite induction for any strictly increasing ordinal-valued enumeration. If equality occurs at some κn​, that cardinal number is already a witness. Otherwise the sequence is strictly increasing. Put κ=supn<ω​κn​. This is an uncountable singular cardinal of cofinality ω.
For every α<κ, some n has α<κn​, and therefore
σα​<σκn​​=κn+1​<κ.
(9)
Also supn​σκn​​=κ, so supα<κ​σα​=κ. At a limit ordinal index, if the supremum of all preceding enumerated cardinals is itself singular, it is exactly the next member: every smaller singular cardinal already has a preceding index. Thus
σκ​=κ​.
(10)
This argument uses continuity only at a singular supremum; the enumeration need not be continuous at a weakly inaccessible cardinal.
