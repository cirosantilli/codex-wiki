It suffices to obtain a model of ZFC without weakly inaccessible cardinals. Starting with any model of ZFC, pass to its constructible universe, which satisfies ZFC and the Generalized continuum hypothesis. If it has no inaccessible cardinal, use that model. Otherwise pass to its rank segment at its least inaccessible cardinal δ. This segment satisfies ZFC, retains the Generalized continuum hypothesis, and has no inaccessible cardinals. Under the Generalized continuum hypothesis, every weakly inaccessible cardinal is strongly inaccessible: if λ<κ and κ is a limit cardinal, then 2λ=λ+<κ. Thus in either case the resulting model N has no weakly inaccessible cardinals.
Work inside N. If α is a nonzero limit ordinal, let λ=supβ<α​σβ​. It is an uncountable limit cardinal. If it were regular, it would be a weakly inaccessible cardinal, which is impossible in N. It is therefore singular, and the singular cardinal enumeration is continuous at this index:
σα​=supβ<α​σβ​.
(11)
The cofinality of an increasing ordinal supremum now gives
cf(σα​)=cf(α)​
(12)
for every nonzero limit ordinal α in N. Hence N satisfies the negation of the proposed existential assertion. By the soundness theorem for first-order logic, consistency of ZFC prevents ZFC from proving that assertion. The model construction is a relative-consistency argument; it does not assume that consistency alone supplies a countable transitive model. Here, as usual, a limit ordinal excludes zero.
