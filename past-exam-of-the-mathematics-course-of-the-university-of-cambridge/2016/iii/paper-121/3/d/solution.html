Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2016 / iii / Paper 121 / 3 / d / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 iii Paper 121 3 d
OurBigBook.com Words: 130
Weakly inaccessible cardinals are unbounded below the given cardinal. In fact, they form a stationary set there. Let κ be the weakly Mahlo cardinal, so it is a regular uncountable limit cardinal and the set R={α<κ:α=cf(α)} is stationary.
The set C of uncountable limit cardinals below κ is a club set. For unboundedness, above any starting point choose a strictly increasing countable sequence of cardinal numbers below κ; its supremum remains below κ by regularity and is an uncountable limit cardinal. For closure, a limit of such limit cardinals is again a limit cardinal.
Every α∈R∩C is an uncountable regular cardinal and a limit cardinal, hence a weakly inaccessible cardinal. The intersection of a stationary set with a club set is stationary, because its intersection with any further club is nonempty. Therefore
{λ<κ:λ is weakly inaccessible} is stationary, hence unbounded.​
(24)
This proves the stronger form of the requested conclusion.
