 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 iii Paper 121 3 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 135
Fix a regular uncountable cardinal number κ. A club set C⊆κ is unbounded in κ and closed under suprema below κ of nonempty increasing sequences with no last term. Equivalently, every limit ordinal δ<κ with sup(C∩δ)=δ belongs to C. The club filter is
Dκ​={A⊆κ:∃C⊆A with C club in κ}​.
(21)
It consists of all sets containing a club set, not only the club sets themselves.
An omega-measurable cardinal is a cardinal number carrying a nonprincipal ultrafilter closed under countable intersections, that is, a countably complete ultrafilter. Such a cardinal must be uncountable: on a countable set the intersection of the complements of all singletons would be empty.
A measurable cardinal is an uncountable cardinal number κ carrying a nonprincipal κ-complete ultrafilter. A kappa-complete filter is closed under intersections of every family of size strictly below κ. Thus for an omega-measurable cardinal the completeness required is countable completeness, not merely closure under finitely many intersections.
