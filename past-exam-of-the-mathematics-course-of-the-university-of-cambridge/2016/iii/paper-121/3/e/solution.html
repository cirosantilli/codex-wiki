Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2016 / iii / Paper 121 / 3 / e / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 iii Paper 121 3 e
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
Take the least omega-measurable cardinal κ, with a countably complete ultrafilter U on κ that is nonprincipal. We prove that U is κ-complete. This will give κ is measurable​.
Suppose μ<κ, Ai​∈U for i<μ, and ⋂i<μ​Ai​∈/U. Then B=κ∖⋂i<μ​Ai​ belongs to U. For x∈B let g(x) be the least i<μ with x∈/Ai​. The pushforward ultrafilter
W={E⊆μ:g−1[E]∈U}
(25)
is a countably complete ultrafilter on μ; this uses B∈U, so U restricts to an ultrafilter on B. For each i, the fibre g−1[{i}] is contained in κ∖Ai​, hence is not in U. Thus W is nonprincipal. If μ is finite or countable, countable completeness already rules this out. Otherwise ∣μ∣<κ would be an omega-measurable cardinal, contradicting minimality of κ.
Therefore every intersection of fewer than κ members of U lies in U. Since κ is uncountable, it is a measurable cardinal. This proves that the least omega-measurable cardinal is measurable, even if the originally supplied cardinal carried only a countably complete ultrafilter.
