 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 iii Paper 121 4 e
OurBigBook.com Words: 155
Let T be ZFC together with the assertion of unboundedly many inaccessible cardinals. Given a model of T, if it has no inaccessible limit of inaccessibles, it already witnesses the required nonimplication. Otherwise let ι be its least inaccessible limit of inaccessibles and pass to its rank segment Vι​.
Because ι is an inaccessible cardinal, Vι​ satisfies ZFC. Its ordinals are precisely those below ι, and below ι the inaccessible cardinals are unbounded by the choice of ι. Inaccessibility of any smaller ordinal is absolute to this segment: it has the same subsets and functions on all smaller ordinals, as in strong-inaccessibility absoluteness from rank agreement. Thus Vι​ satisfies the unbounded-inaccessibles assertion.
No smaller ordinal can be an inaccessible limit of inaccessibles, by minimality of ι and the same absoluteness. Also ι is not an ordinal of Vι​ itself. Consequently
Vι​⊨T+¬ILI​.
(35)
This rank cutoff at the first inaccessible limit of inaccessibles proves Con(T)⇒Con(T+¬ILI). The soundness theorem for first-order logic then gives T⊢ILI if T is consistent. This is a model-theoretic relative-consistency argument, and does not infer external transitivity from mere consistency.
