The continuum hypothesis holds. Let A be the assumed Lusin set of cardinality c=2ℵ0​. If c>ℵ1​, choose B⊆A with ∣B∣=ℵ1​. The size assumption makes B a meagre set, but then A∩B=B is uncountable, contradicting the Lusin set property. Thus
2ℵ0​=ℵ1​​.
(20)
One can also use (d) directly: well-order A in type c. Each proper initial segment has size below c, so is meagre and consequently countable, since it is a subset of the Lusin set. These initial segments form a linear increasing union of countable sets equal to A. The bound in (d) gives c≤ℵ1​.
