Here a formal group means a one-dimensional commutative formal group law. Over a ring R, it is a formal power series F(X,Y)∈R[[X,Y]] satisfying
F(X,0)=X,F(0,Y)=Y,F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)),F(X,Y)=F(Y,X).
(26)
In particular, F(X,Y)=X+Y+ terms of total degree at least 2. A formal inverse ι(T)=−T+⋯ with F(T,ι(T))=0 is obtained recursively. An isomorphism of formal group laws from F to G is a series h(T)∈TR[[T]] with invertible linear coefficient and
h(F(X,Y))=G(h(X),h(Y)).
(27)
Its compositional inverse exists over R by coefficient recursion.
For R=OK​, all these series converge on πOK​; the operations preserve that set and make it the group F(πOK​). Use the uniformizer π and normalize the valuation by vK​(π)=1, and write e=vK​(p).

This is a formal series over K with linear coefficient 1. Differentiating associativity in its last variable at Z=0 gives

It follows that the Y-derivative of logF​(F(X,Y)) is 1/f(Y), the same as that of logF​(Y). Evaluating at Y=0 therefore proves
logF​(F(X,Y))=logF​(X)+logF​(Y).​

The differential dT/f(T) is the invariant differential of a formal group law.

For any a∈OK​, the equation H(U)=a is equivalent to U=a−πR(U), a strict contraction, to which the contraction mapping theorem applies on the complete ring OK​. It has exactly one solution.
The deep logarithm subgroup of a formal group is obtained as follows. The formal group exponential is the formal compositional inverse expF​(T) of logF​(T), obtained recursively; equivalently it solves expF′​(T)=f(expF​(T)), with expF​(0)=0. The preceding contraction, applied to the scaled series, constructs its convergent inverse on πmOK​ and agrees with that formal inverse. This supplies the required convergence sketch for expF​ as well as a bijectivity proof for the logarithm.
The integral formal law and inverse preserve πmOK​, so this is a subgroup. The logarithm identity and bijectivity give
