Use the orientation selected by the volume form, and let volg​ be its normalized Riemannian volume form. The Euclidean metric induces an inner product on the exterior algebra of covectors. The Hodge star operator is the unique map from p-forms to (4−p)-forms satisfying
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩g​volg​.
(1)
For an oriented orthonormal coframe e1,…,e4, it sends a basis wedge to the complementary wedge with the sign of the permutation that restores e1∧⋯∧e4. Applying the Hodge star operator twice exchanges blocks of p and 4−p covectors, so
∗2=(−1)p(4−p),∗2=1on two-forms.​
(2)
Here the supplied volume is interpreted in the usual metric-normalized sense. If instead one defines the operator with an arbitrary unnormalized vol=fvolg​, that operator is f∗g​ and its square on two-forms is f2. The usual self-duality statements use the metric-normalized Hodge star operator, with the supplied volume specifying orientation.
The projections P±​=(1±∗)/2 give the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms:
Λ2=Λ+2​⊕Λ−2​,α±​=21​(α±∗α),∗α±​=±α±​.​
(3)
Both spaces have dimension three. With e4=dt, bases are
Σi±​=ei∧dt ± 21​ϵijk​ej∧ek,i=1,2,3.
(4)
The Hodge star operator is an orthogonal involution on two-forms and therefore is self-adjoint. For a self-dual two-form H and an anti-self-dual two-form G,
⟨H,G⟩=⟨∗H,G⟩=⟨H,∗G⟩=−⟨H,G⟩=0.
(5)
Consequently
H∧G=⟨H,∗G⟩volg​=0.​
(6)
This is the wedge orthogonality of opposite-duality two-forms.
For the Yang-Mills action, take an anti-Hermitian special unitary group connection and the fundamental matrix trace, so the positive invariant pairing on its Lie algebra is ⟨X,Y⟩=−tr(XY). Write its gauge curvature as F=F+​+F−​ using the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms, and define
∥F±​∥2=−∫tr(F±​∧∗F±​),SYM​=−gYM2​1​∫tr(F∧∗F).
(7)
Cross terms vanish by the wedge orthogonality of opposite-duality two-forms. Thus
SYM​=gYM2​∥F+​∥2+∥F−​∥2​,∫tr(F∧F)=−∥F+​∥2+∥F−​∥2.
(8)
With this anti-Hermitian convention the Second Chern number is
k=c2​(E)[S4]=8π21​∫tr(F∧F).
(9)
For example, this normalization follows by expanding det(1+iF/(2π)) and using trF=0. Interpreting the integral as an integer Second Chern number on R4 assumes the usual decay and gauge behavior that allow extension over the point at infinity. The following norm inequality itself does not require integrality:
SYM​≥gYM2​8π2​∣k∣.​
(10)
The Yang-Mills instanton Bogomolny bound is saturated precisely when one component vanishes: F=∗F or F=−∗F. In the stated convention the self-dual case has k≤0 and the anti-self-dual case has k≥0. Reversing orientation, or defining topological charge with the opposite sign, reverses this assignment while leaving the absolute-value bound unchanged.
For the self-dual Yang-Mills equations in temporal gauge, choose volg​=dx1∧dx2∧dx3∧dt and ϵ123​=1. The relevant Hodge star operator identities are
∗(dt∧dxi)=−21​ϵijk​dxj∧dxk,∗(dxj∧dxk)=−ϵijk​dt∧dxi.
(11)
Writing F=Fti​dt∧dxi+21​Fjk​dxj∧dxk, the self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=∗F give
Fti​=−21​ϵijk​Fjk​.
(12)
In temporal gauge, At​=0, so the gauge curvature component is Fti​=∂t​Ai​−∂i​At​+[At​,Ai​]=∂t​Ai​. Hence
∂t​Ai​=−21​ϵijk​Fjk​,Fjk​=∂j​Ak​−∂k​Aj​+[Aj​,Ak​].​
(13)
The minus sign follows from placing dt last in the orientation and first in the mixed curvature component.
