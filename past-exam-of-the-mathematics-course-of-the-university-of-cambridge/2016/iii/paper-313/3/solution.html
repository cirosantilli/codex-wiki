A principal bundle separates the geometry of internal symmetry from a chosen local gauge potential. Let π:P→X be a principal G-bundle with a free right Lie group action. Each fiber is a copy of G, but generally no single identification works globally. A principal connection tells us how to compare fibers over nearby base points. Its physical interpretation is a prescription for parallel transport of internal states.
For ξ∈g, the vertical fundamental vector field is ξp#​=dsd​​0​pexp(sξ). A principal connection is a Lie algebra-valued one-form A on P satisfying
A(ξ#)=ξ,Rg∗​A=Adg−1​A.​
(27)
Equivalently, the horizontal distribution of a principal connection Hp​=kerAp​ complements the vertical tangent space and is preserved by right translation. Given a curve in X and an initial point in its fiber, there is a unique horizontal lift. For a closed curve its endpoint differs from its start by a group element: the holonomy of a connection. Thus a principal connection contains both infinitesimal and global transport information.
Choose local sections sα​:Uα​→P. Their local principal connection forms Aα​=sα∗​A are the usual gauge potentials. If sβ​=sα​gαβ​ on an overlap, equivariance and reproduction of vertical generators give
Aβ​=gαβ−1​Aα​gαβ​+gαβ−1​dgαβ​.​
(28)
This inhomogeneous law means that a gauge potential is not an ordinary globally defined tensor. Its local formulas glue to a global principal connection. The same law describes changing a local section by a gauge function. For example, to impose temporal gauge locally, solve ∂t​g=−At​g so the transformed time component vanishes.
The curvature of a principal connection is
F=dA+21​[A∧A],Fα​=dAα​+Aα​∧Aα​.​
(29)
Unlike the principal connection form, its curvature is horizontal and equivariant. Consequently Fβ​=gαβ−1​Fα​gαβ​, and the gauge curvature is globally a two-form on X with values in the adjoint bundle adP=P×Ad​g. The curvature measures the failure of horizontal directions to close under brackets: for horizontal lifts U,V, F(U,V)=−A([U,V]). A flat principal connection has an integrable horizontal distribution, although nontrivial global holonomy of a connection can remain around noncontractible curves.
The covariant exterior derivative on adjoint bundle-valued forms is locally DA​=d+[A,⋅]. Expanding F=dA+A∧A and using d2=0 yields the Bianchi identity
DA​F=0.​
(30)
For a representation ρ of G, the same principal connection induces transport in the corresponding associated vector bundle; locally its covariant derivative is d+ρ∗​(A). This explains why charged matter and the gauge curvature use the same gauge potential.
Now put an oriented Riemannian metric on a four-dimensional base and take G=SU(n). Use anti-Hermitian matrices with positive pairing −tr(XY), as in the preceding solution. The Yang-Mills action is
S[A]=−gYM2​1​∫X​tr(F∧∗F).
(31)
Its gauge invariance follows from curvature conjugation and invariance of the trace. Under a compactly supported variation a=δA, δF=DA​a. Integration by parts therefore gives
δS=−gYM2​2​∫X​tr(a∧DA​∗F),DA​∗F=0.​
(32)
This is a second-order equation for the gauge potential. The self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=∗F and the Anti-self-dual Yang-Mills equations F=−∗F are first-order equations. Either implies the full Yang-Mills equations immediately, since DA​∗F=±DA​F=0 by the Bianchi identity. This is self-duality implies Yang-Mills equations.
A Yang-Mills instanton is a smooth finite-action Euclidean solution with self-dual or anti-self-dual gauge curvature. Its defining first-order condition depends on the principal connection, the metric and the orientation. Since the Hodge star operator on two-forms is unchanged under g↦e2fg in four dimensions, self-duality and the Yang-Mills action are conformally invariant. In particular Euclidean solutions can be studied through conformal compactification, with appropriate behavior at infinity.
The global topology is encoded by Chern-Weil theory. Since trF=0, the Second Chern form and Second Chern number are
c2​(A)=8π21​tr(F∧F),k=∫X​c2​(A)∈Z
(33)
on a compact oriented four-manifold. The Bianchi identity makes c2​(A) closed. More explicitly, under a connection variation,
δtr(F∧F)=2tr(DA​a∧F)=2dtr(a∧F).
(34)
Thus the integrated Second Chern number is independent of the principal connection on a fixed bundle, with the usual fixed-boundary condition on a noncompact base. The space of connections is affine, so integrating this variation along a straight path also proves that their characteristic forms differ by an exact form.
Locally the same characteristic form has a Chern-Simons 3-form primitive:
tr(F∧F)=dtr(A∧dA+32​A∧A∧A).
(35)
On R4 the underlying bundle is trivial, but a finite-action instanton with the standard extendible behavior at infinity can define a nontrivial bundle after adding the point at infinity. The transition map on an equatorial S3 takes values in SU(n); for n≥2, its homotopy class lies in π3​(SU(n))≅Z. The boundary integral of the Chern-Simons 3-form computes that integer with the chosen sign convention. This reconciles a local matrix-valued gauge potential on R4 with nonzero global Second Chern number on S4.
Finally the Hodge splitting of Euclidean two-forms produces the Yang-Mills instanton Bogomolny bound
S[A]≥gYM2​8π2​∣k∣.​
(36)
Equality holds exactly when the opposite-duality component of the gauge curvature vanishes. Thus a Yang-Mills instanton is an absolute action minimum in its fixed topological sector, not merely a stationary solution. In this trace convention self-dual instantons have k<0 and anti-self-dual instantons have k>0. In the zero sector, a self-dual or anti-self-dual finite-action solution has S=0 and hence F=0.
Different principal connections related by bundle gauge transformations describe the same physical configuration. Fixing k and taking the quotient of instanton solutions by this gauge equivalence of principal connections gives the instanton moduli space. Near an anti-self-dual solution, write a variation as a∈Ω1(X,adP). The linearized instanton equation and infinitesimal gauge transformations are
P+​DA​a=0,a∼a+DA​ε.

This connects the instanton moduli space to geometric analysis, while the characteristic number and transport law explain its topological and physical meaning.
