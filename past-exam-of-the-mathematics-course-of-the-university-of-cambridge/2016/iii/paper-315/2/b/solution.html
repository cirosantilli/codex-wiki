Assume a hydrogen-rich gas with approximately solar elemental ratios and local thermochemical equilibrium when identifying the limiting compositions. At T≲500K, a representative set of dominant molecules is H2​,H2​O,CH4​,NH3​. In the deeper T≳1300K regime, it is H2​,H2​O,CO,N2​. Helium is an abundant atom, not a molecule. The carbon and nitrogen switches, carbon monoxide–methane quenching and nitrogen–ammonia quenching when frozen by mixing, are represented by
CO+3H2​⇌CH4​+H2​O,N2​+3H2​⇌2NH3​.
(7)
Low temperature and high pressure favor the right-hand sides, while hotter gas favors carbon monoxide and N2​. Exact boundaries depend on pressure and composition; CO2​ can become important at high metallicity. If mixing is strong, the cool upper atmosphere need not retain its local-equilibrium four-species ordering.
To preserve the hot-region reactant A1​ aloft, require transport to beat its conversion at the quench region. With a mixing length ℓ and vertical eddy diffusivity Kzz​,
τmix​∼Kzz​ℓ2​≲105s,Kzz​≳ℓ2/(105s).​
(8)
For the simplest use of the supplied atmospheric scale height reference, assume the same temperature and mean molecular weight as the reference atmosphere and take ℓ=H. Since H∝1/g, H≃30/0.4=75km, giving Kzz​≳5.6×104m2s−1=5.6×108cm2s−1.
The hotter quench layer requires a temperature correction if the reference is ordinary Jupiter; the temperature of that scale-height reference was not specified. An explicit estimate using Tq​=1300K, dimensionless mean molecular weight μ=2.3, and g≃0.4×25=10ms−2 gives
H=μmH​gkB​Tq​​≃4.7×105m,Kzz​≳2.2×106m2s−1=2.2×1010cm2s−1.
(9)
Thus a scale-height-based estimate using the hot layer is of order 1010cm2s−1. Both estimates state their assumptions: the reference scaling alone does not include the temperature ratio. Choosing ℓ=0.1H lowers the threshold by a factor of 100, and a full quench calculation needs the reaction timescale along the profile. The dependence of a quench diffusivity on mixing length shows why the stated reaction timescale supplies a mixing constraint, not a unique measured coefficient.
