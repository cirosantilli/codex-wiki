Take the convention that the coordinates of planar Brownian motion are independent standard real Brownian motions, so its infinitesimal generator is 21​Δ. Define the forward conformal Brownian clock
A(s)=∫0s​∣ϕ′(Br​)∣2dr,T=A(T−),τ=A−1.
(1)
Since a conformal bijection has nonzero derivative, A is a strictly increasing continuous map from [0,T) onto [0,T). The time used inside the original path is its inverse:
τ:[0,T)⟶[0,T),Bt​=ϕ(Bτ(t)​).​
(2)
Thus the interval direction for τ is the inverse-clock direction.
Write ϕ=u+iv. The Cauchy-Riemann equations and the Itô formula make u(Bs​),v(Bs​) continuous local martingales, with
d[u(B)]s​=d[v(B)]s​=∣ϕ′(Bs​)∣2ds,d[u(B),v(B)]s​=0.
(3)
Indeed both components are harmonic functions, and their gradients are orthogonal with the same squared norm. After the time change of a continuous process by τ, their quadratic variations are t and their quadratic covariation is zero. The Lévy characterization of multidimensional Brownian motion therefore identifies B as planar Brownian motion started at z′, up to its lifetime.
It remains to identify that lifetime as the exit time, rather than merely produce a local Brownian path. Boundedness of D gives T<∞ almost surely. For every compact subset C⊂D′, its inverse image under ϕ is compactly contained in D. As s↑T, continuity gives Bs​→BT​∈/D, so ϕ(Bs​) eventually leaves C. Consequently the transformed path leaves every compact subset of D′ at its lifetime. If T<∞, its Brownian extension has a finite limit, and that limit is outside D′; if T=∞, the path never exits. In both cases its maximal lifetime is precisely the exit time from D′.
The killed paths, together with their lifetimes, have the same law:
(T,(Bt​)t<T​)=d(T′,(Bt′​)t<T′​).​
(4)

A finite original exit time alone does not imply that the integral defining the conformal Brownian clock is finite. To establish this, let ψ=ϕ−1. On the event {T=∞}, the Brownian path of part (a) lives forever in D′, while its inverse clock satisfies
τ(t)=∫0t​∣ψ′(Br​)∣2dr<T.
(5)
Choose a closed disc C compactly contained in D′. Its radius may be decreased so that ∣ψ′∣2≥c>0 on C. Recurrence of planar Brownian motion, together with the Strong Markov property, gives infinite total Brownian occupation time in C: return repeatedly to a smaller concentric disc, and use the fixed positive probability of remaining in C for a fixed positive duration. The successive trials imply infinitely many such durations. This is the same mechanism as the divergence of a positive planar Brownian occupation integral.
Hence, on any infinite-lifetime Brownian path,
∫0∞​∣ψ′(Br​)∣2dr≥c∫0∞​1C​(Br​)dr=∞.
(6)
This contradicts τ(t)<T<∞. Thus T is finite almost surely, and equality in law from part (a) gives finite exit from a conformal image of a bounded planar domain:
Pz′​(T′<∞)=1.​
(7)
This asserts almost-sure finiteness, not finiteness of the expected exit time.

No such conformal bijection exists. A prescribed point is a polar point for planar Brownian motion: a planar Brownian motion started away from zero hits zero with probability zero. For example, inside an annulus the probability of reaching radius ε before radius R is
logR−logεlogR−log∣z′∣​,
(8)
by the harmonic function log∣w∣ and the optional stopping theorem. For fixed R>∣z′∣ this tends to zero as ε↓0. Any finite-time visit to zero would occur before leaving some sufficiently large disc; the countable union over integer R still has probability zero.
The exit time from C∖{0} is therefore infinite almost surely, contradicting part (b) if this were the image of a bounded domain. Equivalently, a hypothetical inverse ψ:C∖{0}→D would be bounded and holomorphic. The Riemann removable singularity theorem extends it over zero, and the Liouville theorem makes it constant, contradicting bijectivity.
D bounded⟹D is not conformally equivalent to C∖{0}.​
(9)

Use the Green function of killed planar Brownian motion, normalized as the density of expected occupation with respect to area:
Ez​∫0T​f(Bt​)dt=∫D​GD​(z,w)f(w)dA(w)
(10)
for nonnegative measurable f. Equivalently, GD​(z,w)=∫0∞​pD​(t,z,w)dt, where pD​ is the killed Brownian transition density. With generator 21​Δ, the distributional normalization is −21​Δw​GD​(z,w)=δz​(w), and the singularity is −π−1log∣w−z∣ plus a locally harmonic function. This fixes the normalization of the Dirichlet Green function explicitly.
By conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion and its conformal Brownian clock,
Ez′​∫0T′​f(Bs′​)ds=Ez​∫0T​f(ϕ(Bt​))∣ϕ′(Bt​)∣2dt=∫D​GD​(z,w)f(ϕ(w))∣ϕ′(w)∣2dA(w).
(11)
The Jacobian determinant of a conformal map is ∣ϕ′(w)∣2. Changing the area variable to w′=ϕ(w) gives
∫D′​GD​(z,ϕ−1(w′))f(w′)dA(w′).
(12)
Uniqueness of the occupation density proves the desired equality almost everywhere. Both functions are continuous and harmonic away from their pole, so it holds at every w=z. The Green function is conformally invariant:
GD′​(ϕ(z),ϕ(w))=GD​(z,w).​
(13)
If the Dirichlet Green function is instead normalized for −Δ, both kernels are divided by two and the invariance statement is unchanged.

The mapping-out function of a compact H-hull is the unique conformal bijection from H=H∖K onto the complex upper half-plane with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity:
gK​:H→H,gK​(z)=z+zaK​​+O(∣z∣−2)(z→∞).​
(14)
The nonnegative coefficient aK​ is the half-plane capacity of K. The leading coefficient one and constant term zero remove the affine ambiguity of a half-plane map. In the compact H-hull convention, K is bounded and relatively closed in H, and its complement is a simply connected domain; boundary attachment points can be included in its closure.
We will use these standard properties explicitly: gK​ and its inverse extend holomorphically by Schwarz reflection principle across real intervals outside the hull's real attachment region; their real boundary restrictions are strictly increasing; and their normalized Laurent series hold in a full reflected neighbourhood of infinity. In particular, when K lies in the unit disc, these extensions exist across (−∞,−1) and (1,∞).

Compare the hull with the empty hull and the filled unit half-disc J={z∈H:∣z∣≤1}. Their mapping-out functions of compact H-hulls are g∅​(z)=z and gJ​(z)=z+1/z, the latter also giving the half-plane capacity of a half-disc.
Use one planar Brownian motion started at iy, with y>1. For x>1, exiting H∖J through (x,∞) entails avoiding K, while exiting H∖K through this interval entails reaching it before exit from the whole half-plane. Thus the tail harmonic measures satisfy
pJ​(y,x)≤pK​(y,x)≤p∅​(y,x).
(19)
For any of these fixed hulls L, the Poisson kernel for the upper half-plane gives the exact tail formula
pL​(y,x)=21​−π1​arctanImgL​(iy)gL​(x)−RegL​(iy)​=21​−πygL​(x)​+o(y−1).
(20)
Subtract from 1/2, multiply by πy, and take the limit. The inequalities reverse, yielding the real boundary bounds:
x≤gK​(x)≤x+x1​(x>1).​
(21)
Applying this result to the reflected hull −K also gives
x+x1​≤gK​(x)≤x(x<−1).
(22)
The comparison is of Brownian exit events, so it does not assume that arbitrary conformal maps are pointwise ordered by domain inclusion.

Write g=gK​ and f=g−1. We use the real boundary bounds for a unit-disc H-hull from part (c), including their reflected version. Set
α=limx↑−1​g(x),β=limx↓1​g(x).
(23)
Strict monotonicity and those bounds give −2≤α≤−1 and 1≤β≤2. The inverse f extends across R∖[α,β] and maps these intervals onto (−∞,−1) and (1,∞).
Consider the holomorphic function F(w)=w−f(w) on H. If w approaches a real u outside [α,β], its inverse tends to a real x with ∣x∣>1. Part (c) gives
∣u−f(u)∣=∣g(x)−x∣≤∣x∣1​≤1.
(24)
If u∈[α,β], every cluster point of f(w) as w→u lies in the closed unit disc. To justify this without a boundary regularity assumption, first note that f(w) cannot tend to infinity for bounded w, since g(z)=z+O(1/z) there. An interior cluster point in H would be mapped to the real number u, impossible for g:H→H. A real cluster point x with ∣x∣>1 would, by the reflected extension and strict monotonicity, have g(x) outside [α,β], also impossible. All remaining finite boundary points lie in K or in [−1,1], hence in the unit disc.
It follows for every such u that
limsupw→uw∈H​​∣F(w)∣≤∣u∣+1≤3.
(25)
Finally f(w)=w+O(1/w) at infinity, so F(w)→0 there. Apply the maximum modulus principle on large upper half-discs, using the boundary limsup just obtained, to conclude ∣F(w)∣≤3 everywhere in H. Taking w=g(z) proves the sharp displacement bound for a compact H-hull, including irregular hulls:
∣gK​(z)−z∣≤3(z∈H).​
(26)

A nearly closed semicircular slit makes the constant sharp. Put
m(z)=1−zz+1​,KL​=m−1({iy:0<y≤L}),L>0.
(27)
This is the unit-circle arc attached at −1 and ending just short of 1. Its complement is a simply connected domain. There remains a narrow passage near 1 connecting the interior bay to infinity. Thus KL​ is a compact H-hull inside the closed unit disc.
For an explicit check, let S=1+L2​ and let qL​(z)=m(z)2+L2​ denote the mapping-out function of a vertical slit applied to m(z), with the branch asymptotic to its argument in the slit half-plane. Its normalized map is
gKL​​(z)=1+S2L2​−S[qL​(z)+S]2​.
(28)
The final Möbius transformation takes the image of the original infinity to infinity and gives hydrodynamic normalization at infinity. For a fixed z in the open unit half-disc, Rem(z)>0, so qL​(z)∼L and gKL​​(z)→2 as L→∞. Now take zδ​=−1+δ+iδ, with 0<δ<1. First let L→∞, then let δ↓0. The nearly closed semicircular slit gives

(29)

In the half-plane-capacity parameterization, let W be standard real Brownian motion and put ξt​=κ​Wt​. Solve the Chordal Loewner equation
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−ξt​2​,g0​(z)=z.
(30)
The points for which this flow ceases to exist form the growing compact H-hulls Kt​, with half-plane capacity 2t. The process is an SLE trace when its initial segments generate these hulls and
γt​=y↓0lim​gt−1​(ξt​+iy),ξt​=κ​Wt​.​
(31)
The limit is the continuous trace of a Loewner chain started at zero. If the curve disconnects a region from infinity, that region is included in Kt​; the hull is not generally just the visited curve. For κ=0 the driver is identically zero and the trace is the deterministic vertical slit γt​=2it​.

Use the following deterministic boundary fact from the Chordal Loewner equation. For a continuous trace of a Loewner chain started at zero and generating its hulls, the real flow at b>0 has a boundary swallowing time Tb​. Until that time it is the reflected boundary value of gt​, and
Xt​=gt​(b)−ξt​>0.
(32)
A finite Tb​ is its first collision with the driver, Xt​→0 as t↑Tb​. Topologically, the boundary point b is then visited or separated from infinity precisely when the trace has reached some point in [b,∞). A boundary crosscut can swallow an interval, so this is not a claim that the trace visits the particular point b.
Consequently the SLE boundary swallowing criterion is
{γ hits [b,∞)}={Tb​<∞}.​
(33)
For the SLE driver this real flow obeys
dXt​=Xt​2​dt−κ​dWt​,X0​=b,
(34)
up to its first hit of zero. This is the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE.

For κ>0, divide the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE by κ​ and replace W by −W. The resulting Bessel process has dimension
δ=1+κ4​,
(35)
since its drift is 2Rt​δ−1​=2/(κRt​). The Hitting-zero classification for a Bessel process suggests the threshold δ=2, or κ=4. Here is a direct verification including accessibility in finite time.
The infinitesimal generator of X is L=2κ​∂x2​+(2/x)∂x​. An increasing scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion is
s(x)=⎩⎨⎧​1−4/κx1−4/κ​,logx,​κ=4,κ=4.​
(36)
It satisfies Ls=0. For 0<ε<b<R, the boundary hitting probability from a diffusion scale function, or optional stopping theorem applied to s(X), gives
Pb​(Tε​<TR​)=s(R)−s(ε)s(R)−s(b)​.
(37)
For 0<κ<4, s(ε)→−∞, so this probability tends to zero as ε↓0. A finite zero hit has a bounded path before the hit and hence precedes TR​ for some integer R; taking a countable union proves that zero is never hit.
For κ>4, write a=1−4/κ>0. Taking the inner boundary to zero gives 1−(b/R)a. This limit really concerns a finite zero hit: the Itô formula gives
d(Xt2​)=(κ+4)dt−2κ​Xt​dWt​.
(38)
Stopped on [ε,R], it implies
Eb​(Tε​∧TR​)≤κ+4R2−b2​,
(39)
uniformly in ε. The increasing limit of these exit times is finite almost surely; the stopped squared process has a continuous extension, so its limiting lower endpoint is zero. Thus
Pb​(T0​<TR​)=1−(b/R)a.
(40)
Let R→∞ to obtain Pb​(T0​<∞)=1. When κ=0, the explicit solution is Xt​=b2+4t​, which stays positive.
Combining this with the SLE boundary swallowing criterion gives the critical parameter and the two regimes:
κc​=4,P(γ hits [b,∞))={0,1,​0≤κ<4,κ>4.​​

For κ<4, each event {γ hits [1/n,∞)} has probability zero by part (c). Every positive real point lies in one of these intervals. The countable union therefore has probability zero, proving simultaneous avoidance of the entire positive real axis:
γ([0,∞))∩(0,∞)=∅almost surely.​
(42)
For κ>4, every event {γ hits [n,∞)} has probability one. Their countable intersection still has probability one. On that event the set of positive real points visited contains a point at least n for every integer n, so the visited positive boundary set is unbounded:
sup(γ([0,∞))∩(0,∞))=∞almost surely.​
(43)
This uses countably many SLE boundary swallowing criteria; no intersection over an uncountable family of probability-one events is needed.

At κ=4, the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE has dimension two. Its scale function of a one-dimensional diffusion is s(x)=logx, giving
Pb​(Tε​<TR​)=log(R/ε)log(R/b)​⟶0(ε↓0).
(44)

P(γ hits [b,∞))=0(b>0, κ=4).​
(45)
The countable argument of part (d) gives no visits to any positive real point almost surely. A conformal automorphism of the upper half-plane is a real Möbius transformation
ϕ(z)=cz+daz+b​,a,b,c,d∈R,ad−bc>0,
(46)
with coefficients determined up to a common nonzero factor. The boundary interval (∞,1) is the oriented real boundary arc from infinity to 1, namely the finite real interval (−∞,1). The map must take the left endpoint −1 to infinity and the right endpoint infinity to 1, while fixing zero. These three boundary values determine the Möbius transformation uniquely:
ϕ(z)=1+zz​.​
(47)
Its determinant is positive, Imϕ(z)=Imz/∣1+z∣2>0, and on (−1,∞) its derivative is (1+x)−2>0. Its endpoint limits are −∞ at −1+ and 1 at +∞, so it has exactly the required boundary extension.
