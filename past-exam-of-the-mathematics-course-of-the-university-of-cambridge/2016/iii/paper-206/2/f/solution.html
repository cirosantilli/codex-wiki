Assume the known covariance matrix Σ is positive definite. The Gaussian likelihood, up to constants, is
ℓ(β)=−21​(Y−Xβ)TΣ−1(Y−Xβ).
(29)
Its gradient is XTΣ−1(Y−Xβ). The negative Hessian matrix XTΣ−1X is positive definite because X has full column rank. Thus the unique maximum-likelihood estimator is the generalized least squares estimator
β​GLS​=(XTΣ−1X)−1XTΣ−1Y.​
(30)
It is unbiased, and multiplication of its linear coefficient matrix by Σ gives
Cov(β​GLS​)=(XTΣ−1X)−1.​
(31)
In fact β​GLS​∼Np​(β,(XTΣ−1X)−1). Equivalently, a whitening transformation premultiplies Y and X by Σ−1/2, after which ordinary least squares applies. There is no extra σ2 factor when Σ is the complete known covariance; such a factor appears if only a shape matrix Ω is known and Σ=σ2Ω.
