The fitted values are Y=Xβ​, so the hat matrix is
H=X(XTX)−1XT.​
(18)
It is symmetric, and
H2=X(XTX)−1(XTX)(XTX)−1XT=H.
(19)
It fixes every vector in the column space of X, and annihilates its orthogonal complement. Thus H is the orthogonal projection onto that column space, has matrix rank and trace p, and I−H is the complementary orthogonal projection. Consequently
Cov(Y)=σ2HHT=σ2H,Cov(e)=σ2(I−H).​
(20)
Since (I−H)X=0, the covariance matrix between the regression residual vector and the coefficient estimate is
Cov(e,β​)=σ2(I−H)X(XTX)−1=0.
(21)
These quantities have a joint normal distribution, so they are also independent, a stronger conclusion than zero covariance.
Now e=(I−H)ε has mean zero. Using the expectation of a quadratic form,
ERSS=E(eTe)=trCov(e)=σ2(n−p).
(22)
Thus the maximum-likelihood estimator has
EσML2​=nn−p​σ2,Bias(σML2​)=−np​σ2.
(23)
The unbiased alternative is
s2=n−pRSS​.​
(24)
The orthogonal projection of a Gaussian vector also yields RSS/σ2∼χn−p2​, explaining the residual degrees of freedom.
