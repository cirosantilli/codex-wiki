Write R=k/(μmH​) for the specific gas constant, with the printed mH​ denoting the atomic mass unit, and ar​=4σ/c for the radiation constant. In the low-mass envelope the enclosed mass is approximately M and the outward luminosity is approximately L. The stellar hydrostatic equation, stellar radiative diffusion, and ideal gas equation give
drdP​=−r2GMρ​,drdT​=−16πar​cr2T33κρL​,P=RρT.
(1)
The Kramers' opacity law becomes κ=(κ0​/R)PT−9/2. Dividing the two differential equations eliminates radius and mass density:
PdTdP​=3κ0​L16πar​cGMR​T15/2.
(2)
Integrating from a surface matching point (Ps​,Ts​) gives
P2=Ps2​+51κ0​L64πar​cGMR​(T17/2−Ts17/2​).
(3)
Deep compared with the photosphere, the surface terms are negligible. The radiative-zero white-dwarf envelope therefore has
P(T)=(51κ0​L64πar​cGMR​)1/2T17/4,ρ∝T13/4.​
(4)
This is the leading envelope approximation used in the later parts, rather than an exact atmospheric boundary condition at Teff​.
At the base, temperature, mass density and pressure match the isothermal degenerate core. Let KNR​=K1​μe−5/3​, where μe​ is the mean molecular weight per electron. Equating the gas and electron degeneracy pressure gives
Rρb​Tc​=KNR​ρb5/3​,ρb​=(KNR​RTc​​)3/2=μe5/2​(K1​R​)3/2Tc3/2​.​
(5)
Hence Pb​=μe5/2​R5/2K1−3/2​Tc5/2​. Substituting this into the envelope relation at T=Tc​ yields
ML​=51κ0​64πar​cG​μe5​R4K13​​Tc7/2​=51κ0​256πσG​μe5​K13​​(kμmH​​)4Tc7/2​.​
(6)
The matching of a white-dwarf envelope to a degenerate core thus gives L/M∝Tc7/2​, the envelope relation underlying Mestel's cooling law. It depends on pressure as well as mass density continuity at the interface; otherwise the supplied core equation would not determine the matching mass density.
