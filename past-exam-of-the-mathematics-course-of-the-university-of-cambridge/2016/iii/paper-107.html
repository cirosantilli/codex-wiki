The Hopf boundary point lemma requires an interior sphere condition, strictness of the boundary maximum, and quantitative control of the uniformly elliptic operator near the contact point. Assume that y∈∂Ω, that BR​(z)⊂Ω and BR​(z)​∖{y}⊂Ω, and that ∣y−z∣=R. After shrinking the tangent open ball, we may suppose that the coefficients are bounded there and that, for constants 0<λ≤Λ and B<∞,
λ∣ξ∣2≤aij(x)ξi​ξj​≤Λ∣ξ∣2,∣b(x)∣≤B.
(1)
The coefficient matrix may be replaced by its symmetric matrix part because the Hessian matrix is symmetric. Assume also that u(x)<u(y) throughout the tangent open ball. In the usual statement, one assumes u(x)<u(y) throughout Ω; when Ω is connected, the strong maximum principle for elliptic operators supplies this strictness from a nonconstant solution attaining its global maximum at y.
Put n=(y−z)/R, the outward unit normal of the tangent open ball. With only the given continuity at y, the precise conclusion is
t↓0liminf​tu(y)−u(y−tn)​>0.​
(2)
If the outward normal derivative exists, it is strictly positive. In particular, if u is continuously differentiable up to y and the domain has this outward unit normal, then ∂n​u(y)>0. The stated C0 boundary regularity alone does not assert existence of a normal derivative.
For the proof, use the annulus A={R/2<∣x−z∣<R} and the barrier for the Dirichlet problem
v(x)=e−k∣x−z∣2−e−kR2.
(3)
Writing q=x−z, direct differentiation gives
Lv=e−k∣q∣2(4k2aijqi​qj​−2ktra−2kb⋅q)≥e−k∣q∣2(k2λR2−2k(nΛ+BR)).
(4)
Choose k>2(nΛ+BR)/(λR2), so that Lv>0 on A. On the inner sphere, compactness and the strict maximum give m=min∣x−z∣=R/2​(u(y)−u(x))>0. Choose ε>0 with εmax∣x−z∣=R/2​v≤m. On the outer sphere v=0 and u−u(y)≤0, including at y by continuity. Consequently w=u−u(y)+εv has Lw≥0 and nonpositive boundary values. The allowed comparison principle, or the weak maximum principle for elliptic operators, yields w≤0 in A.
Along the inward radius this gives
tu(y)−u(y−tn)​≥εte−k(R−t)2−e−kR2​⟶2εkRe−kR2>0.
(5)
This proves the Hopf boundary point lemma. There is no sign restriction on u(y) here, because the operator has no zeroth-order term.

A bounded-domain hypothesis is needed for the requested global construction and uniqueness. The printed question does not include it. For example, the upper half-plane satisfies the stated exterior cone condition, but both u=0 and u(x1​,x2​)=x2​ are harmonic functions with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. Thus part (iv), in its printed unrestricted solution class, is false. In this question's solutions we add that Ω is bounded; the exterior cone condition and all other data remain as printed.
The half-plane also rules out the printed global separated barrier. In its angular interval (0,π), varying r on a fixed ray forces ν=2 if rν−2(h′′+ν2h)≥δ>0 for every r>0. We would then have h′′+4h≥δ and h<0. Put a=π/4 and b=3π/4. Twice integration by parts gives
∫ab​(h′′+4h)sin(2(θ−a))dθ=2(h(a)+h(b))<0,
(6)
contradicting the positive integrand. Thus a source qualification is necessary for part (i) as well as part (iv).
Fix x0​ and its exterior cone of half-angle α. Choose 0<α0​<min(α,π/2) and set
β=2(π−α0​)π​,0<ν<β<1.
(7)
On the complement of the cone, unwrap the angle in polar coordinates as ϑ∈(α,2π−α), measured from the cone axis. Then ψ=ϑ−π is the angle from the opposite axis and ∣ψ∣≤π−α on Ω∖{x0​}. Define the power barrier for an exterior cone by
g(r,ϑ)=−rνcos(β(ϑ−π)),g(x0​)=0.​
(8)
This has the requested separated form rνh(ϑ). In the printed signed-angle convention, the same function is −rνcos(β(π−∣θ∣)) outside the cone. It is smooth across the negative axis: near that axis the angular expression is the even function cos(βψ).
Since β(π−α)<π/2, put m=cos(β(π−α))>0. The cosine is at least m, so g≤−mrν<0 away from x0​, and g is continuous at x0​. The Laplacian in polar coordinates gives
Δg=(β2−ν2)rν−2cos(βψ).
(9)
Let D=supx∈Ω​∣x−x0​∣<∞. Since ν<2, the barrier for the Dirichlet problem satisfies
Δg≥δ:=(β2−ν2)mDν−2>0.​
(10)
Both the uniform lower bound and the later global comparison use boundedness. In particular, −g is positive at every other boundary point, and is bounded away from zero on boundary sets staying a positive distance from x0​.

For the bounded-domain correction, let S be the family of Perron subfunctions for the Poisson equation v∈C0(Ω) with v≤φ on ∂Ω. The subfunction condition is ball comparison: whenever B⊂Ω and a classical solution H of ΔH=f on B satisfies v≤H on ∂B, it also satisfies v≤H throughout B. For a C2 function this is the sign condition Δv≥f. A superfunction reverses both inequalities.
The Perron solution is the pointwise upper envelope of the subfunctions:
u(x)=v∈Ssup​v(x),x∈Ω.​
(11)
This is the Perron method for the Dirichlet problem with the Poisson equation replacing the homogeneous Laplace equation. The power barrier for an exterior cone supplies a global member of S and a global superfunction, as constructed below. The permitted subfunction-superfunction comparison therefore makes this envelope finite. The standard interior Perron theorem, available in the question, gives u∈C2(Ω) and Δu=f; neither result needs to be proved in this part.

A(−g(x))≥∣φ(x)−φ(x0​)∣on ∂Ω∖Bρ​(x0​).
(12)
Then the Perron subfunction for the Poisson equation and its superfunction counterpart
v−​(x)=φ(x0​)−ε+Ag(x),v+​(x)=φ(x0​)+ε−Ag(x)
(13)
satisfy Δv−​≥M≥f, Δv+​≤−M≤f, and v−​≤φ≤v+​ on the whole boundary. The near-boundary ordering uses the choice of ρ; the remaining ordering uses the choice of A. In particular the Perron family is nonempty and bounded above.

φ(x0​)−ε≤liminfx→x0​​u(x)≤limsupx→x0​​u(x)≤φ(x0​)+ε.
(14)
Letting ε↓0 proves
Ω∋x→x0​lim​u(x)=φ(x0​).​
(15)
The interior Perron theorem gives continuity inside Ω, and the displayed limit together with continuity of φ proves the continuous extension u∈C0(Ω) with the required boundary trace. Thus every boundary point is a regular boundary point.

For the corrected bounded-domain problem, the interior theorem for the Perron method for the Dirichlet problem gives a classical solution of the Poisson equation, and the power barrier for an exterior cone argument gives its continuous extension with the required Dirichlet boundary condition. Hence existence holds in C2(Ω)∩C0(Ω).
If u1​,u2​ are two such classical solutions, then w=u1​−u2​ is a harmonic function with zero boundary trace. The weak maximum principle for elliptic operators, applied to w and −w on the bounded domain, gives w≤0 and −w≤0. Consequently
u1​=u2​.​
(16)
There is exactly one solution after adding boundedness of Ω. For the printed unbounded-domain version, the half-plane counterexample in part (i) disproves uniqueness; an additional condition at infinity or a suitable restricted solution class would instead have to be specified.

Choose a smooth cutoff function η supported in B2​, equal to one on B1​, with 0≤η≤1 and ∣Dη∣≤C. For i<n, put vi​=Di​u. The zero Dirichlet boundary condition implies that the tangential boundary derivative vi​ vanishes on the flat face. Differentiating the Poisson equation gives Δvi​=Di​f. ∫B2+​​η2∣Dvi​∣2=∫B2+​​fDi​(η2vi​)−2∫B2+​​ηvi​Dvi​⋅Dη.

∫η2∣Dvi​∣2≤21​∫η2∣Dvi​∣2+C∫(f2+vi2​∣Dη∣2).

∑i<n​∑j=1n​∥Dij​u∥L2(B1+​)2​≤C(n)(∥Du∥L2(B2+​)2​+∥f∥L2(B2+​)2​).

Thus the backward difference quotient solves a linear equation in divergence form:

DF(p)=1+∣p∣2​I​−(1+∣p∣2)3/2p⊗p​,

Fix nested relatively compact subsets of Ω and let M bound the gradient on the larger one. The fundamental theorem of calculus along a line segment gives ∣wh​∣≤M on the smaller one. Part (i) gives coefficient bounds independent of small h, so the De Giorgi-Nash-Moser theorem gives uniform interior Hölder continuity estimates for these weak solutions. Since Du is continuous, the difference quotients wh​ converge locally uniformly to Dℓ​u. Passing to the limit in the Hölder seminorm estimate, and repeating for every coordinate, yields
u∈C1,α on each compactly contained subdomain, for some α>0.​
(31)
The first derivatives are locally Hölder continuous. Here the initially obtained exponent depends on n and the local gradient bound. This is the interior interpretation of the printed regularity conclusion; a uniform global estimate does not follow from u∈C1(Ω) alone.

Assume u∈W1,2(B2​(0)) is nonnegative almost everywhere, and that the real coefficient matrix A=(aij) is measurable and satisfies, for almost every x and every ξ∈Rn,
ξ⋅A(x)ξ≥λ∣ξ∣2,∣A(x)ξ∣≤Λ∣ξ∣,0<λ≤Λ<∞.

∫B2​​aijDj​uDi​ζ=0(ζ∈Cc∞​(B2​)).
(33)
Then the Harnack inequality for uniformly elliptic divergence-form equations is
B1​esssup​u≤C(n,Λ/λ)B1​essinf​u.​
(34)
A nonnegative weak solution has its supremum controlled by its infimum on the smaller ball. Nonnegativity and the larger ball are essential hypotheses; there is no differentiability requirement on the coefficients.

Take Ω′ nonempty and compactly contained in Ω. The argument is a compactness proof of a nonnegative elliptic solution estimate, with the global Schauder estimate retaining control all the way to the boundary. The operator and the Hölder space exponent are fixed throughout.
Suppose the estimate fails. For each integer k there is an admissible nonnegative solution uk​, with forcing fk​, such that
Mk​:=supΩ​uk​>k(infΩ′​uk​+∥fk​∥C0,μ(Ω)​).
(38)
Put vk​=uk​/Mk​ and gk​=fk​/Mk​. Then 0≤vk​≤1, maxΩ​vk​=1, vk​=0 on the boundary, Lvk​=gk​, and
infΩ′​vk​<k1​,∥gk​∥C0,μ(Ω)​<k1​.

∥vk​∥C2,μ(Ω)​≤C(∥vk​∥C0(Ω)​+∥gk​∥C0,μ(Ω)​)≤2C.

This estimate includes the C0 term, so it does not require invertibility of L or a sign restriction on c. The compact embedding of Hölder spaces, or the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem applied through second partial derivatives, gives a subsequence converging in C2(Ω) to v. Thus v≥0, Lv=0, v=0 on the boundary, and maxv=1.

Use a signed power test for a Laplacian eigenfunction. For γ≥2, test the Dirichlet Laplacian eigenfunction equation with ∣u∣γ−2u. Its derivative is (γ−1)∣u∣γ−2Du; at γ=2 the test function is just u. Integration by parts gives the exact energy estimate
(γ−1)∫Ω​∣u∣γ−2∣Du∣2=λ∫Ω​∣u∣γ.
(44)
Set v=∣u∣γ/2. It belongs to the zero-boundary Sobolev space and obeys the Sobolev chain rule:
∫Ω​∣Dv∣2=4γ2​∫Ω​∣u∣γ−2∣Du∣2=4(γ−1)λγ2​∫Ω​∣u∣γ.
(45)
For γ>2, the power map is C1 with bounded derivative on the bounded range of u. For γ=2, the absolute value map is Lipschitz, and D∣u∣=sgn(u)Du almost everywhere; on the zero set, use the fact that a gradient of a Sobolev function vanishes on a level set. These facts justify the identity even when u changes sign.

(∫Ω​∣u∣γκ)1/κ≤4(γ−1)C(n)λγ2​∫Ω​∣u∣γ.​
(46)
Testing with a signed power raises the integrability exponent from γ to γκ. Absorbing the factor 1/4 into C(n) gives precisely the requested inequality.
