Use signature (−,+,+,+), so the future-directed unit normal obeys nμnμ​=−1. The spatial projection tensor and the induced metric are
Pμα​=δμα​+nμnα​,γαβ​=gαβ​+nα​nβ​.

Take the orientation induced by the future-directed unit normal and positive lapse function α. In coordinates adapted to the 3+1 decomposition of spacetime, any component ϵℓijk​ with all four indices spatial vanishes: only three distinct spatial indices are available. The shift vector therefore contributes nothing, and
ϵμijk​nμ=α−1ϵ0ijk​=α−1∣g∣​[ijk]=γ​[ijk].
(3)
Here [ijk] is the Levi-Civita symbol, and the last equality uses the metric determinant in a 3+1 decomposition, ∣g∣=α2γ. The right side is precisely the spatial metric volume tensor, with component γ​ at ijk=123, the appropriate permutation sign, and zero for repeated indices. Hence
ϵ~ijk​=nμϵμijk​.​
(4)
More invariantly, the spatial volume form is the restriction to the hypersurface of the contraction of the spacetime volume form with n.

For the Vacuum Einstein equations with zero cosmological constant, Rαβ​=0, and its tensor contraction gives R=0. Every Ricci tensor and scalar curvature term in the Weyl tensor consequently disappears:
Cαβγδ​=Rαβγδ​.​
(5)
Thus the Weyl tensor contains all vacuum curvature. Here vacuum means Ricci-flat; a nonzero cosmological constant would retain a constant-curvature contribution to the Riemann curvature tensor.

The electric part of the Weyl tensor has one normal in each antisymmetric pair of the Weyl tensor. Therefore
nαEαβ​=nαnρCαρβσ​nσ=0,nβEαβ​=nβnσCαρβσ​nρ=0.
(6)
For the magnetic part of the Weyl tensor, contracting the first slot gives nαnλϵαλμν​=0 by antisymmetry of the metric volume tensor; contracting the second gives nβnρCμνβρ​=0 by antisymmetry of the last curvature pair. Consequently both tensors are entirely spatial:
Eαβ​=(⊥E)αβ​,Bαβ​=(⊥B)αβ​.​
(7)
These spatial projection tensor identities actually hold for any Weyl tensor; the vacuum assumption is needed subsequently to replace it by the Riemann curvature tensor.

All free indices below are spatial, and K=γμνKμν​. Contract the vacuum Ricci tensor using gμν=γμν−nμnν:
0=(⊥Rαβ​)=γμν(⊥R)μανβ​−nμnνRμανβ​.

Reversing both antisymmetric curvature pairs identifies the last term as −Eαβ​. The spatial Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface then give
Eαβ​=γμν(Rμανβ​+Kμν​Kβα​−Kμβ​Kνα​)=Rαβ​+KKαβ​−Kνβ​Kνα​.
(9)
For the magnetic part of the Weyl tensor, moving the normal to the first slot of the metric volume tensor introduces a minus sign:
ϵαλμν​nλ=−ϵ~αμν​.
(10)
The remaining curvature indices are spatial, so the normal projection in the Gauss–Codazzi equations for a spatial hypersurface gives
Bαβ​​=−21​ϵ~α​μνRμνβρ​nρ=−21​ϵ~α​μν(−Dμ​Kνβ​+Dν​Kμβ​)=ϵ~α​μνDμ​Kνβ​.​
(11)
The interchange μ↔ν makes the two spatial covariant derivative terms equal. Thus the constants, with the paper's orientation and extrinsic-curvature convention, are
(k1​,k2​,k3​,k4​)=(1,1,−1,1).​
(12)
In particular, the sign of the magnetic expression must include the minus from moving n past the first volume-tensor index. These formulas reconstruct the spatial electric part of the Weyl tensor and magnetic part of the Weyl tensor from the induced metric and the extrinsic curvature of a spatial hypersurface.

For radial motion, take an affine parameter λ and use dots for d/dλ. The radial null geodesics of the Vaidya metric satisfy the two radial geodesic equations
v¨+rM​2v˙2=0,r¨+(r2fM​+rM′​)v˙2−r22M​v˙r˙=0.

The radial squared norm is −fv˙2+2v˙r˙. If v is constant it vanishes, and the geodesic equation reduces to r¨=0. Thus constant-v radial curves are null geodesics, with r=aλ+b an affine parametrization. Future ingoing motion has a<0.
For the other family let dr/dv=f/2. Its tangent k=∂v​+(f/2)∂r​ is a null vector. Using the Christoffel symbols of the Vaidya metric, direct differentiation gives

Write V=Tp​M and V∗ for its dual space. A type-(1,1) tensor is equivalently a linear map A:V→V: the corresponding scalar-valued multilinear map is (X,η)↦η(A(X)). Thus the composition defines
C(X,η)=η(B(A(X))).
(25)
It is linear in X because A and B are linear maps, and linear in the covector η by evaluation. This proves directly that the composition is a tensor of type (1,1).
Choose a basis eα​ of the tangent space and its dual basis eβ. With the paper's component order A(eα​)=Aα​γeγ​,
B(A(eα​))=Aα​γBγ​βeβ​,Cα​β=Aα​γBγ​β.​
(26)
Equivalently, in the usual output-first matrix notation, Cβα​=Bβγ​Aγα​: the matrix product is BA, not AB. This composition of mixed tensors contracts one output index with one input index. Under the tensor component transformation law, the two Jacobian factors at that contracted index cancel, leaving exactly one factor for each free index.

The Lie derivative of a function is differentiation along the flow, while the Lie derivative of a vector field compares a vector with its pullback by that flow. Their index-free expressions are
LX​f=X(f)=df(X),LX​Y=[X,Y].​
(27)
Here X(f) is the directional derivative of the smooth function, and [X,Y] is the Lie bracket of vector fields, characterized by [X,Y](f)=X(Y(f))−Y(X(f)). To see the second expression directly, if ϕt​ is the local flow of X, differentiating (dϕt​)−1Y∘ϕt​ at t=0 gives dY(X)−dX(Y)=[X,Y]. This uses the flow definition of the Lie derivative of a tensor field.

On smooth functions, the Lie derivative of a function satisfies
[LX​,LY​]f=X(Y(f))−Y(X(f))=[X,Y](f)=L[X,Y]​f.
(28)
On an arbitrary vector field W, the Lie derivative of a vector field gives
[LX​,LY​]W=[X,[Y,W]]−[Y,[X,W]]=[[X,Y],W]=L[X,Y]​W.
(29)
The middle equality follows from the Jacobi identity for the Lie bracket of vector fields. One can verify that identity without assuming the result being proved: view vector fields as derivations acting on smooth functions, expand their commutators, and cancel the six compositions. Equality as derivations implies equality as vector fields.
For the second identity, let A,B,C be any operators on either smooth functions or vector fields. Associativity of composition gives
[[A,B],C][[B,C],A][[C,A],B]​=ABC−BAC−CAB+CBA,=BCA−CBA−ABC+ACB,=CAB−ACB−BCA+BAC.​
(30)
Each term cancels in the sum. Applying this to A=LX​, B=LY​, C=LZ​ proves both requested identities on both kinds of arguments:
[LX​,LY​]=L[X,Y]​,[[LX​,LY​],LZ​]+[[LY​,LZ​],LX​]+[[LZ​,LX​],LY​]=0.​
(31)
The commutator identity for Lie derivatives expresses that the Lie derivative of a tensor field represents the Lie bracket of vector fields.

Use t as an affine parameter on each member of the geodesic variation, so ∇T​T=0. The parameter directions commute, giving [T,S]=0 along the variation. The torsion-free connection then gives
∇T​S−∇S​T=[T,S]=0.
(32)
Consequently the covariant derivative and curvature definition give
∇T​∇T​S​=∇T​∇S​T=R(T,S)T+∇S​∇T​T+∇[T,S]​T=R(T,S)T.​
(33)
This is the geodesic-deviation equation with the stated curvature sign:
∇T​∇T​S=R(T,S)T=Rαβγδ​TβTγSδeα​.​
(34)
The component expression defines the slot order: β is the vector acted on, and γ,δ are the two derivative directions of the Riemann curvature tensor. Both the commuting parameter directions and affine parametrization are essential; a general connecting vector field or a nonaffine parameter would add terms.

Let δhμν​=∂μ​Vν​+∂ν​Vμ​. Substitution into the linearized Levi-Civita connection cancels four terms and leaves
δΓμνρ​=∂ν​∂ρ​Vμ.
(35)
To first order in the metric perturbation, the products of Christoffel symbols are quadratic and may be discarded. Therefore the change in the Riemann curvature tensor is
δRμνρσ​=∂ρ​δΓμσν​−∂σ​δΓμρν​=∂ρ​∂σ​∂ν​Vμ−∂σ​∂ρ​∂ν​Vμ=0.
(36)
This is the gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor. Lowering the first index with the Minkowski metric does not change the conclusion; the change from using the full metric tensor instead is of second order because the background curvature is zero. Hence
δRμνρσ(1)​=0.​
(37)
The result is invariance at linear order about flat spacetime, not componentwise invariance of arbitrary nonlinear curvature under finite coordinate changes. Directly in the four-term formula, the eight third derivatives of V cancel in pairs by commuting partial derivatives.

Put A,B∈{1,2} and write hAB​=HAB​eik⋅x, where H11​=H+​, H22​=−H+​ and H12​=H21​=H×​. Physical fields are the real parts. Use c=1 here, and take the initial separation at equal time, so L0=0. For propagation in the positive x3 direction one may set kμ​=(−ω,0,0,ω); only k02​=ω2 enters the answer.
The linearized Levi-Civita connection in this transverse-traceless gauge has the potentially nonzero components
Γ0AB​=21​∂0​hAB​,Γ3AB​=−21​∂3​hAB​,ΓA0B​=ΓAB0​=21​∂0​hAB​,ΓA3B​=ΓAB3​=21​∂3​hAB​,
(38)
with all others zero to this order. In particular Γμ00​=0, so a particle initially at rest has fixed spatial coordinates in transverse-traceless gauge, and t equals its proper time. Expanding the Riemann curvature tensor gives
Rμνρσ(1)​=21​(∂ρ​∂ν​hμσ​+∂σ​∂μ​hνρ​−∂ρ​∂μ​hνσ​−∂σ​∂ν​hμρ​),R0A0B(1)​=−21​h¨AB​,RA00B(1)​=21​h¨AB​.
(39)
The plus sign in the last expression agrees with R(T,S)T in the geodesic deviation convention above.
For the separation measured in a parallel-propagated orthonormal frame, write Sμ^​=Lμ^​+ξμ^​. The frame removes the connection terms from the time derivative, so the geodesic deviation equation reduces to ξ¨​A^=RA^0^0^B^​LB^=h¨AB​LB^/2 at first order. The observable transverse relative acceleration is
ξ¨​0^ξ¨​1^ξ¨​2^​=0,ξ¨​3^=0,=−2k02​​(H+​L1^+H×​L2^)eik⋅x,=−2k02​​(H×​L1^−H+​L2^)eik⋅x.​​
(40)
These are the plus polarization and cross polarization tidal patterns; there is no longitudinal acceleration. The curvature is evaluated along the reference worldline, and replacing S by L on the right changes only second-order terms. This is the infinitesimal-separation approximation intrinsic to geodesic deviation.
There is an important component distinction if the symbols Lμ+ξμ are instead interpreted literally in the transverse-traceless gauge coordinate basis. Expanding the left side, rather than replacing a covariant derivative by an ordinary derivative, gives
(∇T​∇T​S)μ=ξ¨​coordμ​+∂0​Γμ0j​Lj+O(ϵ2),Rμ00j​Lj=∂0​Γμ0j​Lj+O(ϵ2).
(41)
Thus in TT coordinates
ξ¨​coordμ​=0.​
(42)
With zero initial coordinate displacement and velocity, ξcoord​=0 throughout. This does not remove the physical gravitational wave strain. To first order an orthonormal coframe in spacetime along the reference worldline is ϑA^=(δAB​+hAB​/2)dxB; its dual frame is parallel propagated because ∂0​(δAB​−hAB​/2)+ΓA0B​=0. Hence
SA^=SA+21​hAB​LB,ξA^=ξcoordA​+21​hAB​LB,
(43)
after choosing the initial flat-frame reference. The TT coordinate separation and measured separation distinction accounts exactly for the apparently different accelerations. In a region initially free of the wave, the measured displacement is ξA^=hAB​LB^/2 with the corresponding initial conditions.
