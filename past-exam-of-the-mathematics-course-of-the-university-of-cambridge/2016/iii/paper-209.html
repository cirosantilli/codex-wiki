The printed assertion is false as a supremum bound uniform over shrinking bandwidths. The supplied hint proves a pointwise stochastic order with constants uniform in x. Taking the supremum inside the probability changes the problem: a growing number of spatial windows produces a logarithmic cost. We first establish the valid consequence of the hint, then give a counterexample satisfying the printed assumptions.
For I=Ix,h​=[x−h,x+h], let p=μ(I), AT​=∫0T​1I​(Xt​)dt and NT​=∫0T​1I​(Xt​)σ(Xt​)dWt​. The Invariant distribution of an Itô diffusion has a probability density function bounded above and bounded away from zero on the required compact interval. Choosing h0​<M/4 therefore gives constants c0​,C0​>0 such that c0​h≤p≤C0​h, uniformly for ∣x∣≤M/2 and 0<h<h0​.
For the centered function f=1I​−p, ∫∣f∣dμ=2p(1−p)≤2p. Outside [−M,M], f=−p. The given second-moment estimate and the Chebyshev inequality yield
E(AT​−Tp)2≤5C(1+T)p2,P(AT​<Tp/2)≤T220C(1+T)​≤T40C​(T≥1).
(21)
On the event AT​≥Tp/2, the drift coefficient contributes an average whose error is at most Rhα, by Hölder continuity. The Itô isometry and stationarity give ENT2​≤∥σ∥∞2​Tp. Another use of the Chebyshev inequality proves
∣x∣≤M/2sup​P(∣bT​(x,h)−b(x)∣>Rhα+Th​z​)≤T40C​+c0​z24∥σ∥∞2​​.​
(22)
This also accounts for the zero-denominator convention through the first exceptional event. The bound proves pointwise Rhα+OP​((Th)−1/2), uniformly in the location's tail probabilities. It supplies no bound for the probability of a supremum over all locations.
For an explicit counterexample, take σ≡1 and the bounded globally Lipschitz function
b(x)=−sgn(x)min{(∣x∣−1)+​,1}.
(23)
It has Lipschitz constant R=1, exponent α=1, and satisfies the inward-drift conditions with M=2, γ=2. The stochastic differential equation has a unique strong solution of a stochastic differential equation. Its Invariant distribution of an Itô diffusion has density
ρ(x)=Z−1exp(2∫0x​b(y)dy),Z=∫R​exp(2∫0x​b(y)dy)dx<∞.
(24)
The exponent is zero on [−1,1], equals −(∣x∣−1)2 when 1<∣x∣<2, and equals 3−2∣x∣ outside [−2,2]. This verifies integrability, positivity and a constant density ρ0​=Z−1 on the central interval. Start the Itô diffusion in this Invariant distribution of an Itô diffusion to obtain the required stationary process.
Set h=T−1/3 and choose N=⌊(4h)−1⌋ windows with centers xj​=−1/2+(4j−2)h, 1≤j≤N. Their intervals Ij​=[xj​−h,xj​+h] are disjoint and lie in [−1,1], where the drift is zero. Write
Aj​(t)=∫0t​1Ij​​(Xs​)ds,Mj​(t)=∫0t​1Ij​​(Xs​)dWs​,q=2ρ0​Th.
(25)
Then bT​(xj​,h)=Mj​(T)/Aj​(T) whenever Aj​(T)>0. The same occupation bound as above gives E(Aj​(T)−q)2≤C1​Th2 for T≥1. With δ=T−1/6, a union bound and the Chebyshev inequality imply
P(maxj≤N​∣Aj​(T)−q∣>δq)≤Tδ2C2​N​=O(T−1/3)⟶0.
(26)
The quadratic variations are ⟨Mj​⟩=Aj​, and the cross quadratic covariations vanish since the windows are disjoint. Each clock tends to infinity almost surely: for any fixed window, the occupation estimate at times 2k, followed by the Borel-Cantelli lemmas, gives Aj​(2k)/(2kμ(Ij​))→1. The Knight theorem for orthogonal martingales therefore represents Mj​(t)=Bj​(Aj​(t)) using independent standard Brownian motions B1​,…,BN​. This theorem is applied to the finite collection for each T; no growing-dimensional central limit theorem is being assumed.
On the preceding clock event, compare each time-changed Brownian motion with its value at deterministic time q. The Brownian reflection principle and a union bound show that, for every fixed ε>0,
P(maxj≤N​sup∣s−q∣≤δq​∣Bj​(s)−Bj​(q)∣>εq​)≤8Nexp(−2δε2​)⟶0.
(27)
This bound does not require the Brownian motions to be independent of their clocks. In particular, maxj​∣Mj​(T)−Bj​(q)∣/q​→0 in probability. The variables Bj​(q)/q​ are independent N(0,1) random variables. Their Gaussian maximum exceeds logN​ with probability tending to one: for r≥1, integration of the normal density over [r,r+1/r] gives P(∣Z∣>r)≥cr−1e−r2/2, and hence
P(maxj≤N​∣Zj​∣≤logN​)≤exp(−clogN​N​​)⟶0.
(28)
Since Aj​(T)≤(1+δ)q on the clock event, it follows that, for some c3​>0,
P(Th​sup∣x∣≤1​∣bT​(x,h)−b(x)∣≥c3​log(1/h)​)⟶1.
(29)
But Th​Rh=1 for this bandwidth. Thus the error after subtraction of Rh and multiplication by Th​ is not bounded in probability, contradicting the printed stochastic order. The valid pointwise bound above is provable; the uniform shrinking-bandwidth assertion is not. This counterexample shows why a uniform rate needs at least a log(1/h)​ enlargement of the noise scale. If h is instead held fixed and constants may depend on h, this counterexample does not contradict that different interpretation.

Let m=E∥X1​∥2​ and q=n​K≥2. The Lipschitz continuity of cosine and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality imply

The centered expectation term contributes nm; omitting it would leave the Lipschitz bound incomplete. In particular Sn​ has continuous sample paths on the compact cube, so its maximum exists and is measurable.
Put

Choose a Cartesian finite net G⊂[−K,K]d whose coordinate spacings are at most η, including the endpoints. Every point of the cube is within Euclidean norm distance d​η of G, and
∣G∣≤(2K/η+2)d≤4dqd(1+a).

On the event Ln​≤nqa, replacing any u by a nearby grid point changes Sn​ by at most dn​. Since R>8d​ and log(nK2)≥log4, this is less than 4R​nlog(nK2)​. Therefore
