Write W(Σ)=νˉΣ. In a small radial patch r=rc​+χ with disturbance scale much smaller than rc​, the leading disk diffusion equation is ∂t​Σ≃3∂χ2​W. Taking the linearization around a uniform surface density Σc​ gives
∂t​δΣ=3W′(Σc​)∂χ2​δΣ.
(19)
For a local Fourier mode δΣ∝est+ikχ,
s=−3k2W′(Σc​)>0,​
(20)
because W∝Σ−1 has W′<0. Thus the effective density diffusion coefficient is negative and the disk has a viscous instability of an accretion disk.
A localized overdensity is a maximum of Σ but a minimum of W. At its center ∂χ2​W>0, so ∂t​Σ>0: it grows. The local radial mass flux is proportional to −∂χ​W, directing matter toward that minimum from both sides. This is the physical feedback: an overdense patch has a smaller viscous torque in an accretion disk and attracts further accumulation. The arbitrarily rapid k→∞ growth in this ideal negative-diffusion model is not a resolved prediction below the validity scale of the thin-disk and local viscous closure.
