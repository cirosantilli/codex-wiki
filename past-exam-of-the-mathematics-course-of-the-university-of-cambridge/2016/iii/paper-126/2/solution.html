A weight-k modular form for Γ=SL2​(Z) is a holomorphic function f on the complex upper half-plane satisfying
f(cτ+daτ+b​)=(cτ+d)kf(τ)((ac​bd​)∈Γ),
(8)
and holomorphic at a cusp. Since the full modular group has one cusp class, this last condition means that the period-one function has a convergent expansion f(τ)=∑n≥0​an​qn, q=e2πiτ, near infinity. A cusp form has a0​=0. The element −I makes a nonzero weight odd form impossible.
For weight zero, f descends to a holomorphic function on the quotient of the complex upper half-plane by the modular group. At an elliptic fixed point, invariance under its finite stabilizer makes the local Taylor series a series in the quotient coordinate. At the cusp, the q expansion extends it over q=0. The standard fundamental domain of the modular group, with its boundary identified and its cusp added, is a compact Riemann surface, the compactified modular curve X(1). The maximum modulus principle now proves
M0​(SL2​(Z))=C.​
(9)
Holomorphy at the cusp is essential to this conclusion.
For positive even k≥4, define the unnormalized Eisenstein series
Gk​(τ)=∑(m,n)∈Z2∖{(0,0)}​(mτ+n)−k.
(10)
The real-linear map (m,n)↦mτ+n is invertible, and on a compact subset of the complex upper half-plane its norm is uniformly comparable to m2+n2​. Thus the sum converges absolutely and locally uniformly for k>2, proving holomorphy. For γ=(ac​bd​),
mγτ+n=cτ+d(ma+nc)τ+(mb+nd)​.
(11)
The map (m,n)↦(ma+nc,mb+nd) is a bijection on the nonzero integer pairs, giving Gk​(γτ)=(cτ+d)kGk​(τ).
For the expansion, the cotangent partial-fraction Fourier kernel follows by differentiating the partial-fraction expansion of πcotπz and its geometric-series expansion −πi(1+2∑r≥1​e2πirz), valid for Imz>0. It gives, for even k≥2,
∑n∈Z​(z+n)−k=(k−1)!(2πi)k​∑r≥1​rk−1e2πirz.

The m=0 part of Gk​ is 2ζ(k), where ζ is the Riemann zeta function; positive and negative m contribute equally. Apply the kernel at z=mτ for m>0, and regroup the absolutely convergent sum according to N=mr. With the divisor sum σk−1​(N)=∑r∣N​rk−1, this proves
Gk​(τ)=2ζ(k)+(k−1)!2(2πi)k​N≥1∑​σk−1​(N)qN.​

The expansion has no negative powers and converges for ∣q∣<1, so it also proves holomorphy at a cusp and completes the proof of modularity.

Both E43​ and E62​ are modular forms of weight twelve. Their constant terms cancel, so their difference divided by 1728 is a cusp form. Integrality requires an argument: dividing an integral series by 1728 alone would not suffice. Expanding directly gives the integrality identity for the modular discriminant
Δ=125S3​+7S5​​+100S32​+8000S33​−147S52​.
