Use the simple-root form of Hensel lemma: if F∈Zp​[T] and t0​∈Zp​ satisfy F(t0​)≡0(modp) and F′(t0​)≡0(modp), there is a unique t∈Zp​ with F(t)=0 and t≡t0​(modp). More generally the same assertion holds over a complete discrete valuation ring, with its maximal ideal in place of (p).
Every nonzero 3-adic number has a unique expression
x=3kϵu,k∈Z,ϵ∈{1,−1},u∈U1​:=1+3Z3​.
(1)
Indeed its normalized P-adic valuation fixes k, and its unit residue modulo three fixes ϵ. Cubing is an automorphism on {1,−1}, so it remains to compute the cube map on the principal units.
For t∈Z3​,
(1+3t)3=1+9(t+3t2+3t3).
(2)
Thus U13​⊆U2​:=1+9Z3​. Conversely, given 1+9a∈U2​, apply Hensel lemma to
F(T)=T+3T2+3T3−a.
(3)
Modulo three it is T−a, and F′(T)=1+6T+9T2 is always a unit. The residue root amod3 therefore lifts, giving t with (1+3t)3=1+9a. Hence
U13​=U2​.​
(4)
This is the cube map on 3-adic principal units. Introducing T is essential: applying simple-root lifting directly to X3−u would fail because its derivative is divisible by three at every unit.
The map 1+3t↦tmod3 induces an isomorphism U1​/U2​≅(Z/3Z,+), since multiplication adds the first principal-unit digits modulo three. The valuation factor contributes Z/3Z independently, so the cube-class group of the 3-adic numbers is
Q3×​/(Q3×​)3≅Z/3Z×Z/3Z.​
(5)
Explicit generators are the classes of 3 and 4=1+3. Representatives are 3a4b with 0≤a,b<3. The class of 3 is detected by valuation modulo three, and that of 4 by its nonzero first principal-unit digit, so the two generators are independent.
