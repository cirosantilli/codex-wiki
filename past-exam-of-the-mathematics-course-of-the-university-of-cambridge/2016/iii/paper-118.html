The complex structure splits the complexified cotangent bundle into its (1,0) and (0,1) parts. Thus the space of smooth differential forms of type (p, q) is
Ap,q(M)=Γ(M,⋀p​(T1,0M)∗⊗⋀q​(T0,1M)∗).​
(1)
Here Γ denotes smooth sections of a vector bundle; exterior multiplication identifies the displayed bundle with a subbundle of the complexified exterior algebra. In holomorphic coordinates, such a differential form of type (p, q) has the expression
α=∑∣I∣=p,∣J∣=q​aIJ​(z,zˉ)dzI​∧dzˉJ​,
(2)
where the coefficients are smooth and both multi-indices are increasing. Negative bidegrees, or bidegrees exceeding the complex dimension, give the zero space.
On a complex manifold, the exterior derivative has just two type components, d=∂+∂ˉ. The conjugate Dolbeault operator and the Dolbeault operator are, respectively,
∂α=∑I,J,k​∂zk​∂aIJ​​dzk​∧dzI​∧dzˉJ​,∂ˉα=∑I,J,k​∂zˉk​∂aIJ​​dzˉk​∧dzI​∧dzˉJ​.
(3)
Keeping the differentiating factor at the front fixes the signs. These definitions are intrinsic because holomorphic coordinate changes preserve type. The two operators are the type projections of the exterior derivative, raising p and q, respectively.
For a holomorphic map f:M→N, its differential is a complex-linear map, so the pullback of a differential form sends (1,0) covectors to (1,0) covectors and (0,1) covectors to (0,1) covectors. In coordinates w on N,
f∗dwa​=∑j​∂zj​∂fa​​dzj​,f∗dwˉa​=∑j​∂zj​∂fa​​​dzˉj​.
(4)
The pullback of a differential form commutes with the wedge product of differential forms, so it preserves the counts of these factors. Consequently
f∗:Ap,q(N)⟶Ap,q(M).​
(5)
It also commutes separately with the Dolbeault operator and the conjugate Dolbeault operator, because it commutes with d and preserves type.

Let πi​:Cgi​→Xi​ be the quotient covering maps. Since the source universal cover is simply connected, the lifting criterion for a covering space gives a lift F:Cg1​→Cg2​ of f∘π1​. The lift is a holomorphic map because π2​ is locally a biholomorphism.
For every λ∈Λ1​, the difference F(z+λ)−F(z) belongs to the discrete Euclidean lattice Λ2​. It is a continuous function of z on a connected space, hence is constant. Differentiating shows that each coefficient of DF is Λ1​-periodic. Such a coefficient is bounded on a compact fundamental parallelepiped, and periodicity bounds it on all of Cg1​. Choose a constant positive Hermitian form on V. Its underlying flat Kähler metric is translation-invariant and descends through the Euclidean lattice to the compact complex torus X. The Dolbeault Hodge decomposition gives a unique Dolbeault Laplacian-harmonic representative of every Dolbeault cohomology class. By the Kähler Laplacian identity, these are also Hodge Laplacian-harmonic.
Choose a constant coframe on V. A lifted harmonic form is a sum of constant wedge basis forms with smooth periodic coefficients. For the flat metric, the Hodge Laplacian acts coefficientwise by the scalar constant-coefficient Laplacian. Each coefficient is a harmonic function on a compact flat real torus, hence is constant: integration by parts gives ∫∣Da∣2=0. Conversely every constant-coefficient form is harmonic. Thus the harmonic representatives are exactly the translation-invariant differential forms of type (p, q).
The constant (1,0) covectors form V∗=HomC​(V,C); the constant (0,1) covectors form the space of antilinear maps V∗=HomC​(V,C). Sending a constant wedge form to its cohomology class gives
H∂ˉp,q​(X)≅⋀p​V∗⊗⋀q​V∗.​

A connection on a vector bundle is a real-linear map (complex-linear for a complex bundle)
D:Γ(E)⟶A1(E),D(fs)=df⊗s+fDs.
(24)

Θ=D2∈A2(EndE).​
(25)
In a local frame with D=d+θ, it is Θ=dθ+θ∧θ.
Define the tensor product connection on decomposable sections by
D(s⊗t)=D1​s⊗t+s⊗D2​t.​
(26)
The Leibniz rule for D1​,D2​ makes this well-defined over smooth functions and makes it a connection on a vector bundle. Applying its covariant exterior derivative again gives

Treat 0 as the trivial group if G is not abelian. The restriction maps satisfy the presheaf identities. To verify the sheaf gluing axiom, take an open cover (Vi​) of U. If x∈/U, every section group is trivial. If x∈U, at least one Vi​ contains x. Every two such opens have an overlap containing x, so compatibility forces all their sections to have the same value g∈G. For the stalk of a sheaf, take the direct limit over neighbourhoods of y. If some neighbourhood V of y omits x, neighbourhoods inside V are cofinal and all their groups are trivial. Gy​≅{G,0,​y∈{x}​,y∈/{x}​.​​
(47)

Px​=MX,x​/OX,x​.
(48)

Px​≅m≥1⨁​Ct−m.​
(49)
This coordinate description is a vector-space identification; the intrinsic space is the quotient of germs, so a coordinate change need not preserve the displayed basis.
For each point inclusion ix​:{x}→X, a principal part defines a local meromorphic germ near x, whose quotient class is zero off x. Gluing with zero on the complement gives a map from its skyscraper sheaf to P. These maps induce an isomorphism on every stalk and therefore an isomorphism of sheaves:
P≅x∈X⨁​(ix​)∗​Px​.​
(50)
The direct sum of sheaves means the sheafification of the sectionwise direct-sum presheaf. Its sections can have infinitely many nonzero components globally, but their point supports form a locally finite family of subsets. This matches the fact that poles of a meromorphic function are locally finite. On a compact Riemann surface only finitely many points can occur; on a noncompact one a discrete infinite family is allowed.
For a global principal-part family P, choose local meromorphic lifts mi​ on a sufficiently small open cover. The differences mj​−mi​ are holomorphic functions and form a Čech cocycle. The connecting homomorphism sends P to the resulting class in H1(X,OX​). δ(P)=0⟺there is a global meromorphic function with exactly the prescribed principal parts.​
(51)
The connecting class is the obstruction to the Mittag-Leffler problem on a Riemann surface. It rules on the specified poles and their finite negative Laurent tails, with no additional poles allowed. When a solution exists, any two solutions differ by a global holomorphic function.

ResY​(α)=Ftr​​(−1)j+r−1​dt1​∧⋯∧dtr​​∧⋯∧dtn​​Y​.​
(58)
The remaining t coordinates form a holomorphic coordinate system on Y, and Ftr​​ is nonzero in this neighbourhood. The displayed top form is therefore nowhere zero. These neighbourhoods cover Y and part (a) guarantees agreement on their overlaps. The residue is a nowhere-vanishing global holomorphic section:
KY​≅OY​.​
(59)
This is the explicit residue trivialization for a degree n+1 projective hypersurface, consistent with the adjunction formula KY​≅OY​((n+1)−n−1), but obtained here directly from the indicated form.
