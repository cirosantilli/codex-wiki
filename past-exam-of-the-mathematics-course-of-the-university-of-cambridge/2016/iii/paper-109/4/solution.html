We prove both the general modular intersection bound for a set family and its sharper uniform version, the Frankl-Wilson theorem. Let p be prime, let L⊆Fp​ have size s, and let F be a family of subsets of [n]. Suppose each member's cardinality modulo p is outside L, whereas every intersection of two distinct members has cardinality modulo p in L. Then the general modular intersection bound is
∣F∣≤j=0∑s​(jn​),​
(27)
with (jn​)=0 for j>n. If every member has the same size k and s≤min{k,n−k}, the uniform Frankl-Wilson theorem sharpens this to
∣F∣≤(sn​).​
(28)
Here kmodp∈/L, so in particular s<p. Primality matters because the argument takes place over the finite field Fp​.
For each A∈F, define the polynomial
PA​(X1​,…,Xn​)=∏ℓ∈L​(∑i∈A​Xi​−ℓ).
(29)
At the indicator vector 1B​ of another member B, this polynomial is zero if B=A and nonzero if B=A. Replace every positive power of Xi​ by Xi​ in its expanded monomials, obtaining a multilinear polynomial PA​ of degree at most s. This multilinear reduction on the Boolean cube preserves values at every zero-one vector.
The resulting polynomials are linearly independent. Indeed, if ∑A∈F​cA​PA​=0, evaluating at 1B​ leaves cB​PB​(1B​)=0; the nonzero diagonal value forces cB​=0 for every B. But multilinear polynomials of degree at most s lie in the vector space with basis {∏i∈S​Xi​:∣S∣≤s}, whose dimension is ∑j=0s​(jn​). The bound on the dimension of a vector space gives the general modular intersection bound for a set family. This is the polynomial method in combinatorics: the intersection conditions produce an evaluation matrix with nonzero diagonal and zero off-diagonal entries.
To prove the sharper uniform bound, we first establish injectivity of inclusion between adjacent set layers. Over a field with field characteristic zero or greater than d+1, suppose coefficients cS​, indexed by d-sets in [n], satisfy
∑S⊂T∣S∣=d​​cS​=0for every (d+1)-set T,n≥2d+1.
(30)
Then all coefficients are zero. For d=0 this is immediate. For d≥1, fix distinct i,j and subtract the equations for U∪{i} and U∪{j}, where U is a d-set avoiding i,j. The result is
∑S⊂U∣S∣=d−1​​(cS∪{i}​−cS∪{j}​)=0.
(31)
Apply mathematical induction with degree d−1 on the n−2 remaining coordinates; its size condition holds because n−2≥2(d−1)+1. This forces cS∪{i}​=cS∪{j}​ for every such S. Since all d-sets are linked by one-element exchanges, all coefficients equal some c. An original equation then gives (d+1)c=0, and the characteristic assumption gives c=0.
For every S with ∣S∣≤s−1, now form the multilinear polynomial
GS​=∑i∈/S​XS∪{i}​+(∣S∣−k)XS​,XS​=∏i∈S​Xi​.
(32)
This is the multilinear reduction of XS​(∑i​Xi​−k), so it vanishes at every indicator vector of a k-set. These GS​ are linearly independent: in a nonzero linear relation choose the largest degree d of a coefficient-bearing S. The degree-(d+1) terms have coefficients ∑S⊂T, ∣S∣=d​cS​. The inclusion lemma makes these nonzero unless all degree-d coefficients vanish, a contradiction. It applies because d≤s−1, p>s, and n≥2s.
The GS​ are also independent jointly with the PA​. Evaluating a relation at the indicator vectors of the family first forces every coefficient of PA​ to vanish, since the GS​ vanish there; their own independence then kills the remaining coefficients. All these polynomials have degree at most s, so
∣F∣+∑j=0s−1​(jn​)≤∑j=0s​(jn​).
(33)
Subtracting the lower-degree contribution proves the sharp Frankl-Wilson theorem. For s=0 the general bound already gives ∣F∣≤1=(0n​), so no auxiliary polynomials are needed.
For the even cross-intersection assertion, work instead in the vector space over a finite field F2n​ and let
U=span{1A​:A∈A},V=span{1B​:B∈B}.
(34)
The parity assumption is exactly 1A​⋅1B​=0 in F2​. By bilinearity, all vectors in U are orthogonal to all vectors in V, giving V⊆U⊥. The coordinate dot product is nondegenerate over F2​, so dimU⊥=n−dimU. There is no requirement that U∩U⊥ be zero; only this dimension formula is used. Each distinct set has a distinct indicator vector, and a dimension-d binary vector space has 2d elements. Therefore the even cross-intersection bound is
∣A∣∣B∣≤2dimU2dimV≤2n.​
(35)
This is the even cross-intersection bound, including empty families, for which the product is zero.
For odd intersections, append a final coordinate one to every indicator vector:
a=(1A​,1),b=(1B​,1)in F2n+1​.
(36)
Their dot product is ∣A∩B∣+1=0 in F2​. Applying the same orthogonal complement over the binary field argument to their spans, now in dimension n+1, gives the required odd cross-intersection bound:
∣A∣∣B∣≤2n+1.​
(37)
Appending the coordinate preserves distinctness of the vectors, so the span-cardinality estimates remain valid. This proves the odd cross-intersection bound directly by reducing the odd condition to an even one in a larger vector space.
