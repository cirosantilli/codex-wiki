For the Monge-gauge Hamiltonian of a three-dimensional string, in Monge gauge, X′m=(0,1,Φ′) and Pm​=(P0​,P1​,Π). The momentum constraint gives P1​=−Φ′Π. The other Virasoro constraint then reads
−P02​+(Φ′Π)2+Π2+1+Φ′2=0.
(26)
Choosing the positive-energy branch,
P1​=−Φ′Π,P0​=−(1+Φ′2)(1+Π2)​.​
(27)
Substituting into the phase-space action leaves I=∫dtdσ(ΠΦ˙−H), so
F(z)=1+z2,H=(1+Φ′2)(1+Π2)​,H=∫dσH.​
(28)
For an infinite string this is an energy-density expression; even the undeformed string has infinite total energy. One may regulate the length or subtract its constant reference energy when a finite total energy is needed. The positive branch is unambiguous at the density level.
The canonical Hamiltonian field equations are
Φ˙=Π1+Π21+Φ′2​​,Π˙=∂σ​(Φ′1+Φ′21+Π2​​).
(29)
Consequently Π=Φ′ implies
Φ˙=Φ′,Φ=f(σ+t).​
(30)
The second equation also becomes Π˙=Φ′′, consistent with differentiating the first. These are one-direction traveling profiles along the string.
For the linear profile, the spatial string is the straight line X2=kX1+kt. It is tilted, and its coordinate intercept moves with speed k. This is also a uniformly moving straight string: the physical transverse velocity of a tilted string is the velocity normal to the line. With unit tangent (1,k)/1+k2​ and coordinate velocity (0,k), the normal speed is
v⊥​=1+k2​∣k∣​<1.​
(31)
Thus k>1 does not imply superluminal physical motion. Fixed-σ labels have spacelike trajectories when k>1, but labels on a string can slide tangentially and are not identifiable material particles. Choosing σ˙=−k2/(1+k2) removes the tangential velocity and gives the subluminal velocity (−k2,k)/(1+k2).
The induced worldsheet metric supplies a coordinate-invariant check:
htt​=k2−1,htσ​=k2,hσσ​=1+k2,deth=−1.​
(32)
The string worldsheet remains Lorentzian for every finite k, including k=1 and k>1. Its constant energy density is 1+k2. This linear profile is a tilted boosted straight string, rather than a localized finite-energy wave.
