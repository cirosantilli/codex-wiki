Define the open-string endpoint momentum flux along the open string by Jm​=eT2Xm′​+uPm​. Variation of the Nambu-Goto phase-space action gives
X˙m=ePm+uX′m,P˙m​=∂σ​Jm​,​
(16)
P2+T2X′2=0,X′⋅P=0.​
(17)
The two last equations are the Virasoro constraints. Integration of the momentum equation gives
P˙m​=Jm​(t,π)−Jm​(t,0).​
(18)
Thus the necessary and sufficient condition for total momentum conservation is equality of the two endpoint fluxes, component by component. Vanishing of both fluxes is a sufficient local boundary condition.
The spatial boundary term in the action variation is −∫dt[Jm​δXm]0π​. For free ends the endpoint variations are arbitrary, so
Jm​∣0,π​=0⟹P˙m​=0.​
(19)
In a gauge with u=0 and nonzero e this reduces to the usual Neumann boundary condition X′m=0. The conclusion is conservation of total momentum, not that total momentum must vanish. The PDF contains the derivative in this conclusion; the TeX transcription drops it.
For endpoints confined to x1=0, the Dirichlet boundary condition fixes δX1=0. It therefore imposes no requirement that J1​ vanish. Preserving the fixed position requires X˙1=eP1+uX′1=0 at each endpoint, but that is a different condition. For example, in u=0 gauge the endpoint momentum P1 is zero while eT2X′1 can be nonzero. Consequently P1​ is generally not conserved: the normal endpoint forces transfer momentum to the hyperplane. A dynamical D-brane recoils and carries the missing momentum. If the hyperplane is idealized as fixed, its external support absorbs the momentum. The combined string-and-support system conserves momentum; freely varying directions tangent to the hyperplane retain their vanishing-flux condition.
