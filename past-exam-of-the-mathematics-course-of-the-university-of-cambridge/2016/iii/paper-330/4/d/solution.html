Put a=ϕ1​, b=ϕ2​=a/2, zm​=4H/5, L=H−zm​ and D=(1−b)zm​. The quadratic hindered-settling flux is convex. At the initial internal interface its lower particle volume fraction is smaller than its upper particle volume fraction, so characteristic curves diverge: the entropy solution has a settling rarefaction fan, not a compression shock. Within this fan,
ϕ(z,t)=21​(1+us​tz−zm​​),zm​+us​(2b−1)t<z<zm​+us​(2a−1)t.​
(52)
The two bounding straight shocks initially have
V1​=−us​(1−a),V2​=us​b=2us​a​.​
(53)
The upper front's nominal meeting with the fan's upper edge occurs at
tc1​=us​aL​=5us​aH​,hc1​=H−a(1−a)L​=5aH(6a−1)​.​
(54)
The lower front meets the fan's lower edge at
tc2​=us​(1−b)zm​​=5us​(1−a/2)4H​,hc2​=1−bbzm​​=5(2−a)4aH​.​
(55)
The lower meeting is always physical in the stated range. The upper meeting need not happen before the suspension disappears; this distinction matters for sufficiently small a.
For the curved settling fronts within a rarefaction fan, the Rankine-Hugoniot condition gives z˙u​=us​(ϕ−1) at the clear-fluid front and z˙d​=us​ϕ at the deposit front. Substitution of the fan particle volume fraction and matching to the straight sections gives
zu​=zm​−us​t+2us​aLt​,zd​=zm​+us​t−2us​Dt​.​
(56)
The first curve applies after tc1​ if that meeting occurs, and the second after tc2​ until it leaves the fan or deposition ends. If both fronts meet inside the fan, their intersection is
tc​=us​(aL​+D​)2​,hc​=aL+bzm​=53​aH.​
(57)
The last surviving characteristic of a settling fan joins (zm​,0) to (hc​,tc​). It has constant particle volume fraction
ϕ∗​=aL​+D​aL​​,tc​hc​−zm​​=us​(2ϕ∗​−1).​
(58)
It is swallowed simultaneously by the clear-fluid and deposit shocks at final deposition. This requires b≤ϕ∗​≤a, which in the given geometry is equivalent to
a≥a∗​=1−32​​.​
(59)
Equality places the last characteristic curve on the upper fan edge. In the generic fan-intersection regime a>a∗​, both nominal meetings and the two curved fronts are realized.
For 0<a<a∗​ there is early extinction of a settling rarefaction fan. The deposit shock reaches the upper fan edge before the clear-fluid front does, at
te​=us​(1−a)2D​,ze​=zm​+us​(2a−1)te​.
(60)
It then travels through the remaining uniform particle volume fraction a with speed us​a. The upper front retains V1​ until the final intersection. Since the final bed height is still hc​=3aH/5, the actual stopping time is
tc​=us​(1−a)H−hc​​=us​(1−a)H(1−3a/5)​,0<a<a∗​.​
(61)
In this regime (hc1​,tc1​) is only an extrapolated intersection; for a<1/6 its extrapolated height is even negative. The line joining the initial internal interface to the final point would have a particle volume fraction greater than a, so it is not a characteristic curve emitted by that fan. The single fan-characteristic curve stopping-time construction does not apply to the entire printed interval 0<a<1/2; the missing subregime must be included.
Both panels label the actual final deposit height and time; the second shows the deposit shock leaving the fan while the upper front remains straight. Finally, the density inversion in a settling suspension puts more particles, hence denser fluid, above lighter fluid. Rayleigh-Taylor instability and overturning can accompany this profile in a real container. They are excluded by the one-dimensional quiescent kinematic sedimentation model used for these calculations.
