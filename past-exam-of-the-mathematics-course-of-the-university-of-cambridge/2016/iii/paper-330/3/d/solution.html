For a steady no-source particle-laden gravity current, Bx​=−vs​g′<0. Simply entraining particle-free ambient fluid increases volume and dilutes particle volume fraction; it cannot create particle buoyancy flux.
A steady increase can occur if sediment resuspension or bed erosion adds more suspended particle buoyancy than settling removes, if a tributary or lateral particle source supplies a positive flux, or if the buoyancy per unit particle amount increases through a change of the surrounding fluid's density. Changes of particle properties or another buoyancy source can also alter the flux, but they go beyond the fixed-particle, fixed-reference model. In a model with a source SB​,
∂t​(g′h)+Bx​=SB​−vs​g′.
(41)
Thus the buoyancy production in a particle-laden current requires SB​>vs​g′ in a steady current. In an unsteady current, Bx​ can be positive even without external addition: local depletion has ∂t​(g′h)<−vs​g′, so more buoyancy leaves a section than enters it while stored particles are being released. An instantaneous spatial flux increase is not by itself proof of particle production.
