For identical suspended particles, reduced gravity is proportional to particle volume fraction. If particles settle through the bed at speed vs​ and are not resuspended, the downward particle deposition flux removes a buoyancy-equivalent amount vs​g′ per unit bed area. The depth-integrated scalar mass conservation equation is
∂t​(g′h)+∂x​B=−vs​g′,B=ug′h.​
(37)
For a steady particle-laden gravity current, Bx​=−vs​B/(uh). If vs​/u≪1, with fixed positive E and drag/slope coefficients, the locally adjusted leading solution of the momentum and volume equations is u∼β∗​B1/3 and h∼h0​+E(x−x0​). The weak-settling attenuation of a sloping gravity current then gives
dxd​B1/3∼−3β∗​[h0​+E(x−x0​)]vs​​,
(38)
and hence
B(x)∼[Bo1/3​−3β∗​Evs​​ln(1+h0​E(x−x0​)​)]3.​
(39)
For an inlet at x0​=0 this has the requested logarithmic form. If the inlet is genuinely at x0​=0, the argument must contain x−x0​ in order to satisfy the specified inlet condition.
The speed factor must be the β∗​ derived in the previous part. The printed β omits the factor one-half multiplying Ecosθ and uses an undefined uppercase C instead of the governing equation's c. Taking C=c still leaves a nonzero leading momentum residual −21​E(B/u)cosθ for the printed speed; this discrepancy cannot be repaired by a correction of order vs​/u. With the given hydrostatic momentum equation, the logarithmic law is valid with β∗​, not the printed speed factor.
A controlled local approximation also needs vs​/(Eu)≪1 if E is allowed to become small. Indeed the exact volume balance is hx​=E−hux​/u; under u∼β∗​B1/3 its second term is vs​/(3u). The neglected depth and acceleration corrections become significant as B approaches zero.
