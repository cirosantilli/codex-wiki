Take logarithmic height ξ=ln(z/z∗​) with an arbitrary fixed length z∗​, so d/dξ=zd/dz. This is dimensionally meaningful and has the same derivatives as the abbreviated lnz. For example,
dξdQ​​=Qs​zQz​​−qQ​=Qo​2αMo​​​z1+m/2−qM1/2−qQ​.

The exponent vanishes and the coefficient is q, by the plume similarity in power-law stratification. Likewise the momentum coefficient is m and the buoyancy coefficient is f. The logarithmic-height plume equations are therefore
Q​ξ​Mξ​Fξ​​=−q(Q​−M1/2),=−m(M−FQ​/M),=−f(F−Q​).​​
(28)
The reference similarity solution becomes the equilibrium (1,1,1) of an autonomous differential equation. Normalizing each flux by its own height-dependent similarity value is essential; simply differentiating the unnormalized fluxes would miss the three subtraction terms.
