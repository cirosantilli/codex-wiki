OurBigBook.com Words: 133
Use the steady Boussinesq top-hat plume in a stratified ambient, with negligible ambient vertical momentum and no externally added heat or particles. The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis supplies inward velocity αw across circumference 2πb. Volume conservation gives πQz​=2πbαw, and bw=M​, hence
Qz​=2αM​.​
(19)
The momentum flux increases by the integrated buoyancy force. Writing g′=gΔρ/ρo​, the force per reference density and per π is g′b2. Since F=g′Q and b2=Q2/M, this gives
Mz​=g′b2=MFQ​.​
(20)
For the buoyancy flux, entrained material has the local environmental mass density. Let ρp​ be the plume's averaged density. Its scalar mass conservation is (ρp​Q)z​=ρa​Qz​. Since F=(g/ρo​)(ρa​−ρp​)Q, differentiating cancels both entrainment terms and gives
Fz​=ρo​g​[(ρa​Q)z​−(ρp​Q)z​]=Qρo​g​ρa′​.​
(21)
In stable density stratification, ρa′​<0 and a rising plume loses buoyancy flux. These are kinematic fluxes with the common factor πρo​ removed, so no additional factor of π belongs in the three differential equations.
