For every real α, direct substitution gives the axially equivariant monomial rational map identity
R(eiαz)=einαR(z),R(1/z)=1/R(z).​
(21)
The first is a spatial rotation through α about the third axis compensated by a target rotation through nα, hence by an isospin rotation of the Skyrmion. The second pairs the spatial half-turn about the first axis with a target half-turn about that axis. For n>1, these generate the continuous axial rotation group together with perpendicular half-turns, often written D∞​; it is the group of rotations preserving an axis in the three-dimensional special orthogonal group. Under rotations about the axis, the pure spatial stabilizer of the map itself includes the cyclic group Cn​, whereas the larger symmetry just described is combined spatial-target equivariance. Also R(z)=R(z)​ gives a paired reflection symmetry of its angular density.
The angular Jacobian of a rational map is
JR​=n2(1+∣z∣2n)2∣z∣2n−2(1+∣z∣2)2​.
(22)
It depends only on latitude. Put u=log∣z∣; then JR​=n2cosh2u/cosh2(nu). Since cosh(nu)≥coshu for n≥1, the maximum is n2 at u=0, the equator. For n>1 it vanishes at the two poles, producing an axially symmetric ring of angular Skyrme baryon density. For n=1, R is the identity: every spatial rotation is compensated by the same target rotation, and the rational map approximation for Skyrmions becomes the fully spherically symmetric Skyrmion hedgehog ansatz. The charge and uses are
B=n;n=1 gives the spherical hedgehog,n=2 gives a toroidal two-Skyrmion approximation.​
(23)
The degree-one construction captures the symmetry of the unit Skyrmion exactly; its radial profile still has to be solved. The degree-two map supplies the appropriate toroidal two-Skyrmion symmetry and useful initial data. Higher monomials give axial charge-n competitors, but axial symmetry need not minimize the energy: the usual lowest-charge examples already include the tetrahedral three-Skyrmion and the cubic four-Skyrmion. Topological charge determines neither shape nor energy optimality by itself.
