The four equations describe, respectively, vertical hydrostatic equilibrium in the central object's gravity, the vertical divergence of radiative flux balancing viscous dissipation, optically thick radiative diffusion, and the equation of state combining ideal gas gas pressure with radiation pressure. Here μ is dynamic viscosity, not the mean molecular weight μm​; κ is the opacity, σ the Stefan-Boltzmann constant, and Ω the local orbital frequency.
In the radiation-pressure limit, p=4σT4/(3c), so radiative diffusion gives
dzdp​=3c16σT3​dzdT​=−cκρF​.
(13)
Compare this with vertical hydrostatic equilibrium. Wherever ρ=0,
F(z)=κcΩ2​z.
(14)

μ=9κ4c​,​
(15)

Use characteristic, vertically averaged quantities at fixed radius. Let H be a disk thickness, ρ∼Σ/H, and let the alpha disk prescription give μ∼αp/Ω, up to a convention-dependent constant. From the preceding radiation pressure and constant-opacity balance, μ∼c/κ is independent of Σ; thus
p∼ακcΩ​.
(16)
Vertical hydrostatic equilibrium gives p∼ρΩ2H2∼ΣΩ2H, hence
H∼ακΩΣc​∝Σ−1.
(17)
The vertically averaged kinematic viscosity satisfies νˉΣ=∫μdz∼μH. Consequently
νˉΣ∼ακ2Ωc2​Σ−1.​
(18)
At fixed radius the omitted factors are independent of Σ, so this is the requested inverse-density scaling of the viscous torque in an accretion disk.
This is an order-of-magnitude vertical model, as requested. Imposing both exact pointwise μ=constant and exact pointwise μ∝p(z) would force p to be constant, contrary to nontrivial vertical hydrostatic equilibrium. The characteristic-pressure prescription supplies a scaling, not an exact simultaneous vertical solution to all those idealized equations.

Write W(Σ)=νˉΣ. In a small radial patch r=rc​+χ with disturbance scale much smaller than rc​, the leading disk diffusion equation is ∂t​Σ≃3∂χ2​W. Taking the linearization around a uniform surface density Σc​ gives
∂t​δΣ=3W′(Σc​)∂χ2​δΣ.
(19)
For a local Fourier mode δΣ∝est+ikχ,
s=−3k2W′(Σc​)>0,​
(20)
because W∝Σ−1 has W′<0. Thus the effective density diffusion coefficient is negative and the disk has a viscous instability of an accretion disk.
A localized overdensity is a maximum of Σ but a minimum of W. At its center ∂χ2​W>0, so ∂t​Σ>0: it grows. The local radial mass flux is proportional to −∂χ​W, directing matter toward that minimum from both sides. This is the physical feedback: an overdense patch has a smaller viscous torque in an accretion disk and attracts further accumulation. The arbitrarily rapid k→∞ growth in this ideal negative-diffusion model is not a resolved prediction below the validity scale of the thin-disk and local viscous closure.
