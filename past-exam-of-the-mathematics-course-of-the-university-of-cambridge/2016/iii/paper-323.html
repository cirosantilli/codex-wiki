The useful object is the Choi state of the entanglement-breaking channel. Put dA​=dimHA​, introduce a reference R with the same computational basis as A, and use the normalized maximally entangled state
∣Φ⟩RA​=dA−1/2​∑j​∣j⟩R​∣j⟩A​,CRB​=(idR​⊗E)(∣Φ⟩⟨Φ∣).
(1)
The entanglement-breaking channel makes CRB​ a separable quantum state. Its partial trace is TrB​CRB​=IR​/dA​, because E is a quantum channel. Write a separable positive operator decomposition
CRB​=∑y​αy​⊗βy​,αy​≥0,βy​≥0.
(2)
Discard any term with Trβy​=0: a positive operator with zero trace is zero. Define
τy​=Trβy​βy​​,Ey​=dA​(Trβy​)αyT​.
(3)
Each τy​ is a density operator. Each Ey​ is a positive operator, and taking the matrix transpose of the partial trace identity gives
∑y​Ey​=dA​(∑y​(Trβy​)αy​)T=IA​.
(4)
Thus the Ey​ form a POVM. In this reference-first convention, the Choi reconstruction formula is
E(L)=dA​TrR​[(LT⊗IB​)CRB​]=∑y​τy​Tr(Ey​L).
(5)
The factor dA​ is essential because we used a normalized Choi state. A measurement channel storing the outcome y, followed by the prescribed quantum state preparation of τy​, has precisely this action on every linear operator L. The desired factorization is therefore
EB←A=PB←YMY←A.​
(6)

Write X and Y for the classical quantum registers X and Y. By the quantum mutual information formula I(U:V)=S(U)+S(V)−S(UV), expanding the right-hand side of the quantum mutual information balance identity gives
​I(X:B)+I(XB:D)−I(B:D)=S(X)+S(B)−S(XB)+S(XB)+S(D)−S(XBD)−S(B)−S(D)+S(BD)=S(X)+S(BD)−S(XBD)=I(X:BD).​
(7)
All Von Neumann entropies here are evaluated in ω.
We use three facts: quantum mutual information is nonnegative by nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy; a local quantum channel cannot increase it by data processing for quantum mutual information; and the quantum mutual information of a classical-quantum state is its ensemble's Holevo quantity. Write χ(T) for the Holevo capacity, the supremum of the output Holevo quantity over finite input ensembles.
The XB marginal is an output ensemble for E, with inputs ρ(x)A​=TrC​ρ(x)AC​. Consequently I(X:B)ω​≤χ(E). Also ωXBD​ is obtained from σXYD​ by a quantum channel on XY which retains X and prepares B from Y. Hence
I(XB:D)ω​≤I(XY:D)σ​.
(8)
To bound the latter even for entangled states ρ(x)AC​, exhibit the conditional input ensemble after a local measurement. Set
q(y∣x)=Tr[(Ey​⊗IC​)ρ(x)AC​],ρCx,y​=q(y∣x)TrA​[(Ey​​⊗IC​)ρ(x)AC​(Ey​​⊗IC​)]​
(9)
when q(y∣x)>0; zero-weight outcomes can be omitted. The numerator is a positive operator, its trace is q(y∣x), and the q(y∣x) sum to one. Thus
σXYD​=∑x,y​PX​(x)q(y∣x)∣x,y⟩⟨x,y∣⊗N(ρCx,y​).
(10)
This is a classical-quantum state with an output ensemble for N, so I(XY:D)σ​≤χ(N). Combining these bounds with I(B:D)ω​≥0 yields
I(X:BD)ω​≤χ(E)+χ(N),χ(E⊗N)≤χ(E)+χ(N).​
(11)
The last step takes the supremum over all input ensembles on AC. The left-hand channel direction is B←A, as established in part (i); the printed A←B in the last inequality is a typographical reversal. Independent product ensembles also give the reverse inequality, so this proves Holevo-capacity additivity for entanglement-breaking channels.

Use an orthonormal eigenbasis of the Hermitian operator A and set bj​=⟨αj​∣B∣αj​⟩. Since B is a positive contraction,
0≤bj​≤1,∑j​bj​=r,Tr(AB)=∑j​λj​bj​.
(12)
For 0<r<d, the missing weight among the first r entries equals the weight in the remaining entries:
t:=∑j<r​(1−bj​)=∑j≥r​bj​.
(13)
The ordered eigenvalues satisfy λj​≥λr−1​ for j<r and λj​≤λr−1​ for j≥r. Therefore
∑j<r​λj​−Tr(AB)=∑j<r​λj​(1−bj​)−∑j≥r​λj​bj​≥λr−1​t−λr−1​t=0.
(14)
This argument does not require the eigenvalues of A to be positive. For r=0, positivity and zero trace give B=0; for r=d, the same argument applied to I−B gives B=I. Thus in every case
Tr(AB)≤j<r∑​λj​.​
(15)
The orthogonal projection onto the leading r eigenvectors attains equality. This is the Hermitian effect variational principle, extending the Ky Fan maximum principle to all positive contractions with the prescribed trace.

Choose Kraus representations
C(L)=∑a​Ca​LCa†​,D(M)=∑b​Db​MDb†​,∑a​Ca†​Ca​=IQ​,∑b​Db†​Db​=IK​.
(16)
The composite quantum channel has Kraus operators Tba​=Db​Ca​. Each has matrix rank at most k, because it factors through the k-dimensional space K, and
∑a,b​Tba†​Tba​=IQ​.
(17)
The operation fidelity uses the unsquared quantum fidelity on a purification of a density operator, so its square is entanglement fidelity. The Kraus formula for entanglement fidelity gives
Fop​(DC,ρQ​)2=∑a,b​∣Tr(ρQ​Tba​)∣2.
(18)
Indeed each overlap ⟨Ψρ​∣(IR​⊗Tba​)∣Ψρ​⟩ equals Tr(ρQ​Tba​).
For any one of these Kraus operators T, let ΠT​ be the orthogonal projection onto its image, of matrix rank rT​≤k. Since ΠT​T=T, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the Hilbert-Schmidt inner product gives the rank bound for a weighted operator trace:
∣Tr(ρQ​T)∣2​=∣Tr[(ΠT​ρQ​​)†(TρQ​​)]∣2≤Tr(ρQ​ΠT​)Tr(ρQ​T†T)≤​j<k∑​λj​​Tr(ρQ​T†T).​
(19)
The last step uses part (i) at rT​, followed by nonnegativity of the eigenvalues of the density operator ρQ​. Summing over the Kraus operators and using their completeness relation proves the finite-dimensional quantum compression converse:
1−ϵ≤Fop​(DC,ρQ​)2≤j<k∑​λj​.​
(20)
No invertibility of ρQ​, or restriction to an isometric decoder, was used.

The absolute value of an operator, trace norm, and operator norm are respectively
∣L∣=L†L​,∥L∥1​=Tr∣L∣,∥L∥op​=sup∥v∥=1​∥Lv∥.
(21)
The positive square root of an operator makes ∣L∣ well defined. Its eigenvalues sj​≥0 are the singular values of L, so ∥L∥1​=∑j​sj​ and ∥L∥op​=maxj​sj​.
Use the polar decomposition of a bounded operator L=U∣L∣, with the given unitary operator U. In an orthonormal eigenbasis ej​ for ∣L∣,
Tr(ZL)=∑j​sj​⟨ej​∣ZU∣ej​⟩.
(22)
The operator norm assumption and unitarity imply ∣⟨ej​∣ZU∣ej​⟩∣≤∥Z∥op​≤1. The triangle inequality therefore yields the required trace estimate:
∣Tr(ZL)∣≤j∑​sj​=∥L∥1​.​
(23)
Choosing Z=U† attains equality, which also gives the finite-dimensional trace duality formula ∥L∥1​=max∥Z∥op​≤1​∣Tr(ZL)∣. A singular L causes no difficulty: in a finite-dimensional square space the polar factor can be extended to a unitary operator on the complementary subspaces.

Let Γ=TB​ denote the partial transpose. The partial transpose of a maximally entangled projector is
(ϕ+)Γ=d1​∑i,j​∣i⟩⟨j∣⊗∣j⟩⟨i∣=dS​,
(24)
where S is the swap operator, satisfying S†S=I and ∥S∥op​=1. If σ has positive partial transpose, then σΓ is a positive operator of trace one, hence ∥σΓ∥1​=1.
For a pure state ϕ+=∣Φ⟩⟨Φ∣, the unsquared quantum fidelity obeys F(ϕ+,σ)2=⟨Φ∣σ∣Φ⟩. Using the trace-adjoint identity for partial transpose and part (i),
F(ϕ+,σ)2=Tr(ϕ+σ)=d1​Tr(SσΓ)≤d1​∥σΓ∥1​=d1​.
(25)
The overlap is nonnegative, so taking its square root proves the PPT maximally entangled overlap bound:
F(ϕ+,σ)≤d​1​.​
(26)
The product state ∣00⟩⟨00∣ attains the bound, so it is sharp.

For any normalized pure state on finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces HA​⊗HB​, the Schmidt decomposition gives
∣ψ⟩AB​=j=1∑r​pj​​∣uj​⟩A​∣vj​⟩B​,pj​>0,j∑​pj​=1,r≤min(dA​,dB​).​
(33)
The uj​ and vj​ form orthonormal sets in their respective spaces. The positive numbers pj​​ are the Schmidt coefficients, and r is the Schmidt rank. Taking a partial trace gives the reduced density matrices
ρA​=∑j​pj​∣uj​⟩⟨uj​∣,ρB​=∑j​pj​∣vj​⟩⟨vj​∣.
(34)
Thus the nonzero eigenvalues of the two reduced density matrices agree.
One construction is a singular value decomposition of the coefficient matrix M in ∣ψ⟩=∑a,b​Mab​∣a⟩∣b⟩. If M=UΣV†, the columns of U give uj​, the complex conjugates of the columns of V give vj​, and the singular values give pj​​. This also explains why the Schmidt coefficients are real and nonnegative.

Write ∣ζ⟩=∑i,j​Mij​∣i⟩A​∣j⟩B​. Normalization says ∥M∥HS​=1, and the Schmidt rank equals the matrix rank of M. Its nonzero singular values are therefore s1​,…,sr​ with ∑j​sj2​=1.
The overlap with the maximally entangled state is ⟨ϕ+∣ζ⟩=Tr(M)/d​. The trace norm estimate of Q3(i), with test linear operator I, followed by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, gives
∣TrM∣≤∥M∥1​=∑j=1r​sj​≤r∑j​sj2​​=r​.
(39)
For two pure states, the unsquared quantum fidelity is the absolute value of their overlap. Hence the maximally entangled overlap bound from Schmidt rank is
F(∣ϕ+⟩⟨ϕ+∣,∣ζ⟩⟨ζ∣)≤dr​​.​
(40)
The bound is attained by ∣ζ⟩=r−1/2∑j=0r−1​∣jj⟩. Thus uniform Schmidt coefficients on r aligned basis pairs give the greatest possible overlap for that Schmidt rank.

Use logarithms to base two, so Von Neumann entropy and quantum relative entropy are measured in bits. For density operators ρ,σ,
S(ρ)=−Tr(ρlog2​ρ),D(ρ∥σ)=Tr[ρ(log2​ρ−log2​σ)].​
(41)
The quantum relative entropy formula applies when the support of a positive operator ρ is contained in that of σ; otherwise D(ρ∥σ)=+∞. Use 0log2​0=0 in the Von Neumann entropy formula.
The binary density operator ω[p] is diagonal with eigenvalues 1−p,p. Its Von Neumann entropy is the binary entropy:
S(ω[p])=h2​(p)=−(1−p)log2​(1−p)−plog2​p.​
(42)
The two diagonal density operators commute, so their quantum relative entropy is
D(ω[p]∥ω[q])=(1−p)log2​1−q1−p​+plog2​qp​.​
(43)
A term with zero numerator has value zero; a positive numerator and zero denominator give +∞. In particular, D(ω[0]∥ω[0])=D(ω[1]∥ω[1])=0, whereas q=0,p>0 or q=1,p<1 gives +∞.

Choose PX​ uniform on AM​ and zero outside it, so the marginal density operator of Q is ρˉ​Q​=k−1∑m​ρ(m)Q​. Define the binary test for quantum decoding success by
F(0)=m∈AM​∑​∣m⟩⟨m∣X​⊗E(m)Q​,F(1)=IXQ​−F(0).​
(44)
Each E(m) is a positive contraction. The computational basis blocks of F(0) are these effects on AM​ and zero elsewhere. Thus 0≤F(0)≤I, making F a POVM on the whole space, including labels outside AM​.
On the correlated classical-quantum state, its acceptance probability is
Tr[F(0)ρXQ​]=k1​∑m​Tr[E(m)ρ(m)]=1−ϵ.
(45)
On the product of the marginal density operators,
Tr[F(0)(ρX​⊗ρˉ​Q​)]=k1​∑m​Tr[E(m)ρˉ​Q​]=k1​Trρˉ​Q​=k1​,
(46)
since ∑m​E(m)=IQ​. The two requested probabilities are therefore 1−ϵ and 1/k. In the notation for binary output density operators, the measurement channel sends the joint input to ω[ϵ] and the product input to ω[1−1/k]; their parameters describe outcome one, not outcome zero.

For the distribution and POVM from part 2, apply the data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy to the measurement channel. The quantum mutual information is the quantum relative entropy from the joint density operator to the product of its marginals, so for k≥2,
I(X:Q)ρ​​=D(ρXQ​∥ρX​⊗ρˉ​Q​)≥D(ω[ϵ]∥ω[1−1/k])=(1−ϵ)log2​k−h2​(ϵ)−ϵlog2​(1−1/k)≥(1−ϵ)log2​k−1.​
(47)
The last step uses h2​(ϵ)≤1 for the binary entropy and log2​(1−1/k)≤0. If 0≤ϵ<1, rearrangement and then the supremum over PX​ give the one-shot classical-quantum coding converse:
log2​k≤1−ϵI(X:Q)ρ​+1​≤1−ϵsupPX​​I(X:Q)ρXQ​​+1​.​
(48)
For k=1, the sole POVM effect is IQ​, forcing ϵ=0, and the bound is trivial. For ϵ=1, division by 1−ϵ is undefined: the undivided inequality remains valid and gives no size restriction. The displayed coding bound is therefore understood for ϵ<1, or with a vacuous +∞ right-hand side at ϵ=1.
An alternative uses the original decoder directly. Let M be the uniform message and M its measured estimate. For k≥2, Fano's inequality bounds the conditional entropy by H(M∣M)≤h2​(ϵ)+ϵlog2​(k−1). The Holevo bound gives
I(X:Q)ρ​≥I(M:M)=log2​k−H(M∣M)≥log2​k−h2​(ϵ)−ϵlog2​(k−1)≥(1−ϵ)log2​k−1.
(49)
Thus the same coding bound follows by bounding the classical mutual information obtainable from the POVM.
