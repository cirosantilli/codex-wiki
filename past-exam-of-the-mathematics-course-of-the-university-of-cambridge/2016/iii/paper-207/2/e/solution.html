Age changes during follow-up, so the generator matrix Q(s) changes with time. The transition matrix must solve ∂t2​​P(t1​,t2​)=P(t1​,t2​)Q(t2​), with P(t1​,t1​)=I. It is generally not the matrix exponential of one constant matrix; even exp(∫Q(s)ds) is not generally valid because the matrices at different times need not commute.
For example, write the age-dependent onset rate as λ(s) and keep progression rate ν constant. Then
P12​(t1​,t2​)=∫t1​t2​​λ(u)exp(−∫t1​u​λ(v)dv)e−ν(t2​−u)du.
(14)
Even when the integrated onset rate is elementary, this remaining integral need not have an elementary closed form. Special cases can have closed forms; time dependence does not by itself prove universal impossibility.
For a tractable likelihood function, freeze age at its left-endpoint value, or a midpoint value, on each observation interval [tj​,tj+1​). The genetic covariate is already constant. This piecewise-constant covariate approximation gives a constant matrix Qj​ on each interval and a product of closed-form interval probabilities:
L=j∏​[eQj​(tj+1​−tj​)]xj​xj+1​​.​
(15)
Here xj​ is the observed state at tj​, and the likelihood function is conditional on the initial state. The approximation improves when intervals are short relative to the variation in the age effect.
