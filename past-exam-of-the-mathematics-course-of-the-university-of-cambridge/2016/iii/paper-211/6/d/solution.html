Take the original measure as the risk-neutral measure for this zero-interest model, or interpret S as a discounted stock price. ξt​ is the arbitrage-free value of the European contingent claim with payoff g(ST​), and πt​ is its option delta and replicating stock holding. Part (b) supplies the self-financing portfolio:
stock holding πt​=VS​(t,St​),cash holding ξt​−πt​St​.​
(63)
With the cash asset identically one, its value is ξt​ and its gains satisfy dξt​=πt​dSt​, so it exactly replicates g(ST​). This delta hedge is admissible since the assumed nonnegative V gives nonnegative wealth. In this continuous-path setting the holdings can be taken predictable.
The auxiliary change of measure in part (c) expresses the option delta as an expectation of terminal payoff sensitivity. It changes the stock drift to aa′ and is generally not the original risk-neutral measure for the zero-interest stock market. Its role is to value sensitivity under the tilted state dynamics, while the actual replicating strategy trades under the original dynamics. No general claim of market completeness is needed, including when a can vanish.
