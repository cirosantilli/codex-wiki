Both Poisson processes start at zero and have càdlàg paths. On disjoint time intervals their increments are independent, and independence of the two processes makes the difference increments independent as well. Their increment laws depend only on interval length, so X has stationary increments. For a time interval of length h, the probability that either Poisson process has a jump is 1−e−h→0. This proves stochastic continuity. Consequently the difference is a Lévy process, equivalently a continuous-time symmetric simple random walk with total jump rate one and jumps +1 and −1 each chosen with probability 1/2.
