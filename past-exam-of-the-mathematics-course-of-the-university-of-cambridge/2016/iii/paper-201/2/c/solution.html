The overshoot hypothesis as printed cannot hold for every R>0. For R≤1, TR​=0 and MTR​​=1; choosing R<1/τ contradicts MTR​​≤τR. The intended and sufficient assumption is the overshoot bound for R≥1, on the event {TR​<∞}. We use those thresholds below; when R≤1, the requested stopped-time bound is trivial because T∧TR​=0.
Fix R≥1 and set S=T∧TR​. By part (a), the stopped martingale satisfies 0≤MS∧n​≤τR: before stopping it is below R, at T it is zero, and at TR​ the assumed overshoot bound applies. Thus the square of this stopped process is integrable. For ΔMn​=Mn+1​−Mn​,
Xn+1​−Xn​=1{S>n}​(2Mn​ΔMn​+(ΔMn​)2−σ2).
(17)
The event {S>n} is Fn​-measurable and contained in {T>n}. Conditional expectation of the linear term is zero, while the variance assumption makes the remaining conditional expectation nonnegative. The stopped increments here are bounded by the stopping and overshoot bounds, so all conditional expectations are legitimate. Consequently
(Xn​)n≥0​ is a submartingale.​
(18)
Since X0​=1, we have σ2E(S∧n)≤EMS∧n2​−1. Bounded-time optional stopping theorem also gives EMS∧n​=1, so
EMS∧n2​≤τREMS∧n​=τR.
(19)
Apply the monotone convergence theorem to S∧n. This proves the stronger estimate (τR−1)/σ2 and hence the requested absorption-time bound from conditional variance and overshoot:
E(T∧TR​)≤σ2τR−1​≤σ2τ2R​(R≥1).​
(20)
In particular this stopping time is finite almost surely. For 0<R≤1, S=0 and Xn​=1 is a constant submartingale, while the stopping-time expectation is zero.
