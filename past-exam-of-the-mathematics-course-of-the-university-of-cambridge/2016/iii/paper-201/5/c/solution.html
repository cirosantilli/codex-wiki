The domain is the union of the horizontal and vertical strips. The four connected components of its boundary carry the constants a,b,c,d in the northeast, northwest, southwest and southeast, respectively.
First justify finiteness of T. A small open ball around (2,2) lies outside the domain. By part (b), planar Brownian motion visits that ball almost surely in finite time. A continuous path from z∈D to that ball must meet ∂D first. Hence T<∞ almost surely, and BT​∈∂D by continuity.
The harmonic function u is smooth inside D. The Itô formula, localized on relatively compact subdomains, makes u(Bt∧T​) a local martingale. Its boundary definition uses the continuous extension of u. Since u is bounded, this stopped process is a true martingale with uniform integrability: pass through the compact exhaustion using bounded convergence theorem. In particular Ez​u(Bt∧T​)=u(z).
Now Bt∧T​→BT​ almost surely as t→∞. The dominated convergence theorem, boundedness of u, and its continuous boundary values give the bounded harmonic representation with almost sure Brownian exit:
u(z)=Ez​u(BT​)=Ez​f(BT​).​
(48)
Almost sure exit and boundedness of the harmonic function are both used. The domain's unboundedness causes no loss of expectation here because the stopped function values are uniformly bounded.
