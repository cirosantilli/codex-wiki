Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2016 / iii / Paper 201 / 5 / b / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 iii Paper 201 5 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 208
Translate the centre of the ball to zero. Choose an inner radius ρ strictly smaller than the ball's radius. If the starting point already lies in the ball there is nothing to prove; otherwise write d=∣z∣>ρ and choose R>d. Let Hρ​,HR​ be the first hits of the two circles.
The Brownian exit time from the annulus ρ<∣z∣<R is finite almost surely: it is no later than exit of the first coordinate from (−R,R), which is finite by part (a). The function log∣z∣ is harmonic on this annulus. The Itô formula and optional stopping theorem apply to its bounded stopped values, yielding
logd=Pz​(Hρ​<HR​)logρ+Pz​(HR​<Hρ​)logR.
(45)
Solving gives the planar Brownian annulus hitting probability
Pz​(Hρ​<HR​)=logR−logρlogR−logd​.​
(46)
As R→∞ this tends to one, while the event in the formula is contained in {Hρ​<∞}. Thus the inner circle is hit almost surely. That circle lies strictly inside the original open ball, proving the recurrence of planar Brownian motion in the form requested:
Pz​(hit the given nonempty open ball)=1.​
(47)
A probability-one intersection over balls with rational centres and positive rational radii makes this conclusion simultaneous for all nonempty open balls, since every such ball contains one from that countable family.
Using a smaller inner circle ensures an actual visit to the open ball, rather than only a hit of its boundary.
