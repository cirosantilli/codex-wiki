The full-measure subset Eα​=Cα[0,1] is measurable in C[0,1], because continuity identifies its seminorm with a countable supremum:
∥w∥α​=sups<t, s,t∈Q∩[0,1]​(t−s)α∣w(t)−w(s)∣​.

Its finiteness event is a countable union of countable intersections of coordinate-measurable events. The sigma-algebra specified on Eα​ is precisely the trace of the coordinate sigma-algebra on C[0,1]. Define ν(A)=μ(A) for trace-measurable A⊆Eα​; equivalently restrict the Brownian law to its full-measure subset. Since μ(Eα​)=1, no renormalization is needed, and all finite-dimensional coordinate laws are unchanged. This is the required probability measure:
ν(Cα[0,1])=1,(Xt​)0≤t≤1​ is Brownian under ν.​
