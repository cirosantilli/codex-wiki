Let x denote the generating object, the image of the unique object of the terminal category. The free braided monoidal category on one object can be described syntactically. Its objects are all fully parenthesized expressions built from x, the monoidal unit object I, and a binary monoidal tensor product. Thus x⊗(I⊗x) and (x⊗I)⊗x are distinct objects, though canonically isomorphic. Its morphisms are generated by the associators, unitors, braidings and their inverses, closed under composition and tensor product, subject to the pentagon, triangle, naturality and hexagon axioms. No symmetry relation is imposed on the braiding. The embedding of the terminal category selects x.
The braid category B has objects the nonnegative integers, with
B(m,n)={Bn​,∅,​m=n,m=n,​
(1)
where Bn​ is the braid group on n strands and B0​,B1​ are trivial. Composition is stacking braids, with the first morphism followed by the second. The monoidal tensor product is addition on objects and side-by-side juxtaposition of braids; its monoidal unit object is 0. The associators and unitors are identities, so it is a strict monoidal category.
Choose the positive crossing convention once and for all. Its braiding cm,n​:m+n→n+m is the block braid moving the first m strands over the next n strands while retaining the order within each block. In particular c1,1​=σ1​. The usual braid group relations express the naturality and hexagon laws for these block braids. The generating functor 1→B selects 1.
The distinction is parenthesized tensor expressions versus strand counts: the first construction keeps the structural isomorphisms visible, while B has strict tensor arithmetic. These descriptions give the two requested free constructions without needing to establish their universal properties.

Define the length of a formal tensor expression recursively by
∣I∣=0,∣x∣=1,∣u⊗v∣=∣u∣+∣v∣.
(2)
The canonical strict monoidal functor Q:Fbr→B sends u to ∣u∣, sends every associator and unitor to an identity braid, and sends cu,v​ to the block braiding c∣u∣,∣v∣​. The pentagon and triangle become identity equations; the braiding axioms become the corresponding block-braid equations. Thus the assignment respects the defining relations, and
Q(u⊗v)=Qu+Qv,QI=0
(3)
on the nose. It is a braided monoidal functor with identity comparison maps.
To prove that Q is an equivalence of categories, choose a standard parenthesization x[n] of n copies of x, with x[0]=I. On x[n], define the image of σi​ by canonically exposing the ith and (i+1)st factors, applying cx,x​ there, and restoring the chosen parentheses. The monoidal coherence theorem makes this independent of the structural rebracketing. Crossings on disjoint pairs commute by the tensor interchange law. The adjacent braid group relations follows from the hexagon laws and naturality of the braiding, as in the Yang–Baxter calculation below. Hence these assignments give group homomorphisms Bn​→Aut(x[n]) and a functor S:B→Fbr.
Equip S with the canonical rebracketing maps x[m]⊗x[n]→x[m+n]. Their monoidal functor axioms follow from the monoidal coherence theorem; the block-braiding compatibility follows by iterating the two hexagon laws. Therefore S is a strong monoidal functor compatible with the braiding.
We have QS=1B​, including its comparison maps. For every formal expression u, there is a canonical structural isomorphism
θu​:x[∣u∣]⟶u
(4)
obtained by rebracketing and inserting the units appearing in u. These maps form a natural isomorphism SQ⇒1Fbr​. To check naturality, it suffices to check the generating morphisms: for associators and unitors it is exactly monoidal coherence; for cu,v​ it follows from the hexagon expansion into the elementary crossings defining S. Composition and tensor product then preserve the equation. The same structural coherence shows that θ is monoidal.
Thus Q has a specified quasi-inverse and is the required equivalence:
Q:Fbr≃Bis braided and strict monoidal.​
(5)
Only ordinary monoidal coherence, together with the defining braiding axioms, has been used; a separate braided coherence theorem is unnecessary.

An opmonoidal functor F:(C,⊗,I)→(D,⊗,J) consists of a functor, a natural transformation
F2​(X,Y):F(X⊗Y)⟶FX⊗FY,
(6)

An opmonoidal monad on a monoidal category is a monad (T,η,μ) whose endofunctor is an opmonoidal functor and whose unit and multiplication of a monad are opmonoidal natural transformations. Suppress only the canonical parentheses. Explicitly,
T2​ηX⊗Y​=ηX​⊗ηY​,T0​ηI​=1I​,
(10)
T2​(X,Y)μX⊗Y​=(μX​⊗μY​)T2​(TX,TY)T(T2​(X,Y)),T0​μI​=T0​T(T0​).
(11)
The composite opmonoidal functor T2 has tensor comparison T2​(TX,TY)T(T2​(X,Y)) and unit comparison T0​T(T0​), explaining the last two equations.
For two algebras for a monad (X,a) and (Y,b), define
(X,a)⊗(Y,b)=(X⊗Y,(a⊗b)T2​(X,Y)),I=(I,T0​).​
(12)
Let c=(a⊗b)T2​(X,Y). The unit law for a monad algebra follows at once from the opmonoidality of η:
cηX⊗Y​=(aηX​)⊗(bηY​)=1.
(13)
For the multiplication law, naturality of T2​, the algebra laws, and the opmonoidality of μ give
cT(c)​=(a⊗b)(Ta⊗Tb)T2​(TX,TY)T(T2​(X,Y))=(aμX​⊗bμY​)T2​(TX,TY)T(T2​(X,Y))=(a⊗b)T2​(X,Y)μX⊗Y​=cμX⊗Y​.​
(14)
The two unit-comparison equations above similarly make (I,T0​) a algebra for a monad. If f,g are morphisms of algebras for a monad, naturality of T2​ shows that f⊗g is an algebra morphism.
For a third algebra (Z,d), the base associator is also an algebra morphism: its intertwining equation is precisely the opmonoidal associativity axiom, followed by a⊗b⊗d. The two base unitors are algebra morphisms by the opmonoidal unit axioms. Their pentagon and triangle commute because they commute after the faithful forgetful functor, and the lifted maps have exactly the same underlying morphisms.
The Eilenberg-Moore category is therefore monoidal, with these lifted constraints. Its forgetful functor U:CT→C preserves the tensor product, unit object and constraints exactly, so it is a strict monoidal functor.

On the monoidal category of modules over the commutative ring k, consider the monad T=H⊗k​− coming from the unit and multiplication of the bialgebra H. Its opmonoidal functor structure has comparison maps
T2​(h⊗(x⊗y))=∑(h(1)​⊗x)⊗(h(2)​⊗y),T0​=ε:H⊗k​k≅H→k.

We use the left internal-hom convention in which [M,N] is right adjoint to −⊗k​M, so its evaluation morphism has domain [M,N]⊗k​M. For a Hopf algebra over the commutative ring k, take the full k-module
[M,N]=Homk​(M,N)
(18)
and give it the internal hom for modules over a Hopf algebra
(h⋅f)(u)=∑h(1)​⋅f(S(h(2)​)⋅u).​
(19)
The antipode is an antihomomorphism, S(hg)=S(g)S(h), so
(h⋅(g⋅f))(u)=∑h(1)​g(1)​⋅f(S(g(2)​)S(h(2)​)⋅u)=((hg)⋅f)(u).
(20)
The unit acts identically. Thus this is a left H-module.
The evaluation morphism is H-linear. Using the diagonal bialgebra action and the antipode identity,
∑(h(1)​⋅f)(h(2)​⋅u)=∑h(1)​⋅f(S(h(2)​)h(3)​⋅u)=h⋅f(u).
(21)
Now let q:L⊗k​M→N be H-linear and define its ordinary curried map q​(ℓ)(u)=q(ℓ⊗u). To see that it too is H-linear, compute
(h⋅q​(ℓ))(u)​=∑h(1)​⋅q(ℓ⊗S(h(2)​)⋅u)=∑q(h(1)​⋅ℓ⊗h(2)​S(h(3)​)⋅u)=q(h⋅ℓ⊗u)=q​(h⋅ℓ)(u).​
(22)
Conversely, any H-linear map L→[M,N] uncurries to an H-linear map by the already established linearity of evaluation. The ordinary tensor–hom adjunction therefore restricts to a natural bijection
HomH​(L⊗k​M,N)≅HomH​(L,[M,N]).​
(23)
Postcomposition by an H-linear map makes [M,−] a functor. This proves that the category of left H-modules is a left closed monoidal category. All modules are allowed; no inverse antipode or finite-dimensional dual is required. Stating the tensor–hom convention explicitly avoids confusing this construction with the closure on the opposite side.

σi​σj​=σj​σi​ (∣i−j∣>1),σi​σi+1​σi​=σi+1​σi​σi+1​.

Inverting the naturality and monoidal equations shows that the inverses form a monoidal natural transformation g∗​⇒f∗​. Every such monoidal transformation is therefore invertible, without requiring a bijective antipode.

The induced internal hom for modules over a Hopf algebra on Homk​(H,H) is
(h⋅T)(x)=∑h(1)​T(S(h(2)​)x),
(58)
where both occurrences of H have their left regular action. If T is a morphism of left H-modules, then
(h⋅T)(x)=∑h(1)​S(h(2)​)T(x)=ε(h)T(x).
(59)
So every such morphism is an invariant vector of a module over a Hopf algebra.
For the converse, a calculation valid for every k-linear T is
∑(h(1)​⋅T)(h(2)​x)=∑h(1)​T(S(h(2)​)h(3)​x)=hT(x).
(60)
If T is invariant, its left-hand side is instead ∑ε(h(1)​)T(h(2)​x)=T(hx). Hence T(hx)=hT(x) for all h,x, which is precisely the R-module homomorphism condition.
In fact an endomorphism of the left regular module is determined by a=T(1) and has T(x)=xa. Conversely each right multiplication map is left H-linear. Thus the invariant space has the explicit description
Homk​(H,H)H=EndH​(H)={Ra​:x↦xa∣a∈H}.​
(61)
As a vector space it is isomorphic to H, and under composition its algebra is the opposite algebra Hop. No dimension or semisimplicity hypothesis is needed.

Because the direction of a lax monoidal functor compares the target tensor with the image of the source tensor, the given maps φX,Y​:X∙Y→X⋄Y have the correct direction. They need not be invertible unless a strong monoidal functor is intended.
Their associativity condition is the following commutative diagram, with the associators of the two structures labelled explicitly:
(X∙Y)∙Zα∙↓X∙(Y∙Z)​φX,Y​∙1​1∙φY,Z​​​(X⋄Y)∙ZX∙(Y⋄Z)​φX⋄Y,Z​​φX,Y⋄Z​​​(X⋄Y)⋄Z↓α⋄X⋄(Y⋄Z).​
(62)
The two unit conditions, since φ0​=1I​, are the unit commutative diagrams
I∙XλX∙​↘​φI,X​​X​I⋄X↙λX⋄​​X∙IρX∙​↘​φX,I​​X​X⋄I↙ρX⋄​.​
(63)
Together with the assumed naturality, these are exactly the axioms for the requested monoidal structure. There are no further coherence conditions.
The faithful strict monoidal functor U allows all three diagrams to be checked after applying it. Both tensor structures then have the same underlying tensor and constraints in V. In particular, the unit conditions are equivalent to
UφI,X​=1UI⊗UX​,UφX,I​=1UX⊗UI​.​
(64)
The associativity square becomes the displayed diagram with every tensor replaced by ⊗, both associators by αV, and every φ by Uφ. Faithfulness reflects equality of the two resulting composites. One should not identify φI,X​ itself with an identity in C: its domain and codomain can be distinct objects with the same image under U. Intrinsically it is (λX⋄​)−1λX∙​, and similarly on the right.

Use right comodules with coaction u↦∑u(0)​⊗u(1)​. Fix the convention that ⋄ is induced by m and ∙ by n. We classify lax monoidal structures first; the strong case is identified at the end.
Every natural transformation of the indicated tensor bifunctors is determined by a linear functional γ:H⊗H→k, and its components have the comodule tensor transformation formula
φM,N​(u⊗v)=∑u(0)​⊗v(0)​γ(u(1)​,v(1)​).​
(65)
To prove the assertion, set γ=(ε⊗ε)φH,H​. On the cofree comodules V⊗H,W⊗H, naturality with respect to h↦v⊗h forces the component to act as φH,H​ on the two H factors. Apply naturality to the pair of coactions M→M⊗H, N→N⊗H, then apply their counits. This yields the formula. Conversely the formula is plainly natural with respect to comodule morphisms, and recovers γ from the regular comodules.
It remains to impose exactly three kinds of equations. First, the component must be H-colinear from the tensor with product n to the tensor with product m. Expanding its two coactions gives (C), the multiplication compatibility for a comodule tensor transformation
∑m(a(1)​,b(1)​)γ(a(2)​,b(2)​)=∑γ(a(1)​,b(1)​)n(a(2)​,b(2)​).​
(66)
Necessity follows by taking M=N=H and applying the two counits; sufficiency follows by substituting the identity into the coaction formula for arbitrary M,N.
Second, the two unit triangles in the preceding part are exactly (U):
γ(1,a)=ε(a),γ(a,1)=ε(a).​
(67)
Here 1=j(1k​) is the common algebra unit. These identities are forced by the regular comodule and sufficient by the counit law.
Third, expand the associativity diagram on three right comodules. The two scalar factors, after their counits are removed, are (A):
∑γ(m(a(1)​,b(1)​),c)γ(a(2)​,b(2)​)=∑γ(a,m(b(1)​,c(1)​))γ(b(2)​,c(2)​).​
(68)
The product in this expression is m, because the intermediate arguments M⋄N and N⋄P are source-tensor objects. Necessity follows from the three regular comodules; sufficiency is the same substitution for arbitrary coactions. Using (C), the same condition can equivalently be written with the target product n:
∑γ(a(1)​,b(1)​)γ(n(a(2)​,b(2)​),c)=∑γ(b(1)​,c(1)​)γ(a,n(b(2)​,c(2)​)).
(69)
This is the normalized bialgebra scalar cocycle equation for that convention.
Thus the required structures correspond bijectively to all linear maps γ satisfying (C), (U) and (A). No convolution invertibility has been imposed for a lax monoidal functor structure. If “monoidal” is required to mean strong, add precisely that γ is invertible under the convolution product for coalgebra maps on Homk​(H⊗H,k). Its convolution inverse γˉ​ supplies the inverse component by the same displayed formula; conversely natural inverse components recover a convolution inverse by the regular-comodule argument. In that case (C) is the explicit bialgebra cocycle twist relation
n(a,b)=∑γˉ​(a(1)​,b(1)​)m(a(2)​,b(2)​)γ(a(3)​,b(3)​).​
(70)
This also records which multiplication lies on each side of the twist. Interchanging the names of m,n interchanges the two tensor conventions, rather than silently changing the equation.
For a concrete lax example, take the two-dimensional bialgebra with basis 1,e, product e2=e, and Δ(e)=e⊗e, ε(e)=1. Set m=n and γ(1,1)=γ(1,e)=γ(e,1)=1, γ(e,e)=0. Normalization and (C) hold, and (A) follows by checking the eight basis triples: triples involving 1 reduce to normalization, and the triple (e,e,e) has both sides zero. But the (e,e) tensor component is zero, so this lax monoidal functor is not strong.
