Expand the fractional CMB temperature fluctuation as Θ(n)=∑ℓm​aℓm​Yℓm​(n). Under statistical isotropy, its CMB angular power spectrum is defined by
⟨aℓm​aℓ′m′∗​⟩=Cℓ​δℓℓ′​δmm′​,Cℓ​=2ℓ+11​m∑​⟨∣aℓm​∣2⟩.​
(21)
The normalization is for dimensionless temperature, rather than temperature in kelvin. The spherical harmonics separate angular scales, with large ℓ corresponding to small angles.
For approximately constant R,ϕ,ψ, a useful solution of the photon-baryon acoustic oscillator is
δγ​=−4(1+R)ψ+Acos(krs​)+Bsin(krs​),rs​(η)=∫0η​3[1+R(η′)]​dη′​.​
(22)
Here rs​ is the comoving sound horizon. With slowly varying R, the oscillatory phase is still krs​ and its homogeneous amplitude varies approximately as (1+R)−1/4 under the short-period approximation. Evolving potentials add gravitational driving rather than erase this phase coherence.
The regular growing adiabatic initial conditions produce an initial density displacement and negligible initial acoustic velocity outside the horizon. They therefore select the cosine phase, with B approximately zero, for all modes. At last scattering, extrema of compression and rarefaction occur at krs​(η∗​)≃nπ. The observed monopole-plus-potential Sachs-Wolfe combination is Θ0​+ψ=δγ​/4+ψ, so baryon loading shifts its equilibrium and makes alternating peaks unequal. The velocity produces a phase-shifted Doppler CMB anisotropy.
Projection from the Cosmic microwave background last-scattering surface places a mode near ℓ≃kχ∗​, where χ∗​=η0​−η∗​ is its comoving radial distance in a flat universe. The resulting adiabatic acoustic-peak spacing is
ℓn​≃nπrs​(η∗​)χ∗​​,n=1,2,….​
(23)
Coherent acoustic phases turn successive compressions and rarefactions into the CMB peak series. Finite-width last scattering, Doppler projection, evolving potentials and diffusion shift or broaden actual peaks, so this is the requested approximate spacing, not an exact prediction for every peak.
