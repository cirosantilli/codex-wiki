The test-function space is D(Rn)=Cc∞​(Rn). Its topology is the strict inductive limit topology of the spaces DK​ of smooth functions supported in a fixed compact set K, each with the seminorms pK,j​(φ)=max∣α∣≤j​supK​∣∂αφ∣. In particular, a sequence converges in D precisely when its supports lie in one compact set and all its derivatives converge uniformly. The indices α use multi-index notation.
The distribution space D′(Rn) consists of continuous complex-linear forms on D. Equivalently, for every compact K there are CK​ and an integer qK​≥0 such that
∣⟨u,φ⟩∣≤CK​max∣α∣≤qK​​supK​∣∂αφ∣,suppφ⊂K.
(1)
We use distributional convergence: uj​→u means ⟨uj​,φ⟩→⟨u,φ⟩ for every φ∈D. The pairings are bilinear, with no complex conjugation. A test function f is identified with its regular distribution ⟨f,φ⟩=∫fφ.
For a distribution u and a test function ψ, their smoothing convolution with a test function is
(u∗ψ)(x)=⟨uy​,ψ(x−y)⟩.​
(2)
It is a smooth function, with ∂xα​(u∗ψ)(x)=⟨uy​,∂αψ(x−y)⟩. Indeed, for x in a compact neighborhood all translated tests have support in one compact set, and their difference quotients converge in the space of test functions. Notice that u∗ψ need not be compactly supported.
Choose a nonnegative mollifier ρ∈Cc∞​(B1​) with ∫ρ=1, and write ρε​(x)=ε−nρ(x/ε). If ρ​(x)=ρ(−x), then
⟨u∗ρε​,φ⟩=⟨u,ρ​ε​∗φ⟩.
(3)
The right-hand test tends to φ in D: its supports lie in suppφ+B1​ for ε≤1, and every derivative converges uniformly by the approximate-identity argument. Thus the smooth regularizations converge to u as distributions.
To obtain actual compactly supported approximants, choose a smooth cutoff function χ equal to one on B1​ and supported in B2​, and set
fj​(x)=χ(x/j)(u∗ρ1/j​)(x)∈D(Rn).​
(4)
For each fixed test function φ, the cutoff is identically one on its support once j is large. Hence ⟨fj​,φ⟩=⟨u,ρ​1/j​∗φ⟩→⟨u,φ⟩. This proves D is dense in D′, and in fact establishes the density of test functions in distributions and gives a convergent approximating sequence for each distribution. The expanding cutoff is essential when u has noncompact support of a distribution.
For the radial limit, take a test function φ and introduce t=r2−1 in polar coordinates. The Jacobian rdr=dt/2 gives
⟨um​,φ⟩=∫−1∞​msin(m∣t∣)A(t)dt,A(t)=21​∫02π​φ(1+t​(cosω,sinω))dω.

When φ is supported away from the origin, A vanishes near t=−1 and extends to a compactly supported smooth function on the whole line. The folded sine approximation to a Dirac delta is exposed by setting B(s)=A(s)+A(−s): integration by parts gives
∫0∞​msin(ms)B(s)ds=B(0)+∫0∞​cos(ms)B′(s)ds⟶2A(0),

by the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma. Therefore
um​⟶δS1​in D′(R2∖{0}),⟨δS1​,φ⟩=∫02π​φ(cosω,sinω)dω.​
(7)
This surface delta distribution is arclength measure on the unit circle. By the level-set normalization of a surface delta, it is 2δ(∣x∣2−1): the factor two cancels the gradient magnitude ∣∇(∣x∣2−1)∣=2 on the circle. It is not twice arclength measure.
For a test function that can meet the origin, the endpoint cannot be discarded. Now A(−1)=πφ(0). Its right derivative is integrable, with A′(−1+s)=(π/4)Δφ(0)+O(s) from differentiated Taylor expansion: the angular average cancels odd Taylor terms, so A(t)=πφ(0)+(π/4)(1+t)Δφ(0)+O((1+t)2) near t=−1. Applying integration by parts separately on the negative and positive intervals gives
⟨um​,φ⟩=2A(0)−A(−1)cosm−∫−10​cos(mt)A′(t)dt+∫0∞​cos(mt)A′(t)dt.

The last two integrals tend to zero by the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma. Thus the radial quadratic oscillation defect in two dimensions gives the stronger oscillating point-mass defect formula
um​=δS1​−πcosmδ0​+oD′​(1).​
