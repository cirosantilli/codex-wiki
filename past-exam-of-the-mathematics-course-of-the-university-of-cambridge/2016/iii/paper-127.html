A Serre fibration p:E→B has the homotopy lifting property for every disc: given h:Dk×I→B and h0​:Dk→E with ph0​=h(−,0), there is a lift h extending h0​. Equivalently one may test this property on CW complexes.
For a map g:E→B, its mapping-path fibration replacement is
Eg​={(e,γ):γ(0)=g(e)},p(e,γ)=γ(1).
(1)
Path spaces have the compact-open topology, with the usual compactly generated convention. The inclusion s(e)=(e,constg(e)​) satisfies ps=g. Forgetting the path is a homotopy inverse to s: shorten γ to u↦γ(tu), from its constant initial path at t=0 to the original path at t=1. The equivalence here means a commuting triangle over the same base whose map of total spaces is a homotopy equivalence; it does not assert a fibrewise homotopy inverse over the base. Appending the successive segments of a moving base path gives the homotopy lifting property for p, even for arbitrary parameter spaces, so p is in particular a Serre fibration. Its ordinary fibre over b0​ is the homotopy fibre
hofibb0​​(g)={(e,γ):γ(0)=g(e), γ(1)=b0​}.
(2)
For a based Serre fibration, with fibre inclusion j:F↪E, the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration is
⋯⟶πk​(F)j∗​​πk​(E)p∗​​πk​(B)∂​πk−1​(F)⟶⋯⟶π1​(B)∂​π0​(F)⟶π0​(E)⟶π0​(B).
(3)
The maps j∗​,p∗​ compose based sphere representatives with j,p; the maps on path components do the same with points. To define ∂, represent a class by (Ik,∂Ik)→(B,b0​) and lift the cube starting at e0​ on its bottom face, keeping its vertical sides at e0​. The opposite face then gives (Ik−1,∂Ik−1)→(F,e0​). Relative lifting for a CW pair gives this construction; its based homotopy class is independent of choices. At k=1 it records the component of the endpoint of the lifted loop. The last terms are pointed sets rather than groups in general.
Now write W=S1∨Sn and let f:W→Sn be the collapse map. Inclusion of the n-sphere is a section, so f∗​:πk​(W)→πk​(Sn) is surjective in every positive degree. Thus all connecting maps in its homotopy fibre sequence vanish. The fibre is path-connected, and
π1​(F)≅π1​(W)≅Z,πn​(F)≅ker(πn​(W)→πn​(Sn)).
(4)
The section also gives the first assertion when n=2, by killing the connecting map from π2​(S2).
The universal cover of W consists of a line with an n-sphere attached at each integer. It is homotopy equivalent to the wedge sum ⋁k∈Z​Sn. This cover is (n−1)-connected, so the Hurewicz theorem identifies its nth homotopy group with its nth homology. Choose a generator t of the fundamental group whose action shifts the sphere indexed by k to that indexed by k+1. The resulting homotopy groups as modules over the fundamental group give
πn​(W)≅R=Z[t,t−1].
(5)
Every lifted sphere maps to Sn with degree one. Consequently f∗​ is the augmentation ε(∑ak​tk)=∑ak​, with the trivial R-action on Z. The inclusion of the fibre respects the fundamental group actions, so
πn​(F)≅kerε=(t−1)R≅Ras a Z[π1​(F)]-module​.
(6)
The augmentation ideal of the infinite cyclic group is generated by t−1: multiply a Laurent polynomial by a power of t and use divisibility by t−1 when its value at one vanishes. Multiplication by t−1 is injective, giving the last isomorphism. This is a free module of rank one, whose underlying abelian group has countably infinite rank; its π1​-action is not trivial.

Choose the Hopf map η and orientations so its Hopf invariant is one. The cellular cohomology of Yd​ is Z in degrees 0,2,4 and zero otherwise. Let x,z be generators in degrees two and four. Precomposing η with a degree-d self-map of S3 represents dη. The induced map of mapping cones is the identity on the two-cell and has degree d on the four-cell. Since the Hopf mapping cone has cup square equal to its top generator, naturality of the cup product gives x2=dz. Equivalently, precomposition scales the Hopf invariant by degree. Hence
H∗(Yd​;Z)=Z[x,z]/(x2−dz,xz,z2),∣x∣=2,∣z∣=4​.
(7)
The coefficient is d, not d2: multiplication in π3​(S2) is being used, rather than postcomposition by a degree-d map of the target sphere.
For X=Xd,f​, the only nonzero cellular boundary is C4​→C3​, multiplication by f. Thus its cellular cohomology gives
Hq(X;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,ker(f:Z→Z),Z/fZ,0,​q=0,2,q=3,q=4,otherwise.​
(8)
Let x again generate H2, and let z be the class of the four-cell cochain. Collapsing the three-sphere gives a map X→Yd​ which pulls the degree-two generator back to x and the degree-four generator back to z. Hence x2=dz, now interpreted modulo f. Every other product of positive-degree classes vanishes by dimension. This is the cohomology ring of a Hopf attachment with a sphere summand. More explicitly, if f=0,
H∗(X;Z)=Z[x,z]/(fz,x2−dz,xz,z2)​.
(9)
If f=0, there is additionally a generator y of degree three, with xy=y2=yz=0. The displayed description of the groups uses Z/0Z=Z, so the zero case is included. In particular, a nonzero f kills the degree-three cohomology but can leave torsion in degree four.
There are no one-cells, and attaching cells of dimension at least three does not change π1​. Thus X is simply connected. The Hurewicz theorem in degree two gives
π1​(X)=0,π2​(X)≅H2​(X;Z)≅Z​.
(10)
To compute π3​, choose a map g:X→CP∞=K(Z,2) representing x. This Eilenberg–MacLane space can be realized by the classifying space of S1. On S2 the map is the standard inclusion; on S3 it is constant, and it extends over the four-cell since π3​(K(Z,2))=0. Its homotopy fibre T is the pullback of the universal circle bundle S∞→CP∞. The map on π2​ is an isomorphism, so the long exact sequence of homotopy groups of a fibration gives π1​(T)=π2​(T)=0 and π3​(T)≅π3​(X). Therefore the Hurewicz theorem identifies the latter with H3​(T;Z).
Over W=S2∨S3, the circle-bundle total space is
P=S3∪S1​(S1×S3).
(11)
The first summand is the Hopf fibration total space over S2, and the second is the trivial bundle over S3. Their intersection is the fibre over the wedge point. The Mayer–Vietoris sequence gives H3​(P)=ZA⊕ZB, with generators from the two three-spheres. These are the lifts of η1​ and i2​. The circle bundle over W also has a 2-connected total space, so lifting and the Hurewicz theorem identify dη1​+fi2​ with dA+fB.
Over the attached four-disc the bundle is trivial. The resulting relative pair has the homology of (S1×D4,S1×S3), so H4​(T,P)=Z and H3​(T,P)=0. Its boundary map sends a generator to the lift of the attaching map, namely dA+fB. The long exact sequence in relative homology now gives
H3​(T)=⟨dA+fB⟩ZA⊕ZB​.
(12)
Applying Smith normal form to this one relation proves the third homotopy group of a Hopf attachment with a sphere summand:
π3​(Xd,f​)≅Z⊕Z/gcd(d,f)Z​.
(13)
Since d>0, the greatest common divisor is positive even when f=0. This derivation uses the required map to an Eilenberg–MacLane space and determines the extension, rather than merely the orders of its pieces.

Choose a contractible free G-space EG, with BG=EG/G=K(G,1) the classifying space of a discrete group. The Borel construction gives the fibration
Sn−1⟶E=EG×G​Sn−1⟶BG.
(14)
Because the action on the sphere is free, the map E→Sn−1/G has contractible fibre EG and is a homotopy equivalence. The quotient is a closed (n−1)-dimensional manifold, so Hk(E;F)=0 for k>n−1.
The hypothesis on integral sphere cohomology makes the G-action on Hn−1(Sn−1;F) trivial as well. Thus the Serre spectral sequence has just two rows, both copies of H∗(BG;F), in fibre degrees zero and n−1. If u is the fibre generator, the only possible differential is dn​, and its transgression defines
Δ=dn​(u)∈Hn(BG;F).
(15)
The multiplicative differential rule gives dn​(au)=(−1)∣a∣a⌣Δ. Up to the graded sign, this is multiplication by Δ. For i>0, the terms E∞i,n−1​ and E∞i+n,0​ must both vanish because their total degrees exceed n−1. The first vanishing says the relevant multiplication map has zero kernel; the second says it has zero cokernel. Therefore
Δ⌣−:Hi(BG;F)∼​Hi+n(BG;F),i>0​.
(16)
This is periodic group cohomology from a free sphere action. Degree zero was excluded for a reason: E∞0,n−1​ can contribute to the quotient's top cohomology.
For Cp​=Z/p, use its periodic free resolution with alternating maps g−1 and N=1+g+⋯+gp−1. Applying HomZ[Cp​]​(−,Fp​) makes every differential zero, so Hi(BCp​;Fp​) is one-dimensional in every nonnegative degree. The two-step shift of the resolution supplies a nonzero degree-two class u whose cup product gives the periodicity isomorphisms. For odd p, let v be a nonzero degree-one class. Graded commutativity gives v2=0, and the shift gives nonzero vuk and uk in every degree. For p=2, the two resolution maps agree over F2​[C2​], giving a one-step shift whose degree-one class has nonzero powers. Hence the cohomology ring of a finite cyclic group over its prime field is
H∗(BCp​;Fp​)={F2​[v],ΛFp​​(v)⊗Fp​[u],​p=2,∣v∣=1,p odd,∣v∣=1, ∣u∣=2.​​
(17)
Here the exterior factor is an exterior algebra and the polynomial factor is a polynomial ring. For odd p, u may be chosen as the Bockstein homomorphism of v.
If G contained Cp​×Cp​, restriction would give that subgroup the same free, cohomologically trivial sphere action. It would therefore also have period n in positive-degree cohomology. But B(Cp​×Cp​)≃BCp​×BCp​, and the Künneth theorem gives
dimFp​​Hi(B(Cp​×Cp​);Fp​)=∑a+b=i​1=i+1.
(18)
These dimensions strictly increase, so the groups in degrees i and i+n cannot be isomorphic. Thus G contains no subgroup isomorphic to Z/p×Z/p for any prime p.

The quaternionic projective space HPn is the space of one-dimensional right quaternion subspaces of Hn+1. Equivalently it is the quotient of the unit sphere S4n+3 by simultaneous right multiplication by unit quaternions. Its coordinate filtration has one open cell Hk≅R4k in each dimension 4k, for 0≤k≤n. Hence its cellular cohomology is Z in those dimensions and zero otherwise.
Let H be the quaternionic tautological line bundle. Its unit sphere bundle is S4n+3→HPn, with fibre S3. The Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle shows that multiplication by its Euler class e∈H4 is an isomorphism from H4k to H4k+4 for k<n. Choose the generator u=−e. Its powers generate every nonzero positive degree, giving the cohomology ring of quaternionic projective space
H∗(HPn;Z)=Z[u]/(un+1),∣u∣=4​.
(19)
This also accounts for n=0.
First take n=2. Under the coordinate inclusion i:CP2→HP2, the pulled-back quaternionic line is the quaternionic extension of the complex tautological line L. As a complex rank-two bundle it is L⊕L: a transition scalar c∈C acts on the two complex coordinates of a quaternion by c and c. Put x=c1​(L∗), the degree-two generator of the cohomology ring of complex projective space. The Whitney sum formula for Chern classes gives
i∗e=c2​(L⊕L)=(−x)x=−x2,i∗u=x2​.
(20)
Here the Euler class of a complex vector bundle is its top Chern class, using the complex orientation. In particular this degree-four pullback has coefficient one; it is not a multiple of larger absolute value. The compatible tautological bundles on the projective filtrations give the same equality for every n, and multiplicativity then determines the whole ring map:
i∗(uk)=x2k,xn+1=0​.
(21)
It is zero whenever 2k>n. These facts are the complex inclusion into quaternionic projective space.
For an odd prime p, the Steenrod reduced powers are natural stable cohomology operations
Pi:Hq(−;Fp​)⟶Hq+2i(p−1)(−;Fp​).
(22)
They satisfy P0=1, the Cartan formula, Pia=0 when 2i>∣a∣, and Pia=ap when ∣a∣=2i. In particular, on CP∞ one has P1x=xp and Pix=0 for i>1. The Cartan formula and the binomial theorem give
Pi(x2k)=(i2k​)x2k+i(p−1).
(23)
Pass to the infinite projective spaces, where i∗:H∗(HP∞;Fp​)→H∗(CP∞;Fp​), u↦x2, is injective. The equality just obtained determines the Steenrod powers on quaternionic projective space; restricting to the finite spaces gives
Pi(uk)=(i2k​)uk+i(p−1)/2​
(24)
with coefficients modulo p and powers above n set to zero. In particular P1u=2u(p+1)/2, P2u=up, and Piu=0 for i>2. The infinite-space argument matters: the finite inclusion cannot detect those degrees for which x2k=0.
Finally put Q=HP15/HP12 and S=Σ48HP3. Both have reduced cohomology Z in degrees 52,56,60 and zero otherwise. Choose integral generators a13​,a14​,a15​ for Q whose pullbacks under the quotient map are u13,u14,u15, and suspended integral generators b1​,b2​,b3​ for S from u,u2,u3.
Use p=5. Naturality for the quotient and the formula above give
P1a13​=(126​)a15​=a15​modulo 5.
(25)
Stability under the suspension isomorphism instead gives
P1b1​=Σ48(P1u)=2b3​modulo 5.
(26)
Any homotopy equivalence h:Q→S would induce isomorphisms on the rank-one integral groups, so h∗b1​=εa13​ and h∗b3​=δa15​ with ε,δ∈{1,−1}. Reducing modulo five and commuting h∗ with P1 would require ε=2δ in F5​. Neither 1 nor −1 equals 2 or −2 modulo five. Therefore
HP15/HP12≃Σ48HP3​.
(27)
The essential point is that integral generator signs constrain Steenrod comparisons. Arbitrary changes of basis over F5​ could rescale these two nonzero coefficients into agreement; a genuine equivalence must also preserve the integral lattices, where only the two signs are available.
