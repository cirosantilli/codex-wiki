The dispersion relation gives the zero-group velocity saddle
k0​=−2(cd​−i)U​,ω0​=iμ−4(cd​−i)U2​=−4(1+cd2​)U2cd​​+i[μ−4(1+cd2​)U2​].
(35)
Thus the absolute growth rate is σ(0)=μ−U2/[4(1+cd2​)]. More generally the saddle growth rate along a ray is
σ(V)=μ−4(1+cd2​)(U−V)2​,
(36)
so a frame travelling at V=U sees the maximal rate μ.
For U>0, the three open regions are
μ<00<μ<4(1+cd2​)U2​μ>4(1+cd2​)U2​​: linear stability,: convective hydrodynamic instability,: absolute hydrodynamic instability.​​
(37)
The line μ=0 is the temporal marginal boundary; the parabola μ=U2/[4(1+cd2​)] is the convective-to-absolute boundary. The Green function of the linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation has the prefactor t−1/2, so zero exponential rate on the latter boundary still allows algebraic decay of the impulse response. This is the stability diagram of the linear complex Ginzburg-Landau equation.
