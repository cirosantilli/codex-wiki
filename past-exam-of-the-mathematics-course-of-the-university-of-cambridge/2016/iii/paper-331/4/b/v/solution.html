At the optimum, d=q∗1/3​ and the initial direction is (1,ρ+). Since the final direction has ratio ρ−,
Gmax​=q∗2/3​(ρ−ρ+​)21+(ρ+)21+(ρ−)2​.
(57)
Using ρ±=2R(1±s), R2(1−s2)=1, and q∗​=(5−3s)/(5+3s) simplifies this to the exact maximum gain:
Gmax​=1−s1+s​(5+3s5−3s​)5/3,s=1−R−2​,R>1.​
(58)
As R→∞, s=1−1/(2R2)+O(R−4), so q∗​→1/4 and (1+s)/(1−s)∼4R2. Consequently the requested large-Reynolds-number transient growth scaling is
T∗∼34log4​R,Gmax​∼4−2/3R2,n=1, A=34log4​, m=2, B=4−2/3.​
(59)
These are the optimal time and gain of a Reynolds-scaled triangular model. The leading gain can also be read from the off-diagonal matrix exponential entry: with τ=t/R, b=(4R/3)(e−τ/4−e−τ) is maximized at τ=4log4/3 and has leading maximum R4−1/3.
