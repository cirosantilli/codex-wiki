Use the kinetic-energy inner product ⟨v,u⟩=∫v∗⋅udV, with perturbation energy ∥u∥2/2. On divergence-free fields the primal generator, before the pressure projection, is
A(t)u=−(U⋅∇)u−(u⋅∇)U+Re−1∇2u.
(42)
The base state is also divergence-free. The periodic boundary terms in x,z cancel, and decay at ∣y∣→∞ removes the remaining boundary terms. Integration by parts changes −U⋅∇ to +U⋅∇; the Laplacian is self-adjoint under these conditions. Writing Jij​=∂j​Ui​, the shear term −Ju has adjoint operator −JTv. A pressure gradient pairs to zero with a divergence-free field. Hence
A†(t)v=(U⋅∇)v−(∇U)Tv+Re−1∇2v−∇π,
(43)
where the last term enforces the divergence-free projection.
To conserve ⟨v(t),up​(t)⟩ over the finite interval, the physical-time adjoint obeys −∂t​v=A†(t)v. Put τ=−t and ud​(τ)=v(−τ); then ∂τ​ud​=A†(−τ)ud​. This reverses the interval from t=T towards t=0, or τ=−T towards τ=0.
For Ω=∇×U, the vorticity cross-product identity and the curl of a cross product
Ω×ud​=(ud​⋅∇)U−(∇U)Tud​,∇×(U×ud​)=(ud​⋅∇)U−(U⋅∇)ud​
(44)
hold when both fields are divergence-free. Their difference equals the transport and shear terms in the adjoint operator. Therefore the adjoint linearized Navier-Stokes evolution is
∂τ​ud​=Ω(−τ)×ud​−∇×[U(−τ)×ud​]−∇pd​+Re−1∇2ud​,∇⋅ud​=0.​
(45)
The Leray-Helmholtz projection determines the adjoint pressure. The time-dependent base state is evaluated at −τ; no extra time derivative of that base state appears in the spatial adjoint operator.
