Set Li​=λi​+k−i, for 1≤i≤k. These are distinct nonnegative integers with L1​>⋯>Lk​. Using the falling factorial, the matrix entry can be written
(λi​−i+j)!1​=Li​!(Li​)k−j​​.
(52)
When k−j>Li​, the falling factorial vanishes, agreeing with the stipulated convention for a negative factorial argument.
The column polynomials (X)k−1​,(X)k−2​,…,(X)0​ are monic of successive descending degrees. Subtracting lower-degree combinations of columns turns them into Xk−1,Xk−2,…,1 without changing the determinant. The Vandermonde determinant therefore gives
det((λi​−i+j)!1​)=∏i​Li​!∏i<j​(Li​−Lj​)​.
(53)
The positive sign comes from reversing the usual increasing-degree Vandermonde columns at the same time as writing the differences as Li​−Lj​.
We now match this denominator with the hook-length formula. The hook lengths in row i are precisely
{1,2,…,Li​}∖{Li​−Lr​:r>i}.
(54)
To verify the claim, the first hook in the row has length Li​. As the column index passes from j to j+1, its hook length drops by 1 plus the number of lower rows ending at column j. The skipped lengths are exactly Li​−Lr​ for those rows: if λr​=j, then Li​−Lr​=λi​−j+r−i. At the right-hand end, lower rows having the same length as row i account for the missing smallest lengths by the same formula. This describes all omitted values, and the remaining count is Li​−(k−i)=λi​.
Consequently
∏x∈λ​h(x)=∏i​∏r>i​(Li​−Lr​)Li​!​=∏i<j​(Li​−Lj​)∏i​Li​!​.
(55)
Combining the two identities with the hook-length formula proves the determinantal dimension formula
fλ​=n!det((λi​−i+j)!1​)​.
(56)
