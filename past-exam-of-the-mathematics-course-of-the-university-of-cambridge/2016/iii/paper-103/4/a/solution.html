For a cell x=(i,j) in a Young diagram, its hook of a Young diagram consists of x, all cells to its right in row i, and all cells below it in column j. If λ′ is the conjugate partition, its hook length is
h(i,j)=λi​−j+λj′​−i+1.
(34)
The hook-length formula is
fλ​=∏x∈λ​h(x)n!​​.
(35)
In the Greene–Nijenhuis–Wilf hook walk, a walk at a noncorner cell chooses uniformly one of the other h(i,j)−1 cells in its hook, and stops at a corner. Moves are strictly rightwards or downwards, so termination is certain. A terminal cell must be a Removable node of a Young diagram. For a chosen corner (α,β), its probability is zero unless the start satisfies a≤α and b≤β.
For a start in that northwest rectangle, define
Ai​=h(i,β)−1=λi​−β+α−i,Bj​=h(α,j)−1=β−j+λj′​−α.
(36)
The corner conditions are λα​=β and λβ′​=α, so Aα​=Bβ​=0, and all earlier Ai​,Bj​ are positive. Define
Fα​=1,Fa​=Aa​1​∏i=a+1α−1​(1+Ai​1​)(a<α),
(37)
and
Gβ​=1,Gb​=Bb​1​∏j=b+1β−1​(1+Bj​1​)(b<β).
(38)
Then the closed conditional probability is
p(α,β∣a,b)=Fa​Gb​​.
(39)

(h(a,β)−1)(h(α,b)−1)1​∏i=a+1α−1​(1+h(i,β)−11​)∏j=b+1β−1​(1+h(α,j)−11​).

The separate definitions of Fα​,Gβ​ handle starts in the terminal row, in the terminal column, or at the corner itself without division by zero.
To prove the formula, the product definitions give the identities
Ai​Fi​=∑r=i+1α​Fr​,Bj​Gj​=∑s=j+1β​Gs​,

h(i,j)−1=Ai​+Bj​.

The first-step equation for the hook walk terminal probability is
(h(i,j)−1)pi,j​=∑r=i+1α​pr,j​+∑s=j+1β​pi,s​.

Jumps beyond the target rectangle contribute zero. Substituting pi,j​=Fi​Gj​ proves this equation, and pα,β​=1 gives its boundary value. Since every step increases a coordinate, backward recursion determines the probability uniquely. This proves the claimed expression.
