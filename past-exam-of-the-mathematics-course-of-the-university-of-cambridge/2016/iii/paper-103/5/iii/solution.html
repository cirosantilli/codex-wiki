Assume n≥2. The standard Young tableaux of shape (n−1,1) are Tk​, for 2≤k≤n: the sole second-row entry is k, and the first row is
1, 2,…,k−1, k+1,…,n.
(65)
This lists all of them, since 1 must occupy the first box and the rest of the first row is forced to increase. Their contents are
cTk​​(r)=⎩⎨⎧​r−1,−1,r−2,​r<k,r=k,r>k.​​
(66)
Write wk​=wTk​​. The only nontrivial adjacent swaps exchange the lower entry with k−1 or k+1. Substituting the content differences into Young orthogonal form yields all generator actions:
sj​wk​=⎩⎨⎧​−k−11​wk​+1−(k−1)21​​wk−1​,k1​wk​+1−k21​​wk+1​,wk​,​j=k−1,j=k,j∈/{k−1,k}.​​
(67)
In the first line at k=2, the square-root coefficient is zero and the action is −w2​, so no vector w1​ is needed. The second line is used only when k<n, since the generator sn​ does not exist.
The corresponding Specht module is the sum-zero standard representation of the symmetric group:
S(n−1,1)≅{(z1​,…,zn​)∈Cn:∑zi​=0}​.
(68)
A concrete orthonormal realization is
wk​=k(k−1)​e1​+⋯+ek−1​−(k−1)ek​​.
(69)
Permuting coordinates gives exactly the actions above, linking this orthogonal tableau basis with the Gelfand–Tsetlin basis found in Question 1. Here the printed sj​ are the adjacent Coxeter generators, rather than products of all generators.
