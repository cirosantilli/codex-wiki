Use the normal ordering identity for up and down operators, placing every U before every D. The commutator from part (a) implies
DUi=UiD+iUi−1,
(20)
by induction on i. Multiplying the normal-ordered expansion on the left by D+U therefore gives
ai,j​(ℓ+1)=ai−1,j​(ℓ)+ai,j−1​(ℓ)+(i+1)ai+1,j​(ℓ),
(21)
where negative indices have coefficient zero and a0,0​(0)=1.
We prove that ai,j​(ℓ)=0 unless i,j≥0 and ℓ−i−j=2m for some integer m≥0, and otherwise
ai,j​(ℓ)=2mi!j!m!ℓ!​​.
(22)
The formula holds at ℓ=0. For the next step with ℓ+1−i−j=2m, the three contributions in the recurrence, after factoring out ℓ!/(2mi!j!m!), are respectively i, j, and 2m. Terms with a negative index or m−1<0 are simply absent. Their sum is i+j+2m=ℓ+1, giving the required (ℓ+1)! numerator. Parity and nonnegativity exclude every remaining case. This proves the coefficient formula.
Apply the identity to the empty partition. Since D(∅)=0, only terms with j=0 survive. To finish at λ⊢n, only Un can contribute, and its coefficient of λ is fλ​, by the Young branching graph correspondence with standard Young tableaux. For ℓ=n+2m,
N(ℓ,λ)=2mn!m!ℓ!​fλ​=(nℓ​)(2m−1)!!fλ​​.
(23)
Here (2m−1)!!=1⋅3⋯(2m−1) is the odd double factorial, with (−1)!!=1. Thus
N(2m,∅)=2mm!(2m)!​=(2m−1)!!​.
(24)
The operator (D+U)ℓ counts oscillating tableaux, allowing both upward and downward steps. A strictly upward path to level n would necessarily have length n; the printed operator specification is what determines N when ℓ>n.
