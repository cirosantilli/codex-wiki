For an infinite cardinal number κ, the gimel function is
ℷ(κ)=κcf(κ)​.
(1)
The singular cardinals hypothesis asserts that for every infinite singular cardinal κ,
2cf(κ)<κ ⟹ ℷ(κ)=κ+.​
(2)
Here κ+ is the successor cardinal. Equivalently, for every infinite singular cardinal,
κcf(κ)=max{κ+,2cf(κ)}.
(3)
To see why the second formulation adds nothing in the other case, put θ=cf(κ). If κ≤2θ, then 2θ≤κθ≤(2θ)θ=2θ by infinite cardinal arithmetic. Equality κ=2θ is impossible here: the König theorem for cardinal numbers gives cf(2θ)>θ, whereas cf(κ)=θ. Thus in this case 2θ≥κ+, as required by the maximum formula. For a strong limit cardinal that is singular, the hypothesis also yields 2κ=κ+: restrictions of a subset of κ to a cofinal sequence of smaller cardinals give 2κ≤κθ, while the reverse inequality is immediate. The quantified implication above is the precise general statement.

It suffices to obtain a model of ZFC without weakly inaccessible cardinals. Starting with any model of ZFC, pass to its constructible universe, which satisfies ZFC and the Generalized continuum hypothesis. If it has no inaccessible cardinal, use that model. Otherwise pass to its rank segment at its least inaccessible cardinal δ. This segment satisfies ZFC, retains the Generalized continuum hypothesis, and has no inaccessible cardinals. Under the Generalized continuum hypothesis, every weakly inaccessible cardinal is strongly inaccessible: if λ<κ and κ is a limit cardinal, then 2λ=λ+<κ. Thus in either case the resulting model N has no weakly inaccessible cardinals.
Work inside N. If α is a nonzero limit ordinal, let λ=supβ<α​σβ​. It is an uncountable limit cardinal. If it were regular, it would be a weakly inaccessible cardinal, which is impossible in N. It is therefore singular, and the singular cardinal enumeration is continuous at this index:
σα​=supβ<α​σβ​.
(11)
The cofinality of an increasing ordinal supremum now gives
cf(σα​)=cf(α)​
(12)
for every nonzero limit ordinal α in N. Hence N satisfies the negation of the proposed existential assertion. By the soundness theorem for first-order logic, consistency of ZFC prevents ZFC from proving that assertion. The model construction is a relative-consistency argument; it does not assume that consistency alone supplies a countable transitive model. Here, as usual, a limit ordinal excludes zero.

First assume the Continuum hypothesis and fix a well-order (rα​)α<ω1​​ of the real numbers. Define f(rα​)={rβ​:β≤α}. Each value is countable. The construction gives a Lusin set A={xα​:α<ω1​}​ with all xα​ distinct.
Every meagre set is contained in a meagre Fσ​ set: replace each nowhere dense set in a countable covering by its closure. There are at most 2ℵ0​ such covers, since each closed subset of R is determined by a countable rational basis for its open complement, and a countable sequence of such codes is again coded by a real. Under the Continuum hypothesis, enumerate all meagre Fσ​ sets as (Mα​)α<ω1​​, allowing repetitions.
By transfinite recursion, choose
xα​∈R∖(⋃β≤α​Mβ​ ∪ {xβ​:β<α}).
(17)
For each α<ω1​, the excluded union is a meagre set: it is a countable union of meagre sets and singletons. The Baire category theorem ensures that it does not cover R, so the choice is possible. The resulting A is uncountable. If M is meagre, choose γ with M⊆Mγ​. Every xα​ with α≥γ avoids Mγ​, hence
A∩M⊆{xα​:α<γ},
(18)
which is countable. Thus A has precisely the Lusin set property. Enumerating covers rather than all meagre sets avoids an unjustified claim that there are only continuum many arbitrary meagre subsets.

The cardinal bound for an increasing chain of countable sets is ∣X∣≤ℵ1​​. If X is countable, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise choose Y⊆X with ∣Y∣=ℵ1​, and for every y∈Y choose an index i(y) such that y∈Xi(y)​.

X=⋃y∈Y​Xi(y)​.
(19)
By infinite cardinal arithmetic, this union of ℵ1​ countable sets has cardinality at most ℵ1​. No well-order or cofinality assumption on I was used; a linear increasing union of countable sets has the same bound even for an arbitrary linear index order.

Fix a regular uncountable cardinal number κ. A club set C⊆κ is unbounded in κ and closed under suprema below κ of nonempty increasing sequences with no last term. Equivalently, every limit ordinal δ<κ with sup(C∩δ)=δ belongs to C. The club filter is
Dκ​={A⊆κ:∃C⊆A with C club in κ}​.
(21)
It consists of all sets containing a club set, not only the club sets themselves.
An omega-measurable cardinal is a cardinal number carrying a nonprincipal ultrafilter closed under countable intersections, that is, a countably complete ultrafilter. Such a cardinal must be uncountable: on a countable set the intersection of the complements of all singletons would be empty.
A measurable cardinal is an uncountable cardinal number κ carrying a nonprincipal κ-complete ultrafilter. A kappa-complete filter is closed under intersections of every family of size strictly below κ. Thus for an omega-measurable cardinal the completeness required is countable completeness, not merely closure under finitely many intersections.

The club filter completeness argument works for every regular uncountable κ. Let μ<κ and let Ci​ be a club set for each i<μ. Their intersection is closed. To prove it unbounded, start above any prescribed β<κ and choose an increasing sequence (δn​)n<ω​ so that δn+1​ lies above a chosen point of every Ci​ greater than δn​. κ is a regular cardinal, so the supremum of these μ choices remains below κ. Take the least omega-measurable cardinal κ, with a countably complete ultrafilter U on κ that is nonprincipal. We prove that U is κ-complete. This will give κ is measurable​.
Suppose μ<κ, Ai​∈U for i<μ, and ⋂i<μ​Ai​∈/U. Then B=κ∖⋂i<μ​Ai​ belongs to U. For x∈B let g(x) be the least i<μ with x∈/Ai​. The pushforward ultrafilter
W={E⊆μ:g−1[E]∈U}
(25)
is a countably complete ultrafilter on μ; this uses B∈U, so U restricts to an ultrafilter on B. For each i, the fibre g−1[{i}] is contained in κ∖Ai​, hence is not in U. Thus W is nonprincipal. If μ is finite or countable, countable completeness already rules this out. Otherwise ∣μ∣<κ would be an omega-measurable cardinal, contradicting minimality of κ.
Therefore every intersection of fewer than κ members of U lies in U. Since κ is uncountable, it is a measurable cardinal. This proves that the least omega-measurable cardinal is measurable, even if the originally supplied cardinal carried only a countably complete ultrafilter.

The Von Neumann hierarchy and the constructible hierarchy are defined by transfinite recursion:
V0​=∅,Vα+1​=P(Vα​),Vλ​=α<λ⋃​Vα​​,
(26)
L0​=∅,Lα+1​=Def(Lα​),Lλ​=α<λ⋃​Lα​​.
(27)

Definability over that structure is essential; it is not unrestricted definability in the ambient universe. The full constructible universe is L=⋃α∈Ord​Lα​.

Hκ​={x:∣tc({x})∣<κ}​,
(28)
where tc is transitive closure. Using tc(x) instead gives the same size criterion for infinite κ. This bounds the whole membership ancestry, not just ∣x∣.

Use the Lévy hierarchy, with bounded formulas in set theory as the quantifier-free level for this purpose. Let Ord(κ) be the bounded ordinal predicate, and let Bij(f,α,κ) be the bounded predicate saying that f is a graph of a bijection from α onto κ. Then cardinalhood is expressed by
Ord(κ) ∧ ∀f∀α∈κ ¬Bij(f,α,κ).​
(31)
All quantifiers in Ord and Bij are bounded by the displayed sets and the graph. Ordered-pair membership, graph functionality, injectivity and surjectivity have bounded expansions using the usual set coding of ordered pairs. The only unbounded quantifier shown is universal over f. The formula is therefore Π1​ and in particular a Pi-one formula modulo ZF.
In ZF, a cardinal number is an ordinal not in bijection with any smaller ordinal, so this formula has exactly the required meaning. Thus cardinalhood is Pi-one definable; the argument does not use the axiom of choice.

Let Map(f,δ,κ) be the bounded graph predicate for a function δ→κ. For an infinite cardinal number, regularity is expressed by
InfCard(κ) ∧ ∀f∀δ∈κ(Map(f,δ,κ) ⟹ ∃β∈κ ∀ξ∈δ f(ξ)<β).​

Let T be ZFC together with the assertion of unboundedly many inaccessible cardinals. Given a model of T, if it has no inaccessible limit of inaccessibles, it already witnesses the required nonimplication. Otherwise let ι be its least inaccessible limit of inaccessibles and pass to its rank segment Vι​.
Because ι is an inaccessible cardinal, Vι​ satisfies ZFC. Its ordinals are precisely those below ι, and below ι the inaccessible cardinals are unbounded by the choice of ι. Inaccessibility of any smaller ordinal is absolute to this segment: it has the same subsets and functions on all smaller ordinals, as in strong-inaccessibility absoluteness from rank agreement. Thus Vι​ satisfies the unbounded-inaccessibles assertion.
No smaller ordinal can be an inaccessible limit of inaccessibles, by minimality of ι and the same absoluteness. Also ι is not an ordinal of Vι​ itself. Consequently
Vι​⊨T+¬ILI​.
(35)
This rank cutoff at the first inaccessible limit of inaccessibles proves Con(T)⇒Con(T+¬ILI). The soundness theorem for first-order logic then gives T⊢ILI if T is consistent. This is a model-theoretic relative-consistency argument, and does not infer external transitivity from mere consistency.

Inside M, recursively strengthen p to decide each value h˙(ξ), for ξ<μ. At a limit stage take a lower bound of the previously chosen descending sequence; its length is below λ, so λ-closure supplies one. After all μ values have been decided, closure supplies a common lower bound q. The decided values form a function h∈M by the Axiom schema of replacement, and
q⊩h˙=hˇ.
(42)
The recursion is carried out within M using the definable forcing theorem and a ground-model choice of deciding extensions, so every sequence to which closure is applied belongs to M. Repeating the construction below any stronger condition shows that such full-decision conditions are dense below a forcing condition p. The dense-below generic meeting lemma ensures that a generic filter containing p meets them. Thus the interpreted function is in M.
If λ ceased to be a cardinal number in M[G], there would be a surjection μ→λ for some ordinal μ<λ. The forcing theorem gives a name and a condition in G forcing this. The argument above makes its interpretation a ground-model function, but M regards λ as a cardinal number and admits no such surjection. Therefore
M[G]⊨“λ is a cardinal”​.
(43)
No regularity of λ is required; every recursion used has length strictly below λ. Finite cardinals are preserved automatically. This is cardinal preservation by closed forcing.

Inside M, the finite-function order has the countable chain condition for finite-function forcing. In an uncountable family of binary conditions there are uncountably many distinct finite domains, since each domain supports only finitely many conditions. These domains would have an uncountable Delta-system subfamily by the Delta-system lemma. There are only finitely many assignments to the common finite root, so thin further to an uncountable family agreeing there. Any two such conditions have a union that is again a function and is a common stronger extension. Thus no uncountable antichain in a forcing order exists.
Here is the relevant preservation argument, rather than an appeal to the condition alone. Fix p∈G forcing that a forcing name h˙ is a function ω→α1​. For each n, choose inside M a maximal antichain in a forcing order below p deciding h˙(n). By the countable chain condition for forcing, this antichain is countable in M, so the possible values form a countable Bn​⊆α1​. The union B=⋃n​Bn​ is countable in M, hence bounded below its regular ω1​=α1​. The interpreted h has range contained in B, by maximality and the dense-below generic meeting lemma. It therefore cannot be a surjection onto α1​.
If α1​ were not a cardinal number in M[G], it would be equinumerous with a smaller ordinal, which was countable already in M; composing with that ground-model enumeration would give a surjection ω→α1​. This is impossible. Therefore
M[G]⊨“α1​ is a cardinal”​.
(47)
This is the possible-values lemma for chain-condition forcing specialized to ω1​. Countability and regularity in this proof are computed inside M, not inferred from the external countability of M.
