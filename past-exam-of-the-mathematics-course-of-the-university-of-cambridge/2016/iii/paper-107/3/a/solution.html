The zero Dirichlet boundary condition implies that the tangential boundary derivative vi​ vanishes on the flat face. Differentiating the Poisson equation gives Δvi​=Di​f. Test this equation with η2vi​. The boundary terms vanish because vi​=0 on the flat face and η=0 near the curved face. Integration by parts in the tangential direction moves Di​ off f, yielding

All mixed second partial derivatives are now controlled, since Dni​u=Din​u. The remaining normal derivative is recovered from the equation:

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds its squared L2 norm by n times the sum of the squared L2 norms on the right. Including the already controlled zeroth and first partial derivatives, and taking square roots, gives the second-derivative estimate at a flat Dirichlet boundary:

The full second-order Sobolev norm is controlled without any norm of Df.
