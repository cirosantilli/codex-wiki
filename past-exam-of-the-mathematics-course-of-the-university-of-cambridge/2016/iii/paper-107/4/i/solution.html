Write the minimal surface equation for a graph as divF(Du)=0 in its weak formulation, where F(p)=p/1+∣p∣2​. For negative increments use the same difference quotient convention, so
wh​=δℓ,−h​u=hu(x)−u(x−heℓ​)​.

For ζ∈Cc1​(Ω′′), the function δℓ,h​ζ is an admissible test function in Ω. Commuting the first partial derivative with translation, then applying discrete integration by parts, gives
0=∫Ω​Fi​(Du)δℓ,h​(Di​ζ)=−∫Ω′′​δℓ,−h​(Fi​(Du))Di​ζ.

The fundamental theorem of calculus along a line segment gives the averaged linearization of a nonlinear divergence-form equation
δℓ,−h​(Fi​(Du))=Ahij​(x)Dj​wh​,Ah​(x)=∫01​DF((1−t)Du(x−heℓ​)+tDu(x))dt.

Thus the backward difference quotient solves a linear equation in divergence form:
Di​(Ahij​Dj​wh​)=0weakly on Ω′′.​

To verify the ellipticity of the minimal surface flux, compute
DF(p)=1+∣p∣2​I​−(1+∣p∣2)3/2p⊗p​,
(28)
and hence
(1+∣p∣2)3/2∣ξ∣2​≤ξ⋅DF(p)ξ≤∣ξ∣2.
(29)
The eigenvalue parallel to p is (1+∣p∣2)−3/2; every orthogonal eigenvalue is (1+∣p∣2)−1/2. The same lower and upper bounds pass to the averaged symmetric matrix Ah​ whenever both endpoint gradients have norm at most M:
(1+M2)−3/2∣ξ∣2≤Ahij​ξi​ξj​≤∣ξ∣2.​
(30)
The printed C1(Ω) hypothesis supplies such an M on each relatively compact subset, uniformly for sufficiently small h. It therefore establishes a locally uniformly elliptic operator. A single lower bound on all of Ω′′ additionally requires bounded gradients there and at the shifted points; this is automatic for bounded Ω and fixed h, but is not supplied on an arbitrary open set.
