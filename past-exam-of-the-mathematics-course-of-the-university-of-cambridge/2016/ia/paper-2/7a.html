Classification and characteristic exponents. For a normalized second-order linear differential equation, an ordinary point at zero means p and q are real analytic functions there. A regular singular point is a singular point where xp and x2q nevertheless extend as real analytic functions to zero. Write p0​=limx→0​xp(x) and q0​=limx→0​x2q(x). The lowest-power coefficient in the Frobenius method gives the indicial equation
σ(σ−1)+p0​σ+q0​=0.
(29)
Its roots are the characteristic exponents at a regular singular point. If their difference is not an integer, there are two linearly independent solutions xσj​Aj​(x), with Aj​ real analytic and Aj​(0)=0. For distinct real roots σ1​>σ2​ with integer difference, a solution for the larger root exists, and a second has the form xσ2​B(x)+cy1​logx; the logarithmic coefficient can vanish. For a repeated root, the second solution has the form y1​logx+xσ1​B(x) after normalization. This is a Logarithmic Frobenius solution. Complex conjugate roots a±ib give real solutions whose leading behaviour is xacos(blogx) and xasin(blogx), with analytic-series corrections. All these descriptions are on x>0, or on a fixed complex branch.
For the present equation, division by x gives p=1+(1−m)/x and q=(1−m)/x. Therefore xp=x+1−m and x2q=(1−m)x are real analytic. Zero is regular singular when m=1; when m=1, it is ordinary after removing the common factor x. In that exceptional case the normalized equation is y′′+y′=0.
