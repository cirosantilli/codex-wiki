 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2016 ia Paper 1 5A c
OurBigBook.com Words: 194
The chosen unit normal points toward the plane from the origin, so the plane equation is
n⋅r=d.​
(31)
To locate the circle centre, project p onto this plane. Put
γ=d−n⋅p,h=p+γn.
(32)
Then n⋅h=d, so h lies in the plane. For any other point r of the plane, r−h is perpendicular to n. The Pythagorean theorem therefore gives
∣r−p∣2=∣r−h∣2+γ2.
(33)
Restricting the sphere equation to the plane consequently gives a circle centred at h. Thus m=h, proving that the displacement of its centre is purely normal:
m−p=γn,γ=d−n⋅p.​
(34)
The same Pythagorean theorem gives q2=ρ2+γ2, and hence
γ2=q2−ρ2,γ=±q2−ρ2​.​
(35)
The sign of γ is fixed by which side of the plane contains p; q and ρ alone determine only its magnitude. Substituting the signed offset yields the sphere-plane intersection radius
ρ=q2−(d−n⋅p)2​.​
(36)
A genuine circle requires ∣d−n⋅p∣<q; equality gives tangency and ρ=0, while a larger distance gives no intersection. The signed distance from a point to a plane in the direction n is n⋅p−d=−γ; the ordinary distance from a point to a plane is its absolute value.
