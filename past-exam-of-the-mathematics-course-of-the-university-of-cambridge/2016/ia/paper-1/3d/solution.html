A sequence (xn​) is a convergent sequence with limit x if
∀ε>0 ∃N∈N ∀n≥N:∣xn​−x∣<ε.
(15)
The index N may depend on ε, but must work for every later term.
Fix ε>0. Choose N such that ∣xi​−x∣<ε/2 for i≥N, and put
C=∑i=1N−1​∣xi​−x∣.
(16)
This is a fixed finite number. For n≥N, the triangle inequality gives
​n1​∑i=1n​xi​−x​≤n1​∑i=1n​∣xi​−x∣≤nC​+nn−N+1​2ε​≤nC​+2ε​.
(17)
Choose n sufficiently large that C/n<ε/2 as well. The error is then less than ε, proving
n1​i=1∑n​xi​⟶x.​
(18)
Taking successive arithmetic averages preserves the limit. This is the Cesaro theorem for convergent sequences: any troublesome initial terms contribute only a fixed numerator divided by n.
