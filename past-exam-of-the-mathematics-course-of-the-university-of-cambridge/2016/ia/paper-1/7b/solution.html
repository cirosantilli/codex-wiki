A matrix is a diagonalizable matrix if there is an invertible matrix P for which P−1MP is diagonal. Its columns then form a basis of eigenvectors, and the diagonal entries are their corresponding eigenvalues.
Here the given eigenvectors form an orthonormal basis. Put them into the columns of Q:
Q=(e1​ ⋯ en​),QTQ=I,Q−1=QT.
(48)
Define Λ=QTMQ. Its entries are
Λab​=eaT​Meb​=λb​ea​⋅eb​=λb​δab​.
(49)
This proves the claimed diagonalization of a matrix, rather than merely guessing it:
Λ=diag(λ1​,…,λn​),M=QΛQT.​
(50)
The trace follows directly from this factorization:
trM=∑i​∑a​λa​Qia2​=∑a​λa​∑i​Qia2​=∑a​λa​,
(51)
because each column of Q has unit length. Trace is the sum of the eigenvalues, with multiplicity.
For the particular matrix, (M2)ij​=∑k​Mik​Mkj​. If i=j, every k=i contributes one and k=i contributes zero. If i=j, precisely k=i and k=j contribute zero; the other n−2 terms contribute one. Thus
(M2)ij​={n−1,n−2,​i=j,i=j,​M2=(n−1)I+(n−2)M.
(52)
Applying this identity to a nonzero eigenvector gives
λ2=(n−1)+(n−2)λ,(λ−(n−1))(λ+1)=0.
(53)
Every eigenvalue is therefore n−1 or −1.
If r is the multiplicity of n−1, the trace formula for a diagonalizable matrix and the zero diagonal give
0=r(n−1)+(n−r)(−1)=n(r−1),
(54)
so r=1. Up to ordering, the required diagonal form is
Λ=diag(n−1,−1,…,−1).​
(55)
For n=1 this means just (0). For n≥2, the eigendirections are also transparent: the all-ones vector has eigenvalue n−1, and the (n−1)-dimensional subspace whose coordinate sum is zero has eigenvalue −1, since (Mx)i​=∑j​xj​−xi​. The spectrum consists of one collective mode and n−1 zero-sum modes.
