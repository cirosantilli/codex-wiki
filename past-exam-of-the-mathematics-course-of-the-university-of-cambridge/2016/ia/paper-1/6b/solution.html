For the reality of the eigenvalues, one may initially allow a nonzero complex eigenvector v with Mv=λv. Since M is real symmetric, it is a Hermitian matrix, and
λ=v†vv†Mv​
(37)
is real: the numerator equals its own complex conjugate and the denominator is positive. Once λ is real, a nonzero real or imaginary part of v supplies a real eigenvector. We work with the real unit eigenvectors of the problem.
For two such eigenvectors, symmetry gives
