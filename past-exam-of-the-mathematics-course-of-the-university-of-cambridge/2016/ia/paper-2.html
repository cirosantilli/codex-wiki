The independent and identically distributed random variables have a continuous probability distribution, so any tie has probability zero. Their joint probability density is unchanged by a permutation of their indices: this is order symmetry of independent random variables. The n! strict orderings are disjoint and exhaust the sample space apart from the zero-probability ties. Each ordering therefore has the same probability, and their probabilities sum to one.
The probability is
P(X1​>X2​>⋯>Xn​)=n!1​.​
(10)

Away from the impulse, the harmonic oscillator equation has a homogeneous solution. Before x=π/2, the initial conditions select y=cosx. The jump condition for an impulse requires continuity of y and a unit jump in y′: a jump in y would produce a derivative of the Dirac delta distribution, which is absent from the forcing, while integrating through the impulse gives [y′]=1.
Just before the impulse, y=0 and y′=−1. Just afterwards, y=0 and y′=0, so uniqueness for the harmonic oscillator equation makes the subsequent solution identically zero. Equivalently the causal response is H(x−π/2)sin(x−π/2), with H the Heaviside step function, and it cancels the original oscillation.
The solution is
y(x)=cosx+H(x−π/2)sin(x−π/2)={cosx,0,​0≤x<π/2,x≥π/2.​​
(13)
The impulse cancellation of a harmonic oscillator produces a continuous graph with a corner at (π/2,0), followed by a horizontal zero line.

Classification and characteristic exponents. For a normalized second-order linear differential equation, an ordinary point at zero means p and q are real analytic functions there. A regular singular point is a singular point where xp and x2q nevertheless extend as real analytic functions to zero. Write p0​=limx→0​xp(x) and q0​=limx→0​x2q(x). The lowest-power coefficient in the Frobenius method gives the indicial equation
σ(σ−1)+p0​σ+q0​=0.
(29)
Its roots are the characteristic exponents at a regular singular point. If their difference is not an integer, there are two linearly independent solutions xσj​Aj​(x), with Aj​ real analytic and Aj​(0)=0. For distinct real roots σ1​>σ2​ with integer difference, a solution for the larger root exists, and a second has the form xσ2​B(x)+cy1​logx; the logarithmic coefficient can vanish. For a repeated root, the second solution has the form y1​logx+xσ1​B(x) after normalization. This is a Logarithmic Frobenius solution. Complex conjugate roots a±ib give real solutions whose leading behaviour is xacos(blogx) and xasin(blogx), with analytic-series corrections. For each unordered triple of distinct vertices, let IA​ be the indicator variable that its three edges are present. This is a binomial random graph, so edge independence gives E[IA​]=p3. The triangle count in a binomial random graph is T=∑A​IA​. There are (3n​) such triples; all are triangles in the complete graph.
The combinatorial maximum and expectation are
Tmax​=(3n​),E[T]=(3n​)p3.​
(63)
The maximum is attainable with positive probability if p>0; for p=0, the random variable is identically zero. The linearity of expectation requires no independence between different triangle indicators.
