For finite sets E1​,…,Es​, the inclusion-exclusion principle states
​⋃j=1s​Ej​​=∑∅=I⊆{1,…,s}​(−1)∣I∣+1​⋂j∈I​Ej​​.
(26)
It counts each element once: an element in exactly q of the sets contributes ∑j=1q​(−1)j+1(jq​)=1, by the binomial theorem.
For the cyclic digit run problem, let Ej​ mean that the three digits starting at position j form a run, for j=1,2,3,4. Every run of length at least three contains one of these triples, so we need ∣E1​∪E2​∪E3​∪E4​∣. Introduce the five differences
di​≡ai+1​−ai​(mod10),i=1,…,5.
(27)
A starting digit and these five residues determine a unique string, including runs that cross 9 and 0. In these coordinates Ej​ requires (dj​,dj+1​)=(1,1) or (−1,−1).
Each single event fixes two differences, with two choices of sign; the initial digit and the other three differences are free. Thus ∣Ej​∣=10⋅2⋅103=20000. For intersections of two events, adjacent starting positions force three consecutive differences to have the same sign, giving 10⋅2⋅102=2000. There are three such pairs. The other three pairs have disjoint constrained difference positions, with independent signs and one free difference, giving 10⋅4⋅10=400 each. Consequently the sum of pair-intersection sizes is 3⋅2000+3⋅400=7200.
For triple intersections, E1​∩E2​∩E3​ and E2​∩E3​∩E4​ each force four consecutive differences to have the same sign, leaving one free difference, and each has size 10⋅2⋅10=200. The other two triples, E1​∩E2​∩E4​ and E1​∩E3​∩E4​, divide the five differences into two blocks with independent signs; each has size 10⋅4=40. The triple-intersection sum is therefore 480. Finally, all four events force all five differences to have the same sign, giving 20 strings.
The inclusion-exclusion principle now gives the number of strings
4⋅20000−7200+480−20=73260​.
(28)
Using differences makes the overlap conditions explicit and prevents double-counting long cyclic digit runs.
