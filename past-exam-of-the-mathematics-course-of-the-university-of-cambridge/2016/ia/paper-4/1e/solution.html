The extended Euclidean algorithm gives
29=17+12,17=12+5,12=2⋅5+2,5=2⋅2+1.
(1)
Back-substituting in these equations produces the Bezout identity
1=5−2(12−2⋅5)=5⋅5−2⋅12=5⋅17−7⋅12=12⋅17−7⋅29.
(2)
Thus one integer solution is x=12, y=−7​. In fact, subtracting this Bezout identity from any other solution and using that 17 and 29 are coprime integers gives all solutions: x=12+29t, y=−7−17t, where t∈Z.
For the modular arithmetic calculation, 29 is a prime number and 17 is coprime to it, so Fermat little theorem gives 1728≡1(mod29). Since 138=5⋅28−2, and the preceding Bezout identity identifies 12 as the modular multiplicative inverse of 17, we obtain
17138≡17−2≡122=144≡28(mod29).
(3)
Consequently the least positive representative is 28​.
