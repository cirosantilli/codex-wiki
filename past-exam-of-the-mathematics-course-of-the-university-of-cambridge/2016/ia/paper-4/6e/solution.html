Multiplying the desired representation by b1​b2​ turns it into the integer equation
a=a1​b2​+a2​b1​+nb1​b2​.
(20)
Since b1​ and b2​ are coprime integers, the necessary modular multiplicative inverses exist. Choose the unique representatives
a1​≡ab2−1​(modb1​),0≤a1​<b1​,a2​≡ab1−1​(modb2​),0≤a2​<b2​.
(21)
Then a−a1​b2​−a2​b1​ is divisible by both b1​ and b2​, hence by their product, because they are coprime. Defining
n=b1​b2​a−a1​b2​−a2​b1​​
(22)
therefore gives an integer and proves existence. Any other representation, reduced modulo b1​ and b2​, must have the same a1​ and a2​, since their allowed ranges contain exactly one representative of each residue. The integer equation then forces the same n. This proves uniqueness.
For the prime-power fraction decomposition, put di​=pini​​ and Bi​=b/di​. The distinct prime-power factors di​ are pairwise coprime, and Bi​ has a modular multiplicative inverse modulo di​. Define
qi​≡aBi−1​(moddi​),0≤qi​<di​.
(23)
Every term qj​Bj​ with j=i is divisible by di​, while qi​Bi​≡a(moddi​). Thus a−∑i​qi​Bi​ is divisible by all the pairwise coprime di​ and therefore by b. With n=(a−∑i​qi​Bi​)/b, division by b yields the required representation. Reducing any such representation modulo each di​ proves uniqueness exactly as above. This is the residue-solving mechanism of the Chinese remainder theorem.
For 315=9⋅5⋅7, the complementary factors are 35,63,45. The required modular inverses give
q9​=8,q5​=2,q7​=5,
(24)
because 35⋅8≡1(mod9), 63⋅2≡1(mod5), and 45⋅5≡1(mod7). Their numerator sum is 280+126+225=631=1+2⋅315. Thus the unique decomposition is
3151​=98​+52​+75​−2​.
(25)
