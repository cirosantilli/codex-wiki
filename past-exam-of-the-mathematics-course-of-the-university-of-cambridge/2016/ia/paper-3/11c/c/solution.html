The identity divergence of a curl is zero follows by the symmetry of second partial derivatives and antisymmetry of the Levi-Civita symbol. Therefore
∇⋅J=∇⋅(∇×B)=0.​
(52)
If B=∇×A and A is in Coulomb gauge, the curl of the curl identity gives J=−∇2A. Apply the supplied scalar Poisson equation representation to each component of the decaying vector potential. In the units of this problem the Coulomb-gauge vector potential of a localized steady current is
A(x)=∫R3​4π∣x−y∣J(y)​d3y.​
(53)
Here one needs A=O(∣x∣−1), ∇A=O(∣x∣−2) and enough localization of J for the stated representation and differentiations. A smooth compactly supported current density is a sufficient convenient case. The local differential equations and gauge alone do not impose these conditions: A=(−y,x,0), B=(0,0,2), J=0 satisfy them but do not satisfy the displayed integral formula. The formula selects the decaying solution, excluding nondecaying homogeneous harmonic functions.
With r=x−y, the gradient of the kernel is ∇x​(1/∣r∣)=−r/∣r∣3. Since J(y) is independent of x,
∇x​×(4π∣r∣J(y)​)=4π∣r∣3J(y)×r​.
(54)
Thus taking the curl gives the Biot-Savart law in the problem's normalization:
B(x)=∫R3​4π∣x−y∣3J(y)×(x−y)​d3y.​
(55)
Finally, change variables z=x−y in the integral for A. This keeps the singular kernel independent of the differentiation variable:
A(x)=∫R3​4π∣z∣J(x−z)​d3z,∇⋅A(x)=∫R3​4π∣z∣(∇⋅J)(x−z)​d3z=0.
(56)
For smooth localized J, differentiation under the integral sign is legitimate: 1/∣z∣ is locally integrable in three dimensions, and on a bounded neighbourhood of x the translated sources and their derivatives have common compact support. The integral indeed satisfies Coulomb gauge.
