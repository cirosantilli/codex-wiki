Every permutation is a product of transpositions, since a cycle can be written
(a1​a2​…ak​)=(a1​ak​)⋯(a1​a2​)
(21)
with rightmost factors acting first. Define the sign of a permutation to be (−1)r for a product of r transpositions. To prove that it is well defined without assuming parity, let Pσ​ be the permutation matrix specified by Pσ​ei​=eσ(i)​. Then Pστ​=Pσ​Pτ​, and a transposition exchanges two columns of the identity, so its determinant is −1. Any decomposition into r transpositions therefore has
detPσ​=(−1)r.
(22)
The left-hand side depends only on σ, proving well-definedness. Multiplicativity of the determinant now gives the sign homomorphism:
sgn(στ)=sgn(σ)sgn(τ).​
(23)
For the first requested group embedding, use the faithful four-point action of GL2 over F2: label the four vectors in F22​ and let an invertible 2×2 matrix act on them by multiplication. Composition of these actions gives a group homomorphism ψ:GL2​(F2​)→S4​. If a matrix fixes every vector, it fixes both standard basis vectors and is the identity, so the action is faithful. The matrix
B=(01​10​)
(24)
fixes (0,0) and (1,1) and exchanges (1,0) with (0,1). Its image is a single transposition, hence sgn(ψ(B))=−1. The composite sign map is nontrivial.
For the second group embedding, simply take
ϕ(σ)=Pσ​∈GLn​(R),detϕ(σ)=sgn(σ).​
(25)
The preceding multiplication formula proves the group homomorphism property, and Pσ​=I forces σ(i)=i for every i, proving injectivity.
