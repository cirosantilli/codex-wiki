For a group action of a finite group G on a set and a point x, the orbit-stabilizer theorem is
∣Gx∣=[G:Gx​],∣G∣=∣Gx​∣∣Gx∣,​
(26)
where Gx​={g:gx=x} is the stabilizer subgroup. The map gGx​↦gx is well defined because elements of the same coset act identically on x. If gx=hx, then h−1g∈Gx​, so their cosets agree; every orbit point has the form gx, giving surjectivity. This is a bijection. Lagrange's theorem, ∣G∣=[G:H]∣H∣ for any subgroup H, yields the counting formula.
Apply the conjugation action of the finite p-group G to its nontrivial normal subgroup N. Normality ensures the action stays inside N. The singleton orbits of a group action are exactly the elements of N∩Z(G). Every larger orbit of a group action has size a positive power of p, by the orbit-stabilizer theorem and Lagrange's theorem. Therefore
∣N∣≡∣N∩Z(G)∣(modp).
(27)
Since N is nontrivial, its order is divisible by p. The intersection contains the identity and has cardinality a positive multiple of p, so it contains a nonidentity element. This proves the central intersection property of normal subgroups of finite p-groups.
For the proper subgroup H, let H act by left multiplication on the left cosets G/H. A coset gH is fixed by every h∈H exactly when g−1Hg⊆H. Both groups have the same finite size, so this inclusion is equality; the fixed cosets are precisely those with g∈NG​(H). Hence their number is [NG​(H):H]. All non-singleton orbits of a group action have size divisible by p, so
[G:H]≡[NG​(H):H](modp).
(28)
Since H is proper, [G:H] is a positive power of p greater than one. The fixed-coset count is nonzero, because H itself is fixed, and is divisible by p. Therefore the normalizer strictly contains H:
[NG​(H):H]≥p,∃g∈G∖H: g−1Hg=H.​
(29)
This is the normalizer condition for finite p-groups. The proof also covers H={1}, whose action fixes all cosets.
