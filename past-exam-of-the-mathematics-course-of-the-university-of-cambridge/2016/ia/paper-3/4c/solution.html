Let R be an orthogonal matrix describing a Cartesian change of coordinates, with transformed vector components vi′​=Ria​va​ and wj′​=Rjb​wb​. Their outer product transforms as
Tij′​=vi′​wj′​=Ria​Rjb​Tab​,

which is the transformation law of a second-order Cartesian tensor. Here “rank two” counts tensor indices, not the rank of a matrix; a nonzero outer product has matrix rank one.
For an isotropic second-rank tensor, invariance means RTRT=T for every proper rotation matrix. The half-turn matrices diag(1,−1,−1), diag(−1,1,−1) and diag(−1,−1,1) force all off-diagonal entries to vanish. Quarter-turns around coordinate axes then force the three diagonal entries to be equal. Conversely RIRT=I, so the most general second-rank isotropic tensor is
Tij​=λδij​,λ∈R.​
(11)
