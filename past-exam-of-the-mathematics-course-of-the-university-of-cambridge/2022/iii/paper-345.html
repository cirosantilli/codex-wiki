Dν​=∂t​−ν∇2,Dκ​=∂t​−κ∇2.

Dν​u=−ρ0​1​px​,Dν​w=−ρ0​1​pz​+b,Dκ​b=−N2w.

Let the group velocity make an angle θ above the horizontal. A convenient right-handed choice of rotated unit vectors is

The shallow-water approximation requires h/L≪1, a nearly hydrostatic pressure, negligible vertical acceleration, and approximately depth-independent horizontal velocity, concentration, and density. The large Reynolds number allows viscous stress to be neglected away from thin boundary layers, while the deep ambient is taken to remain stationary.
For unit channel width, volume conservation, chemical conservation, and mass conservation are respectively
ht​+(uh)x​=we​−wd​,
(21)
(ϕh)t​+(ϕuh)x​=−ϕwd​,
(22)
and
(ρh)t​+(ρuh)x​=ρ0​we​−ρwd​.
(23)
Entrained ambient fluid contains no chemical and enters with density ρ0​; detrained fluid carries the local concentration and density. The ambient has no horizontal momentum, whereas detrained fluid carries horizontal momentum ρu per unit volume. The depth-integrated momentum conservation law is therefore
∂t∂(ρuh)​+∂x∂​(ρu2h+21​(ρ−ρ0​)gh2)=−ρuwd​​,
(24)
so M=−ρuwd​.

The Batchelor entrainment hypothesis takes the inflow speed through the plume's exposed outer edge to be αW, where α is the entrainment coefficient. A wall plume has only one such edge, so dV/dz=αW.
The Boussinesq approximation replaces density by a constant reference value ρ0​ in inertia and mass flux while retaining the small density deficit in buoyancy. It requires ∣ρ​−ρ∣/ρ0​≪1. A sufficiently hot radiator can violate this near the source, where thermal expansion is large and the developed-plume description may also fail.
Put B0​=F0​/ρ0​ for the kinematic buoyancy flux per unit length. Dimensional analysis for a line plume gives
W∼B01/3​,b∼z,V∼B01/3​z.
(43)
The plume rise time is therefore trise​∼H/B01/3​. Changing the room stratification requires a plume volume comparable with XH, so troom​∼XH/V(H)∼X/B01/3​. Hence
troom​trise​​∼XH​≪1.
(44)
The plume consequently follows the slowly changing ambient through a quasi-steady approximation when X≫H.

The source supplies the kinematic buoyancy flux B0​=F0​/ρ0​. The unstratified line plume velocity scale is B01/3​. Comparing this with the stratification time scale N0−1​ gives the stratified line-plume height scale
zs​∼N0​B01/3​​=(ρ0​N03​F0​​)1/3.​
(51)
If zs​≫H, the initial plume is only weakly affected by the stable density stratification and reaches the ceiling much like an unstratified wall plume. If zs​∼H, its buoyancy flux falls substantially during the rise; it approaches neutral buoyancy near the upper room, overshoots because of its momentum flux, and spreads as a horizontal buoyant intrusion. If zs​≪H, the plume reaches neutral buoyancy low in the room and forms a low intrusion after a modest overshoot. In each sketch the plume widens by entrainment, while increasing N0​ lowers the neutral-buoyancy and overshoot heights.

The final state is displacement ventilation: fresh air of density ρ0​ forms a cool lower layer, radiator-heated air forms a well-mixed upper layer, and the wall plume crosses their interface at height h. Steady volume conservation requires the plume volume flux there to equal the imposed ventilation flux,
V(h)=A.
(52)
Below the interface the ambient is uniform, so B0​=F0​/ρ0​ is constant. Put J=M/ρ0​. The triangular-profile wall line plume equations reduce to
dzdV​=αW=2V3αJ​,dzdJ​=JB0​V​.
(53)
The pure plume conditions V,J→0 at the radiator select the similarity solution
V(z)=CL​B01/3​z,CL​=(23α​)2/3.
(54)
Consequently the displacement-ventilation interface height is
h=CL​(F0​/ρ0​)1/3A​=(3α2​)2/3(F0​/ρ0​)1/3A​.​
(55)
The upper-layer reduced gravity follows from its steady buoyancy balance as gu′​=B0​/A=F0​/(ρ0​A). The two-layer solution applies when the calculated interface satisfies 0<h<H.
