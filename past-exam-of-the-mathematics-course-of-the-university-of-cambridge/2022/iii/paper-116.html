An uncountable cardinal number κ is weakly compact when every κ-satisfiable theory in an infinitary language Lκ,κ​ with at most κ nonlogical symbols is satisfiable. A cardinal is inaccessible when it is uncountable, regular, and a strong limit cardinal.
Two standard results supply the proof. First, every weakly compact cardinal is inaccessible. Second, every weakly compact κ has the Keisler extension property: there is a transitive set X⊋Vκ​ such that
(Vκ​,∈)≺(X,∈)
(1)
and κ∈X. Since κ is inaccessible, the relevant downward absoluteness makes X⊨ “κ is inaccessible”. Hence X satisfies “there is an inaccessible cardinal”. By elementarity, Vκ​ satisfies the same sentence, so it contains some inaccessible λ. Every ordinal in Vκ​ is below κ, and inaccessibility is absolute here, giving
λ<κ and λ is inaccessible.​
(2)

An uncountable cardinal κ is measurable when it carries a nonprincipal ultrafilter U that is κ-complete. For an inaccessible λ>κ, the cardinal κ is 1-strong when there is an elementary embedding
j:Vλ​⟶M
(3)
into a transitive model, with critical point κ and Vκ+1​⊆M.
The fundamental theorem on measurable cardinals constructs from U the well-founded ultrapower and its ultrapower embedding j:Vλ​→M, whose critical point is κ. The embedding fixes Vκ​. If A∈Vκ+1​, then A⊆Vκ​, and elementarity gives
A=j(A)∩Vκ​.
(4)
Both j(A) and Vκ​ belong to the transitive target, so A∈M. Thus Vκ+1​⊆M, proving that every measurable cardinal is 1-strong.

Let U be a nonprincipal κ-complete ultrafilter on the measurable cardinal κ, and fix λ<κ. Suppose for a contradiction that 2λ≥κ. Choose distinct subsets Aα​⊆λ for α<κ. For each ξ<λ, the ultrafilter property chooses exactly one of
Sξ​={α<κ:ξ∈Aα​},κ∖Sξ​
(5)
as a member Hξ​ of U. Since λ<κ, κ-completeness gives H=⋂ξ<λ​Hξ​∈U.
Any two indices in H give subsets having the same membership decision at every ξ<λ, so they give the same Aα​. The chosen subsets were distinct, hence ∣H∣≤1. This contradicts the fact that a small set is absent from a complete nonprincipal ultrafilter. Therefore 2λ<κ for every λ<κ, which is precisely the strong limit cardinal condition. This is the measurable cardinal is a strong limit cardinal argument.

Suppose that a first-order formula φ described the inaccessible cardinal κ, so that κ were the least ordinal with Vκ​⊨φ. Since κ is inaccessible, Vκ​ is a model of ZFC. Apply the Lévy reflection theorem inside this model to the single formula φ. There is some α<κ for which
Vα​⊨φ⟺Vκ​⊨φ.
(6)
The right side holds, so the left side contradicts the asserted minimality of κ. Hence no first-order formula describes an inaccessible cardinal, as recorded by ordinal described by a first-order formula.

Let I(κ), W(κ), and M(κ) mean respectively that κ is inaccessible, weakly compact, and measurable. Define a fourth cardinal property
Θ(κ)⟺(WC∧M(κ))∨(¬WC∧I(κ)).
(7)
Take Φ0​=I, Φ1​=W, and Φ2​=Θ.
If inaccessible and weakly compact cardinals exist, Question 1a shows that an inaccessible lies below every weakly compact cardinal. Thus I<1​W. If WC and ΘC both hold, then Θ is exactly measurability. Every measurable cardinal is weakly compact, and the usual ultrapower reflection theorem gives weakly compact cardinals below every measurable cardinal. Therefore W<1​Θ.
Now assume the consistency of ZFC with an inaccessible cardinal but no weakly compact cardinal. In such a model Θ(κ) is exactly I(κ), so
ιΘ​=ιI​.
(8)
Consequently I<1​Θ. This is an explicit nontransitivity of the least-occurrence order on cardinal properties, even though I<1​W and W<1​Θ.

Write
T=ZFC+“there is a worldly cardinal”,T∗=ZFC+Con(ZFC).
(9)
If κ is a worldly cardinal, then Vκ​⊨ZFC. The existence of this set model proves Con(ZFC) in the universe. By arithmetic absoluteness for a rank-initial model, the same formal consistency statement holds in Vκ​. Hence Vκ​⊨T∗, so T proves Con(T∗).
Conversely, suppose T∗ proved Con(T). The theory T proves every axiom of T∗, since a worldly cardinal proves Con(ZFC). It would therefore also prove Con(T), contrary to the Gödel second incompleteness theorem when T is consistent. Thus T∗ cannot prove Con(T), and
T∗<Cons​T.​
(10)
This is the consistency strength of a worldly cardinal comparison.

j1​(κ0​)=κ0​.
(11)
To evaluate j0​(κ1​), first use the regularity of the measurable cardinal κ1​. Every function f:κ0​→κ1​ has bounded range, so every ordinal below j0​(κ1​) lies below j0​(β) for some β<κ1​. Hence
j0​(κ1​)=supβ<κ1​​j0​(β).

Fix i∈{0,1} and write κ=κi​, j=ji​, and M=Mi​. Every ordinal below j(κ) is represented in the ultrapower by a function κ→κ. Consequently, in Vλ​,
∣j(κ)∣≤κκ=2κ=κ+,
(14)
where the last equality uses the Generalized continuum hypothesis.
By elementarity, M regards j(κ) as measurable and hence as a strong limit cardinal. Moreover Vκ+1​⊆M, so M and Vλ​ have the same subsets of κ and the same κ+. It follows inside M that
κ+=2κ<j(κ).
(15)
Thus, in the ambient Vλ​, the ordinal j(κ) is strictly larger than κ+ but has cardinality at most κ+. It cannot be a cardinal number. Applying this argument to both i=0 and i=1 proves that neither j0​(κ0​) nor j1​(κ1​) is a cardinal in Vλ​, exactly as in moved critical point is not an ambient cardinal under GCH.
