A morphism of schemes is proper when it is a finite type morphism, a separated morphism, and a universally closed morphism. The valuative criterion for properness says, under the usual finite-type and Noetherian hypotheses, that f:X→Y is proper exactly when every commutative square
SpecK↓⏐​SpecR​⟶⟶​X↓⏐​fY​
(1)
with R a discrete valuation ring and K=FracR has a unique diagonal lift SpecR→X.
For Pk2​→Speck, a K-point is [x0​:x1​:x2​] with not all xi​ zero. If π is a uniformizer, multiply all coordinates by one power of π so that mini​v(xi​)=0. The resulting coordinates lie in R and at least one is a unit, so they define an R-point extending the given K-point. If two extensions exist, on a chart where one coordinate is a unit their affine coordinate ratios agree in K and therefore in the integral domain R; hence the extensions agree. This verifies existence and uniqueness directly.

Take the projective line with a doubled point over C, obtained by gluing two copies of PC1​ along the complement of one point. After every base change of a morphism of schemes, a closed subset has closed image from each of the two projective-line charts, so its total image, the union of those two images, is closed. The structure morphism is therefore universally closed. The two doubled points have no disjoint neighborhoods, so the scheme is not separated and hence is not proper.

The coincidence locus of two scheme morphisms is the fibre product
E=X×(f,g),Y×k​Y,ΔY​​Y.
(4)
The diagonal morphism ΔY​ is a locally closed immersion, and this property is stable under base change of a morphism of schemes, so E↪X is locally closed. The universal property of a fibre product says that a morphism T→X factors through E exactly when f∣T​=g∣T​. Thus E is the largest locally closed subscheme on which they coincide. If Y is separated, ΔY​ is a closed immersion, and its base change E↪X is closed.

The standard affine charts of P3 are copies of A3, and the product charts of P1×P1 are copies of A2. Their local rings are localizations of polynomial rings over k and hence are regular local rings. Both schemes are therefore regular.
On a regular integral scheme every Weil divisor is Cartier, so the divisor class group is naturally the Picard group. Pullback of line bundles along the Segre embedding therefore defines
ι∗:Cl(P3)⟶Cl(P1×P1).
(11)
The two groups are
Cl(P3)=Z[O(1)],Cl(P1×P1)=Z[O(1,0)]⊕Z[O(0,1)].
(12)
The Segre coordinates are bihomogeneous of bidegree (1,1), so ι∗OP3​(1)=O(1,1). In these bases the map is m↦(m,m) and
imι∗=Z(1,1)⊂Z2.​
(13)
