The Malgrange–Ehrenpreis theorem states that every nonzero constant-coefficient linear partial differential operator P(D) on Rn has a fundamental solution of a linear differential operator: there is an E∈D′(Rn) such that P(D)E=δ0​.
Write D=−i∂. After an orthogonal change of coordinates and multiplication by a nonzero constant, its polynomial symbol may be written as a monic polynomial in the last frequency,
P(ξ′,z)=zM+∑m=0M−1​am​(ξ′)zm.
(24)
For each real μ′, this polynomial has M complex roots counted with multiplicity. Among a fixed finite collection of horizontal lines at bounded heights, one can choose a line that stays a positive distance from all those roots. Continuity of the roots preserves the choice on a neighborhood N(μ′). Take a countable locally finite cover by such neighborhoods, refine it to a measurable disjoint partition Rn−1=⨆j​Δj​, and let cj​ be the chosen height on Δj​. The resulting Hörmander staircase
Σ=⋃j​{(ξ′,s+icj​):ξ′∈Δj​, s∈R}
(25)
has bounded heights and may be chosen so that ∣P(ξ′,s+icj​)∣≥1 on each step.
For a test function φ, define
⟨E,φ⟩=(2π)n1​∑j​∫Δj​​∫R+icj​​P(ξ′,z)φ​(−ξ′,−z)​dzdξ′.
(26)
The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem gives rapid decay in the real frequency directions and at most a fixed exponential factor in the bounded imaginary direction. Together with ∣P∣≥1, this proves that the integral defines a continuous distribution. Applying P(D) cancels the denominator. The remaining integrand is entire in z, so the Cauchy integral theorem shifts every horizontal contour to the real axis; the partition then recombines into Rn−1. The Fourier inversion theorem gives
⟨P(D)E,φ⟩=φ(0)=⟨δ0​,φ⟩,
(27)
which proves the theorem.
