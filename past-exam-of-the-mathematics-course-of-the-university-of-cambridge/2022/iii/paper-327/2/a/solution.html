Let K contain the support of u∈E′(Rn). Choose a cutoff function that equals one near K. It makes
u(λ)=⟨u(x),e−iλ⋅x⟩
(12)
well-defined, and differentiating the parameter under the pairing gives
∂λγ​u(λ)=⟨u(x),(−ix)γe−iλ⋅x⟩.
(13)
Thus the Fourier transform of a compactly supported distribution is a smooth function. Since a compactly supported distribution has finite order, some N and C satisfy
∣⟨u,ψ⟩∣≤C∑∣α∣≤N​supK​∣∂αψ∣.
(14)
Applying this estimate to the exponential yields ∣u(λ)∣≤C′⟨λ⟩N.
Now take v∈E′(X), multiply by a cutoff function supported in X and equal to one near suppv, and regard the result as an element of E′(Rn). Choose m so large that
g(λ)=⟨λ⟩−2mv(λ)
(15)
is Lebesgue integrable. The inverse Fourier transform f=F−1g is a bounded continuous function, and the Fourier transform of a derivative gives
v=(1−Δ)mf
(16)
as a distributional identity. This is the Bessel potential proof of the structure theorem for compactly supported distributions.
The function f itself need not have compact support. Choose another cutoff ρ∈D(X) equal to one near suppv. Then v=ρ(1−Δ)mf. Repeatedly using

expresses v as a finite sum ∑β​∂βfβ​, where every coefficient fβ​ is continuous and compactly supported in X.
