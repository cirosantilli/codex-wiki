A maximal intersecting family F⊆P([n]) is an up-set: if A∈F and A⊆B, then B intersects every member, so maximality forces B∈F. For every complementary pair {A,Ac}, at most one member lies in F. If neither did, maximality would give B∈F disjoint from A; then B⊆Ac, and the up-set property would put Ac in F, a contradiction. Exactly one set from each complementary pair occurs, so
∣F∣=2n−1.​
(5)
For n≥4, three pairwise nonisomorphic examples are:
  • the star {A:1∈A};
  • the triangle family {A:∣A∩{1,2,3}∣≥2};
  • the majority family {A:∣A∣>n/2} when n is odd, and, when n is even, this family together with the n/2-sets containing 1.
Their smallest members have different sizes except at n=4, where the inclusion graphs of the minimal two-sets are respectively a triangle and a three-edge star. Thus they are nonisomorphic.
The Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem says that if n≥2r and F⊆[n](r) is intersecting, then
∣F∣≤(r−1n−1​).
(6)
For the Kruskal-Katona theorem proof, the family of complements Fc⊆[n](n−r) is disjoint from the (n−r)th upper shadow of F: otherwise one member of F would be contained in the complement of another. Kruskal–Katona says that when ∣F∣=(r−1n−1​) this upper shadow has at least (rn−1​) members, with a strict corresponding inequality above that threshold. Since these two disjoint families lie in one level of size (rn​), the EKR bound follows.
For the averaging proof, place [n] in a cyclic order. Among the n cyclic intervals of length r, an intersecting family contains at most r: after fixing one interval, the possible intersecting intervals can be paired by their first separating endpoint. Double-counting pairs consisting of a cyclic order and a member of F that appears as an interval gives
(rn​)∣F∣​≤nr​,
(7)
which is the same bound. This is the Katona circle method.
The minimum size fr​(n) does not tend to infinity. Fix a core S⊆[n] of size 2r−1 and take
F=[S](r).
(8)
Any two members intersect. If an r-set A is not contained in S, then ∣A∩S∣≤r−1, so the remaining at least r points of S contain a member of F disjoint from A. Thus F is maximal intersecting in [n](r), and
fr​(n)≤(r2r−1​)
(9)
independently of n.
