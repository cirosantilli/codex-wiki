Let q=(q1​,…,qN​) denote the particle positions. In the Ghirardi--Rimini--Weber model, the wavefunction obeys the ordinary Schrödinger equation
iℏ∂t∂ψ​=Hψ
(1)
between random collapses. Each particle i has an independent Poisson process of collapse times with rate λ. At such a time the state jumps according to
ψ(q)⟼∥Li​(X)ψ∥Li​(X)ψ(q)​,Li​(X)=(πrC2​)3/41​exp[−2rC2​(qi​−X)2​],
(2)
where the random centre has probability density ∥Li​(X)ψ∥2. The normalization is arranged so that ∫d3XLi​(X)2=I.
The original GRW scales are approximately
rC​∼10−7 m,λ∼10−16 s−1.
(3)
An isolated microscopic particle is therefore exceedingly unlikely to collapse during a laboratory experiment. A macroscopic pointer containing about N∼1023 relevant particles has total collapse rate Nλ∼107 s−1 and collapse time about 10−7 s. After a measurement interaction correlates different microscopic outcomes with pointer positions separated by much more than rC​, one constituent's localization suppresses all incompatible pointer branches. This GRW amplification mechanism produces one definite macroscopic outcome with probabilities given by the Born rule, while leaving ordinary microscopic unitary time evolution almost unchanged.
For one spatial coordinate, average over the random centre of one collapse. The resulting density operator has position-space kernel
ρ′(x,x′)=exp[−4rC2​(x−x′)2​]ρ(x,x′).
(4)
Its diagonal is unchanged, so ⟨x⟩ and ⟨x2⟩ are unchanged. The first derivative of the Gaussian factor vanishes at x=x′, so ⟨p⟩ is also unchanged. Its second derivative does not vanish, and the position representation of the momentum operator gives
⟨p2⟩′=⟨p2⟩+2rC2​ℏ2​​.
(5)
In three dimensions the increase in total p2 is 3ℏ2/(2rC2​), corresponding to kinetic-energy increase 3ℏ2/(4mrC2​) per collapse. These are ensemble statements: conditioning on one specified collapse centre can shift the position moments. Repetition at rate λ predicts GRW spontaneous heating, so precision searches for anomalous bulk heating, spontaneous radiation, momentum diffusion, and loss of matter-wave interference test the model.
