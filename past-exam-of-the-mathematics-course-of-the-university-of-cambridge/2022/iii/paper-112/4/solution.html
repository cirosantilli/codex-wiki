A Wirtinger presentation from a connected knot diagram has one generator per arc and one relator per crossing, with one relator redundant. Its presentation complex is a finite two-dimensional CW complex X with one zero-cell, n one-cells, and n−1 two-cells, and the usual diagrammatic construction gives a homotopy equivalence X≃EK​.
The abelianization of the knot group is H1​(EK​;Z)≅Z, generated by a meridian of a knot. Every homomorphism to the cyclic group Z/2 factors through this abelianization, and reduction modulo two is its unique surjection. Thus the requested map α is unique.
Its kernel determines a two-sheeted covering space X. The nontrivial deck transformation acts on cellular chains and homology, giving them module structures over the group ring
R=Z[Z/2]≅Z[t]/(t2−1).
(11)
Lift one copy of every cell of X; its two deck translates form a free R-basis. Hence C0​≅R, C1​≅Rn, and C2​≅Rn−1. If X is the infinite cyclic cover, its cellular chains are free over R=Z[t±1], and imposing t2=1 gives
C∗cell​(X)≅C∗cell​(X)⊗R​R.
(12)
For an odd prime p, the two idempotents e+​=(1+t)/2 and e−​=(1−t)/2 split the group algebra
Fp​[Z/2]≅R+​⊕R−​,R+​=Fp​[t±1]/(t−1),R−​=Fp​[t±1]/(t+1).
(13)
The plus summand is the cellular chain complex of X with Fp​ coefficients, while the minus summand is C−​=C∗cell​(X)⊗R​R−​. Therefore
H∗​(X;Fp​)≅H∗​(EK​;Fp​)⊕H∗​(C−​).
(14)
On the minus summand, the boundary t−1:C1​→C0​ becomes multiplication by −2, which is invertible in Fp​, so H0​(C−​)=0. After the corresponding cancellation, the remaining square boundary matrix is an Alexander matrix specialized at t=−1. It is singular over Fp​ exactly when
ΔK​(−1)≡0(modp),
(15)
or equivalently when p divides the knot determinant detK. Thus H∗​(C−​) is nonzero exactly in that case.
Finally, detK=∣ΔK​(−1)∣ is a nonzero odd integer, so the minus complex is acyclic over Q. Since a knot exterior has the rational homology of a circle,
Hi​(X;Q)≅{Q,0,​i=0,1,i≥2.​​
(16)
