One strong form of the Hensel lemma is this: if a complete discretely valued field K, a polynomial f∈OK​[X], and a∈OK​ satisfy
v(f(a))>2v(f′(a)),
(18)
then there is a unique root α in the ball v(α−a)>v(f′(a)).
Set an+1​=an​−f(an​)/f′(an​). Taylor expansion shows that the valuation of the error at least doubles at each step, while v(f′(an​)) remains constant. Thus the corrections tend to zero geometrically, so completeness gives a limit α. Continuity gives f(α)=0. Applying the same Taylor estimate to two roots in the stated ball proves uniqueness. This is Newton iteration over a valued field.
