In potential outcome notation, a valid instrumental variable Z must satisfy:
  • Instrument relevance: the conditional law of A changes with Z.
  • Instrumental-variable independence: Z is independent of the joint collection of potential treatments and outcomes, such as {A(z),Y(a):z,a}.
  • The exclusion restriction: Y(z,a)=Y(a), so Z can affect Y only through A.
  • Consistency of potential outcomes and no interference in causal inference connect the potential variables to the observations.
In the graph, Z1​ has open noncausal paths to Y that do not pass through A, including
Z1​←M1​→C→YandZ1​←S→Z3​→Y.
(1)
Equivalently, these paths remain after deleting the causal edge A→Y. Thus Z1​ is associated with potential outcomes through the latent variables C and S, violating instrumental-variable independence; it is not a valid marginal instrument.

Among observed pretreatment variables, every sufficient set must contain M1​ to block
Z1​←M1​→C→Y
(2)
and Z3​ to block
Z1​←S→Z3​→Y.
(3)
Conditioning on Z3​ opens the collider on
Z1​←S→Z3​←M3​→C→Y,
(4)
so M3​ must also be included. The resulting minimal sufficient adjustment set is
X0​={M1​,M3​,Z3​}.
(5)
It blocks every path from Z1​ to Y that remains after removing A→Y, while the open path
Z1​←S→Z2​→A
(6)
preserves instrument relevance. Adding M2​ blocks no required relevance path, so
X1​={M1​,M2​,M3​,Z3​}
(7)
is also sufficient.
There are no others. In particular, adding Z2​ blocks the displayed relevance path. Without M2​, conditioning on Z2​ also opens
Z1​←S→Z2​←M2​→C→Y,
(8)
which violates independence; adding M2​ closes that path but leaves no open path from Z1​ to A. Hence the complete list is X0​ and X1​.

For one unit write M=(M1​,M2​,M3​) and W=(M,Z1​,Z3​). Since the graph makes S independent of M, the known distributions determine the conditional assignment law
q(z2​∣w)=∑s​∑z2′​​p(s)p(z1​,z2′​,z3​∣m,s)∑s​p(s)p(z1​,z2​,z3​∣m,s)​.

Under the sharp causal null hypothesis that A has no effect on Y, delete A→Y. The D-separation criterion then gives
Z2​⊥Y∣W:
(10)
M2​ blocks the route through C, Z3​ blocks the route through S, M1​ and M3​ block the collider-opened detours through Z1​ and Z3​, and A remains a collider on routes through U.
For independent units i=1,…,n, choose a test statistic T(Z2,1:n​,Y1:n​,W1:n​) that measures residual association between Z2​ and Y. Hold (Yi​,Wi​) fixed and independently draw
Z2i(b)​∼q(⋅∣Wi​),b=1,…,B.
(11)
Recompute T(b) after each draw. A valid Monte Carlo conditional randomization test uses
pB​=B+11+∑b=1B​1{T(b)≥Tobs}​​,
(12)
with a two-sided statistic or absolute value when appropriate.
Under the null, conditional on (Y1:n​,W1:n​), the observed Z2,1:n​ and its B resamples are exchangeable because they have the same product law ∏i​q(⋅∣Wi​). The rank of Tobs among the B+1 values is therefore uniform after randomized tie breaking and conservative without it. Consequently
PH0​​(pB​≤α∣Y1:n​,W1:n​)≤α.

Take all components to be centered and let their marginal variances be VA​,VC​,VE​. A linear structural equation model is
Ci1​=Ci2​=Ci​,Yij​=Aij​+Ci​+Eij​,
(14)
where Ci​,Ei1​,Ei2​ are mutually independent and the Eij​ are identically distributed. The causal directed acyclic graph has arrows
Ai1​→Yi1​←Ci​→Yi2​←Ai2​,Ei1​→Yi1​,Ei2​→Yi2​.
(15)
For monozygotic twins, set Ai1​=Ai2​=Gi​ with Var(Gi​)=VA​; the genetic cause is completely shared. For dizygotic twins, one explicit construction is
Ai1​=Gi​+Gi1​,Ai2​=Gi​+Gi2​,
(16)
where Gi​,Gi1​,Gi2​ are independent with variance VA​/2. Then both additive genetic terms have variance VA​, while their covariance is VA​/2 and their correlation coefficient is 1/2. In a fuller graph, the shared Gi​ points to both genetic components and the unique Gij​ points only to its own component.

The common marginal trait variance is
VY​=VA​+VC​+VE​.

The no unmeasured confounding assumption, or conditional exchangeability, is
(Y(0),Y(1))⊥A∣X.
(23)
Also assume consistency of potential outcomes, no interference in causal inference, positivity in causal inference, and finite expectations. For a∈{0,1}, the law of total expectation, exchangeability, and consistency give
E[Y(a)]​=E{E[Y(a)∣X]}=E{E[Y(a)∣A=a,X]}=E{E[Y∣A=a,X]}.​
(24)
Positivity ensures that the observed conditional means exist on the covariate support being averaged. Subtracting the two cases identifies the average treatment effect as
β1​=E{E[Y∣A=1,X]}−E{E[Y∣A=0,X]}.​
(25)

For fixed β, conditional least squares is minimized by
gβ​(X)=E[Y−βA∣X]=μ(X)−βe(X).
(28)
Therefore the remaining objective is
E[{Y−μ(X)−β(A−e(X))}2],
(29)
whose normal equation gives
β3​=E[(A−e(X))2]E[(A−e(X))(Y−μ(X))]​.
(30)
Let
τ(X)=E[Y(1)−Y(0)∣X]
(31)
be the conditional average treatment effect. Since A is binary, conditional exchangeability implies
Cov(A,Y∣X)=e(X){1−e(X)}τ(X),
(32)
and Var(A∣X)=e(X){1−e(X)}. Hence
β3​=E[e(X){1−e(X)}]E[e(X){1−e(X)}τ(X)]​.
(33)
Thus β3​ is the overlap-weighted average treatment effect. It weights covariate strata by the overlap weight and generally differs from the ordinary ATE β1​=E[τ(X)] when treatment effects are heterogeneous and overlap varies with X.

i OurBigBook.com
The administrative data include only encounters with M=1. Conditioning on the stop indicator selects on the collider
D→M←U,
(36)
which opens the noncausal path D↔U→Y and creates collider bias. The equal entries estimate only the selected risks P(Y=1∣D=d,M=1). They ignore racial differences in the probability of being stopped and therefore do not identify the total causal effect of race on violence. This is also selection bias, because encounters with M=0 never enter the dataset.

The graph has no cause of D, so D is independent of its potential outcomes. By consistency of potential outcomes, the G-formula therefore gives
E[Y(d)]=E[Y∣D=d].
(38)
Condition on M and use the structural zero from part ii:
E[Y(d)]​=E[Y∣D=d,M=1]P(M=1∣D=d)+E[Y∣D=d,M=0]P(M=0∣D=d)=E[Y∣D=d,M=1]P(M=1∣D=d).​
(39)
Consequently the causal risk ratio is
E[Y(0)]E[Y(1)]​=E[Y∣D=0,M=1]E[Y∣D=1,M=1]​P(M=1∣D=0)P(M=1∣D=1)​.
(40)
Applying Bayes theorem to the second factor gives
P(M=1∣D=0)P(M=1∣D=1)​=P(D=1)/P(D=0)P(D=1∣M=1)/P(D=0∣M=1)​,
(41)
which proves the stated formula. The unmeasured common cause U of M and Y does not obstruct this total-effect argument because no mediator effect is being identified.

The stop records estimate
E[Y∣D=d,M=1]andP(D=d∣M=1),
(42)
but they cannot estimate the population encounter probabilities P(D=1) and P(D=0) because encounters without a stop are absent. Equivalently, they do not determine the race-specific stop-probability ratio.
The scientist needs a representative denominator for all police-civilian encounters, including those with M=0. Suitable sources could include a carefully designed population or travel survey, systematic street and traffic observation, dispatch or body-camera sampling that records non-stop encounters, or an external administrative source measuring exposure to police by race. Combining its estimate of the population race odds with the estimable stop-data terms identifies the displayed causal risk ratio, provided the external sample targets the same city, period, and encounter population.
