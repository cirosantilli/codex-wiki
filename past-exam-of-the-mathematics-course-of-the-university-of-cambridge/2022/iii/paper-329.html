(2)

Write the perturbation as ηeikx+st and use the same factor for all velocity and pressure amplitudes. At the upper interface, the linearized kinematic boundary condition, zero tangential traction, and normal-stress balance are
v=sη,uy​+vx​=0,−p+2μvy​=(h04​3V​−γk2)η(y=h0​).
(21)
The first term in the normal stress is the linearization of the attractive disjoining pressure V/h3, while the second is the stabilizing capillary pressure. Symmetry about y=0 makes u even and v odd, and supplies the lower-interface conditions.
For a two-dimensional Fourier mode, the Papkovich–Neuber representation is equivalently expressed by the odd Biharmonic stream function for planar Stokes flow
ψ(y)=Asinh(ky)+Bycosh(ky),u=ψy​,v=−ikψ.
(22)
The tangential-stress condition at y=h0​, with K=kh0​, gives
AksinhK+B(sinhK+KcoshK)=0,
(23)
whereas the kinematic condition gives iBsinhK=sη. The associated normal traction is
−p+2μvy​=μksηsinh2K2K+sinh2K​.
(24)
Equating this with the linearized interfacial traction yields the dispersion relation
s=μh03​3V​K(2K+sinh2K)(1−ΓK2)sinh2K​,Γ=3Vγh02​​.​
(25)
This is the Van der Waals rupture instability of a viscous sheet.
For Γ=0, the growth rate is positive, starts from 3V/(4μh03​) as K→0, and decreases to zero like 3V/(2μh03​K) as K→∞. For Γ=1, it has the same long-wave limit, vanishes at K=1, and is negative for K>1: surface tension damps wavelengths shorter than the cutoff. Long waves feel the attractive interaction but require coherent flow over a large distance; at short wavelengths viscous resistance suppresses the clean-film instability, while capillarity adds direct decay. A finite film size, fluid inertia, surrounding-fluid stresses, gravity, surface viscosity, and failure of the continuum disjoining pressure law can shift the observable most unstable wavelength.
During a growing thin spot, interfacial flow stretches the surface and dilutes its surfactant, thereby increasing the local surface tension above γ0​. Adjacent less-stretched regions retain more surfactant and lower tension. The resulting surface-tension gradient pulls toward the thin spot and opposes the outward flow that drives thinning. This is surfactant stabilization of film rupture; in the strong limit the surfaces behave almost as immobile boundaries.
For strong surfactant and Γ≫1, instability requires K=O(Γ−1/2). The Taylor expansion
4Kcosh2Ksinh2K−2K​=3K2​+O(K4)
(26)
reduces the supplied relation to
s∼μh03​V​K2(1−ΓK2).
(27)
It is maximal at
Kmax2​=2Γ1​,smax​=4μh03​ΓV​=4μγ0​h05​3V2​.​
(28)
Thus the characteristic rupture time is smax−1​=4μγ0​h05​/(3V2). Surfactant greatly extends the life of a soap bubble while its film is moderately thick, but the h0−5​ growth-rate dependence predicts rapid final rupture after drainage has made the film sufficiently thin.

i OurBigBook.com
Reflect the configuration in a plane perpendicular to the tube axis. The geometry and radial displacement are unchanged, whereas the imposed pressure gradient and every axial velocity reverse. By kinematic reversibility of Stokes flow, a radial migration velocity would also have to reverse; the spatial reflection leaves that component unchanged. Uniqueness of Stokes flow therefore forces it to vanish. Reflection in the meridional plane through the two axes similarly excludes azimuthal drift, so c remains constant.

The nearly occluding sphere in a cylindrical tube has gap thickness H=O(ϵa) and axial length L=O(aH​)=O(aϵ​). In the gap, lubrication theory therefore gives
px​=O(ϵ2a2μU​),p=O(aϵ3/2μU​),σxy​=O(ϵaμU​).
(30)
Far ahead of and behind the sphere, the length and width scales are both a, so
px​=O(a2μU​),σrx​=O(aμU​).
(31)
In the sphere frame the tube wall at y=0 moves with velocity −U and the sphere surface at y=h is stationary. The local Couette-Poiseuille flow in a thin gap is
u=−U+hU​y+2μpx​​y(y−h).
(32)
Its axial volume flux per unit circumferential width is
q=−12μh3​px​−2Uh​.​
(33)
Differentiating the profile and eliminating px​ gives the two wall stresses
μσxy​​​y=0​=h4U​+h26q​,μσxy​​​y=h​=−h2U​−h26q​.​
(34)
The continuity equation integrated across the gap says that changes of q along x are balanced by circumferential flux divergence. Circumferential variations occur on scale a, much longer than the axial scale aϵ​, so q=q(θ) is independent of x at leading order.
Put h=h0​(1+ξ2) with x=2ah0​​ξ. The leading O(ϵ−3/2) pressure jump must vanish in the global balance from part ii. Equivalently, the pressure recovery condition in lubrication flow gives
0=∫−∞∞​px​dx=−12μq∫h−3dx−6μU∫h−2dx.

The total flux through the gap is consequently
Q=a∫02π​qdθ=−34​πϵUa2.

Far from the sphere, translation of the tube contributes −πa2U, while Hagen-Poiseuille flow contributes πa4px​/(8μ). Equating these fluxes gives
px​∼a28μU​(1−34​ϵ).​

At the next order, substitute q=−2Uh0​/3 into the tube-wall shear and integrate through the gap:
