At radius r, radiation of luminosity L gives matter of flux-mean opacity κ the outward acceleration
arad​=4πr2cκL​.
(1)
Equating this with the gravitational acceleration GMBH​/r2 gives the Eddington luminosity
LEdd​=κ4πGMBH​c​.​
(2)
Let L=fLEdd​ and let M(r) be the radiative force multiplier relative to the Compton or electron-scattering acceleration. A steady spherical wind obeys mass conservation M˙w​=4πr2ρv and the radial Euler momentum equation
vdrdv​=−ρ1​drdP​−r2GMBH​​[1−fM(r)].​
(3)
When gas pressure is negligible, radiation accelerates the wind locally if fM>1. Electron scattering alone has M≃1, so it launches a wind only near or above the Eddington ratio f=1. Bound-free absorption, spectral lines, or dust can give M≫1 and allow a sub-Eddington source to launch a wind, provided the gas is not so highly ionized that those extra opacities disappear. A wind that has already acquired sufficient kinetic energy may coast through a region where fM<1, but such a region cannot launch a cold wind from rest.
Write the innermost stable circular orbit as
RISCO​=rISCO​(sBH​)c2GMBH​​.
(4)
Estimating the emitting area as 4πRISCO2​, the Stefan–Boltzmann law gives
4πRISCO2​σSB​TBB4​∼fLEdd​,
(5)
and hence
TBB​∼(κσSB​GMBH​fc5​)1/4rISCO−1/2​.​
(6)
Thus TBB​∝MBH−1/4​ at fixed f and opacity: a larger black hole radiates from an area growing as MBH2​, faster than its Eddington luminosity grows. Prograde black-hole spin moves the ISCO inward and raises the characteristic temperature, whereas retrograde spin moves it outward and lowers the temperature. Relativistic transfer and the radial temperature profile change the numerical coefficient, not these leading scalings.
