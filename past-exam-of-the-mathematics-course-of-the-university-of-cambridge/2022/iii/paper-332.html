h(x,t)∼KtnL2​F(X),X=Lx​.

Let ρs​ be the grain density and g′=g/g0​. Vertical force balance for the poroelastic aquifer gives
dzdσe​​=ϕ(ρs​−ρ)g,
(15)
with compression taken negative in σe​=E(ϕ0​−ϕ)/ϕ0​. Hence
dzdϕ​=−ℓc​g′​ϕ,ℓc​=ϕ0​(ρs​−ρ)g0​E​.​
(16)
The zero-effective-stress condition ϕ(h)=ϕ0​ then gives
ϕ(z)=ϕ0​exp[ℓc​g′(h−z)​].​
(17)
This identifies the poroelastic compaction length.
The water volume per unit horizontal distance is
V=∫0h​(1−ϕ)dz.
(18)
For h≪ℓc​, a Taylor expansion yields
V=(1−ϕ0​)h−2ℓc​ϕ0​g′h2​+O(h3/ℓc2​).​
(19)
Storage conservation is Vt​+qx​=R. Integrating from river to divide and using q(L,t)=0 gives the river influx
Qr​(t)=−q(0,t)=RL−dtd​∫0L​V(x,t)dx.​

∣δQr​∣∼2ℓc​ϵωϕ0​​∫0L​h2dx.

In the frame of a planar interface moving at speed V, the steady liquid temperature obeys −VTz​=κTzz​ and approaches T∞​. Thus
T=T∞​+(Ti​−T∞​)e−Vz/κ.
(23)
The Stefan condition, with negligible solid-side heat flux, gives
LV=cp​(Ti​−T∞​)V,
(24)
so Ti​−T∞​=L/cp​=SΔT. The kinetic undercooling law then gives
V=G(Tm​−Ti​)=GΔT(1−S),​
(25)
which requires S<1 for advancing solidification.
Scale length with κ/V, time with κ/V2, and temperature with ΔT. The base liquid temperature is Θ0​=Se−z. For an interface displacement ζeiαx+σt, write the thermal perturbation as
θ=Ae−λz+iαx+σt.
(26)
The heat equation gives
λ2−λ−α2−σ=0,λ=21​[1+1+4(α2+σ)​].​
(27)
Linearizing the Stefan condition gives
λA=S(1+σ)ζ,
(28)
while the kinetic law with stabilizing curvature undercooling gives
A=[S−(1−S)(σ+Γα2)]ζ,Γ=κγG​.
(29)
Eliminating A gives the general implicit dispersion relation
λS(1+σ)​=S−(1−S)(σ+Γα2),λ2−λ=α2+σ.​
(30)
At marginal stability σ=0,
λ=21​(1+1+4α2​),
(31)
and the dispersion relation becomes
1−SS​=Γλ−1λ​α2.​
(32)
Since α2=λ(λ−1) on this curve, the right-hand side is simply Γλ2. A positive cutoff wavenumber therefore exists exactly when
Γ(1−S)S​>1,that is,S>1+ΓΓ​.​
(33)
The unstable band is 0<α<αc​, where
λc​=[Γ(1−S)S​]1/2,αc2​=λc​(λc​−1).
(34)
A sketch of Γλ2 against the constant S/(1−S) shows no crossing above α=0 below threshold and one cutoff above it. As S↑1, λc​ and αc​ diverge like (1−S)−1/2, so the range of the morphological instability of a kinetically limited solidification front becomes unbounded.

The Newtonian shallow-ice flux on the flat bed is
q=−3νgh3​hx​,
(35)
so mass conservation gives
ht​=3νg​(h3hx​)x​+A(0<x<xs​),ht​=3νg​(h3hx​)x​−A(xs​<x<xN​).​
(36)
At the ice divide, symmetry gives hx​(0,t)=q(0,t)=0. At the terminus, h(xN​,t)=0 and the moving-front mass balance applies; in a steady state q(xN​)=0. At the snowline, h(xs​,t)=hs​ and both h and q are continuous, hence hx​ is continuous because hs​>0.
In a steady state, qx​=A in the accumulation region and qx​=−A in the ablation region. Therefore
q=Ax(0<x<xs​),q=A(xN​−x)(xs​<x<xN​).
(37)
Flux continuity gives xN​=2xs​. Integrating the flux law gives the piecewise shape
h4=h04​−g6νA​x2(0≤x≤xs​),​
(38)
h4=g6νA​(xN​−x)2(xs​≤x≤xN​).​
(39)
Matching h(xs​)=hs​ yields
xs​=(6νAg​)1/2hs2​,xN​=2xs​,h0​=21/4hs​.​
(40)
The largest surface slope is of order νA/g​/hs​, so the shallow-ice approximation requires
A≪νghs2​​.​
(41)

qG​=6νgg′​​hG3​,g′=gρw​ρw​−ρ​.​

The accumulation region still has q=Ax. In the ablation region, q=A(2xs​−x), so q(xG​)=qG​ gives
