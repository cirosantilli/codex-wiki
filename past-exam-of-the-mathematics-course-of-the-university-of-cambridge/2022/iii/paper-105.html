The Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem states in particular that for a bounded open set U⊂Rn with smooth boundary, the embedding
H1(U)↪L2(U)
(13)
is compact. More generally it is compact into Lq(U) for 1≤q<2n/(n−2) when n≥3, for every finite q when n=2, and into C0(U) in dimension one.
Boundedness is essential. Choose a nonzero φ∈Cc∞​(Rn) and set uk​(x)=φ(x−ke1​) with pairwise disjoint supports. Their H1(Rn) norms are equal, while
∥uj​−uk​∥22​=2∥φ∥22​
(14)

Let B and F satisfy the hypotheses of the Lax-Milgram theorem. By the Riesz representation theorem, there are a bounded linear operator T:H→H and z∈H such that
B(u,v)=(Tu,v)H​,F(v)=(z,v)H​.
(21)
Coercivity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality imply
α∥u∥H2​≤(Tu,u)H​≤∥Tu∥H​∥u∥H​,
(22)
so ∥Tu∥H​≥α∥u∥H​. Thus T is injective and its range is closed. If y is orthogonal to its range, then B(x,y)=0 for every x; taking x=y and using coercivity gives y=0. The range is therefore dense as well as closed, hence all of H. There is a unique u=T−1z, and it satisfies B(u,v)=F(v) for all v. The lower bound also gives ∥u∥H​≤∥F∥/α.
