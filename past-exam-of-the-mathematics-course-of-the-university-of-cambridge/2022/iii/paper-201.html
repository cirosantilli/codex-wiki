Project Brownian motion modulo Z3 to the compact three-dimensional torus. The projected process has normalized volume as invariant probability measure and is irreducible, so it visits every nonempty open set infinitely often almost surely. The image of S3​ contains the radius-ϵ ball about the origin. Hence the original Brownian motion hits S3​ at an unbounded set of times.

Yes. Quotient only the first coordinate modulo Z. The resulting process lives on T×R2 and the image of S1​ is the radius-ϵ ball about (0,0,0).
