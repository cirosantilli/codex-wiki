Write Z=X−μ, so the covariance operator is
CX​h=E[⟨Z,h⟩Z].
(1)
For g,h∈L2[0,1],
⟨CX​h,g⟩=E[⟨Z,h⟩⟨Z,g⟩]=⟨h,CX​g⟩,
(2)
so CX​ is self-adjoint. Moreover,
⟨CX​h,h⟩=E[⟨Z,h⟩2]≥0,
(3)
so it is a positive operator.
Let (ej​) be any orthonormal basis. Tonelli theorem and Parseval identity give
trCX​=∑j​⟨CX​ej​,ej​⟩=E∑j​∣⟨Z,ej​⟩∣2=E∥Z∥2<∞.
(4)
A positive operator with finite trace is a trace-class operator, completing the proof.

Under H0​, the Hilbert-space central limit theorem gives
n​(μ​−μ0​)d​G,
(11)
where G is a centered Gaussian random element with covariance CX​. Distinct leading eigenvalues imply consistency of the empirical eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, up to irrelevant signs. The first K standardized functional principal component scores of G are independent N(0,1) variables, so Slutsky theorem yields
TPC​d​χK2​.
(12)
An asymptotic level-α test rejects above the (1−α) quantile of the chi-squared distribution.
Under a fixed alternative, put δ=μ−μ0​. The weak law of large numbers and eigenpair consistency give
nTPC​​p​∑k=1K​λk​⟨δ,ϕk​⟩2​.
(13)

Let C∗=CX​+δ⊗δ, where (δ⊗δ)h=⟨δ,h⟩δ. Suppose for contradiction that
⟨δ,ϕl∗​⟩=0(l=1,…,K).
(14)
Then C∗ϕl∗​=CX​ϕl∗​, so every leading eigenpair (λl∗​,ϕl∗​) is also an eigenpair of CX​. Since C∗≥CX​, the Courant–Fischer min-max principle gives λl∗​≥λl​. If λl∗​=λjl​​ in the strictly decreasing spectrum of CX​, this inequality implies jl​≤l. Orthogonality and distinctness force
{j1​,…,jK​}={1,…,K}.
(15)
Hence the first K eigenvectors of C∗ span the same space as ϕ1​,…,ϕK​. Their assumed orthogonality to δ would imply ⟨δ,ϕk​⟩=0 for every k≤K, contradicting the hypothesis. Therefore some l≤K satisfies ⟨δ,ϕl∗​⟩=0.
