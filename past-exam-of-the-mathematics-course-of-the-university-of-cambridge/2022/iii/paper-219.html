The vector (y1​,y2​,y3​) is Jointly Gaussian. Assuming R>0, Gaussian conditional independence gives
y3​⊥y1​∣y2​⟺Cov(y1​,y3​∣y2​)=R13​−RR12​R23​​=0.
(17)
Thus the required condition is RR13​=R12​R23​. Under it, conditioning on y1​ supplies no further information after y2​, and the Gaussian process regression posterior is
y3​∣y2​,y1​∼N(μ+RR23​​(y2​−μ),R−RR232​​).
(18)
Both the conditional expectation and conditional variance depend only on y2​,t2​,t3​; neither contains y1​ or t1​.

A Gibbs sampler draws each variable from its full conditional distribution, so every proposed update is accepted. At the start of a sweep define cs​=cs​−Es​, ys​=Ms​−REs​, and
ds​=Ms​−M0​−βcs​,vE​=(σc2​1​+σM2​(R−β)2​)−1,
(31)
mE,s​=vE​[σc2​cs​−c0​​+σM2​(R−β)ds​​−τ1​].
(32)
First update the reddenings independently as
Es​∣rest∼TN[0,∞)​(mE,s​,vE​).
(33)
Recompute cs​ and ys​, then make the Gaussian updates
c0​∣rest∼N(cˉ,Nσc2​​),
(34)
M0​∣rest∼N(N1​∑s​(ys​−βcs​),NσM2​​),
(35)
β∣rest∼N(∑s​cs2​∑s​cs​(ys​−M0​)​,∑s​cs2​σM2​​),
(36)
and
R∣rest∼N(∑s​Es2​∑s​Es​[Ms​−M0​−βcs​]​,∑s​Es2​σM2​​).
(37)
With
Sc​=∑s​(cs​−c0​)2,SM​=∑s​(ys​−M0​−βcs​)2,
(38)
the remaining full conditionals, in the parameterization given in the question, are the scaled inverse chi-squared laws
σc2​∣rest∼Inv-χ2(N−2,N−2Sc​​),
(39)

(41)
Repeating these updates in the displayed order gives a complete Gibbs sweep. The formulas assume N>2 and nondegenerate sampled predictors; posterior propriety must be checked because the hyperpriors are improper.

Let S=σ2+τ2. For one posterior draw, the Gaussian quadratic-exponential moment is finite precisely when τ<σ, and then
Eθ∣y​[p(y∣θ)−2]=2πσ2σ2−τ2S​​exp[S(σ2−τ2)σ2y2​].
