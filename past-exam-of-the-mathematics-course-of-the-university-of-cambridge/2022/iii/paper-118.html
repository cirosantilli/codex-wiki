An almost complex structure on a smooth manifold M is a smooth bundle endomorphism J:TM→TM satisfying J2=−1. On the complexified tangent bundle, J has the eigenbundle decomposition
TM⊗C=T1,0M⊕T0,1M,
(1)
with eigenvalues i and −i. A differential form of type (p, q) is a section of ⋀p(T1,0M)∗⊗⋀q(T0,1M)∗. The operators ∂ and ∂ˉ are the type-(1,0) and type-(0,1) components of the exterior derivative.
We prove the three stated conditions are equivalent. If X,Y have type (1,0) and α has type (0,1), the formula for the exterior derivative gives
dα(X,Y)=−α([X,Y]).
(2)
Thus closure of T1,0M under the Lie bracket is equivalent to the vanishing of the (2,0) component of dα for every (0,1)-form. Complex conjugation of differential-form type gives the corresponding vanishing of the (0,2) component on (1,0)-forms. Since every complex one-form is a sum of these two types, this is precisely
dα=∂α+∂ˉα.
(3)
This proves (i)⟺(ii).
Under (ii), the (2,0) component of d2f=0 is ∂2f, proving (iii). Conversely, for X,Y of type (1,0),
(∂2f)(X,Y)=df([X,Y]0,1).
(4)
If this vanishes for every smooth complex-valued f, then [X,Y]0,1=0, so (iii) implies (i). These conditions define an integrable almost complex structure.
For a complex manifold, a holomorphic chart zj=xj+iyj defines JM​(∂xj​)=∂yj​ and JM​(∂yj​)=−∂xj​. The derivative of a holomorphic coordinate change is complex linear, hence commutes with multiplication by i; the definitions therefore glue and are independent of coordinates. Locally T1,0M is spanned by the commuting fields ∂/∂zj, so it is closed under brackets and JM​ is integrable. This is the almost complex structure induced by a complex atlas.
We next prove the local Dolbeault-Poincaré lemma. Write a ∂ˉ-closed (0,q)-form on a slightly larger polydisc as
φ=α+dzˉn​∧β,
(5)
where neither α nor β contains dzˉn​. Apply the supplied one-variable Cauchy-Green operator coefficientwise to β, obtaining γ with ∂γ/∂zˉn​=β. Then φ−∂ˉγ contains no dzˉn​, and ∂ˉφ=0 says that its coefficients are holomorphic in zn​ and ∂ˉ-closed in the first n−1 variables. Induction on n, with the one-variable formula as the base case, makes this remainder ∂ˉ-exact. Hence every ∂ˉ-closed (0,q)-form with q>0 is ∂ˉ-exact on each bounded polydisc.
Finally, translation by i leaves dzˉ unchanged, so this form descends to C/(z∼z+i). It is in fact exact on this noncompact complex cylinder, because the invariant function z+zˉ=2Rez descends and satisfies
∂ˉ(z+zˉ)=dzˉ.​
(6)

A holomorphic line bundle is a complex line bundle with holomorphic transition functions, and a holomorphic section is one whose coefficient in every holomorphic local frame is holomorphic. Given a Hermitian metric on a holomorphic vector bundle h, its Chern connection is the connection ∇ satisfying
∇0,1=∂ˉL​,dh(s,t)=h(∇s,t)+h(s,∇t).
(7)
Let e be a nonvanishing holomorphic local frame, put H=h(e,e)>0, and write ∇e=Ae. The first condition forces A0,1=0, while metric compatibility forces
A=H−1∂H=∂logH.
(8)
This determines ∇ uniquely and also constructs it. If e′=ge for a nowhere-zero holomorphic function g, then A′=A+g−1∂g, exactly the connection one-form transformation law, so the local constructions glue.
For a line bundle, A∧A=0, and the curvature form of a connection is
F(∇)=dA=∂ˉ∂logH=−∂∂ˉlogH.
(9)
It has type (1,1). Under e′=ge, the extra term g−1∂g is closed, so the curvature is unchanged and therefore global. This is the local formula for the Chern connection on a line bundle.
Any other Hermitian metric has the form h=euh for a global smooth real function u. Its local squared norm is H=euH, whence
F(∇)−F(∇)=∂ˉ∂u.
(10)
Connections ∇1​ and ∇2​ induce the tensor product connection
(∇1​⊗∇2​)(s1​⊗s2​)=∇1​s1​⊗s2​+s1​⊗∇2​s2​.
(11)
Its connection form in a product frame is A1​+A2​, so the curvature of a tensor product connection is F1​+F2​. For Chern connections, equip L1​⊗L2​ with the product metric
h(s1​⊗s2​,t1​⊗t2​)=h1​(s1​,t1​)h2​(s2​,t2​).
(12)
The tensor product connection has the correct (0,1) part and preserves this metric, so uniqueness identifies it with the Chern connection of h.

The tautological bundle over Complex projective space is
O(−1)={([z],v)∈CPn×Cn+1:v∈Cz}.
(13)
On Ui​={zi​=0}, the vector ei​([z])=z/zi​ is a holomorphic frame. On Ui​∩Uj​,
ej​=zj​zi​​ei​,
(14)
so the transition functions are holomorphic. Define O(1)=O(−1)∗ and, for k∈Z, define O(k)=O(1)⊗k when k≥0 and O(k)=O(−1)⊗(−k) when k<0. Its transition functions are the corresponding kth powers of those of O(1), equivalently the (−k)th powers of those of O(−1).
A nonzero linear functional ℓ∈(Cn+1)∗ restricts on each projective line to define a global holomorphic section sℓ​ of O(1). Its zero divisor is the projective hyperplane Hℓ​={[z]:ℓ(z)=0}, so every hyperplane is an effective divisor of O(1).
Conversely, let D be the divisor of a nonzero section of O(1). In the affine chart z0​=1, the section is represented by an entire function f on Cn. Compatibility with the other projective charts gives the linear growth bound in the question along every complex affine line. The supplied Liouville-type result makes f affine-linear, and homogenizing it gives a linear functional ℓ on Cn+1. Thus D=Hℓ​.
In local coordinates centred at x, the blowup of a complex manifold at a point is modeled by
mathbbCn={(z,[v])∈Cn×CPn−1:z∈Cv},σ(z,[v])=z.
(15)
On the chart vi​=0, write u=zi​ and tj​=vj​/vi​; then zj​=utj​, giving holomorphic coordinates (u,(tj​)j=i​). These charts show that the blowup is a complex manifold, that σ is holomorphic, and that its exceptional fiber is CPn−1. A biholomorphic coordinate change at the centre lifts by sending a punctured point together with its limiting tangent direction to its image; the chart formulas extend across the exceptional divisor. Hence the construction is independent of coordinates up to biholomorphism.
For a(z)=−z on C2, define
a(z,[v])=(−z,[v]).
(16)
This is holomorphic and satisfies σ∘a=a∘σ. The blowup mathbbC2 is the total space of O(−1)→CP1, and a acts as multiplication by −1 in every fiber. The invariant fiber coordinate is w=u2. If uj​=gji​ui​ are the transition laws for O(−1), then wj​=gji2​wi​, which are the transition laws for O(−2). Thus the local quotient maps (t,u)↦(t,u2) glue, give the quotient a complex-manifold atlas even along the fixed zero section, and yield
mathbbC2/⟨a⟩≅TotO(−2).​
(17)

Write g=Reh for the underlying Riemannian metric of the Hermitian manifold. Its fundamental form of a Hermitian manifold is
ω(u,v)=g(Ju,v).
(18)
It is real and skew-symmetric. In a unitary coframe it is ω=∑j=1n​ej∧Jej, which also shows that it has type (1,1) and that
volg​=n!ωn​.
(19)
The Hodge star operator is characterized, after complex-linear extension, by
α∧∗β​=⟨α,β⟩volg​.
(20)
Expanding in the same unitary coframe gives
∗ω=(n−1)!ωn−1​.
(21)
The Hodge Laplacian and Dolbeault Laplacian are
Δd​=dd∗+d∗d,Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ.
(22)
The Dolbeault Hodge decomposition on a compact Hermitian manifold says that every Dolbeault class has a unique Δ∂ˉ​-harmonic representative and
Ωp,q=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉΩp,q−1⊕∂ˉ∗Ωp,q+1.
(23)
If Δ∂ˉ​η=0, then
0=⟨Δ∂ˉ​η,η⟩=∥∂ˉη∥22​+∥∂ˉ∗η∥22​,
(24)
so η is ∂ˉ-closed and ∂ˉ∗-closed. If also η=∂ˉξ, then ∥η∥22​=⟨∂ˉ∗η,ξ⟩=0, hence η=0.
Now suppose X is compact and Kähler. With dc=i(∂ˉ−∂) and d∗=∂∗+∂ˉ∗, the Kähler identities make the mixed anticommutators vanish and give Δ∂​=Δ∂ˉ​. Consequently
dcd∗+d∗dc=0.
(25)
Let α=dcγ and dα=0. The Kähler Laplacian identity implies that the d-Laplacian commutes with dc. Since a harmonic form is dc∗-closed, α is orthogonal to every harmonic form. If G is the Green operator of the Hodge Laplacian, then
α=Δd​Gα=dd∗Gα,
(26)
where the d∗dGα term vanishes because dGα=Gdα=0. The Green operator commutes with dc, and the anticommutation identity just proved gives
d∗Gα=d∗dcGγ=−dcd∗Gγ.
(27)
Therefore, for the (k−2)-form β=−d∗Gγ,
α=ddcβ.​
(28)
This is the d d c lemma in the form required here.
