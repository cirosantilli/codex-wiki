a OurBigBook.com
For a binary fluid mixture, the scalar compositional order parameter ϕ(r) may be taken as the local concentration difference between its two molecular species. Its spatial integral is fixed by their separately fixed total amounts:
∫V​ϕ(r)dr=Vϕˉ​.
(1)
Locally, ϕ can change only through transport and therefore obeys a continuity equation. By contrast, p is the local polar order parameter measuring the mean tail-to-head orientation of the surfactant molecules. Individual molecules can rotate in place, so the integral of p need not be conserved.

b OurBigBook.com
The composition changes most rapidly across an interface, so ∇ϕ points along its normal. The term
λp⋅∇ϕ
(2)
therefore couples the surfactant's head-to-tail orientation to the interface normal. Minimization aligns p antiparallel to ∇ϕ when λ>0 and parallel when λ<0. The sign is fixed by which fluid component is called positive ϕ and by which molecular end defines positive p, together with the preferential affinity of the head and tail for the two components.

Surfactants accumulate at an interface and orient their two chemically distinct ends toward their preferred fluid components. The relaxation found in part c lowers the coefficient of the gradient energy, and hence generally lowers the surface tension. If the reduction makes κ<0, the positive fourth-gradient term proportional to γ can stabilize structure at a nonzero wavevector, producing the modulated correlations characteristic of a microemulsion.

Traverse the large rectangle counterclockwise, taking its lower edge at y→−∞ and upper edge at y→+∞. Along the lower edge the director angle changes by −π/2; along the upper edge, traversed from x=L to x=0, it changes by another −π/2. The anchored director is constant along the two vertical edges. The net continuous angle change is therefore
Δθ=−π.
(26)
The topological charge of a two-dimensional nematic disclination enclosed by a circuit is q=Δθ/(2π), so
qtotal​=−21​.​
(27)
A nonsingular director field on the enclosed disk would have zero winding. At least one nematic disclination must therefore lie inside.

The least costly configuration contains one charge-−1/2 nematic disclination, placed near (L/2,0) by the reflection symmetries of the boundary data. Away from its small core, the director interpolates as smoothly as possible between the wall anchoring and the two far-field textures. Locally around the defect one may sketch
θ(r,φ)=−2φ​+θ0​.
(28)
The elastic energy of an isolated defect scales as q2log(R/acore​). Splitting the required total −1/2 charge into additional allowed half-charge defects is impossible without also adding compensating defects, which raises both the logarithmic elastic energy and the positive core energy. The single centered −1/2 defect is therefore the lowest-energy topology, up to smooth distortions and symmetry-related core placement.

If ∇×F=0 on a simply connected region containing the bounded trajectory, then the Poincare lemma gives a scalar potential U with F=−∇U. The work is
W=∫F⋅dx=U(x1​)−U(x2​),
(48)
which remains bounded when x and F remain bounded. Since H is also bounded, ΔQ=W−ΔH cannot grow linearly with the observation time.
Thus sustained linear heat dissipation in this setting requires a nonconservative force, and, under the stated simply connectedness assumption,
∇×F=0.​
(49)
Such a force can perform nonzero work on repeated bounded cycles. On a multiply connected domain, a curl-free force can have nonzero circulation, so the topological assumption is essential.
