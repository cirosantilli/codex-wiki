The Cameron-Martin theorem on Wiener space says that the translated measure Ph​(A)=P(X+h∈A) is equivalent to Wiener measure precisely when h is absolutely continuous, h(0)=0, and h˙∈L2(R+​). In that case
dPdPh​​=exp(∫0∞​h˙s​dXs​−21​∫0∞​h˙s2​ds).
(13)
For such h, the exponential is a uniformly integrable stochastic exponential. Under the measure defined by this density, the Girsanov theorem makes Xt​−∫0t​h˙s​ds=Xt​−h(t) a Brownian motion. This identifies the translated law and proves equivalence; replacing h by −h gives the inverse density.
If h fails the Cameron-Martin condition on some finite interval, the finite-horizon theorem gives singularity there. If it belongs locally but ∫0∞​h˙s2​ds=∞, the log likelihood is a Brownian motion run at that diverging energy clock minus half the clock. It tends to −∞ under one measure and to +∞ under the translate, producing disjoint full-measure events. Thus the measures are singular.
