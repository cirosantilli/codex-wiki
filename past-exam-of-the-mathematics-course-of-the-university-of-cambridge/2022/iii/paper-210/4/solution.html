The pushforward measure of μ under g is
(g∗​μ)(B)=μ(g−1(B)),B∈B.
(35)
The Lebesgue decomposition theorem says that for sigma-finite measures P,Q there are unique measures Pac​ and Ps​ such that
P=Pac​+Ps​,Pac​≪Q,Ps​⊥Q.
(36)
For a convex lower-semicontinuous function f with f(1)=0, choose any measure λ dominating probability measures P,Q, put p=dP/dλ and q=dQ/dλ, and define the f-divergence
Df​(P∥Q)=∫qf(p/q)dλ,
(37)
using the lower-semicontinuous perspective value when q=0. This definition includes the singular part and is independent of λ.
To prove the data processing inequality for f-divergences, let G=σ(g) and use λ=P+Q. The densities of g∗​P and g∗​Q, pulled back to G, are Eλ​(p∣G) and Eλ​(q∣G). Since the perspective
Φ(a,b)=bf(a/b)
(38)
is jointly convex, conditional Jensen inequality gives
Φ{E(p∣G),E(q∣G)}≤E{Φ(p,q)∣G}.
(39)
Integration proves
Df​(g∗​P∥g∗​Q)≤Df​(P∥Q).
(40)
The chi-squared divergence is
χ2(P,Q)=∫(dQdP​−1)2dQ
(41)
when P≪Q, and is infinite otherwise.
Fix a probability measure Q. Let J be uniform on {1,…,M} and, conditionally on J=j, draw X from Pj​. Compare this joint law with the reference law under which J is uniform and independent of X∼Q. For
H(J,X)=1{X∈AJ​}​,
(42)
the target expectation is M−1∑j​Pj​(Aj​), while its reference expectation is
M1​∑j​Q(Aj​)=M1​
(43)
because the Aj​ form a partition. Its reference variance is M−1(1−M−1). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality applied to the likelihood ratio gives
M1​∑j​Pj​(Aj​)−M1​≤M1​(1−M1​)​χ2(PJ,X​,UM​⊗Q)​.
(44)
The last divergence separates over J:
χ2(PJ,X​,UM​⊗Q)=M1​∑j=1M​χ2(Pj​,Q).
(45)
Taking the infimum over all probability measures Q proves the required inequality.
