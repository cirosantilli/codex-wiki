A morphism of schemes is proper when it is a finite type morphism, a separated morphism, and a universally closed morphism. The valuative criterion for properness says, under the usual finite-type and Noetherian hypotheses, that f:X→Y is proper exactly when every commutative square
SpecK↓⏐​SpecR​⟶⟶​X↓⏐​fY​
(1)
with R a discrete valuation ring and K=FracR has a unique diagonal lift SpecR→X.
For Pk2​→Speck, a K-point is [x0​:x1​:x2​] with not all xi​ zero. If π is a uniformizer, multiply all coordinates by one power of π so that mini​v(xi​)=0. The resulting coordinates lie in R and at least one is a unit, so they define an R-point extending the given K-point. If two extensions exist, on a chart where one coordinate is a unit their affine coordinate ratios agree in K and therefore in the integral domain R; hence the extensions agree. This verifies existence and uniqueness directly.
