A reduced word is either the empty word or a product
u1​u2​⋯un​
(2)
in which every syllable ui​ is a nonidentity element of G or H, and consecutive syllables belong to different factors.
Let W be the set of reduced words. Each g∈G acts on the right of W: if the last syllable lies in H, append g; if it lies in G, multiply it by g and delete it when the product is the identity. Define the action of each h∈H analogously. These rules give genuine actions of the two factors by permutations of W, hence an action of the free group F(X⊔Y). Every relation in R⊔S acts trivially, so the action factors through the displayed presentation of G∗H.
The element represented by a reduced word w sends the empty word to w. Therefore two reduced words representing the same element induce the same permutation and have the same value on the empty word. They must be identical. This proves the normal form theorem for a free product.

Let w be a nonempty reduced word. If its first syllable belongs to G, choose any nonidentity h∈H. The reduced form of hw begins with an H-syllable, whereas that of wh begins with the original G-syllable; reduction at the right end cannot change the first syllable. Hence hw=wh. The same argument with a nonidentity g∈G applies when w begins in H. No nonidentity element is therefore central, and
Z(G∗H)=1.​

Let N be the subgroup whose upper-left block is I2​. It is a finitely generated nilpotent subgroup of the integer upper unitriangular group UT4​(Z) and is normal in L. The block-diagonal matrix
t=diag(A,I2​)
(9)
generates an infinite cyclic quotient, so
L=N⋊⟨t⟩.
(10)
Finitely generated nilpotent groups are polycyclic, and an extension of polycyclic groups is polycyclic. Hence L is polycyclic.
Inside L, retain only t and the entries in positions (1,3) and (2,3). They form a subgroup
Z2⋊A​Z.
(11)
The characteristic polynomial of A is λ2−5λ+1, so its eigenvalues are
25±21​​.
(12)
One has modulus greater than one, and the resulting semidirect product has exponential growth. Every finitely generated virtually nilpotent group has polynomial growth, as does each of its finitely generated subgroups. Therefore L cannot be virtually nilpotent.

The empty word is the unique vertex of degree three in the underlying tree; every other vertex has degree four. Every graph automorphism therefore fixes the empty word and permutes its three neighbours. Those neighbours are precisely {0,1,2}, so this set is invariant.

An element g fixing the first level restricts to an automorphism gi​ on each rooted subtree, and composition is coordinatewise. Therefore
ϕ(g)=(g0​,g1​,g2​)
(16)
is a homomorphism. If all three sections are trivial, g fixes every word, so ϕ is injective.
Directly from the recursions,
ϕ(b)=(a,1,b),qquadϕ(aba−1)=(b,a,1),qquadϕ(a−1ba)=(1,b,a).

A group G is residually finite when, for every 1=g∈G, there are a finite group Q and a group homomorphism q:G→Q such that q(g)=1. Equivalently, the intersection of all finite-index normal subgroups of G is trivial.

By the Fundamental theorem of finitely generated abelian groups,
A≅Zr⊕T
(22)
with T finite. If a nonzero element has a nonzero component in T, projection to T separates it. Otherwise some integer coordinate is a nonzero n; choose a prime p not dividing n and reduce that coordinate modulo p. This gives a finite quotient in which the element survives, so every finitely generated abelian group is residually finite.

Let α:G→G be surjective and suppose that 1=g∈kerα. By residual finiteness, choose q:G→Q with Q finite and q(g)=1. The preceding part makes the sequence
q, qα, qα2,…
(23)
repeat, so qαi=qαj for some i<j. Surjectivity of αi permits cancellation on the right and gives q=qαj−i. But g∈kerαj−i, which would imply q(g)=1, a contradiction. Thus α is injective. Every finitely generated residually finite group is therefore a Hopfian group.

Let θ:H≀G→Q be any homomorphism to a finite group. Because G is infinite, two distinct elements r,s∈G have θ(r)=θ(s). For h∈H, denote by hg​ the lamp with value h at g. Conjugation translates lamps, so
θ(hr​)=θ(hs​)
(29)
for every h∈H. Choose h,k∈H with [h,k]=1. Lamps at different coordinates commute, and therefore
θ([hr​,kr​])=θ([hs​,kr​])=1.
(30)
But [hr​,kr​] is the nonidentity lamp [h,k]r​. This same nonidentity element is killed by every finite quotient, so H≀G is not residually finite.
