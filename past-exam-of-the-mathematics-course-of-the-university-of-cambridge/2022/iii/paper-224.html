Stein's lemma states that for distinct probability mass functions P,Q on a finite alphabet, the best exponential decay rate of the Type II error βn​=Q⊗n(Bn​) among tests whose Type I error αn​=P⊗n(Bnc​) is eventually at most any fixed ϵ∈(0,1) is
limn→∞​−n1​log2​βn​=D(P∥Q).
(1)
For the direct part, define the information-density typical region
Bn​={x1n​:n1​log2​Q⊗n(x1n​)P⊗n(x1n​)​≥D(P∥Q)−δ}.
(2)
The weak law of large numbers under P gives αn​→0, while
βn​​=x1n​∈Bn​∑​Q⊗n(x1n​)≤2−n{D(P∥Q)−δ}x1n​∈Bn​∑​P⊗n(x1n​)≤2−n{D(P∥Q)−δ}.​
(3)
For the converse, let
Cn​={x1n​:n1​log2​Q⊗n(x1n​)P⊗n(x1n​)​≤D(P∥Q)+δ}.
(4)
Again P⊗n(Cn​)→1. Any test with P⊗n(Bn​)≥1−ϵ satisfies
P⊗n(Bn​∩Cn​)≥1−ϵ−o(1).
(5)
On Cn​, Q⊗n≥2−n(D+δ)P⊗n, hence
βn​≥2−n(D+δ){1−ϵ−o(1)}.
(6)
Thus limsupn​−n−1log2​βn​≤D+δ. Letting δ↓0 proves optimality.

For an independent identically distributed source with mass function P on a finite alphabet and a fixed compression rate R>H(P), the optimal probability of decoding error has exponent
E(R)=minQ:H(Q)≥R​D(Q∥P),
(12)
meaning that the best fixed-rate codes satisfy
limn→∞​−n1​log2​Pe(n)​=E(R)
(13)
at continuity points of the exponent.
For the direct part, let m=∣A∣ and
ηn​=nmlog2​(n+1)​.
(14)
Encode every sequence whose type Q satisfies H(Q)≤R−ηn​. The method of types bounds the number of such sequences by
(n+1)m2n(R−ηn​)=2nR,
(15)
so they fit into a rate-R codebook. The error probability obeys
Pe(n)​​≤Q:H(Q)>R−ηn​∑​P⊗n(T(Q))≤(n+1)m2−nminQ:H(Q)>R−ηn​​D(Q∥P).​
(16)
Compactness of the probability simplex and continuity of entropy and relative entropy on the support of P give
liminfn→∞​−n1​log2​Pe(n)​≥minQ:H(Q)≥R​D(Q∥P)=E(R),
(17)
which is the direct bound.

The binary Kraft inequality says that codeword lengths l1​,l2​,… of a prefix code satisfy
∑i​2−li​≤1.
(33)
Conversely, suppose positive integer lengths obey this inequality and arrange them in nondecreasing order. Construct codewords greedily in the infinite binary tree. Before assigning length li​, each earlier codeword of length lj​≤li​ excludes exactly 2li​−lj​ nodes at depth li​. Thus the number excluded is
∑j<i​2li​−lj​=2li​∑j<i​2−lj​<2li​,
(34)
where strictness follows because the remaining term 2−li​ occurs in the full Kraft sum. A free depth-li​ node therefore exists. Assign it as the next codeword; choosing a node not below an earlier codeword preserves prefix-freeness. Induction constructs the required prefix code.

A distribution on {1,2,…} must have μ≥1. For μ>1, put p=1/μ and let
G(k)=p(1−p)k−1.
(37)
For any mass function P with mean μ, Gibbs inequality gives
D(P∥G)​=−H(P)−log2​p−(μ−1)log2​(1−p)=H(G)−H(P)≥0.​
(38)
Thus the geometric distribution uniquely maximizes entropy. For μ=1, the only admissible law is the point mass at one, which is the limiting geometric case p=1.
