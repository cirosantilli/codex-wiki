An m by n matrix has matrix rank m exactly when at least one of its m by m minors has nonzero determinant. For each multi-index I, the set
UI​={M:detMI​=0}
(1)
is open in the vector space of all real m by n matrices, because the determinant is continuous. Their union is Xm,n​, so Xm,n​ is itself open in Rmn. It therefore inherits the standard smooth manifold structure and has dimension mn. This is the full-row-rank matrix manifold.

For x∈S2⊂R3, let F(x)=xT, viewed as a nonzero 1 by 3 matrix. This defines a smooth map F:S2→X1,3​. Its pulled-back fiber is
(F∗E1,3​)x​=kerxT=x⊥=Tx​S2,
(7)
so the evident fiberwise identity gives an isomorphism of vector bundles F∗E1,3​≅TS2.
If E1,3​ were a trivial vector bundle, its pullback would be trivial. A trivial rank-two bundle has a nowhere-zero section, whereas the assumed form of the Hairy ball theorem says that the tangent bundle TS2 does not. Hence E1,3​ is nontrivial.

Give ∂X the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. The Generalized Stokes theorem states that, for every compactly supported (n−1)-form ω,
∫X​dω=∫∂X​F∗ω.
(8)
Choose an oriented coordinate cover by charts into Rn or the half-space Hn={x1≥0}, and choose a partition of unity (ρi​) subordinate to it. Since the family is locally finite and ω has compact support, only finitely many ρi​ω are nonzero. It is therefore legitimate to write both integrals as finite sums and prove the identity for a form supported in one chart.
In an interior chart the integral of an exact compactly supported top form is zero by the fundamental theorem of calculus. In a boundary chart write
ω=∑j=1n​(−1)j−1aj​dx1∧⋯∧dxj∧⋯∧dxn.
(9)
Integrating dω=(∑j​∂j​aj​)dx1∧⋯∧dxn coordinate by coordinate kills every tangential derivative. The normal derivative leaves precisely the restriction to x1=0, with the sign selected by the outward-normal-first convention. This is ∫∂X​F∗ω, proving the theorem.

c OurBigBook.com
Suppose first that the left-invariant 1-form α is closed. The scalar function α(lξ​) is constant for every ξ, because both the form and vector field are left-invariant. Cartan's magic formula therefore gives
Llξ​​α=d(α(lξ​))+ιlξ​​dα=0.
(20)
Part b says that g↦Rg∗​α is locally constant. Since G is connected, it is constant, and its value at the identity is α. Hence Rg∗​α=α for every g, so α is bi-invariant.
Conversely, if α is bi-invariant, then all these Lie derivatives vanish. Cartan's formula and the constancy of α(lξ​) give ιlξ​​dα=0. The left-invariant vector fields span every tangent space, so dα=0.
Connectedness cannot be omitted. The orthogonal group O(2) has an Abelian Lie algebra, so every left-invariant 1-form is closed by the Maurer-Cartan equation in a Lie-algebra basis. Conjugation by a reflection acts as −1 on its one-dimensional Lie algebra, so a nonzero left-invariant 1-form is not right-invariant. This is the standard obstruction recorded by the closed left-invariant 1-form criterion.

The differential-forms version of the Frobenius theorem says that a constant-rank distribution D=⋂i​kerαi is integrable exactly when
dαi=∑j​βij​∧αj
(21)
for suitable 1-forms βij​. For a plane distribution D=kerα on R3, this reduces to the integrability criterion for a plane distribution α∧dα=0.
For example, kerdz is integrable: its integral surfaces are the horizontal planes z=constant. In contrast, for α=dz−xdy,
α∧dα=(dz−xdy)∧(−dx∧dy)=−dz∧dx∧dy=0.
(22)
Thus ker(dz−xdy) is not integrable; it is the standard contact structure on R3.

For a principal connection with connection form A, the horizontal distribution of a principal connection is H=kerA, the complement of the tangent spaces to the G-orbits. Its curvature of a principal connection is
F=dA+21​[A∧A].
(23)
If X,Y are horizontal vector fields, then A(X)=A(Y)=0, and hence
F(X,Y)=dA(X,Y)=−A([X,Y]).
(24)
The Frobenius theorem says that H is integrable exactly when [X,Y] is horizontal for all horizontal X,Y. The displayed identity makes this equivalent to the vanishing of the horizontal two-form F, hence to F=0. Thus the horizontal distribution is integrable exactly for a flat principal connection.

The vertical tangent space of the trivial principal R-bundle is spanned by ∂z​. Since
D=span{∂θ​+f∂z​, ∂t​+g∂z​}
(25)
projects isomorphically onto the tangent space of S1×R, it is always complementary to the vertical direction. It is the horizontal distribution of a principal connection precisely when it is invariant under the principal translations z↦z+a. The horizontal lifts of ∂θ​ and ∂t​ are unique, so this invariance is equivalent to
∂z​f=∂z​g=0.
(26)
The functions must also be smooth and periodic in θ, as is already required for them to be functions on the cylinder.
Under these conditions the connection form is
A=dz−fdθ−gdt.
(27)
It sends ∂z​ to 1, is translation-invariant, and has kernel D, proving sufficiency as well. Since the structure group R is abelian, the bracket term vanishes and
F=dA=(∂t​f−∂θ​g)dθ∧dt.​
(28)

For f=tcosθ+1 and g=sinθ,
∂t​f−∂θ​g=cosθ−cosθ=0.

The connection is therefore flat, so the Frobenius theorem gives horizontal sections locally.
A global section has the form z=h(θ,t) and is horizontal exactly when
∂θ​h=tcosθ+1,∂t​h=sinθ.

The second equation gives h=tsinθ+k(θ), and the first then forces k′(θ)=1. No such k is periodic on S1, so no global horizontal section exists. Equivalently, the horizontal lift of one positive circuit in the θ direction changes z by
∫02π​(tcosθ+1)dθ=2π,

which is nontrivial holonomy.
