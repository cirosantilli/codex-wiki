Put δ=exp(−ε−C), with the absolute constant C chosen sufficiently large below, and suppose for a contradiction that no g2​ with 0≤g2​≤1 satisfies ∥g1​−g2​∥F​≤ε.
Let
K={g∈HN​:0≤g≤1},B={h∈HN​:∥h∥F​≤ε}.
(64)
The set K is compact and convex, while B is closed and convex, so K+B is closed and convex. We use the following finite-dimensional form of the Hahn-Banach separation theorem: if a point lies outside a nonempty closed convex set, there is a linear functional whose value at the point is strictly greater than its supremum over that set. Identifying linear functionals on HN​ through the inner product, there is therefore a function ψ such that
⟨g1​,ψ⟩>supg∈K​⟨g,ψ⟩+ε∥ψ∥F∗​.
(65)
The separating functional cannot have zero dual norm, so rescale it to make ∥ψ∥F∗​=1. Because F is closed, convex, and symmetric, the finite-dimensional Bipolar theorem for a dual pair says that the unit ball of ∥⋅∥F∗​ is precisely F. Thus ψ∈F and ∣ψ(n)∣≤1.
The support function of K is obtained by choosing g(n)=1 where ψ(n)>0 and g(n)=0 where ψ(n)<0. In terms of the positive part of a real-valued function ψ+​=max(0,ψ), the separating inequality becomes
⟨g1​,ψ⟩>⟨1,ψ+​⟩+ε.
(66)
Since 0≤g1​≤ν, pointwise we have g1​ψ≤νψ+​, and hence
⟨ν−1,ψ+​⟩>ε.
(67)
Apply the supplied polynomial approximation of the positive part to ψ. If Pε​(t)=∑i=0m​ai​ti, then its uniform approximation error and Eν≤1 imply
∣⟨ν−1,ψ+​−Pε​(ψ)⟩∣≤100ε​E(ν+1)≤50ε​.
(68)
The constant function 1 and ψ belong to the dual unit ball. By the assumed submultiplicativity, ∥ψi∥F∗​≤1 for every i≥0. Dual seminorm therefore gives
∣⟨ν−1,Pε​(ψ)⟩∣≤∑i=0m​∣ai​∣∥ν−1∥F​∥ψi∥F∗​≤(m+1)maxi​∣ai​∣δ.
(69)
The stated coefficient bound, with C in δ chosen larger than the absolute constant in that bound, makes this last quantity at most ε/50. Together with the polynomial-approximation error, this contradicts ⟨ν−1,ψ+​⟩>ε. The required g2​ consequently exists. This proves the dense model theorem for a multiplicative test family.
