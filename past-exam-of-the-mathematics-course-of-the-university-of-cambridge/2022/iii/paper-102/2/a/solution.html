A finite root system in a real Euclidean vector space E is a finite spanning set Φ⊂E∖{0} such that, for every α∈Φ, the root reflection
sα​(v)=v−(α,α)2(v,α)​α
(11)
preserves Φ, and the Cartan integer 2(β,α)/(α,α) is an integer for all α,β∈Φ. It is reduced when the only scalar multiples of α in Φ are α and −α. Its Weyl group is the subgroup of the orthogonal group generated by the reflections sα​. A base of a root system Δ is a basis of E such that every root is an integer combination of elements of Δ whose nonzero coefficients all have the same sign.
The coroot of α is
α∨=(α,α)2α​.
(12)
Let C be the Weyl chamber determined by Δ:
C={v∈E:(v,α)>0 for every α∈Δ}.
(13)
The roots α and α∨ are positive scalar multiples, so their reflecting hyperplanes and their positive half-spaces are identical. The same chamber C therefore defines positivity in the coroot system Φ∨. Its walls correspond exactly to the rays R>0​α∨ for α∈Δ. Hence its simple roots are
Δ∨={α∨:α∈Δ},​
(14)
which is therefore a base of Φ∨.
