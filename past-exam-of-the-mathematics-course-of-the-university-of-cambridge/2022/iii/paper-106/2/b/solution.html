Thus Lp[0,1] is uniformly convex.
Now let X be uniformly convex. It is enough to show that every Φ∈SX∗∗​ lies in the canonical image of X. Given ε>0, choose the corresponding uniform-convexity constant δ, and choose f∈BX∗​ with Φ(f)>1−δ. If x,y∈BX​ both satisfy f(x),f(y)>1−δ, then
​2x+y​​>1−δ,
(16)
so ∥x−y∥<ε. By Goldstine theorem, every weak-star neighbourhood of Φ contains some Jx with x∈BX​. Directing these neighbourhoods produces a norm-Cauchy net (xα​); completeness gives xα​→x∈BX​, and weak-star convergence then gives Jx=Φ. Scaling handles the whole bidual ball, so X is reflexive.
For p≥2, uniform convexity therefore makes Lp reflexive. If 1<p<2, its conjugate exponent q is greater than two, so Lq is reflexive. Since (Lp)∗=Lq and a Banach space whose dual is reflexive is itself reflexive, Lp is reflexive for every 1<p<∞.
