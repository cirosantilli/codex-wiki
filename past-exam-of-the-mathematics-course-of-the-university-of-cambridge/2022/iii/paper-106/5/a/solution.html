Let pC​(x)=inf{t>0:x∈tC} be the Minkowski functional of C. Openness, convexity, and 0∈C make pC​ sublinear, with pC​(x)<1 for x∈C and pC​(x0​)≥1. Define a linear functional on Rx0​ by f(tx0​)=tpC​(x0​). The real Hahn-Banach theorem extends it to X with f≤pC​. Hence
f(x)<1≤f(x0​)(x∈C).
(28)
Apply this separation to the open ball of radius ∥x0​∥ and rescale to obtain f∈SX∗​ with f(x0​)=∥x0​∥. For a closed subspace Y, apply it to
C=Y+{x:∥x∥<d(x0​,Y)}.
(29)
The separator must vanish on Y because C contains every translate along Y, and normalization gives
Y⊆kerf,f(x0​)=d(x0​,Y).
(30)
Let F⊆X∗ be finite-dimensional and Φ∈BX∗∗​. Consider
S:X→F∗,Sx(f)=f(x).
(31)
If Φ∣F​ did not belong to S((1+ε)BX​), finite-dimensional strict separation would produce some f∈F with
Φ(f)>(1+ε)supx∈BX​​f(x)=(1+ε)∥f∥,
(32)
contradicting ∥Φ∥≤1. Thus there is x∈X with ∥x∥<1+ε and f(x)=Φ(f) for every f∈F.
Given a basic weak-star neighbourhood of Φ in BX∗∗​, apply this result to the finite-dimensional span of its defining functionals and then replace x by x/(1+ε). As ε↓0, the resulting points of J(BX​) enter that neighbourhood. Hence J(BX​) is weak-star dense in BX∗∗​, proving Goldstine theorem.
