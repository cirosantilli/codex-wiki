The Commutative Gelfand--Naimark theorem says that every commutative unital C-star algebra A is isometrically star-isomorphic to C(Δ(A)), where Δ(A) is its compact character space and the map is the Gelfand transform
Γ(a)(χ)=χ(a).
(25)
Indeed, maximal ideals give enough characters to identify σ(a) with the range of a. The C-star identity and the spectral radius formula give
∥a∥∞2​=r(a∗a)=∥a∗a∥=∥a∥2,
(26)
so Γ is isometric and has closed range. Characters send a∗ to χ(a)​, so the range is self-conjugate; it contains constants and separates distinct characters. The complex Stone-Weierstrass theorem makes the range dense in C(Δ(A)), and closedness makes it all of that algebra. This proves the theorem.
An element x of a C-star algebra is positive when it is self-adjoint and σ(x)⊆[0,∞). Consider the commutative C-star subalgebra C∗(1,x). Under its Gelfand–Naimark isomorphism, x becomes a nonnegative continuous function x. The function x​ is continuous and nonnegative, so its inverse image y is positive and satisfies
y2=x.​
(27)
