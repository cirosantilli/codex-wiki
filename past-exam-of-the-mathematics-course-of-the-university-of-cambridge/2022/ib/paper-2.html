A Sylow subgroup of a finite group G is a subgroup whose order is the largest power of a prime p dividing ∣G∣. The Sylow theorems say that such subgroups exist, that every p-subgroup lies in one, that all Sylow p-subgroups are conjugate, and that their number np​ divides ∣G∣/pa and obeys np​≡1(modp).
For the last congruence, let one Sylow p-subgroup P act by conjugation on the set of all Sylow p-subgroups. Every orbit other than a fixed point has size divisible by p. If Q is fixed, then P,Q≤NG​(Q). They are Sylow subgroups of this normalizer, so they are conjugate within NG​(Q); because every element of NG​(Q) fixes Q under conjugation, this forces P=Q. Thus there is exactly one fixed point and np​≡1(modp).
Now let H<An​ have index m with 1<m<n. The coset action gives a homomorphism
An​⟶Sm​.
(41)
Its kernel is a normal subgroup of the simple group An​. It cannot be all of An​, since the action is transitive and nontrivial, so it is trivial. This would embed An​ into Sm​, contrary to ∣An​∣=n!/2>m!=∣Sm​∣. Hence no such subgroup exists.
Suppose finally that a group G of order 90 were simple. The Sylow count satisfies
n5​∣18,n5​≡1(mod5),
(42)
and simplicity excludes n5​=1, so n5​=6. Conjugation on these six subgroups gives a nontrivial homomorphism G→S6​, which simplicity makes injective. Its image lies in A6​, because the composite with the sign homomorphism S6​→{±1} must be trivial. It would therefore be a subgroup of A6​ of index 360/90=4, contradicting the result just proved. Thus
no group of order 90 is simple.​
(43)
