The rank is r(α)=dimimα and the nullity is n(α)=dimkerα. Extending a basis of the kernel to a basis of V, the images of the added vectors form a basis of the image, proving the rank-nullity theorem
dimV=r(α)+n(α).
(21)
Since im(α+β)⊆imα+imβ, the upper sum bound follows. Applying it to α=(α+β)−β and interchanging α,β gives the lower bound. For products, the image lies in imα and is the image under α of imβ, giving the upper bound; rank-nullity on that restriction gives
r(αβ)≥r(β)−n(α)=r(α)+r(β)−n.
(22)
All four bounds are sharp. In a fixed basis, nested diagonal projections attain the product upper bound; placing imβ with the smallest possible intersection with kerα attains its lower bound. Taking β=−α on a subspace of dimension min(r(α),r(β)) attains the sum lower bound, while choosing the two images in general position and avoiding cancellation attains the sum upper bound.
Both upper bounds need not be attainable simultaneously over every field. Over F2​ with n=1 and both ranks one, necessarily α=β=I. Then r(αβ)=1 but r(α+β)=0, below its upper bound one.
