The Bezout identity states that for integers a,b, not both zero, there are integers r,s such that
ra+sb=gcd(a,b).
(29)
If the prime number p divides ab but does not divide a, then gcd(p,a)=1. Thus rp+sa=1 for some integers r,s, and multiplication by b gives
b=rpb+sab.
(30)
Both terms on the right are divisible by p, so p∣b. Hence
p∣ab⟹p∣a or p∣b​.
(31)
If gcd(m,n)=1, choose r,s with rm+sn=1. Then
x=a(sn)+b(rm)
(32)
satisfies x≡a(modm) and x≡b(modn). If x,x′ are two simultaneous solutions, both m and n divide x−x′. Coprimality implies mn∣x−x′, so the solution is unique modulo mn. This proves the two-modulus Chinese remainder theorem.
Let p be odd and suppose
x2≡1(modpd).
(33)
Then pd∣(x−1)(x+1). Since gcd(x−1,x+1) divides 2, the odd prime p cannot divide both factors. The full power pd must therefore divide one of them, giving
x≡1(modpd)orx≡−1(modpd).
(34)
These are distinct, so there are exactly two solutions.
