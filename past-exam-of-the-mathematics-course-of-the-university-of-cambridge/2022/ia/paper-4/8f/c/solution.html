A finite Cartesian product of countable sets is countable by repeated application of the diagonal enumeration in part (b)(i). Hence B is countable.
Conversely, suppose infinitely many factors contain at least two elements. Choose increasing indices n1​<n2​<⋯ and distinct elements

Fix one element in every remaining factor. Each binary sequence ε=(ε1​,ε2​,…) then defines an element of B by placing ajεj​​ in coordinate nj​ and the fixed element elsewhere. This map is injective.
The set {0,1}N is uncountable by Cantor's diagonal argument: from any proposed list of binary sequences, form a new sequence whose jth digit differs from the jth digit of the jth listed sequence. It is absent from the list. Thus B contains an uncountable subset and cannot be countable.
