Suppose there were only finitely many prime numbers congruent to 2 modulo 3, and list them as p1​,…,pk​. Consider
N=3p1​⋯pk​−1.
(1)
No pi​ divides N, and 3 does not divide N. In the prime factorization of N, not every prime factor can be congruent to 1 modulo 3, since their product would then also be congruent to 1, whereas
N≡2(mod3).
(2)
Thus some prime factor is congruent to 2 modulo 3, contradicting the completeness of the list. Hence
there are infinitely many primes of the form 3n+2​.
(3)
Now let p be prime. If p=3, then either p=2, giving
2p2+1=9,
(4)
or p is not divisible by 3, so p2≡1(mod3). In the latter case
2p2+1≡0(mod3)
(5)
and is greater than 3, hence is composite. For p=3 the number is 19, which is prime. Therefore
p=3 is the only possibility​.
