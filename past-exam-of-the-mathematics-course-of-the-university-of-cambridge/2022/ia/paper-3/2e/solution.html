A group G is abelian when xy=yx for every x,y∈G. It is cyclic when some generator of a group g satisfies
G=⟨g⟩={gn:n∈Z}.

If x=gr and y=gs belong to a cyclic group, then
xy=gr+s=gs+r=yx,

so every cyclic group is abelian.
The Klein four-group C2​×C2​ is abelian, but every nonidentity element has order two, so no element generates all four elements. Thus an abelian group need not be cyclic.
