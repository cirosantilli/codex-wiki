A Möbius transformation of the Riemann sphere is a map
z⟼cz+daz+b​,ad−bc=0,
(31)
with its natural values at the pole and at infinity.
For three distinct points z1​,z2​,z3​, define
Tz​(ζ)=(ζ−z3​)(z1​−z2​)(ζ−z2​)(z1​−z3​)​.
(32)
The usual limiting conventions cover an infinite zi​. This Möbius transformation sends (z1​,z2​,z3​) to (1,0,∞). Defining Tw​ similarly, the map
f=Tw−1​∘Tz​​
(33)
sends zi​ to wi​. If two Möbius transformations do so, their quotient fixes 0,1,∞. A Möbius transformation fixing infinity is affine, and fixing zero and one then makes it the identity. This proves uniqueness.
With this convention, the cross-ratio is
[z1​,z2​,z3​,z4​]=Tz​(z4​)=(z4​−z3​)(z1​−z2​)(z4​−z2​)(z1​−z3​)​.
(34)
Substitution shows that translations, nonzero scalings, and inversion preserve it; since these generate the Möbius group, every Möbius transformation preserves cross-ratios.
Conversely, suppose a bijection f of the Riemann sphere preserves every cross-ratio. Let m be the unique Möbius transformation agreeing with f at three chosen points z1​,z2​,z3​. For any other z, preservation by f and m gives
[z1​,z2​,z3​,z]=[f(z1​),f(z2​),f(z3​),f(z)]=[f(z1​),f(z2​),f(z3​),m(z)].
(35)
The last coordinate in a cross-ratio with three fixed distinct entries is injective, so f(z)=m(z). The equality already holds at the three base points, hence f=m everywhere and f is Möbius.
Finally, the map z↦az+b is constant when a=0 and therefore is not Möbius. If a=0, it is bijective and fixes infinity. Were it Möbius, it would have the affine form αz+β. Equality on real z forces α=a and β=b, whereas equality at z=i would require ai=−ai, contradicting a=0. Thus
there are no such a,b​.
