The set S(N) consists of all bijections N→N. The composition of two bijections is a bijection, composition is associative, the identity map is an identity, and every bijection has an inverse, so S(N) is a group.
An element of Sfin​(N) has finite support. The support of a composition is contained in the union of the two supports, and a permutation and its inverse have the same support. The identity has empty support. Hence Sfin​(N) is a subgroup.
Let σ be a cycle and choose n that it moves. Because σ has finite support, the sequence
n,σ(n),σ2(n),…
(45)
must repeat. Since σ is invertible, its first repetition returns to n; let the least positive return time be l. The cycle condition says that every moved point occurs in this orbit, so σl fixes every point. No smaller positive power fixes n. Therefore
ord(σ)=l​,
(46)
which is finite.
For τ∈Sfin​(N), partition its finite support into the orbits of the cyclic group ⟨τ⟩. On each orbit, let σi​ agree with τ and fix every point outside that orbit. Then each σi​ is a permutation cycle, their supports are pairwise disjoint, and
τ=σ1​⋯σk​.
(47)
Disjoint cycles commute because at every point at most one of them acts nontrivially.
Writing li​=ord(σi​), a power τm is the identity exactly when every σim​ is the identity, equivalently when every li​ divides m. Thus
