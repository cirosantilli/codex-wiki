Two forms are properly equivalent when one is obtained from the other by a change of variables in SL2​(Z). A reduced positive definite binary quadratic form satisfies
∣b∣≤a≤c,

with b≥0 when ∣b∣=a or a=c.
To prove reduction, choose in a proper equivalence class a form whose positive leading coefficient a is minimal. Replacing x by x+ny changes b by 2an, so choose n to arrange ∣b∣≤a. If the resulting c<a, the determinant-one substitution (x,y)↦(−y,x) would put c in the leading position, contradicting minimality. Thus a≤c. Sign changes on the boundary give the stated convention. Hence every positive definite form is properly equivalent to a reduced one.
