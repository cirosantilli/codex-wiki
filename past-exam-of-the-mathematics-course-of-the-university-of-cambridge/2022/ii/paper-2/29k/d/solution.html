The risk of Xn​ is constantly 1/n. Suppose an estimator δ dominates it, and put
m(θ)=Eθ​δ and b(θ)=m(θ)−θ. The biased Cramer-Rao bound, using total information n, gives
R(θ,δ)≥nm′(θ)2​+b(θ)2.
(167)
Domination would imply
(1+b′(θ))2+nb(θ)2≤1
(168)
for every real θ. The standard differential-inequality argument shows that the only globally defined differentiable solution is b≡0: a nonzero value forces b′ to retain a sign and magnitude that makes b leave the permitted bounded interval in one time direction.
Thus m(θ)=θ. The Cramer--Rao inequality now gives
Varθ​δ≥1/n, so domination forces equality everywhere. Equality in Cramer--Rao makes the centred estimator proportional to the Gaussian score:
δ−θ=n1​∑i​(Xi​−θ),
(169)
and hence δ=Xn​ almost surely. No strict improvement exists, so Xn​ is admissible.
