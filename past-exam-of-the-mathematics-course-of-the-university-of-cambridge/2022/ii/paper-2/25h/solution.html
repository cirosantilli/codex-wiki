The Riemann-Hurwitz formula for a nonconstant degree-n morphism
f:C→C′ of smooth projective connected curves in characteristic zero is
2g(C)−2=n(2g(C′)−2)+degRf​,

where Rf​ is the effective ramification divisor. If g(C′)≥2 and
g(C)<g(C′), the right side is at least 2g(C′)−2>2g(C)−2, impossible. If g(C′)=1, then g(C)=0 and the left side is −2 while the right side is nonnegative. Thus every morphism is constant.

For a smooth plane quartic, projection from a point of the curve gives a degree-three map, so its gonality is at most three. Its genus is three and
KC​∼D, so its plane embedding is the canonical map. A genus-three curve with a degree-two map to P1 would be hyperelliptic, and its canonical map would factor through that double cover and map onto a conic rather than embed the curve. Thus no degree-two map exists, and a degree-one map is excluded by the genus. The quartic's gonality is
