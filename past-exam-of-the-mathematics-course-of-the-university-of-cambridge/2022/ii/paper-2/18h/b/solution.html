An element α is separable over K when its minimal polynomial has distinct roots in a splitting field. A polynomial has a repeated root exactly when it has a common root with its formal derivative. Hence
gcd(f,f′)=1 implies that f has no repeated root and α is separable.
Let f be the minimal polynomial of β. Repeatedly factor through the Frobenius until
f(X)=g(Xph),
(83)
where g′=0. Irreducibility of f makes g irreducible, and
α=βph has separable minimal polynomial g. Put
M=K(α)=K(βph).
(84)
Then M/K is separable and
[L:M]=ph.
(85)
Every γ∈L=K(β) is a polynomial in β of degree below [L:K]. The freshman's-dream identity gives
γph∈K(βph)=M,
(86)
so L/M is purely inseparable.
Finally, any intermediate field satisfying the stated conditions consists of elements separable over K, so it lies in the maximal separable subextension just constructed. Conversely, the condition that all relevant phth powers lie in that field puts βph, and hence M, inside it. The two inclusions prove uniqueness.
