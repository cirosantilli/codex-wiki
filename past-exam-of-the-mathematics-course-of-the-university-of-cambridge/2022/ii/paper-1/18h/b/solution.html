If L/K is Galois, form the norm polynomial
F(X)=∏σ∈Gal(L/K)​σ(f)(X).
(95)
The Galois group permutes the factors, so F∈K[X]. Since the identity factor is f, taking g=F/f∈L[X] proves the claim.
If L/K is merely finite separable, place it in a finite Galois closure M/K and use the same product over Gal(M/K). It gives F∈K[X] divisible by f in M[X]. Polynomial division of F by f uses only coefficients in L, and the remainder is zero, so the quotient g=F/f actually lies in L[X]. It is nonzero and satisfies fg=F∈K[X].
