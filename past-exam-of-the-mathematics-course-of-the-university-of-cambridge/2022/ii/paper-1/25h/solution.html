For any morphism φ:V→V′ with V′ affine, its restriction to the exceptional curve E≅P1 is constant: every regular function on a projective line is constant, and the affine coordinate functions of V′ therefore have constant pullbacks. Since E has more than one point, φ is not injective. If V itself were affine, its identity morphism would contradict this conclusion, so V is not affine.
Over C, V is not compact in the Euclidean topology: the sequence (n,0,[1:0]) has no convergent subsequence. Every complex projective variety is Euclidean compact. Since compactness is preserved by homeomorphisms, V cannot be homeomorphic to a projective variety.
