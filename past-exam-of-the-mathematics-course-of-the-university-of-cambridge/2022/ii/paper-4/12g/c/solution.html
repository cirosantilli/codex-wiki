The set E=Δ2​×Δ2​ is a compact convex square. Define
ui​(p,q)=max{0,A(ei​,q)−A(p,q)},p′=1+u1​(p,q)+u2​(p,q)p+u(p,q)​.
(103)
Similarly, set
vj​(p,q)=max{0,B(p,ej​)−B(p,q)},q′=1+v1​(p,q)+v2​(p,q)q+v(p,q)​.
(104)
These formulae define a continuous self-map (p,q)↦(p′,q′) of E. The square is homeomorphic to a closed disc, so the Brouwer fixed-point theorem gives a fixed point (p∗,q∗).
Let U=u1​+u2​ at that fixed point. From p′=p,
ui​=pi​U.
(105)
If U>0, every i with pi​>0 has A(ei​,q)−A(p,q)>0. This contradicts bilinearity, because
∑i​pi​(A(ei​,q)−A(p,q))=0.
(106)
Hence U=0, all ui​=0, and
A(p∗,q∗)≥A(ei​,q∗)
(107)
for both pure strategies. By linearity this holds for every mixed strategy p. The same argument with vj​ gives
B(p∗,q∗)≥B(p∗,q)
(108)
for every q. Therefore
∀(p,q)∈E,A(p∗,q∗)≥A(p,q∗),B(p∗,q∗)≥B(p∗,q)​.
(109)
In game terms, p and q are the players' mixed strategies, and A,B are their expected payoffs in a two-by-two bimatrix game. The inequalities say that neither player can improve unilaterally, so (p∗,q∗) is a Nash equilibrium. This is the Brouwer proof of Nash equilibrium for a two-by-two game.
