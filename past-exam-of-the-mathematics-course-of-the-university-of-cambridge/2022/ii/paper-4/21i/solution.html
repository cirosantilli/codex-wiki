Cut the square model of the Klein bottle along the midline parallel to the pair of sides whose identification reverses orientation. Each half becomes a Möbius band, and the two new boundary circles are identified. If a and b are the core loops of these two bands, each boundary travels twice around its core. The Seifert-van Kampen theorem therefore gives
π1​(K,k0​)≅⟨a,b∣a2b−2=1⟩​.
(205)
Writing x=a−1b and y=a converts this into the standard Klein-bottle presentation
⟨x,y∣yxy−1=x−1⟩.
(206)
The orientation homomorphism sends both a and b to the nonzero element of C2​. Its kernel is the index-two subgroup
H=⟨a2,a−1b⟩≅Z2.
(207)
By the classification of connected covering spaces, H determines the orientation double cover of the Klein bottle
p:(T2,x0​)⟶(K,k0​).
(208)
Choosing torus generators α,β along the two translation directions, we may take
p∗​(α)=a2,p∗​(β)=a−1b​.
(209)
These commute: the standard relation says that a conjugates a−1b=x to x−1, so a2 centralizes x.
The space Y is the mapping cylinder of p with its unused end omitted. Sliding (x,t) to (x,0) gives a deformation retraction of Y onto the bottom quotient T2/∼≅K. Hence
π1​(Y,y0​)≅π1​(K,k0​)≅⟨a,b∣a2b−2⟩​.
(210)
Finally suppose U⊆X is open and homeomorphic to Y, and identify the bottom copy of K inside U. This K is compact and therefore closed in the Hausdorff space X. Apply van Kampen, in its groupoid form if intersections are disconnected, to
X=U∪(X∖K).
(211)
The intersection U∖K deformation retracts to a positive-height torus, and its map to π1​(U)≅π1​(K) has image precisely
p∗​π1​(T2)=H.
(212)
The orientation homomorphism
π1​(U)⟶π1​(U)/H≅C2​
(213)
is zero on this intersection, so it is compatible with the trivial homomorphism from π1​(X∖K) to C2​. The pushout property in van Kampen extends it to a surjection
π1​(X)↠C2​.
(214)
Therefore
X cannot be simply connected​.
(215)
This is the Klein-bottle mapping-cylinder obstruction to simple connectivity.
