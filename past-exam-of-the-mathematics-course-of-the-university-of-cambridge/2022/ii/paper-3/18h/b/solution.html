An algebraic closure of a field K is an algebraically closed field that is an algebraic extension of K.
By definition, every element of
Q​={z∈C:z is algebraic over Q}
(145)
is algebraic over Q, and the question allows us to use that this set is a field. It remains to prove that it is algebraically closed.
Let f∈Q​[X] be nonconstant. Its finitely many coefficients generate a finite extension E/Q. By the fundamental theorem of algebra, f has a root z∈C. Since z is a root of a polynomial over E, it is algebraic over E; since E/Q is algebraic, transitivity of algebraic extensions makes z algebraic over Q. Thus z∈Q​. Repeating after division by X−z shows that f splits there, so Q​ is algebraically closed and is an algebraic closure of Q.
