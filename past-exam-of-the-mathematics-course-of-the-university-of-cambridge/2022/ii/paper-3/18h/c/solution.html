Because charK=0, the finite extension M/K is separable. By the primitive element theorem, write M=K(θ). The minimal polynomial of θ over K has a root in the algebraically closed field L, so sending θ to that root defines a K-field embedding ι:M↪L.
The cyclic group ⟨σ⟩ acts faithfully on L after replacing d by the order of σ. The Artin fixed-field theorem gives
[L:K]=∣⟨σ⟩∣
(146)
and says that L/K is a finite Galois extension with cyclic Galois group ⟨σ⟩. The image E=ι(M) is an intermediate field. Every subgroup of a cyclic group is normal, so the normal subextension criterion shows that E/K is Galois, and its Galois group is a quotient of ⟨σ⟩, hence cyclic. Transporting this structure through ι proves that M/K is Galois with cyclic Galois group. This is the finite extensions of the fixed field of a finite-order automorphism of an algebraically closed field theorem.
Algebraic closedness is necessary. Take L=Q and σ=id, so K=Lσ=Q, and let M=Q(32​). The extension has degree three but is not normal, since the two nonreal roots of X3−2 do not belong to M. It is therefore not Galois.
