Choose a local coordinate w with w(p)=0. Differentiation at p gives a homomorphism
λ:H→C×,λ(h)=h′(p).
(183)
It is injective. Indeed, a nonidentity Möbius transformation fixing p and having derivative one there is parabolic, hence conjugate to a nonzero translation and of infinite order; no such element can belong to the finite group H. Every finite subgroup of C× is a cyclic group of roots of unity, so H is cyclic. Write n=∣H∣.
The averaged coordinate
z(q)=n1​∑h∈H​λ(h)−1w(hq)

has derivative one at p, so it is a coordinate on a smaller neighbourhood. For g∈H, reindexing by k=hg gives
z(gq)=λ(g)z(q).
(185)
After shrinking to an H-invariant disc U, the group therefore acts as all rotations z↦ζjz, where ζ is a primitive nth root of unity. The invariant coordinate
u=zn

identifies H\U with a disc and gives it a Riemann-surface chart. In these coordinates the quotient map is exactly
z⟼zn​.
(187)
This is the local cyclic quotient of a Riemann surface.
