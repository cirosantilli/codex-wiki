The Papkovich–Neuber representation writes a homogeneous Stokes flow in terms of a harmonic vector field Φ and a harmonic scalar χ as
μu=Φ−21​∇(x⋅Φ)+∇χ,p=−∇⋅Φ.
(1)
For translation, rotational symmetry and decay at infinity restrict the trial harmonic fields to the fundamental harmonic 1/r and its directional derivatives contracted with U. For rotation, the only decaying isotropic axial-vector field with the required boundary value is proportional to Ω×x/r3. Matching the no-slip boundary condition u=U+Ω×x at r=a gives the superposition of the translating sphere in Stokes flow and the rotating sphere in Stokes flow:
u=4r3a​(I+r2xx​)U+4r3a3​(I−3r2xx​)U+r3a3​Ω×x,p=2r33μa​U⋅x.​
(2)
Each term decays at infinity, and direct substitution at r=a gives the prescribed rigid velocity.
When U=0, the pressure is constant and may be set to zero. Differentiating the rotational velocity and using the Newtonian fluid stress tensor gives
σ=−r53μa3​[(Ω×x)x+x(Ω×x)].​
(3)

In the cylinder frame the wall moves at velocity −U. The parabolic lubrication gap and its natural stretched coordinate are
h(x)=2ϵa​+2ax2​=2ϵa​(1+ξ2),ξ=aϵ​x​.
(37)
The wall values are u(0)=−U and u(h)=Ωa to leading order. The Couette-Poiseuille flow in a thin gap is therefore
u(x,y)=−U+hU+Ωa​y+2μpx​​y(y−h),
(38)
and its constant flux is
q=2h​(Ωa−U)−12μh3​px​.
(39)
The pressure recovery condition in lubrication flow gives ∫−∞∞​px​dx=0. Since
∫h−3dx∫h−2dx​=32ϵa​,
(40)
it follows that
q=31​ϵa(Ωa−U),​px​=h26μ(Ωa−U)​−h312μq​.
(41)
Differentiating the velocity profile gives the two surface shear stresses
μσxy​​​y=0​=h4U−2Ωa​+h26q​,μσxy​​​y=h​=h−2U+4Ωa​−h26q​.​

Using ∫h−1dx=2π/ϵ​ and ∫h−2dx=2π/(aϵ3/2), the shear force exerted by the fluid on the wall is
Fwall(s)​=ϵ​4πμU​,​
(43)

Fcyl(s)​=−ϵ​4πμΩa​.​

Fcyl(p)​=∫hpx​dx=ϵ​4πμ(Ωa−U)​,
