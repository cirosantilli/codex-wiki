The total variation distance is
dTV​(μ,ν)=supB∈B(X)​∣μ(B)−ν(B)∣.
(30)
The problem is a well-posed Bayesian inverse problem in total variation when every v∈RN determines a unique posterior μv and
vn​→v⟹dTV​(μvn​,μv)→0.
(31)
The heat solution operator at positive time is bounded from L2[0,1] to C[0,1], so the finite sensor map G:X→RN is bounded and continuous. Therefore Φ(u;v) is jointly continuous and 0<e−Φ≤1. The normalizer satisfies 0<Z(v)≤1. If vn​→v, the dominated convergence theorem gives both
Z(vn​)→Z(v)
(32)
and convergence in L1(μ0​) of the normalized posterior densities. Since total variation is one half of this L1 distance for absolutely continuous measures, dTV​(μvn​,μv)→0. Existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence all follow.
