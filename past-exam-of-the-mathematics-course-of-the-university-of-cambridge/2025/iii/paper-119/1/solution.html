The Yoneda lemma states that for A∈C and P:C→Set there is a natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),P)≅P(A),α⟼αA​(1A​).
(1)
If P↠Q is an epimorphism in the functor category, it is pointwise surjective, so P(A)→Q(A) is surjective. Yoneda identifies this map with
[C,Set](C(A,−),P)⟶[C,Set](C(A,−),Q).
(2)
Thus every representable functor is a projective object in a category.
The colimit form of the Special adjoint functor theorem says that a colimit-preserving functor from a locally small, cocomplete, well-copowered category with a small generating family into a locally small category has a right adjoint functor. For small C, the category [C,Set] is locally small and has colimits pointwise. Quotients of P are represented by compatible equivalence relations on the sets P(C), so they form a set; hence the category is well-copowered. The set of representables {C(C,−):C∈C} generates it by the Yoneda lemma. The theorem therefore gives a right adjoint to every small-colimit-preserving functor
[C,Set]⟶[D,Set].
(3)
In particular, product with a fixed functor P is computed pointwise, and −×P(C) preserves colimits in the Category of sets. Hence −×P preserves all small colimits and has a right adjoint (−)P. Thus [C,Set] is a Cartesian closed category.
Now work in [Cop,Set] and write yA=C(−,A). If C has binary products, then
(PyA)(C)≅Nat(yC×yA,P)≅Nat(y(C×A),P)≅P(C×A).
(4)
Thus exponentiation by yA is precomposition with −×A. Precomposition between functor categories has a right adjoint given by Right Kan extension, so yA is a tiny object.
Conversely, suppose C has a terminal object 1 and F is tiny. The representable y1 is the terminal presheaf, and the exponential adjunction plus Yoneda gives
[Cop,Set](F,P)≅(PF)(1).
(5)
Since F is tiny, (−)F is a left adjoint and preserves all colimits; evaluation at 1 also preserves pointwise colimits. Therefore the hom functor [Cop,Set](F,−) preserves coproducts and epimorphisms. Preservation of epimorphisms makes F projective, while preservation of coproducts makes it indecomposable.
