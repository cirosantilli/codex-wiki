For F:C⇄D:G with F⊣G, the unit and counit of an adjunction are
η:1C​→GF,ε:FG→1D​,
(6)
obtained by transposing identity morphisms. They satisfy the triangular identities εF​Fη=1F​ and GεηG​=1G​.
If G is full and faithful, fullness gives a map u:B→FGB with G(u)=ηGB​; the second triangular identity and faithfulness show that u is inverse to εB​. Conversely, if ε is an isomorphism, then for h:GB→GB′ the unique arrow whose transpose is h is
BεB−1​​FGBFh​FGB′εB′​​B′,
(7)
which proves that G is full, and the triangular identities prove faithfulness. Hence (i) and (ii) are equivalent. Condition (ii) immediately gives (iii). Conversely, a natural isomorphism FG≅1D​ combines with the adjunction bijection to show naturally that D(B,B′)→C(GB,GB′) is bijective; by naturality and the triangular identities this is the map induced by G up to invertible natural conjugation, so G is full and faithful. Thus all three conditions are equivalent.
Suppose L⊣F⊣R. If L is full and faithful, the unit 1→FL is an isomorphism. For A,B in the codomain of F, the two adjunctions then give natural bijections
D(A,FRB)≅C(LA,RB)≅D(FLA,B)≅D(A,B).
(8)
By the Yoneda lemma, the counit FRB→B is an isomorphism, so the fully faithful adjoint criterion makes R full and faithful. The converse is dual.
Now assume F is full and faithful. For L⊣F, its counit ϵ:LF→1 is invertible; for F⊣R, its unit α:1→RF is invertible. Define
θ=(αL​)−1Rη:R⟶L.
(9)
Applying F, then using naturality and the triangular identities, reduces this composite to the same map as
R(ϵR​)−1​LFRLβ​L.
(10)
Since F is faithful, the two displayed composites are equal.
For a morphism u:FB→A, let u=RuαB​:B→RA be its transpose under F⊣R. The identities just proved give
θA​u=LuϵB−1​.
(11)
If every θA​ is monic and Lu=Lv, this equation gives u=v, hence u=v; thus L is faithful on arrows whose domains are in the image of F. Conversely, if θA​f=θA​g for f,g:B→RA, naturality of ϵ and the second formula for θ give
L(βA​Ff)=L(βA​Fg).
(12)
Both maps have domain FB, so the assumed faithfulness gives βA​Ff=βA​Fg. These are the transposes of f and g, hence f=g. Therefore θ is pointwise monic exactly under the stated faithfulness condition.
