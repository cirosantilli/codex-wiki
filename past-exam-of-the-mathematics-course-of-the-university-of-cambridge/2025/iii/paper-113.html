Take X=R, U=(0,1), and let F be the skyscraper sheaf with value a nonzero abelian group A at p=3/4. Cover V=(−1,1) by V1​=(−1,2/3) and V2​=(1/2,1). A prime Weil divisor is an integral closed subscheme of codimension one. If Y has generic point ηY​, regularity in codimension one makes the local ring OX,ηY​​ a discrete valuation ring with fraction field K(X). Its normalized discrete valuation
νY​:K(X)∗⟶Z
(12)
is the order of vanishing along Y: writing f=uπm for a unit u and uniformizer π gives νY​(f)=m. Additivity of exponents makes this a group homomorphism.

Restriction sends a Weil divisor on X to the sum of its components meeting U. Every prime divisor on U has a codimension-one closure in the Noetherian scheme X, so the induced map Cl(X)→Cl(U) is surjective.
If the class of D restricts to zero, then D∣U​=divU​(f) for some f∈K(X)∗. The divisor D−divX​(f) is supported on Z, hence is an integral combination of Z1​,…,Zn​. Conversely, every such combination restricts to zero. Therefore
Zn⟶Cl(X)⟶Cl(U)⟶0,
(13)

Suppose Pkn​ had a cover by n affine opens. Since projective space is separated, every finite intersection in this cover is affine. The acyclic cover theorem would therefore compute the cohomology of every quasi-coherent sheaf by its Čech complex. A cover with only n members has no degree-n cochains, so it would imply
Hn(Pkn​,O(−n−1))=0.
(20)
But top cohomology of projective space gives
Hn(Pkn​,O(−n−1))≅k,
(21)
a contradiction. Hence no such affine cover exists.

A Cartier divisor on an integral scheme is given by an open cover (Ui​) and rational functions fi​∈K(X)∗ such that fi​/fj​∈OX∗​(Ui​∩Uj​). Two are linearly equivalent when their quotient is represented by one global rational function. The Cartier class group is the group of Cartier divisors modulo these principal divisors.
Let KX∗​ be the sheaf of nonzero rational functions. Cartier divisors are the global sections of KX∗​/OX∗​, and the exact sequence
1⟶OX∗​⟶KX∗​⟶KX∗​/OX∗​⟶1
(23)
gives a long exact cohomology sequence. On an integral scheme KX∗​ is flasque: every nonempty restriction map is the identity on K(X)∗. Hence H1(X,KX∗​)=0, and exactness gives
CaCl(X)=Γ(X,KX∗​/OX∗​)/K(X)∗≅H1(X,OX∗​).​
(24)
