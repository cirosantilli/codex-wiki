The axioms for information entropy give the formula H(X)=−∑x​p(x)logp(x) and hence the chain rule for information entropy
H(X,Y)=H(X)+H(Y∣X).
(1)
Because conditioning reduces entropy, H(Y∣X)≤H(Y), and therefore
H(X,Y)≤H(X)+H(Y).
(2)
This is subadditivity of information entropy.
For the entropy submodularity rule, apply the chain rule for information entropy twice:
H(X,Y)+H(Y,Z)−H(Y)−H(X,Y,Z)​=H(X∣Y)−H(X∣Y,Z)=I(X;Z∣Y)≥0.​
(3)
The last quantity is conditional mutual information, whose nonnegativity again expresses that conditioning reduces entropy.

Because X,Y,Z are independent random variables, adding Y is an independent noise channel, so
X⟶X+Z⟶X+Y+Z
(7)
is a Markov chain. The data processing inequality for mutual information gives
I(X;X+Y+Z)≤I(X;X+Z).
(8)
Translation in the finite additive group preserves conditional entropy, and independence therefore gives
I(X;X+Y+Z)=H(X+Y+Z)−H(Y+Z)
(9)
and
I(X;X+Z)=H(X+Z)−H(Z).
(10)
Substitution proves the required entropy submodularity for three independent sums.

For random variables A,B in a finite additive group, take independent copies A′,B′ with the same respective distributions and define the Entropic Ruzsa distance by
dR​(A,B)=H(A′−B′)−21​H(A′)−21​H(B′).
(11)
For the independent variables in the question, expansion gives
dR​(X,Z)+dR​(Y,Z)−dR​(X,Y)=H(X−Z)+H(Y−Z)−H(X−Y)−H(Z).

Write r=∣V0​∣, s=∣V1​∣, and e=∣E(G)∣=α∣X∣∣Y∣. We construct a random graph homomorphism Ψ:T→G. Choose a root of the tree. Map it according to the degree-biased vertex distribution on the corresponding side of the bipartite graph; after mapping any vertex, map each child independently and uniformly to a neighbour of its parent's image.
Every oriented tree edge is then mapped uniformly onto the e edges of G. Every tree vertex in V0​ has the degree-biased marginal on X, of entropy HX​, and every vertex in V1​ has the analogous marginal of entropy HY​. Repeated use of the chain rule for information entropy along the rooted tree gives
H(Ψ)=(k−1)loge−∑v∈V0​​(degT​v−1)HX​−∑v∈V1​​(degT​v−1)HY​.
(15)
Since a k-vertex tree has k−1 edges,
∑v∈V0​​(degT​v−1)=s−1,∑v∈V1​​(degT​v−1)=r−1.
(16)
The maximum entropy distribution on a finite set gives HX​≤log∣X∣ and HY​≤log∣Y∣. Hence
H(Ψ)​≥(k−1)log(α∣X∣∣Y∣)−(s−1)log∣X∣−(r−1)log∣Y∣=log(αk−1∣X∣r∣Y∣s).​
(17)
If N is the number of bipartition-respecting graph homomorphisms T→G, the support of Ψ has size N, so the maximum entropy distribution on a finite set also gives H(Ψ)≤logN. Thus
N≥αk−1∣X∣r∣Y∣s.
(18)
There are ∣X∣r∣Y∣s bipartition-respecting maps in total, so a uniformly chosen one is a graph homomorphism with probability at least αk−1. This proves the Sidorenko inequality for trees.

Let

where φ is the golden ratio. If the union-closed family consists of one nonempty set, any element of that set has frequency one, so assume its cardinality exceeds one. Choose independent uniform members A,B and let X,Y∈{0,1}n be their characteristic vectors of sets. Then H(X)=H(Y)=log∣A∣>0.
Suppose for a contradiction that every element has frequency pi​<c. Put qi​=1−pi​>1/φ, and let Z=XORY, the characteristic vector of a set of A∪B. The chain rule for information entropy and conditioning reduces entropy give
H(Z)=∑i​H(Zi​∣Z<i​)≥∑i​H(Zi​∣X<i​,Y<i​),

Let Q be the set of valid quintuples and choose (A1​,…,A5​) uniformly from Q. Then
H(A1​,…,A5​)=log∣Q∣.
(24)
Apply Shearer's inequality to the ten pairs {i,j}⊆[5]. Every index occurs in four pairs, so
4H(A1​,…,A5​)≤∑1≤i<j≤5​H(Ai​,Aj​).
(25)
Put Uij​=Ai​∪Aj​. Since Uij​∈A, it has cardinality k. For each element of Uij​, its membership bits in (Ai​,Aj​) are one of (1,0),(0,1),(1,1); outside Uij​ they are forced to be (0,0). Thus at most 3k ordered pairs have any prescribed union. The chain rule for information entropy, conditional entropy, and the support bound for information entropy give
H(Ai​,Aj​)≤H(Uij​)+H(Ai​,Aj​∣Uij​)≤log∣A∣+klog3,
(26)
because every Uij​ belongs to A. There are ten pairs, hence
log∣Q∣≤410​(log∣A∣+klog3).
(27)
Exponentiating proves
∣Q∣≤35k/2∣A∣5/2,
(28)
which is the five-variable case of the entropy bound for pairwise-union tuples.

The Entropic Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem states that for finitely supported random variables A,B in an abelian group,
dR​(A;B∥A+B)≤3I(A;B)+2H(A+B)−H(A)−H(B),
(29)
where the left-hand side is the Simultaneous conditional entropic Ruzsa distance.
Put S=A+B and take two copies (A1​,B1​) and (A2​,B2​) that are conditionally independent given S. Thus both sums equal S, and, given S, A1​ and B2​ are independent with the required conditional marginals. By conditioning reduces entropy,
dR​(A;B∥S)≤H(A1​−B2​)−21​H(A1​∣S)−21​H(B2​∣S).
(30)
The two conditional entropies are equal, since either A or B together with S determines the other, and the chain rule for information entropy gives
H(A∣S)=H(B∣S)=H(A)+H(B)−I(A;B)−H(S).
(31)
Set D=A1​−B2​. By entropy submodularity,
H(D)≤H(D,A1​)+H(D,B1​)−H(D,A1​,B1​).
(32)
The first term is H(A1​,B2​)≤H(A)+H(B) by subadditivity of information entropy. Since A1​+B1​=A2​+B2​, we also have D=A2​−B1​, so the second term is at most H(A)+H(B). Finally (D,A1​,B1​) determines all four copied variables, and conditional independence gives
H(D,A1​,B1​)​=H(A1​,B1​,A2​,B2​)=H(S)+2H(A,B∣S)=2H(A,B)−H(S).​
(33)
As H(A,B)=H(A)+H(B)−I(A;B), these estimates imply
H(D)≤H(S)+2I(A;B).
(34)
Subtracting the common conditional-entropy term proves the stated Entropic Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem.

Write δ=dR​(X,Y), A=U1​+U2​, B=V1​+V2​, and S=A+B. Distances depend only on distributions, so all variables used in any one application may be realized as independent random variables.
First, the entropy submodularity for three independent sums implies
dR​(U1​+U2​,X)≤21​(2dR​(U,X)+dR​(U,U)),
(35)
and analogously for V. The Entropic Ruzsa triangle inequality gives dR​(U,U)≤2dR​(U,X) and dR​(V,V)≤2dR​(V,Y). Consequently the relevance of independent self-sums gives
p+q:=dR​(A,X)+dR​(B,Y)≤2Cδ.
(36)
Apply the Conditioned entropic Ruzsa distance of a summand first to (A,B,X) and then to (B,A,Y):
dR​(A∣S;X)dR​(B∣S;Y)​≤21​(dR​(A,X)+dR​(B,X)+dR​(A,B)),≤21​(dR​(B,Y)+dR​(A,Y)+dR​(A,B)).​
(37)
Three applications of the Entropic Ruzsa triangle inequality give
dR​(B,X)≤q+δ,dR​(A,Y)≤p+δ,dR​(A,B)≤p+δ+q.
(38)
Adding all these bounds yields
dR​(A∣S;X)+dR​(B∣S;Y)​≤2(p+q+δ)≤(4C+2)δ.​
(39)
Thus the required absolute constants may be taken as a=4 and b=2.
