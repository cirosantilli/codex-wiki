a OurBigBook.com
For a compact convex set K⊂Rn, let
HK​(η)=supx∈K​x⋅η.
(1)
The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem says that if u is a compactly supported distribution with support in K, its Fourier--Laplace transform
u(z)=⟨u(x),e−ix⋅z⟩
(2)
is entire and, for some C,N,
∣u(z)∣≤C(1+∣z∣)NeHK​(Imz).
(3)
Conversely, every entire function satisfying such an estimate is the transform of a distribution supported in K.
For the forward direction, compact support lets u act on the exponential after insertion of a cutoff equal to one near K. Differentiation in z may be passed under the pairing, proving entire analyticity. The finite-order estimate for u bounds derivatives of the exponential on K by a polynomial in ∣z∣ times eHK​(Imz).
Conversely, restrict the entire function F to Rn. Its polynomial growth defines a tempered distribution u by inverse Fourier transform. If a test function is supported outside K, separate its compact support from K by a real vector η. Shifting the Fourier inversion contour from Rn to Rn+itη is allowed by entire analyticity. The exponential gained from the test function beats the bound etHK​(η) as t→∞, so the pairing vanishes. Hence suppu⊂K, completing the converse.

If u∈E′(R) solves P(D)u=v, Fourier transformation gives
P(z)u(z)=v(z).
(4)
The Paley–Wiener–Schwartz theorem makes u entire, so v/P is entire.
Conversely, suppose F(z)=v(z)/P(z) is entire. Polynomial division estimates away from the finitely many zeros of P, together with the maximum principle on fixed disks around those zeros, show that F retains a Paley--Wiener--Schwartz bound, with only the polynomial exponent changed. The converse theorem therefore gives u∈E′(R) with u=F. Then P(D)u=v. Thus
∃u∈E′:P(D)u=v⟺v/P is entire​.
(5)

c OurBigBook.com
The derivative hypothesis implies that 1/Q(ξ) is a symbol of order −δN at high frequency. Choose cutoffs χ≺ψ in X and a high-frequency cutoff in ξ. The corresponding Fourier multiplier is a parametrix for Q(D), and the symbol calculus, together with the product formula from part (b), gives the localized estimate
∥χu∥Hs+δN​≤C(∥ψQ(D)u∥Hs​+∥ψu∥Ht​)
(11)
for some sufficiently negative t. The commutator terms contain derivatives Q(α); the assumed factor ∣ξ∣−δ∣α∣ lowers their order and lets them be absorbed inductively. Therefore
Q(D)u∈Hlocs​(X)⟹u∈Hlocs+δN​(X)​.
(12)
If Q(D)u is smooth, it belongs locally to Hs for every s. Starting from the fact that every compactly supported distribution has some negative Sobolev order and repeatedly applying the gain δN>0 places u in every local Sobolev space. The Sobolev embedding theorem then gives u∈C∞(X). Thus Q(D) is a hypoelliptic differential operator.

A phase function is a real smooth function Φ on X×(Rk∖{0}), positively homogeneous of degree one in θ, with dx,θ​Φ=0. The symbol class
Sym(X,Rk;N)
(14)
consists of smooth amplitudes satisfying, on each compact K⊂X,
∣Dxα​Dθβ​a(x,θ)∣≤CK,α,β​⟨θ⟩N−∣β∣.
(15)
To define the oscillatory integral, insert a cutoff χ(εθ) equal to one near zero and set
⟨IΦ​(a),f⟩=limε↓0​∫X​∫Rk​eiΦ(x,θ)a(x,θ)f(x)χ(εθ)dθdx.
(16)
Repeated integration by parts with an operator L satisfying LeiΦ=eiΦ makes the integral absolutely convergent after enough iterations and shows that the limit defines a distribution.
