The Freiman-Ruzsa theorem over a finite field states that if A⊆Fpn​ and ∣A+A∣≤K∣A∣, then A is contained in a vector subspace H with
∣H∣≤K2pK4∣A∣.
(1)
After translating A, assume 0∈A. Put S=A−A, and choose X⊆A+S maximal subject to the translates x+A, x∈X, being pairwise disjoint. Since X+A⊆2A+S=3A−A, the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality gives
∣X∣∣A∣=∣X+A∣≤∣3A−A∣≤K4∣A∣,
(2)
so ∣X∣≤K4.
Maximality gives A+S⊆X+S: if y∈A+S is not already in X, then (y+A)∩(x+A)=∅ for some x∈X, whence y∈x+A−A=x+S. Inductively, mA+S⊆⟨X⟩+S for every positive integer m. Because 0∈A, every element of ⟨A⟩ belongs to some mA in the finite vector space, and therefore
H:=⟨A⟩⊆⟨X⟩+S.
(3)
Finally, ∣⟨X⟩∣≤p∣X∣ and ∣S∣≤K2∣A∣ by the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality, so
∣H∣≤p∣X∣∣S∣≤K2pK4∣A∣.​
(4)

Let H≤Fpn​ be a subspace with ∣H∣ much larger than K2, and choose linearly independent vectors e1​,…,eK−1​ whose images are independent modulo H. Set
A=H∪{e1​,…,eK−1​}.
(5)
Then ∣A∣=∣H∣+K−1∼∣H∣, while A+A is the union of H, the K−1 disjoint cosets ei​+H, and at most K2 exceptional sums ei​+ej​. Hence
∣A+A∣∼K∣A∣.
(6)
Every subspace containing A must contain H and all ei​, so it has at least pK−1∣H∣∼pK−1∣A∣ elements. Thus the exponential dependence on K in the Freiman-Ruzsa theorem over a finite field cannot in general be replaced by a subexponential one.

The hypothesis says that the normalized additive energy of A is at least η. By the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem, for an absolute C0​ there is A′⊆A such that
∣A′∣≥ηC0​∣A∣,∣A′+A′∣≤η−C0​∣A′∣.
(7)
The finite-field Bogolyubov-Ruzsa consequence of the Freiman-Ruzsa theorem over a finite field says that a set of doubling at most K has a vector subspace
V⊆A′+A′−A′−A′
(8)
with ∣V∣≥p−KC1​∣A′∣ for an absolute C1​. Taking K=η−C0​ and enlarging the absolute exponent gives
V⊆A+A−A−A,∣V∣≥p−η−C∣A∣.
(9)
This is an energy form of the Bogolyubov lemma: the usual lemma assumes positive density in an ambient group, whereas the Balog-Szemerédi-Gowers theorem first extracts a dense structured model from the many additive quadruples. The resulting bound depends on the energy parameter rather than on the possibly tiny ambient density of A.

One standard normalized form of the Croot-Sisask almost-periodicity theorem is this. Let A,S be finite subsets of an abelian group with ∣A+S∣≤K∣A∣, let q≥2, let 0<ϵ<1, and let f be a complex function. There is T⊆S with
∣T∣≥(2K)−O(q/ϵ2)∣S∣
(23)
such that every t∈T−T satisfies
∥τt​(1A​∗f)−1A​∗f∥Lq​≤ϵ∥1A​∥L1​∥f∥Lq​.
(24)
For the proof, sample k=O(q/ϵ2) independent points of A and approximate 1A​∗f by the empirical average of the corresponding translates of f. A moment inequality bounds the expected Lq error, so many samples are good. The small size of A+S lets a translation and pigeonhole argument find many shifts in S producing the same good approximation. Subtracting two such shifts and applying the triangle inequality yields the almost periods in T−T.
