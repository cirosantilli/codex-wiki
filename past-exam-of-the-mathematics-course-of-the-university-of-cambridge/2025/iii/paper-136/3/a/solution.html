One useful form of Hensel lemma is this: if R is a complete discrete valuation ring, f∈R[X], and
f(a1​)≡0(modπ),f′(a1​)≡0(modπ),
(19)
then there is a unique a∈R such that f(a)=0 and a≡a1​(modπ). Indeed, after constructing ar​ with f(ar​)≡0(modπr), choose the unique t modulo π for which
f(ar​)+πrtf′(ar​)≡0(modπr+1)
(20)
and put ar+1​=ar​+πrt. The resulting Cauchy sequence converges by completeness, and the same first-order congruence proves uniqueness.
Apply this to Xp−1−1. Every nonzero class in Fp​ is a simple root, so it has a unique Teichmuller representative in Zp​. These give all roots of unity of order prime to p. For odd p, the group 1+pZp​ has no nontrivial torsion: if u=1, then the binomial theorem gives vp​(up−1)=vp​(u−1)+1, which is incompatible with finite p-power order. Hence
μ(Qp​)≅Cp−1​qquad(p odd).
(21)
For p=2, the subgroup 1+4Z2​ is torsion-free by the same argument, while −1 supplies the extra torsion element. Thus μ(Q2​)={±1}≅C2​. This describes the roots of unity in a p-adic field for K=Qp​.
