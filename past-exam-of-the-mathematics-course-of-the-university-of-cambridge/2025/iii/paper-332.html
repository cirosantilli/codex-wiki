Put ice in x<a(t) and ocean in x>a(t), with x increasing into the ocean. This is a saline Stefan problem. The ice and liquid temperatures satisfy

The last two equations are the Stefan condition and solute conservation. Salt is taken to be absent from the ice. The field sketch has two broad thermal boundary layers of width O(κt​) and a liquid-side salinity layer of width O(Dt​).
The diffusion equation is invariant under (x,t)↦(cx,c2t), so a solution with constant far-field data and no fixed length has a/Dt​ constant. Write

(12)
Since F has the sign of λ, ice grows when Tm​−T−∞​>2mC0​, is stationary at equality, and ablates when Tm​−T−∞​<2mC0​.
During growth, salt rejection gives Ci​>C0​. Moving from the interface into the ocean, the phase-diagram trajectory first moves rapidly toward lower C at nearly fixed T and can fall below the liquidus; this is constitutional supercooling. During ablation, Ci​<C0​, so the near-interface trajectory moves toward larger C and into the stable liquid region above the liquidus. Ablation occurs because the heat conducted from the warmer ocean to the interface exceeds the heat that the colder ice can remove; melting and salt diffusion then maintain local liquidus equilibrium.

Because the wavelength is much smaller than the shelf thickness, the ice-shelf corrugation relaxation may be treated as Stokes flow in the half-space z>0, with the mean ice-ocean boundary at z=0. Let
η=η​eikx+σt,u=∇×(ψy​),
(37)
so u=−ψz​ and w=ψx​. Taking the curl of the Stokes equation gives the Biharmonic stream function for planar Stokes flow equation ∇4ψ=0. Decay as z→∞ removes the growing modes, leaving
ψ=(A+Bz)e−kzeikx+σt.​
(38)
Thus
u=(kA−B+kBz)e−kzeikx+σt,w=ik(A+Bz)e−kzeikx+σt,
(39)
and the perturbation pressure obtained from the Stokes equation is
p′=2iμkBe−kzeikx+σt.​
(40)
The linearized zero-shear stress condition at z=0 is
μ(uz​+wx​)=2μk(B−kA)eikx+σt=0,
(41)
so B=kA. The water is hydrostatic, and displacement of the density interface gives the normal-stress condition
−p′+2μwz​=(ρw​−ρ)gη.
(42)
After B=kA, one has wz​(0)=0, so
−2iμk2A=(ρw​−ρ)gη​.
(43)
The kinematic boundary condition ηt​=w(0) gives ση​=ikA. Eliminating A yields
σ=−2μk(ρw​−ρ)g​<0.​
(44)
Hence a corrugation of wavelength λ=2π/k decays on the timescale
τ(λ)=∣σ∣1​=(ρw​−ρ)gλ4πμ​.​
(45)
Hydrostatic buoyancy supplies the restoring stress, while viscous deformation over depth O(k−1) supplies the resistance. Shorter wavelengths deform a shallower but more strongly sheared layer and therefore have a longer decay time in this gravity-only model. As ice is advected away from the grounding line, long corrugations should disappear first, leaving progressively shorter-wavelength structure farther downstream.
