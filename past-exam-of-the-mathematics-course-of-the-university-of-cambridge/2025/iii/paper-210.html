Writing Pn​g=n−1∑i=1n​g(Xi​) and Pg=Eg(X), the class G satisfies a uniform law of large numbers when
supg∈G​∣Pn​g−Pg∣⟶0
(1)
in probability; a strong ULLN uses almost-sure convergence.
Fix ε>0 and choose finitely many brackets [gjL​,gjU​] of L1(P) width at most ε. The weak law of large numbers, simultaneously for their finitely many endpoints, gives
maxj​{∣Pn​gjL​−PgjL​∣,∣Pn​gjU​−PgjU​∣}→0.
(2)
If gjL​≤g≤gjU​, bracketing both Pn​g and Pg shows
∣Pn​g−Pg∣≤max{∣Pn​gjL​−PgjL​∣,∣Pn​gjU​−PgjU​∣}+P(gjU​−gjL​).
(3)
Taking the supremum gives a limit superior at most ε. Since ε is arbitrary, the ULLN follows.

The Glivenko-Cantelli theorem states that for the empirical distribution function Fn​ of iid real observations with distribution function F,
supt∈R​∣Fn​(t)−F(t)∣⟶0
(4)
almost surely.
Apply part (a) to G={1(−∞,t]​:t∈R}. For each ε>0, choose finitely many quantile cutpoints so that the P-mass between consecutive cutpoints is at most ε, treating atoms as cutpoints themselves. Indicators at adjacent cutpoints give finite L1(P) brackets of width at most ε. Using the strong law for the finite bracket endpoints and then intersecting the probability-one events for ε=1/m gives the almost-sure conclusion.
