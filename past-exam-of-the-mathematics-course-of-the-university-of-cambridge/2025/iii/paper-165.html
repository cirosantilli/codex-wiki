The simplex category Δ has objects [n]={0<1<⋯<n} for n≥0 and order-preserving maps as morphisms. A simplicial set is a functor X:Δop→Set.
The standard simplex is the representable simplicial set
Δmn​=HomΔ​([m],[n]).
(1)
For 0≤i≤n, the simplicial horn Λin​⊆Δn is the union of the images of all coface maps Δn−1→Δn except the face opposite vertex i.

Consider a lifting square with Λ13​↪Δ3 on the left, Q→N(C) on the right, and prescribed bottom simplex σ:Δ3→N(C). Since Q is a quasicategory, the top horn has some filler σ:Δ3→Q.
The two simplices pσ and σ of the nerve of a category restrict to the same inner horn. Inner horns in a category's nerve have unique fillers, because composition in a category is defined and unique. Hence pσ=σ, so σ is the required lift.

The contravariant simplicial mapping space functor takes the given pushout to the stated strict pullback. Since X is a Kan complex, all four mapping spaces are Kan complexes. Moreover, the monomorphism A↪B induces a Kan fibration
Hom​(B,X)⟶Hom​(A,X).
(5)
Indeed, a lifting problem against a horn is adjoint to a lifting problem for X against the pushout-product of A↪B with that horn inclusion; this pushout-product is an anodyne monomorphism, and X fills it.
A strict pullback of fibrant simplicial sets along a fibration computes the homotopy pullback. The displayed pullback square is therefore also a homotopy pullback square.

The Dold–Kan correspondence says that
N∗​:sAb⟶Ch≥0​(Ab)

Under the Dold–Kan correspondence, a 1-simplex of K(C∗​) is a pair (a,x)∈C0​⊕C1​ with
d1​(a,x)=a,d0​(a,x)=a+∂1​x.

(a,x,y)∈C0​×C1​×C1​,

Let A[n] be the nonnegative chain complex having A in degree n, zero in every other degree, and zero differential. The simplicial Eilenberg–MacLane space is
K(A,n):=K(A[n]).
(11)
Its underlying simplicial set is a Kan complex, with πn​K(A,n)≅A and all other positive homotopy groups zero.

Regard the given short exact sequence as a degreewise short exact sequence of chain complexes concentrated in degree n. The inverse functor in the Dold–Kan correspondence is exact, so it produces a degreewise short exact sequence of simplicial abelian groups
0⟶K(A1​,n)⟶K(A2​,n)⟶K(A3​,n)⟶0.
(12)
The last map is degreewise surjective and hence a Kan fibration. Its strict fiber is K(A1​,n), and a strict fiber of a fibration computes the homotopy fiber. This proves the asserted homotopy fiber sequence of pointed Kan complexes.

Write F=K(Z/4,1), E=K(Z,2), and B=K(Z,2). In total degree at most two, the E2​ page of the mod-two Serre spectral sequence has
E20,0​=E20,1​=E20,2​=E22,0​=F2​,
(28)
and E21,0​=E21,1​=0. A periodic free resolution of the cyclic group gives H1​(F;Z)=Z/4 and H2​(F;Z)=0. Hence H1(F;F2​)≅Hom(Z/4,F2​), while the universal coefficient theorem for cohomology gives H2(F;F2​)≅Ext(Z/4,F2​); both are one-dimensional.
The edge map H2(B;F2​)→H2(E;F2​) is induced by multiplication by 4 and is therefore zero modulo two. Consequently
d2​:E20,1​⟶E22,0​
(29)
is an isomorphism. The differential out of E20,2​ is zero, because the total space has a one-dimensional H2 which must survive in filtration zero. Thus for every r≥3 and p+q≤2,
Er0,0​=Er0,2​=F2​
(30)
and all other groups in that range vanish.
It follows that
H0(F;F2​)=H1(F;F2​)=H2(F;F2​)=F2​.
(31)
The surviving filtration-zero class is the restriction of the degree-two class of E, so
imH2(f;F2​)=H2(F;F2​).​
(32)

The group H1(K(Z/4,1);F2​) is one-dimensional, so it suffices to consider its nonzero class x. This class is represented by the quotient homomorphism Z/4→Z/2, which lifts to the identity homomorphism with coefficients in Z/4. Its Bockstein homomorphism for
0⟶Z/2⟶Z/4⟶Z/2⟶0
(37)
therefore vanishes. Since the first Steenrod square is this Bockstein and Sq1x=x2 for every degree-one class,
x2=0.
(38)
The zero degree-one class plainly has square zero as well.
