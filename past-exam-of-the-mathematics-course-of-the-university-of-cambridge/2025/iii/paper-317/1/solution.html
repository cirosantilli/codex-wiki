For a homologous star, hydrostatic equilibrium, mass conservation, and the ideal gas equation give the central scalings
ρc​∝R3M​,Pc​∝R4GM2​,Tc​∝RμM​,
(1)
where μ is the mean molecular weight. Integrating the nuclear energy-generation law over a fixed homologous profile gives
Lnuc​∝Xρc​Tc13​M∝Xμ13R16M15​.
(2)
Radiative stellar structure gives independently
Lrad​∝κρc​RTc4​​∝κμ4M3​.
(3)
Equating the two luminosities yields
R16∝κXμ9M12.
(4)
At fixed zero-age composition, the stars are therefore homologous with
R∝M3/4​,L∝M3​.
(5)
The effective temperature satisfies L=4πR2σTe4​, so Te​∝M3/8. The zero-age main sequence consequently has
dlogTe​dlogL​=8​.
(6)
It is a steep line rising toward high luminosity and high temperature on a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram.
For fully ionized hydrogen and helium with Y=1−X,
μ1​=2X+43​Y=43+5X​,
(7)
while κ∝1+X. At fixed mass,
L∝(1+X)−1(3+5X)−4​,
(8)
and the corresponding radius relation is
R∝[X(1+X)]1/16(3+5X)−9/16​.
(9)
At the pure-hydrogen zero-age point X=1,
dXdlogL​=−1+X1​−3+5X20​=−3,
(10)
whereas
dXdlogR​=161​(X1​+1+X1​−3+5X45​)=−12833​.
(11)
Hence
dXdlogTe​​=41​(dXdlogL​−2dXdlogR​)=−256159​,
(12)
and
dlogTe​dlogL​=53256​≃4.83​.
(13)
As hydrogen is consumed, X falls, so both L and Te​ rise. In the usual diagram with temperature increasing leftward, the evolutionary track initially moves upward and leftward from the zero-age main sequence.
