A character of an algebra is a nonzero multiplicative complex-linear functional φ:A→C, and the character space of an algebra is the set ΦA​ of all such characters. Since A is unital, φ(1)=1. Moreover φ(a)∈σA​(a): otherwise a−φ(a)1 would be invertible, while applying φ to its inverse identity would give 0=1. The spectral radius estimate therefore yields
∣φ(a)∣≤r(a)≤∥a∥.
(1)
Thus every character is continuous and has norm one.
Let M be a maximal ideal. Its norm closure is again an ideal. It cannot equal A, because then some m∈M would satisfy ∥1−m∥<1, making m invertible by the Neumann series and forcing M=A. Hence M is closed. The quotient A/M is a complex unital Banach division algebra, so the Gelfand-Mazur theorem identifies it with C. Composing the quotient map with this isomorphism gives a character with kernel M. Conversely, a character kernel is maximal because its quotient is C.
Now λ∈σA​(x) exactly when x−λ1 is not invertible, equivalently when it lies in some maximal ideal. The preceding result turns that ideal into kerφ, giving φ(x)=λ. The reverse implication follows from the first paragraph, so
σA​(x)={φ(x):φ∈ΦA​}.
(2)
The Gelfand topology is the weak-star topology on ΦA​⊆A∗. The Gelfand transform is
x⟼x,x(φ)=φ(x).
(3)
Its values are continuous by the definition of the topology, and multiplicativity and linearity of characters show that it is a unital algebra homomorphism. Finally
∥x∥∞​=supφ∈ΦA​​∣φ(x)∣≤∥x∥,
(4)
so it is continuous.

For a closed unital subalgebra A⊆B containing x, invertibility in A implies invertibility in B, so σB​(x)⊆σA​(x). On a connected component of the resolvent set of x in B, the set of λ for which (x−λ1)−1∈A is both open, by a local Neumann series, and closed, by closedness of A. It contains all sufficiently large λ, hence the entire unbounded component. Thus spectrum in a closed unital subalgebra says that σA​(x) is σB​(x) with some bounded complementary components filled in.
Now let A be the Banach subalgebra generated by one element x and put K=σA​(x). If D were a bounded component of C∖K, choose λ∈D. Since (x−λ1)−1∈A, polynomials pn​(x) converge to it. The polynomials
qn​(z)=(z−λ)pn​(z)
(10)
satisfy qn​(λ)=0 and qn​(x)→1. Applying the contractive Gelfand transform gives qn​→1 uniformly on K, hence on ∂D. But the maximum modulus principle applied to 1−qn​ gives
1=∣1−qn​(λ)∣≤supz∈∂D​∣1−qn​(z)∣⟶0,
(11)
a contradiction. Therefore C∖K is connected.
The map
ΦA​⟶K,φ⟼φ(x)
(12)
is continuous and surjective by part a. It is injective because characters agreeing on x agree on every polynomial in x, hence by continuity on their norm closure A. The character space is compact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem, while K is Hausdorff, so this continuous bijection is a homeomorphism.
Under this identification, the Gelfand transform θ:A→C(K) obeys
θ(f(x))(z)=f(z)
(13)
by the calculation in part b. Since f(x)∈A, choose polynomials pn​ with pn​(x)→f(x). Contractivity of θ gives
supz∈K​∣pn​(z)−f(z)∣≤∥pn​(x)−f(x)∥⟶0.
(14)
Thus every function holomorphic near K is uniformly approximable there by polynomials.

For a real locally convex space (X,P), the continuous dual space X∗ consists of all continuous real-linear maps X→R. If g∈Y∗ on a subspace Y, continuity gives seminorms p1​,…,pk​∈P and C>0 such that
∣g(y)∣≤Cmaxi​pi​(y)(y∈Y).
(47)
The right side is a continuous sublinear functional on X. The dominated Hahn-Banach theorem extends g to a linear f:X→R satisfying the same bound, so f∈X∗.
If Y is closed and x0​∈/Y, the Hausdorff locally convex quotient X/Y has a continuous seminorm p with p(x0​+Y)>0. On Y+Rx0​, define g(y+tx0​)=t and rescale p so that ∣g∣≤p. Hahn--Banach extends it to f∈X∗ with f∣Y​=0 and f(x0​)=1.
The separation of a point and an open convex set says that if C⊆X is nonempty, open, and convex and x0​∈/C, there is f∈X∗ such that
f(c)<f(x0​)(c∈C).
(48)

If K is closed and convex and x0​∈/K, a Hahn-Banach separation theorem gives f∈X∗ and a real number separating x0​ from K. The corresponding inverse image of an open interval is a weak neighbourhood of x0​ disjoint from K, so K is closed in σ(X,X∗).
The unit sphere SX​ of a normed space is norm closed. If X is infinite-dimensional, every basic weak neighbourhood of an interior point x∈BX​ constrains only finitely many functionals f1​,…,fn​. Their common kernel contains a nonzero y. Continuity of t↦∥x+ty∥, together with its value below one at t=0 and divergence as ∣t∣→∞, supplies t with ∥x+ty∥=1. Since all fi​ have the same values at x+ty and x, every weak neighbourhood of x meets SX​. Points outside BX​ are separated from it by Hahn--Banach, so the weak closure of the unit sphere is exactly BX​. In particular, SX​ is not weakly closed.
