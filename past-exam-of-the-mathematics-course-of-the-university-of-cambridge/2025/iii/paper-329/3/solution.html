In the cylinder frame the wall moves at velocity −U. The parabolic lubrication gap and its natural stretched coordinate are
h(x)=2ϵa​+2ax2​=2ϵa​(1+ξ2),ξ=aϵ​x​.
(37)
The wall values are u(0)=−U and u(h)=Ωa to leading order. The Couette-Poiseuille flow in a thin gap is therefore
u(x,y)=−U+hU+Ωa​y+2μpx​​y(y−h),
(38)
and its constant flux is
q=2h​(Ωa−U)−12μh3​px​.
(39)
The pressure recovery condition in lubrication flow gives ∫−∞∞​px​dx=0. Since
∫h−3dx∫h−2dx​=32ϵa​,
(40)
it follows that
q=31​ϵa(Ωa−U),​px​=h26μ(Ωa−U)​−h312μq​.
(41)
Differentiating the velocity profile gives the two surface shear stresses
μσxy​​​y=0​=h4U−2Ωa​+h26q​,μσxy​​​y=h​=h−2U+4Ωa​−h26q​.​
(42)
Using ∫h−1dx=2π/ϵ​ and ∫h−2dx=2π/(aϵ3/2), the shear force exerted by the fluid on the wall is
Fwall(s)​=ϵ​4πμU​,​
(43)
and that exerted by the fluid on the cylinder is
Fcyl(s)​=−ϵ​4πμΩa​.​
(44)
They are not equal and opposite because pressure acting on the sloping cylinder surface also transfers tangential momentum. Indeed, integration by parts gives the cylinder's pressure force
Fcyl(p)​=∫hpx​dx=ϵ​4πμ(Ωa−U)​,
(45)
so its total leading hydrodynamic force is −4πμU/ϵ​.
The cylinder's excess weight per unit axial length is πa2Δρg in the falling direction, and it has no gravitational couple about its axis. Force and couple balance therefore give
U=4μa2Δρg​ϵ​,​Ω=0at leading order.​
(46)
The second result follows because the leading viscous couple is −4πμΩa2/ϵ​.
For Ω=0, write h=(ϵa/2)(1+ξ2). Then
px​=h22μU​1+ξ21−3ξ2​,σxy​(x,h)=h2μU​1+ξ21−ξ2​.
(47)
Thus p is an odd pressure disturbance that vanishes at x=0 and at both infinities; its extrema occur at ξ=±1/3​. The cylinder shear is positive near the narrowest point, negative in the outer parts of the gap, and vanishes at
x=±aϵ​.​
(48)
The streamlines pass through the gap in the wall's direction overall. Pressure-driven backflow bends the interior streamlines and creates the two shear-reversal locations on the cylinder; the streamline sketch is symmetric under a half-turn combined with reversal of the flow direction.
For the final Couette flow, the lower and upper minimum gaps are ϵa(1+λ)/2 and ϵa(1−λ)/2. If the cylinder translates at speed V, the two leading lubrication drags are proportional to
−1+λ​U+V​and1−λ​U−V​.
(49)
The force-free condition gives
V=U1+λ​+1−λ​1+λ​−1−λ​​=1+1−λ2​λU​.​
(50)
For λ=0, V=0 and the streamlines in the two equal gaps are mirror images with opposite directions. The O(μUa) subleading wall-driven couple must balance the leading rotational resistance O(μΩa2/ϵ​), so
Ω=O(ϵ1/2U/a).​
(51)
