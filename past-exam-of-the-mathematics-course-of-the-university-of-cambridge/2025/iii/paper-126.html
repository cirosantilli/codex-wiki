A useful form of the Mumford rigidity lemma says that if X is complete and connected and h:X×Y→Z is constant on one fiber X×{y0​}, then under the pointed separated hypotheses it factors through Y.
For f:X→G with f(eX​)=eG​, define
h(x,y)=f(x+y)f(y)−1.
(16)
At x=eX​ this is constantly eG​. Applying rigidity with the complete factor X in the y variable shows that h(x,y) is independent of y. At y=eX​ its value is f(x), so
f(x+y)=f(x)f(y).
(17)
Thus f is a homomorphism of group varieties.
Completeness is essential. On the additive group variety Ga​ over a field of characteristic different from two, the morphism f(t)=t2 fixes zero but is not additive.

The map
τ(L1​,L2​)=pr1∗​L1​⊗pr2∗​L2​
(26)
is a homomorphism. Pullback along i1​(x)=(x,e2​) recovers L1​, up to tensoring with a fixed one-dimensional vector space, which is a trivial line bundle; similarly i2∗​ recovers L2​. Thus τ is injective.
It need not be surjective. For an elliptic curve E, the line bundle OE×E​(Δ) of the diagonal restricts to E×{y} as OE​(y), whose class varies with y. A line bundle pulled back separately from the two factors has constant class on these fibers. Hence O(Δ) is not in the image of τ.
