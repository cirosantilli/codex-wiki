a OurBigBook.com
Let ∣O⟩ be the state associated with a scalar conformal primary operator of scaling dimension Δ. In radial quantization,
Ka​∣O⟩=0,Mab​∣O⟩=0,D∣O⟩=iΔ∣O⟩,
(1)
and Pa†​=−Ka​. The norm of a level-one conformal descendant is
⟨O∣(−Ka​)Pb​∣O⟩=−⟨O∣[Ka​,Pb​]∣O⟩=2Δδab​.
(2)
Unitarity first gives Δ≥0. If Δ=0, every Pa​∣O⟩ is null, so the local operator is translation invariant and belongs to the identity conformal family. Excluding the identity therefore gives Δ>0.
Now consider the scalar level-two descendant P2∣O⟩. The conformal algebra and the scalar-primary conditions give
[Ka​,P2]∣O⟩=−2(2Δ−d+2)Pa​∣O⟩.
(3)
Applying the second Ka​ and summing over a yields
∥P2∣O⟩∥2=⟨O∣K2P2∣O⟩=8dΔ(Δ−2d−2​)⟨O∣O⟩.
(4)
Positivity of this norm, together with Δ>0, proves the scalar conformal unitarity bound
Δ≥2d−2​​.
(5)
At equality the level-two descendant is null; in position space this is the free scalar equation of motion.

For the Poincaré metric on AdS6​,
∣g∣​=z−6,gab=z2ηab.
(6)
The massless Klein-Gordon equation, evaluated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator, is
∇2ϕ=z6∂z​(z−4∂z​ϕ)+z2□5​ϕ=0,
(7)
or
z2ϕzz​−4zϕz​+z2□5​ϕ=0​.
(8)
A boundary power ϕ∼zα obeys the indicial equation
α(α−5)=0.
(9)
Thus
ϕ(z,x)=J(x)+z5A(x)+⋯.
(10)
The constant branch is the nonnormalizable source and the z5 branch is the normalizable response. Since m2=Δ(Δ−d)=0 with d=5, the roots are Δ−​=0 and Δ+​=5. Only standard quantization is unitary here: the putative alternative operator of dimension zero would be the identity, whereas the field is a nontrivial fluctuating operator. The permissible conformally invariant source-free boundary condition is therefore Dirichlet,
J=0,ϕ=O(z5)​.
(11)
More generally one may prescribe J as an external source. The holographic dictionary gives
[O]=5,[J]=d−Δ=0​.
(12)

The state–operator correspondence maps O to the lowest one-particle state on S4×R. On a sphere of radius r, cylinder energy equals scaling dimension divided by r. The primary has Δ=5.
A translation generator raises the dimension by one and transforms as a vector of SO(5). States with no angular momentum arise from scalar descendant pairs P2, so the allowed descendants are
(P2)nO,n=0,1,2,….
(20)
Their dimensions are 5+2n, and hence the one-quantum, zero-angular-momentum energies are
En​=r5+2n​,n=0,1,2,…​.
(21)
These are the l=0 normal-mode energies of a massless scalar in global Anti-de Sitter spacetime.

The Ryu–Takayanagi formula gives the leading entropy of a static boundary region:
S(D1​)=4GArea(γD1​​)​.
(22)
On the t=0 slice of Poincaré AdS4​,
ds2=z2dz2+dρ2+ρ2dθ2​.
(23)
The minimal surface anchored on a boundary circle of radius R1​ is the hemisphere
z2+ρ2=R12​.
(24)
Cutting it off at z=ϵ, its area is
Aϵ​​=2πR1​∫0R12​−ϵ2​​(R12​−ρ2)3/2ρdρ​=2π(ϵR1​​−1).​
(25)
Therefore
S(D1​)=2GϵπR1​​−2Gπ​​.
(26)
The universal requirement was the perimeter-law divergence πR1​/(2Gϵ); the finite constant depends on the stated pure AdS4​ vacuum geometry and equals −π/(2G) here. Since G∼N−3/2, both terms are of order N3/2.

Let γ1​ and γ2​ be the individual Ryu–Takayanagi surfaces for D1​ and D2​. Their disconnected union γ1​∪γ2​ is homologous to D1​∪D2​ and is therefore an admissible competitor in the minimization that defines γ12​. Minimality gives
Area(γ12​)≤Area(γ1​)+Area(γ2​).
(27)
Dividing by 4G proves
S(D1​∪D2​)≤S(D1​)+S(D2​),
(28)
and hence the leading holographic mutual information obeys
Ic​≥0​.
(29)
This is the geometric realization of the nonnegativity of quantum mutual information.

There are two competing extremal-surface topologies. The disconnected candidate γ1​∪γ2​ has area independent of the separation L and gives Ic​=0. A connected surface joining the two boundary circles can have smaller area when the gap is small; as L→0, short-distance entanglement across the nearby boundaries makes Ic​ positive and divergent.
Increasing L makes the connected candidate less favorable. At a critical separation L=C(R1​,R2​) its area equals the disconnected area, and beyond that point the disconnected candidate is minimal. This entanglement-wedge phase transition gives
Ic​>0(L<C),Ic​=0(L>C).​
(30)

Write Sconn​(L) for the connected candidate and
Sdisc​=S(D1​)+S(D2​)
(31)
for the L-independent disconnected candidate. The physical entropy is the lower envelope
S(D1​∪D2​)=min{Sconn​(L),Sdisc​}​.
(32)
Near L=C, the connected branch rises to meet the horizontal disconnected branch. The entropy is continuous but generically has a kink there: its derivative jumps from the positive slope of Sconn​ to zero. Correspondingly, Ic​=Sdisc​−S(D1​∪D2​) falls to zero and remains zero.

A pair of boundary circles has one dimensionless invariant under the conformal field theory group. If their radii are R1​,R2​ and their center separation is
d=R1​+R2​+L,
(33)
a convenient invariant is the inversive distance
χ=2R1​R2​d2−R12​−R22​​.
(34)
The choice between connected and disconnected bulk surfaces can depend only on χ. Let the transition occur at the single theory-independent numerical value χ=X, with X>1. Then
(R1​+R2​+C)2=R12​+R22​+2XR1​R2​,
(35)
so
C(R1​,R2​)=R12​+R22​+2XR1​R2​​−R1​−R2​​.
(36)
All dependence on the two radii is fixed by conformal symmetry; only the pure number X requires the explicit minimal-surface calculation.
