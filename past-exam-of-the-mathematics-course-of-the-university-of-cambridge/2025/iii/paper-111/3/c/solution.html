Suppose σ∗(w)(C) meets σ∗(v)(C). Applying the homeomorphism σ∗(v−1) shows that C meets σ∗(v−1w)(C). If v−1w=1, choose one of its left descents. Part b places its image of C in the corresponding negative half-space, while C lies in the positive half-space. This is impossible, so v=w. The union

When W is finite, the closures σ∗(w)(D) are the simplicial chambers cut out by the reflecting hyperplanes. Intersecting them with a sphere centred at the origin gives simplices. A face of type J⊆I has stabilizer the standard parabolic subgroup WJ​, so its translates are indexed by cosets wWJ​, with reverse inclusion of cosets encoding incidence. This is precisely the Coxeter complex of (W,I).
