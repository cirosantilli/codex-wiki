In quantum channel discrimination, prepare a density operator ρHR​ on the channel input H and an optional quantum ancilla R. Under hypothesis j∈{1,2} the output is
ωj​=(Tj​⊗idR​)(ρHR​).
(1)
Use a two-outcome measurement in quantum mechanics {Q,I−Q} and decide for T1​ on outcome Q. The conditional Type I error and Type II error are
α=Tr[(I−Q)ω1​],β=Tr[Qω2​].
(2)
With prior probabilities p and 1−p, symmetric Bayesian discrimination minimizes the average error pα+(1−p)β over the input and measurement.

For a fixed input, the Holevo–Helstrom theorem gives the optimal error for the two output states. Optimizing the input and quantum ancilla therefore gives
Perranc​=21​(1−∥pT1​−(1−p)T2​∥⋄​)​.
(3)
The stabilization in the diamond norm is exactly the optimization over ancillary systems. Without an ancilla the same argument instead gives
Perrno anc​=21​(1−∥pT1​−(1−p)T2​∥1​)​,
(4)
where ∥⋅∥1​ is the induced trace norm.

For p=(d+1)/(2d), the weighted difference of the two Werner–Holevo channels is
(pT+​−(1−p)T−​)(X)​=2d1​(Tr(X)I+XT)−2d1​(Tr(X)I−XT)=d1​XT.​
(5)
Thus the map is the transposition map divided by d. The diamond norm of the transposition map and invariance of the trace norm under matrix transpose give
∥pT+​−(1−p)T−​∥⋄​=d1​∥Θ∥⋄​=1​,∥pT+​−(1−p)T−​∥1​=d1​∥Θ∥1​=d1​​.
(6)
Substitution into the optimal-error formulas shows that an entangled quantum ancilla permits perfect discrimination, whereas every ancilla-free strategy has
Perrno anc​=21​(1−d1​)​.

A bipartite density operator is a separable quantum state when it has a convex combination decomposition
ρAB​=∑r​qr​ρrA​⊗ρrB​,qr​≥0,∑r​qr​=1,
(8)
into product states. A state for which no such decomposition exists is an entangled state.

The positive partial transpose criterion states that every separable quantum state satisfies
ρABTB​​≥0.
(9)
Consequently, a negative eigenvalue of the partial transpose proves entanglement. Positivity of the partial transpose is also sufficient for separability in dimensions 2⊗2 and 2⊗3, equivalently 3⊗2, but it is not sufficient in general higher dimensions.

At the proposed boundary p0​=1/(d+1), the identity from part (iv) yields
ρp0​​=d+1d​σ+d+11​dD​.
(15)
Both σ and D/d are convex combinations of product states, so ρp0​​ is a separable quantum state. For 0≤p≤p0​, the state ρp​ is a convex combination of ρp0​​ and the maximally mixed product state I/d2, and is therefore separable.
For the converse, the partial transpose of the maximally entangled projector is F/d, where F is the swap operator. Therefore
ρpTB​​=dp​F+d21−p​I.
(16)
On the antisymmetric subspace, F has eigenvalue −1, so the corresponding eigenvalue of ρpTB​​ is
d21−p​−dp​,
(17)
which is negative exactly when p>1/(d+1). The positive partial transpose criterion then proves that ρp​ is entangled. Thus
ρp​ is separable exactly when p≤d+11​​.

Apply the assumed data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy to the normalized partial trace over C, with the two input states
ρABC​,dA​IA​​⊗ρBC​.
(19)
The channel sends them to ρAB​⊗IC​/dC​ and IA​/dA​⊗ρB​⊗IC​/dC​. Additivity over the common maximally mixed factor reduces data processing to
D(ρABC​​dA​IA​​⊗ρBC​)≥D(ρAB​​dA​IA​​⊗ρB​).
(20)
Expanding the Umegaki relative entropy in terms of Von Neumann entropy gives
−S(ρABC​)+logdA​+S(ρBC​)≥−S(ρAB​)+logdA​+S(ρB​).
(21)
After cancelling logdA​, this is precisely the Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy
S(ρB​)+S(ρABC​)≤S(ρAB​)+S(ρBC​)​.
(22)

A real function f on I=[0,1] is an operator convex function when, for all Hermitian operators X,Y whose spectra lie in I and every 0≤λ≤1,
f(λX+(1−λ)Y)⪯λf(X)+(1−λ)f(Y)​,
(23)
where ⪯ is the Loewner order. Reversing the inequality defines an operator concave function, equivalently −f is operator convex.

Because the two flags EC​ and FC​ are orthogonal projections, the flagged state is block diagonal. If h(λ) denotes the binary entropy, then
S(ρABC​)=h(λ)+λS(ρAB′​)+(1−λ)S(ρAB′′​),

H(A∣B)λρ′+(1−λ)ρ′′​≥λH(A∣B)ρ′​+(1−λ)H(A∣B)ρ′′​​.

This is exactly the concavity of quantum conditional entropy.

Extend the quantum conditional entropy from normalized states to positive operators by
F(XAB​)=−Tr(XAB​logXAB​)+Tr(XB​logXB​).
(29)
Since TrXAB​=TrXB​, the two terms involving logt cancel under X↦tX, so F(tX)=tF(X). Thus F is positively homogeneous, and part (b) extends its concavity from states to the positive cone.
Apply part (c) with X=σAB​ and Y=ρAB​. Differentiating the matrix logarithm under the trace gives
dtd​​t=0​F(σ+tρ)=−Tr(ρlogσ)+Tr(ρB​logσB​).
(30)

D(ρAB​∥σAB​)≥D(ρB​∥σB​)​.
(31)
This is the data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy under partial trace. Tensoring each output with the appropriate maximally mixed state does not change either side, so it also proves data processing under normalized partial traces. The Umegaki relative entropy is
D(ρ∥σ)=Trρ(logρ−logσ)
(32)
when the support of ρ is contained in that of σ, and +∞ otherwise. Its additivity of quantum relative entropy is
D(ρA​⊗ρB​∥σA​⊗σB​)=D(ρA​∥σA​)+D(ρB​∥σB​),
(33)
its superadditivity of quantum relative entropy is
D(ρAB​∥σA​⊗σB​)≥D(ρA​∥σA​)+D(ρB​∥σB​),
(34)
and its data-processing inequality for quantum relative entropy is D(T(ρ)∥T(σ))≤D(ρ∥σ) for every quantum channel T.
Additivity follows from the logarithm of a tensor product,
log(ρA​⊗ρB​)=logρA​⊗I+I⊗logρB​,
(35)
and the analogous identity for σA​⊗σB​. For superadditivity, subtract the two marginal relative entropies from the joint one. The reference-state terms cancel, leaving
S(ρA​)+S(ρB​)−S(ρAB​)=I(A:B)ρ​≥0.
(36)
This is the nonnegativity of quantum mutual information, equivalently Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy.

Apply bipartite superadditivity of quantum relative entropy first to system 1 and systems 2,…,n, and then repeat on the remaining joint state. Mathematical induction gives
D(ρ1…n​∥σ1​⊗⋯⊗σn​)≥j=1∑n​D(ρj​∥σj​)​.

D(ρ1​⊗⋯⊗ρn​∥σ1​⊗⋯⊗σn​)=j=1∑n​D(ρj​∥σj​)​.

Let ρn,j′​ be the jth one-system marginal of ρn′​, and put εn​=∥ρn′​−ρ⊗n∥1​. Contractivity of trace distance under partial trace gives
∥ρn,j′​−ρ∥1​≤εn​
(39)
uniformly in j. Because the one-system state space is a compact space, continuity of f(⋅∥σ) implies uniform continuity. Hence there is a function δ(ε)→0 such that
f(ρn,j′​∥σ)≥f(ρ∥σ)−δ(εn​)
(40)
for every j.
Multipartite superadditivity and additivity now imply
f(ρn′​∥σ⊗n)f(ρ⊗n∥σ⊗n)​≥j=1∑n​f(ρn,j′​∥σ)≥nf(ρ∥σ)−nδ(εn​),=nf(ρ∥σ).​
(41)
Therefore
n1​(f(ρn′​∥σ⊗n)−f(ρ⊗n∥σ⊗n))≥−δ(εn​)⟶0,
(42)
which proves the required lower asymptotic semicontinuity of quantum relative entropy argument.
