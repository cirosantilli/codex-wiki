Writing Φ=(ϕ1​,ϕ2​)T, the scalar sector has a global SU(2) flavour symmetry in addition to the gauged common U(1) phase. A vacuum can be chosen as Φ0​=(0,v)T. A diagonal combination of the gauge phase and the SU(2) rotation generated by σ3​ leaves it invariant.
The radial scalar has mh2​=2λv2, the gauge boson has mA2​=2e2v2, and two real scalars remain massless. The four real scalar directions consist of one radial mode and three angular modes; one angular mode is eaten, leaving the two Goldstone bosons expected from the two broken physical global generators. The vacuum set before quotienting is S3, and the manifold of gauge-inequivalent vacua is the complex projective line
S3/U(1)=CP1≅S2.​
(5)

A gauge anomaly makes gauge redundancy inconsistent and must cancel. A chiral anomaly is the quantum nonconservation of a classically conserved axial current and may be a physical effect. A 't Hooft anomaly is an obstruction to gauging a global symmetry; it is preserved by renormalization-group flow and must be reproduced by the infrared theory, through massless degrees of freedom, symmetry breaking, or topological order.

Normalize a fundamental cubic anomaly to A(Nf​)=1 and its Dynkin index to I(Nf​)=1. The two gauge colours give
A[SU(Nf​)L3​]=2,A[SU(Nf​)L2​U(1)V​]=2.
(14)
The left and charge-conjugated right fermions have opposite vector charges and equal multiplicities, so
A[U(1)V3​]=0,A[U(1)V​-gravity]=0.
(15)
If the theory confined with unbroken chiral symmetry, gauge-invariant operators contain an even number of SU(2) fundamentals and are bosonic, so no massless composite fermions could match the nonzero chiral anomalies. 't Hooft anomaly matching therefore forces chiral symmetry breaking, with its infrared Goldstone theory carrying the anomaly.

Two complex N×N quark Yukawa matrices contain 4N2 real parameters. Flavour-basis transformations U(N)Q​×U(N)u​×U(N)d​ remove 3N2−1, because the common baryon-number phase remains unbroken. Thus there are
N2+1=2N+2N(N−1)​+2(N−1)(N−2)​
(25)
physical parameters: 2N quark masses, N(N−1)/2 mixing angles, and (N−1)(N−2)/2 complex phases. A physical phase exists only for N≥3 and produces CP violation, equivalently time-reversal violation when CPT is conserved.
