A galaxy contains so many stars that its two-body relaxation time is generally much longer than its age. Individual encounters can therefore be neglected and each star moves in the smooth collective potential. Liouville conservation along these Hamiltonian trajectories gives the Collisionless Boltzmann equation
dtdF​=0.
(10)
In spherical phase-space coordinates this is
Ft​+r˙Fr​+θ˙Fθ​+φ˙​Fφ​+v˙r​Fvr​​+v˙θ​Fvθ​​+v˙φ​Fvφ​​=0​.
(11)
The spherical line element is ds2=dr2+r2dθ2+r2sin2θdφ2, so
r˙=vr​,θ˙=vθ​/r,φ˙​=vφ​/(rsinθ)​.
(12)
For a unit-mass star in a spherical potential,
L=21​(r˙2+r2θ˙2+r2sin2θφ˙​2)−Φ(r).
(13)
The Euler--Lagrange equations, followed by differentiating vθ​=rθ˙ and vφ​=rsinθφ˙​, give
v˙r​=rvθ2​+vφ2​​−Φ′(r)​,
(14)
v˙θ​=rvφ2​cotθ−vr​vθ​​​,v˙φ​=−rvφ​vr​+vφ​vθ​cotθ​​.
(15)
Integrating the Boltzmann equation over velocity space, with vanishing velocity-space boundary terms, gives spherical mass conservation:
ρt​+∂r​(ρ⟨vr​⟩)+r2​ρ⟨vr​⟩=0​.
(16)
Multiplication by vr​ and integration gives the radial Jeans equation. With isotropic dispersion
⟨vr2​⟩=σ2+⟨vr​⟩2,⟨vθ2​⟩=⟨vφ2​⟩=σ2,
(17)
the geometric dispersion terms cancel, and use of continuity yields
∂r​(ρσ2)+ρ∂t​⟨vr​⟩+ρ⟨vr​⟩∂r​⟨vr​⟩=−ρΦ′(r)​.
(18)
The factor ρ on the right is required dimensionally.
In a Lambda-CDM background, the local excess mass contributes GM(r)/r2, homogeneous matter inside radius r contributes 4πGρb​r/3, and the cosmological constant contributes outward acceleration Λr/3. Hence
Φ′(r)=r2GM(r)​+34πGρb​​r−3Λ​r​.
(19)
Since Ωm​=8πGρb​/(3H2), ΩΛ​=Λ/(3H2), and q=Ωm​/2−ΩΛ​,
Φ′(r)=r2GM(r)​+qH2r​.
(20)
Write ⟨vr​⟩=Hr+vp​, where vp​ is the peculiar velocity. Then
∂t​⟨vr​⟩+⟨vr​⟩∂r​⟨vr​⟩=(H˙+H2)r+vp,t​+Hvp​+Hrvp,r​+vp​vp,r​.
(21)
The acceleration equation gives H˙+H2=a¨/a=−qH2, which cancels the background term in Φ′. Therefore
∂r​(ρσ2)=−ρ[r2GM(r)​+S(r,t)]​,
(22)
S=vp​vp,r​+H(vp​+rvp,r​)+vp,t​​.
