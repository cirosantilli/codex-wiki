In the Levi-Civita connection, the background metric is constant and replacing gαρ by its O(ϵ) correction would multiply a derivative of h and produce an O(ϵ2) term. Hence the linearized Levi-Civita connection is
Γαβγ​=21​ηαρ(∂β​hγρ​+∂γ​hβρ​−∂ρ​hβγ​)​.

Rμραβ(1)​=Rμραβ(1)​​.
(7)
This is the gauge invariance of the linearized Riemann tensor.

All time components of the perturbation vanish. For μ=t this immediately gives
∂νhμν​=0.
(8)
For a spatial index μ=i,
∂νhiν​=−∂t​hit​+∂m​him​=∂m​him​=0
(9)
by the stated property. Thus the perturbation is transverse.
Its Minkowski trace is purely spatial:
h=ημνhμν​=hxx​+hyy​+hzz​.
(10)
Adding the displayed components and collecting the coefficients of the independent functions A, B, and C makes each coefficient vanish separately, so
ημνhμν​=0​.
(11)
Together with htμ​=0 and ∂νhμν​=0, this proves that the wave is in transverse-traceless gauge.

On the positive z axis, x=y=0 and r=z. Taking this limit in the spatial components gives
hxx​=−A+2C,hyy​=A−2C,hxy​=hxz​=hyz​=hzz​=0,
(12)
with A,B,C evaluated at retarded time t−r. A wave propagating toward the observer along the z direction has polarization matrix
hijTT​=​h+​h×​0​h×​−h+​0​000​​.
(13)
Consequently the observed gravitational wave polarization amplitudes are
h+​=−A+2C,h×​=0​.
(14)
The B mode and the orthogonal combination of A,C do not contribute on this symmetry axis. The observer therefore sees a purely plus-polarized wave; a rotation of the transverse x,y axes would represent the same physical polarization as the corresponding spin-two mixture of plus and cross.

Write the Kruskal–Szekeres plane with r^ horizontal and t^ vertical. The relation
t^2−r^2=−er/(2M)(r−2M)
(43)
shows that constant-r curves are hyperbolae.
For r>2M, their right and left exterior branches satisfy
r^2−t^2=er/(2M)(r−2M)>0.
(44)
For 0<r<2M, their future and past interior branches satisfy
t^2−r^2=er/(2M)(2M−r)>0.
(45)
The event horizons r=2M are the null diagonals
t^=±r^​,
(46)
and the curvature singularities r=0 are the spacelike hyperbolae
t^2−r^2=2M​
(47)
in the normalization stated in the question.
Constant Schwarzschild-t curves are straight rays through the origin. In the exteriors their slopes obey
r^t^​=tanh4Mt​,
(48)
while in the interiors
t^r^​=tanh4Mt​.
(49)
Thus the qualitative diagram is the usual four-region Kruskal diagram: two exterior wedges separated from black-hole and white-hole interiors by the two null horizons, with spacelike singularities bounding the interior wedges.

In geodesic slicing, α=1 and βi=0. The normal acceleration therefore vanishes:
nρ∇ρ​nμ​=aμ​=Dμ​logα=0.
(50)
Hence each integral curve of nμ is an affinely parametrized timelike geodesic.
On the initial surface t^=0, the diagonal Kruskal metric makes the unit normal point purely in the t^ direction. The observer starts at r^=0 with r^˙=0, so its initial unit four-velocity equals that normal. The observer's geodesic and the normal integral curve solve the same geodesic initial-value problem. Uniqueness therefore gives
uμ=nμ​
(51)
throughout their common domain.
