The seminorms in R have inverse images of intervals that constrain one coordinate at a time. Finite intersections of these sets are exactly the standard basic neighbourhoods for the product topology, so (X×Y,R) is the product of locally convex spaces. If F∈(X×Y)∗, then
f(x)=F(x,0),g(y)=F(0,y)
(49)
belong to X∗ and Y∗ and F(x,y)=f(x)+g(y). Conversely every such pair defines a continuous functional, so (X×Y)∗=X∗⊕Y∗.
For the open convex sets K1​,…,Kn​, consider
C={(x1​−xn​,…,xn−1​−xn​):xi​∈Ki​}⊆Xn−1.
(50)
This set is open and convex, and 0∈/C because the Ki​ have empty intersection. Separate 0 from C by a continuous linear functional on the product. By the dual description just proved, it has the form
L(z1​,…,zn−1​)=∑j=1n−1​fj​(zj​)
(51)
for fj​∈X∗, and L is nonzero with one strict sign on C. Define
T:X→Rn−1,T(x)=(f1​(x),…,fn−1​(x)).
(52)
If some vector belonged to every T(Ki​), choose xi​∈Ki​ with the same image. Then every fj​(xj​−xn​)=0, making L(x1​−xn​,…,xn−1​−xn​)=0, a contradiction. Hence ⋂i​T(Ki​)=∅, proving finite-dimensional separation of open convex sets.
