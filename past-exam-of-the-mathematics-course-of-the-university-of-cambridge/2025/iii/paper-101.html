For R-modules M,N, the tensor product of modules is an R-module M⊗R​N together with the balanced map
τ:M×N⟶M⊗R​N,(m,n)⟼m⊗n,

(3)

A multiplicative subset S⊆R contains 1 and satisfies s,t∈S⇒st∈S. The localization of a ring consists of fractions r/s modulo the relation
sr​=s′r′​⟺some u∈S satisfies u(rs′−r′s)=0.
(18)

f​(r/s)=f(r)f(s)−1.​

ii OurBigBook.com
The local criterion states that an R-module M is flat if and only if Mp​ is flat over Rp​ for every prime ideal p. It is enough equivalently to test maximal ideals.
If M is flat, localization of an exact sequence and the natural isomorphism
(M⊗R​N)p​≅Mp​⊗Rp​​Np​
(20)
show immediately that every Mp​ is flat.
Conversely, let N′→N be injective and let K be the kernel of
M⊗R​N′⟶M⊗R​N.
(21)
After localization at any prime p, flatness of Mp​ gives Kp​=0. A module whose localization at every maximal ideal is zero must itself be zero: if 0=x∈K, its annihilator is contained in a maximal ideal m, and then x/1=0 in Km​. Hence K=0, tensoring by M preserves every injection, and M is flat. This proves that flatness is local.

Let m be maximal in R/I and let n⊆R be its contraction. The ideal n is maximal and contains I. It is disjoint from S=1+I: if 1+i∈n with i∈I⊆n, then 1∈n. The prime ideal correspondence for localization therefore defines the proper ideal S−1n, and
S−1R/S−1n≅R/n
(26)
is a field. Thus f(m)=S−1n is maximal.
Conversely, let q be the contraction to R of a maximal ideal of S−1R. Then q is maximal among ideals disjoint from S. Since q+I is disjoint from S—otherwise an equation q+i=1+j would put 1+(j−i)∈q∩S—maximality gives I⊆q. If a proper ideal strictly contained q in a maximal ideal n of R, then n would also contain I and hence remain disjoint from S, a contradiction. Thus q is maximal and contains I.
The two standard extension-contraction bijections, first for R→R/I and then for R→S−1R, now give inverse maps
MaxSpec(R/I)≅MaxSpec(S−1R)​.

V(I)={a∈kn:f(a)=0 for every f∈I}.

(T1​−a1​,…,Tn​−an​)

The polynomial ring C[X,Y] is a two-dimensional unique factorization domain. Let p be a prime ideal containing (f,g). Since f=0, the prime p is nonzero. If it were not maximal, it would have height one, so the height-one prime in a unique factorization domain would give p=(h) for an irreducible polynomial h. Then h would divide both f and g, contrary to the hypothesis. Every prime of
R=C[X,Y]/(f,g)
(52)
is therefore maximal.
The Hilbert basis theorem makes R Noetherian, and it has Krull dimension zero by the preceding paragraph. The Noetherian dimension-zero criterion for an Artinian ring now shows that
R is Artinian​.
(53)

A discrete valuation on a field K is a surjective group homomorphism
v:K×⟶Z
(54)
satisfying
v(x+y)≥min{v(x),v(y)}
(55)
whenever x+y=0. Its discrete valuation ring is
Rv​={0}∪{x∈K×:v(x)≥0}​.
(56)
One standard characterization defines a Dedekind domain as a Noetherian integrally closed domain of Krull dimension one. Equivalently, all its localizations at nonzero prime ideals are discrete valuation rings.

Because 0∈/S, the localization S−1A remains an integral domain. Part (a) makes it Noetherian. Integral closedness is preserved by localization: if x in the common fraction field is integral over S−1A, clearing the finitely many denominators in a monic equation shows that sx is integral over A for some s∈S, whence sx∈A and x∈S−1A.
The prime ideal correspondence for localization shows that every chain of primes in S−1A comes from a chain in A, so
dimS−1A≤1.
