Let w(x) be the downward velocity between the vertical plates. A steady vertical momentum balance gives
dxdτxz​​=−ρg.
(45)
Symmetry requires τxz​(0)=0, so the stress magnitude is
∣τxz​(x)∣=ρg∣x∣.
(46)
The wall stress first reaches the yield stress when ρglc​=τy​. The onset measurement therefore gives
τy​=ρglc​​.
(47)
For l>lc​, define Δ=l−lc​. The central region ∣x∣≤lc​ is a plug flow of a yield-stress fluid, while the layers lc​<∣x∣≤l are yielded. The Herschel–Bulkley fluid law gives, for x≥lc​,
∣w′(x)∣=[Kρg(x−lc​)​]1/n.
(48)
Integrating from the no-slip wall w(l)=0 gives
w(x)=(Kρg​)1/nn+1n​[Δ1+1/n−(x−lc​)1+1/n]
(49)
in the yielded layer, and the plug moves at
wp​=(Kρg​)1/nn+1n​Δ1+1/n.
(50)
The volumetric flow rate per unit span is consequently
Q=2(Kρg​)1/n[2n+1n​Δ2+1/n+n+1nlc​​Δ1+1/n]​.
(51)
Near onset, the plug term dominates and
Q∼(l−lc​)1+1/n.
(52)
The observed exponent two therefore corresponds to n=1, a linear post-yield constitutive law at small shear rate. Far above onset, the fully yielded contribution scales as
Q∼l2+1/n.
(53)
The observed exponent four corresponds to n=1/2, a shear-thinning square-root law at high shear rate.
A plausible stress curve is therefore odd in γ˙​, starts at the two yield-stress values τ=±τy​, and has finite linear slope just after yield:
τ−τy​∼K1​γ˙​(γ˙​>0 small).
(54)
It then crosses smoothly to the concave high-rate behavior
τ−τy​∼K2​γ˙​1/2(γ˙​>0 large),
(55)
with the negative-rate branch fixed by odd symmetry. This combines the two power laws inferred independently from the measured flux limits.
