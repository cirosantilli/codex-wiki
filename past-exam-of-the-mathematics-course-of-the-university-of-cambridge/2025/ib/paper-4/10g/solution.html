A map f:Rn→Rm is differentiable at x if there is a linear map L such that
f(x+h)=f(x)+Lh+o(∥h∥);
(28)
then Df∣x​=L. The inverse function theorem says that if f is continuously differentiable near x and Df∣x​ is invertible, then f restricts to a C1 diffeomorphism between neighborhoods of x and f(x).
Since F(A)=ATA is polynomial,
DF∣A​(H)=HTA+ATH.
