A space is connected when it is not a union of two disjoint nonempty open subsets. If f(X) had such a separation, its inverse images would separate connected X, so continuous images of connected spaces are connected. Equivalently, a nonconstant continuous map to discrete {0,1} records a separation, and every separation defines such a map.
If R were separated, points in opposite pieces and the intermediate value theorem applied to the associated {0,1}-valued map would give a contradiction. Thus R is connected. Its quotient by x∼y is connected because the quotient map is continuous and surjective.
Finally let A⊂B⊂Cl(A) and suppose B=U∪V were a separation. Connectedness puts A wholly in one side, say U. Every point of V nevertheless lies in the closure of A, while V is relatively open and disjoint from A, a contradiction. Hence B is connected.
