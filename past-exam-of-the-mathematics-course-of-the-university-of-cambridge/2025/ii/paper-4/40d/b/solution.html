Write
A=D+L+U,
(568)
where D=diag(a1​,…,an​) and L,U are the strict lower and upper triangular parts. The Jacobi method is
x(k+1)=D−1(b−(L+U)x(k)),
(569)
with iteration matrix
H=−D−1(L+U).
(570)
Here A is symmetric, so U=LT. Regard the iteration as the splitting
A=M−N,M=D,N=−(L+LT).
(571)
Let
S=diag(1,−1,1,−1,…).
(572)
Because A is tridiagonal, changing alternating signs reverses the sign of every off-diagonal entry while preserving every diagonal entry. Therefore
MT+N=D−L−LT=SAS.
(573)
Since ST=S=S−1 and A is positive definite, SAS is positive definite. The Householder-John theorem now gives
ρ(H)<1.
(574)
The Jacobi iterates therefore converge to the unique solution A−1b for every starting vector, which is the jacobi convergence for a symmetric positive-definite tridiagonal matrix.
