Let F be the class of all finite sets. It is proper. If F were a set, then for every set x the singleton {x} would belong to F, so
x∈⋃F.
(172)
Thus ⋃F would be a universal set. Separation applied to the property z∈/z would then give the usual Russell contradiction.
The class F is not transitive. For example, {ω} is finite, but its member ω is infinite. It is nevertheless φ-closed for every formula φ: if x is finite, then every subset
{z∈x:φ(z)}
(173)
is finite.
