The diagonal Ramsey number R(t) is the least positive integer n such that every red-blue colouring of the edges of Kn​ contains a monochromatic Kt​.
More generally, let R(s,t) be the least n forcing either a red Ks​ or a blue Kt​. We have
R(1,t)=R(s,1)=1.
(184)
Assuming the two smaller numbers exist, colour
KR(s−1,t)+R(s,t−1)​
(185)
and choose a vertex v. At least R(s−1,t) of its incident edges are red, or at least R(s,t−1) are blue. In the first case, the corresponding neighbourhood contains a red Ks−1​, which extends with v to a red Ks​, or a blue Kt​. The second case is symmetric. Thus
R(s,t)≤R(s−1,t)+R(s,t−1),
(186)
which proves existence by induction.
