The subset construction theorem gives L(D)=L(N). Removing inaccessible states does not alter any computation from the initial state, so
L(D′)=L(N).
(84)
Every state of D′ is accessible by construction. It remains to separate distinct subset states S,T of D′. Choose q∈S△T, and without loss of generality take q∈S∖T. By (Br2), some word w has a witnessing sequence from q to the unique final state q∗​. Part (b)(iii) implies
q∗​∈Δ(q,w),
(85)
so the state reached from S on w is accepting.
If the state reached from T on w were also accepting, there would be some p∈T with a witnessing sequence labelled w from p to q∗​. But q has such a sequence too, and (Br3) says that its starting state is unique. Hence p=q, contradicting q∈/T. Thus w distinguishes S and T.
No two distinct states of D′ are indistinguishable, and all are accessible. Therefore D′ is irreducible and accepts L(N).
