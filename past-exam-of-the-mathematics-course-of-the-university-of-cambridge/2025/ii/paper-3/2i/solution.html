Runge's polynomial approximation theorem says that if K⊂C is compact, C∖K is connected, and f is holomorphic on a neighborhood of K, then for every ε>0 there is a polynomial p such that
supK​∣p−f∣<ε.
(8)
Let
S={z:∣z∣=1, Rez≤0}.
(9)
This arc is compact, its complement is connected, and 1/z is holomorphic on a neighborhood of it. Applying Runge with error 1/n gives a polynomial qn​ satisfying
supz∈S​∣qn​(z)−z−1∣<n1​,
(10)
which is the requested uniform approximation.
For the pointwise construction, for n≥2 set
Kn+​Kn−​Kn0​​={z:∣z∣≤1−1/n, Rez≥1/n},={z:∣z∣≤1−1/n, Rez≤−1/n},={iy:∣y∣≤1−1/n},Kn​=Kn+​∪Kn−​∪Kn0​.​
(11)
The three pieces are disjoint compact sets and C∖Kn​ is connected. Define a function on a neighborhood of Kn​ to equal 1, −1, and 0 on neighborhoods of Kn+​, Kn−​, and Kn0​, respectively. It is holomorphic because those neighborhoods may be chosen disjoint. Runge supplies a polynomial Pn​ such that
∣Pn​−1∣<1/n on Kn+​,∣Pn​+1∣<1/n on Kn−​,∣Pn​∣<1/n on Kn0​.
(12)
Every fixed point with ∣z∣<1 eventually belongs to the appropriate one of these three sets, according to the sign of its real part. These inequalities therefore give precisely the asserted pointwise limits.
