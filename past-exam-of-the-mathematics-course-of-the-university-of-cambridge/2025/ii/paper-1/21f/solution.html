The simplicial Mayer-Vietoris theorem says that if a simplicial complex X=M∪N for subcomplexes M,N, there is a natural long exact sequence
⋯→Hn​(M∩N)(i∗​,−j∗​)​Hn​(M)⊕Hn​(N)→Hn​(X)→Hn−1​(M∩N)→⋯.
(119)
The reduced version extends through dimension zero.
For every simplex σ of K, all faces of σ∪{ci​} are either faces of σ or have the form τ∪{ci​} with τ a face of σ, so they lie in L. Each ci​∗K is a cone and hence a simplicial complex. For distinct i,j, the rays in the last two coordinates through ci​ and cj​ are not positive multiples. Thus the two cones meet exactly in K, so their simplices have common faces only in K. Hence L is a simplicial complex.
Topologically, L is the union of three cones on K along their common base. The first two cones form the suspension ΣK. Attaching the third contractible cone along the equatorial copy of K gives
L≃ΣK∨ΣK.
(120)
This also follows by applying reduced Mayer--Vietoris twice: every cone has zero reduced homology, and the two inclusion maps from the common base are null-homotopic inside the cones. Therefore
Hn​(L)≅Hn−1​(K)⊕Hn−1​(K)​
(121)
for every n, with the convention that negative reduced homology vanishes. Since K is nonempty, L is connected, so explicitly
H0​(L)≅Z,H1​(L)≅H0​(K)⊕2,
(122)
Hn​(L)≅Hn−1​(K)⊕2(n≥2).
(123)
