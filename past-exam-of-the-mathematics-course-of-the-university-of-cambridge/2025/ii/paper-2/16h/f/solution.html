We describe a collection of finite multiplication patterns. For finite sets
E⊆{1,…,n}3,D⊆{1,…,n}2,
(149)
write
PE,D​(x1​,…,xn​)=⋀(i,j,k)∈E​xi​xj​=xk​ ∧⋀(i,j)∈D​xi​=xj​.
(150)
Call (E,D) T-forbidden if the following theory, in L expanded by unary symbols f1​,…,fn​, is inconsistent:
  • T, together with the assertion that every fi​ is an automorphism;
  • ∀zfi​(fj​(z))=fk​(z) for every (i,j,k)∈E;
  • ∃zfi​(z)=fj​(z) for every (i,j)∈D.
Let T∗ contain the group axioms and, for every T-forbidden finite pattern, the sentence
∀x1​⋯∀xn​¬PE,D​(x1​,…,xn​).
(151)
This is a first-order theory in the language of groups.
Suppose G is T-good. Choose M⊨T and an embedding ρ:G↪Aut(M). If a tuple (g1​,…,gn​) in G realized a forbidden pattern, interpreting fi​ as ρ(gi​) would give a model of its supposedly inconsistent automorphism theory. Thus no such tuple exists, and G⊨T∗.
Conversely, suppose G is T-bad. By part (e), there is a finite set X={g1​,…,gn​}⊆G for which TG​∩LX​ is inconsistent. Let
E={(i,j,k):gi​gj​=gk​},D={(i,j):gi​=gj​}.
(152)
The associated automorphism theory is precisely TG​∩LX​, up to renaming its function symbols, so (E,D) is T-forbidden. But (g1​,…,gn​) realizes PE,D​ in G, and therefore G violates the corresponding forbidding axiom. Hence G⊨T∗.
We have proved
G⊨T∗⟺G is T-good.​
