Since r is even,
(ar/2−1)(ar/2+1)=ar−1≡0(modN).
(127)
The first factor is not divisible by N, since that would contradict minimality of r, and the second is not divisible by N by hypothesis. Nevertheless their product is divisible by N. Therefore
1<gcd(ar/2−1,N)<N,
(128)
and Euclid's algorithm computes a nontrivial factor. One can also compute gcd(ar/2+1,N); together the two gcds expose factors lying on opposite sides of the congruence ar/2≡±1 modulo the prime-power divisors of N.
