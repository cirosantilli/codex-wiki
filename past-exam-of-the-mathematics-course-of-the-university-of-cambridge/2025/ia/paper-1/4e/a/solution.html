The Darboux–Riemann criterion says that a bounded function on [a,b] is Riemann integrable exactly when, for every ε>0, some partition P satisfies U(f,P)−L(f,P)<ε. Indeed, every lower sum is at most every upper sum. Taking the supremum of lower sums and infimum of upper sums, the criterion makes their difference smaller than every positive ε, so they are equal; this common value is the Riemann integral.
