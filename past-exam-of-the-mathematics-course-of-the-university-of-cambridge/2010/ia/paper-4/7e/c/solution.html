Let Ω be the set of all functions from the n-element domain to the k-element codomain, and let Cj​ consist of the functions which omit codomain element j. A function is surjective precisely when it lies outside ⋃j​Cj​.
For J⊆[k] of size i, the intersection ⋂j∈J​Cj​ consists of maps taking all their values in the remaining k−i elements, so it has (k−i)n members. There are (ik​) choices of J. Applying the complement form of the inclusion-exclusion principle gives
#{surjections}=i=0∑k​(−1)i(ik​)(k−i)n(n≥k≥1).​
(34)
When k=n, every fibre of a surjective function has at least one member, and the n fibres partition an n-element domain. Thus every fibre has exactly one member and the function is a bijection. The count of these bijections is n! by the successive distinct-image choices from part (a). Hence
n!=i=0∑n​(−1)i(in​)(n−i)n.​
