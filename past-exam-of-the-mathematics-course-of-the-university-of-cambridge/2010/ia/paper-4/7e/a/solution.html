Enumerate A as a1​,…,aa​. A function is specified by choosing the image of each of these elements independently, with b possibilities each. The multiplication principle gives ba functions​.
For an injective function, successive images must be distinct. If a≤b, there are b choices for the first, b−1 for the second, and so on. If a>b, the pigeonhole principle makes injectivity impossible. Thus the number of injective functions is
⎩⎨⎧​b(b−1)⋯(b−a+1)=(b−a)!b!​,0,​a≤b,a>b.​​
(31)
The product in the first case is a falling factorial.
