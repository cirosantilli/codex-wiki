Work in an inertial frame, take all masses to be constant, and assume the internal forces obey the pairwise relation from Newton's third law Fij​=−Fji​. For the angular momentum result, also assume these pair forces are central, so Fij​ is parallel to ri​−rj​ and its pair torque vanishes. The equations from Newton's second law are
mi​r¨i​=Fi​+∑j=i​Fij​.
(61)
Summing them and pairing the internal forces gives
dtdP​=F,P=i∑​mi​r˙i​,F=i∑​Fi​.​
(62)
About a fixed point a, define the total angular momentum and external torque by
La​=∑i​(ri​−a)×mi​r˙i​,Ga​=∑i​(ri​−a)×Fi​.
(63)
Differentiation produces no velocity term because r˙i​×mi​r˙i​=0. Grouping internal terms into pairs gives
dtdLa​​=Ga​+∑i<j​[(ri​−a)×Fij​+(rj​−a)×Fji​]=Ga​+∑i<j​(ri​−rj​)×Fij​.
(64)
Each final cross product is zero by centrality, proving dLa​/dt=Ga​​. Equal and opposite internal forces give momentum conservation when the total external force is zero, but do not in general make their internal torques cancel; the central-force assumption supplies that extra step.
Let M=∑i​mi​ and let the centre of mass be R=M−1∑i​mi​ri​, so P=MR˙. For an arbitrary moving reference point a(t), the same differentiation gives
dtdLa(t)​​=Ga(t)​−a˙×P.
(65)
Taking a=R, the extra term is −R˙×MR˙=0. Therefore the corresponding angular momentum about the centre of mass satisfies
dtdLR​​=GR​.​
(66)
Its definition using velocities relative to R˙ agrees with the one above, since ∑i​mi​(ri​−R)=0 makes ∑i​(ri​−R)×mi​R˙=0. Thus this result remains true even when the centre of mass accelerates.
Finally take the common mass to be m and Fi​=−kr˙i​, with constant k. About either a fixed point or the centre of mass,
G=−k∑i​(ri​−a)×r˙i​=−mk​L.
(67)
The angular-momentum equation becomes L˙=−(k/m)L. Multiplying by the integrating factor ekt/m gives dtd​(ekt/mL)=0, hence the angular momentum decay under uniform linear drag is
L(t)=L(0)e−kt/m.​
(68)
The common mass and common drag coefficient ensure that the same decay factor applies to every term.
