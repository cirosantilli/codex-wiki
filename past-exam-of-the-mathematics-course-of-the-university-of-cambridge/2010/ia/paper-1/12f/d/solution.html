Away from zero and the zeros of cos(π/x), the sign function is locally constant and the absolute value function is differentiable at the value of the cosine. The product rule and chain rule give
f′(x)​=2xsign(x)​cosxπ​​+x2sign(x)sign(cosxπ​)x2π​sinxπ​=2∣x∣​cosxπ​​+πsign(x)sign(cosxπ​)sinxπ​.​

Thus the triangle inequality and the bounds on sine and cosine imply ∣f′(x)∣≤2∣x∣+π. Part (c) rules out differentiability at every nonzero cosine zero, and part (b) gives f′(0)=0, so this estimate covers every point where the derivative exists.
For a finite interval I, choose L<∞ such that ∣x∣≤L throughout I. Then
∣f′(x)∣≤CI​:=2L+π(x∈I where f′(x) exists).​
(77)
The apparent x−2 factor in the derivative of the oscillation cancels against the multiplying x2, which is what makes a uniform bound possible even near zero.
