An orthogonal matrix satisfies RTR=I, so it preserves the Euclidean norm. For a nonzero real eigenvector v,
∥v∥2=∥Rv∥2=∥λv∥2=λ2∥v∥2.
(41)
Therefore λ=±1​. Geometrically, eigenvalue 1 fixes every vector on the linear span of v; eigenvalue −1 reverses every vector on that line. In either case the line is preserved as a set.
For M, solving (M−I)v=0 gives y=x, z=y, x=z. For N, the equations are −2y−2z=0 and −2z=0, hence y=z=0. For P, let a=(1,1,1)T and J=aaT, so that P=I−32​J. Then (P−I)v=0 is equivalent to x+y+z=0. The resulting eigenspaces and their bases are
E1​(M)E1​(N)E1​(P)​=span{(1,1,1)T},=span{(1,0,0)T},=span{(1,−1,0)T,(1,0,−1)T},​dimE1​(M)dimE1​(N)dimE1​(P)​=1,=1,=2.​​
(42)
Solving all the equations shows that these lists contain the maximum possible numbers of independent eigenvectors with eigenvalue 1.
The columns of M form an orthonormal basis, so M is an orthogonal matrix. Also detM=1. Its fixed axis is the line through a, and on the perpendicular plane it acts as a two-dimensional rotation. If θ denotes the rotation angle, its matrix trace is 1+2cosθ. Since trM=0, we obtain cosθ=−1/2. To fix the orientation relative to u=a/3​, note that Me1​=e3​ and
u⋅(e1​×Me1​)=u⋅(e1​×e3​)=−3​1​<0.
(43)
The component of e1​ perpendicular to u has squared length 2/3, and this scalar triple product equals (2/3)sinθ. Hence sinθ=−3​/2. Thus
M: axis R(1,1,1),θ=−32π​ about 3​(1,1,1)​.​
(44)
Equivalently, its unsigned angle is 2π/3, or its positive angle is 2π/3 about the opposite oriented axis.
For P, the reflection in a hyperplane formula gives
P=I−2aTaaaT​.
(45)
It fixes the plane a⋅v=0 pointwise and maps a to −a, so P reflects in x+y+z=0​. These actions also show that P is an orthogonal matrix. In contrast, the second column of N has squared length 5, so N is not orthogonal and cannot represent either of these Euclidean motions.
A diagonalizable matrix must have a basis of eigenvectors over the field in question. For M, a direct determinant computation gives χM​(t)=1−t3. Its eigenvalues are 1,ω,ω2, where ω=e2πi/3. Only one is real, with one-dimensional eigenspace, so M is not diagonalizable over R. Over C the three eigenvalues are distinct, and the vectors (1,λ,λ2)T for λ=1,ω,ω2 give an eigenbasis. The upper triangular matrix N has only eigenvalue 1, of algebraic multiplicity 3, but its eigenspace has dimension 1 over either field. Hence N is not diagonalizable over either field.
