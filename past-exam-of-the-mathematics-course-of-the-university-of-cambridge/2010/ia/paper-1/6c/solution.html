In an oriented orthonormal coordinate system, the dot product and cross product are
a⋅b=∑k=13​ak​bk​,a×b=(a2​b3​−a3​b2​, a3​b1​−a1​b3​, a1​b2​−a2​b1​).
(26)
The scalar triple product is a⋅(b×c); equivalently it is the determinant of the matrix whose columns are a,b,c. The three vectors have linear independence when
t1​a1​+t2​a2​+t3​a3​=0 ⟹ t1​=t2​=t3​=0.
(27)
Let U=(a1​ a2​ a3​) and V=(b1​ b2​ b3​) be column matrices. Since (UTV)ij​=ai​⋅bj​, we have S=UTV. Multiplicativity of the determinant and its invariance under matrix transpose yield
detS=det(UT)detV=detUdetV=(a1​⋅(a2​×a3​))(b1​⋅(b2​×b3​))​.
(28)
A square matrix has maximal matrix rank exactly when its determinant is nonzero, equivalently when its columns have linear independence. A product of two real numbers is nonzero exactly when both factors are nonzero. Hence rankS=3⟺(ai​)i=13​ and (bj​)j=13​ are both independent​.
The same argument works in every dimension. Set Un​=(c1​ ⋯ cn​) and Vn​=(d1​ ⋯ dn​). Then
T=UnT​Vn​,detT=detUn​detVn​,
(29)
so rankT=n⟺(ci​)i=1n​ and (dj​)j=1n​ are both independent​.
For the last construction, choose two standard basis vectors e1​,e2​ in Rn and take cj​=e1​+je2​ for j=1,…,n. If αci​+βcj​=0 with i=j, its first two coordinates give α+β=0 and iα+jβ=0, hence α=β=0. Every pair therefore has linear independence. Every triple lies in the two-dimensional linear span of e1​,e2​ and has linear dependence. More explicitly, for distinct i,j,k,
(j−k)ci​+(k−i)cj​+(i−j)ck​=0,
(30)
a nontrivial dependence. Thus cj​=e1​+je2​ (1≤j≤n) works for every n>2​.
