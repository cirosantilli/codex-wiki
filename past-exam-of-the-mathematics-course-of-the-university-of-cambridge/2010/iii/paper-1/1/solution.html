OurBigBook.com Words: 115
The defining rule for a derivation of a Lie algebra is linear in the operator, so Der(g) is a vector subspace of the general linear Lie algebra gl(g). Its Lie bracket is the operator commutator. If D,E are derivations of a Lie algebra, expand both compositions:
DE[x,y]ED[x,y]​=[DEx,y]+[Ex,Dy]+[Dx,Ey]+[x,DEy],=[EDx,y]+[Dx,Ey]+[Ex,Dy]+[x,EDy].​
(1)
Subtracting cancels the mixed terms and gives
[D,E][x,y]=[[D,E]x,y]+[x,[D,E]y].
(2)
Thus [D,E] is again a derivation of a Lie algebra, proving that the derivation Lie algebra is a Lie subalgebra of gl(g). This calculation works in every characteristic of a field.
The Jacobi identity states [x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]]. It first shows that adx​ is a derivation of a Lie algebra, and then gives
[adx​,ady​](z)=[x,[y,z]]−[y,[x,z]]=[[x,y],z].
(3)
The map x↦adx​ is linear, so this identity proves it is a Lie algebra homomorphism.
Finally, for any derivation of a Lie algebra D,
[D,adx​](y)=D[x,y]−[x,Dy]=[Dx,y].
(4)
Consequently
[D,adx​]=adDx​,[Der(g),adg]⊆adg.​
(5)
This proves that the inner derivations of a Lie algebra form an ideal of a Lie algebra in Der(g).
