Define
h={X∈g:exp(tX)∈H for every t∈R}.
(27)
It contains zero, and X∈h implies aX∈h for every real scalar a, by replacing t with at.
For X,Y∈h, we prove closure under addition using the Lie product formula in a Lie group. In the local exponential chart, let Z(s)=log(exp(sX)exp(sY)). Smooth multiplication has differential (U,V)↦U+V at the identity, so Taylor's theorem gives
Z(s)=s(X+Y)+O(s2).
(28)
For each fixed real t and all sufficiently large positive integers m,
[exp(tX/m)exp(tY/m)]m=exp(mZ(t/m))⟶exp(t(X+Y)).
(29)
Every term belongs to H, and H is closed, proving X+Y∈h. Thus h is a real vector subspace. It is also closed: if Xj​→X with Xj​∈h, continuity of the Exponential map of a Lie group gives exp(tX)∈H for each t.
For the Lie bracket, use the Adjoint representation of a Lie group. Conjugating a one-parameter subgroup gives
exp(sX)exp(tY)exp(−sX)=exp(tAdexp(sX)​Y)∈H.
(30)
Consequently Adexp(sX)​Y∈h. For s=0, linearity puts (Adexp(sX)​Y−Y)/s in h. Its limit as s→0 is adX​Y=[X,Y]. Closedness of h therefore gives [X,Y]∈h, proving that the Lie algebra of a closed subgroup is a Lie subalgebra of g.
