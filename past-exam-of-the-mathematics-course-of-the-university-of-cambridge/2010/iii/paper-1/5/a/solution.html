Suppose that H is not a discrete subgroup. The identity is then an accumulation point of nonidentity subgroup elements: translate any failure of isolation to the identity. Choose hj​∈H∖{e} with hj​→e, and use a local exponential chart to write hj​=expXj​, where Xj​=0 and Xj​→0 in the real Lie algebra g.
Choose a norm on this finite-dimensional vector space. By compactness of its unit sphere, pass to a subsequence with Xj​/∥Xj​∥→X, where ∥X∥=1. For an arbitrary real t, choose an integer mj​ nearest to t/∥Xj​∥. Then mj​∥Xj​∥→t and
hjmj​​=exp(mj​Xj​)⟶exp(tX).
(26)
Every left-hand element belongs to H, including for negative mj​. Closedness gives exp(tX)∈H. Since t was arbitrary and X=0, the limit directions of a closed subgroup provide a nontrivial one-parameter subgroup in H. Its derivative at zero is X, so it cannot be the constant subgroup.
