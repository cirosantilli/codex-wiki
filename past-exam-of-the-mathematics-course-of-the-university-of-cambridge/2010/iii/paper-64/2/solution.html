Use the usual fractional Dirichlet domain scale: A=−ΔD​ on H=L2(Ω), and Hρ​=D(Aρ). Its Sobolev embedding theorem gives
Hδ​↪H2δ(Ω)↪C0,β(Ω),0<β<min(1,2δ−n/2).
(8)
Such a positive β exists precisely because the assumed δ is greater than n/4. Consequently v=ut​ is continuous in time with values in this Hölder space on every compact positive-time interval. This embedding alone gives no two spatial derivatives for v; those must come from the equation.
First establish a bound that will also justify the nonlinear regularity steps. Put F(s)=λs−s3 and
K=max{∣m∣,∣M∣,max(λ,0)​}.​
(9)
The constants K and −K enclose both the initial data and the zero Dirichlet boundary data. Moreover F(K)≤0 and F(−K)≥0, so they are a supersolution and a subsolution for the parabolic comparison principle. A direct energy proof avoids assuming pointwise continuity at time zero. Let h=(u−K)+​. This truncation has zero trace under the Sobolev trace operator; testing the evolution with h and using integration by parts gives
21​dtd​∥h∥22​=−∥∇h∥22​+∫Ω​F(u)hdx.
(10)
For u>K, the mean value theorem and F′(s)=λ−3s2≤λ imply F(u)≤F(K)+λ(u−K). Therefore
21​dtd​∥h∥22​≤λ∥h∥22​.
(11)
The Gronwall inequality gives h=0, since its initial L2 norm vanishes. For the initially rough datum, apply the inequality from ε>0 and let ε↓0, using u(ε)→u0​ in L2 space. The same argument for −u, which satisfies the same equation because F is odd, proves
−K≤u(x,t)≤Kthroughout its existence interval.​
(12)
This is the invariant interval for a cubic reaction-diffusion equation; the energy calculation is valid by the Sobolev chain rule and approximation even before the stronger pointwise regularity is established.
Now fix 0<τ<t0​ and, if necessary, reduce the positive Hölder exponent β. Rearranging the evolution gives the elliptic identity
Δu(t)=v(t)−F(u(t)),u(t)∣∂Ω​=0.
(13)
Its right side is bounded on [τ,t0​]×Ω, because v∈C([τ,t0​],C0,β) and ∣u∣≤K. Boundary elliptic regularity first gives u(t)∈W2,p(Ω) for every finite p, uniformly on this time interval. For p>n, the Sobolev embedding theorem makes u(t) spatially continuously differentiable, with a positive Hölder exponent. Thus F(u(t)) lies in C0,β after reducing β if necessary. The global Schauder estimate then gives u(t)∈C2,β(Ω).
These are continuous-in-time, not just separate-time, regularity statements. Since v∈C(C0,β), the identity u(t)−u(s)=∫st​v(r)dr, initially an identity in L2 space, holds in C0,β once u(s) is known to belong to that space. Hence u is locally Lipschitz in time into C0,β. Apply the global Schauder estimate to the difference of the two elliptic identities. Continuity of v and of F(u) into C0,β proves u∈C([τ,t0​],C2,β).
To gain the spatial regularity of the time derivative itself, take time difference quotients of the evolution. If dh​(t)=[u(t+h)−u(t)]/h, their equations are
(dh​)t​=Δdh​+ah​(x,t)dh​,dh​∣∂Ω​=0,
(14)
where
ah​=λ−[u(t+h)2+u(t+h)u(t)+u(t)2].
(15)
The coefficients have uniformly bounded spatial Hölder norms and temporal Hölder norms on compact positive-time cylinders: this follows from the spatial regularity just proved and the local time Lipschitz bound in C0,β. Also dh​=∫01​v(t+sh)ds is uniformly bounded and tends to v in C0,β. Positive-time parabolic Schauder estimates, applied away from the lower time edge, give uniform C1+β/2,2+β bounds for dh​, with a slightly smaller exponent if needed. Passing to the limit proves that v is continuous into C2(Ω) and satisfies
vt​=Δv+(λ−3u2)v,v∣∂Ω​=0.
(16)
The Dirichlet boundary condition for v follows also by differentiating the identically zero boundary value in C0. Since τ was arbitrary, this proves the requested, genuinely stronger regularity:
u∈C1((0,t0​],C2(Ω)).​
(17)
This is positive-time smoothing for a semilinear heat equation; it is the extra parabolic step for v, not just the Sobolev embedding theorem, that gives the C1 topology asserted here.
Finally, a uniform bound excludes a finite maximal existence time for this semilinear partial differential equation. Restart at any positive time s, using the heat semigroup on the Banach space X={g∈C(Ω):g∣∂Ω​=0} with the supremum norm. Its variation-of-constants formula is
u(s+t)=e−tAu(s)+∫0t​e−(t−r)AF(u(s+r))dr.
(18)
The heat semigroup is a contraction semigroup on X. On the ball of radius K+1, F is Lipschitz with constant at most ∣λ∣+3(K+1)2, and bounded by ∣λ∣(K+1)+(K+1)3. Choose a time length η>0 so that the integral is at most 1 and its Lipschitz constant is less than 1. The Banach fixed-point theorem then gives a local solution on [s,s+η], with η independent of s, because ∥u(s)∥∞​≤K. If a finite maximal time existed, choosing s less than η before it would extend the same solution past it, a contradiction. Uniqueness identifies this continuation with the original solution, and positive-time smoothing restores the classical solution on each extended interval. The solution is globally defined and remains between −K and K.
