For a nonconstant invasion travelling wave, the relevant end states are w(−∞)=k and w(+∞)=0. These fronts do not exist at every nonnegative speed: the phase plane calculation gives the threshold 2k​. The constant solutions w≡0 and w≡k are, of course, nonnegative travelling waves for every speed, including zero.
Put z=x−ct and p=w′. The travelling-wave reduction of a reaction-diffusion system gives
w′′+cw′+w(k−w)=0,(w′p′​)=(p−cp−w(k−w)​).
(19)
The equilibrium points in the phase plane are (k,0) and (0,0). At (k,0) the characteristic equation is r2+cr−k=0, so there is one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Thus it is a saddle equilibrium. Its descending unstable manifold has w<k, p<0 and locally
p=r+​(w−k)+o(∣w−k∣),r+​=2−c+c2+4k​​>0.
(20)
At (0,0) the eigenvalues are
r=2−c±c2−4k​​.
(21)
For c>2k​ it is a stable node; for 0<c<2k​ it is a stable spiral. The repeated-root case c=2k​ must be included in the existence argument rather than inferred from distinct roots.
Suppose c≥2k​. Choose a>0 with a(c−a)≥k; for example a=(c−c2−4k​)/2. Consider the closed triangular region
T={(w,p):0≤w≤k, −aw≤p≤0}.
(22)
On the horizontal side p=0, the vector field has p′=−w(k−w)≤0, pointing into T. On the vertical side w=k, it has w′=p≤0, again pointing inward. For the sloping side use h=p+aw, so that on h=0,
h′=p′+aw′=(a−c)p−w(k−w)=w[a(c−a)−k+w]≥w2≥0.
(23)
Thus trajectories cannot cross into h<0. The remaining boundary point (0,0) is an equilibrium point and cannot be crossed in finite time by a nonconstant trajectory, by uniqueness theorem for ordinary differential equations. This proves forward invariance of T. The argument works unchanged at c=2k​ with a=k​.
The descending unstable manifold of (k,0) initially enters T. Its forward trajectory remains in this compact region, so it exists for all positive z. Within the interior, p<0: a first contact with p=0 at 0<w<k would have p′<0, contrary to arrival from below. Hence w decreases strictly and has a limit ℓ∈[0,k). To identify the limit, its omega-limit set lies on the line w=ℓ and is invariant. Any trajectory remaining on this line has p=w′=0; its second equation then forces ℓ(k−ℓ)=0. Since ℓ<k, it follows that ℓ=0, and the only possible omega-limit set is (0,0). Backward in z, the unstable manifold tends to (k,0) by its construction. We have therefore obtained a positive decreasing heteroclinic orbit and hence a nonnegative travelling wave for every c≥2k​.
For completeness, the threshold is necessary for such a front. When 0<c<2k​, put ν=k−c2/4​, X=w, and Y=(p+cw/2)/ν. Near the zero equilibrium point the system becomes
X′=−2c​X+νY,Y′=−νX−2c​Y+νX2​.
(24)
For a nonzero trajectory its polar angle satisfies
ϑ′=X2+Y2XY′−YX′​=−ν+ν(X2+Y2)X3​=−ν+O(X2+Y2​).
(25)
A trajectory approaching zero therefore makes infinitely many rotations and must cross X=w=0. It cannot be a nonnegative front. At c=0, the first integral
E=21​p2+2k​w2−31​w3
(26)
is conserved. It has values k3/6 and 0 at the two desired end states, so no heteroclinic orbit connects them. Consequently
nonnegative invasion fronts exist exactly for c≥c∗​=2k​.​
(27)
This is the phase-plane construction of a Fisher travelling front, including the critical speed. The statement for all c≥0 must distinguish these nonconstant fronts from the two constant travelling waves.
