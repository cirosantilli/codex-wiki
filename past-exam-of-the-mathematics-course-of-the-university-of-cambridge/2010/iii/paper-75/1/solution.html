Use the elementary topos definition: a category with finite limits, exponential objects, and a subobject classifier. The indexing category C is assumed small, as usual, and C=[Cop,Set] denotes its presheaf category. A finite limit of categorical presheaves is formed objectwise: at c take the corresponding limit of sets, and apply the universal property to induce restriction maps. The terminal categorical presheaf is the constant singleton, so this supplies all required finite limits.
For categorical presheaves A,B, write yc=C(−,c) and define
(BA)(c)=Nat(yc×A,B).
(1)
For h:d→c, restriction is precomposition with yh×1A​. Evaluation sends (θ,a) at c to θc​(1c​,a). Given ϕ:H×A→B, its transpose sends x∈H(c) to the natural transformation whose component at d is
(h:d→c,a∈A(d))⟼ϕd​(H(h)x,a).
(2)
Naturality of ϕ proves this is a natural transformation; naturality in c proves it is a map H→BA. Conversely evaluation recovers ϕ, and these constructions are inverse. Therefore
C(H×A,B)≅C(H,BA),
(3)
so the presheaf category is Cartesian closed.
Define Ω(c) to be the set of sieves on a category with codomain c. For h:d→c, let Ω(h)S=h∗S={k:e→d:hk∈S}, and let ⊤:1→Ω select the maximal sieve at each object. For a subobject U↪X, define its characteristic map by
χc​(x)={h:d→c:X(h)x∈U(d)}.
(4)
The restriction closure of U makes this a sieve on a category, and the formula commutes with pullback of sieves on a category, so χ is natural. It is maximal exactly when 1c​ belongs to it, equivalently when x∈U(c); hence the pullback of ⊤ along χ is U. Uniqueness is also explicit: naturality forces h∈χc​(x) exactly when the restriction X(h)x lies in the truth pullback at d. Thus the sieve presheaf is a subobject classifier, and every small presheaf category is an elementary topos.
