A coherent category is a regular category with finite unions of subobjects, including the empty union, stable under pullback. Thus it has finite limits, pullback-stable regular-epimorphism/monomorphism image factorizations, and pullback-stable finite joins in each subobject lattice.
Write U=A∨B↪X and C=A∧B=A×X​B. Their inclusions give a commutative square
C↓A​⟶⟶​B↓U.​
(34)
To establish the pushout property, take arrows a:A→Y, b:B→Y agreeing on C. Regard their graphs as subobjects of U×Y and form their union R. Internally, this relation says that a point of U is related to its a-value when it lies in A, or to its b-value when it lies in B.
The relation is total because every point of U belongs to at least one of A,B. It is single-valued: two witnesses in the same summand give the same value by functionality of that map; witnesses in different summands have their shared point in C, where the two maps agree. By the relation argument proved in Question 7's unheaded continuation, the projection R→U is both a regular epimorphism and monic, hence an isomorphism. It consequently gives a map h:U→Y restricting to a,b.
For uniqueness, the equalizer of two possible maps contains A and B, hence contains their union U and is all of U. Thus the square is a pushout, proving
A∨B≅A⨿A∧B​B​.
(35)
This is the unions of subobjects are pushouts property; it does not assume arbitrary pushouts already exist in a coherent category.
