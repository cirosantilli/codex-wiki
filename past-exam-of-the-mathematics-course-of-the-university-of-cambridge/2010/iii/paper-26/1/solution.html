In particular, p is unramified precisely when m has no repeated irreducible factor. The exclusion of primes dividing the index is essential.
Here is the quotient-ring proof of the Kummer-Dedekind theorem. The finite group OK​/A has order coprime to p. Localizing at the integers coprime to p therefore gives A⊗Z(p)​=OK​⊗Z(p)​, and reduction yields
OK​/pOK​≃A/pA≃Fp​[X]/(m).
(3)
The second isomorphism follows from A≃Z[X]/(m). The Chinese remainder theorem gives
OK​/pOK​≃∏j​Fp​[X]/(gjej​​).
(4)
Each factor is a local ring with unique maximal ideal generated by gj​ and residue field Fp​[X]/(gj​). Pulling its maximal ideal back to OK​ gives exactly pj​; these exhaust the primes above p. This proves the asserted residue degrees and their independence of the chosen lifts.
It remains to prove the exponents rather than simply identify the primes. Unique prime ideal factorization in the Dedekind domain OK​ writes (p)=∏j​pjbj​​. At pj​, its discrete valuation ring has p=uπbj​, with u a unit. Thus the maximal ideal of OK,pj​​/pOK,pj​​ has nilpotence index of an ideal bj​: its bj​th power is zero and its (bj​−1)st is nonzero. In Fp​[X]/(gjej​​) this index is ej​, since gjej​​=0 but gjej​−1​=0. The displayed product isomorphism identifies these local factors, hence bj​=ej​. This completes the theorem's proof. The same local argument works over a base number field whenever OK​[α] equals the full relative ring of integers after localizing at the chosen base prime.
For the required cyclotomic field, the allowed ring of integers formula makes the index one. Its cyclotomic polynomial is

The quartic over F2​ has no linear factor. Its only possible irreducible quadratic divisor would be X2+X+1, whose roots have order three, whereas its own roots have order five. Hence it is irreducible. The two quartics over F5​ are also irreducible: their roots have multiplicative order sixteen because their fourth powers are 2 or 3, both of order four; the finite-field Frobenius automorphism has orbit length four on an element of order sixteen, since 54≡1(mod16) and neither 5 nor 52 is 1 modulo sixteen.
Consequently the prime ideal factorization above 2 has one prime with ramification index eight and residue degree four. Above 5 it has two primes, each with ramification index four and residue degree four. In particular,
The ramified rational primes are exactly 2 and 5.​
