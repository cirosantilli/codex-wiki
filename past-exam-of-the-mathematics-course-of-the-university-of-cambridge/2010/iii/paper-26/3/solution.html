The three quadratic subfields are Q(−2​), Q(−3​) and Q(6​), with respective fundamental discriminants −8,−3,24. Their associated quadratic Dirichlet characters are the Kronecker symbols
χ−8​(n)=(n−8​),χ−3​(n)=(n−3​),χ24​(n)=(n24​).
(19)
The regular representation of C2​×C2​ is the direct sum of the trivial character and these three nontrivial characters. At an unramified prime the character is the sign by which Frobenius automorphism acts on the square root defining its quadratic subfield; at a ramified prime the linear character has no inertia group invariants. The local factor of an Artin L-function consequently gives
L(ρ,s)=ζ(s)L(χ−8​,s)L(χ−3​,s)L(χ24​,s).
(20)
The zero values of a quadratic Dirichlet character at conductor primes mean that its local factor is one, not that the whole Artin L-function vanishes there.
Only the rational primes 2,3,5,7 can contribute to coefficients with index at most ten. Their character values are
p2357​χ−8​(p)01−1−1​χ−3​(p)−10−11​χ24​(p)001−1​Lp​(ρ,s) with T=p−s(1−T2)−1(1−T)−2(1−T2)−2(1−T2)−2​​
(21)
For the odd unramified primes these entries follow by checking squares: modulo five, both −8 and −3 are the nonsquare 2, while 24 is the square 4; modulo seven they are respectively 6,4,3. At 3, −8≡1 is a square. At 2, the −3 character is −1 because the integral quadratic generator has polynomial X2+X+1, irreducible modulo two. The other entries at 2 and 3 are zero exactly where the corresponding fundamental discriminant is divisible by that prime. This verifies every local factor, including the ramified ones.
The Euler product makes an​ a multiplicative arithmetic function. Expanding the first two factors gives
(1−T2)−1=1+T2+T4+⋯,(1−T)−2=1+2T+3T2+⋯.
(22)
Therefore a2​=0, a4​=1, a8​=0, a3​=2 and a9​=3. Both factors for 5 and 7 have zero coefficient of T, so a5​=a7​=0. Multiplicativity gives a6​=a2​a3​=0 and a10​=a2​a5​=0, while a1​=1. The answer is
(a1​,…,a10​)=(1,0,2,1,0,0,0,0,3,0)​.
(23)
As an arithmetic check, the regular representation Artin L-function is the Dedekind zeta function of F. The local factors say there is one prime of norm four above two and two primes of norm three above three. Their powers give respectively one ideal of norm four and three ideals of norm nine, matching the nonzero coefficients above.
