Write G=Gal(F/K) and
Dq​={σ∈G:σq=q}.
(9)
Every member of this decomposition group preserves OF​ and q, so reduction defines a homomorphism
Dq​⟶Gal(kq​/kp​).
(10)
Its kernel is the inertia group. We prove surjectivity of decomposition group reduction without presupposing a lift of Frobenius.
If the two residue fields coincide, surjectivity is immediate. Otherwise choose a nonzero a∈kq​ generating it over kp​; a generator exists by the primitive element theorem for finite fields. Use the Chinese remainder theorem in OF​ to choose a whose residue is a at q and zero at every other prime above p. Consider
P(T)=∏σ∈G​(T−σa).
(11)
Its coefficients are integral and fixed by G, hence lie in OK​: an element of K integral over OK​ belongs to this integrally closed ring of integers.
Reduce at q. When σ∈/Dq​, the chosen residue of a at σ−1q is zero, so σa reduces to zero at q. When σ∈Dq​, its residue is σ(a), which is nonzero. Therefore
P(T)=T∣G∣−∣Dq​∣∏σ∈Dq​​(T−σ(a)).
(12)
Although this product is written in kq​[T], its coefficients belong to kp​. Put q=∣kp​∣. The finite-field Frobenius automorphism b↦bq therefore permutes its nonzero roots. In particular, aq is σ(a) for some σ∈Dq​. Since a generates kq​, that σ agrees with Frobenius on the whole field. The Galois group of a finite field extension is cyclic and generated by Frobenius, so its generator lies in the image. Thus
Dq​↠Gal(kq​/kp​),Dq​/Iq​≃Gal(kq​/kp​)​.
(13)
