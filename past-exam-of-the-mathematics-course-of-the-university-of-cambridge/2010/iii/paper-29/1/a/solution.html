Set hn​=2−n and tn​=hn​⌈t/hn​⌉. Replace the last sampled value Xtn​​ by Xt​, and call the resulting absolute-increment sum Wn​(t). This is a sum over a genuine partition of an interval of [0,t], and
∣Vtn,∣⋅∣​−Wn​(t)∣≤∣Xtn​​−Xt​∣⟶0
(1)
by right continuity. This is the dyadic approximation of total variation. Let V(t) be the total variation of a function X on [0,t], allowing +∞. Every Wn​(t) is at most V(t). Conversely, take any finite partition of an interval of [0,t]. Approximate its interior points from the right by dyadic grid points, retaining the endpoint t. For large n these approximating points remain distinct, and right continuity makes their absolute-increment sum converge to that of the original partition. The triangle inequality makes Wn​(t) at least this selected sum. Taking the supremum over all partitions proves
n→∞lim​Vtn,∣⋅∣​=V(t),V(t)=Var[0,t]​X∈[0,∞].​
(2)
The limit is the total-variation function; for locally finite variation it is the total-variation process.
There is a missing hypothesis in the assertion about being càdlàg. A general càdlàg function can have infinite total variation of a function arbitrarily close to zero. For example,
X0​=0,Xs​=ssin(1/s)(s>0)
(3)
is continuous. At the successive points sj​=(π/2+jπ)−1 its values alternate in sign with magnitude sj​. The variation along these points dominates a divergent harmonic series. Thus V(t)=∞ for every t>0, but V(0)=0, so even extended-valued right continuity fails at zero.
The correct càdlàg conclusion holds when X has finite variation on every compact interval. Indeed V is then finite and nondecreasing, so it has finite left limits. To prove right continuity at t, fix s>t and choose a partition of [t,s] whose sum is within ε of its variation. If u>t lies before the first interior partition point, changing the first endpoint from t to u changes that sum by at most ∣Xu​−Xt​∣. Additivity of total variation of a function therefore gives
0≤V(u)−V(t)≤ε+∣Xu​−Xt​∣.
(4)
Let u↓t and then ε↓0. This proves right continuity. This proves càdlàg regularity of finite total variation. Under that qualification, the jump formula is
ΔVt​=∣ΔXt​∣=∣Xt​−Xt−​∣(t>0).​
(5)
If the variation is infinite, a difference of two infinite variation values is not a defined jump.
