Put Lr​=θTr​​. The hitting times Tr​ are finite almost surely by recurrence of planar Brownian motion, and increase with r. At Tr​, the position is e−reiLr​. By the Strong Markov property, Brownian scaling, and rotational invariance of planar Brownian motion, the future process
Bu​=ere−iLr​BTr​+e−2ru​
(53)
is a standard planar Brownian motion started at 1, independent of FTr​​. The random rotation does not change its conditional law, since that law is rotationally invariant.
The first time this rescaled process reaches radius e−h corresponds to the original time Tr+h​. Its continuous argument starts at zero and equals the increment of the original winding angle. Hence
Lr+h​−Lr​ is independent of FTr​​ and has the law of Lh​.​
(54)
For any ordered finite collection of levels, apply this assertion successively. Earlier winding values are measurable at the current hitting time, so all increments are independent and their laws depend only on the level differences. This proves the Lévy process property of winding at logarithmic radial passage levels.
One can also verify stochastic continuity and identify this process. The logarithmic-radius and argument martingales have the common clock
Ht​=∫0t​∣Bs​∣−2ds.
(55)
The same Cauchy-Riemann equations calculation as in part (a), applied locally to the logarithm and joined along the continuous argument, gives independent Brownian motions U,V such that logRt​=UHt​​ and θt​=VHt​​. This clock diverges: if it had a finite limit, convergence of martingales with finite total quadratic variation would make logRt​ converge to a finite value, forcing the clock integral itself to diverge.
Thus, with ηr​=inf{u:Uu​=−r}, we have Lr​=Vηr​​. The Brownian first-passage Laplace transform is Ee−qηr​=e−r2q​. It follows either from the Exponential martingale for Brownian motion stopped at ηr​∧n, or from the one-dimensional exit equation. Conditioning on the independent process U gives
EeiλLr​=Ee−λ2ηr​/2=e−r∣λ∣.​
(56)
So this is a symmetric Cauchy process, with level parameter r. The formula implies stochastic continuity at zero and, by stationary increments, everywhere. Under the usual definition requiring càdlàg paths, use the right-continuous passage clock ηr+​=inf{u:Uu​<−r}. It agrees with ηr​ for each fixed level almost surely, and Vηr+​​ is the càdlàg modification. This distinction concerns simultaneous passage levels and does not alter any increment law.
