Let π be pressure divided by the reference density and b the buoyancy perturbation. The linear, inviscid Boussinesq equations about U(z) are
ut​+Uux​+U′w=−πx​,wt​+Uwx​=−πz​+b,bt​+Ubx​+N2w=0,ux​+wz​=0.
(14)
For a stationary Fourier component eikx, incompressibility gives u=iw′/k. The horizontal momentum equation then gives π=i(U′w−Uw′)/k, while buoyancy conservation gives b=iN2w/(kU). Substitute these into the vertical momentum equation to obtain the stationary Taylor–Goldstein equation:
w′′+(U2N2​−UU′′​−k2)w=0.
(15)
Hence its local WKB approximation is
m2=(k2U2N2​−k2UU′′​−1)k2.​
(16)
For U′′ negligible, an outgoing upward wave has mU/k>0. In particular m>0 for k,U>0. In an upper evanescent region choose the branch with Imm>0 in ei∫mdz so the solution decays upward. These choices express the radiation condition; a reflected wave has the opposite real branch and downward group velocity.
There are two different limiting levels. A critical layer in a shear flow occurs at U=0, where the intrinsic frequency vanishes, ∣m∣→∞, and upward propagation slows while the vertical wavelength contracts. Dissipation or nonlinear effects can absorb the wave and transfer its momentum to the mean flow. For a stable, slowly varying critical layer with Richardson number Ri=N02​/(U′)2>1/4, the usual causal inviscid continuation suppresses transmission; it is not the same as reflection at m=0. When the Richardson number is small, a general absorption-only conclusion needs extra stability assumptions.
Reflection instead occurs at the turning level of a stationary internal wave k∣U∣=N0​, beyond which m2<0. It is sometimes called a critical reflection height, but the wind there is nonzero. Assuming 0<kU0​<N0​, the specified profiles give
U=U0​(1+z/H):U=U0​(1−z/H):​reflection at ζ=H(2πU0​N0​L​−1),critical-layer absorption at ζ=H.​​
(17)
The decreasing-wind profile never encounters m=0 before its zero-wind layer. Both profiles have U′′=0; a well-separated WKB description requires N0​H/U0​≫1, and a turning-layer transition is still required for the increasing profile.
