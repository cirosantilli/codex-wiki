Assume κ,ω>0 and impose upward group velocity in z>0. The two positive-frequency components then have wave vectors k1​=(κ,m) and k2​=(−κ,m) with m<0. Equal lengths are automatic, and orthogonality gives m2=κ2. Hence
m=−κ,ω=N/2​.​
(43)
The given orthogonal-wave condition is therefore possible only at this forcing frequency. The upward radiation condition excludes the m=+κ branch.
Use superposition with vertical amplitude W in each component and internal-wave polarization:
w1​u1​​=Wcos(κx−κz−ωt)+Wcos(−κx−κz−ωt)=2Wcos(κx)cos(κz+ωt),=Wcos(κx−κz−ωt)−Wcos(−κx−κz−ωt)=2Wsin(κx)sin(κz+ωt).​
(44)
The two sets of crests have slopes +1 and −1; their energy propagates upward and outward at angles of 45∘, while their phases travel downward. Their interference makes fixed horizontal bands of vertical-velocity nodes, at cosκx=0, and horizontal-velocity nodes, at sinκx=0.
Put A=κx, B=κz+ωt. The first-order displacement relative to mean parcel labels is
ξ1​=−ω2W​sinAcosB,ζ1​=ω2W​cosAsinB.
(45)
The horizontal displacement correction vanishes identically, while
ξ1​w1x​+ζ1​w1z​=ω4κW2​(sin2Acos2B−cos2Asin2B).
(46)
Consequently the formal cross-wave Stokes drift is
us​=(0,−ω2κW2​cos(2κx)).​
(47)
It has alternating downward and upward columns, no depth decay in this ideal infinite plane-wave field, and zero horizontal-period-averaged vertical transport. Each wave alone has zero drift; the interference terms create this result.
This calculation is a kinematic second-order consequence of the linear field, not a complete prediction of mean transport. In particular, an impermeable oscillating lower boundary cannot have that nonzero mean normal parcel velocity. Its first-order displacement is ζb​=(2W/ω)cos(κx)sinωt. Expanding the exact kinematic boundary condition on z=ζb​ gives
w2​(x,0,t)=u1​(x,0,t)∂x​ζb​−ζb​∂z​w1​(x,0,t),⟨w2​(x,0,t)⟩=ω2κW2​cos(2κx).
(48)
This Eulerian mean velocity cancels the computed normal Stokes drift at the material boundary. Away from it, the second-order mean-flow and buoyancy equations must also be satisfied. Thus the alternating drift is a valid formal cross-wave effect, but treating it alone as persistent transport with zero Eulerian mean would be physically incomplete. A prescribed permeable boundary would require its own second-order flux specification instead.
