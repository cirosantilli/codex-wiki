Write a qubit density operator as ρ=(I+r⋅σ)/2, with Bloch vector ∣r∣≤1. The depolarizing channel sends r to pr. Its output eigenvalues are (1±∣p∣∣r∣)/2, so its Von Neumann entropy is minimized at ∣r∣=1. Define
q=(1−p)/2​.
(17)
The minimum output entropy is h(q), since binary entropy is symmetric under q↦1−q. The usual probabilistic-mixture convention has 0≤p≤1. The argument also holds throughout the physical qubit CPTP range −1/3≤p≤1, where this q still lies in [0,1].
To handle multiple channel uses, let a classical message x select a product input ρx​=⨂j=1n​ρxj​ with probability πx​. Memorylessness gives product outputs σx​=⨂j​Φ(ρxj​). Additivity of Von Neumann entropy on tensor products gives S(σx​)≥nh(q). The average output σˉ=∑x​πx​σx​ acts on n qubits, so S(σˉ)≤log2​(2n)=n. Thus the Holevo quantity obeys the product-input block bound for a qubit depolarizing channel
χ=S(σˉ)−∑x​πx​S(σx​)≤n[1−h(q)].
(18)
The Holevo bound says the classical mutual information obtained by any measurement, including a joint measurement on all outputs, is at most χ.
For a reliable code with M equiprobable messages and decoding error Pe​, Fano's inequality gives H(X∣X^)≤h(Pe​)+Pe​log2​(M−1). Consequently (1−Pe​)log2​M≤n[1−h(q)]+h(Pe​). Letting the error tend to zero proves I(Φ)≤1−h((1−p)/2)​.
The bound is tight: equal use of ∣0⟩,∣1⟩ and computational-basis output measurements produce a binary symmetric channel with crossover probability q. Ordinary classical coding achieves 1−h(q) bits per use.
