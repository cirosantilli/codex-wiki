Take the boundary to be horizontal, put z=0 on it, and let z=h(x,t) be the interface. Write Δρ=ρ−ρ2​>0. Under the long-thin hydrostatic approximation, the deep ambient fluid has pressure p2​=p∗​−ρ2​gz to leading order, with negligible horizontal pressure gradient. Matching pressure at the interface gives, inside the dense porous gravity current,
p=p∗​−ρgz+Δρgh(x,t),px​=Δρghx​.
(1)
The Darcy velocity is superficial volume flux per total area, not pore speed. The horizontal Darcy law and depth-integrated flux therefore give
uD​=−μkΔρg​hx​,q=∫0h​uD​dz=−μkΔρg​hhx​.
(2)
The porosity ϕ multiplies storage, so mass conservation is ϕht​+qx​=0. Consequently
ht​=λ(hhx​)x​,λ=ϕμkΔρg​.​
(3)
The deep-ambient and long-thin assumptions are what permit neglecting the ambient return-flow pressure variation, vertical inertia, and nonhydrostatic pressure corrections.
For a localized finite release, first use the reflecting half-line x≥0, so hx​(0,t)=0. Let S=V/ϕ be the conserved cross-sectional area of the current; V here is actual pore-fluid volume per unit cross-flow distance. A similarity solution of the porous medium equation has thickness proportional to t−α and extent proportional to tβ. Conservation gives α=β, and balancing ht​ against (hhx​)x​ gives α+2β=1. Thus α=β=1/3. Put

Here [a]+​=max(a,0). Its Darcy flux vanishes at the moving front because h=0 there, and its physical volume is ϕ∫0L​hdx=V. This is the exact concentrated-release Barenblatt solution of the depth equation, physically used after the short initial adjustment to a long-thin current; an arbitrary finite initial patch requires its initial profile as well as its volume, although the same profile describes the usual late spreading regime. If a line release spreads symmetrically in both directions and V denotes the total volume, use the same parabola on −L<x<L with L3=9λVt/(2ϕ). Let L be the streamwise evolution scale, δ the jet width, and Uc​ its velocity scale. A slender jet has ε=δ/L≪1; mean incompressibility gives V=O(εUc​). Both mean advection terms are then O(Uc2​/L). For turbulent parcels crossing a transverse distance l, retention of their original streamwise momentum gives a fluctuation u^∼−lUy​; a comparable transverse fluctuating speed gives the down-gradient mixing length closure
u^v^=−l2∣Uy​∣Uy​=−νT​Uy​,νT​=l2∣Uy​∣.
(23)
The mixing length is an effective distance of momentum retention, with statistical coefficients absorbed into l; it is not the net transverse displacement of a mean streamline. This closure models the Reynolds stress rather than deriving it exactly.
In a slender equilibrium jet, the fluctuation magnitude inferred from momentum balance is u′2∼Uc2​δ/L=εUc2​. The transverse Reynolds stress gradient then has size u′2/δ∼Uc2​/L, whereas the streamwise normal-stress gradient is u′2/L∼εUc2​/L. At sufficiently high Reynolds number, molecular transverse diffusion is smaller provided
Uc2​/LνUc​/δ2​=ReL​ε21​≪1,ReL​=νUc​L​.
(24)
Streamwise molecular diffusion is smaller still by ε2. A free jet has ambient mean pressure constant to leading order; transverse momentum corrections imply only a higher-order mean streamwise pressure force. Thus high speed must be accompanied by the slender-jet ordering, not used alone to discard every other term.
For a symmetric jet decreasing away from the centre, Uy​<0 on y>0 and Uy​>0 on y<0. Accordingly u^v^=sgn(y)l2(Uy​)2 off the centreline, and the leading equation is
UUx​+VUy​=−∂y​u^v^=−sgn(y)∂y​{l2(Uy​)2}.​
(25)
At the centreline the signed form requires the stress to tend continuously to zero; otherwise differentiation would add a distributional term. The undifferentiated Reynolds stress form is the appropriate global equation.

The proposed mixing length can be motivated as a local phenomenological estimate: on either half of a self-similar jet, the centreline supplies a distinguished reference plane, and an eddy at distance ∣y∣ might retain momentum over a fixed fraction C1​∣y∣ of that transverse distance. This gives
l=C1​∣y∣.​
(26)
It is compatible with the dilation x↦ax, y↦ay and introduces no independent outer length. In the turbulent plane jet similarity regime its typical value at ∣y∣∼δ is C1​δ, so the scaling from part (b) has δ/L∼C12​.
This argument is a modeling assumption, not a physical necessity: a jet centreline is not a wall that prevents turbulent eddies from crossing it. Thus a mixing length vanishing there cannot automatically be justified throughout the core. Parts (e) and (f) exhibit the precise difficulty: the resulting finite-velocity similarity profile has singular centreline shear and zero shear at a finite free edge, contradicting the printed edge definition. The local dimensional motivation does not make all the stipulated global jet properties consistent.

First derive the formal turbulent plane jet similarity equations, then check the printed edge conditions. Define a streamfunction by U=ψy​, V=−ψx​, so mean incompressibility is automatic. Momentum conservation gives Uc2​δ∼M0​. With l=C1​∣y∣, the closure balance Uc2​/x∼C12​Uc2​/δ gives δ∝x. Hence Uc​∝M01/2​x−1/2 and the consistent self-similar ansatz is
ψ=M01/2​x1/2f(η),η=y/x.​
(30)
An odd f gives symmetric U and antisymmetric V. Direct differentiation gives
U=M01/2​x−1/2f′,V=M01/2​x−1/2(ηf′−f/2),Uy​=M01/2​x−3/2f′′.
(31)
The advection and Reynolds stress terms become
UUx​+VUy​=−2x2M0​​{(f′)2+ff′′},−∂y​u^v^=−x2C12​M0​​sgn(η)[η2(f′′)2]′.
(32)
Thus the two required consistency equations, the first for η>0 and the second for a symmetric finite-width profile, are
21​(ff′)′=C12​[η2(f′′)2]′,2∫0ηw​​(f′)2dη=1.​
(33)
The second is momentum normalization; it is the equation referenced as (2) in the PDF, although its display in part (d) has no printed number. Use an infinite upper limit for a decaying profile of infinite support. Symmetry sets f(0)=0. A finite centreline velocity and continuous zero centreline stress give the integrated first equation
ff′=2C12​η2(f′′)2.​
(34)
These equations establish the similarity reduction. They also give a direct finite-edge stress condition for a mixing-length jet. At a finite edge ηw​>0, U=0 requires f′(ηw​)=0. For finite f(ηw​), the integrated equation forces f′′(ηw​)=0. A nontrivial decreasing velocity profile has nonzero shear somewhere inside, so zero edge shear cannot be its maximum. Allowing a one-sided nonzero edge shear instead produces nonzero edge stress and an unbalanced jump in the conservative momentum equation.
For a concrete diagnostic, take f′=1−η2 on 0≤η≤1, so f=η−η3/3. It has zero edge velocity and its largest one-sided shear magnitude at the edge, matching that descriptive condition. Yet at η=1 the integrated equation has left-hand side zero and right-hand side 8C12​>0: such a profile is not a solution of the stated momentum closure.
The centreline is also problematic for a smooth interpretation. If f′(0)=a>0, then f∼aη, and the decreasing branch of the integrated equation gives
f′′∼−2​C1​η​a​,f′=a−C1​2​a​η​+o(η​).
(35)
A continuous formal profile can have this cusp, but it is not a differentiable symmetric velocity field, and molecular diffusion cannot be neglected uniformly in that core. Therefore there is no nontrivial regular similarity solution satisfying all the printed edge requirements. The boxed consistency equations are valid; the additional coincidence of a zero-velocity edge with maximum shear is not. A consistent jet model must relax that edge definition and regularize the core or use a different mixing length.

To connect the prescribed interfacial drag coefficient to the energy supply, state the lumped interfacial power model for entrainment explicitly:
PI​=SτuI​=cD​ρl​SuI3​,E˙P​=CW​PI​.

For stable shear-driven turbulence, let P be the rate of production of turbulent kinetic energy by mean shear and Bm​>0 the rate consumed by buoyancy work. The Flux Richardson number is
Rif​=PBm​​.​
(51)
With an upward coordinate and vertical turbulent buoyancy flux w′b′, Bm​=−ρl​∫w′b′dV, while shear production is P=−ρl​∫u′w′∂z​UdV. In a locally steady balance without net turbulent-energy transport or storage, P=Bm​+D, so 0≤Rif​≤1 and the dissipation-normalized mixing coefficient is Bm​/D=Rif​/(1−Rif​). A global ratio CW​=E˙P​/PI​ need not equal this local production-normalized Flux Richardson number when energy transport, storage, or bulk acceleration is important.
The interfacial Richardson number in this tank is
RiI​=uI2​g′dI​​=huI2​BdI​​.
(52)
In model S,
RiI​=Ω2R2hBdI​​∝h−1.​
(53)
Thus the relative importance of interface stratification changes continually. If mixing efficiency depends on interfacial Richardson number, constant CW​ is not generally self-consistent over substantial deepening; it can at best describe a regime where that dependence is weak. In model K,
RiI​=CI2​CK2​Ω2R2h0​BdI​​=constant.​
(54)
A local efficiency depending only on this ratio can therefore remain constant, provided the fixed interface thickness and the other empirical coefficients remain appropriate.
The energetic reason to expect intermediate behavior is that entrainment must both lift and homogenize dense fluid and mobilize it. The whole upper-layer kinetic energy budget has the form Plid​=K˙+E˙P​+D, apart from additional boundary losses. Keeping the characteristic speed constant makes K∝h and requires a continuing power supply to accelerate new fluid, together with dissipation in an ever larger layer. Keeping K constant instead forces the speed to decline as h−1/2. A finite lid forcing plausibly produces an initial regime near the former and a deeper regime near the latter, rather than indefinite constant-speed entrainment.
One can make this expected bracket quantitative after matching the models' initial interface speed u0​. Suppose the actual speed lies between u0​h0​/h​ and u0​, and the same local coefficients are used. Set D0​=2CW​cD​u03​/B. Then
D0​(h0​/h)3/2≤h˙≤D0​.
(55)
Integrating these inequalities gives
h0​(1+2h0​5D0​t​)2/5≤h(t)≤h0​+D0​t.​
(56)
Equivalently, if K∝hq with 0≤q≤1, the local power closure yields a late-time depth exponent 2/(5−3q) between 2/5 and 1. This is the intended energetic interpolation between K and S, not a universal bound following from the printed assumptions alone. Different efficiencies or unmatched initial coefficients can invalidate pointwise ordering; quantitative prediction requires the lid-to-interface power transfer and the actual energy budget.
