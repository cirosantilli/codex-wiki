For an orthonormal wavelet construction, set
ϕj,k​(x)=2j/2ϕ(2jx−k),ψj,k​(x)=2j/2ψ(2jx−k),k∈Z.
(26)
The scaling function ϕ generates the approximation spaces Vj​, while ψ generates the detail spaces Wj​ in the multiresolution analysis, with Vj+1​=Vj​⊕Wj​. At coarse resolution zero, the family {ϕ0,k​}k​∪{ψj,k​}j≥0,k​ is an orthonormal basis of L2(R).
For the given square-integrable function the coefficients and wavelet series are
a0,k​=∫R​m(x)ϕ0,k​(x)dx,bj,k​=∫R​m(x)ψj,k​(x)dx,
(27)
m=k∈Z∑​a0,k​ϕ0,k​+j=0∑∞​k∈Z∑​bj,k​ψj,k​.​
(28)
For real wavelets these are the real L2 inner products; use complex conjugates for a complex basis. The series converges in the L2 norm, and its coefficients satisfy the Parseval identity for a Hilbertian basis
∥m∥22​=∑k​∣a0,k​∣2+∑j≥0,k​∣bj,k​∣2.
(29)
An L2 hypothesis alone does not assert pointwise convergence.
The resolution-J wavelet projection is the orthogonal projection onto VJ​:
PJ​m=k∑​a0,k​ϕ0,k​+j=0∑J−1​k∑​bj,k​ψj,k​=k∑​aJ,k​ϕJ,k​,aJ,k​=⟨m,ϕJ,k​⟩.​
(30)
The two expressions coincide by iterating Vj+1​=Vj​⊕Wj​. Orthogonality gives ∥m−PJ​m∥22​=∑j≥J,k​∣bj,k​∣2, which tends to zero. One can equivalently write the projection with kernel AJ​(x,y)=∑k​ϕJ,k​(x)ϕJ,k​(y), interpreted as the same orthogonal projection.
In fixed-design nonparametric regression, the observations satisfy
Yi​=m(xi​)+εi​,xi​ deterministic,Eεi​=0,Var(εi​)=σ2,​
(31)
with independent errors. In the usual Gaussian version εi​∼N(0,σ2). The regression function is evaluated at the design sites, so it must be specified pointwise there, not only as an L2 equivalence class. For xi​=i/n, the observations concern m on [0,1]. They cannot identify its values outside this interval. Use an interval-adapted wavelet basis on [0,1], consisting of coarse functions ϕ0,k​ and detail functions ψj,k​; a Haar wavelet basis is one explicit choice.
Here is a wavelet regression estimator valid for every n. Divide [0,1] into cells Ii​=((i−1)/n,i/n], with the value at zero assigned arbitrarily, and form the observed step function Yn​(t)=Yi​ on Ii​. For any normalized basis function bλ​, estimate its coefficient by
cλ​=∫01​Yn​(t)bλ​(t)dt=∑i=1n​wλi​Yi​,wλi​=∫Ii​​bλ​(t)dt.
(32)
The wavelet regression estimator at resolution J=Jn​ is therefore
mJ​(t)=k∑​a0,k​ϕ0,k​(t)+j=0∑J−1​k∑​bj,k​ψj,k​(t)=PJ​Yn​(t).​
(33)
Only finitely many interval basis functions occur. Its expectation is PJ​mngrid​, where mngrid​ equals m(i/n) on Ii​. Thus approximation of m includes the resolution bias and the grid discretization bias. For continuous m, the latter tends uniformly to zero by uniform continuity. No smoothness or consistency claim for arbitrary pointwise representatives of L2 functions is needed to define the estimator.
The common point-evaluation version replaces each weight by bλ​(i/n)/n, giving
a0,k​=n1​∑i​Yi​ϕ0,k​(i/n),bj,k​=n1​∑i​Yi​ψj,k​(i/n).
(34)
These are Riemann-sum approximations to the coefficient integrals. For Haar wavelets, n=2L, and J≤L, they equal the integrated-weight coefficients exactly if the dyadic cells and pointwise basis versions are chosen right-closed. Each retained basis function is then constant on every sampling cell. This gives a concrete implementation by the discrete wavelet transform. A sensible finest resolution has 2J≤n; beyond the grid scale there are details the data cannot resolve.
For wavelet coefficient thresholding, retain the coarse coefficients but discard detail coefficients dominated by noise. A hard-threshold version is
mJthr​=k∑​a0,k​ϕ0,k​+j<J,k∑​bj,k​1{∣bj,k​∣>τj,k​}​ψj,k​.​
(35)
Alternatively, soft thresholding replaces each detail coefficient by sgn(b)(∣b∣−τ)+​. The unthresholded wavelet regression estimator retains all details at the chosen resolution; thresholding retains evidence for local features while suppressing small fluctuations.
The thresholds can be derived from a simultaneous wavelet coefficient noise bound. Write qλ2​=∑i​wλi2​. Independence gives
Var(cλ​)=σ2qλ2​,qλ2​≤n1​∫01​bλ​(t)2dt=n1​,
(36)
where the inequality applies the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality on each cell of length 1/n. In the dyadic Haar implementation, qλ2​=1/n exactly. For Gaussian errors, the normalized coefficient noise is standard normal. More generally, independent centred sub-Gaussian random variables with parameter σ give
P(∣cλ​−Ecλ​∣>t)≤2exp(−2σ2qλ2​t2​).
(37)
If NJ​ detail coefficients are considered, a union bound consequently justifies
τλ​=σqλ​2log(2NJ​/δ)​.​
(38)
With probability at least 1−δ, every retained detail's noise is below its corresponding threshold; independence between the coefficient errors is unnecessary. With NJ​ of order n and qλ​=n−1/2, these thresholds have the familiar order σlogn/n​. Coefficients larger than their noise level represent potential signal, and a coefficient zero in the discretized mean is not selected on this simultaneous event. If σ is unknown, a consistent noise-scale estimate can be substituted; for Gaussian errors and predominantly negligible finest-level signal, the median absolute finest-level coefficients divided by the standard-normal absolute median estimates σ/n​. Without a Gaussian or sub-Gaussian tail assumption, finite variance alone does not justify the logarithmic thresholds: Chebyshev inequality and the union bound instead permit τλ​=σqλ​NJ​/δ​.
