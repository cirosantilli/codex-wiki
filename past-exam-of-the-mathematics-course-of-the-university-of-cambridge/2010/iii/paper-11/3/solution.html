The printed statement needs A=∅: for the empty set every linear subspace has cardinality at least one, so the requested bound is impossible. We prove the intended nonempty version of the Freiman-Ruzsa theorem over a finite field. Necessarily C≥1, because any one translate of A inside A+A has size ∣A∣.
We first prove all sumset estimates needed below. For finite nonempty X and finite U,V in an abelian group, choose one representation z=uz​−vz​ for each z∈U−V. The map
(U−V)×X⟶(U+X)×(V+X),(z,x)⟼(uz​+x,vz​+x)
(20)
is injective: subtracting the outputs recovers z, hence its chosen representation and then x. This proves the Ruzsa triangle inequality
∣U−V∣≤∣X∣∣U+X∣∣V+X∣​.
(21)
Choose nonempty X⊆B minimizing c′=∣X+B∣/∣X∣≤C, where B is any set with ∣B+B∣≤C∣B∣. We prove the Petridis minimal-growth lemma ∣X+B+D∣≤c′∣X+D∣ for every finite D. List D={d1​,…,ds​}, and let Xi​ consist of those x∈X for which x+di​ was not already in X+{d1​,…,di−1​}. The disjoint pieces Xi​+di​ partition X+D.
Every (X∖Xi​)+B+di​ was already covered in X+B+{d1​,…,di−1​}. The new part at step i therefore has size at most
∣X+B∣−∣(X∖Xi​)+B∣≤c′∣X∣−c′∣X∖Xi​∣=c′∣Xi​∣.
(22)
Here minimality of X applies to every nonempty subset X∖Xi​, and the empty case has size zero. Summing proves the lemma. Iteration gives ∣X+kB∣≤Ck∣X∣. Apply the Ruzsa triangle inequality with U=kB, V=ℓB to obtain
∣kB−ℓB∣≤Ck+ℓ∣X∣≤Ck+ℓ∣B∣.
(23)
This derives the required Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. In characteristic two, subtraction is addition, so ∣rB∣≤Cr∣B∣ for every positive integer r.
Fix a∈A and translate to B=A+a, which contains zero, has size ∣A∣, and has the same doubling constant. Choose m=⌈log2​(2∣12B∣)⌉. A uniformly random linear map L:F2N​→G′=F2m​ kills each specified nonzero vector with probability 2−m. The union bound shows that some L satisfies
kerL∩12B={0},∣G′∣=2m<4∣12B∣≤4C12∣B∣.
(24)
Because 0∈B, all lower iterated sumsets lie in 12B. In particular L is injective on B, since the difference of two elements belongs to 2B. For B′=L(B), the density α=∣B′∣/∣G′∣ is at least 1/(4C12). This proves injective linear modelling of a small sumset.
We next prove the Finite-field Bogolyubov lemma in this model. Write f=1B′​ and use normalized real Fourier coefficients on a finite abelian group f​(ξ)=Ex​f(x)(−1)ξ⋅x. Let
S={ξ:∣f​(ξ)∣≥α3/2/2​},W={w:ξ⋅w=0 for every ξ∈S}.
(25)
By Parseval identity, ∣S∣≤2α−2. Thus W is a vector subspace of codimension at most D=⌈32C24⌉.
For w∈W, Fourier inversion of the fourfold normalized convolution gives
f∗f∗f∗f(w)=∑ξ​f​(ξ)4(−1)ξ⋅w.
(26)
All terms with ξ∈S are nonnegative, and the zero-frequency term is α4. The absolute contribution from the remaining frequencies is at most
∑ξ∈/S​∣f​(ξ)∣4≤2α3​∑ξ​∣f​(ξ)∣2=2α4​.
(27)
Hence the convolution is positive and W⊆4B′. Also ∣W∣≥2−D∣G′∣≥2−D∣B∣.
For each w∈W, choose its lift σ(w)∈4B. It is unique because two lifts differ by an element of 8B∩kerL, which is zero. For w1​,w2​∈W, the discrepancy
σ(w1​)+σ(w2​)+σ(w1​+w2​)
(28)
belongs to 12B∩kerL, so it vanishes. Also σ(0)=0. Therefore σ is a linear map and its image W⊆4B is a vector subspace with ∣W∣=∣W∣≥2−D∣B∣. This establishes lifting a subspace through a Freiman model without assuming that arbitrary choices of preimages preserve addition.
Finally, B+W⊆5B is a union of W-cosets. The number of these cosets is at most
t=∣W∣∣B+W∣​≤C52D.
(29)
Choose representatives b1​,…,bt​ from B. Then B⊆W+{b1​,…,bt​}, so adjoining these representatives and a to W gives a vector subspace V containing A. Over F2​, adjoining each vector multiplies the size by at most two. Since ∣W∣≤∣4B∣≤C4∣B∣, the Freiman-Ruzsa bound from Bogolyubov and linear modelling yields
∣V∣≤K(C)∣A∣,K(C)=C421+⌈C52⌈32C24⌉⌉.​
(30)
This bound depends only on C, as required, and every sumset estimate used in its derivation has been proved.
