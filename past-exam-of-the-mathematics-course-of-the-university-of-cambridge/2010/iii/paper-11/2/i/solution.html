For disjoint nonempty vertex sets U,W, define their edge density of a bipartite graph by d(U,W)=e(U,W)/(∣U∣∣W∣). They form an ε-regular pair of vertex sets if every X⊆U, Y⊆W with ∣X∣≥ε∣U∣, ∣Y∣≥ε∣W∣ satisfies ∣d(X,Y)−d(U,W)∣≤ε.
The Szemerédi regularity lemma asserts that, given ε>0 and a positive integer m0​, there are M,n0​ such that every graph on n≥n0​ vertices has a set partition V0​,V1​,…,Vk​ with
m0​≤k≤M,∣V0​∣≤εn,∣V1​∣=⋯=∣Vk​∣,

and at most εk2 irregular ordered pairs (Vi​,Vj​) with 1≤i=j≤k.
Assume 0<ε≤1/2, which suffices since a smaller regularity parameter gives the claim for a larger one. Treat the exceptional vertices as singleton cells in the full partition P, and use equitable regularity energy
q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∣A∣∣B∣d(A,B)2.
(11)

q(P′)−q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∑A′⊆AB′⊆B​​∣A′∣∣B′∣(d(A′,B′)−d(A,B))2≥0.

For every irregular ordered pair of nonexceptional cells, choose witnesses Xij​,Yij​ of relative sizes at least ε and density discrepancy greater than ε. Split each Vi​ along all witnesses involving it; symmetry allows at most one witness for each other cell, hence at most 2k atoms in each Vi​. In the witness rectangle, the weighted Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the variance identity contributes more than ε4∣Vi​∣∣Vj​∣/n2.

This operation is a further refinement, so the energy does not decrease. There are at most k2k atoms, and fewer than ℓ leftover vertices in each, giving at most k2kℓ≤n2−k new exceptional vertices.
Here is a uniform bound ensuring that the iteration is legitimate. Put R=⌈4ε−5⌉ and choose
k0​≥max{m0​,⌈log2​(2R/ε)⌉,2}.

At every refinement the number of nonexceptional cells does not decrease: each old cell loses at most L2−k≤L/2 vertices and is cut into pieces of size at most L/4k. Thus each of the at most R rounds adds at most εn/(2R) exceptional vertices.
When L≥2⋅4k, the new cell count is at most
ℓn​≤L2n4k​≤4k4k,

This completes the energy-increment proof of Szemerédi regularity, including the equal-size and exceptional-set conditions.
