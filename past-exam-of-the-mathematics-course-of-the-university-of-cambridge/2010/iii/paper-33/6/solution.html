In this maximization the conditional distribution defining the expectation is held at the old parameter; it is not recomputed as ϑ varies. The Jensen inequality gives
logL(ϑ)−logL(ϑ(t))≥Q(ϑ∣ϑ(t))−Q(ϑ(t)∣ϑ(t)),

by writing the likelihood function ratio as a conditional expectation of f(x,Z;ϑ)/f(x,Z;ϑ(t)). Hence an exact M-step does not decrease the observed-data likelihood function. The algorithm does not in general guarantee convergence to its global maximum; initialization and the likelihood function's geometry matter.
