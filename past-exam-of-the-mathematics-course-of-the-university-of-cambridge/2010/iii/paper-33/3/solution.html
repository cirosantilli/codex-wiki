Choose a proposal probability density function g and a finite envelope constant M such that f(y)≤Mg(y) wherever the target has positive density. A rejection sampling trial draws Y∼g and, independently, U∼Uniform(0,1); accept Y when
U≤Mg(Y)f(Y)​,
(18)
otherwise repeat with fresh independent draws. For any measurable set D,
P(Y∈D,accept)=∫D​g(y)Mg(y)f(y)​dy=M1​∫D​f(y)dy.
(19)
In particular P(accept)=1/M, so the conditional accepted density is f. More explicitly, summing over the possible first accepted trial gives
∑k≥1​(1−1/M)k−1M1​∫D​f(y)dy=∫D​f(y)dy.
(20)
Thus the algorithm terminates almost surely and its output has exactly the requested law.
The envelope constant satisfies M≥1 and is the expected number of proposal trials for one output, by the geometric distribution of the waiting time. This is the quantity often called the rejection rate M. The actual probability of rejecting a particular proposal is 1−1/M, and the expected number of rejections before one acceptance is M−1; these conventions should not be conflated.
