The chiral superfield condition is Dˉα˙​Φ=0​ for both dotted components. Use left Grassmann derivatives with the displayed supersymmetric covariant derivatives. The odd product rule gives ∂(θσμθˉ)/∂θˉα˙=−(θσμ)α˙​, so
Dˉα˙​yμ=0,yμ=xμ+iθσμθˉ,Dˉα˙​=−∂θˉα˙∂​​y,θ​.
(13)
The chirality constraint therefore says that Φ is independent of θˉ at fixed y. The Grassmann algebra has only two independent θ components, so every cubic product of them vanishes. Its general even scalar superfield consequently has a scalar coefficient, a term linear in θ with a Weyl spinor coefficient, and one quadratic term. Choosing the conventional component normalization gives the chiral-superfield component expansion
Φ(y,θ,θˉ)=ϕ(y)+2​θαψα​(y)+(θθ)Faux​(y)​.
(14)
Here ϕ is a complex scalar field, ψ an odd Weyl spinor and Faux​ a complex nonpropagating auxiliary field. Conversely this expansion is annihilated by Dˉ because y and θ are. At fixed ordinary x, the same result is the terminating translation Φ=eiθσμθˉ∂μ​[ϕ(x)+2​θψ(x)+(θθ)Faux​(x)]; this retains the printed contraction convention without changing the order in the barred square.
For a local function of scalar superfields, initially allow f(Φ,Φ†). The chain rule gives
Dˉα˙​f=fΦ​Dˉα˙​Φ+fΦ†​Dˉα˙​Φ†=fΦ†​Dˉα˙​Φ†.
(15)
For arbitrary chiral component fields the last factor is not constrained to vanish, so chirality requires fΦ†​=0. Thus f must be holomorphic in the chiral fields​ on its domain, with no generic conjugate-field dependence. Its ordinary holomorphic derivatives must exist where its nilpotent expansion is evaluated; a pole or branch point there does not define a regular local superfield. Using (θψ)2=−21​(θθ)(ψψ) gives the explicit holomorphic closure of chiral superfields:
f(Φ)=f(ϕ)+2​θf′(ϕ)ψ+(θθ)[f′(ϕ)Faux​−21​f′′(ϕ)ψψ].
(16)
All three component coefficients are evaluated at y. For several chiral superfields, replace f′ψ by fi​ψi​ and f′′ψψ by fij​ψi​ψj​. This requirement concerns a function that is to be chiral for arbitrary field configurations; a special constant field configuration does not impose the same functional constraint.
The ungauged two-derivative supersymmetric action is obtained from a real Kähler potential and a holomorphic superpotential:
L=∫d2θd2θˉK(Φ,Φ†)+[∫d2θW(Φ)+h.c.]​.
(17)
The first integral is a D-term; the second is an F-term. The Kähler metric Kijˉ​​ must be positive for positive scalar kinetic energy. The auxiliary part is Kijˉ​​Fi​Fˉjˉ​​+Wi​Fi​+Wjˉ​​Fˉjˉ​​, so eliminating it gives the scalar potential V=Kijˉ​Wi​Wjˉ​​ in the bosonic sector.
For the single-field Wess–Zumino model, take the canonical Kähler potential K=Φ†Φ and spacetime signature (+−−−). Component superspace integration gives
L=∂μ​ϕ∗∂μϕ+iψˉ​σˉμ∂μ​ψ+Faux∗​Faux​+[W′(ϕ)Faux​−21​W′′(ϕ)ψψ+h.c.].
(18)
Since W′=mϕ+gϕ2 and W′′=m+2gϕ, the auxiliary equation is Faux​=−(mϕ+gϕ2)∗. Completing its square leaves −∣W′∣2 in the Lagrangian. Hence the tree-level effective potential is
Vtree​(ϕ,ϕ∗)=∣mϕ+gϕ2∣2​,Vtree​=∣m∣2∣ϕ∣2+m∗gϕ∗ϕ2+mg∗ϕ(ϕ∗)2+∣g∣2∣ϕ∣4.
(19)
For nonzero m,g, its two zero-energy supersymmetric vacua are ϕ=0 and ϕ=−m/g. The potential is nonnegative because it is an absolute square.
The Trilinear scalar vertices in the Wess–Zumino model come from
L3​=−m∗gϕ∗ϕ2−mg∗ϕ(ϕ∗)2.
(20)
With all field legs incoming, expanding eiS and differentiating each cubic monomial gives
Vϕϕϕ∗​=−2im∗g,Vϕ∗ϕ∗ϕ​=−2img∗​.
(21)
The factor 2! counts the two identical legs; there is no 1/2! in L3​ to cancel it. A factor enforcing four-momentum conservation is implicit. These labels specify fields in an all-incoming Feynman rule; physical outgoing particles are assigned by crossing. There are no ϕ3 or (ϕ∗)3 scalar vertices from this on-shell potential.
Equivalently, if m,g are real and ϕ=(A+iB)/2​ with canonically normalized real scalars A,B, then L3​=−mgA(A2+B2)/2​. Differentiation gives VAAA​=−32​img,VABB​=−2​img​, with vanishing AAB and BBB vertices. This is the same pair of complex-field interactions in a real basis.

Each superpotential term is neutral under the ordinary symmetry. Under the R-symmetry, both terms have charge two, which is canceled by the charge −2 of d2θ. In particular, m is R-neutral as required. The scalar, spinor and auxiliary components of Φ have R-charges 1,0,−1. These are formal symmetries when the couplings transform as spurions; replacing them by fixed nonzero numbers need not leave an actual continuous field symmetry.
A local loop-corrected Wilsonian effective action superpotential Feff​(Φ,m,g) must be holomorphic in all three chiral variables and obey the same charges. The ratio z=gΦ/m is neutral under both symmetries, while mΦ2 has the required charges and mass dimension three. Therefore, on a patch with m=0, the most general symmetry-allowed form is
Feff​=mΦ2h(mgΦ​)​,
(22)
with h holomorphic on its domain. This form follows directly for a monomial Φambgc: ordinary charge gives a−2b−3c=0 and R-charge gives a−c=2, hence a=c+2, b=1−c. Each allowed monomial is gcm1−cΦc+2 and already has mass dimension three. Charge symmetry alone leaves h undetermined.
To constrain perturbative loops, retain the original elementary chiral superfield and a nonzero infrared cutoff, rather than integrating out the entire field. A regular perturbative superpotential can be expanded about g=0, so h(z)=∑n≥0​cn​zn and
Feff​=∑n≥0​cn​gnm1−nΦn+2.
(23)
Terms with n≥2 have poles at m=0. They are excluded by regularity of the local Wilsonian coefficients in the massless limit with the infrared cutoff fixed. At g=0 the theory is Gaussian, so the quadratic coefficient remains c0​=1/2. The term linear in g is the single original cubic vertex: a diagram with three external fields and only that one interaction vertex has no internal loop. Holomorphy disallows coefficients involving g∗ or m∗, so it cannot conceal the usual nonholomorphic wave-function corrections in c1​. Matching the one-vertex term gives c1​=1/3. Thus the holomorphy argument for superpotential non-renormalization gives
h(z)=21​+31​z,Feffpert​=Wtree​​.
(24)
The infrared qualification matters: a singular one-particle-irreducible expression or the action after eliminating an entire massive multiplet is not the same local Wilsonian effective action for the retained field.
Consequently the holomorphic superpotential and its parameters m and g receive no perturbative vertex renormalization. The Kähler potential does undergo wave-function renormalization. If its quadratic term is ZΦ†Φ, then canonical normalization gives Φc​=Z1/2Φ and
mc​=m/Z,gc​=g/Z3/2​.
(25)
These canonically normalized running couplings can therefore be renormalized despite the non-renormalization theorem for the holomorphic superpotential. Loop corrections can also change the effective scalar potential through the corrected Kähler metric. The distinction between holomorphic coefficients and canonically normalized couplings is essential to answering the renormalization question.
