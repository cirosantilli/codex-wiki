Use hypercharge normalized by Qem​=T3​+Y, and express all matter fermions as left-handed Weyl spinors. Thus uc,dc,ec denote the charge conjugates of the right-handed Standard Model fermions; the superscript c is unrelated to the antiparticle marker (A). Each chiral superfield contains a complex scalar field and a Weyl spinor, while each vector superfield contains a gauge boson and a gaugino. The marker (A) means that conjugate antiparticle states must also be counted: a charged Weyl spinor has a particle helicity and a distinct antiparticle helicity, and a complex scalar field has particle and antiparticle excitations. The real adjoint gauge bosons and adjoint Majorana fermions already contain their conjugate states, so no extra marker is attached to them. For example, the charged combinations W± are both contained in the one real weak triplet. There are no right-handed-neutrino chiral superfields, no gravitino, and no propagating auxiliary fields in this field table. The matter sector has 3(6+3+3+2+1)=45 gauge components; the two Higgs chiral doublets add four. Thus the count is 2(49)+2(12)=122 bosonic and 122 fermionic on-shell states​, before mass effects are considered.
R-parity is the multiplicative discrete quantum number Rp​=(−1)3(B−L)+2s​, with baryon number B, lepton number L and spin s. Ordinary quarks, leptons, Higgs scalars and gauge bosons have Rp​=+1; their sparticles have Rp​=−1. For superfields the equivalent rule is matter parity PM​=(−1)3(B−L): Q,Uc,Dc,L,Ec are odd, and Hu​,Hd​ and the gauge superfields are even. With A⋅B=ϵab​AaBb contracting weak indices and implicit color contractions, the renormalizable R-parity-conserving MSSM superpotential is
WRPC​=(yu​)ij​Uic​(Qj​⋅Hu​)+(yd​)ij​Dic​(Qj​⋅Hd​)+(ye​)ij​Eic​(Lj​⋅Hd​)+μHu​⋅Hd​.
(1)
Signs of the three Yukawa couplings can be absorbed into their matrices. Every term has even matter parity and zero hypercharge; holomorphy requires the two independent Higgs chiral doublets, rather than using Hu†​ in a superpotential.
Conserved R-parity forces an even number of odd particles in a process whose incoming particles are all ordinary particles, so colliders produce sparticles in pairs or another even multiplicity. Their decay chains end in the stable lightest supersymmetric particle. A neutral, weakly interacting lightest supersymmetric particle, commonly a neutralino after electroweak symmetry breaking, escapes the detector and gives missing transverse momentum; it is also a possible dark matter constituent. Stability alone does not establish its relic abundance, and a charged or colored stable lightest supersymmetric particle is not the usual viable dark matter candidate. Conserved R-parity forbids the renormalizable interactions responsible for the decay below. It does not by itself forbid higher-dimensional proton-decay operators: the superpotential operators QQQL/Λ and UcUcDcEc/Λ have even matter parity. Thus the renormalizable R-parity-conserving MSSM protects the proton, while ultraviolet physics can still induce its decay.
The gauge-invariant renormalizable R-parity-violating superpotential is
WRPV​=21​λijk​(Li​⋅Lj​)Ekc​+λijk′​(Li​⋅Qj​)Dkc​+21​λijk′′​ϵabc​Uic,a​Djc,b​Dkc,c​+κi​Li​⋅Hu​.
(2)
Here λijk​=−λjik​ and λijk′′​=−λikj′′​ because of the weak and color antisymmetric contractions. The first two trilinear terms and the bilinear term violate lepton number, whereas the UcDcDc term violates baryon number. The λ,λ′,λ′′ are dimensionless and κi​ has mass dimension one.
For a concrete squark-mediated proton decay channel, take λ112′′​ and λ112′​ nonzero. Antisymmetry allows these indices: the exchanged scalar is a strange squark, not a repeated down flavor in λ′′. Two quarks in the proton undergo uR​dR​→sR∗​→e+uˉ; the spectator u combines with uˉ into the uuˉ component of π0. The baryon-violating Feynman vertex and lepton-violating Feynman vertex give p→e+π0.
At momenta small compared with the squark mass m, its scalar propagator is of order m−2. Eliminating it generates a dimension-six four-fermion proton-decay operator, schematically (ucdc)†(eu), with coefficient C∼λ112′′​∗λ112′​/m2. Consequently the dimensionless decay amplitude is of order Cmp2​, and phase space supplies one more power of mp​ to the width:
Γp​∼∣C∣2mp5​∼∣λ′λ′′∣2m4mp5​​,τp​∼∣λ′λ′′∣2mp5​m4​​.
(3)
This is a dimensional estimate; phase-space constants and hadronic matrix elements change its coefficient, not its fourth-power dependence on m. For order-one couplings and mp​∼1GeV, the stated lifetime limit is τp​≳1040×1024GeV−1=1064GeV−1. Therefore m4≳1064GeV4, giving m≳1016GeV​.
Such a mass is incompatible with the intended weak-scale MSSM explanation of the hierarchy problem: squarks that cancel the Higgs-sector sensitivity to high scales must not introduce a new enormous splitting from their light quark partners. For example, m∼103GeV with order-one couplings would give only τp​∼1012GeV−1∼10−12s. The field content alone does not mathematically prohibit extremely heavy superpartners, but that choice sacrifices the weak-scale naturalness motivation. The usual resolution is conserved R-parity, or sufficiently suppressed simultaneous baryon number and lepton number violation, rather than making the exchanged squark this heavy.
