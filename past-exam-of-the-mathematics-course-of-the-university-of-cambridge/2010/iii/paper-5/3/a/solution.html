As usual, the bounded set is assumed Lebesgue measurable and an Lq exponent is positive; the conventional Banach-space assertion concerns 1≤q<2∗=2n/(n−2). No boundary regularity of Ω is needed. Choose a smooth compactly supported cutoff η equal to one on a ball containing Ω. Weak convergence and the Uniform boundedness principle give a common H1 bound for fj​, hence also for gj​=ηfj​. These functions have a common compact support.
We prove their local L2 compactness directly. Translation and the fundamental theorem along line segments give, by density from smooth functions,
∥gj​(⋅−h)−gj​∥2​≤∣h∣∥∇gj​∥2​.
(19)
For a compactly supported mollifier ρε​, integration of this estimate gives
∥gj​∗ρε​−gj​∥2​≤Cε∥∇gj​∥2​≤C′ε.
(20)
At fixed ε, Hölder's inequality bounds ∥gj​∗ρε​∥∞​ and ∥∇(gj​∗ρε​)∥∞​ uniformly by the L2 norm of the corresponding smoothing kernels. Their supports lie in one fixed compact set, so the Arzelà-Ascoli theorem makes this smoothed family precompact in L2. The uniform O(ε) approximation makes the original family totally bounded in L2: choose a finite net for the smoothed family and add the approximation error. Completeness gives relative compactness.
Every strongly convergent subsequence of gj​ has limit ηf, since multiplication by η preserves weak L2 convergence. Consequently the whole sequence converges strongly: otherwise a subsequence with distance at least a fixed positive number would have a further convergent subsequence and contradict that unique limit. In particular ∥χΩ​(fj​−f)∥2​→0.
The critical Sobolev inequality supplies a uniform bound in L2∗. For clarity, this estimate follows from part 2(a), not from compactness at the critical exponent. For a compactly supported smooth v, integrating by parts against the vector field (x−y)/∣x−y∣n gives
∣v(x)∣≤Cn​∫∣x−y∣1−n∣∇v(y)∣dy.
(21)
Its distributional divergence is the sphere-area multiple of a point mass; applying the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality with α=1,p=2 yields ∥v∥2∗​≤C∥∇v∥2​. Density extends this to H1(Rn).
For 2<q<2∗ choose 0<θ<1 with 1/q=θ/2+(1−θ)/2∗. Hölder's inequality gives
∥χΩ​(fj​−f)∥q​≤∥χΩ​(fj​−f)∥2θ​∥χΩ​(fj​−f)∥2∗1−θ​⟶0.
(22)
For 1≤q<2, use ∥v∥Lq(Ω)​≤∣Ω∣1/q−1/2∥v∥L2(Ω)​. The same integral estimate also works for 0<q<1 if Lq is understood with its usual quasi-norm. Thus χΩ​fj​→χΩ​f strongly for every positive q<2∗. We have never asserted that multiplying by the possibly nonsmooth indicator preserves H1.
