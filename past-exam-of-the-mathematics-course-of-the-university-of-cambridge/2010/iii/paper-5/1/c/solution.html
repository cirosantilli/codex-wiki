Suppose a subsequence converged weakly in L1 to f. For each m, all sufficiently late supports lie inside B1/m​(0). Testing against every bounded measurable function supported outside that ball shows that f=0 almost everywhere there. Taking the countable union over m proves f=0 almost everywhere on Rn. But testing against the bounded constant function one gives ∫f=limj​∫fj​=1, a contradiction.
The same argument applies to every subnet whose indices tend to infinity, so the sequence has no weak cluster point in the ball and the ball is not weakly compact. It converges against smooth compactly supported tests to a point mass, which is not an L1 density. Thus boundedness in L1 does not imply the weak compactness proved in part (b); concentration is the obstruction.
