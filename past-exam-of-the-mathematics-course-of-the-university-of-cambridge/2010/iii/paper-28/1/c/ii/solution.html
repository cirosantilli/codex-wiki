Let r=q/p>1 and Hk​ be the first hitting time of level k. Nearest-neighbour steps imply SHk​​=k whenever Hk​<∞: the walk cannot overshoot its first upward crossing. On this event the stopped martingale eventually has the constant value rk. On {Hk​=∞}, it is Zn​ and tends to zero by part (i). Therefore
Zn∧Hk​​⟶rk1{Hk​<∞}​=eλk​1{Hk​<∞}​,λk​=klog(q/p).​
(8)
For every n, 0≤Zn∧Hk​​≤rk. Before the hit the walk is below k, and at the hit it equals k. The optional stopping theorem for the bounded stopping time n∧Hk​ gives EZn∧Hk​​=EZ0​=1. The dominated convergence theorem now yields 1=rkP(Hk​<∞). Consequently
P(Hk​<∞)=(p/q)k.​
(9)
The uniform bound on the stopped variables justifies the limit; applying optional stopping directly at the possibly infinite Hk​ without this argument would not be valid.
