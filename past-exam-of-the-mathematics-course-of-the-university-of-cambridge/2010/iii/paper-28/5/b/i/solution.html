Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2010 / iii / Paper 28 / 5 / b / i / Solution

Let u=ℜf, and suppose u has a local maximum at z0​∈U. Choose R>0 such that the closed disk D(z0​,R)​ lies in U and u(z)≤u(z0​) throughout it. Start planar Brownian motion B at z0​, and for 0<r<R let τr​ be its first exit from D(z0​,r).
By part (a) and Itô formula, u(Bt∧τr​​) is a local martingale; boundedness of u on the compact disk makes it a true bounded martingale. The exit time is finite almost surely. One direct check applies the same formula to ∣Bt∧τr​​−z0​∣2, giving 2E(t∧τr​)=E∣Bt∧τr​​−z0​∣2≤r2. Thus Eτr​≤r2/2.
The optional stopping theorem, first at t∧τr​ and then by bounded convergence, gives
u(z0​)=Ez0​​u(Bτr​​).
(49)
The exit point is uniformly distributed on the circle: rotational invariance of planar Brownian motion about its starting point makes its exit law rotation invariant, and normalized arc length is the unique rotation-invariant probability measure on a circle. Since every boundary value is at most u(z0​), equality of the mean forces every boundary value to equal u(z0​); continuity rules out a strict deficit at any boundary point. This holds for every 0<r<R, so u is constant on the entire disk.
The Cauchy-Riemann equations then give both partial derivatives of v=ℑf equal to zero there. Thus f is constant on that disk. The identity theorem for holomorphic functions propagates this constant to the connected component of z0​ in U. Applying the same Brownian argument to v proves the assertion for the imaginary part as well:
A local maximum of ℜf or ℑf forces f to be constant on that component of U.​
(50)
If U is connected, this is constancy on all of U. For a disconnected open set the printed global wording needs this qualification: a function taking different constants on two disjoint disks is holomorphic and has local maxima on each disk while not being globally constant.
