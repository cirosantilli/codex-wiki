For each positive integer m, apply part (i) to the grid Dm​={j/m:0≤j≤m}. Intersect the resulting probability-one events over all m, a countable intersection. Work on this common event.
For x∈[j/m,(j+1)/m], monotonicity of the empirical distribution function gives Fn​(j/m)≤Fn​(x)≤Fn​((j+1)/m). Consequently
Fn​(x)−x≤Fn​((j+1)/m)−(j+1)/m+1/m,
(13)
and
x−Fn​(x)≤j/m−Fn​(j/m)+1/m.
(14)
The endpoint x=1 is itself on the grid. Therefore
supx∈[0,1]​∣Fn​(x)−x∣≤maxt∈Dm​​∣Fn​(t)−t∣+m1​.
(15)
For each fixed m, take the upper limit as n→∞; part (i) makes the maximum vanish. Then let m→∞. This proves
x∈[0,1]sup​∣Fn​(x)−x∣⟶0almost surely.​
(16)
This is the uniform-sample case of the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem, proved by a finite-grid approximation rather than assumed.
