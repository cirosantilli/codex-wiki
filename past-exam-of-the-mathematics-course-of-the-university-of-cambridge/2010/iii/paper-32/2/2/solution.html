Set Sn​=∑i=1n​Yi​ and an​=∑i=1n​2−i=1−2−n. For the Poisson distribution observations, factors independent of θ can be removed from the likelihood function, leaving
Ln​(θ)∝θSn​e−an​θ,ℓn​(θ)=Sn​logθ−an​θ+constant.
(19)
When Sn​>0, the log-likelihood derivative is Sn​/θ−an​ and its second derivative is negative. The unique positive maximum-likelihood estimator is therefore
θn​=1−2−nSn​​(Sn​>0).​
(20)
There is a genuine boundary qualification: if Sn​=0, the likelihood decreases strictly on θ>0, so no maximum is attained in the stated open parameter space. On the closure θ≥0, its maximizer is 0. Defining the usual extended maximum-likelihood estimator by the same displayed formula for every Sn​ allows its statistical consistency to be examined; the all-zero event cannot be ignored because its probability does not tend to zero.
Indeed, sums of independent Poisson distribution variables give Sn​∼Poi(θan​), so
Pθ​(∣θn​−θ∣>θ/2)≥Pθ​(Sn​=0)=e−θan​⟶e−θ>0.
(21)
Thus the extended maximum-likelihood estimator is inconsistent. More precisely, Sn​ increases to S∞​=∑i≥1​Yi​, and ES∞​=θ<∞ shows that this total count is finite almost surely. Its distribution is Poisson distribution with mean θ, by taking limits of the finite-sum laws. Hence
θn​⟶S∞​∼Poi(θ)almost surely.
(22)
This finite-exposure Poisson inconsistency leaves a nondegenerate random limit even with infinitely many observations. Correspondingly, the total Fisher information is an​/θ→1/θ, rather than diverging. The common-distribution hypothesis in the introductory maximum likelihood estimation theorem is absent here.
