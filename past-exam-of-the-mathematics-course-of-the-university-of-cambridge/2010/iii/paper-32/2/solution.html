One sufficient maximum likelihood estimation statistical consistency theorem is the following. Let the observations be independent random variables with a common density fθ​ relative to a fixed measure, let Θ be a compact metric parameter set containing the true value θ0​, and suppose that the expected log-likelihood
m(θ)=Eθ0​​logfθ​(Y1​)
(10)
is finite, continuous, and uniquely maximized at θ0​. Assume also the uniform law of large numbers
supθ∈Θ​​n1​∑i=1n​logfθ​(Yi​)−m(θ)​P​0,
(11)
and assume that a measurable global maximum-likelihood estimator exists. Then every such maximum-likelihood estimator converges in probability to the true parameter. A useful sufficient condition for this uniform law of large numbers is almost-sure continuity of θ↦logfθ​(Y1​) on the compact parameter set and an integrable envelope supθ​∣logfθ​(Y1​)∣≤M(Y1​), Eθ0​​M(Y1​)<∞. Under these conditions, identifiability supplies uniqueness through the Kullback-Leibler divergence identity
m(θ0​)−m(θ)=DKL​(fθ0​​∥fθ​)≥0,
(12)
with equality only at θ=θ0​.
To see the statistical consistency conclusion, fix ε>0. Compactness and the unique maximum give a strictly positive separation gap
δε​=m(θ0​)−supd(θ,θ0​)≥ε​m(θ)>0
(13)
whenever the set outside the neighbourhood is nonempty. If Δn​ denotes the supremum discrepancy in the uniform law of large numbers, maximization of the empirical log-likelihood yields
0≤m(θ0​)−m(θn​)≤2Δn​.
(14)
Hence the probability of d(θn​,θ0​)≥ε is at most P(2Δn​≥δε​), which tends to zero. This is the argmin consistency under uniform convergence in probability argument applied to the negative log-likelihood. These are sufficient conditions, not necessary ones; parameter-dependent support can require a direct argument, and independent observations need not have a common distribution in other models.
