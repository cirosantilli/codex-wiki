For a finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field, basic algebra means A/J(A)≅kn, so its simple modules are one-dimensional and occur once in the semisimple quotient of the regular module. A hereditary ring has every submodule of a projective module projective; equivalently its global dimension is at most one.
Choose representatives S1​,…,Sn​ of the simple modules. Our Ext quiver convention gives one vertex i for Si​ and
#{i→j}=dimk​ExtA1​(Si​,Sj​).
(19)
With primitive orthogonal idempotents ei​, put Pi​=Aei​ and J=J(A). Applying HomA​(−,Sj​) to 0→JPi​→Pi​→Si​→0 gives
ExtA1​(Si​,Sj​)≅HomA​(JPi​/J2Pi​,Sj​).
(20)
The restriction map from Hom(Pi​,Sj​) is zero since every map to a simple kills JPi​, and Pi​ is projective. Thus the arrow multiplicity is dimej​(J/J2)ei​. This specifies the orientation rather than silently replacing the quiver by its opposite.
Because A is hereditary, JPi​ is projective. Its semisimple top has aij​ copies of Sj​, where aij​ is the arrow multiplicity. Its projective cover therefore supplies an epimorphism
⨁j​Pjaij​​↠JPi​.
(21)
This splits by projectivity of JPi​. Its kernel is a summand contained in the radical of the source; projection onto that summand and Nakayama lemma force the kernel to be zero. Hence
JPi​≅j⨁​Pjaij​​,dimPi​=1+j∑​aij​dimPj​.​
(22)
Every arrow i→j forces dimPi​>dimPj​, so Q has no directed cycles.
Choose lifts in ej​Jei​ of bases of the arrow spaces. Send the vertices and arrows of the path algebra to these idempotents and lifts, multiplying paths in the order βα for consecutive arrows α:i→j, β:j→l. This defines an algebra homomorphism kQ→A. It is surjective: vertices span A/J, arrows span J/J2, and products show that paths of length r span Jr/Jr+1. Nilpotence of J then gives all of A.
Let Ni​ be the number of directed paths starting at i, including the length-zero path. Acyclicity gives the same recursion Ni​=1+∑j​aij​Nj​, with value one at a sink. Induction from sinks gives Ni​=dimPi​. Therefore
dimkQ=∑i​Ni​=∑i​dimPi​=dimA.
(23)
The surjective homomorphism is an isomorphism. This proves the basic hereditary algebra path-algebra theorem:
A≅kQ.​
(24)
For each connected component C of the underlying unoriented graph, eC​=∑i∈C​ei​ is a central idempotent. Paths never connect distinct components, so kQ=⨁C​eC​kQ as a direct sum of two-sided ideals. To prove each is a block, let z be any central idempotent. Modulo the arrow ideal it has coordinates ci​∈{0,1}. Commuting z with an arrow i→j and reducing modulo the square of that ideal gives ci​=cj​. Thus these coordinates are constant on each component. If they are zero on a component, the restriction of z there is a nilpotent idempotent, hence zero; if they are one, apply that argument to 1−z. Consequently
blocks of A ⟷ connected components of the underlying graph of Q.​
(25)
