Use hypercharge normalized by Qem​=T3​+Y, and express all matter fermions as left-handed Weyl spinors. Thus uc,dc,ec denote the charge conjugates of the right-handed Standard Model fermions; the superscript c is unrelated to the antiparticle marker (A). Each chiral superfield contains a complex scalar field and a Weyl spinor, while each vector superfield contains a gauge boson and a gaugino. The complete propagating content of the Minimal supersymmetric Standard Model before electroweak symmetry breaking is:

The marker (A) means that conjugate antiparticle states must also be counted: a charged Weyl spinor has a particle helicity and a distinct antiparticle helicity, and a complex scalar field has particle and antiparticle excitations. The real adjoint gauge bosons and adjoint Majorana fermions already contain their conjugate states, so no extra marker is attached to them. For example, the charged combinations W± are both contained in the one real weak triplet. There are no right-handed-neutrino chiral superfields, no gravitino, and no propagating auxiliary fields in this field table. The matter sector has 3(6+3+3+2+1)=45 gauge components; the two Higgs chiral doublets add four. Thus the count is 2(49)+2(12)=122 bosonic and 122 fermionic on-shell states​, before mass effects are considered.
R-parity is the multiplicative discrete quantum number Rp​=(−1)3(B−L)+2s​, with baryon number B, lepton number L and spin s. Ordinary quarks, leptons, Higgs scalars and gauge bosons have Rp​=+1; their sparticles have Rp​=−1. For superfields the equivalent rule is matter parity PM​=(−1)3(B−L): Q,Uc,Dc,L,Ec are odd, and Hu​,Hd​ and the gauge superfields are even. With A⋅B=ϵab​AaBb contracting weak indices and implicit color contractions, the renormalizable R-parity-conserving MSSM superpotential is
WRPC​=(yu​)ij​Uic​(Qj​⋅Hu​)+(yd​)ij​Dic​(Qj​⋅Hd​)+(ye​)ij​Eic​(Lj​⋅Hd​)+μHu​⋅Hd​.
(1)
Signs of the three Yukawa couplings can be absorbed into their matrices. Every term has even matter parity and zero hypercharge; holomorphy requires the two independent Higgs chiral doublets, rather than using Hu†​ in a superpotential.
Conserved R-parity forces an even number of odd particles in a process whose incoming particles are all ordinary particles, so colliders produce sparticles in pairs or another even multiplicity. Their decay chains end in the stable lightest supersymmetric particle. A neutral, weakly interacting lightest supersymmetric particle, commonly a neutralino after electroweak symmetry breaking, escapes the detector and gives missing transverse momentum; it is also a possible dark matter constituent. Stability alone does not establish its relic abundance, and a charged or colored stable lightest supersymmetric particle is not the usual viable dark matter candidate. Conserved R-parity forbids the renormalizable interactions responsible for the decay below. It does not by itself forbid higher-dimensional proton-decay operators: the superpotential operators QQQL/Λ and UcUcDcEc/Λ have even matter parity. Thus the renormalizable R-parity-conserving MSSM protects the proton, while ultraviolet physics can still induce its decay.
The gauge-invariant renormalizable R-parity-violating superpotential is
WRPV​=21​λijk​(Li​⋅Lj​)Ekc​+λijk′​(Li​⋅Qj​)Dkc​+21​λijk′′​ϵabc​Uic,a​Djc,b​Dkc,c​+κi​Li​⋅Hu​.
(2)
Here λijk​=−λjik​ and λijk′′​=−λikj′′​ because of the weak and color antisymmetric contractions. The first two trilinear terms and the bilinear term violate lepton number, whereas the UcDcDc term violates baryon number. The λ,λ′,λ′′ are dimensionless and κi​ has mass dimension one.
For a concrete squark-mediated proton decay channel, take λ112′′​ and λ112′​ nonzero. Antisymmetry allows these indices: the exchanged scalar is a strange squark, not a repeated down flavor in λ′′. Two quarks in the proton undergo uR​dR​→sR∗​→e+uˉ; the spectator u combines with uˉ into the uuˉ component of π0. The baryon-violating Feynman vertex and lepton-violating Feynman vertex give p→e+π0.
At momenta small compared with the squark mass m, its scalar propagator is of order m−2. Eliminating it generates a dimension-six four-fermion proton-decay operator, schematically (ucdc)†(eu), with coefficient C∼λ112′′​∗λ112′​/m2. Consequently the dimensionless decay amplitude is of order Cmp2​, and phase space supplies one more power of mp​ to the width:
Γp​∼∣C∣2mp5​∼∣λ′λ′′∣2m4mp5​​,τp​∼∣λ′λ′′∣2mp5​m4​​.
(3)
This is a dimensional estimate; phase-space constants and hadronic matrix elements change its coefficient, not its fourth-power dependence on m. For order-one couplings and mp​∼1GeV, the stated lifetime limit is τp​≳1040×1024GeV−1=1064GeV−1. Therefore m4≳1064GeV4, giving m≳1016GeV​.
Such a mass is incompatible with the intended weak-scale MSSM explanation of the hierarchy problem: squarks that cancel the Higgs-sector sensitivity to high scales must not introduce a new enormous splitting from their light quark partners. For example, m∼103GeV with order-one couplings would give only τp​∼1012GeV−1∼10−12s. The field content alone does not mathematically prohibit extremely heavy superpartners, but that choice sacrifices the weak-scale naturalness motivation. The usual resolution is conserved R-parity, or sufficiently suppressed simultaneous baryon number and lepton number violation, rather than making the exchanged squark this heavy.

A BPS state is a state whose mass saturates a lower bound set by conserved charges in the Super-Poincaré algebra. Saturation leaves some linear combinations of supercharges unbroken and produces a shortened massive supermultiplet. Both statements follow from the positivity of the physical Hilbert-space norm, rather than from an assumed classical field configuration.
For four-dimensional extended supersymmetry, choose the normalization
{QαA​,Qˉ​β˙​B​}=2δAB​σαβ˙​μ​Pμ​,{QαA​,QβB​}=ϵαβ​ZAB,ZAB=−ZBA.
(4)
The conjugate algebra is obtained by Hermitian conjugation. The antisymmetry of the central charge in supersymmetry matrix follows because the anticommutator is symmetric in the combined labels (α,A) while ϵαβ​ is antisymmetric. These central charges commute with momentum and supercharges, so their eigenvalues are fixed in an irreducible massive representation. In the rest frame, Pμ​=(M,0,0,0) and {QαA​,(QβB​)†}=2MδABδαβ​.
For any operator S and physical state ∣v⟩,
⟨v∣{S,S†}∣v⟩=∥S∣v⟩∥2+∥S†∣v⟩∥2≥0.
(5)
Apply this to SαA​=QαA​−ϵαβ​UAB(QβB​)†, where U is unitary. This is the rest-frame version of the positive operator constructed from Q−Γ. Summing over spinor and internal labels gives
H=8NM−4Retr(U†Z)≥0.
(6)
The two diagonal terms each contribute 4NM, because U†U=I; each mixed term contains ∑αβ​ϵαβ2​=2. For N=2, write Z12=∣Z∣eiφ and choose U=eiφ(0−1​10​). Then H=16M−8∣Z∣, so M≥∣Z∣/2. The factor of one-half reflects the stated convention with Z, rather than 2Z, in the equal-chirality anticommutator.
To obtain the sharp general bound and exhibit the shortening, use unitary skew-diagonalization of an antisymmetric matrix to put Z into two-by-two blocks Zr​(0−1​10​), with a possible zero row and column for odd N. In one block, relabel its two internal indices 1,2 and write Zr​=∣Zr​∣eiφr​. Define
ar,1±​=2​Q11​±eiφr​(Q22​)†​,ar,2±​=2​Q21​∓eiφr​(Q12​)†​.
(7)
Direct substitution, using ϵ12​=1, gives
{ar,i±​,(as,j±​)†}=δrs​δij​(2M±∣Zr​∣),{a+,a−†}=0,
(8)
and all annihilator-annihilator anticommutators vanish. The minus operators must have nonnegative norm in every block, hence the BPS bound in supersymmetry is
M≥21​rmax​∣Zr​∣​.
(9)
Equivalently, defining zr​=Zr​/2 gives M≥maxr​∣zr​∣. Applying the positive Q−Γ construction to each block gives the same bound; applying only its full trace need not isolate the largest block. For M>0 and no central charge saturation, each operator can be normalized by 2M±∣Zr​∣​ to an ordinary fermionic annihilation operator. The unmatched internal index, if any, contributes two more such operators.
There are consequently 2N complex creation operators in a generic massive supermultiplet. Starting with a spin-j Clifford vacuum, each creation operator can be occupied zero or one times, so the long representation contains (2j+1)22N states. If precisely k central-charge blocks saturate 2M=∣Zr​∣, then the two ar,i−​ and their adjoints have zero anticommutator. The norm identity forces each of them to annihilate every physical state in that representation. Removing these 2k complex oscillators gives
dimRshort​=(2j+1)22N−2k,4k of 4N real supercharges preserved​.
(10)
Thus a massive N=2 BPS supermultiplet is half-BPS. For j=0, its four states consist of two spin-zero states and a spin-one-half doublet, instead of the sixteen states of a long representation. A charged representation generally needs its conjugate for CPT completion of a supermultiplet, doubling this four-state count; this yields the eight states of a full charged hypermultiplet. For N=4, one saturated block gives a quarter-BPS representation and two give a half-BPS representation. This rest-frame argument assumes M>0; massless multiplets follow instead from the null-momentum algebra.
The same bound has a geometric realization for solitons. Consider a static magnetic monopole in an SU(2) gauge theory with an adjoint Higgs field ϕa, asymptotic magnitude v, vanishing scalar potential and magnetic field Bia​. For magnetic charge nm​, the energy can be written
E=21​∫d3x(Bia​∓Di​ϕa)2±∫d3xBia​Di​ϕa=21​∫d3x(Bia​∓Di​ϕa)2±gYM​4πvnm​​.
(11)
The second equality uses the covariant magnetic Bianchi identity to turn the cross term into the surface integral ∮ϕaBia​dSi​. Choosing its sign proves the Bogomolny bound E≥4πv∣nm​∣/gYM​​. Equality gives the first-order Bogomolny equations Bia​=±Di​ϕa. In a suitable N=2 supersymmetric embedding, the surface charge is the appropriate central charge and the soliton obeys precisely the algebraic BPS bound in supersymmetry. Its fermionic variation vanishes for the preserved supersymmetry parameters, while the broken supercharges supply its fermionic zero modes.
Shortening is useful because an isolated short multiplet cannot continuously acquire the additional states of a long representation. While the state exists and stays short, its exact mass is determined by the exact central charge, so masses of strongly interacting solitons can be found without computing a generic interacting-particle self-energy. This does not mean that its numerical mass is independent of coupling or vacuum expectation values: those can enter the central charge itself. Supersymmetric indices and the reduced fermionic state content provide ways to count protected states, subject to possible recombination or continuum effects.
For example, in rank-one Seiberg–Witten theory the electric and magnetic charges (ne​,nm​) enter through periods a,aD​. In the normalization above, Z12=22​(ne​a+nm​aD​), so a BPS state has M=2​∣ne​a+nm​aD​∣​. A magnetic monopole can become massless where aD​=0, even though the original electric description is strongly coupled. Including this light magnetic hypermultiplet supplies a useful local description there; states with both electric and magnetic charge are dyons. Thus the protected spectrum identifies important singularities of the vacuum space and tests electric-magnetic dual descriptions.
The charge bound also controls stability. For any proposed decay with additive complex charges Z=∑i​Zi​, each daughter satisfies Mi​≥∣Zi​∣/2. A BPS parent satisfies
M=2∣∑i​Zi​∣​≤∑i​2∣Zi​∣​≤∑i​Mi​.
(12)
Energy conservation permits decay only if both inequalities are equalities: all daughters must be BPS and their nonzero complex charges must have the same phase. With nonaligned phases the parent lies strictly below the multiparticle threshold. A wall of marginal stability is a locus where such phases align, and a short bound state can disappear across it. Therefore BPS saturation protects a mass-charge relation and short representation, while stability and existence still depend on the spectrum and parameters.

The chiral superfield condition is Dˉα˙​Φ=0​ for both dotted components. Use left Grassmann derivatives with the displayed supersymmetric covariant derivatives. The odd product rule gives ∂(θσμθˉ)/∂θˉα˙=−(θσμ)α˙​, so
Dˉα˙​yμ=0,yμ=xμ+iθσμθˉ,Dˉα˙​=−∂θˉα˙∂​​y,θ​.
(13)
The chirality constraint therefore says that Φ is independent of θˉ at fixed y. The Grassmann algebra has only two independent θ components, so every cubic product of them vanishes. Its general even scalar superfield consequently has a scalar coefficient, a term linear in θ with a Weyl spinor coefficient, and one quadratic term. Choosing the conventional component normalization gives the chiral-superfield component expansion
Φ(y,θ,θˉ)=ϕ(y)+2​θαψα​(y)+(θθ)Faux​(y)​.
(14)
Here ϕ is a complex scalar field, ψ an odd Weyl spinor and Faux​ a complex nonpropagating auxiliary field. Conversely this expansion is annihilated by Dˉ because y and θ are. At fixed ordinary x, the same result is the terminating translation Φ=eiθσμθˉ∂μ​[ϕ(x)+2​θψ(x)+(θθ)Faux​(x)]; this retains the printed contraction convention without changing the order in the barred square.
For a local function of scalar superfields, initially allow f(Φ,Φ†). The chain rule gives
Dˉα˙​f=fΦ​Dˉα˙​Φ+fΦ†​Dˉα˙​Φ†=fΦ†​Dˉα˙​Φ†.
(15)
For arbitrary chiral component fields the last factor is not constrained to vanish, so chirality requires fΦ†​=0. Thus f must be holomorphic in the chiral fields​ on its domain, with no generic conjugate-field dependence. Its ordinary holomorphic derivatives must exist where its nilpotent expansion is evaluated; a pole or branch point there does not define a regular local superfield. Using (θψ)2=−21​(θθ)(ψψ) gives the explicit holomorphic closure of chiral superfields:
f(Φ)=f(ϕ)+2​θf′(ϕ)ψ+(θθ)[f′(ϕ)Faux​−21​f′′(ϕ)ψψ].
(16)
All three component coefficients are evaluated at y. For several chiral superfields, replace f′ψ by fi​ψi​ and f′′ψψ by fij​ψi​ψj​. This requirement concerns a function that is to be chiral for arbitrary field configurations; a special constant field configuration does not impose the same functional constraint.
The ungauged two-derivative supersymmetric action is obtained from a real Kähler potential and a holomorphic superpotential:
L=∫d2θd2θˉK(Φ,Φ†)+[∫d2θW(Φ)+h.c.]​.
(17)
The first integral is a D-term; the second is an F-term. The Kähler metric Kijˉ​​ must be positive for positive scalar kinetic energy. The auxiliary part is Kijˉ​​Fi​Fˉjˉ​​+Wi​Fi​+Wjˉ​​Fˉjˉ​​, so eliminating it gives the scalar potential V=Kijˉ​Wi​Wjˉ​​ in the bosonic sector.
For the single-field Wess–Zumino model, take the canonical Kähler potential K=Φ†Φ and spacetime signature (+−−−). Component superspace integration gives
L=∂μ​ϕ∗∂μϕ+iψˉ​σˉμ∂μ​ψ+Faux∗​Faux​+[W′(ϕ)Faux​−21​W′′(ϕ)ψψ+h.c.].
(18)
Since W′=mϕ+gϕ2 and W′′=m+2gϕ, the auxiliary equation is Faux​=−(mϕ+gϕ2)∗. Completing its square leaves −∣W′∣2 in the Lagrangian. Hence the tree-level effective potential is
Vtree​(ϕ,ϕ∗)=∣mϕ+gϕ2∣2​,Vtree​=∣m∣2∣ϕ∣2+m∗gϕ∗ϕ2+mg∗ϕ(ϕ∗)2+∣g∣2∣ϕ∣4.
(19)
For nonzero m,g, its two zero-energy supersymmetric vacua are ϕ=0 and ϕ=−m/g. The potential is nonnegative because it is an absolute square.
The Trilinear scalar vertices in the Wess–Zumino model come from
L3​=−m∗gϕ∗ϕ2−mg∗ϕ(ϕ∗)2.
(20)
With all field legs incoming, expanding eiS and differentiating each cubic monomial gives
Vϕϕϕ∗​=−2im∗g,Vϕ∗ϕ∗ϕ​=−2img∗​.
(21)
The factor 2! counts the two identical legs; there is no 1/2! in L3​ to cancel it. A factor enforcing four-momentum conservation is implicit. These labels specify fields in an all-incoming Feynman rule; physical outgoing particles are assigned by crossing. There are no ϕ3 or (ϕ∗)3 scalar vertices from this on-shell potential.
Equivalently, if m,g are real and ϕ=(A+iB)/2​ with canonically normalized real scalars A,B, then L3​=−mgA(A2+B2)/2​. Differentiation gives VAAA​=−32​img,VABB​=−2​img​, with vanishing AAB and BBB vertices. This is the same pair of complex-field interactions in a real basis.

Each superpotential term is neutral under the ordinary symmetry. Under the R-symmetry, both terms have charge two, which is canceled by the charge −2 of d2θ. In particular, m is R-neutral as required. The scalar, spinor and auxiliary components of Φ have R-charges 1,0,−1. These are formal symmetries when the couplings transform as spurions; replacing them by fixed nonzero numbers need not leave an actual continuous field symmetry.
A local loop-corrected Wilsonian effective action superpotential Feff​(Φ,m,g) must be holomorphic in all three chiral variables and obey the same charges. The ratio z=gΦ/m is neutral under both symmetries, while mΦ2 has the required charges and mass dimension three. Therefore, on a patch with m=0, the most general symmetry-allowed form is
Feff​=mΦ2h(mgΦ​)​,
(22)
with h holomorphic on its domain. This form follows directly for a monomial Φambgc: ordinary charge gives a−2b−3c=0 and R-charge gives a−c=2, hence a=c+2, b=1−c. Each allowed monomial is gcm1−cΦc+2 and already has mass dimension three. Charge symmetry alone leaves h undetermined.
To constrain perturbative loops, retain the original elementary chiral superfield and a nonzero infrared cutoff, rather than integrating out the entire field. A regular perturbative superpotential can be expanded about g=0, so h(z)=∑n≥0​cn​zn and
Feff​=∑n≥0​cn​gnm1−nΦn+2.
(23)
Terms with n≥2 have poles at m=0. They are excluded by regularity of the local Wilsonian coefficients in the massless limit with the infrared cutoff fixed. At g=0 the theory is Gaussian, so the quadratic coefficient remains c0​=1/2. The term linear in g is the single original cubic vertex: a diagram with three external fields and only that one interaction vertex has no internal loop. Holomorphy disallows coefficients involving g∗ or m∗, so it cannot conceal the usual nonholomorphic wave-function corrections in c1​. Matching the one-vertex term gives c1​=1/3. Thus the holomorphy argument for superpotential non-renormalization gives
h(z)=21​+31​z,Feffpert​=Wtree​​.
(24)
The infrared qualification matters: a singular one-particle-irreducible expression or the action after eliminating an entire massive multiplet is not the same local Wilsonian effective action for the retained field.
Consequently the holomorphic superpotential and its parameters m and g receive no perturbative vertex renormalization. The Kähler potential does undergo wave-function renormalization. If its quadratic term is ZΦ†Φ, then canonical normalization gives Φc​=Z1/2Φ and
mc​=m/Z,gc​=g/Z3/2​.
(25)
These canonically normalized running couplings can therefore be renormalized despite the non-renormalization theorem for the holomorphic superpotential. Loop corrections can also change the effective scalar potential through the corrected Kähler metric. The distinction between holomorphic coefficients and canonically normalized couplings is essential to answering the renormalization question.
