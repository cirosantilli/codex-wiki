Write b=g(ρ−ρ0​)/ρ0​>0 for the reduced gravity. The Boussinesq approximation requires ∣ρ−ρ0​∣≪ρ0​: replace density by ρ0​ in inertia and retain its difference in the gravitational force. A shallow-water approximation additionally requires a small aspect ratio, negligible leading-order normal acceleration and approximately hydrostatic pressure relative to the ambient fluid:
p−pambient​=ρ0​b(h−z)cosθ,0<z<h.
(1)
Incompressibility makes the normal velocity smaller than the downslope velocity by the aspect ratio.
Thinness alone does not force the downslope velocity to be uniform across the layer. The additional top-hat plume model assumes that turbulent mixing makes both u(x,t) and b(x,t) approximately uniform inside it, with a sharp transition to the ambient fluid. Then the depth-integrated volume and momentum fluxes are hu and hu2. This is the same integral closure used for a turbulent plume, applied to a shallow layer; the hydrostatic pressure term is retained because gravity has a component normal to the channel.

Assume the ambient fluid has zero downslope velocity, no bed drag, and an entrainment coefficient α>0. The entraining shallow-water layer receives volume at rate α∣u∣, but neither excess buoyancy nor downslope momentum from that ambient fluid. Consequently volume conservation, conservation of excess buoyancy, and momentum conservation give
ht​+(hu)x​(bh)t​+(bhu)x​(hu)t​+(hu2+21​bh2cosθ)x​​=α∣u∣,=0,=bhsinθ.​​
(2)
The pressure contribution is the integral of the hydrostatic pressure from part (a). The last source is the downslope component of the gravitational force.
With D=∂t​+u∂x​, the equivalent equations are
Dh+hux​=α∣u∣,Db=−hα∣u∣​b,Du+bcosθhx​+2h​cosθbx​=bsinθ−hα∣u∣u​.
(3)
The last term is the momentum required to accelerate newly entrained fluid. For variables (h,u,b), the matrix of the spatial derivatives is
A=​ubcosθ0​hu0​0hcosθ/2u​​,λ=u,u±bhcosθ​.​
(4)
Thus it is a strictly hyperbolic system for h,b>0 and 0≤θ<π/2. At θ=π/2 all three characteristic speeds coincide and the matrix is not diagonalizable. The exclusion of that angle presumes the physical range with cosθ≥0: allowing an overhanging channel with cosθ<0 would also destroy hyperbolicity.

(7)

Take U0​>0, k=2π/L>0, and represent the stationary topographic internal gravity wave by phase kx+mz. Its laboratory angular frequency is zero, and its intrinsic frequency is ω=−kU0​. The internal gravity wave dispersion relation gives
m2=U02​N02​​−k2.
(8)
The radiation condition selects m>0, since this makes the vertical group velocity upward. The lower kinematic boundary condition is w=U0​hx​, so a corresponding linear wave field is
w=kU0​h0​cos(kx+mz),ζ=h0​sin(kx+mz),
(9)
where ζ is the vertical fluid displacement. The constant-phase lines of an internal gravity wave slope down to the right, with dz/dx=−k/m.
Let K2=k2+m2. In the frame fixed to the hills the phase pattern is stationary: its phase velocity is zero, while the group velocity is
cg​=K2U0​​(k2,km).​
(10)
Energy travels up and downwind, with ray slope dz/dx=m/k. In coordinates x′=x−U0​t moving with the wind, the phase is kx′+mz+kU0​t. Thus
cp′​=−K2kU0​​(k,m),cg′​=K2U0​​(−m2,km).​
(11)
The intrinsic phase velocity is upstream and downward; the intrinsic group velocity is upstream and upward, perpendicular to the wave vector. Adding (U0​,0) to the group velocity recovers the laboratory energy ray.
Vertically propagating waves require kU0​<N0​, equivalently L>2πU0​/N0​​. At equality the vertical group velocity vanishes; above the mountain-wave cutoff the bounded response is an evanescent wave. The linearization requires small terrain slope kh0​≪1 and small wave-induced displacement gradients ∣m∣h0​≪1, often summarized by N0​h0​/U0​≪1. The model also neglects rotation, viscosity and compressibility; its scales must permit those approximations.

For a stationary component, the stationary internal-wave WKB solution has local vertical wavenumber and vertical-velocity amplitude
m2(z)=U02​N2(z)​−k2,w∼Cm−1/2(z)exp(ikx+i∫0z​m(s)ds),
(12)
with the upward branch m>0. The WKB approximation requires ∣m′∣≪m2 and sufficient smoothness, with ∣m′′∣≪∣m∣3 a useful additional criterion. The background changes little over a vertical wavelength; this excludes a turning level of a stationary internal wave.
For increasing N=N0​(1+z/H), the vertical wavenumber increases. The crest slope −k/m tends toward zero, and successive crests get closer vertically. The laboratory energy ray obeys dx/dz=k/m, so it becomes more nearly vertical as it rises. There is no upper turning level in this ideal increasing-stratification profile.
For decreasing N=N0​(1−z/H), the vertical wavenumber decreases to zero at
zt​=H(1−N0​kU0​​).​
(13)
This assumes propagation at the bottom, kU0​<N0​. The crests become more nearly vertical and the incident energy ray becomes horizontal at zt​. Above it the wave is evanescent, and the incident wave is reflected downward. The divergent WKB amplitude at m=0 is an approximation failure, resolved by an Airy function transition, rather than an infinite physical wave amplitude. The decreasing formula for N is only a positive-frequency background below z=H; the relevant turning height lies below that level.

Let π be pressure divided by the reference density and b the buoyancy perturbation. The linear, inviscid Boussinesq equations about U(z) are
ut​+Uux​+U′w=−πx​,wt​+Uwx​=−πz​+b,bt​+Ubx​+N2w=0,ux​+wz​=0.
(14)
For a stationary Fourier component eikx, incompressibility gives u=iw′/k. The horizontal momentum equation then gives π=i(U′w−Uw′)/k, while buoyancy conservation gives b=iN2w/(kU). Substitute these into the vertical momentum equation to obtain the stationary Taylor–Goldstein equation:
w′′+(U2N2​−UU′′​−k2)w=0.
(15)
Hence its local WKB approximation is
m2=(k2U2N2​−k2UU′′​−1)k2.​
(16)
For U′′ negligible, an outgoing upward wave has mU/k>0. In particular m>0 for k,U>0. In an upper evanescent region choose the branch with Imm>0 in ei∫mdz so the solution decays upward. These choices express the radiation condition; a reflected wave has the opposite real branch and downward group velocity.
There are two different limiting levels. A critical layer in a shear flow occurs at U=0, where the intrinsic frequency vanishes, ∣m∣→∞, and upward propagation slows while the vertical wavelength contracts. Dissipation or nonlinear effects can absorb the wave and transfer its momentum to the mean flow. For a stable, slowly varying critical layer with Richardson number Ri=N02​/(U′)2>1/4, the usual causal inviscid continuation suppresses transmission; it is not the same as reflection at m=0. When the Richardson number is small, a general absorption-only conclusion needs extra stability assumptions.
Reflection instead occurs at the turning level of a stationary internal wave k∣U∣=N0​, beyond which m2<0. It is sometimes called a critical reflection height, but the wind there is nonzero. Assuming 0<kU0​<N0​, the specified profiles give
U=U0​(1+z/H):U=U0​(1−z/H):​reflection at ζ=H(2πU0​N0​L​−1),critical-layer absorption at ζ=H.​​
(17)
The decreasing-wind profile never encounters m=0 before its zero-wind layer. Both profiles have U′′=0; a well-separated WKB description requires N0​H/U0​≫1, and a turning-layer transition is still required for the increasing profile.

Treat the channel as prismatic, with horizontal bottom datum z=0, uniform cross-sectional velocity, and surface height h(x,t). The area and depth-integrated hydrostatic pressure force divided by density are
A(h)=∫0h​z1/2dz=32​h3/2,P(h)=g∫0h​(h−z)z1/2dz=154​gh5/2.
(23)
The prismatic-channel shallow water equations are
At​+(Au)x​=0,(Au)t​+(Au2+P)x​=0.
(24)
Since P′(h)=gA(h), they become
ht​+uhx​+32​hux​=0,ut​+uux​+ghx​=0,c2=A′gA​=32​gh.​
(25)
Their two characteristic speeds are u±c. Using dc/dh=c/(2h) gives the Riemann invariants
dtdx​=u±c,dtd​(u±3c)=0.​
(26)
The factor 3 replaces the familiar 2 for a rectangular channel. It comes from the depth-dependent width, not from a different gravitational acceleration.

For dilute sediment volume concentration ϕ, define G=g(ρp​−ρ0​)/ρ0​. Neglecting particle volume in the fluid-volume balance,
g′=Gϕ,c=32​Gϕh​.​

This applies the Boussinesq approximation to the bulk mixture, requiring Gϕ/g≪1, and uses a deep ambient layer h≪H. A gravity-current front condition closes the unresolved nose. Write it as L˙=Fg′h​, with a prescribed order-one front coefficient F. If the chosen Froude number is instead Fr=L˙/c, then F=2/3​Fr.
Use a parabolic-channel sediment-current box model for one side of the dam: initially its length is L0​, depth h0​, and uniform concentration ϕ0​. Its conserved fluid volume is
V=32​h3/2L=32​h03/2​L0​,h=h0​(L0​/L)2/3.

For the early release, approximate the intact left half by water at rest at depth H, and the right half by a dry bed. Put c0​=2gH/3​. The parabolic-channel dry-bed dam break is a centered rarefaction wave of the u−c family, with the other Riemann invariant fixed at u+3c=3c0​. Inside the fan, ξ=x/t=u−c, so
c=43c0​−ξ​,u=43(c0​+ξ)​.
(32)
Hence
h(x,t)=⎩⎨⎧​H,H(43−x/(c0​t)​)2,0,​x≤−c0​t,−c0​t<x<3c0​t,x≥3c0​t,​u(x,t)=43​(c0​+tx​)inside the fan.​
(33)
The left edge moves into the reservoir at −c0​ and the dry front advances at 3c0​. At the failed dam h=9H/16 and u=c=3c0​/4. There is initially no finite-depth hydraulic bore into the dry bed: the depth falls continuously to zero.
On the x–t diagram, the u−c characteristic curves inside the fan are straight rays from the origin. The u+c curves obey dx/dt=3c0​/2+x/(2t), so they have form x=3c0​t+Ct​. The solution describes the period before the finite lake ends and their reflected signals matter. Immediately after failure, near the dam and very near the thin nose, strong vertical acceleration can invalidate the shallow-water approximation. The outer fan becomes appropriate when the horizontal flow scale is large compared with its depth.

Let states 1 and 2 be just left and right of a hydraulic bore moving at speed s, and let [f]=f2​−f1​. Integrating the conservative prismatic-channel shallow water equations across it gives the Rankine-Hugoniot condition
s[A]=[Au],s[Au]=[Au2+P],A=32​h3/2,P=154​gh5/2.​
(34)
Equivalently the discharge in the shock frame is common on the two sides,
j=A1​(u1​−s)=A2​(u2​−s),j2(A1​1​−A2​1​)=P2​−P1​.
(35)
Thus, for unequal positive depths,
s=A2​−A1​A2​u2​−A1​u1​​,j2=A2​−A1​A1​A2​(P2​−P1​)​.
(36)
Momentum is conserved through the bore in this ideal model, but mechanical energy is dissipated; an energy-conservation jump must not be imposed as well.
For a stationary bore with rightward flow, s=0, A1​u1​=A2​u2​>0, and the momentum jump must still hold. A compressive hydraulic jump has h2​>h1​ and
u1​−c1​>0>u2​−c2​,u1​+c1​>0,u2​+c2​>0.​
(37)
The u−c characteristic curves approach the stationary bore from both sides: upstream flow is supercritical flow, while downstream flow is subcritical flow using the local wave speed ci​=2ghi​/3​. The u+c family travels downstream on both sides. These characteristic directions accompany, rather than replace, the two stationary conservation jumps.

Let a=η0​ and χ=kx−ωt. For a deep-water gravity wave, ω2=gk and the first-order velocity potential is (aω/k)ekzsinχ. Therefore
u1​=aωekzcosχ,w1​=aωekzsinχ.
(38)
Relative to mean parcel labels, integrate at fixed zeroth-order position to obtain the oscillatory fluid displacement
ξ1​=−aekzsinχ,ζ1​=aekzcosχ.
(39)
The Stokes drift is the period mean of the displacement correction to the first-order velocity field:
us​ws​​=⟨ξ1​u1x​+ζ1​u1z​⟩=a2kωe2kz⟨sin2χ+cos2χ⟩,=⟨ξ1​w1x​+ζ1​w1z​⟩=0.​
(40)
Thus us​=η02​kωe2kz​, in the direction of propagation. A parcel samples faster forward motion near the top of its orbit and weaker backward motion lower down. This is the leading second-order drift of small-steepness waves, kη0​≪1, relative to the Eulerian mean velocity.

Choose the positive-frequency branch with k>0, let K=∣k∣, and write k=K(cosθ,sinθ). The internal gravity wave dispersion relation is ω=Nk/K=Ncosθ. The phase velocity and group velocity are
cp​=KN​(cos2θ,cosθsinθ),cg​=KN​(sin2θ,−cosθsinθ).​
(41)
They are perpendicular: the phase advances normal to the crests while energy travels along them. For a general signed k, the positive frequency is N∣k∣/K and the horizontal component of group velocity changes sign with k.
If ω>N, there is no real wave vector satisfying this relation. For prescribed real horizontal wavenumber, m2=k2(N2/ω2−1)<0, so a bounded boundary response is an evanescent wave, rather than a freely propagating plane wave.
For a single incompressible plane wave, internal-wave polarization gives u1​⋅k=0. Its oscillatory displacement ξ1​ is parallel to u1​ and therefore also perpendicular to k. Every spatial derivative of the plane-wave field is proportional to a component of k, so
(ξ1​⋅∇)u1​=0.​
(42)
There is no leading Stokes drift for that single wave. The fluid parcels oscillate along straight lines within phase planes and never sample a spatial change of the wave along their displacement. This conclusion does not prohibit cross terms between different wave vectors.

Assume κ,ω>0 and impose upward group velocity in z>0. The two positive-frequency components then have wave vectors k1​=(κ,m) and k2​=(−κ,m) with m<0. Equal lengths are automatic, and orthogonality gives m2=κ2. Hence
m=−κ,ω=N/2​.​
(43)
The given orthogonal-wave condition is therefore possible only at this forcing frequency. The upward radiation condition excludes the m=+κ branch.
Use superposition with vertical amplitude W in each component and internal-wave polarization:
w1​u1​​=Wcos(κx−κz−ωt)+Wcos(−κx−κz−ωt)=2Wcos(κx)cos(κz+ωt),=Wcos(κx−κz−ωt)−Wcos(−κx−κz−ωt)=2Wsin(κx)sin(κz+ωt).​
(44)
The two sets of crests have slopes +1 and −1; their energy propagates upward and outward at angles of 45∘, while their phases travel downward. Their interference makes fixed horizontal bands of vertical-velocity nodes, at cosκx=0, and horizontal-velocity nodes, at sinκx=0.
Put A=κx, B=κz+ωt. The first-order displacement relative to mean parcel labels is
ξ1​=−ω2W​sinAcosB,ζ1​=ω2W​cosAsinB.
(45)
The horizontal displacement correction vanishes identically, while
ξ1​w1x​+ζ1​w1z​=ω4κW2​(sin2Acos2B−cos2Asin2B).
(46)
Consequently the formal cross-wave Stokes drift is
us​=(0,−ω2κW2​cos(2κx)).​
(47)
It has alternating downward and upward columns, no depth decay in this ideal infinite plane-wave field, and zero horizontal-period-averaged vertical transport. Each wave alone has zero drift; the interference terms create this result.
This calculation is a kinematic second-order consequence of the linear field, not a complete prediction of mean transport. In particular, an impermeable oscillating lower boundary cannot have that nonzero mean normal parcel velocity. Its first-order displacement is ζb​=(2W/ω)cos(κx)sinωt. Expanding the exact kinematic boundary condition on z=ζb​ gives
w2​(x,0,t)=u1​(x,0,t)∂x​ζb​−ζb​∂z​w1​(x,0,t),⟨w2​(x,0,t)⟩=ω2κW2​cos(2κx).
(48)
This Eulerian mean velocity cancels the computed normal Stokes drift at the material boundary. Away from it, the second-order mean-flow and buoyancy equations must also be satisfied. Thus the alternating drift is a valid formal cross-wave effect, but treating it alone as persistent transport with zero Eulerian mean would be physically incomplete. A prescribed permeable boundary would require its own second-order flux specification instead.
