Use the usual asymptotic convention: the output approaches the desired m(n)-copy pure target in trace distance, and any recorded failure probability tends to zero. Define the binary entropy
h2​(x)=−xlog2​x−(1−x)log2​(1−x).
(24)
The entanglement entropies per source and target are Eψ​=h2​(s) and Eϕ​=h2​(t). The asymptotic pure-state entanglement conversion rate is
Rmax​=h2​(sin2β)h2​(sin2α)​.​
(25)
Both achievability and the upper bound matter here.
For entanglement concentration, expand n sources in their correlated computational basis. Every pair of strings of Hamming weight k has the same amplitude (1−s)(n−k)/2sk/2. Alice measures only this weight, without resolving the individual string. Bob's string has the same weight automatically. Conditional on k, the normalized state is maximally entangled on Dk​=(kn​) correlated strings. Its outcome probability is (kn​)sk(1−s)n−k, a binomial distribution. By the weak law of large numbers, k/n→s in probability; Stirling's formula then gives n−1log2​Dk​→h2​(s). A uniform Schmidt vector on Dk​ entries is majorized by a uniform vector on 2⌊log2​Dk​⌋ entries padded with zeros. Thus Nielsen's pure-state conversion theorem converts the conditional state into that many Bell pairs exactly. For any fixed δ>0, one obtains at least n(Eψ​−δ) Bell pairs with probability tending to one.
For entanglement dilution, consider the Schmidt strings of m targets with probabilities qx​. The quantum typical subspace retains strings satisfying
2−m(Eϕ​+δ)≤qx​≤2−m(Eϕ​−δ).
(26)
The retained probability Qm​ tends to one by the weak law applied to the independent variables −log2​qxj​​. The number of retained strings is at most 2m(Eϕ​+δ), because their probabilities sum to at most one. Normalize the target after truncation to those strings. Its squared overlap with the full target is Qm​→1. A uniform Schmidt vector of dimension 2L, with L=⌈m(Eϕ​+δ)⌉, is majorized by the truncated target's Schmidt vector padded with zeros: the sum of its largest k entries is at least k/2L. Hence L Bell pairs prepare the truncated target exactly by LOCC. Its trace distance from the true target is 1−Qm​​→0. Combining concentration and dilution achieves any rate R<Eψ​/Eϕ​. Choosing margins tending sufficiently slowly to zero attains the ratio as a limiting rate.
For the converse, average monotonicity of pure-state entanglement entropy follows from Concavity of Von Neumann entropy: a measurement by one party leaves the other party's reduction unchanged on average, so its average conditional entropy cannot increase. Refine local measurement records, including local discarded systems, to obtain pure output branches {pr​,∣χr​⟩} on the m output qubits of each party. Iterating the concavity argument gives
∑r​pr​E(χr​)≤nEψ​.
(27)
Let ∣Φm​⟩=∣ϕ⟩⊗m and let the squared-overlap error of the average output ρout​ be ε=1−⟨Φm​∣ρout​∣Φm​⟩→0. Define dr​=1−∣⟨Φm​∣χr​⟩∣2​, the pure-state trace distance. Concavity of the square root gives ∑r​pr​dr​≤ε​. Partial trace cannot increase trace distance, so the reduced states are also within dr​. The continuity bound for quantum conditional entropy, with trivial conditioning and local dimension 2m, implies
∣E(χr​)−mEϕ​∣≤2mdr​+g(dr​),g(d)=(1+d)h2​(1+dd​)≤2.
(28)
Consequently
nEψ​≥mEϕ​−2mε​−2,
(29)
which excludes a limiting rate exceeding Eψ​/Eϕ​.
The vanishing-error convention is essential. If exact deterministic conversion were required for every finite block, majorization would also require the largest squared Schmidt coefficient to satisfy (1−s)n≤(1−t)m, hence
nm​≤−log(1−t)−log(1−s)​.
(30)
This can be strictly smaller than the entropy ratio: at s=0.1,t=0.2, the two bounds are approximately 0.4722 and 0.6496. Thus the boxed rate answers the standard asymptotic task, rather than imposing unmentioned exact zero-error conditions on every finite block.
