An upper bound follows directly from the Schmidt-tail entanglement monotone
E2​(χ)=1−λmax​(ρAχ​),
(19)
where ρAχ​ is the reduced density matrix of a bipartite pure state. On these two-qubit states, this is the smaller squared Schmidt coefficient, so E2​(ψ)=s and E2​(ϕ)=t.
Here is the average-monotonicity argument needed for all LOCC strategies. The largest eigenvalue is convex: for any unit vector v, ⟨v∣∑r​pr​ρr​∣v⟩≤∑r​pr​λmax​(ρr​), and maximizing the left side gives the claim. Hence 1−λmax​ is concave. If Alice makes a local measurement, Bob's conditional reductions average to his original reduction. Concavity therefore gives ∑r​pr​E2​(χr​)≤E2​(χ). If Bob measures, use Alice's reductions instead. The two reductions of a pure state have the same nonzero eigenvalues by the Schmidt decomposition. Refining all measurement outcomes into individual Kraus operators keeps conditional states pure; iterating the inequality covers adaptive rounds of classical communication. The same bound persists for limits of such protocols.
If success produces the target with probability p, its contribution to the final average is pt. Failure branches have nonnegative E2​. Thus
pt≤s,pmax​≤ts​=sin2βsin2α​.​
(20)
This is a bound on arbitrary local protocols, not merely on a particular filtering measurement.
