Use mostly-plus Lorentzian signature and {γμ,γν}=2ημν, with antisymmetrized gamma products of unit weight. A Majorana spinor obeys a reality condition under charge conjugation:
ψc=Cψ​T=ψ,CγμC−1=−(γμ)T.​
(1)
A Weyl spinor instead has definite chirality,
P±​ψ=ψ,P±​=21​(1±γ5​).​
(2)
A four-dimensional Majorana spinor has four real components, while a Weyl spinor has two complex components. Charge conjugation reverses chirality in this signature, so a nonzero spinor cannot satisfy both conditions simultaneously.
For the Dirac equation (∂+m)ψ=0, the derivative term reverses chirality because γ5​ anticommutes with every gamma matrix, whereas the mass term preserves it. Projecting a purely chiral spinor equation onto its two chiralities therefore gives both ∂ψ=0 and mψ=0. Thus a nonzero four-component Weyl spinor satisfying this uncoupled Dirac equation must be massless. A Majorana spinor contains conjugate opposite-chirality components and can have a nonzero mass. Its charge-conjugation reality condition identifies particle and antiparticle operators, so a Majorana particle is its own antiparticle. A Weyl field does not by itself impose that identification.
This does not forbid writing a Majorana mass term for a neutral two-component Weyl field: such a term couples the field to its conjugate, and the resulting four-component massive field is Majorana, not a purely chiral solution of the uncoupled Dirac equation.
For the Rarita-Schwinger field, set χ=γμψμ​ and d=∂μψμ​. The antisymmetric kinetic operator is invariant under
δψμ​=∂μ​η,
(3)
because commuting derivatives contract to zero against γμνρ. Expanding its gamma product gives
Eμ=γμνρ∂ν​ψρ​=∂ψμ−∂μχ+γμ(∂χ−d).
(4)
Gamma contraction gives γμ​Eμ=2(∂χ−d), so the equation implies d=∂χ and ∂ψμ=∂μχ.
For a Fourier mode with momentum k, if k2=0 the latter identity gives k2ψμ=kμkχ. The mode is proportional to kμ and hence pure gauge. Every physical nonzero-momentum mode therefore has k2=0. For a null mode, choose an index with kμ=0. The same equation makes χ an element of the image of k, allowing a gauge choice with χ=0. The field equations then reduce to
γ⋅ψ=0,∂⋅ψ=0,∂ψμ​=0.​
(5)
Residual gauge transformations obey ∂η=0.
Take a null momentum in the third spatial direction. Residual gauge freedom and transversality remove the temporal and longitudinal vector components, leaving two transverse vector polarizations. Their helicities are ±1, while on-shell spinor helicities are ±1/2. The gamma-trace constraint removes the two combinations of total helicity ±1/2 and retains the aligned combinations of helicity ±3/2. Explicitly, in a chiral representation the transverse gamma contractions use σ1​±iσ2​, which annihilate the correspondingly aligned spin states and act nontrivially on the opposite states. Thus a Majorana Rarita-Schwinger field has two physical massless polarizations. The Majorana assumption is the one relevant to simple supergravity; a complex vector-spinor has these helicities for its particle and independent antiparticle modes.
A consistent free massive deformation is the algebraic term
Lm​=2m​ψ​μ​γμνψν​
(6)
added to a kinetic term −21​ψ​μ​γμνρ∂ν​ψρ​. Its equation is
γμνρ∂ν​ψρ​−mγμρψρ​=0.
(7)
For m=0, divergence and gamma contraction give, respectively,
γμρ∂μ​ψρ​=0,2γνρ∂ν​ψρ​−3mγρψρ​=0.
(8)
These imply the massive Rarita-Schwinger constraints χ=0 and d=0, and the remaining equation is (∂+m)ψμ​=0. Squaring gives (□−m2)ψμ​=0 in the chosen signature. The gauge symmetry is lost. In the rest frame, transversality sets the temporal component to zero; three spatial vector components times two on-shell spinor states give six states, and gamma trace removes the two spin-one-half states. The result is four massive spin-3/2 states, with spin projections ±3/2,±1/2. In supergravity the additional longitudinal states can be supplied by the super-Higgs mechanism.

Choose the Riemann curvature tensor convention
[∇μ​,∇ν​]ψ=41​Rμνab​γabψ,{γμ,γν}=2gμν,
(9)
with positive scalar curvature for a round sphere. The connection preserves the gamma matrices. If D=γμ∇μ​, its square is
D2ψ=∇2ψ+21​γμν[∇μ​,∇ν​]ψ=∇2ψ+81​γμνRμνab​γabψ.
(10)
Here ∇2 is the rough Laplacian, including the connection on the derivative index. In the product of two antisymmetric gamma pairs, the fully antisymmetric four-gamma term vanishes by the first Bianchi identity. The two-gamma contractions vanish because the Ricci tensor is symmetric, while the scalar contractions give −2R. Thus the Lichnerowicz spinor-square formula is
D2=∇2−4R​.​
(11)
Multiplying (D+m)ψ=0 by −D+m, with constant m, proves
−∇2ψ+4R​ψ+m2ψ=0.​
(12)
In Riemannian signature D in this positive-Clifford convention is formally anti-self-adjoint; the more usual self-adjoint Dirac operator iD has square −∇2+R/4. Stating this convention avoids an apparent sign conflict between the two forms of the formula.
Now let Dψ=0 on the compact Riemannian spin manifold. Use the positive-definite spinor inner product and integrate the squared equation. Integration by parts has no boundary contribution, giving
0=∫M​(⟨ψ,−∇2ψ⟩+4R​∣ψ∣2)dV=∫M​(∣∇ψ∣2+4R​∣ψ∣2)dV.
(13)
Both terms are nonnegative. Smoothness then forces ∇ψ=0 pointwise. Consequently every harmonic spinor is parallel, and R∣ψ∣2=0. A nonzero parallel spinor has constant positive norm on its connected component, so its scalar curvature vanishes there. This is compact harmonic-spinor rigidity under nonnegative scalar curvature.
The full Ricci conclusion follows from integrability, not merely from vanishing scalar curvature. Since a parallel spinor has zero curvature for its spin connection, contract its Ricci identity with γν:
0=γν[∇μ​,∇ν​]ψ=41​Rμνab​γνγabψ=−21​Rμν​γνψ.
(14)
The three-gamma term vanishes by the Bianchi identity; the remaining contractions give the displayed Ricci term. For each fixed μ, put vν​=Rμν​. Clifford multiplication twice gives
0=(vν​γν)2ψ=∣v∣2ψ.
(15)
Positive definiteness and ψ=0 imply v=0. Thus the Riemannian parallel-spinor Ricci-flatness result is
Rμν​=0.​
(16)
On a connected manifold a nontrivial parallel solution is nowhere zero, so this holds everywhere. On a disconnected manifold it holds on every component supporting a nonzero spinor; the stated nowhere-vanishing hypothesis ensures all components are covered. The positive-definite metric tensor is essential to the last inference.

Use the mostly-plus Clifford algebra and Riemann curvature tensor conventions of the preceding solutions, with e=det(eμ​a), κ2=8πG, and a Majorana gravitino. An action for minimal four-dimensional supergravity in the exact transformation normalization given is
S0​=∫d4xe[2κ2R​−2ψˉ​μ​γμνρ∇ν​ψρ​]+O(ψ4).​
(17)
The kinetic coefficient is tied to the normalization of the supersymmetry transformations: if Ψμ​=2ψμ​ and η=2ϵ, this becomes the canonical coefficient −1/2, with δeμ​a=(κ/2)ηˉ​γaΨμ​ and δΨμ​=∇μ​η/κ. Mixing those two normalizations would spoil the cancellation.
To the retained order, use the torsion-free connection. More generally the 1.5-order formalism allows its variation to be omitted after imposing its own algebraic equation; the resulting contorsion is quadratic in fermions and first affects the displayed action at quartic order. The metric tensor variation induced by the vierbein is δgμν​=4κϵˉγ(μ​ψν)​, so the Einstein-Hilbert action varies as
δSEH​=−κ2​∫eGμνϵˉγμ​ψν​+boundary.
(18)
For the fermionic action, varying both Majorana factors gives the same contribution after integration by parts and the Grassmann bilinear interchange rule. Thus
δSRS​=−κ4​∫e(∇μ​ϵˉ)γμνρ∇ν​ψρ​=κ4​∫eϵˉγμνρ∇μ​∇ν​ψρ​+boundary.
(19)
The vector-index Riemann curvature tensor contribution vanishes by the first Bianchi identity. The spin-index contribution is determined by
γρμν∇μ​∇ν​ζ=81​γρμνRμνab​γabζ=21​Gρσγσ​ζ.
(20)
For the last equality, expand the antisymmetric three-gamma/two-gamma product: the totally antisymmetric Riemann curvature tensor terms vanish by Bianchi, and the surviving terms are the Ricci contraction minus half its trace. Hence
δSRS​=κ2​∫eGμνϵˉγμ​ψν​+boundary,
(21)
which cancels the gravitational variation. Variations of vierbeins, gamma matrices and the connection in the fermionic kinetic term carry three dynamical fermions. They lie beyond this linear-fermion cancellation and are cancelled by the omitted four-fermion completion. Thus the action is invariant up to boundary terms at the order specified, rather than claiming that a truncated action is exactly invariant at every fermion order.
For the cosmological extension, add a gravitino mass parameter m and deform the derivative:
Sm​=∫e[2κ2R+6m2​−2ψˉ​μ​γμνρ∇ν​ψρ​+2mψˉ​μ​γμνψν​],δψμ​=κ1​Dμ​ϵ,Dμ​=∇μ​+2m​γμ​.​
(22)
Indeed the fermion terms combine as −2ψˉ​μ​γμνρDν​ψρ​ because γμνργν​=−2γμρ. The modified spinor connection curvature is
[Dν​,Dρ​]=41​Rνρab​γab+2m2​γνρ​,
(23)
and contraction gives
γμνρDν​Dρ​ϵ=21​(Gμν−3m2gμν)γν​ϵ.
(24)
The same action-variation cancellation therefore works with G replaced by G+Λg precisely for
Λ=−3m2.​
(25)
This is the Anti-de Sitter deformation of minimal supergravity. A cosmological term alone would leave its variation uncancelled; the mass term and the gamma shift of the gravitino transformation are both necessary. A positive cosmological constant cannot arise from this real unbroken minimal deformation.
For electromagnetism, retain N=1 and introduce an Abelian vector multiplet (Aμ​,λ), with Majorana gaugino λ. A complete quadratic-fermion extension is
Lvec​=e[−41​Fμν​Fμν−21​λˉγμ∇μ​λ−2κ​ψˉ​μ​γνργμλFνρ​],F=dA.​
(26)
The accompanying leading transformations are
δAμ​=ϵˉγμ​λ,δλ=−21​γμνFμν​ϵ.​
(27)
They preserve the rigid Maxwell-gaugino action for constant ϵ. For a local parameter, the variation contains
δLrigid​=e(∇μ​ϵˉ)jμ+boundary,jμ=21​Fab​γabγμλ.
(28)
To obtain this, vary F2, vary both gaugino factors, and use γabγμ=γabμ+gbμγa−gaμγb. The terms with ∇λ cancel between the two actions; integrating the three-gamma term uses ∇[μ​Fνρ]​=0, leaving exactly the displayed derivative of the parameter. The interaction is −κeψˉ​μ​jμ, whose variation under δψˉ​μ​=∇μ​ϵˉ/κ cancels that term.
The other linear-fermion variation is also fixed. Put F=Fab​γab and TEMμν​=FμρFνρ​−gμνF2/4. Clifford multiplication gives
FγμF=8TEMμν​γν​.
(29)
Varying λ in the gravitino-current interaction then gives 2κeTEMμν​ψˉ​μ​γν​ϵ=−2κeTEMμν​ϵˉγν​ψμ​, which cancels the Maxwell vierbein variation. This proves the required supergravity coupling of an Abelian vector multiplet through the retained order.
Beyond that order one uses a supercovariant gauge field strength, here Fμν​=Fμν​−2κψˉ​[μ​γν]​λ, and the associated higher-fermion transformation terms and four-fermion interactions. An auxiliary scalar may be retained for off-shell closure, or eliminated in this on-shell formulation. A Maxwell field without a fermionic partner cannot be coupled by merely adding −F2/4 while leaving all transformations unchanged. An alternative is an N=2 gravity multiplet, where the vector is a graviphoton and a second gravitino supplies its extra fermionic states; the explicit construction above is the minimal N=1 matter extension.

A Killing spinor is a nonzero spinor parameter for which the fermionic supersymmetry variations vanish on a bosonic background. In minimal four-dimensional supergravity without a cosmological term this means
∇μ​ϵ=0.​
(30)
It is an overdetermined equation and expresses an unbroken supersymmetry of the background. In the cosmological deformation the corresponding equation is Dμ​ϵ=0; in theories with additional bosonic fields their contributions also enter the fermion variations.
For the undeformed theory the connection preserves both the spinor adjoint and gamma matrices. Consequently the Dirac current
Kμ=ϵˉγμϵ
(31)
obeys ∇ν​Kμ​=0. In particular ∇(ν​Kμ)​=0, so K is a Killing vector, and in fact is parallel. For the real cosmological deformation, ∇μ​ϵ=−(m/2)γμ​ϵ and ∇μ​ϵˉ=(m/2)ϵˉγμ​ instead give ∇μ​Kν​=(m/2)ϵˉ[γμ​,γν​]ϵ, whose symmetric part again vanishes.
The causal property uses an ordinary commuting spinor. Take an orthonormal frame with (γ0)†=−γ0, (γi)†=γi, and ϵˉ=ϵ†γ0. Then K0=−ϵ†ϵ. Reverse its time orientation and write V=−K, so
V0=ϵ†ϵ,Vi=ϵ†αiϵ,αi=−γ0γi.
(32)
The matrices αi are Hermitian and obey {αi,αj}=2δij. For every spatial unit vector u, (ui​αi)2=1, whence ∣ui​Vi∣≤V0. Choose u along the spatial part of V to obtain
Kμ​Kμ=Vμ​Vμ=−(V0)2+∣V∣2≤0.​
(33)
A nonzero Killing spinor cannot vanish at an isolated point: its defining connection equation transports its value along every curve. Thus on a connected background this is a nonzero, nowhere-spacelike causal Dirac spinor current. The estimate does not need the Majorana condition, but it does need commuting components; Grassmann bilinears have no such ordinary positivity order.
For the positive energy theorem, take a complete asymptotically flat spacelike hypersurface with a spin structure Σ, with suitable decay, Einstein constraint equations, and matter satisfying the dominant energy condition. Assume there are no inner boundaries, or that any inner-boundary contribution has the required nonnegative sign. The auxiliary commuting spinor approaches an arbitrary constant ϵ∞​ at spatial infinity. Define the real Nester two-form
Bμν=ϵˉγμνρ∇ρ​ϵ−∇ρ​ϵ​γμνρϵ.
(34)
Antisymmetry removes second derivatives except through the Riemann curvature tensor. Differentiation and the three-gamma spinor curvature identity used above give
∇ν​Bμν=2(∇ν​ϵˉ)γμνρ∇ρ​ϵ+GμνKν​.
(35)
The first term comes from differentiating the two spinor factors. For the second term, replace ∇ν​∇ρ​ by half their commutator; its spin-curvature contraction is Gμνγν​/2 from each conjugate term. This is the reason the Einstein tensor, and hence the matter energy density, enters the boundary-charge identity.
In an orthonormal frame adapted to the future normal of Σ, put χi​=∇i​ϵ. Since γ0γ0ij=−γij,
2(∇i​ϵˉ)γ0ij∇j​ϵ=2(∑i​∣χi​∣2−​∑i​γiχi​​2).
(36)
Here ∇i​ is the spacetime spin connection pulled back to Σ, including its extrinsic curvature. Choose the auxiliary spinor to solve the elliptic Witten spinor equation
γi∇i​ϵ=0,ϵ⟶ϵ∞​.
(37)
The standard asymptotic-flatness and completeness hypotheses permit this boundary-value problem; the positivity identity also excludes a decaying homogeneous zero mode. It is this hypersurface equation that removes the negative square. A spacetime Killing spinor is not assumed for a general initial-data set.
Stokes' theorem and Gμν​=κ2Tμν​ now give
Q[ϵ∞​]=κ21​∫S∞​​B0idSi​=κ22​∫Σ​∣∇i​ϵ∣2dV+∫Σ​Tμν​nμVνdV≥0.
(38)
The dominant energy condition makes the second integrand nonnegative because n and V are future causal. Evaluating the two-form with the asymptotic spin connection identifies its surface integral with the ADM energy and momentum:
Q[ϵ∞​]=−Pμ​V∞μ​=ϵ∞†​(E1−Pi​αi)ϵ∞​.
(39)
For example its energy term uses B0i=21​(∂j​hij​−∂i​hjj​)∣ϵ∞​∣2 to first order in the asymptotic spatial metric tensor perturbation. With κ2=8πG this is exactly the ADM normalization. Since Pi​αi has eigenvalues ±∣P∣, positivity for every boundary spinor proves
E≥∣P∣≥0.​
(40)
Killing spinors describe saturation: their spatial derivative term vanishes, and the contracted matter term must also vanish for the corresponding charge to be zero. They characterize preserved supersymmetry rather than being prerequisites for positivity. Under the usual rigidity hypotheses, zero total four-momentum makes the nonnegative integral vanish for a full basis of asymptotic spinors; their parallel extensions force a vanishing Riemann curvature tensor and give the Minkowski vacuum. A single Killing spinor with null current alone does not justify asserting that every component of the total momentum vanishes.

The gravitational variable and the fields held fixed in a variation must be specified. For bosonic matter a second-order metric tensor formulation is sufficient. For spinorial matter a vierbein and a spin connection are needed to define the local Lorentz representation and its covariant derivative. “Second order” refers to a connection already expressed through derivatives of the vierbein; “first order” treats that connection as an independent variable.
For a metric tensor, a scalar field and an Abelian gauge field, consider the Einstein-Hilbert action with matter,
S=∫d4xe[2κ2R−2Λ​−21​gμν∂μ​ϕ∂ν​ϕ−V(ϕ)−41​Fμν​Fμν],F=dA.
(41)
Here e=−g​ and the connection in R is Levi-Civita. The metric tensor variation uses δe=−egμν​δgμν/2 and
δR=Rμν​δgμν+gμνδRμν​,δRμν​=∇ρ​δΓρμν​−∇ν​δΓρμρ​.
(42)
Metric compatibility makes the last two terms a divergence. Thus
δS=21​∫e[κ−2(Gμν​+Λgμν​)−Tμν​]δgμν+matter variations+boundary,Tμν​=−e2​δgμνδSmatter​​.
(43)
Stationarity for arbitrary compactly supported variations gives
Gμν​+Λgμν​=κ2Tμν​.​
(44)
Explicitly,
Tμνϕ​=∂μ​ϕ∂ν​ϕ−gμν​[21​(∂ϕ)2+V],TμνA​=Fμρ​Fν​ρ−41​gμν​F2.
(45)
Scalar field and gauge-potential variations give ∇2ϕ−V′=0 and ∇μ​Fμν=0. Their equations imply stress-energy conservation, consistently with the contracted Bianchi identity. If the boundary metric tensor is fixed instead of using compactly supported variations, add the Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary term, κ−2∫∂M​ε∂M​∣h∣​K, with the outward-normal orientation. Fixing the metric tensor alone does not otherwise remove the normal derivatives of its variation.
For spinorial matter, write gμν​=eμ​aeν​bηab​ and ∇μ​χ=∂μ​χ+ωμab​γabχ/4. In the second-order theory set ω=ω(e) before varying. A symmetrized Dirac action, in the same no-i, mostly-plus convention as the earlier solutions, is
Sχ​=∫e[−21​(χˉ​γμ∇μ​χ−(∇μ​χˉ​)γμχ)−mχˉ​χ].
(46)
Varying χˉ​ and integrating by parts gives (∇+m)χ=0; varying χ gives the adjoint equation. The spinor components held fixed in a gravitational variation are components in the Lorentz frame. Both γμ=ea​μγa and ω(e) vary with the vierbein. The former gives the canonical stress, and integrating the spin-connection variation supplies its spin-current improvement. On the Dirac equations the resulting symmetric stress tensor is
Tμνχ​=41​[χˉ​γμ​∇ν​χ+χˉ​γν​∇μ​χ],χˉ​γμ​∇ν​χ=χˉ​γμ​∇ν​χ−(∇ν​χˉ​)γμ​χ.
(47)
The off-shell expression also contains gμν​Lχ​/e; this vanishes on the matter equations for the displayed symmetrized action. Treating the coordinate gamma matrices as fixed while varying the metric tensor would miss the displayed stress. Local Lorentz invariance removes the independent antisymmetric vierbein equation, leaving the symmetric Einstein field equations with this matter source.
In a first-order vierbein formulation, ea and an antisymmetric Lorentz connection ωab are independent. Define
Ta=dea+ωab​∧eb,Rab=dωab+ωac​∧ωcb,
(48)
and use R(e,ω)=ea​μeb​νRμν​ab. The curvature form of a connection variation is δRab=Dω​δωab. For the Einstein-Hilbert term, integration by parts in form notation gives a connection equation proportional to
εabcd​Tc∧ed=0
(49)
when matter has no connection dependence. For an invertible vierbein this forces Ta=0, so ω=ω(e). The vierbein equation then becomes the second-order Einstein field equations. This applies to the scalar and Maxwell examples: use F=dA, which is independent of ω and remains gauge invariant even if torsion is present.
A Dirac field changes the connection equation. Directly varying its two covariant derivatives gives
δω​Lχ​=−8e​χˉ​{γμ,γab}χδωμab​=−4e​χˉ​γμabχδωμab​.
(50)
Thus its spin density sources totally antisymmetric torsion. This is Einstein-Cartan theory, and the connection equation is algebraic rather than a propagation equation for an extra field. To see both its solution and its effect, write
ωμab​=ω(e)μab​+Kμab​,Kabc​=ea​μKμbc​,Babc=χˉ​γabcχ.
(51)
For the totally antisymmetric component sourced by this field, the Ricci scalar is R(e,ω)=R(e)−Kabc​Kabc up to a divergence. The other irreducible contorsion components have no spinor source and their connection equations set them to zero. The auxiliary part of the Lagrangian is therefore
LK​=−2κ2e​Kabc​Kabc−4e​Kabc​Babc.
(52)
Its algebraic variation gives the algebraic torsion of a Dirac field,
Kabc​=−4κ2​Babc​,Tabc​=−2Kabc​=2κ2​Babc​.​
(53)
Here the first index of Kabc​ labels the derivative, and the torsion convention is Tabc​=Kbac​−Kcab​; specifying this convention fixes the sign. Eliminating K leaves the second-order effective action with contact interaction
Lcontact​=32eκ2​(χˉ​γabcχ)(χˉ​γabc​χ).​
(54)
It is a local axial-current four-fermion interaction. Hence first-order gravity with Dirac spin density is equivalent to torsion-free second-order gravity with this additional interaction. It is not equivalent to simply declaring the spin connection torsion-free and dropping the contact term. In the effective formulation the Einstein field equations have the stress tensor of the full effective matter action. Before eliminating the connection, the antisymmetric stress and its spin current are related by the local Lorentz Noether identity.
Finally, the 1.5-order formalism is a convenient way to vary this effective action. Let ω(e,χ) solve the algebraic connection equation and let S2​(e,χ)=S1​(e,ω(e,χ),χ). The chain rule gives
δS2​=δeδS1​​​ω​δe+δχδS1​​​ω​δχ+δχˉ​δS1​​​ω​δχˉ​+0δωδS1​​​ω​​​δω.
(55)
Thus vary as in first order, then insert the solved connection. One does not need to calculate the complicated induced connection variation. This procedure is justified by its connection equation, not by discarding that variation arbitrarily. In supergravity the gravitino sources a contorsion quadratic in fermions; substituting it generates the four-fermion terms, while the 1.5-order method simplifies the local supersymmetry cancellation through the lower orders considered above.
