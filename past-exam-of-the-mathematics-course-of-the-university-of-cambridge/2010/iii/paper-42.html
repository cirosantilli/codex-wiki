Use ημν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) and ℏ=c=1. In the interaction picture, fields evolve with the free Hamiltonian, while interactions enter the state evolution through the Dyson series. Write the Klein-Gordon field as ϕ=ϕ(+)+ϕ(−), with the positive-frequency part of a quantum field containing annihilation operators and ϕ(−)=(ϕ(+))† containing creation operators. The mode normalization in the PDF corresponds to [ap​,aq†​]=(2π)3δ3(p−q) and Ep​=∣p∣2+m2​.
The time-ordered product places fields with the latest time on the left. For two bosonic insertions,
T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}=θ(x0−y0)ϕ(x)ϕ(y)+θ(y0−x0)ϕ(y)ϕ(x).
(1)
The normal-ordered product :ϕ(x1​)⋯ϕ(xn​): instead moves every creation operator to the left of every annihilation operator, without adding the commutators generated by that rearrangement. Its vacuum expectation value is zero whenever a nonempty set of free-field factors remains.
The mode commutator gives the positive-frequency Wightman function
D+(x−y)=[ϕ(+)(x),ϕ(−)(y)]=⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩=∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​e−ip⋅(x−y).
(2)
Define the Feynman propagator here to be the two-point function itself, rather than that function divided by i. Then
ΔF​(x−y)=⟨0∣T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}∣0⟩=θ(x0−y0)D+(x−y)+θ(y0−x0)D+(y−x).​
(3)
Equivalently,
ΔF​(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+i0ie−ip⋅(x−y)​.
(4)
The positive-energy pole is below and the negative-energy pole above the real axis. Closing the energy contour integral below for positive time separation and above for negative time separation gives the two terms above. Thus this is the scalar Feynman propagator pole prescription, and (□+m2)ΔF​=−iδ4. In particular, T{ϕ(x)ϕ(y)}=:ϕ(x)ϕ(y):+ΔF​(x−y): the difference between time ordering and normal ordering is the Wick contraction.
For n free bosonic fields, the Wick theorem states
T{ϕ1​⋯ϕn​}=P∑​​(i,j)∈P∏​ΔF​(xi​−xj​)​:r∈/V(P)∏​ϕr​:,​
(5)
where P runs over all sets of disjoint pairs of indices, including the empty set, and V(P) is the set of paired indices. The empty normal-ordered product equals one. This is an operator identity for free fields, understood with smearing or a regulator when products of operator-valued distributions require it.
Here is an inductive proof of the scalar Wick theorem. Sort the arguments chronologically so that x10​ is the latest. For any remaining set I, commuting the annihilation part of ϕ1​ through the creation parts in a normal-ordered product gives
ϕ1​:∏r∈I​ϕr​:=:ϕ1​∏r∈I​ϕr​:+∑j∈I​[ϕ1(+)​,ϕj(−)​]:∏r∈I∖{j}​ϕr​:.
(6)
The creation part of ϕ1​ is already in the correct position. Every commutator on the right is a scalar, so no further operator rearrangement is needed for it. Because x1​ is latest, that commutator is ΔF​(x1​−xj​). Assume the Wick theorem for the other n−1 fields and multiply its expansion by ϕ1​. The first term of the displayed identity gives every pairing in which index 1 is unpaired; the sum gives every pairing in which 1 is paired with exactly one j. Each final partial pairing has exactly one such construction. The one-field identity supplies the base case, proving the theorem by mathematical induction. Applying time ordering restores arbitrary argument order.
Taking the vacuum expectation value retains only complete pairings. Odd free correlators vanish; for example the free scalar four-point function is Δ12​Δ34​+Δ13​Δ24​+Δ14​Δ23​. These facts explain why propagators are the edges of Feynman diagrams.
For the interacting quartic scalar field theory, the interaction Hamiltonian density is λϕ4/4!. With a single time ordering acting on external insertions and interaction vertices, the unnormalized numerator expands as
Nn​=∑v=0∞​v!(4!)v(−iλ)v​∫d4z1​⋯d4zv​⟨0∣T{ϕ(x1​)⋯ϕ(xn​)ϕ(z1​)4⋯ϕ(zv​)4}∣0⟩.
(7)
Apply the proved Wick theorem term by term. Each complete pairing connects external insertions and four-legged vertices by Feynman propagators. There are 4! assignments of four distinct incident legs at a vertex, canceling its factorial. Permuting identical integrated vertices cancels the corresponding part of v!. Permutations still leaving the labeled graph unchanged give its Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. For example,
N4,connected(1)​=−iλ∫d4z∏r=14​ΔF​(xr​−z),
(8)
whereas the two-point tadpole diagram has coefficient −iλ/2, because choosing the two vertex legs contracted with the labeled external fields gives 4⋅3 possibilities and the other two contract with each other.
Thus the position-space rules attach ΔF​ to every edge, −iλ∫d4z to every four-legged interaction vertex, and divide by the graph's Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. Fourier transformation gives the momentum-space Feynman rules:
line: p2−m2+i0i​,vertex: −iλ,loop: ∫(2π)4d4ℓ​.​
(9)
Enforce momentum conservation at each vertex, retaining the overall four-momentum delta function. External lines of these correlation functions remain unamputated. The unnormalized expression includes Vacuum Feynman diagrams; dividing by N0​=⟨0∣S∣0⟩ cancels those disconnected vacuum factors. They should not be discarded without that normalization.
There is a time-ordering convention in the printed correlation-function notation worth making explicit. The usual interacting correlator uses the single global time ordering in Nn​ above. If the displayed T{ϕ1​⋯ϕn​}S is read literally with S outside that ordering, the external block and the vertex block are separately time ordered: their cross-contractions are D+(xi​−z) because each external field stands to the left of every vertex field. The same pairing construction then uses ΔF​ for lines within either block and D+ for lines between blocks; in momentum space the latter is 2πθ(p0)δ(p2−m2). This literal product is different from the conventional globally time-ordered interacting correlator, and does not have the ordinary external Feynman propagators derived above.

The supplied gamma matrices use the mostly-minus metric signature. The Pauli matrix multiplication law gives σ1σ2σ3=iI2​, hence block multiplication yields
γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3=(−I2​0​0I2​​).​
(10)
The off-diagonal blocks of every γμ connect opposite eigenspaces of this diagonal matrix. Explicitly,
γ5γ0=(0I2​​−I2​0​)=−γ0γ5,γ5γi=(0−σi​−σi0​)=−γiγ5.
(11)
Thus {γ5,γμ}=0, and (γ5)2=I4​. The chiral projectors (1∓γ5)/2 select the upper and lower two-component blocks, respectively.
Write the Dirac spinor as ψ=(χL​,χR​)T. Substituting the block matrices into the massless Dirac equation gives two independent Weyl spinor equations:
i(∂t​−σ⋅∇)χL​=0,i(∂t​+σ⋅∇)χR​=0.​
(12)
They are decoupled because the mass term, which would join the two chirality components, is absent.
For a left-handed plane wave, use p⋅x=p0t−p⋅x. Its coefficient ξ obeys
(p0I2​+σ⋅p)ξ=0.
(13)
Since (σ⋅p)2=∣p∣2I2​, the determinant is (p0)2−∣p∣2. A nonzero coefficient therefore requires the massless mass shell p2=0​. For positive energy and nonzero p, put P=∣p∣=p0 and parameterize its direction by angles ϑ,φ. Then
σ⋅p​=(cosϑeiφsinϑ​e−iφsinϑ−cosϑ​),χ−​=(−e−iφsin(ϑ/2)cos(ϑ/2)​).
(14)
Multiplication gives (σ⋅p​)χ−​=−χ−​ and χ−†​χ−​=1. Hence the left-handed massless plane-wave spinor can be written
λL​(p)=N(p)​−e−iφsin(ϑ/2)cos(ϑ/2)00​​,ψL​(x)=λL​(p)e−ip⋅x.​
(15)
Here N(p) is an arbitrary complex normalization; ∣N∣=2P​ gives λL†​λL​=2P. Away from the negative z axis one can equivalently take
χ−​=2P(P+pz​)​1​(−px​+ipy​P+pz​​).
(16)
At p=−Pz choose (1,0)T instead. These are phase patches for the same one-dimensional kernel. The other frequency branch p0=−P uses the positive eigenvector χ+​=(cos(ϑ/2),eiφsin(ϑ/2))T for the direction of p. At pμ=0, the algebraic equation vanishes and any two-component coefficient is possible, but no momentum direction or helicity is defined there.
A spatial rotation by α about p​ acts on the two-component coefficient by exp(−iασ⋅p​/2). The negative eigenvector therefore acquires the phase eiα/2. Since a state with helicity h acquires e−iαh, the positive-energy left-handed particle has h=−1/2​. There is only one such spin state at a fixed nonzero momentum, rather than two independent polarizations of the same chiral particle.
The helicity content of a quantized Weyl field also includes an antiparticle with opposite helicity. In the field expansion, the negative-frequency term multiplies an antiparticle fermionic creation operator, while the positive-frequency term multiplies a particle fermionic annihilation operator. Their adjoint roles give conjugate phases for the corresponding created states, so a left-handed field creates particles with h=−1/2 and antiparticles with h=+1/2. The right-handed Weyl spinor has the opposite assignments. Quantizing the full massless Dirac field, rather than one chiral component, therefore gives both particle helicities and both antiparticle helicities; every excitation has spin one-half, while chirality determines the helicity of each massless sector.

Keep the printed coupling g symbolic and use the Dirac adjoint ψˉ​=ψ†γ0 and γ5=iγ0γ1γ2γ3. Varying the action with respect to the independent adjoint variable gives the Euler-Lagrange field equation
(iγμ∂μ​−m−gϕγ5)ψ=0.​
(17)
Varying with respect to ψ and integrating its derivative term by parts gives the corresponding left-acting adjoint Dirac equation
i(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμ+mψˉ​+gϕψˉ​γ5=0.
(18)
Now differentiate the axial current j5μ​=ψˉ​γμγ5ψ. The first equation supplies γμ∂μ​ψ=−i(m+gϕγ5)ψ, and the second supplies (∂μ​ψˉ​)γμ=iψˉ​(m+gϕγ5). Since {γμ,γ5}=0 and (γ5)2=1,
∂μ​j5μ​​=(∂μ​ψˉ​)γμγ5ψ+ψˉ​γμγ5∂μ​ψ=iψˉ​(m+gϕγ5)γ5ψ+iψˉ​γ5(m+gϕγ5)ψ.​
(19)
Therefore the axial-current divergence for a pseudoscalar Yukawa interaction is
∂μ​j5μ​=2imψˉ​γ5ψ+2igϕψˉ​ψ.​
(20)
Both the fermion mass and the interaction can break the axial symmetry. This is the classical field-equation identity being asked for, not a claim that quantum composite operators need no renormalization.
There is a convention issue in the original PDF: its interaction is written without an explicit i. The Hermiticity of a pseudoscalar Dirac bilinear follows directly from
(ψˉ​γ5ψ)†=ψ†γ5γ0ψ=−ψ†γ0γ5ψ=−ψˉ​γ5ψ.
(21)
For real ϕ, the printed interaction is Hermitian if g∗=−g, rather than if g is real. The PDF does not specify that g is real, so a consistent physical convention is g=igP​ with real gP​. Its interaction is −igP​ϕψˉ​γ5ψ, and the divergence becomes 2imψˉ​γ5ψ−2gP​ϕψˉ​ψ. If g were instead required to be real, the printed action would be non-Hermitian; the variational formulas above would still be formal calculations, but the variational adjoint equation would not be its Hermitian-conjugate equation. No missing factor is silently inserted into the solution.

Use the printed interaction, so the Dyson series contributes −ig per vertex before its spinor matrix. The momentum-space Feynman rules are
Dϕ​(q)=q2−μ2+i0i​,Sψ​(q)=q2−m2+i0i(q+m)​,Vϕψˉ​ψ​=−igγ5.​
(22)
Each interaction vertex has one pseudoscalar line and one incoming and one outgoing fermion-number line. Conserve four-momentum at every vertex, integrate each independent loop momentum with d4ℓ/(2π)4, and divide by any Feynman-diagram symmetry factor. A closed fermion loop contributes an extra minus sign. The external wavefunctions are u for incoming fermions, uˉ for outgoing fermions, vˉ for incoming antifermions and v for outgoing antifermions; spinor matrices are multiplied in order along the fermion line. An external pseudoscalar has unit wavefunction factor in standard relativistic normalization. Relative fermionic signs also occur when the external contractions are permuted.
The leading scattering amplitudes are of order g2. Label the incoming momenta p1​,p2​ and outgoing momenta p3​,p4​, all with positive energy and pi2​=m2. Use the Mandelstam variables s=(p1​+p2​)2, t=(p1​−p3​)2 and u=(p1​−p4​)2. The tree scattering with pseudoscalar exchange is shown below; dashed edges are pseudoscalar propagators, and arrows indicate fermion-number flow, opposite to the motion of an antifermion.
Define the scattering amplitude by ⟨f∣S−1∣i⟩=i(2π)4δ4(p3​+p4​−p1​−p2​)M. Use standard relativistic normalization for external states and spinors. To fix even the overall signs, suppress the positive state-normalization factors and write the creator order of the two-fermion states as ∣i⟩=b1†​b2†​∣0⟩, ∣f⟩=b3†​b4†​∣0⟩, and the fermion-antifermion states as ∣i⟩=b1†​d2†​∣0⟩, ∣f⟩=b3†​d4†​∣0⟩. Spin labels on the ui​,vi​ are implicit.
For two fermions, the direct t graph connects 1→3 and 2→4, while the exchanged u graph connects 1→4 and 2→3. The canonical anticommutation relations give signs + and − for these contractions in the chosen state order. Multiplication of the two vertices and the scalar propagator gives (−ig)2i=−ig2. Hence
Mψψ​=−g2[t−μ2+i0(uˉ3​γ5u1​)(uˉ4​γ5u2​)​−u−μ2+i0(uˉ4​γ5u1​)(uˉ3​γ5u2​)​]+O(g4).​
(23)
Interchanging the labels of either pair of identical external fermions reverses this amplitude, as required for the antisymmetric states of fermionic Fock space.
For a fermion and an antifermion, the graphs are t exchange and s annihilation. Their signs can be derived without guessing an analogy with the first process. The normal-ordered product of the pseudoscalar bilinear contains terms of the forms
b†buˉγ5u,qquad−d†dvˉγ5v,qquadb†d†uˉγ5v,qquaddbvˉγ5u.
(24)
The minus in the second term is the antifermion sign of a normal-ordered bilinear. Between the chosen external states, exchange has sign − and pair annihilation followed by pair creation has sign +. Therefore
Mψψˉ​​=g2[t−μ2+i0(uˉ3​γ5u1​)(vˉ2​γ5v4​)​−s−μ2+i0(vˉ2​γ5u1​)(uˉ3​γ5v4​)​]+O(g4).​
(25)
Changing an external-state phase can change the common overall sign; it cannot change the relative minus between the two channels. Both amplitudes use the coefficient exactly as printed. For the Hermitian convention g=igP​ discussed in part (a), substitute g2=−gP2​ throughout, or equivalently use vertex gP​γ5 from the outset.

Use the free Maxwell Lagrangian L=−Fμν​Fμν/4 in mostly-minus signature. In terms of the spatial electromagnetic four-potential, define E=−A˙−∇A0​ and B=∇×A. Thus L=(E2−B2)/2. Taking the Cartesian components of A as coordinates gives the canonical momenta
Π0=0,Πi​=∂A˙i​∂L​=A˙i​+∂i​A0​=−Ei​.​
(26)
This sign convention treats Ai​ here as Cartesian spatial-vector labels; it is not a claim that covariant and contravariant four-vector components coincide.
The temporal momentum vanishes because no A˙0​ occurs. This is the primary momentum constraint of the electromagnetic potential, so one cannot treat all four components as independent unconstrained oscillators. The Legendre transformation for the spatial components gives Πi​A˙i​−L=(Π2+B2)/2−Π⋅∇A0​. After integration by parts, the Hamiltonian is
H=∫d3x[21​(Π2+B2)+A0​∇⋅Π].​
(27)
Surface terms vanish for decaying fields or the corresponding regulated boundary conditions. The total constrained Hamiltonian also contains an arbitrary multiplier of Π0. Preserving Π0=0 under time evolution requires
Π˙0=−δA0​δH​=−∇⋅Π=0,
(28)
which is the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory. Both constraints are first-class constraints; they express the gauge invariance A↦A+∇α, A0​↦A0​−α˙. The electric and magnetic fields are unchanged, so different gauge potentials need not describe different physical states.
One consistent canonical quantization of the electromagnetic field first reduces this redundancy. Choose Coulomb gauge ∇⋅A=0. In the absence of charges, the Gauss law constraint in gauge theory gives ∇2A0​=0; decay at infinity sets A0​=0. Residual spatially constant gauge parameters do not change A. The remaining variables are the transverse pairs AT,ΠT=A˙T. Their gauge reduction of the Maxwell Hamiltonian is
HT​=21​∫d3x[(ΠT)2+(∇×AT)2].​
(29)
The two transverse coordinates for each nonzero momentum are the physical degrees of freedom. Treating the constraints as operator identities while retaining unconstrained componentwise commutators would be inconsistent: differentiating [AiT​,ΠjT​]=iδij​δ3 would give a nonzero right side even though ∂i​AiT​=0.
The reduced Dirac bracket, promoted to a canonical commutation relation, instead gives the transverse equal-time commutator:
[AiT​(t,x),ΠjT​(t,y)]=iδijT​(x−y),[AiT​,AjT​]=[ΠiT​,ΠjT​]=0,​
(30)
where
δijT​(r)=∫(2π)3d3k​eik⋅r(δij​−∣k∣2ki​kj​​).
(31)
Indeed the spatial transverse projector of a vector field PijT​=δij​−ki​kj​/∣k∣2 is symmetric, has PTk=0 and (PT)2=PT. Projecting both entries of the unconstrained canonical bracket onto the transverse subspace therefore produces PTδ3, equivalently the constrained Dirac bracket. The spatial zero mode is excluded by the decay condition; in a finite-volume regulator it must be treated separately.
Choose two orthonormal transverse photon polarization vectors ϵr​(k), so that
k⋅ϵr​=0,ϵr∗​⋅ϵs​=δrs​,∑r=12​ϵi(r)​ϵj(r)∗​=PijT​.
(32)
The canonical transverse photon field has mode expansion
AiT​(x)=∑r=12​∫(2π)32ωk​​d3k​[ϵi(r)​(k)ar​(k)e−ik⋅x+ϵi(r)∗​(k)ar†​(k)eik⋅x],ωk​=∣k∣.
(33)
Differentiate in time to obtain ΠiT​: the annihilation coefficient acquires −iωk​ and the creation coefficient +iωk​. The oscillator canonical commutation relations are
[ar​(k),as†​(q)]=(2π)3δrs​δ3(k−q),[ar​,as​]=[ar†​,as†​]=0.​
(34)
Substitution into [AiT​,ΠjT​] gives i/2 times the sum of the two Fourier kernels with phases ±ik⋅(x−y). The polarization completeness relation and the evenness of PT(k) make that sum exactly iδijT​. This derives, rather than presumes, the correspondence between the oscillator and field commutators.
Substitute the mode expansions into HT​. Spatial integration sets paired momenta equal or opposite. The aa and a†a† terms from the electric and magnetic energies cancel because ωk2​=∣k∣2 and k⋅ϵr​=0. The remaining terms are harmonic-oscillator energies. Removing the state-independent zero-point energy by normal ordering gives
:HT​:=r=1∑2​∫(2π)3d3k​ωk​ar†​(k)ar​(k).​
(35)
The corresponding momentum operator is P=∑r​∫d3kkar†​ar​/(2π)3. These expressions imply [HT​,ar†​(k)]=ωk​ar†​(k) and [P,ar†​(k)]=kar†​(k).
Define the Fock vacuum by ar​(k)∣0⟩=0. Applying ar†​(k) creates a photon of energy ∣k∣, momentum k, and one of two transverse polarizations. Its mass is zero because k2=0. The relativistically normalized state is ∣k,r⟩=2ωk​​ar†​(k)∣0⟩, with inner product 2ωk​(2π)3δrs​δ3(k−q). Smearing these momentum eigenstates gives normalizable wave packets. Repeated commuting bosonic creation operators construct the symmetric multiphoton bosonic Fock space. Circular combinations of the two transverse photon polarization vectors transform with opposite phases under rotations about k, giving the two physical helicities h=±1​.
The same physical states can be obtained while retaining manifest Lorentz covariance through Gupta-Bleuler quantization. Add the Feynman gauge term −(∂μ​Aμ)2/2 and quantize four auxiliary oscillator polarizations. Their algebra has an indefinite metric, including a negative-norm temporal excitation. Impose (∂μ​Aμ)(+)∣phys⟩=0 and take the Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient. Temporal and longitudinal excitations then do not survive as independent physical states. The result agrees with the positive, two-polarization space obtained by Coulomb gauge reduction; covariant gauge fixing is a different implementation of the same physical constraint, not four physical photon states.
