The eddy viscosity in the prescribed mixing length model is
νT​=l2∣Uy​∣=C12​M01/2​x1/2η2∣f′′(η)∣.​
(36)
Thus at a fixed similarity coordinate η, every nonzero value grows as x1/2. Its maximum occurs at a fixed coordinate maximizing η2∣f′′∣, so the maximum itself has the same downstream scaling.
The formal argument intended by the edge assumption is also clear. If one independently stipulates ∣f′′(η)∣≤∣f′′(ηw​)∣ for ∣η∣≤ηw​, then η2∣f′′(η)∣≤ηw2​∣f′′(ηw​)∣. This would put the maximum eddy viscosity at the edge and give
νT​(x,yw​)U(x,0)yw​​=C12​ηw​∣f′′(ηw​)∣f′(0)​,
(37)
independent of x, provided f′′(ηw​)=0. That is a conditional scaling calculation, not an admissible solution of the full momentum problem.
The printed edge claim fails for the reason established in part (e): f′(ηw​)=0 forces f′′(ηw​)=0, and therefore
νT​(x,±yw​)=0.​
(38)
A nontrivial profile has positive eddy viscosity in the interior; hence its maximum cannot occur at that finite free edge. Moreover U(x,0)yw​/νT​(x,±yw​) has a zero denominator, so it is not a finite effective Reynolds number under the literal assumptions.
The intended downstream cancellation is valid if one uses a consistent interior reference point η∗​∈(0,ηw​) with f′′(η∗​)=0, for example the maximum of νT​. Since U(x,0)=M01/2​x−1/2f′(0) and yw​=ηw​x,
νT​(x,η∗​x)U(x,0)yw​​=C12​η∗2​∣f′′(η∗​)∣f′(0)ηw​​,​
(39)
which is independent of x. This is a qualified correction of the reference point, not a proof of the false edge maximum.
