The proposed mixing length can be motivated as a local phenomenological estimate: on either half of a self-similar jet, the centreline supplies a distinguished reference plane, and an eddy at distance ∣y∣ might retain momentum over a fixed fraction C1​∣y∣ of that transverse distance. This gives
l=C1​∣y∣.​
(26)
It is compatible with the dilation x↦ax, y↦ay and introduces no independent outer length. In the turbulent plane jet similarity regime its typical value at ∣y∣∼δ is C1​δ, so the scaling from part (b) has δ/L∼C12​.
This argument is a modeling assumption, not a physical necessity: a jet centreline is not a wall that prevents turbulent eddies from crossing it. Thus a mixing length vanishing there cannot automatically be justified throughout the core. Parts (e) and (f) exhibit the precise difficulty: the resulting finite-velocity similarity profile has singular centreline shear and zero shear at a finite free edge, contradicting the printed edge definition. The local dimensional motivation does not make all the stipulated global jet properties consistent.
