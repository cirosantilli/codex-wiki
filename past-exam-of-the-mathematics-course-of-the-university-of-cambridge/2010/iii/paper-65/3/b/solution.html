Let L be the streamwise evolution scale, δ the jet width, and Uc​ its velocity scale. A slender jet has ε=δ/L≪1; mean incompressibility gives V=O(εUc​). Both mean advection terms are then O(Uc2​/L). For turbulent parcels crossing a transverse distance l, retention of their original streamwise momentum gives a fluctuation u^∼−lUy​; a comparable transverse fluctuating speed gives the down-gradient mixing length closure
u^v^=−l2∣Uy​∣Uy​=−νT​Uy​,νT​=l2∣Uy​∣.
(23)
The mixing length is an effective distance of momentum retention, with statistical coefficients absorbed into l; it is not the net transverse displacement of a mean streamline. This closure models the Reynolds stress rather than deriving it exactly.
In a slender equilibrium jet, the fluctuation magnitude inferred from momentum balance is u′2∼Uc2​δ/L=εUc2​. The transverse Reynolds stress gradient then has size u′2/δ∼Uc2​/L, whereas the streamwise normal-stress gradient is u′2/L∼εUc2​/L. At sufficiently high Reynolds number, molecular transverse diffusion is smaller provided
Uc2​/LνUc​/δ2​=ReL​ε21​≪1,ReL​=νUc​L​.
(24)
Streamwise molecular diffusion is smaller still by ε2. A free jet has ambient mean pressure constant to leading order; transverse momentum corrections imply only a higher-order mean streamwise pressure force. Thus high speed must be accompanied by the slender-jet ordering, not used alone to discard every other term.
For a symmetric jet decreasing away from the centre, Uy​<0 on y>0 and Uy​>0 on y<0. Accordingly u^v^=sgn(y)l2(Uy​)2 off the centreline, and the leading equation is
UUx​+VUy​=−∂y​u^v^=−sgn(y)∂y​{l2(Uy​)2}.​
(25)
At the centreline the signed form requires the stress to tend continuously to zero; otherwise differentiation would add a distributional term. The undifferentiated Reynolds stress form is the appropriate global equation.
