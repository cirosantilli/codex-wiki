On the polydisc, put bi​(z)=fi​(z)−fi​(a) and define
F(z)=∑k=1n​∫ak​zk​​bk​(z1​,…,zk−1​,t,ak+1​,…,an​)dt.
(1)
Each integral is along the straight coordinate segment, which remains in the polydisc. Holomorphic dependence on parameters and differentiation under the integral show that F is a holomorphic function. For a fixed j, terms with k<j do not depend on zj​, and the k=j term contributes bj​(z1​,…,zj​,aj+1​,…,an​). If k>j, use the assumed symmetry ∂j​bk​=∂k​bj​ to obtain
∂j​∫ak​zk​​bk​(…,t,…)dt=bj​(z1​,…,zk​,ak+1​,…,an​)−bj​(z1​,…,zk−1​,ak​,…,an​).
(2)
These differences telescope. Therefore
∂zj​∂F​=fj​(z)−fj​(a),F(a)=0.​
(3)
This is the coordinate-integral primitive on a polydisc.
For a closed holomorphic one-form β=∑i​ci​dzi​, closedness is exactly ∂i​cj​=∂j​ci​. The preceding construction plus ∑i​ci​(a)(zi​−ai​) gives a local holomorphic function G with ∂G=β. The kernel of ∂ on the structure sheaf of a complex manifold consists of locally constant functions: holomorphicity already gives ∂ˉG=0, and ∂G=0 then implies dG=0. Thus the holomorphic Poincaré lemma gives the short exact sequence of sheaves
0⟶C​⟶OM​∂​ΩM1​⟶0.​
(4)
The first object is the complex constant sheaf, and surjectivity is local, on stalks. It does not say that every global closed holomorphic one-form has a global primitive.
Now assume compactness. The constancy assertion is for a connected complex manifold; otherwise it holds on each connected component. A smooth complex-valued u with ∂∂ˉu=0 is a pluriharmonic function. Restricting to each complex coordinate line makes it a harmonic function of one complex variable. Choose x0​ where ∣u∣ has its global maximum and a coordinate polydisc about x0​. The allowed harmonic maximum principle makes the first coordinate-line restriction constant. Every point on that line still has the same maximal modulus and the same value, so the second coordinate may then vary without changing u. Continue through all coordinates to conclude that u equals u(x0​) on the whole polydisc. The set where it has this value is closed and, by the same argument at each of its points, open. Connectedness therefore gives u constant. In particular global holomorphic functions on a compact connected complex manifold are constant.
For the top-degree pairing, write locally θ=gdz1​∧⋯∧dzn​. With the complex orientation,
in2θ∧θ=2n∣g∣2dx1​∧dy1​∧⋯∧dxn​∧dyn​.
(5)
It is everywhere nonnegative and is positive on an open set if θ is not identically zero. Hence
in2∫M​θ∧θ>0,so∫M​θ∧θ=0.​
(6)
The phase factor is necessary: the unnormalized integral need not be a positive real number. This is the integral pairing of complex top forms.
If ψ is a holomorphic differential form of degree n−1, then dψ=∂ψ has type (n,0) and ddψ​=0. The graded Leibniz rule and Stokes theorem give
∫M​dψ∧dψ​=∫M​d(ψ∧dψ​)=0.
(7)
The positive pairing just proved forces dψ=0. Thus every holomorphic (n−1)-form on a compact complex n-manifold is closed, without a Kähler metric assumption.
On a compact complex surface, this proves closedness of all holomorphic one-forms. Sending such a form to its de Rham cohomology class defines the required inclusion. Indeed, if α=df, its type (1,0) gives ∂ˉf=0, so f is a global holomorphic function and is constant. Therefore α=0.
To prove the zero intersection, let α,β be holomorphic one-forms and suppose [α]=[β​]. There is a smooth complex-valued f with α−β​=df. Its type components are ∂f=α and ∂ˉf=−β​. Closedness of β gives ∂β​=0, hence ∂∂ˉf=0. Compact pluriharmonic rigidity now makes f constant and α=β=0. The two spaces are therefore linearly independent in de Rham cohomology, and conjugation preserves their dimensions:
H0(M,ΩM1​)∩H0(M,ΩM1​)​=0,2h1,0≤b1​.​
(8)
For the other bound, take the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology of the short exact sequence above. The map H0(M,C​)→H0(M,OM​) is an isomorphism, componentwise. Since every global holomorphic one-form is closed, the relevant portion is
0⟶H0(M,ΩM1​)⟶H1(M,C​)⟶H1(M,OM​).
(9)
Identify the middle term with first de Rham cohomology, and the last with H∂ˉ0,1​(M) by the Dolbeault theorem. Taking dimensions gives
2h1,0≤b1​≤h1,0+h0,1,h1,0≤h0,1.​
(10)
These are the Betti and Hodge bounds for compact complex surfaces.
A strict example is the scalar Hopf surface X=(C2∖{0})/⟨z↦2z⟩. Polar coordinates identify it smoothly with S3×S1: the dilation fixes the angular coordinate and shifts log∣z∣ by log2. Its first Betti number is one, since its fundamental group is Z. The proved lower bound then forces h1,0=0, and the upper bound forces h0,1≥1. Thus h1,0<h0,1 on this compact complex surface; no unsupported cohomology computation is needed.

Fix the input-index convention
Dei​=∑j​θij​⊗ej​,D2ei​=∑j​Θij​⊗ej​.
(11)
Extend the connection on a vector bundle by the graded Leibniz rule, D(α⊗s)=dα⊗s+(−1)degαα∧Ds. Applying it twice gives
D2ei​=∑j​dθij​⊗ej​−∑j,k​θij​∧θjk​⊗ek​,
(12)
so
Θ=dθ−θ∧θ.​
(13)
This is the Cartan curvature equation with input indices. In the more usual coefficient-column convention A=θt, and (A∧A)t=−θ∧θ because the entries have degree one. Thus F=dA+A∧A=Θt; the sign difference is entirely conventional.
The Chern connection of a Hermitian vector bundle with holomorphic structure is characterized by metric compatibility and D0,1=∂ˉE​. To construct it, use a holomorphic local frame and let hij​=⟨ei​,ej​⟩, with the Hermitian inner product linear in the first variable. The second defining property makes θ type (1,0). The first gives
dh=θh+hθt,∂h=θh,
(14)
and therefore the only possible matrix is
θ=(∂h)h−1.​
(15)
Conversely, this formula has type (1,0) and satisfies both type components of the metric identity, because h=ht. Under a holomorphic frame change e′=ge, h′=ghg​t and direct differentiation gives
θ′=∂gg−1+gθg−1.
(16)
This is precisely the transformation obtained by differentiating e′=ge. The locally constructed connections glue, proving existence, and the forced formula proves uniqueness of the Chern connection.
The derivative of h−1 gives ∂θ=θ∧θ. Consequently the Chern curvature is
Θ=∂ˉθ∈A1,1(EndE),∂ˉEndE​Θ=0.​
(17)
The second assertion is ∂ˉ2θ=0 in a holomorphic local frame; holomorphic transition functions make it intrinsic. Thus the curvature Dolbeault class is Ψ=[Θ]∈H1(M,ΩM1​⊗EndE) by the Dolbeault theorem. Here Ω1(EndE) denotes this tensor product. We use the identification with bundle-valued Dolbeault forms in which the antiholomorphic exterior factor is placed first, so ∂ˉθ has the stated positive descent sign.
For the stated transitions e(β)=gαβ​e(α), differentiating the frame relation yields
θβ​=gαβ​θα​gαβ−1​+(∂gαβ​)gαβ−1​.
(18)
Transport the alpha matrix to the beta frame before subtracting it. The difference is the holomorphic section
σαβ​=(∂gαβ​)gαβ−1​.​
(19)
On a triple intersection, gαγ​=gβγ​gαβ​, so
σαγ​=σβγ​+gβγ​σαβ​gβγ−1​.
(20)
This is exactly the Čech cocycle condition after transporting all representatives to the gamma frame.
To identify its class, let Dα​ be the local holomorphic connection making the alpha frame parallel. Then tα​=D−Dα​ is a genuine local section of A1,0(EndE), represented by θα​, with ∂ˉtα​=Θ. Its Čech coboundary, using tβ​−tα​, is the intrinsic section represented by σαβ​ in the beta frame. This local-primitive construction is the Čech–Dolbeault identification, and proves that (σαβ​) maps to Ψ. For an arbitrary trivializing cover use its natural map to sheaf cohomology; a Stein refinement gives the usual Čech computation. The resulting object is the Čech cocycle for the curvature Dolbeault class.
If the metric changes, the difference B=D′−D is a global smooth (1,0) form with values in EndE. The local curvature formulas give Θ′−Θ=∂ˉB, so Ψ is metric independent. If holomorphic frames change by eα′​=uα​eα​, the corresponding local tensors tα′​−tα​, expressed in the old frame, are Bα​=uα−1​∂uα​. They are holomorphic, and their differences change the cocycle by δB. Hence Ψ depends only on the holomorphic bundle, not on its metric or trivializations.
Apply exterior product and endomorphism composition to curvature to define
Ψ(k)=[Θ∧k]∈Hk(M,ΩMk​⊗EndE),trΨ(k)∈Hk(M,ΩMk​).​
(21)
These are closed for the Dolbeault operator by the product rule. Their independence can also be verified without commuting matrices. If Θ1​−Θ0​=∂ˉB, then
Θ1k​−Θ0k​=∂ˉ(∑j=0k−1​Θ1j​∧B∧Θ0k−1−j​).
(22)
This follows by the noncommutative telescoping identity and the even total degree of curvature. The matrix products are interpreted in the fixed frame convention; powers of the degree-two curvature transpose to the corresponding usual curvature powers. The trace contraction is invariant under frame changes, so its class is well defined. This proves metric independence of curvature Dolbeault powers; for k>dimM the forms and groups in question vanish.
Finally take a smooth unitary frame. Metric compatibility gives θ†=−θ, and the Cartan curvature equation with input indices gives Θ†=−Θ. Thus
tr(Θk)​=(−1)ktr(Θk),(2πi​)ktr(Θk)​=(2πi​)ktr(Θk).
(23)
The second expression is therefore a real differential form. It is also d-closed: differentiating Cartan's equation gives dΘ=θ∧Θ−Θ∧θ, and cyclicity of trace cancels the resulting commutator in dtr(Θk). On a compact Kähler manifold, the Hodge decomposition theorem for compact Kähler manifolds identifies Hk(M,ΩMk​) with the (k,k) component of complex de Rham cohomology. Hence
(2πi​)ktr(Ψ(k)) determines a class in HDR2k​(M,R).​
(24)
It is k! times the degree-2k component of the Chern character. Reality does not assert integrality of these individual power-sum classes.

The specified bidegree is the conjugate-linear Hodge star, not the complex-linear extension of the real star. If the pointwise Hermitian inner product is linear in its first argument, it is defined by
α∧∗β=⟨α,β⟩dVg​,∗:Ap,q⟶An−p,n−q.
(25)
It is the real Hodge star operator extended complex-linearly and composed with complex conjugation; ∗2=(−1)p+q. On a compact Hermitian manifold,
(α,β)L2​=∫M​α∧∗β​
(26)
is a positive definite Hermitian inner product on smooth forms. The formal adjoint is ∂ˉ∗=−∗∂ˉ∗ in this convention; the final star in this identity is present in the PDF but is corrupted in the TeX aid.
State the relevant Hodge theorem as follows. On a compact manifold without boundary, the Dolbeault Laplacian Δ∂ˉ​=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂ˉ is elliptic, self-adjoint and nonnegative. Its harmonic kernel H∂ˉp,q​ is finite-dimensional, and smooth forms split orthogonally as this kernel plus the image of Δ∂ˉ​. There are the orthogonal projection H and a smooth Green operator G with
Δ∂ˉ​G=GΔ∂ˉ​=I−H,GH=HG=0.
(27)
For any form u, expand the Laplacian term to obtain
u=Hu+∂ˉ∂ˉ∗Gu+∂ˉ∗∂ˉGu.
(28)
Harmonic forms are both closed and coclosed, since (Δ∂ˉ​v,v)=∥∂ˉv∥2+∥∂ˉ∗v∥2. They are orthogonal to both other terms; the two images are orthogonal because (∂ˉa,∂ˉ∗b)=(∂ˉ2a,b)=0. This proves the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition
Ap,q=H∂ˉp,q​⊕∂ˉAp,q−1⊕∂ˉ∗Ap,q+1.​
(29)
The closed forms are exactly the first two summands: if ∂ˉu=0, then u is orthogonal to the adjoint image, and conversely the first two summands are closed. It follows that every Dolbeault cohomology class has a unique harmonic representative. Negative or out-of-range bidegrees mean zero spaces. Compactness and the absence of boundary are necessary for this stated global decomposition.
Now assume a Kähler metric. Set L=ω∧− and Λ=L∗. The permitted Kähler identities, stated with signs, are
[Λ,∂]=i∂ˉ∗,[Λ,∂ˉ]=−i∂∗,
(30)
with ordinary commutators because Λ has even degree. Thus ∂ˉ∗=−i[Λ,∂], and
∂∂ˉ∗+∂ˉ∗∂=−i{∂(Λ∂−∂Λ)+(Λ∂−∂Λ)∂}=0
(31)
by ∂2=0. The companion identity gives ∂ˉ∂∗+∂∗∂ˉ=0 in the same way.
Define Δd​=dd∗+d∗d and Δ∂​=∂∂∗+∂∗∂. Expanding d=∂+∂ˉ and d∗=∂∗+∂ˉ∗ makes the two mixed anticommutators vanish, so Δd​=Δ∂​+Δ∂ˉ​. To prove equality of the latter two, substitute the same commutator formulas and use ∂∂ˉ=−∂ˉ∂:
Δ∂​Δ∂ˉ​​=i(∂Λ∂ˉ−∂∂ˉΛ−Λ∂∂ˉ−∂ˉΛ∂),=−i(∂ˉΛ∂+∂∂ˉΛ+Λ∂∂ˉ−∂Λ∂ˉ).​
(32)
The displayed operators are identical. Therefore the Kähler Laplacian identity is
Δd​=Δ∂​+Δ∂ˉ​=2Δ∂​=2Δ∂ˉ​.​
(33)
Let η be a ∂ˉ-exact form. It is closed and orthogonal to harmonic forms. The Dolbeault Hodge decomposition, or the Green identity with α=Gη, therefore gives
η=∂ˉ∂ˉ∗α,α∈Ap,q.​
(34)
For the Green construction, ∂ˉα=0 follows since G commutes with ∂ˉ; equivalently the coexact component of a closed form vanishes. This yields exactly the stated factorization.
If also ∂η=0, put β=∂ˉ∗α and ξ=∂β. The identities already proved give
∂ˉξ=−∂∂ˉβ=−∂η=0,∂ˉ∗ξ=−∂(∂ˉ∗)2α=0.
(35)
Thus ξ is harmonic for Δ∂ˉ​ and hence for Δ∂​. But it is ∂-exact, so it is orthogonal to the harmonic summand in the Hodge decomposition theorem. Consequently ξ=0, proving that β=∂ˉ∗α is ∂-closed.
Furthermore β is orthogonal to the common harmonic space, since (β,h)=(α,∂ˉh)=0. Its ∂-Hodge decomposition then has neither a harmonic term nor a coexact term: closedness kills the coexact term. Therefore β=∂χ for a form χ∈Ap−1,q−1. It follows that
η=∂ˉ∂χ=−∂∂ˉχ,η=∂∂ˉϕ,ϕ=−χ∈Ap−1,q−1.​
(36)
This proves the requested ddbar lemma, including the intermediate harmonicity and orthogonality steps. The hypotheses p,q≥1 ensure that the potential has a valid bidegree.
Finally η=ω2​−ω1​ is a real closed (1,1) form and is d-exact. Integration by parts makes it orthogonal to every d-harmonic form. By the Kähler Laplacian identity it is also orthogonal to the Dolbeault harmonic space; because it is ∂ˉ-closed, the Dolbeault Hodge decomposition makes it ∂ˉ-exact. Apply the proved ddbar lemma to get η=∂∂ˉϕ for a smooth complex-valued function. Reality gives η=−∂∂ˉϕ​, so averaging yields
η=21​∂∂ˉ(ϕ−ϕ​)=i∂∂ˉImϕ.
(37)
With f=Imϕ,
ω2​=ω1​+i∂∂ˉf,f∈C∞(M,R).​
(38)
The potential is defined up to a real constant on each connected component. This is a statement about cohomologous Kähler forms, not about an arbitrary pair of such forms.

The canonical bundle is KM​=⋀m(T1,0M)∗, the holomorphic line bundle of holomorphic top forms on a complex m-manifold. For an embedded complex submanifold V of dimension n, the holomorphic normal bundle is the quotient
0⟶T1,0V⟶T1,0M∣V​⟶NV/M​⟶0.
(39)
Taking determinants and duals gives adjunction for a smooth submanifold:
KV​≅KM​∣V​⊗detNV/M​.​
(40)
For a closed smooth hypersurface, [V]=OM​(V) is its divisor line bundle, equipped with a holomorphic section whose zero divisor is V with multiplicity one. If fα​ locally defines V, local meromorphic generators 1/fα​ transform by the holomorphic units fα​/fβ​. The stated normal-bundle relation is
[V]∣V​≅NV/M​,KV​≅(KM​⊗[V])∣V​.​
(41)
Equivalently the conormal is OM​(−V)∣V​. The closed-divisor interpretation is needed to define [V]; an arbitrary nonclosed local submanifold does not define that global divisor bundle.
For a holomorphic subbundle F⊂E, define DF​s=πDE​s on sections of F, with π acting on the bundle factor of a bundle-valued form. It obeys the Leibniz rule. Since F is holomorphic, DE0,1​s=∂ˉE​s lies in F, so DF0,1​=∂ˉF​. For s,t∈F, their inner products with the normal components of DE​s,DE​t vanish; hence projecting preserves metric compatibility. The uniqueness proved for the Chern connection identifies
DF​=π∘DE​∣F​.​
(42)
This is the projected Chern connection.
Choose the adapted smooth unitary frame and retain Question 2's input-index convention. The connection matrix is skew-Hermitian. Its upper-left block is θ1​ for DF​; writing the lower-left block as −A forces the upper-right block to be At:
θE​=(θ1​−A​Atθ2​​).​
(43)
Holomorphicity of F makes the normal part of DE0,1​s vanish, so B=At has type (1,0), and A has type (0,1). The matrix B represents the second fundamental form of a holomorphic subbundle in this input-index convention. These types would appear in the opposite blocks with coefficient-column indices; they must not be swapped while retaining the printed minus-sign Cartan equation.
Take the upper-left block of the Cartan curvature equation with input indices. It is
(ΘE​)11​=dθ1​−θ1​∧θ1​+At∧A=ΘF​+At∧A.
(44)
Therefore the curvature formula for a holomorphic subbundle is
ΘF​=(ΘE​)11​−At∧A=(ΘE​)11​−B∧Bt.​
(45)
The sign and the order of the factors both follow from the displayed block multiplication.
Now let M be a complex torus and V a closed smooth hypersurface. The torus has a translation-invariant flat Kähler metric, and T1,0M and KM​ are holomorphically trivial. Give E=T1,0M∣V​ its flat metric and apply the preceding formula to F=T1,0V. Since ΘE​=0,
ΘTV​=−B∧Bt,ΘKV​​=−trΘTV​=tr(B∧Bt).
(46)
For a (1,0) tangent vector X, the latter coefficient is tr(B(X)B(X)†)≥0, so iΘKV​​ is a semipositive real (1,1) form. Thus KV​ is a semipositive holomorphic line bundle. This proves the semipositive canonical bundle of a submanifold of a complex torus assertion directly from curvature. By adjunction for a smooth submanifold, KV​≅[V]∣V​.
Restrict a positive metric on L to V. The tensor-product metric on
Pa​=(L⊗[V]⊗a)∣V​
(47)
has curvature iΘL∣V​​+aiΘKV​​, which is positive for every nonnegative integer a. Hence
(L⊗[V]⊗a)∣V​ is a positive holomorphic line bundle for every a∈Z≥0​.​
(48)
The integer condition is implicit in a tensor power. No assertion of strict positivity of [V]∣V​ is required.
For vanishing, write Ea​=L⊗[V]⊗a. The allowed Kodaira vanishing theorem says that on a compact Kähler manifold X, Hi(X,KX​⊗P)=0 for i>0 when P is a positive holomorphic line bundle. Since KM​ is trivial, its application on the torus gives Hi(M,E0​)=Hi(M,L)=0 for i>0.
For every a≥1, the divisor section gives the exact sequence
0⟶Ea−1​⟶Ea​⟶i∗​(Ea​∣V​)⟶0,
(49)
where i:V↪M. Crucially,
Ea​∣V​≅KV​⊗Pa−1​,
(50)
and Pa−1​ is positive by the proved restriction statement. The induced Kähler metric makes V compact Kähler, so Kodaira vanishing theorem on V gives Hi(V,Ea​∣V​)=0 for all i>0. In the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology, the segment
Hi(M,Ea−1​)⟶Hi(M,Ea​)⟶Hi(V,Ea​∣V​)
(51)
then has zero outside terms by induction. Therefore
Hi(M,L⊗[V]⊗a)=0(i>0, a∈Z≥0​).​
(52)
This proves vanishing for divisor twists on a complex torus using restriction positivity and the divisor exact sequence, rather than presuming ambient positivity of every divisor twist.
