The general adjoint functor theorem says that, for a functor U:A→B between locally small categories, with A a complete category, U has a left adjoint if and only if it preserves small limits and satisfies the solution-set condition. The solution-set condition means that for each B∈B there is a set of arrows bi​:B→UAi​ such that every b:B→UA factors as U(a)bi​ for some i and some a:Ai​→A.
Here is the smallness argument behind the theorem. A complete category that is locally small and has a weakly initial set (Wi​) has an initial object. Form the product in a category W=∏i​Wi​. It is weakly initial, since a projection W→Wi​ followed by a chosen arrow Wi​→X gives an arrow W→X. Take the simultaneous equalizer e:E→W of all endomorphisms of W with 1W​; the family is a set by local smallness. The object E is also weakly initial. For parallel arrows a,b:E⇉X, take their equalizer j:Y→E and a map t:W→Y. Since ejt is an endomorphism of W, the defining property of e gives ejte=e. Cancellation of the monomorphism e gives jte=1E​. Thus the monomorphism j has a right inverse and is an isomorphism, so a=b. Existence of arrows out of E was already established, proving the initial-object lemma for complete categories with a weakly initial set.
If U preserves small categorical limits, the comma category (B↓U) is complete: take the limiting object in A, and use preservation to assemble the arrows from B. It is locally small, and the solution-set condition provides a weakly initial set. Its initial object is an arrow ηB​:B→ULB with a natural bijection
A(LB,A)≅B(B,UA).
(1)
For a morphism v:B→B′, define Lv by U(Lv)ηB​=ηB′​v. Uniqueness proves functoriality and naturality, giving a left adjoint L⊣U. Conversely, a right adjoint preserves categorical limits, because applying the adjunction converts a limiting cone over a diagram into the corresponding limiting cone of hom-sets. Its adjunction unit at B is a singleton solution set. This proves both directions of the general adjoint functor theorem.
Apply it to the inclusion J:KHaus↪Top, where KHaus is the full subcategory of compact Hausdorff spaces. A small product in a category of compact Hausdorff spaces is again compact Hausdorff by the Tychonoff theorem. An equalizer of two continuous functions into a Hausdorff space is closed, hence compact Hausdorff. Products and equalizers construct all small categorical limits, and the inclusion preserves these constructions. Both categories are locally small.
Fix a topological space X and a continuous function f:X→K into a compact Hausdorff space. The closure Kf​=f(X)​ is compact Hausdorff and has a dense subset of cardinality at most ∣X∣. The permitted cardinal estimate gives
∣Kf​∣≤22∣X∣.
(2)
For X=∅, use Kf​=∅. Choose a set of representatives of all compact Hausdorff topologies on underlying sets of cardinality at most this bound, and all continuous functions from X to those representatives. Every f factors through one of them, using the inclusion Kf​↪K. This is the solution-set condition. Consequently the inclusion of compact Hausdorff spaces has a left adjoint, the compact Hausdorff reflection:
KHaus(RX,K)≅Top(X,JK).​
(3)
The universal arrow X→RX has dense image: factoring it through its closed image and applying uniqueness supplies an inverse to that image inclusion. It need not be an embedding when X is not sufficiently separated; the assertion is a compact Hausdorff reflection, rather than a claim that every topological space embeds in a compact Hausdorff space.
For completeness, the alternative Special adjoint functor theorem can be proved from the same initial-object argument. Its limit form assumes that A is complete, locally small and well-powered, with a small cogenerating family (Qi​)i∈I​; then U:A→B, with B locally small, has a left adjoint precisely when it preserves small limits. To prove sufficiency, start with b:B→UA and intersect all subobjects M↪A through which b factors after applying U. Well-poweredness makes this a small intersection of subobjects, and preservation of limits gives a smallest supporting object (M,bM​). For maps s,t:M⇉Qi​, equality U(s)bM​=U(t)bM​ makes their equalizer another supporting subobject. Minimality forces that equalizer to be invertible, so s=t. Therefore
A(M,Qi​)↪B(B,UQi​),s⟼U(s)bM​
(4)
is an injective function. The evaluation embedding into cogenerator products embeds M in ∏i​QiSi​​, where Si​ is the realized subset of the fixed set B(B,UQi​). There are only a set of choices of these subsets, only a set of subobjects of each resulting product, and only a set of arrows from B to each U-image. They give a solution set, so the already proved general adjoint functor theorem applies. Necessity is again preservation of limits by a right adjoint. For KHaus, monomorphisms are embeddings onto closed subspaces, and [0,1] is a coseparator because continuous functions to it separate points. Thus the Special adjoint functor theorem gives the same reflection directly.
