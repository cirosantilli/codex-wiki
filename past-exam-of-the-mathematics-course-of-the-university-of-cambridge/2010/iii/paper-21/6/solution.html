A regular category has finite limits, coequalizers of kernel pairs, and regular epimorphisms stable under pullback. Equivalently, every morphism factors as a regular epimorphism followed by a monomorphism, and these factorizations are stable under pullback. A regular functor preserves finite limits and regular epimorphisms, hence also preserves these image factorizations.
For this question, the capital regular category convention is the one in which every well-supported object is a well-pointed object in a category. Here an object A is well-supported if A→1 is a regular epimorphism. It is well-pointed if a subobject m:B↪A through which every point 1→A factors must be the whole object. This convention is documented in Definitions 1.1–1.3. It imposes no such point-detection requirement on objects whose support is a proper subobject of 1. In particular, it should not be replaced here by the stronger assertion that A(1,−) reflects every isomorphism.
First prove two facts about a capital regular category A. Every well-supported object A has a point. Otherwise A×A is well-supported and has no points, so its diagonal A↪A×A contains all points and is invertible. That makes A a subterminal object; the regular epimorphism A→1 is then also monic and hence invertible, contradicting the absence of a point. If e:X→Y is a regular epimorphism and y:1→Y is a point, its pullback in a category is a well-supported object over 1, so it has a point lifting y. Thus the representable functor
Γ=A(1,−):A→Set
(25)
preserves regular epimorphisms; it already preserves all existing limits. Hence Γ is a regular functor. Second, if ΓX is a singleton, its unique point x:1→X is a monomorphism, X is well-supported, and x contains all points of X. Well-pointedness makes x invertible. Thus Γ reflects terminal objects, even though it need not reflect arbitrary isomorphisms.
Now let C be a small category that is a regular category. For each object B, its slice category C/B is small and regular: finite limits, images and the stability of regular epimorphisms are computed using the corresponding constructions in C. By the permitted capitalization result choose a conservative functor that is regular,
JB​:C/B→AB​,
(26)
with AB​ a small capital regular category. The base-change functor B∗:C→C/B, given by X↦(X×B→B), preserves finite limits and regular epimorphisms. So does
RB​=ΓB​JB​B∗:C→Set.
(27)
Taking these as the coordinates defines a regular functor
R:C→SetobC.
(28)
The exponent is a set of indices, so this is a power of the Category of sets, with finite limits and regular epimorphisms computed coordinatewise.
To prove reflection of isomorphisms, suppose R(f) is invertible for f:A→B. In C/B, the object (A,f) is the pullback of B∗f:(A×B→B)→(B×B→B) along the point given by the diagonal B→B×B. Applying ΓB​JB​ shows that ΓB​JB​(A,f) is the fibre of the bijection RB​(f) over this diagonal point, and is a singleton. The preceding lemma gives JB​(A,f)≅1 through its canonical arrow to 1. Since JB​ reflects isomorphisms, (A,f)→(B,1B​) is invertible. Its underlying morphism is f. Therefore
R:C→SetobC is regular and reflects isomorphisms.​
(29)
In the small-category setting of this representation result, the elementary condition for replacing the power by one copy of Set is:
Every object is either well-supported or a strict initial object.​
(30)
This is almost total support for a regular category. “Strict initial” means initial and every arrow into it is invertible; the condition allows the case in which all objects are well-supported and no initial object exists.
For necessity, let V:C→Set be regular and conservative. If VA=∅, factor A→1 through its support of an object in a regular category S↪1. Preservation of images gives VS=1. Conservativity makes S→1 invertible, so A is well-supported. If VA=∅, then for every X the projection A×X→A is sent to the bijection ∅→∅, hence is invertible. Its inverse followed by the other projection supplies an arrow A→X. For any two such arrows, their equalizer is sent to a bijection onto VA, hence is invertible, proving uniqueness. Thus A is initial. If X→A exists, then VX→∅ exists, so VX=∅ and that arrow is sent to a bijection. Conservativity makes it invertible. Hence A is strict initial.
For sufficiency, choose the permitted conservative regular functor J:C→A into a small capital regular category, and put V=ΓJ. This is regular. A well-supported object of C is sent to a well-supported object of A, which has a point. A non-well-supported object is strict initial by the condition, and is consequently a proper subterminal object. The functor J preserves subterminality and reflects the noninvertibility of its arrow to 1; its image is a proper subterminal object and has no points. Thus VA is empty exactly on the non-well-supported objects.
Suppose Vf is a bijection for f:A→B. If both sets are empty, A and B are strict initial objects and f is invertible. Otherwise A,B are well-supported. Factor Jf as a regular epimorphism followed by a monomorphism m:I↪JB. Surjectivity of Vf makes every point of JB factor through m; capitality of A makes m invertible. Hence Jf is a regular epimorphism. Its kernel pair P=JA×JB​JA is well-supported, because P→JA and JA→1 are regular epimorphisms. Injectivity of Vf implies that every point of P has equal coordinates, and therefore factors through the diagonal JA↪P. Since P is well-pointed, this diagonal is invertible. Thus Jf is also monic, hence an isomorphism, and conservativity of J makes f invertible. This proves sufficiency with actual point and image arguments, rather than asserting that global sections of every capital regular category is conservative.
