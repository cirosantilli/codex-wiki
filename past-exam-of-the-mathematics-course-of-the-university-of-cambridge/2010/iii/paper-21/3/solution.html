A balanced category is one in which every morphism that is both a monomorphism and an epimorphism is an isomorphism. A strong monomorphism m:A→B is a monomorphism right orthogonal to all epimorphisms: whenever e:X→Y is epic and a:X→A, b:Y→B satisfy ma=be, there is a unique d:Y→A with de=a and md=b. A regular monomorphism is an equalizer of some parallel pair of morphisms. Its equalizer property supplies the indicated lift after epic cancellation, so every regular monomorphism is strong.
If every monomorphism is strong and m:A→B is also epic, apply strongness to the square whose left and right sides are both m, with horizontal arrows 1A​ and 1B​. The lift d:B→A satisfies dm=1A​ and md=1B​. Thus m is invertible and the category is balanced.
Conversely, suppose the category is balanced and has pullbacks in a category. Given the lifting square above, form P=A×B​Y with projections p:P→Y, q:P→A. The pair (a,e) gives h:X→P with ph=e, qh=a. The pullback of the monomorphism m is monic, so p is monic. It is also epic: rp=sp implies re=se, hence r=s because e is epic. Balancedness makes p invertible. Now d=qp−1 satisfies both required equations, and monicity of m proves uniqueness. Hence in a balanced category with pullbacks, every monomorphism is strong.
For the finite example, choose a strong monomorphism m:A→B that is not regular. It is not an isomorphism, since an isomorphism is the equalizer of an identical pair. It is not epic either, since an epic strong monomorphism is invertible by the first argument. Consequently there are distinct arrows r,s:B⇉D with rm=sm. Since m is not the equalizer of this pair, there is a map h:X→B with rh=sh that does not factor through m. Monicity rules out failure of uniqueness, so failure of existence is the only possible failure of its equalizer property.
Define C0​ to have four formally distinct objects a,b,x,d and exactly the following six nonidentity arrows:
m0​:a→b,h0​:x→b,r0​,s0​:b⇉d,k0​:a→d,l0​:x→d.
(8)
Their only nontrivial composites are
r0​m0​=s0​m0​=k0​,r0​h0​=s0​h0​=l0​.
(9)
There are no longer composable strings of nonidentity arrows, so these rules define an associative category. Sending a,b,x,d to A,B,X,D, respectively, and the six arrows to m,h,r,s,rm,rh, defines a functor. It is a faithful functor: every hom-set has at most one arrow except C0​(b,d)={r0​,s0​}, and their images are distinct. Faithfulness does not require the four image objects to be distinct.
The map m0​ is monic, by direct inspection of arrows into a. Neither m0​ nor h0​ is epic, since the distinct pair r0​,s0​ agrees after either. Every other nonidentity arrow has target d and is epic, because there is only one arrow out of d. A lifting square against m0​ whose left side is one of these epimorphisms would require an arrow d→b, which does not exist. Squares whose left side is an identity have the evident unique lift. Thus m0​ is a strong monomorphism.
It is not a regular monomorphism. The only distinct parallel pair out of b is r0​,s0​, and h0​ equalizes it but has no factorization through m0​, since there is no arrow x→a. An identical pair has 1b​ as its equalizer and cannot have the noninvertible m0​ as its equalizer. Therefore
C0​ has four objects, six nonidentity arrows, and a strong nonregular monomorphism.​
