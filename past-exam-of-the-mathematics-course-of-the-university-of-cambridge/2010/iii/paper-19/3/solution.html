It can be obtained from normal coordinates tubes of radius smaller than the local injectivity radius, or from normal neighbourhoods with geodesic convexity. Then r2 is smooth on U. A heat parametrix is an approximate kernel with the same delta initial limit and an error under L that is sufficiently regular, or flat, at t=0 to be corrected by time convolution. On a closed compact manifold choose a smooth cutoff χ(p,q) equal to one near the diagonal and supported in U. A finite-order explicit parametrix is
PN​(p,q,t)=χ(p,q)(4πt)−d/2e−r2/(4t)∑i=0N​tiwi​(p,q).
(36)
Near the diagonal its error is −χgtNΔq​wN​. Derivatives of χ produce errors supported a positive distance from the diagonal, where e−r2/(4t) decays faster than every power of t. Taking N large makes the whole error as regular at time zero as any specified finite number of derivatives requires. Its initial delta limit follows from J(p,0)=w0​(p,p)=1 and the Euclidean Gaussian function change of variables x=t​y.
One may obtain a smooth error flat at zero by summing all coefficients with time cutoffs. Let ρ∈C∞([0,∞)) equal one near zero and zero for arguments at least one, and choose ϵi​↓0 sufficiently fast. Set
P(p,q,t)=χ(p,q)g(p,q,t)∑i=0∞​ρ(t/ϵi​)tiwi​(p,q).
(37)
For each t>0 the sum is locally finite. Choosing ϵi​ successively so that the ith term and its derivatives through order ⌊i/2⌋ have bounds 2−i on their cutoff transition regions gives the usual smooth asymptotic sum. For any fixed derivative order, its tail is then smaller than an arbitrarily high power of t; the transport identities cancel all earlier orders. Consequently R=Lq​P extends smoothly and flatly to t=0.
Here is the Volterra parametrix correction, with the order of the Volterra convolution of kernels factors specified. Define
(A∗B)(p,q,t)=∫0t​∫M​A(p,z,s)B(z,q,t−s)dVz​ds.

Q=j=1∑∞​(−1)jR∗j,H=P+Q∗P.​
(39)
Indeed, Q+R+Q∗R=0 by the geometric-series cancellation, whence Lq​H=0. If ∣R∣≤C on a bounded time interval and v=vol(M), then
∣R∗j∣≤Cjvj−1(j−1)!tj−1​.
(40)
This proves convergence; the smooth flat error permits the corresponding derivative estimates. The correction has zero initial limit, so H retains the delta initial condition. Uniqueness of the heat equation on a closed compact manifold identifies this kernel with the global Riemannian heat kernel.
The local coefficient construction requires no compactness. The global uniformly bounded convolution argument just given uses a closed compact manifold, the usual spectral-geometry setting. For noncompact or incomplete Riemannian manifolds one must specify the heat realization and justify global convergence separately; a canonical choice is the minimal kernel obtained as the increasing limit of Dirichlet kernels on a smooth relatively compact exhaustion. This gives the minimal heat semigroup rather than asserting an unqualified uniqueness statement at infinity or at a missing boundary.
