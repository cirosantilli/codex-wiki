We use Δ=div∇, whose spectrum on a compact manifold is nonpositive; write P=−Δ for the positive Laplace-Beltrami operator. This convention will also make the heat operator in Question 3 equal to ∂t​−Δ.
Let e1​,…,ed​ be an orthonormal basis of Tp​M, and let γi​ be the unit-speed geodesic with γi​(0)=p and γ˙​i​(0)=ei​. Since a geodesic has vanishing acceleration for the Levi-Civita connection, differentiation twice along it gives the Riemannian Hessian:
dt2d2​f(γi​(t))​t=0​=Hessg​f(ei​,ei​).
(1)
Taking its metric trace yields the geodesic trace formula for the Laplace-Beltrami operator
Δf(p)=i=1∑d​dt2d2​f(γi​(t))​t=0​.​
(2)
The positive-sign convention P puts a minus sign in front of this sum.
For the unit sphere, take p∈Sd and tangent unit vectors ei​ orthogonal to p. Its great circle geodesics are γi​(t)=pcost+ei​sint. For a smooth ambient function F near the sphere, the ordinary chain rule gives
dt2d2​F(γi​(t))​0​=D2Fp​(ei​,ei​)−DFp​(p).
(3)
The ambient orthonormal basis is e1​,…,ed​,p. Thus, writing Fr​(p)=∂r​F(rp)∣r=1​ and similarly for Frr​,
ΔSd​(F∣Sd​)=(ΔF)∣Sd​−Frr​∣r=1​−dFr​∣r=1​.​
(4)
This proves the ambient restriction formula for the spherical Laplacian and shows why ambient derivatives normal to the sphere must be subtracted. The equivalent formula in spherical coordinates is
ΔF=Frr​+rd​Fr​+r21​ΔSd​(F(r,⋅)).
(5)
Let Pℓ​ be the vector space of homogeneous polynomials of degree ℓ in d+1 variables, and Hℓ​=kerΔ∩Pℓ​ its harmonic polynomials. If H∈Hℓ​, homogeneous polynomial scaling gives H(rω)=rℓH(ω). Substitution into the spherical coordinates formula gives
PSd​(H∣Sd​)=ℓ(ℓ+d−1)H∣Sd​.​
(6)
The restrictions are the degree-ℓ spherical harmonics. A homogeneous polynomial vanishing on the sphere vanishes on every nonzero ray and hence identically, so restriction is injective on Hℓ​.
We prove the harmonic decomposition of homogeneous polynomials that both counts these eigenfunctions and establishes completeness. For an ambient harmonic homogeneous polynomial Hm​ of degree m and j≥1, the spherical coordinates formula gives
Δ(r2jHm​)=2j(2m+2j+d−1)r2j−2Hm​.
(7)
For d≥1 its coefficient is nonzero. Inductively assume homogeneous polynomials of degree ℓ−2 have been decomposed into sums of r2jHm​. Apply this decomposition to ΔQ for Q∈Pℓ​, and lift each summand by multiplying by r2 and dividing by the displayed nonzero coefficient. The resulting R∈r2Pℓ−2​ satisfies ΔR=ΔQ, so Q−R∈Hℓ​. The same coefficient formula shows that no nonzero element of r2Pℓ−2​ can be harmonic. Starting at degrees zero and one therefore proves the direct decomposition
Pℓ​=Hℓ​⊕r2Pℓ−2​.
(8)
Since dimPℓ​=(dℓ+d​), the multiplicity is
mℓ​=(dℓ+d​)−(dℓ+d−2​),​
(9)
where the second term is zero for ℓ<2.
On r=1, iterating the decomposition writes every polynomial restriction as a finite sum of spherical harmonics. The Stone-Weierstrass theorem makes these polynomial restrictions dense in C(Sd), since coordinate functions separate points and constants are included; they are consequently dense in L2(Sd). Integration by parts makes P symmetric on smooth functions and gives orthogonality between distinct eigenvalues. Normalizing within each finite-dimensional eigenspace gives a complete orthonormal eigenbasis Yℓa​. To check the full operator spectrum, let cℓa​=⟨f,Yℓa​⟩. For smooth f, integration by parts gives the coefficients of Pf as λℓ​cℓa​. Therefore finite harmonic partial sums approximate both f and Pf in L2, hence approximate in the graph norm. Conversely every coefficient sequence with ∑ℓ,a​λℓ2​∣cℓa​∣2<∞ is the graph norm limit of its finite harmonic sums. Thus the closure of P has exactly this operator domain and is the real diagonal self-adjoint operator with entries λℓ​=ℓ(ℓ+d−1). If z is not one of these entries, the inverse has entries (λℓ​−z)−1 and is bounded because λℓ​→∞. This proves that there are no missing spectral values. The spectrum of the Laplacian on a sphere is
σ(P)={ℓ(ℓ+d−1):ℓ=0,1,2,…},​
(10)
with the above multiplicities; for Δ, negate every eigenvalue. For example S1 has eigenvalues ℓ2, with multiplicities one at zero and two for ℓ≥1, and S2 has multiplicity 2ℓ+1. The zero-dimensional sphere consists of two isolated points and has only the zero eigenvalue, with multiplicity two.
