Fix a bounded tile in Euclidean space Ω with labelled flat boundary faces. For each face label s, encode an assembly of n copies by an involutive real symmetric signed permutation matrix Ms​: a paired interface contributes a two-by-two swap, an unglued face with a Neumann boundary condition contributes +1 on the diagonal, and an unglued face with a Dirichlet boundary condition contributes −1. Use the same face-identification convention on each copy. If a second assembly has matrices Ns​ and there is an invertible constant matrix C satisfying
CMs​=Ns​Cfor every face label s,​
(11)
then the map u=(u1​,…,un​)↦Cu is an isomorphism between corresponding Laplace-Beltrami operator eigenspaces. This is the transplantation theorem.
To prove it, restrict an eigenfunction to the tile copies and identify each copy with Ω. Along a face, let a be its vector of boundary values and b its vector of outward normal derivatives. At a glued interface the values agree and the outward derivatives are opposite. At an unglued face with a Neumann boundary condition the derivative vanishes, and at a face with a Dirichlet boundary condition the value vanishes. All three cases are encoded by
Ms​a=a,Ms​b=−b.
(12)
The intertwining identities give Ns​(Ca)=Ca and Ns​(Cb)=−Cb, so the transplanted function satisfies the second assembly's interface and boundary conditions. On each tile its Laplace-Beltrami operator is the same constant linear combination of the original Laplacians, hence it has the same eigenvalue. Across an interface, matching values and opposite normal derivatives make it a weak solution; elliptic regularity, or the allowed reflection principle, makes it a genuine smooth solution there. At corners the operator is understood by its finite-energy, Friedrichs extension; the same matching argument applies to its quadratic form domain. Applying C−1 reverses the construction. Thus dimensions of all eigenspaces agree, including multiplicities, and the two specified boundary-value spectra coincide.
The requested pair exists with explicitly specified mixed boundary conditions and polygonal boundary. Take four copies Q0​,Q1​,Q2​,Q3​ of an octagonal regular polygon in Euclidean space. Label four alternate sides a,b,c,d in cyclic order; the four intervening sides always have a Neumann boundary condition. The labelled sides are disjoint even at their endpoints. Parameterize every side in the positive boundary direction of the reference octagon. When pairing same-labelled sides, identify equal parameters. This produces a locally flat surface with boundary: a neighbourhood of an interior point of an interface consists of two half-disks glued along their diameters. All tile vertices remain boundary points, with boundary angles 3π/4 or 3π/2; there are no interior cone points.

Thus the mixed boundary condition is defined without requiring classical differentiability at a corner or at a Dirichlet–Neumann junction.

This four-tile mixed-boundary transplantation between a disk and a nonorientable surface gives a bijection of every pair of eigenspaces, preserving eigenvalues and multiplicities.
There is also a direct argument on the quadratic form domains that handles all boundary corners. The orthogonal matrix U=C/2 acts on tile restrictions, preserves their L2 inner product and the sum of their Dirichlet energies, and commutes with taking a Sobolev trace. The condition Ms​u=u on each face encodes equality of the two traces at a paired interface and vanishing trace at an unpaired Dirichlet face. Intertwining takes these conditions exactly to Ns​(Uu)=Uu. Consequently U maps the whole form domain onto the other form domain and preserves its quadratic form. The associated self-adjoint operators are unitarily equivalent, proving isospectrality for the stated mixed boundary conditions without any assumption of smoothness at a Dirichlet–Neumann junction.
We now verify the actual topology using the topology of disk tiles glued along disjoint boundary arcs. Because the gluing arcs in each tile are disjoint, the resulting surface with boundary is a thickening of its gluing graph, with one vertex for each closed disc tile and one edge for each paired arc. One can construct this homotopy equivalence by taking a small central closed disc in each tile and narrow strips to its paired arcs. What remains is a collar neighbourhood of the boundary, which retracts onto this connected system of closed discs and strips. Shrinking each central closed disc and each strip then gives the gluing graph. Equivalently, begin with a spanning tree of the gluing graph: its closed disc tiles joined along boundary arcs form one closed disc, and every remaining pair of boundary arcs adds one band. Thus for n tiles and e paired arcs the fundamental group is a free group of rank e−n+1, and the Euler characteristic is n−e.

Our same-parameter gluing preserves boundary directions, so a global orientation requires opposite orientation signs on paired tiles: the two induced boundary orientations at an interior interface must be opposite. Such signs exist exactly when the gluing graph is bipartite. In B the cycle

It has nonempty boundary because every intervening octagon side remains unpaired. Thus D is a simply-connected orientable surface with boundary, whereas its isospectral partner B is neither simply connected nor orientable, exactly as required, for the specified mixed boundary problem.
The boundary qualification is essential. With smooth boundary and the same pure Dirichlet boundary condition or pure Neumann boundary condition on both surfaces, the following existing obstruction applies. Polygonal corners and the mixed boundary conditions in the construction above place it outside that obstruction.
With the interpretation of compact smooth Riemannian surfaces with the same pure Dirichlet boundary condition or pure Neumann boundary condition, the printed simply-connected/nonorientable pair cannot exist. The two-dimensional heat trace has the following expansion (the smooth-boundary coefficients are given in the heat-coefficient formulas, Theorems 5.1–5.2):
Tr(e−tP)=4πtA​∓8πt​L​+12π1​(∫M​KdA+∫∂M​κds)+O(t1/2),
(22)
where the boundary sign is minus for Dirichlet and plus for Neumann. The Gauss-Bonnet theorem identifies the constant term with χ(M)/6. Isospectrality therefore forces equal Euler characteristics. A compact connected simply-connected surface with boundary is a disk and has χ=1. A connected nonorientable surface with c≥1 crosscaps and b≥1 boundary components has χ=2−c−b≤0. Thus no such smooth pure-Dirichlet or pure-Neumann pair exists. This is the Euler characteristic from the heat trace of a bordered surface.
Piecewise-smooth boundaries introduce corner terms, and mixed boundary conditions change the heat coefficients, as the explicit construction above demonstrates. Moreover, in the usual pure-Neumann polygonal reflection-transplantation setup, the pure Neumann reflection transplantation preserves Euler characteristic argument applies even with corners. The numbers of tiles agree. For each face label the number of edge classes is (n+trMs​)/2, so these agree too. For each tile-vertex label, vertex classes are orbits of the subgroup generated by incident-face involutions. Their number is the dimension of invariant vectors in that permutation representation, which is unchanged by the intertwiner. Hence the numbers of vertices agree, proving equal V−E+F. Thus this standard tiled construction also cannot supply a disk on one side and a nonorientable surface on the other.
For comparison, the following existing construction gives an orientable and a nonorientable pair with the same spectrum for the pure Neumann boundary condition. Both have nontrivial fundamental groups; this is a separate example, not the construction used to meet the simply-connected requirement above. This eight-tile Neumann transplantation across orientability makes the comparison explicit. The finite seed is the affine modulo-eight example in the original orientability construction; the intertwiner and the topology checks are derived below. We prove their topology as well. Their gluing graphs are connected: the first contains the spanning path 0,1,3,6,2,7,5,4, and the second contains the spanning cycle 0,1,7,2,6,3,5,4,0. Each has eight tile vertices and ten paired-face edges. Since tiles are disks and gluing arcs are disjoint, each surface retracts up to homotopy to this multigraph; thus its Euler characteristic is 8−10=−2 and its fundamental group is a free group of rank three.
An orientation assigns a sign to each reference tile, and a reflection gluing requires opposite signs at the ends of every graph edge. The second graph admits this assignment: take sign + on {0,5,6,7} and sign − on {1,2,3,4}. Every paired face joins opposite signs, proving orientability. In the first graph the cycle

has odd length. Transporting orientation around it reverses the initial sign, proving nonorientability. Hence the first surface is also not simply connected. The second surface is orientable but likewise not simply connected; the disk required in the question is supplied instead by the four-tile mixed-boundary construction above.
The four-tile construction resolves the printed simply-connected requirement under explicit mixed boundary conditions. The eight-tile comparison only concerns orientation of a surface under a pure Neumann boundary condition.
