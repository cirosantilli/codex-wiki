Let η>0 denote the uniform magnetic diffusivity. The resistive induction equation for a solenoidal vector field in an incompressible flow is
∂t​B+(u⋅∇)B=(B⋅∇)u+η∇2B,∇⋅B=0.
(13)
Here the radial compression and axial stretching have zero total divergence: r−1∂r​(−ωr2)+∂z​(2ωz)=0. A purely axial field independent of z is automatically solenoidal, and the stretching term is 2ωBz​z^. Thus the ansatz closes under the equation. For its angular mode, with m a nonnegative integer,
∂t​Bz​−ωr∂r​Bz​=2ωBz​+η(∂r2​+r−1∂r​+r−2∂ϕ2​)Bz​.
(14)
This is magnetic concentration by an incompressible stagnation flow.

For m=0, smoothness on the axis requires b(0) finite and b′(0)=0. Set s=−q2/2 and b=h(s). The radial equation becomes the Kummer differential equation
sh′′+(1−s)h′−2λ​h=0.
(21)
Its regular solution is
b(q)=b(0)M(2λ​,1,−2q2​),​
(22)
where M is the confluent hypergeometric function of the first kind. The second independent solution has a logarithmic singularity at the axis and is inadmissible for a smooth field. For real λ>0, the regular solution vanishes at infinity: generically it has a q−λ tail; at positive even integer λ it instead has a Gaussian times a polynomial. Thus decay at infinity alone does not select a unique separation parameter. Generically finite magnetic energy further requires λ>1.
The simplest finite-flux solution is λ=2, for which M(1,1,s)=es and
b(q)=b0​e−q2/2,f(t)=f0​g(t)2g02​​.​
(23)
Direct differentiation gives b′′+q−1b′=(q2−2)b and qb′=−q2b, verifying the radial equation. Its axial magnetic flux is 2πb0​fg2=2πb0​f0​g02​, while its magnetic energy grows by g02​/g2 if the original width is larger than η/ω​. For positive η this Gaussian amplification saturates, rather than giving indefinite exponential growth. The other rapidly decaying regular axisymmetric modes have λ=2(n+1) and profiles e−q2/2Ln​(q2/2), where Ln​ solves yLn′′​+(1−y)Ln′​+nLn​=0.
The energy mechanism is transparent from a separate integral calculation. For any sufficiently decaying field of this axial form, integration by parts in the transverse plane gives
dtdE​=2ωE−μ0​η​∫R2​∣∇⊥​Bz​∣2dS.​
(24)
The positive term represents work by the axial stretching, with radial compression accounted for in the area integral. In the ideal limit, g=g0​e−ωt and f=f0​e2ωt for any smooth profile, so E=E0​e2ωt even when m=0 and the angular mean is zero. For sufficiently weak initial diffusion, this also gives genuine finite-time energy amplification at positive η.
Even sustained energy growth is possible in this unbounded model if slowly decaying profiles are admitted. For example, with ω>0, take the constant width g=η/ω​, m=1 and 1<λ<2. The regular radial solution b=qM((1+λ)/2,2,−q2/2) has a q−λ tail and finite Ib​. It gives E∝e2(2−λ)ωt and zero signed flux through every centered disk. Its absolute flux integral over the whole plane diverges, so the zero-flux statement here means the limit of these disk integrals. This example is not an isolated bounded-body dynamo: the velocity is unbounded at infinity and the field has an algebraic tail. The dynamo property concerns conversion of fluid energy into magnetic energy, not production of a nonzero signed magnetic flux; growth and its boundary assumptions must be specified.
