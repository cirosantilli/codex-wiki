Let Ct​ be the cumulative claim amount and Ut​=u+ct−Ct​ the surplus in the classical risk model. The supplied adjustment coefficient satisfies λ(MX​(R)−1)=cR. The Compound Poisson process has independent increments, and the random-sum transform identity gives
E[eR(Ct​−Cs​)]=exp((t−s)λ(MX​(R)−1)).
(23)
It follows that Zt​=exp(R(Ct​−ct)) is a nonnegative martingale with Z0​=1: conditional on the past, the multiplicative increment has expected value one. This is the exponential surplus martingale.
Let τ=inf{t≥0:Ut​<0} be the ruin stopping time. Surplus decreases only at claim arrivals, so on {τ≤t} the deficit at ruin gives Cτ​−cτ>u and Zτ​>eRu. The optional stopping theorem at the bounded stopping time τ∧t yields
1=EZτ∧t​≥E[Zτ​1{τ≤t}​]≥eRuP(τ≤t).
(24)
As t increases without bound, the events {τ≤t} increase to eventual ruin. Hence the ultimate ruin probability satisfies the Lundberg inequality
ψ(u)=P(τ<∞)≤e−Ru,u≥0.​
(25)
The proof uses only bounded-time stopping; it does not presume that the martingale can be stopped with equality of expected values at an unbounded ruin time.
