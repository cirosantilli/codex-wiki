The PDF gives an inverse-gamma distribution with shape k and scale θ, and a gamma distribution prior with shape α and rate λ. Write μ(θ)=θ/(k−1). The supplied conditional moments, together with Eθ=α/λ and Eθ2=α(α+1)/λ2, give the three structural quantities of the Bühlmann model:
mvs2​=Eμ(θ)=λ(k−1)α​,=Var(μ(θ))=λ2(k−1)2α​,=E[Var(Xi​∣θ)]=λ2(k−1)2(k−2)α(α+1)​.​

Here v is the variance of hypothetical means and s2 the expected process variance. The law of total covariance and conditional independence of different years imply
EXi​=m,Cov(μ(θ),Xi​)=v,Cov(Xi​,Xj​)=v+s21{i=j}​.

Its normal equations are s2ci​+v∑j​cj​=v for every i. Since s2>0, subtracting any two equations shows that all ci​ agree. The quadratic part is strictly positive for every nonzero coefficient change, so the solution is the unique global minimizer. Solving gives
ci​=s2+nvv​=n(k−2)+α+1k−2​(1≤i≤n),c0​=λ(k−1)[n(k−2)+α+1]α(α+1)​.​

A credibility estimate combines individual experience with the collective expected value, using an experience weight Z∈[0,1]. In this equal-exposure setting, the Bühlmann credibility premium is
μ​lin​=(1−Z)m+ZX,Z=s2+nvnv​=n(k−2)+α+1n(k−2)​.​
(38)
Thus the computed coefficients give exactly a credibility estimate, with a fixed credibility factor determined by known parameters and the number of observed years. This is Bühlmann credibility for inverse-gamma observations.
For the final Bayesian calculation, the likelihood function in θ is proportional to θnkexp(−θ∑i​xi−1​). Multiplication by the gamma distribution prior gives the Gamma prior for an inverse-gamma scale update
θ∣x1​,…,xn​∼Gamma(α+nk, rate λ+∑i​xi−1​).
(39)
Under quadratic loss, the conditional risk of an estimate d is Var(μ(θ)∣x)+(d−E[μ(θ)∣x])2. Its minimizer is therefore the posterior mean:
μ​Bayes​=(k−1)(λ+∑i​xi−1​)α+nk​.​
(40)
This is not a fixed-weight arithmetic-mean credibility estimate. For n=2, the observations (1,3) and (2,2) have the same sample mean, but reciprocal sums 4/3 and 1, hence different posterior means. For any n>2, append the same positive observations to these two samples. For n=1, the estimate is Cx/(1+λx), with C=(α+k)/(k−1); its second derivative is −2Cλ/(1+λx)3=0, so it is not affine either. Symmetry in the observations would force equal slopes in any affine representation, so these examples also exclude a general fixed-coefficient linear representation.
One can express the posterior mean as a broader data-dependent blend. With A=∑i​xi−1​ and harmonic mean H=n/A, it is
μ​Bayes​=(1−ZB​)m+ZB​k−1kH​,ZB​=λ+AA​.
(41)
This weights a harmonic-mean-based experience estimate, and the weight itself depends on the observations. It is not the credibility estimate based on X and the fixed Bühlmann credibility factor derived above.
