Use logarithms to base two, so all Von Neumann entropies and Shannon entropies are measured in bits. Write the nonzero eigenvalues of ρi​ as λij​. Orthogonality of the supports makes the mixture a block diagonal matrix, with eigenvalues pi​λij​. Therefore
S(ρ)​=−i,j∑​pi​λij​log2​(pi​λij​)=−i∑​pi​log2​pi​j∑​λij​−i∑​pi​j∑​λij​log2​λij​=H(p)+i∑​pi​S(ρi​)​.​
(1)
Each ρi​ has trace one. Terms with zero probability or zero eigenvalue vanish under the convention 0log0=0. This proves the entropy of an orthogonal quantum mixture.

For a bipartite density operator ρAB​, the Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy is
S(AB)≤S(A)+S(B)​.
(2)
Here S(A)=S(TrB​ρAB​) and likewise for B, using the partial trace. For a tripartite density operator, the Strong subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy is
S(ABC)+S(B)≤S(AB)+S(BC)​.
(3)
Equivalently, quantum conditional entropy cannot increase upon adding a conditioning system: S(A∣BC)≤S(A∣B).

Introduce an orthonormal classical label register X and the classical-quantum state
ωXQ​=∑i​pi​∣i⟩⟨i∣X​⊗ρi​.
(4)
The blocks have orthogonal supports because their label vectors are orthogonal, regardless of whether the original ρi​ have orthogonal supports. Part (a) gives S(XQ)=H(p)+∑i​pi​S(ρi​). The marginals are ωX​=∑i​pi​∣i⟩⟨i∣ and ωQ​=∑i​pi​ρi​, so S(X)=H(p). Apply the Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy and cancel H(p):
H(p)+∑i​pi​S(ρi​)≤H(p)+S(∑i​pi​ρi​).
(5)
Thus S(∑i​pi​ρi​)≥∑i​pi​S(ρi​). This is the Concavity of Von Neumann entropy.

Implement the quantum channel by a unitary dilation with a pure initial environment. The final state on RBE is pure. The Schmidt decomposition across either bipartition gives equal complementary Von Neumann entropies, so S(RB)=S(E) and S(B)=S(RE). By the Subadditivity of Von Neumann entropy,

(8)

Use the unsquared quantum fidelity convention in the question. For any matrix X, its trace norm is ∥X∥1​=TrX†X​=TrXX†​: the two positive matrices have the same nonzero eigenvalues, namely the squared singular values. This follows directly from the singular value decomposition.
Set X=ρ​σ​. Then XX†=ρ​σρ​ and X†X=σ​ρσ​. Their positive square roots therefore have the same trace, proving
F(ρ,σ)=∥ρ​σ​∥1​=F(σ,ρ)​.
(12)
The argument also covers singular density operators, since zero singular values contribute zero.

We first establish the purification-overlap formula for quantum fidelity, rather than assume the desired monotonicity. Write a canonical purification of a density operator as
∣ρ​⟩⟩=∑i,j​(ρ​)ij​∣i⟩∣j⟩.
(14)
Tracing the second factor gives ρ. The Schmidt decomposition shows that any other purification on a sufficiently large fixed ancillary space differs by a unitary on that space: its orthonormal ancillary vectors can be mapped to those of the canonical purification and the map extended to a unitary operator. Zero eigenvalues are handled by padding the ancillary basis.
The overlap of two canonical purifications, with a relative ancillary unitary U, is Tr(ρ​σ​UT), with zero padding when the ancillary dimension is larger. The unitary variational formula for the trace norm is
maxV unitary​∣Tr(XV)∣=∥X∥1​.
(15)
Indeed if X=PDQ† is a singular value decomposition, the trace is Tr(DW) with W=Q†VP unitary. Its modulus is at most ∑j​Djj​ because ∣Wjj​∣≤1, and V=QP† attains that bound. Thus the maximal absolute overlap of purifications equals F(ρ,σ); this also proves the needed form of Uhlmann's theorem.
Choose purifications ∣ψ⟩ABE​ and ∣ϕ⟩ABE​ attaining F(ρAB​,σAB​). Regard the very same vectors as purifications of ρA​,σA​ with ancillary system BE. Their overlap is among those maximized in the marginal purification formula, so
F(ρAB​,σAB​)=∣⟨ψ∣ϕ⟩∣≤F(ρA​,σA​)​.
(16)
This proves the monotonicity of quantum fidelity under partial trace.

Write a qubit density operator as ρ=(I+r⋅σ)/2, with Bloch vector ∣r∣≤1. The depolarizing channel sends r to pr. Its output eigenvalues are (1±∣p∣∣r∣)/2, so its Von Neumann entropy is minimized at ∣r∣=1. Define
q=(1−p)/2​.
(17)
The minimum output entropy is h(q), since binary entropy is symmetric under q↦1−q. The usual probabilistic-mixture convention has 0≤p≤1. The argument also holds throughout the physical qubit CPTP range −1/3≤p≤1, where this q still lies in [0,1].
To handle multiple channel uses, let a classical message x select a product input ρx​=⨂j=1n​ρxj​ with probability πx​. Memorylessness gives product outputs σx​=⨂j​Φ(ρxj​). Additivity of Von Neumann entropy on tensor products gives S(σx​)≥nh(q). The average output σˉ=∑x​πx​σx​ acts on n qubits, so S(σˉ)≤log2​(2n)=n. Thus the Holevo quantity obeys the product-input block bound for a qubit depolarizing channel
χ=S(σˉ)−∑x​πx​S(σx​)≤n[1−h(q)].
(18)
The Holevo bound says the classical mutual information obtained by any measurement, including a joint measurement on all outputs, is at most χ.
For a reliable code with M equiprobable messages and decoding error Pe​, Fano's inequality gives H(X∣X^)≤h(Pe​)+Pe​log2​(M−1). Consequently (1−Pe​)log2​M≤n[1−h(q)]+h(Pe​). Letting the error tend to zero proves I(Φ)≤1−h((1−p)/2)​.
The bound is tight: equal use of ∣0⟩,∣1⟩ and computational-basis output measurements produce a binary symmetric channel with crossover probability q. Ordinary classical coding achieves 1−h(q) bits per use.

They need a pre-shared entanglement resource that permits deterministic production of a Bell pair. In particular, one shared Bell pair and two classical bits suffice for exact quantum teleportation. This condition is stronger than merely having some entanglement.
Let Alice's input be ∣ψ⟩C​=α∣0⟩+β∣1⟩, and let her system A and Bob's system B share ∣Φ+⟩AB​=(∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​. Use the Bell states ∣Φ±⟩=(∣00⟩±∣11⟩)/2​ and ∣Ψ±⟩=(∣01⟩±∣10⟩)/2​. Direct expansion gives
∣ψ⟩C​∣Φ+⟩AB​=21​[∣Φ+⟩CA​∣ψ⟩B​+∣Φ−⟩CA​Z∣ψ⟩B​+∣Ψ+⟩CA​X∣ψ⟩B​+∣Ψ−⟩CA​XZ∣ψ⟩B​].
(19)
Alice performs a projective measurement in this Bell basis and sends its two-bit outcome. Each outcome has probability 1/4. Bob applies respectively I,Z,X,ZX, recovering ∣ψ⟩ exactly, without either party knowing α,β. Before he receives the outcome, his averaged state is I/2, so the protocol does not communicate without the classical message. Alice's measurement consumes the input and the shared entanglement.
For necessity, exact transmission of every pure qubit means the induced quantum channel is the identity: pure-state projectors span the operator space, and a physical channel is linear. Alice can therefore prepare a Bell pair locally and transmit one half by the proposed protocol, leaving a Bell pair shared with Bob. The protocol would itself be a deterministic local operations and classical communication conversion of their resource into a Bell pair. This proves the exact teleportation resource criterion. With no shared entanglement, it is impossible: local operations and classical communication preserve separable quantum states, whereas the resulting Bell pair would be entangled.

For an ensemble E={pi​,ρi​}, write its channel-output Holevo quantity as
χ(Φ,E)=S(∑i​pi​Φ(ρi​))−∑i​pi​S(Φ(ρi​)),χ∗(Φ)=supE​χ(Φ,E).
(20)
Take ensembles E1​={pi​,ρi​} and E2​={qj​,σj​} independently, giving the product ensemble {pi​qj​,ρi​⊗σj​}. Its average output factors:
∑i,j​pi​qj​Φ1​(ρi​)⊗Φ2​(σj​)=(∑i​pi​Φ1​(ρi​))⊗(∑j​qj​Φ2​(σj​)).
(21)
The Von Neumann entropy of a tensor product is the sum of its entropies. Applying this both to the average and to every labelled output gives
χ(Φ1​⊗Φ2​,E1​⊗E2​)=χ(Φ1​,E1​)+χ(Φ2​,E2​).
(22)
Choose each ensemble within ε of its supremum. The supremum for the joint channel includes this product ensemble, so its value is at least χ∗(Φ1​)+χ∗(Φ2​)−2ε. Let ε↓0. The superadditivity of Holevo capacity follows:
χ∗(Φ1​⊗Φ2​)≥χ∗(Φ1​)+χ∗(Φ2​)​.
(23)
This argument does not restrict the joint supremum to product ensembles and does not require the individual suprema to be attained.

In the generalized measurement postulate, outcome m is described by a Kraus operator Mm​, with ∑m​Mm†​Mm​=I. On a density operator ρ its probability and conditional output, for nonzero probability, are
pm​=Tr(Mm​ρMm†​),ρm​=Mm​ρMm†​/pm​​.
(24)
For a pure input the conditional vector is Mm​∣ψ⟩/pm​​. The effects Em​=Mm†​Mm​ are positive and sum to the identity, defining a positive operator-valued measure.
More generally an outcome can have several Kraus operators Mmα​. Then sum over α in both the numerator and the probability, and require ∑m,α​Mmα†​Mmα​=I.
The usual projective measurement with the Lüders rule is recovered when the measurement operators are mutually orthogonal projections, Mm​=Pm​=Pm†​=Pm2​, Pm​Pn​=0 for m=n, and ∑m​Pm​=I. Projector-valued effects alone give projective outcome statistics; to obtain the Lüders state update as well, the instrument must implement ρ↦Pm​ρPm​, without an additional conditional state change.

The Schmidt decomposition of a normalized bipartite pure state is
∣ψ⟩AB​=j=1∑r​λj​​∣uj​⟩A​∣vj​⟩B​​,λj​>0,∑j​λj​=1.
(25)
The two sets of local vectors are orthonormal. The Schmidt coefficients are the nonnegative numbers λj​​, and the Schmidt number for this pure state, also called its Schmidt rank, is r, the number of nonzero coefficients. It equals the rank of either reduced density operator and of the coefficient matrix in any product bases. The general mixed-state Schmidt number specializes to this same value on a pure state.
