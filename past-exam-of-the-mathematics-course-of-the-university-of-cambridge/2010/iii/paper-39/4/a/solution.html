OurBigBook.com Words: 232
On a fixed horizon T, an arbitrage is an admissible trading strategy that is self-financing, has X0​=0, and satisfies XT​≥0 almost surely with P(XT​>0)>0. Here admissibility means that wealth in units of the strictly positive riskless numéraire has a deterministic lower bound throughout the horizon. An equivalent martingale measure Q is equivalent to the physical measure on FT​ and makes each discounted risky price Si=Si/B a martingale.
For a predictable, stochastically integrable risky holding vector π, the discounted self-financing identity is
Xt​:=Bt​Xt​​=B0​X0​​+∫0t​πs​⋅dSs​.
(32)
Indeed, since the riskless account is a finite-variation process, its quadratic covariations vanish, and the Itô formula for X/B, combined with X=ϕB+π⋅S and dX=ϕdB+π⋅dS, gives d(X/B)=π⋅d(S/B).
Use two standard facts from stochastic calculus: a stochastic integral against a continuous local martingale is a local martingale, and a local martingale bounded below by a deterministic constant is a supermartingale. The latter follows by adding the constant to make the process nonnegative, stopping at a localizing sequence, and applying the conditional Fatou lemma. Thus admissible discounted wealth is a Q-supermartingale, and an arbitrage would satisfy
0≤EQ​[XT​]≤X0​=0.
(33)
It follows that XT​=0 Q-almost surely, hence also physically almost surely by equivalence, a contradiction. Therefore an equivalent martingale measure rules out arbitrage among admissible self-financing strategies. The lower-bound restriction is essential to this argument; stochastic integrals need not be true martingales for arbitrary unbounded holdings.
