The integral identity requires compactness of M, compact support of X, or suitable conditions eliminating flux at infinity. The printed question does not state such a hypothesis. We prove the Riemannian divergence theorem for a compact manifold, and more generally for a compactly supported smooth vector field on an oriented Riemannian manifold with boundary.
Write j:∂M↪M for the inclusion. Give ∂M the outward-normal-first boundary orientation, and let N be the outward unit normal vector. The Riemannian volume form of the induced Riemannian metric is
ωg​​=j∗(ιN​ωg​).
(31)
Indeed, if (e1​,…,en−1​) is a positively oriented orthonormal basis tangent to the boundary, (N,e1​,…,en−1​) is a positively oriented orthonormal basis in M. Both sides therefore evaluate to one on the boundary basis.
At a boundary point decompose X=g(X,N)N+X⊤, where X⊤ is tangent to ∂M. Evaluating on n−1 tangent vectors, the term with X⊤ vanishes: all n arguments of the alternating volume form then lie in the (n−1)-dimensional boundary tangent space. Thus the interior product of a differential form satisfies
j∗(ιX​ωg​)=g(X,N)ωg​​.
(32)
The general Stokes theorem for an oriented manifold with boundary states ∫M​dη=∫∂M​j∗η for any smooth differential form η of degree n−1 with compact support; compactness of M makes the support condition automatic. Applying it to η=ιX​ωg​ and using the defining equation for the divergence of a Riemannian vector field proves
∫M​(divX)ωg​=∫∂M​g(X,N)ωg​​.​
(33)
For a concrete failure without the extra hypothesis, take M=[0,∞) with Riemannian metric dx2 and X=(arctanx)∂x​. The divergence of a Riemannian vector field is divX=(1+x2)−1, so
∫M​divXdx=2π​,∫∂M​g(X,N)ωg​​=0,
(34)
because X vanishes at the only boundary point. Both integrals are finite. The missing π/2 is the limiting flux at infinity, so mere integrability of the divergence cannot repair the unrestricted assertion.
