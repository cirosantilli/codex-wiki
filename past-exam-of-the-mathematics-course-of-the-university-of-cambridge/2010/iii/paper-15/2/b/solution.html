The expression dϕ(X) is in general a vector field along a map, rather than a vector field on M. Its intended meaning in the required formula is pointwise evaluation of the vector-bundle-valued differential form ∇s:
(∇X′​(ϕ∗s))p​=(∇s)ϕ(p)​(dϕp​Xp​),
(17)
using the canonical identification (ϕ∗E)p​=Eϕ(p)​. This interpretation works for every smooth map, including a constant map or a map with self-intersections.
Choose a frame of a vector bundle over U, written as a row e=(e1​,…,er​). Define its connection one-form Ω by
∇(eu)=e(du+Ωu)
(18)
for component columns u. The pulled-back frame eϕ=(ϕ∗e1​,…,ϕ∗er​) is a frame of a vector bundle over ϕ−1U. For any component column v of smooth functions on that open set, define
∇′(eϕv)=eϕ(dv+(ϕ∗Ω)v).​
(19)
Here the matrix entries of ϕ∗Ω are ordinary pullbacks of a differential form. The displayed operator is linear and satisfies the Leibniz rule, hence is locally a connection on a vector bundle.
To prove these local operators glue, change the original frame of a vector bundle to e=eh. Expanding ∇(ehu) gives the change of frame of a vector-bundle connection
Ω=h−1Ωh+h−1dh.
(20)
The pullback of a differential form and the chain rule give
ϕ∗Ω=(h∘ϕ)−1(ϕ∗Ω)(h∘ϕ)+(h∘ϕ)−1d(h∘ϕ).
(21)
This is precisely the required transformation law for the pulled-back frame eϕ=eϕ(h∘ϕ). Thus the definitions agree and give a global pullback connection.
For s=eu, substitute v=u∘ϕ in the defining formula and use d(u∘ϕ)(X)=du(dϕ(X)). This proves the required identity. Conversely that identity fixes ∇X′​(ϕ∗ea​); the Leibniz rule then fixes the derivative of every ∑a​vaϕ∗ea​, proving uniqueness. If the identity is initially imposed only on global sections of a vector bundle, multiply each local frame section by a smooth bump function equal to one near the point in question and supported in U, and extend by zero. These global sections agree with the frame locally, so the same uniqueness argument applies. The pullback connection exists and is unique.
