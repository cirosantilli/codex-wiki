The curvature form of a connection is the endomorphism-valued differential two-form defined by
R∇(X,Y)s=∇X​∇Y​s−∇Y​∇X​s−∇[X,Y]​s.
(22)
It is alternating in X,Y. To see its tensoriality, replacing X by fX produces an extra −Y(f)∇X​s from the second term and an extra +Y(f)∇X​s from [fX,Y]=f[X,Y]−Y(f)X, so these cancel. Alternation gives linearity over smooth functions in Y too. Replacing s by fs produces the additional coefficient (X(Yf)−Y(Xf)−[X,Y]f)s=0; all terms involving one derivative of s also cancel. Thus R∇ belongs to Ω2(M;EndE).
In the coefficient-column convention of part (b), expanding the definition gives
R∇(X,Y)(eu)=e(X(Ω(Y))−Y(Ω(X))−Ω([X,Y])+Ω(X)Ω(Y)−Ω(Y)Ω(X))u.
(23)
The first three terms are dΩ(X,Y), and the last two are (Ω∧Ω)(X,Y). This proves the Cartan curvature matrix equation F=dΩ+Ω∧Ω, with matrix multiplication combined with the wedge product of differential forms.
The pullback connection has matrix Ω′=ϕ∗Ω. Since the pullback of a differential form commutes with the exterior derivative and the wedge product of differential forms, its curvature form of a connection has matrix
F′=d(ϕ∗Ω)+(ϕ∗Ω)∧(ϕ∗Ω)=ϕ∗(dΩ+Ω∧Ω)=ϕ∗F.
(24)
Changes of frame of a vector bundle conjugate both sides by the same pulled-back transition matrix, so this identity is intrinsic. In fibre notation the answer is
Rp∇′​(u,v)=Rϕ(p)∇​(dϕp​u,dϕp​v),u,v∈Tp​M′.​
(25)
Both sides act on the same fibre Eϕ(p)​. No claim that dϕ preserves Lie brackets of vector fields for an arbitrary map is needed.
