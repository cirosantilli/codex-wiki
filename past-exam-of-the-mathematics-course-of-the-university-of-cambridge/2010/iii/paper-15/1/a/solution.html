An integral curve of a vector field X through p is a smooth curve c:I→M, on an interval containing zero, satisfying c(0)=p and c˙(t)=Xc(t)​. The local flow is the smoothly varying family of these integral curves of a vector field:
ϕ:D⟶M,ϕt​(p)=cp​(t),ϕ0​(p)=p,∂t​ϕt​(p)=Xϕt​(p)​.
(1)
Here D is an open neighborhood of {0}×M; for the maximal local flow, its fibre over p is the maximal existence interval of cp​. Local existence, uniqueness and smooth dependence for a smooth ordinary differential equation give this family. Uniqueness also gives ϕt+s​(p)=ϕt​(ϕs​(p)) whenever the relevant curves and compositions are defined, and ϕ−t​=ϕt−1​ on their common domains.
Reparametrizing the integral curves of a vector field gives
dtd​ϕ2t​(p)=2Xϕ2t​(p)​.
(2)
Thus the flow of 2X is ϕ​t​=ϕ2t​​, with domain {(t,p):(2t,p)∈D}.
