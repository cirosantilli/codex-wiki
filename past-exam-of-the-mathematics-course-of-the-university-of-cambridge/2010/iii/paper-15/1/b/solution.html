Commutation of the two local flows means ϕt​ψs​=ψs​ϕt​ for all sufficiently small independent parameters s,t, on their common local domains. Differentiate this identity in s at zero. The chain rule gives
dϕt​∣p​(Yp​)=Yϕt​(p)​.
(3)
In a coordinate chart, differentiating again in t at zero gives DXp​Yp​=DYp​Xp​. But the Lie bracket of vector fields has coordinate expression
[X,Y]p​=DYp​Xp​−DXp​Yp​,
(4)
so commuting local flows imply [X,Y]=0.
For the converse, suppose the Lie bracket of vector fields vanishes. Write u(t)=ϕt​(p) in a coordinate chart, and put J(t)=dϕt​∣p​. Differentiating the local flow equation with respect to the initial point gives the variational ordinary differential equation
J˙(t)=DXu(t)​J(t),J(0)=I.
(5)
Consequently W(t)=Yu(t)​−J(t)Yp​ satisfies
W˙(t)=DYu(t)​Xu(t)​−DXu(t)​J(t)Yp​=DXu(t)​W(t),W(0)=0,
(6)
where the zero Lie bracket of vector fields was used in the second equality. Uniqueness for this linear ordinary differential equation gives W=0. This is a local argument and can be continued through successive coordinate charts, proving dϕt​(Y)=Y∘ϕt​ wherever the local flow is defined.
For fixed t, the curve s↦ϕt​(ψs​(p)) therefore has derivative Y at its current point and starts at ϕt​(p). Uniqueness of the integral curve of a vector field Y identifies it with s↦ψs​(ϕt​(p)). Hence ϕt​ψs​=ψs​ϕt​ ⟺ [X,Y]=0​ locally. No complete vector field hypothesis is needed.
