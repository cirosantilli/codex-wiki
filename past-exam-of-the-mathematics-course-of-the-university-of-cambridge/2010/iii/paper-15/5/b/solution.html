We use the following existence and uniqueness statement for parallel transport: for a smooth affine connection, a smooth curve γ:[0,1]→M, an initial time t0​ and a vector v∈Tγ(t0​)​M, there is a unique smooth vector field along a map V on the whole curve satisfying Dt​V=0 and V(t0​)=v. In a frame of a vector bundle this is the linear ordinary differential equation V˙=−Γ(γ˙​)V. Its smooth coefficient matrix is bounded on every compact time subinterval in a trivializing neighborhood, and the linear existence theorem gives a solution throughout that subinterval. A finite subdivision of [0,1] into such neighborhoods and uniqueness patch the solutions. No completeness hypothesis on M is needed.
For arbitrary fields U,V along a curve, the definition of the induced covariant derivative of the metric tensor gives
dtd​g(U,V)=(∇γ˙​​g)(U,V)+g(Dt​U,V)+g(U,Dt​V).
(45)
This can also be checked by differentiating gij​(γ(t))Ui(t)Vj(t) and substituting the formula in part (a). If ∇ is a metric connection and V is parallel, it follows that d(g(V,V))/dt=0. Positivity of the Riemannian metric makes ∣V∣=g(V,V)​ constant. Thus parallel transport preserves lengths.
Conversely assume every parallel field along every smooth curve has constant length. Fix p∈M and arbitrary x,v∈Tp​M. Choose a curve through p with velocity x at an interior time t0​. Explicitly, in a coordinate chart z centered at p, take γ(t)=z−1(h(t)dzp​(x)), where h(t0​)=0, h′(t0​)=1, and h has sufficiently small amplitude and support near t0​. A smooth bump function produces such h, so the curve is defined on all of [0,1] and remains inside the chart. By the stated parallel transport theorem there is a parallel field with V(t0​)=v. Evaluating the derivative of its squared length gives
0=dtd​g(V,V)​t0​​=(∇x​g)p​(v,v).
(46)
For fixed x, the bilinear form Bx​(v,w)=(∇x​g)p​(v,w) is symmetric. Its diagonal values vanish, so polarization identity gives
2Bx​(v,w)=Bx​(v+w,v+w)−Bx​(v,v)−Bx​(w,w)=0.
(47)
Since p,x,v,w were arbitrary, ∇g=0. A connection is metric-compatible if and only if all parallel fields have constant length. Polarization also shows that its parallel transport preserves all inner products, not just lengths.
