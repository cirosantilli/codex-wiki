Take p as excess pressure relative to the fluid far from the gap, and let h˙0​<0 for approach. The lower surface of the sphere is h0​+a−a2−r2​ above the plane. In the dominant region r=O(ah0​​)≪a, this is the parabolic lubrication gap
h(r,t)=h0​+2ar2​+O(r4/a3).
(47)
Its slope is O(h0​/a​)≪1, justifying lubrication theory. Both rigid surfaces have zero radial velocity, so the no-slip boundary condition and radial Stokes equation give the radial volume flux per unit circumference
q=−12μh3​pr​.
(48)
Axisymmetric conservation of mass gives ht​+r−1(rq)r​=0. Symmetry at r=0 therefore yields q=−rh˙0​/2, hence pr​=6μh˙0​r/h3. Matching to p→0 outside the pressure-bearing region gives
p=−3μah˙0​(h0​+2ar2​)−2.​
(49)
Extending the parabolic profile to infinity in this integral is a leading-order matching device; the main pressure and force come from r=O(ah0​​).
The radial scale ah​ balances the two contributions to the gap. The time scale h/v is the time for a typical gap change, and the lubrication pressure scale μav/h2 follows either from the formula above or from p∼μvℓ2/h3 with ℓ2=ah. The elastic surface's upward displacement is −ηp, so the actual gap is increased by +ηp. Thus the dimensionless gap in weakly compliant sphere-plane squeeze flow is
H=H0​+2R2​+εP,ε=h3ημav​.​
(50)
A dot now means differentiation with respect to T. The dimensionless Reynolds lubrication equation is
12HT​=R1​∂R​(RH3PR​).
(51)
Even without fluid inertia, the elastic surface has a pressure-dependent normal velocity, so HT​ includes εPT​; omitting it would wrongly discard the acceleration term below.
Write d=H0​+R2/2 and abbreviate H=H0​, v=H˙0​, b=H¨0​ during the calculation. The rigid term is
P0​=−d23v​.​
(52)
At first order the Reynolds equation gives
R1​(Rd3P1R​)R​=12P0T​−R1​(3Rd2P0​P0R​)R​=−d236b​−d3144v2​+d4324Hv2​.

Because RdR=dd, one integration gives
Rd3P1R​=2k(T)+d36b​+d272v2​−d3108Hv2​.

Set ξ=R2/(2H), so d=H(1+ξ). Dividing and changing variables yields
P1ξ​=H3ξ(1+ξ)3k​+H4ξ(1+ξ)418b​−H5ξ(1+ξ)518v2​+H5(1+ξ)654v2​.
(55)
The first three terms are derivatives of I3​,I4​,I5​, respectively. Integrating from infinity to enforce P1​→0 gives
P1​=H3k​I3​(ξ)+H418b​I4​(ξ)−H518v2​I5​(ξ)−5H554v2​(1+ξ)−5.
(56)
The rational expansion of In​ follows by differentiating log[ξ/(1+ξ)]+∑j=1n−1​[j(1+ξ)j]−1, obtaining 1/[ξ(1+ξ)n], and fixing its value to zero at infinity.
Near ξ=0 every In​ has the same singular term logξ. Its coefficient must vanish to keep the pressure regular, so
H3k​+H418b​−H518v2​=0,k=18(H2v2​−Hb​).​
(57)
Equivalently, the integrated radial flux above must vanish at R=0; otherwise P1R​ would have a 1/R singularity. Using I4​=I3​+[3(1+ξ)3]−1 and I5​=I4​+[4(1+ξ)4]−1 cancels all logarithms and gives the simplified regular expression
P1​=H04​6H¨0​​(1+ξ)−3−H05​H˙02​​[6(1+ξ)−3+29​(1+ξ)−4+554​(1+ξ)−5].​
(58)
The upward hydrodynamic force has scale μa2v/h. Its leading contribution is pressure integrated over horizontal projected area; vertical shear forces are smaller in the lubrication limit. Thus F=2π∫0∞​(P0​+εP1​+⋯)RdR. Use RdR=H0​dξ and ∫0∞​(1+ξ)−ndξ=1/(n−1). The two contributions are
2π∫P0​RdR=−H0​6πH˙0​​,2π∫P1​RdR=6π(H03​H¨0​​−512​H04​H˙02​​).
(59)
Therefore
F=6π[−H0​H˙0​​+ε(H03​H¨0​​−512​H04​H˙02​​)+⋯].​
(60)
For weight-controlled sedimentation, let the fixed effective load be F=6πA, A>0, and neglect particle inertia as well as fluid inertia. At leading order H˙0​=−AH0​ and H¨0​=A2H0​. The first-order bracket evaluated on this trajectory is −(7/5)A2/H02​. Solving the force balance perturbatively therefore gives
H˙0​=−AH0​−ε5H0​7A2​+O(ε2).​
(61)
Hence weak compliance initially increases the sedimentation speed in the regime where the perturbation is valid. The squeeze pressure indents the coating away from the sphere, widens the flow passage and lowers resistance. This is a load-controlled conclusion on the slow sedimenting branch, not a claim about an arbitrary externally imposed acceleration at startup.
The load-controlled breakdown of weak squeeze-flow compliance occurs when the relative correction O(εA/H02​) becomes order one:
H0∗​∼εA​,H0∗​∼ε​ for A=O(1).​
(62)
The central rigid pressure has size P0​(0)∼A/H0​, so the relative elastic gap change is also εP0​(0)/H0​∼εA/H02​. Thus breakdown means that elastic deflection is comparable with the nominal undeformed gap. The regular expansion about a rigid plane then ceases to be uniform and the coupled elastic-lubrication problem must be retained. It does not by itself imply contact, fluid inertia or failure of the thin-gap geometry.
