Nuclear burning initially waits at the deuterium bottleneck: although deuterium is bound by about 2.2MeV, the many photons per baryon can still photodissociate it. Roughly, ηb−1​e−BD​/(kB​TN​)∼1 sets the bottleneck temperature, with refinements from reaction rates and phase space. Around kB​TN​∼0.1MeV, deuterium survives, reactions through deuterium, tritium and helium-3 rapidly build tightly bound helium-4, and nearly all surviving neutrons are captured.

For the standard estimate rN​≃1/7, neutron and proton constituent fractions are 1/8 and 7/8, respectively, and YHe​≃1/4. Free neutrons are negligible after capture. The remaining hydrogen mass fraction is about 3/4, and the helium-to-hydrogen number ratio is about 1/12.
The printed numerical time coefficients are schematic, not a precision prediction of this quarter fraction: their literal crossing with g∗​=10.75 gives kB​Tf​≃1.28MeV. Keeping physical weak-rate coefficients and the gradual freeze-out instead gives an effective freeze-out around 0.7–0.8MeV, followed by decay over a few minutes. The abundance formulas above explain the standard quarter fraction and its parameter dependence without treating the rounded time formulas as exact nuclear-rate data.
