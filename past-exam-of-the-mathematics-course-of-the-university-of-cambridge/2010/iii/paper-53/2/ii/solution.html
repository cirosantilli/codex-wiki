For an equilibrium relativistic bath, P=ρ/3 and ρ∝g∗s​T4, so s=(ρ+P)/T∝g∗s​T3. Cosmological entropy conservation gives g∗s​T3a3=constant. A decoupled relativistic species instead has aTrelic​=constant, so annihilation heats the bath relative to that relic.
Use the sharp relativistic degree-counting approximation specified by the temperature interval. Just before muon annihilation, the coupled bath contains photons, electron-positron pairs, muon-antimuon pairs and three neutrino-antineutrino species:
g∗sbefore​=2+87​(4+4+6)=457​.
(11)
After muon annihilation, neutrinos are still coupled and g∗safter​=2+(7/8)(4+6)=43/4. The decoupled H particles are excluded from these bath counts. Equality of bath and relic temperatures at decoupling therefore gives the relic cooling across muon annihilation
Tγ​Trelic​​=(5743​)1/3≃0.910​.
(12)
By the later electron-mass epoch, neutrinos have decoupled. Electron-positron annihilation heats only the electromagnetic bath, reducing its entropy degree count from 2+(7/8)4=11/2 to 2. Photons are heated by (11/4)1/3 relative to both neutrinos and H particles. After this transition,
Tγ​Trelic​​=(5743​114​)1/3=(627172​)1/3≃0.650​.
(13)
The neutrino-to-photon ratio is (4/11)1/3, while the H-particle-to-neutrino ratio remains (43/57)1/3. The printed 0.59MeV marks the electron-positron annihilation epoch approximately; it is not an exact annihilation threshold.
