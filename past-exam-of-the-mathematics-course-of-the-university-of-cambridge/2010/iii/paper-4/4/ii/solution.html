Replace the determinant condition by preservation of a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form B on F2m. In a symplectic basis the symplectic group over a finite field is
Sp2m​(q)={g∈GL2m​(q):gTJg=J},J=(0−Im​​Im​0​).
(36)
To count symplectic bases, choose the first vector e in q2m−1 ways, then choose f with B(e,f)=1 in q2m−1 ways. Their span is nondegenerate; its symplectic orthogonal complement has dimension 2m−2 and supplies the rest of the basis. The resulting recurrence gives the finite symplectic group order
∣Sp2m​(q)∣=qm2i=1∏m​(q2i−1).​
(37)
The elementary replacements are symplectic transvections
Tv,c​(x)=x+cB(x,v)v,v=0, c∈F.
(38)
Expanding B(Tx,Ty) shows that the two cross terms cancel and the last term vanishes because B(v,v)=0. Thus these maps preserve B; their inverses are Tv,−c​.
We need both generation and primitivity to use the argument from part (i). For nonzero vectors v,w with B(v,w)=0, the symplectic transvection with direction w−v and parameter 1/B(v,w) sends v to w. When B(v,w)=0, choose z nonorthogonal to both and use two such steps. Such z exists because two proper hyperplanes cannot cover a vector space: if the two linear forms are independent prescribe both values to be one, and if dependent prescribe either a nonzero value. Hence symplectic transvections act transitively on nonzero vectors.
They generate the whole symplectic group over a finite field. Indeed first use them to make a given symplectic transformation fix e1​. Its image f′ of f1​ still satisfies B(e1​,f′)=1. A symplectic transvection with direction f1​−f′ fixes e1​ and sends f′ to f1​ if the pairing is nonzero. If that pairing is zero, pass through f′+ce1​ for any c=0: it has nonzero pairing with both partners, and both required directions lie in e1⊥​. We have now fixed the first hyperbolic pair, and induction applies in its nondegenerate orthogonal complement. The final two-dimensional case is exactly the elementary generation of SL2​(q)=Sp2​(q) proved in part (i).
For a fixed projective point ⟨e⟩, the symplectic transvections Te,c​ form an abelian subgroup normal in its stabilizer subgroup; conjugation scales c by the square of the scalar multiplying e. Their conjugates supply all symplectic transvections. A central element commutes with every symplectic transvection, so it preserves every direction line and must be scalar. Conversely scalars commute with the whole group. The kernel of the projective action consists of scalar matrices, and preservation of B restricts the scalar to λ2=1. Thus the center of a group has size gcd(2,q−1) and the projective symplectic group over a finite field acts faithfully.
For m≥2 the stabilizer subgroup has precisely three orbits: its fixed point, the other points in e⊥, and the points outside e⊥. Transitivity on each follows by extending, respectively, an independent isotropic pair or a hyperbolic pair to a symplectic basis. Write
N=q−1q2m−1​,h=q−1q2m−1−1​.
(39)
A nontrivial proper block through the fixed point would be a union of these stabilizer subgroup orbits, hence would have size either h or q2m−1+1. A block size divides N. But N−qh=1, so h>1 cannot divide N, and N/2<q2m−1+1<N, excluding the other size. This proves primitivity. For m=1 the projective action is the two-transitive action of PSL2​(q).
Here is a direct check of perfectness of finite symplectic groups, including the small fields where the diagonal group commutator needs replacement. If q>3, every symplectic transvection lies in an embedded SL2​(q) on a hyperbolic plane containing its direction, and that subgroup is perfect by part (i). Since the symplectic transvections generate, the whole symplectic group over a finite field is perfect.
For q=3 and m≥2, all Tv,1​ are conjugate, so their common image z generates the abelianization and satisfies 3z=0. Choose independent perpendicular vectors e,f. The four commuting symplectic transvections with directions e,f,e+f,e−f and parameter one multiply to the identity: the sum of their rank-one matrices is zero in characteristic 3. Thus also 4z=0, giving z=0.
For q=2 and m≥3, use the seven nonzero vectors of a three-dimensional isotropic subspace of a symplectic vector space. Their symplectic transvections commute, and their product is the identity. To see this explicitly, write a vector in three isotropic basis coordinates: in the sum of the outer products vvT each diagonal coefficient occurs four times and each off-diagonal coefficient twice, all zero in characteristic 2. The common abelianization image z therefore satisfies 2z=7z=0. Again the group is perfect.
The simplicity argument from part (i) now applies to the projective symplectic group over a finite field. The remaining exceptional group besides Sp2​(2) and PSp2​(3) is Sp4​(2). It is not simple: it is isomorphic to S6​. One can verify this identification without assuming it. On F24​ set
Q0​(x1​,x2​,y1​,y2​)=x1​y1​+x2​y2​,Qa​(v)=Q0​(v)+B(a,v).
(40)
These are all 16 quadratic forms with polar form B; the Arf invariant of a quadratic form gives Arf(Qa​)=Q0​(a). There are six with invariant one. The symplectic group over a finite field permutes these six forms. Differences of their parameter vectors span F24​: writing ei​,fi​ for the symplectic basis, the six parameters are
e1​+f1​, e1​+f1​+e2​, e1​+f1​+f2​, e2​+f2​, e2​+f2​+e1​, e2​+f2​+f1​.
(41)
Their pairwise differences include each of e1​,e2​,f1​,f2​. An element fixing all six forms therefore fixes all linear forms B(a−b,−), hence is the identity. This gives a faithful action on six objects; the group order is 720, so its image is all of S6​. Consequently
∣PSp2m​(q)∣=gcd(2,q−1)qm2∏i=1m​(q2i−1)​,PSp2m​(q) simple except (m,q)=(1,2),(1,3),(2,2).​
(42)
