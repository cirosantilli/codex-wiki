For a subset S of a finite p-group, its images span the Frattini quotient precisely when ⟨S⟩Φ(G)=G. By the generating property proved in part (ii), this is equivalent to ⟨S⟩=G.
If S is a minimal generating set of a group, its images must be an irredundant spanning set in the vector space G/Φ(G). A linear dependence would express one image using the others; those other images would still span, so the other elements would still generate G, a contradiction. Thus the images form a basis. Conversely lifts of a basis generate G, and deleting a lift destroys spanning and hence generation.
This proves the Burnside basis theorem, including equality of sizes for every inclusion-minimal generating set of a group:
