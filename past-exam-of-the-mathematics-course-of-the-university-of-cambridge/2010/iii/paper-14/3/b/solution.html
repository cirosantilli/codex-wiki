First the common boundary distance function determines the induced tangential Riemannian metric. For a boundary curve c(t) with c(0)=x and c′(0)=w∈Tx​∂M, the local first-order distance expansion gives
limt→0​∣t∣dgi​​(x,c(t))​=∣w∣gi​​.
(29)
For clarity, the upper bound follows from the length of the boundary curve. In a small coordinate neighbourhood the Riemannian metric is arbitrarily close to its value at x, so the length of any sufficiently short joining path is bounded below by that constant-metric displacement norm with an arbitrarily small relative error. A minimizing short path cannot leave that neighbourhood. These two bounds prove the limit. Equality of the boundary distance functions then gives ∣w∣g1​​=∣w∣g2​​. The polarization identity yields
g1​∣T∂M​=g2​∣T∂M​=:h.
(30)
This alone fixes no choice of normal coordinates, so a boundary-fixing gauge is needed to compare the full tangent-space Riemannian metrics.
Let ni​ be the inward unit normals and let
Ci​(x,r)=expxgi​​(rni​(x))
(31)
be sufficiently small boundary normal coordinates. Their pullbacks have the form Ci∗​gi​=dr2+hi​(r), with hi​(0)=h. On a collar define ψ=C2​∘C1−1​. It fixes the boundary, has Dψ(w)=w for tangential w, and sends n1​ to n2​. For tangent-space vectors w+an1​ and z+bn1​ at the boundary, therefore,
(ψ∗g2​)(w+an1​,z+bn1​)=h(w,z)+ab=g1​(w+an1​,z+bn1​).
(32)
This proves the desired full Riemannian metric equality locally at the boundary.
We also justify extending this local identification to a global diffeomorphism. Interpolate the positive Riemannian metrics by gs​=(1−s)g1​+sg2​, for 0≤s≤1. Let Cs​ be their inward unit-normal exponential collars. Compactness of the boundary and parameter interval gives a common small collar width for all s. The intermediate Riemannian metrics need not be simple; only these local collars are used.
On the image of Cs​, define the time-dependent vector field
Ys​(Cs​(x,r))=χ(r)∂s​Cs​(x,r),
(33)
where χ is one near zero and vanishes before the outer edge of the collar; extend Ys​ by zero elsewhere. This is a smooth vector field on M, vanishing at the boundary because Cs​(x,0)=x. Its time-dependent flow Ψs​ is a global diffeomorphism fixing the boundary. For every sufficiently small fixed r, the curve s↦Cs​(x,r) solves its equation, since χ(r)=1. Uniqueness gives Ψs​(C0​(x,r))=Cs​(x,r). Consequently Ψ1​ agrees near the boundary with C2​C1−1​. Taking ψ=Ψ1​ proves
ψ∣∂M​=id,ψ∗g2​=g1​ on every Tx​M (x∈∂M)​.
(34)
