If X is an affine scheme, each reduced irreducible component is a closed subscheme, hence an affine scheme. We prove the converse by explicit affine gluing along closed subschemes.
First suppose X is the scheme-theoretic union of two closed subschemes which are affine schemes Y and Z: their defining ideal sheaves have zero intersection. Their scheme-theoretic intersection W is a closed subscheme of both, hence an affine scheme. Write
Y=SpecA,Z=SpecB,W=SpecC.
(35)
The two maps A→C and B→C are surjective. Form the fiber product of rings
R=A×C​B={(a,b):a∣W​=b∣W​}.
(36)
Both projections are surjective. Let I=ker(R→A) and J=ker(R→B). Then
I∩J=IJ=0,R/I=A,R/J=B,R/(I+J)=C.
(37)
For the last equality, the common image map R→C is surjective, and its kernel of a ring homomorphism consists exactly of pairs whose two coordinates map to zero. Therefore SpecR has closed pieces isomorphic to Y and Z, with intersection W. They cover it because each prime ideal contains I or J, as IJ=0.
A finite closed cover determines the topology: a subset is closed if and only if its intersections with both closed pieces are closed. The identifications of Y,Z,W therefore give a homeomorphism between X and SpecR. To check the structure sheaf, on X the elementary ideal-intersection sequence gives
0⟶OX​⟶i∗​OY​⊕j∗​OZ​(a,b)↦a∣W​−b∣W​​k∗​OW​⟶0.
(38)
It is exact on stalks, by IY​∩IZ​=0. The same sequence holds on SpecR, using I∩J=0. Under the homeomorphism, the two middle sheaves and their restriction maps identify, so their kernels identify as sheaves of rings. This identifies the structure sheaves, giving an isomorphism of schemes X≃SpecR. The intersection W need not be reduced.
Now a Noetherian scheme has finitely many irreducible components Y1​,…,Yr​. Induct on r. For r=1, the reduced scheme is its single reduced irreducible component, so is an affine scheme. For r>1, let Y=Y1​ and let Z be the reduced union of the remaining irreducible components. Its components are the corresponding Yi​, so Z is an affine scheme by induction. The defining ideal sheaves of Y and Z have zero intersection: their intersection is the ideal sheaf of the reduced union of all components, which is zero because X is reduced. The two-piece argument proves that X is an affine scheme. The empty scheme is Spec0 and causes no exception. Hence
X affine ⟺ every reduced irreducible component of X is affine.​
(39)
