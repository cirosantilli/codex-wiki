The affine space X=Akn​ is an integral scheme, hence its underlying topological space is irreducible. Every nonempty open subset of an irreducible topological space is irreducible and in particular connected. A section of the constant sheaf with value the function field K=k(t1​,…,tn​) is a locally constant function, so on any such open subset it is constant. Thus its sections are K on each nonempty open subset and zero on the empty set. All restriction maps are either identities on K or maps onto zero.
The constant sheaf is therefore a flasque sheaf. It has its natural OX​-module structure because a regular function on a nonempty open subset embeds in the function field and acts by multiplication. Part (i) now gives
H0(X,F)=K,Hp(X,F)=0(p>0).​
(34)
This is an instance of constant sheaves on irreducible spaces are flasque; no restriction on n is needed.
