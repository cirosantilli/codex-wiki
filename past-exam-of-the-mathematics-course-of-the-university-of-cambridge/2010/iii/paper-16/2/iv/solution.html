We first justify surjectivity is preserved by base change for morphisms of schemes. Given y′ over y and a point x over y, the fiber after the residue-field extension contains a point because
κ(x)⊗κ(y)​κ(y′)=0.
(12)
Indeed, tensoring a nonzero vector space over a field with a field extension remains nonzero; a nonzero commutative ring has a prime ideal, giving the required point. Thus every base change of f is surjective.
Now make an arbitrary base change Z′→Z. For a closed subset C⊆Y′=Y×Z​Z′, its inverse image under f′:X′→Y′ is closed. The proper morphism (gf)′:X′→Z′ therefore has closed image on that inverse image. Surjectivity of f′ gives
g′(C)=(g′f′)((f′)−1(C)),
(13)
so g′(C) is closed. This proves that g is a universally closed morphism. Together with the given separatedness and finite type, it proves
gf proper, f surjective, g separated and of finite type ⟹ g proper.​
(14)
