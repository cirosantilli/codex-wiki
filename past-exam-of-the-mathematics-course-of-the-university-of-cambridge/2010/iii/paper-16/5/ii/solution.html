Put P=Pk2​ and S=k[t0​,t1​,t2​]. A homogeneous polynomial F of positive degree d is nonzero, and S is an integral domain. Multiplication by F therefore gives an exact sequence of coherent sheaves
0⟶OP​(−d) F ​OP​⟶i∗​OX​⟶0,
(42)
where i:X↪P is the closed immersion.
We compute the necessary cohomology of twisting sheaves on projective space. The standard three affine open subsets D+​(ti​) and all their intersections are affine schemes. For a quasi-coherent sheaf, vanishing of quasi-coherent cohomology on an affine scheme and the acyclic cover theorem identify its sheaf cohomology with the Čech cohomology of this cover. In degree m, the Čech cochain complex is
⨁i​(Sti​​)m​⟶⨁i<j​(Sti​tj​​)m​⟶(St0​t1​t2​​)m​.
(43)
Decompose it into subcomplexes indexed by Laurent monomials t0a0​​t1a1​​t2a2​​ with a0​+a1​+a2​=m. Let N={i:ai​<0}. Such a monomial occurs in the summand for a nonempty index set J precisely when N⊆J, since only the variables in J are inverted.
If N is empty, the monomial contributes the cochain complex of a full two-simplex: its degree-zero cohomology is k and its higher cohomology vanishes. If ∣N∣=1, the dimensions in degrees 0,1,2 are 1,2,1; the first map has rank one and the second map has rank one with the same kernel as the first map's image. If ∣N∣=2, there is one copy of k in each of degrees 1 and 2 and the map between them is an isomorphism. These two cases are acyclic over every field, including characteristic two. If N={0,1,2}, only the degree-two term occurs, contributing one copy of k. It follows that
H1(P,OP​(m))=0for every m,
(44)
and that H0(P,OP​)=k, H0(P,OP​(−d))=0, and H2(P,OP​)=0. Moreover the Laurent-monomial description of top cohomology on projective space gives a basis of H2(P,OP​(−d)) consisting of
t0−b0​​t1−b1​​t2−b2​​,bi​≥1,b0​+b1​+b2​=d.
(45)
For d≥3, subtracting one from each bi​ counts these as (2d−1​). For d=1,2 there are no such triples, and the formula (d−1)(d−2)/2 is also zero.
Use the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology of the initial sequence, and cohomology under a closed immersion to identify the terms for i∗​OX​. The vanishings just proved give
H0(X,OX​)≃k,H1(X,OX​)≃H2(P,OP​(−d)).
(46)
Therefore
dimk​H0(X,OX​)=1,dimk​H1(X,OX​)=2(d−1)(d−2)​.​
(47)
This proves the cohomology of a projective plane hypersurface without imposing smoothness, irreducibility or reducedness. In particular, the second number is an arithmetic genus, not a claim about the genus of a normalization.
