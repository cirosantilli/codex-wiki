Use the local-presentation definition of a quasi-coherent sheaf: locally it admits a presentation O(I)→O(J)→F→0 by direct sums of copies of the structure sheaf, with no finiteness requirement on the index sets. If M is an A-module, choose a free module presentation A(I)→A(J)→M→0. Exactness of localization of a module gives the corresponding local presentation of M. Hence a sheaf which is associated with a module on every affine open subset is a quasi-coherent sheaf.
For the converse, begin with a quasi-coherent sheaf and a local presentation on an affine open subset V=SpecR. The free sheaves have global sections R(I) and R(J): a section has locally finite support, and quasi-compactness of V makes its support finite globally. Part (i) identifies the presentation map with an R-module homomorphism. Exactness of localization then identifies its cokernel sheaf with MV​ for the module cokernel MV​. Thus there is an affine open cover on which F is associated with modules.
It remains to show this on an arbitrary affine open subset U=SpecA, rather than only the selected cover. Around each point of U, choose an affine neighborhood contained in U with such a local presentation. Choose a finite cover U=⋃i=1r​D(si​) refining these neighborhoods. On each D(si​) the restriction is associated with a module. Its restriction to D(si​sj​) is therefore the corresponding localization, and the same is true after intersecting with D(a). Put M=Γ(U,F).
