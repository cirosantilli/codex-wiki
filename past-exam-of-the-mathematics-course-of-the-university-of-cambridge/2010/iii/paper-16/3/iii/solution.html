Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2010 / iii / Paper 16 / 3 / iii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2010 iii Paper 16 3 iii
OurBigBook.com Words: 307
Work on an affine open subset U=SpecA and write F∣U​=M and E∣U​=N using part (ii). We first show that any section e∈Γ(U,E) lifts to Γ(U,G).
A surjection of sheaves supplies local lifts gi​ on a finite cover by principal open subsets D(si​). Their differences
cij​=gi​−gj​∈Msi​sj​​
(24)
satisfy cij​+cjk​=cik​ on triple intersections. We spell out why this Čech cocycle is a coboundary. Choose a common sufficiently large power n so that sjn​cij​ can be represented in Msi​​ for every pair. Increasing n also kills the finitely many localization errors in the cocycle identities: after extending skn​cik​ to Msi​​ and skn​cjk​ to Msj​​, their difference in Msi​sj​​ is skn​cij​. This follows because any error which vanishes after localizing at sk​ is killed by a power of sk​; there are only finitely many indices.
The D(sin​) still cover U, so the ideal generated by the sin​ is A. Choose ai​∈A with ∑i​ai​sin​=1 and put
bi​=∑k​ak​skn​cik​∈Msi​​,
(25)
using the extended representatives just chosen. On an overlap, the cocycle identity gives
bi​−bj​=∑k​ak​skn​cij​=cij​.
(26)
Thus gi​−bi​ agree and glue to a lift of e. This is the degree-one argument behind the exactness of the unit-ideal localization Čech complex.
The same reasoning applies to every D(a)⊆U. Hence we have short exact sequences of modules of sections on U and on D(a). Localize the first sequence at a and compare them. The maps on the outer modules are the isomorphisms from part (ii):
00​→→​Γ(U,F)a​↓≃Γ(D(a),F)​→→​Γ(U,G)a​↓Γ(D(a),G)​→→​Γ(U,E)a​↓≃Γ(D(a),E)​→→​00.​
(27)
An elementary diagram chase makes the middle map an isomorphism: subtract a lift from the right to reduce surjectivity to the left, and use injectivity on the left to prove injectivity. These isomorphisms identify G∣U​ with the quasi-coherent sheaf associated with Γ(U,G). Doing this on every affine open subset proves
G is quasi-coherent.​
(28)
The proof even works without the Noetherian assumption.
