For a<b, give the pair {a,b} the color determined by v2​(b−a)mod2, where v2​ is the 2-adic valuation: use red for even valuation and blue for odd valuation. This finite coloring works because the pairs {a,a+d} and {a,a+2d} have valuations v2​(d) and v2​(d)+1. They therefore have opposite colors. In particular, no three-term arithmetic progression has all its pairs monochromatic.
For the second assertion, m=1 is immediate: every singleton is a blue complete arithmetic progression, since it has no pairs to check. Assume m≥2. If some m-term arithmetic progression has all its pairs blue, we are done. Otherwise, every such arithmetic progression contains a red pair. For each (a,d)∈N2, choose, say lexicographically, indices 0≤r<s<m for which {a+rd,a+sd} is red, and color (a,d) by this chosen pair (r,s). This is a finite coloring with at most (2m​) colors.
We need a lattice pattern that works whichever pair becomes the common color. Fix, before applying the Gallai theorem for an integer lattice, the finite set
F=⋃0≤r<s<m​{(si−rj,j−i):0≤i,j<m}⊆Z2.
(1)
Translate F into the positive quadrant and apply the Gallai theorem for an integer lattice to that translated pattern. Absorbing the translation into the base point gives integers a0​,d0​ and q>0 such that (a0​,d0​)+qF⊆N2 is monochromatic. Write (r,s) for its common color. Since (0,0)∈F, the base point itself has positive coordinates.
For each 0≤i,j<m, the parameter point
(a,d)=(a0​+q(si−rj), d0​+q(j−i))
(2)
belongs to that monochromatic copy and has chosen red pair (r,s). Its red endpoints simplify to
a+rd=a0​+rd0​+q(s−r)i,a+sd=a0​+sd0​+q(s−r)j.
(3)
Consequently, putting
A={a0​+rd0​+q(s−r)i:0≤i<m},B={a0​+sd0​+q(s−r)j:0≤j<m},
(4)
gives two m-term arithmetic progressions of positive common difference q(s−r), with every cross-pair red. These arithmetic progressions really are disjoint: the parameter point corresponding to i=m−1,j=0 has positive second coordinate, so d0​>q(m−1). Hence
minB−maxA=(s−r)(d0​−q(m−1))>0.
(5)
This proves the arithmetic-progression bipartite Ramsey dichotomy, including the required disjointness.
