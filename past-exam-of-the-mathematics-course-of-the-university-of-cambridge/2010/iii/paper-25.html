For ℜs>1, the Euler products are

The Dirichlet character is completely multiplicative, and unique prime factorization expands each finite product into the terms of the corresponding Dirichlet series supported on those primes. Absolute convergence permits passage to all primes. More strongly, the absolutely convergent Euler product logarithm gives
L(s,χ)=exp(∑p​∑m≥1​mpmsχ(p)m​)=0,
(29)
since the sum of absolute values is bounded by ∑n≥2​∑m≥1​n−mℜs/m<∞.
We prove nonvanishing of a nonprincipal Dirichlet L-function at one, including real Dirichlet characters. If χ is nonprincipal modulo q, periodicity and the given Orthogonality of Dirichlet characters bound Aχ​(x)=∑n≤x​χ(n) by q. Partial summation gives
L(s,χ)=s∫1∞​Aχ​(x)x−s−1dx,
(30)
which is holomorphic for ℜs>0 by locally uniform convergence, including after differentiation. The principal Dirichlet character instead has

(31)
with a simple pole at one.
For a nonreal Dirichlet character, consider P(s)=∏χmodq​L(s,χ). For real σ>1, its Euler product logarithm is

The given character inversion with a=1 makes each inner sum either φ(q) or zero, so P(σ)≥1. All nonprincipal factors are holomorphic at one, and only the principal factor has a simple pole. Their two zeros would force P(σ)→0, a contradiction.
For a real nonprincipal Dirichlet character, suppose L(1,χ)=0. Then G(s)=ζ(s)L(s,χ) is holomorphic throughout ℜs>0: the supplied continuation of the Riemann zeta function has no other pole there, and the zero cancels the pole at one. On ℜs>1 its Dirichlet series has coefficients

(33)
To check the sign explicitly, for a prime power pj the local sum is j+1 if χ(p)=1, is 1 for even j and 0 for odd j if χ(p)=−1, and is 1 if χ(p)=0. Multiplication over prime factors proves nonnegativity and gm2​≥1. Also gn​≤τ(n), so termwise derivatives at s=2 are justified by absolute convergence:
(−1)jG(j)(2)=∑n​n2gn​(logn)j​≥0.
(34)
The disc ∣s−2∣<2 lies in ℜs>0. Its Taylor series therefore converges at s=1/2, and Tonelli theorem gives
G(1/2)=∑j≥0​j!(3/2)j​∑n​n2gn​(logn)j​=∑n​n1/2gn​​≥∑m≥1​m1​=∞.
(35)
This contradicts holomorphy at 1/2. It is the positive-coefficient argument behind the Landau theorem for a Dirichlet series with nonnegative coefficients, proved here rather than invoked. Thus L(1,χ)=0 for every nonprincipal Dirichlet character.
It remains to detect the primes. For real σ>1, the Euler product logarithm satisfies

uniformly as σ↓1: terms with m≥2 are bounded in absolute value by ∑n≥2​∑m≥2​n−m/m<∞. ∑p​χ0​(p)p−σ=logσ−11​+Oq​(1).

Write e(t)=exp(2πit) and use ∥t∥ for distance to the nearest integer. The Dirichlet approximation theorem, in its pigeonhole form, says that for every real α and integer Q≥1 there are coprime integers a,q with 1≤q≤Q and ∣α−a/q∣≤1/(qQ). Indeed put the Q+1 fractional parts of 0,α,…,Qα into Q equal intervals; two lie in the same interval. Their difference gives ∣qα−a∣≤1/Q, and reducing the fraction preserves the claimed bound. This includes rational α.
For t≥s, the finite geometric series has length L=t−s+1. When θ∈/Z,
​∑n=st​e(θn)​=​1−e(θ)e(θs)(1−e(Lθ))​​≤∣sinπθ∣1​≤2∥θ∥1​.
(39)
The last inequality uses the concavity of sine on [0,π/2]. The triangle inequality also gives L. At integer θ the sum has absolute value L, interpreting the reciprocal as infinity. Thus the precise exponential geometric sum bound is
​n=s∑t​e(θn)​≤min(L,2∥θ∥1​).​
(40)
The interval must be nonempty; for t<s its sum is zero and its length should be defined as max(0,t−s+1), rather than using a potentially negative bound.
For the separated points, assume Q≥2 and δ>0. An arc of length 2r≤1 contains at most 1+2r/δ of the points, by ordering them along the arc. Points with ∥θi​∥≤1/Q contribute O(Q+δ−1). In the dyadic annulus 2j/Q<∥θi​∥≤2j+1/Q, each summand is O(Q2−j) and there are at most 1+2j+2/(Qδ) points. There are O(log(2Q)) nonempty annuli, giving the stronger separated reciprocal-distance sum
i∑​min(Q,2∥θi​∥1​)≪Q+δ−1log(2Q)≪(Q+δ−1)logQ.​

If there is only one point the bound is immediate; otherwise δ≤1/2. The printed logQ requires a lower bound away from Q=1; log(2Q) avoids that endpoint defect.

(44)

For q<8 or N<2, the trivial bound supplies the same assertion with an absolute constant. The same proof applies to ∑n=1N​e(αn2), whose difference sums also have interval endpoints.
We finally prove a polynomial rate of quantitative quadratic recurrence, without using the optional finite-group assumption. For 0<ε≤1/2, use the periodic triangular bump b(t)=(1−∥t∥/ε)+​.
