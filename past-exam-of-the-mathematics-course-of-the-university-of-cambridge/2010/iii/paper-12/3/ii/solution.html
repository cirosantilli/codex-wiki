We use the directed set version of the Talagrand convex distance:

Here a coordinate is an entire sampled point, so xi​=yi​ means the two planar points differ. This is the distance from the upper event A to the lower event B; its order is relevant because the convex distance need not be symmetric.
Fix x∈A and choose exactly a indices I whose points form a convex chain with the endpoints. Such a choice exists even if Ln​(x)>a, by taking a subchain. It is a certificate of the certifiable function Ln​. For any y∈B, let J={i∈I:xi​=yi​}. The a−∣J∣ unchanged selected points remain a convex chain in configuration y, so
a−∣J∣≤Ln​(y)≤b,∣J∣≥a−b.
(19)
Choose the nonnegative weights αi​=a−1/2 for i∈I and zero otherwise. They have squared sum one, and for every y∈B,
∑i:xi​=yi​​αi​=a​∣J∣​≥a​a−b​.
(20)
Taking the infimum over y, then the supremum over weights, and finally the infimum over x∈A proves
dT​(A,B)≥a​a−b​.​
(21)
This proof also works for b=0 if zero-length chains are admitted, and gives the usual infinite separation when one of the events is empty. For the uniform sampling model, Ln​≥1 almost surely when n≥1.
