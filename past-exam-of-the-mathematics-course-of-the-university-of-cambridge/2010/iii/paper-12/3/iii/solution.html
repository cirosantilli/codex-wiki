The precise external concentration result we use is Talagrand's convex distance inequality: for a finite product of independent standard probability spaces, a measurable event B of positive probability, and u≥0,
P(B)P(dT​(X,B)≥u)≤e−u2/4.
(22)
Our coordinates are the independent uniformly sampled points of the triangle, so the hypothesis holds. Part (ii) therefore gives, for integers a>b≥0,
P(Ln​≥a)P(Ln​≤b)≤exp(−4a(a−b)2​).
(23)
Indeed every configuration in the first event has at least the distance from part (ii) to the second event. If one event is empty, the displayed product bound is automatic. This is the only use of the product-space theorem; the geometric separation was proved directly in part (ii). A precise source for that theorem is Section 4.1 of Talagrand's original product-space concentration paper.
Choose an integer median m=mn​, so P(Ln​≤m)≥1/2 and P(Ln​≥m)≥1/2. First set a=m+t, b=m in the product bound, and then set a=m, b=m−t. For positive integers t this yields the convex-chain median concentration estimates
P(Ln​≥m+t)≤2e−t2/(4(m+t)),P(Ln​≤m−t)≤2e−t2/(4m).​
(24)
The lower event is empty if m−t<1 in our sampling model; the upper event is empty if m+t>n. Thus the stated bounds remain valid for all positive integers t. Also m≥1 for n≥1.
By part (i), m≤Cn1/3 with C=4e. Let hn​=ω(n)n1/6/2, take tn​=⌊hn​⌋+1, and use the deterministic interval In​=[m−hn​,m+hn​]. Its length is exactly ω(n)n1/6. As Ln​ and m are integers, falling outside this interval implies one of the two tail events with t=tn​. Therefore
P(Ln​∈/In​)≤4exp(−4(m+tn​)tn2​​).
(25)
For positive m,t,
4(m+t)t2​≥81​min{mt2​,t}.
(26)
Since tn​≥hn​ and m≤Cn1/3, this exponent is at least
81​min{4Cω(n)2​,2ω(n)n1/6​}⟶∞.
(27)
Consequently
P(Ln​∈[mn​−21​ω(n)n1/6, mn​+21​ω(n)n1/6])⟶1.​
(28)
Thus Ln​ lies with high probability in the required interval. This proof works for every ω(n)→∞, including growth faster than n1/6; no extra restriction on ω is needed. The scale comes from the square root of the O(n1/3) certificate size at the median.
