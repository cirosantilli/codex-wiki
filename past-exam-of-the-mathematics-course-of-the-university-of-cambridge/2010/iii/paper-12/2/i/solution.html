Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2010 / iii / Paper 12 / 2 / i / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 341
Use the uniform probability measure on the symmetric group Sn​ and the unnormalized Hamming distance
dH​(σ,τ)=∣{j:σ(j)=τ(j)}∣.
(5)
Let f:Sn​→R have Lipschitz constant K>0 for this distance. We will prove the one-sided concentration inequalities
P(f−Ef≥t), P(f−Ef≤−t)≤exp(−2nK2t2​),t≥0.​
(6)
Consequently P(∣f−Ef∣≥t)≤2e−t2/(2nK2) for t>0. A zero Lipschitz constant makes f constant, so that case is immediate.
Let σ be a uniform permutation, reveal σ(1),…,σ(k) successively, and define the Doob exposure martingale
Mk​=E[f(σ)∣σ(1),…,σ(k)],M0​=Ef,Mn​=f(σ).
(7)
Fix the first k−1 images. For an available value a, let h(a) be the conditional mean when σ(k)=a. Given two available values a,b, left-composition with the transposition exchanging a,b is a bijection from completions with next image a to completions with next image b. It fixes the previously exposed images and changes exactly two coordinates of each complete permutation. Since conditional completions are uniform, averaging the Lipschitz condition over this pairing gives ∣h(a)−h(b)∣≤2K.
Thus, conditionally on the first k−1 images, Mk​−Mk−1​ has mean zero and takes values in an interval of length at most 2K. We need the range-length version of the Hoeffding lemma: if a real random variable Z has mean zero and lies in an interval of length ℓ, then
EeλZ≤eλ2ℓ2/8(λ∈R).
(8)
Here is a proof. For ψ(λ)=logEeλZ, differentiation gives ψ′′(λ)=Varλ​(Z) under the exponentially tilted probability distribution. Any distribution on an interval of length ℓ has variance at most ℓ2/4, because its squared distance from the interval midpoint is at most ℓ2/4. Since ψ(0)=ψ′(0)=0, integrating this second-derivative bound gives the lemma for both signs of λ. The same argument holds for every conditional distribution.
Apply it conditionally to each martingale difference and use the tower property of conditional expectation repeatedly:
Eeλ(Mn​−M0​)≤enλ2K2/2.
(9)
The exponential Markov bound now gives, for λ>0,
P(Mn​−M0​≥t)≤exp(−λt+nλ2K2/2).
(10)
Choose λ=t/(nK2) to obtain the upper tail in the box, and apply the argument to −f for the lower tail. This proves concentration of Lipschitz functions on the symmetric group. Using only the bound ∣Mk​−Mk−1​∣≤2K in the absolute-increment Azuma-Hoeffding inequality would lose a factor four in the exponent; the conditional range length is what yields the constant needed in part (ii).
