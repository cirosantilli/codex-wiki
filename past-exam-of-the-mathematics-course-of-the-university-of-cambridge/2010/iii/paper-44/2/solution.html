Use the free-boson operator product expansion
Xμ(z,zˉ)Xν(w,wˉ)∼−2α′​ημνlog∣z−w∣2.
(12)
Write Ep​=:eip⋅X: and ϵ=z−w. The needed Wick contractions are ∂Xμ(z)∂Xν(w)∼−α′ημν/(2ϵ2) and ∂Xμ(z)Ep​(w)∼−iα′pμEp​(w)/(2ϵ). For Vζ​=ζμ​:∂XμEp​:, the cross double contraction in the worldsheet stress tensor has a cubic pole: either of its two derivatives can contract with the derivative in Vζ​, while the other contracts with Ep​. Including the factor −1/α′ gives
T(z)Vζ​(w)∼−2iα′​ϵ3p⋅ζ​Ep​(w)+ϵ21+α′p2/4​Vζ​(w)+ϵ1​∂Vζ​(w).
(13)
A primary operator has no cubic pole. Hence the first vertex is primary precisely when
p⋅ζ=0,h=1+α′p2/4.​
(14)
The antiholomorphic conformal weight is hˉ=α′p2/4. Primarity alone puts no mass-shell condition on p.
For Wζ​=ζμν​:∂Xμ∂ˉXνEp​:, the holomorphic cubic pole is −iα′pμζμν​:∂ˉXνEp​:/(2ϵ3). The antiholomorphic operator product expansion gives the analogous pole involving pνζμν​. Thus the transversality of derivative-exponential primary vertices requires
pμζμν​=0,pνζμν​=0,(h,hˉ)=(1+α′p2/4,1+α′p2/4).​
(15)
There is no trace-free requirement for primarity. A physical integrated string vertex operator must additionally have conformal weights (1,1); for this vertex that gives p2=0.
Now keep the fermionic signs in the real chiral Majorana fermion calculation. Contracting the external ψ(w) through :ψ(z)∂ψ(z): gives
:ψ(z)∂ψ(z):ψ(w)∼−ϵ∂ψ(z)​−ϵ2ψ(z)​.
(16)
Multiplying by −1/2 and Taylor expanding ψ(z) at w produces
T(z)ψ(w)∼2ϵ2ψ(w)​+ϵ∂ψ(w)​.
(17)
This proves that ψ is a primary operator with weight hψ​=1/2. To find the central charge, in T(z)T(w) the two double-contraction pairings contribute, before the overall factor 1/4,
−⟨ψ(z)ψ(w)⟩⟨∂ψ(z)∂ψ(w)⟩=ϵ42​,⟨ψ(z)∂ψ(w)⟩⟨∂ψ(z)ψ(w)⟩=−ϵ41​.
(18)
The single Wick contractions give 2T(w)/ϵ2+∂T(w)/ϵ, so
T(z)T(w)∼4ϵ41​+ϵ22T(w)​+ϵ∂T(w)​.
(19)
Comparing the leading term with c/(2ϵ4) proves cψ​=1/2, as in the free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory.
With four embedding bosons and the ordinary reparameterization bc ghost system, cancellation of the worldsheet Weyl anomaly in each chirality requires
4+2Nf​​−26=0,Nf​=44.​
(20)
Thus there are 44 real left-moving and 44 real right-moving fermions, equivalently 44 two-dimensional nonchiral Majorana fermions. These are internal free fermions in a four-dimensional bosonic string. Adding internal matter to the bosonic string does not introduce local worldsheet supersymmetry or its superghosts.
For the requested polynomial string vertex operators, work in the unprojected Neveu–Schwarz sector. A full model also chooses spin structures and a consistent projection, which the central-charge condition alone does not specify. If the holomorphic and antiholomorphic oscillator contributions to the conformal weights are ℓL​,ℓR​, physicality requires
ℓL​+4α′p2​=ℓR​+4α′p2​=1,M2=α′4​(ℓL​−1),ℓL​=ℓR​.
(21)
Therefore only equal levels at most one can contribute to the requested nonpositive bosonic string mass spectrum.
With zero fermion insertions, level zero gives the tachyon vertex operator Ep​, a scalar with M2=−4/α′. Level one gives the massless closed-string vertex operator ζμν​:∂Xμ∂ˉXνEp​:, with the transverse conditions above and the string-state gauge redundancy that removes longitudinal polarizations. The physical transverse polarization tensor decomposes into a symmetric trace-free graviton, an antisymmetric Kalb–Ramond field, and a scalar dilaton. In four spacetime dimensions these have respectively two, one, and one physical polarizations; the two-form can be dualized to a scalar.
With two fermion insertions there are two possibilities. One fermion in each chirality gives
Cij​:ψiψˉ​jEp​:,qquad(ℓL​,ℓR​)=(1/2,1/2),M2=−2/α′.​
(22)
These are 442=1936 scalar tachyons before any projection. There is no spacetime derivative or spacetime polarization to impose a vector transversality condition.
Two different fermions in the same chirality form an antisymmetric fermion bilinear, a weight-one current Jij=:ψiψj:; equal-species undifferentiated bilinears vanish. Closed-string level matching then requires a weight-one bosonic derivative in the opposite chirality. The resulting string vertex operators are
Aμij​:ψiψj∂ˉXμEp​:,qquadAμij​:∂Xμψˉ​iψˉ​jEp​:,qquadi<j.
(23)
They have M2=0 and are gauge vectors, with p⋅Aij=0 and Aμij​∼Aμij​+pμ​λij, and likewise for A. Each chirality supplies (244​)=946 gauge vectors. The fermion bilinears generate the two so(44) current algebras associated with the Special orthogonal Lie algebra, giving the unprojected gauge group SO(44)L​×SO(44)R​. The tempting vertex :ψiψjEp​: alone fails closed-string level matching because its weights are (1,0) before the common momentum contribution. Additional derivatives raise the levels above the nonpositive-mass range; a product of a left and a right bilinear has four fermion insertions and is outside this requested sector. This completes the zero- and two-fermion vertices of an internally fermionized bosonic string; a specified global projection can remove some of the listed states.
