Choose a Euclidean string worldsheet and the usual normalization of the Polyakov action. For a closed oriented worldsheet Σ, the string nonlinear sigma model is
S=​4πα′1​∫Σ​d2σg​gabGμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+4πα′i​∫Σ​d2σεabBμν​(X)∂a​Xμ∂b​Xν+4π1​∫Σ​d2σg​Φ(X)R(2).​​
(33)
Here ε12=1 is the alternating density, not the tensor with components 1/g​. The imaginary coefficient in the Kalb–Ramond field term is the Euclidean continuation; in Lorentzian signature this term is real. G is the spacetime metric and Φ the dilaton.
All three terms have worldsheet diffeomorphism invariance, and are covariant under spacetime coordinate transformations when X, G, B and Φ are transformed together. Under a Weyl transformation gab​↦e2ωgab​, g​gab is invariant in two dimensions, so the metric coupling has classical Weyl invariance. The antisymmetric coupling is independent of the worldsheet metric, so it too has classical Weyl invariance.
The curvature coupling requires care. An infinitesimal Weyl transformation gives δω​(g​R(2))=−2g​Δg​ω. Integration by parts on the closed worldsheet therefore gives the Weyl variation of a nonconstant dilaton coupling:
δω​SΦ​=−2π1​∫Σ​g​Φ(X)Δg​ω=−2π1​∫Σ​g​ωΔg​Φ(X).
(34)
A constant dilaton coupling is topological and Weyl invariant; a general varying dilaton coupling is not separately classically Weyl invariant. Its classical variation participates in canceling the quantum worldsheet Weyl anomaly, producing the improved background equations below.
To relate these background couplings to the flat-space bosonic string mass spectrum, expand Gμν​=ημν​+hμν​(X) and express h and B as superpositions of plane waves. The perturbation of the action is a sum of integrated string vertex operators of the form
∫d2zϵμν​:∂Xμ∂ˉXνeip⋅X:.
(35)
This is the vertex of α−1μ​α−1ν​∣p⟩ in the massless first closed-string level. Its conformal weights are (1+α′p2/4,1+α′p2/4), so marginality gives p2=0. The primary operator conditions give transversality, and the string-state gauge redundancy removes longitudinal components. The symmetric trace-free polarization tensor is the graviton and perturbs G; its antisymmetric part is the Kalb–Ramond field and perturbs B. The scalar physical polarization is the dilaton. At the level of background fields its vertex involves the metric trace and the curvature improvement, so an arbitrary off-shell metric trace should not simply be identified with the physical dilaton without this distinction.
The spacetime gauge invariance of the antisymmetric coupling is
B⟼B+dΛ,δBμν​=∂μ​Λν​−∂ν​Λμ​,H=dB.​

δSB​=2πα′i​∫Σ​d(X∗Λ)=2πα′i​∫∂Σ​X∗Λ=0
(37)
for a closed worldsheet, by Stokes theorem. In components this is the total derivative ∂a​(εabΛν​(X)∂b​Xν): the term containing ∂a​∂b​Xν vanishes by antisymmetry. Thus only the Kalb-Ramond field strength H can enter the local spacetime equations. With boundaries, a compensating boundary gauge-field transformation is needed.
Separate the dilaton into a constant Φ0​ and a varying part. The Gauss-Bonnet theorem gives
4π1​∫Σ​g​R(2)=χ(Σ)=2−2g
(38)
for a connected closed orientable genus-g worldsheet. Its Euclidean path-integral weight contains
e−SΦ0​​=e−(2−2g)Φ0​=gs2g−2​,gs​=eΦ0​.​
(39)
Consequently the sum over worldsheet topologies is the string genus expansion ∑g≥0​gs2g−2​Ag​, with each coefficient integrating over that genus's worldsheet moduli. Each extra handle costs gs2​, which is the loop-counting factor of string perturbation theory. With the usual normalization of n external closed-string vertices, the connected amplitude carries gs2g−2+n​. This dilaton Euler-characteristic weighting is distinct from the expansion in α′ of a fixed-worldsheet string nonlinear sigma model.
Finally quantize that two-dimensional theory. The background fields are its couplings. The background field expansion of a string sigma model, X=x+ξ in target-space normal coordinates, contains curvature vertices quadratic in the fluctuation ξ. Contracting their two fluctuation indices produces the Ricci tensor multiplying ∂xμ∂ˉxν. Logarithmic short-distance contractions renormalize G, B, and Φ, giving functional sigma-model beta functions. Gauge independence requires vanishing of the worldsheet Weyl anomaly, including the classical improvement from the varying dilaton, not merely an arbitrary choice of renormalization scale.

Thus quantum consistency of a two-dimensional string nonlinear sigma model determines the dynamics of its spacetime background fields.
