Write gt​=gKt​​ and, for s<t, let Ks,t​ be the filled image hull gs​(Kt​∖Ks​). The half-plane-capacity composition rule gives
gt​=gKs,t​​∘gs​,hcap(Ks,t​)=2(t−s).
(16)
The Loewner local growth property says that these mapped increments shrink uniformly on bounded time intervals: for every A<∞,
ωA​(h):=sup0≤s<t≤A, t−s≤h​rad(Ks,t​)⟶0as h↓0.
(17)
Using diameter instead of radius is equivalent, since a nonempty compact H-hull has its closure meeting the real axis. This is a condition on the increments after mapping out the past, not merely on the Euclidean size of Kt​∖Ks​ in the original domain.
For fixed t, the nonempty compact sets Kt,t+h​​, h>0, are nested as h decreases. Their diameters tend to zero by Loewner local growth property. The nested-compact-set property gives a single intersection point, and its imaginary part is zero because Kt,t+h​ lies in a real-centred disc of radius tending to zero. Define the Loewner transform by
{ξt​}=h>0⋂​Kt,t+h​​,ξt​∈R.​
(18)
One may restrict the intersection to sufficiently small h. Every point of Kt,t+h​ is within 2ωA​(h) of ξt​ when t+h≤A.
Here is a direct proof of continuity, using the permitted displacement estimate. Fix s<t and put δ=t−s. Take 0<h≤δ and a point v∈Kt,t+h​. Set z=gKs,t​−1​(v). The hull composition rule puts z∈Ks,t+h​, while the displacement estimate gives
∣v−z∣≤Crad(Ks,t​)≤CωA​(δ).
(19)
Since z is within 2ωA​(δ+h) of ξs​, let h↓0, taking v→ξt​, to obtain
∣ξt​−ξs​∣≤CωA​(δ)+2ωA​(2δ).​
(20)
Use a slightly larger fixed time interval if t is its endpoint. Both terms tend to zero with δ. This proves continuity from both sides, and indeed uniform continuity on every compact time interval. The real boundary value is obtained as a limit; evaluating the displacement estimate directly on a possibly rough boundary is unnecessary.
The Loewner correspondence theorem states that a capacity-2t increasing hull family with Loewner local growth property has the unique continuous real Loewner driving function just defined, and its mapping-out functions solve the Chordal Loewner equation
∂t​gt​(z)=gt​(z)−ξt​2​,g0​(z)=z.​
(21)
For each z∈H, this solution is taken up to its maximal lifetime; H∖Kt​ is precisely the set of points whose lifetimes exceed t. Conversely a continuous real driver gives such a hull family through this ordinary differential equation. A continuous driver alone need not have a continuous Loewner trace; that additional assertion is part of the trace theory of SLE, not of the deterministic correspondence theorem.
A continuous process γ:[0,∞)→H is chordal Schramm–Loewner evolution with parameter κ from 0 to infinity when γ0​=0, its filled past generates these capacity-2t hulls, and their Loewner driving function is
ξt​=κ​Bt​,​
(22)
with B standard real Brownian motion. The filling includes bounded components cut off from infinity. Equivalently its Loewner trace is the continuous extension of γt​=limy↓0​gt−1​(ξt​+iy). This last boundary limit is the trace-existence input for SLE.
Fix r>0 and scale space and time together:
Kt​=r−1Kr2t​,g​t​(z)=r−1gr2t​(rz),ξ​t​=r−1ξr2t​.
(23)
The half-plane capacity of Kt​ is 2t by part (a). Differentiation gives
∂t​g​t​(z)=g​t​(z)−ξ​t​2​.
(24)
By Brownian scaling, r−1Br2t​ is again standard Brownian motion, so the new driver has the same law as the old one. Uniqueness of the Chordal Loewner equation transfers this equality to the hulls and their continuous Loewner traces. Thus
(r−1γr2t​)t≥0​ =d (γt​)t≥0​.​
(25)
This is capacity-parametrized scale invariance of a Loewner chain. It includes κ=0, when the driver is zero and the trace is the deterministic vertical slit γt​=2it​.
