 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2010 iii Paper 27 1 a
OurBigBook.com Words: 276
A compact H-hull is a bounded relatively closed set K⊂H whose complement D=H∖K is a simply connected domain. Equivalently one can retain its compact closure in H; real boundary points do not affect the mapping-out function. The Riemann mapping theorem and Schwarz reflection principle near infinity give a unique conformal map gK​:D→H with hydrodynamic normalization at infinity. Its Laurent series is
gK​(z)=z+zaK​​+O(∣z∣−2),aK​∈R.
(1)
The half-plane capacity is hcap(K)=aK​. The missing constant term is part of the normalization, not a choice to be made again when comparing different hulls.
Here are the conformal map facts we use to justify positivity and comparison. The inverse fK​:H→D⊂H has expansion fK​(w)=w−aK​/w+O(w−2). The upper-half-plane Schwarz-Pick theorem, applied to w and iY and then letting Y→∞, implies ImfK​(w)≥Imw. Indeed the inequality between pseudohyperbolic distances gives
∣fK​(w)−fK​(iY)​∣24ImfK​(w)ImfK​(iY)​≥∣w+iY∣24YImw​,
(2)
and multiplying by Y gives the height comparison in the limit. At w=iY, the inverse expansion reads ImfK​(iY)=Y+aK​/Y+O(Y−2), so aK​≥0.
For r>0, uniqueness of the mapping-out function gives
grK​(z)=rgK​(z/r)=z+zr2aK​​+O(z−2).
(3)
Consequently
hcap(rK)=r2hcap(K).​
(4)
Translation by a real number b similarly gives gK+b​(z)=b+gK​(z−b) and leaves half-plane capacity unchanged.
If K⊂A are compact H-hulls, their mapping-out functions compose as gA​=gL​∘gK​, where L is the filled image of A∖K. Comparing Laurent series yields
hcap(A)=hcap(K)+hcap(L)≥hcap(K).
(5)
This proves monotonicity of half-plane capacity, rather than assuming it from inclusion alone. The filled half-disc A={z∈H:∣z−b∣≤R} has mapping-out function
gA​(z)=z+z−bR2​,
(6)
which takes its semicircular boundary to the real line and is a conformal bijection onto H. Thus its half-plane capacity is R2. Enclose K in such a half-disc, compare capacities, and minimize over real centres and radii. This proves the sharp bound
hcap(K)≤rad(K)2.​
(7)
If one defines the radius by an infimum, take R strictly larger than that infimum and then let R decrease; no attainment assumption is needed.
