Let t(u) be the inverse image-capacity clock. Part (a) and the quadratic variation of a stochastic integral give
ξ​u​=ξt(u)∗​,[ξ​]u​=6u.

The Dambis-Dubins-Schwarz theorem, or the Lévy characterization of Brownian motion, now shows that ξ​u​/6​ is standard Brownian motion up to the terminal image time. If that terminal clock is finite, it can be extended by a fresh Brownian continuation; this does not assert anything about the subsequent image trace. The initial value is zero because Φ(0)=0.
Differentiating the image Loewner chain with respect to its own half-plane capacity time gives
∂u​g​u​(z)=g​u​(z)−ξ​u​2​,g​u​=gt(u)∗​.
(47)
Thus, until its first hit of [1,∞), the reparametrized image Φ(γ) has the ordinary chordal SLE law from 0 to infinity. This follows from the uniqueness of the Chordal Loewner equation for a given continuous driver; the continuous Loewner trace is recovered from the inverse-map limit. Localization can be removed by exhausting times strictly before this boundary contact. The terminal event is defined from the trace itself, so equality of the stopped trace laws follows as well.
On the other hand, the definition of Conformal invariance of SLE says that the full unparameterized image Φ(γ) has the law of chordal SLE6​ from Φ(0)=0 to Φ(∞)=1, namely the law of γ′. Its stopping event at [1,∞) is exactly the image of the original stopping event at (−∞,−1]. Therefore the same stopped image has both laws: ordinary SLE6​ from 0 to infinity stopped at T, and SLE6​ from 0 to 1 stopped at T′. We conclude
(γt​:0≤t<T) =d (γt′​:0≤t<T′)as curves up to increasing reparametrization.​
(48)
The statement does not identify T with T′ in their original clocks. It is target-change locality of SLE6, derived from the zero drift and capacity time change rather than assumed as a theorem.
