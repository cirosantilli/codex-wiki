One convenient version of Watson's lemma is the following. Suppose G(t) is integrable away from zero and has, as t↓0, an asymptotic expansion G(t)∼∑j≥0​cj​tβ+j−1, where β>0. On a finite interval, or on an infinite interval with suitable exponential growth control,
∫0T​e−λtG(t)dt∼∑j≥0​λβ+jcj​Γ(β+j)​,λ→+∞.
(3)
To prove a finite truncation, split the integral at a fixed small δ>0. The part away from zero is exponentially small. On (0,δ) subtract the first N local terms and bound the remainder by Ctβ+N−1; its integral is O(λ−β−N). Extending each polynomial integral to infinity changes it only exponentially, and the substitution u=λt produces the Gamma integral. A little-o local remainder gives the corresponding little-o asymptotic remainder. This is a proof by localization and remainder bounds, rather than merely formal integration of a series.
For the monotone-phase integral, assume the derivatives needed below exist and are bounded near a, and the rest of the integral is controlled. Put t=ϕ(a)−ϕ(x). Since ϕ′(a)<0, this is a regular endpoint coordinate and
f(λ)=eλϕ(a)∫0ϕ(a)−ϕ(b)​e−λtG(t)dt,G(t)=−ϕ′(x(t))g(x(t))​.

An O(eλϕ(a)λ−3) remainder follows, for example, from a sufficiently smooth transformed amplitude with bounded second derivative near zero. The displayed second coefficient can vanish for special amplitudes without changing the expansion.
The unheaded transition request concerns a cubic endpoint-to-saddle transition. Near x=0 its exponent has the expansion
−x+αsinx=−(1−α)x−6αx3​+O(x5).

p=32​,q=31​,h(ν)=∫0∞​e−νt−t3/6dt,f(λ,1−νλ−2/3)∼λ−1/3h(ν).​

For fixed ν, the substitution x=λ−1/3t makes the omitted exponent terms O(λ−2/3) on bounded t intervals. Cubic decay controls the remaining tail, while the original interval away from zero is exponentially negligible. The estimate is uniform for ν in compact sets. In the printed range α≤1, ν≥0; the same transition integral also makes sense for negative fixed ν.
At ν=0, the Gamma integral gives h(0)=61/3Γ(1/3)/3, recovering the cubic endpoint regime. For ν→+∞, scale t=u/ν and expand e−u3/(6ν3) to obtain h(ν)=ν−1−ν−4+O(ν−7). Its leading term gives λ−1/3/ν=1/[λ(1−α)], recovering the ordinary endpoint regime in their overlap. Both matching limits are therefore checked explicitly.
