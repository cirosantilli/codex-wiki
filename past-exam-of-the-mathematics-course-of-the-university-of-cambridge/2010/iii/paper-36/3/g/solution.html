The alternative effective count is
pV​=21​Var{D(θ)∣y}.​
(37)
Under the locally flat-prior approximation, the constant D(θ) contributes no variance, and the remaining chi-squared distribution term has variance 2p. Hence pV​≈p, the same calibration as the usual effective parameter count in DIC. It can be estimated by half the sample variance of deviance draws, without evaluating deviance at a parameter average.
This is a calibrated alternative, not a general identity with pD​. For example, with a quadratic deviance and a multivariate normal distribution posterior of mean m and covariance Σ, putting b=m−θ gives
pV​=tr{(JΣ)2}+2bTJΣJb,pD​=tr(JΣ).
(38)
They agree when b=0 and Σ=J−1, but informative priors can change both the covariance and the displacement of the posterior mean. Their equality should therefore not be assumed outside the stated regime.
