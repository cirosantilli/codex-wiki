For an r-uniform set family F, its lower shadow is the set family of all (r−1)-sets contained in a member. Write a positive integer m in its unique binomial representation
m=(rar​​)+(r−1ar−1​​)+⋯+(jaj​​),ar​>ar−1​>⋯>aj​≥j≥1.
(1)
The Kruskal-Katona theorem states that
∣F∣=m⟹∣∂F∣≥(r−1ar​​)+(r−2ar−1​​)+⋯+(j−1aj​​).​
(2)
For m=0 the bound is zero. Equality is attained by the first m r-sets in colexicographic order. In that order A precedes B when the largest element of their symmetric difference belongs to B.
Here is a complete UV-compression proof of the Kruskal-Katona theorem. For disjoint equal-sized sets U,V, put C(A)=(A∖V)∪U when A contains every element of V and no element of U, and put C(A)=A otherwise. The UV-compression of a set family replaces an eligible A only if its image is absent. It preserves cardinality: a retained collision keeps both the original set and its already present image.
We first prove the required Shadow lemma for UV-compressions. Suppose that, for each x∈U, a y∈V can be chosen so the set family is fixed by CU∖{x},V∖{y}​. We claim
∂CU,V​(F)⊆CU,V​(∂F).
(3)
Consider a lower shadow member obtained by deleting z from a moved set A′=(A∖V)∪U. If z∈/U, it is the compressed image of A∖{z}, an old lower shadow member. If z=x∈U, the smaller compression of A produces A′∖{x}∪{y}, which is in F by the assumed invariance. Thus A′∖{x} already belongs to the old lower shadow; it is not eligible to move since it lacks x.
It remains to consider a lower shadow member B from a retained original member A=B∪{z}. If B is not eligible to move, it stays in the compressed lower shadow. If it is eligible, let B′=(B∖V)∪U. When z∈/U, retaining the eligible A means its image already belongs to F, and that image contains B′. When z=x∈U, the smaller compression of A belongs to F and equals B′∪{y}. In either case B′ is in the old lower shadow too. Consequently the collision rule retains B as well. This proves the claimed containment in every case. Since compression preserves cardinality, it cannot increase lower shadow size.
If F is not a colexicographic initial segment, an earlier absent set A and a later present set B give U=A∖B, V=B∖A with maxU<maxV, and a nontrivial UV-compression. Choose such a compression with ∣U∣ smallest. If ∣U∣>1, choose y∈V∖{maxV}. For every x∈U, the smaller compression still has its largest differing element on the V side and must fix F by minimality. If ∣U∣=1, the smaller compression is the identity. Thus the lower shadow argument applies.
Each nontrivial step strictly decreases the positive integer weight
w(F)=∑A∈F​∑i∈A​2i,
(4)
because the largest differing coordinate belongs to V. Iteration therefore terminates. A terminal set family is a colexicographic initial segment, since an earlier gap and a later member would otherwise provide another nontrivial compression. The final set family has m members and no larger lower shadow.
To compute its lower shadow, take ar​ maximal with (rar​​)≤m. All r-sets of [ar​] occur first. The next block consists of sets containing ar​+1 whose remaining (r−1)-set runs through the initial segment of length m−(rar​​). By Pascal's identity, the remainder is smaller than (r−1ar​​), so its next leading top index is strictly less than ar​. Repeating gives the stated unique binomial representation. The first block contributes all (r−1ar​​) (r−1)-sets in [ar​] to the lower shadow. Deleting ar​+1 from the next block contributes only sets already counted; deleting another coordinate contributes ar​+1 joined to the lower shadow of the smaller initial segment. This recursive decomposition gives exactly the displayed sum. The base r=1 has lower shadow {∅} for every nonempty set family. This completes the proof and the sharpness assertion.
Now let C be the set family of vertex sets of the graph's cliques of order four. Its twice-iterated lower shadow consists of edges of the graph. If there were at least sixteen such copies, choose a sixteen-member subfamily. The relevant binomial representation is
16=(46​)+(33​).
(5)
The proved theorem gives at least (36​)+(23​)=23 triples in its first lower shadow. Apply it again to any twenty-three of those triples, using 23=(36​)+(23​). Their edge lower shadow has at least
(26​)+(13​)=18
(6)
members, contradicting the fifteen available edges. Conversely, the complete graph K6​ has (26​)=15 edges and (46​)=15 copies of K4​. Therefore the maximum number of copies is exactly 15.

Take the natural parameter range 1≤t≤r≤n and admissible indices 0≤i≤r−t. Larger indices give empty set families. Write q=r−t+1 and set n0​=∞ when discussing the first interval. For the nontrivial interval calculation, t>1 and n>2r−t+1; all prefixes used below then lie inside the ground set.
To compare consecutive complete-intersection candidate families, put a=t+2i and b=t+i. A set in Ai+1​∖Ai​ has exactly b−1 points in [a] and both of the next two points. A set in Ai​∖Ai+1​ has exactly b points in [a] and neither next point. Therefore
Gi​=∣Ai+1​∖Ai​∣=(t+i−1t+2i​)(r−t−i−1n−t−2i−2​),
(7)
Li​=∣Ai​∖Ai+1​∣=(t+it+2i​)(r−t−in−t−2i−2​).
(8)
For 0≤i<r−t these quantities are positive in the present range, and cancellation of binomial coefficients gives
Li​Gi​​=(i+1)(n−r−i−1)(t+i)(r−t−i)​.
(9)
The numerator minus the denominator is
(i+1)(ni+1​−n),ni+1​=q(2+i+1t−1​).
(10)
Thus ∣Ai+1​∣ is larger, equal or smaller than ∣Ai​∣ according as n is smaller, equal or larger than ni+1​. Since these thresholds decrease with the index, nk+1​<n<nk​ implies strict increase up to Ak​ and strict decrease thereafter:
imax​∣Ai​∣=∣Ak​∣.​
(11)
At an interior boundary n=nk+1​, the two consecutive maxima tie. The interval assertion concerns admissible k; taking k>r−t would instead refer to an empty set family and is not a valid interpretation.
The Ahlswede-Khachatrian theorem, in its cardinality form, states
M(n,r,t)=0≤i≤r−tmax​∣Ai​∣.​
(12)
Here Ai​ is interpreted exactly by its intersection with [t+2i], even if that prefix extends beyond [n]. Its size can always be written with mi​=min{n,t+2i} as
∣Ai​∣=∑j=t+ir​(jmi​​)(r−jn−mi​​),
(13)
where impossible binomial coefficients are zero. When n≤2r−t, the whole level is t-intersecting and the formula gives M=(rn​). The nontrivial case has n>2r−t.
First, each candidate is a t-intersecting family: two members contain at least 2(t+i)−(t+2i)=t common points in the prefix. This proves the lower bound for M. We now prove the upper bound, using only the compression and generating-family facts expressly allowed in the question.
The first auxiliary fact is that the usual left coordinate shifts of a set family preserve cardinality and t-intersection. Iterating them produces a left-compressed set family of the same size. The shift Sab​ with a<b replaces b by a only when b is present, a is absent, and the replacement is not already in the set family. A set family is left-compressed when it is fixed by every such shift. Right compression is the reflected notion.
For the second auxiliary fact, a generating family for a uniform set family G means a collection of sets of size at most r with
F={A∈[n](r):G⊆A for some G∈G}.
(14)
The permitted small-support generating lemma for extremal intersecting families is this precise statement: if F is a maximum-cardinality left-compressed t-intersecting set family, n>2r−t, and
n>(r−t+1)(2+j+1t−1​)(j≥0),
(15)
then it has a generating set family supported on [t+2j]. Reflection gives the same assertion for a right-compressed extremal set family, with the supporting interval at the right end. These two auxiliary results are being used as permitted lemmas; the extremal conclusion of the theorem is established in the remaining argument.
Choose a maximum left-compressed set family F. Its complement set family
F∗={[n]∖A:A∈F}
(16)
is right-compressed, has uniformity r′=n−r and intersection parameter t′=n−2r+t, and is extremal for those parameters. Indeed
∣([n]∖A)∩([n]∖B)∣=n−2r+∣A∩B∣,
(17)
so complementation gives a cardinality-preserving bijection between the two classes of intersecting set families. Notice that r′−t′+1=q too.
We need a compatibility observation, which we prove. If G generates F and H generates F∗, then
∣G∪H∣≥n−r+t.​
(18)
Otherwise extend G∪H to a set T of size n−r+t−1. This size is at least both r and n−r because n≥2r−t+1 and t≥1. Choose an r-set A⊆T containing G, and an (n−r)-set B⊆T containing H. Their generating properties imply A,[n]∖B∈F, but
∣A∩([n]∖B)∣=∣A∪B∣−∣B∣≤(n−r+t−1)−(n−r)=t−1,
(19)
a contradiction. This compatibility bound for complementary generating families is the link between the two compression directions.
Suppose first that t>1 and n>2r−t+1 lies strictly between nk+1​ and nk​. Put m=t+2k and h=r−t−k. The small-support lemma gives generators G for F supported on [m]. It also gives generators H for F∗ supported on [m+1,n]. For completeness, the dual inequality required here is
n>q(2+h+1t′−1​).
(20)
Writing D=n−2q, it is equivalent to kD<q(t−1), exactly the upper interval inequality n<nk​; for k=0 it follows from t>1. The dual support length is t′+2h=n−m.
Either every G∈G has ∣G∣≥t+k, or every H∈H has ∣H∣≥n−r−k. If neither assertion held, a pair of smaller generators would have union size at most
(t+k−1)+(n−r−k−1)=n−r+t−2,
(21)
contradicting the compatibility bound. In the first alternative, every member of F has at least t+k points in [m], so F⊆Ak​. In the second alternative, each complement has at least n−r−k points in [m+1,n], leaving its original set with at most r−t−k points there; again F⊆Ak​. Therefore M≤∣Ak​∣, proving the theorem in every strict interval.
At an interior boundary n=nk+1​, with 0≤k≤r−t−1, apply the small-support lemma at index k+1 to the original set family, since n>nk+2​. Its generators are supported on [t+2k+2]. The dual generators are still supported on [t+2k+1,n], by the same dual calculation with h=r−t−k. Either all original generators have size at least t+k+1, or all dual generators have size at least n−r−k: failure of both would give union size at most n−r+t−1, again impossible. The first alternative places F inside Ak+1​, and the second places it inside Ak​. The comparison already proved gives ∣Ak​∣=∣Ak+1​∣, so the common size is M.
It remains to cover the endpoint cases, not to assume them implicitly. For t=1 and n>2r, the small-support lemma with j=0 puts an extremal left-compressed set family's generators inside {1}. Its members must all contain one, giving M≤(r−1n−1​), attained by A0​. For t=1, n=2r, at most one member of each complementary pair can occur, so M≤21​(r2r​)=(r−12r−1​), again attained by A0​. If n<2r, the whole level is intersecting.
For t>1 and n=2r−t+1, complementation changes the problem into a one-intersection problem with uniformity q=r−t+1. Since n=2q+t−1>2q, the preceding case gives
M(n,r,t)=(q−1n−1​)=(rn−1​),
(22)
attained by Ar−t​, the set family of r-sets avoiding the final point. For n≤2r−t, every two r-sets meet in at least 2r−n≥t, so M=(rn​) and Ar−t​ is the whole level. Finally, t=r gives M=1 directly. These cases, the strict intervals and their boundaries cover all 1≤t≤r≤n, completing the proof of the stated maximum formula.

An r-uniform hypergraph is strongly saturated with respect to a complete uniform hypergraph of order r+t if adding any missing edge creates a new complete uniform hypergraph on r+t vertices containing that edge. Equivalently, for every missing r-set e, there is an (r+t)-set T⊇e for which all other r-subsets are edges. Existing complete subhypergraphs are allowed in this strong-saturation definition.
We prove the bound by an exterior-power quotient bound for strong hypergraph saturation. Assume r,t≥1 and n≥r+t. Let V=Rn with basis e1​,…,en​. Choose distinct real numbers a1​,…,an​ and let U be the kernel of the t×n matrix whose column i is
(1,ai​,…,ait−1​)T.
(23)
Every t columns are linearly independent by the Vandermonde determinant, so dimU=n−t. Form the quotient vector space
W=⋀rV/⋀rU,dimW=(rn​)−(rn−t​).
(24)
For each r-set e={i1​<⋯<ir​}, let ve​ be the image in W of the exterior product ei1​​∧⋯∧eir​​. These images span W.
For an (r+t)-set T, let VT​ be its coordinate subspace. The matrix restricted to T has rank t, so U∩VT​ has dimension r. The wedge of a basis of this intersection is a nonzero vector
αT​=∑e∈T(r)​ce​ei1​​∧⋯∧eir​​in ⋀rU.
(25)
Every coefficient ce​ is nonzero. Indeed, projection from U∩VT​ onto the r coordinates in e is an isomorphism: a vector in its kernel would be supported on T∖e, where the t columns are independent, and hence would be zero. That projection's determinant is precisely the corresponding wedge coefficient. Thus, in the quotient vector space,
∑e∈T(r)​ce​ve​=0(ce​=0).
(26)
For a missing edge, choose its strong-saturation witness T. All other vectors in this relation come from present edges, so the missing edge's vector is in their span. Consequently the present-edge vectors already span all of W. Their number is at least its dimension:
∣E(H)∣≥(rn​)−(rn−t​).​
(27)
This is sharp. Take all r-sets that meet a fixed t-set D. A missing edge e is disjoint from D, and on e∪D every other r-set meets D; adding e completes the required clique. The number of edges is exactly the boxed difference. If r≤n<r+t, no missing edge could have a witness, so a strongly saturated hypergraph must be complete; the substantive construction above is for the usual range n≥r+t.
For the set-pair problem, write m=∣I∣ and choose xi​∈Ri​∩Si​. If i=j, the cross-disjointness implies that xi​ is in neither Rj​ nor Sj​. In particular, the witnesses are distinct. Let D={xi​:i∈I}; each Ri​ and Si​ contains exactly its own witness from D.
Choose two different indices i,j. The sets Ri​∖{xi​} and Sj​∖{xj​} are disjoint, lie outside D, and have sizes r−1 and s−1. Hence
n−m≥(r−1)+(s−1),∣I∣≤n−r−s+2.​
(28)
For equality, partition the ground set into disjoint sets P,Q,D with sizes r−1,s−1,n−r−s+2, respectively, and enumerate D as x1​,…,xm​. Set
Ri​=P∪{xi​},Si​=Q∪{xi​}.​
(29)
Then Ri​∩Sj​ is {xi​} for i=j and empty otherwise. This realizes equality whenever the bound permits m≥2. The argument is a diagonal-intersection set-pair bound, with no additional assumptions on intersections among the Ri​ or among the Si​.

A set family shatters a set Z when its traces on Z include all of P(Z). The Sauer-Shelah lemma says that a set family F⊆P([n]) which shatters no k-set satisfies
∣F∣≤i=0∑k−1​(in​).​
(30)
Equivalently, if ∣F∣ exceeds this sum, it shatters a k-set. Binomial coefficients with index greater than n are zero; if k>n the inequality reduces to the whole-power-set bound. The VC dimension formulation puts k=d+1.
We give the full inductive proof. Split F according to the last coordinate, deleting that coordinate from the present section, and write these two set families on [n−1] as F0​,F1​. Put
C=F0​∪F1​,D=F0​∩F1​.
(31)
The inclusion-exclusion principle gives ∣F∣=∣C∣+∣D∣. If C shattered a k-set, then F would shatter it too. If D shattered a (k−1)-set Z, both versions of each required trace, with and without the last coordinate, would occur in F, which would shatter Z∪{n}. Thus the two mathematical induction bounds apply:
∣F∣≤∑i=0k−1​(in−1​)+∑i=0k−2​(in−1​)=∑i=0k−1​(in​),
(32)
using Pascal's identity. For n=0 the set family has at most one member. For k=1, the intersection section must be empty, since any nonempty set family shatters the empty set. These supply the mathematical induction bases; the forbidden-empty-set case has an empty set family and an empty sum. Sharpness is attained by all subsets of size at most k−1, which cannot shatter a k-set.
Now fix k≥1 in the infinite-ground-set problem. Choose, using the hypothesis with 2k, a set Y of size 2k with at least 22k−1 distinct traces. If its trace set family shattered no k-set, the Sauer-Shelah lemma would give
∣A∣Y​∣≤∑i=0k−1​(i2k​)=222k−(k2k​)​<22k−1,
(33)
a contradiction. Therefore some Z⊆Y of size k is shattered by the trace set family, and hence by A itself. Thus every finite shattering size occurs. The use of 2k makes the contradiction strict, even though the hypothesis only gives a half-power-set lower bound.
An infinite realization does not follow. For a concrete unbounded finite shattering without an infinite universal trace construction, partition X=N into disjoint finite blocks
Bj​={2j(j−1)​+1,…,2j(j+1)​},∣Bj​∣=j,
(34)
and take
A=j≥1⋃​P(Bj​).​
(35)
Every member is finite. Given k, choose Y=Bk​; its trace set family is the full power set, so it has 2k≥2k−1 members and is shattered. However, an infinite Z cannot be contained in one finite block. Choose x,y∈Z from different blocks. No member of A contains both, so {x,y} is not a trace on Z. Consequently there need not be an infinite Z whose traces contain every finite subset of Z.
