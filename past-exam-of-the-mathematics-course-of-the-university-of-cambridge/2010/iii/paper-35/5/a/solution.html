It is useful to prove the general core of the miners game calculation when r miners carry one unit: v(S)=⌊∣S∣/r⌋, with r≥2. Individual rationality gives xi​≥0, and efficiency gives ∑i​xi​=q:=⌊n/r⌋.
If n<r, the grand coalition value is zero and the only core allocation is zero. If n≥r, every r-person coalition must receive at least one. Sum these inequalities over all (rn​) such coalitions. Each player appears (r−1n−1​) times, so
(r−1n−1​)q≥(rn​),nrq​≥1.
(49)
If r does not divide n, then rq<n, a contradiction. Thus the core of a cooperative game is empty in these cases.
If n=r, every proper coalition has value zero. The core is exactly the simplex of nonnegative allocations with total one. If n is a multiple of r with n≥2r, the averaged inequality is equality. Since each r-person coalition sum is at least one, every such sum equals one. For any two players i,j, choose r−1 other players and compare the two resulting r-person coalition sums. They imply xi​=xj​. Hence xi​=1/r for all players. This vector belongs to the core because ∣S∣/r≥⌊∣S∣/r⌋ for every coalition.
For two miners per lump, therefore,
C2​(n)=⎩⎨⎧​{(0)},{(x1​,x2​):xi​≥0, x1​+x2​=1},{(1/2,…,1/2)},∅,​n=1,n=2,n≥4 even,n≥3 odd.​​
(50)
In particular the even case has a small-group exception at n=2.
For three miners per lump, the same proof gives
C3​(n)=⎩⎨⎧​{(0,…,0)},{x∈R+3​:x1​+x2​+x3​=1},{(1/3,…,1/3)},∅,​n=1,2,n=3,n≥6, 3∣n,n≥4, 3∤n.​​
(51)
Thus divisibility controls the existence of a stable allocation once enough miners are present, while exactly one possible lump leaves freedom to divide its value.
