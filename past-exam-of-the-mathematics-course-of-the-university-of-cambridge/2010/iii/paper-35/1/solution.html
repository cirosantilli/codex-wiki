Use the optimization Lagrangian with the sensitivity sign convention
Lb​(x,λ)=f(x)−λT(h(x)−b),λ∈Rm.​
(1)
For equality constraints the Lagrange multipliers have unrestricted signs. The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem says that if h(x∗)=b and, for some λ∗, x∗ globally minimizes Lb​(⋅,λ∗) over X, then x∗ globally minimizes f subject to the constraint. Indeed every feasible x satisfies
f(x)=Lb​(x,λ∗)≥Lb​(x∗,λ∗)=f(x∗).
(2)
This proves the theorem without a convexity or differentiability assumption. Merely finding a stationary point of the optimization Lagrangian would not establish its required global minimum.
Let ϕ(u)=inf{f(x):x∈X,h(x)=u}, with value +∞ when the feasible set is empty, and suppose ϕ(b) is finite. The problem has the Strong Lagrangian property if a finite multiplier λ satisfies
x∈Xinf​Lb​(x,λ)=ϕ(b).​
(3)
This includes attainment of the dual bound by a multiplier. It does not by itself assert attainment of the primal infimum. For every multiplier, restricting the infimum to feasible x gives infX​Lb​(x,λ)≤ϕ(b), the relevant weak duality. A non-vertical supporting hyperplane of a value function is a plane
r=ϕ(b)+λT(u−b)
(4)
with ϕ(u)≥ϕ(b)+λT(u−b) for every u. Its vertical coefficient is nonzero and has been normalized to one; the epigraph lies above it. The two directions of the equivalence are proved in parts (a) and (b).
For the sampling allocation put A=∑i​ai​vi​​. Finite variance requires every xi​>0; interpret the objective at a zero coordinate as +∞. An optimum uses all the budget, because scaling every positive xi​ up would otherwise reduce the objective while remaining feasible. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the global bound
A2=(∑i​vi​/xi​​ai​xi​​)2≤(∑i​xi​vi​​)(∑i​ai​xi​)≤b∑i​xi​vi​​.
(5)
Equality requires vi​/xi​ to be proportional to ai​xi​. The budget then determines the unique allocation:
xi∗​(b)=Ab​ai​vi​​​,ϕ(b)=bA2​.​
(6)
This is the optimal cost-constrained stratified sampling allocation.
For an inequality ∑i​ai​xi​≤b, our sign convention uses λ≤0, so Lb​=f−λ(∑i​ai​xi​−b) is a lower bound on f at feasible points. Its coordinate stationary equations are −vi​/xi2​−λai​=0, and the displayed allocation gives
λ∗(b)=−b2A2​.​
(7)
The function vi​/xi​+(−λ∗)ai​xi​ has a strict global minimum at xi∗​: its second derivative is 2vi​/xi3​>0 and its value diverges at both endpoints of (0,∞). Thus the multiplier also supplies the Lagrangian sufficiency theorem certificate, with the tight inequality giving complementary slackness.
Finally, for b+δb>0,
ϕ(b+δb)−ϕ(b)=−b(b+δb)A2δb​=λ∗(b)δb+O((δb)2).
(8)
Hence the first-order change in minimal variance is λ∗δb​. Increased resources decrease the variance. With the alternative convention L=f+μ(∑i​ai​xi​−b), the multiplier is μ=−λ∗>0 and the sensitivity is −μδb.
