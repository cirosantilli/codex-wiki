a OurBigBook.com
Use geometric units G=c=1, metric signature (−+++), and a four-velocity normalized by the physical metric. Write ϕ=1+Φ, where ∣Φ∣≪1. For a pressureless perfect fluid, the dust stress-energy tensor is Tij​=ρUi​Uj​ and gijUi​Uj​=−1. Since ηij=ϕ2gij, its background trace is
ηijTij​=−ρϕ2.
(1)
Consequently the field equation is (−∂t2​+Δ)ϕ=4πρϕ3. Neglecting time derivatives and higher weak-field orders gives the Newtonian limit of Nordstrom gravity:
ΔΦ=4πρ,Φ=ϕ−1.​
(2)
The constant shift between ϕ and Φ does not affect the Poisson equation or the force. Here ρ is the physical rest density; if fluid indices are instead defined using the background metric, the intermediate powers of ϕ change but the same leading-order equation results.
For the motion, the Levi-Civita connection of the conformally flat metric is
Γijk​=δji​∂k​logϕ+δki​∂j​logϕ−ηjk​ηiℓ∂ℓ​logϕ.
(3)
In particular Γα00​=∂α​logϕ. Replacing the affine parameter in the geodesic equation by coordinate time gives, with wi=(1,v) and v=dx/dt,
dt2d2xα​=−Γαij​wiwj+Γ0ij​wiwjvα=−(1−∣v∣2)(∂α​logϕ+vα∂t​logϕ).
(4)
At small speeds and for a nearly static field, this becomes
dt2d2x​=−∇Φ.​
(5)
Thus the Newtonian gravitational potential and freely falling trajectories agree to the stated weak-field, slow-motion order. The comparison is an approximation, not an exact equivalence of relativistic trajectories.

Yes, at the weak-field accuracy of the experiment. The Pound-Rebka experiment measures gravitational redshift, which Nordstrom theory of gravitation predicts even though it does not predict bending of light.
In a static field, a stationary clock has dτ=ϕdt. A photon has constant Killing energy E=−pt​, and an observer at rest has U=ϕ−1∂t​. Its measured frequency is proportional to −pi​Ui=E/ϕ. Emission at e and reception at r therefore give
νe​νr​​=ϕr​ϕe​​=1+Φe​−Φr​+O(Φ2).​
(6)
An upward ray has Φr​−Φe​≃gH/c2 when ordinary units are restored, so Δν/ν≃−gH/c2. This is the shift tested using the Mössbauer effect. The gravitational redshift follows from the physical clock rates; a claim that straight light paths imply no frequency shift would confuse two different observables.

c OurBigBook.com
No: the theory predicts zero gravitational deflection of light. The reason is conformal preservation of null geodesic paths. Along a null geodesic with tangent ki, the geodesic equation for a conformally flat metric reduces to
dλdki​+2dλdlogϕ​ki=0,
(7)
because ηij​kikj=0. The acceleration is parallel to the tangent and changes only its parametrization. Equivalently, with ℓi=ϕ2ki, dℓi/dλ=0, so the unparametrized ray is a straight null geodesic of Minkowski spacetime. A positive conformal factor also preserves local angles, so this straight-path result cannot be hidden by changing the local angle measurement. It contradicts the nonzero solar deflection observed in gravitational lensing, while remaining compatible with the gravitational redshift in part (b).

A linearized coordinate gauge transformation changes the identification of points of the perturbed spacetime with its Minkowski spacetime background. With x′i=xi+ϵξi, the same physical metric has perturbation
hij′​=hij​−∂i​ξj​−∂j​ξi​.
(8)
These changes are coordinate freedom, not extra physical gravitational wave polarizations. Raising indices with η, define h=ηijhij​ and the trace-reversed metric perturbation hˉij​=hij​−ηij​h/2. Then
hˉij′​=hˉij​−∂i​ξj​−∂j​ξi​+ηij​∂k​ξk.
(9)
The first-order Levi-Civita connection is Γ(1)kij​=21​ηkℓ(∂i​hjℓ​+∂j​hiℓ​−∂ℓ​hij​). Contracting the Riemann curvature tensor in the stated convention gives
Rij(1)​=21​(∂i​∂k​hkj​+∂j​∂k​hki​−□hij​−∂i​∂j​h),R(1)=∂i​∂j​hij−□h.
(10)
Put Aj​=∂ihˉij​. The corresponding Einstein tensor is
Gij(1)​=−21​□hˉij​+21​(∂i​Aj​+∂j​Ai​−ηij​∂kAk​).
(11)
Under the linearized coordinate gauge transformation, Aj′​=Aj​−□ξj​. Choosing a solution of □ξj​=Aj​ imposes Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, Aj′​=0. Thus the vacuum Einstein field equations reduce to
∂ihˉij​=0,□hˉij​=0.​
(12)
There remains residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity with □ξj​=0.
For the transverse profiles, write both symmetric off-diagonal entries as hxy​=hyx​=h×​(t−z) and the diagonal entries as hxx​=−hyy​=h+​(t−z). The trace-reversed metric perturbation has zero trace and therefore equals h. Its divergence vanishes: its only nonzero index directions are x,y, while its coefficients depend only on t−z. Also (−∂t2​+∂z2​)F(t−z)=0 for every twice differentiable profile F. These are consequently plane gravitational waves in linearized gravity in transverse-traceless gauge, with the two independent gravitational wave polarizations h+​ and h×​.
To calculate the detector response, use a parallel-propagated orthonormal frame along the centre of a short nonrotating stick. To first order its clock reads t, and the tidal curvature is
RA0B0​=−2ϵ​h¨AB​+O(ϵ2),A,B∈{x,y}.
(13)
For a sliding-bead gravitational wave detector, the stick constrains transverse motion but supplies no axial restoring force. Projecting geodesic deviation along an x-directed stick gives
L¨=2ϵ​h¨+​(t−z0​)L0​+O(ϵ2).
(14)
Here L is the proper separation and L0​=L(t0​), with L˙(t0​)=0. Integrating with those actual initial conditions yields
L(t)=L0​[1+2ϵ​(h+​(t−z0​)−h+​(t0​−z0​)−(t−t0​)h˙+​(t0​−z0​))]+O(ϵ2).​
(15)
For beads at rest before an incident wave, both initial profile terms vanish and the familiar result is L(t)=L0​[1+ϵh+​(t−z0​)/2]+O(ϵ2)​. The cross polarization produces a transverse tidal force, balanced by the guide, and no axial first-order displacement in this orientation. In transverse-traceless gauge the same plus response can be read from the proper line element, dℓ=1+ϵh+​​dx; coordinate distances alone are not measured distances. When a cross polarization is present, keeping the guide physically nonrotating may require transverse coordinate adjustments, which do not change the axial answer at this order.
For a z-directed stick, Rz0z0​=0 and the wave is transverse. Therefore beads initially at rest retain their proper separation to first order: L(t)=L0​+O(ϵ2). The tidal calculation is the usual linear detector limit, with bead spacing small compared with the wavelength; it does not treat a macroscopic rigid body as exactly rigid over arbitrary relativistic distances.

Assume M>0 and use geometric units. The stationary Killing vector field K=∂t​ gives the conserved Killing energy E=−g(K,U)=ft˙. For radial motion, normalization of the four-velocity gives −ft˙2+f−1r˙2=−1. Combining these identities yields
ft˙=E,r˙2+f=E2.​
(16)
At rest at infinity, f→1 and r˙→0, so E=1. Choose the inward branch r˙=−2M/r​. Its proper time from the Schwarzschild event horizon to the curvature singularity is
Δτ=∫02M​2Mr​​dr=34M​.​
(17)
In ordinary units this is 4GM/(3c3). The endpoint r=0 is not a regular centre of the spacetime. This is the Schwarzschild horizon-to-singularity proper time for fall from infinity, rather than a universal time for all infall energies.
For this geodesic congruence, set w(r)=2M/r​. Its four-velocity and metric dual are
U=f−1∂t​−w∂r​,Ua​dxa=−dt−f−1wdr.
(18)
The right side is an exact one-form because w/f depends only on r. Thus Ua​=−∂a​τ, with free-fall proper time in Schwarzschild spacetime determined by
dτ=dt+fw​dr,τ=t+22Mr​+2Mlog​r​+2M​r​−2M​​​+constant.​
(19)
Indeed Ua∂a​τ=−UaUa​=1, so this scalar advances by the actual proper time on every member of the infalling geodesic congruence, with a synchronized choice of additive offsets.
Substitution of dt=dτ−wf−1dr in the Schwarzschild metric gives
−fdt2+f−1dr2=−fdτ2+2wdτdr+dr2.
(20)
Using f=1−w2, the Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates therefore have
ds2=−dτ2+(dr+wdτ)2+r2dΣ2.​
(21)
All coefficients are finite at r=2M; the determinant of the τ,r block is −f−w2=−1. Hence the horizon is a coordinate singularity of Schwarzschild time, not a degeneracy of the metric. The new spatial slices have dℓ2=dr2+r2dΣ2, the flat Euclidean metric, and gab∂a​τ∂b​τ=−1 makes their normals the freely falling four-velocities. By contrast, the curvature scalar Rabcd​Rabcd=48M2/r6 diverges at r=0; this genuine singularity is not removed.
For Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, use v=t+r∗​ with dr∗​/dr=f−1, for example r∗​=r+2Mlog∣r/(2M)−1∣. Then
ds2=−fdv2+2dvdr+r2dΣ2.
(22)
Both constructions cross the future horizon. Their time functions have different meanings: v=constant is an ingoing null hypersurface, whereas τ=constant is a flat spacelike hypersurface orthogonal to the infallers. Thus the former is adapted to ingoing null geodesics, and the latter to a timelike free-fall clock congruence.
For the Kruskal diagram, choose Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates with UK​=−e−(t−r∗​)/(4M), VK​=e(t+r∗​)/(4M) in the right exterior and extend them across its horizon. They obey
UK​VK​=(1−2Mr​)er/(2M),ds2=−r32M3​e−r/(2M)dUK​dVK​+r2dΣ2.
(23)
The four regions of Kruskal spacetime are I, the right exterior (UK​<0,VK​>0); II, the future black hole (UK​>0,VK​>0); III, the other exterior (UK​>0,VK​<0); and IV, the past white hole (UK​<0,VK​<0). The null axes are horizons, their intersection is the bifurcation two-sphere, and the spacelike boundaries UK​VK​=1 are the future and past curvature singularities. A point in this radial diagram represents a symmetry two-sphere.
The domain of the ingoing Painleve-Gullstrand chart is region I together with region II and their common future horizon. More precisely, it is VK​>0, UK​VK​<1, or r>0 with finite τ. To verify the boundary rather than infer it from the locally regular metric, put ρ=r/(2M)​ and write
τ=v+S(r),S(r)=4Mρ−2Mρ2−4Mlog(1+ρ).
(24)
This follows by subtracting r∗​ from the preceding radial primitive, and S is finite at r=2M. Thus VK​=exp[(τ−S(r))/(4M)]>0 throughout the chart. Conversely every point with VK​>0 and r>0 gives a finite τ. The past horizon VK​=0, the bifurcation sphere, region III and region IV are absent. Reversing the sign of the radial term produces the outgoing chart through the white hole instead.

The radial part of the Minkowski metric becomes −dudv, and r=(v−u)/2. With u=tanp, v=tanq and the positive conformal factor Ω=2cospcosq, multiplication by Ω2 gives
Ω2(−dudv)=−4dpdq=−dT2+dR2,Ω2r2=(cospcosq)2(tanq−tanp)2=sin2(q−p).
(25)
Consequently the Minkowski conformal compactification is
ds2=Ω−2(−dT2+dR2+sin2RdΣ2),Ω=cosT+cosR.​
(26)
The spatial metric dR2+sin2RdΣ2 is the round unit three-sphere. The extended unphysical metric is therefore that of the Einstein static universe, R×S3 with unit spatial radius. Physical Minkowski spacetime occupies only the diamond-shaped causal patch of this cylinder, or a triangle after restricting to nonnegative spherical radius; it is not the whole cylinder.
The complete coordinate ranges and joint restrictions are
t∈R,r∈[0,∞),θ∈[0,π],ϕ∈[0,2π),u,v∈R,u≤v,−2π​<p≤q<2π​,−π<T<π,0≤R<π,∣T∣+R<π.​
(27)
The polar angles have the usual coordinate degeneracies at their poles and at r=0. In the extended Einstein static universe one may let T∈R and 0≤R≤π, but these are not the ranges of the original physical patch. In its interior Ω>0. The line R=0 is the regular centre r=0, not a component of infinity.
The conformal completion has future timelike infinity i+=(π,0), past timelike infinity i−=(−π,0), and spacelike infinity i0=(0,π). The open sloping boundaries are
I+:T+R=π,0<R<π,I−:T−R=−π,0<R<π,
(28)
namely future null infinity and past null infinity. Each ordinary interior point of the radial diagram represents a two-sphere, and each point on either open null boundary represents a two-sphere of null directions.
A timelike geodesic is an inertial worldline x=b+βt with ∣β∣<1. As t→+∞, both t−r and t+r tend to +∞, giving p,q→π/2 and endpoint i+. At past infinity both tend to −∞, giving i−. These endpoints are reached at infinite physical proper time, although at finite conformal coordinate time T. A null geodesic has r∼∣t∣. In its outgoing future, u approaches a finite retarded time while v→+∞, giving an endpoint on I+; in its incoming past, v has a finite limit and u→−∞, giving an endpoint on I−. A spacelike geodesic is a straight line with spatial tangent larger than its temporal tangent, so u→−∞, v→+∞ at either end and both endpoints are i0 in the compactified radial description. All these physical geodesics are complete. Only the unparametrized null geodesics are necessarily preserved as geodesics of the unphysical metric; timelike and spacelike images need not be its geodesics.
For horizons, distinguish a property of the spacetime from a property of an observer or of an initial-data hypersurface. Complete inertial observers have no particle horizon: in the flat cosmological slicing a(t)=1, the past light-travel distance ∫−∞t0​​dt is infinite. There is no finite-age initial singularity truncating communication between comoving observers. This statement does not say that an event receives signals from spacelike-separated events at the same time.
There is also no black-hole event horizon, because every event can send a future-directed null ray to future null infinity. Nevertheless accelerated timelike observers can have both future and past observer event horizons. For an entire observer worldline γ, define them by Hγ+​=∂I−(γ) and Hγ−​=∂I+(γ), using its complete future and past respectively. An inertial observer's chronological past and future of the whole worldline are both all of Minkowski spacetime. By contrast, for the uniformly accelerated worldline in a Cartesian spatial direction,
t=a−1sinh(aτ),x=a−1cosh(aτ),y=z=0,
(29)
its null coordinates obey t−x=−a−1e−aτ<0 and t+x=a−1eaτ>0. An event with t−x<0 can signal to a sufficiently late point of this worldline, whereas an event with t−x≥0 cannot; finite transverse displacements do not change that conclusion as t+x→∞. Similarly the worldline can signal to precisely the events with t+x>0. Thus its two observer event horizons are
Hγ+​: t−x=0,Hγ−​: t+x=0.​
(30)
They are the Rindler horizons, and their accessible common region is x>∣t∣. They do not indicate a curvature singularity or a failure of global predictability.
Finally Minkowski spacetime is a globally hyperbolic spacetime: every inextendible causal curve crosses each complete spacelike plane t=t0​ exactly once. Indeed t is strictly monotone along future-directed causal curves, and the speed bound ∣dx/dt∣≤1 prevents an inextendible curve from ending at finite t by escaping to spatial infinity. Its time range is therefore all of R. These planes are Cauchy hypersurfaces with full domain of dependence, so there are no Cauchy horizons associated with complete Cauchy data.
A Cauchy horizon is, however, defined relative to a specified initial surface. Under a literal existence reading of “admits”, non-Cauchy initial surfaces can have such horizons even in flat spacetime. For example the edgeless spacelike hyperboloid Σa​: t=a2+r2​ has D(Σa​)={t>r}. To see this, inside the future light cone t−r and t+r are positive and nondecreasing on future-directed causal curves. Their product t2−r2 increases past a2 on every future-inextendible curve from below the hyperboloid; every past-inextendible curve from above it crosses down through a2 before leaving the cone. Outside the cone there is an inextendible null curve avoiding the hyperboloid. The future light cone itself is its past Cauchy horizon. The time-reversed hyperboloid gives a future Cauchy horizon. This is the dependence of a Cauchy horizon on the initial hypersurface, not an intrinsic loss of predictability of full Minkowski spacetime. Thus a categorical claim that no choice of partial initial surface can ever have a Cauchy horizon would be too strong.
