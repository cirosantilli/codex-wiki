Let L/K be a finite Galois extension of non-Archimedean local fields, with Galois group G, normalized valuation vL​, integer ring OL​, and residue characteristic p. The lower ramification numbering is
G−1​=G,Gi​={σ∈G:vL​(σa−a)≥i+1 for every a∈OL​},i≥0.
(37)
These higher ramification groups measure successively finer agreement with the identity. The group G0​ is the inertia group, the kernel of the action on the residue field. Each Gi​ is normal in G: conjugation permutes integral elements and preserves their valuations. The groups eventually become trivial, since each nonidentity automorphism moves some integral element and its displacement has finite valuation.
Let K0​=LG0​, the maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension. Then G/G0​ is the Galois group of the residue extension, cyclic and generated by a Frobenius automorphism. For a uniformizer π of L, the uniformizer criterion for lower ramification groups gives
Gi​={σ∈G0​:vL​(σπ−π)≥i+1}.
(38)
To see why one uniformizer suffices, the extension L/K0​ is totally ramified and its integer ring is OK0​​[π]. Elements of G0​ fix its coefficients. Differences of powers of π are divisible by σπ−π, so the valuation bound for π implies it for every integral element. The converse follows by testing π itself.
The tame inertia quotient is seen explicitly from
G0​⟶kL×​,σ⟼σπ/π​.
(39)
It is a homomorphism because inertia acts trivially on the residue field, and its kernel is G1​. Thus G0​/G1​ is cyclic of order not divisible by p. For i≥1, the homomorphism
Gi​⟶(kL​,+),σ⟼πi+1σπ−π​​
(40)
has kernel Gi+1​. Indeed composing two automorphisms adds the leading displacements, since σπ/π≡1 and inertia fixes residue coefficients. Hence each Gi​/Gi+1​ is an elementary abelian p-group. Eventual triviality makes G1​ a p-group; the prime-to-p quotient makes it the unique Sylow subgroup of G0​. This is the wild inertia group. Tame ramification means G1​=1.
The filtration also measures the different exponent. For the totally ramified extension over K0​, the minimal polynomial of an algebraic element g of π has polynomial roots σπ, σ∈G0​. The derivative formula gives
vL​(g′(π))=∑σ∈G0​∖{1}​vL​(π−σπ).
(41)
For a fixed σ, its displacement valuation is the number of indices i≥0 for which it belongs to Gi​. Interchanging the finite sums therefore gives different exponent from ramification groups:
δ(L/K)=i≥0∑​(∣Gi​∣−1).​
(42)
The unramified part contributes zero. More generally different in a tower follows by taking products of bases of a module dual for the trace pairing, and gives
δ(L/K)=δ(L/E)+e(L/E)δ(E/K).
(43)
Thus a tame extension has different exponent e−1, while nontrivial wild groups contribute additional positive terms.
The lower numbering restricts to subgroups: if H=Gal(L/E), its groups are Hi​=H∩Gi​, directly from the definition using the same top-field valuation. Quotients need a different indexing. For real s≥0 put Gs​=G⌈s⌉​ and define the Herbrand function
φ(s)=∫0s​[G0​:Gt​]dt​,ψ=φ−1,Gu=Gψ(u)​.
(44)
This upper ramification numbering stretches or compresses intervals according to the inertia indices. The quotient theorem says that for a normal subgroup H, upper ramification groups commute with quotients:
(G/H)u=GuH/H.
(45)
In particular the same upper index has a meaning compatible with passing to a smaller Galois extension, which lower indices generally lack.
For a tame totally ramified extension the picture is simple: G0​=G, all Gi​ with i≥1 are trivial, and the different exponent is ∣G∣−1. The extension Qp​(ζp​) for odd p illustrates this, giving exponent p−2.
A mixed-characteristic wild family is the lower ramification filtration of a prime-power cyclotomic extension. Take L=Qp​(ζpr​) with odd p and π=ζpr​−1. If σa​(ζ)=ζa and a=1 modulo pr, then
vL​(σa​π−π)=vL​(ζa−1−1)=pvp​(a−1).
(46)
The last equality follows because ζa−1 has order pr−vp​(a−1) and its uniformizer valuation scales by the ramification index in that cyclotomic tower. The lower breaks are 0,p−1,p2−1,…,pr−1−1. Integrating the successive indices (p−1),p(p−1),… gives upper breaks 0,1,…,r−1.
In equal characteristic there is an equally explicit wild example. Start with K=Fq​((t)) and an Artin–Schreier extension
yp−y=t−m,m>0,p∤m.
(47)
There is no polynomial root in K: a negative-valuation element has v(xp−x)=pv(x), while the right side has valuation −m. Adjoining a polynomial root splits the polynomial, whose polynomial roots are y+c for c∈Fp​, so the extension is cyclic of degree p. The valuation equation forces total ramification, vL​(t)=p and vL​(y)=−m. Choose 1≤b≤p−1 with −bm≡1(modp) and set a=(1+bm)/p. Then π=tayb is a uniformizer. For a nonidentity automorphism y↦y+c,
πσπ​=(1+c/y)b,vL​(σπ−π)=1+m;
(48)
the first binomial coefficient b is nonzero modulo p, and all subsequent terms have strictly higher valuation. Therefore
G0​=⋯=Gm​=Cp​,Gm+1​=1,δ=(m+1)(p−1).
(49)
This is the ramification break of an Artin–Schreier pole. It shows concretely that wild breaks, unlike tame inertia, can occur arbitrarily far out in the filtration.
The lower groups measure integral displacements, the upper groups make quotient comparison possible, and the sizes of the groups determine the different.
