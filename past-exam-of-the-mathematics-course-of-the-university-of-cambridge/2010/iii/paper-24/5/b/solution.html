The polynomial is Eisenstein at every prime number p, so a polynomial root α generates a totally ramified cubic field F/Qp​. The splitting-field Galois group is a transitive subgroup of S3​, hence C3​ or S3​. Its polynomial discriminant is
Δ=−p2(4p+27).
(50)
The product of the three polynomial root differences is a square root of Δ, transformed by the sign of the polynomial root permutation. Thus the group is C3​ exactly when Δ is a square, and otherwise it is S3​. In the latter case the splitting field is
L=FQp​(Δ​).
(51)
Indeed adjoining one polynomial root leaves a quadratic factor, whose polynomial discriminant is Δ/f′(α)2, so the square root of Δ supplies its remaining polynomial roots.
Suppose first that p>3. The unit −(4p+27) reduces to −27, and Hensel's lemma makes it a square exactly when −3 is a square modulo p. Quadratic reciprocity gives
(p−3​)={1,−1,​p≡1(mod3),p≡2(mod3).​
(52)
If p≡1(mod3), the field generated by α is already the cyclic splitting field, totally ramified of degree three. If p≡2(mod3), the nonsquare unit gives an unramified quadratic field E=Qp​(Δ​). Since F and E have coprime degrees, [FE:Qp​]=6. Its residue degree is at least two and its ramification index at least three; their product is six, so they are exactly two and three. Thus its inertia group is the normal C3​ inside S3​.
In both cases the ramification index is three and is not divisible by p. The wild inertia group is trivial, so all positive lower groups are trivial. As a different check, f′(α)=3α2+p has valuation two in F when p>3, agreeing with tame different exponent 3−1.
For p=3 the polynomial discriminant is −351=−33⋅13, which is nonsquare. Hence G=S3​. The quadratic subfield can be written E=Q3​(β) with β2=−39. It is ramified of degree two, by the Eisenstein polynomial T2+39. Together with the totally ramified cubic field this forces e(L/Q3​) to be divisible by both two and three. Since [L:Q3​]=6, the splitting field is totally ramified. In its normalized valuation,
vL​(3)=6,vL​(α)=2,vL​(β)=3,π=β/α has vL​(π)=1.
(53)
All differences between distinct polynomial roots have the same valuation, since S3​ permutes the polynomial root pairs and preserves the absolute value on a field. Also
f′(α)=3(α2+1)=(α−α2​)(α−α3​)
(54)
has valuation six, so each polynomial root difference has valuation three. A nonidentity three-cycle fixes β, and therefore
vL​(σπ−π)=vL​(ασαβ(α−σα)​)=3+3−2−2=2.
(55)
The transposition fixing α negates β, so sends π to −π and has displacement valuation vL​(2π)=1. The other transpositions have the same value by conjugacy. The uniformizer criterion now determines every group:
pp>3, p≡1(mod3)p>3, p≡2(mod3)p=3​GC3​S3​S3​​e336​f121​(G0​,G1​,G2​,…)(C3​,1,1,…)(C3​,1,1,…)(S3​,C3​,1,1,…)​​
(56)
For the wild case the different exponent is 5+2=7. Independently, the cubic subfield has different exponent vF​(3(α2+1))=3, and the quadratic extension L/F is tame with exponent one. The tower formula gives 1+2⋅3=7, confirming the filtration. Its positive upper break is 1/2, because [G0​:G1​]=2. This completes ramification of the splitting field of X3 plus pX plus p for every odd prime number.
