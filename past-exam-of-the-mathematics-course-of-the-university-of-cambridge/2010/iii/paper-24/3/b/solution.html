The intended statement uses a nontrivial Non-Archimedean absolute value. Under that convention choose t with 0<∣t∣<1. A compact neighborhood C of zero contains an open ball of some radius r>0. For sufficiently large N, tNOK​ is contained in that ball. The valuation ring is closed, so tNOK​ is a closed subset of C and is compact. Scaling shows that OK​ itself is compact.
Its maximal ideal m={x:∣x∣<1} is open. The residue field k=OK​/m is therefore discrete and compact, hence finite: the open cover by its singleton sets has a finite subcover. Since m has finite index, its complement is a finite union of open cosets. Thus m is also closed and compact.
The continuous absolute value on a field attains its maximum on m. Nontriviality supplies a nonzero element there, so this maximum is ρ=∣π∣ for some π=0, with 0<ρ<1. If x∈m, then ∣x/π∣≤1, so m=πOK​.
For arbitrary x=0, choose the integer n for which ρn+1<∣x∣≤ρn. Then ρ<∣x/πn∣≤1. This element cannot lie in m, by maximality of ρ, so its absolute value on a field is one. Therefore ∣x∣=ρn. This proves discrete valuation from nontrivial local compactness:

The nontrivial valuation in the local compactness criterion is essential. If the trivial absolute value is admitted, the displayed source claim is false: take Q with that absolute value on a field. It is complete and discrete, hence locally compact, but its valuation ring and residue field are both Q, which is infinite. The intended nontrivial case has been proved above.
