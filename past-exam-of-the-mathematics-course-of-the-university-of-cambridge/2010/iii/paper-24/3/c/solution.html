A local field is nondiscrete and locally compact. In the non-Archimedean case its valuation ring is compact by the scaling argument in part (b). Any Cauchy sequence has a tail in a translate of a compact ball, so has a convergent subsequence; the Cauchy property forces the whole sequence to converge. Thus the field is complete. Part (b) gives a discrete valuation, a uniformizer π, and a finite residue field k=Fq​, q=pf.
We construct the coefficient field rather than presuming it exists. The polynomial Tq−T has derivative −1 in characteristic p. Every residue element therefore has a unique polynomial root lift by Hensel's lemma. The set S of these q lifts is a field: qth powers preserve addition and multiplication, so sums and products remain polynomial root lifts; a nonzero lift has inverse aq−2. Reduction identifies S with Fq​. This is the finite coefficient field in positive-characteristic local fields.
For x∈OK​, choose a0​∈S with the same residue, write x=a0​+πx1​, and repeat with x1​∈OK​. Completeness gives
x=∑j≥0​aj​πj,aj​∈S.
(24)
The first nonzero coefficient determines the valuation, so the expansion is unique. Multiplying an arbitrary field element by a suitable power of π reduces it to this case. Consequently substitution T↦π gives a valued-field isomorphism
K≅Fpf​((T)),f≥1.​
(25)
Addition and multiplication agree with those of Laurent series by convergence, and a nonzero series has a nonzero leading coefficient, establishing injectivity as well as surjectivity.
Conversely, Fq​((T)) is complete for its T-adic absolute value on a field. Its valuation ring Fq​[[T]] is compact: fixing finitely many coefficients gives the finite partitions into balls, and the successive coefficient choices realize the inverse limit of the finite rings Fq​[T]/(Tn). Equivalently it is the product of countably many finite discrete coefficient sets. Thus it is a non-Archimedean local field. Distinct q give nonisomorphic valued fields because their residue fields have different sizes. Replacing ∣T∣ by any number in (0,1) gives an equivalent absolute value on a field.
