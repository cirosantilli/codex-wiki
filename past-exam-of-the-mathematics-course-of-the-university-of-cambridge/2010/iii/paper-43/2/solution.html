A Lie algebra representation is a linear map ρ:g→End(V) preserving brackets: ρ([X,Y])=[ρ(X),ρ(Y)]. Differentiating a smooth unitary representation D of a Lie group gives ρ(X)=dtd​D(etX)​t=0​, an anti-Hermitian operator for each real algebra element X. In the physics convention one uses Hermitian generators tX​=iρ(X) and writes D(etX)=e−ittX​. Conversely, integration of a Lie-algebra representation gives a representation of the connected simply connected group. A representation of another group with the same algebra additionally has to be trivial on the kernel of its covering map.
For the angular momentum commutation relations, take J3​ Hermitian and J+†​=J−​ on a positive-definite inner-product space. A highest-weight vector has J3​∣j,j⟩=j∣j,j⟩ and J+​∣j,j⟩=0. Set vℓ​=(J−​)ℓ∣j,j⟩. Commuting J3​ through each lowering operator gives J3​vℓ​=(j−ℓ)vℓ​. Furthermore,
[J+​,(J−​)ℓ]=∑r=0ℓ−1​(J−​)r(2J3​)(J−​)ℓ−1−r,
(13)
so acting on the highest-weight state and summing the coefficients gives
J+​vℓ​=ℓ(2j−ℓ+1)vℓ−1​.
(14)
Taking the inner product with vℓ−1​ produces the unitary highest-weight termination recursion
∥vℓ​∥2=ℓ(2j−ℓ+1)∥vℓ−1​∥2.
(15)
The first factor requires j≥0. If 2j is not an integer, the first integer ℓ>2j+1 would make the norm negative, while every preceding norm is positive. Thus
2j∈Z≥0​.​
(16)
For these values, v0​,…,v2j​ have strictly positive norms and distinct eigenvalues, while v2j+1​ has zero norm and hence is zero. They therefore span a space of dimension 2j+1, invariant under all three generators. Its weights are j,j−1,…,−j. This proves integer or half-integer spin and finite dimension 2j+1. The positive-definite unitary assumption matters: an unrestricted algebraic highest-weight module need not terminate. Normalizing the ladder states yields
J±​∣j,m⟩=(j∓m)(j±m+1)​∣j,m±1⟩.
(17)
This is the normalized highest-weight lowering formula with the usual positive ladder phases and units ℏ=1.
In the ordered spin-one-half basis (∣1/2,1/2⟩,∣1/2,−1/2⟩), the matrices are
J3​=21​(10​0−1​),J+​=(00​10​),J−​=(01​00​).​
(18)
Thus Ji​=σi​/2, where the σi​ are Pauli matrices.
Choose the active-rotation convention. The SU(2) lift of an axis-angle rotation is
U1/2​(ϑ,n)=e−iϑn⋅σ/2=cos(ϑ/2)I−isin(ϑ/2)n⋅σ.
(19)
The Pauli matrix multiplication law shows that conjugating x⋅σ by this matrix produces (Rx)⋅σ, with
Rx=xcosϑ+(n×x)sinϑ+n(n⋅x)(1−cosϑ).
(20)
This is the ordinary three-dimensional rotation. In any spin-j representation the corresponding unitary operator and its matrix elements are
Uj​(R)=e−iϑn⋅J(j),Dmm′(j)​(R)=⟨j,m∣Uj​(R)∣j,m′⟩.​
(21)
For half-integer spin, R in this notation includes a choice of lift to SU(2); it does not define a single-valued representation of SO(3).
For the specified Euler-angle order, the rightmost rotation acts first:
Uj​(R)=e−iϕJ3​e−iθJ2​e−iψJ3​,Dmm′(j)​(R)=e−imϕdmm′(j)​(θ)e−im′ψ.​
(22)
Here the Wigner D-matrix middle factor is dmm′(j)​(θ)=⟨j,m∣e−iθJ2​∣j,m′⟩. It can be calculated explicitly by realizing spin j as the symmetric product of 2j spin-one-half factors. Write c=cos(θ/2) and s=sin(θ/2). The fundamental rotation sends ∣+⟩ to c∣+⟩+s∣−⟩ and ∣−⟩ to −s∣+⟩+c∣−⟩. Expanding a normalized symmetric state with j+m′ plus signs gives
dmm′(j)​(θ)=(j+m)!(j−m)!(j+m′)!(j−m′)!​∑r​(j+m′−r)!r!(m−m′+r)!(j−m−r)!(−1)m−m′+rc2j+m′−m−2rsm−m′+2r​,
(23)
where max(0,m′−m)≤r≤min(j+m′,j−m). The exponent r counts initially positive spinors that become negative, while m−m′+r counts initially negative ones that become positive; their minus signs and binomial coefficients give the displayed expression. Every factorial argument is an integer even for half-integer spin.
For spin one-half this reduces to the explicit matrix
D(1/2)(ϕ,θ,ψ)=(e−i(ϕ+ψ)/2cos(θ/2)ei(ϕ−ψ)/2sin(θ/2)​−e−i(ϕ−ψ)/2sin(θ/2)ei(ϕ+ψ)/2cos(θ/2)​).
(24)
Replacing θ by θ+2π reverses both sine and cosine, so every entry changes sign. In particular, a pure 2π rotation gives D(1/2)=−I, while a 4π rotation gives I.
Finally, the Adjoint double cover from SU(2) to SO(3) is surjective by the axis-angle construction. Its kernel consists of matrices commuting with all Pauli matrices, hence scalar matrices; within SU(2) these are ±I. Thus
SO(3)≅SU(2)/{±I}.​
(25)
Their Lie algebras are isomorphic, but their global topology and representations differ. In spin j, the central element −I acts as (−1)2jI, so exactly the integer-spin representations descend to ordinary SO(3) representations. This is the descent of an SU(2) representation to SO(3) criterion.
