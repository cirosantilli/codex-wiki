Although n2 vanishes on the hypersurface, it need not vanish off it. Its tangential derivatives vanish, so ∇a​(n2) must be proportional to the normal na​. Hence ∇n​n=κn on the hypersurface for some smooth local coefficient κ. The normal's integral curves of a vector field remain in the hypersurface and satisfy the nonaffine geodesic equation. Rescale to k=hn, where n(logh)=−κ along each generator. Direct substitution gives ∇k​k=0. Thus the normal generates null geodesics within the hypersurface, with an affine parameter after this rescaling. This proves the null hypersurface normal generates null geodesics property without incorrectly setting a transverse derivative of n2 to zero.
