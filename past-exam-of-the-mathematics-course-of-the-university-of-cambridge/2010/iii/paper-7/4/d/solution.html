For a closed manifold M with an oriented Riemannian metric, define δ to be the formal L2 adjoint operator of the exterior derivative, equivalently δ=(−1)n(q+1)+1∗d∗ on q-forms. The Hodge Laplacian is Δq​=dδ+δd. The Hodge decomposition theorem states that its space Hq=kerΔq​ of harmonic differential forms is finite-dimensional and smooth, and that
Ωq(M)=Hq⊕⊥dΩq−1(M)⊕⊥δΩq+1(M),HdRq​(M)≅Hq.​
(54)
The last isomorphism sends a harmonic differential form to its de Rham cohomology class: every class has exactly one harmonic representative. Out-of-range degree spaces are zero.
We first justify the analytic facts needed in the proof. In local coordinates and a smooth frame for the form bundle, the principal symbol of Δq​ is gijξi​ξj​I. This follows from the exterior/contraction identities for d and δ. Freezing the leading coefficients, the Fourier transform estimate for the constant positive quadratic form gives, for each nonnegative integer s,
∥u∥Hs+2​≤Cs​(∥Δq​u∥Hs​+∥u∥L2​).
(55)
Here is how to pass to variable coefficients. On a sufficiently small coordinate ball the difference of leading coefficients has arbitrarily small supremum norm. After differentiating s times, its top-order contribution is bounded by ε∥u∥Hs+2​; derivatives of the coefficients and first-order terms contribute Cs​∥u∥Hs+1​. A partition of unity gives the same types of terms from commutators with the cutoffs. Absorb the epsilon term and use Sobolev interpolation to absorb ∥u∥Hs+1​ into a small multiple of ∥u∥Hs+2​ plus C∥u∥2​. This proves the estimate.
The first-order energy form Q(u,u)=∥du∥22​+∥δu∥22​ satisfies Q(u,u)+c∥u∥22​≥c′∥u∥H12​ for a sufficiently large c>0. This follows from the same frozen-symbol argument at first order, or by integrating the principal part and absorbing the lower-order terms. The Lax-Milgram theorem thus constructs a bounded inverse R=(Δq​+c)−1:L2→H1 in the weak sense. To obtain H2 regularity of this weak solution, use a local difference quotient δh​u and test the weak equation with −δ−h​(χ2δh​u). Positivity of gij bounds ∥χδh​∇u∥22​; derivatives of χ and difference quotients of the smooth coefficients give terms bounded by C∥u∥H1​∥χδh​∇u∥2​ and C∥u∥H12​. The forcing contributes at most C∥f∥2​(∥χδh​∇u∥2​+∥u∥H1​). Absorption gives a bound independent of h, so the difference-quotient criterion yields u∈H2. Differentiating the equation and repeating gives the higher estimates and smoothness for smooth forcing. Thus the use of elliptic regularity here has been justified rather than assumed.
The inverse R is compact as an L2 operator by the Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem, and self-adjoint by symmetry of the weak energy form. It is positive and injective with dense range. The spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators now gives an orthonormal eigenbasis of Δq​, with nonnegative eigenvalues tending to infinity. Each eigenfunction is smooth by the estimates above. In particular Hq is finite-dimensional and there is a positive gap above its zero eigenvalue.
Let Hq​ be the orthogonal projection onto Hq. Define the Green operator of the Hodge Laplacian by multiplying a positive-eigenvalue coefficient by 1/λ and sending the zero coefficients to zero. The gap makes this bounded on L2, and the elliptic estimate shows it maps Hs to Hs+2; hence it sends smooth forms to smooth forms. It obeys
Δq​Gq​=Gq​Δq​=I−Hq​.
(56)
For a smooth q-form α, expand the left side to obtain
α=Hq​α+dδGq​α+δdGq​α.
(57)
These three summands are mutually orthogonal. The energy identity
⟨Δq​h,h⟩=∥dh∥22​+∥δh∥22​
(58)
shows that a harmonic differential form has both dh=0 and δh=0, making it orthogonal to the exact and coexact summands by integration by parts. Also ⟨dβ,δγ⟩=⟨d2β,γ⟩=0. This proves the claimed direct sum and its uniqueness.
Finally let dα=0 and write u=Gq​α. We have dΔq​u=Δq+1​du because d2=0. Since d(α−Hq​α)=0, du is harmonic. It is also exact, so its orthogonality to harmonic forms gives du=0. The decomposition therefore reduces to α=Hq​α+dδu: every closed differential form is cohomologous to a harmonic one. If a harmonic h is exact, h=dβ, then ∥h∥22​=⟨δh,β⟩=0. Hence its class is zero only when h=0, establishing the isomorphism with de Rham cohomology.
