Let M be a closed manifold of even dimension n=2m, with a Riemannian metric and a spin structure. Let S=S+⊕S− be its spinor bundle, and let W be a Hermitian vector bundle with a unitary connection of vector-bundle curvature FW=(∇W)2. We use Clifford multiplication satisfying c(v)c(w)+c(w)c(v)=−2⟨v,w⟩, so the Twisted Dirac operator
DW​=∑i​c(ei)∇ei​S⊗W​=(0DW+​​DW−​0​)
(1)
is self-adjoint and DW−​=(DW+​)∗. Here (ei​) is an oriented local orthonormal basis and (ei) its dual. The Atiyah-Singer index theorem in this setting is
indDW+​=∫M​[A(RTM)∧trexp(2πiFW​)]2m​.​
(2)
The second factor is the Chern character; the first is the A-hat form
A(R)=det1/2(sinh(R/(4πi))R/(4πi)​).
(3)
The square root has constant term one. All functions of the vector-bundle curvature matrices mean power series truncated above the dimension of M. The brackets select the top-degree differential form. The Bianchi identity shows that these forms are closed, and Chern-Weil connection transgression shows that their de Rham cohomology classes do not depend on the chosen connections on a vector bundle.
The global step is supersymmetric quantum mechanics. On the Hilbert space L2(S⊗W), take the grading operator Γ, the odd supercharge DW​, and the nonnegative Hamiltonian H=DW2​. They obey ΓDW​=−DW​Γ and DW2​=H. The principal symbol of DW​ is invertible away from the zero covector, so elliptic regularity and Rellich-Kondrachov compactness theorem give a smooth discrete orthonormal eigenbasis for H, finite-dimensional eigenspaces, and a trace-class operator e−tH for t>0. Define its supertrace by StrA=Tr(ΓA).
For every positive eigenvalue λ of H, the map λ−1/2DW​ is an isomorphism between its positive and negative grading eigenspaces, with inverse itself. Their contributions to the supertrace cancel. The zero eigenspaces are kerDW+​ and kerDW−​. Consequently
Stre−tDW2​=dimkerDW+​−dimkerDW−​=indDW+​(t>0).
(4)
This also proves independence of t without differentiating an unbounded operator inside a trace. If Kt​ is the heat kernel, the heat kernel trace formula gives
indDW+​=∫M​strS⊗W​Kt​(x,x)dvolg​(x).
(5)
The remaining task is to compute this local integrand as t↓0.
Fix p∈M, use geodesic normal coordinates x, and trivialize S⊗W by radial parallel transport. Expanding DW2​ at p gives the Lichnerowicz formula for a twisted Dirac operator:
DW2​=∇∗∇+41​Scal+∑i<j​c(ei)c(ej)FijW​.
(6)
Indeed the terms with identical indices give the rough Laplacian. The other terms are ∑i<j​c(ei)c(ej)[∇i​,∇j​]. Decompose this vector-bundle curvature into spin curvature and FW; substituting the spin curvature into the Clifford algebra relations and the algebraic Bianchi identity leaves Scal/4. Thus the calculation retains the curvature of the twisting bundle explicitly.
There is an especially strong cancellation in the spinor supertrace. With Γ=imc(e1)⋯c(e2m), every ordered product of distinct Clifford generators of degree less than 2m has zero supertrace, while
strS​(c(e1)⋯c(e2m))=(−2i)m.
(7)
For an even product of lower degree, conjugate Γ times that product by a missing Clifford generator: it changes sign, so its matrix trace is zero. An odd product exchanges the grading summands and also has zero supertrace. For the full product, (c(e1)⋯c(e2m))2=(−1)m, and dimS=2m, giving the displayed constant.
To exploit this, identify the Clifford algebra as a filtered vector space with the exterior algebra by sending an ordered Clifford product to the corresponding wedge product of differential forms. Implement Getzler rescaling: put x=εy, t=ε2s, multiply the degree-k exterior component of the kernel by ε−k, and multiply the whole kernel by ε2m. A derivative and a Clifford generator have rescaling degree one; a coordinate has degree minus one. Clifford contractions lower the degree by two relative to the wedge product of differential forms, so the leading product becomes exterior multiplication.
In the radially parallel frame, the linear Taylor term of the connection is determined by its vector-bundle curvature: if Ai​ is its matrix, then Ai​(x)=21​xjFji​(p)+O(∣x∣2). To check the coefficient, radial gauge means xiAi​=0, and integration of xjFji​ along the radial segment gives Ai​(x)=∫01​sxjFji​(sx)ds. Combining this formula with the spin representation, the symmetries of Riemann curvature tensor, and the Lichnerowicz formula for a twisted Dirac operator, the rescaled square has leading model
Hp​=−∑i​(∂yi​​−41​∑j​Rij​yj​)2+FpW​.
(8)
Here R is the skew matrix of tangent-bundle curvature two-forms at p; changing the curvature-matrix sign convention changes the sign inside the covariant derivatives but not the determinant below. The coefficients act in ΛTp∗​M⊗End(Wp​). The scalar-curvature term has degree zero and disappears after multiplication by ε2; the linear spin-connection term and the Clifford action of FW have degree two and survive. Higher Taylor terms have smaller degree and disappear.
For clarity, the analytic meaning of this limiting calculation is the following. Construct the local heat kernel expansion to any fixed order and insert the Taylor expansion of the rescaled coefficients into its transport equations. At each exterior degree, the leading equation is the heat equation of Hp​; the remaining terms acquire positive powers of ε. Equivalently, successive substitutions in
e−sPε​−e−sHp​=−∫0s​e−(s−u)Pε​(Pε​−Hp​)e−uHp​du
(9)
give the same expansion. Polynomial spatial factors are integrable against the local Gaussian kernels, and only finitely many exterior degrees exist. A cutoff outside a fixed normal neighbourhood contributes O(e−c/t). Choosing the initial expansion order arbitrarily large bounds its differentiated remainder to any required order. This proves convergence of the rescaled diagonal kernel to the model diagonal kernel, uniformly in p on the compact manifold. Its degree-2m component has no net power of ε, precisely the component detected by the spinor supertrace.
We can compute the model kernel rather than invoke a local index formula. Set F=FpW​ and seek its kernel from the origin in the form
ks​(y,0)=a(s)exp(−41​yTQ(s)y)e−sF.
(10)
The scalar even exterior coefficients of R commute. On this ansatz the rotational first-order term vanishes because Q is a function of R2. Expanding the model operator gives −∑∂i2​+21​(Ry)⋅∇+161​yTR2y+F, so substitution into the heat equation gives
Q′=41​R2−Q2,aa′​=−21​trQ.
(11)
The Euclidean initial condition Q(s)∼s−1I, a(s)∼(4πs)−m selects
Q(s)=2R​coth(2sR​),a(s)=(4πs)−mdet1/2(sinh(sR/2)sR/2​).
(12)
These expressions have well-defined power series at R=0. This is the curvature version of the Gaussian calculation underlying the Mehler formula for Hermite functions.
At s=1, y=0, multiply the top exterior coefficient by the spinor supertrace constant. Since (4π)−m(−2i)m=(2πi)−m, and every curvature factor is a two-form, the result is
limt↓0​strKt​(p,p)dvolg​(p)=[det1/2(sinh(RTM/(4πi))RTM/(4πi)​)trexp(−2πiFW​)]2m​(p).
(13)
Integration and the constant supertrace established above prove the boxed Atiyah-Singer index theorem. The mechanism is that odd symmetry pairs the nonzero spectrum globally, while the Clifford filtration makes the surviving local contribution an explicitly solvable curvature oscillator. Odd-dimensional spin Dirac operators have no canonical chiral index of this type; their self-adjoint Fredholm index is zero. For comparison of normalization conventions, Raphaël Ponge's local index calculation writes the same unnormalized curvature model and spinor supertrace constant.
