Use aij=gij, r2=∣x∣2, and E=x⋅∇. The Gauss lemma from part (a) gives
aijxi​=xj​,aijxi​xj​=r2,∑i,j​∂j​(aijxi​)=n.
(62)
For the Gaussian qt​(x)=(4πt)−n/2e−r2/(4t), we have ∂i​qt​=−xi​qt​/(2t). Applying precisely the divergence-form operator in the paper yields
Lqt​=−∂j​(aij∂i​qt​)=2t1​∂j​(xj​qt​)=(2tn​−4t2r2​)qt​.
(63)
Since ∂t​qt​=(−n/(2t)+r2/(4t2))qt​, the Gaussian itself already satisfies (∂t​+L)qt​=0.
For completeness, the product rule gives for an arbitrary smooth G,
(∂t​+L)(qt​G)=qt​(∂t​G+LG+t1​EG).
(64)
Indeed the cross term in L(qt​G) is −2aij(∂i​qt​)(∂j​G)=qt​EG/t. Substituting the formal power series G=∑k≥0​tkBk​, with B0​=1, gives the radial transport equations
(E+k)Bk​=−LBk−1​,k≥1.
(65)
Along a radial segment their unique smooth solution is
Bk​(x)=−∫01​sk−1(LBk−1​)(sx)ds.
(66)
The integral is smooth including at the centre, since each derivative adds a nonnegative power of s to the integrable factor sk−1. Uniqueness follows because a homogeneous solution h has d(skh(sx))/ds=0; smoothness makes its limit at s=0 zero, so h(x)=0. This also excludes singular ray-dependent homogeneous solutions.
Here L1=0, so the recursion successively gives Bk​=0 (k≥1),G(x,t)=1​. Thus both existence and uniqueness hold, with no convergence issue because the formal solution is the exact Gaussian. This is the normal-coordinate divergence-form heat parametrix. The positive Laplace-Beltrami operator −ρ−1∂j​(ρgij∂i​), where ρ=detg​, has an additional first-order term and a different leading amplitude; it is essential to use the operator actually specified here.
