Use a one-sided top-hat plume model with Boussinesq approximation and neglect the solid/liquid density difference in the interfacial volume source. Define the positive ablation speed U=−a˙, volume flux per unit wall span Q=bw, and kinematic momentum flux J=bw2. Let ambient entrainment per height be E=εw, with entrainment coefficient ε, and take ρ=ρ∞​[1+βC​(C−C∞​)] for the salinity contribution to density. Conservation of volume and vertical momentum, with negligible wall stress and negligible vertical momentum of entering fluid, gives
dzd(bw)​=εw+U=εw−a˙,dzd(bw2)​=gβC​(C∞​−C)b.​
(32)
Only the ocean-facing edge entrains. Using two edges would change the entrainment coefficient in these formulas.
To derive the heat balance, choose liquid specific enthalpy hl​=cp​T+L and solid specific enthalpy hs​=cp​Ts​. A horizontal slab receives ambient volume Edz with enthalpy h∞​=cp​T∞​+L, and solid mass-equivalent volume Udz with enthalpy hs​. Thus
dzd​[Q(cp​T+L)]=E(cp​T∞​+L)+Ucp​Ts​.
(33)
Subtract (cp​T∞​+L)Q′ and divide by cp​:
dzd​[bw(T−T∞​)]=a˙[cp​L​+T∞​−Ts​].​
(34)
Similarly, the incoming salt flux is EC∞​+UCs​, so (QC)′=EC∞​+UCs​. Subtracting C∞​Q′ proves
dzd​[bw(C−C∞​)]=a˙(C∞​−Cs​).​
(35)
Both anomalies are negative for fresh, cold melt entering the ocean. These balances account for the heat needed to melt the entering ice, rather than treating it as already-liquid water at Ts​.
Now use L/cp​≫T∞​−Ts​, the large latent-to-sensible heat ratio convention of the paper. Put ΔC=C∞​−Cs​>0. The stated small plume-deficit limits allow us to replace plume T,C by their ambient values in the diffusive interfacial fluxes. The Stefan condition and interfacial salt balance are then
cp​L​U=δκ​(T∞​−Ti​),U(Ci​−Cs​)=δD​(C∞​−Ci​).
(36)
For the compositional Péclet number P≡Pec​=Uδ/D, solve the second equation and impose the liquidus:
Ci​=Cs​+1+PΔC​,Ti​=Tm​−1+PΓΔC​.​
(37)
Substitution in the heat balance gives the diffusive glacier-ablation interface law
cp​κLD​P=T∞​−Tm​+1+PΓ(C∞​−Cs​)​,a˙=−δD​P.​
(38)
Here Γ is the liquidus slope, unrelated to the surface-energy parameter of the previous question. If AT​=LD/(cp​κ), Θ=T∞​−Tm​ and BT​=ΓΔC, the physical root is
P=2AT​Θ−AT​+(AT​+Θ)2+4AT​BT​​​.​
(39)
It is positive when Θ+BT​>0, exactly the condition that the ocean is warmer than its own liquidus. Since δ and ambient properties are fixed, U is constant with height in this approximation.
For the melting-driven saline wall plume, measure height Z=z−z0​ from a virtual origin. The integrated anomaly balances are
Q(T∞​−T)=Ucp​L​Z,Q(C∞​−C)=UΔCZ.
(40)
In the specified small-deficit regime the meltwater volume is negligible compared with entrainment. Indeed, the solution below gives (C∞​−C)/ΔC=4U/(3εw), so a small compositional plume deficit implies U≪εw. Thus retain Q′=εw for the leading power laws, although U remains essential in the salt and heat sources. Try b=BZ, w=WZq. The salt deficit is proportional to Z−q, and the momentum balance has powers Z2q=Z1−q, hence q=1/3. The volume balance gives (4/3)BW=εW, and the momentum coefficient gives (5/3)BW2=gβC​UΔC/W. Therefore
b=43​εZ,w=WZ1/3,W=(5ε4gβC​UΔC​)1/3,​
(41)
C∞​−C=3εW4UΔC​Z−1/3,T∞​−T=3cp​εW4UL​Z−1/3.​
(42)
These are entrainment-dominated power laws, not an exact solution with the U term retained in the volume equation. They fail close to the virtual origin, where the formal temperature and salinity deficits diverge. Their buoyancy source is distributed melting along the wall, unlike a line plume with a constant buoyancy flux from a localized source.
The profiles rise from Ti​,Ci​ across the laminar boundary layer to the almost-ambient plume values T,C, then approach T∞​,C∞​ across the entraining edge. The sketch uses the linear-gradient approximation implicit in the prescribed diffusive fluxes; it is not an exact normal advection–diffusion profile at large P.
For P≪1,
Ci​=C∞​−ΔCP+O(P2),Ti​=TL​(C∞​)+BT​P+O(P2),P≃AT​+BT​Θ+BT​​.​
(43)
The last formula retains both first-order terms and is appropriate near the no-ablation threshold. Salt diffusion keeps the interface saline, and freezing-point depression allows ice dissolution in saline water even when T∞​<Tm​. Latent heat is still needed, but the thermodynamic permission to ablate comes from the depressed liquidus; small P does not mean a negligible energy cost.
For P≫1, Ci​−Cs​∼ΔC/P and Ti​∼Tm​−BT​/P. Rapid production of fresh melt dilutes the interface, so its temperature approaches the pure-ice melting point. In the usual thermally driven regime Θ>0 and Θ≫BT​/P,
P≃Θ/AT​,U≃Lδcp​κ​(T∞​−Tm​).​
(44)
This is predominantly thermal melting. More generally the retained large-P balance is AT​P≃Θ+BT​/P. For example, Θ=0 gives P∼BT​/AT​​, which can be large while the driving term is still saline freezing-point depression. Thus the melting/dissolution interpretation must also consider the ocean's temperature relative to Tm​, not the compositional Péclet number alone.
