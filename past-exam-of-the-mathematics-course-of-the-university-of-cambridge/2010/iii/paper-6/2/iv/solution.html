True. Choose a norm-dense sequence (xn​) in the unit ball of the separable normed vector space E. On the dual unit ball B′, define
d(ϕ,ψ)=n=1∑∞​2−n∣ϕ(xn​)−ψ(xn​)∣.​
(17)
Each summand is at most 21−n, so the series converges uniformly. Symmetry and the triangle inequality follow term by term. If d(ϕ,ψ)=0, the two continuous functionals agree on the dense sequence, hence on the unit ball and then on all of E. Thus d is a metric.
A relative weak-star neighbourhood controls finitely many evaluations. Conversely, to make d small, first choose a tail whose sum is small and then control the finitely many evaluations at x1​,…,xN​. This proves that each metric ball is weak-star open. For the other direction, let x∈E and ϵ>0. Approximate x/∥x∥ by xn​ when x=0. Since ∥ϕ−ψ∥≤2 on B′,
∣ϕ(x)−ψ(x)∣≤∥x∥∣ϕ(xn​)−ψ(xn​)∣+2∥x∥∥x/∥x∥−xn​∥.
(18)
The second term can be made smaller than ϵ/2 by choosing n; the first is smaller than ϵ/2 whenever d(ϕ,ψ) is sufficiently small, because ∣ϕ(xn​)−ψ(xn​)∣≤2nd(ϕ,ψ). Every evaluation is therefore metric-continuous. These two inclusions show that d induces exactly the weak-star topology, proving weak-star metrizability of the dual ball without a completeness assumption on E.
