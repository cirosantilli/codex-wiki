Assume the finite-generator identity holds whenever there is no common zero. Let φ∈ΦA​. For every finite family f1​,…,fn​∈kerφ, their zero sets must have nonempty intersection: otherwise an identity ∑fj​gj​=1 would give 0=φ(1)=1. These zero sets are closed in compact K. The finite intersection property therefore supplies x∈K at which every member of kerφ vanishes. For any f∈A, the element f−φ(f)1 is in that kernel, so f(x)=φ(f). Thus φ=δx​, proving
the finite-generator identity holds⟺A is natural.​
(31)
For C(K), the identity is explicit. If the fj​ have no common zero, the continuous function q=∑j​∣fj​∣2 is strictly positive on compact K. Set
gj​=qfj​​​∈C(K).
(32)
Then ∑fj​gj​=1. The equivalence just proved shows that C(K) is natural.
Next consider the disc algebra A(D), the continuous functions on the closed unit disk that are holomorphic in its interior. We show directly that each algebra character is evaluation. Polynomials are uniformly dense in this algebra: for 0<r<1, fr​(z)=f(rz) tends uniformly to f as r↑1, by uniform continuity. The function fr​ is holomorphic on ∣z∣<1/r, so its Taylor polynomials converge uniformly on ∣z∣≤1.
Every algebra character of a unital Banach algebra is bounded, with ∣φ(f)∣≤∥f∥: if f−φ(f)1 were invertible, applying φ would contradict invertibility, so φ(f) belongs to the spectrum of an element, which is contained in the disk of radius ∥f∥ by the Neumann series. For the coordinate function z, put λ=φ(z); then ∣λ∣≤1. Multiplicativity gives φ(p)=p(λ) for every polynomial. By polynomial density and continuity, φ(f)=f(λ) for every member of the disc algebra. Hence
ΦA(D)​≅D,A(D) is natural.​
(33)
Finally consider the algebra C in the last request. For each f∈C, its holomorphic extension g from the boundary is unique by the maximum modulus principle. Define T(f)=g∈A(D). This is a surjective unital algebra homomorphism, since it restricts to the identity on the disc algebra. Also
∥T(f)∥∞​=sup∣z∣=1​∣f(z)∣≤∥f∥∞​.
(34)
The algebra C is closed: if fn​→f uniformly, the associated T(fn​) are uniformly Cauchy by this bound, so their limit belongs to the closed disc algebra and agrees with f on the boundary. Constants and the coordinate function belong to C, so it is itself a uniform algebra on the disk. Its ideal
I=kerT={f∈C(D):f∣∂D​=0}
(35)
is contained in C, and C/I is the disc algebra.
If a algebra character χ on C vanishes on I, it factors through T and the preceding result gives
χ(f)=T(f)(λ)for some λ∈D.
(36)
If χ does not vanish on I, choose h∈I with χ(h)=0. For F∈C(D), define
χ​(F)=χ(h)χ(hF)​.
(37)
The product hF is in I, so this is well defined. It is unital and linear, and
χ(hF)χ(hG)=χ(h2FG)=χ(h)χ(hFG)
(38)
shows multiplicativity. Since C(D) is natural, χ​ is evaluation at a point x of the closed disk. The equality h(x)=χ​(h)=χ(h)=0 places x in the open disk. On C, χ​(f)=χ(f), so χ itself is evaluation at that interior point.
We therefore obtain two families: actual evaluations f↦f(x) on one closed disk, and analytic-extension evaluations f↦T(f)(λ) on a second closed disk. They agree on the boundary. They are distinct at interior points: functions in I distinguish actual interior evaluations from every analytic-extension evaluation, and the coordinate function distinguishes points within each family. Thus
ΦC​≅D ∪∂D​ D≅S2.​
(39)
This is also a topological identification. Each disk family is continuous in the Gelfand topology, and they agree along their boundary, giving a continuous bijection from the glued disks to ΦC​. The glued disks form a compact space, explicitly homeomorphic to the sphere by sending (x,y) in the two copies to (x,y,±1−x2−y2​). Since the character space is Hausdorff, the continuous bijection is a homeomorphism. This boundary-analytic disk algebra with doubled character space has more algebra characters than evaluations on its original disk.
