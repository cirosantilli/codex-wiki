In first Born order the unknown potential enters linearly. Let Mi​ multiply by the known incident field, G be the outgoing Green integral over the known support D, and R restrict the resulting field to the measurement set. With y the measured scattered data,
y=AV,A=RGMi​.​
(43)
The formal least-squares, minimum-norm least-squares solution is V=A†y, or V=(A∗A)−1A∗y on a domain where that inverse is meaningful. The refractive index is then
n(r)=1+V(r)/k2​,​
(44)
choosing the physical branch approaching one outside the scatterer. At weak contrast n−1≃V/(2k2).
A noisy stable reconstruction uses Tikhonov regularization:
Vαδ​=argminV​{∥AV−yδ∥Y2​+α∥V∥X2​},Vαδ​=(A∗A+αI)−1A∗yδ,α>0.​
(45)
A smoothness penalty α∥LV∥2 may be substituted when appropriate, giving A∗A+αL∗L in the normal equation for a linear inverse problem, under the corresponding coercivity assumptions. The parameter balances noise amplification and smoothing bias.
The data geometry matters: knowing a scattered field on a measurement surface does not automatically mean knowing it throughout the support. A single incident wave and fixed-frequency far field sample only a shifted sphere of the potential's Fourier transform, and in general do not determine an arbitrary three-dimensional potential uniquely. Tikhonov regularization stabilizes a selected minimum-norm least-squares solution; it cannot create missing information or guarantee uniqueness from inadequate data. If the total field and its derivatives were known throughout D, the first-Born equation could instead be inverted formally as V≃−(Δ+k2)ψs​/ψi​ where ψi​=0, but differentiation still amplifies measurement noise.
