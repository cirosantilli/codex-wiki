Illuminate a surface window of length D, and define hD​(Q)=∫−D/2D/2​h(x)e−iQxdx. In the far field put (x,z)=r(sinθs​,cosθs​), with ∣θs​∣<π/2 away from grazing. The spectral phase ξsinθs​+β(ξ)cosθs​ has stationary point ξs​=ksinθs​, value k, and second derivative −1/(kcos2θs​). Stationary phase therefore gives
ψs[1]​∼iqi​Ai​πr2k​​cosθs​hD​(Q)eikr−iπ/4,Q=ksinθs​−pi​.
(32)
Let Ch​(s)=⟨h(x+s)h(x)⟩ and Sh​(Q)=∫Ch​(s)e−iQsds. Statistical stationarity gives exactly
⟨∣hD​(Q)∣2⟩=∫−DD​(D−∣s∣)Ch​(s)e−iQsds.
(33)
If Ch​ is integrable, division by D and dominated convergence yield ⟨∣hD​(Q)∣2⟩/D→Sh​(Q). Thus the leading rough-surface far-field intensity per illuminated length is
D⟨∣ψs[1]​(r,θs​)∣2⟩​∼πr2kqi2​∣Ai​∣2cos2θs​​Sh​(ksinθs​−pi​).​
(34)
This uses the field-intensity convention I=∣ψ∣2; if ψ is acoustic pressure, multiply by 1/(2ρc) for physical far-field energy flux. The variance normalization is σ2=Ch​(0)=(2π)−1∫Sh​(Q)dQ. Consequently the diffuse intensity is of order σ2 and samples the surface spectrum at the tangential momentum transfer. Its value is not determined by the r.m.s. height alone.
The finite window must first be in its far field, for example r≫kD2, before interpreting the large-window intensity density. An infinitely long stationary surface has infinite illuminated power and no finite total far-field intensity at a fixed point. For nonintegrable correlations the analogous result is a spectral measure, possibly with atoms. Zero mean height makes the linear rough mean field zero, but does not make its mean intensity zero; the flat specular reflection remains separate.
