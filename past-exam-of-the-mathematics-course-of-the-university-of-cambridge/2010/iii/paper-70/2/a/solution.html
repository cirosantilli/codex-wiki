Write r=(y,z) and prescribe a deterministic entrance field E0​(r). Substitution of the carrier into the Helmholtz equation gives
2ikEx​+Exx​+Δ⊥​E+k2(n2−1)E=0.
(9)
The usual weak-index paraxial model drops Exx​ and linearizes n2−1=2μW+O(μ2), giving
Ex​=DE+iaWE,D=2ki​Δ⊥​,a=kμ.
(10)
All the mean-field formulas below use this model. If the μ2W2 term in n2 is retained, its extra mean and fluctuations must also be included; Gaussian averaging of the linear random potential alone is then insufficient.
For a colored medium, taking expectations gives the exact propagation identity
mx​=Dm+ia⟨WE⟩,m(0)=E0​,m=⟨E⟩.​
(11)
Stationarity and Gaussianity alone do not close the last term. To make its dependence precise, let C(s,r)=⟨W(x+s,r0​+r)W(x,r0​)⟩ and let UW​(x,s;r,r′) be the random paraxial propagator. Assuming a jointly Gaussian random field, rather than only Gaussian one-point marginals, the functional derivative of the solution is
δW(s,r′)δE(x,r)​=iaUW​(x,s;r,r′)E(s,r′),0<s<x.
(12)
The Furutsu–Novikov formula therefore yields
mx​(x,r)=Dm(x,r)−a2∫0x​ds∫R2​dr′C(x−s,r−r′)⟨UW​(x,s;r,r′)E(s,r′)⟩.​
(13)
This is a propagation equation for the mean, with the remaining correlation displayed rather than silently replacing it by a product of means.
The general solution can be written explicitly as a Gaussian-averaged phase-screen product. Put Δ=x/N, xj​=jΔ, rN​=r and let
KΔ​(r)=2πiΔk​exp(2Δik∣r∣2​)
(14)
be the integral kernel of the Fresnel propagator. Successive free-propagation and random-phase steps, followed by Gaussian averaging, give the colored Gaussian paraxial mean propagator
m(x,r)=N→∞lim​∫(R2)N​E0​(r0​)j=1∏N​KΔ​(rj​−rj−1​)exp​−2a2Δ2​j,l=1∑N​C(xj​−xl​,rj​−rl​)​j=0∏N−1​drj​.​
(15)
For smooth colored media this is the time-slicing representation, with oscillatory integrals understood by the usual Fresnel regularization. At finite N the exponential follows directly from the characteristic function of the jointly Gaussian phase sum. It exhibits why a general transverse covariance cannot be replaced by its value at zero separation along every diffraction path.
For completeness, the unlinearized paraxial model has Ex​=DE+iaWE+i(kμ2/2)W2E and the corresponding mean equation includes i(kμ2/2)⟨W2E⟩. Its colored-medium solution uses the same time-slicing integral, replacing the Gaussian factor by
det(I−ikμ2ΔC)−1/2exp[−2a2Δ2​1T(I−ikμ2ΔC)−1C1],Cjl​=C(xj​−xl​,rj​−rl​).
(16)
This follows by completing the square in the finite-dimensional Gaussian integral, with the determinant branch continuous from μ=0. It supplies the generic colored-medium mean when the W2 term is retained. A square of ideal white noise is not defined by the covariance in part (b); that limit uses the linear random-potential model and its stated drift.
If transverse diffraction of the fluctuations is neglected, the simpler straight-ray approximation gives
m(x,r)=E0​(r)exp[−a2∫0x​(x−s)C(s,0)ds],mx​=−a2[∫0x​C(s,0)ds]m.​
(17)
Indeed E=E0​exp[ia∫0x​W(s,r)ds] and the variance of its phase is 2a2∫0x​(x−s)C(s,0)ds. If W is constant transversely, this formula is exact with E0​ replaced by the free diffracted field exDE0​. For example W=Z, a single standard normal variable constant throughout space, satisfies stationarity and unit variance but gives m=e−a2x2/2exDE0​. Thus a constant-rate exponential attenuation is not a consequence of part (a)'s assumptions alone. It becomes the closed paraxial result under the longitudinal white-noise assumption in the next part.
