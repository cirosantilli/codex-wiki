On the polydisc, put bi​(z)=fi​(z)−fi​(a) and define
F(z)=∑k=1n​∫ak​zk​​bk​(z1​,…,zk−1​,t,ak+1​,…,an​)dt.
(1)
Each integral is along the straight coordinate segment, which remains in the polydisc. Holomorphic dependence on parameters and differentiation under the integral show that F is a holomorphic function. For a fixed j, terms with k<j do not depend on zj​, and the k=j term contributes bj​(z1​,…,zj​,aj+1​,…,an​). If k>j, use the assumed symmetry ∂j​bk​=∂k​bj​ to obtain
∂j​∫ak​zk​​bk​(…,t,…)dt=bj​(z1​,…,zk​,ak+1​,…,an​)−bj​(z1​,…,zk−1​,ak​,…,an​).
(2)
These differences telescope. Therefore
∂zj​∂F​=fj​(z)−fj​(a),F(a)=0.​
(3)
This is the coordinate-integral primitive on a polydisc.
For a closed holomorphic one-form β=∑i​ci​dzi​, closedness is exactly ∂i​cj​=∂j​ci​. The preceding construction plus ∑i​ci​(a)(zi​−ai​) gives a local holomorphic function G with ∂G=β. The kernel of ∂ on the structure sheaf of a complex manifold consists of locally constant functions: holomorphicity already gives ∂ˉG=0, and ∂G=0 then implies dG=0. Thus the holomorphic Poincaré lemma gives the short exact sequence of sheaves
0⟶C​⟶OM​∂​ΩM1​⟶0.​
(4)
The first object is the complex constant sheaf, and surjectivity is local, on stalks. It does not say that every global closed holomorphic one-form has a global primitive.
Now assume compactness. The constancy assertion is for a connected complex manifold; otherwise it holds on each connected component. A smooth complex-valued u with ∂∂ˉu=0 is a pluriharmonic function. Restricting to each complex coordinate line makes it a harmonic function of one complex variable. Choose x0​ where ∣u∣ has its global maximum and a coordinate polydisc about x0​. The allowed harmonic maximum principle makes the first coordinate-line restriction constant. Every point on that line still has the same maximal modulus and the same value, so the second coordinate may then vary without changing u. Continue through all coordinates to conclude that u equals u(x0​) on the whole polydisc. The set where it has this value is closed and, by the same argument at each of its points, open. Connectedness therefore gives u constant. In particular global holomorphic functions on a compact connected complex manifold are constant.
For the top-degree pairing, write locally θ=gdz1​∧⋯∧dzn​. With the complex orientation,
in2θ∧θ=2n∣g∣2dx1​∧dy1​∧⋯∧dxn​∧dyn​.
(5)
It is everywhere nonnegative and is positive on an open set if θ is not identically zero. Hence
in2∫M​θ∧θ>0,so∫M​θ∧θ=0.​
(6)
The phase factor is necessary: the unnormalized integral need not be a positive real number. This is the integral pairing of complex top forms.
If ψ is a holomorphic differential form of degree n−1, then dψ=∂ψ has type (n,0) and ddψ​=0. The graded Leibniz rule and Stokes theorem give
∫M​dψ∧dψ​=∫M​d(ψ∧dψ​)=0.
(7)
The positive pairing just proved forces dψ=0. Thus every holomorphic (n−1)-form on a compact complex n-manifold is closed, without a Kähler metric assumption.
On a compact complex surface, this proves closedness of all holomorphic one-forms. Sending such a form to its de Rham cohomology class defines the required inclusion. Indeed, if α=df, its type (1,0) gives ∂ˉf=0, so f is a global holomorphic function and is constant. Therefore α=0.
To prove the zero intersection, let α,β be holomorphic one-forms and suppose [α]=[β​]. There is a smooth complex-valued f with α−β​=df. Its type components are ∂f=α and ∂ˉf=−β​. Closedness of β gives ∂β​=0, hence ∂∂ˉf=0. Compact pluriharmonic rigidity now makes f constant and α=β=0. The two spaces are therefore linearly independent in de Rham cohomology, and conjugation preserves their dimensions:
H0(M,ΩM1​)∩H0(M,ΩM1​)​=0,2h1,0≤b1​.​
(8)
For the other bound, take the long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology of the short exact sequence above. The map H0(M,C​)→H0(M,OM​) is an isomorphism, componentwise. Since every global holomorphic one-form is closed, the relevant portion is
0⟶H0(M,ΩM1​)⟶H1(M,C​)⟶H1(M,OM​).
(9)
Identify the middle term with first de Rham cohomology, and the last with H∂ˉ0,1​(M) by the Dolbeault theorem. Taking dimensions gives
2h1,0≤b1​≤h1,0+h0,1,h1,0≤h0,1.​
(10)
These are the Betti and Hodge bounds for compact complex surfaces.
A strict example is the scalar Hopf surface X=(C2∖{0})/⟨z↦2z⟩. Polar coordinates identify it smoothly with S3×S1: the dilation fixes the angular coordinate and shifts log∣z∣ by log2. Its first Betti number is one, since its fundamental group is Z. The proved lower bound then forces h1,0=0, and the upper bound forces h0,1≥1. Thus h1,0<h0,1 on this compact complex surface; no unsupported cohomology computation is needed.
