Measure the number-density profile n(z) and vertical velocity dispersion σz​(z) of a tracer stellar population. The vertical Jeans equation estimates the restoring field from
Kz​=−n1​∂z∂(nσz2​)​−Rn1​∂R∂(RnvR​vz​​)​.
(7)
The second term accounts for the tilt of the velocity ellipsoid; an exponential tracer with constant velocity dispersion and negligible tilt gives Kz​=σz2​/h above the plane. The tracer need not supply all the gravitating mass. To infer the dynamical surface density from vertical stellar motions, integrate the axisymmetric Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity:
Σdyn​(<∣z∣)=2πG1​[Kz​(z)+∫0z​R1​∂R​(R∂R​Φ)dz′],z>0.
(8)
For a nearly flat galaxy rotation curve with a small radial correction, this reduces to Kz​/(2πG). Estimate stellar surface density independently from counts, the initial mass function and faint objects, and add the gas surface density and stellar remnants. The residual
Σexcess​=Σdyn​−Σstars​−Σgas​
(9)
limits the allowed unseen material within the same height interval. A fitted dark matter halo contributes too: measurements at several heights help distinguish its extended density from a thin dark component in the galactic disk. Under a specified decomposition, the disc dark-matter fraction is the inferred dark-disc surface density divided by the total disc surface density, with uncertainty or an upper limit if the residual is consistent with zero. Selection effects, disequilibrium, the tilt term and uncertain baryonic counts must enter the error budget; an excess alone does not establish that all of it is in the galactic disk.
