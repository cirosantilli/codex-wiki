A linearized coordinate gauge transformation changes the identification of points of the perturbed spacetime with its Minkowski spacetime background. With x′i=xi+ϵξi, the same physical metric has perturbation
hij′​=hij​−∂i​ξj​−∂j​ξi​.
(8)
These changes are coordinate freedom, not extra physical gravitational wave polarizations. Raising indices with η, define h=ηijhij​ and the trace-reversed metric perturbation hˉij​=hij​−ηij​h/2. Then
hˉij′​=hˉij​−∂i​ξj​−∂j​ξi​+ηij​∂k​ξk.
(9)
The first-order Levi-Civita connection is Γ(1)kij​=21​ηkℓ(∂i​hjℓ​+∂j​hiℓ​−∂ℓ​hij​). Contracting the Riemann curvature tensor in the stated convention gives
Rij(1)​=21​(∂i​∂k​hkj​+∂j​∂k​hki​−□hij​−∂i​∂j​h),R(1)=∂i​∂j​hij−□h.
(10)
Put Aj​=∂ihˉij​. The corresponding Einstein tensor is
Gij(1)​=−21​□hˉij​+21​(∂i​Aj​+∂j​Ai​−ηij​∂kAk​).
(11)
Under the linearized coordinate gauge transformation, Aj′​=Aj​−□ξj​. Choosing a solution of □ξj​=Aj​ imposes Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, Aj′​=0. Thus the vacuum Einstein field equations reduce to
∂ihˉij​=0,□hˉij​=0.​
(12)
There remains residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity with □ξj​=0.
For the transverse profiles, write both symmetric off-diagonal entries as hxy​=hyx​=h×​(t−z) and the diagonal entries as hxx​=−hyy​=h+​(t−z). The trace-reversed metric perturbation has zero trace and therefore equals h. Its divergence vanishes: its only nonzero index directions are x,y, while its coefficients depend only on t−z. Also (−∂t2​+∂z2​)F(t−z)=0 for every twice differentiable profile F. These are consequently plane gravitational waves in linearized gravity in transverse-traceless gauge, with the two independent gravitational wave polarizations h+​ and h×​.
To calculate the detector response, use a parallel-propagated orthonormal frame along the centre of a short nonrotating stick. To first order its clock reads t, and the tidal curvature is
RA0B0​=−2ϵ​h¨AB​+O(ϵ2),A,B∈{x,y}.
(13)
For a sliding-bead gravitational wave detector, the stick constrains transverse motion but supplies no axial restoring force. Projecting geodesic deviation along an x-directed stick gives
L¨=2ϵ​h¨+​(t−z0​)L0​+O(ϵ2).
(14)
Here L is the proper separation and L0​=L(t0​), with L˙(t0​)=0. Integrating with those actual initial conditions yields
L(t)=L0​[1+2ϵ​(h+​(t−z0​)−h+​(t0​−z0​)−(t−t0​)h˙+​(t0​−z0​))]+O(ϵ2).​
(15)
For beads at rest before an incident wave, both initial profile terms vanish and the familiar result is L(t)=L0​[1+ϵh+​(t−z0​)/2]+O(ϵ2)​. The cross polarization produces a transverse tidal force, balanced by the guide, and no axial first-order displacement in this orientation. In transverse-traceless gauge the same plus response can be read from the proper line element, dℓ=1+ϵh+​​dx; coordinate distances alone are not measured distances. When a cross polarization is present, keeping the guide physically nonrotating may require transverse coordinate adjustments, which do not change the axial answer at this order.
For a z-directed stick, Rz0z0​=0 and the wave is transverse. Therefore beads initially at rest retain their proper separation to first order: L(t)=L0​+O(ϵ2). The tidal calculation is the usual linear detector limit, with bead spacing small compared with the wavelength; it does not treat a macroscopic rigid body as exactly rigid over arbitrary relativistic distances.
