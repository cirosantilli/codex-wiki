The radial part of the Minkowski metric becomes −dudv, and r=(v−u)/2. With u=tanp, v=tanq and the positive conformal factor Ω=2cospcosq, multiplication by Ω2 gives
Ω2(−dudv)=−4dpdq=−dT2+dR2,Ω2r2=(cospcosq)2(tanq−tanp)2=sin2(q−p).
(25)
Consequently the Minkowski conformal compactification is
ds2=Ω−2(−dT2+dR2+sin2RdΣ2),Ω=cosT+cosR.​
(26)
The spatial metric dR2+sin2RdΣ2 is the round unit three-sphere. The extended unphysical metric is therefore that of the Einstein static universe, R×S3 with unit spatial radius. Physical Minkowski spacetime occupies only the diamond-shaped causal patch of this cylinder, or a triangle after restricting to nonnegative spherical radius; it is not the whole cylinder.
The complete coordinate ranges and joint restrictions are
t∈R,r∈[0,∞),θ∈[0,π],ϕ∈[0,2π),u,v∈R,u≤v,−2π​<p≤q<2π​,−π<T<π,0≤R<π,∣T∣+R<π.​
(27)
The polar angles have the usual coordinate degeneracies at their poles and at r=0. In the extended Einstein static universe one may let T∈R and 0≤R≤π, but these are not the ranges of the original physical patch. In its interior Ω>0. The line R=0 is the regular centre r=0, not a component of infinity.
The conformal completion has future timelike infinity i+=(π,0), past timelike infinity i−=(−π,0), and spacelike infinity i0=(0,π). The open sloping boundaries are
I+:T+R=π,0<R<π,I−:T−R=−π,0<R<π,
(28)
namely future null infinity and past null infinity. Each ordinary interior point of the radial diagram represents a two-sphere, and each point on either open null boundary represents a two-sphere of null directions.
A timelike geodesic is an inertial worldline x=b+βt with ∣β∣<1. As t→+∞, both t−r and t+r tend to +∞, giving p,q→π/2 and endpoint i+. At past infinity both tend to −∞, giving i−. These endpoints are reached at infinite physical proper time, although at finite conformal coordinate time T. A null geodesic has r∼∣t∣. In its outgoing future, u approaches a finite retarded time while v→+∞, giving an endpoint on I+; in its incoming past, v has a finite limit and u→−∞, giving an endpoint on I−. A spacelike geodesic is a straight line with spatial tangent larger than its temporal tangent, so u→−∞, v→+∞ at either end and both endpoints are i0 in the compactified radial description. All these physical geodesics are complete. Only the unparametrized null geodesics are necessarily preserved as geodesics of the unphysical metric; timelike and spacelike images need not be its geodesics.
For horizons, distinguish a property of the spacetime from a property of an observer or of an initial-data hypersurface. Complete inertial observers have no particle horizon: in the flat cosmological slicing a(t)=1, the past light-travel distance ∫−∞t0​​dt is infinite. There is no finite-age initial singularity truncating communication between comoving observers. This statement does not say that an event receives signals from spacelike-separated events at the same time.
There is also no black-hole event horizon, because every event can send a future-directed null ray to future null infinity. Nevertheless accelerated timelike observers can have both future and past observer event horizons. For an entire observer worldline γ, define them by Hγ+​=∂I−(γ) and Hγ−​=∂I+(γ), using its complete future and past respectively. An inertial observer's chronological past and future of the whole worldline are both all of Minkowski spacetime. By contrast, for the uniformly accelerated worldline in a Cartesian spatial direction,
t=a−1sinh(aτ),x=a−1cosh(aτ),y=z=0,
(29)
its null coordinates obey t−x=−a−1e−aτ<0 and t+x=a−1eaτ>0. An event with t−x<0 can signal to a sufficiently late point of this worldline, whereas an event with t−x≥0 cannot; finite transverse displacements do not change that conclusion as t+x→∞. Similarly the worldline can signal to precisely the events with t+x>0. Thus its two observer event horizons are
Hγ+​: t−x=0,Hγ−​: t+x=0.​
(30)
They are the Rindler horizons, and their accessible common region is x>∣t∣. They do not indicate a curvature singularity or a failure of global predictability.
Finally Minkowski spacetime is a globally hyperbolic spacetime: every inextendible causal curve crosses each complete spacelike plane t=t0​ exactly once. Indeed t is strictly monotone along future-directed causal curves, and the speed bound ∣dx/dt∣≤1 prevents an inextendible curve from ending at finite t by escaping to spatial infinity. Its time range is therefore all of R. These planes are Cauchy hypersurfaces with full domain of dependence, so there are no Cauchy horizons associated with complete Cauchy data.
A Cauchy horizon is, however, defined relative to a specified initial surface. Under a literal existence reading of “admits”, non-Cauchy initial surfaces can have such horizons even in flat spacetime. For example the edgeless spacelike hyperboloid Σa​: t=a2+r2​ has D(Σa​)={t>r}. To see this, inside the future light cone t−r and t+r are positive and nondecreasing on future-directed causal curves. Their product t2−r2 increases past a2 on every future-inextendible curve from below the hyperboloid; every past-inextendible curve from above it crosses down through a2 before leaving the cone. Outside the cone there is an inextendible null curve avoiding the hyperboloid. The future light cone itself is its past Cauchy horizon. The time-reversed hyperboloid gives a future Cauchy horizon. This is the dependence of a Cauchy horizon on the initial hypersurface, not an intrinsic loss of predictability of full Minkowski spacetime. Thus a categorical claim that no choice of partial initial surface can ever have a Cauchy horizon would be too strong.
