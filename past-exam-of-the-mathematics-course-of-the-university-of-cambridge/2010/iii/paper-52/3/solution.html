Assume M>0 and use geometric units. The stationary Killing vector field K=∂t​ gives the conserved Killing energy E=−g(K,U)=ft˙. For radial motion, normalization of the four-velocity gives −ft˙2+f−1r˙2=−1. Combining these identities yields
ft˙=E,r˙2+f=E2.​
(16)
At rest at infinity, f→1 and r˙→0, so E=1. Choose the inward branch r˙=−2M/r​. Its proper time from the Schwarzschild event horizon to the curvature singularity is
Δτ=∫02M​2Mr​​dr=34M​.​
(17)
In ordinary units this is 4GM/(3c3). The endpoint r=0 is not a regular centre of the spacetime. This is the Schwarzschild horizon-to-singularity proper time for fall from infinity, rather than a universal time for all infall energies.
For this geodesic congruence, set w(r)=2M/r​. Its four-velocity and metric dual are
U=f−1∂t​−w∂r​,Ua​dxa=−dt−f−1wdr.
(18)
The right side is an exact one-form because w/f depends only on r. Thus Ua​=−∂a​τ, with free-fall proper time in Schwarzschild spacetime determined by
dτ=dt+fw​dr,τ=t+22Mr​+2Mlog​r​+2M​r​−2M​​​+constant.​
(19)
Indeed Ua∂a​τ=−UaUa​=1, so this scalar advances by the actual proper time on every member of the infalling geodesic congruence, with a synchronized choice of additive offsets.
Substitution of dt=dτ−wf−1dr in the Schwarzschild metric gives
−fdt2+f−1dr2=−fdτ2+2wdτdr+dr2.
(20)
Using f=1−w2, the Painlevé–Gullstrand coordinates therefore have
ds2=−dτ2+(dr+wdτ)2+r2dΣ2.​
(21)
All coefficients are finite at r=2M; the determinant of the τ,r block is −f−w2=−1. Hence the horizon is a coordinate singularity of Schwarzschild time, not a degeneracy of the metric. The new spatial slices have dℓ2=dr2+r2dΣ2, the flat Euclidean metric, and gab∂a​τ∂b​τ=−1 makes their normals the freely falling four-velocities. By contrast, the curvature scalar Rabcd​Rabcd=48M2/r6 diverges at r=0; this genuine singularity is not removed.
For Ingoing Eddington-Finkelstein coordinates, use v=t+r∗​ with dr∗​/dr=f−1, for example r∗​=r+2Mlog∣r/(2M)−1∣. Then
ds2=−fdv2+2dvdr+r2dΣ2.
(22)
Both constructions cross the future horizon. Their time functions have different meanings: v=constant is an ingoing null hypersurface, whereas τ=constant is a flat spacelike hypersurface orthogonal to the infallers. Thus the former is adapted to ingoing null geodesics, and the latter to a timelike free-fall clock congruence.
For the Kruskal diagram, choose Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates with UK​=−e−(t−r∗​)/(4M), VK​=e(t+r∗​)/(4M) in the right exterior and extend them across its horizon. They obey
UK​VK​=(1−2Mr​)er/(2M),ds2=−r32M3​e−r/(2M)dUK​dVK​+r2dΣ2.
(23)
The four regions of Kruskal spacetime are I, the right exterior (UK​<0,VK​>0); II, the future black hole (UK​>0,VK​>0); III, the other exterior (UK​>0,VK​<0); and IV, the past white hole (UK​<0,VK​<0). The null axes are horizons, their intersection is the bifurcation two-sphere, and the spacelike boundaries UK​VK​=1 are the future and past curvature singularities. A point in this radial diagram represents a symmetry two-sphere.
The domain of the ingoing Painleve-Gullstrand chart is region I together with region II and their common future horizon. More precisely, it is VK​>0, UK​VK​<1, or r>0 with finite τ. To verify the boundary rather than infer it from the locally regular metric, put ρ=r/(2M)​ and write
τ=v+S(r),S(r)=4Mρ−2Mρ2−4Mlog(1+ρ).
(24)
This follows by subtracting r∗​ from the preceding radial primitive, and S is finite at r=2M. Thus VK​=exp[(τ−S(r))/(4M)]>0 throughout the chart. Conversely every point with VK​>0 and r>0 gives a finite τ. The past horizon VK​=0, the bifurcation sphere, region III and region IV are absent. Reversing the sign of the radial term produces the outgoing chart through the white hole instead.
