Set α=log{β(1)/β(0)} and absorb the common multiplier into h0​(t)=m(t)β(0). The model becomes
hj​(t)=h0​(t)eαgj​.
(23)
It is a Cox proportional-hazards model; the unknown baseline does not need a parametric form. The equality hypothesis is precisely α=0. The absolute multipliers and m have a scale ambiguity, but their hazard ratio eα is identifiable when both groups supply information.
For an untied event at ak​, let Rk​ be the risk set just before the event, and let ik​ be the subject who fails. The conditional event probability is proportional to that subject's hazard, so the common baseline cancels:
Pα​(ik​∣Rk​,one event)=∑i∈Rk​​eαgi​eαgik​​​.
(24)
Multiply these factors to form the Cox partial likelihood. One can maximize it and compare its maximum with its value at α=0 using a partial likelihood-ratio test, or use the corresponding score test.
Explicitly, with group risk-set sizes rk0​,rk1​ and observed event indicator dk1​ for group 1, the score at zero and its information are
U=∑k​(dk1​−rk0​+rk1​rk1​​),V=∑k​(rk0​+rk1​)2rk0​rk1​​.
(25)
Under the null the event label has exactly this Bernoulli distribution conditional on its current risk set. Hence U2/V is the usual one-degree-of-freedom log-rank test statistic, with an asymptotic chi-squared distribution when V>0 and enough informative events are observed. Censoring can differ between groups, provided it is independent of event time within the conditioning model. If there are recorded ties, use the appropriate tied-risk-set version: for dk​ events the null variance contribution is dk​(rk​−dk​)rk0​rk1​/[rk2​(rk​−1)].
