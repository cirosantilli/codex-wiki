Write the logistic regression as
logit{p(A,x)}=α+βA+γTx,
(4)
where A=1 denotes elective exposure and x contains the measured background predictors. At fixed x, changing A from zero to one changes the log odds by β, so the adjusted odds ratio is eβ. If V is the estimated coefficient covariance matrix, then
ORadjusted​=eβ​,CI95%​=[eβ​−1.96Vββ​​, eβ​+1.96Vββ​​].​
(5)
Use the clinic-cluster sandwich covariance matrix if that is how within-clinic dependence is allowed for. The interval is first calculated on the coefficient scale and then exponentiated; one does not add and subtract a standard error directly from the odds ratio. This is a large-sample Wald confidence interval; profile-likelihood limits are another option when the likelihood shape warrants them. If exposure interacts with background variables, there is no single common conditional odds ratio: its logarithm includes the corresponding interaction terms.
