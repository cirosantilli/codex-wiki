X0​=0,Xs​=ssin(1/s)(s>0)

The conformal invariance of planar Brownian motion is a time-change statement. If ϕ:D→D′ is a conformal bijection of planar domains and B is planar Brownian motion started in D, let τD​ be its exit time and define
At​=∫0t∧τD​​∣ϕ′(Bs​)∣2ds.
(47)
Before exit this clock is strictly increasing. Its inverse Cu​ makes ϕ(BCu​​) a planar Brownian motion started at ϕ(B0​), up to its transformed lifetime AτD​​. The image of the stopped trajectory is therefore invariant in law after this change of time. A holomorphic map with nonzero derivative gives the corresponding local statement even without global injectivity.
Here is the proof. Write ϕ=u+iv and B=(B1,B2). The real and imaginary parts are harmonic. The Itô formula therefore gives the two continuous local martingales
u(Bt​)−u(B0​)=∫0t​∇u(Bs​)⋅dBs​,v(Bt​)−v(B0​)=∫0t​∇v(Bs​)⋅dBs​,
(48)

(49)
Thus both coordinate quadratic variations equal At​ and their quadratic covariation is zero. After inverse time change, the covariance matrix of the two continuous local martingales is uI2​. Apply it after subtracting ϕ(B0​), and exhaust D by compact subsets. This proves the theorem, with killing at the transformed exit lifetime. The clock is the conformal Brownian clock; omitting it would generally give an incorrect speed.
