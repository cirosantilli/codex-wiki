An r-uniform hypergraph is strongly saturated with respect to a complete uniform hypergraph of order r+t if adding any missing edge creates a new complete uniform hypergraph on r+t vertices containing that edge. Equivalently, for every missing r-set e, there is an (r+t)-set T⊇e for which all other r-subsets are edges. Existing complete subhypergraphs are allowed in this strong-saturation definition.
We prove the bound by an exterior-power quotient bound for strong hypergraph saturation. Assume r,t≥1 and n≥r+t. Let V=Rn with basis e1​,…,en​. Choose distinct real numbers a1​,…,an​ and let U be the kernel of the t×n matrix whose column i is
(1,ai​,…,ait−1​)T.
(23)
Every t columns are linearly independent by the Vandermonde determinant, so dimU=n−t. Form the quotient vector space
W=⋀rV/⋀rU,dimW=(rn​)−(rn−t​).
(24)
For each r-set e={i1​<⋯<ir​}, let ve​ be the image in W of the exterior product ei1​​∧⋯∧eir​​. These images span W.
For an (r+t)-set T, let VT​ be its coordinate subspace. The matrix restricted to T has rank t, so U∩VT​ has dimension r. The wedge of a basis of this intersection is a nonzero vector
αT​=∑e∈T(r)​ce​ei1​​∧⋯∧eir​​in ⋀rU.
(25)
Every coefficient ce​ is nonzero. Indeed, projection from U∩VT​ onto the r coordinates in e is an isomorphism: a vector in its kernel would be supported on T∖e, where the t columns are independent, and hence would be zero. That projection's determinant is precisely the corresponding wedge coefficient. Thus, in the quotient vector space,
∑e∈T(r)​ce​ve​=0(ce​=0).
(26)
For a missing edge, choose its strong-saturation witness T. All other vectors in this relation come from present edges, so the missing edge's vector is in their span. Consequently the present-edge vectors already span all of W. Their number is at least its dimension:
∣E(H)∣≥(rn​)−(rn−t​).​
(27)
This is sharp. Take all r-sets that meet a fixed t-set D. A missing edge e is disjoint from D, and on e∪D every other r-set meets D; adding e completes the required clique. The number of edges is exactly the boxed difference. If r≤n<r+t, no missing edge could have a witness, so a strongly saturated hypergraph must be complete; the substantive construction above is for the usual range n≥r+t.
For the set-pair problem, write m=∣I∣ and choose xi​∈Ri​∩Si​. If i=j, the cross-disjointness implies that xi​ is in neither Rj​ nor Sj​. In particular, the witnesses are distinct. Let D={xi​:i∈I}; each Ri​ and Si​ contains exactly its own witness from D.
Choose two different indices i,j. The sets Ri​∖{xi​} and Sj​∖{xj​} are disjoint, lie outside D, and have sizes r−1 and s−1. Hence
n−m≥(r−1)+(s−1),∣I∣≤n−r−s+2.​
(28)
For equality, partition the ground set into disjoint sets P,Q,D with sizes r−1,s−1,n−r−s+2, respectively, and enumerate D as x1​,…,xm​. Set
Ri​=P∪{xi​},Si​=Q∪{xi​}.​
(29)
Then Ri​∩Sj​ is {xi​} for i=j and empty otherwise. This realizes equality whenever the bound permits m≥2. The argument is a diagonal-intersection set-pair bound, with no additional assumptions on intersections among the Ri​ or among the Si​.
