For an r-uniform set family F, its lower shadow is the set family of all (r−1)-sets contained in a member. Write a positive integer m in its unique binomial representation
m=(rar​​)+(r−1ar−1​​)+⋯+(jaj​​),ar​>ar−1​>⋯>aj​≥j≥1.
(1)
The Kruskal-Katona theorem states that
∣F∣=m⟹∣∂F∣≥(r−1ar​​)+(r−2ar−1​​)+⋯+(j−1aj​​).​
(2)
For m=0 the bound is zero. Equality is attained by the first m r-sets in colexicographic order. In that order A precedes B when the largest element of their symmetric difference belongs to B.
Here is a complete UV-compression proof of the Kruskal-Katona theorem. For disjoint equal-sized sets U,V, put C(A)=(A∖V)∪U when A contains every element of V and no element of U, and put C(A)=A otherwise. The UV-compression of a set family replaces an eligible A only if its image is absent. It preserves cardinality: a retained collision keeps both the original set and its already present image.
We first prove the required Shadow lemma for UV-compressions. Suppose that, for each x∈U, a y∈V can be chosen so the set family is fixed by CU∖{x},V∖{y}​. We claim
∂CU,V​(F)⊆CU,V​(∂F).
(3)
Consider a lower shadow member obtained by deleting z from a moved set A′=(A∖V)∪U. If z∈/U, it is the compressed image of A∖{z}, an old lower shadow member. If z=x∈U, the smaller compression of A produces A′∖{x}∪{y}, which is in F by the assumed invariance. Thus A′∖{x} already belongs to the old lower shadow; it is not eligible to move since it lacks x.
It remains to consider a lower shadow member B from a retained original member A=B∪{z}. If B is not eligible to move, it stays in the compressed lower shadow. If it is eligible, let B′=(B∖V)∪U. When z∈/U, retaining the eligible A means its image already belongs to F, and that image contains B′. When z=x∈U, the smaller compression of A belongs to F and equals B′∪{y}. In either case B′ is in the old lower shadow too. Consequently the collision rule retains B as well. This proves the claimed containment in every case. Since compression preserves cardinality, it cannot increase lower shadow size.
If F is not a colexicographic initial segment, an earlier absent set A and a later present set B give U=A∖B, V=B∖A with maxU<maxV, and a nontrivial UV-compression. Choose such a compression with ∣U∣ smallest. If ∣U∣>1, choose y∈V∖{maxV}. For every x∈U, the smaller compression still has its largest differing element on the V side and must fix F by minimality. If ∣U∣=1, the smaller compression is the identity. Thus the lower shadow argument applies.
Each nontrivial step strictly decreases the positive integer weight
w(F)=∑A∈F​∑i∈A​2i,
(4)
because the largest differing coordinate belongs to V. Iteration therefore terminates. A terminal set family is a colexicographic initial segment, since an earlier gap and a later member would otherwise provide another nontrivial compression. The final set family has m members and no larger lower shadow.
To compute its lower shadow, take ar​ maximal with (rar​​)≤m. All r-sets of [ar​] occur first. The next block consists of sets containing ar​+1 whose remaining (r−1)-set runs through the initial segment of length m−(rar​​). By Pascal's identity, the remainder is smaller than (r−1ar​​), so its next leading top index is strictly less than ar​. Repeating gives the stated unique binomial representation. The first block contributes all (r−1ar​​) (r−1)-sets in [ar​] to the lower shadow. Deleting ar​+1 from the next block contributes only sets already counted; deleting another coordinate contributes ar​+1 joined to the lower shadow of the smaller initial segment. This recursive decomposition gives exactly the displayed sum. The base r=1 has lower shadow {∅} for every nonempty set family. This completes the proof and the sharpness assertion.
Now let C be the set family of vertex sets of the graph's cliques of order four. Its twice-iterated lower shadow consists of edges of the graph. If there were at least sixteen such copies, choose a sixteen-member subfamily. The relevant binomial representation is
16=(46​)+(33​).
(5)
The proved theorem gives at least (36​)+(23​)=23 triples in its first lower shadow. Apply it again to any twenty-three of those triples, using 23=(36​)+(23​). Their edge lower shadow has at least
(26​)+(13​)=18
(6)
members, contradicting the fifteen available edges. Conversely, the complete graph K6​ has (26​)=15 edges and (46​)=15 copies of K4​. Therefore the maximum number of copies is exactly 15.
