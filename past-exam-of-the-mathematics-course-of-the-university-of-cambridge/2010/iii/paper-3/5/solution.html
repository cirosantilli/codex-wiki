Put D=Q/Z. The character module M∗=HomZ​(M,D) has the natural action
(aϕ)(m)=ϕ(am).​
(20)
Additivity is immediate, and a(bϕ)(m)=ϕ(bam)=ϕ(abm), using commutativity of A. The identity of A acts as the identity. We establish the extension and duality tools used in the four implications, so no flatness or injectivity criterion is assumed without proof.
An injective module E is one for which every homomorphism from a submodule U⊆V extends to V. The Baer criterion says it suffices to test the inclusions of ideals I⊆A. Necessity follows directly from the definition. For sufficiency, suppose every I→E extends to A. Given h:U→E, use Zorn's lemma to choose a maximal extension (W,hW​) inside V; extensions along a chain have a well-defined union. If v∈V∖W, let
I={a∈A:av∈W},θ(a)=hW​(av).
(21)
This is an ideal and an A-linear map. Its extension to A has the form a↦ae for some e∈E. Then
h′(w+av)=hW​(w)+ae
(22)
is well-defined: if two expressions represent the same element, their coefficient difference lies in I, where the two prescribed values agree. It extends hW​ to the strictly larger module W+Av, a contradiction. Thus W=V, proving the Baer criterion for arbitrary modules and ideals.
The group D is divisible. A homomorphism nZ→D, for n>0, extends to Z by choosing d∈D with nd equal to its value at n; the zero ideal causes no restriction. The proved Baer criterion over Z therefore makes D injective. It is also a cogenerator: a nonzero element x of an abelian group generates either a finite cyclic group of order n>1, which maps x to 1/n+Z, or an infinite cyclic group, which maps x to 1/2+Z. Injectivity extends this character to the whole group. Thus every nonzero element is detected by an additive character.
It follows that a group homomorphism u:T→U is injective exactly when
u∗:HomZ​(U,D)⟶HomZ​(T,D)
(23)
is surjective. For an injection, extension into the injective group D proves surjectivity. Conversely, a character nonzero on an element of keru cannot be the pullback of a character on U. This is the detection property of the injective cogenerator of abelian groups.
Finally, for any A-module N there is a natural character form of the Tensor-hom adjunction:
HomA​(N,M∗)≃HomZ​(N⊗A​M,D).​
(24)
An A-linear map h gives the pairing (n,m)↦h(n)(m). It is balanced because h(an)(m)=(ah(n))(m)=h(n)(am). Conversely a balanced additive pairing determines h by this formula. The universal property of the tensor product of modules makes these constructions mutually inverse, and compatible with restriction maps.
