Let K=Q(A) be the fraction field of the integral domain A. The Picard group of a ring consists of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective modules that have rank one after localization at every prime ideal. Its product is induced by the tensor product of modules, its identity is [A], and the inverse of [L] is [L∨], where L∨=HomA​(L,A). Evaluation L⊗A​L∨→A is an isomorphism: after localization it is evaluation on a free rank-one module, and a map whose kernel and cokernel vanish at every prime is an isomorphism.
An invertible fractional ideal is a nonzero finitely generated A-submodule I⊆K admitting another fractional ideal J with IJ=A. The Cartier divisors of SpecA can be represented by these ideals. Concretely a Cartier divisor has local nonzero rational equations fi​ on a cover by distinguished open sets, with fi​/fj​ a regular unit on each overlap. We use the convention assigning to it O(−D), whose local fractional ideals are fi​Asi​​. Their unit ratios make these modules agree on overlaps. Thus the Cartier divisor group Cart(A) is realized by invertible fractional ideals with multiplication. The identity is A and a principal Cartier divisor of f∈K× corresponds to fA.

Conversely, suppose L is a finitely generated projective module of rank one. It is a direct summand of a finite free module, so it is torsion-free and injects into L⊗A​K. The latter is a one-dimensional K-space. Choose a basis to identify it with K, and let I be the image of L. Its finitely many generators have a common nonzero denominator, making I a fractional ideal. Every A-linear map I→A extends to a K-linear map K→K, which is multiplication by some v∈K. Thus I∨ identifies with I−1. Evaluation is an isomorphism locally, so its image ideal II−1 localizes to Ap​ at every prime. A proper ideal would be contained in a maximal ideal, contradicting that localization; hence II−1=A. This proves that every Picard group class has an invertible fractional ideal representative, and also that locally principal fractional ideals on a finite open cover are invertible.
For completeness, these local tests do detect a zero module without a finite-generation assumption. If 0=x∈N, its annihilator is proper; choose a maximal ideal containing it. Then x/1 is nonzero in that localization, since any denominator killing x would belong to its annihilator. Applying this observation to a kernel and cokernel justifies the local isomorphism tests above.
The natural maps in the Cartier divisor exact sequence for an integral domain are now
A×↪K×,K×⟶Cart(A),f⟼fA,Cart(A)⟶Pic(A),I⟼[I].

Next [I]=[A] precisely when some isomorphism A→I sends 1 to an element f∈K× generating I, so I=fA. Finally the construction from L proves surjectivity onto the Picard group. We have therefore checked exactness at every term:
1⟶A×⟶K×⟶Cart(A)⟶Pic(A)⟶0.​
