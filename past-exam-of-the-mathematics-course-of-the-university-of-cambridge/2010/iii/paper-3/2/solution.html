The spectrum of a ring A is the set of its proper prime ideals. For an ideal I, set V(I)={p:I⊆p}. These are the closed sets of the Zariski topology. They satisfy
V(0)=SpecA,V(A)=∅,⋂λ​V(Iλ​)=V(∑λ​Iλ​),V(I)∪V(J)=V(IJ).
(6)
The union identity follows because a prime ideal containing IJ must contain I or J: otherwise choose a∈I∖p and b∈J∖p, contradicting ab∈p. These identities verify the closed-set axioms. The maximal spectrum has the subspace topology, whose closed sets are V(I)∩MaxSpecA.
For a finitely generated C-algebra, the needed form of the Hilbert Nullstellensatz is
I​=m⊇Im maximal​⋂​m.​
(7)
Here an empty intersection means A. We prove this formula rather than assume the desired correspondence.
First establish the residue-field fact. If a field L is a finitely generated algebra over C, choose a maximal algebraically independent subset t1​,…,tr​ of a finite generating set, with remaining generators u1​,…,us​. Each uj​ is algebraic over C(t1​,…,tr​). Clearing the finitely many denominators in their monic equations gives a nonzero polynomial d such that each uj​ is integral over
R=C[t1​,…,tr​,1/d].
(8)
Since L is a field, it contains 1/d, and L=R[u1​,…,us​] is an integral extension of R. If a field is integral over a domain, the domain is itself a field: for 0=a∈R, an integral equation for a−1, multiplied by an−1, expresses a−1 in R.
But if r>0, choose c∈C for which t1​−c does not divide d. Such a choice exists because a nonzero polynomial has only finitely many distinct factors t1​−c. The element t1​−c is a nonunit even after inverting d: an inverse would imply that t1​−c divides a power of d. Thus R would not be a field, a contradiction. Hence r=0, and L is algebraic over the algebraically closed field C, forcing L=C. This proves the Zariski lemma in the form used here.
Now if f∈/I​, the localization of a ring B=(A/I)[1/f] is nonzero: vanishing would mean fn∈I for some n. It is still a finitely generated algebra over C, since one need only adjoin an inverse of f. Choose a maximal ideal n of B. By the preceding argument, B/n=C. The composite A→B→C is surjective because it contains the constant scalars. Its kernel is consequently a maximal ideal m of A, contains I, and does not contain f, whose image is invertible. This proves that the displayed intersection is contained in I​. The reverse inclusion follows because every maximal ideal is prime and contains the radical of an ideal whenever it contains that ideal.
For a closed subset Z⊆MaxSpecA, put I(Z)=⋂m∈Z​m. It is a radical ideal. The formulas now show
I⟼V(I)∩MaxSpecA,Z⟼I(Z)
(9)
are inverse maps on radical ideals and closed sets. Indeed the first followed by the second gives I​=I, while if Z=V(J)∩MaxSpecA, the second gives J​ and V(J​)=V(J). Thus the correspondence is a bijection reversing inclusion. This is the relation between radical ideals and closed subsets of the maximal spectrum.
For A=C[x], the maximal ideals are mc​=(x−c), for c∈C. Every nonzero proper radical ideal is generated by a squarefree polynomial, and hence has the form
IS​=(∏c∈S​(x−c))
(10)
for a finite nonempty subset S⊂C. The associated closed set is {mc​:c∈S}. A repeated factor of a polynomial does not change its zero set, but passing to its radical ideal removes that repeated factor. The two endpoint cases are
(0)⟷C,(1)⟷∅,IS​⟷S for finite S.​
(11)
The empty-product convention makes I∅​=(1). Under c↦mc​, the closed sets are exactly the finite subsets and the whole affine line.
