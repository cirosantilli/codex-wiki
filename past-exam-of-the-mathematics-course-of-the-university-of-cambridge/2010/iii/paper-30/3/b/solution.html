For the link-route incidence matrix A, the reduced-load approximation and Erlang loss formula give
Bj​=E(aj​,Cj​),aj​=r∑​Ajr​νr​k=j∏​(1−Bk​)Akr​.​
(17)
Introduce yj​=−log(1−Bj​) and accepted route flows xr​=νr​e−(ATy)r​. Multiplying the reduced load by the link's own acceptance probability gives
aj​(1−Bj​)=∑r​Ajr​xr​=U(yj​,Cj​).
(18)
These are precisely the first-order optimality conditions for the convex potential for the Erlang fixed point, since
∂yj​∂F​=−∑r​Ajr​νr​e−(ATy)r​+U(yj​,Cj​).
(19)
Each exponential term is a convex function, and the integral of the strictly increasing function U(⋅,Cj​) is a strictly convex function. Hence F is a strictly convex function. Moreover U(z,Cj​)→Cj​>0 as z→∞, by the saturation of the carried load of an Erlang loss resource. Its integrals tend to infinity at least linearly, so F is a coercive function on the nonnegative orthant and has a unique minimizer.
At yj​=0, U(0,Cj​)=0. If link j is used by any positive-rate route, then ∂j​F<0, excluding a boundary minimizer there. An unused link instead minimizes at yj​=0 and has zero derivative. Thus the minimizer satisfies the displayed flow equations for every link. Conversely, those equations imply ∇F=0 and therefore minimize the convex potential for the Erlang fixed point. Finally U(y,C)=a[1−E(a,C)] with y=−log(1−E(a,C)) converts the flow equations back to Bj​=E(aj​,Cj​). The Erlang fixed point approximation has exactly one solution under fixed routing.
