Let K=Q(α) with α an algebraic integer, let m∈Z[X] be its monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element, and write A=Z[α]. The Kummer-Dedekind theorem applies to a rational prime p not dividing the finite index [OK​:A]. If
m=∏j=1r​gjej​​in Fp​[X]
(1)
with distinct monic irreducible polynomials gj​, choose monic integer lifts g​j​. Then the distinct prime ideals above p are
pj​=(p,g​j​(α)),pOK​=j=1∏r​pjej​​,[OK​/pj​:Fp​]=deggj​​.
(2)
In particular, p is unramified precisely when m has no repeated irreducible factor. The exclusion of primes dividing the index is essential.
Here is the quotient-ring proof of the Kummer-Dedekind theorem. The finite group OK​/A has order coprime to p. Localizing at the integers coprime to p therefore gives A⊗Z(p)​=OK​⊗Z(p)​, and reduction yields
OK​/pOK​≃A/pA≃Fp​[X]/(m).
(3)
The second isomorphism follows from A≃Z[X]/(m). The Chinese remainder theorem gives
OK​/pOK​≃∏j​Fp​[X]/(gjej​​).
(4)
Each factor is a local ring with unique maximal ideal generated by gj​ and residue field Fp​[X]/(gj​). Pulling its maximal ideal back to OK​ gives exactly pj​; these exhaust the primes above p. This proves the asserted residue degrees and their independence of the chosen lifts.
It remains to prove the exponents rather than simply identify the primes. Unique prime ideal factorization in the Dedekind domain OK​ writes (p)=∏j​pjbj​​. At pj​, its discrete valuation ring has p=uπbj​, with u a unit. Thus the maximal ideal of OK,pj​​/pOK,pj​​ has nilpotence index of an ideal bj​: its bj​th power is zero and its (bj​−1)st is nonzero. In Fp​[X]/(gjej​​) this index is ej​, since gjej​​=0 but gjej​−1​=0. The displayed product isomorphism identifies these local factors, hence bj​=ej​. This completes the theorem's proof. The same local argument works over a base number field whenever OK​[α] equals the full relative ring of integers after localizing at the chosen base prime.
For the required cyclotomic field, the allowed ring of integers formula makes the index one. Its cyclotomic polynomial is
Φ80​(X)=X8+1X40+1​=X32−X24+X16−X8+1.
(5)
For p∈/{2,5}, the polynomial X80−1 has derivative 80X79 coprime to it modulo p. Its divisor Φ80​ is therefore squarefree, so the Kummer-Dedekind theorem gives no ramification.
The cyclotomic reduction at a conductor prime is explicit at the remaining two primes:
Φ80​=(X4+X3+X2+X+1)8in F2​[X],
(6)
Φ80​=(X8+1)4=(X4+2)4(X4+3)4in F5​[X].
(7)
The quartic over F2​ has no linear factor. Its only possible irreducible quadratic divisor would be X2+X+1, whose roots have order three, whereas its own roots have order five. Hence it is irreducible. The two quartics over F5​ are also irreducible: their roots have multiplicative order sixteen because their fourth powers are 2 or 3, both of order four; the finite-field Frobenius automorphism has orbit length four on an element of order sixteen, since 54≡1(mod16) and neither 5 nor 52 is 1 modulo sixteen.
Consequently the prime ideal factorization above 2 has one prime with ramification index eight and residue degree four. Above 5 it has two primes, each with ramification index four and residue degree four. In particular,
The ramified rational primes are exactly 2 and 5.​
(8)

Write G=Gal(F/K) and
Dq​={σ∈G:σq=q}.
(9)
Every member of this decomposition group preserves OF​ and q, so reduction defines a homomorphism
Dq​⟶Gal(kq​/kp​).
(10)
Its kernel is the inertia group. We prove surjectivity of decomposition group reduction without presupposing a lift of Frobenius.
If the two residue fields coincide, surjectivity is immediate. Otherwise choose a nonzero a∈kq​ generating it over kp​; a generator exists by the primitive element theorem for finite fields. Use the Chinese remainder theorem in OF​ to choose a whose residue is a at q and zero at every other prime above p. Consider
P(T)=∏σ∈G​(T−σa).
(11)
Its coefficients are integral and fixed by G, hence lie in OK​: an element of K integral over OK​ belongs to this integrally closed ring of integers.
Reduce at q. When σ∈/Dq​, the chosen residue of a at σ−1q is zero, so σa reduces to zero at q. When σ∈Dq​, its residue is σ(a), which is nonzero. Therefore
P(T)=T∣G∣−∣Dq​∣∏σ∈Dq​​(T−σ(a)).
(12)
Although this product is written in kq​[T], its coefficients belong to kp​. Put q=∣kp​∣. The finite-field Frobenius automorphism b↦bq therefore permutes its nonzero roots. In particular, aq is σ(a) for some σ∈Dq​. Since a generates kq​, that σ agrees with Frobenius on the whole field. The Galois group of a finite field extension is cyclic and generated by Frobenius, so its generator lies in the image. Thus
Dq​↠Gal(kq​/kp​),Dq​/Iq​≃Gal(kq​/kp​)​.

Put a=32​ and ζ=ζ3​. The polynomial X3−2 is irreducible over Q by the Eisenstein criterion at 2, so [Q(a):Q]=3. This field is real and cannot contain the nonreal ζ. Adjoining ζ gives a quadratic extension and hence [F:Q]=6. Since F is the splitting field of X3−2, its Galois group acts faithfully on a,ζa,ζ2a and has order six. Consequently
Gal(F/Q)≃S3​.
(14)
For a prime q over a rational prime p, its absolute residue degree is the order of the cyclic quotient group Dq​/Iq​ from part (i). If that degree were six, then Dq​, a subgroup of S3​, would have order at least six, so Dq​=S3​ and Iq​=1. This would make S3​ cyclic, a contradiction. The argument applies also at ramified primes. Thus
No prime of F has absolute residue degree 6.​
(15)
For the second request printed with the same subpart label, work over E=Q(ζ). The Galois group of F/E is cyclic of order three, with generator σ(a)=ζa and σ(ζ)=ζ. The ideal norm of (ζ+3) is seven, since
(ζ+3)(ζ2+3)=7.
(16)
In its residue field we have ζ≡−3≡4(modp1​). The polynomial X3−2 has no root in F7​, because its cube residues are 0,1,6. It is therefore irreducible there. Its discriminant −108 is a unit at p1​. To check the local index condition, the trace pairing matrix of 1,a,a2 has this unit determinant. For any integral element b of F, its traces against these three basis elements are integral; solving with this invertible matrix shows that its basis coefficients are integral locally. Thus the relative ring of integers equals OE​[a] at p1​. The relative Kummer-Dedekind theorem gives one unramified prime above p1​, of residue degree three.
Use arithmetic Frobenius, characterized by b↦b7 on this residue field. As a3=2,
a7=4a≡ζa(modq).
(17)
The three possible images a,ζa,ζ2a remain distinct modulo q, since a is nonzero and 1,4,2 are distinct modulo seven. This congruence therefore identifies the actual automorphism uniquely:
Frobp1​​(a)=ζ3​a,Frobp1​​(ζ3​)=ζ3​.​
(18)
The geometric Frobenius convention would give its inverse. Since F/E is abelian, the arithmetic element is independent of the choice of prime above p1​.

The three quadratic subfields are Q(−2​), Q(−3​) and Q(6​), with respective fundamental discriminants −8,−3,24. Their associated quadratic Dirichlet characters are the Kronecker symbols
χ−8​(n)=(n−8​),χ−3​(n)=(n−3​),χ24​(n)=(n24​).
(19)
The regular representation of C2​×C2​ is the direct sum of the trivial character and these three nontrivial characters. At an unramified prime the character is the sign by which Frobenius automorphism acts on the square root defining its quadratic subfield; at a ramified prime the linear character has no inertia group invariants. The local factor of an Artin L-function consequently gives
L(ρ,s)=ζ(s)L(χ−8​,s)L(χ−3​,s)L(χ24​,s).
(20)
The zero values of a quadratic Dirichlet character at conductor primes mean that its local factor is one, not that the whole Artin L-function vanishes there.
Only the rational primes 2,3,5,7 can contribute to coefficients with index at most ten. Their character values are
p2357​χ−8​(p)01−1−1​χ−3​(p)−10−11​χ24​(p)001−1​Lp​(ρ,s) with T=p−s(1−T2)−1(1−T)−2(1−T2)−2(1−T2)−2​​

For the odd unramified primes these entries follow by checking squares: modulo five, both −8 and −3 are the nonsquare 2, while 24 is the square 4; modulo seven they are respectively 6,4,3. At 3, −8≡1 is a square. At 2, the −3 character is −1 because the integral quadratic generator has polynomial X2+X+1, irreducible modulo two. The other entries at 2 and 3 are zero exactly where the corresponding fundamental discriminant is divisible by that prime. This verifies every local factor, including the ramified ones.
The Euler product makes an​ a multiplicative arithmetic function. Expanding the first two factors gives
(1−T2)−1=1+T2+T4+⋯,(1−T)−2=1+2T+3T2+⋯.
(22)
Therefore a2​=0, a4​=1, a8​=0, a3​=2 and a9​=3. Both factors for 5 and 7 have zero coefficient of T, so a5​=a7​=0. Multiplicativity gives a6​=a2​a3​=0 and a10​=a2​a5​=0, while a1​=1. The answer is
(a1​,…,a10​)=(1,0,2,1,0,0,0,0,3,0)​.
(23)
As an arithmetic check, the regular representation Artin L-function is the Dedekind zeta function of F. The local factors say there is one prime of norm four above two and two primes of norm three above three. Their powers give respectively one ideal of norm four and three ideals of norm nine, matching the nonzero coefficients above.

We use the Dirichlet density form of the Chebotarev density theorem, the version directly proved by the permitted information at s=1. If L/K is a finite Galois extension of number fields with group G and C is a conjugacy class, let SC​ be the unramified prime ideals of K whose Frobenius conjugacy class is C. Then
δ(SC​):=s↓1lim​log(1/(s−1))p∈SC​∑​(Np)−s​=∣G∣∣C∣​​.
(24)
The same conclusion holds for a union of conjugacy classes, replacing ∣C∣ by the size of that union. The denominator may equivalently be the sum over all primes of K. Here and below s>1 is real. This specifies the density being proved; an unweighted prime-counting asymptotic is a stronger formulation and is not being inferred merely from a limit at one.
First establish the necessary pole for a general base field. The Dedekind zeta function as a permutation Artin L-function gives it using exactly the allowed assumptions. Take a finite normal closure E of K/Q, and let W be the permutation representation on HomQ​(K,E). Its trivial constituent has multiplicity one: an invariant function on the transitive set of embeddings is constant. At a rational prime, a basis of WI consists of the sums over inertia orbits of embeddings. Frobenius automorphism permutes these orbit sums. Within the decomposition-group orbit belonging to a prime p of K, the resulting cycle has length fp​, because the residue embeddings are permuted cyclically by finite-field Frobenius. A cycle of length f has determinant det(1−Tρ(Frob))=1−Tf. Consequently the local factor of an Artin L-function gives
LQ​(W,s)=∏p​∏p∣p​(1−p−fp​s)−1=ζK​(s),
(25)
including at ramified primes. Decomposing W=1⊕⨁ψ=1​mψ​ψ gives
ζK​(s)=ζ(s)∏ψ=1​LQ​(ψ,s)mψ​.
(26)
All factors after ζ are holomorphic and nonzero at one by the allowed hypothesis. Thus ζK​ has a simple pole there and
logζK​(s)=logs−11​+O(1).
(27)
This argument also explains why the trivial relative Artin L-function is ζK​, rather than always the Riemann zeta function.
Now let χ be an irreducible character of G, of degree dχ​. At an unramified prime the Euler product has local factor det(1−ρχ​(gp​)(Np)−s)−1, where gp​ is a Frobenius representative. The eigenvalues of ρχ​(g) are roots of unity, since g has finite order. Expanding the logarithm for s>1 therefore gives
logLK​(χ,s)=∑p unramified​∑m≥1​m(Np)msχ(gpm​)​+Rχ​(s),
(28)
where the finitely many ramified local factors contribute a function bounded as s↓1. They are nonzero and finite near one because their eigenvalues have modulus one while (Np)−1<1.
The terms m≥2 are uniformly bounded. Indeed ∣χ(g)∣≤dχ​, and for Np≥2 and s≥1,
∑m≥2​m(Np)−ms​≤∑m≥2​(Np)−m≤2(Np)−2.
(29)
There are at most [K:Q] primes of K above each rational prime and each has norm at least that prime. Hence ∑p​(Np)−2<∞. Isolating m=1 proves the character prime sums in the Chebotarev proof identity
∑p unramified​χ(gp​)(Np)−s=logLK​(χ,s)+O(1).
(30)
For nontrivial χ, the allowed holomorphy and nonvanishing at one give a local holomorphic logarithm and a bounded right side. The logarithm specified by the Euler series differs from that local branch by a constant multiple of 2πi on the real interval near one, so it too is bounded. For the trivial character, the right side is log(1/(s−1))+O(1) by the pole of ζK​. Thus
∑p unramified​χ(gp​)(Np)−s={log(1/(s−1))+O(1),O(1),​χ=1,χ=1.​
(31)
Expand the conjugacy-class indicator by character orthogonality. For c∈C,
1C​(g)=∣G∣∣C∣​∑χ∈G​χ(c)​χ(g).
(32)
To see the coefficient, take its inner product with χ: ∣G∣−1∑g∈C​χ(g)​=∣C∣χ(c)​/∣G∣. The irreducible characters form an orthonormal basis of class functions, so this is the full expansion. Substituting the prime-sum estimates leaves only the trivial character's logarithmic term:
∑p∈SC​​(Np)−s=∣G∣∣C∣​logs−11​+O(1).
(33)
Division by the diverging logarithm proves the theorem. The same trivial-character calculation shows that all primes of K have density one; adding or removing finitely many primes does not change a Dirichlet density.
There is a necessary qualification for the final polynomial application: the printed claim is false for degree one. For example, X is monic and irreducible in Z[X], yet has the root zero modulo every prime. We prove the intended assertion for degree n≥2.
Let L be the splitting field of the monic irreducible polynomial over Q, and let Ω be its n roots. The Galois group G acts transitively on Ω. There is a derangement in a transitive group action: count the pairs (g,α) satisfying gα=α. The orbit-stabilizer theorem gives
∑g∈G​∣FixΩ​(g)∣=∑α∈Ω​∣Gα​∣=nn∣G∣​=∣G∣.
(34)
If every g fixed at least one root, the identity, which fixes n≥2, would make this sum at least ∣G∣+n−1, a contradiction. Therefore some g fixes no root, and neither does any conjugate of g.
For completeness, relate this group action to reduction without assuming the desired splitting result. All roots are algebraic integers, since the polynomial is monic. Exclude the finitely many primes ramifying in L or dividing the nonzero polynomial discriminant. At a prime q over any remaining p, the roots reduce to distinct elements of kq​ and give all roots of the reduced polynomial. Arithmetic Frobenius permutes them by b↦bp. A reduced root belongs to Fp​ exactly when it is fixed by this map. Because reduction is injective on the original root set, this happens exactly when the Frobenius permutation fixes an original root. This proves the Frobenius permutation and roots modulo a prime criterion.
The Chebotarev density theorem assigns the conjugacy class of our fixed-point-free g the positive Dirichlet density ∣Cg​∣/∣G∣. All but finitely many of these primes have rootless reduction, so there are infinitely many. More precisely, applying the same argument to the union of all fixed-point-free classes gives
δ{p:fmodp has no root in Fp​}=∣G∣∣{g∈G:FixΩ​(g)=∅}∣​>0(n≥2).​
(35)
