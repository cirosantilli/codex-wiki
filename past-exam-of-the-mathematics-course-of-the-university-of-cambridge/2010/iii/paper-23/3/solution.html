The map
[i](x,y)=(−x,iy)
(16)
is an automorphism of the elliptic curve over K, fixes its identity point, and squares to [−1]. For the invariant differential on an elliptic curve ω=dx/(2y), it satisfies [i]∗ω=iω. Thus Z[i]⊆EndK​(E), with the indicated embedding of K.
Under analytic complex uniformization of an elliptic curve E(C)≃C/L, this automorphism acts as multiplication by i, so L is stable under Z[i]. It is a rank-one torsion-free module over the Gaussian integers. Since that ring is a principal ideal domain, L=ΩZ[i]. Every analytic endomorphism is multiplication by an a∈C with aL⊆L, which here says exactly a∈Z[i]. Therefore the geometric endomorphism ring of an elliptic curve is Z[i], and all its elements are generated over Z by the displayed K-defined automorphism. Thus EndK​(E)=Z[i].
If an endomorphism is defined over Q, its pullback on the rational differential ω has scalar in Q. The differential action is injective in characteristic zero, because a nonzero isogeny of elliptic curves is separable and has nonzero differential. Its scalar must therefore lie in Q∩Z[i]=Z. Conversely integer multiplication is defined over Q. Hence
EndQ​(E)=Z,EndQ(i)​(E)=Z[i].​
(17)
Put λ=1+i. The valuations of the differences of the three nonidentity fourth roots of unity from 1 are
vλ​(−1−1)=2,vλ​(i−1)=1,vλ​(−i−1)=1.
(18)
For instance 2=−iλ2, and the other two differences are associates of λ. None is divisible by λ3. The group (Z[i]/λ3)× has 8−4=4 elements, so the four units map bijectively to it. Every ideal prime to λ consequently has a unique primary Gaussian integer generator.
The Grössencharacter of the square-lattice elliptic curve is explicitly
ψE​(a)=α,(α)=a,α≡1(modλ3),f=λ3.​
(19)
For integral ideals prime to 2, the generator is an odd Gaussian integer; extend multiplicatively to fractional ideals prime to 2. Products of primary generators are primary, so this is a Hecke character with ideal infinity type (1,0): on a principal ideal with generator a≡1(modλ3), its value is a. Its conductor divides λ3, and cannot divide λ2, since −1≡1(modλ2) would force ψE​((−1))=−1, whereas the unit ideal has value 1. Thus the displayed conductor is exact.
We next verify directly that this character gives the Frobenius values of this particular curve, fixing the unit ambiguity. The point
P=(i,1−i)∈E(K)
(20)
satisfies, by the chord-and-tangent addition law,
[λ]P=(1,0),[λ]2P=(0,0),[λ]3P=O.
(21)
For the first addition, [i]P=(−i,1+i) and the chord has slope −1. Since P is killed by λ3 but not by λ2, it generates the eight-element Z[i]-module E[λ3]≃Z[i]/λ3. Thus all this torsion is rational over K.
The model has discriminant 64, so it has good reduction at every γ=(λ). First suppose γ lies over a split rational prime p. Then its residue field is Fp​, p≥5, and π=ψE​(γ) has norm p. Reduction of [π] still has degree p and has zero differential, since its differential scalar π belongs to γ. It is therefore purely inseparable of degree p. Factoring out the Frobenius isogeny gives
[π]​=u∘Frobp​
(22)
for an automorphism u of the reduction. In characteristic at least five, the automorphisms of this model fixing O are the four Gaussian units: comparing coefficients in (x,y)↦(v2x,v3y) gives v4=1. Prime-to-p torsion reduces injectively. Both [π] and Frobp​ fix the reduction of E[λ3], the former by the primary congruence and the latter by its rationality over the residue field. Thus u fixes this entire module. The unit congruences proved above force u=1.
For an inert rational prime p≡3(mod4), we have γ=(p), Nγ=p2, and its primary generator is π=−p, because 4∣p+1 implies λ3∣(−p−1). To determine Frobenius without excluding p=3, write
ap​=−∑x∈Fp​​(px3−x​).
(23)
Replacing x by −x changes each nonzero quadratic-character value to its negative, because −1 is a nonsquare. Hence ap​=0. The Frobenius characteristic equation Frobp2​−[ap​]Frobp​+[p]=0 gives Frobp2​=[−p]. This proves the asserted Frobenius value at inert primes as well, and identifies the displayed Hecke character with that of E.
Finally, the formal parameter t=−x/y is defined over the local ring at γ. Since the reduced endomorphism is the q-power Frobenius isogeny, with q=Nγ, its pullback sends t to (−x/y)q=tq. Passing to the completion at the identity gives the equality of formal series
[π](t)≡tNγ(modγ).​
(24)
