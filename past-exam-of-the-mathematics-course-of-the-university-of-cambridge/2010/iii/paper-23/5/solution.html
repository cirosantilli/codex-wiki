A one-variable main conjecture for CM elliptic curves compares a torsion arithmetic Iwasawa module with a measure interpolating special Hecke L-function values. The classical bounded-measure formulation uses a prime of good reduction which splits in the CM field, hence gives ordinary reduction. We first describe the one-prime division-tower formulation, including the analytic construction, and then explain its cyclotomic Selmer counterpart and the change at supersingular primes.
For a precise basic setting, take E/K with complex multiplication by OK​, K of class number one, and a split prime p>3 of good reduction, prime to the Hecke conductor f. Write pOK​=ppˉ​, choose the p-adic embedding corresponding to p, and let π=ψE​(p). Its formal group of an elliptic curve has height one, with [π](t)≡tp(modp). The one-prime division tower
F∞​=K(E[p∞]),G=Gal(F∞​/K)=Δ×Γ
(32)
has Δ of order prime to p and Γ≃Zp​. Its CM character κ:G→Zp×​ describes the action on the prime-primary Tate module. Let F=F∞Γ​ and Λ=I[[Γ]]≃I[[T]]. We extend coefficients to the complete unramified ring I to include character values and a CM period.
Let X be the Galois group over F∞​ of the maximal abelian pro-p extension unramified outside primes above p. Let A be the analogous everywhere-unramified group, equivalently the norm inverse limit of the p-parts of the ideal class groups. Write U for the norm limit of principal local units at p, E for the closures of global units in U, and C for the closures of elliptic units. Roots of unity and finite initial-layer errors are retained when formulating integral statements; after passage to a finite-character component they do not change a characteristic ideal.
The class-field unit sequence, obtained by taking norm limits in global Artin reciprocity, gives
0⟶E/C⟶U/C⟶X⟶A⟶0.
(33)
In this ordinary one-prime tower, the unit and global-unit terms have the same Iwasawa rank, and the displayed quotients and X,A are torsion on their character components. The equality
charΛ​(E/C)χ​=charΛ​Aχ​
(34)
is the global-units/class-groups form of the CM main theorem. Multiplicativity of characteristic ideals in the sequence then gives
charΛ​Xχ​=charΛ​(U/C)χ​.
(35)
The analytic construction must identify the latter ideal with a genuinely interpolating p-adic L-function; it is not enough to rename an arbitrary characteristic power series as an L-function.
Here is that construction. For an auxiliary ideal a prime to 6pf, a suitably normalized rational elliptic function has divisor a multiple of
Na(O)−∑P∈E[a]​(P).
(36)
Translate by a generator of the conductor torsion and take the product over its ray-class orbit. This produces a rational function fa​(t) defined over K which is a unit series at the identity. Evaluating it on compatible pn-division points gives elliptic units. The reason for the norm relations is geometric: multiplication by π groups the translated zeros and poles into fibers, so the product over E[p] has the divisor of fa​([π]t). The normalization fixes the constant, giving the local identity
∏Q∈E[p]​fa​(t+E​Q)=fa​([π]t).
(37)
This is also the norm relation of the Coleman power series for a CM formal group interpolating the resulting local units.
An integral logarithmic correction makes the passage from those units to a bounded measure explicit. In the chosen split completion Kp​=Qp​, put
Lf​(t)=logf(t)−p1​logf([π]t)=p1​logf([π]t)f(t)p​.
(38)
Since [π]t≡tp(modp) and the coefficients of f reduce to Fp​, the ratio is in 1+pZp​[[t]]. For p>2, its logarithm divided by p is integral. Constants are treated using the usual p-adic logarithm on units. This proves the integrality needed for the Frobenius-corrected logarithm; applying an uncorrected logarithm alone would not prove boundedness.
Choose an isomorphism η:Gm​→E over I, and normalize the CM period by
logE​(η(T))=Ωp​log(1+T).
(39)
Then Af​(T)=Lf​(η(T))∈I[[T]] is the Amice transform of a unique I-valued p-adic measure on Zp​. The norm relation above gives
∑ζp=1​Af​(ζ(1+T)−1)=0:
(40)
the first logarithmic sum is logf([π]t) and the second is the same sum, because [π]Q=0 and there are p points. On the measure side the sum selects pZp​ and multiplies by p, so this identity says that the measure is supported on Zp×​. Transport it to G by the CM character and project to the desired component.
The moments can now be calculated rather than postulated. With D=(1+T)d/dT,
∫xkdμf​(x)=DkAf​(0)=Ωpk​(1−pπk​)dzkdk​logf(expE​z)​z=0​(k≥1).
(41)
The factor πk appears because [π] is multiplication by π in the additive formal-logarithm coordinate. The remaining derivative is a Coates–Wiles homomorphism. The complex product for the elliptic function makes it a sum of shifted inverse powers of lattice elements. Grouping these lattice elements by ray-class ideals identifies that sum with (k−1)!Lf​(ψˉ​Ek​,k)/Ω∞k​, multiplied by the auxiliary factor Na−ψE​(a)k. Here Lf​ omits primes dividing the original conductor, including when the conductor of the power character becomes smaller.
Normalize the rational functions and matching complex and p-adic periods consistently, and remove the auxiliary factors by the distribution relations. They are evaluations of Na−[a] in the Iwasawa algebra. On each finite-character component an auxiliary ideal can be chosen making this element a unit: its residues at p and pˉ​ can be prescribed independently by the Chinese remainder theorem. The resulting integral measure μE​ satisfies, on its branch and with k in the corresponding residue class,
Ωp−k​∫G​κ(g)kdμE​(g)=(k−1)!(1−pψE​(p)k​)Ω∞k​Lf​(ψˉ​Ek​,k)​.​
(42)
The exact normalization of periods matters for this equality; multiplication by an integral unit does not affect the ideal in the main conjecture. Finite-order twists are obtained by inserting a character in the ray-class sums, with its local Gauss sum. The lattice calculation proves algebraicity of the normalized complex values, while the integral series construction proves their p-adic interpolation. These are different steps.
The Coleman measure map identifies Uχ​ with a rank-one Iwasawa algebra lattice, up to finite errors, and takes Cχ​ to the ideal generated by the corresponding power series Lχ​. The conjecture therefore has the concrete form
charI[[Γ]]​Xχ​=(Lχ​).​
(43)
Its proof uses the Euler system of elliptic units. Adjoining auxiliary ray moduli supplies norm relations with factors 1−Frobq−1​. Derivative operators in the auxiliary cyclic groups produce Kolyvagin classes; their controlled valuations relate a new auxiliary prime to its ideal class. Chebotarev density theorem permits the required Frobenius choices. Successively choosing these primes bounds the class-group elementary divisors by the elliptic-unit index, giving the difficult characteristic-ideal divisibility. The opposite comparison comes from the class-number/unit-index formula and passage through the tower. Their equality, combined with the exact sequence above and the logarithmic computation, is the proof mechanism. The ordinary split-prime setting of this formulation is specified in Rubin's one-variable main-conjecture paper.
For completeness, the familiar cyclotomic formulation concerns a different tower. If E/Q has complex multiplication and good ordinary reduction at p, put Γcyc​=Gal(Q∞​/Q) and
XE​=Homcont​(Selp∞​(E/Q∞​),Qp​/Zp​).
(44)
For a finite layer L, its Selmer group of an elliptic curve is
ker(H1(GS​(L),E[p∞])⟶∏v∈S​im(E(Lv​)⊗Qp​/Zp​)H1(Lv​,E[p∞])​),
(45)
where S contains the bad primes and primes above p; at infinite level take the direct limit. Thus it imposes the local Kummer conditions and sits between rational points tensored with Qp​/Zp​ and the p-primary Tate–Shafarevich group. Its dual XE​ is a torsion Iwasawa module, and the cyclotomic main conjecture is
charZp​[[Γcyc​]]​XE​=(Lp​(E)).​
(46)
With compatible reciprocity conventions, the ordinary function has the finite-character interpolation
χ(Lp​(E))=α−nτ(χ)ΩE+​L(E,χˉ​,1)​(condχ=pn>1),Lp​(E)(1)=(1−α−1)2ΩE+​L(E,1)​,
(47)
where α is the p-adic unit root of X2−ap​X+p and τ(χ)=∑amodpn​χ(a)e2πia/pn; characters of this cyclotomic Zp​-extension are even. The two-variable CM measure specializes to this line after the CM-character twist. Kummer theory, class-field descriptions of the prime-primary local conditions and control of finite-layer errors identify its algebraic counterpart with the displayed Selmer dual. The one-prime division tower is not itself the cyclotomic tower, so this requires the twist and specialization argument, not just changing its name.
Finally, at a good inert prime the curve has supersingular reduction. The ordinary unit root is absent, the unmodified cyclotomic Selmer dual is not torsion, and the ordinary bounded-function argument does not apply. For CM curves over Q and odd supersingular p, signed local trace conditions and signed functions give instead charXE±​=(Lp±​(E)), as proved in Pollack and Rubin's supersingular main-conjecture theorem. Thus the ordinary hypotheses and the analytic local correction are essential parts of the essay's main-conjecture statement.
