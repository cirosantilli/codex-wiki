The Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions says that, for every δ>0, every sufficiently long integer interval contains a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression in every subset of subset density at least δ. We prove this by a density increment, giving the intermediate Fourier and partition arguments.
Let A⊆I=[N] have subset density α>0 and contain no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Embed I in the odd-order cyclic group G=Z/MZ, where M=2N+1, and extend its indicator function by zero. Use normalized Fourier coefficients on a finite abelian group
h(r)=M1​∑x∈G​h(x)e−2πirx/M.
(1)
For the normalized progression count,
T(h1​,h2​,h3​)=M21​∑x+z=2y​h1​(x)h2​(y)h3​(z),
(2)
the orthogonality of characters and Fourier inversion give
T(h1​,h2​,h3​)=∑r∈G​h1​(r)h2​(−2r)h3​(r).
(3)
There is no modular wraparound when x,y,z∈I, since both x+z and 2y lie between 2 and 2N. Only constant progressions occur in A, so T(1A​,1A​,1A​)=αN/M2. In the interval I, endpoints of the same parity have an integer midpoint, giving at least N2/2 ordered progressions.
Put b=α1I​ and f=1A​−b, so f​(0)=0. If N≥4α−2, the difference in progression counts satisfies
T(b,b,b)−T(1A​,1A​,1A​)≥4M2α3N2​.
(4)
Telescope the difference in the other order as T(f,1A​,1A​)+T(b,f,1A​)+T(b,b,f). By Parseval identity and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, each term has absolute value at most
∥f​∥∞​MαN​.
(5)
Indeed ∥1A​∥22​=αN/M, ∥b∥22​=α2N/M, and multiplication by −2 permutes the frequencies because M is odd. It follows that a nonzero frequency r satisfies
∣f​(r)∣≥12Mα2N​≥36α2​=:β.
(6)
Thus the unnormalized Fourier correlation has absolute value at least βN.
Write θ=r/M and q=⌊N​⌋. To prove the needed case of the Dirichlet approximation theorem, place the q+1 fractional parts 0,θ,…,qθ in q equal subintervals of [0,1). The pigeonhole principle gives 1≤d≤q with ∥dθ∥R/Z​≤1/q. Set η=β/(16π) and L=⌊ηq⌋, taking N large enough that ηq≥2. Every residue-class chain in [N] of step d has length at least q. Divide each chain into blocks of length L, adjoining a final shorter remainder to its preceding block. This partitions I into arithmetic progressions P with L≤∣P∣<2L.
The linear phase e−2πiθx varies by at most 4πL/q≤β/4 on each cell. Replace it there by its value at one endpoint. Since ∣f(x)∣≤1, the total error in the Fourier correlation is at most βN/4. Consequently, writing D(P)=∑x∈P​f(x),
∑P​∣D(P)∣≥43βN​.
(7)
But ∑P​D(P)=0, so the positive discrepancies sum to at least 3βN/8. Some cell therefore has D(P)/∣P∣≥3β/8≥β/4. As q≥N​/2 and L≥ηq/2, this proves the one-frequency density increment on an integer interval:
∣P∣≥2304πα2​N​,∣P∣∣A∩P∣​≥α+144α2​.
(8)
Finally, reindex this arithmetic progression as a new integer interval. An affine reindexing preserves three-term arithmetic progressions, so the new subset is again progression-free. Starting with density at least δ, each step raises it by at least δ2/144 and retains an interval of length at least cN​, where c=δ2/(2304π).
Let R(δ) be a size threshold large enough for all the preceding estimates. It works at every later density α≥δ. Choose J=⌈144/δ2⌉ and initial size at least (R(δ)/c2)2J. After j≤J steps the size is at least
c2(1−2−j)N2−j≥c2N2−j≥R(δ).
(9)
Thus all J increments are permitted. They would give density at least δ+Jδ2/144>1, a contradiction. This proves the Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions.

For disjoint nonempty vertex sets U,W, define their edge density of a bipartite graph by d(U,W)=e(U,W)/(∣U∣∣W∣). They form an ε-regular pair of vertex sets if every X⊆U, Y⊆W with ∣X∣≥ε∣U∣, ∣Y∣≥ε∣W∣ satisfies ∣d(X,Y)−d(U,W)∣≤ε.
The Szemerédi regularity lemma asserts that, given ε>0 and a positive integer m0​, there are M,n0​ such that every graph on n≥n0​ vertices has a set partition V0​,V1​,…,Vk​ with
m0​≤k≤M,∣V0​∣≤εn,∣V1​∣=⋯=∣Vk​∣,
(10)
and at most εk2 irregular ordered pairs (Vi​,Vj​) with 1≤i=j≤k.
Assume 0<ε≤1/2, which suffices since a smaller regularity parameter gives the claim for a larger one. Treat the exceptional vertices as singleton cells in the full partition P, and use equitable regularity energy
q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∣A∣∣B∣d(A,B)2.
(11)
Here the adjacency indicator function defines density even for diagonal cells. Clearly 0≤q≤1. If P′ refines P, weighted averaging of densities gives the refinement variance identity for regularity energy
q(P′)−q(P)=n21​∑A,B∈P​∑A′⊆AB′⊆B​​∣A′∣∣B′∣(d(A′,B′)−d(A,B))2≥0.
(12)
To verify it, expand each square and use ∑A′,B′​∣A′∣∣B′∣d(A′,B′)=∣A∣∣B∣d(A,B).
For every irregular ordered pair of nonexceptional cells, choose witnesses Xij​,Yij​ of relative sizes at least ε and density discrepancy greater than ε. Split each Vi​ along all witnesses involving it; symmetry allows at most one witness for each other cell, hence at most 2k atoms in each Vi​. In the witness rectangle, the weighted Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the variance identity contributes more than ε4∣Vi​∣∣Vj​∣/n2.
If more than εk2 pairs are irregular and the equal cell size is L, the energy increase is greater than
ε5n2k2L2​=ε5(1−n∣V0​∣​)2≥4ε5​.
(13)
Restore equal cell sizes by cutting each witness atom into pieces of size ℓ=⌊L/4k⌋, with leftover vertices added to V0​ as singleton cells. This operation is a further refinement, so the energy does not decrease. There are at most k2k atoms, and fewer than ℓ leftover vertices in each, giving at most k2kℓ≤n2−k new exceptional vertices.
Here is a uniform bound ensuring that the iteration is legitimate. Put R=⌈4ε−5⌉ and choose
k0​≥max{m0​,⌈log2​(2R/ε)⌉,2}.
(14)
Start with k0​ equal classes and fewer than k0​ exceptional vertices. Taking n≥2k0​/ε makes the initial exception at most εn/2. At every refinement the number of nonexceptional cells does not decrease: each old cell loses at most L2−k≤L/2 vertices and is cut into pieces of size at most L/4k. Thus each of the at most R rounds adds at most εn/(2R) exceptional vertices.
When L≥2⋅4k, the new cell count is at most
ℓn​≤L2n4k​≤4k4k,
(15)
using ∣V0​∣≤εn≤n/2. Define K0​=k0​ and Kj+1​=4Kj​4Kj​, and take M=KR​. Choosing n0​≥max{2k0​/ε,4M4M} ensures L≥n/(2M)≥2⋅4M at every stage. All rounds are therefore defined, with cell count between m0​ and M and exceptional size at most εn.
If the lemma still failed after R refinements, the energy would have increased by more than Rε5/4≥1, impossible because it lies in [0,1]. Hence some stage has the required regular pairs. This completes the energy-increment proof of Szemerédi regularity, including the equal-size and exceptional-set conditions.

The printed statement needs A=∅: for the empty set every linear subspace has cardinality at least one, so the requested bound is impossible. We prove the intended nonempty version of the Freiman-Ruzsa theorem over a finite field. Necessarily C≥1, because any one translate of A inside A+A has size ∣A∣.
We first prove all sumset estimates needed below. For finite nonempty X and finite U,V in an abelian group, choose one representation z=uz​−vz​ for each z∈U−V. The map
(U−V)×X⟶(U+X)×(V+X),(z,x)⟼(uz​+x,vz​+x)
(20)
is injective: subtracting the outputs recovers z, hence its chosen representation and then x. This proves the Ruzsa triangle inequality
∣U−V∣≤∣X∣∣U+X∣∣V+X∣​.
(21)
Choose nonempty X⊆B minimizing c′=∣X+B∣/∣X∣≤C, where B is any set with ∣B+B∣≤C∣B∣. We prove the Petridis minimal-growth lemma ∣X+B+D∣≤c′∣X+D∣ for every finite D. List D={d1​,…,ds​}, and let Xi​ consist of those x∈X for which x+di​ was not already in X+{d1​,…,di−1​}. The disjoint pieces Xi​+di​ partition X+D.
Every (X∖Xi​)+B+di​ was already covered in X+B+{d1​,…,di−1​}. The new part at step i therefore has size at most
∣X+B∣−∣(X∖Xi​)+B∣≤c′∣X∣−c′∣X∖Xi​∣=c′∣Xi​∣.
(22)
Here minimality of X applies to every nonempty subset X∖Xi​, and the empty case has size zero. Summing proves the lemma. Iteration gives ∣X+kB∣≤Ck∣X∣. Apply the Ruzsa triangle inequality with U=kB, V=ℓB to obtain
∣kB−ℓB∣≤Ck+ℓ∣X∣≤Ck+ℓ∣B∣.
(23)
This derives the required Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. In characteristic two, subtraction is addition, so ∣rB∣≤Cr∣B∣ for every positive integer r.
Fix a∈A and translate to B=A+a, which contains zero, has size ∣A∣, and has the same doubling constant. Choose m=⌈log2​(2∣12B∣)⌉. A uniformly random linear map L:F2N​→G′=F2m​ kills each specified nonzero vector with probability 2−m. The union bound shows that some L satisfies
kerL∩12B={0},∣G′∣=2m<4∣12B∣≤4C12∣B∣.
(24)
Because 0∈B, all lower iterated sumsets lie in 12B. In particular L is injective on B, since the difference of two elements belongs to 2B. For B′=L(B), the density α=∣B′∣/∣G′∣ is at least 1/(4C12). This proves injective linear modelling of a small sumset.
We next prove the Finite-field Bogolyubov lemma in this model. Write f=1B′​ and use normalized real Fourier coefficients on a finite abelian group f​(ξ)=Ex​f(x)(−1)ξ⋅x. Let
S={ξ:∣f​(ξ)∣≥α3/2/2​},W={w:ξ⋅w=0 for every ξ∈S}.
(25)
By Parseval identity, ∣S∣≤2α−2. Thus W is a vector subspace of codimension at most D=⌈32C24⌉.
For w∈W, Fourier inversion of the fourfold normalized convolution gives
f∗f∗f∗f(w)=∑ξ​f​(ξ)4(−1)ξ⋅w.
(26)
All terms with ξ∈S are nonnegative, and the zero-frequency term is α4. The absolute contribution from the remaining frequencies is at most
∑ξ∈/S​∣f​(ξ)∣4≤2α3​∑ξ​∣f​(ξ)∣2=2α4​.
(27)
Hence the convolution is positive and W⊆4B′. Also ∣W∣≥2−D∣G′∣≥2−D∣B∣.
For each w∈W, choose its lift σ(w)∈4B. It is unique because two lifts differ by an element of 8B∩kerL, which is zero. For w1​,w2​∈W, the discrepancy
σ(w1​)+σ(w2​)+σ(w1​+w2​)
(28)
belongs to 12B∩kerL, so it vanishes. Also σ(0)=0. Therefore σ is a linear map and its image W⊆4B is a vector subspace with ∣W∣=∣W∣≥2−D∣B∣. This establishes lifting a subspace through a Freiman model without assuming that arbitrary choices of preimages preserve addition.
Finally, B+W⊆5B is a union of W-cosets. The number of these cosets is at most
t=∣W∣∣B+W∣​≤C52D.
(29)
Choose representatives b1​,…,bt​ from B. Then B⊆W+{b1​,…,bt​}, so adjoining these representatives and a to W gives a vector subspace V containing A. Over F2​, adjoining each vector multiplies the size by at most two. Since ∣W∣≤∣4B∣≤C4∣B∣, the Freiman-Ruzsa bound from Bogolyubov and linear modelling yields
∣V∣≤K(C)∣A∣,K(C)=C421+⌈C52⌈32C24⌉⌉.​
(30)
This bound depends only on C, as required, and every sumset estimate used in its derivation has been proved.
