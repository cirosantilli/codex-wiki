Each one-particle momentum state occupies volume (2πℏ)3/V in momentum space. Counting the spherical shell gives the density of states
g(ϵ)=2π2ℏ3V​ϵ′(p(ϵ))p(ϵ)2​​.

The increasing dispersion law permits inversion, and the density is zero below any minimum allowed energy. No additional spin degeneracy is included here.
For noninteracting particles, the classical Boltzmann sum factorizes into zN. Dividing by N! accounts for indistinguishability without quantum exchange corrections, giving the partition function Z=zN/N!, with z=∫g(ϵ)e−ϵ/(kT)dϵ. Differentiation with respect to inverse temperature gives
E=zN​∫0∞​g(ϵ)ϵe−ϵ/(kT)dϵ​.
(81)
Fixed mode indices have momenta proportional to L−1 because the boundary quantization condition fixes pL/ℏ. As V=L3, ∂p/∂V=−p/(3V) and ∂ϵ/∂V=−pϵ′(p)/(3V). The pressure P=kT∂V​logZ, evaluated by differentiating the mode sum, therefore obeys
PV=3zN​∫0∞​g(ϵ)pϵ′(p)e−ϵ/(kT)dϵ​.
(82)
For nonrelativistic particles ϵ=p2/(2m), so PV=2E/3 and E=3NkT/2. For massless relativistic particles ϵ=cp, so PV=E/3 and E=3NkT. Both obey PV=NkT.
The nonrelativistic thermal wavelength is λth​=h/2πmkT​, and z=V/λth3​. If λth​≪(V/N)1/3, then Nλth3​/V≪1: occupation per thermal quantum state is small. Wave packets scarcely overlap and exchange corrections to Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution are negligible, justifying the classical formulas.
