The rotation unitary operator is U(n,θ)=exp(−iθn⋅J/ℏ). A state with zero orbital angular momentum has a rotationally invariant orbital factor; a spinless j=0 state is wholly unchanged. Zero orbital angular momentum alone does not rule out a nontrivial rotation of intrinsic spin angular momentum.
For spin one-half, J=ℏσ/2. Direct multiplication of the Pauli matrices gives σi​σj​=δij​I+iϵijk​σk​. The antisymmetric part vanishes when contracted with ai​aj​, hence
(J⋅a)2=4ℏ2∣a∣2​I​.
(74)
For a=0, the operator has trace zero, so its eigenvalues are ±ℏ∣a∣/2; for a=0 both are zero. With unit n, the even and odd powers in the exponential give
U(n,θ)=Icos(θ/2)−in⋅σsin(θ/2).
(75)
Take n′=(sinθ,0,cosθ). The rotated state ∣m′⟩θ​=U(y,θ)∣m′⟩ satisfies (n′⋅J)∣m′⟩θ​=m′ℏ∣m′⟩θ​, because UJ3​U†=n′⋅J. By the Born rule, the probability of this outcome in the initial state ∣m⟩ is ∣θ​⟨m′∣m⟩∣2=∣⟨m∣U(y,θ)∣m′⟩∣2. In the J3​ eigenbasis,
U(y,θ)=(cos(θ/2)sin(θ/2)​−sin(θ/2)cos(θ/2)​).
(76)
The transition from m=1/2 to m′=−1/2 thus has probability sin2(θ/2)=1−cos2(θ/2). A=1,B=−1​.
