Taking the divergence and curl of the elastic equation gives
(∇⋅u)tt​=cP2​Δ(∇⋅u),(∇×u)tt​=cS2​Δ(∇×u),cP2​=(λ+2μ)/ρ,cS2​=μ/ρ​.
(91)
Substitution of the plane wave gives ω2A=cP2​k(k⋅A)−cS2​k×(k×A). Resolve A parallel and perpendicular to k. A P wave has longitudinal polarization and ω2=cP2​∣k∣2; an S wave has transverse polarization and ω2=cS2​∣k∣2, with two transverse polarizations.
For the Rayleigh wave, take displacement potentials in the x,z plane,
ux​=ϕx​−ψz​,uz​=ϕz​+ψx​,ϕ=Aekαzeik(x−ct),ψ=Bekβzeik(x−ct),
(92)
where α=1−c2/cP2​​, β=1−c2/cS2​​. For 0<c<cS​<cP​, both waves decay into z<0. These are an evanescent longitudinal wave and an evanescent vertically polarized shear wave. At z=0, the Cauchy stress tensor σij​=λ(∇⋅u)δij​+μ(ui,j​+uj,i​) gives
σxz​=μk2[2iαA−(1+β2)B]eik(x−ct),σzz​=μk2[(1+β2)A+2iβB]eik(x−ct).
(93)
The third traction component is already zero. The two remaining stress-free conditions have a nonzero solution (A,B) exactly when their determinant vanishes:
(1+β2)2=4αβ​.
(94)
Substituting α,β gives the required Rayleigh wave equation. The nonzero subsonic root supplies a self-sustained surface wave; no existence proof for that root is required. The formal zero-speed root is degenerate and does not describe a propagating wave.
