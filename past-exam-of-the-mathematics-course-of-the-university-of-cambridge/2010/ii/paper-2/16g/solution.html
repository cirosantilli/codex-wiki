The map β↦ωβ is strictly increasing and continuous at limit ordinals. Also ωβ≥β, so the set S={β:ωβ≤α} is nonempty and bounded by α. If supS is a successor, it is already in S; if it is a limit, continuity gives ωsupS=supβ∈S​ωβ≤α. Thus S has a greatest element β.
The initial segment of the well-order α of order type ωβ leaves a tail of some order type γ, giving ωβ+γ=α. For fixed left summand, ordinal addition is strictly increasing in its right argument, so this γ is unique.
Maximality gives α<ωβ+1=ωβω. Choose the least positive integer m such that α<ωβm. Then m≥2 and ωβ(m−1)≤α. If γ≥ωβ(m−1), monotonicity of ordinal addition would give α=ωβ+γ≥ωβm, a contradiction. Hence γ<ωβ(m−1)≤α. If α is not a power of ω, both ωβ and γ are nonzero and smaller than α, proving decomposability.
Conversely, prove by transfinite induction that any two ordinals below ωβ have sum below ωβ. For β=0 the only smaller ordinal is zero. For β=δ+1, choose finite m,n with x<ωδm and y<ωδn. Monotonicity gives x+y<ωδ(m+n)<ωδ+1. For limit β, choose δ<β sufficiently large that x,y<ωδ, and apply the induction hypothesis. Thus the nonzero indecomposable ordinals are exactly the powers of ω.
