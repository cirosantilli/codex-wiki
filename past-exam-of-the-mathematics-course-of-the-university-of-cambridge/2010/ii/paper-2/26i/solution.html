A lambda-system contains the whole space and is closed under complements and countable disjoint unions. Let D be the smallest lambda-system containing a pi-system P. For A∈P, the family of B∈D with A∩B∈D is a lambda-system containing P, hence is all of D. Fixing B∈D and repeating the argument shows D is closed under intersections. A lambda-system closed under intersections is a sigma-algebra, so D=σ(P). This proves the pi-lambda theorem. If two probability measures agree on P, the sets on which they agree form a lambda-system; thus they agree on σ(P).
The nonnegative form of Fubini's theorem, also called Tonelli theorem, states that for a nonnegative product-measurable function on two sigma-finite measure spaces, the product integral equals either iterated integral, allowing the value infinity.
For Lebesgue measure on the plane, a bounded interval rectangle I×J has measure ∣I∣∣J∣. Its preimage under f is (λI)×(λ−1J), which has the same measure. Under g, its vertical slice at x∈I is J−sx, whose one-dimensional Lebesgue measure is ∣J∣ by translation invariance. Tonelli therefore gives μ(g−1(I×J))=∣I∣∣J∣. Horizontal slices likewise prove the equality for h. The interval rectangles form a generating pi-system. Sigma-finite measure uniqueness, obtained by the finite-measure pi-lambda argument on an increasing rectangular exhaustion, extends these equalities to all Borel sets. Completion extends them to all Lebesgue-measurable sets, since Borel null sets and their subsets remain null.
