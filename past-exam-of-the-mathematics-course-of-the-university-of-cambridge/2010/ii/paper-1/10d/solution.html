The Hubble time is the instantaneous inverse expansion rate H0−1​, where H=a˙/a. A radial light ray covers comoving distance ∫cdt/a(t). Since a(t0​)=1, the present proper radius of the particle horizon is
R0​=c∫0t0​​a(t)dt​.​
(29)
For the power-law scale factor, a(0)=0 requires α>0. The integral converges only when α<1, in which case
R0​=1−αct0​​,H0​=t0​α​,t0​=αH0−1​.​
(30)
At α=1 the divergence is logarithmic, and at α>1 it is a power divergence. The familiar flat-universe epochs have α=1/2 for radiation and α=2/3 for pressureless matter, obtained from ρ∝a−4 or a−3 and the Friedmann equation. Both have 0<α<1, so their ages are less than the present Hubble time. These power laws describe idealized single-component epochs rather than the full late-time expansion history.
