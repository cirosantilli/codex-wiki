A Poisson structure is a smooth antisymmetric matrix field ωab defining a bracket
{f,g}=∑a,b​ωab∂a​f∂b​g
(105)
that obeys the Jacobi identity. Bilinearity, antisymmetry and the product rule follow from this formula; Jacobi is the remaining condition. Applying Jacobi to the coordinate functions gives
d∑​(ωdc∂d​ωab+ωdb∂d​ωca+ωda∂d​ωbc)=0.​
(106)
These are also sufficient: expanding Jacobi for arbitrary functions cancels all second-derivative terms by antisymmetry, leaving these coefficients multiplying their first derivatives.
For ωab=ϵabcxc, the nonconstant function f=∑a​(xa)2 is a Casimir function of a Poisson manifold, because
{f,xa}=2∑b,c​xbϵbacxc=0
(107)
by symmetry of xbxc. This is the usual three-dimensional Lie-Poisson bracket.
Since the matrix M is symmetric, ∂d​H=∑b​Mdbxb. The Hamilton equations are x˙a={xa,H}, and hence
x˙a=∑b,c,d​ϵadcMdbxbxc.
(108)
One valid choice of coefficients is Qabc=∑d​ϵadcMdb. If symmetric coefficients in b,c are preferred, the same equations use
Qabc=21​d∑​(ϵadcMdb+ϵadbMdc).​
(109)
In vector notation the evolution is (Mx)×x, consistent with the bracket convention and with preservation of ∣x∣2.
