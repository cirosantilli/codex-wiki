A ring of sets is closed under finite unions and differences. A premeasure on it is a nonnegative set function with value zero on the empty set that is countably additive whenever a disjoint union remains in the ring. Caratheodory extension theorem extends a premeasure to a measure on the generated sigma-algebra; this extension is unique there when the premeasure is sigma-finite. It is obtained from the outer measure
μ∗(E)=inf{∑j​μ(Aj​):E⊆⋃j​Aj​, Aj​∈A},
(81)
whose measurable sets satisfy μ∗(E)=μ∗(E∩B)+μ∗(E∖B) for every E. The resulting complete measurable sigma-algebra contains the sigma-algebra generated by the ring.
For the required continuity, let Bn​↓∅ in the bounded-interval ring and fix ϵ>0. From each component (a,b] of Bn​, remove a sufficiently short initial piece and retain the compact interval [a+δ,b]. The union is a compact set Kn​⊂Bn​, and Bn​∖Kn​ is covered by a ring set Cn​ of total length less than ϵ2−n; one can take unions of (a,a+δ]. We do not incorrectly assume the compact sets themselves belong to the half-open interval ring.
Since ⋂n​Kn​=∅ and K1​ is compact, the finite intersection property supplies N with ⋂n=1N​Kn​=∅. For x∈BN​, membership in all earlier Bn​ means some failure of membership in Kn​ puts x in Cn​. Thus
BN​⊆⋃n=1N​Cn​,μ(BN​)≤∑n=1N​μ(Cn​)<ϵ.
(82)
Finite additivity gives the finite subadditivity used here. Monotonicity now proves μ(Bn​)→0.
If a disjoint union A=⋃j​Aj​ lies in the ring, its remainders A∖⋃j≤n​Aj​ decrease to the empty set. The proved continuity and finite additivity imply countable additivity, so the length function is a premeasure. The ring generates the Borel sigma-algebra and covers the line by bounded intervals of finite measure. Its unique sigma-finite extension is Borel length measure; its completion is Lebesgue measure.
