Let the stellar radius be R and assume zero external surface pressure. A spherical shell has mass dm=4πr2ρdr and contributes −Gm(r)dm/r to the gravitational potential energy. Using hydrostatic pressure support,
Egrav​=−∫0R​4πGmρrdr=4π∫0R​r3P′dr=−12π∫0R​r2Pdr=−3⟨P⟩V​.
(49)
The center boundary term vanishes and P(R)=0 eliminates the outer term. Nonzero external pressure would add 3P(R)V.
The total energy is (α−3)⟨P⟩V, so energetic gravitational binding requires α<3. For an isotropic gas, kinetic theory gives P=(1/3)∫pvdn. Nonrelativistic kinetic energy is pv/2, giving U=3PV/2 and α=3/2. Ultrarelativistic energy is pv, giving U=3PV and α=3: the ultrarelativistic limit is marginal in this Newtonian virial balance.
For a white dwarf, electron degeneracy pressure becomes ultrarelativistic as density increases. Its equation of state then scales as P=Kρ4/3. Pressure support and gravitational stress have the same radius scaling, leaving a limiting mass of order (K/G)3/2 rather than allowing arbitrary mass to be supported by contraction. The Chandrasekhar limit is this maximum mass for a cold electron-degenerate star at specified composition, of order (ℏc/G)3/2/(μe​mp​)2.
The virial calculation by itself does not establish a universal numerical maximum mass for every stellar composition and equation of state; its precise consequence is loss of binding/support as the pressure-bearing particles become ultrarelativistic. An accreting carbon-oxygen white dwarf approaching its limiting mass typically undergoes runaway carbon burning and thermonuclear disruption, a Type Ia supernova. For other compositions or evolution, electron captures can instead lead to collapse into a neutron star. The subsequent evolution needs that physical distinction.
