The Banach-Steinhaus theorem, or Uniform boundedness principle, states that for a family T of bounded linear operators from a Banach space E to a normed space F, pointwise boundedness supT∈T​∥Tx∥<∞ for every x∈E implies supT​∥T∥<∞.
For its proof let En​={x:supT​∥Tx∥≤n}. These are closed and their union is E. The Baire category theorem gives an En​ with nonempty interior, containing a ball B(x0​,r). For ∥h∥<r, both x0​ and x0​+h belong to En​, so ∥Th∥≤2n for every T. Scaling h and taking a limit at the ball boundary gives ∥T∥≤2n/r, proving the theorem. The target need not be complete.
For the asserted bounded-set criterion, use the Banach space X∗, which is complete even if X is not. Each x∈S defines the bounded linear evaluation functional Jx​(f)=f(x) on X∗. The given hypothesis says this family is pointwise bounded. The Hahn-Banach theorem consequence in the question gives ∥Jx​∥=∥x∥, and uniform boundedness yields
x∈Ssup​∥x∥<∞.​

This proves that weak boundedness implies norm boundedness; the two forms of boundedness coincide for a subset of a normed space.
Finally suppose the two continuous duals for ∥⋅∥1​ and ∥⋅∥2​ were equal as sets. The unit ball for norm two is bounded under every common continuous functional, using its norm-two continuity. Applying the preceding criterion in norm one bounds that ball in norm one; scaling gives ∥v∥1​≤C∥v∥2​. Reversing the roles gives ∥v∥2​≤C′∥v∥1​. The norms would be equivalent. Contrapositively, inequivalent norms have different continuous duals, supplying a linear functional continuous for one and discontinuous for the other.
