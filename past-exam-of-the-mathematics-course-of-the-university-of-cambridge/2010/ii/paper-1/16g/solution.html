Work with well-ordered cardinals, as usual with the axiom of choice. We first prove the infinite cardinal arithmetic identity κ2=κ. If a least infinite counterexample κ existed, order pairs of ordinals below κ first by their maximum coordinate, then lexicographically within each level. Every initial segment ending at (ξ,η) lies inside (max(ξ,η)+1)2. Its cardinal is less than κ, by the smaller-cardinal identity or finite counting. This well-order cannot have an element with κ predecessors, so its order type is at most the initial ordinal κ. Thus ∣κ×κ∣≤κ, contrary to its being a counterexample; the reverse injection is ξ↦(ξ,0). Hence
ℵα2​=ℵα​for every α.​

Likewise fewer than ℵ2​ summands means at most ℵ1​ summands, each of size at most ℵ1​. Their sum is at most ℵ12​=ℵ1​<ℵ2​. Thus ℵ2​ is regular. In contrast,
