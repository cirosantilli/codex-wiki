After reducing x/y=p/q, we still have ∣α−p/q∣<1/(2q2). Here is the continued fraction convergent criterion with its proof. If p/q is reduced and obeys this inequality, it is a strict best approximation of the second kind: for any different r/s with 0<s≤q, the assumption ∣sα−r∣≤∣qα−p∣ would give
1≤∣ps−qr∣≤s∣p−qα∣+q∣r−sα∣<2qs+q​≤1,
(2)
a contradiction. Choose consecutive continued fraction convergents pj​/qj​,pj+1​/qj+1​ with qj​≤q<qj+1​. Their determinant is ±1, their errors ej​=qj​α−pj​ alternate in sign, and every integer pair (r,s) is uniquely a(pj​,qj​)+b(pj+1​,qj+1​). For 0<s<qj+1​, either b=0, or a,b have opposite signs: positive a,b would make s≥qj+1​, negative a,b make s<0, and a=0 is impossible. Opposite signs make aej​ and bej+1​ have the same sign, so ∣sα−r∣≥∣ej​∣. If b=0, reduction gives the jth convergent itself. Thus a strict best approximation must be a convergent. The determinant and alternating-error facts follow immediately from the recurrence pj​=aj​pj−1​+pj−2​, qj​=aj​qj−1​+qj−2​ and the positive continued-fraction tail formula. Therefore the reduced ratio x/y is a convergent of N​. Without assuming gcd(x,y)=1, the unreduced pair may be a common multiple of the convergent's numerator and denominator; for example (10,2) for N=24,M=4 reduces to 5/1.
