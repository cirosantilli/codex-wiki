Substitute ϕ=ei(kx−ωt) into the mixed-derivative equation. Its left side is kωϕ, so for k=0 the dispersion relation, phase velocity and group velocity are
ω=−kα​,c(k)=−k2α​,cg​(k)=k2α​.​
(126)
The graph of a function for each velocity is symmetric about k=0, singular at zero, and approaches the horizontal axis as ∣k∣ grows. Energy packets travel right while their crests travel left.
At a distant fixed observer x>0, stationary components obey x/t=cg​(k), so ∣k∣=αt/x​. As time increases their wavelength 2π/∣k∣ decreases and their frequency magnitude ∣ω∣=αx/t​ decreases; the crests pass toward decreasing x.
For the given initial spectrum the solution is
ϕ(x,t)=∫R​A(k)exp[i(kx+kαt​)]dk,A(−k)=A(k)​ for real ϕ.​
(127)
The value of a regular spectrum at the single point k=0 is irrelevant to this integral; a nonzero spatially constant mode is incompatible with the PDE. For asymptotic calculation assume the usual smooth localized spectrum, with sufficient integrability for the following stationary-phase steps.
At x=Vt, V>0, the phase Φ(k)=Vk+α/k has stationary points k=±k0​, k0​=α/V​. Their values are ±2αV​ and their second derivatives are ±2α/k03​. Expanding quadratically and using the oscillatory Gaussian integral gives the stationary phase leading term
ϕ(Vt,t)∼αtπk03​​​[A(k0​)ei(2αV​t+π/4)+A(−k0​)e−i(2αV​t+π/4)].​
(128)
For real data this is twice the real part of the positive-frequency term. If a stationary coefficient vanishes, the formula instead asserts that its contribution has smaller order. At x=−Vt, the phase derivative is −V−α/k2<0, so there is no real stationary point and no corresponding t−1/2 wave packet. For smooth compactly supported spectra away from zero, repeated integration by parts gives decay faster than any power of t. More general spectra require endpoint/tail control; absence of stationary points alone does not justify that stronger rate for arbitrary A.
