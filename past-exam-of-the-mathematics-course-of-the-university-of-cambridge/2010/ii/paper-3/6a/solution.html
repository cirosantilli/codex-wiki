With no chemotaxis, the stationary oxygen equation is Dc​c′′=knˉ. Symmetry and the two prescribed surface values give
c(z)=c0​+2Dc​knˉ​(z2−4d2​).​
(15)
By Fick's first law, the vertical oxygen flux is jc​=−Dc​c′=−knˉz. Thus each face supplies an inward flux knˉd/2 per unit area, and the total influx is knˉd, equal to the integrated consumption across the layer. The profile is physically nonnegative only if c0​≥knˉd2/(8Dc​); otherwise the constant-consumption approximation must be modified.
For bacteria retained in the fluid, impose zero bacterial flux at the faces. The stationary bacterial equation says that jn​=−Dn​n′+μnc′ is constant, hence zero. Integrating n′/n=(μ/Dn​)c′ gives n(z)=n0​exp(μc(z)/Dn​), with n0​ fixed by the total bacterial population. Substitution into the oxygen equation proves
c′′−Dc​kn0​​exp(μc/Dn​)=0.​
(16)
Zero bacterial through-flux is the boundary assumption behind this equilibrium relation.
