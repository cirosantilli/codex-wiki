The Bloch theorem says that for a translation-invariant lattice Hamiltonian, eigenstates can be chosen as ψk​(x+a)=eikaψk​(x), or ψk​(x)=eikxuk​(x) with uk​ periodic of period a. The localized site orbitals are translates, ϕn​(x)=ϕ0​(x−na) and χn​(x)=χ0​(x−na). In the tight-binding model, neglect intersite overlap and use
ψ(x,t)=∑n​(bn​(t)ϕn​(x)+cn​(t)χn​(x)).

The extrema occur at k=0 and at the zone edge; they may be nonunique when E0​=E1​, in which case the gap vanishes. In the trial wavefunction, replacing x by x+a and reindexing n produces exactly the factor eika, verifying Bloch theorem directly.
An electrical conductor has available nearby unoccupied states in a partially filled band or overlapping bands. A band insulator has filled lower bands separated from empty higher bands by a large gap. A semiconductor has the same filled-band structure with a smaller gap, so thermal excitation or doping supplies mobile electrons and holes. Band filling and carrier availability, not just the existence of two bands, determine which description applies.
