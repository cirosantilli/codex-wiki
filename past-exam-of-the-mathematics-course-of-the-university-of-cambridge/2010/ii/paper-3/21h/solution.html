The real Stone-Weierstrass theorem states that if X is compact Hausdorff and A⊆C(X,R) is a subalgebra containing the constants and separating points, then A is uniformly dense in C(X,R). Let B be its uniform closure, still an algebra over a field. If h∈B and M≥∥h∥∞​, scale the permitted polynomial approximation of absolute value on [−1,1] to [−M,M]. Applying these polynomials to h proves ∣h∣∈B. Therefore
max(h,k)=21​(h+k+∣h−k∣),min(h,k)=21​(h+k−∣h−k∣)
(62)
belong to B: it is a lattice.
Fix f∈C(X,R) and ε>0. For every x,y∈X, point separation and an affine adjustment give gx,y​∈A with gx,y​(x)=f(x) and gx,y​(y)=f(y); if x=y, a constant suffices. For fixed x, the open sets where gx,y​>f−ε cover X. Select finitely many and take their maximum hx​∈B. Then hx​>f−ε everywhere and hx​(x)=f(x). By continuity, hx​<f+ε on some neighborhood Vx​ of x. Choose finitely many Vx​ covering X and take the minimum of the corresponding hx​. The result h∈B satisfies f−ε<h<f+ε everywhere. Since B is closed and ε arbitrary, f∈B. Thus A=C(X,R), proving the theorem.
