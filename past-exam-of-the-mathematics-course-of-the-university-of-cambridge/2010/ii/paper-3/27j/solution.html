In the normal form of a Bayesian decision problem, choose a measurable decision rule δ before seeing the data to minimize the prior Bayes risk EL(Θ,δ(X)). In the extensive form, after observing X=x, choose an action minimizing posterior expected loss. Conditional expectation makes these equivalent, subject to the usual measurable selection conditions. If Πx​ is the posterior distribution and R0​(Π)=infa​∫L(θ,a)Π(dθ) is the best no-data loss, then
R1​=EX​R0​(ΠX​)≤R0​(Π).​
(78)
The inequality follows by ignoring the data.
For a measurable region S, posterior or prior expected loss is ∫S​(c−π(θ))dθ. It is minimized by including exactly the locations where the integrand is negative; including equality points is harmless. Thus the highest density region S∗={π≥c} is a Bayes act and
R0​(Π)=−∫(π−c)+​dθ≤0.​

For N(μ,σ2), if c≥1/(σ2π​) the answer is zero. Otherwise put zc​=2log[1/(cσ2π​)]​. The optimal interval is [μ−σzc​,μ+σzc​], and
R0​=2cσzc​−(2Φ(zc​)−1).​

For the specified prior and c=1/2, the prior density is everywhere below c, so R0​=0. Completing the square gives Θ∣X=x∼N(9x/10,1/10). Its peak is 10/(2π)​>1/2, so a positive-measure region has posterior density strictly above c and its optimal expected loss is strictly negative. That loss is independent of its mean, hence independent of x. Therefore R1​<0=R0​. Explicitly, with zc​=2log(210/(2π)​)​, R1​=zc​/10​−2Φ(zc​)+1.
