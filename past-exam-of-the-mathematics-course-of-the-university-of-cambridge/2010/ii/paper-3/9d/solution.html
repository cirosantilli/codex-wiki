The quadratic first integrals of the torque-free Euler equations for a torque-free rigid body are
2E=I1​ω12​+I2​ω22​+I3​ω32​,L2=I12​ω12​+I22​ω22​+I32​ω32​.​
(22)
Differentiation and substitution of all three Euler equations make each derivative vanish: the coefficients respectively sum to (I2​−I3​)+(I3​−I1​)+(I1​−I2​)=0 and I1​(I2​−I3​)+I2​(I3​−I1​)+I3​(I1​−I2​)=0. At either endpoint these constants are 2E=I2​Ω2, L2=I22​Ω2, proving L2=2EI2​.
Eliminating the other squared components gives, with s=ω2​/Ω,
ω12​=I1​(I3​−I1​)I2​(I3​−I2​)​Ω2(1−s2),ω32​=I3​(I3​−I1​)I2​(I2​−I1​)​Ω2(1−s2).
(23)
Hence ∣s∣≤1 and the second Euler equation gives ds/dτ=±(1−s2), where τ=Ωt(I2​−I1​)(I3​−I2​)/(I1​I3​)​ increases with t. The specified orbit goes from s=1 to s=−1, so its sign must be negative. The sign in the original PDF's reduced equation is therefore inconsistent with its endpoint conditions. The corrected heteroclinic satisfies
dτds​=s2−1,s(τ)=−tanh(τ−τ0​).​
(24)
Integrating by separation gives this family for ∣s∣<1; the integration constant is a time translation. The printed positive-sign equation instead has the increasing solutions s=tanh(τ−τ0​) and cannot meet the stated endpoints. For completeness its other real solutions are s=±1 and the branches s=coth(τ−τ0​) on intervals avoiding their pole. These latter branches are excluded by the conserved-energy constraint ∣s∣≤1. The scalar heteroclinic is unique up to translation. For the full angular-velocity vector there are two sign-related branches: simultaneously reversing ω1​ and ω3​ preserves all three Euler equations and both endpoints. Full-vector uniqueness therefore also requires choosing one of those branches.
