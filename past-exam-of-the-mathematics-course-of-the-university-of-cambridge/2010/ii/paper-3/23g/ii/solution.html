Work geometrically over an algebraic closure; the degree and ramification do not change under this base extension. The coordinate squaring map lands in W because its defining equation pulls back to that of V. Above a generic target point with all coordinates nonzero there are 23 square-root choices, identified in pairs by simultaneous sign change. Hence
degϕ=4.​
(65)
The effective sign-change group is C2​×C2​. At a point with all coordinates nonzero its stabilizer is trivial, and the squaring map is locally étale, since characteristic is not two. Thus ep​=1 there. If one coordinate is zero, a sign change of that coordinate fixes the point, giving stabilizer order two. More explicitly, if X0​=0, use t=X0​/X2​ as a local parameter; the curve equation has nonzero derivative in the other coordinate, so this is valid, and the corresponding target parameter is t2. Therefore
ep​=2 when exactly one coordinate vanishes, and ep​=1 otherwise.​
(66)
Two coordinates cannot vanish on either curve. For each vanishing coordinate, the other-coordinate ratio solves z8=−1, giving eight distinct points; there are 24 ramification points. As a check, the smooth plane-curve genera are 21 and 3, and Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives 2⋅21−2=4(2⋅3−2)+24.
