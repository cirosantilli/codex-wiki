Its centralizer contains that Sylow subgroup, so its class size is a power of q. Every nontrivial irreducible representation is faithful by simplicity. If it sent g to a scalar, faithfulness would make g central in the group, impossible in a nonabelian simple group. Induct on the order: every nonsimple group has a proper nontrivial normal subgroup, and that subgroup and the quotient have smaller orders with at most these two prime divisors. By induction both are soluble; their derived-series extensions make the original group soluble. Simple abelian groups have prime order and start the induction. This proves Burnside's theorem.
