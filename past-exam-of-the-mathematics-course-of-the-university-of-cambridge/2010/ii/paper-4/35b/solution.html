Write the superconducting order parameter as ψ=ns​​eiϑ, with carrier mass m. Minimal electromagnetic coupling gives mechanical momentum ℏ∇ϑ−qA, so the current density is
j=mns​q​(ℏ∇ϑ−qA).​
(161)
It also follows by substituting ψ into (qℏ/m)Im(ψ∗∇ψ)−(q2/m)∣ψ∣2A. In a gauge where the phase is constant, this reduces to the London equation j=−ns​q2A/m.
Under A↦A+∇χ, the phase changes by ϑ↦ϑ+qχ/ℏ. The two gradient changes cancel, proving gauge invariance of the actual current. A formula omitting the phase term is valid only in its chosen gauge.
For a uniform superconductor without vortices, taking a curl gives ∇×j=−ns​q2B/m. Combine this with the stationary Ampère's law ∇×B=μ0​j and ∇⋅B=0 to obtain
∇2B=λL2​B​,λL​=μ0​ns​q2m​​.
(162)
For a magnetic field parallel to the planar interface, translation symmetry reduces the equation to B′′=B/λL2​. Boundedness and decay as z→∞, together with the tangential boundary value, give the Meissner effect
B(z)=B0​e−z/λL​,z>0.​
(163)
Here λL​ is the London penetration depth.
The parallel-field assumption is necessary for this one-dimensional Meissner solution. If the printed arbitrary constant B0​ has a nonzero normal component, ∇⋅B=0 forces that component to be constant across a translation-invariant half-space, whereas the London equation forces it to vanish. Thus no decaying planar Meissner state with that boundary value exists; one must change the geometry or allow a different magnetic state.
