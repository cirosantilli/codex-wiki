The literal assertion needs q=p. If q=p, the two geodesic circles coincide, have equal tangent lines at every point, and do not intersect transversally; their intersection is not finite. We prove that close geodesic circles intersect twice for nearby distinct centres, and in fact find exactly two intersections.
Choose a strongly convex normal neighbourhood about p and a sufficiently small r>0 so that every circle considered and all geodesics joining its points to nearby centres remain there. Squared distance d(q,x)2 is then smooth in both arguments, and the exponential map is nonsingular on the relevant radius-r tangent circles. Consequently all these distance spheres are smooth embedded one-dimensional manifolds.
Write q=expp​(εv) with ∣v∣=1 and ε>0, and parameterize Sr​(p) by x(θ)=expp​(re(θ)). Define
F(ε,v,θ)=d(expp​(εv),x(θ))2−r2.
(88)
The first variation of geodesic energy gives
∂ε​F(0,v,θ)=−2r⟨v,e(θ)⟩.
(89)
Indeed differentiating the energy of the joining geodesic cancels the interior term by the geodesic equation, leaving the initial endpoint term −2⟨re(θ),v⟩. This is also the endpoint form of the Gauss lemma.
Since F(0,v,θ)=0, the quotient F/ε extends smoothly to ε=0 by integrating ∂ε​F from zero to ε. At zero it is −2rcos(θ−argv), with exactly two simple zeros. The implicit function theorem gives two nearby simple zeros for small positive ε. Compactness of the unit vector circle makes the choice of small ε uniform in v; away from fixed neighbourhoods of those two zeros the cosine is bounded away from zero, so there are no additional zeros.
A nonzero angular derivative at either zero says that the defining function of Sr​(q) has a nonzero derivative along Sr​(p). Thus their tangent lines differ: the intersections are transverse. Shrinking the neighbourhood of centres accordingly proves
#(Sr​(p)∩Sr​(q))=2,#(Sr​(p)∩Sr​(q))≡0(mod2)(q=p, q sufficiently near p).​
(90)
