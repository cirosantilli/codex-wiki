Suppose first that the unique interior minimum is nondegenerate, ϕ′′(c)>0, and f(c)=0. Outside a small neighbourhood of c the phase exceeds ϕ(c) by a fixed positive amount, so that region is exponentially smaller. Inside, expand

If the minimum is at a and ϕ′(a)>0, the scale is t−a=O(λ−1), giving f(a)e−λϕ(a)/[λϕ′(a)]. At b with ϕ′(b)<0, replace the denominator by λ∣ϕ′(b)∣. A stationary quadratic endpoint gives half the interior Gaussian factor.
The printed assumptions do not guarantee nondegeneracy or f(c)=0. More generally, if ϕ(t)−ϕ(c)∼A∣t−c∣m with A>0 and even m at an interior minimum, the leading phase scale is λ−1/m and, for f(c)=0, the factor is
m(Aλ)1/m2f(c)Γ(1/m)​e−λϕ(c).
(134)
At a one-sided endpoint the factor two is absent and the first positive one-sided phase power need not be even. If the amplitude vanishes, expand it to its first contributing term; parity can cancel interior terms. A phase flat to all orders requires its actual local behavior rather than a nonexistent quadratic Taylor coefficient.
