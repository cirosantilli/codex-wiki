The Baire category theorem states that a complete metric space is not a countable union of closed sets with empty interior. To prove it, let G1​,G2​,… be open dense sets and start with any nonempty open set V. Inductively choose closed balls
B(xn​,rn​)⊂B(xn−1​,rn−1​)∩Gn​,0<rn​<2−n,
(116)
with the first ball inside V∩G1​. The centres are Cauchy. Completeness supplies a limit lying in every ball, hence in V∩⋂n​Gn​. Thus the intersection is dense, which is the equivalent form of the theorem.
Now each nS is closed and ⋃n≥1​nS=X. Baire gives
B(x,r)⊂nS
(117)
for some x,r,n. Symmetry gives B(−x,r)⊂nS. If ∥z∥<r, then x+z and −x+z lie in these two balls; convexity makes their midpoint z belong to nS. Hence
B(0,r/n)⊂S,
(118)
which proves the closed convex absorbing set has an origin neighbourhood result.
