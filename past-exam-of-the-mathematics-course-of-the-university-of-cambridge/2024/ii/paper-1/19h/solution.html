A complex representation of G is a complex vector space V together with a homomorphism
ρ:G⟶GL(V).
(131)
It is faithful when kerρ={e}, equivalently when ρ is injective. Every finite group has a faithful complex representation: in the regular representation, G permutes the basis {eh​:h∈G} by
ρ(g)eh​=egh​,
(132)
and a group element fixing every basis vector must be the identity.
If g is conjugate to gk, then ρ(g) is similar to ρ(gk)=ρ(g)k. Thus the two matrices have the same spectrum, while an eigenvalue λ of ρ(g) gives the eigenvalue λk of ρ(g)k. Hence
λ∈S(g)⟹λk∈S(g).
(133)
Now take a p-cycle g∈Sp​. It is conjugate to gk for every 1≤k<p. Faithfulness makes ρ(g) have order p, so its spectrum contains a nontrivial pth root of unity λ. The preceding implication puts all the p−1 distinct values
λ,λ2,…,λp−1
(134)
in the spectrum. Therefore dimV≥p−1. This is the spectrum orbit bound for a faithful symmetric-group representation.
Finally let G be the group of all permutations of N. It has no faithful finite-dimensional complex representation. For every n, G contains a subgroup (C2​)n generated by n disjoint transpositions. In a d-dimensional complex representation, commuting involutions are simultaneously diagonalizable and hence map into the group of diagonal sign matrices, which has order 2d. A faithful restriction would require 2n≤2d for every n, an impossibility.
