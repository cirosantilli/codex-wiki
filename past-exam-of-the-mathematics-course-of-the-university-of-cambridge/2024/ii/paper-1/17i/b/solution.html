Let P=v0​…vℓ​ be a longest path. All neighbours of v0​ lie on P, in the colour class opposite to v0​, so ℓ≥2k−1. If ℓ≥2k, the first 2k edges give the required path. Otherwise ℓ=2k−1, and all k vertices of the opposite colour on P must be neighbours of v0​; in particular v0​vℓ​ is an edge, giving a cycle of length 2k. The graph Kk,k​ has minimum degree k but only 2k vertices, so it has no path of length 2k.
For the stronger assertion, assume there is no 4-cycle. Rotate a longest path about each edge from v0​ to obtain k possible endpoints in the colour class of v0​. Every neighbour of each rotated endpoint lies on P, since otherwise the corresponding path could be extended. In a 4-cycle-free bipartite graph, two vertices in one colour class have at most one common neighbour. The neighbourhood bound for a square-free bipartite graph therefore shows that the colour class opposite to v0​ contains at least 2k−1 vertices of P. If the endpoints have opposite colours, repeating the argument from vℓ​ gives the same bound for the other class. If they have the same colour, their class has one more vertex on P than the opposite class. In either case P has at least 4k−2 vertices and length at least 4k−3. Consequently G contains either such a path or a 4-cycle.
