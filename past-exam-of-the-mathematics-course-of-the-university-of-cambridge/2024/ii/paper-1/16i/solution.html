The soundness theorem for propositional logic says that if Γ⊢t, then every valuation satisfying every member of Γ also satisfies t.
The proposed function need not respect the connectives. For example, if a primitive proposition p is independent of S, then neither p nor ¬p is provable from S, so the definition gives
v(p)=v(¬p)=0.
(117)
Order the consistent supersets of S by inclusion. The union of a chain is consistent, since a finite proof of a contradiction would already use assumptions from one member of the chain. Zorn lemma therefore gives a maximal consistent extension T. It is deductively closed: if T⊢t, adjoining t preserves consistency, so maximality forces t∈T. Moreover, for every t, exactly one of t and ¬t belongs to T. They cannot both belong by consistency; if neither belonged, the inconsistency of both proper extensions would give T⊢¬t and T⊢¬¬t, again a contradiction. The usual induction on formulae now shows that
v(t)=1⟺t∈T
(118)
defines a valuation satisfying T, and hence S.
Now suppose every finite subset of S has a model. By soundness every finite subset is consistent. Any proof of a contradiction from S uses only finitely many assumptions, so S itself is consistent. Applying the preceding maximal-consistent-extension construction gives a model of S. This proves the propositional compactness theorem.
