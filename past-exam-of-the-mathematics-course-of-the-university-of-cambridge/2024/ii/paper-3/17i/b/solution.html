OurBigBook.com Words: 129
Set N=(100δ)g and take G∼G(N,4δ/N). Its expected number of edges is asymptotic to 2δN, and a Chernoff bound makes e(G)>3δN/2 with positive probability. The expected number of cycles of length below g is at most
∑ℓ=3g−1​2ℓ(4δ)ℓ​<(4δ)g,
(74)
which is far below δN/2; Markov's inequality shows that, simultaneously with positive probability, there are fewer than δN/2 such cycles.
Choose such a graph and delete one edge from every cycle of length below g. The resulting graph has girth at least g and more than δN edges, hence average degree greater than 2δ. Part (a) supplies a subgraph of minimum degree at least δ. It uses at most N=(100δ)g vertices and cannot acquire any shorter cycle.
