The real Stone-Weierstrass theorem says that if X is compact Hausdorff and A⊂C(X,R) is a subalgebra containing the constants and separating points, then A is uniformly dense in C(X,R).
Let A be the uniform closure. Polynomial approximation to t​ on a bounded interval shows that ∣f∣∈A whenever f∈A. Hence A is closed under
max(f,g)=2f+g+∣f−g∣​,min(f,g)=2f+g−∣f−g∣​.
(95)
Given h∈C(X), separation and the constants provide, for each x,y, a function in A agreeing with h at x and y. Compactness first combines finitely many such functions by minima to obtain one that agrees at x and lies below h+ε everywhere; a second finite cover and maxima produces f∈A with ∣f−h∣<ε. Thus h∈A.
The space Cb​(R) is Banach because a uniform Cauchy sequence converges uniformly to a bounded continuous function. Compactness is essential: the algebra of bounded continuous functions having finite limits at both +∞ and −∞ contains constants and separates points, for example using tanhx, but its uniform closure has the same limiting property. It cannot uniformly approximate sinx, so it is not dense in Cb​(R).
