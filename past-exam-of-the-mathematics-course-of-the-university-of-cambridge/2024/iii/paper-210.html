A cubic spline with knots x1​<⋯<xn​ is twice continuously differentiable and restricts to a polynomial of degree at most three between consecutive knots. It is a natural cubic spline when it is linear outside [x1​,xn​], equivalently when its second derivative vanishes at the two outer knots and on the exterior intervals.
Let s be the natural cubic spline interpolating prescribed values and let g​=s+h be any other interpolant in S2​[a,b]. Then h(xi​)=0. Piecewise integration by parts, using that s′′′′=0 between knots, s′′ is continuous, s′′=0 on the exterior intervals, and every jump of s′′′ is multiplied by h(xi​)=0, gives
∫ab​s′′(x)h′′(x)dx=0.
(20)
Consequently
R(g​)=R(s)+R(h)≥R(s).
(21)
Equality forces h′′=0 almost everywhere. Then h is affine and its zeros at at least two distinct knots force h=0, proving uniqueness.

One form of Assouad's lemma is as follows. Let {Pω​:ω∈{0,1}m} be a statistical experiment and suppose parameters satisfy
d(θω​,θω′​)2≥ρ2dH​(ω,ω′).

(31)
then every estimator obeys
supω​Eω​d(θ,θω​)2≥8mρ2​(1−α),
(32)

Construct a hypercube inside the convex cone. Start from f∗​(x)=x2. Partition most of [0,1] into m=⌊n/k⌋ consecutive blocks of k grid intervals. On block j, let ϕj​ be the chord joining the two endpoint values of f∗​ minus f∗​ inside the block, and zero outside. For ω∈{0,1}m, set

Replacing a convex arc by its chord leaves a convex, nondecreasing function. Its values remain in [0,1], so every θω​ belongs to Cn​∩Mn​∩[0,1]n, and hence also to the larger parameter set in the first claim.
The perturbations have disjoint supports. For neighboring hypercube vertices their squared Euclidean separation is independent of the block and, using the supplied sum, satisfies
