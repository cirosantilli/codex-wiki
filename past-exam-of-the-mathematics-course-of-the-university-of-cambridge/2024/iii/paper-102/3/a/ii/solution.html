Write a positive nonsimple root as
α=∑β∈Δ​nβ​β,nβ​≥0.

If (α,β)≤0 for every simple root with nβ​>0, then
(α,α)=∑β​nβ​(α,β)≤0,

which is impossible. Hence (α,β)>0 for some simple β. The root-string property then gives α−β∈Φ, and its simple-root coefficients remain nonnegative. This is the simple-root subtraction lemma.
Induct on the height ∑nβ​. Applying the induction hypothesis to α−β and appending β writes
α=α1​+⋯+αk​
(37)
so that every partial sum is a root.
Finally let α be simple and let γ=α be positive. In the simple-root expansion of
sα​(γ)=γ−⟨γ,α∨⟩α,
(38)
all coefficients except possibly that of α are unchanged, and at least one of those unchanged coefficients is positive. Since a root has coefficients all of one sign, the image cannot be negative. Thus sα​ permutes Φ+∖{α}, as stated by action of a simple reflection on positive roots.
