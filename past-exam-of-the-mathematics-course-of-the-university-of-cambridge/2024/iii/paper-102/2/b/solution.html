Use the nondegenerate restriction of the Killing form to t to define tα​∈t by
κ(tα​,t)=α(t)(t∈t).
(21)
Killing-form invariance and the root-space decomposition show that gα​ pairs nondegenerately with g−α​ and orthogonally with every other root space. Choose nonzero e∈gα​ and f∈g−α​ with κ(e,f)=0. For t∈t,
κ([e,f],t)=κ(e,[f,t])=α(t)κ(e,f),
(22)
so
[e,f]=κ(e,f)tα​=0.
(23)
We need α(tα​)=0. If it were zero, the span of e,f,tα​ would be a solvable Heisenberg-type Lie algebra with central commutator [e,f]. By Lie theorem, its adjoint action on g can be upper triangularized, so ad[e,f] is nilpotent. But [e,f]∈t, and elements of the Cartan subalgebra act semisimply; hence ad[e,f]=0. A semisimple Lie algebra has zero center, contradicting [e,f]=0.
Set
hα​=α(tα​)2tα​​.
(24)
Rescale f so that [eα​,fα​]=hα​. Since eα​ and fα​ lie in the α and −α root spaces,
[hα​,eα​]=2eα​,[hα​,fα​]=−2fα​.
(25)
Thus mα​=⟨eα​,hα​,fα​⟩ is the sl2 subalgebra associated with a root.
