Put U=X/σ. The standardized efficient score function for scale is
Λ(u)=−1−uf(u)f′(u)​.
(1)
An optimal B-robust scale M-estimator solves
∑i=1n​ψ(Xi​/σ)=0,
(2)
where the optimal bounded score has the clipped-score form
ψ(u)=clip(A{Λ(u)−z},−b,b).
(3)
The constants A,z enforce Fisher consistency, the chosen normalization, and the clipping bound b on the influence function.
For the standard normal distribution, f′(u)/f(u)=−u, hence Λ(u)=u2−1. The score therefore simplifies to
ψ(u)=clip(A(u2−1−z),−b,b),
(4)
with z chosen so that Eψ(Z)=0 for Z∼N(0,1).

Changing at most n−h observations leaves at least h original observations unchanged. At θ=0, each unchanged observation has residual yi​ and loss at most ρ(My​). The sum of the h smallest losses cannot exceed the sum over any chosen h observations, so
LZ′​(0)≤hρ(My​).
(10)
The L1 regularization term vanishes at zero.

Suppose for contradiction that ∥θ(Zγ,τ​)∥2​≤M uniformly in γ,τ. Choose γ>M+1, so part d applies to every fitted value and gives L(θ)≥ρ(τ). For this fixed γ, part c gives the competing bound

d OurBigBook.com
For P0​=N(−θ0​,1) and P1​=N(θ0​,1), the nominal log-likelihood ratio for one observation is 2θ0​x. Under sufficiently small epsilon-contamination neighborhoods, the least-favourable pair clips this likelihood ratio between two constants. Symmetry turns its log into a positive multiple of the winsorized score
qc​(x)=clip(x,−c,c).
(28)
The robust form of the Neyman-Pearson lemma therefore rejects for large values of
∑i=1n​qc​(Xi​),
(29)
with boundary randomization and threshold chosen so that the worst-case null rejection probability is α. Extreme observations contribute only ±c, preventing a few contaminants from dominating the test.
