For a commutative ring R, the Jacobson radical is
J(R)=⋂m∈MaxSpecR​m.
(11)
Let A⊆B be integral. If n is maximal in B, then its contraction n∩A is maximal in A. Therefore every a∈J(A) belongs to every n, and
J(A)⊆J(B)∩A.
(12)
Conversely, the Lying-over theorem puts a maximal ideal n of B above every maximal ideal m of A. Hence an element of J(B)∩A lies in every m, proving
J(A)=J(B)∩A​.
(13)
This is the Jacobson radical under an integral extension formula.
