For the simple symmetric random walk, Sn​ is a martingale and the square-minus-time martingale of a simple symmetric random walk is
Mn​=Sn2​−n
(1)
Indeed, conditioning on Fn​ and using E[Xn+1​]=0 and Xn+12​=1 gives E[Sn+12​∣Fn​]=Sn2​+1.
Apply the optional sampling theorem for a supermartingale to the bounded stopping time T∧n:
E[ST∧n2​]=E[T∧n]≤E[T].
(2)
Thus the stopped martingale (ST∧n​) is bounded in L2. The L2 martingale convergence theorem gives convergence in L2, and because T<∞ almost surely its limit is ST​. Meanwhile the monotone convergence theorem gives E[T∧n]→E[T]. Therefore
E[ST2​]=E[T].
(3)
Finite mean is essential. Let T=inf{n≥1:Sn​=0} be the first return to zero. The one-dimensional simple symmetric random walk is recurrent, so T<∞ almost surely, but its first-return time has infinite mean. Since ST​=0,
E[ST2​]=0=∞=E[T].​
