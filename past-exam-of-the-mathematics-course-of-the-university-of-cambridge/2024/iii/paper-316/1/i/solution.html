OurBigBook.com Words: 135
Put the star at the origin, take z^ along the line of sight toward the observer, and let x^ point North in the plane of the sky. The y^​ direction is then 90∘ anticlockwise from North. Draw the ascending node on the sky plane at position angle Ω, tilt the circular Kepler orbit through the orbital inclination I about that nodal line, and place the planet an angle f along the orbit from the ascending node. The direction of increasing f must cross the sky plane toward +z^ at f=0.
A convenient orthonormal basis in the orbital plane is
e1​=(cosΩ,sinΩ,0),e2​=(−sinΩcosI,cosΩcosI,sinI).
(1)
Here e1​ points along the nodal line and e2​ fixes the required sense of motion. The planet lies at r=a(cosfe1​+sinfe2​); this relation also specifies all the labels required in the sketch.
