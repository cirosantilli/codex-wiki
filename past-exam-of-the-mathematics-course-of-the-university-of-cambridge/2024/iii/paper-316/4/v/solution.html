At conjunction, the planetesimal's mean anomaly is
Mc​=λc​−ϖ=3ϕ1​+2πk​,k=0,1,2.
(73)
If ϕ1​=π+δ with ∣δ∣≤Δϕ1​≤π, the conjunction branch that can come closest to periapsis has
∣Mc​∣min​=3π−Δϕ1​​​.
(74)
Let Emin​ solve Kepler's equation
Emin​−esinEmin​=3π−Δϕ1​​.
(75)
At that phase the orbital radius is rmin,c​=a(1−ecosEmin​). A geometrical close encounter is possible only if
a(1−ecosEmin​)≲a1​+Renc​​,
(76)
where Renc​ may be chosen as the planet's Hill radius or another encounter distance. With a point planet, set Renc​=0. This implicit inequality is the requested eccentricity constraint as a function of Δϕ1​.
The weaker necessary condition that the orbits cross is
e≥1−aa1​​=1−(85​)2/3​.
(77)
It becomes sufficient for phase access only as Δϕ1​→π, when a conjunction can approach periapsis. Smaller libration amplitude keeps conjunctions farther from periapsis and requires a larger eccentricity than this orbit-crossing bound.
