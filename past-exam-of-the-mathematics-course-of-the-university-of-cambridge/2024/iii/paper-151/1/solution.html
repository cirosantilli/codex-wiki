Let P∙​→Z be a projective resolution of the trivial ZG-module. The projective-resolution definition of group cohomology is
Hn(G,M)=Hn(HomZG​(P∙​,M)).
(1)
This is independent, up to a natural isomorphism, of the chosen projective resolution.
The degreewise natural isomorphisms
HomZG​(Pj​,M1​⊕M2​)≅HomZG​(Pj​,M1​)⊕HomZG​(Pj​,M2​)
(2)
commute with the coboundary maps. Taking cohomology proves that group cohomology commutes with finite direct sums:
Hn(G,M1​⊕M2​)≅Hn(G,M1​)⊕Hn(G,M2​).
(3)
Now restrict P∙​ from G to a subgroup K. The group ring ZG is free as a ZK-module, so restriction carries free modules to free modules and projective modules to projective modules. Thus the restricted complex is a projective resolution of the trivial ZK-module. For the coinduced module Y=HomZK​(ZG,X), the Hom functor adjunction for a coinduced module gives an isomorphism of cochain complexes
HomZG​(P∙​,Y)≅HomZK​(P∙​,X).
(4)
Explicitly, a map F is sent to p↦F(p)(1); the inverse sends a ZK-linear map a to p↦(r↦a(rp)). Taking cohomology proves Shapiro's lemma:
Hn(G,Y)≅Hn(K,X).
(5)
For the conjugation module of a group ring N=ZGconj​, the basis G is the disjoint union of its conjugacy classes. Hence N is the direct sum of the integral permutation modules on those classes. The class of a representative gi​ is the transitive G-set G/CG​(gi​), where CG​(gi​) is its centralizer. Since G is finite, this permutation module is both induced and coinduced from the trivial CG​(gi​)-module Z. Applying group cohomology commutes with finite direct sums and Shapiro's lemma yields the group cohomology of a conjugation module:
Hn(G,N)≅⨁i​Hn(CG​(gi​),Z).
(6)
The symmetric group S3​ has three conjugacy classes, represented by the identity, a transposition, and a three-cycle. Their centralizers are respectively
S3​,C2​,C3​.
(7)
For any finite group L acting trivially on Z,
H1(L,Z)=Hom(L,Z)=0,
(8)
because a group homomorphism sends an element of finite order to an element of finite order, while the additive group of the integers contains no nonzero torsion elements. Therefore
H1(S3​,N)=0.
(9)
The periodic resolution of a finite cyclic group alternates the maps t−1 and 1+t+⋯+tm−1. After applying HomZCm​​(−,Z) with the trivial action, these become alternately zero and multiplication by m, proving
H2(Cm​,Z)≅Z/mZ.
(10)
Combining this calculation with the supplied H2(S3​,Z)≅Z/2Z gives
H2(S3​,N)≅Z/2Z⊕Z/2Z⊕Z/3Z.​
(11)
