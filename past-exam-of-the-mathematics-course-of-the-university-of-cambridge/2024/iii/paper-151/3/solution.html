For a,b∈I, the square-zero ideal condition gives
(1+a)(1+b)=1+a+b+ab=1+a+b,(1+a)−1=1−a.
(20)
Thus the square-zero unit subgroup 1+I is abelian, and
Iadd​⟶1+I,a⟼1+a
(21)
is a group isomorphism from the additive group of I.
Use the specified ring isomorphism R/I≅ZG. For x∈ZG, choose a lift r∈R and define x⋅a=ra for a∈I. Two lifts differ by an element of I, whose product with a vanishes, so this is well defined. Right multiplication is handled identically. The two actions commute by associativity, making I a bimodule. If u∈R lifts g∈G, then u is a unit: a lift v of g−1 makes both uv and vu elements of 1+I, hence units, and a ring element with both a left and a right inverse is invertible. Conjugation therefore defines
g⋅a=uau−1.
(22)
Changing u by an element of I does not change this expression because I2=0. Moreover,
u(1+a)u−1=1+uau−1,
(23)
so a↦1+a is an isomorphism of ZG-modules for these conjugation actions.
Let R×→(R/I)× be reduction on unit groups, and define U as the inverse image of the distinguished subgroup G⊆(ZG)×. Every g∈G has a unit lift by the preceding argument, and the kernel consists exactly of the units congruent to 1, namely 1+I. Multiplication in R therefore gives the group extension
1⟶1+I⟶U⟶G⟶1.
(24)
Choose a set-theoretic section s:G→U with s(1)=1. Its extension cocycle
c(g,h)=s(g)s(h)s(gh)−1∈1+I
(25)
satisfies the two-cocycle identity by associativity. A different section changes c by a group coboundary, so second group cohomology classifies group extensions gives a well-defined class
x=[c]∈H2(G,1+I).
(26)
The same construction for (R1​,I1​) gives U1​, an extension cocycle c1​, and x1​=[c1​]∈H2(G,1+I1​).
The answer to the final question is no. An abstract ring isomorphism R1​≅R need not carry the distinguished ideal I1​ to I, need not induce the identity under the two chosen identifications of the quotient rings with ZG, and need not induce the prescribed ZG-module isomorphism θ. Hence it need not give an isomorphism of the two displayed group extensions, so it imposes no equality ϕ(x1​)=x. That equality does hold if the ring isomorphism has all three compatibility properties, because it then carries one extension cocycle to the other up to a group coboundary.
