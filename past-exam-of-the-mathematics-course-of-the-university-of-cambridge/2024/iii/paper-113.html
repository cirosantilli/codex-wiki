A morphism f:X→S is a separated morphism when its diagonal morphism
ΔX/S​:X⟶X×S​X
(1)
is a closed immersion.
For the requested example, take the affine plane with doubled origin: glue two copies U,V≅Ak2​ by the identity on
U∩V≅Ak2​∖{0}.
(2)
The opens U and V are affine, while their intersection is the punctured affine plane, which is not affine. Indeed, its regular functions are still k[x,y]; if it were affine, the canonical map to Speck[x,y]=Ak2​ would be an isomorphism, contrary to the missing origin. The resulting scheme is not separated: in a separated scheme, the intersection of two affine opens is the inverse image of the closed diagonal inside their affine product and is therefore affine.

On the standard charts D+​(x) and D+​(y) of Pk1​, a section of the twisting sheaf on projective space O(d) is represented by a degree-zero element of the corresponding localization of k[x,y](d). A global section is therefore a homogeneous polynomial of degree d when d≥0. There are no nonzero global sections for d<0. Hence
dimk​H0(Pk1​,O(d))={d+1,0,​d≥0,d<0.​​
(3)
Local isomorphism on every member of a fixed affine cover does not imply a global isomorphism: the local identifications may have different transition functions. For example, O and O(1) are both trivial on the two standard affine charts of P1, but they are not isomorphic because their spaces of global sections have dimensions one and two.
An injective map between line bundles need not be an isomorphism. Multiplication by a nonzero section gives
O(−1)↪O
(4)
on P1; its cokernel is a nonzero skyscraper sheaf supported at the zero of the section.

The fibre product of schemes X×S​Y comes with projections to X and Y having equal composites to S, and is universal with this property. A morphism is universally closed when every base change is closed on underlying topological spaces.
The structural morphism Ak1​→Speck is closed because its target has one point, but it is not universally closed. After base change by Ak1​, it becomes the projection Ak2​→Ak1​; the closed hyperbola V(xy−1) has image D(x), which is not closed.
For a finite-type universally closed nonseparated example, glue two copies of Pk1​ along the complement of one rational point, producing a projective line with a doubled point. It is of finite type. After every base change, each of its two projective-line charts maps closedly to the base, so the image of any closed subset, being the union of the two closed images, is closed. Thus the structural morphism is universally closed. It is not separated because the two doubled points violate uniqueness in the valuative criterion for separatedness, or equivalently because its diagonal is not closed.

Fix x∈X and choose affine opens x∈U⊆X and f(x)∈W⊆S with f(U)⊆W. The open subset U×W​U of X×S​X contains Δ(x). If U=SpecA and W=SpecR, its diagonal is induced by the surjection
A⊗R​A⟶A,a⊗b⟼ab,
(9)
so it is a closed immersion. Hence every diagonal morphism is locally a closed immersion into an open subset, and therefore is a locally closed immersion.
For Ak1​, the product is Ak2​=Speck[x,y] and the diagonal is V(x−y). Its complement is the principal affine open
D(x−y)=Speck[x,y,(x−y)−1].​
(10)
Take instead the separated scheme X=Ak2​. The complement of its diagonal in X×k​X≅Ak4​ is Ak4​∖Ak2​, where the removed diagonal has codimension two. Its global functions still form the polynomial ring of Ak4​. Were the complement affine, its canonical morphism to Spec of this ring would identify it with all of Ak4​, which is impossible. Thus this diagonal complement is not affine.

For a Noetherian separated integral scheme regular in codimension one, a Weil divisor is a finite integer combination of integral codimension-one closed subschemes. Principal divisors are the valuation divisors of nonzero rational functions, and the divisor class group is
Cl(X)=Div(X)/Prin(X).
(11)
For an open immersion U⊆X, restriction induces a surjection
Cl(X)↠Cl(U):
(12)
every prime divisor of U closes to a prime divisor of X, while divisors supported in X∖U form the kernel. Therefore Cl(X)=0 implies Cl(U)=0.
Affine schemes need not have trivial class group. For example,
X=Speck[x,y,z]/(xy−z2)
(13)
is a normal affine surface with Cl(X)≅Z/2Z. The height-one prime (x,z) represents the nonzero class: twice this divisor is the principal divisor of x, but the divisor itself is not principal.

The complement Y is covered by the m principal affine opens D(f1​),…,D(fm​). Every finite intersection is again a principal affine open, so this is an acyclic cover for OY​. Its Čech cochain complex has no degree-m term because there is no intersection of m+1 distinct cover members. Therefore
Hm(Y,OY​)=0.​
(16)
Translate p to the origin. The complement Ak3​∖{0} has the affine cover D(x),D(y),D(z), and its second Čech cohomology is
k[x±1,y±1,z]+k[x±1,y,z±1]+k[x,y±1,z±1]k[x±1,y±1,z±1]​.
(17)
The class of x−1y−1z−1 is nonzero, so H2(Ak3​∖{p},O)=0. On the other hand, Ak3​∖ℓ=D(x)∪D(y), and the first part with m=2 gives H2(Ak3​∖ℓ,O)=0. Since sheaf cohomology is invariant under scheme isomorphism, the two complements are not isomorphic.

Two nonisomorphic punctual schemes are
SpeckandSpeck[ε]/(ε2).
(18)
Their underlying spaces each have one point, but the second has a nonzero nilpotent element and the first is reduced.
After translating their common support to the origin, punctual closed subschemes of Ak1​ correspond to ideals of k[t] whose radical is (t). Since k[t] is a principal ideal domain, each such ideal is (tr) for a unique r≥1. Its coordinate ring has basis 1,t,…,tr−1 and hence dimension r. Equal dimensions force equal exponents, so Z and Z′ are in fact the same closed subscheme after the common coordinate choice, and in particular are isomorphic.
In Ak2​, the ideals
I=(x,y2),I′=(x2,y)
(19)
define distinct punctual closed subschemes supported at the origin. Both quotient rings have dimension two over k, with bases 1,y and 1,x respectively. Thus they have equal-dimensional global-section spaces despite being distinct embedded closed subschemes.
