Associate a nonnegative Lagrange multiplier λi​ with each inequality. The Lagrangian dual problem begins with
L(x,λ)=cTx+λT(Ax−b)=(c+ATλ)Tx−bTλ,λ≥0.
(6)
Its infimum over x∈Rn is finite exactly when ATλ+c=0, in which case it equals −bTλ. The dual linear program is consequently
λ∈Rmmax​{−bTλ:ATλ+c=0, λ≥0}​.
(7)

−bTλ≤−xTATλ=cTx,

Let λ∗ be an optimal dual multiplier and define the maximum violation
v(x)=max{0,(Ax−b)1​,…,(Ax−b)m​}.
(9)
Dual stationarity and strong duality imply, for every x,
cTx+(λ∗)T(Ax−b)=−bTλ∗=p∗.
(10)
Since every component of Ax−b is at most v(x) and λ∗≥0,
(λ∗)T(Ax−b)≤∥λ∗∥1​v(x).
(11)
It follows that the exact maximum-violation penalty obeys
cTx+Mv(x)≥p∗+(M−∥λ∗∥1​)v(x).

The objective is strictly convex, so the minimizer is unique. The Slater condition makes the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions necessary and sufficient. Absorb the box constraints into the Euclidean projection onto a convex set and attach a scalar multiplier ν to aTx=b. Stationarity over the box is equivalent to
x∗=P[0,1]n​(y−νa),
(18)
while primal feasibility requires aTx∗=b. Coordinatewise, these conditions are
xi∗​=min{1,max{0,yi​−νai​}}​,i=1∑n​ai​min{1,max{0,yi​−νai​}}=b​.
(19)
They are also sufficient because they minimize the Lagrangian over the box and satisfy the equality constraint. Thus the projection onto a box-constrained hyperplane reduces to solving the displayed one-dimensional continuous, nonincreasing equation for ν. The multiplier need not be unique on a flat interval, but the projected vector is unique.
