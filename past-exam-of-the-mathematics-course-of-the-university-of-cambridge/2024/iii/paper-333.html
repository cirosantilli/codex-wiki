At latitude θ0​, resolve the planetary angular velocity as
Ω=(0,Ωcosθ0​,Ωsinθ0​).
(1)
For velocity (u,v,w), the Coriolis acceleration is
2Ω×u=2Ω(wcosθ0​−vsinθ0​,usinθ0​,−ucosθ0​).
(2)
The traditional approximation drops the terms involving the horizontal rotation component Ωcosθ0​. The retained horizontal force is therefore
fz×uh​,f=2Ωsinθ0​.
(3)
Its direct velocity-scale requirement is
Wcosθ0​≪Usinθ0​.
(4)
For an incompressible flow, W/U=O(H/L), so a sufficient condition is
LH​∣cotθ0​∣≪1​,
(5)
together with the small aspect ratio that makes hydrostatic pressure the leading vertical momentum balance. The approximation consequently becomes delicate near the equator.
The beta plane is the local Taylor expansion
f(y)=2Ωsin(θ0​+y/a)=f0​+βy+O(ΩL2/a2),
(6)
where
f0​=2Ωsinθ0​,β=a2Ωcosθ0​​,
(7)
and a is the planetary radius. It requires a local Cartesian region,
L/a≪1​,
(8)
and H/a≪1. Away from the equator, treating the variation as a perturbation of an f-plane also requires βL/∣f0​∣≪1; near the equator one instead retains the linear term as the leading Coriolis parameter.

Assume an inviscid homogeneous ocean, no horizontal pressure gradient, and no wind stress. Horizontal uniformity then removes advective acceleration, and the parcel equations on a beta plane are
u˙−f(y)v=0,v˙+f(y)u=0,f(y)=f0​+βy,
(9)
with u=x˙ and v=y˙​.
Since u˙=f(y)y˙​, integration from the stated initial data gives
x˙=u=f0​y+21​βy2​.
(10)
This is conservation of the parcel's zonal canonical momentum: its zonal speed records the meridionally accumulated Coriolis acceleration. Multiplying the two momentum equations by u and v and adding gives
dtd​2u2+v2​=0.
(11)
Thus kinetic energy and speed are constant:
x˙2+y˙​2=V2​.
(12)
Eliminating x˙ gives the required ordinary differential equation
y˙​2=V2−(f0​y+21​βy2)2​.
(13)
For f0​,β>0, the equator is at ye​=−f0​/β. There
f0​ye​+21​βye2​=−2βf02​​.
(14)
The parcel must encounter a meridional turning point before reaching the equator if it is to remain strictly in the Northern Hemisphere. The energy relation therefore requires
V<2βf02​​​,
(15)
or βV/f02​<1/2. Equality is the limiting trajectory that reaches the equator with zero meridional speed.

Let D=H−h be the constant interior depth. Assume a homogeneous hydrostatic interior with depth-independent horizontal velocity, negligible friction, steady small-Rossby number flow, and no normal flow through the flat bottom. The vertical velocity varies from w(−H)=0 to w(−h)=wE​, so incompressible flow gives
ux​+vy​=−DwE​​.
(33)
The leading horizontal momentum balance is geostrophic balance:
−fv=−ρ1​px​,fu=−ρ1​py​.
(34)

f(ux​+vy​)+βv=0.
(35)
Combining the last two equations yields Sverdrup balance
βDv=fwE​​,v=βDf​wE​​.
(36)

Put D(x,y)=H(x,y)−h(x,y) and let u=(u,v) be the depth-independent interior velocity. The kinematic boundary conditions on the sloping upper and lower surfaces are
wt​=wE​−u⋅∇h,wb​=−u⋅∇H.
(39)
Integrating mass conservation through the layer gives
DtDD​+D∇h​⋅u=−wE​.
(40)
The inviscid vertical-vorticity equation is
DtD​(f+ζ)=−(f+ζ)∇h​⋅u,ζ=vx​−uy​.
(41)
Consequently the shallow-water potential vorticity
q=Df+ζ​
(42)
obeys the forced evolution equation
DtDq​=Dq​wE​​.
(43)
Positive upward Ekman pumping removes layer thickness and raises the potential vorticity of the remaining column.
For a steady small-Rossby number flow, q≃f/D, so
u⋅∇(Df​)=D2f​wE​.
(44)
Equivalently,
βDv−fu⋅∇D=fwE​​.
(45)
The same result follows from the integrated vortex-stretching balance
βDv=f(wt​−wb​).
(46)
When ∇D=(c,0) with c>0,
βDv−fcu=fwE​.
(47)
If the upper pumping is absent or weak and the upper surface is locally level, the impermeable-bottom condition is wb​=−cu. The stipulated wb​<0 then gives u>0, and in the Northern Hemisphere
v≃βDfc​u>0.
(48)
The steady interior flow is therefore directed northeastward, along contours of f/D in the unforced limit. As a parcel moves eastward into deeper water, its vortex column stretches; a poleward displacement increases f and preserves potential vorticity. Nonzero Ekman pumping drives motion across the f/D contours. This is topographic potential-vorticity steering and the associated topographic Sverdrup balance.

For an inviscid Boussinesq approximation fluid in a nonrotating frame, write buoyancy as σ and kinematic pressure as p. The governing equations are
DtDu​=−∇p+σz,DtDσ​=0,∇⋅u=0.
(49)
They require density variations to be small compared with a constant reference density, while retaining those variations in buoyancy; the flow scale must be small compared with the background density scale height. Ideal flow additionally neglects viscosity and scalar diffusion.

u=u(z)x,dzdσ​=N2(z)>0,
(50)

Dt​=∂t​+u∂x​.

For a ridge-fixed disturbance, c=0. If the Scorer parameter varies on a height scale much longer than the vertical wavelength, the local WKB approximation gives
w∼m−1/2exp(±i∫zm(s)ds)
(65)
where m2=l2−k2>0. The disturbance is vertically oscillatory there and can carry wave activity upward. Where l2<k2, m is imaginary and the solution is vertically evanescent.
A rigid ridge supplies the lower boundary condition and an upper radiation or decay condition selects the physical solution. If l2 decreases through k2, the crossing is a turning level: the wave is reflected or decays above it and is trapped beneath it. Increasing u normally reduces N2/u2, while decreasing N2 reduces it directly; subject to the curvature term −uzz​/u, either change therefore promotes vertical trapping of an atmospheric gravity wave.

Begin with the Boussinesq approximation primitive equations on a beta plane, decompose every field into a zonal mean and a disturbance, and average over longitude. The zonal momentum equation then contains the divergence of the eddy momentum flux u′v′, while the mean density equation contains the divergence of the eddy density flux ρ′v′​. At small Rossby number, use geostrophic balance, hydrostatic pressure, thermal-wind balance, and the leading eddy equations to combine those fluxes.
Define
A=dρs​/dzρ′v′​​
(76)
and introduce the residual mean circulation
va∗​=va​−Az​,wa∗​=wa​+Ay​​.
(77)
The added eddy-induced velocity is nondivergent, so
va,y∗​+wa,z∗​=0.
(78)
It absorbs the eddy density-flux divergence into advection by the transformed circulation, giving
ρ​t​+overlinewa∗​dzdρs​​=0.
(79)
The mean zonal momentum equation becomes
ut​−f0​va∗​=∇⋅F,
(80)
where the zonally averaged Eliassen–Palm flux in the meridional-vertical plane is
F(y)=−u′v′,F(z)=f0​dρs​/dzρ′v′​​​.
(81)
Thus the transformed Eulerian mean gathers the wave forcing into one flux divergence and makes density evolve under one residual circulation.

Define the Eulerian-mean streamfunction by
(va​,wa​)=(χ​a,z​,−χ​a,y​).
(111)
With
A=dρs​/dzρ′v′​​=f0​F(z)​,
(112)
the transformation in part a gives
va∗​=(χ​a​−A)z​,wa∗​=−(χ​a​−A)y​.
(113)
Hence, up to an irrelevant additive constant,
χ​a​=χ​a∗​+f0​F(z)​​.
(114)
For the step-profile flux,
χ​a​=χ​a∗​+f0​F0​​Θ(z)sin2Lπy​​.
(115)
The residual streamfunction χ​a∗​ vanishes on the rigid boundaries, decays away from the absorption level, and has opposite-signed values immediately below and above z=H. Its vertical derivative gives a zonal acceleration ut​ concentrated around H and largest near the channel center. Its meridional derivative gives a dipolar density tendency: ρ​t​ changes sign across the channel center and reverses vertical structure across the absorption level.
The eddy term in χ​a​ has a compensating downward jump at H, so the Eulerian-mean streamfunction is continuous even though χ​a∗​ jumps in the idealized step limit. Below the critical layer, the Eulerian view contains a broad circulation associated with the eddy density flux; the transformed view subtracts that reversible eddy-induced motion and isolates the residual circulation forced where the waves dissipate.
In the Eulerian density budget, vertical advection by wa​ and the divergence of ρ′v′​ can be individually large and largely cancel. The transformed Eulerian mean combines them into advection by wa∗​, making the irreversible mean response to wave-activity deposition much clearer.

f02​D2χ∗​,N2L2χ∗​.

f02​χzz∗​≃−f0​(∇⋅F)z​.
