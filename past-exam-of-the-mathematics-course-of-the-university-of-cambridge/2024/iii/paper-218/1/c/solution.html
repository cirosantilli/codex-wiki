The Pearson goodness-of-fit test has null hypothesis that the independent counts follow the fitted Poisson regression, in particular Var(Yi​∣di​)=μi​, against the alternative that the model does not fit; in this setting the scientifically relevant direction is overdispersion, Var(Yi​∣di​)>μi​. Under the null, XP2​ is approximately chi-squared distribution with 16 degrees of freedom. Its tiny p-value decisively rejects the Poisson variance assumption.
The negative binomial regression keeps the logarithmic mean model but allows Var(Yi​∣di​)=μi​+μi2​/θ. It improves the residual deviance from 75.806 to 18.011, close to its 16 residual degrees of freedom, and lowers the Akaike information criterion from 172.34 to 141.66. Both comparisons strongly favour the negative-binomial fit, although its dose coefficient remains statistically insignificant.
