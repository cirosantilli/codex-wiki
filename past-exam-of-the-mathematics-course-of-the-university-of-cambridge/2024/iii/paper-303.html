The equilibrium magnetization is a global minimum of the Landau free energy. Its stationary values solve the polynomial equation
∂m∂f​=4a4​m3+6a6​m5−B=0,
(1)
and a local minimum must satisfy
∂m2∂2f​=12a4​m2+30a6​m4≥0.
(2)
One compares the value of f at every such local minimum and chooses the smallest. Since a6​>0, the polynomial tends to positive infinity as ∣m∣ tends to infinity, so a global minimum exists.

Put t=T−Tc​, so a4​∼t. At zero field the stationary equation factors as
2m3(2a4​+3a6​m2)=0.
(3)
For t>0, m=0 is the unique minimum. For t<0, it is unstable and the two minima are
m±​=±−3a6​2a4​​​.
(4)
The order parameter therefore tends continuously to zero, and its order-parameter critical exponent is β=1/2​.
At either ordered minimum the singular free-energy density is
fmin​=a4​m±4​+a6​m±6​=27a62​4a43​​∼−∣t∣3,
(5)
whereas it is zero for t>0. Two temperature derivatives give a singular heat capacity proportional to ∣t∣ below the transition and zero above it, so the heat-capacity critical exponent is α=−1​.
The inverse magnetic susceptibility at a stable minimum is the curvature χ−1=∂2f/∂m2. Below Tc​,
χ−−1​=12a4​m±2​+30a6​m±4​=3a6​16a42​​,
(6)
and hence χ−​∼∣t∣−2 and γ−​=2​. Above Tc​, however, the curvature at m=0 vanishes for every t>0. Indeed, at small field B=4a4​m3+O(m5), so m∼(B/(4a4​))1/3 and the linear susceptibility is already infinite away from the critical point. Consequently the usual magnetic-susceptibility critical exponent γ+​ is not defined for this exceptional free energy; assigning it a finite value would incorrectly assume a quadratic term.
At T=Tc​, the equation of state is B=6a6​m5, so m∼∣B∣1/5 and the critical-isotherm exponent is δ=5​. Thus the transition is continuous, although its missing quadratic term makes the high-temperature linear response singular throughout that phase.

For every T<Tc​, the two zero-field minima m+​ and m−​ coexist. A positive field selects m+​ and a negative field selects m−​, so crossing B=0 makes the equilibrium magnetization jump between nonzero values. Hence B=0, T<Tc​, is a line of first-order phase transitions, ending at the continuous critical point (Tc​,0).

iv OurBigBook.com
For finite p=4, the clock model has a discrete symmetry. Domain walls have finite energy per unit boundary area, so thermal disorder destroys long-range order in one dimension but a finite-temperature ordered phase can exist in two dimensions. Its lower critical dimension is therefore dl​=1​.
As p→∞, the permitted angles become continuous and the model becomes the XY model with O(2) symmetry. The Mermin-Wagner theorem forbids spontaneous long-range order at positive temperature in two dimensions, so the lower critical dimension for conventional symmetry breaking is dl​=2​. The two-dimensional model can nevertheless undergo a Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless transition between algebraic and exponential correlation decay.

a OurBigBook.com
A momentum-shell renormalization group step has three parts. First split the Fourier transform of the field into slow modes ϕ<​ with ∣k∣<Λ/ζ and fast modes ϕ>​ with Λ/ζ<∣k∣<Λ, then perform the functional integral over ϕ>​. Second rescale momenta by k′=ζk, equivalently coordinates by x′=x/ζ, to restore the cutoff from Λ/ζ to Λ. Third rescale the field so that the coefficient of (∇ϕ)2/2 again has its chosen normalization. The effective free energy contains every operator allowed by the symmetries, with transformed coefficients. Repeating the step composes these coefficient maps and produces a renormalization-group flow.

Classify the connected contractions by the numbers (r,4−r) of external slow legs on the two sextic vertices and by the number c of fast propagators joining them. Besides the displayed (2,2;c=4) graph, the distinct topologies are:
  • (2,2;c=2): two joining lines and one tadpole on each vertex.
  • (1,3;c=3): three joining lines and one tadpole on the one-external-leg vertex.
  • (0,4;c=2): two joining lines and two tadpoles on the vertex with no external legs.
  • (1,3;c=1): one joining line, two tadpoles on the one-external-leg vertex, and one tadpole on the three-external-leg vertex.
Exchanging the two vertices gives no new topology. All four are connected Feynman diagrams selected by the logarithm in the cumulant expansion. With an ideal sharp momentum shell and a projection at exactly zero external momentum, the single joining line in the last topology cannot carry shell momentum, so that topology gives zero to the local quartic coupling; it is still the remaining formal connected contraction.

When all masses are equal, the free energy depends on the real vector ϕ only through inner products, so its symmetry is the orthogonal group O(N). The mean-field potential is
V(ϕ)=21​μ02​ϕ2+g0​(ϕ2)2.
(28)
For μ02​>0, its unique minimum is ϕ=0, which preserves O(N). For μ02​<0, the minima form the sphere
ϕ2=−4g0​μ02​​.
(29)
Choosing one minimum leaves the subgroup O(N−1) that fixes its direction, so spontaneous symmetry breaking gives O(N)→O(N−1)​.

Write ra​=μ0,a2​. The stationary equations for
V=21​r1​ϕ12​+21​r2​ϕ22​+g0​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)2
(41)
are
ϕa​[ra​+4g0​(ϕ12​+ϕ22​)]=0.

The origin, where the two continuous lines meet the first-order line, is a bicritical point.
