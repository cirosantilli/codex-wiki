For A∈C and F:C→Set, the Yoneda lemma gives a natural bijection
Nat(C(A,−),F)≅F(A),α⟼αA​(1A​).
(1)
Assume C is small. For every pair (A,x) with x∈F(A), let αA,x:C(A,−)→F be the corresponding natural transformation. Their copairing is
∐A∈C​∐x∈F(A)​C(A,−)⟶F.
(2)
At an object B, the element x∈F(B) is the image of 1B​ in the summand indexed by (B,x). The map is therefore pointwise surjective and hence an epimorphism in the functor category.

Let P:E→D be a discrete fibration and let m:A→A′ be monic in E. If u,v:B→P(A) satisfy P(m)u=P(m)v, lift u,v uniquely to u,v with codomain A. The composites mu,mv are lifts of the same arrow with codomain A′, so uniqueness gives equality. Since m is monic, u=v, hence u=v. Thus P(m) is monic.
Use the convention that (F↓B) has objects (A,u:FA→B). Its forgetful functor sends (A,u) to A. Given h:C→A, the unique arrow above h with codomain (A,u) has domain (C,uFh). Hence the forgetful functor is a discrete fibration.

Assume every morphism of C is monic. For a representable presheaf F=C(−,A), the category (1↓F) is the category of elements, equivalently the slice C/A. A morphism from f:B→A to g:C→A is an h:B→C satisfying gh=f. Since g is monic, there is at most one such h. Thus (1↓F) is a preorder.

Suppose a preorder P admits a discrete fibration q:P→C that is surjective on objects. Given f:B→A in C, choose a∈P above A. The discrete-fibration property lifts f to f​:b→a. Every arrow in a preorder is monic, and a discrete fibration preserves monomorphisms by part (b), so f=q(f​) is monic. This proves the remaining implication and hence the equivalence.

A categorical limit of D:J→C is a terminal cone (L,(pj​)): every cone (X,(xj​)) has a unique map X→L commuting with all legs.
Assume all small products and equalizers exist. Put
P=∏j∈J​D(j),Q=∏u:i→j​D(j).
(4)
Define s,t:P⇉Q so that the u:i→j components are
su​=D(u)πi​,tu​=πj​.
(5)
A map X→P equalizes s,t exactly when its components form a cone over D. Therefore eq(s,t) represents cones and is the limit. This is the construction of small limits from products and equalizers.

Let D:J→Fld be connected and nonempty. The limit of the underlying diagram of commutative rings is the subring
L={(xj​)∈∏j​D(j):D(u)(xi​)=xj​ for every u:i→j}.
(6)
If x∈L is nonzero in one component, it is nonzero in every component: field homomorphisms are injective, and connectedness propagates this fact along zigzags. Hence the componentwise inverses (xj−1​) are defined and compatible. Thus L is a field. Since the inclusion Fld↪CRng is full, the same cone is limiting in the category of fields.
If J is disconnected, choose two components and use the constant field F2​ on one and F3​ on the other. There is no cone in fields because its apex would map to fields of two different characteristics. Hence Fld does not have all limits of any disconnected shape.

Consider one connected component K of the category of cocones under a small diagram D:J→Fld. Replace every apex by the subfield generated by the images of the fields D(j). These generated fields have cardinality bounded in terms of the small diagram, so they admit a small skeleton, cofinal within K.
The apex functor on this small connected category has a limit LK​ in Fld by part (b). For each j, the maps from D(j) to all apexes form a compatible cone and therefore induce D(j)→LK​. These maps form a cocone λK​ under D. Its limiting projections give a unique morphism from λK​ to every cocone in K, so λK​ is initial in that component. Choosing one λK​ for each component gives a multicolimit.

The adjoint functor theorem for complete lattices says that a monotone map f:A→B between complete lattices preserves arbitrary joins exactly when it has a right adjoint
f∗(b)=⋁{a:f(a)≤b}.
(7)
A right adjoint preserves arbitrary meets. Regard it as the join-preserving map
f∗:Bop⟶Aop.
(8)
Thus define A∗=Aop and send f to its right adjoint. Since a left adjoint is the right adjoint of its right adjoint after reversing orders, (f∗)∗=f and (A∗)∗=A. This gives the involutive self-duality
(−)∗:CSLatop⟶CSLat.​
(9)

Let [A,B] be the set of arbitrary-join-preserving maps A→B, ordered pointwise. Pointwise joins remain join-preserving because the two joins may be interchanged:
(⋁i​fi​)(⋁j​aj​)=⋁i,j​fi​(aj​).
(10)
Precomposition and postcomposition preserve these joins, so (A,B)↦[A,B] is the required CSLat-valued hom functor.
Sending f:A→B to its right adjoint gives
[B∗,A∗]≅[A,B].
(11)
It is order-preserving because taking a right adjoint reverses pointwise order once, while the order on A∗ reverses it again.
A join map χa​:A→2 is associated with a∈A by
χa​(x)=0⟺x≤a.
(12)
Every join map to 2 is of this form, and a↦χa​ identifies A∗ with [A,2]. Finally, a map A→[B,C] is a function A×B→C preserving arbitrary joins separately in each variable. Swapping the variables gives naturally
[A,[B,C]]≅[B,[A,C]].​
(13)
The assumed expression of C as a limit of copies of 2 reduces the verification to the preceding 2-valued description.

For an adjunction F⊣U:D→C with induced monad T=UF, the Eilenberg-Moore comparison functor is
K:D→CT,K(D)=(UD,UεD​).
(14)
The adjunction is monadic when K is an equivalence.
The Crude monadicity theorem states that a right adjoint is monadic if it reflects isomorphisms, its source has coequalizers of reflexive pairs, and it preserves those coequalizers. The dual statement is the crude comonadicity theorem.

Assume the free functor reflects isomorphisms. If f,g:X⇉Y satisfy Ff=Fg, let q:Y→Q be their coequalizer in sets. As a left adjoint, F preserves it. Since the coequalizer of an equal pair is an identity, Fq is an isomorphism. Reflection makes q an isomorphism, so f=g. Thus F is faithful.

Let (A,a) be a T-algebra with at least two elements, and suppose Ff:FX→FY is an isomorphism. For every T-algebra A, precomposition with Ff gives a bijection
SetT(FY,A)⟶SetT(FX,A).
(16)
By the free-forgetful adjunction this is
Set(Y,UA)⟶Set(X,UA),h⟼hf.
(17)
For one set UA with at least two elements, bijectivity of this precomposition forces f to be bijective: surjectivity detects injectivity of f, and injectivity detects surjectivity. Thus f is an isomorphism. The free functor reflects isomorphisms, completing the cycle of equivalences.

Assume the equivalent conditions and that T preserves finite coproducts. The free functor already reflects isomorphisms. By the dual Crude monadicity theorem, it remains to preserve the relevant coreflexive equalizers.
A coreflexive equalizer diagram
A⟶B⇉C
(18)
with A=∅ can be equipped with the extra sections making it a split equalizer: choose a point of A and use the common retraction to define the missing splitting maps on the complementary fibres. Every functor preserves split equalizers. If A=∅, preservation follows from preservation of the initial object, which follows from preservation of finite coproducts. Consequently F preserves all required coreflexive equalizers, and the adjunction is comonadic.

Let (V,⊗,I) be a symmetric monoidal category. A V-enriched category has objects, hom-objects C(A,B)∈V, composition morphisms
C(B,C)⊗C(A,B)→C(A,C),
(19)
and unit morphisms I→C(A,A) satisfying the associative and unit diagrams. Its underlying ordinary category has hom-sets
V(I,C(A,B)).
(20)
If V is closed, take its internal hom [A,B] as hom-object. Composition is the transpose of evaluation
[B,C]⊗[A,B]⊗A→[B,C]⊗B→C,
(21)
and the unit is the transpose of I⊗A≅A. This is the self-enrichment of a closed symmetric monoidal category.

Identities are self-adjoint. If f⊣g and f′⊣g′, then
f′fgg′=f′(fg)g′≤f′g′≤1,1≤gf≤gg′f′f,
(24)
with the second inequality written after inserting the two units in the appropriate order. Hence f′f⊣gg′, so left adjoints form a subcategory.
In Poset, these are exactly monotone maps possessing right adjoints, equivalently lower adjoints; when all joins exist, they are precisely the arbitrary-join-preserving maps.
In the inclusion-ordered category of relations, a relation R:A⇝B is left adjoint exactly when it is total and single-valued. It is therefore the graph of a function, and its right adjoint is Rop. This is the left adjoint relation is a function criterion.

A complex in an abelian category is a sequence (An​,dn​) with dn​dn+1​=0. A sequence is exact when the image of every incoming map equals the kernel of the outgoing map.
The Five lemma says that in a morphism between exact five-term sequences, suitable epimorphism assumptions on the left and monomorphism assumptions on the right, together with isomorphisms in the four surrounding positions, force the middle map to be an isomorphism.
The Snake lemma associates to a morphism of short exact sequences the exact sequence
kerf′→kerf→kerf′′∂​cokerf′→cokerf→cokerf′′.​
(25)

Apply the Snake lemma degree by degree to a short exact sequence of complexes
0→A∙​→B∙​→C∙​→0.

If [c]∈Hn​(C), lift a cycle c to b∈Bn​. Its boundary maps to zero in Cn−1​, so it comes from a cycle a∈An−1​; define ∂[c]=[a]. The Snake-lemma exactness and independence checks yield

The commutative diagram of short exact sequences of complexes induces a commutative diagram between the two long exact homology sequences from part (b). If any two vertical chain maps induce isomorphisms in every degree, then in each five-term window four of the five vertical homology maps are isomorphisms. The Five lemma makes the remaining map an isomorphism. Rotating the window handles each of the three possible missing columns, proving the two-out-of-three assertion.
