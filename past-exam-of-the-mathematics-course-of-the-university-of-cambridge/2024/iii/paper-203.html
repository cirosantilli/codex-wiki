A compact H-hull is a bounded relatively closed set A⊂H for which H∖A is a simply connected domain. The Riemann mapping theorem and hydrodynamic normalization at infinity give a unique mapping-out function of a compact H-hull gA​:H∖A→H with
gA​(z)=z+za​+O(∣z∣−2).
(1)
The coefficient a≥0 is the half-plane capacity hcap(A).
For λ>0 and x∈R, uniqueness of the normalized map gives
gλA+x​(z)=x+λgA​(λz−x​).
(2)
Substituting the expansion of gA​ yields
gλA+x​(z)=z+z−xλ2a​+O(∣z∣−2)=z+zλ2a​+O(∣z∣−2).
(3)
Therefore the scaling and translation of half-plane capacity is
hcap(λA+x)=λ2hcap(A).​
(4)

Let d=diam(A). Unless A is empty, its closure meets the real axis; otherwise a loop in H∖A surrounding A could not contract, contrary to H∖A being a simply connected domain. Choose x∈A∩R. Then A⊆{z∈H:∣z−x∣≤d}.
The unit half-disc is a compact H-hull with mapping-out function z+z−1, so its half-plane capacity is one. The monotonicity of half-plane capacity and its scaling rule now give
hcap(A)≤hcap{z∈H:∣z−x∣≤d}=d2.
(5)
Thus the assertion holds with the universal constant c=1 under this normalization.

For Schramm–Loewner evolution in (H,0,∞), the Scaling invariance of SLE states that, for every a>0,
Kt​=a−1Ka2t​
(14)
has the same law as Kt​. The scaled Loewner driving function is Ut​=a−1Ua2t​. Since Ut​=κ​Bt​, the Brownian scaling identity a−1Ba2t​=dBt​ proves the claim.
The Conformal Markov property of SLE states that, conditionally on the hull through time t, the future hull mapped by gt​−Ut​ is an independent SLEκ​ in (H,0,∞). More precisely,
Ks​=(gt​(Kt+s​∖Kt​)−Ut​)fill
(15)
has driving function Us​=Ut+s​−Ut​. The stationary increments and independent increments of Brownian motion show that U is independent of Ft​ and has the same law as κ​B. The deterministic correspondence between continuous drivers and Loewner chains completes the proof.

Put w=gT−1​(UT​+z) and define
fs​(z)=gT−s​(w)−UT​.
(16)
Then f0​(z)=z. Differentiating with the Chordal Loewner equation gives
∂s​fs​(z)=−gT−s​(w)−UT−s​2​=−fs​(z)−(UT−s​−UT​)2​.
(17)
Uniqueness for this ordinary differential equation shows that fs​=hs​. At s=T,
hT​(z)=w−UT​=gT−1​(UT​+z)−UT​,
(18)
which is the endpoint identity for the Reverse Loewner flow.
For 0<s<T, the pathwise identity hs​(z)=gs−1​(Us​+z)−Us​ is generally false. The left side is built from the reversed final driver segment (UT−r​−UT​)0≤r≤s​, whereas the right side is built from the initial segment (Ur​)0≤r≤s​. By time reversal and symmetry of Brownian motion they have the same probability distribution, but they are not equal for the given Brownian path.

For z=x+iy with ∣x∣≤1 and 0<y≤1, the given Reverse SLE derivative martingale starts from
M0​=(1+y2x2​)4/κ≤C0​y−8/κ.
(19)
It is a nonnegative local martingale and therefore a supermartingale. Since its second factor is at least one,
E∣hT′​(x+iy)∣2≤EMT​≤C0​y−8/κ.
(20)
Choose
0<α<21​(1−κ8​),
(21)
which is possible exactly because κ>8. At height yn​=2−n, take a horizontal grid of spacing comparable to yn​ in [−1,1]. The Markov inequality gives, at each grid point,
P(∣hT′​(z)∣>yn−1+α​)≤Cyn2−2α−8/κ​.
(22)
There are O(yn−1​) grid points, so the probability that the bound fails anywhere on level n is at most Cyn1−2α−8/κ​. These probabilities are summable. The Borel-Cantelli lemmas therefore give an almost surely finite random constant controlling every sufficiently fine grid, and enlarging it handles the finitely many remaining levels.
Every point of the half-rectangle lies within a fixed hyperbolic distance of one of these grid points at comparable height. The Koebe distortion theorem compares the two derivatives by a universal factor. Hence an almost surely finite random C satisfies
∣hT′​(x+iy)∣≤Cy−1+α
(23)
for all x∈[−1,1] and 0<y≤1. This proves the Reverse SLE derivative bound above the space-filling threshold.

Set κ=4/(d−1)>4. For chordal Schramm–Loewner evolution, the centered image of a real boundary point, divided by κ​, follows the Boundary-point Bessel flow for SLE; changing B to −B matches the sign convention in the question. Thus τx​ and τy​ are the times at which the marked boundary points x and y are swallowed, or equivalently disconnected from infinity, by the Loewner chain.

Because F solves the differential equation from part (c), the Itô formula makes F(Vt​) a local martingale before τ. The defining improper integral converges at both endpoints: its integrand is asymptotic to (−u)1−d near zero and to ∣u∣d−3 at minus infinity. Since 1<d<2, both exponents are integrable. Hence
0≤F(v)≤I:=∫−∞0​(−u)d−1(1−u)4−2ddu​<∞,
(32)
so the stopped local martingale is a bounded martingale.
If τx​<τy​, then Vt​→0 and F(Vt​)→0. If τy​<τx​, then Vt​→−∞ and F(Vt​)→I. The hitting times cannot coincide because Xt​−Yt​ stays positive. The optional sampling theorem for a supermartingale and bounded convergence theorem therefore give
F(v)=IP(τy​<τx​)=IF(v).
(33)
Consequently
F(v)=cF(v),c=I−1>0,
(34)
which is the Two-sided SLE boundary swallowing probability.

The Zero-boundary Gaussian free field on the unit disc is the centered Gaussian process indexed by finite Borel measures of finite Green energy, with covariance
E[(h,ρ)(h,ν)]=∬D×D​GD​(x,y)ρ(dx)ν(dy).
(35)
This covariance determines all its finite-dimensional distributions.
The Domain Markov property of the Gaussian free field says that for every suitable open U⊂D one can write
h=hU​+hD∖U,
(36)
where hU​ is a zero-boundary Gaussian free field on U, independent of hD∖U, while hD∖U is harmonic on U and carries the information from the field outside U.

Part (c), iterated over disjoint nested annuli, gives independent increments, and the law Xs​−Xt​=dXs−t​ gives stationary increments. Every finite vector is jointly Gaussian by the definition of the Zero-boundary Gaussian free field, and a continuous version was assumed. Moreover X0​=0, because the field has zero boundary values.
Thus X is a continuous centered Gaussian process with stationary increments and independent increments. Its variance is a continuous additive function on the nonnegative real numbers, so Var(Xt​)=σ2t for some σ2≥0. The Gaussian-process characterization of Brownian motion now gives
(Xt​)t≥0​=d(σBt​)t≥0​
(40)
for standard Brownian motion B. This is the Circle-average process of the Gaussian free field is Brownian motion.
