Let En​ and E be the projection-valued measures of An​ and A. Using the projection Pn​:H→Hn​, define
μv,w(n)​(B)=⟨En​(B)Pn​v,Pn​w⟩.
(23)
The scalar spectral measures converge weakly when
∫R​fdμv,w(n)​⟶∫R​fdμv,w​​
(24)
for every bounded continuous function f and every v,w∈H. By the spectral theorem for normal operators on a separable Hilbert space, this is equivalent to
⟨f(An​)Pn​v,Pn​w⟩⟶⟨f(A)v,w⟩.
(25)
The assumed moment identities say precisely that this convergence holds for every monomial f(x)=xm. It follows by linearity for every polynomial. For v=w, the m=2 identity gives
∫x2dμv(n)​(x)⟶∫x2dμv​(x),
(26)
so Markov inequality makes the positive measures (μv(n)​) tight. Higher even moments similarly control the tails of any fixed polynomial.
Given a bounded continuous f and ϵ>0, choose R so that the measure tails are uniformly small. The Weierstrass approximation theorem supplies a polynomial p with
sup∣x∣≤R​∣f(x)−p(x)∣<ϵ.
(27)
Moment convergence handles p; tightness and a sufficiently high even moment handle the two tails. Hence ∫fdμv(n)​→∫fdμv​. The polarization identity then gives the same conclusion for μv,w(n)​. This proves weak convergence of scalar spectral measures.
The assertion fails if only m=1 is assumed. Let H=ℓ2(N), let Hn​=span{e1​,…,en​}, take A=0, and let
An​ej​=en+1−j​(1≤j≤n).
(28)
The reversal matrices are self-adjoint unitaries. For fixed v,w∈ℓ2,
⟨An​Pn​v,Pn​w⟩⟶0,
(29)
because the finite head of one vector is paired with the vanishing tail of the other. Thus the m=1 condition holds. However, An2​=IHn​​, so
⟨An2​Pn​v,Pn​w⟩⟶⟨v,w⟩=0=⟨A2v,w⟩
(30)
in general. Taking f(x)=x2 shows that the spectral measures do not converge weakly.
