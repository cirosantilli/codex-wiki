For a two-by-two contingency table, Fisher's exact test conditions on both row totals and both column totals. Under the null of no association, the upper-left count K then has the hypergeometric distribution
P(K=k∣margins)=(r1​n​)(kc1​​)(r1​−kn−c1​​)​,
(4)
where r1​ and c1​ are the first row and column totals. A one-sided p-value sums the appropriate hypergeometric tail; a common two-sided p-value sums the probabilities of all feasible tables no more likely under the null than the observed table. Because the conditional distribution is discrete, the p-value is super-uniform random variable rather than generally exactly uniform.

Cross-classify the binary exposure Ai​=1{Zi​≥1}​ against the binary outcome Yi​. The four cells count placebo survivors or deaths and active-treatment survivors or deaths.
Under the sharp causal null hypothesis, every observed outcome equals the fixed value Yi​(0)=Yi​(1)=Yi​(2), regardless of assignment. With fixed arm sizes, the active set is a uniformly chosen subset of size n1​+n2​. Under independent assignment, Ai​ are independent Bernoulli variables with probability π1​+π2​, and conditioning on their total again makes the active set uniform. Conditional on the table margins, the active-group outcome count therefore has exactly the hypergeometric distribution used by Fisher's exact test.
The conditional p-value is super-uniform for every set of margins. The law of total probability then gives
P(PA​≤α)=E[P(PA​≤α∣margins)]≤α.
(5)
Hence the test is valid under either assignment mechanism.

A positive density factorizes according to an undirected graphical model Gu=(V,E) when there are nonnegative clique potentials ψC​ such that
f(v)=Z1​∏C∈C​ψC​(vC​),
(9)
where C may be taken as the maximal cliques and Z normalizes the density. Equivalently, each potential involves only variables in one complete subgraph.

The global Markov property for an undirected graph says that whenever a vertex set S separates disjoint sets A and B in the graph,
VA​⊥⊥VB​∣VS​.
(10)
The Hammersley-Clifford theorem states that a strictly positive density factorizes over the cliques of an undirected graph if and only if it satisfies this global Markov property. Strict positivity is essential for the converse from conditional independences to factorization.

Let K=Σ−1 be the precision matrix, let R={1,…,p}∖{j,k}, and condition on VR​=vR​. In the Gaussian exponent, all terms depending jointly on vj​ and vk​ are contained in
−21​(Kjj​vj2​+2Kjk​vj​vk​+Kkk​vk2​+2bj​(vR​)vj​+2bk​(vR​)vk​).
(11)
If Kjk​=0, this conditional density is a product of one function of vj​ and one function of vk​, so the conditional independence of Vj​ and Vk​ given VR​ holds.
Conversely, conditional independence makes this everywhere-positive conditional density factorize. Its mixed second derivative must therefore vanish:
∂vj​∂vk​∂2​logf(vj​,vk​∣vR​)=−Kjk​=0.
(12)
Hence the conditional independence holds exactly when (Σ−1)jk​=0.

A density factorizes according to a Directed acyclic graph when
f(v1​,…,vp​)=∏j=1p​f(vj​∣vpa(j)​),
(13)
where pa(j) is the set of parents of vertex j.
The required undirected graph is the moral graph: join every pair of parents having a common child, retain the parent-child adjacencies, and remove all arrowheads. Each DAG family {j}∪pa(j) is then a clique, so each conditional factor is a clique potential and the DAG factorization is also an undirected factorization. These edges are minimal for a guarantee covering every DAG-factorizing density, because an arbitrary conditional factor can couple every pair of variables in its family.

A nonparametric structural equation model for the graph can be written
X=fX​(UX​),A=fA​(UA​),M=fM​(A,X,UM​),Y=fY​(M,X,UY​).
(14)
The bidirected edge A↔Y permits dependence between UA​ and UY​; apart from this pair the exogenous variables are mutually independent. The basic potential outcomes are
M(a,x)=fM​(a,x,UM​),Y(m,x)=fY​(m,x,UY​),
(15)
and the natural nested outcome is Y(a)=Y(M(a,X),X).
The exogenous independences imply that the whole family {M(a,x):a,x} is independent of {X,A,Y(m,x):m,x}, while X is independent of A and the basic response potentials. The bidirected edge means that A and Y(m,x) need not be independent. Useful observed-counterfactual consequences include
M(a)⊥⊥A∣X,M⊥⊥Y(m)∣A,X.
(16)

In an acyclic directed mixed graph, a fixable vertex v is one whose bidirected district contains no proper directed descendant of v:
dis(v)∩de(v)={v}.
(17)
Here A and Y lie in the same district because A↔Y, and Y is a directed descendant of A along A→M→Y. Thus A is not fixable.

For binary A, conditional covariance satisfies
Cov(A,Y∣X)=π(X){1−π(X)}{E(Y∣A=1,X)−E(Y∣A=0,X)}.
(22)
By conditional exchangeability and consistency of potential outcomes, the difference in braces is E[Y(1)−Y(0)∣X]. Consequently
β=E[π(X){1−π(X)}{Y(1)−Y(0)}],
(23)
so the required weight is
w(X)=π(X){1−π(X)}.
(24)
This overlap weight emphasizes covariate strata with treatment probabilities near one half and downweights strata near a violation of positivity in causal inference.

Condition on the independent external sample, and abbreviate δπ​=πm​−π and δμ​=μ​m​−μ. Expanding the plug-in estimator around the true nuisance functions gives
β​−β=(Pn​−P){(A−π)(Y−μ)}+P(δπ​δμ​)+Rn,m​,
(28)
where n​Rn,m​=op​(1) because the two nuisance mean squared errors vanish, the evaluation sample is independent of the nuisance fits, A is bounded, and Y has bounded support. The linear term is the empirical average of the influence function, so the central limit theorem gives
n​(Pn​−P){(A−π)(Y−μ)}d​N(0,Varψ).
(29)
The remaining bias obeys the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
∣P(δπ​δμ​)∣≤MSE(πm​)MSE(μ​m​)​.
(30)
Thus the claimed conclusion follows under the standard product-rate condition
nMSE(πm​)MSE(μ​m​)⟶0,
(31)
equivalently n​MSE(πm​)operatornameMSE(μ​m​)​→0. Slutsky theorem then yields
n​(β​−β)d​N(0,Var(ψ(X,A,Y))).
(32)
As printed, the paper instead assumes only n​MSE(πm​)MSE(μ​m​)→0, which is insufficient with its stated definition of MSE. For example, deterministic nuisance errors of size n−3/16 have both MSEs equal to n−3/8 and satisfy the printed condition, while the scaled product bias is n​n−3/8=n1/8. The result therefore requires the stronger condition above, or “MSE” in the printed rate must be read as root mean squared error.
