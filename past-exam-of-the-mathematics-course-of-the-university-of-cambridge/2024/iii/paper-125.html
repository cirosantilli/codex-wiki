a OurBigBook.com
Let E be a smooth plane cubic whose identity O is an inflection point. A line through P and Q, using the tangent when P=Q, has a third intersection R counted with multiplicity. The chord-and-tangent group law defines P+Q by drawing the line through R and O and taking its third intersection.
The clean verification of the group axioms uses divisors. The line at infinity meets a Weierstrass cubic in 3(O), so three collinear points P,Q,R satisfy
(P)+(Q)+(R)−3(O)=div(ℓ/ℓ∞​).
(1)
Consequently the map
E⟶Pic0(E),P⟼[(P)−(O)]
(2)
sends the chord-and-tangent construction to addition of divisor classes. The principal divisor criterion on an elliptic curve shows that this map is bijective. Associativity and commutativity therefore follow from the abelian group law on Pic0(E). The tangent convention handles repeated intersections, O represents the zero class, and the third point on the line through P and O represents the inverse of P. Hence all group axioms hold.

The discriminant is 1909=23⋅83, so 3,5,7 are primes of good reduction of an elliptic curve. The reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve injects the prime-to-p torsion into E(Fp​).
If a prime ℓ divided the order of E(Q)tors​, its primary subgroup would inject at every one of 3,5,7 except possibly when p=ℓ. For ℓ=3, the counts at 5 and 7 have greatest common divisor one; for ℓ=5 use the counts at 3 and 7; for ℓ=7 use those at 3 and 5; every other ℓ would divide all three counts. Each possibility is excluded. Hence

The Hasse theorem for elliptic curves states that, for an elliptic curve over Fq​,
∣#E(Fq​)−(q+1)∣≤2q​.
(14)
Let π be the Frobenius isogeny of an elliptic curve and put a=q+1−#E(Fq​). The fixed points of π are E(Fq​), and 1−π is separable, so
deg(1−π)=#E(Fq​).
(15)
Hence the trace of an elliptic-curve endomorphism is
tr(π)=1+degπ−deg(1−π)=a,
(16)
while degπ=q.
The degree on End(E) is a nonnegative quadratic form. Polarization and the identities for the dual isogeny give, for integers m,n,
deg([m]+[n]π)=m2+amn+qn2.
(17)
If a2>4q, this real binary quadratic form is indefinite. An open cone on which it is negative contains a nonzero rational point and therefore a nonzero integer point, contradicting nonnegativity of isogeny degree. Thus a2≤4q, which is exactly the claimed inequality.

A one-dimensional commutative formal group law over OK​ is a power series F(X,Y)∈OK​[[X,Y]] satisfying
F(X,0)=X,F(X,Y)=F(Y,X),F(F(X,Y),Z)=F(X,F(Y,Z)).
(21)
A morphism h:F→G is a series h(T)∈TOK​[[T]] satisfying
h(F(X,Y))=G(h(X),h(Y)).
(22)
If h(T)=uT+O(T2), the invertible morphism criterion for formal group laws says that h is an isomorphism whenever u∈OK×​. Indeed, recursive coefficient comparison constructs a unique compositional inverse j(T); applying j to the morphism identity shows that j is a morphism in the opposite direction.
The multiplication series has
[n]F​(T)=nT+O(T2).
(23)
Since p∤n, its linear coefficient is a unit, so [n]F​ is an automorphism of the group F(πOK​). Its kernel is therefore zero, and
F(πOK​)[n]=0.​
(24)

An integral Weierstrass equation has good reduction outside the finitely many primes dividing its nonzero discriminant. This proves finiteness of the set of bad primes. To prove finiteness of rational torsion, choose two distinct good primes. The reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve injects each primary component at a good prime of different residue characteristic, so the two finite reduced point groups bound every primary component of E(Q)tors​.
For
E:y2=x3+30x+30,
(29)
the displayed equation is minimal and
Δ=−16(4⋅303+27⋅302)=−2116800=−26335272.
(30)
Its bad primes are therefore exactly
{2,3,5,7}.​
(31)
The good reductions at 11 and 13 have
#E(F11​)=8,#E(F13​)=15.
(32)
Their coprime orders exclude every rational torsion primary component, including the residue-characteristic components by using the other prime. Hence
E(Q)tors​=0.​

The natural map
E(K)/nE(K)⟶E(L)/nE(L)
(34)
has finite image by hypothesis. It remains to bound its kernel. If P∈E(K) becomes nQ for Q∈E(L), then
cσ​=σQ−Q∈E(L)[n]
(35)
is a one-cocycle for G=Gal(L/K). Changing Q by an n-torsion point changes this cocycle by a coboundary, producing a well-defined map from the kernel to
H1(G,E(L)[n]).
(36)

If A is finitely generated, the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain immediately makes A/nA finite.
Conversely, first replace the given height by a quadratic one. Set
h(P)=limr→∞​4−rh(2rP).
(38)
Condition (ii) makes this limit converge and gives
∣h(P)−h(P)∣≤∑r≥0​c1​,4−r−1=3c1​​.
(39)
Thus h still has finite bounded subsets. Condition (i) also gives a global lower bound for h, so h≥0. Applying condition (iii) to 2rP,2rQ, dividing by 4r and passing to the limit gives one direction of the parallelogram identity. Applying the same inequality to P+Q and P−Q, and using h(2P)=4h(P), gives the reverse direction. Hence
h(P+Q)+h(P−Q)=2h(P)+2h(Q),
(40)
and induction yields h(mP)=m2h(P) for every integer m.
Now suppose A/nA is finite and choose representatives R1​,…,Rs​. Put R=maxi​h(Ri​). For any P, write P=nQ+Ri​. Nonnegativity and the parallelogram identity give
n2h(Q)=h(P−Ri​)≤2h(P)+2h(Ri​),
(41)
so, since n≥2,
h(Q)−R≤21​(h(P)−R).
(42)
Repeated division modulo nA therefore reaches the finite set B={P:h(P)≤R+1}. Reversing the recursion expresses every element of A using B and the finitely many Ri​. This is the height descent lemma, and proves
A is finitely generated⟺∣A/nA∣<∞.​
(43)

For a reduced rational number x=a/b with b>0, the height of a rational number is
H(x)=max{∣a∣,b}.
(44)
Condition (i) holds because only finitely many coprime integer pairs have bounded maximum.
Condition (iii) also holds. The standard height inequality
H(x±y)≤2H(x)H(y)
(45)
gives
h(x+y)+h(x−y)≤2h(x)+2h(y)+2log2.
(46)

h(2m)−4h(m)=log2−3logm,
(47)
