Let δT​ be the thickness of the thermal boundary layer immediately below z=h, let ν be the kinematic viscosity, let κ be the thermal diffusivity, and let Rac​ be the critical local Rayleigh number. The density contrast driving the boundary layer follows from the density anomaly of water:
Δρ=ρ(Th​)−ρ(Tw​)=ρm​α[(Tw​−Tm​)2−(Th​−Tm​)2].
(24)
A Local Rayleigh-number closure sets
ρm​νκgΔρδT3​​=Rac​,
(25)
and therefore
δT​=[gα{(Tw​−Tm​)2−(Th​−Tm​)2}Rac​νκ​]1/3.
(26)
By Fourier's law, the upward heat flux through this layer is
Fc​=k(Tw​−Th​)[Rac​νκgα{(Tw​−Tm​)2−(Th​−Tm​)2}​]1/3​,
(27)
where k is the water's thermal conductivity. This expression applies while the quantity inside braces is positive.
