a OurBigBook.com
Let v1​,…,vr​ be the primitive lattice generators of the rays of the strongly convex rational cone σ⊆NR​. The smoothness criterion for a toric variety says that the affine toric variety Uσ​ is smooth exactly when v1​,…,vr​ form part of a Z-basis of the cocharacter lattice of an algebraic torus N. Equivalently, there is a basis e1​,…,en​ of N such that
σ=Cone(e1​,…,er​).
(1)
In particular, the cone must be a simplicial polyhedral cone and its ray generators must be primitive; for a full-dimensional cone the criterion says that those generators form a lattice basis.

The orbit-cone correspondence is an inclusion-reversing bijection
{σ∈Σ}⟷{TN​-orbits in XΣ​},σ⟼O(σ).
(2)

dimO(σ)=n−dimσ,O(σ)≅Hom(M∩σ⊥,C×).
(3)

Uσ​=⋃τ⪯σ​O(τ),V(σ)=O(σ)​=⋃τ⊇σ​O(τ).
(4)
In particular, the zero cone corresponds to the dense algebraic torus, while maximal cones correspond to torus-fixed points.

c OurBigBook.com
For every t∈TN​, the torus action map
x⟼t⋅x
(5)
is an automorphism of XΣ​. It induces isomorphisms of local rings, so it carries smooth points to smooth points and singular points to singular points. The singular locus is consequently invariant under TN​. If it contains one point x, it contains the entire orbit TN​⋅x; hence the torus-invariance of the singular locus of a toric variety proves that XΣsing​ is a union of the orbits in the orbit-cone correspondence.

Write m=ae1∗​+be2∗​∈M. The inequalities defining the dual cone are
⟨m,3e1​−2e2​⟩=3a−2b≥0,⟨m,e2​⟩=b≥0.
(6)
Thus
σ∨∩M={(a,b)∈Z2:b≥0,3a≥2b}.
(7)
Its Hilbert basis of a rational cone is
(1,0),(1,1),(2,3).
(8)
Indeed, after subtracting copies of (2,3) one reduces to b=0,1, or 2, and the remaining point is generated by (1,0) and (1,1). The coordinate ring of an affine toric variety is therefore
C[σ∨∩M]=C[x,xy,x2y3]⊆C[x±1,y±1].
(9)
Putting A=x, B=xy, and C=x2y3 gives the alternative presentation
C[Uσ​]≅C[A,B,C]/(B3−AC).​
(10)

The primitive ray generators of σ are
v0​=(3,−2),v3​=(0,1),
(11)
and ∣det(v0​,v3​)∣=3. They do not form a lattice basis, so the smoothness criterion for a toric variety shows that Uσ​ is singular. The presentation from part (i) gives the same conclusion by the Jacobian criterion: all derivatives of B3−AC vanish at A=B=C=0.
Insert the primitive rays
v1​=(2,−1),v2​=(1,0).
(12)
Let Σ consist of the cones
Cone(v0​,v1​),Cone(v1​,v2​),Cone(v2​,v3​)
(13)
and all their faces. Since
det(v0​,v1​)=det(v1​,v2​)=det(v2​,v3​)=1,
(14)
every cone of Σ is smooth. Its support is σ, so this fan subdivision induces a proper birational toric morphism
p:XΣ​⟶Uσ​.
(15)
The source is smooth, and p is an isomorphism over the dense algebraic torus; it is therefore a toric resolution of singularities.

Let D1​ and D2​ be the toric divisors corresponding respectively to the primitive ray generators v1​=(3,−2) and v2​=(0,1) of the original cone. The principal divisor on a toric variety formula applied to the characters x=χe1∗​ and y=χe2∗​ gives
div(x)=3D1​,div(y)=−2D1​+D2​.
(16)
The toric divisor class sequence therefore presents the divisor class group as
Cl(Uσ​)≅⟨3D1​,−2D1​+D2​⟩ZD1​⊕ZD2​​≅Z/3Z.
(17)
The class of D1​ is a generator and [D2​]=2[D1​].

The images of the three rays of σ are
φ​(e1​)=f1​,qquadφ​(e2​)=f2​,qquadφ​(e3​)=−f1​−f2​.
(32)
No cone of the complete fan of P2 contains all three rays, so φ​(σ) is not contained in one target cone. Thus φ​ is not a morphism of fans and does not define a regular toric morphism on all of Uσ​.
On the dense algebraic torus, the pullbacks of the two standard target characters are
u⟼x1​/x3​,v⟼x2​/x3​.
(33)
Under the standard torus chart [u:v:1]∈P2, the resulting rational map of toric varieties from a lattice homomorphism is
f:A3⇢P2,(x1​,x2​,x3​)⟼[x1​:x2​:x3​].
(34)
Its homogeneous coordinates vanish simultaneously exactly at the origin. Hence its indeterminacy locus is {0}.

Cone(v,e2​,e3​),Cone(e1​,v,e3​),Cone(e1​,e2​,v).
