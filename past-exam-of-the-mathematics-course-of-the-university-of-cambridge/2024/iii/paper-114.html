Suppose that f is not surjective, and choose y∈/f(Y). If y lies in the open two-cell, then Y∖{y} deformation retracts onto the target circle of the degree-n attaching map, so its first integral homology is Z. If y∈Z, the punctured space deformation retracts onto a finite graph and again has free abelian first homology. The factorization
Yf​Y∖{y}↪Y
(3)
therefore makes f∗​:H1​(Y)→H1​(Y) factor through a free abelian group. Every homomorphism from the finite group H1​(Y)≅Z/n to a free abelian group is zero, contradicting the assumed surjectivity of f∗​.
The converse is false. The radial coordinate descends to a surjection r:Y→[0,1]. The finite CW complex Y is a Peano continuum, so the Hahn-Mazurkiewicz theorem supplies a continuous surjection g:[0,1]→Y. Then g∘r:Y→Y is surjective, but its induced map on H1​ is zero because it factors through the contractible interval.

At a point of the open two-cell, the local link is a circle, so local homology in degree two has rank one. At a point of Z, a neighborhood consists of n half-discs sharing their boundary interval; its link is the Theta graph Θn​. The local homology from a link calculation gives
H2​(Y,Y∖{z};Z)≅H1​(Θn​;Z)≅Zn−1.
(4)
When n>2, this local rank characterizes the points of Z. Since local homology is invariant under a homeomorphism, every homeomorphism satisfies f(Z)=Z, and in particular f(Z)⊂Z.
For n=2, the two pages form an ordinary disc neighborhood, and Y is RP2. The distinguished circle Z is a projective line, but a projective linear homeomorphism can carry it to a different projective line. Thus the conclusion does not hold.

Write F2​=Z/2. A double covering has a deck transformation τ that exchanges the two points in every fibre. Every singular simplex σ:Δq→Y has exactly two lifts σ and τσ. Define the mod-two transfer chain map of a double covering by
T(σ)=σ+τσ,

Assume for contradiction that the involution ϕ is fixed-point-free. The finite group action is then a covering space action, so the quotient map
q:Rn⟶Y=Rn/⟨ϕ⟩
(7)
is a double covering and Y is an n-manifold.
Apply the long exact sequence in homology to the transfer chain map of a double covering. Since Rn is contractible, its positive-dimensional mod-two homology vanishes. The degree-zero portion, together with the fact that q∗​:H0​(Rn;F2​)→H0​(Y;F2​) is an isomorphism, gives
H1​(Y;F2​)≅H0​(Y;F2​)≅F2​.
(8)
In every higher degree the same exact sequence gives
Hi​(Y;F2​)≅Hi−1​(Y;F2​).
(9)
Thus Hi​(Y;F2​)≅F2​ for every i≥0. This contradicts homology above the dimension of a manifold, which gives Hi​(Y;F2​)=0 for i>n. Therefore ϕ has a fixed point.

A closed manifold of odd dimension has Euler characteristic zero by the Euler characteristic of an odd-dimensional closed manifold. Decomposing M into P and the removed closed ball along S2k gives
0=χ(M)=χ(P)+χ(D2k+1)−χ(S2k)=χ(P)+1−2,
(10)
so χ(P)=1.
If the antipodal boundary map extended to a fixed-point-free involution of P, the quotient map P→P/⟨τ⟩ would be a double covering. The formula for Euler characteristic under a finite covering would imply
1=χ(P)=2χ(P/⟨τ⟩),
(11)
which is impossible. Hence no such extension exists.

The standard CW complex structure on CPn has one cell in every even dimension from 0 to 2n, and CPk is its 2k-skeleton. Collapsing that subcomplex leaves one zero-cell and one cell in each dimension 2i for k+1≤i≤n. All cellular boundaries vanish, so
Hq(CPn/CPk;Z)≅{Z,0,​q=0 or q=2i,quadk+1≤i≤n,otherwise.​​
(12)
Let x∈H2(CPn;Z) be the usual generator. For i>k, choose ui​ whose pullback under the quotient map is xi. Naturality of the cup product and the cohomology ring of complex projective space give
ui​uj​={ui+j​,0,​i+j≤n,i+j>n.​
(13)
Together with the unit, this determines the ring; equivalently, its reduced part is the ideal (xk+1)/(xn+1) with the inherited multiplication. This is the cohomology ring of a collapsed projective subspace.
If n=k+1, the quotient has just a zero-cell and a 2n-cell, so it is S2n and is a compact manifold. Conversely, suppose n≥k+2 and the quotient is homotopy equivalent to a compact manifold. Its top cohomology is Z, so that manifold must be closed, orientable, and 2n-dimensional. But H2n−2≅Z while H2=0, contradicting Poincare duality. Therefore
CPn/CPk is homotopy equivalent to a compact manifold exactly when n=k+1.​
(14)

Let a and b be the pullbacks of the standard generator of Hn(Sn;Z) from the two factors. The Künneth theorem and graded commutativity of the cup product give
H∗(Sn×Sn;Z)=Z⟨1,a,b,ab⟩,qquad∣a∣=∣b∣=n,quada2=b2=0,quadba=(−1)nab.​
(17)
Each homeomorphism acts invertibly on Hn≅Z2. To respect ordinary composition, send h to (h−1)∗; functoriality of induced map on cohomology then defines a homomorphism
Homeo(Sn×Sn)⟶GL(2,Z).
(18)
For n=1, the space is the torus. Every matrix in GL(2,Z) induces a linear homeomorphism R2/Z2→R2/Z2, so the image is all of GL(2,Z).
For n=2, write h∗a=pa+qb. Since a2=0 and ab=ba,
0=(h∗a)2=2pq,ab,
(19)
so pq=0; the same argument applies to h∗b. Invertibility then forces the matrix to be a signed permutation matrix. Every such matrix is realized by swapping the two sphere factors and applying an orientation-reversing homeomorphism to either factor. Thus the image consists exactly of the eight signed permutation matrices.

Fibrewise evaluation identifies
L∗⊗C​L≅EndC​(L).

If a,b are the standard generators of H2(S2×S2;Z), choose complex line bundles L1​,L2​ pulled back from the Hopf fibration on the two factors, with e(L1​)=a and e(L2​)=b. (21)

Write α=pa+qb. In the Gysin sequence of a sphere bundle for the oriented circle bundle S(Eα​)→S2×S2, multiplication by α is
Z⟶Z2,qquad1⟼(p,q),
(22)
in degrees 0 to 2, and
Z2⟶Z,qquad(u,v)⟼qu+pv,
(23)
in degrees 2 to 4. If α=0 and d=gcd(∣p∣,∣q∣), taking the relevant kernels and cokernels gives
Hi(S(Eα​);Z)≅⎩⎨⎧​Z,Z⊕Z/d,Z/d,0,​i=0,3,5,i=2,i=4,otherwise.​​
(24)
When α=0, the bundle is trivial and the groups in degrees 0 through 5 have ranks 1,1,2,2,1,1, respectively. These are precisely the groups recorded in integral cohomology of a circle bundle over a product of two spheres.
The additive cohomology for nonzero α depends only on d. On the other hand, part (a) shows that the homeomorphism group acts on (p,q) only by signed permutations. For example, (1,0) and (1,1) both have d=1, so their sphere bundles have isomorphic additive cohomology, but no signed permutation carries one Euler class to the other. The cohomology therefore does not determine the homeomorphism-group orbit of α.
