The strong form of Hensel lemma says the following. Let v be a discrete valuation on a complete field K. If f∈OK​[X] and
v(f(x0​))>2v(f′(x0​)),
(1)
then f has a root x∈OK​ satisfying v(x−x0​)>v(f′(x0​)).
To prove it, apply Newton iteration over a valued field:
xn+1​=xn​−f′(xn​)f(xn​)​.
(2)
The initial inequality says that the first correction has valuation greater than v(f′(x0​)). Taylor expansion then shows inductively that v(f′(xn​))=v(f′(x0​)), while the valuations of the corrections tend to infinity. Hence (xn​) is a Cauchy sequence. Completeness gives a limit x, and continuity gives f(x)=0.
Now decompose the multiplicative group as
Q3×​=3Z×{±1}×U1​,U1​=1+3Z3​.
(3)
The P-adic valuation gives
3Z/33Z≅Z/3Z,
(4)
and cubing is the identity on {±1}. Put U2​=1+9Z3​. Expansion gives U13​⊆U2​. Conversely, for u=1+9a∈U2​, choose b≡a(mod3) and put x0​=1+3b. For f(X)=X3−u,
v3​(f(x0​))≥3>2=2v3​(f′(x0​)),
(5)
so the strong form of Hensel lemma produces a cube root in U1​. Thus U13​=U2​. Finally,
U1​/U2​⟶Z/3Z,1+3b⟼b(mod3),
(6)
is an isomorphism. Combining the valuation and principal-unit factors proves the cube-class group of the 3-adic numbers identity
Q3×​/(Q3×​)3≅Z/3Z×Z/3Z.​

For a finite extension L/K of local fields, let e=e(L/K) be its ramification index and f=f(L/K)=[kL​:kK​] its residue-field degree. An unramified extension has e=1. A totally ramified extension has e=[L:K], equivalently f=1. A tamely ramified extension has separable residue extension and ramification index coprime to the residue characteristic.
Let θˉ generate the finite extension kL​/kK​, and let gˉ​ be its minimal polynomial. Lift gˉ​ to a monic g∈OK​[X] and choose any lift t∈OL​ of θˉ. Since finite fields are perfect fields, gˉ​′(θˉ)=0. The simple-root form of Hensel lemma, applied inside L, gives θ∈OL​ with
g(θ)=0,θ≡t(modmL​).
(10)
Set K0​=K(θ). Its residue field contains kK​(θˉ)=kL​, so
[K0​:K]≥[kL​:kK​]=f.
(11)
The equation g(θ)=0 gives the reverse inequality. Thus [K0​:K]=f, its residue-field degree is f, and e(K0​/K)=1; hence K0​/K is unramified. Since kK0​​=kL​, the extension L/K0​ has residue-field degree one and is totally ramified. This constructs the maximal unramified subextension of a local field extension.

Let G=Gal(L/K) and normalize vL​. In lower numbering,
G−1​=G,Gs​={σ∈G:vL​(σ(x)−x)≥s+1 for every x∈OL​}(s≥0).
(12)
These are the ramification groups; G0​ is the inertia group and G1​ is the wild inertia group.
Because OL​=OK​[α], every x∈OL​ is P(α) for some P∈OK​[X]. The polynomial identity P(Y)−P(X)=(Y−X)Q(X,Y) has integral coefficients. Consequently the inequality for α implies it for every x, and the converse follows by taking x=α. Therefore
Gs​={σ∈G:vL​(σ(α)−α)≥s+1}.
(13)
Since L/K is a Finite Galois extension, the minimal polynomial factors as
f(X)=∏σ∈G​(X−σ(α)).
(14)
Differentiating and evaluating at α gives
f′(α)=∏1=σ∈G​(α−σ(α)),
(15)
and hence
vL​(f′(α))=∑1=σ∈G​vL​(σ(α)−α).
(16)

vL​(f′(α))=∑s≥0​(∣Gs​∣−1).

vL​(f′(α))=e(L/K)−1
(18)

A Lubin–Tate series for π is a power series f(X)∈OK​[[X]] such that
f(X)≡πX(modX2),f(X)≡Xq(modπ).
(19)
The key Lubin–Tate lemma is that if f and g are such series and L(X1​,…,Xr​)=∑i​ai​Xi​ with ai​∈OK​, there is a unique series F∈OK​[[X1​,…,Xr​]] satisfying
F≡L(mod(X1​,…,Xr​)2),F(f(X1​),…,f(Xr​))=g(F(X1​,…,Xr​)).
(20)
Taking g=f and L=X+Y defines Ff​(X,Y). Uniqueness applied to the two sides of each identity proves the identity, associativity, and commutativity axioms, so Ff​ is a formal group law. Taking L=aX defines endomorphisms [a]f​, and uniqueness gives
[a+b]f​=Ff​([a]f​,[b]f​),[ab]f​=[a]f​∘[b]f​.
(21)
Thus Ff​ is the Lubin–Tate formal group as a formal OK​-module, with [π]f​=f.
For another Lubin–Tate series g, apply the lemma with L=X to obtain
h(X)≡X(modX2),h∘f=g∘h.
(22)
Uniqueness shows that h respects both formal addition and every scalar endomorphism. Its linear coefficient is the unit one, so it has a compositional inverse; equivalently, applying the lemma with f and g reversed supplies the inverse. Hence this Lubin–Tate change of series is an isomorphism Ff​≅Fg​ of formal OK​-modules.

Define the Lubin–Tate torsion by
μf,n​={x∈m:[πn]f​(x)=f∘n(x)=0}.
(23)
The scalar endomorphisms make this a module over OK​/πnOK​.
The Newton polygon or Weierstrass preparation theorem applied to the Lubin–Tate congruences shows that [πn]f​ has exactly qn distinct roots in m. More precisely, the quotient of the distinguished factors for [πn]f​ and [πn−1]f​ has degree qn−1(q−1), and its roots are precisely the points killed by πn but not by πn−1.
Choose such a point ωn​. If a∈OK​, write a=πru with u a unit. Since [u]f​ is an automorphism, [a]f​(ωn​)=0 exactly when r≥n. Thus
OK​/πnOK​⟶μf,n​,a⟼[a]f​(ωn​),
(24)
is injective. Both sides have qn elements, so it is an isomorphism of modules. Therefore μf,n​ is a free module of rank one.

f(X)=(1+X)p−1,g(X)=Xp+pX.

The Ostrowski theorem says that every nontrivial absolute value on a field defined on Q is equivalent either to the usual absolute value or to ∣⋅∣p​ for a unique prime p.
Let an absolute value on the number field K extend ∣⋅∣p​. Its valuation ring determines
p={x∈OK​:∣x∣<1},
(37)
a prime ideal satisfying p∩Z=(p). Conversely, each prime p above p defines the normalized absolute value
∣x∣p​=p−vp​(x)/ep​,
(38)
where ep​=vp​(p). It restricts to ∣⋅∣p​. The correspondence between extensions and primes follows either from the valuation ring or from local factorization and extended absolute values; distinct primes give inequivalent valuations. Thus these ∣⋅∣p​ are exactly the extensions, up to equivalence.
For the tensor-product assertion, choose a primitive element of a field extension α for K/Q, with minimal polynomial f. Because number fields are separable, over Qp​ it factors into distinct irreducibles
f=f1​⋯fr​,
(39)
indexed by the primes p∣p. The Chinese remainder theorem gives
K⊗Q​Qp​≅Qp​[X]/(f)≅∏i=1r​Qp​[X]/(fi​).
(40)
The ith factor is the completion of a number field at a prime ideal Kpi​​. Under these identifications the isomorphism is the natural diagonal map x⊗a↦(ax)p​, proving the p-adic tensor decomposition of a number field
K⊗Q​Qp​≅p∣p∏​Kp​.​
(41)
