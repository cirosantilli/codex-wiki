ii OurBigBook.com
A theory is κ-satisfiable when every subtheory of size below κ has a model. An uncountable cardinal κ is weakly compact when every κ-satisfiable theory in an infinitary language Lκ,κ​ with at most κ nonlogical symbols is satisfiable.
Equivalently, κ→(κ)22​: every coloring c:[κ]2→2 has a homogeneous subset of cardinality κ.

A filter U is κ-complete when intersections of fewer than κ members remain in U. An uncountable cardinal κ is a measurable cardinal when it carries a κ-complete nonprincipal ultrafilter.

An uncountable cardinal κ is a strongly compact cardinal when every κ-satisfiable theory in any Lκ,κ​ language is satisfiable. Unlike weak compactness, there is no cardinality bound on the language.

b OurBigBook.com
Let U witness that κ is measurable. Regularity is given, so it remains to prove the strong limit cardinal property. First, every A∈U has cardinality κ: if ∣A∣<κ, then
κ∖A=⋂α∈A​(κ∖{α})∈U
(2)
by nonprincipality and κ-completeness, contradicting A∈U.
Suppose λ<κ and 2λ≥κ. Choose an injection f:κ→P(λ). For each ξ<λ, exactly one of
Aξ​={α<κ:ξ∈f(α)},κ∖Aξ​
(3)
lies in U. Their chosen intersection lies in U by κ-completeness. On that intersection every f(α) is the same subset of λ, contradicting injectivity because every member of U has size κ. Thus 2λ<κ, and κ is strongly inaccessible.

Let F be a κ-complete filter on κ. Use an Lκ,κ​ propositional language with a sentence PA​ for every A⊆κ. Form a theory containing PA​ for A∈F, the Boolean identities
Pκ∖A​↔¬PA​,
(4)
and, for every δ<κ,
P⋂i<δ​Ai​​↔⋀i<δ​PAi​​.
(5)
Every subtheory of size below κ mentions fewer than κ required members of F. Their intersection is nonempty by κ-completeness; choosing a point in it and interpreting PA​ as membership of that point satisfies the subtheory. The theory is therefore κ-satisfiable. Strong compactness supplies a model. Then
U={A⊆κ:PA​ holds in the model}
(6)
is an ultrafilter, contains F, and is κ-complete by the infinitary intersection axioms.

For regular κ, the cobounded filter on a regular cardinal is κ-complete. Part (c) extends it to a κ-complete ultrafilter U. Since κ∖{α} is cobounded for every α<κ, no singleton belongs to U; hence U is nonprincipal. Thus every strongly compact cardinal is measurable.

Let λ be inaccessible and let j:Vλ​→M be an elementary embedding into a transitive set with critical point κ. It is β-strong when
Vκ+β​⊆M.
(7)
A formula Φ(x,κ) is a beta-stable cardinal property when it is absolute between the universe and every transitive set containing Vκ+β​.
To say that the embedding reflects such a property means that whenever Φ(κ) holds,
{μ<κ:Φ(μ)}
(8)
is unbounded in κ: for every γ<κ there is such a μ with γ<μ<κ. Indeed, stability gives M⊨Φ(κ), so M sees the witness κ between γ and j(κ). Elementarity reflects a witness between γ and κ. This is reflection by a beta-strong embedding.

The standard closure lemma for the ultrapower embedding says that every κ-sequence of members of M which belongs to Vλ​ is itself in M. If κ<α<κ+, choose in Vλ​ a surjection
s:κ⟶α.
(9)
All ordinal values of s belong to the transitive model M, so closure gives s∈M. Therefore
M⊨∣α∣≤κ<α,
(10)
and M does not regard α as a cardinal.

Every ordinal below
η=(j(κ)+)M
(11)
has an ultrapower representative f:κ→κ+: by Łoś theorem, a representative below the successor of j(κ) may be chosen below κ+ on a set in the ultrafilter. Hence, in Vλ​,
∣η∣≤(κ+)κ=2κ.
(12)
The ultrapower is closed under κ-sequences, so it contains every subset of κ and computes 2κ correctly. By elementarity M regards j(κ) as measurable, hence strongly inaccessible by Question 1(b). Consequently
2κ<j(κ)<η.
(13)
Thus Vλ​ has a set of cardinality at most 2κ whose order type is η>2κ, so
Vλ​⊨“η is not a cardinal”.​
(14)

Let κ=supn<ω​jn(κ). Every term of the critical sequence is below δ. If cf(δ)>ℵ0​, then its countable supremum also satisfies κ<δ, and because δ is a limit ordinal,
κ+2<δ.
(18)

j′′κ∈Vκ+1​∖Vδ​,
(19)

cf(δ)=ℵ0​.​
