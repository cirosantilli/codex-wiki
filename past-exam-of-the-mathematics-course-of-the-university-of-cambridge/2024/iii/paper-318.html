The Chebyshev alternation theorem says that p∗∈Pn​ is the unique best uniform approximation to f∈C[−1,1] if and only if there are at least n+2 ordered points

A B-spline Nj​ is positive exactly in the interior of its support (tj​,tj+k​). If the B-spline collocation matrix Ax​ is invertible, its determinant contains a nonzero permutation term. Hence there is a permutation π such that
Nπ(r)​(xr​)>0,r=1,…,n.
(33)
If xi​≤ti​, then every one of the first i points satisfies xr​<ti​. Any support containing such a point must have left endpoint tj​<xr​, hence j<i. Only i−1 B-splines are available to match these i rows, contradicting that π is a permutation. Thus xi​>ti​. Similarly, if xi​≥ti+k​, each of the last n−i+1 points can only be matched to an index j>i, but only n−i such indices exist. Therefore xi​<ti+k​. We have proved
ti​<xi​<ti+k​​,Ni​(xi​)>0​.
(34)
The Schoenberg–Whitney theorem states, for strictly increasing knots and interpolation sites, that
Ax​=(Nj​(xi​))i,j=1n​ is invertible⟺ti​<xi​<ti+k​⟺Ni​(xi​)>0∀i​.
(35)
Indeed its determinant is positive under these inequalities.
