For the injective translationally invariant case, the fundamental theorem of matrix product states states that two tensors A={Ai} and B={Bi} of the same minimal bond dimension generate the same periodic MPS for every sufficiently large length if and only if
Ai=eiθXBiX−1
(31)
for every physical index i, with one invertible matrix X. Equality as normalized rays permits the phase eiθ; equality as vectors for all lengths restricts the resulting factor eiNθ accordingly. The converse is immediate from cyclicity of the matrix trace:
Tr(Ai1​⋯AiN​)=eiNθTr(Bi1​⋯BiN​).
(32)
For the nontrivial direction, block enough sites that both tensors are injective and define
ΓAL​(X)=∑i1​,…,iL​​Tr(XAi1​⋯AiL​)∣i1​⋯iL​⟩,
(33)
with ΓBL​ defined similarly. Injectivity means that ΓAL​ and ΓBL​ have trivial kernels. Equality of all sufficiently long periodic states implies equality of the local support spaces imΓAL​=imΓBL​, so there is an invertible linear map F on the virtual matrix algebra satisfying
ΓAL​=ΓBL​∘F.
(34)
Compare two adjacent blocks and contract arbitrary environments on their left and right. Because both block maps are injective, equality of the physical contractions forces
F(XY)=F(X)F(Y),F(I)=I.
(35)
Thus F is a unital algebra automorphism of Mχ​(C). By every automorphism of a full matrix algebra is inner, F(X)=X0​XX0−1​ for an invertible X0​. Applying this relation to a block with one physical site exposed gives
Ai=eiθX0​BiX0−1​.
(36)
This proves the theorem and identifies the freedom as the gauge equivalence of injective matrix product state tensors. For noninjective tensors, their canonical forms first split into injective blocks; equality then permits a permutation of equivalent blocks together with a similarity transformation and phase on each block.
