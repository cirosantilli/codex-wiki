Use the standard toric code convention
Av​=∏j∋v​Xj​,Bp​=∏j∈∂p​Zj​.
(1)
An open Z string anticommutes with Av​ at its endpoints and creates the electric particles e there; an open dual-lattice X string creates magnetic particles m at its endpoint plaquettes. Joining two strings cancels every shared Pauli because Xj2​=Zj2​=1. Bringing two identical endpoints together therefore annihilates them:
e×e=1,m×m=1.
(2)
Taking e around m makes its closed Z string cross the X string ending on m once. At that crossing ZX=−XZ, so the state acquires −1. The same argument with the roles reversed gives
e2iθem​=e2iθme​=−1.
(3)
Identical Z strings commute with one another, as do identical X strings. Their exchanges can be deformed without a crossing between anticommuting operators, so eiθee​=eiθmm​=1. Thus e and m are bosons and are mutual semions, as summarized by the surface-code anyon model.

The composite is f=e×m. Exchanging two composites exchanges the two e constituents and the two m constituents and produces one mutual winding between unlike constituents. The identical-particle phases are both +1, while the mutual contribution is −1, hence
eiθff​=eiθee​eiθmm​e2iθem​=−1.
(4)

sf​=21​(mod1).
(5)
This is precisely the two-dimensional spin-statistics relation for a fermion, so the result is consistent.

In the charge-flux composite model, particle 1 acquires the Aharonov-Bohm effect phase q1​Φ2​/ℏ when it circles the flux of particle 2. The reciprocal Aharonov-Casher effect contributes q2​Φ1​/ℏ. Thus the full braid is
exp(2iθ12​)=exp[ℏi​(q1​Φ2​+q2​Φ1​)].
(6)
The assumption for a=(q0​,0) and b=(0,Φ0​) says
ℏq0​Φ0​​=π(mod2π).
(7)
For any allowed particle (nq0​,mΦ0​), the full braid with a2=(2q0​,0) is e2imq0​Φ0​/ℏ=1; similarly every particle braids trivially with b2=(0,2Φ0​). Under the stated operational identification,
a×a=b×b=1.
(8)
For c=a×b=(q0​,Φ0​), exchanging two identical composites is half their full braid and gives
eiθcc​=eiq0​Φ0​/ℏ=−1.
(9)
Therefore a and b reproduce the bosonic e and m particles, in either order, and c reproduces their fermionic fusion product f.

Every element of the n-qubit Pauli group squares to either 1 or −1. If a stabilizer generator Sj​ had Sj2​=−1, closure of the group would put −1 in S, contrary to the definition. Thus Sj2​=1. Since Pauli operators are unitary,
Sj−1​=Sj†​,
(14)
and Sj−1​=Sj​ proves Sj​=Sj†​.
If two stabilizers g,h anticommute and a nonzero vector ∣ψ⟩ belonged to the codespace, then
gh∣ψ⟩=∣ψ⟩,gh∣ψ⟩=−hg∣ψ⟩=−∣ψ⟩,
(15)
a contradiction. Hence a nonzero stabilizer code requires S to be Abelian. Its centralizer of a stabilizer group is
C(S)={P∈Pn​:Pg=gP for every g∈S}.​
(16)

Measure each generator Sj​. If a Pauli error E has occurred, its outcome is (−1)sj​, where
Sj​E=(−1)sj​ESj​,sj​∈{0,1}.
(17)
The bit vector s=(s1​,…,sm​) is the error syndrome. Choose one representative Es​ with this syndrome. Another Pauli E has the same syndrome exactly when Es†​E commutes with every Sj​, namely when Es†​E∈C(S). Therefore
E∈Es​C(S).
(18)
This stabilizer-syndrome coset is all the syndrome reveals: multiplication by a stabilizer changes nothing on the code, while multiplication by an element of C(S)∖S can change the logical state without changing any syndrome bit.

Choose both circular ends of the cylinder to be electric, or rough, boundaries. In the convention of part 1, retain plaquette generators
Bp​=∏e∈∂p​Ze​
(24)
with their boundary truncations, and use star generators
Av​=∏e∋v​Xe​
(25)
only at vertices not lying on an electric boundary. Omitting the endpoint star checks allows a Z string to terminate at either boundary, which is precisely anyon condensation at a boundary for e.
Relative homology now has one nontrivial class represented by a primal Z path γ joining the two boundaries. The dual absolute homology has one class represented by an X loop γ​ around the cylinder. Their strings intersect once and anticommute, so they form one logical pair Zˉ,Xˉ. Every other closed or boundary-ending string is a product of stabilizers, so the code has exactly one logical qubit.
In the Random-bond Ising model mapping, there is no Ising spin for an omitted boundary star. An edge e joining an interior vertex v to an electric boundary is toggled by only Av​, so its bond factor ηe​σv​σv′​ is replaced by the boundary-field factor ηe​σv​. Equivalently, attach exterior spins fixed to +1 and retain the same bond formula. Edges wholly on a boundary contribute fixed constants. The bulk Hamiltonian is therefore supplemented by
H∂​=−J∑e=(v,∂)​ηe​(s,q)σv​,
(26)
with the signs still determined by Es​Xˉq. This is the required boundary modification.

Position-space particle-hole symmetry complex-conjugates plane waves, so the Fourier transform sends k to −k. Fourier transforming the stated relation therefore gives
HBdG​(k)=−Σ1​HBdG∗​(−k)Σ1​.
(30)

HBdG​(−k)(Σ1​uk∗​)=−εk​(Σ1​uk∗​).
(31)

specHBdG​(−k)=−specHBdG​(k),
(32)

The two new boundaries surround opposite sides of the same bulk. Their induced orientations are opposite, so their chiral edge modes counterpropagate and sgnv1​=−sgnv2​.
For v1​=v and v2​=−v, define the mass
m(x)=tcos2ϕ(x)​.
(36)
It approaches +t on the left and −t on the right. The zero-energy first-order equations have one normalizable real solution, whose envelope can be chosen as
f(x)=Cexp[ℏv1​∫0x​m(s)ds]
(37)
after choosing the constant Majorana spinor with the appropriate relative sign. The corresponding operator
γ=∫dxf(x)[c1​(x)±c2​(x)]
(38)
is self-adjoint and commutes with the Hamiltonian, so it is a Majorana zero mode at a mass domain wall.
Outside the core it decays on
ξ=∣t∣ℏv​.
(39)
If the phase varies approximately linearly across the core, then m(x)≃−πtx/Rc​ near zero and the central envelope is Gaussian with width
ℓcore​∼π∣t∣ℏvRc​​​.
(40)
Thus the spatial extent is of order max(ξ,ℓcore​), up to constants depending on the detailed vortex profile. When ∣v1​∣=∣v2​∣, continuously changing their magnitudes to equality never changes their opposite signs or closes the asymptotic mass gap. The mass domain wall therefore retains its odd, particle-hole-protected zero mode; only its two component amplitudes and localization lengths change.

Each of the M vortices contributes one bulk Majorana zero mode. A finite fermionic system must have an even total number of Majorana zero modes. The boundary Chiral Majorana edge mode has no zero momentum in the antiperiodic sector but has one k=0 Majorana mode in the periodic sector. Therefore
c(x+L)={−c(x),+c(x),​M even,M odd,​
(41)
or compactly c(x+L)=(−1)M+1c(x). This boundary condition of a chiral Majorana edge mode supplies the extra boundary zero mode exactly when the vortex count is odd.
