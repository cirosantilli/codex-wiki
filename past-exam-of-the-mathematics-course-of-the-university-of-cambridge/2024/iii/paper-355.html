Reflection symmetry makes the two outer free tails identical. Their combined energy is
Eo​=ξR2kc​δo2​​.
(12)
Between the cylinders the free profile is even. Up to an irrelevant additive height it has the form
hi​(x)=Ccosh(x/ξ),∣x∣≤L−δi​.
(13)
Slope matching at x=L−δi​ gives
C=Rsinh[(L−δi​)/ξ]ξδi​​.
(14)
Direct integration, or the boundary expression from part (a), yields
Ei​=ξR2kc​δi2​​cothξL−δi​​.
(15)
At the retained order δi​/ξ≪1, replace the argument by L/ξ. The four contact halves contribute bending plus adhesion energy
Ec​=R2kc​​(1−2U)(δo​+δi​).
(16)
Thus
E2​=ξR2kc​​[δo2​+δi2​coth(L/ξ)−2ξ(U−21​)(δo​+δi​)].
(17)
Independent minimization gives
δo​=ξ(U−21​),δi​=ξ(U−21​)tanh(L/ξ),
(18)
and hence
E2​(L)=−R2kc​ξ​(U−21​)2[1+tanh(L/ξ)].
(19)
At infinite separation the energy is twice the one-cylinder minimum. The membrane-mediated interaction potential is therefore
V(L)=E2​(L)−E2​(∞)=R2kc​ξ​(U−21​)2[1−tanh(L/ξ)]​.
(20)
It is positive and decreases monotonically to zero, so the two cylinders repel. The physical cause is the overlap of their exponentially relaxing membrane deformations.

For a cylinder of radius r and length L, the mean curvature is H=1/(2r) and the area is 2πrL. Its Helfrich membrane energy per unit length is therefore
LEm​​=2πr(σ+2r2kc​​)=2πσr+rπkc​​.

(22)

Let the unit normal n point from the fluid toward the free surface, let t lie along the surface, and write the swimmer direction as
p=cosθt+sinθn.
(37)
Reflecting the swimmer position across the plane and reflecting its orientation to
p∗=cosθt−sinθn
(38)
constructs the free-surface image of a force dipole. At the surface the two dipoles have equal tangential velocity and opposite normal velocity. Their sum therefore satisfies the no-penetration boundary condition; tangential velocity is even across the plane and normal velocity is odd, so the tangential traction vanishes and the stress-free boundary condition is also satisfied.
The vector from the image to the swimmer is r=−2hn, for which (p∗⋅r)2=4h2sin2θ. Evaluating the image force-dipole flow at the swimmer gives
Usurf​=32πμh2P​(1−3sin2θ)n​.
(39)
There is no tangential image velocity at the swimmer in this point-dipole approximation. With the convention that P>0 is an extensile pusher microswimmer, a nearly parallel pusher is attracted toward the surface, whereas a nearly parallel contractile puller microswimmer is repelled. For ∣sinθ∣>1/3​ the normal drift reverses because the image samples the axial rather than equatorial part of the dipolar flow.
