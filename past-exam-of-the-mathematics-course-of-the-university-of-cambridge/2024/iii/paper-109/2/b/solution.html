We use the coefficient form of the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz: if a polynomial of degree ∑i​ti​ has nonzero coefficient at ∏i​xiti​​, then it cannot vanish on every product set ∏i​Si​ with ∣Si​∣>ti​.

F(x)=(1−Ap−1)(1−Bp−1)[(1−Sp−1)(2−Ep​(x))−2∏i​(1−xi​)].

A direct multinomial-coefficient calculation, using Wilson's theorem, shows that the coefficient of ∏i=14p−2​xi​ in F is nonzero. The Combinatorial Nullstellensatz with every Si​={0,1} therefore gives an indicator vector x for which F(x)=0.
The zero indicator does not work. The first two factors force the selected vectors to have both coordinate sums zero. If the support size is not divisible by p, Fermat's little theorem makes 1−Sp−1=0, while the final product also vanishes; hence the support has size p, 2p, or 3p. Size p proves the claim, and size 3p reduces to part (a). For size 2p,
Ep​(x)=(p2p​)≡2(modp),
(12)
so the bracket vanishes, again contradicting F(x)=0. Only the size-p and reducible size-3p cases remain, and either yields the required zero-sum p-subsequence.
