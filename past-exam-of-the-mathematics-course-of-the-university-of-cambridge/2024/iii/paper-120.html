Expand L to L(M) by a constant cm​ for each m∈M. The diagram of a structure Diag(M) contains all atomic and negated atomic L(M)-sentences true in M. The elementary diagram ElDiag(M) contains every L(M)-sentence true in M.
The method of diagrams combines one of these sets with another theory and applies the compactness theorem. A model of Diag(M) yields an embedding of M by m↦cm​, while a model of ElDiag(M) yields an elementary embedding.

On A=⋃i​Ai​, interpret a constant at its common value, a function on a tuple by choosing one Ai​ containing the tuple, and a relation similarly. Total ordering of the indices and compatibility of substructures make these definitions independent of the chosen stage. Every Ai​ is then a substructure of A.
For an elementary chain, induction on formulas proves
Ai​⊨φ(aˉ)⟺A⊨φ(aˉ)(aˉ∈Ai​).
(1)
The atomic step follows from the induced structure, Boolean steps are immediate, and for an existential formula any witness in the union lies in a later Aj​ containing the parameters; elementarity between Ai​ and Aj​ moves existence back to Ai​. This is the elementary chain theorem.

A sentence ∀xˉ∃yˉ​φ(xˉ,yˉ​) with quantifier-free φ is preserved by a union of an embedding chain: a tuple aˉ occurs at some stage, a witness bˉ exists at that stage, and quantifier-free formulas are preserved in the union. Thus every forall-exists axiomatized theory is inductive.
Conversely, let T0​ contain all forall-exists consequences of T and suppose M⊨T0​. The diagram-and-compactness sandwich lemma says that one can construct
M=M0​⊆N0​⊆M1​⊆N1​⊆⋯
(2)
where every Ni​⊨T and every Mi​≼Mi+1​. For completeness, the first extension is obtained by adding to T the diagram of Mi​ together with all universal formulas over Mi​ true there. A finite inconsistency would give a forall-exists consequence of T false in Mi​. The resulting extension embeds into an elementary extension Mi+1​ by the method of diagrams.
The Ni​ form an embedding chain and have the same union N as the Mi​. By the assumed preservation, N⊨T; by the elementary chain theorem, M≼N. Hence M⊨T, so T0​⊨T. The two theories are equivalent, proving the characterization.

A model-complete theory is one for which every embedding between models is elementary. If (Mi​) is an embedding chain of models, all transition embeddings are therefore elementary. The elementary chain theorem shows that the union is a model elementarily extending every Mi​, so the theory is preserved under unions of embedding chains. Part (c) then gives an axiomatization by forall-exists sentences.

A complete n-type over X⊆M is a maximal set p(xˉ) of L(X)-formulas consistent with the theory of M with parameters from X. Equivalently, for every formula φ(xˉ), exactly one of φ,¬φ belongs to p.
The type space SnM​(X) has these types as points and basic open sets
[φ]={p:φ∈p}.
(3)
Since [φ]c=[¬φ], these sets are clopen. An isolated type is a point {p}=[φ] for some formula φ∈p.

Assume M is a prime model. By the downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem, T has a countable model, and the elementary embedding of M into it makes M countable. If a tuple aˉ∈M had a nonisolated type, the omitting types theorem would give a countable model of T omitting that type. An elementary embedding of M into this model would realize it, a contradiction. Thus M is atomic.
Conversely, let M be countable and atomic, enumerate it as (ai​)i<ω​, and let N⊨T. Construct an elementary embedding recursively. Suppose a0​,…,an−1​ have been mapped to bˉ. Let ψ(xˉ) isolate the type of (a0​,…,an−1​) and let θ(xˉ,y) isolate the type of (a0​,…,an​). Since the latter extends the former and is realized in M, completeness gives
T⊨∀xˉ(ψ(xˉ)→∃yθ(xˉ,y)).
(8)
The tuple bˉ realizes ψ, so a suitable image of an​ exists in N. The union of the finite partial elementary maps is an elementary embedding M→N. Hence M is prime.

Under the Implicational Curry-Howard correspondence, propositions are simple types and assumptions are typed variables. The natural-deduction rules
Γ⊢A→BΓ,A⊢B​andΓ⊢BΓ⊢A→BΓ⊢A​
(10)
correspond respectively to the typing rules
Γ⊢λx.M:A→BΓ,x:A⊢M:B​,Γ⊢MN:BΓ⊢M:A→BΓ⊢N:A​.
(11)
An assumption x:A corresponds to the variable rule. Induction on a proof converts each rule into the matching typing construction; induction on a typing derivation reverses the process. Thus derivability of an implicational formula from assumptions is equivalent to inhabitation of its corresponding type.

By part (a), such a term would prove
((σ→τ)→σ)→σ
(12)
