The Hahn-Banach separation theorem for two convex sets states that if A and B are disjoint nonempty convex subsets of a real locally convex space X and A is open, then there are a continuous linear functional f∈X∗ and a real number c such that
f(a)<c≤f(b)(a∈A,b∈B).
(19)
To prove it, form
C=A−B={a−b:a∈A,b∈B}.
(20)
This is open and convex and does not contain zero. The separation of a point and an open convex set, proved from the Minkowski functional and the Hahn-Banach theorem, gives a nonzero continuous f with f(c)<0 for every c∈C. Hence f(a)<f(b) for all a,b. Taking c between supA​f and infB​f gives the stated form.
For a dual pair (E,F), the topology σ(E,F) has a neighbourhood base at zero consisting of
{x:∣fj​(x)∣<ε,1≤j≤n},fj​∈F.
(21)
Every f∈F is continuous by definition. Conversely, if a linear functional g is continuous, some such neighbourhood lies in {∣g∣<1}. Therefore ⋂j​kerfj​⊆kerg. Elementary linear algebra then gives g∈span{f1​,…,fn​}⊆F. Thus
(E,σ(E,F))∗=F​.
(22)
For a normed space X, the weak topology is σ(X,X∗); on X∗ the weak-star topology is σ(X∗,X), using the canonical image of X in X∗∗. If X is reflexive, X∗∗=JX​(X), so the weak and weak-star topologies on X∗ coincide. Conversely, if they coincide, every x∗∗∈X∗∗ is weak-star continuous. The dual-pair result says that every such functional is evaluation at some x∈X, so JX​ is onto and X is reflexive.
For A⊆E, every a∈A and zero satisfy every inequality defining A∘∘, so A∪{0}⊆A∘∘. The latter is an intersection of weakly closed convex half-spaces, hence contains
C=convσ(E,F)(A∪{0}).
(23)
If x∈/C, choose an open convex neighbourhood V of zero with (x+V)∩C=∅. Applying the separation theorem to x+V and C gives f∈F with
supc∈C​f(c)<f(x).
(24)
Because 0∈C, the supremum is nonnegative. After multiplying f by a positive scalar, f≤1 on C while f(x)>1. Thus f∈A∘ but x∈/A∘∘. We conclude with the Bipolar theorem for a dual pair:
A∘∘=convσ(E,F)(A∪{0}).​
