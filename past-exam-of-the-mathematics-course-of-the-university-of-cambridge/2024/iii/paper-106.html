First suppose that X is separable. Choose a norm-dense sequence (xn​) in BX​. On every norm-bounded subset of X, a countable norm-dense subset (fn​) of BX∗​ in the weak-star topology separates points, and
d(x,y)=∑n=1∞​2−n1+∣fn​(x−y)∣∣fn​(x−y)∣​
(1)
metrizes the weak topology. Indeed, convergence for all fn​, boundedness, and weak-star density imply convergence for every f∈X∗. Thus a weakly compact set K is a compact metric space and hence is sequentially compact.
For general X, take a sequence (xn​) in K and let
Y=span​{xn​:n≥1}.
(2)
The space Y is separable and norm closed. The Hahn-Banach theorem shows both that Y is weakly closed in X and that its own weak topology is the subspace topology inherited from X. Hence K∩Y is weakly compact and, by the separable case, contains a weakly convergent subsequence of (xn​). Its limit lies in K∩Y. Therefore every weakly compact subset of a Banach space is weakly sequentially compact, which is one direction of the Eberlein-Smulian theorem.

If X is reflexive, then X∗ is reflexive. The weak topology σ(X∗,X∗∗) and weak-star topology σ(X∗,X) therefore coincide under the canonical identification X∗∗=X. Thus every weak-star convergent sequence in X∗ is weakly convergent, so X is a Grothendieck space.
Conversely, suppose that X is separable and Grothendieck. The Banach-Alaoglu theorem and weak-star metrizability of the dual ball make BX∗​ weak-star compact and metrizable, hence weak-star sequentially compact. Every convergent subsequence is weakly convergent by the Grothendieck property. Thus BX∗​ is weakly sequentially compact. The stated converse to part (a), equivalently the other direction of the Eberlein-Smulian theorem, makes BX∗​ weakly compact. Hence X∗ is reflexive, and therefore so is X.
Finally let T:X→Y be bounded and onto, with X Grothendieck, and suppose yn∗​→y∗ weak-star in Y∗. Then
T∗yn∗​⟶T∗y∗
(4)
weak-star in X∗, hence weakly. The open mapping theorem implies that T∗:Y∗→X∗ is an isomorphism onto its closed range. Given y∗∗∈Y∗∗, the functional
T∗y∗⟼y∗∗(y∗)
(5)
is bounded on T∗(Y∗) and extends by the Hahn-Banach theorem to some x∗∗∈X∗∗. Therefore
y∗∗(yn∗​)=x∗∗(T∗yn∗​)longrightarrowx∗∗(T∗y∗)=y∗∗(y∗).
(6)
This is weak convergence in Y∗, so Y is Grothendieck.

In a unital C-star algebra A, an element is Hermitian when x=x∗, unitary when x∗x=xx∗=1, and normal when x∗x=xx∗. It is a Positive element of a C-star algebra when x=y∗y for some y, equivalently when it is Hermitian and its spectrum lies in [0,∞).
Suppose first that ∥τ∥=τ(1), and normalize so that both equal one. If x=x∗, the C-star identity gives
∥x+it1∥2=∥(x−it1)(x+it1)∥=∥x2+t21∥≤∥x∥2+t2.
(7)
Writing τ(x)=a+ib, we obtain
a2+(b+t)2=∣τ(x+it1)∣2≤∥x∥2+t2
(8)
for every real t, which forces b=0. Decomposing an arbitrary x into its Hermitian real and imaginary parts now gives
τ(x∗)=τ(x)​​.
(9)
Let x=x∗. Every character of an algebra on the commutative unital Banach algebra generated by x has norm and value at one equal to one, so the preceding argument makes its value on x real. The character description of the spectrum of an element therefore gives σ(x)⊂R. Iterating the C-star identity,
∥x∥=∥x2n∥1/2n⟶r(x),

so ∥x∥=r(x). If c=∥x∥, then x+c1 has nonnegative spectrum and is positive, as is c1; hence

(11)

If 0≤x and ∥x∥≤1, then spectral translation gives

Thus 1−x is positive and, by the norm--spectral-radius equality for Hermitian elements, ∥1−x∥≤1.
Return to a norm-one τ with τ(1)=1. For 0≤x≤1, the preceding paragraph and reality on Hermitian elements give
τ(x)=1−τ(1−x)≥1−∣τ(1−x)∣≥0.

Scaling proves that τ is a positive functional on a C-star algebra. Every character has norm and value at one equal to one, so every character is positive. On C[0,1], the functional
f⟼∫01​f(t)dt

Conversely, let τ be positive. Writing a Hermitian element as a difference of positive elements shows that τ is real on Hermitian elements. Positivity of
τ((xλ+y)∗(xλ+y))

∣τ(y∗x)∣2≤τ(x∗x)τ(y∗y)​.

Convex combinations preserve positivity and value one at the identity, so the state space S(A) is convex. If x is normal, the unital C-star subalgebra C∗(1,x) is commutative and its Gelfand transform identifies it with C(σ(x)). Choose λ∈σ(x) with ∣λ∣=∥x∥. Evaluation at λ is a state taking x to λ. Its norm-preserving Hahn--Banach extension to A still takes 1 to one, so the norm criterion makes the extension a state τ satisfying ∣τ(x)∣=∥x∥.
The state space is nonempty, convex, and weak-star compact by the Banach-Alaoglu theorem. The Krein-Milman theorem gives an extreme point, so a pure state on a C-star algebra exists. For positive x, the preceding norm-attainment result makes

The Hahn-Banach separation theorem for two convex sets states that if A and B are disjoint nonempty convex subsets of a real locally convex space X and A is open, then there are a continuous linear functional f∈X∗ and a real number c such that
f(a)<c≤f(b)(a∈A,b∈B).
(19)
To prove it, form
C=A−B={a−b:a∈A,b∈B}.
(20)
This is open and convex and does not contain zero. The separation of a point and an open convex set, proved from the Minkowski functional and the Hahn-Banach theorem, gives a nonzero continuous f with f(c)<0 for every c∈C. Hence f(a)<f(b) for all a,b. Taking c between supA​f and infB​f gives the stated form.
For a dual pair (E,F), the topology σ(E,F) has a neighbourhood base at zero consisting of
{x:∣fj​(x)∣<ε,1≤j≤n},fj​∈F.
(21)
Every f∈F is continuous by definition. Conversely, if a linear functional g is continuous, some such neighbourhood lies in {∣g∣<1}. Therefore ⋂j​kerfj​⊆kerg. Elementary linear algebra then gives g∈span{f1​,…,fn​}⊆F. Thus
(E,σ(E,F))∗=F​.
(22)
For a normed space X, the weak topology is σ(X,X∗); on X∗ the weak-star topology is σ(X∗,X), using the canonical image of X in X∗∗. If X is reflexive, X∗∗=JX​(X), so the weak and weak-star topologies on X∗ coincide. Conversely, if they coincide, every x∗∗∈X∗∗ is weak-star continuous. The dual-pair result says that every such functional is evaluation at some x∈X, so JX​ is onto and X is reflexive.
For A⊆E, every a∈A and zero satisfy every inequality defining A∘∘, so A∪{0}⊆A∘∘. The latter is an intersection of weakly closed convex half-spaces, hence contains
C=convσ(E,F)(A∪{0}).

supc∈C​f(c)<f(x).

Because 0∈C, the supremum is nonnegative. After multiplying f by a positive scalar, f≤1 on C while f(x)>1. Thus f∈A∘ but x∈/A∘∘. We conclude with the Bipolar theorem for a dual pair:
A∘∘=convσ(E,F)(A∪{0}).​

The Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem says that there is a unique finite regular positive Borel measure μ with
φ(f)=∫K​fdμ.

More generally, C(K)∗=M(K) is the Banach space of finite regular complex Borel measures with the total-variation norm.
Now let A⊆B(H) be commutative, unital, and C-star, and put K=ΦA​. The Gelfand transform is an isometric star-isomorphism A≅C(K). For ξ,η∈H, the functional

⟨E(S)ξ,η⟩=μξ,η​(S)
(30)

Ψ(f)=∫K​fdE,f∈L∞(K),
