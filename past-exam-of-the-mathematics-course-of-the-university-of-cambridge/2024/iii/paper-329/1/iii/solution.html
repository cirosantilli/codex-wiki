A rotation by π about the vertical symmetry axis maps a horizontal angular velocity Ω′ to −Ω′ but leaves a possible vertical force unchanged. Linearity and uniqueness of Stokes flow therefore require that vertical force to equal its own negative, so it vanishes. A horizontal force and a horizontal couple are allowed by the same symmetry.
Write λ=1−ϵ and examine the lower pole, where θ≪1. Since cosθ=1−θ2/2+O(θ4),
h=Δ[ϵ+2θ2​+O(ϵθ2+θ4)].
(14)
Thus h=O(ϵΔ) for θ=O(ϵ1/2), a circular patch of radius
ℓ=O(aϵ1/2)​.
(15)
In the broad region, the surface speed is O(Ω′a), the shear is O(μΩ′a/Δ), the area is O(a2), and the moment arm is O(a). Hence
Gbroad′​=O(ΔμΩ′a4​).
(16)
In the narrow patch, the shear rises to O(μΩ′a/(ϵΔ)), while its area falls to O(ϵa2); the moment arm remains O(a). Therefore
Gpatch′​=Gbroad′​=O(ΔμΩ′a4​)​.
(17)
Dropping the moment arm gives the shear-force scale. Pressure produces the same horizontal order after multiplication by the small surface slope. Both regions consequently contribute
Fpatch′​=Fbroad′​=O(ΔμΩ′a3​)​.
(18)
