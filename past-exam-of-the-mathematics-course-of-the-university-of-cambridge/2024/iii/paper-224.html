a OurBigBook.com
If X→Y→Z is a Markov chain, the two forms of the data processing inequality for mutual information are
I(X;Z)≤I(X;Y),I(X;Z)≤I(Y;Z).
(1)
The chain rule for mutual information and the Markov property I(X;Z∣Y)=0 give
I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Y)+I(X;Z∣Y)=I(X;Y).
(2)
Using the other order,
I(X;Y,Z)=I(X;Z)+I(X;Y∣Z)≥I(X;Z),
(3)
because conditional mutual information is nonnegative. This proves the first inequality. Applying the same result to the reversed Markov chain Z→Y→X, which has the same conditional-independence statement, proves the second.

Let V,V′,U be mutually independent, with V,V′ identically distributed. Entropy submodularity for three independent sums gives
H(V+V′+U)+H(U)≤H(V+U)+H(V′+U).
(4)
Independent addition cannot decrease discrete entropy, because H(V+V′+U)≥H(V+V′+U∣U)=H(V+V′). Hence
H(V+V′)+H(U)≤H(U+V)+H(U+V′).
(5)
Take U to have the distribution of −X and take V,V′ to be independent copies of X, all mutually independent. Then U+V and U+V′ both have the distribution of X−Y, whereas V+V′ has the distribution of X+Y and H(U)=H(X). Hence
H(X+Y)+H(X)≤2H(X−Y),
(6)
or
H(X+Y)−H(X)≤2{H(X−Y)−H(X)}.
(7)
The denominator is nonnegative because conditioning on Y recovers X from X−Y, so H(X−Y)≥H(X−Y∣Y)=H(X).

Let X1​,…,Xn​ be IID with full-support mass function Q on a finite alphabet A, and let the empirical distribution be
Pn​(a)=n1​∑i=1n​1{Xi​=a}​.
(21)
Suppose E is a set of probability mass functions satisfying E=E∘ and that the information projection P∗ minimizes D(P∥Q) over E. Then the limiting Sanov theorem is
limn→∞​−n1​log2​Qn(Pn​∈E)=D(P∗∥Q).
(22)
For each n-type P, the method of types gives
(n+1)−∣A∣2−nD(P∥Q)≤Qn(TP​)≤2−nD(P∥Q),
(23)
and there are at most (n+1)∣A∣ types. Summing the upper bounds over types in E gives the large-deviation upper bound. For the lower bound, choose types Pn​∈E converging to an interior distribution arbitrarily close to P∗ and use the lower type-class bound. Polynomial factors disappear after applying −n−1log2​, and continuity of divergence finishes the proof.

For an IID finite-alphabet source Q and a fixed rate H(Q)<R<log2​∣A∣, let Pe∗​(n,R) be the smallest probability that a block is not represented by a fixed-to-fixed code having at most 2nR codewords. The fixed-rate source-coding error exponent theorem states
limn→∞​−n1​log2​Pe∗​(n,R)=D∗(R,Q),D∗(R,Q)=minP:H(P)≥R​D(P∥Q).
(26)
For the direct part, encode all type classes whose empirical entropy is at most R−δn​, where δn​↓0 and nδn​−∣A∣log2​(n+1)→∞. Since a type class has at most 2nH(P) sequences and there are at most (n+1)∣A∣ types, this codebook has at most 2nR entries for large n. An error can occur only when H(Pn​)>R−δn​. The method of types bounds its probability by
(n+1)∣A∣2−ninfP:H(P)>R−δn​​D(P∥Q).
(27)
Taking the lower limit of the exponent and using compactness and continuity gives at least D∗(R,Q), proving achievability.
