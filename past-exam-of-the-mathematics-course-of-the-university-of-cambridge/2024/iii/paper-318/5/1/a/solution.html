Suppose first that f−u∗ is orthogonal to Un​. Every u∈Un​ can be written u=u∗+v with v∈Un​. The Pythagorean theorem in an inner-product space gives
∥f−u∥2=∥(f−u∗)−v∥2=∥f−u∗∥2+∥v∥2≥∥f−u∗∥2.
(54)
Thus u∗ is a best approximation.
Conversely, if u∗ minimizes the distance, then for every v∈Un​ the quadratic
q(t)=∥f−u∗−tv∥2
(55)
has its minimum at t=0. Differentiating there gives q′(0)=−2(f−u∗,v)=0 in the real case; varying real and imaginary parts gives the complex case. Therefore
u∗ is best⟺(f−u∗,v)=0∀v∈Un​​.
(56)
