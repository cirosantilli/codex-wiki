For any n≥0, take fn​=Tn+1​, the Chebyshev polynomial of degree n+1. It has n+2 alternating extrema of magnitude one on [−1,1]. The Chebyshev alternation theorem shows that the zero polynomial is best from Pn​, with error one. Since it also lies in Pn−1​ when n≥1,
En−1​(Tn+1​)=En​(Tn+1​)=1​.
(23)
Now suppose f(n)(x)>0 throughout [−1,1] and, contrary to the claim, En−1​(f)=En​(f). A best p∈Pn−1​ would then also be best in Pn​. Its error e=f−p would have n+2 alternating extrema by the alternation theorem, hence at least n+1 distinct zeros. Applying the Rolle theorem n times gives a zero of
e(n)=f(n)−p(n)=f(n),
(24)
contradicting positivity. Therefore
En−1​(f)>En​(f)​.
(25)
