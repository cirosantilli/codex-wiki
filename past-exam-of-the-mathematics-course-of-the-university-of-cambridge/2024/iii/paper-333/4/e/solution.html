Define the Eulerian-mean streamfunction by
(va​,wa​)=(χ​a,z​,−χ​a,y​).
(111)
With
A=dρs​/dzρ′v′​​=f0​F(z)​,
(112)
the transformation in part a gives
va∗​=(χ​a​−A)z​,wa∗​=−(χ​a​−A)y​.
(113)
Hence, up to an irrelevant additive constant,
χ​a​=χ​a∗​+f0​F(z)​​.
(114)
For the step-profile flux,
χ​a​=χ​a∗​+f0​F0​​Θ(z)sin2Lπy​​.
(115)
The residual streamfunction χ​a∗​ vanishes on the rigid boundaries, decays away from the absorption level, and has opposite-signed values immediately below and above z=H. Its vertical derivative gives a zonal acceleration ut​ concentrated around H and largest near the channel center. Its meridional derivative gives a dipolar density tendency: ρ​t​ changes sign across the channel center and reverses vertical structure across the absorption level.
The eddy term in χ​a​ has a compensating downward jump at H, so the Eulerian-mean streamfunction is continuous even though χ​a∗​ jumps in the idealized step limit. Below the critical layer, the Eulerian view contains a broad circulation associated with the eddy density flux; the transformed view subtracts that reversible eddy-induced motion and isolates the residual circulation forced where the waves dissipate.
In the Eulerian density budget, vertical advection by wa​ and the divergence of ρ′v′​ can be individually large and largely cancel. The transformed Eulerian mean combines them into advection by wa∗​, making the irreversible mean response to wave-activity deposition much clearer.
