A symmetric function k:X×X→R is a positive-semidefinite kernel when, for every m, every x1​,…,xm​∈X, and every c∈Rm,
∑i,j=1m​ci​cj​k(xi​,xj​)≥0.
(1)
Equivalently, every associated kernel matrix is a positive semidefinite matrix.
A Reproducing kernel Hilbert space H on X is a Hilbert space of real-valued functions such that every point-evaluation map f↦f(x) is continuous. The Riesz representation theorem then gives a function k(x,⋅)∈H satisfying the reproducing property
f(x)=⟨f,k(x,⋅)⟩H​.
(2)
Its reproducing kernel is k(x,y)=⟨k(x,⋅),k(y,⋅)⟩H​.

Suppose a feature map ϕ:Rd→Rp represented the Gaussian kernel. Choose n>p, a unit vector e, and points xi​=iae for i=0,…,n−1. Their kernel matrix is
Kij​=exp(−2σ2a2(i−j)2​)=r(i−j)2,r=e−a2/(2σ2).
(3)
For sufficiently large a, hence sufficiently small r, every row satisfies
∑j=i​∣Kij​∣≤2∑m≥1​rm2<1=Kii​.
(4)
Thus K is a symmetric strictly diagonally dominant matrix with positive diagonal and is therefore a positive-definite matrix, so rankK=n.

One standard construction uses the Debiased Lasso. Starting from the Square-root Lasso estimate β​, estimate a vector Θj​ that approximately inverts the jth column of the empirical Gram matrix Σ=XTX/n, for example by a Nodewise Lasso. Define
β​j​=β​j​+ΘjT​nXT(Y−Xβ​)​,Vj​=ΘjT​ΣΘj​,
(33)
and estimate σ by σ=∥Y−Xβ​∥2​/n​. The approximate two-sided level-α test rejects Hjβ​ when
​σVj​​n​β​j​​​>z1−α/2​,
(34)
where z1−α/2​ is a standard normal quantile.
Sufficient high-dimensional conditions include a Compatibility condition for the Lasso bounded away from zero, γ≍logp/n​, logp=o(n), and
n​slogp​⟶0,
(35)
together with the corresponding sparsity and consistency conditions for the nodewise inverse-Gram estimate. Under these assumptions the Debiased-Lasso asymptotic normality makes the rejection probability under Hjβ​ tend to α.
