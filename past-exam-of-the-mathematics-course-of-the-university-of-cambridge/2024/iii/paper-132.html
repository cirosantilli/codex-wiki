Apply the Shearer independence bound for a triangle-free graph. It states that an n-vertex triangle-free graph of maximum degree at most d has an independent set of size
α(G)≥cdnlogd​
(1)
for an absolute c>0. The result is immediate for bounded d after reducing c, while the theorem gives the asserted logarithmic gain for d large. Thus
α(G)≥cnlogd/d.​
(2)
For completeness, the key input in Shearer's proof is to expose a random independent set one degree scale at a time. Triangle-freeness makes every neighbourhood independent, so conditioning on earlier choices creates no edges inside the available neighbours. The expected gain at degree scale j is Ω(nj​/d); summing over the Θ(logd) nonempty scales gives Ω(nlogd/d). The entropy, or hard-core-model, form of the argument makes this scale calculation rigorous without losing vertices counted at adjacent scales.

Let the blue edges form a graph B on N vertices, and suppose there is no blue copy of H. For every vertex x, the graph B[NB​(x)] has maximum degree at most one. Indeed, if some y∈NB​(x) had two neighbours z,w inside NB​(x), then

The uniform hypergraph Ramsey number R(r)(k) is the least N such that every red-blue colouring of the r-element subsets of an N-element set contains a k-element set all of whose r-subsets have one colour.
Colour each triple of an N-element set independently and uniformly red or blue. For a fixed k-set, the probability of being monochromatic is
21−(3k​).
(9)
The expected number of monochromatic k-sets is therefore
(kN​)21−(3k​)≤21+klog2​(eN/k)−(3k​).
(10)
Take N=⌊2ck2⌋ with any sufficiently small absolute c>0. The exponent is negative for large k, since its leading terms are ck3−k3/6. Thus some colouring has no monochromatic k-set, proving
R(3)(k)≥2ck2.​
(11)

We use the Erdős-Rado selection argument. Put
m=R(2)(k−1)+1.
(12)
Starting with a sufficiently large reservoir, choose vertices v1​,…,vm​ in order. After choosing vj​, successively halve the remaining reservoir for each i<j so that the colour of
{vi​,vj​,x}
(13)
is constant as x ranges over the final reservoir. The total number of halvings is at most (2m​), so 2(2m​)+m initial vertices suffice.
Colour the pair ij, for i<j<m, by the stabilized colour of {vi​,vj​,vℓ​} for ℓ>j. Among v1​,…,vm−1​, the definition of R(2)(k−1) gives a monochromatic set of k−1 vertices. Adjoining vm​ gives a monochromatic k-set for the original triple colouring. Hence
R(3)(k)≤2(2R(2)(k−1)+1​)+R(2)(k−1)+1.
(14)
The binomial upper bound for a Ramsey number gives R(2)(k−1)<22k, so the right-hand side is at most
22Ck​
(15)
for an absolute C>0.

The blue hypergraph in the question is the three-edge hypergraph K4(3)−​. The Erdős-Hajnal bound for the three-edge hypergraph on four vertices states that every K4(3)−​-free three-uniform hypergraph on N vertices has an independent set of size at least
cloglogNlogN​.
(16)
Its proof exposes vertices successively and studies their link graphs. A blue triangle in a link is exactly a blue K4(3)−​, so all links are triangle-free; applying the Shearer independence bound for a triangle-free graph in dyadic degree ranges either adds vertices to the independent set or leaves a reservoir whose logarithm decreases by only O(loglogN) per selected vertex. Iteration yields the displayed lower bound.
Now take N=kCk. For sufficiently large C,
cloglogNlogN​=clog(Cklogk)Cklogk​≥k.
(17)
Thus, if there is no blue copy of H, the blue hypergraph has an independent k-set, which is a red Kk(3)​. Therefore
r(H,Kk(3)​)≤kCk.​
(18)

A set R⊆V(G) is (s,k)-rich if every s-element subset of R has at least k common neighbours.
Choose x1​,…,xt​ independently and uniformly from V(G), with repetition, and put
W=N(x1​)∩⋯∩N(xt​).
(19)
By convexity,
E∣W∣=∑v∈V(G)​(nd(v)​)t≥n(n22m​)t=n2t−1(2m)t​.
(20)
Let Y count the s-subsets S⊆W having fewer than k common neighbours in G. For each such S,
P(S⊆W)=(n∣N(S)∣​)t<(nk​)t,
(21)
and therefore
EY≤(sn​)(nk​)t.
(22)
Delete one vertex from every bad s-subset of W. The remaining set R is (s,k)-rich and satisfies ∣R∣≥∣W∣−Y. The hypothesis gives
E(∣W∣−Y)≥r,
(23)
so some choice has ∣R∣≥r.

Choose a bipartition V(H)=A⊔B and inject A into the (d,k)-rich set R. For each b∈B, the images of its neighbours form a set of at most d vertices of R. Extend it, if necessary, to a d-element subset of R. Richness supplies at least k common neighbours in G.
Embed the vertices of B one at a time. At every step fewer than k vertices have already been used, while at least k common neighbours are available, so one unused choice remains. This greedy embedding preserves every edge of H and proves
H⊆G.​
(24)

For disjoint nonempty vertex sets A,B, write
d(A,B)=∣A∣∣B∣e(A,B)​.
(29)
The pair (A,B) is a regular pair of vertex sets with parameter ϵ if
∣d(X,Y)−d(A,B)∣≤ϵ
(30)
whenever X⊆A, Y⊆B, ∣X∣≥ϵ∣A∣, and ∣Y∣≥ϵ∣B∣.
The Szemerédi regularity lemma says that for every ϵ>0 and m0​ there are M,n0​ such that every graph on at least n0​ vertices has a partition
V=V0​⊔V1​⊔⋯⊔Vm​,
(31)
where m0​≤m≤M, ∣V0​∣≤ϵ∣V∣, the classes V1​,…,Vm​ have equal size, and all but at most ϵm2 pairs (Vi​,Vj​) are ϵ-uniform.

Because χ(H)=3, every bipartite graph is H-free. The balanced complete bipartite graph therefore gives
liminfn→∞​(2n​)ex(n,H)​≥21​.
(32)
For the reverse bound, fix δ>0 and suppose that G has more than (1/2+δ)(2n​) edges. Apply the Szemerédi regularity lemma with parameters much smaller than δ. Form the reduced graph whose vertices are the regularity classes and whose edges are the regular pairs of density above a small fixed threshold. Edges inside classes, irregular pairs, and regular pairs below the threshold account for oδ​(n2) edges. The remaining edges force the reduced graph to have more than m2/4 edges.
By the Turan theorem, the reduced graph contains a triangle. The three corresponding regular pairs all have positive density, and the graph embedding lemma for regular pairs embeds every fixed three-colourable graph, in particular H, across suitable repeated subclusters of these three classes. Thus every sufficiently large graph of density above 1/2+δ contains H. Letting δ↓0 gives
n→∞lim​(2n​)ex(n,H)​=21​.​
(33)
This is the chromatic-number-three case of the Erdős-Stone theorem.

Let R=R(C5​) be the fixed graph Ramsey number of the five-cycle. Partition ⌊n/R⌋R vertices into ⌊n/R⌋ disjoint blocks of size R. For any one block, the probability that G(n,1/2) induces a complete graph is
q=2−(2R​)>0.
(34)
These events are independent for the disjoint blocks. Therefore the probability that none of them induces KR​ is
(1−q)⌊n/R⌋⟶0.
(35)
With probability tending to one, G contains a copy of KR​. Every red-blue colouring of this copy contains a monochromatic C5​ by the definition of R(C5​). Hence
n→∞lim​P(G→C5​)=1.​
(36)
