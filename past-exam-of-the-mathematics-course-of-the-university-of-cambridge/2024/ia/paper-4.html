The Wilson theorem states that for a prime p,
(p−1)!≡−1(modp).
(1)
Indeed, every nonzero residue modulo p has a unique multiplicative inverse. The only residues equal to their own inverses solve x2≡1(modp), hence are 1 and −1. Pairing every other residue with its distinct inverse leaves
(p−1)!≡1⋅(−1)≡−1(modp).
(2)
The Fermat little theorem states that for prime p,
ap≡a(modp);
(3)
equivalently, if p∤a, then ap−1≡1(modp).
Wilson's theorem at p=31 gives
30!=30⋅29⋅28!≡2⋅28!≡−1(mod31),
(4)
so 28!≡15(mod31). Also 729≡16(mod31), and therefore
28!⋅729≡15⋅16≡23(mod31)​.

Since N2 is countable by diagonal enumeration, the array (n,m)↦an,m​ enumerates ⋃n​An​ after repetitions are removed. This proves that a countable union of countable sets is countable.
