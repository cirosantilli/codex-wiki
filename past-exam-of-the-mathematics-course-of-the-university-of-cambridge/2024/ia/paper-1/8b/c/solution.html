First note that if A is normal, then
∥(A−λI)v∥=∥(A†−λI)v∥
(88)
for every v. Hence an eigenvector v with Av=λv also satisfies
A†v=λv.
(89)
Normalize v and extend it to an orthonormal basis. Let U have these basis vectors as columns. The first column of U†AU is (λ,0,…,0)T. For every w⊥v,
⟨v,Aw⟩=⟨A†v,w⟩=⟨λv,w⟩=0,
(90)
so the first row also has no off-diagonal entries. Therefore
U†AU=(λ0​0B​).
(91)
Comparing the two block products in the normality identity shows that BB†=B†B, so B is normal.
The result is trivial in dimension one. Applying the induction hypothesis to B and adjoining the eigenvector v gives an orthonormal eigenbasis for A. This proves the unitary diagonalization of a normal matrix.
