To say that f is twice differentiable at a means that f′ is defined in a neighbourhood of a and is differentiable at a; its derivative is f′′(a).
By the chain rule, near a,
(g∘f)′=(g′∘f)f′.
(120)
Since f is differentiable and g′ is differentiable at f(a), another use of the chain rule shows that g′∘f is differentiable at a. The product rule then proves that the displayed product is differentiable. Hence g∘f is twice differentiable, with the second derivative chain rule
(g∘f)′′(a)=g′′(f(a))[f′(a)]2+g′(f(a))f′′(a)​.
