A Möbius transformation is generated by translations z↦z+b, nonzero scalings z↦az, and inversion z↦1/z. Translations and scalings plainly take circles and lines to circles and lines. More uniformly, every circle or line has an equation
α∣z∣2+βz+β​z+γ=0,
(1)
where α,γ∈R; the case α=0 is a line. Under inversion, put z=1/w and multiply by ∣w∣2 to obtain
γ∣w∣2+β​w+βw+α=0,
(2)
which is again such an equation. The generators, and hence every Möbius transformation, therefore preserve Generalized circle under a Möbius transformation.
Now let
f(z)=cz+daz+b​.
(3)
If c=0, its finite pole −d/c lies on some Euclidean circle, and that circle maps to a line because its image contains infinity. Thus f takes every circle to a circle exactly when c=0, so the subgroup in question is the Affine subgroup of the Möbius group, consisting of z↦az+b with a=0.
It is not normal. If I(z)=1/z and Tb​(z)=z+b with b=0, then Tb​ is affine but
ITb​I(z)=1+bzz​
(4)
has a finite pole and is not affine. Hence conjugation by I does not preserve the subgroup.

The special orthogonal group is
SO(n)={A∈Mn​(R):ATA=I, detA=1}.
(5)
If
A=(ac​bd​)∈SO(2),
(6)
its first column is a unit vector, say (cosθ,sinθ)T. The second column is the unique unit vector perpendicular to it that gives positive determinant, namely (−sinθ,cosθ)T. Thus
A=(cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​),
(7)
so A is rotation through θ about the origin.
For A∈SO(3), its real characteristic polynomial of odd degree has a real eigenvalue. Every eigenvalue of an orthogonal matrix has modulus one, so every real eigenvalue is ±1. Nonreal eigenvalues occur in conjugate pairs whose product is one, while detA=1; if all eigenvalues are real, their product likewise forces at least one to be +1. Hence A fixes a nonzero vector u. The plane u⊥ is A-invariant, and the restriction to it is an orientation-preserving orthogonal map. By the SO(2) result it is a planar rotation. Therefore A is a rotation about the axis Ru.

The Cauchy theorem for groups says that if a prime p divides ∣G∣, then G contains an element of order p.
Consider
X={(x1​,…,xp​)∈Gp:x1​x2​⋯xp​=1}.
(36)
The first p−1 entries determine the last, so ∣X∣=∣G∣p−1, which is divisible by p. Cyclic rotation acts on X: if the product is one, then
x2​⋯xp​x1​=x1−1​(x1​⋯xp​)x1​=1.
(37)
