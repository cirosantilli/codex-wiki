A topological space is compact if every open cover has a finite subcover. It is Hausdorff if every pair of distinct points has disjoint open neighbourhoods.
Let Y be closed in compact X, and let {Ui​} be an open cover of Y by sets open in X. Adding the open set X∖Y gives a cover of X, which has a finite subcover. Removing X∖Y leaves a finite subcover of Y. Thus every closed subspace of a compact space is compact.
Now let A,B be disjoint closed subsets of a compact Hausdorff space. They are compact. For each a∈A and b∈B, choose disjoint open sets Ua,b​∋a and Va,b​∋b. Fixing a, finitely many Va,bj​​ cover B. Put
Ua​=⋂j​Ua,bj​​,Va​=⋃j​Va,bj​​.
(80)
Then Ua​ contains a, Va​ contains B, and they are disjoint. Finitely many Uai​​ cover A. Therefore
U=⋃i​Uai​​,V=⋂i​Vai​​
(81)
are disjoint open neighbourhoods of A and B. This proves the normality of a compact Hausdorff space.
