An ideal of R/I has the form J/I for an ideal J⊇I of R. If R is Noetherian, write
J=(a1​,…,ak​).
(70)
Then
J/I=(a1​+I,…,ak​+I),
(71)
so every ideal of R/I is finitely generated. Hence
R/I is Noetherian​.
(72)
The Hilbert basis theorem states that R[X] is Noetherian whenever R is Noetherian. The integers form a principal ideal domain, so every ideal of Z has one generator and Z is Noetherian. It follows that Z[X] is Noetherian.
For a nonsquare integer d, evaluation at d​ gives
Z[X]⟶Z[d​],X⟼d​.
(73)
It is surjective and its kernel is (X2−d). Therefore
Z[d​]≅Z[X]/(X2−d)​
(74)
is Noetherian by the quotient result. This is the noetherianity of a quadratic integer order.
