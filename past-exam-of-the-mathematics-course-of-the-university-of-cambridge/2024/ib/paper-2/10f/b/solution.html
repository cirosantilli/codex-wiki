Let X be compact Hausdorff, let x∈X, and let U be a neighbourhood of x. Choose an open O with x∈O⊆U. Apply the separation result from part (a) to the disjoint closed sets {x} and X∖O. There is an open V∋x whose closure lies in O. Then
K=V
(82)
is compact, contains the neighbourhood V of x, and lies in U. Thus X is locally compact.
Conversely, suppose X is locally compact Hausdorff and A∩K is closed in every compact K⊆X. For x∈/A, choose a compact neighbourhood K of x and an open set U with
x∈U⊆K.
(83)
Since A∩K is closed in K, there is an open set W⊆X such that
K∖(A∩K)=K∩W.
(84)
Then U∩W is an open neighbourhood of x disjoint from A. Thus X∖A is open and
A is closed​.
(85)
This is the compactly detected closed-set theorem in a locally compact Hausdorff space.
