as a monic quadratic in X over Q[Y]. By the Gauss lemma for polynomials, reducibility in Q[X,Y] would imply reducibility in Q(Y)[X], so 1−Y2 would be a square in Q(Y). This is impossible: its zero at Y=1 has odd order one, whereas every zero or pole of a square in a rational-function field has even order. Hence
X2+Y2−1 is irreducible over Q​.
(8)
The same odd-valuation obstruction to a rational-function square works over every field of characteristic different from two, since the zeros Y=1 and Y=−1 are then distinct. It does not work over every field. In characteristic two,
X2+Y2−1=(X+Y+1)2,
(9)
so the polynomial is reducible.
