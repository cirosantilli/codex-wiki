The set Y is path-connected as the continuous image of [1,∞), hence connected. Its closure is Y together with the unit circle. Indeed g(k+θ/(2π))→eiθ as k→∞, and any convergent sequence of spiral points either has bounded parameters, giving a point of Y, or has parameters tending to infinity along a subsequence, giving radius one. The closure of a connected set is connected: a separation of its closure would induce a separation of the dense original set, with neither open part missing that set. The closed disk is connected and meets this closure in the unit circle; their union Y∪D is therefore connected.
To show it is not path-connected, suppose a path α:[0,1]→Y∪D starts in Y and ends in the disk. Let u∗​ be its first contact with the disk. The disk is closed, so this first contact exists, and u∗​>0. Before it, the path belongs to Y and has a continuous uniquely determined parameter
t(u)=∣α(u)∣−11​,α(u)=g(t(u)).
(36)
Continuity at contact forces ∣α(u∗​)∣=1, and hence t(u)→∞ as u↑u∗​. The intermediate value theorem supplies times approaching u∗​ at which t(u) is an integer, and other times at which it is an integer plus 1/2. These times must approach contact because t is bounded on every compact interval strictly before it. Along the first sequence α(u)→1; along the second it tends to −1. This contradicts continuity at u∗​. Thus no path joins the spiral to the disk, proving failure of path-connectedness for the spiral accumulating on a circle.
