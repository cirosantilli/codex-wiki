Model the two samples as independent multinomial counts with fixed row totals NA​=NB​=500 and three category probabilities. The null hypothesis is pAj​=pBj​=pj​ for every category j. Up to combinatorial factors the likelihood is ∏ij​pijOij​​. Without the null restriction its maximizers are Oij​/Ni​; under the null, maximizing ∑j​(OAj​+OBj​)logpj​ subject to ∑j​pj​=1 gives
p​j​=1000OAj​+OBj​​,Eij​=Ni​p​j​.
(31)
The observed column totals are 243,281,476, giving fitted expected counts (121.5,140.5,238) in each row.
To derive the null distribution, put D=OA​−OB​. Under the null its mean is zero and covariance is 1000(diagp−ppT). The multivariate central limit theorem gives, asymptotically, the standardized contrast Zj​=Dj​/1000pj​​ a centered normal distribution with covariance
I−uuT,u=(p1​​,p2​​,p3​​)T,uTu=1.
(32)
This is the orthogonal projection onto a two-dimensional plane. In an orthonormal basis of that plane, the two nonzero coordinates are independent standard normal variables, so ∑j​Zj2​ tends to χ22​. The pooled estimates are consistent; substituting them for pj​ leaves this limit unchanged by Slutsky's theorem. Since OAj​−EAj​=Dj​/2 and OBj​−EBj​=−Dj​/2, the resulting statistic is precisely the Pearson chi-squared test of homogeneity statistic
X2=∑i,j​Eij​(Oij​−Eij​)2​=∑j​1000p​j​Dj2​​,X2d​χ22​.
(33)
This supplies the two degrees of freedom rather than treating all six cells as independent. Every fitted expected count is large, supporting the approximation.
For these data,
X2=2(121.518.52​+140.54.52​+238142​)=7.56906.​
(34)
It exceeds the two-degree-of-freedom 95th percentile 5.99, but not the 99th percentile 9.21. Hence reject equal score distributions at the 5% level, but not at the 1% level. The asymptotic p-value is Pr(χ22​≥7.56906)=e−7.56906/2≃0.02272.
