Put zi​=xi​−xˉ, so ∑zi​=0. The ordinary least squares criterion is S(α,β)=∑i​(Yi​−α−βzi​)2. Setting both partial derivatives to zero gives ∑i​(Yi​−α−βzi​)=0 and ∑i​zi​(Yi​−α−βzi​)=0. Therefore, provided Sxx​=∑zi2​>0, the centred simple linear regression estimators are
α=Yˉ,β​=∑i​(xi​−xˉ)2∑i​(xi​−xˉ)Yi​​.​
(5)
The Hessian of S is 2diag(n,Sxx​), which is positive definite, so this stationary point is the unique minimum. From the mean-zero errors, both estimators are unbiased; independence and common error variance give Varα=σ2/n, Varβ​=σ2/Sxx​, and zero covariance. Normality is not needed for this derivation. If all xi​ coincide, the slope is not identifiable and only the intercept is determined.
Using the PDF data gives xˉ=6.2, yˉ​=42.4, Sxx​=86.8 and Sxy​=−257.4. In particular, the fourth cost is 37 in the PDF; the TeX's 75 is a transcription error and would contradict the supplied covariance sum. The fitted simple linear regression is
y​=42.4−4341287​(x−6.2).​
(6)
Here the centred intercept estimate is 42.4; an intercept at x=0 would instead be α−β​xˉ. For eight units the predicted unit cost is
y​(8)=42.4−4341287​(1.8)≈37.06 pounds.​
