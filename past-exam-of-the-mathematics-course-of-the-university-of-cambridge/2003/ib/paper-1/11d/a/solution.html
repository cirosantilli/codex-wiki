Away from the source point, the Green function obeys the homogeneous equation and must satisfy the appropriate endpoint condition. Thus it has the form Asin(kx) to the left of ξ and Bsin(k(π−x)) to the right. It must be continuous at ξ: otherwise its second distributional derivative would contain an unwanted derivative of a Dirac delta. Integrating across ξ gives the jump condition Gx​(ξ+,ξ)−Gx​(ξ−,ξ)=1.
The left and right homogeneous solutions u=sin(kx), v=sin(k(π−x)) have constant Wronskian
uv′−u′v=−ksin(kπ).
(19)
This is nonzero for real noninteger k. Dividing the product of the left and right solutions by that Wronskian therefore gives the Dirichlet Helmholtz Green function on an interval
G(x,ξ)=−ksin(kπ)sin(kmin(x,ξ))sin(k(π−max(x,ξ)))​.​
(20)
The formula vanishes at both endpoints, is continuous at the source and has derivative jump one, verifying all distributional and boundary conditions. A difference of two such Green functions would be a homogeneous Dirichlet solution; it vanishes because sin(kπ)=0, proving uniqueness. Although zero is excluded by the stated noninteger condition, the removable k→0 limit is −min(x,ξ)(π−max(x,ξ))/π.
