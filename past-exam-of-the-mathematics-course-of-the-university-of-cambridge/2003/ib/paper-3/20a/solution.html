The time-independent Schrödinger equation is [−ℏ2∇2/(2m)+V]ψ=Eψ, with attractive Coulomb potential V(r)=−κ/r, where κ=e2/(4πε0​). Separating ψ=R(r)Yℓm​(θ,φ) uses the spherical harmonic angular eigenvalue −ℓ(ℓ+1) of the unit-sphere Laplacian. The radial kinetic contribution is −ℏ2(R′′+2R′/r)/(2m), the angular kinetic contribution is the centrifugal term ℏ2ℓ(ℓ+1)R/(2mr2), and the remaining term is the attractive potential energy. The right side is the energy eigenvalue times the radial wavefunction. Here the stated m neglects nuclear recoil; including it replaces the electron mass by the reduced mass.
For ℓ=0, substitution of the ground-state exponential gives
−2mℏ2​(α2−2α/r)−rκ​=E1​.
(84)
Its 1/r coefficient must vanish and its constant coefficient determines the energy, so
α=ℏ2mκ​=a0​1​,E1​=−2ℏ2mκ2​.​
(85)
Here a0​=ℏ2/(mκ) is the Bohr radius.
For R2​=N2​(r+b)e−βr, direct differentiation yields
R2′′​+r2​R2′​=N2​e−βr[β2(r+b)−4β+r2−2βb​].
(86)
Cancel the nonzero exponential in the radial Schrödinger equation and compare coefficients of r, the constant term, and 1/r. They give, respectively,
E2​=−2mℏ2β2​,m2ℏ2β​=κ,mℏ2​(βb−1)−κb=0.
(87)
Consequently
β=2a0​1​,b=−2a0​,E2​=−8ℏ2mκ2​=41​E1​.​
(88)
The positive decay constants ensure normalizability; the second radial wavefunction has one positive-radius node at 2a0​, as appropriate to the first radial excitation.
If the entire energy gap is carried by one photon, neglecting atomic recoil, conservation of energy gives the requested frequency
νgap​=hE2​−E1​​=8hℏ23mκ2​=8hℏ2(4πε0​)23me4​.​
(89)
There is a physical qualification: these are the 2s and 1s states, so the usual single-photon electric-dipole transition is forbidden by the electric-dipole selection rules for hydrogen. Explicitly, both angular factors are Y00​, and ∫∣Y00​∣2r^dΩ=0, so every component of ⟨1s∣r∣2s⟩ vanishes. The leading isolated-atom decay is instead two-photon emission, for which ν1​+ν2​=νgap​ and individual frequencies are not fixed. This metastability and the two-photon channel are documented in NIST's hydrogen spectral-data compilation. Thus the boxed answer is the energy-gap frequency under the printed single-photon assumption, rather than an assertion of an allowed electric-dipole line.
