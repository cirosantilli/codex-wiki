Write the cone as z=rcotα, with r the distance from its axis and 0<α<π/2. Its Euclidean metric gives
ds2=dr2+r2dθ2+dz2=csc2αdr2+r2dθ2.

The endpoint conditions determine C and θ0​ on the selected angular branch.
To verify that this stationary path really minimizes arc length, use the local isometry from a circular cone to the plane. Set s=r/sinα and ϕ=θsinα; then ds2+s2dϕ2 is the plane metric. The displayed path is a straight line scos(ϕ−ϕ0​)=C/sinα, so its segment realizes the plane distance. Choose endpoint angular lifts with ∣Δθ∣≤π; then ∣Δϕ∣<π, and the joining segment avoids the apex. Its length is
sinα1​r12​+r22​−2r1​r2​cos(sinαΔθ)​.
(8)
Other lifts with developed angular difference less than π give at least this distance; an angular change of at least π has infimum at least s1​+s2​, attained only if passage through the apex is allowed. This proves the global choice above. Endpoints on a common generator have the radial segment as shortest path, which is the limiting case omitted by the r(θ) parametrization. If an endpoint is the apex, its shortest path is a generator. Thus shortest paths are straight segments after developing the cone.
