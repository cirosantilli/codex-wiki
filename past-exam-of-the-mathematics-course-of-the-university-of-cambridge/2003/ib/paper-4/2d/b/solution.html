For the single-frequency response, use separation of variables with u=R(r)sinωt. Set k=ω/c and V=rR; then V′′+k2V=0, so V=Csinkr+Dcoskr. A finite origin value forces D=0, since coskr/r is singular. Since sinkr/r→k, the prescribed origin amplitude gives C=A/k. The regular time-harmonic spherical wave is therefore
u(r,t)=Aωr/csin(ωr/c)​sinωt​,
(4)
with the quotient defined by its limit at zero.
This answers the literal finite-origin condition in the class of regular single-frequency responses. It is a standing combination of inward and outward waves: the numerator is proportional to cos(kr−ωt)−cos(kr+ωt). A purely outgoing wave from an ideal point source would instead be proportional to r−1sin[ω(t−r/c)], which has no finite prescribed value u(0,t). Such a source must be specified by a singularity strength, for example limr↓0​ru. The source's wording supplies neither an initial field nor an outgoing singular-source normalization; without restriction to the stated harmonic response, a general initial-value problem is not uniquely determined by the one origin condition.
