Introduce nonnegative slack variables s1​,s3​, a nonnegative surplus variable s2​, and an artificial variable a. Phase I of the two-phase simplex method minimizes w=a. The initial simplex dictionary is
s1​as3​w​=9−4x1​−5x2​,=12−6x1​−4x2​−x3​+s2​,=3−3x1​−2x2​+x3​,=12−6x1​−4x2​−x3​+s2​.​
(44)
With the nonbasic variables zero this is feasible for the artificial problem. Enter x1​; the minimum ratios are 9/4,12/6,3/3, so s3​ leaves. Solving its row for x1​ and substituting gives
x1​s1​aw​=1−32​x2​+31​x3​−31​s3​,=5−37​x2​−34​x3​+34​s3​,=6−3x3​+s2​+2s3​,=6−3x3​+s2​+2s3​.​
(45)
Now enter x3​. The ratios from decreasing basic variables are 5/(4/3)=15/4 and 6/3=2, so a leaves. The resulting dictionary is
x3​x1​s1​w​=2+31​s2​+32​s3​−31​a,=35​−32​x2​+91​s2​−91​s3​−91​a,=37​−37​x2​−94​s2​+94​s3​+94​a,=a.​
(46)
Setting the nonbasic variables zero gives a=0, so Phase I has reached its minimum and found feasibility. Remove a and reinstate the true objective z. Substitution of the basic variables gives
z=3103​−346​x2​+944​s2​+973​s3​.
(47)
Enter x2​. The decreasing rows give ratios (5/3)/(2/3)=5/2 for x1​ and (7/3)/(7/3)=1 for s1​, so s1​ leaves. Phase II then has
x2​x1​x3​z​=1−73​s1​−214​s2​+214​s3​,=1+72​s1​+215​s2​−215​s3​,=2+31​s2​+32​s3​,=19+746​s1​+21164​s2​+21109​s3​.​
(48)
Every nonbasic variable is nonnegative and every reduced cost is positive. Thus the simplex optimality criterion proves
(x1​,x2​,x3​)=(1,1,2),zmin​=19​.
(49)
All three original constraints are active. Positivity of all three reduced costs also proves uniqueness: equality in the objective lower bound forces s1​=s2​=s3​=0, which fixes the displayed solution.
