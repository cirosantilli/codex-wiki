For constrained optimization in minimization form, let x∈X, gi​(x)≤0 and hj​(x)=0, and define the Lagrangian
L(x,λ,ν)=f(x)+∑i​λi​gi​(x)+∑j​νj​hj​(x).
(9)
The Lagrangian sufficiency theorem says: if x∗​ is feasible, λi​≥0, λi​gi​(x∗​)=0 for every i, and x∗​ minimizes L(⋅,λ,ν) globally over X, then x∗​ minimizes f over the feasible set. Equality multipliers νj​ may have either sign.
Indeed, for every feasible x,
f(x)≥L(x,λ,ν)≥L(x∗​,λ,ν)=f(x∗​).
(10)
The first inequality uses the inequality-multiplier signs and the equality constraints; the last equality uses complementary slackness. This proves global optimality without assuming convexity or differentiability. The maximization version reverses the relevant signs, or follows by replacing f with −f.
When X is convex and the Lagrangian is differentiable and convex, the first-order condition ∇x​L(x∗​)=0 implies its global minimum, because L(x)≥L(x∗​)+∇L(x∗​)⋅(x−x∗​). Thus feasible stationarity plus complementary slackness is sufficient in that convex setting. Mere stationarity in a general nonconvex problem is not the theorem's global-minimum hypothesis.
