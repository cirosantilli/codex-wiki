Irrotational flow means ∇×u=0; incompressible flow means ∇⋅u=0. The given streamfunction automatically satisfies the second condition, since ∂x​ψy​−∂y​ψx​=0. For an oriented path with unit tangent (dx/ds,dy/ds), choose its right-hand unit normal n=(dy/ds,−dx/ds). Then
u⋅nds=ψy​dy+ψx​dx=dψ,∫x1​x2​​u⋅nds=ψ(x2​)−ψ(x1​)​.
(56)
The opposite normal reverses the sign; the source's unspecified normal orientation must be chosen consistently. The volume flux between two streamlines is thus their streamfunction difference. A closed contour has zero net flux, expressing mass conservation for constant-density flow without a source inside.
For the channel, a rigid boundary has zero normal velocity and hence constant streamfunction. Choose zero on the lower wall. Uniform inflow at infinity must have horizontal velocity −m, so there ψ=−my. The upper wall then has constant −m. The unbroken left wall connects to it and also has constant −m. The jump by m between the walls at the corner represents the withdrawn flux. Finally the vorticity is vx​−uy​=−Δψ, so irrotationality gives Δψ=0.
Write ψ=−my+w. Then w vanishes at y=0,1 and at infinity, while w(0,y)=−m(1−y) for 0<y<1. Separation of variables gives the harmonic sine mode in a half-strip e−nπxsinnπy. The Fourier sine series coefficients of the left boundary are
bn​=2∫01​[−m(1−y)]sin(nπy)dy=−nπ2m​.
(57)
Therefore the corner sink in a semi-infinite channel is
ψ(x,y)=−my−π2m​n=1∑∞​ne−nπxsin(nπy)​​.

For x>0 the series and its differentiated series converge on compact subsets, making the sum harmonic. At x=0, ∑n≥1​sin(nπy)/n=π(1−y)/2 for 0<y<1, so the left-wall value is exactly −m. The other wall values and far-field limit follow directly.
The plus sign printed in the final series is inconsistent with the printed boundary conditions. It would give m−2my on the left wall, not −m. The negative sign above is required. As a local flux check, near the corner the wall values give ψ∼−2mθ/π; the radial velocity is −2m/(πr) and its outward flux across a quarter-circle is −m, precisely withdrawal.
