For a partition a=x0​<⋯<xk​=b and tags ξj​∈[xj−1​,xj​], form the Riemann sum ∑j=1k​f(ξj​)(xj​−xj−1​). The Riemann integral is the unique real number I such that for every ε>0 there is δ>0 for which every tagged partition with mesh maxj​(xj​−xj−1​)<δ has its sum within ε of I. Then I=∫ab​f(x)dx. The stated continuity ensures existence, which need not be proved here.
The properties required are linearity, the integral of a constant, and monotonicity of the Riemann integral. Monotonicity follows directly because a nonnegative function has nonnegative Riemann sums, and therefore a nonnegative integral. Integrating f−m≥0 and M−f≥0 gives
m(b−a)≤∫ab​f(x)dx≤M(b−a).​
(44)
Since g≥0, multiplication preserves the pointwise inequalities: mg≤fg≤Mg. The continuous products are integrable, so the same monotonicity and linearity give
m∫ab​g(x)dx≤∫ab​f(x)g(x)dx≤M∫ab​g(x)dx.​
(45)
Neither m nor f needs to be nonnegative for this argument.
For the first limit, set rn​=n−1/2, gn​(x)=ne−nx, and εn​=max0≤x≤rn​​∣f(x)−f(0)∣. By continuity at zero, εn​→0. On this shrinking interval f(0)−εn​≤f(x)≤f(0)+εn​, and
∫0rn​​gn​(x)dx=1−e−n​.
(46)
Applying the weighted bounds just proved gives
(f(0)−εn​)(1−e−n​)≤∫0rn​​nf(x)e−nxdx≤(f(0)+εn​)(1−e−n​).
(47)
Both bounding expressions tend to f(0), so the squeeze theorem proves the first limit.
For the full interval, continuity on [0,1] gives a finite bound B=max[0,1]​∣f∣ by the extreme value theorem. The remaining tail has absolute value at most
​∫rn​1​nf(x)e−nxdx​≤B∫rn​1​ne−nxdx=B(e−n​−e−n)⟶0.
(48)
Adding this to the already evaluated near-end integral proves
n→∞lim​∫01/n​​nf(x)e−nxdx=n→∞lim​∫01​nf(x)e−nxdx=f(0).​
(49)
These are instances of a one-sided exponential approximate identity: the positive weight has total mass tending to one, concentrated in a shrinking neighborhood of zero. No interchange of limit and integral has been assumed.
