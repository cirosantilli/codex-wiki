There are nontrivial proper normal subgroups. The determinant defines a surjective group homomorphism G→{1,−1}. Its kernel is the rotational symmetry group of a cube, a normal subgroup of order twenty-four. Also {I,−I} is a central subgroup of order two: central inversion preserves the cube and commutes with every matrix. Both provide affirmative examples.
For the body diagonal through (1,1,1), write a general cube symmetry as Dε​P, with P a permutation matrix and Dε​=diag(ε1​,ε2​,ε3​), each εj​=±1. Since P(1,1,1)T=(1,1,1)T, preserving the unoriented diagonal requires (ε1​,ε2​,ε3​) to be either (1,1,1) or (−1,−1,−1). Thus
H={εP:ε∈{1,−1}, P a 3×3 permutation matrix}.
(24)
The map (π,ε)↦εPπ​ from S3​×C2​ to H is a group homomorphism, because central inversion commutes with every coordinate permutation. It is surjective by the displayed description and injective because a permutation matrix cannot equal the negative of another permutation matrix. Consequently the body-diagonal stabilizer in the cube symmetry group is
H≅S3​×C2​,∣H∣=12.​
(25)
This also agrees with the orbit-stabilizer theorem for the transitive action on the four body diagonals. The line is preserved as a set, so exchanging its opposite endpoints is allowed.
