To prove the Archimedean property, suppose the positive integers were bounded above in R. By the least upper bound axiom they would have a finite supremum s. Since s−1 is not an upper bound, some positive integer k satisfies k>s−1. Then the positive integer k+1>s, contradicting the definition of s. Thus the positive integers are unbounded. Equivalently, for any positive real a,b, some positive integer n satisfies na>b, by applying unboundedness to b/a.
