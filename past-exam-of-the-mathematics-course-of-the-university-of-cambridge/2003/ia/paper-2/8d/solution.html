Differentiate the proposed first integral along the vector field:
K˙=2x(2α​x+y−2y3)+(2y−4y3)(−x)=αx2​.
(43)
At α=0, K is conserved and the system is an inverted quartic oscillator phase portrait. Its equilibria are O=(0,0) and S±​=(0,±1/2​). The Jacobian matrix is
J=(0−1​1−6y20​).
(44)
At the origin its eigenvalues are ±i. Moreover K has a strict local minimum there, and its nearby positive contours are closed, so the origin is a genuine center equilibrium, not merely an inconclusive linear centre. The arrows circulate clockwise: on the positive x-axis, y˙​<0. At either S±​, the eigenvalues are ±2​, giving saddle equilibria. The stable eigendirection is δx=2​δy, and the unstable one is δx=−2​δy.
The special energy level factors as
K=41​⟺x2=(y2−21​)2,x=±(y2−21​).​
(45)
Between the two saddles, its left arc has x<0 and travels upward from S−​ to S+​; its right arc has x>0 and travels downward from S+​ to S−​. These two heteroclinic orbits bound the central eye. Trajectories inside the eye are closed periodic orbits and do not tend to the centre as t→∞.
More explicitly, a level K=C has
x2=y4−y2+C.
(46)
For 0<C<1/4, its bounded central component is periodic, while its exterior components turn at ∣y∣=(1+1−4C​)/2​ and then escape. For C>1/4, x never vanishes and the two branches run from one vertical infinity to the other. Levels C≤0 have only exterior nonconstant components, apart from the origin at C=0.
On the upper outer separatrix, the x>0 branch arrives from positive infinity and approaches S+​ in forward infinite time; the x<0 branch leaves S+​ towards positive infinity. On the lower outer separatrix, the x<0 branch arrives from negative infinity and approaches S−​, while the x>0 branch leaves towards negative infinity. Approaching a saddle along its stable manifold is exponential at rate 2​. A trajectory close to but off a separatrix can spend a long time near the saddle before completing a periodic orbit or escaping.
The escape is finite-time blowup, not an asymptote at t=∞. On any escaping level, ∣x∣=y4−y2+C​∼y2, so the remaining travel time is bounded by a convergent integral proportional to ∫∞dy/y2. Thus an outward upper branch has y∼(T−t)−1, x∼−(T−t)−2; an outward lower branch has y∼−(T−t)−1, x∼(T−t)−2. The statement about approaching from infinity likewise refers to a finite past endpoint. Only equilibria, periodic trajectories and trajectories tending to saddles have a future defined for all time in this conservative portrait.
For small positive α, the equilibria remain in place, but the origin's eigenvalues become α/4±i1−α2/16​, so it becomes a repelling focus. The saddles retain one eigenvalue of each sign, α/4±2+α2/16​. Since K˙≥0, and its time integral is strictly positive on every nonconstant trajectory, trajectories cross K-contours towards larger values. Near the centre this means outward spiralling. No nonconstant periodic orbit can remain, and the former saddle-to-saddle connections at equal K=1/4 are broken. Stable saddle trajectories approach from K<1/4; unstable ones depart into K>1/4. Generic central trajectories eventually escape, while the exceptional stable saddle manifolds still approach their saddles.
