Put v=x−a and decompose it into axial and perpendicular parts, v=(v⋅n)n+v⊥​. For the nondegenerate semi-angle 0<α<π/2, the equation is equivalent to
∣v⊥​∣2cos2α=(v⋅n)2sin2α,∣v⊥​∣=∣v⋅n∣tanα.
(10)
Thus each plane perpendicular to n at axial distance s from a meets the surface in a circle of radius ∣s∣tanα. Both signs of s are allowed, and s=0 gives the vertex. This proves that it is a double circular cone with the specified vertex and axis; α is the angle each generator makes with either direction of the axis.
For the intersection calculation, define the symmetric matrix Q=cos2αI−nnT. The two equations are (x−ai​)TQ(x−ai​)=0. Their difference gives
−2x⋅Q(a1​−a2​)+a1​⋅Qa1​−a2​⋅Qa2​=0.
(11)
With b=a1​−a2​ and m=(a1​+a2​)/2, symmetry of Q makes the last two terms 2m⋅Qb. Hence the intersection plane of parallel congruent double cones is
(Qb)⋅(x−m)=0​.
(12)
The unnormalized normal vector is v=Qb=bcos2α−n(n⋅b). Expanding its squared norm gives
∣v∣2=∣b∣2cos4α+(n⋅b)2(1−2cos2α).
(13)
Equivalently, if b=b⊥​+(n⋅b)n, this is cos4α∣b⊥​∣2+sin4α(n⋅b)2>0. Thus normalization is legitimate and yields
N=∣b∣2cos4α+(n⋅b)2(1−2cos2α)​bcos2α−n(n⋅b)​​.
(14)
The assumption of a genuine cone matters: at the degenerate limits α=0 or π/2, the surface collapses respectively to an axis or a plane and this normal need not exist.
