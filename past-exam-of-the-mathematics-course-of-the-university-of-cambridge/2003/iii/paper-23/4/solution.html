A central simple algebra over K is a finite-dimensional associative K-algebra with no nonzero proper two-sided ideals and with center K. The Artin–Wedderburn theorem writes it uniquely as Mr​(D) with D a central division algebra. After a finite separable splitting field extension it becomes Md​, so its dimension is d2, and d is its degree of a central simple algebra. Passing from D to a matrix algebra over D does not change its Brauer class.
The Brauer group consists of these classes, with product induced by tensor product. Extending to a common splitting field shows that a tensor product of central simple algebras is again central simple; its two-sided ideals and center descend from the corresponding full matrix algebra. The neutral class is [K], and the inverse of [A] is [Aop], because
A⊗K​Aop≅EndK​(A),a⊗b↦(x↦axb).
(34)
The displayed map becomes the standard isomorphism for matrices over a splitting field, so it is already an isomorphism over K. Thus Brauer classes measure precisely the obstruction to being a split matrix algebra.
An explicit family is supplied by cyclic algebras. Let L/K be cyclic of degree n, with generator σ, and let a∈K×. Define
A=(L/K,σ,a)=⨁i=0n−1​Lui,ux=σ(x)u,un=a.
(35)
This is an associative algebra: form the skew polynomial ring with relation ux=σ(x)u and quotient by the central relation un−a. Reduction of powers gives the displayed n2-dimensional K-basis. An element commuting with every x∈L has no ui coefficient for i=0, since σi is not the identity. Commuting also with u forces the remaining coefficient into K. Hence the center is K.
Here is an explicit splitting construction. On Ln, with basis e0​,…,en−1​, put
D(x)ej​=σj(x)ej​,Uej​=ej−1​ (j≥1),Ue0​=aen−1​.
(36)
Then UD(x)=D(σx)U and Un=aI. After extending scalars to L, the separable decomposition L⊗K​L≅∏j=0n−1​L supplies all diagonal matrices, and their products with powers of U supply all matrix units. Therefore
A⊗K​L≅Mn​(L).
(37)
Any nonzero proper two-sided ideal of A would extend to one of Mn​(L), which is impossible. Thus this construction really gives a central simple algebra, with splitting field L.
Its central arithmetic feature is the splitting criterion for a cyclic algebra:
(L/K,σ,a) splits⟺a∈NL/K​L×.​
(38)
If a=Nc, let L act by multiplication on the K-vector space L, and let u act as cσ. Its nth power is multiplication by Nc=a, so this gives a representation A→EndK​(L). It is injective by simplicity and an isomorphism by equal dimension. Conversely, if A≅EndK​(V) with dimK​V=n, its embedded field L makes V one-dimensional over L. The relation for u says that u is σ-semilinear. In an L-basis it must have the form cσ, whose nth power is Nc. The relation un=a forces a=Nc.
More generally the crossed-product algebra of a Galois extension attaches an algebra to a multiplicative two-cocycle. Changing the cocycle by a coboundary rescales its basis and preserves the Brauer class. Descent of a split matrix algebra identifies
ker(Br(K)→Br(L))≅H2(Gal(L/K),L×).
(39)
For cyclic L/K, cyclic Tate cohomology identifies this second cohomology group with K×/NL×. In a crossed product, rescale successive cyclic basis elements to powers of a single u; the remaining datum is un=a, changed by a field norm when u is rescaled. This explains why cyclic algebras give every class split by a cyclic extension, and why multiplication of their parameters is addition of their Brauer classes.
For a finite field K=Fq​, every finite splitting field L=Fqn​ is cyclic over K. The field norm is the power map x↦x(qn−1)/(q−1) on its cyclic multiplicative group, and is onto Fq×​. Thus every relative Brauer class is zero, and every central simple algebra has zero class:
Br(Fq​)=0.​
(40)
For a non-Archimedean local field K, the valuation classification of central division algebras shows that every Brauer class splits over a finite unramified extension. For the degree-n unramified extension L/K with arithmetic Frobenius σ, units are norms and norm valuations are multiples of n. Hence the cyclic relative Brauer group is Z/nZ, represented by a=πr. The compatible local Brauer invariant is
invK​[(L/K,σ,a)]=nvK​(a)​(modZ).
(41)
The union over all n gives
Br(K)≅Q/Z.​
(42)
A class with invariant r/n in lowest terms has a central division algebra representative of degree of a central simple algebra n. This is the local valuation classification used above; the explicit cyclic algebras exhibit all of its invariants. At the Archimedean places, Br(C)=0, while Br(R)=Z/2Z: its nontrivial class is the quaternion algebra (C/R,conjugation,−1), since complex norms are positive.
For a number field, the Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether theorem describes the Brauer group by the exact sequence
0⟶Br(K)⟶⨁v​Br(Kv​) ∑v​invv​ ​Q/Z⟶0.
(43)
Thus its classes are exactly finite-support families of local invariants with sum zero; real invariants are 0 or 1/2 and complex invariants are zero. Every global Brauer class has a cyclic splitting extension, so cyclic algebras also realize all global classes. If χ(σ)=1/n, their local invariants are χv​(recKv​​(a)), with χv​ restricted to the local decomposition subgroup. The sum-zero relation for a principal parameter is the invariant form of Artin reciprocity. Injectivity supplies the local-to-global splitting test, which yields the Hasse norm theorem for cyclic extensions as explained in the preceding solution. Here the local valuation classification and the global invariant theorem are the structural theorems used to describe the Brauer groups; the construction and norm-splitting calculation have been proved explicitly.
