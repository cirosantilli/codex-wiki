For a local field F containing the Nth roots of unity, fix the Local Artin map with arithmetic Frobenius convention. The Hilbert norm residue symbol can be defined by
(a,b)F,N​=a1/NrecF​(b)(a1/N)​∈μN​.
(19)
It is independent of the chosen root, continuous and bimultiplicative, factors through F×/(F×)N in each variable, and gives a nondegenerate pairing on that finite group. It satisfies (a,b)F,N​=(b,a)F,N−1​ and (a,1−a)F,N​=1 for a=1. Its norm interpretation is
(a,b)F,N​=1⟺b∈NF(a1/N)/F​F(a1/N)×.
(20)
For N=2 the quadratic Hilbert symbol is symmetric and sign-valued. It is also characterized by the existence of a nonzero solution of z2=ax2+by2. Moreover (a,−a)F​=1, and therefore (a,a)F​=(a,−1)F​. We now compute both the square-class groups and the pairing explicitly.
For odd p, write a=pαu with u∈Zp×​. An element is a square exactly when α is even and the residue of u is a square in Fp×​. Necessity follows by reduction; sufficiency follows from the simple-root form of Hensel lemma applied to X2−u, since 2x is then a unit. If η is any unit with nonsquare residue, this gives
Qp×​/(Qp×​)2≅C2​×C2​,representatives 1,η,p,ηp(p odd).​
(21)
The nonsquare unit defines an unramified quadratic extension. Its field norms include every unit and have even valuation, as follows from the local norm calculation below. Thus (η,u)p​=1 for every unit, but (η,p)p​=−1. Finally (p,p)p​=(p,−1)p​=(−1/p), by symmetry and the preceding unit calculation. Bimultiplicativity now proves explicit quadratic Hilbert symbols over the p-adic numbers:
(pαu,pβv)p​=(−1)αβ(p−1)/2(pu​)β(pv​)α(p odd).​
(22)
Here the factors are Legendre symbols of the nonzero residues. The formula works for arbitrary integer valuations α,β, since only their parity matters.
At p=2, the square of every odd integer is one modulo eight. Conversely, for an odd u≡1(mod8), apply the strong form of Hensel lemma to X2−u at 1: the error has valuation at least three, while the derivative has valuation one. Thus the unit square classes of the p-adic integers are precisely their four residues modulo eight. Combining this with parity of the valuation gives
Q2×​/(Q2×​)2≅C23​,basis [−1],[2],[5],representatives ±1,±2,±5,±10.​
(23)
For an odd unit put
ϵ(u)=2u−1​(mod2),ω(u)=8u2−1​(mod2).
(24)
Inspection of the residues 1,3,5,7 gives [u]=[−1]ϵ(u)[5]ω(u). It remains to compute six basis pairings. Explicit field norms give
(−1,2)2​=(−1,5)2​=(2,2)2​=(5,5)2​=1:
(25)
indeed 2=12+12, 5=12+22, 2=22−2⋅12, and 5=52−5⋅22.
In contrast (−1,−1)2​=−1. To justify the obstruction even for rational 2-adic coordinates, write x=2tx0​, y=2ty0​ with x0​,y0​ integral and at least one odd. Then v2​(x02​+y02​) is zero if exactly one is odd, and one if both are odd. A sum of squares equal to the unit −1 consequently forces t=0 and exactly one coordinate odd. Its residue modulo four would then be one, whereas −1 is three.
Similarly (2,5)2​=−1. If x2−2y2 is a unit, the valuations of the two terms have opposite parity, so cannot cancel. It follows that x is an odd integral element and y is integral. Modulo eight its norm is then 1 or 7, never 5. These signs, symmetry, and bimultiplicativity give
(2αu,2βv)2​=(−1)ϵ(u)ϵ(v)+αω(v)+βω(u).​
(26)
The two boxed formulas determine the quadratic Hilbert symbol on all nonzero elements, including every rational prime p.
