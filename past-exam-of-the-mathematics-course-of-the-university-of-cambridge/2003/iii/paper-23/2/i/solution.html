Write O for the valuation ring of K, π for a uniformizer, v(π)=1, and k=Fq​ for the residue field. A useful strong form of Hensel lemma is the following: if f∈O[X] and a0​∈O satisfy
v(f(a0​))>2v(f′(a0​)),

Here is a proof by successive correction. Put s=v(f′(a0​)), dj​=v(f(aj​)), and
aj+1​=aj​−f′(aj​)f(aj​)​.

dj+1​≥2(dj​−s),dj+1​−2s≥2(dj​−2s).

Thus dj​−2s grows at least geometrically, the corrections tend to zero, and completeness gives a limit a with f(a)=0. The first correction has strictly smaller valuation than every later nonzero correction, proving the asserted distance. If x,y are in the ball v(x−a0​),v(y−a0​)>s, then
f(x)−f(y)=(x−y)(f′(a0​)+c),v(c)>s.

The second factor cannot vanish. Two roots in the ball must therefore coincide. This proves Hensel lemma; in particular a simple root modulo π has a unique lift with that residue.
We use this to establish existence and uniqueness of unramified local extensions. For each integer d≥1, choose a monic irreducible polynomial fˉ​∈k[X] of degree d and lift it to a monic f∈O[X]. Reduction and Gauss lemma show that f remains irreducible over K. Let
A=O[X]/(f),L=K[X]/(f).
(16)
The ring A is finite free and complete over O, and A/πA≅Fqd​. An element outside πA is invertible: lift an inverse from the residue field and invert the remaining 1+πz by a convergent geometric series. Every nonzero element of A is πt times such a unit, since ⋂t​πtA=0 in a finite free module. Thus A is a discrete valuation ring, its fraction field is L, and
[L:K]=d=[kL​:k],e(L/K)=1.
(17)
This constructs an unramified extension of each degree.
Conversely, let M/K be an unramified extension of degree d. Its residue field is the unique degree-d finite field extension Fqd​/Fq​. Choose a residue root of fˉ​ in it. The root is simple, so Hensel lemma lifts it to a root of f in M. This embeds L into M, and equality of degrees makes it an isomorphism. All d residue conjugates lift in L itself; hence L/K is Galois, and reduction identifies its Galois group with the cyclic Galois group of the residue field extension. Its distinguished Frobenius automorphism reduces to x↦xq.
There is exactly one unramified extension Kd​ of each degree inside a fixed algebraic closure, and
Gal(Kd​/K)≅Cd​,Kd​=K(μqd−1​),Ka​⊆Kb​⟺a∣b.​

A primitive such residue generates the finite field extension, so its lift generates Kd​. Taking the union gives the maximal unramified extension, with Galois group Z.
