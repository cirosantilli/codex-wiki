A modulus of a number field is m=m0​m∞​, with m0​ a nonzero integral ideal and m∞​ a subset of the real places. Let Im​ be the group of fractional ideals prime to m0​, and let Pm​ consist of principal ideals (a) with a≡1 at each prime power in m0​ and a>0 at each selected real place. The ray class group is
Clm​(K)=Im​/Pm​.
(46)
The ray class field Km​ is the finite abelian extension for which the Artin map has kernel Pm​ and induces an isomorphism Clm​(K)≅Gal(Km​/K). Equivalently it is the maximal abelian extension whose conductor divides m.
To compute its order use the ray class exact sequence, including signatures:
OK×​⟶(OK​/m0​)××{±1}∣m∞​∣⟶Clm​(K)⟶Cl(K)⟶1.
(47)
The middle map lifts a residue and sign tuple to an element of K by weak approximation theorem, then takes its principal ideal as a ray class. Different lifts differ by a principal ray factor. Its kernel consists exactly of tuples represented by global units. Every ordinary ideal class has a representative prime to the finite modulus, again by weak approximation theorem; this gives surjectivity of the last map. If Um​ denotes the ray units, the kernel of the unit map is Um​. The Chinese remainder theorem and counting units in each prime-power quotient give the ray class number formula
∣Clm​(K)∣=[OK×​:Um​]hK​2∣m∞​∣ΦK​(m0​)​,ΦK​(m0​)=Nm0​p∣m0​∏​(1−Np1​).​
(48)
First take K=Q(−3​). Its ring of integers is Z[ω], where ω=(−1+−3​)/2 and ω2+ω+1=0. The Minkowski bound for ideal classes is (2/π)3​<2, so every ideal class has a representative of norm one and hK​=1. Its units are the six roots of unity. Modulo seven, the polynomial X2+X+1 has distinct roots 2,4, and therefore
OK​/(7)≅F7​×F7​,ω↦(2,4).
(49)
The unit group of this quotient has order 36. The unit 1+ω maps to (3,5), which has order six; consequently unit reduction is injective and U(7)​={1}. The ray class number formula gives 36/6=6.
We also identify the quotient explicitly. Conjugation interchanges the two factors, so the residue norm is (x,y)↦xy. Its kernel has order six and equals the image of the six units, since 3⋅5=1(mod7). Hence
Cl(7)​(K)≅F7×​≅C6​.
(50)
The field M=K(ζ7​)=Q(ζ21​) has degree six over K, since the degrees over Q are twelve and two. It is unramified outside seven by the cyclotomic computation and the base-change argument above. At primes over seven, K has completion Q7​, and M/K is the tame degree-six cyclotomic extension; its conductor exponent is one. Its relative arithmetic Artin symbol at an ideal a prime to seven acts by
ζ7​⟼ζ7Namod7​.
(51)
If a=(a) with a≡1(mod7OK​), then NK/Q​(a)≡1(mod7). Thus the Artin map kills the principal ray subgroup. It factors through the order-six ray class group; the residue norm quotient computed above shows that it is onto the degree-six Galois group, so the fields coincide. This proves the explicit ray class field of Q of square root minus three modulo seven:
K(7)​=K(ζ7​)=Q(ζ21​),[K(7)​:K]=6.​
(52)
Now take K=Q(−6​), and write s=−6​. Its ring of integers is Z[s], its discriminant is −24, and its units are ±1. The quotient by (s) is
Z[s]/(s)≅Z/6Z,
(53)
whose two units are both images of ±1. The ray class exact sequence therefore gives Cl(s)​(K)≅Cl(K).
For its class number, the Minkowski bound for ideal classes is (2/π)24​<4, so each ideal class contains an integral ideal of norm at most three. The primes of norms two and three are
p2​=(2,s),p3​=(3,s),p22​=(2),p32​=(3),p2​p3​=(s).
(54)
These are the only nontrivial possibilities within the bound, and their ideal classes coincide and have order at most two. The ideal p2​ is not principal: its being principal would give an integer solution to x2+6y2=2, which is impossible. Thus hK​=2, the ideal class group is C2​, and the ray field is the Hilbert class field.
We verify its actual identity rather than stopping at the degree. Put
H=K(2​)=Q(2​,−3​),b=21+−3​​.
(55)
The two quadratic fields Q(2​) and Q(−3​) are distinct, so [H:Q]=4 and [H:K]=2. The integral elements 1,2​,b,2​b are a rational basis. Their trace matrix is the Kronecker product of the two quadratic trace matrices, with discriminants 8 and −3. Its determinant is therefore 82(−3)2=576. The discriminant-index formula for an integral lattice shows that the field discriminant divides 576. On the other hand the relative discriminant ideal formula gives
∣DH​∣=∣DK​∣[H:K]NK/Q​(dH/K​)=576NK/Q​(dH/K​).
(56)
The norm on the right is a positive integer, so equality is forced. Thus the relative discriminant ideal is the unit ideal, and H/K is unramified at every finite prime. There are no real places of K. The extension is quadratic and therefore Abelian; its degree equals hK​, identifying it with the Hilbert class field of Q of square root minus six. Hence the ray class field of Q of square root minus six modulo square root minus six is
K(−6​)​=K(2​)=Q(2​,−3​),[K(−6​)​:K]=2.​
(57)
