We give the full local-field norm-index argument. Let G=⟨σ⟩ be cyclic of order n. On an additive G-module set D=σ−1 and N=1+σ+⋯+σn−1. The two cyclic Tate cohomology groups are
H0(G,M)=MG/NM,H−1(G,M)=kerN/DM.
(27)
When these are finite, the Herbrand quotient is hG​(M)=∣H0(G,M)∣/∣H−1(G,M)∣. The periodic complex alternating D and N yields a six-term periodic exact sequence from a short exact sequence of modules. Taking alternating products of orders in that exact sequence proves
hG​(M)=hG​(M′)hG​(M′′)(0→M′→M→M′′→0).
(28)
For a finite module, ∣NM∣=∣M∣/∣kerN∣ and ∣DM∣=∣M∣/∣kerD∣ show that the two Tate cohomology groups have equal order. Thus the Herbrand quotient of a finite module is one. For the trivial integral module Z, the two groups are Z/nZ and zero, giving hG​(Z)=n.
We also need cyclic cohomology of a regular lattice. In R[G], fixed coefficient vectors are constant, and every such vector is a norm of a basis vector times its coefficient. The kernel of N consists of vectors whose coefficient sum is zero. For such a vector (ai​), solve bi−1​−bi​=ai​ successively around the cycle; the final equation holds precisely because ∑i​ai​=0. Thus kerN=DR[G]. Both Tate cohomology groups vanish, so hG​(R[G])=1, for R=Z or Zp​, and likewise for finite direct sums.
Now take a cyclic extension L/K of p-adic fields of degree n. Write ULr​=1+pLr​. For r>vL​(p)/(p−1), the convergent p-adic logarithm and p-adic exponential give mutually inverse G-equivariant group isomorphisms
ULr​≅pLr​,
(29)
where the right side is additive. The convergence assertion follows from the estimates vL​(xj/j)≥jvL​(x)−vL​(p)vp​(j) and vL​(xj/j!)≥jvL​(x)−vL​(p)j/(p−1); formal inverse identities therefore hold on this ball.
By the normal basis theorem, choose θ so that {σiθ} is a K-basis of L. The lattice A=∑i​OK​σiθ is a regular OK​[G]-module. It is a finite direct sum of regular Zp​[G]-modules, hence its Tate cohomology vanishes. The lattices A and pLr​ are commensurable: their intersection has finite index in both. The exact sequences with these finite quotients establish finiteness of the Tate cohomology groups and show hG​(pLr​)=1. Thus hG​(ULr​)=1. Since OL×​/ULr​ is finite, hG​(OL×​)=1 as well.
The G-equivariant valuation exact sequence is
1⟶OL×​⟶L×vL​​Z⟶0.
(30)
Its last term has trivial G-action. The multiplication rule gives the Herbrand quotient of the local multiplicative group:
hG​(L×)=n.
(31)
All the Tate cohomology groups here are finite by the lattice argument and the exact sequence; finiteness of the norm index has not been assumed.
For completeness we prove the needed Hilbert theorem 90. If a∈L× has field norm one, put A0​=1 and Ai​=∏j=0i−1​σj(a). The linear map c↦b=∑i=0n−1​Ai​σi(c) is not zero by linear independence of distinct field embeddings. Choose c with b=0. The relation An​=1 gives aσ(b)=b, so a=b/σ(b). This proves H−1(G,L×)=0. The zeroth Tate cohomology group is K×/NL/K​L×. Consequently the cyclic local norm index is
[K×:NL/K​L×]=[L:K]=n.​
(32)
Writing n=ef, the valuation of a field norm is vK​(Nx)=fvL​(x). The valuation quotient has order f, and a norm with valuation zero comes from a unit. Therefore
[OK×​:NL/K​OL×​]=e.​
(33)
In particular every unit is a norm in an unramified extension. These results give the norm assertions used in the quadratic Hilbert symbol computation.
We finish with the Hasse norm theorem: for a cyclic extension of number fields, an element is a global norm if and only if it is a norm at every place. More precisely the local norm is the norm from the product L⊗K​Kv​; in a Galois extension its factors have the same norm subgroup. The forward implication is immediate. For the converse associate to a∈K× the cyclic algebra (L/K,σ,a). The splitting criterion for a cyclic algebra, proved below, identifies its splitting with a∈NL/K​L×. At a place where the tensor product of fields has several factors, this algebra is a matrix algebra over the cyclic algebra of one decomposition-field factor, with the generator restricted to its cyclic decomposition subgroup. Thus the local norm assumption makes its Brauer class zero at every place. Injectivity in the Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether theorem makes the global Brauer class zero, giving the desired global norm. This is the required proof outline; cyclicity is essential to this reduction.
