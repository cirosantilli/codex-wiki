The event rate counts unordered pairs of reacting nuclei. For unlike species there are ni​nj​ pairs per unit-volume normalization; for identical species there are approximately ni2​/2 pairs, since counting ordered pairs counts each collision twice. The Kronecker delta therefore supplies exactly the required divisor 1+δij​. It is the event rate, not the destruction rate of identical nuclei: one identical-pair reaction destroys two nuclei.
The temperature dependence combines the high-energy tail of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution with quantum tunnelling through the Coulomb barrier. The principal exponential factor in the energy integral is exp[−E/(kB​T)−b/E​]. Its stationary point, the Gamow peak, has E0​∝T2/3 and exponent η=3E0​/(kB​T)∝T−1/3. Integrating around this peak gives a prefactor proportional to T−2/3, or η2, when the nuclear factor varies slowly. Thus the supplied thermonuclear reaction rate is locally proportional to η2e−η. The slow weak interaction in the proton-proton reaction also makes its overall coefficient very small; its local thermal slope follows the supplied formula.
At fixed number densities, define the local temperature exponent of a thermonuclear reaction by a logarithmic derivative. Since dη/dlogT=−η/3,
dlogTdlogλij​​=(2/η−1)(−η/3)=3η−2​.

α≃4,β≃16,γ≃16,γ>β​.

These are local powers near the specified temperature, not constant exponents over an arbitrarily large range. The greater reduced nuclear mass in the 34 reaction increases its tunnelling exponent and its thermal sensitivity.
For the effective proton-proton reaction network, denote the event rates by
R11​=21​λ11​n12​,R21​=λ21​n2​n1​,R33​=21​λ33​n32​,R34​=λ34​n3​n4​,R17​=λ17​n1​n7​.

The short-lived beryllium intermediate can be eliminated, so production through R34​ feeds n7​, representing predominantly lithium-7. This rapid conversion is electron capture. Count the nuclei consumed and produced in each reaction: 11 consumes two protons and makes one deuterium; 21 consumes a proton and a deuterium and makes one helium-3; 33 consumes two helium-3 and makes one helium-4 and two protons; 34 consumes one of each helium isotope; 17 consumes a proton and lithium-7 and makes two helium-4. For closure, n˙7​=λ34​n3​n4​−λ17​n1​n7​. These nuclear terms conserve n1​+2n2​+3n3​+4n4​+7n7​, providing an independent stoichiometric check. They describe a fixed local volume; compression or mixing would add transport terms.
The roughly one-second destruction time of deuterium is negligible compared with the proton-burning and helium-3 evolution times. Its abundance rapidly adjusts to the slowly evolving production rate. Set n˙2​≃0, giving the deuterium quasi-equilibrium in proton-proton burning
λ21​n2​n1​≃21​λ11​n12​,n2​≃2λ21​λ11​n1​​.
(21)
Substitution, without setting n˙1​=0, gives
n˙1​n˙3​​≃−23​λ11​n12​+λ33​n32​−λ17​n1​n7​,≃21​λ11​n12​−λ33​n32​−λ34​n3​n4​.​​
(22)
The helium-3 relaxation time of about 6×105 years is likewise short compared with the central proton-burning time and the age of the Sun. On this relaxation time the proton and helium-4 abundances, and the background temperature, can be treated as fixed. Put A=λ33​, B=λ34​n4​, C=λ11​n12​/2. Then n˙3​=C−An32​−Bn3​, and its nonnegative equilibrium is
n3e​=−2λ33​λ34​n4​​+(2λ33​λ34​n4​​)2+2λ33​λ11​n12​​​​.

x˙=−(2λ33​n3e​+λ34​n4​)x−λ33​x2.

x˙=−x/τ,τ=(2λ33​n3e​+λ34​n4​)−1​.

which yields about 6.8×106K with the same normalization and fixed-density approximation. Thus the robust estimate is several million kelvin. The supplied data do not fix the local density, composition or pp-II fraction away from the centre, so they cannot determine an exact transition temperature or radius. In particular, using τ∝T−16 alone would omit the temperature dependence of the equilibrium helium-3 abundance.
The solar hydrogen and helium-3 abundance profiles follow from burning, finite equilibration time and convection. Central hydrogen has been consumed, so X1​ is lowest in the centre and increases toward its much less processed outer value. In the hot centre, rapid destruction keeps helium-3 small. Moving outward initially raises its equilibrium abundance because λ11​/λ33​ rises strongly as temperature falls. Further out, equilibration is no longer reached within the solar age, and still farther out even production is slow. Hence X3​ has an off-centre maximum and then declines toward its small outer abundance. The outer convective envelope mixes each abundance toward a constant value.
