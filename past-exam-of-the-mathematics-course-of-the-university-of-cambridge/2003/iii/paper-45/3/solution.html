Work first in a finite-dimensional SU(2) representation, with its invariant positive inner product. In the complexified Lie algebra, choose H†=H and (E−)†=E+. Let v0​ be a highest-weight vector with Hv0​=mv0​ and E+v0​=0, and put vr​=(E−)rv0​. The commutator with H gives
Hvr​=(m−2r)vr​.
(16)
Induction also gives
E+vr​=r(m−r+1)vr−1​.
(17)
For r=1 this is E+E−v0​=Hv0​=mv0​. For the induction step, use E+E−=E−E++H on vr​: the coefficient becomes r(m−r+1)+m−2r=(r+1)(m−r). In particular
E+E−vr​=(r+1)(m−r)vr​=41​(m+n)(m−n+2)vr​,n=m−2r,
(18)
which proves the assumed formula rather than merely invoking it.
The norm identity ∥E−vr​∥2=(r+1)(m−r)∥vr​∥2 shows that, for nonnegative integer m, the ladder is nonzero for 0≤r≤m and stops with E−vm​=0. Distinct eigenvalues make these vectors independent. Their span is stable under H,E±, and in the irreducible module it is the full space. Thus
n=m,m−2,…,−m,dimV(m)=m+1,j=m/2.​
(19)
For normalized states, E−∣n⟩=21​(m+n)(m−n+2)​∣n−2⟩, with the last state killed. General finite-dimensional SU(2) representations are direct sums of these ladders.
The representation category matters here. The Lie-algebra relations alone allow an infinite Verma module even at integral highest weight: its nonzero vector vm+1​ is singular. The finite representation can instead be constructed algebraically by taking the basis v0​,…,vm​ with the displayed actions and declaring E−vm​=0; all three Lie brackets then hold, including at the endpoints. Thus the finite-dimensional assertion uses the integrable SU(2) module, or this explicit finite quotient, rather than assuming that a singular vector must vanish in every highest-weight module.
For rank two, take a Cartan subalgebra h with commuting basis elements. A root α is a nonzero linear functional on h for which a root space contains an element Eα​ satisfying
[h,Eα​]=α(h)Eα​(h∈h).
(20)
Choose simple roots α1​,α2​ and their coroots Hi​=αi∨​. In the PDF's index convention, the Cartan matrix is
Kij​=αi​(Hj​)=(αj​,αj​)2(αi​,αj​)​.
(21)
Normalize Ei±​=E±αi​​ so [Ei+​,Ei−​]=Hi​. The difference of distinct simple roots is not a root, so the mixed positive-negative bracket is zero. This gives
[Hi​,Hj​]=0,[Ei+​,Ej−​]=δij​Hj​,[Hi​,Ej±​]=±Kji​Ej±​.
(22)
The transpose in the last formula is essential and is printed correctly in the original PDF.
A highest-weight vector of labels (n1​,n2​) is killed by both Ei+​ and is an eigenvector of the two Hi​. Repeated words in E1−​,E2−​ generate its highest-weight representation; each lowers the weight by the corresponding simple root. Restricting to the sl2 subalgebra associated with a root for either simple root gives the preceding integer ladder, so finite-dimensional highest labels must be nonnegative integers. The finite irreducible module is the highest-weight quotient imposing (Ei−​)ni​+1v=0 and the submodules generated by these relations, together with the Serre relations. Choosing independent nonzero lowering words gives a simultaneous weight basis; an extra multiplicity index is needed where a weight space has dimension greater than one.
For the specified B2 root system, the simple-root squared lengths are 1,2 and their inner product is −1, giving
K=(2−2​−12​).
(23)
The positive root α1​+α2​ is generated by [E1+​,E2+​]=0, and 2α1​+α2​ by [E1+​,[E1+​,E2+​]]=0. No further positive sums occur, so (adE1+​)3E2+​=0 and (adE2+​)2E1+​=0. These are the relevant Serre relations. Applying the Cartan commutators to a weight vector proves
E1−​: (n1​,n2​)↦(n1​−2,n2​+1),E2−​: (n1​,n2​)↦(n1​+2,n2​−2),​
(24)
whenever the image is nonzero.
To enumerate the requested representation without guessing when arrows stop, realize it concretely as the Adjoint representation. For a Euclidean weight (x,y), its labels are (n1​,n2​)=(2x,−x+y). The highest root 2α1​+α2​=(1,1) has labels (2,0), so it generates the irreducible adjoint module of this simple rank-two Lie algebra. Its nonzero weights are exactly its eight roots, and its zero weight space is the two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra. This gives the B2 adjoint representation weight diagram and the nine distinct states labels:
(2,0), (0,1), (−2,2), (2,−1), (0,0), (−2,1), (2,−2), (0,−1), (−2,0).​
(25)
Their actual production by lowering can be seen from
(2,0)(0,1)(−2,2)(0,0)(−2,1)(0,−1)​1​(0,1),1​(−2,2),(0,1)2​(2,−1),2​(0,0),(2,−1)1​(0,0),1​(−2,1),(0,0)2​(2,−2),2​(0,−1),(2,−2)1​(0,−1),1​(−2,0).​
(26)
An arrow labelled i denotes a nonzero action of Ei−​ between the indicated weight spaces. The two paths into zero produce independent Cartan vectors. Hence there are nine distinct pairs, as requested when degeneracy is disregarded, but the full representation has dimension ten, not nine.
