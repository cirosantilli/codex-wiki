The standard CW complex structure on Complex projective space CP3 has one cell in each dimension 0,2,4,6 and no other cells. Its cellular chain complex therefore has zero differentials: each boundary has an odd-dimensional target group, which is zero. The product of the usual two-cell structures on S2 and S4 likewise has exactly one cell in dimensions 0,2,4,6. Thus cellular homology gives, for both spaces,
Hi​(−;Z)≅{Z,0,​i=0,2,4,6,otherwise.​​
(1)
The cohomology rings detect more than these additive groups. Let c∈H2(CP3;Z) be the hyperplane class. The cohomology ring of complex projective space is
H∗(CP3;Z)=Z[c]/(c4),∣c∣=2.
(2)
For the normalization, a hyperplane is Poincare dual to c; intersecting three generic hyperplanes gives one point with positive complex orientation, so ⟨c3,[CP3]⟩=1. Together with the computed additive groups this makes c,c2,c3 generators in their respective degrees.
Let u and v be the pullbacks of the orientation classes of S2 and S4. The Künneth theorem and naturality of the cup product give
H∗(S2×S4;Z)=Z[u,v]/(u2,v2),∣u∣=2,∣v∣=4,
(3)
where uv generates degree six. In particular, every degree-two class on the product has square zero, whereas c2=0. Any graded ring isomorphism would send c to ±u, contradicting this product relation. A homotopy equivalence induces an isomorphism of cohomology rings, so the spaces have isomorphic integral homology but are not homotopy equivalent.
