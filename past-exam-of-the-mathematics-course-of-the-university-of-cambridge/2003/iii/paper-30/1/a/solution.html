Orient each edge both ways. A unit flow j from x to y is antisymmetric, j(u,v)=−j(v,u), with divergence of a flow equal to +1 at x, −1 at y and zero elsewhere. Its energy is
Er​(j)=∑e∈E​re​j(e)2,
(1)
where each unoriented edge is counted once. Put ce​=1/re​. The electrical unit current is i(u,v)=cuv​(V(u)−V(v)), where the potential V solves the Kirchhoff node law with those source and sink divergences. Because G is a connected graph, this potential is unique up to an additive constant. The effective resistance is the electric potential difference needed for unit current,
R(x,y)=V(x)−V(y).
(2)
Discrete summation by parts gives Er​(i)=∑u​V(u)divi(u)=R(x,y).
The Thomson principle says that this current minimizes energy among all unit flows. Indeed write any other unit flow as j=i+k, where divk=0. Then
∑e​re​i(e)k(e)=∑e=(u,v)​(V(u)−V(v))k(u,v)=∑u​V(u)divk(u)=0.
(3)
Consequently
Er​(j)=R(x,y)+Er​(k)≥R(x,y),R(x,y)=j unit flow x→ymin​Er​(j).​
(4)
This also proves uniqueness of the minimizing current.
If re′​≥re​ for every edge, then Er′​(j)≥Er​(j) for every admissible flow. The admissible set is unchanged, so taking its minimum proves the Rayleigh monotonicity principle:
Rr′​(x,y)≥Rr​(x,y).​
(5)
Deletion is the limiting operation re​→∞: finite-energy flows must then carry zero current on that edge. If the terminals become disconnected, the effective resistance is infinite.
