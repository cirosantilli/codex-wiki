First prove strict creation for the canonical forgetful functor U:CT→C. Let a diagram of algebras (Ai​,ai​) have a chosen underlying limit cone pi​:L→Ai​. The maps ai​T(pi​):TL→Ai​ form a cone: if h:Ai​→Aj​ is a diagram arrow, then hai​=aj​T(h) and hpi​=pj​. Thus the limit property gives a unique action a:TL→L with
pi​a=ai​T(pi​).​
(22)
The projections are jointly monic. By naturality of η and the algebra unit laws, pi​aηL​=ai​ηAi​​pi​=pi​, hence aηL​=1L​. Likewise
pi​aμL​=ai​μAi​​T2pi​=ai​T(ai​)T2pi​=pi​aT(a),
(23)
so aμL​=aT(a). Therefore (L,a) is an algebra and all pi​ are algebra morphisms.
For any algebra cone hi​:(B,b)→(Ai​,ai​), let h:B→L be its unique underlying factor. Then pi​hb=hi​b=ai​T(hi​)=pi​aT(h), so joint monicity gives hb=aT(h). Thus the factor is an algebra morphism, unique as such. The action on the prescribed L was itself forced by the cone equations. This proves monad algebra forgetful functor creates limits, without assuming T preserves them.
For a monadic G with comparison an equivalence, transport (L,a) and its cone across that equivalence. This yields a limiting cone in D whose image is identified with the prescribed base cone by an isomorphism, uniquely up to the corresponding cone-compatible isomorphism. Thus a monadic functor creates limits in the equivalence-invariant sense. If monadicity uses a comparison isomorphism over the base, the lift is literally on the prescribed underlying cone, proving strict creation as well.
A qualification is necessary if equivalence is used to define monadicity but literal unique lifting is demanded. Let C be the indiscrete category on two objects 0,1, let D be the terminal category, and let G select 0. The unique F:C→D is left adjoint to G, and the comparison to the induced algebra category is an equivalence: the two algebra objects have exactly one map between each pair. Yet the chosen terminal cone with vertex 1 in C has no literal lift, since the only object in the image of G is 0. The creation of limits up to isomorphism conclusion holds, because 0 and 1 are uniquely isomorphic. This distinguishes the two conventions rather than claiming an invalid strict conclusion from equivalence alone.
