Let M be an oriented smooth m-dimensional manifold with boundary and α a smooth compactly supported (m−1)-differential form. Give ∂M the outward-normal-first orientation. A partition of unity subordinate to oriented interior and boundary charts reduces the proof to a compactly supported form on Rm or on a half-space. Indeed α=∑j​χj​α, so dα=∑j​d(χj​α); the sum of terms involving dχj​ is zero. Exterior derivative commutes with chart pullbacks, so each summand can be calculated in coordinates.
Write the local form as
α=∑j=1m​(−1)j−1fj​dx1∧⋯∧dxj∧⋯∧dxm,dα=(∑j​∂j​fj​)dx1∧⋯∧dxm.
(1)
For an interior chart, integrate each derivative using the fundamental theorem of calculus: its integral is zero because fj​ has compact support. For a boundary chart modeled on xm≥0, all tangential derivatives again integrate to zero, while ∫0∞​∂m​fm​dxm=−fm​(x′,0). The outward normal is −∂m​; its contraction with the volume form gives exactly this sign in the induced boundary integral of α. Summing the chart identities proves the Generalized Stokes theorem:
∫M​dα=∫∂M​α.​
(2)
Compact support can be replaced by compactness of M; on a noncompact manifold, sufficient support or convergence conditions are necessary.

Keep the metric and hence the Hodge star fixed. Write α=δA, so δF=dα, and use the Bianchi identity for an Abelian p-form dF=d2A=0. The bilinear pairing U∧⋆V on real four-forms is symmetric even for a fixed indefinite metric. Consequently the variation of the kinetic term is dα∧⋆F.
The two differentiated four-form factors in the cubic term have the same sign, since moving a four-form through another four-form or through a three-form contributes an even exponent. Thus
δ(F∧F∧A)=2dα∧F∧A+α∧F∧F.
(3)
The graded Leibniz rule gives
d(α∧⋆F)=dα∧⋆F−α∧d⋆F,d(α∧F∧A)=dα∧F∧A−α∧F∧F.
(4)
Using the Generalized Stokes theorem, the complete first variation is therefore
δS=∫M​α∧(d⋆F+3F∧F)+∫∂M​(α∧⋆F+2α∧F∧A).
(5)
For compactly supported variations, a closed M, or boundary conditions removing this last term, the Euler-Lagrange equation and the Bianchi identity for an Abelian p-form are
d⋆F+3F∧F=0,dF=0.​
(6)
The coefficient three follows from the normalization actually present in the action; inserting a differently normalized supergravity coupling would change it. This is the eleven-dimensional three-form action variation.
Under the gauge transformation A↦A+dΛ, the four-form F is unchanged because d2Λ=0. The kinetic density is unchanged, and the cubic density changes by
F∧F∧dΛ=d(F∧F∧Λ).
(7)
It is an exact eleven-form, so the action changes only by a boundary integral. The Euler-Lagrange equations depend only on F and are invariant even when this boundary integral is nonzero. Invariance of the action itself additionally requires suitable boundary or support conditions.

Let B(X,Y)=tr(adXadY) be the Killing form. A real semisimple Lie algebra has nondegenerate B, and its invariance gives B([X,Y],Z)=B(X,[Y,Z]). Translating B defines a bi-invariant pseudo-Riemannian metric g=B on the Lie group.
For left-invariant vector fields, all derivatives of their pairwise inner products vanish. The Koszul formula reduces to
2B(∇X​Y,Z)=B([X,Y],Z)−B([Y,Z],X)+B([Z,X],Y)=B([X,Y],Z).
(12)
Nondegeneracy gives ∇X​Y=[X,Y]/2. With R(X,Y)Z=∇X​∇Y​Z−∇Y​∇X​Z−∇[X,Y]​Z, the Jacobi identity now gives
R(X,Y)Z=−41​[[X,Y],Z].
(13)
Taking the trace of X↦R(X,Y)Z=−41​adZadY(X) yields the Ricci tensor
Ric(Y,Z)=−41​B(Y,Z)=−41​g(Y,Z).​
(14)
Thus the Killing-form Einstein metric makes the group an Einstein manifold, allowing an indefinite metric. Its scalar curvature in this convention is −dimG/4.
For a connected compact semisimple Lie group, B is negative definite: the adjoint operators are skew-adjoint for an invariant positive inner product, so B(X,X)=tr((adX)2)<0 for X=0. The conventional positive Riemannian metric is instead g+​=−B. Its Ricci tensor is g+​/4, and its sectional curvature is
Kg+​​(X,Y)=4(∥X∥g+​2​∥Y∥g+​2​−⟨X,Y⟩g+​2​)∥[X,Y]∥g+​2​​≥0.
(15)
It can vanish on commuting two-planes; positivity is not automatic in every direction. With the literal negative metric B, the corresponding sectional-curvature signs are reversed.
For a noncompact real semisimple group, a Cartan decomposition g=k⊕p has B negative on k and positive on p. The Killing metric is indefinite and cannot be made positive by an overall sign. Its Einstein constant remains −1/4 for g=B, but that does not assert a uniform sign for sectional curvature of all nondegenerate planes. For connected semisimple groups, negative-definite B characterizes the compact case. Disconnected groups require a separate condition on the number of components before compactness follows.

A Poisson manifold is a smooth manifold P with a bilinear bracket on C∞(P) that is antisymmetric, satisfies the Jacobi identity and obeys {f,gh}={f,g}h+g{f,h}. Equivalently it has a bivector Π defining {f,g}=Π(df,dg) with vanishing Schouten bracket [Π,Π]; nondegeneracy is not required.
For a symplectic manifold, define the Hamiltonian vector field by ιXf​​ω=df and put {f,g}=ω(Xf​,Xg​). Nondegeneracy gives a unique Xf​, antisymmetry is inherited from ω, and d(gh)=gdh+hdg proves the Leibniz rule. Moreover LXf​​ω=dιXf​​ω+ιXf​​dω=0 by Cartan's magic formula. Since Xf​g=−{f,g},
ι[Xf​,Xg​]​ω=LXf​​(ιXg​​ω)=d(Xf​g)=−d{f,g},[Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​.
(16)
For completeness, evaluate dω=0 on Xf​,Xg​,Xh​. Its three derivative terms sum to minus the cyclic sum of {f,{g,h}}, and its three bracket terms sum to minus the same cyclic sum, using the displayed identity. Hence that sum is zero. This proves the Jacobi identity for the Poisson bracket, not merely the Jacobi identity modulo constants. The inverse of a closed nondegenerate two-form therefore defines a Poisson manifold.
The Symplectic Darboux theorem says that near every point of a 2n-dimensional symplectic manifold there are coordinates (q1,…,qn,p1​,…,pn​) with
ω=i=1∑n​dqi∧dpi​,{f,g}=i∑​(∂qi∂f​∂pi​∂g​−∂pi​∂f​∂qi∂g​).​
(17)
Thus every symplectic structure has the same local normal form, despite possible global topological differences.

A smooth principal bundle with structure Lie group G can be specified by a locally trivial map π:E→B whose fiber coordinate changes have the form (x,g)i​↦(x,hji​(x)g)j​, with the usual transition functions of a principal bundle cocycle on triple overlaps. In each chart define (x,g)⋅k=(x,gk). On overlaps, hji​(x)(gk)=(hji​(x)g)k, so these local right actions agree. They therefore give a global smooth right group action, free and transitive on every fiber. Equivalently, this action together with equivariant local triviality is the definition of a principal bundle.
Use five-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with ambient metric η=diag(−1,1,1,1,1) and realize unit-radius de Sitter spacetime as η(x,x)=1. Its tangent space is x⊥, of Lorentzian signature (3,1). An oriented pseudo-orthonormal frame (e0​,e1​,e2​,e3​) of x⊥, together with the last column x, is precisely a matrix
g=(e0​,e1​,e2​,e3​,x)∈SO(4,1).
(20)
Conversely the last column of such a matrix lies on the hyperboloid, and its other columns give that frame. Thus
SO(4,1)⟶dS4​,g⟼ge4​,G=SO(3,1).​
(21)
Right multiplication by diag(h,1) fixes x and changes the tangent frame by h. It is free and transitive on frames over x, proving the principal bundle description. Smooth local choices of frames provide its local trivializations. The dimensions are 10=4+6.
This is the oriented frame bundle of de Sitter spacetime. Allowing orientation reversal gives O(4,1) and structure group O(3,1); imposing a time orientation as well selects SO0​(4,1) and SO0​(3,1). For radius a, take the last column x/a.

Equivalently its Lie-algebra-valued connection one-form A satisfies A(ξE​)=ξ and Rg∗​A=Adg−1​A. Every base vector has a unique horizontal lift because dπ:He​→Tπ(e)​B is an isomorphism. A base curve lifts from a chosen initial fiber point by solving A(e˙)=0; this defines parallel transport. Closed curves can give nontrivial holonomy, and failure of horizontal fields to close under brackets is measured by the curvature of a principal connection.
For a smooth strictly convex rigid body, assume contact with the plane is unique and varies smoothly with orientation. Write a configuration as (R,c∥​)∈SO(3)×R2. Contact determines the reference point's height h(R), so the full reference position is c=(c∥​,h(R)). Let r(R) be the spatial vector from that point to the contacting material point and let Ω=R˙R−1 encode the spatial angular velocity. The instantaneous contact velocity is
vcontact​=c˙+Ω×r(R).
(22)
The no-slip condition is its vanishing. Its vertical component follows already from differentiating contact: the normal component of the changing contact point along the body surface is zero, giving h˙=−(Ω×r)z​. The two remaining equations are
c˙∥​=−(Ω×r(R))∥​.
(23)
Translations of c∥​ define a free right R2 action, with quotient SO(3). The translational connection for a convex body rolling on a plane is
A=dc∥​+(Ω×r(R))∥​.​
(24)
Here Ω denotes the vector-valued right Maurer-Cartan form on SO(3). This one-form is invariant under translations and returns a translation vector on a vertical translation, so it satisfies both principal connection axioms. A prescribed orientation path has the unique rolling horizontal lift
c∥​(t)=c∥​(0)−∫0t​(Ω(s)×r(R(s)))∥​ds.
(25)
This is a kinematic connection; inertia and gravity determine which of the allowed paths is dynamically realized.

Write ξP​(p)=dtd​​0​exp(tξ)⋅p for the fundamental vector field of a left Lie group action, and use ιXf​​ω=df throughout. Such fundamental fields satisfy [ξP​,ηP​]=−[ξ,η]P​. A moment map has linear components μξ​=⟨μ,ξ⟩ satisfying
dμξ​=ιξP​​ω.
(26)
For the action to be Hamiltonian, these components can be chosen equivariantly, or, infinitesimally,
{μξ​,μη​}=μ[ξ,η]​.​
(27)
This identifies the span of the component Hamiltonians with the Lie algebra image, rather than identifying the entire infinite-dimensional Poisson algebra C∞(P) with g. For an actual isomorphism with g, injectivity is also needed; a faithful infinitesimal action supplies it.
Preservation of ω alone gives dιξP​​ω=0, not necessarily exactness. For example, translation generated by ∂q​ on the symplectic two-torus has contraction dp, whose period is nonzero. That action has no globally defined component Hamiltonian. Once the contractions are exact, choose Hamiltonians linearly in ξ. On each connected component of P, the discrepancy
κ(ξ,η)={μξ​,μη​}−μ[ξ,η]​
(28)
is constant: apply [Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​ and the fundamental-field bracket. The Jacobi identity makes κ a Lie algebra two-cocycle. Replacing μξ​ by μξ​+b(ξ) changes it to κ(ξ,η)−b([ξ,η]). Thus the exactness condition and vanishing of this moment-map equivariance obstruction are precisely the required infinitesimal conditions.
For a semisimple Lie algebra, both hold. First Cartan's magic formula gives
ι[ξP​,ηP​]​ω=d(ιξP​​ιηP​​ω)=−d[ω(ξP​,ηP​)],ι[ξ,η]P​​ω=d[ω(ξP​,ηP​)].
(29)
Semisimplicity implies [g,g]=g, so every generator is a sum of commutators and has a globally exact contraction. This proves Hamiltonian existence for a semisimple symplectic action.
Next use the nondegenerate Killing form to define D by B(Dξ,η)=κ(ξ,η). Antisymmetry makes D skew for B. The cocycle identity and invariance of B imply D[ξ,η]=[Dξ,η]+[ξ,Dη], so D is a derivation. Choose Z by
B(Z,ξ)=tr(Dadξ).
(30)
Then, using cyclicity of trace and [D,adξ]=ad(Dξ),
B([Z,ξ],η)=tr(D[adξ,adη])=tr([D,adξ]adη)=B(Dξ,η).
(31)
Hence D=adZ and κ(ξ,η)=B(Z,[ξ,η]). Taking b(ξ)=B(Z,ξ) removes the cocycle and proves semisimple moment-map equivariance without merely invoking a cohomology theorem. For a connected group, integrate the infinitesimal equivariance along its one-parameter subgroups to obtain group equivariance. A disconnected group can require an additional discrete-equivariance check.

U=(α−β​​βα​),∣α∣2+∣β∣2=1.
(42)
The four real coordinates of (α,β) give a smooth bijection with smooth inverse onto the unit three-sphere. Matrix multiplication identifies this model with the unit quaternions. Its round metric is invariant under left and right multiplication by unit quaternions. The action
(a,b):q⟼aqb−1
(43)
has kernel {(1,1),(−1,−1)}: a kernel pair must have a=b from q=1 and must commute with all quaternions. Its derivative is injective, and both dimensions are six, giving
Isom0​(S3)=SO(4)≅(SU(2)×SU(2))/{±(I,I)}.​
(44)
The full isometry group is O(4). Quaternion conjugation reverses orientation and supplies the additional component. Thus an arbitrary isometry has either the displayed left-right form or that form composed with conjugation.
For the Anti-de Sitter spacetime model, write
M=(x0​+x1​x2​−x3​​x2​+x3​x0​−x1​​),−detM=−x02​−x32​+x12​+x22​.
(45)
The hyperboloid with this quadratic form equal to −1 is exactly SL(2,R). Its induced Lorentzian metric is preserved by M↦AMB−1 for A,B∈SL(2,R). At the identity the metric is g(U,V)=21​tr(UV) on traceless matrices, so it is the Killing metric divided by eight. This proves the geometric identification, including the metric rather than only the underlying manifold.
A trivial left-right action again forces A=B to be a scalar commuting with all determinant-one matrices, hence A=B=±I. The differential is injective and both dimensions are six, so
Isom0​(AdS3​)=SO0​(2,2)≅(SL(2,R)×SL(2,R))/{±(I,I)}.​
(46)
This is the existing left-right double cover of SO0(2,2). The full hyperboloid isometry group is O(2,2). Its other components can be obtained by also using M↦MT and M↦CMC−1, C=diag(1,−1). Transposition reverses one timelike coordinate; conjugation by C reverses one timelike and one spacelike coordinate, so these generate the four component classes. All isometries of these constant-curvature quadrics come from ambient orthogonal transformations: a value and an orthonormal derivative at one point determine an isometry, and the ambient group realizes every such choice.
Here AdS3​ means the hyperboloid with periodic time. The physically common causally unwrapped spacetime is its universal cover, modeled by the universal covering Lie group of SL(2,R), and is not itself SL(2,R). Its isometries lift the above local symmetries, with the global covering and central identifications changed accordingly.

Geometric quantization constructs quantum state spaces from the geometry of a classical symplectic manifold (P,ω). Its first task is to represent classical observables and their Poisson bracket by operators; its second is to reduce the excessive phase-space dependence of the resulting states. These are different steps, called prequantization and choice of a polarization in geometric quantization.
Use ιXf​​ω=df and {f,g}=ω(Xf​,Xg​), so [Xf​,Xg​]=−X{f,g}​. A prequantum line bundle is a Hermitian complex line bundle L→P with unitary connection on a vector bundle of curvature
F∇​=ℏi​ω.
(47)
Its existence requires the integrality of all symplectic periods:
2πℏ1​∫Σ​ω∈Zfor every integral closed two-cycle Σ.​
(48)
One way to see the obstruction is to choose local primitives ω=−dθi​ on contractible charts. The local connections are d−iθi​/ℏ. On overlaps the differences of θi​ are exact and determine unitary transition phases. On triple overlaps their exponentials obey the cocycle condition exactly when the periods have the stated integral values. Conversely that integral class is realized by a line bundle; adjusting a connection by a global one-form makes its curvature equal to the prescribed representative. Connections with this curvature can still differ by a flat connection, so integrality need not specify unique quantum data.
On smooth sections define the Kostant-Souriau prequantum operator
f​=−iℏ∇Xf​​+f.
(49)
Its defining algebraic property follows directly from the curvature commutator:
[∇Xf​​,∇Xg​​]=−∇X{f,g}​​+ℏi​{f,g}.
(50)
The multiplication terms contribute −iℏ(Xf​g−Xg​f)=2iℏ{f,g}, so
[f​,g​]=iℏ{f,g}​,1=I.​
(51)
The preliminary inner product integrates the Hermitian pairing against the symplectic volume ωn/n!, where dimP=2n. Hamiltonian flows preserve this measure, so real observables give formally symmetric operators on suitable compactly supported sections. Actual self-adjoint realizations and their domains require analysis, especially on a noncompact phase space.
The prequantum representation is generally reducible and depends on all 2n variables, whereas a configuration-space wave function should depend on n. A polarization in geometric quantization is an involutive complex rank-n distribution F⊂TC​P that is isotropic for ω. A real polarization is tangent to a Lagrangian foliation; a compatible complex polarization is supplied by a Kähler manifold. Polarized sections satisfy
∇X​s=0(X∈F).
(52)
This condition is locally consistent because the connection curvature vanishes on pairs of polarization vectors, and involutivity closes the differential constraints. Globally, leaf holonomy can obstruct nonzero polarized sections. To construct the quantum Hilbert space, one must also choose the appropriate quotient measure or density data and complete the polarized sections; it need not be a literal closed subspace of the original phase-space L2 space.
For P=T∗Rn, set θ=∑j​pj​dqj, so ω=−dθ and the line bundle is trivial with ∇=d−iθ/ℏ. The vertical polarization is spanned by ∂pj​​. Its equations say ∂pj​​ψ=0, hence ψ=ψ(q). Before this restriction,
f​=−iℏXf​+f−θ(Xf​),q​j=iℏ∂pj​​+qj,p​j​=−iℏ∂qj​.
(53)
After restricting and using the configuration-space measure, the Schrödinger representation is
H=L2(Rn,dq),q​jψ=qjψ,p​j​ψ=−iℏ∂qj​ψ.​
(54)
Their commutator is iℏδjk​ on a common smooth test-function domain. Functions of q act by multiplication. Observables affine in momenta have flows preserving this polarization and can act directly, with density terms when required for symmetry. An arbitrary observable does not: already p2/(2m) has a flow taking a vertical fiber to a slanted one. Thus merely restricting its prequantum operator does not produce the free Schrödinger Hamiltonian.
The Blattner-Kostant-Sternberg pairing compares different polarizations. One can transport a state by a Hamiltonian flow to its transported polarization, pair back with the chosen state space and differentiate to obtain additional operators when the pairing is well defined. The metaplectic correction tensors polarized sections with a suitable square root of the polarization's canonical bundle. Half-forms improve the invariant state pairing and contribute to operator transport; their global existence is additional data.
A closed real-polarization leaf illustrates the topological restriction. Since ω vanishes along a Lagrangian leaf, the restricted prequantum connection is flat. A nonzero parallel section exists around a closed loop only if its holonomy is trivial. In cotangent coordinates this is
exp(ℏi​∮pdq)=1,∮pdq=2πℏN.
(55)
This is the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition before the half-form correction. With the standard Maslov correction it becomes ∮pdq=2πℏ(N+ν/4), where ν is the Maslov index. For an oscillator ellipse, the enclosed phase-space area is 2πE/ω0​ and the index is two; the corrected rule gives EN​=ℏω0​(N+1/2) for N≥0, exhibiting the zero-point shift. Singular real polarizations can require distributional sections rather than ordinary smooth polarized sections.
The construction thus separates local operator algebra from global integrality, polarization and holonomy. It recovers familiar quantum representations while exposing their geometric choices. A polarization also limits which observables preserve the state space, and exact Poisson-to-commutator correspondence cannot in general be extended to every classical polynomial in an irreducible canonical quantization. Changes of polarization, operator domains, half-form existence and singular reductions are substantive issues, so geometric quantization is a framework with specified additional data rather than a unique automatic procedure for every classical system.
