The logarithmic derivative D(z)=ϑ00′​(z)/ϑ00​(z) is periodic under z↦z+1 and satisfies D(z+τ)=D(z)−2πi, by the spatial transformation formulas. Choose a fundamental parallelogram with zero-free boundary. Its vertical-side integrals cancel, and subtracting the top horizontal integral from the bottom one gives 2πi. The argument principle therefore counts exactly one zero inside, with multiplicity. Thus the displayed zero is simple and is the unique one modulo Λ. All are simple. In particular the three even theta constants are nonzero and ϑ11′​(0)=0.
Let R(z) be the product of these four functions divided by ϑ11​(2z). The denominator's simple zeros occur exactly at the four half-periods modulo Λ, and each is cancelled by the corresponding simple numerator zero. Hence R extends to an entire function. Under z↦z+1, the four numerator signs multiply to one, as do the two successive shifts of the denominator. Under z↦z+τ, the numerator has multiplier q−2t−4, where t=e2πiz; the denominator, whose argument shifts by 2τ, has the same multiplier. Thus R is an entire elliptic function with periods 1,τ. It is bounded on a fundamental parallelogram and hence on the plane, so Liouville's theorem makes it constant.
