Fix an even weight k and write Γ=SL2​(Z). Extend the slash operator for modular forms to positive-determinant rational matrices by the determinant-normalized slash operator
(f∣k​α)(τ)=(detα)k/2(cτ+d)−kf(ατ).
(10)
The automorphy factor identity makes this a right action. Let Dn​ be the integral matrices of determinant n>0. Their left Γ-cosets have unique determinant-n matrix representatives for Hecke operators
αa,b,d​=(a0​bd​),ad=n,a,d>0,0≤b<d.
(11)
An integral row operation obtained from Bézout's identity makes the first column (a,0)T, and a row shear reduces the other top entry modulo d, proving this description. The usual Hecke operator is
Tn​f=nk/2−1Γ\Dn​∑​f∣k​α=nk−1ad=n∑​d−kb=0∑d−1​f(daτ+b​).​
(12)
This normalization has T1​=1. Right multiplication by Γ permutes the left cosets in Dn​, so Tn​f has the same modular transformation law as f. Each summand is holomorphic in H. Holomorphy at the cusp and preservation of cusp forms follow directly from the coefficient formula below; the full modular group has only one cusp orbit. Thus the operators act on both Mk​ and Sk​.
If f=∑r≥0​ar​qr, summing over b kills terms unless d∣r. Writing r=dℓ gives output exponent aℓ, so a term of exponent m requires a∣m and has original index r=dm/a=nm/a2. Its multiplier is nk−1d1−k=ak−1. The Fourier coefficients of a composite-index Hecke operator are consequently
am​(Tn​f)=a∣gcd(n,m)∑​ak−1anm/a2​(f).​
(13)
For m=0, this means a0​(Tn​f)=σk−1​(n)a0​(f). In particular a1​(Tn​f)=an​(f). For a prime number p,
Tp​f=pk−1f(pτ)+p1​∑b=0p−1​f(pτ+b​),am​(Tp​f)=apm​+pk−1am/p​,
(14)
with a nonexistent fractional-index coefficient interpreted as zero.
The Hecke multiplication relations follow from these coefficients. For coprime indices, the two divisor choices combine uniquely and give Tm​Tn​=Tmn​. For prime powers put A=pk−1, and, on formal Fourier series, let Up​(∑am​qm)=∑apm​qm and Vp​(∑am​qm)=∑am​qpm. These individual maps need not preserve level one, but Up​Vp​=1, and the coefficient formula gives
Tpr​=∑j=0r​AjVpj​Upr−j​.
(15)
Multiplying on the left by Tp​=Up​+AVp​ and cancelling Up​Vp​ yields
Tp​Tpr​=Tpr+1​+pk−1Tpr−1​(r≥1).
(16)
Induction gives Tpu​Tpv​=∑j=0min(u,v)​pj(k−1)Tpu+v−2j​; combining distinct prime factors proves
Tm​Tn​=d∣gcd(m,n)∑​dk−1Tmn/d2​.​
(17)
The modular Hecke algebra is therefore commutative and generated by its prime-index operators. The identity Up​Vp​=1, rather than the generally false reversed identity, is what proves the prime-power recurrence.
On Sk​ use the positive definite Petersson inner product
⟨f,g⟩=∫Γ\H​f(τ)g(τ)​yky2dxdy​.
(18)
Exponential cusp decay makes this integral finite. The Hecke operators are self-adjoint for the Petersson inner product. Here is the correspondence argument. For a positive-determinant matrix α, set H=Γ∩α−1Γα. The finite sum over the double coset ΓαΓ unfolds its pairing into
∫H\H​(f∣k​α)(τ)g(τ)​ykdμ(τ).
(19)
Changing variables z=ατ preserves hyperbolic area, and Im(ατ)=det(α)y/∣cτ+d∣2 shows that the determinant-normalized slash factors transfer α from f to α−1 on g. The new quotient is by αHα−1=Γ∩αΓα−1; folding it back gives the operator for the reversed double coset. For α∈Dn​, its adjugate nα−1 again lies in Dn​. Positive scalar matrices act trivially in the determinant normalization, so α−1 and its adjugate give the same action. The adjugate involution preserves the entire determinant-n correspondence, and the external normalization nk/2−1 is real. Thus
⟨Tn​f,g⟩=⟨f,Tn​g⟩.
(20)
Finite-dimensional spectral theory now gives a simultaneous orthonormal eigenbasis on Sk​, since the operators commute and are self-adjoint. For a common Hecke eigenform f with eigenvalues λn​, the first-coefficient formula gives an​(f)=λn​a1​(f). If a1​(f)=0, every coefficient vanishes and f=0, a contradiction. Rescale each eigenform to have a1​=1. The resulting basis is orthogonal, and
Tn​f=an​(f)f.​
(21)
All these eigenvalues and coefficients are real. A fixed simultaneous eigenspace is one-dimensional, because its normalized Fourier coefficients are uniquely determined by the eigenvalues.
Applying the multiplication relations to an eigenform gives
am​an​=∑d∣gcd(m,n)​dk−1amn/d2​,amn​=am​an​ if gcd(m,n)=1,
(22)
apr+1​=ap​apr​−pk−1apr−1​.
(23)
The recurrence yields ∑r≥0​apr​Xr=(1−ap​X+pk−1X2)−1. Multiplicativity then gives the Euler product of a Hecke eigenform
L(f,s)=p∏​(1−ap​p−s+pk−1−2s)−1,​
(24)
initially in a right half-plane, for example Res>k/2+1 using the Fourier coefficient bound for a cusp form. Modular inversion and the Mellin transform of a cusp-form L-function continue Λ(f,s)=(2π)−sΓ(s)L(f,s) to an entire function and give Λ(f,s)=ikΛ(f,k−s).
There is also an integral structure. If d=dimSk​, choose Fj​=ΔjRk−12j​, 1≤j≤d, with Rw​ a monomial in E4​,E6​ of weight w and constant term one. These forms have integral Fourier coefficients and start with qj; their first d coefficients form a unitriangular matrix. They are a basis, and every cusp form with integral coefficients is an integral combination of them, by successive elimination of its first d coefficients. The coefficient formula shows that Tn​ preserves this lattice. Its characteristic polynomial is consequently monic with integer coefficients, proving that the Hecke eigenvalues are algebraic integers. Together with self-adjointness, this gives real algebraic integers without requiring any unproved bound for their sizes.
For even k≥4, the full space decomposes as Mk​=CEk​⊕Sk​. The normalized Eisenstein series is a simultaneous eigenform with
Tn​Ek​=σk−1​(n)Ek​.
(25)
Its coefficients are −2kσk−1​(m)/Bk​, and the elementary divisor identity σk−1​(m)σk−1​(n)=∑d∣gcd(m,n)​dk−1σk−1​(mn/d2) verifies the eigenvalue formula, including the constant coefficient. Thus the cusp eigenbasis plus Ek​ diagonalizes the operators on Mk​. Weight two and odd weights have no forms; in weight zero the constant function has eigenvalue σ−1​(n). As a concrete cuspidal example, S12​=CΔ, so Tn​Δ=τ(n)Δ for its Fourier coefficients, and the recurrence gives τ(p2)=τ(p)2−p11.
