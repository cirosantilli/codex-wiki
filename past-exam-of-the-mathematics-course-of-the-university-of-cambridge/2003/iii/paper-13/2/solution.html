For 0≤t≤k≤n, define the complete-intersection candidate families
Fi​={A∈[n](k):∣A∩[t+2i]∣≥t+i},0≤i≤min{k−t,⌊(n−t)/2⌋}.
(10)
Each is a t-intersecting family, since two members have at least 2(t+i)−(t+2i)=t common points in the distinguished block. The cardinality form of the Ahlswede-Khachatrian theorem is
M(n,k,t)=imax​j=t+i∑min{k,t+2i}​(jt+2i​)(k−jn−t−2i​)=imax​∣Fi​∣.​
(11)
A binomial coefficient with lower argument outside its usual range is zero. For t=0, or n≤2k−t, the answer is (kn​); in the latter case every pair of k-sets already intersects in at least t points. The displayed candidate formula also attains this value: its largest allowed index is at least n−k, so every k-set has enough points in that candidate's block. For t=k the answer is one. We prove the remaining cases without assuming the complete intersection theorem as an auxiliary result.
We first record the t=1 case of the Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem. For n≥2k, put the ground set in a cyclic ordering. An intersecting family contains at most k of its length-k intervals. To see this, rotate a selected interval to end at position n. Intervals ending at k,…,n−k miss it. The other possible endpoints, except n, are paired as j,j+n−k, 1≤j≤k−1, and the two intervals in each pair are disjoint. Thus at most 1+(k−1)=k intervals are selected. Averaging over all cyclic orders, each k-set has the same chance to occur among the n intervals, yielding
∣A∣(kn​)n​≤k,∣A∣≤(k−1n−1​)=∣F0​∣.
(12)
For n<2k the whole layer works. This proves the t=1 case, including n=2k without any uniqueness assertion.
We may now suppose 2≤t<k and n>2k−t. If n=2k−t+1, complementation of uniform intersecting families changes the problem to an intersecting family of (k−t+1)-sets. The just-proved case gives M(n,k,t)=(k−tn−1​)=(kn−1​). This is ∣Fk−t​∣, the family of all k-sets avoiding the last coordinate. Hence it remains to treat n≥2k−t+2.
Apply coordinate shifts of a set family with i<j. They preserve cardinality and t-intersection. The only way a moved member could lose an intersection point with an unmoved member is that the former moves j to i while the latter contains j and avoids i. The latter's shifted partner must already belong to the original family, since otherwise it too would move; that partner would have had an intersection of size less than t with the original moved member, a contradiction. If both members move, their intersection size is unchanged. Every nontrivial shift decreases the sum of all coordinates in all members, so repeated shifts terminate at a left-compressed set family. We can therefore choose a maximum family A left-compressed.
We prove the small-support generating lemma for extremal intersecting families, including the replacement argument responsible for the threshold. A generating family for a uniform set family is a family G of sets of size at most k whose k-element supersets are exactly A. Start with G=A. A left shift of a generator still has every k-element extension in A: if the extension contains the removed coordinate it already contains the old generator; otherwise reverse that shift in the extension and use left-compression of A. Taking all left shifts of generators, then deleting inclusion-redundant generators, therefore gives an antichain of generators with the following property: every left shift of a generator contains a generator. This normalization never enlarges the support. Choose a normalized generating family whose largest support coordinate m is as small as possible.
The generators themselves are t-intersecting. Indeed, if two generators intersect in fewer than t points, they can be extended to k-sets with intersection size max{∣G∩H∣,2k−n}<t: fill their two residual quotas with disjoint points as far as possible. This contradicts the defining property of the generated family. A generator containing m has the maximum-support generator fibre
{A∈A:A∩[m]=G},size (k−∣G∣n−m​),
(13)
as its exclusively generated members. In fact, any extra coordinate i<m in the prefix supplies a generator inside G−{m}+{i}, which avoids m and also generates that member. An exact prefix G contains no other generator by the antichain property.
If two generators containing m have intersection exactly t, they must have union [m]: any earlier coordinate missing from both permits shifting m out of one generator and lowers their intersection below t. Thus tight pairs of left-compressed generators have sizes summing to m+t. Write Ga​ for the generators of size a containing m, and put N=n−m. Generators avoiding m still meet a shortened generator in at least t points, and shortened generators in classes a,b still meet in at least t unless a+b=m+t.
Suppose
n>(k−t+1)(2+r+1t−1​),m>t+2r.
(14)
We will contradict extremality or the minimality of m. If all the nonempty classes have a≤k−N, no two of them can have sizes summing to m+t: for any partner of size at most k, their sum is at most 2k−N<m+t, since n>2k−t. We may therefore shorten every generator containing m, preserving t-intersection and generating a family containing A. By maximal cardinality it generates exactly A, with support at most m−1, a contradiction. There must be a class with a>k−N.
If such a class has 2a=m+t, put b=m+t−a. Remove classes a,b and insert all shortened generators from class a, or instead all shortened generators from class b. Both replacements remain t-intersecting. The first loses exactly ∣Gb​∣(k−bN​) exclusively generated members and gains at least ∣Ga​∣(k−a+1N​) new members with shortened exact prefixes and tails beyond m; the second gives the reversed comparison. If class b is empty, the first gain is positive and contradicts extremality. Otherwise a,b≤k and a,b>k−N, so all four binomial coefficients are positive. If neither replacement increases cardinality, multiplication of their two inequalities gives
(k−a+1N​)(k−b+1N​)≤(k−aN​)(k−bN​).
(15)
But the ratio of the left side to the right side is
(k−a+1)(k−b+1)(N−k+a)(N−k+b)​>1.
(16)
Indeed, using a+b=m+t, the numerator factors exceed the opposite denominator factors by n+t−2k−1≥1. This contradiction excludes every noncentral class with positive fibre.
Consequently any class with a>k−N is central: m=t+2j and a=t+j for an integer j≥r+1. Shorten the central generators. Each shortened set misses exactly j of the m−1 earlier coordinates, so some coordinate h<m is absent from at least j∣Ga​∣/(m−1) of them. Call that subfamily T. Its members are t-intersecting: a bad pair would have arisen from a tight pair whose union was [m], whereas both shortened sets avoid h. They also meet all unchanged generators in at least t points; a conflicting class would have to be the central class itself. Remove the entire central class and insert T. The loss relative to the unaffected generators is exactly ∣Ga​∣(k−aN​). Each selected shortened prefix may be extended using the N+1 coordinates m,…,n; these extensions are distinct and not generated by an unaffected generator, by the original antichain property. The number restored or added is at least ∣T∣(k−a+1N+1​). Their gain-to-loss ratio is therefore at least
m−1j​k−t−j+1N+1​>1.
(17)
The last inequality is equivalent, by multiplying out, to n>(k−t+1)(2+(t−1)/j), which follows from j≥r+1 and the assumed strict threshold. This finishes the generator replacement proof of the small-support intersection lemma:
n>(k−t+1)(2+r+1t−1​)⟹G⊆P([t+2r]).
(18)
Reflection gives the analogous supporting suffix for a right-compressed optimum.
The second ingredient is the compatibility bound for complementary generating families. Let H generate Ac={[n]∖A:A∈A}, a (n−2k+t)-intersecting family of (n−k)-sets. For any G∈G, H∈H,
∣G∪H∣≥n−k+t.
(19)
If not, put their union in a set T of size n−k+t−1, which is at least both k and n−k. Extend G within T to a k-set A∈A and H within T to an (n−k)-set B∈Ac. Then Bc∈A, but ∣A∩Bc∣=∣A∪B∣−∣B∣≤t−1, a contradiction.
Set
c=k−t+1,nj​=c(2+jt−1​) (j≥1),n0​=∞.
(20)
First suppose nr+1​<n<nr​. These intervals cover the non-boundary parameters in our remaining range, with 0≤r≤k−t. The support lemma gives generators on the prefix [t+2r]. Write k′=n−k, t′=n−2k+t and r′=k−t−r. Direct rearrangement gives
n>c(2+r′+1t′−1​),k′−t′+1=c.
(21)
After multiplication by r′+1=c−r, the strict inequality becomes c(t−1)+r(2c−n)>0. For r>0 this is exactly n<nr​; for r=0 it is c(t−1)>0. Thus the reflected support lemma gives generators for Ac on a suffix of length t′+2r′=n−t−2r, disjoint from the prefix.
If every prefix generator has size at least t+r, then A⊆Fr​. If every suffix generator has size at least t′+r′=n−k−r, every complemented member contains that many suffix points, and its original member has at least t+r prefix points; again A⊆Fr​. At least one of these alternatives holds: otherwise generators of sizes at most t+r−1 and n−k−r−1 would have union of size at most n−k+t−2, violating compatibility. Since Fr​ itself is t-intersecting and A is maximum,
M(n,k,t)=∣Fr​∣(nr+1​<n<nr​).
(22)
Finally suppose n=nr+1​>2k−t+1. The support lemma, now with index r+1, places G in [t+2r+2]. For the complement, the strict support inequality still places H in the suffix beginning at t+2r+1. These supports overlap in two coordinates, which is harmless for the union bound in compatibility. Either every G has size at least t+r+1, placing A inside Fr+1​, or every H has size at least n−k−r, placing A inside Fr​. If neither held, a pair of generators would have union of size at most n−k+t−1, again impossible.
The two candidate sizes agree at this boundary. In passing from Fr​ to Fr+1​, the gained sets have t+r−1 points in the old block and both new points; the lost sets have t+r old-block points and neither new point. Their counts are
(t+r−1t+2r​)(k−t−r−1n−t−2r−2​),(t+rt+2r​)(k−t−rn−t−2r−2​).
(23)
The ratio is one exactly when (r+1)n=c(t+2r+1), that is, n=nr+1​. Therefore M=∣Fr​∣=∣Fr+1​∣ there. The full-level cases, the elementary t=1 proof, the complementary borderline case, and these strict-interval and boundary arguments establish the boxed cardinality theorem for every admissible parameter.
