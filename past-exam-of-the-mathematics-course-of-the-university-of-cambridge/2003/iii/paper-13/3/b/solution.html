Let H be the r-uniform hypergraph and use strong saturation of a uniform hypergraph in its immediate-completion sense: each missing edge e lies in an (r+t)-set T whose other r-subsets are already edges of H. The argument allows existing hypergraph cliques. Take n≥r+t, the usual parameter range; if r≤n<r+t, strong saturation forces every possible edge to be present, and the inequality is immediate whenever its binomial terms are defined. The case t=0 has the trivial lower bound zero.
For t≥1, choose distinct real numbers z1​,…,zn​ and form the t×n Vandermonde matrix M whose ith column is (1,zi​,…,zit−1​)T. Every t columns are linearly independent by the Vandermonde determinant. Set U=kerM⊆Rn, so dimU=n−t, and work in the quotient vector space
W=⋀rRn/⋀rU,dimW=(rn​)−(rn−t​).
(26)
Here the inclusion of ⋀rU is injective: extend a basis of U to a basis of Rn and use the resulting wedge bases of the exterior powers. Write we​ for the image in W of the coordinate wedge indexed by an r-set e. All these images span W.
For any (r+t)-set T, let VT​ be its coordinate subspace. The restriction of M has rank t, so U∩VT​ has dimension r. Wedge a basis of this intersection to obtain a nonzero element
ωT​=∑e⊆T∣e∣=r​​ce​ee​∈⋀rU.
(27)
Every coefficient ce​ is nonzero. Indeed, projection of U∩VT​ onto the e coordinates is an isomorphism: a vector in its kernel is supported on T∖e, whose t columns of M are independent. The coefficient is the determinant of that projection in the chosen bases. Passing to W therefore gives a linear dependence
∑e⊆T∣e∣=r​​ce​we​=0(ce​=0).
(28)
If e is missing from H, take its strong-saturation witness T. The dependence expresses we​ in the span of the wf​ for the other edges f⊆T, all of which are present. Thus the vectors indexed by the present edges span all of W. The exterior-power quotient bound for strong hypergraph saturation follows:
∣E(H)∣≥dimW=(rn​)−(rn−t​).​
(29)
The bound is sharp: include precisely the r-sets meeting one fixed t-set S. A missing edge e is disjoint from S, and every other r-subset of e∪S meets S, so adding e immediately completes a hypergraph clique of order r+t. The number of included edges is exactly the displayed bound.
