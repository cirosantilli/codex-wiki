The term inside the square is congruent to 3 modulo 4, and its smallest possible absolute value is one, attained exactly at ∣A∩B∣=p−1. Such pairs exist. Consequently the diameter pairs of this finite set in dimension D=(2N​) are precisely the edges of G. Every part of strictly smaller diameter is an independent set and has at most (p−1N​) points. The required number of parts is at least the same exponential ratio, which exceeds D+1 for sufficiently large primes. Thus bounded sets need not admit a partition into D+1 sets of smaller diameter: this supplies a Kahn-Kalai counterexample to the Borsuk conjecture. Both applications use the prime case of the proved prime-power result.
