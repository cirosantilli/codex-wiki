The four functions theorem concerns nonnegative functions α,β,γ,δ on the Boolean lattice. Its pointwise hypothesis and conclusion are
α(A)β(B)≤γ(A∪B)δ(A∩B)for every A,B,
(35)
(A∑​α(A))(B∑​β(B))≤(C∑​γ(C))(D∑​δ(D)).​
(36)
We prove it by eliminating coordinates.
The elementary step is the two-point four-functions inequality. Suppose ai​bj​≤cmax(i,j)​dmin(i,j)​ for i,j∈{0,1}. Put x=a0​b1​, y=a1​b0​, M=c1​d0​, R=c0​d1​. Then x,y≤M and, using the two diagonal bounds,
xy=(a0​b0​)(a1​b1​)≤(c0​d0​)(c1​d1​)=MR.
(37)
If M>0, (M−x)(M−y)≥0 yields x+y≤M+xy/M≤M+R. If M=0, both cross terms vanish and the same conclusion holds. Adding the diagonal bounds gives
(a0​+a1​)(b0​+b1​)≤(c0​+c1​)(d0​+d1​).
(38)
For subsets S,T⊆[n−1], take ai​=α(S∪Ei​), bj​=β(T∪Ej​), ci​=γ((S∪T)∪Ei​) and di​=δ((S∩T)∪Ei​), where E0​=∅, E1​={n}. The original hypothesis supplies precisely the four inequalities of the elementary step. Therefore the marginal functions
α′(S)=α(S)+α(S∪{n}),
(39)
and similarly β′,γ′,δ′, satisfy the same pointwise hypothesis on [n−1]. Induction, starting with the one-element Boolean lattice on the empty ground set, proves the four functions theorem. This proof includes zero-valued functions without division by zero.
For the Harris-Kleitman inequality, let F,G be increasing set families, meaning they contain every superset of each member. In the theorem take α=1F​, β=1G​, γ=1F∩G​ and δ=1. If A∈F and B∈G, their union belongs to both increasing families, so the hypothesis holds. Thus, for the uniform probability measure μ on subsets,
μ(F∩G)≥μ(F)μ(G).​
(40)
Mapping every subset to its complement proves the same Harris-Kleitman inequality for two decreasing families. An increasing and a decreasing family have the reverse inequality: apply positive correlation to the increasing family and the complement, as a family, of the decreasing one. More generally, increasing nonnegative functions obey the corresponding positive-correlation bound by the same four-functions substitution, since both function values at the union dominate the original values. For an independent-coordinate product measure, multiply all four functions by its weight w; the identity w(A)w(B)=w(A∪B)w(A∩B) preserves the hypothesis and proves the weighted Harris inequality too.
Finally, replace each intersecting family Ai​ by its upward closure of a set family Ui​. It is still intersecting: two supersets of intersecting members have nonempty intersection. It contains at most one of each pair S,Sc, and hence ∣Ui​∣≤2n−1. Let Di​ be the complement of Ui​ as a family. These families are decreasing and have measure at least one half. Their intersections are decreasing too, so repeated Harris-Kleitman inequality gives
μ(⋂i=1k​Di​)≥∏i=1k​μ(Di​)≥2−k.
(41)
Taking complements and using Ai​⊆Ui​ proves the union bound for intersecting families:
​i=1⋃k​Ai​​≤2n−2n−k.​
(42)
For k≤n, equality is attained by the families of sets containing coordinate i, 1≤i≤k. For k>n, the displayed real bound implies the integer bound 2n−1, which is attained by using the n coordinate families and repeating families as necessary.
