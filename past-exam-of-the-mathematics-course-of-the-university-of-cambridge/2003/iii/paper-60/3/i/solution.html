Because ⟨B⟩=0, a periodic primitive H exists. Fix its additive constant by ⟨H⟩=0. Integrating the conserved-field equation once in space gives HT​=σ(HX​+δA2)X​: the possible integration constant vanishes on taking the spatial mean. Treat the spatial average as the inner product defining functional derivatives. Integration by parts, with no boundary terms under periodic boundary conditions, gives
δAδV​=μA+αA2−A3+AXX​−AHX​=AT​,δHδV​=21​(A2)X​+2δHXX​​=2δσHT​​.
(15)
Thus the conserved-field real amplitude equation is a gradient flow ascending V, or descending −V, with positive diagonal mobilities. Along a classical solution,
dTdV​=⟨AT2​+2δσHT2​​⟩≥0.​
(16)
For the mixed term put P=⟨A4⟩1/2 and Q=⟨HX2​⟩1/2. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a completed square give
⟨−21​A2HX​−4δHX2​​⟩≤2PQ​−4δQ2​=4δP2​−4δ(Q−δP)2​≤4δ​⟨A4⟩.
(17)
Consequently, for β=1−δ>0,
V≤⟨2μ​A2+3α​A3−4β​A4−21​AX2​⟩≤maxa∈R​(2μ​a2+3α​a3−4β​a4)<∞.
(18)
The negative leading quartic coefficient proves the upper bound. The stronger pointwise square completion
V=⟨2μ​A2+3α​A3−4β​A4−21​AX2​−4δ(HX​+δA2)2​⟩
(19)
is also useful: a superlevel set V≥V(0) bounds the amplitude in L4, its spatial derivative in L2, and B=HX​ in L2, because a quartic polynomial with negative leading coefficient dominates its lower-order terms.
The dynamics dissipates toward the steady-state set rather than sustaining nonconstant periodic motion. Indeed V increases to a finite limit, and the time integral of ⟨AT2​+HT2​/(2δσ)⟩ is finite. For global regular trajectories with the usual parabolic precompactness, every limit point is stationary, by the LaSalle invariance principle. A nonconstant periodic or recurrent orbit would force a strictly increasing V to return to its original value and is impossible. Boundedness of a monotone functional alone does not prove convergence to one particular equilibrium; isolation of the limiting equilibrium or an additional convergence theorem supplies that stronger claim.
