Consider a triangle of three equal links, each with capacity 1000. Each vertex pair receives direct calls of offered load 945. A call tries its direct link first; on finding it full, it tries the two-link path through the third vertex. Take exponential distributions of mean one, so the arrival rates are also 945.
In a symmetric Erlang fixed point approximation, let B be the blocking probability of each link. A given link sees its own direct load 945. Each of the other two call classes offers overflow only if its direct link blocks and the other link of its two-link path accepts. The reduced load into the given link therefore has two extra terms 945B(1−B). The scalar consistency equation is
B=E(945[1+2B(1−B)],1000).
(21)
Write its continuous residual as f(B)=E(945[1+2B(1−B)],1000)−B. The following strict intervals certify alternating signs:
0.002722−0.0027140.010042−0.113238​<f(0)<0.002723,<f(1/100)<−0.002713,<f(1/10)<0.010043,<f(2/5)<−0.113237.​
(22)
These are exact rational certificates, not rounded floating-point evidence. For each rational argument, start with b0​=1 and iterate bj​=abj−1​/(j+abj−1​) for j=1,…,1000; all operations are rational. Direct integer comparisons with the displayed thresholds certify the intervals. This uses rational certificates for the Erlang loss recursion.
The intermediate value theorem now supplies at least one zero in each of
(0,1/100),(1/100,1/10),(1/10,2/5).​
(23)
Thus the alternative-routing approximation has at least three distinct symmetric solutions. The mechanism is positive overflow feedback at moderate blocking, followed by saturation at high blocking. This does not give several stationary distributions for the exact finite loss network: its reachable irreducible Markov chain still has a unique stationary distribution. Multiple approximate fixed points concern the self-consistency model.
