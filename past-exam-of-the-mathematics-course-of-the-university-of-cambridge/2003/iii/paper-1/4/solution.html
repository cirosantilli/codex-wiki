 ... Mathematics course of the University of Cambridge Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2003 iii Paper 1 4
OurBigBook.com Words: 306
Use the finite-dimensional complex-module setting of the course. We prove the Weyl complete reducibility theorem by Haar averaging produces invariant complements, defining its constructions rather than invoking complete reducibility itself.
Choose a compact real form k⊂g: it is a real Lie subalgebra with g=k⊕ik, and its simply connected Lie group K is compact. Existence of this form and compact group is a standard fact for complex semisimple Lie algebras. Restrict the representation to k; integration of a Lie-algebra representation gives a group representation R:K→GL(V). Since K is connected and generated by exponentials, the g-invariant subspace W is also K-invariant.
Choose any positive-definite Hermitian inner product on V. With the Haar measure of K normalized to mass one, define
⟨v,w⟩K​=∫K​⟨R(k)v,R(k)w⟩0​dk.
(29)
This is a Hermitian inner product, and it is positive-definite: for v=0 every R(k)v is nonzero, so the continuous positive integrand has positive integral. Right invariance of Haar measure gives ⟨R(a)v,R(a)w⟩K​=⟨v,w⟩K​ for a∈K.
Let W′=W⊥ for this averaged inner product. Finite-dimensional inner-product linear algebra gives V=W⊕W′. If v∈W′ and w∈W, then
⟨R(a)v,w⟩K​=⟨v,R(a−1)w⟩K​=0,
(30)
because R(a−1)w∈W. Thus W′ is K-invariant. Differentiating makes it k-invariant, and since it is a complex vector subspace, g=k+ik also preserves it. Therefore
W′=W⊥K​ is a g-submodule and V=W⊕W′.​
(31)
The zero or whole-space submodule cases are included. This proves the required complement theorem without circularly assuming it.
The finite-dimensional hypothesis is essential to the statement. The nonsplit zero-weight Verma extension for sl2 is a counterexample for unrestricted modules: M(0) has basis vn​ with hvn​=−2nvn​, fvn​=vn+1​ and evn​=n(1−n)vn−1​. Its submodule spanned by vn​ for n≥1 has trivial one-dimensional quotient. A complement would be trivial and contained in the zero-weight line Cv0​, yet fv0​=v1​=0. Hence no such complement exists in that infinite-dimensional case. The paper's finite-dimensional representation setting supplies the necessary hypothesis.
