For the supplied normal form, the four local topological phase portraits are as follows. When μ2​<0, only the origin is present and it is a saddle equilibrium. When μ2​>0,μ1​<0, the origin is attracting, between the two outer saddle equilibria; inward-facing separatrices enter its basin. When μ2​>0 and 0<μ1​<h(μ2​), the origin is repelling and a stable limit cycle surrounds it, inside the separatrix boundary formed by the outer saddle equilibria. Finally, when μ2​>0,μ1​>h(μ2​), the origin remains repelling but the local limit cycle has disappeared, and trajectories leave the central region through the opened separatrix channels. Whether the origin is a node or a focus within one of these regions does not change this orbit topology.
On the connection curve, the two outer saddle equilibria are joined in both directions; the period of the disappearing limit cycle diverges. This is a heteroclinic cycle, not a pair of saddle homoclinic orbits. A codimension-three degeneracy is possible when the double-zero eigenvalue condition and the vanishing cubic restoring coefficient of the pitchfork bifurcation coincide. Equating qD​ and qTB​ gives
k=1+σ2​,h=1+σ+2σ28σ2​,α2=2σ2π2(1+σ)​.​

Varying the aspect ratio supplies the third parameter, in addition to r,q. At a fixed generic aspect ratio it is not a codimension-two event available by varying only r,q.
It is not a triple-zero eigenvalue: the three-mode characteristic polynomial still has the noncritical factor s+1+2σ, while the c,e eigenvalues remain strictly negative. The extra degeneracy is nonlinear. A fifth-order, two-dimensional normal form is needed for its complete unfolding; the one-dimensional quintic pitchfork bifurcation used away from qTB​ cannot be substituted at the double-zero point.

We count periodic orbits whose trajectories remain near the two specified homoclinic orbits; the local hypotheses impose no bound on unrelated distant cycles. Choose the return domain small enough that Aδ∣x∣δ−1 and Bδ∣x∣δ−1 are less than one. Each admissible return branch is then a contraction mapping.

With reflection symmetry, choose μ=ν=m and A=B. The two primary homoclinic orbit curves are then encountered simultaneously at m=0. For m<0 the return has two attracting single-lobe cycles; for m>0 it has one attracting two-lobe cycle. This is the symmetric homoclinic gluing bifurcation: two cycles meet the two saddle loops and are replaced by a cycle traversing both lobes. Since δ>1, the saddle equilibrium contracts strongly enough for the nearby cycles to be stable.
One can also use the signed gluing-map reduction z=−xy. In the symmetric return map it gives z′=m+Asgn(z)∣z∣δ. A negative fixed point lifts to two reflection-related flow cycles; a positive fixed point lifts to one period-two orbit of the two-sheet section map, hence one two-lobe flow cycle. This distinction prevents counting the two section intersections as two different flow cycles.
Without reflection symmetry the two splitting parameters are independent. The primary loops occur separately, and the two compound curves bound small coexistence wedges where a single-lobe and a two-lobe cycle are both stable. Thus the six-region picture is the generic two-parameter unfolding of the symmetric gluing event. The simultaneous encounter of both primary loops is codimension two for a general planar flow but codimension one in the reflection-symmetric family. The contraction argument applies to the small return domain; it makes no assertion that a globally arbitrary P,Q system has only these cycles everywhere.

Hence there is no ordinary branch of isolated invariant tori on either side in this quadratic system. The secondary event has vanishing radial saturation coefficients; its imaginary linear pair alone does not establish a nondegenerate torus bifurcation.

The normal form is invariant under z↦eiφz. This continuous rotational symmetry makes the axis invariant and reduces the nontrivial geometry to two amplitude variables. The axis connection from E+​ to E−​ persists without parameter tuning, while rotating the outer connection produces a family of connections from E−​ back to E+​. A two-dimensional autonomous amplitude flow cannot by itself produce the proposed saddle-focus return chaos: symmetry reduction hides the phase-dependent reinjection needed for that mechanism.
Generic higher-order terms need not retain this exact rotational symmetry. A normal form can preserve it to a finite truncation order while the full equations have small symmetry-breaking remainders. These unfold the special connection geometry into phase-dependent returns; nearby parameter curves can contain homoclinic orbits to a saddle-focus, and the Shilnikov bifurcation mechanism becomes available. Their exact placement depends on the omitted terms and is not fixed by the displayed truncation alone.
For example, at the perturbed positive-axis saddle, the real unstable eigenvalue is λu​=2a and the stable complex pair has real part μ1​−2a+a2. Near the calculated connection curve μ1​=−a2/4+o(a2), the contracting ratio is
δ=−λu​Reλs​​=1−83a​+o(a)<1.
(42)
This is the positive-saddle-value regime, explaining why the relevant saddle-focus return can be expanding.
In the Shilnikov return map, take a positive return coordinate y and 0<δ<1, with nondegenerate A=0,B=0. At the connection parameter μ=0, the fixed point condition is
cos(Blogy+Φ)=y1−δ/A.
(43)
The right side tends to zero, whereas the phase passes through infinitely many oscillations as y↓0. Hence there are infinitely many positive fixed points accumulating at zero, with logarithmically spaced amplitudes. For these points,
f′(y)=Ayδ−1{δcos(Blogy+Φ)−Bsin(Blogy+Φ)},
(44)
whose magnitude tends to infinity along the accumulating fixed-point sequence. They represent long-period saddle periodic orbits of the full return, with period growing like λu−1​log(1/y), rather than infinitely many stable cycles.
Successive turning branches of this oscillatory return can stretch and fold across suitable intervals. The full saddle-focus Poincaré return map then admits invariant Smale horseshoes and chaotic itineraries near a generic homoclinic orbit at a Shilnikov bifurcation. Varying μ unfolds accumulating saddle-node and period-doubling events. Whether any resulting invariant set attracts trajectories depends on the other return direction and the omitted terms; the leading scalar map alone does not prove a chaotic attractor for every perturbation. Its domain also excludes negative iterates, which leave the chosen return branch. The degeneracies A=0 or B=0 would remove the oscillatory mechanism, so the conclusion uses generic, genuinely saddle-focus reinjection.
