Set L=8πGρΛ​/3>0. The Friedmann effective potential for matter, radiation and vacuum now reads
V(a)=−aM​−a2Q​−La2,at2​+V(a)=E,E=−k.
(7)

(8)
Equivalently V′(a)=0 when 2L=M/a3+2Q/a4; the right-hand side decreases strictly, so there is exactly one stationary point. It is a maximum because
V′′(a)=−a32M​−a46Q​−2L<0.
(9)
The energy line in the preceding sketch therefore gives five broad types of history:
  • Monotonic expansion. For E>V(aE​), the universe expands from a=0 to arbitrarily large a.
  • Monotonic contraction. Reversing cosmic time gives the corresponding contracting history.
  • Recollapse. For E<V(aE​), the inner allowed interval ends at a−​: the universe expands from zero to a−​ and contracts back to zero.
  • Bounce. The outer allowed interval starts at a+​. A universe contracting from large a reaches a+​ and re-expands. Here V′(a+​)<0, so att​>0 at the turning point.
  • Static equilibrium. For E=V(aE​), a=aE​ and at​=0 give the Einstein static universe.
At the critical energy there are also marginal branches within the monotonic classes: expansion from zero can approach aE​ asymptotically, or expansion can start asymptotically at aE​ and proceed to infinity; contraction gives their reversals. E−V(a)≃−21​V′′(aE​)(a−aE​)2,
(10)
so dt≃da/[−V′′(aE​)/2​∣a−aE​∣] diverges logarithmically. The static equilibrium is unstable. Since V(aE​)<0, the recollapsing, bouncing and static cases require positive spatial curvature k>0.

The emission term comes from the local photon temperature. A blackbody radiation density is proportional to T4, so linearization gives δT/T=δγ​/4. It is evaluated at last scattering, with the source position on the observed ray.
The velocity term is the Doppler CMB anisotropy: motion of the emitting plasma changes the measured photon frequency by the component of its peculiar velocity along the line of sight. Its sign here follows the paper's choice of the sky direction n.
The metric integral follows the null geodesic energy equation in synchronous gauge. Time dependence of the spatial metric perturbation changes the photon energy by −hij′​ni​nj​/2 along the ray. It includes gravitational redshift endpoint terms as well as the part due to evolving gravitational potentials. In particular, the entire synchronous metric integral is not just the integrated Sachs-Wolfe effect. Integrating by parts to express it in conformal Newtonian gauge separates the ordinary Sachs-Wolfe effect from the evolving-potential contribution.

The positive conformal factor leaves the causal structure unchanged. In the strip −π/2<η<π/2, a future-directed causal curve obeys
​dηdξ​​≤1,
(54)
and null geodesics have slopes ±1. Its past and future conformal boundaries η=∓π/2 are spacelike. A finite conformal time interval limits the range of light signals: the observer at ξ=0 can receive a signal from (η,ξ) before future infinity exactly when ∣ξ∣<π/2−η. Equality describes its observer-dependent cosmological event horizon.
For a finite Penrose diagram of the unwrapped spatial coordinate, set U=arctanu, V=arctanv, X=(U+V)/2, and Y=(V−U)/2. Then the metric is conformal to −dY2+dX2; light rays remain at 45 degrees, and the boundaries are the images of v−u=±π. The figure also shows the spatially periodic de Sitter cylinder so that the global distinction is explicit.
Both the covering spacetime and the de Sitter cylinder are globally hyperbolic. To see this directly in the covering spacetime, suppose an inextendible future-directed causal curve had a future limiting conformal time strictly below π/2. The slope bound makes ξ Cauchy as that time is approached, so the curve has a limiting point inside the spacetime and can be extended, a contradiction. The past endpoint similarly approaches −π/2. Thus every constant-η line is crossed exactly once and is a Cauchy hypersurface. On the cylinder the same argument uses compactness of the spatial circle. Equivalently causal diamonds between finite events are bounded and closed within the strip, hence compact.
Initial field data on a complete Cauchy hypersurface determine a unique solution for a well-posed hyperbolic Cauchy problem, with the relevant constraints imposed for constrained systems. Spacelike conformal infinity does not require additional timelike-boundary conditions. An observer's cosmological horizon limits which data that observer can see; it does not destroy global uniqueness from data on the whole hypersurface.

Away from the source, the decaying radial solution is proportional to e−∣n∣r/R/r. Its distributional normalization follows from integrating the Laplacian over a small sphere: the radial flux of 1/r is −4π, so
ϕn​(r)=2πrq1​​e−∣n∣r/R.
(64)
This is the contribution of the nth Kaluza-Klein mode, with inverse range ∣n∣/R. At equal compact coordinate, the sum is a geometric series,
ϕ(r,0)=2πrq1​​(1+2∑n=1∞​e−nr/R)=2πrq1​​coth2Rr​.
(65)
Therefore the pair interaction energy, without the individual self-energies, is
U(r)=2πrq1​q2​​coth2Rr​.​
(66)
Its normalization is fixed by the stated −4πRρ source, rather than by importing another convention for Coulomb's law. More generally summing the phases gives the periodic electrostatic Green function with one compact dimension,
ϕ(r,z)=2πrq1​​cosh(r/R)−cos(z/R)sinh(r/R)​.
(67)
An independent check uses the method of images in four spatial dimensions. Since ∇42​s−2=−4π2δ4, its periodic image sum is Rq1​∑n​[r2+(z+2πnR)2]−1/π, with the same normalization and short-distance singularity.
For r≫R, only the zero Kaluza-Klein mode survives:
U(r)=2πrq1​q2​​[1+2e−r/R+O(e−2r/R)].​
(68)
For r≪R, cothx=x−1+x/3+O(x3) gives
U(r)=πr2q1​q2​R​+12πRq1​q2​​+O(r2/R3).​
(69)
The leading potential thus changes from inverse distance to inverse squared distance. Differentiating gives the signed outward force
Fr​=2πq1​q2​​[r2coth(r/(2R))​+2Rrcsch2(r/(2R))​].
(70)
It scales as r−2 at large distance and as 2q1​q2​R/(πr3) at small distance. A precision inverse-square-law test over separations approaching R would reveal the extra dimension through this change of force law or its exponential corrections. The model assumes localized sources at equal compact coordinate and an electromagnetic field able to explore the circle; a field confined to ordinary spacetime would not show this effect. Finite source size and ordinary screening must be controlled when comparing a real force measurement with this point-source prediction.
