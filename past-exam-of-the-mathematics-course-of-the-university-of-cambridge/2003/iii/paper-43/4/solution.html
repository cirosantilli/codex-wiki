For minimization of an energy E(ϑ), simulated annealing at temperature t>0 targets the Boltzmann distribution πt​(ϑ)∝e−E(ϑ)/t relative to a specified parameter reference measure. With proposal distribution q(ϑ′∣ϑ), accept with
at​(ϑ,ϑ′)=min{1,exp[−tE(ϑ′)−E(ϑ)​]q(ϑ′∣ϑ)q(ϑ∣ϑ′)​}.
(28)
At each temperature run the chain long enough to explore that law, then cool and retain the best state seen. For symmetric proposals every improvement is accepted, while a worsening move is accepted with probability e−ΔE/t. Equilibrium laws concentrate near global minima when their normalizing integrals are finite and the minima are suitably isolated. A particular rapid cooling schedule does not automatically inherit this guarantee.
Likelihood maximization uses energy E=−ℓ, not E=ℓ. The source's later wording “f(p) equal to the log-likelihood” conflicts with its preceding minimization convention. The intended maximum-likelihood Boltzmann distribution is proportional to eℓ/t=L1/t. With the opposite sign, likelihood maxima would be disfavored; for binomial data with both successes and failures the density often would not even be integrable near the endpoints as t decreases.
For the maximum-likelihood estimates, write s=∑j=1m​xj​, xˉ=s/m and S=∑j​(xj​−xˉ)2. In the binomial distribution model with known positive integer n, the log-likelihood is
ℓB​(p)=CB​+slogp+(mn−s)log(1−p),CB​=∑j=1m​log(xj​n​).
(29)
Differentiating gives s/p−(mn−s)/(1−p)=0, hence p​=s/(mn). The second derivative is negative in the interior. If s=0 or s=mn, the maximum is at the corresponding endpoint. This model requires integer observations in {0,…,n}; otherwise its likelihood is zero.
For the normal distribution model write v=σ2. Its log-likelihood is
ℓN​(μ,v)=−2m​log(2πv)−2vS+m(μ−xˉ)2​.
(30)
For fixed v, the unique maximizing mean is xˉ. Substituting it and differentiating in v gives v=S/m, so, assuming S>0,
p​=mns​,μ​=xˉ,σ2=mS​.​
(31)
If S=0, the normal density likelihood grows without bound as v↓0 with μ=xˉ; there is no finite interior maximum or ordinary finite AIC for that fit.
For Gaussian likelihood Gibbs annealing, use LN1/t​ relative to dμdv. Completing the square and identifying an inverse-gamma distribution gives the Gibbs sampler conditionals
μ∣v,t∼N(xˉ,tv/m),v∣μ,t∼InvGamma(2tm​−1,2tS+m(μ−xˉ)2​).​
(32)
Integrating out μ contributes a factor proportional to v1/2, so the marginal is v∼InvGamma(m/(2t)−3/2,S/(2t)). The joint target is proper for 0<t<m/3 when S>0; starting the Lebesgue-based annealing above this range would require a proper reference factor or bounded parameter domain. For sufficiently small t, its moments satisfy
Et​[v]=m−5tS​,Vart​(v)=(m−5t)2(m−7t)2tS2​,Et​[(μ−xˉ)2]=m(m−5t)tS​.
(33)
Thus equilibrium draws concentrate at (xˉ,S/m) as t↓0.
There is also a direct convergence proof for the actual successive Gibbs sampler updates here. Choose deterministic 0<tr​≤m/10 with tr​→0, start at a finite deterministic v0​>0, draw μr​ from the first conditional using vr−1​, then draw vr​ from the second using μr​. Conditional normal moments give E[(μr​−xˉ)2∣vr−1​]=tr​vr−1​/m and E[(μr​−xˉ)4∣vr−1​]=3tr2​vr−12​/m2. The permitted inverse-gamma moments therefore yield
E[vr​]=m−4tr​S+tr​E[vr−1​]​,E[vr2​]=(m−4tr​)(m−6tr​)S2+2Str​E[vr−1​]+3tr2​E[vr−12​]​.
(34)
The coefficient on the preceding first moment is at most 1/6, and that on the preceding second moment is at most 1/8. The remaining coefficients are uniformly bounded, so both moment sequences are bounded by iteration. Since tr​→0, the recursions then give E[vr​]→S/m and E[vr2​]→(S/m)2. Also E[(μr​−xˉ)2]=tr​E[vr−1​]/m→0. Hence
E[(vr​−S/m)2]⟶0,E[(μr​−xˉ)2]⟶0.​
(35)
This proves convergence in mean square, and thus in probability, to the normal maximum-likelihood estimates for this explicit cooling/update scheme, rather than merely assuming equilibration.
For binomial likelihood-power annealing, normalizing ps/t(1−p)(mn−s)/t on (0,1) gives
p∼Beta(ts​+1,tmn−s​+1).​
(36)
Its mean is (s+t)/(mn+2t), and its variance is
Vart​(p)=(mn+2t)2(mn+3t)t(s+t)(mn−s+t)​.
(37)
The mean tends to s/(mn) and the variance tends to zero, proving concentration at the binomial maximum-likelihood estimate, including the endpoint cases. Independent exact draws from these beta laws are a simple annealing implementation within this model.
For model selection, the Akaike information criterion is 2d−2ℓ with dB​=1 and dN​=2. Thus
AICB​AICN​​=2−2{CB​+slogp​+(mn−s)log(1−p​)},=4+m{log(2πS/m)+1}.​​
(38)
Use the endpoint convention 0log0=0. For a trans-dimensional annealing for penalized likelihood construction, target the disjoint model spaces with unnormalized densities
Πt​(B,p)=e(ℓB​(p)−1)/t,Πt​(N,μ,v)=e(ℓN​(μ,v)−2)/t
(39)
relative to dp and dμdv, respectively. The energies are half the unoptimized AIC, d−ℓ; their joint minimizer is the fitted model with smaller AIC. Keep constants such as CB​ and the normal density normalization, which do not cancel across models. The within-model conditional draws are unchanged by these constant penalties.
Here is a complete dimension-changing proposal. Select a binomial-to-normal move with probability b>0 and a normal-to-binomial move with probability d>0, both fixed; other steps can use the within-model updates. From B,p, draw u∼N(0,1) with density h(u)=(2π)−1/2e−u2/2 and set
μ′=log1−pp​,v′=eu.
(40)
This bijection from (p,u)∈(0,1)×R onto (μ′,v′)∈R×(0,∞) has inverse p=(1+e−μ′)−1, u=logv′. Its absolute Jacobian determinant is v′/[p(1−p)]. The Binomial-normal reversible-jump annealing acceptance ratio is therefore
RB→N​=exp[tℓN​(μ′,v′)−2−ℓB​(p)+1​]bh(u)d​p(1−p)v′​,aB→N​=min(1,RB→N​).​
(41)
For a reverse proposal from N,μ,v, compute p′=(1+e−μ)−1 and u′=logv deterministically and accept with
RN→B​=exp[tℓB​(p′)−1−ℓN​(μ,v)+2​]dbh(u′)​vp′(1−p′)​,aN→B​=min(1,RN→B​).​
(42)
These ratios are reciprocal at inverse states and enforce detailed balance on the dimension-matched augmented spaces. They specify the proposal density, model-selection probabilities and Jacobian explicitly. The map is a valid example, not a claim of optimal mixing. Positive-temperature beta draws stay in the open interval even when the limiting estimate is an endpoint. At each temperature ensure adequate model switching; retain the best fitted penalized likelihood encountered. If minima tie, limiting concentration need not choose a unique model.
There is a material measurement qualification. A binomial probability mass and an unrounded normal density use different observation reference measures. Their raw AIC values are not an invariant, scientifically calibrated comparison of the same recorded data. The expressions and jump algorithm above implement the formal density comparison suggested by the question, with its units fixed. For integer-recorded measurements, a compatible normal model instead assigns probabilities Pμ,v​(x−1/2<Z≤x+1/2), suitably adjusting boundaries or truncation if required. Then both models describe the same recorded outcomes relative to counting measure. Use those masses in the normal likelihood and the same reversible-jump formula; its simple normal MLE and conjugate Gibbs updates will generally no longer apply. This observation-model correction cannot be silently combined with the continuous-normal formulas.
