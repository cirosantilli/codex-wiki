Here a supersymmetry-preserving combination means a real combination of Hermitian Majorana spinor supercharges, in a positive-norm unitary representation of the ordinary Super-Poincaré algebra. This reality qualification is essential. Let ∣ψ⟩ be normalized and in the charge domains, and define the real symmetric matrix
Mab​=21​⟨ψ∣{Qa​,Qb​}∣ψ⟩.
(9)
For real u, the Hermitian operator qu​=uaQa​ satisfies
∥qu​ψ∥2=⟨ψ∣qu2​∣ψ⟩=uTMu.
(10)
Therefore M is a positive semidefinite matrix, and
qu​ψ=0⟺u∈kerM.
(11)
The reverse implication uses positivity: diagonalize M, so a zero quadratic form has zero component in every positive-eigenvalue direction. This converts the problem into nullity of real N=1 supercharges, not just a condition on the determinant of an operator-valued expression.
In the equivalent Weyl spinor form, {qα​,qβ†​}=2σαβ˙​m​Pm​. After a fixed invertible change of real charge basis, M is the realification of the Hermitian two-by-two matrix
B=EI+p⋅σ,E=⟨P0⟩,p=⟨P⟩,
(12)
up to the harmless spatial-sign convention. A complex Hermitian matrix has the same eigenvalues in its realification, each repeated twice. The Pauli matrix multiplication law gives (p⋅σ)2=∣p∣2I, so these eigenvalues are E+∣p∣ and E−∣p∣, each twice. Positivity forces E≥∣p∣. If one nonzero real u annihilates the state, M has a kernel: either E=∣p∣>0, with exactly two zero eigenvalues, or E=∣p∣=0, with four. Thus
dimR​{u:(u⋅Q)∣ψ⟩=0}=2 or 4.​
(13)
For a momentum eigenstate these are the null-momentum and zero-momentum cases; the expectation-matrix proof also covers states not assumed to have definite momentum. There are no massive states preserving a real charge in this unextended algebra.
If “linear combination” instead allows arbitrary complex coefficients, the unrestricted claim is false. In a massive fermionic Fock space with two lowering operators a1​,a2​ and a spin-half Clifford vacuum spectator, take
∣ψ⟩=2​∣00⟩⊗∣↑⟩+∣01⟩⊗∣↓⟩​.
(14)
Then a1​ψ=0. The three vectors a2​ψ, a2†​ψ and a1†​ψ are linearly independent, by their occupation numbers and spectator spin labels. The complex annihilator is therefore only one-dimensional. Such a lowering operator is not a Hermitian generator of preserved real supersymmetry. The boxed conclusion applies to the physical real-charge interpretation; it also assumes no domain-wall charge in N=1 supersymmetry.

In a massive four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry representation, the two normalized raising supercharges transform as a spin-half doublet. Start with a Clifford vacuum of superspin j=1/2. At occupation levels zero, one and two, the ordinary spins are
21​,21​⊗21​=0⊕1,21​.
(15)
The two-creation state has a spin-singlet oscillator factor because the creation operators anticommute. Thus the massive N=1 vector multiplet contains
1 ⊕ 21​ ⊕ 21​ ⊕ 0.​
(16)
There are four bosonic polarizations, 3+1, and four fermionic polarizations, 2+2, as required by boson-fermion degeneracy in a supermultiplet.
In the massless limit the helicity multiplicities become +1,−1, two copies each of +1/2,−1/2, and two zero-helicity states. They separate into the four irreducible helicity pairs
(1,21​),(21​,0),(0,−21​),(−21​,−1).​
(17)
With the lower-helicity convention for superhelicity, a pair (κ,κ+1/2) has labels κ=1/2,0,−1/2,−1, respectively. A midpoint convention shifts each label by 1/4; the explicit helicity pairs remove any naming ambiguity.
Pairing under the CPT theorem, the first and last pairs form a massless vector multiplet, and the middle two form a massless chiral multiplet. This is the massless limit of a massive N=1 vector multiplet. The longitudinal vector polarization supplies one of the chiral multiplet's two real scalar modes; the original spin-zero state supplies the other. Simply discarding the longitudinal polarization would lose a physical degree of freedom and give the wrong decomposition.

A scalar superfield transformation is a pullback under a supertranslation. Since that transformation mixes x and θ, an odd-coordinate derivative acquires an extra spacetime-derivative term under the coordinate Jacobian. It therefore does not transform as a spinor superfield merely by differentiating the scalar transformation law. This is partial derivatives and superfield covariance.
For explicit signs, split the Majorana spinor coordinates into θα,θˉα˙ and use left Grassmann derivatives. Choose the differential generators
Qα​=∂α​−iσαβ˙​m​θˉβ˙​∂m​,Qˉ​α˙​=−∂ˉα˙​+iθβσβα˙m​∂m​.
(18)
For example, {∂α​,Qˉ​β˙​​}=iσαβ˙​m​∂m​, which is nonzero. Commuting the even supersymmetry variation through ∂α​ consequently gives the unwanted translation term.
Replace the ordinary odd-coordinate derivatives by the supersymmetric covariant derivatives
Dα​=∂α​+iσαβ˙​m​θˉβ˙​∂m​,Dˉα˙​=−∂ˉα˙​−iθβσβα˙m​∂m​.​
(19)
The graded product rule cancels the mixed coordinate/derivative terms, giving {D,Q}={D,Qˉ​}={Dˉ,Q}={Dˉ,Qˉ​}=0. Thus Dα​V and Dˉα˙​V transform as spinor superfields. Their own mixed algebra is {Dα​,Dˉβ˙​​}=−2iσαβ˙​m​∂m​, with equal-chirality anticommutators zero. This covariant differentiation is also what makes a chiral superfield constraint Dˉα˙​Z=0 supersymmetry invariant. Negating every barred derivative is another convention, but changes the displayed mixed-algebra sign.

In the ordinary local two-derivative matter class, the general ungauged supersymmetric action for a chiral superfield is
S=∫d4xd4θK(Z,Zˉ)+[∫d4xd2θW(Z)+h.c.].​
(20)
The Kähler potential K is real, and the superpotential W is a holomorphic function. Adding f(Z)+fˉ​(Zˉ) to K changes the action only by a boundary term: full superspace integration of a chiral or antichiral function vanishes modulo that term. This is a Kähler transformation. A positive Kähler metric G=Kzzˉ​ is required for a nonsingular positive-norm kinetic term, not just for derivative counting.
To see the differential order, the bosonic component Lagrangian in mostly-plus signature contains
Lbos​=−G∂m​z∂mzˉ+GFFˉ+Wz​F+Wz​​Fˉ.
(21)
The auxiliary field equation is algebraic: F=−G−1Wz​​ on a purely bosonic configuration. It gives the scalar potential U=G−1∣Wz​∣2. The scalar equations have at most two spacetime derivatives, the fermion equations from the sigma-model kinetic term have one, and the remaining interaction terms do not raise this order. The fermionic corrections to the auxiliary equation are still algebraic.
For four-dimensional renormalizability, assign engineering dimensions [Z]=1, [d4θ]=2, and [d2θ]=1. Hence, up to a Kähler transformation and a constant field normalization, the restrictions are
K=ZˉZ,W=w0​+aZ+2m​Z2+3!g​Z3.​
(22)
A mixed higher-degree term in K or a term of degree above three in W introduces a negative-mass-dimension coupling. The constant w0​ has no local effect in global supersymmetry, as in the constant superpotential in global supersymmetry.
The boxed general-action statement uses the conventional two-derivative matter interpretation. It is not a classification of all derivative-dependent effective superfield actions by the order of their bosonic equations alone. For example, a scalar Lagrangian quartic in first derivatives still yields a second-order Euler–Lagrange equation, and supersymmetric derivative-dependent interactions can contain such terms. Their auxiliary and fermionic equations require separate checking. This distinction is explicit in the original construction of supersymmetric derivative-dependent scalar actions. Such actions are outside the ordinary K+W class used for the renormalizable answer here.

Spontaneous supersymmetry breaking preserves the action and conserved supercharges, but the vacuum fails to be invariant. This differs from explicit breaking, which changes the symmetry of the action itself. In global four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry, summing diagonal entries of the Super-Poincaré algebra writes the Hamiltonian as a sum of positive charge anticommutators. Its expectation is a sum of squared norms. Consequently a zero-energy vacuum is supersymmetric, whereas an existing vacuum with broken global supersymmetry has positive energy density. The energy zero is fixed by the algebra. Neither this positivity statement nor the particle-multiplet mass relations should be transferred without qualification to supergravity.
For supersymmetric quantum mechanics, let H={q,q†}/2, q2=0, and let fermion parity anticommute with both charges. On a positive-energy eigenspace, (q+q†)/2E​ is an odd involution: its square is one, it preserves energy, and it reverses parity. Thus every positive-energy bosonic state has a fermionic partner and conversely. Their contributions cancel in the Witten index
I(β)=Tr((−1)Fe−βH)=nB(0)​−nF(0)​.
(28)
For a discrete spectrum with a well-defined trace, this proves independence of β. Continuous changes of parameters also preserve the index if the trace stays well defined and no state escapes to infinity or crosses an unregulated continuum threshold. A nonzero index guarantees an unbroken supersymmetric ground state; a zero index is inconclusive. For example, de Rham supersymmetric quantum mechanics on a circle has a bosonic constant zero-form and a fermionic constant one-form, both normalizable zero modes, so it is unbroken with index zero.
A more explicit one-dimensional family illustrates how normalizability determines the answer. Define
A=dxd​+w(x),A†=−dxd​+w(x),H−​=21​A†A,H+​=21​AA†.
(29)
Positivity of these Hamiltonian operators shows that a zero mode must solve Aψ−​=0 or A†ψ+​=0. Thus the only candidates are
ψ−​(x)∝e−∫xw(s)ds,ψ+​(x)∝e+∫xw(s)ds.
(30)
For w(x)=x, the Gaussian minus-sector wavefunction is normalizable and its reciprocal is not, giving an unbroken model with I=1 when the minus sector is assigned even parity. For w(x)=x2+a, a>0, the exponent x3/3+ax tends to opposite infinities at the two ends, so neither candidate is normalizable. Both partner potentials [w2∓w′]/2 grow quartically; their spectra are discrete and their lowest energies cannot be zero. The lowest states are therefore paired at positive energy, I=0, and supersymmetry is spontaneously broken. This is supersymmetric factorization and zero-mode normalizability. In quantum mechanics the paired ground states illustrate the charge algebra; a relativistic massless-particle conclusion requires field-theoretic hypotheses.
In relativistic global field theory, Goldstone's theorem has a fermionic version. If a conserved supercurrent charge does not annihilate the vacuum, its Ward identity has a nonzero vacuum contact term. Fourier transforming, the divergence of the current correlator cannot tend to zero smoothly at zero momentum. Its spectral representation therefore contains a massless fermionic pole: the Goldstino, with spin one-half. This statement assumes a Poincaré-invariant vacuum and the usual local unitary field-theory conditions. It does not require a bosonic Goldstone particle for the fermionic symmetry. In supergravity the Goldstino can instead supply the longitudinal polarization of a massive gravitino.
For canonical chiral superfields Φi​, elimination of their auxiliary fields gives
Fi​=−Wi​​,U=∑i​∣Wi​∣2.
(31)
A supersymmetric vacuum solves every Wi​=0. At a stationary broken vacuum,
0=∂i​U=∑j​Wij​Wj​​.
(32)
The same matrix Wij​ is the chiral-fermion mass matrix, so the nonzero vector Wj​​ is a zero mode. This derives the Goldstino zero mode from vacuum stationarity. The transformation δψi​=2​ϵFi​+⋯ independently identifies that direction through its inhomogeneous shift.
For one canonical chiral field, W=fZ, f=0, has U=∣f∣2 everywhere, a massless fermion and a classically flat scalar direction. It is flat F-term breaking with a linear superpotential, rather than an isolated vacuum. In contrast, a nonconstant polynomial W′ of positive degree has a complex root and hence a supersymmetric vacuum; in particular a generic quadratic or cubic single-field superpotential cannot remove all such roots. This argument assumes canonical nonsingular kinetic terms and does not claim that every runaway or nonpolynomial model has a vacuum.
A renormalizable three-field O'Raifeartaigh model gives a less degenerate illustration:
W=fX+2h​XΦ12​+mΦ1​Φ2​,fm=0.
(33)
The auxiliary equations require W2​=mϕ1​=0, then W1​=hXϕ1​+mϕ2​=0, leaving WX​=f=0. They cannot vanish simultaneously. For real positive f,h,m with m2>fh, the potential obeys
U​=∣f+hϕ12​/2∣2+∣hXϕ1​+mϕ2​∣2+m2∣ϕ1​∣2≥f2+(m2−fh)∣ϕ1​∣2+4h2​∣ϕ1​∣4.​
(34)
Therefore its global tree-level minimum has ϕ1​=ϕ2​=0, arbitrary X, and energy f2. The massless ψX​ is the Goldstino, while X is a pseudomodulus. At X=0, the scalar mass-squared spectrum is 0,0,m2,m2,m2+fh,m2−fh, and the fermion masses are 0,m,m. This exhibits both the stability condition and the loss of ordinary supermultiplet mass degeneracy in a broken vacuum. Quantum corrections can lift the pseudomodulus and must be analysed rather than assumed away; the noncompact classical valley also prevents an unqualified finite-volume Witten index trace argument. The index, the vacuum equations and the Goldstino theorem consequently answer complementary questions.

Ordinary electromagnetism uses a one-form gauge potential, its gauge transformation and its curvature. On superspace the corresponding object is a superspace gauge connection, with both vector and spinor components. Write ∇A​=DA​+iAA​. Its graded commutator contains the flat-frame torsion plus supercurvature. The conventional constraints set the two undotted-spinor curvatures, the two dotted-spinor curvatures and the conventional mixed spinor curvature to zero; the mixed derivative algebra then determines the vector connection. The remaining independent gauge curvature is packaged into a chiral field-strength superfield. A real vector superfield V=V† is a prepotential solving these constraints, rather than four unrelated ordinary gauge potentials.
Use the left-derivative conventions of Question 5, with D2=DαDα​ and Dˉ2=Dˉα˙​Dˉα˙. An Abelian supergauge transformation has a chiral parameter Λ, and one consistent normalization is
V′=V+i(Λ−Λˉ),Φ′=e−2iqΛΦ,Dˉα˙​Λ=0.
(35)
The parameter is chiral so that the transformed matter is still a chiral superfield; its complex components remove more of the prepotential than an ordinary real gauge parameter. In Wess-Zumino gauge, one convenient expansion is
V=−θσmθˉAm​+iθ2θˉλˉ−iθˉ2θλ+21​θ2θˉ2D.
(36)
Its components are the gauge potential Am​, the gaugino λ and the real auxiliary field D. Wess-Zumino gauge removes the extra scalar and spinor components of a general real superfield. Supersymmetry transformations usually leave that gauge slice and require a compensating supergauge transformation. For a residual chiral parameter with real lowest component a(x), the expansion gives Am′​=Am​+2∂m​a and ϕ′=e−2iqaϕ; thus α=2a is the ordinary Maxwell gauge parameter.
The Abelian field strength is the Abelian field-strength chiral projection
Wα​=−41​Dˉ2Dα​V.​
(37)
Three barred derivatives vanish because there are only two anticommuting dotted components, so Dˉβ˙​​Wα​=0. Gauge invariance follows directly: Dα​Λˉ=0 by antichirality, while the mixed derivative algebra gives Dˉ2Dα​Λ=0 for chiral Λ. In chiral coordinates its component structure is
Wα​=−iλα​+θα​D−i(σmnθ)α​Fmn​+θ2(σm∂m​λˉ)α​,Fmn​=∂m​An​−∂n​Am​,
(38)
where σmn=(σmσˉn−σnσˉm)/4 fixes the tensor normalization. Overall gaugino phase choices do not change the gauge-invariant field strength or its Bianchi constraint.
Reality of V and the derivative algebra imply the superspace Yang-Mills Bianchi identity
DαWα​=Dˉα˙​Wˉα˙.​
(39)
Indeed, the two sides are the same differential operator on V, because DαDˉ2Dα​=Dˉα˙​D2Dˉα˙. This is the superspace counterpart of dF=0, and its components contain ∂[m​Fnp]​=0. It is a defining curvature constraint, not the Maxwell equations. Varying the pure gauge action additionally sets the common real superfield DαWα​ to zero; matter supplies a current instead.
The Supersymmetric Yang-Mills action and minimal matter coupling are
S=4g21​∫d4xd2θWαWα​+h.c.+∫d4xd4θΦˉe2qVΦ+[∫d4xd2θW(Φ)+h.c.].
(40)
The chiral phase factors cancel in Φˉe2qVΦ. The component matter derivative is (∂m​+iqAm​)ϕ, which transforms covariantly under the residual gauge transformation just derived. The same superspace term generates the associated matter–gaugino Yukawa coupling and qD∣ϕ∣2 auxiliary coupling, so minimal coupling is not only replacing an ordinary derivative in the scalar action. The gauge action contains −Fmn​Fmn/(4g2), the gaugino kinetic term and D2/(2g2). Rescaling the vector multiplet to canonical kinetic normalization gives the physical coupling qg. The holomorphic superpotential must be gauge invariant; a single field of nonzero continuous Abelian charge admits no nonconstant polynomial superpotential without additional fields. For several fields, invariance requires ∑i​qi​Φi​Wi​=0. A Fayet–Iliopoulos term is additionally possible for an Abelian factor, shifting the algebraic D equation.
For a non-Abelian gauge group, V and Λ are Lie-algebra-valued, and multiplication cannot be reordered. For matter in a specified representation, set
Φ′=e−2iΛΦ,e2V′=e−2iΛ†e2Ve2iΛ.​
(41)
The same matter quadratic form remains invariant. Its curvature is
Wα​=−81​Dˉ2(e−2VDα​e2V),Wα′​=e−2iΛWα​e2iΛ.​
(42)
At first order in an Abelian V, this reduces to −Dˉ2Dα​V/4, fixing the factor 1/8. In the chiral gauge frame, ∇ˉ=Dˉ and ∇α​=e−2VDα​e2V as an operator. The gauge-covariant Bianchi identity is ∇αWα​=∇ˉα˙​Wˉα˙, where Wˉ=e−2VWˉe2V is the conjugate curvature expressed in that same frame. It follows from the graded Jacobi identity of the covariant derivatives. Taking a trace of WαWα​ makes the chiral gauge action invariant. Its bosonic curvature becomes the ordinary Yang-Mills curvature, and the gaugino transforms in the Adjoint representation. A constant Fayet–Iliopoulos coefficient cannot be inserted for a simple non-Abelian group, since it would need an invariant linear functional annihilating every Lie bracket. These constructions connect the constrained superspace connection, its prepotential and its component gauge multiplet.

A canonical Wess–Zumino model with a cubic superpotential already supplies a microscopic membrane-like object. Choose positive real μ,g and
W(Φ)=gμ2​Φ−3g​Φ3,U(ϕ)=​gμ2​−gϕ2​2.

T=∫dz(∣ϕ′∣2+∣W′∣2)=∫dz∣ϕ′−eiαW′∣2+2Re(e−iαΔW).

Choosing α=argΔW gives T≥2∣ΔW∣. The real increasing solution saturates the bound:

The wall breaks transverse translations: changing z0​ gives a normalizable scalar zero mode proportional to ϕ′(z). It also preserves only half the four real supercharges. In the chiral-fermion transformation, F=−W′=−ϕ′ and the derivative term combine into a rank-half condition on the constant supersymmetry parameter, for example ϵ=iσ3ϵˉ in a compatible Weyl convention. The orthogonal two real parameters are broken and generate localized fermion zero modes. One such profile satisfies (∂z​+2gϕ)f=0, giving f∝sech2(μ(z−z0​)). The scalar and a three-dimensional Majorana fermion therefore form the low-energy wall multiplet. The domain-wall charge in N=1 supersymmetry is a boundary/tensor charge contributing to the extended algebra; the preserved combinations saturate T=2∣ΔW∣. This avoids falsely applying the unextended particle-algebra argument of Question 3 to a wall with nonzero tension.
Promote the translation collective coordinate to a slowly varying worldvolume field Y(ξ0,ξ1,ξ2). At wavelengths long compared with the wall thickness, its leading relativistic geometric action is the Dirac membrane action
Smem​=−T∫d3ξ−dethij​​,hij​=∂i​Xm∂j​Xnηmn​.​
(46)
It is invariant under worldvolume reparametrizations and the target Poincaré group. In static gauge Xi=ξi, X3=Y, the matrix determinant lemma gives
Smem​=−T∫d3ξ1+∂i​Y∂iY​=−T∫d3ξ(1+21​∂i​Y∂iY+⋯).
(47)
Directly substituting the leading collective-coordinate profile into the scalar kinetic term gives the same coefficient, since ∫dz(ϕ′)2=T/2. Target Lorentz symmetry fixes the leading first-derivative nonlinear completion to the invariant area; finite-thickness physics can add higher-curvature terms. The area action is therefore a long-wavelength effective field theory, not an exact replacement for the microscopic wall at arbitrary momenta. Its embedding equation is ∂i​(−h​hij∂j​Xm)=0.
For the four-dimensional supermembrane, extend the embedding to (Xm(ξ),θα(ξ)) with a four-real-component Majorana spinor. Pull back the invariant coframe of Question 7:
Πim​=∂i​Xm+iθˉγm∂i​θ,hij​=Πim​Πjn​ηmn​.
(48)
Replacing ∂X by Π makes the area term invariant under rigid target supersymmetry, but does not yet give the appropriate fermionic gauge symmetry. Add a Wess-Zumino brane coupling to a superspace three-form potential C3​. In one orientation and matching normalization,
S=−T∫d3ξ−h​+T∫Σ3​​Z∗C3​,G4​=dC3​=2i​Πm∧Πn∧dθˉγmn​dθ.
(49)
The supermembrane closed four-form uses the distinct four-dimensional Fierz identity (Cγmn​)(αβ​(Cγn)γδ)​=0. Differentiating G4​ reduces its coefficient to this symmetric tensor, so dG4​=0. A local potential therefore exists. Since the curvature is supertranslation invariant, the potential can vary by an exact form; its pullback integral is invariant modulo boundary terms. This is why the three-form H3​ of Question 7 is not itself the membrane field strength: integrating a membrane potential requires three-form degree and hence four-form curvature.
The required additional local fermionic gauge invariance is kappa symmetry. Define the worldvolume gamma matrices γi​=Πim​γm​ and
Γκ​=3!−h​ϵijk​γijk​,δκ​θ=(1+Γκ​)κ(ξ),δκ​Xm=−iθˉγmδκ​θ.​
(50)
With mostly-plus target signature, a local orthonormal worldvolume frame gives Γκ​=γ0​γ1​γ2​, so Γκ2​=1 and trΓκ​=0. Thus (1±Γκ​)/2 are rank-two kappa symmetry projectors. The embedding variation gives δΠim​=2iδθˉγm∂i​θ. The area variation is consequently proportional to −2iT−h​δθˉγi∂i​θ. Contracting the displayed G4​ with the fermionic variation and using ϵijkγij​=2−h​Γκ​γk gives the opposite Wess-Zumino contribution with an extra Γκ​. Their sum is proportional to
δθˉ(1−Γκ​)γi∂i​θ.
(51)
For the boxed kappa variation, δθˉ=κˉ(1+Γκ​), so it vanishes by (1+Γκ​)(1−Γκ​)=0. Reversing membrane orientation reverses the Wess-Zumino sign and the matching projector. The cancellation fixes the relative charge magnitude to the tension; rigid supersymmetry alone would not fix it.
Worldvolume reparametrizations remove three of the four embedding scalars, leaving one transverse mode. Kappa symmetry removes half the four real embedding spinor components, leaving a two-component three-dimensional Majorana field, whose first-order equation has one on-shell fermionic polarization. The physical worldvolume modes are one scalar and one Majorana fermion, matching the wall zero modes. Without kappa symmetry the fermionic degrees would be doubled. A planar configuration preserves the target supersymmetries that can be compensated by the kappa projector, reproducing the wall's half-supersymmetry condition. This is the four-dimensional effective domain-wall interpretation of a supermembrane, rather than the eleven-dimensional M2-brane particle-content problem.
