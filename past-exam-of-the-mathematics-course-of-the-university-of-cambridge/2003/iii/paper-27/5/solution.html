These matrices represent the complex Grassmannian as orthogonal projections, a compact manifold of complex dimension k(n−k). The constants ci​ are real. Let A∗=−A and use the given tangent directions [A,P]. Cyclicity of the matrix trace gives
dfP​([A,P])=tr(C[A,P])=tr([P,C]A).
(17)
The commutator B=[P,C] is skew-Hermitian. If this derivative vanishes for every skew-Hermitian A, choose A=B to obtain
0=tr(B2)=−tr(B∗B),
(18)
so B=0. Conversely [P,C]=0 makes every tangent derivative zero. Because the diagonal entries of C are distinct, a commuting matrix P is diagonal. Its idempotent entries are zero or one. Thus the critical projections are exactly
PI​=diag(11∈I​,…,1n∈I​),∣I∣=k,​
(19)
with (kn​) critical points and values f(PI​)=∑i∈I​ci​.
To compute the Hessian matrix, write E=span{ei​:i∈I} and E⊥=span{ej​:j∈/I}. A neighboring plane is the graph of a complex linear map Z:E→E⊥. With W=(ZI​), its orthogonal projection is P(Z)=W(I+Z∗Z)−1W∗. Writing CE​,CE⊥​ for the diagonal blocks, we get
f(P(Z))=tr(CE​(I+Z∗Z)−1)+tr(CE⊥​Z(I+Z∗Z)−1Z∗).
(20)
Expanding (I+Z∗Z)−1=I−Z∗Z+O(∥Z∥4) yields
f(P(Z))=f(PI​)+∑i∈I, j∈/I​(cj​−ci​)∣zji​∣2+O(∥Z∥4).
(21)
Each complex coordinate has two real components, each with Hessian coefficient 2(cj​−ci​). Every coefficient is nonzero, proving that f is a Morse function. Its negative directions correspond to j<i. For I={i1​<⋯<ik​} there are ia​−a complementary indices below ia​, giving
ind(PI​)=2#{(i,j):i∈I, j∈/I, j<i}=2a=1∑k​(ia​−a).​
(22)
This is the diagonal trace Morse function on a complex Grassmannian. Its critical values need not be distinct; ties between sums do not make its critical points degenerate.
All Morse indices are even. Choose a Morse-Smale gradient flow and form its integral Morse chain complex. The odd-degree chain groups vanish, so every differential vanishes. Equivalently the Morse handle-attachment theorem supplies only even-dimensional cells. The integral homology of complex Grassmannians is therefore
H2r​(Gr(k,n);Z)=ZNr​,H2r+1​(Gr(k,n);Z)=0,​
(23)
where Nr​ counts the subsets with ∑a​(ia​−a)=r, and 0≤r≤k(n−k). In particular there is no torsion. The nondecreasing sequence ia​−a lies between zero and n−k, so reversing it identifies these subsets with partitions of an integer r fitting in a k by (n−k) rectangle. The Poincaré polynomial is explicitly
∑∣I∣=k​t2∑a​(ia​−a).
(24)
For the permitted example Gr(2,4), the subsets 12,13,14,23,24,34 have indices 0,2,4,4,6,8. Thus
H0​=H2​=H6​=H8​=Z,H4​=Z2,Hodd​=0.​
(25)
The general calculation above applies throughout 2≤k≤n−2; it does not reduce to any of the excluded rank-one cases.
