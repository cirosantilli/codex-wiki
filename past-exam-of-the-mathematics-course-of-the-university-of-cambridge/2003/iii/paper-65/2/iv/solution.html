These profiles illustrate different boundary-controlled similarity classes of the Keplerian viscous diffusion equation. The a=1 solution is compatible with a zero-torque inner boundary condition and conserved finite angular momentum, and can describe a late-time spreading accretion disk after the detailed initial profile has been forgotten. Its mass diverges as t↓0, so the formal zero-radius origin is not a finite-mass initial disk. The a=5/4 solution has finite initial mass concentrated at the origin and initially zero disk angular momentum, but requires the specified inner torque for t>0; that torque's t−3/4 singularity is time-integrable. It may describe a spreading nonaccreting disk for compatible torque-driven boundary conditions, but cannot be an isolated angular-momentum-conserving disk's generic asymptotic state. Similarity scaling suggests their possible asymptotic roles; it does not establish convergence for arbitrary initial and boundary data.
