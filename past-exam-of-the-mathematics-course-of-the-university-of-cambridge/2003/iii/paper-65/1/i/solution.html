Write horizontal components with a subscript h. The steady ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation gives ∇×(u×B)=0. Since ∂z​=0, its horizontal components imply
∂x​(u×B)z​=∂y​(u×B)z​=0.
(1)
Thus (u×B)z​=C is a spatial constant. A vanishing axial electric field, Ez​=−C=0, is needed to infer horizontal alignment. It is satisfied, for example, if the constant electric field vanishes at a boundary or at infinity. With this additional condition and Bh​=0, define the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading k by ρuh​=kBh​, and put U=uz​−kBz​/ρ. Then
u=ρkB​+Uez​​.
(2)
The steady continuity equation and the solenoidal magnetic-field constraint give
0=∇⋅(ρu)=∇⋅(kB+ρUez​)=B⋅∇k.
(3)
Also u×B=Uez​×B. Using the vector identity for the curl of a cross product, together with ∂z​B=0 and ∇⋅B=0, its curl is ez​B⋅∇U. Hence
B⋅∇k=B⋅∇U=0​.
(4)
These are steady planar ideal-MHD field-line invariants: both quantities are constant along a magnetic field line.
The zero-electric-field condition does not follow from the printed stationarity assumptions alone. Constant ρ,p,s, Φ=0, u=u0​ex​ and B=B0​ey​, with u0​B0​=0, satisfy every displayed ideal magnetohydrodynamics equation but have perpendicular horizontal velocity and magnetic field. Their C=u0​B0​ is nonzero, so no k,U give the stated decomposition. The field-line decomposition is valid on the Ez​=0 branch; as an unrestricted implication, the first claim has this counterexample. The subsequent calculations use that branch.
