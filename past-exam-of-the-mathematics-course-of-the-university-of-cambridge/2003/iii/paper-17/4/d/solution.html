True for a smooth complex projective surface, the usual manifold convention in this question. Choose an ample divisor H. The auxiliary pencil-existence theorem states that, for all sufficiently large m, a general two-dimensional space of sections of OX​(mH) defines a Lefschetz pencil: its base points are transverse and finite, its general fibre is a smooth connected curve, and each singular fibre has exactly one ordinary node, with distinct critical values. High powers separate the required jets, so a generic pencil has precisely these singularities.
Choose such an m divisible by four, and write D=mH. The adjunction formula for a smooth connected curve of class D on a smooth projective surface is 2g−2=D⋅(D+KX​). Consequently
g=1+2m2H2+mKX​⋅H​.
(19)
Writing m=4ℓ gives g=1+8ℓ2H2+2ℓKX​⋅H, an odd integer. Thus a sufficiently positive four-divisible polarization gives an odd-genus Lefschetz pencil. The surface is taken to be smooth; a singular projective variety need not support a Lefschetz pencil in this smooth-manifold sense.
