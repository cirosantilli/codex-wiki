Define
F:B4(1)⟶CP2∖{z3​=0},F(w1​,w2​)=[w1​:w2​:1−∣w∣2​].
(27)
Every projective point with z3​=0 has a unique unit representative whose third coordinate is positive real, so F is a diffeomorphism. More explicitly, in the affine coordinate u=(z1​/z3​,z2​/z3​) its inverse is w=u/1+∣u∣2​. Lift F to the unit sphere by F(w)=(w,1−∣w∣2​). The last coordinate is real, so its contribution dx3​∧dy3​ pulls back to zero. The reduction identity therefore yields
F∗Ω=F∗ωst​=ωst​∣C2​.​
(28)
This proves the symplectic ball chart in complex projective space directly, including the precise unit radius.
For the final unheaded request, embed the two disjoint balls in this chart. Fix arbitrary 0<ρi​<ri​, and choose intermediate radii ρi​<ρi′​<ri​. On the images of the balls of radius ρi′​, transport the standard complex structure by the given symplectic embeddings. It is compatible with Ω. Their smaller closed neighborhoods are compact and disjoint. Choose a global Riemannian metric agreeing there with the associated compatible metrics, using a partition of unity outside these neighborhoods, and apply the metric construction. The resulting global compatible almost complex structure J agrees with the transported standard structure on each ball of radius ρi​ and on a slightly larger neighborhood. This extends metrics, rather than averaging almost complex structures.
Let p1​,p2​ be the centres. They are distinct. The degree-one J-holomorphic curve supplied in the question has total area π, because its homology class is the projective line class. We use the sharp Euclidean version of the Monotonicity theorem for a J-holomorphic curve: a nonconstant holomorphic curve through the centre of a standard complex ball, with no boundary in the ball and counted with its parametrization multiplicities, has area in the radius-ρ ball at least πρ2. This includes branched points: their density is a positive integer m, and the stronger lower bound is mπρ2. One way to see the constant is that complex curves are calibrated minimal surfaces; their stationary area ratio area(C∩B(ρ))/ρ2 is nondecreasing and its limit at the centre is mπ.
The closed degree-one curve cannot stay entirely inside either chart ball: there the form is exact, so Stokes theorem would give zero total area, contradicting its positive area. Thus the branch through each centre crosses the surrounding spheres, and the stated monotonicity bound applies to each restriction. The two ball images are disjoint, so their area contributions add without overlap. It follows that
πρ12​+πρ22​≤∫C​Ω=π.
(29)
Letting both smaller radii increase to their original radii gives
r12​+r22​≤1.​
(30)
This two-ball packing obstruction in the projective plane is sharper than volume alone: a volume comparison would only give r14​+r24​≤1.
