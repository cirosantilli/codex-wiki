A cyclic code of length n over Fq​ is a linear code invariant under (c0​,…,cn−1​)↦(cn−1​,c0​,…,cn−2​). Associate the word with c(T)=∑i=0n−1​ci​Ti. The shift is multiplication by T modulo Tn−1, so shift-invariant linear subspaces are exactly the ideals of Fq​[T]/(Tn−1). The inverse image of such an ideal in Fq​[T] is principal and contains Tn−1; hence it is generated by a unique monic divisor g of Tn−1. The code has basis g,Tg,…,Tn−degg−1g, and dimension n−degg.
The defining zeros of a cyclic code are the roots of its generator g in a splitting field. Since g∣Tn−1, each is a root of unity over a finite field. Every codeword vanishes at each such root. If the characteristic divides n, root multiplicities must additionally be retained; in the Hamming construction below the modulus has no repeated roots.
The unrestricted ternary cyclic-equivalence assertion is false. At s=2 it would give a cyclic [4,2,3]3​ code. But
T4−1=(T−1)(T+1)(T2+1)
(18)
over F3​, with the quadratic irreducible. A dimension-two cyclic code needs a degree-two generator, so its only possibilities are T2−1 and T2+1. Each is a weight-two codeword. Therefore neither has minimum distance three, and no code equivalent to the ternary [4,2,3] Hamming code can be cyclic. A valid general version of the asserted construction assumes odd s. The usual distance-three Hamming parameters also require s≥2; when s=1 the code is the length-one zero code.
Here is the construction with its precise qualification. A ternary Hamming code has a parity-check matrix whose columns represent each one-dimensional subspace of F3s​ once. There are n=(3s−1)/2 such subspaces. Its rank is s, because the coordinate lines are included. Its minimum distance is at least three, because no column is zero and no two are proportional. For s≥2, two independent vectors and their sum determine three distinct columns, and, after adjusting their nonzero representative scalars, give a weight-three linear relation. Thus its parameters are [n,n−s,3]3​. Permuting columns and changing their nonzero representatives produces a monomial equivalence of linear codes.
Take odd s≥3 and choose α∈F3s×​ of order n. Since n is odd, −1 is not in the cyclic subgroup generated by α. Hence 1,α,…,αn−1 lie in distinct one-dimensional F3​-subspaces: a proportional pair would have ratio 1 or −1, neither possible for two distinct powers in this list. By their number they represent all projective points. Consequently
C={c∈F3n​:∑i=0n−1​ci​αi=0}
(19)
is monomially equivalent to the ternary Hamming code. Since αn=1, evaluation at α descends to F3​[T]/(Tn−1) and its kernel is an ideal, so C is cyclic.
The degree of α over F3​ is s. If it were d<s, its generated field would have 3d elements and its element order would give
n≤3d−1≤3s−1−1<23s−1​=n,
(20)
a contradiction. Its minimal polynomial of an algebraic element therefore has degree s. Applying Frobenius to a polynomial relation shows that α,α3,…,α3s−1 are its roots; they are distinct by the same field-degree argument. Their product polynomial is the monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element, and it divides Tn−1. Thus
g(T)=j=0∏s−1​(T−α3j),Z(C)={α3j:0≤j<s}.​
(21)
This identifies all the defining zeros. For s=3, n=13, the defining exponents are 1,3,9 modulo 13.
There is also a uniform construction for all s≥2, explaining the missing qualification. Let β generate F3s×​, which has order 2n. The powers 1,β,…,βn−1 represent all projective points, since proportional powers differ by exponent 0 or n modulo 2n. But βn=−1, so the kernel of c↦c(β) is an ideal modulo Tn+1, a negacyclic code. Multiplication by T gives (−cn−1​,c0​,…,cn−2​), preserving its kernel. This code is always equivalent to the ternary Hamming code. If s is odd, n is odd too, and the substitution T↦−T, or coordinate scaling ci​↦(−1)ici​, changes this negacyclic construction into the cyclic one. In fact α=−β then has order n. These explicit constructions prove the qualified assertion rather than relying on a theorem name.
For decoding, use the printed 13×3 matrix H with received words as row vectors; the conventional 3×13 parity-check matrix is HT. The received word's syndrome is
s=rH=(2,0,2)in F33​.
(22)
A single-symbol error of value a at position i has syndrome ahi​, where hi​ is row i of the printed matrix. Here
(2,0,2)=2(1,0,1)=2h6​.
(23)
Therefore subtract 2 at the sixth coordinate, changing its received value 1 to 2 modulo three. The decoded codeword is
(2,1,2,0,1,2,0,0,2,1,1,2,0).​
(24)
Its syndrome is zero. The thirteen projective rows and the two nonzero scalars give all 26 nonzero syndromes exactly once, so this is the unique codeword at distance one. It is the transmitted word under the usual assumption of at most one symbol error. If positions 1,2,5 are designated check positions, their independent unit rows make the other ten symbols systematic data, namely (2,0,2,0,0,2,1,1,2,0).
