A root of unity over a finite field is an element a∈Fq​ satisfying an=1, with n≥1; the algebraic closure is essential because all roots need not lie in the base field. Write p=charFq​ and n=pam, where p∤m. In characteristic p,
Tn−1=(Tm−1)pa.
(13)
The latter polynomial has m distinct roots, since the derivative mTm−1 is nonzero at every root. Those roots are closed under multiplication and inversion and contain 1, so E(n,q) is a finite multiplicative subgroup of a field. The proof in the preceding solution makes it a cyclic group. In particular
∣E(n,q)∣=m,∣E(n,q)∣=n if gcd(n,q)=1.​
(14)
This also explains why the coprimality qualification is needed.
The multiplicative group of a finite field Fqs×​ is cyclic of order qs−1. It contains the entire group of mth roots precisely when m∣qs−1. For necessity, a generator of E(n,q) has order m and Lagrange's theorem applies. For sufficiency, a cyclic group whose order is divisible by m contains m roots of Tm−1; since there are exactly m roots in the algebraic closure, these are all of them. Hence the least positive extension degree is
s=ordm​(q)(m>1),s=1(m=1).​
(15)
Here ordm​(q) is the multiplicative order of the residue class of q modulo m. If n is coprime to q, replace m by n. Existence of this order follows because q is a unit modulo m.
A primitive nth root of unity over a finite field has multiplicative order exactly n. If p∣n, no such element exists, since all roots have order dividing m<n. If gcd(n,q)=1, choose a generator ζ of E(n,q). The element ζj has order n/gcd(n,j), so it is primitive precisely when gcd(n,j)=1. Therefore there are φ(n) primitive roots, with φ the Euler totient function.
If ω is primitive of order n, then ω∈Fqℓ​ exactly when ωqℓ=ω, equivalently n∣qℓ−1. The implication from that equality to membership follows because the roots of Tqℓ−T form Fqℓ​, as proved in the preceding solution. Consequently
min{ℓ≥1:ω∈Fqℓ​}=ordn​(q)​
(16)
for n>1, and the degree is one for n=1. This is also the length of the finite-field Frobenius automorphism orbit of ω.
Using the same (1,u) basis of F9​ as before, the fourth roots of unity are
E(4,9)={1,−1,u,−u}={(1,0),(2,0),(0,1),(0,2)}.​
(17)
There are four, they are already in F9​, and the primitive fourth roots are the two vectors (0,1) and (0,2).
