Put q=b/a>1. Each successive crossing time Nj​, j≥1, is a stopping time, since in discrete time the event that a threshold is first met after the previous stopping time can be written as a finite union of events known at the candidate time. Use inf∅=∞, with all later crossing times then infinite as well.
For the supermartingale assertion, build a process by finitely many switches. Begin with the constant supermartingale one. At N1​ switch to Xn​/a; the new value at that time is at most one. At N2​ switch to the constant q; the old value there is XN2​​/a≥q. At N3​ switch to qXn​/a, whose value is at most q, and at N4​ switch to the constant q2, whose value is at most the previous process. More generally, the switches at N2ℓ−1​ and N2ℓ​ are respectively from cash qℓ−1 to qℓ−1Xn​/a and from that stock value to cash qℓ. Every switch is downward, including possible overshoots of either threshold.
Each candidate continuation is a supermartingale, being either constant or a fixed positive multiple of X. Part (a) therefore proves by induction that the process M(j) obtained after the first j switches is a supermartingale. It agrees with Y through Nj​. For fixed j,n, its values are bounded in absolute value by a deterministic constant depending on j times 1+Xn​, which also verifies the required integrability rather than assuming it for an infinite sequence of pastings.
Stopping a discrete supermartingale preserves the property. Indeed its stopped increment is 1{Nj​>n}​(Mn+1(j)​−Mn(j)​), with an Fn​-measurable indicator; integrability at any fixed time follows from a finite sum of the absolute values at earlier times. Hence
Yn∧Nj​​=Mn∧Nj​(j)​ is a nonnegative supermartingale for j≥1.​
(36)
This is the multiplicative upcrossing supermartingale construction. The printed j=0 case uses N0​=−1, while Y was only defined from time zero. With the natural pre-start convention Y−1​=1, it is the constant process one and the assertion also holds for j=0. Without such an extension, that single displayed stopped process is undefined.
For the probability bound, the actual initial value is
Y0​=min(X0​/a,1),
(37)
because N1​=0 exactly when X0​≤a. For an integer k≥1, stop after the kth completed upcrossing, at N2k​. The nonnegative stopped supermartingale satisfies
qkP(N2k​≤n)≤EYn∧N2k​​≤EY0​,
(38)
since YN2k​​=qk on the event in the left side. Letting n→∞, and identifying {U≥k}={N2k​<∞}, proves the Dubins upcrossing inequality:
P(U≥k)≤(ba​)kEmin(X0​/a,1),k=1,2,….​
(39)
Neither finiteness of N2k​ nor uniform integrability is needed. The bound is for positive integers k; extending it to k=0 would generally be false, because its left side would be one while Emin(X0​/a,1) can be less than one.
