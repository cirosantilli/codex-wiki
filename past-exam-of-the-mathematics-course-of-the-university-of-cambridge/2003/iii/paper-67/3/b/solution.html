Use the corrected same-node method identified above, with δ=1/M, interior indices 1,…,M−1 and zero endpoint values. Its spatial matrix Lδ​ is real symmetric. Summation by parts gives, for a vector extended by zero at the endpoints,
−vTLδ​v=δ−2∑j=0M−1​aj+1/2​(vj+1​−vj​)2.
(23)
Hence −Lδ​ is positive definite, and ∑j​(vj+1​−vj​)2≤4∑j​vj2​ bounds its eigenvalues by 4a+​/δ2. The Forward Euler method has amplification matrix I+kLδ​. Its eigenvalues lie in [1−4μa+​,1], so their moduli are at most one when
0<μ≤2a+​1​.​
(24)
Symmetry makes this a contraction in the mesh-weighted discrete L2 norm, uniformly over all grids and all coefficient samples satisfying the bounds. There is also a maximum norm proof: the update weights are μam−1/2​, 1−μ(am−1/2​+am+1/2​), and μam+1/2​. They are nonnegative and sum to one, giving a contraction after the boundary values are included.
This mesh-independent bound is sharp. For a≡a+​, the spatial eigenvectors are sin(jπm/M) and their amplification factors are
1−4μa+​sin22Mjπ​,j=1,…,M−1.
(25)
If μ>1/(2a+​), sufficiently fine grids have a highest-frequency factor below −1, giving exponential growth in the step number.
On one fixed grid, rather than uniformly under refinement, the sharp universal stability of a numerical method bound in the discrete L2 norm is slightly larger:
μ≤2a+​cos2(π/(2M))1​.
(26)
Indeed the quadratic form above is bounded by a+​ times the constant-coefficient difference form, whose largest eigenvalue is 4cos2(π/(2M))/δ2; equality is realized by the constant coefficient. Its limit as M→∞ is the boxed bound. No such fixed-grid matrix conclusion repairs the ill-defined shifted-boundary update in the literal printed formula.
