The circulant matrix is singular, so the meaning of convergence needs care. If C is the cyclic shift, A=C+C−1−2I has Fourier eigenvalues −4sin2(πj/n). Its null space consists of constants. Therefore Ax=b is solvable exactly when ∑j​bj​=0, and any solution is determined only up to a constant. Neither iteration can converge to a unique solution for arbitrary right-hand sides.
For a compatible b, fix one solution x∗​ and examine the error. The iteration matrix of the Jacobi method is (C+C−1)/2, with eigenvalues cos(2πj/n). The constant mode has eigenvalue one. For odd n all other eigenvalues have modulus less than one, so the iterates converge to x∗​+c1, with the constant selected by the initial mean. For even n, the alternating vector has eigenvalue −1, producing an undamped oscillation. With b=0 and alternating initial vector this is an explicit nonconvergent example. For even n convergence occurs only when that error component vanishes. When ∑bj​=0, the mean instead drifts by −(∑bj​)/(2n) at each step, so Jacobi cannot converge.
For Gauss-Seidel iteration, put K=−A=D+L+U, so K is positive semidefinite with diagonal two. The homogeneous error iteration is e+=Be, B=−(D+L)−1U. Updating a coordinate exactly minimizes E(e)=eTKe/2 in that coordinate; completing the square gives an energy decrement ∣Δej​∣2. Summing within a sweep gives
E(e)−E(Be)=∑j=1n​∣Δej​∣2≥0.
(35)
The same identity holds for the Hermitian energy of complex vectors. If Be=λe and e is nonconstant, its energy is positive, so ∣λ∣≤1. Equality forces every update to be zero, whence Ke=0 and e is constant, a contradiction. Thus all nonconstant eigenmodes have modulus strictly below one.
The eigenvalue one has only the constant eigenspace. To exclude a generalized eigenvector at one, observe that
w=(D+L)T1=(0,1,…,1,2)T,wTB=wT,wT1=n.
(36)
If (B−I)v=1, multiplication by wT would give 0=n. Hence the constant eigenvalue is algebraically simple, and
Br⟶1wT/n.
(37)
For compatible b, Gauss-Seidel therefore converges from every start to some solution, for either parity of n. Its limit differs from x∗​ by 1wT(x(0)−x∗​)/n. For an incompatible b, the affine update gives wTx(r+1)−wTx(r)=−∑j​bj​, so the iterates again cannot converge.
Thus semiconvergence of cyclic Poisson iterations gives the precise answer:
∑bj​=0: neither iteration converges;∑bj​=0: Gauss-Seidel converges to a solution for all n;Jacobi converges from every start iff n is odd.​​
(38)
Both have spectral radius one on the unrestricted space, so neither meets the strict stationary-iteration criterion for a unique limit independent of the starting constant. A spectral radius of one alone is not a proof that Gauss-Seidel's compatible iterates fail to converge. A normalization or projection removing the constant mode makes this distinction explicit.
