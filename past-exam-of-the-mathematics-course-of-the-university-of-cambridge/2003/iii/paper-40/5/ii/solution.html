Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2003 / iii / Paper 40 / 5 / ii / Solution

 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2003 iii Paper 40 5 ii
OurBigBook.com Words: 217
Distinguish the disease alleles D (normal) and d (recessive disease) from the marker alleles A,a. The affected mother and two affected children are dd. The unaffected father must be Dd, since he has affected children; the unaffected child is Dd, since the mother always transmits d. The mother is AA at the marker, so the marker genotypes identify the father's transmissions to the three children as a,A,A. Their disease transmissions from the father are d,d,D.
The father's unphased double heterozygosity has two possible pairs of haplotypes:
s1​:AD/ad,s2​:Ad/aD.
(25)
Under s1​, the three paternal transmissions (ad,Ad,AD) are respectively nonrecombinant, recombinant and nonrecombinant. Under s2​, they are recombinant, nonrecombinant and recombinant. Each particular gamete has probability (1−θ)/2 or θ/2, giving the conditional pedigree likelihoods
L1​(θ)=8θ(1−θ)2​,L2​(θ)=8θ2(1−θ)​.
(26)
Assuming linkage equilibrium among founder haplotypes, the two phases have equal prior weights conditional on the observed paternal genotype. Phase averaging in a linkage likelihood therefore gives
L(θ)=21​L1​(θ)+21​L2​(θ)=16θ(1−θ)​,0≤θ≤21​.​
(27)
Any factors for the parental genotypes omitted by this conditioning are constant in θ. The derivative is (1−2θ)/16, so the maximum likelihood estimate is θ=1/2 and there is no evidence for linkage: the maximum LOD score is zero. Choosing the best phase after observing the offspring would give a different and inappropriate calculation; the unknown phase must be averaged. The linkage-equilibrium assumption is modified explicitly in part (v).
