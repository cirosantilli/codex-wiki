Genetic linkage analysis asks whether a genetic marker and a disease genetic locus cosegregate within families more often than would be expected under independent assortment. It uses pedigrees and Mendelian segregation to estimate the recombination fraction θ, with evidence for linkage arising when the data favor θ<1/2. Even when founders are in linkage equilibrium, linked loci can show familial cosegregation.
Genetic association analysis instead asks whether observed allele or genotype frequencies differ with disease status in a sampled population, often comparing cases with controls. It can detect a causal variant or a correlated genetic marker through linkage disequilibrium; population structure and selection can also create association. Linkage is about familial transmission; association is about population dependence. Neither an association alone nor a low family recombination estimate establishes that the marker itself causes disease.
