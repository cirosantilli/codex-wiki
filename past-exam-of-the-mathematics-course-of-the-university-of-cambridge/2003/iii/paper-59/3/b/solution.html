Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2003 / iii / Paper 59 / 3 / b / Solution

We count periodic orbits whose trajectories remain near the two specified homoclinic orbits; the local hypotheses impose no bound on unrelated distant cycles. Choose the return domain small enough that Aδ∣x∣δ−1 and Bδ∣x∣δ−1 are less than one. Each admissible return branch is then a contraction mapping.
A right-lobe periodic orbit is a fixed point (p,+1) with p>0 and p=−μ+Apδ. For small amplitudes it exists exactly when μ<0, and p=−μ+o(∣μ∣). Similarly, a left-lobe periodic orbit has (x,y)=(−q,−1), q>0, q=−ν+Bqδ, and exists for ν<0. Their Floquet multipliers tend to zero, so both are attracting.
A period-two orbit of the section map corresponds to one flow periodic orbit traversing both lobes. Write its states as (p,−1) and (−q,+1). Its positive amplitudes satisfy
q=μ+Apδ,p=ν+Bqδ.​
(30)
The two-step derivative is ABδ2pδ−1qδ−1, again tending to zero. The asymmetric planar gluing return map therefore gives at most one such attracting two-lobe cycle in the small domain. Its boundaries are obtained by setting one amplitude to zero:
CR​: μ=−Aνδ (ν>0),CL​: ν=−Bμδ (μ>0).​
(31)
For the original flow these are leading equations, with corrections o(νδ) or o(μδ). On CR​, the negative unstable branch makes both excursions before returning to the positive stable branch; on CL​ the positive unstable branch returns to the negative stable branch. Each is a compound homoclinic orbit, with one extra passage near the saddle equilibrium. They are distinct from the primary loop curves μ=0 and ν=0.
The six open regions and their local cycles are:
RegionAttracting flow cycles
μ<0, ν<0Right and left single-lobe cycles
ν>0, μ<−AνδRight single-lobe cycle only
ν>0, −Aνδ<μ<0Right single-lobe cycle and two-lobe cycle
μ>0, ν<−BμδLeft single-lobe cycle only
μ>0, −Bμδ<ν<0Left single-lobe cycle and two-lobe cycle
μ>0, ν>0Two-lobe cycle only
There is no additional small alternating cycle when both parameters are negative. Indeed the two positive-amplitude equations would imply q<Apδ and p<Bqδ, giving p<BAδpδ2, impossible for sufficiently small positive p. On each mixed-sign quadrant the contraction equations give the two-lobe cycle precisely on the side of the compound curve toward the positive quadrant.
On μ=0, there is the primary right homoclinic orbit, together with a left cycle if ν<0 or a two-lobe cycle if ν>0. On ν=0 the analogous statement holds with left and right interchanged. On CR​ there is a compound homoclinic orbit and the right single-lobe cycle; on CL​ there is a compound homoclinic orbit and the left single-lobe cycle. At (0,0) the two primary loops meet at the same saddle equilibrium. The approach to each homoclinic orbit has a diverging period; it is not a saddle-node collision of two finite-period cycles.
