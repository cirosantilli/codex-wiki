Let τ>0 be the first conjugate time and choose a nonzero Jacobi field J with J(0)=J(τ)=0. It is normal by part 1(b), and Dt​J(τ)=0, since zero value and derivative there would force J=0. Fix any T>τ and extend J by zero to a continuous piecewise smooth vector field V on [0,T].
For endpoint-vanishing fields, the Riemannian index form is
I(X,Y)=∫0T​(⟨Dt​X,Dt​Y⟩−⟨R(X,γ˙​)γ˙​,Y⟩)dt.
(12)
Integration by parts on [0,τ] and the Jacobi field equation give I(V,V)=0 and
I(V,W)=⟨Dt​J(τ),W(τ)⟩.
(13)
Choose a smooth normal field W, zero at 0,T, with W(τ)=−Dt​J(τ). Then
I(V+εW,V+εW)=−2ε∣Dt​J(τ)∣2+ε2I(W,W)<0
(14)
for sufficiently small positive ε. The corner in V causes no difficulty: smooth endpoint-vanishing fields approximate V+εW in the Sobolev space H1, and the Riemannian index form is continuous in this norm on the compact segment. A smooth approximant therefore still has negative index form.
Realize this smooth field as a fixed-endpoint curve variation using the Riemannian exponential map. The allowed second variation of geodesic energy gives E′(0)=0 and E′′(0)<0, so some nearby curve has energy less than T/2, the energy of the unit-speed reference geodesic. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality bounds its length by
L2≤T∫0T​∣c˙∣2dt=2TE<T2.
(15)
It is shorter than the reference segment. Thus a geodesic cannot minimize beyond its first conjugate time. This argument deliberately uses T>τ; it does not rule out minimization up to the conjugate point itself.
