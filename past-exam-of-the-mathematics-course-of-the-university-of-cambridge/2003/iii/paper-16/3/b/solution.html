Let t0​ be the finite cut time and put q=γ(t0​). Continuity of Riemannian distance shows that the segment to q still minimizes and d(p,q)=t0​. Choose tj​↓t0​ with tj​>t0​. By the Hopf-Rinow theorem, there is a minimizing geodesic from p to qj​=γ(tj​) with length ℓj​=d(p,qj​)<tj​ and initial unit vector vj​. Continuity gives ℓj​→t0​.
The unit tangent sphere is compact, so after a subsequence vj​→v. Smooth dependence of the Riemannian exponential map gives expp​(t0​v)=q. The geodesic with initial vector v has length t0​=d(p,q) up to q, hence minimizes. If v=γ˙​(0), this is the required second minimizing geodesic.
Suppose instead v=γ˙​(0) and q is not a conjugate point. Then the differential of expp​ at t0​γ˙​(0) is nonsingular. The inverse function theorem makes expp​ injective on a neighborhood of this vector. For large j, both ℓj​vj​ and tj​γ˙​(0) belong to that neighborhood and exponentiate to qj​. They must be equal, implying ℓj​=tj​, a contradiction. Thus either a distinct minimizing geodesic exists or q is conjugate. In the latter case it is the first conjugate point, since part (a) excludes any earlier one on a minimizing segment. We have proved the cut-point dichotomy for complete Riemannian manifolds.
Here the relevant concept is a Riemannian cut point, not the topological cut point obtained by disconnecting a space through removal of a point.
