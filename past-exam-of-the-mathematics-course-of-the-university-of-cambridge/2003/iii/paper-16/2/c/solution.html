By part (b), M=Sn/Γ, where the group of deck transformations acts freely by round isometries. Every round isometry is the restriction of an orthogonal transformation of Rn+1: it preserves the inner product ⟨x,y⟩=cosd(x,y), so its images of an orthonormal basis determine its linear extension.
Suppose n is even. Every A∈SO(n+1) has eigenvalue one. Indeed, the nonreal eigenvalues come in conjugate pairs with product one, while the remaining real eigenvalues are ±1. Their number is odd and their product is one, forcing at least one +1. Such a transformation fixes a unit vector. Freeness of the deck transformation action consequently implies that the determinant map Γ→{±1} has trivial kernel. Thus ∣Γ∣≤2.
If Γ is nontrivial with generator A, then A2=I. Its eigenvalues are ±1, and freeness excludes the eigenvalue +1. Hence A=−I, the antipodal involution. We obtain
M≅SnorM≅RPn.​
(11)
This proves the even-dimensional part of the classification of complete positive constant-curvature manifolds.
The conclusion fails in odd dimensions. For n=2m−1≥3, identify S2m−1 with the unit sphere in Cm. Scalar multiplication by ζ=e2πi/r, with r≥3, generates a freely acting cyclic group of isometries: ζjz=z for nonzero z forces ζj=1. The quotient is complete with constant sectional curvature one and has fundamental group Cr​, so it is neither the simply connected sphere nor Real projective space with fundamental group C2​. In particular S3/C3​ is a lens space counterexample.
