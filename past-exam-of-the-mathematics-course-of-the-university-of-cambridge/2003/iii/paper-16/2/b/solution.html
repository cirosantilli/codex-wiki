 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2003 iii Paper 16 2 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 167
Let π:M→M be the universal cover and equip it with the pullback Riemannian metric g​=π∗g. The covering map is a local isometry, so M has constant sectional curvature one.
We must check completeness of the lifted Riemannian metric. A geodesic on M projects to a geodesic on M. By geodesic completeness of M, the projected geodesic extends for all real time. Its unique lift with a specified initial point extends the original lifted geodesic, because π is a local isometry. Hence M is geodesically complete, and the Hopf-Rinow theorem also gives metric completeness.
The allowed constant-curvature form of Cartan-Ambrose-Hicks theorem says that a linear tangent-space isometry between complete simply connected Riemannian manifolds of the same constant sectional curvature extends to a global isometry. The unit round sphere Sn is complete, simply connected for n≥2, and has sectional curvature one. Comparing it with M therefore yields
(M,g​)≅Sn.​
(10)
This is the covering step in the classification of complete positive constant-curvature manifolds. As usual for this assertion, n≥2 is understood: in dimension one there are no sectional two-planes, and the universal cover of the circle is the line, not the circle.
