Consider the orientation-preserving affine group of the real line, realized as
G={(a0​b1​):a>0, b∈R}.
(37)
The coordinates (loga,b) identify it smoothly with R2, so this Lie group is connected. Choose the Lie algebra element E and the group element D:
E=(00​10​),D=(20​01​)∈G.
(38)
Direct matrix multiplication gives AdD​E=DED−1=2E.
If a bi-invariant Riemannian metric existed, every conjugation map x↦DxD−1 would be an isometry fixing the identity. Its differential there, the Adjoint representation of a Lie group, would preserve the positive-definite inner product at the identity. Thus
∥E∥2=∥AdD​E∥2=∥2E∥2=4∥E∥2,
(39)
a contradiction since E=0. Therefore this connected Lie group admits no bi-invariant Riemannian metric. This is the adjoint dilation obstruction to a bi-invariant Riemannian metric, an elementary alternative permitted by the question. The positive definite Riemannian metric interpretation matters; no claim about indefinite metrics is needed.
