The concavity inequality is true, but its printed equality condition is false. At b=1 equality always holds, even for distinct states; for example take ρ1​=∣0⟩⟨0∣ and ρ2​=∣1⟩⟨1∣. The correct statement on 0≤b≤1 is
Equality holds iff b∈{0,1} or ρ1​=ρ2​.​
(19)
For 0<b<1, entropy is therefore strictly concave.
We give the matrix inequality needed for the proof. Define the quantum relative entropy D(τ∥σ)=trτ(log2​τ−log2​σ) if the support of τ is contained in that of σ, and +∞ otherwise. Diagonalize the two states with eigenvalues ri​,sj​, and put qij​=∣⟨i∣j⟩∣2. Each row and column of q sums to one. The elementary scalar inequality
uln(u/v)≥u−v
(20)
follows from xlnx−x+1≥0, with equality exactly at x=1; use limits at zero. Consequently
D(τ∥σ)=ln21​∑ij​qij​ri​ln(ri​/sj​)≥ln21​∑ij​qij​(ri​−sj​)=0.
(21)
Equality forces ri​=sj​ whenever qij​>0. Hence every eigenvector ∣i⟩ of τ is also a σ-eigenvector with eigenvalue ri​, so τ=σ. Conversely identical states give zero relative entropy. This proves nonnegativity of quantum relative entropy and its equality condition, including states with zero eigenvalues under the support convention.
Now set ρ=bρ1​+(1−b)ρ2​. For 0<b<1 its support contains both component supports. Direct expansion gives
S(ρ)−bS(ρ1​)−(1−b)S(ρ2​)=bD(ρ1​∥ρ)+(1−b)D(ρ2​∥ρ)≥0.
(22)
Since both coefficients are positive, equality holds exactly when both component states equal ρ, equivalently ρ1​=ρ2​. At either endpoint the mixture has only one component and equality is automatic. This proves the corrected strict concavity of Von Neumann entropy.
