The Mordell-Weil theorem gives E(Q)≅E(Q)tors​⊕Zr. Move the rational point of order two to (0,0) and clear denominators to obtain an integral Weierstrass equation of an elliptic curve
E:y2=x(x2+ax+b),b(a2−4b)=0.
(1)
The two-isogeny formula gives E′:Y2=X(X2−2aX+a2−4b) and dual isogenies ϕ:E→E′, ϕ​:E′→E with ϕ​ϕ=[2]. Compute the torsion subgroup using reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve at several good primes, followed by division polynomials of an elliptic curve to identify the possible points.
For the free part, use two-isogeny descent. The two-torsion square-class homomorphism is
α(O)=1,α((0,0))=[b],α((x,y))=[x]
(2)
in Q∗/Q∗2, with kernel ϕ​E′(Q); define α′ on E′ similarly. The prime-support bound in two-isogeny descent restricts the first image to signed square-free divisors of b and the second to those of a2−4b. For each candidate d, solve its two-isogeny descent quartic
N2=dU4+aU2V2+db​V4.
(3)
Test local solubility at the real place and the relevant p-adic fields, discard impossible classes, and search the surviving coverings for rational points. A point with UV=0 gives x=d(U/V)2, y=dUN/V3. Include the identity and rational 2-torsion cases separately. Once both rational images are determined, the two-isogeny rank formula gives
2r=4#α(E(Q))#α′(E′(Q))​.
(4)
The point searches give explicit candidates for a basis. Their canonical height pairing can certify independence; Mordell-Weil saturation and effective height bounds then certify that they generate the full free part. Rank alone does not certify generators of the whole Mordell-Weil group. If the rational images are not fully determined, the procedure instead supplies lower and upper bounds on the rank of an elliptic curve.
