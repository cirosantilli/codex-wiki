 ... Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge 2003 iii Paper 21 2 b
OurBigBook.com Words: 108
Use the two-prime bound on rational elliptic torsion with the counts six at three and eight at five from solution. For a prime ℓ=3,5, the ℓ-primary torsion subgroup injects into both reduced groups by reduction of torsion points on an elliptic curve. Its order therefore divides the corresponding primary part of gcd(6,8)=2. The three-primary subgroup injects into the group of order eight at five and is trivial; the five-primary subgroup injects into the group of order six at three and is trivial. Hence the entire rational torsion subgroup has order at most two.
The factorization x3+x2+x+1=(x+1)(x2+1) supplies T=(−1,0), a nonidentity point of order two under the elliptic curve group law. Therefore
E(Q)tors​={O,(−1,0)}≅Z/2Z.​
