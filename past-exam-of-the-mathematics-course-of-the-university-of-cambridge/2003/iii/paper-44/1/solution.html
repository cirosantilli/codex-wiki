Use units ℏ=c=1 and the Minkowski metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), so p⋅x=Ep​t−p⋅x. The canonical momentum of the real scalar field is π=ϕ˙​, and the Hamiltonian operator is
H=21​∫d3x[π2+(∇ϕ)2+m2ϕ2].
(1)
Canonical quantization of a real scalar field promotes ϕ and π to operator-valued distributions satisfying the equal-time canonical commutation relation
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδ3(x−y),[ϕ(t,x),ϕ(t,y)]=[π(t,x),π(t,y)]=0.
(2)
Products and operator commutators can first be interpreted after spatial smearing. An additive vacuum constant in H does not affect the following equations. In the Heisenberg picture, O˙=i[H,O]. Applying the canonical commutation relation to the quadratic Hamiltonian operator, and integrating the gradient term by parts, gives
ϕ˙​=π,π˙=∇2ϕ−m2ϕ,qquad(□+m2)ϕ=0​.
(3)
Thus the Klein-Gordon equation is an operator equation. The spatial Fourier transform reduces it to ϕ¨​p​+Ep2​ϕp​=0, with Ep​=p2+m2​. Its two frequency branches, together with the Hermitian adjoint condition ϕ†=ϕ, give the mode expansion of a free field
ϕ(x)=∫dμ(p)[a(p)fp​(x)+a†(p)fp∗​(x)],dμ(p)=(2π)32Ep​d3p​,fp​=e−ip⋅x.
(4)
The measure dμ(p) is Lorentz invariant; its normalization fixes that of the creation and annihilation operators rather than imposing any extra physical assumption.
For invariant-normalized scalar mode extraction, take the Klein-Gordon inner product of fp​ with the field. The positive-frequency contribution has coefficient Ep​+Eq​ and spatial integral (2π)3δ3(p−q). The negative-frequency contribution has coefficient Ep​−Eq​ and spatial integral (2π)3δ3(p+q), so it vanishes because E−p​=Ep​. Consequently
i∫d3x[fp∗​ϕ˙​−ϕf˙​p∗​]=∫dμ(q)(Ep​+Eq​)(2π)3δ3(p−q)a(q)=a(p).
(5)
Taking the Hermitian adjoint, with the order of the scalar factors immaterial, yields
a†(p)=−i∫d3x[fp​ϕ˙​−ϕf˙​p​]​.
(6)
Equivalently a(p)=∫d3xfp∗​(Ep​ϕ+iπ) and a†(q)=∫d3yfq​(Eq​ϕ−iπ). At a common time their operator commutator therefore is
[a(p),a†(q)]=∫d3xd3yfp∗​(t,x)fq​(t,y)(Ep​+Eq​)δ3(x−y)=(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−q)​.
(7)
The same calculation gives [a(p),a(q)]=[a†(p),a†(q)]=0: the surviving factor is an energy difference supported at opposite spatial momenta. These are the bosonic canonical commutation relations in relativistic normalization of a one-particle state.
Choose the Fock vacuum by a(p)∣0⟩=0 and ⟨0∣0⟩=1. Only the annihilator-creator product contributes to the Wightman function, giving
⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩=∫dμ(p)e−ip⋅(x−y).
(8)
Using time ordering and the reverse product for y0>x0, the Feynman propagator, with the convention DF​=iΔF​ in this calculation, is
DF​(x−y)=∫dμ(p)[θ(x0−y0)e−ip⋅(x−y)+θ(y0−x0)e−ip⋅(y−x)]​.
(9)
In particular the denominator in the spatial measure is 2Ep​ in both terms.
To verify the four-dimensional Fourier transform representation, let τ=x0−y0 and integrate the energy variable first:
I(τ)=limϵ↓0​∫−∞∞​2πdp0​(p0)2−Ep2​+iϵie−ip0τ​.
(10)
The Feynman i-epsilon prescription puts the positive-energy pole at Ep​−i0 and the negative-energy pole at −Ep​+i0. For τ>0, the contour integral closes in the lower half-plane with clockwise orientation. The residue theorem multiplies the residue ie−iEp​τ/(2Ep​) by −i, giving e−iEp​τ/(2Ep​). For τ<0, it closes counterclockwise above: multiplying the residue −ieiEp​τ/(2Ep​) by i gives eiEp​τ/(2Ep​). Thus I(τ)=e−iEp​∣τ∣/(2Ep​). After inserting the spatial exponential, reversing p in the negative-time term gives exactly the time ordering expression above. Hence
iΔF​(x−y)=ϵ↓0lim​∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+iϵie−ip⋅(x−y)​​.
(11)
This equality is understood as a distribution limit; the same prescription gives (□+m2)DF​(x−y)=−iδ4(x−y).
