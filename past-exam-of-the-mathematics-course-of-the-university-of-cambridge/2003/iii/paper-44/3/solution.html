All three fields have mass m. In the interaction picture the quantum fields evolve with the free Hamiltonian operator, so their equations are
(□+m2)ϕI​=(□+m2)ψI​=(□+m2)ΦI​=0​.
(21)
The interactions evolve the states instead. For χ∈{ϕ,ψ,Φ} the appropriate mode expansion of a free field is
χI​(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​[aχ​(p)e−ip⋅x+aχ†​(p)eip⋅x],Ep​=p2+m2​.
(22)
Independent species have commuting creation and annihilation operators. The full set of canonical commutation relations is
[aχ​(p),aη†​(q)]=δχη​(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−q),[aχ​,aη​]=[aχ†​,aη†​]=0.
(23)
These follow from the separate equal-time canonical commutation relations for the three real scalar fields. For comparison, in the Heisenberg picture the full Euler-Lagrange field equations contain interactions: (□+m2)ϕH​=−gϕH​ΦH​, (□+m2)ψH​=−gψH​ΦH​, and (□+m2)ΦH​=−2g​(ϕH2​+ψH2​). These are distinct from the free equations used in the interaction picture.
There are no derivative couplings, so the interaction Hamiltonian is HI​(t)=−∫d3xLI​(x). The interaction picture evolution operator obeys
i∂t​UI​(t,t0​)=HI​(t)UI​(t,t0​),UI​(t0​,t0​)=1.
(24)
Iterating its integral equation gives the Dyson series
UI​(t,t0​)=1+∑n≥1​(−i)n∫t0​t​dt1​∫t0​t1​​dt2​⋯∫t0​tn−1​​dtn​HI​(t1​)⋯HI​(tn​)=Texp[−i∫t0​t​HI​(t′)dt′].
(25)
The ordered integration region is equivalent to the unrestricted region with time ordering and the factor 1/n!. Taking the scattering limit t0​→−∞, t→∞, with the usual adiabatic prescription for perturbation theory, therefore yields the S-matrix
S=Texp[i∫d4xLI​(x)]​.
(26)
All fields inside this expression are interaction picture fields.
Use relativistic normalization of a one-particle state and take ∣i⟩=aϕ†​(p)aψ†​(q)∣0⟩, ∣f⟩=aϕ†​(p′)aψ†​(q′)∣0⟩. Define the connected scattering amplitude by ⟨f∣S∣i⟩conn​=i(2π)4δ4(p′+q′−p−q)M. The identity contribution describes no scattering; the term linear in g cannot connect these four external particles. At quadratic order, the connected term in the Dyson series is
S(2)=−8g2​∫d4xd4yT{[ϕI2​ΦI​+ψI2​ΦI​](x)[ϕI2​ΦI​+ψI2​ΦI​](y)}.
(27)
By Wick theorem, a connected Wick contraction must attach the two external ϕ legs to ϕI2​ at one vertex, the two external ψ legs to ψI2​ at the other, and contract the remaining two ΦI​ fields. The two assignments of species to x,y cancel the Dyson factor 1/2!. At either vertex, the 2! assignments of its identical fields to the incoming and outgoing particle cancel the interaction factor 1/2. Equivalently, the coefficient −g2/8 is multiplied by 2×2×2, giving −g2. Therefore, writing DF​(x−y)=⟨0∣TΦI​(x)ΦI​(y)∣0⟩,
⟨f∣S(2)∣i⟩conn​=−g2∫d4xd4yei(p′−p)⋅x+i(q′−q)⋅yDF​(x−y).
(28)
Insert the Feynman propagator DF​(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4r​r2−m2+i0ie−ir⋅(x−y)​. The two vertex integrals impose r=p′−p and p′+q′=p+q. With the Mandelstam invariant t=(p−p′)2, this gives
⟨f∣S(2)∣i⟩conn​=(2π)4δ4(p′+q′−p−q)t−m2+i0−ig2​,M=−t−m2+i0g2​​.
(29)
This is distinguishable scalar scattering by a shared mediator with mediator mass m. In physical elastic kinematics t≤0, so the denominator has no pole for m>0. There is only the displayed t-channel tree-level Feynman diagram: an s- or u-channel would require an absent mixed ϕψΦ vertex. Each vertex contributes −ig and the exchanged Φ contributes i/(t−m2+i0), agreeing with the direct Wick contraction calculation.
