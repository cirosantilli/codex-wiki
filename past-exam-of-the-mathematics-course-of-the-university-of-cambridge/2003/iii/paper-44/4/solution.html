Use gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) throughout. Vary the Maxwell action with respect to the electromagnetic four-potential. Antisymmetry of the electromagnetic field tensor gives δL=−Fμν∂μ​δAν​. After integration by parts, the Euler-Lagrange field equation is
∂μ​Fμν=0​,□Aν−∂ν(∂μ​Aμ)=0.
(30)
A gauge transformation Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​χ leaves Fμν​ unchanged because partial derivatives commute. It therefore leaves the Maxwell action and observable electromagnetic fields unchanged: different potentials represent the same physical field. This redundancy matters for canonical quantization: before gauge fixing, the momentum conjugate to A0​ vanishes and the four potential components cannot be treated as four independent physical oscillators.
Choose the Lorenz gauge D=∂μ​Aμ=0, attainable by solving □χ=−D with suitable boundary data. The Euler-Lagrange field equation then reduces to
□Aμ=0​.
(31)
Residual Lorenz gauge symmetry consists of transformations with □χ=0. The gauge condition and this residual redundancy ultimately leave two transverse polarizations of a free photon.
For the specified kinetic density LC​=−21​∂μ​Aν​∂μAν, one has ∂LC​/∂(∂μ​Aν​)=−∂μAν. Its Euler-Lagrange field equation is therefore −□Aν=0. This density is the Feynman-gauge Maxwell kinetic density after a boundary-term subtraction, since
−41​Fμν​Fμν−21​D2=LC​+∂μ​Kμ,Kμ=21​(Aν​∂νAμ−AμD).
(32)
The divergence follows by expanding both sides and commuting the second derivatives. Thus the wave equation follows from a Feynman gauge action; the additional physical-state condition will implement the Lorenz gauge. One must not impose D=0 inside the original action before varying it.
Take the covariant components Aν​ as canonical coordinates. The canonical momentum computed from LC​ is
Πν=∂(∂0​Aν​)∂LC​​=−∂0​Aν​.
(33)
The equal-time canonical Poisson brackets are {Aμ​(t,x),Πν(t,y)}=δμ​νδ3(x−y), with coordinate-coordinate and momentum-momentum brackets zero. Applying canonical quantization gives
[Aμ​(t,x),Πν(t,y)]=iδμ​νδ3(x−y)​,[Aμ​,Aν​]=[Πμ,Πν]=0.
(34)
In particular [Aμ​,A˙ν​]=−igμν​δ3. The negative temporal sign is the source of the indefinite state-space norm in covariant photon quantization.
The spatial Fourier transform of the wave equation gives an oscillator of frequency ω=∣k∣ for each component. The Hermitian adjoint condition on the electromagnetic four-potential then yields the mode expansion of a free field
Aμ​(x)=∫(2π)32ωd3k​[aμ​(k)e−ik⋅x+aμ†​(k)eik⋅x]​,kμ=(ω,k).
(35)
These are massless on shell modes. Consistently with the preceding equal-time canonical commutation relation,
[aμ​(k),aν†​(l)]=−gμν​(2π)32ωδ3(k−l),[aμ​,aν​]=[aμ†​,aν†​]=0.
(36)
The normalization is the same as for the scalar modes, with −gμν​ replacing the species identity matrix.
Gupta-Bleuler quantization starts with the Fock vacuum annihilated by every aμ​ and imposes only the annihilation, or positive-frequency part of a quantum field, of the Lorenz gauge condition:
(∂μ​Aμ)(+)∣phys⟩=0​⟺kμaμ​(k)∣phys⟩=0for every k.
(37)
Imposing the entire Hermitian field divergence as a strong operator identity would be incompatible with the four-component canonical commutation relation. The weaker condition suffices: between two physical states its positive-frequency part vanishes on the ket, and its negative-frequency part vanishes on the bra. Hence ⟨phys′∣∂μ​Aμ∣phys⟩=0.
For a one-photon state ∣k,ϵ⟩=ϵνaν†​(k)∣0⟩, commuting the constraint through the creator gives
lμaμ​(l)∣k,ϵ⟩=−(k⋅ϵ)(2π)32ωδ3(l−k)∣0⟩.
(38)
Thus the allowed photon polarization vectors satisfy k⋅ϵ=0​. Their state inner product, with the common momentum-normalization factor suppressed, is ⟨ϵ′∣ϵ⟩=−ϵ′∗⋅ϵ. Momentum eigenstates are delta-normalized; the same statements hold with finite norms for normalizable wave packets.
Rotate the spatial axes so that k=ω(1,0,0,1). The constraint is ϵ0=ϵ3, so the complete allowed family is
ϵ=α(1,0,0,1)+β(0,1,0,0)+γ(0,0,1,0)​,α,β,γ∈C.
(39)
The first vector is the allowed longitudinal gauge polarization, proportional to the null four-momentum k. Its state has zero norm because k2=0; it is also orthogonal to every constrained state because k⋅ϵ=0. A purely spatial vector (0,0,0,1) alone is not an allowed physical polarization: its time component must accompany it to satisfy the constraint. The other two vectors are transverse polarizations, perpendicular to k, with positive unit norms. For the displayed family,
−ϵ∗⋅ϵ=∣β∣2+∣γ∣2.
(40)
The timelike negative-norm excitation has been excluded by the constraint, and the remaining form is positive semidefinite. The Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient identifies ϵ and ϵ+λk, removing the null direction. It leaves exactly two transverse physical photon polarizations with a positive inner product; their bosonic Fock space is the physical photon state space.
Finally use Feynman slash notation, a=γμaμ​, and impose four-momentum conservation p+q=k+k′. The external Dirac spinors obey
(p−m)u(p)=0,vˉ(q)(q+m)=0.
(41)
Set r=p−k and r′=p−k′. Replacing ϵ by k in the first term of the electron-positron annihilation into two photons amplitude, the rightmost contracted vertex obeys
ku=(p−m)u−(r−m)u=−(r−m)u.
(42)
Since the Clifford algebra implies (r+m)(r−m)=(r2−m2)I4​, that term becomes
vˉϵ′r2−m2r+m​ku=−vˉϵ′u.
(43)
For the second term, four-momentum conservation gives k=q+r′. The contracted vertex is now adjacent to the outgoing positron spinor on the left, so
vˉk=vˉ(q+r′)=vˉ(r′−m).
(44)
Consequently
vˉkr′2−m2r′+m​ϵ′u=+vˉϵ′u.
(45)
The two terms cancel, proving the two-photon fermion Ward identity
vˉ(q)M​ϵ=k​u(p)=0,T​ϵ=k​=0​.
(46)
Exchanging the two photon labels proves the same result for ϵ′=k′. By linearity the amplitude is invariant under ϵ↦ϵ+λk on either external photon leg. Therefore the null longitudinal gauge polarization has zero production amplitude: physical scattering produces only the two transverse photon polarizations. This cancellation also shows why both photon orderings in the tree-level Feynman diagrams are essential; either term alone fails the Ward identity.
