Let V=R4 have symplectic basis (e1​,f1​,e2​,f2​). Fix vol=e1​∧f1​∧e2​∧f2​ and define a symmetric bilinear form Q on Λ2V by u∧v=Q(u,v)vol. The pairings between complementary basis bivectors split this six-dimensional space into three hyperbolic planes, so Q has signature (3,3).
The symplectic group preserves volume and the bivector Ω=e1​∧f1​+e2​∧f2​, obtained from the inverse symplectic form, up to its fixed sign convention. Since Q(Ω,Ω)=2, its orthogonal complement W=Ω⊥ has dimension five and signature (2,3). On W use −Q, which has signature (3,2). This constructs the exterior-square double cover of SO0(3,2).
If an element acts trivially on W, it also fixes Ω, so its action on the entire exterior square is the identity. Every decomposable bivector then shows that its two-plane is preserved. A line is the intersection of two such planes; hence every line is preserved, and the matrix is scalar. Its exterior-square action forces its scalar square to equal one, giving precisely ±I.
The derivative is injective by the same infinitesimal argument: zero action on Λ2V forces an infinitesimal scalar, whose induced scalar there is twice its value and therefore zero. The symplectic Lie algebra has dimension 22+2(2⋅3/2)=10, from blocks (AC​B−AT​) with symmetric B,C. The orthogonal Lie algebra in five dimensions also has dimension ten. Thus the image is open. Real symplectic polar decomposition has connected compact factor U(2) and a contractible positive symplectic factor, so Sp(4,R) is connected and its image is exactly the identity component:
Sp(4,R)/{±I}≅SO0​(3,2).​
(41)
As in the preceding noncompact cases, this does not identify the quotient with the disconnected full SO(3,2).
