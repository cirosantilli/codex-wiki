Let μ:P→R be the moment map for the circle group, assume c is a regular value, and assume the circle action on N=μ−1(c) is free. The action is proper because the circle is compact. Then N has dimension 2n−1 and Pc​=N/SO(2) is a smooth manifold of dimension 2n−2.
If ξP​ generates the action, ω(ξP​,v)=dμ(v)=0 for every v∈TN. In fact (TN)ω is exactly the line spanned by ξP​, since TN=kerdμ is a hyperplane and ω is nondegenerate. Therefore the kernel of the restricted two-form is precisely the orbit direction. The restriction is invariant and horizontal, so it descends to a unique nondegenerate closed two-form ωc​ with
π∗ωc​=ι∗ω.​
(32)
This proves the Marsden-Weinstein theorem in the circle case. If the Hamiltonian H is invariant, then {μ,H}=0: its Hamiltonian flow stays on N, projects to the quotient and is generated there by the descended function Hc​. This is a reduced Hamiltonian system in 2n−2 local variables. At a critical level or a nonfree orbit the quotient can be singular, so the smooth dimension assertion needs the stated hypotheses.
