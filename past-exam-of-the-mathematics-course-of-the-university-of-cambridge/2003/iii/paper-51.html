The pullback of the flat Euclidean metric under x′μ=xμ+ϵωμ(x) is
δμν​+ϵ(∂μ​ων​+∂ν​ωμ​)+O(ϵ2).
(1)
For a conformal transformation it must be a scalar multiple of δμν​. Taking the trace determines that multiple. With ρ=∂α​ωα, the necessary and sufficient infinitesimal condition is the Conformal Killing equation
∂μ​ων​+∂ν​ωμ​=d2ρ​δμν​​.
(2)
Differentiate its (α,β) instance with respect to xγ, its (α,γ) instance with respect to xβ, and its (β,γ) instance with respect to xα. Adding the first two and subtracting the last, commuting partial derivatives, leaves 2∂β​∂γ​ωα​. Hence flat conformal Killing integrability gives
∂β​∂γ​ωα​=d1​(δαβ​∂γ​ρ+δαγ​∂β​ρ−δβγ​∂α​ρ)​.
(3)
Contracting β with γ gives Δωα​=(2−d)∂α​ρ/d. Its divergence is Δρ=(2−d)Δρ/d, so 2(d−1)Δρ=0: thus Δρ=0 for d>1​. Taking instead the α divergence of the displayed second-derivative identity gives
d∂β​∂γ​ρ=2∂β​∂γ​ρ−δβγ​Δρ.
(4)
Consequently ∂β​∂γ​ρ=0 for d>2​.
On a connected flat domain, write ρ=dλ+2db⋅x, with constant λ and b. Integrating the second-derivative identity, and imposing the Conformal Killing equation on its remaining affine part, gives the complete Conformal Killing vector field
ωμ(x)=aμ+Rμν​xν+λxμ+2(b⋅x)xμ−bμx2​,Rμν​=−Rνμ​.
(5)
Indeed the quadratic part has precisely the required Hessian matrix, while the symmetric part of the remaining linear term must be λδμν​. The constants generate translations, rotations, dilations and special conformal transformations, respectively. There are d, d(d−1)/2, 1 and d independent parameters, so the full conformal group has dimension
2(d+1)(d+2)​​.
(6)
Here “full” means local transformations, or global transformations of the conformal compactification of Euclidean space; its connected group is locally SO(d+1,1). Requiring every transformation to be nonsingular on all of uncompactified Rd would exclude the special conformal transformations with finite poles.

The stress-energy tensor generates translations. One may improve it by adding a divergence without changing the translation charges under suitable boundary conditions, and choose it symmetric in a relativistic theory. If Θμν is symmetric and conserved, a spacetime vector field ω defines the current jμ=Θμνων​. For a Conformal Killing vector field,
∂μ​jμ=Θμν∂μ​ων​=d∂⋅ω​Θμμ​.
(7)
Thus a traceless conserved stress-energy tensor produces all the conformal charges. The dilation current is xν​Θμν, and the special conformal currents are (2xλ​xν​−x2ηλν​)Θμν. A trace that is merely the divergence of a virial current gives scale invariance; conformal invariance requires the additional improvement that removes the trace. These statements distinguish an actual traceless tensor from a trace discarded without justification.
For a free massless scalar field, the canonical stress-energy tensor has trace (1−d/2)(∂ϕ)2. For d>1, the improved stress-energy tensor of a free massless scalar is
Θμν​=∂μ​ϕ∂ν​ϕ−21​ημν​(∂ϕ)2+4(d−1)d−2​(ημν​□−∂μ​∂ν​)ϕ2.
(8)
Its improvement is identically conserved. On □ϕ=0, the identity □ϕ2=2(∂ϕ)2 shows that its trace vanishes. In d=2, no improvement is needed: the canonical stress-energy tensor is already traceless. In Lorentzian null coordinates, conservation and tracelessness imply that its two surviving components depend separately on x+ and x−. After Euclidean continuation, these become the holomorphic stress-energy tensor T(z) and its antiholomorphic counterpart Tˉ(zˉ), with
∂ˉT=0,∂Tˉ=0.
(9)
Local conformal changes are arbitrary holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinate changes, not just the three global Möbius transformation generators in either sector.
To make the free-field quantum normalization explicit, rescale the field to φ=4π​ϕ, so that its plane contraction is φ(z,zˉ)φ(w,wˉ)∼−log∣z−w∣2. Define J=i∂φ, so J(z)J(w)∼(z−w)−2. Normal ordering gives
T(z)=21​:J(z)2:=−21​:(∂φ)2:.
(10)
A single Wick contraction shows that J is a weight-one primary operator. The two double contractions in T(z)T(w) produce 21​(z−w)−4; single contractions, followed by Taylor expansion at w, produce 2T(w)/(z−w)2+∂T(w)/(z−w). Thus the operator product expansion is
T(z)T(w)∼(z−w)41/2​+(z−w)22T(w)​+z−w∂T(w)​​,c=1​.
(11)
The same holds independently for Tˉ, with cˉ=1. The undifferentiated noncompact scalar has a logarithmic contraction and a zero-mode subtlety; its derivative current has the ordinary primary transformation law.
The holomorphic stress-energy tensor implements infinitesimal conformal transformations through contour charges Qv​=2πi1​∮dzv(z)T(z). A primary field O of weight h obeys
T(z)O(w)∼(z−w)2hO(w)​+z−w∂O(w)​,[Qv​,O]=(v∂+hv′)O.
(12)
For example the free-field vertex operator :eiαφ: has holomorphic weight h=α2/2, as follows by contracting ∂φ with the exponential twice. In correlators the contour may encircle each insertion, giving the conformal Ward identity ⟨T(z)∏i​Oi​(zi​)⟩=∑i​[hi​/(z−zi​)2+∂zi​​/(z−zi​)]⟨∏i​Oi​(zi​)⟩. This also yields the global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators.
Writing T(z)=∑n​Ln​z−n−2 and integrating the stress-tensor operator product expansion gives the Virasoro algebra
[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12c​(m3−m)δm+n,0​​.
(13)
Its central charge is the quantum correction to the classical conformal algebra; the correction vanishes on L−1​,L0​,L1​. Equivalently, in the pullback convention a finite holomorphic change z↦f(z) sends T to (f′)2T(f)+12c​{f,z}, where {f,z}=f′′′/f′−23​(f′′/f′)2 is the Schwarzian derivative. This anomalous term vanishes for Möbius transformations. On a curved background the related trace anomaly is proportional to its curvature; it does not destroy the flat-space conformal symmetry of this massless theory.

Let Gn​(z1​,…,zn​) denote the ordered vacuum correlator. Inserting the vacuum-preserving Möbius transformation operator and its inverse gives
Gn​(z1​,…,zn​)=∏i​γ′(zi​)hi​Gn​(γ(z1​),…,γ(zn​)).
(14)
For γ(z)=z+ϵv(z), the three vector fields v=1,z,z2 yield the global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators
∑i​∂i​Gn​=0,∑i​(zi​∂i​+hi​)Gn​=0,∑i​(zi2​∂i​+2hi​zi​)Gn​=0.
(15)
All formulas below concern separated insertion points and a consistent local choice of branches for fractional powers.
For one insertion, the translation identity makes G1​ constant and the dilation identity gives h1​G1​=0. Thus
G1​=C1​ if h1​=0;G1​=0 if h1​=0​.
(16)
For two insertions, translation invariance gives G2​=f(z12​), where zij​=zi​−zj​. Dilations imply z12​f′+(h1​+h2​)f=0, so f=C12​z12−h1​−h2​​. Substituting into the special conformal identity leaves (h1​−h2​)z12​f=0. Therefore
G2​=z122h​C12​​,h1​=h2​=h​,
(17)
with G2​=0 when the weights differ.
For three insertions write G3​=C123​z12−a​z13−b​z23−c​. Under a Möbius transformation, γ(zi​)−γ(zj​)=zij​γ′(zi​)γ′(zj​)​ with compatible local branches. The covariance condition therefore fixes
a+b=2h1​,a+c=2h2​,b+c=2h3​.
(18)
Solving gives
G3​=z12h1​+h2​−h3​​z13h1​+h3​−h2​​z23h2​+h3​−h1​​C123​​​.
(19)
This is the complete solution because Möbius transformations act transitively on ordered triples of distinct points: the ratio of any two covariant nonzero solutions has no remaining invariant variable and is constant. Three-point covariance imposes no equality or triangle inequality among the weights. Additional fusion rules or other symmetries may force C123​=0, but none follow from the stated covariance and vacuum assumptions alone.
For more than three generic points, three positions may be fixed by a Möbius transformation, leaving n−3 independent complex conformal cross-ratios. Correlators consequently have a covariant prefactor multiplied by an arbitrary function of these invariants. For example one convenient four-point form is
G4​=z12h1​+h2​​z34h3​+h4​​(z24​/z14​)h1​−h2​(z14​/z13​)h3​−h4​​F(χ),χ=z13​z24​z12​z34​​.
(20)
The Möbius invariance of the cross-ratio guarantees covariance for any F. Thus global conformal symmetry fixes one-, two- and three-point functions up to constants, but does not fix higher-point functions. Their remaining functions encode dynamics.

For d≥2, let ω be a Conformal Killing vector field, put σ=∂⋅ω/d, and assign the massless scalar field scaling dimension Δ=(d−2)/2. In a finite conformal transformation with metric scale Ω2, the transformation law is ϕ′(x′)=Ω(x)−Δϕ(x). Its infinitesimal fixed-coordinate form is
δ0​ϕ=−ωμ∂μ​ϕ−Δσϕ.
(21)
For L=(∂ϕ)2/2, contract the Conformal Killing equation with ∂μ​ϕ∂ν​ϕ. Direct differentiation then gives
δ0​L​=−ω⋅∂L−(1+Δ)σ(∂ϕ)2−2Δ​∂μ​(ϕ2)∂μσ=−∂μ​(ωμL)−2Δ​∂μ​(ϕ2∂μσ)+2Δ​ϕ2□σ.​
(22)
Here 2(1+Δ)=d. Flat conformal Killing integrability gives □σ=0, with the Lorentzian version obtained by replacing the flat metric consistently. Thus the action variation is a boundary integral, vanishing for suitable boundary conditions. This proves conformal invariance with field dimension (d−2)/2, without using the field equation. The connected finite symmetry follows by integrating the infinitesimal generators. Equivalently its conformal currents follow from the improved stress-energy tensor of a free massless scalar.
In one dimension the metric conformality condition is vacuous. The same action-variation calculation contains −ϕ2ω′′′/4, so the free action is invariant under the projective subgroup ω′′′=0, rather than arbitrary reparametrizations. The usual field-theory conformal-invariance claim above concerns d≥2.
In d=2, Δ=0, so the field transforms simply as a scalar. There is no improvement term and no derivative of the conformal scale in the variation. All local independent reparametrizations of the two null coordinates preserve the action; after Euclidean continuation they are holomorphic and antiholomorphic maps. The conformal algebra is therefore infinite-dimensional, in contrast to d>2.
Now use Lorentzian coordinates with spatial period ℓ. The canonical momentum is π=ϕ˙​, and canonical quantization imposes [ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδℓ​(x−y), with the periodic delta distribution δℓ​(s)=ℓ−1∑n∈Z​e2πins/ℓ. Solving the wave equation and imposing reality gives a convenient oscillator normalization:
ϕ(t,x)=q+ℓpt​+4π​i​∑n=0​n1​[αn​e−2πin(t−x)/ℓ+αn​e−2πin(t+x)/ℓ].
(23)
Strict spatial periodicity excludes a winding term. The canonical commutation relation is equivalent to
[q,p]=i,[αm​,αn​]=mδm+n,0​,[αm​,αn​]=mδm+n,0​,[αm​,αn​]=0,
(24)
with αn†​=α−n​ and the corresponding tilded relation. Both oscillator families commute with q,p. To verify the normalization, the zero-mode commutator supplies i/ℓ, while each oscillator family supplies i(2ℓ)−1∑n=0​e2πin(x−y)/ℓ. Their sum is exactly iδℓ​(x−y). The positive-index modes are annihilation operators and the negative-index modes are creation operators, giving two independent bosonic Fock spaces together with the free-particle zero mode.
Define α0​=α0​=p/4π​ and the normal ordering quadratic generators
Lm​=21​∑n∈Z​:αm−n​αn​:,Lm​=21​∑n∈Z​:αm−n​αn​:.
(25)
These are the Fourier modes of the two chiral components of the stress-energy tensor. The oscillator operator commutators immediately give [Lm​,αn​]=−nαm+n​. Applying this to both factors of Ln​ gives (m−n)Lm+n​, except for a scalar term when m+n=0, produced by reordering creators and annihilators. Its value is independent of the momentum sector. Evaluate it in the formal zero-momentum oscillator vacuum. For m>0,
L−m​∣0⟩=21​∑r=1m−1​α−r​α−(m−r)​∣0⟩,⟨0∣Lm​L−m​∣0⟩=21​∑r=1m−1​r(m−r)=12m3−m​.
(26)
The two contractions of each pair explain the factor 1/2. Hence the free-boson Virasoro central term yields
[Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12m3−m​δm+n,0​​,c=1​.
(27)
The tilded modes give a second commuting Virasoro algebra with cˉ=1. The adjoint condition Lm†​=L−m​ and the positive oscillator norms make this a unitary highest-weight Virasoro representation in each momentum sector. In a fixed momentum sector, L0​=p2/(8π)+∑n>0​α−n​αn​, so the oscillator ground state is a highest-weight state of weight p2/(8π) in either sector. For a noncompact field the zero mode is a free particle: definite momentum ground states are generalized states, rather than a normalizable zero-mode vacuum. The oscillator representation and central term remain well defined. With the standard cylinder vacuum-energy subtraction, the Hamiltonian is
H=ℓ2π​(L0​+L0​−121​)​.
(28)
The constant is the Casimir energy of a two-dimensional conformal field theory; omitting it amounts to a different additive Hamiltonian convention, not a change of the plane Virasoro algebra.

Use [Lm​,Ln​]=(m−n)Lm+n​+12c​(m3−m)δm+n,0​ and Ln†​=L−n​. A normalized highest-weight vector obeys L0​∣h⟩=h∣h⟩ and Ln​∣h⟩=0 for n>0. Its Virasoro descendants are products of negative modes. Their inner products are determined entirely by c,h and the Virasoro algebra; for example ∥L−1​∣h⟩∥2=2h, so unitarity requires h≥0. An irreducible unitary highest-weight Virasoro module is the Virasoro Verma module quotient by its null submodules, and its surviving inner product must be positive definite.
The classification for c<1 is the Unitary Virasoro discrete series:
c=1−m(m+1)6​,h=hr,s​=4m(m+1)[(m+1)r−ms]2−1​​,
(29)
where m is an integer at least 2, 1≤r≤m−1, and 1≤s≤m. The identification (r,s)∼(m−r,m+1−s) leaves m(m−1)/2 distinct modules. The two generating singular-vector levels are rs and (m−r)(m+1−s); all their Virasoro descendants are removed in the irreducible quotient. For m=2 the only module is the trivial c=h=0 module; for m≥3 these are the nontrivial unitary discrete-series modules. There are no other irreducible unitary highest-weight representations with c<1. This is the classification theorem, rather than a condition inferred only from finitely many descendant norms.
At c=1/2, m=3 and the possible weights are
h=0,h=21​,h=161​​.
(30)
Construct them with a single free chiral Majorana fermion:
ψ(z)=∑r​br​z−r−1/2,{br​,bs​}=δr+s,0​,br†​=b−r​.
(31)
The free chiral Majorana fermion conformal field theory has T=−:ψ∂ψ:/2. In the Neveu–Schwarz sector, r∈Z+1/2, while in the Ramond sector, r∈Z. With positive modes moved to the right and the zero-mode product antisymmetrized, the corresponding generators are
Ln​=21​∑r​(r−2n​):bn−r​br​:+aδn,0​,a=0 in NS,a=161​ in R.
(32)
The oscillator relation gives [Ln​,br​]=−(r+n/2)bn+r​. The stress-tensor operator product expansion, or the reordering of the quadratic generators, then gives the Virasoro algebra with c=1/2. One can check both constants directly in the ground states. In the NS sector,
L−2​∣0⟩=21​b−3/2​b−1/2​∣0⟩,∥L−2​∣0⟩∥2=41​=2c​.
(33)
In the R sector, b02​=1/2 and L−1​∣R⟩=21​b−1​b0​∣R⟩, whose norm squared is 1/8. The relation [L1​,L−1​]=2L0​ requires 2hR​=1/8, so hR​=a=1/16. Thus the Ramond sector zero-mode shift is essential.
The three constructions in Ising Virasoro modules from a Majorana fermion are explicit. The NS vacuum is a highest-weight vector of weight 0. The state b−1/2​∣0⟩ is a highest-weight vector of weight 1/2: applying any positive Ln​ gives −(n/2−1/2)bn−1/2​∣0⟩=0, including the zero coefficient for n=1. Since Ln​ is quadratic in fermions, the two cyclic modules lie in the even and odd fermion parity sectors, respectively. For R, choose a normalized ground state annihilated by all bn​ with n>0 and with b0​∣R⟩=±∣R⟩/2​. All positive Ln​ annihilate it and its weight is 1/16. The two zero-mode sign choices give isomorphic Virasoro modules; one choice suffices to construct the third module.
The oscillator inner products are positive, and each cyclic highest-weight representation just constructed is irreducible. Indeed any nonzero proper invariant graded submodule would have a lowest-energy vector w of weight greater than the cyclic ground weight. Every positive Ln​ annihilates w; thus w is orthogonal to every negative-mode descendant of the original ground vector, by moving the modes across the inner product. It is also orthogonal to that ground vector by its different L0​ eigenvalue. Since those Virasoro descendants span the cyclic module, w would have zero norm, a contradiction. This proves irreducibility directly inside the positive fermionic Fock space.
In fact the cyclic modules fill the entire even NS, odd NS and chosen R Fock spaces. Otherwise an orthogonal remaining lowest vector would be the ground vector of a further unitary highest-weight Virasoro module; its weight would lie in Z>0​, 1/2+Z>0​ or 1/16+Z>0​, respectively. None belongs to the classified list {0,1/2,1/16}. As a check, their Virasoro characters, defined by χh​(q)=TrqL0​−c/24, are
χ0​(q)χ1/2​(q)χ1/16​(q)​=2q−1/48​[n≥0∏​(1+qn+1/2)+n≥0∏​(1−qn+1/2)],=2q−1/48​[n≥0∏​(1+qn+1/2)−n≥0∏​(1−qn+1/2)],=q1/24n≥1∏​(1+qn).​
(34)
The NS signs project onto even and odd occupation number; the R product uses the single chosen ground state. Null vectors of the abstract Virasoro Verma modules are automatically zero in this construction. In particular L−1​∣0⟩=0, and the weight-1/2 and weight-1/16 ground states obey, respectively, (L−2​−43​L−12​)∣1/2⟩=0 and (L−2​−34​L−12​)∣1/16⟩=0, as is also verified by their level-two Gram matrices.

Write the affine modes as Jna​=Tna​, and put Q=Qg. The Sugawara construction requires a restricted module, meaning that sufficiently large positive current modes annihilate each fixed vector, and a noncritical level 2k+Q=0. A positive-energy highest-weight representation has the required restriction. Define
Sm​=∑a,p∈Z​:Jm−pa​Jpa​:,Lmg​=2k+QSm​​​.
(35)
Normal ordering puts positive modes to the right, making each sum finite on a fixed finite-energy vector. The invariant sum over the current index a is the quadratic Casimir operator construction for the affine current algebra.
Here is the normal ordering calculation responsible for its denominator. In commuting Sm​ with Jnb​, the two affine central terms supply −2knJm+nb​. The remaining terms contain ifabc times the two ways to replace a current by Jp+nc​. Relabeling the summation index and using antisymmetry of fabc cancels the un-reordered terms. Reordering the modes that cross the creation/annihilation boundary leaves n signed terms, whose two structure constants contract by fabcfabd=Qδcd. Their sum is −QnJm+nb​. Thus
[Sm​,Jnb​]=−(2k+Q)nJm+nb​,[Lmg​,Jnb​]=−nJm+nb​​.
(36)
The adjoint-Casimir contribution is genuinely quantum. For an explicit check of its coefficient, let ∣0⟩ be the affine vacuum, with all Jna​∣0⟩=0 for n≥0. Then
S0​=∑a​(J0a​)2+2∑a,p>0​J−pa​Jpa​,S0​J−1b​∣0⟩=(Q+2k)J−1b​∣0⟩.
(37)
The first term acts by the adjoint Casimir operator Q, and only p=1 contributes 2k to the second. This also verifies that a current has conformal weight one under the displayed normalization.
Now commute the quadratic generators with one another. Acting on either current factor produces (m−n)Lm+ng​; the residual full contractions are scalar and vanish unless m+n=0. The Jacobi identity fixes their cubic dependence on m, and the plane vacuum convention L−1​∣0⟩=L0​∣0⟩=L1​∣0⟩=0 fixes the combination m3−m. To determine its coefficient explicitly, note
L−2g​∣0⟩=2k+Q1​∑a​J−1a​J−1a​∣0⟩.
(38)
Commuting a single J1a​ through the numerator gives (2k+Q)J−1a​∣0⟩: the two central contractions give 2kJ−1a​, and the zero-mode commutator in the structure-constant term gives QJ−1a​. The remaining J1a​ contraction gives k for each adjoint index. Therefore
∥L−2g​∣0⟩∥2=(2k+Q)2k(2k+Q)dimg​=2k+Qkdimg​=2cg​.
(39)
For an algebraic module without a positive inner product, the same calculation is its vacuum matrix element and fixes the universal central term. Consequently
[Lmg​,Lng​]=(m−n)Lm+ng​+12cg​(m3−m)δm+n,0​​,cg=2k+Q2kdimg​​.
(40)
This is a representation of the Virasoro algebra, not merely its centerless part.
For a complex semisimple Lie algebra, a Cartan subalgebra h is a maximal abelian subalgebra of semisimple elements; equivalently it is the complexification of a maximal torus algebra in the compact real form. Its dimension is the rank of a semisimple Lie algebra, r. Choose an orthonormal Cartan basis with currents Hni​. They have no structure-constant term:
[Hmi​,Hnj​]=kmδijδm+n,0​.
(41)
For k=0, their abelian Sugawara construction is
Lmh​=2k1​∑i=1r​∑p​:Hm−pi​Hpi​:,ch=r.
(42)
Each Cartan current has weight one under both Lg and Lh, so Km​=Lmg​−Lmh​ commutes with all Hni​, hence also with Lnh​. Subtracting the two commuting stress tensors yields the Coset construction
[Km​,Kn​]=(m−n)Km+n​+12cg−r​(m3−m)δm+n,0​.
(43)
In the intended nontrivial compact unitary theory at positive admissible level, this coset acts on a positive inner-product space, with Km†​=K−m​. Its vacuum satisfies Kn​∣0⟩=0 for n≥−1, so
0≤∥K−2​∣0⟩∥2=⟨0∣[K2​,K−2​]∣0⟩=2cg−r​.
(44)
Thus cg≥r. Also Q>0 for a compact simple Lie algebra and k>0, so 2k/(2k+Q)<1, which proves the upper bound directly. Hence, under these physical representation hypotheses,
rankg≤cg≤dimg​.
(45)
The upper inequality is strict at finite positive level; cg tends to dimg as the level grows.
The positivity hypothesis is necessary and is not implied by the bare affine commutation relation. For example, at k=0 its trivial representation has all currents zero and gives cg=0<rankg. Even positive arbitrary levels need not satisfy the bound: with g=su(2), fabc=ϵabc, Q=2 and k=1/4, the formula gives cg=3/5<1. Such a level does not give the intended compact unitary positive-energy theory. Thus the last bound is a statement about nontrivial unitary admissible-level representations; it cannot hold for every representation in the unrestricted algebraic formulation. At 2k+Q=0 the displayed Sugawara construction itself is undefined.
