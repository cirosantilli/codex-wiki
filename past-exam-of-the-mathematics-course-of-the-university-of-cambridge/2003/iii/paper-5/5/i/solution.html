For a finite symmetric generating alphabet Σ, the growth function of a finitely generated group is
βG,Σ​(n)=∣BΣ​(1,n)∣=#{g∈G:∣g∣Σ​≤n}​.
(21)
It counts distinct elements, not words. Relations affect which words coincide, while the resulting coarse growth type is independent of the finite generating set.
Let f:G→H be a quasi-isometry between the vertex word metrics, with constants λ,ϵ. A quasi-isometry of the metric graphs can be rounded to vertices with only bounded changes of these constants. If f(g)=f(g′), its lower distortion bound gives dG​(g,g′)≤λϵ. Thus every nonempty fibre has at most M=βG​(⌈λϵ⌉) elements, independently of its centre. Also f(BG​(1,n)) lies in the ball of radius λn+ϵ about f(1). Left translation in H preserves ball cardinalities, so
βG​(n)≤MβH​(⌈λn+ϵ⌉).
(22)
Apply the same argument to a quasi-inverse of a quasi-isometry to get the reverse comparison. Enlarging all constants to an integer K gives the two inequalities required by the paper's ≲ relation, and therefore βG​∼e​βH​. This proves quasi-isometry invariance of the stated growth class.
For the exponential upper bound, put q=∣Σ∣. Every element in the radius-n ball has a representative of length at most n. Hence
βG​(n)≤∑j=0n​qj≤(q+1)n(n≥0),
(23)
where the last inequality follows by comparison with the binomial expansion of (q+1)n; it also holds for the empty alphabet of the trivial group. Thus every finitely generated group has at most exponential growth, with no finite-relator assumption needed.
