For the classical subgroup-covering construction, assume X is path-connected, locally path-connected, and semilocally simply connected. These are the usual hypotheses underlying the classification of connected covering spaces; an arbitrary space, as literally allowed by the wording, need not admit every subgroup covering.
Construct the path-class construction of a subgroup covering directly. Consider paths α starting at x0​, and identify α,β when they have the same endpoint and the loop class [αβ−1] belongs to H. The subgroup properties make this an equivalence relation. Let Y be its set of classes and define p([α])=α(1).
Choose a path-connected open neighbourhood U of α(1) whose loops become null-homotopic in X. Such neighbourhoods form a local basis under the stated local hypotheses. Define a sheet
N([α],U)={[αη]:η is a path in U starting at α(1)}.
(2)
For each point of U the resulting class is independent of the chosen path η, since the difference is a loop in U, conjugated by α, and is trivial in π1​(X,x0​). Thus p maps this sheet bijectively onto U. Give Y the topology generated by these sheets. Restrictions to smaller such neighbourhoods show that the sheets form a basis and that their endpoint maps are homeomorphisms. Two sheets over U that intersect coincide, by concatenating and reversing paths within U; otherwise they are disjoint. Therefore p−1(U) is a disjoint union of copies of U, and p is a covering map.
The basepoint is the class of the constant path. The path α lifts to its successive prefix classes, giving a path from this basepoint to [α]; hence Y is path-connected. A loop γ lifts closed exactly when its final prefix class agrees with the constant class, namely when [γ]∈H. Consequently p∗​(π1​(Y))=H. Part (i) supplies injectivity, so
π1​(Y)≅H​.
(3)
The missing local hypothesis is substantive. In the Hawaiian earring, every neighbourhood of the common basepoint contains a whole sufficiently small circle. Its loop is nontrivial in the whole space, since retracting onto that circle preserves it. An evenly covered neighbourhood would lift that loop to a closed loop in one sheet, so the image subgroup of any covering contains a nontrivial element. Therefore the trivial subgroup cannot be realized by a simply connected covering of this space. The intended result is valid under the local covering-space hypotheses above, not for every topological space.
