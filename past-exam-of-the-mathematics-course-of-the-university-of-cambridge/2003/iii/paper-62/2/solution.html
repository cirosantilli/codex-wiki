The orbits in a self-gravitating isothermal slab separate into horizontal and vertical motions. There is no horizontal force, so x=xi​+vx​t and y=yi​+vy​t, with constant horizontal velocities. For collisionless test particles, or between gas collisions, conservation of energy fixes the vertical energy
Ez​=21​z˙2+Φ(z).
(14)
Every finite Ez​>0 has two turning points ±A, where
A=Harcosh(eEz​/(2σ02​)),Tz​=4∫0A​2[Ez​−Φ(z)]​dz​.

Thus the general orbit drifts uniformly in the plane while oscillating periodically across it. A particle with Ez​=0 stays in the midplane; an orbit with nonzero horizontal velocity is generally not closed.
Near the plane, Φ(z)=σ02​z2/H2+O(z4), so the vertical motion is a harmonic oscillator with ν2=2σ02​/H2=4πGρ0​. Far from the plane, Φ∼2σ02​∣z∣/H and the force tends to a constant magnitude. Indeed the surface density is Σ=∫ρdz=2ρ0​H, and that magnitude is 2πGΣ. Large-amplitude vertical motion is therefore approximately uniformly accelerated on each side, rather than sinusoidal. Because this ideal slab extends infinitely in the horizontal directions and its potential grows without bound as ∣z∣→∞, no finite-energy particle escapes vertically.
