There is a genuine inconsistency in this part. For any nonnegative metallicity distribution function, its maximum is at least its mean. A population of positive mean solar metallicity cannot have a maximum equal to half that mean. For example, a uniform abundance distribution on [0,2Z⊙​] has mean Z⊙​ and maximum 2Z⊙​. Thus the printed maximum-to-mean ratio cannot be proved. The following calculation explains the supplied function, gives the consistent distribution, and also treats the literal birth-metallicity interpretation separately.
Let f(μ) denote the supplied abundance function. Algebraically it is
f(μ)=(Y+1)(1−μ)Y−(Y+1)μ+μY+1​.
(25)
With δ=1−μ, a Taylor series of (1−δ)Y+1 gives
f(1−δ)=2Yδ​+6Y(1−Y)δ2​+O(δ3).
(26)
Consequently
f(1−δ)≃2Yδ​.​
(27)
This is an early-consumption expansion: its fractional correction is (1−Y)δ/3+O(δ2). Small yield alone does not make the expression linear throughout most of the gas-consumption history. At fixed 0<μ<1, the small-Y expansion instead is
f(μ)=Y[1+1−μμlogμ​]+O(Y2).
(28)
In a well-mixed closed-box model of galactic chemical evolution, the gas fraction of a galaxy also labels the permanently locked stellar mass: dMs​=−Mg,init​dμ. Newly formed stars inherit the current gas-phase metallicity Zg​(μ). Therefore the mass-weighted mean stellar metallicity satisfies the cumulative and birth stellar metallicities relation
Z∗​(μ)=1−μ1​∫μ1​Zg​(u)du,Zg​(μ)=−dμd​[(1−μ)Z∗​(μ)].
(29)
Applying the derivative to f gives
Z∗​(μ)=f(μ)⟺Zbirth​(μ)=Zg​(μ)=1−μY.​
(30)
Thus the supplied function has the natural interpretation of a cumulative mean, not an individual star's birth abundance. Direct integration of 1−uY verifies that it gives exactly f. This is the saturating closed-box enrichment law: it corresponds to effective stellar yield Y(1−Zg​), with Zg​ measured as a fraction. The usual constant-yield trace-metal model instead gives Zg​=−Ylogμ. Both agree to first order when Y∣logμ∣≪1; their exact formulae should not be identified outside that regime.
Let the final gas fraction be μf​>0 and δf​=1−μf​. In the consistent mean-metallicity interpretation, Z∗​(μf​)=Z⊙​ while the most recently formed stars have the maximum zf​=1−μfY​. At early consumption,
Z∗​≃2Yδf​​,zf​≃Yδf​,
(31)
so the corrected result is
Z∗,max​≃2Z⊙​.​
(32)
The assumption here includes δf​≪1, equivalently 2Z⊙​/Y≪1 in this approximation. Exactly, zf​=1−μfY​ and Z⊙​=f(μf​) determine the maximum implicitly; it remains larger than the mean, but its ratio to the mean need not equal two after substantial consumption.
For the mass-weighted metallicity distribution function, stars formed while the gas fraction falls by −dμ have probability dP=−dμ/δf​. Writing their individual abundance as z=1−μY gives μ=(1−z)1/Y and the change of variables
dP=F(z)dz,F(z)=Y(1−μf​)(1−z)1/Y−1​,0<z<1−μfY​.​
(33)
The density is zero outside this interval. Its cumulative probability is [1−(1−z)1/Y]/(1−μf​), so it integrates to one; integrating zF(z) reproduces f(μf​). A star-count distribution has the same form if the initial mass function and survival selection give a fixed number of observed stars per unit locked mass. Luminosity-weighted samples require different weights.
For trace abundances, (1−z)1/Y−1≃e−z/Y to leading order at fixed z/Y. The corresponding ordinary closed-box metallicity distribution is
Ftrace​(z)=Y(1−μf​)e−z/Y​,0<z<−Ylogμf​.
(34)
In the early-consumption regime zf​≪Y, this becomes approximately uniform, F≃1/(Yδf​) on [0,Yδf​]. Its mean is half its upper endpoint, again producing the corrected maximum 2Z⊙​.
If the supplied f(μ) is instead retained literally as the individual birth abundance, set q=f(μ) rather than z=1−μY. The observation-time population mean is then δf−1​∫μf​1​f(u)du, not f(μf​). Its exact distribution can be specified parametrically using
dδdf​=1−μ1−μY−f(μ)​>0,Fliteral​(f(μ))=(1−μf​)[1−μY−f(μ)]1−μ​,μf​<μ<1.​
(35)
Positivity follows because f is the average of the increasing function 1−(1−δ)Y. Its support is 0<q<f(μf​), and normalization follows directly from dP=dδ/δf​. At early consumption it is uniform with Fliteral​≃2/(Yδf​), maximum Yδf​/2 and mean Yδf​/4. Even this literal alternative gives maximum twice the population mean, never half. The distinction between birth abundance and cumulative mean resolves the calculation; the printed inverse maximum-to-mean ratio is false under either interpretation.
