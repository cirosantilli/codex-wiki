Let b≃2lp​ be the Kuhn length, N=L/b the number of statistical segments, and v their effective excluded volume. We first assume a good-solvent, strongly confined self-avoiding walk, rather than an ideal chain. A confinement blob contains g links and has diameter D∼bg3/5 in the simple v∼b3 scaling model. Therefore g∼(D/b)5/3. Distant blobs cannot freely pass through one another in a narrow tube, so the N/g blobs form an axial string. The de Gennes confinement scaling is consequently
X∼gN​D∼L(Db​)2/3∼L(Dlp​​)2/3.​
(1)
The free energy is of order kB​T per blob, not the energy of stretching a straight elastic rod.
The role of solvent and chain thickness can be made explicit by a Flory approximation: an axial extension X costs an entropic term kB​TX2/(Nb2), while the confined number density gives an interaction term kB​TvN2/(XD2). Minimizing their sum gives X3∼vN3b2/D2, hence
X∼L(bD2v​)1/3.​
(2)
For a thin statistical segment of effective diameter d, v∼b2d instead gives X∼L(bd/D2)1/3. Thus the simpler answer implicitly fixes the excluded volume; the DNA thickness and ionic environment can matter quantitatively.
Both blob arguments require b≪D≪Rfree​ and N/g≫1, where Rfree​∼bN3/5 in the simple good-solvent model. The printed comparison with L alone does not ensure confinement relative to the unconfined coil. If D≫Rfree​, the tube barely perturbs the coil. An ideal chain without excluded volume has axial spread of order Lb​ and does not acquire the extensive extension of a nonoverlapping blob string. These are distinct physical regimes, not changes to a numerical prefactor.

R∥​∼D(N/g)3/4∼bN3/4(b/D)1/4.​

Let ac​≪R be the contact radius of the adhering neutrophil. Indentation is of order ac2​/R, so the elastic strain is of order ac​/R within a region of volume ac3​. The elastic energy is consequently Gac5​/R2, while adhesion lowers the energy by order Jac2​. Balancing these terms gives
ac​∼(GJR2​)1/3,Rac​​∼(GRJ​)1/3.
(6)
This estimate requires weak adhesion, J≪GR.
For an adhesive-cell rolling threshold, assume peeling the rear bonds dissipates work of order J per unit contact area. Rolling through angle dθ sweeps contact area of order ac​Rdθ, giving resisting moment Mr​∼Jac​R. A surrounding fluid of dynamic viscosity η supplies a moment of order ηγ˙​R3. Equating moments yields
γ˙​c​∼ηR2Jac​​∼ηG1/3R4/3J4/3​.​
(7)
A dimensionless numerical coefficient depends on the contact and near-wall Stokes flow. Fluid viscosity is indispensable to a rate estimate, even though the contact model alone specifies only R,G,J.
A perfectly reversible elastic adhesive contact does not lose net energy when an unchanged contact footprint translates. It therefore does not, by itself, justify a finite rolling threshold. The above estimate assumes adhesive hysteresis or irreversible bond peeling. If equilibrium adhesion is J but the work lost during peeling and rebinding is ΔJ, retain ac​∼(JR2/G)1/3 and replace J by ΔJ only in the resisting moment: γ˙​c​∼ΔJac​/(ηR2).

Write B for the filament bending modulus and C for the filament torsional stiffness, so that the energy per unit length is (Bκ2+CΩ2)/2. For one circular filament,
B1​=EI1​=4πEr4​,C1​=Gs​J1​=2πGs​r4​,Gs​=2(1+ν)E​,
(8)
where I1​ is the second moment of area, J1​ the polar moment, Gs​ the shear modulus, and ν Poisson's ratio. Numerical twisting constants cannot be fixed by Young's modulus alone without specifying ν.
For a filled sliding bundle, N∼(R/r)2. Filaments share the centerline curvature but slide instead of accumulating the additional axial strains of a bonded cross-section. Their bending energies add: B∼NB1​∼Er2R2. If end constraints impose the same material twist on every filament, their torsional energies also add: C∼Gs​r2R2.
For a bonded, one-filament-thick circular tube, the wall thickness h is of order r. Its moments are I∼πR3h and Jp​∼2πR3h, giving B∼ErR3 and C∼Gs​rR3. For a bonded filled rod, I∼πR4/4 and Jp​∼πR4/2, giving B∼ER4 and C∼Gs​R4. Packing fractions and the convention for tube radius affect constants, not these powers.
There is an important qualification to bending and torsion of filament bundles: free axial sliding does not specify whether each filament can also spin freely. If both spin and slip are unconstrained, the common-material-twist calculation is not forced by the geometry. A helix of small twist rate has centerline curvature of order ρΩ2, so its bending energy starts at Ω4, not Ω2. A nonzero linear collective torsional stiffness then requires additional end, contact, or spin constraints.

An optical tweezers force requires the change in optical momentum. Define the optical momentum efficiency Q by F=QP/c, with all ray geometry and medium-index factors absorbed in Q and c the vacuum light speed. The specified relative refractive index does not determine Q without bead size and the beam's convergence, waist, and ray directions. A unique exact force is therefore not supplied by these data.
Retaining the entropic stiffness from part (a), the parameterized answer is
F=3.333×10−15QN,z=F/ks​=0.2684Qm.​
(23)

For u>w>0, the stall force is Fs​=(kB​T/a)log(u/w). Equivalently, the available chemical free energy per effective step is Δμ=kB​Tlog(u/w), and stall balances Fs​a=Δμ. A half-monomer step requires rates defined for that half-step, not automatically single-strand monomer rates with a gratuitous factor of two.
At exactly zero force a load on an unbounded half-line has no normalizable equilibrium gap density. The unloaded expression is the F↓0 limit, or the usual freely available-gap approximation with a weak positional confinement. For a positive load the equilibration time must remain short compared with reaction times. Load inertia, filament bending, monomer depletion, and force-dependent bond rupture have been excluded. For arbitrary finite reaction and diffusion rates one must solve the joint gap dynamics rather than attach −aw to the diffusion-limited first-passage formula: a removal changes the gap and the distribution from which the next addition starts.
