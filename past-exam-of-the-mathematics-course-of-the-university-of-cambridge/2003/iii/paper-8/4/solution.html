The Painlevé property says that the general solution has no movable critical singularities: analytic continuation around a singularity whose location depends on the initial data must not create a different branch. A fixed singularity of a complex differential equation has its location prescribed by the coefficients; a movable singularity of a complex differential equation has a location which varies with the initial data. A commonly used stronger form requires movable singularities to be poles, so that the solution is locally a meromorphic function there. Movable branch points are excluded; a movable pole is permitted. We will prove the stronger form for this equation by finding its complete general solution family as rational functions.
Use the linearization of a Riccati equation. If w′=aw2+bw+c, put w=−v′/(av) wherever a,v=0. Differentiating this expression and cancelling the quadratic terms yields
v′′−(aa′​+b)v′+acv=0.
(23)
Conversely a nonzero solution v of this linear ordinary differential equation gives a solution w, wherever the quotient is defined. Every local finite solution w at an ordinary point arises this way: solve v′=−awv with a nonzero initial value and differentiate to get the second-order equation. Multiplying v by a nonzero constant leaves w unchanged, giving the one effective constant of integration expected for a first-order Riccati equation.
For the present coefficients, a′/a=1/z+1/(z+1), so the linear ordinary differential equation simplifies to
z(z+1)v′′+(z−1)v′−v=0.
(24)

These are rational functions, single-valued meromorphic functions; hence their continuation has no branch points. Their only possible moving singularities are zeros of z2+k. A root z0​ outside {0,−1} is simple, and its residue is
Resz=z0​​w=−z0​(z0​+1)1​.

Equivalently, poles of a Riccati solution from zeros of its linearizing solution are simple at ordinary points, because a nontrivial solution of a linear ordinary differential equation cannot have both v(z0​)=0 and v′(z0​)=0. The exceptional collisions k=0 or k=−1 occur at the fixed coefficient singularities, and still give rational functions. Consequently every movable singularity is a pole, establishing the Painlevé property in its stronger meromorphic sense.
