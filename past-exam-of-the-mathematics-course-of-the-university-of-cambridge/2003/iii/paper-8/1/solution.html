Use the local coordinate t=1/λ. The differential system becomes
dtdy​=−(t4A0​​+t3A1​​+t2A2​​)y.
(1)
For the nonzero quadratic leading term, the coefficient of the local linear ordinary differential equation system has a pole of order four, so infinity is an irregular singular point of Poincaré rank three in this gauge. The matrix-valued function A(λ) itself has a pole of order two; the extra factor dλ/dt=−t−2 is essential when classifying the equation. If A0​ vanishes the degree and these orders decrease. The specified distinct eigenvalues 1,−1 give the nondegenerate unramified case.
The leading exponential function factors have actions q±​=±λ3/3, so their difference is 2λ3/3. Equal exponential magnitudes occur on
Re(λ3)=0,argλ=6π​+3kπ​(k=0,…,5).​
(2)
These are commonly called Stokes rays in the sectorial ODE convention. With the action-phase convention used by the wiki's Stokes line article they are anti-Stokes lines; the same-phase rays instead have
Im(λ3)=0,argλ=3kπ​.​
(3)
Giving both defining equations removes the nomenclature ambiguity; both sets contain six directions.
The standard construction with distinct leading eigenvalues uses six sectorial fundamental solutions per turn, with basic angular spacing π/3. They are fundamental matrices, each containing two independent column solutions. On the logarithm cover choose six consecutive overlapping sectors of width π/3+2δ, with δ>ε/2 small. Their union has angular opening 2π+2δ, covering the requested 2π+ε. One often also writes a seventh index to compare the closing sector with the first after a full turn; that is a closing copy, not a seventh independent sector in the six-sector construction. The number is a generic sectorial fundamental solution normalization count, not a universal minimum for exceptional systems with vanishing Stokes matrices: the exactly diagonal system already has one global elementary fundamental matrix.
There is a missing qualification in the requested product identity. Polynomial coefficients give an entire invertible fundamental matrix, hence trivial actual monodromy about a large circle: the circle contracts through ordinary points. But the formal matrix generally has the form GλΘeQ, and a positive turn changes its logarithm branch by the formal monodromy Mf​=e2πiΘ. This factor cannot be discarded.
To specify the printed product order, let Sj​ act on column vectors of solution coefficients across the jth sector boundary, so adjacent normalized matrices satisfy Yj+1​=Yj​Sj−1​. Six transitions give Y7​=Y1​(S6​⋯S1​)−1. The normalized formal branch after the turn is Y1​Mf​, so sectorial uniqueness gives Y7​=Y1​Mf​. Consequently
S6​S5​S4​S3​S2​S1​=Mf−1​=e−2πiΘ.​
(4)
Using Stokes matrices for right changes of fundamental matrices instead reverses/inverts the corresponding convention; the formal monodromy factor remains necessary. The formal monodromy correction to an entire-system Stokes product shows that the printed identity holds when Mf​=I, including the corrected Lax specialization discussed below.
It does not follow from polynomiality alone. For example take
A(λ)=(10​0−1​)λ2+(0−1​10​)λ+(3/22​1−3/2​).
(5)
The coefficient matching in question 2 gives Θ=21​diag(1,−1), hence Mf​=−I and the ordered Stokes matrix product is −I, not I, despite having no finite singularity. This is an explicit counterexample to the unqualified final clause.
