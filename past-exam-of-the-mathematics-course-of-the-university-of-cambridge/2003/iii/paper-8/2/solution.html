Work locally in z where u=0, and take the matrix entries literally as they appear in the original PDF. At fixed z, abbreviate
D=diag(1,−1),U=u,P=u′,V=−uzw​,R=u22u′w​,d=w+2z​.
(6)
Then A=Dλ2+(0V​U0​)λ+(dR​P−d​). Define
h=d+2UV​,θ=2UR+VP​,ρ=4UV(d+h)​−2PR​.
(7)
The literal coefficients give
h=2z​+(1−2z​)w,θ=2u(2−z)u′w​.​
(8)
In general neither h=z/2 nor θ=0 is valid for the printed system.
The normalized formal fundamental matrix is
Y=(I+λG1​​+λ2G2​​+⋯)λθDexp[(3λ3​+hλ)D],​
(9)
with a chosen logarithm branch. In particular the diagonal normal form has Λ1​=0, Λ2​=hD, Λ3​=θD. Its first two prefactor coefficients are
G1​=(ρV/2​−U/2−ρ​),​
(10)
G2​=(ρ2/2−UV/8+hθ/2(Vρ+R)/2​(Uρ−P)/2ρ2/2−UV/8−hθ/2​).​
(11)
These formulas include the diagonal terms required by the derivative of the prefactor. Setting every diagonal prefactor coefficient to zero while retaining only the displayed exponent would generally fail the differential equation.
Here is the coefficient calculation and the full recursion. Write A1​=(0V​U0​), A2​=(dR​P−d​) and G0​=I, with negative-index Gj​=0. Substitution into the gauge transformation of a linear differential system gives, for m≥1,
[D,Gm​]=−A1​Gm−1​−A2​Gm−2​+Gm−2​hD+Gm−3​[θD−(m−3)I].
(12)
At m=1, the commutator determines (G1​)12​=−U/2, (G1​)21​=V/2 and the absence of a linear diagonal term. The diagonal part at m=2 gives h=d+UV/2. The next diagonal part gives θ=(UR+VP)/2. At m=4 diagonal solvability gives −ρ=PR/2−UV(d+h)/4; the next order fixes the two diagonal entries of G2​ above. The off-diagonal entries are fixed at each stage because [D,X]12​=2X12​ and [D,X]21​=−2X21​. Later diagonal solvability fixes the remaining normalization coefficients. Thus this is a complete formal solution, not a convergence assertion. It is the formal cubic-exponential two-by-two system expansion. The printed hint's second prefactor term must have denominator λ2, and the remainder after the 1/λ term of the diagonal expansion must start at 1/λ2.
Now impose an isomonodromic deformation in the full irregular sense: its Stokes matrices and formal exponent are fixed in z, with the leading formal normalization fixed. Sectorial matrices satisfy Yj+1​=Yj​Cj​ with (Cj​)z​=0, so Bj​=(Yj​)z​Yj−1​ is the same on overlaps. Since the spectral system has no finite singularities, its fundamental matrix and inverse are entire in λ, making B entire. Differentiating the formal expression, using θz​=0, gives
B=Gz​G−1+G(hz​λD)G−1=hz​λD+hz​[G1​,D]+O(λ−1).
(13)
But [G1​,D]=A1​. The sectorial cover makes this a global growth bound, so Liouville's theorem applied to B−hz​(λD+A1​) gives the literal consequence
B=hz​(λ−zw/u​u−λ​).​
(14)
An arbitrary parameter dependence of u,w need not be isomonodromic; this analytic argument presupposes the stated constancy of generalized monodromy data.
The requested matrix would require hz​=1/2 from its diagonal and upper entry, and then its lower entry would require (z−2)w=0. On an open deformation domain this forces w≡0. Thus that requested expression is not the general deformation matrix for the printed coefficients.
There is a consistent nearby correction: replace the lower entry of A1​ by −2w/u. Then V=−2w/u, so h=z/2 and θ=0, and the same derivation proves exactly
Yz​=(λ/2−w/u​u/2−λ/2​)Y.​
(15)
For this corrected system use the same prefactor formulas with V=−2w/u: ρ=−w(w+z)/2−w(u′/u)2 and both diagonal entries of G2​ are ρ2/2+w/4. Compatibility determines the allowed u,w in the next solution. This correction is stated explicitly rather than silently substituting it for the original PDF.
