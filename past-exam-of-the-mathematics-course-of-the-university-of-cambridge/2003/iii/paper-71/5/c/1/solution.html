An optical tweezers force requires the change in optical momentum. Define the optical momentum efficiency Q by F=QP/c, with all ray geometry and medium-index factors absorbed in Q and c the vacuum light speed. The specified relative refractive index does not determine Q without bead size and the beam's convergence, waist, and ray directions. A unique exact force is therefore not supplied by these data.
Retaining the entropic stiffness from part (a), the parameterized answer is
F=3.333×10−15QN,z=F/ks​=0.2684Qm.​
(23)
Using the persistence-length-corrected worm-like chain instead gives z=1.074Qm in its small-force regime.
For a definite scale estimate, take the weak-contrast optical momentum change to be Q∼mr​−1=0.2. This gives F∼6.67×10−16N, z∼5.37cm for part (a)'s model, or z∼21.5cm for the corrected chain. These are estimates with an explicitly chosen ray-momentum efficiency, not a universal formula for a transparent trapped sphere.
Another illustrative optical model makes the uncertainty visible: normal-incidence Fresnel reflectance is R=[(mr​−1)/(mr​+1)]2=1/121. An effective reflector with Q=2R in the simple P/c momentum convention gives F=5.51×10−17N and extensions 4.44mm or 17.7mm, respectively. A refracting bead in a focused beam is not just that reflector; entry and exit rays also transfer momentum. Neither estimate eliminates the missing optical geometry.
