The printed diagram and the word “parallel” describe a Kelvin-Voigt model, not a series Maxwell fluid. In the depicted model both elements have displacement x and their forces add:
F=kx+μx˙.​
(26)
For x=aeiωt and the convention G(ω)=Famplitude​/xamplitude​,
G(ω)=k+iωμ,G′=k,G′′=ωμ.​
(27)
The storage modulus and loss modulus here are element stiffnesses with units Nm−1; conversion to stress/strain moduli requires a geometric factor. If “response” instead denotes displacement per force, it is the compliance 1/(k+iωμ). Specifying which ratio is used removes that convention ambiguity. The mean dissipated power is μω2∣a∣2/2, confirming the positive loss sign for eiωt.
For completeness, the actual series Maxwell fluid analogue has a common force and additive spring/dashpot extensions. Thus x˙=F˙/k+F/μ. With τ=μ/k,
GM​(ω)=1+iωτkiωτ​,GM′​=1+ω2τ2kω2τ2​,GM′′​=1+ω2τ2kωτ​.
(28)
These different functions cannot both describe the printed parallel arrangement. The distinction also changes the elastic beam's response to a constant load.
