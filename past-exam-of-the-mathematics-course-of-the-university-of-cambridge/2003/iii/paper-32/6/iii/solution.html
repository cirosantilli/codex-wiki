An interest-rate cap is the sum of caplets over successive reset and payment dates. Consider one accrual period [T,U] with year fraction δ>0 and strike K≥0. The reset rate is L(T;T,U)=[P(T,U)−1−1]/δ; the unit-notional payment at U is δ(L−K)+. At reset its value is
P(T,U)(P(T,U)−1−1−δK)+=(1−(1+δK)P(T,U))+.
(49)
Hence the caplet is equivalent before reset to c=1+δK puts expiring at T on the maturity-U zero-coupon bond, with strike Kb​=1/c. This algebra accounts for payment at U rather than incorrectly treating the payment date as T.
Under the T-forward measure, use the bond-ratio process Fs​=P(s,U)/P(s,T), which is a martingale. From the Gaussian HJM dynamics its relative volatility is −[Σ(s,U)−Σ(s,T)]. With deterministic volatilities its logarithm at reset is normal with integrated variance
v2=∫tT​∥Σ(s,U)−Σ(s,T)∥2ds=∫tT​​∫TU​σ(s,u)du​2ds.
(50)
For v>0, write d1​=log(Ft​/Kb​)/v+v/2 and d2​=d1​−v. A normal integral gives QT(FT​<Kb​∣Ft​)=Φ(−d2​) and EQT​[FT​1{FT​<Kb​}​∣Ft​]=Ft​Φ(−d1​). Therefore the Gaussian caplet bond-put formula is
Ct​=P(t,T)Φ(−d2​)−(1+δK)P(t,U)Φ(−d1​).​
(51)
For v=0, the value is (P(t,T)−(1+δK)P(t,U))+. This is also obtained from the Gaussian bond-option formula by put-call parity. A cap price is the sum of its caplet prices, multiplied by the notional principal.
In the Vasicek model, the instantaneous forward-rate volatility is σ(s,u)=ηe−κ(u−s). Thus
v2=2κ3η2​(1−e−κ(U−T))2(1−e−2κ(T−t)).​
(52)
Indeed, Σ(s,U)−Σ(s,T)=ηe−κ(T−s)(1−e−κ(U−T))/κ, whose squared integral gives this expression without ambiguity. Gaussian short and instantaneous forward rates do not mean the reset floating rate L is Gaussian: the latter is an inverse bond price minus one. The lognormal bond-ratio calculation, under the proper forward measure, is the reason for the normal-CDF pricing formula.
