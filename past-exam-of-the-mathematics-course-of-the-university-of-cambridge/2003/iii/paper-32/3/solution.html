(14)
The optional stopping theorem at the bounded time s∧Ta,b​ gives expectation one. The contribution from {Ta,b​>s} is at most eha−λs, and tends to zero. Taking e−λ∞=0 therefore yields
Ee−θTa,b​=e−a(b+b2+2θ​),b∈R.​

Letting θ↓0 shows that the hitting probability is one for b≤0 and e−2ab for b>0; in the latter case the hitting-time law has mass at infinity.
To derive the finite-time linear-boundary Brownian first-passage distribution, put Zs​=Ws​−bs. This is Brownian motion with drift μ=−b, and the event is {sups≤t​Zs​≥a}. For driftless Brownian motion, the Brownian reflection principle says that the joint endpoint density on this event equals the normal density pt​(x) when x≥a and the reflected density pt​(2a−x) when x<a, where pt​(x)=(2πt)−1/2e−x2/(2t). The constant-drift change of measure weights these densities by eμx−μ2t/2. Thus the crossing probability is
∫a∞​eμx−μ2t/2pt​(x)dx+∫−∞a​eμx−μ2t/2pt​(2a−x)dx.
(16)
Completing the square gives respectively Φ((μt−a)/t​) and e2μaΦ((−μt−a)/t​). Substituting μ=−b proves
P(Ta,b​≤t)=e−2abΦ(t​bt−a​)+1−Φ(t​a+bt​).​
(17)
The density weighting follows directly from the Exponential martingale for Brownian motion, or the Girsanov theorem, on the fixed finite horizon; it does not require eventual hitting.
