We prove exhaustiveness, including existence of the positive density when neither form of arbitrage occurs. Use the subspace L from part (a)(ii), and consider
K={E[νA1​]:ν>0 almost surely, E[ν∥A1​∥]<∞}⊆L.
(4)
This is a nonempty convex cone closed under positive scaling: ν0​=(1+∥A1​∥)−1 supplies one element and guarantees weighted integrability even when A1​ itself has no first moment. Crucially, K is relatively open in L; it need not be closed.
To prove openness at a point z=E[νA1​], put Z=νA1​. The vectors E[hZ], for bounded real measurable h, span L. Indeed, a vector orthogonal to all of them would satisfy E[hu⊤Z]=0 for every such h, forcing u⊤Z=0 almost surely. Since ν>0, this says u∈L⊥. Choose finitely many bounded hj​ for which E[hj​Z] form a basis of L. For coefficients tj​ sufficiently small,
ν(1+∑j​tj​hj​)>0,z+∑j​tj​E[hj​Z]∈K.
(5)
This gives a relative neighbourhood of z. It also proves the openness step behind the strictly positive barycentre cone lemma without assuming a closed moment cone.
If A0​∈/L, the second arbitrage form was already constructed. If A0​∈L∖K, the separation of a point and an open convex set, applied inside the finite-dimensional space L, gives a nonzero x∈L with x⊤A0​≤infz∈K​x⊤z. Positive scaling in the cone forces
x⊤A0​≤0,x⊤z≥0(z∈K).
(6)
We claim x⊤A1​≥0 almost surely. Otherwise E={x⊤A1​<0} has positive probability, and the admissible positive weights νm​=ν0​(1+m1E​) give
x⊤E[νm​A1​]=E[ν0​x⊤A1​]+mE[ν0​x⊤A1​1E​]⟶−∞,
(7)
contradicting the separation inequality. Since x∈L is nonzero, x⊤A1​ cannot vanish almost surely; thus it is strictly positive on an event of positive probability. This is the first arbitrage form.
Consequently absence of both forms forces A0​∈K, which supplies the required ν. Conversely either form excludes every such density by part (a). We have proved exactly one of arbitrage or a strictly positive pricing density holds. If L={0}, the same argument reduces to the elementary cases A0​=0 and A0​=0, so no nondegeneracy assumption has been hidden.
