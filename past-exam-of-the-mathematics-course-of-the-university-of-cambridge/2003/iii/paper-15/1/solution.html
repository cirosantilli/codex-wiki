We work over an algebraically closed field k, so the assertion concerns a geometric projective line. Let V=k4 and let W be the vector space of homogeneous cubic forms on V. It has dimension (36​)=20, so the parameter space of cubic equations up to nonzero scalar is P(W)=P19. The Grassmannian G=Gr(2,V) parametrizes lines in P3. Its standard graph charts are four-dimensional affine spaces, so its dimension is four. The Plücker embedding realizes it as a closed quadric in P5, proving projectivity. The general linear group GL4​ acts transitively on two-planes and is an irreducible open subset of the affine space of matrices; its orbit map proves that G is irreducible.
Consider the incidence variety
I={(ℓ,[F])∈G×P(W):F∣ℓ​=0}.
(1)
Restriction of a cubic to a fixed line is surjective onto the four-dimensional space of binary cubic forms: after choosing coordinates with that line given by x2​=x3​=0, extend any binary cubic using x0​,x1​ alone. Thus its kernel has dimension sixteen. On each Grassmannian chart these restriction maps depend algebraically on the line parameters and have constant rank four. Their kernels form a rank-sixteen vector bundle, and I is its projective bundle, with fibre P15. In particular, I is irreducible of dimension 4+15=19. It is closed in the product, since restriction vanishes precisely when four polynomial coefficients vanish.
The projection π:I→P(W) is a projective morphism, hence its image is closed. It remains to prove that this image has dimension nineteen. Equal dimensions alone would not suffice: we must control a fibre locally.
Take
F0​=x02​x2​+x12​x3​,ℓ0​={x2​=x3​=0}.
(2)
In the open Grassmannian chart around ℓ0​, every line is uniquely a graph
x2​=a0​x0​+a1​x1​,x3​=b0​x0​+b1​x1​.
(3)
Restriction of F0​ to that line is
a0​x03​+a1​x02​x1​+b0​x0​x12​+b1​x13​.
(4)
The four monomials are linearly independent over every field. Hence the only line in this chart lying on F0​ has a0​=a1​=b0​=b1​=0. The fibre π−1([F0​]) therefore has local dimension zero at (ℓ0​,[F0​]). We do not need F0​ to define a smooth surface, nor do we claim its entire fibre is finite.
The fiber dimension theorem, in its local dimension inequality, gives
0=dim(ℓ0​,[F0​])​π−1([F0​])≥dimI−dimπ(I).
(5)
Thus the closed image has dimension at least nineteen. A proper closed subset of the irreducible P19 has smaller dimension, so π(I)=P19. Every cubic equation consequently has a line in its zero set. In particular, every nonsingular cubic surface contains a line. The proof in fact establishes existence for arbitrary cubic surfaces over an algebraically closed field; it does not assert a rational line over an arbitrary nonclosed ground field.
