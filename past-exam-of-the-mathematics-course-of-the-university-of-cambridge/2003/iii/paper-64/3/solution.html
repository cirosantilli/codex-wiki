Use S(z)=σ+kx​U(z), k2=kx2​+ky2​>0 and primes for z derivatives. Time dependence is eiσt, so exponential growth means Imσ<0. The constant mass density and incompressible velocity give
ikx​u+iky​v+w′=0.
(36)
All magnetic factors below use the source convention μ0​=1.
Linearizing the ideal magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation, the advective perturbation is iSu1​+U′wex​. The magnetic force is (∇×b)×Bex​, whose x component vanishes. Thus the three component equations are
iρSu+ρU′wiρSv−B(ikx​by​−iky​bx​)iρSw−B(ikx​bz​−bx′​)​=−ikx​p1​,=−iky​p1​,=−p1′​.​
(37)
In particular the vertical equation has the pressure-gradient and magnetic signs printed in the PDF.
The incompressible ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation can be written DB/Dt=(B⋅∇)u. Linearizing it gives
iSbx​=ikx​Bu+U′bz​,iSby​=ikx​Bv,iSbz​=ikx​Bw.
(38)
For S=0, these are
bx​=Skx​B​(u−SiU′w​),by​=Skx​B​v,bz​=Skx​B​w.​
(39)
The U′bz​ term is essential: a perturbed vertical magnetic component is stretched by the background shear flow. Differentiating these amplitudes and using incompressibility also verifies ikx​bx​+iky​by​+bz′​=0.
For completeness, substitution into all three momentum equations gives
iρSu+ρU′wiρSviρSw−Sikx2​B2​w+kx​B2(Su​−S2iU′w​)′​=−ikx​p1​,=−iky​p1​+Skx​B2​(ζ−Sky​U′w​),=−p1′​,​
(40)
where ζ=ikx​v−iky​u is the vertical vorticity amplitude. Multiplying the y equation by kx​ and subtracting ky​ times the x equation eliminates pressure:
(ρS−Skx2​B2​)(ζ−Sky​U′w​)=0.
(41)
Therefore, away from ρS2=kx2​B2,
ζ=Sky​U′w​,iρSv=−iky​p1​.​
(42)
This is the nonresonant reduction relevant to the instability calculation. Division at the Alfvén wave resonance would be invalid. For a concrete counterexample to an unrestricted reading, take constant U, ky​=0, S=±kx​B/ρ​, v=a(z) and by​=kx​Ba(z)/S, with all other amplitudes zero. Every smooth a(z) solves the original linear system, but ζ=ikx​a(z) need not vanish. These transverse resonant waves do not drive w. A genuinely growing mode in a real background has complex S and cannot satisfy either S=0 or S2=kx2​B2/ρ on the real z axis. This explains exactly when the requested reductions may be used.
On that nonresonant branch, multiply the horizontal equations by kx​ and ky​ and add. Incompressibility gives kx​u+ky​v=iw′, so
−ρSw′+ρkx​U′w=−ik2p1​,ik2p1​=ρSw′−ρkx​U′w.​
(43)
Combining incompressibility with vorticity, multiply its first equation by kx​ and subtract ky​ times the definition of ζ. This gives
ik2u=−kx​w′−ky​ζ=−(kx​w′+Sky2​U′​w).​
(44)
Consequently
Su​−S2iU′w​=k2ikx​​(Sw′​−S2kx​U′w​)=k2ikx​​(Sw​)′.
(45)
Substitute this and the derivative of the displayed pressure relation into vertical momentum. Multiplication by k2/i gives
[ρSw′−ρkx​U′w]′=k2ρSw+kx2​B2[(Sw​)′′−Sk2w​].
(46)
Expanding the second derivative once gives exactly the requested vertical-velocity equation:
[ρSw′−ρkx​U′w]′​=k2ρSw+kx2​B2[(Sw′​)′−Sk2w​]−kx3​B2(S2U′w​)′.​​
(47)
To take the discontinuous-layer limit without multiplying distributions, introduce f=w/S. It is proportional to the normal displacement: the material kinematic condition is w=iSη, so f=iη. Since S′=kx​U′, Sw′−S′w=S2f′. The same equation becomes the displacement equation for a parallel magnetic shear flow
[(ρS2−kx2​B2)f′]′−k2(ρS2−kx2​B2)f=0.​
(48)
The physical interface condition is continuity of f, because both fluids move the same interface. For constant Uj​ on each side, away from resonance the coefficient is constant and nonzero, so f′′−k2f=0. Requiring decay at infinity and matching f(0) yields
w(z)={A(σ+kx​U2​)e−kz,A(σ+kx​U1​)ekz,​z>0,z<0.​​
(49)
The exponent is z, as in the actual PDF; the converted TeX's x there is a transcription error.
Integrate the conservative displacement equation across a layer of thickness 2ε. Its undifferentiated term has an integral tending to zero, while the derivative gives
[(ρS2−kx2​B2)f′]0−0+​=0.
(50)
Equivalently, the perturbed magnetohydrodynamic total pressure is p1​+Bbx​=(ρS2−kx2​B2)f′/(ik2) and is continuous. With f+′​=−kA and f−′​=kA, a nontrivial amplitude requires
ρ(σ+kx​U2​)2+ρ(σ+kx​U1​)2=2kx2​B2.​
(51)
This is also the rigorous thin-layer integration of the original vertical equation, after combining the derivative terms before taking the limit.
Put U=(U1​+U2​)/2 and ΔU=U2​−U1​. Completing the square gives the dispersion relation
σ=−kx​U±kx​ρB2​−4(ΔU)2​​.​
(52)
For (ΔU)2<4B2/ρ both interface frequencies are real, so there is no exponentially growing interface mode. Above that threshold, the growing sign has growth rate ∣kx​∣(ΔU)2/4−B2/ρ​. This is magnetic stabilization of an equal-density vortex sheet: magnetic tension from bending the field overcomes the kinetic shear driving Kelvin-Helmholtz instability. Equality is marginal and the strict stable condition avoids its double-root degeneracy. The case kx​=0 has no streamwise shear drive and has the neutral interface frequency σ=0; the nonzero-frequency elimination should be interpreted by the original equations or a limiting displacement formulation in that case. The horizontal ky​ affects the exponential decay length through k, but not the stated threshold.
