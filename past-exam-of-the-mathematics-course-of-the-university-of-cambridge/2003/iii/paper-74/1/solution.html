Fluid-loaded plate. Use complex amplitudes with time dependence e−iωt and take the velocity potential in the fluid above the elastic plate to be Φ. Let ρ0​ and c0​ be the undisturbed mass density and speed of sound. The linearized Euler equations give p=iρ0​ωΦ; the kinematic boundary condition is Φy​(x,0)=−iωη(x). We model loading from one fluid half-space, with no fluid loading on the other face. Loading by identical fluids on both faces instead doubles the fluid term below.
Define the Fourier transform and its inverse by
f​(k)=∫R​f(x)e−ikxdx,f(x)=2π1​∫C​f​(k)eikxdk.
(1)
The Sommerfeld radiation condition selects Φ(k,y)=Φ(k,0)e−γy, with γ2=k2−k02​ and k0​=ω/c0​. Here and in the diffraction calculation below, the outgoing square root is specified by first taking Imω>0. Draw a vertical cut upwards from +k0​ and another downwards from −k0​, and put
γ=γu​γl​,γu​=k+k0​​,−2π​<arg(k+k0​)<23π​,γl​=k−k0​​,−23π​<arg(k−k0​)<2π​.
(2)
Thus γu​ is analytic in the upper half-plane and γl​ in the lower half-plane. In the real-frequency limit,
γ=k2−k02​​>0(∣k∣>k0​),γ=−ik02​−k2​(∣k∣<k0​).
(3)
The Fourier transform contour C follows the real axis with the limiting-absorption pole prescriptions. In particular, an outgoing propagating component has positive vertical wavenumber.
The kinematic boundary condition gives Φ(0)=iωη​/γ, so p​(0)=−ρ0​ω2η​/γ. Eliminating the fluid pressure from the bending balance gives the line-forced fluid-loaded bending plate kernel
K(k)=βk4−mω2−γρ0​ω2​,D(k)=γK(k).
(4)
For harmonic line force amplitude F0​ at the origin, the plate displacement and fluid velocity potential are therefore
η(x)=2πF0​​∫C​D(k)γ(k)eikx​dk​,Φ(x,y)=2πiωF0​​∫C​D(k)eikx−γy​dk​.
(5)
An arbitrary localized loading replaces F0​ by its Fourier transform. This representation both calculates the response and identifies its different wave components through poles and branch points.
For an observer x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ, with 0<θ<π, the method of steepest descent uses the saddle point ks​=k0​cosθ. At fixed angle away from grazing, the acoustic far field is
Φrad​∼D(ks​)iωF0​sinθ​2πrk0​​​eik0​r−iπ/4,prad​=iρ0​ωΦrad​.
(6)
The r−1/2 spreading is that of cylindrical acoustic waves. The phase velocity parallel to the plate is ω/∣k∣: components with ∣k∣<k0​ are supersonic in that sense and radiate, whereas those with ∣k∣>k0​ are subsonic and exponentially decay normally to the plate. The latter have reactive fluid loading and zero mean normal acoustic energy flux.
There is also a real guided root κ>k0​. Indeed, K(k0​+)=−∞, K(+∞)=+∞, and
K′(k)=4βk3+γ3ρ0​ω2k​>0(k>k0​).
(7)
The residue theorem then gives an outgoing displacement contribution iF0​eiκ∣x∣/K′(κ). It transports energy along the plate while its fluid field decays as e−γ(κ)y. Other zeros on continued sheets can describe leaky or resonant waves; branch point contributions describe near and grazing fields. A fixed-angle acoustic far field expansion is not uniform near the plate, where these components can matter.
For a propagating spectral component, write ℓ=(k02​−k2)1/2. Its upward normal velocity is iℓΦ and its pressure is iρ0​ωΦ. Applying Parseval's identity to the time-averaged acoustic energy flux gives the upward power per unit length of the forcing line:
Py​=4πρ0​ω3∣F0​∣2​∫−k0​k0​​∣D(k)∣2k02​−k2​​dk=4πρ0​ω3k02​∣F0​∣2​∫0π​∣D(k0​cosθ)∣2sin2θ​dθ​.
(8)
Equivalently, the integrand is ∣η​∣2/ℓ with prefactor ρ0​ω3/(4π). Integrating the radial acoustic intensity of the far field over a semicircle gives the same expression. The input power ωIm(F0∗​η(0))/2 also feeds guided waves along the plate, so it must not be identified with Py​ alone.
Rigid half-plane diffraction. Suppress the time factor and assume 0<θ0​<π. Write a=k0​cosθ0​, b=k0​sinθ0​, and denote the incident spatial field by I=e−iax−iby. The Helmholtz equation, Sommerfeld radiation condition, and square-root branches are as above. Across the open positive axis the scattered field and its normal derivative are continuous. Across the rigid negative axis its normal derivative is also continuous, while its potential can jump. Let J(x) be this jump, supported on x<0. The transformed scattered field is
ϕ​(k,y)=21​sgn(y)Ju​(k)e−γ∣y∣.
(9)
The jump transform is analytic in the upper half-plane. The absence of an even scattered component follows from derivative continuity and the outgoing condition. Its normal derivative on either face is −γJu​/2.
On the negative axis this derivative must cancel −ibe−iax. Since the Half-range Fourier transform of H(−x)e−iax is i/(k+a), the Wiener-Hopf equation has the form
γu​γl​Ju​=k+a2b​+Gl​(k),
(10)
where Gl​ is the transform of an unknown function supported on x>0 and analytic in the lower half-plane. The incident pole k=−a lies below the Fourier contour. Divide by γl​ and perform pole subtraction in a Wiener-Hopf equation:
(k+a)γl​(k)2b​=(k+a)γl​(−a)2b​+k+a2b​(γl​(k)1​−γl​(−a)1​).
(11)
The first term is upper analytic and the second lower analytic, since the latter's apparent pole cancels. Equality of the two continued sides gives an entire function. The finite-energy edge condition, with J(x)=O(−x​), and the decay of the transforms at infinity force that function to vanish by Liouville's theorem. Consequently
Ju​(k)=γl​(−a)(k+a)γu​(k)2b​,ϕ(x,y)=2πγl​(−a)sgn(y)b​∫C​(k+a)γu​(k)eikx−γ∣y∣​dk​.
(12)
This is the Wiener-Hopf solution of rigid half-plane diffraction with the present transform and time conventions.
Deforming to a steepest descent contour gives a residue from the incident pole precisely when x+∣y∣cotθ0​<0. At that pole γ(−a)=−ib; its residue produces
ϕGO​=sgn(y)e−iax+ib∣y∣H(−x−∣y∣cotθ0​).
(13)
Thus the total geometrical optics field is I plus a reflected wave in the upper reflection region; in the lower shadow region the scattered residue cancels I, and outside that shadow the direct incident wave remains. This gives both the amplitude and the spatial support of the geometrical optics fields rather than merely naming a reflected wave.
For x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ, −π<θ<π, the saddle contribution uses γl​(−a)=−i2k0​​cos(θ0​/2) and γu​(ks​)=2k0​​cos(θ/2). The diffracted wave is
ϕd​∼2πk0​r​eik0​r+iπ/4​cosθ+cosθ0​2sin(θ/2)sin(θ0​/2)​=22πk0​r​eik0​r+iπ/4​[sec2θ+θ0​​−sec2θ−θ0​​]​.
(14)
The total field is I+ϕGO​+ϕd​ to this order. This far-field expression applies away from the shadow/reflection boundaries ∣θ∣=π−θ0​. There the pole and saddle coalesce, and a uniform Fresnel integral approximation replaces the divergent separate terms by a smooth transition. The exact contour integral remains well defined.
