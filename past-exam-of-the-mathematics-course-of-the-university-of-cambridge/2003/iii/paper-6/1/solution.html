Put C=R/(1−k). The hypothesis scales to every residual r∈F: if r=0, apply it to r/∥r∥ and multiply the resulting vector by ∥r∥ to obtain an approximate preimage of norm at most R∥r∥ with error at most k∥r∥. For r=0, choose zero.
Start with r0​=y. Inductively choose uj​∈E so that
∥uj​∥≤R∥rj−1​∥,rj​=rj−1​−Tuj​,∥rj​∥≤k∥rj−1​∥.
(1)
Then ∥rj​∥≤kj∥y∥ and ∑j​∥uj​∥≤R∥y∥/(1−k). Completeness of the Banach space E gives x=∑j​uj​. Boundedness of T gives
∥Tx−y∥≤∥T∥​x−∑j=1n​uj​​+∥rn​∥⟶0.
(2)
Thus
Tx=y,∥x∥≤1−kR​∥y∥​.
(3)
This geometric correction for approximate surjectivity uses no completeness assumption on F: every infinite sum was formed in E.
To establish the completeness forced by uniformly bounded lifting, let (yn​) be a Cauchy sequence in F. Choose a subsequence (ynj​​) with ∥ynj+1​​−ynj​​∥≤2−j. Lift yn1​​ to v0​∈E and each difference to vj​∈E with Tvj​=ynj+1​​−ynj​​ and ∥vj​∥≤C2−j. The series v0​+∑j​vj​ converges in E, and its image under T is the limit of the subsequence. The original Cauchy sequence has that same limit, by the triangle inequality. Hence F is complete.
The open mapping theorem states that a bounded surjective linear map between Banach spaces is open. In particular, a bounded linear bijection has a bounded inverse. This is the theorem the question permits us to state without proof.
For the closed graph theorem, suppose T:E→F is everywhere-defined and linear with closed graph G(T). The product norm ∥(x,y)∥=∥x∥+∥y∥ makes E×F a Banach space, so its closed subspace G(T) is complete. Projection π:G(T)→E, (x,Tx)↦x, is a bounded linear bijection. Its inverse is bounded by the open mapping theorem; therefore
∥x∥+∥Tx∥=∥π−1x∥≤C1​∥x∥.
(4)
It follows that T is bounded, proving the closed graph theorem rather than assuming it.
For the final clause, S is the separating space of a linear map. It contains zero, and if xn​→0, Txn​→y and zn​→0, Tzn​→w, then αxn​+βzn​→0 and its image tends to αy+βw. Thus S is a vector subspace. If yj​∈S and yj​→y, choose one vector wj​ from a witnessing sequence for yj​ with
∥wj​∥<1/j,∥Twj​−yj​∥<1/j.
(5)
Then wj​→0 and Twj​→y, so y∈S: it is closed. If T is continuous, every defining image limit is zero, hence S={0}. Conversely, if S={0} and xn​→x, Txn​→y, then xn​−x→0 and T(xn​−x)→y−Tx∈S, so y=Tx. The graph is sequentially closed and therefore closed in the normed, metrizable product. The proved closed graph theorem now gives continuity. Consequently
S is a closed linear subspace of F,T is continuous⟺S={0}​.
(6)
