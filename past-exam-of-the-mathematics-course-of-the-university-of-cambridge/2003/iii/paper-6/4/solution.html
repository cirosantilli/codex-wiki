Write K=σA​(x) and R(λ)=(λ1−x)−1. Choose a bounded finite union of small polygonal regions V with K⊂V, V⊂U and ∂V∩K=∅. Its positively oriented boundary cycle Γ has winding number one on K and zero outside V; boundaries of holes have negative orientation. Such regions can be obtained from a sufficiently fine grid around the compact set K. This accommodates disconnected spectra and neighborhoods with holes.
Define the holomorphic functional calculus by the norm-convergent contour integral
Θx​(f)=2πi1​∫Γ​f(λ)R(λ)dλ.
(21)
The integrand is continuous on a finite rectifiable cycle, so the integral exists in the Banach space A. Its value is independent of the chosen cycle: on U∖K the integrand is holomorphic, and cycles with the stated winding numbers are homologous there. Apply the scalar Cauchy integral theorem to every bounded linear functional and then use the Hahn-Banach theorem to recover equality of the algebra-valued integrals. Equivalently, a common refinement of the polygonal regions gives the same contour-deformation argument.
Linearity is immediate. For the entire functions 1,Z, enlarge the integration cycle to a sufficiently large circle in the resolvent set, using their entire extension. Integrating the uniformly convergent Neumann series term by term gives
Θx​(1)=1,Θx​(Z)=x.
(22)
To prove multiplicativity, choose two nested admissible cycles, Γo​ outside Γi​, both surrounding K and lying in U. The resolvent identity gives
R(λ)R(μ)=λ−μR(μ)−R(λ)​.
(23)
Use Γo​ for f and Γi​ for g, multiply the two integrals, and insert this identity. In the term with R(μ), the outer Cauchy integral formula replaces f(λ)/(λ−μ) by f(μ). In the term with R(λ), the inner integral of g(μ)/(λ−μ) is zero because λ is outside the inner region. Thus
Θx​(f)Θx​(g)=2πi1​∫Γi​​f(μ)g(μ)R(μ)dμ=Θx​(fg).
(24)
The exchanges of integrals are justified by continuous integrands on compact separated cycles. This proves the unital algebra-homomorphism property, not just an identity of algebra character values.
For continuity in local uniform convergence, fix one cycle and bound
∥Θx​(f)∥≤2πlengthΓ​supΓ​∥R(λ)∥supΓ​∣f(λ)∣.
(25)
The last supremum is a defining compact-set seminorm of O(U). Hence the map is continuous.
If another continuous unital algebra homomorphism Ψ sends Z to x, its values on polynomials are forced. For every ζ∈/U, the function (Z−ζ)−1 exists in O(U), so multiplicativity forces
Ψ((Z−ζ)−1)=(x−ζ1)−1.
(26)
Therefore Ψ and Θx​ agree on all rational functions with poles outside U. The permitted Runge theorem makes these functions dense in O(U) for local uniform convergence, including when U is disconnected. Continuity makes the maps agree everywhere. Thus
Θx​ is the unique continuous unital algebra homomorphism with Θx​(Z)=x​.
(27)
This proves the continuity and uniqueness of holomorphic functional calculus with the exact topology required.
For an algebra character ϕ, multiplicativity and nonzeroness give ϕ(1)=1. An invertible element cannot have zero algebra character value, so ϕ(x)∈σA​(x). The same observation for each algebra element and the spectral bound from Question 3 gives ∣ϕ(b)∣≤∥b∥; thus a algebra character is automatically a bounded linear functional. In particular,
ϕ(R(λ))=λ−ϕ(x)1​.
(28)
Apply ϕ inside the contour integral and use the Cauchy integral formula with winding number one at ϕ(x):
ϕ(Θx​(f))=f(ϕ(x))​.
(29)
No separation of algebra elements by algebra characters is used; such separation can fail for a general commutative Banach algebra.
For the final clause, the two closed spectral pieces X,Y are compact and have positive distance. Choose disjoint open neighborhoods UX​,UY​ and let f be identically one on UX​ and zero on UY​. This locally constant function is holomorphic on their union, and f2=f. Set e=Θa​(f). Multiplicativity gives e2=e, and the proved algebra character formula gives
ϕ(e)={1,0,​ϕ(a)∈X,ϕ(a)∈Y.​​
(30)
This is the spectral idempotent from a separated spectrum. It is also nontrivial without assuming that algebra characters separate points. If e=0, choose λ∈X and define g=0 on UX​, g(z)=1/(z−λ) on UY​. Then (Z−λ)g=1−f, so (a−λ1)Θa​(g)=1, with commuting factors, making a−λ1 invertible despite λ∈σA​(a). This is impossible. If e=1, exchange the two pieces and use λ∈Y for the analogous contradiction. Hence e=0,1.
