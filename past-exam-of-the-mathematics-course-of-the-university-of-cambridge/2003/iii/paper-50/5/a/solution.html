Use units kB​=ℏ=c=1. In critical light-cone gauge in string theory, each chiral sector of the closed bosonic string has 24 transverse string oscillator species. Their generating function is
F(q)=∏m≥1​(1−qm)−24=∑N≥0​dN​qN.
(40)
Each factor sums the independent occupation numbers of the string oscillators at level m. For q=e−t with t↓0, the modular transformation of the Dedekind eta function gives
F(e−t)∼(2πt​)12exp(t4π2​−t).
(41)
Extract the coefficient by a contour integral and apply the saddle-point approximation to the exponent (N−1)t+4π2/t. The saddle is t∗​=2π/N−1​, with exponent 4πN−1​ and second derivative (N−1)3/2/π. The factor t12 gives (N−1)−6, and the Gaussian width gives (N−1)−3/4/2​. Hence
dN​∼2​1​(N−1)−27/4e4πN−1​.
(42)
For a noncompact closed string, closed-string level matching imposes equal left and right levels, but does not correlate their transverse polarizations. Therefore the large-level degeneracy of a closed bosonic string is
gN​=dN2​∼21​(N−1)−27/2e8πN−1​.​
(43)
The mass relation is α′M2=4(N−1). Thus 8πN−1​=βH​M with
βH​=4πα′​,TH​=4πα′​1​.​
(44)
Distinguish degeneracy per discrete level from a smoothed density per unit rest mass. Since dN/dM=α′M/2, the latter is
ρ(M)∼CM−26eβH​M.
(45)
The exponential growth comes from the many ways of distributing string oscillator energy along a string; it is much faster than a fixed finite collection of particle species. The corresponding high-energy entropy is S(E)=βH​E+O(logE), so its microcanonical temperature tends to TH​. Additional energy can go into very long strings rather than raising the temperature substantially.
The Boltzmann suppression competes with this exponential density. For the string oscillator rest-mass sum alone the large-M tail is ∫dMM−26e−(β−βH​)M. It converges for β>βH​ and fails for β<βH​. To include translational states in 25 noncompact spatial dimensions, take the one-string partition function per unit spatial volume. At large M,
∫d25pe−βM2+p2​∼(β2πM​)25/2e−βM.
(46)
This follows by expanding the energy as M+p2/(2M)+⋯ and doing a Gaussian momentum integral. Its high-mass contribution is consequently proportional to
∫M0​∞​dMM−27/2e−(β−βH​)M.
(47)
This integral is finite at β=βH​, although nonanalytic there: its first twelve derivatives in β are finite, while the thirteenth diverges. The endpoint expansion contains a term proportional to (β−βH​)25/2. Thus one must not assert that the canonical partition function necessarily diverges exactly at TH​; the polynomial prefactor and the spatial spectrum matter. This is the finite canonical partition function at the bosonic Hagedorn threshold distinction. The multi-string ideal gas has the same leading Hagedorn threshold, since the higher occupation-cycle terms probe ℓβ, ℓ≥2, and are nonsingular at βH​.
There is also a spacetime diagnosis. Euclidean temperature makes time a circle of radius Rβ​=β/(2π). A string oscillator ground state winding once around that circle, with zero momentum number, has
mth2​=α′2Rβ2​​−α′4​=4π2α′2β2​−α′4​.
(48)
It becomes massless at βH​ and tachyonic for T>TH​. This thermal winding instability of a closed bosonic string signals failure of the perturbative low-temperature gas and motivates a transition to a different high-energy phase, often described in terms of long-string or winding-mode condensation. Free state counting fixes the threshold and its singular behavior, not the interacting phase's endpoint or the order of a transition.
Finally, the ordinary bosonic tachyon is already an infrared instability at zero temperature. The above canonical discussion is a formal analysis of the high-level spectrum with that separate infrared problem regulated. It does not establish a stable thermal vacuum of the unmodified bosonic theory; an actual equilibrium transition requires an appropriate stable setting and control of interactions.
