The first massless level of a closed string contains a spin-two field, an antisymmetric two-form and a scalar: the graviton, Kalb–Ramond field and dilaton. The polarization redundancies found in Question 2 become, in position space, the linearized transformations δGμν​=∂μ​ξν​+∂ν​ξμ​ and δBμν​=∂μ​Λν​−∂ν​Λμ​. Thus the massless spin-two field already carries the gauge symmetry of linearized general relativity.

Combining this with the metric variation yields the Ricci equation. Thus the string's quantum conformal consistency reproduces target-space gravity coupled to matter. Equivalently, expanding string scattering amplitudes at small momenta gives the same action: massless exchange poles are reproduced by these fields, while analytic terms from massive exchange become local higher-derivative interactions.

The Ricci term is now the Einstein-Hilbert action; its metric variation gives the Einstein field equations with stress-energy tensor from the scalar and two-form. The leading low-energy theory is general relativity coupled to the massless string fields.
The approximation requires characteristic momenta Eα′​≪1 and small curvature in string units. Massive string modes have masses of order 1/α′​ and can be integrated out, generating corrections with extra powers of α′E2 or α′R. The string genus expansion supplies independent loop corrections controlled by gs​=eΦ0​; at weak coupling tree-level gravity dominates. Dimensional analysis gives κ262​∝gs2​(α′)12.
Compactification supplies lower-dimensional gravity and additional matter: metric and two-form components give gauge fields, internal geometry gives scalar moduli, and light modes of Kaluza-Klein theory must be kept if their masses lie below the chosen cutoff. Open strings add gauge fields, and supersymmetric string theories add spacetime fermions. Their particular matter spectrum depends on the compactification and projection; universal massless spin-two interactions retain the gravitational form. Finally, the bosonic massless-sector action is a formal truncation around a tachyonically unstable vacuum. It does not remove that instability. Tachyon-free superstring backgrounds provide stable applications of this low-energy gravitational interpretation.
