Use a mostly-plus Minkowski metric and string tension T=(2πα′)−1. The Polyakov action is
SP​=−4πα′1​∫Σ​d2σ−h​hab∂a​Xμ∂b​Xνημν​.
(1)
Varying the auxiliary metric sets its worldsheet stress-energy tensor to zero and makes h conformal to the induced metric. Substitution gives the equivalent Nambu–Goto action −T∫−det(∂a​X⋅∂b​X)​. The string coupling is separate from T. A constant dilaton contributes SΦ​=Φ0​χ(Σ) to the Euclidean action, by the Gauss-Bonnet theorem, including its boundary term when necessary. With gs​=eΦ0​, a connected worldsheet has weight gs−χ​. For an oriented closed surface of genus g, this is gs2g−2​; with b boundaries it is gs2g+b−2​. Normalizing each external closed string vertex with a factor gs​ gives gs2g+M−2​ for M external closed strings. This is the string genus expansion.
The local gauge symmetries are worldsheet diffeomorphisms, under which X is a scalar field, and Weyl transformations hab​↦e2ω(σ)hab​. Two coordinate functions and one Weyl function locally fix the three components of the two-dimensional metric, giving conformal gauge hab​=e2ωηab​ and then ω=0. Globally one instead chooses a fiducial metric and integrates over the worldsheet moduli; conformal Killing transformations can remain. Target-space Poincaré transformations are global symmetries, not additional local gauge freedoms.
For an infinitesimal coordinate change v, the trace-free metric variation is
(P1​v)ab​=∇a​vb​+∇b​va​−hab​∇c​vc.
(2)
After the Weyl trace is removed, gauge fixing produces the Faddeev-Popov determinant of this operator. An anticommuting vector ca and symmetric traceless tensor bab​ represent that determinant through a Grassmann Gaussian integral. The resulting worldsheet ghost action is, up to field-normalization conventions,
Sbc​=2π1​∫d2σ∣h∣​bab(P1​c)ab​.
(3)
In complex coordinates this is a sum of chiral b∂ˉc and antichiral b∂c terms. The worldsheet ghost fields have conformal weights two and minus one. Their central charge is −26, while the d free embedding bosons contribute d. In the critical dimension the total anomaly cancels, consistently with the stated nilpotence of the BRST charge.
Physical states are BRST cohomology classes at the appropriate ghost number: kerQ/imQ, with Q∣ϕ⟩=0 and ∣ϕ⟩∼∣ϕ⟩+Q∣χ⟩. Nilpotence ensures that an exact state is closed and that the equivalence is well-defined. For the usual open string representatives the physical ghost number is one. BRST-exact excitations describe gauge or null directions rather than additional physical particles.
To calculate the ghost oscillator Virasoro generators, use the elementary fermionic identities
[AB,C]=A{B,C}−{A,C}B
(4)
when A,B,C are odd. In the first commutator only {c−k​,bn​}=δk,n​ survives, giving
[Lm(b,c)​,bn​]=∑k​(m−k)bm+k​δk,n​=(m−n)bm+n​​.
(5)
In the second only {bm+k​,cn​}=δm+k+n,0​ survives, giving
[Lm(b,c)​,cn​]=−∑k​(m−k)δm+k+n,0​c−k​=−(2m+n)cm+n​​.
(6)
Normal-ordering constants commute with the string oscillators and do not change these results.
For the last request choose the ghost oscillator vacuum ∣↓⟩ with bm>0​∣↓⟩=cm>0​∣↓⟩=b0​∣↓⟩=0. The given conditions restrict a general state to ∣ϕ⟩=∣Ψ⟩⊗∣↓⟩ with no nonzero ghost excitations. Indeed, the positive modes annihilate both members of each creation–annihilation pair, and b0​ selects one member of the ghost zero-mode doublet. Use the ghost Virasoro zero-mode convention compatible with the displayed explicit −c0​ term. Reordering the charge gives
Q=∑m​c−m​Lmα​−21​∑m,n​(m−n):c−m​c−n​bm+n​:−c0​.
(7)
The normal-ordered cubic term annihilates ∣↓⟩. If both c modes are creators, m,n≥0, the bm+n​ mode is an annihilator, including b0​. If either c is an annihilator, normal ordering puts an annihilator on the right. Consequently
Q∣ϕ⟩=c0​(L0α​−1)∣ϕ⟩+∑m>0​c−m​Lmα​∣ϕ⟩.
(8)
These ghost states are independent. Acting with b0​ isolates (L0α​−1)∣Ψ⟩, while bm​, m>0, isolates Lmα​∣Ψ⟩. Thus closure is equivalent to
(L0α​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0,Lmα​∣Ψ⟩=0(m>0).​
(9)
Conversely these conditions make every term in Q∣ϕ⟩ vanish. They are exactly the physical-state Virasoro conditions for an open string; the cohomology quotient additionally identifies physically equivalent representatives.
