Separate the constant embedding coordinate from its nonzero modes. Its integral gives the momentum-conservation delta function; below this is understood and absorbed into the amplitude normalization on the momentum-conserving locus. For the nonzero modes the Euclidean Polyakov action is quadratic, with worldsheet Green function
⟨Xμ(z)Xν(w)⟩=−2α′​ημνlog∣z−w∣2.
(20)
Treat the tachyon vertex operators as normal ordered, removing coincident self-contractions. The Gaussian functional integral, or the same calculation by Wick's theorem, gives
⟨∏r​:eikr​⋅X(zr​):⟩=exp(−∑r<s​kr​⋅ks​⟨X(zr​)X(zs​)⟩)=∏r<s​∣zr​−zs​∣α′kr​⋅ks​.

All remaining determinants are independent of the insertion points and momenta and enter the overall constant. This derives the Koba-Nielsen factor and the requested position integral.
For the Möbius transformation f(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d), ad−bc=1, one has
f(zr​)−f(zs​)=(czr​+d)(czs​+d)zr​−zs​​,d2f(zr​)=∣czr​+d∣−4d2zr​.

The transformed Koba-Nielsen factor contributes, at insertion r, the power −α′∑s=r​kr​⋅ks​ of ∣czr​+d∣. Four-momentum conservation makes this power α′kr2​. The complete differential integrand therefore transforms by
∏r​∣czr​+d∣α′kr2​−4.

It is invariant when α′kr2​=4 for every external tachyon. The correlator alone is covariant; the integration measures are necessary for invariance. The group acting faithfully on the sphere is PSL(2,C), with the two elements ±I of SL(2,C) acting identically.
The apparent integral over all positions includes the residual conformal-group gauge volume. Divide by it and fix three distinct insertions. The Faddeev-Popov determinant, equivalently the three-point worldsheet ghost correlator and its conjugate, contributes
J=∣(z1​−z2​)(z2​−z4​)(z4​−z1​)∣2.
(24)
Set z1​=Λ, z2​=0, z4​=1, then send Λ to infinity on the sphere; the sign of the real direction is irrelevant. The factors involving z1​ behave as ∣Λ∣α′k1​⋅∑s=1​ks​=∣Λ∣−4, while J∼∣Λ∣4. They cancel, leaving z3​=z as the one integration variable.
The remaining distance exponents are
α′k2​⋅k3​=−4−2α′t​,α′k3​⋅k4​=−4−2α′s​.
(25)
For example (k2​+k3​)2=(k1​+k4​)2=−t and each individual squared momentum is 4/α′. Also
s+t+u=−∑r=14​kr2​=−α′16​.

∫C​d2z∣z∣−4−α′t/2∣1−z∣−4−α′s/2.

Apply the supplied complex beta integral with A=4+α′t/2, B=4+α′s/2, and C=4−A−B=4+α′u/2. Its numerator arguments become −1−α′t/4, −1−α′s/4, −1−α′u/4, while its denominator arguments become 2+α′u/4, 2+α′t/4, 2+α′s/4. Thus the four-point amplitude is the Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude
T4​=C0​Γ(2+α′s/4)Γ(2+α′t/4)Γ(2+α′u/4)Γ(−1−α′s/4)Γ(−1−α′t/4)Γ(−1−α′u/4)​,​
(28)
where C0​ is momentum-independent. The integral converges initially when ReA<2, ReB<2, and Re(A+B)>2; the displayed answer elsewhere is its analytic continuation.
The gamma function has simple poles at nonpositive integers and no zeros. At generic fixed t, the amplitude therefore has simple s-channel poles at s=4(n−1)/α′, n=0,1,2,…, with the same towers in t and u by crossing symmetry. They describe exchange of the closed string tachyon, massless states, and the infinitely many massive levels. The reciprocal denominator factors can give zeros at negative values s=−4(ℓ+2)/α′; special kinematics can also make an exchange residue vanish.
To see the structure of a generic residue, set a=−1−α′s/4, b=−1−α′t/4, c=−1−α′u/4, so a+b+c=1. At a=−n, the Gamma function recurrence reduces the remaining factors to a constant times ∏j=1n​(b−j)2. The Virasoro–Shapiro amplitude pole residue is thus a polynomial of degree 2n in the other channel invariant, consistent with exchange up to spin 2n. Crossed numerator poles must not simply be multiplied: near a=−n+δ and b=−m+ε, c=1+n+m−δ−ε, and the zero of 1/Γ(1−c) cancels the putative double pole. The leading singular part is proportional to
(nn+m​)2(δ1​+ε1​).
(29)
The tree amplitude is meromorphic, with no loop threshold branch cuts; its channel singularities express factorization and string dual resonance.
