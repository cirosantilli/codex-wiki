Retain β=1−V2/c2​>0, but normalize the complex potential as F=βH, so that w=ReF/β. The Cauchy-Riemann equations give wx​=ReF′/β and wy​=−ImF′/β. Since ∂x2​​=β∂y​, the two stresses satisfy
βσ13​−iσ23​=μF′(z).​
(27)
Using the same unscaled H from part (a) would instead put an additional β on its derivative; the potential normalization matters.
Take the principal branch of z​, with its branch cut along the negative real axis and positive x​ for x>0. Write Q=F′. For the antisymmetric crack-opening response, reflection gives Q−(s)=−Q+(s)​ on the faces. The prescribed equal face stress gives ImQ±(s)=f(s)/μ. Multiplication by the square-root factor converts this face condition to an additive jump. In fact, with G=z​Q and r=−s​,
G+=irQ+,G−=−irQ−=irQ+​,G+−G−=−μ2​−s​f(s).
(28)
The Sokhotski–Plemelj theorem applied to this jump gives
G(z)=−πiμ1​∫−∞0​s−z−s​f(s)​ds+P(z),
(29)
where P is entire after imposing the usual finite-energy crack-tip regularity. This is the Hilbert problem for an antiplane crack, with the stress-potential phase chosen explicitly above.
For localized face loading with no additional remote loading, the far displacement is bounded and the weighted Cauchy transform decays. In particular, for compactly supported loading, G→0 at infinity and Liouville theorem sets P=0. The resulting Cauchy solution for a steadily moving semi-infinite antiplane crack is
F′(z)=−πμz​i​∫−∞0​z−s−s​f(s)​ds.​
(30)
Integrals and boundary values are understood for loadings with the corresponding convergence and regularity, or as limits of localized loading. The reflection used above is the pure antiplane opening response: reflection with a sign change of displacement leaves both prescribed face stresses unchanged, and the usual no-extra-load uniqueness condition eliminates an unforced symmetric addition.
One can check the face stresses directly. Put Ipv​(s)=PV∫−∞0​−t​f(t)/(s−t)dt. The Cauchy boundary limits give
Q+(s)=−πμ−s​Ipv​(s)​+μi​f(s),Q−(s)=πμ−s​Ipv​(s)​+μi​f(s).
(31)
Both have −μImQ±=−f(s), verifying the prescribed traction and its sign on both faces. The opposite real parts also give the required reflection. Ahead of the tip, the integral is real and Q is purely imaginary, so the bonded line has zero tangential displacement gradient and the two halves match.
For x=x1​−Vt>0, take z=x in the boxed analytic solution. The ahead-of-tip stress is
σ23​(x1​,0,t)=πx​1​∫−∞0​x−s−s​f(s)​ds.​
(32)
No β appears: at fixed co-moving position and the specified co-moving face loading, this stress is independent of the speed. The displacement, however, still scales with 1/β, so speed independence does not apply to the whole elastic field. If ∫−∞0​∣f(s)∣/−s​ds is finite, the near-tip stress is inverse-square-root with coefficient π−1∫f(s)/−s​ds.
The far-field condition is a genuine uniqueness requirement. A homogeneous stress-intensity field of a semi-infinite antiplane crack has Qh​=iC/z​, for real C. It adds no face traction but changes the stress ahead by −μC/x​. Although its stress tends to zero at infinity, its primitive is 2iCz​, which gives unbounded remote displacement and infinite total elastic energy. Thus only asking for stress to vanish at infinity would leave the homogeneous constant undetermined; excluding that added remote stress-intensity loading selects the displayed face-loaded solution. The ordinary inverse-square-root tip singularity itself has finite energy in a bounded neighbourhood and is not being excluded.
