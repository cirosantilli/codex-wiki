Neglecting the rapidly oscillating radiation source on subhorizon scales, stress-energy conservation and the trace of the Einstein field equations give
δm′′​+Hδm′​−4πGa2ρm​δm​=0.
(33)
Equivalently in cosmic time, δm,tt​+2Hδm,t​−4πGρm​δm​=0. Radiation still contributes to the background expansion, even when its perturbation source is averaged away.
Put η=a/aeq​ and let ρeq​ be the density of either component at matter-radiation equality. Then ρm​=ρeq​η−3 and ρr​=ρeq​η−4. With C=(8πG/3)ρeq​aeq2​, the Friedmann equation gives
H2=Cη21+η​,(η′)2=C(1+η),η′′=2C​,4πGa2ρm​=2η3C​.
(34)
Apply the chain rule to the density equation:
C(1+η)δηη​+C(21​+η1+η​)δη​−2η3C​δ=0.
(35)
Thus the Mészáros equation is
2η(1+η)δηη​+(2+3η)δη​−3δ=0.​
(36)
Substituting D+​=1+3η/2 gives (2+3η)(3/2)−3(1+3η/2)=0, so
δm​=A(x)(1+3η/2)​
(37)
is indeed a solution. It is approximately constant during radiation domination and grows as a during matter domination. The second independent solution is
D−​=(1+3η/2)log1+η​−11+η​+1​−31+η​,
(38)
which behaves as log(4/η)−3 at small η. Thus smoothing radiation does not eliminate every logarithmic solution; matching through actual horizon entry fixes the combination excited.
Modes with k≪keq​ enter after equality and retain the P(k)∝k shape. Modes with k≫keq​ enter during radiation domination: their horizon-entry spectrum is PH​∝k−3 and they gain little growth until equality. The late matter power spectrum therefore turns over near keq​ and has a large-scale k branch and a small-scale k−3 branch, with the more accurate radiation-era matching giving
P(k)∝k−3log2(k/keq​)(k≫keq​).
(39)
Equivalently the cold-dark-matter transfer function falls approximately as (keq​/k)2, up to the logarithm. The turnover is the consequence of the Mészáros effect; it is not a change of the primordial scale-invariance condition.
