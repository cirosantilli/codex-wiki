Use units ℏ=c=1 and the Minkowski metric gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), so p⋅x=Ep​t−p⋅x. The canonical momentum of the real scalar field is π=ϕ˙​, and the Hamiltonian operator is
H=21​∫d3x[π2+(∇ϕ)2+m2ϕ2].
(1)
Canonical quantization of a real scalar field promotes ϕ and π to operator-valued distributions satisfying the equal-time canonical commutation relation
[ϕ(t,x),π(t,y)]=iδ3(x−y),[ϕ(t,x),ϕ(t,y)]=[π(t,x),π(t,y)]=0.
(2)
Products and operator commutators can first be interpreted after spatial smearing. An additive vacuum constant in H does not affect the following equations. In the Heisenberg picture, O˙=i[H,O]. Applying the canonical commutation relation to the quadratic Hamiltonian operator, and integrating the gradient term by parts, gives
ϕ˙​=π,π˙=∇2ϕ−m2ϕ,qquad(□+m2)ϕ=0​.
(3)
Thus the Klein-Gordon equation is an operator equation. The spatial Fourier transform reduces it to ϕ¨​p​+Ep2​ϕp​=0, with Ep​=p2+m2​. Its two frequency branches, together with the Hermitian adjoint condition ϕ†=ϕ, give the mode expansion of a free field
ϕ(x)=∫dμ(p)[a(p)fp​(x)+a†(p)fp∗​(x)],dμ(p)=(2π)32Ep​d3p​,fp​=e−ip⋅x.
(4)
The measure dμ(p) is Lorentz invariant; its normalization fixes that of the creation and annihilation operators rather than imposing any extra physical assumption.
For invariant-normalized scalar mode extraction, take the Klein-Gordon inner product of fp​ with the field. The positive-frequency contribution has coefficient Ep​+Eq​ and spatial integral (2π)3δ3(p−q). The negative-frequency contribution has coefficient Ep​−Eq​ and spatial integral (2π)3δ3(p+q), so it vanishes because E−p​=Ep​. Consequently
i∫d3x[fp∗​ϕ˙​−ϕf˙​p∗​]=∫dμ(q)(Ep​+Eq​)(2π)3δ3(p−q)a(q)=a(p).
(5)
Taking the Hermitian adjoint, with the order of the scalar factors immaterial, yields
a†(p)=−i∫d3x[fp​ϕ˙​−ϕf˙​p​]​.
(6)
Equivalently a(p)=∫d3xfp∗​(Ep​ϕ+iπ) and a†(q)=∫d3yfq​(Eq​ϕ−iπ). At a common time their operator commutator therefore is
[a(p),a†(q)]=∫d3xd3yfp∗​(t,x)fq​(t,y)(Ep​+Eq​)δ3(x−y)=(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−q)​.
(7)
The same calculation gives [a(p),a(q)]=[a†(p),a†(q)]=0: the surviving factor is an energy difference supported at opposite spatial momenta. These are the bosonic canonical commutation relations in relativistic normalization of a one-particle state.
Choose the Fock vacuum by a(p)∣0⟩=0 and ⟨0∣0⟩=1. Only the annihilator-creator product contributes to the Wightman function, giving
⟨0∣ϕ(x)ϕ(y)∣0⟩=∫dμ(p)e−ip⋅(x−y).
(8)
Using time ordering and the reverse product for y0>x0, the Feynman propagator, with the convention DF​=iΔF​ in this calculation, is
DF​(x−y)=∫dμ(p)[θ(x0−y0)e−ip⋅(x−y)+θ(y0−x0)e−ip⋅(y−x)]​.
(9)
In particular the denominator in the spatial measure is 2Ep​ in both terms.
To verify the four-dimensional Fourier transform representation, let τ=x0−y0 and integrate the energy variable first:
I(τ)=limϵ↓0​∫−∞∞​2πdp0​(p0)2−Ep2​+iϵie−ip0τ​.
(10)
The Feynman i-epsilon prescription puts the positive-energy pole at Ep​−i0 and the negative-energy pole at −Ep​+i0. For τ>0, the contour integral closes in the lower half-plane with clockwise orientation. The residue theorem multiplies the residue ie−iEp​τ/(2Ep​) by −i, giving e−iEp​τ/(2Ep​). For τ<0, it closes counterclockwise above: multiplying the residue −ieiEp​τ/(2Ep​) by i gives eiEp​τ/(2Ep​). Thus I(τ)=e−iEp​∣τ∣/(2Ep​). After inserting the spatial exponential, reversing p in the negative-time term gives exactly the time ordering expression above. Hence
iΔF​(x−y)=ϵ↓0lim​∫(2π)4d4p​p2−m2+iϵie−ip⋅(x−y)​​.
(11)
This equality is understood as a distribution limit; the same prescription gives (□+m2)DF​(x−y)=−iδ4(x−y).

Use the Minkowski metric g=diag(1,−1,−1,−1). Multiplying the block gamma matrices and using the Pauli matrix multiplication law gives
(γ0)2=I4​,(γj)2=−I4​,{γ0,γj}=0,{γj,γl}=−2δjl​I4​.
(12)
Thus these matrices satisfy the Clifford algebra relation {γμ,γν}=2gμνI4​. Since partial derivatives commute, the antisymmetric part of γμγν drops out of its contraction with ∂μ​∂ν​. Multiplying the Dirac equation on the left by iγμ∂μ​+m gives
(iγ⋅∂+m)(iγ⋅∂−m)ψ=−(□+m2)ψ=0.
(13)
Therefore every component of the Dirac spinor obeys the Klein-Gordon equation with mass m.
For Lorentz covariance, take x′=Lx and ψ′(x′)=S(L)ψ(x), with constant L and S. The chain rule gives ∂μ′​=(L−1)νμ​∂ν​. The given intertwining relation then implies
(iγμ∂μ′​−m)ψ′(x′)​=S[i(S−1γμS)(L−1)νμ​∂ν​−m]ψ(x)=S(iγν∂ν​−m)ψ(x)=0.​
(14)
This proves invariance of the Dirac equation under the joint coordinate and spinor transformation.
The infinitesimal identity follows directly from the spinor Lorentz generator commutator. To derive it, first move γρ through a two-matrix product using the Clifford algebra:
[γμγν,γρ]=2gνργμ−2gμργν.
(15)
Subtract the expression with μ and ν interchanged and multiply by i/2. Thus
[σμν,γρ]=2i(gνργμ−gμργν).
(16)
Writing S=1−4i​σμνωμν​ gives S−1=1+4i​σμνωμν​+O(ω2), and hence
S−1γρS​=γρ+4i​ωμν​[σμν,γρ]+O(ω2)=γρ−21​ωμν​(gνργμ−gμργν)+O(ω2)=γρ+ωρν​γν+O(ω2)=Lρν​γν+O(ω2).​
(17)
The last step uses ωμν​=−ωνμ​, including the metric signs in raising the first index.
For parity symmetry in quantum field theory, set y=xP​=(t,−x). The time derivative of ψ(y) is unchanged and each spatial derivative changes sign. Also γ0 commutes with itself and anticommutes with every γj. Consequently
(iγμ∂μ​−m)γ0ψ(xP​)=γ0(iγμ∂yμ​−m)ψ(y)=0.
(18)
This verifies the specified parity symmetry in quantum field theory action explicitly.
For charge conjugation, put B=iγ2, so ψC​=Bψ∗. In the Dirac representation of the gamma matrices, γ0,γ1,γ3 are real and γ2 is imaginary. The Clifford algebra therefore gives Bγμ∗B−1=−γμ for all four indices. Complex conjugation of the Dirac equation, followed by multiplication by B, yields
(−iγμ∗∂μ​−m)ψ∗=0⟹(iγμ∂μ​−m)ψC​=0.
(19)
This is the antilinear charge-conjugation intertwiner property. No electromagnetic background is being held fixed in this free-field calculation.
Apply the specified parity symmetry in quantum field theory action before charge conjugation. Since γ0 is real and Bγ0=−γ0B,
(ψP​)C​(x)=B[γ0ψ(xP​)]∗=−γ0ψC​(xP​)​.
(20)
Thus the induced parity action on the charge-conjugate spinor has the opposite sign. In particular, if a spinor has intrinsic parity η=±1, its charge conjugate has intrinsic parity −η. Equivalently, at rest the positive-frequency Dirac spinors occupy the upper two components and have γ0 eigenvalue +1, whereas the negative-frequency Dirac spinors occupy the lower two components and have eigenvalue −1. The corresponding particle and antiparticle creation operators inherit these opposite signs under the specified parity convention. Hence electrons and positrons have opposite intrinsic parities; conventionally one assigns +1 to the electron and −1 to the positron. The common real overall parity sign may be reversed without changing this relative sign, as in opposite intrinsic parities of a Dirac particle and antiparticle.

All three fields have mass m. In the interaction picture the quantum fields evolve with the free Hamiltonian operator, so their equations are
(□+m2)ϕI​=(□+m2)ψI​=(□+m2)ΦI​=0​.
(21)
The interactions evolve the states instead. For χ∈{ϕ,ψ,Φ} the appropriate mode expansion of a free field is
χI​(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​[aχ​(p)e−ip⋅x+aχ†​(p)eip⋅x],Ep​=p2+m2​.
(22)
Independent species have commuting creation and annihilation operators. The full set of canonical commutation relations is
[aχ​(p),aη†​(q)]=δχη​(2π)32Ep​δ3(p−q),[aχ​,aη​]=[aχ†​,aη†​]=0.
(23)
These follow from the separate equal-time canonical commutation relations for the three real scalar fields. For comparison, in the Heisenberg picture the full Euler-Lagrange field equations contain interactions: (□+m2)ϕH​=−gϕH​ΦH​, (□+m2)ψH​=−gψH​ΦH​, and (□+m2)ΦH​=−2g​(ϕH2​+ψH2​). These are distinct from the free equations used in the interaction picture.
There are no derivative couplings, so the interaction Hamiltonian is HI​(t)=−∫d3xLI​(x). The interaction picture evolution operator obeys
i∂t​UI​(t,t0​)=HI​(t)UI​(t,t0​),UI​(t0​,t0​)=1.
(24)
Iterating its integral equation gives the Dyson series
UI​(t,t0​)=1+∑n≥1​(−i)n∫t0​t​dt1​∫t0​t1​​dt2​⋯∫t0​tn−1​​dtn​HI​(t1​)⋯HI​(tn​)=Texp[−i∫t0​t​HI​(t′)dt′].
(25)
The ordered integration region is equivalent to the unrestricted region with time ordering and the factor 1/n!. Taking the scattering limit t0​→−∞, t→∞, with the usual adiabatic prescription for perturbation theory, therefore yields the S-matrix
S=Texp[i∫d4xLI​(x)]​.
(26)
All fields inside this expression are interaction picture fields.
Use relativistic normalization of a one-particle state and take ∣i⟩=aϕ†​(p)aψ†​(q)∣0⟩, ∣f⟩=aϕ†​(p′)aψ†​(q′)∣0⟩. Define the connected scattering amplitude by ⟨f∣S∣i⟩conn​=i(2π)4δ4(p′+q′−p−q)M. The identity contribution describes no scattering; the term linear in g cannot connect these four external particles. At quadratic order, the connected term in the Dyson series is
S(2)=−8g2​∫d4xd4yT{[ϕI2​ΦI​+ψI2​ΦI​](x)[ϕI2​ΦI​+ψI2​ΦI​](y)}.
(27)
By Wick theorem, a connected Wick contraction must attach the two external ϕ legs to ϕI2​ at one vertex, the two external ψ legs to ψI2​ at the other, and contract the remaining two ΦI​ fields. The two assignments of species to x,y cancel the Dyson factor 1/2!. At either vertex, the 2! assignments of its identical fields to the incoming and outgoing particle cancel the interaction factor 1/2. Equivalently, the coefficient −g2/8 is multiplied by 2×2×2, giving −g2. Therefore, writing DF​(x−y)=⟨0∣TΦI​(x)ΦI​(y)∣0⟩,
⟨f∣S(2)∣i⟩conn​=−g2∫d4xd4yei(p′−p)⋅x+i(q′−q)⋅yDF​(x−y).
(28)
Insert the Feynman propagator DF​(x−y)=∫(2π)4d4r​r2−m2+i0ie−ir⋅(x−y)​. The two vertex integrals impose r=p′−p and p′+q′=p+q. With the Mandelstam invariant t=(p−p′)2, this gives
⟨f∣S(2)∣i⟩conn​=(2π)4δ4(p′+q′−p−q)t−m2+i0−ig2​,M=−t−m2+i0g2​​.
(29)
This is distinguishable scalar scattering by a shared mediator with mediator mass m. In physical elastic kinematics t≤0, so the denominator has no pole for m>0. There is only the displayed t-channel tree-level Feynman diagram: an s- or u-channel would require an absent mixed ϕψΦ vertex. Each vertex contributes −ig and the exchanged Φ contributes i/(t−m2+i0), agreeing with the direct Wick contraction calculation.

Use gμν​=diag(1,−1,−1,−1) throughout. Vary the Maxwell action with respect to the electromagnetic four-potential. Antisymmetry of the electromagnetic field tensor gives δL=−Fμν∂μ​δAν​. After integration by parts, the Euler-Lagrange field equation is
∂μ​Fμν=0​,□Aν−∂ν(∂μ​Aμ)=0.
(30)
A gauge transformation Aμ​↦Aμ​+∂μ​χ leaves Fμν​ unchanged because partial derivatives commute. It therefore leaves the Maxwell action and observable electromagnetic fields unchanged: different potentials represent the same physical field. This redundancy matters for canonical quantization: before gauge fixing, the momentum conjugate to A0​ vanishes and the four potential components cannot be treated as four independent physical oscillators.
Choose the Lorenz gauge D=∂μ​Aμ=0, attainable by solving □χ=−D with suitable boundary data. The Euler-Lagrange field equation then reduces to
□Aμ=0​.
(31)
Residual Lorenz gauge symmetry consists of transformations with □χ=0. The gauge condition and this residual redundancy ultimately leave two transverse polarizations of a free photon.
For the specified kinetic density LC​=−21​∂μ​Aν​∂μAν, one has ∂LC​/∂(∂μ​Aν​)=−∂μAν. Its Euler-Lagrange field equation is therefore −□Aν=0. This density is the Feynman-gauge Maxwell kinetic density after a boundary-term subtraction, since
−41​Fμν​Fμν−21​D2=LC​+∂μ​Kμ,Kμ=21​(Aν​∂νAμ−AμD).
(32)
The divergence follows by expanding both sides and commuting the second derivatives. Thus the wave equation follows from a Feynman gauge action; the additional physical-state condition will implement the Lorenz gauge. One must not impose D=0 inside the original action before varying it.
Take the covariant components Aν​ as canonical coordinates. The canonical momentum computed from LC​ is
Πν=∂(∂0​Aν​)∂LC​​=−∂0​Aν​.
(33)
The equal-time canonical Poisson brackets are {Aμ​(t,x),Πν(t,y)}=δμ​νδ3(x−y), with coordinate-coordinate and momentum-momentum brackets zero. Applying canonical quantization gives
[Aμ​(t,x),Πν(t,y)]=iδμ​νδ3(x−y)​,[Aμ​,Aν​]=[Πμ,Πν]=0.
(34)
In particular [Aμ​,A˙ν​]=−igμν​δ3. The negative temporal sign is the source of the indefinite state-space norm in covariant photon quantization.
The spatial Fourier transform of the wave equation gives an oscillator of frequency ω=∣k∣ for each component. The Hermitian adjoint condition on the electromagnetic four-potential then yields the mode expansion of a free field
Aμ​(x)=∫(2π)32ωd3k​[aμ​(k)e−ik⋅x+aμ†​(k)eik⋅x]​,kμ=(ω,k).
(35)
These are massless on shell modes. Consistently with the preceding equal-time canonical commutation relation,
[aμ​(k),aν†​(l)]=−gμν​(2π)32ωδ3(k−l),[aμ​,aν​]=[aμ†​,aν†​]=0.
(36)
The normalization is the same as for the scalar modes, with −gμν​ replacing the species identity matrix.
Gupta-Bleuler quantization starts with the Fock vacuum annihilated by every aμ​ and imposes only the annihilation, or positive-frequency part of a quantum field, of the Lorenz gauge condition:
(∂μ​Aμ)(+)∣phys⟩=0​⟺kμaμ​(k)∣phys⟩=0for every k.
(37)
Imposing the entire Hermitian field divergence as a strong operator identity would be incompatible with the four-component canonical commutation relation. The weaker condition suffices: between two physical states its positive-frequency part vanishes on the ket, and its negative-frequency part vanishes on the bra. Hence ⟨phys′∣∂μ​Aμ∣phys⟩=0.
For a one-photon state ∣k,ϵ⟩=ϵνaν†​(k)∣0⟩, commuting the constraint through the creator gives
lμaμ​(l)∣k,ϵ⟩=−(k⋅ϵ)(2π)32ωδ3(l−k)∣0⟩.
(38)
Thus the allowed photon polarization vectors satisfy k⋅ϵ=0​. Their state inner product, with the common momentum-normalization factor suppressed, is ⟨ϵ′∣ϵ⟩=−ϵ′∗⋅ϵ. Momentum eigenstates are delta-normalized; the same statements hold with finite norms for normalizable wave packets.
Rotate the spatial axes so that k=ω(1,0,0,1). The constraint is ϵ0=ϵ3, so the complete allowed family is
ϵ=α(1,0,0,1)+β(0,1,0,0)+γ(0,0,1,0)​,α,β,γ∈C.
(39)
The first vector is the allowed longitudinal gauge polarization, proportional to the null four-momentum k. Its state has zero norm because k2=0; it is also orthogonal to every constrained state because k⋅ϵ=0. A purely spatial vector (0,0,0,1) alone is not an allowed physical polarization: its time component must accompany it to satisfy the constraint. The other two vectors are transverse polarizations, perpendicular to k, with positive unit norms. For the displayed family,
−ϵ∗⋅ϵ=∣β∣2+∣γ∣2.
(40)
The timelike negative-norm excitation has been excluded by the constraint, and the remaining form is positive semidefinite. The Gupta-Bleuler null-state quotient identifies ϵ and ϵ+λk, removing the null direction. It leaves exactly two transverse physical photon polarizations with a positive inner product; their bosonic Fock space is the physical photon state space.
Finally use Feynman slash notation, a=γμaμ​, and impose four-momentum conservation p+q=k+k′. The external Dirac spinors obey
(p−m)u(p)=0,vˉ(q)(q+m)=0.
(41)
Set r=p−k and r′=p−k′. Replacing ϵ by k in the first term of the electron-positron annihilation into two photons amplitude, the rightmost contracted vertex obeys
ku=(p−m)u−(r−m)u=−(r−m)u.
(42)
Since the Clifford algebra implies (r+m)(r−m)=(r2−m2)I4​, that term becomes
vˉϵ′r2−m2r+m​ku=−vˉϵ′u.
(43)
For the second term, four-momentum conservation gives k=q+r′. The contracted vertex is now adjacent to the outgoing positron spinor on the left, so
vˉk=vˉ(q+r′)=vˉ(r′−m).
(44)
Consequently
vˉkr′2−m2r′+m​ϵ′u=+vˉϵ′u.
(45)
The two terms cancel, proving the two-photon fermion Ward identity
vˉ(q)M​ϵ=k​u(p)=0,T​ϵ=k​=0​.
(46)
Exchanging the two photon labels proves the same result for ϵ′=k′. By linearity the amplitude is invariant under ϵ↦ϵ+λk on either external photon leg. Therefore the null longitudinal gauge polarization has zero production amplitude: physical scattering produces only the two transverse photon polarizations. This cancellation also shows why both photon orderings in the tree-level Feynman diagrams are essential; either term alone fails the Ward identity.
