For the first equation use an integrating factor. The Itô product rule gives d(eλtXt​)=σeλtdBt​, so
Xt​=e−λt(1+σ∫0t​eλsdBs​).
(18)
The Gaussianity of deterministic Brownian stochastic integrals follows by approximating its deterministic integrand by step functions and using the Itô isometry. Hence
Xt​∼N​e−λt,⎩⎨⎧​2λσ2(1−e−2λt)​,σ2t,​λ=0,λ=0.​​.​
(19)
The variance is positive also when λ<0, since numerator and denominator then both have negative sign. This is the explicit Ornstein-Uhlenbeck solution with initial value one.
For the second equation, take independent standard Brownian motions U,V and define Rt​=(1+Ut​)2+Vt2​​. The associated planar Brownian motion does not hit the origin. To check this rather than silently differentiate at zero, stop its radius upon hitting ε or L, with 0<ε<1<L. The function logr is harmonic on that annulus, so the optional stopping theorem gives
P1​(Tε​<TL​)=logL−logεlogL​.
(20)
Let ε↓0: hitting zero before TL​ has probability zero. Taking integer L→∞ covers every possible finite hitting time, since continuous paths are bounded on compact intervals. Thus planar Brownian motion avoids a fixed point and R stays positive.
The radial Itô formula is now legitimate and gives
dRt​=Rt​1+Ut​​dUt​+Rt​Vt​​dVt​+2Rt​1​dt.
(21)
The sum of the two martingale terms has bracket t and starts at zero, so it is a standard Brownian motion β by Lévy characterization of Brownian motion. Thus dR=dβ+(2R)−1dt with R0​=1. The law-determination assumption permitted in the question identifies the law of Y with that of this dimension-two Bessel process. For independent standard normal random variables G1​,G2​,
Yt​=d(1+t​G1​)2+tG22​​.​
(22)
This is the two-dimensional Bessel transition law and answers the requested distribution without needing a special-function density.
