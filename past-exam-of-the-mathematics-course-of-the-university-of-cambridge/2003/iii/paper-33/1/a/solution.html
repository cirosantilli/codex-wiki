A continuous adapted process M is a local martingale if there are stopping times τn​↑∞ almost surely such that each stopped process Mtτn​​=Mt∧τn​​ is a martingale. In this definition M0​ is integrable. A continuous adapted process A has finite variation if, almost surely, its paths have finite total variation of a function on each compact time interval:
Vt​(A)=sup0=t0​<⋯<tr​=t​∑j=1r​∣Atj​​−Atj−1​​∣<∞.
(1)
The permitted fact that this total-variation process is continuous and adapted makes its level-hitting times stopping times.
Suppose M=A, and put N=M−M0​. Define
σn​=τn​∧n∧inf{t:Vt​(N)≥n}.
(2)
These times increase to infinity almost surely. The optional stopping theorem applied to the localized martingale shows that Nσn​ is a martingale; it is also bounded in absolute value by n, with total variation at most n. Fix n,t and write L=Nσn​. For a deterministic partition of an interval 0=t0​<⋯<tr​=t, orthogonality of square-integrable martingale increments gives
ELt2​=∑j​E(Ltj​​−Ltj−1​​)2.
(3)
Pathwise the sum on the right is bounded by nmaxj​∣Ltj​​−Ltj−1​​∣. For partitions with mesh tending to zero, continuity makes this converge to zero. It is bounded by 2n2, so dominated convergence gives ELt2​=0. First use rational t, then continuity, to obtain L≡0 almost surely. Letting n→∞ proves
Mt​=M0​for all t≥0 simultaneously, almost surely.​
(4)
This proves continuous finite-variation local martingale is constant without assuming integrability of the unstopped total variation. Continuity matters: compensated jump martingales can have finite variation without being constant.
