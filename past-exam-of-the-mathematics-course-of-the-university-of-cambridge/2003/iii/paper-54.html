In a coordinate chart the Lie derivative of a function and Lie derivative of a vector field are
LX​f=Xa∂a​f,(LX​Y)a=Xb∂b​Ya−Yb∂b​Xa.
(1)
The Lie derivative of a tensor field is the extension that respects tensor products and contractions. For a covector field α, differentiate the scalar pairing α(Y) to obtain (LX​α)(Y)=X(α(Y))−α([X,Y]). In components this gives
(LX​α)a​=Xb∂b​αa​+αb​∂a​Xb.
(2)
Thus for a mixed rank-two tensor field, the coordinate tensor Lie derivative is
(LX​S)ab​=Xc∂c​Sab​−Scb​∂c​Xa+Sac​∂b​Xc.
(3)
Each upper index contributes a negative derivative of the generator and each lower index a positive one. Explicitly, for type (m,n),
(LX​S)a1​⋯am​b1​⋯bn​​=Xc∂c​Sa1​⋯am​b1​⋯bn​​−∑r=1m​(∂c​Xar​)Sa1​⋯c⋯am​b1​⋯bn​​+∑s=1n​(∂bs​​Xc)Sa1​⋯am​b1​⋯c⋯bn​​.
(4)
These rules follow by expanding in coordinate vector and covector bases and using the product rule; they define the extension independently of that basis. For a symmetric connection, replacing the partial derivatives by covariant derivatives leaves these expressions unchanged because the connection terms cancel.
For the commutator identity, [LX​,LY​]f=[X,Y](f) on functions. On a vector field Z, the Jacobi identity gives [X,[Y,Z]]−[Y,[X,Z]]=[[X,Y],Z]. The difference D=[LX​,LY​]−L[X,Y]​ is a tensor derivation respecting contractions. Since it vanishes on functions and vectors, pairing a covector with every vector shows it vanishes on covectors. Product expansions then show it vanishes on every tensor. This proves the commutator identity for Lie derivatives:
[LX​,LY​]=L[X,Y]​.​
(5)
A Killing vector generates infinitesimal isometries, meaning LK​g=0, equivalently the Killing equation ∇a​Kb​+∇b​Ka​=0 for the metric Levi-Civita connection. Linearity in the generator gives LaK+bL​g=0 for real constants a,b whenever K,L are Killing. The identity above likewise gives L[K,L]​g=[LK​,LL​]g=0. Thus the Killing fields form a real Lie algebra; arbitrary nonconstant coefficients would introduce additional derivative terms.
For the rotating star, the hypothesis that there are exactly two independent Killing fields means [T,Φ]=aT+bΦ with constant coefficients. Axial symmetry supplies a periodic rotation flow; with the stated asymptotic normalization its generator is Φ and its period is 2π. Pullback by this flow acts on the Killing algebra, so e2πadΦ=1. In the basis (T,Φ), adΦ has matrix
(−a−b​00​).
(6)
Its real eigenvalues are −a and zero, so the exponential identity forces a=0. The remaining matrix squares to zero; its exponential is then 1+2πadΦ, forcing b=0. The two-dimensional Killing algebra with a periodic generator is abelian result therefore gives [T,Φ]=0​.
This uses genuine periodic axial symmetry and the two-dimensional Killing algebra. Mere limits of vector components do not by themselves justify differentiating those limits to evaluate their bracket; the periodic-flow argument avoids that gap. With the usual differentiable asymptotically flat falloff one can also take the bracket at infinity and use independence of the limiting time and angular generators to set both constants to zero.

Work to first order in a metric perturbation gab​=ηab​+hab​, with η=diag(1,−1,−1,−1), raising perturbation indices with η and discarding quadratic terms. The Christoffel symbols are
Γa(1)bc​=21​ηad(∂b​hdc​+∂c​hdb​−∂d​hbc​).
(7)
Using the printed curvature convention gives the linearized Riemann curvature operator
Rabcd(1)​=21​(∂b​∂d​hac​+∂a​∂c​hbd​−∂a​∂d​hbc​−∂b​∂c​had​).
(8)
For definiteness contract Rbd​=Rabad​. The corresponding first-order Ricci tensor is
Rab(1)​=21​(∂a​∂b​h+□hab​−∂a​∂chcb​−∂b​∂chca​),□=∂t2​−Δ.
(9)
This is the reversed sign convention specified in curvature-sign convention in Killing derivative identities; the vacuum equation is unaffected by an overall curvature sign.
An infinitesimal passive coordinate change x′a=xa+ξa changes the perturbation by δhab​=−∂a​ξb​−∂b​ξa​. Substitution into R(1) gives zero change, since partial derivatives commute. Equivalently the change in curvature is the Lie derivative of the background curvature, which vanishes in Minkowski spacetime. Thus linearized curvature, and the linearized Weyl tensor in vacuum, are gauge-invariant tidal observables, whereas individual components of h are generally gauge dependent.
The trace-reversed metric perturbation hˉab​=hab​−ηab​h/2 transforms by δhˉab​=−∂a​ξb​−∂b​ξa​+ηab​∂c​ξc. Its divergence changes by −□ξb​. Solving □ξb​=∂ahˉab​ therefore imposes Lorenz gauge in linearized gravity, ∂ahˉab​=0. With the stated sign convention the Einstein tensor is
Gab(1)​=21​[□hˉab​−∂a​∂chˉcb​−∂b​∂chˉca​+ηab​∂c∂dhˉcd​],
(10)
so the linearized vacuum field equation becomes □hˉab​=0​. The residual gauge symmetry of linearized gravity has □ξa​=0. This makes a hyperbolic evolution problem after the gauge condition and its initial consistency constraints have been imposed.
To separate actual gauge invariants from a gauge choice, use gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbations. For spatial indices contracted with δij​, decompose
h00​=2A,h0i​=∂i​B+Bi​,hij​=2Cδij​+2(∂i​∂j​−δij​Δ/3)E+∂i​Ej​+∂j​Ei​+hijTT​,
(11)
where ∂i​Bi​=∂i​Ei​=0, ∂i​hijTT​=0 and hiiTT​=0. Spatial falloff or Fourier-mode conventions fix the decomposition. Write ξ0=τ and ξi=∂i​L+Li​ with ∂i​Li​=0. Direct substitution in the gauge change gives
δA=−τ˙,δB=L˙−τ,δC=ΔL/3,δE=L,δBi​=L˙i​,δEi​=Li​,δhijTT​=0.
(12)
Hence the invariant combinations are
Φ=A−B˙+E¨,Ψ=C−ΔE/3,Vi​=Bi​−E˙i​,hijTT​.​
(13)
A representative with E=Ei​=B=0 has h00​=2Φ, h0i​=Vi​ and hij​=2Ψδij​+hijTT​. The explicit vacuum constraints for gauge-invariant Minkowski perturbations follow from
G00(1)​=−2ΔΨ,G0i(1)​=−2∂i​Ψ˙−21​ΔVi​,
(14)
Gij(1)​=(∂i​∂j​−δij​Δ)(Φ−Ψ)−2δij​Ψ¨−21​(∂i​V˙j​+∂j​V˙i​)+21​□hijTT​.
(15)
For a nonzero spatial Fourier mode, or regular decaying fields on all space, G00​=0 forces Ψ=0, then G0i​=0 forces Vi​=0, and the spatial trace gives ΔΦ=0, hence Φ=0. Only □hijTT​=0 remains. This removal of the scalar and vector sectors does not exclude singular Coulomb perturbations outside a source, boundary-driven harmonic potentials or spatially homogeneous modes; those require separate boundary and gauge conventions.
For a propagating plane mode h∝eika​xa, the wave equation gives kaka​=0. A transverse-traceless spatial tensor perpendicular to a fixed propagation direction has two independent components. For propagation along z, define the physical strain Hij​=−hijTT​ in this mostly-minus convention, so the spatial line element is (δij​+Hij​)dxidxj. It has the form
Hij​(t,z)=​H+​(t−z)H×​(t−z)0​H×​(t−z)−H+​(t−z)0​000​​.
(16)
These are the two gravitational wave polarizations, plus polarization and cross polarization. A rotation in the transverse plane rotates the polarization pair through twice the angle; cross is plus rotated through 45 degrees. Lorenz constraints and residual gauge freedom give the same count: ten symmetric metric components minus four constraints and four gauge freedoms leave two radiative degrees of freedom.
In transverse-traceless gauge, particles initially at rest keep fixed spatial coordinates since Γa00​=0, but their measured separation changes. For a short separation of length L in unit direction n, δL/L=Hij​ninj/2. The gauge-invariant curvature gives the corresponding geodesic deviation:
Ri(1)00j​=21​h¨ijTT​=−21​H¨ij​,ξ¨​i=−Ri(1)00j​ξj=21​H¨ij​ξj.
(17)
Here the last equation is in a freely falling orthonormal frame, with the printed reversed curvature convention. Coordinate separation and physical separation must not be confused. The linearized vacuum equations describe propagation and tidal measurements; wave energy transfer and an effective wave stress-energy tensor arise at quadratic order, beyond the linear approximation.

Use the metric Levi-Civita connection and hold the covariant potential Aa​ fixed when varying the metric. Symmetry of the connection cancels its terms in the electromagnetic field tensor:
Fab​=∇a​Ab​−∇b​Aa​=∂a​Ab​−∂b​Aa​.
(18)
The Electromagnetic Bianchi identity follows from commuting partial derivatives, or F=dA and d2=0. Written covariantly it is ∇c​Fab​+∇a​Fbc​+∇b​Fca​=0, since total antisymmetrization also cancels the symmetric connection terms. Hence F[ab;c]​=0​ without using any field equation.
For the potential variation, δFab​=∇a​δAb​−∇b​δAa​. Antisymmetry in the Maxwell action gives
δA​S=−4π1​∫−g​Fab∇a​δAb​d4x=−4π1​∫−g​(∇b​Fab)δAa​d4x,
(19)
where the second equality is integration by parts followed by relabeling; compactly supported variations, or vanishing boundary variations, remove the surface term. Arbitrary δAa​ therefore give the source-free Maxwell equations
∇b​Fab=0.​
(20)
No gauge fixing of A is needed for this variational calculation.
In signature (+−−−) define the Hilbert stress-energy tensor by δg​S=21​∫−g​Tab​δgabd4x. Since the independent covariant potential makes Fab​ metric independent,
δ−g​=−21​−g​gab​δgab,δ(Fcd​Fcd)=2Fa​cFbc​δgab.
(21)
Combining these two terms yields the electromagnetic stress-energy tensor
Tab=4π1​[−Fac​Fbc+41​gabFcd​Fcd].​
(22)
Equivalently δS=−21​∫−g​Tabδgab​. The overall stress-variation sign has been matched to the signature and action. As a check, in an orthonormal frame Fcd​Fcd=2(B2−E2) and T00=(E2+B2)/(8π)>0 for a nonzero field; copying a mostly-plus formula without changing conventions would fail this check.
Let Ja=∇b​Fab. Contract the Bianchi identity with Fbc to obtain 2Fbc∇b​Fac​=21​∇a​(Fcd​Fcd), hence Fbc∇b​Fac​=41​∇a(Fcd​Fcd). Differentiating the stress tensor cancels these derivative terms and gives the electromagnetic stress-energy divergence identity
∇b​Tab=4π1​Fac​Jc.​
(23)
Thus Maxwell's equation implies stress conservation for any field. Conversely, where the matrix Fac​ is nonsingular, conservation implies Jc=0 by matrix inversion. When it is singular, conservation only says that J lies in its kernel, so the converse cannot be inferred without the printed nonsingularity assumption.
For the first-order formulation, temporarily call the independently constructed potential curvature fab​(A)=∇a​Ab​−∇b​Aa​. In the first-order Maxwell action, vary the independent antisymmetric Fab, with Fab​=gac​gbd​Fcd. Its variation is
δF​Sfirst​=8π1​∫−g​[Fab​−fab​(A)]δFabd4x,
(24)
so Fab​=fab​(A). Varying A independently gives exactly ∇b​Fab=0, by the same integration-by-parts calculation as before. Eliminating the auxiliary field algebraically makes the integrand fabfab​−2fabfab​=−fabfab​, recovering the original Maxwell action. The Bianchi identity then follows from f=dA. Metric variation also agrees after elimination: the chain-rule term from the eliminated field multiplies its already vanishing field equation. Thus the two actions give the same source-free electromagnetic dynamics and stress tensor. The Palatini procedure here means independent first-order field variables, not an additional variation of the spacetime connection.

In ordinary spherical coordinates, t∈R, r∈[0,∞), θ∈[0,π] and ϕ∈[0,2π) with periodic identification. The regular coordinate chart excludes the origin and polar axes; the endpoint ranges include the usual spherical identifications. With null coordinates u=t−r and v=t+r, both are real and their joint domain is v≥u. The inverse relations give
ds2=dudv−4(v−u)2​dΣ2.
(25)
The arctangent coordinates obey −π/2<p≤q<π/2. Since du=sec2pdp and dv=sec2qdq, the rescaled metric is
ds2=4dpdq−(tanq−tanp)2cos2pcos2qdΣ2=4dpdq−sin2(q−p)dΣ2.
(26)
Now T=p+q, R=q−p, so 4dpdq=dT2−dR2. Thus the Minkowski conformal compactification has
ds2=dT2−dR2−sin2RdΣ2.​
(27)
The full coordinate ranges are linked, not independent: T∈(−π,π) and R∈[0,π) with ∣T∣+R<π. Inverse coordinates are p=(T−R)/2, q=(T+R)/2, t=[tanq+tanp]/2, r=[tanq−tanp]/2. The conformal factor is
Ω=2cospcosq=cosT+cosR>0,g=Ω2g​.​
(28)
The unphysical metric extends as the unit-radius Einstein static universe on R×S3, where 0≤R≤π with the endpoint two-spheres collapsed. Physical Minkowski spacetime occupies the triangular radial domain R≥0, ∣T∣+R<π; the edge R=0 is the ordinary spatial center, not an infinity or physical boundary.
In the closure, the infinite-coordinate limits have locations
i+=(T,R)=(π,0),i−=(−π,0),i0=(0,π),
(29)
I+:T+R=π, 0<R<π,I−:T−R=−π, 0<R<π.
(30)
These are future timelike infinity, past timelike infinity, spacelike infinity, future null infinity and past null infinity. For example I+ has v→+∞ with finite u, while I− has u→−∞ with finite v. A diagram point with 0<R<π stands for a two-sphere, and each null boundary is topologically R×S2; their three corners are points after the angular collapse. On the null boundaries Ω=0 and dΩ=0, whereas the three corners have a degenerate zero gradient. Thus a compactifying metric can be smooth there even though those infinity points are not ordinary smooth null-boundary points.
The geodesic endpoints in Minkowski conformal compactification can be established directly. A physical inertial timelike geodesic has t=t0​+Eτ, x=x0​+pτ, with E>0 and E2−∣p∣2=1. At future infinity u,v→+∞, so p,q→π/2 and the curve ends at i+. At past infinity both tend to −∞, giving i−. Every complete inertial timelike path has these endpoints, regardless of velocity.
For a future-directed null geodesic, t=t0​+Eλ, x=x0​+Enλ with ∣n∣=1. As λ→+∞, v→+∞ but u has a finite limit; the endpoint lies on I+. As λ→−∞, u→−∞ but v has a finite limit, giving an endpoint on I−. A conformal transformation preserves these null paths as unparametrized geodesics. The null affine parameter under a conformal rescaling obeys dλ/dλ=Ω2; here Ω2=O(λ−2) at the null endpoints, so the unphysical affine parameter reaches them in finite time, although the physical affine parameter is infinite.
A physical spacelike geodesic has t=t0​+aλ, x=x0​+bλ, with ∣b∣>∣a∣. At either end r>∣t∣ asymptotically, u→−∞ and v→+∞, so both ends approach i0. Timelike and spacelike physical geodesic paths are not generally geodesics of the rescaled metric. Their physical proper time or length is infinite, while Ω=O(∣λ∣−2) gives finite unphysical proper time or length near the endpoints. Infinity at finite diagram coordinates does not imply incompleteness of physical Minkowski geodesics.
The original Penrose diagram suppresses the angular spheres. The displayed physical paths illustrate their distinct endpoint types; the two sloping boundaries are null infinity, while the left edge is the regular center.
