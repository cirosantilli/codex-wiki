Write horizontal components with a subscript h. The steady ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation gives ∇×(u×B)=0. Since ∂z​=0, its horizontal components imply
∂x​(u×B)z​=∂y​(u×B)z​=0.
(1)
Thus (u×B)z​=C is a spatial constant. A vanishing axial electric field, Ez​=−C=0, is needed to infer horizontal alignment. It is satisfied, for example, if the constant electric field vanishes at a boundary or at infinity. With this additional condition and Bh​=0, define the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading k by ρuh​=kBh​, and put U=uz​−kBz​/ρ. Then
u=ρkB​+Uez​​.
(2)
The steady continuity equation and the solenoidal magnetic-field constraint give
0=∇⋅(ρu)=∇⋅(kB+ρUez​)=B⋅∇k.
(3)
Also u×B=Uez​×B. Using the vector identity for the curl of a cross product, together with ∂z​B=0 and ∇⋅B=0, its curl is ez​B⋅∇U. Hence
B⋅∇k=B⋅∇U=0​.
(4)
These are steady planar ideal-MHD field-line invariants: both quantities are constant along a magnetic field line.
The zero-electric-field condition does not follow from the printed stationarity assumptions alone. Constant ρ,p,s, Φ=0, u=u0​ex​ and B=B0​ey​, with u0​B0​=0, satisfy every displayed ideal magnetohydrodynamics equation but have perpendicular horizontal velocity and magnetic field. Their C=u0​B0​ is nonzero, so no k,U give the stated decomposition. The field-line decomposition is valid on the Ez​=0 branch; as an unrestricted implication, the first claim has this counterexample. The subsequent calculations use that branch.

Continue on the branch of steady planar ideal-MHD field-line invariants, with k=0 and positive ρ,T. Adiabatic entropy transport gives u⋅∇s=0. As all quantities are z-independent,
u⋅∇=ρk​B⋅∇,B⋅∇s=0​.

The axial component of the ideal magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation has no pressure or gravitational term. Magnetic tension gives
ρuh​⋅∇uz​=μ0​1​Bh​⋅∇Bz​.
(6)
Since k is constant along a magnetic field line, this becomes
B⋅∇V=0,V=uz​−μ0​kBz​​​.
(7)
Subtracting the two axial invariants yields the useful compatibility relation
Bz​(ρμ0​k2​−1)=μ0​k(V−U)​.
(8)
The squared ratio of horizontal flow speed to horizontal Alfvén speed is Mh2​=μ0​k2/ρ; vector equality with the horizontal Alfvén velocity additionally requires k>0. If V−U=0, the right side is a nonzero constant along the field line, so a regular finite Bz​ cannot permit Mh2​=1 anywhere. This is the nondegenerate exclusion.
However, Bz​≡0 does not imply V−U=0. The Alfvénic degeneracy of steady planar MHD is a genuine exception: take constant ρ,p,s, Φ=0, and a constant magnetic field with nonzero horizontal and axial components, and set

(9)
Every equation holds, but the horizontal velocity equals the horizontal Alfvén velocity everywhere despite Bz​=0. The general conclusion is the boxed compatibility relation; when V=U, it says (Mh2​−1)Bz​=0 and allows the Alfvénic branch.

Let b=21​u2+Φ+w. Because the specific entropy is constant along the horizontal flow, the specific enthalpy identity gives u⋅∇w=ρ−1u⋅∇p. Dotting the ideal magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation with u therefore gives

(11)

(13)

These profiles illustrate different boundary-controlled similarity classes of the Keplerian viscous diffusion equation. The a=1 solution is compatible with a zero-torque inner boundary condition and conserved finite angular momentum, and can describe a late-time spreading accretion disk after the detailed initial profile has been forgotten. Its mass diverges as t↓0, so the formal zero-radius origin is not a finite-mass initial disk. The a=5/4 solution has finite initial mass concentrated at the origin and initially zero disk angular momentum, but requires the specified inner torque for t>0; that torque's t−3/4 singularity is time-integrable. It may describe a spreading nonaccreting disk for compatible torque-driven boundary conditions, but cannot be an isolated angular-momentum-conserving disk's generic asymptotic state. Similarity scaling suggests their possible asymptotic roles; it does not establish convergence for arbitrary initial and boundary data.

Vx​=Ccosκt+Dsinκt,Vy​=2Ωκ​[−Csinκt+Dcosκt],
(60)
while Vz​ is constant. A Keplerian shearing sheet has κ=Ω. For a centrifugally unstable rotation law the same mean equations instead give exponential growth, so an oscillation interpretation requires the positive squared frequency.
No divergence of turbulent stress survives the box average, and a zero initial mean radial velocity cannot acquire a secular accretion drift from internal stresses. More generally, a steady horizontal mean flow has Vx​=Vy​=0 for the usual nondegenerate rotating disk. The epicycle has zero time-averaged radial velocity. Opposite radial faces are identified rather than connected to a central sink and an outer mass reservoir: material crossing a face re-enters the opposite one after an azimuthal shift. Thus the shearing box can model angular-momentum transport and its local stresses, but not develop a global disk accretion flow. Such accretion needs radial gradients, boundaries and mass exchange omitted from the homogeneous local model.

Define the perturbation energy per unit mass by E=⟨v2/2+B2/(2μ0​ρ)⟩. Dot the momentum equation with v and the induction equation with B/(μ0​ρ), then take volume averages. The Coriolis force does no work. Pressure work and nonlinear advection are divergences and integrate to zero, as does the background x∂y​ transport. The two magnetic exchange terms add to
μ0​ρ1​⟨v⋅(B⋅∇B)+B⋅(B⋅∇v)⟩=μ0​ρ1​⟨∇⋅[B(v⋅B)]⟩=0.
(61)
The velocity shear term supplies 2A⟨vx​vy​⟩, while magnetic shear supplies −2A⟨Bx​By​⟩/(μ0​ρ). Integration by parts gives the viscous and resistive losses as −ν⟨∣∇v∣2⟩ and −η⟨∣∇B∣2⟩/(μ0​ρ). For a solenoidal field F, another integration by parts shows
⟨∣∇F∣2⟩=⟨∣∇×F∣2⟩.
(62)
All boundary terms cancel by the shearing-periodic identities. The resulting balance is
dtdE​=2A⟨vx​vy​−μ0​ρBx​By​​⟩−ν⟨∣∇×v∣2⟩−μ0​ρη​⟨∣∇×B∣2⟩​.
(63)
The source is work extracted from maintained differential rotation by Reynolds stress and the magnetic part of the Maxwell stress tensor; internal magnetic/kinetic exchange has canceled.
For the decay bound, the elementary inequality 2∣ab∣≤a2+b2 gives
2A⟨vx​vy​−μ0​ρBx​By​​⟩≤∣A∣⟨v2+μ0​ρB2​⟩=2∣A∣E.
(64)
A uniform spectral gap of a shearing-periodic box is needed; ordinary radial periodicity cannot simply be assumed. At each time the compatible Fourier modes are
exp[2πi(Lx​nx​+Ly​m(y+2Axt)​+Lz​pz​)],(n,m,p)∈Z3.
(65)
They are orthogonal over the box. Their wavevectors are
k=(Lx​2πn​+2AtLy​2πm​,Ly​2πm​,Lz​2πp​).
(66)
For every nonconstant mode, ∣k∣≥2π/L, where L=max(Lx​,Ly​,Lz​): if m or p is nonzero, its corresponding unsheared component supplies the bound; otherwise n=0 and the radial component does. Zero mean excludes precisely the constant mode. The Parseval identity therefore proves the time-uniform Poincaré inequality
⟨∣∇f∣2⟩≥L24π2​⟨∣f∣2⟩.
(67)
Apply it componentwise to v and B. With ν=η, the energy decay criterion for a viscous-resistive shearing box becomes
dtdE​≤2(∣A∣−L24π2η​)E=−L22η​(4π2−Rm)E.
(68)
Hence
Rm<4π2 ⟹ E(t)≤E(0)exp[−L22η​(4π2−Rm)t]⟶0​.
(69)
This excludes sustained finite-amplitude turbulence as well as infinitesimal growth. It is a sufficient stability condition, not a claim that every box above the threshold must become turbulent. The zero-mean restrictions matter: an imposed uniform magnetic field or bulk epicycle is not controlled by this spatial spectral-gap inequality.
