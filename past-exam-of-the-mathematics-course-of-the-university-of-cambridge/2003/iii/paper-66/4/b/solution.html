An initially overdense spherical region first expands approximately with the background, but its self-gravity slows it more strongly. It reaches turnaround of spherical collapse at its maximum radius, recollapses, and forms a bound approximately stationary object when infall energy is redistributed into random motions. An exactly cold, uniform pressureless solution would instead collapse to zero radius; virialization replaces that formal endpoint.
For fixed mass M and no cosmological constant, integrate R¨=−GM/R2 for a bound shell. Its specific energy can be written ε=−GM/(2A), and the cycloidal parametrization is
R=A(1−cosθ),t=B(θ−sinθ),A3=GMB2.
(45)
Substitution in R˙2/2−GM/R=ε verifies this parametrization. At θ=π, Rta​=2A and tta​=πB; formal collapse occurs at θ=2π, so tcoll​=2tta​.
For a uniform sphere, W=−3GM2/(5R). At turnaround the coherent kinetic energy vanishes, so E=Wta​. At the final equilibrium, the virial theorem gives 2Tvir​+Wvir​=0, whence E=Wvir​/2. Conservation of energy therefore implies
Rvir​=2Rta​​,ρvir​=8ρta​.​
(46)
This virial radius from turnaround energy assumes conserved mass and energy, negligible surface-pressure/tidal terms, and the same gravitational profile coefficient. The sketch's settling segment is qualitative; the cycloid is the exact pre-virialization cold model, not a dissipative relaxation solution.
