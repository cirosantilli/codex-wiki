Begin with the configuration model for n labelled vertices of degree r. Let Yℓ​ count cycles in a graph with specified paired edges, so parallel-edge choices are counted separately before conditioning. Choose a cyclic ordering of ℓ distinct vertices in (n)ℓ​/(2ℓ) ways, where division by 2ℓ removes rotation and reversal. At each vertex choose an ordered pair of distinct half-edges for the entering and leaving edges, in r(r−1) ways. These ℓ prescribed pairs appear with the pairing probability from part (i), giving
EYℓ​=2ℓ(n)ℓ​​(rn−1)(rn−3)⋯(rn−2ℓ+1)[r(r−1)]ℓ​⟶2ℓ(r−1)ℓ​.
(27)
It remains to justify conditioning on simplicity: merely dividing by a positive simplicity probability would give an incorrect constant.
Unconditionally, part (i) with no forbidden graph has λ=r−1, so
Pr(simple)=exp(−2r−1​−4(r−1)2​)+o(1).
(28)
Condition on one specified cycle pairing. The unpaired half-edges form a uniform configuration model with degree r−2 at its ℓ vertices and degree r elsewhere. A completion of the full graph is simple exactly when the residual graph is simple and has no additional edge along one of the already prescribed cycle edges. This forbidden graph has maximum degree two. Because r,ℓ are fixed,
M′=rn−2ℓ,λ′=r−1+O(1/n),μ′=O(1/n).
(29)
After permuting labels if needed to sort the degrees, the assumptions of part (i) still hold, including residual degrees at least one. Its bounded-degree pairing avoidance estimate therefore shows that
Pr(simple)Pr(simple∣specified cycle pairs)​⟶1.
(30)
This ratio is the same for each prescribed cycle, by symmetry. Sum over their indicators to obtain E(Yℓ​∣simple)=(1+o(1))EYℓ​. Conditioning is uniform over all simple r-regular graphs because each has (r!)n pairing representations. On simplicity, Yℓ​ is the ordinary cycle count. Hence the short-cycle expectation in a uniform regular graph is
ECℓ​(Gn,r-reg​)⟶2ℓ(r−1)ℓ​.​
(31)
