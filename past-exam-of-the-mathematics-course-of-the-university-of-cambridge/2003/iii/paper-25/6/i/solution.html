We specify the convention for quadratic uniformity of a set. Write δ=∣A∣/N and f=1A​−δ on [1,N], extended by zero elsewhere. Let CN​ be the integer cubes (x,h1​,h2​,h3​) with all eight vertices in [1,N], and let TN​=∣CN​∣. In the eighth-power convention, A is α-quadratically uniform when
TN​1​∑CN​​∏ϵ∈{0,1}3​C∣ϵ∣f(x+ϵ1​h1​+ϵ2​h2​+ϵ3​h3​)≤α,
(47)
where C means complex conjugation. This quantity is nonnegative and is the eighth power of the normalized Gowers U3 norm on an interval. If uniformity instead means that the norm itself is at most α, replace the present parameter by α8. Removing the balance would not define density-relative uniformity.
Embed the interval in G=Z/MZ with M=12N+1, and keep the zero extension of f. This group has invertible 2 and 3. Every cube with all vertices in the interval is an integer cube: its face relations have magnitude at most 2N<M, so their modular equalities are ordinary equalities. Conversely every such integer cube gives one modular cube. Thus
∥f∥U3(G)8​=M4TN​​∥f∥U3(N)8​≤α,
(48)
since TN​≤M4. The cube quantity is nonnegative, for it can be written as an average of squares of two-fold multiplicative-derivative correlations, by expanding the third increment.
We prove the counting estimate needed below. For functions ∣uj​∣≤1, put

(49)

(50)
Here are the elimination steps, to make the generalized von Neumann inequality for four-term progressions explicit. Set Δh​u(x)=u(x)u(x+h)​. Reparametrize by the position x+3d, and apply Cauchy-Schwarz in that position to eliminate u3​. Pairing the two remaining difference variables produces increments 3h,2h,h in u0​,u1​,u2​. Reparametrize by the position of u2​, apply Cauchy-Schwarz to eliminate it, and average over h using Cauchy-Schwarz again. The remaining increments in u0​ are 3h,2k. Finally reparametrize by the position of u1​ and eliminate it in the same way. The resulting bound is
∣Λ4​∣8≤Ex,h,k,l​Δl​Δ2k​Δ3h​u0​(x)=∥u0​∥U3(G)8​.

In the last equality 2k and 3h range freely since their multipliers are units. Each elimination drops only a factor bounded by one; expansion of the squared average gives exactly the stated multiplicative derivative. To target uj​ instead, subtract its slope from all four slopes and eliminate the other three factors. The increments are the three nonzero slope differences times independent variables, all among ±1,±2,±3 and hence invertible. This proves the minimum bound.
Now let a=1A​ and g=δ1[1,N]​ on G. Telescoping the four factors, the difference Λ4​(a,a,a,a)−Λ4​(g,g,g,g) is a sum of four terms with one balanced factor f and three factors bounded by one. Therefore
∣Λ4​(a,a,a,a)−δ4Λ4​(I,I,I,I)∣≤4α1/8.

For N≥16, restricting to 1≤x≤⌊N/4⌋ and 1≤d≤⌊N/8⌋ gives at least N2/128 progressions inside the interval. Since M≤13N, Λ4​(I,I,I,I)≥1/100000. Choose, for example,
α≤(800000δ4​)8.​

Then Λ4​(a,a,a,a)≥δ4/200000. Constant progressions contribute only ∣A∣/M2≤1/N. For N>400000δ−4, this is strictly below the displayed lower bound. A nonconstant progression consequently exists. Its four points are actual integer progression points: the two adjacent three-term relations have magnitude less than M, so they do not wrap. Hence
A contains a nonconstant four-term arithmetic progression.​
(54)
This proves the four-term progressions in a quadratically uniform interval set criterion, with the parameter normalization and the interval boundary both accounted for.
