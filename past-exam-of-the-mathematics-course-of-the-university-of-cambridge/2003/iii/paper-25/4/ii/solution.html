The graph form of Plünnecke inequality states
Dk​(G)1/k≥Dn​(G)1/n(1≤k≤n).​
(30)
Equivalently these roots are nonincreasing along the layers. If Dn​=0 the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise put C=Dn1/n​ and use the weighted cut lemma for commutative graphs proved in part (i). For a minimum endpoint cut S0​∪Sn​,
w(S0​∪Sn​)=∣S0​∣+Dn−1​∣Sn​∣≥∣S0​∣+∣V0​∖S0​∣=∣V0​∣.
(31)
The bottom layer is a cut of this weight. Comparing with (V0​∖Z)∪Im(k)Z gives ∣Im(k)Z∣≥Ck∣Z∣, as required. In an addition graph, D1​≤K when ∣A+B∣≤K∣A∣, so Dm​≤Km: some nonempty bottom subset has growth at most Km at height m.
The useful simultaneous sumset form says that a nonempty X⊆A can be chosen with ∣X+mB∣≤Km∣X∣ for every m. To verify the common-subset assertion directly, choose X minimizing K′=∣X+B∣/∣X∣ among subsets of A. For a finite set T={t1​,…,ts​}, let Xi​ consist of those x∈X for which x+ti​ is a new point of X+T. The translated Xi​ partition X+T. Points contributed by (X∖Xi​)+B+ti​ are already present in earlier translates of X+B, so the new contribution at stage i has size at most
∣X+B∣−∣(X∖Xi​)+B∣≤K′∣X∣−K′∣X∖Xi​∣=K′∣Xi​∣.
(32)
The inequality also holds when the complementary set is empty. Summing gives ∣X+B+T∣≤K′∣X+T∣. Taking T=(m−1)B and iterating proves ∣X+mB∣≤(K′)m∣X∣≤Km∣X∣. This is the Petridis minimal-growth lemma calculation and supplies a single subset for all needed heights.
