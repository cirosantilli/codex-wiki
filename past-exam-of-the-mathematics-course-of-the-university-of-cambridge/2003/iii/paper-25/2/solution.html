We prove the three-term Roth theorem by a density increment, keeping the interval boundary rather than replacing the interval by a cyclic group with a different density. The case δ>1 is vacuous; assume 0<δ≤1.
We first prove the following alternative with absolute constants c,C>0: if A⊆[1,N] has density η and no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression, and N≥Cη−4, then some integer progression P⊆[1,N] has
∣P∣≥cη2N​,∣A∩P∣/∣P∣≥η+cη2.
(10)
For this purpose take M=6⌈4N/6⌉+1, so 4N<M≤5N for large N and 2,3 are invertible modulo M. On G=Z/MZ, put I=1[1,N]​, a=1A​, g=ηI and f=a−g. In particular ∑f=0, and f vanishes outside the interval. Use normalized Fourier coefficients on a finite abelian group, u(r)=M−1∑x​u(x)e(−rx/M). Character orthogonality, obtained by summing a finite geometric series, gives Fourier inversion on a finite group and Parseval identity on a finite group.
For Λ(u,v,w)=Ex,d​u(x)v(x+d)w(x+2d) these identities give
Λ(u,v,w)=∑r​u(r)v(−2r)w(r).
(11)
There is no wrapping ambiguity when all three entries lie in [1,N]: the integer x−2y+z has absolute value below M, so a modular relation is an integer relation. If no nonconstant progression exists, Λ(a,a,a)=∣A∣/M2. Meanwhile the choices 1≤x≤⌊N/4⌋ and 1≤d≤⌊N/8⌋ give Λ(I,I,I)≥1/10000 for sufficiently large N.
Expand the difference by telescoping,
Λ(a,a,a)−Λ(g,g,g)=Λ(f,a,a)+Λ(g,f,a)+Λ(g,g,f).
(12)
If ρ=maxr​∣f​(r)∣, each term on the right has modulus at most ρηN/M. Indeed multiplication of frequencies by 2 permutes them, and Cauchy-Schwarz with Parseval identity bounds the two other Fourier factors by their L2 norms; each squared norm is at most ηN/M. For N≥10000η−2, the diagonal count is less than half the main density term. Therefore
3ρηN/M≥η3/20000.
(13)
In particular there is a nonzero frequency r with
​∑x=1N​f(x)e(−rx/M)​≥γN,γ=η2/20000.
(14)
The frequency is nonzero because f​(0)=0.
Here is the conversion of this coefficient into a density increment. Put Q=⌊N​⌋. Of the Q+1 numbers 0,θ,…,Qθ modulo one, two lie in the same one of Q equal arcs. Their difference gives 1≤q≤Q with ∥qθ∥≤1/Q, where θ=−r/M. This is the needed Dirichlet approximation theorem, proved by the pigeonhole principle.
Partition each residue class modulo q in [1,N] into consecutive progressions of length L=⌊γQ/(100π)⌋, leaving its final short tail. Their total tail size is at most qL≤γN/(100π). On each full progression the character differs from its value at the first point by at most 2πL/Q≤γ/50. Hence removing tails and replacing the character by its initial value shows
∑P full​​∑x∈P​f(x)​≥21​γN.
(15)
The sum of f over all full progressions is the negative of its sum over the tails, so its absolute value is at most γN/(100π). Consequently the sum of the positive block sums is at least γN/5. Since these blocks contain at most N points in total, at least one has average f at least γ/5. It gives a density increment at least η2/100000. For N≥Cη−4 with a sufficiently large absolute C, L≥cη2N​. This proves the alternative, with one small common absolute c.
Reidentify P with [1,∣P∣] by its affine parametrization. Absence of a nonconstant progression is preserved, while density increases. At every step the density is at least the original δ, so the increment is at least cδ2 and the new interval length is at least cδ2Nold​​. After at most T=⌈1/(cδ2)⌉+1 steps, the density would exceed one. Choose the original N so large that the length stays above Cδ−4 throughout these finitely many steps. Such a choice exists explicitly by working backwards: a required next length L0​ is ensured by previous length at least (L0​/(cδ2))2. The alternative could then be applied at every step, giving a contradiction. Thus
for each δ>0, all sufficiently long density-δ intervals contain a nonconstant three-term progression.​
(16)
This is the Roth density increment on an integer progression proof.
For the pattern with offsets 0,1,3, replace the counting functional by Ex,d​u(x)v(x+d)w(x+3d). Its Fourier expression is
∑r​u(2r)v(−3r)w(r),
(17)
coming from the relation 2x−3y+z=0. Both multipliers are invertible modulo the chosen M, so exactly the same Parseval estimates apply. This relation has no wrap in [1,N], since its magnitude is at most 3N<M, and the earlier restricted choices of x,d still give a fixed positive density main term. The same increment and iteration find a nonconstant triple {a,a+d,a+3d}. Negative d is allowed, as requested, and the affine reparametrization used in the iteration preserves this pattern as well.
