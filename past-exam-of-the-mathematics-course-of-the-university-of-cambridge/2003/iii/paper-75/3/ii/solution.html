Away from forcing, set F=0. For a linear sinusoidal disturbance A=Re[Aei(kx−ωt)], the reduced equation gives
−iσk3+iβα1​ω=0,ω=βα1​σ​k3​.
(34)
Since σ,β,α1​>0, the phase velocity cp​=ω/k=σk2/(βα1​) is positive for every nonzero wavenumber. The group velocity is
cg​=dkdω​=3βα1​σk2​=3cp​>0​.
(35)
Thus both the linear phase and wave packet travel downstream; the sign of k does not introduce an upstream branch.
Now seek a permanent nonlinear wave with ξ=x+ct, for which the propagation velocity is −c. Substitution gives σA(3)−βα1​cA′−α2​AA′=0. Integration with vanishing far-field data gives
σA′′−βα1​cA−2α2​​A2=0.
(36)
Multiplying by A′ and integrating again, with zero integration constant, gives
σ(A′)2=A2(βα1​c+3α2​​A)​.
(37)
Here α2​>0, because γ0​>γ1​>0 and R<R+1. A smooth nonzero wave approaching zero requires c>0: for c<0 the right-hand side is negative at sufficiently small nonzero A. At c=0 the allowed positive branch has no nonzero turning point, so it cannot form a smooth localized pulse.
When c>0, the nonzero turning point is Am​=−3βα1​c/α2​. The energy curve is positive between Am​ and zero and negative below Am​, giving a homoclinic negative pulse. The positive branch has no turning point and cannot return to zero. Therefore
A<0,∣A∣max​=α2​3βα1​c​,propagation velocity=−c<0​.
(38)
An explicit solution, also confirming smooth exponential tails, is
A(ξ)=−α2​3βα1​c​sech2[21​(σβα1​c​)1/2(ξ−ξ0​)].
(39)
This establishes the opposite propagation directions in the annular displacement equation: linear waves move downstream while its localized nonlinear depression waves move upstream.
