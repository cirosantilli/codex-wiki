For a population question use a population-averaged logistic model for repeated binary outcomes. Code zi​=1 for the new treatment and 0 for the control, with visit times tj​=2j months for j=1,2,3. Let wij​=(1,zi​,xiT​,tj​)T, γM​=(αM​,ϕM​,βMT​,δM​)T, and
mij​=P(Yij​=1∣zi​,xi​,tj​),logitmij​=wijT​γM​.
(28)
Different subjects are independent. Each response has a Bernoulli distribution with this marginal mean, but no independence of a subject's three responses is assumed. The linear time effect and absence of treatment-by-time interaction are substantive mean assumptions; if inappropriate they can be replaced by time indicators and interaction terms.
For complete data, set mi​=(mi1​,mi2​,mi3​)T, Ai​=diag(mij​(1−mij​)), and Di​=∂mi​/∂γMT​=Ai​Wi​, where Wi​ has rows wijT​. A working exchangeable correlation matrix has unit diagonal and off-diagonal value ρ∈(−1/2,1); set Vi​=Ai1/2​C(ρ)Ai1/2​. The generalized estimating equation is
∑i​DiT​Vi−1​(Yi​−mi​)=0.
(29)
With correct means and independent subjects, a positive-definite working covariance matrix and regularity give consistency even if the working correlation is wrong. Write H=∑i​DiT​Vi−1​Di​ and ui​=DiT​Vi−1​(Yi​−mi​). The fitted subject-level sandwich covariance matrix is
Var(γ​M​)=H−1(∑i​ui​uiT​)H−1,
(30)
evaluated at the estimates. This inference is asymptotic in the number of independent subjects, not the number of visits.
For the actual incomplete data, define Rij​=1 if visit j is observed, with Ri0​=1 and monotone dropout. Let Hi,j−1​ contain treatment, baseline covariates and responses observed before visit j. Under sequential missing at random, specify
qij​=P(Rij​=1∣Ri,j−1​=1,Hi,j−1​),ρij​=∏k=1j​qik​>0.
(31)
The assumption is that the retention decision, conditional on this history, does not additionally depend on unseen current or future outcomes. Fit the qij​, for example by visit-specific logistic regressions. A simple valid choice uses working independence and the inverse-observation-weighted estimating equations for longitudinal dropout:
i∑​j=1∑3​wij​ρij​Rij​​(Yij​−mij​)=0.​
(32)
Terms with Rij​=0 are zero and require no missing response. Given the full response vector, sequential missing at random makes E(Rij​/ρij​∣Yi​,zi​,xi​)=1. Consequently the weighted score has the same expectation as the complete-data score, proving its mean-zero property. Correct retention probabilities, positivity and a correctly specified marginal mean are required. Use cluster-robust uncertainty, accounting for fitted retention-model coefficients, or resample entire subjects and refit both models. Under missing completely at random, the simpler unweighted observed-response GEE can suffice; ordinary unweighted GEE is not valid under arbitrary history-dependent missing at random.
The treatment coefficient gives a population conditional-on-baseline odds ratio eϕM​ at each visit. To communicate an absolute population treatment benefit, standardize predicted probabilities over the full baseline population, rather than only completers:
p​z​(t)=m1​∑i​logit−1(αM​+ϕ​M​z+β​MT​xi​+δM​t),p​1​(t)−p​0​(t).
(33)
A causal reading additionally uses the randomized controlled trial assignment, consistency of the treatment definition, no interference and the dropout assumptions; the statistical model alone does not supply those conditions.
