Use the usual aggregate claims model assumption that the claim count N is independent of the sequence of severities. Conditional on N=n, the probability generating function of the sum is the product of the n severity generating functions. The empty sum has value zero. Thus the law of total expectation gives the random-sum transform identity
GS​(z)=∑n≥0​pn​GX​(z)n,GS​(z)=GN​(GX​(z))​,
(1)
for ∣z∣<1, with the usual continuous interpretation at z=1.
For a claim count distribution in the Panjer claim-count class, multiply the count recurrence by nzn−1 and sum. With m=n−1,
GN′​(z)=∑n≥1​(an+b)pn−1​zn−1=a∑m≥0​mpm​zm+(a+b)∑m≥0​pm​zm.
(2)
Consequently (1−az)GN′​=(a+b)GN​, or
GN′​(z)=1−aza+b​GN​(z).​
(3)
The chain rule applied to the composition then gives
(1−aGX​)GS′​=(a+b)GS​GX′​,GS′​=aGS′​GX​+(a+b)GS​GX′​.​
(4)

Move the j=0 term to the left and combine the remaining coefficients, since a(r−j)+(a+b)j=ar+bj. The Panjer recursion with zero severities is therefore
gr​=1−af0​1​j=1∑r​(a+rbj​)fj​gr−j​(r≥1).​
(6)
For the usual nondegenerate proper Panjer laws, a<1, so 1−af0​>0. If a degenerate count N≡0 is represented by redundant constants making this denominator zero, the aggregate is identically zero and must be handled directly rather than dividing by zero.
Because severities are nonnegative, the aggregate is zero exactly when every included severity is zero. Count-severity independence gives the initialization
g0​=P(S=0)=n≥0∑​pn​f0n​=GN​(f0​).​
(7)
In particular g0​ generally exceeds p0​ when zero severities are possible.
The count-severity independence is necessary and is implicit, rather than explicitly stated, in the printed random-sum setup. For a concrete counterexample without it, take independent Bernoulli-1/2 variables Xj​ and put N=X1​. Then S=X1​, so GS​(z)=(1+z)/2, whereas GN​(GX​(z))=(3+z)/4. Independence of the Xj​ among themselves alone cannot justify the requested formula.
