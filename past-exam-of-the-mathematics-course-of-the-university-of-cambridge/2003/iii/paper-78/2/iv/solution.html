At a welded interface, elastic-interface boundary conditions require continuous SH displacement and shear traction. Incident and reflected vertical slownesses have signs q1​,−q1​, giving
1+RSH​=TSH​,μ1​q1​(1−RSH​)=μ2​q2​TSH​.
(16)
Thus the local displacement coefficient is
TSH​(p)=μ1​q1​+μ2​q2​2μ1​q1​​​.
(17)
It multiplies the leading waveform discontinuity as well as a harmonic displacement. At the interface the incident amplitude from SH ray-tube amplitude transport is C/r1​​, where r1​=h/(β1​q1​) is the incident path length and C is the source normalization from the previous part.
The transmitted geometrical spreading is not simply its total path length. Differentiate the ray position with respect to p at fixed travel time and project onto the unit direction transverse to the transmitted ray, m2​=(β2​q2​,−β2​p). Since dxI​/dp=h/(β12​q13​), the transverse width per unit p is
J2​(t,p)=m2​⋅∂p​(x,z)=β2​[β12​q13​hq2​​+q2​t−t1​​].
(18)
The derivative of t1​ contributes only in the ray direction and drops out of this projection. At entry,
J2I​=β12​q13​β2​hq2​​,J1I​J2I​​=cosθ1​cosθ2​​,J1I​=β1​q12​h​.
(19)
Within uniform medium 2 the leading amplitude varies as J2−1/2​, so the required refraction of a cylindrical SH wavefront amplitude is
aT​(t,p)=r1​​CTSH​(p)​J2​(t,p)J2I​​​=β1​q1​h​+q22​β1​q12​​[t−t1​(p)]​CTSH​(p)​​.
(20)
The local transmission coefficient and ray-tube spreading together incorporate both impedance contrast and refraction. Indeed the normal energy transmission fraction is μ2​q2​∣TSH​∣2/(μ1​q1​), while the change of transverse width supplies the refraction factor in the conserved tube flux. If the media coincide, q1​=q2​ and TSH​=1, reducing this expression to C/β1​t​ as required. The formula holds for the stated propagating slownesses, without a critical or head-wave contribution.
