The full Love wave problem replaces the rigid base by a decaying substrate displacement. With Y=Acos(qz) in the layer and Y=Be−κ(z−h) below it,
q2=β′2ω2​−k2,κ2=k2−β2ω2​>0,tan(qh)=μ′qμκ​,
(24)
where μ′=ρ′β′2 and μ=ρβ2. Displacement continuity gives B=Acos(qh) and traction continuity gives μ′qsin(qh)=μκcos(qh). The rigid base is therefore a good approximation only when μκ≫μ′q: then the substrate displacement is small and qh lies close to χn​=(n+1/2)π.
Put ϵ=β′/β, η=μ′/μ, y=qh, W=ωh/β′ and K=kh. The complete dispersion branch can be parameterized without root finding:
W(y)=1−ϵ2​y1+η2tan2y​​,K(y)=1−ϵ2​yϵ2+η2tan2y​​,nπ<y<χn​​.
(25)
Then c=β′W/K and U=β′W′/K′. At the lower end, c→β and U→β. The Love-wave cutoff frequencies are ωc,n​=nπβ′/[h1−ϵ2​], with zero cutoff for the fundamental. Thus the rigid-layer formula cannot describe the fundamental's low-frequency limit or the low-frequency part of an overtone: it would either give no propagating mode or predict c>β, outside the trapping interval β′<c<β.
For comparable densities and a fixed mode index, η=(ρ′/ρ)ϵ2≪1. Write y=χn​−δ near the strong-dispersion transition. In the region controlling the group-velocity minimum of a high-contrast Love wave, δ≪1 and η/δ≫ϵ. The leading derivatives give
β′U​≃ηχn​δ2​+δη​.
(26)
Their minimum is at
δmin​∼(2η2χn​​)1/3,ωmin,n​∼hβ′χn​​=ωn​​,β′Umin,n​​∼22/33​(χn​η​)1/3.
(27)
The frequency estimate follows by inserting δmin​ into W≃(χn​−δ)[1+η2/(2δ2)+ϵ2/2]: the two leading terms of size δ cancel. The real group velocity has a finite minimum and turns upwards near the rigid-layer threshold, whereas the rigid formula has zero group speed at its artificial threshold.
For a useful estimate of the validity range, put Δ=W−χn​>0. The rigid prediction has KR2​=W2−χn2​≃2χn​Δ. The finite-load phase correction is δ≃ηχn​/(κh); its changes to both K and dK/dW are small if
Δ≫max{χn​ϵ2,(η2χn​)1/3}​.
(28)
The first condition keeps KR​ away from the substrate propagation boundary; the second controls the rapidly changing interface phase. Equivalently, the rigid formulas are reliable sufficiently above the actual group-velocity minimum, beyond its narrow transition interval. At fixed ω/ωn​>1 separated from one, they become asymptotically accurate as the contrast grows. They are particularly simple at ω≫ωn​, where both velocities approach β′. Within the transition and below it the full dispersion relation is required.
The small speed ratio alone is insufficient if the layer density grows so strongly that μ′/μ is not small. For such materials the exact load criterion μκ≫μ′q and its frequency derivatives should be used; the preceding contrast estimates assume a bounded order-one density ratio and fixed n.
