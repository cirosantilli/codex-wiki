Let d(y,t)=[u[n]] denote the signed opening, positive when the faces separate, and let S be the crack surface. Its displacement jump is b=dn. The singular strain associated with this jump has moment density cijkl​bk​nl​. For the isotropic elastic stiffness tensor, contraction gives
mij​(y,t)=d(y,t)[λδij​+2μni​(y)nj​(y)].
(38)
The equivalent distributional body force is the negative divergence of this prescribed moment density:
fi​(x,t)=−∂xj​​∫S​d(y,t)[λδij​+2μni​(y)nj​(y)]δ(3)(x−y)dSy​​.
(39)
The resulting forced elastic wave in an isotropic solid equation is ρui,tt​=∂j​σij​+fi​. This is a tensile-crack seismic source; it need not be represented as an ordinary smooth volume force. For a small nearly planar patch of area S0​, uniform normal n and opening d(t), define its volume change V(t)=S0​d(t). Its seismic moment tensor and point-source force are
Mij​(t)=V(t)(λδij​+2μni​nj​),fi​=−Mij​(t)∂xj​​δ(3)(x−y0​)​.
(40)
The sign convention makes a positive opening a positive extensional moment. Reversing the convention for the displacement jump changes both signs together; the force is independent of reversing the orientation of the crack normal.
