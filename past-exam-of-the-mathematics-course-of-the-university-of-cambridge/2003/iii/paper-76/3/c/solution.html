Assume the jump is stationary, short compared with the scale of width variation, hydrostatic on either side, and has negligible bed stress integrated over its short length. Treat its width bj​=32/27​ as constant. It must occur on the downstream supercritical branch, at xj​=Lbj​−1​>0. The discharge from the control gives the local unit-width discharge
qj​=bj​Q​,qj2​=4g′H3​.
(20)
Before the jump the energy is still H. Writing y=h/H, its equation is y+1/(8y2)=1. Its positive roots are 1/2 and (1+5​)/4; the former is supercritical. Thus
h1​=2H​,u1​=g′H​,F12​=2.
(21)
Mass conservation gives h1​u1​=h2​u2​=qj​. The hydraulic jump momentum balance conserves qj2​/h+g′h2/2. Factoring out the non-jump solution h2​=h1​ gives the conjugate-depth relation
h1​h2​​=21+8F12​​−1​.
(22)
Consequently the conditions immediately after the jump are
h2​=417​−1​H,u2​=17​−12g′H​​=817​+1​g′H​.​
(23)
Its Froude number is below one. The specific-energy loss is
E1​−E2​=4h1​h2​(h2​−h1​)3​>0.​
(24)
Selecting the deeper root of the unchanged energy equation would be wrong: that would conserve the energy across a turbulent jump instead of the momentum.
