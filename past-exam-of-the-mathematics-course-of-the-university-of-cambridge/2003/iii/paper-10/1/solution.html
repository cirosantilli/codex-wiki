Here “subspace” is understood to mean a closed closed linear subspace. Closedness matters: the finitely supported sequences c00​ are an infinite-dimensional algebraic subspace of ℓp, but contain no infinite-dimensional complete subspace. Indeed, any such subspace would be the countable union of its intersections with the finite-dimensional coordinate spaces, contrary to the Baire category theorem. We prove the assertion with the intended closedness convention, for 1≤p<∞.
Write E=ℓp, the l-p sequence space. Choose positive numbers δn​ with ∑n​2δn​<1. Starting with m0​=0, choose xn​∈X with ∥xn​∥p​=1 and its first mn−1​ coordinates zero. This is possible because the kernel of finitely many coordinate linear functionals on an infinite-dimensional vector space is still infinite-dimensional. Choose mn​>mn−1​ so that the part of xn​ after coordinate mn​ has norm below δn​. Let vn​ be its restriction to In​=(mn−1​,mn​], and put un​=vn​/∥vn​∥p​. The triangle inequality gives
∥xn​−un​∥p​≤∥xn​−vn​∥p​+∣1−∥vn​∥p​∣<2δn​.
(1)
The vectors un​ form a normalized block basic sequence. Their supports are disjoint, so
∥∑n​an​un​∥pp​=∑n​∣an​∣p.
(2)
Consequently U=span​{un​} is isometric to the l-p sequence space.
For each block choose a norm-one linear functional ϕn​ supported on In​ with ϕn​(un​)=1. The Hahn-Banach theorem supplies such a functional, including when p=1. Define
Qz=∑n​ϕn​(z)un​.
(3)
The series converges in the l-p sequence space, since
∥Qz∥pp​=∑n​∣ϕn​(z)∣p≤∑n​∥z∣In​​∥pp​≤∥z∥pp​.
(4)
Also Qun​=un​, so Q is a bounded projection onto U. Now define the bounded linear operator
A=I+∑n​ϕn​(⋅)(xn​−un​).
(5)
This series converges in operator norm and ∥A−I∥≤∑n​2δn​<1. The Neumann series therefore makes A invertible. Disjoint support gives Aun​=xn​. Thus Y=AU=span​{xn​} lies in the closed subspace X, is isomorphic to the l-p sequence space, and is the range of the bounded projection AQA−1 on the whole space E. This proves the required copy of ℓp is complemented in the ambient ℓp, not just in X.
Now let Z be an infinite-dimensional complemented subspace of E. The construction supplies Y⊂Z with Y≅E and a bounded projection R:E→Y. Its restriction to Z gives the topological direct sum
Z=Y⊕W,W=Z∩kerR.
(6)
If P:E→Z is a bounded projection, then (I−R)P is a bounded projection onto W: its range lies in W, and it fixes every element of W. Hence W is a complemented subspace of E, so E≅W⊕V for some closed V.
We give the absorption argument in the Pełczyński decomposition method explicitly. Reindexing coordinates by a bijection between N2 and N yields E≅(⨁n≥1​E)p​. Applying the fixed Banach space isomorphism E≅W⊕V in every coordinate gives
E⊕W≅(⨁n≥1​(W⊕V))p​⊕W≅(⨁n≥1​W)p​⊕(⨁n≥1​V)p​⊕W≅E.
(7)
The extra W is absorbed by shifting the countably many W summands. The maps are bounded with bounded inverses because the same two-space decomposition is used uniformly in each summand; we may equip each finite direct sum with its equivalent p-sum norm. Since Z≅E⊕W, Z≅ℓp.
