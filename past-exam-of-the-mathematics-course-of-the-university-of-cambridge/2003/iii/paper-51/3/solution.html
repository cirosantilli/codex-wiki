Let Gn​(z1​,…,zn​) denote the ordered vacuum correlator. Inserting the vacuum-preserving Möbius transformation operator and its inverse gives
Gn​(z1​,…,zn​)=∏i​γ′(zi​)hi​Gn​(γ(z1​),…,γ(zn​)).
(14)
For γ(z)=z+ϵv(z), the three vector fields v=1,z,z2 yield the global conformal Ward identities for chiral correlators
∑i​∂i​Gn​=0,∑i​(zi​∂i​+hi​)Gn​=0,∑i​(zi2​∂i​+2hi​zi​)Gn​=0.
(15)
All formulas below concern separated insertion points and a consistent local choice of branches for fractional powers.
For one insertion, the translation identity makes G1​ constant and the dilation identity gives h1​G1​=0. Thus
G1​=C1​ if h1​=0;G1​=0 if h1​=0​.
(16)
For two insertions, translation invariance gives G2​=f(z12​), where zij​=zi​−zj​. Dilations imply z12​f′+(h1​+h2​)f=0, so f=C12​z12−h1​−h2​​. Substituting into the special conformal identity leaves (h1​−h2​)z12​f=0. Therefore
G2​=z122h​C12​​,h1​=h2​=h​,
(17)
with G2​=0 when the weights differ.
For three insertions write G3​=C123​z12−a​z13−b​z23−c​. Under a Möbius transformation, γ(zi​)−γ(zj​)=zij​γ′(zi​)γ′(zj​)​ with compatible local branches. The covariance condition therefore fixes
a+b=2h1​,a+c=2h2​,b+c=2h3​.
(18)
Solving gives
G3​=z12h1​+h2​−h3​​z13h1​+h3​−h2​​z23h2​+h3​−h1​​C123​​​.
(19)
This is the complete solution because Möbius transformations act transitively on ordered triples of distinct points: the ratio of any two covariant nonzero solutions has no remaining invariant variable and is constant. Three-point covariance imposes no equality or triangle inequality among the weights. Additional fusion rules or other symmetries may force C123​=0, but none follow from the stated covariance and vacuum assumptions alone.
For more than three generic points, three positions may be fixed by a Möbius transformation, leaving n−3 independent complex conformal cross-ratios. Correlators consequently have a covariant prefactor multiplied by an arbitrary function of these invariants. For example one convenient four-point form is
G4​=z12h1​+h2​​z34h3​+h4​​(z24​/z14​)h1​−h2​(z14​/z13​)h3​−h4​​F(χ),χ=z13​z24​z12​z34​​.
(20)
The Möbius invariance of the cross-ratio guarantees covariance for any F. Thus global conformal symmetry fixes one-, two- and three-point functions up to constants, but does not fix higher-point functions. Their remaining functions encode dynamics.
