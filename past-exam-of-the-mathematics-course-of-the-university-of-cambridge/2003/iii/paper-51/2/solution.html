The stress-energy tensor generates translations. One may improve it by adding a divergence without changing the translation charges under suitable boundary conditions, and choose it symmetric in a relativistic theory. If Θμν is symmetric and conserved, a spacetime vector field ω defines the current jμ=Θμνων​. For a Conformal Killing vector field,
∂μ​jμ=Θμν∂μ​ων​=d∂⋅ω​Θμμ​.
(7)
Thus a traceless conserved stress-energy tensor produces all the conformal charges. The dilation current is xν​Θμν, and the special conformal currents are (2xλ​xν​−x2ηλν​)Θμν. A trace that is merely the divergence of a virial current gives scale invariance; conformal invariance requires the additional improvement that removes the trace. These statements distinguish an actual traceless tensor from a trace discarded without justification.
For a free massless scalar field, the canonical stress-energy tensor has trace (1−d/2)(∂ϕ)2. For d>1, the improved stress-energy tensor of a free massless scalar is
Θμν​=∂μ​ϕ∂ν​ϕ−21​ημν​(∂ϕ)2+4(d−1)d−2​(ημν​□−∂μ​∂ν​)ϕ2.

Its improvement is identically conserved. On □ϕ=0, the identity □ϕ2=2(∂ϕ)2 shows that its trace vanishes. In d=2, no improvement is needed: the canonical stress-energy tensor is already traceless. In Lorentzian null coordinates, conservation and tracelessness imply that its two surviving components depend separately on x+ and x−. After Euclidean continuation, these become the holomorphic stress-energy tensor T(z) and its antiholomorphic counterpart Tˉ(zˉ), with
∂ˉT=0,∂Tˉ=0.
(9)
Local conformal changes are arbitrary holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinate changes, not just the three global Möbius transformation generators in either sector.

On a curved background the related trace anomaly is proportional to its curvature; it does not destroy the flat-space conformal symmetry of this massless theory.
