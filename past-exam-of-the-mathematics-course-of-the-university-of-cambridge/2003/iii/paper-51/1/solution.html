Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2003 / iii / Paper 51 / 1 / Solution

OurBigBook.com Words: 233
The pullback of the flat Euclidean metric under x′μ=xμ+ϵωμ(x) is
δμν​+ϵ(∂μ​ων​+∂ν​ωμ​)+O(ϵ2).
(1)
For a conformal transformation it must be a scalar multiple of δμν​. Taking the trace determines that multiple. With ρ=∂α​ωα, the necessary and sufficient infinitesimal condition is the Conformal Killing equation
∂μ​ων​+∂ν​ωμ​=d2ρ​δμν​​.
(2)
Differentiate its (α,β) instance with respect to xγ, its (α,γ) instance with respect to xβ, and its (β,γ) instance with respect to xα. Adding the first two and subtracting the last, commuting partial derivatives, leaves 2∂β​∂γ​ωα​. Hence flat conformal Killing integrability gives
∂β​∂γ​ωα​=d1​(δαβ​∂γ​ρ+δαγ​∂β​ρ−δβγ​∂α​ρ)​.
(3)
Contracting β with γ gives Δωα​=(2−d)∂α​ρ/d. Its divergence is Δρ=(2−d)Δρ/d, so 2(d−1)Δρ=0: thus Δρ=0 for d>1​. Taking instead the α divergence of the displayed second-derivative identity gives
d∂β​∂γ​ρ=2∂β​∂γ​ρ−δβγ​Δρ.
(4)
Consequently ∂β​∂γ​ρ=0 for d>2​.
On a connected flat domain, write ρ=dλ+2db⋅x, with constant λ and b. Integrating the second-derivative identity, and imposing the Conformal Killing equation on its remaining affine part, gives the complete Conformal Killing vector field
ωμ(x)=aμ+Rμν​xν+λxμ+2(b⋅x)xμ−bμx2​,Rμν​=−Rνμ​.
(5)
Indeed the quadratic part has precisely the required Hessian matrix, while the symmetric part of the remaining linear term must be λδμν​. The constants generate translations, rotations, dilations and special conformal transformations, respectively. There are d, d(d−1)/2, 1 and d independent parameters, so the full conformal group has dimension
2(d+1)(d+2)​​.
(6)
Here “full” means local transformations, or global transformations of the conformal compactification of Euclidean space; its connected group is locally SO(d+1,1). Requiring every transformation to be nonsingular on all of uncompactified Rd would exclude the special conformal transformations with finite poles.
