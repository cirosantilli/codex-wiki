Use units in which the proton electric charge is one. The up quark and down quark both have spin angular momentum 1/2 and form a flavour isospin doublet:
u: Q=32​, I=21​, I3​=21​;d: Q=−31​, I=21​, I3​=−21​.​
(1)
Their antiquarks have the opposite charges and third components of isospin, with the same total isospin and spin. This is strong-interaction flavour isospin, not the chiral weak isospin assignment.
The Clebsch-Gordan decomposition for SU2 says that spins j1​,j2​ combine into every j from ∣j1​−j2​∣ to j1​+j2​ in unit steps. It applies independently to spin and isospin. Thus
21​⊗21​=0⊕1,21​⊗21​⊗21​=23​⊕21​⊕21​.
(2)
Before exchange symmetry is imposed, three light quarks have total spin S=1/2 or 3/2, and total isospin I=1/2 or 3/2; the doublet occurs twice in the uncoupled tensor cube. With zero orbital angular momentum, J=S.
Physical baryons must also be colour singlets. The three-quark colour singlet is completely antisymmetric, so the Pauli exclusion principle requires symmetric spatial-spin-flavour symmetry. For a symmetric spatial S-wave state, the spin-flavour factor is symmetric. The I=3/2 flavour quartet is symmetric, whereas the I=1/2 flavour doublets have mixed permutation symmetry; the spin factors have the analogous symmetries. Symmetric flavour therefore pairs with symmetric spin, while the two mixed factors pair in their unique symmetric combination. This is the Pauli constraint on three-quark flavour multiplets; it leaves
(I,J)=(21​,21​)and(23​,23​).​
(3)
Equivalently the symmetric cube of the four-dimensional two-flavour spin space has dimension (36​)=20=2⋅2+4⋅4. The unmatched spin-isospin pairings in the bare tensor product do not describe the usual symmetric-spatial ground-state baryons.
For three light quarks, charge addition gives Q=I3​+1/2. The observed nucleon doublet and Delta baryon quartet have the following assignments; the listed contents specify flavour counts, not the complete spin-flavour wavefunction:
statepnΔ++Δ+Δ0Δ−​contentuududduuuuududdddd​I1/21/23/23/23/23/2​I3​1/2−1/23/21/2−1/2−3/2​Q10210−1​​
(4)
The proton and neutron have J=1/2; all four Delta baryons have J=3/2. These states have positive parity in the zero-orbital ground-state model.
For a light quark-antiquark meson, both spin and flavour doublets combine into singlet plus triplet. In a conventional choice of phases the flavour states are
(I,I3​)(1,1)(1,0)(1,−1)(0,0)​flavour stateud(uu−dd)/2​du(uu+dd)/2​​Q10−10​​
(5)
Each row can have J=0 or 1, since the two constituent spins couple to zero or one. At zero orbital angular momentum their parity is negative, following the meson parity and charge conjugation rule P=(−1)L+1. The spin-zero isotriplet is the pion multiplet, and the spin-one isotriplet the rho meson multiplet. The nonstrange spin-one isosinglet is the idealized omega meson flavour direction. Its spin-zero counterpart is the nonstrange direction contributing to the eta and eta prime mesons; it is not automatically a physical mass eigenstate once other flavours and mixing are included.
Now introduce an isospin-zero quark h of spin 1/2 and charge qh​. For baryons with one isospin-singlet quark, the two light quarks have either the antisymmetric flavour singlet [ud]=(ud−du)/2​ or the symmetric triplet uu,{ud},dd. Their colour pair is antisymmetric. Hence light-diquark spin-isospin correlation gives Sud​=0 for I=0 and Sud​=1 for I=1. Coupling the h spin produces
light pair[ud]uu{ud}dd​I0111​J1/21/2 or 3/21/2 or 3/21/2 or 3/2​Qqh​+1/3qh​+4/3qh​+1/3qh​−2/3​​
(6)
The three triplet rows have I3​=1,0,−1. For a strange-like charge qh​=−1/3, these are the neutral spin-one-half Lambda baryon, the spin-one-half Sigma baryon triplet of charges 1,0,−1, and its spin-three-half starred partners. For a charm-like charge qh​=2/3, they are the spin-one-half Lambda c baryon of charge 1, and the Sigma c baryon triplets of charges 2,1,0 with spins 1/2 and 3/2. Both possible new charge assignments have thus been covered; charge-conjugate antibaryons have opposite charges and unchanged I,J.
The meson doublets with one isospin-singlet quark are hu,hd, with
I=21​,I3​=(−21​,21​),Q=(qh​−32​,qh​+31​),J=0 or 1.​
(7)
For qh​=−1/3 their charges are −1,0, giving K−,K0-like kaons and their vector partners; the conjugate doublet has uh,dh and charges 1,0. For qh​=2/3 the charges are 0,1, giving D0,D+-like D mesons and their vector partners, with charge-conjugate partners as well. Finally a hidden-flavour pair hh, if included, is neutral with I=0 and J=0 or 1. These are quantum-number classifications; charge and isospin alone do not determine particle masses or mixing angles.

The invariant tensors of SU(3) include δβα​, ϵαβγ​ and its upper-index counterpart. Consequently an antisymmetric pair of upper fundamental indices can be converted into one lower antifundamental index, and conversely; a completely antisymmetric triple is a determinant singlet. In the Young-symmetrizer tensor decomposition, complete columns of three can therefore be removed. A remaining diagram has rows of lengths k+l,l: its two-box columns become l dual indices and its remaining boxes give k defining indices. The irreducible highest-weight component is represented by a mixed SU(3) tensor representation symmetric in all upper indices, symmetric in all lower indices, and with every upper-lower contraction removed. Such contractions produce the lower-weight components. The resulting symmetric traceless SU(3) tensor representation has Dynkin labels (k,l); this is an equivalent canonical realization, not a claim that an arbitrary tensor already has those symmetries.
There are (2k+2​) symmetric upper-index components and (2l+2​) symmetric lower-index components. All contractions are equivalent under the separate symmetries. Their image is the separately symmetric tensor space with (k−1,l−1) indices. To subtract its dimension legitimately, we must show that contraction is onto.
Represent such tensors by polynomials of bidegree (k,l) in three variables x and three dual variables y. Up to a nonzero normalization, contraction is
Δ=∑a=13​∂xa​​∂ya​​,q=∑a=13​xa​ya​.
(8)
If P has bidegree (k−1,l−1), differentiation gives
Δ(qj+1ΔjP)=qj+1Δj+1P+(j+1)(k+l+1−j)qjΔjP.
(9)
Choose a0​=1/(k+l+1) and aj​=−aj−1​/[(j+1)(k+l+1−j)]. Then
Q=∑j=0min(k−1,l−1)​aj​qj+1ΔjP
(10)
has bidegree (k,l) and ΔQ=P: consecutive terms cancel, and the last further derivative vanishes. This constructs a preimage of every contraction target. If k=0 or l=0, there is no contraction. Therefore the number of trace-free components is
dimR(k,l)​=(2k+2​)(2l+2​)−(2k+1​)(2l+1​)=41​(k+1)(k+2)(l+1)(l+2)−41​k(k+1)l(l+1)=21​(k+1)(l+1)(k+l+2)​.​
(11)
The highest-weight vector x1k​y3l​ lies in this kernel. The Young-symmetry construction identifies the traceless highest-weight component with the irreducible representation of labels (k,l); equivalently its Weyl dimension formula agrees with the independently calculated kernel dimension above.
For traceless matrices T,S, the tensor square of the SU(3) adjoint representation decomposes as
8⊗8=1⊕8S​⊕8A​⊕10⊕10⊕27.​
(12)
The component maps make the decomposition concrete. The singlet is tr(TS). The two octets are the antisymmetric commutator [T,S] and symmetric traceless anticommutator {T,S}−32​tr(TS)I. Symmetrizing the two upper and two lower indices in Tβα​Sδγ​ and removing contractions gives R(2,2), the 27. Its highest component is already visible in E13​⊗E13​.
For the 10, contract the two lower indices with ϵβδρ and symmetrize the three upper indices α,γ,ρ; for its conjugate, contract the upper pair with ϵαγρ​ and symmetrize the three lower indices. These maps are antisymmetric under exchange of T,S. They are nonzero: T=E12​,S=E13​ produces a nonzero all-upper-1 component, and T=E21​,S=E31​ produces the conjugate all-lower-1 component. Thus they reach the irreducible R(3,0) and R(0,3), each of dimension ten. The octet maps are also nonzero, for example on a pair of noncommuting matrix units and on equal nonzero traceless diagonal matrices. These equivariant maps supply distinct irreducible constituents, with the two octets separated by exchange symmetry. Their dimensions exhaust the product:
dimSym28=36=1+8+27,dim⋀28=28=8+10+10,36+28=64.
(13)
An octet operator between baryon octet states defines an intertwining operator 8operator​⊗8state​→8state​. Its covariance follows from the commutator transformation law in the question. By Schur lemma, the number of independent reduced matrix elements is the multiplicity of the output octet in the product: there are exactly two independent coefficients. To display them, use a Hermitian octet matrix basis Bs​=λs​, ti​=λi​/2, normalized by tr(λr​λs​)=2δrs​. The two allowed actions are
B⟼F[ti​,B]+D({ti​,B}−32​tr(ti​B)I).
(14)
With [λi​,λs​]=2ifisk​λk​ and {λi​,λs​}=34​δis​I+2disk​λk​, this gives the two reduced octet matrix elements:
⟨Br​∣Vi​∣Bs​⟩=Ddris​+iFfris​,​
(15)
up to the common normalization absorbed into F,D. All component dependence is fixed by the invariant symmetric and antisymmetric tensors. For a Hermitian operator and this Hermitian basis, F,D may be chosen real; neither their values nor a relation between them follows from symmetry alone.

Work first in a finite-dimensional SU(2) representation, with its invariant positive inner product. In the complexified Lie algebra, choose H†=H and (E−)†=E+. Let v0​ be a highest-weight vector with Hv0​=mv0​ and E+v0​=0, and put vr​=(E−)rv0​. The commutator with H gives
Hvr​=(m−2r)vr​.
(16)
Induction also gives
E+vr​=r(m−r+1)vr−1​.
(17)
For r=1 this is E+E−v0​=Hv0​=mv0​. For the induction step, use E+E−=E−E++H on vr​: the coefficient becomes r(m−r+1)+m−2r=(r+1)(m−r). In particular
E+E−vr​=(r+1)(m−r)vr​=41​(m+n)(m−n+2)vr​,n=m−2r,
(18)
which proves the assumed formula rather than merely invoking it.
The norm identity ∥E−vr​∥2=(r+1)(m−r)∥vr​∥2 shows that, for nonnegative integer m, the ladder is nonzero for 0≤r≤m and stops with E−vm​=0. Distinct eigenvalues make these vectors independent. Their span is stable under H,E±, and in the irreducible module it is the full space. Thus
n=m,m−2,…,−m,dimV(m)=m+1,j=m/2.​
(19)
For normalized states, E−∣n⟩=21​(m+n)(m−n+2)​∣n−2⟩, with the last state killed. General finite-dimensional SU(2) representations are direct sums of these ladders.
The representation category matters here. The Lie-algebra relations alone allow an infinite Verma module even at integral highest weight: its nonzero vector vm+1​ is singular. The finite representation can instead be constructed algebraically by taking the basis v0​,…,vm​ with the displayed actions and declaring E−vm​=0; all three Lie brackets then hold, including at the endpoints. Thus the finite-dimensional assertion uses the integrable SU(2) module, or this explicit finite quotient, rather than assuming that a singular vector must vanish in every highest-weight module.
For rank two, take a Cartan subalgebra h with commuting basis elements. A root α is a nonzero linear functional on h for which a root space contains an element Eα​ satisfying
[h,Eα​]=α(h)Eα​(h∈h).
(20)
Choose simple roots α1​,α2​ and their coroots Hi​=αi∨​. In the PDF's index convention, the Cartan matrix is
Kij​=αi​(Hj​)=(αj​,αj​)2(αi​,αj​)​.
(21)
Normalize Ei±​=E±αi​​ so [Ei+​,Ei−​]=Hi​. The difference of distinct simple roots is not a root, so the mixed positive-negative bracket is zero. This gives
[Hi​,Hj​]=0,[Ei+​,Ej−​]=δij​Hj​,[Hi​,Ej±​]=±Kji​Ej±​.
(22)
The transpose in the last formula is essential and is printed correctly in the original PDF.
A highest-weight vector of labels (n1​,n2​) is killed by both Ei+​ and is an eigenvector of the two Hi​. Repeated words in E1−​,E2−​ generate its highest-weight representation; each lowers the weight by the corresponding simple root. Restricting to the sl2 subalgebra associated with a root for either simple root gives the preceding integer ladder, so finite-dimensional highest labels must be nonnegative integers. The finite irreducible module is the highest-weight quotient imposing (Ei−​)ni​+1v=0 and the submodules generated by these relations, together with the Serre relations. Choosing independent nonzero lowering words gives a simultaneous weight basis; an extra multiplicity index is needed where a weight space has dimension greater than one.
For the specified B2 root system, the simple-root squared lengths are 1,2 and their inner product is −1, giving
K=(2−2​−12​).
(23)
The positive root α1​+α2​ is generated by [E1+​,E2+​]=0, and 2α1​+α2​ by [E1+​,[E1+​,E2+​]]=0. No further positive sums occur, so (adE1+​)3E2+​=0 and (adE2+​)2E1+​=0. These are the relevant Serre relations. Applying the Cartan commutators to a weight vector proves
E1−​: (n1​,n2​)↦(n1​−2,n2​+1),E2−​: (n1​,n2​)↦(n1​+2,n2​−2),​
(24)
whenever the image is nonzero.
To enumerate the requested representation without guessing when arrows stop, realize it concretely as the Adjoint representation. For a Euclidean weight (x,y), its labels are (n1​,n2​)=(2x,−x+y). The highest root 2α1​+α2​=(1,1) has labels (2,0), so it generates the irreducible adjoint module of this simple rank-two Lie algebra. Its nonzero weights are exactly its eight roots, and its zero weight space is the two-dimensional Cartan subalgebra. This gives the B2 adjoint representation weight diagram and the nine distinct states labels:
(2,0), (0,1), (−2,2), (2,−1), (0,0), (−2,1), (2,−2), (0,−1), (−2,0).​
(25)
Their actual production by lowering can be seen from
(2,0)(0,1)(−2,2)(0,0)(−2,1)(0,−1)​1​(0,1),1​(−2,2),(0,1)2​(2,−1),2​(0,0),(2,−1)1​(0,0),1​(−2,1),(0,0)2​(2,−2),2​(0,−1),(2,−2)1​(0,−1),1​(−2,0).​
(26)
An arrow labelled i denotes a nonzero action of Ei−​ between the indicated weight spaces. The two paths into zero produce independent Cartan vectors. Hence there are nine distinct pairs, as requested when degeneracy is disregarded, but the full representation has dimension ten, not nine.

Let Ta​=adTa​​ on the Lie algebra itself. The Jacobi identity says, for every X,
[Ta​,[Tb​,X]]−[Tb​,[Ta​,X]]=[[Ta​,Tb​],X].
(27)
Therefore [Ta​,Tb​]=cabc​Tc​, proving that the displayed structure-constant matrices form the Adjoint representation of a Lie algebra.
The bilinear form κab​=tr(Ta​Tb​) is the Killing form. It is symmetric by cyclicity of trace. Its invariance follows by expanding the two commutators:
κ([Tc​,Ta​],Tb​)+κ(Ta​,[Tc​,Tb​])​=tr([Tc​,Ta​]Tb​+Ta​[Tc​,Tb​])=tr([Tc​,Ta​Tb​])=0.​
(28)
In components this is precisely
κdb​ccad​+κad​ccbd​=0.​
(29)
Equivalently, the adjoint action preserves the bilinear form, so it is an invariant tensor.
The invariant abelian subalgebra is an abelian ideal of a Lie algebra a. Assume it is nonzero, as required for the conclusion. For X∈a and Y∈g, the map adX​adY​ sends g into a, because it is an ideal. It vanishes on a, because adY​ preserves the ideal and adX​ kills it. Relative to a and a vector-space complement, this map has zero diagonal blocks, so its trace is zero. Thus
κ(X,Y)=0(X∈a, Y∈g).
(30)
This proves that abelian ideals lie in the radical of the Killing form. Choose a nonzero X=xaTa​ in the ideal. Then κba​xa=0 for every b, so the coordinate vector x is a nonzero null eigenvector of the Killing matrix. The nonzero-ideal qualification cannot be dropped: the zero ideal is abelian even in a semisimple algebra with nondegenerate Killing form.
Now suppose κ is invertible and let Cκ​=κbctb​tc​ in any Lie algebra representation. In matrix notation invariance is AaT​κ+κAa​=0, where (Aa​)db​=cabd​. Multiplying by the inverse form gives Aa​κ−1+κ−1AaT​=0. Hence
[ta​,Cκ​]​=κbc(cabd​td​tc​+cacd​tb​td​)=(cadb​κdc+κbdcadc​)tb​tc​=0.​
(31)
No commutativity of the representation matrices has been assumed. This proves the centrality of the quadratic Casimir operator formed with the inverse Killing form. Schur lemma makes it a scalar on every finite-dimensional complex irreducible representation.
For SU(2), make the normalization explicit. In a Hermitian angular-momentum basis with [Ja​,Jb​]=iϵabc​Jc​, the structure-constant matrices obey
κab​=∑c,d​(iϵadc​)(iϵbcd​)=2δab​.
(32)
Thus Cκ​=21​∑a​Ja2​. In the compact anti-Hermitian basis Ta​=−iJa​, the brackets have real structure constants ϵabc​ and the Killing form of the SU(2) Lie algebra is instead −2δab​; the inverse contraction gives the same Cκ​. This sign change is a basis convention, not a different spectrum.
In the complex highest-weight basis (H,E+,E−) used earlier, the same Killing form and its inverse are
κ=​800​004​040​​,κ−1=​1/800​001/4​01/40​​.
(33)
Consequently
Cκ​=8H2​+4E+E−+E−E+​.
(34)
On a highest vector of weight m=2j, the ladder calculation gives E+E−v=mv and E−E+v=0. Its Casimir eigenvalue is therefore
8m2​+4m​=8m(m+2)​=21​j(j+1),j=0,21​,1,….​
(35)
Centrality gives the same value throughout the irreducible multiplet. This is the Killing-normalized SU(2) quadratic Casimir; the familiar angular-momentum operator ∑a​Ja2​ has eigenvalue j(j+1) and is twice this inverse-Killing-form contraction.
