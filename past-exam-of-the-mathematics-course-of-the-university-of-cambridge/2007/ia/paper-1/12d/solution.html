For a bounded function, a partition of an interval is P:0=x0​<x1​<⋯<xN​=1. Put mj​=inf[xj−1​,xj​]​f and Mj​=sup[xj−1​,xj​]​f. Its lower Darboux sum and upper Darboux sum are
L(f,P)=∑j=1N​mj​(xj​−xj−1​),U(f,P)=∑j=1N​Mj​(xj​−xj−1​).
(49)
The lower Darboux integral is supP​L(f,P) and the upper Darboux integral is infP​U(f,P). Riemann integrability means that these two real numbers coincide, and their common value is the Riemann integral.
Equivalently, the Riemann integrability criterion says that for every ε>0 some partition satisfies U(f,P)−L(f,P)<ε. To see equivalence, all lower sums are at most all upper sums: refine any two partitions to their common refinement and use Darboux sum refinement monotonicity. Thus a small gap forces the difference of the two integrals below ε. Conversely, if both integrals equal I, choose a lower sum above I−ε/2 and an upper sum below I+ε/2; their common refinement has gap below ε.
This also agrees with the tagged Riemann sum definition: there is I such that every tagged sum on a partition of sufficiently small mesh is within any prescribed tolerance of I. Each tagged sum lies between the two Darboux sums. From a partition with small gap, an arbitrary small-mesh partition differs from its refinement at only the cells crossing the finitely many original division points; boundedness makes their total contribution tend to zero. Conversely, on a fine partition tags approximating each cell supremum or infimum force both Darboux sums close to the same tagged-sum limit.
Now suppose f is continuous on [0,1]. It is bounded by the result of Question 11. It is also uniformly continuous: otherwise there are xn​,yn​∈[0,1] with ∣xn​−yn​∣→0 but ∣f(xn​)−f(yn​)∣≥ε0​>0. The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem gives a convergent subsequence of xn​; the corresponding yn​ have the same limit, contradicting continuity. Given ε>0, this uniform continuity gives δ>0 such that points less than δ apart have function values less than ε/2 apart. A partition of mesh below δ then satisfies
U(f,P)−L(f,P)≤2ε​∑j​(xj​−xj−1​)=2ε​<ε.
(50)
Hence every continuous function on the interval is Riemann integrable, with the required proof supplied by its oscillation estimates.
