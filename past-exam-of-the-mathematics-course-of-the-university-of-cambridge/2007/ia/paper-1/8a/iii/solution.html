For real x at which the denominator is nonzero, mapping to the unit circle gives ∣ax+b∣2=∣cx+d∣2. There are infinitely many such real x, so the resulting quadratic polynomial identity has all coefficients zero:
∣a∣2=∣c∣2,Re(ab)=Re(cd),∣b∣2=∣d∣2.
(37)
If c=0, the first equality gives a=0, contradicting the nonzero determinant ad−bc. Thus a,c=0. Put λ=a/c, k=b/a and ℓ=d/c. The equalities become
∣λ∣=1,Rek=Reℓ,∣k∣=∣ℓ∣.
(38)
Two complex numbers with equal real parts and equal moduli have imaginary parts equal in magnitude. Consequently ℓ=k or ℓ=k. The first option would give ad−bc=ac(ℓ−k)=0, so it is excluded. The second gives
w=λz+kz+k​,∣λ∣=1.​
(39)
Moreover k must be nonreal, since otherwise the two options coincide and the map would be constant. This proves the Möbius transformations from the real axis to the unit circle representation directly from coefficients.
Conversely, for real x, the numbers x+k and x+k are complex conjugates, and their moduli agree. Nonreal k ensures the denominator is nonzero on the real line; multiplication by λ preserves modulus. At the point at infinity on the Riemann sphere, the map takes the value λ. Thus the representation also respects the extended real axis.
