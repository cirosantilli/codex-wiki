Write u(x,y)=U(ξ,η). The chain rule gives ∂x​=∂ξ​+∂η​ and ∂y​=2∂ξ​+3∂η​. Consequently the coefficients of Uξξ​ and Uηη​ cancel, while that of Uξη​ is 12−25+12=−1. The transformed partial differential equation is −Uξη​=1. Integrating once in each variable gives the most general twice differentiable solution,
u(x,y)=−(x+2y)(x+3y)+F(x+2y)+G(x+3y),​
(14)
where F,G are arbitrary twice differentiable functions. The change of variables is invertible because its Jacobian determinant is 3−2=1.
On y=0, the value condition gives F(x)+G(x)=x2. Differentiating it yields F′(x)+G′(x)=2x. The derivative condition gives −5x+2F′(x)+3G′(x)=x. Subtraction therefore gives G′(x)=2x and F′(x)=0. The constants in F and G cancel in their sum, leaving
u(x,y)=(x+3y)2−(x+2y)(x+3y)=xy+3y2.​
(15)
Indeed uxx​=0, uxy​=1, uyy​=6, so the original operator gives −5+6=1, and the two initial-line conditions hold.
