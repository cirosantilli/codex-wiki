The divergence of a cross product identity and the stated static field equations give
∇⋅(E×B)=B⋅(∇×E)−E⋅(∇×B)=−E⋅J.
(31)
For an outward-oriented closed surface, the divergence theorem therefore gives the flux of the specified Poynting vector normalization:
∬∂V​P⋅dS=−∭V​E⋅JdV.​
(32)
No open-surface conclusion follows from this equation without including its closing pieces.
For the particular fields, direct calculation gives
J=∇×B=(0,−z,−y),P=E×B=(x2y,−xz2,−xyz),∇⋅P=xy.
(33)
The fields also have ∇×E=0, ∇⋅B=0, and ∇⋅J=0, consistently with the required equations. Close the outward spherical patch by the two planar half-disks on x=0 and y=0, forming the quarter unit ball V. Its total closed flux is
∭V​xydV=∫0π/2​cosφsinφdφ∫01​r3dr∫−1−r2​1−r2​​dz=∫01​r31−r2​dr=152​,
(34)
where r in this integral is cylindrical radius. The x=0 face has outward normal −ex​ and zero flux because Px​=0 there. The y=0 face has outward normal −ey​, so its flux is
∫−11​∫01−z2​​xz2dxdz=21​∫−11​z2(1−z2)dz=152​.
(35)
Subtracting these planar contributions from the closed flux gives the requested open-patch answer
∬spherical patch​P⋅dS=0.​
(36)
As a direct check, on the unit sphere the outward normal is (x,y,z) and P⋅n=xy(x2−2z2). With spherical angles 0≤ϑ≤π, 0≤φ≤π/2, its surface integral is 41​∫0π​sin5ϑdϑ−∫0π​sin3ϑcos2ϑdϑ=41​(16/15)−4/15=0. The curved patch's vanishing flux is a cancellation, not an inference that its flux density vanishes everywhere.
