Define the binomial coefficient (kn​) to be the number of k-element subsets of an n-element set. Equivalently, count ordered selections and divide by the k! orders of each subset to obtain (kn​)=n!/[k!(n−k)!], with (0n​)=(nn​)=1.
Fix one distinguished element. The k-subsets not containing it are counted by (kn−1​); those containing it correspond bijectively to (k−1)-subsets of the remaining n−1 elements. These are disjoint and exhaustive classes, proving Pascal's identity:
(kn−1​)+(k−1n−1​)=(kn​).​
(4)
For the summation, use mathematical induction on k, retaining an arbitrary fixed n≥0. At k=0, both sides equal one. Suppose ∑j=0k​(jn+j​)=(kn+k+1​). Adding the next term and applying Pascal's identity gives
∑j=0k+1​(jn+j​)=(kn+k+1​)+(k+1n+k+1​)=(k+1n+k+2​).
(5)
This is the required statement for k+1, completing the induction, including n=0. Hence the hockey-stick identity in this form is
j=0∑k​(jn+j​)=(kn+k+1​)(n,k≥0).​
(6)
