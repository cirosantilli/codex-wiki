The permutation representation on the complex vector space with basis X has permutation character πX​(g)=∣FixX​(g)∣. The multiplicity of the trivial character is ⟨πX​,1G​⟩=∣G∣−1∑g​∣Fix(g)∣. Count pairs (g,x) with gx=x: each orbit contributes ∣G∣, by the orbit-stabilizer theorem. Hence this multiplicity equals the number of orbits.
For a transitive action the average number of fixed points is one. The identity fixes ∣X∣>1 points, so not every other element can fix at least one; otherwise the average would exceed one. Thus there is a derangement g with πX​(g)=0.
If πX​=1G​+mχ, evaluating at that derangement gives χ(g)=−1/m. Character values are algebraic integers, since a finite-order representation matrix has roots of unity as eigenvalues, and the sum of algebraic integers is integral. A rational algebraic integer is an integer. Therefore −1/m is an integer, forcing m=1.
