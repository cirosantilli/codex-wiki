Choose coordinates z at p and w at f(p) vanishing at those points. The local expansion is w∘f∘z−1(ζ)=aζv+O(ζv+1), a=0. Define the branching order vf​(p)=v. A change of either coordinate has nonzero linear coefficient, so it preserves the leading exponent, proving well-definedness.
Ramification means v>1, equivalently vanishing of the derivative in local coordinates. These zeros are isolated unless the local derivative is identically zero, which by analytic continuation would make f constant. An infinite set of ramification points on a compact surface would have an accumulation point, contradicting isolation. Thus that set is finite.
A nonconstant holomorphic map of compact connected Riemann surfaces is onto, since its image is open by the local form and closed by compactness. It has a finite degree d≥1. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula is
2gR​−2=d(2gS​−2)+∑p∈R​(vf​(p)−1).
(46)
If both genera equal g>1, positivity of 2g−2 forces d=1 and every ramification term zero. The map is bijective and has nonzero derivative everywhere. The holomorphic inverse function theorem says that a holomorphic function with nonzero derivative has a local holomorphic inverse; these inverses patch, so f is biholomorphic. For g=1, multiplication by two on C/Λ is a degree-four holomorphic map of an elliptic curve to itself and is not biholomorphic.
