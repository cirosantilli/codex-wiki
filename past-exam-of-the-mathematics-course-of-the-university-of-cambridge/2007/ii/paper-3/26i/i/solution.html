For a prior Π and loss L, the Bayes risk of a rule d is r(Π,d)=∫Eθ​L(θ,d(X))Π(dθ). A Bayes decision rule minimizes this integrated risk. An Extended Bayes rule is a rule whose excess over the optimal Bayes risk can be made arbitrarily small by a suitable proper prior: for every ε>0 some Π has r(Π,d)≤infd′​r(Π,d′)+ε, with finite risks.
Condition on the observation and use iterated expectation. A rule is Bayes precisely when it chooses a minimizer of the posterior expected loss almost surely, up to the usual measurability requirement. Under squared Euclidean loss, with a finite posterior second moment, write m=E[θ∣X]. The identity
E[∥θ−a∥2∣X]=E[∥θ−m∥2∣X]+∥m−a∥2
(57)
shows that the Bayes rule is the posterior mean.
