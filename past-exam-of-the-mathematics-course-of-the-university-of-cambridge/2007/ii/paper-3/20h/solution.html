An action assigns each g∈G a homeomorphism of X, with e⋅x=x and g⋅(h⋅x)=(gh)⋅x. For the covering assertion use a properly discontinuous free action: every x has an open neighbourhood U such that gU∩U=∅ for every g=e. Then the translates gU are pairwise disjoint. The quotient map p is open because p−1(p(U))=⋃g​gU is open. Each restriction p:gU→p(U) is a continuous open bijection and hence a homeomorphism. Thus p(U) is evenly covered and p:X→X/G is a covering map.
For the surjective homomorphism one additionally needs X path connected; choose x0​ and its image y0​. Lift a based loop at y0​ from x0​. Its endpoint has a unique form gx0​. Homotopy lifting makes this assignment well-defined on the fundamental group. With concatenation meaning first the first loop and then the second, the second lift from gx0​ is g times the lift from x0​, so the endpoint is ghx0​. This proves the homomorphism property. For each g, a path in X from x0​ to gx0​ projects to a loop realizing g. Hence
π1​(X/G,y0​)↠G.​

Pointwise freeness alone is insufficient for a covering: an irrational rotation action of Z on the circle is free but its orbits are dense. Path connectedness is also essential for the final surjectivity claim as written: the discrete space X=G with left translation is a free properly discontinuous action whose quotient is a point, and its trivial fundamental group cannot surject onto nontrivial G. The proof supplies the standard connected covering-space interpretation rather than hiding these hypotheses.
