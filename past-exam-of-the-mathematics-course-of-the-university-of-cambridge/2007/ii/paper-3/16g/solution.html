The first exponent is determined by the preceding band, the first coefficient by maximality, and the tail by the ordinal interval left after that initial segment. Recursion therefore proves uniqueness of every exponent and coefficient. The PDF's second coefficient is printed α1​ rather than a1​; the stated natural-number coefficients and the construction identify the intended expression.
For the final assertion, powers of ω are additively indecomposable: if τ<ωη, then τ+ωη=ωη for η>0. One proves this by induction on η. At a successor, bound τ by a finite multiple of ωη−1 and use the supremum over all such multiples; at a limit, bound τ by a smaller power and use continuity. For η=0 the only possible τ is zero. Thus a smaller leading exponent is absorbed when a larger-leading ordinal is added on its right. If β,γ have unequal leading exponents, either β+γ=γ or γ+β=β. If their leading exponents coincide, the leading coefficient of either sum is the sum of their two positive leading coefficients; it exceeds each coefficient, so neither absorption equality holds. Zero is never commensurable with an ordinal. Consequently commensurability is exactly equality of the leading exponent, equivalently membership of both positive ordinals in one of the stated bands.
