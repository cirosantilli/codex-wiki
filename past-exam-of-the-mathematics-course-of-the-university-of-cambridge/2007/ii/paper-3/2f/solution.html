The polynomial form of Runge theorem says that a function holomorphic on a neighbourhood of a compact set K⊂C with connected complement can be approximated uniformly on K by polynomials.
For n≥3, take
Kn​={0}∪{z:1/n≤∣z∣≤1−1/n, 0≤argz≤2π−1/n}.
(3)
The complement is connected: the missing angular sector connects the inner disk to the exterior, and removing the single point zero from the inner disk does not disconnect it. On a neighbourhood of the annular sector choose an analytic logarithm with arguments between −1/(2n) and 2π−1/(2n). Its exponential exp(3logz/2) agrees with the prescribed function on the sector. On a disjoint small neighbourhood of zero prescribe the analytic function zero. These two neighbourhoods define a holomorphic function gn​ near Kn​ with gn​=f on Kn​.
By Runge theorem, choose a polynomial pn​ with supKn​​∣pn​−gn​∣<1/n. Every fixed nonzero point of the disk eventually belongs to all Kn​, including points on the positive real axis; zero belongs to every Kn​. Consequently
pn​(z)⟶f(z)for every z∈D.​
(4)
Each polynomial restricts to an analytic function on D. The excluded sector lies immediately below the positive axis and shrinks with n, so the construction correctly preserves the chosen value on the branch cut. It gives pointwise convergence, not local uniform convergence across that cut. This is pointwise approximation by a Runge exhaustion.
