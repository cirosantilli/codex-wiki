With σ=1, substitute x=(u+v)/2, y=(u−v)/2, r=1+μ. The equations become
u˙=21​(μ−z)(u+v),v˙=−2v−21​(μ−z)(u+v),z˙=41​(u2−v2)−bz,μ˙​=0.​
(32)
The extended centre variables are u,μ. The centre manifold theorem gives smooth graph functions V,Z with V(0,0)=Z(0,0)=0 and all first derivatives zero. Their invariance equations are Vu​u˙=v˙ and Zu​u˙=z˙, after substitution of the graph.
Count μ as order u2. Then u˙=O(u3) on the graph, so the left side of the Z equation contributes only at higher order. Its quadratic terms give 0=u2/4−bZ, hence Z=u2/(4b)+O(u3). The cubic terms of the V equation similarly give 0=−2V−μu/2+uZ/2. Thus
Z=4bu2​+O(u3),V=−4μu​+16bu3​+O(u4).​
(33)
Terms purely in μ vanish because the origin remains an equilibrium for every parameter; these expansions describe the weighted scaling μ=O(u2), not an unrestricted one-variable Taylor series. Substitution gives the reduced equation
u˙=2μ​u−8b1​u3+O(u4).​
(34)
Its cubic coefficient is negative. For μ<0 the origin is attracting in the centre direction; for μ>0 it loses stability and two attracting branches emerge with u∼±2bμ​. Therefore the bifurcation is a supercritical pitchfork. These branches agree with the exact Lorenz equilibria x=y=±bμ​, z=μ.
