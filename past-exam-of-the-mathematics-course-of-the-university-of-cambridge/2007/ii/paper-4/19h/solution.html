We consider finite-dimensional continuous complex representations of SU(2). Its maximal torus is diag(eiθ,e−iθ). The irreducible representations are
Vn​=Symn(C2),n=0,1,2,…,dimVn​=n+1.​
(55)
They may be realized as homogeneous polynomials of degree n in two variables, with the induced linear substitution action. Their torus weights are n,n−2,…,−n, each once. The centre −I acts by (−1)n, so precisely the even-n representations descend to SO(3). In physics n=2j labels integral or half-integral spin j.
Here is a classification argument. Average any positive definite Hermitian form over the compact group using normalized Haar measure. The averaged form is invariant, so the orthogonal complement of an invariant subspace is invariant. Repeatedly splitting gives complete reducibility. Differentiate an irreducible representation and complexify to the Lie algebra sl2​, with generators H,E,F satisfying [H,E]=2E, [H,F]=−2F, and [E,F]=H. Torus periodicity gives integer H-weights. Choose a vector v of maximal weight n; then Ev=0. Induction on k using the commutators gives
EFkv=k(n−k+1)Fk−1v.
(56)
The chain terminates: if Fpv=0 and Fp+1v=0, the displayed formula forces n=p≥0. The vectors v,Fv,…,Fnv have distinct weights, span an invariant subrepresentation, and hence span the irreducible representation. Their generator action agrees with SymnC2. Invariance under the Lie algebra is equivalent to invariance under the connected group, completing the classification. Conversely, a nonzero invariant subspace of a symmetric power contains a weight vector by projection onto torus weights. Raising it to the highest weight and then lowering generates every monomial, proving that the symmetric power is irreducible.
The character on the torus is
χn​(θ)=∑k=0n​ei(n−2k)θ=sinθsin((n+1)θ)​,
(57)
with the endpoints interpreted by continuity. Every representation has a unique decomposition V≅⨁n≥0​mn​Vn​. Its multiplicities can be computed from character inner products,
mn​=∫SU(2)​χV​(g)χn​(g)​dg=π2​∫0π​χV​(θ)χn​(θ)sin2θdθ.​
(58)
The sine orthogonality directly verifies orthonormality of the irreducible characters. Alternatively, if wk​ is the dimension of the weight-k subspace, the weight lists give wn​=mn​+mn+2​+⋯, so mn​=wn​−wn+2​ for n≥0. This gives a constructive decomposition from torus weights. In particular, multiplying weight characters yields the Clebsch-Gordan decomposition Vm​⊗Vn​≅Vm+n​⊕Vm+n−2​⊕⋯⊕V∣m−n∣​. Arbitrary unitary Hilbert-space representations of this compact group similarly decompose into Hilbert direct sums of these finite-dimensional irreducibles; the finite-dimensional statement above is the standard representation-theory setting.
