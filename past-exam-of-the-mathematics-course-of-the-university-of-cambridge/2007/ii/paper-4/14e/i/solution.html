The fixed-point equation is x[ax2+bx+μ−1]=0. Thus
x=0,x±​=2a−b±b2+4a(1−μ)​​​

when the discriminant is nonnegative. At zero the multiplier is F′(0)=μ, so it is linearly stable for −1<μ<1 and unstable for ∣μ∣>1. At a nonzero fixed point, eliminate μ to obtain the branch and its multiplier:
μ=1−bx−ax2,M=1+bx+2ax2.

The branch is stable precisely when −2<x(b+2ax)<0. These formulas specify stability throughout the diagram, including any extra flip points outside the region requested.
If b=0 and a>0, the two nonzero branches x=±(1−μ)/a​ exist for μ<1 and have multiplier 3−2μ>1. Thus μ=1 is a subcritical pitchfork bifurcation. If b=0, the branch through zero has x∼(1−μ)/b and M∼2−μ; its stability exchanges with zero at μ=1, a transcritical bifurcation. The two nonzero roots meet at
μsn​=1+b2/(4a),xsn​=−b/(2a),
(37)
where M=1. The graph of μ(x) has nonzero second derivative −2a, proving a saddle-node bifurcation. At zero and μ=−1, the multiplier is −1, indicating a period-doubling bifurcation; generically its nondegeneracy coefficient is a+b2. If a+b2=0, necessarily b=0 here, composition at μ=−1 instead gives F2(x)−x=4b4x5+O(x6). For δ=μ+1>0 small, the leading return equation is −2δx+4b4x5=0, producing a two-cycle with leading amplitudes x∼±[δ/(2b4)]1/4. Its return multiplier is 1+8δ+o(δ)>1. Thus this special case still has a flip bifurcation, but it is degenerate and subcritical rather than the generic cubic normal form.
For a,b>0, the fold lies above μ=1. Between it and the transcritical point, the branch −b/(2a)<x<0 is stable provided its multiplier remains above −1, while the other branch is unstable. For a,b<0, the fold lies below μ=1: immediately above it the branch x<−b/(2a) is stable and the branch between −b/(2a) and zero is unstable; the branch crossing zero becomes stable for μ>1. All these statements are subject to the displayed exact multiplier criterion if the diagram is extended. The sketch uses representative parameter values and stops before additional flips, as stipulated.
