Reduce the polynomial modulo p, obtaining a nonzero degree-d polynomial over the field Fp​. If a is a root, polynomial division gives fˉ​(X)=(X−a)h(X). Any different root b is a root of h, because b−a=0 is invertible. Induction on degree therefore proves there are at most d distinct roots modulo p.
Every nonzero residue is a root of Xp−1−1 by Fermat's little theorem. The difference between this monic polynomial and the product over all its p−1 nonzero roots has degree at most p−2, yet has p−1 roots. The root bound forces it to vanish identically over Fp​. Thus all coefficients of the corresponding integer polynomial are divisible by p.
For the requested failure modulo p2, take p=7: 6!+1=721=7⋅103, and 103 is not divisible by seven. Hence the prime-square strengthening fails, even though some special primes do satisfy it.
