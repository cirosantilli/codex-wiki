Take an open neighbourhood U on which p is one-to-one and a homeomorphism onto its image. By invariance of domain for topological surfaces, that image is open. Shrink U so the image lies in a complex chart (V,z) of R, and use z∘p as a chart of R. These charts cover R. On any overlap their transition is the transition between the original charts of R, restricted to an open set, so is holomorphic with holomorphic inverse. They define the desired complex structure, and in these charts p is locally the identity. Thus p is holomorphic. The openness supplied by invariance of domain is important when the hypothesis is phrased only as a homeomorphism onto the image.
Let f:C→Y be holomorphic and let π:D→Y be the stated covering. Choose a lift of f(0). The covering-space lifting theorem says that a map from a simply connected, locally path-connected domain has a unique lift after fixing this initial point: lift paths from zero, and homotopy lifting makes their endpoint independent of path. Since C is simply connected, this constructs f​:C→D with π∘f​=f. On a small neighbourhood, the lift is a biholomorphic local inverse of π composed with f, so it is holomorphic. Its modulus is less than one, and Liouville theorem therefore makes it constant. Hence every holomorphic map C→Y is constant.
