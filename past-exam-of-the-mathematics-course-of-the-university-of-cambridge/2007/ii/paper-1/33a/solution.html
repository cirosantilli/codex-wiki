The incoming and outgoing coefficients give the scattering matrix
S(k)=g(−k)g(k)​=(k−iκ)(k−iα)(k+iκ)(k+iα)​.​
(56)
It obeys S(−k)=S(k)−1 and S(k∗)∗=S(k)−1, so ∣S(k)∣=1 for real k, as required by elastic unitarity. With S=e2iδ, the scattering phase shift satisfies tanδ=k(κ+α)/(k2−κα). The inverse tangent needs a continuous branch: taking δ(∞)=0 gives δ(0)=π. Equivalently δ=arctan(κ/k)+arctan(α/k) for k>0.
The scattering length follows from kcotδ=−1/as​+O(k2):
as​=κακ+α​.​
(57)
For a putative bound state put k=iη, η>0. The incoming term grows as eηr, so its coefficient must vanish. The incoming Jost function is F(k)=g(−k)=(k−iκ)/(k+iα); its only upper-half-plane zero is iκ. Under the regular-potential assumption this gives one bound state, with E=−ℏ2κ2/(2m).​ The pole at iα in S instead comes from the outgoing numerator: it is a redundant pole of a scattering matrix, not a second normalizable state. The S-matrix alone, without its incoming/outgoing analytic structure, would tempt an incorrect count of two states.
For completeness, the nonsingular Eckart realization of this Jost function requires α>κ, as discussed in dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/373712/4/2306.12216.pdf. The supplied positive constants alone do not establish such a realization in every ordering; a coincident pole is likewise not evidence for two states.
There is no positive-energy scattering resonance. Indeed δ′(k)=−κ/(k2+κ2)−α/(k2+α2)<0, and the s-wave total partial-wave total scattering cross-section is 4π(κ+α)2/[(k2+κ2)(k2+α2)], strictly decreasing for k>0. All poles are on the imaginary axis, with no oscillatory decaying resonance pole.
