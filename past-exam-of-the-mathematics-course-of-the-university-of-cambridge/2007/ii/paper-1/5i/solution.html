The reference category is adult females. The age and gender coefficients are conditional log odds ratios: the fitted odds for a child are multiplied by e−1.27079≈0.281, and those for a male by e−0.37211≈0.689.
The coefficients are maximum-likelihood estimates, found by Newton's method or iteratively reweighted least squares. With design matrix X and Wjj​=Nj​p​j​(1−p​j​), the estimated covariance matrix is (XTWX)−1; the printed standard errors are square roots of its diagonal entries. Each Wald test compares a coefficient with zero using its estimate divided by its standard error, approximately a standard normal distribution under the null. The age and gender effects are strongly significant. The intercept test instead tests whether the reference probability is 1/2.
The fitted probabilities are about 0.04476 for adult females, 0.03128 for adult males, 0.01298 for female children, and 0.008981 for male children. Adult females have the largest fitted probability, about 4.48%. The residual deviance is 0.06514 on 4−3=1 degree of freedom; comparison with a chi-squared distribution gives p≈0.799. There is no evidence here that an age–gender interaction is needed, under the assumed independent binomial distribution model.
