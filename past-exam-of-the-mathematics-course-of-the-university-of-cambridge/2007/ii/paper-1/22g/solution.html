The continuous dual space X∗ consists of bounded linear functionals with operator norm ∥v∥=sup∥x∥≤1​∣v(x)∣. For an operator-norm Cauchy sequence (vn​), each (vn​(x)) is Cauchy in R. Define v(x)=limn​vn​(x). It is linear, and bounded because the Cauchy sequence has uniformly bounded norms. Passing to the pointwise limit in ∣(vn​−vm​)(x)∣≤ε∥x∥ gives ∥vn​−v∥≤ε. Thus X∗ is a Banach space, even when X is incomplete.
The canonical map ϕ(x)(v)=v(x) is linear and satisfies ∥ϕ(x)∥≤∥x∥. For x=0, the norm-one functional tx↦t∥x∥ on its span extends by the Hahn-Banach theorem to a norm-one functional on X. Hence ∥ϕ(x)∥≥∥x∥, proving that ϕ is an isometry and therefore injective.
For a nonsurjective example take X=c0​ with the supremum norm. It is complete as a closed subspace of ℓ∞. Every functional is given by an ℓ1 sequence: finite-coordinate sign tests give summability of its coefficients, and truncation of each c0​ vector gives the series representation. Conversely any ℓ1 sequence defines such a bounded functional. Similarly (ℓ1)∗=ℓ∞, so c0∗∗​=ℓ∞. The canonical image is the null sequences; the constant sequence one is outside it. Thus c0​ is a nonreflexive Banach space.
