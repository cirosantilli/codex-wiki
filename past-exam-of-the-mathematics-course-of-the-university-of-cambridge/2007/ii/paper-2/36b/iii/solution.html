Let x increase away from the tip along the wall and y point normally into the fluid. Put u=ux​, v=uy​ for velocity components and U(x)=−A/x2 for the outer tangential velocity. The local Cartesian momentum and pressure equations are
u∂x​u+v∂y​u=−ρ−1px​+ν∂y2​u=UU′+ν∂y2​u,py​=0.
(91)
For a two-dimensional flat layer the usual continuity equation is ∂x​u+∂y​v=0. For this axisymmetric cone, however, the circumference is proportional to x, so its leading continuity equation is
∂x​u+xu​+∂y​v=0,or∂x​(xu)+∂y​(xv)=0.​
(92)
The circumference term is of the same order as ∂x​u and cannot be omitted when deriving the cone's similarity equation. Conditions at the stationary wall are u=v=0; at the layer edge u→U.
Balance U2/x∼ν∣U∣/δ2. With ∣U∣=A/x2 this gives
δ(x)=Aν​​x3/2,xδ​=Re(x)−1/2≪1.​
(93)
The choice of multiplicative constant fixes the normalization of the similarity variable. To enforce axisymmetric continuity set xu=Ψy​, xv=−Ψx​, with
Ψ=−Aν​x1/2F(η),η=δy​.
(94)
Then
u=−x2A​F′(η),v=Aν​x−3/2(21​F−23​ηF′).
(95)
Differentiation gives u∂x​u+v∂y​u=−(A2/x5)[2F′2+FF′′/2], whereas UU′=−2A2/x5 and νuyy​=−A2F′′′/x5. Consequently the conical sink boundary layer satisfies
F′′′−21​FF′′+2(1−F′2)=0,F(0)=0,F′(0)=0,F′(∞)=1.​
(96)
The first condition is no penetration, the second no slip, and the last matches the inward core flow. If one instead makes a strictly planar approximation and omits the circumference term, the streamfunction is −Aν​x−1/2F and the equation becomes F′′′+FF′′/2+2(1−F′2)=0. The thickness exponent is the same, but that planar equation is not the axisymmetric cone equation.
Across a cap near R=x, the inward flux in a layer truncated at y=Y is, to leading order,
Qlayer​(R,Y)=2πRsinα∫0Y​(−u)dy=2πAsinαRδ(R)​F(Y/δ).
(97)
To match to the core, use the finite displacement-flux deficit rather than integrate the uniform outer flow to infinite Y. If I=∫0∞​(1−F′)dη=limη→∞​(η−F), then
ΔQ=2πAsinαRδ(R)​I=2πsinαIAν​R1/2.
(98)
Conservation of the fixed total extraction rate requires an extra core flux of this order. Dividing by cap area, proportional to R2, proves
uR,outer​=−R2A​+O(Aν​R−3/2).​
(99)
The correction is a relative O(νR/A​) contribution. Its angular structure requires outer matching; the requested radial power follows from the displacement flux alone. Its cap-average direction increases the inward core speed to compensate for the wall deficit.
