Write p=8k+7. In Gauss's lemma, for the multiplier 2, precisely the integers j with p/4<j≤(p−1)/2 give residues 2j>p/2. Their number is
2p−1​−⌊4p​⌋=(4k+3)−(2k+1)=2k+2.
(1)
It is even, so the Legendre symbol (2/p)=1: 2 is a quadratic residue modulo p. For completeness, the sign rule follows by replacing those large residues by their negatives; the resulting absolute residues permute 1,…,(p−1)/2. Multiplying them and cancelling their nonzero product gives 2(p−1)/2≡(−1)2k+2=1(modp), equivalent to the residue assertion by Euler's criterion.
Now take the prime p=2q+1. The condition q≡3(mod4) gives p≡7(mod8), so 2q≡1(modp). Thus p divides 2q−1. For integers q>3, 2q−1>2q+1 (true at 4 and preserved on increasing q), so this divisor is proper and 2q−1 is composite. At q=3, the divisor is the number itself: 23−1=2⋅3+1=7. The congruence still holds, but it does not establish compositeness.
