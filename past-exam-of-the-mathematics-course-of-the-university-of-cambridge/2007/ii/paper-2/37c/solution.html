For an isentropic perfect gas, p=Kργ and c2=dp/dρ. The one-dimensional Euler equations give Dρ=−ρux​ and Du=−c2ρx​/ρ, where D=∂t​+u∂x​. Since dc/dρ=(γ−1)c/(2ρ), these become
Du=−γ−12c​cx​,Dc=−2γ−1​cux​.
(100)
Adding the appropriate ±c∂x​ terms shows
(∂t​+(u±c)∂x​)[u±γ−12(c−c0​)​]=0.​
(101)
These are the Riemann invariants. A simple wave is a solution in which one invariant is spatially and temporally constant, so all local state variables depend on the other one. For a right-moving wave connected to rest, the minus invariant is zero and c=c0​+(γ−1)u/2. The plus characteristic then has speed u+c=c0​+(γ+1)u/2, giving
ut​+(c0​+2γ+1​u)ux​=0.​
(102)
For the supplied damped equation put b=(γ+1)/2. Along x˙=c0​+bu, u˙=−αu, so an initial label x0​ gives
u=v(x0​)e−αt,x−c0​t=x0​+αb​(1−e−αt)v(x0​).​
(103)
This solves the initial-value problem while the label map remains invertible. Its derivative is 1+(b/α)(1−e−αt)v′(x0​). Nonnegative slopes cannot cause crossing; for negative slopes, the condition α>bsupv′<0​∣v′∣ keeps that derivative strictly positive. Hence the stated damping condition prevents shock formation at every finite time. Equality also avoids a finite-time crossing, although the derivative may tend to zero as time tends to infinity. The strict inequality in the question is sufficient rather than necessary.
