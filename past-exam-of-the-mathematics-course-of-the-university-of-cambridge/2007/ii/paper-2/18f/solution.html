An automorphism sends the primitive root of unity ξn​ to another primitive root of unity, hence uniquely to ξna​ with a∈(Z/nZ)∗. Define χ(σ)=a. Composition multiplies exponents, and an automorphism with exponent one fixes the generator and thus all of L. Therefore χ is an injective group homomorphism and G is abelian.
The extension is finite Galois: it is the splitting field of Xn−1, whose roots are all present and distinct. Existence of a root of order n in positive characteristic implies that the characteristic does not divide n. The Fundamental theorem of Galois theory identifies an intermediate field with LH. Since G is abelian, H is normal; the normal-subgroup criterion gives LH/K Galois, with group G/H.
For a non-surjective nontrivial example, take K=Q(i) and n=8. Its cyclotomic extension has degree two, and its image is {1,5} inside the four-element group (Z/8Z)∗. For K=Q and prime p, Φp​(X)=1+⋯+Xp−1 is irreducible: Φp​(X+1) is Eisenstein at p. Thus the extension has degree p−1, equal to the unit group's order, proving surjectivity.
