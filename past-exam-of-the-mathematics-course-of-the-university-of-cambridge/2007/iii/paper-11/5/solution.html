On a connected Riemann surface R, a Perron family of subharmonic functions is a nonempty family P of real continuous subharmonic functions closed under pairwise maxima and under harmonic lifting on every relatively compact coordinate disk. A harmonic lifting of a continuous subharmonic function replaces its values inside such a disk by the harmonic Poisson integral of its boundary values, leaving the function unchanged outside. Harmonic comparison shows that the lift dominates the original function. The lift is continuous and remains subharmonic by harmonic comparison across the circle.
Write U=supv∈P​v. Because the family is nonempty and its members are finite, U cannot be minus infinity. Suppose first U(p)<∞, and choose a relatively compact coordinate disk V containing p. Choose vn​∈P approaching U(p) at p. Lift v1​ on V to get h1​, and recursively lift max(hn−1​,vn​) to get hn​. These are members of the family, harmonic on V, and increasing. Also hn​(p)→U(p).
The nonnegative harmonic functions hn​−h1​ are bounded at p. The Harnack inequality for harmonic functions, on chains of compact subdisks, bounds them uniformly on every compact subset of V. The Poisson integral then bounds their derivatives on smaller subdisks. Compact convergence and the mean value property show that their increasing limit is finite and harmonic; denote the limit of hn​ by h.
Fix an arbitrary v∈P, and let kn​ be the lift of max(v,hn​) on V. These functions increase, are harmonic on V, and satisfy hn​≤kn​≤U. They are bounded at p, so the same Harnack inequality for harmonic functions argument gives a harmonic limit k. At p, k(p)=h(p)=U(p), whereas k≥h on V. The nonnegative harmonic difference has an interior zero, so the strong maximum principle for harmonic functions gives k=h. Since v≤kn​, we have v≤h. Taking the supremum over v gives U≤h, and hn​≤U gives the opposite inequality. Thus U=h on V.
The finite locus of U is open by this argument. It is also closed: if a point is a limit of finite points, choose a coordinate disk around it and a finite point in that disk; the preceding argument applied with that finite point shows that U is finite on the entire disk. The family of all continuous subharmonic functions bounded above by zero is a Perron family of subharmonic functions, since the maximum principle for harmonic functions keeps every lift below zero; it contains zero, so its supremum is zero. The family of constant functions with positive integer values is also a Perron family of subharmonic functions, and its supremum is everywhere infinite.
