For a finite-dimensional Lie algebra, the Killing form is defined using the Adjoint representation on the algebra itself, rather than the given Lie algebra representation d:
κ(X,Y)=TrL​(adX​adY​),adX​(Z)=[X,Z].
(1)
The Jacobi identity gives ad[X,Y]​=[adX​,adY​]. Using the cyclic property of the trace,
κ([X,Y],Z)​=Tr(adX​adY​adZ​−adY​adX​adZ​)=Tr(adX​[adY​,adZ​])=κ(X,[Y,Z]).​
(2)
The same trace identity makes the Killing form symmetric. For a basis (ta​), the lowered structure constants of a Lie algebra are cabc​=κ([ta​,tb​],tc​). Antisymmetry of the Lie bracket gives cabc​=−cbac​, while invariance gives cabc​=κ(ta​,[tb​,tc​])=−cacb​. The two adjacent transpositions generate every permutation, proving the antisymmetry of Killing-lowered structure constants: cabc​ is totally antisymmetric, even if the Killing form is degenerate.
A semisimple Lie algebra has no nonzero solvable ideal; equivalently, its solvable radical is zero, or, in characteristic zero, its Killing form is nondegenerate. A real compact Lie algebra is the algebra of some compact Lie group, equivalently an algebra admitting a positive-definite invariant bilinear form on a Lie algebra. For a real semisimple Lie algebra the compactness criterion from the Killing form says precisely that κ is negative-definite. A compact algebra with a nonzero abelian center has a degenerate Killing form, so the semisimplicity qualification matters.
For a semisimple Lie algebra, let κab denote the inverse matrix of κab​=κ(ta​,tb​). Its Casimir operator in d is
C=κabd(ta​)d(tb​).​
(3)
This contraction is independent of the chosen basis. To prove centrality directly, put [X,tb​]=Aab​ta​. Invariance gives ATκ+κA=0, hence Aκ−1+κ−1AT=0. The Lie algebra representation property and the product rule for a commutator now give
[d(X),C]=κab(Aca​d(tc​)d(tb​)+Acb​d(ta​)d(tc​))=0.
(4)
Indeed, the coefficient of d(tc​)d(tb​) in this expression is (Aκ−1+κ−1AT)cb. Thus [d(X),C]=0 for every X. No irreducibility of d is required.
The matrix units obey Eij​Epq​=δjp​Eiq​. Consequently the general linear Lie algebra M=gln​(R) has
[Eij​,Epq​]=δjp​Eiq​−δqi​Epj​,cij,pq​mn=δjp​δim​δqn​−δqi​δpm​δjn​.​
(5)
For its Killing form, write LX​(Z)=XZ and RX​(Z)=ZX, so that adX​=LX​−RX​. Left multiplication acts identically on each of the n columns, and right multiplication identically on each of the n rows. Thus Tr(LX​LY​)=Tr(RX​RY​)=ntr(XY). The diagonal coefficient of LX​RY​ on Eij​ is Xii​Yjj​, giving Tr(LX​RY​)=trXtrY; the other mixed trace is identical. This proves the Killing form of the general linear Lie algebra:
κ(X,Y)=2ntr(XY)−2trXtrY,κij,pq​=2nδjp​δiq​−2δij​δpq​.​
(6)
The scalar matrices form a nonzero central abelian ideal, so M is not semisimple for any n≥1. More explicitly, the radical of the Killing form is RI, since vanishing against every Y forces nX−(trX)I=0 by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing. For n≥2, take H=E11​−E22​. Then κ(H,H)=4n>0, whereas a positive-definite invariant inner product would make every adjoint map skew and hence give κ(X,X)≤0. Therefore M is not compact when n≥2. For the allowed exceptional value n=1, the abstract abelian algebra R is a compact Lie algebra, being isomorphic to the algebra of U(1); the particular matrix group GL1​(R) is nevertheless noncompact. This distinguishes compactness of an algebra from that of a chosen group realizing it.
For the final algebra, the printed basis specifies strictly upper triangular matrices. If i<j and p<q, the first term in [Eij​,Epq​] can be nonzero only when i<j=p<q, and then Eiq​ is strictly upper triangular; the second can be nonzero only when p<q=i<j, and then Epj​ is strictly upper triangular. Bilinearity proves closure under the Lie bracket. To compute its own Killing form, grade Eij​ by the positive integer j−i. Bracketing with any basis element increases that degree whenever the result is nonzero. In a basis ordered by degree, all adjoint maps are strictly triangular, as are their products. Every such product has zero trace, so the Killing form of a nilpotent Lie algebra vanishes here. Therefore the subspace denoted by a prime in the PDF is
L′=L,dimL′=2n(n−1)​.​
(7)
This is the defined radical of the Killing form, not the derived algebra; for n=1 both L and this radical are zero.
