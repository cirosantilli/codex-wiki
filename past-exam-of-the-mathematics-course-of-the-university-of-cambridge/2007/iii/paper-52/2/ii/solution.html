The Clebsch-Gordan decomposition for SU2 is
Vj1​​⊗Vj2​​≅j=∣j1​−j2​∣⨁j1​+j2​​Vj​,dimVj​=2j+1,​
(14)
where j increases by one and every summand occurs once. The tensor product of Lie algebra representations is antihermitian for the product inner product, so an invariant subspace has an invariant orthogonal complement; this gives complete reducibility. To see the particular summands, suppose j1​≥j2​. The product weight diagram has one state of weight j1​+j2​, two of weight j1​+j2​−1, and increasing multiplicities down to the plateau determined by j1​−j2​. Subtracting the weight string of Vj1​+j2​​ leaves a single highest state of weight j1​+j2​−1. Repeating highest-weight character subtraction yields exactly the displayed sequence, down to j1​−j2​. Its dimensions exhaust the tensor product because
∑j=j1​−j2​j1​+j2​​(2j+1)=(2j1​+1)(2j2​+1).
(15)
The case j2​≥j1​ is identical with the factors exchanged.
Let Cmj​(m1​,m2​)=⟨m1​:m2​∣j,m⟩ be the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients. Since the total J3​ is J3(1)​⊗1+1⊗J3(2)​, orthogonality of its eigenspaces gives the selection rule Cmj​(m1​,m2​)=0 unless m1​+m2​=m. Normalize the coupled states by lowering from a unit highest-weight vector, as in part (i); a common phase for an entire summand is immaterial. Then
J∓​∣j,m⟩=2(j±m)(j∓m+1)​​∣j,m∓1⟩.
(16)
Take the inner product with ⟨m1​:m2​∣. On the other hand, the total ladder operator is J∓(1)​⊗1+1⊗J∓(2)​, and J∓†​=J±​. Its action on the product bra is therefore
⟨m1​:m2​∣J∓​​=2(j1​∓m1​)(j1​±m1​+1)​​⟨m1​±1:m2​∣+2(j2​∓m2​)(j2​±m2​+1)​​⟨m1​:m2​±1∣.​
(17)
Equating the two evaluations and multiplying by 2​ proves both signs of the ladder recurrence for Clebsch-Gordan coefficients:
(j±m)(j∓m+1)​Cm∓1j​(m1​,m2​)​=(j1​∓m1​)(j1​±m1​+1)​Cmj​(m1​±1,m2​)+(j2​∓m2​)(j2​±m2​+1)​Cmj​(m1​,m2​±1).​​
(18)
Terms with an index outside its allowed weight range are zero. Merely specifying eigenvectors of J3​ would allow unrelated phases for different m; the coherent positive-ladder convention above is what makes these recurrences hold with the displayed positive square roots.
