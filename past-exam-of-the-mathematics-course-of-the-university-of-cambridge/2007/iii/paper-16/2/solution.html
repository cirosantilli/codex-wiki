Use the standard CW complex structure on the real projective plane, with one cell er​ for each r=0,1,2. The attaching map of the two-cell winds twice around the one-cell, so the cellular chain complex has de2​=2e1​ and de1​=0.
The product cells eij​=ei​×ej​, 0≤i,j≤2, give nine cells, in dimensions 0,1,2,3,4 with counts 1,2,3,2,1. The cellular chains of a product of finite CW complexes have boundary d(ei​×ej​)=dei​×ej​+(−1)iei​×dej​. In the ordered bases
C1​=(e10​,e01​),C2​=(e20​,e11​,e02​),C3​=(e21​,e12​),C4​=(e22​),
(3)
the nonzero matrices are
d2​=(20​00​02​),d3​=​020​0−20​​,d4​=(22​),d1​=0.
(4)
In particular d2​d3​=d3​d4​=0. The degree-one homology is Z2/2Z2. The degree-two cellular cycles are Ze11​, and the cellular boundaries there are 2Ze11​. The degree-three cycles are Z(e21​+e12​), whose double is the boundary of e22​. Finally d4​ is injective. This computes the integral homology of two real projective planes:
Hr​(RP2×RP2;Z)=⎩⎨⎧​Z,(Z/2)2,Z/2,0,​r=0,r=1,r=2,3,otherwise.​​
(5)
The degree-three torsion is the Tor functor contribution in the Künneth theorem; it would be missed by retaining only tensor products of the two factors' homology groups.
