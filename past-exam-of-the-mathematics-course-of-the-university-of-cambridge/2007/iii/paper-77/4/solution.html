Define the quasi-geostrophic streamfunction by p′/ρ0​=f0​ψ. Leading horizontal geostrophic balance, f0​z^×ug​=−∇H​P, gives (ug​,vg​)=(−ψy​,ψx​). The hydrostatic approximation gives Pz​=σ, hence
σ=f0​ψz​,Dg​=∂t​−ψy​∂x​+ψx​∂y​.​
(38)
Thus Dg​ is the material derivative along the leading geostrophic flow, and the vertical derivative of pressure determines the buoyancy perturbation. This also gives thermal wind: ugz​=−σy​/f0​ and vgz​=σx​/f0​.
The balanced regime is favored by small Rossby number, a shallow aspect ratio, and a Burger number N2H2/(f02​L2) of order one. Equivalently the stratified Froude number U/(NH)=Ro/Bu​ is small. On a beta plane, require βL/∣f0​∣=O(Ro), and take slow, initially balanced motion away from the equator. Under the traditional approximation, the horizontal Coriolis force generated by vertical velocity is small because vertical velocity is small, while the vertical rotational force is small compared with the leading hydrostatic pressure and gravity terms. Strong stratification and small aspect ratio justify retaining only the vertical component of Earth's rotation in this regime; this argument is not uniform near the equator.
Put A(z)=f02​/N2(z). The buoyancy equation gives w=−f0​Dg​ψz​/N2. Substitution into the vertical vorticity equation yields
Dg​∇H2​ψ+βψx​=−∂z​(ADg​ψz​).
(39)
There is an important commutator cancellation. With Jxy​(a,b)=ax​by​−ay​bx​,
∂z​(ADg​ψz​)=Dg​∂z​(Aψz​)+AJxy​(ψz​,ψz​)=Dg​∂z​(Aψz​).
(40)
Also Dg​f=βψx​ for f=f0​+βy. Adding terms therefore proves potential-vorticity conservation:
Dg​Q=0,Q=f+∇H2​ψ+∂z​(N2f02​​ψz​).​
(41)
This is the three-dimensional quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity.
At an impermeable stationary lower boundary z=b(x,y), the kinematic condition is w=uH​⋅∇H​b=Dg​b. For small elevation and slope, evaluate this at the reference level z=0 to leading order. Combining it with the buoyancy equation gives the rigid-boundary buoyancy condition for quasi-geostrophic waves
Dg​(N2f0​​ψz​+b)=0at z=0.​
(42)
The quantity in parentheses is the boundary displacement plus its buoyancy-equivalent displacement.
Now take constant N,f and the mean quasi-geostrophic streamfunction ψˉ​=−(U0​+Λz)y. Its mean buoyancy perturbation is −fΛy, consistent with thermal wind, and its interior potential vorticity is constant. In the boundary condition, the perturbation velocity v′=ψx′​ advects the mean boundary buoyancy gradient. Keeping this contribution gives
(∂t​+U0​∂x​)ψz′​−Λψx′​+fN2U0​​bx​=0at z=0.​
(43)
In particular the −Λψx′​ term must not be omitted.
The linear interior potential-vorticity equation is (∂t​+U(z)∂x​)Q′=0. Starting with zero interior perturbation potential vorticity, and with forcing only through the boundary, it remains zero. For a steady solution this is also the regular zero-interior-PV choice, continued through any isolated zero of U. The inversion equation for a horizontal mode is
ψzz′​−K2ψ′=0,K=fNk2+ℓ2​​>0.
(44)
The printed convention K>0 takes f>0; for either hemisphere the decay rate is defined using ∣f∣. Boundedness aloft selects the decaying mode. Write
ψ′=A(t)cos(ℓy)eikxe−Kz
(45)
with real part understood. The linear boundary condition becomes
A′+ik(U0​+KΛ​)A=KfikϵN2U0​​.​
(46)
For KU0​+Λ=0, a steady quasi-geostrophic mountain wave is therefore
ψs′​=f(KU0​+Λ)ϵN2U0​​cos(ℓy)e−Kzeikx.​
(47)
On y=0, its real meridional velocity is
vs′​=−f(KU0​+Λ)kϵN2U0​​e−Kzsin(kx),b(x,0)=ϵcos(kx).
(48)
For the positive-parameter convention k,ϵ,f,U0​>0 and KU0​+Λ>0, the velocity minimum lies one quarter wavelength downstream of each topographic maximum; its maximum lies one quarter wavelength downstream of each topographic minimum. If the velocity prefactor changes sign, maxima and minima interchange. The condition KU0​+Λ>0 alone does not fix that prefactor if U0​ is allowed to be negative. The upper panel shows the positive-current convention.
At KU0​+Λ=0, the free boundary Eady edge wave has zero frequency in the ground frame. If kϵU0​=0, a steady amplitude cannot satisfy the forced amplitude equation. With A(0)=0, integration gives
A(t)=KfikϵN2U0​​t,ψ′(t)=KfikϵN2U0​​tcos(ℓy)e−Kzeikx.​
(49)
Consequently
ψt′​=KfikϵN2U0​​cos(ℓy)e−Kzeikx.​
(50)
The symbol multiplying i here is the horizontal wavenumber k, as in the PDF. Taking the real part and differentiating in x gives
ψreal′​=−KfkϵN2U0​t​cos(ℓy)e−Kzsin(kx),v′=−Kfk2ϵN2U0​t​cos(ℓy)e−Kzcos(kx).
(51)
For U0​=−Λ/K>0, positive ϵ,f, and t>0, the meridional-velocity minima coincide with topographic maxima, and its maxima with topographic minima. This is the lower panel. The change from quadrature to opposition is the phase of a resonantly forced topographic edge wave.
The stationary relief continuously excites a boundary Rossby wave whose intrinsic propagation exactly cancels mean advection. The forcing therefore remains coherent with that wave; with no damping or radiation loss for this trapped mode, its linear amplitude grows secularly. This is a resonant topographic quasi-geostrophic wave. Unlimited growth is a conclusion of the linear idealization: eventually the small-disturbance approximation fails.
The assertion that resonance must be unsteady needs nonzero forcing. If k=0, then bx​=0 and the boundary current does not cross the relief; if U0​=0, the forcing also vanishes. In either case the zero disturbance is a steady solution even if KU0​+Λ=0. For example, choose k=0, ℓ=0, and Λ=−KU0​; this is a concrete exception to an unconditional resonance claim. When both horizontal wavenumbers vanish, K=0 and the stated decaying-wave ansatz itself does not apply.
