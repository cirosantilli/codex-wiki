The claimed universality is false for the particular gate specified in part (d). Every allowed single-qubit gate is local, and Cphase​=−Z⊗Z is local too. Products of these gates remain of the form eiχA⊗B. They preserve all product states and cannot, for example, implement the CNOT gate that sends ∣+⟩⊗∣0⟩ to (∣00⟩+∣11⟩)/2​. Equivalently, its diagonal two-qubit phase entanglement criterion gives the alternating phase π−0−0+π=0 modulo 2π. No construction with only the printed phase gate and local rotations implements arbitrary two-qubit gates.
The intended universality argument works after replacing that local gate by an entangling controlled phase. The same tunable Ising coupling supplies
V=e−iπZ⊗Z/4,CZ=e−iπ/4{Rz​(−π/2)⊗Rz​(−π/2)}V=diag(1,1,1,−1).
(32)
Thus this quarter-area Ising pulse, with local corrections, realizes the Controlled-Z gate. Conjugating it by the Hadamard gate on the second qubit gives CNOT=(I⊗H)CZ(I⊗H), with harmless scalar phases if the Hadamards are implemented using traceless control Hamiltonians.
The Cartan decomposition of a two-qubit gate writes a target as
U=eiχ(A1​⊗A2​)e−i(cx​X⊗X+cy​Y⊗Y+cz​Z⊗Z)(B1​⊗B2​),
(33)
with local SU(2) factors. The three central Pauli-product operators commute, so their exponential is a product of three variable interaction gates. From two fixed CNOT gates and a variable local rotation about the z-axis,
CNOT{I⊗Rz​(2c)}CNOT=e−icZ⊗Z,
(34)
because CNOT(I⊗Z)CNOT=Z⊗Z. Conjugating this interaction by Ry​(π/2) on both qubits yields e−icX⊗X; conjugating by Rx​(−π/2) on both yields e−icY⊗Y. Part (c) implements all outer local factors. This gives the full requested Cartan construction with an entangling phase gate, and pinpoints the defect in the printed version. Exact arbitrary scalar phase requires an identity Hamiltonian term; the physical gate construction is up to global phase.
