A general single-qubit quantum gate belongs to U(2). Remove its global phase to obtain V∈SU(2); the displayed expression in the question is literally a special-unitary gate and represents a general physical gate up to that phase. Every V∈SU(2) can be written
V=(u−v∗​vu∗​),∣u∣2+∣v∣2=1,
(25)
and consequently
V=q0​I−i(qx​σx​+qy​σy​+qz​σz​),q0​,qx​,qy​,qz​∈R,q02​+qx2​+qy2​+qz2​=1.
(26)
Choose θ with q0​=cos(θ/2) and ∥q∥=sin(θ/2), and set n=q/∥q∥ when q=0. The Pauli matrix multiplication law shows (n⋅σ)2=∥n∥2I=I. Applying part (a) proves
U=eiχe−iθ(n⋅σ)/2,∥n∥=1.​
(27)
For q=0, V=±I and any axis can be used with angle zero or 2π. Thus the phase-free gate is a rotation gate by angle θ about the unit axis n. An arbitrary exact U(2) operator also needs the scalar phase eiχ, which cannot in general be produced by a traceless Quantum Hamiltonian.
