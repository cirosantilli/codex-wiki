The individual rates describe population relaxation: γ12​ transfers population from level one to level two, and γ21​ transfers it back. With p1​+p2​=1 and the conventional qubit coordinate rz​=p1​−p2​,
p˙​1​=−γ12​p1​+γ21​p2​,r˙z​=−(γ12​+γ21​)rz​+(γ21​−γ12​).
(10)
Thus T1−1​=γ12​+γ21​ is the longitudinal relaxation rate. The parameter Γ=T2−1​ is the transverse relaxation rate: it damps the off-diagonal coherence, or the x,y components of the Bloch vector. In the usual completely positive two-level model,
Γ=21​(γ12​+γ21​)+γϕ​,γϕ​≥0,
(11)
so Γ includes both the coherence loss caused by population relaxation and additional pure dephasing.
There is a normalization switch in the displayed qubit Affine Bloch equation. Its constant term is appropriate to the conventional Pauli matrices and unit-radius coordinate rk​=Tr(ρσkPauli​). With the orthonormal generators used earlier, s=r/2​, the same physical rates would instead give a constant term (γ21​−γ12​)/2​. The drift matrix is unchanged. We interpret the supplied qubit equation in its conventional r coordinates.
With zero controls and g=γ12​+γ21​>0, Γ>0, the steady state is unique:
r∗​=(0,0,(γ21​−γ12​)/g),ρ∗​=diag(γ21​/g,γ12​/g).​
(12)
The earlier normalized vector is s∗​=r∗​/2​. Indeed rx​,ry​ decay as e−Γt and rz​−r∗,z​ as e−gt.
For vanishing rates, the full equilibrium conditions of the Affine Bloch equation are Γrx​=Γry​=0 and grz​=γ21​−γ12​, together with ∥r∥≤1. In particular, for nonnegative rates, g=0 means both population rates vanish. If then Γ>0, all diagonal density operators are stationary; if also Γ=0, every density operator is stationary. The formal case g>0, Γ=0 permits extra transverse equilibrium coordinates, but is excluded by the completely positive rate bound above.
