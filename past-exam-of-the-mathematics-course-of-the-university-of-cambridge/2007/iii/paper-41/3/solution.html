Let Mt​=sups≤t​Ws​ and write ϕt​(y)=(2πt)−1/2e−y2/(2t) for the Brownian terminal density. By the Brownian reflection principle, for y<a the reflected paths that hit a and end at y have terminal density ϕt​(2a−y). Hence
P(Mt​≤a, Wt​∈dy)=[ϕt​(y)−ϕt​(2a−y)]1{y<a}​dy.
(19)
The Girsanov theorem changes zero drift into drift ν using the finite-horizon density eνWt​−ν2t/2. The finite-horizon maximum of Brownian motion with drift therefore has distribution function
P(s≤tsup​(Ws​+νs)≤a)​=∫−∞a​eνy−ν2t/2[ϕt​(y)−ϕt​(2a−y)]dy=Φ(t​a−νt​)−e2aνΦ(t​−a−νt​)​.​
(20)
In the first integral term, complete the square to get a normal density with mean νt. In the second, substitute z=2a−y and complete the square with mean −νt; the prefactor is e2aν. Here Φ is the standard normal distribution function, a>0 and t>0.
Let Ta,b​=inf{s≥0:Ws​=a+bs}, with b real. Survival means that Ws​−bs stays below a. Tilt by eθWt​−θ2t/2; under the tilted probability, Ws​ has drift θ. Applying the maximum formula with drift θ−b gives the exponential terminal moment below a linear Brownian boundary
E[eθWt​1{Ta,b​>t}​]=eθ2t/2[Φ(t​a+(b−θ)t​)−e2a(θ−b)Φ(t​−a+(b−θ)t​)].​
(21)
This holds for every real θ. The maximum distribution has no atom at the positive barrier, so strict or weak survival inequalities give the same probability here.
For the hitting-time moment, first use drift −b in the maximum formula and take the complementary probability:
Fb​(t)=P(Ta,b​≤t)=Φ(t​−a−bt​)+e−2abΦ(t​−a+bt​).
(22)
If z1​=(−a−bt)/t​ and z2​=(−a+bt)/t​, their standard normal densities satisfy ϕ(z1​)=e−2abϕ(z2​). Differentiating the two terms, with z1′​=(a−bt)/(2t3/2) and z2′​=(a+bt)/(2t3/2), yields the drifted Brownian first-passage density
fb​(s)=2πs3​a​exp[−2s(a+bs)2​],s>0.
(23)
Its total mass need not be one, because the upward-sloping boundary may never be reached.
For 2θ≤b2, put κ=b2−2θ​≥0. Multiplication by eθs gives eθsfb​(s)=ea(κ−b)fκ​(s). Integrating to t produces truncated discounted Brownian first passage, with negative discount rate allowed when the square root remains real:
E[eθTa,b​1{Ta,b​≤t}​]=ea(κ−b)Φ(t​−a−κt​)+e−a(κ+b)Φ(t​−a+κt​).​
(24)
This includes the equality case κ=0, where it becomes 2e−abΦ(−a/t​). The expression integrates only finite hitting times up to t; no value is assigned to an exponential at an infinite hitting time.
For the cash-at-hit digital call, use the risk-neutral measure in the Black-Scholes model and denote expiry by T=t0​. Then
log(Ss​/S0​)=(ρ−σ2/2)s+σWsQ​.
(25)
Hitting c>S0​ corresponds to WsQ​=a+bs, where a=log(c/S0​)/σ>0 and b=(σ2/2−ρ)/σ. The unit-cash payment is made at the hitting time, so risk-neutral valuation discounts at that time, not at expiry. Set θ=−ρ and
κ=b2+2ρ​=σ∣ρ+σ2/2∣​.
(26)
The identity on the right verifies the square-root condition even for negative interest rates. The time-zero price is
D0​=ea(κ−b)Φ(T​−a−κT​)+e−a(κ+b)Φ(T​−a+κT​).​
(27)
This is in pounds for the unit-pound contract. It differs from a maturity-paid digital call option, whose discounting and exercise event are different.
