Use a risk-neutral measure Q, and let Ps,t​ be the price at time s of a zero-coupon bond paying one at its maturity t. The information available at s is the filtration Fs​. For positive differentiable bond prices, the instantaneous forward rate is
f(s,t)=−∂t​logPs,t​,Ps,t​=exp(−∫st​f(s,u)du),
(43)
with Pt,t​=1. The short rate is Rs​=f(s,s). The continuous-time bank account Bs​=exp(∫0s​Ru​du) is the discounting numéraire.
If Ps,t​/Bs​ is a true martingale for each fixed maturity, then its terminal value is Bt−1​ and
Bs​Ps,t​​=EQ​[Bt−1​∣Fs​].
(44)
Multiplication by the Fs​-measurable Bs​ gives
Ps,t​=EQ​[e−∫st​Ru​du∣Fs​].​
(45)
Conversely this conditional-expectation formula makes the discounted bond a martingale by the tower property of conditional expectation. The equivalence uses integrability and true martingales, rather than merely local martingales.
Forward-rate modelling specifies the stochastic evolution of f(s,t) as s varies at fixed maturity. For example, with one Brownian driver, write df(s,t)=α(s,t)ds+β(s,t)dWsQ​. Differentiation of the integral representation and Itô formula give the bond's relative volatility −∫st​β(s,u)du and drift Rs​−∫st​α(s,u)du+21​(∫st​β(s,u)du)2. The discounted-bond condition therefore imposes
∫st​α(s,u)du=21​(∫st​β(s,u)du)2,
(46)
or, after differentiation in maturity, α(s,t)=β(s,t)∫st​β(s,u)du. This illustrates how the no-arbitrage condition constrains forward-rate drift.
For the Gaussian short-rate model with constant coefficients, let dRs​=θds+σdWsQ​ and fix Δ=t−s. Future Brownian increments are independent of Fs​, giving
∫st​Ru​du=ΔRs​+2θ​Δ2+σ∫st​(t−v)dWvQ​.
(47)
The last stochastic integral is Gaussian with mean zero and variance Δ3/3, by the Itô isometry. Its Gaussian moment-generating function therefore gives
Ps,t​=exp[−ΔRs​−2θ​Δ2+6σ2​Δ3],bs,t​=Δ,as,t​=−2θ​Δ2+6σ2​Δ3.​
(48)
When σ=0 and the initial rate is deterministic, the Brownian and short-rate filtrations coincide because WsQ​=(Rs​−R0​−θs)/σ. If σ=0, the same bond formula holds deterministically, while the asserted filtration equivalence is unnecessary.
For a deterministic drift function θv​, the same calculation gives
Ps,t​=exp[−(t−s)Rs​−∫st​(t−v)θv​dv+6σ2​(t−s)3].
(49)
Let the observed initial curve be P0,tobs​>0, normalized by P0,0obs​=1, and define f0​(t)=−dtd​logP0,tobs​. For a differentiable initial forward curve choose
R0​=f0​(0),θt​=f0′​(t)+σ2t.​
(50)
Indeed the model's initial forward rate is R0​+∫0t​θv​dv−σ2t2/2=f0​(t), so integrating it gives exactly the observed bond curve. This is forward-curve calibration of a shifted Brownian short rate, with Rt​=f0​(t)+σ2t2/2+σWtQ​. Every finite collection of positive bond quotes can first be fitted by a smooth positive curve; an arbitrary entire curve requires the stated smoothness or an appropriate weaker integral interpretation. The freedom is in the deterministic drift, and the initial short rate must match the initial forward rate.
