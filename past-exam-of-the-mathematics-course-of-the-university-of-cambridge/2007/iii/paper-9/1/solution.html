Assume X is nonempty, as is necessary for a probability measure to exist. Give its isometry group IX​ the uniform metric
D(g,h)=supx∈X​d(gx,hx).
(1)
Every distance-preserving self-map of a compact metric space is onto. Indeed, if x0​∈/gX, then δ=d(x0​,gX)>0, and for n>m the iterates satisfy d(gmx0​,gnx0​)=d(x0​,gn−mx0​)≥δ. This infinite separated set contradicts compactness. Consequently IX​ is a group under composition, with the identity and inverses again isometries.
The metric D is invariant under both left and right multiplication: left invariance uses preservation of distances, and right invariance uses surjectivity. Inversion is an isometry for D, and D(gh,g′h′)≤D(g,g′)+D(h,h′) proves continuity of multiplication. Moreover, IX​ is compact. A sequence of isometries has a subsequence converging at every point of a countable dense subset of X, by a diagonal argument. Their common Lipschitz constant 1 and finite epsilon-nets of X make this subsequence uniformly Cauchy. Its uniform limit preserves distances and is onto by the preceding argument. Thus IX​ is a compact metrizable topological group. A transitive group action means that for every x,y∈X some g∈IX​ has gx=y.
We construct the invariant Borel probability measure directly. The Hall marriage theorem says that a finite bipartite graph with equal-sized vertex classes A,B has a perfect matching precisely when every subset S⊆A has at least ∣S∣ neighbours in B. Its following consequence is useful. If A,B are ε-separated subsets of maximum cardinality in a compact metric space, join points whose distance is less than ε. If some S⊆A had fewer than ∣S∣ neighbours, replacing those neighbours in B by S would produce a larger ε-separated set. This contradicts maximal cardinality. Hence there is a bijection between A and B moving each point by less than ε.
Choose εn​↓0 and maximum-cardinality εn​-separated sets An​⊂IX​. Such sets exist: a finite covering by balls of radius less than εn​/2 bounds every separated set's cardinality. Fix x0​∈X and define the empirical probability measures
μn​=∣An​∣1​∑a∈An​​δax0​​.
(2)
For f∈C(X) let ωf​(r)=supd(x,y)≤r​∣f(x)−f(y)∣, which tends to zero with r by uniform continuity. For every h∈IX​, left invariance of D makes hAn​ another maximum-cardinality separated set. Match it to An​ as above. Then
​∫fdμn​−∫f(hx)dμn​(x)​≤ωf​(εn​).
(3)
The allowed compactness of probability measures on a compact metric space supplies a subsequence converging to μ in the weak-star topology. Passing to that limit for each fixed h,f gives ∫fdμ=∫f∘hdμ. The same subsequence works for every h,f, since the displayed estimate held for all of them before taking the limit.
For uniqueness, put Fn​(x)=∣An​∣−1∑a∈An​​f(ax). For x=hx0​, right invariance of D lets us match An​h with An​. Transitivity therefore gives the uniform estimate ∣Fn​(x)−Fn​(x0​)∣≤ωf​(εn​) for every x∈X. If ν is any invariant Borel probability measure, then
∫fdν=∫Fn​dν,​∫fdν−Fn​(x0​)​≤ωf​(εn​).
(4)
Two invariant probability measures consequently differ on the integral of f by at most 2ωf​(εn​), hence agree on every continuous function. Continuous functions determine Borel probability measures on a compact metric space, so the invariant probability measure exists and is unique.
