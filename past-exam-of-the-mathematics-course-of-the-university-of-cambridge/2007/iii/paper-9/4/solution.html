For ℓ1n​, the unit ball has volume 2n/n!: its intersection with each orthant is the standard simplex of volume 1/n!. Its John ellipsoid is n−1/2B2n​. To verify maximum volume, let AB2n​ be any centered inscribed ellipsoid. For each sign vector ε∈{−1,1}n, containment gives ∣ATε∣≤1. Averaging over independent Rademacher random variables yields tr(AAT)≤1. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for its squared singular values gives ∣detA∣≤n−n/2. Centered ellipsoids suffice: if a+AB2n​ is contained in the symmetric convex unit ball, averaging it with −a+AB2n​ gives the centered copy with the same volume.

Its John ellipsoid is B22k​: an inscribed AB22k​ has every row of A of Euclidean length at most 1, so the Hadamard inequality gives ∣detA∣≤1, attained by A=I. The valid orthogonal splitting with bounded volume ratio is that L may depend on V=vr(E), and L=32V2 suffices. This supplies a dimension-independent constant for the preceding ℓ12k​ case, or whenever V is bounded uniformly. We prove the complete qualified assertion, including its epsilon-net ingredient.

A maximal ε-separated subset of Sk−1 is an epsilon-net. Balls of radius ε/2 about its points have disjoint interiors and lie in the ball of radius 1+ε/2. Comparing volumes bounds its cardinality by (1+2/ε)k. Finite existence follows from this packing bound, and maximality proves the net property without assuming a separate covering theorem.
Choose two orthogonal k-dimensional subspaces F0​,F0⊥​ and one such net on each unit sphere. Rotate both by a common uniformly distributed orthogonal matrix U. This distribution can be obtained from Question 1 applied to the compact orthogonal group with its Frobenius metric; left and right multiplication are transitive isometries. Consequently Uv is uniformly distributed on S2k−1 for every fixed unit vector v. The union bound shows that the probability that some rotated net point has norm less than 2ε is at most
2(1+2/ε)k(2Vε)2k=2[4V2(ε2+2ε)]k.

The triangle inequality and ∥x∥E​≤∣x∣ imply that ∥⋅∥E​ is 1-Lipschitz for the Euclidean metric. Every unit vector of UF0​ or UF0⊥​ is within ε of a good net point and thus has norm at least ε. By homogeneity, on both orthogonal subspaces,
