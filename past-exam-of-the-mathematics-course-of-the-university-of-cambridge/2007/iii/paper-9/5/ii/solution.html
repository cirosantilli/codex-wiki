Past exam of the mathematics course of the University of Cambridge / 2007 / iii / Paper 9 / 5 / ii / Solution

Fix ε>0 and a finite epsilon-net x1​,…,xN​ in X. Choose a Borel map r:X→{x1​,…,xN​} with d(x,r(x))≤ε, for example the nearest point with ties broken by index. Its cells Ai​ give discrete probability measures with masses pi​=P(Ai​) and qi​=Q(Ai​). Let
Wε​=min{∑i,j​d(xi​,xj​)πij​:πij​≥0, ∑j​πij​=pi​, ∑i​πij​=qj​}.
(30)
The feasible set is nonempty and compact, so the minimum exists. Pushing any coupling forward by (r,r) changes its cost by at most 2ε, hence Wε​≤W+2ε. Conversely, lift a discrete coupling by the measure
∑i,j:pi​qj​>0​πij​pi​P∣Ai​​​⊗qj​Q∣Aj​​​.
(31)
Terms with zero row or column masses vanish. This lift has marginals P,Q, and its cost differs by at most 2ε. Therefore
∣W−Wε​∣≤2ε.
(32)
Here is the finite linear programming duality proof. Write dij​=d(xi​,xj​) and consider the convex cone
C={((∑j​πij​)i​,(∑i​πij​)j​,∑i,j​dij​πij​+s):πij​≥0, s≥0}.
(33)
It is closed and finitely generated. One elementary justification of closedness is to reduce each nonnegative representation to linearly independent generators, eliminating a coefficient along any linear dependence; after choosing a subsequence using the same subset of generators, their independent coordinates have bounded, convergent coefficients whenever the represented vectors converge. For η>0, the point (p,q,Wε​−η) lies outside C. Separation from this closed convex cone gives coefficients (a,b,τ) with
τ≥0,ai​+bj​+τdij​≥0,∑i​ai​pi​+∑j​bj​qj​+τ(Wε​−η)<0.
(34)
If τ=0, summing the middle inequalities against any feasible π contradicts the last one. Thus τ>0, and fi​=−ai​/τ, gj​=−bj​/τ obey fi​+gj​≤dij​ with dual objective greater than Wε​−η. The weak inequality already proved gives the reverse bound. Letting η↓0 proves finite strong duality.
Reduce this discrete dual to a single Lipschitz function. For a feasible pair put ϕi​=minj​(dij​−gj​). The triangle inequality gives ∣ϕi​−ϕl​∣≤dil​, while fi​≤ϕi​ and gi​≤−ϕi​. Consequently
∑i​pi​fi​+∑i​qi​gi​≤∑i​(pi​−qi​)ϕi​.
(35)
Conversely, any 1-Lipschitz vector ϕ gives a feasible pair (ϕ,−ϕ). Adding a constant leaves its objective unchanged, so impose ϕ1​=0. The resulting feasible set is compact, and its maximum is Wε​.
For a maximizing vector, the McShane extension theorem has the following explicit proof in this situation. Define
ϕ(x)=mini​{ϕi​+d(x,xi​)}.
(36)
The triangle inequality proves that this extension is 1-Lipschitz, and the discrete Lipschitz condition makes ϕ(xi​)=ϕi​. Thus (ϕ,−ϕ) is a continuous feasible pair. Because ∣ϕ(x)−ϕ(r(x))∣≤ε,
md​≥∫ϕdP−∫ϕdQ≥Wε​−2ε≥W−4ε.
(37)
Letting ε↓0, together with part i, proves md​(P,Q)=W(P,Q). The infimum defining W is also attained: the set of couplings is a closed subset of the compact space of probability measures on X×X, and integrating the continuous cost is continuous for the weak-star topology.
