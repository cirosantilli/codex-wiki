Use the Lipschitz constant convention ∥f∥L​=supx=y​∣f(x)−f(y)∣/d(x,y) in the final quantity; it is a seminorm, with constants having value zero. Also interpret the coupling space as P(X×X). The printed P(X,Y) has an undefined Y. The third quantity has a duplicated printed label; it is placed in part iii here, with the fourth in part iv.
First prove weak optimal transport duality. For any feasible continuous pair f,g and any coupling of probability distributions π with marginals P,Q,
∫fdP+∫gdQ=∫X×X​(f(x)+g(y))dπ(x,y)≤∫X×X​d(x,y)dπ(x,y).
(29)
Taking the supremum over pairs and infimum over couplings gives md​(P,Q)≤W(P,Q). These quantities are finite: P⊗Q is a coupling and the continuous cost d is bounded on the compact metric space X×X. The zero pair is feasible, so 0≤md​≤W≤diamX.
The reverse inequality is established in part ii by finite approximation and a proof of the required finite linear programming duality. Parts iii and iv then show that all the dual formulations agree.
