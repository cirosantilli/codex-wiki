Every function h with Lipschitz constant at most 1 yields a feasible pair (h,−h), and replacing h by −h reverses the objective. Hence
γ(P,Q)=sup∥h∥L​≤1​(∫hdP−∫hdQ)≤mL​(P,Q).
(40)
Conversely, the Lipschitz regularization of transport potentials in part iii dominates every feasible pair by (h,−h) with ∥h∥L​≤1, so mL​≤γ. Together with the preceding parts this proves the Kantorovich–Rubinstein theorem here in all four requested forms:
md​(P,Q)=W(P,Q)=mL​(P,Q)=γ(P,Q)​.
(41)
The seminorm convention is essential. If instead ∥h∥L​ meant ∥h∥∞​+Lip(h), the equality would fail: on a two-point space at distance 2, with point masses at the two different points, W=2 whereas the latter unit-ball supremum is 1. Indeed, an oscillation a needs supremum norm at least a/2 and Lipschitz constant a/2, so the sum norm bounds a by 1, attained by values −1/2,1/2. This distinguishes the Lipschitz seminorm used above from a full bounded-Lipschitz norm.
