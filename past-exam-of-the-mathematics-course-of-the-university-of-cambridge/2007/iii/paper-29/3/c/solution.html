From part (a), ΘF=E2​F. Its product has integer coefficients and begins with q, so write F=∑n≥1​τ(n)qn, with τ(1)=1. Comparing the coefficient of qn gives
nτ(n)=τ(n)−24∑ℓ=1n−1​σ1​(ℓ)τ(n−ℓ),
(25)
which rearranges to
(1−n)τ(n)=24ℓ=1∑n−1​σ1​(ℓ)τ(n−ℓ).​
(26)
This is a recurrence for the Ramanujan tau function; for example it gives τ(2)=−24 and τ(3)=252.
For the congruence, use the normalized Eisenstein expansions
E4​=1+240∑n≥1​σ3​(n)qn,E6​=1−504∑n≥1​σ5​(n)qn.
(27)
The modular form E43​−E62​ is a cusp form of weight twelve, and its leading coefficient is 720−(−1008)=1728. Since S12​ is one-dimensional and F starts with q,
E43​−E62​=1728F.
(28)
Also D6​E6​ is a weight-eight form, with constant coefficient −1/2. Since M8​ is one-dimensional and E42​ has constant coefficient one, D6​E6​=−E42​/2, or
2ΘE6​=E2​E6​−E42​.
(29)
These identities have rational coefficients whose only displayed denominators are prime to five, so coefficientwise reduction modulo five is legitimate. We have E4​≡1. Furthermore d5≡d(mod5) gives σ5​(n)≡σ1​(n), while −504≡−24≡1, so E6​≡E2​. Therefore
F≡31−E62​​=2E62​−1​≡ΘE6​(mod5).
(30)
The equality in the middle is in F5​, where −1/3=1/2. Comparing coefficients in ΘE6​=−504∑n≥1​nσ5​(n)qn gives
τ(n)≡nσ5​(n)(mod5).​
(31)
This proves the Ramanujan tau congruence modulo five from the given small-dimensional modular-form spaces, without assuming that congruence as a theorem.
