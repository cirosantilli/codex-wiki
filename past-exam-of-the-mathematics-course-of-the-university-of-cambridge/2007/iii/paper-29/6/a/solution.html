A genuine period is convenient when −I is absent and the weight is odd: the smaller effective cusp width may supply a negative rather than positive translation in Γ, but an actual period always exists, possibly twice that width. Using an actual period avoids imposing an incorrect periodicity. The zero-value condition is independent of the chosen representative or period.
