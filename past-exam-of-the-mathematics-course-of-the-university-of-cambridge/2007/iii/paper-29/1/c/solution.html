For a complex lattice, put g2​=60G4​ and g3​=140G6​. The complex lattice form of the Klein j-invariant is
j(Λ)=1728g23​−27g32​g23​​.
(7)
The denominator is nonzero for a complex lattice: after normalization it is a nonzero scalar multiple of E43​−E62​, whose absence of zeros was proved in part (b). The equal lattice Eisenstein sums therefore give equal j values, so parts (a) and (b) give Λ′=αΛ for some α=0. Scaling the absolutely convergent lattice Eisenstein sums gives
G4​(αΛ)=α−4G4​(Λ),G6​(αΛ)=α−6G6​(Λ).
(8)
If both sums are nonzero, α4=α6=1, so α2=1 and αΛ=Λ.
If G6​=0, then G4​=0 and j=1728. The complex lattice is homothetic to Zi+Z, which is preserved by multiplication by i. The constraint α4=1 allows only powers of i, each already preserving the complex lattice. If G4​=0, then G6​=0 and j=0. The complex lattice is homothetic to Zρ+Z. Multiplication by ρ preserves this complex lattice because ρ2=−1−ρ, and multiplication by −1 always preserves it. The constraint α6=1 allows exactly the sixth roots generated by these two multiplications. Again αΛ=Λ. Both sums cannot be zero simultaneously because the discriminant is nonzero. Hence in every case
Λ′=Λ.​
(9)
The exceptional cases are essential in a complex lattice is determined by its fourth and sixth Eisenstein sums; merely deducing homothety of complex lattices would not prove equality.
