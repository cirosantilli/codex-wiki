Let Λ=Zω1​+Zω2​ be a complex lattice. Orient its basis so that Im(ω1​/ω2​)>0. Multiplication by ω2−1​ gives the normalized complex lattice Zτ+Z with τ=ω1​/ω2​∈H. Thus every homothety of complex lattices class has a normalized representative.
If γ=(ac​bd​)∈SL2​(Z), then
Z(γτ)+Z=(cτ+d)−1(Z(aτ+b)+Z(cτ+d))=(cτ+d)−1(Zτ+Z).
(1)
The last equality follows because the two integer vectors are an integral basis: their determinant is one. Hence points in the same modular orbit give homothetic complex lattices.
Conversely, suppose Λτ′​=αΛτ​ for α=0. There is an integer basis-change matrix of determinant ±1 such that
τ′=α(aτ+b),1=α(cτ+d).
(2)
Both normalized bases have positive orientation, and multiplication by α preserves real orientation because its real determinant is ∣α∣2>0. The basis-change determinant is therefore +1. Dividing the two equalities gives τ′=(aτ+b)/(cτ+d). Thus the modular orbits and homothety of complex lattices classes correspond bijectively.
