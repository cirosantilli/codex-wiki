Pass to the effective subgroup Γ⊂PSL2​(Z). Its index is μ: adjoining −I halves the original index precisely when −I∈/Γ. The natural map of compactified modular curves
π:X(Γ)⟶X(1)≅P1
(48)
has degree μ, since a generic orbit has that many lifts. It branches only over the order-two elliptic orbit, the order-three elliptic orbit, and the modular cusp. Away from these, the modular quotient charts are genuine unramified covering charts.
Over i, a lift with its own effective stabilizer of order two is unramified; there are ν2​ such lifts. Every other lift has ramification index two. As the sum of all ramification indices in that fibre is μ, their number is (μ−ν2​)/2 and their contribution to the ramification divisor is (μ−ν2​)/2. Similarly, over ρ there are ν3​ unramified elliptic lifts and (μ−ν3​)/3 lifts of ramification index three, contributing 2(μ−ν3​)/3.
At a modular cusp of width h, the local parameters are qh​=e2πiτ/h upstairs and q=e2πiτ downstairs. Thus q=qhh​ and the ramification index is h. Summing the widths gives μ, so all ν∞​ modular cusps contribute μ−ν∞​ to ramification. The Riemann-Hurwitz formula now gives
2g−2=−2μ+2μ−ν2​​+32(μ−ν3​)​+μ−ν∞​=6μ​−2ν2​​−32ν3​​−ν∞​.
(49)
Therefore
g=1+12μ​−4ν2​​−3ν3​​−2ν∞​​.​
(50)
The effective index and the projective modular cusp widths ensure that the formula also applies when the original subgroup does not contain −I; no spurious factor of two is introduced.
