Let h be the standard Cartan element of the sl2 Lie algebra, with [h,e]=2e and [h,f]=−2f. For a finite-dimensional representation, its formal character records its weight multiplicities:
χV​(q)=j∈Z∑​(dimVj​)qj,Vj​={v:hv=jv}.​
(33)
The classification of finite-dimensional sl2 representations and the Weyl complete reducibility theorem give V=⨁m≥0​am​Lm​, with finitely many nonzero am​. Each Lm​ has weights m,m−2,…,−m, each of multiplicity one. Therefore
χV​(q)=∑m≥0​am​(qm+qm−2+⋯+q−m),cj​=∑m≥∣j∣m≡j (2)​​am​.
(34)
In particular cj​=c−j​, and for j≥0 one has cj​−cj+2​=aj​≥0. This proves parity unimodality of an sl2 character: within each parity, the coefficients increase towards zero and decrease away from zero. For a representation whose weights have a single parity, deleting the intervening zero coefficients gives a symmetric unimodal sequence.
The parity convention is necessary for the printed assertion. If unimodality means the full coefficient sequence at every consecutive integer exponent, it is false: the standard two-dimensional representation has character q+q−1, whose coefficients at exponents −1,0,1 are 1,0,1. The statement established above is unimodality on cosets of the root lattice for sl2​, and is the version needed in part ii. Infinite-dimensional representations need not even have Laurent-polynomial characters.
