Antiparticles are positive-energy quanta of relativistic quantum fields, not physical particles with negative energies. The negative-frequency part of a quantum field creates these quanta; the distinction between its classical frequency and a state's excitation energy is central to their interpretation.
For a real scalar field, Hermiticity identifies the two frequency branches:
ϕ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap​e−ipx+ap†​eipx).
(30)
There is one species of excitation. Its particle is its own antiparticle; a single Hermitian scalar cannot carry the nonzero continuous phase charge of a complex field. This does not prevent self-conjugate particles from having other quantum numbers.
A complex scalar field is not Hermitian. Its two independent sets of oscillators give
Φ(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap​e−ipx+bp†​eipx),Φ†(x)=∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ap†​eipx+bp​e−ipx).
(31)
The canonical commutation relations make a† and b† create different positive-energy species. With charge convention fixed by [Q,Φ]=−qΦ, normal ordering gives
H=∫(2π)3d3p​Ep​(ap†​ap​+bp†​bp​),Q=q∫(2π)3d3p​(ap†​ap​−bp†​bp​).
(32)
Thus a†∣0⟩ and b†∣0⟩ have the same mass and spin but opposite charge. Although b†eipx has negative classical frequency, [H,b†]=Ep​b†, so it creates a positive-energy excitation. Two real scalar fields can be combined into one complex field; the particle–antiparticle basis diagonalizes the conserved phase charge. Neutrality alone does not require a particle to be self-conjugate.
Locality explains why both branches occur. For the complex scalar field,
[Φ(x),Φ†(y)]=∫(2π)32Ep​d3p​(e−ip⋅(x−y)−eip⋅(x−y)).
(33)
At spacelike separation, Lorentz invariance of the mass shell measure permits a frame with equal times; reversing p makes the two terms cancel. This is microcausality. If the antiparticle creation term were omitted, the commutator would be just a Wightman function, generally nonzero at spacelike separation. For example, for a massless scalar at equal times and separation r>0, it is 1/(4π2r2). The cancellation is the role of antiparticle modes and spacelike commutativity. In a real scalar field its self-conjugate creation modes supply the same cancellation.
A nonrelativistic particle field obeys a first-order time equation and need not contain an antiparticle-creation branch. For example,
L=iΨ†∂t​Ψ−2m∣∇Ψ∣2​,i∂t​Ψ=−2m∇2​Ψ,
(34)
allows an expansion using only ap​e−ip2t/(2m)+ip⋅x in Ψ; its adjoint creates those particles. The basic theory conserves particle number and does not impose a Lorentz-invariant light cone locality condition. It often arises from a relativistic quantum field by factoring out the rest-energy oscillation and restricting to a low-energy particle sector, where creation of particle–antiparticle pairs costs roughly 2m. An antiparticle species can certainly also be treated nonrelativistically with its own field; antiparticles are simply not forced by this nonrelativistic quantum field equation.
Historically, the Dirac equation has one-particle energy branches ±p2+m2​. Treating both as ordinary electron energies would leave no lowest-energy one-particle vacuum: an electron could fall through arbitrarily negative levels. The Dirac sea interpretation fills every negative-energy electron level. The Pauli exclusion principle then blocks further occupation. A missing negative-energy electron is a hole: removing energy −E increases energy by E, and removing charge −e leaves charge +e. This predicts the positron's mass, spin and opposite electric charge.
The quantum field theory account quantizes a Dirac field instead:
ψ(x)=∑r​∫(2π)32Ep​​d3p​(ar​(p)ur​(p)e−ipx+br†​(p)vr​(p)eipx).
(35)
The independent particle and antiparticle operators obey canonical anticommutation relations. Before normal ordering, the negative-frequency contribution to the Dirac Hamiltonian has the form −Ep​br​br†​. Anticommutation rewrites it as +Ep​br†​br​ plus a vacuum constant. Removing the constant leaves positive particle and antiparticle energies. The charge has opposite signs on the two occupation numbers, and interactions permit pair creation and annihilation while conserving the relevant charges.
There is therefore no need for a literal infinitely populated material sea. The hole picture is a useful fermionic reinterpretation, but it cannot explain bosonic antiparticles through filled-level exclusion, since bosons can multiply occupy a state. Canonical quantization of a complex scalar field supplies them just as consistently. More generally charge conjugation relates field representations with opposite internal charges; real scalar fields and Majorana fermions are self-conjugate exceptions. The relativistic Fock space description handles these cases, statistics and changing particle number within one framework.
