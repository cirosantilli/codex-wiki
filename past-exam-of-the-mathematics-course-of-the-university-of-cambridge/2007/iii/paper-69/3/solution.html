For a bound Kepler orbit, the semimajor axis is half the long diameter of the ellipse; if rp​,rmathrma​ are its apsidal distances, a=(rp​+rmathrma​)/2. The orbital eccentricity is e=(rmathrma​−rp​)/(rmathrma​+rp​), with 0≤e<1 and semiminor axis b=a1−e2​. The gravitating centre is a focus, displaced by ae from the ellipse's geometric centre.
Choose a reference plane, a positive normal and a fixed reference direction in that plane. The ascending node is where the orbit crosses toward positive normal displacement. The longitude of ascending node Ω is the reference-plane angle from the fixed direction to that node. The argument of periapsis ω is the angle from the ascending node to periapsis, measured in the orbital plane in the direction of motion. The orbital inclination i is the angle between the reference normal and the orbital angular-momentum normal, with 0≤i≤π. The diagram separates the ellipse's shape from these orientation angles.
Let μ=GM and use actions per unit test-particle mass. In the prograde spherical-action chart underlying the stated Hamiltonian, Jϕ​=Lz​ and Jθ​=L−Lz​, so Jθ​+Jϕ​=L. The radial action then satisfies Jr​+L=μa​, consistently with HK​=−μ/(2a). Define
J1​=Jr​+Jθ​+Jϕ​,J2​=Jθ​+Jϕ​,J3​=Jϕ​.​
(22)
If (wr​,wθ​,wϕ​) are the old angles, a type-two generating function is
F2​=(J1​−J2​)wr​+(J2​−J3​)wθ​+J3​wϕ​.
(23)
Differentiation with respect to the old angles recovers Jr​,Jθ​,Jϕ​, and differentiation with respect to the new actions gives the canonical transformation
w1​=wr​,w2​=wθ​−wr​,w3​=wϕ​−wθ​.​
(24)
Its triangular integer action matrix has determinant one, so it preserves the angle periodicities. In the new coordinates,
HK​=−2J12​μ2​,w˙1​=J13​μ2​=a3μ​​,w˙2​=w˙3​=0.​
(25)
These are the Delaunay variables:
J1​=L=μa​,J2​=G=μa(1−e2)​,J3​=H=Gcosi.
(26)
With the usual choices of angle origins, w1​=ℓ is mean anomaly, w2​=ω is argument of periapsis, and w3​=Ω is longitude of ascending node. The generator G=L rotates the orbit within its plane, whereas H=Lz​ rotates that plane about the reference normal; this explains the angular identification. The fast angle obeys Kepler's equation ℓ=E−esinE, where E is eccentric anomaly.
For retrograde orbits, signed Jϕ​=Lz​ gives Jθ​=L−∣Lz​∣ and the spherical Hamiltonian uses Jr​+Jθ​+∣Jϕ​∣. The stated plus-sign form is therefore a prograde chart, not a global signed-action formula. The Delaunay variables still use H=Lz​; on the retrograde branch the generating function is (L−G)wr​+(G+H)wθ​+Hwϕ​. At e=0, periapsis is undefined, and at i=0 or π the node is undefined: these are angle-chart degeneracies, not physical singularities.
For the perturbed planet orbit, the radial and vertical frequencies mean small oscillations about a circular equatorial orbit of cylindrical radius R. Write r=R2+z2​ and retain the stated quadrupole potential. On the equator,
ϕ(R,0)=−Rμ​−R3α2μ​,Ωc2​=R1​∂R​ϕ=R3μ​+R53α2μ​.
(27)
At fixed axial specific angular momentum Lz​, the radial effective potential is ϕ(R,0)+Lz2​/(2R2). Its second derivative at circular balance Lz​=R2Ωc​ gives the radial epicyclic frequency
κ2=∂R2​ϕ+3Ωc2​=RdRdΩc2​​+4Ωc2​=R3μ​−R53α2μ​.​
(28)
Reflection symmetry decouples vertical motion to first order. Expanding the potential in z gives
ϕ(R,z)=ϕ(R,0)+21​(R3μ​+R59α2μ​)z2+O(z4),
(29)
so the vertical epicyclic frequency is
ν2=R3μ​+R59α2μ​.​
(30)
The circular orbit is radially stable only for R2>3α2. For α=0 in that domain, ν>Ωc​>κ. Vertical node crossings repeat at frequency ν while azimuth advances at Ωc​, so the line of nodes has nodal precession rate Ωc​−ν<0. Radial periapsis passages repeat at κ, giving apsidal precession rate Ωc​−κ>0. To leading quadrupole order, with n=μ/R3​,
Ω˙node​≃−3nR2α2​,ϖ˙≃+3nR2α2​.​
(31)
This is oblate-quadrupole epicyclic precession. Here ϖ denotes the nearly equatorial longitude of periapsis, not ω alone.
The precession signs above have a small-eccentricity, small-inclination domain. They are not a universal claim for every inclined Kepler ellipse. To see the distinction with the same Delaunay variables, average the perturbation over an unperturbed orbit. Since z/r=sinisin(ω+f) and dt=r2df/G, the term linear in ecosf integrates to zero and the sin2(ω+f) average in the r−3 integral is 1/2. Thus
HQ​=2a3(1−e2)3/2α2μ​(1−3cos2i)=2L3G3α2μ4​(1−G23H2​).
(32)
Differentiating at fixed other actions yields the first-order secular rates
Ω˙=−a2(1−e2)23nα2cosi​,ω˙=2a2(1−e2)23nα2(5cos2i−1)​.
(33)
Their nearly equatorial sum reproduces the prograde ϖ˙. For a polar orbit, however, the node is fixed and ω˙<0. This supplies a concrete counterexample to extending the requested forward periapsis precession beyond the intended epicyclic regime.
