For an isolated, virialized three-dimensional Newtonian system with negligible boundary-pressure terms, the virial theorem gives 2K+W=0. Hence its total energy is E=K+W=−K. Defining the kinetic temperature by K=3NkT/2 gives
dTdE​=−23​Nk<0.​
(34)
Thus losing energy makes the system hotter, while adding energy makes it cooler. This negative heat capacity of a virialized gravitational system uses the inverse-distance potential and the stated boundary assumptions; it is not a dimension-independent consequence of self-gravity.
A gravothermal catastrophe occurs when a stellar core loses heat to its halo through two-body relaxation, contracts and becomes hotter because of its negative heat capacity. The larger temperature gradient then drives more heat outward and accelerates contraction. In the confined three-dimensional isothermal sequence this runaway is associated with loss of a regular thermodynamic equilibrium at a finite total energy. It is different from a fast collisionless instability.
For the rods, use energies and masses per unit axial length, consistently with their logarithmic interaction. Define
q=2GMβ​,x=Re2​R2​,F(x)=1−q+qx,ψe​=−GMlog(V/V0​).
(35)
Integrating the two-dimensional Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distribution gives ρ=Aeβψ, with a positive normalization A. The proposed potential has ψ=ψe​−2logF/β, so this requires ρ∝F−2. Direct differentiation gives
ψ′=−Re2​F2GMR​,R1​(Rψ′)′=−Re2​F24GM(1−q)​.
(36)
Consequently the Poisson equation for Newtonian gravity is satisfied precisely by
ρ(R)=πRe2​F2M(1−q)​.​
(37)
The free normalization of the velocity distribution can be chosen to give this density. Its enclosed line mass verifies total-mass normalization:
M(<R)=M(1−q)∫0x​(1−q+qu)2du​=F(x)Mx​​,M(<Re​)=M.
(38)
At the wall F(1)=1, so the required potential value is also satisfied. This establishes the two-dimensional self-gravitating rod equilibrium, including its field equation, distribution law, mass and boundary condition.
A regular positive solution needs F(0)=1−q>0. Since β=m/(kT),
T>Tmin​,kTmin​=21​GMm.​
(39)
The minimum is an infimum of regular equilibrium temperatures. At equality the central logarithm is singular; for T<Tmin​, F vanishes inside the box. Thus equality is not a regular isothermal rod configuration.
Each of the two velocity components has second moment 1/β, so the kinetic energy is K=M/β=NkT. With the given reference potential, the gravitational potential energy is W=−21​∫ρψd2R. Using dM=M(1−q)dx/F2 gives
W=−2Mψe​​+β1​∫logFdM.
(40)
The remaining integral can be evaluated explicitly:
∫logFdM​=qM(1−q)​∫1−q1​F2logF​dF=qM(1−q)​[−FlogF+1​]1−q1​=M[1+q1​log(1−q)].​
(41)
Adding K therefore yields
E=2GM2​log(V/V0​)+βM​[2+q1​log(1−q)].
(42)
Now NkTmin​=GM2/2, Θ=T/Tmin​=1/q and M/β=NkTmin​Θ. The energy-temperature curve of a two-dimensional self-gravitating rod gas is consequently
E=NkTmin​[log(V/V0​)+2Θ+Θ2log(1−Θ−1)],Θ>1.​
(43)
At fixed box area, differentiation gives
CV​=Nk[2+2Θlog(1−Θ−1)+Θ−1Θ​].
(44)
To establish its sign rather than infer it from a plot, expand the logarithm for Θ>1:
2Θ+Θ2log(1−Θ−1)=Θ−21​−∑j=3∞​jΘj−21​,NkCV​​=1+j=3∑∞​jΘj−1j−2​>1.​
(45)
The series and its derivative converge locally uniformly in Θ>1. Moreover E→−∞ as Θ↓1 and E→+∞ as Θ→∞. Thus every finite energy on this mean-field isothermal sequence has a unique regular equilibrium temperature, and there is no finite-energy termination or turning point. The central density M/[V(1−q)] diverges only in the infinite-negative-energy limit.
There is no finite-energy gravothermal catastrophe in this two-dimensional rod model. Its logarithmic interaction gives a different caloric curve from the three-dimensional inverse-distance problem. This microcanonical conclusion does not prohibit a distinct fixed-temperature collapse: in the canonical ensemble, a bath at or below Tmin​ admits no regular equilibrium of this family.
