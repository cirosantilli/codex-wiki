Let u,v,w be the consecutive primitive toric ray vectors around the compact invariant curve F=Fv​. Smoothness gives oriented determinants det(u,v)=det(v,w)=1, hence u+w=bv for an integer b. Choose an algebraic torus character m with ⟨m,u⟩=0 and ⟨m,v⟩=1. The principal divisor on a toric variety has coefficient one at F, coefficient b at Fw​, and zero at Fu​. The other invariant curves do not meet F, while each adjacent one meets it transversely once. Intersecting this principal divisor with the proper curve F gives 0=F2+b. This proves the self-intersection formula for a toric surface divisor even when the ambient surface is not complete.
If F2=−1, then b=1 and v=u+w. The vectors u,w form a lattice basis since det(u,w)=1, and their toric cone is the union of the two toric cones adjacent to v. Deleting v is consequently a smooth toric fan coarsening. Its inverse is precisely the toric blowup at a torus-fixed point, obtained by inserting the sum of the two primitive basis vectors. Conversely, that insertion gives u+w=v, and hence algebraic self-intersection −1. Thus the toric contraction of a minus-one curve satisfies
F contracts torically to a smooth point ⟺ F2=−1.​
(14)
For the cyclic quotient description, first take a strongly convex toric cone of dimension two. Write its primitive boundary vectors as a,b and put N0​=Za+Zb, r=[N:N0​]. Since a is primitive, extend it to a basis of N and write b=ua+rc with gcd(u,r)=1. Thus N/N0​ is cyclic of order r. In the lattice N0​, the toric cone is smooth and its toric variety is A2. Passing to the larger lattice N takes the finite quotient whose invariant monomials are precisely those in the smaller dual lattice M=N∗⊂N0∗​. Relative to the two boundary coordinates, c=(b−ua)/r, so a generator acts with weights (−u,1) modulo r. Replacing that generator by a suitable power makes the first weight one and the second some q with 0<q<r and gcd(q,r)=1. Therefore, for the singular case,
Uσ​≅A2/μr​,ζ⋅(x,y)=(ζx,ζqy).​
(15)
The cyclic quotient surface singularity can equivalently be described by the first-quadrant toric cone in
L=Z2+Zr(1,q)​,L∗={(i,j)∈Z2:i+qj≡0(modr)}.
(16)
Indeed its coordinate ring is k[xiyj:i,j≥0, i+qj≡0(modr)]=k[x,y]μr​. Characteristic zero ensures the stated ordinary finite-group quotient interpretation.
The restriction to singular toric cones of dimension two is necessary for r>1. A smooth toric cone has r=1 and gives A2 with the trivial quotient. A toric cone of dimension one gives A1×k∗, and the zero toric cone gives (k∗)2. These latter surfaces cannot be a quotient of the displayed kind, since they have nonconstant invertible regular functions, whereas k[x,y]μr​ has only constant units. Thus the blanket affine-surface wording requires these separate cases.
For the singular case, compute the negative continued fraction by the ceiling form of the Euclidean algorithm. Start with a0​=r, a1​=q, and, while ai​>0, set
bi​=⌈ai​ai−1​​⌉,ai+1​=bi​ai​−ai−1​.
(17)
Then 0≤ai+1​<ai​, so the process terminates at as+1​=0. The gcd is unchanged at each step, hence as​=1. Since ai−1​>ai​>0, every bi​≥2. Rearranging the recurrence gives
qr​=[b1​,…,bs​]−=b1​−b2​−⋯−1/bs​1​1​.​
(18)
Now define lattice vectors
v0​=(0,1),v1​=r(1,q)​,vi+1​=bi​vi​−vi−1​.
(19)
To verify this Hirzebruch–Jung resolution algorithm, write vi​=(pi​,ai​)/r with p0​=0, p1​=1 and pi+1​=bi​pi​−pi−1​. Induction gives ai​≡qpi​(modr), so vi​∈L. It also gives pi​ai+1​−ai​pi+1​=−r. Thus every adjacent pair has absolute determinant 1/r, the covolume of L, and is a basis of L. The pi​ increase strictly, while the ai​ decrease to zero, so these toric rays occur in order inside the first quadrant. At termination, as​=1 and the determinant identity gives ps+1​=r, hence vs+1​=(1,0). All boundary and inserted vectors are primitive because they occur in lattice bases.
Subdivide the quadrant by v1​,…,vs​. Every resulting toric cone is smooth by the smoothness criterion for a toric variety, and the fan subdivision gives a proper birational toric morphism, hence a resolution. Each interior toric ray has a complete one-dimensional star, so its exceptional curve of a surface resolution is P1. The recurrence yields
Fi2​=−bi​≤−2,Fi​⋅Fi+1​=1,Fi​⋅Fj​=0 (∣i−j∣>1).​
(20)
This proves minimality: no exceptional curve of a surface resolution can be contracted to a smooth point by the Castelnuovo contraction criterion. There is also a direct toric fan check. For fixed i, the determinants of vi​ with vi+h​, measured in units of the lattice covolume, satisfy the same recurrence with coefficients at least two and initial values zero and one. They are at least h. Omitting any intervening toric ray therefore leaves a toric cone of determinant at least two, so no nontrivial coarsening is smooth. Thus this is the minimal resolution of a cyclic quotient surface singularity.
If all exceptional algebraic self-intersections are −2, all bi​=2 and induction gives [2,…,2]−=(s+1)/s. Since r,q are coprime, this forces r=s+1, q=s. Conversely r/(r−1) has exactly r−1 coefficients equal to two. Therefore
Fi2​=−2 for every exceptional curve ⟺ q=r−1.​
(21)
For the final toric fan, the minimal smooth fan subdivision of the toric cone from (0,1) to (r,1) inserts the toric rays (j,1) for 1≤j<r. Adjacent determinants have absolute value one, and (j−1,1)+(j+1,1)=2(j,1), so its inserted curves have algebraic self-intersection −2 and it is the minimal fan subdivision just described. The second original toric cone, from (r,1) to (1,0), is already smooth.
Write vj​=(j,1) and e=(1,0). In the resolved toric fan, the rightmost interior toric ray obeys vr−1​+e=vr​, so its curve has algebraic self-intersection −1 and can be blown down to a smooth point. After deleting it, the same relation vr−2​+e=vr−1​ applies to the new rightmost interior toric ray. Continue deleting vr​,vr−1​,…,v1​ in that order. Each deletion is the inverse of a toric blowup at a torus-fixed point. The last toric fan has only the quadrant toric cone and defines A2. Consequently
XΣ′​⟶A2 is the composite of exactly r smooth-point toric blowdowns.​
(22)
All maps induce the identity on the dense algebraic torus, so this composite is the same morphism as XΣ′​→XΣ​→A2. The intermediate smooth toric fans need not include the singular toric fan Σ.
