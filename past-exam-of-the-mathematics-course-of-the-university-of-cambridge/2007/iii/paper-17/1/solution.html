Write C=σˇ=σ∨, S=C∩M, and Uσ​=Xσˇ​=Speck[S]. The semigroup algebra has basis χm=xm, m∈S, with χmχm′=χm+m′. The vertex assumption means that σ is strongly convex, so its dual cone C has full dimension. By the Gordan lemma, S is finitely generated: choose integral toric cone generators and subtract integer parts of their nonnegative coefficients; the remainder lies in a bounded parallelepiped with only finitely many lattice points. This argument also applies when C has lineality space, by including generators in both directions there.
Choose an integral interior point u∈C. For every m∈M, both tu and tu+m belong to S for all sufficiently large integers t. Thus S−S=M. Inverting all monomial generators of k[S] gives the group algebra k[M], which becomes k[z1±1​,…,zn±1​] after a basis of M is chosen. This proves the dense torus of an affine toric variety assertion:
XM​=Speck[M]≅(k∗)n is a dense open subset of Uσ​.​
(1)
Density follows because the localization of a ring is of a domain. The algebra map χm↦χm⊗χm from k[S] to k[M]⊗k[S] defines the extended algebraic torus action; on XM​ it is ordinary multiplication.
For a face of a polyhedral cone ν⪯C, set Iν​=(χm:m∈S∖ν). The convex face property ensures that this is an ideal, and quotienting leaves k[ν∩M]. Hence Xν​=Speck[ν∩M] is a closed subvariety of Uσ​. Its open algebraic torus consists of points where precisely the monomials with exponent in ν are nonzero.
To see that these algebraic tori exhaust the points, let p be a point of Uσ​ and put Sp​={m∈S:χm(p)=0}. Multiplicativity gives m+m′∈Sp​ if and only if both m,m′∈Sp​. Thus Sp​ is a semigroup face. It is S∩ν for a unique polyhedral convex face ν: among a finite generating set of S, take the toric cone spanned by generators nonzero at p. A relation expressing a sum with a generator outside this toric cone as a combination of generators inside would, after clearing rational denominators, contradict the displayed multiplicativity property. Therefore the toric cone is a convex face. Conversely, if m∈S lies in that convex face, a positive multiple of m is an integral sum of those generators, so χm(p) is nonzero as well. This also proves uniqueness.
The point p lies in XM​ exactly when its convex face is all of C. Therefore
Uσ​∖XM​=ν≺C⋃​Xν​.​
(2)
One can equivalently write this as the disjoint union of the open algebraic tori of the proper convex faces. For μ∈S, the same description shows that χμ(p)=0 exactly when μ is not in the convex face supporting p. On every Xν​ with μ∈/ν the monomial vanishes identically. Consequently the monomial zero locus in an affine toric variety is
V(χμ)red​=ν⪯Cμ∈/ν​⋃​Xν​.​
(3)
This is an equality of reduced subvarieties, or of their underlying closed sets. The principal subscheme itself may have multiplicity, as x2=0 already shows on A1.
The face duality for polyhedral cones is the order-reversing pair of maps
τ⪯σ ⟼ C∩τ⊥,ν⪯C ⟼ σ∩ν⊥.​
(4)
Their values are convex faces because the relevant pairings are nonnegative and a sum pairs to zero only if each summand does. More explicitly, finitely many nonnegative exposing functionals can be summed to expose the indicated intersection. For a convex face τ, choose m0​∈C exposing it: τ=σ∩m0⊥​. Then m0​∈C∩τ⊥, so σ∩(C∩τ⊥)⊥⊆τ, while the reverse inclusion is immediate. Apply the same argument to a convex face ν of C, using C∨=σ, to prove the other composite is the identity. Inclusion reversal follows directly from annihilators. This proves the bijection, not just the existence of the maps.
For τ⪯σ, write ν=C∩τ⊥ and L=M∩τ⊥. An exposing functional m0​ for τ is strictly positive on every toric ray outside τ. Small perturbations of m0​ within τ⊥ preserve those finitely many strict inequalities, so ν has nonempty relative interior in τ⊥ and spans it. Therefore ν∩M generates L, by the interior-point argument used above within that subspace. Thus the open algebraic torus of Xν​ is Speck[L]=Xτ⊥​. Here Xτ⊥​ uses the lattice in the whole annihilator, rather than the toric cone semigroup. The sublattice L is a saturated sublattice of M and consequently a direct summand; restriction Hom(M,k∗)→Hom(L,k∗) is surjective. The extended algebraic torus action is multiplication on this open algebraic torus and hence transitive. All other monomials vanish there. It follows that
O(τ)=Xτ⊥​,Fτ​=O(τ)​=XC∩τ⊥​.​
(5)
This is the torus orbit-cone correspondence and its orbit closure in a toric variety description. Under convex face duality, Fγ​=⋃η⪰γ​O(η).
Finally, choose integral mτ​∈C exposing τ. Localizing k[C∩M] at χmτ​ gives k[τ∨∩M]: for m∈τ∨∩M, adding a sufficiently large multiple of mτ​ makes it nonnegative on every toric ray of σ outside τ, while preserving nonnegativity on τ. Hence Xτˇ​=Uτ​=D(χmτ​) is the usual affine open chart inside Uσ​. An orbit O(γ) belongs to it exactly when γ⪯τ. The requested complement of an affine toric face chart is therefore
Xσˇ​∖Xτˇ​=γ⪯σγ⋠τ​⋃​Fγ​.​
(6)
All sets on the right are closed, and if γ is not contained in τ, neither is any convex face containing γ. This justifies replacing the excluded orbits by their whole closures.
