The dense algebraic torus has coordinate ring k[M], a Laurent polynomial ring and hence a unique factorization domain. Its divisor class group is therefore zero. Restrict an arbitrary Weil divisor D to the algebraic torus and choose a rational function on an algebraic variety f whose divisor there is D∣T​. Then D−div(f) is supported on the algebraic torus boundary. The prime divisors in that boundary are precisely the Fj​ corresponding to the toric rays, by the torus orbit-cone correspondence. Consequently
D∼j=1∑d​aj​Fj​for some aj​∈Z.​
(23)
Since the variety is smooth, every Weil divisor is Cartier. This gives invariant representatives for all divisor classes, without assuming that the original divisor was invariant.
Now use such a representative. The associated vector space is
L(D)={f∈k(X)∗:div(f)+D≥0}∪{0}=H0(X,OX​(D)).
(24)
The principal divisor on a toric variety formula gives div(χm)=∑j​⟨m,ej​⟩Fj​. Define the lattice polytope of a toric divisor
PD​={m∈MR​:⟨m,ej​⟩≥−aj​ for every j}.
(25)
For a maximal toric cone σ, its basic toric ray vectors are a basis of N, so there is a unique integral mσ​ with ⟨mσ​,ej​⟩=−aj​ on its toric rays. The algebraic torus character χmσ​ is a local frame for O(D) on Uσ​. Every global section restricts on the algebraic torus to a finite Laurent polynomial ∑m​cm​χm. Relative to this frame, regularity on Uσ​ means ∑m​cm​χm−mσ​∈k[σ∨∩M]. Since distinct algebraic torus characters are independent, this holds exactly when each exponent with nonzero coefficient satisfies the inequalities for the toric rays of σ. Taking all charts proves
L(D)=m∈PD​∩M⨁​kχm.​
(26)
Completeness makes PD​ bounded when nonempty: a nonzero recession vector would be nonnegative on every toric ray of a complete toric fan, and therefore on all of NR​, which is impossible. The displayed space is consequently finite-dimensional.
At the torus-fixed point pσ​ in the smooth chart Uσ​≅An, a section χm has local monomial χm−mσ​. Its value is nonzero exactly when this monomial is constant, namely when m=mσ​. Thus if the complete linear system of a divisor has no base point, some section is nonzero at pσ​, forcing mσ​∈PD​. Conversely, if mσ​∈PD​ for every maximal toric cone, its algebraic torus character is a global section whose local expression on Uσ​ is one. It therefore vanishes nowhere on that whole chart, and these charts cover X. This proves the toric basepoint-free criterion:
∣D∣ is basepoint-free ⟺ ∀σ∈Σ(n), ⟨mσ​,ej​⟩≥−aj​ for all j, with equality on σ.​
(27)
The analogous toric ampleness criterion, stated without proof, replaces the inequalities off σ by strict ones:
D is ample ⟺ ∀σ∈Σ(n), ⟨mσ​,ej​⟩=−aj​ on σ,⟨mσ​,ej​⟩>−aj​ off σ.​
(28)
Equivalently, the normal fan of a polytope PD​ is exactly Σ.
For the final fan subdivision, take n≥2 so that e~ is a new toric ray, as the construction requires. The vectors e1​,…,en​ are a lattice basis, so their sum is primitive. Replacing any one basis vector by this sum gives another basis. Thus the star subdivision gives a smooth toric blowup at a torus-fixed point π:XΣ′​→XΣ​, with algebraic exceptional divisor E=F~. On the old chart, a local equation of D is χ−mσ​, whose order on the new toric ray is −⟨mσ​,e~⟩=∑i=1n​ai​=a. All old toric ray coefficients are unchanged. Therefore
D′=π∗D.​
(29)
We prove ampleness of Dc​=cD′−E directly by inequalities, giving the ample divisor after blowing up a torus-fixed point result.
Let u1​,…,un​ be the basis of M dual to e1​,…,en​. On the new maximal toric cone σi​, which omits ei​, the required local algebraic torus character is
mi,c​=cmσ​+ui​.
(30)
Indeed its pairings on ej​, j=i, are −caj​, while its pairing on e~ is −ca+1, exactly the negative of the new coefficient ca−1. Its inequality on the omitted old toric ray has strict gap one:
⟨mi,c​,ei​⟩+cai​=1.
(31)
For any toric ray ej​ outside the original toric cone, put δj​=⟨mσ​,ej​⟩+aj​. The original ampleness gives positive integers δj​, and the new strict gaps are
⟨mi,c​,ej​⟩+caj​=cδj​+⟨ui​,ej​⟩>0
(32)
for all sufficiently large c.
For any unchanged maximal toric cone η=σ, the local algebraic torus character is cmη​. All strict inequalities on old toric rays follow from those for D. Its inequality on the new toric ray has gap
⟨cmη​,e~⟩+ca−1=cϵη​−1,ϵη​=∑i=1n​(⟨mη​,ei​⟩+ai​).
(33)
Each summand is nonnegative, and at least one is positive because η=σ cannot contain all its toric rays. Hence ϵη​ is a positive integer, making this gap positive for c≥2. There are only finitely many remaining inequalities. For example, any integer satisfying
c>max{1, max1≤i≤nj>n​​δj​−⟨ui​,ej​⟩​}
(34)
works, with the inner maximum omitted when its index set is empty. The toric ampleness criterion now gives
cD′−F~ is ample for every sufficiently large integer c.​
(35)
The new-ray assumption is essential to the printed construction. In dimension one, e~=e1​ and the fan subdivision is unchanged, so the two purported distinct divisors coincide. If the displayed definition of D′ is applied literally on P1 with coefficients a1​=−2, a2​=3, then D has degree one but D′ has degree −1, contradicting the conclusion. The argument above applies to the intended blowup in dimension at least two.
