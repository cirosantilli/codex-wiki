Quark mixing arises because the up-type and down-type quark Yukawa matrices need not be diagonal in the same weak interaction basis. In the Standard Model the three left-handed quark doublets QL′​ transform as (3,2,1/6) under SU(3)c​×SU(2)L​×U(1)Y​, whereas uR′​ and dR′​ transform as (3,1,2/3) and (3,1,−1/3). With a Higgs doublet H and its conjugate H=iσ2H∗, the gauge-invariant Yukawa interaction is
LY​=−Qˉ​L′​Yd​HdR′​−Qˉ​L′​Yu​HuR′​+h.c.
(18)
The generation indices are matrix-multiplied. Electroweak symmetry breaking, ⟨H⟩=(0,vH​/2​)T, produces Mu​=vH​Yu​/2​ and Md​=vH​Yd​/2​.
Each complex fermion mass matrix admits a biunitary diagonalization,
UuL†​Mu​UuR​=Du​,UdL†​Md​UdR​=Dd​,
(19)
where the diagonal entries are real nonnegative quark masses. Define uL′​=UuL​uL​ and dL′​=UdL​dL​, and similarly for right-handed fields. The kinetic terms retain their canonical form because the transformations are unitary matrices. However, the weak charged current becomes
Lcc​=−2​g2​​uˉL​γμVdL​Wμ+​+h.c.,V=UuL†​UdL​​.
(20)
This unitary matrix is the Cabibbo-Kobayashi-Maskawa matrix. Its entries multiply transitions between up-type and down-type mass eigenstates. The minimal Standard Model has no analogous right-handed charged weak current.
Quark mixing does not introduce tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents. Photon, gluon and weak neutral current couplings are proportional to the identity within each fixed-charge quark sector, and U†IU=I. The single Higgs doublet also has diagonal neutral scalar field couplings after the same transformations that diagonalize the masses. At higher loop orders, flavor changes are possible. For example, an s→d amplitude contains ∑i​Vis∗​Vid​f(mi2​). Unitarity of the CKM matrix sets the sum of coefficients to zero, cancelling any mass-independent part. This is the GIM mechanism; mass splittings leave a suppressed, generally nonzero amplitude.
For N generations a unitary matrix has N2 real parameters: N(N−1)/2 mixing angles and N(N+1)/2 phases. Rephasing the N up-type and N down-type quark fields removes 2N−1 phases; a common baryon number phase leaves every matrix entry unchanged. Hence the physical numbers are
Nangles​=2N(N−1)​,Nweak CP phases​=2(N−1)(N−2)​.
(21)
Two generations therefore give a real Cabibbo rotation,
V=(cosθC​−sinθC​​sinθC​cosθC​​),
(22)
with strangeness-changing charged-current transitions suppressed by sinθC​. Three generations have three mixing angles and one irreducible weak CP phase. This counting assumes nondegenerate masses; degeneracies allow additional basis rotations.
Writing sij​=sinθij​ and cij​=cosθij​, a standard exact parametrization is
V=​c12​c13​−s12​c23​−c12​s23​s13​eiδs12​s23​−c12​c23​s13​eiδ​s12​c13​c12​c23​−s12​s23​s13​eiδ−c12​s23​−s12​c23​s13​eiδ​s13​e−iδs23​c13​c23​c13​​​.
(23)
Complex entries alone do not establish CP violation, because quark quantum field rephasings can change their phases. For nondegenerate quark masses the weak mixing sector conserves CP precisely when a basis makes V real. A useful invariant test is the Jarlskog invariant,
J=Im(Vud​Vcs​Vus∗​Vcd∗​)=c12​c23​c132​s12​s23​s13​sinδ.
(24)
A nonzero J requires all three mixing angles and a nontrivial phase. If two same-charge quark masses coincide, the additional basis freedom removes the corresponding physical CP effect. The possible QCD theta angle is a separate CP parameter, not part of this weak-mixing count.
The Wolfenstein parametrization displays the hierarchy of quark mixing economically. Put λW​=s12​, s23​=AλW2​, and s13​eiδ=AλW3​(ρ+iη). Expanding the exact matrix gives
V=​1−λW2​/2−λW​AλW3​(1−ρ−iη)​λW​1−λW2​/2−AλW2​​AλW3​(ρ−iη)AλW2​1​​+O(λW4​).
(25)
The observed pattern is predominantly diagonal, with 1-2 mixing largest and 2-3, 1-3 mixing successively suppressed. The expansion implies J≃A2λW6​η. The truncated matrix must not be treated as an exactly unitary matrix.
Column orthogonality gives
Vud​Vub∗​+Vcd​Vcb∗​+Vtd​Vtb∗​=0.
(26)
The three complex numbers form a CKM unitarity triangle, with area ∣J∣/2. Dividing by −Vcd​Vcb∗​ puts its vertices at 0, 1 and ρˉ​+iηˉ​, where ρˉ​+iηˉ​=−Vud​Vub∗​/(Vcd​Vcb∗​). The barred parameters include higher-order corrections to the Wolfenstein parameters. Comparing independent measurements of its sides and angles tests both quark mixing and the absence of additional flavor dynamics.
Semileptonic decay rates determine magnitudes of CKM entries through factors ∣Vij​∣2 once hadronic matrix elements are controlled. Neutral kaon and neutral B-meson mixing constrain products of entries through weak box scattering amplitudes. CP asymmetries probe their invariant relative phases, with kaon mixing providing indirect CP violation and decay amplitudes providing direct CP violation. These processes complement tests of the unitarity constraints on rows and columns. The minimal three-generation Standard Model describes weak quark mixing by a unitary matrix with three angles and one physical CP phase; it does not predict the Yukawa couplings or their hierarchy.
