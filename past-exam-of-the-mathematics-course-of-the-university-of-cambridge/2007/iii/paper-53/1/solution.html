Adopt the phase convention CP∣K0⟩=−∣Kˉ0⟩ and CP∣Kˉ0⟩=−∣K0⟩. Changing both phases changes the convention for the mixing parameter, not physical predictions. The flavor contents are K0=dsˉ and Kˉ0=sdˉ. A pair of charged weak currents changes strangeness by two units through the following box contractions, with ui​,uj​=u,c,t summed over.
The drawing uses unitary gauge: the W boson gauge-boson propagator contains its longitudinal part. In a gauge of a renormalizable quantum field theory, charged Goldstone boson boxes must also be included to obtain the same gauge-independent result. The external lines represent the valence quarks; the meson matrix element also contains strong interaction binding effects.
Put λi​=Vis∗​Vid​, xi​=mi2​/mW2​ and PL​=(1−γ5)/2. The charged weak box contribution to kaon mixing has the effective four-fermion interaction structure
HΔS=2​=C(sˉγμ​PL​d)(sˉγμPL​d)+h.c.,C∼16π2GF2​mW2​​∑i,j​λi​λj​S(xi​,xj​).
(1)
Here GF​ is the Fermi constant, V is the CKM matrix, and S is a dimensionless loop function from a Feynman integral; the estimate suppresses convention-dependent numerical factors. Four weak interaction Feynman vertices and a one-loop Feynman integral give the coupling and loop order suppression. Unitarity of the CKM matrix gives ∑i​λi​=0, so the GIM mechanism cancels the mass-independent part. With a negligible up quark mass, the leading charm quark term has S(xc​,xc​)∼xc​. Define fK​ by ⟨0∣sˉγμ​γ5d∣K0(p)⟩=ifK​pμ​. Estimating the hadronic matrix element by fK2​mK​BK​, with the dimensionless kaon bag parameter BK​ of order unity, gives
∣H21′​∣charm​∼16π2GF2​mc2​​∣Vcs∗​Vcd​∣2fK2​mK​BK​​.
(2)
The hadronic normalization includes division by 2mK​ when relativistically normalized states are converted to the effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing. Since ∣Vcs∗​Vcd​∣≃sinθC​cosθC​, the Cabibbo suppression is explicit. Illustrative scales GF​∼10−5GeV−2, mc​∼1GeV, fK​∼0.1GeV and mK​∼0.5GeV give an order 10−16 to 10−15GeV estimate. The full sum includes charm quark-top quark and top quark-top quark terms, important for CP violation; long-distance strong interaction contributions prevent this dimensional estimate from being a precision prediction. A meson matrix element necessarily needs hadronic input in addition to electroweak parameters.
For the decaying kaon system write H′=M−iΓ/2, where M and Γ are Hermitian matrices. The entries denoted Mij​ in the question are entries of this effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing; they need not themselves form a Hermitian matrix. CPT invariance equates the diagonal masses and diagonal decay widths, giving
H11′​=H22′​​.
(3)
One can see why there is no complex conjugation on the right as follows. In the flavor subspace let J=(01​10​) and represent the antiunitary CPT operation by J followed by complex conjugation. CPT changes a projected forward propagator into its adjoint. Indeed, if P projects onto the flavor subspace and Hfull​ is the Hermitian microscopic generator, its invariance and the antiunitary reversal of i give ΘPe−iHfull​tPΘ−1=Pe+iHfull​tP=(Pe−iHfull​tP)†. In the time-independent two-state approximation, the effective generator this means JH′∗J=H′†, whose diagonal entries give (H22′​)∗=(H11′​)∗; the off-diagonal entries impose no further condition. Equivalently, apply CPT separately to the dispersive and absorptive Hermitian matrices. Treating the decaying effective Hamiltonian for neutral-meson mixing as a Hermitian matrix would incorrectly discard the absorptive part.
In the chosen flavor phase convention CP is represented by −J. Its invariance requires JH′J=H′. Thus it gives diagonal equality and additionally
H12′​=H21′​​.
(4)
The normalized CP eigenstates are
∣K10​⟩=2​∣K0⟩−∣Kˉ0⟩​,∣K20​⟩=2​∣K0⟩+∣Kˉ0⟩​​.
(5)
Applying CP directly gives eigenvalues +1 and −1 respectively.
Now use CPT to put H′=(ac​ba​) and take correlated square roots α=b​, β=c​. Its characteristic polynomial equation is (a−μ)2−bc=0, and direct multiplication shows
H′(−βα​)=(a−αβ)(−βα​),H′(βα​)=(a+αβ)(βα​).
(6)
Expressing these two eigenvectors in the CP eigenstate basis gives, respectively, coefficients proportional to (α+β,α−β) and (α−β,α+β). Therefore the kaon CP mixing parameter is
ϵ=α+βα−β​=M12​​+M21​​M12​​−M21​​​,pq​=αβ​=1+ϵ1−ϵ​,
(7)
and, because the CP eigenstate basis is orthonormal, the normalized states at production are
∣KS0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K10​⟩+ϵ∣K20​⟩​,∣KL0​⟩=1+∣ϵ∣2​∣K20​⟩+ϵ∣K10​⟩​​.
(8)
The kaon mixing square-root branch convention correlates the roots continuously with the limit of conserved CP b=c, where α=β and ϵ=0. Reversing one root interchanges the two eigenstates. The labels short and long are fixed by their decay widths: their complex eigenvalues are μS,L​=mS,L​−iΓS,L​/2, and subsequent evolution supplies e−iμS,L​t. The displayed formula assumes nonzero mixing and α+β=0, as in the physical kaon system; degenerate or defective matrices require a separate limiting treatment.
