Use the conventional kinematic plume fluxes
Q=b2w,M=b2w2,F=b2wg′.​
(49)
Then πQ is the volume flux, πρr​Q the dimensional mass flux, πρr​M the dimensional momentum flux, and πF the kinematic buoyancy flux. Calling πQ a mass flux uses a density-normalized convention: the reference density has been suppressed. If density-weighted momentum and buoyancy fluxes are desired, multiply M and F by the same constant ρr​ throughout. Here the conventional kinematic definitions make the Boussinesq approximation explicit.
For an upward plume with Q,M>0, the inverse relations are
A=MQ2​,w=QM​,b=M​Q​,B=Ag′=MFQ​.
(50)
The volume conservation, vertical momentum conservation, and buoyancy balances from part a become
∂t​(MQ2​)+Qz​Qt​+Mz​∂t​(MFQ​)+Fz​​=2αM​,=MFQ​,=−N2Q.​​
(51)
Here the vertical momentum conservation equation uses ρr​ in the inertia and retains (ρ0​−ρ)gA=ρr​B in the buoyancy force. The ambient has no imposed vertical momentum at entrainment.
To classify the resulting partial differential equations, work in the nonsingular primitive variables (A,w,g′) with A>0. Subtract w times the volume equation from the momentum equation and g′ times the volume equation from the buoyancy equation. This gives
At​+wAz​+Awz​wt​+wwz​gt′​+wgz′​​=2αwA​,=g′−A​2αw2​,=−N2w−A​2αwg′​.​
(52)
The principal matrix is therefore
K=​w00​Aw0​00w​​,det(K−λI)=(w−λ)3.
(53)
All three characteristic speeds are real and equal to w, so the model is a hyperbolic system if that term means only reality of the characteristic roots. However, for A>0, (K−wI)v=0 requires vw​=0, leaving only two independent eigenvectors. Hence
the top-hat system is weakly hyperbolic, not strictly or strongly hyperbolic.​
(54)
This weak hyperbolicity of the unsteady top-hat plume prevents a complete set of independent characteristic fields. For example, freezing A,w in the homogeneous principal part gives Dt​δw=0 and Dt​δA=−A∂z​δw. A Fourier perturbation of wave number k therefore produces a term proportional to kt in δA. Real characteristic roots alone do not provide uniform control of high-frequency perturbations. The flux-variable transformation above is invertible in the stated region, so changing to (Q,M,F) does not remove this degeneracy.
