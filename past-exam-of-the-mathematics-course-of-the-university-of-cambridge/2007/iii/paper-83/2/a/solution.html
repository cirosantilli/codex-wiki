Interpret ρ2​ as the ambient density: the PDF's phrase assigning it a “depth” is a typographical error. Take 0<ρ1​−ρ2​≪ρ2​, and use a common reference density in inertia while retaining the density difference in buoyancy. This is the Boussinesq approximation, with reduced gravity g′=g(ρ1​−ρ2​)/ρref​>0. For the shallow water approximation, the lower-layer depth must be small compared with longitudinal variation scales, vertical accelerations must be negligible and pressure hydrostatic. The deep ambient is approximated as a hydrostatic pressure reservoir away from the nose; assume a uniform velocity over each lower-layer cross-section and neglect drag and entrainment.
Write the prismatic triangular area as A(h)=kh2 with fixed k>0, so the width at height z is 2kz. The excess hydrostatic pressure force divided by reference density is
P(h)=g′∫0h​(h−z)2kzdz=3g′kh3​.
(16)
The prismatic-channel shallow water equations are therefore
(kh2)t​+(kh2u)x​=0,(kh2u)t​+(kh2u2+3g′kh3​)x​=0.​
(17)
For positive depth, divide the first equation by 2kh and use it in the second to obtain
ht​+uhx​+2h​ux​=0,ut​+uux​+g′hx​=0.​
(18)
The coefficient matrix in variables (h,u) is (ug′​h/2u​). Its two distinct real characteristic speeds are
λ±​=u±c,c=g′h/2​.​
(19)
Thus the system is strictly hyperbolic for h>0 and g′>0, though it degenerates at zero depth. Since dc/dh=c/(2h), direct substitution in the two equations gives
[∂t​+(u±c)∂x​](u±4c)=0.​
(20)
Hence R±​=u±4c are the Riemann invariants conserved along their respective characteristic curves. The factor four, rather than the rectangular-channel factor two, comes from the triangular relation A∝h2.
