In the energy-conserving blast regime, neglect ambient pressure, radiative losses, ejecta mass and gravity compared with the explosion. The cold ambient ideal gas has no sound speed scale, and its power-law mass density has no preferred radius. The point explosion introduces only its energy E and elapsed time t. This scale-free setting supports a self-similar blast wave; extra physics would limit the time interval over which that description applies.
The mass swept up to radius R is
M(R)=4π∫0R​Cr2−βdr=3−β4πC​R3−β.
(1)
It is finite because β<3. With no energy loss, the kinetic energy and internal energy both scale as CR3−βR˙2. Therefore R3−βR˙2 is constant and integration gives
R(t)=A(CE​)1/(5−β)t2/(5−β)​.
(2)
The dimensionless coefficient A depends on the adiabatic exponent and ambient exponent through the similarity profiles. Indeed, [E/C]=L5−βT−2, which independently verifies the scaling. The shock wave decelerates for 0<β<3, since its time exponent lies between 2/5 and 1.

Let the equilibrium ideal gas be at rest, with uniform ρ0​,p0​ and B0​=B0​ez​. This local wave calculation neglects gravity, viscosity and magnetic diffusion. Define the adiabatic sound speed c≡vs​=(γp0​/ρ0​)1/2, the Alfvén speed va​=B0​/(μ0​ρ0​)1/2, and a wavevector k=k(sinθ,0,cosθ), where k=∣k∣ and θ is its angle to the equilibrium magnetic field. Perturbations have dependence eik⋅x−iωt and phase speed v=ω/k.
The linearized ideal magnetohydrodynamic equations for mass conservation, pressure, induction and momentum give
δρ=ωρ0​​k⋅u′,δp=c2δρ,b=ωB0​(k⋅u′)−u′(k⋅B0​)​,

ωρ0​u′=kδp−μ0​1​[(k⋅B0​)b−k(B0​⋅b)].
(20)
The relation between pressure and mass density follows from eliminating k⋅u′ between the two material evolution equations; it applies to the nonzero-frequency wave sector. The induction result automatically has k⋅b=0.

ω2uy′​=va2​k2cos2θuy′​.
(21)
Thus the transverse Alfvén wave has
vA2​=va2​cos2θ​.

vf​=c[1+2ϵ​sin2θ+O(ϵ2)],vslow​=va​∣cosθ∣[1−2ϵ​sin2θ+O(ϵ2)]​.

The fast magnetosonic wave is predominantly an acoustic wave, with velocity almost parallel to k and appreciable mass density and gas pressure variations. Its magnetic pressure correction is weak. The Alfvén wave is exactly incompressible, polarized perpendicular to the k-B0​ plane and restored by magnetic tension.
The slow magnetosonic wave is polarized inside that plane and almost perpendicular to k. Indeed, the leading sound speed part of the matrix in (a) annihilates a vector perpendicular to k, so only the smaller magnetic restoring force acts on that MHD wave polarization. Its compression is smaller by order ϵ, with gas pressure changes almost cancelling magnetic pressure changes. It is often called the pseudo-Alfvén wave MHD wave polarization in this high pressure limit. Thus two distinct almost-incompressible magnetic MHD wave polarizations have the same leading speed, while the third is predominantly sound. For exactly perpendicular propagation the Alfvén wave and slow magnetosonic wave frequencies vanish; for parallel propagation the two transverse magnetic MHD wave polarizations are degenerate.

Take B0​=Bz​>0, va​=B0​/μ0​ρ0​​ and ζ=k(z−va​t). The transverse ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation and magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation are satisfied by ux​=−Bx​/μ0​ρ0​​, uy​=uz​=0, with constant mass density. However, the longitudinal magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation also requires
0=−∂z​(p+2μ0​Bx2​​).
(28)
Here Bx2​=a2B02​cos2ζ is not constant. Balancing its gradient would require
p=p∗​(t)−2μ0​a2B02​​cos2ζ.
(29)
The adiabatic pressure equation instead gives ∂t​p=0, since this velocity is divergence-free and has no component along the spatial variation. No choice of p∗​(t) removes the spatially varying time derivative. Thus a nontrivial finite-amplitude linearly polarized sinusoidal Alfvén wave is not an exact pure traveling wave in a compressible ideal fluid. The magnetic-pressure obstruction to a linearly polarized Alfvén wave is quadratic in a, so the wave is a valid linear approximation and drives a compressive response at higher order. In an incompressible model pressure is a constraint variable; it is not governed by the same compressible adiabatic evolution law.
Allowing longitudinal flow and varying mass density does not rescue a regular one-dimensional periodic traveling solution with precisely this transverse magnetic field. To see this, let U=uz​−va​ and use the traveling coordinate. Integrated continuity, induction and transverse momentum give
ρU=m,UBx​−B0​ux​=A,mux​−μ0​B0​Bx​​=D,
(30)
where m,A,D are constants. Eliminating ux​ gives
(ρm2​−μ0​B02​​)Bx​=mA+B0​D.
(31)
At any zero of Bx​, finite mass density and velocity require the right side to vanish. Wherever Bx​=0, the coefficient then vanishes and fixes ρ=μ0​m2/B02​, constant; continuity fixes U, and adiabatic entropy transport fixes p. The longitudinal magnetohydrodynamic momentum equation again contradicts the varying Bx2​. The alternative m=0 makes transverse momentum force Bx​ constant. Trivial cases such as a=0 or k=0 are excluded from the nontrivial wave conclusion.

For the circular MHD wave polarization, Bx2​+By2​=a2B02​ is constant. Choose any constant ρ0​>0,p0​>0, put va​=B0​/μ0​ρ0​​ and take
u=−ava​(cosζ,sinζ,0),ρ=ρ0​,p=p0​,ζ=k(z−va​t)​.

Use a steady axisymmetric flow governed by ideal magnetohydrodynamics and take the Newtonian gravitational potential to be axisymmetric. Work locally on connected regular magnetic flux surfaces with nonzero poloidal flow. As usual for a wind without an imposed toroidal loop voltage, Eϕ​=0; regularity on an included rotation axis guarantees this condition. It is needed to exclude steady cross-field flow in an annular domain.
Since ∇ϕ=eϕ​/R, the poloidal magnetic flux function gives
Bp​=R1​∇ψ×eϕ​,BR​=−R1​∂z​ψ,Bz​=R1​∂R​ψ,Bp​⋅∇ψ=0.
(34)
The poloidal magnetic field is divergence-free. The zero toroidal electric field, with E=−u×B, gives up​∥Bp​. Define k by ρup​=kBp​. Steady mass conservation then gives
0=∇⋅(ρup​)=∇⋅(kBp​)=Bp​⋅∇k.
(35)
Thus k=k(ψ) is the magnetohydrodynamic mass loading, the mass flux per unit poloidal magnetic flux. Subtracting (k/ρ)B from the full velocity leaves only an azimuthal component. The cross product is
u×B=(uϕ​−ρkBϕ​​)R∇ψ​≡ω∇ψ.
(36)
The steady ideal magnetohydrodynamic induction equation requires 0=∇×(ω∇ψ)=∇ω×∇ψ. Consequently the field-line angular velocity is ω=ω(ψ) and
u=ρk(ψ)B​+Rω(ψ)eϕ​​.

ρup​⋅∇(Ruϕ​)=μ0​1​Bp​⋅∇(RBϕ​).

(41)
These flux-label functions are local on connected regular surfaces; disconnected components with the same numerical label need not share constants without an additional matching condition.

On one poloidal flux surface, the field-line angular velocity ω(ψ) is constant. In a frame rotating at that rate the relative velocity is v=u−Rωeϕ​=kB/ρ, parallel to the magnetic field. The Lorentz force is perpendicular to this motion, and the Coriolis force also does no work. Centrifugal acceleration comes from the effective potential −R2ω2/2. Since entropy is constant along the surface, pressure work integrates to specific enthalpy w, with dw=dp/ρ along the flow.
Therefore kinetic energy relative to the rotating poloidal flux surface, enthalpy and effective gravitational-plus-centrifugal potential have a constant sum:
21​∣u−Rωeϕ​∣2+w+Φ−21​R2ω2=ε(ψ)​.
(42)
This is a corotating energy integral, not the laboratory energy alone: Maxwell stress tensor stresses can transfer energy and angular momentum in the laboratory frame. In fact the expression equals ∣u∣2/2+w+Φ−ωRuϕ​, namely the total magnetohydrodynamic Bernoulli invariant minus ωℓ. Differential rotation between surfaces does not invalidate the argument, because the chosen angular velocity is fixed along the particular surface being followed.

e−η(1+tanhη)=eη(1−tanhη)=sechη.
(64)
Therefore the marginal fragmentation mode of an isothermal slab has
∣k∣=H1​,Φ′=ϕ∝sech(z/H),δρ∝sech3(z/H)​.
(65)
At n=1 the second independent solution obtained by reduction of order grows at infinity, so the coincidence of the two explicit formulas does not supply an extra admissible mode. At k=0 a uniform vertical translation gives another neutral disturbance, and mass-preserving relabeling displacements can have δρ=ϕ=0. Neither is the nonzero-wavenumber fragmentation threshold just calculated.
The unstable fragmentation modes have wavelengths greater than 2πH. Gas-pressure support becomes less effective relative to self-gravity at long wavelength. The side of the threshold can also be checked from the energy identity: at ∣k∣=1/H the marginal mass density disturbance has zero quadratic energy. Keep that mass density disturbance and decrease ∣k∣; the pressure integral stays fixed, while the gravitational term becomes more negative because the positive inverse operator (−∂z2​+k2)−1 increases as k2 decreases. Thus the quadratic energy becomes negative, giving an unstable mode on the longer-wavelength side. This argument concerns the gravitational sector and its stated decay conditions.
