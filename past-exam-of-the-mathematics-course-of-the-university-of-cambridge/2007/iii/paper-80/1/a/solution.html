Use time dependence e−iωt, positive wavenumber k and aperture normal n=ex​. Write F(y′,z′)=Einc,y​(0,y′,z′) on the aperture and extend F by zero outside it. Define
F(ν,η)=∫A​F(y′,z′)e−i(νy′+ηz′)dy′dz′,κ=k2−ν2−η2​,q=(κ,ν,η).
(1)
The outgoing branch has κ≥0 for propagating components and Imκ>0 for evanescent components.
Fix the Green function normalization by G0​(r)=eik∣r∣/(4π∣r∣), so (Δ+k2)G0​=−δ. Its transverse Fourier transform satisfies (∂x2​+κ2)G0​=−δ(x). An outgoing solution on both sides is proportional to eiκ∣x∣, and its derivative jump fixes G0​=ieiκ∣x∣/(2κ). Thus the Weyl plane-wave representation for observation at x>0 is
G0​=2(2π)2i​∫R2​κeiκx+iν(y−y′)+iη(z−z′)​dνdη.
(2)
Since n×Einc​=Fez​, the given aperture integral becomes a superposition of curls of ez​eiq⋅r. Each curl supplies iq×ez​=i(ν,−κ,0). Therefore, with the unit prefactor in that integral,
E(x,y,z)=2(2π)21​∫R2​F(ν,η)(−κν​,1,0)eiκx+iνy+iηzdνdη​.
(3)
Every vector amplitude is transverse to q, as Maxwell's equations require. The polarizer specifies the incident aperture field; diffraction can regenerate an x component outside the aperture. In contrast, its z component remains zero in this representation. Evanescent plane waves are needed for the complete near field, not just the propagating disk ν2+η2≤k2.
The factor 1/2 follows explicitly from the standard outgoing Green function for the three-dimensional Helmholtz equation and the printed unit curl prefactor. If the intended kernel is the doubled half-space kernel 2G0​, or the aperture formula carries the usual compensating factor 2, delete that 1/2. The latter normalization makes the tangential output trace equal to F. Keeping the convention explicit avoids silently changing the supplied representation; the angular dependence and transversality of an electromagnetic plane wave are the same in either normalization. Reversing the specified normal also reverses the overall sign.
