Represent the thin layer by its wave phase jump at the exit x=0 and take the incident envelope to have amplitude one. Then E(0,z)=eiϕ(z). Stationarity makes the wave phase mean a constant μ; it need not be zero, since the question specifies only the variance. For a normal distribution marginal, write ϕ=μ+σZ with Z∼N(0,1). If F(t)=E[eitZ], integration by parts in the Gaussian density gives F′(t)=−tF(t) and F(0)=1, hence F(t)=e−t2/2. Thus its characteristic function gives
⟨E(0,z)⟩=⟨eiϕ(z)⟩=eiμ−σ2/2≡M.
(17)
The Fourier transform of this constant is 2πMδ(ν), interpreted distributionally for the infinite plane wave. Applying the propagator from part (a),
⟨E(x,ν)⟩=2πeiμ−σ2/2δ(ν)e−iν2x/(2k)=2πeiμ−σ2/2δ(ν)​.
(18)
If the wave phase has zero mean, omit eiμ. A nonunit incident amplitude simply multiplies the expression. Only the one-point Gaussian distribution is needed for this expected value; no assumption about joint Gaussian distributions at separated points is needed.
