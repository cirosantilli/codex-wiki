Inverse Fourier transform of the expected value gives
⟨E(x,z)⟩=eiμ−σ2/2​,
(19)
independent of both coordinates. Its squared modulus is the coherent scalar wave intensity e−σ2, which must not be confused with the mean wave intensity.
For the latter use the stationary two-point statistics. Let F0​(dν) be the uncentered spectral measure of a stationary random field of E(0,z), normalized by
⟨E(0,z+ζ)E(0,z)∗⟩=∫eiνζF0​(dν).
(20)
Since ∣E(0,z)∣=1, its total mass is ∫F0​(dν)=1. Under free paraxial propagation each spectral component is multiplied by e−iν2x/(2k); in its wave-field correlation this wave phase multiplies its complex conjugate and cancels. The stationary intensity under free paraxial propagation is consequently
⟨I(x,z)⟩=∫F0​(dν)=1​.
(21)
Stationarity is essential to this pointwise statement: it makes the spectral covariance diagonal. Conservation of integrated power alone would not show that a particular spatial point keeps the same scalar wave intensity.
The same result follows locally from Ix​+Jz​=0, where J=Im(E∗Ez​)/k. Taking expectations makes ⟨J⟩ independent of z, so ∂x​⟨I⟩=0. Thus coherent scalar wave intensity and mean wave intensity are both constant in propagation, with different values. The incoherent contribution has mean 1−e−σ2. Individual realizations can show focusing and changing scalar wave intensity even though the expected value remains constant. All these propagation statements concern the free parabolic wave equation from part (a).
