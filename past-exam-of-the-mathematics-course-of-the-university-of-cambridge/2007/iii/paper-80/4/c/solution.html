To illustrate Tikhonov regularization precisely, take the unknown to be the trace g on a chosen enclosing sphere of radius R. Use Hilbert spaces X=Y=L2(S2) and the forward linear operator
(Ag)nm​=dn​gnm​​,dn​=kin+1hn(1)​(kR).
(35)
At real positive kR, dn​=0, because the real Spherical Bessel functions jn​,yn​ have a nonzero Wronskian and cannot vanish simultaneously. Moreover ∣dn​∣→∞. Thus A is an injective compact operator: truncating to finitely many harmonic orders approximates it in operator norm, since the remaining diagonal entries tend uniformly to zero. Its inverse is unbounded. This is a linear field reconstruction; estimating the obstacle from the reconstructed field remains a further nonlinear step.
Given coefficients bnm​ of measured data yδ, minimize the quadratic functional
∥Ag−yδ∥2+α∥g∥2,α>0.
(36)
Varying g in every direction gives (A∗A+αI)gαδ​=A∗yδ. The operator A∗A+αI is bounded below by αI, so it is invertible and the minimizer is unique. In coefficients the minimization is separable: A∗y has coefficient bnm​/dn​​ and A∗A has multiplier ∣dn​∣−2. Hence the Tikhonov regularization of spherical far-field continuation is
(gαδ​)nm​=1+α∣dn​∣2dn​bnm​​.​
(37)
The implied regularized far-field coefficients are bnm​/(1+α∣dn​∣2). This suppresses precisely the high-order coefficients that make backward continuation unstable.
Let g† be the exact trace and y=Ag†, with ∥yδ−y∥≤δ. The noise-bias decomposition for linear regularization is
gαδ​−g†=(A∗A+αI)−1A∗(yδ−y)−α(A∗A+αI)−1g†.
(38)
For a singular value s=∣dn​∣−1, the noise multiplier is s/(s2+α). Since s2+α≥2sα​, its supremum is at most 1/(2α​). Consequently
∥gαδ​−g†∥≤2α​δ​+B(α),B(α)=​α(A∗A+αI)−1g†​.​
(39)
This Tikhonov stability bound measures the trace norm, not a distance between obstacle boundaries. The bias coefficient is α/(∣dn​∣−2+α) times the exact coefficient. It tends to zero for each fixed n,m, is bounded by one, and ∑∣gn†,m​∣2<∞. Splitting off a finite sum and bounding its tail therefore proves B(α)→0 as α→0.
A convergent regularization parameter choice thus satisfies
α(δ)→0,α(δ)​δ​→0.​
(40)
In nondimensionalized variables, α=δp with 0<p<2 is one such rule. Without a smoothness assumption on the exact trace there is no universal bias rate and no universal optimal value of α. For a concrete normal-operator source condition, suppose g†=A∗Aw and ∥w∥≤C. Each bias multiplier on w is αs2/(s2+α)≤α, so B(α)≤Cα. Minimizing the resulting bound gives, for C>0,
dαd​(Cα+2α​δ​)=C−4α3/2δ​,α=(4Cδ​)2/3,∥gαδ​−g†∥=O(C1/3δ2/3).​
(41)
This optimizes the displayed worst-case bound under that additional source condition, rather than guaranteeing an exact optimum for every datum.
If only the effect of measurement error is minimized, the multiplier bound decreases when α increases. Indeed ∥(A∗A+αI)−1A∗∥≤∥A∥/α→0 as α→∞. But the reconstruction then approaches zero and its bias approaches ∥g†∥. Suppressing noise alone does not optimize reconstruction: α must balance noise amplification against regularization bias. A geometrically accurate obstacle estimate additionally needs justified shape constraints and a stable procedure for extracting a boundary from the regularized field.
