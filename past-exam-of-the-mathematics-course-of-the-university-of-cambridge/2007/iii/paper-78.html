For a fixed fluid domain with prescribed velocity on its whole boundary, the Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow compares the actual incompressible Stokes flow u with every admissible incompressible velocity field v having the same boundary values. The trial fields need not themselves satisfy the momentum equation. With e(u)=(∇u+∇uT)/2 and constant dynamic viscosity μ, define the viscous dissipation
D[u]=2μ∫e(u):e(u)dV.
(1)
Assume no nonconservative body force, or absorb a conservative force into the pressure. Set w=v−u, so ∇⋅w=0 and w=0 on the boundary. The Stokes stress tensor σ=−pI+2μe(u) obeys ∇⋅σ=0. Because pressure has zero contraction with e(w),
2μ∫e(u):e(w)dV=∫σ:∇wdV=∫∂V​w⋅σndS−∫V​w⋅(∇⋅σ)dV=0.
(2)
Expanding the square proves
D[v]=D[u]+2μ∫e(w):e(w)dV≥D[u].​
(3)
Equality requires e(w)=0, so w is a rigid motion; the homogeneous boundary values force it to vanish. The theorem concerns fixed boundary velocities, not fixed applied forces or arbitrary comparisons between different fluid domains.
For the concentric-sphere motion, seek u=F(r)Ω×x. Incompressibility is automatic. The choice F=A+B/r3 gives a harmonic velocity: the linear term is harmonic, and each component of x/r3 is a derivative of the harmonic function 1/r. The no-slip conditions F(a)=1, F(b)=0 give
B=b3−a3a3b3​,A=−b3−a3a3​.
(4)
Thus the Rotational Stokes flow between concentric spheres is
u(x)=b3−a3a3​(r3b3​−1)Ω×x.​
(5)
It satisfies the Stokes equation with constant pressure, and the uniqueness implied by the theorem identifies it as the required flow.
In the Unscaled Papkovich–Neuber representation, take Φ=−u/2 and χ=0. Both potentials are harmonic and x⋅Φ=0, so the representation gives u=−2Φ. Also
∇⋅Φ=−21​{∇F⋅(Ω×x)+F∇⋅(Ω×x)}=0,
(6)
and therefore p=0, after choosing the arbitrary pressure constant.
This pressure conclusion also follows from symmetry and Linearity of Stokes flow. Pressure is a scalar linear in the axial vector Ω, and isotropy leaves only a possible term proportional to Ω⋅x. That is a pseudoscalar, forbidden by reflection symmetry of the concentric spherical domain. Hence the pressure perturbation vanishes. Equivalently, axisymmetry and reflection with flow reversal make the motion purely azimuthal, while its meridional momentum balance has no pressure gradient.
Let v=Ω×x in the following stress calculation. The symmetric velocity gradient is
eij​=2rF′(r)​(xj​vi​+xi​vj​),F′=−r43B​.
(7)
The full stress tensor, in the zero-pressure gauge, is therefore
σij​=−r53μB​(xj​vi​+xi​vj​).​
(8)
For n=x/r, the traction on a radial surface is σn=−3μB(Ω×n)/r3. This completely specifies the stress field; with the polar axis along Ω, its only nonzero spherical shear component is σrϕ​=−3μBΩsinθ/r3.
The fluid couple on the inner sphere is the integral of x×σn with the normal pointing from the solid into the fluid. Using ∫ni​nj​dω=(4π/3)δij​,
∫r=a​x×σndS=−3μB∫{Ω−n(n⋅Ω)}dω=−8πμBΩ.
(9)
Hence the sustaining applied Torque in rotational Stokes flow between concentric spheres is
G=b3−a38πμa3b3​Ω.​
(10)
For a≪b, it approaches the isolated rotating sphere in Stokes flow result 8πμa3Ω. For a narrow gap d=b−a≪a, it becomes 8πμa4Ω/(3d): the divergent resistance is that of a thin Couette shear layer, with speed scale aΩ and gradient scale aΩ/d.
Now add the force-free, couple-free rigid particles and denote the resulting velocity by up​. Extend up​ through each particle as its rigid translational and rotational velocity. No slip makes this extension continuous across every particle surface; it is incompressible and has zero rate-of-strain tensor inside each particle. It therefore gives an admissible trial velocity in the original particle-free annulus, with exactly the same sphere boundary velocities. Its full-annulus dissipation is the actual fluid dissipation Dp​, since the added interior integrals are zero. The Minimum-dissipation theorem for Stokes flow consequently gives Dp​≥D0​.
The boundary-work identity follows by integrating ∇⋅(σu): dissipation equals the mechanical power supplied at all rigid boundaries. The outer sphere is stationary; the inner sphere has no translation, so any force needed to hold its centre does no work; and each added particle contributes zero power because its net force and couple are zero. Thus Dp​=Gp​⋅Ω, while D0​=G0​⋅Ω. Therefore
(Gp​−G0​)⋅Ω≥0.​
(11)
For Ω=0 and at least one particle of positive volume in the open annulus, the inequality is strict. Equality would make the extended velocity identical to the particle-free solution, yet that solution has nonzero strain on every open region away from the rotation axis and cannot be rigid throughout any particle interior. Hence the applied couple component along the imposed angular velocity increases. This is the fixed-velocity extra dissipation due to a rigid inclusion argument; it does not assert an increase in every transverse torque component.

For a slender viscous bubble, approximate the interface normal by the radial direction and its curvature by 1/a. Axial curvature and axial derivatives of the leading external motion are smaller by the slenderness factors. The exterior leading Stokes flow is locally radial. Incompressibility gives ∂r​(rur​)=0, while the kinematic boundary condition gives ur​(a)=at​ to leading order, hence
ur​(r,z,t)=ra(z,t)at​(z,t)​.
(12)
The radial viscous Laplacian of this 1/r velocity vanishes, so exterior pressure is constant to leading order; set its reference value to zero. Its radial normal stress at the interface is 2μ∂r​ur​∣a​=−2μat​/a. Inner viscous normal stress is smaller by the viscosity ratio λ, and the bubble's leading normal stress is −P. The interfacial stress balance with variable surface tension, here with constant γ, consequently gives
P−a2μat​​=aγ​,P=a2μat​​+aγ​.​
(13)
These are local leading-order relations: the outer axial flow and tangential traction enter at higher slenderness order. The sign also has a useful check: a uniform cylinder with no imposed excess pressure contracts, at​=−γ/(2μ), under surface tension.

An insoluble surfactant is advected with the surface fluid and has no exchange with the bulk. With negligible surface diffusion, its amount on a material surface strip is conserved. Under the small-slope lubrication approximation, surface arclength equals projected horizontal length to leading order, giving
