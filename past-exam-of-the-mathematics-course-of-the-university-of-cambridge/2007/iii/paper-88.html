A Freiman 2-isomorphism is an injective map onto its image preserving pair-sum equality in both directions, with repeated summands allowed. Put D=2A−2A, so D=−D, 0∈D, and ∣D∣<N. We prove the half-size cyclic Freiman model with a sharp difference-set bound. Assume A=∅; for an empty set the conclusion is immediate.
Choose an odd auxiliary prime P>N large enough that reduction modulo P is injective on A and no nonzero element of D reduces to zero. Choose a multiplier t uniformly in ZP×​. The forbidden residues are
F={jN(modP):j∈Z, 0<∣jN∣<P}.
(22)
They form a symmetric set, exclude zero since P>N, and have cardinality at most 2⌊(P−1)/N⌋. For each nonzero d∈D, the product td is uniform on the P−1 nonzero residues. Moreover, td∈F is the same event as t(−d)∈F, so one need only sum over the (∣D∣−1)/2 opposite pairs. The union bound gives
Pr(td∈F for some d∈D∖{0})≤2∣D∣−1​P−12⌊(P−1)/N⌋​≤N∣D∣−1​<1.
(23)
Fix a multiplier avoiding all forbidden events. The use of opposite pairs is what achieves the threshold N>∣D∣ without an extra factor of two.
Represent each ta(modP) by an integer r(a)∈{0,…,P−1}. Partition these residues into the two half-intervals
I0​={0,…,(P−1)/2},I1​={(P+1)/2,…,P−1}.
(24)
At least half of A has representatives in one of them; call this subset A′. Both intervals have diameter less than P/2, so for a1​,a2​,a3​,a4​∈A′ the integer
R=r(a1​)+r(a2​)−r(a3​)−r(a4​)
(25)
satisfies ∣R∣<P.
Define ϕ(a)=r(a)(modN). If a1​+a2​=a3​+a4​, then R≡0(modP), hence R=0 and the image relation holds. Conversely, if the image relation holds, then R=jN. If the original difference d=a1​+a2​−a3​−a4​ were nonzero, R≡td(modP) would be nonzero; since ∣R∣<P, this would be a forbidden residue in F. Thus d=0.
Finally ϕ is injective. If ϕ(a)=ϕ(b), choose any c∈A′ and apply the converse to ϕ(a)+ϕ(c)=ϕ(b)+ϕ(c), obtaining a+c=b+c. Therefore
∣A′∣≥∣A∣/2,ϕ:A′⟶ϕ(A′)⊆ZN​ is a Freiman 2-isomorphism.​
(26)
The construction actually works for any integer N>∣D∣; the prime hypothesis in the question is sufficient.

For disjoint nonempty vertex sets U,V, write d(U,V)=e(U,V)/(∣U∣∣V∣). A regular pair of vertex sets is ε-regular if every U′⊆U,V′⊆V with ∣U′∣≥ε∣U∣, ∣V′∣≥ε∣V∣ satisfies ∣d(U′,V′)−d(U,V)∣≤ε.
The Szemerédi regularity lemma states that, for every ε>0 and integer m0​, there are M,n0​ such that every finite simple graph on n≥n0​ vertices has a set partition
V=V0​⊔V1​⊔⋯⊔Vm​
(30)
with m0​≤m≤M, ∣V0​∣≤εn, ∣V1​∣=⋯=∣Vm​∣, and at most εm2 irregular unordered pairs among the nonexceptional classes. Prove it for 0<ε≤1/2; larger parameters follow by using a smaller one.
For the energy-increment proof of Szemerédi regularity, keep every exceptional vertex as a singleton cell in the set partition used to measure regularity energy. Define the equitable regularity energy
q(P)=n21​∑U,V∈P​∣U∣∣V∣d(U,V)2.
(31)
The sum is ordered, and on diagonal cells subset density means the average of the adjacency indicator, with zero on loops. Thus 0≤q≤1. If U,V are refined into Ua​,Vb​, expanding squares gives
∑a,b​∣Ua​∣∣Vb​∣d(Ua​,Vb​)2−∣U∣∣V∣d(U,V)2=∑a,b​∣Ua​∣∣Vb​∣[d(Ua​,Vb​)−d(U,V)]2≥0.
(32)
The cross term vanishes because the weighted arithmetic mean of the refined subset densities is the original subset density. Consequently every refinement of a set partition increases or preserves regularity energy.
Suppose the current nonexceptional classes have common size t and there are k of them. For each irregular pair Vi​,Vj​, choose witnesses Aij​,Bij​ of sizes at least εt and discrepancy greater than ε. Refine each class by all its incident witness subsets. There are at most k−1 cuts in each class and at most 2k resulting atoms. The witness rectangle is a union of refined rectangles. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, its contribution to the variance identity is at least
∣Aij​∣∣Bij​∣[d(Aij​,Bij​)−d(Vi​,Vj​)]2>ε4t2.
(33)
Indeed, the sum of squared deviations on the witness rectangle is at least its total weight times the square of their weighted arithmetic mean. If more than εk2 unordered pairs are irregular, summing even just one orientation of each gives
Δq>ε5n2k2t2​≥4ε5​,
(34)
provided the exceptional set has at most n/2 vertices. This is the strictly positive regularity energy increment.
Restore equal class sizes without losing that increment. Divide every witness atom into blocks of size ℓ=⌊t/4k⌋ and make every leftover vertex an exceptional singleton. This is a further refinement of a set partition, so it cannot decrease q. There are at most k2k atoms, hence fewer than k2kℓ≤n2−k newly exceptional vertices. If t≥2⋅4k, then ℓ≥t/(2⋅4k), so the number of full blocks is at most 2k4k. Each original class produces at least one full block: its leftovers occupy less than 2kℓ, whereas t≥4kℓ>2kℓ. Thus the new class count is at least k.
Choose R∗​=⌈4ε−5⌉+1, and choose an initial integer k0​≥m0​ large enough that R∗​2−k0​≤ε/2. Initially partition into k0​ equal classes and fewer than k0​ exceptional vertices. Take n0​ large enough that this initial exception is at most εn/2. During at most R∗​ rounds, the additional exception is at most R∗​n2−k0​≤εn/2, so it never exceeds εn≤n/2.
A bound on class counts is obtained by iterating k↦2k4k at most R∗​ times starting at k0​; call the resulting bound M. Increasing n0​ further to satisfy n0​≥4M4M guarantees t≥n/(2k)≥2⋅4k at every round, so all full-block constructions are valid. If the desired regularity had not been reached after R∗​ refinements, the regularity energy increments would total more than one, impossible. Therefore the process terminates with the required partition and bounds. The singleton treatment of discarded vertices is what preserves regularity energy monotonicity during equalization.

First derive the triangle removal lemma from the Szemerédi regularity lemma. Given an allowed deletion fraction 0<η<1, take d=η/10, m0​=⌈10/η⌉, and 0<ε≤min(d/4,η/100,1/8). Apply part (i), with upper class bound M. Delete edges incident with V0​, inside each nonexceptional class, between irregular pairs, and between regular pairs of subset density less than d. Their total number is at most
(2ε+2m0​1​+2d​)n2<ηn2.
(35)
These four terms are bounded respectively by εn2, n2/(2m0​), εn2, and dn2/2.
If a triangle in a graph remains, its three vertices lie in three distinct equal classes, all three regular pairs with subset density at least d. The permitted regular triangle counting lemma gives at least (d3/4)t3 triangles in a graph in the original graph, where t is the class size. This lower bound can also be checked directly: all but 2εt vertices of the first class have at least (d−ε)t neighbors in each of the other two. Those neighborhoods are large enough to use the regular pair condition on the remaining pair, giving at least (d−ε)3t2 edges between them. Hence the total is at least (1−2ε)(d−ε)3t3≥(d3/4)t3.
Since t≥n/(2M), a surviving triangle in a graph forces at least d3n3/(32M3) original triangles in a graph. Thus a graph with fewer than ξn3 triangles in a graph, where ξ=d3/(64M3)>0, can be made triangle-free by deleting fewer than ηn2 edges, for all sufficiently large n. This proves the removal lemma needed here.
Now use the triangle-removal proof of Roth theorem. Fix a subset density lower bound δ>0 and suppose A⊆[N], ∣A∣≥δN, has no nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression. Set m=2N+1 and G=Zm​. Use three disjoint copies X,Y,Z of G and put edges according to
xy∈E⟺y−x∈A,yz∈E⟺z−y∈A,xz∈E⟺(z−x)/2∈A.
(36)
Division by two is valid because m is odd. A triangle in a graph corresponds to a=y−x, b=z−y, c=(z−x)/2 with a,b,c∈A and a+b=2c. Since A⊆[N] and ∣a+b−2c∣<m, this congruence is an integer equality. Progression-freeness therefore forces a=b=c.
Every triangle in a graph is consequently of the form (x,x+a,x+2a) with x∈G,a∈A, so there are exactly m∣A∣ triangles in a graph. These triangles in a graph are edge-disjoint: an XY edge determines (x,a); a YZ edge determines a and then x; and an XZ edge determines a=(z−x)/2 and x. Eliminating them all requires at least m∣A∣ edge deletions.
The graph has 3m vertices but only m∣A∣≤mN=O(m2) triangles in a graph. Take η=δ/100. For large enough N its triangle in a graph count is below ξ(3m)3, so removal requires fewer than η(3m)2=9δm2/100 deletions. Yet
m∣A∣≥δmN≥3δm2​>1009δm2​,
(37)
a contradiction. Therefore every fixed positive-density subset of a sufficiently long interval contains a nonconstant three-term arithmetic progression.
This proves the qualitative Roth theorem on three-term arithmetic progressions. The quantitative bound from part 2(i) came from the explicit Fourier iteration; the regularity-removal argument gives a density-dependent threshold without that double-logarithmic estimate.

Use octahedral quasirandomness of a three-uniform hypergraph, with its eighth-power normalization. Let h:X×Y×Z→{0,1} be the hypergraph edge indicator function, define p=Ex,y,z​h(x,y,z), and put g=h−p. All expectations are uniform and independent on the indicated finite sets. The hypergraph is α-quasirandom when
∥g∥□38​=Ex0​,x1​,y0​,y1​,z0​,z1​​i,j,k∈{0,1}∏​g(xi​,yj​,zk​)≤α.​
(38)
The eight triples form the faces of the tripartite octahedral configuration. Repeated choices within a part are included in this normalized average. For real g, the expression is nonnegative, since it equals
Ex0​,x1​,y0​,y1​​(Ez​∏i,j∈{0,1}​g(xi​,yj​,z))2.
(39)
Thus the condition says that the balanced hypergraph edge indicator function has three-dimensional box norm at most α1/8. If the norm itself is used as the quasirandomness parameter instead, the eighth-power parameter here is the eighth power of that parameter; stating this convention fixes the quantitative meaning of the counting error.

This counts labelled copies preserving the specified vertex of a hypergraph classes, for arbitrary intersection patterns among the hypergraph edges, and includes the three-uniform tetrahedron as a special case.
For a common host vertex of a hypergraph set of size n, the same product average counts homomorphisms. At most (2v​)nv−1 assignments identify a pair of distinct labelled vertices of a hypergraph, by the union bound. Thus restricting to injective copies changes the estimate by at most this amount. With a common normalized subset density p the injective count is therefore pmnv+O(mα1/8nv+v2nv−1). For induced copies, include the nonhypergraph edge indicator functions as additional factors; their balanced functions are negatives of the corresponding edge-balanced functions and have the same box norm. The same proof applies provided all the relevant triple parts satisfy the stated quasirandomness condition.
