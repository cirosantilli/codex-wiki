The hierarchy problem concerns the enormous separation between the electroweak scale and a possible fundamental ultraviolet scale such as the reduced Planck mass: v≃246GeV whereas MPl​≃2.4×1018GeV. For an elementary Higgs field, heavy states or a physical high-energy ultraviolet cutoff generically generate
δmH2​∼16π2κ​ΛUV2​.
(1)
Here κ represents the appropriate dimensionless couplings. Maintaining a small physical Higgs mass then requires a very accurate cancellation against its bare mass parameter. The problem is sensitivity to actual high-energy physics, rather than merely the presence of a quadratic divergence in one choice of regulator.
The cosmological constant problem concerns the much smaller gravitationally observable vacuum energy. In the Einstein equation, a constant term in the effective action contributes to the cosmological constant, with Λeff​=Λbare​+ρvac​/MPl2​ in the usual normalization. Generic zero-point contributions scale as ΛUV4​, and vacuum transitions also change ρvac​. The dark-energy density is only of order 10−47GeV4∼(a few meV)4. The puzzle is why the total remains so small despite much larger individual contributions. A constant shift of the nongravitational Hamiltonian cannot remove its gravitational effect.
Supersymmetry relates bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom, including their interactions. Their opposite loop signs cancel the quadratic contributions to a scalar field mass in the unbroken theory. With soft supersymmetry breaking, the surviving correction is controlled by the mass splitting, schematically
δmH2​∼16π2g2​msoft2​logmsoft​ΛUV​​.
(2)
Thus a sufficiently low soft-breaking scale protects a small electroweak scale against a much higher ultraviolet scale. This is the useful supersymmetric response to the hierarchy problem.
Exact global supersymmetry also cancels vacuum energy: the supersymmetry algebra expresses the Hamiltonian as a positive sum of anticommutators, so a vacuum annihilated by every supercharge has zero energy. Equivalently, with canonical kinetic terms,
V=∑i​∣Fi​∣2+21​∑a​(Da)2
(3)
vanishes in a supersymmetric vacuum. However, ordinary particles and their superpartners cannot have the identical mass spectrum of unbroken supersymmetry, so a useful phenomenological theory needs supersymmetry breaking. Its vacuum energy is generically of order the fourth power of the breaking scale; even a scale of order 102GeV gives about 108GeV4, enormously above the required value. In supergravity, a negative term can cancel positive breaking contributions:
VF​=eK/MPl2​(Kijˉ​Di​WDjˉ​​W−MPl2​3∣W∣2​),Di​W=Wi​+MPl2​Ki​W​,
(4)
but arranging a tiny residual is a new tuning. Consequently supersymmetry can protect the hierarchy after soft breaking, while its exact vacuum-energy cancellation does not by itself survive realistic breaking.
One higher-dimensional proposal uses large extra dimensions. Take n flat compact dimensions of common radius R, with gravity propagating in the bulk and Standard Model fields confined to a four-dimensional brane. Reducing the gravitational Einstein-Hilbert action gives
2M∗n+2​​∫d4xdny−G​R4+n​ ⟶ 2M∗n+2​(2πR)n​∫d4x−g​R4​,
(5)
so
MPl2​=M∗n+2​(2πR)n.​
(6)
A fundamental gravitational scale M∗​ near a TeV then produces apparently weak four-dimensional gravity through dilution into the large compact volume. For n=2 and M∗​=1TeV, R=MPl​/(2πM∗2​) is about 0.075mm, using 1GeV−1≃1.97×10−16m. At distances much smaller than R, the gravitational potential scales as r−(n+1); at distances much larger than R it scales as r−1. Confining charged Standard Model fields to the brane avoids a light tower of charged Kaluza-Klein modes associated with the large radius. The primary explanatory challenge is radius stabilization: one must generate and stabilize RM∗​≫1, rather than simply postulate a huge volume and transfer the original hierarchy into a modulus.
A second proposal is the Randall–Sundrum model. Compactify on an interval, equivalently an orbifold with branes at y=0 and y=L, and use the warped metric
ds2=e−2k∣y∣ημν​dxμdxν+dy2.
(7)
A negative bulk cosmological constant and suitably related brane tensions support this geometry. Integrating over the full orbifold interval [−L,L] gives
MPl2​=M53​∫−LL​e−2k∣y∣dy=kM53​​(1−e−2kL).
(8)
For a scalar field h on the brane at L, its induced metric gives the kinetic coefficient e−2kL and mass coefficient e−4kLm02​. The canonical field is hc​=e−kLh, hence
mphys​=e−kLm0​.​
(9)
With k and M5​ of the same fundamental order, an exponent kL≃35--37 converts a fundamental mass into a weak-scale mass, without requiring a large proper compactification volume.
Again the separation must be selected dynamically. The original warped solution leaves a massless radion, so merely choosing L does not complete the explanation. For example, the Goldberger–Wise stabilization mechanism introduces a bulk scalar field with boundary values v0​,vL​ and a small mass mχ​. Its static equation and solution are
χ′′−4kχ′−mχ2​χ=0,χ=Ae(4+ϵ)ky+Be−ϵky,ϵ=4+mχ2​/k2​−2≃4k2mχ2​​.
(10)
For small backreaction and large kL, the leading radius-dependent potential is proportional to e−4kL(vL​−v0​e−ϵkL)2. Its leading minimum obeys kL≃ϵ−1log(v0​/vL​), so a modest boundary-value ratio and a small ϵ can give the required exponent. Corrections and the residual four-dimensional cosmological constant must still be controlled. Both proposals need a stable dynamical origin for the compactification geometry; their volume or warp relations alone do not supply it.

Use the canonical chiral-superfield component expansion Φ=ϕ+2​θψ+θθF in chiral coordinates, with ψψ=ψαψα​. Multiplication of the Grassmann variables gives
[Φ2]θθ​=2ϕF−ψψ,[Φ3]θθ​=3ϕ2F−3ϕψψ.
(11)
For example, the two linear-in-θ factors in Φ2 contribute −θθψψ, while each of the three choices of the remaining scalar in Φ3 gives the same contribution. Thus the required F-term is
[W]θθ​=(mϕ+gϕ2)F−21​(m+2gϕ)ψψ.​
(12)
Adding its Hermitian conjugate and the canonical F∗F term eliminates the auxiliary field through F=−mϕ+gϕ2​ and yields the F-term scalar potential
V=∣mϕ+gϕ2∣2=∣ϕ∣2∣m+gϕ∣2.
(13)
It is nonnegative, and for g=0 its absolute minima are ϕ=0 and ϕ=−m/g, each with zero energy. They coincide when m=0. If g=0,m=0, only ϕ=0 is a minimum; if g=m=0, every ϕ is a minimum. These exhaust the local minima as well: at any other stationary point Vϕ​=W′′W′=0 forces W′′=0; for mg=0 this is ϕ=−m/(2g), whose quadratic variation has opposite signs in two real directions and is a saddle.
Expand around ϕ=0, writing ϕ=h=(h1​+ih2​)/2​. The scalar potential and fermionic terms are
VLfermion​​=∣m∣2∣h∣2+m∗gh2h∗+mg∗h(h∗)2+∣g∣2∣h∣4,⊃−21​mψψ−ghψψ+h.c.​
(14)
The canonical kinetic term is 21​[(∂h1​)2+(∂h2​)2]. Its quadratic potential is 21​∣m∣2(h12​+h22​), while the Weyl fermion has mass ∣m∣. Hence
mh1​​=mh2​​=mψ​=∣m∣.​
(15)
At the other minimum, W′′=−m, so the same mass equality holds. The quartic and Yukawa interaction are both fixed by g: defining V⊃λ∣h∣4 and L⊃−yhψψ+h.c. gives
y=g,λ=∣g∣2=∣y∣2.​
(16)
This is the supersymmetric relation between quartic and Yukawa couplings. The printed equality of the two couplings refers to their common supersymmetric origin; literal equality of the conventional coefficients λ and y is not true for arbitrary g. If instead the Yukawa term is defined as −21​yhψψ, then y=2g and λ=∣y∣2/4.
To establish perturbative superpotential non-renormalization, use a Wilsonian effective action with a fixed positive infrared cutoff, so integrating out modes does not introduce massless infrared singularities. Promote m,g to nondynamical chiral superfields, or spurions. The holomorphic superpotential depends holomorphically on Φ,m,g, not on their complex conjugates. Assign an ordinary U(1) charge and an R-charge by
qR​Φ11​m−20​g−3−1​​.
(17)
The superspace coordinate has R(θ)=1, so d2θ has charge −2 and W must have R-charge 2. Both original monomials satisfy the two selection rules. A holomorphic perturbative monomial magbΦc must obey
−2a−3b+c=0,−b+c=2,
(18)
which gives c=b+2 and a=1−b. Equivalently,
Weff​=mΦ2f(gΦ/m),Weffpert​=∑b≥0​cb​gbm1−bΦb+2.
(19)
The nonnegative integer b follows from perturbative analyticity in g. Regularity as m→0 at the fixed Wilsonian cutoff excludes b≥2, which would require negative powers of m. The remaining quadratic coefficient is fixed to c0​=1/2 by the free theory at g=0. The coefficient c1​ is 1/3: a three-point contribution at first order in g has just one cubic vertex, uses all three legs externally, and cannot contain a loop. A loop correction would require additional interactions, whereas the selection rules and holomorphy have already excluded the corresponding monomials. Therefore
Weffpert​=21​mΦ2+31​gΦ3.​
(20)
This proof concerns the local Wilsonian superpotential. The Kähler potential can acquire a wave-function factor Z, and canonical normalization changes the couplings to mc​=m/Z and gc​=g/Z3/2. Such running, and nonlocal infrared terms in a one-particle-irreducible action, do not contradict the holomorphic result.

Assume global four-dimensional N=1 supersymmetry with canonical kinetic terms and no explicit gaugino mass. Write Wi​=∂i​W and Wij​=∂i​∂j​W. To match the displayed stationarity equation, use Fi=Wi​​; this differs by a minus sign from the eliminated auxiliary field in the component convention of Solution 2. The real D-terms are Da=−ga(ϕˉ​i​(Ta)ij​ϕj+ξa), with Hermitian gauge generators. A constant Fayet–Iliopoulos term is permitted only for an appropriate Abelian factor. The scalar potential is
V=Wi​Wi​​+21​∑a​(Da)2.
(21)
Differentiate with respect to ϕi, holding ϕˉ​ independent:
∂i​Da=−gaϕˉ​j​(Ta)ji​,∂i​V=Wij​Fj−∑a​gaDaϕˉ​j​(Ta)ji​.
(22)
Thus stationary point conditions gives the first requested identity. Gauge invariance of the superpotential gives Wi​(Ta)ij​ϕj=0. Conjugating it and using Hermiticity gives ϕˉ​j​(Ta)ji​Fi=0, the second condition in the form needed below.
At the vacuum, define Hij​=⟨Wij​⟩ and Gia​=ga⟨ϕˉ​j​⟩(Ta)ji​. The two conditions combine as
M0​(⟨F⟩⟨D⟩​)=0,M0​=(H−GT​−G0​).​
(23)
Here the upper equation is stationarity and the lower equation is conjugated gauge invariance. The chiral-superfield fermion mass matrix contributes −21​Hij​ψiψj, and the gauge Yukawa interaction contributes the mixed chiral-fermion/gaugino bilinear. With canonical Weyl fermion and gaugino kinetic terms, and choosing the gaugino phase to put the mixed bilinear in real-sign convention, these terms are
Lmass​=−21​(ψ​λ​)M(ψλ​)+h.c.,M=(H−2​GT​−2​G0​).
(24)
The factor 2​ is important: the matrix directly acting on the printed (F,D) is the same mass bilinear with rescaled gauginos, rather than literally the canonical matrix. In detail, for S=diag(I,2​I),
M=SM0​S,M(⟨F⟩⟨D⟩/2​​)=0.​
(25)
Since supersymmetry breaking makes this vector nonzero, there is at least one zero mass eigenvalue. A normalized massless fermion is
η=(∑i​∣⟨Fi⟩∣2+21​∑a​⟨Da⟩2)1/2∑i​⟨Fi⟩∗ψi+∑a​⟨Da⟩λa/2​​.
(26)
With the consistent gaugino phase, the inhomogeneous vacuum supersymmetry transformation of (ψ,λ) is proportional to the same vector (F,D/2​). Therefore η shifts by a nonzero constant times the transformation parameter: it is the Goldstino, the Goldstone fermion of broken global supersymmetry. The argument guarantees at least one Goldstino zero mode, not the absence of other massless fermions.
For the second calculation, first take only canonical chiral superfields and F-term breaking. At a stationary vacuum v, let h=ϕ−v, mij​=Wij​(v) and
Aij​=∑k​Wki​​Wkj​,Bij​=∑k​Wk​​Wkij​.
(27)
These follow by differentiating V=∑k​∣Wk​∣2 twice. The quadratic scalar potential is
V(2)=h†Ah+21​(hTBh+h.c.),A=m†m.
(28)
The doubled scalar mass matrix has blocks
Ms2​=(AB​B†AT​).
(29)
Transforming to the 2N canonically normalized real components of h preserves its trace, so the sum of real scalar mass squares is trMs2​=2trA. The Weyl fermion mass squares are the eigenvalues of m†m=A and their sum is trA. Each Weyl mass eigenstate has two physical spin states. The printed supertrace has scalar sign −1 and fermion weight +2, hence
STrM2=−trMs2​+2tr(m†m)=−2trA+2trA=0.​
(30)
The frequently used boson-minus-fermion definition has the opposite overall sign and gives the same zero. The mixing block B can split scalar masses but cannot change this trace. This proves the tree-level supertrace mass sum rule; it uses canonical kinetic terms and a renormalizable global theory, rather than a general supergravity or noncanonical effective theory.
In the MSSM, trying to make all squarks and sleptons heavy through direct canonical tree-level F-term breaking encounters this constraint. In an unbroken electric-charge and color sector, the scalar mass-squared trace is twice the trace for its ordinary fermionic partners. More explicitly, take a unit vector u minimizing u†Au. For h=eiαu, choose α so that Re(e2iαuTBu)≤0. The curvature along this real scalar direction is then at most u†Au, so at least one scalar mass square is no larger than the smallest fermion mass square in that sector. For one isolated pair the scalar masses are mf2​±∣B∣. Thus light quarks can force an unacceptably light squark, or a tachyon, under these assumptions. A hidden supersymmetry-breaking sector with mediation produces effective soft supersymmetry breaking; radiative effects, noncanonical kinetic terms, or supergravity can change the assumptions and allow a viable spectrum. The trace over an arbitrary enlarged theory alone would not locate the light state in the visible sector; the MSSM conclusion uses the conserved-charge block structure.

Use an unfluxed toroidal zero-mode dimensional reduction, with no projection removing fields. A ten-dimensional gauge field has 10−2=8 physical polarizations per gauge generator. Its Majorana-Weyl spinor partner has sixteen real components before the massless equation, and eight propagating fermionic states. Thus ten-dimensional super Yang-Mills theory has sixteen real supercharges and eight bosonic plus eight fermionic states per generator.
Split the spacetime index as M=(μ,m), with μ=0,…,3 and six internal directions. The zero modes of AM​ become Aμ​ and six real adjoint scalar fields Xm​=Am​. The spinor representation decomposes under Spin(1,3)×Spin(6) into four four-dimensional Weyl spinors; the Majorana condition supplies their conjugates rather than independent additional spinors. All sixteen supercharges survive, giving four-dimensional N=4 super Yang-Mills theory. The supermultiplet is therefore one gauge vector, four Weyl gauginos and six real scalars, all in the Adjoint representation, with 2+6=8 bosonic and 4×2=8 fermionic states. The internal Spin(6)≃SU(4) acts as R-symmetry, with the scalars in its six-dimensional representation and the Weyl gauginos in its four-dimensional representation.
Since the internal derivatives vanish, the internal Yang-Mills field strength is Fmn​=−ig4​[Xm​,Xn​] in a Hermitian-generator convention. The internal part of the gauge kinetic term becomes the scalar potential
V=4g42​​m,n,a∑​(fabcXmb​Xnc​)2=4g42​​m,n∑​tr([Xm​,Xn​]†[Xm​,Xn​]).​
(31)
Here the invariant trace is normalized by tr(TaTb)=δab and [Ta,Tb]=ifabcTc; the coupling and scalar kinetic terms use the same normalization. The sum includes both orders of each pair. For Hermitian Xm​, the commutator is anti-Hermitian, so equivalently V=−4g42​​∑m,n​tr[Xm​,Xn​]2. It is nonnegative and vanishes exactly when all six adjoint scalars commute. This gives the classical vacuum moduli space of a supersymmetric gauge theory.
Interpret AMNP​ as a massless three-form gauge field, with transformation A3​↦A3​+dΛ2​ and kinetic term built from F4​=dA3​. The massless p-form gauge field count is an on-shell count, rather than the raw (3D​) tensor components. For a null plane wave, gauge transformations remove the components along the null gauge direction, while the field equation removes the complementary longitudinal components. The independent polarization is consequently an antisymmetric tensor on the D−2 transverse directions. Thus
n3​(D)=(3D−2​).​
(32)
This argument incorporates the redundancies in the two-form gauge parameter; subtracting (2D​) once from (3D​) would not do so.
Let n=D−4 and split indices into four-dimensional and internal ones. The toroidal reduction of a p-form gauge field gives one Aμνρ​, n two-forms Aμνi​, (2n​) vectors Aμij​ and (3n​) scalars Aijk​. A massless three-form in four dimensions has no local propagating state; each massless two-form has one, each vector two and each scalar one. The two-form state can equivalently be represented by a scalar using massless p-form duality: its three-form field strength satisfies H3​=∗da. Consequently
n4D​​=0+n+2(2n​)+(3n​)=n+n(n−1)+6n(n−1)(n−2)​=6n(n+1)(n+2)​=(3D−2​).​
(33)
This verifies the required matching, including D=6 where the count is 2+2=4. The nondynamical four-dimensional three-form can carry a constant flux parameter, but that is not an additional propagating degree of freedom.
Eleven-dimensional supergravity contains the metric GMN​, a three-form AMNP​ and a Majorana gravitino ΨM​. The massless graviton is a symmetric traceless tensor of the nine-dimensional transverse rotation group, giving 9×10/2−1=44 states. The three-form gives (39​)=84. The gravitino is a gamma-traceless transverse vector-spinor: a spinor representation of Spin(9) has sixteen real components, so it gives 9×16−16=128 states. Hence the eleven-dimensional balance is 44+84=128 bosonic states and 128 fermionic states.
Reduction on a circle yields type IIA supergravity, with thirty-two real supercharges and opposite ten-dimensional chiralities. The metric decomposes into the ten-dimensional metric, the Kaluza-Klein vector Cμ​=Gμ11​ and the radius scalar, or dilaton. Their counts are 35+8+1=44. The three-form gives a ten-dimensional three-form Cμνρ​ and a two-form Bμν​=Aμν11​, with (38​)+(28​)=56+28=84 states. The eleven-dimensional Majorana spinor decomposes into two opposite-chirality Majorana-Weyl spinors. The external gravitino components give two gravitini, each with 8×8−8=56 states; the internal component supplies two spin-one-half dilatini, each with eight states. Therefore
10D IIA: 35+8+1+56+28=128,2×56+2×8=128.​
(34)
The two-form is in the NS--NS sector and the one-form and three-form are the Ramond--Ramond potentials. This reduction is nonchiral IIA rather than IIB.
Reduction on a seven-torus yields four-dimensional N=8 supergravity, again preserving thirty-two real supercharges. From GMN​ one gets one graviton, seven vectors Gμi​ and 7×8/2=28 scalars Gij​, giving 2+7×2+28=44 states. From AMNP​ one gets a nonpropagating four-dimensional three-form, seven two-forms, (27​)=21 vectors and (37​)=35 scalars, giving 0+7+21×2+35=84 states. Dualizing the seven two-forms to scalars gives the familiar field content
4D: one graviton, 28 vectors, 70 real scalars;2+28×2+70=128.​
(35)
The gravitino reduces to eight four-dimensional Majorana gravitini and 7×8=56 spin-one-half Majorana fermions, after the external gravitini are separated from their gamma-trace mixing with the internal components. Their count is 8×2+56×2=128. Together these fields form the single maximal supergravity multiplet.

Use left-chiral superfields throughout, abbreviating the conjugate singlets by Ui​=uˉic​, Di​=dˉic​, Ei​=eˉic​ and Ni​=νˉic​. The printed hypercharge convention is the negative of the usual one, so Qem​=T3​−Y; consequently H1​ is the down-type and H2​ the up-type Higgs doublet. Write A⋅B=ϵαβ​AαBβ for the weak-doublet contraction. Assign baryon number B(Q)=1/3, B(U)=B(D)=−1/3, and lepton number L(L)=1, L(E)=L(N)=−1; both Higgs fields have B=L=0.
The baryon number and lepton number conserving homogeneous cubic superpotential is
W3,cons​=(yu​)ij​(Qi​⋅H2​)Uj​+(yd​)ij​(Qi​⋅H1​)Dj​+(ye​)ij​(Li​⋅H1​)Ej​+(yν​)ij​(Li​⋅H2​)Nj​.​
(36)
Each term has zero hypercharge, is a color and weak singlet, and has B=L=0. Higgs expectation values give the usual quark and charged-lepton Yukawa interaction masses and, here, Dirac neutrino masses. The complete remaining cubic part is
W3,viol​=​21​λijk​(Li​⋅Lj​)Ek​+λijk′​(Li​⋅Qj​)Dk​+21​λijk′′​ϵabcUi,a​Dj,b​Dk,c​+κi​Ni​(H1​⋅H2​)+61​ρijk​Ni​Nj​Nk​.​
(37)
The first two terms have ΔL=+1, the third has ΔB=−1, the fourth has ΔL=−1, and the last has ΔL=−3. Their Hermitian conjugates reverse these signs. Commutativity of chiral superfields and the antisymmetric weak/color contractions give λijk​=−λjik​ and λijk′′​=−λikj′′​; ρ can be taken completely symmetric. All other coupling tensors are unrestricted complex tensors.
To check completeness, a cubic color singlet containing a quark doublet and an antiquark singlet must contain a colorless weak doublet; the allowed hypercharges give QH2​U, QH1​D and QLD. Three color-antitriplet singlets can form a singlet with ϵabc, but hypercharge permits only UDD. Three quark doublets cannot be a weak singlet. With no colored fields, an even number of weak doublets is necessary. Two doublets and a singlet give precisely LH1​E, LLE, LH2​N and H1​H2​N; contractions of two identical Higgs superfields vanish. With no doublets the only neutral triple is NNN. Thus W3​=W3,cons​+W3,viol​ is the most general homogeneous cubic superpotential for the printed fields, including their singlet neutrinos.
If “cubic” is intended to mean a polynomial of degree at most three, also add
W≤2​=μH1​⋅H2​+ϵi​Li​⋅H2​+21​Mij​Ni​Nj​+ti​Ni​+W0​,Mij​=Mji​.
(38)
The μ term conserves both charges; the other nonconstant terms have respectively ΔL=1,−2,−1. This distinction will matter for the printed claim about R-parity.
For squark-mediated proton decay, choose λ11k′​ and λ11k′′​ with k=2 or 3. The same down-type singlet squark can appear in both vertices; k=1 would give λ111′′​=0 by antisymmetry. The first term includes an electron/up-quark/squark vertex, and the second an up-quark/down-quark/squark vertex. Exchanging a squark of mass M and expanding its propagator at momenta much smaller than M produces the dimension-six effective operator
Leff​⊃Cϵabc​(uLa​eL​)(uRb​dRc​)+h.c.,C∼M2λ11k′​λ11k′′​∗​.
(39)
The left- and right-handed pairs are separately contracted spinor bilinears. This operator converts an up/down quark pair into a positron and an antiquark; the spectator up quark can combine with the antiquark into the neutral pion. Equivalently its hadronic matrix element between a proton and a pion is permitted, so it induces p→e+π0. Having both the baryon number and lepton number violating vertices is what enables this channel.
Since C has mass dimension −2, dimensional analysis gives
Γ(p→e+π0)∼ch​M4∣λ11k′​λ11k′′​∣2​mp5​.​
(40)
The dimensionless ch​ includes the hadronic matrix element and two-body phase space; the pion mass changes this factor rather than the leading dimensions. Dimensional grounds alone do not give a precise hadronic coefficient. Using the lifetime supplied in the paper, rather than substituting a different bound, gives Γ<1/(2.4×1064)GeV=4.17×10−65GeV and hence
∣λ11k′​λ11k′′​∣<ch​(2.4×1064GeV−1)mp5​​M2​≃6.5×10−27ch−1/2​(1TeVM​)2(mp​1GeV​)5/2.​
(41)
For an illustrative 100GeV squark this is about 6.5×10−29ch−1/2​ when mp​ is rounded to 1GeV. The bound constrains the relevant flavour product and depends on the exchanged mass; separate smallness of each coupling does not follow without an additional assumption.
Define matter parity and R-parity by
PM​=(−1)3(B−L),Rp​=(−1)3(B−L)+2s=PM​(−1)2s.​
(42)
Every matter superfield Q,U,D,L,E,N has PM​=−1, whereas H1​,H2​ and the vector superfields have PM​=+1. For ordinary quarks and antiquarks, 3(B−L)=±1 is odd and 2s=1, so Rp​=+1. For charged leptons, neutrinos and their antiparticles, 3(B−L)=∓3 is odd and 2s=1, again giving Rp​=+1; the singlet neutrino fermion obeys the same rule. Gluons, the photon, W and Z have B=L=0 and spin one, hence Rp​=+1; Higgs bosons have B=L=s=0 and also Rp​=+1. Thus every Standard Model particle is R-even, including the corresponding antiparticles. Changing spin by one half within a supermultiplet reverses Rp​, so squarks, sleptons, gauginos and Higgsinos are R-odd.
Each conserving cubic term contains two R-odd matter superfields and one even Higgs superfield, so it is even under matter parity. Each term in W3,viol​ has an odd number of matter superfields: three for LLE,LQD,UDD,NNN, and one for NH1​H2​. All are therefore forbidden. At the component level the two fermion spin factors in a Yukawa interaction multiply to +1, so the same selection rule follows directly from R-parity. This verifies all cubic baryon/lepton-number violating terms are forbidden while the Yukawa fermion mass terms survive.
The blanket assertion needs a qualification beyond homogeneous cubic terms. The Higgs μ term is even; LH2​ and N are odd and forbidden. But Ni​Nj​ is even and permits a Majorana neutrino mass despite violating lepton number by two. Likewise the higher-dimensional operators QQQL and (LH2​)2 are even. Consequently R-parity alone does not enforce all-order conservation of baryon number and lepton number, or absolute proton stability; the stated precise exclusion holds for the violating cubic interactions above.
Two physical implications follow from multiplicative conservation of R-parity. Superpartners are produced in even numbers from an initial state consisting only of Standard Model particles, leading to pair production at the lowest threshold and parity-conserving cascades. The lightest supersymmetric particle is stable, since one R-odd particle cannot decay exclusively into R-even particles and no lighter R-odd state is available. A neutral weakly interacting lightest supersymmetric particle can therefore be a dark-matter candidate and produce missing-energy signatures; neutrality and a suitable abundance require further model assumptions.
